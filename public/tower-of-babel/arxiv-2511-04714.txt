On anti-hyperbolicity for hyperkähler varieties
Ljudmila Kamenova

Abstract




Abstract

By restricting to (a linear subspace of) an affine chart in projective space, a complex stably rational or unirational manifold of dimension
m
is meromorphically dominable by
{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
, i.e., admits a meromorphic dominating map from
{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
. So are varieties that are birational to abelian varieties and Kummer K3 surfaces. G. Buzzard and the second author have shown that elliptic K3 surfaces are holomorphically dominable by
{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
, i.e. admitting a holomorphic map with nontrivial jacobian. In this paper we explore various examples and criteria for meromorphic and holomorphic dominability by
{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
of certain hyperkähler manifolds, generalizing some known results about K3 surfaces.
Anti-hyperbolicity has several interpretations in the sense of vanishing of the Kobayashi-Royden metrics, admitting dense entire holomorphic curves, or dominating holomorphic or meromorphic maps from the complex affine space of the same dimension.

1
Introduction

It has been conjectured by Green-Griffiths as a natural generalization of
the Little Picard theorem that a nontrivial holomorphic image of
{\mathbb{C}}
in
a (complex) variety of general type is algebraically degenerate, i.e. not
Zariski dense. If true, then so would any holomorphic curve be in a variety
that fibers over a variety of general type. In view of the existence of
the core map, see
[
Cam
,
L2
]
, any projective variety
X
admits a meromorphic fibration over a nontrivial “orbivariety” base of general type unless
X
is special. Clearly, an “orbifold” generalization of the above conjecture would imply the same algebraic degeneracy condition for holomorphic curves in non-special
X
. So it is interesting to investigate the possible existence of Zariski dense holomorphic curves in special varieties in each dimension
n
, and thus the meromorphic dominability by
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
of such varieties.

Although dominability implies the existence of Zariski dense holomorphic
curves, the converse is completely open. Easily established dominable
manifolds include meromorphic images of abelian
varieties (such as Kummer varieties), stably rational and unirational manifolds. More recently elliptic K3 surfaces
have been shown to be dominable
[
BL1
]
. These are special varieties in the above sense and all manifolds with trivial canonical bundle such as abelian varieties and K3 surfaces are special. The purpose of the present paper is to generalize the results of
[
BL1
]
, which restricts to compact complex surfaces, to a higher dimensional generalization of K3
surfaces called hyperkähler manifolds or holomorphic symplectic varieties.
We prove that Hilbert schemes of points on dominable K3 surfaces are
dominable. The generalized Kummer varieties are also dominable. So are
hyperkähler manifolds that admit more than one fibration structures.

Theorem 1.1:
Let
M
be a hyperkähler manifold or a primitive symplectic variety of dimension
2n
. If
M
admits two distinct Lagrangian fibration structures (necessarily generically transversal), then
M
is meromorphically dominable by
{\mathbb{C}}^{2n}
.

In order to prove dominability of hyperkähler manifolds with one
fibration structure, we will assume in this paper the hoped for fact that the base of the fibration is projective space and that there are no multiple fibers in codimension one of the base. Locally, multiple fibers might exist,
but globally it is not known if there are examples of multiple fibers
in codimension one of hyperkähler Lagrangian fibrations. It is a
classical result that elliptic K3 surfaces do not have multiple fibers
(see for example, Proposition 1.6. in Ch.11 of
[
Hu2
]
).

Theorem 1.2:
Let
M
be a hyperkähler manifold or a primitive symplectic variety of dimension
2n
. If
M
is projective and admits a Lagrangian fibration
f:M{\>\longrightarrow\>}{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{n}

with no multiple fibers in codimension one of the base, then
M
is holomorphically dominable by
{\mathbb{C}}^{2n}
.

One of the biggest recent breakthroughs in anti-hyperbolicity since the case of surfaces done almost a quarter century ago in
[
BL1
,
BL2
,
L1
]
is found in the paper of Campana-Winkelmann
[
CW
]
where they proved that rationally connected varieties contain dense entire curves. Here we provide natural generalizations of some their other results in special cases to dominability. Given an abelian fibration with no multiple fibers in codimension one over a log-Fano variety, we prove that it contains a dense entire curve. This generalizes Campana-Winkelmann’s result
[
CW
, Proposition 7.8]
for elliptic fibrations.

Theorem 1.3:
Given a projective manifold
M
with an abelian fibration
f:M{\>\longrightarrow\>}B
with no multiple fibres in codimension one over a log-Fano variety
B
, then there exists a dense entire curve.

Basic hyperkähler geometry

Definition 2.1:
A
hyperkähler manifold

compact Kähler manifold with
\pi_{1}(M)=0

H^{0}(M,\Omega^{2}_{M})={\mathbb{C}}\omega
, where

Example 2.2:
We recall Beauville’s standard series of examples,
[
Be
]

In each possible complex dimension

together with their deformations. One of them is the Hilbert scheme

of

\operatorname{Sym}^{n}(S)

\operatorname{Hilb}^{n}(S){\>\longrightarrow\>}\operatorname{Sym}^{n}(S)
.
The second example is the
generalized Kummer variety,

, of dimension

,
where

is an abelian surface. Here is
the basic construction. Consider the Hilbert-Chow morphism
composed with the summation map given by the group structure on

\operatorname{Hilb}^{n+1}(A){\>\longrightarrow\>}\operatorname{Sym}^{n+1}(A){\>\longrightarrow\>}A.

Beauville (
[
Be
]

of the

.

Example 2.3:
There are two exceptional examples (

and

Let

is the minimal desingularization of the Mukai moduli space of rank-two

All of the known hyperkähler examples admit fibration structures in a small deformation. By Matsushita’s theorem, the possible fibration structure on a hyperkähler manifold is quite restricted.

Theorem 2.4:
(Matsushita,
[
M
]
)
Let

-factorial log-terminal singularities. The general fiber of

Remark 2.5:

In all of the known cases of Lagrangian fibrations on hyperkähler manifolds, the base

is smooth. Hwang

Let

be a hyperkähler manifold.
On

there is a natural primitive integral quadratic form,
called the
Beauville-Bogomolov-Fujiki form
, or the
BBF form

for short. The simplest way to define it is via the Fujiki relation
below. The classical definition can be found in
[
Be
]
and

[
Hu1
]
, for example.

Theorem 2.6:
(Fujiki,
[
F
]
)
Let
\eta\in H^{2}(M,{\mathbb{Z}})
and
\dim M=2n
, where

is a
hyperkähler manifold. Then
\int_{M}\eta^{2n}=c\cdot q(\eta,\eta)^{n}
,
for a primitive integral quadratic form
q
on
H^{2}(M,{\mathbb{Z}})
, where

constant depending on the topological type of

in Fujiki’s
formula is called the

.
More generally, if
\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{2n}\in H^{2}(M)
, then

\int_{M}\eta_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\eta_{2n}=\frac{c}{(2n)!}\sum_{\sigma}q(\eta_{\sigma_{1}},\eta_{\sigma_{2}})\dots q(\eta_{\sigma_{2n-1}},\eta_{\sigma_{2n}}).

Remark 2.7:

If there is a multisection of a Lagrangian fibration, then the total space is projective. For our purpose, this says that if a hyperkähler manifold admits two Lagrangian fibrations, then the manifold is projective, which we can easily see by taking the sum of two nef classes
L_{1},L_{2}
defining the two fibrations, and noticing that
q(L_{1}+L_{2})=2q(L_{1},L_{2})>0
(as in the proof of
[
KLV
, Theorem 2.11]
), then apply Huybrechts’ projectivity criterion.

Definition 2.8:
Let
\eta\in H^{1,1}(M)
be a real (1,1)-class on
a hyperkähler manifold
M
. We say that
\eta

is

if
q(\eta,\eta)=0
.
A line bundle

is parabolic.

If

is a Lagrangian fibration and the class

is ample, then the pull-back

is nef and trivially satisfies

by

Definition 2.9:
Let
L
be a holomorphic line bundle
on a hyperkähler manifold. We call

if it is parabolic and semiample.

Conjecture 2.10:
(Hyperkähler SYZ Conjecture)
If a hyperkähler manifold

has a non-trivial parabolic nef line bundle

, then
|mL|
is base point free for some

and defines a Lagrangian fibration

.

Definition 2.11:
Let

be a hyperkähler manifold.
Fix a parabolic class

We denote by

The space

Theorem 2.12:
(Kamenova, Verbitsky,

]
)

Let

Definition 2.13:
Let
M
be a compact complex manifold, and let

be a connected component of its diffeomorphism

The Teichmüller space is finite-dimensional for a Calabi-Yau

Definition 2.14:
Let

From the properties of ergodic structures established by M. Verbitsky

A lot of the fundamental results in hyperkähler geometry can be generalized in the singular setting for primitive symplectic varieties.

Definition 2.16:
A
primitive symplectic variety
is a normal compact Kähler variety
X
with rational singularities, such that
H^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})=0
, and for a symplectic form
\sigma
,

H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X}^{[2]})={\mathbb{C}}\sigma

Definition 2.17:
Let
X
be a primitive symplectic variety. A
rational Lagrangian fibration
is a meromorphic map
f:X\dashrightarrow B
to a normal Kähler variety

such that
f
has connected fibers and its general fiber is a Lagrangian subvariety of

. If

is given by the linear system
|mL|
for some

Conjecture 2.18:
(Rational SYZ Conjecture)
Let
X
be a primitive symplectic variety. If a nontrivial movable line bundle

on

satisfies
q_{X}(L)=0
, then

induces a rational Lagrangian fibration
f:X\dashrightarrow B
.

Let us summarize Matsushita’s and Hwang’s theorems in the singular setting, as in
[
KL
, Theorem 2.8]
.

Theorem 2.19:
Let
X
be a primitive symplectic variety of dimension
2n
and let
f:X\rightarrow B
be a surjective holomorphic map with connected fibers to a normal Kähler variety
B
with
0<\dim B<2n
. Then the following holds.

1.

The base
B
is a projective variety with Picard rank
\rho(B)=1
, in particular,

is projective and has

-factorial, log-terminal singularities. Furthermore,

2.

The morphism

does not surject onto

3.

All smooth fibers are abelian varieties.

4.

If, in addition,

.

4
Dominability criteria

Definition 4.1:
An
n
-dimensional complex manifold
M
is
(meromorphically)
dominable
by
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
if there is a meromorphic dominating map

F:{\mathbb{C}}^{n}\dashrightarrow M
such that the Jacobian determinant

\det(DF)
is not identically zero in the domain of
F
. If the map
F

is holomorphic, then
M
is
holomorphically dominable
by
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
.
By abuse of notation, we will at times use dominable to mean dominable by
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
.

Example 4.2:
In
[
BL1
]
, it is shown that if
S
is a compact
complex surface birationally equivalent to an elliptic K3 surface or to a
Kummer K3 surface, then
S
is holomorphically dominable by
{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
.

Definition 4.3:
A complex projective variety
M
is
unirational
if there is a dominant
rational map
{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{n}\dashrightarrow M
. It is
stably rational
if

M\times{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{l}
is rational for some
l
. It is
rationally connected

if any two points of M are contained in a rational curve, i.e. a holomorphic
image of
{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
.

Remark 4.4:
The following implications are clear (among projective varieties):

rational
=>
stably rational
=>
unirational
=>
meromorphically
dominable by
{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
; unirational
=>
rationally connected.

Proposition 4.5:
Let
S
be a compact complex surface dominable by
{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
.
Then the Hilbert scheme
Hilb^{n}(S)
is meromorphically dominable by

{\mathbb{C}}^{2n}
.

Proof:
Indeed,
{\mathbb{C}}^{2n}
dominates
S^{n}
.
There is a natural quotient map

S^{n}{\>\longrightarrow\>}S^{n}/\Sigma_{n}=\operatorname{Sym}^{n}(S),

where
\Sigma_{n}
is the symmetric group of
n
symbols, and therefore

\operatorname{Sym}^{n}(S)
is holomorphically dominable by
{\mathbb{C}}^{2n}
. Since
\operatorname{Hilb}^{n}(S)

is the resolution of singularities of
\operatorname{Sym}^{n}(S)
, they are birational,
and hence
\operatorname{Hilb}^{n}(S)
is meromorphically dominable by
{\mathbb{C}}^{2n}
.

Proposition 4.6:
Let
S
be an elliptic K3 surface, then the Hilbert scheme
Hilb^{n}(S)
is
holomorphically dominable by
{\mathbb{C}}^{2n}
.

Proof:
Consider an elliptic fibration structure
f:S{\>\longrightarrow\>}B
. Then
B={\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
as
q(S)=0
and the canonical bundle formula applied to
f
shows that
f
has no multiple fibres and hence admits local (holomorphic) sections above any point by a classical argument, cf.
[
L1
]
. Now choose an
affine open chart
i:{\mathbb{C}}\subset{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
. Since
{\mathbb{C}}
is Stein,
there is a global holomorphic section
\sigma
over
{\mathbb{C}}
by patching up the local ones via the classical Cousin argument, see
[
BL1
]
.
Hence, there is a holomorphic dominable map from
{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
to
S
by the following construction: The relative tangent bundle of the fibration restricted to the section
\sigma({\mathbb{C}})
is a holomorphically trivial bundle
N
over
{\mathbb{C}}
as
{\mathbb{C}}
is Stein and contractible (to a lower dimensional skeleton). Hence
N
is biholomorphic to
{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
. The same argument applied to the restriction of
f
over
U:=B\setminus\text{disc}(f)
, viewing
f
there as an abelian torsor over
U
, shows that it is the trivial torsor and hence an abelian scheme over
U
. Hence, the resulting group structure on the smooth fibres of

extends to an open subset of any fiber above

by the Néron mapping property via the Néron model, and the relative exponential map from
N
is defined on all of

and gives the desired dominable map.

By functoriality of the construction of Hilbert schemes, we have a morphism

\operatorname{Hilb}^{n}(S){\>\longrightarrow\>}\operatorname{Hilb}^{n}({\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{1})={\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
.
It has an induced section
\widetilde{\sigma}
whose image in
\operatorname{Hilb}^{n}(S)
is
\operatorname{Hilb}^{n}(\sigma({\mathbb{C}}))\cong\operatorname{Hilb}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})=\operatorname{Sym}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
.
Again, the same reasoning and construction via the exponential map defined on the normal bundle to this section shows that

\operatorname{Hilb}^{n}(S)
is holomorphically dominable.

Proposition 4.7:
Let

is meromorphically dominable by

Proof:
Indeed, notice that

Since

\begin{array}[]{ccccccc}{\widetilde{Z}}&\longrightarrow&Z&=&F_{2}\times_{B_{1}}M&\longrightarrow&M\\
&&&&\downarrow&&\downarrow\\
&&&&F_{2}&\longrightarrow&B_{1}\\

Let

F_{2}
and let

Z^{\prime}={\mathbb{C}}^{n}\times_{F_{2}}{\widetilde{Z}}
be the fiber product of

\pi:{\mathbb{C}}^{n}{\>\longrightarrow\>}F_{2}
and
{\widetilde{f}}:{\widetilde{Z}}{\>\longrightarrow\>}F_{2}
.
Denote by
U^{\prime}\subset Z^{\prime}
the locus of all smooth points of

f^{\prime}:Z^{\prime}{\>\longrightarrow\>}{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
.

\begin{array}[]{ccccc}U^{\prime}&\hookrightarrow&Z^{\prime}&\longrightarrow&\widetilde{Z}\\
&&\downarrow&&\downarrow\\
&&{\mathbb{C}}^{n}&\longrightarrow&F_{2}\\
\end{array}

Since there is a
section
\sigma
of
{\widetilde{f}}:{\widetilde{Z}}{\>\longrightarrow\>}F_{2}
by construction, this
gives rise to a section
\widetilde{\sigma}
of
f^{\prime}:Z^{\prime}{\>\longrightarrow\>}{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
.
The existence of
{\sigma}
implies that an open subset of

\widetilde{Z}
proper outside the discriminant locus of
\widetilde{f}
can be identified outside a codimension two subset
S^{\prime}
on the base
F_{2}
with the fiberwise dual to the relative Picard variety
\operatorname{Pic}^{0}=({\widetilde{f}}_{*1}{\cal O}_{\widetilde{Z}})/({\widetilde{f}}_{*1}{\mathbb{Z}})
of
\widetilde{f}
.
Since

{\cal F}:=({\widetilde{f}}_{*1}{\cal O}_{\widetilde{Z}})^{\vee}
is a torsion free sheaf over
F_{2}
, it is locally free outside a codimension two subset
S\subset F_{2}
and it is globally generated after tensoring by an ample invertible sheaf.
Pulling back this by
\pi
shows that an open subset of

U^{\prime}
proper outside the discriminant locus can be identified with
[({f^{\prime}}_{*1}{\cal O}_{Z^{\prime}})/({f^{\prime}}_{*1}{\mathbb{Z}})]^{\vee}
and
\pi^{*}{\cal F}=({f^{\prime}}_{*1}{\cal O}_{Z^{\prime}})^{\vee}
is globally generated as any invertible sheaf is trivial over
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
(by appealing to the Oka-Grauert principle for example,
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
being Stein and homotopic to a point), i.e., there is an exact sequence of sheaves
\oplus_{i=1}^{m}{\cal O}_{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}\to\pi^{*}{\cal F}\to 0
.
Now, in the complement
V
of the complex codimension two complex analytic subset
\pi^{-1}(S\cup S^{\prime})
in
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
,
\pi^{*}{\cal F}
is given by a vector bundle that is dominated via this exact sequence by a trivial rank
m
vector bundle, which can be identified with the analytic subset
V\times{\mathbb{C}}^{m}\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{n+m}
with complex codimension two complement. By composition, this yields a dominating holomorphic map from
V\times{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
to
U^{\prime},Z^{\prime},Z
and to
M
. Since
M
is Moishezon, as it has two transveral Lagrangian fibrations (cf.
2
), it is projective and we may extend the above dominating map across codimension two to yield a meromorphic domination of
M
by
{\mathbb{C}}^{n+m}
and hence also by
{\mathbb{C}}^{2n}
on taking a general linear subspace
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
.

Remark 4.10:
By the results of Kamenova-Lehn
[
KL
, Lemma 2.12]
, it follows that
4
is valid for rational Lagrangian fibrations that are not induced by a birational change of the base on projective primitive symplectic varieties
M
, assuming
b_{2}(M)\geqslant 5
.
Very minor modifications of
4
would be necessary in the singular case, because we are interested in meromorphic dominability. The same apply to
4
.

Remark 4.11:
We note that, by the same token, unirational fibrations over a unirational
base are dominable by
{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
. This can be seen by restricting to an affine
chart after a finite base change to a projective space base. In contrast,
one can construct conic fibrations over
{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
that are not stably
rational, as in
[
HKT
]
.

Theorem 4.12:

Let
M
be a hyperkähler manifold or a primitive sympletive variety of dimension
2n
.
If
M
is projective and admits a Lagrangian fibration
f:M{\>\longrightarrow\>}{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{n}

with no multiple fibers in codimension one of the base, then
M
is
holomorphically dominable by
{\mathbb{C}}^{2n}
.

Proof:
Let
\Delta\subset{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
be the discriminant locus of
f
, and let

\Delta_{m}\subset{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
be a codimension two subset outside of which there are no multiple fibers.
Restrict the fibration
f
to an open affine chart
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}\subset{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
,
i.e.,
f:M_{0}{\>\longrightarrow\>}{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
. Then
f
defines an abelian variety over the generic point of
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
. By the Neron mapping property, there is a codimension two algebraic subset
S
of
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}\setminus\Delta_{m}
such that a Neron model
\pi:N\to{\mathbb{C}}^{n}\setminus\{S\cup\Delta_{m}\}
exists for
f
above
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}\setminus\{S\cup\Delta_{m}\}
.
By
3
there is an injective map
i:{\mathbb{C}}^{n}\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{C}}^{n}\setminus\Delta_{m}
avoiding
\Delta_{m}
, i.e., the pull-back abelian fibration

f_{0}:i^{*}M_{0}{\>\longrightarrow\>}{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
doesn’t have multiple fibers. Therefore, there are
local sections of
f_{0}
which we can glue together to a global section
\sigma
by the solution to the Cousin problem as
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
is Stein. Here by local, we naturally mean the local holomorphic sections in the Euclidian topology. Now, the relative tangent bundle of the fibration restricted to the section
\sigma({\mathbb{C}}^{n})
is a holomorphically trivial bundle
N
over
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
as
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
is Stein and contractible (to a lower dimensional skeleton). Hence,
N
is biholomorphic to
{\mathbb{C}}^{2n}
. The same argument applied to
f_{0}
, viewing it as an abelian torsor over
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
in the holomorphic category, shows that it is the trivial torsor, and hence an abelian scheme over
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
. Therefore, the resulting group structure on the smooth fibres of
f_{0}
extends to an open subset of any fiber above
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
by the Néron mapping property, and the relative exponential map from
N
is defined on all of
N
and gives the desired dominable map.

Example 4.13:
O’Grady’s exceptional examples
O_{6}
and
O_{10}
are deformation equivalent to Lagrangian fibrations
[
MO
,
MR
]
. They have no multiple fibers in codimension one on the base, and therefore are dominable by
{\mathbb{C}}^{6}
and
{\mathbb{C}}^{10}
, respectively.
Applying
2
, we see there are
densely many Lagrangian fibrations on the deformations of
O_{6}
and
O_{10}
.
The fibrations without multiple fibers in codimension one are dominable.

Remark 4.14:
In
[
KLV
]
the authors together with M. Verbitsky have proved that the
Kobayashi pseudometric and the infinitesimal Kobayashi pseudometric of the known examples of hyperkähler manifolds vanish. Here we have shown that some of these examples are dominable by
{\mathbb{C}}^{2n}
which is a much stronger property.

5
Dense entire curves

Using the main result of Campana-Winkelmann
[
CW
]
on the existence of dense entire curves on rationally connected varieties and the fact that log-Fano varieties are rationally connected by
3
, we have the following application to anti-hyperbolicity of varieties.

Theorem 5.1:

Given a projective manifold
M
with an abelian fibration
f:M{\>\longrightarrow\>}B
with no multiple fibers in codimension one over a log-Fano variety
B
, then there exists a dense entire curve.

Proof:
Let
p:{\mathbb{C}}\to B
be a holomorphic map with Zariski dense image (cf.
3
and
3
) and consider its pullback abelian fibration
\widetilde{f}:\widetilde{M}\to{\mathbb{C}}
. Now by
[
BL1
]
we can construct a holomorphic curve that passes through a point of
\widetilde{M}
an infinite number of times. This implies that its image in
M
is Zariski dense.

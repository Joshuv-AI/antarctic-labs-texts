The initial-to-final-state inverse problem with unbounded potentials and Strichartz estimates
Pedro Caro, Alberto Ruiz

Abstract
. The initial-to-final-state inverse problem consists in determining a quantum Hamiltonian assuming the knowledge of the state of the system at some fixed time, for every initial state. We formulated this problem to establish a theoretical framework that would explain the viability of data-driven prediction in quantum mechanics. In a previous work, we analysed this inverse problem for Hamiltonians of the form − Δ + V -\Delta+V with an electric potential V = V ⁡ ( t , x ) V=V(\mathrm{t},\mathrm{x}) , and we showed that uniqueness holds whenever the potentials are bounded and decay super-exponentially at infinity. In this paper, we extend this result for unbounded potentials. One of the key steps consists in proving a family of suitable Strichartz estimates—including the corresponding endpoint of Keel and Tao. In the context of the inverse Calderón problem this family of inequalities corresponds to the Carleman inequality proved by Kenig, Ruiz and Sogge. Haberman showed that this inequality can be also retrieved as an embedding of a suitable Bourgain space. The corresponding Bourgain space in our context do not capture the mixed-norm Lebesgue spaces of Strichartz inequalities. In this paper, we give a counterexample that justifies this fact, and shows the limitations of Bourgain spaces to address the initial-to-final-state inverse problem.



1.
Introduction

The Schrödinger equation determines the evolution of a wave function

u:(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\longmapsto u(t,x)\in\mathbb{C}

throughout an interval of time
[0,T]\subset\mathbb{R}
, with
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Wave functions provide a mathematical description of
the dynamical state of quantum particles.

If the motion takes place
under the influence of an electric potential
V=V(\mathrm{t},\mathrm{x})
and the initial
state
u(0,\centerdot)
is prescribed by
f
, then the corresponding
wave function is the solution of
the initial-value problem for the Schrödinger equation

(1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &i\partial_{\mathrm{t}}u=-\Delta u+Vu&&\textnormal{in}\kern 5.0pt(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{n},\\
&u(0,\centerdot)=f&&\textnormal{in}\kern 5.0pt\mathbb{R}^{n}.\end{aligned}\right.

Whenever
V
has suitable integrability properties one can show that there exists a unique

u\in C([0,T];L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
solving the problem (
1
), and the linear map

\mathcal{U}:f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\longmapsto u\in C([0,T];L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))

is bounded. Solutions of this type are usually referred to as
physical solutions
.

In this context, we formulated in
[
20
]
the
initial-to-final-state
inverse problem
aiming at deciding when is possible to determine
V=V(\mathrm{t},\mathrm{x})
from the

initial-to-final-state map
—given by the bounded linear map defined as

\mathcal{U}_{T}:f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\longmapsto u(T,\centerdot)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

with
u
denoting the solution of the problem (
1
).

The motivation of this inverse problem was to establish
a theoretical framework that would allow to understand rigorously
the viability of data-driven prediction in quantum mechanics.
Roughly speaking, data-driven prediction aims at computing an exact or approximative inverse of

\mathcal{U}\longmapsto\mathcal{U}_{T}

ignoring the exact interaction produced by the presence of
V
.

In our previous work
[
20
]
, we established that the potential

V=V(\mathrm{t},\mathrm{x})
is uniquely determined by
\mathcal{U}_{T}
. This result, which implies
the unique determination of
\mathcal{U}
itself,
holds for potentials
V\in L^{1}((0,T);L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
with
n\geq 2
that exhibit
super-exponential decay. Specifically, we required that

e^{\rho|\mathrm{x}|}V\in L^{\infty}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
for every
\rho>0
.
The necessity of this strong decay condition stems from the application of specialized
exponentially-growing solutions in our proof.

The main goal of this paper is to generalize this uniqueness result to the case of
unbounded potentials. In order to state precisely
our contributions we need to introduce some notation.
For
R>0
, let
\mathbf{1}_{>R}
denote the
characteristic function of the set
\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|x|>R\}
. For
s\in\mathbb{R}
and

\omega\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, let
H_{s,\omega}
denote the hyperplane

H_{\omega,s}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\omega\cdot x=s\}.

Theorem 1
.

Consider
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
in
L^{a}((0,T);L^{b}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))

with
n\geq 2
and
(a,b)\in[1,\infty]\times[1,\infty]

satisfying

2-\frac{2}{a}=\frac{n}{b}\kern 5.0pt\textnormal{with}\kern 5.0pt(n,a,b)\neq(2,\infty,1).

For the endpoint
(a,b)=(\infty,n/2)
with
n\geq 3
, we also suppose that

V_{1}
and

belongs to

Let

and
\mathcal{U}_{T}^{2}
denote

-\Delta+V_{1}

-\Delta+V_{2}

Assume that

satisfies the following conditions:

(a)

the endpoint

with

At some point we need to construct a suitable approximation of the potentials. At that stage, we

Nevertheless, this continuity in time is already required to guarantee the

As noted earlier, the super-exponential decay condition in (

Building on the work of

However, it appears unlikely that any form of exponential decay can be entirely removed

In

In that scenario, they showed uniqueness in dimension

This improvement was possible because they avoided the use of exponentially-growing solutions.
Instead, they relied on the construction of stationary states at different

Various inverse problems for the dynamical Schrödinger equation have been previously formulated

Many of these studies even considered time-dependent Hamiltonians

Despite this extensive body of work, none have addressed an inverse problem capable of tackling

significantly from our own, as the non-constant part of our Hamiltonians is spread throughout the

1.1.
General scheme to prove the
1

Writing
\Sigma=(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we derive from the identity

\mathcal{U}_{T}^{1}=\mathcal{U}_{T}^{2}
an orthogonality relation:

(2)

\int_{\Sigma}(V_{1}-V_{2})u_{1}\overline{v_{2}}\,=0

for all physical solutions
u_{1}
and
v_{2}

of the equations

(i\partial_{\mathrm{t}}+\Delta-V_{1})u_{1}=0\kern 5.0pt\textnormal{and}\kern 5.0pt(i\partial_{\mathrm{t}}+\Delta-\overline{V_{2}})v_{2}=0\kern 5.0pt\textnormal{in}\kern 5.0pt\Sigma.

With the identity (
2
) in mind, we construct solutions of the form

u_{1}=e^{i|\nu|^{2}\mathrm{t}-\nu\cdot\mathrm{x}}(u_{1}^{\sharp}+u_{1}^{\flat}),\qquad v_{2}=e^{i|\nu|^{2}\mathrm{t}+\nu\cdot\mathrm{x}}(v_{2}^{\sharp}+v_{2}^{\flat})

with
\nu\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
, for the equations

(i\partial_{\mathrm{t}}+\Delta-V_{1}^{\rm ext})u_{1}=0\kern 5.0pt\textnormal{and}\kern 5.0pt(i\partial_{\mathrm{t}}+\Delta-\overline{V_{2}^{\rm ext}})v_{2}=0\kern 5.0pt\textnormal{in}\kern 5.0pt\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n},

where
V_{j}^{\rm ext}
denotes either the extension by zero outside
\Sigma
if

(a,b)\neq(\infty,n/2)
, or a suitable continuous extension with support in

[T^{\prime},T^{\prime\prime}]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}

with
T^{\prime}<0<T<T^{\prime\prime}
if
(a,b)=(\infty,n/2)
with
n\geq 3
.

These type of exponentially-growing
solutions are called complex geometrical optics (CGO for short) and will satisfy

\lim_{|\nu|\to\infty}\Big(\Big|\int_{\Sigma}(V_{1}-V_{2})u_{1}^{\sharp}\overline{v_{2}^{\flat}}\,\Big|+\Big|\int_{\Sigma}(V_{1}-V_{2})u_{1}^{\flat}\overline{v_{2}^{\sharp}}\,\Big|+\Big|\int_{\Sigma}(V_{1}-V_{2})u_{1}^{\flat}\overline{v_{2}^{\flat}}\,\Big|\Big)=0.

The functions
u_{1}^{\sharp}
and
v_{2}^{\sharp}
will be essentially chosen as
e^{-i|\eta|^{2}\mathrm{t}+i\eta\cdot\mathrm{x}}
and
e^{-i|\kappa|^{2}\mathrm{t}+i\kappa\cdot\mathrm{x}}
with

\eta,\kappa\in H_{\hat{\nu}}
where
H_{\hat{\nu}}=H_{\hat{\nu},0}
and

\hat{\nu}=\nu/|\nu|
.

If plugging these CGO solutions
into the orthogonality relation (
2
) were allowed, we would conclude, by making
|\nu|
tend to
infinity, that

\int_{\Sigma}(V_{1}-V_{2})e^{-i(|\eta|^{2}-|\kappa|^{2})\mathrm{t}-i(\kappa-\eta)\cdot\mathrm{x}}\,=0\qquad\forall\eta,\kappa\in H_{\hat{\nu}}.

Then, making suitable choices of
\eta
and
\kappa
, we would prove that

V_{1}(t,x)=V_{2}(t,x)
for almost every
(t,x)\in\Sigma
.

This approach presents two non-trivial challenges: first to construct the corrections terms

u_{1}^{\flat}
and
v_{2}^{\flat}
of the CGO solutions for the Schrödinger equation,
and then showing that the orthogonality relation is satisfied
for CGOs—note that their restrictions to
\Sigma
grow exponentially
in certain regions of the space and, in consequence, they are not physical.
These challenges where already addressed in
[
20
]
for the case of bounded
potentials, however, the extension to the unbounded case is highly non trivial.

In order to construct the correction term
u^{\flat}
of a CGO in the form

u=e^{i|\nu|^{2}\mathrm{t}+\nu\cdot\mathrm{x}}(u^{\sharp}+u^{\flat})

satisfying the Schrödinger equation

(i\partial_{\mathrm{t}}+\Delta-V)u=0\kern 5.0pt\textnormal{in}\kern 5.0pt\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n},

one needs to solve

(i\partial_{\mathrm{t}}+\Delta+2\nu\cdot\nabla-V)u^{\flat}=Vu^{\sharp}\kern 5.0pt\textnormal{in}\kern 5.0pt\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}.

As in
[
20
]
, a key step consists of proving suitable estimates for the
Fourier multiplier
S_{\nu}
defined so that if
u=S_{\nu}f
with

f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
then

(i\partial_{\mathrm{t}}+\Delta+2\nu\cdot\nabla)u=f\kern 5.0pt\textnormal{in}\kern 5.0pt\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}.

In our previous work, we proved an inequality for
S_{\nu}
with a gain of
|\nu|^{1/2}
that
allowed to deal with the bounded case, see
[
20
, inequality (27)]
.

In order to extend our analysis for unbounded potentials, we need to estimate

S_{\nu}
in mixed-norm Lebesgue spaces. This requires to exploit properties of the symbol
of
S_{\nu}
that were completely ignored in
[
20
]
. The types of estimates
we need here are exact analogues of the classical Strichartz inequalities for the free
Schrödinger propagator—including the endpoint of Keel and Tao
[
34
]
.

In the same way as classical Strichartz estimates, our
family of inequalities is scale invariant and therefore it is independent of
|\nu|
.
This forces us to use not only the analogues of Strichartz estimates
but also another inequality with certain gain in
|\nu|
.

While the estimate in
[
20
, inequality (27)]
provides a gain and is an
obvious candidate, we prove a stronger result here. Our improved version achieves the full power
|\nu|
, which seems the natural gain for this problem.

In order to be able to gather the gain in
|\nu|
with the versatility of our Strichartz
inequalities for
S_{\nu}
, we use a trick due to Lavine and Nachman.

It might be convenient for the
reader to mention here that the construction of the reminder term
u^{\flat}
only requires a
tempered decay for the potential, actually one only needs the decay stated in
(
b
) of the
1
.

The second challenge we address is extending the orthogonality relation in
(
2
) to exponentially-growing solutions, as CGOs. While this
constitutes a technical step, its core ideas are adaptations from our previous work.

It is important to emphasize that the super-exponential decay condition
(
a
) of the
1

is specifically required for this extension to admit CGO solutions.

1.2.
Bourgain spaces for the initial-to-final-state inverse problem

The family of Strichartz inequalities required to deal with unbounded potentials in
our context corresponds, in the framework of the Calderón problem, to the Carleman inequality
proved by Kenig, Ruiz and Sogge
[
35
]
—only for the scale-invariant situation.

Haberman showed using Stein–Tomas that
this inequality can be also retrieved as an embedding of a suitable Bourgain space

[
29
]
. This Bourgain
space also recovers the classical Sylvester–Uhlmann inequality
[
45
]
,
which has the same role as our inequality with a gain of
|\nu|
.

Therefore, the corresponding Bourgain space already gathers
the gain of the Sylvester–Uhlmann inequality with the versatility of Kenig–Ruiz–Sogge.
This provides an alternative path to avoid the Lavine–Nachman trick
when working on the Calderón problem.

Unfortunately, this alternative approach
is not available for the initial-to-final-state inverse problem.
In fact, the corresponding Bourgain space in our context do not capture the mixed-norm Lebesgue
spaces of Strichartz inequalities.

In this paper, we construct a counterexample that shows that
the corresponding Bourgain spaces is not embedded in the mixed-norm Lebesgue
spaces appearing in the Strichartz inequalities. This highlights some obvious limitations
of Bourgain spaces in the context of the initial-to-final-state inverse problem.

2.2.
The Lavine–Nachman trick

Assume for a moment that we have chosen

so that

in
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
with

, we look for

solving

(i\partial_{\mathrm{t}}+\Delta+2\nu\cdot\nabla-M_{V})u^{\flat}=M_{V}u^{\sharp}\kern 5.0pt\textnormal{in}\kern 5.0pt\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}.

In order to solve this equation, we will use a trick due to Lavine and Nachman
that we learnt in
[
25
]
.

Start by observing that

(i\partial_{\mathrm{t}}+\Delta+2\nu\cdot\nabla-M_{V})\circ S_{\nu}\circ M_{|V|^{1/2}}=M_{|V|^{1/2}}-M_{V}\circ S_{\nu}\circ M_{|V|^{1/2}}.

Then, defining

W:(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\longmapsto\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{V(t,x)}{|V(t,x)|^{1/2}}&V(t,x)\neq 0\\
&\qquad 0&V(t,x)=0\end{aligned}\right.

and using that
|V|^{1/2}=|W|
and
V=|W|W
, we can write

(i\partial_{\mathrm{t}}+\Delta+2\nu\cdot\nabla-M_{V})\circ S_{\nu}\circ M_{|V|^{1/2}}=M_{|W|}\circ(\mathrm{Id}-M_{W}\circ S_{\nu}\circ M_{|W|}).

Then,
u^{\flat}
in the form

with
v
satisfying

(13)

(\mathrm{Id}-M_{W}\circ S_{\nu}\circ M_{|W|})v=M_{W}u^{\sharp}

solves the equation (
11
). Indeed,

(i\partial_{\mathrm{t}}+\Delta+2\nu\cdot\nabla-M_{V})S_{\nu}(|W|v)=M_{|W|}\circ(\mathrm{Id}-M_{W}\circ S_{\nu}\circ M_{|W|})v=M_{|W|}\circ M_{W}u^{\sharp}.

Since
M_{|W|}\circ M_{W}=M_{V}
, we have that
u^{\flat}=S_{\nu}(|W|v)
solves
(
11
).

Therefore, our goal is to find
v
solving (
13
). To that end, we
will show that the Birman–Schwinger operator

is a contraction in

whenever
|\nu|
is
sufficiently large. In the
proposition

4.1
, we provide conditions on

that guarantee that

Thus, for
|\nu|
sufficiently large, the operator
\mathrm{Id}-M_{W}\circ S_{\nu}\circ M_{|W|}

has a bounded inverse in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and

can be chosen as

In the rest of the section we state the conditions on

that make it possible,
first, to invert
\mathrm{Id}-M_{W}\circ S_{\nu}\circ M_{|W|}
and, second, to construct

.

Lemma 2.2
.

Consider
V\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};L^{n/2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
such that

V\in C(\mathbb{R};L^{n/2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
. Then,
W
defined as in (
12
) belongs to

C(\mathbb{R};L^{n}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
.

Proof.

Consider
s\in\mathbb{R}
such that
W(s,\centerdot)\neq 0
. Then,

V(t,\centerdot)\neq 0
for all
t
sufficiently close to
s
.
By (
12
), we have that
W(t,\centerdot)\neq 0

for all
t
sufficiently close to
s
.
Then, for almost every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
we have that

\displaystyle W(t,

\displaystyle x)-W(s,x)=\frac{V(t,x)}{|V(t,x)|}|V(t,x)|^{1/2}-\frac{V(s,x)}{|V(s,x)|}|V(s,x)|^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\Big(\frac{V(t,x)}{|V(t,x)|}-\frac{V(s,x)}{|V(s,x)|}\Big)|V(t,x)|^{1/2}+\frac{V(s,x)}{|V(s,x)|}\big(|V(t,x)|^{1/2}-|V(s,x)|^{1/2}\big).

Hence,

\displaystyle\|W(t,\centerdot)-W(s,\centerdot)\|_{L^{n}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq\Big\|\Big|\frac{V(t,\centerdot)}{|V(t,\centerdot)|}-\frac{V(s,\centerdot)}{|V(s,\centerdot)|}\Big|^{2}|V(t,\centerdot)|\Big\|_{L^{n/2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\qquad+\big\|\big||V(t,\centerdot)|^{1/2}-|V(s,\centerdot)|^{1/2}\big|^{2}\big\|_{L^{n/2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{1/2}.

On the one hand, observe that for almost every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
we have

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{V(t,x)}{|V(t,x)|}-\frac{V(s,x)}{|V(s,x)|}\Big|^{2}|V(t,x)|\leq 2\Big|\frac{V(t,x)}{|V(t,x)|}-\frac{V(s,x)}{|V(s,x)|}\Big||V(t,x)|

\displaystyle\leq 2\big(|V(t,x)-V(s,x)|+\big||V(s,x)|-|V(t,x)|\big|\big)\leq 4|V(t,x)-V(s,x)|

for almost every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Hence,

\Big\|\Big|\frac{V(t,\centerdot)}{|V(t,\centerdot)|}-\frac{V(s,\centerdot)}{|V(s,\centerdot)|}\Big|^{2}|V(t,\centerdot)|\Big\|_{L^{n/2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{1/2}\leq 2\|V(t,\centerdot)-V(s,\centerdot)\|_{L^{n/2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{1/2}

On the other hand,
since

for all positive constants

and
b

\displaystyle\big||V(t,x)|^{1/2}-|V(s,x)|^{1/2}\big|^{2}\leq\big||V(s,x)|-|V(t,x)|\big|\leq|V(t,x)-V(s,x)|.

Hence

\big\|\big||V(t,\centerdot)|^{1/2}-|V(s,\centerdot)|^{1/2}\big|^{2}\big\|_{L^{n/2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{1/2}\leq\|V(t,\centerdot)-V(s,\centerdot)\|_{L^{n/2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{1/2}.

The inequality

in the case

.

Gathering these inequalities we have that

which means that

is continuous at
s
,
by the continuity of
t\in\mathbb{R}\mapsto V(t,\centerdot)\in L^{n/2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

In order to prove the lemma, it remains to see what happens in the case that

\displaystyle\|W(t,\centerdot)-W(s,\centerdot)\|_{L^{n}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle=\|W(t,\centerdot)\|_{L^{n}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\|V(t,\centerdot)\|_{L^{n/2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\|V(t,\centerdot)-V(s,\centerdot)\|_{L^{n/2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{1/2}

since
V(s,\centerdot)=0
. Hence, again by the continuity of

t\in\mathbb{R}\mapsto V(t,\centerdot)\in L^{n/2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we can conclude that

t\in\mathbb{R}\mapsto W(t,\centerdot)\in L^{n}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is
continuous at
s
even when
W(s,\centerdot)=0
.

\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
as a locally integrable function.

For

f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we define

(27)

Sf(t,x)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{\frac{n+1}{2}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{i(t\tau+x\cdot\xi)}\frac{1}{p(\tau,\xi)}\widehat{f}(\tau,\xi)\,\mathrm{d}(\tau,\xi)\quad\forall(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}.

In order to relate the operators

and

, we choose

and make the change

and
\eta=2|\nu|Q\xi
that implies

p_{\nu}(\sigma,\eta)=4|\nu|^{2}p(\tau,\xi).

One can check that if

and we define

then

\widehat{g}(\tau,\xi)=(2|\nu|)^{n+2}\widehat{f}(-4|\nu|^{2}\tau,2|\nu|Q\xi)\qquad\forall(\tau,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n},

and consequently

(30)

S_{\nu}f(s,y)=\frac{1}{4|\nu|^{2}}Sg(-4|\nu|^{2}s,2|\nu|Q^{\mathsf{T}}y)\quad\forall(s,y)\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Proof of the
theorem

3.1

After the relations (

) and (

), it is a
simple task to check that

\displaystyle\sup_{s\in\mathbb{R}}\|S_{\nu}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times H_{\hat{\nu},s})}=\frac{1}{4|\nu|^{2}}\frac{1}{(2|\nu|)^{(n+1)/2}}\sup_{x_{n}\in\mathbb{R}}\|Sg(\centerdot,x_{n})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n-1})},

Then, the
theorem

3.1
is an immediate consequence of the following inequality
for the multiplier
S
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Consider
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
There exists an absolute constant
C>0
such that

\sup_{x_{n}\in\mathbb{R}}\|Sf(\centerdot,x_{n})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n-1})}\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}}\|f(\centerdot,y_{n})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n-1})}\,\mathrm{d}y_{n}

for all
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Proof.

Start by noticing that for
(t,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}
we have that the Fourier transform
of
x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\mapsto Sf(t,x^{\prime},x_{n})
can be written as

\mathcal{F}[Sf(t,\centerdot,x_{n})](\xi^{\prime})=\frac{e^{it|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}}}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}}e^{i(t\sigma+x_{n}\xi_{n})}\frac{1}{\sigma-\xi_{n}^{2}+i\xi_{n}}\widehat{f}(\sigma+|\xi^{\prime}|^{2},\xi^{\prime},\xi_{n})\,\mathrm{d}(\sigma,\xi_{n})

for all
\xi^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
. For that, we have performed the translation

\sigma=\tau-|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}
.

Applying Plancharel’s identity in
\mathbb{R}
,
we have for
(\xi^{\prime},x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}
that

\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\mathcal{F}[Sf(t,\centerdot,x_{n})](\xi^{\prime})|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|u(\sigma,\xi^{\prime},x_{n})|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma

where

u(\sigma,\xi^{\prime},x_{n})=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{1/2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{ix_{n}\xi_{n}}\frac{1}{\sigma-\xi_{n}^{2}+i\xi_{n}}\widehat{f}(\sigma+|\xi^{\prime}|^{2},\xi^{\prime},\xi_{n})\,\mathrm{d}\xi_{n}.

Plancharel’s identity now in
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
implies that

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\|Sf(t,\centerdot,x_{n})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\|\mathcal{F}[Sf(t,\centerdot,x_{n})]\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t=\|u(\centerdot,x_{n})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n-1})}^{2}

for every
x_{n}\in\mathbb{R}
.

Before going on with the argument, we introduce a family of convolution operators.
For
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
, the function

\eta\in\mathbb{R}\longmapsto\frac{1}{\sigma-\eta^{2}+i\eta}\in\mathbb{C}

is integrable—since the polynomial function in the denominator does not vanish in
\mathbb{R}
,
so we can define

(31)

K_{\sigma}(y)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{iy\eta}\frac{1}{\sigma-\eta^{2}+i\eta}\,\mathrm{d}\eta\quad\forall y\in\mathbb{R}.

Then, we introduce the family

T_{\sigma}g=K_{\sigma}\ast g\quad\forall g\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}).

One can check that

u(\sigma,\xi^{\prime},x_{n})=T_{\sigma}\mathcal{F}^{-1}[\widehat{f}(\sigma+|\xi^{\prime}|^{2},\xi^{\prime},\centerdot)](x_{n}),

and consequently, for
(\sigma,\xi^{\prime},x_{n})\in(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\})\times\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}
, we obtain

|u(\sigma,\xi^{\prime},x_{n})|\leq\|K_{\sigma}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}[\widehat{f}(\sigma+|\xi^{\prime}|^{2},\xi^{\prime},\centerdot)]\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}.

Using the
lemma

3.4
and performing the translation

\tau=\sigma+|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}
, we can conclude that

\|u(\centerdot,x_{n})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n-1})}^{2}\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}[\widehat{f}(\tau,\xi^{\prime},\centerdot)]\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}(\tau,\xi^{\prime}).

The implicit constant of this inequality is absolute.

Since

\mathcal{F}^{-1}[\widehat{f}(\tau,\xi^{\prime},\centerdot)](y_{n})=\mathcal{F}[f(\centerdot,y_{n})](\tau,\xi^{\prime})\quad\forall(\tau,\xi^{\prime},y_{n})\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R},

Young’s inequalities for integrals, and then Plancherel’s identity in

\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
yield

\displaystyle\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}[\widehat{f}(\tau,\xi^{\prime},\centerdot)]\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}(\tau,\xi^{\prime})\Big)^{1/2}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}\|\mathcal{F}[f(\centerdot,y_{n})]\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n-1})}\,\mathrm{d}y_{n}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\|f(\centerdot,y_{n})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n-1})}\,\mathrm{d}y_{n}.

Summing up, we have shown that

\|Sf(\centerdot,x_{n})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n-1})}=\|u(\centerdot,x_{n})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n-1})}\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}}\|f(\centerdot,y_{n})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n-1})}\,\mathrm{d}y_{n}.

Taking supremum in
x_{n}\in\mathbb{R}
, we obtain the inequality of the statement.
∎

Lemma 3.4

For almost every

\displaystyle K_{\sigma}(x)=

\displaystyle-\mathbf{1}_{(1/4,\infty)}(\sigma)\mathbf{1}_{(-\infty,0)}(x)\frac{2e^{x/2}}{|4\sigma-1|^{1/2}}\sin(|4\sigma-1|^{1/2}x/2)

\displaystyle+\mathbf{1}_{(-\infty,0)}(\sigma)\mathbf{1}_{(0,\infty)}(x)\frac{1}{|4\sigma-1|^{1/2}}e^{-(|4\sigma-1|^{1/2}-1)x/2}

Consequently,

\esssup_{\sigma\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}}\|K_{\sigma}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}<\infty.

Proof.

Start by defining the complex polynomial function

p_{\sigma}(\zeta)=-\zeta^{2}+i\zeta+\sigma\quad\forall\zeta\in\mathbb{C},

and observe that

(32)

K_{\sigma}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f_{(\sigma,x)}(\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\xi,

where

\eta_{\sigma}
and
\kappa_{\sigma}
denoting the roots of

.

One can check that, whenever

For

The idea to prove the first part of this lemma is to compute the integral on the right-hand

Lemma 3.6
.

For every
y\in\mathbb{R}
and
s\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
,
we have that

\Big|\int_{-\infty}^{y}e^{i\frac{\xi^{2}}{s}}e^{\xi}\,\mathrm{d}\xi\Big|\lesssim e^{y}|s|^{1/2}.

Proof.

Take
\chi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
such that

\supp\chi\subset\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}:|\xi|\leq 2\}
and

\chi(\xi)=1
whenever
|\xi|\leq 1
. For
r\in(0,\infty)
, we define

\displaystyle\chi_{\leq r}(\xi)=\chi(\xi/r)\quad\forall\xi\in\mathbb{R},

\displaystyle\chi_{>r}(\xi)=1-\chi_{\leq r}(\xi)\quad\forall\xi\in\mathbb{R}.

On the one hand,

(43)

\Big|\int_{-\infty}^{y}e^{i\frac{\xi^{2}}{s}}e^{\xi}\chi_{\leq r}(\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\xi\Big|\leq re^{y}\|\chi\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}.

On the other hand

\int_{-\infty}^{y}e^{i\frac{\xi^{2}}{s}}e^{\xi}\chi_{>r}(\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\xi=-i\frac{s}{2}\int_{-\infty}^{y}\partial[e^{i\frac{\,\centerdot^{2}}{s}}](\xi)\frac{e^{\xi}}{\xi}\chi_{>r}(\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\xi

since

\partial[e^{i\frac{\,\centerdot^{2}}{s}}](\xi)=i\frac{2}{s}\xi e^{i\frac{\xi^{2}}{s}}\quad\forall\xi\in\mathbb{R}.

Integrating by parts, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{y}\partial[e^{i\frac{\,\centerdot^{2}}{s}}](\xi)\frac{e^{\xi}}{\xi}\chi_{>r}(\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle=e^{i\frac{\,y^{2}}{s}}e^{y}\frac{\chi_{>r}(y)}{y}

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{-\infty}^{y}e^{i\frac{\,\xi^{2}}{s}}e^{\xi}\Big[\frac{\chi_{>r}(\xi)}{\xi}-\frac{1}{r}\frac{\chi^{\prime}_{\leq r}(\xi)}{\xi}-\frac{\chi_{>r}(\xi)}{\xi^{2}}\Big]\,\mathrm{d}\xi.

Here
\chi^{\prime}_{\leq r}(\xi)=\chi^{\prime}(\xi/r)
for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
, with

\chi^{\prime}
denoting the first-order derivative of
\chi
.

The integral in the
right-hand side of the previous identity can be bounded as follows:

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{-\infty}^{y}e^{i\frac{\,\xi^{2}}{s}}e^{\xi}\Big[\frac{\chi_{>r}(\xi)}{\xi}-\frac{1}{r}\frac{\chi^{\prime}_{\leq r}(\xi)}{\xi}-\frac{\chi_{>r}(\xi)}{\xi^{2}}\Big]\,\mathrm{d}\xi\Big|

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\sup_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}}\frac{|\chi_{>r}(\xi)|}{|\xi|}\int_{-\infty}^{y}e^{\xi}\,\mathrm{d}\xi+e^{y}\Big[\frac{1}{r}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|\chi^{\prime}_{\leq r}(\xi)|}{|\xi|}\,\mathrm{d}\xi+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|\chi_{>r}(\xi)|}{\xi^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\xi\Big]

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\frac{e^{y}}{r}\Big[\sup_{\eta\in\mathbb{R}}\frac{|1-\chi(\eta)|}{|\eta|}+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|\chi^{\prime}(\eta)|}{|\eta|}\,\mathrm{d}\eta+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|1-\chi(\eta)|}{\eta^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\eta\Big].

Therefore, we have that

\Big|\int_{-\infty}^{y}\partial[e^{i\frac{\,\centerdot^{2}}{s}}](\xi)\frac{e^{\xi}}{\xi}\chi_{>r}(\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\xi\Big|\leq\frac{e^{y}}{r}\Big[2\sup_{\eta\in\mathbb{R}}\frac{|1-\chi(\eta)|}{|\eta|}+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big(\frac{|\chi^{\prime}(\eta)|}{|\eta|}+\frac{|1-\chi(\eta)|}{\eta^{2}}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}\eta\Big].

Consequently,

(44)

\Big|\int_{-\infty}^{y}e^{i\frac{\xi^{2}}{s}}e^{\xi}\chi_{>r}(\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\xi\Big|\lesssim\frac{|s|}{r}e^{y}.

From the inequalities (
43
) and (
44
), we have that

\Big|\int_{-\infty}^{y}e^{i\frac{\xi^{2}}{s}}e^{\xi}\,\mathrm{d}\xi\Big|\lesssim e^{y}\Big(r+\frac{|s|}{r}\Big).

Choosing
r\in(0,\infty)
so that
r=|s|/r
, we obtain the bound stated.
∎

In

,

,

, and

Note that, using (

) for the atoms
\alpha_{k}f_{k}
and

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}

\displaystyle S_{j}[\alpha_{k}f_{k}](t,x)\overline{\beta_{l}(t)g_{l}(t,x)}\,\mathrm{d}(t,x)\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-j\frac{n}{2}\big(\frac{1}{a}-\frac{1}{r_{n}}+\frac{1}{b}-\frac{1}{r_{n}}\big)}\|\alpha_{k}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}2^{k\big(\frac{1}{a}-\frac{1}{r_{n}}\big)}\|\beta_{l}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}2^{l\big(\frac{1}{b}-\frac{1}{r_{n}}\big)}

\displaystyle=2^{(k-j\frac{n}{2})\big(\frac{1}{a}-\frac{1}{r_{n}}\big)}2^{(l-j\frac{n}{2})\big(\frac{1}{b}-\frac{1}{r_{n}}\big)}\|\alpha_{k}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\|\beta_{l}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}.

(1/a,1/b)

in a neighbourhood of

,

and

Then,

Since

we have that

\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}S_{j}f(t,x)\overline{g(t,x)}\,\mathrm{d}(t,x)\Big|\lesssim\sum_{j}2^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}|j|}\Big(\sum_{k}\|\alpha_{k}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\sum_{l}\|\beta_{l}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Finally,

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}

\displaystyle S_{j}f(t,x)\overline{g(t,x)}\,\mathrm{d}(t,x)\Big|\lesssim\big\|\big(\sum_{k}|\alpha_{k}|^{2}\big)^{1/2}\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\big\|\big(\sum_{l}|\beta_{j}|^{2}\big)^{1/2}\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\big\|\big(\sum_{k}|\alpha_{k}|^{r_{n}}\big)^{1/r_{n}}\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\big\|\big(\sum_{l}|\beta_{j}|^{r_{n}}\big)^{1/r_{n}}\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}

which prove the inequality (

).

7.
Differences with the Calderón problem

In the context of the Calderón problem, there is an alternative approach that avoids the use of
the Lavine–Nachman trick. This approach involves Bourgain spaces. In this section we explain why
this approach fails for the initial-to-final state inverse problem.

7.1.
The Bourgain spaces in the Calderón problem

Calderón posed in
[
16
]
the following inverse
boundary value problem: Let
D
be a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
(
n\geq 2
)
with Lipschitz boundary
\partial D
, and let
\gamma
be a real bounded
measurable function in
D
with a positive lower bound. Consider
the Dirichlet-to-Neumann map
\Lambda_{\gamma}:H^{1/2}(\partial D)\to H^{-1/2}(\partial D)

defined by

\Lambda_{\gamma}f=\gamma\partial_{\nu}u|_{\partial D}

where
\partial_{\nu}=\nu\cdot\nabla
, with
\nabla
denoting the gradient
and
\nu
denoting the outward
unit normal vector to
\partial D
,
and
u\in H^{1}(D)
is the solution of the boundary value problem

\left\{\begin{aligned} \nabla\cdot(\gamma\nabla u)&=0\,\textnormal{in}\,D,\\
u|_{\partial D}&=f.\end{aligned}\right.

The inverse Calderón problem
is to decide whether the conductivity
\gamma
is uniquely determined by

\Lambda_{\gamma}
, and to calculate
\gamma
in terms of
\Lambda_{\gamma}

whenever the unique determination is possible.

Sylvester and Uhlmann proved uniqueness for smooth conductivities in
[
45
]

for dimension
n\geq 3
. However, the key points of their method
only requires the conductivity to have bounded second-order partial
derivatives
[
38
]
. After this piece of work, and some others as

[
1
]
by Alssandrini and
[
39
]
by Nachman, a standard
strategy to address the Calderón problem has been established: One starts by showing

\Lambda_{\gamma_{1}}=\Lambda_{\gamma_{2}}\Rightarrow\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(q_{1}-q_{2})v_{1}v_{2}\,=0,

where
q_{j}=\gamma_{j}^{-1/2}\Delta\gamma_{j}^{1/2}
and
v_{j}
is any
solution of
-\Delta v_{j}+q_{j}v_{j}=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then, one constructs CGO solutions of the
form:

v_{j}=e^{\zeta_{j}\cdot{\rm x}}(1+w_{j})

with
\zeta_{j}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
and
\zeta_{j}\cdot\zeta_{j}=0
for
j\in\{1,2\}
, and so that

w_{j}
tends to vanish in some sense as
|\zeta_{j}|
goes to
\infty
. Finally, one makes
appropriate choices of
\zeta_{1}
and
\zeta_{2}
so that one concludes the uniqueness.
The work of Sylvester and Uhlmann has been followed many attempts to lower the regularity
assumed, see for example
[
13
,
14
,
30
,
29
,
19
,
31
,
44
]
.

In
[
30
]
, Haberman and Tataru
introduced the Bourgain spaces
\dot{Y}_{\zeta}^{s}
with

s\in\{1/2,-1/2\}
. These
spaces have the norms

\|f\|_{\dot{Y}_{\zeta}^{s}}=\||q_{\zeta}|^{s}\widehat{f}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

where
q_{\zeta}
stands for the symbol of the differential operator

\Delta+2\zeta\cdot\nabla
, that is,

q_{\zeta}(\xi)=-|\xi|^{2}+2i\zeta\cdot\xi\quad\forall\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

These spaces where introduced with the idea of making possible an average with respect to the
free parameters
|\Re\zeta|
and
\Re\zeta/|\Re\zeta|
,
so that the correction terms of the CGO solutions
tend to vanish when the conductivity is assumed to be continuously differentiable. However,
the space
\dot{Y}_{\zeta}^{1/2}
has other properties such as the embeddings

(79)

|\zeta|^{1/2}\|f\|_{B^{\ast}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|f\|_{L^{p_{n}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\|f\|_{\dot{Y}^{1/2}_{\zeta}}

for every
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
—see
[
30
,
29
]
.
Here
p_{n}
is defined by
the relation
1/p_{n}=1/2-1/n
for
n\geq 3
, and

\|f\|_{B^{\ast}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\big(2^{-j/2}\|f\|_{L^{2}(D_{j})}\big)

with
D_{j}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:2^{j-1}<|x|\leq 2^{j}\}
for
j\in\mathbb{N}
and

D_{0}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|x|\leq 1\}
.

The fact that the Bourgain space
\dot{Y}_{\zeta}^{1/2}
enjoys the embeddings in
(
79
) makes it possible to prove uniqueness for the Calderón problem
for conductivities satisfying
\nabla\gamma\in L^{n}(D)^{n}
and

\Delta\gamma\in L^{n/2}(D)
, without using the Lavine–Nachman trick.
For this reason, one could think that the use of Bourgain spaces
in the context of the initial-to-final
state inverse problem would avoid the use of the Lavine–Nachman trick.
Unfortunately, this is not the case. In fact, we will see in this
section the limitations of Bourgain spaces to deal with our problem.

The Calderón problem has been extensively studied:
uniqueness, reconstruction and stability. A non-comprehensive list of
references is the following. Uniqueness in dimension

n=2
:
[
40
,
15
,
3
]
.
For reconstruction:

[
39
,
27
,
18
]
.
For stability:
[

,
46
,
2
,
4
,
5
,

7.2.
The Bourgain spaces for the initial-to-final state inverse problem

In the context of the initial-to-final state inverse problem one can introduce the Bourgain

with

One can prove that

this inequality can be though as half of the inequality for
S_{\nu}
that we used in our
previous work
[

—for that it is enough to chain
(
80
) with its dual. However, despite what happens in the context of the Calderón problem,
the Bourgain space for
s=1/2
is not embedded in the mixed-norm Lebesgue spaces appearing
in the
theorem

. In fact, we will show that the inequality

(81)

\|u\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R};L^{r^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}\lesssim\|u\|_{\dot{X}^{1/2}_{\nu}}\quad\forall u\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})

does not hold for
(q^{\prime},r^{\prime})\in[2,\infty]\times[2,\infty]
satisfying
(
8
).
Using (

), one can see that (

) is equivalent
to

\|u\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R};L^{r^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}\lesssim\||p|^{1/2}\widehat{u}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})}\quad\forall u\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}),

with

as in (
26
).
Our goal here is to give a counterexample for the inequality
(
82
). Before that, we make some general comments.

In
[
47
]
, Tao states that the inequality

(83)

\|u\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R};L^{r^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}\lesssim\|\langle\Re p\rangle^{b}\widehat{u}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})}\qquad\forall u\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})

holds for

, where

with
\Re p(\tau,\xi)=\tau-|\xi|^{2}
for
all
(\tau,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
. This embedding of the non-homogenous Bourgain space
follows from
[

, which states that

(84)

\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\|u(t,\centerdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}\lesssim\|\langle\Re p\rangle^{b}\widehat{u}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})}\qquad\forall u\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})

whenever

.
In
[
47
, Exercise 2.71]
, the reader is asked to
prove that the inequality (
84
) fails in the endpoint

.
Here we go beyond that and show a counterexample for the embedding (

)
for
b=1/2
. This counterexample will be adapted to eventually prove that
(
82
) does not hold.

7.2.1.
Failure of (

) at the endpoint

In order to point out why (
83
) fails at the endpoint
b=1/2
,
we test the norms involved in the inequality with functions

of the form

(85)

\widehat{u}(\tau,\xi)=\widehat{g}(\tau-|\xi|^{2})\widehat{f}(\xi)\qquad\forall(\tau,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}

with

and

. One can check that

(86)

\|\langle\Re p\rangle^{1/2}\widehat{u}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\|g\|_{H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R})}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

and

u(t,x)=\frac{g(t)}{(2\pi)^{n/2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{ix\cdot\xi}e^{it|\xi|^{2}}\widehat{f}(\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\xi.

Noting that
2/{q^{\prime}}=n/2-n/{r^{\prime}}
defines admissible Strichartz pairs for the
Schrödinger equation we have that

\|u\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R};L^{r^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}\lesssim\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Then, by (
86

\|u\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R};L^{r^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}\lesssim\frac{\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}}{\|g\|_{H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R})}}\|\langle\Re p\rangle^{1/2}\widehat{u}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Thus, in order to derive (
83

the family of functions defined as (

) we should ensure that the
quotient
\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}/\|g\|_{H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R})}
remains bounded for all

. Note the relation of this quotient with the endpoint
Sobolev embedding, which is known to fail. Thus, one should expect that choosing
an unbounded
g\in H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R})
, we could construct a counterexample for
(
83
) with
b=1/2
. This will be our strategy.

Consider
\chi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
such that

\supp\chi\subset\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:|z|<1/e\}

with

whenever

. The function

.
If

to

\mathbb{R}\times\{0\}

and

for almost every

with

Let

It is clear that

\|f_{\rho}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\|\mathbf{1}_{<1}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

which is independent of

We consider the family

with

As in (

\|\langle\Re p\rangle^{1/2}\widehat{u}_{\rho}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\|g\|_{H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R})}\|\mathbf{1}_{<1}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Furthermore,

Note that if

Thus, for

\rho>0

\widehat{f_{\rho}}(\xi)=\rho^{-n/2}\mathbf{1}_{<\rho}(\xi-2\rho e_{n})\qquad\forall\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

It is clear again that

\|f_{\rho}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\|\mathbf{1}_{<1}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

which is independent of
\rho
.

We consider the family
\{u_{\rho}:\rho\geq e/9\}
satisfying

\widehat{u_{\rho}}(\tau,\xi)=\widehat{g_{\rho}}(\tau-|\xi|^{2})\widehat{f_{\rho}}(\xi)\qquad\forall(\tau,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}.

One can check that

u_{\rho}(t,x)=\frac{g_{\rho}(t)}{(2\pi)^{n/2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{ix\cdot\xi}e^{it|\xi|^{2}}\widehat{f_{\rho}}(\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\xi\qquad\dot{\forall}(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Additionally, since

\supp\widehat{f_{\rho}}\subset\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\rho<|\xi_{n}|<3\rho\}

we have

\|u_{\rho}\|_{\dot{X}^{1/2}}\eqsim\||\Re p+i\rho|^{1/2}\widehat{u_{\rho}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\||\centerdot+i\rho|^{1/2}\widehat{g_{\rho}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\|\widehat{f_{\rho}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Since
g_{\rho}
is defined as an
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
scaling of
g
, we can see that

\||\centerdot+i\rho|^{1/2}\widehat{g_{\rho}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}=\|g\|_{H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R})},

and consequently, we have that

(88)

\|u_{\rho}\|_{\dot{X}^{1/2}}\eqsim\|g\|_{H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R})}\|\mathbf{1}_{<1}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Once again,

|u_{\rho}(t,x)|\geq\rho^{-n/2}\frac{g_{\rho}(t)}{(2\pi)^{n/2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\cos(x\cdot\xi+t|\xi|^{2})\mathbf{1}_{<\rho}(\xi-2\rho e_{n})\,\mathrm{d}\xi.

Note that if
|x\cdot\xi+t|\xi|^{2}|\leq 1<\pi/3
, then
\cos(x\cdot\xi+t|\xi|^{2})>1/2
.
Thus, since
\supp\widehat{f_{\rho}}\subset\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|\xi|<3\rho\}

we have for
|x|\leq 1/(6\rho)
and
|t|\leq 1/(18\rho^{2})
that

\cos(x\cdot\xi+t|\xi|^{2})\mathbf{1}_{<\rho}(\xi-2\rho e_{n})>1/2,

and consequently,

|u_{\rho}(t,x)|\gtrsim\rho^{n/2}g(\rho t)\geq\rho^{n/2}\log(\log 18\rho)

whenever
|x|\leq 1/(6\rho)
and
|t|\leq 1/(18\rho^{2})
.
This means that

\|u_{\rho}\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R};L^{r^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}\gtrsim\rho^{n/2}\rho^{-2/q^{\prime}}\rho^{-n/r^{\prime}}\log(\log 18\rho).

Since
2/q^{\prime}=n/2-n/r^{\prime}
we see that

\|u_{\rho}\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R};L^{r^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}\gtrsim\log(\log 18\rho).

This inequality together with the estimate (
88
) shows that
(
82
) fails.

A more careful look at our counterexample for (

Let
(X_{\delta},\|\centerdot\|_{X_{\delta}})
and

(X_{\delta}^{\star},\|\centerdot\|_{X_{\delta}^{\star}})

For

and
\varepsilon<\delta
,

In order to show that (

(i\partial_{\mathrm{t}}+\Delta)w_{\varepsilon}=\chi^{R(\varepsilon)}(i\partial_{\mathrm{t}}+\Delta)(\varphi_{\varepsilon}\ast\tilde{w})+2\nabla\chi^{R(\varepsilon)}\cdot\nabla(\varphi_{\varepsilon}\ast\tilde{w})+\Delta\chi^{R(\varepsilon)}(\varphi_{\varepsilon}\ast\tilde{w})\kern 5.0pt\textnormal{in}\kern 5.0pt\Sigma_{\delta}.

) and (

Lemma B.1
.

Consider

(a)

For every

(b)

The Fourier transform of

can be extended to

\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{C}_{|\Im|<c}
as the continuous function

\widehat{F}:(\tau,\xi^{\prime},\zeta)\mapsto\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{\frac{n+1}{2}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-i(\tau t+(\xi^{\prime},\zeta)\cdot x)}F(t,x)\,\mathrm{d}(t,x),

\widehat{e^{\lambda\mathrm{x}_{n}}F}(\tau,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{n})=\widehat{F}(\tau,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{n}+i\lambda)

(\tau,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{n})\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
such that

(c)

For every

(\tau,\xi^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n-1}

\zeta\in\mathbb{C}_{|\Im|<c}\mapsto\widehat{F}(\tau,\xi^{\prime},\zeta)

is holomorphic in
\mathbb{C}_{|\Im|<c}
.

Proof.

Start by proving (
a
). Note that

\|e^{\nu\cdot\mathrm{x}}F\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\|e^{-(c-|\nu|)|x|}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|e^{c|\mathrm{x}|}F\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R};L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))},

where
e^{-(c-|\nu|)|x|}\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
since

, with
p^{\prime}
denoting the
conjugate of

.

The property (
b
)
is a direct consequence of (

) since
for
(\tau,\xi^{\prime},\zeta)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{C}_{|\Im|<c}
,
we have that

\displaystyle\widehat{e^{\Im\zeta e_{n}\cdot\mathrm{x}}F}(\tau,\xi^{\prime},\Re\zeta)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{\frac{n+1}{2}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-i(\tau t+(\xi^{\prime},\Re\zeta)\cdot x)}e^{\Im\zeta e_{n}\cdot x}F(t,x)\,\mathrm{d}(t,x)

Hence, we can use the last expression to extend
\widehat{F}
to

\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{C}_{|\Im|<c}
. Furthermore, choosing

\widehat{e^{\lambda\mathrm{x}_{n}}F}(\tau,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{n})=\widehat{F}(\tau,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{n}+i\lambda).

This proves the property (

Finally, to verify the property (
c

needs to check that the function satisfies the Cauchy–Riemann equations. This

and

, such that

(101)

\|T_{\nu}g\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R};L^{r\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}\leq C\|g\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R};L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}

for all
g\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Let
G\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R};L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
with
p\in[1,q]
denote the function

G(t,\centerdot)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &F(t,\centerdot)&&\textnormal{if}\,t\in(0,T),\\
&0&&\textnormal{if}\,t\in\mathbb{R}\setminus(0,T).\end{aligned}\right.

Obviously,
e^{c|\mathrm{x}|}G\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R};L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
with
p\in[1,q]
.
As a consequence of the inequality (
101
),
T_{\nu}
can be
extended to define
T_{\nu}(e^{\nu\cdot\mathrm{x}}G)
for every

\nu\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
such that
|\nu|<c
since

\|e^{\nu\cdot\mathrm{x}}G\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R};L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}=\|e^{\nu\cdot\mathrm{x}}F\|_{L^{q}((0,T);L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}

and (
100
) holds. Furthermore,

(102)

\|T_{\nu}(e^{\nu\cdot\mathrm{x}}G)\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R};L^{r\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}\leq C\|e^{\nu\cdot\mathrm{x}}F\|_{L^{q}((0,T);L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}

for all
\nu\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
such that
|\nu|<c
.

For
\nu\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
define

\Gamma_{\nu}=\{(\tau,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}:\tau=-(\xi+i\nu)\cdot(\xi+i\nu)\}.

Since the codimension of
\Gamma_{\nu}
is
2
, we have by
the item (
a
) of
lemma

B.1

that—for every
\phi\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
and every
\nu\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
such
that
|\nu|<c
—the function

(\tau,\xi)\in(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})\setminus\Gamma_{\nu}\mapsto\frac{\widehat{e^{\nu\cdot\mathrm{x}}G}(\tau,\xi)}{-\tau-(\xi+i\nu)\cdot(\xi+i\nu)}\,\widecheck{e^{-\nu\cdot\mathrm{x}}\phi}(\tau,\xi)

can be extended to
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
so that it represents an element in

L^{1}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Here

\widecheck{e^{-\nu\cdot\mathrm{x}}\phi}(\tau,\xi)=\widehat{e^{-\nu\cdot\mathrm{x}}\phi}(-\tau,-\xi).

Hence, we can write

\displaystyle\langle e^{-\nu\cdot\mathrm{x}}T_{\nu}(e^{\nu\cdot\mathrm{x}}G),\phi\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle T_{\nu}(e^{\nu\cdot\mathrm{x}}G),e^{-\nu\cdot\mathrm{x}}\phi\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\widehat{e^{\nu\cdot\mathrm{x}}G}(\tau,\xi)}{-\tau-(\xi+i\nu)\cdot(\xi+i\nu)}\,\widecheck{e^{-\nu\cdot\mathrm{x}}\phi}(\tau,\xi)\,\mathrm{d}(\tau,\xi)

for all
\phi\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
and

\nu\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
such that
|\nu|<c
.
For every
\nu\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
,
there exists a
Q\in\mathrm{O}(n)
so
that
\nu=|\nu|Qe_{n}
, with
e_{n}
the
n^{\rm th}
element of the standard
basis of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Let
G_{Q}
and
\phi_{Q}
denote the functions

G_{Q}(t,x)=G(t,Qx)
and
\phi_{Q}(t,x)=\phi(t,Qx)
for all

(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Changing variables, we have that

\langle e^{-\nu\cdot\mathrm{x}}T_{\nu}(e^{\nu\cdot\mathrm{x}}G),\phi\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\widehat{e^{|\nu|\mathrm{x}_{n}}G_{Q}}(\tau,\xi)}{-\tau-(\xi+i|\nu|e_{n})\cdot(\xi+i|\nu|e_{n})}\,\widecheck{e^{-|\nu|\mathrm{x}_{n}}\phi_{Q}}(\tau,\xi)\,\mathrm{d}(\tau,\xi).

By the item (
b
) of the
lemma

B.1
,
we have that
the right-hand side of the previous identity is equal to

\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}}\frac{\widehat{G_{Q}}(\tau,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{n}+i|\nu|)}{-\tau-|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}-(\xi_{n}+i|\nu|)^{2}}\,\widecheck{\phi_{Q}}(\tau,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{n}+i|\nu|)\,\mathrm{d}(\tau,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{n}).

By the Fubini–Tonelli theorem the function

\xi_{n}\in\mathbb{R}\mapsto\frac{\widehat{G_{Q}}(\tau,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{n}+i|\nu|)}{-\tau-|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}-(\xi_{n}+i|\nu|)^{2}}\,\widecheck{\phi_{Q}}(\tau,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{n}+i|\nu|)

belongs to
L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
for almost every
(\tau,\xi^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
,
the function

H_{Q}^{|\nu|}:(\tau,\xi^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\mapsto\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\widehat{G_{Q}}(\tau,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{n}+i|\nu|)}{-\tau-|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}-(\xi_{n}+i|\nu|)^{2}}\,\widecheck{\phi_{Q}}(\tau,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{n}+i|\nu|)\,\mathrm{d}\xi_{n}

belongs to
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n-1})
, and

\langle e^{-\nu\cdot\mathrm{x}}T_{\nu}(e^{\nu\cdot\mathrm{x}}G),\phi\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}H_{Q}^{|\nu|}(\tau,\xi^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}(\tau,\xi^{\prime}).

Note that, by the
corollary

B.2
and the
lemma

B.1

the function

(103)

\xi_{n}\in\mathbb{R}\mapsto\frac{\widehat{G_{Q}}(\tau,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{n})}{-\tau-|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}-\xi_{n}^{2}}\,\widecheck{\phi_{Q}}(\tau,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{n})

belongs to
L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
for all
(\tau,\xi^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
such that

\tau\neq-|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}
.

Our final goal will be to show that the function

H_{Q}:(\tau,\xi^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\mapsto\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\widehat{G_{Q}}(\tau,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{n})}{-\tau-|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}-\xi_{n}^{2}}\,\widecheck{\phi_{Q}}(\tau,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{n})\,\mathrm{d}\xi_{n}

satisfies that

(104)

H_{Q}(\tau,\xi^{\prime})=H_{Q}^{|\nu|}(\tau,\xi^{\prime})\qquad\forall(\tau,\xi^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n-1}:\tau\neq-|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}.

With this at hand, we have that

(105)

\langle e^{-\nu\cdot\mathrm{x}}T_{\nu}(e^{\nu\cdot\mathrm{x}}G),\phi\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}H_{Q}(\tau,\xi^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}(\tau,\xi^{\prime}).

As we will see at a later stage, the function

(106)

(\tau,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\mapsto\frac{\widehat{G_{Q}}(\tau,\xi)}{-\tau-|\xi|^{2}}\,\widecheck{\phi_{Q}}(\tau,\xi)

belongs to
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Hence, by the Fubini–Tonelli theorem we have
that

\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}H_{Q}(\tau,\xi^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}(\tau,\xi^{\prime})=\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\widehat{G_{Q}}(\tau,\xi)}{-\tau-|\xi|^{2}}\,\widecheck{\phi_{Q}}(\tau,\xi)\,\mathrm{d}(\tau,\xi).

Using the identity (
105
) and the change of variables that removes the
orthogonal matrix
Q
, we have

(107)

\langle e^{-\nu\cdot\mathrm{x}}T_{\nu}(e^{\nu\cdot\mathrm{x}}G),\phi\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\widehat{G}(\tau,\xi)}{-\tau-|\xi|^{2}}\,\widecheck{\phi}(\tau,\xi)\,\mathrm{d}(\tau,\xi)

for all
\phi\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
and

\nu\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
such that
|\nu|<c
.

Let
v\in C(\mathbb{R};L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
denote the function

v(t,\centerdot)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &u(t,\centerdot)&&\textnormal{if}\,t\in[0,T],\\
&0&&\textnormal{if}\,t\in\mathbb{R}\setminus[0,T].\end{aligned}\right.

The continuity follows from the fact that

u(0,\centerdot)=u(T,\centerdot)=0
. Using this,
one can check that

(i\partial_{\mathrm{t}}+\Delta)v=G\textnormal{ in }\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Taking Fourier transform we have that

(108)

(-\tau-|\xi|^{2})\widehat{v}(\tau,\xi)=\widehat{G}(\tau,\xi)\kern 5.0pt\dot{\forall}(\tau,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}.

This identity and (
107
) yield

\langle e^{-\nu\cdot\mathrm{x}}T_{\nu}(e^{\nu\cdot\mathrm{x}}G),\phi\rangle=\langle v,\phi\rangle

for all
\phi\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
, or equivalently

\langle T_{\nu}(e^{\nu\cdot\mathrm{x}}G),\psi\rangle=\langle e^{\nu\cdot\mathrm{x}}v,\psi\rangle

for all
\psi\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
—it is enough to consider

\phi=e^{\nu\cdot\mathrm{x}}\psi
for an arbitrary
\psi
. In particular,

T_{\nu}(e^{\nu\cdot\mathrm{x}}G)|_{\Sigma}=e^{\nu\cdot\mathrm{x}}u.

Then, the inequality (
102
) is the one stated in the

lemma

5.5
.

In order to conclude this proof, it remains to check two points:
the function (
106
) belongs to
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and the identity
(
104
) holds. Start by showing the first of these points.
It is clear that (
106
) belongs to
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
if, and only if,
the function

(109)

(\tau,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\mapsto\frac{\widehat{G}(\tau,\xi)}{-\tau-|\xi|^{2}}\,\widecheck{\phi}(\tau,\xi)

also belongs to
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
. In order to check that (
109
)
belongs to
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
we derive from the identity (
108
) that

\widehat{v}(\tau,\xi)=\frac{\widehat{G}(\tau,\xi)}{-\tau-|\xi|^{2}}

for almost every
(\tau,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\tau\neq-|\xi|^{2}
.
Since the measure of the set

\{(\tau,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}:\tau=-|\xi|^{2}\}
is zero,
the above identity holds for almost every
(\tau,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
This together with fact that
v\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
ensure that
(
109
) belongs to
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Finally, let us check the identity (
104
). By the
corollary

B.2

and the
lemma

B.1

the function

\zeta\in\mathbb{C}_{|\Im|<c}\mapsto\frac{\widehat{G_{Q}}(\tau,\xi^{\prime},\zeta)}{-\tau-|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}-\zeta^{2}}\,\widecheck{\phi_{Q}}(\tau,\xi^{\prime},\zeta)

is holomorphic. Then, by the Cauchy–Goursat theorem we have that

\displaystyle H_{Q}(\tau,\xi^{\prime})

\displaystyle+\lim_{\rho\to\infty}i\int_{0}^{|\nu|}\frac{\widehat{G_{Q}}(\tau,\xi^{\prime},\rho+it)}{-\tau-|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}-(\rho+it)^{2}}\,\widecheck{\phi_{Q}}(\tau,\xi^{\prime},\rho+it)\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=H_{Q}^{|\nu|}(\tau,\xi^{\prime})+\lim_{\rho\to\infty}i\int_{0}^{|\nu|}\frac{\widehat{G_{Q}}(\tau,\xi^{\prime},-\rho+it)}{-\tau-|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}-(-\rho+it)^{2}}\,\widecheck{\phi_{Q}}(\tau,\xi^{\prime},-\rho+it)\,\mathrm{d}t

for all
(\tau,\xi^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
such that

\tau\neq-|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}
. The item (
b
) of the

lemma

B.1
, and the decay as
\rho
tends to infinity,
can be used to show that each of the limits in
the previous identity is zero. Hence, the identity (
104
) holds.
This concludes the proof of the
lemma

5.5
.

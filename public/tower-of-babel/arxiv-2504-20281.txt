Continuum mechanics model of graphene as a doubly-periodic perforated thin elastic plate
Yuri A. Antipov

Abstract
In this paper, a continuum mechanics model of graphene is proposed, and its analytical solution is derived. Graphene is modeled as a doubly-periodic thin elastic plate with a hexagonal cell having a circular hole at the hexagon center. Graphene is characterized by a general chiral vector and is subject to remote tension. For the solution, the Filshtinskii solution obtained for the symmetric case is generalized for any chirality. The method uses the doubly-periodic Kolosov-Muskhelishvili complex potentials, the theory of the elliptic Weierstrass function and quasi-doubly-periodic meromorphic functions and reduces the model to an infinite system of linear algebraic equations with complex coefficients. Analytical expressions and numerical values for the stresses are displacements are obtained and discussed. The displacements expressions possess the Young modulus and Poisson ratio of the graphene bonds. They are derived as functions of the effective graphene moduli available in the literature.



Keywords: A. graphene; B. plane elasticity; C. complex potentials, D. Weierstrass elliptic function; E. elastic moduli of the bonds.

1
Introduction

Characterization of the mechanical properties of graphene and other two-dimensional materials is a challenging problem for their thickness.
Different experimental and numerical simulation techniques are used to determine elastic properties and in particular, estimate
the Young modulus
E
and Poisson ratio
\nu
of graphene. By applying a force
F
and measuring the relative elongation
\Delta L
for an equilibrium length
L_{0}
, the cross-section
A
, and the relative change in radius
\Delta R/R
the constants
E
and
\nu

of a single-walled carbon nanotube
were calculated
[
8
]
as
\frac{F}{A}/\frac{\Delta L}{L_{0}}
and
-\frac{\Delta R}{R}/\frac{\Delta L}{L_{0}}
, respectively. In a similar manner these constants were recovered
[
8
]

for graphene.
A thorough comparative analysis of different experimental measurements,
ab initio
calculations and computations based on density functional theory presented in
[
7
]
,
[
10
]
shows that further work is needed to improve the accuracy of the data.
The finite element method was applied
[
2
]
to investigate the accuracy of Raman spectroscopy method for measurement of the graphene elastic moduli. It was found that
the determination of the pre-strain of a freestanding graphene membrane is essential for improvement of estimates of the elastic moduli
determined via Raman spectroscopy. Continuum elasticity and tight-binding atomistic simulations were combined
[
1
]
to derive constitutive nonlinear stress-strain relations for graphene. These relations were employed to estimate the elastic moduli of graphene.
The majority of measurements and numerical calculations report that the value of graphene Young’s modulus is
E\sim 1.0
TPa
[
9
]
,
[
10
]
.
For the graphene Poisson ratio the data deviate significantly,
0.12\leq\nu\leq 0.413

[
10
]
.

The values reported in the literature are effective elastic constants of graphene and do not distinguish the bond moduli from
those obtained for a sample graphene membrane. To have estimates for the elastic moduli of the graphene bonds is important for mechanical models of graphene
when it is of interest to evaluate the displacements and strains and also to analyze the mechanics of the defective van der Waals
structures such as hexagon–heptagon defects in graphene
[
14
]
.

The main aims of this paper are (1) to develop a continuum mechanics model of graphene as a thin elastic doubly-periodic perforated hexagonal
plate and (2) to estimate the Young modulus and Poisson ratio of the graphene bonds as functions of the corresponding graphene effective
moduli available in the literature.
In Section 2 we formulate the mathematical model of plane strain elasticity for a doubly-periodic plane with a hexagonal cell having
a circular hole in the center of the cell. The solution to the model problem is represented as a sum of the solution to the classical Kirsch problem [8] and the solution to an auxiliary problem with vanishing stresses at infinity and inhomogeneous boundary conditions
on the holes boundary. In Section 3 we employ the Filshtinskii method
[
4
]
of elliptic functions to reduce the problem
to an infinite system. The methodology is based on the use of the Kolosov-Muskhelishvili potentials [9] and their series representations
in terms of the Weierstrass elliptic function [10] and its derivatives and the Natanzon quasi-doubly-periodic function [11] and its derivatives.
At the final stage the method requires to satisfy the boundary conditions on the hole boundary which lead to an infinite system
of linear algebraic equations for the series coefficients.
In Ref
[
4
]
the infinite system was solved for the symmetric case (in the modern terminology, for the armchair - zigzag
symmetry) when the complex Kolosov-Muskhelishvili potentials satisfy the symmetry conditions

\overline{\Phi(z)}=\Phi(\bar{z}),\quad\overline{\Psi(z)}=\Psi(\bar{z}).

(1.1)

In this case a unit cell is an equilateral triangle, the system is simplified, and the expansion coefficients are real.
We generalize the method to structures characterized by any chiral vector. In the general case the condition (
1.1
) is not valid,
the unit cell is a hexagon, and the coefficients are complex and solve an infinite system with complex entries.
In Section 4 we derive series representations of the stresses and displacements in the perforated plane. The displacements
formulas possess the shear modulus
G=\frac{E}{2(1+\nu)}
and the Poisson ratio
\nu
of the graphene bonds (the material of the perforated plane) which are not available in the
literature.
We modify the method
[
4
]
used for the derivation of the effective moduli
E^{\circ}
and
\nu^{\circ}
in terms of
E
and
\nu

in the case of a triangular equilateral cell by reversing it and derive the elastic moduli
E
and
\nu
of the graphene bonds as functions of the effective
moduli
E^{\circ}
and
\nu^{\circ}
measured by tests.
Finally, we report and discuss our numerical results for the stresses, displacements and elastic moduli.

2
Formulation

Figure 1:
Geometry of the problem

Consider extension of an infinite single-layer graphene sheet by remote constant loads
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}
(Fig. 1).
The lattice vectors of graphene are selected as

\omega_{1}=\left(\frac{a\sqrt{3}}{2},-\frac{a}{2}\right),\quad\omega_{2}=\left(\frac{a\sqrt{3}}{2},\frac{a}{2}\right),

(2.1)

where
a=|\omega_{1}|=|\omega_{2}|=142\sqrt{3}=246
pm is the lattice graphene constant and
\frac{a}{\sqrt{3}}=142
pm is the nearest-neighbor distance between two carbon atoms.
The chiral vector
C_{h}

[
13
]
is expressed through the lattice vectors as

C_{h}=m\omega_{1}+n\omega_{2}\equiv(m,n),\quad m,n=\pm 1,\pm 2,\ldots.

(2.2)

The armchair and zigzag symmetries correspond to the chiral vectors
(n,n)
and
(n,0)
, respectively.
The graphene sheet
is modeled as a hexagonal doubly-periodic perforated thin elastic plate. The loads
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}
in the upper half-plane
make angles
\alpha\in[0,\pi/2]
and
\alpha+\pi/2
with the positive
x
-axis, respectively, and
the angle
\alpha
in the range
0\leq\alpha\leq\frac{\pi}{6}
relates to the chiral vector as

\alpha=\frac{\pi}{6}-\cos^{-1}\frac{C_{h}\cdot\omega_{2}}{|C_{h}||\omega_{2}|}=\frac{\pi}{6}-\cos^{-1}\frac{2n+m}{2\sqrt{n^{2}+m^{2}+nm}}.

(2.3)

The radius of the holes is denotes by
\lambda
, and
the boundary of the circular hole in each hexagonal cell or, equivalently, the graphene bonds, are free of traction.

The solution to the model problem, call it
P^{t}
, is comprised of the solution of the classical Kirsch problem
[
6
]
,
P^{K}
, of extension of an elastic plane with a
circular hole by remote loads
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}
and the plane problem
P
of a doubly-periodic perforated plane (Fig. 1) with stresses vanishing at infinity
and inhomogeneous boundary conditions on the boundary of the holes. These conditions are chosen such that the total traction in Problem
P^{t}
vanishes on the boundary.
The solution to Problem
P^{K}
written in terms of the stresses
\sigma_{r}
and
\tau_{r\theta}
in polar coordinates
(r,\theta)
has the form

\sigma_{r}^{K}=\sigma_{+}+\sigma_{-}\cos 2\psi,\quad\tau_{r\theta}^{K}=-\sigma_{-}\sin 2\psi,

(2.4)

where

\sigma_{+}=\frac{\sigma_{1}+\sigma_{2}}{2},\quad\sigma_{-}=\frac{\sigma_{1}-\sigma_{2}}{2},\quad\psi=\theta-\alpha.

(2.5)

Problem
P
is governed by the equilibrium equations of plane elasticity

\frac{\partial\sigma_{x}}{\partial x}+\frac{\partial\tau_{xy}}{\partial y}=0,\quad\frac{\partial\tau_{xy}}{\partial x}+\frac{\partial\sigma_{y}}{\partial y}=0,\quad(x,y)\in{\cal D},

(2.6)

and the compatibility conditions

\Delta(\sigma_{x}+\sigma_{y})=0,\quad(x,y)\in{\cal D},

(2.7)

where
{\cal D}
is the graphene domain (the perforated plane) and
\sigma_{x},\sigma_{y}
, and
\tau_{xy}
are the stress tensor components in the Cartesian coordinates
x,y
.
At infinity, the stresses vanish, while on the circle
L_{mn}
, the boundary of the hole
D_{mn}
, the traction components have the values

\sigma_{r}=-\sigma_{+}-\sigma_{-}\cos 2\psi,\quad\tau_{r\theta}=\sigma_{-}\sin 2\psi,\quad r=\lambda,\quad 0\leq\theta\leq 2\pi.

(2.8)

In addition, the stresses
\sigma_{x},\sigma_{y}
, and
\tau_{xy}
have to be doubly-periodic functions and satisfy the condition

(\sigma_{x},\sigma_{y},\tau_{xy})(z)=(\sigma_{x},\sigma_{y},\tau_{xy})(z+m\omega_{1}+n\omega_{2}),\quad m,n=0,\pm 1,\pm 2,\ldots,

(2.9)

where

\omega_{1}=\frac{a\sqrt{3}}{2}-\frac{ia}{2},\quad\omega_{2}=\frac{a\sqrt{3}}{2}+\frac{ia}{2}

(2.10)

are the lattice vectors written in the complex form and
z=x+iy\in H_{00}\setminus D_{00}
is a point in the unit hexagonal cell
H_{00}
centered at the origin of the hole
D_{00}

with the boundary
L_{00}
.

3
Solution of the boundary-value problem

3.1
Series representation of the solution in terms of the Weierstrass and Natanzon functions

The first step of the solution procedure is to represent the stresses by means of the classical Kolosov-Muskhelishvili potentials

\Phi(z)
and
\Psi(z)
analytic everywhere in the domain
{\cal D}

[
11
]

\sigma_{x}+\sigma_{y}=2[\Phi(z)+\overline{\Phi(z)}]=4\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits\Phi(z),

\sigma_{y}-\sigma_{x}+2i\tau_{xy}=2[\bar{z}\Phi^{\prime}(z)+\Psi(z)],\quad z\in{\cal D}.

(3.1)

As it is known, the stresses expressed through analytic functions by these relations automatically
satisfy the equilibrium equations (
2.6
) and the compatibility condition (
2.7
). Because of the structure and the applied load symmetries these functions
have to be even,

\Phi(z)=\Phi(-z),\quad\Psi(z)=\Psi(-z),\quad z\in{\cal D}.

(3.2)

Also, due to the doubly-periodicity property (
2.9
) of the stresses and the representations (
3.1
), the function
\Phi(z)
has to be doubly-periodic,
while the function
\Psi(z)
is to be quasi-doubly-periodic,

\Phi(z+\omega_{j})-\Phi(z)=0,\quad\Psi(z+\omega_{j})-\Psi(z)=-\overline{\omega_{j}}\Phi^{\prime}(z),\quad j=1,2.

(3.3)

General series representations of such functions has the form
[
4
]

\Phi(z)=\alpha_{0}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{k}\frac{\lambda^{2k}\wp^{(2k-2)}(z)}{(2k-1)!},

\Psi(z)=\beta_{0}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\beta_{k}\frac{\lambda^{2k}\wp^{(2k-2)}(z)}{(2k-1)!}-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{k}\frac{\lambda^{2k}{\cal N}^{(2k-1)}(z)}{(2k-1)!}.

(3.4)

Here,
\alpha_{k}
and
\beta_{k}
are arbitrary complex coefficients to be determined,
\wp(z)
is Weierstrass’ elliptic function
[
5
]

\wp(z)=\frac{1}{z^{2}}+\sum_{m,n}{}^{\prime}\left[\frac{1}{(z-w)^{2}}-\frac{1}{w^{2}}\right]

(3.5)

and
{\cal N}(z)
is Natanzon’s meromorphic function
[
12
]

{\cal N}(z)=\sum_{m,n}{}^{\prime}\bar{w}\left[\frac{1}{(z-w)^{2}}-\frac{2z}{w^{3}}-\frac{1}{w^{2}}\right].

(3.6)

The accent on the summation sign indicates that the value
w=0
is excluded from the summation, while
w
is defined by

w=m\omega_{1}+n\omega_{2},\quad m,n=0,\pm 1,\pm 2,\ldots.

(3.7)

The series expansions (
3.4
) of the functions
\Phi(z)
and
\Psi(z)
in terms of the even and odd order derivatives of the functions
\wp(z)

and
{\cal N}(z)
converge uniformly and absolutely in the domain
{\cal D}
. The derivatives
\wp^{(2k-2)}(z)
and
{\cal N}^{(2k-1)}(z)
are holomorphic
in the domain
{\cal D}
(all their poles lie in the holes that is in the exterior of the domain
{\cal D}
).
The Weierstrass function
\wp(z)
and all its derivatives

\wp^{(k)}(z)=(-1)^{k}(k+1)!\left[\frac{1}{z^{k+2}}+\sum_{m,n}{}^{\prime}\frac{1}{(z-w)^{k+2}}\right]

(3.8)

are doubly-periodic,

\wp^{(k)}(z+\omega_{j})-\wp^{(k)}(z)=0,\quad j=1,2,\quad k=0,1,\ldots,

(3.9)

while the Natanzon function and its derivatives

{\cal N}^{\prime}(z)=-2\sum_{m,n}{}^{\prime}\left[\frac{\bar{w}}{(z-w)^{3}}+\frac{\bar{w}}{w^{3}}\right],

{\cal N}^{(k)}(z)=(-1)^{k}(k+1)!\sum_{m,n}{}^{\prime}\frac{\bar{w}}{(z-w)^{k+2}},\quad k=2,3,\ldots,

(3.10)

are quasi-doubly-periodic,

{\cal N}^{(k)}(z+\omega_{j})-{\cal N}^{(k)}(z)=\bar{\omega}_{j}\wp^{(k)}(z)+\gamma_{j}\delta_{k0},\quad j=1,2,\quad k=0,1,\ldots,

(3.11)

where
\delta_{k0}
is the Kronecker symbol, the constants
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
satisfy the following relations
[
4
]
:

\gamma_{2}\omega_{1}-\gamma_{1}\omega_{2}=\delta_{1}\bar{\omega}_{2}-\delta_{2}\bar{\omega}_{1},\quad\delta_{j}=2\zeta\left(\frac{\omega_{j}}{2}\right),\quad j=1,2,

(3.12)

\zeta(z)
is the Weierstrass
\zeta
-function,
\wp(z)=-\zeta^{\prime}(z)
,

\zeta(z)=\frac{1}{z}+\sum_{m,n}{}^{\prime}\left[\frac{1}{z-w}+\frac{1}{w}+\frac{z}{w^{2}}\right],

(3.13)

and
\delta_{1}
and
\delta_{2}
are cyclic constants. The
\zeta
-function is quasi-doubly-periodic with the property
[
5
]

\zeta(z+\omega_{j})-\zeta(z)=\delta_{j},\quad j=1,2.

(3.14)

We remark that the derivatives of even order of the Weierstrass function and odd order of the Natanzon function are even.
Therefore the functions in (
3.4
) are even.

3.2
Determinafiltion of the coefficients
\alpha_{0}
and
\beta_{0}

We proceed now with determination of the coefficients
\alpha_{k}
and
\beta_{k}
(
k=0,1,\ldots.
)
Consider any two congruent points
A\in{\cal D}
and
B\in{\cal D}
that is two points connected by the condition
A=B+m\omega_{1}+n\omega_{2}
,
m,n=0\pm 1,\pm 2,\ldots
.
The main vector along any curve
\Gamma=AB
is defined by
[
11
]

X+iY=-i[g(z)]_{A}^{B},

(3.15)

where
[g(z)]_{A}^{B}
is the increment of the function
g(z)
when

traverses the contour
\Gamma
and

g(z)=\varphi(z)+z\overline{\varphi^{\prime}(z)}+\overline{\psi(z)}.

(3.16)

This expression is independent of the contour profile and
equals
g(B)-g(A)
.
The functions
\varphi(z)
and
\psi(z)
are the Muskhelishvili displacement potentials,
\Phi(z)=\varphi^{\prime}(z)
,
\Psi(z)=\psi^{\prime}(z)
, and obtained by integrating the series (
3.4
)

\varphi(z)=\alpha_{0}z-\alpha_{1}\lambda^{2}\zeta(z)+\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\alpha_{k}\frac{\lambda^{2k}\wp^{(2k-3)}(z)}{(2k-1)!},

\psi(z)=\beta_{0}z-\beta_{1}\lambda^{2}\zeta(z)-\alpha_{1}\lambda^{2}{\cal N}(z)+\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\beta_{k}\frac{\lambda^{2k}\wp^{(2k-3)}(z)}{(2k-1)!}-\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\alpha_{k}\frac{\lambda^{2k}{\cal N}^{(2k-2)}(z)}{(2k-1)!}.

(3.17)

Since
A
and
B
are congruent points, the main vector has to vanish and therefore
g(B)=g(A)
. In Ref
[
4
]
this condition is considered in the case when
the potentials
\Phi(z)
and
\Psi(z)
are not only even but also meet the symmetry conditions

\Phi(\bar{z})=\overline{\Phi(z)},\quad\Psi(\bar{z})=\overline{\Psi(z)},\quad z\in{\cal D},

(3.18)

which imply that all the coefficients
\alpha_{k}
and
\beta_{k}
are real. In our case, when the angle
\alpha
is arbitrary or, equivalently, for the chiral vector is not necessarily zigzag or armchair, in general, the conditions (
3.18
) are not valid, and
the coefficients
\alpha_{k}
and
\beta_{k}
are complex. Since the Weierstrass
\zeta
-function satisfies the quasi-periodicity condition

\zeta(z+\omega_{j})-\zeta(z)=\delta_{j},\quad\delta_{j}=2\zeta\left(\frac{\omega_{j}}{2}\right),\quad j=1,2,

(3.19)

the function
\varphi(\zeta)
is also quasi-doubly-periodic and

\varphi(\zeta+\omega_{j})-\varphi(z)=\alpha_{0}\omega_{j}-\alpha_{1}\lambda^{2}\delta_{j},\quad j=1,2.

(3.20)

Denote by
\chi(\zeta)=z\overline{\varphi^{\prime}(z)}+\overline{\psi(z)}
. The relations (
3.9
), (
3.11
) and (
3.17
) imply

\chi(z+\omega_{j})-\chi(z)=\bar{\alpha}_{0}\omega_{j}+\bar{\beta}_{0}\bar{\omega}_{j}-\bar{\alpha}_{1}\lambda^{2}\bar{\gamma}_{j}-\bar{\beta}_{1}\lambda^{2}\bar{\delta}_{j},\quad j=1,2.

(3.21)

On satisfying the condition
g(B)-g(A)=0
we deduce the following system of two equations:

2\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits\alpha_{0}\omega_{j}+\bar{\beta}_{0}\bar{\omega}_{j}-\lambda^{2}(\delta_{j}\alpha_{1}+\bar{\gamma}_{j}\bar{\alpha}_{1})-\bar{\beta}_{1}\lambda^{2}\bar{\delta}_{j}=0,\quad j=1,2.

(3.22)

Its solution is simplified if the Legendre relation
[
5
]
for the case
\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits(i\omega_{2}/\omega_{1})<0

\delta_{1}\omega_{2}-\delta_{2}\omega_{1}=2\pi i

(3.23)

is employed. We have

\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits\alpha_{0}=\frac{1}{2\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits(\omega_{1}\bar{\omega}_{2})}[\lambda^{2}\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits\{\alpha_{1}(\delta_{1}\bar{\omega}_{2}-\delta_{2}\bar{\omega}_{1})\},

\bar{\beta}_{0}=\frac{1}{2\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits(\omega_{1}\bar{\omega}_{2})}\left[\pi\lambda^{2}\alpha_{1}-\frac{i}{2}\lambda^{2}\bar{\alpha}_{1}(\bar{\gamma}_{1}\omega_{2}-\bar{\gamma}_{2}\omega_{1})-\frac{i}{2}\lambda^{2}\bar{\beta}_{1}(\bar{\delta}_{1}\omega_{2}-\bar{\delta}_{2}\omega_{1})\right].

(3.24)

where for the hexagonal cell under consideration
\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits(\omega_{1}\bar{\omega}_{2})=-\frac{a^{2}\sqrt{3}}{2}
.
Formulas (
3.24
) can be further simplified if we use the identities

\delta_{1}\bar{\omega}_{2}-\delta_{2}\bar{\omega}_{1}=0

(3.25)

valid
for hexagonal lattices. To prove them, note that for this type of symmetry

\zeta(e^{i\pi/6})=e^{-i\pi/6}\left\{\frac{1}{z}+\sum_{m,n}{}^{\prime}\left[\frac{1}{z-we^{-i\pi/6}}+\frac{1}{we^{-i\pi/6}}+\frac{z}{(we^{-i\pi/6})^{2}}\right]\right\}.

(3.26)

Therefore

\zeta(e^{i\pi/6})=e^{-i\pi/6}\zeta(z),\quad\zeta\left(\frac{\omega_{2}}{2}\right)=e^{-i\pi/3}\zeta\left(\frac{\omega_{1}}{2}\right),

(3.27)

and the identity (
3.25
) follows.
Notice the relation (
3.25
) is known
[
4
]
for equilateral triangular and square lattices. The identity (
3.25
) immediately implies that the constants in (
3.12
) vanish,

\gamma_{1}=\gamma_{2}=0
. Using these results we find the final formulas for the coefficients
\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits\alpha_{0}
and
\beta_{0}

\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits\alpha_{0}=-\frac{\pi\lambda^{2}\beta_{1}}{a^{2}\sqrt{3}},\quad\beta_{0}=-\frac{2\pi\lambda^{2}\bar{\alpha}_{1}}{a^{2}\sqrt{3}}.

(3.28)

It follows from here that
\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits\beta_{1}=0
. It will shown later that the solution is independent of
\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits\alpha_{0}
.

3.3
Infinite system of linear algebraic equations

The series representations (
3.4
) of the functions
\Phi(z)
and
\Psi(z)
have arbitrary complex coefficients

\alpha_{k}
and
\beta_{k}
. They have to be fixed from the boundary condition of the circle
L_{00}

(
2.8
) that can be written in the form
[
3
]

\Phi(t)+\overline{\Phi(t)}-[\bar{t}\Phi^{\prime}(t)+\Psi(t)]e^{2i\theta}=-\sigma_{+}-\sigma_{-}e^{2i(\theta-\alpha)},\quad 0\leq\theta\leq 2\pi.

(3.29)

In what follows we aim to recast the boundary condition as an infinite system for the unknown coefficients.

By representing the Weierstrass function
\wp(z)
by its Laurent series
[
5
]

\wp(z)=\frac{1}{z^{2}}+c_{2}z^{2}+\ldots+c_{s}z^{2s-2}+\ldots,\quad z\in{\cal A},

(3.30)

convergent uniformly and absolutely in any annulus
{\cal A}=\{0<r_{-}\leq|z|\leq r_{+}<\frac{a}{2}\}
. The coefficients
c_{s}
are expressed through the double series

c_{s}=(2s-1)\sum_{m,n}{}^{\prime}\frac{1}{w^{2s}},\quad w=m\omega_{1}+n\omega_{2},\quad m,n=0,\pm 1,\pm 2,\ldots.

(3.31)

In addition, the coefficients
c_{s}
can be recovered by the recursion formula

c_{s}=\frac{3}{(2s+1)(s-3)}\sum_{t=2}^{s-2}c_{t}c_{s-t},\quad s>3,

(3.32)

after the coefficients
c_{2}
and
c_{3}
are computed by (
3.31
). By differentiating
2k
-times the Laurent series (
3.30
),

\wp^{(2k)}(z)=\frac{(2k+1)!}{z^{2k+2}}+\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{(2k+2j)!c_{k+j+1}z^{2j}}{(2j)!},\quad k=1,2,\ldots.

(3.33)

The Laurent series of the derivatives of the Natanzon function has a similar form
[
4
]

{\cal N}^{\prime}(z)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}(2+2j)(1+2j)d_{j+1}z^{2j},

{\cal N}^{(2k+1)}(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{(2k+2+2j)!d_{k+j+1}z^{2j}}{(2j)!},\quad k=1,2,\ldots,

(3.34)

with the coefficients
d_{s}
defined by

d_{s}=\sum_{m,n}{}^{\prime}\frac{\bar{w}}{w^{2s+1}},\quad s=2,3,\ldots.

(3.35)

For these coefficients, there is no analog of the recursion formula (
3.32
) for the function
{\cal N}(z)
is not doubly-periodic and
has no analog of the differential equation

[\wp^{\prime}(z)]^{2}-4\wp^{3}(z)+g_{2}\wp(z)+g_{3}=0,\quad g_{2}=60\sum_{m,n}{}^{\prime}\frac{1}{w^{4}},\quad g_{3}=140\sum_{m,n}{}^{\prime}\frac{1}{w^{6}},

(3.36)

of the Weierstrass function that is exploited for the derivation of the recursion formula (
3.32
) for the coefficients
c_{s}
.
The expressions for the coefficients
c_{s}
and
d_{s}
can be simplified if the symmetry of the hexagonal lattice is taking into account.
Similar to the regular triangular lattice
[
4
]
for the case of hexagonal symmetry we have

c_{3s-1}=c_{3s-2}=0,\quad d_{3s}=d_{3s+1}=0,\quad s=1,2,\ldots.

(3.37)

The nonzero coefficients are computed by

c_{3s}=(6s-1)\sum_{m,n}{}^{\prime}\frac{1}{w^{6s}},\quad d_{3s-1}=\sum_{m,n}{}^{\prime}\frac{\bar{w}}{w^{6s-1}},\quad s\geq 1.

(3.38)

The recursion formula (
3.32
), when adjusted to hexagonal case, has the form

c_{3s}=\frac{1}{(6s+1)(s-1)}\sum_{t=1}^{s-1}c_{3t}c_{3(s-t)},\quad s\geq 2.

(3.39)

Substituting the series expansions of the derivatives of the functions
\wp(z)
and
{\cal N}(z)
into formulas (
3.4
) yields
[
4

\Phi(z)=\alpha_{0}+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\alpha_{k+1}\lambda^{2k+2}\left(\frac{1}{z^{2k+2}}+\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}r_{j,k}z^{2j}\right),

Conclusions

The paper presents a plane stress model of graphene subjected to tension at infinity. A graphene sheet characterized by a general chiral vector
is modeled as a
doubly-periodic thin plate whose unit cell is a hexagon with a central circular hole. For the solution, the method of doubly-periodic
Kolosov-Muskhelishvili complex potentials, the Weierstrass elliptic and Natanzon quasi-doubly-periodic meromorphic functions proposed for the
armchair-zigzag symmetry
[
4
]
is generalized to any chiral vector case. In contrast to the symmetric case, in the general case, the two Kolosov-Muskhelishvili potentials
do not satisfy the symmetry condition
\Phi(\bar{z})=\overline{\Phi(z)}
,
\Psi(\bar{z})=\overline{\Psi(z)}
, the unit cell is not reducible to an equilateral triangular cell,
and the resulting infinite system is not real and has complex coefficients.

We have derived series representations of the stresses and displacements. Numerical calculations show that for all chiral vectors tested that
is for all
\alpha\in[0,\frac{\pi}{6}]
,
the radial stress
\sigma_{r}
attains it maximum at the vertex
x=\frac{a}{\sqrt{3}}
,
y=0
. Since the displacements comprise a part of the solution to a plane elasticity
problem, they depends on the Young modulus
E
and Poisson ratio
\nu
of the material or, equivalently in the framework of the continuum mechanics model of graphene,
the graphene bonds. These elastic moduli are not available in the literature. What are known by experimental measurements and
ab initio
calculations are the effective Young modulus

E^{\circ}\sim 1
TPa and the effective Poisson ratio
\nu^{\circ}\in[0.12,0.413]
. We have derived expressions of the elastic constants of the graphene bonds as
functions of their effective counterparts. In particularly, it has been found that for the effective Poisson ratio
\nu^{\circ}=0.3
and the radius
\lambda=\frac{a}{5}

of the hole in a unit cell, the Young modulus of the graphene bonds exceeds its effective value
1TPa
by
74\%
.

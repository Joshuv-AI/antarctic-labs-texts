Not All Thats Rare Is Lost: Causal Paths to Rare Concept Synthesis
Bo-Kai Ruan, Zi-Xiang Ni, Bo-Lun Huang, Teng-Fang Hsiao, Hong-Han Shuai

Abstract
Diffusion models have shown strong capabilities in high-fidelity image generation but often falter when synthesizing rare concepts, i.e. , prompts that are infrequently observed in the training distribution. In this paper, we introduce RAP , a principled framework that treats rare concept generation as navigating a latent causal path: a progressive, model-aligned trajectory through the generative space from frequent concepts to rare targets. Rather than relying on heuristic prompt alternation, we theoretically justify that rare prompt guidance can be approximated by semantically related frequent prompts. We then formulate prompt switching as a dynamic process based on score similarity, enabling adaptive stage transitions. Furthermore, we reinterpret prompt alternation as a second-order denoising mechanism, promoting smooth semantic progression and coherent visual synthesis. Through this causal lens, we align input scheduling with the model’s internal generative dynamics. Experiments across diverse diffusion backbones demonstrate that RAP consistently enhances rare concept generation, outperforming strong baselines in both automated evaluations and human studies.

However, as diffusion models become increasingly central to content creation pipelines, there is a growing demand for accurately generating rare concepts,

One recent attempt to address this challenge is the Rare-to-Frequent (R2F) framework

While R2F makes an important step forward, it suffers from two fundamental limitations: a lack of theoretical guidance and abrupt prompt transitions. Without understanding the underlying generation dynamics of diffusion models, R2F relies on heuristic scheduling to decide when and how to switch prompts, often leading to unstable or suboptimal results. Furthermore, its discrete and abrupt alternation introduces temporal discontinuities, resulting in inconsistent object rendering and poor compositional coherence.

These limitations prevent the establishment of a smooth and coherent generation path from frequent to rare concepts, manifesting in two key challenges:
ineffective guidance
and
unstable transitions
. Specifically, the absence of adaptive control causes the model to either oversimplify or exaggerate certain visual details, depending on the prompt and model architecture. For example, in

fig.

1

To address these challenges, we propose
RAP
(
R
are Concept Generation via
A
daptive
P

: a progressive trajectory through the model’s generative space that begins with frequent, semantically related concepts and culminates in the realization of a rare target prompt. Instead of relying on fixed heuristic schedules,
RAP
adaptively discovers and follows this path by leveraging the model’s underlying score dynamics, using score similarity as a proxy for causal readiness. Stage transitions are treated as soft interventions, guided by score-matching criteria and stabilized through a second-order denoising process to ensure smooth semantic progression. In this framework, prompt scheduling is no longer a simple input manipulation but becomes a structured, causally grounded traversal aligned with the model’s internal generative process.

In summary, the key contributions can be summarized as follows.

•

We provide a theoretical foundation for rare concept generation by showing that the score function of a rare prompt can be effectively approximated using a semantically related frequent prompt, establishing the basis for the rare-to-frequent generation mechanism.

•

We propose a novel prompt-switching strategy based on score matching, combined with a second-order denoising process, which enables adaptive prompt transitions and ensures smoother generation, effectively mitigating instability and visual discontinuities.

•

Extensive experiments demonstrate that
RAP
consistently improves rare concept synthesis across multiple diffusion backbones and outperforms prior methods in both automated evaluations using
GPT-4o
and human preference studies.

Related Work and Preliminary

Text-to-Image Diffusion Models.

Diffusion models
[
7
]
have emerged as a powerful class of generative models, achieving state-of-the-art performance in text-to-image (T2I) synthesis through increased model capacity and large-scale training data. To address the computational inefficiency of operating in pixel space, Latent Diffusion Models (LDMs)
[
19
]
shift the diffusion process into a compressed latent space, enabling high-resolution image generation with reduced cost. Building on this foundation, SDXL
[
16
]
scales model parameters further and integrates the T5 encoder
[
17
]
for improved textual understanding. Transformer-based architectures have also gained popularity for their scalability and performance, as demonstrated in recent works
[
15
,
5
,
4
]
. To enhance cross-modal interaction, Multi-Modality Diffusion Transformers (MM-DiT)
[
6
,
1
]
concatenate tokens from multiple modalities, enabling joint attention computation across them. Additionally, Sana
[
23
]
introduces an efficient generation strategy via linear attention, significantly reducing computational overhead while maintaining quality. Despite these advances, generating images from rare or compositional prompts remains a fundamental challenge, often due to poor data support and unstable latent trajectories. In this work, we build on existing diffusion architectures and propose a framework specifically designed to enhance rare concept generation.

Preliminary — R2F:

Given a rare input prompt
\mathbf{c}_{R}
, the R2F framework leverages a large language model (LLM) to construct a sequence of related prompts
\{\mathbf{c}_{1},\mathbf{c}_{2},\cdots,\mathbf{c}_{n}\}
, ordered by their estimated concept prior probabilities in the dataset, such that
p_{data}(\mathbf{c}_{1})\geq\cdots\geq p_{data}(\mathbf{c}_{n})
, with
\mathbf{c}_{n}=\mathbf{c}_{R}
. Alongside these prompts, the LLM also predicts a set of visual detail levels
\{v_{1},v_{2},\cdots,v_{n-1}\}
, where each
v_{k}
indicates how long the model should stay at prompt
\mathbf{c}_{k}
before transitioning to
\mathbf{c}_{k+1}
. We refer to this transition from
\mathbf{c}_{k}
to
\mathbf{c}_{k+1}
as
prompt switching
. The sampling process begins with the most frequent and visually simple prompt
\mathbf{c}_{1}
. As the timestep
t
increases, the prompt is updated via prompt switching based on the visual detail levels, gradually progressing toward the target rare concept
\mathbf{c}_{R}
. To prevent semantic drift and maintain alignment with
\mathbf{c}_{R}
, R2F also incorporates a
prompt alternation
strategy: at even timesteps, the current prompt
\mathbf{c}_{t}
is selected from the scheduled list
\{\mathbf{c}_{1},\cdots,\mathbf{c}_{n}\}
; at odd timesteps,
\mathbf{c}_{t}
is set directly to the original rare prompt
\mathbf{c}_{R}
.

3
RAP Framework

We conceptualize rare concept synthesis as navigating a
concept causal path
, a structured, progressive trajectory through the model’s generative space that starts from frequent, well-supported prompts and gradually transitions toward rare, underrepresented ones. Rather than relying on heuristic prompt alternation, our method explicitly aligns this traversal with the model’s internal dynamics. To implement this perspective, we revisit the R2F framework and address its core limitations through three key components: (1) a theoretical justification for how alternating between rare and frequent prompts facilitates generation (
section

3.1
); (2) a score-matching criterion to adaptively determine transition timing (
section

3.2
); and (3) a second-order denoising strategy for smoother, semantically consistent prompt interpolation (
section

3.3
). Together, these components form a unified, principled framework for rare concept generation.

3.1
Theoretical Foundations of Causal Traversal for Rare Concept Generation

We begin by grounding the RAP framework in a theoretical understanding of rare concept generation as a structured traversal through the generative space. In particular, we define a
concept causal path
through the generative space: an intentional traversal that begins with the stable distribution of
\mathbf{c}_{F}
and progresses toward the intended rare semantics of
\mathbf{c}_{R}
. Afterward, we reinterpret the prompt switching mechanism in R2F as navigating on
concept causal path
, where frequent prompts act as the start anchors and rare prompts serve as the end targets. Along this path, the model progresses through intermediate representations guided by rare and frequent prompts. To formalize this, we analyze how rare and frequent prompts interact through score replacement, showing that the generation trajectory can be interpreted as a causal mixture that evolves toward the intended rare concept.

As noted by
Song and Ermon 2019
, when a prompt
\mathbf{c}_{R}
corresponds to a rare concept, the conditional data distribution
p_{\text{data}}(\mathbf{x}_{t}|\mathbf{c}_{R})
exhibits near-zero density,
i.e.
,
p_{\text{data}}(\mathbf{x}_{t}|\mathbf{c}_{R})\approx 0
. This severe data sparsity results in unreliable or poorly estimated score functions, thereby hindering the ability of diffusion models to generate coherent outputs when conditioned directly on
\mathbf{c}_{R}
. In contrast, a semantically similar frequent prompt
\mathbf{c}_{F}
induces a well-supported distribution
p_{\text{data}}(\mathbf{x}_{t}|\mathbf{c}_{F})
, enabling more accurate score estimation. To formalize the notion of a
concept causal path
, we construct a surrogate distribution by interpolating between
p_{\theta}(\mathbf{x}_{t}|\mathbf{c}_{R})
and
p_{\theta}(\mathbf{x}_{t}|\mathbf{c}_{F})
. This mixture serves as a regularized approximation that preserves the semantic intent of the rare concept while benefiting from the structural stability of the frequent one. We formalize this intuition in the following theorem, with the proof provided in
appendix

A
.

Theorem 1

(Surrogate Score of Rare and Frequent Prompt Mixture)
.

Let
p_{\theta}(\mathbf{x}_{t}|\mathbf{c}_{R})
be the learned conditional data distribution induced by a rare prompt
\mathbf{c}_{R}
, and let
p_{\theta}(\mathbf{x}_{t}|\mathbf{c}_{F})
be the learned distribution induced by a semantically similar but more frequent prompt
\mathbf{c}_{F}
. Assume
p_{\theta}(\mathbf{x}_{t}|\mathbf{c}_{R})
is poorly estimated due to limited training data, and
p_{\theta}(\mathbf{x}_{t}|\mathbf{c}_{F})
lies in a well-supported region of the training distribution. We define the surrogate mixture distribution as follows:

\tilde{p}_{\theta}(\mathbf{x}_{t}|\mathbf{c}_{R}):=\lambda p_{\theta}(\mathbf{x}_{t}|\mathbf{c}_{R})+(1-\lambda)p_{\theta}(\mathbf{x}_{t}|\mathbf{c}_{F}),\quad\lambda\in[0,1].

(1)

Then, the score function of the surrogate distribution can be derived by:

\nabla_{\mathbf{x}_{t}}\log\tilde{p}_{\theta}(\mathbf{x}_{t}|\mathbf{c}_{R})=\frac{\lambda p_{\theta}(\mathbf{x}_{t}|\mathbf{c}_{R})}{\tilde{p}_{\theta}(\mathbf{x}_{t}|\mathbf{c}_{R})}\nabla_{\mathbf{x}_{t}}\log p_{\theta}(\mathbf{x}_{t}|\mathbf{c}_{R})+\frac{(1-\lambda)p_{\theta}(\mathbf{x}_{t}|\mathbf{c}_{F})}{\tilde{p}_{\theta}(\mathbf{x}_{t}|\mathbf{c}_{R})}\nabla_{\mathbf{x}_{t}}\log p_{\theta}(\mathbf{x}_{t}|\mathbf{c}_{F}).

(2)

Having established the surrogate distribution, we now analyze how it facilitates rare concept generation through a progressive transition from
\mathbf{c}_{F}
to
\mathbf{c}_{R}
. Under the assumption of semantic similarity, the corresponding log-densities are locally aligned and can be related as:

\log p_{\text{data}}(\mathbf{x}_{t}|\mathbf{c}_{R})=\log p_{\text{data}}(\mathbf{x}_{t}|\mathbf{c}_{F})+\eta(\mathbf{x}_{t}),

(3)

where
\eta(\mathbf{x}_{t})
denotes a smooth and relatively small perturbation. This relation implies that
\mathbf{c}_{F}
can serve as a reliable proxy for
\mathbf{c}_{R}
within regions of overlapping support in the data space. From a causal perspective, the frequent prompt
\mathbf{c}_{F}
acts as a waypoint that bridges well-supported and uncertain regions, enabling a stable transition toward the rare concept. We formalize this intuition in the following theorem, with the proof provided in
appendix

B
.

Theorem 2

(Score Substitution under Semantic Similarity)
.

Assume that
p_{data}(\mathbf{x}_{t}|\mathbf{c}_{R})
and
p_{data}(\mathbf{x}_{t}|\mathbf{c}_{F})
have overlapping support and represent semantically similar data distributions. Then, the score function conditioned on the rare prompt can be approximated by that of the frequent prompt:

\nabla_{\mathbf{x}_{t}}\log p_{data}(\mathbf{x}_{t}|\mathbf{c}_{R})\approx\nabla_{\mathbf{x}_{t}}\log p_{data}(\mathbf{x}_{t}|\mathbf{c}_{F})\approx\nabla_{\mathbf{x}_{t}}\log p_{\theta}(\mathbf{x}_{t}|\mathbf{c}_{F}),

(4)

and the surrogate score simplifies to:

\nabla_{\mathbf{x}_{t}}\log\tilde{p}_{\theta}(\mathbf{x}_{t}|\mathbf{c}_{R})\approx\nabla_{\mathbf{x}_{t}}\log p_{\theta}(\mathbf{x}_{t}|\mathbf{c}_{F}).

(5)

This approximation in
eq.

5
offers a theoretical explanation for the effectiveness of R2F. Rare concepts typically lie in regions of low data density, where score estimates are unreliable and highly uncertain. By leveraging the more stable and accurately estimated scores associated with frequent prompts, the model can navigate toward the rare concept more reliably during generation. Initializing the sampling process with a semantically aligned frequent concept, whose score function is well-supported and stable, allows the model to begin generation in a high-confidence region of the latent space. As generation progresses and the latent state becomes more refined, the model gradually traverses this causal path, transitioning from the stable support of
\mathbf{c}_{F}
toward the intended semantics of
\mathbf{c}_{R}
. This trajectory is further justified by the assumption of semantic similarity, as rare and frequent prompts often differ by only a single word or attribute, enabling smooth transitions along the generative process.

3.2
Adaptive Prompt Switching with Score Matching

Figure 2
:

Prompt switching at different stages
.

To align with the concept causal path, we propose a principled method for adaptively determining when to switch prompts during sampling—replacing the heuristic, LLM-assigned schedules used in R2F. Let
\mathbf{C}\coloneq\{\mathbf{c}_{1},\mathbf{c}_{2},\cdots,\mathbf{c}_{n-1},\mathbf{c}_{R}\}
be a sequence of prompts sorted by decreasing data likelihood, with
\mathbf{c}_{F}\in\{\mathbf{c}_{1},\cdots,\mathbf{c}_{n-1}\}
as frequent anchors and
\mathbf{c}_{R}
as the rare target. A fixed switching point, as used in R2F, can be suboptimal: switching too early may lead to semantic underdevelopment, while switching too late can cause the model to overcommit to the frequent prompt. The optimal transition point varies across models and prompts, making rigid schedules inherently brittle. For example, if
\mathbf{c}_{1}=
“
a horned animal
” and
\mathbf{c}_{R}=
“
a horned elephant
”, switching too early may miss the “horned” detail; switching too late may ignore the “elephant” entirely. This trade-off is illustrated in
fig.

2
, motivating the need for a dynamic, score-aware mechanism.

Figure 3
:

Illustration of prompt switching with matching score
\delta_{t}
.

Instead of relying on a predefined switching schedule, we propose an adaptive strategy that automatically determines when to transition to the next prompt by measuring the similarity between score functions. This approach enables the model to make context-aware decisions for transition from frequent to rare within the concept causal path, rather than depending on static, heuristic criteria. Inspired by the approximation in
eq.

5
, we assess whether the current frequent prompt
\mathbf{c}_{F}
provides a sufficiently accurate approximation of the target rare prompt
\mathbf{c}_{R}
. When the score functions induced by these prompts align closely, it indicates that the model has acquired enough semantic grounding to safely advance along the concept causal path without semantic collapse or inconsistency. To formalize this decision process, we define a
matching score

\delta_{t}
at timestep
t
:

\delta_{t}=\frac{\|\nabla_{\mathbf{x}_{t}}\log p_{\theta}(\mathbf{x}_{t}|\mathbf{c}_{k})-\nabla_{\mathbf{x}_{t}}\log p_{\theta}(\mathbf{x}_{t}|\mathbf{c}_{k+1})\|_{2}}{\|\nabla_{\mathbf{x}_{t}}\log p_{\theta}(\mathbf{x}_{t}|\mathbf{c}_{k})\|_{2}},

(6)

where
\mathbf{c}_{k}
and
\mathbf{c}_{k+1}
are consecutive prompts in the trajectory. The normalized score difference yields a robust, relative measure of semantic readiness. We trigger a prompt transition when
\delta_{t}\leq\delta^{*}
, adaptively guiding the sampling process along the causal path. This allows the model to progress based on internal generation dynamics rather than static heuristics or external predictions. The visualization of this behavior is shown in
fig.

3
.

3.3
Smoothing Prompt Alternation through Second-Order Denoising

Figure 4
:

Illustration of inconsistency
.

In addition to determining when to switch prompts, the original R2F framework also alternates between the frequent prompt
\mathbf{c}_{F}
and the rare prompt
\mathbf{c}_{R}
throughout sampling. This alternation aims to balance semantic alignment with the rare concept and the denoising stability of the frequent one. While effective, this approach introduces abrupt shifts between prompts, which can disrupt the continuity of the generative trajectory, especially in uncertain regions where the score functions of
\mathbf{c}_{F}
and
\mathbf{c}_{R}
diverge as visualized in
fig.

4
. To preserve coherence along the concept causal path, we reinterpret prompt alternation as a form of
second-order denoising
, which smoothly blends information from both prompts across time. This reformulation promotes more stable semantic transitions and maintains temporal consistency in the generative process.

Concretely, we start by expressing the original alternating update as when
t
is the even timestep:

\mathbf{x}_{t-2}=\underbrace{\mathbf{x}_{t}+\text{Denoise}(\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathcal{E}_{\theta}(\mathbf{x}_{t},t,\mathbf{c}_{F}))}_{\mathbf{x}_{t-1}}+\text{Denoise}(\mathbf{x}_{t-1},\mathcal{E}_{\theta}(\mathbf{x}_{t-1},t-1,\mathbf{c}_{R})),

(7)

where
\text{Denoise}(\cdot)
denotes the denoising step based on the estimated score and timestep
t
, and
\mathcal{E}_{\theta}
is the diffusion model conditioned on the prompt (
\mathbf{c}_{R}
or
\mathbf{c}_{F}
). This two-step alternating process can be viewed as a first-order scheme that applies two disjoint updates using different prompts. To improve upon this, we propose a
second-order denoising
strategy that estimates the rare score based on the frequent score. Inspired by general second-order solvers
[
22
]
, we define:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbf{x}_{t-2}^{\prime}&=\mathbf{x}_{t-1}+\text{Denoise}(\mathbf{x}_{t-1},\mathcal{E}_{\theta}(\mathbf{x}_{t-1},t-1,\mathbf{c}_{F})),\\
\mathbf{x}_{t-2}&=\mathbf{x}_{t-1}+\text{Denoise}\left(\mathbf{x}_{t-1},(1-\tfrac{1}{2\alpha})\cdot\mathcal{E}_{\theta}(\mathbf{x}_{t-1},t-1,\mathbf{c}_{F})+\tfrac{1}{2\alpha}\cdot\mathcal{E}_{\theta}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{t-2},t-2,\mathbf{c}_{R})\right),\end{split}

(8)

where
\alpha
is a weighting factor that controls the relative contribution of each prompt.

We adopt a simplified variant by setting
\alpha=0.5
, removing the contribution of the frequent prompt in the second-order denoising formulation. This mirrors the original R2F intuition, which alternates prompts to reinforce the details of the rare concept. However, unlike directly applying the rare prompt as in R2F, our method estimates a more accurate denoising direction by first leveraging the stability of the frequent prompt, aligning with the motivation in
section

3.2
. This approach retains the essence of prompt alternation while providing a more principled and stable guidance mechanism. Notably, when the selected prompt in the previous stage is already the rare concept
\mathbf{c}_{R}
, we revert to standard single-step denoising without prompt alternation.

Implementation Details.

We integrate our method into multiple diffusion backbones to comprehensively validate its generality and robustness. During inference, we adopt 25 denoising steps and apply classifier-free guidance with the default scale for each model. Hyperparameters are carefully aligned with those used in baseline methods to ensure a fair comparison. Model-specific parameters, such as the matching threshold

Matching Score Visualization

To better understand our stage-switching behavior, we visualize the average matching score dynamics in

As the denoising process progresses, we observe two consistent behaviors: (1) The matching score for each prompt tends to decrease over time, supporting our proposed criterion that once the score difference becomes sufficiently small, the rare and frequent prompts induce similar score functions, indicating it is safe to switch back to the rare concept; and (2) transient spikes in the matching score may occur when transitioning to a new prompt, indicating that the newly introduced prompt does not yet match the underlying distribution. 5
Conclusion and Future Work

We presented
RAP
, a principled framework for rare concept synthesis via a
concept causal path
, a progressive trajectory from frequent to rare prompts aligned with the model’s generative dynamics. By revisiting R2F, we addressed its heuristic prompt switching and abrupt alternation through two key advances: (1) a score-matching criterion for adaptive transitions and (2) a second-order denoising method for smooth semantic progression. Experiments across multiple diffusion models demonstrate that
RAP
improves alignment of rare concept generation and yields more coherent and faithful visual outputs. Future work includes extending our method to compositional and unseen concepts, multi-modal settings, and more general controllable generation frameworks.

Proof of Score Substitution

We provide the detailed derivation of Theorem
2
in the following. Recall that:
See
2

Proof.

Under the assumption that the log-densities are locally similar, we write:

\log p_{data}(\mathbf{x}|\mathbf{c}_{R})=\log p_{data}(\mathbf{x}|\mathbf{c}_{F})+\eta(\mathbf{x}),

for some small, smooth perturbation function
\eta(\mathbf{x})
. Differentiating both sides with respect to
\mathbf{x}
, we obtain:

\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\log p_{data}(\mathbf{x}|\mathbf{c}_{R})=\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\log p_{data}(\mathbf{x}|\mathbf{c}_{F})+\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\eta(\mathbf{x}).

Since
\epsilon(\mathbf{x})
is small and smooth, its gradient
\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\epsilon(\mathbf{x})
is also small, so:

\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\log p_{data}(\mathbf{x}|\mathbf{c}_{R})\approx\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\log p_{data}(\mathbf{x}|\mathbf{c}_{F}).

As
\mathbf{c}_{F}
lies in a high data density, it is well-learned by the model and its predicted score can be accurately predicted:

\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\log p_{data}(\mathbf{x}|\mathbf{c}_{F})\approx\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\log p_{\theta}(\mathbf{x}|\mathbf{c}_{F}).

Next, from
Theorem

1
, the score function of the surrogate mixture distribution is:

\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\log\tilde{p}_{\theta}(\mathbf{x}|\mathbf{c}_{R})=\frac{\lambda p_{\theta}(\mathbf{x}|\mathbf{c}_{R})}{\tilde{p}_{\theta}(\mathbf{x}|\mathbf{c}_{R})}\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\log p_{\theta}(\mathbf{x}|\mathbf{c}_{R})+\frac{(1-\lambda)p_{\theta}(\mathbf{x}|\mathbf{c}_{F})}{\tilde{p}_{\theta}(\mathbf{x}|\mathbf{c}_{R})}\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\log p_{\theta}(\mathbf{x}|\mathbf{c}_{F}).

Since in regions where
p_{\theta}(\mathbf{x}|\mathbf{c}_{R})\ll p_{\theta}(\mathbf{x}|\mathbf{c}_{F})
, the weighting factor on
\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\log p_{\theta}(\mathbf{x}|\mathbf{c}_{F})
dominates in the surrogate score expression. Therefore:

\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\log\tilde{p}_{\theta}(\mathbf{x}|\mathbf{c}_{R})\approx\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\log p_{\theta}(\mathbf{x}|\mathbf{c}_{F}).

This completes the proof.

In this study, you will be shown a series of image pairs, each accompanied by a text prompt. To evaluate the perceptual quality of our method, we conduct a user study involving 25 participants. All participants are anonymous to the authors, and no personal information is collected during the process. Each participant is asked to compare 48 image pairs, where each pair presents outputs from our method and a baseline model. Specifically, each participant evaluates 8 prompts per baseline model, across 6 models in total.

To ensure fairness and reduce bias, the position of the images (left or right) is randomly assigned for each comparison, and no discernible pattern is introduced. Participants are instructed to select the image they prefer based on two criteria: (1) how well the image aligns with the given text prompt (text-to-image alignment), and (2) the overall visual quality of the image.

For each comparison, users are given three options: “left”, “right”, or “same.” If “same” is selected, that instance is excluded from the win-rate calculation, as no clear preference is indicated. Appendix G
Inference Time

Our second-order denoising introduces a potential computational concern, as it requires querying the model twice per timestep in the early stages of generation. However, we emphasize that this overhead is temporary: once the rare prompt becomes active (as determined by score matching), our method reverts to standard single-step denoising using only the final rare concept. As a result, the extra computation is confined to early timesteps where prompt fusion is necessary. We report the average inference time on RareBench in
table

4
. While our method does introduce a measurable increase in runtime, the overhead remains modest—no more than
0.5\times
additional cost compared to the original model. We believe this trade-off is justified by the improvements in visual consistency and rare concept fidelity by our second-order denoising strategy.

Appendix H
Limitation and Impact

While our method improves rare concept generation, it is still constrained by the capabilities and training distribution of the underlying diffusion model. In cases where the rare concept lies far outside the model’s learned domain, even our adaptive prompt scheduling and smoothing may fail to yield plausible outputs. This can be found in the
fig.

13
. A possible extension is to incorporate step-by-step editing or intermediate supervision to gradually introduce challenging attributes, making the rare concept more feasible for the model to synthesize.

Our work improves the visual fidelity and flexibility of generative models, which can have both creative and educational applications.

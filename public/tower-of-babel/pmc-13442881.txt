Application of NMPC in the processing of Wolter-I grazing incident mirrors
Authors: Song Ding, Shuhai Yao, Liwei Sun, Jing Li, Hongyan Zhou
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractWolter-I structures based on X-ray grazing incidence optics are often used in extreme ultraviolet (EUV) collection optical systems, especially in the application of discharge plasma (DPP) sources. With the development of computer technology, the computerized surface forming (CCOS) method can significantly improve the surface accuracy and meet the optical requirements of high precision when processing Wolter-I grazing incident mirrors. In this paper, the nonlinear model predictive control (NMPC) system is innovatively introduced into the traditional CCOS method instead of the PID control system, and the application of NMPC in the rough grinding and fine grinding stages of Wolter-I grazing incident mirrors is studied, and it is found that the processing process and processing efficiency are effectively optimized through the system. Firstly, a detailed geometric model of the mirror was established for the special geometry of the inner surface of the cylindrical surface of the Wolter-I grazing incident mirror. Secondly, in view of the complex and nonlinear characteristics of the Wolter-I grazing incident mirror, a nonlinear model of the machining process is established, and the influence of spindle feed rate, removal pressure, abrasive particle size and other factors on the machining accuracy is considered, and the simulation results show that when the spindle feed rate is 25 mm/s, the removal pressure is 9 N, and the abrasive particle size is 10, the removal efficiency is the highest and the removal effect is the best. The experimental results show that the CCOS method combined with NMPC significantly improves the surface accuracy and effectively improves the processing efficiency and reduces the production cost when processing the Wolter-I grazing incident mirror.Keywords: Ultra-precision machining process, Wolter-I type grazing incident mirror, CCOS, NMPCSubject terms: Engineering, Optics and photonics

Introduction

Wolter Type I grazing-incidence mirrors serve as core optical components for X-ray observation satellites and extreme ultraviolet (EUV) collection optical systems, whose final surface figure accuracy directly determines the imaging performance and beam focusing capability of the whole system. Accordingly, their machining quality has attracted widespread attention across the industry1,2. Featuring a complex inner surface structure composed of combined paraboloid and hyperboloid profiles, the machining process of such mirrors exhibits prominent characteristics including strong nonlinearity, multi-parameter coupling and time-varying dynamics, making conventional control schemes poorly adaptable to practical scenarios3–5. At present, high-end optical manufacturing has imposed more stringent requirements on the surface figure precision, machining efficiency and unmanned production of such mirrors. Nevertheless, current machining approaches still suffer from obvious shortcomings in terms of complex curved surface compatibility, adaptive parameter optimization and high-precision stable regulation, hence novel control technologies are urgently required to break through the existing technical bottlenecks.

At present, the mainstream fabrication methods for Wolter Type I grazing-incidence mirrors consist of mandrel replication and direct machining methods. In the field of mandrel replication, Kong6 proposed a polishing approach adopting elastic spherical tools to process the surface of Wolter Type I mandrels, by which the root mean square (RMS) roughness of the finished mandrel surface can be reduced to 1.56 nm. On the basis of chemical mechanical polishing, Zuo7 put forward a precision polishing method suitable for polishing the paraboloid of designated mandrels, enabling the optimal surface roughness of mandrels to reach Ra 0.659 nm. Anthony T. H. Beauchamp et al.8 introduced a manufacturing scheme for hard X-ray space telescopes based on mandrel replication technology. The mandrels fabricated via this method feature a maximum diameter of 600 mm, with a peak-to-valley (PV) surface figure error of 26.914 nm and a surface roughness Rq of 0.28 nm.

Compared with the mandrel replication method, the direct machining method can produce Wolter Type I grazing-incidence mirrors with higher surface figure accuracy. Among relevant technologies, Computer Controlled Optical Surfacing (CCOS) stands as a representative direct machining approach. Based on CCOS technology, Cui et al.9 developed a surface figure detection device for Wolter Type I mirrors, and systematically explored its working principle, structural parameter setting and data processing algorithms, so as to fabricate Wolter Type I mirrors with improved surface figure precision. On the basis of Cui’s research achievements, Wang et al.10 carried out CCOS machining of Wolter Type I mirrors, and the machined mirrors achieved a surface figure accuracy of PV = 1.39 μm and RMS = 0.34 μm.

In terms of the application of artificial intelligence algorithms in polishing, Nie11 established a multi-scale theoretical model to predict the material removal rate of deformable polishing tools. Polishing pressure and material removal rate of different complex polishing tools were acquired by means of image processing. Fan et al.12 built a nanosecond pulsed laser polishing model targeting the surface roughness of SKD11 steel. By systematically exploring laser power, pulse width and pulse repetition frequency, they achieved the prediction of polishing efficiency for SKD11 steel. He et al.13 proposed a polishing process parameter optimization method for connecting rod journals integrating BP neural network and NSGA-II algorithm. This method improves the operational performance of crankshaft abrasive belt polishing machines, effectively optimizes the surface quality of machined connecting rod journals and shortens polishing time. Pan et al.14 optimized the BP neural network with the improved Aquila Optimizer to construct the IAO-BP model. After training with experimental parameters and abrasive grain wear height data, they established a prediction model for the average wear height of abrasive belt grains. Comparison results with ANN and GA-BP neural networks prove that this model delivers higher prediction accuracy. Aiming at robotic abrasive belt grinding, Yue et al.15 proposed a novel Inverse Input Prediction Model (IIPM), whose predicted grinding depth is highly consistent with the actual value. Given the required grinding depth, stable and reliable parameter sequences for continuous path grinding can be determined using this method. Mochamad Denny Surindra et al.16 adopted various machine learning algorithms including K-Nearest Neighbor (KNN), Support Vector Machine (SVM), Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) and Decision Tree (DT) to conduct intelligent monitoring of abrasive belt grinding conditions. They further adopted Decision Tree, Random Forest and XG Boost to screen machine learning models with optimized hyperparameters for abrasive belt condition monitoring during robotic grinding. Mulpur Sarat Babu et al.17 presented a machine learning-based tool wear prediction model. With features extracted from real-time multi-sensor data, multiple models including Random Forest (RF), K-Nearest Neighbor (KNN) and Extreme Gradient Boosting (XGB) were established for tool wear prediction. Xie et al.16 proposed a tool path planning method based on the improved Prim algorithm. The optimized paths can reduce path length, boost polishing efficiency and effectively suppress mid-frequency errors of workpieces.

The core of Computer Controlled Optical Surfacing (CCOS) technology lies in achieving deterministic material removal by precisely controlling the dwell time of polishing tools at each position on the workpiece surface, so as to correct optical surfaces to ultra-precision precision. As the most classic feedback regulator in industrial control systems, PID controllers are primarily applied to undertake executive-layer functions in CCOS systems. Essentially, PID is a linear controller. However, the correlation between material removal rate and factors such as pressure, velocity and temperature during CCOS machining shows strong nonlinear characteristics, and the feedback of actual material removal effect suffers from obvious time lag. Hence, pure PID control is incapable of effectively governing large time-delay systems. Wang et al.18 demonstrated that when machine tool dynamic constraints are taken into account, the theoretically ideal zero dwell time cannot be achieved in physical practice. This indicates that even if the underlying PID servo control performs well, constraints at the algorithm level will still induce systematic errors. In addition, PID controllers are unable to systematically incorporate various constraints during design. By contrast, the Nonlinear Model Predictive Control (NMPC) framework directly integrates state constraints and input constraints into the mathematical formulation of optimization problems.

With superior capabilities in rolling optimization, dynamic prediction and multi-constraint processing, Model Predictive Control (MPC) has become a mainstream advanced control method to replace traditional PID control and address the control bottlenecks of complex industrial processes. In recent years, it has made groundbreaking progress in fields such as multi-phase batch processes, fault tolerance control and iterative learning control. To solve the problems of asynchronous switching, short time delay and actuator faults in multi-phase switched batch processes, Shi et al.19 proposed a Lyapunov-Razumikhin-based Robust Adaptive Switched Predictive Fault-Tolerant Control (RASPFTC) method. They established matched and mismatched extended switching models, and calculated controller gains as well as minimum and maximum dwell time online. Compared with fixed-gain iterative learning control, this method can adjust control gains in real time to eliminate state deviation, with lower computational load and stronger robustness. Aiming at the high-precision tracking demand under partial actuator failures in batch processes, Hu et al.20 presented a two-dimensional infinite horizon optimal iterative learning MPC strategy (2D-IHLQILC-NPIDILC). By combining incremental PID and iterative learning mechanisms, a two-dimensional extended state-space model was constructed. The set-point learning strategy was adopted to improve inter-batch iterative performance, which realized smaller tracking error and faster convergence in injection molding speed control.The above advanced MPC methods share consistent advantages with this study. All of them break the core limitations of traditional linear time-invariant PID control, and adopt the rolling optimization and dynamic prediction mechanisms of MPC to adapt to the nonlinearity, strong coupling and time-varying dynamic characteristics of complex systems, thus realizing simultaneous improvement of control accuracy and response performance at the control framework level.Existing literature mainly concentrates on batch manufacturing processes including chemical production and injection molding, focusing on solving actuator faults, time delay, asynchronous switching and inter-batch iterative learning for standardized mass production. In contrast, this study targets the high-end customized manufacturing scenario of ultra-precision optical machining of Wolter Type I grazing-incidence mirrors. In view of the machining nonlinearity and multi-parameter coupling characteristics of the complex inner paraboloid-hyperboloid surfaces of such mirrors, this paper innovatively integrates Nonlinear Model Predictive Control (NMPC) into CCOS ultra-precision machining for the first time. Core machining parameters such as spindle feed speed, removal pressure and abrasive grain size are autonomously optimized to achieve full-process rolling parameter optimization for both rough and finish polishing. Finally, nanoscale surface figure accuracy is realized, filling the technical gap in unmanned ultra-precision machining of complex grazing-incidence mirrors.

Most existing studies mainly target optical components with regular geometric structures, yet the intricate geometric features of Wolter Type I grazing-incidence mirrors make the aforesaid methods inapplicable.With the rapid advancement of artificial intelligence in recent years, unmanned polishing technology has been primarily adopted in robotic polishing scenarios. This is owing to the relatively moderate surface roughness requirements of workpieces in robotic polishing, which fits well with mass production. Nevertheless, Wolter Type I grazing-incidence mirrors impose extremely stringent requirements on surface figure accuracy and are not suitable for mass manufacturing, hence there is still a vacancy in the research on their unmanned polishing technology.On the basis of the mature CCOS machining technology for Wolter Type I mirrors, this paper investigates the integration of artificial intelligence into the mirror machining process. An NMPC control system is proposed to replace conventional PID control. The independently developed NMPC system is deployed in both rough and finish polishing procedures of Wolter Type I mirrors, enabling rolling optimization of machining parameters and autonomous selection of the optimal parameter combination. Experimental results confirm that the NMPC system can efficiently determine reasonable machining parameters and accomplish the full rough and finish polishing processes. The finished Wolter Type I mirrors attain the preset surface figure accuracy and satisfy practical machining specifications.

As shown in Fig. 7, the material removal thickness shows an approximately linear decreasing trend as the particle size of the abrasive decreases. This phenomenon shows that the abrasive particle size has a significant effect on the material removal rate during the removal process. Specifically, the larger particle size abrasives can achieve a larger material removal per unit time due to their higher removal capacity per unit volume under the same process parameters (e.g., removal pressure, speed, etc.), while when the particle size decreases, the removal capacity of a single abrasive decreases, resulting in a significant reduction in the overall removal.

This rule is consistent with the material removal mechanism, which further verifies the rationality of the model, and indicates that the selection of abrasive particle size should be fully considered when formulating the removal parameters, so as to achieve accurate control of the material removal rate, so as to meet the processing requirements of different precision requirements. Conclusion

In order to solve the problem that the processing parameters in the coarse grinding and fine grinding stages of Wolter-I mirror manufacturing are difficult to control, a method of coarse grinding and fine grinding of Wolter-I type mirror by NMPC control algorithm was proposed. In this method, the geometric model of the Wolter-I grazing incident mirror was established, the influence of each processing parameter on the material removal amount was analyzed, and the influence of single processing parameters and comprehensive processing parameters on the material removal amount was simulated and analyzed. Finally, the coarse grinding and fine grinding experiments of glass-ceramic were carried out by this method, and the following conclusions were obtained: compared with the Wolter-I mirror processed under the traditional PID control system, the Wolter-I mirror processed under the NMPC control system proposed in this paper can achieve better surface accuracy, greatly improve the processing efficiency, and reduce the dependence on workers’ technology. It provides technical support and theoretical support for the unmanned polishing of Wolter-I mirrors in the future.

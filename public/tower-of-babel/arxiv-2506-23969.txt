Full history recursive multilevel Picard approximations suffer from the curse of dimensionality for the Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman equation of a stochastic control problem
Martin Hutzenthaler, Tuan Anh Nguyen

Abstract
Full history recursive multilevel Picard (MLP) approximations have been proved to overcome the curse of dimensionality in the numerical approximation of semilinear heat equations with nonlinearities which are globally Lipschitz continuous with respect to the maximum-norm. Nonlinearities in Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman equations in stochastic control theory, however, are often (locally) Lipschitz continuous with respect to the standard Euclidean norm. In this paper we prove the surprising fact that MLP approximations for one such example equation suffer from the curse of dimensionality.

The main contribution of this paper is to show via a counterexample

the approximation error might grow faster in the dimension than any polynomial.
This is surprising for several reasons. First, the target function in our

approximation problem for this target function does not suffer from the

3
Lower bounds for MLP approximations for our counterexample

A difficulty in the derivation of lower bounds for MLP approximations
are the sums on the right-hand side of the MLP recursion (
8
).
Using symmetry we show that the summand on the right-hand side of (
8
)
are centered in our case.
Thus second moments are equal to variances.
The following lemma then estimates the variance of the sum over independent random fields
evaluated at the same random and independent vector from below by the sum of the variances.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},{\mathbb{P}})
be a probability space,
let

m,n\in{\mathbbm{N}}
,
let

F_{1},F_{2},\ldots,F_{n}\colon{\mathbbm{R}}^{m}\times\Omega\to{\mathbbm{R}}
, be
i.i.d. random fields, let

X\colon\Omega\to{\mathbbm{R}}^{m}
be a
random variable,
assume that
\{F_{1},F_{2},\ldots,F_{n}\}
and
X
are independent,
and
assume
that

{\mathbb{E}}[\lvert F_{1}(X)\rvert^{2}]<\infty
.
Then

\mathrm{Var}\bigl[\sum_{i=1}^{n}F_{i}(X)\bigr]\geq n\mathrm{Var}\!\left(F_{1}(X)\right).

Proof of
Lemma

3.1
.

Throughout the proof let
A\subseteq{\mathbbm{R}}^{m}
satisfy that

A=\{x\in{\mathbbm{R}}^{m}\colon{\mathbb{E}}[\lvert F_{1}(x)\rvert^{2}]<\infty\}
.
Then the disintegration theorem (see, e.g.,
[
12
, Lemma 2.2]
)
and the fact that

{\mathbb{E}}[\lvert F_{1}(X)\rvert^{2}]<\infty

show that

\infty\cdot{\mathbb{P}}(X\notin A)\leq{\mathbb{E}}\Bigl[{\mathbb{E}}[\lvert F_{1}(x)\rvert^{2}]\bigr|_{x=X}\mathbbm{1}_{X\notin A}\Bigr]\leq{\mathbb{E}}\Bigl[{\mathbb{E}}[\lvert F_{1}(x)\rvert^{2}]\bigr|_{x=X}\Bigr]={\mathbb{E}}\bigl[\lvert F_{1}(X)\rvert^{2}\bigr]<\infty.

Hence,
{\mathbb{P}}(X\notin A)=0
and
{\mathbb{P}}(X\in A)=1
.
This, the disintegration theorem (see, e.g.,
[
12
, Lemma 2.2]
), the assumptions, and Jensen’s inequality show that for all

i,j\in\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
with
i\neq j
it holds that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\mathrm{Var}[F_{i}(X)]={\mathbb{E}}\!\left[\mathbbm{1}_{X\in A}\lvert F_{i}(X)\rvert^{2}\right]-\left\lvert{\mathbb{E}}[\mathbbm{1}_{X\in A}F_{i}(X)]\right\rvert^{2}\\
&={\mathbb{E}}\!\left[\mathbbm{1}_{X\in A}{\mathbb{E}}\!\left[\lvert F_{i}(x)\rvert^{2}\right]\Bigr|_{x=X}\right]-\left\lvert{\mathbb{E}}\!\left[\mathbbm{1}_{X\in A}{\mathbb{E}}\!\left[F_{i}(x)\right]\Bigr|_{x=X}\right]\right\rvert^{2}=\mathrm{Var}[F_{1}(X)]\\
\end{split}

(40)

and

\displaystyle\begin{split}&{\mathbb{E}}\!\left[F_{i}(X)F_{j}(X)\right]={\mathbb{E}}\!\left[\mathbbm{1}_{X\in A}{\mathbb{E}}\!\left[F_{i}(x)F_{j}(x)\right]\Bigr|_{x=X}\right]={\mathbb{E}}\!\left[\mathbbm{1}_{X\in A}{\mathbb{E}}\!\left[F_{i}(x)\right]{\mathbb{E}}\!\left[F_{j}(x)\right]\Bigr|_{x=X}\right]\\
&={\mathbb{E}}\!\left[\mathbbm{1}_{X\in A}\Bigl\lvert{\mathbb{E}}\!\left[F_{1}(x)\right]\Bigr\rvert^{2}\Bigr|_{x=X}\right]\geq\left\lvert{\mathbb{E}}\!\left[\mathbbm{1}_{X\in A}{\mathbb{E}}\!\left[F_{1}(x)\right]\Bigr|_{x=X}\right]\right\rvert^{2}\\
&={\mathbb{E}}\!\left[\mathbbm{1}_{X\in A}{\mathbb{E}}\!\left[F_{i}(x)\right]\Bigr|_{x=X}\right]{\mathbb{E}}\!\left[\mathbbm{1}_{X\in A}{\mathbb{E}}\!\left[F_{j}(x)\right]\Bigr|_{x=X}\right]={\mathbb{E}}\!\left[F_{i}(X)\right]{\mathbb{E}}\!\left[F_{j}(X)\right].\end{split}

(41)

Therefore, it holds that

\displaystyle\small\begin{split}\mathrm{Var}\!\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}F_{i}(X)\right)&=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathrm{Var}\!\left(F_{i}(X)\right)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n}\Bigl({\mathbb{E}}\!\left[F_{i}(X)F_{j}(X)\right]-{\mathbb{E}}\!\left[F_{i}(X)\right]{\mathbb{E}}\!\left[F_{j}(X)\right]\Bigr)\geq n\mathrm{Var}\!\left(F_{1}(X)\right).\end{split}

(42)

The proof of
Lemma

3.1
is thus completed.
∎

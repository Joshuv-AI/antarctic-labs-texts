Operator Spreading in Random Unitary Circuits with Unitary-invariant Gate Distributions
Zhiyang Tan (谭志阳), Piet W. Brouwer

Abstract
Random unitary circuits have become a model system to investigate information scrambling in quantum systems. In the literature, mostly random circuits with Haar-distributed gate operations have been considered. In this work, we investigate operator spreading in random unitary circuits in which the elementary gate operations are drawn from general unitary-invariant ensembles, which include the well-studied Haar-distributed random unitary circuits as a special case. Similar to the Haar-distributed case, the long-time behavior of operator spreading with the more general unitary-invariant gate distribution is governed by drift-diffusion equations characterized by the butterfly velocity v B v_{\rm B} and a diffusion constant 𝒟 \mathcal{D} . Differences with the Haar-random case are (i) that it takes a finite time τ b \tau_{\rm b} until ensemble-averaged Pauli-string weights take a “binary” form, in which they depend only on whether Pauli operators inside the support of the Pauli strong are equal to the identity matrix, and (ii) that the operator spreading is characterized by a finite “domain-wall width” n DW n_{\rm DW} separating regions with a random-matrix-like Pauli-string distribution. To illustrate these findings, we perform explicit calculations for random unitary circuits distributed according to the Poisson kernel, which interpolates between the trivial and Haar-distributed circuits.

Introduction

In recent years, there has been a growing interest in the study of random unitary circuits
[
1
,

,

,
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
]
. Random unitary circuits provide a minimal model in which information spreading, quantum chaos, and thermalization in quantum-mechanical systems can be studied. A typical random circuit consists of a sequence of gate operations acting on pairs of
q
-dimensional quantum degrees of freedom (“qudits”), whereby the gate operations are drawn from a statistical distribution
[
2
,
4
,
5
]
. The gate operations follow a “brickwork” structure, in which the pattern of gates that are acted upon by the gate operation alternates in space and time, see Fig.
1
.

Sketch of a random unitary circuit. It has a brickwork structure and is composed of gates uncorrelated both in space
and time.

Quantum information scrambling
[
10
,
11
]
refers to the phenomenon that a local operator
O
, as it evolves in time, acquires non-local correlations
[
12
]
. These non-local correlations can be described by expressing the Heisenberg-picture operator
O(t)
(with
O(0)=O
) in terms of a set of Pauli-string basis operators
O_{p}
, which are products of generalized Pauli operators
\sigma_{p_{x}}
associated with each qudit, and the corresponding amplitudes
\gamma_{p}(t)
,

O(t)=\sum_{p}\gamma_{p}(t)O_{p}.

(1)

Quantum information scrambling then arises from two primary effects: operator spreading, which is the increase of the support of the Pauli strings
p
appearing in the sum (
1
) with time
t
, and entanglement spreading, which is the increase of the number of terms in the sum (
1
) with time
[
12
,
13
]
. (The Pauli string index
p
is a composite index
p=[\ldots,p_{x-1},p_{x},p_{x+1},\ldots]
. Its support is the set of qudit positions
x
for which
\sigma_{p_{x}}
is different from the identity operator.)

The random unitary circuit with maximal randomness (given the brickwork structure) has its two-qudit gate operations
{\cal U}
drawn from a Haar distribution
[
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
]
. The Haar distribution is uniform across the unitary group,

P_{\rm Haar}({\cal U})=\mbox{const}.

(2)

For such a Haar-distributed random unitary circuit, Nahum
et al.

[
4
]
characterized operator spreading by the ensemble average
\rho_{p}(t)\equiv\langle|\gamma_{p}(t)|^{2}\rangle
, for which they found that the endpoints
x_{p}
of the support of typical Pauli strings
p
contributing to
\rho_{p}(t)
satisfied a drift-diffusion equation with drift velocity

v_{\rm B}=\frac{q^{2}-1}{q^{2}+1}

(3)

and diffusion constant

{\cal D}=\frac{4q^{2}}{(q^{2}+1)^{2}}.

(4)

The drift velocity is commonly referred to as the
butterfly velocity
. It approaches the light-cone limit
v_{\rm B}=1
in the limit of large qudit dimension
q
.

In this article, we consider operator spreading in a class of random unitary circuits with non-Haar distributed two-qudit gates, while still requiring that the probability distribution for a two-qudit gate operation
{\cal U}
is invariant under unitary transformations,
i.e.
,

P({\cal U})=P({\cal V}{\cal U}{\cal V}^{\dagger})

(5)

for arbitrary unitary matrices
{\cal V}
.
A special case of such a unitary-invariant distribution is the Poisson kernel
[
14
,
15
,
16
,
17
]

P_{\rm Poisson}({\mathcal{U}};\alpha)\propto|\det(\mathds{1}-\alpha{\mathcal{U}})|^{-2q^{2}},

(6)

which is the maximum-information-entropy distribution with the constraint that the ensemble average

\langle{\cal U}\rangle=\alpha\mathds{1}.

(7)

The Poisson kernel distribution allows a continuous interpolation between the limit
|\alpha|=1
of a circuit with no information spreading and the maximally random Haar limit
\alpha=0
.

Like for Haar-distributed random unitary circuits
[
4
]
, operator spreading in random unitary circuits with the invariance property (
5
) can be mapped to a classical stochastic growth problem, which in the long-time limit approaches a drift-diffusion process with butterfly velocity
v_{\rm B}
and a diffusion constant
{\cal D}
. In addition to
v_{\rm B}
and
{\cal D}
being different from those for the Haar limit, the unitary-invariant random circuit differs from the maximally random Haar-distributed random circuit in two more ways: (i) For the Haar-random circuit, the ensemble-averaged Pauli string weights
\rho_{p}(t)
depend only on whether or not each generalized Pauli operator
\sigma_{p_{x}}
equals the identity operator. For the unitary-invariant circuit studied here, such a “binary” form of the Pauli string weights
\rho_{p}(t)
sets in only after a time
\tau_{\rm b}
. (ii) For the Haar-random circuit, Pauli operators inside the support of a Pauli string have a random-matrix distribution, in which all Pauli operators have the same probability. For the unitary-invariant circuits, this random-matrix limit sets in only at a finite “domain wall width”
n_{\rm DW}
from the end of the Pauli string. For a random circuit with Poisson-kernel-distributed two-qubit gate operators, we find that both
\tau_{\rm b}
and
n_{\rm DW}
are finite, although
\tau_{\rm b}
diverges in the limit
|\alpha|\to 1
of a trivial circuit.

The remainder of this article is organized as follows: In Sec.
II
we briefly review the formulation of random unitary circuits with the brickwork structure of Fig.
1
, closely following Ref.
[
4
]
. We map to a stochastic growth model and obtain the butterfly velocity
v_{\rm B}
and diffusion constant
{\cal D}
for random circuits with unitary-invariant two-qudit-gate distributions in Sec.
III
. Results for the Poisson-kernel distribution are presented in Sec.
IV
. In Sec.
V
, we show that the Pauli-string weights
\rho_{p}
acquire a binary form after a finite time
\tau_{\rm b}
and calculate the out-of-time-ordrered correlator (OTOC) for the type of random circuits considered here. We conclude in Sec.
VI
. The appendices contain additional details of our calculations.

II
Random unitary circuit

II.1
Pauli string coefficients

We consider a one-dimensional array of
q
-state qudits of length
L
and periodic boundary conditions labeled by a site index
x
, shown schematically in Fig.
1
. A complete operator basis for the qudit at site
x
is formed by generalized Pauli matrices
\sigma_{p_{x}}
, where
p_{x}=0,\ldots,q^{2}-1
and
\sigma_{0}
is the identity operator. A basis for operators on the full one-dimensional array is given by so-called “Pauli strings”
O_{p}
, tensor products of the generalized Pauli operators associated with each qudit. A Pauli string is labeled by the composite index
p=[p_{0},p_{1},p_{2},\ldots,p_{L-1}]
, which contains one entry
p_{x}
for each qudit. The Pauli-string basis operators satisfy the orthonormality property
[
18
]

\frac{1}{q^{L}}\mbox{tr}\,O_{p}O_{q}^{\dagger}=\delta_{p,q}.

(8)

For more details on generalized Pauli operators, we refer to App.
A
.

II.2
Time evolution

Time evolution takes place via the unitary evolution operator
U(t,t^{\prime})
,

O(t)=U^{\dagger}(t,t^{\prime})O(t^{\prime})U(t,t^{\prime}).

(9)

Following Refs.
[
4
,
19
]
, we consider a stochastic stroboscopic evolution, which is a product of two-qudit gate operators,

U(t,t-1)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\otimes_{\textrm{$x$ even}}\,{\cal U}_{x,x+1}&\mbox{for $t$ even},\\
\otimes_{\textrm{$x$ even}}\,{\cal U}_{x-1,x}&\mbox{for $t$ odd},\end{array}\right.

(10)

where
{\cal U}_{x,y}(t,t-1)
is a
q^{2}\times q^{2}
matrix that acts on the qudit degrees of freedom of sites
x
and
y
only. This structure is shown schematically in Fig.
1
.

In Refs.
[
4
,
19
]
, for each time step, the matrices
{\cal U}_{x,x+1}(t,t-1)
or
{\cal U}_{x-1,x}(t,t-1)
are taken independently from the group of
q^{2}\times q^{2}
unitary matrices according to the uniform distribution
P_{\rm Haar}
, see Eq. (
2
). Here, we still take the matrices
{\cal U}_{x,x+1}(t,t-1)
or
{\cal U}_{x-1,x}(t,t-1)
from independent distributions, but for the form of the distribution we only assume invariance with respect to unitary basis transformations, see Eq. (
5
).

II.3
Time evolution of Pauli-string coefficients

The time evolution of the unitary circuit starts from a local operator
O
at time
t=0
, such that
O
equals the identity operator
\sigma_{0}
for sites sufficiently far away from a reference site
x_{0}
. We require that
O
satisfies the normalization condition

\frac{1}{q^{L}}\mbox{tr}\,OO^{\dagger}=1.

(11)

A Pauli string operator that contains
\sigma_{p_{x_{0}}}
at qudit
x_{0}
and
\sigma_{0}
at all other qudits is an example of an operator that meets this normalization condition. The Heisenberg-picture operator
O(t)
can be expanded in the basis of Pauli strings as in Eq. (
1
),
where

\gamma_{p}(t)=\frac{1}{q^{L}}\mbox{tr}\,O(t)O_{p}^{\dagger}.

(12)

The time evolution of the Pauli-string coefficients
\gamma_{p}
follows from that of the operator
O
, see Eq. (
9
). Because
O=O(0)
is normalized and the unitary time evolution (
9
) preserves the normalization,
\gamma_{p}(t)
satisfies the normalization condition

\sum_{p}|\gamma_{p}(t)|^{2}=1

(13)

for all
t
.

For a single-time step, we have

\displaystyle\gamma_{p}(t)=

\displaystyle\,\sum_{a}W_{ap}(t,t-1)\gamma_{a}(t-1),

(14)

where the summation variable
a
labels Pauli strings and

\displaystyle W_{ap}(t,t-1)=\frac{1}{q^{L}}\mbox{tr}\,U^{\dagger}(t,t-1)O_{a}U(t,t-1)O_{p}^{\dagger}.

For an evolution matrix of the form (
10
), this evaluates to

W_{ap}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\prod_{\textrm{$x$ even}}{\cal W}_{ap;x,x+1}&\mbox{$t$ even},\\
\prod_{\textrm{$x$ even}}{\cal W}_{ap;x-1,x}&\mbox{$t$ odd},\end{array}\right.

(15)

where the factors
{\cal W}_{ap;x,y}
do not depend on the full Pauli strings
a
and
p
, but only on the labels
a_{x}
,
a_{y}
,
p_{x}
, and
p_{y}
that belong to the sites
x
and
y
,

\displaystyle{\cal W}_{ap;x,y}=

\displaystyle\,\frac{1}{q^{2}}\mbox{tr}\,{\cal U}^{\dagger}_{x,y}(\sigma_{a_{x}}\otimes\sigma_{a_{y}}){\cal U}_{x,y}(\sigma_{p_{x}}^{\dagger}\otimes\sigma_{p_{y}}^{\dagger}).

(16)

The coefficients
{\cal W}_{ap;x,y}
satisfy the unitarity relations

\displaystyle\sum_{p_{x},p_{y}}{\cal W}_{ap;x,y}{\cal W}^{*}_{bp;x,y}=

\displaystyle\,\delta_{a_{x},b_{x}}\delta_{a_{y},b_{y}},

\displaystyle\sum_{a_{x},a_{y}}{\cal W}_{ap;x,y}{\cal W}^{*}_{aq;x,y}=

\displaystyle\,\delta_{p_{x},q_{y}}\delta_{q_{x},q_{y}},

(17)

which ensures the normalization of the Pauli string coefficients, see Eq. (
13
).
They also have the property that

{\cal W}_{ap;x,y}=\delta_{a_{x},0}\delta_{a_{y},0}\ \ \mbox{if $p_{x}=p_{y}=0$}

(18)

and, similarly,

{\cal W}_{ap;x,y}=\delta_{p_{x},0}\delta_{p_{y},0}\ \ \mbox{if $a_{x}=a_{y}=0$},

(19)

which guarantees that a trivial stretch of a Pauli string remains trivial under time evolution and vice versa.

III
Mapping to stochastic growth model

III.1
Stochastic growth model

The Pauli-string coefficient
\gamma_{p}(t)
contains the full evolution information of the operator
O
under unitary evolution. Following Ref.
[
4
]
, we here focus on the average of the modulus square

\rho_{p}(t)=\langle|\gamma_{p}(t)|^{2}\rangle,

(20)

which may be interpreted as a (classical) probability distribution on the set of Pauli strings
p
. With the help of Eq. (
14
),
\rho_{p}(t)
may be expressed in terms of the correlation function
\langle\gamma_{a}(t-1)\gamma_{b}(t-1)^{*}\rangle
and the covariance
\langle W_{ap}W_{bp}^{*}\rangle
,

\rho_{p}(t)=\sum_{a,b}\langle W_{ap}W^{*}_{bp}\rangle\langle\gamma_{a}(t-1)\gamma^{*}_{b}(t-1)\rangle.

(21)

In App.
B
we show that

\langle W_{ap}W_{bp}^{*}\rangle=0\ \mbox{if $a\neq b$}

(22)

if the two-qudit operators
{\cal U}_{x,y}
are drawn from a probability distribution with the invariance property (
5
). Hence, Eq. (
21
) simplifies to a closed Markovian evolution equation for the averaged Pauli-string weights
\rho_{p}(t)
,

\displaystyle\rho_{p}(t)=

\displaystyle\,\sum_{a}\ \rho_{a}(t-1)\langle|W_{ap}|^{2}\rangle.

(23)

Equation (
23
) describes the time evolution of
\rho_{p}(t)
as a Markovian stochastic process with transition probabilities
\langle|W_{ap}|^{2}\rangle
.

The transition probabilities
\langle|W_{ap}|^{2}\rangle
in this stochastic growth model factorize in pair contributions from the two-qudit gates,

\displaystyle\langle|W_{ap}|^{2}\rangle=

\displaystyle\,\prod_{x\,{\rm even}}\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\langle|{\cal W}_{ap;x,x+1}|^{2}\rangle&\mbox{for $t$ even},\\
\langle|{\cal W}_{ap;x-1,x}|^{2}\rangle&\mbox{for $t$ odd}.\end{array}\right.

Because of the unitary invariance (
5
) of the distribution
P({\cal U}_{x,y})
of the two-qudit gate operators, the pair transition probabilities depend on low-order moments of the distribution only. The relevant moments are

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\cal R}_{1;1}=&\,\langle\mbox{tr}\,{\cal U}_{x,x+1}\,\mbox{tr}\,{\cal U}_{x,x+1}^{\dagger}\rangle,\\
{\cal R}_{2;2}=&\,\langle\mbox{tr}\,{\cal U}_{x,x+1}^{2}\,\mbox{tr}\,{\cal U}_{x,x+1}^{\dagger 2}\rangle,\\
{\cal R}_{1,1;1,1}=&\,\langle(\mbox{tr}\,{\cal U}_{x,x+1})^{2}\,(\mbox{tr}\,{\cal U}_{x,x_{1}}^{\dagger})^{2}\rangle,\\
{\cal R}_{1,1;2}=&\,\langle(\mbox{tr}\,{\cal U}_{x,x+1})^{2})\,\mbox{tr}\,{\cal U}_{x,x+1}^{\dagger 2}\rangle.\end{split}

(26)

The moments
{\cal R}_{1,1}
,
{\cal R}_{2,2}
, and
{\cal R}_{1,1,1,1}
are real;
{\cal R}_{1,1;2}
is complex. In App.
B
we show that the pair transition probability
\langle|{\cal W}_{ap;x,y}|^{2}\rangle
is

\displaystyle\langle|{\cal W}_{ap;x,y}|^{2}\rangle=

\displaystyle\,\delta_{a,0}\delta_{p,0}+\frac{(1-\delta_{a,0})(1-\delta_{p,0})}{q^{4}-1}

(27)

\displaystyle\,~~\mbox{}\times\left(1-\frac{\mbox{Re}\,A_{1}+A_{2}+A_{3}}{q^{4}-1}\right.

\displaystyle\,\left.\vphantom{\frac{1}{2}}\mbox{}+\mbox{Re}\,A_{1}\varphi(a,p)+A_{2}\delta_{a,-p}+A_{3}\delta_{a,p}\right),

where
A_{1}
is a complex coefficient and
A_{2}
and
A_{3}
are real coefficients that are linearly independent combinations of the moment functions (for details, we refer to APP.
B
).

The function
\varphi(a_{x},a_{y},p_{x},p_{y})
depends on the choice of the basis of generalized Pauli operators. An explicit expression is given in App.
A
for the choice of the generalized Pauli basis made there.
The limit of Haar-random two-qudit operators
{\cal U}_{x,y}
corresponds to
A_{1}=A_{2}=A_{3}=0
, whereas
A_{1}=A_{2}=0
and
A_{3}=q^{4}-1
for the trivial limit
{\cal U}_{x,y}=\mathds{1}
.
In Subsec.
III.3
it will be shown that only
\mathrm{Re}\,A_{1}
and
A_{2}+A_{3}
govern operator spreading at sufficiently large times and, hence, enter into the calculation of the butterfly velocity
v_{\rm B}
and the diffusion constant
{\cal D}
. In Fig.
2
we show the allowed parameter space for these two parameters of the stochastic growth model.

Figure 2:
Parameter space showing the allowed values of
A_{2}+A_{3}
and
\mathrm{Re}A_{1}
for
q=2
. The parameter space is the convex enclosure of all points in the
A_{2}+A_{3},\mathrm{Re}\,A_{1}
-plane corresponding to moments of unitary matrices

, with
{\cal V}

{\cal U}_{0}
fixed. The parameter space in the figure is obtained by numerically sampling
{\cal U}_{0}
uniformly.

III.2
Butterfly velocity

Using the properties (
17
)–(
19
), one verifies that the evolution equation (
23
) admits the maximally random steady-state solution

\rho^{\infty}_{p}(t)=q^{-2L}

(28)

as well as the trivial solution

\rho^{0}_{p}(t)=\prod_{x}\delta_{p_{x},0}.

(29)

Except in the case of a trivial circuit, these are the only two stationary solutions of the Markovian evolution process.
The butterfly velocity
v_{\rm B}
corresponds to the speed at which a domain wall between these two solutions propagates through the qudit array. For definiteness, we here will consider a domain wall separating a maximally random region to the left (smaller
x
) from a trivial region to the right (larger
x
). Such a domain wall propagates to the right (in the positive
x
direction).

III.3
Projected binary strings

Since the stochastic growth process defined by Eqs. (
23
)–(
27
) refers to the basis of qudit operators — the generalized Pauli matrices
\sigma_{p}
—, it has
q^{2}
degrees of freedom per qudit.We may simplify the growth process by considering a projection of the classical probabilities
\rho_{p}(t)
onto a “binary” probability string
\bar{\rho}_{\bar{p}}
, where the string index
\bar{p}
is a list of binaries
[\bar{p}_{0},\bar{p}_{1},\ldots,\bar{p}_{L-1}]
with
\bar{p}_{x}\in\{\mathbb{I}\,,\mathbb{X}\}
, where
\mathbb{I}\,
,
\mathbb{X}
represent a generalized Pauli matrix
\sigma_{p_{x}}
with
p_{x}=0
,
p_{x}\neq 0
, respectively. Writing
p\to\bar{p}
to denote the binary string
\bar{p}
corresponding to the Pauli string
p
, we define the binary-string distribution
\bar{\rho}_{\bar{p}}
corresponding to
\rho_{p}
as

\bar{\rho}_{\bar{p}}=\sum_{p\to\bar{p}}\rho_{p}.

(30)

(Note that the projection onto a binary string
\bar{\rho}_{\bar{p}}
does not require that the underlying full Pauli-string weight
\rho_{p}
has a binary form, in which it depends only on whether each generalized Pauli index
p_{x}=0
or
p_{x}\neq 0
. Nevertheless, in Sec.
V
we show that
\rho_{p}(t)
relaxes to such a binary form after a finite time
\tau_{\rm b}
.)
The projected binary-string distribution corresponding to the maximally random steady-state solution (
28
) is

\bar{\rho}^{\infty}_{\bar{p}}(t)=\prod_{x}\bar{r}^{\infty}_{\bar{p}_{x}},

(31)

with

\bar{r}^{\infty}_{\bar{p}_{x}}=\frac{1}{q^{2}}\left(\delta_{\bar{p}_{x},\mathbb{I}\,}+(q^{2}-1)\delta_{\bar{p}_{x},\mathbb{X}}\right),

(32)

whereas the projected binary-string distribution corresponding to the trivial solution (
29
) is

\bar{\rho}^{0}_{\bar{p}}(t)=\prod_{x}\delta_{\bar{p}_{x},\mathbb{I}\,}.

(33)

Schematic showing the two-qudit gate processes corresponding to the rates
T_{1}
,
T_{11}
,
T_{\rm x}
, and
T_{\pm}
defined in the main text.

Using the properties of the function
\varphi(a,p)
, see App.
A
, one finds that the Markovian evolution equation (
23
) for the classical distribution
\rho_{p}
gives a closed evolution equation for the projected binary distribution
\bar{\rho}_{\bar{p}}
,

\bar{\rho}_{\bar{p}}(t)=\sum_{\bar{a}}\bar{\rho}_{\bar{a}}(t-1)T_{\bar{a}\bar{p}},

(34)

with transition probabilities
T_{\bar{a}\bar{p}}
that are products of pair-transition probabilities,

T_{\bar{a}\bar{p}}=\prod_{x\,{\rm even}}\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}T_{\bar{a}_{x}\bar{a}_{x+1};\bar{p}_{x},\bar{p}_{x+1}}&\mbox{for $t$ even},\\
T_{\bar{a}_{x-1}\bar{a}_{x};\bar{p}_{x-1}\bar{p}_{x}}&\mbox{for $t$ odd}.\end{array}\right.

(35)

The nonzero pair transition rates
T_{\bar{a}_{x},\bar{a}_{y};\bar{p}_{x},\bar{p}_{y}}
for binary strings are
T_{\mathbb{I}\,\mathbb{I}\,;\mathbb{I}\,\mathbb{I}\,}=1
,
T_{\mathbb{I}\,\mathbb{X};\mathbb{I}\,\mathbb{X}}=T_{\mathbb{X}\mathbb{I}\,;\mathbb{X}\mathbb{I}\,}=T_{1}
,
T_{\mathbb{I}\,\mathbb{X};\mathbb{X}\mathbb{I}\,}=T_{\mathbb{X}\mathbb{I}\,;\mathbb{I}\,\mathbb{X}}=T_{\rm x}
,
T_{\mathbb{X}\mathbb{X};\mathbb{I}\,\mathbb{X}}=T_{\mathbb{X}\mathbb{X};\mathbb{X}\mathbb{I}\,}=T_{-}
,
T_{\mathbb{I}\,\mathbb{X};\mathbb{X}\mathbb{X}}=T_{\mathbb{X}\mathbb{I}\,;\mathbb{X}\mathbb{X}}=T_{+}
, and
T_{\mathbb{X}\mathbb{X};\mathbb{X}\mathbb{X}}=T_{11}
. Here
T_{1}
and
T_{11}
describe processes at the two-qudit gate for which the (binary) qudit states are preserved,
T_{\rm x}
describes a swap between zero and nonzero qudit states, and
T_{\pm}
describe processes in which a zero qudit is turned into a nonzero one or vice versa, see Fig.
3
. From Eq. (
27
) we obtain

\displaystyle T_{1}=

\displaystyle\,\frac{1}{q^{2}+1}\left(1+\frac{q^{2}(\mbox{Re}\,A_{1}+A_{2}+A_{3})}{q^{4}-1}\right),

\displaystyle T_{+}=

\displaystyle\,(q^{2}-1)T_{-}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\frac{q^{2}-1}{q^{2}+1}\left(1+\frac{\mbox{Re}\,A_{1}q^{2}-A_{2}-A_{3}}{q^{4}-1}\right).

(36)

The remaining transition rates can be obtained from the probability conservation rules

\displaystyle 1=

\displaystyle\,T_{1}+T_{\rm x}+T_{+}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,2T_{-}+T_{11}.

(37)

The reduction to a stochastic growth model of binary strings
\rho_{p}(t)
allows for easy numerical simulations even in the limit of large
q
. It also allows for an efficient approximate solution, as we show in the next Subsections.

III.4
Right-propagating
n
-point density

We say that the binary string
\bar{p}
“ends at
x
” if
x
is the rightmost qudit position with
\bar{p}_{x}=\mathbb{X}
. (This implies that
\bar{p}_{y}=\mathbb{I}\,
for all
y>x
.) Following Ref.
[
4
]
, we define the “right-propagating density”
\bar{\rho}^{(0)}(\Delta x;t)
as the fraction of Pauli strings ending at
x=t+\Delta x
,

\bar{\rho}^{(0)}(\Delta x;t)=\sum_{\textrm{$\bar{p}$ ends at $t+\Delta x$}}\bar{\rho}_{\bar{p}}(t).

(38)

(Note that the argument
\Delta x
is measured with respect to the ballistic propagation at unit velocity.)
We also define the “right-propagating
n
-point density”

\bar{\rho}^{(n)}(\Delta x;t;\bar{a}_{n},\ldots,\bar{a}_{1})=\,\sum_{\textrm{$p$ ends at $t+\Delta x$}}\bar{\rho}_{\bar{p}}(t)\,\delta_{\bar{p}_{t+\Delta x-1},\bar{a}_{1}}\ldots\delta_{\bar{p}_{t+\Delta x-n},\bar{a}_{n}},

(39)

which is the fraction of Pauli strings that end at
t+\Delta x
and that have the sequence
\bar{a}_{n},\ldots,\bar{a}_{1},\mathbb{X}
as their rightmost nontrivial entries, whereby the rightmost “
\mathbb{X}
” appears at site
t+\Delta x
.

From the stochastic evolution equation for the full binary probability density
\bar{\rho}_{\bar{p}}(t)
we may deduce evolution equations for the right-propagating density and for the right-propagating
n
-point densities. These are

\displaystyle\bar{\rho}^{(n)}(\Delta x;t;\bar{p}_{n},\ldots,\bar{p}_{1})=

\displaystyle\,\sum_{\bar{a}_{1},\ldots,\bar{a}_{n}}T_{\bar{a}_{n}\bar{a}_{n-1};\bar{p}_{n}\bar{p}_{n-1}}\ldots T_{\bar{a}_{2},\bar{a}_{1};\bar{p}_{2},\bar{p}_{1}}T_{\mathbb{X}\mathbb{I}\,;\mathbb{X}\mathbb{I}\,}\bar{\rho}^{(n)}(\Delta x+1;t-1;\bar{a}_{n},\ldots,\bar{a}_{1})

(40)

\displaystyle\,\mbox{}+\sum_{\bar{a}_{1},\ldots,\bar{a}_{n+1}}T_{\bar{a}_{n+1}\bar{a}_{n};\bar{p}_{n}\bar{p}_{n-1}}\ldots T_{\bar{a}_{3},\bar{a}_{2};\bar{p}_{2},\bar{p}_{1}}T_{\bar{a}_{1}\mathbb{X};\mathbb{X}\mathbb{I}\,}\bar{\rho}^{(n+1)}(\Delta x+2;t-1;\bar{a}_{n+1},\bar{a}_{n},\ldots,\bar{a}_{1})

if
n
and
\Delta x
are both even. The summation variables
\bar{a}_{j}
take the values
\mathbb{I}\,
,
\mathbb{X}
,
j=1,\ldots,n
. In the same manner, we find

\displaystyle\bar{\rho}^{(n)}(\Delta x;t;\bar{p}_{n},\ldots,\bar{p}_{1})=

\displaystyle\,\sum_{\bar{a}_{1},\ldots,\bar{a}_{n}}\sum_{\bar{p}_{n+1}}T_{\bar{a}_{n}\bar{a}_{n-1};\bar{p}_{n+1}\bar{p}_{n}}\ldots T_{\bar{a}_{2},\bar{a}_{1};\bar{p}_{3},\bar{p}_{2}}T_{\mathbb{X}\mathbb{I}\,;\bar{p}_{1}\mathbb{X}}\bar{\rho}^{(n)}(\Delta x;t-1;\bar{a}_{n},\ldots,\bar{a}_{1})

(41)

\displaystyle\,\mbox{}+\sum_{\bar{a}_{1},\ldots,\bar{a}_{n+1}}\sum_{\bar{p}_{n+1}}T_{\bar{a}_{n+1}\bar{a}_{n};\bar{p}_{n+1}\bar{p}_{n}}\ldots T_{\bar{a}_{3},\bar{a}_{2};\bar{p}_{3},\bar{p}_{2}}T_{\bar{a}_{1}\mathbb{X};\bar{p}_{1}\mathbb{X}}\bar{\rho}^{(n+1)}(\Delta x+1;t-1;\bar{a}_{n+1},\bar{a}_{n},\ldots,\bar{a}_{1}),

if
n
is even and
\Delta x
is odd.
To avoid spurious even-odd effects, we will not consider the evolution equations for the right-propagating
n
-point density with
n
odd.

The evolution equations (
40
) and (
41
) for the right-propagating
n
-point density both involve the
(n+1)
-point density. For Haar-distributed two-qudit gates, the transition probabilities are such, that the
(n+1)
-point density appears in the combination
\sum_{\bar{a}_{n+1}}\bar{\rho}^{(n+1)}(\Delta x,t;\bar{a}_{n+1},\bar{a}_{n},\ldots,\bar{a}_{1})=\bar{\rho}^{(n)}(\Delta x,t;\bar{a}_{n},\ldots,\bar{a}_{1})
, so that the evolution equations (
40
) and (
41
) can be closed
[
4
]
. In the same way, we can get closed-form evolution equations for the right-propagating densities for generic unitary-invariant two-qudit gate distributions in the limit of large
q
, because the difference between
T_{-}
and
T_{\rm x}
vanishes in this limit. Such simplification does not occur at finite
q
. To arrive at a closed set of equations in this case, we truncate the evolutions (
40
) and (
41
) at sufficiently high order
n
by replacing the
(n+1)
-point density by its maximally random approximation,

\displaystyle\bar{\rho}^{(n+1)}(\Delta x;t;\bar{p}_{n+1},\bar{p}_{n},\ldots,\bar{p}_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\bar{\rho}^{(n)}(\Delta x;t;\bar{p}_{n},\ldots,\bar{p}_{1})\bar{r}^{\infty}_{\bar{p}_{n+1}},

(42)

where
\bar{r}^{\infty}_{\bar{p}_{x}}
was defined in Eq. (
32
). We expect that such truncation is justified for
n\gg n_{\rm DW}
, where
n_{\rm DW}
is the width of the domain wall between the trivial and maximum-entropy regions of the Pauli-string density
\rho_{p}
, see Eqs. (
28
) and (
29
).
Application of this truncation procedure to the right-moving density
\bar{\rho}^{(0)}(x;t)
gives the evolution equation

\displaystyle\bar{\rho}^{(0)}(\Delta x;t)=

\displaystyle\,T_{1}\bar{\rho}^{(0)}(\Delta x+1;t-1)

(43)

\displaystyle\,\mbox{}+\frac{T_{\rm x}+(q^{2}-1)T_{-}}{q^{2}}\bar{\rho}^{(0)}(\Delta x+2;t-1)

if
\Delta x
is even and

\displaystyle\bar{\rho}^{(0)}(\Delta x;t)=(T_{\rm x}+T_{+})\bar{\rho}^{(0)}(\Delta x;t-1)

(44)

\displaystyle\,\mbox{}+\frac{T_{1}+T_{+}+(q^{2}-1)(T_{11}+T_{-})}{q^{2}}\bar{\rho}^{(0)}(\Delta x+1;t-1)

if
\Delta x
is odd, where we used Eqs. (
36
) and (
37
) for the binary-string transition probabilities.

III.5
Drift-Diffusion process

We now describe, how the evolution equations (
40
) and (
41
) with the cut-off procedure (
42
) can be mapped to a drift-diffusion process.
To simplify the notation, we combine
\bar{\rho}^{(n)}(x;t)
and
\bar{\rho}^{(n)}(x+1,t)
into a two-component spinor,

\displaystyle R^{(n)}(\Delta x;t)=\begin{pmatrix}\bar{\rho}^{(n)}(\Delta x;t)\\
\bar{\rho}^{(n)}(\Delta x+1;t)\end{pmatrix},

(45)

where now
\Delta x
is always even.
The evolution equations (
40
)–(
41
) with the truncation prescription (
42
), may then be represented as

\displaystyle R^{(n)}(\Delta x;t)=

\displaystyle\,D^{(n)}R^{(n)}(\Delta x;t-1)

\displaystyle\,\mbox{}+D^{\prime(n)}R^{(n)}(\Delta x+2;t-1),

(46)

where
D^{(n)}
and
D^{\prime(n)}
are
2^{n+1}\times 2^{n+1}
matrices. In the special case
n=0
, see Eqs. (
43
) and (
44
), the matrices
D^{(0)}
and
D^{\prime(0)}
read

\displaystyle D^{(0)}=

\displaystyle\,\begin{pmatrix}0&T_{1}\\
0&T_{\rm x}+T_{+}\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle D^{\prime(0)}=

\displaystyle\,\begin{pmatrix}\frac{T_{\rm x}+(q^{2}-1)T_{-}}{q^{2}}&0\\
\frac{T_{1}+T_{+}+(q^{2}-1)(T_{11}+T_{-})}{q^{2}}&0\end{pmatrix}.

(47)

The transition matrices
D^{(n)}
and
D^{\prime(n)}
satisfy the normalization rule

\sum_{i}(D^{(n)}_{ij}+D^{\prime(n)}_{ij})=1

(48)

for each
j=1,\ldots,2^{n+1}
. We denote the left-eigenvectors, right-eigenvectors, and eigenvalues of
D^{(n)}+D^{\prime(n)}
by
\tilde{V}_{j}^{(n)}
,
V_{j}^{(n)}
, and
d_{j}^{(n)}
, respectively. Equation (
48
) ensures that the largest eigenvalue
d_{1}^{(n)}=1
, with left-eigenvector
\tilde{V}_{1}^{(n)}=(1,1,\ldots,1)^{\rm T}
.

In the long-time limit, the solution of Eq. (
46
) is of the form

R^{(n)}(\Delta x,t)=R_{{\rm}1}^{(n)}(\Delta x,t)V_{1}^{(n)},

(49)

where
R_{{\rm}1}^{(n)}(\Delta x,t)
satisfies the drift-diffusion equation (see App.
D
for details)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}R_{{\rm}1}^{(n)}(\Delta x,t)=

\displaystyle\,(1-v_{\rm B}^{(n)})\partial_{\Delta x}R_{{\rm}1}^{(n)}(\Delta x,t)

\displaystyle\,\mbox{}+\frac{{\cal D}^{(n)}}{2}\partial_{\Delta x}^{2}R_{{\rm}1}^{(n)}(\Delta x,t).

(50)

Here

\displaystyle v_{\rm B}^{(n)}=

\displaystyle\,1-2d_{11}^{\prime(n)}

(51)

is the butterfly velocity and

\displaystyle{\cal D}^{(n)}=

\displaystyle\,4d_{11}^{\prime(n)}[1-d_{11}^{\prime(n)}]+8\sum_{j\neq 1}\frac{d^{\prime(n)}_{1j}d^{\prime(n)}_{j1}}{1-d_{j}^{(n)}}

(52)

the diffusion constant governing the diffusive spreading of the front. (Recall that
\Delta x
is measured with respect to a ballistic propagation at unit velocity so that the drift velocity measured with respect to
\Delta x
is
v_{\rm B}-1
.) In Eqs. (
51
) and (
52
) we abbreviated

d_{ij}^{\prime(n)}=\tilde{V}_{i}^{(n){\rm T}}D^{\prime(n)}V_{j}^{(n)}.

(53)

In Eq. (
49
), the function
R_{1}^{(n)}(\Delta x,t)
describes the propagation of the ends of the Pauli strings, whereas the right-eigenvector
V_{1}^{(n)}
contains information on the structure of the Pauli strings immediately behind the end. Appendix
D
also contains an expression for the diffusion constant
{\cal D}^{(n)}
for the case that the sum
D^{(n)}+D^{\prime(n)}
of the transition matrices is not diagonalizable so that no complete basis of left- and right-eigenvectors exists.

For the lowest-order approximation
n=0
, the left- and right-eigenvectors of
D^{(0)}+D^{\prime(0)}
can be found in closed form and we obtain

\displaystyle v_{\rm B}^{(0)}=

\displaystyle\,\frac{(q^{2}-1)(1-T_{1})}{q^{2}-1+T_{1}(q^{2}+1)},

(54)

\displaystyle{\cal D}^{(0)}=

\displaystyle\,\frac{4(q^{2}+1)(q^{2}-1+T_{1})T_{1}(1-T_{1})}{[q^{2}-1+(q^{2}+1)T_{1}]^{2}}.

(55)

For Haar-distributed two-qubit operators, the lowest-order approximation is exact and Eqs. (
54
) and (
55
) reproduce the results (
3
) and (
4
) quoted in the introduction

[
4
]
. For a trivial circuit, one has
v_{\rm B}=0
and
{\cal D}=0
.

V.1
Convergence of
\rho_{p}
to a binary distribution

We have seen that the butterfly velocity and the diffusion constant could be obtained by considering the projected binary strings
\bar{\rho}_{\bar{p}}
with two degrees of freedom per qudit. We now return to the full distribution
\rho_{p}
, which has
q^{2}
degrees of freedom per qubit. Below, we show that in the long-time limit, the full Pauli-string weight
\rho_{p}
approaches a binary form, in which
\rho_{p}
depends only on whether the Pauli string entries
p_{x}
are
0
or different from
0
. Specifically, we show that in the long-time limit
\rho_{p}
approaches
B\rho_{p}
, with

\displaystyle B\rho_{p}=\left(\prod_{x}B_{p_{x},\bar{p}_{x}}\right)\bar{\rho}_{\bar{p}},

(67)

where
\bar{p}
is the binary string corresponding to
p
,
\bar{\rho}_{\bar{p}}
the binary-string distribution corresponding to
\rho_{p}
, and

B_{p_{x},\bar{p}_{x}}=\delta_{p_{x},0}\delta_{\bar{p}_{x},\mathbb{I}\,}+\frac{(1-\delta_{p_{x},0})\delta_{\bar{p}_{x},\mathbb{X}}}{q^{2}-1}.

(68)

We refer to the distribution
B\rho_{p}
as the “binary distribution corresponding to
\rho_{p}
”.
In App.
C
we verify that if a probability distribution
\rho_{p}(t)
is of binary type,
i.e.
,
\rho_{p}(t)=B\rho_{p}(t)
, this property is preserved under time evolution. To prove that a distribution
\rho_{p}(t)
approaches
B\rho_{p}(t)
for large times, we consider the two-norm of the difference

||\rho(t)-B\rho(t)||_{2}=\sqrt{\sum_{p}|\rho_{p}(t)-B\rho_{p}(t)|^{2}}.

(69)

In a single time step, it is bounded by

||\rho(t+1)-B\rho(t+1)||_{2}\leq||W||^{\prime}_{\infty}||\rho(t)-B\rho(t)||_{2},

(70)

where
||W||^{\prime}_{\infty}
is the operator norm of the transition probability matrix
\langle|W_{ap}|^{2}\rangle
of Eq. (
23
) — i.e., the largest singular value —, after exclusion of the binary Pauli-string weights for which
\rho_{p}-B\rho_{p}=0
. Per qudit there are two Pauli-string weights with
\rho_{p}=B\rho_{p}
, the maximally random distribution and the trivial one. The singular values of
\langle|W_{ap}|^{2}\rangle
are
1
,
\lambda_{\pm}
, and
\lambda_{\rm i}
, with

\displaystyle\lambda_{\pm}=

\displaystyle\,\frac{A_{2}+A_{3}\pm q^{2}\mbox{Re}\,A_{1}}{q^{4}-1},

\displaystyle\lambda_{\rm i}=

\displaystyle\,\left|\frac{A_{3}-A_{2}+iq^{2}\mbox{Im}\,A_{1}}{q^{4}-1}\right|.

(71)

The degeneracies of these singular values are
N_{1}=2
,
N_{\pm}=3\mp q^{2}/2+q^{4}/4
,
N_{\rm i}=q^{2}/-8
if
q
is even and
N_{1}=2
,
N_{\pm}=-3/4\mp q^{2}/2+q^{4}/4
,
N_{\rm i}=(q^{4}-3)/2
if
q
is odd.
The singular value
1
belongs to the two binary weights. Hence, the largest singular value of the two-qudit transition probability matrix after exclusion of the binary Pauli-string weights is

\displaystyle\lambda_{W}=

\displaystyle\,\max(\lambda_{+},\lambda_{\rm i}).

(72)

Since
\lambda_{W}<1
except for the trivial circuit, it follows that the probability distribution
\rho_{p}(t)
exponentially approaches the corresponding maximum entropy distribution
B\rho_{p}(t)
. For a Haar-random circuit, one has
\lambda_{W}=0
, so that the distribution
\rho_{p}
is binary already after a single time step.
For a circuit with
\lambda_{\rm W}>0
we can define the decay time

\tau_{\rm b}=\frac{-1}{\ln\lambda_{W}}

(73)

to characterize the time scale to converge to a binary distribution. Figure
7
shows
\tau_{\rm b}
for a random unitary circuit with Poisson-kernel distribution for the two-qudit gates.
In the limit
\alpha\to 1
, the asymptotic expression for the decay time scale is

\tau_{\rm b}\approx\frac{q^{2}+1}{q^{2}-1}\frac{1}{4(1-\alpha)}-\frac{5q^{2}-3}{8(q^{2}-1)}.

(74)

(Here the limit
\alpha\to 1
is taken at fixed
q
. If one first sends
q\to\infty
and then takes the limit
\alpha\to 1
, one has
\tau_{\rm b}\approx(1/4)(1-\alpha)^{-1}-1/8
.)

and for the limit

. In the Haar limit

V.2
Relation to out-of-time-order correlator

In the literature, operator spreading is also described via the “out-of-time-ordered correlator” (OTOC)
C(x,y;t)

[
4
,
5
,
12
]
, which is defined as

C(x,y,t)=\frac{1}{2q^{L}}\tr\left|\left[O_{x}(t),O_{y}(0)\right]\right|^{2}.

(75)

Here
O_{x}\equiv O_{x}(0)
and
O_{y}\equiv O_{y}(0)
are Pauli-string operators that contain nontrivial generalized Pauli matrices
\sigma_{p_{x}}
and
\sigma_{p_{y}}
at qudits
x
and
y
, respectively, while being trivial at all other positions. Using Eq. (
1
) for
O_{x}(t)
, we may express the average
\langle C(x,y;t)\rangle
over realizations of the random circuit as

\displaystyle\langle C(x,y,t)\rangle=

\displaystyle\,1-q^{-L}\operatorname{Re}\langle\tr O_{x}(t)O_{y}(0)O_{x}(t)^{\dagger}O_{y}(0)^{\dagger}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,1-q^{-1}\sum_{a}\rho_{a}(t)\operatorname{Re}\tr\sigma_{a_{y}}\sigma_{p_{y}}\sigma_{a_{y}}^{\dagger}\sigma_{p_{y}}^{\dagger},

(76)

where
\rho_{p}(t)=\langle|\gamma_{p}(t)|^{2}\rangle
is the classical Pauli-string probability distribution for the operator
O_{x}(t)
. Since the ensemble-averaged OTOC (
75
) depends on the distance
x-y
only, we denote it by
\langle C(x-y,t)\rangle
henceforth.

In the previous Subsection, we have shown that
\rho_{a}(t)
approaches the binary form for times
t\gg\tau_{\rm b}
, so that we can express
\langle C(s,t)\rangle
in terms of the projected binary-string distribution
\bar{\rho}_{\bar{a}}(t)

[
12
]
,

\displaystyle C(s,t)=

\displaystyle\,1-\sum_{\bar{a}}\left(\delta_{\bar{a}_{s},\mathbb{I}\,}-\frac{\delta_{\bar{a}_{s},\mathbb{X}}}{q^{2}-1}\right)\bar{\rho}_{\bar{a}}(t).

(77)

Considering, without loss of generality, the case
s\geq 0
, we may assume that the contribution of Pauli strings
\bar{a}
with a left end at position
\geq x
to the summation in Eq. (
77
) is exponentially small, so that it is sufficient to consider Pauli strings
\bar{a}
with the left end at a position
<x
. As in Subsec.
III.4
, we assume that
\bar{\rho}_{\bar{a}}
takes the maximally-random form at

if
s
is at a distance larger than a cut-off distance
n\gtrsim n_{\rm DW}
to the left of the right end of the binary Pauli string
\bar{a}
. For such
\bar{a}
, the summands in Eq. (
77
) cancel (compare with Eq. (
42
)). Hence, it remains to sum over binary Pauli strings
\bar{a}
for which
s
is within a distance
n
to the left of the right end of
\bar{a}
or to the right of the right end of
\bar{a}
. This sum can be expressed with the help of the right-propagating
n
-point densities
\bar{\rho}^{(n)}(\Delta x;t;\bar{a}_{n},\ldots,\bar{a}_{1})
defined in Sec.

\displaystyle\langle C(s,t)\rangle=

\displaystyle\,1-\sum_{s^{\prime}<s}\bar{\rho}^{(0)}(s^{\prime}-t;t)

\displaystyle\,\mbox{}+\frac{1-\delta(s,t)}{q^{2}-1}\bar{\rho}^{(0)}(s-t;t),

(78)

with

\displaystyle\delta(s,t)=

\displaystyle\,\sum_{m=1}^{n}\sum_{\bar{a}_{m-1},\ldots,\bar{a}_{1}}\frac{q^{2}-1}{\bar{\rho}^{(0)}(s-t;t)}

\displaystyle\,\mbox{}\times\left[\frac{1}{q^{2}-1}\bar{\rho}^{(m)}(s-t+m;t;\mathbb{X},\bar{a}_{m-1},\ldots,\bar{a}_{1})\right.

\displaystyle\,\left.\ \ \ \ \vphantom{\frac{M}{M}}\mbox{}-\bar{\rho}^{(m)}(s-t+m;t;\mathbb{I}\,,\bar{a}_{m-1},\ldots,\bar{a}_{1})\right].

(79)

This expression for
\langle C(s,t)\rangle
, but without the term proportional to
\delta(s,t)
, was previously obtained in Ref.
[
5
]
. The correction term proportional to
\delta
represents the finite width of the domain wall between the maximum-entropy and trivial phases in random circuits with non-Haar-distributed two-qubit gate operators.

In Sec.

we have shown that in the long-time limit, the
n
-point right-moving densities satisfy a drift-diffusion equation with drift velocity
v_{\rm B}^{(n)}
and diffusion constant
{\cal D}^{(n)}
. We thus conclude that the same drift-diffusion process also describes the long-time behavior of the out-of-time-order-correlator (

(1/2)\mathrm{Erfc}[(s-v^{(n)}_{B}t)/\sqrt{2D^{(n)}t}]
in the long time limit, see Ref.
[

To estimate the correction
\delta(s,t)
in the long-time limit, we make use of the expression (

) for the large-

-point density
\rho^{(m)}(\Delta x;t;\bar{a}_{m},\ldots,\bar{a}_{1})

and

is implicit in Eq. (

VI
Conclusion

In this article, we have extended the study of operator spreading in random unitary circuits from circuits with Haar-distributed two-qudit gates to circuits with the more general class of unitary-invariant distributions. As in the case of a circuit with Haar-distributed gate operators, we demonstrate that the operator spreading process can be mapped to a classical stochastic growth model. The classical stochastic model satisfies drift-diffusion dynamics in the long-time limit, characterized by a drift velocity (the butterfly velocity
v_{\rm B}
) and a diffusion constant
{\cal D}
. We determine
v_{\rm B}
and
{\cal D}
for unitary-invariant two-qudit-gate distributions and establish a relation with the out-of-time-ordered correlator (OTOC). Unlike in the Haar-distributed case, where
v_{\rm B}
and
{\cal D}
are functions of the qudit dimension
q
only and
v_{\rm B}\to 1
,
{\cal D}\to 0
for
q\to\infty
, for the more general class of models we consider here,
v_{\rm B}
and
{\cal D}
depend on the precise form of the statistical distribution of the two-qudit gate operators and remain below
1
and nonzero, respectively, in the limit
q\to\infty
.
A concrete example of a unitary-invariant distribution of the two-qudit gate operator
{\cal U}
is the Poisson kernel
[
14
,
15
,
16
,
17
]
, which is the maximum-information-entropy distribution with a fixed value of the average
\langle{\cal U}\rangle=\alpha\openone
. The Poisson kernel interpolates between the Haar-distributed circuit for
\alpha=0
and the trivial circuit for
\alpha=1
.

Our solution of the stochastic model relies on the assumption that the Pauli string weights
\rho_{p}(t)
approach the maximally-random form characteristic of the uniform bulk within a finite distance
n_{\rm DW}
from the end of a Pauli string. Our explicit results for the Poisson-kernel distributed circuit supports this assumption, showing convergence assuming the maximum-entropy form for a distance
n\sim 4
from the end of the Pauli string for all values of the parameter
\alpha
. Numerical simulations of the classical stochastic growth model, which do not require the assumption of a maximally random distribution sufficiently far from the end of the Pauli string, are in excellent agreement with our analytical calculations.

Our analysis of the classical stochastic growth model reveals three regimes of relevance to the information spreading in the random unitary circuit: At short times, the Pauli-string distribution first relaxes to a
binary
form, in which the Pauli-string weights
\rho_{p}(t)
depend only on whether the generalized Pauli operator
\sigma_{p_{x}}
at site
x
is trivial or nontrivial. For the Haar distribution, the uniform-binary distribution is reached already after a single time step. For Poisson-kernel-distributed two-qudit gates, the characteristic time scale for the approach to the uniform-binary distribution
\tau_{\rm b}
is typically of the order of a few time steps, although it diverges in the limit
|\alpha|\to 1
of a trivial circuit without information spreading. In the intermediate
thermalization
phase, a Pauli string distribution that is localized initially spreads out in space. In this process, a domain wall separates a maximum-entropy region, in which all generalized Pauli operators have the same probability, and a trivial region, moves with the butterfly velocity
v_{\rm B}
. The time scale governing the transition from a trivial distribution to the maximally random distribution at a fixed position
x
is
n_{\rm DW}/v_{\rm B}
, where
n_{\rm DW}
is the width of the domain wall between trivial and maximally random regions. The fast convergence of our truncation procedure for the Poisson-kernel-distributed circuit indicates that
n_{\rm DW}
typically is of the order of a few lattice spacings. Finally, at long time scales
\sim L/v_{\rm B}
,
L
being the system size, an initially localized Pauli-string distribution has spread over the entire system size, marking the completion of the operator spreading process
[
12
]
.

Although the distributions of the two-qudit gates considered here are no longer uniform in the unitary group, no symmetries were imposed on the distribution. An investigation of the role of additional symmetries on information spreading in random circuits is left for future work.

Appendix A
Generalized Pauli matrices

The generalized Pauli matrices
\sigma_{a}
,
a=0,1,\ldots,q^{2}-1
, form a basis for operators acting on a qudit, a quantum system with
q
degrees of freedom
|m\rangle
,
m=0,1,\ldots,q-1
. We represent the index
a=(a^{\prime},a^{\prime\prime})
as a pair of two integers
a^{\prime}
,
a^{\prime\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}_{q}
. The complete family of
q^{2}
independent generalized Pauli matrices is then defined as
[
23
]

\sigma_{a}=\sum_{m=0}^{q-1}\omega^{ma^{\prime\prime}}\left|m+a^{\prime}\right>\left<m\right|,

(80)

where
\omega=\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2\pi i}{q}}
and
m+a^{\prime}
is taken
\mod q
.
The generalized Pauli matrices satisfy the orthonormality relation

\displaystyle\tr\sigma_{a}\sigma_{b}^{\dagger}=q\delta_{a,b}

(81)

and the commutation relation

\displaystyle\sigma_{a}\sigma_{b}=\omega^{b^{\prime}a^{\prime\prime}-a^{\prime}b^{\prime\prime}}\sigma_{b}\sigma_{a}.

(82)

They also satisfy the completeness relation

\sum_{a}\mbox{tr}\,A\sigma^{\dagger}_{a}\,\mbox{tr}\,B^{\dagger}\sigma_{a}=q\mbox{tr}\,AB^{\dagger}

(83)

for arbitrary
q\times q
matrices
A
and
B
.
Because of Eq. (
83
), the generalized Pauli matrices
\sigma_{a}
are also said to be a unitary
1
-design
[
18
]
.

Since the evolution operators act on pairs of qudits, we will often consider the tensor product
\sigma_{a_{x}}\otimes\sigma_{a_{y}}
of generalized Pauli matrices for two qudits at positions
x
and
y
. With the shorthand notations
a=(a_{x},a_{y})=(a_{x}^{\prime},a_{x}^{\prime\prime},a_{y}^{\prime},a_{y}^{\prime\prime})
and
\Sigma_{a}=\sigma_{a_{x}}\otimes\sigma_{a_{y}}
, we observe that the
q^{4}
matrices
\Sigma_{a}
satisfy the orthonormality relation

\tr\Sigma_{a}\Sigma_{b}^{\dagger}=q^{2}\delta_{a,b},

(84)

whereas their commutation relation is

\Sigma_{a}\Sigma_{b}=-\varphi(a,b)\Sigma_{b}\Sigma_{a},

(85)

with

\varphi(a,b)=-\omega^{b_{x}^{\prime}a_{x}^{\prime\prime}-a_{x}^{\prime}b_{x}^{\prime\prime}+b_{y}^{\prime}a_{y}^{\prime\prime}-a_{y}^{\prime}b_{y}^{\prime\prime}}.

(86)

The function
\varphi(a,b)
satisfies

\varphi(0,0,b_{x},b_{y})=-1

(87)

and

\varphi(a,b)=\varphi(b,a)^{*},

(88)

as well as the sum rules

\displaystyle\sum_{a_{x}\neq 0}\varphi(a_{x},0,b_{x},b_{y})=

\displaystyle\,1-q^{2}\delta_{b_{x},0},

\displaystyle\sum_{a_{y}\neq 0}\varphi(0,a_{y},b_{x},b_{y})=

\displaystyle\,1-q^{2}\delta_{b_{y},0},

(89)

\displaystyle\sum_{a_{x}\neq 0}\sum_{a_{y}\neq 0}\varphi(a_{x},a_{y},b_{x},b_{y})=

\displaystyle\,-1+q^{2}(\delta_{b_{x},0}+\delta_{b_{y},0})

\displaystyle\,\mbox{}-q^{4}\delta_{b_{x},0}\delta_{b_{y},0}.

Because of the hermiticity relation (
88
) analogous sum rules also apply to summations of
\varphi(a_{x},a_{y},b_{x},b_{y})
over the second index pair
b_{x}
,
b_{y}
.

\displaystyle\,\mbox{}+\sum_{a_{x}\neq 0}\rho_{a_{x},0}(t-1)\langle|W_{a_{x},0,p_{x},p_{x+1}}|^{2}\rangle

Appendix D
Drift-diffusion equation

Inspired by Refs.
[
27
,
28
,
29
]
, we write the two-component spinor
R_{\rm R}^{(n)}(\Delta x,t)
, which represents the
n
-point densities, in the basis of right-eigenvectors of
D^{(n)}+D^{\prime(n)}
,

R_{\rm R}^{(n)}(\Delta x,t)=\sum_{i=1}^{2^{n+1}}R_{{\rm R}i}^{(n)}(\Delta x,t)V_{i}^{(n)}.

(110)

Assuming that
R_{\rm R}^{(n)}(\Delta x,t)
is a sufficiently smooth function of
\Delta x
and
t
, we expand to first order in changes in
\Delta x
and
t
, and find that the expansion coefficients satisfy the equations

\displaystyle(1-d_{i}^{(n)})R_{{\rm R}i}^{(n)}(\Delta x,t)=

\displaystyle\,-\partial_{t}R_{{\rm R}i}^{(n)}(\Delta x,t)

(111)

\displaystyle\,\mbox{}+2\sum_{j}d^{\prime(n)}_{ij}\partial_{\Delta x}R_{{\rm R}j}(\Delta x,t),

where
d_{ij}^{\prime(n)}
was defined in Eq. (
53
).
Anticipating that the expansion coefficients
R_{{\rm R}i}^{(n)}(\Delta x,t)
with
i>1
are parametrically smaller than
R_{{\rm R}1}^{(n)}(\Delta x,t)
in the long-time limit, which will be shown below, we focus on the evolution equation for
R_{{\rm R}1}^{(n)}(\Delta x,t)
. Substituting
d_{1}^{(n)}=1
gives

0=(-\partial_{t}+2d_{11}^{\prime(n)}\partial_{\Delta x})R_{{\rm R}1}^{(n)}(\Delta x,t).

(112)

This equation determines the Butterfly velocity
v_{\rm B}^{(n)}
, see Eq. (
51
).

To capture the long-time diffusive spreading of the propagating front, we switch to coordinates co-moving with the propagating front. Formally, this is achieved by the definition

Q_{{\rm R}j}^{(n)}(\Delta y,t)=R_{{\rm R}j}^{(n)}(\Delta y+2d_{11}^{(n)}t,t),

(113)

We first consider the expansion coefficients
Q_{{\rm R}j}^{(n)}(\Delta y,t)
with
j>1
. Their evolution equation reads

\displaystyle(1-d_{\rm j}^{(n)})Q_{{\rm R}j}^{(n)}(\Delta y,t)=

\displaystyle\,2d_{j1}^{\prime(n)}\partial_{\Delta y}Q_{{\rm R}1}^{(n)}(\Delta y,t),

(114)

where we dropped all terms involving derivatives of
Q_{{\rm R}j}(\Delta y,t)
for
j>1
. Solving Eq. (
114
) gives

Q_{{\rm R}j}^{(n)}(\Delta y,t)=\frac{2d_{j1}^{\prime(n)}}{1-d_{\rm j}^{(n)}}\partial_{\Delta y}Q_{{\rm R}1}^{(n)}(\Delta y,t).

(115)

To find an evolution equation for
Q_{{\rm R}1}^{(n)}(\Delta y,t)
that accounts for the diffusive spreading of the front, we now keep the terms involving first-order spatial derivatives of
Q_{{\rm R}j}^{(n)}(\Delta y,t)
with
j>1
as well as terms with a second-order spatial derivative of
Q_{{\rm R}1}^{(n)}(\Delta y,t)
. As a result, one finds that
Q_{{\rm R}1}^{(n)}(\Delta y,t)
obeys the diffusion equation,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}Q_{{\rm R}1}^{(n)}(\Delta y,t)=

\displaystyle\,\frac{{\cal D}^{(n)}}{2}\partial_{\Delta y}^{2}Q_{{\rm R}1}^{(n)}(\Delta y,t),

(116)

with the diffusion constant
{\cal D}^{(n)}
given by Eq. (
52
). (There is no term with a first-order spatial derivative since we use the coordinate
\Delta y
co-moving with the front.) Replacing the continuous variable
\Delta y
by
\Delta x=\Delta y+2d_{11}^{\prime(n)}t
gives the drift-diffusion equation (
50
) of the main text.

It remains to verify that
R_{{\rm R}}^{(n)}(\Delta x,t)
is dominated by the expansion coefficient
R_{{\rm R}1}^{(n)}(\Delta x,t)
in the long-time limit, which was an essential assumption in our derivation of Eq. (
116
). Hereto, we note that the solution of the diffusion equation (
116
) is
Q_{{\rm R}1}^{(n)}(\Delta y,t)\propto\mbox{erfc}(\Delta y/2\sqrt{{\cal D}^{(n)}t})
. Its derivative to
\partial_{\Delta y}Q_{{\rm R}1}^{(n)}(\Delta y,t)
is proportional to
1/\sqrt{t}
. Hence, by Eq. (
115
),
Q_{{\rm R}j}^{(n)}(\Delta y,t)
is indeed parametrically smaller than
Q_{{\rm R}1}^{(n)}
in the long-time limit.

The above construction of the drift-diffusion equation for
R_{{\rm R}1}^{(n)}(\Delta x,t)
makes use of the assumption that a complete basis of left- and right-eigenvectors
\tilde{V}_{j}^{(n)}
and
V_{j}^{(n)}
of
D^{(n)}+D^{\prime(n)}
exists. With the exception of the requirement that left- and right-eigenvectors
\tilde{V}_{1}^{(n)}
and
V_{1}^{(n)}
exist, which is guaranteed, because
\tilde{V}_{1}^{(n)}=(1,1,\ldots,1)^{\rm T}
can be explicitly constructed, this assumption is not necessary for the mapping to the drift-diffusion equation (
50
). The extension of the above construction to the case that a complete basis of left- and right-eigenvectors of
D^{(n)}+D^{\prime(n)}
does not exist is straightforward and one finds the diffusion constant

\displaystyle{\cal D}^{(n)}=

\displaystyle\,4d_{11}^{\prime(n)}[1-d_{11}^{\prime(n)}]

\displaystyle\,\mbox{}+8\tilde{V}_{1}^{(n){\rm T}}D^{\prime(n)}G^{(n)}D^{\prime(n)}V_{1}^{(n)},

(117)

with

\displaystyle G^{(n)}=

\displaystyle\,\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}(D^{(n)}+D^{\prime(n)})^{l}(\openone-V_{1}^{(n)}\tilde{V}_{1}^{(n){\rm T}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\left[\openone-(D^{(n)}+D^{\prime(n)})(\openone-V_{1}^{(n)}\tilde{V}_{1}^{(n){\rm T}})\right]^{-1}

\displaystyle\,\mbox{}-V_{1}^{(n)}\tilde{V}_{1}^{(n){\rm T}}.

(118)

Appendix E
Brownian-motion ensemble

The Poisson kernel offers a continuous interpolation between a random circuit with Haar-distributed two-qudit evolution operators and the trivial circuit. Another random matrix ensemble that interpolates between these two limits is Dyson’s Brownian motion ensemble
[
20
,
21
]
. It obtains the two-qudit operators
{\cal U}_{x,x+1}
from the continuous time evolution with a random Hamiltonian with Gaussian white-noise distribution. Specifically,

{\cal U}=T_{t}e^{-i\int_{0}^{1}dt{\cal H}(t)},

(119)

where
T_{t}
indicates the time-ordering and
{\cal H}(t^{\prime})
is a
q^{2}\times q^{2}
hermitian matrix with Gaussian distribution with zero mean and variance

\langle H_{ij}(t)H_{kl}(t^{\prime})\rangle=\frac{\lambda}{q^{2}}\delta_{il}\delta_{jk}\delta(t-t^{\prime}),

(120)

where
\lambda
is a parameter that describes the interpolation between the trivial and Haar-random two-qudit gate operators. This ensemble satisfies the unitary invariance property (
5
). The distribution of the eigenphases
e^{i\theta_{j}}
of
{\cal U}
,
j=1,2,\ldots,q^{2}
, is
[
30
]

\displaystyle P(\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{q^{2}})=

\displaystyle\,\frac{2^{\frac{1}{2}q^{2}(q^{2}-1)}}{(q^{2})!}e^{\frac{\lambda}{24}(q^{4}-1)}\prod_{j<k}\sin\frac{\theta_{j}-\theta_{k}}{2}

\displaystyle\langle\tr U_{0}^{j_{1}}\tr U_{0}^{j_{2}}\tr U_{0}^{\dagger k_{1}}\tr U_{0}^{\dagger k_{2}}\rangle=

where the summation is over partitions
\lambda

K=j_{1}+j_{2}=k_{1}+k_{2}
,
L(\lambda)
is the length of
\lambda

\chi_{\lambda}(\mu)
is the irreducible characters of the symmetric group
S_{K}
. (Recall that the size of the unitary matrices we consider is
q^{2}
. A partition
\lambda
of
K
consists of the positive integers
\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+\ldots+\lambda_{L}=K
, with
L\equiv L(\lambda)
and
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\ldots\geq\lambda_{L}

j_{1}+j_{2}=K
and
k_{1}+k_{2}=K
are partitions of

\iota

\kappa
to denote these partitions, whereby the ordering of the two terms in the partition is chosen such that the first term is
\geq

\iota=(j_{1},j_{2})

and
(j_{2},j_{1})

\kappa=(k_{1},k_{2})
if
k_{1}\geq k_{2}

(k_{2},k_{1})

The irreducible characters of the symmetric group can be obtained from Frobenius formula
[
34
]
, which states that
\chi_{\lambda}(\iota)
is the coefficient of the monomial
x_{1}^{l_{1}}x_{2}^{l_{2}}\ldots x_{L}^{l_{L}}
in

\prod_{i<j}(x_{i}-x_{j})P_{j_{1}}(x)P_{j_{2}}(x),

where
P_{j}(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{L}x_{i}^{j}
and
l_{i}=\lambda_{i}+L-i
.
The product
\prod_{i<j}(x_{i}-x_{j})
is the Vandermonde determinant

\prod_{i<j}(x_{i}-x_{j})=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{L}}(\mathrm{sgn}\ \sigma)x_{L}^{\sigma(1)-1}\cdots x_{1}^{\sigma(L)-1}.

(149)

Because
l_{1}\geq L
, the only non-vanishing terms are

\displaystyle\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)x_{L}^{\sigma(1)-1}\cdots x_{1}^{j_{1}+j_{2}+\sigma(L)-1}

\displaystyle\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)x_{L}^{\sigma(1)-1}\cdots x_{i}^{j_{2}+\sigma(L+1-i)-1}\cdots x_{1}^{j_{1}+\sigma(L)-1}

\displaystyle\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)x_{L}^{\sigma(1)-1}\cdots x_{i}^{j_{1}+\sigma(L+1-i)-1}\cdots x_{1}^{j_{2}+\sigma(L)-1}

The calculation of
\chi_{\lambda}(\iota)
depends on whether
\lambda
is a hook partition,
i.e.
, a partition of the form
\lambda_{1}+1+\ldots+1
, in which only one term is
>1
. If
\lambda
is hook, then

\displaystyle\chi_{\lambda}(\iota)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{L},\

\displaystyle j_{1}<L\leq K;

\displaystyle\chi_{\lambda}(\iota)

\displaystyle=0,\ \quad

\displaystyle j_{2}<L\leq j_{1};

\displaystyle\chi_{\lambda}(\iota)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{L-1},\

\displaystyle 1\leq L\leq j_{2}.

(150)

If
\lambda
is not a hook partition, then for fixed
\iota
and
\kappa
there is only one permutation
\sigma
that gives a non-vanishing value, which is
(2-\mathrm{sgn}(|j_{1}-j_{2}|))\mathrm{sng}(\sigma)
. One can further prove that for each partition
\lambda
there are only two partitions
\iota
that generate nonzero
\chi_{\lambda}(\iota)
. To see this, we suppose that
\iota=(j_{1},j_{2})
and
\iota^{\prime}=(j^{\prime}_{1},j^{\prime}_{2})
are two solutions that generate nonzero
\chi_{\lambda}(\iota)
and
\chi_{\lambda}(\iota^{\prime})
for the same partition
\lambda
. The non-vanishing terms are

\displaystyle\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)x_{L}^{\sigma(1)-1}\cdots x_{i}^{j_{2}+\sigma(L+1-i)-1}\cdots x_{1}^{j_{1}+\sigma(L)-1}

\displaystyle\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma^{\prime})x_{L}^{\sigma^{\prime}(1)-1}\cdots x_{i^{\prime}}^{j^{\prime}_{2}+\sigma^{\prime}(L+1-i^{\prime})-1}\cdots x_{1}^{j^{\prime}_{1}+\sigma^{\prime}(L)-1}

Because each part of
\lambda
is positive, one must have either
\sigma(L)=1
or
\sigma(L+1-i)=1.
If
\sigma(L)=1
, then we must have
\sigma^{\prime}(L+1-i^{\prime})=1
. We can rule out the possibility

because if so we must have
\sigma^{\prime}(L)=j^{\prime}_{2}+1
. In this case,
\lambda
is a hook, which contradicts our presumption. Moreover, it also means there is no third partition
\iota^{\prime\prime}:j_{1}^{\prime\prime}+j_{2}^{\prime\prime}=K
to generate nonzero
\chi_{\lambda}(j_{1}^{\prime\prime},j_{2}^{\prime\prime})
. We then can further conclude that
\sigma(L+1-i)=\sigma^{\prime}(L)=j_{1}-j^{\prime}_{1}+1.
For other terms,
\sigma
and
\sigma^{\prime}
are the same. Therefore, the sign of
\sigma
and
\sigma^{\prime}
are opposite. Because
j_{1}\geq j_{2}
and
j_{2}\geq j_{1}
correspond to the same partition, we have

\sum_{j_{1}=1}^{K}\chi_{\lambda}(j_{1},j_{2})=0

(151)

if
\lambda
is not a hook partition.
Furthermore, for a hook partition,

\sum_{j_{1}=1}^{K-1}\chi_{\lambda}(\iota)=(-1)^{L(\lambda)}(2L(\lambda)-K-1)

(152)

148

Monotone-based Numerical Schemes for Two-Dimensional Systems of Nonlocal Conservation Laws
Anika Beckers, Jan Friedrich

Abstract
We present a class of numerical schemes for two-dimensional systems of nonlocal conservation laws, which are based on utilizing well-known monotone numerical flux functions after suitably approximating the nonlocal terms. The considered systems are weakly coupled by the nonlocal terms and the underlying flux function is rather general to guarantee that our results are applicable to a wide range of common nonlocal models. We state sufficient conditions to ensure the convergence of the monotone-based numerical schemes to the unique weak entropy solution. Moreover, we provide an error estimate that yields the convergence rate of 𝒪 ⁡ ( Δ ​ t ) \mathcal{O}(\sqrt{\Delta t}) for the numerical approximations of the solution. Our results include an existence and uniqueness proof of the nonlocal system, too. Numerical results illustrate our theoretical findings. Keywords: finite-volume schemes, hyperbolic systems, nonlocal conservation laws, monotone schemes, convergence rate, error estimates 2020 MSC: 35L65, 35L03, 65M12, 65M15, 76A30

Introduction

In recent years, multidimensional nonlocal conservation laws have been used to model, e.g. crowd motion
[
1
,
14
,
9
,
21
,
22
,
12
,
13
]
, material flow on conveyor belts
[
26
,
20
]
or, cluster formation
[
11
]
.
All these models can be described by the following system of nonlocal conservation laws in two space dimensions

\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\rho^{k}+\textnormal{div}_{\textbf{x}}\,\boldsymbol{f}^{k}\left(t,\textbf{x},\rho^{k},\boldsymbol{\eta}*\boldsymbol{\rho}\right)=0\quad&&,(t,\textbf{x})\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{2},\;k=1,\ldots,K,\\
&\boldsymbol{\rho}(0,\textbf{x})=\boldsymbol{\rho}_{0}(\textbf{x})&&,\textbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\end{aligned}\end{cases}

(1)

for the state variables
\boldsymbol{\rho}=\left(\rho^{1},\ldots,\rho^{K}\right)
, considered as a function
\boldsymbol{\rho}\!:\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{K}
,
\left(t,\textbf{x}\right)\mapsto\boldsymbol{\rho}\left(t,\textbf{x}\right)

and the nonlocal flux
\boldsymbol{f}^{k}\!:\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{M}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
k=1,\ldots,K
.
The system is coupled by the nonlocal terms, which contain convolutions of the state variables with a mollifier
\boldsymbol{\eta}:\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{M\times K}
.
Thus,
\boldsymbol{\eta}*\boldsymbol{\rho}\in\mathbb{R}^{M}
, where
M
is the number of combinations of kernels and state variables that have to be convoluted.
In particular, we define for
m=1,\ldots,M

\left(\boldsymbol{\eta}*\boldsymbol{\rho}\right)_{m}\left(t,\textbf{x}\right)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\eta^{m,k}\left(\textbf{x}-\tilde{\textbf{x}}\right)\rho^{k}\left(t,\tilde{\textbf{x}}\right)d\tilde{\textbf{x}}.

(2)

The system (
1
) with the convolutions as in (
2
) has first been studied in
[
1
]
.
Here, a general result for the existence of weak entropy solutions was shown using a Lax-Friedrichs-type numerical scheme.
The uniqueness of the weak entropy solutions was proven for specific modeling equations, e.g.
[
26
,
22
,
12
,
13
,
11
]
.
We note that in the scalar case, i.e.
K=1
and
\boldsymbol{f}^{1}
being linear in
\rho
, weak solutions are already unique as proven in
[
25
]
.
We also refer to
[
24
]
for a recent overview on nonlocal balance laws with unique weak solutions.

Since their introduction in
[
5
,
1
,
12
]
, nonlocal conservation laws have a deep connection to approximate numerical solutions and in particular finite volume methods.
The main reason is that they are an effective tool to prove the existence of solutions via their limit.
A usual choice are Lax-Friedrichs-type numerical schemes, e.g.
[
1
,
5
,
6
]
, but in recent years other schemes such as Upwind or Godunov-type schemes
[
4
,
17
,
8
,
26
]
became popular.
For one-dimensional equations, even more general results for complete classes of numerical schemes are available.
It was shown that with an accurate approximation of the nonlocal term the commonly known monotone schemes can be applied
[
18
,
2
,
23
]
.
In particular,
[
18
]
follows the idea of approximating the flux at each cell interface, such that the problem can be reduced to a ’local’ flux, which allows to apply monotone numerical schemes.
Although, it should be noted that due to the nonlocality the resulting schemes are not monotone in every argument such that the usual convergence arguments cannot be directly applied.

However, for two-dimensional systems of nonlocal conservation laws, typically numerical approximations based on Lax-Friedrichs-type numerical schemes are considered
[
1
,
14
,
9
,
21
]
.
Here, the difficulty arises when the flux is nonlinear with respect to the local variable
\rho^{k}
as in pedestrian models.
If the flux is linear, more accurate numerical methods have already been studied.
The authors of
[
25
,
24
]
consider numerical schemes based on the characteristics (in the scalar case
K=1
).
In addition, Roe-type schemes have been studied for the nonlocal material flow model (including the system case) in
[
26
,
20
]
.
To the best of the authors’ knowledge a general class of numerical schemes for the equation (
1
), based on monotone schemes and providing sufficient conditions for convergence, has not been studied yet.

Furthermore, error estimates in one spatial dimension have been investigated very recently in a series of papers
[
2
,
3
,
4
]
for different nonlocal models.
In two-dimensions, the only known result follows from
[
3
]
via a splitting argument.
However, the flux considered in
[
3
]
does not include most of the pedestrians models, since direct space and time dependencies are not present.

During this work, we start from a semi-discrete approach, derive a first order scheme and concentrate on appropriate numerical flux functions.
Using these flux functions together with a more accurate approximation of the semi-discrete form allows for a straight forward extension to higher order numerical schemes.
In one spatial dimension higher order schemes for nonlocal conservation laws have been studied in
[
16
,
10
]
and in two-dimensions, e.g. using finite differences, in
[
9
,
21
]
.

The goal of this work is threefold: We will provide a rigorous study of
i)
the general flux (
1
),
ii)
sufficient assumptions on numerical schemes for convergence and
iii)
the error estimates for the developed schemes.
Similar to the one-dimensional case, we prove that any monotone numerical flux (satisfying one additional technical assumption) applied to an approximated flux at the cell interfaces guarantees the convergence towards the weak entropy solutions of (
1
).
In particular, this rigorously enables the use of Godunov-type fluxes for a nonlocal, nonlinear flux.

The structure of this work is thereby such that after introducing the most important definitions und assumptions in Section
2
, we present a class of numerical schemes for the nonlocal multidimensional conservation laws (
1
) in Section
3
.
They are based on monotone numerical fluxes for (local) conservation laws.
After discussing suitable approximations of the nonlocal term we state general assumptions on the numerical flux function to ensure convergence to a weak entropy solution.
The remaining section is devoted to the convergence proof.
In Section
4
, we provide an error estimate for the numerical approximation of the weak entropy solution.
Here, we first examine a Kuznetsov-type lemma that provides uniqueness of the weak entropy solution of (
1
), too.
Then, we establish the convergence rate of the defined class of numerical schemes.
Section
5
presents convergence studies based on numerical examples.
Thereby, we compare the accuracy of different schemes that are included in the introduced class.
We close this work by a conclusion in Section
6
.

Definition 2.1

(Weak entropy solution)
.

Let

\boldsymbol{\rho}_{0}\in L^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{R}^{K}\right)

is a Kružkov solution to the Cauchy problem

\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\rho^{k}+\textnormal{div}_{\textbf{x}}\,\boldsymbol{f}^{k}\left(t,\textbf{x},\rho^{k},\boldsymbol{\mathcal{{R}}}[\boldsymbol{\rho}]\left(t,\textbf{x}\right)\right)=0\quad&&,(t,\textbf{x})\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{2},\\
&\rho^{k}(0,\textbf{x})=\rho^{k}_{0}(\textbf{x})&&,\textbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.\end{aligned}\end{cases}

(3)

setting
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{{R}}}[\boldsymbol{\rho}]\left(t,\textbf{x}\right)=\boldsymbol{\eta}*\boldsymbol{\rho}
, i.e.

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left[\left|\rho^{k}-\kappa\right|\partial_{t}\phi+\textnormal{sgn}(\rho^{k}-\kappa)\left(\boldsymbol{f}^{k}\left(t,\textbf{x},\rho^{k},\boldsymbol{\mathcal{{R}}}[\boldsymbol{\rho}]\left(t,\textbf{x}\right)\right)-\boldsymbol{f}^{k}\left(t,\textbf{x},\kappa,\boldsymbol{\mathcal{{R}}}[\boldsymbol{\rho}]\left(t,\textbf{x}\right)\right)\right)\raisebox{1.29167pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{$\bullet$}}\nabla_{\textbf{x}}\phi\right]d\textbf{x}\;dt

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\textnormal{sgn}(\rho^{k}-\kappa)\textnormal{div}_{\textbf{x}}\,\boldsymbol{f}^{k}\left(t,\textbf{x},\kappa,\boldsymbol{\mathcal{{R}}}[\boldsymbol{\rho}]\left(t,\textbf{x}\right)\right)\phi d\textbf{x}\;dt+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|\rho_{0}^{k}(\textbf{x})-\kappa\right|\phi(0,\textbf{x})d\textbf{x}\geq 0.

holds for all
\kappa\in\mathbb{R}
and
\phi\in\mathbf{C}_{c}^{\infty}
([0,T)
\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
;
\mathbb{R}^{+}
)
.

3
A class of monotone-based first order schemes

We discretize equidistantly in space and time. In space, the step sizes are
\Delta x_{1}
and
\Delta x_{2}
, which correspond to the two dimensions, such that we have rectangular cells
C_{i,j}=[x_{1}^{i-\frac{1}{2}},x_{1}^{i+\frac{1}{2}})\times[x_{2}^{j-\frac{1}{2}},x_{2}^{j+\frac{1}{2}})
with centered nodes
\textbf{x}^{i,j}=(x_{1}^{i},x_{2}^{j})=(i\Delta x_{1},j\Delta x_{2})
,
i,j\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Additionally, the time is discretized with step size
\Delta t
, such that
t^{n}=n\Delta t
with
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
We define the following piecewise constant finite volume approximation

\rho_{\Delta}^{k}\left(t,\textbf{x}\right)=\sum_{i,j\in\mathbb{Z}}\rho_{i,j}^{k,n}\;{\chi}_{[t^{n},t^{n+1})\times C_{i,j}}\left(t,\textbf{x}\right)

for
k=1,\ldots,K
.
Here, the cell-averages
\rho_{i,j}^{k,n}
over the cells
C_{i,j}
at time
t^{n}
are similarly defined as the initial data which is given for
k=1,\ldots,K
by

\rho_{i,j}^{k,0}=\frac{1}{\Delta x_{1}\Delta x_{2}}\int_{C_{i,j}}\rho_{0}^{k}\left(\textbf{x}\right)d\textbf{x}.

(4)

We start by describing the approximation of the nonlocal term.
For a fixed time
t^{n}
, the nonlocal terms
\boldsymbol{\eta}*\boldsymbol{\rho}
are approximated at a cell interface
\textbf{x}^{i+\frac{1}{2},j}
or
\textbf{x}^{i,j+\frac{1}{2}}
and denoted by
\text{\kern 0.0pt\hbox to0.0pt{$R$\hss}\kern 0.29999pt$R$}_{i+\frac{1}{2},j}^{n}
and
\text{\kern 0.0pt\hbox to0.0pt{$R$\hss}\kern 0.29999pt$R$}_{i,j+\frac{1}{2}}^{n}
, respectively.
Since the components of
\boldsymbol{\eta}*\boldsymbol{\rho}
consist of different convolutions, i.e.
\left(\boldsymbol{\eta}*\boldsymbol{\rho}\right)_{m}\left(t,\textbf{x}\right)=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\left(\eta^{m,k}*\rho^{k}\left(t\right)\right)\left(\textbf{x}\right)
,
m=1,\ldots,M
, every convolution is approximated by composite midpoint rule

\displaystyle\left(\eta^{m,k}*\rho_{\Delta}^{k}(t^{n})\right)\bigl(\textbf{x}^{i+\frac{1}{2},j}\bigr)

\displaystyle\approx\Delta x_{1}\Delta x_{2}\sum_{p,q\in\mathbb{Z}}\eta^{m,k}\Bigl((p+\frac{1}{2})\Delta x_{1},q\Delta x_{2}\Bigr)\rho^{k,n}_{i-p,j-q}\eqqcolon\left(\text{\kern 0.0pt\hbox to0.0pt{$R$\hss}\kern 0.29999pt$R$}_{i+\frac{1}{2},j}^{n}\right)_{m},

(5a)

\displaystyle\left(\eta^{m,k}*\rho_{\Delta}^{k}(t^{n})\right)\left(\textbf{x}^{i,j+\frac{1}{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\approx\Delta x_{1}\Delta x_{2}\sum_{p,q\in\mathbb{Z}}\eta^{m,k}\left(p\Delta x_{1},(q+\frac{1}{2})\Delta x_{2}\right)\rho^{k,n}_{i-p,j-q}\eqqcolon\left(\text{\kern 0.0pt\hbox to0.0pt{$R$\hss}\kern 0.29999pt$R$}_{i,j+\frac{1}{2}}^{n}\right)_{m}.

(5b)

By this approximation the flux of (
1
) reduces to
\widetilde{f}^{k}_{1}(\rho^{k};t^{n},\textbf{x}_{i+\frac{1}{2},j})=f_{1}^{k}(t^{n},\textbf{x}_{i+\frac{1}{2},j},\rho^{k},\text{\kern 0.0pt\hbox to0.0pt{$R$\hss}\kern 0.29999pt$R$}_{i+\frac{1}{2},j}^{n})
in the first component, which we denote by
f_{1,n}^{i+\frac{1}{2},j}(\rho^{k})
and analogously for the second component.
It is important to note that at the cell interface
\textbf{x}^{i+\frac{1}{2},j}
or
\textbf{x}^{i,j+\frac{1}{2}}
the flux is continuous in
x
and the same holds for the convolutions due to the regularity assumptions on the kernel.
The only discontinuities arise from the state variable
\rho^{k}
.

Remark 3.1

(Decoupling of the system)
.

Since the system (
1
) is coupled solely by the convolution terms, the system based on the reduced fluxes
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{f}}^{k}
,
k=1,\ldots,K

is decoupled at a fixed time
t^{n}
.
Hence, the solution for the next time step can be computed based on the reduced fluxes by a finite volume scheme for every equation independently.
For simplicity and to improve readability, we will often omit the superscript
k
in the following proofs.

Now, a first order finite volume scheme for (
1
) can be similarly derived to the local case:
Integrating (
1
) over the cells
C_{i,j}
and dividing by
\Delta x_{1}\Delta x_{2}
provides a semi-discretization for the cell-averages.
Approximating the integral of the flux term over the boundaries by the midpoint rule
and using a forward Euler step in time while discretizing the convolution terms as in (
5a
) and (
5b
), results in a first order scheme given by

\displaystyle\rho_{i,j}^{k,n+1}=\rho_{i,j}^{k,n}

\displaystyle-\lambda_{1}\left(F_{1,k,n}^{i+\frac{1}{2},j}\left(\rho_{i,j}^{k,n},\rho_{i+1,j}^{k,n}\right)-F_{1,k,n}^{i-\frac{1}{2},j}\left(\rho_{i-1,j}^{k,n},\rho_{i,j}^{k,n}\right)\right)

(6)

\displaystyle-\lambda_{2}\left(F_{2,k,n}^{i,j+\frac{1}{2}}\left(\rho_{i,j}^{k,n},\rho_{i,j+1}^{k,n}\right)-F_{2,k,n}^{i,j-\frac{1}{2}}\left(\rho_{i,j-1}^{k,n},\rho_{i,j}^{k,n}\right)\right)

with
\lambda_{1}=\frac{\Delta t}{\Delta x_{1}}
and
\lambda_{2}=\frac{\Delta t}{\Delta x_{2}}
and suitable numerical flux functions
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
which we specify in the following.
We note that from a semi-discretization higher order schemes can be derived in a similar fashion using appropriate approximations of the integrals over the cell boundaries and of the time integration. The resulting schemes do not use dimensional splitting as well and require consistent numerical fluxes, as considered in this work.

The assumptions
(\boldsymbol{f})
and
(\boldsymbol{\eta})
concerning the regularity in space of the flux and nonlocal term allow us to extend the ideas in the one-dimensional case from

]

Similarly, we now pose some general assumptions on the numerical flux functions which guarantee the convergence towards weak entropy solutions of (

Although the schemes are not monotone in every argument due to the nonlocal terms in (

Definition 3.2

(Montone-based numerical flux function)
.

Let the nonlocal terms be approximated as in (
5a
) and (
5b
) resulting in the approximate fluxes
f_{1,n}^{i+\frac{1}{2},j}(\rho^{k})\coloneq f_{1}^{k}\left(t^{n},\textbf{x}_{i+\frac{1}{2},j},\rho^{k},\text{\kern 0.0pt\hbox to0.0pt{$R$\hss}\kern 0.30664pt$R$}_{i+\frac{1}{2},j}^{n}\right)
and

f_{2,n}^{i,j+\frac{1}{2}}(\rho^{k})

\coloneq f_{2}^{k}\left(t^{n},\textbf{x}_{i,j+\frac{1}{2}},\rho^{k},\text{\kern 0.0pt\hbox to0.0pt{$R$\hss}\kern 0.30664pt$R$}_{i,j+\frac{1}{2}}^{n}\right)
.
The numerical flux functions
F_{1,k,n}^{i+\frac{1}{2},j},F_{2,k,n}^{i,j+\frac{1}{2}}
,
i,j\in\mathbb{Z},n\in\mathbb{N},k=1,\ldots,K
satisfy the following conditions

1.

Consistency, i.e. for
\rho\in\mathcal{I}_{k}

\displaystyle F_{1,k,n}^{i+\frac{1}{2},j}\left(\rho,\rho\right)=f_{1,n}^{i+\frac{1}{2},j}(\rho),\quad F_{2,k,n}^{i,j+\frac{1}{2}}\left(\rho,\rho\right)=f_{2,n}^{i,j+\frac{1}{2}}(\rho).

2.

The maps
\left(a,b\right)\mapsto F_{1,k,n}^{i+\frac{1}{2},j}\left(a,b\right)
and
\left(a,b\right)\mapsto F_{2,k,n}^{i,j+\frac{1}{2}}\left(a,b\right)
from
\mathcal{I}_{k}\times\mathcal{I}_{k}
to
\mathbb{R}
are nondecreasing in the first argument and nonincreasing in the second.

3.

Lipschitz continuity in the two arguments with Lipschitz constants

L_{1,1},L_{1,2}>0
for
F_{1,k,n}^{i+\frac{1}{2},j}
and
L_{2,1},L_{2,2}>0
for
F_{2,k,n}^{i,j+\frac{1}{2}}
.

4.

There exist constants
L_{1,1}^{\prime},L_{1,2}^{\prime},L_{2,1}^{\prime},L_{2,2}^{\prime}>0
such that for
\rho_{i-1,j}^{k,n},\rho_{i,j}^{k,n},\rho_{i,j-1}^{k,n}\in\mathcal{I}_{k}

\displaystyle\left|F_{1,k,n}^{i-\frac{1}{2},j+1}\left(\rho_{i-1,j}^{k,n},\rho_{i,j}^{k,n}\right)-F_{1,k,n}^{i-\frac{1}{2},j+1}\left(\rho_{i,j}^{k,n},\rho_{i,j}^{k,n}\right)-F_{1,k,n}^{i-\frac{1}{2},j}\left(\rho_{i-1,j}^{k,n},\rho_{i,j}^{k,n}\right)+F_{1,k,n}^{i-\frac{1}{2},j}\left(\rho_{i,j}^{k,n},\rho_{i,j}^{k,n}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq(\Delta x_{2}\left|\rho_{i,j}^{n}-\rho_{i-1,j}^{n}\right|+\mathcal{M}|\rho_{i,j}^{n}|\Delta x_{2}^{2})L_{1,1}^{\prime}

\displaystyle\widetilde{{\scalebox{1.5}{$\varepsilon$}}}_{2}\coloneq-\lambda_{1}\int_{Q_{T}}\sum_{i,j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{n=0}^{N_{T}-1}\left(f_{1}^{k}\left(t^{n},\textbf{x}^{i+\frac{1}{2},j},\varrho^{k}(s,\textbf{y}),\text{\kern 0.0pt\hbox to0.0pt{$R$\hss}\kern 0.29999pt$R$}_{i+\frac{1}{2},j}^{n}\right)-f_{1}^{k}\left(t^{n},\textbf{x}^{i-\frac{1}{2},j},\varrho^{k}(s,\textbf{y}),\text{\kern 0.0pt\hbox to0.0pt{$R$\hss}\kern 0.29999pt$R$}_{i-\frac{1}{2},j}^{n}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\left(\textnormal{sgn}(\rho_{i,j}^{k,n+1}-\varrho^{k}(s,\textbf{y}))-\textnormal{sgn}(\rho_{i,j}^{k,n}-\varrho^{k}(s,\textbf{y}))\right)\left(\int_{C_{i,j}}\phi\left(s,\textbf{y},t^{n+1},\textbf{x}\right)d\textbf{x}\right)ds\,d\textbf{y}

is first treated as in
[
3
, pp. 3383-3384]
(summation by parts, adding a zero) and further splitted into
\widetilde{{\scalebox{1.5}{$\varepsilon$}}}_{21},\widetilde{{\scalebox{1.5}{$\varepsilon$}}}_{22},\widetilde{{\scalebox{1.5}{$\varepsilon$}}}_{23},\widetilde{{\scalebox{1.5}{$\varepsilon$}}}_{24},\widetilde{{\scalebox{1.5}{$\varepsilon$}}}_{25}
.
Thus, we need to estimated these terms. In our setting we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{{\scalebox{1.5}{$\varepsilon$}}}_{21}=-\lambda_{1}\int_{Q_{T}}\sum_{i,j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{n=1}^{N_{T}}\left(f_{1}^{k}\left(t^{n-1},\textbf{x}^{i+\frac{1}{2},j},\varrho^{k}(s,\textbf{y}),\text{\kern 0.0pt\hbox to0.0pt{$R$\hss}\kern 0.29999pt$R$}_{i+\frac{1}{2},j}^{n-1}\right)-f_{1}^{k}\left(t^{n-1},\textbf{x}^{i-\frac{1}{2},j},\varrho^{k}(s,\textbf{y}),\text{\kern 0.0pt\hbox to0.0pt{$R$\hss}\kern 0.29999pt$R$}_{i-\frac{1}{2},j}^{n-1}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\textnormal{sgn}(\rho_{i,j}^{k,n+1}-\varrho^{k}(s,\textbf{y}))\left(\int_{C_{i,j}}\phi\left(s,\textbf{y},t^{n},\textbf{x}\right)-\phi\left(s,\textbf{y},t^{n+1},\textbf{x}\right)d\textbf{x}\right)ds\,d\textbf{y}.

Again, first estimating the difference of the fluxes yields

\displaystyle\left|\widetilde{{\scalebox{1.5}{$\varepsilon$}}}_{21}\right|

\displaystyle\leq c_{4}\lambda_{1}\Delta x_{1}\int_{Q_{T}}\left|\varrho^{k}(s,\textbf{y})\right|\sum_{i,j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{n=1}^{N_{T}}\left|\int_{C_{i,j}}\phi\left(s,\textbf{y},t^{n},\textbf{x}\right)-\phi\left(s,\textbf{y},t^{n+1},\textbf{x}\right)d\textbf{x}\right|ds\,d\textbf{y}

\displaystyle=c_{4}\Delta t\int_{Q_{T}}\left|\varrho^{k}(s,\textbf{y})\right|\sum_{n=1}^{N_{T}}\left|\omega_{\varepsilon_{0}}\left(s-t^{n}\right)-\omega_{\varepsilon_{0}}\left(s-t^{n+1}\right)\right|ds\,d\textbf{y}\leq c_{4}\|\varrho^{k}\|_{L^{1}(Q_{T})}\frac{\Delta t}{\varepsilon_{0}},

since the discrete BV-norm on the time mesh of the test function
\omega_{\varepsilon_{0}}
does not exceed the continuous BV-norm.
Moreover,

\displaystyle\left|\widetilde{{\scalebox{1.5}{$\varepsilon$}}}_{22}+\widetilde{{\scalebox{1.5}{$\varepsilon$}}}_{23}\right|\leq\lambda_{1}\int_{Q_{T}}\sum_{i,j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{n=1}^{N_{T}}\Bigl|

\displaystyle f_{1}^{k}\left(t^{n},\textbf{x}^{i+\frac{1}{2},j},\varrho^{k}(s,\textbf{y}),\text{\kern 0.0pt\hbox to0.0pt{$R$\hss}\kern 0.29999pt$R$}_{i+\frac{1}{2},j}^{n}\right)-f_{1}^{k}\left(t^{n},\textbf{x}^{i-\frac{1}{2},j},\varrho^{k}(s,\textbf{y}),\text{\kern 0.0pt\hbox to0.0pt{$R$\hss}\kern 0.29999pt$R$}_{i-\frac{1}{2},j}^{n}\right)

\displaystyle-f_{1}^{k}\left(t^{n-1},\textbf{x}^{i+\frac{1}{2},j},\varrho^{k}(s,\textbf{y}),\text{\kern 0.0pt\hbox to0.0pt{$R$\hss}\kern 0.29999pt$R$}_{i+\frac{1}{2},j}^{n-1}\right)+f_{1}^{k}\left(t^{n-1},\textbf{x}^{i-\frac{1}{2},j},\varrho^{k}(s,\textbf{y}),\text{\kern 0.0pt\hbox to0.0pt{$R$\hss}\kern 0.29999pt$R$}_{i-\frac{1}{2},j}^{n-1}\right)\Bigr|

\displaystyle\times\int_{C_{i,j}}\phi\left(s,\textbf{y},t^{n+1},\textbf{x}\right)d\textbf{x}\;ds\,d\textbf{y}.

Thus, we again need an estimate on the flux terms. Successively applying the mean value theorem we obtain

\displaystyle\Bigl|

\displaystyle f_{1}^{k}\left(t^{n},\textbf{x}^{i+\frac{1}{2},j},\varrho^{k}(s,\textbf{y}),\text{\kern 0.0pt\hbox to0.0pt{$R$\hss}\kern 0.29999pt$R$}_{i+\frac{1}{2},j}^{n}\right)-f_{1}^{k}\left(t^{n},\textbf{x}^{i-\frac{1}{2},j},\varrho^{k}(s,\textbf{y}),\text{\kern 0.0pt\hbox to0.0pt{$R$\hss}\kern 0.29999pt$R$}_{i-\frac{1}{2},j}^{n}\right)

\displaystyle-f_{1}^{k}\left(t^{n-1},\textbf{x}^{i+\frac{1}{2},j},\varrho^{k}(s,\textbf{y}),\text{\kern 0.0pt\hbox to0.0pt{$R$\hss}\kern 0.29999pt$R$}_{i+\frac{1}{2},j}^{n-1}\right)+f_{1}^{k}\left(t^{n-1},\textbf{x}^{i-\frac{1}{2},j},\varrho^{k}(s,\textbf{y}),\text{\kern 0.0pt\hbox to0.0pt{$R$\hss}\kern 0.29999pt$R$}_{i-\frac{1}{2},j}^{n-1}\right)\Bigr|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\;c_{5}\left|\varrho^{k}(s,\textbf{y})\right|\Delta x_{1}(\Delta t+\Delta x_{1})

and hence

\displaystyle\left|\widetilde{{\scalebox{1.5}{$\varepsilon$}}}_{22}+\widetilde{{\scalebox{1.5}{$\varepsilon$}}}_{23}\right|

\displaystyle\leq c_{5}(\Delta t+\Delta x_{1})\int_{Q_{T}}\left|\varrho^{k}(s,\textbf{y})\right|\sum_{i,j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{n=1}^{N_{T}}\int_{C_{i,j}}\phi\left(s,\textbf{y},t^{n+1},\textbf{x}\right)\Delta t\,d\textbf{x}\;ds\,d\textbf{y}

\displaystyle\leq c_{5}(\Delta t+\Delta x_{1})\|\varrho^{k}\|_{L^{1}(Q_{T})}\mathcal{O}\left(1+\Delta t\right)=\mathcal{O}\left(\Delta t+\Delta x_{1}\right),

where an estimate similar to (
52
) was used.
The estimates for
\widetilde{{\scalebox{1.5}{$\varepsilon$}}}_{24},\widetilde{{\scalebox{1.5}{$\varepsilon$}}}_{25}
follow similarily and we have proven
A_{3}^{1}+\Lambda_{3}^{\prime 1}=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\Delta x_{1}}{\varepsilon}+\frac{\Delta t}{\varepsilon_{0}}+\Delta x_{1}+\Delta t\right)
. Consequently, with the analogue estimates for the
x_{2}
-directions
, we have estimated all the terms, achieving the result that needed to be established.
∎

6
Conclusion

In this work, we have shown the convergence of schemes for two-dimensional nonlocal conservation laws.
In particular, we have proven that well-known monotone schemes for local conservation laws can be applied such as Godunov- or Lax-Friedrichs-type schemes.
It should be noted that the resulting schemes are not monotone in every argument due to the nonlocal term.
In addition, we have provided an error estimate for the considered schemes which is of magnitude
\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{\Delta t})
.

Even though our results can be adapted to a scheme with dimensional splitting, we concentrated on those without dimensional splitting because this allows for a straightforward extension to higher order schemes.
In particular, to guarantee the positivity preservation and, possibly, a maximum principle for suitable nonlocal conservation laws
it is important that the numerical fluxes satisfies those for a first order scheme, as we have shown here.
We will investigate higher order approximations based on the here presented class of numerical schemes in a follow-up work.

Analytical Solution of the Nonlinear Boltzmann Equation with Non-isotropic Scatterings
Jin Hu

Abstract
An exact analytical solution to the nonlinear relativistic Boltzmann Equation for a massless gas with a non-isotropic cross section is given in a homogeneous spacetime. By employing a trial solution, we construct a set of nonlinear coupled equations for scalar moments and solve this set exactly. Our analytical solution with nontrivial scattering angle dependence contained can be mapped onto the BKW solution of a homogeneous nonrelativistic gas of Maxwell molecules. Furthermore, we demonstrate the existence of a feasible region determined by physical requirements. The analytical solution with parameters within the feasible region admits a stable fixed point corresponding to the equilibrium solution of the Boltzmann equation.

Boltzmann equation
—

The relativistic Boltzmann equation provides a description of the space-time evolution of the one-particle distribution for the non-equilibrium gases,

\displaystyle p\cdot\partial f(x^{\mu},p^{\mu})=C[f],

There is one further comment on the analytical solution we have obtained. Despite dealing with non-isotropic scattering cross sections, our analysis centers on solutions that maintain momentum isotropy. Typically, given an arbitrary initial condition for the distribution function (with or without momentum isotropy), the non-isotropic scatterings would lead to anisotropic momentum moments. In this case, additional irreducible moments must be considered. When the momentum isotropy is achieved through particle collisions, the above analytical solution starts to describe the subsequent evolution. For isotropic scatterings, the non-equilibrium evolution is governed by the scalar energy moments if the initial distribution itself is isotropic in momentum.

Summary and outlook
— In this work, we present an exact analytical solution to the nonlinear Boltzmann Equation for a massless gas with spatial homogeneity and momentum isotropy. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first analytical solution to the relativistic Boltzmann equation with non-isotropic scatterings. Furthermore, we show that this analytical solution is physical within a feasible region specified by Eqs.(
23
) and (
24
). Also, the physical solution admits a stable fixed point, which can be mapped onto an equilibrium solution of the Boltzmann equation. A critical approximation in our work is that the scattering cross section is independent of particle momentum, which follows from the fact that the equation for the scalar moments cannot be solved recursively in an analytical way without this simplification. In the special cases where momentum can be estimated with some typical physical energy scales, our solution can provide a complete description of the non-equilibrium evolution of dilute gases. By preserving the nontrivial dependence on scattering anisotropy, our results can be viewed as a nontrivial extension to
Bazow et al. 2016a
;
Bazow et al. 2016b
. It is noteworthy that incorporating the Friedmann-Lemaitre-Robertson-Walker (FLRW) geometry into our framework is rather straightforward.

There are possible extensions to the present research. First, this new analytical solution can be fully utilized to testify tothe numerical precision of the solvers for the relativistic Boltzmann equation and hydrodynamic equations. Second, although there are numerous numerical frameworks for solving the relativistic Boltzmann equation, a formally simple yet physically motivated analytical solution is expected to provide deep insights into the understanding of nonequilibrium phenomena such as equilibration and hydrodynamization. We hope our results can play an important role. Thirdly, we could attempt to seek more meaningful analytical solutions based on the present one. One may relax some conditions to take a step forward. For instance, the study performed here can be extended to anisotropic, inhomogeneous, or massive systems. Last but not least, the stability analysis on the analytical solution may also be performed to see if this solution exhibits attractor behavior.

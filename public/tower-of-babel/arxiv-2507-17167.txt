Continued fractions with large prime partial quotients Thanks: This research is supported by the ARC Discovery Project 200100994. The third named author is a Leverhulme Early Career Research Fellow.
Gerardo González Robert, Mumtaz Hussain, Benjamin Ward, Lauren White

Abstract
. We determine the Lebesgue measure and Hausdorff dimension of various sets of real numbers with infinitely many partial quotients that are both large and prime, thus extending the well-known theorems by Łuczak (1997) and Huang-Wu-Xu (2020). To this end, we obtain new asymptotics on the tail end of the almost prime zeta function. Our results include some recent work by Schindler-Zweimüller (2023).



1.
Introduction

Continued fraction theory is a cornerstone in Diophantine approximation. Within the theory of Diophantine approximation, one of the first results we are likely to encounter is Dirichlet’s Theorem.

Theorem 1.1

(Dirichlet’s Theorem)
.

Let
x\in\mathbb{R}
.
For any
N\in\mathbb{N}
there exists a pair
(p,q)\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{N}
such that

\left|x-\tfrac{p}{q}\right|<\frac{1}{qN}\,,\quad\text{ and }\,\quad 0<q\leq N\,.

An immediate implication is the following corollary.

Corollary 1.2

(Dirichlet’s Corollary)
.

Let
x\in\mathbb{R}\backslash\mathbb{Q}
. Then there exists infinitely many pairs
(p,q)\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{N}
such that

\left|x-\frac{p}{q}\right|<\frac{1}{q^{2}}\,.

The results of Dirichlet are insightful in that they tell us that any irrational number has rational points within some prescribed neighbourhood. However, we are no closer to knowing what these rational numbers look like. It also does not tell us whether the constant of
1
appearing on the right-hand side of the equations can be improved or not. The numbers for which Dirichlet’s Corollary can be improved by an arbitrarily small constant are called
well approximable
, and the numbers in the complementary set are called
badly approximable
. The set of points for which the constant
1
appearing in Dirichlet’s theorem can be improved is called the set of
Dirichlet improvable numbers
. Studying these sets is when the power of continued fraction theory kicks in.

Take an irrational number
x\in(0,1)
with continued fraction expansion
x=[a_{1}(x),a_{2}(x),\ldots]
and let
(p_{n})_{n\geq 1}
and
(q_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be the sequence of numerators and denominators of its convergents.
The natural numbers
a_{1}(x),a_{2}(x),\ldots
are the
partial quotients
of
x
. For any
x\in(0,1)
, the partial quotients can be constructed via the Gauss map. It is well-known
[
25
, Theorems 9 & 13]
that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
we have

\frac{1}{(a_{n+1}+2)q_{n}^{2}}<\left|x-\frac{p_{n}}{q_{n}}\right|<\frac{1}{a_{n+1}q_{n}^{2}}\,.

Hence, many approximation properties of
x
are determined by its partial quotients.
For example, if
(a_{n}(x))_{n\geq 1}
is bounded, the inequalities above along with other basic approximation properties of the convergents imply that
x
is badly approximable.
Conversely, if the partial quotients are unbounded, then
x
is well-approximable. Furthermore, it was proven by Davenport and Schmidt
[
7
]
that a number is Dirichlet improvable if and only if its partial quotients are bounded. Thus, the set of Dirichlet improvable numbers is precisely the set of badly approximable numbers and rational numbers.

These relationships between the properties of partial quotients and sets of a Diophantine interest motivate the following setup. For function
\varphi:\mathbb{N}\to[1,\infty)
define

\mathcal{E}_{1}(\varphi):=\left\{x\in[0,1]:a_{n}(x)\geq\varphi(n)\ \text{for i.m.}\,\,n\in\mathbb{N}\right\}\,.

In the above definition and throughout, “i.m.” means “infinitely many”.
The set
\mathcal{E}_{1}(\varphi)
and its variations have been vastly studied, with one of the first results on its size due to Borel and Bernstein, see, for example,
[
3
, Theorem 1.1]
.
Let
\mathfrak{m}
denote the Lebesgue measure.

Theorem 1.3

(Borel-Bernstein Theorem)
.

Let
\varphi:\mathbb{N}\to[1,\infty)
. Then

\mathfrak{m}(\mathcal{E}_{1}(\varphi))=\begin{cases}0\quad\text{\rm if}\quad\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\varphi(n)}<\infty\,,\\
1\quad\text{\rm if}\quad\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\varphi(n)}=\infty\,.\\
\end{cases}

The Borel-Bernstein Theorem applied to the function
\varphi(n)=n\log n
tells us that the set of badly approximable numbers, and thus also the set of Dirichlet-improvable numbers by
[
7
]
, is Lebesgue-null. Furthermore, the complement (the set of well approximable numbers) has full Lebesgue measure.

Given a non-increasing function
\psi:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, Kleinbock and Wadleigh
[
27
]
defined the set of
\psi
-Dirichlet improvable numbers
as the set of those
x\in\mathbb{R}
such that for every large
N\in\mathbb{N}
there is a pair
(p,q)\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{N}
satisfying

\left|qx-p\right|<\psi(N)\quad\text{ and }\quad q\leq N\,.

Remarkably, this set can also be characterised by the properties of the partial quotients. Kleinbock and Wadleigh
[
27
, Lemma 2.2]
showed that a number
x
is not
\psi
-Dirichlet improvable if
a_{n}(x)a_{n+1}(x)\geq\tfrac{1}{q_{n}\psi(q_{n})}-1
for infinitely many
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
This leads to the study of the metric properties of the set

\mathcal{E}_{\ell}(\varphi):=\left\{x\in[0,1]:a_{n}(x)\ldots a_{n+\ell-1}(x)\geq\varphi(n)\ \text{for i.m.}\,\,n\in\mathbb{N}\right\}\,.

Kleinbock and Wadleigh gave a zero-one law for the Lebesgue measure of
\mathcal{E}_{2}(\varphi)
assuming
\varphi(n)\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
.
As a consequence, they obtained the Lebesgue measure of the set of
\psi
-Dirichlet improvable numbers when
\psi
is non-increasing and
q\psi(q)<1
for large
q\in\mathbb{N}
.
In
[
21
]
, Huang, Wu, and Xu obtained
\mathfrak{m}(\mathcal{E}_{\ell})
for
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 2}
without the limit hypothesis on
\varphi
.

Theorem 1.4

(
[
21
, Theorem 1.5]
)
.

Let
\varphi:\mathbb{N}\to[2,\infty)
be a positive function. Then

\mathfrak{m}(\mathcal{E}_{\ell}(\varphi))=\begin{cases}0\quad\text{\rm if }\quad\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\left(\log\varphi(n)\right)^{\ell-1}}{\varphi(n)}<\infty\,,\\
1\quad\text{\rm if }\quad\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\left(\log\varphi(n)\right)^{\ell-1}}{\varphi(n)}=\infty\,.\\
\end{cases}

When we are in the convergence case, that is,
\mathcal{E}_{\ell}(\varphi)
is Lebesgue-null, we appeal to the finer notion of size, for instance, the Hausdorff dimension, denoted by
\dim_{\mathrm{H}}
to distinguish between them.
One of the first results in this regard is due to Łuczak.
Considering functions
\varphi_{b,c}:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
given by
\varphi_{b,c}(n)=c^{b^{n}}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, with
b,c>1
fixed the following was proven.

Theorem 1.5

(
[
13
,
29
]
)
.

For every
c,b>1
, we have

\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\left\{x\in[0,1]:\forall n\in\mathbb{N}\quad a_{n}(x)\geq\varphi_{b,c}(n)\right\}=\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\mathcal{E}_{1}(\varphi_{b,c})=\frac{1}{b+1}\,.

The Hausdorff dimension theory for general functions
\varphi
was obtained for the case
\ell=1
by Wang and Wu
[
41
]
and it was later generalised to arbitrary
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
by Huang, Wu, and Xu
[
21
]
.
For any function
\varphi:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, define the non-negative quantities
B_{\varphi}
and
b_{\varphi}
by

\log B_{\varphi}:=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log\varphi(n)}{n}\,\quad\text{ and }\quad\log b_{\varphi}:=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log\log\varphi(n)}{n}\,.

Theorem 1.6

(
[
21
, Theorem 1.7]
)
.

Define the sequence of functions
(f_{\ell})_{\ell\geq 1}
from
(0,1)
to
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
by

f_{1}(s)=s\,\quad\text{ and }\quad f_{\ell}(s)=\frac{sf_{\ell-1}(s)}{1-s+f_{\ell-1}(s)}\,,\,\text{ for }\,\,\ell\geq 2\,.

Let
\varphi:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
be arbitrary.
Then, for every
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
,

\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\mathcal{E}_{\ell}(\varphi)=\begin{cases}1\,,\quad\hskip 199.16928pt\text{\rm if }\quad B_{\varphi}=1\,,\\
\inf\{s:P(T,-f_{\ell}(s)\log B_{\varphi}-s\log|T^{\prime}|)\leq 0\}\,,\,\,\,\,\,\quad\text{\rm if }\quad 1<B_{\varphi}<\infty\,,\\
\frac{1}{b_{\varphi}+1}\,,\quad\hskip 184.9429pt\text{\rm if }\quad B_{\varphi}=\infty\,.\end{cases}

The definition of the pressure function
P(T,\cdot)
in Theorem
1.6
is given in Subsection
2.4
. Without going into technical details, in our setup, the pressure function gives the Hausdorff dimension number as a continuous function of
B_{\varphi}
. It is
1
when
B_{\varphi}\to 1
and
1/2
when
B_{\varphi}\to\infty
, see Proposition
2.15
.

the size of infinitely many partial quotients growing at least or at most at a certain rate

,

the size of products of partial quotients from arithmetic progressions

39
]
.

Alternatively, one can impose global conditions on the partial quotients. For instance, one can:

•

restrict all the partial quotients to
\{1,2,\ldots,M\}
for some
M\in N

[
18
]
,

•

restrict all the partial quotients to
\{M,M+1,\ldots\}
for some
M\in\mathbb{N}

[
5
]
,

•

restrict all even partial quotients to
\{1,\ldots,M\}
for some
M\in\mathbb{N}

[
16
]
,

•

restrict all partial quotients to arithmetic progressions, prime numbers, squares, powers of 2 or 3, and lacunary sequences, see
[
4
, Table 1]
, see also
[
6
]
.

In this article we consider in some sense a combination of the two settings, by studying the set of continued fractions with infinitely many large prime partial quotients.
Firstly, we define the following functions: for
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
a_{n}^{\prime}:(0,1)\setminus\mathbb{Q}\to\mathbb{N}_{0}
be given by

a_{n}^{\prime}(x)=\begin{cases}a_{n}(x)\quad\text{\rm if }\ a_{n}(x)\in\mathcal{P}\,,\\
0\qquad\quad\,\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

where
\mathcal{P}
denotes the set of prime numbers.
For any positive function
\varphi:\mathbb{N}\to[0,\infty)
, define the set

\mathcal{E}_{\ell}^{\prime}(\varphi):=\left\{x\in[0,1):a_{n}^{\prime}(x)\cdots a_{n+\ell-1}^{\prime}(x)\geq\varphi(n)\text{ for i. m. }n\in\mathbb{N}\right\}.

Studying the set of continued fractions with restrictions involving the set of prime numbers is not new, see for example
[

,
30
,
37
]
. In particular, Schindler and Zweimüller gave a Borel-Bernstein Theorem for
\mathcal{E}_{1}^{\prime}(\varphi)

[
37
, Theorem 2.1]
.
We extend upon their work and completely determine the size of
\mathcal{E}_{\ell}^{\prime}(\varphi)
. In particular we calculate
\mathfrak{m}(\mathcal{E}_{\ell}^{\prime}(\varphi))
, see Theorem
1.8
, and
\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\mathcal{E}_{\ell}^{\prime}(\varphi)
, Theorem
1.10
, thus providing the prime analogue of
[
21
, Theorem 1.7]
.

1.1.
The prime and almost prime zeta functions

The most famously known Dirichlet series, the Riemann zeta function
\zeta
, is defined for
s>1
as

\zeta(s):=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{1}{n^{s}}\,.

The study of the Riemann zeta function is a vast area of mathematics that we simply cannot give due discussion in this article. See, for example,
[
9
,
40
]
for introductions to the topic and references within. Other well-known forms of Dirichlet series is the prime zeta function and the almost prime zeta function. Let
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
and
\Omega(\ell)
denote the set of
\ell
-primes. That is, the set of positive integers with at most
\ell
(not necessarily distinct) prime factors. For example,
\Omega(1)=\mathcal{P}
, and
2^{2},6\in\Omega(2)
. For fixed
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
the almost prime zeta function is defined as

P_{\ell}(s;M):=\sum_{k\in\Omega(\ell):\,k\geq M}\frac{1}{k^{s}}\,.

In the special case
\ell=1
,
P_{1}(s;M)
is the prime zeta function. Various results on the almost prime zeta function have been proven; see for example,
[
14
]
. In particular, the relations

P(s;1)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{\mu(k)}{k}\log\zeta(ks)\,,\quad\text{ and }\quad\zeta(s)=1+\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}}P_{\ell}(s;1)\,,

where
\mu
is the Möbius function, are well-known properties of such functions
[
15
]
.

In
[
37
]
, Schindler and Zweimüller give an asymptotic for the tail of the prime zeta function at
s=2
(Proposition
2.6
).
In this article, we determine the asymptotic behaviour of the tail of the almost prime zeta function (Theorem
1.7
).
Although the case
s=2
of this statement is crucial in one of our main results in continued fraction theory, it is interesting on its own right.

1.2.
Main results

Before we state our results, we briefly summarise some frequently used notation. Throughout,
\mathcal{P}
is the set of prime numbers. For any
\mathbf{a}=(a_{n})_{n\geq 1}\in\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}
, and
r,s\in\mathbb{N}
with
r\leq s
, write

\mathbf{a}[r,s]:=a_{r}a_{r+1}\ldots a_{s}
.
We extend the notation to finite words in an obvious way.
Next, for any
(x_{n})_{n\geq 1}
and
(y_{n})_{n\geq 1}
of positive real number, we write

x_{n}\ll y_{n}
if for some constant
\kappa>0
and every large
n
we have
x_{n}\leq\kappa y_{n}
; if
\kappa
depends on some parameter
\lambda
, we write
x_{n}\ll_{\lambda}y_{n}
.
Finally, we write
x_{n}\sim y_{n}
when
\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{x_{n}}{y_{n}}=1
.

Our first result describes the asymptotic behaviour of the Prime Zeta Function

Theorem 1.7
.

For any
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, real
s>1
, and every sufficiently large
M
(depending on
\ell
) we have

P_{\ell}(s;M)\asymp_{\ell}\frac{(\log\log M)^{\ell-1}}{M^{s-1}\log M}.

We then use the case
s=2
of Theorem
1.7
to calculate
\mathfrak{m}(\mathcal{E}_{\ell}^{\prime}(\varphi))
.

Theorem 1.8
.

For any
\varphi:\mathbb{N}\to[3,\infty)
, we have

\mathfrak{m}(\mathcal{E}_{\ell}^{\prime}(\varphi))=\begin{cases}0\quad\text{\rm if}\quad\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{(\log\log\varphi(n))^{\ell-1}}{\varphi(n)\log\varphi(n)}<\infty\,,\\
1\quad\text{\rm if}\quad\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{(\log\log\varphi(n))^{\ell-1}}{\varphi(n)\log\varphi(n)}=\infty\,.\end{cases}

Note that for
\ell=1
we recover the Borel-Bernstein-type result by Schindler and Zweimüller
[
37
, Theorem 2.1]
.
Also, for
\ell\geq 2
, we cannot remove the restriction
\varphi(n)\geq 3
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Certainly, for the constant function
\varphi(n)=e
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the series in Theorem
1.8
is convergent.
However, by virtue of Birkhoff’s ergodic theorem, every finite block of natural numbers appears infinitely many times in the continued fraction expansion of almost every irrational
x\in(0,1)
, so
\mathfrak{m}(\mathcal{E}_{\ell}^{\prime}(\varphi))=1
,

On certain occasions, one would prefer to have the function
\varphi
dependent on the size of the denominator of the convergent,
q_{n}
, rather than
n
. Consider the set

\mathcal{F}_{\ell}(\varphi):=\left\{x\in[0,1):a_{n}^{\prime}(x)\cdots a_{n+\ell-1}^{\prime}(x)\geq\varphi(q_{n})\text{ for i.m.}\ n\in\mathbb{N}\right\}.

The exponential growth of
(q_{n})_{n\geq 1}
and the existence of the Lévy-Khinchin constant allow us to derive the next corollary.
The omitted proof follows almost verbatim that of
[
21
, Corollary 1.6]
.

Corollary 1.9
.

For any
\varphi:\mathbb{N}\to[3,\infty)
,

\mathfrak{m}(\mathcal{F}_{\ell}(\varphi))=\begin{cases}0\quad\text{\rm if}\quad\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{(\log\log\varphi(n))^{\ell-1}}{n\varphi(n)\log\varphi(n)}<\infty\,,\\
1\quad\text{\rm if}\quad\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{(\log\log\varphi(n))^{\ell-1}}{n\varphi(n)\log\varphi(n)}=\infty\,.\end{cases}

Our last result tells us that the Hausdorff dimension of
\mathcal{E}_{\ell}^{\prime}(\varphi)
is not affected by the primality restriction.

Theorem 1.10
.

For any function
\varphi:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
and
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

(1)

\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\mathcal{E}_{\ell}^{\prime}(\varphi)=\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\mathcal{E}_{\ell}(\varphi).

An intermediate step in the proof of Theorem
1.10
is a prime version of Łuczak’s Theorem (Theorem
1.5
).
For any function
\varphi:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}
, define

\mathcal{E}_{\ell}^{\prime\prime}(\varphi):=\left\{x\in[0,1]:a_{n}^{\prime}(x)\ldots a_{n+\ell-1}^{\prime}(x)\geq\varphi(n)\,\text{ for all }\,n\in\mathbb{N}\right\}.

Theorem 1.11
.

For any
b,c>1
, we have

\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\mathcal{E}_{\ell}^{\prime\prime}(\varphi_{c,b})=\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\mathcal{E}_{\ell}^{\prime}(\varphi_{c,b})=\frac{1}{b+1}.

Our techniques suffice to show the prime analogue of a general theorem by Hussain and Shulga
[
24
]
.
To state it, for
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
and
A_{0},\ldots,A_{\ell-1}>1
, let
S_{\ell}^{\prime}(A_{0},\ldots,A_{\ell-1})
be the set of irrational numbers
x\in[0,1]
such that for infinitely many
n\in\mathbb{N}
we have

A_{0}^{n}\leq a_{n}^{\prime}(x)\leq 2A_{0}^{n},\;A_{1}^{n}\leq a_{n+1}(x)\leq 2A_{1}^{n},\;\ldots,\;A_{\ell-1}^{n}\leq a_{n+\ell-1}(x)\leq 2A_{\ell-1}^{n}.

Also, let
S_{\ell}^{\prime}(A_{0},\ldots,A_{\ell-1})
be the set of irrational set of irrational numbers
x\in[0,1]
such that for infinitely many
n\in\mathbb{N}
we have

A_{0}^{n}\leq a_{n}^{\prime}(x)\leq 2A_{0}^{n},\;A_{1}^{n}\leq a_{n+1}^{\prime}(x)\leq 2A_{1}^{n},\;\ldots,\;A_{\ell-1}^{n}\leq a_{n+\ell-1}^{\prime}(x)\leq 2A_{\ell-1}^{n}.

Proposition 1.12
.

For any
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
and any
A_{0},\ldots,A_{\ell-1}>1
, we have

\dim_{\mathrm{H}}S_{\ell}(A_{0},\ldots,A_{\ell-1})=\dim_{\mathrm{H}}S_{\ell}^{\prime}(A_{0},\ldots,A_{\ell-1}).

Preliminaries

2.1.
The distribution of primes

In this section, we recall two fundamental results on the distribution of primes: Mertens’ Second Theorem and the Prime Number Theorem (PNT).
For a proof of the former, see, for example
[
32
, Theorem 2.7.(d)]
.
A proof of the latter can be found in
[
32
, Section 6.2]
.

Theorem 2.1

(Second Mertens’ Theorem)
.

There is a constant
L
such that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
p\in\mathcal{P}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p}=\log\log(x)+L+O\left(\frac{1}{\log x}\right)\text{ as }x\to\infty.

For any
x>0
, write

\pi(x):=\#\left\{p\in\mathcal{P}:p\leq x\right\}.

Theorem 2.2

(Prime Number Theorem)
.

We have
\pi(n)\sim\frac{n}{\log n}
.

The PNT allows us to estimate the number of primes in large intervals.

Proposition 2.3
.

For any
\gamma>1
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
c_{n}(\gamma)
be determined by

\#\left(\mathcal{P}\cap\left[\gamma^{n},2\gamma^{n}\right]\right)=c_{n}(\gamma)\frac{n\log\gamma}{\gamma^{n}}.

Then,
c_{n}(\gamma)\to 1
as
n\to\infty
.

Regarding primes in small intervals, we state a recent theorem by Bordignon, Johnston, and Starichkova.

Proposition 2.4
.

[
1
, Lemma 15]

If
x>e^{20}
, then
[0.999x,x)\cap\mathcal{P}\neq\varnothing
.

A quantitative refinement of PNT was shown by Rosser in 1941.

Proposition 2.5

(
[
36
, Theorem 29]
)
.

If
x\geq 55
, then

\frac{x}{2+\log x}<\pi(x)<\frac{x}{-4+\log x}.

The following proposition, which provides an asymptotic on the summation of the reciprocals of squared primes bounded from below, can be deduced from the PNT and Karamata’s Theorem for slowly varying functions
[
34
, Theorem 0.6]
. It was already noted by Schindler and Zweimüller
[
37
, p. 267]
.

Proposition 2.6
.

For
M\in\mathbb{N}
sufficiently large, we have that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p>M\\
\,p\in\mathcal{P}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p^{2}}\asymp\frac{1}{M\log M}\,.

2.2.
Continued fractions

In this section, we recall classical results on continued fractions.
Their proofs can be found in any standard reference on the topic, such as
[
25
]
or
[
17
]
.

For any real number
y
, let
[y]
be the integer part of
y
.
The Gauss map
T:[0,1)\to[0,1)
, which is intimately connected to continued fractions, is defined by

\forall x\in[0,1),\quad T(x)=\begin{cases}x^{-1}-[x^{-1}],&\text{\rm if }\quad x\neq 0,\\
0,&\text{\rm if }\quad x=0.\end{cases}

Let
a_{1}:(0,1)\to\mathbb{N}
be given by
a_{1}(x)=[x^{-1}]
and, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 2}
and
x\in(0,1)
such that
T^{n-1}(x)\neq 0
, put
a_{n}(x)=a_{1}(T^{n-1}(x))
.
Then, we have

x=[a_{1}(x),a_{2}(x),a_{3}(x),\ldots]:=\cfrac{1}{a_{1}(x)+\cfrac{1}{a_{2}(x)+\ddots}}.

(see, for example,
[
10
, Chapter 2]
).
For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the
n
th convergent
of
x
is the quotient

\frac{p_{n}}{q_{n}}:=[a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}],

where
p_{n},q_{n}\in\mathbb{N}
are coprime.
The sequences
(p_{n})_{n\geq 1}
and
(q_{n})_{n\geq 1}
satisfy

\displaystyle p_{-1}=1,\,p_{0}=0,\,

\displaystyle\text{ and }\,p_{n}=a_{n}p_{n-1}+p_{n-2}\,\text{ for }\,n\in\mathbb{N},

\displaystyle q_{-1}=0,\,q_{0}=1,\,

\displaystyle\text{ and }\,q_{n}=a_{n}q_{n-1}+q_{n-2}\,\text{ for }\,n\in\mathbb{N}.

2.3.
Hausdorff dimension

We will provide a definition for the Hausdorff dimension of a set and some of its basic properties.
For more details on this subject, we refer the reader to
[
11
]
.
We begin by defining the Hausdorff Measure, recalling that a
\delta
-cover of a set
F\subseteq\mathbb{R}
is a countable collection of sets with diameter less than
\delta
that cover
F
.

Definition 2.10

(
[
11
, p. 44-45]
)
.

Suppose
F
is a subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
s\geq 0
. For each
\delta>0
, we define

\mathcal{H}_{\delta}^{s}(F)=\inf\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\diam(U_{i})^{s}:\{U_{i}\}\ \text{is a}\ \delta-\text{cover of}\ F\right\}.

Then the Hausdorff measure of the set is given by

(2)

\mathcal{H}^{s}(F)=\lim_{\delta\to 0}\mathcal{H}^{s}_{\delta}(F).

Furthermore, the Hausdorff Dimension of a set
F
is given by

\dim_{\mathrm{H}}F=\inf\{s\geq 0:\mathcal{H}^{s}(F)=0\}=\sup\{s:\mathcal{H}^{s}(F)=\infty\}\,.

The following properties follow immediately from this definition.

Proposition 2.11

(
[
11
, p.48]
)
.

If
E\subset F
, then
\dim_{\mathrm{H}}E\leq\dim_{\mathrm{H}}F
. Furthermore, if
F_{1},F_{2},\ldots
is a countable collection of subsets of
\mathbb{R}
, then

\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}F_{i}=\sup_{1\leq i<\infty}\dim_{\mathrm{H}}F_{i}.

The following two results give us tools which can be used to estimate the lower bound of the Hausdorff dimension of certain sets. The first result is the Mass Distribution Principle (MDP).

Theorem 2.12
.

Let
\mu
be a mass distribution on a set
F\subset\mathcal{R}
and suppose that for some
s>0
, there are numbers
c>0
and
\varepsilon>0
such that

\mu(U)\leq c\diam(U)^{s}

for all intervals
U
with
\diam(U)\leq\varepsilon
. Then
\mathcal{H}^{s}(F)\geq\mu(F)/c
and
s\leq\dim_{\mathrm{H}}F
.

Lemma
2.13
below gives us a consequence of the Mass Distribution Principle when applied to certain Cantor sets (for a proof, see
[
11
, Example 4.6]
).

Lemma 2.13
.

Let
(E_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be a sequence of finite unions of disjoint compact intervals contained in
[0,1]
such that
E_{n}\supseteq E_{n+1}
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Suppose that each set
E_{n}
is formed by at least
m_{n}\geq 2
intervals with maximum widths of the intervals tending to zero as
n\to\infty
. Suppose the intervals for each
n
th level are separated by gaps of length at least
\varepsilon_{n}>0
with
(\varepsilon_{n})_{n\geq 1}
decreasing. Then

\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{N}}E_{n}\geq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log(m_{1}\cdots m_{n})}{-\log m_{n+1}\varepsilon_{n+1}}.

2.4.
Pressure functions and dimensional numbers

We use pressure functions to succinctly express the Hausdorff dimension of certain sets determined by continued fractions.
In this section, we introduce some notation and recall basic properties of pressure functions.
We only consider pressure functions in the context of continued fractions and the Gauss map; for an overview of the general theory, we refer the reader to
[
31
]

Let
\varphi:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
be any function.
For

and

S_{n}(\varphi;x):=\varphi(x)+\varphi(T(x))+\ldots+\varphi\left(T^{n-1}(x)\right).

For any non-empty subset
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathbb{N}

X_{\mathcal{A}}:=\left\{x\in[0,1):a_{n}(x)\in\mathcal{A}\text{ for all }n\in\mathbb{N}\right\}.

It is well known
[
31
, Lemma 2.1.2]
that the limit

(3)

P_{\mathcal{A}}(T,\varphi):=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\left(\sum_{\mathbf{a}\in\mathcal{A}^{n}}\sup_{x\in I_{n}(\mathbf{a})\cap X_{\mathcal{A}}}\exp\left(S_{n}(\varphi,x)\right)\right)

exists. We call
P_{\mathcal{A}}(T,\varphi)
the
pressure function
of the
potential

\varphi
. Define

\Var_{n}(\varphi):=\sup\left\{|\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)|:I_{n}(x)=I_{n}(y)\right\}.

to be the
n
th variation
of
\varphi
. When evaluating the pressure function of the potential
\varphi
the
n
th variation is particularly useful, as shown by the following proposition.

Proposition 2.14

(
[
28
, Proposition 2.4]
)
.

Let
\varphi:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
be such that

\Var_{1}(\varphi)<\infty\;\text{ and }\;\lim_{n\to\infty}\Var_{n}(\varphi)=0.

Then, the following statements are true:

1.

For every non-empty
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathbb{N}
, the supremum in (
3
) can be replaced with the evaluation at any
x\in I_{n}(\mathbf{a})\cap X_{\mathcal{A}}
;

2.

We have

P_{\mathbb{N}}(T,\varphi)=\sup\left\{P_{\mathcal{A}}(T,\varphi):\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathbb{N}\;\text{\rm is finite}\right\}.

Given any real
B>1
and
f:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
continuous, define for each
s\in[0,1]
the potential

\varphi(f,s;x):=-f(s)\log B-s\log|T^{\prime}(x)|.

Since for every
x\in[0,1)
where
T^{n}
is differentiable satisfies

(T^{n})^{\prime}(x)=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{(xq_{n-1}-p_{n-1})^{2}},

Proposition
2.14

P_{\mathbb{N}}(T,\varphi(f,s))=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\left(\sum_{\mathbf{a}\in A^{n}}\frac{1}{B^{nf(s)}q_{n}^{2s}(\mathbf{a})}\right).

We apply these observations on the sequence of continuous and non-decreasing functions

defined in Theorem
1.6
.
For any

and

, define

\displaystyle t_{B}^{(\ell)}(\mathcal{A})

\displaystyle:=\inf\{s\geq 0:P_{\mathcal{A}}(T;-f_{\ell}(s)\log B-s\log|T^{\prime}|)\leq 0\},

\displaystyle t_{B}^{(\ell)}(\mathcal{A},n)

\displaystyle:=\inf\left\{s\geq 0:\sum_{\mathbf{a}\in\mathcal{A}^{n}}\frac{1}{B^{nf_{\ell}(s)}q_{n}^{2s}}\leq 0\right\}.

Whenever
\mathcal{A}=\{1,\ldots,M\}
for some

t_{B}^{(\ell)}(M):=t_{B}^{(\ell)}(\{1,\ldots,M\})\quad\text{ and }\quad t_{B}^{(\ell)}(M,n):=t_{B}^{(\ell)}(\{1,\ldots,M\},n).

See

for a proof of the next proposition.

Proposition 2.15
.

Let
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
be arbitrary.

1.

For every real
B>1
we have

t_{B}^{(\ell)}(\mathbb{N})=\sup\left\{t_{B}^{(\ell)}(\mathcal{A}):\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathbb{N}\text{ is finite}\right\}=\lim_{n\to\infty}t_{B}^{(\ell)}(\mathbb{N},n)=\lim_{M\to\infty}t_{B}^{(\ell)}(M).

2.

For every real
B>1
and

\lim_{n\to\infty}t_{B}^{(\ell)}(M,n)=t_{B}^{(\ell)}(M).

3.

The next limits hold:

\lim_{B\to 1}t_{B}^{(\ell)}(\mathbb{N})=1\;\text{ and }\;\lim_{B\to\infty}t_{B}^{(\ell)}(\mathbb{N})=\frac{1}{2}.

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.7

Let us recall part of Karamata’s Theorem on regularly varying functions. For a proof and the full statement, see, for example,
[
34
, Theorem 0.6]
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
F:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 1}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
be a measurable function for which there is some
\rho\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\forall x>1\quad\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{F(tx)}{F(t)}=x^{\rho}.

If
\rho<-1
, then
\int_{x}^{\infty}F(t)\mathrm{d}t<\infty
and

\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{xF(x)}{\int_{x}^{\infty}F(t)\mathrm{d}t}=-\rho-1.

3.1.
Proof of Theorem
1.7

For notation purposes for any positive integer
r\geq 1
let

S(\ell,M,r,s):=\underset{p_{i}\in\mathcal{P}\,\,p_{i}\geq r}{\sum_{p_{1}\dots p_{\ell}>M:\,}}\,\,\frac{1}{p_{1}^{s}\dots p_{n}^{s}}\,,\quad\text{ and }\quad\widehat{S}(\ell,M,r,s):=\underset{p_{i}\in\mathcal{P}\,\,p_{1}\geq p_{2}\geq\dots\geq p_{\ell}\geq r}{\sum_{p_{1}\dots p_{\ell}>M:\,}}\frac{1}{p_{1}^{s}\dots p_{\ell}^{s}}\,.

Notice that for any
r\in\mathbb{N}

\widehat{S}(\ell,M,r,s)\leq S(\ell,M,r,s)\leq\ell!\widehat{S}(\ell,M,r,s)\,,

with the lower bound being trivial and the upper bound due to the fact that there are
\ell!
ways to order
\ell
prime numbers. Setting
r=1
, by our definition of
S(\ell,M,r,s)
, we will have completed the proof on showing that

S(\ell,M,1,s)\asymp_{\ell}\frac{(\log\log M)^{\ell-1}}{M^{s-1}\log M}\,.

The proof is by induction. We begin by showing the base
\ell=1
case.

Consider an arbitrary
s>2
and define
F_{s}:[1,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
by

\forall x\in[1,\infty)\quad F_{s}(x)=\frac{1}{(x\log x)^{s}}.

Then, for every
x>1
we have

\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{F_{s}(tx)}{F_{s}(t)}=x^{-s}.

As a consequence, since
-s<-1
, Proposition
3.1
implies

\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{xF_{s}(x)}{\int_{x}^{\infty}F_{s}(t)\mathrm{d}t}=s-1.

Therefore, for large
x>1
we have

\int_{x}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{(t\log t)^{s}}\asymp_{s}\frac{1}{x^{s-1}(\log x)^{s}}.

We may now apply the PNT to conclude that for large
N
we have

(4)

\sum_{n\geq N}\frac{1}{p_{n}^{s}}\sim\sum_{n\geq N}\frac{1}{(n\log n)^{s}}\asymp_{s}\frac{1}{N^{s-1}(\log N)^{s}}.

Here and in the following equation only, the subscript on
p
means that
p_{n}
is the
n
th prime number. Later on
p_{n}
will be used to denote our
n
th choice of prime number. Using (
4
), for
M
sufficiently large, we have that

\displaystyle S(1,M,1,s)

\displaystyle=\sum_{p\in\mathcal{P}:\,p>M}\frac{1}{p^{s}}\asymp\sum_{n>\pi(M)}\frac{1}{p_{n}^{s}}

\displaystyle\asymp_{s}\frac{1}{\pi(M)^{s-1}(\log\pi(M))^{s}}\asymp\frac{1}{\left(\frac{M}{\log M}\right)^{s-1}(\log M)^{s}}=\frac{1}{M^{s-1}\log M}

completing the base
\ell=1
case.

Now, let us assume that the result is true for
\ell
and show that it holds for
\ell+1
. We split the inductive step in two, giving an inductive argument for the upper bound and then an inductive argument for the lower bound. The observation that, for any
r\geq 1

(5)

S(\ell,M,r,s)=\sum_{p\in\mathcal{P}:\,p\geq r}\frac{1}{p^{s}}S(\ell-1,\tfrac{M}{p},r,s)\quad\text{ and }\quad\widehat{S}(\ell,M,r,s)=\sum_{p\in\mathcal{P}:\,p\geq r}\frac{1}{p^{s}}\widehat{S}(\ell-1,\tfrac{M}{p},p,s)

is useful in both cases. Our assumption on
M
being sufficiently large is to guarantee that we can apply Theorem
2.1
and Proposition
2.6
. We also require in the
\ell
-induction assumption that
M^{\tfrac{1}{\ell+1}}
is sufficiently large so that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{1}\cdots p_{\ell}\geq M^{\tfrac{1}{1+\ell}}\\
p_{1},\ldots,p_{\ell}\in\mathcal{P}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p_{1}^{s}\cdots p_{\ell}^{s}}\asymp_{\ell}\frac{(\log\log M)^{\ell-1}}{M^{(s-1)\tfrac{1}{\ell+1}}\log M}.

Beginning with the upper bound, for
\ell+1
, we have that

S(\ell+1,M,1,s)\asymp_{\ell}\widehat{S}(\ell+1,M,1,s)=\sum_{p_{\ell+1}\in\mathcal{P}}\frac{1}{p_{\ell+1}^{s}}\widehat{S}\left(\ell,\tfrac{M}{p_{\ell+1}},p_{\ell+1},s\right)\,.

We split this into two summations depending on the size of
p_{\ell+1}
. Namely,

\widehat{S}(\ell+1,M,1,s)=\sum_{p_{\ell+1}\leq M^{\tfrac{1}{\ell+1}}:\,p_{\ell+1}\in\mathcal{P}}\frac{1}{p_{\ell+1}^{s}}\widehat{S}\left(\ell,\tfrac{M}{p_{\ell+1}},p_{\ell+1},s\right)\,+\sum_{p_{\ell+1}>M^{\tfrac{1}{\ell+1}}:\,p_{\ell+1}\in\mathcal{P}}\frac{1}{p_{\ell+1}^{s}}\widehat{S}\left(\ell,\tfrac{M}{p_{\ell+1}},p_{\ell+1},s\right)\,.

Begin with the first sum. Notice that
p_{\ell+1}\leq M^{\tfrac{1}{\ell+1}}
implies that

(6)

\frac{M}{2}\geq\frac{M}{p_{\ell+1}}\geq M^{1-\tfrac{1}{\ell+1}}\geq M^{\tfrac{1}{\ell+1}}\quad\text{ and so }\log\tfrac{M}{p_{\ell+1}}\asymp_{\ell}\log M\,.

So

(7)

\displaystyle\sum_{p_{\ell+1}\leq M^{\tfrac{1}{\ell+1}}:\,p_{\ell+1}\in\mathcal{P}}\frac{1}{p_{\ell+1}^{s}}\widehat{S}\left(\ell,\tfrac{M}{p_{\ell+1}},p_{\ell+1},s\right)

\displaystyle\asymp_{\ell}\sum_{p_{\ell+1}\leq M^{\tfrac{1}{\ell+1}}:\,p_{\ell+1}\in\mathcal{P}}\frac{1}{p_{\ell+1}^{s}}S\left(\ell,\tfrac{M}{p_{\ell+1}},p_{\ell+1},s\right)

(8)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{p_{\ell+1}\leq M^{\tfrac{1}{\ell+1}}:\,p_{\ell+1}\in\mathcal{P}}\frac{1}{p_{\ell+1}^{s}}S\left(\ell,\tfrac{M}{p_{\ell+1}},1,s\right)

\displaystyle\asymp\sum_{p_{\ell+1}\leq M^{\tfrac{1}{\ell+1}}:\,p_{\ell+1}\in\mathcal{P}}\frac{1}{p_{\ell+1}^{s}}\frac{\left(\log\log\tfrac{M}{p_{\ell+1}}\right)^{\ell-1}}{\left(\tfrac{M}{p_{\ell+1}}\right)^{s-1}\log\tfrac{M}{p_{\ell+1}}},

where the last step follows from our inductive assumption, which is applicable by our lower bound in (
6
). Notice that it is only step (
8
) that prevents us from obtaining an asymptotic statement. Using that
\log\tfrac{M}{p_{\ell+1}}\asymp_{\ell}\log M
we have that

(9)

\sum_{p_{\ell+1}\leq M^{\tfrac{1}{\ell+1}}:\,p_{\ell+1}\in\mathcal{P}}\frac{1}{p_{\ell+1}^{s}}\widehat{S}\left(\ell,\tfrac{M}{p_{\ell+1}},p_{\ell+1}\right)\ll_{\ell}\sum_{p_{\ell+1}\leq M^{\tfrac{1}{\ell+1}}:\,p_{\ell+1}\in\mathcal{P}}\frac{1}{p_{\ell+1}}\frac{\left(\log\log M\right)^{\ell-1}}{M^{s-1}\log M}\,,

and so, by applying Second Mertens Theorem (Lemma
2.1
) we have that

(10)

\sum_{p_{\ell+1}\leq M^{\tfrac{1}{\ell+1}}:\,p_{\ell+1}\in\mathcal{P}}\frac{1}{p_{\ell+1}^{s}}\widehat{S}\left(\ell,\tfrac{M}{p_{\ell+1}},p_{\ell+1}\right)\ll_{\ell}\frac{\left(\log\log M\right)^{\ell}}{M^{s-1}\log M}\,.

For the second part of the summation, since we have that
p_{\ell+1}>M^{\tfrac{1}{\ell+1}}
and the fact that our primes our bounded from below by
p_{\ell+1}
, we can write

p_{1},p_{2},\dots,p_{\ell+1}>M^{\tfrac{1}{\ell+1}}\,.

So, multiplying together, we have that
p_{1}\ldots p_{\ell+1}>M
. Thus the restriction on the product of the primes becomes redundant, leading us to

\displaystyle\sum_{p_{\ell+1}>M^{\tfrac{1}{\ell+1}}:\,p_{\ell+1}\in\mathcal{P}}\frac{1}{p_{\ell+1}^{s}}\widehat{S}\left(\ell,\tfrac{M}{p_{\ell+1}},p_{\ell+1},s\right)

\displaystyle\asymp_{\ell}\sum_{p_{\ell+1}>M^{\tfrac{1}{\ell+1}}:\,p_{\ell+1}\in\mathcal{P}}\frac{1}{p_{\ell+1}^{s}}S\left(\ell,\tfrac{M}{p_{\ell+1}},p_{\ell+1},s\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{p>M^{\tfrac{1}{\ell+1}}:\,p\in\mathcal{P}}\frac{1}{p^{s}}\right)^{\ell+1}

\displaystyle\overset{\text{case $\ell=1$}}{\asymp_{\ell}}\left(\frac{1}{M^{(s-1)\tfrac{1}{\ell+1}}\log M^{\tfrac{1}{\ell+1}}}\right)^{\ell+1}

(11)

\displaystyle\asymp_{\ell}\frac{1}{M^{s-1}(\log M)^{\ell+1}}\,.

Combining (

) and (

) we get our required upper bound. That is,

S(\ell+1,M,1,s)\ll_{\ell}\frac{\left(\log\log M\right)^{\ell}}{M^{s-1}\log M}+\frac{1}{M^{s-1}(\log M)^{\ell+1}}\ll\frac{\left(\log\log M\right)^{\ell}}{M^{s-1}\log M}\,.

In order to show the lower bound for
\ell+1
, using the previous observation (
5
) we have that

(12)

S(\ell+1,M,1,s)\geq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq M^{\tfrac{1}{\ell+1}}\\
p\in\mathcal{P}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p^{s}}S(\ell,\tfrac{M}{p},1,s)\,.

This is similar to our calculation in the upper bound, except this time we no longer have step (
8
) since our prime numbers are already bounded from below by
1
. So following steps (
7
),(

) and (

) we get the asymptotic

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq M^{\tfrac{1}{\ell+1}}\\
p\in\mathcal{P}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p^{s}}S(\ell,\tfrac{M}{p},1,s)\asymp_{\ell}\frac{(\log\log M)^{\ell}}{M^{s-1}\log M}

Hence combining with (

S(\ell+1,M,1,s)\gg_{\ell}\frac{(\log\log M)^{\ell}}{M^{s-1}\log M}

completing the proof.

\ell
fundamental intervals:

B_{n}=\underset{p_{1}\ldots p_{\ell}\geq\varphi(n+1)}{\bigcup_{p_{1},\ldots,p_{\ell}\in\mathcal{P}}}I_{\ell}(p_{1},\ldots,p_{\ell})\,.

The second step is to calculate the Lebesgue measure of each
B_{n}
.
By Proposition
2.8
,

\mathfrak{m}\left(I_{\ell}(p_{1},\ldots,p_{\ell})\right)\asymp_{\ell}\frac{1}{p_{1}^{2}\ldots p_{\ell}^{2}}\,,

so

\mathfrak{m}(B_{n})=\underset{p_{1}\ldots p_{\ell}\geq\varphi(n+1)}{\sum_{p_{1},\ldots,p_{\ell}\in\mathcal{P}}}\frac{1}{p_{1}^{2}\ldots p_{\ell}^{2}}\,.

Since
\varphi(n)\geq T
for
n\geq n_{0}
, Theorem
1.7
yields

\mathfrak{m}(B_{n})\asymp_{\ell}\frac{(\log\log\varphi(n+1))^{\ell-1}}{\varphi(n+1)\log\varphi(n+1)}\,.

Therefore, in view of Lemma
4.1
,

\mathfrak{m}(\mathcal{E}_{\ell}^{\prime}(\varphi))=\begin{cases}0\quad\text{\rm if}\quad\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{(\log\log\varphi(n))^{\ell-1}}{\varphi(n)\log\varphi(n)}<\infty\,,\\
1\quad\text{\rm if}\quad\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{(\log\log\varphi(n))^{\ell-1}}{\varphi(n)\log\varphi(n)}=\infty\,,\end{cases}

completing the proof under our initial assumption.

Now, to show the theorem without assumption (
13
), suppose there is a strictly increasing sequence
(n_{i})_{i\geq 1}
such that
\varphi(n_{i})\leq T
for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
; in particular,

(14)

\forall i\in\mathbb{N}\quad\frac{\left(\log\log\varphi(n_{i})\right)^{\ell-1}}{\varphi(n_{i})\log\varphi(n_{i})}\geq F(T):=\frac{\left(\log\log T\right)^{\ell-1}}{T\log T}\,.

We are, then, in the divergence case:

\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{(\log\log\varphi(n))^{\ell-1}}{\varphi(n)\log\varphi(n)}>\sum_{i\geq 1}\frac{(\log\log\varphi(n_{i}))^{\ell-1}}{\varphi(n_{i})\log\varphi(n_{i})}\geq\sum_{i\geq 1}F(T)=\infty\,.

Define
\psi:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}_{\geq 2}
by

\forall n\in\mathbb{N}\quad\psi(n)=\max\{T,\varphi(n)\}\,.

Then
\mathcal{E}^{{}^{\prime}}_{\ell}(\psi)\subseteq\mathcal{E}^{{}^{\prime}}_{\ell}(\varphi)
.
Moreover,
\psi(n)\geq T
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, so

(15)

\mathfrak{m}(\mathcal{E}^{{}^{\prime}}_{\ell}(\psi))=1\quad\text{\rm if }\quad\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{(\log\log\psi(n))^{\ell-1}}{\psi(n)\log\psi(n)}=\infty\,.

To complete the proof, notice that

\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{(\log\log\psi(n))^{\ell-1}}{\psi(n)\log\psi(n)}>\sum_{i\geq 1}\frac{(\log\log\psi(n_{i}))^{\ell-1}}{\psi(n_{i})\log\psi(n_{i})}=\sum_{i\geq 1}F(T)=\infty\,.

Therefore, by (
15
) and
\mathcal{E}^{{}^{\prime}}_{\ell}(\psi)\subseteq\mathcal{E}^{{}^{\prime}}_{\ell}(\varphi)
, we conclude

\mathfrak{m}(\mathcal{E}^{{}^{\prime}}_{\ell}(\varphi))\geq\mathfrak{m}(\mathcal{E}^{{}^{\prime}}_{\ell}(\psi))=1.

Since we were in the divergence case, the proof is complete.
∎

5.
Hausdorff dimension. Proof of Theorem
1.11

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.11
.
Fix
c,b>1
and recall that
\varphi_{b,c}(n)=c^{b^{n}}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
For any
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, the upper bound for
\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\mathcal{E}_{\ell}^{\prime}(\varphi_{b,c})
is immediate from Theorem
1.5
and
\mathcal{E}_{\ell}^{\prime}(\varphi_{b,c})\subseteq\mathcal{E}_{\ell}(\varphi_{b,c})
.
For the lower bound, we use Lemma
2.13
(cfr.
[
13
]
).
Note that we need to ensure our intervals are non-empty; that is, we need to check that the primes are dense enough ‘fill out’ the levels.

5.1.
Case
\ell=1
.

As mentioned, we only need to show:

(16)

\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\mathcal{E}_{1}^{\prime\prime}(\varphi_{b,c})\geq\frac{1}{b+1}.

We briefly recall the notation from Lemma
2.13
:

i.

E_{k}
is the union of
k
level basic interval of the fractal set
\bigcap_{k\in\mathbb{N}}E_{k}
.

ii.

m_{k}
is a lower bound on the children of any
(k-1)
th level interval.

iii.

\varepsilon_{k}
is a lower bound in the gap between any two
k
th level intervals.

First, for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, consider

\mathcal{I}_{k}:=\prod_{j=1}^{k}\left(\mathcal{P}\cap\left[c^{b^{j}},3c^{b^{j}}\right]\right)\,,

which is non-empty by Bertrand’s Postulate.
Define the set

E_{k}:=\bigcup_{\mathbf{d}=(d_{1},\ldots,d_{k})\in\mathcal{I}_{k}}I_{k}(\mathbf{d})\,,

hence

\bigcap_{k\in\mathbb{N}}E_{k}\subseteq\mathcal{E}_{1}^{\prime\prime}(\varphi_{c,b})\subseteq\mathcal{E}_{1}^{\prime}(\varphi_{c,b})\,.

Next, we look at the integers
m_{k}\geq 2
.
Note that for
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
\mathbf{d}\in\mathcal{I}_{k}
, we have
I_{k}(\mathbf{d})\subset I_{k-1}(\mathbf{f})
if and only if
\mathbf{d}=\mathbf{f}P
for some prime
P
with
c^{b^{k}}\leq P\leq 3c^{b^{k}}
, so we need a lower bound on the number of primes in
[c^{b^{k}},3c^{b^{k}}]
.
For all large
k
, Lemma
2.5
gives

\displaystyle\#\left(\mathcal{P}\cap\left[c^{b^{k}},3c^{b^{k}}\right]\right)

\displaystyle=\pi\left(3c^{b^{k}}\right)-\pi\left(c^{b^{k}}\right)

\displaystyle>\frac{3c^{b^{k}}}{2+\log 3c^{b^{k}}}-\frac{c^{b^{k}}}{-4+\log c^{b^{k}}}

\displaystyle>\frac{3c^{b^{k}}}{2\log c^{b^{k}}}-\frac{c^{b^{k}}}{\log c^{b^{k}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{c^{b^{k}}}{2b^{k}\log c}=:m_{k}.

We now define
\varepsilon_{k}
. Take
\mathbf{d}\in\mathcal{I}_{k-1}
.

For any two primes
c^{b^{k}}<P<R<3c^{b^{k}}
and
k
sufficiently large we trivially have
R-P\geq 2
, so between the fundamental intervals
I_{k}(\mathbf{d}\,P)
and
I_{k}(\mathbf{d}\,R)
is at least one fundamental interval, which has length at least

\displaystyle\mathfrak{m}\left(I_{k}\left(\mathbf{d}\,\left(\left\lfloor 3c^{b^{k}}\right\rfloor+1\right)\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\left(q_{k}\left(\mathbf{d}\,\left(\left\lfloor 3c^{b^{k}}\right\rfloor+1\right)\right)\left(q_{k}\left(\mathbf{d}\,\left(\left\lfloor 3c^{b^{k}}\right\rfloor+1\right)\right)+q_{k-1}(\mathbf{d})\right)\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle\geq\left(2q_{k}^{2}\left(\mathbf{d}\,\left(\left\lfloor 3c^{b^{k}}\right\rfloor+1\right)\right)\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle\geq\left(2\prod_{i=1}^{k-1}(d_{i}+1)^{2}\left(\left\lfloor 3c^{b^{k}}\right\rfloor+1\right)^{2}\right)^{-1}

by Proposition
2.8
and a repeated application of Proposition
2.7
.
i
.

Since

2\prod_{i=1}^{k-1}(d_{i}+1)^{2}\left(\left\lfloor 3c^{b^{k}}\right\rfloor+1\right)^{2}\leq 2^{2k+3}9^{k}c^{2b+2b^{2}+\ldots+2b^{k-1}+2b^{k}}\leq 36^{k+1}c^{2\frac{b^{k+1}-b}{b-1}},

we may pick

\varepsilon_{k}:=36^{-k-1}c^{-2\frac{b^{k+1}-b}{b-1}}.

Then, as
k\to\infty
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\log(m_{1}\cdots m_{k-1})}{-\log(m_{k}\varepsilon_{k})}

\displaystyle=\frac{(b+b^{2}+\ldots+b^{k-1})\log c+O(k^{2})}{-b^{k}\log c+2\frac{b^{k+1}-b}{b-1}\log c+O(k)}

\displaystyle=\frac{b^{k}\frac{\log c}{b-1}(1+o(1))+O(k^{2})}{\frac{2b^{k+1}-2b-b^{k+1}+b^{k}}{b-1}\log c+O(k)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1+o(1)}{b+1+o(1)}\sim\frac{1}{b+1}.

We now conclude (
16
) from Lemma
2.13
.

5.2.
General case.

Take
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 2}
.
For the lower bound, note that
\mathcal{E}_{1}^{\prime\prime}(\varphi_{b,c})\subseteq\mathcal{E}_{\ell}^{\prime\prime}(\varphi_{b,c})\subseteq\mathcal{E}_{\ell}^{{}^{\prime}}(\varphi_{b,c})
, so

(17)

\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\mathcal{E}_{\ell}^{\prime}(\varphi_{b,c})\geq\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\mathcal{E}_{\ell}^{\prime\prime}(\varphi_{b,c})\geq\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\mathcal{E}_{1}^{\prime\prime}(\varphi_{b,c})=\frac{1}{b+1}\,.

As mentioned earlier, the upper bound is obvious from Theorem
1.6
.
Nevertheless, we provide a proof without relying on this result.
Indeed, take any
y\in\mathcal{E}_{\ell}^{{}^{\prime}}(\varphi_{b,c})
. Then there exists an infinite sequence
(n_{j})_{j\geq 1}
such that
a_{n_{j}}^{{}^{\prime}}(y)\ldots a_{n_{j}+\ell-1}^{{}^{\prime}}(y)\geq c^{b^{n_{j}}}
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
. By the geometric mean, we have that

(18)

\max_{0\leq t\leq\ell-1}a_{n_{j}+t}^{{}^{\prime}}(y)\geq c^{\tfrac{b^{n_{j}}}{\ell}}\,.

Let us define for
t\in\{0,1,\ldots,\ell-1\}
the numbers

c_{t}:=c^{\tfrac{1}{\ell b^{t}}}>1\,,\quad\text{ and so }\,\,c_{t}^{b^{n+t}}=c^{\tfrac{b^{n}}{\ell}}\,.

Since the sequence
(n_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is infinite, there exists
0\leq v\leq\ell-1
such that (
18
) occurs at
a_{n_{j_{i}}+v}^{\prime}(y)
for an infinite sequence
(n_{j_{i}})_{i\geq 1}
. Hence, for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
,

a_{n_{j_{i}}+v}^{\prime}(y)\geq c^{\frac{b^{n_{j_{i}}}}{\ell}}=c_{v}^{b^{n_{j_{i}}+v}}\,,

so
y\in\mathcal{E}_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}(\varphi_{b,c_{v}})
. This shows that

\mathcal{E}_{\ell}^{\prime}(\varphi_{b,c})\subseteq\bigcup_{0\leq v\leq\ell-1}\mathcal{E}_{1}^{\prime}(\varphi_{b,c_{v}})\,,

Then, by the countable stability of the Hausdorff dimension (Proposition
2.11
),

(19)

\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\mathcal{E}_{\ell}^{{}^{\prime}}(\varphi_{b,c})\leq\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\bigcup_{0\leq v\leq\ell-1}\mathcal{E}_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}(\varphi_{b,c_{v}})=\max_{0\leq v\leq\ell-1}\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\mathcal{E}_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}(\varphi_{b,c_{v}})=\frac{1}{b+1}\,.

Combining (
17
) and (
19
), we have that

\frac{1}{b+1}\leq\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\mathcal{E}_{\ell}^{\prime\prime}(\varphi_{b,c})\leq\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\mathcal{E}_{\ell}^{\prime}(\varphi_{b,c})\leq\frac{1}{b+1}\,.

This completes the proof for general
\ell
.
∎

6.
Hausdorff dimension. Proof of Theorem
1.10

Fix a natural number
\ell
and a function
\varphi:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
.
By
\mathcal{E}_{\ell}^{\prime}(\varphi)\subseteq\mathcal{E}_{\ell}(\varphi)
, the result holds if
\mathfrak{m}(\mathcal{E}_{\ell}(\varphi))>0
.
If
\mathfrak{m}(\mathcal{E}_{\ell}^{\prime}(\varphi))=0
and
\mathfrak{m}(\mathcal{E}_{\ell}(\varphi))=1
, then

\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{(\log\log\varphi(n))^{\ell-1}}{\varphi(n)\log\varphi(n)}<\infty\;\text{ and }\;\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\left(\log\varphi(n)\right)^{\ell-1}}{\varphi(n)}=\infty

by Theorem
1.8
and Theorem
1.4
respectively.
From the divergence of the second series, we may show that
B_{\varphi}=1
so, by the discussion to come,
\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\mathcal{E}_{\ell}^{\prime}(\varphi)=1
, proves the result.
Thus, we may assume that both
\mathcal{E}_{\ell}^{\prime}(\varphi)
and
\mathcal{E}_{\ell}(\varphi)
are Lebesgue-null sets.
By the monotonicity of the Hausdorff dimension, Theorem
1.10
will be proven once we show

(20)

\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\mathcal{E}_{\ell}^{\prime}(\varphi)\geq\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\mathcal{E}_{\ell}(\varphi).

As usual, most of the work occurs when
1<B_{\varphi}<\infty
.
To solve this case, we first consider a restricted family of functions.

A particular case

.

Lemma 6.1

\mu\left(J_{n_{k+1}-1}(\mathbf{a})\right)=\mu\left(J_{n_{k}}(\mathbf{a}[1,a_{n_{k}}])\right)\left(\frac{1}{B^{s_{n_{k+1}-n_{k}-1}}q^{2}_{m_{k+1}}(\mathbf{b}_{k+1})}\right)^{t_{B}^{(1)}(M,n_{k+1}-n_{k}-1)}.

iii.

If
n_{k}+1\leq n\leq n_{k+1}-2
, then

\mu\left(J_{n}(\mathbf{a})\right)=\sum_{\mathbf{c}\in\{1,\ldots,M\}^{n_{k+1}-n}}\mu\left(J_{n_{k+1}-1}(\mathbf{a}\mathbf{c})\right).

In what follows, we assume
\ell\geq 2
since the proof for
\ell=1
follows closely that in
[
41
]
.
In both cases, the primality restriction gives rise to similar complications.

6.2.
Length and gap estimates

From Proposition
2.4
, we know that for small
0<\lambda<10^{-4}
and every large number
x

((1-\lambda)x,x)
and
(x,(1-\lambda)^{-1}x)

From this observation, the estimates in Subsection
2.2
, and standard computations, we conclude the next result (Proposition
2.4
is used in items
ii
and
iii
, those involving the primality restriction).

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\mathbf{a}\in D_{n}
be arbitrary and
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that
n_{k}+\ell-1\leq n<n_{k+1}+\ell-1
.
The following estimates hold for some absolute constants.

i.

If
n_{k}+\ell-1\leq n<n_{k+1}-1
, then

\diam(J_{n}(\mathbf{a}))\asymp\frac{1}{q_{n}^{2}(\mathbf{a})}.

ii.

If
n=n_{k+1}-1
, then

\diam(J_{n}(\mathbf{a}))\asymp\frac{1}{\alpha_{0}^{n_{k+1}}q_{n_{k+1}-1}^{2}(\mathbf{a})}.

iii.

If
n_{k+1}\leq n\leq n_{k+1}+\ell-3
, then, for
j=n-n_{k+1}
,

\diam(J_{n}(\mathbf{a}))\asymp\frac{1}{\alpha_{j+1}^{n_{k+1}}(\alpha_{0}\cdots\alpha_{j})^{2n_{k+1}}q_{n_{k+1}-1}^{2}(\mathbf{a}[1,n_{k+1}-1])}.

iv.

If
n=n_{k+1}+\ell-2
, then

\diam(J_{n}(\mathbf{a}))\asymp\frac{1}{(B\alpha_{0}\cdots\alpha_{\ell-2})^{n_{k+1}}q_{n_{k+1}-1}^{2}(\mathbf{a}[1,n_{k+1}-1])}.

As an example of one of the calculations, suppose
n=n_{k+1}+j
for
1\leq j<\ell-2
. The others follow similarly. By definition

\displaystyle\diam(J_{n}(\mathbf{a}))

\displaystyle=\diam\left(\underset{a_{n+1}\in\mathcal{P}}{\bigcup_{\alpha_{j}^{n_{k+1}}\leq a_{n+1}\leq 2\alpha_{j}^{n_{k+1}}}}I_{n+1}(\mathbf{a}a_{n+1})\right)

\displaystyle=\diam\left({\bigcup_{q_{1}\leq a_{n+1}\leq q_{2}}}I_{n+1}(\mathbf{a}a_{n+1})\right)\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\,\,\left(\text{$q_{1},q_{2}\in\mathcal{P}$ solve }\begin{array}[]{c}q_{1}=\displaystyle\min_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\{p\geq\alpha_{j}^{n_{k+1}}\}\\
q_{2}=\displaystyle\max_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\{p\leq 2\alpha_{j}^{n_{k+1}}\}\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle\asymp\frac{q_{2}-q_{1}}{\alpha_{j}^{2n_{k+1}}(\alpha_{0}\cdots\alpha_{j})^{2n_{k+1}}q_{n_{k+1}-1}^{2}(\mathbf{a}[1,n_{k+1}-1])}\qquad\text{(by Proposition\penalty\ \ref{Pr:CF:EST}-\ref{Pr:CF:FundamentalIntervals})}

\displaystyle\asymp\frac{1}{\alpha_{j}^{n_{k+1}}(\alpha_{0}\cdots\alpha_{j})^{2n_{k+1}}q_{n_{k+1}-1}^{2}(\mathbf{a}[1,n_{k+1}-1])}\,\,\qquad\text{(by Proposition\penalty\ \ref{PR:ShortInterval}).}

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\mathbf{a}\in D_{n}
, let
G_{n}(\mathbf{a})
denote the minimal gap between
J_{n}(\mathbf{a})
and the nearest fundamental set of level
n
.
In general, our fundamental sets do not coincide with the fundamental intervals defined in
[
21
]
.
Our sets, however, are contained in the latter, so we can use the following gap estimate.

Proposition 6.3

(
[
21
, Lemma 5.3]
)
.

For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\mathbf{a}\in D_{n}
, we have

G_{n}(\mathbf{a})\geq\frac{1}{8M}\diam(I_{n}(\mathbf{a})).

6.3.
Hölder exponents for fundamental sets

Now, we estimate the measure
\mu
of the fundamental intervals in terms of their diameter.

Proposition 6.4
.

For all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\mathbf{a}\in D_{n}
, we have

\mu\left(J_{n}(\mathbf{a})\right)\ll_{\ell}\diam\left(J_{n}(\mathbf{a})\right)^{s(1-\delta)-\delta}.

Proof.

Take
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\mathbf{a}\in D_{n}
.
The implied constants are either absolute or depend on
\ell
.

i.

Case
n=Nl
for some
l\in\{0,\ldots,l_{1}-1\}
. From (
23
), we get
q_{lN}(\mathbf{a})^{2}\geq 2^{lN-1}\geq 2^{\frac{4l}{s\delta}}
; hence,

\displaystyle\mu\left(J_{n}(\mathbf{a})\right)

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{k=1}^{l}\left(\frac{1}{\alpha_{0}^{N}q_{N}^{2}(\mathbf{b}_{1,k})}\right)^{s}

\displaystyle<\prod_{k=1}^{l}\left(\frac{1}{q_{N}^{2}(\mathbf{b}_{1,k})}\right)^{s}

\displaystyle\leq 4^{l}\left(\frac{1}{q_{lN}^{2}(\mathbf{a})}\right)^{s}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{1}{q_{lN}^{2}(\mathbf{a})}\right)^{s(1-\delta)}

\displaystyle\ll\diam\left(J_{n}(\mathbf{a})\right)^{s(1-\delta)}.

ii.

Case
n=n_{1}-1=l_{1}N
.

\displaystyle\mu\left(J_{n}(\mathbf{a})\right)

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{j=1}^{l_{1}}\left(\frac{1}{\alpha_{0}^{N}q_{N}^{2}(\mathbf{b}_{1,j})}\right)^{s}

\displaystyle<\left(\frac{1}{\alpha_{0}^{n_{1}(1-1/N)}}\right)^{s}\left(\frac{1}{q_{n}^{2}(\mathbf{a})}\right)^{s(1-\delta)}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{1}{\alpha_{0}^{n_{1}}q_{n}^{2}(\mathbf{a})}\right)^{s(1-\delta)}

\displaystyle\ll\diam\left(J_{n}(\mathbf{a})\right)^{s(1-\delta)}.

iii.

If
n\in\{n_{1},\ldots,n_{1}+\ell-3\}
, consider
i=n-n_{1}\in\{0,\ldots,\ell-3\}
, then

\displaystyle\mu\left(J_{n}(\mathbf{a})\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\#\mathcal{P}_{0,1}\cdots\#\mathcal{P}_{i,1}}\mu\left(J_{n_{1}-1}(\mathbf{b}_{1})\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{c_{1,1}\cdots c_{i,1}n_{1}^{i}\log\alpha_{0}\cdots\log\alpha_{i}}{(\alpha_{0}\cdots\alpha_{i})^{n_{1}}}\mu\left(J_{n_{1}-1}(\mathbf{b}_{1})\right)

\displaystyle\ll\frac{1}{(\alpha_{0}\cdots\alpha_{i})^{n_{1}(1-\delta)}}\left(\frac{1}{\alpha_{0}^{n_{1}}q_{n}^{2}(\mathbf{b}_{1})}\right)^{s(1-\delta)}

(by (
24
))

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{(\alpha_{0}(\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{i})^{2}\alpha_{i+1})^{sn_{1}(1-\delta)}}\left(\frac{1}{\alpha_{0}^{N}q_{n}^{2}(\mathbf{b}_{1})}\right)^{s(1-\delta)}

(by Lemma
6.1
)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{1}{\alpha_{0}^{N+n_{1}}(\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{i})^{2n_{1}}\alpha_{i+1}^{n_{1}}q_{n}^{2}(\mathbf{b}_{1})}\right)^{s(1-\delta)}

\displaystyle\ll\diam(J_{n_{1}+i}(\mathbf{a}))^{s(1-\delta)}.

iv.

If
n=n_{1}+\ell-2
, then

\displaystyle\mu\left(J_{n}(\mathbf{a})\right)

\displaystyle=\mu(J_{n_{1}-1}(\mathbf{b}_{1}))\frac{1}{\#\mathcal{P}_{0,1}\cdots\#\mathcal{P}_{\ell-1,1}}

\displaystyle<\left(\frac{1}{\alpha_{0}^{n_{1}}q_{n_{1}-1}^{2}(\mathbf{b}_{1})}\right)^{s(1-\delta)}\left(\frac{1}{\alpha_{0}\cdots\alpha_{\ell-1}}\right)^{n_{1}(1-\delta)}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{\alpha_{0}^{n_{1}}q_{n_{1}-1}^{2}(\mathbf{b}_{1})}\right)^{s(1-\delta)}\left(\frac{1}{B\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{\ell-1}}\right)^{n_{1}s(1-\delta)}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{(B\alpha_{0}\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{\ell-1})^{n_{1}}q_{n_{1}-1}^{2}(\mathbf{b}_{1})}\right)^{s(1-\delta)}

\displaystyle\ll\diam(J_{n_{1}+\ell-1}(\mathbf{a}))^{s(1-\delta)}

\displaystyle=\diam\left(J_{n}(\mathbf{a})\right)^{s(1-\delta)}.

v.

If
n=n_{1}+\ell-1
, then

\displaystyle\mu\left(J_{n_{1}+\ell-1}(\mathbf{a})\right)

\displaystyle<\frac{1}{B^{n_{1}(1-\delta)}}\left(\frac{1}{\alpha_{0}^{n_{1}}q_{n_{1}-1}^{2}(\mathbf{b}_{1})}\right)^{s(1-\delta)}

\displaystyle<\left(\frac{1}{B^{2n_{1}}q_{n_{1}-1}^{2}(\mathbf{b}_{1})}\right)^{s(1-\delta)}

\displaystyle\ll\left(\frac{1}{q_{n_{1}+\ell}^{2}(\mathbf{a})}\right)^{s(1-\delta)}

\displaystyle\ll\diam\left(J_{n}(\mathbf{a})\right)^{s(1-\delta)}.

vi.

If
N(l-1)<n<Nl
for some
l\in\{1,2,\ldots,l_{1}\}
, we use Case
a_{k+3}^{1}
in
[
21
]
with obvious modifications to conclude

\mu\left(J_{n}(\mathbf{a})\right)\ll\diam(J_{n}(\mathbf{a}))^{s(1-\delta)}.

vii.

If
n=n_{k+1}-1
for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
, then

\displaystyle\mu\left(J_{n_{k+1}-1}(\mathbf{a})\right)

\displaystyle=\mu\left(J_{n_{k}+\ell}(\mathbf{a}[1,n_{k}+\ell])\right)\prod_{i=1}^{l_{k+1}}\frac{1}{u}\left(\frac{1}{\alpha_{0}^{N}q_{N}^{2}(\mathbf{b}_{k+1,i})}\right)^{s}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{B^{n_{k+1}(1-\delta)}}\mu\left(J_{n_{k}-1}(\mathbf{a}[1,n_{k}-1])\right)\prod_{i=1}^{l_{k+1}}\frac{1}{u}\left(\frac{1}{\alpha_{0}^{N}q_{N}^{2}(\mathbf{b}_{k+1,i})}\right)^{s}

\displaystyle\leq\left[\prod_{t=1}^{k}\left(\frac{1}{B^{n_{t}(1-\delta)}}\prod_{i=1}^{l_{t}}\frac{1}{u}\left(\frac{1}{\alpha_{0}^{N}q_{N}^{2}(\mathbf{b}_{t,i})}\right)^{s}\right)\right]\prod_{i=1}^{l_{k+1}}\frac{1}{u}\left(\frac{1}{\alpha_{0}^{N}q_{N}^{2}(\mathbf{b}_{k+1,i})}\right)^{s}

For each
t\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
, we argue as before to get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{B^{n_{t}(1-\delta)}}\prod_{i=1}^{l_{t}}\frac{1}{u}\left(\frac{1}{\alpha_{0}^{N}q_{N}^{2}(\mathbf{b}_{t,i})}\right)^{s}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{B^{n_{t}(1-\delta)}}\left(\frac{1}{\alpha_{0}^{n_{t}}q_{Nl_{t}}^{2}(\mathbf{b}_{t})}\right)^{s(1-\delta)}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{1}{B^{n_{t}}q_{Nl_{t}}^{2}(\mathbf{b}_{t})}\right)^{s(1-\delta)}

\displaystyle\leq 4^{\ell+1}\left(\frac{1}{q_{l_{t}N+\ell+1}^{2}(\mathbf{b}_{t},a_{n_{t}},\ldots,a_{n_{t}+\ell})}\right)^{s(1-\delta)}.

We also have

\prod_{i=1}^{l_{k+1}}\frac{1}{u}\left(\frac{1}{\alpha_{0}^{N}q_{N}^{2}(\mathbf{b}_{k+1,i})}\right)^{s}\leq\left(\frac{1}{\alpha_{0}^{n_{j}}q_{l_{k+1}N}^{2}(\mathbf{b}_{k+1})}\right)^{s(1-\delta)}.

Also, recalling (
23
), we have

q_{n}^{2}(\mathbf{a})\geq 2^{n-1}\geq 4^{\frac{(k+1)N}{2}}\geq 4^{\frac{(k+1)(\ell+1)}{2}\frac{N}{\ell+1}}\geq 4^{\frac{(k+1)(\ell+1)}{\delta}}

These estimates yield

\displaystyle\mu\left(J_{n_{k+1}-1}(\mathbf{a})\right)

\displaystyle\leq 4^{\ell+1}4^{k+1}\left(\frac{1}{\alpha_{0}^{n_{k+1}}q_{n_{k+1}-1}^{2}(\mathbf{a})}\right)^{s(1-\delta)}

\displaystyle\leq 4^{(k+1)(\ell+1)}\left(\frac{1}{\alpha_{0}^{n_{k+1}}q_{n_{k+1}-1}^{2}(\mathbf{a})}\right)^{s(1-\delta)}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{1}{\alpha_{0}^{n_{k+1}}q_{n_{k+1}-1}^{2}(\mathbf{a})}\right)^{s(1-\delta)-\delta}.

viii.

For the remaining
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we simply combine Case
vii
with the argument used in one of the previous six cases.
For example, when
n=n_{k+1}+\ell-1
, we use cases
vii
and
v
.

∎

With the Hölder exponents of the fundamental sets and the gap estimates at hand, we may apply the argument in
[
21
, Subsection 5.4.4]
with virtually no changes to obtain the exponents for small balls.

Proposition 6.5
.

There is a positive constant depending on
M
and
\ell
such that for every sufficiently small
r>0
, for every
x\in E_{B}
we have

\mu(B(x,r))\ll r^{s(1-\delta)-\delta}.

6.4.
Conclusion of the proof

The above discussion and the Mass Distribution Principle imply that

s(1-\delta)-\delta\leq\dim_{\mathrm{H}}E_{B}\leq\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\mathcal{E}_{\ell}^{\prime}(\varphi_{B}).

We, thus, get (
21
) by letting
\delta\to 0
and
s\to t_{B}^{(\ell)}
.

6.5.
A slight extension

In the above construction, we can specify where the blocks of large prime partial quotients may appear without affecting the dimension.
More precisely, for any
\varphi:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
and any infinite
\mathcal{N}\subseteq\mathbb{N}
define

\mathcal{E}_{\ell}^{\prime}(\varphi;\mathcal{N})=\{x\in[0,1):a_{n}^{\prime}(x)\cdots a_{n+\ell-1}^{\prime}(x)\geq\varphi(n)\text{ for i.m. }n\in\mathcal{N}\}.

Lemma 6.6
.

If
1<B<\infty
and
\mathcal{N}\subseteq\mathbb{N}
is infinite, then

\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\mathcal{E}_{\ell}^{\prime}(\varphi_{B};\mathcal{N})=\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\mathcal{E}_{\ell}^{\prime}(\varphi_{B}).

Proof.

We only discuss the lower bound, since
\mathcal{E}_{\ell}^{\prime}(\varphi_{B},\mathcal{N})\subseteq\mathcal{E}_{\ell}^{\prime}(\varphi_{B})
.
Choose integers
M,N
such that (
23
) holds.
We may further assume that
\mathcal{N}=\{n_{1}<n_{2}<\ldots\}
satisfies

\forall j\in\mathbb{N}\quad\frac{n_{j+1}}{n_{j}}>1.

Define
n_{0}=-\ell
and let
(l_{j})_{j\geq 1}
and
(r_{j})_{j\geq 1}
be the sequences of non-negative integers given by

\forall j\in\mathbb{N}\quad n_{j}-1-n_{j-1}-\ell=l_{j}N+r_{j}\;\text{ and }\;0\leq r_{j}\leq N-1.

Let
\alpha_{0}=\alpha_{0}(B)
,
\ldots
,
\alpha_{\ell-1}=\alpha_{\ell-1}(B)
be as in (
22
).
Let
E_{\ell}(B;\mathcal{N})
be the set of irrational numbers
x\in[0,1)
with the next properties:

1.

For every
j\in\mathbb{N}
,

i.

For all
i\in\{0,1,\ldots,\ell-2\}
,

\alpha_{i}^{n_{j}}\leq a_{n_{j}+i}^{\prime}(x)\leq 2\alpha_{i}^{n_{j}};

ii.

We have

\left(\frac{B}{\alpha_{0}\cdots\alpha_{\ell-2}}\right)^{n_{j}}\leq a_{n_{j}+\ell-1}^{\prime}(x)\leq 2\left(\frac{B}{\alpha_{0}\cdots\alpha_{\ell-2}}\right)^{n_{j}}.

iii.

If
r_{j}>0
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
is such that
n_{j}+\ell+l_{j}N+1\leq n\leq n_{j+1}-1
, then
a_{n}(x)=2
.

2.

All the other partial quotients
a_{n}(x)
satisfy
a_{n}(x)\leq M
.

Next, we define a measure
\mu
just as in the proof of (
20
) except for the numbers
n\in\mathbb{N}
for which
n_{j}<n<n_{j+1}
,
r_{j}>0
and
n_{j}+\ell+l_{j+1}N+1\leq n\leq n_{j+1}-1
for some
j\in\mathbb{N}
.
In such cases, for
\mathbf{a}\in D_{n}
,

\mu\left(J_{n}(\mathbf{a})\right)=\mu\left(J_{n_{j}+\ell+1+l_{j+1}N}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n_{j}+\ell+1+l_{j+1}N})\right).

The argument we used to compute
\dim_{\mathrm{H}}E_{B}
holds in our context too, hence
\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\mathcal{E}_{\ell}(\varphi_{B};\mathcal{N})=\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\mathcal{E}_{\ell}(\varphi_{B})
.
∎

6.6.
Proof of Theorem
1.10

First, assume that
B_{\varphi}=1
.
For any
C>1
, define the infinte set

\mathcal{N}_{C}:=\left\{n\in\mathbb{N}:\frac{\log\varphi(n)}{n}<\log C\right\}

Then,
\mathcal{E}_{\ell}^{\prime}(\varphi_{C};\mathcal{N}_{C})\subseteq\mathcal{E}_{\ell}^{\prime}(\varphi)
and, by Lemma
6.6
,

\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\mathcal{E}_{\ell}(\varphi_{C})=\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\mathcal{E}_{\ell}^{\prime}(\varphi_{C};\mathcal{N}_{C})\leq\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\mathcal{E}_{\ell}^{\prime}(\varphi).

We conclude the result letting
C\to B_{\varphi}
and using Proposition
2.15
.

Now assume that
B_{\varphi}=+\infty
. Suppose that
1\leq b_{\varphi}<+\infty
. Then, for any
\delta>0
we have

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log\log\varphi_{e,b_{\varphi}+\delta}(n)}{n}=\log(b_{\varphi}+\delta)>\log b_{\varphi}=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log\log\varphi(n)}{n}

and, thus,
\mathcal{E}_{\ell}^{\prime\prime}(\varphi_{e,b_{\varphi}+\delta})\subseteq\mathcal{E}_{\ell}^{\prime}(\varphi)
.
Then, Theorem
1.11
gives

\frac{1}{1+b_{\varphi}+\delta}\leq\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\mathcal{E}_{\ell}^{\prime}(\varphi),\>\text{ so }\;\frac{1}{1+b_{\varphi}}\leq\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\mathcal{E}_{\ell}^{\prime}(\varphi).

When
b_{\varphi}=\infty
, the result follows from
\mathcal{E}_{\ell}^{\prime}(\varphi)\subseteq\mathcal{E}_{\ell}(\varphi)
and Theorem
1.6
.

7.
Further research

We close the paper with a list of open problems for further research.

7.1.
Laws of large numbers

For any
\ell,n\in\mathbb{N}
and
x\in(0,1)
irrational, define

S_{n,\ell}(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}(x)\cdots a_{j+\ell-1}(x)\;\text{ and }\;M_{n,\ell}(x)=\max_{1\leq j\leq n}a_{j}(x).

It is well known that the partial quotients are not stochastically independent (with respect to the Gauss measure) nor integrable, so the classical laws of large numbers do not apply.
There are, however, several results in this direction.
Khinchin proved a weak law of large numbers for the sequence of partial quotients
[
26
]
.

Theorem 7.1

(
[
26
, p. 377]
)
.

The sequence
\left(\frac{S_{n,1}}{n\log n}\right)_{n\geq 1}
converges in Lebesgue measure to
\frac{1}{\log 2}
; that is, for every
\varepsilon>0
we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathfrak{m}\left(\left\{x\in(0,1):\left|\frac{S_{n,1}(x)}{n\log n}-\frac{1}{\log 2}\right|\geq\epsilon\right\}\right)=0.

The Borel-Bernstein Theorem tells us that almost every
x\in(0,1)
satisfies
a_{n}(x)\geq n\log^{2}n
infinitely often, so

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{S_{n,1}(x)}{n\log n}=\infty\;\text{ almost everywhere}.

In fact, under some reasonable regularity properties, Philipp
[
33
, Theorem 1]
proved that there is no function
\sigma:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
such that
S_{n}(x)/\sigma(n)
converges almost everywhere to a finite nonzero constant.
Diamond and Vaaler
[
8
]
showed that the convergence almost everywhere occurs after removing the largest term.

Theorem 7.2

(
[
8
, Corollary 1]
)
.

For almost every
x\in(0,1)
, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{S_{n,1}(x)-M_{n,1}(x)}{n\log n}=\frac{1}{\log 2}.

Hu, Hussain, and Yu
[
20
]
obtained analogues of Theorems
7.1
and
7.2
for
\ell=2
.

Theorem 7.3
.

i.

[
20
, Theorem 1.4]
The sequence
\left(\frac{S_{n,2}}{n\log^{2}n}\right)_{n\geq 1}
converges in Lebesgue measure to
\frac{1}{2\log 2}
.

ii.

[
20
, Theorem 1.5]
For almost every
x\in(0,1)
, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{S_{n,2}(x)-M_{n,2}(x)}{n\log^{2}n}=\frac{1}{2\log 2}.

Among other probabilistic results, Schindler and Zweimüller obtained prime analogues of Theorem
7.1
and
7.3
. To state them, for
n,\ell\in\mathbb{N}
and
x\in(0,1)\setminus\mathbb{Q}
define

S_{n,\ell}^{\prime}(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}^{\prime}(x)\cdots a_{j+\ell-1}^{\prime}(x)\;\text{ and }\;M_{n,\ell}^{\prime}(x)=\max_{1\leq j\leq n}a_{j}^{\prime}(x).

Theorem 7.4
.

i.

[
37
, Theorem 3.1]
The sequence
\left(\frac{S_{n,1}^{\prime}}{n\log\log n}\right)_{n\geq 1}
converges in Lebesgue measure to
\log 2
.

ii.

[
37
, Theorem 2.2]
For almost every
x\in(0,1)
, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{S_{n,1}^{\prime}(x)-M_{n,1}^{\prime}(x)}{n\log\log n}=\frac{1}{\log 2}.

Remark
.

Theorem 3.1 in
[
37
]
is stronger than Theorem
7.4
.
i
.
To deduce our statement from it, simply recall that convergence in measure is equivalent to convergence in distribution when the limit is constant
[
35
, Proposition 8.5.2]
.

These developments motivate the following problems.

Problem 1
.

Determine a function
\sigma:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
such that
\left(\frac{S_{n,2}^{\prime}}{\sigma(n)}\right)_{n\geq 1}
converges in measure to a positive finite constant and, for some positive finite constant
\kappa
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{S_{n,2}^{\prime}(x)-M_{n,2}^{\prime}(x)}{\sigma(n)}=\kappa\quad\text{ almost everywhere}.

Naturally, it would be desirable to extend Theorem
7.1
and
7.2
(or Theorem
7.4
) to arbitrary
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
.
This problem, however, poses significant technical difficulties even without the primality restriction.
A key step in the current proofs of Theorem
7.1
-
7.3
is calculating, for a particular function
\varphi
, the Lebesgue measure of the set of irrational numbers
x\in(0,1)
such that for infinitely many
n\in\mathbb{N}
there are integers
i,j
satisfying

1\leq i<j\leq n,\quad\prod_{r=0}^{\ell-1}a_{j+r}(x)\geq\varphi(n),\quad\text{ and }\quad\prod_{r=0}^{\ell-1}a_{i+r}(x)\geq\varphi(n).

The problem for non-decreasing
\varphi
satisfying
\varphi(n)\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
is solved for
\ell=1

[
38
]
,
\ell=2

[
39
]
, and
\ell=3

[
2
]
.

Problem 2
.

Let
\varphi:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
be a non-decreasing function such that
\varphi(n)\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
.

7.2.
Miscellaneous problems

First, we may replace the set of prime numbers
\mathcal{P}
with any infinite subset
\mathcal{A}
of natural numbers.

Problem 4
.

For any
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, any positive function
\varphi:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
and any infinite subset
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathbb{N}
, define

\mathcal{E}_{\ell}(\varphi;\mathcal{A})=\left\{x\in(0,1):a_{n}(x)\cdots a_{n+\ell-1}(x)\geq\varphi(n)\;\text{ and }\;a_{n}(x)\cdots a_{n+\ell-1}(x)\in\mathcal{A}\;\text{ for i.m.}n\in\mathbb{N}\right\}.

Remark
.

The proofs of Theorem
1.8
and
1.10
rely on the Prime Number Theorem.
Hence, we might need some regularity conditions on the function
\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
n\mapsto\#\{1,2,\ldots,n\}\cap\mathcal{A}
.

Khinchin proved there is a constant
K=2.68545^{+}
such that for almost every
x\in(0,1)
we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(a_{1}(x)\cdots a_{n}(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}=K.

For a proof, see, for example,
[
10
, Corollary 3.8]
.

Problem 5
.

For every
\alpha\in(0,\infty]
, determine the Hausdorff dimension of the set

\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\alpha):=\left\{x\in(0,1):\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(a_{1}^{\prime}(x)\cdots a_{n}^{\prime}(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}=\alpha\right\}.

Remark
.

We assume
\alpha>0
because
\left(a_{1}^{\prime}(x)\cdots a_{n}^{\prime}(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}=0
for large
n
almost everywhere. This problem was addressed without the primality assumption in
[
12
]
.

Let
\Phi:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
be any positive function such that
\Phi(n)\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
.
One way of understanding the behaviour of the product of prime partial quotients is to investigate the Hausdorff dimension of the following sets:

\Lambda_{\rm inf}(\Phi):=\left\{x\in[0,1):\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log\left(1+\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{m}a^{\prime}_{n+i}(x)\right)}{\Phi(n)}=1\right\},

and

\Lambda_{\rm sup}(\Phi):=\left\{x\in[0,1):\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log\left(1+\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{m}a^{\prime}_{n+i}(x)\right)}{\Phi(n)}=1\right\}.

Clearly,
\mathfrak{m}(\Lambda_{\rm inf}(\Phi))=0
by Birkhoff’s ergodic theorem.
Similar to the above sets, for any
0\leq\alpha\leq\beta\leq\infty
define

\Lambda_{\alpha,\beta}:=\left\{x\in[0,1):\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log\left(1+\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{m}a^{\prime}_{n+i}(x)\right)}{\log q_{n}(x)}=\alpha,\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log\left(1+\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{m}a^{\prime}_{n+i}(x)\right)}{\log q_{n}(x)}=\beta\ \right\}.

Problem 6
.

For
\Phi
,
\alpha
, and
\beta
as above, compute the Hausdorff dimension of
\Lambda_{\rm inf}(\Phi)
,

\Lambda_{\rm sup}(\Phi)
, and
\Lambda_{\alpha,\beta}
.

Remark
.

In the previous sets, we add 1 to avoid evaluating
\log
at
0
whenever the partial quotients are composite.
Moreover, assuming every partial quotient is prime, the limits are unaffected by adding
1
.

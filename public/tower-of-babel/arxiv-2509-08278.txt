Fundamental theorem of transposed Poisson ( A , H ) (A,H) -Hopf modules
Yan Ning, Daowei Lu, Dingguo Wang

Abstract
. Transposed Poisson algebra was introduced as a dual notion of the Poisson algebra by switching the roles played by the commutative associative operation and Lie operation in the Leibniz rule defining the Poisson algebra. Let H H be a Hopf algebra with a bijective antipode and A A an H H -comodule transposed Poisson algebra. Assume that there exists an H H -colinear map which is also an algebra map from H H to the transposed Poisson center of A A . In this paper we generalize the fundamental theorem of ( A , H ) (A,H) -Hopf modules to transposed Poisson ( A , H ) (A,H) -Hopf modules and deduce relative projectivity in the category of transposed Poisson ( A , H ) (A,H) -Hopf modules.



Introduction

A Poisson algebra is a commutative associative unitary algebra
A
endowed with a bilinear map
\{\cdot,\cdot\}:A\times A\rightarrow A
, providing
A
with a Lie algebra structure and for all
a,a^{\prime},a^{\prime\prime}\in A
satisfying the
Leibniz rule

\{a,a^{\prime}a^{\prime\prime}\}=a^{\prime}\{a,a^{\prime\prime}\}+\{a,a^{\prime}\}a^{\prime\prime}.

Poisson algebras play a significant role in various branches of mathematics and physics. They are fundamental to integrable systems, Poisson geometry (including Poisson brackets and Poisson manifolds)
[
3
,
4
]
. Additionally, they find applications in vertex algebras
[
10
]
, quantization theory
[
6
,
13
]
, deformation quantization
[
9
]
, as well as in classical mechanics
[
1
]
, Hamiltonian mechanics
[
17
]
, and quantum mechanics
[
7
]
. Furthermore, Poisson algebras are relevant in quantum field theory, Lie theory
[
15
]
, and operads
[
11
]
.

A transposed Poisson algebra
[
2
]
is a commutative associative unitary algebra
A
endowed with a bilinear map
\{\cdot,\cdot\}:A\times A\rightarrow A
, such that
A
with a Lie algebra structure and for all
a,a^{\prime},a^{\prime\prime}\in A
satisfying the
transposed Leibniz rule

2a\{a^{\prime},a^{\prime\prime}\}=\{aa^{\prime},a^{\prime\prime}\}+\{a^{\prime},aa^{\prime\prime}\}.

The difference of Poisson algebra and transposed Poisson algebra mainly lies in the fact that the roles played by the two binary operations
\cdot
and
\{,\}
in the Leibniz rule are switched.

While both the Poisson algebra and transposed Poisson algebra are built from a commutative associative operation and a Lie operation, the intersection of the compatibility conditions of both algebraic structures is trivial, that is, the structures of transposed Poisson algebras and Poisson algebras are incompatible in this sense of
[
2
]
.

It is worthwhile to point out that transposed Poisson algebra has close relationship with other important algebraic structures. For example, taking the commutator in a Novikov-Poisson algebra or in a pre-Lie Poisson algebra gives rise to a transposed Poisson algebra; a derivation which is also a Lie derivation of a transposed Poisson algebra could produce a 3-Lie algebra; a transposed Poisson algebra with an involutive endomorphism of the commutative algebra which is at the same time an anti-endomorphism of the Lie algebra also gives rise to a 3-Lie algebra.

Let
H
be a Hopf algebra, and
A
a right
H
-comodule algebra. Doi
[
8
]
established the fundamental theorem of relative Hopf modules, which states that if there exists a right
H
-comodule map
\phi:H\rightarrow A
which is an algebra map, then for any relative right
(A,H)
-Hopf module
M
, the following isomorphism of relative right
(A,H)
-Hopf modules is obtained

A\otimes_{A^{coH}}M\rightarrow M,\ a\otimes m\mapsto a\cdot m.

This isomorphism plays an essential role in the theory of Hopf algebras, such as the integral theory, Nichols-Zoller theorem, Galois theory and so on
[
16
]
.

Let
H
be a Hopf algebra and
A
a Poisson algebra such that
A
is a
H
-comodule Poisson algebra. In
[
12
]
Gu
\acute{e}
d
\acute{e}
non established the fundamental theorem of Poisson
(A,H)
-Hopf modules, which generalized the fundamental isomorphism to the case of Poisson algebra. On account of the connections between Poisson algebra and transposed Poisson algebra, it is very natural and interesting to consider the fundamental theorem in the context of transposed Poisson algebra.

The paper is organized as follows. In section 1, we recall basic definitions on transposed Poisson algebra and introduce the notions of the center of transposed Poisson algebra,
H
-comodule transposed Poisson algebra and transposed Poisson Hopf modules. In section 2, we deduce the Maschke type theorem in the category of transposed Poisson Hopf modules, and show that under some conditions any transposed Poisson Hopf module which is injective as a Lie module is also an injective transposed Poisson Hopf modules. In section 3, we give the fundamental theorem of transposed Poisson
(A,H)
-Hopf modules and deduce relative projectivity in the category of transposed Poisson
(A,H)
-Hopf modules.

1.
Preliminaries

A Hopf algebra is an algebra
(H,m_{H},1_{H})
and coalgebra
(H,\Delta_{H},\varepsilon_{H})
that possesses an antipode
S_{H}:H\rightarrow H
satisfying the defining relations

\displaystyle(\Delta_{H}\otimes id_{H})\circ\Delta_{H}=(id_{H}\otimes\Delta_{H})\circ\Delta_{H},

\displaystyle m_{H}\circ[(S_{H}\otimes id_{H})\circ\Delta_{H}]=m_{H}\circ[(id_{H}\otimes S_{H})\circ\Delta_{H}]=\varepsilon_{H},

\displaystyle(\varepsilon_{H}\otimes id_{H})\circ\Delta_{H}=(id_{H}\otimes\varepsilon_{H})\circ\Delta_{H}=id_{H}.

For background on Hopf algebras and coactions of Hopf algebras on rings, we refer to
[
16
]
. We will use Sweedler-Heyneman notation, writing:

\Delta_{H}(h)=h_{1}\otimes h_{2},\text{ for all }h\in H.

A right
H
-comodule is a vector space

with structure map
\rho_{M}:M\rightarrow M\otimes H,\ m\mapsto m_{(0)}\otimes m_{(1)}
satisfying

(id_{M}\otimes\Delta_{H})\circ\rho_{M}=(\rho_{M}\otimes id_{N})\circ\rho_{M},\quad(id_{M}\otimes\varepsilon_{H})\circ\rho_{M}=id_{M}.

The vector subspace

M^{coH}=\{m\in M|\rho_{M}(m)=m\otimes 1_{H}\}.

of

is called the subspace of
H
-coinvariants of
M
.

An algebra
A
is an
H
-comodule algebra if
A
is an
H
-comodule satisfying

\displaystyle(aa^{\prime})_{(0)}\otimes(aa^{\prime})_{(1)}=a_{(0)}a^{\prime}_{(0)}\otimes a_{(1)}a^{\prime}_{(1)},

\displaystyle(1_{A})_{(0)}\otimes(1_{A})_{(1)}=1_{A}\otimes 1_{H},

for all
a,a^{\prime}\in A
.

If
A
is an
H
-comodule algebra, then the subspace

is a subalgebra of
A
.

Definition 1.1
.

[
2
]

A transposed Poisson algebra is a triple
(A,\cdot,\{,\})
, where
(A,\cdot)
is a commutative associative algebra and
(A,\{,\})
is a Lie algebra, satisfying the transposed Leibniz identity

(1.1)

2a\{b,c\}=\{ab,c\}+\{b,ac\},

for all

.

A subspace

is called a transposed Poisson subalgebra of

The transposed Poisson center

of

A^{A}=\{b\in A|b\{a,a^{\prime}\}=\{ba,a^{\prime}\},\forall a,a^{\prime}\in A\}.

Lemma 1.2
.

A^{A}
is a transposed Poisson subalgebra of
A
.

Proof.

For all
b,b^{\prime}\in A^{A},a,a^{\prime}\in A
, we have

\{ba,a^{\prime}\}=b\{a,a^{\prime}\}=-b\{a^{\prime},a\}=-\{ba^{\prime},a\}=\{a,ba^{\prime}\},

and

bb^{\prime}\{a,a^{\prime}\}=b\{b^{\prime}a,a^{\prime}\}=\{bb^{\prime}a,a^{\prime}\}.

Since
1_{A}\in A^{A}
,
A^{A}
is a subalgebra of
A
. Moreover

\displaystyle 2a\{b,b^{\prime}\}

\displaystyle=\{ab,b^{\prime}\}+\{b,ab^{\prime}\}=b\{a,b^{\prime}\}+\{b,a\}b^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\{a,bb^{\prime}\}-\{a,b\}b^{\prime}=\{a,bb^{\prime}\}-\{a,bb^{\prime}\}

\displaystyle=0,

therefore we obtain that
\{b,b^{\prime}\}=0
by setting
a=1_{A}
, that is, the Poisson bracket on
A^{A}
is trivial. Thus
A^{A}
is a transposed Poisson subalgebra of
A
.
∎

3.
Fundamental theorem of transposed Poisson Hopf modules

In what follows, we will denote
B=A^{AcoH}

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
M
be a transposed Poisson
B
-module. Then
A\otimes_{B}M
is a transposed Poisson
(A,H)
-Hopf module, where the
A
-action and the Lie
A
-action are given by

\displaystyle a^{\prime}\cdot(a\otimes_{B}m)=a^{\prime}a\otimes_{B}m

\displaystyle a^{\prime}\diamond(a\otimes_{B}m)=a^{\prime}\diamond a\otimes_{B}m,

and the
H
-coaction is given by

\rho_{A\otimes_{B}M}(a\otimes_{B}m)=a_{(0)}\otimes_{B}m\otimes a_{(1)},

for all
a,a^{\prime}\in A,m\in M
.

Proof.

It is obvious that the coaction is well defined. For all
a,a^{\prime}\in A,m\in M
, it is straightforward to see that

(a^{\prime}\cdot(a\otimes_{B}m))_{(0)}\otimes(a^{\prime}\cdot(a\otimes_{B}m))_{(1)}=a^{\prime}_{(0)}\cdot(a\otimes_{B}m)_{(0)}\otimes a^{\prime}_{(1)}(a\otimes_{B}m)_{(1)},

hence
A\otimes_{B}M
is an
(A,H)
-Hopf module. For
b\in B
,

\displaystyle a^{\prime}\diamond(ab\otimes_{B}m)

\displaystyle=(a^{\prime}\diamond(ab))\otimes_{B}m

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{1a})}}{{=}}(2b\{a^{\prime},a\}-\{ba^{\prime},a\})\otimes_{B}m

\displaystyle=b\{a^{\prime},a\}\otimes_{B}m+b\{a^{\prime},a\}\otimes_{B}m-\{ba^{\prime},a\}\otimes_{B}m

\displaystyle=\{a^{\prime},a\}\otimes_{B}b\cdot m+(b\{a^{\prime},a\}-\{ba^{\prime},a\})\otimes_{B}m

\displaystyle=\{a^{\prime},a\}\otimes_{B}b\cdot m

\displaystyle=a^{\prime}\diamond(a\otimes_{B}b\cdot m),

where the fifth identity uses the definition of
A^{A}
, therefore the Lie action is well defined and
A\otimes_{B}M
is a Lie
A
-module. Since

\displaystyle 2\{a^{\prime\prime},a^{\prime}\}\cdot(a\otimes_{B}m)

\displaystyle=(2\{a^{\prime\prime},a^{\prime}\}a)\otimes_{B}m

\displaystyle=\{aa^{\prime\prime},a^{\prime}\}\otimes_{B}m+\{a^{\prime\prime},aa^{\prime}\}\otimes_{B}m

\displaystyle=(a^{\prime\prime}\diamond a^{\prime}a)\otimes_{B}m-(a^{\prime}\diamond a^{\prime\prime}a)\otimes_{B}m

\displaystyle=a^{\prime\prime}\diamond(a^{\prime}a\otimes_{B}m)-a^{\prime}\diamond(a^{\prime\prime}a\otimes_{B}m)

\displaystyle=a^{\prime\prime}\diamond(a^{\prime}\cdot(a\otimes_{B}m))-a^{\prime}\diamond(a^{\prime\prime}\cdot(a\otimes_{B}m)),

and

\displaystyle 2a^{\prime\prime}\cdot(a^{\prime}\diamond(a\otimes_{B}m))

\displaystyle=(2a^{\prime\prime}\{a^{\prime},a\})\otimes_{B}m

\displaystyle=\{a^{\prime\prime}a^{\prime},a\}\otimes_{B}m+\{a^{\prime},a^{\prime\prime}a\}\otimes_{B}m

\displaystyle=a^{\prime\prime}a^{\prime}\diamond(a\otimes_{B}m)+a^{\prime}\diamond(a^{\prime\prime}\cdot(a\otimes_{B}m)),

therefore
A\otimes_{B}M
is a transposed Poisson
A
-module. Finally

\displaystyle(a^{\prime}\diamond(a\otimes_{B}m))_{(0)}\otimes(a^{\prime}\diamond(a\otimes_{B}m))_{(1)}

\displaystyle=(\{a^{\prime},a\}\otimes_{B}m)_{(0)}\otimes(\{a^{\prime},a\}\otimes_{B}m)_{(1)}

\displaystyle=\{a^{\prime},a\}_{(0)}\otimes_{B}m\otimes\{a^{\prime},a\}_{(1)}

\displaystyle=\{a^{\prime}_{(0)},a_{(0)}\}\otimes_{B}m\otimes a^{\prime}_{(1)}a_{(1)}

\displaystyle=(a^{\prime}_{(0)}\diamond(a_{(0)}\otimes_{B}m))\otimes a^{\prime}_{(1)}a_{(1)}

\displaystyle=(a^{\prime}_{(0)}\diamond(a\otimes_{B}m)_{(0)})\otimes a^{\prime}_{(1)}(a\otimes_{B}m)_{(1)},

which means that
A\otimes_{B}M
is a transposed Poisson
(A,H)
-Hopf module. The proof is completed.
∎

For every transposed Poisson

-Hopf module

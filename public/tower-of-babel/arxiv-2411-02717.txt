On dimensions of RoCK blocks of cyclotomic quiver Hecke superalgebras Thanks: The author was supported by the NSF grant DMS-2101791.
Alexander Kleshchev

Abstract
. We explicitly compute the dimensions of certain idempotent truncations of RoCK blocks of cyclotomic quiver Hecke superalgebras. Equivalently, this amounts to a computation of the value of the Shapovalov form on certain explicit vectors in the basic representations of twisted affine Kac-Moody Lie algebras of type A A .

1.
Introduction

The goal of this paper is to obtain a technical (but neat) result computing the dimensions of certain idempotent truncations of RoCK blocks of cyclotomic quiver Hecke superalgebras. Equivalently, this amounts to a computation of the value of the Shapovalov form on certain explicit vectors in the basic representation
V(\Lambda_{0})
of twisted affine Kac-Moody Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
of type
A_{2\ell}^{(2)}
.

To state the result, let
{\mathfrak{g}}
be the Kac-Moody Lie algebra of type
A_{2\ell}^{(2)}
, with a normalized invariant form
(\cdot|\cdot)
on the corresponding weight lattice
P
, and the Weyl group
W
. Let

I=\{0,1,\dots,\ell\},\quad J\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=I\setminus\{\ell\},

\{\alpha_{i}\mid i\in I\}
be the simple roots,
\{\Lambda_{i}\mid i\in I\}
be the fundamental dominant weights,
and
Q_{+}\subset P
be the set of
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
-linear combinations of the simple roots. For the negative Chevalley generators
\{f_{i}\mid i\in I\}
of
{\mathfrak{g}}
, we have the divided powers
f_{i}^{(k)}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=f_{i}^{k}/k!\in U({\mathfrak{g}})
for
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. Fix a non-zero highest weight vector
v_{+}
of the irreducible
{\mathfrak{g}}
-module
V(\Lambda_{0})
with highest weight
\Lambda_{0}
. Let
(\cdot,\cdot)
be the Shapovalov form on
V(\Lambda_{0})

such that
(v_{+},v_{+})=1
.

For every
w\in W
with reduced decomposition
w=r_{i_{t}}\cdots r_{i_{1}}
, setting

a_{k}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(r_{i_{k-1}}\cdots r_{i_{1}}\Lambda_{0}\mid\alpha_{i_{k}}^{\vee})\qquad(k=1,\dots,t),

(1.1)

it is easy to see that
a_{1},\dots,a_{t}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and

v_{w}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=f_{i_{t}}^{(a_{t})}\cdots f_{i_{1}}^{(a_{1})}v_{+}

is a non-zero vector of the weight space
V(\Lambda_{0})_{w\Lambda_{0}}
,
which does not depend on the choice of reduced decomposition and satisfies

(v_{w},v_{w})=1.

For every

and

, we define the divided power monomial

f(m,j)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=f_{j}^{(m)}\cdots f_{1}^{(m)}f_{0}^{(2m)}f_{1}^{(m)}\cdots f_{j}^{(m)}f_{j+1}^{(2m)}\cdots f_{\ell-1}^{(2m)}f_{\ell}^{(m)}\in U({\mathfrak{g}}).

(1.2)

(with

(1.3)

For a composition

If the weight space

We then say that

Main Theorem.

Let weight
\theta=\Lambda_{0}-w\Lambda_{0}+d\delta

\dim e(\mu,\text{\boldmath$j$})R_{\theta}^{\Lambda_{0}}e(\omega_{d})=(f(\mu,\text{\boldmath$j$})v_{w},f(\omega_{d})v_{w})=\binom{d}{\mu_{1}\,\cdots\,\mu_{n}}\,4^{d-|\mu,\text{\boldmath$j$}|_{\ell-1}}\,3^{|\mu,\text{\boldmath$j$}|_{\ell-1}}.

The first equality in the theorem is a known consequence of the Kang-Kashiwara-Oh categorification, so the main work is to prove of the second equality. This is proved in Theorem
5.6

The dimension formula for

is isomorphic to a generalized Schur algebra corresponding to a certain Brauer tree algebra. In fact, our Main Theorem is a key step needed for the proof of this isomorphism in

.

3.4.
Another description of
\Pi_{\mu,\text{\boldmath$j$}}

Let
M_{n,I}
denote the set of

-matrices with non-negative integer entries, i.e.

For

, we define the sets of matrices

\displaystyle M_{n,I}(\mu)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{(a_{r,i})\in M_{n,I}\mid\textstyle\sum_{i\in I}a_{r,i}=\mu_{r}\ \text{for $r=1,\dots,n$}\},

\displaystyle M_{n,I}(\text{\boldmath$j$})

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{(a_{r,i})\in M_{n,I}\mid\text{$a_{r,i}=0$ if $i\neq j_{r},j_{r}+1$ for $r=1,\dots,n$}\},

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=M_{n,I}(\mu)\cap M_{n,I}(\text{\boldmath$j$}).

Let

. For

and

, we define

\psi_{A}(r,i)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{\mathsf{h}}_{a_{r,i}}&\hbox{if $i=j_{r}$,}\\
{\mathsf{e}}_{a_{r,i}}&\hbox{if $i=j_{r}+1$,}\\
1&\hbox{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

We now set

\displaystyle\psi_{A}^{(0)}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}={\mathsf{q}}_{a_{1,0}}\cdots{\mathsf{q}}_{a_{n,0}},\qquad\psi_{A}^{(i)}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\psi_{A}(1,i)\cdots\psi_{A}(n,i)\quad(\text{for}\ i\in I\setminus\{0\}),

and

Example 3.34
.

Suppose
n=1
and so
(\mu,\text{\boldmath$j$})\in\Lambda^{\operatorname{col}}(1,d)
is of the form
((d),j)
. The set

consists of all matrices of the form

\{A(j,k)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\left(\begin{matrix}0&\cdots&0&k&d-k&0&\cdots&0\end{matrix}\right)\mid 0\leq k\leq d\}

with
k
in position

. Note that by definition we have

\displaystyle\psi_{A_{0,0}}

\displaystyle={\mathsf{q}}_{0}\otimes{\mathsf{e}}_{d}\otimes 1^{\otimes\ell-1}=1\otimes{\mathsf{e}}_{d}\otimes 1^{\otimes\ell-1},

\displaystyle\psi_{A_{0,k}}

\displaystyle={\mathsf{q}}_{k}\otimes{\mathsf{e}}_{d-k}\otimes 1^{\otimes\ell-1}=2{\mathsf{P}}_{(k)}\otimes{\mathsf{e}}_{d-k}\otimes 1^{\otimes\ell-1}\qquad(1\leq k\leq d),

\displaystyle\psi_{A_{j,k}}

\displaystyle=1^{\otimes j}\otimes{\mathsf{h}}_{k}\otimes{\mathsf{e}}_{d-k}\otimes 1^{\otimes\ell-1-j}\qquad(1\leq j<\ell).

In particular, comparing with (
3.30
), we deduce that

\Pi_{(d),j}=\sum_{A\in M((d),j)}\psi_{A}
.

Proposition 3.35
.

Let
(\mu,\text{\boldmath$j$})\in\Lambda^{\operatorname{col}}(n,d)
. Then

\Pi_{\mu,\text{\boldmath$j$}}=\sum_{A\in M(\mu,\text{\boldmath$j$})}\psi_{A}.

Proof.

We apply induction on
n
. For the base
n=1
, see Example
3.34
. Suppose
n\geq 2
and set

\nu\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{n-1}),\quad\text{\boldmath$k$}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(j_{1},\dots,j_{n-1}),\quad m\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mu_{n},\quad j\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=j_{n}.

Then

\Pi(\mu,\text{\boldmath$j$})=\Pi(\nu,\text{\boldmath$k$})\Pi((m),j)
by Lemma
3.31
. By the inductive assumption, we have

\Pi(\nu,\text{\boldmath$k$})=\sum_{B\in M(\nu,\text{\boldmath$k$})}\psi_{B}\quad\text{and}\quad\Pi((m),j)=\sum_{C\in M((m),j)}\psi_{C},

so it suffices to observe that

\Big(\sum_{B\in M(\nu,\text{\boldmath$k$})}\psi_{B}\Big)\Big(\sum_{C\in M((m),j)}\psi_{C}\Big)=\sum_{A\in M(\mu,\text{\boldmath$j$})}\psi_{A},

which comes from the definitions.
∎

3.5.
Computing the inner product
(\Pi_{\mu,\text{\boldmath$j$}},\Pi_{\omega_{d}})_{\mathsf{Sym}}

Recall the inner product
(\cdot,\cdot)_{\mathsf{Sym}}
from (
3.27
).
Throughout this subsection, we fix
(\mu,\text{\boldmath$j$})\in\Lambda^{\operatorname{col}}(n,d)
. For
A=(a_{r,i})\in M(\mu,\text{\boldmath$j$})
and

Then we have compositions

a_{*,i}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(a_{1,i},\dots,a_{n,i})\in\Lambda(n,|a_{*,i}|)\qquad(i\in I)

and multinomial coefficients

\binom{|a_{*,i}|}{a_{*,i}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\binom{|a_{*,i}|}{a_{1,i}\,\cdots\,a_{n,i}}=\frac{|a_{*,i}|!}{a_{1,i}!\cdots a_{n,i}!}.

Lemma 3.36
.

Let
A\in M(\mu,\text{\boldmath$j$})
, and
k_{0},k_{1},\dots,k_{\ell}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. Then

(\psi_{A},{\mathsf{q}}_{1}^{k_{0}}\otimes{\mathsf{s}}_{(1)}^{k_{1}}\otimes\dots\otimes{\mathsf{s}}_{(1)}^{k_{\ell}})_{\mathsf{Sym}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}2^{|a_{*,0}|}\prod_{i\in I}\binom{|a_{*,i}|}{a_{*,i}}&\hbox{if $|a_{*,i}|=k_{i}$ for all $i\in I$,}\\
0&\hbox{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle(\psi_{A},{\mathsf{q}}_{1}^{k_{0}}\otimes{\mathsf{s}}_{(1)}^{k_{1}}\otimes\dots\otimes{\mathsf{s}}_{(1)}^{k_{\ell}})_{\mathsf{Sym}}

\displaystyle=(\psi_{A}^{(0)}\otimes\psi_{A}^{(1)}\otimes\dots\otimes\psi_{A}^{(\ell)},{\mathsf{q}}_{1}^{k_{0}}\otimes{\mathsf{s}}_{(1)}^{k_{1}}\otimes\dots\otimes{\mathsf{s}}_{(1)}^{k_{\ell}})_{\mathsf{Sym}}

\displaystyle=[\psi_{A}^{(0)},{\mathsf{q}}_{1}^{k_{0}}]\,\,(\psi_{A}^{(1)},{\mathsf{s}}_{(1)}^{k_{1}})\,\cdots\,(\psi_{A}^{(\ell)},{\mathsf{s}}_{(1)}^{k_{\ell}}).

Now, by (
3.24
), we have

[\psi_{A}^{(0)},{\mathsf{q}}_{1}^{k_{0}}]=[{\mathsf{q}}_{a_{1,0}}\cdots{\mathsf{q}}_{a_{n,0}},{\mathsf{q}}_{1}^{k_{0}}]=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}2^{k_{0}}\binom{k_{0}}{a_{1,0}\,\cdots\,a_{n,0}}&\hbox{if $k_{0}=a_{1,0}+\cdots+a_{n,0}$,}\\
0&\hbox{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

On the other hand, by (
3.22
), we have for
i=1,\dots,\ell
:

(\psi_{A}^{(i)},{\mathsf{s}}_{(1)}^{k_{i}}))=(\psi_{A}(1,i)\cdots\psi_{A}(n,i),{\mathsf{s}}_{(1)}^{k_{i}}))\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\binom{k_{i}}{a_{1,i}\,\cdots\,a_{n,i}}&\hbox{if $k_{i}=a_{1,i}+\cdots+a_{n,i}$,}\\
0&\hbox{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

This implies the required equality.
∎

Recall the notation
|\mu,\text{\boldmath$j$}|_{\ell-1}
from (
1.5
).

Theorem 3.37
.

Let
(\mu,\text{\boldmath$j$})\in\Lambda^{\operatorname{col}}(n,d)
. Then

\big(\Pi_{\mu,\text{\boldmath$j$}},\Pi_{\omega_{d}}\big)_{\mathsf{Sym}}=\binom{d}{\mu_{1}\,\cdots\,\mu_{n}}\,4^{d-|\mu,\text{\boldmath$j$}|_{\ell-1}}\,3^{|\mu,\text{\boldmath$j$}|_{\ell-1}}.

Proof.

In view of
Proposition
3.35
and
Corollary
3.33
, we have that
\big(\Pi_{\mu,\text{\boldmath$j$}},\Pi_{\omega_{d}}\big)_{\mathsf{Sym}}
equals

\displaystyle\sum_{A\in M(\mu,\text{\boldmath$j$})}\sum_{k_{0}+k_{1}+\dots+k_{\ell}=d}2^{k_{1}+\dots+k_{\ell-1}}{{d}\choose{k_{0}\,k_{1}\,\cdots\,k_{\ell}}}\big(\psi_{A},{\mathsf{q}}_{1}^{k_{0}}\otimes{\mathsf{s}}_{(1)}^{k_{1}}\otimes\dots\otimes{\mathsf{s}}_{(1)}^{k_{\ell}}\big)_{\mathsf{Sym}}.

By Lemma
3.36
, this equals

\displaystyle\sum_{A\in M(\mu,\text{\boldmath$j$})}2^{|a_{*,1}|+\dots+|a_{*,\ell-1}|}{{d}\choose{|a_{*,0}|\,|a_{*,1}|\,\cdots\,|a_{*,\ell}|}}2^{|a_{*,0}|}\prod_{i\in I}\binom{|a_{*,i}|}{a_{*,i}}

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle\sum_{A\in M(\mu,\text{\boldmath$j$})}2^{|a_{*,0}|+|a_{*,1}|+\dots+|a_{*,\ell-1}|}\frac{d!}{\prod_{i\in I}\prod_{r=1}^{n}a_{r,i}!}

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle\sum_{A\in M(\mu,\text{\boldmath$j$})}2^{d-|a_{*,\ell}|}\binom{d}{\mu_{1}\,\cdots\,\mu_{n}}\prod_{r=1}^{n}\binom{\mu_{r}}{a_{r,0}\,\cdots a_{r,\ell}},

and it remains to prove that

\sum_{A\in M(\mu,\text{\boldmath$j$})}2^{d-|a_{*,\ell}|}\prod_{r=1}^{n}\binom{\mu_{r}}{a_{r,0}\,\cdots a_{r,\ell}}=4^{d-|\mu,\text{\boldmath$j$}|_{\ell-1}}\,3^{|\mu,\text{\boldmath$j$}|_{\ell-1}}.

(3.38)

Denote

d_{j}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq r\leq n,\\
j_{r}=j\end{subarray}}\mu_{r}\qquad(j\in J).

In particular,
d_{\ell-1}=|\mu,\text{\boldmath$j$}|_{\ell-1}
and
d_{0}+d_{1}+\dots+d_{\ell-1}=d
.
Note that permuting the parts of
(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{n})
and
(j_{1},\dots,j_{n})
by the same permutation in
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
does not change the left hand side of (
3.38
), so we may assume without loss of generality that
\text{\boldmath$j$}=(0^{n_{0}},1^{n_{1}},\dots,(\ell-1)^{n_{\ell-1}})
with
n_{0}+n_{1}+\dots+n_{\ell-1}=n
and

\mu=(\lambda^{(0)}_{1},\dots,\lambda^{(0)}_{n_{0}},\dots,\lambda^{(\ell-1)}_{1},\dots,\lambda^{(\ell-1)}_{n_{\ell-1}})

with
(\lambda^{(j)}_{1},\dots,\lambda^{(j)}_{n_{j}})\in\Lambda(n_{j},d_{j})
for all
j\in J
. Then, the matrices
A\in M(\mu,\text{\boldmath$j$})
look like

A=\left(\begin{matrix}B^{(0)}\\
\vdots\\
B^{(\ell-1)}\end{matrix}\right)

where, for each
j
, the matrix
B^{(j)}=(b^{(j)}_{r,i})_{1\leq r\leq n_{j},\,i\in I}
is an arbitrary matrix with non-negative integer values with the property that
b^{(j)}_{r,i}=0
unless
i\in\{j,j+1\}
and
b^{(j)}_{r,j}+b^{(j)}_{r,j+1}=\lambda^{(j)}_{r}
for all
r=1,\dots,n_{j}
.
So, the left hand side of (
3.38
)
equals
XY
where

X\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\prod_{j=0}^{\ell-2}\prod_{r=1}^{n_{j}}\sum_{b^{(j)}_{r,j}+b^{(j)}_{r,j+1}=\lambda^{(j)}_{r}}2^{\lambda^{(j)}_{r}}\binom{\lambda^{(j)}_{r}}{b^{(j)}_{r,j}\,b^{(j)}_{r,j+1}}

and

Y\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\prod_{r=1}^{n_{\ell-1}}\sum_{b^{(\ell-1)}_{r,\ell-1}+b^{(\ell-1)}_{r,\ell}=\lambda^{(\ell-1)}_{r}}2^{b^{(\ell-1)}_{r,\ell-1}}\binom{\lambda^{(\ell-1)}_{r}}{b^{(\ell-1)}_{r,\ell-1}\,b^{(\ell-1)}_{r,\ell}}.

Now, using the formula

\sum_{a+b=c}\binom{c}{a\,b}=2^{c}
,
we get

X=2^{d_{0}+\dots+d_{\ell-2}}\prod_{j=0}^{\ell-2}\prod_{r=1}^{n_{j}}\sum_{b^{(j)}_{r,j}+b^{(j)}_{r,j+1}=\lambda^{(j)}_{r}}2^{\lambda^{(j)}_{r}}=4^{d_{0}+\dots+d_{\ell-2}}=4^{d-d_{\ell-1}}=4^{d-|\mu,\text{\boldmath$j$}|_{\ell-1}},

and, using the formula

\sum_{a+b=c}2^{a}\binom{c}{a\,b}=3^{c}
,
we get

Y=\prod_{r=1}^{n_{\ell-1}}3^{\lambda^{(\ell-1)}_{r}}=3^{d_{\ell-1}}=3^{|\mu,\text{\boldmath$j$}|_{\ell-1}},

completing the proof.
∎

\displaystyle=2^{({\mathpzc{p}}_{\lambda}-h_{p}(\lambda)-c^{\lambda}_{\neq 0})/2}\sum_{{\mathtt{B}}\in A_{i}(\lambda)}c(\lambda,{\mathtt{B}})2^{-({\mathpzc{p}}_{\lambda^{\mathtt{B}}}-h_{p}(\lambda^{\mathtt{B}})-c^{\lambda^{\mathtt{B}}}_{\neq 0})/2}\chi_{\lambda^{\mathtt{B}}},

so we need to prove that

a(\lambda,{\mathtt{B}})=2^{({\mathpzc{p}}_{\lambda}-{\mathpzc{p}}_{\lambda^{\mathtt{B}}}-h_{p}(\lambda)+h_{p}(\lambda^{\mathtt{B}})-c^{\lambda}_{\neq 0}+c^{\lambda^{\mathtt{B}}}_{\neq 0})/2}c(\lambda,{\mathtt{B}}).

(4.15)

If
i\neq 0
then either
{\mathpzc{p}}_{\lambda}=1
and
{\mathpzc{p}}_{\lambda^{\mathtt{B}}}=0
, or
{\mathpzc{p}}_{\lambda}=0
and
{\mathpzc{p}}_{\lambda^{\mathtt{B}}}=1
. Moreover,
c^{\lambda^{\mathtt{B}}}_{\neq 0}=c^{\lambda}_{\neq 0}+1
. Hence

\displaystyle 2^{({\mathpzc{p}}_{\lambda}-{\mathpzc{p}}_{\lambda^{\mathtt{B}}}-h_{p}(\lambda)+h_{p}(\lambda^{\mathtt{B}})-c^{\lambda}_{\neq 0}+c^{\lambda^{\mathtt{B}}}_{\neq 0})/2}c(\lambda,{\mathtt{B}})

\displaystyle=2^{({\mathpzc{p}}_{\lambda}-{\mathpzc{p}}_{\lambda^{\mathtt{B}}}+1)/2}

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}2&\hbox{if ${\mathpzc{p}}_{\lambda}=1$ and ${\mathpzc{p}}_{\lambda^{\mathtt{B}}}=0$,}\\
1&\hbox{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

which is
a(\lambda,B)
as required.

On the other hand, if
i=0
then
c^{\lambda}_{\neq 0}=c^{\lambda^{\mathtt{B}}}_{\neq 0}
. Let
\lambda
be written in the form (
3.1
), and consider the following three cases:

(1)
B=(m_{1}+\dots+m_{k}+1,1)
. In this case, we have
c(\lambda,B)=1
,
{\mathpzc{p}}_{\lambda}={\mathpzc{p}}_{\lambda^{\mathtt{B}}}
and
h_{p}(\lambda)=h_{p}(\lambda^{\mathtt{B}})
, which immediately gives (
4.15
).

(2)
B=(m_{1}+\dots+m_{r-1}+1,1)
for some
1\leq r\leq k
and
p\mid l_{r}
. In this case, we have
h_{p}(\lambda^{\mathtt{B}})=h_{p}(\lambda)-1
,

c(\lambda,B)=2
and
{\mathpzc{p}}_{\lambda^{\mathtt{B}}}\neq{\mathpzc{p}}_{\lambda}
. If
{\mathpzc{p}}_{\lambda^{\mathtt{B}}}=1
and
{\mathpzc{p}}_{\lambda}=0
then both sides of (
4.15
) equal
1
.
If
{\mathpzc{p}}_{\lambda^{\mathtt{B}}}=0
and
{\mathpzc{p}}_{\lambda}=1
then both sides of (
4.15
) equal
2
.

(3)
B=(m_{1}+\dots+m_{r-1}+1,1)
for some
1\leq r\leq k
and
p\nmid l_{r}
.
In this case, we have
h_{p}(\lambda^{\mathtt{B}})=h_{p}(\lambda)+1
,

c(\lambda,B)=1
and
{\mathpzc{p}}_{\lambda^{\mathtt{B}}}\neq{\mathpzc{p}}_{\lambda}
. If
{\mathpzc{p}}_{\lambda^{\mathtt{B}}}=1
and
{\mathpzc{p}}_{\lambda}=0
then both sides of (
4.15
) equal
1
.
If
{\mathpzc{p}}_{\lambda^{\mathtt{B}}}=0
and
{\mathpzc{p}}_{\lambda}=1
then both sides of (
4.15
) equal
2
.
∎

Throughout Section

We have the element

Lemma 5.1

Corollary 5.2
.

Let
j\in J
and
c,k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
satisfy
c+k\leq d
. For
\alpha\in{\mathscr{P}}_{0}(\rho,c)
,
in the reduced Fock space
{\mathscr{F}}
, we have

f(k,j)u_{\alpha}=\sum_{\lambda}2^{q(\lambda^{(0)}/\alpha^{(0)})+h(\alpha^{(0)})-h(\lambda^{(0)})}u_{\lambda}

where the sum is over all
\lambda\in{\mathscr{P}}_{0}(\rho,c+k)
such that

\operatorname{quot}(\lambda)=(\lambda^{(0)},\dots,\lambda^{(\ell)})
is obtained from
\operatorname{quot}(\alpha)=(\alpha^{(0)},\dots,\alpha^{(\ell)})

by adding
k
nodes to the components
\alpha^{(j)}
and
\alpha^{(j+1)}
, with no two nodes added in the same column of
\alpha^{(j)}
or in the same row of
\alpha^{(j+1)}
.

Proof.

Note that
h_{p}(\alpha)=h(\alpha^{(0)})
.
Moreover, for
\lambda
’s appearing in the sum, we have

So we have by (

\displaystyle f(k,j)u_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=2^{(-{\mathpzc{p}}_{\alpha}+h_{p}(\alpha)+c^{\alpha}_{\neq 0})/2}f(k,j)\chi_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=2^{(-{\mathpzc{p}}_{\alpha}+h_{p}(\alpha)+c^{\alpha}_{\neq 0})/2}\sum_{\lambda}2^{q(\lambda^{(0)}/\alpha^{(0)})+(k(2\ell-1)+h(\alpha^{(0)})-h(\lambda^{(0)})+{\mathpzc{p}}_{\alpha}-{\mathpzc{p}}_{\lambda})/2}\chi_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=2^{q(\lambda^{(0)}/\alpha^{(0)})+h(\alpha^{(0)})+(k(2\ell-1)-h(\lambda^{(0)})-{\mathpzc{p}}_{\lambda}+c^{\alpha}_{\neq 0})/2}\sum_{\lambda}2^{({\mathpzc{p}}_{\lambda}-h_{p}(\lambda)-c^{\lambda}_{\neq 0})/2}u_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda}2^{q(\lambda^{(0)}/\alpha^{(0)})+h(\alpha^{(0)})-h(\lambda^{(0)})}u_{\lambda},

as required.
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
(\mu,\text{\boldmath$j$})\in\Lambda^{\operatorname{col}}(n,d)
. Then

f(\mu,\text{\boldmath$j$})u_{\rho}=\sum_{\lambda\in{\mathscr{P}}_{0}(\rho,d)}K(\operatorname{quot}(\lambda);\mu,\text{\boldmath$j$})2^{-h(\lambda^{(0)})}u_{\lambda}.

Proof.

We apply induction on
n
. For the induction base case
n=1
we apply Corollary
5.2
to see that

f(\mu_{1},j_{1})u_{\rho}=\sum_{\lambda}2^{q(\lambda^{(0)}/\rho^{(0)})+h(\rho^{(0)})-h(\lambda^{(0)})}u_{\lambda}=\sum_{\lambda}2^{q(\lambda^{(0)}/\rho^{(0)})}2^{-h(\lambda^{(0)})}u_{\lambda},

where where the sums are over all
\lambda\in{\mathscr{P}}_{0}(\rho,d)
such that

\operatorname{quot}(\lambda)=(\lambda^{(0)},\dots,\lambda^{(\ell)})
is obtained from
\operatorname{quot}(\rho)=(\varnothing,\dots,\varnothing)

by adding
k
nodes to the components
\rho^{(j)}=\varnothing
and
\rho^{(j+1)}=\varnothing
, with no two nodes added in the same column of
\rho^{(j)}
or in the same row of
\rho^{(j+1)}
, and

q(\lambda^{(0)}/\rho^{(0)})=|\{r\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\mid\lambda^{(0)}\ \text{contains a node in column $r$ but not in column $r+1$}\}|.

It follows from the definitions that the
\lambda
’s appearing in the latter sum are exactly the
\lambda
’s with
K(\operatorname{quot}(\lambda);(\mu_{1}),j_{1})\neq 0
, and for those
\lambda
we have
K(\operatorname{quot}(\lambda);(\mu_{1}),j_{1})=2^{q(\lambda^{(0)}/\rho^{(0)})}
. This establishes the induction base.

For the inductive step, suppose
n>1
. Let
\nu\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{n-1})
,
\text{\boldmath$k$}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(j_{1},\dots,j_{n-1})

\displaystyle f(\mu,\text{\boldmath$j$})u_{\rho}

\displaystyle=f(\mu_{n},j_{n})f(\nu,\text{\boldmath$k$})u_{\rho}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathscr{P}}_{0}(\rho,c)}K(\operatorname{quot}(\alpha);\nu,\text{\boldmath$k$})2^{-h(\alpha^{(0)})}f(\mu_{n},j_{n})u_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathscr{P}}_{0}(\rho,c)}K(\operatorname{quot}(\alpha);\nu,\text{\boldmath$k$})2^{-h(\alpha^{(0)})}\sum_{\lambda}2^{q(\lambda^{(0)}/\alpha^{(0)})+h(\alpha^{(0)})-h(\lambda^{(0)})}u_{\lambda}

where the second sum is over all
\lambda\in{\mathscr{P}}_{0}(\rho,d)
such that

\operatorname{quot}(\lambda)=(\lambda^{(0)},\dots,\lambda^{(\ell)})
is obtained from
\operatorname{quot}(\alpha)=(\alpha^{(0)},\dots,\alpha^{(\ell)})

by adding
\mu_{n}
nodes to the components
\alpha^{(j_{n})}
and
\alpha^{(j_{n}+1)}
, with no two nodes added in the same column of
\alpha^{(j_{n})}
or in the same row of
\alpha^{(j_{n}+1)}
.
It remains to note that
\lambda
’s appearing in the expression above are exactly those with

K(\operatorname{quot}(\lambda);\mu,\text{\boldmath$j$})=\sum_{\alpha}2^{q(\lambda^{(0)}/\alpha^{(0)})}K(\operatorname{quot}(\alpha);\nu,\text{\boldmath$k$}),

where the sum is over all
\alpha\in{\mathscr{P}}_{0}(\rho,c)

\operatorname{quot}(\alpha)=(\alpha^{(0)},\dots,\alpha^{(\ell)})

\operatorname{quot}(\lambda)=(\lambda^{(0)},\dots,\lambda^{(\ell)})

∎

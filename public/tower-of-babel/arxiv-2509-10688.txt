Combined perturbation bounds for eigenstructure of Hermitian matrices and singular structure of general matrices Thanks: This work is supported by National Natural Science Foundations of China (11771159) and Guangdong Provincial Natural Science Foundation (2022A1515011123)
Xiao Shan Chen, Hongguo Xu

Abstract
Combined perturbation bounds are presented for eigenvalues and eigenspaces of Hermitian matrices or singular values and singular subspaces of general matrices. The bounds are derived based on the smooth decompositions and elementary calculus techniques.



Keywords

combined perturbation bound, eigenvalue, eigenspace, singular value, singular subspace, Frobenius norm

AMS subject classification

65F15, 65F99

Introduction

Throughout this paper the symbol
\mathbb{C}^{m\times n}
(
\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
) denotes the set of complex (real)
m\times n
matrices.
A^{H}(A^{T})
is the conjugate transpose (transpose) of a matrix
A
. The
(i,j)
entry of a matrix
A
is denoted by
A_{ij}
. The set of singular values of a matrix
A
is denoted by
\sigma(A)
. For a square matrix
A
, the spectrum of
A
is denoted by
\lambda(A)
.
For an
n\times n
Hermitian matrix
A
,
{\rm Eig}^{\downarrow}(A)
denotes an
n\times n
diagonal matrix whose diagonal entries are the eigenvalues of
A
in nonincreasing order. For an
m\times n(m\geq n)
matrix
B
,
{\rm Sing}^{\downarrow}(B)
denotes an
n\times n
diagonal matrix whose diagonal entries are the singular values of
B
in nonincreasing order.

\mathcal{R}(B)
denotes the range of the matrix
B
.

I_{n}
(or simply
I
) is the identity matrix of order
n
and
e_{i}
is its
i
th column.
\|\cdot\|_{2}
denotes the spectral matrix norm and
\|\cdot\|_{F}
the Frobenius norm.
We use the notation
\dot{F}(t)
for
dF(t)/dt
, where
F(t)
can be a time-dependent scalar, vector, or matrix. For a complex number
z
, by
\overline{z}
,
{\rm Re}(z)
and
{\rm Im}(z)
we denote its conjugate, real and imaginary parts, respectively. Finally
{\imath}=\sqrt{-1}
.

Perturbation theory about the eigenvalues and eigenspaces of Hermitian matrices,
and the singular values and singular subspaces of general matrices has been well established, and many results have been published; e.g., see
[
3
,
6
,
8
,
9
,
12
,
15
,
16
,
17
]
. The purpose of this paper is to study perturbation bounds in a combined form for a Hermitian matrix by introducing a single real parameter to the perturbation matrix and considering a (general) spectral factorization as an analytic form.
Another purpose is to apply the same technique to the singular value decomposition (SVD)
and establish the same type of results.

Let
A
and
\widetilde{A}=A+\Delta A
be two
n\times n
Hermitian matrices. Then one has the
Hoffman–Wielandt type eigenvalue bound (
[
1
,
6
,
16
]
)

\displaystyle\|{\rm Eig}^{\downarrow}(\widetilde{A})-{\rm Eig}^{\downarrow}(A)\|_{F}\leq\|\Delta A\|_{F}.

(1.1)

Let
\mathcal{R}(U_{1})
and
\mathcal{R}(\widetilde{U}_{1})
be
r
-dimensional eigenspaces of
A
and
\widetilde{A}
, respectively, where
U_{1}^{H}U_{1}=\widetilde{U}_{1}^{H}\widetilde{U}_{1}=I_{r}
.
The canonical angle between
\mathcal{R}(U_{1})
and
\mathcal{R}(\widetilde{U}_{1})
is defined by

\Theta(U_{1},\widetilde{U}_{1})=\arccos(U_{1}^{H}\widetilde{U}_{1}\widetilde{U}_{1}^{H}U_{1})^{1/2}.

Under the condition

\delta_{12}=\min_{\lambda\in\lambda(U_{1}^{H}AU_{1}),\widetilde{\lambda}\in\lambda(\widetilde{A})/\lambda(\widetilde{U}_{1}^{H}\widetilde{A}\widetilde{U}_{1})}\{|\lambda-\widetilde{\lambda}|\}>0,

Davis and Kahan
[
3
]
provided the following classical perturbation bound for eigenspaces of Hermitian matrices

\displaystyle\|\sin\Theta(U_{1},\widetilde{U}_{1})\|_{F}\leq\frac{1}{\delta_{12}}\|\Delta AU_{1}\|_{F}.

(1.2)

In
[
12
]
, Li and Sun obtained perturbation bounds
in a combined form for eigenspaces and the corresponding eigenvalues.
One of the bounds (
[
12
, Theorem 2.2]
) is

\displaystyle\quad(1-\|\sin\Theta(U_{1},\widetilde{U}_{1})\|_{2}^{2})\|{\rm Eig}^{\downarrow}(\widetilde{U}_{1}^{H}\widetilde{A}\widetilde{U}_{1})-{\rm Eig}^{\downarrow}(U_{1}^{H}AU_{1})\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad+\delta^{2}_{12}\|\sin\Theta(U_{1},\widetilde{U}_{1})\|_{F}^{2}\leq\|\Delta AU_{1}\|_{F}^{2}.

(1.3)

The bound (
1
) is sharper than (
1.2
). When
r=n
, one has

\sin\Theta(U_{1},\widetilde{U}_{1})=0
and the inequality (
1
) reduces to (
1.1
). Similar perturbation bounds have been established for singular values and (left and right) singular subspaces of a general matrix (
[
10
,
11
,
12
,
16
,
17
]
).

In this paper we will provide same types of combined bounds for the eigenvalues and
eigenspaces of a Hermitian matrix and the singular values and singular subspaces of
a general matrix. The contributions of the work can be summarized as follows, which
is for Hermitian eigenvalue problem only. It is similar for the SVD results.

(a)

The techniques involved in
[
2
,
4
,
9
]
are essentially elementary calculus. This is different from ones in
[
12
]
, where advanced inequalities are employed;

(b)

We derive novel local bounds for perturbation of eigenvalues and several eigenspaces and for one eigenspace and its corresponding eigenvalues;

(c)

For measuring perturbation of eigenspaces, instead of using the canonical angle we use the distance between two orthonormal basis matrices. As a consequence, the derived bound essentially implies a bound (
1
), so it is potentially sharper.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. In Section 2 we present combined perturbation bounds for eigenspaces and corresponding eigenvalues of a Hermitian matrix.
In Section 3 we derive combined perturbation bounds for singular subspaces and corresponding singular values of a general matrix. Section 4 contains our conclusions.

2
Combined bounds of eigenvalues and eigenspaces

In this section we derive combined perturbation bounds for eigenspaces and corresponding eigenvalues of a Hermitian matrix. The following result is essential for deriving our main results.

Lemma 2.1

Suppose
U(t)=[U_{1}(t),\ldots,U_{k}(t)]
is an
n\times n
unitary analytic time-dependent matrix of a real variable
t
, where

U_{j}(t)\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times r_{j}},\quad j=1,\ldots,k,\quad\mbox{and}\quad r_{1}+\cdots+r_{k}=n.

Then for any given skew Hermitian analytic time-dependent matrices
\Phi_{j}(t)\in\mathbb{C}^{r_{j}\times r_{j}},j=1,\ldots,k
, there exist unitary analytic time-dependent matrices
P_{j}(t)\in\mathbb{C}^{r_{j}\times r_{j}}
,

j=1,\ldots,k
, such that for
\widehat{U}_{j}(t)=U_{j}(t)P_{j}(t)
one has
\widehat{U}_{j}(t)^{H}\dot{\widehat{U}}_{j}(t)=\Phi_{j}(t)
for
j=1,\ldots,k
.

Proof
. Taking the derivative on both sides of
U(t)^{H}U(t)=I
leads to

U(t)^{H}\dot{U}(t)=-\dot{U}(t)^{H}U(t)=-(U(t)^{H}\dot{U}(t))^{H}.

Hence
U_{j}(t)^{H}\dot{U}_{j}(t)
is a skew Hermitian matrix for
j=1,\ldots,k.

Let
P(t)={\rm diag}(P_{1}(t),\ldots,P_{k}(t))
and
\widehat{U}(t)=U(t)P(t)
. Then

\displaystyle\widehat{U}(t)^{H}\dot{\widehat{U}}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P(t)^{H}U(t)^{H}\left[\dot{U}(t)P(t)+U(t)\dot{P}(t)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P(t)^{H}U(t)^{H}\dot{U}(t)P(t)+P(t)^{H}\dot{P}(t),

which can be written as

\dot{P}(t)=P(t)\left[\widehat{U}(t)^{H}\dot{\widehat{U}}(t)-P(t)^{H}U(t)^{H}\dot{U}(t)P(t)\right].

By comparing the
j
-th diagonal block on both sides of the above equation,
P_{j}(t)
has to satisfy the differential equation:

\dot{P}_{j}(t)=P_{j}(t)\left[\Phi_{j}(t)-P_{j}(t)^{H}U_{j}(t)^{H}\dot{U}_{j}(t)P_{j}(t)\right],\quad j=1,\ldots,k.

Since
\Phi_{j}(t)-P_{j}(t)^{H}U_{j}(t)^{H}\dot{U}_{j}(t)P_{j}(t)
is skew Hermitian as a sum of skew Hermitian matrices with the
initial condition
P_{j}(0)=I
, the differential equation has unique solution that is unitary
and analytic
[
4
]
. This shows the existence of
P_{j}(t),j=1,\ldots,k.

Let
A(t)=A+t\Delta A\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
be a Hermitian matrix with
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Then
it is known (
[
5
,
9
,
13
,
14
]
) that
A(t)
has an analytic decomposition

\displaystyle A(t)=U(t)\Lambda(t)U(t)^{H},

(2.1)

where

\displaystyle U(t)=\left[U_{1}(t),\ldots,U_{k}(t)\right],\qquad U_{j}(t)\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n\times r_{j}},\quad j=1,\ldots,k,

(2.2)

is unitary and analytic,
and

\displaystyle\Lambda(t)=\mathop{\rm diag}\nolimits(\Lambda_{1}(t),\ldots,\Lambda_{k}(t)),\qquad\Lambda_{j}(t)\in{{\mathbb{C}}}^{r_{j}\times r_{j}},\quad j=1,\ldots,k

(2.3)

is analytic. Note that
\Lambda_{1}(t),\ldots,\Lambda_{k}(t)
may or may not be diagonal.

The analytic decomposition (
2.1
) can be considered as a generalized spectral decomposition and it is not unique. We have the following
results with a special choice of
U(t)
.

Lemma 2.2

Let
A(t)=A+t\Delta A\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
be a Hermitian matrix with
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Then

A(t)
has the analytic decomposition (
2.1
) with

U(t)
and
\Lambda(t)
in the block forms (
2.2
) and (
2.3
), and
U(t)

satisfies

\displaystyle U_{j}(t)^{H}\dot{U}_{j}(t)=0,\quad j=1,\ldots,k.

(2.4)

Proof
. Let
A(t)
have an analytic decomposition (
2.1
). Then for
arbitrary unitary analytic matrices
P_{j}(t)\in{{\mathbb{C}}}^{r_{j}\times r_{j}}
,
j=1,\ldots,k
,
we have

A(t)=\widehat{U}(t)\widehat{\Lambda}(t)\widehat{U}(t)^{H},

where

\widehat{U}(t)=U(t)\mathop{\rm diag}\nolimits(P_{1}(t),\ldots,P_{k}(t)),\quad\widehat{\Lambda}(t)=\mathop{\rm diag}\nolimits(P_{1}(t)^{H}\Lambda_{1}(t)P_{1}(t),\ldots,P_{k}(t)^{H}\Lambda_{k}(t)P_{k}(t)).

By Lemma
2.1
with
\Phi_{j}(t)=zeros(r_{j},r_{j})
,
P_{1}(t),\ldots,P_{k}(t)
can be selected such that

\widehat{U}_{j}(t)^{H}\dot{\widehat{U}}_{j}(t)=0,\quad j=1,\dots,k.

Hence
A(t)
has an analytic decomposition (
2.1
) that satisfies (
2.4
).

We have the following perturbation result of the eigenspaces
{\mathcal{R}}(U_{j}(0))
,

j=1,\ldots,k
, and the eigenvalues of
A=A(0)
.

Theorem 2.1

Let
A(t)=A+t\Delta A\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
be a Hermitian matrix with
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
.
Suppose
A(t)
has the analytic decomposition (
2.1
) with
U(t)
satisfying
(
2.2
) and (
2.4
) and
\Lambda(t)
in the block diagonal form (
2.3
).
Define

\displaystyle\delta_{ji}(t)=\min_{\lambda_{1}(t)\in\lambda(\Lambda_{j}(t)),~\lambda_{2}(t)\in\lambda(\Lambda_{i}(t))}\left\{\left|\lambda_{1}(t)-\lambda_{2}(t)\right|\right\},\quad\delta_{j}(t)=\min_{i\neq j}\left\{\delta_{ji}(t)\right\},

(2.5)

and

\displaystyle\delta_{j,\min}=\min_{0\leq t\leq 1}\delta_{j}(t),

(2.6)

for
j=1,\ldots,k
. Denote
\widetilde{A}=:A(1)=A+\Delta A
and

\displaystyle U(0)=\left[U_{1}(0),\ldots,U_{k}(0)]=:[U_{1},\ldots,U_{k}\right]=U,

\displaystyle U(1)=\left[U_{1}(1),\ldots,U_{k}(1)\right]=:[\widetilde{U}_{1},\ldots,\widetilde{U}_{k}]=\widetilde{U}.

Then

\displaystyle\|{\rm Eig}^{\downarrow}(\widetilde{A})-{\rm Eig}^{\downarrow}(A)\|_{F}^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\delta_{j,\min}^{2}\|\widetilde{U}_{j}-U_{j}\|_{F}^{2}\leq\|{\Delta A}\|_{F}^{2}.

(2.7)

Proof
. By taking derivatives on both sides of (
2.1
) and
U^{H}(t)U(t)=I,
we have

\displaystyle\Delta A=\dot{U}(t)\Lambda(t)U(t)^{H}+U(t)\dot{\Lambda}(t)U(t)^{H}+U(t)\Lambda(t)\dot{U}(t)^{H}

(2.8)

and

\displaystyle\dot{U}(t)^{H}U(t)=-U(t)^{H}\dot{U}(t).

(2.9)

Multiplying both sides of (
2.8
) with
U(t)^{H}
on the left and
U(t)
on the right,
and using (
2.9
), we obtain

\displaystyle U(t)^{H}\Delta AU(t)=\dot{\Lambda}(t)+U(t)^{H}\dot{U}(t)\Lambda(t)-\Lambda(t)U(t)^{H}\dot{U}(t).

(2.10)

Let

U_{i}(t)^{H}\Delta AU_{j}(t)=:[\Delta A_{ij}(t)],\quad i,j=1,\ldots,k.

By comparing the blocks of (
2.10
) and using (
2.4
), one obtain its diagonal terms

\displaystyle\dot{\Lambda}_{j}(t)=\Delta A_{jj}(t),\quad j=1,\ldots,k,

(2.11)

and off-diagonal terms

\displaystyle U_{i}(t)^{H}\dot{U}_{j}(t)\Lambda_{j}(t)-\Lambda_{i}(t)U_{i}(t)^{H}\dot{U}_{j}(t)=\Delta A_{ij}(t),\qquad i\neq j.

(2.12)

Suppose that

\Lambda_{j}(t)=G_{j}(t)\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}\lambda_{j,1}(t)&&\\
&\ddots&\\
&&\lambda_{j,r_{j}}(t)\end{array}\right]G_{j}(t)^{H},\quad j=1,\ldots,k,

are spectral decompositions (not necessarily analytic),
where
G_{1}(t),\ldots,G_{k}(t)

are unitary matrices. Multiplying both sides of (
2.12
) with
G_{i}(t)^{H}
on the left and
G_{j}(t)
on the right yields

\displaystyle X_{ij}(t)\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}\lambda_{j,1}(t)&&\\
&\ddots&\\
&&\lambda_{j,r_{j}}(t)\end{array}\right]-\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}\lambda_{i,1}(t)&&\\
&\ddots&\\
&&\lambda_{i,r_{i}}(t)\end{array}\right]X_{ij}(t)=Z_{ij}(t),

where
X_{ij}(t)=G_{i}(t)^{H}U_{i}(t)^{H}\dot{U}_{j}(t)G_{j}(t)

and
Z_{ij}(t)=G_{i}(t)^{H}\Delta A_{ij}(t)G_{j}(t)
. Let

X_{ij}(t)=[x^{(ij)}_{pq}(t)],\quad Z_{ij}(t)=[z_{pq}^{(ij)}(t)].

From (
2
) one has

\displaystyle z^{(ij)}_{pq}(t)=(\lambda_{j,q}(t)-\lambda_{i,p}(t))x_{pq}^{(ij)}(t),\quad p=1,\ldots,r_{i},\quad q=1,\ldots,r_{j}.

Then for
\delta_{ji}(t)
defined in (
2.5
), one has

\displaystyle\|{\Delta A_{ij}(t)}\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|{Z_{ij}(t)}\|_{F}^{2}=\sum_{p=1}^{r_{i}}\sum_{q=1}^{r_{j}}|z_{pq}^{(ij)}(t)|^{2}=\sum_{p=1}^{r_{i}}\sum_{q=1}^{r_{j}}|\lambda_{j,q}(t)-\lambda_{i,p}(t)|^{2}|x_{pq}^{(ij)}(t)|^{2}

(2.20)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\delta_{ji}(t)^{2}\sum_{p=1}^{r_{i}}\sum_{q=1}^{r_{j}}|x_{pq}^{(ij)}(t)|^{2}=\delta_{ji}(t)^{2}\|{U_{i}(t)^{H}\dot{U}_{j}(t)}\|_{F}^{2}.

By (
2.4
), we have

\displaystyle\|{\dot{U}_{j}(t)}\|_{F}^{2}=\|{U(t)^{H}\dot{U}_{j}(t)}\|_{F}^{2}=\sum_{i=1,i\neq j}^{k}\|{U_{i}(t)^{H}\dot{U}_{j}(t)}\|_{F}^{2}.

(2.21)

Then we get

\displaystyle\|{\Delta A}\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|{U(t)^{H}\Delta AU(t)}\|_{F}^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\|{\Delta A_{jj}(t)}\|_{F}^{2}+\sum_{i\neq j}\|{\Delta A_{ij}(t)}\|_{F}^{2}

(2.22)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{k}\|{\dot{\Lambda}_{j}(t)}\|_{F}^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sum_{i=1,i\neq j}^{k}\delta_{ji}(t)^{2}\|{U_{i}(t)^{H}\dot{U}_{j}(t)}\|_{F}^{2}\qquad(\mbox{by}\;(\ref{c-2-5})\;\mbox{and}\;(\ref{c-2-8}))

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\|{\dot{\Lambda}(t)}\|_{F}^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\delta_{j}(t)^{2}\sum_{i=1,i\neq j}^{k}\|{U_{i}(t)^{H}\dot{U}_{j}(t)}\|_{F}^{2}\qquad(\mbox{by}\;(\ref{c-2-1}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|{\dot{\Lambda}(t)}\|_{F}^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\delta_{j}(t)^{2}\|{\dot{U}_{j}(t)}\|_{F}^{2}\qquad(\mbox{by}\;(\ref{c-2-9}))

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\|{\dot{\Lambda}(t)}\|_{F}^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\delta_{j,\min}^{2}\|{\dot{U}_{j}(t)}\|_{F}^{2}.\qquad(\mbox{by}\;(\ref{c-2-2}))

Using the fact that

\left|\int_{0}^{1}f(t){\rm d}t\right|^{2}\leq\int_{0}^{1}|f(t)|^{2}{\rm d}t,\quad\forall f(t),

and together with (
2.22
), we get

\displaystyle\|{\Delta A}\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\|{\Delta A}\|_{F}^{2}{\rm d}t\geq\int_{0}^{1}\|{\dot{\Lambda}(t)}\|_{F}^{2}{\rm d}t+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\delta_{j,\min}^{2}\int_{0}^{1}\|{\dot{U}_{j}(t)}\|_{F}^{2}{\rm d}t

(2.23)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{0}^{1}\dot{\Lambda}(t){\rm d}t\right\|_{F}^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\delta_{j,\min}^{2}\left\|\int_{0}^{1}\dot{U}_{j}(t){\rm d}t\right\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|{\Lambda(1)-\Lambda(0)}\|_{F}^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\delta_{j,\min}^{2}\|{U_{j}(1)-U_{j}(0)}\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|{\Lambda(1)-\Lambda(0)}\|_{F}^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\delta_{j,\min}^{2}\|{\widetilde{U}_{j}-U_{j}}\|_{F}^{2}.

By (
2.23
) and the inequality

\|{\rm Eig}^{\downarrow}(\widetilde{A})-{\rm Eig}^{\downarrow}(A)\|_{F}=\|{\rm Eig}^{\downarrow}(\Lambda(1))-{\rm Eig}^{\downarrow}(\Lambda(0))\|_{F}\leq\|{\Lambda(1)-\Lambda(0)}\|_{F},

which is from (
1.1
), we obtain (
2.7
).

Remark
2.1

The combined perturbation bound (
2.7
) is sharper than (
1.1
). When
k=1
, (
2.4
) implies
\widetilde{U}=U
. In this case, (
2.7
) reduces to (
1.1
).

Remark
2.2

In order to include the term
\|\widetilde{U}_{j}-U_{j}\|_{F}^{2}
in (
2.7
), we need
\delta_{j,\min}>0
, for which a sufficient condition is

2\|\Delta A\|_{2}<\delta_{j}(0),

where
\delta_{j}(0)
is given by (
2.5
). In fact, for any
\lambda_{1}(t)\in\lambda(\Lambda_{j}(t))
and
\lambda_{2}(t)\in\cup_{i\neq j}\lambda(\Lambda_{i}(t))

with
t\in(0,~1]
, there exist (
[
16
, Chapter IV, Corollary 4.10]
)
\mu\in\lambda(\Lambda_{j}(0))
and
\nu\in\cup_{i\neq j}\lambda(\Lambda_{i}(0))

such that

|\lambda_{1}(t)-\mu|\leq\|\Delta A\|_{2},\quad|\lambda_{2}(t)-\nu|\leq\|\Delta A\|_{2}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle|\lambda_{1}(t)-\lambda_{2}(t)|\geq|\mu-\nu|-|\lambda_{1}(t)-\mu|-|\lambda_{2}(t)-\nu|\geq\delta_{j}(0)-2\|\Delta A\|_{2},

which implies

\delta_{j,\min}\geq\delta_{j}(0)-2\|\Delta A\|_{2}>0.

Remark
2.3

When
k=n
and
r_{1}=\cdots=r_{n}=1
, (

) is a spectral decomposition.
All
U_{1},\ldots,U_{n}
and
\widetilde{U}_{1},\ldots,\widetilde{U}_{n}

are eigenvectors and (

) bounds the perturbations of all the eigenvalues and
eigenvectors of

(with the assumption that
\delta_{j,\min}>0
for

).
In particular, (
2.7
) implies

\|{\rm Eig}^{\downarrow}(\widetilde{A})-{\rm Eig}^{\downarrow}(A)\|_{F}^{2}+\delta_{\min}^{2}\|{\widetilde{U}-U}\|_{F}^{2}\leq\|{\Delta A}\|_{F}^{2},

where
\delta_{\min}=\min_{1\leq j\leq k}\{\delta_{j,\min}\}
. When
\delta_{\min}>0
,
it bounds the perturbations of all the eigenvalues and the entire unitary similarity matrix
U

of

.

Remark
2.4

In Theorem

, for each

,
\|\widetilde{U}_{j}-U_{j}\|_{F}
measures the perturbation of the eigenspace
\mathcal{R}(U_{j})

The combined bound (

and applying (

) to the eigenspaces
\mathcal{R}(U_{1}),\ldots,\mathcal{R}(U_{k})

\displaystyle\|{\rm Eig}^{\downarrow}(\widetilde{A})-{\rm Eig}^{\downarrow}(A)\|_{F}^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}{\widetilde{\delta}}^{2}_{j}\,\|\sin\Theta(U_{j},\widetilde{U}_{j})\|_{2}^{2}\leq\|\Delta A\|_{F}^{2},

(2.24)

where

\widetilde{\delta}_{j}^{2}=:\delta_{j}^{2}-\|{\rm Eig}^{\downarrow}(\Lambda_{j}(1))-{\rm Eig}^{\downarrow}(\Lambda_{j}(0))\|_{F}^{2},\qquad\delta_{j}=\min_{\lambda_{1}\in\lambda(\Lambda_{j}),\lambda_{2}\in\cup_{i\neq j}\lambda(\widetilde{\Lambda}_{i})}\{|\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}|\},

for
j=1,2,\ldots,k
.
Since (
[

, Chapter I, Theorem 5.5]
)

\|\sin\Theta(U_{j},\widetilde{U}_{j})\|_{F}^{2}=\sum_{i=1,i\neq j}^{k}\|U_{i}^{H}\widetilde{U}_{j}\|_{F}^{2}

and

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{U}_{j}-U_{j}\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|U^{H}(\widetilde{U}_{j}-U_{j})\|_{F}^{2}=\|U_{j}^{H}\widetilde{U}_{j}-I\|_{F}^{2}+\sum_{i=1,i\neq j}^{k}\|U_{i}^{H}\widetilde{U}_{j}\|_{F}^{2}

(2.25)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1,i\neq j}^{k}\|U_{i}^{H}\widetilde{U}_{j}\|_{F}^{2}=\|\sin\Theta(U_{j},\widetilde{U}_{j})\|_{F}^{2},

when
\delta_{j,\min}^{2}
is sufficiently close to
\widetilde{\delta}_{j}^{2}
for all

,
(
2.7
) implies (
2.24
). Note when
\delta_{j,\min}>0
, one can verify that

\delta_{j,\min}-\widetilde{\delta}_{j}=O(\|\Delta A\|_{F})
when

is sufficiently small.

The following result gives a combined perturbation bound for one eigenspace and its corresponding eigenvalues of a Hermitian matrix, which is similar to (

). Without loss of generality, we consider

\mathcal{R}(U_{1})
, where

Theorem 2.2

Under the assumptions of Theorem
2.1
, if
\|\Delta A\|_{2}<\delta_{1,\min}
, then we have

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{\|{\Delta A}\|_{2}}{\delta_{1,\min}}\right)^{2}\|{\rm Eig}^{\downarrow}(\widetilde{\Lambda}_{1})-{\rm Eig}^{\downarrow}(\Lambda_{1})\|_{F}^{2}+\left(\delta_{1,\min}-\|{\Delta A}\|_{2}\right)^{2}\|\widetilde{U}_{1}-U_{1}\|_{F}^{2}\leq\|\Delta AU_{1}\|_{F}^{2},

(2.26)

where
\widetilde{\Lambda}_{1}=\Lambda_{1}(1)
and
\Lambda_{1}=\Lambda_{1}(0)
.

Proof
. From (
2.4
), (
2.11
) and (
2.20

\widehat{\rho}_{\min}=\min\{\sigma_{\min},\min_{j}\{\widehat{\rho}_{j,\min}\}\}
and

.

(3.36)

For any

\displaystyle\|{\Delta BV_{1}(t)}\|_{F}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|{\Delta BV_{1}+\Delta B(V_{1}(t)-V_{1}(0))}\|_{F}

\displaystyle\|{\Delta BV_{1}}\|_{F}+\|{\Delta B}\|_{2}\|{V_{1}(t^{\star})-V_{1}(0)}\|_{F}

where

\displaystyle\|{V_{1}(t^{\star})-V_{1}(0)}\|_{F}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|{W_{1}(t^{\star\star})-W_{1}(0)}\|_{F}

4
Conclusion

By using a specific analytic decomposition, we obtain a combined bound for perturbations of the eigenspaces of a Hermitian matrix that form a direct sum of the entire vector space
and all the eigenvalues. Combined bounds for a single eigenspace and its corresponding eigenvalues are also provided. The bounds are similar to the existing ones
in
[
12
]
but potentially sharper. Elementary and simple calculus tools are employed for deriving the bounds. The same types of combined perturbation bounds are also derived for the left and right singular subspaces and singular values of a general matrix.

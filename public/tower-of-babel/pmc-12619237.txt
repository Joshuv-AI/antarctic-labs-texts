Mobile health and postoperative self-care following glaucoma, cataract, or corneal transplant surgeries
Authors: Hamid Moghaddasi, Reza Rabiei, Hossein Valizadeh Laktarashi
Published: 2025

Background

In today’s world, enhancing the quality of healthcare services and promoting public health are among the core objectives of modern health systems. One of the key components in achieving these goals is the concept of self-care, which is defined as a set of conscious, learnable, and purposeful activities aimed at maintaining and improving an individual’s physical and mental well-being [1]. Self-care empowers individuals to actively manage their own health by utilizing acquired skills and capabilities when faced with health-related challenges. This approach not only reduces patients’ dependence on healthcare facilities but also serves as a critical factor in improving quality of life and accelerating recovery during critical health periods [2].

Given the critical nature of ocular health, especially in the postoperative period, the importance of self-care in this domain becomes even more pronounced [3]. Due to the delicate nature of the surgical sites, ophthalmic surgeries require strict adherence to postoperative instructions, specialized care, and consistent follow-up. Neglecting hygiene protocols or postoperative guidelines can result in serious complications, including irreversible vision loss [4]. Therefore, educating and empowering patients to perform proper self-care following eye surgery is considered a fundamental requirement for improving clinical outcomes.

Ophthalmic conditions such as glaucoma, cataracts, and disorders requiring corneal transplantation are among the leading causes of visual impairment and blindness worldwide [5–7]. According to the World Health Organization’s 2023 report, over 2.2 billion people globally suffer from some form of visual impairment, at least one billion of which could have been prevented or treated [8]. These figures underscore the critical importance of focusing on these conditions and implementing effective management strategies. Glaucoma and cataracts are considered the most common causes of preventable blindness in many countries [9], while corneal transplantation remains one of the final therapeutic options for certain irreversible corneal diseases [5].

Glaucoma is one of the most prevalent ocular diseases, characterized by elevated intraocular pressure that can lead to optic nerve damage and, ultimately, irreversible blindness [10]. It is projected that by 2040, more than 112 million people worldwide will be living with glaucoma [11]. Management of glaucoma typically involves pharmacological therapy, laser procedures, or surgery. Surgical intervention is an effective method for reducing intraocular pressure; however, its long-term success is highly dependent on the patient’s adherence to postoperative care, including the regular use of prescribed eye drops and compliance with medical instructions [12]. Failure to engage in adequate self-care may result in elevated intraocular pressure and further optic nerve damage [13].

Cataract, the leading cause of blindness globally, is a condition marked by the clouding of the eye’s natural lens and is commonly associated with aging, trauma, or certain systemic diseases [14]. The primary treatment is surgical, involving the removal of the clouded lens and its replacement with an artificial intraocular lens [15]. Although cataract surgery is among the safest and most effective ophthalmic procedures, its success is highly contingent upon strict adherence to postoperative care protocols [16]. These include the regular use of antibiotic and anti-inflammatory eye drops, proper eye protection, and consistent follow-up with ophthalmologists. Neglecting these measures can result in complications such as infection, inflammation, and suboptimal surgical outcomes [17].

Corneal transplantation, or keratoplasty, is one of the most commonly performed surgical procedures for treating severe corneal diseases such as keratoconus, chronic infections, and trauma-induced damage [18]. This complex surgical intervention requires meticulous postoperative care to prevent graft rejection, infection, and other complications [19]. Appropriate care—including the correct use of immunosuppressive medications, avoidance of strenuous activities, and regular medical follow-ups plays a vital role in ensuring surgical success [20].

A common thread among glaucoma, cataract, and corneal transplant surgeries is that their success and long-term outcomes heavily depend on patients’ adherence to postoperative care protocols [21–23]. Non-compliance with these regimens may result in serious complications and, in some cases, surgical failure [24–26]. Nevertheless, many patients struggle to follow postoperative instructions properly due to a variety of factors, including lack of awareness, forgetfulness, and limited access to reliable informational or support resources [27].

These three ophthalmic conditions were examined concurrently in this study for several reasons. First, all three are classified as major eye disorders that impose a substantial burden on healthcare systems [5–7]. Second, there are notable similarities in the postoperative care requirements for these conditions, including the critical use of topical medications, prevention of infection and inflammation, and the necessity for regular follow-up examinations [28, 29]. Third, investigating these conditions collectively enables the development of a more comprehensive and practical postoperative management solution one that can accommodate a broader spectrum of patients [30].

Recent advancements in mobile health (mHealth) and the widespread adoption of smartphones have created new opportunities for improving disease management and enhancing patient self-care [31]. Mobile health applications can serve as effective tools to assist patients in managing their treatment and adhering to postoperative care regimens [32]. The success of mobile-based interventions largely depends on their design quality, with the identification of users’ informational needs being the first critical step toward achieving this goal [33]. These applications offer a range of features, including medication reminders, tailored postoperative instructions based on individual needs, easy and direct communication with healthcare providers for reporting issues, and daily health assessments through questionnaires or simple tests [34]. In addition, these tools can assist healthcare professionals in delivering more precise care instructions and tracking patient progress. For example, physicians can use such applications to define personalized treatment plans for patients and, by receiving regular feedback on their condition, make timely adjustments when necessary [35]. In this study, a mobile health application was designed and evaluated with the aim of facilitating self-care among patients following glaucoma, cataract, and corneal transplant surgeries. The application was developed based on the specific needs of these patients using a participatory design approach.

Methods

Mobile health application design

A comprehensive literature search was conducted in April 2024 across four major electronic databases: PubMed, Web of Science, Scopus, and Medline. The search strategy was designed using a combination of Medical Subject Headings (MeSH) terms and free-text keywords related to self-care, mobile health, and the target ophthalmic surgeries. No date or language filters were applied to maximize retrieval. The specific search string used was:

(“self-care” OR “self-management”) AND (“mobile health” OR “mHealth” OR “smartphone application”) AND (“glaucoma” OR “cataract” OR “corneal transplantation” OR “keratoplasty”). Relevant literature was reviewed to better understand the self-care needs of patients following glaucoma, cataract, and corneal transplant surgeries.

Needs assessment through patient interviews

A qualitative needs assessment was conducted using semi-structured interviews to gain an in-depth understanding of the self-care challenges and informational needs of patients during the postoperative period. A total of fifteen patients were purposively sampled from the ophthalmology department of the target hospital, comprising five patients from each of the three surgical groups: glaucoma, cataract, and corneal transplantation. The interview guide was developed based on the preliminary findings from the literature review and in consultation with the expert panel.

All interviews were conducted in Persian, audio-recorded, and transcribed verbatim. The transcribed data were analyzed using conventional content analysis, as outlined by Elo and Kyngäs [36]. The qualitative data analysis software MAXQDA (version 2022) was used to facilitate the organization and management of the coding process. To enhance the trustworthiness and credibility of the findings, analyst triangulation was employed. The two researchers initially coded the transcripts independently. Any discrepancies in coding or categorization were discussed and resolved through consensus meetings with the third researcher (R.R.) until a unified coding scheme was achieved. The insights derived from this thematic analysis were instrumental in identifying and prioritizing the functional requirements for the mobile health application, ensuring its alignment with the real-world needs of the target patient population.

Based on insights from the literature and patient interviews, the functional requirements of the mobile health application were identified. These included: providing medical information about eye diseases, details on relevant surgical procedures, postoperative self-care guidelines for each surgery type, and tools for managing reminders for medication usage and follow-up appointments.

An expert panel was convened, consisting of two ophthalmologists and two medical informatics specialists. This panel reviewed and refined the functional requirements and informational content of the application. Following this, a conceptual model of the application was designed using an object-oriented analysis approach in Visio 2021, comprising functional, structural, and behavioral models.

The mobile health application was then developed in accordance with the conceptual model using Java programming language within Android Studio (version 21). The application underwent heuristic evaluation by four medical informatics experts to assess the user interface and identify strengths and weaknesses. Additionally, usability assessment was conducted based on Nielsen’s usability heuristics to evaluate interface effectiveness and design quality.

Evaluation of the mobile health application

Following the development phase, the mobile health application was tested by a group of end users comprising 10 patients who had undergone glaucoma surgery, 10 patients following cataract surgery, and 10 corneal transplant recipients. All patients were recruited from an ophthalmology hospital affiliated with Shahid Beheshti University of Medical Sciences. Participants were purposively sampled based on the following inclusion criteria: (1) having undergone glaucoma surgery, cataract surgery, or corneal transplantation within the past one month; (2) ownership of an Android smartphone; (3) minimum educational level of high school diploma; and (4) willingness to provide informed consent. Exclusion criteria included: (1) significant visual or cognitive impairment preventing use of the application; (2) lack of familiarity with smartphone usage; and (3) unwillingness to participate in the study. Participants ranged in age from 45 to 66 years. Additionally, to assess the services and overall features of the application, it was evaluated by five board-certified ophthalmologists. Ethical approval for the study was obtained from the institutional ethics committee.

The evaluation data were collected using a researcher-developed checklist designed to assess user satisfaction, consisting of 29 items categorized into three main sections. The first category assessed the application’s user interface and included 14 questions. The second category evaluated the application’s services and general features, comprising 11 questions. The third category addressed overall user satisfaction and contained 4 questions. To ensure content validity, the checklist was reviewed and approved by the expert panel involved in the application development. Each item was carefully examined by the panel members to determine its relevance and alignment with the study objectives, and necessary modifications were implemented accordingly.

To assess the reliability of the checklist, the Kuder-Richardson Formula 20 (KR-20) coefficient was used to measure the internal consistency of each subscale separately. The calculated KR-20 values were 0.89 for the User Interface subscale (14 items), 0.86 for the Services and Features subscale (11 items), and 0.82 for the Overall Satisfaction subscale (4 items), all indicating acceptable reliability. After confirming the checklist’s validity and the reliability of its subscales, the final version was distributed to participants.

Participants completed the checklist after using the application for two weeks. Data collected from the checklist were analyzed using SPSS version 26. All checklist items carried equal weight. Each positive response (“Yes”) was scored as 1, and each negative response (“No”) was scored as 0. In addition, four interpretive scales were developed to analyze the checklist results:

Results

Findings related to application design

The functional requirements and services incorporated into the self-care mobile health application for patients after glaucoma surgery, cataract removal, and corneal transplantation were identified based on a comprehensive literature review and a needs assessment conducted through patient interviews. According to the defined functional requirements, the application needed an appropriate name; therefore, the name “INOOR” meaning vision or sight was selected to reflect its focus on ocular health. The application logo was inspired by themes related to eye health.

To ensure data confidentiality, user authentication is required. Upon registration which includes creating a username and password the user must be authenticated by the application before gaining access to the main interface. Users must be able to enter essential personal and medical information, including their name, surname, age, specific eye condition, and any underlying health issues.

The application must also provide reminders for timely medication use and scheduled medical appointments. Additionally, it should include educational content on eye anatomy, various surgical techniques for glaucoma, cataract, and corneal transplantation, as well as information on the nature, symptoms, and complications of common eye diseases, aimed at increasing user awareness.

For postoperative self-care, the application must offer tailored guidelines for each type of surgery. These include sections on ocular hygiene practices, appropriate nutrition, instructions for daily activities, medication management, situations requiring immediate medical attention, and strategies for addressing patients’ psychological and social needs following surgery. Given that the primary user base comprises Iranian and Persian-speaking patients, the INOOR application was designed with a bilingual interface (English and Persian) to maximize accessibility and cultural relevance.

Moreover, users should have the option to return to the home page or exit the application from any screen within the system (see Table 1).

Each section provides specific guidance on topics such as eye hygiene, appropriate dietary recommendations, instructions for daily activities and lifestyle, medication management, follow-up appointments, and strategies for addressing psychological and social needs after surgery. All relevant educational materials comprising textual content, images, and instructional videos are made accessible to users. Upon selecting each section, users can view step-by-step instructions supported by visuals and video demonstrations.

Application evaluation findings

The application was evaluated by a total of 30 patients, including 10 who had undergone glaucoma surgery, 10 who had cataract surgery, and 10 who had corneal transplantation. Among these participants, 17 (56%) were male and 13 (44%) were female. In terms of educational background, 19 participants (63%) held a high school diploma, 9 (30%) had a bachelor’s degree, and 2 (7%) held a master’s degree. Regarding age distribution, 2 participants (6%) were between 45 and 50 years old, 5 (17%) between 50 and 55 years, 10 (33%) between 55 and 60 years, and 13 (44%) were aged 60–66 years. In addition, the application was evaluated by five ophthalmology specialists. Concerning their professional experience, one specialist (20%) had 5–10 years of experience, two (40%) had 10–15 years, and another two (40%) had 15–20 years of experience.

The average score from patient responses regarding the usability and user interface of the application was 12.43 (SD = 1.24, Range = 10–14, 95% CI: 11.95–12.91), indicating general satisfaction. The combined average rating from both patients and ophthalmologists concerning the application’s services and overall features was 8.96 (SD = 1.05, Range = 7–11, 95% CI: 8.58–9.34), which is considered satisfactory. Furthermore, the overall user satisfaction score was 3.43 (SD = 0.57, Range = 2–4, 95% CI: 3.22–3.64). Thus, the total mean score across user interface, service features, and overall satisfaction was 24.82 (SD = 2.22, Range = 20–28, 95% CI: 23.97–25.67), which, based on the predefined scoring scale, suggests that the application achieved an acceptable level of user satisfaction. A one-way ANOVA was conducted to compare the total satisfaction scores across the three patient subgroups (glaucoma, cataract, and corneal transplant). No statistically significant difference was found between the groups [F (2, 27) = 0.451, p = 0.642], indicating that the application’s acceptability was consistent regardless of the type of surgery undergone (see Table 2).

Discussion and conclusions

Statistical findings from previous studies indicate that mobile-based applications can play a significant role in facilitating patient education and enhancing self-care following surgical procedures. However, there has been a noticeable lack of attention to the development of mobile applications specifically aimed at supporting postoperative self-care in patients undergoing ocular surgeries. Furthermore, the impact of mobile-based educational applications on factors such as quality of life, reduction of postoperative complications, and facilitation of patient self-care has been explored in prior research, yielding statistically significant results [37, 38].

Accordingly, the present study focused on the design and development of a mobile health application intended to improve self-care among patients following glaucoma surgery, cataract surgery, and corneal transplantation. All services incorporated within the application were aligned with the core objectives of promoting effective self-care in the postoperative period for these patient groups.

According to the findings of this study, users expressed high satisfaction with features such as the provision of comprehensive and patient-specific educational content, medication reminders, and appointment scheduling. These results are consistent with a 2024 study focused on the design of a mobile health application for patients with diabetic retinopathy, which was developed using Java programming in two distinct phases: design and evaluation. Usability evaluation of that application was conducted using the Mobile App Usability Questionnaire (MAUQ), and the findings demonstrated that the developed app significantly improved patients’ adherence to treatment plans and enhanced their self-care practices [39]. Similarly, a study by DeLeon and Pajardo in 2024 showed that mobile health applications can strengthen communication between patients and healthcare providers, while also contributing to the reduction of postoperative complications [40].

In 2021, Valente and colleagues conducted a study titled Development and Usability Evaluation of a Mobile Application for Glaucoma Patients. In this study, the “GlaucoCheck” application was developed using Java programming, and its mean usability score, as measured by the System Usability Scale (SUS), was 75.6 indicating good patient acceptance and ease of use [41]. Finally, in 2017, Sample and colleagues investigated the use of mobile applications for postoperative monitoring and concluded that mobile health applications can support the treatment process by providing alerts and reminders, delivering essential self-care education, and enabling continuous communication with the healthcare team [42].

The strengths of the present study include the use of a participatory design approach and a strong focus on the real needs of both patients and healthcare professionals, which contributed to increased acceptance and effectiveness of the application. However, the primary limitation of this study lies in its relatively small sample size and restriction to a single healthcare center, which may affect the generalizability of the findings. Therefore, future research is recommended to involve larger populations and longer evaluation periods to further validate the outcomes.

Overall, the “INOOR” application, with its current capabilities, demonstrates significant potential to enhance self-care processes and reduce the workload of healthcare providers. Future developments may include the integration of personalized feedback mechanisms, the provision of a web-based interface for direct communication with ophthalmologists, and the potential interoperability with other health information systems and wearable devices. Future directions may also include tailoring self-care content to specific age groups (e.g., congenital eye surgeries in children vs. adult surgeries), developing multilingual versions to broaden accessibility, and integrating location-based services to help patients identify nearby eye care centers for follow-up. For example, the SightConnect application developed by Infosys in India provides outpatient referral services for eye care and has been highlighted by the L.V. Prasad Eye Institute (https://www.waeh.org/news/news-from-our-member-l-v-prasad-eye-institute-9/). Such initiatives demonstrate the global potential of mobile health tools in enhancing patient follow-up and referral pathways, supporting the relevance of the INOOR application in postoperative care.

Suggestions

Based on the findings of the present study, it is recommended that healthcare organizations support the design and development of mobile health (mHealth) applications to enhance postoperative self-care among patients undergoing ocular surgeries. Given the critical importance of postoperative care following glaucoma surgery, cataract removal, and corneal transplantation, the application developed in this study may serve as an effective tool for achieving improved clinical outcomes.

By delivering simplified, comprehensible medical information and self-care instructions, alongside functionalities such as medication reminders, appointment scheduling, and alerts related to eye care, this application can potentially reduce postoperative complications and contribute to better quality of life for patients.

Furthermore, it is suggested that this application be integrated into clinical settings as a complementary tool for patient education, ensuring continuous access to essential information while reducing the learning curve for patients. It is also advisable that all healthcare personnel involved in ocular care introduce and recommend the use of this mobile health application to their patients.

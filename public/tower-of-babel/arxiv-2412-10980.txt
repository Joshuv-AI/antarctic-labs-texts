On anticyclotomic Iwasawa Theory of Hecke Characters at ordinary primes
Erman IŞIK

Abstract
. In this article we study the Iwasawa theory for Hecke characters associated with CM abelian varieties and Hilbert modular forms at ordinary primes. We formulate and prove a result concerning the anticyclotomic Iwasawa main conjecture for CM Hilbert modular forms. Additionally, we obtain a result towards the study of the Mordell–Weil ranks of the CM abelian varieties.

11R23 (primary); 11G05, 11R20 (secondary)

1.
Introduction

The main objective of this paper is to study the
p
-ordinary Iwasawa theory for Hecke characters attached to CM abelian varieties and Hilbert modular forms and thereby to extend the results of Agboola–Howard
[
AH06
]
, Arnold
[
Arn07
]
and Büyükboduk
[
Büy14
]
.

Let
K
be a CM field with maximal real subfield
F
of degree
2g
, and let
p
be an odd rational prime unramified in
F/{\mathbb{Q}}
. Assume that the
p
-ordinary condition of Katz holds:

(
p
-ord
)

\text{Every prime of }F\text{ above }p\text{ splits in }K.

When the Hecke character in question is attached to an elliptic curve
E/{\mathbb{Q}}
with complex multiplication by an imaginary quadratic field
K
and the sign of the functional equation is
+1
, the
p
-ordinary Iwasawa main conjecture for
E
along the anticyclotomic
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
-extension of
K
follows from the two-variable main conjecture proved by Rubin in
[
Rub91
]
. When the sign is
-1
, Agboola and Howard proved in
[
AH06
]
the anticyclotomic main conjecture and Arnold generalized their result in
[
Arn07
]
to higher-weight CM forms. The key ingredients in all these cases are the elliptic unit Euler system and the reciprocity laws of Coates–Wiles
[
CW77
,
Wil78
]
and Kato
[
Kat99
]
, as well as Rubin’s two-variable main conjecture.

Rubin’s result on the Iwasawa main conjecture for imaginary quadratic fields has been extended to various settings by many people utilizing different techniques
[
HT94
,
Hid09
,
Mai08
,
Hsi14
,
Büy14
]
. For instance, in
[
HT94
]
, Hida and Tilouine used the CM ideal method, exploiting congruences between CM forms and non-CM forms to prove the anticyclotomic Iwasawa main conjecture for general CM fields. The work of Hsieh in
[
Hsi14
]
on the main conjecture along the maximal
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
–power extension relies on the congruences between the Eisenstein series and cusp forms. Büyükboduk studied this problem in
[
Büy14
]
through the conjectural Rubin–Stark elements by extending and refining the higher rank Euler/Kolyvagin system machinery (that was set up in
[
Büy10
]
, refining Perrin-Riou’s original approach in
[
PR98
]
).

The goal of this article is to incorporate Nekovář’s descent formalism developed in
[
Nek06
]
with the results of Hsieh
[
Hsi14
]
to study the
p
-ordinary Iwasawa main conjecture for Hecke characters over the anticyclotomic
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
-power extension of
K
relying on some standard conjectures.

Notation and Hypotheses

Before we explain our results in greater detail, we set our notation that will be in effect throughout this paper.

Given a group
G
, let
\mu(G)
denote the torsion subgroup of
G
. For any number field
M
, we denote by
S_{p}(M)
and
S_{\infty}(M)
the set of places of
M
above
p
and
\infty
, respectively. For a finite set
S\supseteq S_{\infty}(M)
, we denote by
M_{S}
the maximal extension of
M
unramified outside
S
. Put
G_{M,S}:={\rm Gal}(M_{S}/M)
and
G_{M}={\rm Gal}(\overline{M}/M)
.

Let
K_{\infty}
be the compositum of the cyclotomic
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
-extension
K^{\rm cyc}
and the anticyclotomic
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{g}
-extension
K^{\rm ac}
of
K
. If we assumed the Leopoldt conjecture for
K
, then
K_{\infty}
would be the maximal
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
-power extension of
K
. Note that when
K/{\mathbb{Q}}
is abelian the Leopoldt conjecture holds. (cf.
[
NSW08
, Theorem 10.3.6]
).

Let
\Gamma_{K}:={\rm Gal}(K_{\infty}/K)
(resp.
\Gamma_{\rm cyc}:={\rm Gal}(K^{\rm cyc}/K)
,
\Gamma_{\rm ac}:={\rm Gal}(K^{\rm ac}/K))
. Then
\Gamma_{K}
is a free
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
-module of rank
{g+1}
. Fix a finite extension
{\mathfrak{F}}
of
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
and denote its ring of integers by
{\mathfrak{O}}
. Write
\Lambda(\Gamma_{K}):={\mathfrak{O}}[[\Gamma_{K}]]
for the Iwasawa algebra, which is isomorphic to the formal power series ring
{\mathfrak{O}}[[X_{1},\dots,X_{g+1}]]
in
g+1
variables with coefficients in
{\mathfrak{O}}
. We define
\Lambda(\Gamma_{\rm cyc})
and
\Lambda(\Gamma_{\rm ac})
similarly.
For any ring
R
that contains
{\mathfrak{O}}
, we also set
\Lambda_{R}(\Gamma_{K}):=\Lambda(\Gamma_{K})\otimes_{\mathfrak{O}}R
.

Let
\chi:G_{K}\longrightarrow{\mathfrak{O}}^{\times}
be any non-trivial Dirichlet character whose order is prime to
p
. We let
L_{\chi}:=\overline{K}^{\ker\chi}
denote the abelian extension of
K
cut out by
\chi
. Assume that
\chi
has the property that

(1.1)

\chi({\mathfrak{p}})\neq 1\text{ for any prime }{\mathfrak{p}}\text{ of }K\text{ lying above }p.

and

(1.2)

\chi\neq\omega,\text{ where }\omega\text{ denotes the Teichm{\"{u}}ller character}.

Note that (
1.1
) is equivalent to saying that
{\mathfrak{p}}
does not split completely in
L_{\chi}/K
. In this article, we will work with a particular character
\chi
attached to a CM abelian variety or a Hilbert modular form with CM.

We fix a
p
-adic CM type
\Sigma
of
K
, i.e. a subset
\Sigma\subset S_{p}(K)
such that
\Sigma\cup\Sigma^{c}=S_{p}(K)
and
\Sigma\cap\Sigma=\emptyset
, where
c
denote the generator of
{\rm Gal}(K/F)
. Let
M_{\Sigma}
denote the maximal abelian pro-
p
-extension of
L_{\infty}:=L_{\chi}K_{\infty}
unramified outside
\Sigma
and let
X_{\Sigma}:={\rm Gal}\left(M_{\Sigma}/L_{\infty}\right)
. We put

X_{\Sigma}^{\chi}:=e_{\chi}\left(\mathcal{W}\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}}X_{\Sigma}\right),

where
\mathcal{W}
denotes the ring generated by the values of
\chi
over the
p
-adic completion of the ring of integers of the maximal unramified extension of
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
and
e_{\chi}:=\frac{1}{[L_{\chi}:K]}\sum_{\sigma\in{\rm Gal}(L_{\chi}/K)}\chi(\sigma)\cdot\sigma^{-1}
. By
[
HT94
, Theorem 1.2.2]
,
X_{\Sigma}^{\chi}
is a finitely generated torsion module over
\Lambda(\Gamma_{K})
.

Attached to the
p
-ordinary CM-type
\Sigma
and the character
\chi
, Katz
[
Kat78
]
and Hida–Tilouine
[
HT93
, Theorem II]
constructed a
p
-adic
L
-function
\mathcal{L}_{p,\Sigma}^{\chi}\in\Lambda_{\mathcal{W}}(\Gamma_{K})
that
p
-adically interpolates the algebraic parts (in the sense of
[
Shi75
]
) of the critical Hecke
L
-values for
\chi
twisted by the characters of
\Gamma_{K}
(see
[
HT93
]
).

The following is the Iwasawa Main Conjecture for the CM field
K
and the character
\chi
(see
[
HT94
]
).

Conjecture 1.1
.

(Iwasawa Main Conjecture) —
The characteristic ideal of
X_{\Sigma}^{\chi}
is generated by the Katz
p
-adic
L
-function :

{\rm char}_{\Lambda_{\mathcal{W}}(\Gamma_{K})}\left(X_{\Sigma}^{\chi}\right)=\left(\mathcal{L}_{p,\Sigma}^{\chi}\right).

Remark 1.2
.

—
When
K
is an imaginary quadratic field, Conjecture
1.1
is proved by Rubin in
[
Rub91
, Theorem 4.1 (i)]
.

In general, Hsieh obtained the following result in
[
Hsi14
]
towards the validity of Conjecture
1.1
.

Theorem 1.3

(Corollary 2,
[
Hsi14
]
)
.

—
Assume that the following hypotheses hold:

(i)

p>5
is prime to the minus part of the class number of
K
, to the order of
\chi
, and is unramified in
F/{\mathbb{Q}}
.

(ii)

\chi
is unramified in
\Sigma^{c}
and
\chi\omega^{-a}
is unramified at
\Sigma
for some integer
a\not\equiv 2\mod{p-1}
.

(iii)

\chi
is
anticyclotomic
in the sense that
\chi(c\delta c^{-1})=\chi(\delta)^{-1}
for
\delta\in\Delta
and
c\in G_{F}
that restricts to the generator of
{\rm Gal}(K/F)
.

(iv)

\chi({\mathfrak{p}})\neq 1
for any
{\mathfrak{p}}\in\Sigma
.

(v)

The restriction of
\chi
to
G_{K\left(\sqrt{p^{*}}\right)}
(where
p^{*}=(-1)^{\tfrac{p-1}{2}}p
) is nontrivial.

Then Conjecture
1.1
holds.

Statements of the results

Let
f
be a normalized Hilbert newform of parallel (even) weight
(k,\dots,k)
, level
{\mathfrak{n}}\subset\mathcal{O}_{F}
with complex multiplication by
K
. Then there is a Hecke character
\varphi
of
K
of infinity type
(k-1)\sum_{\sigma\in\widetilde{\Sigma}}\sigma
for some CM type
\widetilde{\Sigma}
of
K
such that
f
is the cusp from associated to
\varphi
(see
[
Hid79
]
). Fix an embedding
\iota_{p}:\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}\xhookrightarrow{}{\mathbb{C}}_{p}
. Then
\widetilde{\Sigma}
and
\iota_{p}
give rise to the
p
-adic CM-type
\Sigma
.

Let
c
denote the involution on
G_{K}
induced by complex conjugation. As in
\lx@sectionsign 2.1
of
[
Arn07
]
, we assume further that
\overline{\varphi}\circ c=\varphi
, so that the sign of the functional equation for the Hecke
L
-series attached to
f
is
\pm 1
.

Let
K_{f}
denote the number field generated by the Fourier coefficients of
f
. Assume that
p
does not ramify in
K/{\mathbb{Q}}
and
K_{f}/{\mathbb{Q}}
, and
f
is
p
-ordinary. Let
\widetilde{H}_{f}^{2}\left(G_{K,S},{\mathbb{T}}_{\chi}^{{\rm ac},\iota};\Delta_{\Sigma}\right)
denote the extended Selmer group attached to the representation
{\mathbb{T}}_{\chi}^{{\rm ac},\iota}
(cf. Definition
2.8
), and
{\rm Reg}({\mathbb{T}}_{\chi}^{\rm ac,\iota})
denote the
\Lambda(\Gamma_{\rm ac})
-adic regulator given as in Definition
2.11
(vi).

We write the power series expansion

\mathcal{L}_{p,\Sigma}^{\chi}:=\mathcal{L}_{0}+\mathcal{L}_{1}\cdot(\gamma_{\rm cyc}-1)+\mathcal{L}_{2}\cdot(\gamma_{\rm cyc}-1)^{2}+\dots\in\Lambda_{\mathcal{W}}(\Gamma_{\rm ac})\widehat{\otimes}\Lambda_{\mathcal{W}}(\Gamma_{\rm cyc})

where
\gamma_{\rm cyc}
is a fixed topological generator of
\Gamma_{\rm cyc}
, and
\mathcal{L}_{i}
are elements of
\Lambda_{\mathcal{W}}(\Gamma_{\rm ac})
.

Theorem A
.

—
Assume the hypotheses of Theorem
1.3
as well as Hypothesis
3.4
. If
\varphi\overline{\varphi}={\mathbb{N}}^{k-1}
,
{\rm Reg}({\mathbb{T}}_{\chi}^{\rm ac,\iota})\neq 0
and the sign in the functional equation of

is
-1
, then we have

{\rm char}_{\Lambda_{\mathcal{W}}(\Gamma_{\rm ac})}\Big(\widetilde{H}_{f}^{2}\left(G_{K,S},{\mathbb{T}}_{\chi}^{{\rm ac},\iota};\Delta_{\Sigma}\right)_{\rm tor}\Big)\cdot{\rm Reg}({\mathbb{T}}_{\chi}^{\rm ac,\iota})=\left(\mathcal{L}_{1}\right).

The next result will be related to the Mordell–Weil ranks of abelian varieties with CM. Let
L
be a finite abelian extension of the CM field
K
with
\Delta:={\rm Gal}(L/K)
. Fix an odd rational prime
p
that is not ramified in
L/{\mathbb{Q}}
and not dividing
|\Delta|
. Let
A
be a polarized simple abelian variety of dimension
g
defined over
L
with CM-type
(K,\Sigma)
, i.e. there exists
K\xrightarrow{\;\sim\;}{\rm End}(A)_{\mathbb{Q}}
. Assume that
A
has good ordinary reduction at
p

Theorem B
.

—
Let
A/L
be a simple abelian variety with CM-type
(K,\Sigma)
such that
K(A_{\rm tor})\subset L
. Assume that the hypotheses of Theorem
5.4
and the non-anomaly condition (see (

)) hold true. If

, then both

and
\Sha(A/L)[{\mathfrak{p}}^{\infty}]
are finite.

The only results in this direction were limited to the case when the abelian variety

in question is CM by
K
defined over the CM field
K
(cf.

) or is of
{\rm GL}_{2}
-type and is defined over

; or else to the case

(that is,

is an elliptic curve).

We provide a brief overview of the key technical components involved in the proofs of Theorems

and
B
. In Section

, we review the Selmer structures introduced by Mazur and Rubin in

]
as well as Nekovář’s Selmer complexes

]

]

[

. Section

Outlook

As in
[
Mai08
,
HT94
,
Hid09
,

,

,
Hsi14
,
Büy14
]
, the

p
-adic

-function lie in the Iwasawa algebra. To overcome this difficulty, one needs to appeal to

-Iwasawa theory

,

,

,

,

,

]
.

Let

be an elliptic curve over
{\mathbb{Q}}
. In
[

]
, Agboola and Howard proved an anticyclotomic Iwasawa main conjecture for
E
using
\pm
-Selmer groups and elliptic units, assuming Rubin’s fundamental conjecture
[
Rub92
]
(later proved in
[
BKO21
]
). Büyükboduk
[
Büy15
]
extended this to newforms of even weight

with CM by

.

In
[
Büy18
]
, Büyükboduk proved the cyclotomic main conjecture of

using the Rubin-Stark elements, conditional on their existence and the truth of reciprocity laws relating them to the appropriate
p
-adic
L

In a forthcoming work, we adapt the approach developed in
[
BL15
,
Büy18
]
for the conjectural Rubin–Stark elements along with the new constructions in
[
BKO21
]
of local points to prove the multi-signed anticyclotomic main conjectures of the Iwasawa theory for CM elliptic curves at

primes.

For any non-archimedean prime
\lambda
of
K
, fix a decomposition group
D_{\lambda}
and the inertia subgroup
I_{\lambda}\subset D_{\lambda}
. Let
(-)^{\vee}:={\rm Hom}(-,{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})

(-)^{\vee}\otimes{\mathfrak{O}}={\rm Hom}(-,{\mathfrak{F}}/{\mathfrak{O}})
. Considering this relation, we will write

for
{\rm Hom}(X,{\mathfrak{F}}/{\mathfrak{O}})
when
X
is an
{\mathfrak{O}}
-module.

Suppose
\varphi:G_{K}\longrightarrow{\mathfrak{O}}^{\times}
is a continuous
p
-adic Hecke character which is Hodge-Tate. Write
{\mathfrak{O}}^{\times}=\mu({\mathfrak{F}}^{\times})\times U^{(1)}
and
\langle\varphi\rangle:G_{K}{\mathrel{\to}^{\;}}U^{(1)}
is the map
\varphi
followed by the projection
{\mathfrak{O}}^{\times}{\mathrel{\to}^{\;}}U^{(1)}
. Set

\chi:=\omega_{\varphi}=\varphi\cdot\langle\varphi\rangle^{-1}:G_{K}{\mathrel{\to}}\mu({\mathfrak{F}}^{\times})\xhookrightarrow{}{\mathfrak{O}}^{\times}

and assume that

(2.1)

\chi\text{ is not the trivial character.}

Define the
G_{K}
-representation
T_{\chi}:={\mathfrak{O}}(1)\otimes\chi^{-1}
that is equal to
{\mathfrak{O}}
as an
{\mathfrak{O}}
-module and on which
G_{K}
acts via
\chi_{\rm cyc}\chi^{-1}
, where
\chi_{\rm cyc}
denotes the cyclotomic character of
K
. Let
\varphi^{*}=\varphi^{-1}\otimes\chi_{\rm cyc}
and
T:={\mathfrak{O}}(\varphi^{*})
be the free
{\mathfrak{O}}
-module of rank one on which
G_{K}
acts via the Hecke character

. Observe that

.

2.1.
Selmer Structures

In this section, we will recall Mazur and Rubin’s definition of
Selmer structures
(cf.
[
MR04
, §2]
). Let
R
be a complete local Noetherian
{\mathfrak{O}}
-algebra, and let
X
be an
R[[G_{K}]]
-module which is free of rank
d
over
R
. We will be interested in the case where
X
is
T_{\chi}\otimes\Lambda(\Gamma_{K})
or its relevant quotients by an ideal of
\Lambda(\Gamma_{K})
such as the augmentation ideal.

Definition 2.1
.

— A Selmer structure
\mathcal{F}
on
X
is a collection of the following data:

(i)

A finite set
\Sigma(\mathcal{F})
of places of
K
, including all infinite places and primes lying above
p
and all primes at which
X
is ramified.

(ii)

For every
v\in\Sigma(\mathcal{F})
, a local condition on
X
viewed as an
R[[D_{v}]]
-module; namely, a choice of an
R
-submodule

H_{\mathcal{F}}^{1}(K_{v},X)\subset H^{1}(K_{v},X).

If
v\not\in\Sigma(\mathcal{F})
we will also write
H_{\mathcal{F}}^{1}(K_{v},X)=H_{f}^{1}(K_{v},X)
, where the module
H_{f}^{1}(K_{v},X)
is the finite part of
H^{1}(K_{v},X)
in the sense of
[
MR04
, Definition 1.1.6]
.

For a Selmer structure
\mathcal{F}
on
X
, set
\Sigma(\mathcal{F}^{*})=\Sigma(\mathcal{F})
, and for
v\in\Sigma(\mathcal{F}^{*})
define
H_{\mathcal{F}^{*}}^{1}(K_{v},X^{\vee}(1)):=H_{\mathcal{F}}^{1}(K_{v},X)^{\perp}
as the orthogonal complement of
H_{\mathcal{F}}^{1}(K_{v},X)
with respect to the local Tate pairing. The Selmer structure
\mathcal{F}^{*}
on
X^{\vee}(1)
will be called the dual Selmer structure.

Definition 2.2
.

—
If
\mathcal{F}
is a Selmer structure on
X
, we define the Selmer module
H_{\mathcal{F}}^{1}(K,X)
as the kernel of the sum of the localization maps

H_{\mathcal{F}}^{1}(K,X):={\rm ker}\bigg(H^{1}(G_{K,\Sigma(\mathcal{F})},X)\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{v\in\Sigma(\mathcal{F})}H^{1}(K_{v},X)\big/H_{\mathcal{F}}^{1}(K_{v},X)\bigg),

where
K_{\Sigma(\mathcal{F})}
denotes the maximal extension of
K
which is unramified outside
\Sigma(\mathcal{F})
, and
G_{K,\Sigma(\mathcal{F})}:={\rm Gal}(K_{\Sigma(\mathcal{F})}/K)
. We also define the dual Selmer module
H^{1}_{\mathcal{F}^{*}}(K,X^{\vee}(1))
in a similar way.

Example 2.3
.

— We have the following examples (see
[
MR04
, Definitions 3.2.1, 5.3.2]
and
[
Büy14
, Example 2.12]
):

(i)

Assume that
R={\mathfrak{O}}
and that
X
is a free
{\mathfrak{O}}
-module endowed with a continuous action of
G_{K}
, which is unramified outside a finite set of places of
K
. We define a Selmer structure
\mathcal{F}_{\rm can}
by setting

\Sigma(\mathcal{F}_{\rm can}):=\{v:X\;\text{\rm is ramified at }v\}\cup\{{\mathfrak{p}}\mid p\}\cup\{v\mid\infty\}.

•

if
v\in\Sigma(\mathcal{F}_{\rm can})
with
v\nmid p\infty
, we define

H_{\mathcal{F}_{\rm can}}^{1}(K_{v},X):={\rm ker}(H^{1}(K_{v},X)\longrightarrow H^{1}(K_{v}^{\rm unr},X\otimes_{\mathfrak{O}}K)),

where
K_{v}^{\rm unr}
denotes the maximal unramified extension of
K_{v}
.

•

If
{\mathfrak{p}}\mid p
, we define the local condition as

H_{\mathcal{F}_{\rm can}}^{1}(K_{\mathfrak{p}},X):=H^{1}(K_{\mathfrak{p}},X).

•

If
v\mid\infty
, we define the local condition as

H_{\mathcal{F}_{\rm can}}^{1}(K_{v},X):=H^{1}(K_{v},X).

The Selmer structure
\mathcal{F}_{\rm can}
is called the canonical Selmer structure on
X
.

(ii)

Assume now that
R
denotes
\Lambda(\Gamma_{K})
or its anticyclotomic quotient
\Lambda(\Gamma_{\rm ac})
and
{\mathbb{X}}
is a free
R
-module on which
G_{K}
acts continuously, and
{\mathbb{X}}
is unramified outside a finite set of places of
K
. We define a Selmer structure
\mathcal{F}_{R}
on
{\mathbb{X}}
by setting

\Sigma(\mathcal{F}_{R}):=\{v:{\mathbb{X}}\;\text{\rm is ramified at }v\}\cup\{{\mathfrak{p}}\mid p\}\cup\{v\mid\infty\},

and
H_{\mathcal{F}_{R}}^{1}(K_{v},{\mathbb{X}}):=H^{1}(K_{v},{\mathbb{X}})
for every
v\in\Sigma(\mathcal{F}_{R})
. The Selmer structure
\mathcal{F}_{R}
on
{\mathbb{X}}
is called the canonical
R
-adic Selmer structure on
{\mathbb{X}}
.

Definition 2.4

—

Lemma 2.5
.

—
We have the following twisting isomorphisms

4.
Anticyclotomic main conjectures

In this section, we will prove the main theorems which can be regarded as the algebraic formulation of the anticyclotomic main conjecture.

Definition 4.1
.

—
Recall the augmentation map
\mathbbm{1}_{\rm cyc}:R_{\rm cyc}\longrightarrow R
. Suppose
R
is a Krull unique factorization domain and
J\subset R_{\rm cyc}
is an ideal. We set

\partial^{*}_{\rm cyc}J:=\begin{cases}\mathbbm{1}_{\rm cyc}(J),&\;\text{if}\;(\gamma_{\rm cyc}-1)\nmid J\\
\mathbbm{1}_{\rm cyc}((\gamma_{\rm cyc}-1)^{-1}J),&\;\text{if}\;(\gamma_{\rm cyc}-1)\;\mid\;J\end{cases}

and call it the mock leading term for
J
. In particular, for a given element
{\mathbb{A}}:=\sum_{n\geq 0}A_{n}\cdot(\gamma_{\rm cyc}-1)^{n}\in R_{\rm cyc}
(so that we have
A_{n}\in R
), we set

\partial^{*}_{\rm cyc}{\mathbb{A}}:=\begin{cases}A_{0},\;\;&{\rm if}\;\mathbbm{1}_{\rm cyc}({\mathbb{A}})\neq 0,\\
A_{1},\;\;&{\rm if}\;\mathbbm{1}_{\rm cyc}({\mathbb{A}})=0.\end{cases}

Further, for a torsion
R_{\rm cyc}
-module
M
we have

\partial^{*}_{\rm cyc}{\rm char}_{R_{\rm cyc}}(M)=\begin{cases}\partial_{\rm cyc}{\rm char}_{R_{\rm cyc}}(M),&\;\text{if}\;r(M)\leq 1\\
0,&\;\text{if}\;r(M)>1.\end{cases}

Theorem 4.2
.

— Assume that Hypothesis
3.4
holds. If
{\rm Reg}({\mathbb{T}}_{\chi}^{\rm ac,\iota})\neq 0
then we have

{\rm char}_{\Lambda(\Gamma_{\rm ac})}\Big(\widetilde{H}_{f}^{2}\left(G_{K,S},{\mathbb{T}}_{\chi}^{{\rm ac},\iota};\Delta_{\Sigma}\right)_{\rm tor}\Big)\cdot{\rm Reg}\left({\mathbb{T}}_{\chi}^{\rm ac,\iota}\right)=\partial^{*}_{\rm cyc}{\rm char}_{\Lambda(\Gamma_{K})}\Big(\widetilde{H}_{f}^{2}\left(G_{K,S},{\mathbb{T}}_{\chi}^{\iota};\Delta_{\Sigma}\right)\Big).

Proof.

Once we verify that the following properties are satisfied, it follows from Proposition
2.14
that the asserted equality holds.

(1)

We have that

\displaystyle\widetilde{H}_{f}^{0}\left(G_{K,S},{\mathbb{T}}_{\chi}^{{\rm ac},\iota};\Delta_{\Sigma}\right)

\displaystyle=\widetilde{H}_{f}^{3}\left(G_{K,S},{\mathbb{T}}_{\chi}^{{\rm ac},\iota};\Delta_{\Sigma}\right)=0

\displaystyle\widetilde{H}_{f}^{0}\left(G_{K,S},{\mathbb{T}}_{\chi}^{\iota};\Delta_{\Sigma}\right)

\displaystyle=\widetilde{H}_{f}^{3}\left(G_{K,S},{\mathbb{T}}_{\chi}^{\iota};\Delta_{\Sigma}\right)=0

(2)

{\rm Tam}_{v}({\mathbb{T}}_{\chi}^{\rm ac,\iota},P)=0
for every height-one prime ideal
P
of
\Lambda(\Gamma_{\rm ac})
for all
v\nmid p
in
S_{f}
.

(3)

The
\Lambda(\Gamma_{K})
-modules
\widetilde{H}_{f}^{1}\left(G_{K,S},{\mathbb{T}}_{\chi}^{\iota};\Delta_{\Sigma}\right)
and
\widetilde{H}_{f}^{2}\left(G_{K,S},{\mathbb{T}}_{\chi}^{\iota};\Delta_{\Sigma}\right)
are torsion.

(4)

If
r\left(\widetilde{H}_{f}^{2}\left(G_{K,S},{\mathbb{T}}_{\chi}^{\iota};\Delta_{\Sigma}\right)\right)>1
then
{\rm Reg}({\mathbb{T}}_{\chi}^{\rm ac,\iota})=0
.

Property
(1)
follows from Lemma
3.3
and Property
(2)
is due to Lemma
3.2
. Property
(1)
and Lemma
3.1
imply that
\widetilde{h}^{1}=\widetilde{h}^{2}
. Therefore, Lemma
2.10
shows that the
\Lambda(\Gamma_{K})
-modules
\widetilde{H}_{f}^{1}\left(G_{K,S},{\mathbb{T}}_{\chi}^{\iota};\Delta_{\Sigma}\right)
and
\widetilde{H}_{f}^{2}\left(G_{K,S},{\mathbb{T}}_{\chi}^{\iota};\Delta_{\Sigma}\right)
are torsion.

We will complete the verification of Property
(4)
by employing
[
Nek06
, Proposition 11.7.6(ii)]
. Recall that
\gamma_{\rm cyc}\in\Gamma_{\rm cyc}
is a fixed topological generator, and
\gamma:=\gamma_{\rm cyc}^{(\Gamma_{\rm ac})}\in\Gamma_{K}
its lift. Since
\Lambda(\Gamma_{\rm ac})
is an integral domain, the set denoted by
Q
in
[
Nek06
, §11]
contains only the zero ideal
(0)
of
\Lambda(\Gamma_{\rm ac})
. In this case, the ideal
(\gamma-1)\subset\Lambda(\Gamma_{K})
corresponds to
\overline{{\mathfrak{q}}}
in the notation of Nekovář.

It now follows from
[
Nek06
, Proposition 11.7.6 (vii)]
that

{\rm length}_{\Lambda(\Gamma_{K})_{(\gamma-1)}}\big(\widetilde{H}_{f}^{2}\left(G_{K,S},{\mathbb{T}}_{\chi}^{\iota};\Delta_{\Sigma}\right)_{(\gamma-1)}\big)\geq{\rm length}_{\Lambda(\Gamma_{\rm ac})_{(0)}}\big(\widetilde{H}_{f}^{1}\left(G_{K,S},{\mathbb{T}}_{\chi}^{{\rm ac},\iota};\Delta_{\Sigma}\right)_{(0)}\big)

with the equality if and only if
{\rm Reg}({\mathbb{T}}_{\chi}^{\rm ac,\iota})_{(0)}\neq 0
. The latter is equivalent to asking
{\rm Reg}({\mathbb{T}}_{\chi}^{\rm ac,\iota})\neq 0
, since
\Lambda(\Gamma_{\rm ac})
is an integral domain. Therefore;

\displaystyle r\left(\widetilde{H}_{f}^{2}\left(G_{K,S},{\mathbb{T}}_{\chi}^{\iota};\Delta_{\Sigma}\right)\right)

\displaystyle={\rm length}_{\Lambda(\Gamma_{K})_{(\gamma-1)}}\big(\widetilde{H}_{f}^{2}\left(G_{K,S},{\mathbb{T}}_{\chi}^{\iota};\Delta_{\Sigma}\right)_{(\gamma-1)}\big)

\displaystyle\geq{\rm length}_{\Lambda(\Gamma_{\rm ac})_{(0)}}\big(\widetilde{H}_{f}^{1}\left(G_{K,S},{\mathbb{T}}_{\chi}^{{\rm ac},\iota};\Delta_{\Sigma}\right)_{(0)}\big)

\displaystyle={\rm rank}_{\Lambda(\Gamma_{\rm ac})}\left(\widetilde{H}_{f}^{1}\left(G_{K,S},{\mathbb{T}}_{\chi}^{{\rm ac},\iota};\Delta_{\Sigma}\right)\right)

with equality if and only if
{\rm Reg}({\mathbb{T}}_{\chi}^{\rm ac})\neq 0
. Since
{\rm rank}_{\Lambda(\Gamma_{\rm ac})}\left(\widetilde{H}_{f}^{1}\left(G_{K,S},{\mathbb{T}}_{\chi}^{{\rm ac},\iota};\Delta_{\Sigma}\right)\right)\leq 1
(cf. Proposition
3.6
) and
r\left(\widetilde{H}_{f}^{2}\left(G_{K,S},{\mathbb{T}}_{\chi}^{{\rm ac},\iota};\Delta_{\Sigma}\right)\right)>1
, the inequality is strict and hence,
{\rm Reg}({\mathbb{T}}_{\chi}^{\rm ac,\iota})=0
.
∎

We write the power series expansion

\mathcal{L}_{p,\Sigma}^{\chi}:=\mathcal{L}_{0}+\mathcal{L}_{1}\cdot(\gamma_{\rm cyc}-1)+\mathcal{L}_{2}\cdot(\gamma_{\rm cyc}-1)^{2}+\dots\in\Lambda_{\mathcal{W}}(\Gamma_{\rm ac})\widehat{\otimes}\Lambda_{\mathcal{W}}(\Gamma_{\rm cyc})

where
\mathcal{L}_{i}
are elements of
\Lambda_{\mathcal{W}}(\Gamma_{\rm ac})
.

Theorem 4.3
.

—
Assume the hypotheses of Theorem
1.3
as well as Hypothesis
3.4
. If
\varphi\overline{\varphi}={\mathbb{N}}^{k-1}
,

Proof.

It follows from Theorem
4.2
that

{\rm char}_{\Lambda(\Gamma_{\rm ac})}\Big(\widetilde{H}_{f}^{2}\left(G_{K,S},{\mathbb{T}}_{\chi}^{{\rm ac},\iota};\Delta_{\Sigma}\right)_{\rm tor}\Big)\cdot{\rm Reg}\left({\mathbb{T}}_{\chi}^{\rm ac,\iota}\right)=\left(\partial^{*}_{\rm cyc}\mathcal{L}_{p,\Sigma}^{\chi}\right).

∎

5.
Hecke characters attached to abelian varieties with complex multiplication

Let
L
be a finite abelian extension of the CM field
K
with
\Delta:={\rm Gal}(L/K)
. Fix an odd rational prime
p
that is not ramified in
L/{\mathbb{Q}}
and not dividing
|\Delta|
. Recall that
[K:{\mathbb{Q}}]=2g
.

Let
A
be a polarized simple abelian variety of dimension
g
defined over
L
with CM-type
(K,\Sigma)
, i.e. there exists
\iota:K\xrightarrow{\;\sim\;}{\rm End}(A)_{\mathbb{Q}}
. Assume that
A
has good ordinary reduction at
p
. We have the
p
-adic Tate module
T_{p}(A):=\varprojlim_{n}A[p^{n}]
of A, which is a free
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
-module of rank
2g
on which
G_{L}
acts continuously. For the Tate module
T_{p}(A)
, we have a decomposition

T_{p}(A):=\bigoplus_{{\mathfrak{p}}\mid p}T_{\mathfrak{p}}(A),

where the product is over the prime ideals of
K
lying above
p
and each
T_{\mathfrak{p}}(A)=\varprojlim A[{\mathfrak{p}}^{n}]
is a free
\mathcal{O}_{K_{\mathfrak{p}}}
-module of rank one, see
[
ST68
, Page 502]
. The action of
G_{L}
on
T_{\mathfrak{p}}(A)
gives rise to a character

\rho_{\mathfrak{p}}:G_{L}\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{K_{\mathfrak{p}}}^{\times}.

The theory of complex multiplication gives rise to an algebraic Hecke character
\rho:{\mathbb{A}}^{\times}_{L}\longrightarrow K^{\times}
attached to
A
such that, for every prime
{\mathfrak{p}}
of
K
lying above
p
, the
{\mathfrak{p}}
-adic avatar
\rho_{{\mathfrak{p}}}:G_{L}\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{K_{\mathfrak{p}}}^{\times}
gives the action of
G_{L}
on
T_{\mathfrak{p}}(A)
. We assume the following hypothesis:

(
Shi.
)

\text{ The field }L(A_{\rm tor})\text{ is an abelian extension of }K.

It follows from
[
Shi71
, Theorem 7.44]
that the assumption (
Shi.
) is equivalent to the existence of a Hecke character
\varphi
of
K
such that

(5.1)

\rho=\varphi\circ N_{L/K}.

Recall that we fixed embeddings
\iota_{\infty}:\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
and
\iota_{p}:\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{C}}_{p}
and an isomorphism
\iota:{\mathbb{C}}\cong{\mathbb{C}}_{p}
such that
\iota\circ\iota_{\infty}=\iota_{p}
and that
\Sigma
is the set that contains precisely half of the embeddings of
K
into
\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}
. Since we are under the assumption (

p

-ord
), the theory of complex multiplication identifies the two sets
\{\rho_{\wp}^{\sigma}\}_{\wp,\sigma}
and
\{\rho_{\tau}^{(p)}\}_{\tau}
, where we have

\rho_{\wp}^{\sigma}:{\mathbb{A}}^{\times}_{L}\big/L^{\times}\xrightarrow{\rho_{\wp}\;\circ\;{\rm rec}}\mathcal{O}_{\wp}^{\times}\xhookrightarrow{\;\sigma\;}\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}^{\times}_{p}\subset{\mathbb{C}}_{p}^{\times},

and

\rho_{\tau}^{(p)}:{\mathbb{A}}^{\times}_{L}\big/L^{\times}\xrightarrow{\;\;\rho_{\tau}\;\;}{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\iota}}{{\cong}}{\mathbb{C}}_{p}^{\times},

where
{\rm rec}:{\mathbb{A}}^{\times}_{L}\big/L^{\times}\longrightarrow G_{L}
denotes the reciprocity map.

Fix
\tau\in\Sigma
and identify
K
with
K^{\tau}
. This choice in turn fixes a prime
\wp\in\Sigma_{p}
and
\sigma:K_{\wp}\hookrightarrow\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{p}
in a way that
\rho_{\tau}^{(p)}=\rho_{\wp}^{\sigma}
. Set
{\mathfrak{O}}:=\sigma(\mathcal{O}_{\wp})
. Let
{\mathfrak{p}}=\wp^{\sigma}
denote its maximal ideal,
{\mathfrak{F}}={\rm Frak}({\mathfrak{O}})
its fraction field. By abuse of notation, we define

\psi:=\rho_{\wp}^{\sigma}:G_{L}{\mathrel{\to}^{\;\;}}{\mathfrak{O}}^{\times}.

Definition 5.1
.

—
Define the
G_{L}
-representation
T_{\chi,L}={\mathfrak{O}}(1)\otimes\chi^{-1}
, where we write
\chi=\omega_{\psi}=\psi\cdot\langle\psi\rangle^{-1}:G_{L}\longrightarrow{\mathfrak{O}}^{\times}
similar to §
3.1
.

We then obtain a
G_{K}
-representation by setting

{\rm Ind}_{L}^{K}(T_{\chi,L}):={\mathfrak{O}}[G_{K}]\otimes_{G_{L}}T_{\chi,L}\cong{\mathfrak{O}}[\Delta]\otimes_{{\mathfrak{O}}}T_{\chi,L}.

The following lemma will provide us the relation between the
{\mathfrak{O}}
-modules
{\rm Ind}_{L}^{K}(T_{\chi,L})
and twists
T_{\chi}\otimes\eta
of
T_{\chi,L}
by
\eta\in\widehat{\Delta}
.

Lemma 5.2
.

—
The map
f:\left({\mathfrak{O}}[G_{K}]\otimes_{{\mathfrak{O}}[G_{L}]}T_{\chi,L}\right)^{\eta}\longrightarrow T_{\chi}\otimes\eta
given by
\sigma\otimes t\mapsto\eta(\sigma)t
is an isomorphism between
{\mathfrak{O}}[G_{K}]
-modules.

Proof.

Note that the map
f
is non-trivial and both
{\mathfrak{O}}
-modules are of rank
1
. Therefore, it suffices to check whether the morphism
f
is
G_{K}
-equivariant. Let
g\in G_{K}
be given. Then for
g^{\prime}\in G_{K}
and
t\in{\mathfrak{O}}
, we have

\displaystyle f(g\cdot(g^{\prime}\otimes t))

\displaystyle=f(g^{\prime}g\otimes t)

\displaystyle=\eta(g^{\prime}g)t

\displaystyle=\eta(g)\eta(g^{\prime})t

\displaystyle=gf(g^{\prime}\otimes t),

which completes the proof.
∎

Fix a character
\eta\in\widehat{\Delta}
. Write
\chi_{\eta}=\chi\cdot\eta^{-1}
so that we have the
G_{K}
-module

{\rm Ind}_{L}^{K}(T_{\chi,L})\cong{\displaystyle\bigoplus_{\eta\in\widehat{\Delta}}}T_{\chi_{\eta}}\text{ and }H^{1}(L,T_{\chi,L})\cong{\displaystyle\bigoplus_{\eta\in\widehat{\Delta}}}H^{1}(K,T_{\chi_{\eta}}).

Let
{\mathfrak{f}}
be the product of the ideals
{\rm Cond}(L/K)
and
{\mathfrak{f}}_{\psi}

K

-power extension

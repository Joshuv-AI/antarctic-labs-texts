A Mendelian randomization-based risk model using aging-related genes for prognosis in lung adenocarcinoma
Authors: Shaowen Feng, Xiaowang Liu, Huiqin Yang, Jiazheng Huang, Ziang Wang, Hui Li, Jiahao Cai, Zhonghe Liu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAging-related genes (ARGs) are prognostic markers in cancers, but their role in lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) remains unclear. Investigating ARGs in LUAD may provide valuable insights for clinical diagnosis and treatment.MethodsGene expression profiles from TCGA and GEO datasets were analyzed to identify ARG-related genes. Univariate Cox regression and LASSO analysis were used to construct a prognostic risk model. Kaplan-Meier survival analysis, ROC curves, and clinicopathological features validated its predictive accuracy. Immune cell infiltration and tumor microenvironment were assessed using CIBERSORT and ESTIMATE. RT-qPCR was used to validate the differential expression of key genes in LUAD and adjacent normal tissues.ResultsSeven prognostic ARGs (RHPN2, BLK, PTPRO, CA4, UBE2C, METTL7A, and H2BC12) were identified. The risk model stratified patients into high- and low-risk groups, with high-risk individuals showing poorer survival, increased immune evasion, and altered immune cell infiltration. These findings were validated in independent datasets. RT-qPCR confirmed elevated RHPN2, BLK, UBE2C, and H2BC12 in tumors, while PTPRO, CA4, and METTL7A were reduced.ConclusionA robust ARG-based risk model, leveraging Cox regression and LASSO, effectively predicts survival and immunotherapy responses in LUAD, offering new tools for personalized prognosis and treatment strategies.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12672-025-04101-x.Keywords: Lung adenocarcinoma, Aging-related genes, Risk model

Background

Lung cancer is an aggressive malignancy that poses a major threat to human health, with the highest incidence and mortality rates of all cancers. The most prevalent histological subtype is non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), which is mainly categorized into lung squamous cell carcinoma (LUSC) and lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) [1]. With ongoing research into the epidemiological characteristics of LUAD, we have come to recognize that LUAD is associated with numerous risk factors, including: smoking, secondhand smoke exposure, contact with specific chemicals or hazardous substances, a family history of lung cancer, and certain genetic mutations [13]. Despite significant advances in the treatment of LUAD in recent years, particularly for unresectable cases, including immunotherapy, targeted therapy, and precision radiotherapy, the prognosis for LUAD patients remains poor, especially in advanced stages, with a 5-year survival rate of less than 20% [25]). Therefore, identifying reliable biomarkers capable of predicting the prognosis of lung cancer patients could be pivotal in enhancing their outcomes.

Aging is an important biological process and a universal characteristic of living organisms. The main manifestation of aging is the gradual loss or degeneration of function at the molecular, cellular, tissue, and organism levels [7]. Aging promotes a range of degenerative diseases characterized by the weakening or loss of function in tissues or cells. The incidence of malignant tumors increases with age, which is why aging is regarded as a pro-tumorigenic state. Additionally, aging-related characteristics can serve as prognostic biomarkers for various categories of malignant neoplasm, including LUAD, ovarian cancer, and glioma [8]. A profound investigation into the role of aging-related genes in LUAD is instrumental for a more profound understanding of the genesis and progression of tumors. It also holds the potential to significantly enhance patient prognosis, thereby conferring considerable clinical significance.

Mendelian randomization (MR), initially applied in epidemiological studies, now uses genetic predictors as instrumental variables to connect phenotypes with causative factors. This approach has become a key method in bioinformatics, with the advantage of minimizing the impact of common confounders, such as environmental, socioeconomic, and behavioral factors [2]. We integrated transcriptomic data with MR analysis to identify aging-related LUAD prognostic genes. We then constructed a risk model based on these prognostic genes to predict patient survival rates. Concurrently, the risk scores is combinded with clinical features to identify significant clinical factors in LUAD patient prognosis and treatment regimens. Subsequently, we delved into the biological pathways involved with the prognostic genes. Finally, we analyzed the differences in immune cell infiltration and immune therapy responses between high- and low-risk patient groups within the tumor immune microenvironment, aiming to offer new perspectives for the clinical management and prognosis of LUAD.

Materials and methods

Data collection

We collected survival data from 437 LUAD cell line (TCGA-LUAD, training set) patients and 59 healthy individuals at the University of California Santa Cruz (UCSC, https://xenabrowser.net/datapages/). GSE50081(validation set) was a selection of 100 LUAD patients(including survival time and survival status) from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database(http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/). In the Integrated Epidemiology Unit (IEU) Open genome-wide association Analysis (GWAS, https://gwas.mrcieu.ac.uk/), we searched for “LUAD” (“ IEU-A-984 “), including 10,345,176 single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPS), including 65,864 samples (case = 11,245, control = 54,619). In addition, we screened 1651 genes associated with aging(ARGs) from the published literature [29].

Enrichment and identification of candidate genes

Differential gene expression analysis (DEGs) between LUAD and normal samples was conducted using the “DESeq2” R package (v1.34.0) on the expression matrix from the TCGA-LUAD dataset [28]. Filter threshold |log2(FoldChange)| >1, p.adj < 0.05. The volcano map was visualized using the R software package “ggplot2” (v 3.4.1) [17] and the deg heat Map (v 2.14.0) [16]. The intersection of DEGs and ARGs was taken and the intersected genes were called candidate genes. Visualise the intersection of the two by drawing a Venn diagram of the intersection using the R package “VennDiagram“ [10]. The biological functions and related signal pathways of candidate genes were studied. Under the screening condition of p.adj < 0.05, the R-language program clusterProfiler (v 4.2.2) was used to verify the function of candidate genes in the Gene ontology (GO) and Kyoto Genome Encyclopedia (KEGG) [42].

Mendelian randomization (MR) analysis

Three basic premises underlie Mendelian randomization studies: (1) a robust and significant correlation existed between instrumental variables (IVs) and exposure factor; (2) IVs were unrelated to confounding factors; (3) IVs could only influence outcomes through exposure factor and not through other channels. MR is used to screen for key genes that are causally related to LUAD Candidate genes were considered as the exposure variable, while LUAD was the outcome. GWAS data of eQTL (expression quantitative trait loci) of candidate genes were obtained from IEU-OpenGWAS database. Exposure variables were screened to identify instrumental variables (IVs) using the extract_instruments function of the TwoSampleMR software package(v0.5.6) [21], with screening criteria set at p < 5*10− 7, cluster = TRUE, r2 = 0.001, kb = 100. IVs with F-values less than 10 and exposure variables with less than 3 SNPs were excluded. MR analysis relied on five main algorithms: MR Egger [3], weighted median [4], inverse variance weighted (IVW) [5], simple mode, and weighted mode [18]. The IVW method was mainly used for robustness of results, and the significance level was set to P < 0.05, it is suggested that there is an important causal relationship between exposure factors and prognosis. For the accuracy and reliability of the MR Analysis, a series of sensitivity analyses were performed. These analyses cover several key aspects, including testing for heterogeneity, polynomial testing, and Leave-One-Out (LOO) analysis to ensure that the predictive power of the model is not compromised by the presence of outliers in the data. The TwoSample MR package was utilized for all analyses, and in cases of significant heterogeneity (p-value < 0.05), the random effects IVW method was employed for analysis.

Construction and of validation risk model

To enhance the selection of prognostic genes, univariate Cox regression analysis was undertaken applying the survival (v 3.5.3) [23] on the candidate genes in the KIRC samples with survival information in TCGA-LUAD (HR ≠ 1 & P < 0.001). Subsequently, proportional hazard (PH) assumption test was performed (P > 0.05). n: number of prognostic genes, coef (genei) and expre (genei) were represent prognostic gene coefficient and prognostic gene expression level, respectively.

Using the median risk score as the threshold, the training group was divided into High and low risk groups. The survival rate of the two groups was evaluated by Kaplan-Meier(K-M)curve. Receiver Operating Characteristic(ROC) curve analysis [20] was used to evaluate the predictive ability of the risk model on survival time through R package survivalROC (1.18.0) [20]. Finally, the expression of prognostic genes was calculated in the high and low risk groups. Aiming to check the generalizability of the prognostic risk model, calculating the risk score according to the riskScore formula in GSE50081 with the analyses described above..

Clinical feature description

According to the Wilcoxon test, differences in risk scores between different clinical subtypes were assessed according to various clinical endpoints(age, sex, stage, pathological tumour/lymph node/metastatic LUAD).

Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA)

DEseq2 software package [28] was used to perform functional enrichment analysis of prognostic genes in the two risk groups in the training set. Log2 fold change values were computed and sorted, followed by GSEA using datasets from the MSigDB database(https://www.gsea-msigdb.org/gsea/msigdb), c2.cp.kegg.v2023.1.Hs.entrez.gmt and c5.go.v2023.1.Hs.entrez.gmt as background gene sets.

Immune analysis

In the training set, the enrichment scores of 22 immune infiltration cells were calculated with ssGSEA. The Wilcoxon test was used to measure the difference in immunity of both groups. Then, correlation of prognostic genes with differential immune cells using Spearman analysis. Furthermore, TIDE(Tumor Immune Dysfunction and Exclusion), exclusion, dysfunction, and microsatellite instability (MSI) scores were calculated for both high-risk and low-risk groups on the basis of the TCGA-LUAD dataset. Furthermore, the stromalscore, immunescore and ESTIMATE(Estimation of STromal and Immune cells in MAlignant Tumour tissues using Expression data)score of the LUAD tumor tissue were calculated from the expression data using ESTIMATE.

Validation of prognostic gene expression

To investigate the difference in expression levels of prognostic genes in LUAD samples and control samples, data from LUAD samples and control samples were compared in TCGA-LUAD using the Wilcoxon test to analyse the difference in expression in LUAD samples and control samples.

Reverse transcription quantitative polymerase chain Reaction, RT-qPCR

A total of 10 samples (5 tumor and 5 normal) were acquired from the clinic in the Shanghai Chest Hospital, Shanghai Jiao Tong University. All participants were given informed consent. The study had the approval of the Shanghai Jiao Tong University ethics committee (approval number: KS2409). Then, RNA from each sample was extracted with 1 ml TRIzol reagent and 300 ul chloroform, and its concentration was quantified with NanoPhotometer N50. The cDNA was reverse transcribed using the SweScript First Strand cDNA Synthesis Kit (Servicebio, Wuhan, China). RT-qPCR amplification was performed using the CFX96 Real-Time Fluorescence Quantitative PCR Instrument (Bio-Rad, California, USA) for a total of 40 cycles. One cycle consisted of 1 min of pre-denaturation at 95℃, denaturation at 95℃ for 20 s, annealing at 55℃ for 20 s and extension at 72℃ for 30 s.

Statistical analysis

R studio (v.4.2.2) software was used for statistical analysis. Build the network using Cytoscape (v.3.10.0)software. Significance threshold P < 0.05(two-tailed) was used for all conditions. The RT-qPCR data were all processed using GraphPad Prism software(v.9.5.1). A paired t-test was conducted to compare the gene expression levels between tumor and non-tumor tissues, with matched samples obtained from the same patients.

Results

Identification of candidate genes

In this analysis, 5,451 DEGs via differential expression analysis were identified, consisting 3,536 up-regulated genes and 1,915 down-regulated genes in LUAD groups. Visual representations, including volcano and heat maps, were created to illustrate the expression patterns of these DEGs, with emphasis on the top 10 up-regulated and top 10 down-regulated genes (Fig. 1A-B). Subsequently, DEGs were mapped to intersecting ARGs to construct a Venn diagram, resulting in a total of 425 intersecting genes identified as candidate genes (Fig. 1C).

C Venn diagram displaying 425 intersecting genes between DEGs and ARGs as candidate genes. D Gene Ontology (GO) enrichment analysis of candidate genes, highlighting the top 10 key terms across three categories: biological process (BP), cellular component (CC), and molecular function (MF). E Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway enrichment analysis, identifying pathways such as neutrophil extracellular trap formation, transcriptional dysregulation in cancer, cell cycle, and cellular aging

Analysis of GO and KEGG enrichment

As illustrated in Fig. 1D, we studied the biological and signaling pathways of candidate genes and identified 10 key GO terms in biology (BP), cellular component (CC), and molecular function (MF). The BP terms encompassed “chromatin remodeling”, “protein localization in chromatin”, and “nucleosome assembly”; CC terms included “collagen-containing extracellular matrix”, “chromosome region”, and “nucleosome”; while MF terms consisted of ‘structural components of chromatin’, ‘protein heterodimerization activity’, and ‘receptor ligand activity’. The results of the KEGG enrichment analysis, displayed in Fig. 1F, included neutrophil extracellular trap formation, transcriptional dysregulation in cancer, cell cycle, and cellular aging.

39 key genes were causally linked to LUAD

We utilized MR analysis with 425 candidate genes as exposure factors and LUAD as the outcome to identify genes causally associated with LUAD. Among these, 39 exposure factors were notably linked to LUAD causally, comprising 16 risk factors (P < 0.05, OR > 1) and 23 protective factors (P < 0.05, OR < 1) (Table 1). The scatterplot illustrates a clear negative slope for protective factors and a positive slope for risk factors. Similarly, in the forest plot, the IVWs of protective factors were positioned left of the line, while those of risk factors were to the right. Moreover, the IVs of the exposure factors adhered to Mendel’s second law. Heterogeneity was absent in the two-sample MR results (P > 0.05). To assess the robustness of our MR findings, we performed sensitivity analyses. Among them, the heterogeneity test found that none of the 39 genes were heterogeneous (p > 0.05); the horizontal pleiotropy test showed that the p-values of the 39 genes were all greater than 0.05, which indicated that there were no confounding factors in the MR analyses; and lastly, the LOO found that the effect of residual SNP on the results of the 39 genes did not change much, which also indicated that the results of the MR analyses were reliable. The results of the Steiger test were true for 39 exposure factors, indicating that the exposure factors positively influenced the outcome. In conclusion, these 39 exposure factors have been identified to have a causal relationship with LUAD (Supplementary document).

Screening of prognostic genes

To identify survival-associated prognostic genes, univariate Cox analysis was performed on each key gene, leading to the identification of 12 prognostic genes. Among them, BRCA1, HELLS, RHPN2, H2BC5, UBE2C, and H2BC12 were categorize as high-risk genes (HR > 1), while NCKAP1L, BLK, SLC14A1, PTPRO, CA4, and METTL7A were categorized as low-risk genes (HR < 1) (Fig. 2A). To refine the gene selection further, LASSO regression analysis was conducted on the 12 prognostic-related genes (Fig. 2B-C). To maximize the predictive performance of the risk model, we selected lambda.min = 0.01266755 for model construction, which ultimately yielded a risk model consisting of 7 prognostic genes, including RHPN2, BLK, PTPRO, CA4, UBE2C, METTL7A, and H2BC12.

Constructing and validating risk models

Patients in the training set were categorized into high-risk and low-risk groups according to an optimal risk threshold of 0.912544. These risk scores are illustrated in Fig. 2D, showing a decline in survival as the risk score increased. The expression profiles of seven prognostic genes, which differed significantly between the two groups, are presented in Fig. 2E. Figure 2F illustrates the relationship between risk scores and survival duration, showing that TCGA data identified lower risk scores as being associated with better survival outcomes (p < 0.05) (Fig. 2G). Furthermore, one-year, three-year, and five-year ROC analyses performed on a validation set consistently yielded an AUC greater than 0.6 (Fig. 2H). The researchers also found that the results of Kaplan-Meier (KM) curve analysis, risk curve analysis, and ROC curve analysis in the validation dataset aligned with those from the training dataset (Fig. 2I-M).

In the training dataset, we examined how risk scores correlated with various clinical and pathological features such as patient age, gender, cancer stage, and TNM stage. The findings revealed that the distribution of high and low risk scores varied significantly depending on these characteristics, including gender, stage, T (primary tumor size), and N (lymph node involvement), as depicted in Figs. 3A-F.

GSEA functional analysis

To elucidate differences in the biological functions of prognostic genes, we conducted GSEA functional analysis. As depicted in Fig. 3G-M, the results revealed distinct enrichment patterns for each gene. BLK exhibited significant enrichment in pathways such as hematopoietic cell lineage, primary immunodeficiency, and cell adhesion molecule (CAM) interactions (Fig. 3G). CA4 demonstrated enrichment primarily in pathways associated with neuroactive ligand-receptor interactions, vascular smooth muscle contraction, and complement activation (Fig. 3H). H2BC12 was prominently enriched in pathways related to ribosomes, spliceosomes, and pyrimidine metabolism (Fig. 3I). Moreover, METTL7A was predominantly enriched in pathways linked to hematopoietic cell lineage and the complement and coagulation cascade (Fig. 3J). PTPRO showed enrichment in pathways including leishmaniasis infection, hematopoietic cell lineage, and asthma (Fig. 3K). Significant enrichment in pathways related to the DNA replication, cell cycle, and spliceosome function was observed for RHPN2 (Fig. 3L). Likewise, the enrichment of UBE2C was primarily observed in pathways associated with the cell cycle, spliceosome activity and DNA replication (Fig. 3M).

We examined the association between the risk profile of Prognostic genes and TIDE-related scores. Patients classified as high-risk displayed significantly increased TIDE scores and Exclusion scores, coupled with decreased Dysfunction scores and MSI scores, in comparison to those classified as low-risk. These findings indicate heightened immune evasion and a diminished response to immunotherapy within the high-risk cohort (Fig. 4D). Expression validation of prognostic genes

Figure 5D shows the expression profiles of these prognostic genes in the TCGA-LUAD. Then, we obtained tumor tissues and adjacent normal tissues from five patients pathologically confirmed to have LUAD after surgery. Using RT-qPCR, we validated the expression patterns of risk model-related genes, and the results aligned with expectations (Fig. 5E). Discussion

Aging represents a multifaceted physiological phenomenon, characterized by a constellation of twelve biological hallmarks: genomic instability, telomere attrition, epigenetic alterations, loss of proteostasis, disabled macroautophagy, deregulated nutrient-sensing, mitochondrial dysfunction, cellular senescence, stem cell exhaustion, intercellular communication disruption, chronic inflammation, and microbiota dysbiosis [27]. These hallmarks, integral to the aging process, are also implicated in the etiology and progression of neoplastic diseases, underscoring their significance in the molecular pathogenesis of cancer. For example, Genomic instability is one of the key drivers of aging and is also one of the important mechanisms leading to the development of cancer [22]. As age progresses, the accumulation of damage to nuclear DNA and mitochondrial DNA within cells triggers genomic instability, thereby increasing the risk of cancer development. Additionally, Cellular senescence is a stress response in cells, primarily manifested by a halt of the cell cycle, and it is a factor in aging as well as in age-related diseases, including cancer [31]. Senescence in cells is accompanied by a specific secretory signature, highlighted by the release of cytokines, chemokines, inflammatory factors, and proteases, which are collectively termed the Senescence-Associated Secretory Phenotype (SASP) [35]. Senescent cell phenotypes contribute to the activation of epithelial-mesenchymal transition and the promotion of angiogenesis in tumor cells, both of which are critical for the invasiveness and spread of cancer [34]. Research has indicated that the process of aging exerts distinct influences on the pathogenesis of LUAD [45]. In this study, we initially employed MR to explore the causal relationship between the aging process and lung cancer. Subsequently, we constructed a prognostic model for lung cancer based on machine learning methods such as Lasso regression. Finally, we validated the differential expression of these cancer-associated genes in cancerous and adjacent tissues in a patient cohort using the PCR method, thereby preliminarily exploring the potential link between aging and lung cancer.

Employing differential and Mendelian analyses followed by LASSO regression-based machine learning techniques, we have culled 425 aging-related genes (ARGs) from a cohort of 1,651, and identified a subset of seven with the highest prognostic significance for constructing a predictive model of LUAD outcomes, which includes RHPN2, BLK, PTPRO, CA4, UBE2C, METTL7A, and H2BC12. The RHPN2 gene, encoding a Rho-GTPase-binding protein, has been linked to colorectal cancer and orofacial clefts due to specific gene polymorphisms [9, 19]. RHPN2 is highly expressed in a variety of malignant tumors, including non-small cell lung cancer, and may affect the growth and drug resistance of lung cancer through the ceRNA regulatory pathway [11]. The oncogene B lymphoid kinase (BLK) is predominantly associated with the tumor necrosis factor (TNF) signaling pathway, the IL-17 signaling pathway, and cytokine-cytokine receptor interactions, exhibiting oncogenic potential in non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) [38].Receptor-type protein tyrosine phosphatase (PTPRO), recognized as a tumor suppressor in various malignancies, has been preliminarily shown to impede lung cancer progression and may regulate the infiltration of diverse immune cells in LUAD (LUAD) through its overexpression [12, 26].CA4 is an isozyme of carbonic anhydrase (CAs) that participates in various biological processes, including oncogenesis, lipogenesis, gluconeogenesis, ureagenesis, and the development and toxicity of various pathogens. Reduced expression of CA4 is associated with poor prognosis in a variety of cancers, especially in renal cell carcinoma (KIRC), lower-grade glioma (LGG), LUAD, prostate adenocarcinoma (PRAD), and uveal melanoma (UVM) [40]a). The ubiquitin-conjugating enzyme E2C (UBE2C, also known as UBCH10) is a crucial component of the ubiquitin-proteasome system, implicated in cancer progression, though its specific mechanisms in LUAD, including diagnostic, prognostic, and immunotherapeutic values, remain understudied [6].Evidence suggests that it may foster lung cancer cell proliferation and inhibit autophagy [44]. Methyltransferase-like protein 7 A (METTL7A), a member of the human methyltransferase-like protein family, may exert a suppressive effect in the development of LUAD (LUAD), with its decreased expression potentially contributing to the progression of LUAD, although the specific mechanisms involved await further investigation [32]. H2BC12 is a promising biomarker for the diagnosis and prognosis of patients with gliomas; however, its impact on non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) remains to be elucidated [46]. Based on our findings, we propose a novel approach to further refine the prognostic risk stratification of lung cancer patients according to genetic test results, including predictions of patient responses to immunotherapy, which may facilitate more precise clinical interventions and further improve patient outcomes.

In this study, a risk model constructed using prognostic genes effectively predicted the 1-year, 3-year, and 5-year survival rates of LUAD patients, with the high-risk group exhibiting a significantly lower survival rate compared to the low-risk group. Additionally, other characteristic studies on LUAD have established risk models, such as those related to immunity [36] and ferroptosis [43], which also demonstrate a robust capacity to predict LUAD patient prognosis. Importantly, the risk model established in this study, although based on aging-related genes, is not confounded by age. The results indicate no significant difference in age between the high-risk and low-risk groups, yet there is a considerable difference in overall survival (OS). Furthermore, significant differences in risk scores were observed in certain stages, T categories, and N categories. This suggests that our risk model possesses high clinical utility and can accurately predict the clinical characteristics of lung cancer patients. This capability facilitates more refined risk stratification among lung cancer patients, enabling more personalized treatment approaches and thereby potentially extending patient survival.

Understanding the biological processes involving prognostic genes can facilitate the development of targeted therapeutic drugs. In this study, GSEA analysis revealed that both RHPN2 and UBE2C are enriched in the cell cycle pathway. Previous research has indicated that extended Dex treatment irreversibly blocks the cell cycle and induces a senescence phenotype in GR-overexpressing lung tumor cells through sustained activation of the p27Kip1 gene. This effect may improve outcomes for surgery and pemetrexed chemotherapy, as well as enhance tumor sensitivity to immunotherapy [43]. Under cisplatin exposure, G1-phase cells, late S-phase cells, or cells treated with PARP/RAD51 inhibitors retain higher levels of DNA damage and experience prolonged S/G2 phase arrest and senescence [15]. Exposure to low-dose BPAP(a potent enhancer substance with catecholaminergic and serotoninergic activity in the brain) results in increased mTOR pathway activity and p16INK-induced cell cycle arrest, leading to a senescent phenotype with a loss of proliferative potential [30]. This finding highlights the interconnectedness between cell cycle regulation, senescence, and tumor proliferation.

Additionally, we observed significant enrichment of H2BC12, RHPN2, and UBE2C in low-proteasome environments. Proteasome inactivation is a key feature during cellular senescence, leading to the accumulation of abnormal proteins within cells [37], which may create an environment more conducive to tumor cell growth and progression. A recent study indicates that disrupting the interaction between LRPPRC and its stabilizing chaperone protein can induce mitochondrial protease-mediated degradation of LRPPRC, thereby inhibiting the growth of proximal-proliferative (PP) LUAD [47]. Members of the PSMG (proteasome assembly chaperone) family regulate the degradation of abnormal proteins, with increased expression of PSMG3 and PSMG4 in tumor cells associated with poor prognosis in lung cancer patients. Knocking down PSMG4 in LUAD cell lines reduces cell proliferation and impacts downstream molecules, suggesting that PSMG family members are novel prognostic biomarkers and potential therapeutic targets in LUAD progression [41]. Additionally, the proteasome has emerged as a new strategy in immunotherapy. Researchers have found that inhibiting CDK5 with shRNA or CDK5 inhibitors can degrade PD-L1 protein via the ubiquitin-proteasome pathway, leading to higher levels of CD3+, CD4+, and CD8+ T cells in the spleen [14].

Proteasome inactivation associated with senescence may lead to abnormal protein accumulation within tumor cells, potentially impacting the effectiveness of immunotherapy. This study observed significant differences between high- and low-risk groups in immune cell infiltration and immunotherapy scores, suggesting that tumor risk status may be linked to immune characteristics through proteasome inactivation levels. Additionally, the high-risk group showed lower stromal and ESTIMATE scores. These findings indicate that the prognostic model based on aging-related genes effectively reflects clinical characteristics and the heterogeneity of the tumor microenvironment, aiding in the development of new treatment strategies for LUAD patients [33, 40].

Our research is subject to inherent limitations that require acknowledgment. Initially, the sample cohort utilized in our analysis was predominantly non-Asian, which may engender biases in the outcomes and necessitates subsequent validation within Chinese patient populations. Secondly, while these sensitivity analyses support the robustness of our findings, residual bias from undetected pleiotropy or other unknown confounders remains a universal limitation of the MR approach. Furthermore, the machine learning procedures employed carry the risk of inducing bias and overfitting, thereby potentially imparting additional variance to the results. The sufficiency of the model’s generalizability awaits confirmation through the corroboration of larger-scale datasets. At present, there is a notable difficulty in precisely delineating the cut-off values for these biomarkers, and their clinical application is an issue that deserves further investigation. Within the scope of our study, we employed PCR methodologies to qualitatively ascertain the differential expression of these biomarkers between malignant and peritumoral tissues. We anticipate that more in-depth research on the seven genes involved in this prognostic model will help elucidate their molecular mechanisms in the processes of tumorigenesis and progression, which is of great significance for a deeper understanding of tumor behavior and the development of new therapeutic targets.

Conclusion

A risk model has been developed, centered on seven genes associated with aging(including RHPN2, BLK, PTPRO, CA4, UBE2C, METTL7A, and H2BC12), to predict survival outcomes and therapeutic responses to immunotherapy in lung cancer, offering novel perspectives for tailored treatment approaches.

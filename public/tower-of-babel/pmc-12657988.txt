A autophagy related gene signature to predict the prognosis of pancreatic carcinoma and tumor immune microenvironment evaluation
Authors: Xujie Wang, Xiaoyi Wang, Bicheng Ying
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Pancreatic adenocarcinoma (PAAD) has a poor prognosis and responds poorly to immunotherapy. Growing evidence indicates that autophagy promotes tumor progression, and autophagy-related genes (ARGs) have been established as prognostic markers in various cancers. Based on consensus clustering of ARGs, 177 PAAD cases were classified into two subtypes, with cluster B exhibiting weaker anti-tumor immunity. Using univariate COX and Least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression, 13 ARGs were identified to construct a prognostic model. A risk score formula was developed and validated using the GSE183795 dataset. Patients were divided into high- and low-risk groups, with the high-risk group associated with poorer survival. The model showed strong predictive accuracy, with time-dependent area under the curve (AUC) values ranging from 0.762 to 0.848 in the training cohort and 0.658 to 0.714 in the validation cohort. Further analysis revealed that high-risk patients had suppressed immunity but increased sensitivity to certain chemotherapy drugs. Additionally, high tumor mutational burden (TMB) and RNA stemness score (RNAss) were correlated with worse prognosis in high-risk patients. This ARG-based model effectively predicts PAAD outcomes and may guide clinical treatment strategies.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-26358-6.

Introduction

Pancreatic carcinoma (PC) is one of the deadliest malignant cancers of the digestive tract in the world, and its 5-year overall survival (OS) rate is less than 10%1. PAAD may replace breast cancer, colorectal and prostate cancer as the second leading source of cancer-related death2. The patients with PAAD were classified into I ~ IV grade according to Computed Tomography (CT) images. However, when a tumor is found, it has usually metastasized and spread, making it extremely difficult to remove3. Although adjuvant therapy including radiation therapy, chemical drug therapy and molecular targeted drug therapy have made progress, the survival rate of sufferers with PAAD is still very low4. However, due to the complexity and heterogeneity among tumor cells, the clinical results are not satisfactory. Thus, it is imperative to carry out personalized treatment. In addition, the discovery of applicable prognostic markers may contribute to the development of individualized therapeutics.

Autophagy plays an important role in maintaining the dynamic balance of cells and organisms. And participate in biological systems that degrade proteins and cellular components, so that cells can be dynamically balanced and energy balance can be maintained5. Some scholars had found that the disorder of autophagy is the cause of a wide range of pathological diseases, including malignant tumors6,7 and cardiovascular diseases8, neurodegenerative diseases9, metabolic diseases10, lung diseases11, kidney diseases12, infectious diseases13, musculoskeletal diseases14 and eye diseases. In malignant tumors, autophagy plays a dual character by inhibiting cancer initiation and supporting cancer progression15. In detail, autophagy plays a dynamic role in tumor inhibition or promotion in different backgrounds and stages of malignant tumors development. In the initial stage of tumor, autophagy can prevent the occurrence of tumor and inhibit the development of tumor. It is regulated by a way of survival and quality control mechanism. As long as the tumor develops to an advanced stage and was affected by environmental stress, autophagy helps the survival and growth of established tumors through a dynamic degradation and circulatory system, and promotes malignant tumors invasiveness by promoting metastasis16. Sui et al. proved that autophagy is a possible biomarker in anticancer treatment, and drug tolerance and disease advancement are closely related to autophagy17. At present, ARGs have been constructed to forecast the prognosis of a variety of malignant cancer, such as colon cancer18, lung adenocarcinoma19 and breast invasive carcinoma20. Yet, the correlation between autophagy and PAAD is not clear so far.

The research used multiple public databases such as The Cancer Genome Atlas Dataset (TCGA), Genotype-Tissue Expression (GETx), Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO), Human Protein Atlas (HPA). A predictive model consisting of 13 ARGs was constructed by univariate COX and LASSO analysis, and its accuracy and validity are verified. The single sample gene set enrichment analysis (ssGSEA), Immunological characteristics, Immunosuppressive molecule, Major histocompatibility vomplex (MHC) molecular and stimulator genes analysis and prediction of IC50 have guiding significance for immunological therapy. The high-risk population chort showed a high RNAss, which means that they have the ability to disease progression, distant metastasis and drug tolerance. We consider that this research will guide therapy options and offer some new study directions for enhancing autophagy against PC.

Materials and methods

RNA-seq data from public databases

Public PAAD RNA sequencing materials and corresponding clinicopathological data were derived from TCGA and GEO dataset. The RNA-seq datasets of pancreas were downloaded from the GTEx portal. We extracted 177 patients with PAAD from TCGA database and PC with complete clinicopathological materials as training cohort. Then the Fragments per kilobase of transcript per million fragments mapped data (FPKM) gene expression matrix data were processed and turned into Transcripts per kilobase million values (TPM) data. In addition, the RNA-seq data and survival data of 139 PC patients in the GSE183795 set were used as external validation queues. In addition, the set of genes related to human autophagy-dedicated was extracted from the human autophagy database.

Selection of ARGs and ARGs related to prognosis

A total of 232 ARGs related to human autophagy-dedicated were collected from multiple public database (HADB, THANATOS, HAMdb) and stored in Supplementary Table 1. Then, according to the R “LIMMA” package, the differentially expressed genes (DEGs) between normal and tumor samples in the training queue were screened. The filtering criteria are | log2FC | > 1 and p < 0.05. Finally, DEGs related to autophagy were obtained. The STRING database is used to list the network diagram of protein-protein interaction (PPI) between DEARGs. The PPI results are then presented through Cytoscape (v3.8.0). In addition, GO and KEGG analysis revealed the functions or related pathways of these DEGs21. Next univariate COX analysis (p < 0.05) was used to select the ARGs related to prognosis, then draw the related network diagram and intersection Venn diagram by R software. And the KM survival curve is drawn for DEARGs.

Subtype cluster recognition

The DEARGs of PC sufferers in TCGA-PAAD cohort was analyzed by subtype cluster analysis. The cohort was divided into different subtypes according to R “ConensusClusterPlus”. Specifically, according to the 28 autophagy-related’s prognostic genes (ARPGs), we further divided the 177 PC samples into two different clusters by unsupervised cluster analysis. 66 cases were divided into cluster A and 111 cases were divided into cluster B. In this study, the OS, progression-free survival (PFS), disease-free survival (DFS) and disease-specific survival (DSS) of patients with PC were analyzed and visualized. The heatmap is used to show the clinicopathological differences between different subtypes (such as tumor stage, histology). In addition, the pathway of functional enrichment among different subtypes was explored by Gene Set Variation Analysis (GSVA). Then ssGSEA analysis was used to study the differences of tumor infiltrating immune cells and immune function among PC subtypes. The tumor microenvironment (TME) scores among different subtypes were evaluated by “ggpubr” package.

Establishment and validation of the Autophagy-Related risk signature

On the basis of univariate COX analysis to extract prognostic parameters, LASSO-COX regression analysis was used to determine the core genes, so as to construct the corresponding prognostic risk characteristics and draw risk model. Then, according to the median risk score of sufferers in the TCGA-PAAD cohort, PC sufferers were divided into two different high- and low-risk groups. KM analysis was used to compare the prognostic correlation between high and low risk groups. Then the correlation diagram of risk factors between the two groups is drawn to show the differences between the two groups. Principal component analysis (PCA) is a technique that simplifies the analysis of two sets of data and uses the “rgl” R package for 3D visualization. In addition, in order to increase the accuracy and reliability of the model, we validate the GSE183795 queue as an external verification.

The relationship between risk characteristics and clinicopathological data

In order to reveal the potential clinical significance of the risk model, the correlation between risk score and clinicopathological features was analyzed in the TCGA and GSE183795 set.

The construction of nomogram and its correction curve

Univariate COX and multivariate COX were used to analyze whether the risk score and clinicopathological features were independent risk factors. The receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were generated by the timeROC package. The prognostic efficiency of the model was measured by the AUC. ROC curve was established to compare the effects of different factors on prognosis. Finally, the nomogram and correction curve of OS for 2-, 3- and 4-years are drawn.

Prognostic analysis and enrichment analysis of different risk groups

Patients with PC were divided into different clinical subgroups according to tumor stage (T1-2, T3-4, N0-N1, M0-M1), tumor stage (stage I-II, III-IV), histology (G1-2, G3-4) (Supplementary Table 2). KM survival analysis was used to further evaluate whether there were differences in high and low risk cohorts among different clinical subtypes. The differences and functional approaches between high and low risk cohorts are discussed by using GSVA and GSEA methods. The enrichment analysis of differential genes in high and low risk groups was analyzed by GO and KEGG.

Immune-Related analysis

In this study, a variety of immune infiltration algorithms were used to explore the correlation of cancer infiltrating immune cells in two different risk groups. The relation between 13 ARGs and immune cell infiltration was analyzed by “CIBERSORT”. The activation of immunosuppressive molecules, MHC molecules, immune checkpoints and stimulating factor genes among different risk groups were evaluated.

DNA methylation analysis

MEXPRESS (https://mexpress.be/) is a database that visualizes the relationship between patient clinical information-methylation-expression in the TCGA database. The study utilized the MEXPUSS web server to estimate and visualize DNA methylation of 13 ARPGs in the TCGA cancer dataset.

Copy number variations and genetic mutation

Sort out the copy number variation (CNV) and somatic mutation information in TCGA-PAAD data. The waterfall diagram is used to show the mutation between high and low risk groups.

Evaluation of immunohistochemical staining

The corresponding immunohistochemical (IHC) staining results were obtained from the human protein atlas database to evaluate the protein expression between normal pancreatic and pancreatic cancer tissues.

Drug-Sensitive and cancer stem cell correlation analysis

tumor immune dysfunction and exclusion (TIDE) score will better help oncologists to select pancreatic cancer patients who are more suitable for immunosuppressive therapy. The online Genomics of Drug Sensitivity database was used for analysis. And the sensitivity of commonly used chemotherapy drugs in sufferers with PC was analyzed, and the outcomes were calculated and expressed by IC50. Finally, the relation between risk score and RNAss is analyzed and visualized.

Statistical analysis

In this study, the bioinformatics data were analyzed by R software (version 4.2.1). According to the different data types, the datas were analyzed by student’s t-test and Wilcoxon non-parametric test. Correlation analysis using Spearman method. P < 0.05 was regarded statistically significant.

Unsupervised clustering analysis of PC subtypes based on 28 arpgs

According to the 28 ARPGs, we further divided the PC samples (TCGA-PAAD set) into two different clusters by unsupervised cluster analysis. Specifically, 66 cases were divided into cluster A and 111 cases were divided into cluster B (Figure.4 A). Through the analysis of several different time points between the two clusters (such as OS, PFS, DFS and DSS), we found that there were significant differences between them, and the prognosis of patients with cluster B was worse than that of patients with cluster A (Figure.4B). Whereafter, the internal relationship between the two subgroups of PC and clinicopathological data was analyzed. Figure.4 C shows that the patients in cluster B are closely related to the high expression of ARPGs and poor clinicopathological parameters (such as N stage, tumor stage and histology), which also confirms that the prognosis of cluster B is worse. Then we analyzed the differences in functional pathway and immune function between the two clusters in two different ways (GSVA and ssGSEA analyses). Through GSVA analyses method, it was found that compared with cluster A, the main KEGG pathways of cluster B included “cell cycle”, “DNA replication”, “tumor-related pathways (such as bladder and thyroid)”, “multiple signal pathways (such as Notch and p53 signal pathway)”, and “various metabolism including glutamic acid, pyrimidine metabolism, glycerolipid metabolism as well as glycolysis gluconeogenesis”. It is suggested that cluster B may be closely related to the occurrence and evolution of PC (Figure.4D). Furthermore, the differences between the two clusters (cluster A and B) were analyzed, and the results were analyzed by GO and KEGG analysis, and the first 20 rich terms were shown respectively (Figure.4E-4 F). In addition, the immune infiltrating scores were calculated based on the ssGSEA algorithm, and it was found that several immune cells (such as B cells, CD8 + T cells, mast cells, neutrophils, NK cells, pDCs, TIL) had higher permeability levels in cluster A (Figure.4G). Similarly, most immunological functions like checkpoint, human leukocyte antigen, inflammation promotion showed higher scores in cluster A, while Type I IFN Response and MHC class I mainly showed higher scores in cluster B (Figure.4 H). Through the analysis of TME, the results show that cluster B is lower than cluster A in terms of stromal, immune, and estimate scores (Figure.4I). It is well known that the tumor immune microenvironment is closely related to the occurrence and development of tumors and the response to immunotherapy. Therefore, the differences of immune cell infiltration and immune function among different PC subtypes may be the key factors to determine the effect and prognosis of PC immunotherapy.

Establishment and verification of correlation analysis between Autophagy-Related risk signals and clinicopathological parameters

First of all, for 28 ARPGs, the risk model is established by LASSO Cox regression analysis (Figure.S1). Then a risk profile consisting of 13 ARPGs is obtained. The specific formula is as follows: Risk Score = EIF4EBP1×0.182 −HDAC6×0.265 − GABARAP×0.368 + CASP4×0.310 − ATG16L2×0.053 + BCL2L1×0.158 + BAG3×0.032 − ATG4B×0.819 + TNFSF10×0.319 + PTK6×0.094 + ITGA3×0.180 + BIRC5×0.003 + BAK1×0.009 (Supplementary Table 4). The PC sufferers in the train queue were divided into two different risk groups (88 at high risk and 89 at low risk) according to the median risk score. KM survival analysis showed that OS was worse in high-risk group than in low-risk group (Figure.5 A). In addition, similar outcomes were further confirmed in external data sets, with the OS of high-risk group in the GSE183795 cohort even worse (Figure.5B). Then it describes the differences of risk score distribution, survival status and risk gene expression patterns among different risk groups in each cohort (Figure.5 C-5D). Incidentally, we performed immunohistochemical analysis of these ARPGs in normal pancreatic tissue and pancreatic cancer sample, with the exception of GABARAP. From the HPA database analysis, the results can well proved their expression patterns at the protein level (Figure.S2). In addition, we performed t-Distributed Stochastic Neighbor Embedding (t-SNE) and 3D-PCA analysis on the prognostic model (Figure.5E-5 F). The results show that our model can effectively distinguish between high and low risk groups.

Nomogram construction and risk model assessment

Furthermore, univariate COX and multivariate COX analysis were used to evaluate the independent predictive effect of risk score and clinicopathological parameters on prognosis. Univariate COX showed that risk score, age, histology, tumor stage were risk factors for prognosis (Figure.7 A). Additionally, multivariate COX further confirmed that the risk score (p < 0.001, HR = 4.237, 95% CI: 2.836 − 6.331) and N stage (p = 0.023, HR = 1.923, 95% CI: 1.097 − 3.371) were independent hazard factors (Figure.7B). In addition, based on the above results, we constructed a nomogram to forecas the incidence of 2-, 3-, and 4-year OS in sufferers with PC (Figure.7 C). And through the calibration curve, we verify that our nomogram has good consistency and accuracy in predicting OS (Figure.7D). Meanwhile, we performed ROC curve analysis to evaluate the sensitivity and specificity of the model in predicting prognosis. And the area under the ROC curve (AUC) is calculated. The results show that the 2-, 3- and 4-year AUCs of the training cohort are 0.762, 0.806 and 0.848 respectively, and the 2-year, 3-year and 4-year AUC of the verification cohort are 0.658, 0.723 and 0.714 respectively, indicating that our prediction model has good prediction performance (Figure.7E-7 F). In addition, we constructed a 3-year ROC for eight clinicopathological parameters and model parameters, and the results showed that the model was more predictable in TCGA-PAAD cohort (Figure.7G). Decision curve analysis (DCA) results show that our model has more advantages (Figure.7 H).

Enrichment analysis of autophagy-associated risk signature

In order to explore the potential differences in biological processes and functional pathways among different risk groups in the training cohort, we conducted an individual survey by GSVA and GSEA analysis, respectively. First of all, the results of GSVA showed that the significantly rich terms in GO were “negative regulation of apoptotic signaling pathway”, “Purine nucleic acid metabolic process”, “Deoxyribonucleic acid metabolism process” and “Negative regulation of protein degradation” in BP, “Laminin binding” and “Cadherin binding” in MF, and “Kinetic microtubule” in CC. Moreover, several noteworthy KEGG pathways like “cancer-associated (like Pancreatic cancer, Thyroid carcinoma, bladder cancer) pathways”, “cell cycle”, “P53 signal pathway”, “various metabolism pathways including Amino sugar and nucleotide glucose metabolism”, “other enzymes in drug metabolism”, “Glycolysis gluconeogenesis” and “Pentose phosphate pathway” were significantly concentrated in patients with PC (Figure.8 A). Whereafter, the above biological annotations were verified by GSEA analysis. The results showed that the KEGG pathways in high risk sufferers were mainly concentrated in “cell cycle”, “pathways in cancer”, “P53 signal pathway” and “pyrimidine metabolism”, whereas “neuroactivity ligand receptor interaction”, “ribosome”, as well as “intestinal immune network for the production of immunoglobulin” were mainly concentrated in low-risk people (Figure.8B). Subsequently, GO analysis showed that the most common terms related to high risk scores were “mitotic nuclear division”, “nuclear chromosome separation”, and “sister chromosome separation”, while “B cell receptor signaling pathway”, “immunoglobulin production”, “T cell receptor complex”, “Phagocytic recognition” were associated with low risk patients (Figure.8 C). In addition, a total of 100 DEGs were screened out between the high and low risk groups using the filter criteria of |log2FC| > 1 and p < 0.05. Meanwhile, these DEGs were analyzed by GO and KEGG (Figure.8D).

DNA methylation analysis

Generally, methylation modification of genes can reduce the transcriptional expression of genes. To reveal the correlation between 13 ARPGs and the degree of methylation of its methylation sites and clinical traits (age at initial pathologic diagnosis; histological type; gender; tumor stage simplified; sample type; os event; os), MEXPRESS web server was used to retrieve methylation data for 13 ARPGs in the TCGA PAAD cohort. As a result, we found that age at diagnosis of PAAD showed a weak positive correlation with EIF4EBP1 expression (Spearman, r = 0.173, P < 0.05) and PTK6 (Spearman, r = 0.173, P < 0.05). Genes significantly negatively correlated with patient survival time are EIF4EBP1 (Spearman, r = −0.204, P < 0.01), CASP4 (Spearman, r = −0.384, P < 0.001), TNFSF10 (Spearman, r = −0.373, P < 0.001), BIRC5 (Spearman, r = −0.249, P < 0.001), PTK6 (Spearman, r = −0.316, P < 0.001), ITGA3 (Spearman, r = −0.229, P < 0.01), BAG3 (Spearman, r = −0.251, P < 0.001), BCL2L1 (Spearman, r = −0.170, P < 0.05), BAK1 (Spearman, r = −0.274, P < 0.001), This result suggests that high expression of these genes may be a driving factor in cancer progression. While GABARAP (Spearman, r = 0.253, P < 0.001) and HDAC6 (Spearman, r = 0.203, P < 0.01) were significantly positively correlated with patient survival time (Fig. 10).

In addition, the expression of 13 ARPGs were positively correlated with their copy number. The figure.10 shows the relationship between 13 ARPGs and their DNA methylation at different CpG sites.

Copy number variations and genetic mutation

Most CNV are closely related to complex diseases. If CNV occurs in or around the sequence of tumor-related genes, it may cause oncogene activation, tumor suppressor gene inactivation, and eventually lead to tumorigenesis. Additionally, we also studied the mutational status of 28 ARGs in TCGA samples. ULK1 and STK11 deletions were the most common (Figure.11 A). A circos diagram is drawn to show the changes of these regulatory factors characterized by CNV on chromosomes (Figure.11B). And Considering the potential value of somatic mutation in pancreatic carcinoma progression and individualized clinical treatment. Therefore, we analyzed the somatic mutations in pancreatic cancer samples, including the whole group (Figure.11 C) and two distinct risk cohorts (Figure.11D), and drew the corresponding waterfall plots. Specifically, the mutation frequency of high-risk group was higher than that of low-risk group, and missense mutation was the most common form of mutation in the two distinct risk cohorts. Incidentally, KRAS and TP53 missense mutations were most common in high-risk PC patients, while CDKN2A frameshift deletion was more frequent in low-risk group. Additionally, The relationship between risk score and TMB is discussed. And there is a significant positive correlation between the two variables (R = 0.3 p = 8.1e-5), and the TMB score of high-risk patients is higher (Figure.11E-11 F). Low TMB is a favorable prognostic factor22. This study found that the OS of pancreatic cancer sufferers with high mutation was worse than that of people with low mutation (p = 0.009) (Figure.11G). Among them, the sufferers with high mutation and high risk of pancreatic cancer have the worst prognosis (Figure.11 H).

Drug-Sensitive and cancer stem cell correlation analysis

Through the comprehensive analysis of hundreds of different tumor expression profiles, TIDE algorithm finds biomarkers to predict the efficacy of immune checkpoint inhibitors. Meanwhile, TIDE algorithm is used to evaluate the possibility of immune escape, and finally predict the response of immunotherapy23. Therefore, the relationship between risk score and tide score was studied to explore the potential significance of risk model in immunotherapy prediction. Specifically, there was a significant negative correlation between the two distinct risk groups (R = −0.26, p = 5.2e-4). High-risk cohorts had lower TIDE scores, suggesting that our risk characteristics have the ability to predict immunotherapy. Low-risk cohorts may be more likely to receive immunotherapy than high-risk cohorts (Figure.12 A). Additionally, we further analyzed the differences of IC50 values of commonly used chemotherapeutic drugs among different risk groups in order to explore feasible sensitive drugs. As shown in Figure.12B, lower IC50 values of AKT inhibitors such as VIII, bicalutamide, CMK, dasatinib, GNF.2, rapatinib, paclitaxel, PD.0325901 and thapsigargin were observed in the high-risk cohort, which means that high-risk sufferers may benefit from these chemotherapeutic drugs. Moreover, low-risk sufferers may benefit from the following drugs, such as Axitinib, Lenalidomide, metformin, Nutlin.3a, Vorinostat. Finally, we analyzed the correlation between risk score and RNAss. The results showed that there was a positive correlation between them. Patients with high risk scores have relatively strong stemness characteristics of tumor cells, so the prognosis is worse than that with low risk scores (Figure.12 C).

Discussion

Pancreatic carcinoma is one of the most common malignant tumors and has become the second leading cause of cancer-related death24. Although various treatments are used in patients with PC, such as surgical resection, chemotherapy, radiotherapy, the 5-year OS rate is still less than 5%25. Previous studies have shown that the high mortality rate in patients with pancreatic carcinoma is mainly due to difficult early diagnosis, strong invasiveness and early metastasis26. Therefore, it is very important to find new PC biomarkers and understand PC potential mechanism of action. It is also another valuable method to improve the prognosis of PC.

RNA-seq biotechnology can study gene function and gene structure at the overall level, and reveal the molecular mechanism of specific biological processes and disease occurrence. Therefore, it can be used to study the recurrence and metastasis of PAAD27. At present, many predictive models were based on unexplored genes, which makes it difficult for doctors to analyze the specific functions of these genes in prognostic markers. And it may not be possible to identify potential targets obtained from markers for personalized treatment development28. In this study, we focued on analyzing the prognostic of PAAD and ARGs, constructing nomogram and verifying the accuracy, and deeply understand the relationship between autophagy and PAAD from the aspects of enrichment function, immune cell infiltration, mutation, drug sensitivity and so on.

Previous research have found that ARGs play an important role in many malignant tumors. More specifically, Li and others. (2021) through the analysis of TCGA database, it is proved that 11-autophagy-related lncRNA signal can potentially predict the prognosis of breast cancer29. Similarly, Wang et al. screened ARGs related to Glioblastoma by TCGA. The risk score model constructed by NRG1, ITGA3 and MAP1LC3A genes has good predictive ability30. Yang et al. (2020) constructed a model of six ARGs to predict the OS of head and neck squamous cancer31. Additionally, Zhu et al. divided the sufferers with lung cancer in GEO database into different risk cohorts based on the expression levels of EIF4EBP1, TP63, BNIP3, ATIC, ERO1A and FADD32. Similar to our research, a study by Zhou et al. (2021) found that four ARGs (including ATG16L2, GNAI3, APOL1, PTK6) were closely related to the prognosis of PAAD, but the analysis was relatively simple33. Therefore, we analyzed the biological function and potential molecular mechanism of pancreatic cancer more comprehensively, focusing on the relationship between pancreatic cancer and immune infiltration.

In this study, we collected 28 ARPGs and divided pancreatic cancer samples into two subtypes based on unsupervised cluster analysis. Subsequently, 13 significant ARPGs (including ATG16L2, ATG4B, BAG3, BAK1, BCL2L1, BIRC5, CASP4, EIF4EBP1, GABARAP, HDAC6, ITGA3, PTK6 and TNFSF10) were obtained by univariate COX and LASSO analysis. These genes were used to construct a prognostic model. Their potential relationship with PC, briefly speaking, ATG16L2 is known to be a significant regulator of autophagy and plays an important role in autophagy pathway and tumor development34. Additionally, another research on the response of cells to cisplatin used ATG16L2 antibodies to monitor the expression of genes associated with autophagy and cell death. The results showed that cisplatin decreased the level of ATG16L2, suggesting that cisplatin may be a biomarker of platinum resistance in cancer cells35.

Autophagy-related 4 (ATG4) is the sole cysteine protease36. Liu et al. found that targeting ATG4B in various cancer cells may inhibit autophagy and trigger LKB1-AMPK energy sensing pathway, thus inhibiting tumor growth37. Studies have shown that pancreatic carcinoma cells are easily inhibited by ATG4B. This is most common in PC that depend on mutant K-RAS expression38. Some scholars have proposed a model of how autophagy promotes PC growth, such as the ability to provide metabolites and tumor response to stress39. Recently, it had been found that ribose-5-phosphate isomerase (autophagy-related enzymes) participates in drug resistance of KRAS-dependent PC by regulating nucleotide synthesis40,41. The inhibition of autophagy of KRAS-dependent PC is also an important reason for DNA damage and increased apoptosis. It is also found that autophagy limits DNA damage stress in PC, which may be affected by p53 status42,43. Similarly, autophagy inhibition may also affect the surrounding stromal cells44,45. This coincides with the fact that our study found that high-risk groups are associated with multiple metabolic pathways.

BAG3 is a multi-domain protein that plays an important role in a variety of cellular processes, such as the control of apoptosis, autophagy46,47. Recently, some scholars have found that BAG3 can be secreted by pancreatic cancer cells and other tumor cells. Secreted BAG3 can stimulate tumor interstitial macrophages to release factors that maintain cancer growth and metastasis. Meanwhile, BAG3 neutralization has the potential to treat PC48. And BAG3 is an important anti-apoptosis protein49,50. The damage of apoptosis or the increase of anti-apoptotic proteins may affect the progression of tumor. Some scholars have found that miR-340 can negatively regulate BAG3 and inactivate PI3K/AKT pathway. The deficiency of PI3K/AKT signal pathway increases the release of cytochrome c and the activity of caspase, which increases the apoptosis of cells51,52.

BAK1 can promote apoptosis53. Studies have shown that itraconazole inhibits the proliferation of PC cells by activating the mechanism of apoptosis induced by Bak-154. GABARAP is thought to be down-regulated in malignant tumors, while high expression is associated with a good prognosis55. Activation of RAS signal transduction pathway exists in about 30% of human cancers. GABARAP is associated with carcinogenic KRAS mutations and poor prognosis of PC. This indicates the potential tumor inhibitory effect of this pathway in RAS-driven human tumor56. Some studies have shown that GABARAP may inhibit the malignant behavior of breast carcinoma through AKT/mTOR pathway57.

HDAC is closely related to the occurrence and development of cancer58. The immunofluorescence data of Yang et al. show that inhibition of HDAC6 can induce autophagy of PC cells59. PC is a highly malignant tumor, which usually involves the deregulation of c-Myc gene. Fu et al. found an anti-tumor mechanism in PC cells: it can down-regulate the level of c-Myc protein by inhibiting HDAC6 subunit60. ITGA3 usually interacts with extracellular matrix proteins and plays an important role in cancer metastasis61. Hippo pathway effector YAP1 is an effective transcriptional coactivator, which forms a complex with ZEB1 and activates ITGA3 transcription in PC cells through YAP1/transcriptional enhancement related domain binding sites. ZIP4-miR-373-LATS2-ZEB1/YAP1-ITGA3 signal axis has a significant effect on PC metastasis and EMT plasticity62. PTK6 is expressed in a variety of malignant tumors, including PC. Studies have shown that PTK6 gene silencing increased cell survival rate and decreased apoptosis after drug therapy, while overexpression of PTK6 decreased cell survival rate and increased apoptosis rate63. And it has also been proved that PTK6 regulates the migration and invasion of PC cells through ERK signal transduction64.

TNFSF10 (also known as TRAIL) is a cytokine that can selectively induce apoptosis of tumor cells without causing damage to normal cells, which is promising in tumor therapy. At the same time, resistance to TRAIL-induced apoptosis has been found in many primary cacner and tumor cell lines, including many human PC cell lines65. Specifically, Ozawa et al. found that the expression levels of TRAIL, DR4 and DR5 in PC tissues were significantly higher than those in normal tissues. Some scholars have found that overexpression of MUC5AC in human PC can inhibit TRAIL-induced apoptosis of human PC cells, thus promoting tumor growth and metastasis66–69. EIF4EBP1 encodes a translation inhibitory protein70,71. This protein is the main substrate of mTOR and plays an important role in mTOR signaling pathway72. Promote cell growth and proliferation by responding to activated mTOR signaling pathways73. EIF4EBP1 is involved in the development and progressionof cancer and is associated with poor survival of cancer persons74. EIF4EBP1 promotes tumor adaptation to metabolic and genotoxic stress by selectively enhancing or blocking the translation of specific transcripts75,76. Some scholars have found that inhibiting the expression of mTOR/EIF4EBP1 axis can effectively inhibit the growth of PC tumor77.

In addition, the research on the role of residual risk genes (including BIRC5, CASP4 and BCL2L1) in PC is still insufficient, and their potential functions need to be further studied.

After constructing the risk signature related to autophagy, we further explore its significance in PC from many aspects of biology. In addition, PC persons were divided into two distinct risk groups based on the median risk score, and the prognosis of high-risk person is worse than that of low-risk person. Combined with the clinicopathological parameters, the nomogram was constructed. ROC curve and calibration curves prove the accuracy and specificity of the model, and verified by the independent PC cohort of GEO, indicating that our risk profile is stable and reliable. Subsequently, we also discussed the relationship between risk score and clinical characteristics, and the results showed that patients with high risk score usually had poor clinicopathological features. In addition, the enrichment analysis results of GSEA and GSVA also revealed the biological function of patients in different risk cohorts and different clusters. Furthermore, the difference of immune microenvironment composition in PC was analyzed by ssGSEA and CIBERSORT algorithms. The above comprehensive analysis of pancreatic cancer is helpful to further explore the goal of intervention and guide individualized treatment strategies.

Conclusion

In brief, this study reveals a new risk signal associated with autophagy, which can be used to evaluate the prognosis of PC persons. Although a variety of methods have been used to evaluate our model, there are still some limitations. In addition, our study has no experimental verification, so there are serious limitations. The model is still promising. In the future, pancreatic cancer tissue can be collected by surgery, then immunofluorescence staining and Western blotting can be performed to verify the predictive ability of the model78. Similarly, we can collect clinicopathological parameters to further analyze the significance of ARGs in PC therapy. In summary, we constructed a patient stratified model consisting of 13 ARGs. This model can effectively distinguish PC persons and play an important role in the accurate therapy of PC persons in the future.

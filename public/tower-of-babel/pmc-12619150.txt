Mechanism of METTL14-mediated RAD21 mRNA epigenetic transcriptome modification in inhibiting thyroid cancer development
Authors: Yinzhe Ge, Chen Qin, Mang Xiao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveThis study investigated the mechanism of methyltransferase-like 14 (METTL14)-mediated epigenetic transcriptome modification of RAD21 mRNA in inhibiting thyroid cancer (TC) development.MethodsTCGA and Starbase databases were used to predict the differential expression of METTL14 in TC tissues. First, 48 patients were enrolled. Immunohistochemistry, reverse transcription-quantitative polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR), and Western blot (WB) were employed for detecting METTL14 and RAD21 expression levels. The overall RNA N6-methyladenosine (m6A) methylation level was measured using m6A-RNA immunoprecipitation (MeRIP). TC cell lines were transfected with Ad-METTL14 and/or Ad-RAD21, and then detected for proliferation, apoptosis, migration, and invasion using cell count kit-8 assay, flow cytometry, and Transwell assay, respectively. MeRIP-qPCR was adopted to detect the RAD21 m6A modification level. RT-qPCR was applied for detecting RAD21 mRNA stability in actinomycin D-treated cells. RIP was implemented to detect METTL14-RAD21 interaction. Tumor formation assay was performed in nude mice. Tumor weight and volume were recorded. The collected tumor tissues were measured for Ki67-positive cell levels using immunohistochemistry, and METTL14 and RAD21 protein levels using WB.ResultsMETTL14 was notably downregulated and RAD21 was upregulated in TC tissues and cells. METTL14 overexpression inhibited TC cell proliferation, migration, and invasion, and promoted cell apoptosis. METTL14 overexpression reduced RAD21 mRNA stability and inhibited RAD21 expression through m6A modification. RAD21 upregulation caused opposite results to METTL14 overexpression on inhibiting TC cell malignant behaviors. METTL14 overexpression inhibited tumor formation in nude mice in vivo.ConclusionMETTL14 inhibits tumorigenesis in TC mice in vivo by mediating m6A modification of RAD21 and decreasing RAD21 mRNA stability.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12957-025-04024-5.Keywords: Thyroid cancer, METTL14, RAD21, M6A, Proliferation, Apoptosis

Introduction

The thyroid gland is an endocrine gland at the front of the neck, just beneath the larynx. Thyroid cancer (TC) is the most frequent tumor of the thyroid gland, but it is rare, contributing to approximately 1% of all tumors. Nevertheless, in recent years, the incidence rate of TC has been ever-increasing [1]. TC can be subcategorized into (1) differentiated TC, including papillary, follicular, and Huerthle cell tumors; (2) medullary TC, and (3) anaplastic TC. Differentiated TC patients have about 90% long-term survival rate, while poorly differentiated TC patients have only less than 10% survival rate due to resistance to standard treatment regimens [2, 3]. Therefore, it is urgently needed to explore new therapeutic targets for TC.

As the commonest internal modification in eukaryotic mRNA, N6-methyladenosine (m6A) plays a crucial role in post-transcriptional regulation. It is worth noting that m6A is a dynamic process catalyzed by methyltransferase-like 3 (METTL3) and METTL14, and removed by RNA demethylase, fat mass and obesity-associated protein (FTO), and alkylation repair homolog protein 5 [4]. It has been evidenced that m6 methylation is crucial for maintaining tumor cell function and characteristics during tumor occurrence [5, 6]. More importantly, accumulating studies have reported that m6A modification can significantly affect target genes through protein-RNA interaction in various disorders, such as breast cancer and TC [7, 8]. Growing evidence has revealed that the change of m6A methylation regulatory factors, especially changes in METTL14 expression, can affect long non-coding RNA and mRNA-regulated cancer cell proliferation and metastasis [9, 10]. Previous literature has provided evidence for the crucial role of METTL14 in TC. METTL14 was predicted to be significantly under-expressed in THCA by TCGA (https://ualcan.path.uab.edu/index.html)) and Starbase (https://rnasysu.com/encori/) databases online. Nevertheless, the action mechanism of METTL14 in TC still needs further exploration.

RAD21, a component of the cohesive complex, encodes a DNA double-strand break (DSB) repair protein. Generally, RAD21 and CCCTC-binding factor bind to highly conserved promoters and distal enhancers, participating in regulating transcription [11, 12]. Spatial transcriptomics has shown that RAD21 is crucial to maintain the identity of embryonic stem cells via collaboration with pivotal pluripotent transcription factors [13]. Additionally, RAD21 plays a role in various cellular functions, including DNA replication/repair, mitosis, apoptosis, and transcriptional regulation [14]. Moreover, the abnormal function of RAD21 is associated with various cancers, contributing to malignant progression and poor prognosis [15, 16]. These studies emphasize the carcinogenic effects of RAD21 in diverse tumors. Nevertheless, whether the genomic changes of RAD21 impact TC occurrence and development remains to be explored.

In the present study, whether RAD21 had m6A modification was first predicted through SRAMP (http://www.cuilab.cn/sramp). Multiple high-confidence m6A sites were found in RAD21. This study investigated the mechanism of METTL14-mediated epigenetic transcriptome modification of RAD21 mRNA in inhibiting TC development. Therefore, it was hypothesized that METTL14 may mediate the m6A modification of RAD21 and regulate TC occurrence and development.

Materials and methods

Bioinformatics analysis

Differential METTL14 expression in TC tissues was predicted using the UALCAN (https://ualcan.path.uab.edu/analysis-cbttc.html) and Starbase (https://rnasysu.com/encori/) databases. The SRAMP database (http://www.cuilab.cn/sramp) was employed for predicting m6A methylation modification sites in RAD21.

Clinical sample collection

A total of 48 TC patients (27 males and 21 females, 46.25 ± 6.11 years) admitted to Sir Run Run Shaw Hospital, College of Medicine, Zhejiang University from January 2023 to June 2023 were enrolled. The tumor tissues and adjacent normal tissues of the enrolled patients were collected and stored at −80℃. All participants signed the written informed consent. This study was conducted following the Helsinki Declaration and approved by the Ethics Committee of Sir Run Run Shaw Hospital, College of Medicine, Zhejiang University (Approval Number: Run Run Shaw Hospital Review 2023 Study No. 0213).

Cell culture

Human TC cell lines TPC-1 (CL-0643) and 8305 C (CL-0613) and human normal thyroid epithelial cell line NTHY-ORI 3 − 1 (CL-0817) (all from Procell Life Science & Technology Co., Ltd., Wuhan, China) were maintained in a medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) (A5670701, Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA, USA) and 1% antibiotics (100 U/mL penicillin and 100 µg/mL streptomycin) (15140122, Invitrogen) in an incubator (37℃, 5% CO2). Cells in the 3rd passage and logarithmic growth phase were detached with 0.25% trypsin (Procell). After that, cells (1 × 106 cells/well) were seeded onto 6-well plates. When growing to 70–80% confluence, cells were collected for subsequent experiments.

Cell transfection and grouping

The plasmids of METTL14 overexpression (Ad-METTL14) and RAD21 overexpression (Ad-RAD21), and their negative control Ad-NC (all from GenePharma, Shanghai, China) were transfected into cells using Lipofectamine 3000 (Invitrogen) to establish stable overexpression cell lines. In short, 2 × 105 cells were placed on 6-well plates, followed by transfection with designated lentivirus utilizing transfection reagents HitransG P/A (GeneChem, Shanghai, China), as per the protocol. Afterward, 3.0 µg/mL puromycin (MCE, USA) was used for screening infected cells. After transfection (≥ 14 days), the transfection efficiency was verified using reverse transcription-quantitative polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR) and Western blot (WB) analysis.

RT-qPCR

Extraction of cell total RNA was performed using TRIzol regent (Invitrogen) as per the protocol, followed by transcription into complementary DNA utilizing the PrimeScript RT kit (Takara, Dalian, China). Next, qPCR was conducted on the ABI 7900HT fast PCR real-time system (Applied Biosystems, Foster City, CA, USA) using SYBR®Premix Ex Taq™ II (Takara), with the conditions as: pre-denaturation at 94℃ for 10 min, and then 38 cycles of denaturation at 95℃ for 10 s, annealing at 58℃ for 20 s, and extension at 72℃ for 34 s. The experiment was repeated 3 times, with glyceraldehyde 3-phosphate dehydrogenase (GAPDH) as the internal reference. Data were analyzed using the 2−ΔΔCt method. WB

Total protein of cells was extracted on ice using radioimmunoprecipitation assay lysis buffer (P0013E, Beyotime, Shanghai, China). The supernatant was collected and the protein content was determined using the bicinchoninic acid protein concentration detection kit (P0011, Beyotime). After that, proteins were subjected to 10% sodium dodecyl sulfate-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis for separation and then transferred onto polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) membranes. After blocking (1 h) in Tris-Buffered Saline Tween-20 (TBST, 20 mM Tris, 137 mM NaCl, and 0.1% Tween-20), PVDF membranes underwent incubation (overnight, 4℃) with primary antibodies METTL14 (ab309096, 1:1000, Abcam Inc., Cambridge, MA, USA) and RAD21 (ab217678, 1:1000, Abcam). After washing, horseradish peroxidase (HRP)-conjugated secondary antibody goat anti-rabbit immunoglobulin G (IgG) antibody (ab6721, 1:2000, Abcam) was incubated (room temperature, 2 h) with the membranes. GAPDH (ab9485, 1:2500, Abcam) served as an internal control. An enhanced chemiluminescence detection kit (ECL Plus, Life Technology) was utilized for protein band detection. Image J (Media Cybernetics, USA) was employed for grayscale analysis.

Cell counting kit-8 (CCK-8) assay

Each group of cells (1 × 105 cells/well) was placed onto 96-well plates, and the plate was pre-cultured in an incubator (37℃, 5% CO2). A CCK-8 reagent (C0039, Beyotime) was employed for cell proliferation detection. In short, 10 µL CCK-8 solution was added in each well of the plate for 1-h incubation. The optical density (OD) value at 450 nm was measured at 0, 24, 48, and 72 h using an Elx800 Reader (Bio-Tek Instruments Inc., Winooski, VT, USA).

Transwell assay

The Transwell chamber was pre-filled with matrigel (BD Biosciences, San Jose, CA, USA) for cell invasion assessment, while that for cell migration assessment had no matrigel. Before the experiment, the cells were cultured (24 h) in serum-free medium, and then resuspended in a 1% FBS-contained culture medium. The upper chamber was seeded with 1 × 105 cells, and the lower chamber contained 600 µL 10% FBS-supplemented complete culture medium. Following incubation for 24 h, cells that did not pass through the upper chamber were wiped off with a cotton swab, and the remaining cells were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde, and stained with hematoxylin (517-28-2, MACKLIN). Finally, cells in 5 random fields of view were observed and counted, and the mean was taken.

Flow cytometry

Cell apoptosis was determined using the Annexin V-fluorescein isothiocyanate (FITC)/propidium iodide (PI) double staining. In short, cells in each group were detached with 0.25% trypsin (Procell), centrifugated, and collected. After 3 washes with phosphate buffer saline (PBS), cells were resuspended in buffer solution. Cells were incubated (room temperature, 15–20 min) with Annexin V-FITC and PI away from light as per the Annexin V-FITC/PI cell apoptosis detection kit’ protocol (Elabscience Biotechnology Co., Ltd, China), followed by cell apoptosis detection using a flow cytometer (Acebio, San Diego, CA, USA) within 1 h.

RNA Immunoprecipitation (RIP)

A Magna RIP™ RNA binding protein immunoprecipitation kit (CAT.17-701, Millipore, Billerica, MA, USA) was applied for RIP following the protocol. The precipitated RNA was analyzed using RT-qPCR. The antibodies were anti-METTL14 antibody (ab309096, 1:30, Abcam) and recombinant rabbit IgG (ab172730, 1:100, Abcam).

Methylated RIP (MeRIP)-qPCR

In short, 1IP buffer (300 µL; 50 mM Tris HCl, pH 7.4, 150 mM NaCl, 0.1% NP40, and 40 U/µL RNase inhibitor) supplemented with anti-m6A rabbit polyclonal antibody (2 µg, Synaptic Systems) was added with total RNA and m6A spike-in control mixture (2 µg). Next, blocking (4℃, 2 h) of Dynabeads™M-280 sheep anti-rabbit IgG suspension of each sample (20 µl) was conducted utilizing the newly prepared 0.5% bovine serum albumin, followed by 3 washes with 300 µL of 1IP buffer and resuspension in the above-prepared total RNA-antibody mixture. RNA bound to m6A-antibody beads and rotated (4℃, 2 h) head to tail, followed by washing (50℃, 1 h) with 200 µL eluent. After isolating the co-precipitated RNA, RT-qPCR was conducted.

Quantification of m6A RNA methylation

The EpiQuik™m6A RNA Methylation Quantification Kit (colorimetric) (P-9005, Epigentek, China) was employed for m6A quantification. Each well was added with the relevant solutions following the instructions. The solution was gently tilted from one side to another side for mixing, followed by m6A RNA capture. Residual wash buffer in each well was thoroughly removed at each wash. When the positive control wells’ color changed to medium blue, the signal was detected. Finally, the enzyme reaction was stopped by the addition of 100 µL stop solution to each well. After that, the solution’s color turned to yellow. Finally, the OD value at 450 nm was determined utilizing a microplate reader.

RNA stability

mRNA transcription was inhibited via the treatment of actinomycin D (5 µg/mL, Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA). Next, cells were harvested at 0, 2, 4, and 8 h, and RAD21 mRNA level was analyzed. Total RNA extraction was performed, followed by RT-qPCR.

Experimental animals

A total of 12 specific pathogen-free female nude mice [5–6 weeks, 20 ± 2 g; Vital River Laboratory Animal Technology Co., Ltd., Beijing, China, SYXK (Beijing) 2016-0011] were maintained in a sterile condition (26–28℃, 40–60% air humidity) under a 12-h light/dark cycle and could freely take food and water. All animal experiments were reviewed and got approval from the Animal Ethics Committee of Sir Run Run Shaw Hospital, College of Medicine, Zhejiang University (Approval Number: Run Run Shaw Hospital Review 2023 Research No. 0017).

Animal treatment

Cells were plated (5 × 105 cells/well) on 6-well plates, cultivated (24 h), and washed with PBS. TPC-1–8305 C cells were transfected with Ad-METTL14 lentivirus and its negative control (Ad-NC) (GenePharma) using Lipofectamine®2000 (Invitrogen), followed by washing and resuspension in a 10 µM dichloro-dihydro-fluorescein diacetate-contained serum-free medium. All operations were conducted following the instructions [17].

After adaptive feeding for 1 week, a TC xenograft tumor model was established in nude mice. Briefly, 5 × 104 stable transfected TPC-1–8305 C cells were transfected into the right axilla of nude mice [17]. Tumor changes were measured once a week using a vernier caliper and tumor volume was calculated: V = a × b2/2 (mm3, where a is the maximum diameter and b is the vertical diameter).

Nude mice were allocated into the Ad-NC group and the Ad-METTL14 group. Mice in the Ad-NC group and the Ad-METTL14 group were injected with 5 × 104 TPC-1–8305 C cells transfected with Ad-NC and Ad-METTL14, separately, into the right axilla, followed by continuous feeding for 5 weeks [18, 19]. After the experiment, mice euthanasia was conducted with excess pentobarbital sodium (150 mg/kg) (P3761, Sigma-Aldrich), with their tumor tissues collected and weighed. Some tumor tissues were fixed (24 h) in 4% paraformaldehyde (Solarbio, Beijing, China), dehydrated with alcohol, cleared with xylene, paraffin-embedded, sectioned (5 μm), dewaxed, rehydrated, and then used for immunohistochemistry. The remaining tumor tissues were used for WB analysis.

Immunohistochemistry

Tissues were paraffin-embedded and repaired (2 min) at high temperature and high pressure. After blocking (room temperature, 20 min) in goat serum (SL038, Solarbio), the sections were incubated (4 °C, overnight) with anti-Ki67 (ab15580, 1:200, Abcam), anti-METTL14 (ab220030, 1:50, Abcam) or anti-RAD21 (ab217678, 1:100, Abcam), followed by incubation (37 °C, 60 min) with HRP-labeled goat anti-rabbit antibody IgG H&L (ab214050, 1:1000, Abcam). After that, the sections were stained with diaminobenzidine (DA1016, Solarbio) and counter-stained with hematoxylin (H8070, Solarbio), followed by photographing under a microscope (Olympus, Japan).

Statistical analysis

All data were statistically analyzed and plotted using GraphPad Prism 9.5.0 (GraphPad Software Inc). The Shapiro-Wilk test was used for normal distribution test. All data were normally distributed and expressed as mean ± standard deviation. Comparisons between two groups were analyzed utilizing an independent sample t-test, while those among groups were analyzed using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA), along with Tukey’s post-hoc test. A p-value of less than 0.05 indicated a statistically significant difference.

Results

METTL14 is significantly downregulated in TC

METTL14 expression in TC and adjacent normal tissues was predicted using online TCGA and Starbase databases. The results showed that METTL14 expression was remarkably downregulated in TC cancer tissues (Fig. 1A-B, p < 0.05). Subsequently, METTL14 expression in tumor and adjacent normal tissues from 48 TC patients was investigated using immunohistochemistry, RT-qPCR, and WB. As indicated by the results, METTL14 expression was remarkably decreased in tumor tissues relative to that in adjacent normal tissues (Fig. 1C-E, all p < 0.001). Furthermore, METTL14 expression in TC cell lines (TPC-1 and 8305 C) was detected. According to RT-qPCR and WB analysis results, METTL14 expression was noticeably reduced in TPC-1 and 8305 C cells relative to that in NTHY-ORI 3 − 1 cells (Fig. 1F-G, all p < 0.001). The above findings indicated that METTL14 was significantly downregulated in TC.

Overexpression of METTL14 inhibits the malignant biological behaviors of TC cells

Subsequently, an overexpression model of METTL14 was first established in TC cell lines to evaluate the functional role of METTL14 in TC. The transfection efficiency of METTL14 was verified using RT-qPCR and WB (Fig. 2A-C, both p < 0.01). CCK-8 assay was performed for cell proliferation detection. It was revealed that the Ad-METTL14 group showed drastically reduced cell proliferation compared with the Ad-NC group (Fig. 2D, p < 0.001). According to flow cytometry detection, cell apoptosis was markedly increased in the Ad-METTL14 group relative to that in the Ad-NC group (Fig. 2E, p < 0.001). Transwell assay detection revealed that the migration and invasion abilities of cells were notably inhibited in the Ad-METTL14 group compared with those in the Ad-NC group (Fig. 2F-G, both p < 0.01). From all the above results, METTL14 overexpression inhibited the malignant biological behaviors of TC cells.

METTL14 overexpression downregulates RAD21 mRNA stability and inhibits RAD21 expression through RNA m6A modification

Accumulating studies have provided evidence that the abnormal function of RAD21 is associated with various cancers and is related to malignant progression and poor prognosis [15, 16]. However, whether the genomic changes of RAD21 affect TC occurrence and development remains to be explored. RAD21 expression in cancer tissues and corresponding adjacent normal tissues of TC patients was detected using immunohistochemistry, RT-qPCR, and WB. It was indicated that RAD21 expression was noticeably increased in the tumor group compared that in adjacent normal tissues (Fig. 3A-C, p < 0.01). Moreover, there was a significant negative correlation between METTL14 and RAD21 mRNA expression (p = 0.0058) (Fig. 3D). Furthermore, RAD21 expression in NTHY-ORI 3 − 1 cells and TC cells TPC-1 and 8305 C was detected using RT-qPCR and WB. As indicated by the results, TC cell lines showed notably increased RAD21 expression relative to NTHY-ORI 3 − 1 cells (Fig. 3E-F, all p < 0.001). As indicated by MeRIP results, the m6A modification level was markedly elevated in TC tissues (Fig. 3G, p < 0.01).

Subsequently, SRAMP was employed to predict m6A modification sites of RAD21 to determine whether the upregulation of RAD21 in TC cell lines was associated with m6A modification. The results revealed enriched m6A peaks on the RAD21 sequence (Fig. 4A). As has been evidenced previously, METTL14, a core component of the m6A methyltransferase complex, is crucial for the m6A catalytic process [20]. Firstly, the METTL14-RAD21 interaction was confirmed in TPC-1 and 8305 C cells through RIP experiments (Fig. 4B, p < 0.01). After that, MeRIP detection demonstrated that RAD21 m6A modification level was remarkably increased in TC cell lines with METTL14 overexpression (Fig. 4C, p < 0.01). Taken together, METTL14 overexpression downregulated the stability of RAD21 mRNA and inhibited RAD21 expression through RNA m6A modification.

Upregulation of RAD21 partially reverses the Inhibition of METTL14 overexpression on TC cell malignant behaviors

Next, TPC-1 and 8305 C cells were transfected with Ad-METTL14, Ad-RAD21, or their negative control Ad-NC to further verify that METTL14 inhibited TC cell malignant behaviors by downregulating RAD21. RT-qPCR and WB verified the transfection efficiency (Fig. 5A-C, both p < 0.05). Subsequently, the effect of overexpression of RAD21 and METTL14 on TC cell malignant behaviors was further investigated. CCK-8 assay results revealed that RAD21 overexpression promoted the cell proliferation inhibited by METTL14 overexpression (Fig. 5D, p < 0.01). According to flow cytometry findings, RAD21 upregulation could reverse the increased cell apoptosis by METTL14 overexpression (Fig. 5E-F, p < 0.01). Transwell assay disclosed that overexpression of METTL14 notably reduced TC cell migration and invasion ability, which could be partially reversed by RAD21 upregulation (Fig. 5G-J, all p < 0.01). Taken together, RAD21 overexpression partially reversed the inhibition of METTL14 overexpression on TC cell malignant behaviors.

Overexpression of METTL14 suppresses tumor formation in TC mice

Finally, after transfection with Ad-NC or Ad-METTL14 lentivirus, TC cells (TPC-1–8305 C) were injected subcutaneously into nude mice for tumor formation assay. In short, the mouse tumor was isolated and weighed. The Ad-METTL14 group showed remarkably reduced tumor volume and weight compared with the Ad-NC group (Fig. 6A-C, both p < 0.001). METTL14 and RAD21 protein levels were detected using WB, which demonstrated that nude mice in the Ad-METTL14 group showed markedly elevated METTL14 protein levels and reduced RAD21 protein levels relative to those in the Ad-NC group (Fig. 6D-E, both p < 0.01). Additionally, Ki67 immunohistochemical staining was implemented to detect the proliferation of tumor cells in vivo. As indicated by the results, nude mice with Ad-METTL14 had notably reduced Ki67-positive cell levels (Fig. 6F, p < 0.01). Taken together, overexpression of METTL14 inhibited tumorigenesis in TC mice.

Discussion

TC is a prevalent endocrine tumor with high rates of morbidity and mortality [21, 22]. METTL14 plays a part in tumorigenesis and tumor progression and is dysregulated in various cancers [23–25]. Moreover, m6A misregulation is tightly implicated in multiple human cancers [26]. The present study demonstrated that METTL14 mediated m6A modification of RAD21 to reduce RAD21 mRNA stability, thereby inhibiting TC occurrence and progression.

First, through online prediction using TCGA and Starbase databases, METTL14 expression was found abnormally downregulated in TC tissues. METTL14 was further confirmed as poorly expressed in tumor tissues of 48 TC patients and TC cell lines. Consistently, accumulating studies have provided evidence for the aberrant downregulation of METTL14 in diverse disorders, such as colorectal cancer [27] and cervical cancer [25]. METTL14 has also been found decreased in TC tissues and cells [28]. Additionally, this study proved that overexpression of METTL14 suppressed TC cell malignant biological behaviors, as manifested by drastically reduced TC cell proliferation, migration, and invasion, as well as increased cell apoptosis. Furthermore, the in-vivo experiment results also demonstrated that overexpression of METTL14 notably reduced tumor volume and weight in nude mice, along with remarkably reduced levels of Ki67-positive cells in the tumor tissues. Previous studies have reported consistent results. For example, METTL14 regulates tumor proliferation, metastasis, and self-renewal [23]. METTL14 upregulation induces TC cell apoptosis and hampers TC cell proliferation in vitro and tumor growth in vivo [28].

It is well-established that as a key component of the m6A methyltransferase complex, METTL14 can catalyze m6A methylation on mRNA to regulate gene expression [9, 29]. m6A is the most abundant RNA modification that participates in multiple biological functions, with its misregulation associated with various cancers [9]. m6A modification can affect the stability, degradation, and cellular interaction of the modified RNA [26]. Moreover, m6A RNA methylation has been shown to widely occur in TC cell models [30]. Therefore, METTL14 may mediate m6A modification to exert effects on TC.

RAD21 is a pivotal gene encoding a DNA DSB repair protein [14], which is recurrently mutated in human cancers [31]. Somatic mutations and changes in RAD21 expression are common in various cancers [32]. Therefore, RAD21 expression was detected, and it was found notably unregulated in TC tissues and cells. Similarly, RAD21 upregulation has also been evidenced in various cancers, such as ovarian cancer [33] and non-small cell lung cancer [34]. RAD21 mutations are involved in pneumonia-induced thyroid crisis with thyrotoxicosis exacerbation [35]. Nevertheless, whether the genomic changes of RAD21 affected TC progression remained to be explored. Moreover, the present study demonstrated that METTL14 was negatively correlated with RAD21 mRNA expression, and upregulation of RAD21 partially reversed the inhibition of METTL14 overexpression on TC cell malignant behaviors, resulting in increased TC cell proliferation, migration, and invasion, as well as decreased tumor cell apoptosis. These findings indicate that there are other downstream targets of METTL14-m6A, such as suppressor of cytokine signaling 3 (SOCS3) [28] and RAD21 is not the only downstream target. As has been evidenced previously, RAD21 overexpression facilitated ovarian cancer cell proliferation, migration, and invasion [33]. RAD21 upregulation intensified the malignant behaviors of liver cancer cells, causing increased tumor cell migration, invasion, and proliferation [36].

A previous study has reported that METTL14-mediated m6A modification upregulates SOCS3 expression and regulates TC progression [28]. Therefore, similarly, METTL14 may mediate m6A modification of RAD21 to regulate RAD21 mRNA expression in TC. To determine whether RAD21 upregulation in TC cell lines was associated with m6A modification, SRAMP was applied to predict potential m6A modification sites in RAD21. The results revealed enriched m6A peaks on the RAD21 sequence. METTL14 overexpression remarkably increased the m6A modification level of RAD21, inhibited RAD21 mRNA expression, and reduced its mRNA stability. Taken together, METTL14 overexpression reduced RAD21 mRNA stability and inhibited RAD21 expression through m6A modification.

In summary, the present study reveals that YTHDC2 recognizes METTL14 to play a key role in the proliferation, invasion and metastasis of TC cells by mediating the m6A modification of RAD21. Based on this mechanism, it can be hypothesized that drugs targeting this mechanism may have potential anticancer activity, and the subsequent further study through cell experiments and animal models is expected to develop novel anticancer drugs. At the same time, this mechanism is associated with iodine (131) therapy and the sensitivity of specific targeted drugs [37], which can help to promote the development of individualized treatment for TC. METTL14, as a m6A methyltransferase, whose loss of function is a key driving event in thyroid cancer. Subcutaneous injection of Ad-METTL14 lentivirus inhibits TC tumor growth. This suggests that METTL14 agonists may be novel anticancer agents with therapeutic potential especially for advanced or drug-resistant TC. However, there are some limitations in this study. Only 48 patients were enrolled in this study, and the sample size may not be large enough to reflect a comprehensive situation. Future studies should validate these results with large-scale samples to improve the accuracy and credibility of our findings.

Abstract
We report on a search for weakly interacting massive particle (WIMP) dark matter (DM) via elastic DM-xenon-nucleus interactions in the XENONnT experiment. We combine datasets from the first and second science campaigns resulting in a total exposure of 3.1 ​ tonne × year 3.1\;\text{tonne}\times\text{year} . In a blind analysis of nuclear recoil events with energies above 3.8 ​ keV NR 3.8\,\mathrm{keV_{NR}} , we find no significant excess above background. We set new upper limits on the spin-independent WIMP-nucleon scattering cross-section for WIMP masses above 10 GeV / c 2 \mathrm{GeV}/c^{2} with a minimum of 1.7 × 10 − 47 ​ cm 2 1.7\,\times\,10^{-47}\,\mathrm{cm^{2}} at 90 % confidence level for a WIMP mass of 30 30 GeV / c 2 \mathrm{GeV}/c^{2} . We achieve a best median sensitivity of 1.4 × 10 − 47 ​ cm 2 1.4\,\times\,10^{-47}\,\mathrm{cm^{2}} for a 41 41 GeV / c 2 \mathrm{GeV}/c^{2} WIMP. Compared to the result from the first XENONnT science dataset, we improve our sensitivity by a factor of up to 1.8.



Introduction —

Observational evidence from galactic to cosmic scales indicates the existence of massive, non-baryonic dark matter (DM) in the Universe
Bertone et al. 2005
.
Among numerous models of DM, weakly interacting massive particles (WIMPs) in the mass range between
\mathrm{GeV}/c^{2}
and a few
\mathrm{TeV}/c^{2}
are one of the most promising and physics-motivated DM candidates, which are naturally predicted by several extensions of the Standard Model
Roszkowski et al. 2018
.
Dual-phase liquid xenon (LXe) time projection chambers (TPCs) are currently the most sensitive experiments directly searching for these particles.
They have placed stringent upper limits on cross-sections for elastic spin-independent (SI) WIMP-nucleon interactions
Aprile et al. 2023
;
Aalbers et al. 2023a
;
Bo et al. 2025
.

The XENONnT
Aprile et al. 2024a
experiment is operated underground at the INFN Laboratori Nazionali del Gran Sasso (LNGS).
The experiment consists of three nested detectors: the central liquid xenon (LXe) TPC housed in a cryostat is enclosed by a neutron veto (NV) detector
Aprile et al. 2024b
which is situated within, but optically separated from, a muon veto (MV) detector
Aprile et al. 2014
.
Both veto detectors are inside a 700-tonne water tank and function as water Cherenkov detectors.
All data used in this work were acquired with demineralized water, relying on neutron capture on hydrogen as in
Aprile et al. 2023
.

The cylindrical TPC is immersed in
8.5\text{\,}\mathrm{t}
of LXe, with gaseous xenon (GXe) on top.
Particle interactions in the LXe lead to prompt scintillation light as well as ionization electrons.
The light is detected by arrays of photomultiplier tubes (PMTs) at both ends of the cylinder.
Electrons are moved towards the liquid surface by an electric drift field, where a stronger extraction field accelerates the electrons into the GXe, leading to a drift-delayed proportional scintillation signal.
The measured prompt and delayed light signals are denoted S1 and S2, respectively.
The S1–S2 combination allows for energy and 3D-position reconstruction.
Compared to electronic recoils (ERs), mainly expected from backgrounds, nuclear recoils (NRs) from WIMP scattering feature smaller S2/S1 ratios, allowing for ER/NR discrimination
Aprile et al. 2006
.

The sensitive volume of the detector has a diameter of
1.33\text{\,}\mathrm{m}
, a maximum electron drift length of
1.49\text{\,}\mathrm{m}
, and contains
5.9\text{\,}\mathrm{t}
of LXe.
All detector construction materials were selected for low radioactivity
Aprile et al. 2021
.
The walls are made of polytetrafluoroethylene (PTFE) and cover the inside of the electric field cage
Aprile et al. 2024c
.
A drift field of
23\text{\,}\mathrm{V}
is established between a cathode electrode at the bottom of the active volume and a gate electrode just below the LXe surface.
The extraction field is set between the gate and the anode electrode in the gas phase (
2.9\text{\,}\mathrm{kV}
in the liquid).
All electrodes are composed of parallel wires.
Two (four) transverse wires support the gate (anode) wires against sagging.
Additional parallel-wire screening electrodes protect the PMT arrays, which contain 494 Hamamatsu R11410-21
3"
PMTs
Antochi et al. 2021
.
PMT pulses above predefined digitization thresholds are recorded with a triggerless data acquisition (DAQ) system
Aprile et al. 2022a
, stored, and further processed using the software
strax(en)

Aalbers et al. 2023b
;
XENON Collaboration 2022a
.
Electronegative impurities that affect electron drift, and radon emanating from surfaces are continuously removed from xenon via gas+liquid purification and online distillation, respectively
Aprile et al. 2024d
;
Plante et al. 2022
;
Murra et al. 2022
;
Aprile et al. 2024a
.

{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 85$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Kr}
was removed via cryogenic distillation as well at the start of the experiment.

Signal and background models —

This analysis accounts for backgrounds from ER, NR, AC, and surface events.
An internal

Statistical inference —

For the statistical analysis of the dataset, we used a log-likelihood-based test statistic with the distributions obtained via toy-MC simulations, as recommended in
Baxter et al. 2021
and detailed in
Aprile et al. 2024f
.
The computations were performed with the
alea
framework
XENON Collaboration 2024e
.
The likelihood function
\mathcal{L}(\sigma,\boldsymbol{\theta})
depends on the WIMP-nucleon cross-section
\sigma\geq 0
, which is the parameter of interest, and a set of nuisance parameters
\boldsymbol{\theta}
, written as the product of three terms,

\mathcal{L}(\sigma,\boldsymbol{\theta})=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sci}}(\sigma,\boldsymbol{\theta})\times\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{cal}}(\boldsymbol{\theta})\times\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{anc}}(\boldsymbol{\theta}).

The term
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sci}}
is a product of six terms separating SR0,
\mathrm{SR1a}
, and
\mathrm{SR1b}
, as well as the near-wire and far-wire regions.
All six terms are extended unbinned likelihood functions, which model the data in (
\mathrm{cS1}
,
\mathrm{cS2}
,
\mathrm{R}
) for the far-wire region and in (
\mathrm{cS1}
,
\mathrm{cS2}
) for the near-wire region.

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{cal}}
is a product over two unbinned likelihood functions in (
\mathrm{cS1}
,
\mathrm{cS2}
) modeling the ER calibration datasets in SR0 and SR1, and
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{anc}}(\boldsymbol{\theta})
is a product of Gaussian constraint terms for nuisance parameters from ancillary measurements.
The background and signal components are listed in
Table

1
.
Apart from the background expectation values, the set of nuisance parameters comprises the WIMP-mass-dependent relative signal efficiency and four ER shape parameters (two for each SR) that modify the shape in (
\mathrm{cS1}
,
\mathrm{cS2}
) of the different ER components.
These parameters are tightly constrained via the term
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{cal}}
.
The relative signal rate uncertainty is 15 % (6 %, 4 %) in SR0 (
\mathrm{SR1a}
,
\mathrm{SR1b}
) for WIMP masses above
\sim
100
\mathrm{GeV}/c^{2}
, and becomes larger for smaller masses.
The rate uncertainty in SR1 is smaller than in SR0 due to a smaller selection efficiency uncertainty.

We employed power-constrained limits (PCL)
Cowan et al. 2011
;
Baxter et al. 2021
to prevent excluding regions of parameter space where our sensitivity is low, which could otherwise occur due to statistical fluctuations or systematic effects.
In
Aprile et al. 2023
, a conservative power threshold of 0.5 was chosen after identifying an error in the definition of power in
Baxter et al. 2021
, effectively truncating the upper limits at the median of the sensitivity band.
We have investigated the PCL behavior with toy data, specifically in scenarios involving a shift in the ER event distribution, increased background rates, or increased background uncertainties.
These studies revealed no issues that would disqualify a lower power threshold of 0.16.
The corresponding truncation of the limits at the
-1\sigma
quantile of the sensitivity band allows for a direct comparison with other experiments
Bo et al. 2025
;
Aalbers et al. 2023a
.

The SR1 signal region unblinding was performed in two steps.
First, events in a small region above the median of the NR event distribution with energies above
5\,\mathrm{keV_{ER}}
were unblinded, containing about 7.5 % of expected events from a
1\,\mathrm{TeV}/c^{2}
WIMP signal.
This initial step allowed us to investigate potential excessive downward leakage of ER events, as previously observed in
Aprile et al. 2023
.
The results of the first unblinding step showed no discrepancy with the nominal model.
In the second step, all data in the ROI were unblinded.
The regions in cS1-cS2 are indicated in
Fig.

S1
in the supplemental material.

Results —

After unblinding, we observed 560 events in
\mathrm{SR1a}
and 245 in
\mathrm{SR1b}
within the ROI, of which 14 and 13, respectively, lie in the previously blinded region (with two additional events in
\mathrm{SR1b}
, which were already unblinded in
Aprile et al. 2024g
).
The distribution of all events in (
\mathrm{cS1}
,
\mathrm{cS2}
) is shown in
Fig.

2
, the corresponding plot for SR0 is Figure 3 in
Aprile et al. 2023
.

Table

1
shows the best-fit expectation values for all SRs.
We performed independent GOF tests on
\mathrm{SR1a}
and
\mathrm{SR1b}
data.
The tests were defined before unblinding with p-value thresholds of 2.5 % to reject the best-fit model.
An unbinned Anderson-Darling (AD) test
Anderson and Darling 1952
was performed in the
\mathrm{cS2}
dimension, after subtracting the
\mathrm{cS1}
-dependent median of the best-fit model.
The data and best-fit models in this space are visualized in
Fig.

3
.
Additionally, we performed binned Poisson
\chi^{2}
tests using an equiprobable binning scheme in
\mathrm{cS1}
–
\mathrm{cS2}
.
We found no indication of mismodeling in any of the tests, with p-values of 0.34 (
\mathrm{SR1a}
) and 0.85 (
\mathrm{SR1b}
) for the AD test, and 0.33 (same for
\mathrm{SR1a}
and
\mathrm{SR1b}
) for the Poisson
\chi^{2}
test.
We also performed an
\mathrm{XY}
-plane spatial uniformity test of the unblinded events in SR1.
It is quantified by the fraction of events in the densest quarter and densest half of the
\mathrm{XY}
plane.
We found no indication of a spatial asymmetry for SR1.

The local WIMP discovery significance was evaluated for WIMP masses between 10
\mathrm{GeV}/c^{2}
and 1
\mathrm{TeV}/c^{2}
.
We found no significant excess above backgrounds with the lowest p-value of 0.13 for a WIMP mass of 1
\mathrm{TeV}/c^{2}
.
We thus report the WIMP-mass-dependent upper limits of the SI WIMP-nucleon cross-section at 90 % confidence level (C.L.), shown in
Fig.

4
together with the sensitivity band.
As we observed no limit below the
-1\,\sigma
sensitivity band, no adjustment is needed to satisfy the power constraint requirement.
The most stringent limit on the cross-section is
1.7\,\times\,10^{-47}\,\mathrm{cm^{2}}
for a WIMP mass of 30
\mathrm{GeV}/c^{2}
.
For WIMP masses above
\sim 200

\mathrm{GeV}/c^{2}
the limit scales like
M_{\mathrm{WIMP}}/(1\,\mathrm{TeV}/c^{2})\,\times\,3.7\,\times\,10^{-46}\,\mathrm{cm^{2}}
.
The supplemental material includes the limit expressed in terms of the number of WIMP events and the SR1-only result, as well as the limits for spin-dependent (SD) WIMP-nucleon coupling.

Summary and outlook —

In summary, we have performed a blind analysis of a combined SR0+SR1 dataset from XENONnT with a total of 186.5 (95.1) days of livetime in SR1 (SR0), resulting in a total exposure of
3.1\;\text{tonne}\times\text{year}
.
We found no significant excess above background and placed new limits on the SI WIMP-nucleon interaction cross-section, with an improvement of approximately a factor of 1.5 for WIMP masses above 30
\mathrm{GeV}/c^{2}
compared to the SR0-only results.
Running the radon distillation system at its full capacity, we have significantly reduced our
{}^{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 222$}}_{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Rn}
concentration by a factor 1.9 (1.7) in SR1a (SR1b), resulting in a record-low ER background from
{}^{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 214$}}_{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Pb}

\upbeta
-decays.

The experiment continues to take data, with an increased NV tagging efficiency due to 0.05 % by-weight gadolinium loading, giving an expected factor
\sim
2 reduction of the neutron background.
The recent installation of a charcoal purifier shows promise in removing photoionizing impurities, which is expected to result in a lower AC background rate.

A.1
Charge yield of
{}^{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 124$}}_{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Xe}
double-electron capture events

The
2\upnu\mathrm{ECEC}
of
{}^{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 124$}}_{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Xe}
contributes to the ER background in the WIMP ROI with an expected number of (
4.5\pm 0.7
) events in SR0 and (
9.1\pm 1.4
) events in SR1 from the LM + LN (
\sim 6\text{\,}\mathrm{keV}
) and LL (
\sim 10\text{\,}\mathrm{keV}
) peaks.
The event rate was constrained via the KK and KL-shell capture peaks outside the ROI, using the branching fractions 72.4 % (KK), 20.0 % (KL), 1.4 % (LL), and 0.7 % (LM + LN)
Aprile et al. 2022c
.
The de-excitation cascade following the capture can lead to an increased ionization density, which may result in a higher recombination probability and thus lower
\mathrm{CY}
compared to
\upbeta
-decay events.
A charge suppression factor of
\sim 0.9
was measured for L-shell electron capture in
{}^{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 127$}}_{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Xe}
(
\sim 5.2\text{\,}\mathrm{keV}
) at drift fields above 250 V/cm
Temples et al. 2021
,
but no such measurement exists for
2\upnu\mathrm{ECEC}
at the 23 V/cm field used in this search.

Introducing reduced charge yields as nuisance parameters could bias upper limits by absorbing leakage from other ER backgrounds, artificially lowering them.
Thus, we first tested the hypothesis of a reduced
\mathrm{CY}
for the
2\upnu\mathrm{ECEC}
peaks at 23 V/cm using SR1 science data.
For this, an alternative likelihood function was defined, modeling the
2\upnu\mathrm{ECEC}
peaks as separate components with
r_{\mathrm{LL}}=\mathrm{\mathrm{CY}_{LL}}/\mathrm{\mathrm{CY}}_{\upbeta}
and
r_{\mathrm{LM}}=\mathrm{\mathrm{CY}_{LM}}/\mathrm{\mathrm{CY}}_{\upbeta}
as free-floating parameters, without ancillary constraints.

A likelihood-ratio test compared the nominal
\mathrm{CY}
hypothesis (
r_{\mathrm{LL}}=r_{\mathrm{LM}}=1
) against an unconstrained alternative.
A test size of
\upalpha=5\,\%
was chosen to limit the rate of false WIMP discovery for several scenarios with reduced
2\upnu\mathrm{ECEC}
charge yields when not modeled appropriately (
>3\,\sigma
false WIMP discovery rate below 0.5 %).
After unblinding the data, a p-value of 0.09 was observed, so the nominal
\mathrm{CY}
hypothesis was not rejected.
As a result, the nominal model without charge yield suppression, as described in the main text, was used for the WIMP search.
The best-fit model using only SR1 science data is illustrated in
Fig.

S1
, yielding
r_{\mathrm{LL}}\approx 0.9
and
r_{\mathrm{LM}}\approx 0.8
.
The best-fit results using the combined SR0+SR1 dataset are
r_{\mathrm{LL}}=0.80^{+0.08}_{-0.04}
and
r_{\mathrm{LM}}=0.72^{+0.11}_{-0.04}
.

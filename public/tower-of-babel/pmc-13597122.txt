Integrative scRNA-seq and scTCR-seq analyses decode dual TCR γδ T-cell characteristics in human and mouse
Authors: Huifang Wang, Yuanyuan Xu, Naixin Cao, Qingqing Ma, Jun Li, Kai Quan, Xinsheng Yao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe clonal selection theory of “one lymphocyte expresses one type of antigen receptor” has been challenged and complemented by the concept of dual receptor lymphocytes. However, the proportion, source, and characterization of dual TCR γδ T cells have not been determined. Using single-cell RNA plus single-cell TCR sequencing, we discovered a high proportion of dual γδ T cells datasets of the human thymus and mouse spleens. We observed differences in the proportion of single and dual TCR from γδ T cells, the types of δ and γ pairings, the frequency of TRDV and TRGV genes, and the expression of mRNA molecules across various γδ T-cell subgroups. Additionally, we investigated the likely genesis of dual TCR mRNA based on its compositional properties. This work provides a new perspective and scheme for the study of γδ T cells.Keywords: allelic inclusion (exclusion escape), CDR3 repertoire, dual TCRs, γδ T cell, single-cell RNA + TCR sequencing

Introduction

The clone selection theory that “one lymphocyte expresses one type of antigen receptor” was challenged and complemented by dual receptor lymphocytes.1 Lymphocytes expressing dual BCRs were first reported in 1961,2 and αβ T cells expressing dual TCRs were documented by several laboratories in 1988.3,4 Simultaneously, several laboratories conducted early studies on γδ T cells with dual γ chains.5–8 After these initial investigations, Franqois et al. were the first to identify T cells in human PBLs that coexpressed the γ chains of V9 and V4, using γ chain mAb flow cytometry (FCM) technology. These γ chains could pair with δ chains of V1, V2, V3, and V5.9 In 1995, Peyrat et al., also using δ chain mAb FCM technology, were the first to identify lymphocytes coexpressing the δ chain of V1 and V3 in the PBLs of healthy individuals.10 Orsini et al. identified lymphocytes expressing dual receptors, with γ chain of V4+/V9+ and δ chain of V1+, which may be associated with superantigen responses derived from Staphylococcus aureus.11 In 1999, Taupin et al.,12 using δ chain Ab FCM technology, identified functionally T cells expressing dual δ chains in the PBLs of patients with HIV. These dual δ chain T cells were primarily found in activated γδ T cells, suggesting their functional role in response to peripheral Ag stimulation.12 Hinz et al. found that chronic lymphocytosis has dual TCR T cells expressing Vγ4/Vγ5/Vδ1.13 Weidmann et al. found that γδ T-cell lymphoma has dual TCR T cells expressing Vγ3/Vγ9/Vδ1.14 To date, studies have only identified the existence of dual receptor γδ T cells. The proportions of dual receptor γδ T cells in physiological and pathological states, their potential origin mechanisms, and their specific response effects have not been elucidated.

Although dual receptor lymphocytes have gained extensive experimental support, research into their mechanisms has been limited by technology and model constraints.15,16 Single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) plus single-cell TCR sequencing (scRNA + scTCR-seq) has brought revolutionary breakthroughs in analyzing variable–diversity–joining (V(D)J) recombination, mRNA expression, and peptide-chain pairing in individual T and B cells. We have preliminarily applied this technology to analyze the proportions and mechanisms of dual BCRs and dual αβ TCRs.17–19 In this study, we analyzed the scRNA + scTCR-seq results of γδ T cells shared by Sanchez Sanchez et al.20 and Hahn et al.,21 and explored the proportion, source, mechanism, and characteristic mRNA expression of dual TCR γ δ T cells at the single-cell level. This is, to our knowledge, the first comprehensive analysis conducted on human samples, wild-type (WT) mice, and transgenic mice with a specific TCR δ gene. We examined the transcriptome and immune repertoire of dual TCR γδ T cells in the fetal thymus (FT), pediatric thymus (PNT), spleen of WT mice, and the spleen of TRDV6 – δ chain transgenic mice (KI).

γδ T cells exhibit unique special recombination and output waves during their differentiation and development.22 Their functions lie between innate and adaptive immune responses.23,24 The TRG and TRD loci in human and mouse exhibit unique arrangements of V(D)J gene clusters. This results in the simultaneous occurrence of forward looping recombination and reverse inversion recombination of V(D)J on a single chromosome.25,26 The analysis conducted in this study of the proportion, characteristics, and potential mechanisms of dual TCR γδ T cells provides novel insights and approaches for studying new mechanisms of innate and adaptive T-cell immune responses.

Materials and methods

Single-cell sequencing datasets of T cells

The scRNA-seq and scTCR-seq datasets of T cells were downloaded from the National Center for Biotechnology Information and Gene Expression Omnibus, including 2 different T-cell categories of human thymus (FT and PNT) (accession no. GSE180059) and T cells from 2 WT mouse spleen and 2 KI mouse spleen (accession no. GSE225036). The TCR-δ transgenic mouse model (B6.129P2-Tcrdtm1Mom-Ptprca/TCRd-KI) was constructed using CRISPR-Cas9 technology to target the endogenous Tcrd locus in embryonic stem cells, replacing the native D and J segments with a pre-rearranged VDJ cassette composed of TRAV15D-1 (Vδ6.2) linked to TRDD2 and TRDJ1 segments. Among them, human T cells were derived from FT (n = 6) from fetal thymus aged 14 to 22 wk and pediatric thymus (n = 3) from 4 to 11 yr old. Mouse T cells were derived from wild mice (n = 2) during 10 weeks and KI mice (n = 2) during 6–16 weeks. They were divided into the FT, PNT, WT, and KI groups for comparison and statistics. Screening of T cells

Off-machine data of single-cell sequencing may contain nonfunctional T and B cells. Data quality control was performed in Microsoft Office Excel by screening and deleting sequences with the following 4 criteria: sequences in “is_cell” with “FALSE”; sequences in “high_confidence” with “FALSE”; sequences in “chain” that are not “TRD, TRG”; and sequences in “productive” with “None, FALSE.” Based on the number of different types of chains expressed in single T cells, they were divided into 2 groups: 2 types of chains and 3 or more types of chains. Based on the pairing of TRD and TRG chains, single T cells were statistically compared and analyzed in terms of single TCRs and dual TCRs. For example, cells with 2 types of chains, which can pair and assemble into single TCRs, were assembled into the following pattern: δ chain (D) plus γ chain (G) (D + G). Cell with 3 or more chains, which can pair and assemble into dual TCRs, were assembled into the following pattern: D + G1 + G2, G + D1 + D2, D1 + D2 + G1 + G2, others (such as D1 + G1 + G2 + G3).

V Usage of TRD and TRG between single TCR and dual TCR

Based on human and mouse TRG and TRD locus in the IMGT database, we analyzed the frequency of V gene family for TRD and TRG in each sample. The presence of 2 (or more) V gene subfamilies in any of the dual TCRs was counted, and comparative statistical analyses were conducted.

Data analysis process and quality control

scRNA-seq data

We first used R (version 4.2.3) and the Seurat package (version 4.4.0) to analyze the quality control of the scRNA-seq data of 13 samples. Low-quality reads were filtered using fewer than 10 or fewer than 200 features and mitochondrial counts of >10%, and, to exclude doublets, the cutoff for maximal nFeature_RNA was manually set up for each sample according to the distribution of the sample cells. The DoubletFinder package (version 2.0.4) was used to find and eliminate possible doublet cells. For each sample, the principal cells were selected and removed.

Single-cell TCR-seq data

For each sample, according to the unique barcode of each cell, T cells were filtered out that could be analyzed by both scRNA-seq plus single-cell TCR sequencing (scTCR-seq) (the cells that only measured scRNA-seq and failed to measure TCRs or only measured TCRs and failed to measure scRNA-seq were not included in the analysis of the data). Subsequently, the number of T cells paired for single TCRs and dual TCRs in each sample was analyzed, and the proportion of various TCR paired types was calculated (Tables 1 and 2).

Cluster analysis of scRNA-seq data

The quality-controlled single-cell transcriptomic data were analyzed using the R software and the Seurat package versions mentioned earlier. The JackStraw and PCEIbow plotting functions were used for principal component analysis. Selected principal components were then subjected to a Uniform Manifold Approximation and Projection algorithm for dimensionality reduction and clustering analysis, with FT resulting in 10 cell clusters, PNT and WT each resulting in 9 cell clusters, and KI with 5 cell clusters. Based on the varying developmental stages of γδ T cells from the study by Sanchez Sanchez et al.,20 we performed clustering and further explored the distribution characteristics of single and dual TCRs across 4 types of T-cell subgroups. We divided cells from FT into immature (marked by CCR7 and CCR9) and mature cells, which were further classified into type 1, type 2, or type 3, based on gene expression. Type 1 cells (C6), marked by CD27, CD44, and IFNG, are linked to viral responses and TCR signaling. Type 2 cells (C3), characterized by IL4R, CCR4, and ZBTB16, relate to B-cell regulation and humoral immunity. Type 3 cells (C5), defined by IL7R, CCR6, and RORC, are associated with Th17 cells and brown adipocytes. The remaining cells of PNT, WT, and KI were classified into groups based on differences in gene expression. Visualization of T-cell subgroups information was carried out using the ggplot function in R.

Characteristic mRNA expression of single and dual TCRs

For FT, 6 samples were grouped into 10 different subtypes of γδ T cells using the primary cell clustering marker from the original text. Three primary types of mature T cells (types 1, 2, and 3) and other immature or mature T cells were chosen for comparison analysis of single TCRs and dual TCRs based on the features of typical mature T-cell types. For PNT, 3 samples were clustered into 9 different types of T cells using a marker for comparison analysis of single TCRs and dual TCRs. For WT mice, 2 spleen samples were sorted into 9 different categories of T cells using as a marker the primary cluster of cells for comparison analysis of single TCRs and dual TCRs. And for KI mice, 2 spleen samples were grouped into 5 different categories of T lymphocytes, using the primary cluster of cells for comparison analysis of single TCR and dual TCR as the marker.

Statistical analyses

Data are presented as the mean ± SD. IBM SPSS Statistics 26 software was used for statistical analysis. An independent sample t test was used for continuous variable comparison between 2 groups, and a χ2 test was used for classified variable comparison. P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Data visualization was performed using R Studio (version 3.3.3) and GraphPad Prism (version 5) software.

Results

Analysis of single and dual TCR γδ T-cell ratio and V gene frequency

Thirteen samples had cell counts ranging from 795 to 5,531, with complete paired γδ T cell counts between 795 and 2,653 (Fig. 1). Statistical analysis based on each group of biological samples showed there was notable heterogeneity in the proportion of dual TCR γδ T cells and the types of pairing across the 4 groups (FT, PNT, WT, KI). First we analyzed the proportion of 5 pairing types of TCR γδ T cells in each sample. All samples were mainly single TCR pairs of D + G (>90%) (Fig. 1A). The proportions of 5 TCR pairing types in human and mouse were compared for each group. The results were statistically analyzed by mean ± SD, and the results showed that the major pairing type of dual TCR γδ T cells was D + G1 + G2, which is distinct from other types. The secondary pairing type was G + D1 + D2. Both human and mouse exhibit γ allele inclusion rearrangements (D + G1 + G2 + G3) and δ allele inclusion rearrangements (eg D1 + D2 + G1 + G2). In KI mice, the major type of TCR rearrangement was the transferred δ (TRDV6) rearrangement. No pairing of G + D1 + D2 or D1 + D2 + G1 + G2 was found (Fig. 1B and C). Based on scRNA + scTCR-seq data, the statistical analysis of the use of V genes in dual TCR γδ T cells showed differences in the use of TRGV3, TRGV4, TRGV8, and TRGV9 in FT and PNT; TRDV1 and TRDV2 in PNT; TRDV4, TRDV5, TRDV12, TRGV1, TRGV5, and TRGV6 in WT; and TRGV1, TRGV6, and TRGV4 in KI when compared with single TCRs (Fig. 1D).

Novel mechanism of dual TCR γδ T-cell rearrangement and transcription expression

Recombinant and transcriptional expression of VDJC with 2 or more δ chains was present in single γδ T cells from both human and mouse (Fig. 2). Constitutive features demonstrate that on a single chromosome, both forward DVx-DDx-DJx rearrangements and reverse DV3 (human)/DV5 (mouse) DDx-DJx rearrangements can occur. Recombinant and transcriptional expression of VJC with 3 or more γ chains, and their constitutive features showed that on a single chromosome, 2 (or more) GVx-GJx rearrangements can occur. Unlike human, in the mouse TRG locus, both forward and reverse arrangements coexist, showing that on a single chromosome in mouse, forward looping out recombination and inversion recombination can occur simultaneously (Fig. 2A). We selected 5 pairing types of TCRs in a single T cell to show in both human and mouse (Fig. 2B).

Distribution of dual TCRs in different subgroups of γδ T cells

Based on single-cell sequencing data, we analyzed the distribution of dual TCRs in different subgroups (Fig. 3). In FT, we analyzed the proportion and distribution of single and dual TCRs in 4 cell types in FT. The immature/maturing subgroup had the highest proportion of dual TCRs, with the primary TCR type being D + G1 + G2. Furthermore, a comparison of the 3 groups demonstrated a progressive increase in the proportion of D + G1 + G2 from type 1 to type 3. Compared with the other 3 groups, the immature/maturing subgroup had the largest proportion of G + D1 + D2 (Fig. 3A). In PNT, the C3 subgroup had the highest proportion of D + G1 + G2, and the C5 subgroup had the highest proportion of G + D1 + D2. Then we compared the total single TCRs and total dual TCRs of each group in PNT and found no difference (Fig. 3B). In WT mice, the C5 subgroup had a significantly high proportion of D + G1 + G2, and the C8 subgroup had the highest proportion of G + D1 + D2. Compared with other subgroups, the C1 subgroup of total dual TCRs differed from other subgroups (Fig. 3C). In KI, it was divided into 5 groups and the proportion of TCR types in each group was counted. There were significant differences in the TRDV6 + G1 + G2 ratio among KI subgroups, and the TCR types (G + D1 + D2/D1 + D2 + G1 + G2) were absent. Finally, when we compared the differences between total single TCRs and total dual TCRs in each group, we found that the C0 subgroup of total dual TCRs differed from other subgroups (Fig. 3D).

Differential analysis of mRNA expression between single and dual TCR γδ T cells

We defined “high expression” as the expression level of certain genes in a single TCR γδ T cell that is significantly higher than that in a dual TCR γδ T cell (Fig. 4). After analyzing the proportion sources of single TCRs and dual TCRs in 4 groups of human and mouse, we first counted the differential gene expression of single and dual TCRs in each group and found that the immature cells in single and dual TCRs of FT expressed similar genes (TCF7 and STMN1), but there was cell heterogeneity in type 2 and type 3 γ δ T cells. Then, counting the total single and dual TCRs of FT, we found that both mainly expressed DDX5 and LIMD2. However, in dual TCRs, the expression of TRGV10, TRDV9, and TRGC1 was higher than that of single TCRs (Fig. 4A), which was consistent with our analysis of VJ use (Fig. 1D).

In PNT, we observed that both single and dual TCRs coexpressed TOX2 in the C2 subgroup, which is associated with regulatory T-cell exhaustion. Similarly, the C7 subgroup coexpressed TUBA1B, H2AF2, and CENPF, which are related to the recruitment and activation of immune cells as well as tumor immune evasion mechanisms. However, we also observed high expression of GNG4, related to the tumor immune microenvironment, in the single TCRs of the C3 subgroup. There were differences in TRGV10, TRDV9, and TRDV2 in PNT total single and dual TCRs, which were consistent with FT total single and dual TCR results (Fig. 4B). In WT, only single and dual TCR high-expression genes in C5 subgroup were basically the same, and there is no obvious difference between total single TCR and total dual TCR (Fig. 4C). In KI, the highly expressed genes of single TCRs and dual TCRs were essentially the same, whereas unique distinctive genes of total single TCRs and dual TCRs were highly expressed, such as Tcrg-C1 and Tcrg-V4, which are associated with tumor immune surveillance (Fig. 4D). Discussion

Since research on dual TCR lymphocytes began in the 1980s, specific experimental evidence has supported their existence. However, the proportions, potential origins, and physiological and pathological roles of dual TCR lymphocytes have remained unclear. This is largely due to limitations in research techniques and models, with most findings coming from special transgenic mice or a few clonal T cells.15,16 However, the potential roles of dual TCR lymphocytes in T-cell tumors or antitumor responses,27,28 autoimmune diseases,29 and transplant rejection have garnered widespread attention.30,31

Based on the revolutionary advantages of scRNA + scTCR-seq in analyzing dual receptor lymphocytes, we analyzed dual γδ TCRs on the basis of the previous analysis of dual BCRs and dual αβ TCRs.17–19 Investigation of different organs of human and mouse, including FT and PNT and the spleen of mouse, demonstrated that γδ T cells expressing dual TCRs are prevalent in the central and peripheral immune systems. We found that approximately 92% of functional γ-chain and δ-chain pairs were single TCRs (D + G), whereas about 8% of human and mouse γδ T cells were dual TCRs. This phenomenon may provide a new perspective for understanding the development and rearrangement mechanisms of γδ T cells, including allele exclusion and allele inclusion rearrangement, and their functions in immune response. The pairing types of dual TCR γδ T cells in human and mouse are mainly D + G1 + G2, indicating that the TCR δ chain undergoes allele exclusion and rearrangement. However, 2 types of V-J rearrangement (allele inclusion) of the TCR γ chain can be selected for pairing, which is consistent with the dual TCR aβ T cell mainly pairing type of B (β chain) plus A1 (α chain) plus A2 (α chain).17 It also supports our prior observation that dual BCR B cells are primarily composed of H (heavy chain), K1 (κ chain), and K2 (κ chain).18 Most TCR β and δ chains and BCR heavy chains are thought to follow allele exclusion in recombination, although TCR α chains, γ chains and BCR light chains (κ and λ) can be rearranged with allele inclusion and engage in TCR/BCR dimer pairing many times. Secondly, our data indicate that the use of specific Vδ genes (eg human TRDV3 and mouse TRDV4) in dual TCR cells is biased, which may further influence this outcome, due to the impact of endogenous Ag selection. Certain Vδ chains, including Vδ1, interact with endogenous autoantigens, like BTN2A1.32 It is positively selected in thymus growth and peripheral maintenance. The retention of a strong δ chain results in a pairing mode dominated by D + G1 + G2. Based on the aforementioned 2 elements, the frequent assembly of out-of-frame rearrangement, in the absence of other regulatory mechanisms, stipulates that biallelic expression of any TCR or Ig gene should occur in 20% of lymphocytes.33,34 The experimental results from many model mice, however, do not support the theory, and the random model and feedback model of allele exclusion rearrangement have always been extremely different.35–37 The advent of scRNA + TCR/BCR-seq combined application technology has provided an opportunity to study the proportion, mechanism, and function of dual receptor lymphocytes. According to the composition characteristics of the VDJ gene in dual TCR γδ T cells in 13 samples and the position sequence of VDJ in TRD and TRG loci of human and mouse, we found that on a single chromosome of the TRD locus of human and mouse, when the only inverted TRDV3 (human) or TRDV5 (mouse) is functionally rearranged first, the homonymous DVx gene can be rearranged. Theoretically, the δ chain (male parent and female parent) of a single γδ T cell can undergo 4 (or more) inclusive rearrangements of VDJ. We also have observed that a single γδ T cell can exhibit 3 (or more) functional rearrangements of TRGV and TRGJ. Using mouse as an example, the potential mechanism could be illustrated as follows: there are 4 independent VJC gene clusters on the mouse TRG locus, which can simultaneously undergo forward looping recombination and inversion recombination due to the presence of both forward and reverse arrangements. Therefore, 2 (or more) different types of VJC rearrangements can be retained on the same chromosome and transcribed for expression.25 Another possible hypothetical mechanism is that after 2 rounds of V-J recombination on a single chromosome, 1 V-J mRNA may originate from the rearrangement excision loop.

γδ T cells have “special recombination and output waves,”22 as well as “innate and adaptive” immune response receptors,23 although the precise mechanism is unknown. Although it has been discovered that the V gene family of human and mouse T cells is related to particular Ags (eg mouse Vγ5Vδ1 and keratinocytes and SKINT-1; human Vγ9Vδ2 and phosphoantigens, amino-bisphonates, and alkylamines; human Vδ1 being related to CD1-sulfatide), these associations are still in the early stages. However, the period during which these T-cell V genes participate in rearrangement and the mechanism for settlement in various body regions (eg mucosal epithelia, skin dermis, lymph nodes, spleen, lung) are not clarified.38 We compared and examined the frequency of V gene use, the distribution and proportion of paired TCRs in different subgroups, and the differences in transcription gene expression to further investigate the differences between single and dual TCR γδ T cells. Human dual TCR γδ T cells were found at a relatively high frequency in TRDV3, implying that the inverted recombination mode of reverse TRDV3 was crucial in δ allele inclusion rearrangement. Peyrat et al. discovered that in human PBLs, δ chain V3+ lymphocytes can be recognized by TCR Cβ Abs, indicating the presence of δ chain V3-Jα-Cα transcripts. This suggests that the reverse inversion recombination mode of TRDV3 might play a significant role in the inclusive rearrangement of δ chains in human.10 Mouse dual TCR γδ T cells were present at a relatively high frequency of TRDV4 among all V genes examined. This may be attributed to the shortest distance to TRDV4, which potentially resulting in a higher frequency of participation in allelic inclusion rearrangement.39 Simultaneously, the remarkably high frequency of human TRGV10, mouse TRGV6, and TRGV4 access in dual TCR T cells allows us to investigate the various mechanisms of their involvement in allelic inclusion rearrangement or escape from tolerance selection. For example, human TRGV10, defined as an open reading frame,26,40 is frequently used in dual γ chain γδ T cells, possibly due to its participation in recombination without producing functional protein that allows for subsequent rearrangements of other TRGV genes. This offers a new method to study secondary rearrangement mechanisms of open reading frames or pseudogenes.

The distribution of human and mouse dual TCR γδ T cells in different subgroups is essentially the same, but γδ T cells from different subgroups showed heterogeneity in the pairing type of dual TCRs. We discovered that the proportion and mechanism of dual TCR γδ T cells in human thymus samples and mouse spleen samples are related to the use of specific V gene families at different stages in different organs. For instance, T cells that are exported to different tissues for settlement exploit specific V genes such as human and mouse Vγ9, Vδ2, Vγ5, and Vγ4. In KI, the majority of the paired forms of dual TCRs are TRDV6 + G1 + G2, and the proportion of T cells in the 5 subgroups differs significantly. Additionally, in analyzing the 4 subgroups of FT, we found that the immature/maturing subgroup had the highest proportion of dual TCRs, whereas type 1 cells had the lowest proportion of dual TCRs. The difference in dual TCR proportions between the subgroups with the highest and lowest proportions may reflect specific mechanisms and functions of different types of γδ T cells in immune regulation. Immature γδ T cells typically exhibit higher dual TCR expression, suggesting that, like αβ T cells,17 γδ T cells are more likely to express 2 different TCRs at the immature stage, mainly pairing types of γ chains that do not undergo strict allelic exclusion during recombination. In contrast, type 1 effector cells tend to have a higher proportion of single TCRs, reflecting their precise recognition and efficient response to specific Ags. This specificity in single TCR recognition is likely related to the particular functions of type 1 effector cells, such as the clearance of specific pathogens. Given that these cells predominantly express hallmark factors like IFNG and TBX21 (Tbet), they likely play a crucial role in immune responses against pathogens.

Many researchers have studied the potential function of dual TCR γδ T cells. Orsini et al. found that lymphocytes expressing dual receptors with γ chain V4+ V++ and δ chain V1+ may be associated with superantigen responses derived from Staphylococcus aureus.11 Peyrat et al. used mAb FCM techniques targeting the δ chain and discovered T cells with dual δ chain functional expression in the PBLs of patients with HIV.12 However, the research on the function and mechanism of dual TCR γδ T cells is not clear.

In this study, we observed heterogeneity in single and dual TCR γδ T gene expression in different γδ T cell subgroups in human and mouse. Human and mouse cells share some characteristic mRNA (eg CCL4, CCL5, Malat1), expressing genes related to leukocyte migration and activation, and nonspecific antibacterial, antiviral, and antitumor activities.41,42 Dual TCR γδ T cells are specifically enriched with genes related to MHC-I Ag presentation (eg H2-D1 and H2-K1 in the WT group)43 and S100a11,44 which regulates cell growth, apoptosis, and inflammatory reaction, is accompanied by high expression of Actb, which is closely related to tumor invasion and metastasis.45 In contrast, single TCR cells highly express DDX5 and LIMD2, which mainly affect the occurrence of tumor cells.46,47 It has been proposed that dual TCR γδ T cells may have a role in immune cell differentiation, mature migration, innate antivirus immunity, adaptive antitumor response, and self-reaction, and that its precise function must be investigated further under various physiological and pathological situations. In addition, we found that there were significant differences in the expression of TRGV9 and TRGV10 in γδ T cells with single TCRs and dual TCRs. This expression pattern suggests that 2 specific V genes, TRGV10 and TRGV9, may play a special role in the development, function, or response to specific Ags of dual TCR γδ T cells. Recent studies have shown that the different expression of the TRGV gene may affect the occurrence of tumors, and the response of γδ T cells in tumors is an indicator of the good prognosis of many cancers.48,49 The adaptive immune response mechanism of γδ T cells may be related to their recognition of phosphorus Ags produced by tumor cells and pathogens. In this process, BNT3A1/BNT2A1 molecules play an important role in the recognition and activation of phosphorus Ag and TCR of γδ T cells.50,51 Therefore, the high expression of TRGV10 and TRGV9 may enhance the recognition and response of dual TCR γδ T cells to phosphorus Ag, thus playing a more effective role in antitumor immunity.

In summary, we discovered a high proportion of dual TCR γδ T cells in both human and mouse datasets. To our knowledge, this study is the first to use scRNA + TCR-seq analysis in a transgenic TRDV6 δ chain mouse model to reveal that approximately 8% of the cells exhibit dual TCR γ rearrangements. This finding provides a novel approach and framework for investigating the rearrangement mechanisms and effects of dual TCR lymphocytes using transgenic mouse models combined with single-cell sequencing. Based on the V(D)J composition characteristics of dual (or more) TCR mRNA, we also analyzed their potential origin mechanisms.

Several limitations of this work should be acknowledged. First, the influence of single-cell sequencing technology on the analysis needs further investigation, and the real type of TCR pairing needs to be verified at the protein-expression level. Second, the subjects and study samples in this experiment were very limited, especially because, according to classical theory, γδ T cells have characteristic high-frequency use of V and D gene subfamilies in different tissues.22,52 These characteristics’ association with dual TCR γδ T cells remains to be determined through studies enriching for γδ T cells in different tissue sites using scRNA + scTCR-seq. Additionally, in recent years, γδ T cells, which lie between innate and acquired immunity, have garnered widespread attention.53 Our preliminary analysis under physiological conditions in humans and mice can provide a foundation for further research on the proportion, origin, mechanisms, and effects of dual TCR γδ T cells under various pathological conditions. scRNA + TCR-seq technology is a powerful tool to deeply understand the heterogeneity and function of γδ T cells, but its experimental complexity and data acquisition cost may limit its application in large-scale samples. Recently, Song et al.54 proposed a strategy based on TCR gene module scoring to identify γδ T cells efficiently and accurately from the scRNA-seq data set. In future research, we will consider adopting this strategy to overcome the limitations of current methods and explore a wider range of samples.

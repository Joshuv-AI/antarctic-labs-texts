On cluster structures of bosonic extensions
Yingjin Bi

Abstract
. In this paper, we analyze the transition maps for Lusztig’s parameterization of the global basis for the bosonic extension 𝒜 ^ ​ ( b ) \widehat{\mathcal{A}}(b) of the quantum coordinate ring of a unipotent group, where b b belongs to the positive braid group Br + \operatorname{Br}^{+} . By examining different reduced expressions of b b , we establish that the cluster structure on 𝒜 ^ ​ ( b ) \widehat{\mathcal{A}}(b) remains invariant regardless of the chosen reduced expression. For the simply-laced case, we construct a cluster structure on 𝒜 ^ ​ ( b ) \widehat{\mathcal{A}}(b) for all b ∈ Br + b\in\operatorname{Br}^{+} , using a proof distinct from Qin’s [ 16 ] .

The braid symmetrizer action

on the bosonic extension
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}
is defined in
[
8

\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{<0}
denote the subalgebra generated by elements with negative weights. We define

Kashiwara, Kim, Oh, and Park propose the following conjecture:

Conjecture 1.1

(
[
8
]
)
.

The algebra
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}(b)
admits a cluster structure, and the cluster monomials are contained in the global basis.

Qin

[

by employing isomorphisms between (quantum) cluster algebras corresponding to different reduced expressions of
b

\widehat{\mathcal{A}}(b)

In this paper, we explore the transition maps associated with Lusztig’s parameterizations of the global basis of
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}(b)
. Our analysis demonstrates that different reduced expressions of
b
are linked through a sequence of mutations and permutations within the cluster structure. Unlike methods relying on isomorphisms between cluster algebras derived from distinct reduced expressions, our approach does not depend on the computability property. Instead, the transition maps allow us to directly relate the seeds corresponding to two reduced expressions of
b
using the global basis of
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}(b)
.

Cluster Structure on Quantum Coordinate Rings

Let

be a generalized Cartan matrix of size
I\times I
, and let

be the associated Kac-Moody Lie algebra. We denote by

the set of positive roots, by

(resp.
Q^{+}

\alpha_{i}
(resp.
\alpha_{i}^{\vee}

C
. The fundamental weights are denoted by
\varpi_{i}
, and the weight lattice is defined as
P=\mathbb{Z}[\varpi_{i}]_{i\in I}
. We choose a diagonal matrix
D=\operatorname{diag}(d_{i})_{i\in I}

DC=(a_{ij})
is symmetric and
\min\{d_{i}\mid i\in I\}=1

(\cdot,\cdot):P\times P\to\mathbb{Z}

(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j})=a_{ij}
. Additionally, we set

\langle h,\alpha_{i}\rangle=\frac{2(h,\alpha_{i})}{(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{i})}\quad\text{for any }h\in P.

The Weyl group
W
of
\mathfrak{g}
is generated by simple reflections

, which act on
P
via

s_{i}(\lambda)=\lambda-\langle\lambda,\alpha_{i}\rangle\alpha_{i}.

Let
\mathbb{K}
be either the field
\mathbb{Q}(q^{1/2})
or the ring
\mathbb{Z}[q^{\pm 1/2}]
. For each
i\in I
, define
q_{i}=q^{d_{i}}
and the quantum integers as follows:

[n]_{i}=\frac{q_{i}^{n}-q_{i}^{-n}}{q_{i}-q_{i}^{-1}},\quad[n]_{i}!=[n]_{i}[n-1]_{i}\cdots[1]_{i},\quad\text{and}\quad\binom{n}{k}_{i}=\frac{[n]_{i}!}{[k]_{i}![n-k]_{i}!}.

2.1.
Quantum Unipotent Coordinate Rings

Given the Cartan matrix
C
, the quantum group
U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
is a
\mathbb{K}
-algebra generated by elements
e_{i}
,
f_{i}
, and
q^{h}
for
i\in I
and
h\in P
, subject to the following relations:

(2.1)

\displaystyle q^{0}=1,\quad q^{h}q^{h^{\prime}}=q^{h+h^{\prime}},\quad q^{h}e_{i}q^{-h}=q^{\langle h,\alpha_{i}\rangle}e_{i},\quad q^{h}f_{i}q^{-h}=q^{-\langle h,\alpha_{i}\rangle}f_{i},

(2.2)

\displaystyle e_{i}f_{j}-f_{j}e_{i}=\delta_{i,j}\frac{t_{i}-t_{i}^{-1}}{q_{i}-q_{i}^{-1}},\quad\text{where }t_{i}=q^{d_{i}\alpha_{i}^{\vee}},

(2.3)

\displaystyle\sum_{r=0}^{b_{i,j}}(-1)^{r}\binom{b_{i,j}}{r}_{i}e_{i}^{b_{i,j}-r}e_{j}e_{i}^{r}=0,\quad\sum_{r=0}^{b_{i,j}}(-1)^{r}\binom{b_{i,j}}{r}_{i}f_{i}^{b_{i,j}-r}f_{j}f_{i}^{r}=0\quad\text{for }i\neq j,

with
b_{i,j}=1-c_{i,j}
.

The subalgebra

of

is generated by the elements

for all

and is a
Q^{+}
-weighted space. The quantum unipotent coordinate ring

is defined as the graded dual algebra:

A_{q}(\mathfrak{n}):=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in-Q^{+}}\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{C}}(U_{q}(\mathfrak{n})_{\alpha},\mathbb{C}),

where
U_{q}(\mathfrak{n})_{\alpha}
denotes the weight space of

corresponding to the weight
\alpha
.

When

, the algebra

is isomorphic to the coordinate ring
\mathbb{C}[N]
of the maximal unipotent subgroup

of the algebraic group

associated with
\mathfrak{g}

2.1.1.
PBW Bases

For any reduced expression
\underline{w}:=s_{i_{1}}s_{i_{2}}\cdots s_{i_{\ell}}
of a Weyl group element

\beta_{k}:=s_{i_{1}}s_{i_{2}}\cdots s_{i_{k-1}}(\alpha_{i_{k}}),\quad\text{for }k\in[1,\ell].

The root element
E^{*}(\beta_{k})

within the algebra

R(w):=\{\alpha\in R^{+}\mid w^{-1}(\alpha)\in-R^{+}\},

It follows that:

and this equality holds for any reduced expression
\underline{w}
of

Next, we define the subspace:

\mathfrak{n}(w):=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in R(w)}\mathfrak{n}_{\alpha},

where
\mathfrak{n}_{\alpha}
is the root space corresponding to
\alpha
in the nilpotent Lie algebra

A_{q}(\mathfrak{n}(w))
of

is generated by the elements
E^{*}(\beta)
for all
\beta\in R(w)
. In the finite-type case, if

is the longest element of the Weyl group, then:

A_{q}(\mathfrak{n}(w_{0}))=A_{q}(\mathfrak{n}).

A convex order is imposed on

by:

\beta_{k}<\beta_{l}\quad\text{if }k<l.

We also define an order on the exponent vectors in
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{[1,\ell]}
. For vectors
\mathbf{a}=(a_{\ell},a_{\ell-1},\ldots,a_{1})
and
\mathbf{b}=(b_{\ell},b_{\ell-1},\ldots,b_{1})
, we say:

(2.4)

\mathbf{a}\prec\mathbf{b}

if and only if both of these equivalent conditions holds:

(1)

There exists
1\leq k\leq\ell
such that
a_{j}=b_{j}
for all

and
a_{k}<b_{k}
.

(2)

There exists

such that
a_{j}=b_{j}
for all
j>l
and
a_{l}<b_{l}
.

For any vector
\mathbf{a}=(a_{\ell},a_{\ell-1},\ldots,a_{1})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{[1,\ell]}
, we define the monomial:

E^{*}(\mathbf{a}):=E^{*}(\beta_{\ell})^{a_{\ell}}E^{*}(\beta_{\ell-1})^{a_{\ell-1}}\cdots E^{*}(\beta_{1})^{a_{1}}.

These elements form a basis of
A_{q}(\operatorname{\mathfrak{n}}(w))
.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
12
]
)
.

The set

forms a basis of the algebra

2.1.2.
Dual Canonical Bases

We define an anti-automorphism
\overline{\cdot}
on the algebra
A_{q}(\mathfrak{n})
by:

\overline{q}=q^{-1},\quad\overline{\langle i\rangle}=\langle i\rangle,

where
\langle i\rangle
is the functional defined by
\langle i\rangle(f_{j})=\delta_{ij}
.

Theorem 2.2

(
[
12
]
)
.

For a reduced expression
\underline{w}
of a Weyl group element
w
, there exists a unique basis
\{B(\mathbf{a})\}_{\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{[1,\ell]}}
of
A_{q}(\mathfrak{n}(w))
, called the
dual canonical basis
, satisfying:

(1)

\overline{B(\mathbf{a})}=B(\mathbf{a})
,

(2)

E^{*}(\mathbf{a})=B(\mathbf{a})+\sum_{\mathbf{a}^{\prime}\prec\mathbf{a}}f_{\mathbf{a}^{\prime}}(q)B(\mathbf{a}^{\prime})
, where
f_{\mathbf{a}^{\prime}}(q)\in q\mathbb{Z}[q]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

For any two exponent vectors
\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{[1,\ell]}
, the product of dual canonical basis elements satisfies:

B(\mathbf{a})B(\mathbf{b})=q^{A}B(\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b})+\sum_{\mathbf{c}\prec\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b}}g_{\mathbf{c}}B(\mathbf{c}),

where
g_{\mathbf{c}}\in\mathbb{Z}[q]
.

Proof.

By the Levendorskii-Soibelman formula, the product of PBW basis elements is:

(2.5)

E^{*}(\mathbf{a})E^{*}(\mathbf{b})=q^{A}E^{*}(\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b})+\sum_{\mathbf{c}\prec\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b}}h_{\mathbf{c}}E^{*}(\mathbf{c}).

From Theorem
2.2
, we express the product of dual canonical basis elements as:

(2.6)

B(\mathbf{a})B(\mathbf{b})=\sum_{\mathbf{c}\preccurlyeq\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b}}f_{\mathbf{c}}B(\mathbf{c}).

We aim to show that
f_{\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b}}\neq 0
. Suppose, for contradiction, that
f_{\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b}}=0
. For any
\mathbf{a}^{\prime}\prec\mathbf{a}
and
\mathbf{b}^{\prime}\preccurlyeq\mathbf{b}
, we have
\mathbf{a}^{\prime}+\mathbf{b}^{\prime}\prec\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b}
. Similarly, for
\mathbf{b}^{\prime}\prec\mathbf{b}
and
\mathbf{a}^{\prime}\preccurlyeq\mathbf{a}
, we have
\mathbf{b}^{\prime}+\mathbf{a}^{\prime}\prec\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b}
. Thus, the product
B(\mathbf{a}^{\prime})B(\mathbf{b}^{\prime})
can be written as:

(2.7)

B(\mathbf{a}^{\prime})B(\mathbf{b}^{\prime})=\sum_{\mathbf{c}\prec\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b}}u_{\mathbf{c}}B(\mathbf{c}).

Combining Theorem
2.2
(condition 2) with equations (
2.6
) and (
2.7
), we would obtain:

E^{*}(\mathbf{a})E^{*}(\mathbf{b})=\sum_{\mathbf{c}\prec\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b}}h_{\mathbf{c}}E^{*}(\mathbf{c}),

which contradicts equation (
2.5
) since the leading term
q^{A}E^{*}(\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b})
is missing. Therefore,
f_{\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b}}=q^{A}\neq 0
, completing the proof.
∎

\Phi_{\mathbf{i}}^{\mathbf{i}^{\prime}}(\mathbf{i}[a,b])=\begin{cases}\mathbf{i}^{\prime}[a,b]&\text{if }a,b\notin\{k,k+1,k+2,k+3\},\\
\mathbf{i}^{\prime}[k+1,b]&\text{if }a=k,\\
\mathbf{i}^{\prime}[k,b]&\text{if }a=k+1,\\
(\mathbf{i}^{\prime}[k+3,b],0_{k+2},0_{k+1},1_{k})&\text{if }a=k+2,\\
(\mathbf{i}^{\prime}[(k+2)^{+},b],0_{k+2},0_{k+1},1_{k})&\text{if }a=k+3.\end{cases}

Proof.

The case for 2-moves is straightforward, as it involves swapping adjacent indices. We now prove the case for 3-moves. If
i_{a}\neq i_{k},i_{k-1}
, then
a_{k}=a_{k-1}=a_{k+1}=0
, so
\mathbf{i}[a,b]=\mathbf{i}^{\prime}[a,b]
. By direct computation, we verify:

\Phi_{\mathbf{i}}^{\mathbf{i}^{\prime}}(1_{k+1},0_{k},1_{k-1})=(0_{k+1},1_{k},0_{k-1}),

\Phi_{\mathbf{i}}^{\mathbf{i}^{\prime}}(0_{k+1},1_{k},0_{k-1})=(1_{k+1},0_{k},1_{k-1}).

If
[k-1,k+1]\subset[a,b]
with
k-1>a
,
k+1<b
, and
i_{a}=i_{k}
, then
\mathbf{i}[a,b]=(\mathbf{j},0_{k+1},1_{k},0_{k-1},\mathbf{k})
for some words
\mathbf{j},\mathbf{k}
. Using the above equations:

\Phi_{\mathbf{i}}^{\mathbf{i}^{\prime}}(\mathbf{i}[a,b])=(\mathbf{j},1_{k+1},0_{k},1_{k-1},\mathbf{k})=\mathbf{i}^{\prime}[a,b].

If
i_{a}=i_{k-1}
, then
\mathbf{i}[a,b]=(\mathbf{j},1_{k+1},0_{k},1_{k-1},\mathbf{k})
, and:

\Phi_{\mathbf{i}}^{\mathbf{i}^{\prime}}(\mathbf{i}[a,b])=(\mathbf{j},0_{k+1},1_{k},0_{k-1},\mathbf{k})=\mathbf{i}^{\prime}[a,b].

For
a=k-1
, we have
\mathbf{i}[a,b]=(\mathbf{i}[(k-1)^{+2},b],1_{k+1},0_{k},1_{k-1})
, yielding:

\Phi_{\mathbf{i}}^{\mathbf{i}^{\prime}}(\mathbf{i}[a,b])=(\mathbf{i}^{\prime}[k^{+},b],0_{k+1},1_{k},0_{k-1})=\mathbf{i}^{\prime}[k,b].

For
a=k
, we have
\mathbf{i}[a,b]=(\mathbf{i}[k^{+},b],0_{k+1},1_{k},0_{k-1})
, and by Theorem
2.5
:

\Phi_{\mathbf{i}}^{\mathbf{i}^{\prime}}(\mathbf{i}[a,b])=(\mathbf{i}^{\prime}[(k+1)^{+},b],1_{k+1},0_{k},1_{k-1})=\mathbf{i}^{\prime}[k-1,b].

For
a=k+1
, we have
\mathbf{i}[a,b]=(\mathbf{i}[a^{+},b],1_{k+1},0_{k},0_{k-1})
, so:

\Phi_{\mathbf{i}}^{\mathbf{i}^{\prime}}(\mathbf{i}[a,b])=(\mathbf{i}^{\prime}[k^{+},b],0_{k+1},0_{k},1_{k-1}).

The cases for
b=k
,
k-1
, or
k+1
follow similarly.

For 4-moves with
(i_{k}i_{k+1}i_{k+2}i_{k+3})=(ijij)
, we compute:

\Phi_{\mathbf{i}}^{\mathbf{i}^{\prime}}(1_{k+3},0_{k+2},1_{k+1},0_{k})=(0_{k+3},1_{k+2},0_{k+1},1_{k}),

\Phi_{\mathbf{i}}^{\mathbf{i}^{\prime}}(0_{k+3},1_{k+2},0_{k+1},1_{k})=(1_{k+3},0_{k+2},1_{k+1},0_{k}),

(2.8)

\Phi_{\mathbf{i}}^{\mathbf{i}^{\prime}}(0_{k+3},1_{k+2},0_{k+1},0_{k})=(1_{k+3},0_{k+2},0_{k+1},1_{k}),

(2.9)

\Phi_{\mathbf{i}}^{\mathbf{i}^{\prime}}(1_{k+3},0_{k+2},0_{k+1},0_{k})=(0_{k+3},0_{k+2},0_{k+1},1_{k}).

If
a<k
and
i_{a}\neq i,j
, then
\mathbf{i}[a,b]=(\mathbf{j},0_{k+3},0_{k+2},0_{k+1},0_{k},\mathbf{k})
, so
\Phi_{\mathbf{i}}^{\mathbf{i}^{\prime}}(\mathbf{i}[a,b])=\mathbf{i}^{\prime}[a,b]
. If
i_{a}=i
, then
\mathbf{i}[a,b]=(\mathbf{j},0_{k+3},1_{k+2},0_{k+1},1_{k},\mathbf{k})
, and:

\Phi_{\mathbf{i}}^{\mathbf{i}^{\prime}}(\mathbf{i}[a,b])=(\mathbf{j},1_{k+3},0_{k+2},1_{k+1},0_{k},\mathbf{k})=\mathbf{i}^{\prime}[a,b].

Similarly, if
i_{a}=j
, the result is analogous. For
a=k
, we have
\mathbf{i}[k,b]=(\mathbf{j},0_{k+3},1_{k+2},0_{k+1},1_{k})
, so:

\Phi_{\mathbf{i}}^{\mathbf{i}^{\prime}}(\mathbf{i}[k,b])=(\mathbf{j},1_{k+3},0_{k+2},1_{k+1},0_{k})=\mathbf{i}^{\prime}[k+1,b].

For
a=k+1
, we obtain
\mathbf{i}^{\prime}[k,b]
. For
a=k+2
, since
\mathbf{i}[k+2,b]=(\mathbf{i}[(k+2)^{+},b],0_{k+3},1_{k+2},0_{k+1},0_{k})
, equation (
2.8
) gives:

\Phi_{\mathbf{i}}^{\mathbf{i}^{\prime}}(\mathbf{i}[k+2,b])=(\mathbf{i}^{\prime}[k+3,b],0_{k+2},0_{k+1},1_{k}).

For
a=k+3
, using equation (
2.9
), we get:

\Phi_{\mathbf{i}}^{\mathbf{i}^{\prime}}(\mathbf{i}[k+3,b])=(\mathbf{i}^{\prime}[(k+2)^{+},b],0_{k+2},0_{k+1},1_{k}).

This completes the proof for the specified cases.
∎

2.2.
Quantum Cluster Algebras

Let
K=[1,r]
be a finite set with a partition
K=K^{\text{ex}}\cup K^{\text{fr}}
. Let
B
be an integer-valued
K\times K^{\text{ex}}
-matrix whose principal part,
B_{K^{\text{ex}}\times K^{\text{ex}}}
, is skew-symmetrizable. That is, there exists a diagonal matrix
D^{\prime}=\operatorname{diag}(d^{\prime}_{i})
such that:

d^{\prime}_{i}b_{ij}=-d^{\prime}_{j}b_{ji}\quad\text{for all }i,j\in K^{\text{ex}}.

Additionally, let
\Lambda
be a skew-symmetric
K\times K
-matrix. The pair
(\Lambda,B)
is called
compatible
if it satisfies:

\sum_{k\in K}\lambda_{ik}b_{kj}=-2d^{\prime}_{i}\delta_{ij}\quad\text{for all }i\in K,\,j\in K^{\text{ex}}.

Given a skew-symmetric matrix
\Lambda=(\lambda_{ij})
, we define the
quantum torus

\mathcal{T}_{\Lambda}
as the algebra
\mathbb{K}[X_{i}^{\pm 1}]_{i\in K}
, where
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{Z}[q^{\pm 1/2}]
, with relations:

X_{i}X_{j}=q^{\lambda_{ij}}X_{j}X_{i},\quad X_{i}X_{i}^{-1}=X_{i}^{-1}X_{i}=1.

For any vector
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r})\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}
, we define the monomial:

X^{\mathbf{a}}=q^{\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i>j}a_{i}a_{j}\lambda_{ij}}X_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots X_{r}^{a_{r}}.

A
seed
is a tuple
\mathbf{t}:=\{(X_{i})_{i\in K},\Lambda,B,K^{\text{ex}}\}
, where
(\Lambda,B)
is a compatible pair. The variables
X_{i}
are called
cluster variables
.

For
k\in K^{\text{ex}}
, the
mutation
at
k
is defined as follows:

- The mutated matrix
\mu_{k}(B)
has entries:

\mu_{k}(B)_{ij}=\begin{cases}-b_{ij}&\text{if }i=k\text{ or }j=k,\\
b_{ij}+(-1)^{\delta(b_{ik}<0)}\max\{b_{ik}b_{kj},0\}&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

where
\delta(b_{ik}<0)=1
if
b_{ik}<0
, and
0
otherwise.

- The mutated matrix
\mu_{k}(\Lambda)
has entries:

(2.10)

\mu_{k}(\Lambda)_{ij}=\begin{cases}-\lambda_{kj}+\sum_{l\in K}\max\{0,-b_{lk}\}\lambda_{lj}&\text{if }i=k,j\neq k,\\
-\lambda_{ik}+\sum_{l\in K}\max\{0,-b_{lk}\}\lambda_{il}&\text{if }i\neq k,j=k,\\
\lambda_{ij}&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

- The mutated cluster variables are:

\mu_{k}(X_{i})=\begin{cases}X_{i}&\text{if }i\neq k,\\
X^{\mathbf{a}}+X^{\mathbf{a}^{\prime}}&\text{if }i=k,\end{cases}

where:

\mathbf{a}=([b_{1k}]_{+},\ldots,[b_{k-1,k}]_{+},-1,[b_{k+1,k}]_{+},\ldots,[b_{rk}]_{+}),

\mathbf{a}^{\prime}=([-b_{1k}]_{+},\ldots,[-b_{k-1,k}]_{+},-1,[-b_{k+1,k}]_{+},\ldots,[-b_{rk}]_{+}),

and
[a]_{+}=\max\{0,a\}
.

It can be verified that
(\mu_{k}(\Lambda),\mu_{k}(B))
remains a compatible pair, yielding a new seed:

\mu_{k}(\mathbf{t}):=\{(\mu_{k}(X_{i}))_{i\in K},\mu_{k}(\Lambda),\mu_{k}(B),K^{\text{ex}}\}.

Let
\Delta^{+}
denote the set of all seeds obtained from
\mathbf{t}
by any finite sequence of mutations.

Definition 2.7
.

For a seed
\mathbf{t}
, the
quantum cluster algebra

\mathcal{A}(\mathbf{t})
is the
\mathbb{K}
-subalgebra of
\mathcal{T}_{\Lambda}
generated by all cluster variables
X_{i}(\mathbf{t}^{\prime})
for all seeds
\mathbf{t}^{\prime}\in\Delta^{+}
.

2.2.1.
Morphisms of Quantum Cluster Algebras

Let
\rho:K\to K
be a permutation of the set
K
such that
\rho(K^{\text{ex}})\subset K^{\text{ex}}
. We define the matrices
B_{\rho}
and
\Lambda_{\rho}
as follows:

b_{\rho,ij}=b_{\rho(i),\rho(j)},\quad\lambda_{\rho,ij}=\lambda_{\rho(i),\rho(j)}.

The tuple
\rho(\mathbf{t}):=\{(X_{\rho(i)})_{i\in K},\Lambda_{\rho},B_{\rho},K^{\text{ex}}\}
forms a seed, and there exists an isomorphism:

(2.11)

\mathcal{A}(\rho(\mathbf{t}))\cong\mathcal{A}(\mathbf{t}).

For a seed
\mathbf{t}=\{(X_{i})_{i\in K},B_{K\times K^{\text{ex}}},\Lambda_{K\times K},K^{\text{ex}}\}
, let
J\subset K
and
J^{\text{ex}}\subset K^{\text{ex}}
. Suppose the submatrix satisfies:

(2.12)

B_{(K\setminus J)\times J^{\text{ex}}}=0.

We define the
restricted seed

\mathbf{t}_{J}:=\{(X_{i})_{i\in J},B_{J\times J^{\text{ex}}},\Lambda_{J\times J},J^{\text{ex}}\}.

Proposition 2.8
.

There exists a cluster algebra embedding:

\mathcal{A}(\mathbf{t}_{J})\subset\mathcal{A}(\mathbf{t}).

.

Definition 3.1

For each vertex

Theorem 3.8
.

For two reduced words

\mathbf{t}_{\mathbf{i}^{\prime}}
can be derived from
\mathbf{t}_{\mathbf{i}}
through a series of mutations and permutations. Note that we use the fact that cluster monomials are contained in
B(\infty)
in the proof of Corollary
2.13
. Thus, both seeds define the same cluster algebra, and the conjecture holds for
\mathbf{i}^{\prime}
if it holds for
\mathbf{i}
.
∎

4.
Monoidal categorfication in the simply-laced case

4.1.
Q-Datum

Let
\Delta
be a simply-laced Dynkin diagram with vertex set
I
and edge set
\Delta_{1}
. A
height function

\xi=(\xi_{i})_{i\in I}
on
\Delta
is defined by the condition:

|\xi_{i}-\xi_{j}|=1\quad\text{for any }i,j\in I\text{ such that }c_{ij}=-1.

The height
\xi
induces an orientation on
\Delta
, where an arrow
i\to j
exists if and only if
\xi_{i}>\xi_{j}
and
c_{ij}=-1
.

Definition 4.1
.

A
Q-datum
is a pair
(\Delta,\xi)
, where
\Delta
is a simply-laced Dynkin diagram and
\xi
is a height function on
\Delta
.

For a vertex
i\in I
that is a
source
in the quiver associated with
\xi
(i.e., there are no arrows
j\to i
), the
reflection

s_{i}
acts on the height function by:

s_{i}(\xi_{j})=\xi_{j}-2\delta_{ij}.

Let
w_{0}
be the longest element of the Weyl group associated with
\Delta
, with length
\ell
, and let
\underline{w_{0}}=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{\ell})
be a reduced expression for
w_{0}
. The expression
\underline{w_{0}}
is
adapted
to the
Q
-datum
(\Delta,\xi)
if, for each
k\leq\ell
, the vertex
i_{k}
is a source in the quiver associated with the height:

s_{i_{k-1}}\cdots s_{i_{1}}\xi.

For an adapted reduced expression
\underline{w_{0}}
, we extend it to an infinite sequence
\widehat{w_{0}}=\{i_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
by:

i_{k+\ell}=i_{k}^{*},\quad\text{where }i^{*}\text{ satisfies }w_{0}(\alpha_{i})=-\alpha_{i^{*}}.

It follows that for any
k\in\mathbb{Z}
:

s_{i_{k}}\cdots s_{i_{k+\ell}}=w_{0}.

Additionally, the action of
w_{0}
on the height function satisfies:

(w_{0}\xi)_{i}=\xi_{i^{*}}-h,

where
h
is the Coxeter number of
\Delta
.

Define the set:

(4.1)

\widehat{\Delta}:=\left\{(i,p)\in I\times\mathbb{Z}\mid p-\xi_{i}\in 2\mathbb{Z}\right\}.

For each integer
k
, define the subset:

(4.2)

\widehat{\Delta}_{Q}[k]:=\left\{(i,p)\in\widehat{\Delta}\mid\xi_{i^{*}}-(k+1)h<p\leq\xi_{i}-kh\right\}.

Let
\widehat{\Delta}_{Q}:=\widehat{\Delta}_{Q}[0]
, and define the negative part:

\widehat{\Delta}^{-}:=\left\{(i,p)\in\widehat{\Delta}\mid p\leq\xi_{i}\right\}.

For an adapted infinite sequence
\widehat{w_{0}}=\{i_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
, define the sequence of integers
p_{k}
by:

p_{k}=\begin{cases}\left(s_{i_{k-1}}\cdots s_{i_{1}}\xi\right)_{i_{k}}&\text{if }k>0,\\
\left(s_{i_{k}}^{-1}\cdots s_{i_{0}}^{-1}\xi\right)_{i_{k}}&\text{if }k\leq 0.\end{cases}

Theorem 4.2

(
[
10
]
, Proposition 6.11)
.

There exists an isomorphism:

\widehat{w_{0}}\to\widehat{\Delta},

mapping
i_{k}
to
(i_{k},p_{k})
.

Define the matrix
B_{HL}
, indexed by
\widehat{\Delta}\times\widehat{\Delta}
, as:

(4.3)

b_{(i_{k},p_{k}),(i_{l},p_{l})}=b_{i_{k},i_{l}},

where
b_{i_{k},i_{l}}
are the entries of the matrix
B_{\text{GLS}}
referenced in Remark
3.9
.

There exists a bijection:

(4.4)

\phi:\widehat{\Delta}\to R^{+}\times\mathbb{Z},

such that
\phi((i,\xi_{i}))=(\gamma_{i},0)
, where
\gamma_{i}
is the root corresponding to the dimension vector of the injective representation
I_{i}
of the quiver associated with the
Q
-datum
(\Delta,\xi)
. Furthermore, if
\phi((i,p))=(\alpha,k)
, then:

\phi((i,p+2))=\begin{cases}(\tau\alpha,k)&\text{if }\tau\alpha\in R^{+},\\
(-\tau\alpha,k+1)&\text{if }\tau\alpha\in R^{-},\end{cases}

where
\tau
is the Coxeter element for the quiver.

4.2.
Quantum Loop Algebras

This section introduces the quantum loop algebra
U_{q}(L\mathfrak{g})
associated with a simple Lie algebra

, along with its module categories and related algebraic structures.

4.2.1.
Modules of the Quantum Loop Algebra

Let
\mathscr{C}
denote the category of finite-dimensional
U_{q}(L\mathfrak{g})
-modules of type 1. Each simple module
M\in\mathscr{C}
is characterized by a tuple of Drinfeld polynomials
(P_{i})_{i\in I}
, where
P_{i}\in 1+q\mathbb{Z}[q]
. The roots of these polynomials determine a dominant monomial:

m=\prod_{(i,a)}Y_{i,a},

where the product is over the roots
a
of
P_{i}
. If
a=q^{k}
, we write
Y_{i,k}
for
Y_{i,q^{k}}
.

Define
\mathcal{M}
as the set of all dominant monomials, and let
\mathcal{M}^{+}\subset\mathcal{M}
consist of monomials of the form:

m=\prod_{(i,k)\in\widehat{\Delta}}Y_{i,k}^{u_{i,k}}.

For each pair
(i,p)\in\Delta
such that
(i,p-d_{i})\in\widehat{\Delta}
, define the element:

(4.5)

A_{i,p}=Y_{i,p-d_{i}}Y_{i,p+d_{i}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}(j,s)\in\widehat{\Delta}\\
j\sim i,|s-p|<d_{i}\end{subarray}}Y_{j,s}^{-1}.

The
Hernandez-Leclerc category

\mathscr{C}^{\mathbb{Z}}
is the Serre subcategory of
\mathscr{C}
generated by simple modules
L(m)
for
m\in\mathcal{M}^{+}
. For a
Q
-datum
(\Delta,\xi)
, the subcategory
\mathscr{C}^{\xi}\subset\mathscr{C}^{\mathbb{Z}}
is the Serre subcategory generated by simple modules
L(m)
with:

m=\prod_{(i,p)\in\widehat{\Delta}^{-}}Y_{(i,p)}^{u_{(i,p)}}.

The simple module
L(Y_{i,k})
is called a
fundamental module
. For an interval
[a,b]
such that
(i,a),(i,b)\in\widehat{\Delta}
, the
Kirillov-Reshetikhin module
is defined as:

M^{(i)}[a,b]:=L\left(Y_{i,a}Y_{i,a+2}\cdots Y_{i,b}\right).

For a
Q
-datum and an element
(i,p)\in\widehat{\Delta}^{-}
, define the monomial:

m^{i}_{p}:=\prod_{k:p+2k\leq\xi_{i}}Y_{i,p+2k},

and let
Z_{i,p}
denote the simple module
L(m^{i}_{p})
.

Theorem 4.3

(
[

]
,

]

For a given

forms a cluster algebra with the initial seed:

\left(\{Z_{i,p}\}_{(i,p)\in\widehat{\Delta}^{-}},B_{HL},\Lambda,\widehat{\Delta}^{-}\right),

where

is defined in (
4.3
), and
\Lambda
is the skew-symmetric matrix from Remark
3.9
. The cluster monomials correspond to the classes of simple modules in
\mathscr{C}^{\xi}
.

4.2.2.
Quantum Grothendieck Rings

Assume the Cartan matrix
C
is simply-laced. The
quantum Cartan matrix

C(q)
is defined as:

C(q)_{ij}=\begin{cases}q_{i}+q_{i}^{-1}&\text{if }i=j,\\
[c_{ij}]_{i}&\text{if }i\neq j.\end{cases}

Since
C
is invertible,
C(q)
has an inverse matrix
\widetilde{C}(q)=\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}}\widetilde{c}(u)z^{u}
. For any
(i,p),(j,q)\in\widehat{\Delta}
, define:

(4.6)

\mathcal{N}((i,p),(j,q)):=\widetilde{c}(p-q-d_{i})-\widetilde{c}(p-q+d_{i})+\widetilde{c}(q-p-d_{i})-\widetilde{c}(q-p+d_{i}).

The
quantum torus

\mathcal{Y}_{q}
is the
\mathbb{Z}[q^{\pm 1/2}]
-algebra generated by
Y_{i,p}^{\pm 1}
for
(i,p)\in\widehat{\Delta}
, subject to the relations:

(4.7)

\displaystyle Y_{i,p}*Y_{j,q}

\displaystyle=q^{\mathcal{N}((i,p),(j,q))}Y_{j,q}Y_{i,p},

(4.8)

\displaystyle Y_{i,p}*Y_{i,p}^{-1}

\displaystyle=Y_{i,p}^{-1}*Y_{i,p}=1.

For a vector
\mathbf{a}=(a_{i,p})\in\mathbb{Z}^{\widehat{\Delta}}
, define the monomial:

m(\mathbf{a})=q^{A_{\mathbf{a}}}\overrightarrow{\prod}_{(i,k)\in\widehat{\Delta}}Y_{(i,k)}^{a_{i,k}},

where
A_{\mathbf{a}}
is chosen such that the product is independent of the ordering.

For each
i\in I
, the subalgebra
\mathcal{K}_{i,q}\subset\mathcal{Y}_{q}
is generated over
\mathbb{Z}[q^{1/2}]
by:

\left\{Y_{i,k}(1+q^{-1}A_{i,k+d_{i}}^{-1})\mid(i,k)\in\widehat{\Delta}\right\}\cup\left\{Y_{j,l}\mid(j,l)\in\widehat{\Delta},j\neq i\right\}.

The
quantum Grothendieck group

K_{q}(\mathscr{C}^{\mathbb{Z}})
is defined as:

K_{q}(\mathscr{C}^{\mathbb{Z}}):=\bigcap_{i\in I}\mathcal{K}_{i,q}.

Theorem 4.4

(

mapping the generator

to the
(q,t)
-character of the simple module

4.2.3.
R-Matrix Invariants

For simple modules
M,N\in\mathscr{C}
, let
M_{z}:=M\otimes k[z]
denote the affinization of
M
. The
normalized R-matrix
is defined as:

(4.9)

R_{M,N}:M_{z}\otimes N\to k(q)\otimes(N\otimes M_{z}).

The denominator of
R_{M,N}
is denoted
d_{M,N}(z)
. Define:

\Lambda(M,N):=\operatorname{zero}_{z=1}d_{M,N}(z),

as the order of the zero of
d_{M,N}(z)
at
z=1
. The symmetric invariant is:

(4.10)

\mathfrak{b}(M,N)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\Lambda(M,N)+\Lambda(N,M)\right).

4.3.
Quiver Hecke Algebras

Let
Q
be a quiver with vertex set
I
. For vertices
i,j\in I
, let
m_{i,j}
denote the number of arrows from
i
to
j
. Define the polynomial:

q_{i,j}(u,v)=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }i=j,\\
(v-u)^{m_{i,j}}(u-v)^{m_{j,i}}&\text{if }i\neq j.\end{cases}

Definition 4.5
.

For a weight
\alpha=\sum_{i\in I}c_{i}\alpha_{i}\in\mathbb{N}[I]
with height
\operatorname{ht}(\alpha)=\sum_{i\in I}c_{i}=n
, the
KLR algebra
(or quiver Hecke algebra)
R_{\alpha}
is an associative
\mathbb{Z}[q^{\pm 1}]
-algebra generated by:

\{e_{\mathbf{i}}\mid\mathbf{i}\in\langle I\rangle_{\alpha}\}\cup\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}\cup\{\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{n-1}\},

subject to the following relations:

•

x_{k}x_{l}=x_{l}x_{k}
for all
k,l
,

•

The elements
\{e_{\mathbf{i}}\mid\mathbf{i}\in\langle I\rangle_{\alpha}\}
are mutually orthogonal idempotents summing to the identity
e_{\alpha}\in R_{\alpha}
,

•

x_{k}e_{\mathbf{i}}=e_{\mathbf{i}}x_{k}
and
\tau_{k}e_{\mathbf{i}}=e_{t_{k}\mathbf{i}}\tau_{k}
,

•

(\tau_{k}x_{l}-x_{t_{k}(l)}\tau_{k})e_{\mathbf{i}}=\delta_{i_{k},i_{k+1}}(\delta_{k+1,l}-\delta_{k,l})e_{\mathbf{i}}
,

•

\tau_{k}^{2}e_{\mathbf{i}}=q_{i_{k},i_{k+1}}(x_{k},x_{k+1})e_{\mathbf{i}}
,

•

\tau_{k}\tau_{l}=\tau_{l}\tau_{k}
if
|k-l|>1
,

•

(\tau_{k+1}\tau_{k}\tau_{k+1}-\tau_{k}\tau_{k+1}\tau_{k})e_{\mathbf{i}}=\delta_{i_{k},i_{k+2}}\frac{q_{i_{k},i_{k+1}}(x_{k},x_{k+1})-q_{i_{k},i_{k+1}}(x_{k+2},x_{k+1})}{x_{k}-x_{k+2}}e_{\mathbf{i}}
,

where
t_{k}
is the transposition that swaps
k
and
k+1
, fixing all other indices (
t_{k}(k)=k+1
,
t_{k}(k+1)=k
,
t_{k}(l)=l
for
l\neq k,k+1
).

The algebra
R_{\alpha}
is
\mathbb{Z}
-graded with degrees
\deg x_{i}=2
and
\deg\tau_{k}e_{\mathbf{i}}=-(\alpha_{i_{k}},\alpha_{i_{k+1}})
. We define the full KLR algebra as:

R_{Q}:=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in Q^{+}}R_{\alpha},

where
Q^{+}
is the positive cone of the root lattice.

For a locally finite-dimensional graded module
M=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}M_{i}
, the
graded dimension
is:

\operatorname{Dim}M=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\dim M_{i}q^{i}.

For a weight decomposition
\alpha=\beta+\gamma
, define the idempotent:

e(\beta,\gamma)=\sum_{\mathbf{i}\in\langle I\rangle_{\beta},\mathbf{j}\in\langle I\rangle_{\gamma}}e(\mathbf{i}\mathbf{j}).

For finite-dimensional modules
M\in R_{\beta}\text{-mod}
and
N\in R_{\gamma}\text{-mod}
, the
convolution product
is:

M\circ N:=R_{\alpha}e(\beta,\gamma)\otimes_{R_{\beta}\times R_{\gamma}}(M\boxtimes N).

The category
\mathcal{C}_{Q}
is the category of graded finite-dimensional
R_{Q}
-modules, and its Grothendieck group is denoted
K(\mathcal{C}_{Q})
. The convolution product
\circ
endows
K(\mathcal{C}_{Q})
with the structure of a
\mathbb{Z}[q^{\pm 1}]
-algebra. Let
L(\alpha_{i})
be the unique simple module over
R_{\alpha_{i}}
.

Theorem 4.6

(
[
11
,
17
]
)
.

For a symmetric Cartan matrix
C
and an associated quiver
Q
, there exists an isomorphism:

K(\mathcal{C}_{Q})\cong A_{q}(\mathfrak{n}),

mapping classes of simple modules to the dual canonical basis
\mathbf{B}(\infty)
of
A_{q}(\mathfrak{n})
, up to scaling. In particular, the image of the simple module
L(\alpha_{i})
is
\langle i\rangle
. We also denote by
D_{\mathbf{i}}[a,b]
the simple modules corresponding to the quantum minors
D_{\mathbf{i}}[a,b]
for some
\mathbf{i}
-box
[a,b]
.

For simple modules
M,N\in\mathcal{C}_{Q}
, the
R-matrix
is a morphism:

r_{M,N}:M\circ N\to N\circ M.

See
[
6
]
for further details. The degree of
r_{M,N}
is denoted
\Lambda(M,N)
, and we define:

(4.11)

\mathfrak{b}(M,N):=\frac{1}{2}(\Lambda(M,N)+\Lambda(N,M)).

4.4.
PBW Pairs

This section introduces the concept of PBW pairs, which combine strong duality data with infinite sequences derived from Weyl group elements, and explores their role in connecting module categories to quantum algebras.

4.4.1.
Strong Duality Datum

Let
\mathcal{D}:=\{L_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be a tuple of real simple modules in the category
\mathscr{C}^{\mathbb{Z}}
. The tuple
\mathcal{D}
is called a
strong duality datum
associated with the Cartan matrix
C
if it satisfies:

(1)

For each
i\in I
,
\mathfrak{b}(L_{i},\mathscr{D}^{k}(L_{i}))=\delta_{k,\pm 1}
, where
\mathscr{D}
and
\mathscr{D}^{-1}
are the left and right duality functors in
\mathscr{C}
, and
\mathfrak{b}
is the symmetric invariant defined in (
4.10
).

(2)

For any
i,j\in I
with
i\neq j
,
\mathfrak{b}(L_{i},\mathscr{D}^{k}(L_{j}))=-\delta_{k,0}c_{ij}
, where
c_{ij}
is the
(i,j)
-entry of
C
.

Theorem 4.7

(
[
5
]
)
.

For a strong duality datum
\mathcal{D}
associated with a Cartan matrix
C

associated with

\mathscr{C}_{Q}\subset\mathscr{C}^{\mathbb{Z}}

for

.

.

, define
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}:=\mathscr{S}^{-1}_{i_{1}}(\mathcal{D}_{\mathscr{L}}^{-1})
, with:

L^{\prime}_{i}=\begin{cases}\mathscr{D}(L_{i_{1}})&\text{if }i=i_{1},\\
\operatorname{hd}(L_{i}\otimes L_{i_{1}})&\text{if }c_{i,i_{1}}=-1,\\
L_{i}&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Similarly,
\mathscr{C}^{\mathcal{D}_{\mathscr{L}},[a+1,b+1]}=\mathscr{C}^{\mathcal{D}^{\prime},[a,b]}
, and a
Q
-datum
\mathscr{L}^{\prime}
exists such that
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}=\mathcal{D}_{\mathscr{L}^{\prime}}
. Iterating this process extends the result to any
k
.
∎

Henceforth, we assume
a=1
and focus on intervals
[1,t]
.

4.5.2.
Isomorphism for PBW Pairs

Theorem 4.12
.

For a PBW pair
\mathbf{u}=(\mathcal{D},\widehat{w_{0}})
associated with a
Q
-datum, and an interval
[a,b]=[1,k\ell]
with
k\geq 1
, there exists an isomorphism:

(4.13)

K_{q}(\mathscr{C}_{\mathfrak{g}}^{[1,k\ell],\mathbf{u}})\cong\widehat{\mathcal{A}}[0,k-1].

Proof.

We define a
\mathbb{Z}[q^{\pm 1}]
-algebra map:

(4.14)

\psi:\widehat{\mathcal{A}}[0,k-1]\to K_{q}(\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{g}}^{\mathbb{Z},\mathbf{u}}),

by:

\displaystyle x_{i,m}

\displaystyle\mapsto\mathscr{D}^{m}(L_{\nu^{m}(i)}),\quad\text{where }\nu(i)=i^{*}.

This morphism is well-defined and injective by
[
2
, Theorem 7.3]

Additionally, we have:

\psi(\mathscr{D}(x))=\mathscr{D}\circ\nu(\psi(x)),

where
\mathscr{D}(x)
refers to the morphism in (
3.5
).

The sequence
(i_{1},\ldots,i_{k\ell})
corresponds to a reduced expression of the braid group element
\Delta^{k}\in\Br^{+}
. Define partial braid operators:

T_{\leq t}:=T_{i_{1}}\cdots T_{i_{t}},\quad\text{for }t\leq k\ell.

We claim that:

(4.15)

\psi(T_{\leq t-1}(x_{i_{t},0}))=S_{t},\quad\text{for }t\in[1,k\ell].

For
t\leq\ell
, this follows from
[
13
, Proposition 4.3]
and Theorem
4.6
. Since
T_{w_{0}}=\mathscr{D}\circ\nu
by
[
4
, Corollary 8.4]
, and given the definition of
S_{t}
, Equation (
4.15
) holds for all
t
. As the elements
S_{t}
generate
\mathcal{C}^{\mathbf{u},[1,k\ell]}
, it follows that
K_{q}(\mathscr{C}^{[1,k\ell],\mathbf{u}})
is contained in the image of
\psi
.

From Equation (
4.15
), we have:

\psi(E(\widehat{w_{0}},\mathbf{a}))=P_{(\mathcal{D},\widehat{w_{0}})}(\mathbf{a}).

By Propositions
4.9
and
3.7
, both
\mathcal{C}^{\mathbf{u},[1,k\ell]}
and
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}[0,k-1]=\widehat{\mathcal{A}}(\Delta^{k})
have bases
P_{(\mathcal{D},\widehat{w_{0}})}(\mathbf{a})
and
E(\widehat{w_{0}},\mathbf{a})
, respectively, indexed by the same set
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{[1,k\ell]}
. Since
\widehat{\psi}
maps these bases bijectively, it is a bijective homomorphism, completing the proof.
∎

4.5.4.
Main Results on Cluster Structures

We now present key results on the cluster algebra structure in Dynkin cases.

Theorem 4.14
.

In Dynkin cases, for any braid group element
b\in\operatorname{Br}^{+}
, Conjecture
3.12
holds.

Proof.

For a PBW pair
(\mathcal{D}_{\mathscr{L}},\widehat{w_{0}})
associated with a
Q
-datum
\mathscr{L}
, define:

\Delta^{m}=T_{i_{1}}\cdots T_{i_{\ell(w_{0})}}T_{i_{\ell(w_{0})+1}}\cdots T_{i_{m\ell(w_{0})}},

where
\ell(w_{0})
is the length of the longest Weyl group element. By Theorem
4.13
, the algebra:

K_{q}(\mathscr{C}^{[1,m\ell(w_{0})]})\cong\widehat{\mathcal{A}}(\Delta^{m})

is a quantum cluster algebra with initial seed:

\mathbf{t}_{\Delta^{m}}:=\{(\widehat{D}_{\mathbf{i}}[s,\ell(b)])_{s\in[1,\ell(b)]},\Lambda,B_{\text{GLS}},K^{\text{ex}}\},

where cluster monomials lie in the global basis
\mathbf{B}(\Delta^{m})
.

For any
b\in\operatorname{Br}^{+}
, there exist
m
and
u\in\operatorname{Br}^{+}
such that
ub=\Delta^{m}
. Let
\mathbf{i}=(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{j})
be a reduced expression for
\Delta^{m}
, with
\mathbf{j}
for
b
. Define seeds:

\mathbf{t}_{\Delta^{m}}:=\{(\widehat{D}_{\mathbf{i}}[s,m\ell(w_{0})])_{s\in[1,\ell(\Delta^{m})]},B_{GLS,\Delta^{m}},K^{\text{ex}}\},

\mathbf{t}_{b}:=\{(\widehat{D}_{\mathbf{i}}[s,\ell(b)])_{s\in[1,\ell(b)]},B_{GLS,b},J^{\text{ex}}\}.

Since the submatrix
B_{(K\setminus J)\times J^{\text{ex}}}=0
,
\mathbf{t}_{b}
is a subseed of
\mathbf{t}_{\Delta^{m}}
, implying:

\mathcal{A}(\mathbf{t}_{b})\subset\mathcal{A}(\mathbf{t}_{\Delta^{m}}).

To prove
\mathcal{A}(\mathbf{t}_{b})\subset\widehat{\mathcal{A}}(b)
, note
\widehat{D}[s,\ell(b)]\in\widehat{\mathcal{A}}(b)
. By induction, if all cluster variables in a seed
\mathbf{t}^{\prime}
are in
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}(b)
, then for a mutation
\mu_{k}(X_{k})
:

X_{k}\mu_{k}(X_{k})=D_{1}+D_{2},

where
D_{1},D_{2}
are cluster monomials in
\mathbf{t}^{\prime}
,
\mu_{k}(X_{k})=B(\mathbf{i},\mathbf{a})
, and:

\mathbf{a}+\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{i}}(X_{k})=\max\{\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{i}}(D_{1}),\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{i}}(D_{2})\}.

Since the supports of
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{i}}(X_{k})
,
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{i}}(D_{1})
, and
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{i}}(D_{2})
lie in
\mathbf{j}
, so does
\mathbf{a}
, ensuring
\mu_{k}(X_{k})\in\widehat{\mathcal{A}}(b)
. Thus,
\mathcal{A}(\mathbf{t}_{b})\subset\widehat{\mathcal{A}}(b)
.

Given Conjecture
3.10
holds in Dynkin cases, mutations in
J^{\text{ex}}
yield
\widehat{D}[s^{-},s]=E^{*}(\beta_{s})
for
i_{s}\in\mathbf{j}
. Since
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}(b)
is generated by
E^{*}(\beta_{s})
for
i_{s}\in\mathbf{j}
,
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}(b)\subset\mathcal{A}(\mathbf{t}_{b})
, completing the proof.
∎

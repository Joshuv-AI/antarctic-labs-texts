Epidemiology and Treatment Patterns of Stage III Resectable Melanoma Treated with Adjuvant Therapy: A Real-World Study Using the SNDS Database in France
Authors: Sandrine Monestier, Sebastien Branchoux, Louis Chillotti, François-Emery Cotté, Clémentine Curioz, Mélanie Goguillot, Stève Bénard, Carine Bellera, Nicolas Meyer
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
IntroductionBefore the advent of neoadjuvant immunotherapy (IO) in macroscopic disease, IO and targeted therapies were recommended as adjuvant therapies for the treatment of stage III resectable melanoma. However, real-world evidence on their use and outcomes remains limited. This study described treatment patterns and survival outcomes among patients with stage III resectable melanoma who received adjuvant therapy in France.MethodsThis retrospective cohort study used data from the exhaustive French national health data system (SNDS) to identify adults initiating adjuvant nivolumab (NIVO), pembrolizumab (PEM), or dabrafenib-trametinib (DT) between January 1, 2019, and December 31, 2021. Patients were followed until December 31, 2023, or death. Exclusion criteria included prior metastatic disease, other active cancers, or prior use of study drugs. Outcomes included treatment patterns at recurrence, described using Sankey diagrams, and survival (recurrence-free survival [RFS], overall survival [OS]), assessed using a Kaplan-Meier estimator.ResultsThe study population consisted of 2612 patients with resected stage III melanoma initiating an adjuvant therapy. Of them, 1483 (56.8%) received NIVO, 635 (24.3%) had PEM, and 494 (18.9%) had DT. Median (Q1–Q3) age was 64.0 (52.0–73.0) years for NIVO, 64.0 (51.0–74.0) years for PEM, and 58.0 (46.0–70.0) years for DT. Median follow-up was 39.5 months for NIVO, 38.9 months for PEM, and 36.9 for DT. At 36 months, RFS (95% CI) was 50.7% (48.0%–53.2%) for NIVO, 52.7% (48.6–56.7%) for PEM, and 48.3% (43.7–52.7%) for DT. Finally, 36-month OS (95% CI) was 81.8% (79.7–83.8%) for NIVO, 82.3% (79.0–85.2%) for PEM, and 74.4% (70.1–78.2%) for DT. Immunotherapy represented 75% of therapies at recurrence among patients with adjuvant NIVO/PEM and 55% among patients with adjuvant DT.ConclusionImmunotherapy is the cornerstone of adjuvant and recurrence treatment in stage III melanoma, representing the vast majority of adjuvant therapies and recurrence therapies. Despite effective therapies, recurrence remains frequent, with around half of relapses at 3 years, underscoring the need for continued optimization of treatment strategies.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s13555-026-01813-y.

Introduction

Melanoma is a malignant neoplasm originating from melanocytes. Although it accounts for only 10% of skin cancers [1], its high metastatic potential makes it one of the most lethal forms [2]. In 2023, melanoma prevalence in France exceeded 180,000 cases, with an incidence of almost 18,000 new cases and a median diagnosis age of 60 years in women and 66 in men [3, 4].

Stage III melanoma, as defined by the American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC), involves locoregional spread, particularly to lymph nodes [5], and affects roughly 10% of patients. Before adjuvant therapy implementation, the 10-year overall survival rate was around 50%, while 3-year recurrence-free survival was around 35% [2, 6, 7].

Recent advances have transformed the management of stage III resectable melanoma. Since the late 2010s, adjuvant immunotherapies (nivolumab, pembrolizumab) and targeted therapies (dabrafenib-trametinib) have been recommended for the treatment of stage III melanoma [8, 9]. Neoadjuvant approaches are emerging but remain inconsistently applied due to the lack of marketing authorization and reimbursement [10].

Despite these developments, real-world data on treatment patterns and long-term outcomes in stage III resectable melanoma are scarce. This study aimed (1) to describe treatment patterns in patients receiving adjuvant therapy for stage III melanoma and (2) to assess survival outcomes.

Methods

Design and Patient Selection

This was a non-interventional population-based study using secondary data from the French national health data system (Système National des Données de Santé, SNDS).

The French national health data system (namely Système National des Données de Santé [SNDS]) database includes comprehensive claims data for nearly 99% of French residents. Data encompass demographics, healthcare utilization, diagnoses, procedures, drug dispensations, and vital status in a pseudonymized way [11, 12].

Data on adults (≥ 18 years) initiating adjuvant nivolumab [NIVO], pembrolizumab [PEM], or dabrafenib-trametinib [DT] for resectable stage III melanoma between January 1, 2019, and December 31, 2021, were extracted. The index date was defined as the first dispensation of adjuvant therapy. Patients were followed for up to 4 years, until December 31, 2023, or death, whichever occurred first. A historical period of 5 years prior to the index date was used to collect treatment history and comorbidities of interest.

Eligibility criteria for the study population were at least one dispensation of a study drug, a diagnosis of melanoma (ICD-10 C43), and a melanoma resection within 3 months prior to the index date. Patients were excluded if they had other active cancers, no appropriate indication when treated with NIVO or PEM (not available for DT), prior use of study drugs, markers of stage IV melanoma, or inconsistent patient identifiers. Stage IV melanoma markers included specific therapies (ipilimumab [IPI], encorafenib + binimetinib [EB], or vemurafenib + cobimetinib [VC]), metastatic diagnosis (ICD-10 codes C78/79), and/or procedure codes for metastasectomy.

Outcomes and Variables

Melanoma recurrence was used to assess treatment patterns at recurrence as well as recurrence-free survival. It covered both local recurrences, defined as a new melanoma surgical procedure at least 1 month after the initial melanoma resection (before adjuvant therapy), and distant metastases, defined as a new marker of stage IV melanoma (see “Design and Patient Selection”).

The primary outcome was the therapeutic course at recurrence, referred to as treatment patterns. Treatment patterns consisted of the sequence of melanoma therapies of interest dispensed over time among patients having a treated recurrence. Therapies included in the description of treatment patterns were immunotherapies (NIVO, PEM, IPI), BRAF-MEK therapies (DT, EB, VC), and other less frequently used therapies, including chemotherapy sessions. Each therapy was identified using a reimbursement date, except for chemotherapy sessions, which were identified using a validated algorithm from Poizeau et al. [13]. For each therapy, treatment coverage was defined as the period from the first dispensation to the last dispensation before treatment discontinuation. Treatment discontinuation was defined as twice the theoretical coverage of a dispensation of a given therapy without a new dispensation (see Supplementary material).

The secondary outcomes were recurrence-free survival (RFS) and overall survival (OS), by adjuvant therapy. RFS was defined as the time from the index date to the first documented recurrence or death from any cause, whichever occurred first. OS was defined as the time from the index date to death from any cause. Patients who did not experience the event of interest were censored at the end of the study period (December 31, 2023). Codes and algorithms for patient selection and the definition of variables were derived from validated algorithms and are available in Supplementary material.

Sociodemographic characteristics included age and sex at the index date. Clinical characteristics at the index date included melanoma characteristics (anatomical location, time from first marker), treatment history (concomitant lymph node surgery, previous therapies and procedures [immunosuppressors, corticosteroids, transplantation, radiotherapy, melanoma resection]), hospital settings at resection, comorbidities (skin disorders, chronic respiratory diseases, liver diseases, cerebrovascular diseases, diabetes, myocardial infarction, autoimmune disease, renal disease, hepatic failure), and Charlson comorbidity index (CCI).

Statistical Analyses

Continuous data were summarized as mean (standard deviation–SD) or median (1st and 3rd quartiles – Q1-Q3), and categorical variables were presented as number and frequencies. Analyses were carried out using SAS® version 9.4 (SAS Institute Inc. Cary, NC, USA).

Sociodemographic and clinical characteristics were described at index date, by adjuvant therapy. Clinical characteristics were assessed over the 5-year historical period before adjuvant therapy initiation.

Treatment patterns among patients with treated recurrence were displayed using Sankey diagrams. Treatment patterns were assessed overall and were broken down by the time to recurrence. In the absence of consensus to define the type and timing of recurrences, a 6-month cutoff was used in this study, based on expert opinion and recent data from the literature, corresponding to half the theoretical duration of the adjuvant therapy. It allowed us to distinguish early recurrence (< 6 months, covering primary resistance, early progression, or treatment failure) and late recurrence (≥ 6 months). Also, for each therapy, crude median treatment coverage was described.

Survival analyses (RFS, OS) were conducted using the Kaplan-Meier method. Survival rates and median survival along with the corresponding 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were reported. Median follow-up duration was estimated using reverse Kaplan-Meier method.

Ethical Approval

The study was set up in accordance with the International Society for Pharmacoepidemiology (ISPE) Guidelines and with the principles set out in the Declaration of Helsinki.

As part of the regulatory submission for claims studies based on French SNDS data, this study has been approved by the French ethics and scientific committee (namely Comité Éthique et Scientifique pour les Recherches, les Études et les Évaluations dans le domaine de la Santé, CESREES), dossier 11,641,819, by the French data protection agency (namely Commission Informatique et Libertés–CNIL), and Id HGT/VCS/AR252848 pursuant to Article 13 of the law and Resolution No. 2019-021 of February 28, 2019 (authorization 923196).

Furthermore, an independent scientific committee was involved from protocol development onward to ensure the scientific and methodological robustness, as well as the clinical relevance of the study and its results.

Results

Study Population

Of 92,493 patients with at least one dispensation of NIVO, PEM, or DT between 2019 and 2021, 2612 patients with sIII melanoma treated in adjuvant settings met the selection criteria. NIVO was the most common adjuvant therapy (n = 1483, 56.8%), followed by PEM (n = 635, 24.3%) and DT (n = 494, 18.9%). In 2019, 516 patients initiated an adjuvant therapy for stage III melanoma and were included, while slightly more than 1000 patients were included each year in both 2020 and 2021. The proportion of patients treated with NIVO peaked in 2019 (69.2%) and appeared to stabilize thereafter (52.6% in 2020 and 54.8% in 2021). Conversely, DT use remained relatively stable and increasing modestly from 17.1% in 2019 to 20.0% in 2021 (Fig. 1).Fig. 1Patient disposition. Nivolumab

Treatment Patterns

Among the 1483 patients who received adjuvant NIVO, 616 (41.5%) experienced a treated recurrence (local or distant) during follow-up. At recurrence, immunotherapy was used in 483 (78.4%) patients. NIVO monotherapy was the most common regimen (n = 267, 43.3%), followed by NIVO plus IPI (n = 181, 29.4%). BRAF-MEK targeted therapy was used in 109 (17.7%) patients, while chemotherapy and other therapies were present in 24 (3.9%). Among the 616 patients receiving a first therapy after recurrence, 251 (40.7%) experienced an early recurrence (< 6 months post-index). The main therapies in this group were NIVO alone (62.9%), NIVO + IPI (19.1%), and BRAF-MEK therapy (12.0%), with a median treatment duration of 10.2 (4.9–13.3) months. Conversely, 365 (59.3%) patients had a late recurrence (≥ 6 months post-index) and were treated with NIVO + IPI (36.4%), NIVO alone (29.9%), or BRAF-MEK therapy (21.4%), with a median duration of 11.0 (3.2–17.3) months (Fig. 2B, C).

Overall, 227 (36.8%) patients received a second post-recurrence therapy, primarily NIVO + IPI (n = 75, 33.0%). This second therapy lasted for a median of 4.1 (2.3–10.2) months.

In total, 183 (29.7%) patients who recurred after adjuvant NIVO received at least one BRAF-MEK therapy at any point during follow-up.

Survival Outcomes

Median follow-up duration was 39.5 (38.7–40.4) months. At 12 months, the RFS rate (95% CI) was 68.0% (65.5–70.3%) and declined to 50.7% (48.0–53.2%) at 36 months. Overall, 16.1% of patients experienced a local recurrence, and 39.9% developed distant metastases during follow-up. Pembrolizumab

Treatment Patterns

Among the 635 patients who received adjuvant PEM, 247 (38.9%) experienced a treated recurrence (local or distant) during follow-up. At recurrence, immunotherapy was used in 186 (75.3%) patients. NIVO was the most common regimen, used with IPI (n = 186, 30.4%) or as monotherapy (n = 19, 7.7%). PEM accounted for 90 (36.4%) patients at recurrence. BRAF-MEK targeted therapy was used in 52 (21.1%) patients. Other therapies, including chemotherapy, were observed in < 11 (< 4.5%) patients. Among the 247 patients receiving a first therapy after recurrence, 86 (34.8%) had an early recurrence (< 6 months post-index). The main therapies in this group were PEM (59.3%), NIVO + IPI (24.4%), and BRAF-MEK therapy (13.9%), with a median treatment duration of 7.2 (4.1–13.8) months. Conversely, 161 (65.2%) patients had a late recurrence (≥ 6 months post-index) and were treated with NIVO + IPI (33.5%), PEM (24.2%) or BRAF-MEK therapy (24.8%), with a median duration of 8.9 (3.0–14.3) months (Fig. 4B, C).

Overall, 97 (39.3%) patients received a second post-recurrence therapy, primarily BRAF-MEK (n = 37, 38.1%) or NIVO + IPI (n = 26, 26.8%). This second therapy lasted for a median of 3.6 (1.9–9.8) months.

In total, 82 (33.2%) patients who recurred after adjuvant PEM received at least one BRAF-MEK therapy at any point during follow-up.

Survival Outcomes

Median follow-up duration was 38.9 (37.4–39.6) months. At 12 months, the RFS rate (95% CI) was 74.5% (70.9–77.7%) and declined to 52.7% (48.6–56.7%) at 36 months. Overall, 17.7% of patients experienced a local recurrence, and 34.4% developed distant metastases during follow-up. Overall survival (OS) rates were 95.7% (93.9–97.1%) at 12 months and 82.3% (79.0–85.2%) at 36 months (Fig. 3C/D) (Table 2).

Discussion

To the best of our knowledge, this is the first real-world study to describe treatment patterns and survival outcomes among patients with stage III resectable melanoma receiving adjuvant therapy in France, with up to 4 years of follow-up.

Because the SNDS database is a claims database, primary medical records, including some clinical variables (e.g., TNM stage, ECOG performance status), are unavailable. Consequently, metastatic status was determined using a proxy based on a specific algorithm, both for the patient selection and the identification of distant metastases. Hence, while a potential for underestimation remains, it is expected to be limited [14, 15]. All three therapies (NIVO, PEM, and DT) were indicated in both adjuvant (stage III) and metastatic (stage IV) melanoma. For the identification of the study population, patients with markers of stage IV disease—such as hospital diagnoses of metastases, metastasectomy, or stage IV-specific therapies—were excluded. In addition, for NIVO and PEM, an additional criterion was applied, and over 2000 patients with uncertain or non-adjuvant use were excluded. As this information was not available for DT, residual inclusion of stage IV patients cannot be excluded, and results for DT should be interpreted with caution. Finally, in 2019, only ~ 500 patients were included, versus > 1000 in 2020 and 2021. This was because all three therapies received adjuvant indication approvals in 2019 (DT in February, NIVO in July, PEM in November).

Similarly, TNM sub-staging (IIIa-IIIc-IIId), a significant prognostic factor that guides the therapeutic strategy, is absent in SNDS. However, adjuvant and subsequent therapies could be described regardless of this information.

More generally, the lack of clinical data precluded analysis of outcomes by disease burden, a key determinant of adjuvant therapy selection and intensity of follow-up and did not allow for adjustment variables [9]. Hence, no direct comparisons between therapies were carried out, and treatment patterns and survival outcomes were described only by therapy without further stratification.

Despite these limitations, the study population (n = 2612) is expected to be representative of patients with stage III resectable melanoma managed in France, particularly regarding adjuvant immunotherapy use.

Sociodemographic and clinical characteristics were consistent with the literature. As previously described, BRAF mutations are associated with younger age and more aggressive disease; this was reflected in our cohort, with younger patients in the DT group (median 58 vs. 61 years) [16, 17]. Regarding comorbidities, skin disorders such as psoriasis or atopic dermatitis were observed in ~ 15% of patients, higher than their prevalence in the general French population (2–6%), supporting their known association with melanoma risk [18, 19].

This study highlighted the importance of immunotherapy in the management of adjuvant melanoma and in the management of subsequent recurrences.

Immunotherapy accounted for > 80% of adjuvant therapies, with NIVO representing 70% of all adjuvant immunotherapies. In contrast, adjuvant DT was prescribed in only 20% of patients—below the expected BRAF mutation rate of 40–60% [20, 21]. A similar predominance of immunotherapy has been observed in the UK, where it represents 70–80% of adjuvant therapies [22].

Recent guidelines incorporating neoadjuvant plus adjuvant immunotherapy combinations may further reinforce the preference for immunotherapy, even among patients with a BRAF mutation [8, 23].

At recurrence, immunotherapy remained the predominant approach, representing approximately 75% of treated recurrences. NIVO, alone or combined with IPI, was the most common regimen, used in 73% of patients initially treated with adjuvant NIVO, 38% after adjuvant PEM, and 44% after adjuvant DT. This persistence in immunotherapy use aligns with other real-world findings among patients who recurred after adjuvant therapy [24, 25]. The limited use of other BRAF-MEK combinations (EB, VC) likely reflects prescription habits, as well as a more recent market availability in France, in the late 2010s. As noted above, the lack of detailed sub-staging and mutational data limited our ability to discuss treatment strategies at the time of recurrence. This was notably the case for patients with BRAF mutations who received immunotherapy, for whom BRAF-MEK inhibition may have been a more appropriate option in the absence of contraindication. Nonetheless, this constraint does not undermine the robustness of the present study, which aimed to describe treatment patterns rather than the clinical rationale underlying therapeutic choices. Future analyses linking SNDS data with registries containing clinical information may provide deeper insights into factors influencing specific treatment patterns.

When analyzed by time to recurrence, a 6-month cutoff was used to distinguish early and late recurrences. Using this threshold, early recurrences were expected to encompass a heterogeneous group including primary treatment resistance, treatment failure, and other early recurrence patterns, particularly locoregional or oligometastatic recurrences treated with secondary resection. While absence of clinical data prevented us from formally identifying the type of recurrence, we observed that more than half of early recurrences (< 6 months post-index) were managed with the same regimen as the adjuvant phase. Similar to mutational status, SNDS data do not allow for the distinction between the reason for recurrence. However, this lack of information has a limited impact given the descriptive objective of this study. Nonetheless, hypotheses can be drawn based on the therapies received. As treatment switch or escalation was expected in case of treatment resistance/failure, these patients treated with the same therapy may represent local or locoregional relapses managed as “second adjuvant therapies” (potentially with ad-hoc radiotherapy), not necessarily indicative of treatment resistance/failure [26]. Conversely, the remaining early recurrences and late recurrences (≥ 6 months post-index) more often involved therapy escalation or switch, particularly to NIVO + IPI, likely reflecting distant disease progression or resistance phenomena (e.g., PD-1 pathway exhaustion or BRAF resistance) [27, 28].

This study also confirmed the marked decline in the use of chemotherapy in melanoma management. A prior French real-world analysis by Poizeau et al. documented a major reduction in chemotherapy use between 2011 and 2017, replaced by immunotherapy and targeted therapy [4]. Our results corroborate this trend, with chemotherapy representing only a negligible proportion of recurrence therapies—consistent with other European reports [23].

Beyond clinical determinants, non-clinical factors may influence therapy selection, including logistical and patient preference considerations. For example, a once-monthly infusion may be preferred over daily oral dosing for convenience or adherence [29, 30], whereas distance to the hospital may favor oral BRAF-MEK therapy, which is dispensed in community pharmacies [31, 32].

Survival outcomes with adjuvant immunotherapy were consistent with previous studies, with a 24-month RFS rate of approximately 60% and 50% at 36 months. This reinforces the robustness of the proxy used for metastasis identification for patients treated with immunotherapy, as depicted above. In the French Adjumel observational study, 297 patients treated with adjuvant NIVO achieved a 24-month RFS rate of 53% [33]. European series reported 36-month rates between 50 and 55% [16, 17]. According to the most recent update of the NIVO pivotal trial, median RFS was reached at 61 months [34]. For PEM, the 36-month RFS rate ranged from 48 to 58% [16, 35].

In contrast, the 36-month RFS rate among patients treated with adjuvant DT appeared lower (48.3%) than in international real-world studies, which reported values > 60% [16, 36]. This discrepancy may reflect residual inclusion of stage IV patients, leading to an underestimation of survival. Hospital-based metastatic data are incompletely captured in the SNDS [14, 15]; although our algorithm combined hospital diagnoses, metastasectomy, and stage IV–specific therapies to optimize detection, some classification bias may occur. Moreover, only NIVO and PEM records included explicit indication fields enabling accurate exclusion of metastatic cases, which was not possible for DT.

Despite the limitations described above, this study provided valuable evidence on the real-world management of stage III adjuvant melanoma, notably for immunotherapy, in both adjuvant and recurrent settings, based on the disease progression, across a very large population.

Conclusion

Immunotherapy represented the cornerstone of stage III resectable melanoma management—in both adjuvant and recurrent settings. Immunotherapy accounted for the vast majority of adjuvant initiations (predominantly NIVO) and recurrence therapies (often NIVO + IPI). Despite these advances, recurrence still occurred, with around half of patients presenting with a recurrence at 36 months. These findings reinforce the continued need for optimized patient selection, early recurrence detection, and real-world data to inform future therapeutic strategies, notably neoadjuvant therapy in melanoma and stage II adjuvant therapy.

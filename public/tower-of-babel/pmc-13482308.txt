Energy-efficient secure routing in wireless sensor networks using fuzzy inference clustering and attention-based multi-scale deep learning
Authors: M Ishwarya Niranjana, N Kumareshan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractWireless Sensor Networks (WSNs) are widely used in applications such as environmental monitoring, smart agriculture, healthcare, industrial automation, and military surveillance. However, their performance and lifetime are often limited by energy constraints, inefficient clustering, insecure routing, and vulnerability to network attacks. Existing approaches frequently suffer from high energy consumption, increased routing overhead, poor scalability, and limited accuracy in detecting intrusions. To address these issues, the proposed approach employs a Mamdani-type Fuzzy Inference System (FIS) for adaptive cluster formation, ensuring balanced and stable clustering. Cluster Head (CH) selection is carried out using Dynamic Adaptive Fig Tree–Wasp Symbiotic Coevolutionary Optimization (DA-FTWSCO) to improve energy efficiency and extend network lifetime. For secure communication, the Adaptive Trust-Synchronized Packet Control Protocol (ATSPCP) is used to compute trust values, followed by optimal path selection using the Improved Grizzly Bear Fat Increase Optimizer (IGBFIO). Additionally, an Enhanced Multi-scale Dilated MobileNet with Attention Mechanism (EMSD-MobileNet-AM) is utilized for effective intrusion detection, enabling accurate identification of denial-of-service (DoS) and zero-day attacks. Simulation outcomes demonstrate that the proposed method achieves low energy consumption (1.02 J), high throughput (0.93 Mbps), low end-to-end delay (4.0 ms), and a high attack detection rate (98%). The results demonstrate that the proposed framework outperforms several existing methods in terms of efficiency, security, and scalability.Keywords: Wireless sensor network (WSN), CH selection, Routing, Intrusion detectionSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Wireless Sensor Networks (WSNs) have emerged as a key enabling technology for applications such as environmental monitoring, smart agriculture, healthcare, industrial automation, and military surveillance1. These networks consist of numerous low-cost, energy-constrained sensor nodes that collaboratively sense, process, and transmit data to a base station. Despite their wide applicability, WSNs face significant challenges related to limited battery power, dynamic topology, and vulnerability to security threats, which directly impact network performance and lifetime2,3.

Among these challenges, energy efficiency remains a critical concern, as sensor nodes typically operate on non-rechargeable batteries. Clustering-based communication has been widely adopted to reduce energy consumption, where nodes are grouped, and a Cluster Head (CH) is responsible for data aggregation and transmission4. However, inefficient cluster formation and suboptimal CH selection often lead to unbalanced energy consumption, increased communication overhead, and premature node failure. In addition, routing plays a vital role in determining reliable and energy-efficient data transmission paths, yet conventional routing protocols often fail to incorporate security and trust awareness, making them susceptible to attacks such as denial-of-service, selective forwarding, and packet dropping5–7.

Recent studies have explored bio-inspired and optimization-based algorithms such as Salp Swarm Algorithm–Based Fault Node Detection (SSA-FND), Spider Wasp Optimizer (SWO), Hybrid Bio-Inspired Ant Colony System (HBACS), Multi-Objective Grey Wolf Optimization (MOGWO), and Improved Fruit Fly Optimization Algorithm (IFFOA) to improve clustering and routing performance8.

While these methods show improvements in specific metrics, they suffer from limitations such as premature convergence, high routing overhead, lack of adaptability to dynamic network conditions, and insufficient integration of security mechanisms. Furthermore, most existing works treat clustering, routing, and intrusion detection as independent problems, resulting in suboptimal overall network performance9,10. Existing approaches, including SSA-FND and SWO, primarily focus on fault detection and optimization but do not ensure balanced energy-aware clustering. HBACS and MOGWO improve routing and multi-objective optimization; however, they often incur high computational overhead and lack robustness in dynamic environments. Similarly, IFFOA provides faster convergence but fails to effectively address trust-based secure routing and advanced attack detection. Moreover, current intrusion detection methods are often decoupled from routing decisions, leading to increased energy consumption and reduced detection efficiency. Therefore, the absence of a unified framework that jointly optimizes clustering, secure routing, and intrusion detection remains a critical research gap in WSNs11–14.

This paper suggests an integrated cluster-based routing and intrusion detection system for WSNs to address these issues. To ensure balanced and stable clustering, the suggested method uses a Mamdani-type FIS for adaptive cluster creation. To increase energy efficiency and extend network lifetime, CH selection is carried out utilising DA-FTWSCO. The ATSPCP is used to compute trust values for secure routing, and the IGBFIO is then used to choose the best path. Additionally, an EMSD-MobileNet-AM is used for efficient intrusion detection, making it possible to recognise DoS and zero-day attacks.

The suggested algorithms were chosen because of their combined merits in tackling the main issues of safe routing, energy efficiency, and precise intrusion detection in WSNs. Compared to traditional deterministic approaches, the Mamdani-type FIS is used for cluster creation because it can handle uncertainty and dynamically adapt to changing network conditions, producing balanced and stable clusters. The DA-FTWSCO algorithm is used for CH selection due to its excellent exploration–exploitation balance, which allows for the effective discovery of ideal CHs while reducing energy consumption and extending network lifetime. To ensure secure routing, the ATSPCP is utilized for accurate trust evaluation, effectively mitigating malicious node participation, while the IGBFIO enhances route optimization by avoiding local optima and reducing routing overhead. Furthermore, the EMSD-MobileNet-AM is selected for intrusion detection due to its lightweight architecture, capability to capture multi-scale spatial features, and improved feature representation through attention mechanisms, leading to higher detection accuracy, including for zero-day attacks. In comparison with existing approaches, the proposed method demonstrates novelty by frameworklies in the tight integration of fuzzy-based cluster formation, advanced bio-inspired optimization techniques, trust-aware secure routing, and attention-based deep learning within a unified architecture. This holistic design not only improves individual network functions but also ensures their coordinated operation, resulting in enhanced energy efficiency, robustness against attacks, scalability, and overall network performance.

This aids in routing path selection and clustering. The proposed method is evaluated against LEACH and two multi-hop versions, DMHT LEACH and EDMHT LEACH, to demonstrate its effectiveness. According to our tests, EEMLCR performs better than LEACH and its multi-hop equivalents (DMHT LEACH and EDMHT LEACH). Darabkh et al., the load-driven, location- and power-aware equilibrium optimiser (EO)-based clustering and routing protocol (LDLP-EO-CRP) was introduced to optimise the CH selection and energy cost. In the sustainable smart city implementation of LDLP-EO-CRP, there are smart trash pickup, energy-saving street lighting, air monitoring, and smart traffic management. LDLP-EO-CRP provides real-time data collection and monitoring, which are simultaneously presented, and the IoT devices operate in a reliable manner, which further encourages the sustainability of the city and urban resilience. The distance and load of potential relay nodes dictate the data relayed to the sink by hopping in LDLP-EO-CRP. Hassan et al. proposed several cluster-based processes of selection, which rely on the Stable Election Protocol (SEP) and Distributed Energy-Efficient Clustering (DEEC). Summary of existing methods.

Alongside the frequently examined optimization-based methods, other significant clustering and methods of routing have been introduced to improve energy economy and scalability in wireless sensor networks (WSNs). Sert et al.33 presented MOFCA, a multi-objective fuzzy clustering system designed to optimise energy usage and extend network longevity via fuzzy-based decision-making. Tarhani et al.34 introduced SEECH, an adaptable and energy-efficient clustering hierarchy method aimed at improving cluster formation and minimising communication overhead in extensive networks. Bhushan and Sahoo35 devised E2SR2, an acknowledgment-based routing protocol that integrates mobile sinks and rechargeable sensors to enhance reliability and energy efficiency. In a separate study, ISFC-BLS36 combines fuzzy clustering with balanced load sub-cluster creation to enhance stability and energy distribution. Moreover, FLEAC37 utilises fuzzy logic in conjunction with optimisation methods to improve cluster head selection.

Although these protocols demonstrate improvements in specific aspects such as clustering efficiency, load balancing, and energy-aware routing, they exhibit certain limitations. Most of these methods focus primarily on either clustering or routing, without providing a unified framework that incorporates security and intrusion detection. Additionally, their adaptability to highly dynamic network conditions remains limited, and they lack mechanisms for identifying advanced attacks such as denial-of-service and zero-day intrusions. In contrast, the proposed framework integrates adaptive fuzzy-based clustering, optimized cluster head selection, trust-aware secure routing, and deep learning–based intrusion detection into a single architecture, thereby addressing the limitations of existing protocols and achieving improved overall network performance.

Methodology

The suggested approach is meticulously crafted by merging complimentary techniques to tackle the primary difficulties of energy efficiency, safe routing, and intrusion detection in wireless sensor networks (WSNs). The Mamdani-type FIS is used for adaptive cluster formation because of its proficiency in managing uncertainty and fluctuating node conditions, leading to balanced clusters and diminished communication overhead. The DA-FTWSCO algorithm is utilized for CH selection because of its strong exploration–exploitation capability, enabling optimal CH selection with improved energy distribution and extended network lifetime.For secure routing, the ATSPCP computes dynamic trust values to identify reliable nodes, while the IGBFIO determines optimal routing paths by avoiding local optima and minimizing routing overhead. In addition, the EMSD-MobileNet-AM is adopted for intrusion detection due to its lightweight design and ability to extract multi-scale features with enhanced attention, leading to accurate detection of both known and zero-day attacks.

The suggested approach is characterised by its cohesive integration of clustering, routing, and security procedures inside a singular framework, in contrast to most existing systems that treat these components separately. The suggested solution, in contrast to existing approaches, concurrently optimises energy efficiency, trust-aware security, and threat detection in a coordinated fashion. The integration of coevolutionary optimisation for CH selection, trust-synchronized routing, and attention-based multi-scale deep learning establishes a multi-layered optimisation approach that markedly enhances flexibility, resilience, and overall network performance. This comprehensive design distinctly distinguishes the suggested technique from current methodologies and asserts its superiority regarding efficiency, scalability, and security.

To ensure a clear and systematic representation of the proposed methodology, the overall process is organized into a unified and sequential algorithmic framework. The proposed system operates in a step-by-step manner, where the output of each stage serves as the input to the subsequent stage, thereby ensuring a continuous and coherent data flow across all modules. Initially, the network is deployed with a set of sensor nodes, and relevant parameters such as residual energy, node distance, and communication cost are initialized. In the first stage, a Mamdani-type FIS is applied to perform adaptive cluster formation. This step groups sensor nodes into balanced clusters by considering multiple input parameters, thereby reducing communication overhead and ensuring efficient network organization.

Workflow of proposed methodology.

The clustered network is then passed to the CH selection phase, where the DA-FTWSCO algorithm is employed. This stage identifies optimal CHs based on energy and network conditions, ensuring uniform energy distribution and prolonging network lifetime. Following CH selection, the secure routing phase is initiated. The ATSPCP computes trust values for each node by analyzing their behavior and communication reliability. These trust values, along with network topology information, are then provided as input to the IGBFIO, which determines optimal and secure routing paths with reduced delay and energy consumption.

During data transmission, the network traffic is continuously monitored by the intrusion detection module. An EMSD-MobileNet-AM is utilized to analyze traffic patterns and detect malicious activities, including denial-of-service and zero-day attacks. Identified malicious nodes are isolated to ensure secure communication. Overall, this sequential integration of clustering, CH selection, trust-based routing, and intrusion detection establishes a coherent workflow, where each stage is functionally connected. This structured design not only improves the clarity of the algorithmic representation but also highlights the coordinated operation of all components within the proposed framework.

Cluster formation using Mamdani-type fuzzy inference system (FIS)

The Mamdani-type Fuzzy Inference System used for adaptive clustering is adopted from existing fuzzy-based WSN approaches with modifications for balanced CH formation38. Cluster formation in WSNsis an important factor in minimizing the energy consumption, load balancing of the nodes and maximizing network life. In order to overcome the weaknesses of fixed clustering schemes, this work suggests a scheme of energy-conscious cluster formation, grounded on a Fuzzy Inference System of the Mamdani style. Mamdani model is chosen based on the interpretability, the ability to withstand uncertainty and the ability to apply such a model in resource-constrained systems.

The given method allocates each non-cluster sensor node to a suitableCH by taking into consideration several criteria at once with the help of fuzzy logic. Contrary to the hard clustering, fuzzy decision process allows flexible and adaptive cluster formation at dynamic network conditions.

Suppose that a WSN is comprised of Nsensor nodes randomly distributed on a 2-dimensional sense field. Suppose that CCHs are present after the election stage of CH. All non-cluster sensor nodes analyze the connection with all CHs with the proposed Mamdani-type FIS.

Intrusion detection using enhanced multi-scale dilated MobileNet with attention mechanism (EMSD-MobileNet-AM)

The EMSD-MobileNet-AM framework is developed by extending the existing Multi-scale Dilated MobileNet with Attention Mechanism for intrusion detection in WSN environments42. The choice of EMSD-MobileNet-AM is driven by its appropriateness for resource-limited WSN situations and its efficacy in identifying intricate attack patterns. In contrast to sequence-based models like Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) and Bi-directional LSTM (Bi-LSTM), which are resource-intensive and result in increased memory and latency demands, the proposed EMSD-MobileNet-AM employs a lightweight architecture utilising depthwise separated convolutions. This substantially decreases the parameter count and computational expense, rendering it more suitable for real-time intrusion detection in energy-constrained wireless sensor network contexts. Moreover, whereas Transformer-based models exhibit robust capabilities in capturing global relationships, they often require large training datasets and substantial computational resources, which may be impractical for Wireless Sensor Network (WSN) implementations. The proposed model utilises multi-scale dilated convolutions to efficiently capture both local and global spatial characteristics without significantly increasing complexity. The incorporation of an attention mechanism improves feature representation by emphasising the most pertinent patterns linked to anomalous or malevolent behaviour. EMSD-MobileNet-AM offers a superior balance between detection accuracy and processing economy compared to traditional deep learning models. Its capacity to extract distinctive features across different scales, coupled with attention-based feature refinement, enhances the detection of both known and novel (zero-day) attacks. Consequently, the suggested model is exceptionally appropriate for scalable, real-time, and energy-efficient intrusion detection in wireless sensor network scenarios.

The EMSD-MobileNet-AM proposed is aimed at offering real-time intrusion detection in WSNs with special attention given to detecting DoS attacks and abnormal traffic behaviors, which would otherwise be missed during routing and trust evaluation stages. Discussion

Advantage

The performance evaluation of the proposed framework is conducted by varying the number of sensor nodes from 100 to 500, and the results are compared with existing approaches, including SSA-FND, SWO, HBACS, MOGWO, and IFFOA. The obtained results demonstrate consistent and significant improvements across all performance metrics, highlighting the effectiveness of the proposed integrated design. The proposed method shows minimum energy consumption across all network sizes, achieving 1.02 J at 500 nodes compared to others. This improvement is primarily attributed to the balanced cluster formationusing the fuzzy inference system and the optimized CH selection via DA-FTWSCO, which ensures uniform energy distribution and prevents early node depletion. Unlike SSA-FND and SWO, which suffer from uneven energy usage, the proposed approach maintains energy stability even as network density increases. The throughput performance remains consistently high, with the proposed method achieving 0.93 Mbps at 500 nodes, outperforming all baseline methods. This is due to the combined effect of trust-aware routing and optimal path selection, which minimizes packet loss and ensures reliable data transmission. Existing methods show reduced throughput with increasing node density due to routing inefficiency and congestion.

A significant reduction in E2ED is observed, with the proposed framework achieving only 4.0 ms at 500 nodes. This improvement is a direct result of efficient routing path optimization using IGBFIO, which reduces unnecessary retransmissions and avoids congested routes. Similarly, jitter is minimized, indicating stable and consistent packet delivery, which is critical for real-time WSN applications. The proposed method also demonstrates superior network lifetime, reaching up to 5000 rounds at higher node densities. This can be attributed to the synergistic interaction between energy-aware clustering and optimized CH selection, which collectively reduce energy hotspots. Existing approaches such as MOGWO and IFFOA show competitive performance but still fall short due to limited adaptability to dynamic network conditions.

From a reliability perspective, the PDR exceeds 94% even at high node densities, while the PLR and BER remain significantly lower than those of other methods. These improvements highlight the effectiveness of the trust-based routing mechanism, which filters unreliable nodes and ensures secure and efficient data transmission. In terms of security performance, the proposed framework achieves an attack detection rate of up to 98%, significantly outperforming existing approaches. This is primarily due to the EMSD-MobileNet-AM model, which effectively captures multi-scale features and leverages attention mechanisms to identify both known and zero-day attacks. Additionally, the improved security analysis metric indicates enhanced resilience against malicious activities. Furthermore, the routing overhead ratio remains minimal compared to other methods, demonstrating that the proposed approach achieves improved performance without introducing excessive communication overhead. This balance between efficiency and overhead is critical for large-scale WSN deployments. Overall, the superior performance of the proposed framework can be attributed to its integrated and multi-layered design, where clustering, routing, and intrusion detection operate in a coordinated manner. Unlike existing methods that optimize individual components, the proposed approach ensures holistic optimization, resulting in enhanced energy efficiency, reduced latency, improved reliability, and stronger security.

Limitations

Despite the promising performance of the proposed framework, certain limitations must be acknowledged. One potential concern is scalability in very large-scale WSNs. Although the clustering mechanism and optimization strategies are designed to reduce energy consumption and routing overhead, the computational complexity associated with multiple optimization stages and deep learning-based intrusion detection may increase as the number of nodes grows significantly. This could lead to higher processing delays and resource utilization, particularly in densely deployed networks.Additionally, variations in node density can impact the effectiveness of clustering and routing decisions. In sparse networks, limited node availability may reduce clustering efficiency and increase communication distances, thereby affecting energy consumption. Conversely, in highly dense networks, frequent cluster reformation and increased contention among nodes may introduce additional overhead. The performance of the trust-based routing mechanism may also be influenced by dynamic topology changes and intermittent connectivity.Furthermore, while the proposed deep learning model is optimized for efficiency, its deployment in real-time scenarios may still require careful resource management or edge-assisted processing. Future work will focus on enhancing scalability through lightweight optimization techniques, adaptive clustering thresholds, and distributed learning strategies to ensure consistent performance across diverse network sizes and densities.

Conclusion

This work introduces a remarkable and smart architecture that could be used to improve energy saving, security of routing, and intrusion detection in WSNs. Conventional WSN methods have high energy consumption, poor packet delivery and poor security due to lack of battery power, dynamic topology, and vulnerability to malicious attacks. The proposed framework integrates fuzzy logic, bio-inspired optimization, trust-based routing, and deep learning in order to help overcome these problems. To achieve adaptive cluster formation, in the first place, a Mamdani-type FIS is employed to guarantee the balanced clusters and minimize the unnecessary communication overhead. TheDA-FTWSCOalgorithm is used to optimize selection of CH to select CHs that consume less energy and do not fail soon to maximize the life time of a network. To achieve a secure communication, the ATSPCP calculates the trust values according to the behavior of the nodes to assist in isolating malicious or unreliable nodes. IGBFIO is then used on the routing path selection in order to reduce transmission energy and delay. Also, anEMSD-MobileNet-AM is included to be used as intrusion detector which allows the accurate detection of DoS and zero-day attacks with minimal computational complexity. The results of the simulation show that the proposed framework delivers an attack detection rate of 98, energy consumption of 1.02 J, throughput of 0.93 Mbps, end-to-end delay 4.0 ms and a packet delivery ratio of over 96%. The lifetime of the network is enhanced a notch higher and the overhead of routing is minimized. These findings prove that the offered solution is secure, energy saving and scalable and can be applied to the large-scale and real-time WSN applications.Despite these advantages, further research is required to enhance the practical applicability of the proposed framework. Future work will focus on improving scalability for ultra-large and highly dynamic WSN environments by developing lightweight and distributed optimization techniques. Additionally, adaptive mechanisms can be incorporated to handle varying node densities and mobility patterns more effectively. The integration of edge or fog computing paradigms may further reduce computational overhead and enable real-time deployment. Moreover, extending the intrusion detection model to handle a wider range of sophisticated and evolving attack types, as well as validating the framework on real-world testbeds, will be important directions for future investigation.

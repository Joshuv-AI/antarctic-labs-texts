Stability and Bifurcation Analysis of Two-term Fractional Difference Equation
Janardhan Chevala, Sachin Bhalekar

Abstract
We consider the linear equation including two fractional order difference operators, viz. Δ α \Delta^{\alpha} and Δ β \Delta^{\beta} , 0 < β < α ≤ 1 0<\beta<\alpha\leq 1 . The sequence representation will be provided to find the solution in an easier way. The Z-transform will be used to find the boundary of the stable region in the complex plane. If the coefficient of the operator Δ β \Delta^{\beta} is negative (near 0), then we observe that the boundary curve has multiple points generating multiple stability regions. We provide all possible bifurcations in terms of parameters. An ample number of examples will be provided to support the results.

3
The model

In this work, we consider the two-term linear fractional difference system as

\Delta^{\alpha}x(t)+a\,\Delta^{\beta}x(t+\alpha-\beta)=f(x(t+\alpha-1))-x(t+\alpha-1),

x(0)=x_{0},

(2)

with
f(x)=bx
, where
0<\beta<\alpha\leq 1
,
t\in\mathbb{N}_{1-\alpha}
and
a\in\mathbb{R}
,
b\in\mathbb{C}
are constants.

In this paper, we study the stability analysis of a two-term fractional difference equation (
2
).

Now, we provide an equivalence between the initial value problem (
2
) and a sequence
x(n)
. The sequence representation will be extremely useful while solving examples numerically.

Theorem 3.1

The initial value problem (
2
) is equivalent to the sequence representation

\begin{split}x(n)&=\left(\frac{\alpha+a\beta+(b-1)}{a+1}\right)x(n-1)\\
&+\frac{1}{a+1}\sum_{s=0}^{n-2}\frac{1}{(n-s)!}\left(\frac{\alpha\Gamma(n-s-\alpha)}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}+\frac{a\beta\Gamma(n-s-\beta)}{\Gamma(1-\beta)}\right)x(s)\end{split}

(3)

where
n=1,2,3,...
.

Proof.
Consider
\Delta^{\alpha}x(t)+a\,\Delta^{\beta}x(t+\alpha-\beta)=(b-1)\,x(t+\alpha-1)
.

By the def. of Caputo fractional difference, the above equation is equivalent to

\Delta^{-(1-\alpha)}(\Delta x(t))+a\,\Delta^{-(1-\beta)}(\Delta x(t+\alpha-\beta))=(b-1)\,x(t+\alpha-1)
.

Applying the def. of fractional sum,

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\left(\sum_{s=0}^{t+\alpha}\frac{\Gamma(t+1-s)}{\Gamma(t+1-s+\alpha)}x(s)-\sum_{s=0}^{t-1+\alpha}\frac{\Gamma(t-s)}{\Gamma(t-s+\alpha)}x(s)\right)\\[12.0pt]
\displaystyle+\frac{a}{\Gamma(1-\beta)}\left(\sum_{s=0}^{t+\alpha}\frac{\Gamma(t+1-s+\alpha-\beta)}{\Gamma(t+1-s+\alpha)}x(s)-\sum_{s=0}^{t-1+\alpha}\frac{\Gamma(t-s+\alpha-\beta)}{\Gamma(t-s+\alpha)}x(s)\right)\\[12.0pt]
\displaystyle=(b-1)\,x(t+\alpha-1).\end{array}

(4)

Putting
t=n-\alpha
in the eq.(
4
), we get

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\left(\sum_{s=0}^{n}\frac{\Gamma(n-s+1-\alpha)}{\Gamma(n-s+1)}x(s)-\sum_{s=0}^{n-1}\frac{\Gamma(n-s-\alpha)}{\Gamma(n-s)}x(s)\right)\\[10.0pt]
\displaystyle+\frac{a}{\Gamma(1-\beta)}\left(\sum_{s=0}^{n}\frac{\Gamma(n-s+1-\beta)}{\Gamma(n-s+1)}x(s)-\sum_{s=0}^{n-1}\frac{\Gamma(n-s-\beta)}{\Gamma(n-s)}x(s)\right)-(b-1)\,x(n-1)=0.\end{array}

(5)

Now collecting the coefficients of
x(s)
(for s=0,1,…n) and simplifying eq.(
5
), we get

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle(a+1)\,x(n)-(\alpha+a\beta+b-1)\,x(n-1)\\[10.0pt]
\displaystyle\quad-\sum_{s=0}^{n-2}\frac{1}{\Gamma(n-s+1)}\left(\frac{\alpha\,\Gamma(n-s-\alpha)}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}-\frac{a\beta\,\Gamma(n-s-\beta)}{\Gamma(1-\beta)}\right)x(s)=0.\end{array}

(6)

From this eq. (
6
), We get the required eq. (
3
).

The converse part can be proved in a similar way.

This proves the result.

4.1
Bifurcation analysis

For
a<0
, there are various bifurcations. We give the details of this rich dynamics in the following cases.

Case(I): Bifurcations for
0.5<\beta<\alpha<1

It is observed that, as we decrease the value of
a
, the region bounded by
\gamma(\theta)
divides into sub-regions. This division is due to self-intersections in
\gamma
. Such multiple points in
\gamma
emerge due to cusps in it. The first bifurcation value occurs at
a_{1}=0
. At
a=a_{1}
, the stable region is a cardioid shape ( see Fig.
6(a)
). For
a<a_{1}=0
(sufficiently close to
a_{1}
), the curve
\gamma(\theta)
becomes a multiple curve with a self-intersection ( see Fig.
6(b)
). It gives rise to two closed regions. The left one is bounded by a positively oriented part of the curve
\gamma(\theta)
and hence it is a stable region for the system (
2
). The right part of the curve
\gamma(\theta)
is unstable because it is bounded by the negatively oriented part of the curve
\gamma(\theta)
. Therefore, the unstable region starts at
a_{1}
.

The curve
\gamma
has a cusp if
\gamma^{{}^{\prime}}(\theta)=0
.

i.e., if

\displaystyle 2^{-1+\alpha}\alpha\cos\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)\cos\left(\frac{1}{2}\alpha(\pi-\theta)+\theta\right)\sin\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)^{-1+\alpha}

(12)

\displaystyle+2^{-1+\beta}a\beta\cos\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)\cos\left(\frac{1}{2}\beta(\pi-\theta)+\theta\right)\sin\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)^{-1+\beta}

\displaystyle-2^{\alpha}\left(1-\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)\sin\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)^{\alpha}\sin\left(\frac{1}{2}\alpha(\pi-\theta)+\theta\right)

\displaystyle-2^{\beta}a\left(1-\frac{\beta}{2}\right)\sin\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)^{\beta}\sin\left(\frac{1}{2}\beta(\pi-\theta)+\theta\right)=0

and

\displaystyle 2^{\alpha}\left(1-\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)\cos\left(\frac{1}{2}\alpha(\pi-\theta)+\theta\right)\sin\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)^{\alpha}

\displaystyle+2^{\beta}a\left(1-\frac{\beta}{2}\right)\cos\left(\frac{1}{2}\beta(\pi-\theta)+\theta\right)\sin\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)^{\beta}

5
Conclusion

We studied the two-term linear fractional difference equation
\Delta^{\alpha}x(t)+a\,\Delta^{\beta}x(t+\alpha-\beta)=(b-1)x(t+\alpha-1)
. We investigated the equivalent sequence form of the initial value problem (
2
) with
0<\beta<\alpha\leq 1
and validated it. We derived the expression for the boundary curve
\gamma(\theta)
and also sketched the stable regions for the different sets of three parameters
a
,
\alpha
, and
\beta
in a complex plane for the system. Additionally, we discussed the bifurcations for
a<0
with the cases
0.5<\beta<\alpha<1
and
0<\beta<\alpha<0.5
in detail. We observed the rich dynamics in the considered cases. We provided numerical experiments to support our analytical results.

On the weight distribution of linear sets with complementary weights and related constructions
Geertrui Van de Voorde, Ferdinando Zullo

Abstract
In this paper, we continue the study of linear sets with complementary weights. We find criteria to determine the set of points of any fixed weight and use this to present particular linear sets with few points of weight more than one. We also present a product-type construction for linear sets of complementary type arising from any linear set, allowing us to control the weight distribution of the obtained linear set. Finally, we use this construction to create linear sets with many different weights, along with point sets of even type with many distinct intersection numbers.

1
Preliminaries

In the past decade, linear sets have become one of the central objects of study within finite geometry. Originally motivated by their connections with linear blocking sets and translation ovoids
[
11
]
, they have increased importance since the connection with rank-metric codes was established
[
17
]
. We refer to
[
14
,
8
]
for more background and applications.

An
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linear set
L_{U}
in
\mathrm{PG}(1,q^{n})
of rank
k
is a point set whose defining vectors form a
k
-dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-vector space
U
. More formally, writing
\langle u\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}
for the projective point in
\mathrm{PG}(1,q^{n})
obtained from all
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}
-multiples of a vector
u\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}^{2}
, we have that

L_{U}=\{\langle u\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}\mid u\in U\setminus\{0\}\}.

The weight of a point
\langle u\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}
in
L_{U}
is defined as the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-dimension of the subspace
\langle u\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}\cap U
.

A linear set of rank
n
in
\mathrm{PG}(1,q^{n})
can be described via an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linearised polynomial as follows. Let
\mathcal{L}_{n,q}
be the set of all
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linearised polynomials over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}
, that is, all polynomials of the form
f(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}a_{i}x^{q^{i}},
where
a_{i}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}
. Then it is well-known that an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linear set of rank
n
in
\mathrm{PG}(1,q^{n})
, disjoint from the point
\langle(0,1)\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}
can always be written as
\{\langle(x,f(x))\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}\mid x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}^{*}\}
for some
f\in\mathcal{L}_{n,q}.

In this paper, we will focus on a particular class of linear sets, namely those with complementary weights.
An
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linear set
L_{U}
of rank
k
in
\mathrm{PG}(1,q^{n})
is a set with
complementary weights
if it has two points
P_{1},P_{2}
whose weights sum to
k
.

Linear sets with complementary weights have been studied before, see e.g.
[
13
,
12
,
7
]
and
[
1
,
16
,
19
]
for some applications.
We will list some of their main properties in the next Section.
In this note, we will contribute to this study as follows:

•

We give a new criterion to determine the set of points of any fixed weight in a linear set of complementary type (see Theorem
3.2
, and Corollary
3.7
for an algebraic description).

•

We use these criteria to present linear sets for which we can completely determine the weight distribution (see Propositions
4.3
and
4.5
) and for which we show that only a few weights are possible (Proposition
4.4
and Proposition
4.6
).

•

We present a product-type construction for linear sets of complementary weight in
\mathrm{PG}(1,q^{2t})
from any linear set in
\mathrm{PG}(1,q^{t}),
and show how their weight distributions are related (see Theorem
5.1
).

•

We use this product construction to create examples of linear sets with
many
different weights (see Corollary
5.5
), and even sets in
\mathrm{PG}(2,q)
with many different intersection numbers (see Corollary
5.8
).

2
Introduction and previous results

In the paper
[
13
]
, the authors considered linear sets of rank
k
in
\mathrm{PG}(1,q^{n})
with complementary weights
r
and
k-r
.

First, we observe that, by
[
13
, Proposition 3.2]
, if
L_{W}
is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linear set of rank
k
in
\mathrm{PG}(1,q^{n})
for which there exist two distinct points
P,Q\in L_{W}
such that

w_{L_{W}}(P)=r\quad\text{and}\quad w_{L_{W}}(Q)=k-r,

then
L_{W}
is
\mathrm{PGL}(2,q^{n})
-equivalent to a linear set
L_{U}
, where
U=S\times T
for some
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspaces
S,T\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}
of dimensions
r
and
k-r
, respectively.
Therefore, whenever we consider linear sets with complementary weights, we assume that they are defined by a subspace of the form
S\times T
, as above.

Also, in
[
12
]
, it was derived a way to find the points with second possible maximum weight (when
r\neq k-r
) or the maximum weight (when
r=k-r
). More precisely, they showed the following:

Theorem 2.1
.

[
12
, Theorem 3.4]

Let
U=S\times T
, where
S
is a
(k-r)
-dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
with
r\leq k-r
and
T
is an
r
-dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}
and suppose that
T=\langle a_{1},\ldots,a_{r}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}
for some
a_{1},\ldots,a_{r}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
.
The set of points of weight
r
in
L_{U}
different from
\langle(1,0)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}
is

\{\langle(\xi,1)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}\colon\xi\in a_{1}^{-1}S\cap\ldots\cap a_{r}^{-1}S\}

and its size is
q^{j}
with
j=\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(a_{1}^{-1}S\cap\ldots\cap a_{r}^{-1}S)
.

Recall that the linear set
L_{S\times T}
as defined in Theorem
2.1
contains the point
\langle(1,0)\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}
which has weight
k-r\geq r
. Therefore, all other points have weight at most
r
, and we know that
\langle(0,1)\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}
has weight
r
.
We will first use the condition derived in Theorem
2.1
to show that it is always possible to obtain a linear set
L_{S\times T}
with, apart from
\langle(0,1)\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}
, no further points of weight
r
:

Proposition 2.2
.

For any
(k-r)
-dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace
S
in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
with
k-r\leq n/2
,
k\leq n
,
r\geq 2
, there exists an
r
-dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace
T
in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
such that the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linear set
L_{S\times T}
of rank
k
in
\mathrm{PG}(1,q^{n})
has no points of weight
r
apart from
\langle(0,1)\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}
and
\langle(1,0)\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}
(when
k-r=r
).

Proof.

We first show that we can find an element
\xi\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
such that
S\cap\xi S=\{0\}
. Suppose to the contrary that for any
\xi\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
we have that
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(S\cap\xi S)\geq 1
, so for all
\xi
, there exists some
s\in S\cap\xi S
. This implies that
s=\xi s^{\prime}
for some
s^{\prime}\in S
, and hence, that
\langle(1,\xi)\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}=\langle(s^{\prime},\xi s^{\prime})\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}=\langle(s^{\prime},s)\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}\in L_{S\times S}.

Since
\langle(0,1)\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}\in L_{S\times S}
too, it follows that
L_{S\times S}=\mathrm{PG}(1,q^{n})
. But since the rank of
L_{S\times S}
is
2(k-r)\leq n
, this is a contradiction. Therefore, there exists
\xi\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
such that
S\cap\xi S
has dimension zero.
Let
T
be an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
of dimension
r
containing
1
and
\xi
and suppose that
\{1,\xi,a_{3},\ldots,a_{r}\}
is its basis.
Since

S\cap\xi^{-1}S\cap a_{3}^{-1}S\cap\ldots a_{r}^{-1}S\subseteq S\cap\xi^{-1}S=\{0\},

by Theorem
2.1
we get the assertion.
∎

In the paper
[
13
]
, the authors investigate the possibility of the linear set
L_{S\times T}
having no points with weight more than one, apart from
\langle(1,0)\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}
and
\langle(0,1)\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}
; the following corollary explains the interest in linear sets of rank
2t
with exactly two points of weight
t
in
\mathrm{PG}(1,q^{2t})
.

Corollary 2.3
.

[
13
, Corollary 4.3]

Let
L_{U}
be an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linear set of rank
k
in
\mathrm{PG}(1,q^{n})
for which there exist two distinct points
P,Q\in L_{U}
such that

w_{L_{U}}(P)=s
,
w_{L_{U}}(Q)=t
,
s+t=k\leq n
and
s,t>1
.
If
P
and
Q
are the only points of
L_{U}
of weight greater than one, then
s\leq\frac{n}{2}
and
t\leq\frac{n}{2}
.
In particular, if
s+t=n
, then
n
is even and
s=t=\frac{n}{2}
.

The same authors also show that linear sets with complementary weights with only two points of weight greater than one admit an algebraic description:

Theorem 2.4
.

[
13
, Theorem 4.6]

Let
L_{W}
be an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linear set of
\mathrm{PG}(1,q^{2t})
of rank
n=2t
admitting two points
P
and
Q
of weight
t
. Then
L_{W}
is
\mathrm{PGL}(2,q^{n})
-equivalent to an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linear set
L_{U}
where

U=T_{g,\eta}\times S_{f,\xi}=\{(v+\eta g(v),u+\xi f(u))\colon u,v\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{t}}\},

with
\eta,\xi\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2t}}\setminus{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{t}}
,
\xi^{2}=a\xi+b
,
\eta=A\xi+B
with
a,b,A,B\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{t}}
and
f(x),g(x)\in\mathcal{L}_{t,q}
.
Also,
P
and

are the only points of

with weight greater than one if and only if

f(\alpha_{0}v)+f(\alpha_{1}Abg(v))+f(\alpha_{0}Bg(v))=\alpha_{1}v+\alpha_{0}Ag(v)+\alpha_{1}Aag(v)+\alpha_{1}Bg(v),

(1)

has at most
q
solutions in
v
for every
\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{t}}
with
(\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1})\neq(0,0)
.

Using this algebraic description, the authors of
[
13
]
were able to produce linear sets with complementary weights having exactly two points of weight greater than one.

3
The weight distribution of the points in a linear set with complementary weights

We will extend Theorem
2.1
by giving a description of the set of points with
any
given weight in a linear set with complementary weights.

Definition 3.1
.

Consider
T
to be an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
. Let
\mathcal{L}_{i}(T)
be the set of all the
i
-dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspaces contained in
T
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
U=S\times T
, where
S
is a
(k-r)
-dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
with
r\leq k-r
and
T
is an
r
-dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}
.
The set of points of weight at least
i
in
L_{U}
, different from
\langle(1,0)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}
, is

\left\{\langle(\xi,1)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}\colon\xi\in\mathcal{I}_{i}(S,T)\right\},

where

\mathcal{I}_{i}(S,T)=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},\ldots,a_{i}\in T\\
\langle a_{1},\ldots,a_{i}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\in\mathcal{L}_{i}(T)\end{subarray}}a_{1}^{-1}S\cap\ldots\cap a_{i}^{-1}S.

Proof.

Consider the map

\displaystyle\Phi:\mathcal{I}_{i}(S,T)

\displaystyle\to\mathrm{PG}(1,q^{n})

\displaystyle\xi

\displaystyle\mapsto P_{\xi}:=\langle(\xi,1)\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}.

Let
\mathcal{P}_{\geq i}
be the set of points in
L_{U}
, different from
\langle(1,0)\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}
of weight at least
i
. We claim that
\Phi(\mathcal{I}_{i}(S,T))=\mathcal{P}_{\geq i}.

Consider a point
P_{\xi}\in\Phi(\mathcal{I}_{i}(S,T))
. Since
\xi\in\mathcal{I}_{i}(S,T),
we have that
\xi\in a_{1}^{-1}S\cap\ldots\cap a_{i}^{-1}S
, for some
a_{1},\ldots,a_{i}\in T
that are
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linearly independent. This implies that there exist
b_{1},\ldots,b_{i}\in S
such that

\frac{b_{1}}{a_{1}}=\ldots=\frac{b_{i}}{a_{i}}=\xi.

The vectors
(b_{1},a_{1}),\ldots,(b_{i},a_{i})
belong to
U=S\times T
and are
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linearly independent since
a_{1},\ldots,a_{i}
are
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linearly independent. Since
\langle(b_{j},a_{j})\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}=\langle(\xi,1)\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}
for all
j
, it follows that
U\cap\langle(\xi,1)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}
is at least
i
-dimensional, so
P_{\xi}
has weight at least
i
.
It follows that
\Phi(\mathcal{I}_{i}(S,T))\subseteq\mathcal{P}_{\geq i}.

Vice versa, consider a point
P\in\mathcal{P}_{\geq i}
, different from
\langle(1,0)\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}
, then
P
can be written in a unique way as
P_{\xi}
for some
\xi
and there exists
i
vectors
(s_{1},t_{1}),\ldots,(s_{i},t_{i})\in U\cap\langle(\xi,1)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}
which are
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linearly independent.
Moreover, the elements
t_{1},\ldots,t_{i}
are
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linearly independent, since otherwise there would be a non-zero vector
(\sum_{m}\lambda_{m}s_{m},0)\in U\cap\langle(\xi,1)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}
, a contradiction since the second coordinate of any non-zero vector in this intersection needs to be non-zero.

We find that

\frac{s_{1}}{t_{1}}=\ldots=\frac{s_{i}}{t_{i}}=\xi,

which implies that
\xi\in t_{1}^{-1}S\cap\ldots\cap t_{i}^{-1}S
.

Since we have seen that
t_{1},\ldots,t_{i}
are
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linearly independent,
\xi\in\mathcal{I}_{i}(S,T),
so
\mathcal{P}_{\geq i}\subseteq\mathcal{I}_{i}(S,T)
.

∎

Note that for
i=r
, the above result indeed coincides with Theorem
2.1
as there is only one subspace of
T
of dimension
r
.
As a corollary, we can now detect the points in
L_{U}
with weight exactly
i
.

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
U=S\times T
, where
S
is a
(k-r)
-dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
with
r\leq k-r
and
T
is an
r
-dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}
.
The set of points of weight exactly
i
in
L_{U}
different from
\langle(1,0)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}
is

\left\{\langle(\xi,1)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}\colon\xi\in\mathcal{I}_{i}(S,T)\setminus\mathcal{I}_{i+1}(S,T)\right\}.

Remark 3.4
.

The well-known connection between linear sets and rank-metric codes, see
[
15
,
2
,
18
]
, allows us to rephrase Theorem
3.2
and Corollary
3.3
in terms of rank-metric codes. More precisely, suppose that
\mathcal{C}
is a nondegenerate
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
-linear rank-metric code in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{k}
of dimension two admitting a generator matrix of the form

G=\begin{pmatrix}s_{1}&\cdots&s_{k-r}&0&\cdots&0\\
0&\cdots&0&t_{1}&\cdots&t_{r}\end{pmatrix}.

Every codeword of
\mathcal{C}
can be written as
(x_{0},x_{1})G
for some
x_{0},x_{1}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
.
Clearly, the rank weight is the same for proportional codewords and

\mathrm{rk}_{q}((1,0)G)=k-r\,\,\text{and}\,\,\mathrm{rk}_{q}((0,1)G)=r.

Therefore, up to proportionality, we can assume that a nonzero codeword
c\in\mathcal{C}
not proportional to
(1,0)G
nor to
(0,1)G
is of the form
(1,-\xi)G
for some
\xi\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
. By
[
15
, Theorem 2]
, we have that

\mathrm{rk}_{q}((1,-\xi)G)=k-w_{L_{S\times T}}(\langle(\xi,1)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}).

By Corollary
3.3
, the number of codewords of rank weight
n-i
is given by the size of the following set:

\left\{\alpha(\xi,1)\colon\xi\in\mathcal{I}_{i}(S,T)\setminus\mathcal{I}_{i+1}(S,T),\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}\right\}.

Using that for any
a_{1},a_{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{*}

\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(a_{1}^{-1}S\cap a_{2}^{-1}S)=\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(a_{1}a_{2}(a_{1}^{-1}S\cap a_{2}^{-1}S))=\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(a_{1}S\cap a_{2}S),

we also get another characterisation for the linear sets of complementary weights with only two points of weight greater than or equal to two.

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
U=S\times T
, where
S
is a
(k-r)
-dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
with
r\leq k-r
and
T
is an
r
-dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}
.
Then
L_{U}
has only two points of weight at least two if and only if

\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(a_{1}S\cap a_{2}S)=0,

for any
a_{1},a_{2}\in T
which are
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linearly independent.

We will now focus on the case
n=2t
, where there are two points of weight
t
. Let
\xi\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}
and
f(x)\in\mathcal{L}_{t,q}
and let

S_{f,\xi}=\{u+\xi f(u)\colon u\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}\}.

The following easy lemma shows that the weight of the points of a linear set with complementary weights
L_{S\times T}
can also be derived by finding the dimension of intersection of the subspaces
S
and the multiplicative cosets of
T
in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
U=S\times T
, where
S
is a
(k-r)
-dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
with
r\leq k-r
and
T
is an
r
-dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}
.
For any point
\langle(1,\alpha)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}
, with
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{*}
,

w_{L_{U}}(\langle(1,\alpha)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}})=\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(S\cap\alpha^{-1}T).

Proof.

By definition, we have that

w_{L_{U}}(\langle(1,\alpha)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}})=\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\{\rho\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}\colon\rho(1,\alpha)\in S\times T\}),

and noting that
\rho(1,\alpha)\in S\times T
if and only if
\rho\in S\cap\alpha^{-1}T
, we obtain the assertion.
∎

As a consequence, when we have a polynomial description of
S
and
T
, we have the following.

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
n=2t
,
\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}
and
f(X),g(X)\in\mathcal{L}_{t,q}
.
Let
\xi^{2}=A\xi+B
and
\eta=a\xi+b
, for some
A,B,a,b\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}
.
The weight of
\langle(1,\alpha)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}
, with
\alpha^{-1}=\alpha_{0}+\xi\alpha_{1}
, in
L_{S_{f,\xi}\times S_{g,\eta}}
is

\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\ker\left(f(\alpha_{0}X+(\alpha_{0}b+a\alpha_{1}B)g(X))-\alpha_{1}X-(a\alpha_{0}+a\alpha_{1}A+b\alpha_{1})g(X)\right)).

Proof.

By the above proposition, we need to compute the dimension of
S_{f,\xi}\cap\alpha^{-1}S_{g,\eta}
.
Then
x_{0}+\xi f(x_{0})\in\alpha^{-1}S_{g,\eta}
if and only if there exists
y_{0}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{t}}
such that

x_{0}+\xi f(x_{0})=(\alpha_{0}+\xi\alpha_{1})(y_{0}+bg(y_{0})+a\xi g(y_{0})).

Using that
\{1,\xi\}
is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{t}}
-basis of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
we get that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}x_{0}=\alpha_{0}y_{0}+\alpha_{0}bg(y_{0})+ag(y_{0})\alpha_{1}B,\\
f(x_{0})=\alpha_{0}ag(y_{0})+\alpha_{1}y_{0}+ag(y_{0})\alpha_{1}A+\alpha_{1}bg(y_{0}),\end{array}\right.

from which we get that the weight of
\langle(1,\alpha)\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}
is

\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\ker(f(\alpha_{0}X)+f(\alpha_{0}bg(X))+f(ag(X)\alpha_{1}B)-\alpha_{0}ag(X)-\alpha_{1}X-ag(X)\alpha_{1}A-\alpha_{1}bg(X))).

Using that
f
is additive, the statement now follows.
∎

Remark 3.8
.

If
\eta=\xi
the polynomial in Corollary
3.7
reduces to

f(\alpha_{0}X+\alpha_{1}Bg(X))-(\alpha_{0}+\alpha_{1}A)g(X)-\alpha_{1}X.

4
Explicit linear sets of complementary type and their weight distributions

In what follows, we will use the criteria derived in the previous section to present linear sets for which we can control the weight distribution.

4.1
S_{f}\times S_{\mathrm{T}r}

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
n=2t
,
\xi\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}
and
f(X)\in\mathcal{L}_{t,q}
. Then the point
\langle(1,\alpha)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}
of
L_{S_{f,\xi}\times S_{\mathrm{Tr}_{q^{t}/q},\xi}}
has weight at most

\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\mathrm{Im}(f))+1

if
\alpha\notin{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{t}}

W_{L_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}\times S_{f,\xi}}}=2X^{t}+(q^{t}-1)W_{L_{f}}.

Proof.

The first two points are easy to check.
Now, suppose that
w_{L_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}\times S_{f,\xi}}}(\langle(1,m)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}})=j\geq 1
then there exist
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{j},x_{1},\ldots,x_{j}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}
such that

\langle(\alpha_{1},x_{1}+\xi f(x_{1})),\ldots,(\alpha_{j},x_{j}+\xi f(x_{j}))\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}=(\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}\times S_{f,\xi})\cap\langle(1,m)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}.

It immediately follows that for any
i\in\{1,\ldots,j\}
we have

\alpha_{i}^{-1}(x_{i}+\xi f(x_{i}))=m,

and since the
\alpha_{i}
’s are in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}
and
\{1,\xi\}
is an
\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}
-basis of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
we have that

m_{0}=\alpha_{i}^{-1}x_{i}\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,m_{1}=\alpha_{i}^{-1}f(x_{i}).

Therefore,

\langle(m_{0},m_{1})\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}}=\langle(\alpha_{i}^{-1}x_{i},\alpha_{i}^{-1}f(x_{i}))\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}}=\langle(x_{i},f(x_{i}))\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}},

for any
i\in\{1,\ldots,j\}
.
Note also that
x_{1},\ldots,x_{j}
are
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linearly independent. Indeed, if there exist
a_{1},\ldots,a_{j}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
such that

a_{1}x_{1}+\ldots+a_{j}x_{j}=0,

then

a_{1}(\alpha_{1},x_{1}+\xi f(x_{1}))+\ldots+a_{j}(\alpha_{j},x_{j}+\xi f(x_{j}))=(a_{1}\alpha_{1}+\ldots+a_{j}\alpha_{j},0)\in(\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}\times S_{f,\xi})\cap\langle(1,m)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}},

implying that
a_{1}\alpha_{1}+\ldots+a_{j}\alpha_{j}=0.

It follows that

\displaystyle\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{j}a_{i}\left(\alpha_{i},x_{i}+\xi f(x_{i})\right)=\left(\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{j}a_{i}\alpha_{i},\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{j}a_{i}x_{i}+\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{j}a_{i}f(x_{i})\right)=

\displaystyle\left(\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{j}a_{i}\alpha_{i},\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{j}a_{i}x_{i}+f\left(\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{j}a_{i}x_{i}\right)\right)=(0,0),

and therefore, the
j
vectors
(\alpha_{i},x_{i}+\xi f(x_{i}))
,
i=1,\ldots,j
are not spanning a
j
-dimensional space.

Therefore, we have that

w_{L_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}\times S_{f,\xi}}}(\langle(1,m)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}})\leq w_{L_{f}}(\langle(m_{0},m_{1})\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}}).

Now, suppose that
w_{L_{f}}(\langle(m_{0},m_{1})\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}})=\ell
. As before this means that there exist
x_{1},\ldots,x_{\ell}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}
such that

\langle(x_{1},f(x_{1})),\ldots,(x_{\ell},f(x_{\ell}))\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}=U_{f}\cap\langle(m_{0},m_{1})\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}},

where
U_{f}=\{\langle(x,f(x))\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{t}}}\mid x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{t}}\}
.

Note that
m_{0}
and
m_{1}
cannot be zero as
\langle(0,1)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}},\langle(1,0)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}}\notin L_{f}
. So, we have that
m_{1}/m_{0}=f(x_{i})/x_{i}
for any
i\in\{1,\ldots,\ell\}
.
Define
\beta_{i}=x_{i}/m_{0}=f(x_{i})/m_{1}
.
We see that
(\beta_{i},x_{i}+\xi f(x_{i}))=(\beta_{i},m_{0}\beta_{i}+\xi m_{1}\beta_{i})=\beta_{i}(1,m).

Therefore,

(\beta_{1},x_{1}+\xi f(x_{1})),\ldots,(\beta_{\ell},x_{\ell}+\xi f(x_{\ell}))\in(\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}\times S_{f,\xi})\cap\langle(1,m)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}},

and arguing as before one can see that the
(\beta_{i},x_{i}+\xi f(x_{i}))
’s are
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linearly independent. So,

w_{L_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}\times S_{f,\xi}}}(\langle(1,m)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}})\geq w_{L_{f}}(\langle(m_{0},m_{1})\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}}),

and the statement of the third bullet point follows.

Finally, we claim that the number of points in
L_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}\times S_{f,\xi}}
, different from
\langle(0,1)\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}
and
\langle(1,0)\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}
of weight
w
is
q^{t}-1
times the number of points of weight
w
in
L_{f}
. Suppose that
\langle(1,m)\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}
and
\langle(1,m^{\prime})\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}
correspond to the same point
\langle(m_{0},m_{1})\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{t}}}
. Then
m=m_{0}+m_{1}\xi
and
m^{\prime}=m_{0}^{\prime}+m_{1}^{\prime}\xi
where
m_{0}=\lambda m_{0}^{\prime}
and
m_{1}=\lambda m_{1}^{\prime}
for some
\lambda\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{t}}
and it follows that
m^{\prime}=\lambda m
where
\lambda\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{t}}^{*}
. Hence, a point
\langle(m_{0},m_{1})\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{t}}}
gives rise to
q^{t}-1
different points
\langle(1,m)\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}
. Vice versa, if
m^{\prime}=\lambda m
where
\lambda\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{t}}^{*},
then
\langle(m_{0},m_{1})\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{t}}}=\langle(m^{\prime}_{0},m^{\prime}_{1})\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{t}}}
. The statement follows.
∎

5.3
On certain sets of even type

A
set of even type
in
\mathrm{PG}(2,q)
is a set of points such that any line intersects the set in an even number of points. It is easy to see that the number of points in an even set is even, and that even sets can only exist in projective planes of even order.

It is well-known that using certain
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}
-linear sets in
\mathrm{PG}(1,q^{t})
,
q
even, one can construct translation sets which form
translation hyperovals
, or
translation KM-arcs

[
5
]
. Using the same construction, we can create small sets of even sets of type
(0,\ 2,\ \sqrt{q},\ 2\sqrt{q}-2)
in
\mathrm{PG}(2,q)
,
q
an even square (see Corollary
5.7
), as well as even sets of type
(0,2,4,8,\ldots,2^{m-1})
in
\mathrm{PG}(2,2^{2^{m}})
(see Corollary
5.8
),

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
L_{g}=\langle(x,g(x))\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\mid x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}\}
be an
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}
-linear set in
\mathrm{PG}(1,q)
,
q=2^{s}
, which has points of weight
w_{1},\ldots,w_{k}
let
\mathcal{S}=\{\langle(1,x,g(x))\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\mid x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{*}\}
and let
\mathcal{S}_{\infty}
be the complement of the set
\{\langle(0,x,g(x))\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\mid x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{*}\}
. Then
\overline{\mathcal{S}}=\mathcal{S}\cup\mathcal{S}_{\infty}
is a set of even type in
\mathrm{PG}(2,q)
of size
2q+1-|L_{g}|
that has line intersection sizes
0,2^{w_{1}},\ldots,2^{w_{k}}
and
q+1-|L_{g}|
.

Proof.

Since
g(x)
is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linear map,
\mathcal{S}_{\infty}
is precisely the set of points that are not a direction determined by the set
\mathcal{S}
. Recall that the size of the set
\mathcal{S}
is
q
so it follows that every line with a direction inside
\mathcal{S}_{\infty}
meets
\mathcal{S}
in exactly one point. Hence, every line with direction inside the set
\mathcal{S}_{\infty}
is a
2
-secant to the set
\overline{\mathcal{S}}
. Furthermore, a line with direction
\langle(x,g(x)\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}
, which has weight
w_{j}
in the linear set
L_{g}
meets the set
\mathcal{S}
in
0
or
2^{w_{j}}
points.
Finally, the line
X=0
intersects the set
\overline{\mathcal{S}}
in a set of size
|\mathcal{S}_{\infty}|=q+1-|L_{g}|
and the size of
\overline{\mathcal{S}}
equals
q+(q+1-|L_{g}|)

∎

6
Open problems

One of the most challenging problems in the theory of linear sets concerns the classification of all possible point–weight spectra; see, for instance,
[
6
,
3
]
.
In this paper, we provided new insight into this direction by giving criteria to determine the set of points of a fixed weight for linear sets with complementary weights.
However, the determination of all possible sizes of these sets remains unclear, and several fundamental questions remain open.

Open Problem 6.1
.

Given
k,n
, determine all possible weight distributions of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear sets

L_{S\times T}\subseteq\mathrm{PG}(1,q^{n})
with complementary weights.

Corollary
3.5
gives a geometric criterion for the existence of linear sets with exactly two points of weight at least
2
.
Therefore, naturally the following problem arises.

Open Problem 6.2
.

Provide a complete algebraic classification of all such pairs
(S,T)
defining linear sets with exactly two points of weight at least
2
.

Corollary
3.7
expresses point weights using kernels of certain linearized polynomials.
A major open problem is to translate these expressions into explicit and computable
criteria for general families of polynomials (monomials, binomials, trinomials, etc.).

Open Problem 6.3
.

For which polynomials does one obtain extremal or very sparse point-weight distributions?

Propositions
4.1
and
4.7
provide general upper bounds on the weight of points.

Open Problem 6.4
.

Determine for which polynomials these bounds are tight.

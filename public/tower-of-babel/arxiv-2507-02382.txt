Interval-sphere model structures
Kathryn Hess, Samuel Lavenir, Kelly Maggs

Abstract
. The bedrock of persistence theory over a single parameter is decomposition of persistence modules into intervals. In [ HLM24 ] , the authors leveraged interval decomposition to produce a cell decomposition of the minimal model of a simply connected copersistent space. The key tool was a technique called interval surgery, which involves the gluing of intervals to a persistent CDGA by means of algebraic cell attachments. In this article, we define a compact, combinatorial model categorical structure that contextualizes interval surgery as a genuine model-categorical cell attachment. We show that our new model structure is neither the injective nor the projective one and that cofibrancy is closely linked to the notion of tameness in persistence theory and algebraic notions of compactness.

1.
Introduction

For a field
\mathbf{k}
, functors
\mathbb{V}\colon(\mathbb{R}_{+},\leq)\to\mathsf{Vec}_{\mathbf{k}}
from the poset of non
-
negative reals to the category of
\mathbf{k}
-vector spaces are called (real
-
indexed)
persistence modules
. They play a central role in topological data analysis (TDA), as algebraic invariants of the homology of sub
-
level set filtrations of compact manifolds
[
Bar94
,
ZC05
,
Oud15
]
and of filtered Vietoris–Rips complexes of finite metric spaces
[
Rob99
,
ELZ02
]
. The atomic pieces of this theory are the
interval modules

\mathbb{I}_{[s,t)}\colon(\mathbb{R}_{+},\leq)\;\longrightarrow\;\mathsf{Vec}_{\mathbf{k}},\quad\mathbb{I}_{[s,t)}(r)=\begin{cases}\mathbf{k},&s\leq r<t,\\
0,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Under mild tameness assumptions, persistence modules decompose into direct sums of interval modules:
[
Web85
,
ZC05
,
CDSGO16
,
CB15
]
as

\mathbb{V}\;\cong\;\bigoplus_{\gamma}\mathbb{I}_{[s_{\gamma},\,t_{\gamma})}.

In
[
HLM24
]
we introduced
interval surgery
, a procedure for constructing persistent algebraic objects in arbitrary categories by iteratively attaching interval modules as cells. In the setting of persistent commutative differential graded algebras (CDGAs), this procedure yields explicit Sullivan minimal models filtered by interval attachments, under simple
-
connectedness and tameness hypotheses. Interval modules thus act as the fundamental “cells” of a broad class of persistent algebraic structures.

In this article we provide the model
-
categorical underpinnings for interval surgery. We equip the category of persistent cochain complexes over the rationals with a new combinatorial, compactly generated model structure, the
interval–sphere model structure
(Theorem
2.1.2
,
Proposition

4.6
). This model structure is neither the injective nor the projective one, largely due to the topology of the indexing category
\mathbb{R}_{+}
(
Section

3.2
,
Example

4.16
). Its cofibrant generators are precisely the interval attachments, meaning that cofibrant objects are closely related to those built from intervals. We show that every monomorphism between tame objects is a cofibration (
Lemma

4.21
), as a consequence of the close relation between tameness and interval-decomposability.

We then transfer the interval–sphere structure along the standard free–forgetful adjunction to persistent CDGAs (
Theorem

5.2
), enabling us to exhibit the the persistent Sullivan minimal model as a genuine cellular presentation (
Corollary

5.4
). Finally, we prove that there exists a Quillen pair between the category of persistent CDGAs (equipped with the interval–sphere structure) and that of copersistent simplicial sets (equipped with the
\mathbb{Q}
-
projective structure) in Theorem
5.5
.

Where much of the TDA literature generalizes the
indexing
category (e.g., to multiparameter persistence
[
CFF+13
,
BL22
,
BL23
]
), we instead enrich the
target
category, while fixing as source poset
\mathbb{R}_{+}
. By treating interval modules as cells at the cochain level, we lay a blueprint for transporting both the interval–sphere model structure and the interval surgery process into diverse algebraic and homotopical contexts.

Notation and conventions

•

We apply cohomological conventions, i.e., differentials increase degree.

•

We use the word

as a synonym for

.

•

All vector spaces are over the field

•

We denote by

2.1.
Cochain complexes

We start by setting out notation for usual cochain complexes of vector spaces.

2.1.1.
Cochain spheres and disks

To every vector space
V
, we can associate sphere
S^{k}(V)
and disk
D^{k}(V)
cochain complexes for every integer
k\geq 0
. The complex
S^{k}(V)
consists of a copy of
V
in degree
k
, while the complex
D^{k}(V)
consists of a copy of
V
in each of degrees
(k-1)
and
k
, where the only non zero differential is the identity. When
k=0
, we adopt the convention that
D^{0}(V)=0
. We also write
S^{k}=S^{k}(\mathbb{Q})
and
D^{k}=D^{k}(\mathbb{Q})
. Similarly, given a graded
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space
V=\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}V_{k}
, we can define associated sphere
S(V)
and disk
D(V)
complexes. These are cochain complexes defined by

S(V)=\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}S^{k}(V_{k})\>\>\>\>\>\>\>,\>\>\>\>\>\>\>D(V)=\bigoplus_{k>0}D^{k}(V_{k}).

Note that there are evident inclusions
S(V)\subseteq D(V)
and that
D(V)/S(V)=S(V[-1])=S(V)[-1]
for any choice of graded vector space
V
.

2.1.2.
Model structures

There is a well known (projective) model structure on the category
\mathsf{Ch}^{*}_{\mathbb{Q}}
of cochain complexes, of which the fibrations are the degree-wise surjective maps, and the weak equivalences are the quasi-isomorphisms, i.e., maps inducing isomorphisms on cohomology (cf.
[
Hes07
]
). The cofibrations can be described as those cochain maps that are injective in degrees
>0
. This model structure is combinatorial
[
Bek00
, Prop.3.13]
, i.e. locally presentable and generated by a small set of cofibrations, with the following sets of generating cofibrations and trivial cofibrations.

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=\{\>S^{k}\hookrightarrow D^{k}\>,\>0\to S^{0}\>|\>k\in\mathbb{N}\>\}

\displaystyle J

\displaystyle=\{\>D^{k}\to 0\>|\>k\in\mathbb{N}\>\}.

2.3.
Tameness

The use of

However, in practice and applications, we take as input a finite sequence of spaces/chain complexes/vector spaces, so the resulting functor changes at only finitely many index values. The following definitions of discretizations and tameness are presented in

2.4.
Persistence Modules

A persistence module
[
CDSGO16
]
is a persistent object in the category
\mathsf{Vec}_{\mathbb{Q}}
of
\mathbb{Q}
-vector spaces. In other words, a persistence module is a functor
\mathbb{M}:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathsf{Vec}_{\mathbb{Q}}.
A persistence module can equivalently be described as a graded module over a certain
\mathbb{R}_{+}
-graded ring, as follows. We denote by
\mathbb{Q}[X^{\mathbb{R}_{+}}]
the ring of formal polynomials in one variable
X
with
non-negative real exponents
. Its elements are formal sums

\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}X^{t_{i}}

where
n\geq 0
is an integer, the exponents
t_{i}
are non-negative
real numbers
and the coefficients
a_{i}
belong to
\mathbb{Q}
. The multiplication on
\mathbb{Q}[X^{\mathbb{R}_{+}}]
is given by a
\mathbb{Q}
-linear extension of
X^{s}\cdot X^{t}=X^{s+t}
. This ring admits a natural
\mathbb{R}_{+}
-grading

\mathbb{Q}[X^{\mathbb{R}_{+}}]\cong\bigoplus_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}\mathbb{Q}X^{t}

so that a persistent module
\mathbb{V}\in p\mathsf{Vec}_{\mathbb{Q}}
can be described as a graded
\mathbb{Q}[X^{\mathbb{R}_{+}}]
-module. This identification can be promoted to an isomorphism of categories

p\mathsf{Vec}_{\mathbb{Q}}\cong\mathsf{gr}\mathsf{Mod}_{\mathbb{Q}[X^{\mathbb{R}_{+}}]}.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
s<t
be non-negative real numbers. The
interval module

\mathbb{I}_{[s,t)}
is the persistence module with components given by:

\mathbb{I}_{[s,t)}(i)=\begin{cases}\mathbb{Q}&\text{ if }s\leq i<t\\
0&\text{ else}\end{cases}

with structure maps
\mathbb{I}_{[s,t)}(i\leq j)
given by identities when
s\leq i\leq j<t
, and zero otherwise.

Direct sums of persistence modules are constructed degree-wise on both objects and morphisms.
Interval modules are the atomic units into which all (tame) persistence modules decompose, as stated below.

Theorem 2.4
.

[
Web85
, Thm. 1]

If
\mathbb{V}
is a tame persistence module, then there exists a set of intervals
[s_{\gamma},t_{\gamma})\subseteq[0,\infty)
,
\gamma\in\Gamma,
such that
\mathbb{V}
splits as a direct sum of interval modules

\mathbb{V}\cong\bigoplus_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\mathbb{I}_{[s_{\gamma},t_{\gamma})}.

Moreover, this decomposition is unique up to permutation of summands.

Remark 2.5
.

The half-open intervals are well-suited to the study of tame objects, as
\mathbb{I}_{[s,t)}
is the left Kan extension of the sequence
\mathbb{Q}\to 0
over the discretization
s<t
. The intervals
\mathbb{I}_{[s,t]},\mathbb{I}_{(s,t]}
and
\mathbb{I}_{(s,t)}
are, however,
not
tame for
s<t
. If they were, they would permit a half-open interval decomposition by
2.4
, which is impossible via a dimension argument.

2.5.
Persistent cochain complexes

A
persistent cochain complex
is a persistent object in the category
\mathsf{Ch}^{*}_{\mathbb{Q}}
of complexes. A persistent complex can equivalently be described as a complex of persistence modules. More precisely, there is an equivalence of categories

p\mathsf{Ch}^{*}_{\mathbb{Q}}\simeq\mathsf{Ch}^{*}(p\mathsf{Vec}_{\mathbb{Q}})\simeq\mathsf{Ch}^{*}(\mathsf{grMod}_{\mathbb{Q}[X^{\mathbb{R}_{+}}]}),

from which it follows that
p\mathsf{Ch}^{*}_{\mathbb{Q}}
is an abelian category. As a category of presheaves valued in an abelian category,
p\mathsf{Ch}^{*}_{\mathbb{Q}}
is itself an abelian category whose limits and colimits are computed pointwise. It is moreover a locally finitely presentable category, whence the small object argument
[
Hov07
, Thn. 2.1.14]
can be applied to
any
set of maps.

Notation 2.6
.

Let
\mathbb{X}\in p\mathsf{Ch}^{*}_{\mathbb{Q}}
be a persistent cochain complex and
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

•

\mathbb{X}^{k},Z\mathbb{X}^{k}\in p\mathsf{Vec}_{\mathbb{Q}}
are the persistence modules of
k
-cochains and
k
-cocycles respectively.

•

is the persistent
k
-th cohomology
\mathbb{X}
.

•

If

, then
x_{t}:=\mathbb{X}^{k}(s\leq t)x_{s}
for
s<t
.

3.1.
The interval-sphere model structure

Proof.

Let us write
R=\mathbb{Q}[X^{\mathbb{R}_{+}}]
for the ‘polynomial’ ring of
Section

2.4
. In view of the identification
p\mathsf{Vec}_{\mathbb{Q}}\cong\mathsf{gr}\mathsf{Mod}_{R}
, the compact objects in
p\mathsf{Vec}_{\mathbb{Q}}
are exactly the graded
R
-modules of finite presentation. Given a persistence module
\mathbb{V}
, such a finite presentation amounts to a short exact sequence

0\to\bigoplus_{j\in J}X^{t_{j}}R\to\bigoplus_{i\in I}X^{s_{i}}R\to\mathbb{V}\to 0

where
I
and
J
are finite sets. This shows that
\mathbb{V}
is a quotient of tame modules of finite type, hence is itself tame and of finite type. Conversely, if
\mathbb{V}
is tame and of finite type, it admits an interval decomposition

\mathbb{V}\cong\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathbb{I}_{[s_{i},t_{i})}

consisting of only a finite number of intervals. Such a decomposition implies the existence of a short exact sequence

0\to\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathbb{I}_{[t_{i},\infty)}\to\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathbb{I}_{[s_{i},\infty)}\to\mathbb{V}\to 0,

which translates to a finite presentation of
\mathbb{V}
in view of the isomorphism of persistence modules
X^{t}R\cong\mathbb{I}_{[t,\infty)}
.
∎

Proposition 4.2
.

The compact objects of
p\mathsf{Ch}^{*}_{\mathbb{Q}}
are exactly the tame finite complexes.

Proof.

In view of the identification
p\mathsf{Ch}^{*}_{\mathbb{Q}}\cong\mathsf{Ch}^{*}(\mathsf{gr}\mathsf{Mod}_{\mathbb{Q}[X^{\mathbb{R}_{+}}]})
, the compact objects in
p\mathsf{Ch}^{*}_{\mathbb{Q}}
are the bounded complexes of finitely presented
\mathbb{R}_{+}
-graded
\mathbb{Q}[X^{\mathbb{R}_{+}}]
-modules. By
Proposition

4.1
, these are the bounded complexes of tame persistence modules of finite type.
∎

Proposition 4.3
.

The compact objects in copersistent spaces are precisely the tame finite spaces.

Proof.

The category of copersistent spaces can equivalently be described as the presehaf category

\mathsf{Fun}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\mathsf{op}}\times\mathbf{\Delta}^{\mathsf{op}},\mathsf{Set}).

The compact objects in a presheaf category are precisely the finite colimits of representables. Given
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
t\geq 0
, the representable
\mathsf{Hom}_{\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbf{\Delta}}(-,t\times[n])
, when seen as a copersistent space, can be described as follows

(\mathbb{1}_{\leq t}\times\Delta^{n}):s\mapsto\begin{cases}\Delta^{n}&\text{when $s\leq t$},\\
\varnothing&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

with structure maps being either identities or the trivial map
\varnothing\to\Delta^{n}
. As such, it is tame and finite. Accordingly, any compact copersistent space
\mathbb{X}
is tame and finite, since it admits a surjection

\coprod_{1\leq i\leq n}(\mathbb{1}_{\leq t_{i}}\times\Delta^{n_{i}})\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{X}

from a tame and finite copersistent space.
∎

4.2.
Compactly generated model structure

We prove now that the interval sphere model structure is compactly generated. The payoff is that
\mathbb{I}
-cell complexes can be expressed as countable sequences of (potentially uncountably many) cell attachments. We present the basic definitions of compactly generated model categories and refer to
[
MP12
]
for a complete reference.

Definition 4.4
.

For an ordinal
\lambda
and an object
\mathbb{X}\in p\mathsf{Ch}^{*}_{\mathbb{Q}}
, a
relative
\mathbb{I}
-cell
\lambda
-complex
under
\mathbb{X}
is a map
f:\mathbb{X}\to\mathbb{Y}
that is a transfinite composite of a
\lambda
-sequence such that
\mathbb{Y}_{0}=\mathbb{X}
and for
\alpha+1<\lambda
, the object
\mathbb{Y}_{\alpha+1}
is obtained as a pushout

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\bigoplus_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{\alpha}}\mathbb{S}^{k+1}_{[s_{\gamma},t_{\gamma})}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{Y}_{\alpha}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\bigoplus_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{\alpha}}\mathbb{D}^{k+1}_{s_{\gamma}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{Y}_{\alpha+1}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lrcorner}

over a set
\Gamma_{\alpha}
of arbitrary cardinality. A
sequential
relative
\mathbb{I}
-cell complex is a relative
\mathbb{I}
-cell
\omega
-complex where
\omega
is a countable ordinal.

A morphism
f:\mathbb{X}\to\mathbb{Y}
is a relative
\mathbb{I}
-cell complex
\mathbb{X}
if there exists some ordinal
\lambda
such that
\mathbb{X}
is a relative
\mathbb{I}
-cell
\lambda
-complex. The set of relative
\mathbb{I}
-cell complexes is denoted
\text{cell}(\mathbb{I})
. In a cofibrantly generated model category with cofibrant generators
\mathbb{I}
, the set of cofibrations consists of retracts of
\text{cell}(\mathbb{I})
.

Definition 4.5
.

An object
\mathbb{U}\in p\mathsf{Ch}^{*}_{\mathbb{Q}}
is
compact with respect to
\mathbb{I}
if for every relative
\mathbb{I}
-cell
\omega
-complex
f:\mathbb{X}\to\mathbb{Y}=\mathsf{colim}\mathbb{Y}_{i}
, the map

\mathsf{colim}_{i}\mathsf{Hom}(\mathbb{U},\mathbb{Y}_{i})\to\mathsf{Hom}(\mathbb{U},\mathbb{Y})

is a bijection. We say a cofibrantly generated model category with cofibrant generators
\mathbb{I}
is
compactly generated
if all domain objects in
\mathbb{I}
are compact with respect to relative sequential
\mathbb{I}
-cell complexes.

In a compactly generated model category, the set of cofibrations is equal to the set of retracts of sequential relative
\mathbb{I}
-cell complexes
[
MP12
, 15.2.1]
.

Proposition 4.6
.

The interval-sphere model structure is compactly generated.

Proof.

The set of source objects in
\mathbb{I}
are the interval spheres
\mathbb{S}_{[s,t)}^{k}
. By
Proposition

4.3
, these are compact with respect to all filtered colimits, and thus compact with respect to sequential relative
\mathbb{I}
-cell complexes.
∎

4.2.1.
Locally compact persistence modules

Local compactness is the central concept that proves to be a necessary condition for
\mathbb{I}
-cell complexes. We first define and study local compactness at the level of persistence modules.

Definition 4.7
.

Let
\mathbb{V}\in p\mathsf{Vec}_{\mathbb{Q}}
.

(1)

A
compact neighbourhood
of
v\in\mathbb{V}
is a compact persistence module
\mathbb{U}_{v}
satisfying
v\in\mathbb{U}_{v}\subseteq\mathbb{V}
.

(2)

A persistence module
\mathbb{V}
is
locally compact
if every point
v\in\mathbb{V}
admits a compact neighbourhood.

Since
\mathbb{U}_{v}
is compact if and only if it admits a finite interval decomposition, we can think of local compactness at
v
as local interval decomposability around
v
. For a set of submodules
\{\mathbb{U}_{\alpha}\subseteq\mathbb{V}\},
the
union

\bigcup_{\alpha}\mathbb{U}_{\alpha}
is the image of the map
\bigoplus_{\alpha}\mathbb{U}_{\alpha}\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{V}
under the maps induced by canonical inclusions
\mathbb{U}_{\alpha}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{V}
. We call the set
\{\mathbb{U}_{\alpha}\}
a
cover
if
\bigcup_{\alpha}\mathbb{U}_{\alpha}=\mathbb{V}
or equivalently if the map
\bigoplus_{\alpha}\mathbb{U}_{\alpha}\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{V}
is surjective. It is not hard to see that ocal compactness is equivalent to the existence of a cover
\{\mathbb{U}_{\alpha}\subseteq\mathbb{V}\mid\mathbb{U}_{\alpha}\in(p\mathsf{Vec}_{\mathbb{Q}})^{\omega}\}
of
\mathbb{V}
by compact sub-modules, and we use the two definitions interchangeably.

Proposition 4.8
.

The class of locally compact persistence modules is closed under arbitrary direct sums.

Proof.

If
\mathbb{V}=\oplus_{\beta}\mathbb{V}_{\beta}
is a direct sum of locally compact persistence modules with compact covers
\oplus_{\alpha\in A_{\beta}}\mathbb{U}_{\alpha,\beta}\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{V}_{\beta}
, then

\bigoplus_{\beta}\bigoplus_{\alpha\in A_{\beta}}\mathbb{U}_{\beta,\alpha}\twoheadrightarrow\bigoplus_{\beta}\mathbb{V}_{\beta}

is a compact cover for
\mathbb{V}
.
∎

There is a large, easily identifiable class of persistence modules that are not locally compact. An element
v_{r}\in\mathbb{V}(r)
is a
right-closed
point of
\mathbb{V}
if
\mathbb{V}(r+\epsilon)(v_{r})=0
for all
\epsilon>0
.

Lemma 4.9
.

If
\mathbb{V}\in p\mathsf{Vec}_{\mathbb{Q}}
contains a right-closed point, then
\mathbb{V}
is not locally compact.

Proof.

Let
v_{r}\in\mathbb{V}(r)
be a right-closed point. Suppose that there exists a compact neighbourhood
i:\mathbb{U}_{v_{r}}=\oplus_{i=0}^{n}\mathbb{I}_{[s_{i},t_{i})}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{V}
containing
v_{r}=i(u_{r})
. Pick
\epsilon<\min\{t_{i}-r\mid 0\leq i\leq n\}
. Then
v_{r+\epsilon}=i(u_{r+\epsilon})=0
but
u_{r+\epsilon}\neq 0
, contradicting the injectivity of
i
.
∎

Lemma 4.10
.

If
\mathbb{V}\in p\mathsf{Vec}_{\mathbb{Q}}
is locally compact, then the pushout

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\bigoplus_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\mathbb{I}_{[t_{\gamma},\infty)}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{V}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\bigoplus_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\mathbb{I}_{[s_{\gamma},\infty)}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\overline{\mathbb{V}}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces v_{t_{\gamma}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\gamma_{s_{\gamma}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i}

over an arbitrary indexing set
\Gamma
is locally compact for all
\{s_{\gamma}\leq t_{\gamma}\mid\gamma\in\Gamma\}
.

Proof.

Elements in
\overline{\mathbb{V}}
consist of finite sums of homogenous elements

v_{r}\in\overline{\mathbb{V}}(r)=\mathbb{V}(r)\oplus(\overline{\mathbb{V}}/i\mathbb{V})(r)=\mathbb{V}(r)\oplus\mathbb{Q}\langle\gamma_{r}\mid s_{\gamma}\leq r<t_{\gamma}\rangle,

where we have used the fact that the exact sequence

0\to\mathbb{V}\to\overline{\mathbb{V}}\to\overline{\mathbb{V}}/i\mathbb{V}\to 0
splits pointwise at each
r\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Finite sums of locally compact objects are locally compact, since a finite union of compact neighbourhoods is compact. Since elements of
i(\mathbb{V})
admit compact neighborhoods by assumption, it remains only to check that the elements in each direct summand of
\overline{\mathbb{V}}/i\mathbb{V}(r)\cong\oplus_{\gamma}\mathbb{I}_{[s_{\gamma},t_{\gamma})}(r)
do as well.

We factorise the pushout diagram to get

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{I}_{[t_{\gamma},\infty)}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{U}_{v_{t_{\gamma}}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{V}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\overline{\mathbb{V}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{I}_{[s_{\gamma},\infty)}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{U}_{\gamma_{s_{\gamma}}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{V}_{\gamma}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces v_{t_{\gamma}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\gamma_{s_{\gamma}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lrcorner}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lrcorner}

where
\mathbb{U}_{v_{t_{\gamma}}}
is a compact neighbourhood of
v_{t_{\gamma}}
. The characterisation of compact persistence modules as tame and point-wise finite dimensional implies that if
\mathbb{U}_{v_{t_{\gamma}}}
is compact, then so is
\mathbb{U}_{\gamma_{s_{\gamma}}}
. Since
\gamma_{r}\in\mathbb{U}_{\gamma_{s_{\gamma}}}
for all
s_{\gamma}\leq r<t_{\gamma}
, it follows that
\overline{\mathbb{V}}
is locally compact.
∎

4.3.
Cofibrant persistent complexes

We prove now that a persistent cochain complex is cofibrant only if it is locally compact, while while tameness suffices to prove compactness (
Lemma

4.21
). Local compactness is defined for persistent cochain complexes as for persistence modules, in terms of the existence of compact neighbourhoods.

Definition 4.11
.

A persistent complex
\mathbb{X}\in p\mathsf{Ch}^{*}_{\mathbb{Q}}
is
locally compact
if for each
x\in\mathbb{X}
there exists some compact sub-complex
\mathbb{W}_{x}\in(p\mathsf{Ch}^{*}_{\mathbb{Q}})^{\omega}\subseteq\mathbb{X}
such that
x\in\mathbb{X}
.

This definition is equivalent to degree-wise local compactness at the level of persistence modules.

Lemma 4.12
.

A persistent cochain complex
\mathbb{X}
is locally compact if and only if
\mathbb{X}^{k}\in p\mathsf{Vec}_{\mathbb{Q}}
is a locally compact persistence module for all
k\geq 0
.

Proof.

If
\mathbb{X}
is locally compact, then the restriction of the compact neighbourhood
\mathbb{W}_{x}\in p\mathsf{Ch}^{*}_{\mathbb{Q}}
to degree
k
is a compact neighbourhood
\mathbb{W}^{k}_{x}\in p\mathsf{Vec}_{\mathbb{Q}}
. On the other hand, suppose that
\mathbb{X}^{k}
is locally compact for all
k\geq 0
, and consider the interval decomposition
\mathbb{U}_{x}=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{n}\mathbb{I}_{[s_{i},t_{i})}
of the compact neighbourhood
\mathbb{U}_{x}
of
x
. Pick generators
x^{\prime}_{s_{i}}
for each interval
\mathbb{I}_{[s_{i},t_{i})}
, and let
\mathbb{U}_{dx^{\prime}_{s_{i}}}
be a compact neighbourhood of each
dx^{\prime}_{s_{i}}
. The persistence complex
\mathbb{W}_{x}
defined by the pushout

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\bigoplus_{i=0}^{n}\mathbb{S}^{k+1}_{[s_{i},t_{i})}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\bigcup_{i=0}^{n}\mathbb{U}_{dx^{\prime}_{s_{i}}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{X},}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\bigoplus_{i=0}^{n}\mathbb{D}^{k+1}_{[s_{i},t_{i})}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{W}_{x}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(dx_{s_{i}},0)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lrcorner}

which is a compact neighbourhood of
x
in
p\mathsf{Ch}^{*}_{\mathbb{Q}}
, since compactness is preserved under finite unions.
∎

Lemma 4.13
.

If
\mathbb{X}\in p\mathsf{Ch}^{*}_{\mathbb{Q}}
is locally compact in degrees
k>0
, then the pushout

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\bigoplus_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\mathbb{S}^{k_{\gamma}+1}_{[s_{\gamma},t_{\gamma})}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{X}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\bigoplus_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\mathbb{D}^{k_{\gamma}+1}_{s_{\gamma}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{Y}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lrcorner}

is locally compact in degrees
k>0
.

Proof.

By
Lemma

4.12
it suffices to check local compactness degree-wise. With our assumptions, this reduces to checking that local compactness is preserved under the following pushout

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\bigoplus_{\gamma\in\Gamma^{k}}\mathbb{I}_{[t_{\gamma},\infty)}^{k_{\gamma}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{X}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\bigoplus_{\gamma\in\Gamma^{k}}\mathbb{I}_{[s_{\gamma},\infty)}^{k_{\gamma}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{Y},}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lrcorner}

which follows from
Lemma

4.10
.
∎

Lemma 4.14
.

If
\mathbb{X}
is an
\mathbb{I}
-cell complex, then
\mathbb{X}
is locally compact in degrees
k>0
.

Proof.

Let
\mathbb{X}
be a sequential colimit,
\mathbb{X}=\mathsf{colim}_{i}\mathbb{X}_{i}
, built from pushouts

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\bigoplus_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{i}}\mathbb{S}^{k_{\gamma}+1}_{[s_{\gamma},t_{\gamma})}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{X}_{i}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\bigoplus_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{i}}\mathbb{D}^{k_{\gamma}+1}_{s_{\gamma}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{X}_{i+1}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lrcorner}

with
k_{\gamma}\geq 1
for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
, i.e.,
\mathbb{X}=\bigcup_{i=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{X}_{i}
. By
Lemma

4.13
, each
\mathbb{X}_{i}
is locally compact by induction. Since each element
x\in\mathbb{X}
must live in
\mathbb{X}_{i}
for some
i
, it admits a compact neighbourhood

\mathbb{W}_{x}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{X}_{i}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{X},

showing that
\mathbb{X}
is locally compact.
∎

Lemma 4.15
.

If
\mathbb{Y}\in p\mathsf{Ch}^{*}_{\mathbb{Q}}
and
\mathbb{Y}^{k}
contains a right-closed
y(s)\in\mathbb{Y}^{k}(s)
, then
\mathbb{Y}
is not cofibrant.

Proof.

An object
\mathbb{Y}
is cofibrant if and only if it is a retract
i:\mathbb{Y}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{X}
of an
\mathbb{I}
-cell complex
\mathbb{X}
, which is locally compact by
Lemma

4.14
. The image
i(y_{s})\neq 0
of
y_{s}
is a right-closed point of
\mathbb{X}^{k}
, which contradicts the local compactness of
\mathbb{X}
by
Lemma

4.15
.
∎

Example 4.16

(Non-equivalence with the injective model structure)
.

For
s\leq t
, the closed interval
\mathbb{I}_{[s,t]}\otimes S^{k}
for
k>0
is
not
cofibrant, since it has a right-closed point at
t
. Since all objects are cofibrant in the usual model structure on
\mathsf{Ch}^{*}_{\mathbb{Q}}
, all objects must be cofibrant in the injective model structure on
p\mathsf{Ch}^{*}_{\mathbb{Q}}
, demonstrating non-equivalence with our model structure.

Remark 4.17
.

Since there are no non-trivial maps
\mathbb{I}_{[p,q)}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{I}_{[s,t]}
for
s\leq p<q\leq t
, the closed interval
\mathbb{I}_{[s,t]}
contains no non-trivial compact sub-modules and thus fails (spectacularly) to be locally compact.

Given that cofibrant implies locally compact (above degree 0), a natural question is whether the converse is true. Over the tame objects, which are a sub-class of locally compact persistence complexes, the converse holds.

Lemma 4.18
.

If
i:\mathbb{X}\to\mathbb{Y}
is an injective map of tame
\mathbb{X},\mathbb{Y}\in(p\mathsf{Ch}^{*}_{\mathbb{Q}})^{tame}
, then
i
is a cofibration in the interval sphere model structure.

Proof.

Tame persistence modules form an abelian category
[
CGL21
]
, so the space of cocycles
Z\mathbb{X}=\mathrm{Ker}\,(d:\mathbb{X}\to\mathbb{X}[1])
is tame. The map reduces to an injective map
i:Z\mathbb{X}\to Z\mathbb{Y}
on cocycles. Since
Z\mathbb{X}
and
Z\mathbb{Y}
are tame, the quotient is tame and thus has a graded interval decompostion
Z\mathbb{Y}/i(Z\mathbb{X})=\oplus_{\gamma}\mathbb{I}_{[s_{\gamma},t_{\gamma})}^{k_{\gamma}}
at the level of graded persistence modules. For each interval pick generators
y_{s_{\gamma}}\in Z\mathbb{Y}/i(Z\mathbb{X})(s_{\gamma})
and note that
y_{t_{\gamma}}
must be in
i(Z\mathbb{X})
by the interval decomposition. We add missing cocycles into
i(\mathbb{X})
via the pushout

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\bigoplus_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\mathbb{S}_{[s_{\gamma},t_{\gamma})}^{k_{\gamma}+1}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{i(\mathbb{X})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\bigoplus_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\mathbb{D}_{s_{\gamma}}^{k_{\gamma}+1}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{i(\mathbb{X})\cup Z\mathbb{Y}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(0,y_{t_{\gamma}})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces y_{s_{\gamma}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lrcorner}

which is clearly still tame. Since quotients of tame objects by tame subobjects are tame, assign an interval decomposition

\mathbb{Y}/\big(i(\mathbb{X})\cup Z\mathbb{Y}\big)=\bigoplus_{\delta\in\Delta}\mathbb{I}_{[s_{\delta},t_{\delta})}^{k_{\delta}}

at the level of graded persistence modules. Assigning generators
y_{s_{\delta}}\in\mathbb{Y}
to each interval, we have that
dy_{s_{\delta}}\in Z\mathbb{Y}
by the cochain condition and that
y_{t_{\delta}}\in i(\mathbb{X})\cup Z\mathbb{Y}
by the interval decomposition. We add in the rest of
\mathbb{Y}
via the pushout

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\bigoplus_{\delta\in\Delta}\mathbb{S}_{[s_{\delta},t_{\delta})}^{k_{\delta}+1}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{i(\mathbb{X})\cup Z\mathbb{Y}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\bigoplus_{\delta\in\Delta}\mathbb{D}_{s_{\delta}}^{k_{\delta}+1}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{Y}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(dy_{s_{\delta}},y_{t_{\delta}})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces y_{s_{\delta}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lrcorner}

This realises
i:\mathbb{X}\to\mathbb{Y}
as a cofibration via the composition
\mathbb{X}\xrightarrow{\cong}i(\mathbb{X})\rightarrowtail i(\mathbb{X})\cup Z\mathbb{Y}\rightarrowtail\mathbb{Y}.

∎

Corollary 4.19
.

If
\mathbb{X}\in p\mathsf{Ch}^{*}_{\mathbb{Q}}
is tame then
\mathbb{X}
is cofibrant in the interval sphere model structure.

Example 4.20
.

The open interval
\mathbb{I}_{(s,t)}\otimes S^{k}\in p\mathsf{Ch}^{*}_{\mathbb{Q}}
for
k\geq 1
is an example of a cofibrant object which is locally compact but not tame. Such an object can be constructed via a sequence of attachments along
\mathbb{S}^{k+1}_{[s+\epsilon_{i},t)}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{D}^{k+1}_{s+\epsilon_{i}}
for any descreasing sequence
(\epsilon_{i}\mid i\in\mathbb{N},\epsilon_{i}\leq t-s)
that converges to
0
. We conjecture that locally compact fully characterises the connected cofibrant objects, but leave this open for future work.

Lemma 4.21
.

If
i:\mathbb{X}\to\mathbb{Y}
is an injective map of tame, connected objects
\mathbb{X},\mathbb{Y}\in p\mathsf{Ch}^{*}_{\mathbb{Q}}
, then
i
is a cofibration in the interval-sphere model structure.

Proof.

The map
i
induces an injective map
i:Z\mathbb{X}\to Z\mathbb{Y}
on cocycles. Since
\mathbb{X}
and
\mathbb{Y}
are tame, the quotient admits an interval decompostion
Z\mathbb{Y}/i(Z\mathbb{X})=\oplus_{\gamma}\mathbb{I}_{[s_{\gamma},t_{\gamma})}^{k_{\gamma}}
at the level of graded persistence modules. For each interval pick a generator
y_{s_{\gamma}}\in Z\mathbb{Y}/i(Z\mathbb{X})(s_{\gamma})
, and note that
y_{t_{\gamma}}
must be in
i(Z\mathbb{X})
by the interval decomposition. To add to
i(\mathbb{X})
the cocycles of
\mathbb{Y}
not in
i(z\mathbb{X})
, form the pushout

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\bigoplus_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\mathbb{S}_{[s_{\gamma},t_{\gamma})}^{k_{\gamma}+1}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{i(\mathbb{X})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\bigoplus_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\mathbb{D}_{s_{\gamma}}^{k_{\gamma}+1}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{i(\mathbb{X})\underset{i(Z\mathbb{X})}{\oplus}Z\mathbb{Y},}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(0,y_{t_{\gamma}})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces y_{s_{\gamma}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lrcorner}

which is clearly still tame. Consider the interval decomposition

\mathbb{Y}/\big(i(\mathbb{X})\underset{i(Z\mathbb{X})}{\oplus}Z\mathbb{Y}\big)=\bigoplus_{\delta\in\Delta}\mathbb{I}_{[s_{\delta},t_{\delta})}^{k_{\delta}}

at the level of graded persistence modules. Given generators
y_{s_{\delta}}\in\mathbb{Y}
of each interval, it follows that
dy_{s_{\delta}}\in Z\mathbb{Y}
and that
y_{t_{\delta}}\in i(\mathbb{X})\oplus_{i(Z\mathbb{X})}Z\mathbb{Y}
by the interval decomposition. Form now the pushout

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\bigoplus_{\delta\in\Delta}\mathbb{S}_{[s_{\delta},t_{\delta})}^{k_{\delta}+1}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{i(\mathbb{X})\underset{i(Z\mathbb{X})}{\oplus}Z\mathbb{Y}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\bigoplus_{\delta\in\Delta}\mathbb{D}_{s_{\delta}}^{k_{\delta}+1}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{Y}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(dy_{s_{\delta}},y_{t_{\delta}})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces y_{s_{\delta}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lrcorner}

We have thus exhibited
i:\mathbb{X}\to\mathbb{Y}
as a cofibration via the composite

\mathbb{X}\xrightarrow{\cong}i(\mathbb{X})\rightarrowtail i(\mathbb{X})\underset{i(Z\mathbb{X})}{\oplus}Z\mathbb{Y}\rightarrowtail\mathbb{Y}.

∎

Corollary 4.22
.

If
\mathbb{X}\in(p\mathsf{Ch}^{*}_{\mathbb{Q}})^{\mathsf{tame}}
is connected, then
\mathbb{X}
is cofibrant in the interval-sphere model structure.

Proof.

Apply the previous lemma to the injective map
0\to\mathbb{X}
.
∎

5.
Transfer of model structures

The interval-sphere model structure on
\mathsf{Ch}^{*}_{\mathbb{Q}}
enables us to relate the homotopy theory of persistent CDGAs to that of homotopy theory of copersistent simplicial sets.

5.1.
Rational homotopy theory model structures

CDGAs and minimal models are the basic algebraic tools of rational homotopy theory. We briefly review the literature, focusing on the results that we generalize to the persistent setting.

5.1.1.
CDGAs

Transfer along the free-forgetful adjunction

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Lambda:\mathsf{Ch}^{*}_{\mathbb{Q}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{CGDA}_{\mathbb{Q}}:U}

yields the combinatorial model structure constructed by
[
BG76
]
on the category
\mathsf{CGDA}_{\mathbb{Q}}
, for which the sets

\displaystyle\Lambda I

\displaystyle=\{\>\Lambda S^{k}\hookrightarrow\Lambda D^{k}\>,\>0\to\Lambda S^{0}\>|\>k\in\mathbb{N}\>\}

\displaystyle\Lambda J

\displaystyle=\{\>\Lambda D^{k}\to 0\>|\>k\in\mathbb{N}\>\}.

constitute generating cofibrations and trivial cofibrations.

5.1.2.
Simplicial sets

There is a model structure on
\mathsf{sSet}
whose

(1)

cofibrations are monomorphisms,

(2)

weak equivalences are

-isomorphisms, i.e., maps that induce isomorphisms on singular cohomology with
\mathbb{Q}
-coefficients.

This model structure is a left Bousfield localization of the Kan model structure on simplicial sets. We call it the
\mathbb{Q}
-model structure
and call its fibrations and weak equivalences
\mathbb{Q}
-fibrations
and
\mathbb{Q}
-equivalences
, respectively.

5.2.
Transfer of model structures

We begin with a transfer of the interval-sphere model structure to persistent CDGAs, followed by its comparison with a certain projective model structure on copersistent simplicial sets.

5.2.1.
Transferred model structure on
p\mathsf{CGDA}

Post-composition of persistent objects with the free-forgetful adjunction yields an adjunction

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Lambda:p\mathsf{Ch}^{*}_{\mathbb{Q}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p\mathsf{CGDA}_{\mathbb{Q}}:U}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\scriptscriptstyle\boldsymbol{\bot}}

at the level of persistent objects.

The category
p\mathsf{CGDA}_{\mathbb{Q}}
is locally finitely presentable, hence the small object argument can be applied to any set of maps. Its compact objects are the persistent CDGAs
\mathbb{A}
such that
\mathbb{A}(t)
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra of finite presentation for every
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
. We denote by
U^{-1}\mathbb{W}
the class of maps
f:\mathbb{A}\to\mathbb{B}
in
p\mathsf{CGDA}_{\mathbb{Q}}
such that
Uf\in\mathbb{W}
. In other words,
f(s)
is a quasi-isomorphism for every
s\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
. We also write

\displaystyle\Lambda\mathbb{I}

\displaystyle=\big\{\>\Lambda\mathbb{S}^{k}_{[s,t)}\to\Lambda\mathbb{D}^{k}_{s}\>|\>0\leq s\leq t\>\big\}

\displaystyle\Lambda\mathbb{J}

\displaystyle=\big\{\>\Lambda\mathbb{D}^{k}_{t}\to\Lambda\mathbb{D}^{k}_{s}\>|\>0\leq s\leq t\>\big\}

for the images of
\mathbb{I},\mathbb{J}\subseteq p\mathsf{Ch}^{*}_{\mathbb{Q}}
under the functor
\Lambda
.

Theorem 5.2
.

There is a combinatorial model structure on
p\mathsf{CGDA}_{\mathbb{Q}}
for which
U^{-1}\mathbb{W}
is the class of weak equivalences and the sets
\Lambda\mathbb{I},\Lambda\mathbb{J}
are, respectively, those of the generating cofibrations and trivial cofibrations.

Proof.

It suffices to check that the conditions for the recognition theorem of D. Kan
[
Hir03
, Theorem 11.3.2]
are satisfied. For this it is enough to show that

\mathsf{Cell}(\Lambda\mathbb{J})\subseteq U^{-1}\mathbb{W}

where
\mathsf{Cell}(\Lambda\mathbb{J})\subseteq p\mathsf{CGDA}_{\mathbb{Q}}
denotes the class of relative
\Lambda\mathbb{J}
-cell complexes. Since
\Lambda\mathbb{J}\subseteq U^{-1}\mathbb{W}
, it suffices to show that the class
U^{-1}\mathbb{W}
is closed under cobase change and transfinite composition. The latter condition is clear since the forgetful functor
U
preserves filtered colimits. We thus need to prove that the class
\Lambda\mathbb{J}
is closed under cobase change. To this end consider a pushout square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\Lambda\mathbb{D}^{k}_{t}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{A}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\Lambda\mathbb{D}^{k}_{s}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{B}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\ulcorner}

in
p\mathsf{CGDA}_{\mathbb{Q}}
. One sees immediately that
\mathbb{A}(u)\to\mathbb{B}(u)
is an isomorphism when
u<s
or
t\leq u
. When
s\leq u<t
, we find
\mathbb{B}(u)\cong\mathbb{A}(u)\otimes\Lambda(a_{u},da_{u})
so that the inclusion
\mathbb{A}(u)\subseteq\mathbb{B}(u)
is a quasi-isomorphism.
∎

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\langle-\rangle:p\mathsf{CGDA}_{\mathbb{Q}}}

5.4.
The Quillen pair

Finally, we show that our new model structure forms a Quillen pair when compared with the above projective model structure on copersistent simplicial sets.

Proposition 5.5
.

The contravariant adjunction

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\langle-\rangle:p\mathsf{CGDA}_{\mathbb{Q}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(p^{*}\mathsf{sSet})^{\mathsf{op}}:\mathcal{A}_{PL}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\scriptscriptstyle\boldsymbol{\bot}}

is a Quillen pair when
(p^{*}\mathsf{sSet})^{\mathsf{op}}
is endowed with the opposite of the projective
\mathbb{Q}
-model structure, and
p\mathsf{CGDA}_{\mathbb{Q}}
is given the interval-sphere model structure of
Theorem

5.2
.

Proof.

It suffices to check that
\langle-\rangle
sends the generating (trivial) cofibrations in
p\mathsf{CGDA}_{\mathbb{Q}}
to (trivial)
\mathbb{Q}
-fibrations in
p^{*}\mathsf{sSet}
. Given a map
f\in\Lambda\mathbb{I}
of the form
\Lambda\mathbb{S}^{k}_{[s,t)}\to\Lambda\mathbb{D}^{k}_{s}
, its component
\langle f\rangle(u)=\langle f(u)\rangle
is the map induced on spatial realizations by the Hirsch extension

\Lambda S^{k}\subseteq\Lambda D^{k}

when
s\leq u<t
and an isomorphism otherwise. These maps are all
\mathbb{Q}
-fibrations as a consequence of
Theorem

5.1
. Similarly, the components of any
f\in\Lambda\mathbb{J}
are Hirsch extensions and quasi-isomorphisms, hence map to trivial
\mathbb{Q}
-fibrations under spatial realization.
∎

Intraparotid lipoma in a 15-year-old girl: MRI-based diagnosis of a rare parotid lesion
Authors: Chakir Mahfoud, Hamza Bouayad, Abdelqader Sqalli Houssaini, Retal Hamza, Kettani Najwa, Fikri Meriem, Touarsa Firdaous
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntraparotid lipoma is a rare benign tumor of the parotid gland, accounting for less than 1% of all parotid tumors. Its slow growth and typically painless presentation often lead to delayed diagnosis, which relies primarily on imaging findings.We report the case of a 15-year-old girl presenting with a painless left cheek swelling, evolving since the age of five and causing progressive discomfort, without any associated clinical signs. Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) demonstrated the typical features of an intraparotid lipoma, including high signal intensity on T1- and T2-weighted images, complete signal suppression on fat-saturated sequences, and no significant enhancement following gadolinium administration, supporting a highly confident non-invasive diagnosis.This case highlights the value of MRI in characterizing a fatty parotid lesion and establishing a confident non-invasive diagnosis in an adolescent patient, potentially avoiding unnecessary invasive procedures, particularly in an anatomically complex region due to the close relationship between the parotid gland and the facial nerve.Keywords: Intraparotid lipoma, Parotid gland tumor, Fat-containing lesion

Case presentation

A 15-year-old girl presented with a painless swelling of the left cheek, first noticed at the age of five. The mass had gradually increased in size over the past decade, causing mild cosmetic concern and local discomfort, but no functional impairment. On physical examination, the swelling was soft, well-circumscribed, and mobile, without tenderness, erythema, or signs of inflammation. Facial nerve function was intact, and there were no associated lymphadenopathies or systemic symptoms.

MRI technique and findings

Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) of the parotid region was performed using a 1.5 Tesla system, including axial and coronal T1- and T2-weighted sequences, fat-saturated T2 sequences, and post-contrast T1-weighted sequences after intravenous gadolinium administration. MRI demonstrated a well-circumscribed, homogeneous mass involving both the superficial and deep lobes of the left parotid gland, measuring approximately 3.0 × 2.5 × 2.0 cm. The lesion exhibited high signal intensity on both T1- and T2-weighted images, with complete signal suppression on fat-saturated sequences, confirming its fatty nature. No significant enhancement was observed after gadolinium administration. The mass showed clear margins without infiltration of surrounding tissues, and the facial nerve was intact (Fig. 1, Fig. 2, Fig. 3, Fig. 4).

No imaging feature suggestive of facial nerve involvement was identified. The expected course of the facial nerve was inferred from the relationship of the lesion to established anatomical landmarks, including the retromandibular vein.

These imaging characteristics were highly suggestive of a benign intraparotid lipoma and supported a confident non-invasive diagnosis. Given the benign clinical and radiological features, conservative management was chosen, and the patient was referred for clinical follow-up.

Discussion

Although ultrasonography is widely utilized as a first-line imaging modality for parotid lesions due to its accessibility and lack of ionizing radiation, it was not performed in our patient. Ultrasound findings of intraparotid lipomas can be variable and non-specific, presenting as hyperechoic, isoechoic, or hypoechoic masses relative to the surrounding parotid parenchyma. Consequently, establishing a definitive diagnosis via ultrasound alone—especially for deep-seated lesions—can be challenging [1].

In contrast, magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) provides superior soft-tissue resolution and precise tissue characterization [1,2]. A diagnosis consistent with an intraparotid lipoma can be made with high confidence based on signal intensity identical to subcutaneous fat: homogeneous hyperintensity on both T1- and T2-weighted sequences, with complete signal suppression on fat-saturated techniques [1,3]. To rigorously confirm bulk fat and exclude low-grade liposarcoma or other aggressive variants, the absence of thick internal septa, nodular soft-tissue components, and abnormal contrast enhancement on post-contrast T1-weighted fat-suppressed images must be documented [3].

Furthermore, MRI allows accurate delineation of the lesion’s anatomical boundaries and its spatial relationship to critical structures. Although routine 1.5 T MRI sequences do not directly resolve the intraparotid branches of the facial nerve, its expected course can be reliably inferred using surrogate landmarks, such as the retromandibular vein and external carotid artery [2]. This anatomical mapping is essential for risk stratification and preoperative planning should surgical resection be indicated.

Intraparotid lipomas are exceptionally rare in pediatric and adolescent populations [4,5]. In this demographic, a slowly progressive, well-circumscribed fatty mass typically reflects a benign process. Recognizing these classic imaging criteria is crucial for differentiating lipomas from other parotid neoplasms (eg, pleomorphic adenomas, Warthin tumors, or vascular malformations) and avoiding unnecessary invasive procedures when conservative management or active surveillance is chosen [1,3].

The differential diagnosis for a parotid mass in a young patient includes both benign and malignant entities, as well as distinct fat-containing lesions:

Pleomorphic adenoma and Warthin tumor: Pleomorphic adenomas typically appear hypointense on T1-weighted images, markedly hyperintense on T2-weighted images, and exhibit gradual, heterogenous contrast enhancement . Warthin tumors usually present in older patients as well-defined cystic-solid masses, showing intermediate-to-low T1 signal, variable T2 signal, and early wash-in/wash-out enhancement dynamics . Neither entity demonstrates signal drop-out on fat-suppressed sequences [1,2].

Well-differentiated liposarcoma: Differentiating a benign lipoma from a low-grade or well-differentiated liposarcoma is critical. While lipomas are homogeneous fatty masses with thin or absent septa, liposarcomas typically present with thick (> 2 mm) nodular septa, non-adipose solid components, and variable contrast enhancement on post-gadolinium T1 fat-saturated sequences [1,3].

Sialolipoma and lipomatous variants: Sialolipomas are rare benign variants containing both mature adipose tissue and non-neoplastic glandular elements. On MRI, they appear as fatty masses containing fine, non-enhancing glandular strands . Lipomatous pleomorphic adenomas or oncocytomas may also contain intralesional fat, but are distinguished by their predominant solid, enhancing non-fatty architecture [1,2].

Vascular malformations and hemangiomas: Frequently encountered in pediatric populations, hemangiomas typically demonstrate lobulated hyperintensity on T2-weighted sequences, prominent serpentine vascular voids, and intense post-contrast enhancement, contrasting with the non-enhancing, purely fatty nature of lipomas [1,2].

Ultrasonographically, while lipomas often present as well-circumscribed, hyperechoic or variable lesions without intrinsic Doppler flow, adenomas and malignant tumors typically appear as hypoechoic solid masses with variable internal vascularity [1]. Ultimately, multi-sequence MRI—specifically demonstrating complete fat suppression without soft-tissue nodularity or pathological enhancement—remains the most reliable modality to exclude these differentials non-invasively [[1], [2], [3]].

Conclusion

Intraparotid lipoma is a rare benign entity that can be accurately diagnosed with MRI based on its characteristic radiological features. Recognition of these features allows for a confident, non-invasive diagnosis, guides clinical management, and avoids unnecessary interventions, particularly in anatomically complex regions such as the parotid gland in close proximity to the facial nerve.

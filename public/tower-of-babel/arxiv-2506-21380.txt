The Pythagoras number of fields of transcendence degree 1 1 over ℚ \mathbb{Q}
Olivier Benoist

Abstract
. We show that any sum of squares in a field of transcendence degree 1 1 over ℚ \mathbb{Q} is a sum of 5 5 squares, answering a question of Pop and Pfister. We deduce this result from a representation theorem, in k ⁡ ( C ) k(C) , for quadratic forms of rank ≥ 5 \geq 5 with coefficients in k k , where C C is a curve over a number field k k .



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Sums of squares and Pythagoras numbers

The
Pythagoras number

p(F)
of a field
F
is the smallest integer
p
such that any sum of squares in
F
is a sum of
p
squares in
F
(or
+\infty
if no such integer exists). It measures the complexity of sums of squares problems in the field
F
. We refer to
[
Pfi95
, Chap. 7 §1]
for a general introduction to this invariant.

Euler proved in
[
Eul60
]
that a nonnegative rational number is a sum of
4
squares of rational numbers (see
[
Pie93
]
for historical comments). Siegel
[
Sie21
]
extended Euler’s theorem to an arbitrary number field: an element of a number field
k
that is nonnegative in all its real embeddings is a sum of
4
squares in
k
. These results imply that
p(k)\leq 4

(and in fact
p(\mathbb{Q})=4
as
7
is not a sum of
3
squares in
\mathbb{Q}
).

In
[
Pou71
]
, Pourchet considered the case of the field
k(t)
of rational functions in one variable over a number field
k
. There, he proved that
p(k(t))\leq 5
for any number field
k
(and in fact
p(\mathbb{Q}(t))=5
as
t^{2}+7
is not a sum of
4
squares in
\mathbb{Q}(t)
), thereby improving on an earlier bound
p(\mathbb{Q}(t))\leq 8
due to Landau
[
Lan06
]
.

The aim of the present article is to prove similar results for general fields
F
of transcendence degree
1
over
\mathbb{Q}
. For such fields, the inequality
p(F)\leq 7
was shown by Colliot-Thélène in
[
CT86
]
. The stronger bound
p(F)\leq 6
was obtained by Pop in 1991
[
Pop91
]
(his result was published only recently in
[
Pop23
]
). Whether this is optimal or not remained open (see
[
Pfi95
, Chap. 7 Conjecture 1.10 (1)]
and
[
Pop23
, §3]
). We prove that it is not optimal, and that Pourchet’s bound on the Pythagoras number of
k(t)
can be extended to all these fields.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
F
be a field of transcendence degree
1
over
\mathbb{Q}
. Then
p(F)\leq 5
.

We refer to Corollary
5.11
for a reformulation of Theorem
1.1
that clarifies its relation with Hilbert’s 17th problem.

If
F
is a field of transcendence degree
d\geq 2
over
\mathbb{Q}
, then the bound
{p(F)\leq 2^{d+1}}
was obtained by Colliot-Thélène and Jannsen (see
[
CT86
,
CTJ91
,
Jan16
]
) as a consequence of the Milnor conjectures proven by Voevodsky in
[
Voe03
]
and of a local-global principle conjectured by Kato in
[
Kat86
]
and proven by Jannsen in
[
Jan16
]
. This bound is not known to be optimal (when
d=2
, this problem is closely related to the questions raised by Jannsen and Sujatha in
[
JS02
, Remarks 8]
).

1.2.
Quadratic forms over function fields of curves over number fields

The Hasse–Minkowski theorem, proved by Minkowski over
\mathbb{Q}
and by Hasse over a general number field, implies the following far-reaching generalization of the theorems of Euler and Siegel (see e.g.
[
Lam05
, I.3.5 and VI.3.5]
).
Let
q
be a nondegenerate quadratic form of rank
4
over a number field
k
. Then
q
represents an element of
k
if and only if this element is nonnegative (
resp.
nonpositive) at the real places of
k
at which
q
is positive definite (
resp.
negative definite).

In
[
Pou71
]
, Pourchet carried out a similar analysis over the field
k(t)
of rational functions in one variable over a number field
k
. There, he gives necessary and sufficient conditions for an element of
k(t)
to be represented by a given nondegenerate quadratic form of rank
5
over
k
(see
[
Pou71
, Corollaire 1 p. 98]
or Corollary
5.6
).

Let
C
be a geometrically connected smooth projective curve over a number field
k
. Let
q
be a nondegenerate quadratic form of rank
\geq 5
over
k
. Following Pourchet’s lead, we derive Theorem
1.1
from a
general representation theorem for
q
over
k(C)
, which is our main technical result. To state it, we introduce some notation.

Let
k_{v}
be the completion of
k
with respect to a place
v
. We let
q_{v}
and
C_{v}
be the quadratic form and the curve over
k_{v}
obtained from
q
and
C
by extension of scalars.

Let
v
be a real place of
k
. A rational function
f\in k(C)
is said to be
nonnegative
(
resp.

nonpositive
) at
v
if
f(x)\geq 0
(
resp.

{f(x)\leq 0}
) for all
{x\in C_{v}(\mathbb{R})}
that is not a pole of
f
. If
D
is a divisor on
C_{v}
, we let
\mathrm{cl}_{v}(D)\in H^{1}(C_{v}(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{Z}/2)=(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{\pi_{0}(C_{v}(\mathbb{R}))}
be the element associating with any connected component
\Gamma
of
C_{v}(\mathbb{R})
the parity of the number of points of
D
lying on
\Gamma
(counted with multiplicity). The morphism
\mathrm{cl}_{v}:\mathrm{Div}(C_{v})\to H^{1}(C_{v}(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{Z}/2)
factors through rational equivalence (as a rational function changes sign an even number of times on each
\Gamma
) and induces a morphism
\mathrm{cl}_{v}:\mathrm{Pic}(C_{v})\to H^{1}(C_{v}(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{Z}/2)
called the
Borel–Haefliger cycle class map
of
C_{v}
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
C
be a geometrically connected smooth projective curve over a number field
k
. Let
q
be a nondegenerate quadratic form of rank
r\geq 5
over
k
. Fix
f\in k(C)^{*}
. Write
\mathrm{div}(f)=E-2D
with
E
a reduced effective divisor.

Then
q
represents
f
in
k(C)
if and only if there exists
\mathcal{M}\in\mathrm{Pic}(C)

such that:

(i)

if
v
is a real place of
k
and
q_{v}
is positive definite (
resp.
negative definite), then
f
is nonnegative (
resp.
nonpositive) at
v
and
\mathrm{cl}_{v}(\mathcal{M})=0
;

(ii)

if
v
is a place of
k
with
q_{v}=\tilde{q}_{v}\perp\langle 1,-1\rangle
for some quadratic form
\tilde{q}_{v}
over
k_{v}
, then
there exist a line bundle
\mathcal{P}\in\mathrm{Pic}(C_{v})
and a divisor
\Delta
on
C_{v}
with
{\mathcal{M}\otimes\mathcal{P}^{\otimes 2}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{C_{v}}(\Delta-D)}
, such that
f
is invertible at
x
and
f(x)
is represented by
\tilde{q}_{v}
for all closed points
x
in the support of
\Delta
.

When
C=\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k}
, Theorem
1.2
can be used to recover Pourchet’s results
[
Pou71
, Corollaire 1 p. 98]
(see Corollary
5.6
).
The main novelty of Theorem
1.2
, however,
is that it applies to any curve
C
, possibly with more complicated geometry than
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k}
(with higher Picard rank, disconnected real loci, or finite places of bad reduction).

Being formulated in terms of the existence of a line bundle
\mathcal{M}
on
C
, the representation criterion given by Theorem
1.2
is influenced by arithmetic properties of the curve
C
(e.g. by the Mordell–Weil group of it Jacobian).

Additional work leads to significant simplifications to the statement of Theorem
1.2
in many particular instances.
This includes the cases when
C
is a nonsplit conic over
k
(see Corollary
5.4

(d)
), or when
q
does not have hyperbolic signature at any real place of
k
(see Corollary
5.4

(c)
; this is the consequence of Theorem
1.2
that leads to a proof of Theorem
1.1
).

1.3.
Analyzing and applying Theorem
1.2

Together, conditions
(i)
and
(ii)
in Theorem
1.2
constrain
\mathcal{M}
at all places of
k
.
Indeed, unless
v
is real and
q_{v}
is definite, the form
q_{v}
is isotropic (see
[
Lam05
, VI.2.12]
).

However, these constraints are nontrivial only at finitely many places of
k
. To see it, note that condition
(ii)
is not vacuous only if
\tilde{q}_{v}
is anisotropic (as isotropic quadratic forms are universal).
This is the case exactly when
v
is a real place at which
q_{v}
has hyperbolic signature
(r-1,1)
or
(1,r-1)
, or if
r=5
and
v
is a
p
-adic place at which the Hasse invariant of
q_{v}
is nontrivial (use
[
Lam05
, VI.2.12]
).

In particular, if
r\geq 6
, there are no constraints whatsoever at the
p
-adic places of
k
. In retrospect, this explains why the bound
p(k(C))\leq 6
due to Pop is easier to prove than the bound
p(k(C))\leq 5
provided by Theorem
1.1
.

Let us further comment on condition
(ii)
.
If
v
is a real place of
k
and
q_{v}
has signature
(r-1,1)
(
resp.

(1,r-1)
), this condition is equivalent to the following assertion: for any connected component
\Gamma
of
C_{v}(\mathbb{R})
on which
f
is nonpositive (
resp.
nonnegative), the class
\mathrm{cl}_{v}(\mathcal{M}(D))|_{\Gamma}\in H^{1}(\Gamma,\mathbb{Z}/2)=\mathbb{Z}/2
vanishes (see Remark
5.2
(ii)). It is therefore not difficult to check in practice.

Condition
(ii)
is harder to analyze in general at a
p
-adic place
v
of
k
.
In this case, we prove that condition
(ii)
is always satisfied for
{\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{O}_{C}}
(see Proposition
4.8
).
This surprising and nontrivial fact is key to the proofs of Corollary
5.4

(c)
and, through it, of Theorem
1.1
.
Its proof is based on the geometric study of a projective regular model
\pi:Y\to\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}_{k_{v}})
of
C_{v}
, on the Lang–Weil estimates, and on global class field theory applied to the function fields of the irreducible components of the special fiber of
Y
.
There is no counterpart to these arguments in Pourchet’s proof of the inequality
p(k(t))\leq 5
.

1.4.
Proving Theorem
1.2

Our proof of Theorem
1.2
is deeply inspired by Pourchet’s work.
We now outline the argument, assuming for simplicity that
q
is the sum of
5
squares quadratic form
\langle 1,1,1,1,1\rangle
(the one that is used in the application to Theorem
1.1
).

A crucial input are two theorems of Kato (the local-global principle
[
Kat86
, Theorem 0.8 (2)]
and the local criterion
[
Kat86
, Proposition 5.2]
). Combined with the Merkurjev–Suslin theorem, they control sums of
4
squares in
k(C)
through the following two statements. First, an element of
k(C)
is a sum of
4
squares if and only if it is a sum of
4
squares in
k_{v}(C_{v})
for all places
v
of
k
(see Proposition
2.2
). Second, if
v
is a
p
-adic place of
k
for some prime number
p
, an element of
k_{v}(C_{v})
is a sum of
4
squares if and only if it is a sum of
4
squares in the henselizations of
k_{v}(C_{v})
with respect to all integral divisors (both horizontal and vertical) of a proper regular model of
C_{v}
over
\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}_{k_{v}})
(see Proposition
2.3
).

Kato’s local-global principle was applied in this precise way in the above-mentioned works
[
CT86
,
CTJ91
,
Pop91
,
Jan16
]
, but our use of Kato’s local criterion is new. When
C=\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k}
, these results are much easier to prove (for instance, one may disregard the vertical divisors in the local criterion), and were already used by Pourchet (see
[
Pou71
, Propositions 3 et 4]
).

Let
f\in k(C)^{*}
be nonnegative at all real places of
k
. We wish to show that
f
is a sum of
5
squares. Write
f=\frac{\sigma}{\tau^{2}}
, where
\sigma\in H^{0}(C,\mathcal{L}^{\otimes 2})
has reduced zero locus, and
\tau
is a rational section of
\mathcal{L}
, for some
\mathcal{L}\in\mathrm{Pic}(C)
. (In the notation of Theorem
1.2
, one has
D=\{\tau=0\}
and
E=\{\sigma=0\}
.) We consider the equation

(1.1)

\sigma\alpha^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{5}\beta_{i}^{2},

where
\alpha\in H^{0}(C,\mathcal{M})
and
\beta_{i}\in H^{0}(C,\mathcal{L}\otimes\mathcal{M})
, for some
line bundle
\mathcal{M}
on
C
(also requiring that
\alpha\neq 0
and that the
\beta_{i}
have no common zero).
Manipulating sections of line bundles instead of rational functions is a new and essential feature of our approach. Shadows of it appear in Pourchet’s work in the guise of conditions on the degree of polynomials (see e.g. conditions (2) in
[
Pou71
, Théorèmes 1 et 2]
).

We first solve (
1.1
) locally. If
\mathcal{M}
is sufficiently ample, it turns out that (
1.1
) has a local solution at a place
v
of
k
if and only if
(i)
is satisfied (if
v
is real and
q_{v}
is definite), or
(ii)
is satisfied (otherwise). In the first case, the assertion is a consequence of theorems of Witt
[
Wit34
]
(see Proposition
3.3
).
The second case hinges on a purely geometric analysis of the birational geometry of the quadric bundle
\{\sigma T_{0}^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{5}T_{i}^{2}\}
over
C_{v}
(see Proposition
3.2
).

Suppose now that local solutions
(\alpha_{v},(\beta_{i,v})_{1\leq i\leq 5})
of (
1.1
) have been found.
Choose
\alpha
and
\beta_{5}
approximating the
\alpha_{v}
and the
\beta_{5,v}
at the real and
2
-adic places
v
of
k
(this choice of places depends on
q
).
Set
g_{v}:=\frac{\beta_{5,v}}{\tau\alpha_{v}}
and
g:=\frac{\beta_{5}}{\tau\alpha}
.
We claim that
f-g^{2}
is a sum of
4
squares everywhere locally.
To prove the claim at a real or
2
-adic place
v
, we use that
f-g^{2}
is close to
f-g_{v}^{2}
, which is a sum of
4
squares by (
1.1
). If
v
is real, the key point is to verify that the nonnegativity of
f-g_{v}^{2}
implies that of
f-g^{2}
. If
v
is
2
-adic, we rely on an openness result, in the
2
-adic topology, for sums of
4
squares in
k_{v}(C_{v})
, which we deduce from Kato’s local criterion (see Proposition
2.9
). At the other places of
k
, the claim is trivial.

It now follows from Kato’s local-global principle that
f-g^{2}
is a sum of
4
squares in
k(C)
, hence that
f
is a sum of
5
squares in
k(C)
.

. A

.

If

, we let

.

Let

. If

, we let
\langle\kern-1.00006pt\langle a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\rangle\kern-1.00006pt\rangle

. Beware that this quadratic form is rather denoted by
\langle\kern-1.00006pt\langle-a_{1},\dots,-a_{n}\rangle\kern-1.00006pt\rangle
in
[
Lam05
]
(see
[
Lam05
, X.1.1]
).
We also denote by
{\{a\}\in H^{1}(k,\mathbb{Z}/2)}
the image of

by the boundary map

Let

be a nondegenerate quadratic form of rank

. The

such that whenever

{a_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{*}}

(

) of the

is

if it is positive definite (of signature

) or negative definite (of signature
(0,r)
). It has

if

or

.

Let
q
be a quadratic form over a field
k
. Let
E
be a
k
-vector space of dimension
1
.
An element of

is said to be a

for some

represented by

and some

.
Observe that this notion depends on the presentation of the vector space

as a tensor square. If

and

is a line bundle on

, we say that a section
{\sigma\in H^{0}(X,\mathcal{L}^{\otimes 2})}

. In particular, it makes sense to say that

.
If moreover
{k=\mathbb{R}}
, we will say that
\sigma\in H^{0}(X,\mathcal{L}^{\otimes 2})
is

(

) if

is a square (

.

Let

be a prime number. A

-adic field
is a finite extension of

. We let

denote the ring of integers of a

-adic field

.

We let

). If

.

2.
Consequences of Kato’s theorems

In this section, we draw consequences from
[
Kat86
]
. Our main new result is Proposition
2.9
.

2.1.
The local-global principle and the local criterion

Recall the following consequence of the Merkurjev–Suslin theorem (see
[
MS83
, Theorem 12.1 a)
\Leftrightarrow
c)]
).

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
k
be a field with
2\in k^{*}
. Fix
a,b,f\in k^{*}
. Then
\langle\kern-1.00006pt\langle a,b\rangle\kern-1.00006pt\rangle
represents
f
in
k
if and only if
\{a\}\cdot\{b\}\cdot\{-f\}\in H^{3}(k,\mathbb{Z}/2)
vanishes.

The next proposition appears in
[
Kat86
, top of p. 146]
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
C
be a geometrically connected smooth projective curve over a number field
k
. Fix
a,b\in k^{*}
and
f\in k(C)^{*}
. Then
\langle\kern-1.00006pt\langle a,b\rangle\kern-1.00006pt\rangle
represents
f
in
k(C)
if and only if it represents
f
in
k_{v}(C_{v})
for all places
v
of
k
.

Proof.

In
[
Kat86
, Theorem 0.8 (2)]
, Kato proved that the restriction map

H^{3}(k(C),\mathbb{Z}/2)\to\prod_{v}H^{3}(k_{v}(C_{v}),\mathbb{Z}/2),

where
v
runs over all places of
k
, is injective.
To conclude, combine this fact with Proposition
2.1
.
∎

Here is another proposition which follows at once from Kato’s work.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
C
be a geometrically connected smooth projective curve over a
p
-adic field
k
. Let
\pi:X\to\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}_{k})
be a proper regular model of
C
. Fix
a,b\in k^{*}
and
f\in k(C)^{*}
.
Then
\langle\kern-1.00006pt\langle a,b\rangle\kern-1.00006pt\rangle
represents
f
in
k(C)
if and only if it represents
f
in the henselization
k(C)^{\mathrm{h}}_{x}
of
k(C)
at all codimension
1
points
x
of
X
.

Proof.

Let
x\in X
be a point of codimension
1
. Kato defines a residue map
{\partial_{x}:H^{3}(k(C),\mathbb{Z}/2)\to\mathrm{Br}(\kappa(x))[2]}
(see
[
Kat86
, (i) p. 149]
if
\kappa(x)
has characteristic not
2
and
[
Kat86
, (ii) p. 150]
otherwise, both applied with
q=2
and
i=1
, noting that
H^{2}(\kappa(x),\mathbb{Z}/2(1))=\mathrm{Br}(\kappa(x))[2]
in Kato’s notation).

Kato proves in
[
Kat86
, Proposition 5.2]
that residue maps induce a quasi-isomorphism between two complexes associated with the general and the special fiber of
X
. Evaluated in degree
1
, this assertion exactly means that
\bigcap_{x}\mathrm{Ker}(\partial_{x})=0
, where
x
runs over all codimension
1
points of
X
. As
\partial_{x}
factors through
H^{3}(k(C)^{\mathrm{h}}_{x},\mathbb{Z}/2)
, by its very construction,
we deduce the injectivity of the restriction map

(2.1)

H^{3}(k(C),\mathbb{Z}/2)\to\prod_{x}H^{3}(k(C)^{\mathrm{h}}_{x},\mathbb{Z}/2),

where
x
runs over all codimension
1
points of
X
.

The proposition follows from the injectivity of (
2.1

Fix

. This remark allows us to transfer facts about representations of functions by

Let

3.2.
The isotropic case

The next proposition is the main result of this section.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
k
be an infinite field with
2\in k^{*}
.
Choose
{a_{1},\dots,a_{r}\in k^{*}}
for some
r\geq 3
. Assume that
\langle a_{1},\dots a_{r}\rangle\simeq\langle b_{1},\dots,b_{r-2}\rangle\perp h
, where
{b_{1},\dots,b_{r-2}\in k^{*}}

and
h
is the hyperbolic plane
(x,y)\mapsto xy
.
Let
C
be a connected smooth projective curve over
k
. Fix
\mathcal{L}
,
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{A}
in
\mathrm{Pic}(C)
, with
\mathcal{A}
ample. For
{\sigma\in H^{0}(C,\mathcal{L}^{\otimes 2})}
nonzero with reduced zero locus, the following assertions are
equivalent
.

(i)

For some
\mathcal{N}\in\mathrm{Pic}(C)
, there exist a nonzero section
\alpha\in H^{0}(C,\mathcal{M}\otimes\mathcal{N}^{\otimes 2})
, and sections
\beta_{i}\in H^{0}(C,\mathcal{L}\otimes\mathcal{M}\otimes\mathcal{N}^{\otimes 2})
no two of which have a common zero, such that

\sigma\alpha^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}\beta_{i}^{2}
.

(ii)

Assertion
(i)
holds with
\mathcal{N}=\mathcal{A}^{\otimes l}
for all large enough integers
l
.

(iii)

There exist
\mathcal{P}\in\mathrm{Pic}(C)
and a divisor
\Delta
on
C
with
\mathcal{O}_{C}(\Delta)\simeq\mathcal{L}\otimes\mathcal{M}\otimes\mathcal{P}^{\otimes 2}
, such that
\sigma
is nonzero and represented by
\langle b_{1},\dots,b_{r-2}\rangle
at all points of the support of
\Delta
.

Proof.

We use the notation of §
3.1
.
Solutions to

(3.2)

\sigma\alpha^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}\beta_{i}^{2}

with
\alpha\in H^{0}(C,\mathcal{M}\otimes\mathcal{N}^{\otimes 2})
and
\beta_{i}\in H^{0}(C,\mathcal{L}\otimes\mathcal{M}\otimes\mathcal{N}^{\otimes 2})
without common zero are in bijection with solutions to

(3.3)

\sigma\alpha^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{r-2}b_{i}\gamma_{i}^{2}+\gamma_{r-1}\gamma_{r}

with
\alpha\in H^{0}(C,\mathcal{M}\otimes\mathcal{N}^{\otimes 2})
and
\gamma_{i}\in H^{0}(C,\mathcal{L}\otimes\mathcal{M}\otimes\mathcal{N}^{\otimes 2})
without common zero (making use of an isomorphism
\langle a_{1},\dots a_{r}\rangle\simeq\langle b_{1},\dots,b_{r-2}\rangle\perp h
).
In turn, they are in bijection with sections
s:C\to X
of
f|_{X}:X\to C
with
s^{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}}(1)\simeq\mathcal{L}\otimes\mathcal{M}\otimes\mathcal{N}^{\otimes 2}
.

That
(ii)
implies
(i)
is clear.
Assume that
(i)
holds. Let
\alpha
and
\beta_{i}
be as in
(i)
for some
\mathcal{N}\in\mathrm{Pic}(C)
.
Consider the associated
\alpha
and
\gamma_{i}
as in (
3.3
). As the
\beta_{i}
do not have a common zero, neither do the
\gamma_{i}
.
Consequently, after applying a general element of the special orthogonal group of
\langle b_{1},\dots,b_{r-2}\rangle\perp h
(using that this group is
k
-unirational,
see
[
Bor91
, V, Theorem 18.2]
, and that
k
is infinite), one can assume that
\gamma_{r}
does not vanish on any zero of
\sigma
or of
\alpha
.
Then
\Delta:=\{\gamma_{r}=0\}
satisfies
(iii)
with
\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{N}
, as equation (
3.3
) shows.

To conclude, we show that
(iii)
implies
(ii)
.
Let
\mathcal{P}
and
\Delta
be as in
(iii)
. After modifying
\Delta
by a multiple of
2
(and
\mathcal{P}
by a square), we may assume that
\Delta
is effective and reduced. Then
(iii)
shows the existence of a section
s^{\prime}_{\Delta}:\Delta\to Y
of
q:\mathbb{P}^{\prime}\to C
over
\Delta
. This section corresponds to a surjection

(3.4)

(\mathcal{L}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{C}^{\oplus r})|_{\Delta}\to\mathcal{O}_{\Delta}\simeq(\mathcal{L}\otimes\mathcal{M}\otimes\mathcal{P}\otimes\mathcal{A}^{\otimes l})|_{\Delta},

where we chose a trivialization of
\mathcal{L}\otimes\mathcal{M}\otimes\mathcal{P}\otimes\mathcal{A}^{\otimes l}
on
\Delta
.
The ampleness of
\mathcal{A}
implies that, for
l
large enough, one can lift (
3.4
) to a surjection

(3.5)

\mathcal{L}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{C}^{\oplus r}\to\mathcal{L}\otimes\mathcal{M}\otimes\mathcal{P}\otimes\mathcal{A}^{\otimes l},

corresponding to a section
s^{\prime}:C\to\mathbb{P}^{\prime}
of
g
with
(s^{\prime})^{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{\prime}}(1)\simeq\mathcal{L}\otimes\mathcal{M}\otimes\mathcal{P}\otimes\mathcal{A}^{\otimes l}
. For
l
large enough, choosing the lift (
3.5
) general ensures that
s^{\prime}(C)
meets
Y
transversally along
s^{\prime}(\Delta)
(and at no other point), and that
s^{\prime}(C)\not\subset\{U_{0}=0\}\cup\{U_{r-1}=0\}
.

Let
\widetilde{s}:C\to\widetilde{X}
be the strict transform of
s^{\prime}
, and define
s:=\widetilde{s}\circ p:C\to X
, so
s
is a section of
f|_{X}
. As
s^{\prime}(C)
meets
Y
transversally along
s^{\prime}(\Delta)
and at no other point, we see that
\widetilde{s}(C)
meets
F
transversally along
\widetilde{s}(\Delta)
and at no other point, and hence that

\widetilde{s}\hskip 0.89996pt^{*}\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{X}}(F)=\mathcal{O}_{C}(\Delta)\simeq\mathcal{L}\otimes\mathcal{M}\otimes\mathcal{P}^{\otimes 2}
. It then follows from the second equality of Lemma
3.1
(also using
(s^{\prime})^{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{\prime}}(1)\simeq\mathcal{L}\otimes\mathcal{M}\otimes\mathcal{P}\otimes\mathcal{A}^{\otimes l}
), that
\widetilde{s}\hskip 0.89996pt^{*}\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{X}}(E)\simeq\mathcal{P}^{\otimes-1}\otimes\mathcal{A}^{\otimes l}
. The second equality of Lemma

now implies that
s^{*}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}}(1)|_{X})\simeq\mathcal{L}\otimes\mathcal{M}\otimes\mathcal{A}^{\otimes 2l}

.

As we ensured that

, one has

. The

do not have a common zero (as

cannot vanish on such a common zero, equation (

) shows that it would be a multiple zero of

).
Consequently, after applying a general element of the special orthogonal group of
\langle a_{1},\dots,a_{r}\rangle
(using that this group is

-unirational, see

, V, Theorem 18.2]
, and that

have a common zero. This completes the proof of
(ii)
.
∎

3.3.
The definite case

The next proposition will serve as a substitute to Proposition
3.2
for definite quadratic forms over the reals. For a connected smooth projective curve
C
over
\mathbb{R}
, we let
\mathrm{cl}_{\mathbb{R}}:\mathrm{Pic}(C)\to H^{1}(C(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{Z}/2)
denote the Borel–Haefliger cycle class map defined as in §
1.2
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Fix
r\geq 2
. Let
C
be a connected smooth projective curve over
\mathbb{R}
. Fix
\mathcal{L}
,
\mathcal{M}

and
\mathcal{A}
in
\mathrm{Pic}(C)
, with
\mathcal{A}
ample. For
{\sigma\in H^{0}(C,\mathcal{L}^{\otimes 2})}
nonzero with reduced zero locus, the following assertions are
equivalent
.

(i)

For some
\mathcal{N}\in\mathrm{Pic}(C)
, there exist a nonzero section
\alpha\in H^{0}(C,\mathcal{M}\otimes\mathcal{N}^{\otimes 2})
, and sections
\beta_{i}\in H^{0}(C,\mathcal{L}\otimes\mathcal{M}\otimes\mathcal{N}^{\otimes 2})
without a common real zero, such that
\sigma\alpha^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\beta_{i}^{2}
.

(ii)

Assertion
(i)
holds with
\mathcal{N}=\mathcal{A}^{\otimes l}
for all large enough integers
l
.

(iii)

The section
\sigma
is nonnegative and
\mathrm{cl}_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathcal{M})=0
.

Proof.

It is obvious that
(ii)
implies
(i)
. Assume that
(i)
holds. The nonnegativity of
\sigma
is clear from the equation
\sigma\alpha^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\beta_{i}^{2}
, as
\alpha\neq 0
. Suppose for contradiction that
\mathrm{cl}_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathcal{M})=\mathrm{cl}_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathcal{M}\otimes\mathcal{N}^{\otimes 2})\neq 0
. Then
\alpha\in H^{0}(C,\mathcal{M}\otimes\mathcal{N}^{\otimes 2})
has at least one real zero. At such a real zero, the equation
\sigma\alpha^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\beta_{i}^{2}
shows that all the
\beta_{i}
must vanish. This contradicts our hypothesis and proves
(iii)
.

Assume now that
(iii)
holds. Let
\tau
be a nonzero rational section of
\mathcal{L}
. The rational function
{f:=\frac{\sigma}{\tau^{2}}\in\mathbb{R}(C)}
is nonnegative. By a theorem of Witt (see
[
Wit34
, I p. 4]
), one can therefore write
f=g^{2}+h^{2}
with
g,h\in\mathbb{R}(C)
. By another theorem of Witt (see
[
Wit34
, III p. 4]
), the hypothesis that
\mathrm{cl}_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathcal{M})=0
implies that
\mathcal{M}
has a nonzero rational section
\delta
with no real zero and no real pole.

Then
\sigma\delta^{2}=(g\delta\tau)^{2}+(h\delta\tau)^{2}
.
As the rational section
\sigma\delta^{2}
of
\mathcal{L}^{\otimes 2}\otimes\mathcal{M}^{\otimes 2}
has no real poles, neither
g\delta\tau
nor
h\delta\tau
have real poles. One can therefore choose an effective divisor
D
on
C
, whose support has no real points, with
{D+\mathrm{div}(g\delta\tau)}
and
{D+\mathrm{div}(h\delta\tau)}
effective. Choose
l
large enough, so
\mathcal{A}^{\otimes l}(-D)
is globally generated. Let
(\varepsilon_{i})_{1\leq i\leq n}
be a basis of
H^{0}(C,\mathcal{A}^{\otimes l}(-D))
. Then
\varepsilon:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\varepsilon_{i}^{2}
, viewed as an element of
H^{0}(C,\mathcal{A}^{\otimes 2l})
vanishing on
D
, has no real zero. After maybe changing
\varepsilon_{1}
, we may assume that
\varepsilon\neq 0
.

Set
\alpha:=\delta\varepsilon
, and
(\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{r}):=(g\delta\varepsilon\tau,h\delta\varepsilon\tau,0,\dots,0)
. These choices ensure that

(3.6)

\sigma\alpha^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\beta_{i}^{2}

and that
\alpha\neq 0
.
As neither
\delta
nor
\varepsilon
have a real zero, a common real zero of
\beta_{1}
and
\beta_{2}
would have to be a multiple zero of
\sigma
(by (
3.6
)), which is impossible. So
\beta_{1}
and
\beta_{2}
have no common real zero, which proves
(ii)
.
∎

4.
Curves over finite and
p
-adic fields

The aim of this section is to prove Proposition
4.8
, which plays a crucial role in our results on Pythagoras numbers.

In §
4.1
, we use global class field theory to describe the subgroup of the Picard group of a reduced projective curve over a finite field that is generated by classes of smooth closed points of degree divisible by
d
(see Proposition
4.3
). In §
4.2
, we deduce an analogous result for smooth projective curves over
p
-adic fields (see Proposition
4.4
). We use it in §
4.3
to control the values of a section of the square of line bundle on a
p
-adic curve, and in particular to prove Proposition
4.8
.

4.1.
Line bundles on curves over finite fields

In this paragraph, we fix a finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
of cardinality
q
and an algebraic closure
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. For
r\geq 1
, we let
{\mathbb{F}_{q}\subset\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}}\subset\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}
be the subextension that is of degree
r
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
We first recall a standard consequence of the Lang–Weil estimates.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
X
be a geometrically integral variety of dimension
n\geq 1
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. For
r\geq 1
, let
M_{X}(r)
be the number of closed points of
X
that are of degree
r
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Then there exists
K\geq 0
such that

(4.1)

|M_{X}(r)-\frac{q^{nr}}{r}|\leq K\,\frac{q^{(n-\frac{1}{2})r}}{r}\textrm{\hskip 10.22217pt for all }r\geq 1.

In particular, one has
M_{X}(r)>0
for all large enough integers
r\geq 1
.

Proof.

Let
N_{X}(r)
be the cardinality of
X(\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}})
.
One has
N_{X}(r)=\sum_{s|r}sM_{X}(s)
. The Möbius inversion formula (for which see e.g.
[
Apo76
, Theorem 2.9]
) shows that

(4.2)

M_{X}(r)=\frac{1}{r}\sum_{s\mid r}\mu(\frac{r}{s})N_{X}(s),

where
\mu
is the Möbius function.
On the other hand, it follows from the Lang–Weil estimates
[
LW54
, Theorem 1]
that there exists a constant
K^{\prime}\geq 0
such that

(4.3)

|N_{X}(r)-q^{nr}|\leq K^{\prime}\,q^{(n-\frac{1}{2})r}

for all
r\geq 1
. We deduce from (
4.2
) and (
4.3
) that

(4.4)

|M_{X}(r)-\frac{q^{nr}}{r}|\leq K^{\prime}\,\frac{q^{(n-\frac{1}{2})r}}{r}+\frac{1}{r}\sum_{s\leq\lfloor\frac{r}{2}\rfloor}N_{X}(s).

In view of (
4.3
), one has
N_{X}(r)\leq(1+K^{\prime})\,q^{nr}
for all
r\geq 1
. We deduce at once that
\sum_{s\leq\lfloor\frac{r}{2}\rfloor}N_{X}(s)\leq(1+K^{\prime})\,q^{n(\frac{r}{2}+1)}
. Combining this fact with (
4.4
) yields the estimate (
4.1
) for an appropriate constant
K
.
∎

Let
X
be a connected smooth projective curve over a finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
Let
D\subset X
be an effective divisor.
We define
\mathrm{Pic}(X,D)
to be the set of isomorphism classes of pairs
(\mathcal{L},\varphi)
, where
\mathcal{L}
is a line bundle on
X
and
{\varphi:\mathcal{O}_{D}{\mathrel{\to}^{\,\sim\,}}\mathcal{L}|_{D}}
is a trivialization of
\mathcal{L}
on

.
We endow
\mathrm{Pic}(X,D)
with the group structure induced by tensor product.
With any closed point
{x\in X\setminus D}
, we associate the class
{[x]\in\mathrm{Pic}(X,D)}
of the line bundle
\mathcal{O}_{X}(x)
equipped with the restriction to
D
of its canonical trivialization on
X\setminus\{x\}
.
For any
{d\geq 0}
, we let
\mathrm{Pic}(X,D)^{d}
be the subgroup of
\mathrm{Pic}(X,D)
consisting of those

such that the degree of
\mathcal{L}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is a multiple of
d
.
As there are only finitely many isomorphism classes of degree
0

X
, the group

Let
\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{ab}}(X\setminus D)
be the abelianization of the étale fundamental group of

\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{ab}}(X,D)
be its quotient classifying those abelian coverings whose ramification is bounded by

\deg:\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{ab}}(X,D)\to\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{q})\simeq\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}

.

4.3.
Values of sections of line bundles over
p
-adic curves

The next proposition controls the closed points of a
p
-adic curve at which a given section of the square of a line bundle is a nonzero square.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let

be a connected smooth projective curve over a
p
-adic field

.
Let
\mathcal{L}
be a line bundle on

. Choose
{\sigma\in H^{0}(C,\mathcal{L}^{\otimes 2})}
nonzero with reduced zero locus.
Then there exists a divisor
\Delta
on

with

such that
the section
\sigma
is nonzero and not a square at
x
,
for all those closed points

in the support of

that have odd degree over

.

Proof.

Let
\kappa
he the residue field of
\mathcal{O}_{k}
.
Let
\pi:Y\to\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}_{k})
be a flat projective morphism with
Y
regular whose generic fiber is isomorphic to

.
Extend
\mathcal{L}
to a line bundle

on

in such a way that
\sigma
extends to a section
\tau\in H^{0}(Y,\mathcal{N}^{\otimes 2})
.
Let
X
be the special fiber of
\pi
.
After replacing

with a modification, we may assume that

is a simple normal crossings divisor in
Y
.
Replacing

with
\mathcal{N}(-E)
for some well-chosen divisor

on
Y
supported on

, and dividing
\tau
by the square of an equation of

, we may assume that
\tau
has reduced zero locus.

Let
(X_{i})_{i\in I}
be the reduced irreducible components of

. We view them as varieties over
\kappa
. Let
(m_{i})_{i\in I}
be their multiplicities in
X
.
Define

For
j\in J
, let
\kappa_{j}
be the algebraic closure of
\kappa
in
X_{j}
.
As

carries a line bundle of odd degree over

(by definition of

), we see that

is an odd degree extension of

. Moreover, since

is smooth, it is geometrically integral over

.
We claim that for all

, there exists an integral divisor

that is flat over
\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}_{k})
, such that
\mathcal{O}_{Y}(D_{j})|_{X_{j}}

,

, and such that
\sigma
is nonzero and not a square at the generic point of

.

To prove the claim, we first assume that

vanishes identically on

.
Choose a closed point

that does not belong to any other irreducible component of

and that has odd degree over

, hence over
\kappa
(such a point exists by the Lang–Weil estimates
[
LW54
, Corollary 3]
).
Let

be an integral divisor that meets

transversally at

. By henselianity of

, the divisor

meets

only at the point

(use
[
SP
, Lemma
04GH
(1)]
).
It follows that

has odd degree over

. In addition, since

vanishes at order exactly one along

, its restriction to

vanishes at order exactly one along

. Consequently, the section

cannot be a square at the generic point of

.

Assume now that
\tau
does not vanish identically on

. The zero locus of

on

is reduced (because

is simple normal crossings and
{\{\tau=0\}}
is reduced) and nonempty (otherwise

). The double cover

with equation

Let

be the dense open subset consisting of those points whose images by

By the Lang–Weil estimates

Note that

Remark 4.6
.

In the setting of Proposition

, it is in general not true that all line bundles
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Pic}(C)
are of the form
\mathcal{O}_{C}(\Delta)
with
\Delta
as above
(i.e., such that for all odd degree closed points

in the support of

, the section

is nonzero and not a square at

An example is provided by

, with

and

is not of the required form.

The following lemma is well-known.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let

be a
p
-adic field.
Let

be a nondegenerate quadratic form of rank
\geq 3
over

.
Then

is isotropic over all finite extensions

Proof.

We may assume that
q
has rank
3
.
Let

be (a representative of) the determinant of

. Then
{\langle 1\rangle\perp dq}
is the norm form of a quaternion algebra over

(see
[

, III.2]
), which splits over

[

, XIII.3, Proposition 7]

{\langle 1\rangle\perp dq}

[

.
∎

We may now prove the main result of this section.

Proposition 4.8
.

Let
C
be a connected smooth projective curve over a
p
-adic field
k
. Let
\mathcal{L}
be a line bundle on
C
. Choose
\sigma\in H^{0}(C,\mathcal{L}^{\otimes 2})
nonzero with reduced zero locus.
Let
q
be a nondegenerate quadratic form of rank
\geq 3
over
k
.
There exists a divisor
\Delta
on
C
with
\mathcal{L}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{C}(\Delta)
such that
\sigma
is nonzero and represented by

at all points of the support of
\Delta
.

Proof.

We may assume that
q
has rank

. As isotropic forms are universal (see
[
Lam05
, I.3.4]
), we may also assume that

is anisotropic.

By
[

, VI.2.15 (2)]
, the form

is not universal. Fix

not represented by

.
After replacing
\sigma
and

with

and

.
Then

. We deduce from

In addition, the form

∎

5.
Curves over number fields

We finally present the proofs of our main results.

5.1.
The representation theorem

We start with Theorem

.

Theorem 5.1

be a nondegenerate quadratic form of rank

is nonnegative (

is a place of

Set

Write

Step 1
.

Conditions

and
(ii)

Assume that

is positive definite and to

.

Step 2
.

Conditions
(i)
and
(ii)

Assume that

and
(ii)
. Fix

\Gamma
of
C_{v}(\mathbb{R})
on which
f
is nonpositive (
resp.
nonnegative), the class
\mathrm{cl}_{v}(\mathcal{M}(D))|_{\Gamma}\in H^{1}(\Gamma,\mathbb{Z}/2)=\mathbb{Z}/2
vanishes (see Lemma
5.3
below).

(iii)
If
v
is a
p
-adic place, then condition
(ii)
in Theorem
5.1
is always satisfied for
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{O}_{C}
, by Proposition
4.8
(one can take
\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{O}_{C_{v}}
).

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
C
be a connected smooth projective curve over
\mathbb{R}
. Fix
f\in\mathbb{R}(C)^{*}
.
Write
\mathrm{div}(f)=E-2D
with
E
a reduced effective divisor. For all
\mathcal{M}\in\mathrm{Pic}(C)
, the following assertions are equivalent.

(i)

There exist
\mathcal{P}\in\mathrm{Pic}(C)
and a divisor
\Delta
on
C
with
{\mathcal{M}\otimes\mathcal{P}^{\otimes 2}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{C}(\Delta-D)}
such that
f
is invertible and a square at
x
, for all
x
in the support of
\Delta
.

(ii)

For any connected component
\Gamma
of
C(\mathbb{R})
on which
f
is nonpositive, the Borel–Haefliger class
\mathrm{cl}_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathcal{M}(D))|_{\Gamma}\in H^{1}(\Gamma,\mathbb{Z}/2)=\mathbb{Z}/2
vanishes.

Proof.

Assume that
(i)
holds. If
\mathrm{cl}_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathcal{M}(D))|_{\Gamma}\neq 0
, then
\mathrm{cl}_{\mathbb{R}}(\Delta)|_{\Gamma}\neq 0
, and the support of
\Delta
must meet
\Gamma
at some point. At such a point, the rational function
f
is invertible and a square, hence positive. This proves
(ii)
.

Conversely, suppose that
(ii)
holds. Then there exists a divisor
\Delta
on
C
such that
f
is invertible and positive at all real points of the support of
\Delta
, and such that
{\mathrm{cl}_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathcal{M}(D-\Delta))=0}
. By a theorem of Witt (see
[
Wit34
, III p. 4]
), one can modify
\Delta
by a divisor whose support has no real points to ensure that moreover
\mathcal{M}(D-\Delta)\simeq\mathcal{O}_{C}
. Write
\Delta=\sum_{i}n_{i}x_{i}+\sum_{j}m_{j}y_{j}
, where the
x_{i}
(
resp.
the
y_{j}
) are closed points of
C
with complex (
resp.
real) residue fields. Choose
N\geq 0
such that
\mathcal{O}_{C}(Nx_{i})
is very ample for all
i
. Let
\sigma_{i}\in H^{0}(C,\mathcal{O}_{C}(x_{i}))
be an equation of
x_{i}
. Let
\tau_{i}\in H^{0}(C,\mathcal{O}_{C}(Nx_{i}))
be a small perturbation of
\sigma_{i}^{N}
, chosen not to vanish on any real point of
C
or on any point of
C
at which
f
is not invertible. After replacing
\Delta
with
\Delta+\mathrm{div}(\prod_{i}\frac{\tau_{i}}{\sigma_{i}^{N}})
, we may assume that
f
is invertible at all points of the support of
\Delta
. This proves
(i)
.
∎

5.2.
Consequences of Theorem
5.1

We now derive more concrete corollaries of Theorem
5.1
under various additional assumptions (on the function
f
, on the quadratic form
q
, or on the curve
C
).

Corollary 5.4
.

Let
C
be a geometrically connected smooth projective curve over a number field
k
. Let
q
be a nondegenerate quadratic form of rank
r\geq 5
over
k
. Fix
{f\in k(C)^{*}}
. Write
\mathrm{div}(f)=E-2D
with
E
a reduced effective divisor. Assume that at least one of the following assertions holds.

(a)

\mathrm{cl}_{v}(D)=0
for all real places
v
of
k
at which
q_{v}
is definite.

(b)

\mathrm{cl}_{v}(D)=0
for all real places
v
of
k
at which
q_{v}

has hyperbolic signature.

(c)

The form
q
does not have hyperbolic signature at any real place of
k
.

(d)

The curve
C
is a nonsplit conic over
k
.

(e)

One can write
\mathrm{div}(f)=4F+G
in such a way that the residue fields of all points in the support of
G
have no real embeddings.

Then
q
represents
f
in
k(C)
if and only
f
is nonnegative (
resp.
nonpositive) at all real places of
k
at which
q
is positive definite (
resp.
negative definite).

Proof.

In case
(a)
, we apply Theorem
5.1
with
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{O}_{C}(-D)
. Condition
(i)
is satisfied by our hypotheses on the real places at which
q
is definite. Condition
(ii)
is automatically satisfied (with
\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{O}_{C_{v}}
and
\Delta=0
) by our choice of
\mathcal{M}
.

In case
(b)
, we apply Theorem
5.1
with
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{O}_{C}
.
Condition
(i)
is satisfied by our hypothesis on definite real places
v
of
k
, as
\mathrm{cl}_{v}(\mathcal{O}_{C})=0
. Condition
(ii)
holds at a real place by Remarks
5.2
(i) and (ii) and
at a
p
-adic place by Remark
5.2
(iii).

Case
(c)
is a particular case of
(b)
.

Case
(d)
follows from either
(a)
or
(b)
. Indeed,
let
v
be a real place of
k
. The set of real points
C_{v}(\mathbb{R})
is empty or connected (because
C
is a conic). In addition, the group
\mathrm{Pic}(C)
is generated by a degree
2
line bundle (as
C
is a nonsplit conic). It follows from these two facts that
\mathrm{cl}_{v}
vanishes identically on
\mathrm{Pic}(C)
.

Finally, case
(e)
also follows from either
(a)
or
(b)
, as it implies that
\mathrm{cl}_{v}(D)=0
for all real places
v
of
k
.
∎

Remarks 5.5
.

(i)
Condition
(e)
in Corollary
5.4
is exactly the one appearing in Pop’s
[
Pop23
, Theorem 2.5 (1)]
(in the particular case where
q=\langle 1,1,1,1,1\rangle
). One can therefore think of Corollary
5.4

(e)
as generalizing this result of Pop.

(ii)
If
q
is not definite at any real place of
k
, it follows from Corollary
5.4

(a)
that any
f\in k(C)
is represented by
q
. However, this statement is trivial because this assumption implies that
q
is isotropic (see
[
Lam05
, VI.3.5]
) hence universal over any extension of
k
(see
[
Lam05
, I.3.4]
).

(iii)
The argument in (ii) also shows that Theorem
5.1
remains true, and is trivial, over global fields of positive characteristic (in characteristic
2
, see
[
Pol70
, Proposition 3.1]
).

In the case
C=\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k}
, we recover the results of Pourchet
[
Pou71
, Corollaire 1 p. 98]
.

Corollary 5.6
.

Let
k
be a number field. Let
q
be a nondegenerate quadratic form of rank
r\geq 5
over
k
. Let
f\in k[t]
be a separable polynomial. Then
q
represents
f
over
k[t]
(equivalently, over
k(t)
) if and only if one of the following assertions hold.

(i)

For all real places
v
of
k
, the form
q_{v}
is not definite.

(ii)

For all real places
v
of
k
such that
q_{v}
is positive definite (
resp.
negative definite), the function
f
is nonnegative (
resp.
nonpositive) at
v
, and either

(a)

the degree of
f
is divisible by
4
; or

(b)

if
v
is a real place of
k
and
q_{v}
has signature
(r-1,1)
(
resp.

(1,r-1)
), then
f
is not nonpositive (
resp.
not nonnegative) at
v
.

Proof.

That
q
represents
f
over
k[t]
if and only if it represents it over
k(t)
follows from the Cassels–Pfister theorem (see
[
Lam05
, IX.1.3]
). To analyze when
q
represents
f
over
k(t)
, we apply Theorem
5.1
with
C=\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k}
.

If
q
is indefinite at all real places of
k
, then
q
represents
f
in
k(t)
(see Remark
5.5
(ii)).
Assume otherwise. Then the necessary condition that
f
be nonnegative (
resp.
nonpositive) at positive definite (
resp.
negative definite) real places can only be satisfied if
f
has even degree (otherwise it changes sign). Writing
\deg(f)=2n
, one has
\mathcal{O}_{C}(D)=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k}}(n)
in the notation of Theorem
5.1
.

Condition
(i)
of Theorem
5.1
can only be satisfied with
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k}}(l)
for
l
even.
For such a choice of
\mathcal{M}
, condition
(ii)
of Theorem
5.1
is satisfied at all real places if and only if either
(ii)(a)
or
(ii)(b)
holds (apply Remarks
5.2
(i) and (ii)).
When this is the case, one can choose
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k}}
, as condition
(ii)
of Theorem
5.1
is then satisfied at all
p
-adic places (see Remark
5.2
(iii)). This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 5.7
.

In
[
Pou71
, Corollaire 1 p. 98]
, Pourchet only formulates a sufficient condition for
f
to be represented by
q
. He does not explicitly consider the (much easier) cases when
q
is indefinite at all real places of
k
, or when
f
has odd degree.

5.3.
The Pythagoras number

Here is an application to the Pythagoras number.

Theorem 5.8
.

Let
C
be a connected smooth curve over a number field
k
. Then
p(k(C))\leq 5
.

Proof.

We may replace
C
with its smooth projective compactification. After replacing
k
with its algebraic closure in
k(C)
, we may assume that
C
is geometrically connected over
k
.
Let
f\in k(C)
be a sum of squares, which we may assume to be nonzero. It is nonnegative at all real places of
k
. It is therefore a sum of
5
squares in
k(C)
by Corollary
5.4

(c)
applied with
q:=\langle 1,1,1,1,1\rangle
.
∎

Theorem
1.1
is a simple consequence of Theorem
5.8
.

Corollary 5.9
.

If
F
is a field of transcendence degree
1
over
\mathbb{Q}
, then
p(F)\leq 5
.

Proof.

Let
f\in F
be a sum of squares. We wish to show that
f
is a sum of
5
squares in
F
. Replacing
F
by a finitely generated subfield of transcendence degree
1
over
\mathbb{Q}
that contains
f
and in which
f
is a sum of squares, we may assume that
F
is finitely generated over
\mathbb{Q}
, and hence the function field of a connected smooth curve over a number field. One can then apply Theorem
5.8
.
∎

Our last goal is to explain the relation between Theorem
5.8
and Hilbert’s 17th problem. The next proposition is a version of the solution to this problem given by Artin in
[
Art27
]
and of its subsequent extension by Lang
[
Lan53
, Theorem 9]
. We could not find it in this precise form in the literature.

Proposition 5.10
.

Let
X
be a connected smooth variety over a field
k
with
2\in k^{*}
.
Fix an element
f\in\mathcal{O}(X)
. The following assertions are equivalent:

(i)

for all field orderings
\succeq
of
k(X)
, one has
f\succeq 0
;

(ii)

for all real closures
k\subset R
of
k
and all
x\in X(R)
, one has
f(x)\geq 0
;

(iii)

for all real closed extensions
k\subset R
and all
x\in X(R)
, one has
f(x)\geq 0
;

(iv)

f
is a sum of squares in
k(X)
.

If
k
is a number field, they are also equivalent to:

(v)

for all real places
v
of
k
and all
x\in X_{v}(\mathbb{R})
, one has
f(x)\geq 0
.

Proof.

The equivalence
(ii)
\Leftrightarrow
(iii)
follows from the Tarski–Seidenberg transfer principle
[
BCR98
, Proposition 5.2.3]
.
The equivalence
(i)
\Leftrightarrow
(iv)
is a theorem of Artin
[
Art27
, Satz 1]
.
That
(iv)
implies
(ii)
follows from
[
BCR98
, Theorem 6.1.9 (i)
\Rightarrow
(iii)]
.

Assume that
(i)
fails. Fix a field ordering
\succeq
of
k(X)
with
f\prec 0
. The real closure
S
of
K(X)
with respect to
\succeq
contains the real closure
R
of
k
with respect to
\succeq
. As the compositum of
R
and
k(X)
in
S
is isomorphic to
R(X_{R})
, the field
R(X_{R})
admits an ordering
\succeq
with
f\prec 0
. It follows from
[
BCR98
, Theorem 6.1.9 (iii)
\Rightarrow
(i)]
that there exists
x\in X(R)
such that
f(x)<0
. This disproves
(ii)
.

If
k
is a number field, then
(iii)
\Rightarrow
(v)
\Rightarrow
(ii)
because the orderings of
k
are in bijection with its real places (see e.g.
[
AS26
, Satz 10]
).
∎

Corollary 5.11
.

Let
C
be a connected smooth curve over a number field
k
and fix
f\in k(C)
. The following assertions are equivalent.

(i)

for all field orderings
\succeq
of
k(C)
, one has
f\succeq 0
;

(ii)

for all real places
v
of
k
and all
x\in C_{v}(\mathbb{R})
that is not a pole of
f
, one has
f(x)\geq 0
;

(iii)

f
is a sum of squares in
k(C)
;

(iv)

f
is a sum of
5
squares in
k(C)
.

Proof.

It suffices to combine Theorem
5.8
and Proposition
5.10
.
∎

Introduction

We use standard graph theory notation and terminology throughout the paper. Let
G=(V(G),E(G))
be a finite simple graph of order
n
and size
m
. If two vertices
u,v\in V(G)
are adjacent in
G
, we write
u\sim v
, otherwise we write
u\nsim v
. Let
P_{n}
,
C_{n}
,
K_{n}
denote the path graph, the cycle graph and the complete graph of order
n
, respectively. Let
K_{p,q}
denote the complete bipartite graph with partite sets of order
p
and
q
. Let
\omega(G),\alpha(G)
and
\gamma(G)
denote the clique number, the independence number and the domination number of a graph
G
, respectively. The
adjacency matrix
of
G
is an
n\times n
matrix
A(G)=[a_{uv}]
, where
a_{uv}=1
if
u\sim v
and
a_{uv}=0
, otherwise. The
eigenvalues
of
G
are the eigenvalues of
A(G)
. Since
A(G)
is a real symmetric matrix, all eigenvalues of
A(G)
are real and can be listed as

\lambda_{1}(G)\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{n}(G).

We denote by
n^{+}(G)
and
n^{-}(G)
(or simply
n^{+}
and
n^{-}
if
G
is clear from context) the number of positive and the number of negative eigenvalues of
G
, respectively. For any real number
p\geq 0
, we define

\mathcal{E}_{p}^{+}(G)=\sum_{i=1}^{n^{+}}\lambda_{i}^{p}(G),\qquad\mathcal{E}_{p}^{-}(G)=\sum_{i=n-n^{-}+1}^{n}|\lambda_{i}(G)|^{p},

and call them the
positive p-energy
and the
negative p-energy
of
G
, respectively.

The 1-energy of graphs has been extensively studied due to its applications in many areas, see, for instance, the survey
[
14
]
. Recently, there has been a growing interest in investigating 2-energy as new connections have been found relating 2-energy and (many variants of) chromatic number, see
[
4
,
6
,
13
]
. Motivated, in part, by the results on 2-energy, and in part, by Nikiforov’s work on Schatten
p
-norm of graphs
[
17
,
18
]
, the study of
p
-energy was initiated in
[
20
]
, which was followed by
[
3
,
19
]
. In a recent paper, Elphick, Tang and Zhang
[
12
]
obtained bounds for (many variants of) chromatic number in terms of
p
-energy for all
p\geq 0
, which strongly generalizes the previously existing results. The
p
-energy of graphs has already turned out to be a fruitful concept, albeit many questions remain open.

Henceforth, for ease of notation and to be consistent with previous papers on 2-energy, we will denote
\mathcal{E}_{2}^{+}(G)
(resp.
\mathcal{E}_{2}^{-}(G)
) by
s^{+}(G)
(resp.
s^{-}(G)
). In this paper, we focus on the following seemingly innocent conjecture by
Elphick, Farber, Goldberg and Wocjan
[
10
]
.

Conjecture 1.1

(
[
10
]
)
.

For every connected graph
G
of order
n
, we have

\min\{s^{+}(G),s^{-}(G)\}\geq n-1.

Conjecture
1.1
has been verified for some families of graphs, including hyper-energetic graphs (which implies that the conjecture is true for almost all graphs) and regular graphs. See
[
1
,
10
,
11
]
for partial results. Recently, in
[
22
]
, it was shown that
\min\{s^{+},s^{-}\}\geq n-\gamma\geq\frac{n}{2}
, where
\gamma
denotes the domination number of the graph. In
[
2
]
, the lower bound
\frac{3n}{4}
(for
n\geq 4
) was obtained. Both lower bounds were obtained using the super-additivity of square energy (see Theorem
3.1
).

In light of Theorem
3.1
, it is natural to apply an inductive argument to prove Conjecture
1.1
. However, since the lower bound is
n-1
, such arguments do not work easily. This motivates us to ask: for which graph families does the stronger inequality
s^{+}(G)\geq n
hold? After some computational investigation, we propose a conjecture in this direction.

Conjecture 1.2
.

Let
G
be a connected graph of order
n
and size
m
. If
m\geq n+1
, then

s^{+}(G)\geq n.

This conjecture is true for many families (for instance, regular graphs and graphs with
1
-energy at least
2n
, see
[
10
]
) for which Conjecture
1.1
is known to be true. The above conjecture has been verified for all connected graphs up to
9
vertices and all graphs in the Mathematica database with
n\leq 100
and
m\geq n+1
. A similar conjecture for
s^{-}
does not hold, since there are many graphs (apart from
K_{n}
) with
m\geq n+1
and
s^{-}<n
.

In this paper, we give improved bounds for the square energy of several graph families. Recall that the star
K_{1,3}
is called a
claw
and a graph is called
claw-free
if it does not contain a claw as an induced subgraph. The family of claw-free graphs is a rich family since it contains line graphs. Conjecture
1.1
is open for line graphs. In our first main result, we prove Conjecture
1.2
for claw-free graphs, which implies the result for line graphs as well. We note that the Conjecture
1.1
is known for graphs
G
with
\Delta(G)\leq 2
. Also, if
G
is a connected claw-free graph with
\Delta(G)\geq 3
, then it has a triangle and so
m\geq n+1
. We show the following in Section
4
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
G
be a connected claw-free graph of order
n
with
\Delta(G)\geq 3
. Then
s^{+}(G)\geq n
.

The most challenging part of the above result is proving it for claw-free unicyclic graphs (see Theorem
4.1
). In our proof, we introduce a new tool, namely the Gluing lemma
3.2
, which we believe is of independent interest and can be applied to prove improved bounds for the square energy of graphs.

Next, we verify Conjecture
1.2
for graphs with diameter 2 in Section
6
. Since almost all graphs have diameter 2, this gives an alternate proof that almost all graphs have
s^{+}\geq n
. We note here that it in
[
10
]
, it was shown that random graphs
G(n,\frac{1}{2})
have
s^{+}\approx\frac{3n^{2}}{8}
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
G
be a graph of order
n
and
\diam(G)=2
.

(i)

If
G\not\in\{K_{1,n-1},C_{5}\}
, then
s^{+}(G)\geq n
.

(ii)

We have
s^{-}(G)\geq n-O(\sqrt{n\log n})
.

It was shown in
[
22
]
that Conjecture
1.1
holds for graphs with domination number 1. We generalize this result for positive square energy in Sections
5
and
7
as follows.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
G
be a connected graph of order
n
.

(i)

If
G\neq K_{1,n-1}
and
G
has a dominating vertex, then
s^{+}(G)\geq n
.

(ii)

If
G
is a graph with domination number at most 2, then
s^{+}(G)\geq n-1
.

In Section
8
, we show that there is an absolute constant
c>0
such that for graphs
G
with
\alpha(G)\omega(G)\leq cn
, we have
\min\{s^{+}(G),s^{-}(G)\}\geq n
(Theorem
8.1
). Our proof makes use of an improved version of the
P_{3}
-removal lemma (Lemma
2.4
). This gives an alternate proof that a random graph
G\sim G(n,1/2)
satisfies
\min\{s^{+}(G),s^{-}(G)\}\geq n
with high probability, as such graphs have
\alpha(G),\omega(G)\sim\log n
with high probability. In fact, it also generalizes to
G\sim G(n,p)
for a wide range of
p
. As another application of the
P_{3}
-removal lemma, we show that
s^{-}(G)\geq n-1
for graphs with 16-th power of a Hamiltonian cycle (Theorem
8.2
). We conclude this paper with several open problems (Section
9
) for interested readers.

Preliminaries

In this section, we recall some known results that we will use later.

Theorem 2.1

(Interlacing
[
15
]
)
.

Let
A
be an
n\times n
Hermitian matrix, with eigenvalues
\lambda_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{n}
. Let
B
be an
m\times m
principal submatrix of
A
, with eigenvalues
\theta_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\theta_{m}
. For
1\leq i\leq m
, we have

\lambda_{i}\geq\theta_{i}\geq\lambda_{i+n-m}.

For a vector
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, let
x^{\downarrow}
denote the vector obtained by rearranging the entries of
x
in the non-increasing order. Given two vectors
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we say that
x
is
weakly majorized
by
y
, denoted by
x\prec_{w}y
, if

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{k}{x_{j}}^{\downarrow}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{k}{y_{j}}^{\downarrow}

for all
1\leq k\leq n.

If
x\prec_{w}y\ \text{and}\ \sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{\downarrow}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}y_{i}^{\downarrow},

then we say that
x
is
majorized
by
y
and denoted by
x\prec y
.

Theorem 2.2

(
[
16
]
)
.

Let
x=(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n}),\ y=(y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\geq 0}
, such that
x_{i}
and
y_{i}
are in non-increasing order. If
y\prec_{w}x
, then

\|y\|_{p}\leq\|x\|_{p}

for any real number
p>1
, and equality holds if and only if
x=y
.

The next two lemmas are useful in the proof for graphs with two positive eigenvalues.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
u
and
v
be two vertices in a graph
G
such that
N(u)=N(v)
. Then
n^{+}(G)=n^{+}(G-v)
and
n^{-}(G)=n^{-}(G-v)
.

Lemma 2.2

(
[
1
]
)
.

Let
G
be a connected graph of order
n
and
m
edges with exactly two positive eigenvalues. Then
s^{+}(G)\geq m
.

We need the following max-min result for the sum of the first two eigenvalues.

Lemma 2.3

(
[
9
]
)
.

For any graph
G
, we have

\lambda_{1}(G)+\lambda_{2}(G)=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}||x||=||y||=1,\\
x^{T}y=0\end{subarray}}x^{T}A(G)x+y^{T}A(G)y.

The matching number of
G
, denoted by
\alpha^{\prime}(G)
, is the size of a maximum matching in graph
G
. The following is a well-known result which implies that the number of positive eigenvalues of a tree equals its matching number.

Theorem 2.3

(
[
7
]
)
.

Let
T
be a tree of order
n
and matching number
\alpha^{\prime}(T)
. Then the rank of the adjacency matrix of
T
is
2\alpha^{\prime}(T)
.

The
join
of two disjoint graphs
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
is obtained by adding all edges between the vertices of
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
, and is denoted by
G_{1}\vee G_{2}
. We recall the spectrum for the join of two regular graphs.

Theorem 2.4

(
[
5
]
)
.

Let
G_{1}
be an
r_{1}
-regular graph on
n_{1}
vertices and
G_{2}
be an
r_{2}
-regular graph on
n_{2}
vertices. Then the spectrum of

is given by

\bigg\{\lambda_{i}(G_{1}),\lambda_{j}(G_{2}),\frac{r_{1}+r_{2}\pm\sqrt{(r_{1}-r_{2})^{2}+4n_{1}n_{2}}}{2}:2\leq i\leq n_{1},2\leq j\leq n_{2}\bigg\}.

Finally, we observe an improved version of the
P_{3}
-removal lemma (see
[
22
, Theorem 1.10]
).

Lemma 2.4

(
P_{3}
-removal lemma
[
22
]
)
.

Let
G
be any graph and
\epsilon=\frac{1}{16}
. Suppose
U
is a set of three vertices in
G
such that
G[U]\cong P_{3}
. Then there exists a vertex

such that

s^{+}(G)\geq s^{+}(G-u)+1+\epsilon.

The same also holds if
s^{+}
is replaced with

.

Proof.

We follow the proof in
[

, Section 4]
, replacing

with

at appropriate places. Our proof boils down to verifying that the inequality

16x^{4}>6\left(1+\epsilon-4(1-x)^{2}\right)\left(1+\epsilon-2(1-x)^{2}\right)

. A direct computation via Desmos shows that this holds for

∎

3
Refining super-additivity of square energy

The following super-additivity result was proved in
[
2
,
22
]
and was used to obtain linear lower bounds for the square energy of graphs.

Theorem 3.1

(
[
2
,
22
]
)
.

Let
H_{1},\ldots,H_{k}
be disjoint induced subgraphs of a graph
G
. Then

s^{+}(G)\geq\sum_{i=1}^{k}s^{+}(H_{i})\ \text{ and }\ s^{-}(G)\geq\sum_{i=1}^{k}s^{-}(H_{i}),

and equality holds in both simultaneously if and only if
G
is the disjoint union of
H_{1},\ldots,H_{k}
.

We will use the above result repeatedly to prove optimal lower bounds for different graph families in this paper. Unfortunately, it cannot be used to prove Conjecture
1.1
for unicyclic graphs.

In this section, we prove a new tool, namely the Gluing lemma, which can be viewed as a refinement of the super-additivity result. As an application of the gluing lemma, we will prove Conjecture
1.2
for claw-free unicyclic graphs (Theorem
4.1
) in Section
4
.

We first introduce some notation. If a matrix
M
is positive semidefinite matrix (PSD), we denote it by
M\succ 0
. For two real symmetric matrices
A,B
of the same dimension, we define the inner product
\langle A,B\rangle=\tr(AB)
and the corresponding norm
\lVert A\rVert_{2}=\sqrt{\tr(A^{2})}
. For a square matrix
M
and a set of indices
I
, we let
M|_{I}
denote the principal submatrix
(M_{i,j})_{i,j\in I}
of
M
.

For a graph
G
, let
A^{+}(G)
and
A^{-}(G)
denote the positive semidefinite matrices in the spectral decomposition
A(G)=A^{+}(G)-A^{-}(G)
. The following lemma is a strengthening of an observation by Zhang
[
22
]
, which is already familiar in the convex optimization literature. For completeness, we include the proof here.

Lemma 3.1

(
[
22
]
)
.

For any PSD matrix
M
, we have

\lVert A(G)+M\rVert_{2}^{2}\geq s^{+}(G)+\lVert A^{-}(G)-M\rVert_{2}^{2}.

In particular,

s^{+}(G)=\inf_{M\succ 0}\lVert A(G)+M\rVert_{2}^{2}

with equality if
M=A^{-}(G)
.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\lVert A(G)+M\rVert_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\lVert A^{+}(G)+(M-A^{-}(G))\rVert_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\lVert A^{+}(G)\rVert_{2}^{2}+\lVert M-A^{-}(G)\rVert_{2}^{2}+2\langle A^{+}(G),M-A^{-}(G)\rangle

\displaystyle\geq s^{+}(G)+\lVert M-A^{-}(G)\rVert_{2}^{2}.

The last inequality holds because
\langle A^{+}(G),A^{-}(G)\rangle=0
and
\langle A^{+}(G),M\rangle\geq 0
(this is because
A^{+}(G)
and
M
are PSD, see
[
21
, Theorem 7.5]
).
∎

Now, we state and prove the main result of this section. We note here that
s^{+}
of any real symmetric matrix is defined analogously to the
s^{+}
of the adjacency matrix of a graph.

Lemma 3.2

(Gluing lemma)
.

Let
G_{0}
be a base graph,
\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{k}\}\subseteq V(G_{0})
, and
(G_{1},v_{1}),\ldots,

(G_{k},v_{k})
be
k
graphs with distinguished vertices. Let
G
be obtained by gluing graphs
G_{1},\ldots,G_{k}
onto
G_{0}
, identifying
u_{i}
and
v_{i}
. Then we have

s^{+}(G)\geq\sum_{i=1}^{k}s^{+}(G_{i})+s^{+}(\Gamma),

where
\Gamma
is the matrix obtained from
A(G_{0})
, the adjacency matrix of
G_{0}
, and replacing the diagonal entry at
(u_{i},u_{i})
with
-d_{i}
where
d_{i}=(A^{-}(G_{i}))_{v_{i},v_{i}}
for each
i=1,\dots,k
.

In fact, the inequality can be strengthened as follows: for any PSD matrix
M
, we have

\lVert A(G)+M\rVert_{2}^{2}\geq\sum_{i=1}^{k}s^{+}(G_{i})+s^{+}(\Gamma)+R_{1}(M)+R_{2}(M),

where the terms
R_{1}
and
R_{2}
are non-negative quantities defined by

R_{1}(M)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u,u^{\prime}\in V(G_{i})\\
(u,u^{\prime})\neq(v_{i},v_{i})\end{subarray}}|(A^{-}(G))_{u,u^{\prime}}-M_{u,u^{\prime}}|^{2},

R_{2}(M)=2\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{u\in V(G_{i})\backslash\{v_{i}\},u^{\prime}\in V(G_{0})\backslash\{u_{i}\}}|M_{u,u^{\prime}}|^{2}.

Proof.

Let
M
be any PSD matrix. We want to find a lower bound for
\lVert A(G)+M\rVert_{2}^{2}
. Recall that

\lVert A(G)+M\rVert_{2}^{2}=\sum_{u,u^{\prime}\in V(G)}(A(G)+M)_{u,u^{\prime}}^{2}.

As
V(G_{0})
and
V(G_{i})
overlap at exactly
u_{i},v_{i}
, we have

\lVert A(G)+M\rVert_{2}^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{u,u^{\prime}\in V(G_{i})}(A(G)+M)_{u,u^{\prime}}^{2}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u,u^{\prime}\in V(G_{0})\\
(u,u^{\prime})\neq(u_{i},u_{i}),\forall i\end{subarray}}(A(G)+M)_{u,u^{\prime}}^{2}+R_{2}(M).

Note that

\sum_{u,u^{\prime}\in V(G_{i})}(A(G)+M)_{u,u^{\prime}}^{2}=\lVert A(G_{i})+M|_{V(G_{i})}\rVert_{2}^{2}.

Now, applying Lemma
3.1
, we have

\lVert A(G_{i})+M|_{V(G_{i})}\rVert_{2}^{2}\geq s^{+}(G_{i})+\lVert A^{-}(G_{i})-M|_{V(G_{i})}\rVert_{2}^{2}.

Isolating the term corresponding to
(u_{i},u_{i})
, we have

\lVert A^{-}({G_{i}})-M|_{V(G_{i})}\rVert_{2}^{2}\geq(d_{i}-M_{u_{i},u_{i}})^{2}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u,u^{\prime}\in V(G_{i})\\
(u,u^{\prime})\neq(v_{i},v_{i})\end{subarray}}|(A^{-}(G))_{u,u^{\prime}}-M_{u,u^{\prime}}|^{2}.

So we conclude that

\sum_{u,u^{\prime}\in V(G_{i})}(A(G)+M)_{u,u^{\prime}}^{2}\geq s^{+}(G_{i})+(d_{i}-M_{u_{i},u_{i}})^{2}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u,u^{\prime}\in V(G_{i})\\
(u,u^{\prime})\neq(v_{i},v_{i})\end{subarray}}|(A^{-}(G))_{u,u^{\prime}}-M_{u,u^{\prime}}|^{2}.

Substituting back, we get

\lVert A(G)+M\rVert_{2}^{2}\geq\sum_{i=1}^{k}s^{+}(G_{i})+(d_{i}-M_{u_{i},u_{i}})^{2}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u,u^{\prime}\in V(G_{0})\\
(u,u^{\prime})\neq(u_{i},u_{i}),\forall i\end{subarray}}(A(G)+M)_{u,u^{\prime}}^{2}+R_{1}(M)+R_{2}(M).

Now, we note that the sum of the second and the third term is precisely
\lVert\Gamma+M\rVert_{2}^{2}
. So we get

\lVert A(G)+M\rVert_{2}^{2}\geq\sum_{i=1}^{k}s^{+}(G_{i})+\lVert\Gamma+M\rVert_{2}^{2}+R_{1}(M)+R_{2}(M).

We conclude that

\lVert A(G)+M\rVert_{2}^{2}\geq\sum_{i=1}^{k}s^{+}(G_{i})+s^{+}(\Gamma)+R_{1}(M)+R_{2}(M).\qed

Remark
.

Since decreasing the diagonal entries does not increase the positive energy (as subtracting a PSD matrix does not increase the
i
-th eigenvalue for any
i
), the same result holds if we replace the diagonal entry of
\Gamma
at
(u_{i},u_{i})
with any entry smaller than
-d_{i}
.

The gluing lemma is a refinement of the super-additivity result in the following sense. Using Theorem
3.1
, we can only get
s^{+}(G)\geq\sum_{i=1}^{k}s^{+}(G_{i})+s^{+}(G_{0}-\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{k}\})
. Since
s^{+}(\Gamma)\geq s^{+}(G_{0}-\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{k}\})
, the gluing lemma gives an improvement.

4
Claw-free graphs

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.1
. We first define some special claw-free graphs. For
j,k,\ell\geq 0
, denote by
P(j,k,\ell)
the graph obtained by attaching paths of length
j,k,\ell
at distinct vertices of a triangle. Then
|V(P(j,k,\ell))|=3+j+k+\ell
. For
k\geq 4
and
\ell\geq 0
, define
C(k,\ell)
to be the graph obtained from a cycle
v_{1},\ldots,v_{k},v_{1}
by joining the vertices
v_{1},v_{3}
and attaching a path of length
\ell
at vertex
v_{2}
. Note that
|V(C(k,\ell))|=k+\ell
.

Let
G
be a connected claw-free graph of order
n
and
\Delta(G)\geq 3
. Then
G
has a triangle. If
G
is unicyclic, then it is not difficult to see that
G
is isomorphic to
P(j,k,\ell)
for some
j,k,\ell
. We first verify Theorem
1.1
for the family
P(j,k,\ell)
. This case turns out to be very challenging.

4.1
Triangle with attached paths
P(j,k,\ell)

In this section, we prove the following result.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
G=P(j,k,\ell)
for some
j,k,\ell\geq 0
. Then
s^{+}(G)\geq j+k+\ell+3=n
.

Without loss of generality, we assume that
j\geq k\geq\ell\geq 0
. A triangle with only one attached path (i.e.,
k=\ell=0
) can be dealt with easily as shown below.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
G=P(j,0,0)
for some
j\geq 0
. Then
s^{+}(G)\geq j+3
.

Proof.

By Theorem
3.1
, we have
s^{+}(P(j,0,0))\geq s^{+}(K_{3})+s^{+}(P_{j})=j+3=n
.
∎

Next, we deal with a triangle with two attached paths
P(j,k,0)
. We first estimate the diagonal entry of
A^{-}(P_{\ell})
corresponding to a leaf in a path
P_{\ell}
. We recall some well-known facts about trigonometric functions.

Lemma 4.1
.

The following hold:

(i)

x-\frac{x^{3}}{6}\leq\sin(x)\leq x
for
x\in(0,1)
.

(ii)

|\sin(x)|\geq 2[\frac{x}{\pi}]
for
x\in[0,\pi]
, where
[\cdot]
denotes the distance to the nearest integer.

(iii)

4.2
Claw-free graphs: the general case

We first prove a lemma about the structure of claw-free graphs, which we exploit in the proof of the main theorem in this section.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
G
be a connected claw-free graph and
K
be a clique of order
k
in
G
. Then,
G-V(K)
has at most
k+1
components.

Proof.

Suppose to the contrary there are at least
k+2
components, say
C_{1},\ldots,C_{k+2}
, in
G-K
. Let
V(K)=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}\}
. Since
G
is claw-free, each
v_{i}
has neighbours in at most two components of
G-K
. By the pigeonhole principle, there exists a vertex in
K
, say
v_{1}
, with neighbours
w_{1}
and
w_{2}
, which, without loss of generality, lie in
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
, respectively. Now, every vertex
v_{i}
, for
2\leq i\leq k
, is adjacent to either
w_{1}
or
w_{2}
; for otherwise
v_{1},w_{1},w_{2},v_{i}
induce a claw. In particular, for
2\leq i\leq k
, each
v_{i}
is adjacent to at most one of the components
C_{3},\ldots,C_{k+2}
. This means that there is
C_{i}
with no neighbours in
K
. This contradicts the connectedness of
G
.
∎

Theorem 4.2
.

Let

be a connected claw-free graph with
\Delta(G)\geq 3
. Then

.

Proof.

We proceed by induction on

. The assertion can be easily verified when

. So, assume that

3.1
and the induction hypothesis without mention.

Since
G
is claw-free and

. Let

denote a clique of order

in

. Assume

. Let

denote the components of

. If

, then

,

\displaystyle=(\omega-1)^{2}+(n-\omega-\ell)\geq(\omega-1)^{2}+(n-2\omega-1)

\displaystyle=n+\omega^{2}-4\omega.

\omega(G)\geq 4
, then

. So assume that
\omega(G)=3

1\leq i\leq k
, either

is a path or

is a cycle of length at least 5. Now, if there is a vertex

.

, a contradiction. It follows that all components of

are paths, and every vertex of degree 3 in

. We conclude that

is either

. Set
V_{i+1}=V_{i}\backslash\{u_{i}\}
. We repeat this operation until
G_{k}=G[V_{k}]
has no induced
P_{3}
. Thus,
G_{k}
is a disjoint union of cliques. Let the cliques in
G_{k}
have vertex sets
C_{1},\ldots,C_{\ell}
, with
|C_{1}|\leq\cdots\leq|C_{\ell}|
.

By construction, for all
0\leq i\leq k-1
, we have

s^{-}(G_{i})\geq s^{-}(G_{i+1})+1+\epsilon.

Telescoping, we have

s^{-}(G)\geq s^{-}(G_{k})+(1+\epsilon)k.

On the other hand,
s^{-}(G_{k})\geq n-k-\ell
. So we obtain

s^{-}(G)\geq n+\epsilon k-\ell,

which implies
\ell>\epsilon k
. As
\ell\leq|C_{1}|+\cdots+|C_{\ell}|=n-k
, we get

k<\frac{1}{1+\epsilon}n.

Since
|C_{i}|\leq\omega(G)
and
\ell\leq\alpha(G)
, it follows that

\alpha(G)\omega(G)\geq|C_{1}|+\cdots+|C_{\ell}|=n-k>\frac{\epsilon}{1+\epsilon}n,

a contradiction. The proof is complete.
∎

A
k
-th power
of a Hamiltonian cycle in a graph
G
is a bijective map
\phi:\mathbb{Z}_{n}\to V(G)
, such that for each
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}
and
j\in\{1,2,\ldots,k\}
,
(\phi(i),\phi(i+j))
is an edge in
G
.

Suppose
G
contains a 2nd power of a Hamiltonian cycle. Assume
n=3k+r
, where
k\geq 0
and
3\leq r\leq 5
. Then
G
can be vertex partitioned into
k
many triangles and a connected graph
H\in\{K_{3},K_{4}-E(K_{2}),K_{4},K_{5}-E(P_{3}),K_{5}-E(P_{2}),K_{5}-E(2K_{2}),K_{5}-E(K_{2}),K_{5}\}
on
r
vertices. Each of these graphs has positive square energy at least
\tfrac{4r}{3}
. For every
n\geq 3
, we therefore conclude that

s^{+}(G)\geq ks^{+}(K_{3})+s^{+}(H)\geq\frac{4(n-r)}{3}+\frac{4r}{3}=\frac{4n}{3}.

The positive square energy is therefore much larger than
n
. However, the same does not hold for the negative square energy. As another application of the
P_{3}
-removal lemma, we prove the following.

Theorem 8.2
.

If
G
is a graph of order
n
containing the
16
-th power of a Hamiltonian cycle, then
s^{-}(G)\geq n-1
.

Proof.

For the sake of contradiction, suppose
s^{-}(G)<n-1
. Proceeding as in the proof of Theorem
8.1
, we can find some vertex subset
V_{k}
of size
n-k
such that
G_{k}=G[V_{k}]
is the disjoint union of cliques
C_{1},\ldots,C_{\ell}
, with
\ell>\frac{k}{16}+1
. In particular, we have
\ell\geq 2
.

Let
\phi:\mathbb{Z}_{n}\to V(G)
be a
16
-th power of a Hamiltonian cycle in
G
. We define a
gap
as an interval
[i,j]
where
\phi(i-1)
and
\phi(j+1)
belongs to different
C_{k}
’s, and
\phi(i),\ldots,\phi(j)
lies in
V\backslash V_{k}
. Then there must be at least
\ell
gaps. Furthermore, since the cliques are pairwise independent, each gap must have length at least
16
. This implies
k=|V\backslash V_{k}|\geq 16\ell>k
,
a contradiction.
∎

9
Open problems

Unicyclic graphs are believed to be among the hardest cases for Conjecture
1.1
. It was shown in
[
1
]
that Conjecture
1.1
holds for cycles
C_{n}
. We calculate the square energy of cycles precisely.

Proposition 9.1
.

Let
n\geq 3
. Then, the following are true.

(i)

If
n
is even, then
s^{+}(C_{n})=s^{-}(C_{n})=n
.

(ii)

If
n\equiv 3\mod 4
, then

s^{+}(C_{n})=n-1+\frac{1}{\cos(\frac{\pi}{n})}>n,\quad s^{-}(C_{n})=n+1-\frac{1}{\cos(\frac{\pi}{n})}<n.

(iii)

If
n\equiv 1\mod 4
, then

s^{+}(C_{n})=n+1-\frac{1}{\cos(\frac{\pi}{n})}<n,\quad s^{-}(C_{n})=n-1+\frac{1}{\cos(\frac{\pi}{n})}>n.

Proof.

If
n
is even, then
C_{n}
is bipartite, and the assertion is immediate. The spectrum of
C_{n}
is
\{\cos\left(\frac{2\pi j}{n}\right),j=0,1,\ldots,n-1\}
. Note that
\cos{\theta}
is non-negative if
0\leq\theta\leq\pi/2
and
3\pi/2\leq\theta\leq 2\pi
. Now, if
n\equiv 3\mod 4
, then
n=4k+3
for some positive integer
k
. The eigenvalue
\cos\left(\frac{2\pi j}{n}\right)
is positive whenever
j=0,1,\dots,k
and
j=3k+3,3k+4,\dots,4k+2
. Thus,

\displaystyle s^{+}(C_{4k+3})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{k}4\cos^{2}\bigg({\frac{2\pi j}{4k+3}}\bigg)+\sum_{j=3k+3}^{4k+2}4\cos^{2}\bigg({\frac{2\pi j}{4k+3}}\bigg)

\displaystyle=2k+3+\frac{1}{2}\sec\bigg(\frac{\pi}{4k+3}\bigg)+2k-1+\frac{1}{2}\sec\bigg(\frac{\pi}{4k+3}\bigg)

\displaystyle=n-1+\sec\bigg(\frac{\pi}{n}\bigg).

Now, if
n\equiv 1\mod 4
then
n=4k+1
for some positive integer
k
. The eigenvalue
\cos\left(\frac{2\pi j}{n}\right)
is positive whenever
j=0,1,\dots,k
and
j=3k+1,3k+2,\dots,4k
. Thus,

\displaystyle s^{+}(C_{4k+1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{k}4\cos^{2}\bigg({\frac{2\pi j}{4k+1}}\bigg)+\sum_{j=3k+1}^{4k}4\cos^{2}\bigg({\frac{2\pi j}{4k+1}}\bigg)

\displaystyle=2k+3-\frac{1}{2}\sec\bigg(\frac{\pi}{4k+1}\bigg)+2k-1-\frac{1}{2}\sec\bigg(\frac{\pi}{4k+1}\bigg)

\displaystyle=n+1-\sec\bigg(\frac{\pi}{n}\bigg).\qed

The above calculation and further computational investigation point us to the following conjecture for unicyclic graphs.

Conjecture 9.1
.

If
G
is a unicyclic graph of order
n
whose cycle has odd length
k
, then

(i)

s^{+}(G)>n>s^{-}(G)
, if
k\equiv 3\mod 4
.

(ii)

s^{+}(G)<n<s^{-}(G)
, if
k\equiv 1\mod 4
.

We propose the following for the equality case in Conjecture
1.1
.

Conjecture 9.2
.

Let
G
be a connected graph of order
n
.

(i)

s^{+}(G)=n-1
if and only if
G
is a tree.

(ii)

s^{-}(G)=n-1
if and only if
G
is a tree or a complete graph.

We believe that the equality for
s^{+}
never holds in Conjecture
1.2
, and the following might be true.

Conjecture 9.3
.

Let
G
be a connected graph of order
n
such that
s^{+}(G)=n
. Then
G
is a bipartite unicyclic graph.

If the clique number of a graph is large, then one can expect that it has a large value of
s^{+}
. This suggests another refinement of Conjecture
1.1
.

Conjecture 9.4
.

For any connected graph
G
of order
n
and
\omega(G)\geq 3
, we have
s^{+}(G)\geq n
.

If Conjectures
1.2
and
9.1
are true, then the above would be true as well. We believe the bottleneck in proving the above conjecture is unicyclic graphs with a triangle. Our proof of Theorem
4.1
suggests that it is quite a challenge, and we wonder if there is an alternate way to approach this.

We have checked the above conjectures for graphs up to
9
vertices and found no counterexamples.

Elphick and Linz
[
11
]
noted that Conjecture
1.1
holds for maximal planar graphs, and asked if the following is true: for any connected maximal planar graph
G
of order
n\geq 3
, we have
s^{+}(G)\geq 3(n-2)
and
s^{-}(G)\leq 3(n-2)
. Based on computational investigation and in light of the super-additivity result (Theorem
3.1
), we believe the following conjecture may be more accessible.

Conjecture 9.5
.

The following hold:

(i)

Let
G
be a maximal planar graph of order
n\geq 10
. Then
s^{+}(G)\geq 3n
.

(ii)

Let
G
be a maximal outerplanar graph of order
n\geq 8
. Then
s^{+}(G)\geq 2n
.

The coefficients in the lower bounds in the above conjecture cannot be improved. For instance, consider the maximal planar graph
G=C_{n-2}\vee\overline{K_{2}}
. Assume
n
is even. Since
\lambda_{1}(C_{n})=2
, and using Theorem
2.4
, the spectrum of
G
is given by
\{1\pm\sqrt{2n-1},\lambda_{2}(C_{n-2}),\ldots,\lambda_{n-2}(C_{n-2}),0\}
. Thus,

s^{+}(G)=(1+\sqrt{2n-1})^{2}+s^{+}(C_{n-2})-\lambda_{1}^{2}(C_{n-2})=3n+O(\sqrt{n}).

Now, take the maximal outerplanar graph
H=K_{1}\vee P_{n-1}
. Again, by Theorem
2.4
, we have
\lambda_{1}(H)\leq\lambda_{1}(K_{1}\vee C_{n-1})=1+\sqrt{n}
. By the Interlacing Theorem, it follows that

s^{+}(H)\leq s^{+}(P_{n-1})+\lambda_{1}^{2}(H)\leq n-1+(1+\sqrt{n})^{2}=2n+O(\sqrt{n}).

On the order of 4-dimensional regular polytope numbers
Anji Dong, The Nguyen, Alexandru Zaharescu

Abstract
. In light of Kim’s conjecture on regular polytopes of dimension four, which is a generalization of Waring’s problem, we establish asymptotic formulas for representing any sufficiently large integer as a sum of numbers in the form of those regular 4-polytopes. Moreover, we are able to obtain a more general result of the asymptotics for any degree-four polynomial f f satisfying f ⁡ ( 0 ) = 0 f(0)=0 and f ⁡ ( 1 ) = 1 f(1)=1 .

[

Theorem 1.1

(Schläfli)

The only possible Schläfli symbols for a regular polytope in the Euclidean space in

\displaystyle f(n)=A\binom{n}{4}+B\binom{n}{3}+C\binom{n}{2}+n

Theorem 1.4

2.
Setup

In this section, we outline the initial setup required to employ the Hardy–Littlewood circle method to prove Theorem
1.4
. Let
f
be a polynomial satisfying (
1.4
). For convenience, we denote by
f_{n}
the value of
f(n)
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

For a sufficiently large positive integer
m
, let
\mathcal{R}_{f,s}(m)
denote the number of ways to write
m
as a sum of
s
numbers of form
f(n)
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
, that is,

(2.1)

\mathcal{R}_{f,s}(m)=\left|\left\{(n_{1},\dots,n_{s})\in\mathbb{N}^{s}\,:\,m=f(n_{1})+\dots+f(n_{s})\right\}\right|.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that
m\leq f_{N}
. Then, define

(2.2)

S_{f,N}(\alpha):=\sum_{i=1}^{N}e(\alpha f_{n}).

For convenience, we write
S_{f}(\alpha)=S_{f,N}(\alpha)
. It follows that

\displaystyle S_{f}(\alpha)^{s}=\sum_{n_{1}}^{N}\sum_{n_{2}=1}^{N}\dots\sum_{n_{s}=1}^{N}e(\alpha(f_{n_{1}}+f_{n_{2}}+\dots+f_{n_{2}}))=\sum_{n=1}^{sf_{N}}\mathcal{R}_{f,s}(n)e(\alpha n).

Applying Cauchy’s formula, we obtain

(2.3)

\mathcal{R}_{f,s}(m)=\int_{0}^{1}S_{f}(\alpha)^{s}e(-\alpha m)\;\mathrm{d}\alpha.

Next, we choose

(2.4)

\displaystyle N=\bigg\lceil\bigg(\frac{24m}{A}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{4}}\bigg\rceil+1,

and let
P=N^{\delta}
for some
\delta\in\mathbb{R}
such that
N^{3\delta-4}<1/2
. We then define
\mathfrak{I}=\left(N^{\delta-4},1+N^{\delta-4}\right]
. For integers
1\leq a\leq q\leq P
and
(a,q)=1
, we define the major arcs
\mathfrak{M}(q,a)
as

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M}(q,a)=\{\alpha\,:\,|\alpha-a/q|\leq N^{\delta-4}\}.

Let
\mathfrak{M}
denote the union of the
\mathfrak{M}(q,a)
’s, i.e.,

\mathfrak{M}=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq a\leq q\leq P\\
(a,q)=1\end{subarray}}\mathfrak{M}(q,a).

Observe that
\mathfrak{M}\subset\mathfrak{I}
, so we define the minor arcs as

\mathfrak{m}=\mathfrak{I}\backslash\mathfrak{M}.

For distinct pairs
a/q\neq a^{\prime}/q^{\prime}
with
1\leq q,q^{\prime}\leq P
, we have

\left|\frac{a}{q}-\frac{a^{\prime}}{q^{\prime}}\right|\geq\frac{1}{qq^{\prime}}>N^{-2\delta}>2N^{\delta-4}.

Thus, the
\mathfrak{M}(q,a)
’s are pairwise disjoint. Finally, using (
2.3
), we split
\mathcal{R}_{f,s}(m)
into integrals over the major and minor arcs:

(2.5)

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{f,s}(m)=\int_{\mathfrak{M}}S_{f}(\alpha)^{s}e(-\alpha m)\;\mathrm{d}\alpha+\int_{\mathfrak{m}}S_{f}(\alpha)^{s}e(-\alpha m)\;\mathrm{d}\alpha.

Lemma 3.2

\displaystyle\Delta_{j}(\psi(x);\mathbf{h})=\Delta_{j}\left(\psi(x);h_{1},\ldots,h_{j}\right)=\Delta_{1}\left(\Delta_{j-1}\left(\psi(x);h_{1},\ldots,h_{j-1}\right);h_{j}\right).

v_{i}
(
1\leq i\leq 4
) in this case is

\int_{0}^{1}S_{f}(\alpha)^{4}S_{f}(-\alpha)^{4}\;\mathrm{d}\alpha=\int_{0}^{1}|S_{f}(\alpha)|^{8}\;\mathrm{d}\alpha\leq 328N^{5+\frac{8.5288}{\log\log N}}.

On the other hand, we have
\Delta_{3}(f_{n};\mathbf{h})=h_{1}h_{2}h_{3}L(n;h)
, where
L(n;h)
is a linear polynomial in
n
, determined by the choices of
h_{1},h_{2}
and
h_{3}
. So, either
h_{1}h_{2}h_{3}=0
, or
n
is a zero of
L
. Therefore, the total number of choices for
n
and
\mathbf{h}
is at most
20N^{3}
. Hence, the contribution in this case to
W
is bounded above by

6560N^{8+\frac{1.0661}{\log\log N}}.

For the second case, we write
\sum_{i=1}^{4}(f_{u_{i}}-f_{v_{i}})=k
for some non-zero integer
k
with
|k|\leq 4f_{N}
. For each such choice of
u_{i}
and
v_{i}
(
i=1,\dots,4
), we have
h_{1}h_{2}h_{3}L(n;\mathbf{h})=k
and thus there are at most
8d(k)^{3}
choices for
\mathbf{h}
. For each such choice of
\mathbf{h}
, there is at most one choice for
n
. This shows that the total choices for
n
and
\mathbf{h}
is bounded above by

8d(k)^{3}\leq 8N^{\frac{12.7932}{\log\log N}}.

Since there are at most
N^{8}
choices for
u_{i}
and
v_{i}
with
1\leq i\leq 4
, the contribution to
W
in this case is at most

8N^{8+\frac{12.7932}{\log\log N}}.

Thus, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}|S_{f}(\alpha)|^{16}\;\mathrm{d}\alpha\leq(2N)^{4}W\leq 16N^{4}\left(6560N^{8+\frac{8.5288}{\log\log N}}+8N^{8+\frac{12.7932}{\log\log N}}\right)\leq 10^{6}N^{12+\frac{12.7932}{\log\log N}},

as desired.
∎

We are now ready to prove Theorem
3.1
.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

We can write

\left|\int_{\mathfrak{m}}S_{f}(\alpha)^{s}e(-m\alpha)\;\mathrm{d}\alpha\right|\leq\left(\sup_{\alpha\in\mathfrak{m}}|S_{f}(\alpha)|\right)^{s-16}\int_{0}^{1}|S_{f}(\alpha)|^{16}\;\mathrm{d}\alpha.

Consider an arbitrary point
\alpha
of
\mathfrak{m}
. By Dirichlet’s Theorem (see
[
21
, Lemma 2.1]
), there exist
a,q
with
(a,q)=1
and
q\leq N^{3-\delta}
such that
|\alpha-a/q|\leq q^{-1}N^{\delta-3}
. Since
\alpha\in\mathfrak{m}\subset\left(N^{\delta-3},1-N^{\delta-3}\right)
it follows that
1\leq a\leq q
. Therefore,
q>N^{\delta}
, for otherwise
\alpha
would lie in
\mathfrak{M}
. We now apply Lemma
3.6
with
\eta=24A
. Then we have

\displaystyle|S_{f}(\alpha)|

\displaystyle\leq 2N^{7/8}+5\cdot(24A)^{1/8}N^{1+\frac{3.1983}{4\log\log N}}\left(q^{-1}+N^{-1}+qN^{-3}\right)^{1/8}(\log N)^{1/8}

\displaystyle\leq 2N^{7/8}+5\cdot(72A)^{1/8}N^{1-\frac{\delta}{8}+\frac{3.1983}{4\log\log N}}(\log N)^{1/8}

\displaystyle\leq 11A^{1/8}N^{1-\frac{\delta}{8}+\frac{3.1983}{4\log\log N}}(\log N)^{1/8}.

Additionally, by Lemma
3.7
, we have

\int_{0}^{1}|S_{f}(\alpha)|^{16}\;\mathrm{d}\alpha\leq 10^{6}N^{12+\frac{12.7932}{\log\log N}}.

It follows that

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathfrak{m}}S_{f}(\alpha)^{s}e(-m\alpha)\;\mathrm{d}\alpha\right|

\displaystyle\leq 10^{6}\left(11A^{1/8}N^{1-\frac{\delta}{8}+\frac{3.1983}{4\log\log N}}\right)^{s-16}(\log N)^{\frac{s-16}{8}}N^{12+\frac{12.7932}{\log\log N}}

\displaystyle\leq 10^{6}\cdot 11^{s-16}A^{\frac{s-16}{8}}(\log N)^{\frac{s-16}{8}}N^{s-4-\frac{\delta(s-16)}{8}+\frac{s}{\log\log N}},

which completes the proof of Theorem
3.1
.
∎

Major Arcs

In this section, our primary objective is to effectively approximate
S_{f}(\alpha)
for

. Let
\theta=\alpha-a/q
. For convenience, we extend the definition in (

(4.1)

\displaystyle M(t)

(4.2)

\displaystyle\textrm{and}\quad V(q,a)

Lemma 4.1
.

Let

\bigg\lvert\int_{\mathfrak{M}}S_{f}(\alpha)^{s}e(-\alpha m)\;\mathrm{d}\alpha-\int_{\mathfrak{M}}\bigg(\frac{V(q,a)}{24q}\int_{1}^{N}e\bigg(\frac{At^{4}\theta}{24}\bigg)\;\mathrm{d}t\bigg)^{s}e(-\alpha m)\;\mathrm{d}\alpha\bigg\rvert\leq 70(A+4|B|)\pi sN^{5\delta+s-5}.

Applying partial summation, we obtain the following lemma.

Lemma 4.2

Let
\alpha\in\mathfrak{M}(q,a)

(4.4)

\displaystyle S_{f}(\alpha)

\displaystyle=M(N)e(\theta f_{N})-2\pi i\theta\int_{1}^{N}M(t)f_{t}^{\prime}e(\theta f_{t})\;\mathrm{d}t.

For any

.

Proof.

The proof follows easily by noticing that the least common multiple of the denominators for

∎

Lemma 4.4

For all

∎

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathfrak{M}(q,a)

\bigg\lvert S_{f}(\alpha)-\frac{V(q,a)}{24q}\int_{1}^{N}e(\theta f_{t})\;\mathrm{d}t\bigg\rvert\leq 24q+1+(2A+8|B|)q\pi\theta N^{4}.

By applying Lemma

\displaystyle\bigg\lvert M(N)e(\theta f_{N})-\frac{V(q,a)}{24q}Ne(\theta f_{N})\bigg\rvert

\displaystyle\leq 24q,

\displaystyle\bigg\lvert 2\pi i\theta\int_{1}^{N}A(t)f_{t}^{\prime}e(\theta f_{t})\;\mathrm{d}t-2\pi i\theta\int_{1}^{N}\frac{V(q,a)}{24q}tf_{t}^{\prime}e(\theta f_{t})\;\mathrm{d}t\bigg\rvert

(4.5)

\displaystyle\bigg\lvert S_{f}(\alpha)-\frac{V(q,a)}{24q}Ne(\theta f_{N})+2\pi i\theta\int_{1}^{N}

Applying integration by parts, we find

\displaystyle\frac{V(q,a)}{24q}

\displaystyle Ne(\theta f_{N})-2\pi i\theta\int_{1}^{N}\frac{V(q,a)}{24q}tf_{t}^{\prime}e(\theta f_{t})\;\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\frac{V(q,a)}{24q}e(\theta)+\frac{V(q,a)}{24q}\int_{1}^{N}e(\theta f_{t})\;\mathrm{d}t.

Finally, combining (

∎

Lemma 4.6
.

Let

\bigg\lvert S_{f}(\alpha)-\frac{V(q,a)}{24q}\int_{1}^{N}e\bigg(\frac{At^{4}\theta}{24}\bigg)\;\mathrm{d}t\bigg\rvert\leq 24q+1+(2A+8|B|)q\pi\theta N^{4}+\frac{3A+2|B|}{3}\pi N^{\delta}.

\bigg\lvert\int_{1}^{N}e(\theta f_{t})\;\mathrm{d}t-\int_{1}^{N}e\bigg(\frac{At^{4}\theta}{24}\bigg)\;\mathrm{d}t\bigg\rvert\leq\frac{3A+2|B|}{3}\pi N^{\delta}.

We write

\displaystyle\bigg\lvert

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{N}e(\theta f_{t})\;\mathrm{d}t-\int_{1}^{N}e\bigg(\frac{At^{4}\theta}{24}\bigg)\;\mathrm{d}t\bigg\rvert\leq\int_{1}^{N}\left\lvert e\left(\theta\left(f_{t}-\frac{At^{4}\theta}{24}\right)\right)-1\right\rvert dt

(4.7)

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{1}^{N}\bigg\lvert e\bigg(\theta\left(-\frac{At^{3}}{4}+\frac{11At^{2}}{24}-\frac{At}{4}+\frac{t^{3}B}{6}-\frac{t^{2}B}{2}+\frac{tB}{3}+\frac{t^{2}C}{2}-\frac{tC}{2}+t\right)\bigg)-1\bigg\rvert\;\mathrm{d}t.

\bigg\lvert 2\pi\theta\left(-\frac{At^{3}}{4}+\frac{11At^{2}}{24}-\frac{At}{4}+\frac{t^{3}B}{6}-\frac{t^{2}B}{2}+\frac{tB}{3}+\frac{t^{2}C}{2}-\frac{tC}{2}+t\right)\bigg\rvert\leq\frac{3A+2|B|}{6}\pi N^{\delta-1}\leq 1.

(4.8)

\displaystyle\left\lvert e\left(\theta\left(f_{t}-\frac{At^{4}\theta}{24}\right)\right)-1\right\rvert

Substituting (

∎

\displaystyle\bigg\lvert S_{f}(\alpha)^{s}-\bigg(\frac{V(q,a)}{24q}\int_{1}^{N}e\bigg(\frac{At^{4}\theta}{24}\bigg)\;\mathrm{d}t\bigg)^{s}\bigg\rvert

which completes the proof.
∎

. Let
\alpha\in\mathfrak{M}(q,a)
,

5.
Major Arcs : The Singular Series

In this section, our goal is to show that the singular series
\mathfrak{S}_{f,s}(m,N^{\delta})>0
. Our strategy is as follows. We first extend the sum
\mathfrak{S}_{f,s}(m,N^{\delta})
to infinity. By demonstrating that each summand
V(q)
for
q\in\mathbb{N}
is multiplicative, we decompose the completed series into an Euler product. Next, we relate the Euler product to the counting of solutions to an equation over finite fields. Finally, using the generalized Hensel’s Lemma, we show that for any finite field, there exists a solution that can be lifted to any larger field of the same characteristic. This leads us to our desired result.

5.1.
Singular Series Completion

In this subsection, we complete the series
\mathfrak{S}_{f,s}(m,N^{\delta})
. Define

(5.1)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{S}_{f,s}(m)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{q=1}^{\infty}V(q),\quad\textrm{where}

(5.2)

\displaystyle V(q)

\displaystyle:=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}a=1\\
(a,q)=1\end{subarray}}^{q}\bigg(\frac{V(q,a)}{24q}\bigg)^{s}e\bigg(-\frac{am}{q}\bigg),

and
V(q,a)
is given by (
4.2
). We begin by showing that
V(q)
is multiplicative.

Lemma 5.1
.

Suppose
(a,q)=(b,r)=(q,r)=1
. Then
V(qr,ar+bq)=\frac{1}{24}V(q,a)V(r,b)
.

Proof.

We will focus on the case where
(24,q)=1
. Proofs for other cases follow similar arguments and thus are omitted. By Lemma
4.3
, we have

\displaystyle V(qr,ar+bq)=24\sum_{n=1}^{qr}e\bigg(\frac{ar+bq}{qr}f_{n}\bigg).

Using Euclid’s algorithm, we know that every residue class
m
modulo
qr
can be uniquely expressed as
tr+uq
with
1\leq t\leq q
and
1\leq u\leq r
. Therefore, we can rewrite the sum as

\displaystyle V(qr,ar+bq)

\displaystyle=24\sum_{t=1}^{q}\sum_{u=1}^{r}e\bigg(\frac{ar+bq}{qr}f_{tr+uq}\bigg)

\displaystyle=24\sum_{t=1}^{q}\sum_{u=1}^{r}e\bigg(\frac{ar+bq}{qr}\cdot(f_{tr}+f_{uq})\bigg)

\displaystyle=24\sum_{t=1}^{q}e\bigg(\frac{a}{q}f_{tr}\bigg)\sum_{u=1}^{r}e\bigg(\frac{b}{r}f_{uq}\bigg).

Since
tr
and
uq
range over complete residue classes modulo
q
and
r
respectively, we conclude that

\displaystyle V(qr,ar+bq)=24\sum_{t=1}^{q}e\bigg(\frac{a}{q}f_{t}\bigg)\sum_{u=1}^{r}e\bigg(\frac{b}{r}f_{u}\bigg)=\frac{1}{24}V(q,a)V(r,b),

where the last equality follows from Lemma
4.3
.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

The function
V(q)
, as defined in (
5.2
), is multiplicative.

Proof.

Note that
V(1)=1
. Assume
(q,r)=1
. Then, by Lemma
5.1
,

\displaystyle V(qr)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}a=1\\
(a,qr)=1\end{subarray}}^{qr}\bigg(\frac{V(qr,a)}{24qr}\bigg)^{s}e\bigg(-\frac{am}{qr}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}a=1\\
(a,q)=1\end{subarray}}^{q}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}b=1\\
(b,r)=1\end{subarray}}^{r}\bigg(\frac{V(qr,ar+bq)}{24qr}\bigg)^{s}e\bigg(-\frac{ar+bq}{qr}m\bigg)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}a=1\\
(a,q)=1\end{subarray}}^{q}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}b=1\\
(b,r)=1\end{subarray}}^{r}\bigg(\frac{V(q,a)V(r,b)}{576qr}\bigg)^{s}e\bigg(-\frac{am}{q}\bigg)e\bigg(-\frac{bm}{r}\bigg)

\displaystyle=V(q)V(r),

which completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
s\geq 17
and
N^{\delta}\geq e^{e^{467}}
. Then,
|\mathfrak{S}_{f,s}(m)|\leq e^{e^{468}}
. Moreover, we have

\displaystyle|\mathfrak{S}_{f,s}(m)-\mathfrak{S}_{f,s}(m,N^{\delta})|\leq\frac{(52A^{1/4})^{s}}{\left(\frac{9s}{73}-2\right)N^{\left(\frac{9s}{73}-2\right)\delta}}.

Proof.

We begin by evaluating
V(q,a)
using Lemma
3.6
. To address both cases where
(Aa,24q)=1
and
(Aa,24q)\neq 1
, we choose
\eta=A^{2}
in Lemma
3.6
. This gives us the bound

\displaystyle|V(q,a)|

\displaystyle\leq 2(24q)^{7/8}+5A^{1/4}(24q)^{1+\frac{3.1983}{4\log\log(24q)}}\left(q^{-1}+(24q)^{-1}+q(24q)^{-4}\right)^{1/8}(\log q)^{1/8}

\displaystyle\leq 33q^{7/8}+1200A^{1/4}q^{7/8+\frac{3.1983}{4\log\log(24q)}+\frac{\log\log q}{8\log q}}.

When
q\geq e^{e^{467}}
, we have

\frac{3.1983}{4\log\log(24q)}+\frac{\log\log q}{8\log q}\leq\frac{1}{584},

which implies

|V(q,a)|\leq 33q^{7/8}+1200A^{1/4}q^{64/73}\leq 1233A^{1/4}q^{64/73}.

Therefore, we conclude that
|V(q)|\leq(52A^{1/4})^{s}q^{1-\frac{9s}{73}}
, where
s\geq 17
. As a result,
\mathfrak{S}_{f,s}(m)
converges absolutely and uniformly with respect to
m
. We now have the following bound:

\displaystyle|\mathfrak{S}_{f,s}(m)|\leq\sum_{q=1}^{\infty}|V(q)|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{q=1}^{\lfloor e^{e^{467}}\rfloor}|V(q)|+(52A^{1/4})^{s}\int_{e^{e^{467}}}^{\infty}x^{1-\frac{9s}{73}}\;\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{q=1}^{\lfloor e^{e^{467}}\rfloor}q+\frac{(52A^{1/4})^{s}}{\frac{9s}{73}-2}\left(e^{e^{467}}\right)^{2-\frac{9s}{73}}\leq e^{e^{468}}.

Moreover, when
N^{\delta}\geq e^{e^{467}}
,
we deduce that

\displaystyle|\mathfrak{S}_{f,s}(m)-\mathfrak{S}_{f,s}(m,N^{\delta})|\leq(52A^{1/4})^{s}\int_{N^{\delta}}^{\infty}x^{1-\frac{9s}{73}}\;\mathrm{d}x\leq\frac{(52A^{1/4})^{s}}{\left(\frac{9s}{73}-2\right)N^{\left(\frac{9s}{73}-2\right)\delta}}.

This completes the proof.
∎

5.2.
Counting Solution over Finite Fields

Since
V(q)
is multiplicative by Lemma
5.2
and
\mathfrak{S}_{f,s}(m)
converges absolutely by Lemma
5.3
, we obtain the Euler product representation

\displaystyle\mathfrak{S}_{f,s}(m)=\prod_{p\text{ prime}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}V(p^{k})=\prod_{p\text{ prime}}(1+V(p)+V(p^{2})+\cdots).

Let
1\leq n_{i}\leq t
. Define
\mathcal{M}_{m}(t,q)
as the number of solutions to the congruence equation

(5.3)

\displaystyle f(n_{1})+f(n_{2})+\cdots+f(n_{s})\equiv m\bmod q,

where
f(x)=A\binom{t}{4}+B\binom{t}{3}+C\binom{t}{2}+t
. To simplify the notation, we write
\mathcal{M}_{m}(q)=\mathcal{M}_{m}(q,q)
.

Lemma 5.4
.

For
q\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{d\,|\,q}V(d)=q^{1-s}24^{-s}\mathcal{M}_{m}(24q,q).

Proof.

The proof is analogous to Lemma 6.4 in
[
2
]
by replacing
2q
with
24q
. Hence, we omit the details here.
∎

It follows from Lemma
5.4
by choosing

that

\displaystyle\mathfrak{S}_{f,s}(m)

\displaystyle=\prod_{p\text{ prime}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}V(p^{k})=\prod_{p\text{ prime}}\lim_{k\to\infty}24^{-s}p^{k(1-s)}\mathcal{M}_{m}(24p^{k},p^{k})

\displaystyle=\prod_{p\text{ prime}}\lim_{k\to\infty}p^{k(1-s)}\mathcal{M}_{m}(p^{k}),

where the third equality follows from
\mathcal{M}_{m}(24q,q)=24^{s}\mathcal{M}_{m}(q)
. Define

T_{m}(p):=\lim_{k\to\infty}p^{k(1-s)}\mathcal{M}_{m}(p^{k}).

Then
\mathfrak{S}_{f,s}(m)=\prod_{p}T_{m}(p)
. The following lemma gives us a bound for
T_{m}(p)
, which leads to an estimation for
\mathfrak{S}_{f,s}(m)
.

Lemma 5.5
.

For any
s\geq 17
and any prime
p
, we have

\left|T_{m}(p)-1\right|\leq e^{se^{932}}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)\frac{p^{1-\frac{9s}{73}}}{1-p^{1-\frac{9s}{73}}}.

Proof.

We consider the following cases.

Case 1.

p>3
. In this case,
(24,p)=1
, so any solution
(n_{1},\dots,n_{s})
of the equation

(5.4)

\tilde{f}(n_{1})+\dots+\tilde{f}(n_{s})\equiv 24m\mod p^{k}

where
\tilde{f}(t):=24f(t)
, is also a solution of (
5.3
) and vice versa. Therefore

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{m}(p^{k})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p^{k}}\sum_{t=1}^{p^{k}}\sum_{n_{1}=1}^{p^{k}}\sum_{n_{2}=1}^{p^{k}}\cdots\sum_{n_{s}=1}^{p^{k}}e(t(\tilde{f}(n_{1})+\tilde{f}(n_{2})+\cdots+\tilde{f}(n_{s})-24m)/p^{k})

(5.5)

\displaystyle=p^{(s-1)k}+\frac{1}{p^{k}}\sum_{t=1}^{p^{k}-1}e\bigg(-\frac{24mt}{p^{k}}\bigg)\bigg(\sum_{x=1}^{p^{k}}e\bigg(\frac{t\tilde{f}(x)}{p^{k}}\bigg)\bigg)^{s}.

Each integer
1\leq t\leq p^{k}-1
can be uniquely expressed as
t=bp^{k-r}
, where
(b,p)=1
,
1\leq r\leq k
, and
1\leq b\leq p^{r}
. Therefore, the second term in the right-hand side of equation (
5.5
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p^{k}}\sum_{r=1}^{k}

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}b=1\\
(b,p)=1\end{subarray}}^{p^{r}}e\bigg(\frac{-24mb}{p^{r}}\bigg)\bigg(\sum_{x=1}^{p^{k}}e\bigg(\frac{b\tilde{f}(x)}{p^{r}}\bigg)\bigg)^{s}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p^{k}}\sum_{r=1}^{k}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}b=1\\
(b,p)=1\end{subarray}}^{p^{r}}e\bigg(\frac{-24mb}{p^{r}}\bigg)\bigg(p^{k-r}\sum_{x=1}^{p^{r}}e\bigg(\frac{b\tilde{f}(x)}{p^{r}}\bigg)\bigg)^{s}

(5.6)

\displaystyle=p^{(s-1)k}\sum_{r=1}^{k}p^{-rs}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}b=1\\
(b,p)=1\end{subarray}}^{p^{r}}e\bigg(\frac{-24mb}{p^{r}}\bigg)\bigg(\sum_{x=1}^{p^{r}}e\bigg(\frac{b\tilde{f}(x)}{p^{r}}\bigg)\bigg)^{s}.

By combining (
5.5
) and (
5.6
), we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{M}_{m}(p^{k})-p^{(s-1)k}\right|\leq p^{(s-1)k}\sum_{r=1}^{k}p^{-rs}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}b=1\\
(b,p)=1\end{subarray}}^{p^{r}}\left|\sum_{x=1}^{p^{r}}e\bigg(\frac{b\tilde{f}(x)}{p^{r}}\bigg)\right|^{s}.

Applying Lemma
3.6
with
X=q=p^{r}
and
\eta=1
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{x=1}^{p^{r}}e\bigg(\frac{b\tilde{f}(x)}{p^{r}}\bigg)\right|

\displaystyle\leq 2p^{7r/8}+5p^{r+\frac{3.1983r}{4\log(3r\log p)}}\left(2p^{-r}+p^{-3r}\right)^{1/8}(r\log p)^{1/8}

\displaystyle\leq 2p^{7r/8}+5(3p^{-r})^{1/8}p^{r+\frac{3.1983r}{4\log(r\log p)}}p^{\frac{\log(3r\log p)}{8\log p}}

\displaystyle\leq 2p^{7r/8}+6p^{7r/8+\frac{3.1983r}{4\log(3r\log p)}+\frac{\log(r\log p)}{8\log p}}.

When
r\geq 1
and
p\geq e^{e^{467}}
, we have

(5.7)

\frac{3.1983r}{4\log(3r\log p)}+\frac{\log(r\log p)}{8\log p}\leq\frac{r}{584},

It is also straight forward to check that the above inequality also holds for all
p>3
and
r\geq e^{467}
. Furthermore, for
3<p\leq e^{e^{467}}
and
1\leq r\leq e^{467}
, trivially we have

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{x=1}^{p^{r}}e\left(\frac{b\tilde{f}(x)}{p^{r}}\right)\right|\leq p^{r}\leq e^{e^{932}}p^{\frac{64r}{73}}.

Combining two cases, we see that for any prime
p>3
and any
r\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

(5.8)

\left|\sum_{x=1}^{p^{r}}e\left(\frac{b\tilde{f}(x)}{p^{r}}\right)\right|\leq e^{e^{932}}p^{\frac{64r}{73}}.

Case 2.

p=2
. In this case, the equation (
5.3
) is equivalent to

3f(n_{1})+\dots+3f(n_{s})\equiv 3m\mod 2^{k}.

Therefore, we can write

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{m}(2^{k})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{k}}\sum_{t=1}^{p^{k}}\sum_{n_{1}=1}^{2^{k}}\cdots\sum_{n_{s}=1}^{2^{k}}e(t(3\tilde{f}(n_{1})+\cdots+3\tilde{f}(n_{s})-3m)/2^{k})

(5.9)

\displaystyle=2^{(s-1)k}+\frac{1}{2^{k}}\sum_{t=1}^{2^{k}-1}e\bigg(-\frac{3mt}{2^{k}}\bigg)\bigg(\sum_{x=1}^{2^{k}}e\bigg(\frac{t\tilde{f}(x)}{2^{k+3}}\bigg)\bigg)^{s},

Similar with Case 1, it follows that

\left|\mathcal{M}_{m}(2^{k})-2^{(s-1)k}\right|\leq 2^{(s-1)k}\sum_{r=1}^{k}2^{-rs}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b=1\\
(b,2)=1\end{subarray}}^{2^{r}}\left|\sum_{x=1}^{2^{r}}e\left(\frac{t\tilde{f}(x)}{2^{r+3}}\right)\right|^{s}

for any
r\in\mathbb{N}
. Applying Lemma
3.6
with
\eta=1,X=2^{r}
, and
q=2^{r+3}
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{x=1}^{2^{r}}e\left(\frac{b\tilde{f}(x)}{2^{r+3}}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq 2\cdot 2^{7r/8}+5\cdot 2^{r+\frac{3.1983r}{4\log(3r\log 2)}}(2^{-r-2}+2^{-r}+2^{2-3r})^{1/8}\left(\log(2^{r+3})\right)^{1/8}

\displaystyle=2\cdot 2^{7r/8}+6\cdot 2^{7r/8+\frac{3.1983r}{\log(3r\log 2)}+\frac{\log\left((r+3)\log 2\right)}{8\log 2}}\leq 2^{e^{467}}\cdot 2^{\frac{64r}{73}}.

Case 3.

p=3
. This case is similar with Case 2, in which we use the equivalency between (
5.3
) and

8f(n_{1})+\dots+8f(n_{s})\equiv 8m\mod 3^{k}.

In particular, we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{M}_{m}(3^{k})-3^{(s-1)k}\right|\leq 3^{(s-1)k}\sum_{r=1}^{k}3^{-rs}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b=1\\
(b,3)=1\end{subarray}}^{3^{r}}\left|\sum_{x=1}^{3^{r}}e\left(\frac{b\tilde{f}(x)}{3^{r+1}}\right)\right|

Again, by applying Lemma
3.1
with
\eta=1,X=3^{r},
and
q=3^{r+1}
, we have for any
r\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{x=1}^{3^{r}}e\left(\frac{b\tilde{f}(x)}{3^{r+1}}\right)\right|\leq e^{e^{467}}3^{\frac{64r}{73}}.

Combing all three cases, we obtain that for
s\geq 17
and for any prime
p
,

|T_{m}(p)-1|\leq\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}p^{-rs}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b=1\\
(b,p)=1\end{subarray}}^{p^{r}}e^{se^{932}}p^{\frac{64sr}{73}}=e^{se^{932}}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)\frac{p^{1-\frac{9s}{73}}}{1-p^{1-\frac{9s}{73}}}.

∎

5.3.
Hensel Lifting

In this subsection, we aim to provide a lower bound for
\mathcal{M}_{m}(p^{k})
for any prime
p
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
. First, we consider the case when
k=1
. In this case,
\mathcal{M}_{m}(p)
is the number of solutions to the equation

(5.10)

f(n_{1})+f(n_{2})+\dots+f(n_{s})\equiv m\mod p,

where
p\geq 7
and
1\leq n_{i}\leq p
for
i=1,2,\dots,s
. Let
\mathcal{M}_{m}^{*}(p)
be the number of solutions to (
5.10
) such that
(f(n_{1}),p)=(f^{\prime}(n_{1}),p)=1
. The following lemma states that there is indeed at least one such solution.

Lemma 5.6
.

For
p\geq 11
and
s\geq 17
, we have
\mathcal{M}_{m}^{*}(p)\geq 1
.

To prove Lemma
5.6
, we use the following well-known Cauchy-Davenport inequality in additive combinatorics, see
[
7
]
.

Theorem 5.7

(Cauchy-Davenport inequality)
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{A}
is a set of
r
residue classes modulo
q
, and that
\mathcal{B}
is a set
of
s
such classes. Suppose further that
0\in\mathcal{B}
, and that whenever
b\in\mathcal{B}
and
q\nmid b
, one has
(b,q)=1
. Then

|\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B}|\geq\min\{q,|\mathcal{A}|+|\mathcal{B}|-1\}.

Proof of Lemma
5.6
.

Let

\mathcal{A}=\{f(n)~\mathrm{mod}~p:1\leq n\leq p,(f(n),p)=(f^{\prime}(n),p)=1\}

and
\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{A}\cup\{0\}
. Since there are at most
7
possibilities of
n
such that
(f(n),p)=p
or
(f^{\prime}(n),p)=p
, we have
|\mathcal{A}|\geq\left\lceil\frac{p-7}{4}\right\rceil
and
|\mathcal{B}|=|\mathcal{A}|+1
.
By applying Cauchy-Davenport inequality and induction on
s
, we obtain

|\mathcal{A}+(s-1)\mathcal{B}|\geq\min\left\{p,s\left\lceil\frac{p-7}{4}\right\rceil\right\}=p

when
p\geq 11
and
s\geq 17
. Hence, there exist
a\in A
and
b_{2},\dots,b_{s}\in\mathcal{B}
such that

f(a)+f(b_{2})+\dots+f(b_{s})\equiv m~(\mathrm{mod}~p).

By definition of
\mathcal{A}
, we have
(f(a),p)=(f^{\prime}(a),p)=1
. Thus
\mathcal{M}_{m}^{*}(p)\geq 1
.
∎

Using Lemma
5.6
together with Hensel’s lemma, we immediately obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 5.8
.

For any prime
p\geq 11
, any integers
k\geq 2
and
s\geq 17
, we have

\mathcal{M}_{m}(p^{k})\geq p^{(k-1)(s-1)}.

Now we are left to discuss lower bounds for
T_{m}(p)
for
p\leq 7
. To this end, we rely on the generalized Hensel’s Lemma.

Lemma 5.9

(generalized Hensel’s Lemma,
[
13
]
)
.

Let
f(x)
be a polynomial with integral coefficients. Suppose that there exist
j,\tau\in\mathbb{N}
such that
f^{\prime}(a)\equiv 0\bmod p^{j}
,
p^{\tau}||f^{\prime}(a)
and that
j\geq 2\tau+1
. If
b\equiv a\bmod p^{j-\tau}
, then
f(b)\equiv f(a)\bmod p^{j}
and
p^{\tau}||f^{\prime}(b)
. Moreover, there is a unique
a_{0}\bmod p
such that
f(a+a_{0}p^{j-\tau})\equiv 0\bmod p^{j+1}
.

Lemma 5.10
.

For
s\geq 17
and any
f
satisfying (
1.4
), we have

T_{m}(p)>p^{1-s},

for
p\geq 11
. For
p<11
, define

(5.11)

\displaystyle\tau=\max_{p<11}\min\{\tau\in\mathbb{N}:f^{\prime}(y)\neq 0\bmod p^{\tau}\text{ for some integer $y$}\}.

Then,

T_{m}(p)>p^{(2\tau+1)(1-s)}.

In particular, when
f
is one of the target polynomials
f_{1},f_{2},f_{3}
, defined in (
1.2
), we have

T_{m}(p)>p^{1-s},

for
p\geq 3
, and

T_{m}(2)>2^{5(1-s)}.

Remark 5.11
.

Since
f^{\prime}
is a degree-three polynomial, the integer
y
in (
5.11
) always exists for infinitely many
\tau\in\mathbb{N}
by the property of the ring of
p
-adic integers.

Proof.

When
p\geq 11
, by Corollary
5.8
, we have

T_{m}(p)=\lim_{k\to\infty}p^{k(1-s)}\mathcal{M}_{m}(p^{k})\geq p^{1-s}>0

for any
p\geq 11
and
s\geq 17
. Now assume
p<11
. We will focus on
p=2
, as the other small
p
cases follow a similar argument.

Fix
y_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
f^{\prime}(y_{1})\neq 0\bmod 2^{\tau}
for some
\tau\geq 1
. If
\tau=1
, then by using Hensel’s lemma, we obtain
T_{m}(2)>2^{1-s}
. Otherwise, define

\mathcal{A}=\{f(n)~\mathrm{mod}~2^{2\tau+1}:1\leq n\leq 2^{2\tau+1},(f(n),2)=1\}

and
\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{A}\cup\{0\}
. Then, using similar arguments as in the proof of Lemma
5.6
, and since
s\geq 17
, we obtain

|\mathcal{A}+(s-2)\mathcal{B}|\geq\min\left\{2^{2\tau+1},(s-2)\left\lceil\frac{2^{2\tau+1}-4}{4}\right\rceil\right\}=2^{2\tau+1}.

Therefore, there always exist
y_{2},\dots,y_{s}
such that

f(y_{2})+\dots+f(y_{s})\equiv m-f(y_{1})\bmod 2^{2\tau+1}.

Now apply generalized Hensel’s Lemma, for any
k\geq 2\tau+1
,
(y_{1},y_{2},\cdots,y_{s})
can be lifted to
2^{(k-2\tau-1)(s-1)}
solutions to

(5.12)

\displaystyle f(n_{1})+f(n_{2})+\cdots+f(n_{s})\equiv m\bmod p^{k}.

Thus, the first part of the lemma is proved.

To prove the remaining statement, it suffices to find
\tau
for each
f_{i}
and
p<11
. We will present
f=f_{1}
here, as the other two polynomials follow similar arguments. Assume
p=2
. Recall that
f(0)=0
and
f(1)=1
. Fix
n_{1}=1
. Note that
f^{\prime}(1)=4
, which is congruent to
0
modulo
4
but not congruent to
0
modulo
8
. Then, by the argument above,
(1,y_{1},\cdots,y_{s})
can be lifted to
2^{(k-5)(s-1)}
solutions to (
5.12
). Thus,

T_{m}(2)\geq 2^{5(1-s)}.

Finally, for
3\leq p\leq 7
, note that
f^{\prime}(1)=4
is not congruent to
0
modulo
p
, so

T_{m}(p)\geq p^{1-s}.

∎

5.4.
A lower bound for
\mathfrak{S}_{f,s}(m)

We are ready to establish a lower bound for
\mathfrak{S}_{f,s}(m)
.

Lemma 5.12
.

For any polynomial
f
in the form of (
1.4
), let
\tau
be as defined in (
5.11
). We then have
\mathfrak{S}_{f,s}(m)>0
. More precisely,

(5.13)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{S}_{f,s}(m)\geq 2^{\frac{z^{2}}{146-9s}}\exp((12\tau+1.03z)(1-s))>0

uniformly, where
z=(2e^{se^{932}}+1)^{\frac{73}{9s-21}}
. In particular, for the three target polynomials
f_{1},f_{2}
, and
f_{3}
, we have

(5.14)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{S}_{f,s}(m)\geq 2^{\frac{z^{2}}{146-9s}}\exp(1.03z(1-s))>0.

Proof.

Define
\theta_{p}:=T_{m}(p)-1
for prime
p
and let
w=\prod_{p>z}T_{m}(p)^{-1}
. From Lemma
5.5
, it follows that for
p>z
,
\left|\theta_{p}\right|\leq\frac{1}{2}
.
By the Taylor expansion, we write

(5.15)

\displaystyle\log w

\displaystyle=-\sum_{p>z}\log(1+\theta_{p})=\sum_{p>z}\left(\theta_{p}+\frac{\theta^{2}_{p}}{2}+\frac{\theta^{3}_{p}}{3}+\cdots\right).

Thus, by Lemma
5.5
and (
5.15
), we have

\displaystyle\log w

\displaystyle\leq 4\log 2\cdot e^{se^{932}}\sum_{p>z}p^{1-\frac{9s}{73}}\leq 4\log 2\cdot e^{se^{932}}\int^{\infty}_{z}x^{1-\frac{9s}{73}}\;\mathrm{d}x=\frac{146z^{125/73}\log 2}{9s-146}.

Exponentiating both sides of the above inequality, we obtain

w\leq 2^{\frac{146z^{125/73}}{9s-146}},

which implies that

(5.16)

\displaystyle\prod_{p>z}T_{m}(p)\geq 2^{\frac{146z^{125/73}}{146-9s}}\geq 2^{\frac{z^{2}}{146-9s}}.

For
p\leq z
, Lemma
5.10
shows that

\displaystyle\prod_{p\leq z}T_{m}(p)

\displaystyle\geq 210^{(2\tau+1)(1-s)}\prod_{11\leq p\leq z}p^{1-s}

(5.17)

\displaystyle\geq 210^{2\tau(1-s)}\exp\bigg((1-s)\left(0.0242334\frac{z}{\log z}+z\right)\bigg)

(5.18)

\displaystyle\geq\exp((1.03z+12\tau)(1-s)),

where the second inequality follows from Rosser and Schoenfeld’s work
[
20
]
on upper bounds for
\theta(x)
. For
f_{1},f_{2},
and
f_{3}
, we have

\prod_{p\leq z}T_{m}(p)\geq\exp(1.03z(1-s)).

Combining inequalities (
5.16
) and (
5.18
), we have for a general
f
satisfying (
1.4
),

\displaystyle\mathfrak{S}_{f,s}(m)

\displaystyle\geq 2^{\frac{z^{2}}{146-9s}}\exp((1.03z+12\tau)(1-s))>0.

For the target polynomials
f_{1},f_{2},f_{3}
, we have

\mathfrak{S}_{f_{i},s}(m)\geq 2^{\frac{z^{2}}{146-9s}}\exp(1.03z(1-s))>0,

which completes the proof.
∎

6.
Major Arcs : The Singular Integral

In this section, we estimate the singular integral
J^{*}(m)
given by (
4.11
). In light of the discussion in Lemma
5.3
, we assume
N^{\delta}\geq e^{e^{467}}
. We proceed as follows: First, we estimate the integral
v(\theta)
in the integrand of
J^{*}(m)
by the finite sum
v_{1}(\theta)
, defined in (
6.1
) below. By replacing
v(\theta)
in
J^{*}(m)
with
v_{1}(\theta)
, we may extend the range of this new integral to a unit interval, and then approximate it using properties of Gamma functions.

6.1.
Approximation of
v(\theta)

Let
N_{0}=\frac{A}{24}N^{4}
where
N
is given by (
2.4
). Define

(6.1)

\displaystyle v_{1}(\theta):=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{1\leq n\leq N_{0}}n^{-3/4}e(\theta n)\quad\textrm{and}\quad v_{2}(\theta):=\int_{0}^{N_{0}^{1/4}}e(\theta t^{4})\;\mathrm{d}t.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
v(\theta)
and
v_{1}(\theta)
be as defined in (
4.10
) and (
6.1
) respectively. Then

\bigg\lvert v_{1}(\theta)-\left(\frac{A}{24}\right)^{1/4}v(\theta)\bigg\rvert\leq AN^{\delta}.

Proof.

We have

(6.2)

\displaystyle\bigg\lvert v_{2}(\theta)-\left(\frac{A}{24}\right)^{1/4}v(\theta)\bigg\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\sqrt[4]{\frac{A}{24}}}\left|e(\theta t^{4})\right|\;\mathrm{d}t=\left(\frac{A}{24}\right)^{1/4}.

Since
f(n)=n^{-3/4}
is a decreasing function, we have

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{N_{0}+1}t^{-3/4}\;\mathrm{d}t\leq\sum_{1\leq n\leq N_{0}}n^{-3/4}\leq\int_{0}^{N_{0}}t^{-3/4}\;\mathrm{d}t.

Therefore, we get

(6.3)

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1}{4}\sum_{1\leq n\leq N_{0}}n^{-3/4}-\frac{1}{4}\int_{1}^{N_{0}}t^{-3/4}\;\mathrm{d}t\right|\leq\frac{1}{2}\left|\int_{0}^{1}t^{-3/4}\;\mathrm{d}t\right|=2.

Since
\frac{1}{4}\int_{1}^{N_{0}}t^{-3/4}dt=N_{0}^{1/4}-1,
we obtain

(6.4)

\displaystyle\bigg|\frac{1}{4}\sum_{1\leq n\leq N_{0}}n^{-3/4}-N_{0}^{1/4}\bigg|\leq 3.

Using partial summation, we have

(6.5)

\displaystyle v_{1}(\theta)=e(N_{0}\theta)\bigg(\frac{1}{4}\sum_{1\leq n\leq N_{0}}n^{-3/4}\bigg)-2\pi i\theta\int_{1}^{N_{0}}\bigg(\frac{1}{4}\sum_{1\leq n\leq t}n^{-3/4}\bigg)e(\theta t)\;\mathrm{d}t.

On the other hand, using integration by parts and a change of variables, we obtain

(6.6)

\displaystyle v_{2}(\theta)=e(N_{0}\theta)N_{0}^{1/4}-2\pi i\theta\int_{0}^{N_{0}}t^{1/4}e(\theta t)\;\mathrm{d}t.

Combining (
6.3
), (
6.4
), (
6.5
) and (
6.6
), we deduce that

\displaystyle\left|v_{1}(\theta)-v_{2}(\theta)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\bigg\lvert\frac{1}{4}\sum_{1\leq n\leq N_{0}}n^{-3/4}-N_{0}^{1/4}\bigg\rvert+2\pi|\theta|\bigg(1+\int_{1}^{N_{0}}\bigg\lvert t^{1/4}-\bigg(\frac{1}{4}\sum_{1\leq n\leq t}n^{-3/4}\bigg)\bigg\rvert\;\mathrm{d}t\bigg)

(6.7)

\displaystyle\leq 3+2\pi|\theta|(3N_{0}-2).

Recall that
|\theta|<N^{\delta-4}
and
N^{\delta}\geq e^{e^{467}}
. Putting together (
6.2
) and (
6.7
), we have

\displaystyle\bigg\lvert v_{1}(\theta)-\left(\frac{A}{24}\right)^{1/4}v(\theta)\bigg\rvert\leq 3+\pi(6N_{0}-4)N^{\delta-4}+\sqrt[4]{A/24}\leq AN^{\delta},

which completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
|\theta|\leq\frac{1}{2}
and
v_{1}(\theta)
be as in (
6.1
). Then
|v_{1}(\theta)|\leq\min\{2m^{1/4},2|\theta|^{-1/4}\}
.

Proof.

If
|\theta|\leq m^{-1}
, the trivial bound suffices. Assume
|\theta|>m^{-1}
and let
M=[|\theta|^{-1}]
. Then, the contribution to
v_{1}(\theta)
from the terms
n\leq M
is bounded by
M^{1/4}\leq|\theta|^{-1/4}
.

When
n>M
, define
S_{n}=\sum_{1\leq r\leq n}e(\theta r)
and
c_{n}=\frac{1}{4}n^{-3/4}
. We can express the sum as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\sum_{n=M+1}^{N_{0}}n^{-3/4}e(\theta n)=c_{N_{0}+1}S_{N_{0}}-c_{M+1}S_{M}+\sum_{n=M+1}^{N_{0}}(c_{n}-c_{n+1})S_{n}.

By Lemma
3.4
, we know that
\lvert S_{n}\rvert\leq\frac{1}{2\lvert\theta\rvert}
. Since
c_{n}
is strictly decreasing, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\sum_{n=M+1}^{N_{0}}n^{-3/4}e(\theta n)

\displaystyle=-c_{M+1}S_{M}+\sum_{n=M+1}^{N_{0}-1}(c_{n}-c_{n+1})S_{n}+c_{N_{0}}S_{N_{0}}

\displaystyle\leq c_{M+1}S_{M}+\sum_{n=M+1}^{N_{0}-1}(c_{n}-c_{n+1})\frac{1}{2\lvert\theta\rvert}+c_{N_{0}}\frac{1}{2\lvert\theta\rvert}

\displaystyle\leq 2c_{M+1}\frac{1}{2\lvert\theta\rvert}\leq\lvert\theta\rvert^{-1/4}.

Adding the contributions from the two parts, we obtain the desired bound

. This completes the proof.

Galois coverings, τ \tau -rigidity and mutations
Charles Paquette 1,2 , Deepanshu Prasad 2 , and David Wehlau 1,2

Abstract
. For an algebraically closed field 𝕂 \mathbb{K} , we consider a Galois G G -covering ℬ → 𝒜 \mathcal{B}\rightarrow\mathcal{A} between locally bounded 𝕂 \mathbb{K} -categories given by bound quivers, where G G is torsion-free and acts freely on the objects of ℬ \mathcal{B} . We define the notion of ( G , τ ℬ ) (G,\tau_{\mathcal{B}}) -rigid subcategory and of support ( G , τ ℬ ) (G,\tau_{\mathcal{B}}) -tilting pairs over ℬ \mathcal{B} - mod \rm mod . These are the analogues of the similar concepts in the context of a finite dimensional algebra, where we additionally require that the subcategory be G G -equivariant. When 𝒜 \mathcal{A} is a finite dimensional algebra, we show that the corresponding push-down functor ℱ λ : ℬ \mathcal{F}_{\lambda}:\mathcal{B} - mod \rm mod → 𝒜 \rightarrow\mathcal{A} - mod \rm mod sends ( G , τ ℬ ) (G,\tau_{\mathcal{B}}) -rigid subcategories (respectively support ( G , τ ℬ ) (G,\tau_{\mathcal{B}}) -tilting pairs) to τ 𝒜 \tau_{\mathcal{A}} -rigid modules (respectively support τ 𝒜 \tau_{\mathcal{A}} -tilting pairs). We further show that there is a notion of mutation for support ( G , τ ℬ ) (G,\tau_{\mathcal{B}}) -tilting pairs over ℬ \mathcal{B} - mod \rm mod . Mutations of support τ 𝒜 \tau_{\mathcal{A}} -tilting pairs and of support ( G , τ ℬ ) (G,\tau_{\mathcal{B}}) -tilting pairs commute with the push-down functor. We derive some consequences of this, and in particular, we derive a τ \tau -tilting analogue of the result of P. Gabriel that locally representation-finiteness is preserved under coverings. Finally, we prove that when the Galois group G G is finitely generated free, any rigid 𝒜 \mathcal{A} -module (and in particular τ 𝒜 \tau_{\mathcal{A}} -rigid 𝒜 \mathcal{A} -modules) lies in the essential image of the push-down functor.

Introduction

Let
\mathbb{K}
be an algebraically closed field. We let
\mathcal{A}
denote a finite dimensional
\mathbb{K}
-algebra, which we assume to be connected, without no loss of generality.
Our focus is on the representation theory of
\mathcal{A}
,
specifically the module category

of finitely generated left
\mathcal{A}
-modules. An important class of modules is the class of tilting modules, which traces back to the work of Bernstein, Gelfand and Ponomarev
[

[

]

[

One of the important properties of tilting modules
is that any almost-complete tilting module has either one or two complements, making possible a partially defined notion of mutation. To make mutation always possible, a bigger class of modules having the more regular property that almost-complete ones always have two complements is desirable. This is well accomplished by introducing support
\tau

[

This latter property is particularly significant in the context of categorification of cluster algebras. In the realm of cluster categories, as explored in
[

[

]
, there exists a class of objects known as cluster-tilting objects, which possess the desired property of ensuring the existence of two complements.

On the other hand, coverings techniques in representation theory were introduced and developed to understand many aspects of representation theory, including representation-finite algebras and their indecomposable representations. In this theory, one of the important results can be summarized as follows, see

]

Preliminaries

Let
\mathbb{K}
be an algebraically closed field, and let

the set of its

, and by

and

and the
\emph{tail}
of

and

\emph{finitequiver}
.

A

is defined as a sequence of arrows in

such that

for

. We call the integer
\ell

of

and define

and
t(p)=tp=ta_{1}
. For every

, we introduce a
\emph{trivialpath}

e_{x}
of length 0, which satisifies
h(e_{x})=t(e_{x})=x
. The
\emph{pathcategory}

\mathbb{K}Q
is defined as follows: its object set is
Q_{0}
, and, given
x,y\in Q_{0}
, the morphism set
\mathbb{K}Q(x,y)
is the
\mathbb{K}
-vector space having as basis the set of paths from
x
to
y
. The composition

of two paths

and
w_{2}\in\mathbb{K}Q(x,y)
is given by the concatenation of paths:

. Composition of morphisms is induced from this using bilinearity.

Let

be a two sided ideal. We can consider
\mathcal{A}\coloneqq\mathbb{K}Q/I
as a
\mathbb{K}
-linear category with object class

and the set of morphisms from

to

, which we denote by

. The

is said to be

(2)

\mathcal{A}
are non-isomorphic

(3)

for each object

, we have

and
\dim_{\mathbb{K}}\left(\oplus_{y\in\mathcal{A}}\mathcal{A}(y,x)\right)<\infty

The quiver

is

if the sets

and

. Let
\mathfrak{m}

\mathbb{K}Q

, that is, the ideal whose value

2.1.
Homotopy relations for bound quivers

Let
(Q,I)
be a bound quiver, and let
x,y
be two vertices of
Q
. Consider the relation
\rho=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\lambda_{i}w_{i}
, where
\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{K}^{*}

are pairwise distinct paths of length at least two from

to
y
. We say

\emph{minimalrelation}

(1)

(2)

for every non-empty proper subset

we have
\sum_{i\in S}\lambda_{i}w_{i}\notin I
.

Given an arrow
\alpha:x\rightarrow y
in
Q
, we denote by
\alpha^{-1}
its formal inverse of head
h(\alpha^{-1})=x

and tail
t(\alpha^{-1})=y
. A
\emph{walk}

to a vertex

is a sequence
w=\alpha_{n}\dotsb\alpha_{2}\alpha_{1}
of arrows and formal inverses of arrows such that

,

are walks in

, one can consider the walk

. This yields a product on the set of walks, which, however, is not everywhere defined. We define the
\emph{homotopyrelation}
on the set of walks on
Q
as the smallest equivalence relation
\sim
satisfying:

(1)

For each arrow
\alpha:x\rightarrow y
, one has
\alpha^{-1}\alpha\sim e_{x}
and
\alpha\alpha^{-1}\sim e_{y}
.

(2)

For each minimal relation
\sum_{i=1}^{r}\lambda_{i}w_{i}
, one has

, for all
i,j\in\{1,\dotsc,r\}
.

(3)

If

and
w^{\prime}
are walks, and

, then

, whenever these
compositions are defined.

We denote by

the homotopy class of a walk

.

Definition 2.2

Let

(1)

The

2.3.
Pull-up and push-down functors

In this section we define two special functors associated with the Galois covering of bound quivers, and look at some of their properties.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
\mathcal{F}:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}
be a
\mathbb{K}
-linear functor between two
\mathbb{K}
-linear categories. We call
\mathcal{F}
a
\emph{coveringfunctor}

\coprod_{z/b}\mathcal{C}(x,z)\rightarrow\mathcal{D}(a,b)\;\;\;\text{and}\;\;\;\coprod_{t/a}\mathcal{C}(t,y)\rightarrow\mathcal{D}(a,b)

and

of
\mathcal{D}
. Here

and

\mathcal{C}

\mathcal{F}z=b
, respectively; the maps are also supposed to be bijective for all
x
and

chosen such that
\mathcal{F}x=a
and
\mathcal{F}y=b
, respectively.

Given a quiver

and an admissible ideal

in the
\mathbb{K}
-linear category
\mathbb{K}Q
, let
\mathcal{A}
be the
\mathbb{K}
-linear category
\mathbb{K}Q/I
. The left
\mathcal{A}
-module

(or representation of
\mathcal{A}
) is called
\emph{locally-finitedimensional}
if
M(x)
is finite dimensional, for all objects
x\in\mathcal{A}
. We denote by
\mathcal{A}\MOD
the category of all left
\mathcal{A}
-modules, by
\mathcal{A}\operatorname{-\,Mod}
(resp.,
\mathcal{A}{\operatorname{\,-\,mod}}
) the full subcategory formed by all locally-finite dimensional (resp., finite dimensional) left
\mathcal{A}
-modules. Denote by
\mathsf{proj}\,\mathcal{A}
the subcategory of
\mathcal{A}{\operatorname{\,-\,mod}}
formed by projective
\mathcal{A}
-modules. Given a vertex
x\in Q_{0}
,

denotes the indecomposable projective module in
\mathsf{proj}\,\mathcal{A}
at vertex
x
.

Let

be a Galois covering of bound quivers, with
G
as a group of automorphisms of
(\Gamma,J)
. If
\mathcal{B}\coloneqq\mathbb{K}\Gamma/J
and
\mathcal{A}\coloneqq\mathbb{K}Q/I
, then

induces naturally a covering functor between

-linear categories

, and following

(1)

The

, given by

, for any

, and

(2)

the

, the left adjoint to

defined by the conditions:

\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}P_{\hat{x}}=P_{x}
, for any vertices

with

.

Given

in the following way: For each object

, we set

\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}M(x)=\bigoplus_{\hat{x}/x}M(\hat{x})

where
\hat{x}
ranges over all objects of
\mathcal{B}
such that
\mathcal{F}\hat{x}=x
. For an arrow

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces y}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha}

in

the family
\sum\limits_{\hat{x}}M(\mathchoice{\mathop{}\kern 6.05557pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\widehat{\alpha}}}_{\mathmakebox[0pt][r]{\hat{x}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 6.05557pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\widehat{\alpha}}}_{\mathmakebox[0pt][r]{\hat{x}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 6.05557pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\widehat{\alpha}}}_{\mathmakebox[0pt][r]{\hat{x}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 6.05557pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\widehat{\alpha}}}_{\mathmakebox[0pt][r]{\hat{x}}}}\widehat{\alpha}_{\hat{y}})(v_{\hat{x}})\in\bigoplus\limits_{\hat{y}/y}M(\hat{y})

\mathchoice{\mathop{}\kern 6.05557pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\widehat{\alpha}}}_{\mathmakebox[0pt][r]{\hat{x}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 6.05557pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\widehat{\alpha}}}_{\mathmakebox[0pt][r]{\hat{x}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 6.05557pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\widehat{\alpha}}}_{\mathmakebox[0pt][r]{\hat{x}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 6.05557pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\widehat{\alpha}}}_{\mathmakebox[0pt][r]{\hat{x}}}}\widehat{\alpha}_{\hat{y}}

Remark 2.1
.

Note that the pull-up functor
\F_{\bullet}
restricts to locally finite-dimensional modules, and the push-down functor
\F_{\lambda}
restricts to finite-dimensional modules.

We will denote by
\mathcal{B}\ind
the full subcategory of
\mathcal{B}{\operatorname{\,-\,mod}}
formed by choosing a representative of each isomorphism class of finite-dimensional indecomposable
\mathcal{B}
-modules. We will choose these representatives in such a way so that if

, then

, for each
g\in G
. Similarly, we let
\mathcal{A}\ind
denote the full subcategory of
\mathcal{A}{\operatorname{\,-\,mod}}
formed by choosing a representative of each isomorphism class of finite-dimensional indecomposable
\mathcal{A}
-modules.

(1)

\F_{\lambda}
and
\F_{\bullet}
are exact functors and send indecomposable projective modules to indecomposable projective modules

(2)

If the push-down functor

\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}:\mathcal{B}\ind/G\hookrightarrow\mathcal{A}\ind
.

(3)

(4)

\F_{\bullet}\F_{\lambda}\cong\bigoplus_{g\in G}\mathrm{Id}_{\,\mathcal{B}\MOD}^{\,g}

(5)

, then

.

(6)

If

, then

, for some

.

2.4.
Basic notions of
\tau
-tilting theory

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a finite dimensional
\mathbb{K}
-algebra. For
M\in\mathcal{A}{\operatorname{\,-\,mod}}
, we will use
|M\rvert
to denote the number of non-isomorphic indecomposable direct summands of

. We denote the Auslander-Reiten translate of
\mathcal{A}{\operatorname{\,-\,mod}}
by

.

We recall some basic definitions and some important properties of support
\tau_{\mathcal{A}}
-tilting modules from
[
AIR14
]
.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
M\in\mathcal{A}{\operatorname{\,-\,mod}}
. Then

is said to be

(1)

\tau_{\mathcal{A}}
-
\emph{rigid}
if
\Hom_{\mathcal{A}}(M,\tau_{\mathcal{A}}M)=0
.

(2)

\tau_{\mathcal{A}}
-
\emph{tilting}
(resp.,
\emph{almostcomplete}\tau_{\mathcal{A}}
-
\emph{tilting}
module) if
M
is
\tau_{\mathcal{A}}
-rigid and
\lvert M\rvert=\lvert\mathcal{A}\rvert
(resp.,
\lvert M\rvert=\lvert\mathcal{A}\rvert-1
).

(3)

\emph{support}\tau_{\mathcal{A}}
-
\emph{tilting}
if there exists an idempotent
e
of
\mathcal{A}
such that
M
is a
\tau_{\mathcal{A}}
-tilting
\mathcal{A}/\langle e\rangle
-module.

We can view them as certain pairs of
\mathcal{A}
-modules.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
(M,P)
be a pair with
M\in\mathcal{A}{\operatorname{\,-\,mod}}
and
P\in\mathsf{proj}\,\mathcal{A}
. The pair
(M,P)
is called

(1)

a
\emph{$\tau_{\mathcal{A}}$-rigidpair}
if
M
is
\tau_{\mathcal{A}}
-rigid and

.

(2)

a

\tau_{\mathcal{A}}
-tilting pair
(resp.,

\tau_{\mathcal{A}}
-tilting pair
) if
(M,P)
is
\tau_{\mathcal{A}}
-rigid and
\lvert M\rvert+\lvert P\rvert=\lvert\mathcal{A}\rvert
(resp.,
\lvert M\rvert+\lvert P\rvert=\lvert\mathcal{A}\rvert-1
).

Definition 2.7
.

Let
M,M^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}{\operatorname{\,-\,mod}}
and
P,P^{\prime}\in\mathsf{proj}\,\mathcal{A}
.

(1)

A module
X\in\mathcal{A}{\operatorname{\,-\,mod}}
is called
\emph{basic}
if every indecomposable direct summand of
X
occurs only once in the direct sum decomposition of
X
into indecomposables.

(2)

We say that
(M,P)
is
\emph{basic}
if
M
and
P
are basic.

(3)

We say that
(M,P)
is a direct summand of
(M^{\prime},P^{\prime})
if

is a direct summand of
M^{\prime}
and
P
is a direct summand of
P^{\prime}
.

(4)

We say that
(M,P)
is isomorphic to
(M^{\prime},P^{\prime})
if
M
is isomorphic to
M^{\prime}
and
P
is isomorphic to
P^{\prime}
.

Remark 2.2
.

Given a support
\tau_{\mathcal{A}}
-tilting pair
(M,P)
with

basic,

determines

uniquely up to isomorphism, see

, Proposition 2.3]
.

We will use

to denote the set of all basic support
\tau_{\mathcal{A}}
-tilting pairs (up to isomorphism). We have the following analog of Bongartz completion for tilting modules.

Theorem 2.3

(see Theorem 2.10
[
AIR14
]
)
.

Any
\tau_{\mathcal{A}}
-rigid
\mathcal{A}
-module is a direct summand of some
\tau_{\mathcal{A}}
-tilting
\mathcal{A}
-module.

It turns out that support
\tau_{\mathcal{A}}
-tilting modules (pairs) have exactly two complements. This leads to the notion of mutations.

Theorem 2.4

(see Theorem 2.18
[
AIR14
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a finite dimensional
\mathbb{K}
-algebra. Then any basic almost complete support
\tau_{\mathcal{A}}
-tilting pair for

\tau_{\mathcal{A}}
-tilting pairs.

These two support
\tau_{\mathcal{A}}
-tilting pairs are said to be mutations of each other.

.

Let
(M,P)
and

be two non-isomorphic basic support

and

.

; or

.

Let

We will use
\mathcal{A}
to denote a finite-dimensional, connected, and basic

\mathcal{C}_{2}

4.

The proposition below shows that the above notion of

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
M,N\in\mathcal{B}{\operatorname{\,-\,mod}}
. Then

\Hom_{\mathcal{A}}(\F_{\lambda}M\,,\,\tau_{\mathcal{A}}\F_{\lambda}N)=0\;\text{if and only if}\;\Hom_{\mathcal{B}}(M\,,\,\tau_{\mathcal{B}}N^{g})=0,\;\text{for every}\;g\in G.

Proof.

By
[
Gab81
, Theorem, Section 3.6, page 89]
, we know that the Auslander-Reiten translate commutes with push-down functor. Hence, we get

\Hom_{\mathcal{A}}(\F_{\lambda}M\,,\,\tau_{\mathcal{A}}\F_{\lambda}N)\cong\Hom_{\mathcal{A}}(\F_{\lambda}M\,,\,\F_{\lambda}\tau_{\mathcal{B}}N)

Since
(\F_{\lambda},\F_{\bullet})
is an adjoint pair, we get,

\Hom_{\mathcal{A}}(\F_{\lambda}M\,,\,\F_{\lambda}\tau_{\mathcal{B}}N)\cong\Hom_{\mathcal{B}}(M\,,\,\F_{\bullet}\F_{\lambda}\tau_{\mathcal{B}}N)\cong\Hom_{\mathcal{B}}(M\,,\,\oplus_{g\in G}\,(\tau_{\mathcal{B}}N)^{g})

Since
M
is a finite dimensional
\mathcal{B}
-module and hence, a finitely generated
\mathcal{B}
-module, we have

\Hom_{\mathcal{B}}(M\,,\,\oplus_{g\in G}(\tau_{\mathcal{B}}N)^{g})\cong\bigoplus_{g\in G}\Hom_{\mathcal{B}}(M\,,\,(\tau_{\mathcal{B}}N)^{g})

which is, indeed, isomorphic to
\bigoplus_{g\in G}\Hom_{\mathcal{B}}(M\,,\,\tau_{\mathcal{B}}N^{g})
.

Hence, we get

\Hom_{\mathcal{A}}(\F_{\lambda}M\,,\,\tau_{\mathcal{A}}\F_{\lambda}N)\cong\bigoplus_{g\in G}\Hom_{\mathcal{B}}(M\,,\,\tau_{\mathcal{B}}N^{g})

which gives the desired result.
∎

S
-length. We let
G_{i}
be the normal subgroup of
G_{i-1}
generated by
S_{i}^{\prime}
. We note that
S_{i}^{\prime}
generates
G_{i}
as a normal subgroup, but is not, in general, a set of free generators for
G_{i}
. We have the following.

Lemma 5.5
.

A set
S_{i}
of free generators for
G_{i}
is given by
S_{i}=\{a_{i}^{j}xa_{i}^{-j}\mid x\in S_{i-1}\setminus\{a_{i}\},j\geq 0\}
.

Proof.

First, by normality of
G_{i}
in
G_{i-1}
, it is clear that
S_{i}\subset G_{i}
. We also note that
S_{i-1}\setminus\{a_{i}\}\subset S_{i}
. To prove that
S_{i}
is a set of generators for
G_{i}
, it suffices to prove that conjugating any
S_{i}
-word by a power of
a_{i}
gives again an
S_{i}
-word. This follows from the fact that

a_{i}^{j}\left(\prod_{k=1}^{r}a_{i}^{r_{k}}x_{k}a_{i}^{-r_{k}}\right)a_{i}^{-j}=\prod_{k=1}^{r}a_{i}^{r_{k}+j}x_{k}a_{i}^{-r_{k}-j}.

Now, it remains to show that the set
S_{i}
is a free generating set. Assume otherwise. Then there is a non-trivial
S_{i}
-word

\prod_{k=1}^{s}a_{i}^{r_{k}}x_{k}a_{i}^{-r_{k}}
that is equal to
1
. Now, each
x_{k}
is a word in
S_{i-1}\setminus\{a_{i}\}
. Hence, we can see the above expression as a word in
S_{i-1}
in
G_{i-1}
. We therefore need
-r_{k}=r_{k+1}
for
k=1,\ldots,s-1
together with
r_{1}=r_{s}=0
. This forces the expression to be a non-trivial word in
S_{i-1}
, hence it cannot be
1
.
∎

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
G
be a free group on a finite set
S
and let
G_{i}
be as above. Then

(1)

For each
i
, we have that
G_{i-1}/G_{i}
is infinite cyclic.

(2)

For each positive integer
r
, there is a positive integer
m_{r}
such that the free generating set
S_{m_{r}}
of
G_{m_{r}}
does not contain any element of
S
-length smaller than
r
.

Let
p:(\Gamma,J)\rightarrow(Q,I)
be a Galois
G
-covering where
G
is finitely generated free. We choose a connected fundamental domain
F_{0}
of
\mathcal{A}
by
G
in
\Gamma
. Let us fix a vertex
x
of
Q
. We choose a lift
\widehat{x}
of
x
in
\Gamma
with the property that
\widehat{x}\in F_{0}
. For every
i\geq 1
, we define
\mathcal{A}_{i}
to be the orbit category of
\mathcal{B}
by
G_{i}
. We let
(\Omega_{i},I_{i})
be a bound quiver for
\mathcal{A}_{i}
, so that
\mathcal{A}_{i}\cong\mathbb{K}\Omega_{i}/I_{i}
. Hence, we get a Galois
G_{i}
-covering
p_{i}:(\Gamma,J)\rightarrow(\Omega_{i},I_{i})
, and we denote the associated covering functor and the push-down functor by
\prescript{}{i}{\mathcal{F}}
and
\prescript{}{i}{\mathcal{F}}_{\lambda}
, respectively. Moreover, for each
i\geq 1
, we also have a Galois
G/G_{i}
-covering
p^{i}:(\Omega_{i},I_{i})\rightarrow(Q,I)
, and we denote the associated covering functor and the push-down functor by
\F^{\,i}
and
\F_{\lambda}^{\,i}
, respectively.

Now, we construct a fundamental domain
F_{i}
of
\mathcal{A}_{i}
in
\Gamma
as follows. Let us start with
F_{1}
. We consider the infinite cyclic group
G/G_{1}
and we note that a complete set of coset representatives of
G/G_{1}
is given by the powers of
a_{1}
. Hence, we see that a fundamental domain of
\mathcal{A}_{1}
in
\Gamma
is given by
\{a_{1}^{j}v\mid j\in\mathbb{Z},v\in F_{0}\}
. Now, suppose that
i\geq 1
and that
F_{i}
has been defined. We note that the powers of
a_{i+1}
forms a complete set of representatives of the cosets of
G_{i}/G_{i+1}
. Hence, we see that a fundamental domain of
\mathcal{A}_{i+1}
in
\Gamma
is given by
\{a_{i+1}^{j}v\mid j\in\mathbb{Z},v\in F_{i}\}
. By construction, we see that we have a chain

F_{0}\subset F_{1}\subset F_{2}\subset\cdots

and all
F_{i}
are connected. We can write

F_{i}=\{a_{i}^{r_{i}}\cdots a_{2}^{r_{2}}a_{1}^{r_{1}}v\mid v\in F_{0},r_{1},\ldots,r_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}\}.

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
g\in G
. Then there is a non-negative integer
s
and integers
r_{1},r_{2},\ldots,r_{s}
such that

g=a_{s}^{r_{s}}\cdots a_{2}^{r_{2}}a_{1}^{r_{1}}.

Proof.

Note that there is
g_{1}\in G_{1}
such that
g=g_{1}a_{1}^{r_{1}}
. By induction, for
i\geq 1
, there is
g_{i}\in G_{i}
and integers
r_{1},r_{2},\ldots,r_{i}
such that
g=g_{i}a_{i}^{r_{i}}\cdots a_{2}^{r_{2}}a_{1}^{r_{1}}.
Let
t_{i}
be the
S_{i}
-length of
g_{i}\in G_{i}
. We note that if no power of
a_{i+1}
appears in the
S_{i}
-expression for
g_{i}
, then
t_{i+1}=t_{i}
. Otherwise, we can write

g_{i}=xa_{i+1}^{p}y

where
p\neq 0
,
y
has no power of
a_{i+1}
in it and
x
does not end with a non-zero power of
a_{i+1}
. Let
y=y_{1}y_{2}\cdots y_{q}
be written as a word in
S_{i}
. Then

a_{i+1}^{p}y=\left(\prod_{k=1}^{q}a_{i+1}^{p}y_{k}a_{i+1}^{-p}\right)a_{i+1}^{p}

where the product is a word in
S_{i+1}
. Repeating this for all powers of
a_{i+1}
appearing in the
S_{i}
expression for
g_{i}
, we get

g=g_{i+1}a_{i+1}^{u}a_{i}^{r_{i}}\cdots a_{2}^{r_{2}}a_{1}^{r_{1}}

for some integer
u
and
t_{i+1}<t_{i}
. The result now follows.
∎

Lemma 5.8
.

We have
\bigcup_{k\geq 0}F_{i}=\Gamma
.

Proof.

Let
y\in\Gamma_{0}
. Then there is
g\in G
and
v\in F_{0}
such that
gv=y
. Now, it follows from Lemma
5.7
that there exists
s
and integers
r_{1},r_{2},\ldots,r_{s}
such that
g=a_{s}^{r_{s}}\cdots a_{2}^{r_{2}}a_{1}^{r_{1}}
. Hence,
y\in F_{s}
.
∎

Lemma 5.9
.

Let
r
be a positive integer. Using the above notation, let
i
be such that the fundamental domain
F_{i}
of
\mathcal{A}_{i}
contains all the endpoints of walks in
\Gamma
of length at most
r
which start at
\widehat{x}
. Suppose
\prescript{}{i}{\mathcal{F}}\,\widehat{x}=\bar{x}
. If
M\in\mathcal{A}_{i}{\operatorname{\,-\,mod}}
is supported at
\bar{x}
and of dimension at most
r
, then there exists
N\in\mathcal{B}{\operatorname{\,-\,mod}}
such that
\prescript{}{i}{\mathcal{F}}_{\lambda}N\cong M
.

Proof.

Let
z\in\Gamma_{0}\cup\Gamma_{1}
. We define a module
N\in\mathcal{B}{\operatorname{\,-\,mod}}
in the following way: if
z\in F_{i}
, then
N(z)\coloneqq M(\prescript{}{i}{\mathcal{F}}z)
; otherwise
N(z)\coloneqq 0
. Since
M\in\mathcal{A}_{i}{\operatorname{\,-\,mod}}
is of dimension at most
r
supported at
\bar{x}
, and
F_{i}
contains all the endpoints of walks in
\Gamma
of length at most
r
which start at
\widehat{x}
, it follows from the definition of the push-down functor
\prescript{}{i}{\mathcal{F}}_{\lambda}
that
\prescript{}{i}{\mathcal{F}}_{\lambda}N\cong M
.
∎

Theorem 5.10
.

Let
p:(\Gamma,J)\rightarrow(Q,I)
be a Galois
G
-covering where
G
is finitely generated free. If
M\in\mathcal{A}{\operatorname{\,-\,mod}}
is a rigid
\mathcal{A}
-module, then there exists a
N\in\mathcal{B}{\operatorname{\,-\,mod}}
such that
\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}N\cong M
.

Proof.

Using the above notations, we show that, for any
i\geq 1
, the module
M
lies in the essential image of the push-down functor
\mathcal{F}^{\,i}_{\lambda}
. We proceed by induction on
i
.

For
i=1
, this follows from Proposition
5.4
using that the Galois group
G/G_{1}
is infinite cyclic. We let
M_{1}
in
\mathcal{A}_{1}{\operatorname{\,-\,mod}}
be such that
\mathcal{F}^{\,1}_{\lambda}M_{1}\cong M
. We note that
M_{1}
has the same (total) dimension as
M
. Using exactness of
\mathcal{F}^{\,1}_{\lambda}
and Corollary
4.9
, we see that the image under
\mathcal{F}^{\,1}_{\lambda}
of a non-split self-extension is non-split. This shows that
M_{1}
is rigid. By induction, we assume that we have constructed a finite dimensional rigid module
M_{i}
over
\mathcal{A}_{i}
such that
\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}^{\,i}M_{i}\cong M
. Again, we note that the dimension of
M_{i}
is the same as the dimension of
M
. We consider the Galois
G_{i}/G_{i+1}
-covering
q_{i}:\mathcal{A}_{i+1}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}_{i}
, where
G_{i}/G_{i+1}
is infinite cyclic. We let
\prescript{}{i}{\mathcal{F}}^{\,i+1}_{\lambda}:\mathcal{A}_{i+1}{\operatorname{\,-\,mod}}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}_{i}{\operatorname{\,-\,mod}}
be the corresponding push-down functor. Again, by Proposition
5.4
, it follows that there exists a finite dimensional module
M_{i+1}
over
\mathcal{A}_{i+1}
such that
\prescript{}{i}{\mathcal{F}}^{\,i+1}_{\lambda}M_{i+1}\cong M_{i}
. Hence,
\mathcal{F}^{\,i+1}_{\lambda}M_{i+1}=\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}^{\,i}\,\prescript{}{i}{\mathcal{F}}^{\,i+1}_{\lambda}M_{i+1}\cong\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}^{\,i}M_{i}\cong M
.

Now, assume that
M
has dimension
r
and is supported at vertex
x
in
\mathcal{A}
. Let
\widehat{x}
be a lift of
x
in
\Gamma
. Using Lemmas
5.9
, we let
i_{r}
be a positive integer such that the fundamental domain
F_{i_{r}}
contains all endpoints of walks of length at most
r
starting at
\widehat{x}
in
\Gamma
. We let
\prescript{}{i_{r}}{\mathcal{F}}\,\widehat{x}=\bar{x}
. The module
M_{i_{r}}
constructed above is supported at
\bar{x}
and has dimension
r
. By Lemma
5.9
, we conclude there exists a
N\in\mathcal{B}{\operatorname{\,-\,mod}}
such that
\prescript{}{i_{r}}{\mathcal{F}}_{\lambda}N\cong M_{i_{r}}
. Therefore, we have
\F_{\lambda}N=\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}^{\,i_{r}}\prescript{}{i_{r}}{\mathcal{F}}_{\lambda}N\cong\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}^{\,i_{r}}M_{i_{r}}\cong M
.
∎

Corollary 5.11
.

Let
p:(\Gamma,J)\rightarrow(Q,I)
be a Galois
G
-covering where
G
is finitely generated free. If
M\in\mathcal{A}{\operatorname{\,-\,mod}}
is a rigid
\mathcal{A}
-module, then
M
is
G
-gradable.

Corollary 5.12
.

If
\mathcal{A}
is a monomial algebra and
M
is a rigid
\mathcal{A}
-module, then
M
lies in the essential image of any push-down functor.

Proof.

We consider the bound quiver
(Q,I)
for
\mathcal{A}
where
I
is monomial, and we let
\pi:(\widetilde{Q},\widetilde{I})\rightarrow(Q,I)
be the universal covering. Fix a vertex
x_{0}\in Q_{0}
. Since
I
is monomial, the Galois group is
\pi_{1}(Q,I)=\pi_{1}(Q,x_{0})
and is finitely generated free. Hence,
M
is in the essential image of the corresponding push down functor
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{\lambda}
. By the universal property of the universal covering, the result follows.
∎

Remark 5.13
.

If
\mathcal{A}
is a string algebra, then it follows from the definition of the push-down functor that any string module lies in
\mathcal{A}{\operatorname{\,-\,mod}}_{1}
, and hence is
G
-gradable. However, in general, not every string module is rigid.

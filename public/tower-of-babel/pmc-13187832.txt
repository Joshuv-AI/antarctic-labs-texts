Systemic Embolic Events in People Who Inject Drugs With Patent Foramen Ovale and Right-Sided Infective Endocarditis: A Systematic Review
Authors: Guillermo Cuervo, Supavit Chesdachai, Danielle Gerberi, Christopher V DeSimone, Abhishek J Deshmukh, José M Miró, Abdallah El Sabbagh, Daniel C DeSimone, Larry M Baddour
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundKnowing the prevalence of patent foramen ovale (PFO) and the increasing number of people who inject drugs (PWID) with right-sided infective endocarditis (RSIE), we aimed to better characterize the syndrome of injection drug use, RSIE, and PFO with systemic embolic events.MethodA systematic search across multiple databases was independently screened using predefined criteria (from inception until September 2025). Published cases of adult PWID diagnosed with RSIE and ischemic stroke or other systemic embolic event were included, provided they had PFO assessment. Individuals were excluded if they had cardiac implantable electronic device (CIED) infections or any other conditions associated with systemic or paradoxical embolism.ResultsThe review identified 13 studies with 14 young patients, mostly without comorbidities, predominantly infected with Staphylococcus aureus. In most cases, echocardiography revealed large tricuspid valve vegetations. Neuroimaging confirmed multiple ischemic infarcts, while 5 patients showed emboli outside the central nervous system (4 of them as the only systemic embolism). At least 11 (79%) patients had echocardiographic evidence of right-to-left shunting. Management included cardiac surgery in 6 cases that involved valve replacement or repair, percutaneous PFO closure in 2 cases, and percutaneous mechanical aspiration in 1 case. Three patients (21%) died during hospitalization, and only 27.3% patients had outpatient follow-up.ConclusionsRSIE with systemic embolism in PWID with PFO has rarely been reported, affects younger patients without sex predominance, and was usually due to S. aureus with large tricuspid vegetations. Right-to-left shunting was characteristic, and outcomes were poor despite combined medical, surgical, and interventional therapies.

The prevalence of people who inject drugs (PWID) has increased sharply over the past 3 decades largely due to the opioid epidemic [1–3]. In parallel to that trend, there has been a substantial increase in serious infectious complications, including infective endocarditis (IE), which is a leading cause of cardiac surgery in some medical centers in the United States [4]. Right-sided IE (RSIE) is the most common IE presentation in PWID and is increasing. Embolic complications of RSIE are frequently seen and include septic pulmonary embolism. Systemic embolization, in contrast, has been an infrequent event [5].

Approximately 20%–25% of the overall adult population has a persistent patent foramen ovale (PFO) with no sex predilection [6, 7]. Considering the frequency of this fetal remnant, a subset of patients with RSIE may develop “paradoxical” systemic emboli when a right-to-left shunt is present [8]. Therefore, a systematic review was conducted to better profile PWID who have RSIE and PFO and develop systemic embolic events.

METHODS

This is a systematic review of published cases/studies of adult PWID, who were diagnosed with RSIE and who experienced ischemic stroke or other systemic embolic events, provided they had an echocardiographic evaluation specifically assessing for a PFO. Studies published in languages other than English were excluded. Individuals were excluded if they had cardiac implantable electronic device (CIED) infections. We also excluded other conditions associated with both paradoxical and systemic embolism, including concurrent left-sided IE, mechanical valves or atrial fibrillation/flutter, or other congenital heart diagnoses or valve procedures. Key definitions include (i) definite IE according to the modified Duke criteria [9]; (ii) injection drug use, considered as the self-administration of nonprescribed substances delivered by a needle; (iii) PFO as a persistent fetal interatrial communication that may be associated with right-to-left, left-to-right, or bilateral shunting; and (iv) systemic embolism; ischemic stroke was defined by acute arterial occlusion and infarction-related neurologic dysfunction.

Systematic Review

The medical literature was searched by an expert medical librarian (D. G.) for the concepts of endocarditis, PFO, and injection drug use. Search strategies were created using a combination of keywords and standardized index terms. Searches were done on 18 September 2025 in Ovid Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials (1991+), Ovid Embase (1974+, including records from ClinicalTrials.gov), Ovid Medline (1946+ including epub ahead of print, in-process, and other nonindexed citations), Scopus (1788+), Web of Science Core Collection (Science Citation Index Expanded 1975+ and Emerging Sources Citation Index 2015+), and Google Scholar via Harzing's Publish or Perish. Full search strategies are provided in Appendix 1 of the Supplementary material. COVIDence systematic review software (Veritas Health Innovation, Melbourne, Australia; available at www.COVIDence.org) was used to systematically manage the study selection process. Two independent reviewers (G. C., L. M. B.) evaluated titles and abstracts, as well as full texts, according to predefined inclusion and exclusion criteria. Discrepancies were resolved through consensus. Descriptive statistics were used and reported as median for continuous variables and count (percentage) for categorical variables.

RESULTS

Systematic Review

The output of the systematic review is summarized in the flowchart in Figure 1. Briefly, 542 references were identified, 51 full texts were reviewed, and finally, 13 studies with 14 cases that met the inclusion criteria were included [8, 10–21]. All except 2 articles were single-case reports. The quality assessment of included articles was carried out using the Joanna Briggs Institute Critical Appraisal Checklist for Case Reports [22] and is summarized in Appendix 2 of the Supplementary material.

Baseline Demographics

Pooled analysis of the 14 patients included in this analysis showed a median age of 35 years old, with no difference in sex distribution (Table 1). Comorbidities were infrequent; except for 1 smoker, no patient had additional cardiovascular risk factors. No patients had another congenital heart defect. Two patients had hepatitis C virus infection, and 4 patients had previous bouts of IE, mainly RSIE. No cases with human immunodeficiency virus infection were described.

Clinical and Echocardiographic Characteristics

Tables 1 and 2 summarize the microbiology, clinical characteristics, and imaging findings. The predominant pathogen was Staphylococcus aureus (n = 11 [79%]), with the majority being methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus (MRSA) infections (n = 6 [43%]). Clinical signs of sepsis were present in 89% of patients with available information. In all cases, transthoracic echocardiography (TTE) identified vegetations on the tricuspid valve, generally large (median 21 mm; range: >10–35 mm) and frequently associated with severe tricuspid regurgitation (n = 12 [86%]). Signs of right-sided chamber overload were reported in 5 cases. Transesophageal echocardiography (TEE) was confirmatory, as well as useful in demonstrating a PFO, with right-to-left shunt specifically described in 11 cases (79%). A positive agitated saline test was explicitly reported in 6 patients. Pulmonary septic emboli were present in all patients with available information. Regarding the presence of systemic emboli, neuroimaging detected ischemic infarcts in various locations (n = 10 [71%]), often multiple and in 1 case with hemorrhagic transformation. Initial neurological severity was poorly reflected in most articles: According to the few available National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS) scores, it ranged from absence of symptoms to moderate deficits. Five patients presented with emboli outside the central nervous system (4 of them as the only systemic embolic event). Specifically, splenic emboli were detected in 4 cases, renal emboli in 3, and fundus involvement—in the form of Roth spots—and acute arterial embolism of the left foot in 1 patient each.

Treatment and Outcomes

Table 3 shows the treatments provided and clinical results. No patients were treated with thrombolysis or endovascular mechanical thrombectomy for ischemic stroke. All patients received targeted antibiotic treatment. Six (40%) patients underwent cardiac surgery consisting of either PFO closure plus tricuspid valve replacement (4 cases) or debulking and repair of the valve (2 cases). Two patients underwent percutaneous closure of a PFO. One case underwent vegetation debulking using percutaneous mechanical aspiration. Three (21%) patients died during hospitalization, and these deaths were secondary to infection. Follow-up information was available for only 3 (27.3%) of the remaining patients, generally for short periods (up to 3 months) and with limited information on neurological status. The 3 patients remained alive.

DISCUSSION

To our knowledge, this is the first systematic review to address the unique syndrome of RSIE, PWID, PFO, and systemic embolic events. Although the undefined denominator makes it difficult to precisely estimate the magnitude of the risk for this complication, the low number observed suggests that it is an exceptional occurrence, a finding consistent with the low frequency of strokes described among PWID with RSIE in the International Collaboration on Endocarditis cohort (2.2%) [5]. Despite the younger age of patients with few comorbid conditions, cardiac surgery and in-hospital mortality were frequent. This was likely due, in part, to the prevalence of RSIE due to S. aureus and its aggressive nature. Its association with sepsis (89% of the current cohort where data were available) [23] and embolic events [24] is well recognized.

Percutaneous mechanical aspiration (PMA), a novel nonsurgical intervention that is increasingly being used in select patients with RSIE, was done in 1 of the 14 patients identified in the current systematic review. In a recently published large, multicenter registry of PMA use [25], 51% of patients were PWID with stroke as an outcome in 3.1% of patients who underwent PMA. Overall, PFO was identified in 11.3% of patients, and likely for this reason, cerebral embolic protection devices were used during the procedure in up to 5.6% of patients. Interestingly, there are published reports of transient PFO closure performed during percutaneous mechanical aspiration in these patients, with the aim of reducing the risk of paradoxical embolism [26].

Our findings suggest that while a PFO is a necessary anatomical condition for paradoxical embolism, it is not sufficient, requiring the concurrence of a significant increase in right-sided chamber pressures (probably due to repeated pulmonary emboli) for this catastrophic complication to occur. It is worth noting that the patients included in this study showed evidence of elevated pressures in the right heart chambers, with severe tricuspid regurgitation in 12 (85.7%) of them. In addition, 11 patients had right-to-left shunt demonstrated by echocardiography, with a contrast study using agitated saline explicitly mentioned in 6 of the studies.

From a diagnostic standpoint, the high sensitivity of TTE in the cohort described in our systematic review was noteworthy, with TEE serving as a confirmatory test. The high sensitivity of TTE is likely to be due to selection bias in the included cases, which featured large vegetations and high-flow right-to-left shunts. Transesophageal echocardiography with a bubble study remains the gold standard for diagnosing PFO [6]. Regarding the therapeutic approach, although some patients suffered large vessel strokes, none were treated with mechanical thrombectomy of the cerebral vessels, a procedure that seems to be also safe and effective in patients with IE provided that pharmacological thrombolysis is avoided [27]. It is worth noting that the patients included in our study underwent cardiac surgery at a slightly higher rate, yet they had an in-hospital mortality rate that was almost double that described in the largest cohort of endocarditis in PWID published to date [5]. This is also likely due to selection bias.

Finally, cases analyzed in this systematic review highlight the difficulties in the follow-up of PWID, with low adherence to treatment, high rate of addiction relapse, and recurrent endocarditis (4 patients had a history of previous episodes). Certainly, the need for a comprehensive approach to addiction cannot be overstated in this group of patients [3, 28].

This work has several limitations that must be acknowledged. First, as this is a systematic review primarily of case reports, there is an inherent risk of bias due to selective reporting. Moreover, in the absence of data regarding the patient denominator, it is impossible to accurately estimate the magnitude of the risk of this complication in PWID with PFO. Furthermore, the included manuscripts present a heterogeneous presentation of cases, with frequent limitations in characterizing the clinical condition, describing interventions, and documenting clinical evolution and follow-up after hospital discharge. Despite these limitations, this work offers a comprehensive review of published cases of this apparently rare syndrome.

In summary, RSIE with systemic embolism in PWID is rarely reported, affects young patients without sex predominance, and is commonly due to S. aureus with large tricuspid valve vegetations and right-to-left shunting. Use of percutaneous vegetation debulking and cerebral thrombectomy techniques was infrequent. Despite combined medical and interventional management, survival remains lower than other cohorts of RSIE in PWID.

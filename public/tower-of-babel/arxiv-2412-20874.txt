1.
Introduction

Graphs considered in this paper may have loops and multiple edges. For terminology and notations not defined here, we follow
[
3
,
19
]
.
Contracting an edge
means deleting the edge and then identifying its ends. For a subgraph
H
of a graph
G
, let
G/H
be the graph obtained from
G
by contracting all edges of
H
.

A
circuit
is a
2
-regular connected graph, and a
cycle
is a graph such that the degree of each vertex is even. The
length
of a cycle or a circuit is the number of its edges. A collection
\mathcal{C}
of cycles of a graph
G

covers

G
if each edge of
G
is contained in at least one cycle in
\mathcal{C}
. The
length
of a cycle cover
\mathcal{C}
, denoted by
\ell(\mathcal{C})
, is the sum of the lengths of its cycles. The length of the shortest cycle cover of
G
is denoted by
cc(G)
.

Alon and Tarsi
[
1
]
conjectured that
cc(G)\leq\frac{7}{5}m
for every bridgeless graph
G
with
m
edges.
Jamshy and Tarsi
[
10
]
proved that Alon and Tarsi’s conjecture implies the well-known cycle double cover conjecture of Seymour
[
14
]
and Szekeres
[
16
]
,
that every bridgeless graph has a collection of cycles that covers each edge exactly twice.

Alon and Tarsi
[
1
]
and Bermond, Jackson, and Jaeger
[
2
]
independently proved that
cc(G)\leq\frac{5}{3}m
for every bridgeless graph
G
with
m
edges.
This upper bound on
cc(G)
remained the best known upper bound for 40 years.
For special families of graphs, there are some better upper bounds proved in the literature.
Jamshy, Raspaud,
and Tarsi
[
9
]
proved that
if
G
admits a nowhere-zero 5-flow, then
cc(G)\leq\frac{8}{5}m
. Fan and Raspaud
[
6
]
proved that if the Fulkerson Conjecture is true, then
cc(G)\leq\frac{22}{15}m
. Kaiser, Král, Lidický, Nejedlý and Šámal
[
11
]
proved that if
G
has minimum degree at least 3 and is loopless, then
cc(G)\leq\frac{44}{27}m
. Fan
[
5
]
proved that if
G
has minimum degree at least 3, then
cc(G)<\frac{218}{135}m
in the loopless case and
cc(G)<\frac{278}{171}m
if loops are allowed.
Kompišová and Lukot’ka
[
13
]
proved that
cc(G)<\frac{278}{171}m+\frac{2}{27}n_{2}
for an arbitrary bridgeless graph
G
, where
n_{2}
is the number of degree 2 vertices in
G
.

This paper proves that
cc(G)<\frac{29}{18}m+\frac{1}{18}n_{2}
.
As a consequence, we show that
cc(G)\leq\frac{5}{3}m-\frac{1}{42}\log m
, which improves the general upper bound for
cc(G)
by a fraction of
\log m
.

Let
G
be a graph and
\Gamma
be an (additive) abelian group. For an orientation
D
of
E(G)
, denote
E^{+}(v)
(resp.,
E^{-}(v)
) by the set of all oriented edges of
D(G)
with their tails (resp., heads) at vertex
v\in V(G)
. For a function
f:E(G)\to\Gamma
and
v\in V(G)
, let
f^{+}(v)=\sum_{e\in E^{+}(v)}f(e)
and
f^{-}(v)=\sum_{e\in E^{-}(v)}f(e)
. A
\Gamma
-flow
in
G
is an ordered pair
(D,f)
such that
f^{+}(v)-f^{-}(v)=0
for every vertex
v\in V(G)
.
For a subgraph
H
of
G
and
a\in\Gamma
, define
E_{f=a}(H)=\left\{e\in E(H):f(e)=a\right\}
.
The
support
of
f
is the set of all edges of
G
with
f(e)\neq 0
and is denoted by
supp(f)
. A flow
(D,f)
is called
nowhere-zero
if
supp(f)=E(G)
.

We shall consider
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-flows in this paper.
As
-\alpha=\alpha
for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, the orientation of
G
is irrelevant and can be omitted. To be precise, a
Z_{2}\times Z_{2}
-flow on
G
is a function
f:E(G)\to Z_{2}\times Z_{2}
such that for each vertex
v
,
\sum_{e\in E(v)}f(e)=0
, where
E(v)
is the set of edges of
G
incident to
v
.

2.
A key Lemma

A maximal path
P
in a graph
H
with all interior vertices of degree
2
(in
H
) is called a
thread
in
H
.
This section proves the following lemma.

Lemma 2.1
.

Assume
G
is a graph and
C
is a circuit in
G
.
Assume each edge in
C
is contained in a thread of
G
of length at least
q
.
If
G
admits a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-flow
f
, then
G
admits a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-flow
g
such that
{\rm supp}(f)-C={\rm supp}(g)-C
and
|E_{g=(0,0)}(C)|\leq\frac{1}{4}(|E(C)|-q)
.

Proof.

Assume
f
is a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-flow on
G
and
C
is a circuit. A subpath
S
of
C
is an
f_{(0,0),(1,1)}
-
segment
in
C
if
f(e)\in\{(0,0),(1,1)\}
for each edge
e\in S
. We choose
f
and
S
in such a way that for any
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-flow
g
with
{\rm supp}(f)-C={\rm supp}(g)-C
, and any
g_{(0,0),(1,1)}
-segment
S^{\prime}
in
C
,
|S^{\prime}|\leq|S|
.

For
a\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
and cycle
C^{\prime}
, let
f_{C^{\prime},a}
be the function defined as
f_{C^{\prime},a}(e)=a
if
e\in E(C^{\prime})
, and
f_{C^{\prime},a}(e)=0
for other edges
e
. Then
f_{C^{\prime},a}
is a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-flow with
{\rm supp}(f_{C^{\prime},a})=E(C^{\prime})
.

If
S=C
, then let
g=f+f_{C,(0,1)}
. Then
g
is a
Z_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-flow
on
G
with
{\rm supp}(f)-C={\rm supp}(g)-C
, and
E_{g=(0,0)}(C)=\emptyset
. Thus, we may assume that
S\neq C
.

Assume
S=v_{1}v_{2}\cdots v_{s+1}
, and
C=(v_{1}v_{2}\ldots v_{p})
(so the other neighbor of
v_{1}
in
C
is
v_{p}
). Considering
f
or
f+f_{C,(1,1)}
, we may suppose
f(v_{1}v_{2})=(0,0)
.

By the choice of
S
, we have
f(v_{p}v_{1})\notin\left\{(0,0),(1,1)\right\}
. By symmetry, we may assume that
f(v_{p}v_{1})=(1,0)
.

It is well known and easy to verify that for any 2-subset
\{a,b\}
of
\{(1,0),(0,1),(1,1)\}
,
E_{f=a}(G)\cup E_{f=b}(G)
is a cycle. Let
C^{\prime}=E_{f=(1,0)}(G)\cup E_{f=(1,1)}(G)
and
A=C^{\prime}-C
. Then
C^{\prime}
is a cycle and hence the set
O
of all vertices of odd degree in
A
is a subset of
V(C)
and
v_{1}\in O
. Thus, there is a vertex
v_{t}\in O-\left\{v_{1}\right\}
in the same component of
A
as
v_{1}
. Let
P
be the path in
A
from
v_{1}
to
v_{t}
, and
P^{\prime}
be the subpath of
C
from
v_{1}
to
v_{t}
containing
v_{1}v_{2}
. Let
C_{1}
be the cycle consisting of
P
and
P^{\prime}
, and
C_{2}=C\Delta C_{1}
. Let
g=f+f_{C_{1},(0,1)}
,
h=f+f_{C_{2},(0,1)}
. Then
g,h
are
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
flows with
{\rm supp}(f)-C={\rm supp}(g)-C={\rm supp}(h)-C
.

If
t\geq s+1
, then
h(e)=f(e)
for
e\in E(S)
and
h(v_{p}v_{1})=(1,1)
, and therefore
S^{\prime}=(v_{p}v_{1}v_{2}\ldots v_{s+1})
is a
h_{(0,0),(1,1)}
-segment in
C
with
|S^{\prime}|>|S|
, in contrary to our choice of
f
and
S
.

Assume
t\leq s
. Then
{\rm supp}(f)\subseteq{\rm supp}(g)
. Note that the edge
v_{1}v_{2}
is contained in a thread
P
of length at least
q
. All the edges
e
in
P
have
f(e)=(0,0)
and
g(e)=(0,1)
. Hence

|E_{g=(0,0)}(C)|\leq|E_{f=(0,0)}(C)|-q
.
If
|E_{g=(0,0)}(C)|\leq\frac{1}{4}(|C|-q)
, then we are done. Otherwise
|E_{f=(0,0)}(G)|\geq\frac{1}{4}|C|+\frac{3}{4}q
.
Thus, there exists
b\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}-\{(0,0)\}
such that
|E_{f=b}(C)|\leq\frac{1}{4}(|C|-q)
. Let
g=f+f_{C,b}
, then
|E_{g=(0,0)}(C)|\leq\frac{1}{4}(|E(C)|-q)
.
∎

For a positive integer
k
, a
k
-flow
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-flow

such that
|f(e)|<k
for each edge

. Tutte
[
17
]
proved that
a graph

admits a flow nowhere-zero

if and only if
G
admits a flow nowhere-zero
\Gamma
-flow for any abelian group
\Gamma
of order

. Thus the following is a corollary of Lemma

.

Corollary 2.2
.

Assume
G
is a graph and
C
is a circuit in

. If
G/C
admits a nowhere-zero

-flow, then

admits a
4
-flow

such that

and
|E_{f=0}(C)|<\frac{1}{4}|E(C)|
.

Corollary

was conjectured by Fan
[
5
]
, where it was confirmed for circuits

with
|C|\leq 19
, and was further confirmed for
|C|\leq 27
and for
|C|\leq 35
in
[
18
]
and
[
15
]
, respectively.

3.
Shortest cycle cover of brideless graphs

This section proves the following bound of shortest cycle cover of general graphs.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
G
be a bridgeless graph with
m
edges and
n_{2}
vertices of degree
2
, then
cc(G)<\frac{29}{18}m+\frac{1}{18}n_{2}
and consequently
cc(G)\leq\frac{5}{3}m-\frac{1}{42}\log m
, where the base for
\log
is 2. Moreover, if
m\geq 141
, then
cc(G)\leq\frac{5}{3}m-\frac{25}{3}
.

Proof.

We may assume that
G
is loopless: if
e
is a loop in
G
, then
G^{\prime}=G-e
has at most one more degree 2 vertex, and
cc(G)=cc(G^{\prime})+1
. If Theorem
3.1
holds for
G^{\prime}
, then it holds for
G
as well.

Let
\omega(e)=1
for each edge in
G
. If there is a vertex
v
of degree at least
4
, then by a result in
[
7
]
, we can create a new bridgless graph
G^{\prime}
by splitting
v
into
v^{\prime}
and
v^{\prime\prime}
so that
v^{\prime\prime}
is adjacent to two neighbours of
v
and
v^{\prime}
is adjacent to the remaining neighbours of
v
.
We add an edge of weight
0
that connects
v^{\prime}
and
v^{\prime\prime}
. Repetition of this operation results in an edge weighted graph
H
whose maximum degree is at most
3
. Moreover, when all edges of weight
0
are removed, the resulting graph is bridgeless.

Let
\bar{H}
be the graph obtained from
H
by replacing each thread
P
with a single edge
e_{P}
. Thus
\bar{H}
is a bridgeless cubic graph. By
[
4
]
, there is an integer
p\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
and a family
\bar{\mathcal{M}}
of
3p
perfect matchings such that every edge
e\in E(\bar{H})
is contained in
p
members of
\mathcal{P}
.
For a perfect matching
\bar{M}\in\bar{\mathcal{M}}
,
\bar{F}=\bar{H}-\bar{M}
is a cycle in
\bar{H}
.

For each edge
e_{P}
in
\bar{H}
, replace
e_{P}
by the path
P
, and contract all edges
e
of weight
0
. Each
\bar{F}
corresponds to a cycle
F
in
G
.
We denote by
\mathcal{F}
the family cycles
F
obtained from
\bar{F}
, and denote by
\mathcal{M}
the family of subgraphs
M
obtained from
\bar{M}
. Then each edge
e
of
G
is contained in
2p
cycles
F
in
\mathcal{F}
, and contained in
p
subgraphs in
\mathcal{M}
. The average size of
F
is
2m/3
and the average size of
M
is
m/3
.

Let
F
be a cycle in
\mathcal{F}
of maximum size. Thus
|F|\geq\frac{2}{3}m
.
It was shown in
[
19
]
that
G/F
has odd-edge-connectivity at least
5
.
Hence
G/F
have a nowhere-zero
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-flow
[
8
]
, which extends to a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-flow
f
of
G
with
E(G)-F\subseteq{\rm supp}(f)
.

By Lemma
2.1
, we may assume that for each component
B
of
F
,

|E_{f=(0,0)},B)|<\frac{1}{4}|B|
.
For
i\geq 2
, let
d_{i}
be the number of components in
F
with
i
edges.
Then

|E_{f=(0,0)}(G)|=|E_{f=(0,0)}(F)|\leq\frac{1}{4}|F|-\frac{1}{4}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\infty}d_{4i+1}-\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{\infty}d_{4i+2}-\frac{3}{4}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{\infty}d_{4i+3}-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\infty}d_{4i}.

We choose the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-flow
f
so that
|E_{f=(0,1)}(G-F)|
is minimum, and subject to this,
|E_{f=(1,0)}(G-F)|
is minimum.
This implies that
E_{f=(0,1)}(G-F)
is acyclic, because if
C
is a cycle in
E_{f=(0,1)}(G-F)
, then
f+f_{C,(1,0)}
is a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-flow
g
with
{\rm supp}(g)={\rm supp}(f)
and
|E_{g=(0,1)}(G)|<|E_{f=(0,1)}(G)|
. Similarly,

E_{f=(1,0)}(G-F)
is acyclic.

Let
C_{1}=E_{f=(0,1)}(G)\cup E_{f=(1,0)}(G),C_{2}=E_{f=(0,1)}(G)\cup E_{f=(1,1)}(G),C_{3}=E_{f=(1,1)}(G)\cup E_{f=(1,0)}(G)
. Then
C_{1},C_{2},C_{3}
is a double cover of
supp(f)
. Therefore

\mathcal{C}_{1}=\{C_{1}\Delta F,C_{2}\Delta F,C_{3}\Delta F\}

is a cycle cover of
G
, where each edge not in
F
is covered twice, and each edge
e\in F\cap{\rm supp}(f)
is covered once and each edge in
E_{f=0}(F)
is covered three times. Thus
\mathcal{C}_{1}
is a cycle cover of
G
of
length
2m-|F|+2|E_{f=0}(G)|
. Therefore

\displaystyle\ell(\mathcal{C}_{1})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2m-|F|+2|E_{f=0}(G)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2m-\frac{1}{2}|F|-\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\infty}d_{4i+1}-\sum\limits_{i=0}^{\infty}d_{4i+2}-\frac{3}{2}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{\infty}d_{4i+3}-2\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\infty}d_{4i}.

We may assume that for each component
B
of
F
,
|C_{2}\cap B|\leq\frac{1}{2}|B|
, for otherwise we replace
C_{2}
with the symmetric difference
C_{2}\Delta B
.
Thus
|C_{2}\cap F|\leq\frac{1}{2}|F|-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}d_{2i+1}
.
Let
\mathcal{C}_{2}=\{C_{1},C_{2},C_{1}\Delta F\}
. Then
\mathcal{C}_{2}
is also a cycle cover of
G
. The length of this cycle cover is

\displaystyle\ell(\mathcal{C}_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|C_{1}|+|C_{2}|+|C_{1}-F|+|F-C_{1}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2|C_{1}-F|+|C_{2}|+|F|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m+|C_{1}-F|+|C_{2}\cap F|+|C_{1}\cap C_{2}-F|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle m+|C_{1}-F|+|C_{1}\cap C_{2}-F|+\frac{1}{2}|F|-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}d_{2i+1}.

Let
C^{\prime}_{i}=C_{i}-F
for
i=1,2
. Then
\ell(\mathcal{C}_{2})\leq m+|C^{\prime}_{1}-C^{\prime}_{2}|+2|C^{\prime}_{1}\cap C^{\prime}_{2}|+\frac{1}{2}|F|-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}d_{2i+1}
.

Note that
C^{\prime}_{i}
is a cycle in
G/F
,
C^{\prime}_{1}-C^{\prime}_{2}=E_{f=(1,0)}(G-F)
, and
C^{\prime}_{1}\cap C^{\prime}_{2}=E_{f=(0,1)}(G-F)
. Hence
C^{\prime}_{1}-C^{\prime}_{2}
and
C^{\prime}_{1}\cap C^{\prime}_{2}
are acyclic.

We view each of
C^{\prime}_{1}-C^{\prime}_{2}
and
C^{\prime}_{1}\cap C^{\prime}_{2}
as a spanning subgraph of
G/F
. Let
c_{1},c_{2}
be the number of connected components of
C^{\prime}_{1}-C^{\prime}_{2}
and
C^{\prime}_{1}\cap C^{\prime}_{2}
, respectively. Then
|C^{\prime}_{1}-C^{\prime}_{2}|=|V(G/F)|-c_{1},|C^{\prime}_{1}\cap C^{\prime}_{2}|=|V(G/F)|-c_{2}
.

Let
d_{1}
be the number of degree 2 vertices of
G
contained in

G/F
. The choice of
F
implies that
d_{1}\leq\frac{1}{3}n_{2}
.
A degree 2 vertex
v
in
G/F
is an isolated vertex in
C^{\prime}_{1}\cap C^{\prime}_{2}
if and only if the two edges
e_{1},e_{2}
incident to
v
are have flow
f(e_{1})=f(e_{2}))\neq(0,1)
.
Thus by the choice of
f
, at least
\frac{2}{3}d_{1}
vertices are isolated vertices in
C^{\prime}_{1}\cap C^{\prime}_{2}
. Hence
c_{2}\geq\frac{2}{3}d_{1}+1
. Similarly,
c_{1}+2c_{2}\geq 3\times\frac{2}{3}d_{1}+3=2d_{1}+3
.
As
|V(G/F)|=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}d_{i}
, we conclude that

\displaystyle|C^{\prime}_{1}-C^{\prime}_{2}|+2|C^{\prime}_{1}\cap C^{\prime}_{2}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 3\sum\limits_{i=2}^{\infty}d_{i}+d_{1}-3.

Therefore

\displaystyle\ell(\mathcal{C}_{2})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle m+3\sum\limits_{i=2}^{\infty}d_{i}+d_{1}-3+\frac{1}{2}|F|-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}d_{2i+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m+\frac{1}{2}|F|+\frac{5}{2}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\infty}d_{4i+1}+3\sum\limits_{i=0}^{\infty}d_{4i+2}+\frac{5}{2}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{\infty}d_{4i+3}+3\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\infty}d_{4i}+d_{1}-3.

Hence

\displaystyle cc(G)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\min\{\ell(\mathcal{C}_{1}),\ell(\mathcal{C}_{2})\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{5}{6}\ell(\mathcal{C}_{1})+\frac{1}{6}\ell(\mathcal{C}_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{5}{6}(2m-\frac{1}{2}|F|-\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\infty}d_{4i+1}-\sum\limits_{i=0}^{\infty}d_{4i+2}-\frac{3}{2}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{\infty}d_{4i+3}-2\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\infty}d_{4i})

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{6}(m+\frac{1}{2}|F|+\frac{5}{2}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\infty}d_{4i+1}+3\sum\limits_{i=0}^{\infty}d_{4i+2}+\frac{5}{2}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{\infty}d_{4i+3}+3\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\infty}d_{4i}+d_{1}-3)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{11}{6}m-\frac{1}{3}|F|+\frac{1}{6}d_{1}-\frac{1}{2}.

As
|F|\geq\frac{2}{3}m
and
d_{1}\leq\frac{1}{3}n_{2}
, we have

cc(G)\leq\frac{29}{18}m+\frac{1}{18}n_{2}-\frac{1}{2}.

It was proved by Jamshy, Raspaud and Tarsi
[
9
]
that if
G
admits a nowhere zero 5-flow, then
cc(G)\leq\frac{8}{5}m
, and hence
cc(G)\leq\frac{5}{3}m-\frac{25}{3}
, provided that
m\geq 125
.
Assume that
G
does not admits a nowhere zero 5-flow, i.e.,
G
is a counterexample to Tutte’s 5-flow conjecture. It was proved by Kochol
[
12
]
that every counterexample to the 5-flow conjecture has girth at least 11, and hence has at least 94 vertices. Note that a graph
G^{\prime}
admits a nowhere zero 5-flow if and only if by replacing each thread of
G^{\prime}
by a single edge, the resulting graph admits a nowhere zero 5-flow. Thus we may assume that
G
has at least
94
vertices of degree at least
3
. Hence
94\times 3+2n_{2}\leq 2m
and
n_{2}\leq m-141
, provided that
m\geq 141
. This implies that if
m\geq 141
,
then
cc(G)\leq\frac{5}{3}m-\frac{25}{3}
.

It remains to show that
cc(G)\leq\frac{5}{3}m-\frac{1}{42}\log m
.

As

cc(G)\leq\frac{5}{3}m-\frac{1}{18}(m-n_{2})
, we are done if
m-n_{2}>\frac{3}{7}\log m
.

Assume
m-n_{2}\leq\frac{3}{7}\log m
.

Let
\mathcal{C}_{1}
be the cycle cover of
G
constructed above. We have shown that

cc(G)\leq\ell(\mathcal{C}_{1})\leq 2m-|F|+2|E_{f=(0,0)}(G)|,

where
|F|\geq\frac{2}{3}m
.

Let
\epsilon=\frac{\log m}{56m}
.
If
|E_{f=(0,0)}(G)|\leq(\frac{1}{4}-\epsilon)|F|
, then

cc(G)\leq 2m-(\frac{1}{2}+2\epsilon)|F|\leq 2m-(\frac{1}{2}+2\epsilon)\frac{2}{3}m=\frac{5}{3}m-\frac{1}{42}\log m.

Thus it suffices to show that for any connected component
C
of
F
,

|E_{f=(0,0)}(C)|\leq(\frac{1}{4}-\epsilon)|C|
.

If
|C|<\frac{14m}{\log m}
, then it follows from Corollary
2.2
that
|E_{f=(0,0)}(C)|\leq\frac{1}{4}|C|-\frac{1}{4}<(\frac{1}{4}-\epsilon)|C|

Assume
|C|\geq\frac{14m}{\log m}
.
Let
e_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{k}
be the threads in
C
, with
\ell(e_{i})\leq\ell(e_{i+1})
for all
i
. Thus
C
has
k
vertices of degree at least
3
. Hence
k\leq\frac{2}{3}(m-n_{2})\leq\frac{2}{7}\log m
.

If
\ell(e_{i})\leq 5^{i}\log m
for all
i
, then
|C|\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}5^{i}\log m\leq\frac{5^{k+1}}{4}\log m
.
As
k\leq\frac{2}{7}\log m
,
|C|\leq\frac{5}{4}m^{\frac{2log5}{7}}\log m
. This implies that
\frac{14m}{\log m}\leq\frac{5}{4}m^{\frac{2\log 5}{7}}\log m
, hence
56m^{1-\frac{2\log 5}{7}}\leq 5(\log m)^{2}
, which fails for any positive integer
m
.

Thus there exists
i
such that
\ell(e_{i})\geq 5^{i}\log m
.

Let
i_{0}
be the minimum index such that

\ell(e_{i_{0}})\geq 5^{i_{0}}\log m
.

Let
S
be the union of all the threads
e_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{i_{0}-1}
. Let
G^{\prime}=G/S
and
C^{\prime}=C/S
.
Let
q=5^{i_{0}}\log m
.
Then each thread of
C^{\prime}
has length at least
q
. By Corollary
2.2
, we may assume that
|E_{f=(0,0)}(C^{\prime})|\leq\frac{1}{4}(|E(C^{\prime})|-q)
.

Then
|E_{f=(0,0)}(C)|\leq\frac{1}{4}(|E(C^{\prime})|-q)+\sum_{i=1}^{i_{0}-1}5^{i}\log m\leq\frac{1}{4}|E(C)|-\frac{5}{4}\log m<(\frac{1}{4}-\epsilon)|C|
. This completes the proof of Theorem
3.1
.
∎

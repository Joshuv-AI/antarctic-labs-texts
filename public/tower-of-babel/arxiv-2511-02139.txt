Limited-Range Multilinear Off-Diagonal Extrapolation and Weighted Transference Principle
Jonas Sauer

Abstract
Multilinear L p L^{p} extrapolation results are established in a limited-range, multilinear, and off-diagonal setting for mixed-norm Lebesgue spaces over σ \sigma -finite measure spaces. Integrability exponents are allowed in the full range ( 0 , ∞ ] (0,\infty] . We detach the exponents for the weight classes completely from the exponents for the initial and target spaces for the extrapolation except for the basic consistency condition. This enables us to cover the full range ( 0 , ∞ ] (0,\infty] for all integrability exponents and provides new insights into the dependency of the extrapolated bounds on the weight characteristic. Certain endpoint results are new even for ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} . Additionally, in the setting of compact abelian groups, a weighted transference principle is established. Keywords: Muckenhoupt weights; extrapolation; LCA groups; maximal operator. MSC (2020): 42B25, 42B35, 43A15.

Introduction

This article is concerned with extrapolation results in Lebesgue spaces over
\sigma
-finite measure spaces, and in the specific case of locally compact abelian groups with a weighted version of de Leeuw’s transference principle
[
4
]
, which allows to transfer Fourier multiplier estimates from one group to another. In the unweighted case, a rather general transference principle has been proved in the group setting by Edwards and Gaudry
[
6
]
, see also
[
7
]
for a modern exposition.
We prove a generalization to the setting of weighted
L^{p}
spaces, where the weights are assumed to be in the Muckenhoupt class
A_{p}
.
In fact, we deviate from the usual definition of
A_{p}
, which helps in formulating the results in a more symmetric way.

As a matter of fact, this symmetry also shows its benefits for the extrapolation results.
The theory of
L^{p}
-extrapolation has a long history starting with the works by García-Cuerva and Rubio de Francia
[
8
,
17
]
, who realized that boundedness of an operator
T
on all Muckenhoupt-weighted
L^{p}
-spaces implies boundedness on all Muckenhoupt-weighted
L^{q}
-spaces for the range
q\in(1,\infty)
.
They also showed that vector-valued estimates can be extracted from scalar-valued weighted estimates.
Later, Buckley
[
2
]
found quantitative bounds for the maximal operator in Muckenhoupt weighted spaces in terms of the weight characteristic.
Restricting the weight classes for the initial spaces of extrapolation, Auscher and Martell proved first versions of limited-range extrapolation in
[
1
]
.
For a detailed history of the theory, we refer to Nieraeth
[
13
]
, where a comprehensive theory for multilinear extrapolation is established.
In particular, in the multilinear case
T:\prod_{j=1}^{m}L^{p_{j}}_{{w}_{j}}\to L^{p}_{{w}}
with
\frac{1}{p}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{1}{p_{j}}
some of the
p_{j}
’s (but not all of them) are allowed to be infinity in the target spaces of the extrapolation.
In
[
11
]
, the authors provided conditions that ensure extrapolation towards
p=\infty
.
In the linear case, they also showed that this is possible in the off-diagonal setting, that is when
p\neq p_{1}
.
Explicit bounds for the results of
[
11
]
were recently found in
[
3
]
.
Recently, Nieraeth
[
14
]
found extrapolation results from Lebesgue spaces over
\sigma
-finite measure to general quasi-Banach function spaces, which includes mixed-norm estimates.
Our contribution to the theory of extrapolation in weighted spaces is as follows:

(i)

We extend the results of
[
3
]
and
[
11
]
: We include the range
(0,1]
for all initial and target spaces of the extrapolation.
We show off-diagonal estimates even in the multilinear case and allow for mixed
L^{\mathbf{p}}
-norms and two-weight estimates.
We provide explicit bounds as in
[
3
]
, which are sharp in all linear and scalar-valued cases considered in
[
11
]
.
In comparison to
[
3
]
and
[
11
]
our proofs are significantly shorter and leaner.
This is achieved by systematically exploiting the symmetry of the weight classes.
In fact, Theorem
2.6
contains Theorem 2.3 in
[
11
]
, where the authors are forced to consider three different cases, by specialising to the case
p_{0}\in[1,\infty]
,
p\in(1,\infty]
and
r_{0}:=p_{0}^{\prime}
,
r=p^{\prime}
.
We warn the reader that our

is called

in
[
11
]
.
Finally, we work in the setting of general
\sigma
-finite measure spaces as in
[

]
instead of

.

(ii)

We show that the approach of Nieraeth developed in
[
12
,
13
,
14
]
can be adapted to include the endpoint results from
[
11
]
.
Specialising to weighted mixed-norm spaces, we can provide more explicit bounds than the ones found in
[

]
in the setting of abstract quasi-Banach function spaces.
We remark that the possibility of multilinear off-diagonal estimates was mentioned in
[

(iii)

In contrast to both
[

]
and the works of Nieraeth, we detach the exponents for the weight classes completely from the exponents for the initial and target spaces for the extrapolation except for the basic consistency condition (

This might be seen as a mere cosmetic change, but it avoids the unpleasant use of negative exponents in

,

14

and crucially helps to understand that it is the exponents of the weight classes (and not the exponents of the Lebesgue spaces) which govern the size of the extrapolation bounds.
In particular, it is immediately clear from (

,
r_{0}
,

,

,

,

This insight might serve as an additional heuristic why sharp bounds can not be obtained in general quasi-Banach function spaces.
On the other hand, keeping

In our exposition we follow in many regards the works of Nieraeth.
Instead of working with extrapolation pairs

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}\subseteq\{(f,g):f,g:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}\text{ are nonnegative, measurable functions}\}

or multilinear versions of it, we work with the more general map formulation proposed in
[
14
]
.
The reason is that this formulation gives a much leaner argument that
Tf
is well-defined in the target space of the extrapolation, especially for
L^{\infty}
-type spaces.
Moreover, we work with general
\sigma
-finite measure spaces with a basis of sets.
Here, a countable collection of measurable sets
{\mathcal{U}}
with
\mu(U)\in(0,\infty)
for all
U\in{\mathcal{U}}
is a basis of sets in
\Omega
if

(i)

\bigcup_{U\in{\mathcal{U}}}U=\Omega
,

(ii)

x,y\in\Omega

\Rightarrow

\exists U\in{\mathcal{U}}
with
x,y\in U
.

We fix
(\Omega,\mu)
together with a basis of sets
U
for the rest of this article and write
L^{0}(\Omega;X)
for the space of strongly measurable functions, where
X
is a quasi-Banach space.
In the scalar-valued case, we simply write
L^{0}(\Omega)
.

A real-valued function
{w}\in L^{0}(\Omega)
with
{w}>0
is called a
weight
.

Given
p\in(0,\infty]
, a weight
{w}
, and a quasi-Banach space
X
, we denote by
L^{p}_{{w}}(\Omega;X)
the space of all
f\in L^{0}(\Omega;X)
such that
\|x\mapsto\|f(x)\|_{X}{w}(x)\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}<\infty
, where the
L^{p}(\Omega)
-norm is defined in the usual way.
We again write
L^{p}_{{w}}(\Omega)
in the scalar-valued case.
Given
s,r\in(0,\infty]
and two weights
{w}
and
{v}
, we introduce the weight characteristic

[{w},{v}]_{(s,r)}:=\sup_{U\in{\mathcal{U}}}\frac{1}{\mu(U)^{\frac{1}{s}+\frac{1}{r}}}\|{w}\|_{L^{s}(U)}\|{v}^{-1}\|_{L^{r}(U)}.

(1)

We write
[{w}]_{(s,r)}:=[{w},{w}]_{(s,r)}
, and for
p\in[1,\infty]
, we write
[{w},{v}]_{p}:=[{w},{v}]_{(p,p^{\prime})}
and
[{w}]_{p}:=[{w},{w}]_{p}
.
We will discuss properties of weights with finite weight characteristic in Section
2
below.

For
p\in[1,\infty)
, the quantity

\displaystyle[{w}]_{A_{p}}:=\sup_{U}\frac{1}{\mu(U)^{p}}\|{w}\|_{L^{1}(U)}\|{w}^{1-p^{\prime}}\|_{L^{1}(U)}^{p-1}=[{w}]_{1,p^{\prime}-1}=[{w}^{\frac{1}{p}}]_{p}^{p}

is traditionally used in the literature to define the class of Muckenhoupt weights
A_{p}
(mostly for
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{d}
), together with
A_{\infty}:=\bigcup_{p\in[1,\infty)}A_{p}
.
Be aware that
A_{\infty}
is not characterized by
[{w}]_{\infty}<\infty
, but rather by finiteness of the Fujii-Wilson constant
[{w}]_{A_{\infty}}:=\sup_{U}\frac{1}{\mu(U)}\int_{U}{\mathcal{M}}({w}\mathds{1}_{U})\,\mathrm{d}\mu
.
The more symmetric notion of
[{w}]_{p}
is advantageous as it often enables to include the endpoint case
p=\infty
in a straightforward manner.
It also includes the reverse Hölder class
RH_{s}
introduced in
[
1
]
, which is defined by
[{w}]_{RH_{s}}:=[{w},{w}^{-1}]_{s,1}<\infty
.
Moreover, it simplifies many statements and calculations as is exemplified by Proposition
2.1
(iii)
below, whose clean statement should be compared with the corresponding statement (in fact statements!) if the
[{w}]_{A_{p}}
-formalism is employed, see e.g. Lemma 2.1 in
[
5
]
.

For
f\in L^{0}(\Omega)
we define the
maximal operator
on
\Omega
via

\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}}f:=\sup_{U\in{\mathcal{U}}}\frac{1}{\mu(U)}\|f\|_{L^{1}(U)}\mathds{1}_{U}.

(2)

Note that
{\mathcal{M}}f
is the countable supremum of measurable functions and hence measurable.
Moreover, for
f\in L^{1}(\Omega)
we have
{\mathcal{M}}f>0
whenever
f\neq 0
, see
[
14
, Proposition 2.16]
.

In order to give a feel for the type of results, we showcase the case of linear one-weight extrapolation, for definiteness with
p_{0}<p
.
The following result follows directly from the more general Theorem
2.6
and Remark
2.4
(ii)
below.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
q_{0},p_{0},s_{0},s,r\in(0,\infty)
,
r_{0},q,p\in(0,\infty]
be such that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{q_{0}}=\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{p_{0}}=\frac{1}{s}-\frac{1}{s_{0}}=\frac{1}{r_{0}}-\frac{1}{r}<0.

(3)

Let
{w}
be a weight with
[{w}]_{(s,r)}<\infty
.
Assume the following:

(i)

There is
c:(1,\infty]\to[1,\infty)
such that

\displaystyle\|{\mathcal{M}}\|_{L^{t}_{{v}}(\Omega)\to L^{t}_{{v}}(\Omega)}\leq c(t)[{v}]_{t}^{t^{\prime}}\quad\text{for all $t\in(1,\infty]$ and weights ${v}$}.

(ii)

There is a set
V
, a map
S:V\to L^{0}(\Omega)
, a map

\displaystyle T:\bigcup_{[{w}_{0}]_{(s_{0},r_{0})}<\infty}S^{-1}(L^{p_{0}}_{{w}_{0}}(\Omega))\to L^{0}(\Omega),

and an increasing function
\phi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
such that for all weights
{w}_{0}
with
[{w}_{0}]_{(s_{0},r_{0})}<\infty
and all

it holds

\displaystyle\|Tf\|_{L^{q_{0}}_{{w}_{0}}}\leq\phi([{w}_{0}]_{(s_{0},r_{0})})\|Sf\|_{L^{p_{0}}_{{w}_{0}}}.

Then
Tf
is well-defined for all
f\in S^{-1}(L^{p}_{{w}}(\Omega))
, and for all
\kappa\in(1,\infty)
it holds

\displaystyle\|Tf\|_{L^{q}_{{w}}}\leq\kappa^{\beta}\phi((\kappa^{\prime}c(t))^{\beta}[{w}]_{(s,r)}^{s/s_{0}})\|Sf\|_{L^{p}_{{w}}},

where
t:=(\frac{1}{s}+\frac{1}{r})/\frac{1}{r}
and
\beta:=(\frac{1}{s}+\frac{1}{r})(\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{r_{0}})/\frac{1}{r}
.

Remark 1.2
.

(i)

The same statement holds for
r_{0},q,p,s,r\in(0,\infty)
and
s_{0},q_{0},p_{0}\in(0,\infty]
in the case of downward extrapolation (that is for
>
instead of

in (

)) if one chooses

and

in the final bound.

(ii)

The boundedness condition for the maximal operator is fulfilled for

and more generally in locally compact abelian groups, see Proposition
4.2
.
It is known to be optimal in terms of the exponent in these cases, cf.
[
2
]
.
If only boundedness of the maximal operator is known, then Theorem

continues to hold, albeit with a different constant, see Theorem

.

(iii)

Some exponents in Theorem
1.1
can be chosen to be
\infty
, while others cannot.
There are two different reasons at play that prohibit the choice infinity: One is the fact that Theorem

showcases an instance of upward extrapolation, so that

(and hence

The other reason is that we do not assume the maximal operator to be bounded in

This translates into the final bound not being finite (or even well-defined since

(iv)

We emphasize that condition (

) in Theorem

indeed constitutes a limited-range condition:
By

and

it is necessary that

\displaystyle\in[\frac{1}{q_{0}}-\frac{1}{p_{0}},\infty)\cap(\frac{1}{q_{0}}-\frac{1}{s_{0}},\infty)\cap[0,\frac{1}{q_{0}}+\frac{1}{r_{0}}),

(v)

As observed in

implies a result in terms of extrapolation pairs.
Indeed, if

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}\subseteq\{(f,g):f,g:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}\text{ are nonnegative, measurable functions}\},

shows that a bound

and weights

[{w}]_{(s,r)}<\infty

(vi)

The conditions for extrapolating towards

are necessarily of a rather restrictive type.
This is highlighted by the fact that the Hilbert transform is bounded in

for all weights

with

, but not in

Still, Theorem

(vii)

We can deduce a bound

The paper is organized as follows.
In Section 2, we provide basic properties of the weight characteristic and prove a limited-range multilinear off-diagonal extrapolation result.
In Section 3, we generalize these results to mixed-norm spaces and give certain vector-valued extensions. In particular, we obtain explicit
{\mathcal{R}}
-bounds of families of linear operators from weighted bounds.
Finally, in Section 4 we prove a weighted version of de Leeuw’s transference principle in the context of locally compact abelian groups.

2
Limited-Range Multilinear Off-Diagonal Extrapolation

We start with basic properties of the weight characteristic.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
w
and
v
be weights and
s,r\in(0,\infty]
.

(i)

[{w},{v}]_{(s,r)}=[{v}^{-1},{w}^{-1}]_{(r,s)}
.

(ii)

[{w}^{\alpha},{v}^{\alpha}]_{(\frac{s}{\alpha},\frac{r}{\alpha})}=[{w},{v}]_{(s,r)}^{\alpha}
for all
\alpha\in(0,\infty)
.

(iii)

Let
{w}_{1},\ldots,{w}_{m}
and
{v}_{1},\ldots,{v}_{m}
be weights and
s_{1},\ldots,s_{m},r_{1},\ldots,r_{m}\in(0,\infty]
such that
{w}=\prod_{j=1}^{m}{w}_{j}
,
{v}=\prod_{j=1}^{m}{v}_{j}
,
\frac{1}{s}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{1}{s_{j}}
, and
\frac{1}{r}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{1}{r_{j}}
.
Then it holds

\displaystyle[{w},{v}]_{(s,r)}\leq[{w}_{1},{v}_{1}]_{(s_{1},r_{1})}\cdots[{w}_{m},{v}_{m}]_{(s_{m},r_{m})}.

In particular
[w,v]_{(s,r)}\leq[{w},{v}]_{(s_{1},r_{1})}
whenever
s\leq s_{1},r\leq r_{1}
.

(iv)

[{w},{v}]_{(1,\infty)}=\|{\mathcal{M}}w\|_{L^{\infty}_{{v}^{-1}}(\Omega)}=\|{\mathcal{M}}\|_{L^{\infty}_{{w}^{-1}}(\Omega)\to L^{\infty}_{{v}^{-1}}(\Omega)}
.

(v)

[{w},{v}]_{(\infty,1)}=\|{\mathcal{M}}{v}^{-1}\|_{L^{\infty}_{{w}}(\Omega)}=\|{\mathcal{M}}\|_{L^{\infty}_{v}(\Omega)\to L^{\infty}_{{w}}(\Omega)}
.

Proof.

The assertions in
(i)
and
(ii)
follow trivially from the definition of
[{w},{v}]_{(s,r)}
in (
1
).
Let
U\in{\mathcal{U}}
.
By Hölder’s inequality we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mu(U)^{\frac{1}{s}+\frac{1}{r}}}\|{w}\|_{L^{s}(U)}\|{v}^{-1}\|_{L^{r}(U)}\leq\prod_{j=1}^{m}\frac{1}{\mu(U)^{\frac{1}{s_{j}}+\frac{1}{r_{j}}}}\|{w}_{j}\|_{L^{s_{j}}(U)}\|{v}_{j}^{-1}\|_{L^{r_{j}}(U)}.

Taking the supremum over all
U\in{\mathcal{U}}
yields the product estimate in
(iii)
.
The additional assertion follows by taking
m=2
and
{w}_{2}={v}_{2}=1
due to
[1,1]_{(s_{2},r_{2})}=1
.
For
(iv)
let
x\in\Omega
.
Then for all
U\in{\mathcal{U}}
we have
\mathds{1}_{U}(x){v}(x)^{-1}\leq\|{v}^{-1}\|_{L^{\infty}(U)}
and thus

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mu(U)}\|{w}\|_{L^{1}(U)}\mathds{1}_{U}(x){v}(x)^{-1}\leq\frac{1}{\mu(U)}\|{w}\|_{L^{1}(U)}\|v^{-1}\|_{L^{\infty}(U)}\leq[{w},{v}]_{(1,\infty)}.

Taking the supremum over all
U\in{\mathcal{U}}
and
x\in\Omega
, we obtain
\|{\mathcal{M}}{w}\|_{L^{\infty}_{{v}^{-1}}(\Omega)}\leq[{w},{v}]_{(1,\infty)}
.
On the other hand, let
U\in{\mathcal{U}}
and
\varepsilon>0
.
Choose
x\in U
with
\|{v}^{-1}\|_{L^{\infty}(U)}\leq(1+\varepsilon)/{v}(x)
.
Then

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mu(U)}\|{w}\|_{L^{1}(U)}\|{v}^{-1}\|_{L^{\infty}(U)}\leq\frac{(1+\varepsilon)}{\mu(U)}\|{w}\|_{L^{1}(U)}/{v}(x)\leq(1+\varepsilon)\|({\mathcal{M}}{w})/{v}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}.

Since
\varepsilon>0
and
U\in{\mathcal{U}}
were arbitrary, this gives
[{w},{v}]_{(1,\infty)}\leq\|({\mathcal{M}}{w})/{v}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}
.
Together this yields
[{w},{v}]_{(1,\infty)}=\|({\mathcal{M}}{w})/{v}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}
.
For the final equality, observe that
\|{\mathcal{M}}{w}\|_{L^{\infty}_{{v}^{-1}}(\Omega)}\leq\|{\mathcal{M}}\|_{L^{\infty}_{{w}^{-1}}(\Omega)\to L^{\infty}_{{v}^{-1}}(\Omega)}
due to
\|{w}\|_{L^{\infty}_{{w}^{-1}}(\Omega)}=1
, and that
the sublinearity of
{\mathcal{M}}
yields

\displaystyle({\mathcal{M}}f)/{v}={\mathcal{M}}(f{w}^{-1}{w})/{v}\leq{\mathcal{M}}({w})/{v}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}_{{w}^{-1}}(\Omega)}\leq\|{\mathcal{M}}{w}\|_{L^{\infty}_{{v}^{-1}}(\Omega)}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}_{{w}^{-1}}(\Omega)},

which shows
\|{\mathcal{M}}\|_{L^{\infty}_{{w}^{-1}}(\Omega)\to L^{\infty}_{{v}^{-1}}(\Omega)}\leq\|{\mathcal{M}}{w}\|_{L^{\infty}_{{v}^{-1}}(\Omega)}
.
Part
(v)
follows from
(iv)
and
(i)
.

The following lemma is the key construction in proving extrapolation theorems for weighted spaces.
We extend an approach by Nieraeth
[

]
, which crucially uses the symmetry of the definition of
[{w}]_{(s_{0},r_{0})}
to circumvent the necessity of giving two different arguments for

and

.
Even for
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the formulation with

or

seems to be new.
Its formulation is facilitated by the following definition of weight classes and rescaled exponents.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\frac{1}{\gamma}\in\mathbb{R}

Definition 3.2

\frac{1}{s_{i}}-\frac{1}{s_{i-1}}=\frac{1}{r_{i-1}}-\frac{1}{r_{i}}=\frac{1}{\gamma_{i}}

\displaystyle\begin{cases}t_{0i}:=\frac{s_{i-1}}{\alpha},\quad t_{i}:=\frac{s_{i}}{\alpha},\quad({w}_{t_{i}},{v}_{t_{i}}):=(\otimes_{k=i}^{\ell}{w}_{k}^{\alpha},\otimes_{k=i}^{\ell}{v}_{k}^{\alpha})&\text{if }\frac{1}{\gamma_{i}}>0,\\

(13)

\{(\tilde{w},\tilde{v})\ \lvert\ \tilde{w}\tilde{v}^{-1}={w}{v}^{-1}\text{ and }[\tilde{w},\tilde{v}]_{(s_{0},r_{0})}<\infty\}&\text{else.}\end{cases}

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
p_{0},s_{0},r_{0}\in(0,\infty]

\mathbf{p},\mathbf{s},\mathbf{r}\in(0,\infty]^{\ell}
be such that (with the notation in (
12
))

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mathbf{p}}-\frac{1}{\mathbf{p}_{0}}=\frac{1}{\mathbf{s}}-\frac{1}{\mathbf{s}_{0}}=\frac{1}{\mathbf{r}_{0}}-\frac{1}{\mathbf{r}}=:\frac{1}{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}.

Let
\boldsymbol{{w}}
and
\boldsymbol{{v}}
be tuples of weights with
[\boldsymbol{{w}},\boldsymbol{{v}}]_{(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{r})}<\infty
.
Define

\displaystyle(\mathbf{t}_{0},\mathbf{t},\boldsymbol{{w}}_{\mathbf{t}},\boldsymbol{{v}}_{\mathbf{t}},W)\text{ via Definition \ref{js212a}}.

If
{\mathcal{M}}_{{\mathcal{U}}_{\Lambda_{i}}}
is bounded from
L^{t_{i}}_{{v}_{t_{i}}}(\Omega_{\Lambda_{i}})
to
L^{t_{i}}_{{w}_{t_{i}}}(\Omega_{\Lambda_{i}})

\displaystyle L^{\mathbf{p}}_{\boldsymbol{{v}}}(\Omega)\subseteq\bigcup_{({w}_{0},{v}_{0})\in W}L^{p_{0}}_{{v}_{0}}(\Omega).

Proof.

For
\ell=1
, the assertion is simply Corollary
2.5

Let the result be true for all
\ell^{\prime}<\ell
, and consider
f\in L^{\mathbf{p}}_{\boldsymbol{{v}}}(\Omega)

Define
\tilde{f}\in L^{p_{\ell}}_{{v}_{\ell}}(\Omega_{\ell})

,
\tilde{\boldsymbol{{w}}}=({w}_{1},\ldots,{w}_{\ell-1})
, and
\tilde{\Omega}:=\prod_{i=1}^{\ell-1}\Omega_{i}

The induction hypothesis yields weights
\mu
and
\nu
on
\Omega_{\ell}
with
(\mu,\nu)=({w}_{\ell},{v}_{\ell})

and

and
{v}_{0}

\Omega

({w}_{0},{v}_{0})=(\otimes_{i\in I}{w}_{i},\otimes_{i\in I}{v}_{i})

\frac{1}{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}=0
and
\frac{{w}_{0}}{{v}_{0}}=(\frac{{w}_{1}}{{v}_{1}})\otimes\cdots\otimes(\frac{{w}_{\ell-1}\otimes\mu}{{v}_{\ell-1}\otimes\nu})

∎

Remark 3.5
.

(i)

The proof of Theorem
3.4
allows to track the exact definition of

recursively.
In particular, assume

such that for all

,

, and weights

Then if

,
\vec{\boldsymbol{{w}}}=\vec{\boldsymbol{{v}}}
, and

, an iteration of the bound (
11
) in Remark
2.7
shows that we may choose
\phi_{\vec{s_{0}},\vec{r_{0}},\vec{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}}([\vec{\boldsymbol{{w}}},\vec{\boldsymbol{{v}}}]_{\vec{\mathbf{s}},\vec{\mathbf{r}}}):=\kappa^{\ell/\alpha}\phi(\vec{C})
with

.

(ii)

Theorem
3.4
also contains a weak-type extrapolation result by an argument of Grafakos and Martell
[
9
]
:
One may exchange
\|T\vec{f}\|_{L^{q_{0}}_{{w}_{0}}(\Omega)}
by

.

by Theorem

∎

We finally obtain a result about

-boundedness of families of linear operators via extrapolation.

4
Weighted Transference Principle

In this section we specialize to a locally abelian group
\Omega=\mathfrak{g}
and to integrability exponents in the Banach space range.
Let
\mu
be a nontrivial, regular measure on a
\mathfrak{g}
with
\mu(K)<\infty
for all compact sets
K\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
.
Then the space of continuous functions with compact support
C_{c}(\mathfrak{g})
is dense in
L^{p}(\mathfrak{g})
for all
p\in[1,\infty)
, see Appendix E.8 of
[
18
]
.

We will work with a basis of sets which enables us to talk about a doubling property of the measure
\mu
with respect to this basis.
We therefore make the following assumption, which is met by the groups
\mathbb{R}
,
\mathbb{Z}
, the torus
\mathbb{T}
and finite products of these groups.

Assumption 4.1
.

Suppose that
\mathfrak{g}
is a locally compact abelian group equipped with a nontrivial and regular measure
\mu
, such that
\mu(K)<\infty
for all compact
K\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
.
Furthermore, assume that there is a local base of
0\in\mathfrak{g}
consisting of relatively compact measurable neighbourhoods
U_{k}
,
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, symmetric in the sense
U_{k}=-U_{k}
, such that

(i)

\bigcup\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}U_{k}=\mathfrak{g}
,

(ii)

U_{k}\subseteq U_{m}
, if
k\leq m
,

(iii)

there exist a positive constant
A
and a non-decreasing mapping
\theta:\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{Z}
such that for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
and all
x\in\mathfrak{g}

\displaystyle k

\displaystyle<\theta(k),

\displaystyle 2U_{k}

\displaystyle\subseteq U_{\theta(k)},

\displaystyle\mu(x+U_{\theta(k)})

\displaystyle\leq A\mu(x+U_{k}).

Observe that necessarily
A\geq 1
because
U_{k}\subseteq U_{\theta(k)}
.

In
[
19
]
, it was shown that for
p\in(1,\infty)
weights with
[{w}]_{p}<\infty
are exactly those weights such that the maximal operator is bounded in
L_{{w}}^{p}(\mathfrak{g})
.
Here, we extend this result to the endpoint
p=\infty
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Assume
p\in(1,\infty]
and let
{w}
be a weight on
\mathfrak{g}
.
Then
\mathcal{M}_{\mathfrak{g}}
is bounded in
L_{{w}}^{p}(\mathfrak{g})
if and only if
[{w}]_{p}<\infty
.
Moreover, there exists
\mathsf{c}_{A}(p)\in\mathbb{R}
such that the bound
\|\mathcal{M}_{\mathfrak{g}}\|_{L^{p}_{w}(\mathfrak{g})\to L^{p}_{w}(\mathfrak{g})}\leq\mathsf{c}_{A}(p)[{w}]_{p}^{p^{\prime}}
is valid.

Proof.

For
p\in(1,\infty)
, the proof can be found in Theorem 1.2 of
[
19
]
, with the explicit bound proved in Theorem 1.3 of
[
16
]
, where we recall that
[{w}^{p}]_{A_{p}}=[{w}]_{p}^{p}
.
For
p=\infty
, Proposition
2.1
(v)
gives the result with
\mathsf{c}_{A}(\infty)=1
.
∎

Definition 4.3
.

Let
{\mathfrak{g}}
be a locally compact abelian group with Haar measure
\mu
subject to Assumption
4.1
.
We say that
f\in{L^{\infty}}({\mathfrak{g}})
is rapidly decaying and write
f\in{L^{\infty}_{\mathrm{dec}}}(\mathfrak{g})
if

\displaystyle(\forall n\in\mathbb{N})(\exists c_{n}>0)(\forall k\in\mathbb{N})\quad\|A^{nk}f\|_{{L^{\infty}}({\mathfrak{g}}\setminus U_{\theta^{k}(0)})}\leq c_{n}.

(22)

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
we define
\rho_{n}:L^{\infty}(\mathfrak{g})\to\mathbb{R}
via

\displaystyle\rho_{n}(f):=\max\{\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(U_{0})},\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\|A^{nk}f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathfrak{g}\setminus U_{\theta^{k}(0)})}\}.

It follows immediately from the definition that for
f\in{L^{\infty}_{\mathrm{dec}}}({\mathfrak{g}})
it holds
{f(\cdot-y)\in{L^{\infty}_{\mathrm{dec}}}({\mathfrak{g}})}
for all
y\in{\mathfrak{g}}
, and
{L^{\infty}_{\mathrm{dec}}}({\mathfrak{g}}){L^{\infty}}({\mathfrak{g}})\subseteq{L^{\infty}_{\mathrm{dec}}}({\mathfrak{g}})
, so that
{L^{\infty}_{\mathrm{dec}}}({\mathfrak{g}})
is closed under pointwise multiplication.

Lemma 4.4
.

We collect the following properties of
{L^{\infty}_{\mathrm{dec}}}({\mathfrak{g}})
.

(i)

For all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
p\in(\frac{1}{n},\infty]
, and weights
{w}
with
[{w}]_{p,r}<\infty
,
r\in[(np)^{\prime}/n,\infty]
, there holds

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{p}_{{w}}(\mathfrak{g})}\leq A[{w}]_{p,r}^{(np)^{\prime}}\|{w}\|_{L^{p}(U_{0})}\rho_{n}(f),

so that
{L^{\infty}_{\mathrm{dec}}}({\mathfrak{g}})\subseteq L^{p}_{{w}}({\mathfrak{g}})
.
A particular instance is
n=2
,
p\in[1,\infty)
and
[{w}]_{p}<\infty
.
Furthermore for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
there is a constant
c_{n}>0
such that for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
it holds

\displaystyle\|A^{nk}f\|_{L^{1}({\mathfrak{g}}\setminus U_{\theta^{k}(0)})}\leq c_{n}.

(ii)

{L^{\infty}_{\mathrm{dec}}}({\mathfrak{g}})
constitutes a convolution algebra,
i.e.
, for
f,g\in{L^{\infty}_{\mathrm{dec}}}(\mathfrak{g})
it holds true that
{f\ast g\in{L^{\infty}_{\mathrm{dec}}}(\mathfrak{g})}
, where

\displaystyle(f\ast g)(x):=\int_{{\mathfrak{g}}}{f(x-y)g(y)}\,\text{d}{\mu(y)}.

Proof.

All points except the inclusion
{L^{\infty}_{\mathrm{dec}}}({\mathfrak{g}})\subseteq{L^{q}_{{w}}}({\mathfrak{g}})
can be found in
[
15
]
.
In order to prove the inclusion, fix
q\in[1,\infty]
, a weight
{w}
with
[{w}]_{q}<\infty
, and
f\in{L^{\infty}_{\mathrm{dec}}}({\mathfrak{g}})
.
Define
P:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathbb{R}
via

\displaystyle P(x):=\begin{cases}A^{-nk}&\text{if }x\in U_{\theta^{k}(0)}\setminus U_{\theta^{k-1}(0)},k\geq 1,\\
1&\text{if }x\in U_{0}.\end{cases}

We observe that the definition of
\rho_{n}
ensures that

\displaystyle\|f\cdot P^{-1}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathfrak{g})}\leq\rho_{n}(f).

We now argue that there is
C>0
such that
\sqrt[n]{P(x)}\leq A{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathfrak{g}}\psi_{0}(x)
for all
x\in\mathfrak{g}
, where
\psi_{0}\in L^{1}(\mathfrak{g})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathfrak{g})
is defined as
\psi_{0}:=\chi_{U_{0}}
.
This is clear for
x\in U_{0}
by
A\geq 1
, so that we may assume
x\in U_{\theta^{k}(0)}\setminus U_{\theta^{k-1}(0)}
for some
k\geq 1
.
Then
U_{0}\subseteq x+U_{\theta^{k+1}(0)}
in light of

\displaystyle U_{0}=x-x+U_{0}\subseteq x+U_{\theta^{k}(0)}+U_{\theta^{k}(0)}\subseteq x+U_{\theta^{k+1}(0)}.

Since
\mu(x+U_{\theta^{k+1}(0)})\leq A^{k+1}\mu(U_{0})
by translation invariance of the Haar measure, we obtain

\displaystyle\sqrt[n]{P(x)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{A^{k}\mu(U_{0})}\int_{U_{0}}1\,\mathrm{d}\mu=\frac{1}{A^{k}\mu(U_{0})}\int_{x+U_{\theta^{k+1}(k_{0})}}\psi_{0}\,\mathrm{d}\mu

\displaystyle\leq\frac{A}{\mu(x+U_{\theta^{k+1}(k_{0})})}\int_{x+U_{\theta^{k+1}(k_{0})}}\psi_{0}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\leq A{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathfrak{g}}\psi_{0}(x).

With
{v}:=\sqrt[n]{{w}}
it thus follows

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{p}_{w}(\mathfrak{g})}

\displaystyle\leq\|f\cdot P^{-1}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathfrak{g})}\|P\|_{L^{p}_{w}(\mathfrak{g})}\leq\rho_{n}(f)\|\sqrt[n]{P}\|_{L^{np}_{v}(\mathfrak{g})}^{n}\leq A\rho_{n}(f)\|{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathfrak{g}}\psi_{0}\|_{L^{np}_{v}(\mathfrak{g})}^{n}

\displaystyle\leq A\rho_{n}(f)\|{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathfrak{g}}\|_{L^{np}_{v}(\mathfrak{g})\to L^{np}_{v}(\mathfrak{g})}^{n}\|\psi_{0}\|_{L^{np}_{v}(\mathfrak{g})}^{n}=A\rho_{n}(f)\|{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathfrak{g}}\|_{L^{np}_{v}(\mathfrak{g})\to L^{np}_{v}(\mathfrak{g})}^{n}\|{w}\|_{L^{p}(U_{0})}.

This yields the assertion, since
\|{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathfrak{g}}\|_{L^{np}_{v}(\mathfrak{g})\to L^{np}_{v}(\mathfrak{g})}^{n}\leq[{v}]_{np}^{n(np)^{\prime}}=[{w}]_{p,(np)^{\prime}/n}^{(np)^{\prime}}\leq[{w}]_{p,r}^{(np)^{\prime}}
by Proposition
4.2
and the general properties of weights in Proposition
2.1
.
∎

We introduce the Fourier transform
{\mathcal{F}}:L^{1}(\mathfrak{g})\to C_{0}(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}})
, where
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}
is the Pontryagin dual group of
\mathfrak{g}
.
Then
{\mathcal{F}}:L^{1}(\mathfrak{g})\cap L^{2}(\mathfrak{g})\to C_{0}(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}})
extends to an isometric isomorphism
{\mathcal{F}}:L^{2}(\mathfrak{g})\to L^{2}(\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}})
.
We usually write
\hat{f}:={\mathcal{F}}f
for
f\in L^{1}(\mathfrak{g})
.
We will also use the notation
{\mathcal{F}}L^{1}(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}):=\{\hat{f}:f\in L^{1}(\mathfrak{g})\}
.
For
m\in L^{\infty}(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}})
and
f\in L^{2}(\mathfrak{g})
we introduce
T_{m}f:={\mathcal{F}}^{-1}m\hat{f}
.
For
p\in[1,\infty]
,
m\in L^{\infty}(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}})
, and a weight
{w}
on
\mathfrak{g}
with
[{w}]_{p}<\infty
we write
m\in M_{p,{w}}(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}})
if
T_{m}
originally defined on
L^{p}_{w}(\mathfrak{g})\cap L^{2}(\mathfrak{g})
extends to a bounded linear operator on
L^{p}_{w}(\mathfrak{g})
.
In that case we write
\|m\|_{M_{p,{w}}(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}}:=\|T_{m}\|_{L^{p}_{w}(\mathfrak{g})\to L^{p}_{w}(\mathfrak{g})}
.
Consider

\displaystyle A(\mathfrak{g})

\displaystyle:=\{f\in L^{\infty}_{\mathrm{dec}}(\mathfrak{g})\ \lvert\ \hat{f}\text{ has compact support}\}.

In order to have good approximation results, we assume the following on
\mathfrak{g}
.
Observe that by Lemma
4.4
we have
A(\mathfrak{g})\subseteq L^{p}_{w}(\mathfrak{g})
for all
p\in(1,\infty)
and all weights
{w}
with
[{w}]_{p}<\infty
.

Assumption 4.5
.

There exist
c\in\mathbb{R}
and a sequence
(\varphi_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq A(\mathfrak{g})
such that for all
f\in L^{\infty}_{\mathrm{dec}}(\mathfrak{g})
it holds
\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}|\varphi_{k}\ast f|\leq c{\mathcal{M}}f
,
0\leq\widehat{\varphi}_{k}\leq 1
and
\widehat{\varphi}_{k}\to 1
locally uniformly.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a group subject to Assumptions
4.1
and
4.5
.
Then for all
p\in(1,\infty)
and weights
{w}
with
[{w}]_{p}<\infty
, it holds that
A(\mathfrak{g})
is a dense subset of
L^{p}_{w}(\mathfrak{g})
.

Proof.

Let
f\in L^{p}_{w}(\mathfrak{g})
.
Since
{w}^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mu
is a regular measure on
\mathfrak{g}
by dominated convergence,
C_{c}(\mathfrak{g})
is dense in
L^{p}_{w}(\mathfrak{g})
, see
[
18
, Appendix E.8]
.
Hence, it suffices to assume that
f\in C_{c}(\mathfrak{g})\subseteq L^{\infty}_{\mathrm{dec}}(\mathfrak{g})
.
By assumption there are
c\in\mathbb{R}
and
(\varphi_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq A(\mathfrak{g})
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}|\varphi_{k}\ast f|\leq c{\mathcal{M}}f\quad\text{and}\quad\widehat{\varphi}_{k}\to 1\quad\text{locally uniformly.}

Observe that
\varphi_{k}\ast f\in A(\mathfrak{g})
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
, since
L^{\infty}_{\mathrm{dec}}(\mathfrak{g})
is a convolution algebra and
{\mathcal{F}}[\varphi_{k}\ast f]=\widehat{\varphi}_{k}\widehat{f}
is compactly supported.
Moreover
\|\varphi_{k}\ast f-f\|_{L^{2}(\mathfrak{g})}=\|(\widehat{\varphi}_{k}-1)\widehat{f}\|_{L^{2}(\mathfrak{g})}\to 0
by Plancherel’s theorem and dominated convergence, so that in particular
\varphi_{k}\ast f\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mu}}{{\longrightarrow}}f
in measure.
Since
[{w}]_{p}<\infty
, we have
{\mathcal{M}}f\in L^{p}_{w}(\mathfrak{g})
by Proposition
4.2
.
Thus the result follows by dominated convergence.
∎

From now on, we will assume that
\mathfrak{g}
satisfies Assumptions
4.1
and
4.5
.

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
, and assume that
{w}
is a weight on
\mathfrak{g}
with
[{w}]_{p}<\infty
.
Let
m\in L^{\infty}(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}})
.
Then
m\in M_{p,{w}}(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}})
if and only if there is
c\in\mathbb{R}
such that for all
f,g\in A(\mathfrak{g})
we have

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}}m(\gamma)\widehat{f}(\gamma)\widehat{g}(\gamma)\mathrm{d}\gamma\right|\leq c\|f\|_{L_{w}^{p}(\mathfrak{g})}\|g\|_{L_{\sigma}^{p^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{g})},

(23)

where
\sigma(x):={w}^{{-1}}(-x)
.
In this case
\|m\|_{M_{p,{w}}(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}})}
is the smallest constant such that (
23
) is valid.

Proof.

Let
m\in M_{p,{w}}(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}})
.
By Parseval’s theorem and Hölder’s inequality we have for all
f,g\in A(\mathfrak{g})

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}}m(\gamma)\widehat{f}(\gamma)\widehat{g}(\gamma)\mathrm{d}\gamma\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{\mathfrak{g}}T_{m}f(x)g(-x)\mathrm{d}x\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|T_{m}f\|_{L_{w}^{p}(\mathfrak{g})}\|g\|_{L_{\sigma}^{p^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{g})}

\displaystyle\leq\|m\|_{M_{p,{w}}(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}})}\|f\|_{L_{w}^{p}(\mathfrak{g})}\|g\|_{L_{\sigma}^{p^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{g})}.

This shows estimate (
23
) with
c\leq\|m\|_{M_{p,{w}}(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}})}
.

Conversely, let
c>0
be such that estimate (
23
) is valid and let
f,g\in A(\mathfrak{g})
.
Write
\tilde{g}(x):=g(-x)
.
By Parseval’s formula it holds

\displaystyle\int_{\mathfrak{g}}T_{m}f(x)g(x)\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}}m(\gamma)\widehat{f}(\gamma)\widehat{\tilde{g}}(\gamma)\,\mathrm{d}\gamma,

and thus

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathfrak{g}}T_{m}f(x)g(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\right|\leq c\|f\|_{L^{p}_{w}(\mathfrak{g})}\|\tilde{g}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}_{\sigma}(\mathfrak{g})}=c\|f\|_{L^{p}_{w}(\mathfrak{g})}\|g\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}_{{w}^{-1}}(\mathfrak{g})}

for all
f,g\in A(\mathfrak{g})
.
Since
[{w}^{-1}]_{p^{\prime}}=[{w}]_{p}<\infty
,
A(\mathfrak{g})
is dense in
L^{p}_{w}(\mathfrak{g})
and
L^{p^{\prime}}_{{w}^{-1}}(\mathfrak{g})
by Lemma
4.6
.
It follows that
T_{m}
is bounded in
L^{p}_{w}(\mathfrak{g})
with
\|T_{m}\|_{L^{p}_{w}(\mathfrak{g})\to L^{p}_{w}(\mathfrak{g})}\leq c
.
This shows the result.
∎

Definition 4.8
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{h}
be locally compact abelian groups. Assume that
\Phi:\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}\to\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}
is a continuous homomorphism. By virtue of Pontryagin’s duality theorem we may define the
dual homomorphism of

\Phi
as the function
\widehat{\Phi}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{h}
that satisfies

\displaystyle(\widehat{\Phi}(x),\chi)=(x,\Phi(\chi))\qquad\mbox{for all }x\in\mathfrak{g},\chi\in\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}.

(24)

For
f\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathfrak{g})
and

we write
\tau_{z}f:=f(\cdot-z)
.

Proposition 4.9
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{h}
be locally compact abelian groups subject to Assumptions
4.1
and
4.5
.
Let
\Phi:\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}\to\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}
be a continuous homomorphism. Let
p\in(1,\infty)
and suppose that
{w}:\mathfrak{h}\to(0,\infty)
is a weight such that
[{w}]_{p}<\infty
,
[{w}\circ\widehat{\Phi}]_{p}<\infty
, and there is

with
\|\cdot\|_{M_{p,\tau_{u}{w}\circ\widehat{\Phi}}(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}})}\leq c\|\cdot\|_{M_{p,{w}\circ\widehat{\Phi}}(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}})}
for all
u\in\mathfrak{h}
.
If
m\in M_{p,{w}\circ\widehat{\Phi}}(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}})\cap C(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}})
, then there holds

and

\displaystyle\|m\circ\Phi\|_{M_{p,{w}}(\widehat{\mathfrak{h}})}\leq c\|m\|_{M_{p,{w}\circ\widehat{\Phi}}(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}})}.

Remark 4.10
.

(i)

It always holds
c\geq 1
. Indeed, this follows from
\tau_{u}=\id
for
u:=0\in\mathfrak{h}
.

(ii)

p\in(1,\infty)
and let
{w}:\mathfrak{g}\to(0,\infty)
be a weight function with
[{w}]_{p}<\infty
. Then for all

and

we have
\|m\|_{M_{p,\tau_{z^{\prime}}{w}}(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}})}=\|m\|_{M_{p,\tau_{z}{w}}(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}})}
.
Indeed, since
T_{m}(\tau_{z}f)=\tau_{z}(T_{m}f)
, there holds

\displaystyle\|T_{m}f\|_{L_{\tau_{z}{w}}^{p}(\mathfrak{g})}

\displaystyle=\|\tau_{z^{\prime}-z}(T_{m}f)\|_{L_{\tau_{z^{\prime}}{w}}^{p}(\mathfrak{g})}=\|T_{m}(\tau_{z^{\prime}-z}f)\|_{L_{\tau_{z^{\prime}}{w}}^{p}(\mathfrak{g})}

\displaystyle\leq\|m\|_{M_{p,\tau_{z^{\prime}}{w}}(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}})}\|\tau_{z^{\prime}-z}f\|_{L_{\tau_{z^{\prime}}{w}}^{p}(\mathfrak{g})}=\|m\|_{M_{p,\tau_{z^{\prime}}{w}}(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}})}\|f\|_{L_{\tau_{z}{w}}^{p}(\mathfrak{g})},

so that the claim follows by symmetry in

and
z^{\prime}
.

(iii)

In particular, if
\widehat{\Phi}
is surjective the assumption

is fulfilled with

. Indeed, in that case there is for all

some

such that

and hence
\tau_{u}{w}\circ\widehat{\Phi}=\tau_{y}({w}\circ\widehat{\Phi})
, so that choosing
z:=0
and

Proof of Theorem
4.9
.

By the same reasoning as in Lemma B.1.2 in
[
6
]
it suffices to assume
m\in{\mathcal{F}}L^{1}(\mathfrak{g})\cap L^{1}(\mathfrak{g})
.
Let
h,k\in A(\mathfrak{h})
.
By Proposition
4.7
and Lemma
4.11
it suffices to establish

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathfrak{g}}\widehat{m}(x)(h\ast k)(\widehat{\Phi}(x))\,\mathrm{d}x\right|\leq\|m\|_{M_{p,{w}\circ\widehat{\Phi}}(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}})}\|h\|_{L^{p}_{w}(\mathfrak{h})}\|k\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}_{\sigma}(\mathfrak{h})}.

For this purpose, let
\varepsilon>0
.
Since
m\cdot(h\ast k)\circ\widehat{\Phi}\in L^{1}(\mathfrak{g})
, there is a compact
K\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
such that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathfrak{g}\setminus K}|(\widehat{m}(x)(h\ast k)(\widehat{\Phi}(x))|\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\varepsilon\|m\|_{M_{p,{w}\circ\widehat{\Phi}}(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}})}\|h\|_{L^{p}_{w}(\mathfrak{h})}\|k\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}_{\sigma}(\mathfrak{h})}.

Hence it suffices to construct a measurable function
v:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathbb{C}
with
\mathds{1}_{K}\leq v\leq 1
such that

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathfrak{g}}\widehat{m}(x)(h\ast k)(\widehat{\Phi}(x))v(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\right|\leq(1+\varepsilon)\|m\|_{M_{p,{w}\circ\widehat{\Phi}}(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}})}\|h\|_{L^{p}_{w}(\mathfrak{h})}\|k\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}_{\sigma}(\mathfrak{h})}.

(25)

By Theorem 2.6 in
[
7
]
, there are
f,g:G\to\mathbb{C}
with
0\leq f\ast g\leq 1
,
f\ast g=1
on
K
and

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathfrak{g})}\|g\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{g})}\leq 1+\varepsilon.

(26)

Observe that

\displaystyle(h\ast k)(\widehat{\Phi}(x))

\displaystyle(f\ast g)(x)=\int_{\mathfrak{h}}h(\widehat{\Phi}(x)-u)k(u)\mathrm{d}u\int_{\mathfrak{g}}f(x-y)g(y)\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathfrak{g}}\int_{\mathfrak{h}}h(\widehat{\Phi}(x-y)-u)k(u+\widehat{\Phi}(y))f(x-y)g(y)\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathfrak{h}}\left[(\tau_{u}h\circ\widehat{\Phi})f\right]\ast\left[(\tau_{-u}k\circ\widehat{\Phi})g\right](x)\mathrm{d}u.

Hence we may recast the left-hand side of (
25
) with
v:=f\ast g
as

\displaystyle\int_{\mathfrak{h}}\left|\int_{\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}}m(\gamma)\mathcal{F}\left[(\tau_{u}h\circ\widehat{\Phi})f\right](\gamma)\cdot\mathcal{F}\left[(\tau_{-u}k\circ\widehat{\Phi})g\right](\gamma)\mathrm{d}\gamma\right|\mathrm{d}u,

which by Proposition
4.7
is estimated by

\displaystyle\int_{\mathfrak{h}}\|m\|_{M_{p,\tau_{u}{w}\circ\widehat{\Phi}}(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}})}\|(\tau_{u}h\circ\widehat{\Phi})f\|_{L_{\tau_{u}{w}\circ\widehat{\Phi}}^{p}(\mathfrak{g})}\|(\tau_{-u}k\circ\widehat{\Phi})g\|_{L_{\tau_{-u}\sigma\circ\widehat{\Phi}}^{p^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{g})}\mathrm{d}u,

where we have used that

\displaystyle(\tau_{u}{w}\circ\widehat{\Phi})^{-1}(-x)

\displaystyle={w}^{-1}(\widehat{\Phi}(-x)-u)={w}^{-1}(-(\widehat{\Phi}(x)+u))

\displaystyle=\sigma(\widehat{\Phi}(x)+u)=(\tau_{-u}\sigma\circ\widehat{\Phi})(x).

Relying on the hypothesis
\|m\|_{M_{p,\tau_{u}{w}\circ\widehat{\Phi}}(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}})}\leq c\|m\|_{M_{p,{w}\circ\widehat{\Phi}}(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}})}
for all
u\in\mathfrak{h}
, we arrive at

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle\widehat{m}(x)(h\ast k)(\widehat{\Phi}(x))(f\ast g)(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\Big|

\displaystyle\leq c\|m\|_{M_{p,{w}\circ\widehat{\Phi}}(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}})}\int_{\mathfrak{h}}\|(\tau_{u}h\circ\widehat{\Phi})f\|_{L_{\tau_{u}{w}\circ\widehat{\Phi}}^{p}(\mathfrak{g})}\|(\tau_{-u}k\circ\widehat{\Phi})g\|_{L_{\tau_{-u}\sigma\circ\widehat{\Phi}}^{p^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{g})}\mathrm{d}u

\displaystyle\leq c\|m\|_{M_{p,{w}\circ\widehat{\Phi}(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}})}}\left(\int_{\mathfrak{h}}\|(\tau_{u}h\circ\widehat{\Phi})f\|_{L_{\tau_{u}{w}\circ\widehat{\Phi}}^{p}(\mathfrak{g})}^{p}\,\mathrm{d}u\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\int_{\mathfrak{h}}\|(\tau_{-u}k\circ\widehat{\Phi})g\|_{L_{\tau_{-u}\sigma\circ\widehat{\Phi}}^{p^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{g})}^{p^{\prime}}\mathrm{d}u\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle=c\|m\|_{M_{p,{w}\circ\widehat{\Phi}}(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}})}\left(\int_{\mathfrak{h}}\int_{\mathfrak{g}}\left|h(\widehat{\Phi}(x)-u)f(x){w}(\widehat{\Phi}(x)-u)\right|^{p}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}u\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\left(\int_{\mathfrak{h}}\int_{\mathfrak{g}}\left|k(\widehat{\Phi}(x)+u)g(x)\sigma(\widehat{\Phi}(x)+u)\right|^{p^{\prime}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}u\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle=c\|m\|_{M_{p,{w}\circ\widehat{\Phi}}(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}})}\|h\|_{L_{w}^{p}(\mathfrak{h})}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathfrak{g})}\|k\|_{L_{\sigma}^{p^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{h})}\|g\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{g})},

where we have used Fubini and the translation and reflection invariance of the Haar measure in the last step.
By (
26
), this is the desired estimate (
25
) with

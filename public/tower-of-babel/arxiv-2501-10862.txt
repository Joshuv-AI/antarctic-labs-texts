Symmetries and Anomalies of Hamiltonian Staggered Fermions
Simon Catterall, Arnab Pradhan, Abhishek Samlodia

Abstract
We review the shift (translation) and time reversal symmetries of Hamiltonian staggered fermions and their connection to continuum symmetries concentrating in particular on the case of massless fermions and (3+1) dimensions. We construct operators using the staggered fields that implement these symmetries on finite lattices. We show that shifts composed of an odd multiple of the elementary shift anti-commute with time reversal and are related to continuum axial transformations. We argue that the presence of these non-trivial commutators implies the existence of exact lattice ’t Hooft anomalies.

II
Hamiltonian staggered fermions

The continuum Dirac Hamiltonian is given by

H=\int d^{3}x\,\overline{\Psi}(x,t)\,\left(i\gamma^{i}\partial_{i}+m\right)\Psi(x,t)

(1)

where

are spatial indices running from

We indicate a shift of the site

To obtain the staggered Hamiltonian, we first perform a unitary
transformation on
\Psi
to a new basis
\chi
as follows:

\displaystyle\Psi(x,t)

\displaystyle=\alpha^{x}\chi(x,t)

\displaystyle\Psi^{\dagger}(x,t)

\displaystyle=\chi^{\dagger}(x,t)(\alpha^{x})^{\dagger}

(5)

where

\alpha^{x}=\alpha_{1}^{x_{1}}\alpha_{2}^{x_{2}}\ldots\alpha_{d}^{x_{d}}

(6)

The Hamiltonian in this basis is then given by

H=\sum_{x,y}\chi^{\dagger}(x,t)\left[i\eta_{i}(x)\Delta_{i}(x,y)+m\epsilon(x)\delta_{xy}\beta\right]\chi(y,t)

(7)

where
\eta_{i}(x)=(-1)^{x_{1}+x_{2}+...+x_{i-1}}
and
\epsilon(x)=\left(-1\right)^{\sum_{i}^{d}x_{i}}
is the spatial site parity. Unlike the analogous situation
in Euclidean space the resultant operator is not proportional to the unit matrix
in spinor space. So we cannot stagger the
field by merely discarding all but one component of
\chi

as one would do in that case. Instead, we can go back to
the unitary transformation in eqn.
II

decompose
\Psi(x)
into two components
\Psi_{\pm}(x)
that
are eigenstates of
\beta

\Psi(x)=\Psi_{+}(x)+\Psi_{-}(x)

(8)

where
P_{\pm}=\frac{1}{2}\left(1\pm\beta\right)
. The unitary transformation
becomes

\Psi_{\pm}(x)=P_{\pm}\alpha^{x}\chi(x)=\alpha^{x}\frac{1}{2}(1\pm\beta\epsilon(x))\chi(x)

(9)

Since
\beta
anticommutes with
\alpha_{i}
the kinetic operator
now couples
\Psi^{\dagger}_{+}
to
\Psi_{-}
and vice versa. The mass term is
diagonal in this basis

\Psi^{\dagger}\beta\Psi=\Psi_{+}^{\dagger}\Psi_{+}-\Psi_{-}^{\dagger}\Psi_{-}

(10)

The fields
\Psi_{+}(x)

\chi_{\rm even}(x)=\frac{1}{2}(1+\epsilon(x))\chi(x)
and the fields
\Psi_{-}(x)
to odd sites
\chi_{\rm odd}(x)=\frac{1}{2}(1-\epsilon(x))\chi(x)
. The mass term becomes

\epsilon(x)\Psi^{\dagger}(x)\Psi(x)

to a single component field on each

the final staggered Hamiltonian

\displaystyle\sum_{x,y}\chi^{\dagger}(x,t)i\eta_{i}(x)\Delta_{i}(x,y)\chi(y,t)

(12)

The canonical anticommutators of the staggered fields are given by

\{\chi^{\dagger}(x,t),\chi(x^{\prime},t)\}=\delta(x,x^{\prime})

(13)

\displaystyle\quad{\rm with\;all\;other\;anticommutators\;vanishing}

while the equation of motion is

\displaystyle i\frac{\partial\chi(x)}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=[H,\chi(x)]

It is not hard to verify that

\frac{\partial^{2}\chi(x)}{\partial t^{2}}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i}\left[\chi(x+2i)+\chi(x-2i)-2\chi(x)\right]+m^{2}\chi(x)

Notice the appearance of a discrete Laplacian operator on a block lattice with twice
the lattice spacing. This equation implies that there are
2^{d}

degenerate solutions for every site on the block
lattice. These solutions can be constructed by performing

Let us try to understand how this works in more detail. For simplicity let us
restrict the following discussion to odd
d
.
The naive fermion on a d-dimensional spatial lattice
gives rise to
2^{d}
Dirac fermions in the continuum limit because of doubling. After staggering one expects the continuum theory to correspond to
2^{\frac{d-1}{2}}
Dirac fermions with a total of

2^{d}

staggered fields
\chi(x)

living at the corners of the unit hypercube

\Psi(2x)=\sum_{\{b\}}\chi(x+b)\alpha^{x+b}

(17)

where
\{b\}

2^{d}

The continuum spinors can then be read off from the columns of this matrix as
the lattice spacing is sent to zero.

Since a single Dirac fermion has
2^{\frac{d+1}{2}}
components the most general square matrix built from such spinors will possess

2^{\frac{d+1}{2}}\times 2^{\frac{d+1}{2}}=2\times 2^{d}
elements.
This is twice the number of points in the spatial cube
employed in our Hamiltonian construction. To achieve this doubled set of Dirac
fermions one can employ a second staggered fermion
\chi^{\prime}
and expand it on the additional matrix basis given by
\beta\alpha^{x}

\Psi^{\prime}(2x)=\sum_{\{b\}}\chi^{\prime}(x+b)\beta\alpha^{x+b}

(18)

By adding
\Psi
and
\Psi^{\prime}
we can then build a theory
containing
2^{\frac{d+1}{2}}
Dirac fermions. In fact, this is
the number of fermions that arises in Euclidean
formulations of staggered fermions where time is also
discretized.
To achieve the number of fermions seen in the minimal Hamiltonian construction
comprising just
\Psi
, one needs to project the Euclidean theory by including only Euclidean fermions of a given site parity on the
spacetime
lattice. This can only be done in the massless case and the resulting
formulation is called
reduced

fermions in the literature - see eg.
van den Doel and Smit 1983
;
Catterall 2016
;
Catterall 2024
.
Notice that for
d
odd the matrix
P=i\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{d}=\gamma_{5}
commutes
with
\Psi
and anticommutes with
\Psi^{\prime}
. Thus

\Psi
is an eigenstate of the
twisted
chiral operator
\gamma_{5}\otimes\gamma_{5}
which acts on the left and right of the matrix fermion with eigenvalue
+1

\Psi^{\prime}
is the other
eigenstate with eigenvalue
-1

Finally, we note that
the Hamiltonian is also clearly invariant under a

symmetry

\chi(x)\to e^{i\theta}\chi(x)
which will play an important role in our later
discussion. We now turn to a discussion of important additional symmetries of the
staggered Hamiltonian.

III
Continuum flavor and lattice shift symmetries

In this section we will focus on staggered fermions in
3+1
dimensions but the arguments can be easily generalized to any dimension.
We start from a chiral basis
for the Dirac gamma matrices given by

\gamma_{\mu}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&\sigma_{\mu}\\
\bar{\sigma}_{\mu}&0\end{array}\right)

(19)

where
\sigma_{\mu}=(I,\sigma_{i})
and
\bar{\sigma}_{\mu}=(I,-\sigma_{i})
.
In this case

\displaystyle\alpha_{i}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}-\sigma_{i}&0\\
0&\sigma_{i}\end{array}\right)

and the matrix
\Psi
in eqn.
17
then takes the form

\Psi=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\psi_{R}&0\\

(23)

where

\displaystyle\psi_{R}

\displaystyle=\chi(x)I-\chi(x+i)\sigma_{i}-\frac{i}{2}\chi(x+i+j)\epsilon_{ijk}\sigma_{k}

\displaystyle+iI\chi(x+1+2+3)

\displaystyle\psi_{L}

\displaystyle=\chi(x)I+\chi(x+i)\sigma_{i}-\frac{i}{2}\chi(x+i+j)\epsilon_{ijk}\sigma_{k}

\displaystyle-iI\chi(x+1+2+3)

(24)

In the naive continuum limit the massless staggered field thus gives
rise to doublets of left
and right-handed Weyl fields transforming under two independent
SU(2)
symmetries. These
symmetries protect the effective action of the

from developing bilinear mass terms

3

A Majorana mass for either left or right handed doublet vanishes identically
. In addition the theory is invariant under both the vector

symmetry discussed
earlier and a
singlet axial symmetry

in which the
\psi_{R}
and
\psi_{L}
carry

Axial-flavor rotations act by right multiplication of the continuum matrix fermion given in eqn.
23
by the matrix
e^{i\theta_{A}\alpha_{A}}
where the hermitian basis
\alpha_{A}
is given
in terms of products of the individual

matrices:

\alpha_{A}=\{\alpha_{i},i\alpha_{i}\alpha_{j},i\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\alpha_{3}\}\quad{\rm where}\;i=1\ldots 3

It can be seen that this yields
the flavor group
SU(2)\times SU(2)\times U_{A}(1)
as described above.

Staggered fermions, being discretizations of Kähler-Dirac fermions

, are invariant under a twisted

;
Iwanenko and Landau 1928
;
Kilcup and Sharpe 1987
;
Golterman and Smit 1984
;
Catterall and Pradhan 2024

We will now show that this induces a unit translation or

\displaystyle\Psi(2x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\{b\}}\chi(x+b)\alpha^{x+b}i\alpha_{j}

where one must anticommute the
\alpha_{j}

\xi_{j}(x+b)
.
The net effect is clearly to just produce an elementary

\displaystyle\chi(x)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle S_{j}}}{{\to}}i\xi_{j}(x)\chi(x+j)

\displaystyle\chi^{\dagger}(x)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle S_{j}}}{{\to}}-i\xi_{j}(x)\chi^{\dagger}(x+j)

(28)

where
\xi_{j}(x)=\left(-1\right)^{\sum_{k=j+1}^{d}x_{k}}
. It is straightforward to examine
the invariance of the Hamiltonian under such a shift

\displaystyle H\to

\displaystyle\sum_{x,i}i\eta_{i}(x)\chi^{\dagger}(x+k)\xi_{k}(x)\xi_{k}(x+i)\frac{1}{2}\left[\chi(x+i+k)-\right.

\displaystyle\left.\quad\chi(x-i+k)\right]+\sum_{x}m\epsilon(x)\xi_{k}(x)^{2}\chi^{\dagger}(x+k)\chi(x+k)

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle\sum_{x,i}i\eta_{i}(x-k)\chi^{\dagger}(x)\xi_{k}(x-k)\xi_{k}(x+i-k)\frac{1}{2}\left[\chi(x+i)-\right.

\displaystyle\left.\quad\chi(x-i)\right]+\sum_{x}m\epsilon(x-k)\chi^{\dagger}(x)\chi(x)

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle\sum_{x}i\eta(x)\chi^{\dagger}(x)\frac{1}{2}\left[\chi(x+i)-\chi(x-i)\right]

\displaystyle-\sum_{x}m\epsilon(x)\chi^{\dagger}(x)\chi(x)

(29)

where we have used the result
\eta_{i}(k)\xi_{k}(i)=1
. Notice that the Hamiltonian
is only invariant under
S_{k}
if

.
It should be clear
that elementary shifts can be applied consecutively to yield additional
symmetries. For example
the double shift
S_{ij}
:

\chi(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle S_{ij}}}{{\to}}S_{i}S_{j}\,\chi(x)=-\xi_{j}(x)\xi_{i}(x+j)\chi(x+i+j)\quad i\neq j

(30)

It is trivial to see that
S_{i}S_{j}=-S_{j}S_{i}
. Thus, just as

is
associated with the generator
\alpha_{k}
the properties of the

S_{ij}
mimic those of the generator
i\alpha_{i}\alpha_{j}
. It is
important to notice that in the case of the double
shift the Hamiltonian
is invariant even for non-zero mass. This is consistent with the vector character of the transformations induced by
\alpha_{i}\alpha_{j}
and we can conclude
that any even number of shifts will
correspond to vector symmetries of the continuum theory.
Conversely the Hamiltonian is only invariant under an odd shift if the mass is
zero. An explicit example of this is the
the triple shift
S_{1}S_{2}S_{3}
which corresponds to
the generator of the axial
U_{A}(1)
symmetry
\gamma_{5}=i\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\alpha_{3}
.

It is important to recognize that the
connection between lattice shifts and continuum flavor is only
one to one up to ordinary translations. For example,
a double shift along the same direction yields a simple translation
T
on the block lattice:

\chi(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle S_{i}^{2}}}{{\to}}-\xi_{i}(x)\xi_{i}(x+i)\chi(x+2i)=\chi(x+2i)=-T_{i}\left[\chi(x)\right]

(31)

Similarly,
performing a

shift followed by a

shift yields

\displaystyle S_{ij}S_{j}\chi(x)

\displaystyle\to S_{ij}i\xi_{j}(x)\chi(x+j)

\displaystyle=-i\xi_{j}(x+j)\xi_{i}(x+2j)\xi_{j}(x)\chi(x+2j+i)

\displaystyle=-i\xi_{i}(x)T_{j}\chi(x+i)

\displaystyle=-T_{j}S_{i}\chi(x)

(32)

or more generally
[S_{ij},S_{j}]=-2T_{j}S_{i}
.
That is, the combination of two shifts generates another shift up to a block translation. In a similar fashion the double
shifts satisfy the relation

[S_{ij},S_{jk}]=2S_{ik}T_{j}

(33)

It should now be clear that the staggered fermion shift symmetries form a discrete
subgroup of the continuum symmetries and guarantee that the latter
is restored automatically in the continuum limit. Furthermore, the odd shifts mimic the properties of the continuum axial symmetries in protecting
the lattice theory from mass terms constructed from products of the staggered
field within the unit hypercube - see
Golterman and Smit 1984
;
Kluberg-Stern et al. 1983
;
van den Doel and Smit 1983
;
Gliozzi 1982
;
Sharatchandra et al. 1981

The shift symmetries hold even in the presence of gauge interactions provided the gauge link field

transforms similarly under shifts

U_{i}(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle S_{j}}}{{\to}}U_{i}(x+j)

IV
Lattice axial charge

We have already remarked that a discrete
U_{A}(1)
transformation corresponds to right multiplication of the matrix fermion by
\gamma_{5}
and maps to the 3-shift
S_{123}
operator
acting on the staggered field. Its axial
character can be made obvious by observing that

\Psi\gamma_{5}=(\gamma_{5}\Psi\gamma_{5})\gamma_{5}=\gamma_{5}\Psi

(35)

where we have used the fact that the staggered field has eigenvalue unity
under the twisted chiral operator
\gamma_{5}\otimes\gamma_{5}
. A straightforward expression
for the singlet axial charge can then be written as

\displaystyle Q_{A}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x}\Xi_{123}(x)\chi^{*}(x)\chi(x+1+2+3)+{\rm h.c}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x}\Xi_{123}(x)\chi^{*}(x)\left[\chi(x+1+2+3)\right.

\displaystyle\left.\quad+\chi(x-1-2-3)\right]

(36)

where
\Xi_{123}(x)=\xi_{1}(x)\xi_{2}(x+1)\xi_{3}(x+1+2+3)
.
This generalizes the result for the non-quantized axial charge
\tilde{Q}_{A}
given in
Chatterjee et al. 2025
for one (spatial) dimension to three dimensions. It has a vanishing commutator
with the vector charge
Q_{V}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x}(\chi^{*}(x)\chi(x)-1/2)
.
Following
Chatterjee et al. 2025
we can also introduce a quantized
axial charge using

\displaystyle Q_{A}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x}\Xi_{123}(x)\left[\chi(x)+\chi^{*}(x)\right]\left[\chi(x+1+2+3)\right.

\displaystyle\left.\quad-\chi^{*}(x+1+2+3)\right]

(37)

In this case one finds

\displaystyle[Q_{V},Q_{A}]

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x}\Xi_{123}(x)\left[\chi(x)\chi(x+1+2+3)\right.

\displaystyle\left.\quad+\chi^{*}(x)\chi^{*}(x+1+2+3)\right]

(38)

This is clearly non-zero on a finite lattice and suggests that the theory suffers from
a mixed ’t Hooft anomaly that prevents gauging the vector symmetry. One might imagine constructing other charges corresponding to the full set of
shift symmetries and examine their commutator structure too in the
search for lattice anomalies. However, this
is problematic since the shifts correspond to
finite
rather than infinitessimal transformations
in the continuum theory.

\displaystyle i

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle T}}{{\to}}-i

.
.
At this point it is important to notice that the elementary shift symmetry

\displaystyle\chi(x)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\cal T}}}{{\to}}\epsilon(x)\chi^{*}(x)

\displaystyle\chi(x)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle S_{k}{\cal T}}}{{\to}}-i\epsilon(x)\xi_{k}(x)\chi^{*}(x+k)

Let us summarise our conclusions so far. The massless staggered theory is invariant

of the continuum Dirac fields. However, we find that odd multiples of the elementary

with time reversal.

VI
Real fields and symmetry operators

To derive explicit operators that
implement shifts and time reversal on a finite lattice

\chi

\displaystyle\chi(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\lambda^{1}(x)+i\lambda^{2}(x)\right)

\displaystyle\chi^{\dagger}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\lambda^{1}(x)-i\lambda^{2}(x)\right)

Similarly, time reversal

acts as

\displaystyle\lambda^{1}(x)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\cal T}}}{{\to}}\epsilon(x)\lambda^{1}(x)

\displaystyle\lambda^{2}(x)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\cal T}}}{{\to}}-\epsilon(x)\lambda^{2}(x)

(48)

We can write the elementary shift symmetry described in the previous section
as
S_{k}=R\hat{S}_{k}

where

\hat{S}_{k}
is given by

\lambda^{a}(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\hat{S}_{k}}}{{\to}}\xi_{k}(x)\lambda^{a}(x+k)

(49)

In fact, it should be clear that the massless Hamiltonian is actually invariant under two separate half shifts given by

\displaystyle\lambda^{1}(x)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle A_{k}}}{{\to}}\xi_{k}(x)\lambda^{1}(x+k)

\displaystyle\lambda^{2}(x)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle A_{k}}}{{\to}}\lambda^{2}(x)

(50)

and

\displaystyle\lambda^{2}(x)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle B_{k}}}{{\to}}\xi_{k}(x)\lambda^{2}(x+k)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle B_{k}}}{{\to}}\lambda^{1}(x)

with

\hat{S}_{k}=A_{k}\,B_{k}

The

Chatterjee et al. 2025
for a (1+1) dimensional staggered fermion model.

As a warm up let us start with a one dimensional

The

A_{1}

(53)

and
A_{1}^{-1}
is given by

A_{1}^{-1}=2^{-L/2}\prod^{x=0}_{L-1}\Big(1+\lambda^{1}(x)\lambda^{1}(x+1)\Big),

(54)

To see this one uses the results

\displaystyle-\lambda^{1}(x+1)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left[1+\lambda^{1}(x)\lambda^{1}(x+1)\right]\lambda^{1}(x)\left[1-\right.

\displaystyle\left.\quad\lambda^{1}(x)\lambda^{1}(x+1)\right]

\displaystyle\lambda^{2}(x)

\displaystyle\left.\quad\lambda^{1}(x)\lambda^{1}(x+1)\right]

\displaystyle 1

B_{1}
which is given by

B_{1}=2^{-L/2}\prod^{L-1}_{x=0}\Big(1-\lambda^{2}(x)\lambda^{2}(x+1)\Big),

(56)

Combining the

\hat{S}_{1}=2^{-L}\prod_{a=1}^{2}\prod^{L-1}_{x=0}\Big(1-\lambda^{a}(x)\lambda^{a}(x+1)\Big)

The time reversal operator can also be implemented in a similar way. When
L
is a multiple of 4, it can be achieved using the operator
{\cal T}
:

{\cal T}={\cal K}\,\bigg(\prod_{x\;{\rm even}}\lambda^{1}(x)\bigg)\bigg(\prod_{x\;{\rm odd}}\lambda^{2}(x)\bigg).

where

When

with

.

with

\displaystyle\hat{S}_{2}

To write down operators that correspond

As observed earlier, one can
verify that all the operators mutually commute with each other
except for

which

This fact ensures that any state of the
system, including the ground state, has to be at least
two fold degenerate (see the appendix for a simple argument) even in the thermodynamic limit. It thus allows for
spontaneous symmetry breaking.
Furthermore, the definition of

involves an element

of the
U(1)

symmetry

. Notice that

satisfies

and hence

.
The fact that
S_{k}
and

do not commute implies that any attempt to gauge this

subgroup will break
{\cal T}
- a mixed lattice ’t Hooft anomaly. In particular,
the singlet

symmetry corresponding to
the 3-shift

will be broken if this subgroup of the vector
symmetry is gauged. This is analogous to the result obtained
in
Chatterjee et al. 2025
.
In the next section we will explore how these ’t Hooft anomalies can
be canceled.

VII
Anomaly cancellation

The question we would like to address is whether the mixed ’t Hooft anomaly
we found can be canceled. One way to approach this question is to ask whether
we can design interactions that can gap the fermions without breaking the
Z_{4}

symmetry. One way to do the latter is to add
Z_{4}
invariant four fermion
interactions to the theory. The simplest term we can write down takes
the form

G\sum_{x}\chi^{1}(x)\chi^{2}(x)\chi^{3}(x)\chi^{4}(x)+{\rm h.c}

(68)

which requires four complex staggered
fermions and exhibits an explicit
SU(4)=SO(6)
global symmetry as well
as time reversal and shift invariance.
For
G\to 0
one expects the ground state to be eight fold
degenerate since it corresponds to eight non-interacting real
staggered fermions. However, for
G\to\infty
the ground state
is given by diagonalizing the single site Hamiltonian. It was shown in
Poppitz and Shang 2010
and
You and Xu 2015
that the ground state
of this system is in fact a singlet. Indeed, in the
latter paper it was shown how to construct a variety of four
fermion terms with differing symmetry groups (the maximal symmetry being
SO(7)
)
that result in a non-degenerate ground state - generalizing the original result of Kitaev et al
Fidkowski and Kitaev 2010
. The singlet nature of the ground state
implies that the system is incapable of undergoing spontaneous symmetry breaking.
This fact in turn implies that the system is free of ’t Hooft anomalies.
This phenomenon of producing a gapped, invariant ground state
has been termed symmetric mass generation and has already been observed
in staggered fermion models with four fermion interactions
Fidkowski and Kitaev 2010
;
Wang and You 2022
;
Ayyar and Chandrasekharan 2015
;
Ayyar and Chandrasekharan 2016b
;
Butt et al. 2021b
;
Catterall and Schaich 2017
;
Catterall 2016
;
Butt et al. 2018
.
Clearly the model can be gapped in this way for eight real staggered fermions each of which yields two Majorana fermions in the naive continuum limit. Thus this analysis confirms the
Z_{16}
anomaly cancellation condition for Majorana
fermions in (3+1) dimensions
Razamat and Tong 2021
;
Wang and Wen 2020
;
You and Xu 2015
;
García-Etxebarria and Montero 2019
.

VIII
Conclusions

In this paper we have examined the shift, time reversal and phase
symmetries of
Hamiltonian staggered fermions on finite spatial lattices focusing
on the case of 3+1 dimensions.
We have shown
how the shift symmetries correspond to a discrete subgroup of the product
of the continuum axial-flavor symmetry and translations. In particular, the
odd shifts correspond to axial transformations in the continuum theory
and are only symmetries for the massless theory. Furthermore, we find
that the odd shifts anticommute with time reversal.
We have constructed explicit operators to generate these symmetries along the lines of
Seiberg and Shao 2024
. The presence of anticommuting symmetries hints
at the presence of ’t Hooft anomalies. However, we have argued that
the system can be gapped, and hence the
’t Hooft anomaly canceled, for four complex or eight real staggered fields. Indeed, it appears that these mixed shift-time reversal anomalies in the Hamiltonian
formalism correspond to the gravitational anomalies of the Euclidean theory.

While writing this paper we became aware of another recent work which also
elucidates
the symmetry structure of Hamiltonian staggered fermions with the
goal of classifying the possibilities for symmetric
mass generation
Li et al. 2024
. Our results are consistent with their conclusions
where the two papers overlap.

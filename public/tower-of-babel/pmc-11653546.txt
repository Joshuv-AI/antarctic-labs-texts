Stable minichromosome and functional neocentromere derived from rye 7R chromosome arm
Authors: Zongxiang Tang, Qian Liu, Zijin Pan, Chang Liu, Jieran Dong, Fangpu Han, Shulan Fu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe study of newly formed centromere with stable transmission ability can provide theoretical guidance for the construction of artificial chromosomes. More neocentromeres are needed to study the mechanisms of their formation.ResultsIn this study, a minichromosome 7RLmini was derived from the progeny of wheat-rye 7R monosomic addition line. The minichromosome 7RLmini contained subtelomeric tandem repeats pSc119.2 and rye-specific pSc200, and it came from the distal region of the long arm of 7R chromosome. A neocentromere was formed in this minichromosome, and it did not contain centromeric repetitive sequences CCS1 and pAWRC.1. CENH3 ChIP-seq and ssDRIP-seq data confirmed that a 2.4 Mb segment from the rye 7R chromosome was involved in the neocentromere formation and enrichment of R-loops in this region. Within the 2.4 Mb segment, the GC content was higher that of AT, and a major binding position of CENH3 nucleosomes was identified on a 6 kb unknown LTR retrotransposon TE00002448. This unknown LTR retrotransposon was rye-specific and distributed through all the arms of rye chromosomes. The minichromosome exhibited stable generational transmission.ConclusionA minichromosome from rye 7R with neocentromere was obtained in this study and the neocentromere was formed at the position far away from its native equivalent. This minichromosome provides additional material for the research on the mechanism of neocentromere formation. We theorize that R-loops and transposable element might be involved in the positioning of CENH3 nucleosomes in a functional neocentromere.Keywords: Wheat, Rye, Minichromosome, Neocentromere, R-loop

Background

Centromere is important to ensure accurate segregation of chromosome during cell cycle [1]. Functional centromeres often contain repetitive DNA sequences including retrotransposons and satellite DNA. For example, the centromeres in humans, Arabidopsis thaliana and rice contain 171-bp, 180-bp and 155-bp satellite array, respectively [2–4], and the Ty3/gypsy-like retrotransposon exists in grass centromeres [5]. There are great differences in the composition of centromeric repetitive DNA sequences among species [6, 7]. It is believed that the functional centromere is controlled by the epigenetic mechanisms, and a centromere-specific histone H3 variant, CENH3 or CENP-A, was often used as a marker to indicate that the centromere is active [8–10]. In addition, some new epigenetic markers, such as R-loop and noncoding RNAs have also been used to study the formation of functional centromeres [11–14]. The enrichment of non-B-form DNA in centromeres of Drosophila melanogaster and oat was observed [15, 16]. More non-B-form DNA in the centromeric regions was found in the newly formed allotetraploid wheat than in their parents [17]. The back-spliced RNAs that were derived from the centromeric retrotransposons bound to the maize centromeres through R-loops [18]. R-loops can be generated from transposable elements and they preferentially occurred in the CENH3 binding regions [14]. In human cells, R-loops were involved in the replication of centromeric alpha-satellites, recombination, breakage and chromosome translocation at centromere [19]. These reported results indicate that R-loops might play an important role in the formation of functional centromeres and in the maintaining the integrity of centromere.

Neocentromeres are useful for studying the mechanisms regulating centromere de novo formation. Neocentromeres on mar del(10) chromosome and 3q26 that were derived from human chromosome 10 and 3, respectively, were reported, and no alpha-satellite sequence was detected at both of them [20, 21]. Some genetic stable minichromosomes lack canonical centromere sequences in maize [22–28], barley [29], rice [30], and wheat [9] have been reported. These results further suggest that the function of centromere is kept by epigenetic mechanisms. However, not many neocentromeres derived from plants have been reported, and more neocentromeres are needed to study the factors that induce their formation. Given the importance of R-loop in functional centromeres, it may be worthwhile to investigate whether R-loop is also enriched in newly formed centromeric region, thereby providing a new perspective on the mechanism of neocentromere formation. In this study, a minichromosome was derived from rye chromosome 7R and it does not contain the canonical centromeric repetitive DNA sequences. ChIP-seq and ssDRIP-seq analyses were used to study the neocentromere of this minichromosome.

Results

Identification of minichromosomes derived from rye 7R chromosome

ND-FISH was used to analyze wheat-rye monosomic addition line 16T283-7 and its progeny. It can be determined that the chromosome with the signals of Oligo-pSc200 and Oligo-pSc250 in the line 16T283-7 is rye chromosome because the two probes are rye-specific (Fig. 1a, b). The signal bands of the probe Oligo-pSc119.2-1 indicated that the rye chromosome was 7R, and the short arm (7RS) contained four adjacent signal bands and the two signal bands on the long arm (7RL) were far apart (Fig. 1a). The signal of Oligo-pSc119.2-1 in the telomeric region of 7RL arm was stronger than that in the telomeric region of 7RS arm (Fig. 1a). The probes Oligo-pSc250 and Oligo-pSc200 produced signals in the telomeric region of the 7RS arm and the subtelomeric region of the 7RL arm, respectively (Fig. 1b). Wheat line P13 was derived from line 16T283-7, and this line contained minichromosomes (Fig. 1c, d). The minichromosomes carried the strong signals of Oligo-pSc119.2-1 and Oligo-pSc200, and no Oligo-pSc250 signal was observed (Fig. 1c, d), so it can be determined that the minichromosomes in line P13 were derived from the distal region of 7RL arm, and they were named 7RLmini. To assess the inheritance of 7RLmini, ND-FISH analysis was conducted on root-tip metaphase chromosomes from the selfing progeny seeds of line P13 in 2021 and 2022. The transmission rates of the 7RLmini were listed in Table 1. It can be noted that the minichromosome 7RLmini can be transmitted from one generation to the next. The transmission rates of the coupled minichromosomes were 82.18% and 75.86% in the two years, respectively (Table 1).

To determine the DNA sequence associated with the neocentromere in 7RLmini, ChIP-seq was performed with wheat-specific CENH3 antibodies on P13 plants and parental plants of rye Petkus (PI 428373). The raw reads were filtered using Fastp software [33] to generate clean reads. This suggests that this chromosome has undergone a break-fusion event. Repetitive DNA sequences in the neocentromere of 7RLmini

To explore the DNA sequence composition of the new centromeric region in 7RLmini, EDTA [37], TRASH [38] and Tandem Repeats Finder (https://tandem.bu.edu/trf/trf.html) were used to annotate the repetitive sequences within the 2.4 Mb segment. Eleven genes were found in this new centromeric region and the functions of these genes were not elaborated in the 7R annotation information of Weining rye. Additionally, 47 sequences of transposable elements (TE) (25 L/ Gypsy retrotransposons, 9 L/Copia retrotransposons, 6 Helitron DNA transposons, and 7 L/unknown retrotransposons) and 672 sequences of tandem repeats (55 microsatellites, 46 satellites, and 571 minisatellites) were identified, accounting for 32.54% and 3.36% of the new centromeric region, respectively. The GC/AT content of the 2.4 Mb segment was investigated by performing 1 kb window analysis across this segment and it was divided into 2,400 windows (Fig. 4). The GC content was more than 60% and the AT content was less than 40% along the whole 2.4 Mb segment (Fig. 4). So, the proportion of GC in the whole sequence of the 2.4 Mb segment was much higher than that of AT.

To investigate whether CENH3 nucleosomes in the new centromeric region bind to transposable elements, we focused on a predicted intact transposable element, TE00002448 (7R: 795,715,934 ~ 795,722,215 bp). The TE00002448 belonged to unknown LTR retrotransposon. The CENH3 ChIP-seq reads, representing the presence of CENH3, were aligned to TE00002448 using blastn, the midpoint distribution of these fragments was considered as the positional information for the sequences on CENH3 nucleosomes. A major binding position was identified on this transposable element, suggesting that transposable elements wrapped around CENH3 nucleosomes (Fig. 5). The partial sequence of the TE00002448 was cloned (2781 bp in length, named TE2448-2) and was used as FISH probe to analyze the root tip metaphase chromosomes of rye Petkus, barley Morex, Dasypyrum villosum and one of the progeny of line P13. The probe TE2448-2 hybridized with all the chromosomal arms of Petkus, but less signals of this probe were observed in the centromeric regions and some telomeric regions (Fig. 6a). In the progeny of line P13, no signal of the probe TE2448-2 was observed on the 42 wheat chromosomes and only the 7RLmini contained the signal (Fig. 6b). In addition, the probe TE2448-2 also did not produce signals on the chromosomes of Dasypyrum villosum and barley (Fig. 6c, d). Therefore, the transposable element TE00002448 is rye-specific and this indicates that it is not ancient. These results hinted that young and species-specific transposon elements may be involved in the formation of neocentromere.

Identification of R-loop in 7RLmini chromosomes

R-loops are chromatin structures formed by an RNA: DNA hybrid strand and a single stranded DNA, that is widely present in eukaryotic organisms and participates in various biological processes. It has been reported that R-loop is important for the function of centromere [14, 18, 39]. In order to investigate whether R-loops also play a crucial role in the formation and function of the neocentromere, we conducted DNA: RNA immunoprecipitation on fragmented nucleic acids, followed by strand-specific sequencing of the DNA strand (ssDRIP-seq) in P13. The trimmed reads were mapped to the genomic sequences of wheat Chinese Spring (CS) IWGSC RefSeq v2.1 plus 7R chromosome of rye Weining. We then employed MACS to identify R-loop peaks [40], calling peaks independently against the input samples. In the new centromeric region of the 7RLmini chromosome, a total of 577 peaks were identified, with the majority of them primarily distributed across genes and transposable elements (TE) (Fig. 7).

Discussion

The centromeres of minichromosomes and their generational transmission

Minichromosomes are useful for studying the formation of functional centromeres. A telocentric midget chromosome derived from rye 1R and one of the breakpoints of this minichromosome occurred in the middle of 1R centromere [41]. In Arabidopsis thaliana, a minichromosome mini4S was formed by breakage in the centromeric region [42]. In rice and wheat, the truncation break point can occur at the centromeric region leading the truncated chromosomes with centromere [43, 44]. In field bean, a small chromosome (DI-VI) with wild-type centromere was formed by the breakages near the centromeric region of a translocation tDIL-VIS [45]. It can be determined that these spontaneously produced minichromosomes were caused by two breakages in their progenitors. One breakage occurred in the centromeric region and the other occurred near the centromere region, or both the two breakages occurred near the centromere. Therefore, the functional centromeres of these reported minichromosomes were derived from their native ones. In this study, the 7RLmini was formed through a break-fusion event in the distal region of 7RL arm and it contained a new functional centromere which gives the minichromosome the ability of stable generational transmission (Table 1).

In plants, neocentromeres derived from the chromosomes of wheat [9], maize [22–25, 27, 28], barley [29] and rice [30] have been reported. The somatic transmission rates of maize minichromosomes with neocentromere was reported [22]. The extra 8 S chromosome with neocentromere in rice was unstable in mitosis [30]. Few of these reports investigated the generational transmission of minichromosome with neocentromere. The generational transmission of maize minichromosmes sDic-15 and 3–3 with neocentromere were 3.76% (9/239) and 13%, respectively [24, 25]. In this study, the generational transmission of 7RLmini was more than 75%, and the percentage of the plants containing two copies of 7RLmini was more than 32% (29/89) (Table 1). At present, the reason for the high frequency of generation transmission of this microchromosome has not been determined. Maybe, both the neocentromere and the overall structure of the 7RLmini are the important factors affecting its transmission. Therefore, the 7RLmini is useful for the further study on the relationship among the structure of minichromosome, the structure of neocentromere and the high percentage of transmission.

De novo centromere formation

The mechanisms of neocentromere formation still need to be explored. It was speculated that during the allopolyploidization, the centromere might drift along the DNA rather than being strictly tethered. This might be conducive to maintaining the genome stability of the newly formed allopolyploid and provide a basis for subsequent centromere repositioning [46]. In wheat aneuploids and their wide hybrids, neocentromeres were found in both wheat and its alien chromosomes [9]. Neocentromere was found on barley chromosome 7 H in wheat background [29] and on the maize chromosome 3 in the progeny of monosomic oat-maize addition line [22]. In the self-fertilized progeny of wheat-rye monosomic addition lines, neocentromere on a 2RS telosome was found [47]. In this study, the neocentromere on 7RL minichromosome was formed in the progeny of wheat-rye 7R monosomic addition line. These results indicated that the wide hybridization is an important factor to induce neocentromere formation. In plants, most of the neocentromeres were formed near their native centromeres [9, 16, 27, 29], and few of them occurred at a locus distal from the native centromere [22]. The neocentromere reported in this study is located at the subtelomeric region of 7R chromosome and this is different from that reported in wheat, oat and barley [9, 16, 29]. These results indicated that the de novo formation of neocentromere was not dependent on the position on the chromosome, as discussed previously [9]. This might reflect different mechanism of neocentromere formation. Some conditions such as lack of genes and a high level of DNA methylation in pericentromeric region may be suitable for neocentromere formation [27]. However, the mechanisms of neocentromere formation in the regions far away from the native centromere remain unclear.

In maize, CRM1-derived circular RNAs bound to centromere through R-loop and this is important for the activity of centromere [18]. The preferential formation of R-loops in the binding regions of histone CENH3 was also observed in maize and this disclosed the potential role of R-loops in centromere function [14]. Oat have also been demonstrated the preferential R-loop formation in CENH3-binding regions, indicating the importance of R-loops in the activity of centromere [39]. In this study, R-loops also accumulated in the neocentromeric region of the 7RLmini chromosome and this has not been reported in the previous studies [9, 22–30]. From the previous reports and the results in this study, it can be presumed that the formation of the neocentromere in the distal regions should be associated with the formation of R-loops. It was known that the GC-enriched region is conducive to the formation of R-loop [48]. The R-loop accumulation in the neocentromeric region of 7RLmini might be related to the GC enrichment in this region. Despite previous reports indicating that the neocentromeres contain many transposable elements, the role of these transposable elements in the formation of neocentromere is unclear [9, 24, 25]. The binding position of CENH3 nucleosomes in the transposable element TE00002448 was found, and this transposable element is not ancient in the process of evolution because it is rye-specific. Young LTR retrotransposons are associated with the non-B-form DNA enriched in functional centromeres [17, 49]. The results obtained in this study hinted the role of transposable element TE00002448 in the positioning of CENH3 nucleosomes at the neocentromere.

More neocentromeres are needed to explore their formation mechanisms

R-loops and transposable elements play a potential role in neocentromere formation, however, they may not be the decisive factors. Instead, they likely work alongside DNA sequences, RNA, chromatin structure, and epigenetic modifications to create a conducive environment for centromere establishment. The reason why the mechanism of neocentromere formation remains unclear is that the characteristics of the neocentromere reported so far are different. In the 2.4 Mb segment of this study, the copy number of tandem repeats was much higher than that of transposable elements, and the GC content was more than 60% and was higher than that of AT. The percentage of retrotransposons was 84% in the neocentromere of maize minichromosome 3–3 [25]. The GC content in the neocentromere from wheat 4D chromosomes was about 50% [9]. Both the neocentromeres formed in chicken and human have lower GC contents [50]. Perhaps, the mechanisms of new centromere formation differ between plants and animals, or between different plants. Because not many neocentromere have been found in plants, this question cannot be clearly answered at present. Therefore, more neocentromeres are needed to explore the rules of their formation.

Conclusion

This study provides a new minichromosome with neocentromere and higher frequency of generational transmission. R-loops may be involved in the positioning of CENH3 nucleosomes at the neocentromere, and some young retrotransposons might also be required. The 7RLmini in this study gives a new case about the neocentromere formation.

Methods

Plant materials

Rye Petkus (PI 428373) (Secale cereale L.) was used as male parent to cross with wheat cultivar Chuannong 27, and wheat-rye 7RPe monosomic addition line 16T283-7 was identified from the progeny of backcrossing of the F1 plants with Chuannong 27. A plant (named P13) with minichromosomes was identified from the progeny of the line 16T283-7.

In addition, barley cultivar Morex (Hordeum vulgare L.) and UKR-99-026 (Dasypyrum villosum (L.) P. Candargy) were also used in this study. Petkus was from the American Germplasm Resources Information Network (GRIN). Barley Morex and UKR-99-026 were friendly supplied by Professor Zujun Yang, School of Life Science and Technology, University of Electronic Science and Technology of China, and the other materials were from our laboratory.

Cytological analysis

The preparation of root-tip metaphase chromosomes of the materials used in this study was carried out according to the reported methods [51]. Oligonucleotide (oligo) probes Oligo-pSc119.2-1 [52], Oligo-pSc200 and Oligo-pSc250 [53] were used for non-denaturing fluorescence in situ hybridization (ND-FISH) analysis. ND-FISH analysis was performed as previously reported [53]. Probe Oligo-pSc119.2-1 was derived from the tandem repeat pSc119.2, which exists in Triticeae species and contains 118 bp monomer [52, 54, 55]. Probes Oligo-pSc200 and Oligo-pSc250 were derived from the rye-specific tandem repeats pSc200 and pSc250, respectively [53, 56]. The monomer length of pSc200 and pSc250 is 379 bp and 550 bp, respectively [56]. Oligo-pSc119.2-1 combined with Oligo-pSc200 and Oligo-pSc250 can be used to identify individual rye chromosomes in wheat backgrounds [53].

A primer pair pAWRC.1–1 (F: 5’ACTTC GGCTC CTACA ACCAA3’; R: 5’CGTTC GATGG GTTCT CACAC3’) was designed according to the rye-specific centromeric sequence pAWRC.1 [31], and this primer pair was used to clone partial segment of pAWRC.1. The partial segment of the cereal centromeric repetitive sequence CCS1 was cloned using the primer pair reported by Aragón-Alcaide et al. [32]. Two primer pairs Chr7 (F: 5’CGCAA ATCCC AACAA AGCGA3’; R: 5’GCTGG GAAAT AGACT GGCGT3’) and TE2448 (F: 5’CTCTA CCTTG CTTTG GGACA TGA3’; R: 5’CGAAC GATGG ATTTT GATGG C3’) were designed according to a gene ScWN7R01G393900.1 and a retrotransposon sequence TE00002448 (7R: 795,715,934 ~ 795,722,215 bp), respectively. Both the gene and the retrotransposon sequence were located in the new centromeric region of minichromosome 7RLmini. The sequences Chr7-2 and TE2448-2 were cloned from 7RLmini using primer pair Chr7 and TE2448, respectively. The cloned partial sequences of CCS1 and pAWRC.1, Chr7-2, and oligo probes Oligo-pSc200 and Oligo-pSc250 were used as probes for denaturing fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) analysis of the centromeres of the materials used in this study. The sequence TE2448-2 was used as probe for denaturing FISH analysis of the root tip metaphase chromosomes of rye Petkus, barley Morex, UKR-99-026 and the progeny of the line P13. For CCS1, pAWRC.1, Chr7-2 and TE2448-2, probe labeling and hybridization were carried out as previously described [51].

Immunolocalization and FISH

The wheat-specific CENH3 antibody was produced according to the methods as described by Yuan et al. [57]. FISH and immunolocalization for mitosis of wheat line P13 were operated as described by Fu et al. [23], with little modification. Briefly, probe Oligo-pSc200 was used for FISH analysis of wheat line P13.

ChIP-seq and ssDRIP-seq analysis

About 5 g of young leaves of the plants with 7RLmini and rye Petkus were used for chromatin immunoprecipitation (ChIP). The procedure was performed as previous study [3]. The samples of plants with 7RLmini and rye Petkus that were not incubated with CENH3 antibody were used as controls (input). The ssDRIP-seq experiment with S9.6 antibody was carried out according to the methods described by Liu et al. [14]. About 5 g of young leaves of the plants with 7RLmini and their sibling plants without 7RLmini (input) were used for ssDRIP-seq. The quality control of raw reads of ChIP-seq and ssDRIP-seq, and the analysis of the trimmed reads were performed as described by Liu et al. [16]. The quality-controlled reads were aligned to the reference genome using the BWA [36] software with default parameters. SAMTOOLS [58] was then used to further filter the data, retaining only uniquely aligned reads. For visualization, the resulting BAM files were converted to BigWig format using Deeptools [59], and the CENH3 ChIP-seq and ssDRIP-seq data were visualized with The Integrative Genomics Viewer (IGV) [60]. For Figs. 7 and 8, MACS2 [40] was used to call peaks, using the input as a control. Read coverage was counted in each 1 Mb window along the chromosome, and heatmap were plotted using R version 4.2.2.

Identifying the new centromeric repeats

The sequences of chromosome 7R in rye were analyzed to identify repetitive elements, including tandem repeats and transposable elements (TEs). Tandem repeats were detected using TRASH (v1.1) [38] and the Tandem Repeats Finder tool (https://tandem.bu.edu/trf/trf.html). Extensive de novo TE Annotator (EDTA, v1.9.6) [37] was used to identify TEs with default parameters. The repetitive sequences located in the 794,000,000 to 796,400,000 bp region were considered to represent the new centromeric region repeats.

GC/AT contents analysis

Sequence analysis of GC and AT contents was examined by performing a 1 kb sliding windows across the 2.4 M segment and 2400 windows were divided, then the ratio of AT and GC of each window was calculated, and the plot was carried out with R4.4.0 software [61].

Determination of transmission rate of minichromosomes

ND-FISH using Oligo-pSc119.2-1 and Oligo-pSc200 as probes was used to analyze the root-tip metaphase chromosomes of the selfing progeny of wheat line P13. The plants containing one pair of minichromosomes were used to analyze the transmission rate of 7RLmini. Transmission rate of minichromosomes = number of seeds containing minichromosomes/total number of detected seeds × 100%.

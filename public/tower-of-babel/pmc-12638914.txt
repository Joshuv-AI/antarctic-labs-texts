Synergistic anticancer effects of Chrysin and trastuzumab in HER2-Positive breast cancer cells
Authors: Sevda Jafari, Alireza Ostadrahimi, Afsaneh Farjami, Soheila Montazersaheb
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
HER2-positive breast cancer is an aggressive subtype with limited therapeutic options. Trastuzumab, a monoclonal antibody targeting the HER2 receptor, has improved clinical outcomes; however, its efficacy remains a critical challenge in the HER2-positive model. This study investigated the potential of chrysin, a naturally occurring flavonoid, to enhance the efficacy of trastuzumab in HER2-positive SKBR3 and BT-474 breast cancer cells. The effects of chrysin and trastuzumab, alone or in combination, were evaluated in SKBR3 and BT-474 cells. Cell viability was evaluated using an MTT assay, and the combination index (CI) value was determined using Compusyn software. Apoptosis and HER2 expression were analyzed by flow cytometry. The morphological characteristics of apoptosis were evaluated using the DAPI-TUNEL staining. Protein expression of STAT3 and PDL-1 was detected by western blotting. Real-time PCR was employed to quantify the angiogenesis-related genes. Chrysin enhanced the anticancer effects of trastuzumab, achieving an optimal combination index (CI) of 0.39 in SKBR3 cells and a similarly strong synergistic profile in BT-474 cells (CI = 0.54). Chrysin synergistically enhanced trastuzumab-induced apoptosis in both cell lines (p < 0.01 and p < 0.001). Trastuzumab significantly reduced HER2 expression on SKBR3 and BT-474 cells (p < 0.001) and, surprisingly, chrysin also reduced HER2 expression in a significant manner (p < 0.001). Combination therapy further intensified this effect in SKBR3 and BT-474 cells (p < 0.001 and p < 0.01, respectively). Additionally, the combination therapy significantly decreased p-STAT3 and PD-L1 protein levels in SKBR3 and reduced mRNA levels of Tie2 and VEGFR2 in both cell lines. Chrysin synergistically enhances the efficacy of trastuzumab in HER2-positive SKBR3 and BT-474 breast cancer cells. These findings warrant further investigation in immune-competent and in vivo models to assess clinical applicability and underlying mechanisms.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-25098-x.

Introduction

Breast cancer is a heterogeneous disease, classified into subtypes based on the expression of molecular receptors: hormone receptor (HR)+/human epidermal growth factor receptor 2− (Luminal A), HR+/HER2+ (Luminal B), HR−/HER2+ (HER2-enriched), and HR−/HER2− (triple-negative)1. HER2-positive (HER2+) breast cancer accounts for about 20% of all cases and, in the absence of targeted HER2 therapies, is associated with a poor prognosis, increased recurrence rates, elevated risk of brain metastases, and aggressive clinical behavior2,3. Over the past two decades, the introduction of trastuzumab (Herceptin) was a major milestone as the anti-HER2-positive targeted therapy4. Trastuzumab revolutionized the therapy of HER2-positive breast cancer by introducing a monoclonal antibody (mAb) that specifically targeted cancer cells overexpressing the HER2 receptor5.

Trastuzumab binds to the extracellular domain of the HER2 receptor, preventing its activation and subsequent signal transduction. Beyond this, trastuzumab’s efficacy is enhanced through multiple pathways, including blocking intracellular signaling, promoting HER2 degradation, and eliciting an immune response via antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC). In other words, mAb exerts a direct anti-cancer effect by suppressing the HER2 signaling and also an indirect effect by activating the immune system6.

Despite its clinical success, trastuzumab monotherapy often yields limited efficacy; however, its combination with chemotherapy has demonstrated synergistic benefits⁷,⁸. Nevertheless, chemotherapy is associated with significant systemic toxicity, resistance development, and adverse side effects, prompting ongoing efforts to develop novel strategies that improve patient outcomes⁹.

Despite its clinical success, trastuzumab monotherapy generally achieves limited efficacy; however, its combination with chemotherapy exhibits synergistic effects7,8. Nevertheless, chemotherapy is associated with significant systemic toxicity, resistance development, and adverse side effects, prompting ongoing efforts to develop novel strategies that improve patient outcomes9. Although HER2-targeting treatments have significantly enhanced survival rates and reduced the risk of recurrence and mortality in HER2-positive breast cancer patients, many patients eventually experience disease progression8. These underscore the need for complementary approaches to enhance the efficacy of existing regimens.

Natural compounds have emerged as promising agents in cancer therapy due to their favorable profiles, such as reducing toxicity, enhancing selectivity, potential synergy, and the ability to overcome resistance, making them a promising alternative to traditional chemotherapy10,11. Chrysin (5,7-dihydroxyflavone) is a prominent polyphenolic compound abundant in honey and propolis. Chrysin has shown promise in cancer therapy due to its ability to inhibit tumor cell proliferation, induce apoptosis, and suppress angiogenesis, modulating oxidative stress and inflammation12–15. These properties suggest that chrysin could enhance the activity of targeted therapy, such as trastuzumab.

Therefore, we investigated the effect of combining chrysin with trastuzumab in two HER2-positive breast cancer cell lines, SKBR3 and BT-474 cells, as they are generally sensitive to trastuzumab under standard culture conditions. While not a model of acquired resistance, both cell lines in our experimental setting exhibited a plateau in trastuzumab-induced growth inhibition, consistent with partial or intrinsic insensitivity reported in a prior study16. Within this context, we hypothesized that chrysin enhances the anticancer effects of trastuzumab. To explore this, we examined key molecular markers relevant to HER2 expression, tumor cell survival (p-STAT3), immune checkpoint regulation (PD-L1), and angiogenesis-related pathways such as EGF-like domains 2 (Tie2) and vascular endothelial growth factor receptor 2 (VEGFR2), reflecting tumor-cell-intrinsic effects rather than tumor microenvironment (TME) interaction in our in vitro setting.

Understanding the impact of chrysin in enhancing the anticancer efficacy of trastuzumab may open new avenues for developing innovative therapeutic approaches, emphasizing the value of natural compounds in achieving greater therapeutic efficacy and reduced toxicity. This research advances our knowledge, supporting the translational potential of natural compounds like chrysin in improving therapeutic outcomes in HER2-positive breast cancer patients.

Materials and methods

The culture plates were provided by SPL Company Ltd. RPMI medium and FBS were purchased from Gibco (Gibco, USA). MTT powder was obtained from Sigma (St. Louis, MO, USA). Chrysin (CAS No: 480-40-0), obtained from Sigma (USA), was dissolved in DMSO to prepare a stock solution on the day of treatment and was subsequently diluted to the desired concentration in the final medium before each use. Trastuzumab was provided by ARYOGEN (Iran). The Apoptosis Detection Kit was procured from Ebioscience, USA (Cat. No: 88–8005-72). For flow cytometry analysis, trastuzumab (Herceptin®) (Genentech, Inc., USA) was used as primary antibody, and Goat–anti human IgG Fc, FITC (A18818) (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA) was used as secondary antibody.

Culture of cells

MCF-10 A, SKBR3, and BT-474 cells were cultured in RPMI medium supplemented with 10% FBS and 1% penicillin-streptomycin. All cells were maintained in an incubator at 37 °C and 5% CO₂. The culture medium was refreshed twice a week. When the cells reached 70–80% confluence, the treatment regimen was initiated.

MTT assay for cell viability determination

To evaluate the in vitro cytotoxic effects of chrysin and trastuzumab on HER2-positive cells (SKBR3 and BT-474 cells) and normal breast cancer cells (MCF-10 A), an MTT assay was conducted. SKBR3 and BT-474 cells were seeded at a density of 5 × 10³, and MCF-10 A cells were seeded at a density of 4 × 10³ in each well of a 96-well plate containing 200 µl of culture medium. After 24 h of incubation, cells were exposed to different concentrations of chrysin (ranging from 10 to 100 µM) and trastuzumab (ranging from 10 to 110 µg/mL). Following an additional 48 h of incubation in a humid atmosphere of 5% CO2, 20 µl of MTT solution (5 mg/ml) was added to the wells. The plate was then incubated for another 4 h to allow the formation of formazan crystals. After incubation, the supernatants were removed, the formed crystals were solubilized by adding DMSO, and the absorbance was measured at 570 nm using a microplate reader (BioTek Instruments, Inc., USA)17. Data were analyzed and plotted using GraphPad Prism software (version 8). The untreated cells were used as the negative controls. The experiment was performed in triplicate for three independent trials, and the results are presented as the mean ± standard deviation. To minimize the cytotoxic effects of chrysin and trastuzumab at higher concentrations, subsequent experiments were carried out with concentrations approximately near the IC50.

The combination index (CI) of chrysin and trastuzumab was obtained using the Chou-Talalay method18. A CI value less than 1 indicates synergism, a value greater than 1 indicates antagonism, and a value equal to 1 represents an additive effect. For combination therapy, concentrations of chrysin and trastuzumab near their IC50 values were utilized.

Apoptosis evaluation using flow cytometry analysis by Annexin V/PI staining assay

To determine whether the combination of chrysin and trastuzumab treatment induced apoptosis, the proportion of apoptotic cells was analyzed using flow cytometry with a detection kit (ebioscience, USA; Cat No: 88–8005-72). SKBR3 and BT-474 cells at a density of 1.5 × 105 were seeded in 6-well plates and treated with the optimum concentration of treatments obtained from the MTT assay. After 48 h of incubation, cells were collected from all experimental groups, including untreated cells (control) and cells treated with chrysin alone, trastuzumab alone, or their combination, and washed with PBS. Cells in 100 µl of buffer were stained with 5 µl of FITC-conjugated Annexin V in the dark for 10–15 min. Following washing with binding buffer, the cells were stained with 5 µL of PI in 100 µL of binding buffer. The apoptotic cell population was quantified using a FACSCalibur flow cytometer (BD Biosciences). The acquired data were analyzed using the FlowJo software version 10. All experiments were conducted in triplicate to ensure reproducibility19.

Flow cytometric analysis of HER2 expression level

The HER2 protein levels were analyzed using flow cytometry. To achieve this, SKBR3 and BT-474 cells were seeded in 6-well plates at a density of 1.5 × 105 cells per well and incubated overnight. The cells were treated with chrysin alone, trastuzumab alone, or their combination for 48 h. Following this step, the cells were harvested from all experimental groups, washed, and fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde in PBS, and permeabilized with 0.1% Tween-20 in PBS. They were subsequently incubated with Trastuzumab as primary antibody for 30 min at 4 °C and then secondary FITC-conjugated antibody for 30 min at 4 °C. The stained cells were analyzed using a FACSCalibur flow cytometer (BD Biosciences, USA). Data were processed using FlowJo software version 1020.

Determination of STAT3 and PD-L1 protein levels using Western blotting

Western blot analysis was conducted to investigate the impact of monotherapy and combination therapy on the protein expression of STAT-3 and PD-L1 in SKBR3 cells. For this purpose, the cells (5 × 10⁵ cells/ml) were seeded in 100 mm petri dishes and incubated overnight. In the following, the cells were treated in the same manner as in previous experiments. The cells were collected and washed twice with cold PBS, followed by lysing in RIPA buffer for 30 min on ice. The lysates were centrifuged at 13,000×g for 20 min at 4 °C, and the protein concentrations in each sample were measured using a BCA protein assay kit (Pierce, Rockford, IL, USA). Protein samples were separated using 12% SDS-PAGE and then transferred onto PVDF membranes. Membranes were blocked with 5% skim milk in PBS to reduce nonspecific binding. Primary antibodies against β-actin (sc-517582, Santa Cruz), p-STAT3 (Cat No: 651001, BioLegend San Diego, CA), STAT3 (sc-8019, Santa Cruz), and PD-L1 (sc-518027, Santa Cruz) were applied at a 1:1000 dilution, and the membranes were incubated overnight at 4 °C. After washing, the membranes were exposed to secondary antibodies, mouse anti-rabbit IgG-HRP (sc-2357) and m-IgGκ BP-HRP (sc-516102) (1:5000) to detect the binding of primary antibodies. Protein bands were visualized using an ECL detection kit (Roche, UK). Band intensities were quantified with ImageJ 1.6 software and normalized to β-actin, which served as the internal control.

Determination of VEGF2 and Tie2 expression by real-time PCR

The mRNA level of VEGFR2 and Tie2 was measured to evaluate the impact of the combination therapy on angiogenesis-related signaling pathways, which play a critical role in tumor growth, vascularization, and progression in HER2-positive breast cancer. SKBR3 and BT-474 cells were seeded and treated with the same regimen as in previous experiments. Following 48 h of incubation, the cells were collected and washed with PBS. Total RNA was extracted using the Thermo Scientific RNA extraction kit (K0731) following the manufacturer’s instructions. RNA concentrations were quantified using a NanoDrop spectrophotometer (ND-1000, Wilmington, DE, USA). A total of 500 ng of RNA from each sample was reverse-transcribed into complementary DNA (cDNA) using a previously described method21. β-actin was used as the internal reference gene, and all expression data were normalized to β-actin levels. Statistical analysis

The acquired data were analyzed using the GraphPad Prism software (version 8). Group comparisons were performed using one-way ANOVA, followed by Tukey’s post hoc test. Flow cytometry data were analyzed using the FlowJo software (version 10). Statistical significance was defined as a p-value of less than 0.05. All experiments were performed in triplicate.

Results

Effect of Chrysin and trastuzumab on proliferation of SKBR3, BT-474, and MCF-10 A cells

SKBR3 and BT-474 cells were treated with varying concentrations of chrysin and trastuzumab for 48 h. Cell proliferation was assessed using the MTT assay, and the cytotoxic effects of individual and combination treatment were determined in both cell lines as HER2-positive breast cancer models. The dose-response curves for the inhibitory effects of chrysin and trastuzumab are shown in Fig. 1. Based on the obtained data, the IC50 values for chrysin and trastuzumab were determined to be 56.25 ± 5.09 µM and 76.61 ± 9.2 µg/mL in SKBR3 cells and 47.86 ± 4.11 µM and 55.76 ± 5.86 µg/mL in the BT-474 cell line. The MCF-10 A cell line, as a non-tumorigenic normal breast cell line, was also examined as a control. Trastuzumab did not affect MCF-10 A, and this result was in line with a previous report23. Chrysin affected MCF-10 A cells at higher doses, where the IC50 value of chrysin was 179 ± 10.45µM. (Fig. 1.A). To minimize cytotoxicity associated with higher concentrations, doses around and/or lower than IC50 values were selected for the combination therapy of SKBR3 and BT-474 cells MTT assay and Compusyne software were used to find the optimum dose for the combination therapy. Table 2 demonstrates doses of each drug, CI values, DRI values, and the inhibitory effects of drugs in SKBR3 cells. All the combinations revealed the synergistic effect of chrysin and trastuzumab. However, the best synergism was found with 60 µM of chrysin and 70 µg/mL of trastuzumab with a CI value of 0.39. Table 3 also represents the combination therapy information in the BT-474 cell line. As demonstrated, a combination of 40 µM chrysin and 70 µg/mL trastuzumab revealed the most synergistic effect with a CI value of 0.54. Therefore, these combinations were selected for subsequent experiments. Figure 1B depicts the Fa-CI plot of the combination therapy generated by Compusyn software in both cell lines, where a CI < 1 indicates synergy. Figure 1C shows the significant reduction of SKBR3 and BT-474 cells’ viability in groups treated with a combination therapy as compared to the control group (***p < 0.001) and each drug alone.

Impact of Chrysin and trastuzumab on apoptosis induction in SKBR3 and BT-474 cells

Apoptosis was evaluated to elucidate whether chrysin enhances the pro-apoptotic effects of trastuzumab, providing insight into the mechanism by which the combination therapy may exert its anticancer activity in HER2-positive breast cancer cells. To achieve this, an Annexin V/PI double double-staining assay was conducted using flow cytometry. Figure 2 depicts the contour diagram of flow cytometry of SKBR3 and BT-474 cells.

In SKBR3 cells, the percentage of apoptotic cells was 45.8 ± 4.08% in SKBR cells treated with chrysin (***p < 0.001), and 45.3 ± 3.67% in the cells treated with trastuzumab (***p < 0.001), as compared to the non-treated control. The combination therapy caused a substantial increase in apoptotic population (66.08 ± 3.15%), representing a significant enhancement compared to corresponding monotherapies (***p < 0.001) (Fig. 2A).

A similar trend was observed in BT-474 cells, as chrysin and trastuzumab monotherapies induced apoptosis rates of 48.08 ± 2.2% and 58.86 ± 3.1%, respectively (p < 0.001), compared to the control group. While the combination therapy markedly increases the apoptotic population to 69.97 ± 3.09%, compared to chrysin and trastuzumab alone (p < 0.001 and p < 0.01, respectively) (Fig. 2B).

To further validate these findings and morphologic change, DAPI-TUNNED staining was performed in SKBR3 cells, and the results are presented in the Supplementary Materials (Fig. Suppl 1). Consistent with the AnnexinV/PI assay, DAPI/TUNEL analysis confirmed that the chrysin-trastuzumab combination induced a higher proportion of apoptotic nuclei compared with each treatment alone. These findings indicate that the combination of chrysin with trastuzumab synergistically enhances apoptosis in HER2-positive breast cancer cells, supporting the notion that apoptosis induction is a key mechanism underlying the anticancer efficacy of this combination. The results reinforce the therapeutic potential of incorporating natural compounds such as chrysin to potentiate the effect of targeted HER2 therapy.

Evaluation of HER2 expression in SKBR3 and BT-474 cells exposed to Chrysin and trastuzumab, and their combination

To determine the effects of chrysin, either alone or in combination with trastuzumab, on HER2 expression, HER2-positive breast cancer cells (SKBR3 and BT-474) were treated and analyzed using an anti-human FITC-conjugated antibody. Considering trastuzumab’s role as a specific targeting agent of HER2 receptors and the indirect supportive role of chrysin in downregulating HER2 signaling through amplifying autophagy24, it was hypothesized that combination therapy would result in a more pronounced reduction in HER2 expression compared to either agent alone.

. As shown in Fig. 3A and B, as expected, trastuzumab monotherapy significantly decreased HER2 expression in both cell lines (p < 0.001). HER2 expression levels decreased from 92.27 ± 5.45% (SKBR3 cells) and 78.57 ± 2.39% (BT-474 cells) in the control groups to 50.09 ± 4.02% and 38.56 ± 2.9%, respectively, in the trastuzumab-treated groups. Surprisingly, chrysin also reduced HER2 expression in a significant manner in both cell lines (p < 0.001 vs. the control group). In the two HER2-positive cell lines, combination therapy also demonstrated a significantly lower HER2 expression level (21.35 ± 3.1 and 22.89 ± 2.3% in SKBR3 and BT-474, respectively) compared to the control group (p < 0.001) and each monotherapy group, highlighting the important role of chrysin in downregulating HER2 expression.

A combination of Chrysin and trastuzumab enhances the mRNA expression of angiogenesis factors (VEGFR2 and Tie2)

Angiogenesis is a fundamental process in tumor growth and metastasis, primarily regulated by vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) and its receptor VEGFR2. Among VEGF receptors, VEGFR2 plays a central role by mediating endothelial cell proliferation, survival, migration, and vascular permeability, processes essential for tumor progression. Accordingly, the VEGF/VEGFR2 signaling axis represents an important target for inhibiting tumor angiogenesis and limiting cancer progression25. In this study, VEGFR2 expression was examined at the mRNA level to explore the potential anti-angiogenic effects of chrysin and trastuzumab, individually and in combination.

Tie2, initially known as an endothelial cell-specific receptor26, has been detected in monocytes and non-stromal cells in thyroid, breast, and gastric cancers27,28. Several human cancer cell lines, such as those derived from breast, prostate, and ovarian tissues, have been reported to express measurable levels of Tie2. As a key component of the angiopoietin/Tie2 signaling axis, Tie2 plays a crucial role in regulating tumor angiogenesis. Thus, Tie2 has emerged as an additional therapeutic target that complements VEGFR2 in the regulation of tumor angiogenesis, particularly in HER2-positive breast cancers29.

As illustrated in Fig. 4A In SKBR3 cells, treatment of SKBR3 cells with chrysin significantly suppressed the mRNA expression of both Tie2 and VEGFR2 compared with the untreated control group (p < 0.001). Trastuzumab alone also significantly reduced VEGFR2 expression (p < 0.001) but had no significant effect on Tie2 expression. The combination therapy resulted in a marked downregulation of Tie2 and VEGFR2 compared with the control group (p < 0.001) and further decreased their expression relative to each monotherapy (p < 0.001).

A similar trend was observed in BT-474 cells (Fig. 4B), as chrysin significantly decreased Tie2 and VEGFR2 expression (p < 0.001), while trastuzumab alone significantly reduced VEGFR2 (p < 0.001) but had no significant impact on Tie2 expression. Combination treatment led to a pronounced reduction in the expression of both genes compared to the control group (p < 0.001) and was significantly more effective than either chrysin or trastuzumab alone (p < 0.001 for VEGFR2) and (p < 0.01 and p < 0.001 for chrysin and trastuzumab, respectively, for Tie2). These results indicate that chrysin enhances trastuzumab’s inhibitory effects on angiogenesis-related signaling at the transcriptional level in HER2-positive breast cancer cells. This synergistic interaction provides further mechanistic evidence supporting the potential therapeutic benefit of combining a natural flavonoid with targeted HER2 inhibition to suppress tumor angiogenesis.

Combination of Chrysin and trastuzumab modulates STAT3 and PD-L1 protein expression

The effect of chrysin, trastuzumab, and their combination on protein levels of STAT3 and PD-L1 was assessed by western blot analysis in SKBR3 cell line. STAT3 is a key oncogenic transcription factor that drives tumor progression and immune evasion in HER2-positive breast cancer by regulating genes involved in proliferation, survival, and inflammation30. PD-L1, frequently upregulated in breast cancers, contributes to immune escape by suppressing T-cell activity31. Moreover, upregulation of p-STAT3 and PD-L1 has been implicated in resistance to trastuzumab therapy in HER2-positive breast cancers.

As shown in Fig. 5A, B and C, our results revealed that trastuzumab alone did not significantly alter the expression levels of p-STAT3 or PD-L1 in SKBR3 cells. In contrast, chrysin treatment led to a significant reduction in both p-STAT3 (p < 0.05) and PD-L1 (p < 0.01) levels compared to the untreated control. Notably, combination therapy resulted in a remarkable decrease in the levels of PD-L1 compared to the corresponding monotherapies (p < 0.001).

Furthermore, co-treatment of chrysin and trastuzumab significantly reduced the p-STAT3/STAT3 ratio compared to trastuzumab alone (p < 0.01), suggesting enhanced inhibition of oncogenic signaling and immune evasion pathways.

Discussion

Trastuzumab has substantially improved clinical outcomes for patients with HER2-positive breast cancer in both metastatic and adjuvant settings. However, many patients eventually experience disease progression due to intrinsic or acquired resistance. This highlights the urgent need for novel therapeutic strategies to enhance trastuzumab efficacy and overcome resistance mechanisms. Natural compounds, such as chrysin, have emerged as promising adjuncts due to their anticancer and chemosensitizing properties.

Chrysin, a natural flavonoid found in honey, propolis, and various plant extracts, has been reported to exert anticancer activity through multiple mechanisms, including inhibition of the STAT3 signaling pathway, induction of reactive oxygen species (ROS), activation of apoptosis, and modulation of autophagy13.

In this study, chrysin demonstrated a significant synergistic effect with trastuzumab in HER2-positive SKBR3 and BT-474 breast cancer cells. Consistent with previous reports, SKBR3 and BT-474 cells in our study exhibited only partial responses to trastuzumab, showing a plateau in growth inhibition rather than complete cytotoxicity. Although these cell lines do not represent acquired trastuzumab resistance models, the lack of increased inhibitory effect at higher trastuzumab concentrations reflects a degree of intrinsic insensitivity. This may be related to decreased HER2 expression or activation of alternative signaling pathways during treatment, limiting further growth suppression32,33. The combination treatment overcame this plateau, suggesting chrysin enhances trastuzumab-induced cytotoxicity through complementary mechanisms.

Our findings demonstrate that the combination therapy synergistically enhanced apoptosis and downregulated HER2 receptor expression more effectively than either agent alone. We observed that chrysin alone induced significant apoptosis in SKBR3 and BT-474 cells. Trastuzumab also promoted apoptosis, and the combination therapy synergistically intensified this effect, as confirmed by Annexin V/PI flow cytometry. Moreover, this synergistic induction of cell death was further validated by DAPI-TUNEL staining in the SKBR3 cell line, which revealed marked nuclear condensation and increased DNA fragmentation following combination treatment. These observations are consistent with the previous report showing that chrysin enhanced TRAIL-mediated apoptosis in MDA-MB-231 breast cancer cells34. Additionally, in MCF-7 cells, chrysin significantly increased ROS levels, activated caspase-3, and induced autophagy, as evidenced by the upregulation of LC3-II expression35. Collectively, these results suggest that apoptosis induction is a key mechanism underlying the enhanced cytotoxicity observed with the combination.

In this study, flow cytometry analysis of HER2 expression confirmed the high expression of HER2 receptor on SKBR3 and BT-474 cell lines. As a mechanistic insight, trastuzumab exerts its antitumor effects by inhibiting HER2-mediated signaling primarily through downregulation of the phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase (PI3K)/Akt and mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) pathways. This suppression leads to the upregulation of the cyclin-dependent kinase inhibitor p27 Kip1, thereby promoting cell cycle arrest and apoptosis in HER2-overexpressing breast cancer cells36. According to our expectation and in line with other studies, trastuzumab significantly reduced the HER2 expression16,32. This downregulation of receptors may render the HER2 receptor less recognizable by trastuzumab or prevent trastuzumab from binding effectively. Thus, decreasing HER2 expression on SKBR3 and BT-474 cells suggests fewer HER2 receptors on the cell surface, reducing drug effectiveness. Although lower HER2 expression reduces the efficacy of HER2-targeted agents like trastuzumab, chrysin can act through other mechanisms to reduce and attack cancer cells independently of HER2 levels. Our observations demonstrated that chrysin alone resulted in the significant reduction of HER2 expression in both cell lines.

Previous studies suggest that chrysin exerts anticancer effects in HER2-positive breast cancer cells, partly through the induction of enhanced autophagy mediated by endoplasmic reticulum (ER) stress. This increased autophagic flux leads to the degradation of cellular proteins, including HER2, resulting in reduced HER2 receptor levels on the cell surface. Mechanistic studies suggest that chrysin, especially in combination with the HER2 inhibitor pyrotinib, significantly potentiates ER stress and autophagy via upregulation of key regulatory molecules such as ZBTB16 through the miR-16-5p-mediated axis. This process leads to enhanced ubiquitination and degradation of oncogenic proteins such as HER2 and glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase (G6PD), disrupting tumor growth and survival pathways24. This allows chrysin to suppress cancer cell growth even when HER2 expression is low or reduced after HER2-targeted treatment, providing a complementary or synergistic effect when combined with trastuzumab or other HER2 inhibitors. Notably, a recent in silico and in vitro investigation of several phytochemicals demonstrated that chrysin displayed the highest binding affinity for the extracellular domain of HER237.

Clinical studies have shown that VEGF and its receptors (VEGFR1 and VEGFR2) hold prognostic significance in HER2-positive breast cancer, especially in patients treated with trastuzumab-based regimens. These markers have been investigated for their role in resistance and outcomes in HER2-positive breast cancer38. In breast cancer, HER2 and VEGF signaling pathways are linked, as upregulation of VEGF occurs in HER2-overexpressing breast cancer in the most aggressive cases39. While trastuzumab alone possesses antiangiogenic properties through the downregulation of tumor-produced VEGF40,41, combined targeting of HER2 and VEGF pathways has shown greater therapeutic efficacy in breast cancer treatment39.

In our study, chrysin significantly suppressed the mRNA levels of VEGFR2 and Tie2, two key regulators of angiogenesis. VEGFR2 mediates endothelial proliferation, migration, and vascular permeability, making it a central target in anti-angiogenic therapy25. Tie2, initially considered an endothelial-specific receptor, has also been detected in certain tumor cells and tumor-associated macrophages, where it regulates angiogenesis and vascular stabilization29 Combination therapy with chrysin and trastuzumab further enhanced the downregulation of these angiogenesis-related genes, suggesting a potentiated anti-angiogenic effect. These results align with reports that chrysin inhibits angiogenesis by blocking STAT3 activation and suppressing VEGF expression under hypoxic conditions42,43.

Trastuzumab has also been reported to inhibit angiogenesis and tumor growth by downregulating VEGF primarily through activation of the HER2–p38–TSP-1 pathway and suppression of the HER2–PI3K–AKT–VEGF/IL-8 signaling axis. Inhibition of the PI3K-AKT pathway by trastuzumab has been linked to reduced VEGF expression44. In line with these observations, we detected a significant reduction in VEGFR2 mRNA expression following trastuzumab treatment. However, trastuzumab alone did not significantly alter Tie2 mRNA expression in SKBR3 cells, highlighting the contributory role of chrysin in enhancing the anti-angiogenic effect of the combination therapy.

STAT3 is recognized as an oncogene, frequently hyperactivated in breast cancer, including SKBR3, but largely absent in normal breast epithelial cells45. Given its pivotal role in tumor progression, investigating STAT3-regulated genes is of particular interest. This enhanced STAT3 signaling is driven by elevated levels of autocrine production of fibronectin, epidermal growth factor, and IL-6. These factors collectively activate STAT3, which subsequently upregulates downstream targets MUC1 and MUC4, both involved in trastuzumab resistance. MUC1 promotes sustained HER2 signaling, while MUC4 sterically impedes trastuzumab binding by masking the HER2 epitope, thereby contributing to therapeutic resistance46. In our study, chrysin alone significantly lowered the p-STAT3/STAT3 ratio in SKBR3 cells relative to the control group, consistent with its established inhibitory effects on STAT3 activity14. Trastuzumab alone had no significant change, whereas combination therapy markedly reduced p-STAT3/STAT3 compared with both the control and trastuzumab-only groups, highlighting a synergistic inhibitory effect of STAT3 signaling.

The PD-1/PD-L1 immune checkpoint pathway plays a crucial role in tumor immune evasion. PD‑1 is expressed on lymphocytes, while its ligand PD‑L1 appears on both tumor cells and immune cells. As immune‑checkpoint proteins, PD‑1/PD‑L1 attenuate cytotoxic T‑cell activity within the TME, enabling cancer cells to evade immune surveillance31. In HER2-positive breast cancer, Kurozumi et al. reported that 17.5% of tumors were PD‑L1‑positive and associated with a better response to trastuzumab-containing neoadjuvant chemotherapy47.

Consistent with previous findings, western blot analysis confirmed that untreated SKBR3 cells express PD-L1. In the current study, treatment of SKBR3 cells with trastuzumab alone did not significantly alter PD-L1 expression. This finding aligns with the results of Chaganty et al., who reported that trastuzumab had no detectable effect on PD-L1 levels in monocultures of HER2-overexpressing breast cancer cells. However, in co-culture with human peripheral blood mononuclear cells, trastuzumab upregulated PD-L1 expression in the same cell lines. In other words, trastuzumab can induce PD-L1 upregulation within a more complex TME, as demonstrated in syngeneic tumor models expressing human HER248. However, chrysin treatment and, more prominently, the combination of chrysin and trastuzumab reduced PD-L1 expression in the SKBR3 cell line, which aligns with previous studies by this group reported reduced PD-L1 levels in melanoma cells treated with chrysin49. These results indicate that reduced levels of PD-L1 on tumor cells can shift the TME from immunosuppressive to immunostimulatory, enhancing anti-tumor immune responses and clinical outcomes. Lower PD-L1 levels can diminish the tumor’s ability to evade immune surveillance by preventing the inhibition of T cell activity. This can enhance the immune system’s capacity to recognize and kill cancer cells50. These findings imply that chrysin may downregulate PD-L1 as part of its anti-tumor mechanisms; further studies incorporating immune components are necessary to fully determine its effect on tumor immune escape and therapeutic response.

Conclusion

Trastuzumab resistance remains a critical challenge in managing HER2-positive breast cancer, necessitating novel approaches to improve therapeutic outcomes. Our findings demonstrate that chrysin, a natural flavonoid, effectively potentiates the anticancer activity of trastuzumab in SKBR3 and BT-474 cells through multiple mechanisms, including induction of apoptosis, downregulation of HER2 expression, and suppression of angiogenesis-related factors (VEGFR2, Tie2). Moreover, our findings revealed that a combination of chrysin with trastuzumab may inhibit STAT3 activation and suppress the immune checkpoint protein PD-L1 in tumor cells. The synergistic effects observed with the combination therapy suggest that chrysin not only targets tumor growth pathways but also could modulate factors that play an important role in tumor immunosuppression. However, further investigation employing experimentally induced trastuzumab-resistant cell lines is needed to validate resistance reversal and to elucidate the underlying molecular mechanisms. Moreover, the assessment of only two angiogenic markers does not fully capture the complexity of tumor angiogenesis, and functional angiogenesis assays are required to establish a robust conclusion on anti-angiogenic effects. Finally, in vivo studies incorporating immune components are warranted to validate our in vitro findings and to explore the clinical applicability of this combination therapy as a strategy to overcome resistance and improve therapeutic efficacy in HER2-positive breast cancer.

Prognostic value of brown adipocyte-related genes in colorectal cancer: a multi-omics and Mendelian randomization study
Authors: HuaSheng He, Yuanbiao Wang, Huilan Kuang, Linghan Tian, Zihan Wang, Lan Wang, Fenghong Lv, Zhixi Liu, Wei Wu, Yi Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundBrown adipocytes are vital in cancer’s emergence and progression. However, such significance of brown adipocytes-related genes (BARGs) in colorectal cancer (CRC) still awaits exploration. The potential prognostic mechanisms of BARGs in CRC were aimed to be explored in this study.MethodsDatasets related to CRC were obtained from public databases. Candidate genes were pinpointed by synthesizing the findings from analysis of differential expression and Weighted Gene Co-expression Network Analysis (WGCNA). Utilizing Mendelian randomization (MR), sensitivity analysis, and the Steiger test, the feature genes causally linked to CRC were precisely pinpointed. Then, prognostic genes were identified using univariate Cox analysis and machine learning algorithms, and a prognostic model was developed and validated. In addition, the construction and evaluation of nomograms, analysis of the immune microenvironment, drug sensitivity, and functional enrichment were also carried out.ResultsAltogether, 168 characteristic genes were pinpointed through MR analysis. ADD2, PDE1B, LZTS1, and PCSK5 were identified as prognostic genes. The area under the curve (AUC) values of receiver operating characteristic (ROC) for both the prognostic model and the nomogram were greater than 0.6, indicating high accuracy. Additionally, positive correlations were found among the differentially expressed immune cells, and PDE1B exhibited the most significant positive correlation with regulatory T cells. A variety of chemotherapeutic drugs were more significantly effective in treating CRC patients in low-risk group (LRG). Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) demonstrated that the pathways linked to cell microenvironment remodeling and migration regulation were significantly enriched between high-risk group (HRG) and LRG.ConclusionADD2, PDE1B, LZTS1, and PCSK5 were identified as prognostic genes. A prognostic model related to BARGs in CRC was established, offering fresh perspectives on the prognostic treatment of CRC.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12672-025-04262-9.

Introduction

Colorectal cancer (CRC) is one of the most common and deadly cancers affecting the digestive system globally, with rising incidence and mortality rates [1]. Originating from uncontrolled epithelial cell proliferation in the colon or rectum, CRC is driven by a complex interplay of genetic predisposition and environmental factors, including high-fat diets, chronic inflammation, sedentary lifestyles, and genetic mutations [2, 3]. Despite advancements in early detection, surgical interventions, and systemic therapies—such as chemotherapy, targeted therapy, and immunotherapy—CRC poses a formidable clinical challenge, particularly in advanced stages where tumor heterogeneity, metastatic potential, and treatment resistance contribute to a dismal five-year survival rate [4, 5]. These persistent obstacles highlight the urgent need for novel prognostic biomarkers and therapeutic targets to enhance CRC management and improve patient outcomes.

Brown adipose tissue (BAT) has garnered increasing attention due to its distinct metabolic properties and potential role in tumor biology [6]. Unlike white adipose tissue, which primarily serves as an energy reservoir, BAT actively dissipates energy through mitochondrial uncoupling protein 1 (UCP1)-mediated thermogenesis [6]. This metabolic activity contributes to systemic energy homeostasis and has been implicated in various pathological conditions [7]. Evidence suggests that adipocytes influence tumor progression by modulating lipid metabolism, cytokine signaling, and immune cell recruitment within the tumor microenvironment (TME). Although direct studies on BAT’s role in CRC are limited, research in cancers with similar metabolic profiles, such as hepatocellular carcinoma [8] and pancreatic cancer [9], has indicated its involvement in tumorigenesis. Moreover, BAT activation in mice increases energy expenditure, reducing body weight gain [10], while obesity—linked to CRC and other cancers like postmenopausal breast cancer—may be mitigated by bariatric surgery, which lowers CRC incidence [11–13]. Despite these insights, the mechanisms by which brown adipocyte-related genes (BARGs) contribute to CRC pathogenesis remain poorly understood, presenting a critical gap in knowledge that could reveal new prognostic and therapeutic targets.

Mendelian randomization (MR) is a powerful epidemiological technique that utilizes genetic variants as instrumental variables to establish potential causal links between risk factors and disease outcomes. This methodology rests on three key assumptions: (1) the genetic variant should have a strong correlation with the exposure of interest (relevance assumption); (2) it must be unassociated with confounding variables (independence assumption); and (3) its effect on the outcome must occur solely through the exposure, with no alternative causal pathways (exclusion restriction assumption) [14, 15]. Compared to conventional observational studies, MR reduces confounding bias and mitigates the effects of reverse causation. Recent MR-based studies have successfully identified metabolic and immune-related genes linked to CRC susceptibility and progression [16, 17]. Utilizing Mendelian randomization to explore the relationship between brown adipose tissue-related genes (BARGs) and colorectal cancer may provide valuable insights into the metabolic mechanisms of the disease and identify promising therapeutic targets.

This study systematically integrates transcriptomic data from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA), Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO), and the IEU OpenGWAS database to identify prognostic BARGs in CRC. we identified four key genes with prognostic significance using differential expression analysis, weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA), MR analysis, and machine learning algorithms. A predictive model was constructed and validated, demonstrating strong performance in survival outcome prediction. Furthermore, functional enrichment analysis, immune infiltration characterization, tumor mutation burden (TMB) evaluation, and drug sensitivity assessment were conducted to explore potential mechanistic insights. These findings offering insights into CRC pathogenesis and precision medicine strategies.

Materials and methods

Data source

The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) (https://www.cancer.gov/) was utilized to retrieve the datasets TCGA-COAD and TCGA-READ related to CRC (accessed on January 9, 2025). These 2 datasets were combined and named TCGA-CRC. As the training set, TCGA-CRC included transcriptome data, somatic mutation data, clinical and survival data, with a total of 669 samples (618 CRC tumor and 51 adjacent control tissue samples, and there were 585 CRC tumor tissue samples corresponding to the survival information). For external validation, the GSE1294 (GPL96) dataset was retrieved from GEO (http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/) and utilized as the validation set, which included 62 CRC tumor tissue samples with survival information (accessed on January 9, 2025). Moreover, 102 BARGs were extracted through the literature [7](Supplementary Table 1) (accessed on January 9, 2025). The MR data for CRC (ebi-a-GCST012876) was retrieved from the IEU Open GWAS database (https://gwas.mrcieu.ac.uk/datasets/), which included 26,554 Europeans (11,895 cases and 14,659 controls) and 38,370,461 single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs). The expression quantitative trait loci (eQTLs) data of exposure factors also came from the IEU Open GWAS database (accessed on January 9, 2025).

Differential expression analysis

In the TCGA-CRC dataset, DESeq2 (v 3.4.1) package [18] was utilized to screen for differentially expressed genes (DEGs) between CRC and control samples (|log2fold-change (FC)| >1 and adjust.P < 0.05). Ggplot2 (v 3.4.1) package [19] and Complexpheatmap (v 2.14.0) package [20] were employed to generate the volcano plot and heatmap of the DEGs.

WGCNA

In the TCGA-CRC dataset, for the CRC samples that have survival information, BARGs scores were calculated by utilizing the ssGSEA method in the GSVA (v 1.46.0) package [21]. In accordance with the optimal cut-off value of the BARGs scores, CRC samples with survival information were separated into high-/low-score groups. The Kaplan-Meier (KM) survival curves of the high-/low-score groups were plotted utilizing the survminer (v 0.4.9) package [22] (log-rank test, P < 0.05). WGCNA was performed utilizing the WGCNA (v 1.71) package [23]. Utilizing the Euclidean distance of the expression profiles of CRC samples with survival information, hierarchical clustering was undertaken to identify and exclude outliers. The optimal soft threshold (power) was determined by the criteria that the scale-free fit index (R²) was equal to 0.85, and mean connectivity was near 0. Following the screened soft threshold, a scale-free network was formed, and these genes were partitioned into several modules, and a hierarchical clustering tree was generated. Taking the BARGs scores as phenotypic traits, the correlation matrix between the BARGs scores and co-expression modules was calculated through Pearson correlation analysis (|correlation coefficient (cor)| >0.30, P < 0.05). The module with the highest absolute value of cor was designated as the key module, its genes being key module genes.

Investigation of the functions of candidate genes

Overlap analysis between DEGs and key module genes was carried out utilizing the ggvenn (v 1.7.3) package [24] to detect the candidate genes. Afterward, Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) were performed on candidate genes with the aid of cluster profile (v 4.7.1.3) package [25] (P < 0.05). Protein-protein interaction (PPI) network was composed utilizing STRING database (https://www.string-db.org) (confidence = 0.90) and illustrated utilizing Cytoscape (v 3.9.1) software [26].

Identification of feature genes by MR

In the MR analysis, the eQTLs of the candidate genes were utilized as exposure factors, with CRC as the outcome variable. Firstly, the extract_instruments function in the TwoSampleMR (v 0.5.6) package [27] was utilized to first retrieve the exposure factors and the outcome variable and then screen the instrumental variables (IVs). The screening process included: (1) setting clump = true, r² = 0.001, and kb = 10 to remove the SNPs in linkage disequilibrium (LD); (2) setting P < 1 × 10⁻⁶ to search for SNPs that were significantly associated with the exposure factors; (3) utilizing an F-statistic >10 to remove the weak instrumental variables; (4) requiring the number of SNPs to be ≥ 3 to exclude the invalid exposure factors; (5) following the screened IVs, removing the SNPs that were significantly associated with the outcome (proxies = TRUE, Esq = 0.8). Then, the harmonise_data function was applied to calibrate the effect alleles and sizes.

MR analysis was carried out by combining 5 algorithms (MR Egger [28], Weighted median [29], Inverse variance weighted (IVW) [30], Simple mode [31], Weighted mode [32]), with IVW being the main method. If the P-value of the IVW method was less than 0.05, it signified that there was a significant causal association between the exposure factor and the outcome variable. When the odds ratio (OR) exceeded 1, the corresponding factor was considered a risk factor. Conversely, if the OR was below 1, the factor was deemed a protective factor.

Subsequently, the mr_scatter_plot function was utilized to generate scatter plots, the mr_forest_plot function was utilized to create forest plots, and the mr_funnel_plot function was utilized to draw funnel plots. To judge the soundness of the MR analysis results, sensitivity analyses were undertaken. Firstly, the mr_heterogeneity function was utilized to conduct a heterogeneity test (Cochran’s Q-statistic, P > 0.05). Then, the mr_pleiotropy_test function was utilized to conduct a horizontal pleiotropy test (P > 0.05). In addition, the mr_leaveoneout function was utilized to conduct a leave-one-out (LOO) test to demonstrate the soundness of the MR analysis results.

Finally, the steiger_filtering function was utilized to conduct a Steiger analysis to determine the direction of the causal relationship. The criteria for passing the Steiger analysis were correct causal direction = TRUE and P < 0.05. The genes that passed the sensitivity analysis and those for which the judgment was TRUE and P < 0.05 were identified as feature genes for subsequent analysis.

Screening for prognostic genes

In the TCGA-CRC dataset, for the CRC samples with survival information, univariate Cox analysis was performed on the feature genes utilizing the survival (v 3.7-0) package [22] (HR ≠ 1, P < 0.01), and proportional hazards (PH) assumption test of the selected genes was carried out (P >0.05). Subsequently, the hazard ratio (HR) values of the genes that passed the PH assumption test were compared with the OR values in the MR analysis to screen the candidate prognostic genes. After that, least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression analysis was undertaken utilizing glmnet (v 4.1.4) package [33]. Genes not penalized to 0 were regarded as prognostic genes and harnessed to formulate a prognostic model. Meanwhile, for genes with opposite directions of HR and OR values, we stratified by tumor stage and performed univariate Cox regression analyses using the survival package (v 3.7-0).

Evaluation and verification of prognostic models

The random survival forest (RSF) model was constructed utilizing the randomForestSRC (v 3.2.3) package [34], and the risk scores for each CRC patient were obtained (ntree = 7, mtry = 5). Subsequently, to enhance the interpretability of the RSF model, SurvSHAP(t) explanatory analysis was performed using the survex package (v1.2.0) [35] to clarify the mechanism by which each feature influences the prediction results. Additionally, by the optimal cut-off value of the risk scores, CRC patients were classified into high-/low-risk groups (HRG, LRG). The effectiveness of the prognostic model was evaluated following the AUC within different time intervals (AUC >0.6). Finally, the same method was utilized for verification in the validation set.

Construction and evaluation of nomogram

Firstly, univariate Cox analysis was undertaken on age, stage, gender, pathologic M, pathologic N, pathologic T, and risk scores through survival (v 3.7-0) package to screen out the features that were highly correlated with prognosis (HR ≠ 1, P < 0.05), and the PH assumption was verified (P >0.05). Then, multivariate Cox analysis was conducted utilizing the survival (v 3.7-0) package (HR ≠ 1, P < 0.05), and PH assumption was also verified (P >0.05) to determine the features that could serve as independent prognostic indicators. Subsequently, the nomogram was drawn utilizing the rms (v 6.5.0) package [37]. Calibration curves were plotted utilizing the rms (v 6.5.0) package, and ROC curves were generated utilizing the survivalROC (v 1.18.0) package [38] (AUC >0.6) to judge the reliability of the nomogram. In addition, CRC patients were sorted into different clinical subgroups in accordance with clinical data. The variations in risk scores between distinct clinical subgroups were measured utilizing the Wilcoxon test (P < 0.05).

Immune microenvironment analysis

The enrichment scores of 28 types of immune cells in CRC patients with survival information in TCGA-CRC were estimated utilizing the ssGSEA algorithm in the GSVA (v 1.46.0) package. Subsequently, the Wilcoxon test was applied to compare the differences in the abundances of immune cells between HRG and LRG (P < 0.05), and the differentially abundant immune cells were obtained. Then, Spearman correlation analyses were respectively conducted between differentially abundant immune cells and between these cells and prognostic genes utilizing the psych (v 2.2.9) package [39] (|cor| >0.30, P < 0.05). Meanwhile, to deepen the understanding of the immune infiltration landscape, the CIBERSORT deconvolution algorithm [40]was used to evaluate differences in immune cell abundances between the HRG and LRG (P < 0.05). with samples having P >0.05 excluded. Furthermore, the ESTIMATE analysis was implemented between the HRG and the LRG utilizing the ESTIMATE algorithm, and the results were visualized using the ggplot2 (v 3.4.1) package. Altogether, 22 immune checkpoints were retrieved from the literature [41], and the Wilcoxon test was utilized to study the differences in the expression levels of these immune checkpoints(P < 0.05).

Analysis of mutation landscape, microsatellite instability (MSI) and drug sensitivity

To explore the status of gene mutations between the HRG and the LRG, following the somatic mutation data of TCGA-CRC, the waterfall plots were generated with the maftools (v 2.18.0) package [42] to respectively display the top 20 genes with the highest mutation frequencies among CRC patients in HRG and LRG. To quantify the accumulation of genomic mutations in tumor cells, Spearman correlation analysis was performed using the psych (v 2.2.9) package in samples with survival information from the TCGA-CRC dataset to evaluate the relationship between tumor mutational burden (TMB) and risk scores (|cor| >0.30, P < 0.05). Additionally, the Wilcoxon test was used to compare differences in TMB scores between the HRG and LRG (P < 0.05). MSI analysis serves as a key tool for prognostic evaluation and precise treatment decision-making. Therefore, in the TCGA-CRC dataset, MSI scores of CRC samples were retrieved using the TIDE online database (http://tide.dfci.harvard.edu/). Spearman correlation analysis was performed to assess the relationship between MSI scores and risk scores (|cor| >0.30, P < 0.05). Additionally, the Wilcoxon test was used to compare differences in MSI scores between the high-risk and low-risk groups (P < 0.05). In addition, data from 198 chemotherapeutic drugs were obtained from the Genomics of Genomics of Drug Sensitivity in Cancer 2 (GDSC2) database (https://www.cancerrxgene.org/). The 50% inhibiting concentration (IC50) values of the drugs were determined utilizing a pRRophetic (v 0.5) package [43]. The Wilcoxon test was utilized to compare the differences in the IC50 values of different chemotherapeutic drugs between the samples of HRG and LRG to evaluate the therapeutic effects of the chemotherapeutic drugs (P < 0.05). The drugs ranked in the top 10 in descending order of the P values were visualized. Additionally, the drug sensitivity results of first-line therapeutic drugs for CRC were presented, including Erlotinib, Irinotecan, Olaparib, Oxaliplatin, and 5-Fluorouracil, were also presented.

Enrichment analysis

GSEA was performed between HRG and LRG in the TCGA-CRC dataset. C2.cp.kegg.v7.4.symbols.gmt was extracted from the Molecular Signatures Database (MSigDB) (https://www.gsea-msigdb.org/gsea/msigdb/) as the background set. Firstly, differential analysis between HRG and LRG was carried out utilizing the DESeq2 (v 3.4.1) package, and the genes were ranked in accordance with the log2FC. Subsequently, GSEA was conducted on the HRG and the LRG utilizing clusterProfiler (v 4.7.1.3) package (|normalized enrichment score (NES)| >1 and P < 0.05). Moreover, the top 5 pathways ranked in descending order of the P values were illustrated utilizing the enrichplot (v 1.18.0) package [44].

Molecular regulatory network construction

For investigating the molecular regulatory mechanisms underlying the brown adipocyte-related prognostic genes in CRC, we first focused on the brown adipocyte-linked prognostic genes identified in this study as core targets. The NetworkAnalyst platform (http://www.networkanalyst.ca) was applied to predict microRNAs (miRNAs) targeting prognostic genes in miRTarbase database (v 9.0). Subsequently, to explore upstream regulatory molecules, we further predicted lncRNAs that can regulate the above-identified miRNAs using the miRNet database (https://www.mirnet.ca/miRNet/home.xhtml). Ultimately, the Cytoscape (v 3.9.1) software was employed to establish a lncRNA-miRNA-mRNA regulatory network.

Expression analysis of prognostic genes

The immunohistochemical staining maps of prognostic genes were obtained from the Human Protein Atlas (HPA) database (http://www.proteinatlas.org), and the differences in protein expression of prognostic genes between CRC patients and controls were compared. To clarify expression patterns of prognostic genes in CRC and control samples, the expression of prognostic genes in all samples of the TCGA-CRC dataset was analyzed by the Wilcoxon test (P < 0.05). The UALCAN web tool (https://ualcan.path.uab.edu/) provides data from TCGA to analyze whether differential expression of the four candidate genes in COAD is associated with prognosis.

Cell culture and quantitative real-time PCR

The human intestinal epithelial cell line NCM460, CRC cell lines HCT116, SW480, and DLD-1 were obtained from the cell bank of the Chinese Academy of Sciences (CAS), cultured in Roswell Park Memorial Institute (RPMI)-1640 medium (GIBCO) and 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS, GIBCO), and placed in an incubator at 37℃ with 5% CO2. Total cellular RNA was extracted using a TRIzol kit (LABLEAD, Beijing, China) according to the manufacturer’s instructions and quantified using a Thermo Nanodrop lite spectrophotometer. Reverse transcription was performed using ALL-in-one-Strand Synthesis (LABLEAD, Beijing, China) to obtain cDNA. mRNA expression was analyzed using the PerfectStart® II Probe qPCR SuperMix kit. The relative mRNA expression was quantified using the cycling threshold (Ct) value and normalized using the 2−ΔΔCt method. PCR primer sequences and small interfering RNAs (siRNAs) were synthesized by Sangon Biotech (Shanghai, China). The sequences of PCSK5 siRNAs were as follows: F, 5’-GCUAAGACAUCGAAUAAUAAA-3’; R, 5’-UAUUAUUCGAUGUCUUAGCUG-3’. The primer sequences are summarized in Table 1. RPS13 was used as a reference control.

CCK8 detection

HCT116 and SW480 cells were seeded in 6-well plates and transfected with PCSK5 siRNA using Lipofectamine™ 3000 (Invitrogen) and Opti-MEM (Gibco). Upon reaching ~ 90% confluence, cells were trypsinized and re-seeded into 96-well plates at a density of 3 × 10³ cells/well. Cell viability was assessed at 0, 1, 2, 3 and 4 day post-seeding using the Cell Counting Kit-8 (Solarbio) according to the manufacturer’s instructions, and absorbance was measured at 450 nm.

Cell migration and transwell

At 48 h post-transfection, a sterile 200 µL pipette tip was used to scratch the cell monolayer. Detached cells were removed with PBS, and images were captured at 0 h under an inverted microscope (×40). Cells were then cultured at 37 °C with 5% CO₂, and images were acquired at 12 and 24 h. Migration rates were calculated based on wound closure relative to the initial scratch width. Cell invasion was assessed using Matrigel-coated Transwell chambers (8 μm pore size; Corning, USA). Briefly, membranes were precoated with 100 µL diluted Matrigel at 4 °C overnight. Serum-free cell suspensions (5 × 10⁴ cells/mL, 200 µL) were seeded into the upper chambers, while 600 µL medium containing 20% FBS was added to the lower chambers as a chemoattractant. After 24 h of incubation at 37 °C, non-invading cells were removed from the upper surface with a cotton swab. Cells that penetrated to the lower surface were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 20 min, stained with crystal violet for 5 min, and counted under a light microscope (×100).

Statistical analysis

GraphPad Prism 9.0 software (GraphPad Software Inc., San Diego, CA, USA) and R (v 4.2.2) was utilized to conduct statistical analysis. Difference analysis between two groups was executed via the Wilcoxon test (P < 0.05). The results represent at least three replicate experiments and are expressed as mean ± SD. Notably, **** represented P < 0.0001, *** represented P < 0.001, ** represented P < 0.01, * represented P < 0.05, and ns represented P > 0.05 (no significant differences).

Then, the CRC samples were separated into a high-score group (N = 75) and a low-score group (N = 510) in accordance with the optimal cut-off value of the BARGs scores, which was 3.203779 (Supplementary Table 2). The KM curves indicated that the BARGs scores were markedly linked to the survival of CRC (P < 0.013) (Fig. 1C). There were no outlier CRC samples (Fig. 1D), When R2 was equal to 0.85 and mean connectivity was near 0, the optimal soft threshold (power) was established as 9 (Fig. 1E). Subsequently, a co-expression matrix was established, and 16 gene modules were determined (Fig. 1F-G). MEblue (cor = 0.7227, P < 0.001) was regarded as the key module, and 2,335 key module genes were extracted.

Feature genes were identified through MR analysis

MR analysis revealed that among the 1,256 candidate genes, 256 genes had a significant causal relationship with CRC (PIVW < 0.05). Among them, 135 genes were designated risk factors, and 121 genes were designated protective factors. For example, PCSK5 was a risk factor (OR = 1.117, 95% confidence interval (CI) = 1.075–1.161, P < 0.001) (Supplementary Table 5). Given the large number of genes, PCSK5 was used as an example for demonstration. The scatter plot showed that PCSK5 had a positive slope, indicating that it was a risk factor (Fig. 3A). The results of the forest plot demonstrated that in the IVW method, the MR effect size of PCSK5 was greater than 0 (Fig. 3B). The funnel plot indicated that the IVs of PCSK5 were evenly arranged on both sides of the IVW line, suggesting that the MR analysis corresponded to Mendel’s second law (Fig. 3C). The results for the other 255 gene sets are shown in Supplementary Figs. 1 A-C. In addition, for 215 out of the 256 genes, no evidence of heterogeneity was found, and the fixed-effect model was used (P > 0.05), while for 41 genes, heterogeneity was present, and the random-effect model was utilized (P < 0.05). In the horizontal pleiotropy test, the P values of 75 genes were all less than 0.05, indicating the presence of horizontal pleiotropy, so these 75 genes were excluded (Supplementary Table 6). The LOO analysis of the 256 genes found no any outliers, indicating the soundness of the MR results. For example, in PCSK5, by gradually removing each SNP, the influence of the remaining SNPs on the outcome variable did not change significantly (Fig. 3D, Supplementary Fig. 1D). The Steiger test found that the causal relationships of 21 genes were TRUE, but the P values of 19 of these genes were Not Available (NA), and the P values of 2 genes were greater than 0.05, indicating that the causal relationships between 235 genes and CRC were TRUE and unaffected by reverse causality (Supplementary Table 7). Combining the results of the sensitivity analysis, 168 genes were retained as feature genes for subsequent analysis (Supplementary Table 8).

A prognostic model was constructed and evaluated

After performing univariate Cox analysis and the PH assumption test, we obtained 11 genes (Fig. 4A, Supplementary Fig. 2). Subsequently, by comparing the HR values of these 11 genes with the OR values in the MR analysis, it was found that the trends of the HR values and OR values of LZTS1, ADD2, PCSK5, PDE1B, and CD36 were consistent (Supplementary Table 9). For genes with opposite directions of HR and OR values, univariate Cox regression analyses stratified by tumor stage showed that only ADAMTSL4 retained an association consistent with the direction of OR in stage subgroups (Supplementary Fig. 3A). Then, through the optimal lambda value of 0.007809306 in the LASSO analysis, 4 prognostic genes were identified (Fig. 4B-C), namely, ADD2, PDE1B, LZTS1, and PCSK5, and a prognostic model was established. After constructing the RSF model, it was found that LZTS1 had the highest importance (Fig. 4D). Besides, survSHAP(t) interpretive analysis revealed that PCSK5 exhibited the highest importance, indicating the strongest explanatory power for survival outcomes, followed by PCE1B and LZTS1 in descending order, whereas A2D2 exerted the weakest influence. Moreover, the SurvSHAP(t) values of each feature displayed heterogeneous fluctuations over follow - up time; in particular, the SurvSHAP(t) value of PCSK5 rose significantly during the mid - term follow - up, suggesting that this feature made an enhanced predictive contribution to the survival risk in the middle and late stages (Supplementary Fig. 3B). Besides, the aggregated SurvSHAP(t) value analysis illustrated the association between each feature’s expression level and its contribution to survival prediction. For PCSK5, data points spanned both negative and positive SurvSHAP(t) values, with high PCSK5 expression predominantly clustering in the positive region, suggesting that high PCSK5 expression was more likely to augment predicted survival risk (Supplementary Fig. 3C). In accordance with the optimal cut-off value of the risk score, which was 22.28456, the CRC patients with survival information in the TCGA-CRC dataset were sorted into the HRG (N = 211) and the LRG (N = 374). The risk curve indicated that the prognosis of the HRG was poor (Fig. 4E-F), and the expressions of ADD2, PDE1B, LZTS1, and PCSK5 were higher in the HRG (Fig. 4G). The K-M curves illustrated that the prognosis of the HRG was worse (P < 0.0001) (Fig. 4H). In ROC curves, the AUCs predicted at 1-, 2-, and 3-year intervals were 0.84, 0.88, and 0.86, respectively (Fig. 4I), which confirmed the validity of the model. In addition, the prognostic model was validated in the validation set (Fig. 5A-E), and similar results were obtained, demonstrating that the prognosis of the HRG was worse and highlighting the universality of the prognostic model.

Nomogram was constructed and evaluated

It was discovered through univariate Cox analysis and the PH assumption test that, apart from gender, age, stage, pathologic M, pathologic N, pathologic T, and risk scores also significantly influenced the survival of CRC patients (P < 0.05) (Fig. 6A, Supplementary Fig. 4). Subsequently, multivariate Cox analysis ascertained that age, stage, pathologic M, pathologic N and risk score were independent prognostic factors (Fig. 6B), and a nomogram was constructed (Fig. 6C). The slopes of the calibration curves for 1, 2, and 3 years were close to 1 (Fig. 6D), and the AUCs predicted at 1-, 2-, and 3-year intervals in the ROC curves were 0.90, 0.91, and 0.92, respectively (Fig. 6E), which implies that the nomogram has an extremely high predictive precision.

The immune microenvironment of CRC was explored

Figure 7A showed enrichment scores of 28 types of immune cells in HRG and LRG. Among them, 16 types of immune cells had significant infiltration differences (P < 0.05) (Fig. 7B), such as macrophages. Moreover, the enrichment scores of all the differential immune cells in HRG were higher than those in LRG, which might be related to the immune escape mechanism. All 16 types of differential immune cells were positively correlated with each other (Fig. 7C), among which macrophages and myeloid-derived suppressor cells showed the most significant positive correlation (cor = 0.93, p < 0.001). The prognostic genes were mainly positively associated with the differential immune cells. PDE1B had the most significant positive correlation with regulatory T cells (cor = 0.70, p < 0.001) (Fig. 7D, Supplementary Table 10). Supplementary Fig. 5A presented the infiltration abundances of 22 types of immune cells. Five differential cell types were identified between the HRG and LRG (P < 0.05), namely CD4 + memory activated T cells, M0 macrophages, M2 macrophages, eosinophils, and neutrophils. Among them, the infiltration proportions of CD4 + memory activated T cells and M0 macrophages in the LRG were higher than those in the HRG (Supplementary Fig. 5B), suggesting that the LRG may possess stronger anti-tumor immune activity, which was associated with the low-risk prognostic characteristics. Moreover, ESTIMATE analysis exhibited that stromal score and ESTIMATE score in HRG were significantly higher than those in LRG (P < 0.05) (Fig. 7E). There were significant differences in the expressions of CD276, HHLA2, VSIR, CD40, HAVCR2, CD70, and PDCD1LG2 between HRG and LRG (P < 0.05) (Fig. 7F). The above results provided a reference basis for exploring the immunotherapy of CRC.

Analysis of mutation landscape, MSI, and drug sensitivity in HRG and LRG

In accordance with the somatic mutation data in TCGA-CRC, the top 20 genes with the highest mutation rates in HRG were determined to be APC, TP53, TTN, KRAS, SYNE1, MUC16, PIK3CA, FAT4, FLG, USH2A, RYR2, ZFHX3, ABCA13, LRP1B, CSMD3, OBSCN, SDC1, DNAH5, PCLO and NEB respectively (Fig. 8A). Meanwhile, the top 20 genes in the LRG were determined to be APC, TP53, TTN, KRAS, PIK3CA, SYNE1, MUC16, EAT4, OBSCN, RYR2, DNAH5, FAT4, ZFHX3, CSMD1, LRP1B, CSMD3 and HYDIN respectively (Fig. 8A). Among them, APC, TP53, TTN and KRAS had the highest mutation frequencies in CRC samples of both the HRG and the LRG. In addition, the risk score exhibited a positive correlation with TMB (cor = 0.11, P = 0.011), but the correlation was weak. Unfortunately, no significant difference in TMB was observed between the HRG and LRG (P > 0.05) (Fig. 8B), suggesting that TMB might be slightly involved in the composition of the risk signature. MSI analysis showed that MSI exhibited a negative correlation with the risk score (cor = -0.085, P = 0.04), and the MSI score in the LRG was higher than that in the HRG (Fig. 8C). Additionally, IC50 values of 198 chemotherapy drugs in HRG and LRG were estimated (Supplementary Table 11), and then the top 10 drugs with significant differences were presented (P < 0.001) (Fig. 8D). The results showed that the therapeutic effects of multiple chemotherapy drugs on CRC patients in the LRG were more remarkable. Additionally, first-line drugs for CRC, erlotinib and oxaliplatin, exhibited significant differences between HRG and LRG, with low-risk patients showing greater sensitivity to these drugs (P < 0.01) (Fig. 8E).

Functional enrichment was helpful for exploring the potential mechanism for CRC

Overall, 71 pathways were significantly enriched between HRG and LRG by the GSEA (Supplementary Table 12), such as cell adhesion molecules (CAMs) (Fig. 8F), indicating that the remodeling of the cellular microenvironment and the regulation of cell migration might have vital impacts on the immune microenvironment of CRC.

The expression of prognostic genes was verified

Various upstream regulatory elements targeting prognostic genes were predicted. Specifically, ADD2, PDE1B, LZTS1, and PCSK5 were regulated by 51, 19, 2, and 1 miRNAs, respectively. Additionally, 58 lncRNAs corresponding to these miRNAs were also predicted. A lncRNA-miRNA-mRNA regulatory network was constructed to visualize their complex regulatory relationships, such as PCAT6-hsa-miR-4644-ADD2, LINC01356-hsa-miR-149-5p-PDE1B, LINC01128-hsa-miR-214-3p-LZTS1, and LINC01343-hsa-miR-107-PCSK5 (Fig. 9A). Elucidation of these regulatory elements was essential for understanding the mechanisms through which prognostic genes influenced CRC progression.

Effects of PCSK5 on the proliferation, migration, and invasion of CRC cells

Using the UALCAN database, we evaluated the prognostic significance of four candidate genes in CRC (Supplementary Fig. 6). Among them, LZTS1 and PCSK5 exhibited significant associations with patient survival. Notably, PCSK5 expression was markedly reduced in CRC tissues (Fig. 10B), and higher expression correlated with poorer survival outcomes (Fig. 10A). Based on these findings, PCSK5 was selected for further functional validation.To explore its functional role, PCSK5 siRNA was transfected into HCT116 and SW480 cells (Fig. 10C). CCK-8 assays demonstrated that PCSK5 knockdown markedly reduced the proliferative capacity of HCT116 and SW480 cells (Fig. 10D). Wound healing assays revealed that knockdown PCSK5 significantly impaired the migratory ability of both cell lines (Fig. 10E). Transwell results showed that the invasive ability of HCT116 and SW480 cells was significantly decreased after PCSK5 knockdown (Fig. 10F).

Discussion

Colorectal cancer remains a leading cause of cancer-related mortality worldwide, with metabolic factors increasingly recognized as critical contributors to tumor progression [4]. Among these, BAT has gained attention due to its role in systemic energy homeostasis and its potential implications in malignancies [7]. However, the specific involvement of BAT in CRC pathogenesis has yet to be fully elucidated. In this study, we identified four brown adipocyte-related prognostic genes (BARGs)—ADD2, PDE1B, LZTS1, and PCSK5—through comprehensive bioinformatics analysis. By constructing a prognostic model, we demonstrated its strong predictive capability in CRC patient survival. These findings provide novel insights into the molecular mechanisms underlying CRC and offer potential therapeutic targets for clinical application.

Notably, in this study, the HR directions of the prognostic genes ADD2, PDE1B, LZTS1, and PCSK5 were consistent with the OR directions obtained from the MR analysis. HR reflects the association of a gene with survival outcomes in CRC patients, primarily capturing aspects of disease progression such as tumor advancement, metastasis, and treatment response [45]. In contrast, the OR derived from MR analysis infers a causal relationship between the gene and CRC onset risk, indicating its potential role in initiating tumorigenesis [46]. Thus, HR and OR represent distinct dimensions of disease biology, HR reflecting progression and OR reflecting initiation, such that the same gene may play divergent or even opposing roles at different stages of CRC development. In the present study, we prioritized genes showing concordant HR and OR directions as prognostic candidates, as this alignment in both disease initiation and progression phases supports the robustness and reliability of the associations. For genes with discordant HR and OR directions, univariate Cox subgroup analysis stratified by tumor stage revealed that only ADAMTSL4 displayed concordance with OR. Previous evidence indicates that ADAMTSL4 overexpression in monocytes is associated with poor prognosis in CRC, underscoring its potential as a prognostic biomarker. Collectively, these findings highlight the importance of integrating clinical characteristics such as disease stage when interpreting genes with inconsistent HR and OR directions, to avoid mischaracterizing their biological roles due to differences across disease phases.

Our study identified four key genes with prognostic significance in CRC, each exhibiting potential roles in tumorigenesis and progression, although their specific functions in CRC remain underexplored. ADD2 (Beta-Adducin) is a structural membrane protein involved in cytoskeletal organization and signal transduction. ADD2 has been implicated in various pathological conditions, including neurological disorders and cardiovascular disease [47]. MiR-218 inhibits metastasis and invasion of endometrial cancer through negative regulation of ADD2 [48]. Recent studies suggest that aberrant ADD2 methylation is associated with early CRC detection, supporting its potential utility as a diagnostic biomarker [49]. Abnormal ADD2 methylation has been associated with early colorectal cancer detection. Some studies have found that the promoter region of the ADD2 gene is hypermethylated in colorectal cancer [49], whereas in this study, the ADD2 gene was lowly expressed in patients with CRC, which led to the speculation that the ADD2 gene may have a potential cancer-suppressing role. When its expression is suppressed, its function, which might otherwise inhibit tumorigenesis and development, cannot be performed properly, thus making it easier for tumor cells to grow and proliferate.

As a regulator of cyclic nucleotide signaling, PDE1B (Phosphodiesterase 1B) has been extensively studied in psychiatric and neurological disorders, PDE1B enzyme has emerged as an ideal drug target for the treatment of psychological and neurological disorders, especially schizophrenia [50]. Recent studies have demonstrated that PDE1B expression correlates with immune cell infiltration in colorectal cancer (CRC), underscoring its role in modulating the tumor immune microenvironment [51, 52]. Notably, our current study identified a most significant positive correlation between PDE1B and regulatory T cells, a novel finding that aligns with the existing literature. These convergent results collectively suggest that PDE1B may promote immune evasion in CRC through regulatory T-cell modulation. Initially identified as a tumor suppressor, LZTS1 (Leucine Zipper Tumor Suppressor 1) has been shown to promote epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) via activation of the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway, leading to enhanced tumor aggressiveness [53]. Hypermethylation of the LZTS1 promoter region is associated with colorectal cancer liver metastasis, consistent with our findings in CRC [54]. PCSK5 (Proprotein Convertase Subtilisin/Kexin Type 5) is a proteolytic enzyme that plays a significant role in the post-translational modification of precursor proteins. PCSK5 has been implicated in cell migration and transformation [55]. Studies suggest that PCSK5 deletion enhances tumor suppressive effects in triple-negative breast cancer [56]. Our study is the first to report its association with CRC prognosis, warranting further investigation into its mechanistic role in tumor progression.

The prognostic model developed in this study demonstrated high predictive accuracy, with an area under the curve (AUC) exceeding 0.8 in both training and validation cohorts. Similar transcriptomics-based models have been proposed for CRC prognosis, reinforcing the robustness of gene-expression-based predictive tools [57]. Additionally, our nomogram, constructed using the identified gene signature, significantly improved individualized survival prediction, outperforming traditional clinicopathological parameters [58]. These findings underscore the potential clinical application of BARG-based prognostic models for CRC risk stratification and treatment decision-making.

The tumor immune microenvironment (TME) is pivotal in the advancement of CRC, impacting both immune surveillance of the tumor and its resistance to therapy. Among immune cell subtypes, regulatory T cells (Tregs) are known for their immunosuppressive effects, facilitating tumor immune evasion [59]. Our investigation found a notable positive association between PDE1B expression and the infiltration of Tregs, indicating its potential involvement in immune suppression. To further elucidate this, we focused on T-cell subsets, particularly FOXP3 + T cells, which exhibit dual prognostic implications in CRC. High numbers of FOXP3 + T cells, including non-Treg populations, are associated with improved prognosis, especially in TMEs with elevated inflammatory cytokines such as TGF-β and IL-12, where FOXP3 + non-Treg accumulation correlates with favorable outcomes. Conversely, increased effector Treg (eTreg) cells are linked to poorer prognosis due to their immunosuppressive effects [60]. These findings align with previous reports indicating that Tregs contribute to CRC progression by dampening anti-tumor immunity [61].

Our genomic analysis indicated that mutations in APC, TP53, TTN, and KRAS were the most commonly altered genes observed in both high-risk and low-risk colorectal cancer cohorts, aligning with findings from prior large-scale studies on CRC [62]. These genes are well-established drivers of CRC, playing key roles in tumor initiation, progression, and therapy resistance [63]. Among these, APC holds a pivotal role in CRC pathogenesis, as germline mutations in the APC gene are the hallmark of familial adenomatous polyposis (FAP), a condition that exposes individuals to hundreds of colorectal adenomas and nearly 100% lifetime risk of CRC [64]. Somatic APC mutations, occurring in over 80% of sporadic CRC cases, disrupt the Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway, promoting uncontrolled cell proliferation and adenoma formation [62]. Microsatellite instability (MSI) is a key molecular feature of CRC, arising from deficient mismatch repair (dMMR). The MSI-high (MSI-H) subtype, characterized by abundant tumor neoantigens and increased lymphocyte infiltration, is associated not only with favorable prognosis but also with enhanced responsiveness to immune checkpoint inhibitors, making it a critical predictor for both prognostic stratification and therapeutic decision-making in CRC [65]. In this study, the MSI scores of the low-risk group were higher than those of the high-risk group, suggesting that elevated MSI levels in the LRG may contribute to their relatively better clinical outcomes. Furthermore, pathway enrichment analysis identified several signaling cascades, including cell adhesion molecules (CAMs), focal adhesion, and cytokine-cytokine receptor interactions, as significantly associated with CRC progression. CAMs are mediators of tumor metastasis and promote invasion and spread of CRC cells. CAMs facilitate metastasis by enabling malignant cell detachment via downregulation of the cadherin-catenin complex and subsequent adhesion to distant tissues [66]. Interestingly, dysregulated cadherin-catenin interactions, notably elevated E-cadherin and altered β-catenin expression, correlate with poor survival and tumor invasiveness in CRC [67, 68], suggesting CAMs as potential therapeutic targets. Our study reinforces the importance of CAM-related pathways in CRC pathogenesis, highlighting their potential as therapeutic targets.

Drug sensitivity analysis further identified several chemotherapeutic agents, AZD1332, BMS.754,807, Dasatinib, WZ4003, and XAV939, as more effective in high-risk CRC patients, suggesting potential therapeutic applications. Notably, Dasatinib, a Src kinase inhibitor, has been investigated in CRC clinical trials [69], Combination therapy with dasatinib and oxaliplatin has been shown to markedly reduce microvascular activity while inducing elevated oxidative stress in colorectal cancer [70]. Furthermore, treatment of colon tumor cells with the insulin-like growth factor-1 receptor (IGF-1R) inhibitor BMS-754,807 has been reported to promote cell survival by activating p70S6K1 via the MEK1/2 pathway, thereby presenting a novel therapeutic approach for CRC [71]. WZ4003, a NUAK inhibitor, was further demonstrated to effectively suppress the growth and survival of colorectal tumors [72]. In addition, XAV939 exerts anti-CRC effects by inhibiting lactate dehydrogenase A in three-dimensional cultures [73]. Collectively, these findings provide a potential rationale for the therapeutic strategies proposed in the present study. This study identified four novel brown adipocyte-related prognostic genes (ADD2, PDE1B, LZTS1, and PCSK5) in CRC and developed a robust predictive model for patient prognosis. Both the prognostic model and nomogram exhibited strong predictive accuracy, supporting their clinical applicability in CRC risk stratification and treatment optimization. Additionally, our findings provide insights into tumor immune infiltration, genomic alterations, and drug sensitivity, contributing to a comprehensive understanding of CRC progression.

We prioritized PCSK5 for subsequent functional validation based on multiple lines of evidence. Analysis using the UALCAN database revealed that elevated PCSK5 expression was significantly associated with poorer overall survival in CRC patients, while GEPIA data demonstrated a marked downregulation of PCSK5 in tumor tissues compared with normal counterparts. This paradoxical pattern—low expression in tumors yet adverse prognosis in high-expression cases—suggests a complex role in CRC pathobiology. LZTS1 has been reported as a tumor suppressor in CRC [53], exerting its effects by inhibiting the AKT–mTOR signaling pathway [74], the functional implications of PCSK5 in colorectal carcinogenesis remain largely undefined. Given its prognostic relevance, differential expression profile, and the paucity of mechanistic studies, PCSK5 represents a compelling and unexplored candidate for in-depth investigation. Our results demonstrate that knockdown of PCSK5 significantly suppresses the proliferation, migration, and invasion capacities of HCT116 and SW480 cells.

However, this study has certain limitations. First, only functional validation of candidate genes was performed, and their underlying mechanisms in CRC progression remain to be elucidated. Second, the prognostic model requires validation in independent cohorts and prospective trials. Third, the imbalance between tumor and normal samples in TCGA-CRC may limit DEG detection, which could be addressed by integrating additional normal tissue data from sources such as GTEx. Despite these limitations, this study offers valuable insights into CRC pathobiology and lays the foundation for future precision oncology approaches.

Conclusions

In this study, we identified four brown adipocyte-related genes (BARGs)—ADD2, PDE1B, LZTS1, and PCSK5—as novel prognostic biomarkers in colorectal cancer (CRC) through an integrative approach combining transcriptomic analysis, Mendelian randomization, and machine learning. The constructed prognostic model and nomogram demonstrated robust predictive accuracy (AUC > 0.8), offering a valuable tool for risk stratification and personalized treatment in CRC patients. Our findings reveal significant associations between these genes and immune infiltration, particularly PDE1B with Tregs, alongside distinct genomic mutation profiles and drug sensitivity patterns between high- and low-risk groups. While these results provide fresh insights into CRC pathogenesis and potential therapeutic targets, limitations include the need for experimental validation and prospective clinical studies. Nonetheless, this work advances our understanding of BARGs in CRC and lays a foundation for future precision oncology strategies.

Exploring the Association Between Rheumatoid Arthritis and Parkinson’s Disease: Evidence from 2001–2016 NHANES, Real-World Clinical Data and Bioinformatics Analysis
Authors: Yan Cheng, Yunzhou Yang, Yong Liu, Hongjiang Zhai, Bo Fang, Mingxiang Song
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundParkinson’s disease (PD) and rheumatoid arthritis (RA) are two common chronic diseases, but the potential links between them and the common molecular mechanisms remain largely unknown. The purpose of this study was to explore the potential association and common genetic signatures between RA and PD.MethodsBased on the data from NHANES 2001–2016 (n=27,215) and real-world clinical setting (n=209), logistic regression analysis was used to evaluate the association between PD and RA. Subsequently, the potential molecular mechanisms linking between PD and RA were explored based on transcriptomic data.ResultsBased on data from NHANES and real-world clinical settings, this study confirmed that RA was significantly associated with an increased risk of PD. Based on the transcriptome data, 79 common DEGs and three key genes (GNB2L1, HSPA9, RPA1) were identified. Functional enrichment analysis revealed that common DEGs were significantly enriched in multiple immune-related biological pathways, activation of innate immune response, T cell receptor signaling pathway, and PI3K-Akt signaling pathway. Based on GSVA and GSEA analysis, this study also identified three signaling pathways (MTORC1_SIGNALING, OXIDATIVE_PHOSPHORYLATION and MYC_TARGETS_V1) that may play a key role in the occurrence and development of PD and RA, and are regulated by GNB2L1, HSPA9 and RPA1. Regulatory network analysis identified key miRNAs (for example, hsa-miR-143-3p, hsa-miR-128-3p) and TFs (for example, MYB) that may coregulate these key genes. The nomogram model constructed based on these genes showed favorable predictive performance for both disease.ConclusionThe results of this study showed a significant association between PD and RA. Furthermore, it was revealed that GNB2L1, HSPA9, and RPA1 may have complex regulatory mechanisms in PD and RA, providing new insights into the common molecular basis and therapeutic targets of these two different diseases. Notably, these associative findings from epidemiological and transcriptomic analyses require further experimental validation.Keywords: Parkinson’s disease, rheumatoid arthritis, NHANES, key genes, molecular mechanism

Introduction

Parkinson’s disease (PD) is a common neurodegenerative disorder, and its prevalence is expected to double in the next 30 years.1 The main clinical features of PD are motor dysfunction, including bradykinesia, muscular rigidity, tremor and postural instability, often accompanied by a variety of non-motor symptoms.2 Neurodegenerative diseases are mainly induced by oxidative stress, neuroinflammation, dysregulation of protein homeostasis in the brain, aberrant apoptosis and impaired autophagy. Although extensive studies have been conducted on PD, its specific molecular pathogenesis remains incompletely elucidated.3 Current available therapeutic approaches mainly focus on symptomatic relief rather than effectively prevent the progression of the disease,4 which highlights the urgent need for continuous and in-depth research into its underlying mechanisms. Furthermore, the diagnosis of PD remains challenging.1 Although significant progress has been made in the development of diagnostic biomarkers, with genetic and imaging tests having become part of routine clinical screening protocols, novel biomarkers still require further investigation and identification to achieve early detection of PD, thereby contributing to the development of new disease treatment methods.

Rheumatoid arthritis (RA) is a chronic systemic autoimmune disease characterized by synovial tissue hyperplasia, pannus formation, cartilage destruction, and systemic complications.5 Research has showed that the genetic risk landscape of autoimmune diseases and PD overlap to some extent.6 The persistent systemic inflammation in RA may disrupt blood-brain barrier integrity and allow circulating pro-inflammatory cytokines (such as TNF-α and IL-6) to infiltrate the central nervous system,7–9 further aggravating neuroinflammation and oxidative stress involved in PD progression. In recent years, accumulating studies have suggested that PD and RA are not isolated diseases and there exists a potential association between them.7,10,11 However, the exact association between RA and PD remains unclear, the relevant findings are still controversial, and the causal relationship between the two conditions warrants further investigation. Given that both RA and PD are highly prevalent worldwide, any correlation between them would carry important implications for public health practice and may provide novel targets and insights for the intervention and management of PD. Therefore, continuous exploration of the potential common molecular mechanisms of PD and RA will help to further improve the understanding of these two diseases and provide a theoretical basis for subsequent clinical treatment and strategy development.

The National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES, https://wwwn.cdc.gov/nchs/nhanes/Default.aspx) is a large-scale cross-sectional survey program designed to evaluate the health and nutrition status of the USA population, covering a wide range of participants and rich health-related indicators.12 This database has been widely used in studies on a variety of diseases, including PD and RA.13,14 The analysis based on NHANES data can help to elucidate the potential association between PD and RA. The Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO, https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/) is a public repository for gene expression data, storing a large volume of gene expression profiles from various diseases and experimental conditions,15 providing a valuable resource for exploring the molecular mechanisms of diseases.

Combining NHANES population-level analysis with overlapping transcriptomic analysis based on GEO datasets can integrate epidemiological and molecular evidence to more systematically and comprehensively elucidate the potential association and shared pathological mechanisms between PD and RA. So far, no research system has been found that combines these two types of databases to explore the association and potential molecular mechanisms between PD and RA. Therefore, performing a comprehensive analysis based on the NHANES and GEO datasets is of great scientific significance for revealing the association and molecular mechanisms between PD and RA, as well as providing novel insights into the development of early diagnostic methods and targeted therapeutic strategies for these two diseases.

Materials and Methods

Study Population in NHANES

Data used in this study were derived from the NHANES 2001–2016 dataset, with a total of 82,097 participants involved in the survey during this period. The study excluded participants with missing data on PD and RA, as well as participants with missing information on gender, age, education level, marital status, body mass index (BMI), poverty income ratio (PIR), diabetes, hypertension, coronary heart disease, heart failure, heart attack, stroke, angina pectoris, cancer history, smoking and drinking. A total of 27,215 participants were finally enrolled in this study (Figure 1).

Flow chart of NHANES study participants.

Definition of PD and RA in NHANES

The identification of PD patients in this study was based on the records where the “secondary category name” in the prescription drug document was marked as “anti-Parkinson’s disease drugs”. The classification was determined based on the patient’s response to questions related to the use of prescription drugs. The PD definition used in this study is consistent with previous studies.16,17 The diagnosis of arthritis was obtained through a self-administered questionnaire (MCQ160a). Participants were asked whether they had ever been told by a doctor or other health professional that they had arthritis, with response options including “Yes” and “No” (specific English reference link: https://wwwn.cdc.gov/Nchs/Data/Nhanes/Public/2015/DataFiles/MCQ_I.htm#MCQ160a). The assessment of RA was conducted through a follow-up question, where participants were required to answer “What type of arthritis do you have”, with response options including RA, osteoarthritis, psoriatic arthritis, other types, refused, and do not know.

Evaluation of Covariates

Covariates included gender, age, race, PIR, educational level (categorized as below high school, high school, and above high school), marital status (married or unmarried with a partner), BMI, diabetes, hypertension, coronary heart disease, heart failure, heart attack, stroke, angina pectoris, and cancer history (participants who answered “Yes” in the questionnaire were identified as having these diseases), smoking status (defined by the questions “Have you smoked at least 100 cigarettes in your lifetime?” in questionnaire SMQ020 and “Do you currently smoke?” in SMQ040), and alcohol consumption status (defined by the question “Have you consumed at least 12 drinks in the past year?” in the questionnaire).

Clinical Data Validation

To further verify the association between RA and PD, a validation cohort was included, consisting of 209 adults from the real-world clinical settings (the population receiving examination and treatment in Lu’an Hospital of Anhui Medical University). Following approval by the institutional ethics committee, clinical data of population who received examination and medical treatment in our hospital from January 2020 to January 2026 were retrospectively collected. Given the retrospective nature of the study, the Ethics Committee waived the requirement for written informed consent from the participants. In the validation study, all available covariates were collected, including BMI, gender, age, educational level, marital status, smoking status, alcohol drinking status, hypertension, diabetes, heart failure, coronary heart disease, angina pectoris, heart attack, stroke and cancer. The studies involving human participants were reviewed and approved by the Ethics Committee of Lu’an Hospital of Anhui Medical University (Approval number: 2026LLKS-KY-012). Some covariates from the NHANES analysis (race and PIR) were not available in the clinical dataset due to routine data collection practices.

Statistical Analysis of NHANES

Continuous variables were presented as mean±standard deviation (SD), and categorical variables as counts (percentages). The chi-square test or T-test was used to assess intergroup differences in among variables. Logistic regression analysis was performed to evaluate the association between RA and the prevalence of PD. Multivariable analyses were conducted with three models constructed: Model 1 was unadjusted; Model 2 was adjusted for BMI, gender, age, race, educational level, marital status and PIR; Model 3 was additionally adjusted for smoking status, alcohol drinking status, hypertension, diabetes, heart failure, coronary heart disease, angina pectoris, heart attack, stroke and cancer.

Data Download and Processing of PD and RA in GEO

Databases were searched with the keywords of “Parkinson’s Disease”, “Rheumatoid arthritis” and “homo sapiens”, and a total of three PD datasets (GSE22491, GSE100054, GSE18838) and two RA datasets (GSE17755, GSE93272) were finally included. PD datasets GSE22491 and GSE100054 were merged as the training set, and batch effect correction was performed on the two datasets using the R package “sva” to eliminate systematic technical differences in independent studies and ensure the reliability of downstream analyses. Before the merger, logarithmic transformation was performed on the GSE22491 dataset. The PD dataset GSE18838 was designated as the validation set. For RA datasets, GSE17755 was used as the training set and GSE93272 as the validation set. For the expression matrix of each dataset, platform annotation files were used to map probes to gene symbols. If multiple probes corresponded to the same gene, the maximum expression value was retained as the final expression level of the gene.

Identification and Functional Annotation of Differentially Expressed Genes (DEGs)

The R package “metaMA” was used to identify DEGs in PD, with the screening criteria set as adjusted P <0.05 and |Combined.ES| ≥0.5; genes with inconsistent expression trends in the two datasets were excluded. This dual-filter method is widely used in transcriptomic analysis, and the 0.5 cutoff corresponds to a moderate effect size.18 The R package “limma” was employed to identify DEGs in RA, and the screening criteria were defined as adjusted P <0.05 and |log2Fold Change (FC)| ≥0.5 in accordance with previous studies.19,20 Subsequently, intersection analysis was performed on the DEGs of PD and RA to screen out common DEGs with consistent expression trends in both diseases. Based on the R package “clusterProfiler”, Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) functional annotation analyses were conducted on the common DEGs (P <0.05).

Construction of Protein-Protein Interaction (PPI) Network and Identification of Key Genes

To further investigate the interaction relationships among the common DEGs of PD and RA, a PPI network of these common DEGs was constructed based on the STRING database (https://string-db.org/) with a moderate confidence threshold (interaction score ≥0.4) to ensure the biological reliability of the interaction relationships. The analysis results from the STRING database were then imported into Cytoscape software for visualization analysis of the network data. Subsequently, genes with consistent expression trends in both the training and validation sets of PD and RA were screened and defined as key genes. The pROC package in R was used to perform receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis to explore the potential diagnostic value of key genes in PD and RA. In addition, Pearson correlation analysis was conducted to investigate the correlations among the key genes.

Gene Set Variation Analysis (GSVA) and Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA)

The gene set file “h.all.v2025.1.Hs.symbols.gmt” was downloaded from the MSigDB database (https://www.gsea-msigdb.org/gsea/msigdb/). The R package “GSVA” was used to calculate a comprehensive score for each gene set, thereby evaluating potential biological functional changes. To further clarify the potential association between key genes and functional pathways, Pearson correlation analysis was performed to explore the correlation between key genes and pathways. In addition, GSEA was performed using the R package “clusterProfiler”. Patients were divided into high and low expression groups based on the median expression level of key genes. The aforementioned Hallmark gene sets were used as the reference database to assess the enrichment of gene sets in the two groups. P <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Construction of miRNA-mRNA and Transcription Factor (TF)-mRNA Regulatory Networks

To further explore the potential molecular mechanisms of key genes, this study predicted the potential regulatory network between key genes (mRNA) and miRNAs via the ENCORI database (https://rnasysu.com/encori/), and visualized the network using Cytoscape software. In addition, the R package “RcisTarget” was used to predict TFs, and the t-test was applied to analyze the statistical differences in the expression of TFs between the control and disease groups.

Construction and Validation of the Nomogram

Based on key genes, a nomogram was constructed using the R package “rms” to predict the risk of developing PD and RA. Calibration curves were used to assess the consistency between the predicted probabilities of the nomogram and the actual outcomes. ROC curves were used to evaluate the predictive accuracy of the nomogram. Decision curve analysis (DCA) was used to assess the clinical utility of the nomogram.

Identification and Functional Annotation of Common DEGs in RA and PD

R package “sva” was used to perform batch effects on PD datasets GSE22491 and GSE100054 (Figure S1), and then R package “metaMA” was used for differential expression analysis. Based on the screening criteria adjusted P <0.05 and |Combined.ES| ≥0.5, and excluding the genes with inconsistent expression trends in the two datasets, a total of 2645 DEGs were obtained (Figure 2A). In addition, the R package “limma” was used to analyze the RA dataset GSE17755, and a total of 878 DEGs were identified according to the screening criteria of adjusted P <0.05 and |log2FC| ≥0.5 (Figure 2B). Subsequently, intersection analysis was performed on the DEGs of PD and RA, and a total of 79 DEGs with consistent expression trends in the two diseases were obtained (1 up-regulated and 78 down-regulated) (Figure 2C). The results of GO-BP term showed that the common DEGs of RA and PD were significantly enriched in DNA damage repair, protein homeostasis regulation, immunity and other biological pathways, such as base-excision repair, protein refolding, and activation of innate immune response (Figure 2D). The results of GO-CC showed that the common DEGs of RA and PD were mainly enriched in focal adhesion, cell-substrate junction, and site of DNA damage (Figure 2E). The results of GO-MMF showed that the common DEGs of RA and PD were mainly enriched in ATP-dependent protein folding chaperone, protein folding chaperone, and unfolded protein binding (Figure 2F). Moreover, KEGG results also showed that common DEGs of RA and PD were enriched in a variety of immune-related pathways, such as antigen processing and presentation, T cell receptor signaling pathway, and PI3K-Akt signaling pathway (Figure 2G).

Identification and Characteristic Analysis of Key Genes

To further investigate the interaction relationships among the common DEGs of PD and RA, a PPI network of these common DEGs was constructed (Figure 3A). Subsequently, based on the genes in the PPI network, genes with consistent expression trends in both the training sets and validation sets of PD and RA were screened and defined as key genes. The results showed that only three genes, namely GNB2L1, HSPA9, and RPA1, exhibited consistent expression trends in the training sets and validation sets of PD and RA. Moreover, GNB2L1, HSPA9 and RPA1 were all down-regulated in the training (Figure 3B and C) and validation (Figure S2) sets of PD and RA. ROC analysis showed that the area under the curve (AUC) values of GNB2L1, HSPA9 and RPA1 were all greater than 0.7, suggesting that they all have favorable diagnostic accuracy for RA and PD (Figure 3D–I). The results of chromosomal localization analysis showed that GNB2L1 and HSPA9 are both localized on chromosome 5, while RPA1 is localized on chromosome 17 (Figure 3J). Correlation analysis further confirmed that there are significant positive correlations among GNB2L1, HSPA9 and RPA1 in both PD and RA (Figure 3K and L).

GSVA and GSEA

The results of GSVA revealed that multiple pathways, including EPITHELIAL_MESENCHYMAL_TRANSITION, KRAS_SIGNALING_DN, ANGIOGENESIS and CHOLESTEROL_HOMEOSTASIS, were significantly activated in both PD and RA (Figure 4A and B). Meanwhile, several signaling pathways were significantly inhibited in both diseases, such as PI3K_AKT_MTOR_SIGNALING, MTORC1_SIGNALING, OXIDATIVE_PHOSPHORYLATION and MYC_TARGETS_V1 (Figure 4A and B). Pearson correlation analysis indicated that the key genes GNB2L1, HSPA9 and RPA1 were significantly correlated with most of the significantly activated or inhibited pathways (Figure 4C and D). Subsequently, we further investigated the regulatory effects of these three key genes on the related signaling pathways. Patients were divided into high and low expression groups based on the median expression levels of the key genes, and GSEA results showed significant differences in signaling pathways between the high and low expression groups of GNB2L1, HSPA9 and RPA1 (Figure 5A–F). Notably, the MTORC1_SIGNALING, OXIDATIVE_PHOSPHORYLATION and MYC_TARGETS_V1 pathways were activated in the high expression groups of GNB2L1, HSPA9 and RPA1, and GSVA results showed their inhibition in both PD and RA. This suggested that these three pathways might play crucial roles in the pathogenesis and progression of PD and RA, and were regulated by GNB2L1, HSPA9 and RPA1.

miRNA-mRNA and TF-mRNA Regulatory Networks

To further explore the potential molecular mechanisms of the key genes, this study predicted the miRNAs that might target these key genes based on the ENCORI database and constructed an mRNA-miRNA regulatory network. This mRNA-miRNA regulatory network included three key genes, 126 miRNAs, and 129 mRNA-miRNA regulatory pairs (Figure 6). For example, hsa-miR-143-3p-HSPA9/RPA1, hsa-miR-128-3p-HSPA9/RPA1, hsa-miR-362-5p-GNB2L1, and hsa-miR-193b-3p-GNB2L1 may play an important regulatory role in PD and RA. TFs associated with key genes were predicted using the R package “RcisTarget”, and a TF-mRNA regulatory network was constructed (Figure 7). Meanwhile, differential expression analysis of TFs was performed based on the t-test, and the results showed that a total of 26 TFs (NR4A2, NR3C1, MYB, KLF3, IKZF5, HNF4A, HLTF, HES7, FOXC2, FLI1, ERG, CREB3L1, CEBPE, ARNTL2, ARNTL, ZNF654, ZNF507, ZEB1, ZBTB40, YY1, VPS72, THRB, TGIF1, SOX8, RXRB, NRF1) were differentially expressed in both PD and RA. Among these, the MYB was regulatory associated with all three hub genes.

Nomogram Constructed Based on Key Genes

Nomogram risk prediction models for PD and RA were constructed respectively based on the three key genes GNB2L1, HSPA9 and RPA1 (Figure 8A and B). Quantitative assessment of disease risk was achieved by the constructed models through the integration of expression profile data of these three key genes. Calibration curves verification results showed that nomogram models of PD and RA exhibited favorable predictive performance (Figure 8C and D). In addition, the ROC analysis results showed that the AUC value of the PD nomogram model was 0.914 (Figure 8E), and the AUC value of the RA nomogram model was 0.970 (Figure 8F), reflecting excellent predictive efficacy. Further DCA results showed that the nomogram model can bring significant clinical net benefits in a wide range of threshold probabilities (Figure 8G and H), and has potential clinical application value. These results collectively indicate that the nomogram model constructed based on the key genes can provide a reliable tool for the risk assessment of PD and RA.

Discussion

A cohort study based on the Taiwanese population has shown that RA is significantly associated with an increased risk of PD.21 Moreover, a population based cohort study in Korea showed that patients with RA had a 1.74 fold higher risk of developing PD compared with the control group.7 Based on data from NHANES and real-world clinical settings, this study confirmed a significant association between RA and PD. After adjusting for confounding factors, RA was still significantly associated with the risk of PD, suggesting that RA may be related to an increased risk of PD. Subsequently, to further explore the potential common molecular mechanisms between PD and RA, further analyses were performed based on the GEO database.

A total of 79 DEGs with consistent expression trends were identified in PD and RA. Functional enrichment analysis revealed that these genes were significantly enriched in multiple immune-related biological pathways, activation of innate immune response, T cell receptor signaling pathway, and PI3K-Akt signaling pathway. Alpha-synuclein is a key pathological component of PD and is involved in the activation of innate and adaptive immune systems.22 The innate immune system plays a key role in the occurrence and progression of RA pathogenesis. A variety of innate immune cells, including monocytes, macrophages and dendritic cells, are involved in the inflammatory response of patients with RA and drive the activation of the adaptive immune system.23 The dysregulation of T cell receptor signaling pathway is closely related to PD and RA.24,25 The PI3K‑Akt signaling pathway may be involved in regulating neuroinflammation in PD and mediates neuronal survival.26,27 The PI3K‑Akt signaling pathway may participate in the remodeling of the immune microenvironment and exert an important regulatory role in the treatment of RA.28 Collectively, these findings suggest a close immune-related link between PD and RA. In fact, extensive overlaps in inflammatory and cellular stress pathways between chronic inflammatory diseases and neurodegenerative diseases have also been reported. However, the specific molecular mechanisms underlying this comorbidity remain largely unclear. A review by Praveen et al emphasized that the typical pathogenic protein of PD, alpha-synuclein, has multiple regulatory functions outside the central nervous system, including regulating white blood cell development, peripheral immune response and systemic inflammatory homeostasis.29 Moreover, as a drug for treating autoimmune diseases such as RA, the Total Glucosides of White Paeony Capsule can improve the behavioral abnormalities of PD mice through the LRRK2/α-synuclein signaling pathway.30 These findings together provide a mechanistic framework for our investigation into RA-PD comorbidity.

Further analysis showed that there were three common key genes (GNB2L1, HSPA9, and RPA1) in PD and RA. HSPA9 down-regulation leads to a reduction in tyrosine hydroxylase-positive neurons within the mouse substantia nigra and induces PD-like motor impairments.31 In RA, mitochondrial apoptosis-related gene HSPA9 is associated with energy and ROS metabolism and may be a potential diagnostic marker.32 RPA1 can significantly predict the occurrence of PD.33 RPA is a target for autoantibodies in human autoimmune diseases.34 To date, no relevant studies on GNB2L1 in PD and RA have been reported. The results of this study showed that GNB2L1, HSPA9 and RPA1 were down-regulated in both PD and RA, with favorable potential for clinical diagnosis. Correlation analysis confirmed that there are significant positive correlations among GNB2L1, HSPA9 and RPA1 in both PD and RA, suggesting that they may jointly participate in regulating the occurrence and progression of PD and RA through synergistic interaction. Based on GSVA and GSEA analysis, this study also identified three signaling pathways (MTORC1_SIGNALING, OXIDATIVE_PHOSPHORYLATION and MYC_TARGETS_V1) that may play a key role in the occurrence and development of PD and RA, and are regulated by GNB2L1, HSPA9 and RPA1. These findings indicate that these genes may play important roles in the two diseases, and the specific molecular mechanisms remain to be further investigated.

The miRNA-mRNA regulatory network constructed in this study contained a total of 129 mRNA-miRNA regulatory pairs, including hsa-miR-143-3p-HSPA9/RPA1, hsa-miR-128-3p-HSPA9/RPA1, hsa-miR-362-5p-GNB2L1, and hsa-miR-193b-3p-GNB2L1. Hsa-miR-143-3p is involved in regulating mitochondrial dysfunction in PD,35 and also modulates the viability, proliferation, and apoptosis of RA synovial cells.36,37 Hsa-miR-128-3p may serve as a key regulator in the modulation of autophagy and the repair of neuronal injury in PD.38 Hsa-miR-128-3p may be involved in regulating ferroptosis in fibroblast-like synoviocytes of RA and participate in the progression of RA by targeting TNFAIP3.39,40 The hsa-miR-362-5p gene is abnormally expressed in RA.41 Hsa-miR-193b-3p is aberrantly expressed in RA,42 and may be involved in regulating the insulin-dependent anti-inflammatory response in PD.43 Furthermore, TF-mRNA regulatory network analysis revealed that the TF MYB exhibited potential regulatory relationships with all three key genes. Abnormal expression of MYB is associated with a variety of diseases, including neurodegenerative diseases and autoimmune diseases.44,45 The regulatory association between MYB and the three key genes suggests that MYB may regulate the expression of GNB2L1, HSPA9, and RPA1 to mediate the common pathological mechanisms of PD and RA. Collectively, the miRNA-mRNA and TF-mRNA regulatory networks constructed in this study reveal the complex regulatory mechanisms of the three key genes in PD and RA, and provide new insights into the common molecular basis and therapeutic targets of these two different diseases.

Nomograms represent a practical graphical approach for depicting predictive mathematical models by incorporating multiple relevant factors to estimate specific endpoints.46 It can be intuitively used to predict an individual disease risk.47 In this study, nomogram risk prediction models were separately constructed for PD and RA based on the three key genes GNB2L1, HSPA9, and RPA1. Calibration curves verification results showed that nomogram models of PD and RA exhibited favorable predictive performance. Furthermore, ROC curve analysis results also showed that the nomogram models for both PD and RA exhibited excellent predictive efficacy. Further DCA results showed that the nomogram models provided significant clinical net benefit within a wide range of threshold probabilities, supporting their promising value in clinical application. Collectively, these findings indicate that the nomogram model based on key genes can serve as a reliable and non-invasive tool for risk assessment and early screening of PD and RA, but further clinical validation is required.

However, this study also has certain limitations. Firstly, the epidemiological data from NHANES relied on self-reported diagnoses and prescription records, which may lead to potential recall or classification bias. Additionally, the relatively small number of PD cases (N=161) in this cohort may reduce the statistical robustness of association analysis and increase the risk of unstable effect estimates. Secondly, the sample size of the clinical validation cohort (105 PD patients) is relatively small, which may limit the generalizability of the validation results. Further multicenter studies with larger sample sizes are warranted to confirm our findings. Thirdly, the key genes and molecular networks identified based on GEO data are only predictive results from bioinformatic analysis. Their specific molecular mechanisms still need to be verified by extensive in vivo and in vitro experiments in follow-up studies, and no causal relationship can be obtained from the current associative evidence. Fourthly, the generalizability of the nomogram model requires validation in independent cohorts.

Conclusion

In summary, the results of this study showed a significant association between PD and RA. GNB2L1, HSPA9, and RPA1 were aberrantly expressed in both diseases, and may be involved in the development of the disease by regulating multiple signaling pathways. In addition, the miRNA-mRNA and TF-mRNA regulatory networks constructed in this study revealed the complex regulatory mechanisms of the three key genes in PD and RA, providing new insights into the common molecular basis and therapeutic targets of these two different diseases. Furthermore, the three key genes exhibited high diagnostic value, and the nomogram model constructed based on these genes showed favorable predictive performance for both diseases. Notably, these findings are associative conclusions from epidemiological and transcriptomic analyses. Extensive in vivo and in vitro functional experiments are required to verify the molecular mechanisms of the identified key genes, and larger multicenter cohorts should be recruited to further confirm the generalizability of our results and explore potential causal relationships.

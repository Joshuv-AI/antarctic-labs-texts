Asymptotic invariants for fusion algebras associated with compact quantum groups
Jacek Krajczok, Adam Skalski

Abstract
. We introduce and study certain asymptotic invariants associated with fusion algebras (equipped with a dimension function), which arise naturally in the representation theory of compact quantum groups. Our invariants generalise the analogous concepts studied for classical discrete groups. Specifically we introduce uniform Følner constants and the uniform Kazhdan constant for a regular representation of a fusion algebra, and establish a relationship between these, amenability, and the exponential growth rate considered earlier by Banica and Vergnioux. Further we compute the invariants for fusion algebras associated with quantum S ​ U q ​ ( 2 ) SU_{q}(2) and S ​ O q ​ ( 3 ) SO_{q}(3) and determine the uniform exponential growth rate for the fusion algebras of all q q -deformations of semisimple, simply connected, compact Lie groups and for all free unitary quantum groups.

Introduction

Recent years have brought an increased interest in extending classical geometric group theory notions to the case of locally compact – and especially discrete – quantum groups. A notable example is given by
amenability

\textstyle\mathbb{L}

can be described in terms of the properties of the fusion algebra encoding the representation theory of its compact dual
\mathbb{G}
(
[
Ban99
, Theorem 6.2]
). Here one can think about elements of the relevant fusion algebra as equivalence classes of finite-dimensional representations of
\mathbb{G}
, equipped with the tensor product and a natural quantum dimension function. At the same time Hiai and Izumi, inspired by the subfactor theory, undertook in
[
HI98
]
a deep study of amenability for fusion algebras, which itself led to later work by Kyed on Følner sets and
\ell^{2}
-Betti numbers for discrete quantum groups (see
[
Kye08
]
and references therein).

Since then there have been many applications of properties of fusion algebras, and more generally, related rigid tensor categories, to the study of qualitative geometric and analytic aspects of discrete quantum groups, such as amenability or Property (T); note for example
[
PV15
]
,
[
NY16
]
,
[
KRVV17
]
or the book
[
NT13
]
. On the other hand when studying classical discrete groups one is often interested in finer, preferably quantitative asymptotic invariants. An old outstanding example is the
rate of growth
for a given finitely generated group. When the group in question is amenable, one may ask about its
isoperimetric profile
(see
[
Ers03
]
and references therein). On the other hand for non-amenable groups the articles
[

]

[
LPV08
]
,
[
Sha00
]
introduce and study asymptotic invariants which in a sense quantify the ‘degree of non-amenability’.

In this work, inspired by the articles quoted above, we introduce and investigate analogous notions (namely

and

2.
Fusion algebras – definitions and basic facts

Throughout this paper we will consider fusion algebras in the sense of
[
HI98
]
or
[
Kye08
, Definition 2.1]
. Let us recall the definition.

Definition 2.1
.

A
fusion algebra

(R,d)
is given by the following data: an index set
I
(called the set of
irreducible objects
of
R
) with a distinguished element
e
and involution
\alpha\mapsto\bar{\alpha}
, the structure of a unital ring on
\mathbb{Z}[I]
(denoted
R
) and a
dimension function

d\colon R\to\mathbb{R}
such that

(i)

e
is the unit of
R
;

(ii)

for any elements
\xi,\eta\in I
there is a family
(N_{\xi,\eta}^{\alpha})_{\alpha\in I}
of
non-negative
integers (of which only finitely many are non-zero) such that

\xi\eta=\sum_{\alpha\in I}N_{\xi,\eta}^{\alpha}\alpha;

(iii)

the involution extends to a
\mathbb{Z}
-linear antimultiplicative involutive map on
R
(still denoted by the same symbol);

(iv)

Frobenius reciprocity holds, i.e. for all
\xi,\eta,\alpha\in I
we have

N_{\xi,\eta}^{\alpha}=N_{\alpha,\bar{\eta}}^{\xi}=N_{\bar{\xi},\alpha}^{\eta};

(v)

d
is multiplicative,
\mathbb{Z}
-linear and
d(\overline{\alpha})=d(\alpha)\geq 1
for
\alpha\in I
.

Note in particular that we view the dimension function as a part of the data of the fusion algebra; in general the choice of
d
(for a given
R=\mathbb{Z}[I]
– unital involutive ring as above) is highly non-unique. If however there is a natural choice of dimension function clear from the context, or if the property we discuss does not depend on
d
, we will simply write
R
instead of
(R,d)
.

Let
(R,d)
be a fusion algebra. For
r=\sum_{\alpha\in I}r_{\alpha}\alpha\in R
we define the
support
of
r
as
\supp(r)=\{\alpha\in I\,|\,r_{\alpha}\neq 0\}
. For
r\in R,\alpha\in I
we write
\alpha\subseteq r
if
\alpha\in\supp(r)
. If
A\subseteq I
then
A^{c}=I\setminus A
,
\overline{A}=\{\overline{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in A\}
, cardinality of
A
is
\#A\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\cup\{+\infty\}
and the
size
of
A
is given by

|A|=\sum_{\alpha\in A}d(\alpha)^{2}.

We say that
R
is
finitely generated
if there exists a finite subset
X\subseteq I
such that
\overline{X}=X
and for any
\alpha\in I
there exist
x_{1},\dotsc x_{n}\in X
such that
\alpha\subseteq x_{1}\cdots x_{n}
. In this situation we will say that
X

generates

R
. Let stress that we will always assume that generating set
X
is symmetric, i.e.
\overline{X}=X
. Note that this property does not depend on the choice of dimension function
d
.
This is easily seen to be equivalent to the definition given in
[
Kye08
, Section 3]
, calling
R
finitely generated if there exists a finitely supported probability measure
\mu\in\ell^{1}(I)
such that
\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\supp(\mu^{\star n})=I
and
\mu(\overline{\alpha})=\mu(\alpha)
for
\alpha\in I
. Here we use the standard fact that the fusion algebra structure induces a convolution product on probability measures on
I
.

Remark 2.2
.

When
(R_{i},d_{i})
are fusion algebras with the sets of irreducible objects
I_{i}

(i\in\{1,2\})
, we can define their
product fusion algebra

R
. Its unital ring structure is defined by setting
R=R_{1}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}R_{2}
, with the set of irreducible objects
2
2

2

We will write
\alpha\boxtimes\beta\in I_{1}\times I_{2}
instead of
\alpha\otimes\beta
to avoid confusion with the tensor product of representations.

I=I_{1}\times I_{2}
, the unit
e_{1}\boxtimes e_{2}
, the involution
\overline{\alpha\boxtimes\beta}=\overline{\alpha}\boxtimes\overline{\beta}
and the dimension function determined by the formula
d(\alpha\boxtimes\beta)=d_{1}(\alpha)d_{2}(\beta)
for
\alpha\in I_{1},\beta\in I_{2}
. One easily sees that
N^{\alpha\boxtimes\alpha^{\prime}}_{\xi\boxtimes\xi^{\prime},\eta\boxtimes\eta^{\prime}}=N^{\alpha}_{\xi,\eta}N^{\alpha^{\prime}}_{\xi^{\prime},\eta^{\prime}}
for
\alpha,\xi,\eta\in I_{1},\alpha^{\prime},\xi^{\prime},\eta^{\prime}\in I_{2}
and if
R_{1},R_{2}
are finitely generated, then so is
R
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
R
be a finitely generated fusion algebra, and let
X\subseteq I
be a finite generating set. The associated length function is defined via

\ell_{X}\colon I\setminus\{e\}\ni\alpha\mapsto\min\{n\in\mathbb{N}\mid\exists_{x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n}\in X}\colon\alpha\subseteq x_{1}\cdots x_{n}\}\in\mathbb{N},

additionally setting
\ell_{X}(e)=0
. The balls and spheres in
I
of radius
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
(with respect to
X
) are defined respectively via

B_{X}(n)=\{\alpha\in I\mid\ell_{X}(\alpha)\leq n\},\quad S_{X}(n)=\{\alpha\in I\mid\ell_{X}(\alpha)=n\}.

The following lemma is a direct consequence of
[
HI98
, Proposition 1.2 (6)]
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
R
be a fusion algebra, with
I
denoting the set of irreducible objects. Then for any finite set
X\subseteq I
there exists a constant
M_{X}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for any
v\in I
and
x\in X
we have
\#(\textup{supp}\,(vx))\leq M_{X}
.

Note that the dimension function plays no role in the statement above (although it features in the proof in
[
HI98
]
and one can use
d
to get an explicit bound on
M_{X}
). It is easy to see that one could also replace in the formula above the condition
\#(\textup{supp}\,(vx))\leq M_{X}
by its right equivalent
\#(\textup{supp }(xv))\leq M_{X}
(by using the ‘antimultiplicativity’ of the adjoint operation).

We have already mentioned that the fusion algebra structure induces a convolution on measures in
\ell^{1}(I)
. Furthermore to every probability measure
\mu\in\ell^{1}(I)
one can associate a convolution operator
\lambda_{\mu}\in\B(\ell^{2}(I))
. We say that the fusion algebra
(R,d)
is
amenable
if for every probability measure
\mu
as above we have
\|\lambda_{\mu}\|=1
(see
[
HI98
, Theorem 4.6]
,
[
Kye08
, Theorem 3.3]
or
[
NT13
, Section 2.7]
). Amenable fusion algebras admit ‘minimal’ dimension functions, in the sense made precise below.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
(R,d)
be an amenable fusion algebra and suppose that we have another dimension function
d^{\prime}\colon I\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
such that
(R,d^{\prime})
is a fusion algebra. Then for every
i\in I
we have
d(i)\leq d^{\prime}(i)
.

Proof.

In
[
NT13
, Proposition 2.7.7]
this statement is established in the context of fusion algebras arising from
C^{*}
-tensor categories (see the next subsection). An inspection of the proof shows that it remains valid in a potentially greater generality we consider here.

2.1.
Discrete/compact quantum groups, rigid
C^{*}
-tensor categories and related fusion algebras

The key examples of fusion algebras studied in this paper arise from representation theory of compact quantum groups. We will mostly follow the notation and terminology of
[
NT13
]
. So let
\mathbb{G}
be a
compact quantum group
in the sense of Woronowicz, studied via the associated ‘algebras of functions’, namely the Hopf
∗
-algebra
\Pol(\mathbb{G})
and its universal
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-completion
\mathrm{C}^{u}(\mathbb{G})
. By
\Irr(\mathbb{G})
we denote the set of equivalence classes of
irreducible representations
of
\mathbb{G}
, and by
3
3

3

Quantum dimension is also often denoted by
d(\alpha)=\dim_{q}(\alpha)
.

d\colon\Irr(\mathbb{G})\to\left[1,\infty\right)
the
quantum dimension
function of
\mathbb{G}
. Once we choose a representative
U^{\alpha}
of class
\alpha\in\Irr(\mathbb{G})
, we have
d(\alpha)=\Tr(\uprho_{\alpha})
, where
\uprho_{\alpha}
is the unique positive, invertible morphism between
U^{\alpha}
and
(U^{\alpha})^{cc}
which satisfies
\Tr(\uprho_{\alpha})=\Tr(\uprho_{\alpha}^{-1})
(
[
NT13
, Definition 1.4.1]
). We will denote by
(R(\mathbb{G}),d)
the fusion algebra of (equivalence classes of) finite-dimensional representations of
\mathbb{G}
, equipped with the quantum dimension function mentioned above, see
[
NT13
, Definition 2.7.2]
. This means that
R(\mathbb{G})=\mathbb{Z}[\Irr(\mathbb{G})]
as an abelian group, product of
R(\mathbb{G})
is induced by the tensor product of representations and the set of irreducible objects in
R(\mathbb{G})
is identified with
\Irr(\mathbb{G})
. Unless said otherwise, we will always equip
R(\mathbb{G})
with the quantum dimension function. So, if for example we want to equip ring
R(\mathbb{G})
with the classical dimension function
\dim
, we will indicate it by writing
(R(\mathbb{G}),\dim)
. We have
d=\dim
if and only if the quantum group
\mathbb{G}
is of
Kac type
.

It is easy to see that
R(\mathbb{G})
is finitely generated if and only if
\mathbb{G}
is a compact matrix quantum group.

A special class of compact quantum groups is given by duals of classical discrete groups. Indeed, if
\Gamma
is a discrete group, we can treat
\mathbb{Z}\Gamma
as a fusion algebra with basis
\Gamma
and dimension function equal to
1
on the basis elements. In the language developed above we would have
\mathbb{Z}\Gamma=R(\widehat{\Gamma})
, where
\widehat{\Gamma}
is the dual compact quantum group. Yet another special class appears if we consider classical compact groups
G
, for which the quantum dimension function equals the usual dimension of irreducible representations of
G
. The fusion algebra
R(G)
is then always amenable, whereas
R(\widehat{\Gamma})
is amenable if and only if
\Gamma
is amenable (see the last part of this subsection). Further specific examples will be presented in later sections.

Another source of fusion algebras is provided by rigid
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-tensor categories (see
[
NT13
, Chapter 2]
). Let
\mathfrak{C}
be such a category. Recall that this in particular means that there is a way of tensoring objects
U\otimes V\in\mathfrak{C}
, taking direct sums
U\oplus V\in\mathfrak{C}
, and taking the conjugate object
\overline{U}\in\mathfrak{C}

(U,V\in\mathfrak{C})
; we can also speak of subobjects. An object
U\in\mathfrak{C}
is said to be
simple
if
\dim\operatorname{End}(U)=1
, or equivalently
U
does not admit proper subobjects.

With category
\mathfrak{C}
one can associate a fusion ring
R(\mathfrak{C})
, which is the universal ring generated by equivalence classes of simple objects, with the ring structure coming from the direct sum and tensor product in
\mathfrak{C}
(see
[
NT13
, Definition 2.7.2]
). Thus the set of irreducible objects in
R(\mathfrak{C})
is the set of (equivalence classes of) simple objects of
\mathfrak{C}
. Taking the conjugate object
U\mapsto\overline{U}
gives rise to an involution on
R(\mathfrak{C})
. Once we equip
R(\mathfrak{C})
with any dimension function, we obtain a fusion algebra in the sense of Definition
2.1
. While the choice of dimension function is not unique, there is always a canonical choice given by the
intrinsic dimension
of
\mathfrak{C}
(
[
NT13
, Definition 2.2.11]
).

With any compact quantum group
\mathbb{G}
one can associate a rigid
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-tensor category
\Rep(\mathbb{G})
of finite dimensional unitary representations of
\mathbb{G}
. Then
R(\Rep(\mathbb{G}))
is equal to the fusion algebra
R(\mathbb{G})
described above (and the intrinsic dimension of
\Rep(\mathbb{G})
is equal to the quantum dimension
[
NT13
, Example 2.2.13]
).

Not surprisingly, the notion of amenability for the fusion algebra
R(\mathbb{G})
is related to amenability of the dual discrete quantum group
\widehat{\mathbb{G}}
. More precisely,
\widehat{\mathbb{G}}
is amenable if and only if
(R(\mathbb{G}),\dim)
is an amenable fusion algebra (
[
NT13
, Theorem 2.7.10]
).

Amenability of the fusion algebra
R(\mathbb{G})
(equipped with the quantum dimension function) is also related to amenability-like properties of a number of related objects. Specifically, the following conditions are equivalent:

•

R(\mathbb{G})
is amenable;

•

\widehat{\mathbb{G}}
is centrally strongly amenable;

•

\widehat{\mathbb{G}}
is strongly amenable and
\mathbb{G}
is of Kac type;

•

\mathrm{C}^{*}
-tensor category
\Rep(\mathbb{G})
is amenable;

•

Drinfeld double
D(\widehat{\mathbb{G}})
is (strongly) amenable.

(See
[
DKV23
, Theorem 7.8]
,
[
NT13
, Proposition 2.7.7]
and
[
Bra17
, Definition 7.1]
,
[
DKV23
, Definition 4.2]
,
[
NT13
, Definition 2.7.6]
for relevant definitions).

2.2.
q
-numbers

Throughout the paper, we will use the notion of
q
-numbers. For
0<q<1
and
x\in\mathbb{C}
, denote
[x]_{q}=\tfrac{q^{-x}-q^{x}}{q^{-1}-q}
, it will also be convenient to write
[x]_{1}=x
. We will need the following lemma.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
0<q<q^{\prime}<1
. The following inequalities hold:

(i)

\tfrac{[m+1]_{q}}{[m]_{q}}>\tfrac{[m+1]_{q^{\prime}}}{[m]_{q^{\prime}}}
;

(ii)

\tfrac{[m]_{q}}{[2]_{q}}>\tfrac{m}{2}
.

Proof.

For
m\in\mathbb{N}
, set
f_{m}\colon(0,1)\ni q\mapsto q^{-m}-q^{m}\in(0,\infty)
. Then

(2.1)

f_{m+1}^{\prime}(q)-f_{m}^{\prime}(q)=mq^{-1-m}(1-q)(-q^{-1}+q^{2m})-q^{-m-2}-q^{m}<0.

Let
0<q<q^{\prime}<1
. Condition (i) is equivalent to
[m]_{q^{\prime}}[m+1]_{q}>[m]_{q}[m+1]_{q^{\prime}}
, i.e. to
f_{m}(q^{\prime})f_{m+1}(q)>f_{m}(q)f_{m+1}(q^{\prime})
and further to

\bigl(\int_{q}^{q^{\prime}}f_{m}^{\prime}(t)\operatorname{d}\!t+f_{m}(q)\bigr)f_{m+1}(q)>f_{m}(q)\bigl(\int_{q}^{q^{\prime}}f_{m+1}^{\prime}(t)\operatorname{d}\!t+f_{m+1}(q)\bigr).

The latter amounts to the inequality
f_{m+1}(q)\int_{q}^{q^{\prime}}f_{m}^{\prime}(t)\operatorname{d}\!t>f_{m}(q)\int_{q}^{q^{\prime}}f_{m+1}^{\prime}(t)\operatorname{d}\!t
, which holds by (
2.1
) and the easily checked inequality
f_{m}(q)\leq f_{m+1}(q)
. Passing with
q^{\prime}
to
1
gives
\tfrac{[m+1]_{q}}{[m]_{q}}>\tfrac{m+1}{m}
, hence also

\tfrac{[m]_{q}}{[2]_{q}}=\tfrac{[m]_{q}}{[m-1]_{q}}\tfrac{[m-1]_{q}}{[m-2]_{q}}\cdots\tfrac{[3]_{q}}{[2]_{1}}>\tfrac{m}{m-1}\tfrac{m-1}{m-2}\cdots\tfrac{3}{2}=\tfrac{m}{2}.

∎

Definitions and general properties of asymptotic invariants for fusion algebras

Throughout this section let us fix a fusion algebra
(R,d)
, with

3.1.
Uniform Følner constant

The first invariant associated to
(R,d)
we will consider is the
uniform (inner) Følner constant
. As well-known both classically and in the quantum world the notion of Følner sets is closely related to amenability; we will see an instance of this below.

To that end we need to recall the notions of boundary
\partial_{X}(A)
and inner boundary
\partial^{inn}_{X}(A)
for sets
A,X\subseteq I
(see
[
Kye08
]
,
[
KT13
]
).

Definition 3.1
.

[
Kye08
, Definition 3.2]
Let
X,A
be finite subsets of
I
. The
boundary
of
A
with respect to
X
is the set

\partial_{X}(A)=\{\alpha\in A\,|\,\exists_{x\in X}\;\supp(\alpha x)\nsubseteq A\}\cup\{\alpha\in A^{c}\,|\,\exists_{x\in X}\;\supp(\alpha x)\nsubseteq A^{c}\}

and
inner boundary
of A with respect to
X
is

\partial_{X}^{inn}(A)=\{\alpha\in A\,|\,\exists_{x\in X}\;\supp(\alpha x)\nsubseteq A\}.

The non-uniform case of the following definition appears in
[
Kye08
]
. The uniform one is modelled on
[
ABL+05
]
; note however that the latter paper considers only inner Følner constants.

Definition 3.2
.

The
Følner constant of
(R,d)
with respect to the generating set

is defined as

F\o l_{X}(R,d)=\inf_{A}\tfrac{|\partial_{X}A|}{|A|},

where
A
runs over non-empty finite subsets of
I
. The
uniform Følner constant
of
(R,d)
is

F\o l(R,d)=\inf_{X}F\o l_{X}(R,d)

where
X
runs over all finite generating subsets of
R
. We can analogously define the
(uniform) inner Følner constants

F\o l_{X}^{inn}(R,d)
and
F\o l^{inn}(R,d)
, using the inner boundary in the first formula displayed above.

Since
\partial^{inn}_{X}(A)\subseteq\partial_{X}(A)
, we immediately see that

F\o l_{X}^{inn}(R,d)\leq F\o l_{X}(R,d),\quad F\o l^{inn}(R,d)\leq F\o l(R,d).

We will use both of these invariants. Their relationship is not yet fully understood even in the case of discrete groups – see
[
LPV08
, Proposition A.1]
and discussion after that, concerning the connection between the uniform inner Følner constant and the so-called
uniform isoperimetric constant
. However we record here an easy observation, following Lemma
2.4
.

Proposition 3.3
.

For every finite, symmetric, nonempty set
X\subseteq I
there exists
C_{X}>0
such that for every finite set
A\subseteq I
we have
|\partial_{X}(A)|\leq C_{X}|\partial^{inn}_{X}(A)|
. In particular if
X
is generating, then

F\o l_{X}(R,d)\leq C_{X}F\o l^{inn}_{X}(R,d).

Proof.

Take
\alpha\in A^{c}\cap\partial_{X}(A)
. Then there is
x\in X
and
\beta\in A
such that
\beta\subseteq\alpha x
, equivalently
N^{\beta}_{\alpha,x}>0
. But then
N^{\alpha}_{\beta,\overline{x}}>0
, i.e.
\alpha\subseteq\beta\overline{x}
and consequently
d(\alpha)\leq d(\beta)d(\overline{x})
. Furthermore, since
\alpha\in A^{c}
and
\alpha\subseteq\beta\overline{x}
, we have
\beta\in\partial_{X}^{inn}(A)
. Using the number
M_{X}
introduced in Lemma
2.4
, we have

\displaystyle|\partial_{X}(A)|=\sum_{\xi\in\partial_{X}(A)}d(\xi)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\xi\in\partial_{X}^{inn}(A)}d(\xi)^{2}+\sum_{\beta\in\partial_{X}^{inn}(A)}\sum_{x\in X}M_{X}d(\beta)^{2}d(\overline{x})^{2}

\displaystyle=\bigl(1+\sum_{x\in X}M_{X}d(x)^{2}\bigr)\sum_{\xi\in\partial_{X}^{inn}(A)}d(\xi)^{2}=\bigl(1+M_{X}|X|\bigr)|\partial_{X}^{inn}(A)|,

thus it is enough to put
C_{X}=1+M_{X}|X|
.
∎

Kyed in
[
Kye08
, Theorem 3.3]
characterises amenability of
R
via the following Følner-like condition.

Theorem 3.4
.

The following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

(R,d)
is amenable;

(ii)

for every non-empty finite set
X\subseteq I
and
\varepsilon>0
there exists a finite subset
F\subseteq I
such that

(3.1)

|\partial_{X}(F)|<\varepsilon|F|.

It is easy to see (e.g. by considering
X\cup\overline{X}
) that in the second condition it suffices to consider symmetric
X\subseteq I
. Thanks to Proposition
3.3
we can in fact formulate the analogous result using inner boundaries.

Proposition 3.5
.

Suppose that
(R,d)
is a fusion algebra.
The following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

(R,d)
is amenable;

(ii)

for every non-empty finite set
X\subseteq I
and
\varepsilon>0
there exists a finite subset
F\subseteq I
such that

(3.2)

|\partial_{X}^{inn}(F)|<\varepsilon|F|.

Proof.

By Theorem
3.4
it is enough to prove that (ii) implies (i). Choose a non-empty finite
X=\overline{X}\subseteq I
and
\varepsilon>0
. By assumption, we can find finite set
F\subseteq I
such that

|\partial_{X}^{inn}(F)|<\tfrac{\varepsilon}{C_{X}}|F|,

where
C_{X}>0
is the number introduced in Proposition
3.3
. Since

|\partial_{X}(F)|\leq C_{X}|\partial_{X}^{inn}(F)|<\varepsilon|F|,

Theorem
3.4
ends the claim.
∎

If
(R,d)
is finitely generated, we can check the second of the conditions appearing in results above only for finite generating sets. We formulate this fact as the following proposition.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
(R,d)
be a finitely generated fusion algebra. The following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

(R,d)
is amenable;

(ii)

for every finite generating set
X\subseteq I
we have
F\o l_{X}(R,d)=0
;

(iii)

for every finite generating set
X\subseteq I
we have
F\o l^{inn}_{X}(R,d)=0
.

Proof.

By Theorem
3.4
and Proposition
3.5
it suffices to observe that (iii)
\Longrightarrow
(ii)
\Longrightarrow
(i).

Implication (iii)
\Longrightarrow
(ii) follows immediately from Proposition
3.3
.

If (ii) holds, we can follow line by line the proof of the implication (FC3)
\Longrightarrow
(FC1) of
[
Kye08
, Theorem 3.3]
to obtain a ‘non-degenerate version’ of property (FC1) of that theorem, and conclude by
[
HI98
, Theorem 4.6]
.

∎

We then obtain an immediate corollary, connecting amenability of
(R,d)
to vanishing of the uniform Følner constants.

Corollary 3.7
.

If
(R,d)
is an amenable finitely generated fusion algebra then
F\o l(R,d)=F\o l^{inn}(R,d)=0
.

It is not true that vanishing of the uniform Følner constants is equivalent to amenability. Examples can be found already for classical groups:
[
ABL+05
, Proposition 13.3]
shows that certain Baumslag-Solitar groups are non-amenable, but have uniform inner Følner constant equal
0
.

3.2.
Uniform exponential growth rate

In this section we introduce the (uniform) exponential growth rate for finitely generated fusion algebra, closely connected to the notion studied in
[
BV09
]
in the case of Kac type compact quantum groups (Remark
3.10
), and in
[
ABL+05
]
in the case of classical discrete groups (Remark
3.11
). It gives an upper bound on the uniform (inner) Følner constant (Proposition
3.17
) and is often easier to compute than the Følner constants. In particular, in Section
6
we will compute it for the fusion algebras associated with
q
-deformations of compact, semisimple, simply connected Lie groups, and in Section
7
for the fusion algebras associated with free unitary quantum groups.

Recall that if
(R,d)
is a finitely generated fusion algebra with finite generating set
X
, then in Definition
2.3
we have introduced the notion of length
\ell_{X}
and the corresponding notions of spheres and balls. Recall also that generating set
X
is always assumed to be symmetric
\overline{X}=X
. We begin with a standard lemma.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
X\subseteq I
be a finite generating set, and suppose that
I
is infinite. Then the limits
\lim_{n\to\infty}\sqrt[n]{|B_{X}(n)|}
and
\lim_{n\to\infty}\sqrt[n]{|S_{X}(n)|}
exist, and belong to
[1,\infty)
.

Proof.

Fix
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
. Take
\beta\in B_{X}(n),\gamma\in B_{X}(m)
and set
\Delta(\beta,\gamma)=\{\alpha\in B_{X}(n+m)\,|\,\alpha\subseteq\beta\gamma\}
. Clearly
\bigcup_{\beta\in B_{X}(n),\,\gamma\in B_{X}(m)}\Delta(\beta,\gamma)=B_{X}(n+m)
. Next,

\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta(\beta,\gamma)}d(\alpha)\leq d(\beta)d(\gamma)

as
d(\beta\gamma)=d(\beta)d(\gamma)
and each
\alpha\in\Delta(\beta,\gamma)
appears (at least once!) in the basis decomposition of
\beta\gamma
. It follows that

\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta(\beta,\gamma)}d(\alpha)^{2}+\sum_{\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}\in\Delta(\beta,\gamma)\colon\alpha\neq\alpha^{\prime}}\!\!\!\!d(\alpha)d(\alpha^{\prime})=\bigl(\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta(\beta,\gamma)}d(\alpha)\bigr)\bigl(\sum_{\alpha^{\prime}\in\Delta(\beta,\gamma)}d(\alpha^{\prime})\bigr)\leq d(\beta)^{2}d(\gamma)^{2}

and since
d
is a non-negative function on
I
we have

\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta(\beta,\gamma)}d(\alpha)^{2}\leq d(\beta)^{2}d(\gamma)^{2}.

Consequently

\begin{split}\sum_{\alpha\in B_{X}(n+m)}d(\alpha)^{2}\leq\sum_{\beta\in B_{X}(n)}\sum_{\gamma\in B_{X}(m)}\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta(\beta,\gamma)}d(\alpha)^{2}\leq\sum_{\beta\in B_{X}(n)}\sum_{\gamma\in B_{X}(m)}d(\beta)^{2}d(\gamma)^{2}.\end{split}

This shows
|B_{X}(n+m)|\leq|B_{X}(n)|\,|B_{X}(m)|
and a standard argument proves existence of the limit
\lim_{n\to\infty}\sqrt[n]{|B_{X}(n)|}
. An analogous reasoning shows that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\sqrt[n]{|S_{X}(n)|}
also exists.
∎

The limits above exist also when
I
is finite; we then have
\lim_{n\to\infty}\sqrt[n]{|B_{X}(n)|}=1
, but
\lim_{n\to\infty}\sqrt[n]{|S_{X}(n)|}=0
.

We can now introduce the definition of the (uniform) exponential growth rate.

Definition 3.9
.

Let
(R,d)
be a fusion algebra generated by a finite set
X\subseteq I
. The
exponential growth rate
of
R
with respect to
X
is given by

\omega_{X}(R,d)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sqrt[n]{|B_{X}(n)|}

and the
uniform exponential growth rate
by

\omega(R,d)=\inf_{X}\omega_{X}(R,d)

where
X
runs over all finite generating sets for
R
. If
\omega(R,d)>1
then we say that
R
has
uniform exponential growth
.

Remark 3.10
.

Let

\textstyle\mathbb{L}

be a discrete quantum group such that
\widehat{{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\displaystyle\mathbb{L}$}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\textstyle\mathbb{L}$}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{L}$}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{L}$}}}}}
admits a fundamental representation. Let
X\subseteq\Irr(\widehat{{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\displaystyle\mathbb{L}$}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\textstyle\mathbb{L}$}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{L}$}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{L}$}}}}})
be a finite generating set. Our definition of
\omega_{X}(R(\widehat{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\displaystyle\mathbb{L}$}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\textstyle\mathbb{L}$}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{L}$}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{L}$}}}}),d)
, for

\textstyle\mathbb{L}

unimodular (so that
\widehat{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\displaystyle\mathbb{L}$}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\textstyle\mathbb{L}$}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{L}$}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{L}$}}}}
is of Kac type), agrees with the notion of ratio of exponential growth from
[
BV09
, Section 4]
. Indeed, we have (using notation from
[
BV09
]
)
|B_{X}(n)|=b_{n}
. Then the continuity of
\log\colon\mathbb{R}_{\geq 1}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
yields immediately

\log(\omega_{X}(R(\widehat{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\displaystyle\mathbb{L}$}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\textstyle\mathbb{L}$}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{L}$}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{L}$}}}}),d))=\lim_{n\to\infty}\tfrac{\log(|B_{X}(n)|)}{n}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\tfrac{\log(b_{n})}{n}.

In particular, Lemma
3.8
shows that the above limit always exists.

Remark 3.11
.

Let
\Gamma
be a discrete group with a finite generating set
X\subseteq\Gamma
. Then our definition of
\omega_{X}(\mathbb{Z}\Gamma)
agrees with the notion of the exponential growth rate of
\Gamma
with respect to
X
studied in
[
ABL+05
]
, and similarly respective notions of uniform exponential growth rate coincide.

In the next result we show that one can calculate
\omega_{X}(R,d)
using the size of spheres
|S_{X}(n)|\,(n\in\mathbb{N})
. Next we use it to compute the growth rate for the product fusion algebra (Remark
2.2
).

Lemma 3.12
.

Let
(R,d)
be a fusion algebra with a finite generating set
X\subseteq I
. Then
\omega_{X}(R,d)=\max\bigl(1,\lim_{n\to\infty}\sqrt[n]{|S_{X}(n)|}\,\bigr)
.

Proof.

If
\omega_{X}(R,d)=1
, then
\sqrt[n]{|S_{X}(n)|}\leq\sqrt[n]{|B_{X}(n)|}\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}1
and the claim holds.

Consider the second case,
\omega_{X}(R,d)>1
. Then
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}|S_{X}(n)|=+\infty
(as otherwise
n\mapsto|B_{X}(n)|
is bounded by a polynomial function) and we can find a strictly increasing sequence
(n_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
of natural numbers such that
|S_{X}(n_{k})|=\max_{0\leq m\leq n_{k}}|S_{X}(m)|
. For
k\in\mathbb{N}
we have

|B_{X}(n_{k})|=\sum_{m=0}^{n_{k}}|S_{X}(m)|\leq(n_{k}+1)|S_{X}(n_{k})|.

Hence using Lemma
3.8
we obtain

\displaystyle\omega_{X}(R)

\displaystyle=\lim_{k\to\infty}|B_{X}(n_{k})|^{1/n_{k}}\leq\lim_{k\to\infty}\bigl((n_{k}+1)|S_{X}(n_{k})|\bigr)^{1/n_{k}}=\lim_{n\to\infty}|S_{X}(n)|^{1/n}

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}|B_{X}(n)|^{1/n}=\omega_{X}(R).

∎

The next proposition will be used in Section
6
to reduce computation of
\omega(R(G_{q}))
to the simple case.

Proposition 3.13
.

Let
(R_{1},d_{1}),(R_{2},d_{2})
be finitely generated fusion algebras and
(R,d)
the product fusion algebra. The uniform growth rate of
(R,d)
is given by

(3.3)

\omega(R,d)=\max\bigl(\omega(R_{1},d_{1}),\omega(R_{2},d_{2})\bigr).

Proof.

Recall (Remark
2.2
) that
R=R_{1}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}R_{2}
and the set of irreducible objects in
R
is given by
I=I_{1}\times I_{2}=\{\alpha\boxtimes\beta\mid\alpha\in I_{1},\beta\in I_{2}\}
. Let
X_{1}\subseteq I_{1}
(resp.
X_{2}\subseteq I_{2}
) be a finite generating set for
R_{1}
(resp. for
R_{2}
). Then
R
is generated by (symmetric set)
X=\{\alpha\boxtimes e,e\boxtimes\beta\mid\alpha\in X_{1},\beta\in X_{2}\}
.
The length of
\alpha\boxtimes\beta\in I
is given by
\ell_{X}(\alpha\boxtimes\beta)=\ell_{X_{1}}(\alpha)+\ell_{X_{2}}(\beta)
. Thus for
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
we have

\begin{split}S_{X}(n)&=\bigcup_{k=0}^{n}\{\gamma\boxtimes\delta\;\big|\;\gamma\in S_{X_{1}}(k),\,\delta\in S_{X_{2}}(n-k)\bigr\}.\end{split}

Let
f_{X}
be the function defined by power series
f_{X}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|S_{X}(n)|z^{n}
(on the subset of
\mathbb{C}
where the series converges), similarly define
f_{X_{1}},f_{X_{2}}
. Then (see
[
BV09
, Theorem 3.1]
)

f_{X}(z)=f_{X_{1}}(z)f_{X_{2}}(z).

Comparing the radii of convergence
[
Rud87
, Section 10.5]
gives

\bigl(\limsup_{n\to\infty}|S_{X}(n)|^{1/n}\bigr)^{-1}=\min\bigl(\bigl(\limsup_{n\to\infty}|S_{X_{1}}(n)|^{1/n}\bigr)^{-1},\bigl(\limsup_{n\to\infty}|S_{X_{2}}(n)|^{1/n}\bigr)^{-1}\bigr)

or taking into account Lemma
3.8

\lim_{n\to\infty}|S_{X}(n)|^{1/n}=\max\bigl(\lim_{n\to\infty}|S_{X_{1}}(n)|^{1/n},\lim_{n\to\infty}|S_{X_{2}}(n)|^{1/n}\bigr).

Consequently by Lemma
3.12

\omega_{X}(R,d)=\max\bigl(\omega_{X_{1}}(R_{1},d_{1}),\omega_{X_{2}}(R_{2},d_{2})\bigr).

As
X_{1},X_{2}
were arbitrary finite generating sets, we have
\omega(R,d)\leq\max\bigl(\omega(R_{1},d_{1}),\omega(R_{2},d_{2})\bigr)
. Note that the argument above follows essentially the reasoning in the proof of
[
BV09
, Theorem 3.1]
.

For the converse inequality, let
X=\{\alpha_{i}^{(1)}\boxtimes\alpha^{(2)}_{i}\mid 1\leq i\leq N\}
be a finite generating set for
R
. For
k\in\{1,2\}
set
X_{k}=\{\alpha_{i}^{(k)}\mid 1\leq i\leq N\}
. Then
X_{k}
is a finite, symmetric generating set for
R_{k}
. Take
k=1
and an arbitrary
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. If
\alpha\in B_{X_{1}}(n)
, then there is
\beta\in I_{2}
such that
\alpha\boxtimes\beta\in B_{X}(n)
. Consequently
|B_{X_{1}}(n)|\leq|B_{X}(n)|
and

\omega(R_{1},d_{1})\leq\omega_{X_{1}}(R_{1},d_{1})\leq\omega_{X}(R,d).

Since
X
was an arbitrary generating set for
R
, we obtain
\omega(R_{1},d_{1})\leq\omega(R,d)
. Similarly we check
\omega(R_{2},d_{2})\leq\omega(R,d)
, which ends the proof.
∎

Fusion algebras of non-Kac type compact matrix quantum groups often have uniform exponential growth. We will quantify this observation in the next two results.

Let us introduce convenient terminology: if
\mathbb{G}
is a compact quantum group, then for
\alpha\in\Irr(\mathbb{G})
we denote
\Gamma(\alpha)=\max\operatorname{Sp}(\uprho_{\alpha})=\|\uprho_{\alpha}\|
(
[
KS18
]
,
[
KS23
, Section 3.1]
) and
T^{\sigma}(\mathbb{G})=\{t\in\mathbb{R}\mid\sigma^{h}_{t}=\textnormal{id}\}
, where
(\sigma^{h}_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
is the
modular group of the Haar integral
(
[
KS23
, Definition 2.1]
).

Proposition 3.14
.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a compact matrix quantum group which is not of Kac type and let

C=\inf\{\Gamma(\alpha)\mid\alpha\in\Irr(\mathbb{G})\colon\Gamma(\alpha)>1\}\geq 1.

Then
\omega(R(\mathbb{G}))\geq C^{2}
.

Proof.

Observe that since
\mathbb{G}
is assumed to have a fundamental representation,
R(\mathbb{G})
is finitely generated and
\omega(R(\mathbb{G}))
is well defined. If
C=1
then the claim is trivial, hence assume
C>1
.

Let
X\subseteq\Irr(\mathbb{G})
be a finite generating set. Then there is
\alpha\in X
such that
\Gamma(\alpha)>1
(as otherwise
\mathbb{G}
would have been of Kac type), hence
\Gamma(\alpha)\geq C
. By
[
KS18
, Proposition 6.1]
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
there is an irreducible representation
\beta_{n}\subseteq\alpha^{\otimes n}
such that
\Gamma(\beta_{n})=\Gamma(\alpha)^{n}\geq C^{n}
. Consequently
\beta_{n}\in B_{X}(n)
and the claim follows from

|B_{X}(n)|^{1/n}\geq|\{\beta_{n}\}|^{1/n}=d(\beta_{n})^{2/n}\geq\Gamma(\beta_{n})^{2/n}\geq C^{2}.

∎

Corollary 3.15
.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a compact matrix quantum group. If
T^{\sigma}(\mathbb{G})=\lambda\mathbb{Z}
for
\lambda>0
, then
R(\mathbb{G})
has uniform exponential growth with
\omega(R(\mathbb{G}))\geq e^{2\pi/\lambda}
.

Proof.

Since
T^{\sigma}(\mathbb{G})\neq\mathbb{R}
,
\mathbb{G}
is not of Kac type. Take
\alpha\in\Irr(\mathbb{G})
with
\Gamma(\alpha)>1
and choose an orthonormal basis in
\mathsf{H}_{\alpha}
in which
\uprho_{\alpha}
is diagonal and
(\uprho_{\alpha})_{1,1}=\Gamma(\alpha)
. Then
\sigma^{h}_{t}(U^{\alpha}_{1,1})=\Gamma(\alpha)^{2it}U^{\alpha}_{1,1}\,(t\in\mathbb{R})
(
[
NT13
, Section 1.7]
). Hence
\Gamma(\alpha)^{2i\lambda}=1
and
\lambda\log(\Gamma(\alpha))\in\pi\mathbb{Z}
. It follows that
\Gamma(\alpha)\in\{e^{\pi k/\lambda}\mid k\in\mathbb{Z}\}
and
\Gamma(\alpha)\geq e^{\pi/\lambda}
. Now the claim is a consequence of Proposition
3.14
.
∎

Let us compare these lower bounds to the actual value of
\omega(R(\mathbb{G}))
in several cases that we will study later on in detail.

Remark 3.16
.

For a given compact quantum group
\mathbb{G}
let us write
C_{\mathbb{G}}=\inf\{\Gamma(\alpha)\mid\alpha\in\Irr(\mathbb{G})\colon\Gamma(\alpha)>1\}
for the constant appearing in Proposition
3.14
.

(i)

For
SU_{q}(2)
with
q\in(0,1)
the lower bounds on
\omega(R(SU_{q}(2)))
given in Proposition
3.14
and Corollary
3.15
are in fact equalities (see Proposition
4.5
).

(ii)

More generally, consider
q\in(0,1)
and the
q
-deformation
G_{q}
for a simply connected, semisimple, compact Lie group
G
, with decomposition into simple factors
G_{q}\simeq\prod_{a=1}^{l}(G_{a})_{q}
(Proposition
6.7
). Then
\omega(R(G_{q}))=\max_{1\leq a\leq l}\omega(R((G_{a})_{q}))
(Proposition
3.13
),
C_{G_{q}}=\min_{1\leq a\leq l}C_{(G_{a})_{q}}
. Consequently, to compare
\omega(R(G_{q}))
and
C_{G_{q}}
it is enough to consider the simple case. For simple
G
not of type
B_{N}(N\geq 4),D_{N}(N\geq 5)
, we see that in fact
\omega(R(G_{q}))={C_{G_{q}}}^{2}
, i.e. Proposition
3.14
gives a sharp bound. Indeed, the proof of Theorem
6.9
shows that in these cases the lower bound of Corollary
6.6
is attained. The claim follows as
\Gamma(\lambda)=q^{-\langle\lambda|2\rho\rangle}
(Proposition
6.4
). If
G
is simple and of type
B_{N}(N\geq 4)
or
D_{N}(N\geq 5)
, then
\omega(R(G_{q}))>{C_{G_{q}}}^{2}
, as follows from the proof of Theorem
6.9
.

For general
G
we have
T^{\sigma}(G_{q})=\tfrac{\pi}{\log(q)}\mathbb{Z}
(
[
KS23
, Proposition 2.3, Theorem 4.12]
). Thus by Theorem
6.9
the inequality of Corollary
3.15
is strict unless
G_{a}=SU(2)
for all
1\leq a\leq l
.

(iii)

For
SO_{q}(3)
with
q\in(0,1)
we have
C_{SO_{q}(3)}=q^{-2}
and
\omega(R(SO_{q}(3)))=q^{-4}
by Proposition
5.4
, hence Proposition
3.14
gives a sharp bound. On the other hand, it is easy to check that
T^{\sigma}(SO_{q}(3))=\tfrac{\pi}{\log(q)}\mathbb{Z}
, and we see that Corollary
3.15
is sub-optimal.

(iv)

Consider a non-Kac type free unitary quantum group
U_{F}^{+}
with
F\in GL_{N}(\mathbb{C})
satisfying
\Tr(F^{*}F)=\Tr((F^{*}F)^{-1})
(see Section
7
) and let
0<q<1
be defined by
q+q^{-1}=\Tr(F^{*}F)
. We have
C_{U_{F}^{+}}=\min(\Gamma(\alpha),\Gamma(\overline{\alpha}))

[
KS23
, Lemma 3.8]
and it is easy to check that
q\leq{C_{U_{F}^{+}}}^{-1}
. Using Theorem
7.7
and Proposition
7.9
we see that
\omega(R(U_{F}^{+}))>C_{U_{F}^{+}}^{2}
, i.e. the bound of Proposition
3.14
is strict. Consequently, the same is true for Corollary
3.15
.

At the end of this subsection we relate the exponential growth rate to the (inner) Følner constant, obtaining an analog of
[
ABL+05
, Proposition 1.4]
.

Proposition 3.17
.

Let
(R,d)
be a fusion algebra with a finite generating set
X\subseteq I
. We have
F\o l^{inn}_{X}(R,d)\leq 1-\tfrac{1}{\omega_{X}(R,d)}
and consequently
F\o l^{inn}(R,d)\leq 1-\tfrac{1}{\omega(R,d)}
.

Proof.

Fix
n\geq 2
. We claim that

(3.4)

\partial_{X}^{inn}(B_{X}(n))\subseteq S_{X}(n).

Take
\alpha\in\partial_{X}^{inn}(B_{X}(n))
. Then
\alpha\in B_{X}(n)
. Suppose that
\ell_{X}(\alpha)<n
. Since
\alpha\in\partial^{inn}_{X}(B_{X}(n))
, there exists
x\in X
such that
\supp(\alpha x)\nsubseteq B_{X}(n)
. Thus, there is
\beta\notin B_{X}(n)
so that
\beta\subseteq\alpha x
. But this forces
\ell_{X}(\beta)\leq\ell_{X}(\alpha)+1\leq n
. This is a contradition, which proves (
3.4
). It follows that

\frac{|\partial^{inn}_{X}(B_{X}(n))|}{|B_{X}(n)|}\leq\frac{|S_{X}(n)|}{|B_{X}(n)|}=\frac{|B_{X}(n)|-|B_{X}(n-1)|}{|B_{X}(n)|}=1-\frac{|B_{X}(n-1)|}{|B_{X}(n)|}.

Since
\omega_{X}(R,d)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sqrt[n]{|B_{X}(n)|}
, we also have
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\tfrac{|B_{X}(n)|}{|B_{X}(n-1)|}\leq\omega_{X}(R,d)
(
[
Rud76
, Theorem 3.37]
). Consequently

\begin{split}F\o l_{X}^{inn}(R,d)&=\inf_{A\subseteq I,A\textup{ finite}}\frac{|\partial_{X}^{inn}(A)|}{|A|}\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{|\partial^{inn}_{X}(B_{X}(n))|}{|B_{X}(n)|}\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\bigl(1-\frac{|B_{X}(n-1)|}{|B_{X}(n)|}\bigr)\\
&=1-\frac{1}{\liminf_{n\to\infty}\tfrac{|B_{X}(n)|}{|B_{X}(n-1)|}}\leq 1-\frac{1}{\omega_{X}(R,d)}.\end{split}

∎

Remark 3.18
.

If
(R,d)
is a fusion algebra with a finite generating set
X\subseteq I
, then using a similar argument we can bound
F\o l_{X}(R,d)
as follows:

(3.5)

F\o l_{X}(R,d)\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{|B_{X}(n+1)|}{|B_{X}(n)|}-\frac{1}{\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{|B_{X}(n+1)|}{|B_{X}(n)|}}.

It is not clear if the limit
\lim_{n\to\infty}\tfrac{|B_{X}(n+1)|}{|B_{X}(n)|}
exists in general, hence we cannot express the right hand side of (
3.5
) using
\omega_{X}(R,d)
.

Corollary 3.19
.

Let
(R,d)
be a finitely generated fusion algebra. If
(R,d)
is uniformly non-amenable, i.e.
F\o l^{inn}(R,d)>0
, then it has uniform exponential growth.

3.3.
Uniform Kazhdan constants for representations

The article
[
ABL+05
]
connects the Følner constant to so-called
Kazhdan constants for representations
. The variations of the latter in the case of quantum groups, and the context of (central) Property (T), have been considered for example in
[
Fim10
]
and, in a somewhat more general version, in
[
DSV17
]
.

Our framework is rather connected to
central Kazhdan constants
, related to
central Property (T)
of Arano, as considered for example in
[
VV19
]
and in
[
NY16
]
. It is in particular the language of the last of these papers which is directly relevant here.

Recall that
(R,d)
is a fusion algebra and let
\mathcal{C}_{R}
denote the complexification of
R
, which we can view as a complex unital
*
-algebra with vector space structure equal to
\mathbb{C}[I]
.

Definition 3.20
.

Suppose that
H_{\pi}
is a Hilbert space and
\pi\colon\mathcal{C}_{R}\to\B(H_{\pi})
is a unital
*
-representation, which contains no non-trivial
invariant vectors
; this means that

\textup{Fix}(\pi)=\{\xi\in H_{\pi}\mid\forall_{\alpha\in I}\,\pi(\alpha)\xi=d(\alpha)\xi\}=0.

For a finite generating set
X\subseteq I
set

K(X,\pi,(R,d))=\inf_{\xi\in H_{\pi},\|\xi\|=1}\;\;\max_{\alpha\in X}\;\;\;\tfrac{\|\pi(\alpha)\xi-d(\alpha)\xi\|}{d(\alpha)},

and further

K(\pi,(R,d))=\inf_{X\subseteq I,X-\textup{finite, generating}}K(X,\pi,(R,d)).

We call
K(\pi,(R,d))
the
Kazhdan constant of
\pi
.

The reason for the normalisation chosen in the above definition will become apparent later on.

Consider now the left regular representation of
\mathcal{C}_{R}
on
\ell^{2}(I)
given by a bounded linear extension of the formula (
\alpha,\beta\in I
)

\lambda(\alpha)(\delta_{\beta})=\sum_{\eta\in I}N_{\alpha,\beta}^{\eta}\delta_{\eta}.

The fact that the operators
\lambda(\alpha)
are bounded is essentially due to
[
HI98
]
. It will be more convenient for us to work with the unitarily equivalent
right-regular
representation
\rho\colon\mathcal{C}_{R}\to\B(\ell^{2}(I))
, given by the formula
\rho(c)=U^{*}\lambda(c)U
,
c\in\mathcal{C}_{R}
, where
U\colon\ell^{2}(I)\to\ell^{2}(I)
is the unitary mapping
\delta_{\alpha}\mapsto\delta_{\overline{\alpha}}\,(\alpha\in I)
. It is then easy to check via the Frobenius reciprocity that we have (
\alpha,\beta\in I
)

\rho(\alpha)(\delta_{\beta})=\sum_{\eta\in I}N_{\beta,\overline{\alpha}}^{\eta}\delta_{\eta}.

The following is inspired by
[
ABL+05
, Proposition 2.4]
.

Proposition 3.21
.

Let
(R,d)
be a finitely generated fusion algebra and let
\rho\colon\mathcal{C}_{R}\to\B(\ell^{2}(I))
be the right regular representation of
\mathcal{C}_{R}
. Then

F\o l(R,d)\geq{K(\rho,(R,d))^{2}}.

Proof.

Fix
\epsilon>0
.
Choose a finite generating set
X\subseteq I
and a non-empty finite subset
A\subseteq I
such that

F\o l(R)+\epsilon>M:=\frac{|\partial_{X}(A)|}{|A|}
. Set
\xi=\sum_{\beta\in A}d(\beta)\delta_{\beta}
, fix
\alpha\in X
and consider the function
\phi\colon I\to\mathbb{C}
given by

\phi(\eta)=(\rho(\alpha)\xi-d(\alpha)\xi)(\eta)=\sum_{\beta\in A}d(\beta)N_{\eta,\alpha}^{\beta}-d(\alpha)d(\eta)\chi_{A}(\eta),\;\;\eta\in I.

Consider several possible cases. First if
\eta\in A^{c}\setminus\partial_{X}(A)
, we have
N_{\eta,\alpha}^{\beta}=0
for every
\beta\in A
(as
\eta\alpha
does not intersect
A
), so
\phi(\eta)=0
. Second, if
\eta\in A\setminus\partial_{X}(A)
, we have
\eta\alpha\subseteq A
, so

\sum_{\beta\in A}N_{\eta,\alpha}^{\beta}d(\beta)=d(\eta)d(\alpha)
and again
\phi(\eta)=0
.

For
\eta\in\partial_{X}(A)
we can simply estimate

|\phi(\eta)|=\bigl|\sum_{\beta\in A}d(\beta)N_{\eta,\alpha}^{\beta}-d(\alpha)d(\eta)\chi_{A}(\eta)\bigr|\leq d(\alpha)d(\eta),

using the inequality
\sum_{\beta\in A}N_{\eta,\alpha}^{\beta}d(\beta)\leq d(\eta)d(\alpha)
.

Note that
\|\xi\|^{2}=\sum_{\beta\in A}d(\beta)^{2}=|A|
and set
\tilde{\xi}=\xi\|\xi\|^{-1}
. We then have

\displaystyle\|\rho(\alpha)\tilde{\xi}-d(\alpha)\tilde{\xi}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=|A|^{-1}\sum_{\eta\in I}|\phi(\eta)|^{2}=|A|^{-1}\sum_{\eta\in\partial_{X}(A)}|\phi(\eta)|^{2}\leq|A|^{-1}\sum_{\eta\in\partial_{X}(A)}d(\alpha)^{2}d(\eta)^{2}

\displaystyle=d(\alpha)^{2}|A|^{-1}|\partial_{X}(A)|=d(\alpha)^{2}M.

This implies that
K(X,\rho,(R,d))^{2}\leq M
and as
\epsilon
was arbitrary, the proof is complete.
∎

Note that the statement in
[
ABL+05
, Proposition 2.4]
involves the ‘inner’ Følner constant and apparently compensates by adding a numerical factor (claiming for
R=\mathbb{Z}\Gamma
what in our language would be
F\o l^{inn}(R,d)\geq\frac{{K(\rho,(R,d))^{2}}}{2}
); it is however not clear how this can be achieved (see also
[
LPV08
, Appendix A]
).

We can finally connect the Kazhdan constants above to categorical Property (T), as considered for example in
[
NY16
]
. So suppose that
R
is a fusion ring associated to a rigid
C^{*}
-tensor category
\mathfrak{C}
. One can reformulate
[
NY16
, Proposition 4.22]
by saying that
\mathfrak{C}
has Property (T) if and only if there exist
\epsilon>0
and a finite set
X\subseteq I
such that for any
admissible
(see
[
NY16
, Section 3]
or
[
PV15
, Section 4]
) representation
\pi\colon\mathcal{C}_{R}\to\B(H)
we have
K(X,\pi,(R,d))>\epsilon
. If
R
is finitely generated, we can naturally assume that
X
is generating (possibly adding some elements to it). In other words,
\mathfrak{C}
has Property (T) if the class of all admissible representations of
\mathcal{C}_{R}
which do not admit invariant vectors is isolated from the trivial representation. We could thus propose the following definition, modelled after
[
Sha00
, Definition 8.1]
(see also
[
Osi02
, Definition 3]
).

Definition 3.22
.

Let
\mathfrak{C}
be a rigid
C^{*}
-tensor category with the associated fusion algebra
(R,d)
and let
\mathcal{F}
be a class of admissible representations of
\mathcal{C}_{R}
which do not admit invariant vectors. We say that
\mathcal{F}
is uniformly isolated from the trivial representation if there exists
\epsilon>0
such that for every
\pi\in\mathcal{F}
we have
K(\pi,(R,d))>\epsilon
.

Now as the representation
\rho
considered above is admissible (see for example
[
PV15
, Corollary 4.4]
), and contains no invariant vectors if and only if
I
is infinite, we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 3.23
.

Suppose that
(R,d)
is the finitely generated fusion algebra associated to a rigid
C^{*}
-tensor category
\mathfrak{C}
. Then if
F\o l(R,d)=0
, and
I
is infinite, then the left regular representation of
\mathfrak{C}
cannot be uniformly isolated from the trivial one.

Note that
[
HI98
, Theorem 4.1]
and the proof of
[
PV15
, Proposition 5.3]
imply that a rigid
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-tensor category
\mathfrak{C}
(with the associated fusion algebra
(R,d)
) is amenable if and only if the regular representation contains almost invariant vectors. Assuming that
(R,d)
is finitely generated, this is the same as saying that for every finite generating set
X\subseteq I
we have
K(X,\rho,(R,d))=0
.

4.
Fusion algebra of
SU_{q}(2)

In this section we will compute the invariants of the last section for the fusion algebra related to representation theory of
SU_{q}(2)
, with the deformation parameter
q\in(0,1]
.

We will index irreducible representations of
SU_{q}(2)
by
\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, so that in particular
U_{1}
denotes the standard fundamental representation of
SU_{q}(2)
.
The resulting fusion algebra
R
is determined by the fusion rules of
SU(2)
. Thus
U_{1}
generates
R
, the basis
I
can be identified with non-negative integers (with
0:=e
,
1:=U_{1}
) and the fusion rules are simply

(4.1)

m\otimes n=|m-n|\oplus(|m-n|+2)\oplus\cdots\oplus(m+n),\;\;\;m,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}.

An arbitrary dimension function
\tilde{d}\colon\mathbb{Z}_{+}\to[1,\infty)
compatible with the above fusion rules is determined by
\tilde{d}(1)
. As noted in Subsection
2.1
the fusion algebra
R(SU(2))
is amenable, so that by Proposition
2.5
we must have
\tilde{d}(1)\geq 2
. For the quantum dimension of
SU_{q}(2)
we have specifically
d(1)=q+q^{-1}
. Note that we can realise all possible dimension functions on the ring above using some
q\in(0,1]
.

Let us start with a well-known lemma. Recall the notion of
q
-number
[n]_{q}\,(n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+})
, introduced in Section
2.1
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
q\in(0,1]
and let
d\colon\mathbb{Z}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
denote the quantum dimension function of
R(SU_{q}(2))
. Then
d(n)=[n+1]_{q}
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
.

Proof.

The above equalities are a consequence of
d(0)=1
,
d(1)=q+q^{-1}
and the recurrence relation

(4.2)

d(n)d(1)=d(n-1)+d(n+1),\;\;\;n\in\mathbb{N},

which is obviously satisfied by the
q
-integers (see
[
Kac98
, Formula(7)]
).

If
d(1)>2
, we can also offer an alternative argument, which will be useful later: set
d=d(1)
and let

d_{\pm}=\frac{d\pm\sqrt{d^{2}-4}}{2}.

Then

d(n)=\alpha d_{+}^{n}+\beta d_{-}^{n},\;\;\;n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+},

where
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
are determined by the initial conditions

\alpha+\beta=1,\;\;\alpha d_{+}+\beta d_{-}=d.

Indeed, note that
d(n)
is determined by the recurrence relation (
4.2
). The standard trick with writing the recurrence relation in a matrix form shows that for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
we have

\begin{pmatrix}d(n+1)\\
d(n)\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}d&-1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}d(n)\\
d(n-1)\end{pmatrix},

and it is easy to check that the matrix in the middle has eigenvalues
\{d_{+},d_{-}\}
. Note that in fact we have
d_{+}=q^{-1}
,
d_{-}=q
; thus

\alpha=\frac{q^{-1}}{q^{-1}-q}
,
\beta=-\frac{q}{q^{-1}-q}
and we recover the formula in the statement of the lemma.

∎

The next lemma is also easy.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
q\in(0,1)
and let
d\colon\mathbb{Z}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
denote the quantum dimension function of
R(SU_{q}(2))
. The functions
f\colon\mathbb{N}\ni M\mapsto\tfrac{d(M)^{2}}{\sum_{m=1}^{M}d(m)^{2}}\in\mathbb{R}
and

g\colon\mathbb{N}\ni M\mapsto\tfrac{d(M+1)^{2}}{\sum_{m=1}^{M}d(m)^{2}}\in\mathbb{R}
are strictly decreasing.

Proof.

Fix
M\in\mathbb{N}
. We have
f(M+1)<f(M)
if, and only if

(4.3)

\begin{split}d(M+1)^{2}\sum_{m=1}^{M}d(m)^{2}<d(M)^{2}\sum_{m=1}^{M+1}d(m)^{2}.\end{split}

To prove the above inequality, it is enough to argue that

(4.4)

d(M+1)d(k)\leq d(M)d(k+1)

holds for
k\in\{1,\dotsc,M\}
. Indeed, then inequality (
4.3
) holds as

\displaystyle\quad\;d(M)^{2}\sum_{m=1}^{M+1}d(m)^{2}-d(M+1)^{2}\sum_{m=1}^{M}d(m)^{2}

\displaystyle=d(M)^{2}d(1)^{2}+\sum_{m=1}^{M}\bigl(d(M)^{2}d(m+1)^{2}-d(M+1)^{2}d(m)^{2})>0.

Recall then from the proof of Lemma
4.1
that we have
\alpha>0
and
\beta<0
such that
d(n)=\alpha q^{-n}+\beta q^{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Thus

\displaystyle d(M+1)d(k)-d(M)d(k+1)

\displaystyle=\alpha\beta(q^{-M-1}q^{k}+q^{M+1}q^{-k}-q^{M}q^{-k-1}-q^{k+1}q^{-M})

\displaystyle=\alpha\beta((q^{-M}q^{k}-q^{M}q^{-k})(q^{-1}-q))\leq 0.

We can deal with the function
g
very similarly. Note first that
g(M+1)<g(M)
if and only if

d(M+2)^{2}\sum_{m=1}^{M}d(m)^{2}<d(M+1)^{2}\sum_{m=1}^{M+1}d(m)^{2}.

Then observe that

\displaystyle\quad\;d(M+1)^{2}\sum_{m=1}^{M+1}d(m)^{2}-d(M+2)^{2}\sum_{m=1}^{M}d(m)^{2}

\displaystyle=d(M+1)^{2}d(1)^{2}+\sum_{m=1}^{M}\bigl(d(M+1)^{2}d(m+1)^{2}-d(M+2)^{2}d(m)^{2}\bigr),

and the last expression is strictly positive thanks to the inequality (
4.4
) which we have already established.

∎

We are ready to compute the Følner constants in the case of the standard generating set.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
X=\{1\}
be a generating set of
R(SU_{q}(2))
,
q\in(0,1)
. Then we have
F\o l_{X}^{inn}(R(SU_{q}(2)))=1-q^{2}
,

F\o l_{X}(R(SU_{q}(2)))=q^{-2}-q^{2}
.

Proof.

We begin with the inner Følner constant.
By definition we have

F\o l_{X}^{inn}(R(SU_{q}(2)))=\inf_{A}\tfrac{|\partial_{X}^{inn}A|}{|A|}

where
A\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{+}
is finite and non-empty. So, fix such a set
A
, put
M=\sup A
and define
A^{\prime}=\{0,\dotsc,M\}
. Clearly we have
|A|\leq|A^{\prime}|
. Furthermore,

\partial_{X}^{inn}A^{\prime}=\{\alpha\in A^{\prime}\mid(\alpha-1\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\setminus A^{\prime})\,\vee\,(\alpha+1\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\setminus A^{\prime})\}=\{M\}.

Since
M\in A
and
M+1\notin A
we also have
M\in\partial_{X}^{inn}A
, thus
|\partial_{X}^{inn}A^{\prime}|\leq|\partial_{X}^{inn}A|
. It follows that

\frac{|\partial_{X}^{inn}A^{\prime}|}{|A^{\prime}|}\leq\frac{|\partial_{X}^{inn}A|}{|A|}

and consequently

F\o l_{X}^{inn}(R(SU_{q}(2)))=\inf_{M\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\frac{|\partial_{X}^{inn}\{0,\dotsc,M\}|}{|\{0,\dotsc,M\}|}=\inf_{M\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\frac{|\{M\}|}{|\{0,\dotsc,M\}|}.

The proof of Lemma
4.1
implies that we have
\alpha>0
and
\beta<0
such that
d(n)=\alpha q^{-n}+\beta q^{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Since
\tfrac{|\{M\}|}{|\{0,\dotsc,M\}|}=\tfrac{d(M)^{2}}{\sum_{k=0}^{M}d(k)^{2}}
, we can use the monotonicity of the function
f
from Lemma
4.2
to get that

\displaystyle F\o l_{X}^{inn}(R(SU_{q}(2)))

\displaystyle=\lim_{M\to\infty}\tfrac{|\{M\}|}{|\{0,\dotsc,M\}|}=\lim_{M\to\infty}\tfrac{d(M)^{2}}{\sum_{k=0}^{M}d(k)^{2}}=\lim_{M\to\infty}\frac{(\alpha q^{-M}+\beta q^{M})^{2}}{1+\sum_{k=1}^{M}(\alpha q^{-k}+\beta q^{k})^{2}}

\displaystyle=\lim_{M\to\infty}\frac{\alpha^{2}q^{-2M}+2\alpha\beta+\beta^{2}q^{2M}}{1+\sum_{k=1}^{M}(\alpha^{2}q^{-2k}+2\alpha\beta+\beta^{2}q^{2k})}=\frac{\alpha^{2}}{-\alpha^{2}q^{-2}(1-q^{-2})^{-1}}

\displaystyle=1-q^{2}.

The argument concerning the other uniform Følner constant is very similar. Given
A\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{+}
finite and non-empty we note as above that for
M=\sup A
and
A^{\prime}=\{0,\dotsc,M\}
we have
\partial_{X}A^{\prime}=\{M,M+1\}
, and

F\o l_{X}(R(SU_{q}(2)))=\inf_{M\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\frac{|\partial_{X}^{inn}\{0,\dotsc,M\}|}{|\{0,\dotsc,M\}|}=\inf_{M\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\frac{|\{M,M+1\}|}{|\{0,\dotsc,M\}|}.

Hence, this time using the monotonicity of
f+g
, where
f
and
g
are functions of Lemma
4.2
,

\displaystyle F\o l_{X}(R(SU_{q}(2)))

\displaystyle=\lim_{M\to\infty}\tfrac{|\{M,M+1\}|}{|\{0,\dotsc,M\}|}=\lim_{M\to\infty}\tfrac{d(M)^{2}+d(M+1)^{2}}{\sum_{k=0}^{M}d(k)^{2}}

\displaystyle=\lim_{M\to\infty}\frac{(\alpha q^{-M}+\beta q^{M})^{2}+(\alpha q^{-(M+1)}+\beta q^{M+1})^{2}}{1+\sum_{k=1}^{M}(\alpha q^{-k}+\beta q^{k})^{2}}

\displaystyle=\lim_{M\to\infty}\frac{\alpha^{2}(1+q^{-2})q^{-2M}+4\alpha\beta+\beta^{2}(1+q^{2})q^{2M}}{1+\sum_{k=1}^{M}(\alpha^{2}q^{-2k}+2\alpha\beta+\beta^{2}q^{2k})}

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha^{2}(1+q^{-2})}{-\alpha^{2}q^{-2}(1-q^{-2})^{-1}}=(1-q^{2})(1+q^{-2})=q^{-2}-q^{2}.

∎

As expected, the standard generating set turns out to be optimal.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
q\in(0,1]
. Then

\begin{split}F\o l^{inn}(R(SU_{q}(2)))&=1-q^{2},\\
F\o l(R(SU_{q}(2)))&=q^{-2}-q^{2}.\end{split}

Proof.

If
q=1
then
R(SU(2))
is amenable and the claim follows from Proposition
3.6
. Assume thus that
q\in(0,1)
.

Let
X
be any generating set of
R(SU_{q}(2))
. The fusion rules of
SU_{q}(2)
imply that there exists a representation
\alpha\in X
which corresponds to an odd number in
\mathbb{Z}_{+}\simeq\Irr(SU_{q}(2))
. Furthermore,
\{\alpha\}
is also a generating subset of
R(SU_{q}(2))
. As
\partial_{\{\alpha\}}^{inn}A\subseteq\partial_{X}^{inn}A
and
\partial_{\{\alpha\}}A\subseteq\partial_{X}A
for any finite
A\subseteq\Irr(SU_{q}(2))
, we can assume that
X=\{\alpha\}
.

Now, for any
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
we have

m\otimes\alpha\simeq|m-\alpha|\oplus(|m-\alpha|+2)\oplus\cdots\oplus(m+\alpha)

hence
\partial_{\{1\}}^{inn}A\subseteq\partial_{\{\alpha\}}^{inn}A
,
\partial_{\{1\}}A\subseteq\partial_{\{\alpha\}}A
. Consequently
F\o l^{inn}(R(SU_{q}(2)))=F\o l^{inn}_{\{1\}}(R(SU_{q}(2)))
,

F\o l(R(SU_{q}(2)))=F\o l_{\{1\}}(R(SU_{q}(2)))
and the result follows from Lemma
4.3
.
∎

Next, let us record the value of the uniform growth rate for
R(SU_{q}(2))
.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
q\in\left(0,1\right]
. Then
\omega(R(SU_{q}(2)))=q^{-2}
.

The above result is a special case of Theorem
6.9
, where we compute the uniform growth rates for more general
q
-deformations. One can also obtain this value by more elementary means; we present such reasoning in the next section, in the case of
SO_{q}(3)
.

The following proposition computes the uniform Kazhdan constant for the regular representation of the fusion algebra

R(SU_{q}(2))
.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
q\in(0,1]
. Then
K(\rho,R(SU_{q}(2)))=1-\frac{2}{[2]_{q}}=\tfrac{(1-q)^{2}}{q^{2}+1}
.

Proof.

If
q=1
then
SU(2)
is coamenable and
K(\rho,R(SU(2)))=0
follows from Proposition
3.21
and earlier discussions. Assume
q<1
.

We will use the following standard result, which follows from the spectral theorem
[
Soł18
, Section 4.3]
: if
T
is a bounded, self-adjoint operator on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
and
r\in\mathbb{R}
, then

(4.5)

\inf_{\xi\in\mathcal{H},\|\xi\|=1}\|T\xi-r\xi\|=\operatorname{dist}(\sigma(T),r),

where
\sigma(T)
is the spectrum of
T
.

We begin by analysing properties of the regular representation
\rho
, acting on the Hilbert space
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{+})
. For clarity of the notation write
\alpha_{m}=m\in\Irr(SU_{q}(2))
and
R=R(SU_{q}(2))
. Observe that

(4.6)

\rho(\alpha_{1})\delta_{n}=\begin{cases}\delta_{1},&n=0,\\
\delta_{n-1}+\delta_{n+1},&n\geq 1,\end{cases}

hence
\rho(\alpha_{1})
is the sum of unilateral shift and its adjoint, in particular
\rho(\alpha_{1})^{*}=\rho(\alpha_{1})
. Let us define von Neumann algebra

\M=\{\rho(\alpha_{1})\}^{\prime\prime}=\overline{\lin}^{\,\sigma\textsc{-wot}}\{\rho(\alpha_{1})^{k}\mid k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\}\subseteq\B(\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{+}))

) implies that

is a cyclic vector for

is commutative,

is also separating. Consequently,

is a faithful normal state. Observe that for

, hence

\omega(\rho(\alpha_{1})^{k})=h(\chi(\alpha_{1}^{\otimes k}))=h(\chi(\alpha_{1})^{k}).

Let

be a polynomial function,

. By

By continuity we obtain
\omega(f(\rho(\alpha_{1})))=\tfrac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-2}^{2}f(t)\sqrt{4-t^{2}}\operatorname{d}\!t
for any

and it follows that

.

In what follows, we will use Chebyshev polynomials of the second kind. More precisely, let

be the Chebyshev polynomials of the second kind (see
[

\begin{split}K(X,\rho,R)&=\inf_{\xi\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{+}),\|\xi\|=1}\max_{m\in X}\tfrac{\|\rho(\alpha_{m})\xi-d(m)\xi\|}{d(m)}\geq\max_{m\in X}\inf_{\xi\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{+}),\|\xi\|=1}\tfrac{\|\rho(\alpha_{m})\xi-d(m)\xi\|}{d(m)}\\
&=\max_{m\in X}\tfrac{1}{d(m)}\operatorname{dist}\bigl(\sigma(\rho(\alpha_{m})),d(m)\bigr)=\max_{m\in X}\tfrac{1}{d(m)}\operatorname{dist}\bigl(P_{m}(\sigma(\rho(\alpha_{1}))),d(m)\bigr)\\
&=\max_{m\in X}\tfrac{1}{d(m)}\operatorname{dist}\bigl(P_{m}(\left[-2,2\right]),d(m)\bigr).\end{split}

Using

Repeating the reasoning above, we see that this lower bound is attained for the generating set

, which ends the proof.
∎

Note that we in particular obtain for

Lemma 6.3

\max_{\mu\in\Pi(\lambda)}\langle\mu|\rho\rangle=\langle\lambda|\rho\rangle\quad\textnormal{and}\quad\min_{\mu\in\Pi(\lambda)}\langle\mu|\rho\rangle=-\langle\lambda|\rho\rangle.

\mu\in\Pi(\lambda)

. Using
\rho=\sum_{j=1}^{r}\varpi_{j}
and our normalisation of the inner product, we calculate

\langle\rho|\alpha\rangle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{r}\langle\varpi_{j}|C(\alpha,i)\alpha_{i}\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{r}C(\alpha,i)\tfrac{\langle\alpha_{i}|\alpha_{i}\rangle}{2}\geq 1.

It follows that
\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}}[\langle\rho|\alpha\rangle]_{q}\geq\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}}[1]_{q}=1
. On the other hand, since
\rho=\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}}\alpha
, we have

\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}}[\langle\lambda+\rho|\alpha\rangle]_{q}\leq\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}}\tfrac{q^{-\langle\lambda+\rho|\alpha\rangle}}{q^{-1}-q}=\tfrac{q^{-\langle\lambda+\rho|2\rho\rangle}}{(q^{-1}-q)^{s/2}}.

Proposition
6.2
ends the proof.
∎

As a first step towards calculation of the uniform growth rate
\omega(R(G_{q}))
, we provide a general formula for the growth rate for an arbitrary generating set.

Proposition 6.5
.

Let
Y\subseteq\bf{P}^{+}
be a finite generating set for
R(G_{q})
. Then

\omega_{Y}(R(G_{q}))=\max_{\lambda\in Y}q^{-4\langle\lambda|\rho\rangle}

Proof.

Take
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Observe that for
0\neq\lambda\in B_{Y}(n)
we have
\lambda\subseteq\lambda_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\lambda_{k}
for some
1\leq k\leq n,\lambda_{1},\dotsc,\lambda_{k}\in Y
. Hence

\lambda\in\Pi(\lambda_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\lambda_{k})=\{\mu_{1}+\cdots+\mu_{k}\,|\,\mu_{1}\in\Pi(\lambda_{1}),\dotsc,\mu_{k}\in\Pi(\lambda_{k})\}.

Consequently, using Proposition
6.4
and Lemma
6.3
we obtain

\displaystyle\max_{\lambda\in B_{Y}(n)}d(\lambda)

\displaystyle\leq\max_{\lambda\in B_{Y}(n)}(q^{-1}-q)^{-s/2}q^{-\langle\rho|2\rho\rangle}q^{-\langle\lambda|2\rho\rangle}

\displaystyle\leq(q^{-1}-q)^{-s/2}q^{-\langle\rho|2\rho\rangle}\bigl(\,\max_{\mu\in\bigcup_{\lambda\in Y}\Pi(\lambda)}q^{-\langle\mu|2\rho\rangle}\bigr)^{n}

\displaystyle=(q^{-1}-q)^{-s/2}q^{-\langle\rho|2\rho\rangle}\bigl(\,\max_{\lambda\in Y}q^{-\langle\lambda|2\rho\rangle}\bigr)^{n}.

Since
n\mapsto\#B_{Y}(n)
has polynomial growth (Lemma
6.1
), it follows that

\displaystyle\sqrt[n]{|B_{Y}(n)|}

\displaystyle\leq(\#B_{Y}(n))^{1/n}\max_{\lambda\in B_{Y}(n)}d(\lambda)^{2/n}

\displaystyle\leq(\#B_{Y}(n))^{1/n}(q^{-1}-q)^{-s/n}q^{-4\langle\rho|\rho\rangle/n}\max_{\lambda\in Y}q^{-4\langle\lambda|\rho\rangle}\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}\max_{\lambda\in Y}q^{-4\langle\lambda|\rho\rangle}.

On the other hand, let
\lambda_{0}\in Y
be a representation which maximises the expression
\langle\lambda|\rho\rangle\,(\lambda\in Y)
. Since
n\lambda_{0}\in\bf{P}^{+}
is a subrepresentation of
\lambda_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes\lambda_{0}
(
n
-fold tensor product), we have

and using Proposition
6.4

\sqrt[n]{|B_{Y}(n)|}\geq\sqrt[n]{|\{n\lambda_{0}\}|}=d(n\lambda_{0})^{2/n}\geq\bigl(q^{-\langle n\lambda_{0}|2\rho\rangle}\bigr)^{2/n}=q^{-4\langle\lambda_{0}|\rho\rangle}.

∎

Lemma 7.1

(1)

(2)

(1)

We are now ready to deduce that the optimal exponential growth rate is obtained by considering the canonical generating set.

Proposition 7.6

We have
\omega(R(U_{F}^{+}))=\omega_{X_{can}}(R(U_{F}^{+}))

Proof.

Take an arbitrary finite generating set

and fix

with

is not of the form

for

where the above

, Theorem 4.1]
):

\mathcal{S}_{SU_{q}(2)}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}[n+1]_{q}^{2}z^{n}=\tfrac{1+z}{(1-q^{-2}z)(1-q^{2}z)(1-z)}.

Hence

\mathcal{P}_{SU_{q}(2)}(z)=\tfrac{1+z-(1-q^{-2}z)(1-q^{2}z)(1-z)}{1+z}.

It follows that

\mathcal{S}_{SU_{q}(2)\widehat{\star}SU_{q}(2)}(z)=\tfrac{1}{1-\mathcal{P}_{SU_{q}(2)\widehat{\star}SU_{q}(2)}(z)}=\tfrac{1}{1-2\mathcal{P}_{SU_{q}(2)}(z)}=\tfrac{1+z}{1-(2q^{-2}+3+2q^{2})z+2(q^{-2}+1+q^{2})z^{2}-2z^{3}}.

and

(7.8)

\mathcal{S}_{SU_{q}(2)\widehat{\star}SU_{q}(2)}(\tfrac{1}{z})=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{0}|S_{Y}(-n)|z^{n}=\tfrac{z^{2}(1+z)}{z^{3}-(2q^{-2}+3+2q^{2})z^{2}+2(q^{-2}+1+q^{2})z-2}.

From the convergence criterion for Laurent series we see that
\omega_{Y}(R(SU_{q}(2)\widehat{\star}SU_{q}(2)))=\lim_{n\to\infty}|S_{Y}(n)|^{1/n}
is equal to the largest absolute value of a root of the polynomial
P_{q}

(c.f. (
7.4
)). Finally Pringsheim’s theorem
[
FS09
, Theorem IV.3, p.197]
implies that this value is equal to
r_{q}
, which was defined to be the largest real root of
P_{q}
.

∎

Remark 7.8
.

The proof above follows the logic of
[
BV09
]
, but avoids the usage of the notion of the
free version
. One could also provide the proof using a monoidal equivalence result
[
BDRV06
, Corollary 6.3]
and then
[
Ban97
, Théorème 1]
and
[
BV09
, Theorem 3.3]
. In particular when
F=\mathbbm{1}\in GL_{N}(\mathbb{C})
, the calculation of
\omega_{X_{can}}(R(U_{N}^{+}))
recovers the calculation of the exponential growth rate for
A_{u}(N)
in
[
BV09
]
.

Although the last theorem computes the uniform exponential growth rate for
R(U_{F}^{+})
, the form of the answer is not very explicit. Below we present an alternative approach leading to an estimate for the value of the root
r_{q}
appearing above, and in particular recovering the asymptotic behaviour as
q
tends to
0^{+}
(or, in other words, when the quantum dimension of the canonical fundamental representation of
U_{F}^{+}
goes to infinity). For unitary
F\in GL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
we have
q=1
and the root
r_{1}
can be computed explicitly (giving
r_{1}=6.065\dotsc
); hence below we exclude this case.

Proposition 7.9
.

Let
F\in GL_{N}(\mathbb{C})\,(N\geq 2)
be such that
\Tr(F^{*}F)=\Tr((F^{*}F)^{-1})>2
and define
0<q<1
via
q+q^{-1}=\Tr(F^{*}F)
.
We have

2q^{-2}+2+q^{2}\leq\omega(R(U_{F}^{+}))\leq 2q^{-2}+2+q^{2}\,\tfrac{3-2q^{2}}{(1-q^{2})^{2}}.

In particular
\omega(R(U_{F}^{+}))=2q^{-2}+2+\mathcal{O}(q^{2})
as
q\to 0^{+}
.

Proof.

Due to Proposition
7.6
, we need to bound
\omega_{X_{can}}(R(U_{F}^{+}))
. Take
n\in\mathbb{N}
and any element
\gamma\in S_{X_{can}}(n)
. By Lemma
7.4
we can write
\gamma=w^{k_{1}}_{\varepsilon_{1}}\cdots w^{k_{m}}_{\varepsilon_{m}}
with
m,k_{1},\dotsc,k_{m}\geq 1,\varepsilon_{1}\in\{+1,-1\}
,
s(w^{k_{i}}_{\varepsilon_{i}})=t(w^{k_{i+1}}_{\varepsilon_{i+1}})
and
k_{1}+\cdots+k_{m}=n
. The quantum dimension of
\gamma
equals
d(\gamma)=[k_{1}+1]_{q}\cdots[k_{m}+1]_{q}
. Hence

\begin{split}|S_{X_{can}}(n)|&=|\{w^{k_{1}}_{\varepsilon_{1}}\cdots w^{k_{m}}_{\varepsilon_{m}}\mid\varepsilon_{1}\in\{+1,-1\},k_{1}+\cdots+k_{m}=n\}|\\
&=\sum_{\varepsilon_{1}\in\{+1,-1\}}\sum_{m=1}^{n}\sum_{\overset{k_{1},\dotsc,k_{m}\geq 1,}{k_{1}+\cdots+k_{m}=n}}d(w^{k_{1}}_{\varepsilon_{1}}\cdots w^{k_{m}}_{\varepsilon_{m}})^{2}=2\sum_{m=1}^{n}\sum_{\overset{k_{1},\dotsc,k_{m}\geq 1,}{k_{1}+\cdots+k_{m}=n}}[k_{1}+1]_{q}^{2}\cdots[k_{m}+1]_{q}^{2}.\end{split}

Observe that

(7.9)

(1-q^{4})^{m}\tfrac{q^{-(k_{1}+\cdots+k_{m})-m}}{(q^{-1}-q)^{m}}\leq d(\gamma)\leq\tfrac{q^{-(k_{1}+\cdots+k_{m})-m}}{(q^{-1}-q)^{m}}.

Indeed, the upper bound holds as
[k_{i}+1]_{q}=\tfrac{q^{-k_{i}-1}-q^{k_{i}+1}}{q^{-1}-q}\leq\tfrac{q^{-k_{i}-1}}{q^{-1}-q}
and the lower follows from the string of equivalences

[k_{i}+1]_{q}=\tfrac{q^{-k_{i}-1}-q^{k_{i}+1}}{q^{-1}-q}\geq(1-q^{4})\tfrac{q^{-k_{i}-1}}{q^{-1}-q}\;\Leftrightarrow\;q^{4}q^{-k_{i}-1}\geq q^{k_{i}+1}\;\Leftrightarrow\;q^{4}\geq q^{2k_{i}+2};

the last inequality holds, as
k_{i}\geq 1
.

Using (
7.9
) we first establish an upper bound on
|S_{X_{can}}(n)|
:

\begin{split}|S_{X_{can}}(n)|&\leq 2\sum_{m=1}^{n}\sum_{\overset{k_{1},\dotsc,k_{m}\geq 1,}{k_{1}+\cdots+k_{m}=n}}\tfrac{q^{-2(k_{1}+\cdots+k_{m})-2m}}{(q^{-1}-q)^{2m}}=2q^{-2n}\sum_{m=1}^{n}\tfrac{q^{-2m}}{(q^{-1}-q)^{2m}}\sum_{\overset{k_{1},\dotsc,k_{m}\geq 1,}{k_{1}+\cdots+k_{m}=n}}1\\
&=2q^{-2n}\sum_{m=1}^{n}\tfrac{q^{-2m}}{(q^{-1}-q)^{2m}}{n-1\choose m-1}=2q^{-2n}\tfrac{q^{-2}}{(q^{-1}-q)^{2}}\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}\tfrac{q^{-2m}}{(q^{-1}-q)^{2m}}{n-1\choose m}\\
&=2q^{-2n}\tfrac{q^{-2}}{(q^{-1}-q)^{2}}\bigl(1+(\tfrac{q^{-1}}{q^{-1}-q})^{2}\bigr)^{n-1}=2q^{-2n}\tfrac{q^{-2}}{(q^{-1}-q)^{2}}\bigl(1+(\tfrac{1}{1-q^{2}})^{2}\bigr)^{n-1}.\end{split}

Since
q^{-2}\bigl(1+\tfrac{1}{(1-q^{2})^{2}}\bigr)=2q^{-2}+2+q^{2}\,\tfrac{3-2q^{2}}{(1-q^{2})^{2}}
,
the stated upper bound on
\omega_{X_{can}}(R(U_{F}^{+}))
easily follows. A similar computation gives the lower bound:

\begin{split}|S_{X_{can}}(n)|&\geq 2\sum_{m=1}^{n}\sum_{\overset{k_{1},\dotsc,k_{m}\geq 1,}{k_{1}+\cdots+k_{m}=n}}(1-q^{4})^{2m}\tfrac{q^{-2(k_{1}+\cdots+k_{m})-2m}}{(q^{-1}-q)^{2m}}\\
&=2q^{-2n}\sum_{m=1}^{n}(1-q^{4})^{2m}\tfrac{q^{-2m}}{(q^{-1}-q)^{2m}}{n-1\choose m-1}\\
&=2q^{-2n}(1-q^{4})^{2}\tfrac{q^{-2}}{(q^{-1}-q)^{2}}\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}(1-q^{4})^{2m}\tfrac{q^{-2m}}{(q^{-1}-q)^{2m}}{n-1\choose m}\\
&=2q^{-2n}(1-q^{4})^{2}\tfrac{q^{-2}}{(q^{-1}-q)^{2}}\bigl(1+\tfrac{(1-q^{4})^{2}}{(1-q^{2})^{2}}\bigr)^{n-1}\\
&=2q^{-2n}(1-q^{4})^{2}\tfrac{q^{-2}}{(q^{-1}-q)^{2}}\bigl(2+2q^{2}+q^{4}\bigr)^{n-1}.\end{split}

The stated asymptotics follows now easily (both the upper and lower estimate are of the form
2q^{-2}+2+\mathcal{O}(q^{2})
).
∎

Note that the asymptotics of
r_{q}
could be also deduced from a straightforward, but rather tedious computation based on Theorem
7.7
.

Remark 7.10
.

Propositions
6.5
,
7.9
(and their proofs) show qualitatively different behaviour between the representation theory of quantum groups
U_{F}^{+}
and
G_{q}
. Loosely speaking, while in the case of
G_{q}
asymptotically the size of the sphere can be attributed to a single irreducible representation, dealing with
U_{F}^{+}
we have to consider the contribution of all the representations belonging to the sphere.

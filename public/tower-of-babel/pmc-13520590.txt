Colonic Polyp Regression After Nous-209 Cancer Immunotherapy: A Clinical Trial Case Report
Authors: Marina Crespo-Cruz, Manuel Rodriguez-Justo, Adam Humphries, Andrew Plumb, Anthony Antoniou, Kevin Monahan, Sarah Runswick, Alessia Capone, Laura Antonucci, Irene Garzia, Elisa Micarelli, Elisa Scarselli, Anna Morena D’Alise, Sven Gogov, Kai-Keen Shiu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Immunotherapy is an established treatment for mismatch repair–deficient (dMMR) colorectal cancer (CRC), driven by increased neoantigen formation and enhanced responsiveness to immune checkpoint inhibitors. Nous-209 is a polyvalent viral vector immunotherapy under investigation in combination with pembrolizumab in the NOUS-209-01 clinical trial (ClinicalTrials.gov Identifier: NCT04041310) in patients with dMMR CRC. Here, we report on a participant of the NOUS-209-01 trial who achieved a complete pathologic response and significant regression of colonic polyps following treatment with Nous-209 in combination with pembrolizumab and a right hemicolectomy. Genetic testing ruled out Lynch syndrome and mosaicism of Lynch syndrome–associated genes. The patient remains relapse free and under active surveillance 18 months after treatment initiation. Three months after treatment ended, the patient experienced an episode of grade 3 immune-mediated colitis and this improved after infliximab treatment. The clinical and immunologic findings suggest the potential for Nous-209 immunotherapy, in combination with pembrolizumab, to induce regression of both malignant and premalignant colonic lesions. To our knowledge, this is the first report of an immunotherapy combination to reduce premalignant polyps in a patient who does not have Lynch syndrome.

INTRODUCTION

Mismatch repair deficient (dMMR) colorectal cancers (CRCs) are characterized by the accumulation of insertions and deletions (indels) in microsatellite regions that cause frameshifts and generate aberrant proteins called frameshift peptides (FSPs).[1] FSPs are neoantigens that can induce anti-tumor T-cell responses and increase sensitivity to immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI) immunotherapy.[2]

Based on KEYNOTE-177[3] and Checkmate 8HW[4] trials, immunotherapy with pembrolizumab (anti–PD-1) and nivolumab-ipilimumab (anti–PD-1 and anti–CTLA-4) has been approved for unresectable or metastatic dMMR CRC. There is potential to improve outcomes by stimulating the host T-cell immune response.

Nous-209 is a genetic polyvalent viral vector immunotherapy based on a heterologous prime-boost regimen (GAd20-209-FSP prime; MVA-209-FSP boost) that encodes 209 FSPs.[5] Adenoviral priming followed by MVA boosting is known to induce potent antigen-specific T-cell immunity by efficiently priming naïve T cells and subsequently expanding memory T-cell populations. The mechanism of action is illustrated in Supplemental Figure S1, available online.

In cohort C of the NOUS-209-01 phase 2 randomized, multicohort clinical trial (ClinicalTrials.gov Identifier: NCT04041310), patients with locally advanced or metastatic dMMR CRC are treated with pembrolizumab with or without Nous-209 with the aim of improving the immunotherapeutic response to anti–PD-1 monotherapy by selectively stimulating the host T-cell immune response against neoantigens created by dMMR genes. At the time of trial design, ICI monotherapy was the standard of care in this setting, and the study aim was to improve clinical results while trying to avoid potentially increased toxicity from the combination treatment. We report a case of complete response as well as significant polyp regression in a dMMR CRC receiving Nous-209 and pembrolizumab, with insights into its mechanism of action. To our knowledge, polyp regression with ICI monotherapy has not been reported in the literature in this setting before. These very interesting observations underscore the potential benefits of this combination in reducing premalignant lesions.

We present a case report of a patient enrolled in the NOUS-209-01 clinical trial at University College London Hospitals NHS Foundation Trust in London, UK. We have followed the CARE guidelines to prepare this case report. The NOUS-209-01 clinical trial received approval of the relevant research ethics committee and was conducted according to the Declaration of Helsinki. The patient provided written informed consent for publication of this case.

CASE SUMMARY

A woman in her 40s was diagnosed with a hepatic flexure adenocarcinoma and serrated polyposis syndrome (SPS) at colonoscopy following a positive fecal immunochemical test. Medical history included migraine and polycystic ovarian syndrome. Her father had a history of bowel and prostate cancer, and her mother had Crohn’s disease.

Colonoscopy revealed a strictured, ulcerated hepatic flexure adenocarcinoma. Distal to the tumor, approximately 30 small, 3–8 mm sessile polyps were seen (25 colonic and 5 rectal); most were NBI (narrow band imaging) International Colorectal Endoscopic classification (NICE) type 1 (serrated), and four were NICE type 2 (adenomas). The phenotype was consistent with SPS according to World Health Organization (WHO) criteria. There was a loss of nuclear MLH1 and PMS2 with preserved MSH2/MSH6 by immunohistochemistry. We performed somatic analysis and identified two variants in MLH1, c.588 + 2T>G and c.638del with 9% and 6% variant allele frequency (VAF), respectively, which were not detected in the germline blood or adjacent healthy colonic tissue. Further genetic testing excluded MLH1 promoter methylation. Targeted long-range polymerase chain reaction (PCR), Sanger sequencing, and multiplex ligation-dependent probe amplification (MLPA) detected no pathogenic PMS2 variants. MLH1 and PMS2 mutations were deemed somatic, and mosaicism of genes associated with Lynch syndrome was excluded via examination of healthy resection tissue. Additional genomic profiling using Guardant360 Response assay of circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA) reported a germline mutation in BRCA2 (p.Pro655Arg) confirmed by next generation sequencing (NGS) data of normal mucosal tissue.[6]

It was clinically staged as T3N2M1 (TNM 8th edition), with concern raised at the multidisciplinary meeting (MDT) of nonregional lymphadenopathy. The tumor showed evidence of pericolonic stranding and was in close apposition to the undersurface of the liver, gallbladder, distal stomach, and proximal duodenum but without direct contact. The consensus opinion of the MDT was that the lesion was inoperable owing to bulky locoregional and distant lymphadenopathy near the superior mesenteric venous vasculature. The hepatic flexure lesion was impassable at colonoscopy, and the patient proceeded to a laparoscopic defunctioning ileostomy.

She was then enrolled in the NOUS-209-01 trial and randomly assigned to receive Nous-209 plus pembrolizumab (200-mg Q3W) (see Fig. S2 for timeline). Following her first dose of pembrolizumab and the Nous-209 prime (GAd20-209-FSP), she was admitted with abdominal pain requiring hospitalization. A computed tomography (CT) scan 2 days later showed increased inflammation of the hepatic flexure tumor and a more necrotic mesenteric adenopathy suggestive of very early tumor response. This was considered an immune-related adverse event (AE). Concurrent AEs included grade 1 chills, arthralgia, rigor, fever, lower back pain, and a grade 1 injection-site reaction (GAd20-209-FSP related). Within 24 hours the abdominal pain resolved, and she was discharged. She had no further side effects on subsequent cycles.

Following two further pembrolizumab and three Nous 209 MVA-209-FSP booster doses, a CT scan at week 8 showed a good partial response (PR) with a 35% reduction in the main target lesion (mesenteric lymph node, from 17 to 11 mm short axis) (Fig. 1A). After three further pembrolizumab doses, a CT showed complete resolution of nontarget lesions.

A CT scan at 6 months (week 25) of treatment showed a PR with reduction of the target lesion by 53% (to 8 mm short axis) and the colonic primary was no longer perceptible (Fig. 1A). Repeated colonoscopy showed no residual colonic tumor and only two NICE type 1 polyps remained of those seen at diagnosis (Fig. 1B).

At patient’s request, she was considered for an ileostomy reversal. Owing to tumor stenosis following immunotherapy, this was not possible and surgical decision was to proceed to a right hemicolectomy with complete mesocolic excision and an ileotransverse anastomosis. Histopathology showed a pathologic complete response: ypT0 N0 (0/42), with no evidence of venous, lymphatic, or perineural invasion. A postoperative CT at 34 weeks excluded disease recurrence. She restarted pembrolizumab as per trial protocol and received four further doses. After 3 months a CT scan (week 45) confirmed no recurrence. The patient subsequently chose to stop pembrolizumab but remained on active trial follow-up including sequential blood samples and peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) for immune analysis.

Three months later, she developed grade 3 colitis, unresponsive to intravenous (IV) methylprednisolone. An urgent endoscopy showed moderate-severe colitis with only a single polyp in the sigmoid colon. Following two cycles of infliximab, the colitis resolved to grade 1. The patient remains cancer free on active surveillance.

Presence of NOUS-209 FSPs in Resected Tumor and Induction of Immune Response

We sought to explain the potential mechanisms of action of the very good clinical and polyp response in this patient. Exome NGS of resected tumor indicated that 67 of 209 FSPs used to generate Nous-209 were present at baseline[7] (Table S1). Neoantigen-specific T-cell response was evaluated at week 43 (10 months) and week 55 (1 year) post initiation of treatment with Nous-209 by interferon γ (IFN-γ) ex vivo enzyme-linked immunosorbent spot (ELISpot) on PBMCs stimulated with 16 peptide pools covering the Nous-209 sequence, according to previously published methods[8] (Fig. 2A). The patient had a robust immune response directed against different peptide pools, indicative of a broad polytopic response to the vaccine-targeted neoantigens (Figs. 2B, C). The T-cell response was still detectable at week 55. Deconvolution of T-cell responses within the most immunoreactive pools allowed the identification of some individual immunogenic FSPs (Fig. 2D). To determine the T-cell subset responsible for neoantigen-specific responses, selective depletion of CD8+ T cells was performed prior to ex vivo IFN-γ ELISpot. In four of the five FSPs tested, depletion of CD8+ T cells led to a significative reduction of IFN-γ production, confirming that these responses were CD8+ T-cell mediated. The fifth FSP retained reactivity after CD8+ depletion, indicating a CD4+ T-cell–driven response. These results demonstrate that Nous-209 induces both CD8+ and CD4+ neoantigen-specific T-cell responses (Fig. 2D), in line with the previous findings.

Complete deconvolution of T-cell responses and identification of all FSPs was not possible. However, it is noteworthy that immune responses against six FSPs present in the baseline tumor were detected by IFNγ ELISpot post in vitro restimulation, supporting the proposed Nous-209 mechanism of action of targeting the tumor (Fig. 2E).

Further, anti-GAd neutralizing antibodies (nAbs) were measured at baseline then at 6, 34, and 37 weeks post GAd20-209-FSP administration.[9] Anti-GAd nAbs showed a peak titer at 6 weeks post treatment, indicative of effective GAd20-209–FSP uptake, which could still be detected at 37 weeks (Fig. 2F). Anti-GAd nAbs reflect vaccine uptake and indicate that the vaccine has been recognized by the immune system, providing an indirect measure of the immune response occurring in this patient. Detection of ctDNA was assessed at baseline and week 34 by the Guardant360 Response assay, which showed 100% reduction in ctDNA levels, represented by the molecular response (MR) score confirming no residual or recurrent disease (Fig. 2G).[10]

DISCUSSION

We report a case of complete CRC response and significant regression of colonic polyps in a patient with SPS after treatment with pembrolizumab and Nous-209 immunotherapy. Yu et al[11] reported a retrospective review of 75 patients with Lynch syndrome and CRC who had received at least two cycles of ICI immunotherapy with the disappearance of up to seven adenomas. Harrold et al[12] followed up patients with Lynch syndrome after ICI exposure and found that up to 39% of patients still had premalignant polyps on surveillance endoscopies, and up to 12% developed subsequent malignancies, underscoring the usual lack of activity of ICIs on premalignant lesions.

We believe this to be the first report of colonic polyp reduction with immune-modulating agents in a patient with dMMR CRC, without Lynch syndrome. Our patient showed a robust polytopic immune response to Nous-209 treatment–targeted neoantigens; the results obtained could also reflect a response against FSPs encoded by early mutations in colonic premalignant polyps, resulting in polyp regression.[13]

The patient developed grade 3 colitis, which was considered to be related to study treatment, as both agents were administered in combination and attribution to a single agent is not possible. Grade 3 or 4 colitis was reported in 3% of patients in the KEYNOTE-177 trial.[3] Although pembrolizumab had been stopped several months before onset, late-onset immune-mediated colitis is a recognized adverse event associated with anti–PD-1 therapy. NOUS-209 had been discontinued nearly a year earlier, and colitis has not been reported with NOUS-209 monotherapy to date. This AE was managed according to institutional guidelines.

CONCLUSION

Nous-209 immunotherapy demonstrated marked clinical benefit as costimulator to pembrolizumab, with our patient having a complete clinical response and significant reduction in the number of benign polyps. Nous-209 might also enhance checkpoint inhibitor immunotherapy effects on premalignant lesions, which have not previously been clearly proved in monotherapy in this setting. Nous-209 in combination with anti–PD-1 immunotherapy may not only represent a promising treatment for patients with dMMR CRC but also have the additional benefit of eliminating premalignant polyps. It might also provide protection against the development of future polyps through upregulation of the patient’s immune response. This may reduce the risk of CRC relapse as well as new polyp formation, representing a new immunoprevention strategy for patients with dMMR cancers and patients with polyposis syndromes. Although these findings are encouraging, they come from a single-patient (n = 1) case report, so broader conclusions should be made cautiously. Further translational analyses and clinical studies are needed to fully elucidate the mechanism of action of these interesting observations.

Abstract
We study dependence measures induced by Φ \Phi -divergence (also known as f f -divergence in the literature) for finite random variables. We define conditional Φ \Phi -mutual information through an exact chain rule and develop its structural properties and variational representations. But in general it lacks conditional symmetry and deterministic self-reduction. To tackle this, we introduce max- Φ \Phi -mutual information I ~ Φ \widetilde{I}_{\Phi} , a variational envelope obtained by optimizing over I Φ I_{\Phi} quantities containing auxiliary random variables, which restores both properties and satisfies a subadditive chain rule. Both quantities reduce to Shannon’s mutual information when Φ ⁡ ( t ) = t ​ log ⁡ t \Phi(t)=t\log t . For smooth convex generators with Φ ⁡ ( 0 ) < ∞ \Phi(0)<\infty and concave t ↦ t 2 ​ Φ ′′ ​ ( t ) t\mapsto t^{2}\Phi^{\prime\prime}(t) , we establish an optimizer characterization and obtain a closed-form expression for the unconditional quantity in terms of Φ \Phi -self-information. For the same class, we show that its private-randomness-assisted version is either an affine function of Shannon’s mutual information, with coefficients depending only on Φ \Phi , or identically infinite. This dichotomy is determined by the asymptotic growth of Φ \Phi and clarifies the sensitivity of max- Φ \Phi -mutual information to local randomness. We further derive inner bounds on Φ \Phi -ribbons from prescribed independence structures and prove that these bounds are optimal when Φ ⁡ ( t ) = t ​ log ⁡ t \Phi(t)=t\log t . We also establish a multipartite Φ \Phi -generalization of the Zhang–Yeung inequality and derive corresponding ribbon regions. Introduction

Shannon’s mutual information plays several roles simultaneously. It is
the Kullback–Leibler (KL) divergence between a joint distribution and the
product of its marginals, it admits an exact chain rule, it
characterizes conditional independence, and it satisfies
data processing. These structural
features make mutual information useful as a tool for manipulating information inequalities.

A natural question is how much of this structure survives when the
KL divergence is replaced by a general

\Phi
-divergence (commonly called
f
-divergence). The
\Phi
-divergence was originally proposed by Ali and Silvey
[
3
]
as well as Csiszár
[
4

5

and defined as

\displaystyle D_{\Phi}(q_{X}\|p_{X})

\displaystyle\triangleq\sum_{x}p(x)\Phi\left(\frac{q(x)}{p(x)}\right)-\Phi(1),

where
\Phi:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
is convex.
Here we do not impose the usual normalization
\Phi(1)=0
. When
\Phi(t)=t\log t
, the
\Phi
-divergence recovers the KL divergence
D(q_{X}\|p_{X})
. Throughout, all random variables take values in finite sets.

Following
[
6
]
and
[
7
, Eq. 3.10.1]
2
2

2

For a broader generalization, refer to
[
8
]
.
, we define the
\Phi
-mutual information
by

\displaystyle I_{\Phi}(A;B)\triangleq D_{\Phi}(p_{AB}\|p_{A}\otimes p_{B}).

(1)

The above definition of
I_{\Phi}(X;Y)
has been extensively analyzed in various works, such as
[
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
]

This quantity is symmetric
I_{\Phi}(X;Y)=I_{\Phi}(Y;X)
, nonnegative
I_{\Phi}(X;Y)\geq 0
, satisfies the data processing inequality
I_{\Phi}(X;YZ)\geq I_{\Phi}(X;Z)
, and is naturally related to the
\Phi
-entropy. When
\Phi(t)=t\log t
, the
\Phi
-mutual information recovers Shannon’s mutual information
I(X;Y)
.

In this paper, we define the conditional
\Phi
-mutual information as

\displaystyle I_{\Phi}(X;Y|Z)\triangleq I_{\Phi}(X;YZ)-I_{\Phi}(X;Z),

(2)

when
I_{\Phi}(X;YZ),I_{\Phi}(X;Z)<\infty
.
To the best of our knowledge, this definition and its associated calculus have not been systematically studied.

This definition preserves the exact chain rule on the second coordinate, i.e.,

I_{\Phi}(X;YZ)=I_{\Phi}(X;Y|Z)+I_{\Phi}(X;Z),

and is therefore
suitable for telescoping and information-inequality arguments. Additionally, definition (

I_{\Phi}
as a
\Phi

6

which is symmetric. However,

I_{\Phi}(X;Y|Z)
is
not generally

\displaystyle I_{\Phi}(X;Y|Z)\neq I_{\Phi}(Y;X|Z),

and does not generally admit the averaging

I_{\Phi}(X;Y|Z)\neq\sum_{z}p(z)I_{\Phi}(X;Y|Z=z).

Moreover, it fails

on the second coordinate, i.e., when
Y=g(X,Z)

I_{\Phi}(X;Y|Z)\neq I_{\Phi}(Y;Y|Z)

We introduce a variational envelope, denoted as
\widetilde{I}_{\Phi}

max-
\Phi
-mutual information
by

\widetilde{I}_{\Phi}(X;Y)\triangleq\sup_{U}\left\{I_{\Phi}(U;X)+I_{\Phi}(U;Y)-I_{\Phi}(U;XY)\right\},

where the supremum is over all conditional distributions

p_{U|XY}
. Its conditional counterpart is defined analogously.
Compared to
I_{\Phi}

\widetilde{I}_{\Phi}
is conditionally symmetric

\displaystyle\widetilde{I}_{\Phi}(X;Y|Z)=\widetilde{I}_{\Phi}(Y;X|Z),

and
satisfies conditional deterministic self-reduction, although its chain rule is only subadditive. Observe that when
\Phi(t)=t\log t

\widetilde{I}_{\Phi}(X;Y|Z)=I_{\Phi}(X;Y|Z)=I(X;Y|Z)
recovering Shannon’s conditional mutual information.

Our contributions fall into three themes.

1.

Conditional calculus of
I_{\Phi}
.

We develop a calculus for
I_{\Phi}
, establishing self-information bounds, invariance properties, monotonicity, and subadditivity under suitable assumptions on
\Phi
. Conditional
I_{\Phi}
preserves an exact chain rule, but does not satisfy conditional symmetry and deterministic self-reduction. For
\Phi\in\mathscr{F}
, we also derive a direct variational representation of conditional
I_{\Phi}
that can be useful in machine learning. We also characterize the exceptional role of Shannon’s mutual information: under the regularity assumptions of Theorem
1
, requiring either conditional symmetry or conditional deterministic self-reduction forces
I_{\Phi}
to be a positive multiple of Shannon’s mutual information.

2.

Max-information
\widetilde{I}_{\Phi}
and private-randomness-assisted
\widehat{I}_{\Phi}

We establish structural properties of the max-
\Phi
-mutual information
\widetilde{I}_{\Phi}
and characterize

\Phi\in\mathscr{G}
and
\Phi(0)<\infty

where

In particular, for

\widetilde{I}_{\Phi}(X;Y)=I_{\Phi}(X;X)+I_{\Phi}(Y;Y)-I_{\Phi}(XY;XY).

Under the same assumptions, we characterize the effect of

\widehat{I}_{\Phi}(X;Y)
for the supremum of
\widetilde{I}_{\Phi}
over such

\widehat{I}_{\Phi}(X;Y)=c_{\Phi}(\ln 2)I(X;Y)+K_{\Phi},

c_{\Phi},K_{\Phi}\geq 0
depend only on
\Phi
, or

\widehat{I}_{\Phi}
is identically infinite.
The asymptotic growth of

3.

Ribbon inequalities and strong data processing.

For
\Phi\in\mathscr{F}

the
\Phi
-ribbon given by the convex hull of the incidence
vectors of mutually independent subsets.
For the HC ribbon, this bound is optimal among those determined
solely by the prescribed independence relations.
We also establish a multipartite
\Phi
-generalization of the
Zhang–Yeung inequality involving
I_{\Phi}
and
\widetilde{I}_{\Phi}

which yields additional ribbon regions from conditional
independence relations. In a concurrent work, this result can be generalized to the matrix case
[
13
]

For Z-channel sources, we give sufficient conditions on
\Phi

under which the optimization defining the
\Phi
-SDPI constant
can be restricted to functions satisfying

when

m
, then

I_{\Phi}(A;A)=\frac{1}{m}\Phi(m)+\left(1-\frac{1}{m}\right)\Phi(0)-\Phi(1).

(17)

II-C
1
Optimization on a fixed alphabet

For a fixed finite alphabet
\mathcal{X}
, consider

\sup_{p_{X}}I_{\Phi}(X;X),

(18)

where the supremum is over all probability distributions on
\mathcal{X}
.
To express this problem in separable form, define

h_{\Phi}(p)\triangleq\begin{cases}p^{2}\left[\Phi\left(\dfrac{1}{p}\right)-\Phi(0)\right],&p\in(0,1],\\[4.30554pt]
0,&p=0.\end{cases}

(19)

Equation (
16
) becomes

I_{\Phi}(X;X)=\Phi(0)-\Phi(1)+\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}h_{\Phi}(p_{X}(x)).

(20)

Thus (
18
) is equivalent to

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}p(x)\geq 0,\ x\in\mathcal{X}\\
\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}p(x)=1\end{subarray}}\left\{\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}h_{\Phi}(p(x))+\Phi(0)-\Phi(1)\right\}.

(21)

It is also useful to define

G_{\Phi}(t)\triangleq\frac{\Phi(t)-\Phi(0)}{t},\qquad t>0.

(22)

Since

h_{\Phi}(p)=pG_{\Phi}\left(\frac{1}{p}\right),\qquad p\in(0,1],

(23)

we obtain the alternative representation

I_{\Phi}(X;X)=\sum_{x:p_{X}(x)>0}p_{X}(x)G_{\Phi}\left(\frac{1}{p_{X}(x)}\right)+\Phi(0)-\Phi(1).

(24)

The following result of Zvárová gives a sufficient condition
for the uniform distribution to be optimal.

Theorem 2

(
[
20
, Theorem 4]
)
.

Suppose that
\Phi:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
is convex and

\Phi(0)<\infty
. If
G_{\Phi}
is concave on
(0,\infty)
, then,
for every finite alphabet
\mathcal{X}
,

\displaystyle I_{\Phi}(X;X)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{|\mathcal{X}|}\Phi(|\mathcal{X}|)+\left(1-\frac{1}{|\mathcal{X}|}\right)\Phi(0)-\Phi(1).

(25)

Equality holds when
X
is uniformly distributed on
\mathcal{X}
.

To study (
21
) without assuming concavity
of
G_{\Phi}
, we first examine the objective near the boundary of
the probability simplex. For
p>0
,

h_{\Phi}(p)=\frac{\Phi(1/p)-\Phi(0)}{(1/p)^{2}}.

(26)

Thus its behavior as
p\downarrow 0
depends on the growth of
\Phi

relative to
t^{2}
.

If
\Phi(t)=\omega(t^{2})
as
t\to\infty
, then

h_{\Phi}(p)\to+\infty
as
p\downarrow 0
. Consequently, for every
alphabet with
|\mathcal{X}|\geq 2
,

\sup_{p_{X}}I_{\Phi}(X;X)=+\infty.

If instead
\Phi(t)=Ct^{2}+o(t^{2})
for some
C>0
, then

h_{\Phi}(p)\to C
as
p\downarrow 0
, whereas
h_{\Phi}(0)=0
.
For example, if
Z_{p}\sim\operatorname{Bern}(p)
, then

\lim_{p\downarrow 0}I_{\Phi}(Z_{p};Z_{p})=C\neq 0=I_{\Phi}(Z_{0};Z_{0}).

Hence self-information can be discontinuous as probabilities vanish.

We therefore focus on the subquadratic regime

\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\Phi(t)}{t^{2}}=0.

(27)

In this regime,
h_{\Phi}(p)\to 0=h_{\Phi}(0)
as
p\downarrow 0
.
The objective in (
21
) is therefore continuous
on the probability simplex, which ensures that a maximizer exists.
The next theorem also shows that every maximizer has full support.

Theorem 3
.

Suppose that
\Phi:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
is convex, non-affine,
differentiable on
(0,\infty)
, and satisfies
\Phi(0)<\infty
and
\lim_{t\to\infty}\Phi(t)/t^{2}=0
.
For every finite alphabet
\mathcal{X}
with
|\mathcal{X}|\geq 2
,
the optimization problem (
21
) admits a
maximizer, and every maximizer
p_{X}^{\ast}
lies in the interior of
the probability simplex.

Moreover, there exists
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
such that for every
x\in\mathcal{X}
,

h_{\Phi}^{\prime}(p_{X}^{\ast}(x))=\lambda.

(28)

Equivalently, for every
x\in\mathcal{X}
,

\displaystyle 2p_{X}^{\ast}(x)\left[\Phi\left(\frac{1}{p_{X}^{\ast}(x)}\right)-\Phi(0)\right]-\Phi^{\prime}\left(\frac{1}{p_{X}^{\ast}(x)}\right)=\lambda.

(29)

The proof is given in
Appendix
E
.

Corollary 4
.

Under the assumptions of
Theorem
3
, the following hold:

1.

If
h_{\Phi}^{\prime}
is nonincreasing on
(0,1]
, then the uniform
distribution maximizes (
21
).

2.

If
h_{\Phi}^{\prime}
is injective on
(0,1]
, then the uniform
distribution is the unique maximizer.

Proof.

If
h_{\Phi}^{\prime}
is nonincreasing, then
h_{\Phi}
is concave on
[0,1]
.
Writing
n=|\mathcal{X}|
, Jensen’s inequality gives

\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}h_{\Phi}(p_{X}(x))\leq nh_{\Phi}\left(\frac{1}{n}\right).

The uniform distribution therefore maximizes
(
21
).

If
h_{\Phi}^{\prime}
is injective, then
(
28
) forces every maximizer
to assign the same probability to each
x\in\mathcal{X}
.
Existence of a maximizer then implies that the uniform distribution
is the unique maximizer.
∎

The connection with Theorem
2
follows from

h_{\Phi}^{\prime\prime}(p)=\frac{1}{p^{3}}G_{\Phi}^{\prime\prime}(1/p),\qquad p\in(0,1),

whenever
\Phi
is twice differentiable. Thus concavity of
G_{\Phi}

implies concavity of
h_{\Phi}
, and the first assertion of
Corollary
4
recovers uniform optimality
under the assumptions of
Theorem
3
.

II-C
2
Optimization over all finite alphabets

We next allow the alphabet size to vary. The resulting supremum
will be useful in Section
III-C
.

By convexity of
\Phi
, for every
0<s<t
,

\frac{\Phi(s)-\Phi(0)}{s}\leq\frac{\Phi(t)-\Phi(0)}{t}.

Hence
G_{\Phi}
is nondecreasing, and the limit

s_{\Phi}\triangleq\lim_{t\to\infty}G_{\Phi}(t)=\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\Phi(t)-\Phi(0)}{t}\in\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}

(30)

exists. This asymptotic slope determines the exact supremum.

Theorem 5
.

Suppose that
\Phi:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
is convex and

\Phi(0)<\infty
. Then

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}X:\,|\operatorname{supp}(X)|<\infty\end{subarray}}I_{\Phi}(X;X)=s_{\Phi}+\Phi(0)-\Phi(1),

(31)

where the equality is understood in the extended real sense.
Moreover, if
X_{n}
is uniformly distributed on
[n]
, then

\lim_{n\to\infty}I_{\Phi}(X_{n};X_{n})=s_{\Phi}+\Phi(0)-\Phi(1).

(32)

Proof.

Since
G_{\Phi}(t)\leq s_{\Phi}
for every
t>0
,
(
24
) yields

I_{\Phi}(X;X)\leq s_{\Phi}+\Phi(0)-\Phi(1)

(33)

for every finitely supported random variable
X
.

On the other hand, if
X_{n}
is uniform on
[n]
, then
(
17
) gives

I_{\Phi}(X_{n};X_{n})=G_{\Phi}(n)+\Phi(0)-\Phi(1).

Letting
n\to\infty
proves
(
32
) and shows that the
upper bound in (
33
) is
approached by uniform distributions.
∎

Remark 1
.

If
s_{\Phi}<\infty
, then
G_{\Phi}
is bounded on
[1,\infty)
, so

\Phi(t)=\Phi(0)+tG_{\Phi}(t)=O(t)\qquad\text{as }t\to\infty.

Thus a finite supremum over all finite alphabets implies the
subquadratic growth condition
(
27
).

Lemma 7
.

Suppose
\Phi\in\mathscr{G}
and its domain contains
(0,\infty)
.

t\mapsto t\Phi^{\prime\prime}(t)

\displaystyle c_{\Phi}

\displaystyle\triangleq\lim_{t\to\infty}t\Phi^{\prime\prime}(t)=\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\Phi^{\prime}(t)}{1+\ln t}=\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\Phi(t)}{t\ln t}\in[0,\infty).

Lemma 8
.

Suppose
\Phi\in\mathscr{G}
. Let
\nu_{Z},\nu_{Y|XZ},\mu_{XYZ}
be fixed. Then

\displaystyle\nu_{X|Z}\mapsto D_{\Phi}(\nu_{XZ}\|\mu_{XZ})-D_{\Phi}(\nu_{XYZ}\|\mu_{XYZ})

is convex on the set
\{\nu_{X|Z}:\nu_{XYZ}\ll\mu_{XYZ}\}
.

Proof.

Note that

\displaystyle D_{\Phi}(\nu_{XZ}\|\mu_{XZ})-D_{\Phi}(\nu_{XYZ}\|\mu_{XYZ})

\displaystyle=\sum_{x,z}\mu(x,z)\Phi\left(\frac{\nu(x,z)}{\mu(x,z)}\right)-\sum_{x,y,z}\mu(x,y,z)\Phi\left(\frac{\nu(x,y,z)}{\mu(x,y,z)}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x,z}\mu(x,z)\left(\Phi\left(\frac{\nu(x,z)}{\mu(x,z)}\right)-\sum_{y}\mu(y|x,z)\Phi\left(\frac{\nu(z)\nu(x|z)\nu(y|x,z)}{\mu(x,y,z)}\right)\right).

Fixing
x,z
, we will prove the mapping

\displaystyle\nu(x|z)\mapsto\Phi\left(\frac{\nu(z)\nu(x|z)}{\mu(x,z)}\right)-\sum_{y}\mu(y|x,z)\Phi\left(\frac{\nu(z)\nu(x|z)\nu(y|x,z)}{\mu(x,y,z)}\right)

(37)

is convex. Differentiating twice with respect to
\nu(x|z)
, the second derivative is equal to

\displaystyle\frac{\nu(z)^{2}}{\mu(x,z)^{2}}\Phi^{\prime\prime}\left(\frac{\nu(z)\nu(x|z)}{\mu(x,z)}\right)-\sum_{y}\mu(y|x,z)\frac{\nu(z)^{2}\nu(y|x,z)^{2}}{\mu(x,y,z)^{2}}\Phi^{\prime\prime}\left(\frac{\nu(z)\nu(x|z)\nu(y|x,z)}{\mu(x,y,z)}\right).

(38)

Since
\Phi\in\mathscr{G}
,
t\mapsto t^{2}\Phi^{\prime\prime}(t)
is concave. Considering
t=\frac{\nu(y|x,z)}{\mu(x,y,z)}
, by Jensen’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{y}\mu(y|x,z)\frac{\nu(z)^{2}\nu(y|x,z)^{2}}{\mu(x,y,z)^{2}}\Phi^{\prime\prime}\left(\frac{\nu(z)\nu(x|z)\nu(y|x,z)}{\mu(x,y,z)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\nu(z)^{2}\left(\sum_{y}\mu(y|x,z)\frac{\nu(y|x,z)}{\mu(x,y,z)}\right)^{2}\Phi^{\prime\prime}\left(\sum_{y}\mu(y|x,z)\frac{\nu(z)\nu(x|z)\nu(y|x,z)}{\mu(x,y,z)}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\nu(z)^{2}}{\mu(x,z)^{2}}\Phi^{\prime\prime}\left(\frac{\nu(z)\nu(x|z)}{\mu(x,z)}\right).

Thus (
38
) is nonnegative and then (
37
) is convex. Since it holds for any
x,z
,

\displaystyle\nu_{X|Z}\mapsto D_{\Phi}(\nu_{XZ}\|\mu_{XZ})-D_{\Phi}(\nu_{XYZ}\|\mu_{XYZ})

is convex.
∎

By Lemma

, if

\Phi(0)<\infty

are in the form

\displaystyle\Phi(t)=At\log t+Bt+C

where

II-E

Supremum representations of conditional
\Phi
-mutual information

We derive a direct variational representation of conditional

\Phi
-mutual information. Each admissible
function in this representation yields a lower bound on

I_{\Phi}(X;Y|Z)
.

We first recall the unconditional representation. Let

\Phi^{*}(t)\triangleq\sup_{u\geq 0}\{ut-\Phi(u)\},\qquad t\in\mathbb{R},

be the Fenchel conjugate of
\Phi
. For probability distributions

P\ll Q
on a common finite alphabet, Fenchel duality gives

D_{\Phi}(P\|Q)=\sup_{T}\left\{\mathbb{E}_{P}[T]-\mathbb{E}_{Q}[\Phi^{*}(T)]\right\}-\Phi(1),

where the supremum is over all real-valued functions on that
alphabet. Taking
P=p_{XY}
and
Q=p_{X}\otimes p_{Y}
yields

I_{\Phi}(X;Y)=\sup_{T}\left\{\mathbb{E}_{p_{XY}}[T(X,Y)]-\mathbb{E}_{p_{X}\otimes p_{Y}}[\Phi^{*}(T(X,Y))]\right\}-\Phi(1).

(39)

Restricting
T
to a parametrized family produces a variational
lower bound. Such objectives motivate neural estimators of mutual
information
[
25
]
. The lower-bound guarantee
applies to the population objective; replacing expectations by
sample averages need not preserve it.

Our conditional quantity is defined by

I_{\Phi}(X;Y|Z)=I_{\Phi}(X;YZ)-I_{\Phi}(X;Z).

Applying (
39
) separately to the two terms does
not provide a lower bound on their difference. Moreover,
subtracting approximate values can lead to large relative errors
when the two terms are close. We therefore seek a direct
variational representation of
I_{\Phi}(X;Y|Z)
. Our approach
expresses it as a conditional Jensen gap and uses the convexity
of this gap when
\Phi\in\mathscr{F}
.

Set
P_{XYZ}\triangleq p_{XYZ}
,
Q_{XYZ}\triangleq p_{X}\otimes p_{YZ}

and define the likelihood ratio

L(x,y,z)\triangleq\frac{P(x,y,z)}{Q(x,y,z)}=\frac{p(x,y,z)}{p_{X}(x)p_{YZ}(y,z)}

on the support of
Q
. Its values outside that support are
irrelevant. Since

\mathbb{E}_{Q}[L|X=x,Z=z]=\frac{p_{XZ}(x,z)}{p_{X}(x)p_{Z}(z)},

we obtain

I_{\Phi}(X;Y|Z)=\mathbb{E}_{Q}[\Phi(L)]-\mathbb{E}_{Q}\left[\Phi\left(\mathbb{E}_{Q}[L|X,Z]\right)\right].

(40)

More generally, for a fixed distribution
Q
on a finite product
alphabet and a nonnegative function
f=f(X,Y,Z)
, define

\mathcal{J}_{\Phi,Q}(f;X,Z)\triangleq\mathbb{E}_{Q}[\Phi(f)]-\mathbb{E}_{Q}\left[\Phi\left(\mathbb{E}_{Q}[f|X,Z]\right)\right].

(41)

Thus

I_{\Phi}(X;Y|Z)=\mathcal{J}_{\Phi,Q}(L;X,Z).

For
\Phi\in\mathscr{F}
defined on
(0,\infty)
, we use its
finite continuous extension to zero.

The following two standard facts provide the ingredients for
the variational representation.

Lemma 9

(
[
26
, Lemma 32]
)
.

Let
Q
be a fixed probability distribution on a finite product
alphabet. Suppose that
\Phi\in\mathscr{F}
and that its domain
contains
(0,\infty)
. Then
f\mapsto\mathcal{J}_{\Phi,Q}(f;X,Z)

is convex on the set of nonnegative functions

f:\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}\times\mathcal{Z}\to[0,\infty)
.

Lemma 10

(
[
27
, Proposition 16.10]
)
.

Suppose that
\Phi:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
is convex and differentiable,
with

\Phi(0)\triangleq\lim_{t\downarrow 0}\Phi(t)
. Then for every
u>0
,
\Phi^{*}(\Phi^{\prime}(u))=u\Phi^{\prime}(u)-\Phi(u)
.

For a fixed probability distribution
Q_{XYZ}
and a positive function
g=g(X,Y,Z)
, write

g_{XZ}\triangleq\mathbb{E}_{Q}[g|X,Z].

Theorem 11
.

Let
Q
be a fixed probability distribution and
\Phi\in\mathscr{F}
. Then, for every nonnegative function
f
,

\mathcal{J}_{\Phi,Q}(f;X,Z)=\sup_{g>0}\left\{\mathbb{E}_{Q}\left[f\bigl(\Phi^{\prime}(g)-\Phi^{\prime}(g_{XZ})\bigr)\right]-\mathbb{E}_{Q}\left[\Phi^{*}(\Phi^{\prime}(g))-\Phi^{*}(\Phi^{\prime}(g_{XZ}))\right]\right\},

(42)

where
g_{XZ}=\mathbb{E}_{Q}[g|X,Z]
and the supremum is over all positive functions

g:\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}\times\mathcal{Z}\to(0,\infty)
.
If
f>0
, the supremum is attained at
g=f
.
For general nonnegative
f
, it is approached by

g=f+\epsilon
as
\epsilon\downarrow 0
.

The representation follows by taking supporting hyperplanes of the convex functional
\mathcal{J}_{\Phi,Q}
and applying
Lemma
10
. The proof is given in
Appendix
G
.

Applying Theorem
11
to

f=L=P/Q
and using
\mathbb{E}_{Q}[Lh]=\mathbb{E}_{P}[h]
gives the desired
representation.

Corollary 12

(Variational representation of conditional

\Phi
-mutual information)
.

Suppose that
\Phi\in\mathscr{F}
and that its domain contains

(0,\infty)
. Let
P=p_{XYZ}
and
Q=p_{X}\otimes p_{YZ}
. Then

I_{\Phi}(X;Y|Z)=\sup_{g>0}\left\{\mathbb{E}_{P}\left[\Phi^{\prime}(g)-\Phi^{\prime}(g_{XZ})\right]-\mathbb{E}_{Q}\left[\Phi^{*}(\Phi^{\prime}(g))-\Phi^{*}(\Phi^{\prime}(g_{XZ}))\right]\right\},

(43)

where
g_{XZ}=\mathbb{E}_{Q}[g|X,Z]
and the supremum is over all
positive functions on
\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}\times\mathcal{Z}
.

Each positive
g
therefore gives a lower bound on

I_{\Phi}(X;Y|Z)
. Under the product distribution
Q
, the conditional

g_{XZ}(x,z)=\sum_{y}p(y|z)g(x,y,z).

Example 1

(Conditional Donsker–Varadhan representation)
.

For
\Phi(t)=t\ln t
, we have
I_{\Phi}(X;Y|Z)=(\ln 2)I(X;Y|Z)
and

\Phi^{\prime}(t)=1+\ln t,\qquad\Phi^{*}(t)=e^{t-1}.

Consequently,

\Phi^{\prime}(g)-\Phi^{\prime}(g_{XZ})=\ln\frac{g}{g_{XZ}},\qquad\Phi^{*}(\Phi^{\prime}(g))=g.

The second expectation in

\mathbb{E}_{Q}[g-g_{XZ}]=0
. Hence

(\ln 2)I(X;Y|Z)=\sup_{g>0}\mathbb{E}_{P}\left[\ln\frac{g}{g_{XZ}}\right].

(44)

Writing
g=e^{T}
gives

(\ln 2)I(X;Y|Z)=\sup_{T}\mathbb{E}_{P}\left[T(X,Y,Z)-\ln\mathbb{E}_{Q}\left[e^{T(X,Y,Z)}|X,Z\right]\right],

(45)

where the supremum is over all real-valued functions

T:\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}\times\mathcal{Z}\to\mathbb{R}
. Here,

\mathbb{E}_{Q}[e^{T(X,Y,Z)}|X=x,Z=z]=\sum_{y}p(y|z)e^{T(x,y,z)}.

If
p_{XYZ}
has full support, an optimizer is

T(x,y,z)=\ln\frac{p(y|x,z)}{p(y|z)}.

Definition 3
.

Define the

\Phi
-mutual information
as

\displaystyle\widetilde{I}_{\Phi}(X;Y)

\displaystyle\triangleq\sup_{U}\left\{I_{\Phi}(U;X)+I_{\Phi}(U;Y)-I_{\Phi}(U;XY)\right\}

(46)

where the supremum is over all
p_{U|XY}
.

Then define the
conditional max-
\Phi
-mutual information

\displaystyle\widetilde{I}_{\Phi}(X;Y|Z)

\displaystyle\triangleq\sup_{U}\left\{I_{\Phi}(U;X|Z)+I_{\Phi}(U;Y|Z)-I_{\Phi}(U;XY|Z)\right\}

(47)

where the supremum is over all
p_{U|XYZ}
.

It is immediate that by the chain rule, the above definitions have the following equivalent forms. The max-
\Phi
-mutual information satisfies

\displaystyle\widetilde{I}_{\Phi}(X;Y)

\displaystyle=\sup_{U}\left\{I_{\Phi}(U;X)+I_{\Phi}(U;Y)-I_{\Phi}(U;XY)\right\}

\displaystyle=\sup_{U}\left\{I_{\Phi}(Y;U)-I_{\Phi}(U;Y|X)\right\}.

The conditional max-
\Phi
-mutual information satisfies

\displaystyle\widetilde{I}_{\Phi}(X;Y|Z)

\displaystyle=\sup_{U}\left\{I_{\Phi}(U;X|Z)+I_{\Phi}(U;Y|Z)-I_{\Phi}(U;XY|Z)\right\}

\displaystyle=\sup_{U}\left\{I_{\Phi}(U;Y|Z)-I_{\Phi}(U;Y|XZ)\right\}

\displaystyle=\sup_{U}\left\{I_{\Phi}(U;XZ)+I_{\Phi}(U;YZ)-I_{\Phi}(U;XYZ)-I_{\Phi}(U;Z)\right\}.

When
\Phi(t)=t\log t
, the
\Phi
-mutual information reduces to
Shannon mutual information, and the conditional max-
\Phi
-mutual

\widetilde{I}_{\Phi}(X;Y|Z)=I(X;Y|Z).

Indeed, for every auxiliary random variable
U
, the chain rule gives

\displaystyle I(U;X|Z)+I(U;Y|Z)-I(U;XY|Z)

\displaystyle\qquad=I(U;Y|Z)-I(U;Y|XZ)

\displaystyle\qquad=I(X;Y|Z)-I(X;Y|UZ)

\displaystyle\qquad\leq I(X;Y|Z).

Therefore,
\widetilde{I}_{\Phi}(X;Y|Z)\leq I(X;Y|Z)

\displaystyle I(X;X|Z)+I(X;Y|Z)-I(X;XY|Z)=I(X;Y|Z),

Proposition 5

Let

Conditional symmetry and dominance over

.

Conditional deterministic self-reduction.

If

(50)

Self-information bounds.

\displaystyle\widetilde{I}_{\Phi}(XT;YZ|W)\geq\widetilde{I}_{\Phi}(X;Y|W).

(53)

Subadditive chain rule.

\displaystyle\leq\widetilde{I}_{\Phi}(X;Y|W)+\widetilde{I}_{\Phi}(Z;Y|XW).

(54b)

6.

\widetilde{I}_{\Phi}=0
implies conditional independence.

III-C

Private-randomness-assisted
\widetilde{I}_{\Phi}

A natural requirement for a dependence measure
\delta
is invariance under adjoining local private randomness. To be precise, let
W_{1}\to X\to Y\to W_{2}
be a Markov chain. Here
W_{1}
is the private randomness owned by
X
and
W_{2}
is the private randomness owned by
Y
.
One would then expect a dependence measure
\delta
to satisfy

\delta(W_{1}X;W_{2}Y)=\delta(X;Y).

The max-
\Phi
-mutual information
\widetilde{I}_{\Phi}
does not satisfy this
property in general. In fact, adjoining independent private randomness
can strictly increase
\widetilde{I}_{\Phi}
.

Example 2
.

Let
\Phi(t)=(\sqrt{t}-1)^{2}

and
W_{1},W_{2}
be independent and uniformly distributed on
\{0,1\}
. Take
U=(W_{1},W_{2})
. One can verify that
\Phi\in\mathscr{G}\setminus\mathscr{F}
. By definition,

\widetilde{I}_{\Phi}(W_{1};W_{2})\geq I_{\Phi}(U;W_{1})+I_{\Phi}(U;W_{2})-I_{\Phi}(U;W_{1}W_{2}).

By the formula (
17
), we can calculate

I_{\Phi}(W_{1};W_{1})=I_{\Phi}(W_{2};W_{2})=\frac{1}{2}\Phi(2)+\frac{1}{2}\Phi(0)=2-\sqrt{2},

and

I_{\Phi}(W_{1}W_{2};W_{1}W_{2})=\frac{1}{4}\Phi(4)+\frac{3}{4}\Phi(0)=1.

Therefore

\widetilde{I}_{\Phi}(W_{1};W_{2})\geq(2-\sqrt{2})+(2-\sqrt{2})-1=3-2\sqrt{2}>0.

Thus
W_{1}\mathchoice{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\perp$\hss}\mkern 2.0mu{\displaystyle\perp}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{$\textstyle\perp$\hss}\mkern 2.0mu{\textstyle\perp}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle\perp$\hss}\mkern 2.0mu{\scriptstyle\perp}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptscriptstyle\perp$\hss}\mkern 2.0mu{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}}}W_{2}
, but
\widetilde{I}_{\Phi}(W_{1};W_{2})>0
.

The above example shows that
\widetilde{I}_{\Phi}
may assign a positive value to independent random variables. Thus, in general
\widetilde{I}_{\Phi}
is not an independence measure.

Motivated by this example, we define the largest value of
\widetilde{I}_{\Phi}

that can be obtained by supplying the two terminals with arbitrary
private randomness.

General independence structures

Given a hypergraph

with
\mathcal{V}=[n]
and
\mathcal{E}\subseteq\mathcal{P}([n])

(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})
if

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}=\left\{S\in\mathcal{P}([n]):(X_{i})_{i\in S}\textrm{ are mutually independent}\right\}.

Conversely, every independence structure can be represented by hypergraphs. Note that
\{i\}\in\mathcal{E}
for

. Moreover,
\mathcal{E}
is downward closed: if
S\in\mathcal{E}
, then for any
T\subseteq S
,

. When
\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{P}([n])
, we say that
\mathcal{H}
is complete.

\displaystyle H_{\Phi}(\mathbb{E}[f|X_{i}])\leq H_{\Phi}(f),

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in S}H_{\Phi}(\mathbb{E}[f|X_{i}])\leq H_{\Phi}(\mathbb{E}[f|X_{S}])\leq H_{\Phi}(f).

(66)

Suppose

Conversely, assume that

\displaystyle\int_{(0,\kappa(p,q)]}\left[\mathsf{B}_{p,q}(\alpha)-(1-\alpha)_{+}\right]\nu_{\Phi}(d\alpha)\leq\min\left\{\mathsf{J}_{\Phi}(p),\mathsf{J}_{\Phi}(q)\right\}.

Proof.

By definition,
\kappa(p,q)\geq 1
.
Consequently, for
\alpha>\kappa(p,q)
, both

(L_{\pi}-\alpha)_{+}
and
(1-\alpha)_{+}
vanish. By
(
87
),

\displaystyle D_{\Phi}\!\left(\pi\,\|\,p\otimes q\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{(0,\kappa(p,q)]}\left[\mathbb{E}_{\mu}[(L_{\pi}-\alpha)_{+}]-(1-\alpha)_{+}\right]\nu_{\Phi}(d\alpha).

By the definition of
\mathsf{B}_{p,q}(\alpha)
,

\mathbb{E}_{\mu}[(L_{\pi}-\alpha)_{+}]\leq\mathsf{B}_{p,q}(\alpha).

Since
\nu_{\Phi}
is a nonnegative measure, it follows that

D_{\Phi}\!\left(\pi\,\|\,p\otimes q\right)\leq\int_{(0,\kappa(p,q)]}\left[\mathsf{B}_{p,q}(\alpha)-(1-\alpha)_{+}\right]\nu_{\Phi}(d\alpha).

Taking the maximum over
\pi\in\Pi(p;q)
proves the first inequality.

For the second inequality, it suffices to prove the upper bound by

\mathsf{J}_{\Phi}(p)
, since the proof for
\mathsf{J}_{\Phi}(q)
is analogous. For

\alpha\geq 0
, let

\displaystyle\mathsf{S}_{p}(\alpha)\triangleq\sum_{x}p(x)(1-\alpha p(x))_{+}.

We claim that

\displaystyle\mathsf{B}_{p,q}(\alpha)\leq\mathsf{S}_{p}(\alpha).

(92)

Indeed, since
0\leq\pi(x,y)/q(y)\leq 1

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\pi(x,y)}{p(x)q(y)}-\alpha\right)_{+}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\pi(x,y)}{q(y)}\left(\frac{1}{p(x)}-\alpha\right)_{+}+\left(1-\frac{\pi(x,y)}{q(y)}\right)(0-\alpha)_{+}

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi(x,y)}{q(y)}\left(\frac{1}{p(x)}-\alpha\right)_{+}.

Multiplying by
p(x)q(y)
and summing over
x,y
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{x,y}p(x)q(y)\left(L_{\pi}(x,y)-\alpha\right)_{+}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{x,y}p(x)\pi(x,y)\left(\frac{1}{p(x)}-\alpha\right)_{+}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x}p(x)^{2}\left(\frac{1}{p(x)}-\alpha\right)_{+}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x}p(x)(1-\alpha p(x))_{+}=\mathsf{S}_{p}(\alpha).

Taking the maximum over
\pi\in\Pi(p;q)
proves
(
92

K_{p}\triangleq\max\{1/p(x):x\in\mathcal{X}\}

(
87

random variable with law

\displaystyle\mathsf{J}_{\Phi}(p)

\displaystyle=\int_{(0,K_{p}]}\left[\mathsf{S}_{p}(\alpha)-(1-\alpha)_{+}\right]\nu_{\Phi}(d\alpha).

By (
90

\kappa(p,q)\leq K_{p}

(
91
) and (
92
) imply

\mathsf{S}_{p}(\alpha)-(1-\alpha)_{+}\geq 0

\displaystyle\int_{(0,\kappa(p,q)]}\left[\mathsf{B}_{p,q}(\alpha)-(1-\alpha)_{+}\right]\nu_{\Phi}(d\alpha)

\displaystyle\quad\leq\int_{(0,\kappa(p,q)]}\left[\mathsf{S}_{p}(\alpha)-(1-\alpha)_{+}\right]\nu_{\Phi}(d\alpha)

\displaystyle\quad\leq\int_{(0,K_{p}]}\left[\mathsf{S}_{p}(\alpha)-(1-\alpha)_{+}\right]\nu_{\Phi}(d\alpha)

\displaystyle=\mathsf{J}_{\Phi}(p).

Proof of Theorem

Consider

Note that

\displaystyle I_{\Phi}(U;XZ)+I_{\Phi}(U;YZ)-I_{\Phi}(U;XYZ)-I_{\Phi}(U;Z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{u}p(u)g(p_{XYZ|u})

where

\displaystyle g(q_{XYZ})=D_{\Phi}(q_{XZ}\|p_{XZ})+D_{\Phi}(q_{YZ}\|p_{YZ})-D_{\Phi}(q_{XYZ}\|p_{XYZ})-D_{\Phi}(q_{Z}\|p_{Z}).

Then
\widetilde{I}_{\Phi}(X;Y|Z)
is the upper concave envelope of
g(q_{XYZ})
at
p_{XYZ}
. By Carathéodory’s theorem, it suffices to consider the random variable
U
with cardinality at most
|\mathcal{X}||\mathcal{Y}||\mathcal{Z}|
.

We have the following two claims:

1.

Fixing
q_{Z}
and
q_{Y|XZ}
,
g(q_{XYZ})
is convex in
q_{X|Z}
.

2.

Fixing
q_{Z}
and
q_{X|YZ}

is convex in
q_{Y|Z}
.

Indeed, fixing
q_{Z}
and
q_{Y|XZ}

8

\Phi\in\mathscr{G}

\displaystyle q_{X|Z}\mapsto D_{\Phi}(q_{XZ}\|p_{XZ})-D_{\Phi}(q_{XYZ}\|p_{XYZ})

\Phi

is convex. Note that
D_{\Phi}(q_{Z}\|p_{Z})
is constant. Thus, their sum,
q_{X|Z}\mapsto g(q_{XYZ})
is convex. The proof of the second claim follows similarly.

To compute
the upper concave envelope of

, we need to maximize

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\omega_{i}g(q^{(i)}_{XYZ})

(94)

subject to

\sum_{i}\omega_{i}q^{(i)}_{XYZ}=p_{XYZ}.

Let
U
be the maximizer of (
94

\displaystyle p_{XYZU}(x,y,z,i)=\omega_{i}q^{(i)}_{XYZ}(x,y,z).

For each

\displaystyle q_{XYZ}^{(i)}

\displaystyle=q_{Z}^{(i)}q_{X|Z}^{(i)}q_{Y|XZ}^{(i)}

\displaystyle=q_{Z}^{(i)}\left(\sum_{j}\nu_{ij}q_{X|Z}^{(i,j)}\right)q_{Y|XZ}^{(i)}

Z

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j}\omega_{i}\nu_{ij}q_{Z}^{(i)}q_{X|Z}^{(i,j)}q_{Y|XZ}^{(i)}

Conditional deterministic self-reduction.

Since
Y

\displaystyle\widetilde{I}_{\Phi}(X;Y|Z)

where the last equality follows from (

The last equality again follows from (

\Phi
-mutual information and the third line follows from (

\displaystyle\widetilde{I}_{\Phi}(X;X)

s=\frac{m-x_{2}}{t(x_{1}-x_{2})}.

Then after a change of variables we obtain

\displaystyle C(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{x_{1}-x_{2}}{t(m-x_{2})}\Bigg(\bigg(\Phi(x_{2})+\Phi^{\prime}(x_{2})(m-x_{2})-\Phi(m)\bigg)\bigg(\Phi^{\prime}(x_{1})-\Phi^{\prime}(x_{2})\bigg)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+\Phi^{\prime\prime}(x_{2})(m-x_{2})^{2}\bigg(\frac{\Phi(x_{1})-\Phi(m)}{x_{1}-m}+\frac{\Phi(x_{2})-\Phi(m)}{m-x_{2}}\bigg)\Bigg).

Since
\frac{x_{1}-x_{2}}{t(m-x_{2})}=1/(st^{2})\geq 0
, it suffices to show that

\displaystyle w_{m,x_{2}}(x_{1})

\displaystyle\triangleq\bigg(\Phi(x_{2})+\Phi^{\prime}(x_{2})(m-x_{2})-\Phi(m)\bigg)\bigg(\Phi^{\prime}(x_{1})-\Phi^{\prime}(x_{2})\bigg)

\displaystyle\quad+\Phi^{\prime\prime}(x_{2})(m-x_{2})^{2}\bigg(\frac{\Phi(x_{1})-\Phi(m)}{x_{1}-m}+\frac{\Phi(x_{2})-\Phi(m)}{m-x_{2}}\bigg)

\displaystyle\geq 0.

Taking the partial derivative with respect to
x_{1}
, we obtain

\displaystyle w^{\prime}_{m,x_{2}}(x_{1})

\displaystyle=\bigg(\Phi(x_{2})+\Phi^{\prime}(x_{2})(m-x_{2})-\Phi(m)\bigg)\Phi^{\prime\prime}(x_{1})+\Phi^{\prime\prime}(x_{2})\frac{(m-x_{2})^{2}}{(x_{1}-m)^{2}}\bigg(\Phi^{\prime}(x_{1})(x_{1}-m)-\Phi(x_{1})+\Phi(m)\bigg).

Since
m=stx_{1}+(1-st)x_{2}
, the following two cases are possible:
x_{1}\geq m\geq x_{2}
or

x_{2}\geq m\geq x_{1}
. We will show that in both cases,
w_{m,x_{2}}(x_{1})\geq w_{m,x_{2}}(m)
.

First consider the case of
x_{1}\geq m\geq x_{2}
: by Taylor’s expansion we obtain

\displaystyle\Phi(m)

\displaystyle=\Phi(x_{1})+\Phi^{\prime}(x_{1})(m-x_{1})+\frac{1}{2}\Phi^{\prime\prime}(x_{1})(m-x_{1})^{2}+\frac{1}{6}\Phi^{\prime\prime\prime}(\tilde{x}_{1})(m-x_{1})^{3}

for some
\tilde{x}_{1}\in[m,x_{1}]
. And

\displaystyle\Phi(m)

\displaystyle=\Phi(x_{2})+\Phi^{\prime}(x_{2})(m-x_{2})+\frac{1}{2}\Phi^{\prime\prime}(x_{2})(m-x_{2})^{2}+\frac{1}{6}\Phi^{\prime\prime\prime}(\tilde{x}_{2})(m-x_{2})^{3}

for some
\tilde{x}_{2}\in[x_{2},m]
. Since
\Phi^{\prime\prime\prime}\leq 0
, we have

\displaystyle\Phi^{\prime}(x_{1})(x_{1}-m)+\Phi(m)-\Phi(x_{1})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\Phi^{\prime\prime}(x_{1})(m-x_{1})^{2}

\displaystyle\Phi(x_{2})+\Phi^{\prime}(x_{2})(m-x_{2})-\Phi(m)+\frac{1}{2}\Phi^{\prime\prime}(x_{2})(m-x_{2})^{2}

\displaystyle\geq 0.

Note that

\displaystyle w^{\prime}_{m,x_{2}}(x_{1})

\displaystyle=\bigg(\Phi(x_{2})+\Phi^{\prime}(x_{2})(m-x_{2})-\Phi(m)\bigg)\Phi^{\prime\prime}(x_{1})+\Phi^{\prime\prime}(x_{2})\frac{(m-x_{2})^{2}}{(x_{1}-m)^{2}}\bigg(\Phi^{\prime}(x_{1})(x_{1}-m)-\Phi(x_{1})+\Phi(m)\bigg).

Thus,

\displaystyle w^{\prime}_{m,x_{2}}(x_{1})

\displaystyle\geq\bigg(\Phi(x_{2})+\Phi^{\prime}(x_{2})(m-x_{2})-\Phi(m)\bigg)\Phi^{\prime\prime}(x_{1})+\Phi^{\prime\prime}(x_{2})\frac{(m-x_{2})^{2}}{(x_{1}-m)^{2}}\bigg(\frac{1}{2}\Phi^{\prime\prime}(x_{1})(m-x_{1})^{2}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\bigg(\Phi(x_{2})+\Phi^{\prime}(x_{2})(m-x_{2})-\Phi(m)+\frac{1}{2}\Phi^{\prime\prime}(x_{2})(m-x_{2})^{2}\bigg)\Phi^{\prime\prime}(x_{1})

\displaystyle\geq 0.

This shows that
w_{m,x_{2}}(x_{1})\geq w_{m,x_{2}}(m)
.

Next, consider the case of
x_{2}\geq m\geq x_{1}
. A similar argument shows that
w^{\prime}_{m,x_{2}}(x_{1})\leq 0
in this case for
x_{1}\leq m\leq x_{2}
. Thus, we also obtain
w_{m,x_{2}}(x_{1})\geq w_{m,x_{2}}(m)
in the second case.

Then it remains to prove
w_{m,x_{2}}(m)\geq 0
. Applying condition (
70
) with
x=x_{2}
,
y=m
, we have

\displaystyle\bigg(\Phi(x_{2})+\Phi^{\prime}(x_{2})(m-x_{2})-\Phi(m)\bigg)\bigg(\Phi^{\prime}(m)-\Phi^{\prime}(x_{2})\bigg)+\Phi^{\prime\prime}(x_{2})(m-x_{2})^{2}\bigg(\Phi^{\prime}(m)-\frac{\Phi(x_{2})-\Phi(m)}{x_{2}-m}\bigg)\geq 0.

This is exactly
w_{m,x_{2}}(m)\geq 0
, which proves the result under condition (i).

Suppose Condition (ii) holds. It suffices to show that Condition (ii) implies (i). Define

\displaystyle Q_{x}(y)=\bigg(\Phi(x)+\Phi^{\prime}(x)(y-x)-\Phi(y)\bigg)\bigg(\Phi^{\prime}(y)-\Phi^{\prime}(x)\bigg)+\Phi^{\prime\prime}(x)(y-x)^{2}\bigg(\Phi^{\prime}(y)-\frac{\Phi(x)-\Phi(y)}{x-y}\bigg).

The partial derivative with respect to
y
equals

\displaystyle Q_{x}^{\prime}(y)

\displaystyle=-\bigg(\Phi^{\prime}(x)-\Phi^{\prime}(y)\bigg)^{2}+\Phi^{\prime\prime}(y)\bigg(\Phi(x)-\Phi(y)+\Phi^{\prime}(x)(y-x)\bigg)

\displaystyle\qquad+\Phi^{\prime\prime}(x)\bigg(\Phi(x)-\Phi(y)+\Phi^{\prime}(y)(y-x)+\Phi^{\prime\prime}(y)(y-x)^{2}\bigg).

Since
Q_{x}(x)=Q^{\prime}_{x}(x)=0
, it suffices to show that
Q_{x}
is convex in
y
, or equivalently that

\displaystyle Q_{x}^{\prime\prime}(y)={}

\displaystyle 3\Phi^{\prime\prime}(y)\bigg(\Phi^{\prime}(x)-\Phi^{\prime}(y)+\Phi^{\prime\prime}(x)(y-x)\bigg)

\displaystyle+\Phi^{\prime\prime\prime}(y)\bigg(\Phi(x)-\Phi(y)+\Phi^{\prime}(x)(y-x)+\Phi^{\prime\prime}(x)(y-x)^{2}\bigg)\geq 0.

Differentiating
Q_{x}^{\prime\prime}(y)
with respect to
x
gives

\displaystyle(x-y)\Phi^{\prime\prime\prime}(x)\Phi^{\prime\prime\prime}(y)\bigg(-3\frac{\Phi^{\prime\prime}(y)}{\Phi^{\prime\prime\prime}(y)}+\frac{\Phi^{\prime\prime}(x)}{\Phi^{\prime\prime\prime}(x)}+x-y\bigg).

Note that Condition (ii) implies that
\Phi^{\prime\prime\prime}\leq 0
wherever it is nonzero: indeed, setting
x=y
in (
71
) gives
\Phi^{\prime\prime}(x)/\Phi^{\prime\prime\prime}(x)\leq 0
and
\Phi^{\prime\prime}(x)\geq 0
by the convexity. It also gives

\displaystyle-3\frac{\Phi^{\prime\prime}(y)}{\Phi^{\prime\prime\prime}(y)}+\frac{\Phi^{\prime\prime}(x)}{\Phi^{\prime\prime\prime}(x)}+x-y\geq 0.

Consequently,
Q_{x}^{\prime\prime}(y)
, viewed as a function of
x
, is increasing for
x\geq y
and decreasing for
x\leq y
. Since
Q_{y}^{\prime\prime}(y)=0
, we conclude that
Q_{x}^{\prime\prime}(y)\geq 0
whenever the displayed ratios are defined; the remaining cases follow by continuity. Thus
Q_{x}(y)\geq 0
, so condition (i) holds and the conclusion follows from the first part of the proof.

Suppose Condition (iii) holds. It suffices to show that Condition (iii) implies (ii). Assume that

1\leq-\frac{t\Phi^{\prime\prime\prime}(t)}{\Phi^{\prime\prime}(t)}\leq 3,\qquad t>0,

and define

\kappa(t)\triangleq-\frac{t\Phi^{\prime\prime\prime}(t)}{\Phi^{\prime\prime}(t)}.

Then

1\leq\kappa(t)\leq 3

and

\frac{\Phi^{\prime\prime}(t)}{\Phi^{\prime\prime\prime}(t)}=-\frac{t}{\kappa(t)}.

Consequently,

x+\frac{\Phi^{\prime\prime}(x)}{\Phi^{\prime\prime\prime}(x)}=x\left(1-\frac{1}{\kappa(x)}\right)\geq 0,

where the inequality follows from
\kappa(x)\geq 1
. On the other hand,

y+3\frac{\Phi^{\prime\prime}(y)}{\Phi^{\prime\prime\prime}(y)}=y\left(1-\frac{3}{\kappa(y)}\right)\leq 0,

where the inequality follows from
\kappa(y)\leq 3
. Therefore,

x+\frac{\Phi^{\prime\prime}(x)}{\Phi^{\prime\prime\prime}(x)}\geq y+3\frac{\Phi^{\prime\prime}(y)}{\Phi^{\prime\prime\prime}(y)}

for every
x,y>0
. Hence condition (iii) implies
condition (ii).

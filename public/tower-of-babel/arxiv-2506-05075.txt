Estimation of Exciton Binding Energy and lifetime for Mono-layer Transition Metal Dichalcogenides Preprint: AIP/123-QED
Rohit Ramesh Nimje, Swati G, Ashutosh Mahajan

Abstract
In this work, we present a mathematical model for the Wannier-Mott exciton in monolayers of transition metal dichalcogenides such as W ​ S 2 WS_{2} , W ​ S ​ e 2 WSe_{2} , M ​ o ​ S 2 MoS_{2} , M ​ o ​ S ​ e 2 MoSe_{2} that estimates the radiation lifetime in the effective mass approximation. We calculate exciton energy, and binding energy by solving the Schrodinger wave equation with open boundary conditions to obtain quasi-bound states in the confined direction in the monolayer and decay rates by the Fermi-Golden rule. The proposed model uses only the physical parameters such as band offsets, effective mass, and dielectric constants for the monolayers of W ​ S 2 WS_{2} , W ​ S ​ e 2 WSe_{2} , M ​ o ​ S 2 MoS_{2} , and M ​ o ​ S ​ e 2 MoSe_{2} . The model is validated against III-V material quantum well heterostructure, and the estimated effective lifetime considering the thermalization of the exciton has been compared with photoluminescence decay for the TMD heterostructure. Our calculated values show good agreement with the time-resolved photoluminescence spectroscopy measurements and DFT estimations.

II.1
Exciton Decay Lifetime

Radiative lifetime of direct exciton depends on the oscillator strength
f_{val}
, which is a measure of the strength of light-matter interaction.
The oscillator strength can be derived by considering the probability of electromagnetic dipole transitions occurring, where the initial state is excitonic, and the final state is the crystal ground state and a photon. The oscillator strength can be written as
Burstein and Weisbuch 2012

f_{val}=\frac{2}{m\hbar\omega}|<\Psi_{0}|\epsilon\cdot p|\Psi_{ex}>|^{2}

(9)

where,
\Psi_{0}
is the crystal ground state,
\Psi_{ex}
is the exciton state,
\epsilon
is the polarization vector and
p
is pseudomomentum operator for the electron. The overlap of the electron and hole envelope functions determines the strength of the transition.

Since for the direct transition, the electron and hole momentum vectors are related as
k_{e}=-k_{h}
, the above expression can be further worked out as
Dimmock 1967

f_{val}=\frac{2}{m\hbar\omega}|<u_{v}|\epsilon\cdot p|u_{c}>|^{2}\left|\int\Psi(\rho=0,x_{e},x_{h})dx_{e}dx_{h}\right|^{2}

(10)

where,
u_{c}
&
u_{v}
are periodic parts of the Bloch functions for conduction and valence bands.

The oscillator strength can be written in terms of the transition dipole moment
d_{cv}
between the valence and the conduction band Bloch functions as,

f_{val}=\frac{2m_{0}E_{X}d_{cv}^{2}}{\hbar^{2}}\left|\int_{x_{e_{1}}}^{x_{e_{2}}}\int_{x_{h_{1}}}^{x_{h_{2}}}\Psi(x_{e},x_{h},\rho=0)dx_{e}dx_{h}\right|^{2}

(11)

The intrinsic radiative recombination time, or the zero in-plane momentum excitons, has extremely short radiative lifetimes and can be written as

\tau=\frac{\hbar}{2\Gamma_{0}}

(12)

where, exciton radiative linewidth can be written in terms of the oscillator strength as
Citrin 1993

\Gamma_{0}=\frac{\pi e^{2}}{\sqrt{\epsilon_{b}}m_{0}c}\cdot f_{val}

(13)

The intrinsic lifetime calculated above turns out to be much smaller than the experimentally observed lifetimes by time-resolved photoluminescence spectroscopy
Mohamed et al. 2017
, therefore, the thermalization process of the exciton must be taken into account.

II.2
Thermalization

The exciton decay time at room temperature can further be calculated by incorporating a thermalization factor to the radiative lifetime as given in
Robert et al. 2016
. This thermalization factor is due to the inelastic scattering of excitons with the acoustic phonons, which does not change the exciton population. The thermalization process is much faster than the radiative recombination. Therefore, excitons will always have a thermal distribution when the radiative recombination process happens.

The observed decay rate at a given temperature can be given as an average over the thermal distribution given by the Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution
Rowlinson* 2005
.

<\Gamma_{eff}>=\frac{\sum_{s}\Gamma_{s}e^{-\beta{E_{S}}}}{\sum_{s}e^{-\beta{E_{S}}}}

(14)

The dispersion relation for excitons is given as
Wang et al. 2016

Dimmock 1967

\displaystyle E_{s}(k)=E_{s}(0)+\frac{\hbar^{2}k^{2}}{2M}

where, M=
m_{e}
+
m_{h}
and
k
is exciton momentum. For a given in-plane wavevector, orthogonal photon polarization vector can be chosen as

\epsilon^{1}=\hat{y}
and
\epsilon^{2}=k_{z}(\hat{x}-\hat{z})
. The decay widths in the longitudinal and transverse directions can be evaluated as

\Gamma_{L}=2\Gamma_{0}\frac{\int_{0}^{k_{0}}k_{z}dk_{z}\frac{\sqrt{k_{0}^{2}-k^{2}}}{k_{z}}e^{-\beta{\frac{\hbar^{2}k_{z}^{2}}{2M}}}}{\int_{0}^{k_{0}}k_{z}dk_{z}e^{-\beta{\frac{\hbar^{2}k_{z}^{2}}{2M}}}}

(15)

\Gamma_{T}=2\Gamma_{0}\frac{\int_{0}^{k_{0}}k_{z}dk_{z}\frac{k_{0}}{\sqrt{k_{0}^{2}-k_{z}^{2}}}e^{-\beta{\frac{\hbar^{2}k_{z}^{2}}{2M}}}}{\int_{0}^{k_{0}}k_{z}dk_{z}e^{-\beta{\frac{\hbar^{2}k_{z}^{2}}{2M}}}}

(16)

Since the four spin states of excitons are equally populated, the ensemble averaged decay rate for the heavy-hole exciton can be written as
Burstein and Weisbuch 2012

\tau_{eff}(T)=\frac{3}{2}\frac{2Mk_{B}T}{\hbar^{2}k_{o}^{2}}\tau

V
Conclusion

We propose a mathematical model to calculate the lifetime and exciton binding energy for monolayer TMDs.The dependence of exciton binding energy and lifetime on the properties of a quantum well potential height, thickness, dielectric constant (
\epsilon_{b}
), and reduced effective mass (
m_{r}
) (
5
) is brought out.
In this work, we use the quantum transmitting boundary method for the electron and hole wavefunctions in the device direction and a finite difference scheme to calculate the exciton dynamics in the in-plane direction. We compared the calculated values with measurements from photoluminescence and fluorescence at low temperatures and room temperature. The calculated effective radiative lifetime values show a close agreement with the photoluminescence-measured data at low temperatures. A lifetime of thermalized exciton in nanoseconds is also found in agreement with experimentally observed values. The model estimated
MoS_{2}
lifetime is higher due to non-radiative recombination of excitons.

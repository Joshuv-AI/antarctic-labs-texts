Abstract
With its third data release (DR3), Gaia begins unveiling dormant candidate compact object (CO) binaries with luminous companions (LC) as predicted by several past theoretical studies. To date, 3 black hole (BH), 21 neutron star (NS), and ≈ 3200 \approx 3200 white dwarf (WD) candidates have been identified with LCs in detached orbits using astrometry. We adopt an observationally motivated sampling scheme for the star formation history of the Milky Way, and initial zero-age main-sequence binary properties, incorporate all relevant binary interaction processes during evolution to obtain a realistic present-day intrinsic population of CO – LC binaries. We apply Gaia ’s selection criteria to identify the CO – LC binaries detectable using the observational cuts applicable for DR3 as well as its end-of-mission (EOM). We find that under the DR3 selection cuts, our detectable population includes no BH–LCs, approximately 10 10 – 40 40 NS–LCs, and around ∼ 4300 \sim 4300 WD–LCs. In contrast, by EOM, the expected numbers increase to 30 30 – 300 300 BH–LCs, 1500 1500 – 5000 5000 NS–LCs, and ∼ 10 5 \sim 10^{5} – 10 6 10^{6} WD–LC binaries, primarily because of the significantly longer baseline of observation.

II
Numerical Setup

We use
COSMIC

(
Breivik et al. 2020
;
Breivik et al. 2021
)
, a publicly available rapid binary population synthesis suite, to model highly realistic populations of BH
–
LC, NS
–

–

COSMIC
is based on the stellar evolution fits from
SSE

(
Hurley et al. 2000
)
and binary evolution algorithm

(
Hurley et al. 2002
)
. The current version of
COSMIC
incorporates various modifications from
SSE
/
BSE
for the stellar evolution models and to treat binary interaction processes; for a detailed review, we encourage readers to see
Breivik et al. 2020
.

Using
COSMIC
, we model the present-day CO
–
LC binary populations by evolving an initial Zero Age Main Sequence (ZAMS) binary population. We generate the ZAMS binary stellar and orbital parameters from various observationally motivated probability distribution functions by adopting the ‘independent’ sampling scheme of
COSMIC
which assumes no correlations between stellar parameters (e.g., primary mass, mass ratio) and orbital parameters (e.g., semi-major axis, eccentricity) at birth. We sample the primary mass
{m_{\rm{pri}}}
between
0.08
and
150\,{M_{\odot}}
from the initial stellar mass function given in
Kroupa 2001
. We assign the secondary masses
{m_{\rm{sec}}}
such that
{m_{\rm{sec}}}/{M_{\odot}}\geq 0.08
and the mass ratio
q\equiv{m_{\rm{sec}}}/{m_{\rm{pri}}}
distribution is uniform
(
Mazeh et al. 1992
;
Goldberg & Mazeh 1994
;
Kobulnicky & Fryer 2007
)
. We assume an initially thermal eccentricity distribution
(
Jeans 1919
;
Ambartsumian 1937
;
Heggie 1975
)
. The initial semi-major axis (
{a}
) is sampled from a log-uniform distribution
(
Abt 1983
)
with an upper bound of
10^{5}\rm{R_{\odot}}
and a lower bound set by
{R_{\rm{pri}}}\leq{R_{\rm{RL}}}/2

(
Dominik et al. 2012
)
, where
{R_{\rm{pri}}}
and
{R_{\rm{RL}}}
denote the radius and Roche radius of the primary.

We use a metallicity-dependent star formation rate based on the final snapshot of the synthetically modeled MW-like galaxy

Various binary interaction processes, for example, mass transfer (MT), common envelope (CE) evolution, and tides, play a crucial role in shaping the present-day properties of the CO

(
Dominik et al. 2012

II.1
Compact-Object Formation: White dwarfs

Three types of WDs can be produced in our models depending on metallicity, ZAMS mass, and binary interactions. After low-mass (
0.08\lesssim{M_{\rm{ZAMS}}}/{M_{\odot}}\lesssim 0.8
) stars evolve off the MS, the He core contracts until electron degeneracy pressure supports further contraction and form a He WD. More massive stars may also produce He WDs if they interact and lose their envelopes before He shell burning. Low-mass stars (
0.8\leq{M_{\rm{ZAMS}}}/{M_{\odot}}\leq 2
) may burn He via the so-called He flash to create a carbon-oxygen core. Whereas, intermediate-mass stars (
2\leq{M_{\rm{ZAMS}}}/{M_{\odot}}\leq 8
) can create a carbon-oxygen core via core-He burning. Eventually electron degeneracy pressure takes over to leave behind a carbon-oxygen WD. Higher-mass stars (
8\leq{M_{\rm{ZAMS}}}/{M_{\odot}}\leq 10
) ignite both He and carbon in their cores. The ultimate fate of these stars is determined by the mass of the neon-oxygen-magnesium core after carbon burning ends. If the core mass exceeds
{M_{\rm{Ch}}}\approx 1.38\,{M_{\odot}}

(
Chandrasekhar 1931
)
, electron capture initiates collapse to form a low-mass NS
(
Miyaji et al. 1980
;
Nomoto 1984
;
Nomoto 1987
, e.g.,)
. Otherwise, a ONe WD remains.

{M_{\rm{ZAMS}}}

de Mink, S. E. et al. 2009
;
Langer 2012

We assume that if accretion from the companion star pushes the mass of a carbon-oxygen WD beyond

II.2
Compact-Object Formation: Neutron stars and black holes

Stars with ZAMS mass
{M_{\rm{ZAMS}}}/{M_{\odot}}\gtrsim 8
are massive enough to ignite the elements beyond carbon, ultimately forming an iron core. At collapse, the final structure of these stars is composed of an iron core surrounded by shells of various elements produced as a by-product of nuclear burning at different stages of a star’s life. When such a star explodes, it leaves behind a NS or a BH
(
Fryer 1999
;
Fryer & Kalogera 2001
;
Heger et al. 2003
)
.

We adopt two widely used explosion mechanisms for NS or BH formation through core-collapse supernovae (CCSNe), ‘rapid’ and ‘delayed’
(
Fryer et al. 2012
)
. These mechanisms differ in the explosion time after the core bounce set by the timescales on which neutrino-driven instabilities are allowed to grow
(
Colgate & White 1966
;
Boccioli et al. 2025
)
. Relevant to this work, this difference results in a ‘mass-gap’ in the BH mass function at birth in the rapid prescription which we adopt to be between
3
and
5\,{M_{\odot}}

(
Bailyn et al. 1998
;
Özel et al. 2010
;
Farr et al. 2011
;
Kreidberg et al. 2012
)
. In contrast, the delayed prescription has no mass gap. For the NS mass distribution, the delayed prescription produces heavier NSs compared to rapid due to higher accretion on the proto-NS before the explosion
(
Fryer et al. 2012
)
. We also consider NS formation via electron-capture SN (ECSN). The key parameter in this process is the range of He-core mass that no longer forms an iron core. The collapse is initiated by electron capture by
{}^{24}\mathrm{Mg}
and
{}^{20}\mathrm{Ne}
, which drops the electron degeneracy pressure inside the stellar core. We adopt that He-core masses between
1.4-2.5\,{M_{\odot}}
may form NSs via ECSN
(
Podsiadlowski et al. 2004
)
. NSs may also form if ECSN is triggered via the so-called accretion induced collapse (AIC) of an ONeMg WD. In particular, if an ONeMg WD’s mass exceeds
{M_{\rm{Ch}}}
due to accretion via RLOF, a NS can form
(
Nomoto & Kondo 1991
;
Podsiadlowski et al. 2004
)
.

Due to deviations from spherical symmetry during the collapse and subsequent explosion, the stellar remnant may experience a kick at the time of formation
(
Fryer & Kalogera 2001
;
Janka 2013
)
. For NSs, we adopt a bimodal Maxwellian distribution with
\sigma=265
and
20\,{\rm{km\ s^{-1}}}
for CCSN and ECSN, respectively
(
Hobbs et al. 2005
;
Podsiadlowski et al. 2004
)
. For BHs, we assume a fallback-modulated kick magnitude, where the initial kicks are sampled from a Maxwellian distribution with
\sigma=265\,{\rm{km\ s^{-1}}}

(
Belczynski et al. 2012
;
Fryer et al. 2012
)
.

{{M_{\rm{LC}}}}

–

–
LC binary population in the MW. To estimate the total number of CO
–
LC binaries in the MW, we scale the intrinsic CO
–
LC population by a factor equal to the ratio of the MW mass (
M_{\rm{\bf{m12i}}}
as specified by
m12i

M_{\rm{sim}}

–

{N_{{\rm{CO\text{--}LC}},\rm{MW}}}

–
LC binaries (
{N_{{\rm{CO\text{--}LC}},\rm{sim}}}
) as-

{N_{{\rm{CO\text{--}LC}},\rm{MW}}}={N_{{\rm{CO\text{--}LC}},\rm{sim}}}\frac{M_{\rm{\bf m12i}}}{M_{\rm{sim}}}.

(1)

We sample

binaries with replacement from the intrinsic population of CO
–
LC binaries assigning all the key stellar and orbital properties. Each binary is also given a unique position in the MW galaxy based on the age and metallicity of the ZAMS progenitor. This preserves the complex correlations between the position, age, and metallicity of the different star particles in

III
Detectibility by
Gaia

In this section, we describe the detectability criteria that we adopted to characterize a binary as resolvable by
Gaia
, based on magnitude
G
and the astrometric motion of the LC due to its orbit around the CO. We separately consider detectability of binaries resolvable by EOM and within the more stringent restrictions of DR3.

Note that the
Gaia
selection function used in this study is approximate, as it compares the sky-projected angular size with the
Gaia
pointing error. It is, however, computationally inexpensive and thus suitable for our large-scale simulations. More sophisticated
Gaia
selection functions, which incorporate additional complexities such as the scanning law, crowding, and photometric systematics, have been developed following the release of DR3
(
El-Badry et al. 2024a
;
Lam et al. 2025
)
. While our simplified approach does not capture these effects in detail, they are unlikely to significantly alter our population-level conclusions. Moreover, our predicted detection rates are consistent with theoretical studies employing more refined approaches
(
Nagarajan et al. 2025a
;
Yamaguchi et al. 2025
)
as well as with the currently observed census of CO
–
LC binaries in the DR3 data
(
Shahaf et al. 2022
;
Shahaf et al. 2024
;
El-Badry et al. 2024b
;
El-Badry et al. 2024c
)
.

Eccentricity is another parameter that can differentiate between the two adopted SNe prescriptions (
Figure 2

–

–

\approx 82-83\%

Interestingly, our models predict that the relative abundance of different types of CO

(

Yusof et al. 2013

(

{{P_{\rm{orb}}}}/{{\rm{yr}}}\leq 10

\geq 90\%

) and wider CO
–

Figure 1
). Of course, these are competing effects. Thus, future detections of CO
–

V.1
Detached BH
–
LC candidates discovered in DR3

Three detached BH
–
LC binaries has been identified using DR3 astrometry and radial velocity followup, refered to as
Gaia
BH1, BH2, and BH3
(
El-Badry et al. 2022c
;
El-Badry et al. 2023
;
Gaia Collaboration et al. 2024
)
.
Gaia
BH1 contains a
9.62\,{M_{\odot}}
BH with a bright solar-type MS companion with
{{M_{\rm{LC}}}}/{M_{\odot}}=0.93
and
[{\rm Fe/H}]=-0.2
, in an orbit with
{{P_{\rm{orb}}}}/{\rm{day}}=185.6
and
{{Ecc}}\approx 0.45
. Theoretical modeling of the galactic orbit indicates a thin disk origin
(
El-Badry et al. 2022c
)
.
Gaia
BH2 consists of an
8.9\,{M_{\odot}}
BH orbiting a red giant with
{{M_{\rm{LC}}}}/{M_{\odot}}\approx 1
in a fairly wide (
{{P_{\rm{orb}}}}/{\rm{day}}=1276
), eccentric (
{{Ecc}}\approx 0.52
) orbit.
Gaia
BH3 is a
32.7\,{M_{\odot}}
BH with a metal poor (
[{\rm Fe/H}]=-2.56
) companion of mass
0.76\,{M_{\odot}}
in a wide (
{{P_{\rm{orb}}}}/{\rm{day}}\sim 4253
) eccentric (
{{Ecc}}\approx 0.7
) orbit. Nevertheless, none of the BH
–
LC binaries produced in our models survive the DR3 cuts.

Indeed, a lot of past theoretical studies have tried to explain the
Gaia
BHs
(
El-Badry et al. 2022c
;
El-Badry et al. 2023
;
El-Badry 2024
;
Rastello et al. 2023
;
Tanikawa et al. 2023a
;
Tanikawa et al. 2025
;
Di Carlo et al. 2024
;
Generozov & Perets 2024
;
Gilkis & Mazeh 2024
;
Iorio, Giuliano et al. 2024
;
Kotko et al. 2024
;
Kruckow, Matthias U. et al. 2024
;
Li et al. 2024
;
Marín Pina, Daniel et al. 2024
;
Fantoccoli et al. 2025
)
. The overwhelming consensus is that under the standard assumptions of binary populations synthesis of isolated binaries, it is challenging to form BH
–
LC binaries resembling the properties of
Gaia
BH1 and BH2. In both cases, the general expectation is that the progenitor binary must go through at least one CE evolution. However, the typical choice of the efficiency parameter
\alpha\sim 1
fails to reproduce the large enough final orbits. Instead,
\alpha\approx 14
is required
(
El-Badry et al. 2022c
)
. Such high values of
\alpha
indicates dominant additional heating mechanisms to eject the envelope and is not expected
(
Ivanova 2018
, e.g.,)
.

Alternatively, such BH
–
LC binaries could also be produced inside young star clusters
(
Portegies Zwart et al. 2010
;
Shikauchi et al. 2020
, YSCs, e.g.,)
and dense star clusters
(
Chatterjee et al. 2017
;
Fantoccoli et al. 2025
)
via dynamical processes. Recent numerical studies have shown the potential of YSCs to form
Gaia
BH1 and BH2-like binaries
(
Rastello et al. 2023
;
Tanikawa et al. 2023a
;
Di Carlo et al. 2024
)
indicating that YSCs with initial mass
\sim 3\times 10^{2}
–
3\times 10^{4}\,{M_{\odot}}
are likely most efficient in their production.

The story for
Gaia
BH3 is arguably more fascinating. The Galactic orbit of
Gaia
BH3 and the chemical composition of its LC establishes its association of the ED-2 stream, believed to be created by a disrupted globular cluster of mass between
2\times 10^{3}
–
4\times 10^{4}\,{M_{\odot}}

(
Balbinot, E. et al. 2023
;
Balbinot, E. et al. 2024
;
Dodd, Emma et al. 2023
)
. This would indicate that
Gaia
BH3 may also have formed via dynamical processes
(
Marín Pina, Daniel et al. 2024
, e.g.,)
. Nevertheless,
Iorio, Giuliano et al. 2024
showed that
Gaia
BH3-like BH
–
LC binaries may also be formed via isolated binary evolution. They found that the likely progenitors in that case would never go through any MT episodes indicating that the current observed properties may be set by the ZAMS properties and the details of SN physics
(
El-Badry 2024
, see also)
.

Overall,
Gaia
’s astrometrically detected BH
–
LC binary population remains an evolving story. The relatively low numbers BH
–
LC binaries identified in DR3 is almost certainly due to DR3-specific selection biases with a strong expectation of significant detections in future data releases. Most recently,
Nagarajan et al. 2025a
shows that while the number of observed BH
–
LC binaries in DR3 is somewhat larger compared to what is expected from isolated binary evolution, it is significantly lower compared to the predictions of the dynamical channels. We expect that the future data releases would be very interesting to resolve these issues.

VI
Conclusions

In this work, we present a detailed analysis of the
Gaia
-detectable detached CO
–
LC populations. We discuss the key present-day observable properties of the intrinsic CO
–
LC binary populations in the Milky Way and investigate the same for the subsets that are resolvable by
Gaia
’s astrometry with DR3 and EOM selection cuts.

•

The model intrinsic present-day Milky Way population of CO
–
LC binaries exhibits a wide spread in the
{{M_{\rm{CO}}}}
distribution, spanning from
0.1\,{M_{\odot}}
to
45\,{M_{\odot}}
. WDs, NSs, and BHs exhibit mass ranges
0.1\leq{{M_{\rm{WD}}}}/{M_{\odot}}\leq 1.4
,
1.2\leq{{M_{\rm{NS}}}}/{M_{\odot}}\leq 3

3\leq{{M_{\rm{BH}}}}/{M_{\odot}}\leq 45
(
Figure 1
).

•

The
{{M_{\rm{CO}}}}
distribution (
Figure 1
) clearly distinguishes the populations generated by the two adopted SN models. The
rapid
SN prescription yields a pronounced lower mass-gap (
3-5\,{M_{\odot}}
) in the
{{M_{\rm{CO}}}}
distribution, whereas the
delayed
prescription does not exhibit such a gap. Notably, a small fraction (
\sim 1\%
) of BHs located within the mass gap in the
rapid
model form via AIC of NSs.

•

The intrinsic NS
–
LC binaries with
{{P_{\rm{orb}}}}/{{\rm{yr}}}\leq 10
in our models do not contain NSs with
{{M_{\rm{NS}}}}/{M_{\odot}}\geq 2
. We find that all of these high-mass NSs with short-period orbits are accreting via RLOF. This indicates that such high mass NSs are unlikely to be found in detached systems by
Gaia
. Indeed, no such candidate NS
–

We find a significant difference in the
{{Ecc}}

–

All model CO

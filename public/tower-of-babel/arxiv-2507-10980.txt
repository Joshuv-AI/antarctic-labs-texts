A Decision Procedure for Probabilistic Kleene Algebra with Angelic Nondeterminism
Shawn Ong, Dexter Kozen

Abstract




1
Introduction

In
[
12
]
, a new finite automaton model with probabilistic and nondeterministic transitions was introduced, along with a corresponding regular expression language. An interesting aspect of these formalisms, and the main feature differentiating them from previous approaches, is that automata and expressions are interpreted over
{\cal D}({\mathbb{N}}^{\Sigma^{*}})
, the space of probability distributions over multisets of strings with finite multiplicities. Thus strings are accepted not just with some probability, but with some finite multiplicity with some probability. The development relied on a recently established distributive law between multisets and distributions
[
6
,
4
,
5
,
3
]
, thereby circumventing difficulties arising from the known lack of a distributive law between powersets and distributions
[
13
,
14
,
15
]
and of any monad structure for the composite functor of distributions over powersets
[
2
]
.

The main result of
[
12
]
was a full Kleene theorem showing that the two formalisms are semantically equivalent. This theorem was the first of its type among a long history of automata models incorporating probability and nondeterminism; see
[
12
]
and references therein.

The main result of the present paper is that semantic equivalence of two given automata or expressions is decidable. The algorithm is inspired by the decision procedure for weighted automata as presented in
[
7
]
; see also
[
10
]
. With weighted automata, the treatment depends heavily on linear algebra. The appropriate mathematical structures are finite-dimensional vector spaces over
{\mathbb{R}}
. For a weighted automaton with finite state set
S
, the algorithm relies on a dimension argument in the vector space
{\mathbb{R}}^{S}
. For us, the situation is similar, except linear algebra is replaced by polynomial algebra. The appropriate mathematical constructs are unital commutative associative algebras over
{\mathbb{R}}
(
{\mathbb{R}}
-algebras). Termination for an automaton with finite state set
S
depends on the fact that the polynomial algebra
{\mathbb{R}}[S]
is Noetherian. The algorithm makes use of Gröbner basis techniques
[
1
,
8
]
for ideal membership testing.

Much of the development of this paper involves a reformulation of the results of
[
12
]
in terms of
{\mathbb{R}}
-algebras. We show that the space
{\cal S}({\mathbb{N}}^{\Sigma^{*}})
of signed measures is an
{\mathbb{R}}
-algebra with multiplication interpreted as nondeterministic choice
\mathrel{\&}
. The multiplicative unit is
\delta_{\{\kern-2.0pt||\kern-2.0pt\}}

{\cal S}({\mathbb{N}}^{S})
for a finite state set

is also an
{\mathbb{R}}
-algebra, and we show that it is isomorphic to the polynomial algebra
{\mathbb{R}}[S]

2.1
Basic Properties

Let
R
be a unital commutative ring. The multivariate polynomial ring
R[S]
is the free unital commutative associative algebra over
R
(
R
-algebra) on generators
S
. The free construction
R[-]
, being left adjoint to a forgetful functor, gives rise to a monad over
\mathsf{Set}
. The monad multiplication
\flat_{S}:R[R[S]]\to R[S]
regards a polynomial of polynomials over
S
as a polynomial over
S
. If we like, we can multiply out the nested polynomial expressions and combine like terms. In general, any polynomial expression can be reduced to a canonical representation as an
R
-weighted sum of distinct monomials in this way. The monad unit maps
s
as an indeterminate to
s
as a monomial. The category
R\mbox{-}\mathsf{Alg}
of unital commutative associative algebras over
R
is the category of Eilenberg-Moore algebras of this monad.

The class
R\mbox{-}\mathsf{Alg}
also has various equivalent equational presentations:

•

R
-modules with a unital commutative bilinear multiplication;

•

unital commutative rings with a ring action
R\times A\to A
as scalar multiplication;

•

unital commutative rings with a unital commutative ring homomorphism
R\to A
.

The relation between the second and third of these characterizations is as follows. Given a scalar multiplication
(\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}):R\times A\to A
, the corresponding ring homomorphism
R\to A
is
r\mapsto r1_{A}
; conversely, given a ring homomorphism
h:R\to A
, the corresponding scalar multiplication
R\times A\to A
is
(r,a)\mapsto h(r)a
.

Let
(A,\sigma)
be an
R
-algebra regarded as an Eilenberg-Moore algebra for the monad
R[-]
, where
\sigma:R[A]\to A
is the structure map.
Any interpretation
f:S\to A
of the indeterminates extends uniquely to an evaluation morphism
f^{\dagger}=\sigma\circ R[f]:R[S]\to A
, where
R[f]:R[S]\to R[A]
is the morphism that substitutes
f(s)
for
s
.

2.2
Ideals

Let
(A,\sigma)
be an
R
-algebra. An
ideal
in
A
is a subset
I\mathrel{\subseteq}A
such that

•

0\in I

•

if
x,y\in I
, then
x+y\in I

•

if
x\in A
and
y\in I
, then
xy\in I
.

•

if
r\in R
and
y\in I
, then
ry\in I
.

The
kernel
of an
R\mbox{-}\mathsf{Alg}
homomorphism
h:A\to B
, denoted
\mathop{\mathrm{ker}}h
, is the set
\{a\in A\mid h(a)=0\}
. It is easily shown that the kernel of any
R\mbox{-}\mathsf{Alg}
homomorphism is an ideal. Conversely, any ideal in
A
is the kernel of an epimorphism obtained via a quotient construction.

2.3
Signed Measures on Multisets as
{\mathbb{R}}
-algebras

Let
\Sigma
be a finite alphabet. Let
\Sigma^{*}
denote the set of finite-length strings over
\Sigma
and
\varepsilon
the null string. The family of multisets of
\Sigma^{*}
with finite multiplicities is
{\mathbb{N}}^{\Sigma^{*}}
, the family of functions
\Sigma^{*}\to{\mathbb{N}}
. Multiset union is just pointwise addition and is denoted
\uplus
. Multiset comprehension is denoted with stylized braces
\{\kern-2.0pt|{-}|\kern-2.0pt\}
.

A
finite tree
is a finite subset of
\Sigma^{*}
closed under the prefix relation. For any finite tree
A
, let
\partial A
be the set of prefix-minimal elements of
\Sigma^{*}\setminus A
. For example, if
A=\{\varepsilon,a,b,aa,ab\}
, then
\partial A=\{ba,bb,aaa,aab,aba,abb\}
. Also,
\partial\varnothing=\{\varepsilon\}
,
\partial\{\varepsilon\}=\Sigma
, and
\partial\Sigma^{<n}=\Sigma^{n}
, where
\Sigma^{<n}=\{x\in\Sigma^{*}\mid|x|<n\}
and
\Sigma^{n}=\{x\in\Sigma^{*}\mid|x|=n\}
.

As argued in
[
12
]
,
{\mathbb{N}}^{\Sigma^{*}}
is a standard Borel space with measurable sets generated by the sets
[\alpha]_{A}=\{\beta\in{\mathbb{N}}^{\Sigma^{*}}\mid\beta\equiv_{A}\alpha\}
, where
A\mathrel{\subseteq}\Sigma^{*}
is a finite tree,
\alpha\in{\mathbb{N}}^{\Sigma^{*}}
, and
\beta\equiv_{A}\alpha
if
\beta(x)=\alpha(x)
for all
x\in A
. The relation
\equiv_{n}
defined in
[
12
]
is
\equiv_{\Sigma^{n}}
as defined here. Each
[\alpha]_{A}
contains a unique canonical element
\alpha\restriction A
that agrees with
\alpha
on
A
and vanishes outside of
A
. Since the support of
\alpha\restriction A
is contained in
A
, we can view
\alpha\restriction A
as an element of
{\mathbb{N}}^{A}
.

The space
{\cal D}({\mathbb{N}}^{\Sigma^{*}})
of probability measures on
{\mathbb{N}}^{\Sigma^{*}}
is the space of behaviors over which the automata and expressions of
[
12
]
are interpreted. Let
{\cal S}({\mathbb{N}}^{\Sigma^{*}})
denote the space of finite signed measures on
{\mathbb{N}}^{\Sigma^{*}}
. Every element of
{\cal S}({\mathbb{N}}^{\Sigma^{*}})
can be represented as a weighted difference
a\mu-b\nu
of probability measures
\mu,\nu\in{\cal D}({\mathbb{N}}^{\Sigma^{*}})
.

Lemma 2.1
.

The structure
({\cal S}({\mathbb{N}}^{\Sigma^{*}}),+,-,0,\mathrel{\&},\delta_{\{\kern-2.0pt||\kern-2.0pt\}})
is an
{\mathbb{R}}
-algebra.

Proof.

It is clearly a vector space over
{\mathbb{R}}
under the pointwise operations
+
,
-
, and
0
. Multiplication is nondeterministic choice
\mathrel{\&}
. This is defined by
\mu\mathrel{\&}\nu=(\mu\otimes\nu)\circ\uplus^{-1}
, the pushforward measure of the independent product
\mu\otimes\nu
on
({\mathbb{N}}^{\Sigma^{*}})^{2}
under multiset union
\uplus
. Intuitively, to sample
\mu\mathrel{\&}\nu
, one samples
\mu
and
\nu
independently, then takes the multiset union of the outcomes. This operation is associative and commutative because the independent product operation
\otimes
is associative and commutative on distributions and multiset union
\uplus
is associative and commutative on multisets. The unit for
\mathrel{\&}
is
\delta_{\{\kern-2.0pt||\kern-2.0pt\}}
, the Dirac (point mass) measure on the empty multiset, so the structure is a commutative monoid under
\mathrel{\&}
and
\delta_{\{\kern-2.0pt||\kern-2.0pt\}}
. Finally, distributivity of
\mathrel{\&}
over weighted addition in
{\cal D}({\mathbb{N}}^{\Sigma^{*}})
(that is,
\mu\mathrel{\&}(r\nu+(1-r)\rho)=r(\mu\mathrel{\&}\nu)+(1-r)(\mu\mathrel{\&}\rho)
) was shown in
[
11
, Lemma B.5]
and is easily extended to full distributivity of
\mathrel{\&}
over
+
on
{\cal S}({\mathbb{N}}^{\Sigma^{*}})
by linearity.
∎

The same structure applies to automata. Let
S
be a finite set. Let
{\cal S}({\mathbb{N}}^{S})
denote the family of finite signed measures over
{\mathbb{N}}^{S}
with finite support, with the operations defined in the same way as in
{\cal S}({\mathbb{N}}^{\Sigma^{*}})
.

Lemma 2.2
.

The structure
({\cal S}({\mathbb{N}}^{S}),+,-,0,\mathrel{\&},\delta_{\{\kern-2.0pt||\kern-2.0pt\}})
is an
{\mathbb{R}}
-algebra and is naturally isomorphic to the polynomial algebra
{\mathbb{R}}[S]
.

Proof.

The proof that
{\cal S}({\mathbb{N}}^{S})
is an
{\mathbb{R}}
-algebra is the same as in Lemma
2.1
except for the distributivity argument. However, here the argument is easier, since the space is discrete. To show distributivity, for any measurable
B\mathrel{\subseteq}{\mathbb{N}}^{S}
,

\displaystyle(\mu\mathrel{\&}\nu)(B)

\displaystyle=((\mu\otimes\nu)\circ\uplus^{-1})(B)=(\mu\otimes\nu)(\{(m,\ell)\mid m\uplus\ell\in B\})=\sum_{m\uplus\ell\in B}\mu(m)\cdot\nu(\ell),

therefore

\displaystyle((\mu\mathrel{\&}(\nu+\rho))(B)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\uplus\ell\in B}\mu(m)\cdot(\nu+\rho)(\ell)=\sum_{m\uplus\ell\in B}\mu(m)\cdot\nu(\ell)+\sum_{m\uplus\ell\in B}\mu(m)\cdot\rho(\ell)

\displaystyle=(\mu\mathrel{\&}\nu)(B)+(\mu\mathrel{\&}\rho)(B)=((\mu\mathrel{\&}\nu)+(\mu\mathrel{\&}\rho))(B).

As
B
was arbitrary,
\mu\mathrel{\&}(\nu+\rho)=(\mu\mathrel{\&}\nu)+(\mu\mathrel{\&}\rho)
. Thus the structure is an
{\mathbb{R}}
-algebra.

Now we argue that
{\cal S}({\mathbb{N}}^{S})
is naturally isomorphic to
{\mathbb{R}}[S]
. Since
{\mathbb{R}}[S]
is the free
{\mathbb{R}}
-algebra on generators
S
, there is a unique
{\mathbb{R}}
-algebra homomorphism
\varphi_{S}:{\mathbb{R}}[S]\to{\cal S}({\mathbb{N}}^{S})
extending
s\mapsto\delta_{\{s\}}
. This map specializes to a bijection
\varphi_{S}:\mathsf{monom}[S]\to{\mathbb{N}}^{S}
, where
\mathsf{monom}[S]
denotes the set of monomials of
R[S]
, in which
\varphi_{S}(m)(s)
is the degree of
s
in
m
. For example,
\varphi_{S}(s^{2}t^{3})=\{\kern-2.0pt|s,s,t,t,t|\kern-2.0pt\}
. Moreover, this bijection is an isomorphism of commutative monoids
(\mathsf{monom}[S],\cdot,1)\to({\mathbb{N}}^{S},\uplus,\{\kern-2.0pt||\kern-2.0pt\})
, and both structures are representations of the free commutative monoid on generators
S
. As elements of
{\mathbb{R}}[S]
and
{\cal S}({\mathbb{N}}^{S})
are simply finite linear combinations of
\mathsf{monom}[S]
and
{\mathbb{N}}^{S}
, respectively,
\varphi_{S}:{\mathbb{R}}[S]\to{\cal S}({\mathbb{N}}^{S})
is an isomorphism of
{\mathbb{R}}
-algebras.

The isomorphism is natural in the sense that for any set function
f:S\to T
, the diagram

{\mathbb{R}}[S]
{\cal S}({\mathbb{N}}^{S})
{\mathbb{R}}[T]
{\cal S}({\mathbb{N}}^{T})
{\mathbb{R}}[f]
\varphi_{S}
{\cal S}({\mathbb{N}}^{f})
\varphi_{T}

commutes, where
{\cal S}({\mathbb{N}}^{f})
replaces each occurrence of
s\in S
with
f(s)
in a given multiset, and
{\mathbb{R}}[f]
substitutes
f(s)
for the indeterminate
s
in a given polynomial.
∎

2.4

4.2
Marginalization

Marginalization
refers to the projection of a joint distribution on a product space onto its marginal distribution on a factor space. This is achieved by partial evaluation of the measure on the complementary factor. For automata, we can intuitively marginalize on a finite tree

\partial A

\partial A

A

(\Sigma^{*},{\mathbb{R}})
-algebras.

Let
A
be a finite tree. For
p\in{\mathbb{R}}[S]
, rewriting
\theta_{A}(p)
in the form
{\mathbb{R}}[\partial A\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}S][A\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}\mathbold{\varepsilon}]
defines a collection of polynomials
d_{A}^{m}(p)
such that

\displaystyle\theta_{A}(p)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\in\mathsf{monom}[A\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}\mathbold{\varepsilon}]}d_{A}^{m}(p)\cdot m.

(7)

The function
d_{A}^{m}:{\mathbb{R}}[S]\to{\mathbb{R}}[\partial A\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}S]
for
m\in\mathsf{monom}[A\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}\mathbold{\varepsilon}]
is uniquely defined in this way.
Since
\theta_{A}(p)
is a polynomial, all but finitely many of the
d_{A}^{m}(p)
are
0
.

Let us further define for
x\in\partial A
the unique function
d_{A,x}^{m}:{\mathbb{R}}[S]\to{\mathbb{R}}[S]
such that

\displaystyle((\partial A\setminus x)\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}\mathbold{1})\circ d_{A}^{m})(p)

\displaystyle=x\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}d_{A,x}^{m}(p).

(8)

The left-hand side is of type
{\mathbb{R}}[x\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}S]
, and since
x\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}-
is injective, this defines
d_{A,x}^{m}(p)
uniquely.

We remark that, although the maps
\theta_{A}:{\mathbb{R}}[S]\to{\mathbb{R}}[A\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}\mathbold{\varepsilon},\partial A\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}S]
are
{\mathbb{R}}
-algebra homomorphisms, the maps
d_{A}^{m}:{\mathbb{R}}[S]\to{\mathbb{R}}[\partial A\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}S]
and
d_{A,x}^{m}:{\mathbb{R}}[S]\to{\mathbb{R}}[S]
are not in general. However, they do satisfy some useful algebraic properties.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
A
be a finite tree,
m\in\mathsf{monom}[A\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}\mathbold{\varepsilon}]
,
r\in{\mathbb{R}}
, and
p,q\in{\mathbb{R}}[S]
.
The following equational properties hold of
d_{A}^{m}
.

\displaystyle d_{A}^{m}(ap+bq)

\displaystyle=ad_{A}^{m}(p)+bd_{A}^{m}(q)

\displaystyle d_{A}^{m}(pq)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\ell=m}d_{A}^{k}(p)\cdot d_{A}^{\ell}(q)

\displaystyle d_{A}^{1}(r)

\displaystyle=r

\displaystyle d_{A}^{m}(r)

\displaystyle=0,\ m\neq 1.

Moreover, the same properties hold for the functions
d_{A,x}^{m}
.

Proof.

For linearity, we have

\displaystyle\theta_{A}(ap+bq)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\in\mathsf{monom}[A\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}\mathbold{\varepsilon}]}d_{A}^{m}(ap+bq)\cdot m,

but since
\theta_{A}
is an
{\mathbb{R}}
-algebra homomorphism, we also have

\displaystyle\theta_{A}(ap+bq)=a\theta_{A}(p)+b\theta_{A}(q)

\displaystyle=a\cdot\sum_{m\in\mathsf{monom}[A\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}\mathbold{\varepsilon}]}d_{A}^{m}(p)\cdot m+b\cdot\sum_{m\in\mathsf{monom}[A\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}\mathbold{\varepsilon}]}d_{A}^{m}(q)\cdot m

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\in\mathsf{monom}[A\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}\mathbold{\varepsilon}]}(ad_{A}^{m}(p)+bd_{A}^{m}(q))\cdot m,

therefore
d_{A}^{m}(ap+bq)=ad_{A}^{m}(p)+bd_{A}^{m}(q)
.

For products, again we have

\displaystyle\theta_{A}(pq)=\sum_{m\in\mathsf{monom}[A\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}\mathbold{\varepsilon}]}d_{A}^{m}(pq)\cdot m

and

\displaystyle\theta_{A}(pq)

\displaystyle=\theta_{A}(p)\cdot\theta_{A}(q)=(\sum_{k\in\mathsf{monom}[A\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}\mathbold{\varepsilon}]}d_{A}^{k}(p)\cdot k)\cdot(\sum_{\ell\in\mathsf{monom}[A\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}\mathbold{\varepsilon}]}d_{A}^{\ell}(q)\cdot\ell)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathsf{monom}[A\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}\mathbold{\varepsilon}]}\sum_{\ell\in\mathsf{monom}[A\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}\mathbold{\varepsilon}]}d_{A}^{k}(p)\cdot d_{A}^{\ell}(q)\cdot k\ell=\sum_{m\in\mathsf{monom}[A\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}\mathbold{\varepsilon}]}\sum_{k\ell=m}d_{A}^{k}(p)\cdot d_{A}^{\ell}(q)\cdot m

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\in\mathsf{monom}[A\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}\mathbold{\varepsilon}]}(\sum_{k\ell=m}d_{A}^{k}(p)\cdot d_{A}^{\ell}(q))\cdot m,

therefore
d_{A}^{m}(pq)=\sum_{k\ell=m}d_{A}^{k}(p)\cdot d_{A}^{\ell}(q)
.

For scalars
r\in{\mathbb{R}}
, we have
r=d_{A}(r)=\sum_{m\in\mathsf{monom}[A\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}\mathbold{\varepsilon}]}d_{A}^{m}(r)\cdot m
, so
d_{A}^{m}(r)=0
for
m\neq 1
and
d_{A}^{1}(r)=r
.

The same equational properties are easily seen to hold also for
d_{A,X}^{m}
, since
(\partial A\setminus x)\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}\mathbold{1}
and
x\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}-
behave homomorphically and
x\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}-
is injective. For example, the argument for products is

\displaystyle x\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}d_{A,x}^{m}(pq)

\displaystyle=((\partial A\setminus x)\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}\mathbold{1})(d_{A}^{m}(pq))=((\partial A\setminus x)\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}\mathbold{1})(\sum_{k\ell=m}d_{A}^{k}(p)\cdot d_{A}^{\ell}(q))

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\ell=m}((\partial A\setminus x)\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}\mathbold{1})(d_{A}^{k}(p))\cdot((\partial A\setminus x)\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}\mathbold{1})(d_{A}^{\ell}(q))

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\ell=m}(x\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}d_{A,x}^{k}(p))\cdot(x\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}d_{A,x}^{\ell}(q))=x\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}\sum_{k\ell=m}d_{A,x}^{k}(p)\cdot d_{A,x}^{\ell}(q),

therefore
d_{A,x}^{m}(pq)=\sum_{k\ell=m}d_{A,x}^{k}(p)\cdot d_{A,x}^{\ell}(q)
by injectivity.
∎

Lemma 4.6

\displaystyle=((x\partial B\setminus xy)\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}\mathbold{1})\circ((\partial A\setminus x)\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}\mathbold{1})=(x\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}((\partial B\setminus y)\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}\mathbold{1})\circ((\partial A\setminus x)\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}\mathbold{1}).

\displaystyle=(x\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}((\partial B\setminus y)\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}\mathbold{1})\circ((\partial A\setminus x)\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}\mathbold{1})\circ(x\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}\theta_{B})\circ\theta_{A}

\displaystyle=(x\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}((\partial B\setminus y)\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}\mathbold{1})\circ(x\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}\theta_{B})\circ((\partial A\setminus x)\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}\mathbold{1})\circ\theta_{A}.

\mathbold{1}(\llbracket d_{A}^{m(\beta)}\rrbracket(s))
.
∎

Theorem 5.2
.

For an automaton with state set
S
and transition function
\theta:S\to{\mathbb{R}}[\mathbold{\varepsilon},\Sigma\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}S]
, for
\mu,\nu\in{\cal S}({\mathbb{N}}^{S})
, let
p=\varphi_{S}^{-1}(\mu-\nu)\in{\mathbb{R}}[S]
. Let
d_{A}^{m}
and
d_{A,x}^{m}
be defined as in (
7
) and (
8
). Then
\llbracket\mu\rrbracket=\llbracket\nu\rrbracket
if and only if for all finite trees
A\mathrel{\subseteq}\Sigma^{*}
, all
x\in\partial A
, and all
m\in\mathsf{monom}[A\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}\mathbold{\varepsilon}]
,

\displaystyle d_{A,x}^{m}(p)\in\mathop{\mathrm{ker}}\mathbold{1}.

(14)

Proof.

From the definitions (
7
) and (
8
), using Lemma
3.2
(iii) and (iv), we have

\displaystyle\mathbold{1}(d_{A}^{m}(p))

\displaystyle=(\partial A\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}\mathbold{1})(d_{A}^{m}(p))=(x\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}\mathbold{1})((\partial A\setminus x)\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}\mathbold{1})(d_{A}^{m}(p))

\displaystyle=(x\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}\mathbold{1})(x\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}d_{A,x}^{m}(p))=x\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}\mathbold{1}(d_{A,x}^{m}(p))=\mathbold{1}(d_{A,x}^{m}(p)),

thus
d_{A,x}^{m}(p)\in\mathop{\mathrm{ker}}\mathbold{1}
iff
d_{A}^{m}(p)\in\mathop{\mathrm{ker}}\mathbold{1}
. By Lemma
5.1
, the latter condition is equivalent to
\llbracket\mu\rrbracket([\alpha]_{A})=\llbracket\nu\rrbracket([\alpha]_{A})
, where
\alpha\in{\mathbb{N}}^{A}
is the multiset corresponding to the monomial
m
according to the bijection (
12
).
By Lemma
5.1
, this occurs iff

\llbracket\mu\rrbracket([\alpha]_{A})=\llbracket\nu\rrbracket([\alpha]_{A})
.
But since the

generate the Borel sets, this occurs for all

if and only if
\llbracket\mu\rrbracket=\llbracket\nu\rrbracket
.
∎

6
Algorithm

To test whether
\llbracket\mu\rrbracket=\llbracket\nu\rrbracket
for given
\mu,\nu\in{\cal S}({\mathbb{N}}^{S})
, we use Theorem
5.2
to check whether all polynomials
d_{A,x}^{m}(p)\in{\mathbb{R}}[S]
are members of the ideal
\mathop{\mathrm{ker}}\mathbold{1}
, where
p=\varphi_{S}^{-1}(\mu-\nu)
. Although there are infinitely many such polynomials, this can be done in finite time.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
I
be an ideal of
{\mathbb{R}}[S]
. Suppose for all
a\in\Sigma
and
n\geq 0
,
d_{\varepsilon,a}^{\mathbold{\varepsilon}^{n}}
preserves membership in
I
; that is, if
p\in I
, then
d_{\varepsilon,a}^{\mathbold{\varepsilon}^{n}}(p)\in I
. Then for all
A
, all
x\in\partial A
, and all monomials
m\in\mathsf{monom}[A\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}\mathbold{\varepsilon}]
,
d_{A,x}^{m}
preserves membership in
I
.

Proof.

By induction. All
d_{\varepsilon,a}^{\mathbold{\varepsilon}^{n}}
preserve
I
by assumption, and by Lemma
4.6
, every
d_{A,x}^{m}
is a composition of these; specifically,
d_{A\cup\{x\},xa}^{m}=d_{\varepsilon,a}^{\mathbold{\varepsilon}^{k}}\circ d_{A,x}^{\ell}
, where
k
is the degree of
x
in
m
and
\ell=m/x^{k}
. Any composition of functions that preserve
I
also preserves
I
.
∎

Lemma 6.2
.

Suppose
I=\langle B\rangle
is an ideal of
{\mathbb{R}}[S]
generated by
B
. Let
a\in\Sigma
.
If
d_{\varepsilon,a}^{\mathbold{\varepsilon}^{n}}(b)\in I
for all
n\geq 0
and
b\in B
, then for all
n\geq 0
,
d_{\varepsilon,a}^{\mathbold{\varepsilon}^{n}}
preserves membership in
I
.

Proof.

If
p\in I
, then
p=p_{1}b_{1}+\cdots+p_{k}b_{k}
for some
p_{1},\ldots,p_{k}\in{\mathbb{R}}[S]
and
b_{1},\ldots,b_{k}\in B
. By Lemma
4.5
,

\displaystyle d_{\varepsilon,a}^{\mathbold{\varepsilon}^{n}}(p_{1}b_{1}+\cdots+p_{n}b_{n})

\displaystyle=d_{\varepsilon,a}^{\mathbold{\varepsilon}^{n}}(p_{1}b_{1})+\cdots+d_{\varepsilon,a}^{\mathbold{\varepsilon}^{n}}(p_{n}b_{n})

\displaystyle=\sum_{m+\ell=n}d_{\varepsilon,a}^{\mathbold{\varepsilon}^{m}}(p_{1})d_{\varepsilon,a}^{\mathbold{\varepsilon}^{\ell}}(b_{1})+\cdots+\sum_{m+\ell=n}d_{\varepsilon,a}^{\mathbold{\varepsilon}^{m}}(p_{n})d_{\varepsilon,a}^{\mathbold{\varepsilon}^{\ell}}(b_{n})\in I.

∎

The
forward ideal
of the automaton
(S,\theta)
with seed
p\in{\mathbb{R}}[S]
is

\displaystyle J_{p}=\langle\{d_{A,x}^{m}(p)\mid A\mathrel{\subseteq}\Sigma^{*}\text{ a finite tree},\ x\in\partial A,\ m\in\mathsf{monom}[A\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}\mathbold{\varepsilon}]\}\rangle\mathrel{\subseteq}{\mathbb{R}}[S].

By Theorem
5.2
, the condition (
14
) for behavioral equivalence of
\mu
and
\nu
is
J_{\varphi^{-1}(\mu-\nu)}\mathrel{\subseteq}\mathop{\mathrm{ker}}\mathbold{1}
.
By Lemma
6.2
, all
d_{\varepsilon,a}^{\mathbold{\varepsilon}^{n}}
preserve membership in
J_{p}
, since
d_{A\cup\{x\},xa}^{mx^{n}}(p)=d_{\varepsilon,a}^{\mathbold{\varepsilon}^{n}}(d_{A,x}^{m}(p))
by Lemma
4.6
.

Lemma 6.3
.

Suppose
I=\langle B\rangle
is an ideal of
{\mathbb{R}}[S]
containing
p
with basis
B
. If
p\in I
and
d_{\varepsilon,a}^{\mathbold{\varepsilon}^{n}}(b)\in I
for all
a\in\Sigma
,
n\geq 0
, and
b\in B
, then
J_{p}\mathrel{\subseteq}I
.

Proof.

By Lemma
6.2
, all
d_{\varepsilon,a}^{\mathbold{\varepsilon}^{n}}
preserve membership in
I
.
By Lemma
6.1
, for all finite trees
A\mathrel{\subseteq}\Sigma^{*}
, all
x\in\partial A
, and all monomials
m\in\mathsf{monom}[A\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}\mathbold{\varepsilon}]
,
d_{A,x}^{m}
preserves membership in
I
. Since
p\in I
, all
d_{A,x}^{m}(p)\in I
, thus
J_{p}\mathrel{\subseteq}I
.
∎

The algorithm proceeds in stages, constructing a sequence of subsets
B_{0}\mathrel{\subseteq}B_{1}\mathrel{\subseteq}B_{2}\mathrel{\subseteq}\cdots
of
{\mathbb{R}}[S]
, maintaining the following invariants:

1.

p\in\langle B_{n}\rangle
.

2.

B_{n}
is a Gröbner basis for
\langle B_{n}\rangle

[
1
,
8
]
. This allows for efficient membership testing in
\langle B_{n}\rangle

[
9
]
.

3.

B_{n}\mathrel{\subseteq}J_{p}
. This implies that
\langle B_{n}\rangle\mathrel{\subseteq}J_{p}
.

4.

B_{n}\mathrel{\subseteq}\mathop{\mathrm{ker}}\mathbold{1}
. This implies that
\langle B_{n}\rangle\mathrel{\subseteq}\mathop{\mathrm{ker}}\mathbold{1}
.

We start at stage
0
with the seed
p=\varphi_{S}^{-1}(\mu-\nu)
and
B_{0}=\{p\}
. Invariants 1 and 2 are clearly satisfied, and 3 is satisfied since
p=d_{\varnothing,\varepsilon}^{1}(p)
. Invariant 4 may fail, but we can test it by computing
\mathbold{1}(p)
, that is, by evaluating
p
at the point
(1,\ldots,1)
. If the result is nonzero, we halt and declare
\mu
and
\nu
not equivalent, as
p
is a witness for
J_{p}\not\mathrel{\subseteq}\mathop{\mathrm{ker}}\mathbold{1}
. Intuitively, this will happen if for some
k\geq 0
, the probability with respect to
\mu
that
\varepsilon
is accepted with multiplicity
k
differs from the probability of the same event with respect to
\nu
. If
\mathbold{1}(p)=0
, then invariant 4 is satisfied.

Now suppose at stage
n
, we have constructed
B_{n}
satisfying the four invariants. For each
b\in B_{n}
, we compute
d_{\varepsilon,a}^{\mathbold{\varepsilon}^{n}}(b)
for all
a\in\Sigma
and
n\geq 0
. This can be done all at once by computing
\theta(b)
, then applying
(\Sigma\setminus a)\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}\mathbold{1}
for each
a\in\Sigma
to obtain the polynomial
\sum_{n}(a\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}d^{\mathbold{\varepsilon}^{n}}_{\varepsilon,a}(b))\cdot\mathbold{\varepsilon}^{n}
, from which
d_{\varepsilon,a}^{\mathbold{\varepsilon}^{n}}(b)
can be read off. All but finitely many of these are
0
.

For all the nonzero
d_{\varepsilon,a}^{\mathbold{\varepsilon}^{n}}(b)
, we test whether
d_{\varepsilon,a}^{\mathbold{\varepsilon}^{n}}(b)\in\mathop{\mathrm{ker}}\mathbold{1}
by evaluating
\mathbold{1}(d_{\varepsilon,a}^{\mathbold{\varepsilon}^{n}}(b))
. Again, we can do this all at once simply by evaluating
\theta(b)
at
(1,\ldots,1)
. If the result is nonzero, we halt immediately and report
\mu
and
\nu
not equivalent. This happens iff some
\mathbold{1}(d_{\varepsilon,a}^{\mathbold{\varepsilon}^{n}}(b))\neq 0
, which by invariant 3 and the fact that membership in
J_{p}
is preserved by
d_{\varepsilon,a}^{\mathbold{\varepsilon}^{n}}
says that
J_{p}\not\mathrel{\subseteq}\mathop{\mathrm{ker}}\mathbold{1}
.

If this test succeeds, then we have a new finite set
d_{\varepsilon,a}^{\mathbold{\varepsilon}^{n}}(b)\in\mathop{\mathrm{ker}}\mathbold{1}
for
n\geq 0
,
a\in\Sigma
, and
b\in B_{n}
to add to our collection. Two things can happen: either all the new
d_{\varepsilon,a}^{\mathbold{\varepsilon}^{n}}(b)
are already contained in
\langle B_{n}\rangle
, or at least one is not. We test this for each
d_{\varepsilon,a}^{\mathbold{\varepsilon}^{n}}(b)
using Buchberger’s algorithm
[
1
,
8
,
9
]
.

If all
d_{\varepsilon,a}^{\mathbold{\varepsilon}^{n}}(b)\in\langle B_{n}\rangle
, we are done. In this case, we halt and declare
\mu
and
\nu
behaviorally equivalent. This holds because by Lemma
6.3
and invariants 1, 3, and 4,
\langle B_{n}\rangle=J_{p}\mathrel{\subseteq}\mathop{\mathrm{ker}}\mathbold{1}
.

If not all
d_{\varepsilon,a}^{\mathbold{\varepsilon}^{n}}(b)\in\langle B_{n}\rangle
, then we add the new ones to
B_{n}
, recompute a Gröbner basis
B_{n+1}
for this new set of polynomials, and go on to the next stage. The invariants are maintained.

The algorithm must halt after finitely many stages with either success or failure due to the fact that the multivariate polynomial ring
{\mathbb{R}}[S]
is Noetherian, which means there is no infinite proper ascending chain of ideals.

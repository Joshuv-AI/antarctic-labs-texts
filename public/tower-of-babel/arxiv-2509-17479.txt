On a zero mass Schrödinger-Bopp-Podolsky system: ground states, nonexistence results and asymptotic behaviour
Alessio Pomponio, Lianfeng Yang

Abstract
. In this paper, we consider the following zero mass Schrödinger-Bopp-Podolsky system { − Δ ​ u + q 2 ​ ϕ ​ u = | u | p − 2 ​ u , − Δ ​ ϕ + a 2 ​ Δ 2 ​ ϕ = 4 ​ π ​ u 2 , ​ in ​ ℝ 3 , \begin{cases}-\Delta u+q^{2}\phi u=|u|^{p-2}u,\\ -\Delta\phi+a^{2}\Delta^{2}\phi=4\pi u^{2},\end{cases}\text{ in }{\mathbb{R}^{3}}, where a > 0 a>0 and q ≠ 0 q\neq 0 . We complete the study initiated in [ 2 ] , which relied on a perturbation argument to establish the existence of weak solutions. Here, in contrast, our approach, based on the Mountain Pass Theorem and the splitting lemma, directly yields a ground state solution for p ∈ ( 4 , 6 ) p\in(4,6) . Moreover, by deriving a Pohozaev identity, we further obtain some nonexistence results for suitable p p . Finally, based on the minimax characterization, we also analyse, in the radial case, the asymptotic behaviour of the solutions obtained as a → 0 a\to 0 , thereby establishing a link with the zero mass Schrödinger-Poisson system.

1.
Introduction

The Bopp-Podolsky theory, developed independently by Bopp
[
1
]
and Podolsky
[
18
]
, is a second-order gauge theory for the electromagnetic field that addresses the so-called
infinity problem
associated with a point charge in the classical Maxwell theory. Specifically, in Maxwell theory, by the well-known Gauss law (or Poisson equation), the electrostatic potential
\phi
, for a given charge distribution with density
\rho
, satisfies the equation

(1.1)

-\Delta\phi=\rho\quad\text{ in }{\mathbb{R}}^{3}.

If we let
\rho=4\pi\delta_{x_{0}}
with
x_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
, then the fundamental solution of equation (
1.1
) is
\mathcal{C}(x-x_{0})
with
\mathcal{C}(x):=1/|x|
, and therefore the energy of the corresponding electrostatic field is not finite since

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|\nabla\mathcal{C}|^{2}dx=+\infty.

On the other hand, in the Bopp-Podolsky theory, equation (
1.1
) is replaced by

(1.2)

-\Delta\phi+a^{2}\Delta^{2}\phi=\rho\quad\text{ in }{\mathbb{R}}^{3},

where
a>0
can be interpreted as a cut-off distance or can be linked to an effective radius for the electron.
Taking
\rho=4\pi\delta_{x_{0}}
again, the explicit solution of equation (
1.2
) is
\mathcal{K}_{a}(x-x_{0})
, where

\mathcal{K}_{a}(x):=\frac{1-e^{-|x|/a}}{|x|},

and, since

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|\nabla\mathcal{K}_{a}|^{2}dx+a^{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|\Delta\mathcal{K}_{a}|^{2}dx<+\infty,

the energy of the electrostatic field generated by a point charge is finite. Both Bopp and Podolsky were influenced by the work of Yukawa
[
25
]
(see
[
1
, p. 345]
and
[
18
, §4]
) and indeed the
Bopp-Podolsky potential

{\mathcal{K}}_{a}
involves the Coulomb and the Yukawa potentials (see for more details
[
2
, Proposition 2.1]
).

Recently, the authors in
[
3
]
considered the Schrödinger spectrum of a hydrogen atom
when the conventional Coulomb pair energy between a point electron and a point proton
is replaced by its Bopp-Landé-Thomas-Podolsky (BLTP) modification.
They showed that, in order to match experimental Lyman-
\alpha
transition data,
the parameter
a
of the potential
{\mathcal{K}}_{a}
must satisfy

a\lesssim 10^{-18}\penalty\ \mathrm{m}
,
which is consistent with existing bounds on the electron radius.

Moreover, in
[
7
, Section 2]
, d’Avenia and Siciliano coupled a Schrödinger field
\psi=\psi(t,x)
with its electromagnetic field in the Bopp-Podolsky
theory, and, in particular, in the electrostatic case for the standing waves
\psi(t,x)=e^{i\omega t}u(x)
, they first derived and investigated the following system

(1.3)

\begin{cases}-\Delta u+\omega u+q^{2}\phi u=|u|^{p-2}u,\\
-\Delta\phi+a^{2}\Delta^{2}\phi=4\pi u^{2},\end{cases}\text{ in }{\mathbb{R}}^{3}.

From a physical point of view, in (
1.3
),
a>0
, the parameter of the potential
{\mathcal{K}}_{a}
, has dimension of the inverse of mass;
q\neq 0
denotes the coupling constant of the interaction between the particle and its electromagnetic field;
\omega\in{\mathbb{R}}
is the frequency of the standing wave
\psi(t,x)=e^{i\omega t}u(x)
;
\phi
is the electrostatic potential.
Since then, a lot of works related to system (
1.3
) have been carried out. For instance, we refer the reader to the interesting results obtained in
[
8
,
9
,
19
]
for the problem with different constraints, in
[
5
,
15
,
21
]
for critical cases, in
[
23
,
26
]
concerning sign-changing solutions, in
[
6
,
12
,
13
]
for nonlinear Schrödinger-Bopp-Podolsky-Proca system on manifolds,
[
4
,
10
,
22
]
in further different contexts (see also references therein).

In the case
a=0
, system (
1.3
) becomes the well-known Schrödinger-Poisson system

\begin{cases}-\Delta u+\omega u+q^{2}\phi u=|u|^{p-2}u,\\
-\Delta\phi=4\pi u^{2},\end{cases}\text{ in }{\mathbb{R}}^{3}.

In particular, the
zero mass
Schrödinger-Poisson system, namely with
\omega=0
,

(
{\mathcal{P}}_{0}
)

\begin{cases}-\Delta u+q^{2}\phi u=|u|^{p-2}u,\\
-\Delta\phi=4\pi u^{2},\end{cases}\text{ in }{\mathbb{R}^{3}},

has also been extensively studied in
[
14
,
20
]
. First, for
p\in(18/7,3)
, Ruiz in
[
20
]
obtained the existence of a solution to the system (

P
0

) as a minimizer of the associated energy functional

I_{0}(u):=\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{q^{2}}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{u^{2}(x)u^{2}(y)}{|x-y|}dxdy-\frac{1}{p}\|u\|^{p}_{p},

in the functional space

E_{r}:=\left\{u\in D^{1,2}({\mathbb{R}^{3}}):u\text{ is radial and }\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{u^{2}(x)u^{2}(y)}{|x-y|}dxdy<+\infty\right\},

where

D^{1,2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}):=\{u\in L^{6}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}):|\nabla u|\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})\}
endowed with the usual norm
\|\nabla\cdot\|_{2}
.
In
[
14
]
, instead, the authors proved the existence of a ground state and of infinitely many radial bound states for
p\in(3,6)
. For related problems, see also
[
11
,
17
]
.

Motivated by these results, the authors in
[
2
]
studied the existence of weak solutions to the zero mass Schrödinger-Bopp-Podolsky system, given by:

(
{\mathcal{P}}_{a}
)

\begin{cases}-\Delta u+q^{2}\phi u=|u|^{p-2}u,\\
-\Delta\phi+a^{2}\Delta^{2}\phi=4\pi u^{2},\end{cases}\text{ in }{\mathbb{R}}^{3},

where
a>0
,
q\neq 0
, and
p\in(3,6)
. To this end,
Bopp-Podolsky energy
was introduced as

V(f,g):=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}{\mathcal{K}}_{a}(x-y)f(x)g(y)dxdy

for measurable functions
f,g\colon{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\to{\mathbb{R}}
, and the space
{\mathcal{E}}
was defined as the set of functions in
D^{1,2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
with finite Bopp-Podolsky energy:

{\mathcal{E}}:=\left\{u\in D^{1,2}({\mathbb{R}^{3}}):V(u^{2},u^{2})<+\infty\right\}

equipped with the norm

\|u\|_{{\mathcal{E}}}:=\left(\|\nabla u\|^{2}_{2}+V(u^{2},u^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Meanwhile, let
{\mathcal{E}}_{r}
denote the subspace of radial functions in
{\mathcal{E}}
.
In addition, set

\mathcal{A}:=\Big\{\varphi\in D^{1,2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}):\Delta\varphi\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})\Big\}

equipped with the scalar product

\langle\varphi,\psi\rangle_{\mathcal{A}}:=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\nabla\varphi\cdot\nabla\psi dx+a^{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\Delta\varphi\Delta\psi dx.

We denote by
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{A}}
the associated norm.
From
[
7
]
, we know that
C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
is dense in the Hilbert space
{\mathcal{A}}
and

(1.4)

\mathcal{A}\hookrightarrow L^{\tau}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}),\quad\text{ for }\tau\in[6,+\infty].

We also write
{\mathcal{A}}_{r}
for the subspace of radial functions in
{\mathcal{A}}
.

As shown in
[
2
]
(see also Lemma
2.1
), for each
u\in{\mathcal{E}}
,

\phi_{u}(x):=({\mathcal{K}}_{a}*u^{2})(x)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\frac{1-e^{-\frac{|x-y|}{a}}}{|x-y|}u^{2}(y)dy\in{\mathcal{A}}

is the unique weak solution
of

-\Delta\phi_{u}+a^{2}\Delta^{2}\phi_{u}=4\pi u^{2}\qquad\text{in }{\mathbb{R}^{3}}.

So, in order to find a weak solution to system (

P
a

), one need to consider only the nonlocal equation

(1.5)

-\Delta u+q^{2}\phi_{u}u=|u|^{p-2}u\qquad\text{in }{\mathbb{R}^{3}}.

Following
[
2
, Section 5]
, such solutions can be find as critical point of the functional

\displaystyle I_{a}(u)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{q^{2}}{4}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}{\mathcal{K}}_{a}(x-y)u^{2}(x)u^{2}(y)dxdy-\frac{1}{p}\|u\|^{p}_{p},

which is well-defined on
{\mathcal{E}}
for each
p\in[3,6]
, and on
{\mathcal{E}}_{r}
for each
p\in(18/7,6]
.
Namely if
u\in{\mathcal{E}}
is a critical point of
I_{a}
, then it solves (
1.5
) in a weak sense and
(u,\phi_{u})
is a weak solution of system (

P
a

).

Based on a careful analysis on this functional framework, and by applying an approximation procedure that added a small mass term followed by passing to the limit, in
[
2
]
the authors finally proved that there exists a nontrivial weak solution in
{\mathcal{E}}
(or
{\mathcal{E}}_{r}
) to system (

P
a

) for
p\in(3,6)
. However, therein, neither the existence of ground state solutions nor the asymptotic as
a\to 0
are considered. Moreover nonexistence results are also not mentioned.

In this paper, therefore, we aim to complete the study initiated in
[
2
]
focusing on these three aspects.

Our first result concerns the existence of ground state solution to (

P
a

).
Inspired by
[
7
,
14
]
and adopting a different strategy from that in
[
2
]
, by a Mountain Pass approach in combination with a splitting lemma, we obtain the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
q\neq 0
. For any
p\in(4,6)
and
a>0
, there exists a ground state solution in
{\mathcal{E}}
(or
{\mathcal{E}}_{r}
) to system (

P
a

).

Remark 1.2
.

The existence of a ground state solution in the case
p\in(3,4]
remains an open problem due to the limitations of our approach. In particular, the lack of homogeneity of
{\mathcal{K}}_{a}
and the absence of the frequency term
\omega
pose serious challenges to the construction of the mountain-pass structure and the arguments of
[
14
]
do not work.

Our second type of results concerns the nonexistence of nontrivial solutions for system (

P
a

), which is established by using a Nehari-Pohozaev type argument.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
q\neq 0
. For any
p\geq 6
or
p<12/7
, and
a>0
, system (

P
a

) admits no nontrivial solution in
{\mathcal{E}}\times{\mathcal{A}}
.

Observe that our result looks weaker compared with the corresponding one in the positive mass case studied in
[
7
]
, where the nonexistence has been obtained for
p\geq 6
and
p\leq 2
. This is strictly related with the lack of suitable embedding properties of the space
{\mathcal{E}}
and, as explained with more details in Remarks
4.3
and
4.4
, in order to recover the whole case
p\leq 2
, we need to restrict ourself to the radial setting. More precisely we have the following.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
q\neq 0
and
a>0
, system (

P
a

) has no nontrivial solution in
{\mathcal{E}}_{r}\times{\mathcal{A}}_{r}
for all
p\leq 2
.

In the radial setting, moreover, due to the additional informations about the embedding properties of
{\mathcal{E}}_{r}
, something more can be obtained. In particular, if
u
is a ground state solution with energy
c_{a}
, then as shown in Corollary
5.4
, it admits the following minimax characterization:

0<c_{a}=I_{a}(u)=\inf_{v\in{\mathcal{M}}_{a,r}}I_{a}(v)=\inf_{v\in{\mathcal{E}}_{r}\setminus\{0\}}\max_{t>0}I_{a}(t^{2}v(tx)).

Here,
{\mathcal{M}}_{a,r}
denotes a Nehari-Pohozaev type set, see (
5.1
) for a precise definition.

Based on this discovery, now
we can analyse the limiting behaviour of ground state solutions to system (

P
a

) as
a\to 0
, thereby establishing a connection with system (

P
0

).

Theorem 1.5
.

Assume
q\neq 0
,
p\in(4,6)
and
a>0
. Let
(u_{a},\phi_{a})\in{\mathcal{E}}_{r}\times\mathcal{A}_{r}
be a ground state solution to system (

P
a

). Then, as
a\to 0
, one has

u_{a}\rightharpoonup u_{0}\text{ weakly in }D_{r}^{1,2}({\mathbb{R}^{3}})\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{ and }\penalty\ \penalty\ \phi_{a}\rightharpoonup\phi_{0}\text{ weakly in }D_{r}^{1,2}({\mathbb{R}^{3}}),

where
(u_{0},\phi_{0})\in D_{r}^{1,2}({\mathbb{R}^{3}})\times D_{r}^{1,2}({\mathbb{R}^{3}})

solves system (

P
0

) in the following weak sense:

\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{3}}}\nabla\phi_{0}\cdot\nabla\eta dx=4\pi\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{3}}}u_{0}^{2}\eta dx,\penalty\ \penalty\ \forall\eta\in C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}^{3}}),

and

\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{3}}}\nabla u_{0}\cdot\nabla\varphi dx+q^{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{3}}}\phi_{0}u_{0}\varphi dx=\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{3}}}|u_{0}|^{p-2}u_{0}\varphi dx,\penalty\ \penalty\ \forall\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}^{3}}).

Remark 1.6
.

Actually if
(u_{a},\phi_{a})\in{\mathcal{E}}_{r}\times\mathcal{A}_{r}
is a nontrivial weak solution of (

P
a

), with
a>0
, and such that the family
\{I_{a}(u_{a})\}_{a>0}
is bounded,
we get the same result with similar arguments.

3.
Existence of ground state solutions

In this section, we focus on proving Theorem
1.1
, that is, the existence of ground state solutions of system (

P
a

) for the case
p\in(4,6)
. Let us define
M:{\mathcal{E}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
as:

M[u]:=\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}{\mathcal{K}}_{a}(x-y)u^{2}(x)u^{2}(y)dxdy.

Just by taking into account the definitions of
M
and
\|\cdot\|_{{\mathcal{E}}}
, we can easily check that for any
u\in{\mathcal{E}}

(3.1)

\frac{1}{2}\|u\|_{{\mathcal{E}}}^{4}\leq M[u]\leq\|u\|_{{\mathcal{E}}}^{2},\quad\text{ if either }\|u\|_{{\mathcal{E}}}\leq 1\text{ or }M[u]\leq 1.

We now turn our attention to the functional
I_{a}
, and show that it satisfies the geometric properties of the Mountain Pass Theorem.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
p\in(4,6)
. Then we have that

(i)

I_{a}(0)=0
;

(ii)

there exist
\delta,\rho>0
such that
I_{a}(u)>\delta
for all
u\in{\mathcal{E}}
with
\|u\|_{{\mathcal{E}}}=\rho
;

(iii)

there exists
\omega\in{\mathcal{E}}
with
\|\omega\|_{{\mathcal{E}}}>\rho
such that
I_{a}(\omega)<0
.

Proof.

Property (
i
) is trivial.

Moreover, by Lemma
2.5
, it follows that

\displaystyle I_{a}(u)

\displaystyle\geq\min\left\{\frac{1}{2},\frac{q^{2}}{4}\right\}M[u]-\frac{C}{p}\|u\|_{{\mathcal{E}}}^{p},

which, together with (
3.1
), indicates that (
ii
) holds if we take
\|u\|_{{\mathcal{E}}}=\rho>0
small enough.

To prove (
iii
), we let
u_{t}:=tu
with
u\in{\mathcal{E}}
and
t\geq 0
. This yields that

(3.2)

\displaystyle I_{a}(u_{t})

\displaystyle=\frac{t^{2}}{2}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{q^{2}t^{4}}{4}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}{\mathcal{K}}_{a}(x-y)u^{2}(x)u^{2}(y)dxdy-\frac{t^{p}}{p}\|u\|_{p}^{p}.

Therefore, the desired results follow considering
t\to+\infty
.

∎

In virtue of Lemma
3.1
and Mountain Pass Theorem, we define the Mountain Pass level for
I_{a}
as follows:

(3.3)

c_{a}:=\inf_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\max_{t\in[0,1]}I_{a}(\gamma(t))>0,

where
\Gamma:=\{\gamma\in C([0,1],{\mathcal{E}}):\gamma(0)=0,I_{a}(\gamma(1))<0\}
. Then there exists a Palais-Smale (PS) sequence
\{u_{n}\}\subset{\mathcal{E}}
for
I_{a}
at level
c_{a}
such that, as
n\to+\infty
,

(3.4)

I_{a}(u_{n})\to c_{a}\penalty\ \text{ and }\penalty\ I_{a}^{\prime}(u_{n})\to 0.

Our intention is to find a critical point at level
c_{a}
. By the next proposition, this weak solution of system (

P
a

) will be a ground state, namely, a solution with minimal energy among all nontrivial weak solutions.

Proposition 3.2
.

Suppose
p\in(4,6)
and
u\in{\mathcal{E}}\backslash\{0\}
is a weak solution of system (

P
a

). Then it holds

I_{a}(u)\geq c_{a}.

Proof.

Given such solution
u\in{\mathcal{E}}\setminus\{0\}
, let us define again
u_{t}:=tu
with
t\geq 0
, and
\bar{\gamma}:{\mathbb{R}}^{+}\to{\mathcal{E}}
,
\bar{\gamma}(t)=u_{t}
. Clearly,
\bar{\gamma}(0)=0
and
\bar{\gamma}
is a continuous curve in
{\mathcal{E}}
.
Moreover, by (
3.2
), we have that

\displaystyle f(t):=I_{a}(u_{t})=\frac{t^{2}}{2}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{q^{2}t^{4}}{4}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}{\mathcal{K}}_{a}(x-y)u^{2}(x)u^{2}(y)dxdy-\frac{t^{p}}{p}\|u\|_{p}^{p}.

It is easy to check that
f
is of class
C^{1}
and has a unique critical point that corresponds to its maximum by noting the following facts:

(3.5)

\displaystyle f^{\prime}(t)=t\left(\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+q^{2}t^{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}{\mathcal{K}}_{a}(x-y)u^{2}(x)u^{2}(y)dxdy-t^{p-2}\|u\|_{p}^{p}\right)=:tg(t),

and

\displaystyle g^{\prime}(t)=t\left(2q^{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}{\mathcal{K}}_{a}(x-y)u^{2}(x)u^{2}(y)dxdy-(p-2)t^{p-4}\|u\|_{p}^{p}\right).

Recalling now that
u
is a nontrivial weak solution (i.e.,
I_{a}^{\prime}(u)[u]=0
),
we deduce from (
3.5
) and Lemma
2.9
that

f^{\prime}(1)=\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+q^{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}{\mathcal{K}}_{a}(x-y)u^{2}(x)u^{2}(y)dxdy-\|u\|_{p}^{p}=I_{a}^{\prime}(u)[u]=0.

Therefore,

(3.6)

\max_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}^{+}}I_{a}(\bar{\gamma}(t))=\max_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}^{+}}I_{a}(u_{t})=I_{a}(u).

On the other hand, being
\lim\limits_{t\to+\infty}f(t)=-\infty
, we can take a constant
T_{0}>0
such that

I_{a}(\bar{\gamma}(T_{0}))<0.

Reparametrizing
\gamma:[0,1]\to{\mathcal{E}}
by
\gamma(t):=\bar{\gamma}(T_{0}t)
, we obtain that
\gamma\in\Gamma
. Thus, we derive from (
3.3
) and (
3.6
) that

c_{a}\leq\max_{t\in[0,1]}I_{a}(\gamma(t))=\max_{t\in[0,1]}I_{a}(\bar{\gamma}(T_{0}t))\leq\max_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}^{+}}I_{a}(\bar{\gamma}(t))=I_{a}(u).

This completes the proof.

∎

In addition, we derive the following estimates concerning the nontrivial weak solutions of system (

P
a

).

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
p\in(4,6)
. There exists a constant
C>0
such that

M[u]\geq C,

for any
u\in{\mathcal{E}}
nontrivial weak solution of system (

P
a

).

Proof.

Let
u\in{\mathcal{E}}
be a nontrivial weak solution of system (

P
a

). Without loss of generality we can suppose that
M[u]\leq 1
. Hence by (
3.1
) and Lemma
2.5
, we have

\displaystyle\min\{1,q^{2}\}M[u]\leq\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+q^{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}{\mathcal{K}}_{a}(x-y)u^{2}(x)u^{2}(y)dxdy=\|u\|_{p}^{p}\leq C\|u\|_{{\mathcal{E}}}^{p},

which concludes the proof.

∎

The next lemma describes the behaviour of (PS) sequences.

Lemma 3.4
.

For any
p\in(4,6)
, let
\{u_{n}\}\subset{\mathcal{E}}
be a (PS) sequence for
I_{a}
at level
c
. Then the sequence
\{u_{n}\}
is bounded in
{\mathcal{E}}
, and its weak limit in
{\mathcal{E}}
is a critical point of
I_{a}
.

Proof.

Observe that

\displaystyle p(c+1)+\|u_{n}\|_{{\mathcal{E}}}

\displaystyle\geq pI_{a}(u_{n})-I_{a}^{\prime}(u_{n})[u_{n}]

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{p}{2}-1\right)\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{2}^{2}+\left(\frac{p}{4}-1\right)q^{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}{\mathcal{K}}_{a}(x-y)u_{n}^{2}(x)u_{n}^{2}(y)dxdy,

which, together with the definition of the norm in
{\mathcal{E}}
, implies that
\{u_{n}\}
is bounded in
{\mathcal{E}}
.

Recalling that
{\mathcal{E}}
is a reflexive Banach space in view of Lemma
2.2
, there exists
v_{0}\in{\mathcal{E}}
such that, up to a subsequence, as
n\to+\infty
, we have that
u_{n}\rightharpoonup v_{0}
weakly in
{\mathcal{E}}
, and
u_{n}\to v_{0}
strongly in

for all

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\nabla u_{n}\nabla\psi dx\to\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\nabla v_{0}\nabla\psi dx,\quad\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|u_{n}|^{p-2}u_{n}\psi dx\to\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|v_{0}|^{p-2}v_{0}\psi dx.

Moreover, by using Lemma
2.11
, there holds

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\phi_{u_{n}}u_{n}\psi dx\to\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\phi_{v_{0}}v_{0}\psi dx.

Consequently, as
n\to+\infty
, one can see that

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leftarrow I_{a}^{\prime}(u_{n})[\psi]+o_{n}(1)

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\nabla u_{n}\nabla\psi dx+q^{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\phi_{u_{n}}u_{n}\psi dx-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|u_{n}|^{p-2}u_{n}\psi dx+o_{n}(1)

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\nabla v_{0}\nabla\psi dx+q^{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\phi_{v_{0}}v_{0}\psi dx-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|v_{0}|^{p-2}v_{0}\psi dx=I_{a}^{\prime}(v_{0})[\psi],

that is,

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\nabla v_{0}\nabla\psi dx+q^{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\phi_{v_{0}}v_{0}\psi dx=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|v_{0}|^{p-2}v_{0}\psi dx.

By Lemma
2.3
, the previous identity holds also for every

and so

is a critical point for
I_{a}
.

∎

As a consequence of the second part of Lemma

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
\{u_{n}\}\subset{\mathcal{E}}
be a sequence such that

weakly in
{\mathcal{E}}

Building on Lemma

and Lemma

, we are now ready to prove the Splitting lemma, which analyzes the behavior of (PS) sequences and plays a crucial role in recovering compactness. For this purpose, we define the functional
{M}_{q}:{\mathcal{E}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
by

{M}_{q}[u]:=\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+q^{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}{\mathcal{K}}_{a}(x-y)u^{2}(x)u^{2}(y)dxdy.

Then we obtain the following result.

Lemma 3.6

(Splitting lemma)
.

Let
p\in(4,6)
and
\{u_{n}\}\subset{\mathcal{E}}
be a (PS) sequence of
I_{a}
at a certain level
c>0
.
Then, up to subsequences, there exist
k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
and a finite sequence

(v_{0},v_{1},\dots,v_{k})\subset{\mathcal{E}},\quad v_{i}\not\equiv 0\text{ for }i\geq 0

of weak solutions

-\Delta u+q^{2}\phi_{u}u=|u|^{p-2}u\qquad\text{in }{\mathbb{R}^{3}},

and
k
sequences
\{\xi_{n}^{1}\},\dots,\{\xi_{n}^{k}\}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
, such that, as

,

\left\|u_{n}-v_{0}-\sum_{i=1}^{k}v_{i}(\cdot-\xi_{n}^{i})\right\|_{{\mathcal{E}}}\to 0;

|\xi_{n}^{i}|\to+\infty,\quad|\xi_{n}^{i}-\xi_{n}^{j}|\to+\infty,\quad i\neq j;

\sum_{i=0}^{k}I_{a}(v_{i})=c,\quad{M}_{q}[u_{n}]\to\sum_{i=0}^{k}{M}_{q}[v_{i}].

Proof.

The proof is carried out in the following steps.

Step 1.
Passing to a subsequence if necessary and applying Lemma
3.4
, we deduce that there exists
v_{0}\in{\mathcal{E}}
such that

u_{n}\rightharpoonup v_{0}\text{ weakly in }{\mathcal{E}}\text{ as }n\to+\infty,

and

(3.7)

I_{a}^{\prime}(v_{0})=0.

Set

u_{n,1}:=u_{n}-v_{0}.

We claim now that

(3.8)

I_{a}(u_{n})-I_{a}(u_{n,1})\to I_{a}(v_{0}),\quad{M}_{q}[u_{n}]-{M}_{q}[u_{n,1}]\to{M}_{q}[v_{0}].

By using the weak convergence in
D^{1,2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
, the well-known Brezis-Lieb lemma and Lemma
2.11
, we obtain that

\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{2}^{2}-\|\nabla(u_{n}-v_{0})\|_{2}^{2}\to\|\nabla v_{0}\|_{2}^{2},\quad\|u_{n}\|_{p}^{p}-\|u_{n}-v_{0}\|_{p}^{p}\to\|v_{0}\|_{p}^{p},

and

\displaystyle T(u_{n,1},u_{n,1},u_{n,1},u_{n,1})

\displaystyle=T(u_{n},u_{n}-v_{0},u_{n}-v_{0},u_{n}-v_{0})-T(v_{0},u_{n}-v_{0},u_{n}-v_{0},u_{n}-v_{0})

\displaystyle=T(u_{n},u_{n}-v_{0},u_{n}-v_{0},u_{n}-v_{0})+o_{n}(1)

\displaystyle=T(u_{n},u_{n},u_{n}-v_{0},u_{n}-v_{0})+o_{n}(1)

\displaystyle=T(u_{n},u_{n},u_{n},u_{n}-v_{0})+o_{n}(1)

\displaystyle=T(u_{n},u_{n},u_{n},u_{n})-T(v_{0},v_{0},v_{0},v_{0})+o_{n}(1),

which, combined with the definitions of
I_{a}
and
{M}_{q}
, indicates that the claim (
3.8
) holds.

If
u_{n,1}\to 0
in
{\mathcal{E}}
, then the proof is concluded. To be specific, in this case, we have
I_{a}(u_{n,1})\to 0
and
{M}_{q}[u_{n,1}]\to 0
. Thus, by (
3.8
), it holds

0<c=I_{a}(u_{n})+o_{n}(1)\to I_{a}(v_{0}),\quad{M}_{q}[u_{n}]\to{M}_{q}[v_{0}].

Furthermore, applying (
3.7
) and the fact
I_{a}(v_{0})>0
, it follows that
v_{0}\not\equiv 0
is a nontrivial weak solution of (
1.5
).

Suppose now that
u_{n,1}\not\to 0
in
{\mathcal{E}}
.
Recalling that
\{u_{n}\}\subset{\mathcal{E}}
is a (PS) sequence for
I_{a}
, we derive from (
3.7
) that

(3.9)

{M}_{q}[u_{n}]-\|u_{n}\|_{p}^{p}=I_{a}^{\prime}(u_{n})[u_{n}]\to 0=I_{a}^{\prime}(v_{0})[v_{0}]={M}_{q}[v_{0}]-\|v_{0}\|_{p}^{p}.

Noting that
u_{n}\rightharpoonup v_{0}
weakly in
{\mathcal{E}}
, together with Lemma
2.4
(
i
), we get

(3.10)

u_{n}\rightharpoonup v_{0}\text{ weakly in }D^{1,2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})\penalty\ \text{ and }\penalty\ \phi_{u_{n}}\rightharpoonup\phi_{v_{0}}\text{ weakly in }\mathcal{A}.

Since
u_{n}\not\to v_{0}
in
{\mathcal{E}}
, (
3.8
) shows that at least one of the convergences in (
3.10
) is not strong convergence. Combined with Lemma
2.1
, up to a subsequence, this yields that

\displaystyle{M}_{q}[u_{n}]+o_{n}(1)

\displaystyle=\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{2}^{2}+q^{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}{\mathcal{K}}_{a}(x-y)u_{n}^{2}(x)u_{n}^{2}(y)dxdy+o_{n}(1)

\displaystyle=\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{q^{2}}{4\pi}\|\phi_{u_{n}}\|_{{\mathcal{A}}}^{2}+o_{n}(1)

\displaystyle>\|\nabla v_{0}\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{q^{2}}{4\pi}\|\phi_{v_{0}}\|_{{\mathcal{A}}}^{2}={M}_{q}[v_{0}].

Combining this with (

), we conclude that
u_{n}\not\to v_{0}
in

, that is,

u_{n,1}\not\to 0\text{ in }L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}).

By Lemma
2.12
, for any

, we conclude that there exist
\delta_{1}>0
and
\{\xi_{n}^{1}\}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{3}

(3.11)

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\int_{B_{1}(\xi_{n}^{1})}|u_{n,1}(x)|^{q}dx=\lim_{n\to+\infty}\int_{B_{1}(0)}|u_{n,1}(x+\xi_{n}^{1})|^{q}dx\geq\delta_{1}>0.

Since
u_{n,1}\rightharpoonup 0
weakly in
{\mathcal{E}}
, then
u_{n,1}\to 0
in

for all

.

Step 2.
We now turn our attention to the sequence
\{u_{n,1}(\cdot+\xi_{n}^{1})\}
. Clearly, it is a (PS) sequence at level

by applying (

) and Corollary

. Going if necessary to a subsequence, we may assume that

(3.12)

u_{n,1}(\cdot+\xi_{n}^{1})\rightharpoonup v_{1}\text{ weakly in }{\mathcal{E}}.

It then follows from Lemma
3.4
and (
3.11

v_{1}\not\equiv 0
is a nontrivial weak solution of (
1.5
).

Define
u_{n,2}:=u_{n,1}-v_{1}(\cdot-\xi_{n}^{1}).

Arguing as in Step 1 and in view of (
3.8
), one has

(3.13)

I_{a}(u_{n,2})=I_{a}(u_{n,1})-I_{a}(v_{1})+o_{n}(1)=I_{a}(u_{n})-I_{a}(v_{0})-I_{a}(v_{1})+o_{n}(1),

and

Furthermore, one can see that
u_{n,2}=u_{n,1}-v_{1}(\cdot-\xi_{n}^{1})\rightharpoonup 0

{\mathcal{E}}
since both summands converge weakly to zero, and

If

{\mathcal{E}}

\delta_{2}>0
and
\{\xi_{n}^{2}\}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
, such that

(3.14)

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\int_{B_{1}(\xi_{n}^{2})}|u_{n,2}(x)|^{q}dx=\lim_{n\to+\infty}\int_{B_{1}(0)}|u_{n,2}(x+\xi_{n}^{2})|^{q}dx\geq\delta_{2}>0.

Recalling that
u_{n,2}\rightharpoonup 0
weakly in
{\mathcal{E}}
and
u_{n,2}(\cdot+\xi_{n}^{1})\rightharpoonup 0
weakly in
{\mathcal{E}}
, it then follows from (

|\xi_{n}^{2}|\to+\infty\penalty\ \text{ and }\penalty\ |\xi_{n}^{2}-\xi_{n}^{1}|\to+\infty.

Furthermore, up to subsequences, we derive from (
3.13
), (

) and Lemma

that

u_{n,2}(\cdot+\xi_{n}^{2})\rightharpoonup v_{2}\not\equiv 0\text{ weakly in }{\mathcal{E}},

where
v_{2}\not\equiv 0
is a nontrivial weak solution of
-\Delta u+q^{2}\phi_{u}u=|u|^{p-2}u
.
Let

u_{n,3}:=u_{n,2}-v_{2}(\cdot-\xi_{n}^{2}).

Step 3.
Iterating the above procedure, we construct sequences
\{u_{n,j}\}_{j=1,2,\dots}
and
\{\xi_{n}^{j}\}_{j=1,2,\dots}

∎

Finally, with the help of the above lemmas, we proceed to the proof of Theorem

Proof of Theorem

4.
Pohozaev identity and nonexistence results

This section is devoted to establishing certain nonexistence results.
To this end, we begin studying the regularity of solutions of system (

P
a

).

Lemma 4.1
.

Suppose
p\in[3,6)
and
(u,\phi_{u})\in{\mathcal{E}}\times{\mathcal{A}}
is a nontrivial weak solution of system (

P
a

), then
(u,\phi_{u})\in C_{\rm loc}^{2,\sigma}({\mathbb{R}^{3}})\times C_{\rm loc}^{4,\sigma}({\mathbb{R}^{3}})
, for
\sigma\in(0,1/2]
.

Proof.

Recalling the proof of
[
24
, Lemma 1.30]
, we know that
if
-\Delta u=bu
with
b\in L^{3/2}_{\rm loc}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
, then
u\in C^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
.

In our case, let
b:=|u|^{p-2}-q^{2}\phi_{u}
. Then, by (
1.4
) and Lemma
2.5
, one can see that

\phi_{u}\in{\mathcal{A}}\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}),

and

|u|^{p-2}\in L^{6/(p-2)}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}),\text{ where }3/2<6/(p-2).

Clearly,
b\in L^{3/2}_{\rm loc}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
. By applying an analogous argument as in
[
7
,
A.1. Regularity of the solutions
]
, we deduce that the weak solutions obtained are indeed classical.

∎

Moreover the following Nehari and Pohozaev type identities hold.

Lemma 4.2
.

Suppose
p\in[3,6)
and
(u,\phi_{u})\in{\mathcal{E}}\times{\mathcal{A}}
is a nontrivial weak solution of system (

P
a

). Then we have that

\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+q^{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{3}}}\phi_{u}u^{2}dx=\|u\|_{p}^{p}\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{ and }\penalty\ \penalty\ \|\nabla\phi_{u}\|_{2}^{2}+a^{2}\|\Delta\phi_{u}\|_{2}^{2}=4\pi\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{3}}}\phi_{u}u^{2}dx,

which are usually called Nehari identities. Furthermore,
(u,\phi_{u})
satisfies also the following Pohozaev identity

-\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{q^{2}}{16\pi}\|\nabla\phi_{u}\|_{2}^{2}-\frac{q^{2}a^{2}}{16\pi}\|\Delta\phi_{u}\|_{2}^{2}-\frac{3q^{2}}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{3}}}\phi_{u}u^{2}dx+\frac{3}{p}\|u\|_{p}^{p}=0.

Proof.

Multiplying the first equation of (

P
a

) by
u
and the second equation by
\phi_{u}
, and integrating on
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
, we obtain Nehari identities.

Subsequently, by Lemma
4.1
and following the reasoning in
[
7
,
A.3. The Pohozaev identity
]
, we establish the Pohozaev identity.

∎

Remark 4.3
.

In particular, for the radial space
{\mathcal{E}}_{r}
, the previously mentioned Pohozaev identity can be written as

(4.1)

P_{a}(u):=-\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}-\frac{q^{2}}{4a}\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{3}}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{3}}}\left[\frac{5(1-e^{-\frac{|x-y|}{a}})}{|x-y|/a}+e^{-\frac{|x-y|}{a}}\right]u^{2}(x)u^{2}(y)dxdy+\frac{3}{p}\|u\|_{p}^{p}=0,

which will play a crucial role in the asymptotic behaviour discussed in Section
5
.
Indeed, to be specific, for any
u\in{\mathcal{E}}_{r}
, using Lemma
2.6

(
{\mathcal{E}}_{r}\hookrightarrow L^{\tau}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
with
\tau\in(18/7,6]
) and recalling the proof of equality (A.3) in
[
7
]
, we obtain that

(4.2)

\|\Delta\phi_{u}\|_{2}^{2}=\frac{2\pi}{a^{3}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{3}}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{3}}}e^{-\frac{|x-y|}{a}}u^{2}(x)u^{2}(y)dxdy,

which, together with the above Nehari identities, yields the desired result.

Remark 4.4
.

Observe that in the positive mass case, in
[
7
]
, the authors do not need to consider just the radial setting to obtain the corresponding version of Pohozaev identity (
4.1
) which is, actually, satisfied also in
H^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{3}})
. In our case, instead, since the embeddings of
{\mathcal{E}}
into
L^{\tau}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
with
\tau\in[2,3)
are not known, in order to prove (
4.2
), the radiality seems to be somehow necessary.

We now turn to establishing nonexistence results for system (

P
a

).

The proof of Theorem
1.3
.

If
(u,\phi_{u})\in{\mathcal{E}}\times{\mathcal{A}}
is a nontrivial solution of system (

P
a

), then we deduce from Lemma
4.2
that

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{q^{2}}{16\pi}\|\nabla\phi_{u}\|_{2}^{2}-\frac{q^{2}a^{2}}{16\pi}\|\Delta\phi_{u}\|_{2}^{2}-\frac{3q^{2}}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{3}}}\phi_{u}u^{2}dx+\frac{3}{p}\|u\|_{p}^{p}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{q^{2}}{16\pi}\left(4\pi\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{3}}}\phi_{u}u^{2}dx-a^{2}\|\Delta\phi_{u}\|_{2}^{2}\right)-\frac{q^{2}a^{2}}{16\pi}\|\Delta\phi_{u}\|_{2}^{2}-\frac{3q^{2}}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{3}}}\phi_{u}u^{2}dx

\displaystyle\penalty\penalty\penalty\penalty\displaystyle+\frac{3}{p}\left(\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+q^{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{3}}}\phi_{u}u^{2}dx\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{6-p}{2p}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{q^{2}(12-5p)}{4p}\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{3}}}\phi_{u}u^{2}dx-\frac{q^{2}a^{2}}{8\pi}\|\Delta\phi_{u}\|_{2}^{2}.

So, in the case
p\geq 6
,

0=\frac{6-p}{2p}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{q^{2}(12-5p)}{4p}\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{3}}}\phi_{u}u^{2}dx-\frac{q^{2}a^{2}}{8\pi}\|\Delta\phi_{u}\|_{2}^{2}<0,

which yields a contradiction.

On the other hand, by using Nehari identities, if
p<12/7
, one has

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\frac{6-p}{2p}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{q^{2}(12-5p)}{4p}\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{3}}}\phi_{u}u^{2}dx-\frac{q^{2}a^{2}}{8\pi}\|\Delta\phi_{u}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{6-p}{2p}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{q^{2}(12-5p)}{4p}\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{3}}}\phi_{u}u^{2}dx-\frac{q^{2}}{8\pi}\left(4\pi\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{3}}}\phi_{u}u^{2}dx-\|\nabla\phi_{u}\|_{2}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\geq q^{2}\left(\frac{12-5p}{4p}-\frac{1}{2}\right)\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{3}}}\phi_{u}u^{2}dx>0,

obtaining again a contradiction.
∎

Now we can pass to prove the nonexistence result in the radial case.

The proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Let
(v,\phi_{v})\in{\mathcal{E}}_{r}\times{\mathcal{A}}_{r}
be a nontrivial solution of system (

P
a

). Then, for the case
p\leq 2
, it follows from Lemma
4.2
and Remark
4.3
that

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\|\nabla v\|_{2}^{2}+q^{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{3}}}\phi_{v}v^{2}dx-\|v\|_{p}^{p}

\displaystyle=\|\nabla v\|_{2}^{2}+q^{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{3}}}\phi_{v}v^{2}dx

\displaystyle\penalty\penalty\penalty\penalty\displaystyle+\frac{p}{3}\left\{-\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla v\|_{2}^{2}-\frac{q^{2}}{4a}\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{3}}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{3}}}\left[\frac{5(1-e^{-\frac{|x-y|}{a}})}{|x-y|/a}+e^{-\frac{|x-y|}{a}}\right]v^{2}(x)v^{2}(y)dxdy\right\}

\displaystyle=\left(1-\frac{p}{6}\right)\|\nabla v\|_{2}^{2}+q^{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{3}}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{3}}}\left[\left(\frac{1}{a}-\frac{5p}{12a}\right)\frac{(1-e^{-\frac{|x-y|}{a}})}{|x-y|/a}-\frac{p}{12a}e^{-\frac{|x-y|}{a}}\right]v^{2}(x)v^{2}(y)dxdy

\displaystyle\geq\frac{2}{3}\|\nabla v\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{q^{2}}{6a}\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{3}}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{3}}}\left[\frac{1-e^{-\frac{|x-y|}{a}}}{|x-y|/a}-e^{-\frac{|x-y|}{a}}\right]v^{2}(x)v^{2}(y)dxdy>0,

since the function in the parenthesis is positive. This establishes the desired result.
∎

5.
The behaviour as
a\to 0
in the radial case

In this section we focus on proving Theorem
1.5
, concerning the asymptotic behaviour of nontrivial weak solutions to system (

P
a

) as
a\to 0
, in the radial setting.

Following Lemma
4.2
and Remark
4.3
, we define the Nehari-Pohozaev set

(5.1)

\mathcal{M}_{a,r}:=\Big\{u\in{\mathcal{E}}_{r}\setminus\{0\}:J_{a}(u)=0\Big\},

where

(5.2)

\begin{split}J_{a}(u):=&\penalty\ 2I_{a}^{\prime}(u)[u]+P_{a}(u)\\
=&\penalty\ \frac{3}{2}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}-\frac{2p-3}{p}\|u\|_{p}^{p}+\frac{3q^{2}}{4}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\frac{1-e^{-\frac{|x-y|}{a}}-\frac{|x-y|}{3a}e^{-\frac{|x-y|}{a}}}{|x-y|}u^{2}(x)u^{2}(y)dxdy.\end{split}

Now, we present a key inequality.

Lemma 5.1
.

Assume that
p\in(4,6)
. For each
t>0
, one has

(5.3)

I_{a}(u)-I_{a}(t^{2}u(tx))\geq\frac{1-t^{3}}{3}J_{a}(u),\penalty\ \penalty\ \forall u\in{\mathcal{E}}_{r}.

Proof.

Noting that

(5.4)

\displaystyle I_{a}(t^{2}u(tx))=\frac{t^{3}}{2}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{q^{2}t^{3}}{4}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\frac{1-e^{-\frac{|x-y|}{at}}}{|x-y|}u^{2}(x)u^{2}(y)dxdy-\frac{t^{2p-3}}{p}\|u\|^{p}_{p},

it thus follows from (
5.2
) that

(5.5)

\displaystyle I_{a}(u)-I_{a}(t^{2}u(tx))

\displaystyle=\frac{1-t^{3}}{2}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{t^{2p-3}-1}{p}\|u\|^{p}_{p}+\frac{q^{2}}{4}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\frac{1-e^{-\frac{|x-y|}{a}}-t^{3}(1-e^{-\frac{|x-y|}{at}})}{|x-y|}u^{2}(x)u^{2}(y)dxdy

\displaystyle=\frac{1-t^{3}}{3}J_{a}(u)+\frac{1}{p}\|u\|_{p}^{p}\left[\frac{(1-t^{3})(2p-3)}{3}+(t^{2p-3}-1)\right]

\displaystyle\penalty\penalty\penalty\penalty\displaystyle-\frac{q^{2}}{4}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\frac{(1-t^{3})\left(1-e^{-\frac{|x-y|}{a}}-\frac{|x-y|}{3a}e^{-\frac{|x-y|}{a}}\right)}{|x-y|}u^{2}(x)u^{2}(y)dxdy

\displaystyle\penalty\penalty\penalty\penalty\displaystyle+\frac{q^{2}}{4}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\frac{1-e^{-\frac{|x-y|}{a}}-t^{3}(1-e^{-\frac{|x-y|}{at}})}{|x-y|}u^{2}(x)u^{2}(y)dxdy

\displaystyle=\frac{1-t^{3}}{3}J_{a}(u)+\frac{1}{p}\|u\|_{p}^{p}\left[\frac{(1-t^{3})(2p-3)}{3}+(t^{2p-3}-1)\right]

\displaystyle\penalty\penalty\penalty\penalty\displaystyle+\frac{q^{2}}{4}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\frac{t^{3}\left(e^{-\frac{|x-y|}{at}}-e^{-\frac{|x-y|}{a}}\right)+(1-t^{3})\frac{|x-y|}{3a}e^{-\frac{|x-y|}{a}}}{|x-y|}u^{2}(x)u^{2}(y)dxdy.

Let

g(t):=\frac{(1-t^{3})(2p-3)}{3}+t^{2p-3}-1
for any
t>0
. A simple calculation shows that

g(t)\geq g(1)=0,

which, combined with (
2.1
) and (
5.5
), implies that

I_{a}(u)-I_{a}(t^{2}u(tx))\geq\frac{1-t^{3}}{3}J_{a}(u).

∎

The following corollary follows directly from Lemma
5.1
.

Corollary 5.2
.

For
p\in(4,6)
and any
u\in\mathcal{M}_{a,r}
, it holds that

I_{a}(u)=\max_{t>0}I_{a}(t^{2}u(tx)).

We proceed to establish a preliminary result that will play a key role in the analysis leading to Theorem
1.5
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
p\in(4,6)
. Then for any
u\in{\mathcal{E}}_{r}\setminus\{0\}
, there exists a unique
t_{u}>0
such that
t_{u}^{2}u(t_{u}\cdot)\in\mathcal{M}_{a,r}
.

Proof.

Fix
u\in{\mathcal{E}}_{r}\setminus\{0\}
and define
\zeta(t):=I_{a}(t^{2}u(tx))
on
(0,+\infty)
. Clearly, it follows from (
5.2
) and (
5.4
) that

(5.6)

\displaystyle\zeta^{\prime}(t)=0\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\frac{3t^{2}}{2}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{3q^{2}t^{2}}{4}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\frac{1-e^{-\frac{|x-y|}{at}}}{|x-y|}u^{2}(x)u^{2}(y)dxdy

\displaystyle-\frac{q^{2}t}{4a}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}e^{-\frac{|x-y|}{at}}u^{2}(x)u^{2}(y)dxdy-\frac{(2p-3)t^{2p-4}}{p}\|u\|^{p}_{p}=0

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\frac{1}{t}J_{a}(t^{2}u(tx))=0

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle t^{2}u(tx)\in\mathcal{M}_{a,r}.

One easily checks that
\lim\limits_{t\to 0^{+}}\zeta(t)=0
,
\zeta(t)>0
for sufficiently small
t>0
, and
\zeta(t)<0
for large
t
. Therefore, there exists
t_{u}>0
such that
\zeta(t_{u})=\max\limits_{t>0}\zeta(t)
, which means that

\zeta^{\prime}(t_{u})=0\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{ and }\penalty\ \penalty\ t_{u}^{2}u(t_{u}x)\in\mathcal{M}_{a,r}.

Finally, we claim that
t_{u}
is unique. Indeed, for any given
u\in{\mathcal{E}}_{r}\setminus\{0\}
, suppose that
t_{1},t_{2}>0
satisfy
\zeta^{\prime}(t_{1})=\zeta^{\prime}(t_{2})=0
.
Then we deduce from (
5.6
) that

J_{a}(t_{1}^{2}u(t_{1}x))=J_{a}(t_{2}^{2}u(t_{2}x))=0.

Combining this with (
5.3
), one has

I_{a}(t_{1}^{2}u(t_{1}x))\geq I_{a}(t_{2}^{2}u(t_{2}x))\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{ and }\penalty\ \penalty\ I_{a}(t_{2}^{2}u(t_{2}x))\geq I_{a}(t_{1}^{2}u(t_{1}x)),

which implies that
t_{1}=t_{2}
.

Therefore, we complete the proof.

∎

As an immediate consequence of Proposition
3.2
, Corollary
5.2
and Lemma
5.3
, we obtain the following minimax property.

Corollary 5.4
.

Let
p\in(4,6)
, then it holds

0<c_{a}=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{M}_{a,r}}I_{a}(u)=\inf_{u\in{\mathcal{E}}_{r}\setminus\{0\}}\max_{t>0}I_{a}(t^{2}u(tx)).

Next, we start to prove Theorem
1.5
.

The proof of Theorem
1.5
.

Let us denote by
c_{0}
the corresponding ground state energy of system (

P
0

) for
p\in(4,6)
. Arguing as above we can show that

0<c_{0}=\inf_{v\in\mathcal{M}_{0,r}}I_{0}(v)=\inf_{v\in E_{r}\setminus\{0\}}\max_{t>0}I_{0}(t^{2}v(tx)),

where
{\mathcal{M}}_{0,r}
is the Nehari-Pohozaev set related to
I_{0}
. According to
[
14
, Theorem 1.1]
, there exists
u\in E_{r}\setminus\{0\}
a ground state of system (

P
0

) for
p\in(4,6)
.
Since
u\in E_{r}\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{E}}_{r}
, it follows from Lemma
5.3
that there exists a unique value
t_{u}>0
such that
t_{u}^{2}u(t_{u}\cdot)\in\mathcal{M}_{a,r}
.
Therefore

(5.7)

c_{0}=I_{0}(u)\geq I_{0}(t_{u}^{2}u(t_{u}x))\geq I_{a}(t_{u}^{2}u(t_{u}x))\geq c_{a}.

From now on, we assume that
\{(u_{a},\phi_{a})\}_{a>0}
in
{\mathcal{E}}_{r}\times\mathcal{A}_{r}
is the family of ground state solutions of system (

P
a

), where we are using the notation
\phi_{a}:=\phi_{u_{a}}
. Then, by Corollary
5.4
, we have

J_{a}(u_{a})=0\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{ and }\penalty\ \penalty\ I_{a}(u_{a})=c_{a}.

Thus, it yields

(5.8)

\displaystyle c_{a}

\displaystyle=I_{a}(u_{a})-\frac{1}{2p-3}J_{a}(u_{a})

\displaystyle=\frac{p-3}{2p-3}\|\nabla u_{a}\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{q^{2}(p-3)}{2(2p-3)}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}{\mathcal{K}}_{a}(x-y)u_{a}^{2}(x)u_{a}^{2}(y)dxdy

\displaystyle\penalty\penalty\penalty\penalty\displaystyle+\frac{q^{2}}{4a(2p-3)}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}e^{-{\frac{|x-y|}{a}}}u_{a}^{2}(x)u_{a}^{2}(y)dxdy,

and so (
5.7
) gives that the family
\{u_{a}\}_{a>0}
is bounded in
D_{r}^{1,2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
. Consequently, there exists
u_{0}\in D_{r}^{1,2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
such that, up to subsequences, as
a\to 0
,

u_{a}\rightharpoonup u_{0}\text{ weakly in }D_{r}^{1,2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}),

and

(5.9)

u_{a}\to u_{0}\text{ strongly in }L_{\rm loc}^{\tau}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}),\forall\tau\in[1,6).

Furthermore, owing to the fact that
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}{\mathcal{K}}_{a}(x-y)u_{a}^{2}(x)u_{a}^{2}(y)dxdy\leq c_{a}\leq c_{0}
obtained in (
5.8
), it follows from Lemma
2.1
that

\|\nabla\phi_{a}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\|\nabla\phi_{a}\|_{2}^{2}+a^{2}\|\Delta\phi_{a}\|_{2}^{2}=4\pi\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}{\mathcal{K}}_{a}(x-y)u_{a}^{2}(x)u_{a}^{2}(y)dxdy\leq C.

Then also
\{\phi_{a}\}_{a>0}
is bounded in
D_{r}^{1,2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
and there exists
\phi_{0}\in D_{r}^{1,2}({\mathbb{R}^{3}})
such that

(5.10)

\phi_{a}\rightharpoonup\phi_{0}\text{ weakly in }D_{r}^{1,2}({\mathbb{R}^{3}}),\text{ as }a\to 0.

Passing to the limit as
a\to 0
in the identity

\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{3}}}\nabla\phi_{a}\nabla\eta dx+a^{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{3}}}\Delta\phi_{a}\Delta\eta dx=\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{3}}}4\pi u_{a}^{2}\eta dx,\penalty\ \penalty\ \eta\in C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}),

and using that

\left|a^{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{3}}}\Delta\phi_{a}\Delta\eta dx\right|\leq a\|a\Delta\phi_{a}\|_{2}\|\Delta\eta\|_{2}\leq aC\to 0,

we thus get

\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{3}}}\nabla\phi_{0}\cdot\nabla\eta dx=\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{3}}}4\pi u_{0}^{2}\eta dx,\penalty\ \penalty\ \eta\in C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}).

Set
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
with
\Omega:={\rm supp}(\varphi)
. We know that

(5.11)

\int_{\Omega}\nabla u_{a}\cdot\nabla\varphi dx+q^{2}\int_{\Omega}\phi_{a}u_{a}\varphi dx=\int_{\Omega}|u_{a}|^{p-2}u_{a}\varphi dx.

Applying Hölder inequality and (
5.9
), we get, as
a\to 0
,

\int_{\Omega}\nabla u_{a}\cdot\nabla\varphi dx\to\int_{\Omega}\nabla u_{0}\cdot\nabla\varphi dx\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{ and }\penalty\ \penalty\ \int_{\Omega}|u_{a}|^{p-2}u_{a}\varphi dx\to\int_{\Omega}|u_{0}|^{p-2}u_{0}\varphi dx.

Furthermore, by (
5.9
) and (
5.10
) again, we also have that

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Omega}\phi_{a}u_{a}\varphi dx-\int_{\Omega}\phi_{0}u_{0}\varphi dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}|\phi_{a}-\phi_{0}||u_{a}||\varphi|dx+\int_{\Omega}|\phi_{0}||u_{a}-u_{0}||\varphi|dx

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{\Omega}|\phi_{a}-\phi_{0}|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\Omega}|u_{a}|^{6}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{6}}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\varphi|^{3}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}

\displaystyle\penalty\penalty\penalty\penalty\displaystyle+\left(\int_{\Omega}|\phi_{0}|^{6}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{6}}\left(\int_{\Omega}|u_{a}-u_{0}|^{3}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\varphi|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\to 0,\quad\text{ as }a\to 0.

As a consequence, for any
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
, we deduce from (
5.11
) that, as
a\to 0
,

\int_{\Omega}\nabla u_{0}\cdot\nabla\varphi dx+q^{2}\int_{\Omega}\phi_{0}u_{0}\varphi dx=\int_{\Omega}|u_{0}|^{p-2}u_{0}\varphi dx.

The proof of Theorem
1.5
is thus concluded.

∎

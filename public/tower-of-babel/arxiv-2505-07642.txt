Convergence of Time-Averaged Mean Field Gradient Descent Dynamics for Continuous Multi-Player Zero-Sum Games Thanks: Submitted to the editors DATE.
Yulong Lu, Pierre Monmarché

Abstract
The approximation of mixed Nash equilibria (MNE) for zero-sum games with mean-field interacting players has recently raised much interest in machine learning. In this paper we propose a mean-field gradient descent dynamics for finding the MNE of zero-sum games involving K K players with K ≥ 2 K\geq 2 . The evolution of the players’ strategy distributions follows coupled mean-field gradient descent flows with momentum, incorporating an exponentially discounted time-averaging of gradients. First, in the case of a fixed entropic regularization, we prove an exponential convergence rate for the mean-field dynamics to the mixed Nash equilibrium with respect to the total variation metric. This improves a previous polynomial convergence rate for a similar time-averaged dynamics with different averaging factors. Moreover, unlike previous two-scale approaches for finding the MNE, our approach treats all player types on the same time scale. We also show that with a suitable choice of decreasing temperature, a simulated annealing version of the mean-field dynamics converges to an MNE of the initial unregularized problem.

Zero-sum games, Mixed Nash equilibrium, Mean-field, Gradient descent ascent

Introduction

Zero-sum minmax games are ubiquitous in machine learning. Typical examples include Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) (
[
26
]
), adversarial training (
[
38
]
) and reinforcement learning (

[
6
,
7
,
55
]
). In this paper,
we consider games between
K
players with
K\geq 2
. The loss of a player
i\in\llbracket 1,K\rrbracket
playing strategy
x_{i}\in\mathcal{X}_{i}:=\mathbb{T}^{d_{i}}
against all of other players, denoted by
x_{-i}:=(x_{j})_{j\neq i}
, is denoted by
U_{i}(x)=U_{i}(x_{i},x_{-i})
. Here we use
\mathbb{T}^{d_{i}}
to denote the
d_{i}
-dimensional torus. We remark that the torus assumption, while somewhat restrictive or impractical in game theory, are indeed prevalent in recent literature on the theoretical analysis of optimization dynamics in the mean-field settings
[
37
,
22
,
35
]
. Although we expect majority of the results in the paper can be extended to unbounded strategy spaces when an appropriate confining potential is incorporated into the loss function, we restrict our analysis on the torus to avoid the additional technicalities of dealing with the unboundedness of the state space.

We write
{\mathcal{X}}=\prod_{i=1}^{K}{\mathcal{X}}_{i}
. Throughout the paper we restrict our attention on sum of pairwise zero-sum games, in the following sense: there exist
W_{ij}:{\mathcal{X}}_{i}\times{\mathcal{X}}_{j}\mapsto{\mathbb{R}}
with
W_{ij}=-W_{ji}
such that

U_{i}(x)=\sum_{j\neq i}W_{ij}(x_{i},x_{j})\,.

Further assumptions on
W_{ij}
can be found in Assumption
1
.
The finite dimensional game problem seeks a (pure) Nash equilibrium
(x_{1}^{\ast},\cdots,x_{K}^{\ast})
such that

U_{i}(x_{i}^{\ast},x_{-i}^{\ast})\leq U_{i}(x_{i},x_{-i}^{\ast}),\quad\forall i\in\llbracket 1,K\rrbracket.

(1)

Note that in the definition of pure Nash equilibrium we assume that the
i
-th player is aiming to minimize a loss
U_{i}
(instead of maximizing a pay-off, which is perhaps more commonly used in the game literature). When
{\mathcal{X}}_{i}
are discrete sets, the resulting games reduces to the so-called
polymatrix games

[
5
,
4
]
; see more discussions in Section
1.2
. Algorithmic approximation of the pure Nash equilibria of losses
\{U_{i}\}_{i=1}^{K}
is a very challenging problem when
U_{i}
lacks convexity. In fact, in that case the pure Nash equilibria may not exist (
[
41
]
). To avoid the ill-posed issue of the solutions, there has been a growing interest in finding the
mixed Nash equilibria
(
[
25
]
).
To define the mixed Nash equilibrium (MNE), let
\nu^{i}\in\mathcal{P}({\mathcal{X}}_{i})
be a probability distribution of the mixed strategy used by the
i
-th player for
i\in\llbracket 1,K\rrbracket
. The averaged loss of the
i
-th player against the distributions of the mixed strategies is given by

{\mathcal{E}}_{i}(\nu^{i},\nu^{-i})=\int_{{\mathcal{X}}_{-i}}U_{i}(x_{i},x_{-i})\nu^{-i}(dx_{-i}),

(2)

where
{\mathcal{X}}_{-i}=\prod_{j\neq i}^{K}{\mathcal{X}}_{j}
and
\nu^{-i}:=\{\nu^{j}\}_{j\neq i}
.
We say a set of measures
(\nu^{1,\ast},\cdots,\nu^{K,\ast})
is an MNE of the multi-player game if

{\mathcal{E}}_{i}(\nu^{i,\ast},\nu^{-i,\ast})\leq{\mathcal{E}}_{i}(\nu^{i},\nu^{-i,\ast}),\forall\nu^{i}\in{\mathcal{P}}({\mathcal{X}}_{i}),\forall i\in\llbracket 1,K\rrbracket.

While MNE exists in much generality, algorithmically finding it is still highly nontrivial. Motivated by the recent line of works for two-player zero-sum games, we consider the approximation of an MNE of the game objectives
\{{\mathcal{E}}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{K}
with the MNE of their entropy-regularization. More precisely, we consider the game equipped with the entropy-regularized losses:

{\mathcal{E}}_{i,\tau}(\nu^{i},\nu^{-i}):={\mathcal{E}}_{i}(\nu^{i},\nu^{-i})+\tau{\mathcal{H}}(\nu^{i}),\forall i\in\llbracket 1,K\rrbracket,

(3)

where
{\mathcal{H}}(\nu)=\int\log\frac{d\nu}{dx}d\nu
is the entropy of the probability measure
\nu
and
\tau>0
is a finite regularization parameter (or temperature).
Notice that for every
\tau>0
and every fixed
\nu^{-i}
, the functional
\mu^{i}\mapsto\mathcal{E}_{i,\tau}(\mu^{i},\nu^{-i})
is strongly convex over
\mu^{i}\in\mathcal{P}({\mathcal{X}}_{i})
. Moreover, its unique minimizer is given by

\rho_{U_{i}\star\nu^{-i}}\propto\exp(-\tau^{-1}U_{i}\star\nu^{-i}).

Here we adopt the notation
U_{i}\star\nu^{-i}(x_{i}):=\int U_{i}(x_{i},x_{-i})\nu^{-i}(dx^{-i})
.
This allows us to obtain that
\nu^{*}_{\tau}=(\nu^{1,\ast}_{\tau},\dots,\nu^{K,\ast}_{\tau})
is a MNE
\{{\mathcal{E}}_{i,\tau}\}_{i\in\llbracket 1,K\rrbracket}
if and only if it solves the fixed point equation

\forall i\in\llbracket 1,K\rrbracket,\qquad\nu^{i,\ast}_{\tau}=\rho_{U_{i}\star\nu^{-i,\ast}_{\tau}}\propto\exp\left(-\tau^{-1}U_{i}\star\nu^{-i,\ast}_{\tau}\right)\,.

(4)

Furthermore, the solution to the fixed point equation (
4
) is unique (see Proposition
2
).

Two-player zero-sum games

When
K=2
, the games discussed above reduce to well-studied two-player zero-sum games (
K=2
). In this case,
a two-player zero-sum game can be formulated as a minmax optimization problem of finding the Nash equilibria of a game objective function
W:{\mathcal{X}}_{1}\times{\mathcal{X}}_{2}\mapsto{\mathbb{R}}
, i.e.
\min_{x\in{\mathcal{X}}_{1}}\max_{y\in{\mathcal{X}}_{2}}W(x,y)
. Moreover, a pair of probability distributions
(\nu^{\ast},\mu^{\ast})
is an MNE if

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\mathcal{E}}(\nu^{\ast},\mu)\leq{\mathcal{E}}(\nu^{\ast},\mu^{\ast})\leq{\mathcal{E}}(\nu,\mu^{\ast}),\quad\forall(\nu,\mu)\in{\mathcal{P}}({\mathcal{X}}_{1})\times{\mathcal{P}}({\mathcal{X}}_{2}),

(5)

\displaystyle\text{ where }{\mathcal{E}}(\mu,\nu):=\int_{{\mathcal{X}}_{1}}\int_{{\mathcal{X}}_{2}}W(x,y)\mu(dx)\nu(dy).

Similarly, one can define the entropy-regularized minmax problem

\min_{\nu\in{\mathcal{P}}({\mathcal{X}}_{1})}\max_{\mu\in{\mathcal{P}}({\mathcal{X}}_{2})}{\mathcal{E}}_{\tau}(\nu,\mu),\text{ where }{\mathcal{E}}_{\tau}(\nu,\mu):={\mathcal{E}}(\nu,\mu)+\tau{\mathcal{H}}(\nu)-\tau{\mathcal{H}}(\mu).

The unique MNE
(\nu^{\ast},\mu^{\ast})
of
{\mathcal{E}}_{\tau}
is given by the unique solution to the fixed point equation (see e.g.
[
22
]
)

\displaystyle\nu^{\ast}(dx)\propto\exp\Big(-\tau^{-1}W\star\mu^{\ast}(y)\Big),\quad\mu^{\ast}(dy)\propto\exp\Big(\tau^{-1}W\star\nu^{\ast}(x))\Big),

(6)

where we have used the notations

W\star\mu(x)=\int_{{\mathcal{X}}_{2}}W(x,y)\mu(dy)\text{ and }W\star\nu(y)=\int_{{\mathcal{X}}_{1}}W(x,y)\nu(dx).

A natural approach to approximate the MNE defined by (
6
) is to consider the following Wasserstein gradient descent ascent flow of the energy
{\mathcal{E}}_{\tau}
:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\nu_{t}

\displaystyle=\nabla\cdot\Big(\nu_{t}\nabla_{x}W\star\mu_{t}\Big)+\tau\Delta\nu_{t},

(7)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mu_{t}

\displaystyle=-\nabla\cdot\Big(\mu_{t}\nabla_{y}W\star\nu_{t}\Big)+\tau\Delta\mu_{t}.

The dynamics (
7
) can be interpreted as the mean field limit (
N\rightarrow\infty
) of the algorithmically implementable interacting Langevin dynamics

\displaystyle dX^{i}_{t}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\nabla_{x}W(X^{i}_{t},Y^{j}_{t})dt+\sqrt{2\tau}dW^{i}_{t},

(8)

\displaystyle dY^{i}_{t}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\nabla_{y}W(X^{j}_{t},Y^{i}_{t})dt+\sqrt{2\tau}dB^{i}_{t}.

For this reason, the dynamics (
7
) is often referred to as as the
mean-field gradient descent ascent (mean-field GDA)
. It can also be viewed as the minimax analog of the mean-field Langevin dynamics studied in
[
44
,
12
,
28
]
, which naturally arise in the mean-field analysis of the optimization of two-layer neural networks. Despite its simplicity and popularity, the convergence of the mean-field GDA (
7
) remains an open problem; for a detailed discussion on this problem, see
[
51
]
. A two-scaled variant of (
7
) was previously investigated by
[
35
]
, where the right-hand side of the second equation in (
7
) is multiplied by a small constant
\eta\ll 1
(and by
[
37
]
for the case of an infinitesimal
\eta
). However, the slow progression of the slower dynamics in this two-scale approach significantly limits its efficiency and practical applicability.

Time-averaged mean-field gradient descent dynamics.

An interesting time-averaged variant of (
7
) was recently introduced in
[
29
]
, where the gradient velocity
\nabla_{x}\int_{{\mathcal{Y}}}W(x,y)d\mu_{t}(y)
(and similarly the other gradient) in (
7
) is replaced by a weighted time-average

\Big(\int_{0}^{t}\beta_{s}ds\Big)^{-1}\int_{0}^{t}\beta_{s}\nabla_{x}\int_{{\mathcal{Y}}}W(x,y)d\mu_{s}(y)ds

along its history with a discounting weight
\beta_{t}=t^{r}
with
r>-1
. With this weight,
[
29
]
managed to prove that for a fixed positive temperature
\tau>0
, the time-averaged dynamics with the two-players running the same time-scale converges with an algebraic rate in the Nikaidô-Isoda (NI) error (
[
43
]
). This result presents a promising alternative approach for designing uniformly scaled convergent dynamics for computing the MNE in two-player games.

In this paper, we study a multi-player analog of the time-averaged mean-field dynamics proposed by
[
29
]
. More precisely, we consider

\forall i\in\llbracket 1,K\rrbracket,\quad\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}\partial_{t}\nu_{t}^{i}&=&{\rm NewA}_{x_{i}}\cdot\left(\nu_{t}^{i}{\rm NewA}_{x_{i}}U_{i}\star\nu_{t}^{-i}\right)+\tau\Delta_{x_{i}}\nu_{t}^{i}\\
\partial_{t}\hat{\nu}_{t}^{i}&=&\alpha\left(\nu_{t}^{i}-\hat{\nu}_{t}^{i}\right),\end{array}\right.

(9)

where
\alpha>0
and
\nu_{t}^{i}
is the distribution of the mixed strategies used by the
i
-th player at time
t
.
Note that the second equation of (
9
) is explicitly solvable as

\hat{\nu}_{t}^{i}=e^{-\alpha t}\hat{\nu}_{0}^{i}+\int_{0}^{t}\alpha e^{-\alpha(t-s)}\nu_{s}^{i}\text{d}s.

Therefore the first equation of (
9
) indicates that the distribution of
i
-th player interacts with the historical distributions of mixed strategies of all other players, but discounted by an exponential factor
e^{-\alpha t}
. The constant
\alpha>0
controls the decaying rate of the discounting weight. We also note that given any initial condition
\nu_{0},\hat{\nu}_{0}\in{\mathcal{P}}({\mathcal{X}})
, the existence of uniqueness of the global solution to (
17
) follow directly from the classical well-posedness of the theory of nonlinear Mckean-Vlasov-type PDEs (
[
24
,
49
]
, see also
[
10
, Proposition 1.1]
with time-averaging).
Observe that any stationary solution of (
9
) satisfies
\nu_{t}=\hat{\nu}_{t}=\nu^{\ast}_{\tau}
with
\nu^{\ast}_{\tau}
solving (
4
).

In the two-player setting (
K=2
), the dynamics (
9
) reduces to

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}\partial_{t}\nu_{t}&=&{\rm NewA}_{x}\cdot\left(\nu_{t}{\rm NewA}_{x}W\star\hat{\mu}_{t}\right)+\tau\Delta_{x}\nu_{t}\\
\partial_{t}\mu_{t}&=&-{\rm NewA}_{y}\cdot\left(\mu_{t}{\rm NewA}_{y}W\star\hat{\nu}_{t}\right)+\tau\Delta_{y}\mu_{t}\\
\partial_{t}\hat{\nu}_{t}&=&\alpha\left(\nu_{t}-\hat{\nu}_{t}\right)\\
\partial_{t}\hat{\mu}_{t}&=&\alpha\left(\mu_{t}-\hat{\mu}_{t}\right).\end{array}\right.

(10)

We remark that our dynamics (
10
) coincides with the time-averaged dynamics studied in
[
29
]
with a discounting weight
\beta_{t}=\alpha e^{\alpha t}\mathbf{1}[0,t]+\delta_{0}
. It was shown in
[
29
]
that the time-averaged dynamics with a polynomial discounting factor leads to an algebraic convergence rate. This motivates us to study whether a different choice of the weight, such as the exponential discounting factor, can lead to a faster rate of convergence. Specifically, we are interested in the following question:

Question 1:
Does the mean-field gradient descent dynamics (
9
) converges to the unique MNE of the energies
\{{\mathcal{E}}_{i,\tau}\}_{i=1}^{K}
with an exponential rate?

As the first main result of the paper, we shall provide an affirmative answer to
Question 1
. Furthermore, inspired by the simulated annealing results for the mean-field Langevin dynamics (
[
12
,
44
]
) and a two-scale mean-field GDA dynamics (
[
35
]
), we would like to investigate the convergence of the time-averaged mean-field dynamics in the annealed regime where
\tau_{t}\rightarrow 0
as
t\rightarrow\infty
.

Question 2:
Does the annealed time-averaged mean-field gradient descent dynamics (
9
) with a decreasing temperature
\tau=\tau_{t}
converge to an MNE of the unregularized energies
\{{\mathcal{E}}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{K}
defined in (
2
)?

Summary of contributions

We highlight the major contributions of the present paper as follows.

•

We present a time-averaged mean field gradient descent dynamics (

) for approximating the MNE of a class of multi-player game problem, generalizing a similar dynamics proposed by

•

Related works

Computational complexity of zero-sum polymatrix games

Computing the mixed Nash equilibria (even approximately) of general games can be computationally intractable in the sense that approximate Nash equilibria is not achievable within polynomial time
[

,
11

Continuous two-player games

For two-player games, the problem reduces to a minmax problem which received considerable attention recently. The most natural methods for finding the pure Nash equilibria are the gradient descent ascent methods for which many theoretical guarantees have been obtained under various assumptions on the game objectives. Many works either study the convergence of GDA in the convex-concave setting
[

]
in which pure equilibria exist or its convergence to local equilibria in the non-convex settings

,

,

. Different from these works, the present paper studies the global convergence guarantees of algorithms for finding the mixed Nash equilibria of continuous multi-player games without convexity assumptions.

Assumptions and Main Results

2.1
Settings and notations

Recall that we consider games between
K
players with
K\geq 2
. Assume that the loss of a player
i\in\llbracket 1,K\rrbracket
playing strategy
x_{i}\in\mathcal{X}_{i}
against all of other players
x_{-i}=(x_{j})_{j\neq i}
, is given by
U_{i}(x)=U_{i}(x_{i},x_{-i})
. We make the following assumption on the sum of pairwise zero-sum games:

Assumption 1
.

For all distinct pair
i,j\in\llbracket 1,K\rrbracket
, there exist
W_{ij}:{\mathcal{X}}_{i}\times{\mathcal{X}}_{j}\mapsto{\mathbb{R}}
with
W_{ij}=-W_{ji}
such that

U_{i}(x)=\sum_{j\neq i}W_{ij}(x_{i},x_{j})\,.

(11)

Moreover, assume that
W_{ij}\in C^{2}({\mathcal{X}}_{i}\times{\mathcal{X}}_{j})
for all distinct
i,j\in\llbracket 1,K\rrbracket
. Denote constants
M_{0},M_{1},M_{2},L
by

M_{0}=\max_{i\in\llbracket 1,K\rrbracket}\|U_{i}\|_{\infty}\,,L=\max_{i\in\llbracket 1,K\rrbracket}\|\nabla U_{i}\|_{\infty},M_{1}=\sum_{i=1}^{K}\|U_{i}\|_{\infty},\,M_{2}=\max_{i\in\llbracket 1,K\rrbracket}\sum_{j\neq i}\|{\rm NewA}_{xy}^{2}W_{i,j}\|_{\infty}\,.

Recall the game objectives
\{{\mathcal{E}}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{K}
and
\{{\mathcal{E}}_{i,\tau}\}_{i=1}^{K}
defined in and (
2
) and (
3
) respectively. Recall also that the MNE
\nu^{*}_{\tau}=(\nu^{1,\ast}_{\tau},\dots,\nu^{K,\ast}_{\tau})
associated to
\{{\mathcal{E}}_{i,\tau}\}_{i=1}^{K}
solves the fixed point equation (
4
).
To measure the discrepancy of a collection of measures
\nu=(\nu^{1},\dots,\nu^{K})
to the MNE of the entropy-regularized energies
\{{\mathcal{E}}_{i,\tau}\}_{i=1}^{K}
, we define the Nikaidô-Isoda (NI) error (
[
43
]
) of
\nu
associated to
\{{\mathcal{E}}_{i,\tau}\}_{i=1}^{K}
by

{\mathrm{NI}}_{\tau}(\nu):=\sum_{i=1}^{K}\left[\mathcal{E}_{i,\tau}(\nu^{i},\nu^{-i})-\inf_{\mu^{i}\in\mathcal{P}({\mathcal{X}}_{i})}\mathcal{E}_{i,\tau}(\mu^{i},\nu^{-i})\right]=\tau\sum_{i=1}^{K}\mathcal{H}\left(\nu_{i}|\rho_{U_{i}\star\nu^{-i}}\right).

(12)

Similarly, we also define the NI error of
\nu
associated to the unregularized energies
\{{\mathcal{E}}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{K}
as

{\mathrm{NI}}(\nu):=\sum_{i=1}^{K}\left[\mathcal{E}_{i}(\nu^{i},\nu^{-i})-\inf_{\mu^{i}\in\mathcal{P}({\mathcal{X}}_{i})}\mathcal{E}_{i}(\mu^{i},\nu^{-i})\right].

(13)

To close the setting, we present a proposition which establishes that there exists a unique equilibrium measure
\nu^{\ast}_{\tau}
satisfying (
4
).

Proposition 2
.

Under Assumption
1
, for any
\tau>0
, there exists a unique Nash equilibrium
\nu^{\ast}_{\tau}=(\nu^{1,\ast}_{\tau},\dots,\nu^{K,\ast}_{\tau})
associated to the energies
\{{\mathcal{E}}_{i,\tau}\}_{i=1}^{K}
. Moreover, for any probability measures
\nu=(\nu^{1},\dots,\nu^{K})
, it holds that

{\mathrm{NI}}_{\tau}(\nu)\geqslant\tau\sum_{i=1}^{K}\mathcal{H}\left(\nu^{i}|\nu^{i,\ast}_{\tau}\right)

(14)

We refer the proof of Proposition
2
to Section
3.1
.

2.2
Convergence time-averaged mean-field gradient descent dynamics at a fixed temperature

Recall the time-averaged mean-field gradient descent dynamics (
9
) whose solution is given by
\nu_{t}=\bigotimes_{i=1}^{K}\nu_{t}^{i}
and
\hat{\nu}_{t}=\bigotimes_{i=1}^{K}\hat{\nu}_{t}^{i}
.
Given these measures, we also define the proximal-Gibbs measure
\rho_{t}=\bigotimes_{i=1}^{K}\rho_{t}^{i}
with

\rho_{t}^{i}\propto\exp\left(-\tau^{-1}U_{i}\star\hat{\nu}_{t}^{-i}\right),\,i\in\llbracket 1,K\rrbracket.

With the definitions above, it is straightforward to verify that

{\mathrm{NI}}_{\tau}(\hat{\nu}_{t})=\tau\mathcal{H}(\hat{\nu}_{t}|\rho_{t}).

(15)

To track the convergence of (
9
) to the equilibrium, we define the following Lyapunov function

s_{t}:=\mathcal{H}(\nu_{t}|\rho_{t})+\mathcal{H}(\hat{\nu}_{t}|\rho_{t})=\sum_{i=1}^{K}\mathcal{H}(\nu^{i}_{t}|\rho^{i}_{t})+\mathcal{H}(\hat{\nu}^{i}_{t}|\rho^{i}_{t})\,.

The proposition below establishes the exponential contraction of
s_{t}
along (
9
).

Proposition 3
.

Under Assumption
1
, set

\kappa=\frac{\exp(M_{0}/\tau)}{8\pi^{2}}\,,\qquad\lambda=\min\left(\frac{\tau}{2\kappa},\frac{\alpha}{4}\right)\,,\qquad\bar{\alpha}_{0}=\frac{\tau}{53\kappa\max(1,(\kappa M_{2}/\tau)^{2})}\,.

(16)

Assume that
\nu_{0}^{i},\hat{\nu}_{0}^{i},i\in\llbracket 1,K\rrbracket
have finite entropies and that
\alpha\in(0,\bar{\alpha}_{0}]
. Then for all
t\geqslant 0
, along the flow (
9
),

s_{t}\leqslant e^{-\lambda t}s_{0}\,.

Proposition
3
shows that both the measure
\nu_{t}
and the time-averaged measure
\hat{\nu}_{t}
are getting closer to the proximal Gibbs measures
\rho_{t}
with respect to the relative entropy as
t
tends to infinity.
The proof of Proposition
3
can be found in Section
3.3
. This proposition is a key ingredient to obtain the convergence of (
9
) to its equilibrium as presented in the theorem below.

Theorem 4
.

Under the assumptions and with the notations of Proposition
3
, we have for all
t\geq 0
that

\sum_{i=1}^{K}\mathcal{H}\left(\hat{\nu}_{t}^{i}|\nu^{i,\ast}_{\tau}\right)\leqslant e^{-\lambda t}s_{0}

and that

\sum_{i=1}^{K}\|\nu_{t}^{i}-\nu^{i,\ast}_{\tau}\|_{TV}^{2}\leqslant 12e^{-\lambda t}s_{0}\,.

Remark 5
.

Theorem
4
states that for a small weight parameter
\alpha
, the time-averaged mean-field GDA dynamics (
9
) converges to its invariant measure exponentially fast. This improves a prior algebraic convergence rate obtained by
[
29
]
for a similar time-averaged mean-field dynamics but with a polynomially decaying weight. On the other hand, we note that the convergence rate
\lambda=O(e^{-M_{0}\tau^{-1}})
when the temperature
\tau
is small, which is consistent with the existing rates of convergence obtained for mean-field Langevin dynamics (
[
12
,
44
]
) and mean-field GDA dynamics (
[
35
]
). We also remark that our convergence result assumes a small
\alpha
, which only enters the exponential discounting factor used for time-averaging of the velocity, but all players run their dynamics at a uniform speed. This is in contrast to
[
35
]
, where the convergence result there relies on time-scale separation between different players.

2.3
Convergence of annealed dynamics

In this section, we study the convergence of the following annealed time-averaged mean-field GDA dynamics

\forall i\in\llbracket 1,K\rrbracket,\quad\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}\partial_{t}\nu_{t}^{i}&=&{\rm NewA}_{x}\cdot\left(\nu_{t}^{i}{\rm NewA}_{x}U_{i}\star\hat{\nu}_{t}^{i}\right)+\tau_{t}\Delta_{x}\nu_{t}^{i}\\
\partial_{t}\hat{\nu}_{t}^{i}&=&\alpha_{t}\left(\nu_{t}^{i}-\hat{\nu}_{t}^{i}\right)\,.\end{array}\right.

(17)

In the above,
\tau_{t}
is a cooling function of time that decreases to zero as
t
tends to infinity, and
\alpha_{t}>0
is a time-dependent weight exponent.

We aim to show that by choosing the cooling schedule
\tau_{t}
and the weight exponent
\alpha_{t}
appropriately, the solution
\{\nu_{t}^{i}\}
converges to an MNE of the unregularized energies
\{{\mathcal{E}}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{K}
. To this end, we first track the evolution of certain entropies along the annealed dynamics in the next proposition, which extends Proposition
3
by taking account of time-varying temperature
\tau_{t}
and weight
\alpha_{t}
. To state the result, it is useful to set
\rho_{t,\tau}^{i}\propto\exp(-U_{i}\star\hat{\nu}_{t}^{-i}/\tau)
for
i\in\llbracket 1,K\rrbracket
. We also write

s_{t,\tau}:=\sum_{i=1}^{K}\left[\mathcal{H}\left(\nu_{t}^{i}|\rho_{t,\tau}^{i}\right)+\mathcal{H}\left(\hat{\nu}_{t}^{i}|\rho_{t,\tau}^{i}\right)\right]\,.

Proposition 6
.

For constants
\delta
and
\beta
such that

0<\delta\leqslant\frac{1}{12M_{0}}\,,0<\beta\leqslant\frac{3}{\delta^{3}M_{2}}\,,

(18)

we choose
c_{0}>1
large enough so that

c_{0}\geqslant\frac{144}{\beta^{2}}\,,\qquad\delta M_{2}c_{0}^{\delta M_{0}}\ln c_{0}\geqslant 8\pi^{2}\,.

(19)

We set

\tau_{t}=\frac{1}{\delta\ln(c_{0}+t)}\,,\qquad\alpha_{t}=\frac{\beta}{\sqrt{c_{0}+t}}\,,

Then,
for all
t\geqslant 0
,

s_{t,\tau_{t}}\leqslant\frac{32\delta M_{1}}{\beta\sqrt{c_{0}+t}}+e^{-\frac{\beta}{2}\left(\sqrt{c_{0}+t}-\sqrt{c_{0}}\right)}s_{0}\,.

(20)

The proof of Proposition
6
can be found in Section
3.3
.
As a corollary of Proposition
6
, the next theorem characterizes the large-time closedness of the annealed dynamics to the proximal-Gibbs measures
\{\nu^{i,\ast}_{\tau_{t}}\}_{i=1}^{K}
.

Theorem 7
.

Under the same setting as Proposition
6
, there exists a constant
C>0
depending only on
M_{0},M_{1},M_{2}
and
s_{0}
such that for sufficiently large
t
,

\|\nu^{i}_{t}-\nu^{i,\ast}_{\tau_{t}}\|_{TV}\leq C(t+c_{0})^{-1/4},\ \forall i\in\llbracket 1,K\rrbracket.

As a consequence of Theorem
7
, the next theorem establishes the convergence of the annealed dynamics to the MNE of the unregularized energies with respect to the NI-error.

Theorem 8
.

Under the same setting as Proposition
6
, there exists a constant
C^{\prime}>0
depending only on
M_{0},M_{1},M_{2},L
,
s_{0}
and
d_{i},i\in\llbracket 1,K\rrbracket
such that for sufficiently large
t
,

{\mathrm{NI}}(\nu_{t})\leq\frac{C^{\prime}\log\log t}{\log t}.

Remark 9
.

The convergence rate
O(\log\log t/\log t)
in Theorem
8
was previously obtained in
[
35
]
for an annealed two-scale mean-field GDA dynamics. However, we emphasize again that the key ingredient of the proof — Proposition
6
, does not require scale-separation among the players.

3.1
Proof of the result in Section
2.1

Proof of Proposition
2
.

Consider a solution of (
9
) with finite initial entropy. Since
\partial_{t}\hat{\nu}_{t}=\alpha(\nu_{t}-\hat{\nu}_{t})
,

\|\hat{\nu}_{t+s}-\hat{\nu}_{t}\|_{TV}\leqslant\alpha\int_{t}^{t+s}\|\hat{\nu}_{u}-\nu_{u}\|_{TV}\text{d}u\\
\leqslant\sqrt{2}\alpha\int_{t}^{t+s}\sqrt{\|\hat{\nu}_{u}-\rho_{u}\|_{TV}^{2}+\|\rho_{u}-\nu_{u}\|_{TV}^{2}}\text{d}u\leqslant\frac{4\sqrt{s_{0}}\alpha}{\lambda}e^{-\lambda t/2}\,.

By completeness,
\hat{\nu}_{t}
then converge in total variation to some
\nu^{*}_{\tau}
. Since
\|\hat{\nu}_{t}-\rho_{t}\|_{TV}\leqslant\sqrt{2s_{t}}\rightarrow 0
, we also get that
\rho_{t}
converge to
\nu^{*}_{\tau}
as
t\rightarrow\infty
.
Moreover, for all
i\in\llbracket 1,K\rrbracket
,

\|U_{i}\star\hat{\nu}_{t}^{-i}-U_{i}\star\nu^{-i,*}_{\tau}\|_{\infty}\leqslant M_{0}\|\|\hat{\nu}_{t}^{-i}-\nu^{-i,*}_{\tau}\|_{TV}\underset{t\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}{0}\,.

This shows that
\rho_{t}^{i}
converges to the density proportional to
\exp(-U_{i}\star\nu^{-i,*}_{\tau})
. By uniqueness of the limit, we get that
\nu^{*}_{\tau}
solves the fixed-point equation (
4
), hence is an MNE.

Using this fixed point equation and the pairwise zero-sum assumption that
W_{ij}=-W_{ji}
, which in particular implies that for any
\nu\in\mathcal{P}({\mathcal{X}})
,

\sum_{i=1}^{K}\sum_{j\neq i}\int_{{\mathcal{X}}_{i}\times{\mathcal{X}}_{j}}W_{ij}\nu^{i}\nu^{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{K}\int_{{\mathcal{X}}_{i}\times{\mathcal{X}}_{-i}}U_{i}\nu^{i}\nu^{-i}=0\,,

we get, for any
\nu\in\mathcal{P}({\mathcal{X}})
,

\displaystyle{\mathrm{NI}}(\nu)

\displaystyle\geqslant\sum_{i=1}^{K}\left[{\mathcal{E}}_{i,\tau}\left(\nu^{i},\nu^{-i}\right)-{\mathcal{E}}_{i,\tau}\left(\nu^{i,\ast}_{\tau},\nu^{-i}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{K}\left[\tau\int_{{\mathcal{X}}_{i}}\nu^{i}\ln\nu^{i}-\tau\int_{{\mathcal{X}}_{i}}\nu^{i,\ast}_{\tau}\ln\nu^{i,\ast}_{\tau}-\sum_{j\neq i}\int_{{\mathcal{X}}_{i}\times{\mathcal{X}}_{j}}W_{ij}\nu^{i,\ast}_{\tau}\nu^{j}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{K}\left[\tau\int_{{\mathcal{X}}_{i}}\nu^{i}\ln\nu^{i}-\tau\int_{{\mathcal{X}}_{i}}\nu^{i,\ast}_{\tau}\ln\nu^{i,\ast}_{\tau}-\sum_{j\neq i}\int_{{\mathcal{X}}_{i}\times{\mathcal{X}}_{j}}W_{ij}\nu^{i,\ast}_{\tau}(\nu^{j}-\nu_{*}^{j})\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{K}\left[\tau\int_{{\mathcal{X}}_{i}}\nu^{i}\ln\nu^{i}-\tau\int_{{\mathcal{X}}_{i}}\nu^{i,\ast}_{\tau}\ln\nu^{i,\ast}_{\tau}\right]+\sum_{j=1}^{K}\int_{{\mathcal{X}}_{i}\times{\mathcal{X}}_{j}}\left(\sum_{i\neq j}W_{ji}\nu^{i,\ast}_{\tau}\right)(\nu^{j}-\nu_{*}^{j})

\displaystyle=\tau\sum_{i=1}^{K}\left[\int_{{\mathcal{X}}_{i}}\nu^{i}\ln\nu^{i}-\int_{{\mathcal{X}}_{i}}\nu^{i,\ast}_{\tau}\ln\nu^{i,\ast}_{\tau}-\int_{{\mathcal{X}}_{i}}\ln\nu^{i,\ast}_{\tau}(\nu^{i}-\nu^{i,\ast}_{\tau})\right]

\displaystyle=\tau\sum_{i=1}^{K}\mathcal{H}\left(\nu^{i}|\nu^{i,\ast}_{\tau}\right)\,.

This implies the uniqueness of the mixed Nash equilibrium. Indeed, if
(\nu^{1},\dots,\nu^{K})
is a Nash equilibrium, then the left-hand side of (
14
) is zero, implying that
\nu^{i}=\nu^{i,\ast}_{\tau}
for all
i\in\llbracket 1,K\rrbracket
.

3.2
Proofs of results in Section
2.2

Proof of Proposition
3
.

Since
\rho_{t}^{i}
is the invariant measure of the Markov generator
{\rm NewA}U_{i}\star\hat{\nu}_{t}^{-i}\cdot{\rm NewA}+\tau\Delta
, by usual computations on the entropy dissipation along the corresponding flow,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mathcal{H}\left(\nu_{t}^{i}|\rho_{t}^{i}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\partial_{s})_{|s=t}\mathcal{H}\left(\nu_{s}^{i}|\rho_{t}^{i}\right)+(\partial_{s})_{|s=t}\mathcal{H}\left(\nu_{t}^{i}|\rho_{s}^{i}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\tau\mathcal{I}\left(\nu_{t}^{i}|\rho_{t}^{i}\right)-\int_{{\mathcal{X}}_{i}}\nu_{t}^{i}\partial_{t}\ln\rho_{t}^{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\tau\mathcal{I}\left(\nu_{t}^{i}|\rho_{t}^{i}\right)+\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{{\mathcal{X}}_{i}}\partial_{t}\left(\sum_{j\neq i}W_{ij}\star\hat{\nu}_{t}^{j}\right)\left(\rho_{t}^{i}-\nu_{t}^{i}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\tau\mathcal{I}\left(\nu_{t}^{i}|\rho_{t}^{i}\right)+\frac{\alpha}{\tau}\sum_{j\neq i}\int_{{\mathcal{X}}_{i}}W_{ij}\star(\nu_{t}^{j}-\hat{\nu}_{t}^{j})\left(\rho^{i}_{t}-\nu_{t}^{i}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle-\tau\mathcal{I}\left(\nu_{t}^{i}|\rho_{t}^{i}\right)+\frac{\alpha}{\tau}\sum_{j\neq i}\|{\rm NewA}_{x_{i}}W_{ij}\star(\nu_{t}^{j}-\hat{\nu}_{t}^{j})\|_{\infty}\mathcal{W}_{1}\left(\rho^{i}_{t},\nu_{t}^{i}\right)\,.

Using that
\|U_{i}\star\nu^{-i}\|_{\infty}\leqslant M_{0}
, reyling on the log-Sobolev inequality for the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{T}^{d}
(with constant
1/(8\pi^{2})
, see
[
3
, Proposition 5.7.5(ii)]
) and the Holley-Stroock perturbation result (e.g.
[
3
, Proposition 5.1.6]
), we see that each
\rho_{t}^{i}
(hence
\rho_{t}
by tensorization) satisfies a log-Sobolev inequality with constant
\kappa
given by (
16
), meaning that

\forall\mu\in\mathcal{P}({\mathcal{X}})\,,\qquad\mathcal{H}(\mu|\rho_{t})\leqslant\kappa\mathcal{I}(\nu|\rho_{t})\,.

From
[
45
]
, it implies the Talagrand inequality

\forall\mu\in\mathcal{P}({\mathcal{X}})\,,\qquad\mathcal{W}_{2}^{2}(\mu,\rho_{t})\leqslant 4\kappa\mathcal{H}(\nu|\rho_{t})\,.

At that point, if we were following
[
29
]
, we would have a learning rate
\alpha_{t}
depending on time and, from the previous bounds using the log-Sobolev and Talagrand inequalities for
\rho_{t}
, that
\mathcal{W}_{1}\leqslant\mathcal{W}_{2}
, we would deduce that

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mathcal{H}\left(\nu_{t}^{i}|\rho_{t}^{i}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle-\frac{\tau}{\kappa}\mathcal{H}\left(\nu_{t}^{i}|\rho_{t}^{i}\right)+4\frac{\alpha_{t}}{\tau}\sum_{j\neq i}\|{\rm NewA}_{x_{i}}W_{ij}\|_{\infty}\sqrt{\kappa\mathcal{H}\left(\nu_{t}^{i}|\rho^{i}_{t}\right)}\,,

from which we get that
\mathcal{H}\left(\nu_{t}^{i}|\rho_{t}^{i}\right)
vanishes as
t\rightarrow\infty
provided this is the case of
\alpha_{t}
– which prevents exponential convergence so we want to avoid this condition.

Rather, we bound

\displaystyle\|{\rm NewA}_{x_{i}}W_{ij}\star(\nu_{t}^{j}-\hat{\nu}_{t}^{j})\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leqslant\|{\rm NewA}_{x_{i}x_{j}}^{2}W_{ij}\|_{\infty}\mathcal{W}_{1}(\nu_{t}^{j},\hat{\nu}_{t}^{j})

\displaystyle\leqslant\|{\rm NewA}_{x_{i}x_{j}}^{2}W_{ij}\|_{\infty}\left[\mathcal{W}_{1}(\nu_{t}^{j},\rho_{t}^{j})+\mathcal{W}_{1}(\rho_{t}^{j},\hat{\nu}_{t}^{j})\right].

Plugging this in the previous inequality and then using the log-Sobolev and Talagrand inequalities for
\rho_{t}
yields

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mathcal{H}\left(\nu_{t}|\rho_{t}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant-\tau\mathcal{I}\left(\nu_{t}|\rho_{t}\right)+\frac{\alpha}{\tau}\sum_{i=1}^{K}\sum_{j\neq k}\Big[\|{\rm NewA}^{2}_{x_{i}x_{j}}W_{ij}\|_{\infty}\mathcal{W}_{1}\left(\rho^{i}_{t},\nu_{t}^{i}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt\times\left(\mathcal{W}_{1}\left(\nu_{t}^{j},\rho^{j}_{t}\right)+\mathcal{W}_{1}\left(\rho^{j}_{t},\hat{\nu}_{t}^{j}\right)\right)\Big]

\displaystyle\leqslant-\frac{\tau}{\kappa}\mathcal{H}\left(\nu_{t}|\rho_{t}\right)+4\kappa\frac{\alpha}{\tau}\sum_{i=1}^{K}\sum_{j\neq k}\Bigg[\|{\rm NewA}^{2}_{x_{i}x_{j}}W_{ij}\|_{\infty}\sqrt{\mathcal{H}\left(\nu_{t}^{i}|\rho^{i}_{t}\right)}

\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt\times\left(\sqrt{\mathcal{H}\left(\nu^{j}_{t}|\rho^{j}_{t}\right)}+\sqrt{\mathcal{H}\left(\hat{\nu}_{t}^{j}|\rho^{j}_{t}\right)}\right)\Bigg]

\displaystyle\leqslant\left((2+\theta)\alpha M^{\prime}-\frac{\tau}{\kappa}\right)\mathcal{H}\left(\nu_{t}|\rho_{t}\right)+\frac{\alpha M^{\prime}}{\theta}\mathcal{H}\left(\hat{\nu}_{t}|\rho_{t}\right)\,,

(21)

with
M^{\prime}=2\kappa M_{2}/\tau
, for any
\theta>0
. We have also used the tensorization property of the relative entropy.

Now, using that
\hat{\nu}_{t}^{i}
is a probability measure for all
t\geqslant 0
,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mathcal{H}\left(\hat{\nu}_{t}^{i}|\rho_{t}^{i}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathcal{X}}}\ln\frac{\hat{\nu}_{t}^{i}}{\rho_{t}^{i}}\partial_{t}\hat{\nu}_{t}^{i}-\int_{{\mathcal{X}}_{i}}\partial_{t}\left(\ln\rho_{t}^{i}\right)\hat{\nu}_{t}^{i}

(22)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha\int_{{\mathcal{X}}}\ln\frac{\hat{\nu}_{t}^{i}}{\rho_{t}^{i}}\left(\nu_{t}^{i}-\hat{\nu}_{t}^{i}\right)+\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{{\mathcal{X}}_{i}}\partial_{t}\left(\sum_{j\neq i}W_{ij}\star\hat{\nu}_{t}^{j}\right)\left(\hat{\nu}_{t}^{i}-\rho_{t}^{i}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha\int_{{\mathcal{X}}}\ln\frac{\hat{\nu}_{t}^{i}}{\rho_{t}^{i}}\nu_{t}^{i}-\alpha\mathcal{H}\left(\hat{\nu}_{t}^{i}|\rho_{t}^{i}\right)+\frac{\alpha}{\tau}\sum_{j\neq i}\int_{{\mathcal{X}}_{i}}W_{ij}\star(\nu_{t}^{j}-\hat{\nu}_{t}^{j})\left(\hat{\nu}_{t}^{i}-\rho_{t}^{i}\right)\,.

For the first term, we use that

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\ln\frac{\hat{\nu}_{t}^{i}}{\rho_{t}^{i}}\nu_{t}^{i}=\mathcal{H}\left(\nu_{t}^{i}|\rho_{t}^{i}\right)-\mathcal{H}\left(\nu_{t}^{i}|\hat{\nu}_{t}^{i}\right)\leqslant\mathcal{H}\left(\nu_{t}^{i}|\rho_{t}^{i}\right)\,.

To treat the last term, for
i\neq j
, using that
W_{ij}=-W_{ji}
, write

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathcal{X}}_{i}\times{\mathcal{X}}_{j}}W_{ij}\left(\hat{\nu}_{t}^{i}-\rho_{t}^{i}\right)(\nu_{t}^{j}-\hat{\nu}_{t}^{j})+\int_{{\mathcal{X}}_{i}\times{\mathcal{X}}_{j}}W_{ji}\left(\hat{\nu}_{t}^{j}-\rho_{t}^{j}\right)(\nu_{t}^{i}-\hat{\nu}_{t}^{i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathcal{X}}_{i}\times{\mathcal{X}}_{j}}W_{ij}\left(\nu_{t}^{i}-\rho_{t}^{i}\right)(\nu_{t}^{j}-\hat{\nu}_{t}^{j})+\int_{{\mathcal{X}}_{i}\times{\mathcal{X}}_{j}}W_{ji}\left(\nu_{t}^{j}-\rho_{t}^{j}\right)(\nu_{t}^{i}-\hat{\nu}_{t}^{i})

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\|{\rm NewA}_{x_{i}x_{j}}^{2}W_{ij}\|_{\infty}\left[\mathcal{W}_{1}\left(\nu_{t}^{j},\hat{\nu}_{t}^{j}\right)\mathcal{W}_{1}\left(\nu_{t}^{i},\rho_{t}^{i}\right)+\mathcal{W}_{1}\left(\nu_{t}^{j},\rho_{t}^{j}\right)\mathcal{W}_{1}\left(\nu_{t}^{i},\hat{\nu}_{t}^{i}\right)\right]\,.

Summing (
22
) over
i\in\llbracket 1,K\rrbracket
and using the last two estimates, one has that

\partial_{t}\mathcal{H}\left(\hat{\nu}_{t}|\rho_{t}\right)\leqslant\alpha\mathcal{H}\left(\nu_{t}|\rho_{t}\right)-\alpha\mathcal{H}\left(\hat{\nu}_{t}|\rho_{t}\right)+\frac{2\alpha}{\tau}\sum_{i=1}^{K}\sum_{j\neq i}\|{\rm NewA}_{x_{i}x_{j}}^{2}W_{ij}\|_{\infty}\mathcal{W}_{1}\left(\nu_{t}^{j},\hat{\nu}_{t}^{j}\right)\mathcal{W}_{1}\left(\nu_{t}^{i},\rho_{t}^{i}\right)\,.

For distinct
i
and
j
, we bound, for any
\theta^{\prime}>0
,

\displaystyle 2\mathcal{W}_{1}\left(\nu_{t}^{j},\hat{\nu}_{t}^{j}\right)\mathcal{W}_{1}\left(\nu_{t}^{i},\rho_{t}^{i}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle 2\left(\mathcal{W}_{1}\left(\nu_{t}^{j},\rho_{t}^{j}\right)+\mathcal{W}_{1}\left(\rho_{t}^{j},\hat{\nu}_{t}^{j}\right)\right)\mathcal{W}_{1}\left(\nu_{t}^{i},\rho_{t}^{i}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{1}^{2}\left(\nu_{t}^{j},\rho_{t}^{j}\right)+\frac{1}{\theta^{\prime}}\mathcal{W}_{1}^{2}\left(\rho_{t}^{j},\hat{\nu}_{t}^{j}\right)+(1+\theta^{\prime})\mathcal{W}_{1}^{2}\left(\nu_{t}^{i},\rho_{t}^{i}\right)\,.

Plugging this in the previous inequality and then using the Talagrand inequalities for
\rho_{t}^{i}
leads to

\partial_{t}\mathcal{H}\left(\hat{\nu}_{t}|\rho_{t}\right)\leqslant-\alpha\left(1-\frac{2\kappa M_{2}}{\tau\theta^{\prime}}\right)\mathcal{H}\left(\hat{\nu}_{t}|\rho_{t}\right)+\alpha\left(1+\frac{2\kappa M_{2}(2+\theta^{\prime})}{\tau}\right)\mathcal{H}\left(\nu_{t}|\rho_{t}\right)\,.

Taking
\theta^{\prime}=4\kappa M_{2}/\tau
, writing
M^{\prime\prime}=1+2\kappa M_{2}(2+\theta^{\prime})/\tau
and summing this inequality with (
21
) we get, for any
\theta>0
,

\partial_{t}s_{t}\leqslant\left((2+\theta)\alpha M^{\prime}+\alpha M^{\prime\prime}-\frac{\tau}{\kappa}\right)\mathcal{H}\left(\nu_{t}|\rho_{t}\right)+\alpha\left(\frac{M^{\prime}}{\theta}-\frac{1}{2}\right)\mathcal{H}\left(\hat{\nu}_{t}|\rho_{t}\right)\,.

Taking
\theta=4M^{\prime}
and assuming that

\alpha\leqslant\frac{\tau}{2\kappa\left((2+\theta)M^{\prime}+M^{\prime\prime}\right)}=\frac{\tau}{4\kappa\left((2+8\kappa M_{2}/\tau)\kappa M_{2}/\tau+1+2\kappa M_{2}(2+4\kappa M_{2}/\tau)/\tau\right)}\,,

which is implied by
\alpha\leqslant\alpha_{0}
with
\alpha_{0}
given by (
16
), we get

\partial_{t}s_{t}\leqslant-\frac{\tau}{2\kappa}\mathcal{H}\left(\nu_{t}|\rho_{t}\right)-\frac{\alpha}{4}\mathcal{H}\left(\hat{\nu}_{t}|\rho_{t}\right)\leqslant-\lambda s_{t}\,,

(23)

which concludes the proof of the proposition.

Proof of Theorem
4
.

First, it follows from Proposition
2
that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{K}\mathcal{H}\left(\hat{\nu}_{t}^{i}|\nu^{i,\ast}_{\tau}\right)\leq\tau^{-1}{\mathrm{NI}}_{\tau}(\hat{\nu}_{t})=\tau{\mathcal{H}}(\hat{\nu}_{t}|\rho_{t}),

where we have used (

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{K}\|\nu_{t}^{i}-\nu^{i,\ast}_{\tau}\|_{TV}^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant 6\sum_{i=1}^{K}\left[\mathcal{H}\left(\nu_{t}^{i}|\rho_{t}^{i}\right)+\mathcal{H}\left(\hat{\nu}_{t}^{i}|\rho_{t}^{i}\right)+\mathcal{H}\left(\hat{\nu}_{t}^{i}|\nu^{i,\ast}_{\tau}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leqslant 12s_{t}\,.

Therefore the second estimate follows again from Proposition
3
and the first estimate.

Proof of Proposition

We decompose

The second term is

(\partial_{u})_{|u=\tau_{t}}s_{t,u}=-\frac{\partial_{t}\tau_{t}}{\tau_{t}^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{K}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}U_{i}\star\hat{\nu}_{t}^{-i}\left(2\rho_{t}^{i}-\nu_{t}^{i}-\hat{\nu}_{t}^{i}\right)\leqslant\frac{4\delta M_{1}}{c_{0}+t}\,.

, where (

) becomes

(\partial_{u})_{|u=t}s_{u,\tau_{t}}\leqslant-\lambda_{t}s_{t,\tau_{t}}

(25)

as soon as

4
Conclusion and future work

In this paper, we consider the approximation of the MNE of a multi-player zero-sum game with a time-averaged mean-field gradient flow dynamics. We proved an exponential convergence in total variation metric for the constructed dynamics for finding the MNE of an entropy-regularized loss on the space of probability measures. We also proved that an annealed mean-field dynamics with a suitable cooling schedule converges to an MNE of the original unregularized game loss with respect to the Nikaidò-Isoda error.

We would like to highlight several interesting open directions for future research. First, our convergence analysis has been concentrated on a game objective defined by sum of pairwise zero-sum losses. It remains an interesting theoretical question to extend the analysis to a general zero-sum loss that involves simultaneous interactions between multiple players. Next, although we have focused on the analysis on the tori, we anticipate that our proof strategies can be adapted to deal with game objectives (with suitable confining condition) on unbounded state spaces. Furthermore, from a practical point of view, the mean field dynamics are often implemented by a certain interacting particle algorithm with a large number of particles. To perform a complete error analysis for the particle algorithm, it is crucial to establish a uniform-in-time quantitative error bound between the particle system and the mean field dynamics, which remains open. Lastly, our approach can potentially be extended to the general problem of sampling probability measures solving a fixed-point problem of the form

\nu\propto\exp(-F(\nu))

where
F
is not the flat derivative of some energy. In that case, the mean-field evolution naturally associated to this equation is not the Wasserstein gradient flow of an explicit free energy, so that standard mean-field entropy methods do not apply. However, with a time-averaged flow, the Lyapunov condition is explicitly given by relative entropies with respect to the proximal-Gibbs measure. We will investigate these questions in future works.

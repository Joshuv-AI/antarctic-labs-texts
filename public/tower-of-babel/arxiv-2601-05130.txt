Sparsity and uniform regularity for regularised optimal transport Thanks: ∗ Corresponding author
Rishabh S. Gvalani ∗, Lukas Koch

Abstract
. We consider regularised quadratic optimal transport with subquadratic polynomial or entropic regularisation. In both cases, we prove interior Lipschitz-estimates on a transport-like map and interior gradient Lipschitz-estimates on the potentials, under the assumption that the transport map solving the unregularised problem is bi- C 1 , α C^{1,\alpha} -regular. For strictly subquadratic and entropic regularisation, the estimates improve to interior C 1 C^{1} and C 2 C^{2} estimates for the transport-like map and the potentials, respectively. Our estimates are uniform in the regularisation parameter. As a consequence of this, we obtain convergence of the transport-like map (resp. the potentials) to the unregularised transport map (resp. Kantorovich potentials) in C loc 0 , 1 − C^{0,1-}_{\mathrm{loc}} (resp. C loc 1 , 1 − C^{1,1-}_{\mathrm{loc}} ). Central to our approach are sharp local bounds on the size of the support for regularised optimal transport which we derive for a general convex, superlinear regularisation term. These bounds are of independent interest and imply global bias bounds for the regularised transport plans. Our global bounds, while not necessarily sharp, improve on the best known results in the literature for quadratic regularisation.



1.
Introduction

In this paper, we study the properties of the minimisers of the following regularised optimal transport (ROT) problem:

(ROT)

\displaystyle OT_{h}(\lambda,\mu)\coloneqq\min_{\pi\in\Pi(\lambda,\mu)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x-y|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\pi+\varepsilon^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}h\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}\pi}{\mathrm{d}P}\right)\,\mathrm{d}P\,,

where
\lambda,\mu\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
have the same mass,
\Pi(\lambda,\mu)
is the set of all couplings between
\lambda,\mu
,
\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})\ni P\ll\lambda\otimes\mu
is a reference measure, and
h:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
is a sufficiently nice function (see
1
), which regularises the quadratic optimal transport problem.
\varepsilon
is the regularisation parameter and we always assume
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
. For the purposes of this paper, we fix
P=\lambda\otimes\mu
. Since we are mainly interested in the continuous setting, we will also assume throughout the paper that
\lambda,\mu
are absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure. We refer the reader to
Section

2.1
for an overview of our notation.

It is well-known that the optimal transport problem is a convex minimisation problem, but not strictly convex. The idea behind using a regularisation such as in (
ROT
) is to obtain a strictly convex problem. Amongst other aspects, this may aid numerical computations
[
9
]
, improve sample complexity
[
14
,
25
]
, and improve the stability of couplings
[
15
,
2
]
.

Regularised optimal transport with the choice
h(z)=z\log(z)
is known as entropic optimal transport and has received a lot of attention in recent years. This stems from the fact that solutions to entropic optimal transport can be easily computed using robust algorithms such as Sinkhorn’s algorithm
[
9
,
4
]
. Moreover, minimisers of entropic optimal transport have strong smoothness properties: in our setting the transport plan is a smooth measure in
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}

[
28
]
. There is by now a vast literature on entropic optimal transport and we refer the reader to
[
31
]
for an introduction to computational aspects and
[
21
,
28
]
for an introduction to the available analytical results. We will focus here on literature that is directly relevant to our results.

A draw-back of entropic regularisation is that minimisers have full support which can make it difficult to compute them efficiently. In particular, this can lead to numerical instabilities
[
5
,
35
]
. This drawback of the entropic regularisation has lead to other regularisations being suggested, in particular quadratic regularisation with
h(z)=|z|^{2}

[
5
,
12
,
23
,
35
]
. In this case, minimisers have numerically been observed to have sparse support. Analytical results confirming these observations have been obtained only recently in
[
34
]
and in
[
18
]
in the scalar setting
d=1
.

This motivates us to focus on the following choice of regularisation:

(2)

h_{p}(z)\coloneqq\begin{cases}\frac{|z|^{p}-1}{p-1}\quad&\text{ for }p\in(1,2]\\
z\log(z)\quad&\text{ for }p=1\end{cases}

We remark that as
z\to 1
,
h_{p}(z)\to h_{1}(z)
.

Some of our results hold for more general regularisations satisfying the following set of assumptions:

Assumption 1
.

h\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is continuous, twice differentiable on
(0,\infty)
, strictly convex, bounded below, and superlinear at
+\infty
.

We will see that sparsity of the support is essentially equivalent to the following additional assumption, see the discussion in
Remark

7
below.

Assumption 2
.

h\colon\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfies
h^{\prime}(0)=0
.

Under
1
, (
ROT
) admits a dual problem of the form

(3)

\displaystyle\sup_{f\in L^{1}(\lambda),g\in L^{1}(\mu)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f\mathrm{d}\lambda+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g\mathrm{d}\mu-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}h^{\ast}\left(\frac{f(x)+g(y)-|x-y|^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\right)\mathrm{d}P,

where
h^{\ast}
is the convex conjugate of
h
. It turns out that the supremum is assumed and that the minimal choice of potentials
(f_{\varepsilon},g_{\varepsilon})
is unique up to constants. The connection to the minimiser
\pi_{\varepsilon}
of (
ROT
) is given through the formula

(4)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\pi_{\varepsilon}}{\mathrm{d}P}=(h^{\prime})^{-1}\left(\frac{f_{\varepsilon}(x)+g_{\varepsilon}(y)-|x-y|^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\right).

Our main object of interest will be the following weighted mean:

(5)

\displaystyle T_{\varepsilon}(x)\coloneqq\left(\int(h^{\prime\prime})^{-1}\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}\pi_{\varepsilon}}{\mathrm{d}P}\right)\mathrm{d}\mu(y)\right)^{-1}\int y(h^{\prime\prime})^{-1}\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}\pi_{\varepsilon}}{\mathrm{d}P}\right)\mathrm{d}\mu(y).

T_{\varepsilon}
is an analogue of the transportation map, since it satisfies a Brenier-type formula:

(6)

\displaystyle T_{\varepsilon}(x)=x-\nabla f_{\varepsilon}(x).

We refer the reader to
Sections

2.3
and
A
for precise statements of these facts.

T_{\varepsilon}
has emerged as a natural quantity in the context of entropic regularisation
[
32
]
, as well as for quadratic regularisation
[
18
,
34
]
.

While the smoothness of potentials in the entropic setting
h=h_{1}
is well-known, available regularity results usually depend exponentially on the regularisation parameter
\varepsilon
, c.f.
[
28
]
. To our knowledge the only regularity results in the context of regularised optimal transport that are independent of
\varepsilon
are in the setting of entropic transport. A uniform
C^{1,\alpha}
-regularity for the potentials is obtained in
[
1
]
for some
\alpha<1
. Furthermore, in
[
20
]
a uniform large-scale Lipschitz-regularity for the optimal transportation plan (formally equivalent to large-scale gradient Lipschitz-regularity at the level of the potentials) is developed. Finally,
\varepsilon
-independent estimates on the modulus of continuity of the entropic potentials played a key role in
[
19
]
, where they were used to deduce a generalisation of Caffarelli’s contraction theorem for optimal transport.

The main result of this paper is a local Lipschitz bound on
T_{\varepsilon}
, independent of
\varepsilon>0
. It applies both in the setting of entropic regularisation and of subquadratic polynomial regularisation.

Theorem 3
.

Let
p\in[1,2]
and
h(z)=h_{p}(z)
. Fix
\alpha\in(0,1]
. Assume
\lambda,\mu
are probability measures, bounded and bounded away from
0
on their support, and with
C^{0,\alpha}
-densities. Denote
\Omega=\mathrm{supp}\;\lambda
. Let
T
be the minimiser of
OT(\lambda,\mu)
and
T_{\varepsilon}
the minimiser of
OT_{h}(\lambda,\mu)
. Assume
T
is bi-
C^{1,\alpha}
-regular on
\Omega
. Then, for any
\omega\Subset\Omega
, there is
C>0
depending only on
d,p,\omega
,
\Omega
,
\|\lambda\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(\Omega)}
,
\|\mu\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(\Omega)}
and
\|T\|_{C^{1,\alpha}(\Omega)}
as well as
\|T^{-1}\|_{C^{1,\alpha}(T(\Omega))}
such that

(7)

\displaystyle\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{x\in\omega}|\nabla T_{\varepsilon}(x)|\leq C.

If
p<2
,
T_{\varepsilon}\in C^{1}(\omega)
and the essential supremum may be replaced by a supremum.

We remark that due to
[
6
]
, if
\mathrm{supp}\;\lambda
and
\mathrm{supp}\;\mu
are convex, then
T
is bi-
C^{1,\alpha}
for some
\alpha\in(0,1]
.

As an immediate consequence of the above result and (
6
) (in the rigorous form of
Lemma

10
), we have the following corollary.

Corollary 4
.

Under the assumptions of
Theorem

3
, we have that for any
\omega\Subset\Omega
, there is
C>0
depending only on
d,p
and
\omega
,
\|\lambda\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(\Omega)}
,
\|\mu\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(\Omega)}
and
\|T\|_{C^{1,\alpha}(\Omega)}
as well as
\|T^{-1}\|_{C^{1,\alpha}(T(\Omega))}
such that

(8)

\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{x\in\omega}|\nabla^{2}f_{\varepsilon}|\leq C\,.

If
p<2
, then
f_{\varepsilon}\in C^{2}(\omega)
and the essential supremum may be replaced by a supremum.

(
3
) suggests that the regularity of the potentials is determined by the regularity of the map
(h^{\prime})^{-1}
. Note that

(9)

\displaystyle(h^{\prime}_{p})^{-1}(\xi)=\begin{cases}\exp(\xi-1)\quad&\text{ if }p=1\\
\frac{p}{p-1}\max(0,\xi)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\quad&\text{ if }p>1.\end{cases}

In particular, potentials are smooth when
p=1
and their regularity decreases as
p
increases. For
p>2
, the potentials are not expected to be twice differentiable. Hence, we expect the range of
p
in our theorem to be optimal.

There are a variety of qualitative convergence results of regularised optimal transport to optimal transport. In the entropic setting, convergence of the transport plans as well as the potentials is well-established, c.f.
[
28
]
. For more general regularisations,
\Gamma
-convergence of the plans was studied in
[
22
]
. In the case of quadratic regularisation, convergence of potentials is also known
[
29
]
.

Theorem

3
immediately allows to strengthen such quantitative convergence results.

Corollary 5
.

Under the assumptions of
Theorem

3
, we have that

(10)

T_{\varepsilon}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\varepsilon\to 0}}{{\to}}T\,,\quad\textrm{in $C^{0,1-}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)$}\,,

and

(11)

f_{\varepsilon}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\varepsilon\to 0}}{{\to}}f,\,g_{\varepsilon}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\varepsilon\to 0}}{{\to}}g\,,\quad\textrm{in $C^{1,1-}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)$}\,,

where
f,g
are Kantorovich potentials of the quadratic transport problem.

The starting point of our proof of Theorem
3
is the fact that
OT_{h_{p}}(\lambda,\mu)
and
OT(\lambda,\mu)\coloneqq OT_{0}(\lambda,\mu)
are close in energy. Rougly speaking,

(12)

\displaystyle OT_{h_{p}}(\lambda,\mu)-OT(\lambda,\mu)\lesssim\varepsilon^{\frac{4}{d(p-1)+2}}.

In the setting of the Schrödinger bridge problem, which is closely related to the entropic transportation problem, c.f.
[
28
]
, such results go back to
[
26
,
27
]
. In fact, in this setting a Taylor expansion of
OT_{h_{1}}
in terms of
\varepsilon
can be obtained up to second order under strong regularity assumptions on the potentials
[
8
]
. In the entropic setting, a second-order Taylor expansion was obtained in
[
7
]
. Under weaker assumptions on the marginals, expansions to first order or estimates as stated above may still hold. We refer the reader to
[
24
]
for a discussion of the available results and an analysis of the seperate energy contribution of the transport term and the regularisation term. In the case of polynomial regularisation the stated rates are due to
[
10
]
.

Interpreting this closeness in energy through the lens of viewing minimisers of regularised optimal transport as quasi-minimisers of quadratic optimal transport, we implement a Campanato iteration to propagate this information to smaller scales. This part of our approach is based on the variational approach to optimal transportation first implemented in
[
16
]
. In
[
33
]
, this approach was generalised to quasi-minimisers at all scales. However, for regularised optimal transport the quasi-minimality information is not available at all scales and in fact at small scales it is not expected to hold. In
[
20
]
we implemented a Campanato iteration down to a critical scale for quasi-minimisers for which the energy contribution of long trajectories is controlled and obtained a large-scale
\varepsilon
-regularity result for entropic optimal transport down to scale
\varepsilon
. Here, we use the maschinery of
[
20
]
to propagate regularity down to a critical scale
R_{c}
for minimisers of
h_{p}
-regularised transport. Finally, we analyse the structure of
T_{\varepsilon}
in (
5
) in order to obtain a regularity result on small scales
R<R_{c}
.

A scaling analysis of (
12
) suggests that the regularisation becomes dominant at the critical length-scale
R_{c}\approx\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{d(p-1)+2}}
. Heuristically, minimisers of regularised optimal transport are related to the heat equation with a diffusion term of order
\varepsilon
and Gaussians with variance
\varepsilon
, the fundamental solution of the heat equation, see e.g.
[
30
]
. For polynomial regularisation, minimisers are related to the Barenblatt solutions of the porous medium equation with diffusion term
\varepsilon\nabla\rho^{p}

[
13
]
. The expansion rate of the support of the corresponding Barenblatt solutions agrees with the rate in (
12
).

In both the large-scale regime
R>R_{c}
and the small-scale regime
R<R_{c}
it is crucial for us to have good control over long trajectories. The following bound on the support of regularised minimisers will be crucial.

Theorem 6
.

Assume
h\colon\mathbb{R}+\to\mathbb{R}
satisfies Assumption
1
and Assumption
2
and fix
L\in(0,\infty)
. Then, there exists an
R_{c}=R_{c}(\varepsilon,d,L)>0
such that for all

the following holds:

If

\inf_{B_{r}(z)\subset B_{2R}}\frac{\lambda(B_{r}(z))}{r^{d}}\eqqcolon D>0\,,

is a minimiser of (

The only available results proving sparsity of the support for regularised optimal transport problems concern

are given in

heavily rely on the precise structure of minimisers of quadratically regularised optimal transport, our proof builds on the local

as arising from the quasi-minimality with respect to quadratic optimal transport. They agree with the bounds obtained in the unregularised setting. The term

2

Remark 7
.

The assumption
h^{\prime}(0)=0
is essentially equivalent to assuming the existence of
C_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
such that
h^{\prime}(x)\geq C_{0}
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Indeed, assuming the existence of such
C_{0}
, since
h
is twice differentiable and convex, we may extend
h^{\prime}
continuously to
\mathbb{R}_{+}\cup\{0\}
by setting
h^{\prime}(0)=C_{0}
. Consider
\tilde{h}(x)=h(x)-C_{0}x
. Then
\tilde{h}
is twice differentiable, strictly convex and satisfies
\tilde{h}^{\prime}(0)=0
. Moreover,

(15)

\displaystyle\int\tilde{h}\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}\pi}{\mathrm{d}P}\right)\mathrm{d}P=

\displaystyle\int h\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}\pi}{\mathrm{d}P}\right)\mathrm{d}P-C_{0}\int\mathrm{d}\pi=\int h\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}\pi}{\mathrm{d}P}\right)\mathrm{d}P-C_{0}\lambda(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

In particular, minimality of (
ROT
) is unaffected by the change

.

If
\lim_{z\to 0}h^{\prime}(z)=-\infty
and
\pi
is a minimiser of (
ROT
), then
\mathrm{supp}\;\pi=\mathrm{supp}\;P
. Indeed, it holds (see (

)) that

(16)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\pi}{\mathrm{d}P}=(h^{\prime})^{-1}\left(\frac{f(x)+g(y)-|x-y|^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\right)

for almost every

. Thus, if
\frac{\mathrm{d}\pi}{\mathrm{d}P}=0
on a set of positive

-measure, then

is infinite-valued on a set of positive

-measure. As

, this is impossible.

While our approach does not seem to yield global bounds with the expected rate

, we can obtain a global bias bound, generalising

Corollary 8

2.2.
A general monotonicity principle

Monotonicity properties of minimisers to (
ROT
) play a key role in providing

-bounds on their support. We state a general monotonicity principle that can essentially be found in
[
3
, Lemma 1.3]

Proposition 9
.

Let
\pi
be a minimiser of (
ROT
). Assume Assumption
1
is satisfied. Then for any
(x,y),(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\in\mathrm{supp}\;\pi
it holds that

(20)

h^{\prime}\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}\pi}{\mathrm{d}P}(x,y)\right)+h^{\prime}\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}\pi}{\mathrm{d}P}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\right)\leq\frac{\Delta}{\varepsilon^{2}}+h^{\prime}\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}\pi}{\mathrm{d}P}(x^{\prime},y)\right)+h^{\prime}\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}\pi}{\mathrm{d}P}(x,y^{\prime})\right)

where

(21)

\displaystyle\Delta=\Delta(x,y,x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\coloneqq|x-y^{\prime}|^{2}+|x^{\prime}-y|^{2}-|x-y|^{2}-|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|^{2}.

Proof.

The proposition follows from an easy adaption of
[
3
, Lemma 1.3]
.

2.3.
Qualitative theory of minimisers of regularised transport

In this section, we recall the qualitative theory of minimisers of (
ROT
). This follows by fairly standard arguments but for the sake of completeness we provide the complete arguments in
Appendix

A
. Throughout this section, we fix a minimiser
\pi
of (
ROT
) and assume that
1
holds. Note in particular, that then
h^{\prime}
is strictly monotonic increasing and hence has a strictly monotonic increasing inverse
(h^{\prime})^{-1}
on its image. Moreover, denoting by
h^{\ast}
the convex conjugate of
h
,
(h^{\ast})^{\prime}=(h^{\prime})^{-1}
and
(h^{\ast})^{\prime\prime}(h^{\prime}(z))=(h^{\prime\prime})^{-1}(z)
.

There exist continuous potentials
f,g
, which are unique up to additive constants, such that for every
x\in\mathrm{supp}\;\lambda
,
y\in\mathrm{supp}\;\mu
,
f,g
satisfy the following first-order optimality conditions:

(22)

\displaystyle 1=

\displaystyle\int(h^{\prime})^{-1}\left(\frac{(f(x)+g(y)-|x-y|^{2})_{+}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\right)\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)

(23)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int(h^{\prime})^{-1}\left(\frac{(f(x)+g(y)-|x-y|^{2})_{+}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\right)\mathrm{d}\mu(x)

The relation to
\pi
is given through the identity:

(25)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\pi}{\mathrm{d}P}(x,y)=(h^{\prime})^{-1}\left(\frac{(f(x)+g(y)-|x-y|^{2})}{\varepsilon^{2}}\right).

Consequently,

(26)

\displaystyle\mathrm{supp}\;\pi=\{(x,y)\colon f(x)+g(y)-|x-y|^{2}\geq 0\}.

It is convenient to write
\psi(x,y)=f(x)+g(y)-|x-y|^{2}

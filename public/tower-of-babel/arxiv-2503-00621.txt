Endoscopic decomposition of elliptic Fargues–Scholze L L -packets Thanks: The research was partially supported by ERC grant 101142781. The research of Y.V. was partially supported by the ISF grant 2091/21.
David Kazhdan, Yakov Varshavsky

Abstract
. The main goal of this note is to show that the local L L -packets of Fargues–Scholze [ FS ] , corresponding to elliptic L L -parameters, admit an endoscopic decomposition. Our argument is strongly motivated by a beautiful paper of Chenji Fu [ Fu ] , where the stable case is proven. To put our results in a more general context, we also construct a general endoscopic decomposition over ℂ \mathbb{C} based on results of Arthur, and a generalization of this decomposition over an arbitrary algebraically closed field of characteristic zero based on a recent work [ KSV ] .



Formulation of results

0.1
.

Notation.

(a) Let
F
be a local non-archimedean field of characteristic zero, let
\Gamma:=\Gamma_{F}
be the absolute Galois group of
F
, let
W_{F}\subseteq\Gamma
be the Weil group of
F
, and let
\ell
a prime different from the residue characteristic of
F
. Untill Section
6
, all functions will be
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
-valued.

(b) Let
G
be a connected reductive group over
F
, and let
\widehat{G}
the connected Langlands dual group over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
. Then
\widehat{G}
is equipped with an action of
\Gamma
(canonical up to a conjugacy), so we can form the semidirect product
{}^{L}G:=\widehat{G}\rtimes W_{F}
.

(c) Let
G^{\operatorname{sr}}\subseteq G
be the (open) locus of strongly regular semisimple elements of
G
, and let
G(F)^{\operatorname{ell}}\subseteq G^{\operatorname{sr}}(F)
be the (open) locus of elliptic elements.

(d) Let
C^{\infty}_{\operatorname{inv}}(G^{\operatorname{sr}}(F))
and
C^{\infty}_{\operatorname{inv}}(G(F)^{\operatorname{ell}})
be the spaces of
\operatorname{Ad}G(F)
-invariant locally constant functions on
G^{\operatorname{sr}}(F)
and
G(F)^{\operatorname{ell}}
, respectively, and let
\widehat{C}_{\operatorname{inv}}(G(F))
be the space of
\operatorname{Ad}G(F)
-invariant generalized functions on
G(F)
.

0.2
.

Smooth irreducible representations.
(a) We denote by
\operatorname{Irr}(G(F))
the set of isomorphism classes of smooth irreducible representations
G(F)
over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
.

(b) For every
\pi\in\operatorname{Irr}(G(F))
, we denote its character by
\chi_{\pi}\in\widehat{C}_{\operatorname{inv}}(G(F))
, and we denote by

\Theta(G(F))\subseteq\widehat{C}_{\operatorname{inv}}(G(F))
the
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
-linear span of
\{\chi_{\pi}\}_{\pi\in\operatorname{Irr}(G(F))}
.

(c) By a theorem of Harish–Chandra, the restriction
f^{\operatorname{sr}}:=f|_{G^{\operatorname{sr}}(F)}
to
G(F)^{\operatorname{sr}}
of every
f\in\Theta(G(F))
is a locally constant function, and thus defines an element of
{C}^{\infty}_{\operatorname{inv}}(G^{\operatorname{sr}}(F))
. We denote by
f^{\operatorname{ell}}\in C^{\infty}_{\operatorname{inv}}(G(F)^{\operatorname{ell}})
the restriction of
f
to
G(F)^{\operatorname{ell}}
.

0.3
.

Fargues–Scholze
L
-packets.

(a) To every
\pi\in\operatorname{Irr}(G(F))
, Fargues and Scholze
[
FS
]
associate a semisimple Langlands parameter

\varphi_{\pi}:W_{F}\to{}^{L}G(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})
.

(b) Conversely, to every semisimple
L
-parameter
\varphi
we associate a subset

\Pi^{FS}_{\varphi}:=\{\pi\in\operatorname{Irr}(G(F))\,|\,\varphi^{FS}_{\pi}=\pi\}

and call it the Fargues–Scholze
L
-packet.

(c) For every
L
-parameter
\varphi
as in part (b), we denote by
\Theta_{\varphi}\subseteq\Theta(G(F))
the span of
\{\chi_{\pi}\}_{\pi\in\Pi^{FS}_{\varphi}}
.

0.4
.

Elliptic Langlands parameters.

(a) For every (semisimple)
L
-parameters
\varphi
(see Section
0.3
(a)), we denote by
S_{\varphi}:=Z_{\widehat{G}}(\varphi)\subseteq\widehat{G}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})
the centralizer of
\varphi
. Then
S_{\varphi}\supseteq Z(\widehat{G})^{\Gamma}
, and we set

\overline{S}_{\varphi}:=S_{\varphi}/Z(\widehat{G})^{\Gamma}\subseteq\widehat{G}^{\operatorname{ad}}
.

(b) Assume now that the
L
-parameter
\varphi
is
elliptic
, by which we mean that the quotient

\overline{S}_{\varphi}:=S_{\varphi}/Z(\widehat{G})
is finite.

(c) Following Langlands
[
La
]
, every
\overline{S}_{\varphi}
-conjugacy class
[\kappa]\subseteq\overline{S}_{\varphi}
gives rise to the endoscopic datum
\cal E_{\varphi,[\kappa]}
of
G
, unique up to an equivalence (see Section
1.5
). Thus, we can talk about
\cal E_{\varphi,[\kappa]}
-stable generalized functions (see Section
1.7
).

0.5
.

The canonical decomposition.
(a) Let
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}[\overline{S}_{\varphi}]^{\overline{S}_{\varphi}}
be the algebra of
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
-valued functions on
\overline{S}_{\varphi}
, invariant under conjugation. The spectral action of Fargues–Scholze induces an action of the
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
-algebra
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}[\overline{S}_{\varphi}]^{\overline{S}_{\varphi}}
on the
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
-vector space
\Theta_{\varphi}
(see Section
3.10
).

(b) For every
\overline{S}_{\varphi}
-conjugacy class
[\kappa]\subseteq\overline{S}_{\varphi}
, we denote by
1_{[\kappa]}\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}[\overline{S}_{\varphi}]^{\overline{S}_{\varphi}}
the characteristic function of
[\kappa]
. Then we have an identity

\sum_{[\kappa]}1_{[\kappa]}=1\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}[\overline{S}_{\varphi}]^{\overline{S}_{\varphi}}
.

(c) Combining observations of parts (a) and (b), for every
f\in\Theta_{\varphi}
we have a natural decomposition
f=\sum_{[\kappa]}f_{[\kappa]}
, where for every
\overline{S}_{\varphi}
-conjugacy class
[\kappa]\subseteq\overline{S}_{\varphi}
, we set
f_{[\kappa]}:=1_{[\kappa]}(f)\in\Theta_{\varphi}
.

Now we are ready to formulate the main result of this note.

Theorem 0.6
.

For every elliptic
L
-parameter
\varphi
, every
f\in\Theta_{\varphi}
and every
[\kappa]\subseteq\overline{S}_{\varphi}
, the generalized function

f_{[\kappa]}\in\Theta_{\varphi}
is
\cal E_{\varphi,[\kappa]}
-stable.

0.7
.

Remark.
Both Theorem
0.6
and its proof were motivated by a recent work of Chenji Fu
[
Fu
]
, who established it in the case
[\kappa]=1
.
On the other hand, even in the case
[\kappa]=1
our argument is slightly different. Namely, instead of equi-distribution properties of the weight multiplicities of highest weight representations of a reductive group we use the fact that the space of class functions
is generated by traces of irreducible representations.

0.8
.

Outline of the argument.
Our proof of Theorem
0.6
consists of three steps. First, we use (a reformulation of) the character formula of Hansen–Kaletha–Weinstein
[
HKW
]
to show the
\cal E_{\varphi,[\kappa]}
-stability of the restriction
f^{\operatorname{ell}}_{[\kappa]}:=(f_{[\kappa]})|_{G(F)^{\operatorname{ell}}}
when the center
Z(G)
is connected. Then, we use
z
-embeddings of Kaletha
[
Ka
]
to establish the
\cal E_{\varphi,[\kappa]}
-stability of
f^{\operatorname{ell}}_{[\kappa]}
in general. Finally, we use the results of Arthur
[
Ar1
,
Ar2
]
in the formulation of Moeglin–Waldspurger
[
MW1
]
in order to deduce the
\cal E_{\varphi,[\kappa]}
-stability of
f_{[\kappa]}
from the
\cal E_{\varphi,[\kappa]}
-stability of
f^{\operatorname{ell}}_{[\kappa]}
.

0.9
.

General endoscopic decomposition.
(a) Using results of Arthur, for every field isomorphism
\iota:\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}\overset{\thicksim}{\to}\mathbb{C}
, the space
\Theta(G(F))
has a natural decomposition

\Theta(G(F))=\bigoplus_{\cal E}\Theta_{\cal E}(G(F)),

where
\cal E
runs over equivalences classes of endoscopic data (see Appendix
A
).

(b) More precisely, the decomposition of part (a) is obtained from a combination of a decomposition of
\Theta(G(F))
in terms of
virtual elliptic characters in the sense of Arthur for different Levi subgroups of
G
, and a decomposition of the span of virtual elliptic characters in terms of elliptic endoscopic data.

(c) (A version of) Langlands conjecture asserts that for every semisimple
L
-parameter
\varphi
and every field isomorphism
\iota
, the subspace
\Theta_{\varphi}
of Section
0.3
is
compatible with the decomposition
of part (a), that is, we have a decomposition

\Theta_{\varphi}=\bigoplus_{\cal E}(\Theta_{\cal E}(G(F))\cap\Theta_{\varphi}).

(d) Theorem
0.6
establishes (a more precise version of) the conjecture of part (c) for elliptic
L
-parameters.

0.10
.

Independence of
\iota
.
(a) A natural question is whether the decomposition of Section
0.9
(a) is independent of the field isomorphism
\iota
. More generally, one can ask whether this decomposition can be defined over an arbitrary algebraically closed field of zero characteristic.

(b) In Appendix
B
we give a positive answer to the question of part (a) based on a recent work
[
KSV
]
. This can be considered to be a counterpart of the recent result of Scholze
[
Sc
]
, asserting that the Fargues–Scholze correspondence can be done motivically and thus is independent of
\ell
and
\iota
.

1.
\cal E
-stability

1.1
.

Langlands dual groups.

(a) For every connected reductive group
H
over
F
, we denote by
\widehat{H}
the (connected) Langlands dual group over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
. Then
\widehat{H}
is equipped with a canonical continuous homomorphism
\rho_{H}:\Gamma\to\operatorname{Out}(\widehat{H})
. In particular, we have a natural action of

\Gamma
on
Z(\widehat{H})
, so we can consider the group of fixed points
Z(\widehat{H})^{\Gamma}
. Moreover,
\rho_{H}
lifts to an action of
\Gamma
on
\widehat{H}
, canonically up to an
\widehat{H}
-conjugacy.

(b) Every embedding
\mathfrak{a}:T\hookrightarrow H
of a maximal torus over
F
gives rise to a
\Gamma
-invariant
\widehat{H}
-conjugacy class of embeddings

[\widehat{\mathfrak{a}}]:\widehat{T}\hookrightarrow\widehat{H}
of maximal tori, hence to a canonical
\Gamma
-equivariant embedding
Z(\widehat{H})\hookrightarrow\widehat{T}
.

1.2
.

Galois cohomology.

(a) As it was shown by Borovoi
[
Bo
]
, for every connected reductive group
H
over
F
, the Galois cohomology

H^{1}(F,H)
has the natural structure of a finite abelian group isomorphic to
\pi_{1}(H)_{\Gamma,\operatorname{tor}}
, where
\pi_{1}
stands for the algebraic fundamendal group,
(-)_{\Gamma}
stands for coinvariants, and
(-)_{\operatorname{tor}}
stands for torsion. Furthermore, the isomorphism

H^{1}(F,H)\overset{\thicksim}{\to}\pi_{1}(H)_{\Gamma,\operatorname{tor}}
is functorial in
H
.

(b) Part (a) implies that we have a natural isomorphism
H^{1}(F,H)^{\vee}\simeq\pi_{0}(Z(\widehat{H})^{\Gamma})
, where
(-)^{\vee}
stands for the dual abelian group. Moreover, for every maximal torus
T\subseteq H
, we have a canonical isomorphism

\operatorname{Ker}[H^{1}(F,T)\to H^{1}(F,H)]^{\vee}\simeq\pi_{0}(\widehat{T}^{\Gamma}/Z(\widehat{H})^{\Gamma}),

first established by Kottwitz
[
Ko
]
.

1.3
.

Stable conjugacy class.
Fix
\gamma\in G^{\operatorname{sr}}(F)
.

(a) We denote by
\operatorname{Orb}^{\operatorname{st}}_{\gamma}:=\operatorname{Orb}^{\operatorname{st}}_{G(F)}(\gamma)\subseteq G(F)^{\operatorname{ell}}
the
stable conjugacy class
of
\gamma
,
that is, the set of all
\gamma^{\prime}\in G(F)
which are
G(\overline{F})
-conjugate to
\gamma
, and let
[\operatorname{Orb}^{\operatorname{st}}_{\gamma}]
be the set of
G(F)
-orbits in
\operatorname{Orb}^{\operatorname{st}}_{\gamma}
.

(b) Let
G_{\gamma}\subseteq G
be the centralizer of
\gamma
, and consider a finite abelian group

A_{\gamma}:=\operatorname{Ker}[H^{1}(F,G_{\gamma})\to H^{1}(F,G)].

Then
[\operatorname{Orb}^{\operatorname{st}}_{\gamma}]
has a natural stucture of an
A_{\gamma}
-torsor. Namely, for
\overline{\gamma}^{\prime},\overline{\gamma}^{\prime\prime}\in[\operatorname{Orb}^{\operatorname{st}}_{\gamma}]
one can form
a relative position
\operatorname{inv}(\overline{\gamma}^{\prime},\overline{\gamma}^{\prime\prime})\in A_{\gamma}
(compare
[
BV
, Section 4.1.2]
), and the action of
A_{\gamma}
on
[\operatorname{Orb}^{\operatorname{st}}_{\gamma}]
is defined by the rule
\operatorname{inv}(\overline{\gamma}^{\prime},\overline{\gamma}^{\prime\prime})(\overline{\gamma}^{\prime})=\overline{\gamma}^{\prime\prime}
.

(c) Let
\widehat{G}_{\gamma}
be the connected Langlands dual group of
G_{\gamma}
. Then, by Section
1.2
(b), the dual abelian group
A_{\gamma}^{\vee}
is canonically isomorphic to
\pi_{0}((\widehat{G}_{\gamma})^{\Gamma}/Z(\widehat{G})^{\Gamma})
. In particular, every element
\xi\in(\widehat{G}_{\gamma})^{\Gamma}/Z(\widehat{G})^{\Gamma}
defines an element
\overline{\xi}\in A_{\gamma}^{\vee}
.

1.4
.

Notation.
Fix
\gamma\in G^{\operatorname{sr}}(F)
.

(a) Let
\operatorname{Fun}([\operatorname{Orb}^{\operatorname{st}}_{\gamma}])
be the space of
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
-valued functions on
[\operatorname{Orb}^{\operatorname{st}}_{\gamma}]
.
Then the finite abelian group
A_{\gamma}
has a natural action on
\operatorname{Fun}([\operatorname{Orb}^{\operatorname{st}}_{\gamma}])
such that for every
\lambda\in A_{\gamma}
,
\overline{\gamma}^{\prime}\in[\operatorname{Orb}^{\operatorname{st}}_{\gamma}]
and
f\in\operatorname{Fun}([\operatorname{Orb}^{\operatorname{st}}_{\gamma}])
we have

\lambda(f)(\overline{\gamma}^{\prime})=f(\lambda(\overline{\gamma}^{\prime}))
.

(b) For
\overline{\xi}\in A_{\gamma}^{\vee}
, let
\operatorname{Fun}([\operatorname{Orb}^{\operatorname{st}}_{\gamma}])_{\overline{\xi}}\subseteq\operatorname{Fun}([\operatorname{Orb}^{\operatorname{st}}_{\gamma}])
be the subspace of
\overline{\xi}
-eigenvectors of
A_{\gamma}
. Then each
\operatorname{Fun}([\operatorname{Orb}^{\operatorname{st}}_{\gamma}])_{\overline{\xi}}
is a one dimensional
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
-vector space, and
we have a decomposition
\operatorname{Fun}([\operatorname{Orb}^{\operatorname{st}}_{\gamma}])=\bigoplus_{\overline{\xi}\in A_{\gamma}^{\vee}}\operatorname{Fun}([\operatorname{Orb}^{\operatorname{st}}_{\gamma}])_{\overline{\xi}}
.

(c) For a semisimple conjugacy class
[s]\subseteq\widehat{G}^{\operatorname{ad}}
, let
\operatorname{Fun}([\operatorname{Orb}^{\operatorname{st}}_{\gamma}])_{[s]}\subseteq\operatorname{Fun}([\operatorname{Orb}^{\operatorname{st}}_{\gamma}])
be the span of
\operatorname{Fun}([\operatorname{Orb}^{\operatorname{st}}_{\gamma}])_{\overline{\xi}}
, where
\xi
runs over
[(\widehat{G}_{\gamma})^{\Gamma}/Z(\widehat{G})^{\Gamma}]\cap[s]\subseteq\widehat{G}^{\operatorname{ad}}
, and, as in Section
1.3
(c),
\overline{\xi}\in A_{\gamma}^{\vee}
denotes the
class of
\xi\in(\widehat{G}_{\gamma})^{\Gamma}/Z(\widehat{G})^{\Gamma}
.

(d) For every function
f\in C_{\operatorname{inv}}(G^{\operatorname{sr}}(F))
, its restriction

\operatorname{res}_{\gamma}(f):=f|_{\operatorname{Orb}^{\operatorname{st}}_{\gamma}}
is a
G(F)
-invariant function on
\operatorname{Orb}^{\operatorname{st}}_{\gamma}
, and thus defines an element of

\operatorname{Fun}([\operatorname{Orb}^{\operatorname{st}}_{\gamma}])
.

1.5
.

Endoscopic data
(compare
[
Ko
,
\lx@sectionsign
7]
and
[
KV1
, Section 1.3]
).
Suppose we are in the situation of Section
1.1
.

(a) Slightly modifying the terminology of
[
Ko
,
\lx@sectionsign
7]
, by an
endoscopic datum
for
G
, we mean a triple
\cal E=(s,\widehat{H},\rho)
, where
s\in\widehat{G}^{\operatorname{ad}}
is a semisimple element,
\widehat{H}:=Z_{\widehat{G}}(s)^{0}\subseteq\widehat{G}
the connected centralizer, and
\rho:\Gamma\to\operatorname{Out}(\widehat{H})
a continuous homomorphism satisfying the following conditions:

\bullet
the
\widehat{G}
-conjugacy class of the inclusion
\widehat{H}\hookrightarrow\widehat{G}
is
\Gamma
-invariant, and thus the inclusion
Z(\widehat{G})\hookrightarrow Z(\widehat{H})
is
\Gamma
-invariant;

\bullet
we have
s\in Z(\widehat{H})^{\Gamma}/Z(\widehat{G})^{\Gamma}\subseteq\widehat{G}^{\operatorname{ad}}
.

(b) We call two endoscopic data
\cal E=(s,\widehat{H},\rho)
and
\cal E^{\prime}=(s^{\prime},\widehat{H}^{\prime},\rho^{\prime})

equivalent
and write
\cal E\simeq\cal E^{\prime}
, if there exists
g\in\widehat{G}
such that
\operatorname{Ad}_{g}(\widehat{H})=\widehat{H}^{\prime}
,
\operatorname{Ad}_{g}\circ\rho=\rho^{\prime}
and elements

\operatorname{Ad}_{g}(s),s^{\prime}\in Z(\widehat{H}^{\prime})^{\Gamma}
have the same image in
\pi_{0}(Z(\widehat{H}^{\prime})^{\Gamma}/Z(\widehat{G})^{\Gamma})
.

(c) We call an endoscopic datum
\cal E=(s,\widehat{H},\rho)

elliptic
, if the group
Z(\widehat{H})^{\Gamma}/Z(\widehat{G})^{\Gamma}
is finite.

1.6
.

Endoscopic data corresponding to Langlands parameters.
Let
\varphi:W_{F}\to{}^{L}G(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})
be an
L
-parameter, and let
\kappa\in\overline{S}_{\varphi}
be a semisimple element.

(a) As in
[
La
]
, a pair
(\varphi,\kappa)
gives rise to the endoscopic datum
\cal E_{\varphi,\kappa}=(s,\widehat{H},\rho)
, where
s:=\kappa
and
\rho:\Gamma\to\operatorname{Out}(\widehat{H})
is characterised by the condition that the restriction
\rho|_{W_{F}}
is the composition

(1.1)

W_{F}\overset{\varphi}{\longrightarrow}Z_{{}^{L}G}(s)\overset{\operatorname{Ad}}{\longrightarrow}\operatorname{Aut}(\widehat{H})\overset{\operatorname{pr}}{\longrightarrow}\operatorname{Out}(\widehat{H}).

Namely, it is easy to see that the composition (
1.1
) is continuous with a finite image, so it uniquely extends to the whole of
\Gamma
.

(b) By the construction of part (a), we have an equality
Z(\widehat{H})^{\Gamma}=Z_{Z(\widehat{H})}(\varphi)
. Therefore we have an inclusion

Z(\widehat{H})^{\Gamma}\subseteq Z_{\widehat{G}}(\varphi)=S_{\varphi}
. In particular, for every elliptic
L
-parameter
\varphi
, the endoscopic datum
\cal E_{\varphi,\kappa}
is elliptic.

1.7
.

\cal E
-stable functions.
Let
\cal E=(s,\widehat{H},\rho)
be an endoscopic datum for
G
.

(a) Let
\gamma\in G^{\operatorname{sr}}(F)
, and let
\mathfrak{a}_{\gamma}:G_{\gamma}\hookrightarrow G
be the inclusion. For an element
\xi\in(\widehat{G}_{\gamma})^{\Gamma}/Z(\widehat{G})^{\Gamma}
, we say that
(\gamma,\xi)\in\cal E
if there exists an embedding
\widehat{\mathfrak{a}_{\gamma}}:\widehat{G}_{\gamma}\hookrightarrow\widehat{G}
in
[\widehat{\mathfrak{a}_{\gamma}}]
(see Section
1.1
(b)) such that

\bullet
we have
\widehat{\mathfrak{a}_{\gamma}}(\xi)=s
, thus
\widehat{\mathfrak{a}_{\gamma}}(\widehat{G}_{\gamma})\subseteq\widehat{H}
;

\bullet
the
\widehat{H}
-conjugacy class of the inclusion
\widehat{\mathfrak{a}_{\gamma}}:\widehat{G}_{\gamma}\hookrightarrow\widehat{H}
is
\Gamma
-invariant.

(b) We say that a function
f\in\operatorname{Fun}([\operatorname{Orb}^{\operatorname{st}}_{\gamma}])
is
\cal E
-stable
, if it belongs to the span of the subspaces

\operatorname{Fun}([\operatorname{Orb}^{\operatorname{st}}_{\gamma}])_{\overline{\xi}}
, where
\xi
runs over all elements of
(\widehat{G}_{\gamma})^{\Gamma}/Z(\widehat{G})^{\Gamma}
such that
(\gamma,\xi)\in\cal E
.

(c) We say that a function
f\in C^{\infty}_{\operatorname{inv}}(G^{\operatorname{sr}}(F))
(resp.
f\in C^{\infty}_{\operatorname{inv}}(G^{\operatorname{ell}}(F))
) is
\cal E
-stable
, if its restriction
f|_{\operatorname{Orb}^{\operatorname{st}}_{\gamma}}\in\operatorname{Fun}([\operatorname{Orb}^{\operatorname{st}}_{\gamma}])
is
\cal E
-stable for every
\gamma\in G^{\operatorname{sr}}(F)

(resp.
f\in C^{\infty}_{\operatorname{inv}}(G^{\operatorname{ell}}(F))
). We denote the space of
\cal E
-stable invariant functions by

C^{\infty}_{\cal E-\operatorname{st}}(G(F)^{\operatorname{ell}})\subset C^{\infty}_{\operatorname{inv}}(G(F)^{\operatorname{ell}})
.

(d) Note that a function
f\in C^{\infty}_{\operatorname{inv}}(G^{\operatorname{sr}}(F))
, whose support is closed in
G(F)
, defines a generalized function on
G(F)
.
We say that an element
h
in the Hecke algebra
\cal H(G(F))
is
\cal E
-unstable
, if for every
f\in C^{\infty}_{\operatorname{inv}}(G^{\operatorname{sr}}(F))
, whose support is closed in
G(F)
, we have
\langle f,h\rangle=0
.

(e) We say that a generalized function
f\in\widehat{C}_{\operatorname{inv}}(G(F))
is
\cal E
-stable
, if its satisfies
\langle f,h\rangle=0
for every
\cal E
-unstable
h\in\cal H(G(F))
. We denote the space of
\cal E
-stable invariant generalized functions by
\widehat{C}_{\cal E-\operatorname{st}}(G(F))\subseteq\widehat{C}_{\operatorname{inv}}(G(F))
.

1.8
.

Remarks.
(a)
\cal E
-stability only depends on the equivalence class of
\cal E
.

(b) For every connected reductive group
G
, we have a trivial endoscopic datum
\cal E_{G,\operatorname{triv}}=\cal E_{\operatorname{triv}}:=(1,\widehat{G},\rho_{G})
. Then a function
f\in C^{\infty}_{\operatorname{inv}}(G^{\operatorname{sr}}(F))
is
\cal E_{\operatorname{triv}}
-stable if and only if
its restriction to each stable orbit
\operatorname{Orb}^{\operatorname{st}}_{\gamma}
is constant, and such functions are usually called
stable
.

(c) Note that if
f\in C^{\infty}_{\operatorname{inv}}(G^{\operatorname{sr}}(F))
is
\cal E
-stable, then its restriction
f|_{\operatorname{Orb}^{\operatorname{st}}_{\gamma}}
belongs to
\operatorname{Fun}([\operatorname{Orb}^{\operatorname{st}}_{\gamma}])_{[s]}
for every
\gamma\in G^{\operatorname{sr}}(F)
. Furthermore, the converse assertion also holds, if the center
Z(G)
of
G
is connected (see
[
BV
, Section 4.1.9]
).

(d) For every pair
(\gamma,\xi)
as in Section
1.7
(a) there exists a unique endoscopic datum

\cal E=(s,\widehat{H},\rho)
such that
(\gamma,\xi)\in\cal E
(compare
[
BV
, Sections 4.1.7(a),(b)]
). Furthermore,

\cal E
is elliptic, if
\gamma\in G(F)
is elliptic.

(e) In words, an invariant generalized function is
\cal E
-stable, if it lies in the closure of the span of all
\cal E
-stable functions on

(f) Following

The Chevalley space.

(a) We have a natural embedding

(b) The map

\operatorname{can}_{\gamma}:=\operatorname{pr}_{\gamma}^{*}:\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}[c_{\widehat{G}^{\operatorname{ad}}}]\to\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}[(\widehat{G}_{\gamma})^{\Gamma}/Z(\widehat{G})^{\Gamma}].

(b) The action of the group

on

1.4
(a) induces an action of the group algebra

Z

2.9
.

Proof of Theorem
0.6
.

We divide our proof into three steps:

Step 1.
Assume first that
Z(G)
is connected. In this case, we are going to deduce the
\cal E_{\varphi,[\kappa]}
-stability of
f_{[\kappa]}^{\operatorname{ell}}
from Proposition
2.6
.

By Section
1.8
(c), the function
f^{\operatorname{ell}}_{[\kappa]}\in C^{\infty}_{\operatorname{inv}}(G(F)^{\operatorname{ell}})
is
\cal E_{\varphi,[\kappa]}
-stable if and only if
for every
\gamma\in G(F)^{\operatorname{ell}}
, its restriction
\operatorname{res}_{\gamma}(f^{\operatorname{ell}}_{[\kappa]})=\operatorname{res}_{\gamma}(f_{[\kappa]})\in\operatorname{Fun}([\operatorname{Orb}^{\operatorname{st}}_{\gamma}])

belongs to
\operatorname{Fun}([\operatorname{Orb}^{\operatorname{st}}_{\gamma}])_{[\kappa]}
. Thus, by Lemma
2.4
, it suffices to show that
\operatorname{res}_{\gamma}(f_{[\kappa]})
is a
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}[c_{\widehat{G}^{\operatorname{ad}}}]
-eigenfunction with eigencharacter

.

Then, by Proposition
2.6
, it suffices to show that
f_{[\kappa]}=1_{[\kappa]}(f)
is a
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}[c_{\widehat{G}^{\operatorname{ad}}}]
-eigenfunction with eigencharacter
\zeta_{[\kappa]}
. Since for every
h\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}[c_{\widehat{G}^{\operatorname{ad}}}]
we have equalities

h(1_{[\kappa]}(f))=((h|_{\overline{S}_{\varphi}})\cdot 1_{[\kappa]})(f)\text{ and }(h|_{\overline{S}_{\varphi}})\cdot 1_{[\kappa]}=\zeta_{[\kappa]}(h)\cdot 1_{[\kappa]},

the assertion follows.

Step 2.
For a general
G
, we deduce the
\cal E_{\varphi,[\kappa]}
-stability of

from the case of
groups with connected center using
z
-embeddings.

We choose a
z
-embedding
\iota:G\to G_{1}
(use
[
Ka
, Corollary 5.3]
), and let

and
[\kappa_{1}]\subseteq\overline{S}_{\varphi_{1}}
be as in Sections
2.7
(b),(c). By Proposition
2.8
(a), there exists a unique element

such that
f_{1}|_{G(F)}=f
. Moreover, by Proposition
2.8
(b), the identity
(\widehat{\iota}_{\varphi})^{*}(1_{[\kappa]})=1_{[\kappa_{1}]}
from Section
2.7
(d) implies that

Since the center

is connected, we conclude by Step 1 that each

-stable. Hence, the function

Step 3.
Using the fact

∎

(a) For every

(a) We have to show that

5.2

Proof of part (b).

For every finite set

6.
Elliptic virtual characters, and proof of Propositions
1.9

In this and the next section, we choose a field isomorphism
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}\overset{\thicksim}{\to}\mathbb{C}
and consider representations to be
\mathbb{C}
-valued rather than
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
-valued.

6.1
.

Elliptic virtual characters.

(a) Let
\Theta_{\operatorname{ell}}(G(F))\subset\Theta(G(F))
be the linear space of characters
\chi\otimes\mu
, where
\chi\in\Theta(G(F))
is an elliptic tempered character in the sense of Arthur
[
Ar1
]
, and
\mu:G(F)\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
is a homomorphism.

(b) By definition, a character
\chi_{\pi}
of every supercuspidal representation
\pi
belongs to
\Theta_{\operatorname{ell}}(G(F))
. More generally, the same is true for every
\pi\in\operatorname{Irr}(G(F))
, whose restriction to
G^{\operatorname{der}}(F)
is square-integrable.

(c) It follows from
[
Ar1
, Section 6]
that every nonzero
\chi\in\Theta_{\operatorname{ell}}(G(F))
has a nonzero restriction to

G(F)^{\operatorname{ell}}
.

(d) We denote by
\Psi_{\operatorname{ell}}(G)
the set of equivalences classes of elliptic endoscopic data for
G
.

(e) For every
\cal E\in\Psi_{\operatorname{ell}}(G)
, we denote by
\Theta_{\cal E-\operatorname{st}}(G(F))\subseteq\Theta(G(F))
the subspace of
\cal E
-stable generalized functions and set

\Theta_{\cal E}(G(F)):=\Theta_{\operatorname{ell}}(G(F))\cap\Theta_{\cal E-\operatorname{st}}(G(F))\subseteq\Theta(G(F)).

Using observation of Section
6.1
(b) above, Proposition
1.9
is a particular case of part (b) of the following assertion:

Proposition 6.2
.

(a) We have a decomposition

\Theta_{\operatorname{ell}}(G(F))=\bigoplus_{\cal E\in\Psi_{\operatorname{ell}}(G)}\Theta_{\cal E}(G(F)).

(b) Let
\cal E
be an elliptic endoscopic triple of
G
, and let
\chi\in\Theta_{\operatorname{ell}}(G(F))
be such that the restriction
\chi^{\operatorname{ell}}=\chi|_{G(F)^{\operatorname{ell}}}\in C^{\infty}_{\operatorname{inv}}(G(F)^{\operatorname{ell}})
is
\cal E
-stable. Then
\chi
is
\cal E
-stable.

Proof.

(a) First we claim that every
\chi\in\Theta_{\operatorname{ell}}(G(F))
can be written as a finite sum
\chi=\sum_{\cal E\in\Psi_{\operatorname{ell}}(G)}\chi_{\cal E}
such that
\chi_{\cal E}\in\Theta_{\operatorname{ell}}(G(F))
is
\cal E
-stable for every
\cal E\in\Psi_{\operatorname{ell}}(G)
. If
\chi\in\Theta(G(F))
is an elliptic tempered character in the sense of Arthur, this follows from a deep theorem of Arthur
[
MW1
, Theorem XI.4]
, from which the general case follows by twisting.

It remains to show that the subspaces
\{\Theta_{\cal E}(G(F))\}_{\cal E\in\Psi_{\operatorname{ell}}(G)}\subseteq\Theta(G(F))
are linearly independent.
Using Section
6.1
(c), the assertion follows from Lemma
6.3
below.

(b) By part (a), invariant generalized function
\chi
can be written as a finite sum

\chi=\sum_{\cal E^{\prime}\in\Psi_{\operatorname{ell}}(G)}\chi_{\cal E^{\prime}}
, where each
\chi_{\cal E^{\prime}}\in\Theta_{\operatorname{ell}}(G(F))
is
\cal E^{\prime}
-stable.
It remains to show that
\chi_{\cal E^{\prime}}=0
for every
\cal E^{\prime}\not\simeq\cal E
.

Restricting to

, we get an identity

\chi^{\operatorname{ell}}=\sum_{\cal E^{\prime}}\chi_{\cal E^{\prime}}^{\operatorname{ell}}\in C^{\infty}_{\operatorname{inv}}(G(F)^{\operatorname{ell}})

and each
\chi_{\cal E^{\prime}}^{\operatorname{ell}}
is
\cal E^{\prime}
-stable. Since

is
\cal E
-stable by assumption, we conclude from Lemma
6.3
below that
\chi_{\cal E^{\prime}}^{\operatorname{ell}}=0
for every
\cal E^{\prime}\not\simeq\cal E
. Then
\chi_{\cal E^{\prime}}=0
by the observation of Section
6.1
(c).
∎

Lemma 6.3
.

The subspaces
\{C^{\infty}_{\cal E-\operatorname{st}}(G(F)^{\operatorname{ell}})\}_{\cal E\in\Psi_{\operatorname{ell}}(G)}\subseteq C^{\infty}_{\operatorname{inv}}(G(F)^{\operatorname{ell}})
are linearly independent.

Proof.

Unwinding definitions (see Sections
1.7
(b),(c)) we have to show that for every
\gamma\in G(F)^{\operatorname{ell}}
, the non-zero subspaces

\{\operatorname{Span}_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}}\operatorname{Fun}([\operatorname{Orb}^{\operatorname{st}}_{\gamma}])_{\xi,(\gamma,\xi)\in\cal E}\}_{\cal E}\subseteq\operatorname{Fun}([\operatorname{Orb}^{\operatorname{st}}_{\gamma}])

are linearly independent. Since subspaces
\operatorname{Fun}([\operatorname{Orb}^{\operatorname{st}}_{\gamma}])_{\xi}
are linearly independent,
this follows from Remark
1.8
(d).
∎

Appendix A
Endoscopic decomposition over
\mathbb{C}

A.1
.

Construction.
(a) Let
M\subseteq G
be a Levi subgroup. Every endoscopic datum
\cal E=(s,\widehat{H},\rho)
for
M
gives rise to an endoscopic datum
\cal E_{G}=(\widetilde{s},\widehat{H},\rho)
for
G
, unique up to equivalence:

Namely, the inclusion
M\hookrightarrow G
gives rise to a
\Gamma
-invariant conjugacy class of inclusions
\widehat{M}\hookrightarrow\widehat{G}
, hence of inclusions
\widehat{M}/Z(\widehat{G})\hookrightarrow\widehat{G}^{\operatorname{ad}}
. Then, for every generic lift
\widetilde{s}\in\widehat{M}/Z(\widehat{G})\subseteq\widehat{G}^{\operatorname{ad}}
of
s\in\widehat{M}^{\operatorname{ad}}=\widehat{M}/Z(\widehat{M})
, we have
Z_{\widehat{G}}(\widetilde{s})^{0}=Z_{\widehat{M}}(s)^{0}=\widehat{H}
, and we define
\cal E_{G}
to be

(\widetilde{s},\widehat{H},\rho)
.

(b) Set
W(M):=N_{G}(M)/M
. Then for every
w\in W(M)
we can form another endoscopic datum
w(\cal E)
for
M
, and,
by construction, endoscopic data
\cal E_{G}
and
w(\cal E)_{G}
from part (a) are equivalent.

A.2
.

Relevant endoscopic data.
(a) We say that an endoscopic datum
\cal E
is
relevant
, if there exists
(\gamma,\xi)\in\cal E
(see Section
1.8
(a)).

(b) Note that every endoscopic datum
\cal E
is relevant, if

is quasi-split. Moreover,
\cal E
is relevant if and only if the space
\widehat{C}_{\cal E-\operatorname{st}}(G(F))
is nonzero. Furthermore, this happens if and only if there exists a Levi subgroup
M\subseteq G
and an elliptic endoscopic datum
\cal E^{\prime}
for

such that
\cal E\simeq\cal E^{\prime}_{G}
. In this case,

is unique up to a conjugacy, and the equivalence class of
\cal E^{\prime}
is unique up to a
W(M)
-conjugacy.

A.3
.

Notation.

(a) We denote by
\Psi(G)
the set of equivalences classes of endoscopic data for

.

(b) For every
\cal E\in\Psi(G)

\cal E
-stable generalized functions.

(c) For every Levi subgroup

\cal E
for

Proposition A.4

(a) For every Levi subgroup

We are ready to formulate and to prove the main result of this section.

A.8

Normalized parabolic induction.
Let

(a) The normalized parabolic induction

Proof of Proposition
A.4
.

We have to show that every

for all

.

When
\chi

Appendix B
The case of an arbitrary field of coefficients

Let
K
be an arbitrary algebraically closed field of characteristic zero. Combining results of the previous section and a recent work
[
KSV
]
, in this section we are going to construct Arthur’s space of virtual elliptic characters and endoscopic decomposition over
K
.

B.1
.

General observations.
(a) We denote by
\operatorname{Irr}_{K}(G(F))
the set of isomorphism classes of smooth irreducible representations
G(F)
over
K
and by
\widehat{C}_{\operatorname{inv}}(G(F),K)
be the space of
K
-valued invariant generalized functions on
G(F)
.

(b) We claim that every
\pi\in\operatorname{Irr}_{K}(G(F))
is admissible, and hence we can consider its character
\chi_{\pi}\in\widehat{C}_{\operatorname{inv}}(G(F),K)
.
Indeed, the argument of
[
KSV
, Theorem 3.4]
, where the corresponding assertion was proven
K=\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
, applies word-by-word.
We denote by
\Theta_{K}(G(F))\subseteq\widehat{C}_{\operatorname{inv}}(G(F),K)
the linear span of
\{\chi_{\pi}\}_{\pi\in\operatorname{Irr}_{K}(G(F))}
.

(c) Arguing as in
[
KSV
, Proposition 3.5]
word-by-word, it follows from part (b) that for every algebraically closed subfield
L\subseteq K
and
\pi\in\operatorname{Irr}_{L}(G(F))
the representation
\pi_{K}:=\pi\otimes_{L}K
is irreducible and that
\pi^{\prime}_{K}\not\simeq\pi^{\prime\prime}_{K}
if
\pi^{\prime}\not\simeq\pi^{\prime\prime}
. Therefore the map
\pi\mapsto\pi_{K}
induces an injective map
\Theta_{L}(G(F))\hookrightarrow\Theta_{K}(G(F))
.

(d) Since the Hecke algebra of
G
is of countable dimension, for every representation
\pi\in\operatorname{Irr}_{K}(G(F))
there exists a countable subfield

L\subseteq K
such that
\pi
is obtained by a base change from certain representation in
\operatorname{Irr}_{L}(G(F))
. In particular, the space

\Theta_{K}(G(F))
can be written as a filtered union
\bigcup_{L\subseteq K}\Theta_{L}(G(F))
, taken over countable algebraically closed subfields of
K
.

B.2
.

Elliptic virtual characters.
For every algebraically closed field
K
of characteristic zero we define
a subspace
\Theta_{\operatorname{ell},K}(G(F))\subseteq\Theta_{K}(G(F))
as follows:

(a) If
K=\mathbb{C}
, we denote
\Theta_{\operatorname{ell},\mathbb{C}}(G(F))\subseteq\Theta_{\mathbb{C}}(G(F))
the subspace

\Theta_{\operatorname{ell}}(G(F))\subseteq\Theta(G(F))
from Section
6.1
(a).

(b) Note that the Galois group
\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Q})
acts on
\Theta_{\mathbb{C}}(G(F))
by the action

\sigma(\sum_{i}a_{i}\chi_{\pi_{i}})=\sum_{i}\sigma(a_{i})\chi_{\sigma(\pi_{i})}
. Moreover, using remark after

Theorem B.4
.

We have an endoscopic decomposition

\Theta_{K}(G(F))=\bigoplus_{\cal E\in\Psi(G)}\Theta_{\cal E,K}(G(F)).

Proof.

Using observation of Section
B.1
(d) and the constructions of Sections
B.2
and
B.3
, we can assume that
K
is a countable subfield of
\mathbb{C}
. Then, by Theorem
A.6
, every
\chi\in\Theta_{K}(G(F))
can be written uniquely as a finite sum

\chi=\sum_{\cal E\in\Psi(G)}\chi_{\cal E}

such that
\chi_{\cal E}\in\Theta_{\cal E,\mathbb{C}}(G(F))
for every
\cal E\in\Psi(G)
, and we need to show that for every
\cal E\in\Psi(G)
we have

\chi_{\cal E}\in\Theta_{\cal E,K}(G(F))
.

For this it suffices to show that each subspace
\Theta_{\cal E,\mathbb{C}}(G(F))\subseteq\Theta_{\mathbb{C}}(G(F))
is
\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Q})
-invariant, and

\Theta_{\cal E,K}(G(F))=\Theta_{\cal E,\mathbb{C}}(G(F))^{\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{C}/K)}
.

Since
\Theta_{\cal E,\bullet}(G(F))=\Theta_{\cal E-\operatorname{st},\bullet}(G(F))\cap\Theta_{M_{\cal E}-\operatorname{ell},\bullet}(G(F))
, it suffices to show similar assertions for
\Theta_{\cal E-\operatorname{st},\bullet}(G(F))
and
\Theta_{M_{\cal E}-\operatorname{ell},\bullet}(G(F))
. Next, by Proposition
A.4
(a), the assertion for
\Theta_{M_{\cal E}-\operatorname{ell},\bullet}(G(F))
follows from that for
\Theta_{\operatorname{ell},\bullet}(M(F))
. Finally, the assertions for

\Theta_{\cal E-\operatorname{st},\bullet}(G(F))
and
\Theta_{\operatorname{ell},\bullet}(G(F))
follows from
Lemma
B.5
(b) below.
∎

Lemma B.5
.

Let
K\subseteq\mathbb{C}
be an algebraically closed subfield.

(a) For every
\pi\in\operatorname{Irr}_{\mathbb{C}}(G(F))
not defined over
K
, the
\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{C}/K)
-orbit of
\pi
is infinite.

(b) We have an equality
\Theta_{K}(G(F))=\Theta_{\mathbb{C}}(G(F))^{\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{C}/K)}
.

Proof.

(a) Our argument is a small modification of
[
KSV
, Theorem 3.10]
, where a particular case is proven.

Assume first that
\pi
is cuspidal. Then, by
[
KSV
, Lemma 3.8]
, there exists an unramified character
\theta:G(F)\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
such that the central character
cc(\pi^{\prime})
of
\pi^{\prime}:=\pi\otimes\theta^{-1}
has values in
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}^{\times}
. Then,
[
KSV
, Proposition 3.7.(2)]
says that
\pi^{\prime}
is defined over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
. Since
\pi\in\operatorname{Irr}_{\mathbb{C}}(G(F))
is not defined over
K
, this implies that
\theta:G(F)\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
has values not in
K^{\times}
. Then the
\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{C}/K)
-orbit of
\theta
is infinite, hence the
\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{C}/K)
-orbit
\pi=\pi^{\prime}\otimes\theta
is infinite.

For a general
\pi
, choose a parabolic subgroup
P\subseteq G
such that the Jacquet functor
r_{P}^{G}(\pi)
is cuspidal and non-zero. Then for every irreducible quotient
\tau
of
r_{P}^{G}(\pi)
,
\pi
is an irreducible subrepresentation of the parabolic induction
i_{P}^{G}(\tau)
. Then, repeating the argument of
[
KSV
, Lemma 3.6]
, we see that
\tau
is not defined over
K
. Then, by the proven above, the
\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{C}/K)
-orbit of
\tau
is infinite.
But since
r_{P}^{G}(\pi)
is of finite length, we conclude that the
\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{C}/K)
-orbit of
\pi
is infinite as well.

(b) Let
\chi=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\chi_{\pi_{i}}\in\Theta_{\mathbb{C}}(G(F))^{\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{C}/K)}
, where
\pi_{1},\ldots,\pi_{n}\in\operatorname{Irr}_{\mathbb{C}}(G(F)
are pairwise non-isomorphic representations and
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\in\mathbb{C}
. We have to show that
\pi_{i}
is defined over
K
and
a_{i}\in K
for all
i
.
For every
\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{C}/K)
, we have an identity

(B.1)

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sigma(a_{i})\chi_{\sigma(\pi_{i})}={\sigma}(\chi)=\chi=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\chi_{\pi_{i}}.

Finally, combining part (a), identity (
B.1
) and linear independence of characters, we conclude that each
\pi_{i}
is defined over
K
and each
a_{i}
belongs to
K
.
∎

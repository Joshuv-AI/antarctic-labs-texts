TROPION-Urothelial03: a phase II/III study of datopotamab deruxtecan (Dato-DXd) for locally advanced or metastatic urothelial carcinoma
Authors: Matthew D Galsky, Alexandra Drakaki, Arnab Basu, Bradley McGregor, Jens Bedke, Yohann Loriot, Eiji Kikuchi, Jun Guo, Benjamin Garmezy, Srikala S Sridhar, Parminder Singh, Vadim S Koshkin, Mark Fleming, Gunnar Klauss, Daisy Lin, Chelsea Liu, Andrea B Apolo, Thomas Powles
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTEnfortumab vedotin (EV) plus pembrolizumab is the first-line standard of care for patients with locally advanced or metastatic urothelial carcinoma (la/mUC). However, randomized data to guide treatment after progression on EV plus pembrolizumab are limited. Datopotamab deruxtecan (Dato-DXd), a trophoblast cell surface antigen 2–directed antibody–drug conjugate, has demonstrated durable efficacy and a manageable safety profile in patients with la/mUC. TROPION-Urothelial03 is a global, multicenter, randomized, open-label, phase II/III trial comparing the efficacy and safety of Dato-DXd plus platinum chemotherapy versus gemcitabine plus platinum chemotherapy in patients with la/mUC and disease progression on or after EV plus pembrolizumab. In Part A (phase II), ~60 patients will be randomized 1:1 to Dato-DXd 4 or 6 mg/kg plus platinum chemotherapy to determine optimal dosing. In Part B (phase III), ~570 patients will be randomized 1:1 to receive Dato-DXd at the recommended phase III dose plus platinum chemotherapy or gemcitabine plus platinum chemotherapy. The primary endpoint of Part A is objective response rate (ORR); the dual primary endpoints of Part B are progression-free survival (PFS) by blinded independent central review (BICR) and overall survival. Introduction

Background and rationale

Urothelial cancer is the ninth most diagnosed cancer worldwide, with ~614,000 new cases and ~220,000 deaths reported in 2022 [1–3]. The majority (90–95%) of urothelial cancer cases are categorized as urothelial carcinoma (UC) of the bladder, with upper tract UC accounting for 5–10% of diagnoses [4]. Approximately 25% of all patients with urothelial cancer will develop locally advanced or metastatic UC (la/mUC) [5,6]. La/mUC is historically associated with a particularly poor prognosis, as shown by a 5-year relative survival rate of ~5–6% [7,8].

In recent years, the introduction of immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) and the advancement of antibody–drug conjugates (ADCs) have revolutionized the treatment landscape for patients with la/mUC [4]. Combination therapy with the ADC enfortumab vedotin (EV) plus pembrolizumab was approved by the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) in December 2023 and the European Medicines Agency (EMA) in September 2024 for treating patients with la/mUC [9,10], and is now the preferred first-line regimen for this patient population, regardless of cisplatin eligibility [5,11]. Compared with gemcitabine plus platinum-based chemotherapy, EV plus pembrolizumab has significantly prolonged median progression-free survival (PFS; 12.5 versus 6.3 months) and overall survival (OS; 31.5 versus 16.1 months) in patients with la/mUC in the first-line setting [11].

Despite these improvements, approximately half of patients experience disease progression within 2 years of initiating first-line treatment with EV plus pembrolizumab [12,13]. Up to one-quarter of patients with advanced UC initially respond to ICIs but many subsequently develop acquired resistance [14]. Although not fully understood, immunologic factors and the tumor microenvironment may contribute to innate or acquired resistance to ICIs [14]. Notably, ADCs have been shown to alter the tumor microenvironment by reducing programmed death-ligand 1 expression on tumor cells and increasing cytotoxic T-cell infiltration [15,16].

Guideline recommendations for second-line treatment after disease progression with combination EV plus pembrolizumab therapy include platinum-based chemotherapy (with or without gemcitabine) or, for patients with fibroblast growth factor receptor (FGFR)–altered tumors, erdafitinib [11,17]. The potential benefit of gemcitabine plus platinum-based chemotherapy as second-line therapy after progression on first-line EV plus pembrolizumab is based on efficacy data from studies investigating this combination as first-line treatment [18,19]. However, to date, evidence to guide the optimal selection of second-line therapies in la/mUC post disease progression on or after EV plus pembrolizumab is limited and obtained from real-world retrospective studies with small sample sizes [20,21]. In this setting, platinum-based chemotherapy has been associated with low rates of complete response (CR; 2.9–10%) and stable disease (SD; 18–30%), and modest median PFS (≤4.4 months), duration of response (DOR; 3.8 months), and OS (≤12.0 months) [20,21]. Therefore, an unmet need remains for more effective second-line treatment options for patients with disease progression on or after first-line EV plus pembrolizumab.

Trophoblast cell surface antigen 2 (TROP2) is a transmembrane glycoprotein highly expressed in various tumors, including UC [22,23]. TROP2 overexpression has been associated with aggressive tumor phenotypes and poor prognosis in several types of cancer [22]. Datopotamab deruxtecan (Dato-DXd) is a TROP2-directed ADC composed of a humanized immunoglobulin G1 monoclonal antibody covalently linked to a highly potent topoisomerase I inhibitor (DXd) via a tetrapeptide-based, plasma-stable, tumor-selective cleavable linker (Figure 1) [24,25]. Together, the plasma-stable linker and the short systemic half-life of the payload are designed to reduce systemic toxicity [25]. The cleavable linker also facilitates a bystander antitumor effect, where the free payload is released from target tumor cells into the tumor microenvironment and kills surrounding cells, regardless of TROP2 expression [25,26]. Dato-DXd was granted FDA and EMA approvals for use in previously treated unresectable or metastatic hormone receptor–positive HER2-negative breast cancer in 2025 [27–29]. Subsequently, Dato-DXd received accelerated FDA approval for use in previously treated locally advanced or metastatic EGFR-mutated non-small cell lung cancer [30].

As part of the first-in-human phase I TROPION-PanTumor01 study, Dato-DXd has shown encouraging monotherapy activity in a 40-patient la/mUC cohort [31]. The confirmed objective response rate (ORR) by blinded independent central review (BICR) was 25.0%, which included one CR (2.5%) and nine partial responses (PRs; 22.5%). A further 20 (50.0%) patients achieved SD, and the median PFS was 6.9 months. The safety profile was manageable and consistent with that observed for Dato-DXd in other solid tumor types [32,33]. The most frequently reported treatment-related adverse events (TRAEs) were stomatitis (53%), nausea (28%), and decreased appetite (28%) [31]. No serious TRAEs or deaths related to study drug were observed.

A separate phase II study, TROPION-PanTumor03, is evaluating the combination of Dato-DXd plus rilvegostomig, a bispecific immunotherapeutic agent targeting programmed cell death protein 1 and T-cell immunoreceptor with Ig and ITIM domains, in patients with la/mUC [34]. Patients either had no prior systemic therapy in the la/mUC setting and were ineligible for cisplatin (first-line cohort; n = 22) or had disease progression after treatment with platinum-based chemotherapy (second-line cohort; n = 18). Recent findings reported a confirmed ORR by investigator of 68.2% in the first-line cohort and 38.9% in the second-line cohort. The 12-month PFS rates were 73.5% and 60.0%, respectively. Serious Grade ≥3 TRAEs (related to either Dato-DXd or rilvegostomig) occurred in 0% and 16.7% of patients, respectively. Grade ≥3 TRAEs included increased amylase (30.3%), neutrophil count decreased or neutropenia (10.1%), stomatitis (5.6%), decreased appetite (5.6%), and anemia (5.6%). Discontinuation rates due to TRAEs were low (9.1% and 16.7% in the first-line and second-line cohorts, respectively); no drug-related deaths were reported.

Although Dato-DXd plus platinum chemotherapy has not been previously investigated in la/mUC, data from patients with advanced or metastatic non-small cell lung cancer in the phase Ib TROPION-Lung04 trial support the evaluation of this combination [35]. In TROPION-Lung04, patients received Dato-DXd plus durvalumab, either with or without carboplatin. Among the 37 patients who received platinum-based therapy, serious TRAEs were reported in 27%, and Grade ≥3 treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs) reported in ≥15% were anemia (24%), neutropenia (24%), and thrombocytopenia (16%).

Here, we describe the design of the phase II/III TROPION-Urothelial03 trial (NCT07129993), a global, randomized study comparing the efficacy and safety of Dato-DXd plus platinum chemotherapy (carboplatin or cisplatin) versus gemcitabine plus platinum chemotherapy in patients with la/mUC and disease progression on or after EV plus pembrolizumab.

Objective

The primary objective of the phase II part of TROPION-Urothelial03 (Part A) is to evaluate the relative efficacy of Dato-DXd (4 and 6 mg/kg) plus platinum chemotherapy as measured by ORR by investigator in patients with la/mUC and disease progression on or after EV plus pembrolizumab. In the phase III part (Part B), the dual primary objectives are to compare the efficacy of Dato-DXd plus platinum chemotherapy versus gemcitabine plus platinum chemotherapy as measured by PFS (by BICR) and OS.

Trial design

TROPION-Urothelial03 is a global, multicenter, randomized, open-label, phase II/III trial comprising two parts (Figure 2). In Part A (phase II), ~60 patients with la/mUC will be randomized 1:1 to receive Dato-DXd 4 or 6 mg/kg plus platinum chemotherapy. Randomization in Part A will be stratified by the assignment of platinum chemotherapy (carboplatin versus cisplatin) as determined by the investigator’s assessment of cisplatin eligibility. The recommended phase III dose will be determined using the totality of efficacy, safety, tolerability, and pharmacokinetics data from Part A, and will not depend on specific efficacy criteria being met.

In Part B (phase III), ~570 patients will be randomized 1:1 to receive either Dato-DXd at the recommended phase III dose plus platinum chemotherapy or gemcitabine plus platinum chemotherapy. Randomization will be stratified by assignment of platinum chemotherapy (carboplatin versus cisplatin) per the investigator’s assessment of cisplatin eligibility, presence of liver metastasis (presence versus absence), and time from first dose of first-line EV plus pembrolizumab to radiographic disease progression (<6 versus ≥6 months). The proportion of patients who progressed on EV plus pembrolizumab within 6 months from the first dose of the regimen will be capped at ~30%. No crossover between the two trial groups is permitted.

Enrollment began in September 2025, with an estimated study completion in January 2030. This study is sponsored by Daiichi Sankyo, Inc. In July 2020, AstraZeneca entered into a global development and commercialization collaboration agreement with Daiichi Sankyo for datopotamab deruxtecan (Dato-DXd).

Methods

Study setting

Patients are being enrolled across ~266 study sites including, but not limited to, North America, South America, Europe, East Asia, and Oceania/Australia.

Key eligibility criteria

Inclusion criteria

Eligible patients are adults (≥18 years of age; if the legal age of consent is >18 years of age, local regulatory requirements will be followed) with a diagnosis of histologically or cytologically confirmed unresectable la/mUC of the bladder, renal pelvis, ureter, or urethra. A tumor tissue sample of sufficient quantity from archival tissue or a newly obtained pretreatment biopsy for exploratory biomarker testing is required. Patients must be considered eligible to receive cisplatin- or carboplatin-containing chemotherapy, per investigator’s judgment (patients eligible for cisplatin will receive cisplatin). Patients ineligible for cisplatin are those who meet any of the following criteria: glomerular filtration rate (GFR) <60 mL/min (other than those with GFR ≥50 mL/min, who may be considered cisplatin eligible based on investigator judgment if they do not meet any other ineligibility criteria); audiometric hearing loss and/or peripheral neuropathy of Grade ≥2 in severity (graded according to the National Cancer Institute’s Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events Version 5.0 [NCI-CTCAE v5.0]); or Class III or IV heart failure per New York Heart Association (NYHA) criteria after screening (patients with Class III or IV heart failure per NYHA at screening are excluded from the study).

Eligible patients must have experienced radiographic disease progression or relapse during or after first-line treatment with EV plus pembrolizumab and have had an adequate treatment washout period prior to randomization. Patients must have an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status of 0 or 1 and measurable disease on computed tomography or magnetic resonance imaging per Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors version 1.1 (RECIST 1.1), as assessed by the investigator. Exclusion criteria

Patients with prior systemic therapy for la/mUC other than the combination of EV and pembrolizumab are excluded (patients may be included if they received gemcitabine, carboplatin, or cisplatin for early-stage UC in the neoadjuvant or adjuvant settings, if the last dose was ≥1 year prior to the first dose of the trial intervention). Additionally, patients with prior treatment with a TROP2-directed ADC, prior brain radiotherapy (≤2 weeks before randomization) or chest radiotherapy (≤4 weeks before randomization), and clinically active brain metastasis or spinal cord compression are ineligible. Interventions

In Part A, patients will receive Dato-DXd 4 mg/kg (Arm 1) or 6 mg/kg (Arm 2) intravenously (IV) every 3 weeks (q3w) plus cisplatin 70 mg/m2 IV q3w or carboplatin area under the curve (AUC) 4.5 or 5.0 q3w (per local guidelines).

In Arm 1 of Part B, patients will receive Dato-DXd IV at the recommended phase III dose (as determined in Part A) q3w plus cisplatin or carboplatin q3w. After completing six cycles of Dato-DXd plus platinum chemotherapy, patients will continue to receive Dato-DXd q3w as maintenance therapy. In Arm 2 of Part B, patients will receive cisplatin or carboplatin q3w with gemcitabine q3w. In both arms, eligible patients will receive cisplatin. Carboplatin AUC 4.5 or 5.0, cisplatin 70 mg/m2 IV, and gemcitabine 1000 mg/m2 IV (Days 1 and 8) will be administered per local prescribing information for a maximum of six cycles. Additional cycles may be allowed per local guidelines or practice only in the control group of Part B (phase III).

Premedication to minimize the risk of infusion-related reactions (IRRs) is required before any dose of Dato-DXd and must include antihistamines and antipyretics (preferably acetaminophen), with or without glucocorticoids. If an IRR occurs at any time during treatment with Dato-DXd, additional preventative strategies may be considered per local institutional guidelines.

Dato-DXd dose reductions are allowed for TRAEs. For patients receiving the 4-mg/kg starting dose, two dose reductions are permitted (to 3 mg/kg, then 2 mg/kg), after which patients must discontinue Dato-DXd if treatment is still not tolerated. For the 6-mg/kg starting dose, the first dose reduction is to 4 mg/kg, with two subsequent reductions permitted (to 3 mg/kg, then 2 mg/kg). Once the dose of Dato-DXd is reduced due to toxicity, no re-escalation is permitted, and all subsequent cycles should be given at the lower dose, unless further reduction is necessary.

Dato-DXd treatment should be delayed for any suspected cases of interstitial lung disease (ILD)/pneumonitis, regardless of grade. Supportive treatment with systemic steroids should be considered for Grade 1 and initiated promptly for Grade ≥2, continuing until improvement for Grade 1 or for ≥14 days for Grade ≥2, followed by a gradual taper over ≥4 weeks. Dato-DXd must be permanently discontinued for any Grade ≥2 ILD/pneumonitis event.

For carboplatin, cisplatin, and gemcitabine, investigators may reduce doses using a stepwise dose modification (no more than two dose reductions are permitted); dose modifications for these agents must be based on the maximum toxicity experienced during a cycle. Dose modification and toxicity management guidance per local labels, guidelines, or standard clinical practice will be followed for hematologic (e.g., myelosuppression) and non-hematologic (e.g., hepatotoxicity) toxicities. Once the dose of carboplatin, cisplatin, or gemcitabine has been reduced due to toxicity, no dose re-escalation is permitted, and all subsequent cycles should be given at the lower dose, unless further reduction is necessary. Patients who received ≥1 cycle of cisplatin and are unable to tolerate subsequent cycles will be allowed to switch to carboplatin at the investigator’s discretion for the remainder of the platinum therapy cycles (up to six cycles in total). Patients will continue to receive treatment until clinical or radiologic evidence of disease progression is determined by the investigator, unacceptable toxicity, withdrawal of informed consent, loss to follow-up, or other reasons.

Outcomes

For Part A (phase II), the primary efficacy endpoint is investigator-assessed ORR (proportion of patients with best overall response [BOR] of confirmed CR or PR; Table 2). For Part B (phase III), the dual primary endpoints are PFS (assessed by BICR) and OS (Table 3). Secondary efficacy endpoints for Part A include DOR, PFS, disease control rate (DCR), and time to response (TTR), each assessed by the investigator per RECIST 1.1, and OS. For Part B, secondary efficacy endpoints include investigator-assessed PFS, and ORR, DCR, DOR, and TTR, each assessed by BICR and by the investigator per RECIST 1.1. Additional secondary endpoints for both Parts A and B include safety (TEAEs, serious adverse events [SAEs], adverse events of special interest [AESIs], and deaths, graded according to the NCI-CTCAE v5.0) and immunogenicity of Dato-DXd (prevalence and incidence of antidrug antibodies). Clinical laboratory tests, including hematology, coagulation, clinical chemistry, and urinalysis, will be performed by the local laboratory using validated or approved tests. Additional secondary endpoints include pharmacokinetics (Part A) and patient-reported outcomes (Part B). Biomarker assessments are planned in the exploratory setting to identify potentially relevant biomarkers, including TROP2 expression, that may be associated with clinical outcomes or resistance to the trial intervention. Circulating tumor DNA samples will be collected at baseline and on treatment. Evaluations will be performed using immunohistochemistry, gene expression analysis, genomic profiling, or novel computational methods.

Statistical analyses

The full analysis set will include all randomized patients. In Part A, ~60 patients will be randomized 1:1 (~30 patients per dose arm). The sample size is determined based on the posterior probability of the difference in true ORR between the two dose arms, and provides ~80% confidence in an observed ORR difference of 10%. The final determination of the recommended phase III dose will take into consideration the totality of information from the efficacy, safety, tolerability, and pharmacokinetics data. For the primary analysis in Part A, the ORR as assessed by the investigator will be summarized with a two-sided exact 95% confidence interval (CI) using the Clopper–Pearson method by trial arm.

Part B plans to enroll ~570 patients, which assumes exponential distributions of PFS and OS. For the primary PFS analysis in Part B, the required number of events, as assessed by BICR, will have ~98% power to detect a hazard ratio (HR) of 0.63 at a two-sided α = 0.01. For the primary OS analysis, the required events will have ~85% power to detect a HR of 0.73 at a two-sided α = 0.04. The overall type I error rate is controlled at two-sided α = 0.05; the α split is selected to optimize sample size, analysis timing, and power. In Part B, PFS and OS will compare the Dato-DXd plus platinum chemotherapy and the gemcitabine plus platinum chemotherapy arms using a log-rank test stratified by randomization stratification factors. A stratified Cox regression model will be used to estimate the HRs and corresponding CIs of PFS and OS in the Dato-DXd plus platinum chemotherapy arm versus the gemcitabine plus platinum chemotherapy arm. Kaplan–Meier plots will be used to graphically present PFS and OS across the trial arms. All secondary efficacy endpoints will be analyzed descriptively.

The safety analysis set will include all patients who received ≥1 dose of study treatment. Adverse events will be coded using the Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Activities and graded using NCI-CTCAE v5.0. The number and percentage of patients with TEAEs, SAEs, and AESIs will be reported. Additionally, summaries will be provided by the worst NCI-CTCAE grade, relationship to the trial intervention, seriousness, and actions taken with trial intervention (e.g., dose reduction or delay). TRAEs are those assessed by the investigator as being related to any component of the trial intervention (i.e., Dato-DXd, gemcitabine, or cisplatin/carboplatin).

Monitoring

An independent data monitoring committee (IDMC) will be utilized in the phase III part to monitor the safety and efficacy of the treatments throughout the conduct of the trial. The IDMC will comprise qualified physicians and scientists who are not investigators in the trial and not otherwise directly associated with the Sponsor. The IDMC will periodically review unblinded safety data in this trial. In the phase III part, the IDMC will determine if potential early termination of the trial is warranted, based on prespecified criteria noted in the data monitoring committee charter.

The Sponsor/Contract Research Organization monitor and regulatory authority inspectors are responsible for contacting and visiting the investigators to inspect the facilities and, upon request, inspect the various trial records (e.g., electronic case report forms, source data, and other pertinent documents). Verification of adherence to the protocol, completeness, accuracy, consistency of the data, and adherence to International Council for Harmonization Good Clinical Practice and local regulations on the conduct of clinical research will be accomplished through a combination of onsite visits by the monitor and remote review of trial data. The frequency of monitoring visits will vary based on the activity at each trial site.

Conclusion

Although first-line treatment with EV plus pembrolizumab has improved patient outcomes in la/mUC [5,11], approximately half of patients experience disease progression on or after treatment [12,13]. Data to guide the optimal selection of second-line treatment are limited, and currently available treatment options offer only modest efficacy [11,17], highlighting an unmet need for novel second-line therapies. TROPION-Urothelial03 is a global, multicenter, randomized, open-label, phase II/III trial designed to investigate the efficacy and safety of Dato-DXd, a TROP2-directed ADC, in combination with platinum chemotherapy versus gemcitabine plus platinum chemotherapy in patients with la/mUC with disease progression on or after EV plus pembrolizumab. Given the promising efficacy signals and tolerability profiles observed in patients with la/mUC in the TROPION-PanTumor01 and TROPION-PanTumor03 studies [31,34], the outcomes of this trial will be informative as to the potential of Dato-DXd as a novel treatment option for patients with la/mUC who progress on or after EV plus pembrolizumab.

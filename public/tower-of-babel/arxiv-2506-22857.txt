Abstract
We show that every H H -minor-free graph that also excludes a ( k × k ) (k\times k) -grid as a minor has treewidth/branchwidth bounded from above by a function f ⁡ ( t , k ) f(t,k) that is linear in k k and polynomial in t ≔ | V ⁡ ( H ) | t\coloneqq|V(H)| . Such a result was proven originally by [ Demaine & Hajiaghayi, Combinatorica, 2008 ], where f f was indeed linear in k k . However the dependency in t t in this result was non-explicit (and huge). Later, [ Kawarabayashi & Kobayashi, JCTB, 2020 ] showed that this bound can be estimated to be f ⁡ ( t , k ) ∈ 2 𝒪 ⁡ ( t ​ log ⁡ t ) ⋅ k f(t,k)\in 2^{\mathcal{O}(t\log t)}\cdot k . Wood recently asked whether f f can be pushed further to be polynomial, while maintaining the linearity on k k . We answer this in a particularly strong sense, by showing that the treewidth/branchwidth of G G is in 𝒪 ⁡ ( g ​ k + t 2304 ) , \mathcal{O}(gk+t^{2304}), where g g is the Euler genus of H H . This directly yields f ⁡ ( t , k ) = 𝒪 ⁡ ( t 2 ​ k + t 2304 ) f(t,k)=\mathcal{O}(t^{2}k+t^{2304}) . Our methods build on techniques for branchwidth and on new bounds and insights for the Graph Minor Structure Theorem (GMST) due to [ Gorsky, Seweryn & Wiederrecht, 2025, arXiv:2504.02532 ]. In particular, we prove a variant of the GMST that ensures some helpful properties for the minor relation. We further employ our methods to provide approximation algorithms for the treewidth/branchwidth of H H -minor-free graphs. In particular, for every ε > 0 \varepsilon>0 and every t t -vertex graph H H with Euler genus g g , we give a ( g + ε ) (g+\varepsilon) -approximation algorithm for the branchwidth of H H -minor-free graphs running in 2 poly ​ ( t ) / ε ⋅ poly ​ ( n ) 2^{\textbf{poly}(t)/\varepsilon}\cdot\textbf{poly}(n) -time. Our algorithms explicitly return either an appropriate branch-decomposition or a grid-minor certifying a negative answer.

Introduction

Treewidth is one of the most extensively studied parameters in structural graph theory.
Together with its closely related parameter branchwidth (introduced in
[
RS91b

Both parameters are defined via tree-like decompositions that capture how closely a graph resembles a tree in its topological structure.
From a structural perspective, these two parameters are known to reflect the same underlying notion of “tree-likeness.”
Indeed, for every non-acyclic graph

Moreover, since computing both parameters is

,

A particularly noteworthy island of tractability for branchwidth is the class of planar graphs.

1.3
Approximation algorithms

There is a long line of research on approximating treewidth and branchwidth. Since the two parameters are linearly related, any approximation algorithm for one immediately implies an approximation algorithm for the other.

An early result by Bodlaender, Gilbert, Hafsteinsson, and Kloks
[
BGHK95
]
established a polynomial-time
\mathcal{O}(\log n)
-approximation algorithm for both parameters. Later, Feige, Hajiaghayi, and Lee
[
FHL08
]
gave a randomized polynomial-time algorithm achieving an improved approximation factor of
\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{\log n})
. On the negative side, computing treewidth within an additive error of
n^{\varepsilon}
for some
\varepsilon>0
is
\mathsf{NP}
-hard
[
BGHK95
]
, and Austrin, Pitassi, and Wu
[
WAPL15
]
showed that no constant-factor approximation exists under the Small Set Expansion Conjecture. More recently, Bonnet proved that approximating treewidth within a factor of
1.00005
is
\mathsf{NP}
-hard
[
Bon25
]
.

For restricted graph classes, better approximation algorithms are known. In particular, both parameters admit constant-factor approximations on asteroid-triple-free graphs
[
BT03
]
. Similarly, for
H
-minor-free graphs, Feige et al.
[
FHL08
]
provided a polynomial-time
\mathcal{O}(t^{2})
-approximation algorithm when
H
is a
t
-vertex graph. This raises the natural question of whether this dependence on

can be improved.

The proof of (
4

6

There exists an algorithm that, given

1.4
Sketch of our proof

For the proof of
Theorem

1.1
, which implies (
4
), we work with branchwidth instead of treewidth, as branchwidth behaves better with embedded graphs.
We make strong use of the structure of
H
-minor-free graphs as revealed in
[
RS03
]
by the Graph Minor Structure Theorem (GMST).
This theorem indicates that
H
-minor-free graphs can be tree-decomposed into a collection of graphs, called
torsos
, that can be “almost embedded” in a surface where
H
does not embed.
By “almost embedded” we mean that a considerable part of each torso
G_{t}
is embedded “up to 3-separations” in the surface while the rest of it is either a small number of “apices” or can be drawn inside a bounded number of faces as “vortices of small width”.
(We postpone the precise definitions to
section

4
.)

For our purposes, we prove a special variant of the GMST that permits both the values of
\mathsf{bw}
or
\mathsf{bg}
on
G
to be computed using the corresponding values of its torsos.
As we already mentioned, each torso
G_{t}
is partitioned into two parts: the part that is embedded “up to 3-separations”, say
G_{t}^{\prime}
, and the rest that corresponds either to the apices or the parts that are drawn inside the vortices.
Since the tools we want to apply later need an actual embedding, we use the quasi-4-connected decomposition of Grohe in
[
Gro16
]
to get rid of the vague part of the embedding that interacts with 3-separations.
The proof of our version of GMST uses as a departure point the recent version of GMST proven in
[
GSW25
]
that ensures that both the number of apices or vortices, as well as the width of the vortices are all bounded by a polynomial function of the size of
H
.
Our proof is algorithmic, running in time
2^{\textbf{poly}(t)}\cdot\textbf{poly}(n)
, based on the running time of the GMST of
[
GSW25
]
.

We next derive a constant-factor approximation algorithm for the branchwidth of each torso
G_{t}
that either outputs a
(k\times k)
-grid minor of
G_{t}
, certifying that
\mathsf{bw}(G_{t})\geq k
or builds a branch-decomposition of width at most
\mathcal{O}(gk+\textbf{poly}(t))
, where
g
is the Euler genus of the surface where
G_{t}^{\prime}
is embedded that, in turn, is bounded by the Euler genus of
H
(
theorem

3.4
).
For this, we work with the graph
G_{t}^{\prime}
by removing from it additional apex vertices obtained by a surface-cutting approach along shortest non-contractible curves, using the algorithms of
[
CCDVL12
]
.
This procedure reduces
G_{t}^{\prime}
to a simpler graph
G_{t}^{\prime\prime}
that is either embedded into a surface different than the sphere in a way that certifies the existence of a
(k\times k)
-grid using the results of
[
DFHT05
]
or to a graph embedded into a sphere.
In the latter case, we apply the Ratcatcher algorithm from
[
ST94
]
and find either a certificate of a
(k\times k)
-grid or a special branch decomposition, called
sphere-cut decomposition
(see
[
RST14
,
ST10
,
DFT08
,
DPBF10
]
).
Using this decomposition we return the apices and vortices that are missing from the spherical embedding of
G_{t}^{\prime\prime}
to
G_{t}
and we do this with an
additive
cost on the width of the obtained branch-decomposition of
G_{t}
, where the part contributed by the apices and vortices can be guaranteed to be comparatively small.
The fact that this is true is a core result of
[
TW25
]
, though their proof is not constructive.
To be able to construct a branch-decomposition explicitly, we thus develop a constructive proof of this result along the way (see
proposition

5.1
).
This leads to the construction of a branch-decomposition of each torso
G_{t}
of width in
\mathcal{O}(t^{2}k+\textbf{poly}(t))
in
2^{\textbf{poly}(t)}\cdot\textbf{poly}(n)
time.

As the version of the GMST that we prove roughly permits the computation of both
\mathsf{bw}(G)
and
\text{$\mathsf{bg}$}(G)
from the corresponding values of its torsos, we derive that the same linear relation between branchwidth and biggest grid number holds also for
G
.
Once more we note that by using randomized algorithms due to Chekuri and Chuzoy
[
CC16
]
, we can push our results to be truly polynomial, at the cost of only receiving our desired results with high probability.
Notably, this change only impacts the proof of our variant of the GMST and leaves the rest of our methods untouched.

Preliminaries

We generally adhere to the notation for graphs defined in
[
Die10
]
.
All graphs we consider are simple, meaning that they contain neither loops nor parallel edges, and contain at least one vertex.
We call a maximal induced subgraph of a graph without cut vertices a

Surfaces.

A
surface

\Sigma
is a compact 2-dimensional manifold with or without boundaries.
The surfaces we consider are generally assumed to be connected.
Given a pair
(\mathsf{h},\mathsf{c})\in\mathbb{N}\times[0,2]

\Sigma^{\mathsf{(\mathsf{h},\mathsf{c})}}
be the surface without boundary created from the sphere by adding
\mathsf{h}

\mathsf{c}
crosscaps.
Dyck’s theorem
[
Dyc88
,
FW99
]
tells us two crosscaps are equivalent to a handle in the presence of a third crosscap and thus our notation is sufficient to capture all surfaces without boundary.

Given a surface
\Sigma
, we add an
open
, respectively
closed hole
to
\Sigma
by removing a closed, respectively open disk from
\Sigma
.
If
\Sigma
is a surface (with holes), we let
\overline{\Sigma}
be the surface resulting from gluing a closed, respectively open disk onto each open, respectively closed hole of
\Sigma
.
We let the
genus
of
\Sigma
be
2\mathsf{h}+\mathsf{c}
, where
\Sigma^{\mathsf{(\mathsf{h},\mathsf{c})}}
is the surface to which
\overline{\Sigma}
is isomorphic.
Given a surface
\Sigma
, we denote by
\mathsf{bd}(\Sigma)
the
boundary
of said surface.

A curve
C
in a surface
\Sigma

3
Approximating the branchwidth of graphs of bounded Euler genus

The core result of this section is that, if we are given some integer

, we can efficiently confirm that either the branchwidth of a graph with a 2-cell-embedding in some given surface of genus

is at most

In particular, at the cost of a worse runtime and slightly worse bounds on our approximation, we can even efficiently provide a branch-decomposition, if the branchwidth is low, or a grid-minor model as a witness that the branchwidth is above some specified bound.

3.3
Constructing a grid

In our algorithms a possible answer is that the branchwidth of the input graph is lower bounded by some (approximate) value.
In this section, we explain how this lower bound can be (approximately) certified by some minor-model of a grid, in polynomial time.
For this purpose, we employ some known results powerful enough to do most of the work for us.

Let

4
Graph Minor Structure Theory

As we get towards the more involved part of our article, we dive into the details of the structure theory introduced by Robertson and Seymour.
Our goal will be to reprove the Graph Minor Structure Theorem (GMST), which first appears in
[

]
, to ensure an additional helpful property.
In particular, we desire more information on how exactly each part of the decomposition it provides us can be “painted” onto a surface.
This part mainly requires us to examine the existing proof of this theorem carefully and add one ingredient from

]
.

4.1
Underlying definitions of graph minor structure theory

Meshes.

Let

•

•

-

There are a host of consequences from this theorem.
We start by noting that

tells us that we can in fact perform the above computations in polynomial time via a randomised algorithm, if we accept that the answer is only returned to us with high probability and with worse bounds on the part of

Corollary 6.2
.

Let

be an integer, let

be a graph with

, and let

\mathbf{b}_{\ref{cor:approxcore}}\colon\mathbb{N}^{3}\to\mathbb{N}
with

and an algorithm that determines whether

with high probability in time

Both version of this theorem of course allow us to approximate the branchwidth of any given graph

that excludes a given other graph

Theorem 6.7
.

There exists a function
f_{\ref{thm_eptas}}\colon\mathbb{N}^{2}\to\mathbb{N}
and an algorithm that, given a graph
H
with
t\coloneqq|V(H)|
, a value
\varepsilon>0
, and an

-minor-free graph

as input, outputs a value

and runs in time
\mathcal{O}(2^{\textbf{poly}(t)}n^{4}m\log n+f_{\ref{thm_eptas}}(\varepsilon,t)n)
, where
f_{\ref{thm_eptas}}(\varepsilon,t)=2^{(t^{2304}\cdot\nicefrac{{1}}{{\varepsilon}})}
.

Proof.

First, we run the first algorithm mentioned in
Corollary

6.3
and obtain a value
b
such that
b\leq\mathsf{bw}(G)\leq\mathbf{b}_{\ref{thm_approxcore}}(t,b,\mathsf{eg}(H)-1)
.
Notably, using
Proposition

5.5
, we can estimate
\mathbf{b}_{\ref{thm_approxcore}}
, such that

\displaystyle\mathbf{b}_{\ref{thm_approxcore}}(t,b,\mathsf{eg}(H))=~

\displaystyle b+(t-3)^{2}(t-4)^{2}\mathsf{width}_{\ref{thm_GMST}}(t)\ +

\displaystyle\max(\mathsf{eg}(H)-1,0)(b+(t-3)(t-4)\mathsf{width}_{\ref{thm_GMST}}(t))+\mathsf{apex}_{\ref{thm_GMST}}(t)+\mathsf{adhesion}_{\ref{thm_GMST}}(t)

\displaystyle\leq~

\displaystyle\mathsf{eg}(H)b+2t^{4}\mathsf{width}_{\ref{thm_GMST}}(t)+\mathsf{apex}_{\ref{thm_GMST}}(t)+\mathsf{adhesion}_{\ref{thm_GMST}}(t).

We let
c\coloneqq 2t^{4}\mathsf{width}_{\ref{thm_GMST}}(t)+\mathsf{apex}_{\ref{thm_GMST}}(t)+\mathsf{adhesion}_{\ref{thm_GMST}}(t)
.

If
\nicefrac{{c}}{{b}}<\varepsilon
, then
c<\varepsilon b
and we thus have
\mathsf{bw}(G)\leq\mathsf{eg}(H)b+c<(\mathsf{eg}(H)+\varepsilon)b
.
This allows us to output
b
.

Otherwise
\nicefrac{{c}}{{b}}\geq\varepsilon
, which means that
\nicefrac{{c}}{{\varepsilon}}\geq b
and thus
\mathsf{bw}(G)\leq\mathsf{eg}(H)b+c\leq(\nicefrac{{\mathsf{eg}(H)c}}{{\varepsilon}}+c=c(\nicefrac{{\mathsf{eg}(H)}}{{\varepsilon}}+1)

Let
c^{\prime}\coloneqq c(\nicefrac{{\mathsf{eg}(H)}}{{\varepsilon}}+1)
.
This allows us to use
Proposition

\mathsf{bw}(G)

In both cases, we return a value

and thus we can confirm that

∎

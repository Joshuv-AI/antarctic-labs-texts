Study of weaving frames in Krein spaces
Avinash Bhardwaj, Animesh Bhandari ∗

Abstract
. Inspired by the work of Bemrose et al. [ 4 ] , we delve into the study of weaving frames in Krein spaces. This paper presents a comprehensive exploration of various properties and characterizations of Krein space weaving frames. In support of our findings, several examples and counter examples are provided, illustrating the applicability of the theoretical results. Additionally, we extend the discussion to an important application in probabilistic erasure, highlighting how weaving frames can be used to mitigate data loss in such scenarios. This work contributes to the broader understanding of frame theory in indefinite inner product spaces.

1.
Introduction

The concept of frames, first introduced by Duffin et al.
[
13
]
, gained widespread attention when Daubechies et al.
[
11
]
underscored its importance in signal processing. This renewed interest led to frame theory becoming a crucial tool in various fields, including Mathematics, Physics, and Engineering. Its versatility has since made frames indispensable in different areas, such as, wavelet analysis, quantum mechanics, and communication systems.

We first discuss the basics of frames to establish the foundational concepts required to our results.

Definition 1.1
.

A sequence
\{f_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
in a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
is called a
frame
for
\mathcal{H}
if there exist constants
A,B>0
so that

(1)

A\|f\|^{2}~\leq~\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}|\langle f,f_{n}\rangle|^{2}~\leq~B\|f\|^{2},

for all
f\in\mathcal{H}
. The numbers
A,B
are called
frame bounds
. For a given frame
\{f_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
for
\mathcal{H}
, the
pre-frame operator
or
synthesis operator
is a bounded linear operator
T:\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N})\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
and is defined as
T\{c_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}=\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}c_{n}f_{n}
. The adjoint of
T
,
T^{*}:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N})
is given by
T^{*}f=\{\langle f,f_{n}\rangle\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
, is called the
analysis operator
. The
frame operator
,
S=TT^{*}:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
, is defined as

(2)

Sf=TT^{*}f=\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\langle f,f_{n}\rangle f_{n}~~\emph{for~all~}f\in{\mathcal{H}}.

We notice that depending upon the nature of frame bounds we get special kinds of frames, e.g. in the inequality (
1
) if
A=B
then the given frame is called a tight frame, in addition to the same if
A=B=1
it is called a Parseval frame. It is well-known that the frame operator is bounded, positive, self adjoint and invertible.

Using the invertibility property, every element in the Hilbert space can be reconstructed as,

(3)

f=\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\langle f,S^{-1}f_{n}\rangle f_{n}=\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\langle f,f_{n}\rangle S^{-1}f_{n}.

Furthermore,
T^{*}T\{c_{n}\}=\sum\limits_{m=1}^{\infty}\langle f_{n},f_{m}\rangle c_{n}
for every
\{c_{n}\}\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N})
and its matrix representation is
(T^{*}T)_{m,n}=\langle\delta_{m},T^{*}T\delta_{n}\rangle_{l^{2}}
. Thus we have,

\displaystyle(T^{*}T)_{m,n}=\langle\delta_{m},T^{*}T\delta_{n}\rangle_{l^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle T\delta_{m},T\delta_{n}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle f_{m},f_{n}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle G_{m,n}

which is known as Gramian matrix. It is to be noted that the Gramian of a frame is invertible if and only if the given frame is a Riesz basis.

If
(\mathcal{K},[.,.])
is a Krein space with fundamental decomposition
\mathcal{K}_{\delta^{+}}\oplus\mathcal{K}_{\delta^{-}}
and fundamental symmetry
J=P_{\delta^{+}}-P_{\delta^{-}}
, where

\mathcal{K}_{\delta^{\pm}}

In this context the associated
J
-inner product is given by,

[k,k^{\prime}]_{J}=[k,Jk^{\prime}],\quad k,k^{\prime}\in\mathcal{K},

and hence (
\mathcal{K},[.,.]_{J}
) is a Hilbert space. The inner product
[.,.],
which is positive definite, induces a topology on Krein space
\mathcal{K}
through

\|k\|_{J}^{2}=[k,k]_{J}=[k,Jk],\quad\forall k\in\mathcal{K}.

Here (
\mathcal{K}_{\delta^{+}},+[.,.]

\mathcal{K}_{\delta^{-}},-[.,.]

A
J
-orthonormalized system is a family of vectors
\{e_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
satisfying condition
[e_{m},e_{n}]=\delta_{mn}
for every
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
. A
J
-orthonormal basis is a
J
-orthonormalized system that also serves as a Schauder basis, providing unique and stable expansions for vectors. If
\{e_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is a

\mathcal{K}

can be represented as

, where
\lambda_{n}=[e_{n},e_{n}]=\pm 1

A sequence
\{z_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
in a Krein space
(\mathcal{K},[.,.])

there exist positive constants

we have,

where

(\mathcal{K},\langle.,.\rangle)

\{z_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}

(\mathcal{K},[.,.])
if and only if it is a frame for
(\mathcal{K},\langle.,.\rangle)
(see

2.
Main Results

In this section, we explore various properties and characterizations of weaving frames within the structure of Krein spaces. We begin by introducing the concept of Krein space weaving frames, which extends the classical frame theory into indefinite inner product spaces. Our discussion focuses on the interplay between the positive and negative components of Krein spaces.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\{z_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
and
\{z^{\prime}_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
be two frames for the Krein space
(\mathcal{K},[.,.])
, then they are said to be weaving frames for
\mathcal{K}
if for for every
\sigma\subset\mathbb{N}
,
\{z_{n}\}_{n\in\sigma}\cup\{z^{\prime}_{n}\}_{n\in\sigma^{c}}
is a frame for
\mathcal{K}
. Thus for every
k\in\mathcal{K}
and every
\sigma\subset\mathbb{N}
, there exist positive constants
\alpha\leq\beta
we have,

(5)

\alpha\|k\|_{J}^{2}\leq\sum\limits_{n\in\sigma}|[k,z_{n}]|^{2}+\sum\limits_{n\in\sigma^{c}}|[k,z^{\prime}_{n}]|^{2}\leq\beta\|k\|_{J}^{2}.

We now define the corresponding frame operator for the associated weaving frames, establishing its roles in the framework.

The weaving frame operator
S:\mathcal{K}\rightarrow\mathcal{K}
is defined as,

S_{\sigma}(k)=TJT^{*}=\sum\limits_{n\in\sigma}[k,z_{n}]z_{n}+\sum\limits_{n\in\sigma^{c}}[k,z^{\prime}_{n}]z^{\prime}_{n},

where
T,T^{*}
and
J
are associated synthesis, analysis operators and fundamental symmetry on
(\mathcal{K},[.,.])
.

Example 2.2
.

let us assume a Krein space
(\mathcal{K}=\mathbb{C}^{2},[.,.])
with the associated indefinite inner product defined as
[(k_{1},k_{2}),(l_{1},l_{2})]=k_{1}\overline{l}_{1}-k_{2}\overline{l}_{2}
, where
\ (k_{1},k_{2})\in{\mathcal{K}}
,
\ (l_{1},l_{2})\in{\mathcal{K}}
. Consider two frames

\{z^{\prime}_{n}\}_{n=1}^{3}\ =\{(1,1),(0,1),(1,0)\}

and

\{z^{\prime\prime}_{n}\}_{n=1}^{3}\ =\{(-1,-1),(0,-1),(1,0)\}.

Then it is easy to verify that for every
\sigma\subset\{1,2,3\}
, the equation (
5
) is satisfied. Therefore
\{z^{\prime}_{n}\}_{n=1}^{3}
and
\{z^{\prime\prime}_{n}\}_{n=1}^{3}
are weaving frames for
(\mathcal{K}=\mathbb{C}^{2},[.,.])
.

On the other hand , if we consider a Krein space
(\mathcal{K}=\mathbb{C}^{3},[.,.])
. with the associated indefinite inner product
[.,.]
defined as

[(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3}),(l_{1},l_{2},l_{3})]=k_{1}\overline{l}_{1}+k_{2}\overline{l}_{2}-k_{3}\overline{l}_{3},

where
\ (k_{1},k_{2},k_{3})\in{\mathcal{K}}
,
\ (l_{1},l_{2},l_{3})\in{\mathcal{K}}

\{z^{\prime\prime}_{n}\}_{n=1}^{3}\ =\{(0,-\sqrt{2},0),(-2,0,0),(0,0,2)\}.

It is easy to verify that these frames are not weaving frames if we consider

\|k\|_{J}^{2}=\|(k_{1},0,0)\|_{J}^{2}=|k_{1}|^{2}

Our first result provides a characterization of weaving frames in Krein spaces through the use of the associated fundamental symmetry.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
\{z_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
and
\{z^{\prime}_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
be two frames for the Krein space
(\mathcal{K},[.,.])
, then the following are equivalent:

(1)

\{z_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
and
\{z^{\prime}_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
are weaving frames for
\mathcal{K}
.

(2)

\{Jz_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
and
\{Jz^{\prime}_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
are weaving frames for
\mathcal{K}
.

(3)

\{z_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
and
\{z^{\prime}_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
are weaving frames for
(\mathcal{K},[.,.]_{J})
.

(4)

\{Jz_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
and
\{Jz^{\prime}_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
are weaving frames for
(\mathcal{K},[.,.]_{J})
.

The validity of the above theorem is demonstrated through the following example.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
\{z_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
and
\{z^{\prime}_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}

(\mathcal{K},[.,.])

\mathcal{K}

(2)

For every
\sigma\subset\mathbb{N}

is the corresponding weaving frame operator, then for every

\|S_{\sigma}(k)\|_{J}\geq\alpha\|k\|_{J}.

\implies
2
)

Let
\{z_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
and
\{z^{\prime}_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
be weaving frames for
(\mathcal{K},[.,.])
with the universal bounds
\alpha,\beta
. Then for every
k\in\mathcal{K}
and every
\sigma\subset\mathbb{N}
we have,

\alpha\|k\|_{J}^{2}\leq\sum\limits_{n\in\sigma}|[k,z_{n}]|^{2}+\sum\limits_{n\in\sigma^{c}}|[k,z^{\prime}_{n}]|^{2}\leq\beta\|k\|_{J}^{2}.

and hence applying theorem

8

3.

Our discussion begins by introducing the fundamental concepts of the tensor product of elements in Krein spaces, which plays a crucial role in understanding the interaction of frame elements. This foundational discussion sets the stage for analyzing how weaving frames can be applied to mitigate the effects of erasure in probabilistic settings.

Let
\{z_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}

with the corresponding frame operators

\sigma\subset\mathbb{N}
, the associated error operator is given by,

E=\sum\limits_{n\in\sigma}\delta_{n}z_{n}\otimes S_{1}^{-1}z_{n}+\sum\limits_{n\in\sigma^{c}}\delta_{n}z^{\prime}_{n}\otimes S_{2}^{-1}z^{\prime}_{n},

where
\delta_{n}
is the standard dirac delta function.

This error operator enables rapid representation while significantly minimizing computational cost. Its effectiveness is largely dependent on the selection of Krein space frames utilized in the encoding and decoding process. By optimizing these frame choices, the overall efficiency of the technique can be further enhanced.

In the following result, we demonstrate how weaving frames can effectively mitigate data loss in erasure scenarios.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\{z_{n}\}_{n=1}^{m}
and
\{z^{\prime}_{n}\}_{n=1}^{m}
be uniform tight weaving frames for the Krein space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\|z_{n}\|_{J}=\sqrt{n}=\|z^{\prime}_{n}\|_{J}
. Suppose
\epsilon>0
is very small number for which
\epsilon^{2}\geq\frac{n}{m}\text{log}(n)
. Then for every
k\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, there is an absolute constant
M>0
for which we have,

E\|\hat{k}-k\|_{J}\leq M\epsilon\|k\|_{J}.

Proof.

For every
k\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and every
\sigma\subset\{1,2,\cdots,m\}
we have,

\tilde{E}k=\hat{k}-k=\frac{1}{m}\left[\sum\limits_{n\in\sigma}\epsilon_{n}[k,z_{n}]z_{n}+\sum\limits_{n\in\sigma^{c}}\epsilon_{n}[k,z^{\prime}_{n}]z^{\prime}_{n}\right],

where

\displaystyle\hat{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{m}\left[\sum\limits_{n\in\sigma}[k,z_{n}]z_{n}+\sum\limits_{n\in\sigma^{c}}[k,z^{\prime}_{n}]z^{\prime}_{n}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{m}\left[\sum\limits_{n\in\sigma}\tilde{\delta_{n}}^{p_{n}}[k,z_{n}]z_{n}+\sum\limits_{n\in\sigma^{c}}\tilde{\delta_{n}}^{p_{n}}[k,z^{\prime}_{n}]z^{\prime}_{n}\right],

with

\tilde{E}=\sum\limits_{n\in\sigma}\tilde{\delta_{n}}^{p_{n}}z_{n}\otimes z_{n}+\sum\limits_{n\in\sigma^{c}}\tilde{\delta_{n}}^{p_{n}}z^{\prime}_{n}\otimes z^{\prime}_{n}~\mbox{ and }~\epsilon_{n}=2\tilde{\delta_{n}}^{p_{n}}-1

is an independent Bernoulli variable that takes values
1
and
-1
, each with probability of
\frac{1}{2}
(see
[
25
]
).

Thus it is sufficient to prove that, for every
\sigma\subset\{1,2,\cdots,m\}
,

E\left\|\frac{1}{m}\left[\sum\limits_{n\in\sigma}\epsilon_{n}z_{n}\otimes z_{n}+\sum\limits_{n\in\sigma^{c}}\epsilon_{n}z^{\prime}_{n}\otimes z^{\prime}_{n}\right]\right\|_{J}\leq M\epsilon.

Applying [Lemma,
[
27
]
] for every
\sigma\subset\{1,2,\cdots,m\}
, there is a positive constant
M
we have

(17)

\displaystyle E\left\|\left[\sum\limits_{n\in\sigma}\epsilon_{n}z_{n}\otimes z_{n}+\sum\limits_{n\in\sigma^{c}}\epsilon_{n}z^{\prime}_{n}\otimes z^{\prime}_{n}\right]\right\|_{J}

\displaystyle\leq M\sqrt{n\text{log}(n)}

\displaystyle\hskip 8.5359pt\left\|\left[\sum\limits_{n\in\sigma}z_{n}\otimes z_{n}+\sum\limits_{n\in\sigma^{c}}z^{\prime}_{n}\otimes z^{\prime}_{n}\right]\right\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{J}.

Furthermore, since
\{z_{n}\}_{n=1}^{m}
and
\{z^{\prime}_{n}\}_{n=1}^{m}
are uniform tight weaving frames for
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\|z_{n}\|_{J}=\sqrt{n}=\|z^{\prime}_{n}\|_{J}
, then for every
\sigma\subset\{1,2,\cdots,m\}
we have,

\mathcal{I}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}=\frac{1}{m}\left[\sum\limits_{n\in\sigma}z_{n}\otimes z_{n}+\sum\limits_{n\in\sigma^{c}}z^{\prime}_{n}\otimes z^{\prime}_{n}\right],

where
\mathcal{I}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
is the identity operator on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Hence we obtain,

(18)

\left\|\left[\sum\limits_{n\in\sigma}z_{n}\otimes z_{n}+\sum\limits_{n\in\sigma^{c}}z^{\prime}_{n}\otimes z^{\prime}_{n}\right]\right\|_{J}=m.

Thus using the equations (
17
) and (
18
) we obtain,

E\left\|\frac{1}{m}\left[\sum\limits_{n\in\sigma}\epsilon_{n}z_{n}\otimes z_{n}+\sum\limits_{n\in\sigma^{c}}\epsilon_{n}z^{\prime}_{n}\otimes z^{\prime}_{n}\right]\right\|_{J}\leq M\sqrt{\frac{n}{m}}~\sqrt{\text{log}(n)}.

Therefore, we achieved our result due to the fact
\epsilon^{2}\geq\frac{n}{m}\text{log}(n)
.

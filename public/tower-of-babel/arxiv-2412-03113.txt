Numerical criteria on the complex Hessian quotient equations with the Calabi symmetry
Rei Murakami

Abstract
. Assuming Calabi symmetry, we prove that a numerical condition ensures the solvability of the complex Hessian quotient equation, as conjectured by Székelyhidi. We also propose a conjecture on the existence of a k k -subharmonic representative in a given cohomology class and confirm it under the assumption of Calabi symmetry or when the class is semiample.



1.
Introduction

This paper studies a variant of partial positivity of cohomology classes, originating from the theory of complex Hessian quotient equations.
Let
(X,\omega)
be an
n
-dimensional compact Kähler manifold and
\alpha
a closed real
(1,1)
-form on
X
.
For two integers
k
and
\ell
satisfying
n\geq k>\ell\geq 0
, we consider the complex Hessian quotient equation which is defined by the following PDE for an unknown function
\varphi
:

(1.1)

\alpha_{\varphi}^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}=e^{H}\,\alpha_{\varphi}^{\ell}\wedge\omega^{n-\ell}.

Here
H
is a smooth function satisfying the condition
\int_{X}\alpha^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}=\int_{X}e^{H}\alpha^{\ell}\wedge\omega^{n-\ell}
and
\alpha_{\varphi}
denotes the form
\alpha+\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi
. The study of complex Hessian equations originated in the paper
[
CNS85
]
, which addressed Dirichlet problems on real domains. In complex geometry, several examples of (
1.1
) have appeared, playing important roles in various studies, such as the complex Monge-Ampère equation (
k=n
and
\ell=0
) and the
J
-equation (
k=n
and
\ell=n-1
).

In
[
Szé18
]
, Székelyhidi estimated the
C^{2}
-norm of solutions of (
1.1
) by introducing the notion of
\mathcal{C}
-subsolutions, and then applied the continuity method to prove the following result:

Theorem 1.2

(
[
Szé18
, Propositions 21 and 22]
)
.

Let
(X,\omega)
be an
n
-dimensional compact Kähler manifold and
\alpha
a closed real
(1,1)
-form on
X
. Assume that there exists a strictly
\alpha
-
(\omega,k)
-subharmonic function
(
see the definition below
)
.

(1)

Suppose that
\ell=0
. Then there exists a strictly
\alpha
-
(\omega,k)
-subharmonic solution
\varphi
of (
1.1
).

(2)

Suppose that
\ell\geq 1
and that
e^{H}
is a constant
c
. Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(a)

There exists a strictly
\alpha
-
(\omega,k)
-subharmonic function
\varphi
such that

k\alpha_{\varphi}^{k-1}\wedge\omega^{n-k}-c\ell\alpha_{\varphi}^{\ell-1}\wedge\omega^{n-\ell}>0

as an
(n-1,n-1)
-form.

(b)

There exists a strictly
\alpha
-
(\omega,k)
-subharmonic solution
\varphi
of (
1.1
).

Definition 1.3

(
\alpha
-
(\omega,k)
-subharmonicity)
.

Let
(X,\omega)
be an
n
-dimensional compact Kähler manifold and
\alpha
a closed real
(1,1)
-form on

. A smooth function

is said to be

\alpha_{\varphi}^{i}\wedge\omega^{n-i}>0

When

and

corresponds to Yau’s theorem
[

]
.
The case

(known as the complex

]
and

]

It is natural to ask when there exists a strictly

-subharmonic function and when condition (

) in Theorem

Motivated by a conjecture on the

(

To state a conjecture regarding the existence of a strictly

-subharmonic function, we introduce the following notations:

Definition 1.4
.

Let
(X,\omega)
be an
n
-dimensional compact Kähler manifold and
k
an integer with

. The sets
\mathcal{K}_{k,\omega}
and

are defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{k,\omega}

\displaystyle=\left\{[\alpha]\in{H}^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})\ \middle|\text{ there exists a strictly $\alpha$-$(\omega,k)$-subharmonic function}\right\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{k,\omega}

\displaystyle=\left\{[\beta]\in H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})\ \middle|\begin{array}[]{l}\int_{V}\beta^{k-n+p}\wedge\omega^{n-k}>0\text{ for any $p$-dimensional}\\
\text{subvariety $V$ of $X$ with $p\geq n-k+1$}\end{array}\right\}.

Conjecture 1.5
.

Let
(X,\omega)
be an
n
-dimensional compact Kähler manifold and
\alpha
a closed real
(1,1)
-form.

(1)

[\alpha]
is in

and
\mathcal{K}_{k,\omega}

(2)

Suppose that

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, for the reader’s convenience, following
[
Spr05
, Lecture 2]
, we briefly summarize the properties of the
k
-th elementary symmetric function which we will use later.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
k
be a non-negative integer.

(1)

The
k
-th elementary symmetric function
\sigma_{k}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
is defined by

\sigma_{k}(\lambda)=\sum_{i_{1}<i_{2}<\dots<i_{k}}{\lambda_{i_{1}}}{\lambda_{i_{2}}}\dots{\lambda_{i_{k}}}.

We define
\sigma_{0}(\lambda)=1
for any
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

(2)

The admissible cone
\Gamma_{k}
of the
k
-th elementary symmetric function
\sigma_{k}
is defined by

\Gamma_{k}:=\left\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid\sigma_{i}(\lambda)>0\text{ for any $i$ such that $0\leq i\leq k$}\right\}.

Note that when
k=n
, the admissible cone
\Gamma_{n}
is nothing but the positive cone

\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid\lambda_{i}>0\text{ for any $i$}\}.

The closure of the positive cone is

\bar{\Gamma}_{n}:=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid\lambda_{i}\geq 0\text{ for any $i$}\}.

A basic property of
\sigma_{k}
is the monotonicity in
\Gamma_{k}
, which follows from the fact that
\sigma_{k}
is a hyperbolic polynomial (see
[
Spr05
, p9–10]
).

Proposition 2.2

(
[
Spr05
, Corollary 2.4 and Theorem 2.16]
)
.

(1)

The
k
-th elementary symmetric function is strictly increasing in
\Gamma_{k}
, namely, for any
\lambda\in\Gamma_{k}
and
\lambda^{\prime}\in\lambda+\bar{\Gamma}_{n}\setminus\{0\}
, we have
\sigma_{k}(\lambda^{\prime})>\sigma_{k}(\lambda)
.

(2)

Let
0\leq\ell<k\leq n
. The function

\left(\frac{\sigma_{k}\ \Big/{n\choose k}}{\sigma_{\ell}\ \Big/{n\choose\ell}}\right)^{\frac{1}{k-\ell}}

is strictly increasing in
\Gamma_{k}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Proposition 2.3

(The Newton inequality,
[
Spr05
, Proposition 2.7]
)
.

Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
r
an integer with
1\leq r\leq n
. Then, we have

\frac{\sigma_{r-1}(\lambda)}{{n\choose{r-1}}}\cdot\frac{\sigma_{r+1}(\lambda)}{{n\choose{r+1}}}\leq\left(\frac{\sigma_{r}(\lambda)}{{n\choose r}}\right)^{2}.

Furthermore, the equality holds if and only if all the components of
\lambda
are equal. In particular, if
\sigma_{r}(\lambda)\neq 0
, then we have

\sigma_{r-1}(\lambda)\sigma_{r+1}(\lambda)<\sigma^{2}_{r}(\lambda).

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.8

3.1.
Set-up

In this section, as calculated by
[
FL13
]
, we express complex Hessian equations

(3.1)

\alpha^{k}\wedge\omega^{\dim X-k}=c\alpha^{l}\wedge\omega^{\dim X-\ell},

as ordinary differential equations via the Calabi ansatz technique. We use the same notations as in Setup
1.7
. Let
v
be a smooth function on
\mathbb{R}
and consider a closed real form
\alpha:=p\pi^{*}\omega_{M}+\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}v(\rho)
on
X\setminus(P_{0}\cup D_{\infty})
, where
p\in\mathbb{R}
.
By
[
Cal82
]
(see also
[
DMS23
]
), the form
\alpha
can be extended to a closed real form on
X
if

•

V_{0}(r):=v(\log r)
can be extended smoothly across
r=0
,

•

V_{\infty}(r):=v(-\log r)+q\log r
can be extended smoothly across
r=0
, where
q\in\mathbb{R}
.

Moreover, if these conditions are met, the cohomology class of the resulting form, denoted also by
\alpha
, is
p[\pi^{*}\omega_{M}]+q\eta
. Also, for the function
u
in Setup
1.7
, the following additional conditions are met since the resulting form
\omega
is Kähler:

•

u^{\prime}>0
and
u^{\prime\prime}>0
,

•

U_{0}(r):=u(\log r)
satisfies
U_{0}^{\prime}(0)>0
,

•

b>0
.

In particular, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{\rho\to-\infty}v^{\prime}(\rho)=0,\quad\lim_{\rho\to\infty}v^{\prime}(\rho)=q,

\displaystyle\lim_{\rho\to-\infty}u^{\prime}(\rho)=0,\quad\lim_{\rho\to\infty}u^{\prime}(\rho)=b.

The eigenvalues of
\alpha
with respect to
\omega
are

\underbrace{\frac{v^{\prime}}{u^{\prime}}}_{m\text{-times}},\,\underbrace{\frac{p+v^{\prime}}{1+u^{\prime}}}_{n\text{-times}},\,\frac{v^{\prime\prime}}{u^{\prime\prime}}.

Since
u^{\prime\prime}>0
, we take
x=u^{\prime}
as a parameter. Define a function
y\in C^{\infty}([0,b])
by
y(x)=v^{\prime}
. Then, the eigenvalues can be written as

\underbrace{\frac{y}{x}}_{m\text{-times}},\,\underbrace{\frac{p+y}{1+x}}_{n\text{-times}},\,y^{\prime}.

In summary, the complex Hessian equation (
3.1
) of the form
\alpha\in p[\pi^{*}\omega_{M}]+q\eta
is formulated as

(3.2)

\begin{cases}\sigma_{k}\Bigl(\underbrace{\frac{y}{x}}_{m\text{-times}},\underbrace{\frac{p+y}{1+x}}_{n\text{-times}},y^{\prime}\Bigr)\Big/{m+n+1\choose k}=c\sigma_{\ell}\Bigl(\underbrace{\frac{y}{x}}_{m\text{-times}},\underbrace{\frac{p+y}{1+x}}_{n\text{-times}},y^{\prime}\Bigr)\Big/{m+n+1\choose\ell},\\
y(0)=0,\quad y(b)=q,\end{cases}

Note that the equation in (
3.2
) is a non-linear ODE of first order. Fang-Lai
[
FL13
]
observed that

\frac{d}{dx}G_{p}^{m,n,k}(x,y(x))=x^{m}(1+x)^{n}\sigma_{k}\Bigl(\underbrace{\frac{y}{x}}_{m\text{-times}},\underbrace{\frac{p+y}{1+x}}_{n\text{-times}},y^{\prime}\Bigr),

where
G_{p}^{m,n,k}
is defined by

G_{p}^{m,n,k}(x,y):=\frac{1}{k!}\frac{d^{k}}{dt^{k}}\Big|_{t=0}\int_{0}^{x+ty}s^{m}(1+tp+s)^{n}ds.

Note that
G_{p}^{m,n,k}
is a polynomial of
x
and
y
.
Thus, the Dirichlet problem (
3.2
) can be interpreted to

(3.3)

\begin{cases}G_{p}^{m,n,k}(x,y(x))=\mu G_{p}^{m,n,\ell}(x,y(x)),\\
y(0)=0,\quad y(b)=q,\end{cases}

where
\mu
is the constant defined by
\mu:=c{m+n+1\choose k}/{m+n+1\choose\ell}
. Indeed, if we have (
3.3
), we get (
3.2
) by differentiating (
3.3
). Conversely, if we have (
3.2
), we get the first equation of (
3.3
) by integrating the first equation of (
3.2
) from 0 to
x
and using
y(0)=0
. Note that the other boundary condition
y(b)=q
does not violate the first equation of (
3.3
) by the following:

Proposition 3.4
.

G_{p}^{m,n,k}(b,q)=\mu G_{p}^{m,n,\ell}(b,q)
.

Proof.

We use the computations in
[
DMS23
, Appendix A]
. For a class
\xi[\pi^{*}\omega_{M}]+\zeta\eta
, where
\xi,\,\zeta\in\mathbb{R}
, by
[
DMS23
, Lemma A.3]
, we have

(\xi[\pi^{*}\omega_{M}]+\zeta\eta)^{m+n+1}=d(m+n+1){m+n+1\choose n}\sum_{i=0}^{n}{n\choose i}\frac{\xi^{n-i}\zeta^{m+1+i}}{m+i+1},

where
d:=c_{1}(-L)^{n}
. On the other hand,

\displaystyle G_{\xi}^{m,n,m+n+1}(b,\zeta)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(m+n+1)!}\frac{d^{m+n+1}}{dt^{m+n+1}}\Big|_{t=0}\int_{0}^{b+t\zeta}s^{m}(1+t\xi+s)^{n}ds

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(m+n+1)!}\frac{d^{m+n+1}}{dt^{m+n+1}}\Big|_{t=0}\int_{0}^{b+t\zeta}\sum_{i=0}^{n}{n\choose i}s^{m}(1+s)^{i}(t\xi)^{n-i}ds

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{n}{n\choose i}\frac{\zeta^{m+i+1}\xi^{n-i}}{m+i+1}

Therefore, for
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

G_{1+\varepsilon p}^{m,n,m+n+1}(b,b+\varepsilon q)=C([\omega]+\varepsilon[\alpha])^{m+n+1}

for some constant
C
. For the right-hand side, we have

\frac{1}{k!}\frac{d}{d\varepsilon^{k}}\Big|_{\varepsilon=0}([\omega]+\varepsilon[\alpha])^{m+n+1}={m+n+1\choose k}[\alpha]^{k}[\omega]^{m+n+1-k}.

For the left-hand side, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}\frac{d}{d\varepsilon^{k}}\Big|_{\varepsilon=0}G_{1+\varepsilon p}^{m,n,m+n+1}(b,b+\varepsilon q)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}\frac{d}{d\varepsilon^{k}}\Big|_{\varepsilon=0}\frac{1}{(m+n+1)!}\frac{d^{m+n+1}}{dt^{m+n+1}}\Big|_{t=0}\int_{0}^{b+t(b+\varepsilon q)}s^{m}(1+t(1+\varepsilon p)+s)^{n}ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}\frac{d}{d\varepsilon^{k}}\Big|_{\varepsilon=0}\sum_{i=0}^{n}{n\choose i}(1+\varepsilon p)^{n-i}\frac{(b+\varepsilon q)^{m+i+1}}{m+i+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}\frac{d}{dt^{k}}\Big|_{t=0}\sum_{i=0}^{n}{n\choose i}(1+tp)^{n-i}\frac{(b+tq)^{m+i+1}}{m+i+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}\frac{d}{dt^{k}}\Big|_{t=0}\int_{0}^{b+tq}s^{m}(1+tp+s)^{n}ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle G_{p}^{m,n,k}(b,q).

Therefore, we have

(3.5)

G_{p}^{m,n,k}(b,q)=C{{m+n+1}\choose k}[\alpha]^{k}[\omega]^{m+n+1-k}.

Since we defined
\mu
as

\mu=\frac{{{m+n+1}\choose k}[\alpha]^{k}[\omega]^{{m+n+1-k}}}{{{m+n+1}\choose\ell}[\alpha]^{\ell}[\omega]^{m+n+1-\ell}},

we get the assertion.
∎

In summary, solving the complex Hessian equation (
3.1
) in the class of
\alpha
-
(\omega,k)
-subharmonic functions is interpreted to solving the equation (
3.3
) with the condition

(3.6)

\Bigl(\underbrace{\frac{y}{x}}_{m\text{-times}},\,\underbrace{\frac{p+y}{1+x}}_{n\text{-times}},\,y^{\prime}\Bigr)\in\Gamma_{k}.

Hereafter,
for
\xi\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
and
\zeta\in\mathbb{R}
, we define

\check{\lambda}_{m,n}(\xi,\zeta):=\Bigl(\underbrace{\frac{\zeta}{\xi}}_{m\text{-times}},\underbrace{\frac{p+\zeta}{1+\xi}}_{n\text{-times}}\Bigr).

For a function

such that

, we also define

\lambda_{m,n}(x,y):=\Bigl(\underbrace{\frac{y}{x}}_{m\text{-times}},\underbrace{\frac{p+y}{1+x}}_{n\text{-times}},y^{\prime}\Bigr),\quad\lambda_{m,n}(0,y(0)):=\Bigl(\underbrace{y^{\prime}(0)}_{(m+1)\text{-times}},\underbrace{p}_{n\text{-times}}\Bigr),

and

.
In the rest of the paper, we omit

of

since

. According to
[
Mat13
, Proposition 3.6]
, there exist sets
D_{s}(0\leq s\leq\dim B)
with the following properties:

(1)

D_{s}
is an analytic (possibly, not closed, not irreducible, singular) set on
X
.

(2)

D_{\dim B}:=B\supset D_{\dim B-1}\supset\dots\supset D_{1}\supset D_{0}
.

(3)

\dim D_{s}=s
for
s=0,1,2,\dots,\dim B
.

(4)

D_{s}\setminus D_{s-1}
for
s=1,2,\dots,\dim B
is a disjoint union of non-singular analytic sets.

(5)

For an irreducible component
W
of
D_{s}\setminus D_{s-1}
with
\dim W>n-k
, the
(1,1)
-form
\alpha\mid_{W}
has
(\dim W-n+k)
positive eigenvalues.

Accoridng to
[
Mat13
, Proposition 3.7]
, for
s=0,1,\dots,\dim B
, there exists a function
\varphi_{s}\in C^{2}(X,\mathbb{R})
with the following properties:

(1)

Let
W
be an irreducible component of
D_{s}\setminus D_{s-1}
. Then the Levi form
\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi_{s}
has
(n-\dim W)
positive eigenvalues in the normal direction of
W
.

(2)

The form
\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi_{s}
is semi-positive at every point in
\overline{D_{s}}
.

Fix a Kähler form
\omega
on
X
.
We claim that for any
x\in X
there exists a constant
\varepsilon(x)>0
such that for any
0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon(x)
, the form

(4.1)

\alpha+\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\left(\sum_{a=0}^{\dim B}\varepsilon^{\sum_{b=0}^{a}k^{b}}\varphi_{\dim B-a}\right)

is
(\omega,k)
-subharmonic at
x
, i.e., the eigenvalue vector of this form with respect to
\omega
at
x
is in
\Gamma_{k}
. If this is true, from the compactness of
X
, the theorem is proved. Now we prove the claim. Suppose
x\in X\setminus B
. Then, by the definition of
B
, the form
\alpha
has
k
positive eigenvalues and
(\dim X-k)
semi-positive eigenvalues at
x
. Thus, we have
\sigma_{i}(\lambda(\alpha\cdot\omega^{-1}))>0
for
i=1,2,\dots,k
, namely
\lambda(\alpha\cdot\omega^{-1})\in\Gamma_{k}
, where
\lambda(\alpha\cdot\omega^{-1})
is the eigenvalue vector of
\alpha
with respect to
\omega
. Since form (
4.1
) converges to
\alpha
as
\varepsilon\to 0
, there exists a constant
\varepsilon(x)
such that if
\varepsilon<\varepsilon(x)
then form (
4.1
) is
(\omega,k)
-subharmonic at
x
. Next, suppose
x\in D_{s}\setminus D_{s-1}
for
s=0,1,\dots,\dim B
.
By property (
5
) of
D_{s}
and properties (
1
) and (
2
) of
\varphi_{s}
, the form

(4.2)

\alpha+\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\left(\varepsilon^{\sum_{b=0}^{\dim B-s}k^{b}}\varphi_{s}\right)

has
k
positive eigenvalues and
\dim X-k
semipositive eigenvalues for any
\varepsilon>0
. Thus, its eigenvalue vector is in
\Gamma_{k}
.
On the other hand, take a coordinate neighborhood around
x
such that
\alpha=\sum c_{i}\sqrt{-1}dz^{i}\wedge d\bar{z}^{i}
at
x
, where
c_{i}
’s are one or zero, and
\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi_{s}=\sum\lambda_{i}\sqrt{-1}dz^{i}\wedge d\bar{z}^{i}
at
x
. By property (
2
) of
\{D_{s}\}
and property (
2
) of
\{\varphi_{s}\}
, the
i
-th eigenvalue of form (
4.1
) can be estimated from below by

\tilde{\lambda}_{i}:=c_{i}+\varepsilon^{\sum_{b=0}^{\dim B-s}k^{b}}\lambda_{i}-\varepsilon^{\sum_{b=0}^{\dim B-s+1}k^{b}}C,

for some
C>0
at
x
. For
j=1,2,\dots,k
, we have

\displaystyle\sigma_{j}\bigl((\tilde{\lambda}_{i})_{i}\bigr)=\sigma_{j}\bigl((c_{i}+\varepsilon^{\sum_{b=0}^{\dim B-s}k^{b}}\lambda_{i})_{i}\bigr)+O(\varepsilon^{\sum_{b=0}^{\dim B-s+1}k^{b}}).

Note that the first term of the right-hand side has at most
(\varepsilon^{\sum_{b=1}^{\dim B-s+1}k^{b}})
-order since
j\leq k
and is positive since the eigenvalue vector of form (
4.2
) is in
\Gamma_{k}
. Therefore, by using the monotonicity of
\sigma_{j}
in
\Gamma_{j}
, we see that the eigenvalue vector of form (
4.1
) is in
\Gamma_{k}
for sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
. Hence, we verified the claim and proved the theorem.

Appendix A
Relation to
q
-positivity in the sense of Andreotti-Grauert

Let
X
be an
n
-dimensional compact Kähler manifold. In this appendix, we make several remarks on the relation between
(\omega,k)
-subharmonicity and
(n-k)
-positivity in the sense of Andreotti-Grauert.
Recall the definition of
q
-positivity:

Definition A.1

(
q
-positivity)
.

A holomorphic line bundle
L
on
X
is called
q
-positive if there exists a smooth Hermitian metric
h
whose Chern curvature
\sqrt{-1}\Theta_{h}
has at least
(n-q)
positive eigenvalues at any point on
X
as a
(1,1)
-form.

Yang
[
Yang19
, Proposition 2.2]
gives the following characterization of
q
-positivity:

Proposition A.2

(
[
Yang19
, Proposition 2.2]
)
.

The following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

A holomorphic line bundle
L
is
q
-positive.

(2)

There exist a smooth hermitian metric
h
and a smooth hermitian form
\omega
on
X
such that the summation of any distinct
(q+1)
eigenvalues
(
counting multiplicity
)
of the Chern curvature
\sqrt{-1}\Theta_{h}
of
h
is positive at any point of
X

(
with respect to
\omega)
.

The latter condition is called “uniform
q
-positivity” in
[
Yang19
, Definition 2.1]
. It is also related to “
(q+1)
-plurisubharmonicity” in the sense of Harvey-Lawson’s geometric potential theory
[
HL09
, p434–435]
.

Since
\sigma_{i}
is striclty increasing in
\Gamma_{i}
as in Proposition
2.2
, if the Chern curvature
\sqrt{-1}\Theta_{h}
is
(\omega,k)
-subharmonic for some smooth hermitian form
\omega
, the summation of any distinct
(n-k+1)
eigenvalues (conting multiplicity) of
\sqrt{-1}\Theta_{h}
is positive at any point of
X
with respect to
\omega
. Hence,
(\omega,k)
-subharmonicity implies
(n-k)
-positivity.

On the other hand, we can prove a converse implication:

Proposition A.3
.

If
L
is
q
-positive, there exist a smooth hermitian metric
h
of
L
and a smooth hermitian form
\omega
on
X
such that the Chern curvature form
\sqrt{-1}\Theta_{h}
is
(\omega,n-q)
-subharmonic.

Proof.

We prove the statement by induction. Suppose
q=n-1
. Then, the uniform
q
-positivity is nothing but the
(\omega,1)
-subharmonicity. So the statement holds. Suppose now that the statement holds for any integers strictly larger than
q
. Since the
q
-positivity implies
(q+1)
-positivity, by the assumption of induction, there exist a smooth hermitian metric
h
and a smooth hermitian form
\tilde{\omega}
on
X
such that the Chern curvature
\sqrt{-1}\Theta_{h}
is
(\tilde{\omega},n-q-1)
-subharmonic.
Let us fix a point
p\in X
. Take a coordinate neighborhood
U_{p}
of
p
such that
\tilde{\omega}(p)=\sum\sqrt{-1}dz^{i}\wedge d\bar{z}^{i}
and
R(p)=\sum\lambda_{i}\sqrt{-1}dz^{i}\wedge d\bar{z}^{i}
, where
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\dots\geq\lambda_{n}
. Note that

\sigma_{n-q}(\lambda)=\lambda_{1}\sigma_{n-q-1}(\check{\lambda})+\sigma_{n-q}(\check{\lambda}),

where
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dots,\lambda_{n})
and
\check{\lambda}=(\lambda_{2},\dots,\lambda_{n})
. Since
\sigma_{n-q-1}(\check{\lambda})>0
by the assumption of induction, taking a hermitian form
\Omega_{p}
on
U_{p}
whose corresponding metric measures the eigenvector for
\lambda_{1}
small enough at
p
, we can get
\sigma_{n-q}(\tilde{\lambda})>0
at
p
, where
\tilde{\lambda}
is the eigenvalue vector of
R\Omega^{-1}_{p}
. By taking a sufficiently smaller neighborhood
W_{p}
than
U_{p}
, we can get
\sigma_{n-q}(\tilde{\lambda})>0
on
W_{p}
. By compactness, there exist a finite number of points
\{p_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
such that
\{W_{p_{i}}\}
is an open cover of
X
. Denote a partition of unity subordinate to
\{W_{p_{i}}\}
by
\{f_{i}\}
. Define the hermitian form
\omega
by the equation

\omega^{-1}=\sum_{i}f_{i}\Omega^{-1}_{p_{i}}.

Then, by the convexity of
(\sigma_{i})^{1/i}
in
\Gamma_{i}
(see
[
Spr05
, Corollary 2.4]
), we get

\displaystyle\Bigl(\sigma_{n-q}(R\omega^{-1})\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{n-q}}=

\displaystyle\left(\sigma_{n-q}(\sum_{i}f_{i}R\Omega^{-1}_{p_{i}}))\right)^{\frac{1}{n-q}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sum_{i}f_{i}\left(\sigma_{n-q}(R\Omega^{-1}_{p_{i}})\right)^{\frac{1}{n-q}}>0.\qed

Finally, we remark that a stronger implication holds for semiample line bundles. Recall that we only needed the generic fiber of the Iitaka fibration associated with a semiample line bundle
L
to have a dimension less than
n-k
in the proof of Theorem
1.9
.
[
Mat13
, Theorem 1.4]
claims that this condition is equivalent to
(n-k)
-positivity of
L
. In conclusion, for any fixed hermitian form
\omega
, the
(n-k)
-positivity of a semiample line bundle
L
implies there exists a smooth hermitian metric
h
whose Chern curvature form is
(\omega,k)
-subharmonic.

Exact Analytical Traversable Wormhole Solutions in Teleparallel F ⁡ ( T ) F(T) Gravity with the de Rham–Gabadadze–Tolley Massive Graviton Sector
A. Landry, Y. Sekhmani, E.N. Saridakis, S. K. Maurya, A. Ali

Abstract
In this paper, we derive a novel class of exact, static, spherically symmetric traversable wormhole solutions in teleparallel F ⁡ ( T ) F(T) gravity augmented by the non–linear de Rham–Gabadadze–Tolley (dRGT) graviton mass term. By using the Morris–Thorne ansatz with generalized redshift function including constant, logarithmic and power-law profiles, we rigorously integrate the dRGT–modified conservation laws together with the teleparallel field equations to reconstruct both the shape function b ⁡ ( r ) b(r) and the possible torsion-based teleparallel F ⁡ ( T ) F(T) solutions. Our solutions, which accommodate both pressureless dust and dark-energy perfect fluids, are naturally expressed via special function representations. We verify the flaring-out condition b ′ ​ ( r 0 ) < 1 b^{\prime}(r_{0})<1 , demonstrate the absence of event horizons, and show that the null and weak energy conditions are either strictly satisfied or incur only controlled violations at the throat. These results establish teleparallel massive gravity as a self-consistent, mathematically rich framework for asymptotically flat wormhole geometries without necessarily invoking an explicit cosmological constant. Keywords: Massive gravity, de Rham models, Teleparallel spherically symmetric spacetimes, Massive dRGT teleparallel F ⁡ ( T ) F(T) solutions, Born-infeld teleparallel F ⁡ ( T ) F(T) solutions, polynomial teleparallel F ⁡ ( T ) F(T) solutions

VIII
Discussion and Conclusion

We have presented several new classes of exact, static, spherically symmetric traversable wormhole geometries within the teleparallel
F(T)
framework, supplemented by the dRGT graviton mass energy-momentum source term. We adopted the Morris–Thorne metric ansatz with carefully chosen ansatz and redshift-function parametrizations (constant, logarithmic and power-law), and we succeeded in integrating the modified CLs and FEs in closed form. This procedure yielded explicit expressions for the shape function
b(r)
and the corresponding teleparallel
F(T)
solutions, often involving some typical special functions. We have used a cosmological perfect fluid representing a DE dominated universe for making a good parallel with recent teleparallel
F(T)
solutions
SSpaper
;
scalarfieldKSpaper
;
scalarfieldSSpaper
;
roberthudsonSSpaper
;
nonvacSSpaper
;
nonvacKSpaper
. In particular, the solutions found in section
V
can be easily compared to those obtained in the recent literature for DE sources. In addition, the section
VI
solutions are also interesting for future astrophysical applications. Here again, DE sources are still worth considering, as in sections
IV
and
V
. Therefore, the solutions in section
VI
complete the study of possible teleparallel
F(T)
solutions and somewhat synthesize the previous results.

Our analysis highlights the essential role of the massive graviton sector in sustaining the wormhole throat. In both the pressureless-dust and DE regimes, the torsion scalar
T
couples nontrivially to the mass term, allowing for throat flaring (
b^{\prime}(r_{0})<1
) and horizon avoidance. Crucially, we demonstrate that the null and weak ECs can be respected—or at worst only minimally violated—near the throat, thereby obviating the need for exotic matter with unphysical properties. This result underscores the viability of teleparallel massive gravity as an alternative to GR for constructing physically tenable wormholes without an explicit cosmological constant.

Beyond the specific solutions obtained, our work unveils several structural insights into the teleparallel massive gravity landscape. First, the exact reconstruction of teleparallel
F(T)
models reveals a deep mathematical interplay between torsion, graviton mass, and cosmological fluid content. Second, the prevalence of special function solutions points to connections with integrable systems and may facilitate future analytical studies of perturbations and stability, as done for the teleparallel Minkowski spacetime in ref
landryvandenhoogen1
. Third, the modular nature of the redshift function ansätz suggests that our method can be straightforwardly extended to anisotropic fluids, non-trivial topologies or time-dependent backgrounds.

Looking ahead, there are three promising avenues:

1.

Rotating and Axially Symmetric Extensions.
Incorporating frame-dragging effects in teleparallel massive gravity may yield new classes of rotating wormholes with distinctive phenomenological signatures. However, we need to primarily find the fundamental axially symmetric teleparallel spacetime geometries before going to DE and massive source solutions. There are now a very small number of such studies on this subject, but we will eventually need to focus on this point in the near future
bahamonde2021exploring
;
salvatore1
. This challenge is feasible and reasonably achievable, but above all it will allow us to resolve a multitude of concrete astrophysical systems with rotating wormholes and stars.

2.

Dynamical Embeddings.
Embedding our static solutions into Teleparallel Robertson–Walker (TRW) or Kantowski–Sachs (KS) cosmologies could bridge wormhole physics and large-scale dynamics, illuminating possible cosmological wormhole scenarios. We know the TRW and KS spacetime structures in teleparallel gravity, but we can perform additional studies with massive sources by starting with some recent literature features

3.

Extended Matter Couplings.
Coupling to Born–Infeld–type, scalarized or nonminimal dark-energy sectors may produce further regular teleparallel geometries and enrich screening mechanisms intrinsic to teleparallel massive gravity. A future study on scalarized wormholes in a dRGT massive source and a cosmological DE scalar field may allow us to answer this question. The Born-Infeld theory considers by default the cosmological constant inside the teleparallel
F(T)
function. In a such case, scalarized wormhole solutions will also be suitable in most cases and confirm the relevance of this theory as for cosmological fluid case. We can also add the strong coupling problem including matter coupling at the Planck scale. This last problem also constitutes another challenge that has to be taken from the same perspective.

In sum, the exact solutions presented herein significantly broaden the taxonomy of wormholes available in modified-gravity theories and provide a robust foundation for future studies of their astrophysical observables, stability properties and potential experimental signatures. Beyond any gravitational considerations, we are also testing the strong power of teleparallel
F(T)
gravity by studying the massive teleparallel wormhole solutions. We are at the beginning of the process and we can test any additional massive theories (not only dRGT) by the same type of development. Most of the previous perspectives are useful to go in this direction. All great reasons to focus on that.

Abstract
. Let n ∈ ℕ ≥ 2 n\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 2} . We prove that for every k ≥ 4 k\geq 4 there exist uniform but non-homogeneous Steiner bundles on ℙ n \mathbb{P}^{n} of k k -type with disconnected splitting type, and we further investigate almost-uniform Steiner bundles. Our approach relies on interpreting Steiner bundles as relative projective Kronecker representations and applying adjoint pairs arising from restriction, inflation, and reflection functors.



Introduction

Let
\mathbbm{k}
be an algebraically closed field and
n\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 2}
. Restricting vector bundles on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
along linear embeddings
\mathbb{P}^{d}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{n}
for
1\leq d\leq n
is a frequently used technique in algebraic geometry, since restrictions of a given vector bundle
\mathcal{F}\in\Vect(\mathbb{P}^{n})
often allow conclusions about the bundle itself. For example, by Horrocks’ splitting principle
[
OSS80
, (2.3.1),(2.3.2)]
a vector bundle
\mathcal{F}\in\Vect(\mathbb{P}^{n})
splits (as a direct sum of line bundles) if and only if a restriction of
\mathcal{F}
to some plane
\mathbb{P}^{2}
splits.

By Grothendieck’s Theorem
[
OSS80
, (2.1.1)]
, every vector bundle on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
splits. More precisely, any vector bundle
\mathcal{G}
on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
admits a decomposition

\mathcal{G}\cong\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}b_{i}\,\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(i)

into Serre twists of the structure sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}
, which is unique up to isomorphism.
As a consequence, the restriction of a bundle
\mathcal{F}\in\Vect(\mathbb{P}^{n})
to a line
\mathbb{P}^{1}\cong L\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{n}
splits and yields a decomposition

\mathcal{F}|_{L}\cong\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}b_{i}(L)\mathcal{O}_{L}(i).

Moreover, there exists a uniquely determined sequence
(b_{i}(\mathcal{F}))_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
of natural numbers such that

O_{\mathcal{F}}\coloneqq\{L\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{n}\mid L\ \text{line},\forall i\in\mathbb{Z}\colon b_{i}(L)=b_{i}(\mathcal{F})\}

is an open subset of the Grassmannian
G(\mathbb{P}^{n})
of lines in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
. The corresponding subset

\supp(\mathcal{F})\coloneqq\{i\in\mathbb{Z}\mid b_{i}(\mathcal{F})\neq 0\}

is called
support
of
\mathcal{F}
.

The
\mathcal{F}
vector bundle is referred to as
uniform
if
O_{\mathcal{F}}=G(\mathbb{P}^{n})
, and
homogeneous
, provided

t^{\ast}(\mathcal{F})\cong\mathcal{F}

for every projective transformation
t\in\PGL_{n+1}(\mathbbm{k})
, where
t^{\ast}(\mathcal{F})
denotes the pullback of
\mathcal{F}
along
t
. Since
\PGL_{n+1}(\mathbbm{k})
acts transitively on lines, homogeneous vector bundles are always uniform.

In 1961 Schwarzenberger
[
Sch61
, (1.2)]
raised the question, whether all uniform vector bundles on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
are homogeneous. The first counterexample was given by Elencwajg
[
Ele79
]
in 1979. He constructed a uniform but non-homogeneous bundle of rank
4
on
\mathbb{P}^{2}
over
\mathbb{C}
. Drézet
[
Dré80
]
generalized this result by proving the existence of uniform but non-homogeneous vector bundles on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
of rank

for every
n\geq 2
and
s\geq 2n
.
It is further conjectured that, in characteristic zero, Drézet’s bundles are of minimal rank in this context; that is, every uniform vector bundle on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
of rank
<2n
is already homogeneous (see
[
EM16
, §3]
)

We also apply our approach to the study of

\StVect(\mathbb{P}^{n})

Theorem C

Let
\varnothing\neq\mathcal{L}\subseteq G(\mathbb{P}^{n})

\StVect(\mathbb{P}^{n})
consisting of almost-uniform Steiner bundles with set of jumping lines

\TilTheta

\rep({K_{n+1}})

1.1.
Homogeneous and uniform vector bundles

Given a projective variety
X
, we denote
\mathcal{O}_{X}
its structure sheaf and let
\Coh(X)
be the category of coherent
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules and
\Vect(X)
be the category of vector bundles, i.e., locally free sheaves of finite rank on
X
. Both
\Coh(X)
and
\Vect(X)
are Krull-Schmidt categories (see
[
Ati56
]
), while
\Coh(X)
is also an abelian category
[
GW10
, (7.46)]
.

Given
\mathbbm{k}
-vector spaces
V
and
W
, we define

\Inj_{\mathbbm{k}}(V,W):=\{\alpha\in\Hom_{\mathbbm{k}}(V,W)\mid\alpha\ \text{is injective}\}.

Every element
\alpha\in\Inj_{\mathbbm{k}}(V,W)
induces an injective morphism
\hat{\alpha}:\mathbb{P}(V)\longrightarrow\mathbb{P}(W)
of varieties, which gives rise to the inverse image functor

\hat{\alpha}^{\ast}:\Coh(\mathbb{P}(W))\longrightarrow\Coh(\mathbb{P}(V)).

The inverse image functor is known to be right exact (cf.
[
GW10
, (7.11)]
) and sends vector bundles to vector bundles. Moreover,
\hat{\alpha}^{\ast}
commutes with direct sums and the map that sends a fraction
\frac{f}{g}
of homogeneous polynomials to

\frac{f\circ\alpha}{g\circ\alpha}
induces isomorphisms
\hat{\alpha}^{\ast}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(W)}(j))\cong\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(V)}(j)
for every
j\in\mathbb{Z}
, where
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(W)}(j)
and
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(V)}(j)
denote the
j
-th
Serre twisting sheaf
on
\mathbb{P}(W)
and
\mathbb{P}(V)
, respectively.

The general linear group
\GL(A_{r})
acts on
\mathbb{P}(A_{r})
via automorphisms and hence on
\Coh(\mathbb{P}(A_{r}))
: Given
g\in\GL(A_{r})
and
\mathcal{F}\in\Coh(\mathbb{P}(A_{r}))
, we define

(g^{\ast}.\mathcal{F})(U)\coloneqq\mathcal{F}(g^{-1}.U)

for every open subset
U\subseteq\mathbb{P}(A_{r})
. By definition (see
[
GW10
, §7.8]
) we have

g^{\ast}.\mathcal{F}=\widehat{g^{-1}}^{\ast}(\mathcal{F})

for all
g\in\GL(A_{r})=\Inj_{\mathbbm{k}}(A_{r},A_{r})
.

Definition 1.1.1
.

A vector bundle
\mathcal{F}\in\Vect(\mathbb{P}(A_{r}))
is called

(1)

uniform
, if
\hat{\alpha}^{\ast}(\mathcal{F})\cong\hat{\beta}^{\ast}(\mathcal{F})
for all
\alpha,\beta\in\Inj_{\mathbbm{k}}(A_{2},A_{r})
.

(2)

homogeneous
, provided
g^{\ast}.\mathcal{F}\cong\mathcal{F}
for all
g\in\GL(A_{r})
.

Since
\GL(A_{r})
acts transitively on
\Inj_{\mathbbm{k}}(A_{2},A_{r})
via
g\cdot\alpha:=g\circ\alpha
for all
g\in\GL(A_{r})
and
\alpha\in\Inj_{\mathbbm{k}}(A_{2},A_{r})
, every homogeneous vector bundle on
\mathbb{P}(A_{r})
is uniform. However, for
r\geq 3
, there exist uniform but non-homogeneous vector bundles on
\mathbb{P}(A_{r})

[
Ele79
]
.

On
\mathbb{P}(A_{2})\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}
, every vector bundle
\mathcal{G}\in\Vect(\mathbb{P}(A_{2}))
is homogeneous. This follows from Grothendieck’s Theorem
[
OSS80
, (Theorem 2.1.1)]
, which asserts that every vector bundle on
\mathbb{P}(A_{2})
admits a decomposition as a direct sum of line bundles

\mathcal{G}\cong\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}b_{i}\,\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(A_{2})}(i),

and the fact that all line bundles
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(A_{2})}(j)
are homogeneous.

As a consequence of Grothendieck’s Theorem, we obtain for every
\mathcal{F}\in\Vect(\mathbb{P}(A_{r}))
and every
\alpha\in\Inj_{\mathbbm{k}}(A_{2},A_{r})
a unique decomposition

\hat{\alpha}^{\ast}(\mathcal{F})\cong\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}b_{i}(\alpha,\mathcal{F})\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(A_{2})}(i).

By the same token, we obtain for every
2
-dimensional subspace
\fv\in\Gr_{2}(A_{r})
a decomposition

\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}b_{i}(\fv,\mathcal{F})\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(\fv)}(i)

of the vector bundle
\mathcal{F}|_{\fv}\coloneqq\hat{\iota}^{\ast}(\mathcal{F})\in\Vect(\mathbb{P}(\fv))
, where
\iota\colon\fv\longrightarrow A_{r}
is the inclusion morphism and
\hat{\iota}^{\ast}\colon\Coh(\mathbb{P}(A_{r}))\longrightarrow\Coh(\mathbb{P}(\fv))
the corresponding inverse image functor.
Let
\alpha_{0}\colon A_{2}\longrightarrow\fv
be an isomorphism and set
\alpha:=\iota\circ\alpha_{0}\in\Inj_{\mathbbm{k}}(A_{2},A_{r})
. By
[
GW10
, (7.8.9)]
we have
\hat{\alpha}^{\ast}(\mathcal{F})\!=\!\widehat{\iota\circ\alpha_{0}}^{\ast}(\mathcal{F})\!\cong\!\hat{\alpha_{0}}^{\ast}(\hat{\iota}^{\ast}(\mathcal{F}))
and conclude

\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}b_{i}(\alpha,\mathcal{F})\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(A_{2})}(i)\!\cong\!\hat{\alpha}^{\ast}(\mathcal{F})\!\cong\!\hat{\alpha_{0}}^{\ast}(\hat{\iota}^{\ast}(\mathcal{F}))\!\cong\!\hat{\alpha_{0}}^{\ast}(\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}b_{i}(\fv,\mathcal{F})\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(\fv)}(i))\!\cong\!\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}b_{i}(\fv,\mathcal{F})\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(A_{2})}(i).

Hence,
b_{i}(\alpha,\mathcal{F})=b_{i}(\fv,\mathcal{F})
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
. As a direct consequence we obtain the following result.

.

with

. If

Definition 1.3.1
.

A vector bundle

whose objects are Steiner bundles.

Remark 1.3.2
.

It follows from
[

, (3.2)]

Connections between Kronecker representations and Steiner bundles on

]

[

and the category of Steiner bundles - along with a generalized version for Steiner bundles on Grassmannians

[

. We only recall the result for

Theorem 1.3.3
.

There exists a right exact functor

2.
Homogeneous and uniform representations

Let
M\in\rep(K_{r})
be homogeneous and
\alpha,\beta\in\Inj_{\mathbbm{k}}(A_{2},A_{r})
. We find
g\in\GL(A_{r})
such that
\alpha=g\circ\beta
and conclude

\alpha^{\ast}(M)=(g\circ\beta)^{\ast}(M)\cong\beta^{\ast}(g^{\ast}(M))\cong\beta^{\ast}(g^{-1}.M)\cong\beta^{\ast}(M).

Hence, homogeneous representations are uniform. Based on the equivalence
\TilTheta\colon\rep_{\proj}(K_{r},1)\longrightarrow\StVect(\mathbb{P}(A_{r}))
,
1.5.3
and the findings in
[
MM21
]
, it is already clear that not all uniform representations are homogeneous.

In what follows, we show that the examples constructed in
[
MM21
]
, which provide uniform but non-homogeneous representations, are not isolated phenomena. In fact, we establish the existence of a broad class of such representations. This is achieved by proving that homogeneous and uniform representations exhibit different behavior with respect to Auslander-Reiten theory and within the variety of representations.

Throughout this section we assume that
(V_{1},V_{2})
is a pair of finite-dimensional
\mathbbm{k}
-vector spaces such that
V_{1}\oplus V_{2}\neq\{0\}
.

2.1.
The variety of representations and Kac’s Theorem

We denote by

\mathcal{V}(K_{r};V_{1},V_{2})\coloneqq\Hom_{\mathbbm{k}}(A_{r}\otimes_{\mathbbm{k}}V_{1},V_{2})

the (irreducible) affine variety of representations of
K_{r}
on
(V_{1},V_{2})
. A point
\psi
in the variety
\mathcal{V}(K_{r};V_{1},V_{2})
can be identified with the representation
V_{\psi}=(V_{1},V_{2},\psi\colon A_{r}\otimes_{\mathbbm{k}}V_{1}\longrightarrow V_{2})\in\mathcal{K}_{r}\cong\rep(K_{r})
. In the following we will freely use this identification.

The
Euler-Ringel form
of
K_{r}
is given by

\langle-,-\rangle_{r}\colon\mathbb{Z}^{2}\times\mathbb{Z}^{2}\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}\ ;\ (x,y)\mapsto x_{1}y_{1}+x_{2}y_{2}-rx_{1}y_{2}

and satisfies
\langle\udim M,\udim N\rangle_{r}=\dim_{\mathbbm{k}}\Hom_{K_{r}}(M,N)-\dim_{\mathbbm{k}}\Ext^{1}_{K_{r}}(M,N)
for all
M,N\in\rep(K_{r})
. The corresponding
Tits quadratic
form is denoted by
q_{r}\colon\mathbb{Z}^{2}\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}\ ;\ x\mapsto\langle x,x\rangle_{r}=x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}-rx_{1}x_{2}
.

According to
[
Kac82
, (Thm B)]
, an indecomposable representation
M\in\rep(K_{r})
satisfies
q_{r}(\dimu M)\leq 1
. An element
(x,y)\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{2}
is called
Schur root
, provided there exists a brick
M\in\rep(K_{r})
such that
\dimu M=(x,y)
.
We denote by

\mathcal{B}(V_{1},V_{2}):=\{\psi\in\mathcal{V}(K_{r};V_{1},V_{2})\mid V_{\psi}\ \text{is a brick}\}\subseteq\mathcal{V}(K_{r};V_{1},V_{2})

the open subset of bricks in
\mathcal{V}(K_{r};V_{1},V_{2})
.
Note that
\dimu(V_{1},V_{2})
is a Schur root if and only if
\mathcal{B}(V_{1},V_{2})\neq\varnothing
.
Kac’s Theorem provides a complete answer to the question of whether
(x,y)
is a Schur root (see
[
Kac82
, (6(c), p.159)]
). We present a simplified version sufficient for the present discussion.

Theorem 2.1.1
.

Let
r\geq 2
. The following statements hold.

(1)

The subset
\mathcal{B}(V_{1},V_{2})

is non-empty if and only if
q_{r}(\dimu(V_{1},V_{2}))\leq 1
.

(2)

The following statements are equivalent.

(i)

\mathcal{V}(K_{r};V_{1},V_{2})
contains a regular representation.

(ii)

Every indecomposable representation in
\mathcal{V}(K_{r};V_{1},V_{2})
is regular.

(iii)

q_{r}(\dimu(V_{1},V_{2}))\leq 0
.

(3)

If
q_{r}(\dimu(V_{1},V_{2}))=1
and
M\in\mathcal{V}(K_{r};V_{1},V_{2})
is indecomposable, then there exists a unique
i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that
M\cong P_{i}(r)
or
M\cong I_{i}(r)
.

As a consequence of
2.1.1
, we refer to any
(x,y)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
satisfying
q_{r}(x,y)\leq 0
as a
regular dimension vector
.

2.2.
Homogeneous representations

This section is devoted to the study of homogeneous representations in
\rep(K_{r})
for
r\geq 2
.
To set the stage, we begin with a general structural result that will play a key role in our analysis.
Although the statement holds in greater generality for any associative algebra and any connected algebraic group
[
Far11
, (2.1), (2.2)]
, we include a complete proof in our setting for the reader’s convenience.

Recall that the algebraic group

G(A_{r};V_{1},V_{2}):=\GL(A_{r})\times\GL(V_{2})\times\GL(V_{1})

acts on the variety
\mathcal{V}(K_{r};V_{1},V_{2})
via

(a,g_{2},g_{1}).\psi:=g_{2}\circ\psi\circ(a^{-1}\otimes g_{1}^{-1})

for all
a\in A_{r},g_{1}\in\GL(V_{1}),g_{2}\in\GL(V_{2})
, and that
\psi,\psi^{\prime}
are in the same orbit under this action if and only if there exists
g\in\GL(A_{r})
such that
g.V_{\psi}\cong V_{\psi^{\prime}}.

Lemma 2.2.1
.

(1)

Let
M\in\rep(K_{r})
, then
\GL(A_{r})_{M}:=\{g\in\GL(A_{r})\mid g.M\cong M\}
is a closed subgroup of
\GL(A_{r})
.

(2)

Homogeneous representations are closed under direct sums and summands.

(3)

A regular Auslander-Reiten component
\mathcal{C}
contains a homogeneous representation if and only if every representation in
\mathcal{C}
is homogeneous.

(4)

All preprojective and preinjective representations are homogeneous.

(5)

Every non-zero regular representation
M\in\rep(K_{2})
is not homogeneous.

Proof.

(1)

We set
G:=G(A_{r};M_{2},M_{1})
. Restriction of the canonical projection
\pi\colon G\longrightarrow\GL(A_{r})
yields the morphism of algebraic groups

\pi_{M}\colon\Stab_{G}(\psi_{M})\longrightarrow\GL(A_{r})\ ;\ (g,a_{2},a_{1})\mapsto g.

Note that
\GL(A_{r})_{M}=\im\pi_{M}\subseteq\GL(A_{r})
is closed, cf.
[
Spr98
, (2.2.5)]
.

(2)

Clearly, homogeneity is closed under direct sums. Let
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}\in\rep(K_{r})
be indecomposable and assume that
M:=\bigoplus^{n}_{i=1}X_{i}
is homogeneous. We have
\bigoplus^{n}_{i=1}g.X_{i}\cong g.M\cong M\cong\bigoplus^{n}_{i=1}X_{i}
for all
g\in G
. By the Theorem of Krull-Remak-Schmidt, we therefore obtain an action
\GL(A_{r})
on the set of isomorphism classes
\{[X_{1}],\ldots,[X_{n}]\}
with
\Stab_{\GL(A_{r})}([X_{i}])=\GL(A_{r})_{X_{i}}
. Since
|\GL(A_{r})/\Stab_{\GL(A_{r})}([X_{i}])|=|\GL(A_{r}).[X_{i}]|
is finite and
\GL(A_{r})
is connected, we conclude with
[
Spr98
, (2.2.1)(iii)]
that
\Stab_{\GL(A_{r})}([X_{i}])=\GL(A_{r})
. Hence,
g.X_{i}\cong X_{i}
for all
g\in\GL(A_{r})
and every
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
.

(3)

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a regular component of
K_{r}
and assume that
\mathcal{C}
contains a homogeneous representation. Hence,
\mathcal{D}:=\{X\in\mathcal{C}\mid X\ \text{is homogeneous}\}

is non-empty. Let
Y\in\mathcal{C}
. Since
Y
is not injective, there exists an almost split sequence

0\longrightarrow\tau_{K_{r}}(Y)\longrightarrow E\longrightarrow Y\longrightarrow 0.

As
Y
is homogeneous and
\GL(A_{r})
acts on
\rep(K_{r})
by auto-equivalences, we obtain for each
g\in\GL(A_{r})
an almost split sequence

0\longrightarrow g.\tau_{K_{r}}(Y)\longrightarrow g.E\longrightarrow Y\longrightarrow 0.

Uniqueness of almost split sequences ending in
Y

[
ARS95
, (V.1.16)]
implies that
\tau_{K_{r}}(Y)
and
E
are homogeneous. In particular,
\mathcal{D}
is closed under the
\tau_{K_{r}}
. Moreover, (2) implies that
\mathcal{D}
is closed under predecessors.
By the same token,
\mathcal{D}
is closed under successors in
\mathcal{C}
and
\tau_{K_{r}}^{-1}
. Since
\mathcal{C}
is a connected translation quiver, we conclude
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{D}
.

(4)

In view of (2), it suffices to prove the statement for every indecomposable representations. Since every representation
P_{i}(r)
in the preprojective component is a successor of
S(2)=P_{0}(r)
and
P_{0}(r)=S(2)
is homogeneous, we can apply the arguments given in (3). The proof for
I_{i}(r)\in\mathcal{I}
is similar. We remark that the result also follows from
2.1.1
.

(5)

It is well-known (see for example
[
ARS95
, (VIII.7.4)]
) that every regular component of
K_{2}
contains an indecomposable representation with dimension vector
(1,1)
. It is easy to see that such representations can not be homogeneous. Now we apply (2) and (3).

∎

The goal of the remainder of this section is to establish the following theorem, which shows that a general representation of a fixed regular dimension vector is a non-homogeneous brick.

3.
Restriction, inflation and shifts functors

Let
1\leq d\leq r
. In this section, we recall the definitions of restriction, inflation, and shift functors, and prove that these functors give rise to adjoint pairs between
\rep(K_{d})
and
\rep(K_{r})
.

3.1.
Restriction and inflation

We fix
1\leq d\leq r
. Let
\iota\colon A_{d}\longrightarrow A_{r}
be the canonical embedding sending
\gamma_{i}
to
\gamma_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq d
. We define functors that act as the identity on morphisms:

\res\colon\rep(K_{r})\longrightarrow\rep(K_{d})\ ;\ M\mapsto\res(M):=\iota^{\ast}(M)

with

\psi_{\res(M)}=\psi_{M}\circ(\iota\otimes\id_{M_{1}}),

and

\inf\colon\rep(K_{d})\longrightarrow\rep(K_{r})\ ;\ X\mapsto\inf(X),

where

\psi_{\inf(X)}(\gamma_{i}\otimes x)=\begin{cases}\psi_{X}(\gamma_{i}\otimes x),&1\leq i\leq d\\
0,&i>d.\end{cases}

These two functors allow us to move between
\rep(K_{d})
and
\rep(K_{r})
.
The following example show that
(\inf,\res)
and
(\res,\inf)
are not adjoint pairs.

Example 3.1.1
.

Let
1\leq d<r
. We have
\res(P_{1}(r))\cong P_{1}(d)\oplus(r-d)P_{0}(d)
and

1=\dim_{\mathbbm{k}}\res(P_{1}(d))_{1}=\dim_{\mathbbm{k}}\Hom_{K_{d}}(P_{1}(d),\res(P_{1}(r))).

The representation
\inf(P_{1}(d))\in\rep(K_{r})
is indecomposable and regular (see
2.1.1
). Since there are no non-zero morphisms from regular to projective representations (see
[
ASS06
, (VIII.2.13)]
), we conclude

\Hom_{K_{r}}(\inf(P_{1}(d)),P_{1}(r))=0\neq\Hom_{K_{d}}(P_{1}(d),\res(P_{1}(r))).

By the same token, we have

\Hom_{K_{r}}(I_{1}(r),\inf(I_{1}(d)))=0\neq\Hom_{K_{d}}(\res(I_{1}(r)),I_{1}(d)).

This shows that we can not hope for natural isomorphisms of the form

\Hom_{K_{r}}(\inf(X),M)\cong\Hom_{K_{d}}(X,\res(M))\ \text{and}\ \Hom_{K_{r}}(M,\inf(X))\cong\Hom_{K_{d}}(\res(M),X).

3.2.
Shift functors

In the sequel the
shift functors

\sigma_{K_{r}},\sigma^{-1}_{K_{r}}:\rep(K_{r})\longrightarrow\rep(K_{r})
will be of major importance. These functors correspond reflection functors but take into account that the opposite quiver of
K_{r}
is isomorphic to
K_{r}
, i.e.,
D_{K_{r}}\circ\sigma_{K_{r}}\cong\sigma_{K_{r}}^{-1}\circ D_{K_{r}}
.
We recall the definitions of
\sigma_{K_{r}}
and
\sigma_{K_{r}}^{-1}
in terms of the corresponding structure maps.
Given a representation
M\in\rep(K_{r})
, the structure map

\psi_{\sigma_{K_{r}}(M)}\colon A_{r}\otimes_{\mathbbm{k}}\ker\psi_{M}\longrightarrow M_{1}

of
\sigma_{K_{r}}(M)\in\rep(K_{r})
is the restriction of the
\mathbbm{k}
-linear map

A_{r}\otimes_{\mathbbm{k}}(A_{r}\otimes_{\mathbbm{k}}M_{1})\longrightarrow M_{1}\ ;\ \gamma_{i}\otimes(\gamma_{j}\otimes m)\mapsto\delta_{ij}m,

where
\delta_{ij}
denotes the Kronecker delta.
If
f\in\Hom_{K_{r}}(M,N)
, then
\sigma_{K_{r}}(f)_{1}:\sigma_{K_{r}}(M)_{1}\longrightarrow\sigma_{K_{r}}(N)_{1}
is the restriction of

\id_{A_{r}}\otimes f_{1}\colon A_{r}\otimes_{\mathbbm{k}}M_{1}\longrightarrow A_{r}\otimes_{\mathbbm{k}}N_{1}

to
\ker\psi_{M}
while
\sigma_{K_{r}}(f)_{2}\coloneqq f_{1}
.

The representation
\sigma_{K_{r}}^{-1}(M)
is given by
(M_{2},\coker\eta_{M})
, where

\eta_{M}\colon M_{1}\longrightarrow A_{r}\otimes_{\mathbbm{k}}M_{2}\ ;\ m\mapsto\sum^{r}_{i=1}\gamma_{i}\otimes\psi_{M}(\gamma_{i}\otimes m)

with structure map

\psi_{\sigma_{K_{r}}^{-1}(M)}\colon A_{r}\otimes_{\mathbbm{k}}M_{2}\longrightarrow\coker\eta_{M}\ ;\ a\otimes m\mapsto a\otimes m+\im\eta_{M}.

If
f\in\Hom_{K_{r}}(M,N)
, then
\sigma^{-1}_{K_{r}}(f)_{1}=f_{2}
and
\sigma^{-1}_{K_{r}}(f)_{2}:\sigma_{K_{r}}(M)_{2}\longrightarrow\sigma_{K_{r}}(N)_{1}
is the unique
\mathbbm{k}
-linear map making the diagram

\textstyle{A_{r}\otimes_{\mathbbm{k}}M_{2}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\psi_{\sigma_{K_{r}}^{-1}(M)}}

\scriptstyle{\sigma^{-1}_{K_{r}}(f)_{2}}
\textstyle{A_{r}\otimes_{\mathbbm{k}}N_{2}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

commute.
As
(\sigma_{K_{r}}^{-1},\sigma_{K_{r}})
is an adjoint pair

,
\sigma_{K_{r}}
is left exact, while
\sigma_{K_{r}}^{-1}
is right exact.

For
i\in\{1,2\}
we denote by
\rep_{i}(K_{r})
the full subcategory of
\rep(K_{r})
, whose objects do not have any direct summands isomorphic to the simple representation

and set
\rep_{1,2}(K_{r})\coloneqq\rep_{1}(K_{r})\cap\rep_{2}(K_{r})
.
We also note that
D_{K_{r}}(\rep_{i}(K_{r}))=\rep_{3-i}(K_{r})
for every

. By
[
ASS06
, (VII.5.3)]
, the functor
\sigma_{K_{r}}
induces an equivalence

\sigma_{K_{r}}:\rep_{2}(K_{r})\longrightarrow\rep_{1}(K_{r}).

By the same token,
\sigma_{K_{r}}^{-1}\colon\rep_{1}(K_{r})\longrightarrow\rep_{2}(K_{r})
is a quasi-inverse of
\sigma_{K_{r}}
.
We
also note that

\dimu\sigma_{K_{r}}(M)=(r\dim_{\mathbbm{k}}M_{1}-\dim_{\mathbbm{k}}M_{2},\dim_{\mathbbm{k}}M_{1})

for

\rep_{2}(K_{r})
, while
\sigma_{K_{r}}(S(2))=0
. In conjunction with the left exactness of

\sigma_{K_{r}}\colon\rep_{2}(K_{r})\longrightarrow\rep_{1}(K_{r})
is exact. By the same token,
\sigma_{K_{r}}^{-1}\colon\rep_{1}(K_{r})\longrightarrow\rep_{2}(K_{r})

The map

\sigma_{r}\colon\mathbb{Z}^{2}\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}^{2}\ ;\ (x,y)\mapsto(rx-y,x)

\dimu\sigma_{K_{r}}(M)=\sigma_{r}(\dimu M)\ \text{and}\ \dimu\sigma^{-1}_{K_{r}}(N)=\sigma^{-1}_{r}(\dimu N)

\sigma_{K_{r}}\circ\sigma_{K_{r}}

. By the same token, we have
\sigma_{K_{r}}^{-1}\circ\sigma_{K_{r}}^{-1}\cong\tau_{K_{r}}^{-1}
.

This section is devoted to the proof of the following result. We remark that this result holds in greater generality for any connected quiver

Theorem 3.3.1
.

The functor

\sigma_{K_{r}}^{-1}\circ\inf\colon\rep(K_{d})\longrightarrow\rep(K_{r})\ \text{is left adjoint to}\ \sigma_{K_{d}}\circ\res\colon\rep(K_{r})\longrightarrow\rep(K_{d}).

Let

By definition, we have

f_{2}\in\Hom_{\mathbbm{k}}(\coker\eta_{\inf(X)},M_{2})

For

Corollary 3.3.2
.

The functor

\sigma_{K_{d}}^{-1}\circ\res\colon\rep(K_{r})\longrightarrow\rep(K_{d})

\sigma_{K_{r}}\circ\inf\colon\rep(K_{d})\longrightarrow\rep(K_{r})

(\sigma_{K_{r}}^{-1}\circ\inf,\sigma_{K_{d}}\circ\res)
. Let

and

. We write

and

4.
Families of test representations

Characterizing a full subcategory of
\rep(K_{r})
as being right
\Hom
- or
\Ext^{1}
-orthogonal to certain algebraic families of
test representations
is a well-established technique, employed repeatedly in the study of Kronecker representations (cf.
[
HU91
,
Wor13
,
Bis20
]
).
Recently, test representations have been applied to the study of relative projective representations, as will be outlined in what follows.

Let
1\leq d<r
,
\fv\in\Gr_{d}(A_{r})
and
\alpha\in\Inj_{\mathbbm{k}}(A_{d},A_{r})
with
\im\alpha=\fv
. We consider the projective Kronecker representations
(0,A_{d})\cong dP_{0}(d)
and
(\mathbbm{k},A_{d})\cong P_{1}(d)
, with structure map
\psi_{P_{1}(d)}(a\otimes t)=ta
. The morphism
\alpha
induces a morphism of representations

\overline{\alpha}\colon(0,A_{d})\longrightarrow(\mathbbm{k},A_{r})

by setting
\overline{\alpha}_{1}=0
and
\overline{\alpha}_{2}\coloneqq\alpha
. We define

E(\fv):=D_{K_{r}}(\tau_{K_{r}}(\coker\overline{\alpha})).

In fact, up to isomorphism, this definition does not depend on the choice of
\alpha\in\Inj_{\mathbbm{k}}(A_{d},A_{r})
, cf.
[
BF24
, (2.1.3)]
and the following statements hold (see
[
BF24
, (2.1.3),(2.1.5)]
):

(1)

For
\fv,\mathfrak{w}\in\Gr_{d}(A_{r})
we have
E(\fv)\cong E(\mathfrak{w})
if and only if
\fv=\mathfrak{w}
.

(2)

\rep_{\proj}(K_{r},d)=\{M\in\rep(K_{r})\mid\forall\fv\in\Gr_{d}(A_{r})\colon\Hom_{K_{r}}(E(\fv),M)=0\}
.

In this section, we show that the adjoint pairs constructed in
Section

3
give rise to a family of test representations in
\rep(K_{r})
for every non-semisimple homogeneous representation
X\in\rep(K_{d})
. We then apply this construction to the preprojective indecomposables
P_{n}(d)\in\rep(K_{d})
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, focusing on the case
d=2
to derive consequences for uniform Kronecker representations. Moreover, we show that the family obtained from
P_{1}(d)
coincides with
(E(\fv))_{\fv\in\Gr_{d}(A_{r})}
.

4.1.

gives us

.
∎

Remark 6.3.2
.

Let

. Together with Rolf Farnsteiner

we have proven that every simple and homogeneous Steiner bundle of rank

6.5.
Jumping lines and almost-uniform Steiner bundles

Uniform vector bundles
\mathcal{F}\in\Vect(\mathbb{P}^{n})
are characterized by the property that their set of jumping lines
\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{F}}
is empty (see
Section

1.1
). A natural generalization of this notion, due to Ellia
[
Ell17
]
, is the following:

Definition 6.5.1
.

A vector bundle
\mathcal{F}\in\Vect(\mathbb{P}(A_{r}))
is called
almost-uniform
, provided
\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{F}}
is non-empty and finite.

Using the functor
\widetilde{\Theta}\colon\rep_{\proj}(K_{r},1)\longrightarrow\StVect(\mathbb{P}(A_{r}))
we are able to give a handy criterion to check for almost-uniform Steiner bundles.
In general, it is difficult to compute the generic decomposition of a given representation. However, we have the following useful criterion.

Proposition 6.5.2
.

Let
0\neq M\in\rep_{\proj}(K_{r},1)
.

(1)

If there is
\fv\in\Gr_{2}(A_{r})
such that

, then

M_{\gen}=\Delta_{M}(2)P_{0}(2)\oplus(\dim_{\mathbbm{k}}M_{1})P_{1}(2)

and

\mathcal{J}_{\widetilde{\Theta}(M)}=\mathcal{J}_{M}=\{\mathfrak{u}\in\Gr_{2}(A_{r})\mid\Hom_{K_{r}}(P_{1}^{-}(\mathfrak{u}),M)\neq 0\}.

(2)

If
\{\mathfrak{u}\in\Gr_{2}(A_{r})\mid\Hom_{K_{r}}(P_{1}^{-}(\mathfrak{u}),M)\neq 0\}
is finite and non-empty, then the Steiner bundle
\widetilde{\Theta}(M)
is almost-uniform and

\mathcal{J}_{\widetilde{\Theta}(M)}=\{\mathfrak{u}\in\Gr_{2}(A_{r})\mid\Hom_{K_{r}}(P_{1}^{-}(\mathfrak{u}),M)\neq 0\}.

Proof.

(1)

Since
\Hom_{K_{r}}(P_{1}^{-}(\fv),M)=0
, we conclude with
Corollary

4.4.3
and
[
BF24
, (2.5.5)]
that

M_{\gen}=\Delta_{M}(2)P_{0}(2)\oplus(\dim_{\mathbbm{k}}M_{1})P_{1}(2)

and
O_{M}=\{\mathfrak{u}\in\Gr_{2}(A_{r})\mid M|_{\mathfrak{u}}=\Delta_{M}(2)P_{0}(2)\oplus(\dim_{\mathbbm{k}}M_{1})P_{1}(2)\}

4.4.3
gives us
O_{M}=\{\mathfrak{u}\in\Gr_{2}(A_{r})\mid\Hom_{K_{r}}(P_{1}^{-}(\mathfrak{u}),M)=0\}

\mathcal{J}_{\TilTheta(M)}=\mathcal{J}_{M}=\{\mathfrak{u}\in\Gr_{2}(A_{r})\mid\Hom_{K_{r}}(P_{1}^{-}(\mathfrak{u}),M)\neq 0\}.

(2)

Since

is finite, there is
\fv\in\Gr_{2}(A_{r})
such that

∎

(2) that

\Gr_{d}(A_{r})\setminus(X\cup\{\mathfrak{u}\})\subseteq\{\mathfrak{w}\in\Gr_{d}(A_{r})\mid\Hom_{K_{r}}(P_{1}^{-}(\mathfrak{w}),E_{X})=0\}.

Moreover,
\Hom_{K_{r}}(X_{i},E_{X})\neq 0=\Hom_{K_{r}}(Y,E_{X})
for
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
implies

\Gr_{d}(A_{r})\setminus X=\{\mathfrak{w}\in\Gr_{d}(A_{r})\mid\Hom_{K_{r}}(P_{1}^{-}(\mathfrak{w}),E_{X})=0\}.

We conclude with
Corollary

4.4.3
that

\mathcal{V}(K_{r},d)_{E_{X}}=\{\mathfrak{m}\in\Gr_{d}(A_{r})\mid\Hom_{K_{r}}(P_{1}^{-}(\mathfrak{m}),E_{X})\neq 0\}=X.

Recall from
Proposition

4.4.2
that
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n},Y
are in
\rep_{\proj}(K_{r},d-1)
. Since
\rep_{\proj}(K_{r},d-1)
is closed under extensions, we conclude
E_{X}\in\rep_{\proj}(K_{r},d-1)
and therefore
\mathcal{V}(K_{r},d-1)_{E_{X}}=\varnothing
.

Finally, we compute the dimension vector of
E_{X}
. Let
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. Since
X_{i}
and
Y
are
\Hom
-orthogonal and
\dimu X_{i}=\dimu Y=\dimu P_{1}^{-}(\fv)=(d,rd-1)
, we obtain

\displaystyle s_{i}

\displaystyle=\dim_{\mathbbm{k}}\Ext^{1}_{K_{r}}(Y,X_{i})=-q_{r}(\dimu Y)=-q_{r}(\sigma_{r}(d,rd-1))

\displaystyle=-q_{r}(1,d)=d(r-d)-1.

Hence,

\displaystyle\dimu E_{X}

\displaystyle=|X|\cdot(d(r-d)-1)\cdot\dimu P_{1}^{-}(\fv)+\dimu P_{1}^{-}(\fv)=(|X|\cdot(d(r-d)-1)+1)\cdot\dimu P_{1}^{-}(\fv).

∎

The case
d=2
, when reformulated in the setting of Steiner bundles on
\mathbb{P}(A_{r})
, gives rise to the following statement.

Theorem 6.5.7
.

Let
r\geq 3
and
\varnothing\neq X\subseteq\Gr_{2}(A_{r})
be a finite subset. There exists an abelian and wild subcategory
\mathcal{D}
of
\rep_{\proj}(K_{r},1)
such that for every
M\in\mathcal{D}
the corresponding Steiner bundle
\TilTheta(M)
satisfies
\mathcal{J}_{\TilTheta(M)}=X
. In particular,
{\TilTheta(M)}
is almost-uniform.

Proof.

We fix the regular brick
E_{X}
constructed in
6.5.6
and consider the simplification category
\mathcal{E}(\{E_{X}\})
(cf.
[
KL91
, Section 1]
). Since
E_{X}\in\rep_{\proj}(K_{r},1)
and
\rep_{\proj}(K_{r},1)
is closed under extensions, we conclude
\mathcal{E}(\{E_{X}\})\subseteq\rep_{\proj}(K_{r},1)
. Since
E_{X}
is regular, Kac’s Theorem implies that
q_{r}(\dimu E_{X})<0
and therefore

\dim_{\mathbbm{k}}\Ext^{1}_{K_{r}}(E_{X},E_{X})=-q_{r}(\dimu E_{X})+1\geq 2.

Now the Remark following
[
KL91
, (1.4)]
implies that
\mathcal{D}\coloneqq\mathcal{E}(\{E_{X}\})
is a wild subcategory of
\rep_{\proj}(K_{r},1)
. Let
0\neq M\in\mathcal{E}(\{E_{X}\})\subseteq\rep_{\proj}(K_{r},1)
. By definition, we find
n\in\mathbb{N}
and a filtration

0=M_{0}\subsetneq M_{1}\subsetneq M_{2}\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq M_{n-1}\subsetneq M_{n}=M

such that
M_{i}/M_{i-1}\cong E_{X}
for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. By
6.5.6
, we have
\mathcal{V}({K_{r}},2)_{E_{X}}=X
and conclude with
[
BF24
, (2.1.2)]
that
\mathcal{V}({K_{r}},2)_{M}=X
. Now
Corollary

4.4.3
implies that
\{\mathfrak{u}\in\Gr_{2}(A_{r})\mid\Hom_{K_{r}}(P_{1}^{-}(\mathfrak{u}),M)\neq 0\}=\mathcal{V}(K_{r},2)_{M}=X
is a non-empty finite set and it follows

\mathcal{J}_{\TilTheta(M)}=\mathcal{J}_{M}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\ref{6.5.2}}}{{=}}\{\mathfrak{u}\in\Gr_{2}(A_{r})\mid\Hom_{K_{r}}(P_{1}^{-}(\mathfrak{u}),M)\neq 0\}=X.

∎

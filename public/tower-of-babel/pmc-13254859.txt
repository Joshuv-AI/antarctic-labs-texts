Large-scale circulation shapes the spatial organization of anomalous precipitation over Northern Jiangsu
Authors: Ying Xu, Hongyuan Fang, Wenpeng Zhao, Jian Zhang, Shuping Ma, Chuanqiao Zhou, Mingrui Shi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
SummaryAnomalous precipitation events often exhibit coherent spatial organization rather than isolated intensity extremes, reshaping regional flood risk in subtropical monsoon zones. Using 1-km daily precipitation data from 1961 to 2019, we developed an unsupervised framework combining a convolutional autoencoder and a self-organizing map to identify event-scale anomalous precipitation patterns over Northern Jiangsu. A total of 543 anomalous days were classified into five distinct spatial types characterized by pronounced north-south contrasts and east-west differentiation. Interaction analysis reveals suppressive and co-occurring relationships among patterns, indicating structured atmospheric control. Phase variations of the Western North Pacific Subtropical High and the East Asian jet significantly modulate the probability of different patterns. Incorporating circulation-conditioned pattern probabilities into runoff forecasting improves peak discharge simulation. These findings highlight the importance of circulation-structured precipitation in linking atmospheric variability with hydrological response under nonstationary climate conditions.Subject areas: earth sciences, atmospheric science, meteorology

Introduction

Precipitation extremes have intensified over recent decades and are widely recognized as one of the most prominent manifestations of ongoing climate change.1,2,3 The increasing frequency and magnitude of such extremes have amplified hydroclimatic risks, posing serious challenges to societal sustainability. Recent high-impact flood disasters across Europe and East Asia further underscore the growing vulnerability of human systems to precipitation extremes.4,5

The East Asian monsoon region is among the most climate-sensitive areas globally, where precipitation has intensified and undergone marked spatiotemporal reorganization.6,7 This intensification has manifested in several high-impact regional events. During the 2020 Meiyu season, for example, the Huaihe River Basin experienced its most severe flooding since 1954, with multiple stations in Jiangsu recording daily precipitation exceeding historical records.8,9,10 These events underscore the importance of clarifying the spatiotemporal organization and dynamical drivers of precipitation extremes in monsoon regions.

Northern Jiangsu lies within this monsoon regime at the climatic boundary between warm temperate and subtropical zones (Figure 1). The region exhibits pronounced precipitation variability and an increasing occurrence of precipitation extremes,11 shaped by the combined influence of the East Asian summer monsoon, the Western Pacific Subtropical High (WNPSH), and the Meiyu frontal system. As a key section of the Eastern Route of the South-to-North Water Diversion Project, it plays a crucial role in inter-basin water transfer and flood regulation.12 Variability in precipitation can disrupt water management operations and increase environmental pressures on the system.13,14,15,16 Improving mechanistic understanding in this subtropical monsoon setting is therefore critical for enhancing flood resilience and supporting sustainable water management.17,18

Precipitation extremes usually arise from the combined effects of moisture transport and dynamic lifting, processes regulated by multiscale atmospheric circulation systems in the East Asian monsoon region.19,20 Among these systems, the WNPSH plays a dominant role by controlling low-level moisture pathways and the positioning of the rain belt. Tropical cyclones, the East Asian summer monsoon, and low-level jets can further enhance moisture convergence and upward motion, intensifying rainfall intensity and spatial extent.21,22 Under a warming climate, the WNPSH has further exhibited a tendency toward strengthening and westward extension,23 altering the background circulation environment over eastern China. Such shifts modulate both the intensity and spatial configuration of rainfall systems. The record-breaking Meiyu event over the Yangtze River Basin in June–July 2020 was closely associated with an anomalously intensified and westward-extended WNPSH that reinforced large-scale moisture convergence.24 Meanwhile, interactions between cold and warm air masses, influenced by mid- to high-latitude blocking systems and the East Asian subtropical jet, further regulate vertical ascent and precipitation organization.25,26

Importantly, these circulation components interact nonlinearly. Precipitation extremes are therefore not determined solely by moisture availability or isolated dynamical triggers but by the spatial coordination and temporal evolution of multiple circulation elements.27 This nonlinear coupling suggests that extreme events may exhibit structured spatial organization under specific circulation phases rather than occurring as random intensity fluctuations.

Accurate identification of precipitation extremes is essential for disaster preparedness and water resource management. Conventional statistical approaches, including percentile-based extreme indices, standardized precipitation metrics, and extreme value theory, primarily quantify intensity departures from climatology.28,29,30,31,32 While effective for characterizing magnitude extremes, these methods focus predominantly on intensity thresholds and are less capable of capturing spatial coherence, displacement, and concentration patterns that frequently accompany high-impact events.33,34,35 Their reliance on distributional assumptions may also limit robustness under nonlinear and nonstationary climate conditions.36,37 Importantly, extreme precipitation cannot be defined solely by intensity magnitude. Many high-impact events are characterized by distinctive spatial organization,4,5 such as coherent clustering, rain-belt displacement, or atypical spatial concentration. To incorporate both intensity and structural dimensions, we use the term anomalous precipitation to describe events that exhibit not only intensity departures but also non-typical spatial organization.

Recent advances in machine learning provide alternative pathways for identifying complex spatial patterns in high-dimensional precipitation fields. Supervised deep-learning architectures, including convolutional neural networks and long short-term memory models, have demonstrated strong predictive capabilities,38,39 but typically require large labeled datasets. In contrast, unsupervised models such as autoencoders can detect anomalous structures directly from data without predefined thresholds.40 Self-organizing maps further offer a topology-preserving framework that enables intuitive clustering of atmospheric patterns.41,42

Despite these developments, most studies still focus on precipitation intensity rather than the spatial organization of individual events.33,43,44,45,46,47 The relationship between organized precipitation patterns and specific circulation phases remains insufficiently quantified. This gap limits our ability to translate atmospheric diagnostics into hydrologically relevant information.

In this study, we focus on Northern Jiangsu as a representative subtropical monsoon region. We identify event-scale anomalous precipitation patterns, examine their spatiotemporal evolution, and quantify their linkage with large-scale circulation configurations. By integrating spatial structure and dynamical drivers, this work provides new insights into precipitation organization in subtropical monsoon climates and offers hydrologically relevant guidance for risk-informed water management.

Spatial characteristics of anomalous precipitation patterns

A total of 543 anomalous precipitation days were identified over Northern Jiangsu during 1961–2019. Based on SOM classification, these events were grouped into five spatially distinct patterns: Northwest, Central-West, North, South, and Central-South types (Figure 3).

Five spatial patterns of anomalous precipitation identified over Northern Jiangsu and the climatological mean daily precipitation (1961–2019)

Each pattern is characterized by a well-defined anomaly center, indicating that anomalous precipitation tends to organize into coherent regional structures. The Central-West type is the most frequent, accounting for 27.6% (150 days) of all events. Its anomaly core extends from southern Suqian through Huaian to western Yancheng. The Northwest type represents 22.5% (122 days), with anomalies concentrated over Xuzhou and northern Suqian. The South (19.5%, 106 days) and North (19.0%, 103 days) types occur at comparable frequencies but display contrasting meridional structures. The Central-South type is least frequent (11.4%, 62 days), with anomaly centers primarily located around Yangzhou and Taizhou.

Notably, several documented high-impact rainfall events correspond closely to the identified spatial patterns. The July 2018 extreme rainfall event over central Northern Jiangsu, during which the 24-h maximum exceeded 300 mm,48 exhibits a spatial distribution consistent with the Central-West pattern. Similarly, the June–July 2016 heavy rainfall episode over the lower Yangtze–Huai region aligns with the Central-South type.49 These consistencies indicate that the classification captures spatially coherent structures associated with major precipitation events.

Temporal characteristics of anomalous precipitation patterns

The five anomalous precipitation patterns display distinct interannual variability over 1961–2019 (Figure 4A). Among them, only the South type exhibits a statistically significant upward trend (p = 0.01), while the other patterns show no significant long-term changes.

Figure 4B further reveals clear interaction relationships among the five patterns. The strongest suppression occurs between the Northwest and South types, indicating that these two patterns rarely occur within the same year. In contrast, the Central-West and Central-South types tend to co-occur more frequently, though not statistically significant, suggesting partially shared large-scale moisture transport conditions. In particular, a significant positive association is identified between the Central-South and South types (ratio = 5.97, p < 0.05), indicating that Central-South events increase in frequency during years when the South type is active. More broadly, the South type shows consistent suppressive relationships with most other patterns.

Seasonal distributions provide additional insight into the temporal organization of the five patterns (Figure 4C). The Central-West type spans the longest active period, primarily occurring during July–August. The North type is active from late June to mid-August, with occasional occurrences in early autumn. The Central-South type is concentrated in early July and remains relatively infrequent, whereas the Northwest type is mainly observed between late June and late August. By comparison, the South type exhibits a broader seasonal window, with one peak in mid-July and another from late September to October.

Circulation background of anomalous precipitation patterns

The distinct spatial patterns of anomalous precipitation identified above suggest that rainfall events are not randomly distributed but are regulated by different large-scale circulation configurations. To clarify the underlying dynamical controls, composite analyses were performed for 500 hPa geopotential height (GPH), 850 hPa winds (u850–v850), vertically integrated moisture divergence (VIMD), vertically integrated water vapor flux components (viwve–viwvn), sea surface temperature (SST), outgoing longwave radiation (OLR), and 200 hPa zonal wind (200 hPa u200) (Figure 5; Figure S1).

The composite results indicate that all five anomalous precipitation patterns are closely linked to variations in WNPSH configuration, although its position and intensity differ among types. During Northwest-type events, the WNPSH shifts northward, accompanied by an anomalous anticyclonic circulation south of Japan (Figure 5A). This configuration enhances moisture transport from the western Pacific toward the study region. Southerly flow converges with southward intrusions of higher-latitude air over the northwestern sector, forming a low-level convergence zone (Figure S1A). The Central-West type corresponds to a westward-extending and southward-shifted WNPSH (Figure 5B). Compared with the Northwest type, the associated anticyclonic anomaly is located farther south and east over the northwestern Pacific. This configuration enhances southwesterly and southeasterly moisture transport toward the study region, leading to strengthened low-level moisture convergence (Figure S1B). During the North type, the WNPSH shifts eastward over the northwestern Pacific, while the associated GPH anomalies remain positive but relatively weak (Figure 5C). The South type is similar to the Central-West type but exhibits stronger positive GPH anomalies (Figure 5D). The associated moisture convergence is concentrated in the southern portion of the study region (Figure S1D). For the Central-South type, the WNPSH is positioned farther south and more westward, with stronger positive GPH anomalies than the other types, approaching the South China coast (Figure 5E). The accompanying anticyclonic anomaly channels warm and moist air toward the Yangtze River corridor, producing low-level convergence over the southern sector of Northern Jiangsu (Figure S1E).

Composite air-sea anomalies further reveal differences in convective background among the precipitation patterns. As shown in Figures 5F–5J, positive SST anomalies are generally observed within the WNPSH-controlled region for most types, except the North and Northwest types. The magnitude and spatial extent of these SST anomalies vary across patterns, consistent with differences in WNPSH configuration. To characterize deep convection, OLR anomalies are examined. Positive OLR anomalies are evident within the WNPSH-controlled region, indicating suppressed convection under prevailing subsidence. In contrast, negative OLR anomalies are mainly located to the northwest or north of the subtropical high, coinciding with areas of enhanced moisture convergence and upward motion.

Further analysis of u200 reveals distinct East Asian jet (EAJ) anomaly structures among the precipitation patterns (Figures 5K–5O). For the Central-West type, positive zonal wind anomalies appear north of the climatological jet axis and negative anomalies to the south, indicating a northward displacement of the EAJ core. The study region is located near the right-side sector of the shifted jet and overlaps with low-level convergence (Figure S1). The North, South, and Central-South types exhibit a similar structure, but the latitude of the positive anomaly center differs. The North type shows the northernmost EAJ position, followed by the Central-West type, whereas the South and Central-South types are located farther south. In contrast, the Northwest type displays relatively weak meridional displacement of the EAJ, with weaker upper-level wind anomalies.

Quantitative influence of WNPSH and EAJ

Clear differences in occurrence probability are observed under different WNPSH and EAJ phases. Based on historical observations (Figure 6), the mean occurrence probabilities are 1.12% for the Northwest type, 1.38% for the Central-West type, 0.95% for the North type, 0.98% for the South type, and 0.57% for the Central-South type. Under the positive WNPSH phase (westward extension), most precipitation patterns show increased occurrence probabilities. The increases are most pronounced for the Central-West, South, and Central-South types, reaching 15.8%, 18.9%, and 33.6%, respectively. In contrast, during the negative WNPSH phase (eastward retreat), the North type exhibits a 12.1% increase, whereas the other types generally decrease. These phase-dependent differences align with the circulation configurations shown in Figure 5. Westward extension of the WNPSH corresponds to enhanced moisture transport toward the southern part of the study region, favoring southern precipitation patterns, whereas eastward retreat is associated with a higher frequency of northern anomalies. EAJ phase variations are also associated with noticeable changes in occurrence probability. During the positive EAJ phase (southward displacement), the South and Central-South types increase to 1.01% and 0.67%, corresponding to relative increases of 3.0% and 16.5%, respectively. In contrast, under the negative phase (northward displacement), the Northwest and North types rise to 1.64% and 1.45%, representing increases of 46.3% and 52.9%.

Implications for flood early warning and risk reduction

Anomalous precipitation in Northern Jiangsu exhibits clear circulation-dependent spatial organization rather than behaving as isolated intensity extremes. Distinct precipitation patterns correspond systematically to different WNPSH and EAJ phases, indicating that large-scale atmospheric variability shapes not only rainfall magnitude but also its spatial concentration. This circulation conditioning provides a clearer physical basis for understanding flood formation in subtropical monsoon regions.

The forecasting experiment further demonstrates the practical value of this perspective (Figure 7). Incorporating circulation-informed precipitation pattern probabilities into the runoff model reduced root-mean-square error (RMSE) by 11.79%, with markedly larger improvements during high-flow events. In several extreme runoff cases, forecast errors decreased by more than 30%. These gains indicate that circulation-based pattern information contributes predictive skill, particularly when rainfall is dynamically organized at the regional scale. This finding is consistent with previous work showing that large-scale climate information can enhance hydrological forecast performance.50

Runoff forecasting framework incorporating anomalous precipitation pattern information

The results also show that flood risk is redistributed under different circulation regimes (Figures 4 and 6). Westward extension or southward displacement of the WNPSH increases the likelihood of southern precipitation patterns, whereas retreating or northward-shifted circulation states favor northern anomalies. Because certain precipitation types suppress others (Figure 4B), dominance of a given atmospheric phase effectively shifts hydrological stress within the region rather than uniformly intensifying rainfall. Flood exposure therefore evolves with circulation state.

Large-scale circulation also varies more gradually than local convective rainfall, making WNPSH and EAJ phases useful early indicators of spatial rainfall transitions. Integrating precipitation pattern diagnostics into operational monitoring may therefore improve anticipatory flood management by signaling where rainfall concentration is more likely to occur.

This mechanism-based perspective is particularly relevant for the Eastern Route of the South-to-North Water Diversion Project, where water transfer efficiency and flood regulation depend on regional rainfall distribution. Circulation-conditioned precipitation patterns imply that hydrological stress may alternately concentrate in southern or northern sections of the system under different atmospheric states. Recognizing these regime-dependent shifts can support more flexible coordination between flood control and water diversion during monsoon seasons.

This study investigated anomalous precipitation over Northern Jiangsu using a combined convolutional autoencoder and self-organizing map framework applied to high-resolution daily rainfall data (1961–2019). The results reveal that regional anomalous precipitation is characterized by structured spatial organization under specific large-scale circulation regimes rather than occurring as isolated intensity extremes. This circulation-conditioned structure also provides physically meaningful information for improving runoff forecasting and regional water management. The main findings are summarized as follows.

Overall, the findings demonstrate that anomalous precipitation in this subtropical monsoon region is structured by large-scale circulation in both space and probability. Linking spatial rainfall organization with circulation phases provides a physically grounded basis for incorporating atmospheric diagnostics into flood early warning and regional water management. More broadly, recognizing precipitation as a circulation-conditioned spatial process helps bridge atmospheric variability and hydrological response under nonstationary climate conditions. Although developed for Northern Jiangsu, this analytical framework may be adaptable to other monsoon-influenced basins where rainfall organization is strongly shaped by large-scale circulation variability. Future work could extend this approach using climate model projections to examine how circulation changes may reorganize precipitation patterns under warming.

Limitations of the study

Two limitations should be noted. First, the precipitation data used in this study are derived from gridded reanalysis (ERA5) and interpolated observational datasets, which may underrepresent localized convective events and fine-scale spatial heterogeneity, particularly in areas with sparse station coverage along the northern Jiangsu coast. The inherent smoothing in gridded products could lead to an underestimation of extreme precipitation intensity, which may affect the characterization of anomalous precipitation patterns identified through the autoencoder-SOM framework. Second, the associations identified between large-scale circulation indices (WNPSH and EAJ) and anomalous precipitation patterns are statistical in nature. Although the Bayesian inference framework quantifies these linkages probabilistically, establishing physical causality remains challenging, because mesoscale processes, local land-sea interactions, and soil moisture feedbacks are not explicitly resolved in the current analysis and may substantially modulate event-level precipitation responses. Future studies integrating convection-permitting regional climate simulations would help disentangle these multiscale interactions and further validate the findings under projected warming scenarios.

Method details

Study area

Northern Jiangsu is located in a subtropical monsoon region influenced by the East Asian summer monsoon and is characterized by pronounced climate variability and frequent precipitation anomalies (Figure 1). The study area lies along the eastern coast of China (32°34′–34°28′N, 116°22′–121°18′E), covering approximately 75,500 km2. The region is dominated by low-lying plains, with most elevations below 50 m above sea level. Under the influence of the East Asian monsoon, precipitation exhibits strong seasonal concentration, with the majority of annual rainfall occurring between June and August. Meteorological variables show marked spatiotemporal variability, particularly during the summer rainy season.47 In addition, Northern Jiangsu constitutes a core conveyance corridor of the Eastern Route of the South-to-North Water Diversion Project. The interaction between natural hydroclimatic processes and large-scale water management activities adds complexity to the regional hydroclimate system, making it a representative setting for investigating the spatial organization of anomalous precipitation and its dynamical drivers.

HRLT precipitation dataset

Daily precipitation data were obtained from the High-Resolution and Long-Term Dataset (HRLT) for China.51 The dataset covers mainland China for the period 1961–2019 with a spatial resolution of 1 km, enabling detailed representation of regional precipitation variability and spatial heterogeneity.

HRLT is constructed based on gridded daily observations from the China Meteorological Administration and incorporates multiple geographic covariates through machine-learning and statistical interpolation techniques. Previous evaluations have demonstrated its reliability in representing precipitation variability and spatial patterns, and it has been widely used in climate change and hydrological studies.33,52,53,54

ERA5 reanalysis data

Large-scale atmospheric circulation variables were derived from the fifth-generation global reanalysis dataset ERA5 produced by the European Center for Medium-Range Weather Forecasts (ECMWF).55 ERA5 provides hourly data at a spatial resolution of approximately 0.25 ° × 0.25 ° and maintains dynamical consistency suitable for circulation diagnostics. These variables were used to diagnose moisture transport pathways and circulation anomalies associated with anomalous precipitation events.

Following previous work (e.g.,54), all variables were first converted to anomaly fields by subtracting the climatological mean. For precipitation, anomalies were calculated relative to the daily climatology during the rainy season (May–October). A 5-day moving average was then applied to reduce high-frequency variability and emphasize subseasonal-to-seasonal signals. For circulation variables, anomaly fields were processed in the same manner, with a 5-day moving average applied, and interannual variability removed by subtracting the seasonal mean of each year.

Autoencoder for anomalous precipitation detection

To identify spatial structures of precipitation fields, a convolutional autoencoder was developed to perform unsupervised learning on daily precipitation anomaly fields, following previous work.56 The autoencoder was trained using daily samples from May to October, corresponding to the main rainy season in the study region. This restriction reduces the influence of strong seasonal contrasts and ensures that the model captures variations in spatial organization within the rainy season rather than broad seasonal differences. The autoencoder learns latent spatial representations by minimizing reconstruction error, allowing detection of deviations from the dominant climatological structure.40

The input data consist of standardized daily precipitation anomaly fields with spatial dimensions of 422 × 675 (Figure 2). During encoding, the input field passes through convolutional layers and is progressively compressed into a 64-dimensional latent feature vector. During decoding, the latent representation is reconstructed back to the original spatial resolution. Reconstruction error reflects the degree to which a given precipitation field deviates from the learned spatial structure.

The model was implemented using TensorFlow. The encoder consists of convolutional layers with 32, 64, and 128 filters, using a kernel size of 3 × 3, a stride of 2, and “same” padding. Each layer is followed by batch normalization and a LeakyReLU activation function (α = 0.2). The decoder mirrors the encoder structure, and a sigmoid activation function is applied at the output layer. Model parameters are initialized using the Glorot uniform scheme. Historical daily samples were randomly divided into training (80%) and validation (20%) subsets. Model parameters were optimized using the Adam optimizer with a learning rate of 10−3, a batch size of 10, and a maximum of 200 epochs. Early stopping based on validation loss (patience = 10) and learning rate reduction on plateau were applied to reduce overfitting. Reconstruction error was quantified using mean squared error (MSE). Days with reconstruction error exceeding the 95th percentile of the error distribution were classified as anomalous precipitation events. The reconstruction error quantifies the deviation between the observed precipitation field and its reconstructed counterpart, capturing discrepancies in spatial organization and overall magnitude. Events with high MSE therefore represent precipitation patterns that deviate substantially from the dominant climatological structures learned by the model.56

Self-organizing map classification

To extract dominant spatial structure modes of the identified anomalous events, a self-organizing map (SOM) was applied. The SOM preserves topological relationships while projecting high-dimensional anomaly fields onto a lower-dimensional representation.57 Daily precipitation anomaly fields classified as anomalous by the autoencoder were used as SOM input samples. The clustering process groups events with similar spatial structures into the same category, thereby identifying recurrent spatial organization patterns.

To determine the appropriate SOM size, clustering performance under different node configurations was evaluated using the Elbow method.58 A 5 × 1 SOM configuration was selected to balance pattern separability and interpretability. The resulting five clusters represent the primary anomalous precipitation patterns used in subsequent circulation analysis.

Quantification of WNPSH and EAJ modulation

To quantify the influence of large-scale circulation on anomalous precipitation patterns, indices representing the Western North Pacific Subtropical High (WNPSH) and the East Asian jet (EAJ) were constructed. The WNPSH index was defined as the area-averaged 500 hPa geopotential height anomaly over 10°–30°N and 110°–150°E.59 Positive values indicate westward extension, and negative values indicate eastward retreat.The EAJ index was defined as the difference in 200 hPa zonal wind anomalies between 30°–35°N and 45°–50°N within 110°–140°E, representing meridional displacement of the jet core.60 All variables were detrended relative to the climatological mean and smoothed to emphasize subseasonal-to-seasonal variability.

Quantification and statistical analysis

Quantification and statistical analyses were conducted to evaluate the relationships between circulation patterns and anomalous precipitation, as well as their hydrological implications.

The occurrence probability of each precipitation pattern under different circulation phases was estimated using a Bayesian framework based on the Beta distribution, allowing probabilistic characterization of pattern modulation by large-scale circulation.

Interactions among precipitation patterns were quantified using annual relative frequency and interaction ratios, which measure the tendency for co-occurrence or suppression between different pattern types.

Statistical significance of differences in pattern occurrence was assessed using a two-sample t test at the annual scale.

For the runoff forecasting application, model performance was evaluated using the root-mean-square error (RMSE). The reported improvement reflects the relative reduction in RMSE achieved by incorporating circulation-informed Bayesian post-processing compared to the baseline LSTM simulation.

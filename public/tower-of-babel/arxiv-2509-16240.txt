Off-Critical Zeros Contradict Contraction in the Dynamical Reformulation of the Riemann Hypothesis
Hendrik W. A. E. Kuipers

Abstract
We continue the dynamical reformulation of the Riemann Hypothesis initiated in [ 1 ] . The framework is built from an integer map in which composites advance by π ⁡ ( m ) \pi(m) and primes retreat by their prime gap, producing trajectories whose contraction properties encode the distribution of primes. In this setting, RH is equivalent to the persistence of contraction inequalities for trajectory-based error functionals E ​ ( X ) , E ~ ​ ( X ) E(X),\widetilde{E}(X) across multiplicative scales. In the present paper we prove that if ζ ⁡ ( s ) \zeta(s) has a zero off the critical line ℜ ⁡ ( s ) = 1 2 \Re(s)=\tfrac{1}{2} , then the Landau–Littlewood Ω \Omega -theorem forces oscillations of size x β x^{\beta} in ψ ⁡ ( x ) − x \psi(x)-x . A window-capture lemma shows that these oscillations are inherited by the composite-only window suprema ℰ ⁡ ( X ) \mathcal{E}(X) , and hence by E ⁡ ( X ) E(X) , producing lower bounds that contradict contraction. Thus any off–critical zero is incompatible with contraction. Headline result. Part I introduced the dynamical system and showed that RH is equivalent to the persistence of contraction inequalities. Part II proves that off–critical zeros force oscillations in ψ ⁡ ( x ) − x \psi(x)-x that inevitably violate contraction. Taken together, these two steps close the circle: contraction characterizes RH, and off–critical zeros contradict contraction. Hence every nontrivial zero of ζ ⁡ ( s ) \zeta(s) lies on the critical line. More generally, the present result shows that whenever contraction holds, the critical line is forced as the only location of nontrivial zeros. In this sense, the critical line is not merely the conjectured locus of zeros, but the equilibrium point singled out by contraction itself.

1
Introduction

The Riemann Hypothesis (RH) asserts that all nontrivial zeros of the Riemann zeta function
\zeta(s)
lie on the critical line
\Re(s)=\tfrac{1}{2}
.

In a recent work
[
1
]
, we introduced a
dynamical reformulation
of RH based on a simple integer map: composites advance by
\pi(m)
, the number of primes
\leq m
, while primes step backward by their preceding prime gap. This system generates trajectories whose large-scale contraction properties are equivalent to RH. In particular, we established:

•

one-visit and parent-window lemmas restricting local composite hits,

•

macro-step alignment and core overlap across scales,

•

a frequency-netting lemma ensuring oscillatory coherence,

•

and from these, contraction inequalities for the normalized error functionals
E(X),\widetilde{E}(X)
.

Iterating these inequalities yields the von Koch–level bound, known to be equivalent to RH
[
3
]
. Thus RH holds if and only if contraction persists uniformly across scales.

The aim of the present paper is to close this circle.
We prove that if any nontrivial zero of
\zeta(s)
lies off the critical line, then the
contraction inequalities are violated. The argument relies on classical
\Omega
-results of
Landau
[
4
]
and Littlewood
[
5
]
, which show that an off-critical
zero forces oscillations of size
x^{\beta}
in the Chebyshev error
\psi(x)-x
.
We show that such oscillations are inherited by the composite-only window suprema
\mathcal{E}(X)
,
contradicting contraction.

In contrast to Part I, which worked with
E_{\pi}(x):=\pi(x)-\mathrm{Li}(x)
, the present
paper is formulated in terms of
E_{\psi}(x):=\psi(x)-x
, which is more natural for the analytic
arguments below (see Section
2
and Appendix A).

On relation to Part I.

This is Part II of a two–paper project. Part I
[
1
]
introduced the dynamical system
and established that RH is equivalent to the persistence of contraction inequalities for

E(X)
. Part II shows that any off–critical zero forces oscillations that inevitably
violate contraction. Taken together, the two parts close the circle: contraction characterizes
RH, and off–critical zeros contradict contraction. Hence all nontrivial zeros of
\zeta(s)
lie
on the critical line.

Background and Setup

For
X\geq 2
, define the one-visit and parent windows

W_{X}\;=\;\bigl[X,(1+0.1/\log X)X\bigr],\qquad\widetilde{W}_{X}\;=\;\bigl[X,(1+2/\log X)X\bigr].

Let

denote the set of composite positive integers, and write

E_{\psi}(y)\;:=\;\psi(y)-y.

Error functionals.

For
X\geq X_{0}
, define

E(X)\;:=\;\sup_{\,y\in W_{X}\cap\mathbb{N}_{\mathrm{comp}}}\;|E_{\psi}(y)|,\qquad\widetilde{E}(X)\;:=\;\sup_{\,y\in\widetilde{W}_{X}\cap\mathbb{N}_{\mathrm{comp}}}\;\Biggl|\;\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\in\mathcal{T}(y;\,\widetilde{W}_{X})\\
m\in\mathbb{N}_{\mathrm{comp}}\end{subarray}}E_{\psi}(m)\;\Biggr|.

Remark

(Bridge to Part I)
.

Switching from

E_{\psi}(x):=\psi(x)-x

integer-map setting to the Chebyshev function.

Lemma 2.1

(Parent functional dominates window supremum)
.

For all sufficiently large
X
, we have

E(X)\;\leq\;\widetilde{E}(X).

Proof.

Let
y^{\ast}\in W_{X}\cap\mathbb{N}_{\mathrm{comp}}
attain
E(X)
. The trajectory started at
y^{\ast}
passes through
y^{\ast}
while inside
\widetilde{W}_{X}
, so the parent-window sum for this trajectory includes the term
|E_{\psi}(y^{\ast})|=E(X)
, and hence
\widetilde{E}(X)\geq E(X)

∎

Proof (sketch).

Let

Contribution of an Off-Critical Zero

Assume
\zeta(\rho)=0
with
\rho=\beta+i\gamma
and
\beta>\tfrac{1}{2}
.

4.1
Landau–Littlewood lower bound

If
\beta>1/2
, there exist
c_{\rho}>0
and
x_{n}\to\infty
with

|\psi(x_{n})-x_{n}|\;\geq\;c_{\rho}\,x_{n}^{\beta};

see Titchmarsh
[
6
, §14.25]
and Edwards
[
13
, Ch. 8]
.

4.2
Window capture

If
x=(1+\delta)X
with
0<\delta\leq 0.1/\log X
, then
x\in W_{X}
. Since
\log x\asymp\log X
on
W_{X}
, we have

\Big(\frac{x}{X}\Big)^{\!\beta}\;=\;1+O\!\Big(\frac{1}{\log X}\Big).

4.3
Lower bound for the window/trajectory functionals

Lemma 4.1

(Composite selection lemma)
.

Let
X
be large and
W_{X}=[X,(1+0.1/\log X)X]
. Suppose
x\in W_{X}
satisfies

|\psi(x)-x|\geq M
. Then there exists a composite
y\in W_{X}
with
|y-x|\leq 1
such that

|\psi(y)-y|\ \geq\ M-(\log(2X)+1).

In particular, if
M\geq 2(\log(2X)+1)
then
|\psi(y)-y|\geq\tfrac{1}{2}M
.

Proof.

Pick
n\in\{\lfloor x\rfloor,\ \lceil x\rceil\}
so that
|n-x|\leq 1
. Among
\{n,n\pm 1\}
there is an even integer
y\geq 4
, hence composite, with
|y-x|\leq 1
. Because
W_{X}
has length
\asymp X/\log X\gg 1
, we still have
y\in W_{X}
.

Between integers,
\psi
is constant and
t\mapsto\psi(t)-t
has slope
-1
. At an integer
m
,

\psi
jumps by
\Lambda(m)\leq\log m\leq\log(2X)
on
[X,2X]
. Thus for
u,v\in[X,2X]
,

|\psi(u)-u-(\psi(v)-v)|\leq|u-v|+\sum_{m\in(u,v]}\Lambda(m)\leq|u-v|+\log(2X)\cdot\#\{m\in(u,v]\}.

With
|u-v|\leq 1
this is
\leq\log(2X)+1
. Taking
u=x
,
v=y
gives the claim, i.e.

|\psi(y)-y|\ \geq\ |\psi(x)-x|\;-\;\log(2X)\;-\;1\qquad(|y-x|\leq 1,\ y\in\mathbb{C}\cap W_{X}).

∎

Proposition 4.2

(Lower bound for
E(X)
and
\widetilde{E}(X)
)
.

Assume
\zeta(\rho)=0
with
\rho=\beta+i\gamma
and
\beta>\tfrac{1}{2}
. Then there exist
X_{n}\to\infty
and
c>0
such that

E(X_{n})\ \geq\ c\,X_{n}^{\beta}\qquad\text{and hence}\qquad\widetilde{E}(X_{n})\ \geq\ c\,X_{n}^{\beta}.

Proof.

Let
x_{n}
be given by the Landau–Littlewood bound. Set
X_{n}:=x_{n}/(1+0.1/\log x_{n})
so
x_{n}\in W_{X_{n}}
and
\log x_{n}\asymp\log X_{n}
. Apply Lemma
4.1
with
M=c_{\rho}x_{n}^{\beta}
to obtain a composite
y_{n}\in W_{X_{n}}
,
|y_{n}-x_{n}|\leq 1
, with

|\psi(y_{n})-y_{n}|\ \geq\ c_{\rho}x_{n}^{\beta}-(\log(2X_{n})+1).

Since
x_{n}^{\beta}\gg\log x_{n}
for
\beta>1/2
, the subtraction is
\leq\tfrac{1}{2}c_{\rho}x_{n}^{\beta}
for large
n
, giving
|\psi(y_{n})-y_{n}|\geq\tfrac{1}{2}c_{\rho}x_{n}^{\beta}\asymp X_{n}^{\beta}
.
Hence
E(X_{n})\geq|\psi(y_{n})-y_{n}|\gg X_{n}^{\beta}
. Lemma
2.1
then gives
\widetilde{E}(X_{n})\geq E(X_{n})
.
∎

5
Contradiction and Conclusion

From Corollary
3.2
, the contraction framework of
[
1
]
yields

E(X)\ \ll\ X^{1/2}\log X,\qquad(X\to\infty).

(1)

Since
\widetilde{E}(X)\geq E(X)
by Lemma
2.1
, any lower bound for
E(X)

transfers immediately to
\widetilde{E}(X)
.

On the other hand, if
\zeta(s)
has a zero
\rho=\beta+i\gamma
with
\beta>1/2
, then
by §
4.3
we obtain a subsequence
X_{n}\to\infty
such that

E(X_{n})\ \gg\ X_{n}^{\beta}.

(2)

Comparing (
1
) and (
2
), the ratio

\frac{X^{\beta}}{X^{1/2}\log X}\ =\ \frac{X^{\beta-1/2}}{\log X}

tends to infinity as
X\to\infty
for every
\beta>1/2
. Thus the lower bound (
2
)
eventually dominates the contraction bound (
1
), giving a contradiction.

We use the smoothed explicit formula with kernel

W(t)=(1+t^{2})^{-3}
and truncation height
T=\tfrac{1}{2}(\log X)^{3}
; the remainder is
bounded uniformly by
10X^{1/2}
for
X\geq e^{120}
.
The derivation of this bound with explicit constants is given in Appendix E, while Appendix D
contains the large–sieve budget ledger used in the contraction engine.

Theorem 5.1

(Riemann Hypothesis, assuming
[
1
]
)
.

Assume the contraction inequality established in
[
1
]
.
Then every nontrivial zero of
\zeta(s)
lies on the critical line
\Re(s)=\tfrac{1}{2}
.

Proof.

If
\zeta(\rho)=0
with
\rho=\beta+i\gamma
and
\beta>1/2
, then §
4.3
gives a
subsequence
X_{n}\to\infty
with
E(X_{n})\gg X_{n}^{\beta}
. This contradicts the contraction bound

E(X)\ll X^{1/2}\log X
from
[
1
]
. Hence no off–critical zeros exist, and every
nontrivial zero lies on the critical line.
∎

6
Conclusion and Outlook

Assuming the contraction inequality established in
[
1
]
, the contradiction proved
in Section
5
rules out all off–critical zeros. Thus, off–critical
zeros are incompatible with contraction; taken together with
[
1
]
, this closes the
circle and yields the Riemann Hypothesis.

The key feature is a rigidity principle: contraction across multiplicative scales cannot
coexist with the oscillations of size
x^{\beta}
in
\psi(x)-x
that an off–critical zero
would force. This incompatibility is analytic in nature, independent of any particular
realization of contraction. In the dynamical reformulation of
[
1
]
, contraction
emerges from the integer map and the critical line appears as its unique equilibrium.
More generally, the present result shows that whenever contraction holds, the critical
line is forced as the only location of nontrivial zeros. In this sense, the critical line is not merely the conjectured locus of zeros,
but the equilibrium point singled out by contraction itself.

Further directions.

•

Operator–theoretic formulation.

Recast contraction as a spectral property of an associated transfer operator, potentially
linking the dynamical reformulation to the Nyman–Beurling approach.

•

Sharper error budgets.

Optimizing the remainder term in the smoothed explicit formula could enlarge the safety
margin in the contraction budget and clarify the robustness of the method.

•

Numerical investigations.

Simulations of contraction phenomena at finite scales may illuminate how oscillations
manifest and inspire further generalizations.

Appendix A. Bridging
\pi(x)-\mathrm{Li}(x)
and
\psi(x)-x

In Part I the contraction inequalities were formulated in terms of

E_{\pi}(x):=\pi(x)-\mathrm{Li}(x),\qquad E_{\psi}(x):=\psi(x)-x.

In this part we work with
E_{\psi}
to leverage sharper
\Omega
-results. The next lemmas show that switching between
E_{\pi}
and
E_{\psi}
is harmless at the von Koch scale
x^{1/2}\log x
.

Lemma A.1

(Partial-summation bridge from
\psi
to
\pi
)
.

For
x\geq 3
one has

\pi(x)-\mathrm{Li}(x)=\frac{\psi(x)-x}{\log x}\;+\;\int_{2}^{x}\frac{\psi(t)-t}{t\,(\log t)^{2}}\,dt\;-\;R_{\mathrm{pp}}(x),

(3)

where

R_{\mathrm{pp}}(x)=\sum_{k\geq 2}\left(\frac{\theta(x^{1/k})}{\log x}\;+\;\int_{2}^{x}\frac{\theta(t^{1/k})}{t\,(\log t)^{2}}\,dt\right),\qquad R_{\mathrm{pp}}(x)\ \ll\ x^{1/2}.

In particular,

\pi(x)-\mathrm{Li}(x)=\frac{\psi(x)-x}{\log x}\;+\;\int_{2}^{x}\frac{\psi(t)-t}{t\,(\log t)^{2}}\,dt\;+\;O\!\bigl(x^{1/2}\bigr).

(4)

Proof.

Partial summation yields

\pi(x)=\frac{\theta(x)}{\log x}+\int_{2}^{x}\frac{\theta(t)}{t(\log t)^{2}}\,dt,\qquad\mathrm{Li}(x)=\frac{x}{\log x}+\int_{2}^{x}\frac{dt}{(\log t)^{2}}.

Since
\theta=\psi-\sum_{k\geq 2}\theta(\,\cdot\,^{1/k})
, subtract to get (
3
). Using
\theta(y)\leq y\log y
and that the
k=2
term dominates shows
R_{\mathrm{pp}}(x)\ll x^{1/2}
.
∎

Lemma A.2

(Reverse bridge from
\pi
to
\psi
)
.

For
x\geq 3
one has

\psi(x)-x=\log x\,\bigl(\pi(x)-\mathrm{Li}(x)\bigr)\;-\;\int_{2}^{x}\frac{\pi(t)-\mathrm{Li}(t)}{t}\,dt\;+\;\widetilde{R}_{\mathrm{pp}}(x),

(5)

with
\widetilde{R}_{\mathrm{pp}}(x)=\sum_{k\geq 2}\theta(x^{1/k})-C_{0}
and
\ \widetilde{R}_{\mathrm{pp}}(x)\ll x^{1/2}
.

Proof.

Stieltjes integration by parts gives

\theta(x)=\pi(x)\log x-\int_{2}^{x}\frac{\pi(t)}{t}\,dt,\qquad x=\mathrm{Li}(x)\log x-\int_{2}^{x}\frac{\mathrm{Li}(t)}{t}\,dt+C_{0}.

Now write
\psi=\theta+\sum_{k\geq 2}\theta(\,\cdot\,^{1/k})
and subtract to get (
5
). The bound
\widetilde{R}_{\mathrm{pp}}(x)\ll x^{1/2}
again follows from
\theta(y)\leq y\log y
with the
k=2
term dominant.
∎

Remark

(Remainder sizes at the von Koch scale)
.

R_{pp}(x)\ \ll\ x^{1/2},\qquad\widetilde{R}_{pp}(x)\ \ll\ x^{1/2}.

These bounds are negligible against

and leave all equivalences below unchanged.

(Equivalence of von Koch–level bounds)
.

As

,

with absolute implied constants.

Proof.

Apply (
4
) and (
5
). If
E_{\psi}(x)\ll x^{1/2}\log x
, then the RHS of (
4
) is
\ll x^{1/2}\log x
. Conversely, if
E_{\pi}(x)\ll x^{1/2}\log x
, then the RHS of (
5
) is

since the integral term is softer by one logarithm and

∎

core contraction, macro–step distortion, and overlap terms. With
U=\log X\geq 120
,

\alpha_{\mathrm{eff}}\ \leq\ \underbrace{\tfrac{5}{6}}_{\text{core contraction}}\;\underbrace{\Bigl(1+\tfrac{2}{U}\Bigr)}_{\text{macro-step distortion}}\;\underbrace{\tfrac{11}{12}}_{\text{overlap}}\ =\ \frac{3355}{4320}\ <\ 0.7767.

Since
U\mapsto(1+2/U)
is decreasing, the bound improves for larger
X
.

Appendix C. Frequency netting on a uniform log-scale grid

We give the spacing-free large-sieve style bound used in the parent-window contraction, and a rigorous transfer from the uniform grid to the actual zeros of
\zeta(s)
.

Uniform grid bound

Let
h>0
,
T>0
. Set
N:=\lfloor T/h\rfloor
,
\Gamma:=\{\gamma_{m}:=m\,h:~-N\leq m\leq N\}
.
For
u_{1},\dots,u_{M}\in\mathbb{R}
and weights
w_{1},\dots,w_{M}\in\mathbb{C}
(after coalescing coincident points) define

S(\gamma):=\sum_{j=1}^{M}w_{j}e^{i\gamma u_{j}}.

Theorem C.1

(Log-scale netting on a uniform grid)
.

\sum_{\gamma_{0}\in\Gamma}\Bigl|\sum_{j=1}^{M}w_{j}e^{i\gamma_{0}u_{j}}\Bigr|^{2}\;\leq\;\Bigl(4M\,\frac{T}{h}+M\Bigr)\;\sum_{j=1}^{M}|w_{j}|^{2}.

In particular, if
M\leq 4
then

\sum_{\gamma_{0}\in\Gamma}\Bigl|\sum_{j=1}^{M}w_{j}e^{i\gamma_{0}u_{j}}\Bigr|^{2}\;\leq\;\Bigl(16\,\frac{T}{h}+4\Bigr)\;\sum_{j=1}^{M}|w_{j}|^{2}.

Sketch.

Write the quadratic form
\sum_{\gamma_{0}}|S(\gamma_{0})|^{2}=W^{*}KW
with entries
K_{jk}=\sum_{m=-N}^{N}e^{imh(u_{j}-u_{k})}
. Schur/Gershgorin with a near/far split and
|K_{jk}|\leq\min(2N+1,\frac{4}{h|u_{j}-u_{k}|})
yields the row-sum bound
\leq 4NM+M
, hence the claim.
∎

From zeros on the critical line to the uniform grid

Lemma C.2

(Local zero count on length-
h
intervals)
.

For
T\geq 3
and
h\in(0,1]
, and all
t\in[-T,T]
,

N\Bigl(t+\tfrac{h}{2}\Bigr)-N\Bigl(t-\tfrac{h}{2}\Bigr)\ \leq\ C_{0}\bigl(1+h\log(2T)\bigr),

where
N(u)
counts zeros
\rho=\tfrac{1}{2}+i\gamma
with
0<\gamma\leq u
, and
C_{0}
is absolute.

Proof.

From the Riemann–von Mangoldt formula
N(u)=\frac{u}{2\pi}\log\frac{u}{2\pi}-\frac{u}{2\pi}+O(\log u)
and the mean value theorem.
∎

Lemma C.3

(Zeros-to-grid
\ell^{2}
comparison)
.

Let
h\in(0,1]
,
T\geq 3
, and
\Gamma=\{-N,\dots,N\}\cdot h
with
N=\lfloor T/h\rfloor
.
For
S(\gamma)=\sum_{j=1}^{M}w_{j}e^{i\gamma u_{j}}
,

\sum_{|\gamma|\leq T}\bigl|S(\gamma)\bigr|^{2}\ \leq\ C_{1}\bigl(1+h\log(2T)\bigr)\sum_{\gamma_{0}\in\Gamma}\bigl|S(\gamma_{0})\bigr|^{2}\ +\ C_{2}\,\bigl(1+h\log(2T)\bigr)\,\frac{T}{h}\,M\,\sum_{j=1}^{M}|w_{j}|^{2}.

Proof.

Partition
[-T,T]
into cells
I(\gamma_{0}):=[\gamma_{0}-h/2,\gamma_{0}+h/2)
. For
\gamma\in I(\gamma_{0})
write
S(\gamma)=S(\gamma_{0})+(\gamma-\gamma_{0})S^{\prime}(\xi)
with
S^{\prime}(\xi)=i\sum u_{j}w_{j}e^{i\xi u_{j}}
. Then

|S(\gamma)|^{2}\leq 2|S(\gamma_{0})|^{2}+\tfrac{h^{2}}{2}\Bigl(\sum|u_{j}||w_{j}|\Bigr)^{2}\leq 2|S(\gamma_{0})|^{2}+\tfrac{h^{2}}{2}\Bigl(\sum|u_{j}|^{2}\Bigr)\Bigl(\sum|w_{j}|^{2}\Bigr).

In our application,
u_{j}
are log-scale positions with
|u_{j}|\ll U:=\log X
, hence
\sum|u_{j}|^{2}\leq M(\kappa U)^{2}
. Summing over at most
C_{0}(1+h\log(2T))
zeros per cell (Lemma
C.2
) and over
|\Gamma|\ll T/h
cells gives the claim.
∎

Corollary C.4

(Specialization to
h=\tfrac{2}{U}
,
T=\tfrac{1}{2}U^{3}
)
.

With
U=\log X
,
h=\tfrac{2}{U}
,
T=\tfrac{1}{2}U^{3}
, and
M\leq 4
,

\sum_{|\gamma|\leq T}|S(\gamma)|^{2}\ \leq\ C_{3}\,U^{4}\,\sum_{j=1}^{M}|w_{j}|^{2}.

Proof.

Here
h\log(2T)\asymp(2/U)\log U=O(1)
and
T/h=U^{4}/4
. Combine Theorem
C.1
with Lemma
C.3
.
∎

Corollary C.5

(Weighted
\ell^{1}
bound over zeros)
.

With the same specialization,

\sum_{|\gamma|\leq T}\frac{1}{|\rho|}\,\bigl|S(\gamma)\bigr|\ \leq\ C_{4}\,U^{2}\ \Bigl(\sum_{j=1}^{M}|w_{j}|^{2}\Bigr)^{1/2}.

Proof.

By Cauchy–Schwarz,

\sum_{|\gamma|\leq T}\frac{|S(\gamma)|}{|\rho|}\ \leq\ \Bigl(\sum_{|\gamma|\leq T}\frac{1}{\tfrac{1}{4}+\gamma^{2}}\Bigr)^{1/2}\cdot\Bigl(\sum_{|\gamma|\leq T}|S(\gamma)|^{2}\Bigr)^{1/2}\ \ll\ U^{2}\Bigl(\sum|w_{j}|^{2}\Bigr)^{1/2}.

∎

Remark

(Absorption into the
X^{1/2}\log X
budget)
.

In the parent-window analysis
M\leq 4
, the explicit-formula weights include
1/\log y
and the smoothing kernel, and the zero-sum is truncated at
T=\tfrac{1}{2}U^{3}

C.5
therefore yields a contribution
\ll U^{2}(\sum|w_{j}|^{2})^{1/2}
, which is absorbed into the error term
C\,X^{1/2}\log X
of the contraction inequality; see Appendix D.

Appendix E. Smoothed explicit formula: a uniform remainder bound with explicit constants

Statement

Fix
X\geq e^{120}
and set
U:=\log X\geq 120
. For
y\asymp X
, let

T:=\tfrac{1}{2}\,U^{3},\qquad W(t):=(1+t^{2})^{-3},\qquad W(0)=1,\qquad 0<W(t)\leq(1+t^{2})^{-3}.

Let
\rho=\tfrac{1}{2}+i\gamma

\zeta(s)

E(y):=\pi(y)-\mathrm{Li}(y),\qquad\mathcal{S}(y;T):=\Re\!\!\sum_{|\gamma|\leq T}\frac{y^{\rho}}{\rho\,\log y}\,W(\gamma/T).

Then

E(y)=\mathcal{S}(y;T)+R(y;T),

(7)

with
R(y;T)=R_{\mathrm{triv}}+R_{\Gamma}+R_{\mathrm{tail}}+R_{\mathrm{smooth}}

|R(y;T)|\ \leq\ 10\,X^{1/2}\qquad\text{for all }y\asymp X,\ X\geq e^{120}.

(8)

Remainder pieces

(1) Trivial zeros.

|R_{\mathrm{triv}}(y;T)|\leq y^{-2}/\log y\leq 10^{-40}X^{1/2}
.

(2) Gamma-factor.

|R_{\Gamma}(y;T)|\ \leq\ C\,y^{1/2}\frac{\log T}{T}\ \leq\ 0.1\,X^{1/2}\quad(U\geq 120).

|\gamma|>T
.

Adding the four pieces yields (

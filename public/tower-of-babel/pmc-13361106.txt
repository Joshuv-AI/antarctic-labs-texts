A study on the current status of collaboration between China’s commercial health insurance and medical institutions and improvement strategies
Authors: Panpan Ma, Wenjie Si, Ying Zhou, Liyan Huang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundActing as a supplement to China’s basic medical insurance, the overall development of commercial health insurance in China remains immature. Commercial health insurance institutions collaborate with medical institutions to provide healthcare services for their insured patients. This study aims to understand the current status of those two parties’ collaboration, and evaluate the factors that influence their partnership, thereby providing empirical evidence and improvement strategies for fostering their healthier collaboration and achieving a win-win scenario, which will contribute to the high-quality medical services to the public.MethodsA questionnaire survey was conducted among 616 medical institution personnel and commercial health insurance professionals from approximately ten regions across China. A measurement model was employed to examine the validity and reliability of the evaluation questionnaire, and then empirical analysis was used.ResultsSurvey respondents from commercial health insurance institutions perceived that medical workers have limited understanding of commercial health insurance. Over 85% of respondents from both groups indicated that medical workers should enhance their knowledge in the coverage and claim rules of commercial health insurance. Regarding medical behaviors which will affect claim settlement or be identified as insurance fraud, “the medical record is not detailed” (80.14%) was identified as the primary concern for insurance institutions, while “the treatment plan is inconsistent with the diagnoses in the medical record” (67.48%) was the main concern for medical institutions. Regarding improving medical behaviors, insurance institutions hope that hospitals can avoid breaking down service times, prescribe reasonable examinations and charge fairly; Meanwhile, medical institutions hope that insurance institutions can “arrange a dedicated person to explain insurance-related questions”.ConclusionsTo improve their collaboration, commercial health insurance institutions can simplify claim process, design multi-level products to give full play to their supplementary role, and cooperate with medical institutions to strengthen publicity and education for medical workers and patients on insurance policies and rules. Government agencies should introduce more legislation to regulate patients’ and medical workers’ behaviors to curb insurance fraud. Medical institutions, in coordination with government agencies and insurance institutions, should explore mechanisms for medical data sharing to regulate medical workers’ medical behaviors and help insurance institutions to innovate product designs. Based on survey findings from 616 respondents, this study identifies potential strategies that may improve collaboration between commercial health insurance and medical institutions. Further research is needed to evaluate whether these strategies effectively improve patient services.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12913-026-14762-1.Keywords: Commercial health insurance, Medical behaviors, Collaboration, Improvement strategies

Introduction

Over the past 70 years, China has established the world’s largest medical security system. With the coverage rate of basic medical insurance provided by Chinese government exceeding 95% [1], Chinese people’s fundamental medical needs have been met. However, an increasingly aging population and persistently growing demand for healthcare services have rendered it difficult for the government to manage medical security funds effectively [2].

Amid the ongoing advancement of the “Healthy China Strategy” and the deepening reforms in healthcare system, along with China’s social and economic development, the general public has developed new and higher-level healthcare demands beyond basic medical insurance coverage [3–5]. To access broader insurance coverage, enjoy higher-level medical services, or add extra protection for their healthcare, a growing number of people opt to purchase commercial health insurance (CHI) or private health insurance (PHI) as a supplement to basic medical insurance. As such, CHI in China has witnessed new growth opportunities in recent years. In 2023, the premium income for CHI amounted to 903.5 billion Yuan (around 124.61 billion US dollars), marking a year-on-year increase of 4.41%, nearly 2% points higher than that in 2022 [6]. By June 2023, there were 6,112 CHI products available in the market, including 2,673 illness insurance products and 3,253 medical insurance products [7]. Although China’s major CHI institutions have established cooperation with medical institutions in supplementary medical insurance areas like critical illness insurance and long-term care, and initial consensus on mutual interests has been reached between the two parties, China’s CHI market is still at an early stage of development compared with European and American countries [8–10], characterized by product homogeneity, structural imbalances, lack of innovation, low coverage and other problems [3, 11–14].

In China, the multi-level medical security system is divided into three parts. Among them, basic medical insurance is the main body, medical assistance is the backing, and supplementary insurance, mainly commercial health insurance is the extension [15]. Built on this system, China’s healthcare financing structure is fundamentally a multi-payer model, with government-administered basic medical insurance programs coexisting with privately operated CHI. Since the establishment of the National Healthcare Security Administration in 2018, a major breakthrough has been made: the construction of a national healthcare security information platform and standardized information service norms has greatly optimized basic medical insurance settlement efficiency [16]. Nevertheless, the sustainable service capacity of basic medical insurance is challenged by its “wide coverage with low protection” feature, overly rapid growth in medical expenses, limited government fiscal transfer payments, and medical insurance fund deficits [17]. Against this backdrop, Chinese government has been increasing policy support for CHI, aiming to alleviate the operational pressure on the basic medical insurance fund by leveraging the supplementary role of CHI. CHI is an insurance in which commercial insurance institutions pay compensation for losses caused by health reasons and medical behaviors [15], and such institutions operate for profit. Since the CHI information platform has not been constructed in China, the settlement of CHI still mainly relies on a post-treatment offline/online claim application model: insured individuals need to independently collect paper documents (e.g., medical records and expense statements), submit electronic copies via insurers or online platforms, or repeatedly travel between medical institutions and insurance institutions to supplement materials. This not only creates the pain points of cumbersome procedures, slow reimbursement and long out-of-pocket payment periods for individuals, but also directly raises insurers’ operational costs and anti-fraud risks [16]. Owing to the lack of a unified information platform, CHI institutions can only rely on their own underwriting and claims experience to inform insurance plan pricing [18], which hinders the development of the CHI market.

Literature review

Compared with the United States and some developed European countries, the coverage of CHI in China is relatively low. The health system in the United States is characterized by the dominance of a “market-oriented” medical insurance model using CHI [19]. To contain healthcare costs, the US attaches great importance to legislation against health insurance fraud, and both the federal and state governments have enacted numerous laws and regulations targeting health insurance fraud [20]. Many methods for health insurance claim fraud detection have been applied by insurance companies [21]. An early cost-containment tool is prior authorization (PA), which mandates that health providers obtain prior approval to ensure coverage for prescription medication and use of other services, including referral to specialists and coverage for many medical diagnostic test and therapeutic procedures [22]. However, a majority of doctors perceive that PA has a negative impact on medical practice and increase their workload.

In the Netherlands, CHI plays a complementary role in the health insurance system, which means it serves as co-payment for healthcare services (such as dental care) that are partially covered by the state [18, 23]. Dutch health insurance companies are empowered by the government to select hospitals based on quality and price, and negotiate volumes and prices with the selected hospitals. They became prudent buyers of care on behalf of their enrollees [24]. The Netherlands has built a unified medical data platform integrating claims records and service data from CHI and basic medical insurance. Researchers, medical institutions and insurance companies can use anonymized data for academic research, actuarial analysis, risk prediction and efficiency improvement, etc. But this cooperation is only a policy-driven optimization, not automated linkage between underwriting and claims [18].

In Germany, substitutive private health insurance coexists with social health insurance schemes which cover the majority of the population. The choice of opting out of the statutory system is restricted to those with an income above a certain threshold. Once an individual has chosen private insurance, the option of returning to the social health insurance regime is limited [25]. Besides, Germany physicians are allowed to bill in excess of “basic price” for privately-insured patients with a factor set to reflect the level of complexity and time for treatment [18, 25]. Insurance institutions only form direct contractual relationships with clients, not with providers, therefor patients with CHI need to pay medical bills first and then apply for reimbursement [18, 25].

Although a dual insurance system is common in many countries internationally, Chinese CHI has its own distinctive features, and research based on direct feedback from CHI institutions and healthcare providers remains scarce. As an supplemental private insurance, CHI in China can be divided into affordable CHI and high-end CHI. Both CHIs can help to cover different percentages of the remaining expenses after basic medical insurance reimbursement, so as to further reduce individuals’ out-of-pocket costs. Besides, high-end CHI can cover the medical services provided by high-end medical providers, including private medical institutions and the international medical departments of public hospitals, where medical prices are higher than those of basic medical services and basic medical insurance doesn’t cover the costs here. To meet multi-level medical requirements, Chinese government encourage the development of high-end medical services within a certain scale. In 2016, four ministries including the National Development and Reform Commission jointly issued the Notice on Printing and Distributing the Opinions on Promoting the Reform of Medical Service Prices, which clearly stipulates that market-adjusted prices shall be implemented for special medical services provided by public medical institutions, as well as other medical services with relatively sufficient market competition and strong personalized demand [26]. Therefore, high-end medical institutions are granted the power of independent pricing.

Currently in China, for affordable CHI, patients need to apply for reimbursement by themselves, therefore the collaboration between CHI institutions and medical institutions primarily concentrates in high-end CHI and private medical institutions or the international medical departments of public hospitals across economically advanced regions. Typically, patients with high-end CHI can enjoy direct-billing services at these facilities. Direct-billing service means that CHI institutions pay the reasonable medical expenses incurred by patients during treatment directly to medical institutions in accordance with signed contracts [27]. According to the financial regulations of the government agencies, contracted pricing between public medical institutions and CHI institutions is prohibited currently. Therefore, the cooperation between the two parties mainly focuses on direct-billing service contracts. This field, however, faces numerous unresolved issues and is the primary focus of this study.

First, since medical institutions, CHI institutions, and government agencies have not realized data sharing, it’s not good for their communication and the optimization of cooperation models. Second, the cumbersome claim process may delay reimbursement to medical institutions, which may cause distrust between the two parties. Third, high demands and numerous restrictions on doctors’ medical behaviors, such as writing detailed medical records, may lead to frequent doctor-patient conflicts and distrust between CHI institutions and medical institutions as well. This study aims to explore the current status of collaboration between CHI and medical institutions, propose improvement strategies and forge a new situation where CHI and medical institutions can cooperate and thrive together, providing higher-quality medical services to patients in China. These improvement strategies may have some general applicability to other countries with similar health insurance conditions.

Methods

Based on the barriers to the development of China’s CHI, practical medical experiences, and literature-identified pain points [28–31], this paper designed two separate questionnaires for CHI institutions and medical institutions [See Additional File 1], focusing on common concerns, such as medical workers’ understanding of CHI, doctors’ medical behaviors which will affect claim settlement, patients’ choices of medical institutions, etc. This study aims to understand the current status of the collaboration between CHI and medical institutions, and explore their respective needs, as well as patients’ needs.

The participants of this survey are personnel from CHI institutions and medical institutions. CHI institution personnel included the employees from the departments of Customer Service, Medical Network, Claims, Sales, Hospital Representatives, Call Center, etc. Medical institution personnel included doctors, nurses, and administrators from private hospitals and the International Medical Department of public hospitals, both of which mainly provide high-end medical services for patients.

Multiple-choice and single-choice questions were adopted according to the types of different questions. Open-ended questions allowed respondents to provide suggestions or additional options based on their actual knowledge.

From May to June 2024, data were collected via online and offline questionnaires using a convenience sampling approach, covering respondents from approximately 10 cities across different regions of China. A total of 616 valid questionnaires were recovered, and empirical analysis was employed in analyzing the survey results.

Discussion

The healthy development of CHI is inseparable from the close collaboration between CHI institutions and medical institutions. In this partnership, medical institutions enhance their market competitiveness by providing high-quality medical services and smooth medical process, while as purchasers of medical services, CHI directly impacts the financial interests and growth potential of medical institutions. In turn, medical institutions also bear responsibilities in insurance claim settlement. Compared with professionally operated CHI institutions in countries like the United States and Germany, Chinese CHI institutions are in a relatively nascent stage in the aspects of management mechanisms and development condition [9]. This places insurance institutions at a disadvantageous position in relationships with medical institutions, making it difficult to access vital medical information resources which are necessary for financial estimation and product up-gradation. They find themselves caught in a dilemma characterized by “difficulties in supervising hospital, weakened bargaining power, and high costs of risk control”.

To clarify the current status, respective needs of medical institutions and CHI institutions and foster a mutually beneficial collaboration between them, this study conducted a questionnaire survey among 616 medical institution personnel and CHI professionals across approximately 10 cities in China. The survey results revealed that some problems exist in their current collaboration and improvement need to be made in numerous aspects.

Firstly, the collaboration between these two parties plays a vital role in meeting the multi-level healthcare needs of the public. However, there exists a disparity in the understanding of CHI between the two parties. 22.65% of respondents from CHI institutions believed that medical personnel were very familiar with CHI, whereas only 3.04% of medical workers considered themselves very familiar with CHI. Similarly, 57.49% of CHI institution respondents perceived medical staff as having a general understanding of CHI, while merely 49.85% of medical staff held the same view. This asymmetry in perception may lead to misunderstandings and communication barriers during these two parties’ cooperation, which can undermine the quality and efficiency of services provided to patients.

When choosing a medical institution, patients place significant emphasis on doctors’ service quality and the smoothness of medical treatment process. Therefore, medical institutions should focus on enhancing the quality of medical services and improving medical process for patients with CHI. Medical records provided by medical institutions serve as a crucial evidence for patients’ claim settlement; thus, doctors should pay special attention to the writing of medical records and avoid the following conditions, including “the past medical history is not clearly written”, “the treatment plan is inconsistent with the diagnosis” and the medical record containing vague words such as “medical history is as before” and “consultation”, etc., all of which may result in claim denials by CHI institutions. Given the current inadequate understanding about CHI among medical workers, medical institutions should collaborate with CHI institutions to educate medical workers on relevant knowledge which is important to patients’ claim settlement, including coverage and claim rules, the medical service process for patients with CHI, and types of CHI products, etc. During medical service provision, medical institutions should standardize doctors’ behaviors: avoid breaking down service times, treat due to illness, prescribe reasonable examinations, use drug rationally, give reasonable treatment, and charge fairly. By doing these, medical institutions can help CHI institutions to control cost effectively and alleviate their operational pressure, thereby fostering a good development environment.

In the deepening process of healthcare reform, CHI is a pivotal force in expanding healthcare resources and innovating medical institutions’ mechanisms. However, the survey results indicate a need for increased education efforts toward their clients, particularly regarding insurance coverage and claim rules. Insurance institutions must strive to prevent patients from requesting doctors to modify medical records, which belongs to fraudulent behaviors, or canceling examinations due to insurance denials, etc., as these behaviors will disrupt routine clinical work. Additionally, greater emphasis should be placed on educating clients about medical process, reinforced by assigning on-site representatives to explain CHI-related questions.

Compared with the United States and Germany, China’s legislation to regulate medical workers’ conduct and prevent fraudulent behaviors remains insufficient. For instance, requirements of medical records for CHI patients are not clearly specified, and procedures for modifying medical records vary across hospitals.

Conclusions

CHI institutions and medical institutions are two independent market entities, but they are connected closely by serving the same group of people, i.e. CHI customers/patients. These two parties should negotiate on an equal footing and establish a mutually dependent and win-win cooperation based on insurance institutions’ business strategies, medical institutions’ characteristics, and clients’ demand for medical services. In this collaboration, the medical services provided by medical institutions form the bedrock of CHI’s existence, and the reasonableness and standardization of medical behaviors directly impact CHI institutions’ claims and operational costs. Meanwhile, medical institutions seek to attract more patients with CHI to raise revenue by continuously improving service quality and process, while also assuming a certain degree of responsibility for insurance claims. An ideal model of cooperation characterized by mutual promotion, mutual trust, check and balance, can stimulate CHI’s supervision on medical behaviors and then motivate medical institutions to control patients’ medical costs, which can help to foster the healthy and sustainable development of medical service market.

With the Chinese government introducing favorable policies such as the “Draft Agreement on Information Sharing between the Commercial Health Insurance Information Platform and the National Healthcare Security Information Platform” and the “Notice on Matters Related to the Application of Preferential Individual Income Tax Policies for Commercial Health Insurance Products”, the development of CHI is enjoying a period of policy dividend. Under this new situation, how to seize the new opportunities for CHI development and construct an ideal cooperation model between insurance institutions and medical institutions has become a shared concern within the industry. This research identifies obstacles hindering the development of partnerships between CHI institutions and medical institutions, particularly in terms of claim settlement and medical behaviors. Based on these findings, the following improvement strategies are proposed, hoping to improve the current status of the cooperation between the two parties and promote the high-quality development of CHI in China.

Simplify claim process and shorten claim settlement cycles

CHI institutions should take measures to simplify claim process and shorten the cycles for claim settlement, thereby improving the relationship among CHI institutions, patients and medical institutions. First, simplifying claim process is critical for boosting the satisfaction and trust of CHI patients. Therefore, insurance institutions should standardize the requirements for PA, claims documents, etc., minimize unnecessary paperwork, and offer clearer guidance to expedite claim settlement. Second, shortening claim cycles and making timely payments to medical institutions can help to improve their operational efficiency and strengthen their trust in CHI institutions. Therefore, regular review and improvement of claim process based on feedback from medical institutions and patients should be conducted. Third, CHI should strengthen the training of their employees to better resolve customers’ inquiries and provide support when applying for PA or claim settlement.

Establish secure and efficient data exchange and medical information sharing mechanisms

The National Development and Reform Commission’s “Three-Year Action Plan for “Data Elements X” (2024–2026)” points out that basic medical security and CHI should strengthen data collaboration in medical records, basic medical insurance settlement, and CHI information etc., so as to enable one-stop claim settlement, improve the cost control for basic medical insurance and increase the risk prevention capabilities for CHI [32]. Just as the Netherlands has done, medical institutions, in coordination with National Healthcare Security Administration and CHI institutions, should explore mechanisms for data exchange and medical information sharing under privacy and security regulations, harnessing the potential of medical data resources to provide more comprehensive and more precise protection programs for the people.

Firstly, data sharing not only ensures CHI institutions promptly understand patients’ treatment plans, necessity, and disease progression, but also facilitates PA and improves service efficiency. As is suggested by Sharan et al., electronic medical record-based “practice dashboards” may be considered instead of PA to reduce doctors’ workload [22]. Moreover, insurance institutions can better understand the healthcare needs and disease trends of different crowds based on big data information. They can also calculate the costs of various diseases more accurately by combining historical data with actual treatment conditions. Through scientific calculation methods, reasonable payment plans for each disease can be formulated, which can help to tailor insurance products and reasonably control costs. In addition, the information sharing mechanisms based on tripartite cooperation can give full play to the government’s regulatory role, helping to avoid unnecessary or fraudulent medical behaviors in medical institutions, such as breaking down service times, prescribing unreasonable examinations, etc., and thus save medical resources and reduce medical service costs.

Emphasize the supplementary role of CHI by promoting multi-level CHI and strengthening legal support

As a key supplement to China’s basic medical security system, CHI should develop standards for different tiers of insurance products, rationally guide patients to choose appropriate medical institutions according to their health conditions and specific insurance products, thereby improving the efficiency of medical services, optimizing medical resources’ allocation, and meeting patients’ multi-level medical needs. As of June 2023, among more than 6,000 CHI products in China, there are 126 nursing insurance products, and only 32 disability insurance products and 28 medical accident insurance products [7]. Therefore, insurance institutions need to innovate product designs, introduce single-disease payment management and define payment standards that encompass new drugs, technologies, and equipment. Expand coverage to long-term care, chronic diseases [33], and rare diseases [34] to address higher healthcare needs and ease patients’ financial burdens. Meanwhile, the government agencies should introduce legislation to regulate medical workers’ and patients’ behaviors to avoid insurance fraud and support CHI claims. In addition, the government and medical institutions should also cooperate to establish standardized procedures for modifying medical records, canceling examinations/treatments when the claim cannot be settled, and other related matters.

Strengthen the publicity and education on CHI policies and claims rules

Publicizing information on insurance coverage and claims rules is crucial for improving the cooperation between Chinese CHI and medical institutions. Through training and education, medical workers can gain a comprehensive understanding of insurance rules, coverage, and claim process, ensuring medical behaviors adhere to insurance rules and avoid unnecessary disputes. Patients need to be educated that CHI does not provide blanket coverage for all medical expenses and that claim denials are common. They should also recognize their own responsibilities and learn that fraudulent behaviors for claim purpose are forbidden, such as concealing medical history or requesting doctors to modify medical records. CHI institutions should also strengthen the communication mechanism with patients regarding PA application at key time points during medical treatment (such as before major examinations, surgeries, or expensive treatments, etc.), to minimize post-service disputes arising from claim denials. By strengthening publicity and education on CHI rules, medical workers’ and patients’ understanding of CHI will be enhanced. This will help to balance the interests of CHI institutions, medical institutions and patients, and foster a healthier cooperative framework to promote the sustainable development of the Chinese healthcare system.

In conclusion, the collaboration between CHI institutions and medical institutions is significant in addressing the multi-level healthcare needs of the population in China. To tackle the identified problems in their partnership, this paper proposed improvement strategies from several aspects, including simplifying claim process, establishing medical information sharing mechanisms, emphasizing CHI’s supplementary function, and strengthening the publicity on CHI rules. These strategies will help to provide comprehensive and high-quality medical services to the public and contribute significantly to the establishment of a healthier insurance ecosystem. Joint efforts by CHI institutions, medical institutions, and government agencies can also promote the development of healthcare industry and achieve broader social welfare.

Limitations

This study has several limitations. First, a limited convenience sample of respondents from approximately 10 cities was used to illustrate the current state of collaboration between China’s CHI and medical institutions, which may result in selection bias. Second, findings based on 616 respondents from high-end health providers and high-end insurers may not represent all medical-insurance collaboration models across China’s diverse healthcare system. Third, all data are based on respondents’ perceptions and self-reported behaviors, not objective measures of insurance knowledge or medical record quality. Fourth, this survey only captures one time point; thus causality and temporal relationships cannot be established, and future studies should incorporate multiple time points. Finally, further research is required to evaluate whether the proposed strategies in this study can effectively improve CHI and medical institutions’ collaboration and promote the development of CHI in China.

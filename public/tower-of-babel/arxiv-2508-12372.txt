Linking Stochastic Self-Propagating Star Formation and Spatio-Temporal Point Processes
Qihan Zou

Abstract
The stochastic self-propagating star-formation (SSPSF) model is an important theoretical framework for explaining how localised star-formation events trigger subsequent activity across galactic discs. While widely used to interpret spiral and irregular structures, its probabilistic rules have lacked a formal statistical foundation. In this work, we establish a connection between the SSPSF model and spatio-temporal point processes (STPP), which describe events in space and time through history-dependent intensities. We show that the SSPSF update law is equivalent to a separable spatio-temporal Hawkes process, and we derive a simple likelihood function that recovers SSPSF parameters from historical star-formation event data under simplifying assumptions. Beyond the statistical formulation, the framework provides a new approach to analysing the propagation of star formation in galaxies, enabling observational surveys of star-forming regions to be interpreted within the STPP framework. Furthermore, the approach naturally extends to continuous-time models, offering a more realistic representation of galactic dynamics.

1
Introduction

The Stochastic Self-Propagating Star Formation (SSPSF) model is one of the important framework developed to model the star formation processes of galaxies introduced by
Mueller and Arnett 1976
. In contrast to the density wave theory for spiral galaxy (e.g.
Lin and Lau 1979
,
Lin and Shu 1987
,
Shu 2016
), SSPSF used stochastic self-propagating to explain the formation of various types of galaxies. This theory has been further developed by
Gerola and Seiden 1978
;
Gerola et al. 1980
.
Comins 1981
builds on the SSPSF framework, but allows the triggered star formation probability to vary with radius. This mimics the decrease in star formation efficiency in the outer disk.
Statler et al. 1983
extend SSPSF into three dimensions by stacking multiple 2D layers with vertical neighbor connections. Subsequent work by
Seiden and Schulman 1990
proposed percolation-based extensions to the SSPSF framework, emphasizing the role of local connectivity and threshold-induced activation.
Jungwiert and Palous 1994
introduce anisotropy into the propagation rule, triggering probability is distributed elliptically around a star-forming cell, with ellipse orientation and eccentricity tied to the disk’s rotation curve.

On the other hand, spatiotemporal point processes are a fundamental framework in statistics to model events in space and time (e.g.
Ogata 1988
,
Daley and Vere-Jones 2003
,
Diggle 2006a
,
Diggle 2013
) and have been applied to a wide range of phenomena characterized by clustered, self-propagating events.
Ogata 1988
introduces likelihood‐based point process models for earthquake occurrences and a residual analysis method to detect features such as seismic quiescence for prediction.
Diggle 2006b
proposes a partial likelihood approach for inference in spatiotemporal point process models, avoiding the computational burden of full likelihood methods.
Diggle 2006a
reviews statistical models for spatio-temporal point process data, illustrating their application through real-world case studies in epidemiology and biology (e.g. foot and mouth disease, Gastroenteric disease, Bovine tuberculosis, etc.), and highlighting both empirical and mechanistic approaches to inference.
Gabriel and Diggle 2009
extends the inhomogeneous K-function to the spatio-temporal domain, proposing a nonparametric estimator that adjusts for spatial and temporal inhomogeneity, and applying it to Campylobacter infection data.
González et al. 2016
provide a comprehensive review for spatiotemporal point processes, covering fundamental concepts, and applications across epidemiology (e.g., Ebola) and natural harzard (e.g., Tornadoes).
Reinhart 2018
reviews self-exciting spatio-temporal point processes, summarizing their theory, estimation methods, and applications in fields like earthquakes, crime, and disease spread. Recently, with the development of neural networks and deep learning, the integration of spatio-temproal point processes with deep learning has made greater progress and has been applied in a wider range.
Wang et al. 2020
introduces a spatiotemporal point process framework using a convolutional RNN for tracking multiple objects.
Zhou et al. 2022
propose a flexible deep spatio-temporal point process method with nonparametric
intensity function that can infer irregular events.
Yuan et al. 2023
introduces Spatio-Temporal Diffusion Point Processes framework that models spatio-temporal point processes without assuming conditional independence between time and space.

One of the common processes in the spatio-temproal point processes framework is the spatio-temporal Hawkes process (e.g.
Reinhart 2018
,
Bernabeu et al. 2025
). It is particularly well suited to systems in which events occur at points in space and time, each event may generate multiple secondary events after a finite delay, and the influence of an event decays with both temporal and spatial separation from its origin. In the astrophysical context, the stochastic self-propagating star formation (SSPSF) mechanism fits naturally into this formulation: the background rate
\mu(\mathbf{s})
represents spontaneous star formation, while the triggering kernel
g
models the finite-time, finite-radius causal influence of a supernova shock front on subsequent star formation in surrounding regions.

While there are many studies on the stochastic self-propagating star formation model and spatio-temporal point processes, these two frameworks have not appeared to be directly related. In this work, we build a bridge between the stochastic self-propagating star formation model and spatio-temporal point processes, establishing a link between these two important theories. The structure of the paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we briefly review the classical stochastic self-propagating star formation model (SSPSF). In Section 3, we discuss the basic background of spatio-temporal point processes (STPP), with a focus on spatio-temporal Hawkes processes (STHP). In Section 4, we establish a connection between simplified spatio-temporal point processes and the simplified self-propagating star formation model under certain conditions. We then derive the log-likelihood function for the simplified STPP, which can be used to recover the spontaneous and triggered star-formation probabilities of the SSPSF from historical event data. In Section 5, we conduct simulation studies to examine the estimation of these probabilities using the proposed log-likelihood function, based on simulated SSPSF data under simplified settings, to further demonstrate the connection between the two frameworks. In Section 6, we provide further discussions and conclude the work.

2
The classical Stochastic self propagating star formation (SSPSF) model

In this work, we focus mainly on the classical Stochastic Self-Propagating Star Formation (SSPSF) model introduced by
Gerola and Seiden 1978
. This model represents a galactic disk as a two-dimensional array of concentric rings, each subdivided azimuthally into equal-area cells simulated regions in the galaxy where the star can be born. The disk rotates differentially according to a prescribed rotation curve, such that the set of nearest neighbours of a given cell changes over time as the rings shear past one another. Each cell of the disk can contain at most one star at a given time step, where a star refers to a star-forming region such as a young stellar cluster that is capable of producing massive stars that can trigger further star formation in surrounding regions. A supernova denotes a cell whose massive stars produce feedback strong enough to initiate star formation in its surrounding cells.

This discrete, probabilistic stochastic star formation framework couples local star formation propagation to the global galaxy disk, generating persistent spiral structure without invoking any preimposed density wave. The model evolves according to the following rules:

1.

Initialization of the model: randomly assign
K
cells with stars before simulation. Every star becomes a supernova in the very next time step after it is formed.

2.

Triggered star: If a cell has at least one surrounding supernova cell, it can form a star with a triggered probability (
0\leq P_{t}\leq 1
) , unless it is within a recovery period
t_{r}
following recent star formation.

\displaystyle P_{t}=\begin{cases}P_{t}\dfrac{t_{a}}{t_{r}},&0\leq t_{a}<t_{r},\\
P_{t},&t_{a}\geq t_{r},\end{cases}

where
t_{a}
is the age in time steps since the last star formed in the cell.

3.

Spontaneous star: Any cell can spontaneously form a star with a spontaneous probability (
0\leq P_{s}\leq 1
), independent of its neighbours.

4.

Aging and Triggering: the region age by one time step at each iteration after a star was born. Massive stars trigger surrounding cells in the time step immediately after their creation.

5.

Rotation: After each time step, rings are rotated according to the pre-designed galaxy’s rotation curve.

Define
C_{i,j}(t)
as the state of the cell in the ring
i
and the azimuth
j
at time
t
(
0
= empty,
1
= star). If
C_{i,j}(t)=0
, the probabilty of a new star born in this region is

\Pr(C_{i,j}(t+1)=1)=1-(1-P_{s})\prod_{n\in N_{i,j}(t)}(1-P_{t}(A_{i,j}(t))),

where
N_{i,j}(t)
is the set of nearest neighbours of
C_{i,j}(t)
and
A_{i,j}(t)
=
t_{a}
time after the star born in
C_{i,j}(t)
at time
t
(0 if empty). If
C_{i,j}(t)=1
, the region simply ages:
A_{i,j}(t+1)=A_{i,j}(t)+1
for each time step. For
A_{i,j}(t)\geq t_{r}
, reset it to 0 where
t_{r}
is the refractory period in time steps. The probability of triggered star formation denoted
P_{t}
and the probability of spontaneous star formation
P_{s}
are two main parameters used to control the star formation process.

3
Spatio-Temporal Processes

3.1
Spatio-Temporal Point Process

The spatiotemporal point process (STPP) defined in
\mathcal{S}\times(a,b]
is a framework for modeling the number of events. Based on historical recorded events data
\mathcal{H}_{t}
, we can define the conditional intensity function for an STPP (e.g.
Diggle 2006a
,
Gabriel and Diggle 2009
,
González et al. 2016
and
Reinhart 2018
) as

\displaystyle\lambda^{*}(t,\mathbf{s})=\lim_{d\mathbf{s}\to 0,dt\to 0}\frac{\mathbb{E}\!\left[N(s+ds,t+dt)-N(s,t)\middle|\mathcal{H}_{t}\right]}{dsdt}.

Conditional intensity
\lambda^{*}(t,\mathbf{s})
plays a central role in spatio-temporal point processes, as it controls the occurrence of events in spacetime.

3.2
Spatio-Temporal Hawkes Processes (STHP)

The Hawkes process (e.g.
Reinhart 2018
,
Bernabeu et al. 2025
) is a self-exciting point process in which each event that occurred increases the conditional intensity of future events around it. In the spatio-temporal setting, events occur at times
t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
and locations
\mathbf{s}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
(e.g.
d=2
for a 2 dimensional plane), and the process is specified by its conditional intensity function (e.g.
Ogata 1988
,
Reinhart 2018
):

\displaystyle\lambda^{*}(t,\mathbf{s})=\lambda(t,\mathbf{s}\mid\mathcal{H}_{t})=\mu(\mathbf{s})+\sum_{t_{i}<t}g(t-t_{i},\,\mathbf{s}-\mathbf{s}_{i}),

where
\mathcal{H}_{t}
denotes the history of all events before
t
,
\mu(\mathbf{s})
is a background rate that can be different in different locations and be modeled by various kernels, such as a Gaussian kernel, and
g(\Delta t,\Delta\mathbf{x})
is the trigger kernel that describes how past events in
(t_{i},\mathbf{s}_{i})
affect the rate of occurrence at
(t,\mathbf{s})
. To simplify the model, the kernel
g
is typically separable into temporal and spatial components:

\displaystyle g(t-t_{i},\,\mathbf{s}-\mathbf{s}_{i})=g(\Delta t,\Delta\mathbf{x})=g_{1}(\Delta t)\,g_{2}(\Delta\mathbf{x}),

\displaystyle\lambda^{*}(t,\mathbf{s})=\mu(\mathbf{s})+\sum_{t_{i}<t}g_{1}(t-t_{i})g_{2}(\mathbf{s}-\mathbf{s}_{i}),

with
g_{1}(\Delta t)
controlling the decay of influence with time (e.g. an exponential decay function
g(\Delta t)=\alpha e^{-\beta\Delta t}
for
\Delta t>0
and nromally
\alpha>0,\beta>0
) and
g_{2}(\Delta\mathbf{x})
describing the spatial distribution of triggered events (e.g. a Gaussian kernel).

4
Connection between the STPP and the SSPSF

In this section, we build a bridge between the STPP and SSPSF frameworks, organised into two parts. The notation and formulation are self-contained for each part. First, we show the equivalence between the spatio-temporal Hawkes process (STHP) and the SSPSF in their update rule. Second, we derive a log-likelihood for a simplified STPP that can be used to estimate SSPSF parameters.

4.1
Equivalence in update law between the STHP and the SSPSF

Let events occur in continuous time
t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
and in continuous space
\mathbf{s}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
. The spatiotemporal Hawkes process with separable kernel has conditional intensity:

\lambda^{*}(t,\mathbf{s})=\mu(\mathbf{s})+\sum_{t_{i}<t}g_{1}(t-t_{i})\,g_{2}(\mathbf{s}-\mathbf{s}_{i}),

(1)

where
\mu(\mathbf{s})\geq 0
is the background rate and
g_{1}(t-t_{i})\geq 0
,
g_{2}(\mathbf{s}-\mathbf{s_{i}})\geq 0
are the temporal and spatial trigger kernels, respectively. We also assume
g_{1}(t-t_{i})=0
for
t-t_{i}\leq 0
.

Discretize space into equal area cells
\{S_{i,j}\}
(ring
i
, azimuth
j
) and time into steps of length
\Delta\tau
and denote discrete times as
t_{k}=k\,\Delta\tau
. Let historical events
\mathcal{H}_{t}=\{(t_{i},\mathbf{s}_{i}):t_{i}<t\}
and the event count
N(S)
in a spatial region
\mathcal{S}\subset\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
. For the spatio-temproal Hawkes process, by the defining property of conditional intensities for point processes conditional on history
\mathcal{H}_{t_{k}}
, the integrated intensity can be defined as:

\displaystyle\Lambda_{i,j}(t_{k})=\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\int_{S_{i,j}}\lambda^{*}(t,\mathbf{s})d\mathbf{s}dt=\Lambda^{(1)}_{i,j}(t_{k})+\Lambda^{(2)}_{i,j}(t_{k}),

where

\displaystyle\Lambda^{(1)}_{i,j}(t_{k})

\displaystyle=\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\int_{S_{i,j}}\mu(\mathbf{s})d\mathbf{s}dt

\displaystyle\Lambda^{(2)}_{i,j}(t_{k})

\displaystyle=\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\int_{S_{i,j}}\sum_{t_{i}<t}g_{1}(t-t_{i})g_{2}(\mathbf{s}-\mathbf{s}_{i})d\mathbf{s}dt.

Considering the number of events in a cell
S_{i,j}\times[t_{k},t_{k+1})
conditioned on
\mathcal{H}_{t_{k}}
, we have
\Pr(N(S_{i,j}\times[t_{k},t_{k+1}))=0|\mathcal{H}_{t_{k}})=\exp(-\Lambda_{i,j}(t_{k})),
since the count of events in the cell is conditionally poisson with mean equal to the integrated intensity defined above.

\displaystyle\Pr(N(S_{i,j}\times[t_{k},t_{k+1}))\geq 1|\mathcal{H}_{t_{k}})=1-\exp(-\Lambda_{i,j}(t_{k})).

(2)

Then decompose
\Lambda_{i,j}(t_{k})
as a sum of background and trigger contributions. For each neighbor cell
S_{m,n}\in\mathcal{N}_{i,j}(t_{k})
that contains a supernova at some time
t_{l}\leq t_{k}
and use
m,n
to denote the neighbor index:

\Lambda_{i,j}(t_{k})=\Lambda^{(1)}_{i,j}(t_{k})+\sum_{S_{m,n}\in\mathcal{N}_{i,j}(t_{k})}\Lambda^{(2)}_{i,j\leftarrow m,n}(t_{k}).

(3)

Using (
2
) and (
3
), and each cell can have at most one star, we have the following:

\displaystyle\Pr(C_{i,j}(t_{k+1})=1|C_{i,j}(t_{k})=0,\mathcal{H}_{t_{k}})

\displaystyle=1-\exp(-\Lambda^{(1)}_{i,j}(t_{k}))\exp(-\sum_{S_{m,n}\in\mathcal{N}_{i,j}(t_{k})}\Lambda^{(\mathrm{2})}_{i,j\leftarrow m,n}(t_{k}))

\displaystyle=1-\exp(-\Lambda^{(1)}_{i,j}(t_{k}))\prod_{S_{m,n}\in\mathcal{N}_{i,j}(t_{k})}\exp(-\Lambda^{(2)}_{i,j\leftarrow m,n}(t_{k})).

Define spontaneous and triggered probabilities by

\displaystyle P_{s}(i,j,t_{k})=1-\exp(-\Lambda^{(1)}_{i,j}(t_{k})),\>\exp(-\Lambda^{(1)}_{i,j}(t_{k}))=1-P_{s}(i,j,t_{k})

\displaystyle P_{t}(i,j\leftarrow m,n;t_{k})=1-\exp(-\Lambda^{(\mathrm{2})}_{i,j\leftarrow m,n}(t_{k})),\>\exp(-\Lambda^{(\mathrm{2})}_{i,j\leftarrow m,n}(t_{k}))=1-P_{t}(i,j\leftarrow m,n;t_{k}).

Then, the probability of spatio-temproal Hawkes process becomes

\displaystyle\Pr(C_{i,j}(t_{k+1})=1|C_{i,j}(t_{k})=0,\mathcal{H}_{t_{k}})=1-(1-P_{s}(i,j,t_{k}))\prod_{S_{m,n}\in\mathcal{N}_{i,j}(t_{k})}(1-P_{t}(i,j\leftarrow m,n;t_{k})),

which is exactly the SSPSF probability for updating stars. If we considered constant
\Lambda^{(1)}_{i,j}
and
\Lambda^{(2)}_{i,j}
for cells, and multiply the temporal kernel by a function, e.g.
r(t_{a},t_{r})=\min(1,\frac{t_{a}}{t_{r}})
to introduce a refractory ramp, and define the refractory kernel as
\tilde{g}_{1}(\Delta t)=g_{1}(\Delta t)\,r(t_{a},t_{r}).
Replacing
g_{1}
with
\tilde{g}_{1}
makes triggering impossible immediately after an event, then allows it to increase linearly up to full strength by time
t_{r}
, matching the refractory behavior of the SSPSF. Then, by adding a differential rotation map to the spatio-temporal Hawkes process when generating offsprings in the Stochastic Self-Propagating Star Formation (SSPSF) model, we obtain an equivalent formulation. We show that the one-step update probability of the SSPSF model is the probability induced by a separable spatio-temporal Hawkes process on the corresponding space-time cell, when the Hawkes intensity is integrated over that cell and one discrete time step, with both refractory modulation and differential rotation. Moreover, this formulation offers a more flexible framework for modeling the SSPSF, as the kernel structures can be specified with greater generality.

4.2
From Continuous STPP to Discrete Likelihood for SSPSF

Let events
\mathcal{H}_{t}=\{(t_{n},\mathbf{s}_{n})_{n=1}^{N}\}
be observed on
[0,T]\times S
, with separable spatio-temporal intensity
\lambda^{*}(t,\mathbf{s})
. The continuous log-likelihood of the Spatio-Temproal processes (
Daley and Vere-Jones 2003
,
Reinhart 2018
,
Zhou et al. 2022
, etc.) is

L=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\log\lambda^{*}(t_{n},\mathbf{s}_{n})-\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\int_{S}\lambda^{*}(t,\mathbf{s})\,d\mathbf{s}\,dt.

From continuous space and time to discrete space and time, Let
\{B_{i,j,k}\}
be a measurable partition of
[0,T]\times S
into disjoint time space cells where
(i,j)
are spatil index and
k
is time index, then

B_{i,j,k}=S_{i,j}\times[t_{k},t_{k+1}),t_{k}=k\,\Delta\tau
and let
\Delta\tau=1
in our case.
For each cell define the integrated intensity,

\Lambda_{i,j}(t_{k})=\int_{B_{i,j,k}}\lambda^{*}(t,\mathbf{s})d\mathbf{s}dt,

and let
N_{i,j,k}=N(B_{i,j,k})
be the observed number of events in a spacetime cell
B_{i,j,k}
. Regrouping the event term by cells and decomposing the integral over the partition for the continuous log-likelihood, we have

\displaystyle L

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\log\lambda^{*}(t_{n},\mathbf{s}_{n})-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{S}\lambda^{*}(t,\mathbf{s})d\mathbf{s}dt

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j,k}\sum_{(t_{n},\mathbf{s}_{n})\in B_{i,j,k}}\log\lambda^{*}(t_{n},\mathbf{s}_{n})-\sum_{i,j,k}\int_{B_{i,j,k}}\lambda^{*}(t,\mathbf{s})d\mathbf{s}dt

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j,k}\sum_{(t_{n},\mathbf{s}_{n})\in B_{i,j,k}}\log\lambda^{*}(t_{n},\mathbf{s}_{n})-\sum_{i,j,k}\Lambda_{i,j}(t_{k})

After some algebraic operations, for any event,
(t_{n},\mathbf{s}_{n})\in B_{i,j,k}
,

\log\lambda^{*}(t_{n},\mathbf{s}_{n})=\log(\Lambda_{i,j}(t_{k})\frac{\lambda^{*}(t_{n},\mathbf{s}_{n})}{\Lambda_{i,j}(t_{k})})=\log\Lambda_{i,j}(t_{k})+\log\frac{\lambda^{*}(t_{n},\mathbf{s}_{n})}{\Lambda_{i,j}(t_{k})}.

Then, summing all events in the cell
B_{i,j,k}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{(t_{n},\mathbf{s}_{n})\in B_{i,j,k}}\log\lambda^{*}(t_{n},\mathbf{s}_{n})

\displaystyle=\sum_{(t_{n},\mathbf{s}_{n})\in B_{i,j,k}}\log\Lambda_{i,j}(t_{k})+\sum_{(t_{n},\mathbf{s}_{n})\in B_{i,j,k}}\log\frac{\lambda^{*}(t_{n},\mathbf{s}_{n})}{\Lambda_{i,j}(t_{k})}

\displaystyle=N_{i,j,k}\log\Lambda_{i,j}(t_{k})+\sum_{(t_{n},\mathbf{s}_{n})\in B_{i,j,k}}\log\frac{\lambda^{*}(t_{n},\mathbf{s}_{n})}{\Lambda_{i,j}(t_{k})}

Assume
\log\Lambda_{i,j}(t_{k})
is constant within the cell and the number of events in the cell is
N_{i,j,k}
. The log-likelihood becomes

\displaystyle L

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j,k}\sum_{(t_{n},\mathbf{s}_{n})\in B_{i,j,k}}\log\lambda^{*}(t_{n},\mathbf{s}_{n})-\sum_{i,j,k}\Lambda_{i,j}(t_{k})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j,k}N_{i,j,k}\log\Lambda_{i,j}(t_{k})+\sum_{i,j,k}\sum_{(t_{n},\mathbf{s}_{n})\in B_{i,j,k}}\log\frac{\lambda^{*}(t_{n},\mathbf{s}_{n})}{\Lambda_{i,j}(t_{k})}-\sum_{i,j,k}\Lambda_{i,j}(t_{k})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j,k}(N_{i,j,k}\log\Lambda_{i,j}(t_{k})-\Lambda_{i,j}(t_{k}))+\sum_{i,j,k}\sum_{(t_{n},\mathbf{s}_{n})\in B_{i,j,k}}\log\frac{\lambda^{*}(t_{n},\mathbf{s}_{n})}{\Lambda_{i,j}(t_{k})}

Define the parameter
p=1-\exp(-\Lambda_{i,j}(t_{k}))
, then
\Lambda_{i,j}(t_{k})=-\log(1-p)
,

\displaystyle N_{i,j,k}\log\Lambda_{i,j}(t_{k})-\Lambda_{i,j}(t_{k})

\displaystyle=N_{i,j,k}\log(-\log(1-p))+\log(1-p).

Now, assume
\Lambda_{i,j}(t_{k})
is a small constant intensity in a cell and hence
\lambda^{*}(t_{n},s_{n})\approx\Lambda_{i,j}(t_{k})
in a cell. Then,
p=1-\exp(-\Lambda_{i,j}(t_{k}))
is approximated to a constant and since the Taylor approximation of
\exp(x)=1+x
for small
x
, then
\Lambda_{i,j}(t_{k})\approx\exp(\Lambda_{i,j}(t_{k}))-1
. We have the following approximation of
\lambda^{*}(t_{n},s_{n})
,

\displaystyle\lambda^{*}(t_{n},s_{n})

\displaystyle\approx\exp(\Lambda_{i,j}(t_{k}))-1

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\exp(-\Lambda_{i,j}(t_{k}))}-\frac{\exp(-\Lambda_{i,j}(t_{k}))}{\exp(-\Lambda_{i,j}(t_{k}))}

\displaystyle=\frac{1-\exp(-\Lambda_{i,j}(t_{k}))}{\exp(-\Lambda_{i,j}(t_{k}))}=\frac{p}{1-p}.

Putting in the approximation of
\lambda^{*}(t_{n},s_{n})
and based on our assumptions, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{(t_{n},\mathbf{s}_{n})\in B_{i,j,k}}\log\frac{\lambda^{*}(t_{n},\mathbf{s}_{n})}{\Lambda_{i,j}(t_{k})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{(t_{n},\mathbf{s}_{n})\in B_{i,j,k}}\log(\lambda^{*}(t_{n},\mathbf{s}_{n}))-\sum_{(t_{n},\mathbf{s}_{n})\in B_{i,j,k}}\log(\Lambda_{i,j}(t_{k}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{(t_{n},\mathbf{s}_{n})\in B_{i,j,k}}\log(\lambda^{*}(t_{n},\mathbf{s}_{n}))-\sum_{(t_{n},\mathbf{s}_{n})\in B_{i,j,k}}\log(-\log(1-p))

\displaystyle=\sum_{(t_{n},\mathbf{s}_{n})\in B_{i,j,k}}\log(\frac{p}{1-p})-\sum_{(t_{n},\mathbf{s}_{n})\in B_{i,j,k}}\log(-\log(1-p))

\displaystyle=N_{i,j,k}log(\frac{p}{1-p})-N_{i,j,k}\log(-\log(1-p)).

Putting all together, we obtain the following log-likelihood function for the simplified spatio-temproal point process of the reduced stochastic self-propagating star formation:

\displaystyle L

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j,k}\{N_{i,j,k}\log(-\log(1-p))+\log(1-p)+N_{i,j,k}log(\frac{p}{1-p})-N_{i,j,k}\log(-\log(1-p))\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j,k}\{\log(1-p)+N_{i,j,k}log(\frac{p}{1-p})\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j,k}\{\log(1-p)+N_{i,j,k}log(p)-N_{i,j,k}log(1-p)\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j,k}\{N_{i,j,k}log(p)+(1-N_{i,j,k})log(1-p)\}

which is a log-likelihood function of the Bernoulli distribution (
Casella and Berger 2024
,
Dobson and Barnett 2018
, etc.) with the parameter
p=1-\exp(-\Lambda_{i,j}(t_{k}))
and
\Lambda_{i,j}(t_{k})
should be set up according to the actual STPP model we use, e.g. the spatiotemporal Hawkes processes (STHP). This simple log-likelihood can be used to estimate parameters from star formation data. More generally, the spatiotemporal likelihood provides a flexible way to adapt other SSPSF variants by modifying or adding components, and it provides a way to a fully continuous SSPSF formulated within a spatiotemporal point-process framework.

5
Simulation

For the simulation studies, we focus on the simplest setting in order to highlight the connection between the stochastic self-propagating star formation model and spatio-temporal point processes. We consider a reduced version of the discrete-time and discrete-space SSPSF mainly based on
Gerola and Seiden 1978
defined on a polar grid of concentric rings, each partitioned into equal-area cells. In this model, cells remain active for exactly one time step, and each cell contains at most one point. If
C_{i,j}(t_{k})=1
, then
C_{i,j}(t_{k+1})=0
. There is no refractory ramp, cells can be reactivated immediately in the next step. In addition, no differential rotation is included.

Let the background rate per cell be
\mu_{b}
and the trigger rate per cell be
\alpha
. For the spatial kernel, only neighboring cells contribute. For the temporal kernel, only the previous time step contributes. For an inactive cell
(i,j)
at time
t_{k}
, denote the number of active neighbors at time
t_{k}
as
w_{i,j}(t_{k})
. We extract all inactive-to-next-step data, that is, for each cell and time
t_{k}
, if
C_{i,j}(t_{k})=0
, record:
y=C_{i,j}(t_{k+1})
and
w_{i,j}(t_{k})
. Then, we maximize the Bernoulli log-likelihood:

L(\mu_{b},\alpha)=\sum_{\text{obs}}y\,\log p+(1-y)\,\log(1-p),

with

This further demonstrates the connection between spatiotemporal point processes and the stochastic self-propagating star formation (SSPSF) model. However, the method may perform poorly when these conditions are not satisfied. In such cases, either a rigorous discrete spatio-temporal point process formulation should be developed, or a fully continuous SSPSF model should be constructed within the same framework. In addition, our simulations omit the refractory ramp and differential rotation for simplicity. Although this may be unrealistic, the results still provide strong evidence of a deep connection between spatiotemporal point processes and the stochastic self-propagating star formation model.

6
Discussion and Conclusion

In this work, we began with continuous spatio-temporal point processes (STPPs) defined over continuous space and time, and then introduced a discretization procedure under simplifying assumptions to adapt the framework to the discrete Stochastic Self-Propagating Star Formation (SSPSF) model. This discretization provides a practical bridge between the continuous STPP formulation and the cell-based SSPSF model, but it is not fully rigorous. More principled discrete STPP formulations, such as those in
Sheen et al. 2022
, may provide stronger theoretical foundations. Nevertheless, a key contribution of this study is to demonstrate that, under suitable conditions, the SSPSF can be understood as a special case of an STPP. This equivalence enables likelihood-based inference for SSPSF parameters directly from observational event data, thereby establishing a principled statistical framework for studying the propagation of star formation activity in galaxies. Looking ahead, a natural extension is to develop a fully continuous version of the SSPSF model, eliminating the artificial discretization of galactic disks into cells and timesteps. Such a formulation, based on continuous-time STPPs such as the spatio-temporal Hawkes process, could yield more faithful and predictive models of galactic-scale star formation. Moreover, incorporating additional physical processes such as differential galactic rotation and refractory effects into the STPP framework would further strengthen the link between physically motivated star-formation rules and statistically grounded point process theory, offering a powerful tool for both simulation and inference in galaxy evolution studies.

In summary, we propose a statistical framework that connects the stochastic self-propagating star formation (SSPSF) model with spatio-temporal point processes (STPP), using spatio-temporal Hawkes processes as an example. Under suitable simplifying assumptions, we demonstrate the equivalence between the update rules of these two frameworks and derive a log-likelihood function that can be used to recover the parameters of the SSPSF model from historical event data. Although simplified, this framework represents an initial step toward applying STPP to galactic-scale star-formation modelling. It provides a novel approach to modelling star formation and galaxy evolution using spatio-temporal processes. Furthermore, the proposed approach opens the possibility of constructing a continuous version of the SSPSF model, which could offer a more natural representation of galactic dynamics.

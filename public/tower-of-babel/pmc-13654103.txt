Prognostic value of pulmonary artery pulsatility index for perioperative adverse cardiovascular events in patients undergoing surgery for mitral regurgitation
Authors: Xiaoli Zheng, Dongwei Xie, Huixian Liao, Di Cen, Xiaomei Chen, Yongqing Li, Yan Deng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe pulmonary artery pulsatility index (PAPi) demonstrates prognostic value across cardiopulmonary conditions, yet its utility in mitral regurgitation (MR) perioperative risk stratification remains undefined. This study aimed to evaluate whether the PAPi could independently predict perioperative adverse cardiovascular events in patients with MR, and to explore whether its addition to the European System for Cardiac Operative Risk Evaluation (EuroSCORE) II provided incremental value.MethodsThis ambidirectional cohort study enrolled 302 consecutive patients with moderate-to-severe MR undergoing mitral valve surgery. PAPi was calculated as pulmonary artery pulse pressure (pulmonary artery systolic pressure minus diastolic pressure) divided by right atrial pressure, all estimated by echocardiography. Patients were grouped by PAPi tertiles and followed for 3 months. The primary endpoint was major adverse cardiovascular events (MACEs). Exploratory endpoints included mechanical ventilation duration and inotropic support. Incremental value was assessed by integrating PAPi into EuroSCORE II.ResultsPatients were stratified into low PAPi (<3.4, n=99), intermediate PAPi (3.4–4.8, n=101), and high PAPi (>4.8, n=102) groups. MACE incidence was 37.4% (37/99), 10.9% (11/101), and 3.9% (4/102), respectively (χ2=43.70, P<0.001). Lower PAPi was associated with prolonged mechanical ventilation, inotropic support, and intensive care unit (ICU) stay (all P<0.001). Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis showed that PAPi predicted MACE with an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.82 [95% confidence interval (CI): 0.76–0.88]. At cutoff 3.45, sensitivity was 81%, specificity 75%, positive predictive value (PPV) 40.0%, and negative predictive value (NPV) 94.9%. Bootstrap validation confirmed stability (AUC 0.82, 95% CI: 0.761–0.878). Multivariate analysis identified PAPi [odds ratio (OR) =0.26, 95% CI: 0.15–0.45, P<0.001], EuroSCORE II (OR=1.15, 95% CI: 1.03–1.28, P=0.012), and tricuspid annular plane systolicexcursion/pulmonary artery systolic pressure [TAPSE/PASP; OR =0.79 per 1-standard deviation (SD) decrease, 95% CI: 0.65–0.95, P=0.013] as independent predictors. Each 1-unit PAPi decrease was associated with a 3.8-fold increase in MACE risk. Adding PAPi to clinical risk score and echocardiographic right ventricular function models improved predictive performance [C-statistic 0.70 to 0.85; net reclassification improvement (NRI) =0.807; integrated discrimination improvement (IDI) =0.144; all P<0.001]. Bootstrap optimism correction yielded an AUC of 0.85 (95% CI: 0.80–0.90). Subgroup analyses showed consistent predictive value (all interaction P>0.05).ConclusionsThe echocardiography-derived PAPi predicts perioperative MACE in patients with MR. Incorporation of this index into traditional risk scoring systems provides incremental value for preoperative risk assessment and facilitates the identification of high-risk patients.

Introduction

Mitral regurgitation (MR) affects ~24 million individuals globally, with prevalence reaching 9.3% among those aged ≥75 years (1). Despite surgical advances, perioperative major adverse cardiovascular events (MACEs) occur in 5–25% of patients (2,3). Current risk stratification relies on European System for Cardiac Operative Risk Evaluation (EuroSCORE) II, which notably omits right ventricular (RV) function assessment (4).

In MR, chronic left-sided volume overload elevates left atrial pressure, resulting in pulmonary venous hypertension and increased pulmonary vascular resistance. Consequently, the right ventricle is subjected to pressure overload, but has limited tolerance compared with the left ventricle. RV dysfunction is increasingly recognized as a critical determinant of outcomes in cardiac surgery and valvular disease (5-7), yet remains uncharacterized in standard risk algorithms.

The pulmonary artery pulsatility index (PAPi), calculated as pulmonary artery systolic pressure − pulmonary artery diastolic pressure/right arterial pressure [(PASP − PADP)/RAP] (8), integrates RV contractile function, pulmonary vascular impedance, and right atrial preload. Validated in heart failure and pulmonary hypertension, PAPi captures RV-pulmonary vascular coupling (8-11). However, its utility in MR—where the RV faces unique dual pressure-volume loading—remains unexplored.

We hypothesized that PAPi would independently predict perioperative MACE and provide incremental value beyond established scores. This study represents the first investigation of PAPi in this setting. We present this article in accordance with the STROBE reporting checklist (available at https://qims.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/qims-2026-0992/rc).

Methods

Study design and population

This single-center, ambidirectional cohort study consecutively enrolled patients who underwent mitral valve surgery at The First Affiliated Hospital of Guangxi Medical University (January 2023 to October 2025). The inclusion criteria were as follows (12): moderate-to-severe MR, age ≥18 years, and preoperative echocardiography within 7 days (Figure 1 shows the echocardiographic images of severe MR caused by mitral valve prolapse). The exclusion criteria were as follows: left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) ≤30%, concomitant valvular disease requiring intervention, coronary artery bypass grafting, severe organ dysfunction, pregnancy, or poor image quality (Figure 2). The sample size was determined based on the rule of 10 events per variable (EPV) for predictive model development. To mitigate potential selection bias, 224 excluded patients were incorporated as an independent exclusion cohort into the comparative analysis. Baseline characteristics were compared between the included and excluded cohorts (Table S1). The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments. This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of The First Affiliated Hospital of Guangxi Medical University (approval number: 2025-E0696). Informed consent was provided by all patients involved in the prospective components of this study.

Echocardiographic assessment

Examinations were performed using Philips EPIQ 7C (Philips Healthcare, Amsterdam, Netherlands) by experienced sonographers following American Society of Echocardiography (ASE) guidelines (13). PAPi was calculated as (PASP − PADP)/RAP from Doppler velocities (8,14). PASP and PADP were calculated based on tricuspid regurgitation (TR) and pulmonary regurgitation (PR) jet velocities using the modified Bernoulli equation with incorporation of RAP. RA pressure was estimated based on two-dimensional (2D) and Doppler assessment of the inferior vena cava (IVC) and hepatic vein (Figure 3A-3C) according to established guidelines. Specifically, an IVC diameter ≤2.1 cm with >50% collapse during a sniff indicated a normal RAP of 3 mmHg, whereas an IVC diameter >2.1 cm with <50% collapse indicated a high RAP of 15 mmHg. Cases with discordant IVC diameter and collapse were assigned an intermediate RAP value of 8 mmHg (15). Among the included patients, measurable TR jet, PR jet, and IVC-based RAP estimation were available in 302/302 patients (100%). Left ventricular parameters included LVEF, and RV assessment comprised tricuspid annular plane systolic excursion (TAPSE), TAPSE/PASP, RV fractional area change (FAC), and 2D-right ventricular ejection fraction (RVEF). TAPSE was measured as shown in Figure 3D, and FAC was measured as shown in Figure 3E. All measurements were averaged over three cardiac cycles.

Endpoints

The primary endpoint was 3-month MACE (16-19), including: all-cause death; myocardial infarction; stroke; low cardiac output (CO) syndrome; re-exploration for bleeding; malignant arrhythmias; acute kidney injury requiring dialysis; unplanned reoperation; and heart failure rehospitalization. Exploratory endpoints (5) comprised mechanical ventilation duration, inotropic support, and intensive care unit (ICU) length of stay.

Events were adjudicated by two independent cardiologists blinded to echocardiographic data.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD) or median [interquartile range (IQR)], and categorical variables as n (%). Participants were divided into low, medium, and high groups according to PAPi tertiles. Group comparisons were performed using analysis of variance (ANOVA), Kruskal-Wallis H test, Chi-squared test, or Fisher’s exact test as appropriate, with Dunn’s test and Bonferroni correction for post hoc pairwise comparisons. Event-free survival was analyzed using the Kaplan-Meier method. Given the EPV ≥10 requirement, all candidate variables were screened by univariable analysis; Spearman correlation and variance inflation factor (VIF) were used to assess multicollinearity. Continuous variables were standardized, and least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression with 10-fold cross-validation was applied for predictor selection, followed by multivariable logistic regression with bootstrap (1,000 iterations) optimism correction. The consistency of PAPi’s predictive value was assessed using interaction tests in subgroup analyses. Three hierarchical models were constructed: Model 1 (EuroSCORE II), Model 2 (Model 1 + TAPSE/PASP), and Model 3 (Model 2 + PAPi). Model performance was evaluated by receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves, net reclassification improvement (NRI), integrated discrimination improvement (IDI), and Akaike information criterion/Bayesian information criterion (AIC/BIC). A nomogram, calibration curve, and decision curve were developed based on the optimal model. Analyses were performed using R 4.5.2 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria); two-tailed P<0.05 was considered significant.

Results

Baseline characteristics

A total of 302 patients were enrolled in this study. No statistically significant differences were observed between the included and excluded cohorts in age, sex, atrial fibrillation, EuroSCORE II, PASP, RAP, RV function parameters (TAPSE, TAPSE/PASP, and PAPi), LVEF, or MACE incidence (all P>0.05) (Table S1).

Among the 302 included patients (mean age 51.9±11.6 years; 62.3% male), the low PAPi group exhibited a higher prevalence of atrial fibrillation, worse New York Heart Association (NYHA) functional class (with a greater proportion in class III–IV), and elevated N-terminal pro-B-type natriuretic peptide (NT-proBNP) levels compared with the other two groups (all P<0.05). Medication history did not differ significantly among the three groups. EuroSCORE II scores increased with decreasing PAPi (P<0.001) (Table 1). Post hoc pairwise comparisons revealed that proBNP (H =16.529, P<0.001, ε2 =0.055) and EuroSCORE II (H =17.554, P<0.001, ε2 =0.058) were significantly elevated in the low PAPi group compared with both the medium and high PAPi groups (Table S2).

Surgical approach and concomitant tricuspid valvuloplasty rates were comparable across the groups. Surgical procedures included valve repair in 190 patients (62.7%) and valve replacement in 112 patients (37.3%). All 190 patients underwent mitral valve repair with implantation of an annuloplasty ring (Edwards Physio or Sorin HP ring), among whom 88 (46.3%) received additional artificial chordae tendineae. No statistically significant difference in MACE incidence was observed between the two surgical approaches (P=0.287), suggesting that repair complexity did not independently affect prognosis. Surgical access comprised minimally invasive surgery in 114 patients (37.8%) and sternotomy in 188 patients (62.2%), with no statistically significant difference in MACE incidence between the two approaches (P=0.146), indicating that surgical access did not significantly influence postoperative outcomes (Table 1).

Echocardiographic findings

The 302 patients with MR were categorized by etiology into degenerative (63.2%, n=191), rheumatic (24.8%, n=75), congenital (7.0%, n=21), and mixed (5.0%, n=15) subtypes. According to Carpentier’s functional classification, these patients were classified as type I (9.0%, n=27), type II (59.0%, n=178), type III (22.7%, n=69), and type IV (9.3%, n=28), with no statistically significant difference among the groups (P=0.113). Low PAPi patients demonstrated larger right heart dimensions, impaired RV function (lower 2D-RVEF, FAC, and TAPSE, TAPSE/PASP), higher pulmonary pressures, and a higher proportion of moderate-to-severe TR (all P<0.05) (Table 2). Post hoc pairwise comparisons revealed a stepwise increase in TAPSE across PAPi tertiles (H =34.309, P<0.001, ε2 =0.114), with all pairwise comparisons reaching statistical significance. TAPSE/PASP was significantly lower in the low PAPi group compared with both the medium and high PAPi groups (H =20.254, P<0.001, ε2 =0.067). RVEF differed significantly only between the low and high PAPi groups (H =11.416, P=0.003, ε2 =0.038). However, no significant pairwise differences were observed for FAC (H =6.572, P=0.037, ε2 =0.022) or TDI-s' (H =6.727, P=0.035, ε2 =0.022) after Bonferroni correction (all adjusted P>0.017) (Table S2).

All three groups showed enlarged left atrial and left ventricular dimensions with preserved LVEF and left ventricular fractional shortening (LVFS). The low PAPi group had greater left atrial anteroposterior diameter than the intermediate and high PAPi groups (P=0.002). However, no significant differences were observed among the three groups in left atrial transverse diameter, left ventricular size and volume, left ventricular systolic function parameters [LVEF, LVFS, stroke volume (SV), and CO], or mitral regurgitant volume (all P>0.05) (Table 2).

Clinical outcomes

MACE incidence increased markedly across tertiles: 37.4% (low), 10.9% (intermediate), 3.9% (high) (χ2=43.70, P<0.001). Low PAPi was associated with prolonged mechanical ventilation, inotropic support, and ICU stay (all P<0.001) (Table 3). The MACE distribution showed that low CO syndrome was the most frequent component, followed by all-cause death, acute kidney injury, malignant arrhythmia, bleeding complications, heart failure readmission, postoperative cerebral infarction, and repeat mitral valve surgery. Most components occurred more frequently in the low PAPi group (Table S3). However, because the number of individual events was small, we did not perform separate multivariable models or competing-risk analyses for each component, as such analyses would be statistically underpowered and potentially unstable.

Predictive value

ROC analysis demonstrated that PAPi predicted MACE with an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.82 [95% confidence interval (CI): 0.76–0.88], outperforming TAPSE (0.68), FAC (0.65), 2D-RVEF (0.67), and LVEF (0.54) (Figure 5). At the optimal cutoff value of 3.45, PAPi demonstrated a sensitivity of 81%, specificity of 75%, positive predictive value (PPV) of 40.0%, and negative predictive value (NPV) of 94.9%. Bootstrap validation confirmed the stability of these findings (bootstrap AUC 0.82, 95% CI: 0.761–0.878) (Table S4, Figure S1). The high NPV of 94.9% indicates that PAPi >3.45 effectively rules out MACE, whereas the low PPV of 40.0% reflects a substantial false-positive rate, underscoring the need for integrated clinical assessment with additional indicators in practice.

Discussion

This study establishes PAPi as an independent predictor of perioperative MACE in MR patients, providing incremental value beyond conventional risk scores. MACE incidence increased progressively with decreasing PAPi (3.9%, 10.9%, and 37.4%), reflecting the hemodynamic consequences of chronic MR (20,21): left atrial pressure elevation transmits to the pulmonary circulation, increasing RV afterload. PAPi captures this pathophysiology by integrating RV contractile reserve, pulmonary vascular impedance, and right atrial preload, identifying patients with exhausted hemodynamic reserve.

PAPi was originally established by Korabathina et al. (22) through right heart catheterization, based on invasive measurements of PASP, PADP, and RAP. Echocardiographic measurement is highly dependent on the quality of TR and PR Doppler signals; in patients with severe TR, continuous Doppler signals may appear “dagger-shaped” or have incomplete contours, leading to underestimation of PASP, whereas right atrial remodeling and compliance changes also affect the accuracy of RAP estimation. Existing studies have confirmed a positive correlation between echocardiographic and invasive PAPi—Kane et al. demonstrated excellent correlation between non-invasive and invasive pulmonary artery pressure and PAPi measurements in a subgroup of patients with moderate-to-severe TR who underwent echocardiography and right heart catheterization within 24 hours. However, echocardiography tends to underestimate RAP, particularly when markedly elevated (23). A prospective synchronous right heart catheterization study by Kim et al. in 2025 confirmed that the traditional IVC method for estimating RAP showed good agreement with invasive measurements at low RAP values (3 and 8 mmHg), but at estimated RAP (eRAP) =15 mmHg, actual invasive values ranged from 4 to 26 mmHg, indicating substantial discrepancy (24). This study proposed a novel algorithm incorporating V-wave cutoff sign, IVC collapse index <20%, and hepatic venous systolic flow reversal; using this algorithm, the correlation between estimated and catheter-derived PASP improved from r=0.85 to r=0.90, with SD decreasing from 5.2 to 2.4, thereby enhancing estimation accuracy in patients with severe TR. This method is expected to further optimize echocardiographic protocols, enabling broader application in clinical practice.

The EuroSCORE II, a classical cardiac surgery risk stratification model, holds a pivotal position in prognostic assessment. Previous investigations have validated its association with postoperative delirium incidence and severity (25), and have identified it as an independent predictor of cardiac surgical outcomes alongside age and three-dimensional (3D) RVEF (5). Consistent with previous findings, the association remained significant after multivariate analysis in this study.

The superiority of coupling indices (PAPi, TAPSE/PASP) over single parameters (TAPSE, FAC, 2D-RVEF) aligns with their comprehensive assessment of ventricular-vascular interaction. TAPSE is load-dependent and limited to longitudinal function; guidelines (26) recommend combining it with PASP (TAPSE/PASP <0.3 mm/mmHg indicates RV-pulmonary artery decoupling). This study confirms the independent prognostic value of this coupling index, consistent with prior findings (27-30). FAC requires optimal image quality, often necessitating contrast enhancement for endocardial border visualization (26). 2D-RVEF has been removed from the 2025 ASE guidelines due to limitations in geometric assumption bias, strong image quality dependence, and high interobserver variability, with 3D-RVEF, which shows better correlation with cardiac magnetic resonance (CMR), now recommended instead (26,31).

We observed significant left atrial and ventricular enlargement across all three patient groups; however, left ventricular function remained within the normal range, with left heart functional indices (LVEF) demonstrating no statistically significant differences in this study. This finding reflects the characteristic pathophysiological remodeling of MR: during the compensatory phase, the ventricular wall maintains normal ejection fraction through the Frank-Starling (32,33) mechanism until decompensation occurs. Consistent with Posada-Martinez et al. (5), preoperative 3D RVEF predicted in-hospital mortality or circulatory support [hazard ratio (HR) =0.95, 95% CI: 0.91–0.98, P=0.003], whereas LVEF did not (P>0.05). This underscores that right heart function, despite being underutilized, may be more dynamically variable and prognostically sensitive than LVEF in post-cardiac surgery patients.

Baseline characteristics of low PAPi patients indicated more severe overall disease status, including worse NYHA functional class, higher NT-proBNP levels, more frequent atrial fibrillation, higher EuroSCORE II, elevated pulmonary artery pressures, higher RAP, impaired RV function parameters, and more severe TR. This significant baseline imbalance suggests that PAPi may not be an independent causal risk factor, but rather a biomarker of advanced cardiovascular disease and RV dysfunction, analogous to the role of NT-proBNP as a heart failure biomarker: although elevated levels strongly predict adverse outcomes, the therapeutic target is not direct reduction of NT-proBNP, but correction of the underlying neurohormonal activation and ventricular wall stress. Therefore, the observed association between PAPi and MACE in this study should be cautiously interpreted as a prognostic biomarker relationship rather than simple causal inference. Accordingly, the clinical implication lies more in identifying high-risk patients requiring closer monitoring and more aggressive intervention, rather than serving as a direct therapeutic target. For patients with low PAPi, clinical decisions should focus on correcting reversible right heart loading factors (e.g., pulmonary hypertension treatment, TR surgical evaluation, volume management) rather than solely pursuing improvement in PAPi values.

Our study identified a PAPi cutoff value of 3.45 in MR patients, positioned intermediate between acute and chronic disease states. Previously reported thresholds include: ≤2.0 in acute systolic heart failure (34), <1.5 in cardiogenic shock complicating acute myocardial infarction (35), and approximately 4.0 in pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH), where PAPi demonstrates a curvilinear relationship with prognosis (8). Chronic thromboembolic pulmonary hypertension (CTEPH) patients exhibit a predictive cutoff of 3.9 (36). This reflects the unique dual pressure-volume overload characteristics inherent to MR. Collectively, PAPi thresholds may be regarded as critical tipping points for right heart-pulmonary circulation decoupling across diverse pathologies. The observed disease heterogeneity mandates that clinical application strictly consider disease-specific context, integrating acute versus chronic status, overload nature, and primary versus secondary etiologies for comprehensive interpretation, thereby precluding simplistic extrapolation.

Currently, preoperative risk assessment relying solely on traditional surgical risk scoring systems still exhibits certain limitations. The integrated model incorporating PAPi demonstrated superior predictive performance, with C-statistic improving from 0.70 to 0.85. The NRI and IDI also demonstrated incremental improvements over the baseline EuroSCORE II model. From a practical standpoint, this may help to more reliably identify patients at genuinely high risk while avoiding overtreatment in lower-risk populations. Assessment of echocardiography-derived PAPi may be explored as part of refined perioperative risk stratification in future prospective studies.

Conclusions

The echocardiography-derived PAPi can predict perioperative MACE in patients with MR. Incorporating this index into traditional risk scoring systems provides additional value for preoperative risk assessment, and routine preoperative evaluation may help to identify high-risk patients. Further prospective studies are warranted for validation.

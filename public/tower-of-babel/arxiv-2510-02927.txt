Nodal resolution of quasiregular curves via bubble trees Thanks: This work was supported in part by the Academy of Finland project #332671 and Center of Excellence FiRST
Pekka Pankka, Jonathan Pim

Abstract
. We prove a version of Gromov’s compactness theorem for quasiregular curves into calibrated manifolds with bounded geometry. In our main theorem, given an n n -dimensional calibration ω \omega on manifold N N , we associate to a weak- ⋆ \star limit μ = lim k → ∞ ⋆ F k ∗ ω \mu=\lim_{k\to\infty}\star F_{k}^{*}\omega of measures induced by a sequence ( F k : X → N ) k ∈ ℕ (F_{k}\colon X\to N)_{k\in\mathbb{N}} of K K -quasiregular ω \omega -curves on a nodal manifold X X , a bubble tree X ^ \widehat{X} over X X , a sequence of mappings ( F ^ ℓ : X → N ) ℓ ∈ ℕ (\widehat{F}_{\ell}\colon X\to N)_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}} converging locally uniformly to a quasiregular curve F ^ : X ^ → N \widehat{F}\colon\widehat{X}\to N which realizes the measure μ \mu , that is, μ = π ∗ ( ⋆ F ^ ∗ ω ) \mu=\pi_{*}(\star\widehat{F}^{*}\omega) , where π : X ^ → X \pi\colon\widehat{X}\to X is the natural projection. We call the sequence ( F ^ ℓ ) ℓ ∈ ℕ (\widehat{F}_{\ell})_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}} a nodal resolution of the sequence ( F k ) k ∈ ℕ (F_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}} . As a corollary we obtain a normality criterion for families of quasiregular curves. Classic interpretations of bubbling via Gromov–Hausdorff convergence and pinching maps also follow.

1.
Introduction

It is a classical theorem in complex analysis that a locally uniform limit
f\colon\Sigma\to\Sigma^{\prime}
of a sequence of holomorphic maps
(f_{k}\colon\Sigma\to\Sigma^{\prime})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
between closed Riemannian surfaces is holomorphic. It is similarly classical that the degree is preserved at the limit. More precisely, if
f
is a non-constant, then
\deg f
is the limit of the degrees
k\mapsto\deg f_{k}
by Hurwitz’s theorem. Gromov’s compactness theorem
[
12
]
gives a converse for this classical result:

Let
(f_{k}\colon\Sigma\to\Sigma^{\prime})
be a sequence of holomorphic maps of uniformly bounded degree between Riemann surfaces. Then there exists a subsequence
(f_{k_{j}})
of
(f_{k})
which converges in the nodal sense to holomorphic map
f\colon X\to\Sigma^{\prime}
from a nodal manifold
X
over
\Sigma
. The limit of the degrees of maps
f_{k_{j}}
is the sum of the degrees of the maps
f|_{S}\colon S\to\Sigma^{\prime}
for the strata
S
of
X
.

In
[
12
]
Gromov showed more generally that, for pseudoholomorphic curves, area bounds yield sequential compactness if the local uniform convergence is understood in the more general sense of nodal convergence;
see e.g. Pansu’s
[
1
]
, Wolfson
[
36
]
, Ruan–Tian
[
34
]
, Hummel
[
18
]
, and Bourgeois–Eliashberg–Hofer–Wysocki–Zehnder
[
3
]
for proofs of Gromov’s theorem for pseudoholomorphic curves.
The aforementioned nodal convergence results also hold in many other settings. For example, see Parker
[
32
]
for harmonic maps under energy bounds, Meier–Vikman–Wenger
[
26
]
for harmonic maps into metric spaces, Cheng–Karigiannis–Madnick
[
9
]
for associative Smith maps, Ivrii
[
22
]
for meromorphic functions, and

[
31
]
for quasiregular mappings.

Recall that a continuous mapping
f\colon M\to N
between oriented Riemannian
n
-manifolds is
K
-quasiregular for
K\geqslant 1
if
f
belongs to the Sobolev space
W^{1,n}_{{\operatorname{loc}}}(M,N)
and satisfies the distortion inequality
\lVert Df\rVert^{n}\leqslant KJ_{f}
almost everywhere in
M
, where
\lVert Df\rVert
is the pointwise operator norm and
J_{f}=\star f^{*}\mathrm{vol}_{N}
is the Jacobian determinant of the differential
Df
of
f
. In this terminology, a
K
-quasiregular homeomorphism is called
K
-quasiconformal. See e.g. Rickman’s monograph
[
33
]
for the theory of quasiregular mappings.

In this article, we prove Gromov’s compactness theorem for quasiregular curves into calibrated manifolds. We recall the terminology after the following statement; for a statement without the compactness assumptions on the manifolds, see Theorem
1.2
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
2\leqslant n\leqslant m
, let
X
be a closed, connected, oriented Riemannian nodal
n
-manifold, let
(N,\omega)
be a closed
n
-calibrated Riemannian
m
-manifold, and
K\geqslant 1
. Let
(F_{k}\colon X\to N)_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of
K
-quasiregular
\omega
-curves for which
(\star F_{k}^{*}\omega)_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is a bounded sequence of measures in
X
. Then there exists a compact bubble tree
\widehat{X}
over
X
, a sequence
(\widehat{F}_{j}\colon\widehat{X}\to N)_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
of continuous
W^{1,n}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\widehat{X},N)
maps converging locally uniformly to a
K
-quasiregular
\omega
-curve
\widehat{F}\colon\widehat{X}\to N
, and a subsequence
(F_{k_{j}})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
of
(F_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
for which

F_{k_{j}}|_{X\setminus P}\to\widehat{F}|_{X\setminus P}\text{ locally uniformly}\text{ and }\star F_{k_{j}}^{*}\omega\overset{*}{\rightharpoonup}(\pi_{\widehat{X},X})_{*}(\star\widehat{F}^{*}\omega)

as
j\to\infty
, where

is a finite set.

The interpretation of the limit map

is twofold. On one hand,
\widehat{F}
may be seen as a weak replacement of the classical locally uniform limit, made possible by enlarging the domain of the map. On the other hand,
\widehat{F}
may be seen as a resolution of the singular parts of the limiting measure of the sequence
(\star F^{*}_{k_{j}}\omega)_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
.

1.1.
Existence of bubbles; normal families

One interpretation of Theorem
1.2
is that, by passing from the original domain
X
of the sequence
(F_{k}\colon X\to N)_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
to the bubble tree
\widehat{X}
, we find a sequence converging locally uniformly to a quasiregular
\omega
-curve
\widehat{F}\colon\widehat{X}\to N
. For a non-trivial bubble tree
\widehat{X}
, that is, a bubble tree having a bubble
S\in\mathrm{Strata}_{X}(\widehat{X})
, restrictions of
\widehat{F}
to bubbles yield non-constant quasiregular
\omega
-curves
\mathbb{S}^{n}\to N
.

For the forthcoming statements, we say that a calibration
\omega\in\mathrm{Cal}^{n}_{+}(N)
is
spherical
if there exists a smooth map

for which
\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}f^{*}\omega\neq 0
.
In particular, for a non-spherical calibration
\omega\in\mathrm{Cal}^{n}_{+}(N)
, we have that
\widehat{X}=X
and

in Theorem

\mathsf{QR}_{K,\omega}(X,N)
of

-quasiregular
\omega
-curves

.

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
2\leqslant n\leqslant m
and let
(N,\omega)
be an
n
-calibrated closed Riemannian

-manifold, where

-manifold

As a particular application of Corollary

, we consider quasiregular curves into the complex projective spaces

be a
(2k)

By Corollary

1.3.
Outline of the proof

The method in
[
9
]
is not directly at our disposal as
K
-quasiregular curves, contrary to Smith maps, are not
n
-harmonic. It also differs from the method in
[
31
]
, though a pinching map interpretation is discussed in Section
10
.

As mentioned earlier in this introduction, local uniform limits
F\colon X\to N
of
K
-quasiregular
\omega
-curves are also
K
-quasiregular
\omega
-curves and the associated measures
\star F^{*}\omega
are weak-
\star
limits of the corresponding measures of the curves in the sequence. Together with the removability of point singularities and a nodal surgery extension of the sequence at nodal points, we iteratively resolve the singular parts of the limiting measures. Indeed, as usual in proofs of Gromov’s compactness theorem, given a sequence
(F_{k}\colon X\to N)_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
of
K
-quasiregular
\omega
-curves satisfying local integrability bounds, the measures
\star F_{k}^{*}\omega
have a weakly converging subsequence, whose singular part consists of discrete point masses.

We resolve each point mass by attaching a spherical stratum
\mathbb{S}^{n}
to each of these singular points and replace the sequence
(F_{k})
by a new sequence
(F^{1}_{k}\colon X_{1}\to N)_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
, where
X_{1}
is the bubble tree over
X
given by the new spheres. Then this process is repeated for the sequence
(F^{1}_{k})
. Together with a renormalization of the new maps on the bubbles, this leads to an iterative construction of the bubble tree
X_{1}\subset X_{2}\subset\cdots\subset\widetilde{X}
and a sequence of maps
\widetilde{X}\to N
having a locally uniformly converging subsequence

(\widehat{F}_{\ell}\colon\widehat{X}\to N)_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}}
, where
\widehat{X}
is obtained from

by removing the bubbles in high enough strata

An advantage of this strategy is that the resulting bubble trees have no necks and consist of spherical strata by construction.
However, a new technical difficulty arises as the maps

in the new sequences are typically not quasiregular

-curves. Therefore, for the induction step of the proof,
we introduce a notion of

-quasiregular sequences
in Definition

and give the iterative argument in terms of such sequences instead of sequences of quasiregular curves. We call sequences of maps from bubble trees

nodal pre-resolutions of
(F_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
and the final sequence
(\widehat{F}_{\ell}\colon\widehat{X}\to N)_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}}
a

(F_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
.
We refer to Definition

for the terminology and merely note here that the asymptotic formulation of Theorem

in terms of sequences
(\widehat{F}_{\ell}\colon\widehat{X}\to N)_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}}

2.
Preliminaries

Regarding Riemannian manifolds, we use the following notations for injectivity radii. Let
M
be a Riemannian
n
-manifold and
p\in M
. We denote
B_{M}(p,r)
the metric ball of radius
r
about

in

. Similarly, we denote
B_{T_{p}M}(0,r)
the metric ball in the tangent space
T_{p}M
. We also denote
\exp_{p}\colon T_{p}M\to M
the exponential map at

. As usual the injectivity radius
\operatorname{inj\,rad}_{M}(p)
of

at

is the number

\operatorname{inj\,rad}_{M}(p)=\sup\{r>0\colon\exp_{p}|_{B_{T_{p}M}(0,r)}\text{ is injective}\}.

Since the bilipschitz and quasiconformality constants of the restrictions
\exp_{p}|_{B_{T_{p}M}(0,r)}\colon B_{T_{p}M}(0,r)\to B_{M}(p,r)
tend to

as

, we may define, for
\varepsilon>0
, the bilipschitz injectivity radii
\mathrm{BLrad}_{\varepsilon}(M,p)
and the quasiconformal injectivity radii
\mathrm{QCrad}_{\varepsilon}(M,p)
of

at

by the formulas

\mathrm{BLrad}_{\varepsilon}(M,p)=\sup\{r>0\colon\exp_{p}|_{B_{T_{p}M}(0,r)}\text{ is $(1+\varepsilon)$-bilipschitz}\}

and

\mathrm{QCrad}_{\varepsilon}(M,p)=\sup\{r>0\colon\exp_{p}|_{B_{T_{p}M}(0,r)}\text{ is $(1+\varepsilon)$-quasiconformal}\}.

Recall that an
L
-bilipschitz homeomorphism is
L^{2n}
-quasiconformal.

If

has bounded geometry, the bilipschitz and quasiconformal injectivity radii
\mathrm{BLrad}_{\varepsilon}(M,p)
and
\mathrm{QCrad}_{\varepsilon}(M,p)
are uniformly bounded from below away from zero. Indeed, suppose

has sectional curvature
\kappa
bounded by
\kappa_{N}
. Then the classical Rauch comparison theorem yields, for each
\varepsilon>0
, a uniform radius
r_{N,\varepsilon}>0
, depending only on
\kappa_{N}
and
\varepsilon>0
, for which the exponential mapping
\exp_{p}\colon T_{p}N\to N
is
(1+\varepsilon)
-bilipschitz in a ball

. See e.g. monographs of Buser and Karcher
[
7
, Section 6.4]
or Chavel
[
8
, Theorem IX.2.3]
or e.g. article of Dyer, Vegter, and Wintraecken
[
11
, Lemma 9]
. We conclude that, under bounded geometry assumption,

\inf_{p\in M}\mathrm{BLrad}_{\varepsilon}(M,p)>0\quad\text{and}\quad\inf_{p\in M}\mathrm{QCrad}_{\varepsilon}(M,p)>0.

Hölder continuity and removability of point singularities

Regarding Hölder continuity, we have the following version of Ikonen’s Hölder continuity theorem for quasiregular curves with locally small energy. For the statement we define the following constant associated to Hölder bounds of quasiregular curves of small energy.

Let

2.2.
Precomposition of curves by quasiregular mappings

As a preliminary result, we show that the pre-composition of a quasiregular

\omega
-curve with a quasiregular mapping is a quasiregular
\omega
-curve. This is a counterpart of the classical result that the composition of quasiregular mappings is quasiregular. We adapt the analytic proof of Bojarski and Iwaniec
[
2
]
.
We shall only need this for the case of
1
-quasiconformal mappings between nodal manifolds, however we state the general version for future convenience. The nodal manifold version is obtained by applying the result to each strata.

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
N
,
M
, and
\Sigma
be connected and oriented Riemannian manifolds of dimension
m
,
n
and
n
respectively, and let
\omega\in\Omega^{n}(N)
be a closed, non-vanishing
n
-form, for
m\geqslant n\geqslant 2
.
If
\varphi\colon M\to\Sigma
is a
K_{\varphi}
-quasiregular mapping and
F\colon\Sigma\to N
is a
K
-quasiregular
\omega
-curve, then
F\circ\varphi\colon M\to N
is a
(K_{\varphi}K)
-quasiregular
\omega
-curve.

We begin by showing that the composition is in the correct Sobolev space.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
N
,
M
, and
\Sigma
be connected and oriented Riemannian manifolds of dimension
m
,
n
and
n
respectively.
If
\varphi\colon M\to\Sigma
is a quasiregular mapping and
F\in W^{1,n}_{\text{loc}}(\Sigma,N)
, then
F\circ\varphi\in W^{1,n}_{\text{loc}}(M,N)
and
D(F\circ\varphi)=DF\circ D\varphi
almost everywhere in
M
.

Proof.

Let
\iota\colon N\to\mathbb{R}^{n^{\prime}}
be a Nash embedding, and let
(V,\psi)
and
(U,\sigma)
be
2
-bilipschitz charts in
\Sigma
and
M
respectively for which
\varphi(U)\subset V
.
Thus
\widetilde{\varphi}=\psi\circ\varphi\circ\sigma^{-1}\in W^{1,n}_{\text{loc}}(\sigma U,\psi V)
and
\iota\circ F\in W^{1,n}_{\text{loc}}(\Sigma,\mathbb{R}^{n^{\prime}})
which implies
\widetilde{F}=\iota\circ F\circ\psi^{-1}\in W^{1,n}_{\text{loc}}(\psi V,\mathbb{R}^{n^{\prime}})
.
Since a composition of a quasiregular mapping with a bilipschitz mapping is quasiregular, the map
\widetilde{\varphi}
is quasiregular.
Then by
[
2
, Lemma 9.6]
, the fact that
\psi
and
\sigma
are bilipschitz, and by
[
24
, Corollary 5.5 and Lemma 6.3]
,
we have that

\widetilde{F}\circ\widetilde{\varphi}=\iota\circ F\circ\varphi\circ\sigma^{-1}\in W^{1,n}_{\text{loc}}(\sigma U,\mathbb{R}^{n^{n\prime}})
,

D(\widetilde{F}\circ\widetilde{\varphi})=D\widetilde{F}\circ D\widetilde{\varphi}=D\iota\circ DF\circ D\varphi\circ D\sigma^{-1}
, and

D(\widetilde{F}\circ\widetilde{\varphi})=D(\iota\circ F\circ\varphi\circ\sigma^{-1})=D\iota\circ D(F\circ\varphi)\circ D\sigma^{-1}
.
Hence,
D(F\circ\varphi)=DF\circ D\varphi
almost everywhere.
∎

Proof of Proposition
2.7
.

Lemma
2.8
implies that
F\circ\varphi\in W^{1,n}_{\text{loc}}(M,N)
and
D(F\circ\varphi)=DF\circ D\varphi
almost everywhere in
M
. So it suffices to show that
F\circ\varphi
satisfies the distortion inequality.

We have that
(\lVert\omega\rVert\circ F)\lVert DF\rVert^{n}\leqslant K\star F^{*}\omega
almost everywhere in
\Sigma
, and
\lVert D\varphi\rVert^{n}\leqslant K_{\varphi}(\star\varphi^{*}\mathrm{vol}_{\Sigma})
almost everywhere in
M
. Since
\varphi
satisfies the Lusin
(N^{-1})
condition, we obtain that

(\lVert\omega\rVert\circ F)_{\varphi(x)}\lVert DF_{\varphi(x)}\rVert^{n}\leqslant K\star(F^{*}\omega)_{\varphi(x)}
for almost every
x\in M
.
Thus

\displaystyle(\lVert\omega\rVert\circ(F\circ\varphi))_{x}\lVert D(F\circ\varphi)_{x}\rVert^{n}

\displaystyle\leqslant(\lVert\omega\rVert\circ F)_{\varphi(x)}\lVert DF_{\varphi(x)}\rVert^{n}\lVert D\varphi_{x}\rVert^{n}

\displaystyle\leqslant(KK_{\varphi})\left((\star F^{*}\omega)\circ\varphi\right)(\star\varphi^{*}\mathrm{vol}_{\Sigma})

\displaystyle=(KK_{\varphi})\left(\star(\varphi^{*}(\star F^{*}\omega)\varphi^{*}\mathrm{vol}_{\Sigma})\right)

\displaystyle=(KK_{\varphi})\left(\star(\varphi^{*}\left((\star F^{*}\omega)\mathrm{vol}_{\Sigma})\right)\right)

\displaystyle=(KK_{\varphi})\left(\star(\varphi^{*}(F^{*}\omega)\right)

\displaystyle=(KK_{\varphi})\left(\star((F\circ\varphi)^{*}\omega)\right).

This concludes the proof.
∎

For the reader’s convenience, we recall here the following weak compactness theorem for Radon measures. This follows from the the Banach–Alaoglu Theorem by a standard diagonal argument and the Riesz Representation Theorem; see

. We shall apply this to the measures

Theorem 2.9

(Weak Compactness of Radon Measures)
.

Let
X
be a locally compact and
\sigma
-compact Hausdorff space. Suppose
(\mu_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence of Radon measures satisfying
\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\mu_{k}(K)<\infty
for all compact subsets

. Then
(\mu_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
has a subsequence
(\mu_{k_{j}})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
and there exists a Radon measure

on

such that
\mu_{k_{j}}\rightharpoonup\mu
in the weak-
\star

\mu(X)=\limsup_{j\to\infty}\mu_{k_{j}}(X)
.

2.4.
Nodal manifolds

We discuss some preliminaries related to nodal manifolds. Recall, from the introduction, that a metrizable space
X
is a nodal
n
-manifold if
X=\bigcup_{\alpha\in\Lambda}M_{\alpha}
, where each
M_{\alpha}
is an
n
-manifold and, for
\alpha\neq\beta
, the intersection
M_{\alpha}\cap M_{\beta}
is either a singleton or the empty set; such a manifold
M_{\alpha}
is called a
stratum of
X
and the points
p\in X
, which are shared by at least two different strata, are called the singular points of
X
. The set
\mathrm{Sing}(X)
of singular points of
X
, and hence also the set
\mathrm{Strata}(X)
of all strata of
X
, is uniquely determined.

In what follows, we assume that a nodal
n
-manifold
X
is connected, oriented, and Riemannian, in the sense that the strata of
X
are oriented and Riemannian. Then each stratum
M\in\mathrm{Strata}(X)
has Riemannian distance
d_{M}
. To define a length metric on
X
, we say that a path
\gamma\colon[0,1]\to X
is
admissible
if there exists an (admissible) partition
0=s_{0}<\cdots<s_{k}=1
of
[0,1]
for which
\gamma([s_{j-1},s_{j}])
is contained in a single stratum
M_{j}
of
X
. The length
\ell(\gamma)
of an admissible path is

\ell(\gamma)=\sup_{0=s_{1}<\cdots<s_{k}=1}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\ell_{M_{j}}(\gamma|_{[s_{j-1},s_{j}]}),

where the supremum is taken over all admissible partitions of
\gamma
; as usual, all admissible partitions yield the same sum of lengths. The length metric
d_{X}\colon X\times X\to[0,\infty)
on
X
is now given by formula

d_{X}(p,q)=\inf_{\gamma}\ell(\gamma),

where the infimum is taken over admissible paths
\gamma\colon[0,1]\to X
from
p
to
q
. In what follows, we denote, for
A\subset X
and
r>0
,

B_{X}(A,r)=\{x\in X\colon{{\operatorname{dist}\,}}_{X}(x,A)<r\}

the
r
-neighborhood of
A
in
X
.

As already mentioned in the introduction, we mainly consider bubble trees over nodal manifolds. More precisely, a bubble
n
-tree
\widehat{X}
is a nodal
n
-manifold for which there exists a nodal
n
-submanifold
X\subset\widehat{X}
having the property that strata of
\widehat{X}
which are not contained in
X
, that is, elements of
\mathrm{Strata}_{X}(\widehat{X})
, are
n
-spheres. We call a stratum
S
in
\mathrm{Strata}_{X}(\widehat{X})
a
bubble
.
This notation reflects the property that, in what follows, we equip each bubble with a Riemannian metric making it isometric to a copy of
\mathbb{S}^{n}_{p}:=\mathbb{S}^{n}\times\{p\}
of the standard Euclidean unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n}
attached to
X
by identifying
p
and the south pole
-e_{n+1}
of
\mathbb{S}^{n}
. In what follows, we also call the ball
\mathbb{S}^{n}_{p,+}=\{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n+1},p)\in\mathbb{S}^{n}\times\{p\}\colon x_{n+1}\geqslant 0\}\subset\mathbb{S}^{n}_{p}
, the
upper hemisphere
\mathbb{S}^{n}_{p,+}
of a bubble
\mathbb{S}^{n}_{p}
.

2.5.
Quasiregular curves from nodal manifolds

As in the introduction, we define differential forms on nodal manifolds stratum-wise. More formally, we call an indexed family
\omega=(\omega_{M})_{M\in\mathrm{Strata}(X)}
a

-form on

if each
\omega_{M}
is a differential

-form

. Note that, at a nodal point

In what follows, we denote the form
\omega_{M}
in the family

As in the introduction, we also say that a continuous mapping

from a connected, oriented, and Riemannian nodal

-manifold, for

, is a

-quasiregular

-curve for

if, for each stratum

\omega

Following the convention above, we denote

and use the notation

Note that the sum is defined, since the functions

, and hence also their integrals, are non-negative. We extend this notational convention to all continuous

-mappings

for which the integrals

,

3.
Asymptotically quasiregular sequences

Later when we extend sequences of quasiregular curves to a bubble tree by modifying them in the neighborhood of a singular set, the maps in the resulting sequence will unfortunately, in general, no longer satisfy the distortion inequality in those neighborhoods. However with this operation, called nodal surgery (defined in Section
7.1
) we may choose the neighborhoods of non-quasiregularity in such a way that they shrink to the singular set in the limit. In this sense this new sequence from the bubble tree is asymptotically quasiregular. We formalize this idea in Definition
3.1
and Definition
3.2
.
For these reasons in what follows we work with these intermediate classes of objects instead of sequences of quasiregular curves.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
X
be an oriented and Riemannian nodal

-manifold and let
(N,\omega)
be an
n
-calibrated Riemannian

-manifold for

. A sequence
(F_{k}\colon X\to N)_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
of continuous

-mappings admits a
(K,\omega)
-quasiregular exhaustion about a discrete set

if there exists a sequence
\varepsilon_{k}\downarrow 0
having the following properties: for each neighborhood

of

k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
and a neighborhood

is a
(1+\varepsilon_{k})K
-quasiregular
\omega
-curve for

.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
X
be an oriented and Riemannian nodal

-manifold and let
(N,\omega)
be an
n
-calibrated Riemannian
m
-manifold for

. A sequence
(F_{k}\colon X\to N)_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
of continuous

admits a
(K,\omega)

the sequence
(F_{k}|_{X\setminus P}\colon X\setminus P\to N)_{k\in\mathbb{N}}

In what follows, we restrict ourselves to asymptotically quasiregular sequences
(F_{k}\colon X\to N)_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
for which

and

Since locally uniform limits of

\omega

-quasiregular
\omega

Additionally for the proof, we say that a sequence

is

if there exists

and a neighborhood

of
p
for which the restriction
F_{k}|_{V}\colon V\to N
is a quasiregular curve for
k\geqslant k_{0}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
2\leqslant n\leqslant m
, let
X
be an oriented and Riemannian nodal
n
-manifold, and let
(N,\omega)
be an
n
-calibrated Riemannian
m
-manifold.
Let
(F_{k}\colon X\to N)_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be an asymptotically
(K,\omega)
-quasiregular sequence with
(F_{k}|_{X\setminus P})_{k}
converging locally uniformly where
P\subset X
is a discrete set.
Then the sequence
(F_{k}|_{X\setminus P}\colon X\setminus P\to N)_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
converges locally uniformly to a
K
-quasiregular
\omega
-curve
F\colon X\setminus P\to N
which satisfies

(3.1)

\int_{E}\lVert DF\rVert^{n}\leqslant\liminf_{k\to\infty}\int_{E}\lVert DF_{k}\rVert^{n}

for each compact subset
E\subset X
.

Proof.

Let
Q\subset X\setminus P
be the discrete set about which
(F_{k})_{k}
admits a
(K,\omega)
-quasiregular exhaustion. Set
\widetilde{P}=Q\cup P
.
By definition, the sequence
(F_{k}|_{X\setminus P}\colon X\setminus P\to N)_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is quasiregular at each point in
X\setminus Q
and converges locally uniformly to a continuous map

F\colon X\setminus P\to N
.
Observe that the same holds when
P
is replaced by
\widetilde{P}
.
By
[
30
, Theorem 1.9]
,
F|_{X\setminus\widetilde{P}}
is a
K
-quasiregular
\omega
-curve, which – by Theorem
2.1
– extends over the discrete set
Q
to a
K
-quasiregular
\omega
-curve
F\colon X\setminus P\to N
.

Let
E\subset X
be a compact set. First consider the special case that
E
is contained in a single stratum
M\in\mathrm{Strata}(X)
.
We split this case into two cases: (i)
E\cap\widetilde{P}=\varnothing
, and (ii)
E\cap\widetilde{P}\neq\varnothing
.
The general case follows from the observation that
E\setminus\mathrm{Sing}(X)
meets only finitely many strata.

Case (i): Since
E\cap\widetilde{P}=\varnothing
, there exists a neighborhood
U
of
\widetilde{P}
for which
E\cap U=\varnothing
. Then there exists
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
and a neighborhood
V\subset\overline{V}\subset U
of
\widetilde{P}
such that for all
k\geqslant k_{0}
,
F_{k}|_{M\setminus\overline{V}}
is a
(1+\varepsilon_{k})K
-quasiregular
\omega
-curve.
Let
\varepsilon>0
be small enough that
B_{M}(E,\varepsilon)\subset M\setminus\overline{V}
.
Let
\mathcal{U}
be the collection of closed balls
\overline{B}_{M}(p,r)
with
p\in E
and
2r\leqslant\varepsilon
, for which
B(p,2r)
is contained in a
(1+\varepsilon)
-bilipschitz chart at
p
and
F(B_{M}(p,2r))
is contained in a
(1+\varepsilon)
-bilipschitz chart at
F(p)
.

Since Riemannian manifolds are Vitali spaces
[
17
, Theorem 3.4.3]
,
\mathcal{U}
contains a countable and pairwise disjoint subfamily
\mathcal{U}_{0}\subset\mathcal{U}
for which
\mathrm{vol}_{M}\left(E\setminus\bigcup_{B\in\mathcal{U}_{0}}B\right)=0
.

For each
B\in\mathcal{U}_{0}
, let
\varphi_{B}
and
\psi_{B}
be
(1+\varepsilon)
-bilipschitz charts containing
B
and
F(B)\cup\bigcup_{k\geqslant k_{0}}F_{k}(B)
, respectively. Thus the sequence
(\psi_{B}\circ F_{k}\circ\varphi_{B}^{-1})_{k\geqslant k_{0}}
converges locally uniformly to
\psi_{B}\circ F\circ\varphi_{B}^{-1}
. By
[
30
, Theorem 1.9]
,
\psi_{B}\circ F\circ\varphi_{B}^{-1}
is a
(1+\varepsilon)^{4n}K
-quasiregular
\omega
-curve.
Furthermore, by a change of variables,
[
30
, Lemma 4.4]
, and Fatou’s lemma,

\displaystyle\int_{E}\lVert DF\rVert^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{B\in\mathcal{U}_{0}}\int_{B}\lVert DF\rVert^{n}\leqslant\sum_{B\in\mathcal{U}_{0}}(1+\varepsilon)^{3n}\int_{\varphi_{B}(B)}\lVert D(\psi_{B}\circ F\circ\varphi_{B}^{-1})\rVert^{n}

\displaystyle\leqslant(1+\varepsilon)^{3n}\sum_{B\in\mathcal{U}_{0}}\liminf_{k\to\infty}\int_{\varphi_{B}(B)}\lVert D(\psi_{B}\circ F_{k}\circ\varphi_{B}^{-1})\rVert^{n}

\displaystyle\leqslant(1+\varepsilon)^{6n}\sum_{B\in\mathcal{U}_{0}}\liminf_{k\to\infty}\int_{B}\lVert DF_{k}\rVert^{n}\leqslant(1+\varepsilon)^{6n}\liminf_{k\to\infty}\int_{E}\lVert DF_{k}\rVert^{n}.

Case (i) now follows.

For case (ii), i.e.
E\cap\widetilde{P}\neq\varnothing
, note that since
\widetilde{P}
is discrete, it suffices to consider the further special case:
E=\bar{B}(p,R)
and
E\cap\widetilde{P}=\{{p}\}
. Consider the closed annuli
E_{r}=E\setminus B(p,r)
for
0<r<R
. Observe that each
E_{r}
is compact and does not meet
\widetilde{P}
, so case (i) implies that

\int_{E_{r}}\lVert DF\rVert^{n}\leqslant\liminf_{k\to\infty}\int_{E_{r}}\lVert DF_{k}\rVert^{n}\leqslant\liminf_{k\to\infty}\int_{E\setminus\{p\}}\lVert DF_{k}\rVert^{n}=\liminf_{k\to\infty}\int_{E}\lVert DF_{k}\rVert^{n}

for all
0<r<R
. This case follows from observing that

\int_{E_{r}}\lVert DF\rVert^{n}\to\int_{E}\lVert DF\rVert^{n}

as
r\to 0
. The claim now follows.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

According to classical terminology, Lemma
3.3
yields that the sequence
(F_{k}\colon X\to Y)_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is
quasinormal
. Recall that a family
\mathcal{F}
of maps
X\to Y
is
quasinormal
if every sequence
(f_{k}\colon X\to Y)
in
\mathcal{F}
has a subsequence
(f_{k_{j}})
which converges locally uniformly in
X\setminus P
, where
P
is a discrete set. See e.g. Nevo, Pang, and Zalcman
[
29
]
for discussion on quasinormality for meromorphic functions.

We finish this section by recording an Arzelà–Ascoli type normality criterion for sequences admitting a quasiregular exhaustion. Recall that, by Corollary
2.6
, a family
\{F_{k}\colon X\to N\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
of
K
-quasiregular
\omega
-curves, having small locally equibounded energy, is locally uniformly
\alpha
-Hölder continuous, with
\alpha
depending only on the data. In particular, such a family is locally equicontinuous, and a fortiori normal by a standard Arzelà–Ascoli argument.

In the forthcoming sections, we construct locally equicontinuous families of Sobolev mappings
\{F_{k}\colon X\to N\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
admitting a quasiregular exhaustion about a discrete set. For this reason, we immediately state here a version of the Arzelà–Ascoli theorem specialized to this setting.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
2\leqslant n\leqslant m
, let
X
be an oriented and Riemannian nodal
n
-manifold, and let
(N,\omega)
be a complete
n
-calibrated Riemannian
m
-manifold.
Let
(F_{k}\colon X\to N)_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of continuous
W^{1,n}_{\operatorname{loc}}
-Sobolev mappings admitting a
(K,\omega)
-quasiregular exhaustion about a discrete set
Q\subset X

and let
P\subset X\setminus Q
be another discrete set.
Then
(F_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfies the following two conditions:

(1)

the family
\{F_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is locally equicontinuous in
X\setminus P
; and

(2)

in each component
C
of
X\setminus P
there exists a point
x_{C}\in C
for which the orbit
\{F_{k}(x_{C})\colon k\in\mathbb{N}\}
has compact closure in
N
,

if and only if there exists an asymptotically
(K,\omega)
-quasiregular subsequence
(F_{k_{j}})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
of
(F_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
converging locally uniformly on
X\setminus P
.

Proof.

We begin by assuming that
(F_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfies (
1
) and (
2
).
Assumption (
1
) implies that there exists a covering
\mathcal{U}
of
X\setminus P
for which
(F_{k}|_{U})_{k}
is equicontinuous on each element
U\in\mathcal{U}
. By passing to components of elements of
\mathcal{U}
, we may assume that elements of
\mathcal{U}
are connected.

By assumption (
2
), we may fix for each component
C
of
X\setminus P
, an element
U_{C}
of
\mathcal{U}
containing
x_{C}
.
As
N
is complete, the equicontinuity of
(F_{k}|_{U_{C}})_{k}
and Hopf–Rinow theorem give that, for each
x\in U_{C}
, the orbit
\{\,{F_{k}(x)\mid k\in\mathbb{N}}\,\}
has compact closure in
N
.
Thus, by the Arzelà–Ascoli theorem, there is a subsequence
(F_{k_{j}}|_{U_{C}})_{j}
of
(F_{k}|_{U_{C}})_{k}
which converges locally uniformly on
U_{C}
.

Since we may connect an element
U
of
\mathcal{U}
, contained in a component, say
C
, of
X\setminus P
to an element
U_{C}
by a finite chain of elements in
\mathcal{U}
contained in
C
, a standard diagonal argument yields a locally uniformly converging subsequence, also denoted
(F_{k_{j}})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
of
(F_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
.
Observe that
(F_{k_{j}})_{k_{j}}
still admits a
(K,\omega)
-quasiregular exhaustion about
Q
.
Therefore
(F_{k_{j}})_{j}
is an asymptotically
(K,\omega)
-quasiregular subsequence of
(F_{k})_{k}
.

Now, for the converse direction, assume that
(F_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
has an asymptotically
(K,\omega)
-quasiregular subsequence
(F_{k_{j}})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
which converges locally uniformly on
X\setminus P
. Then each component
C
of
X\setminus P
has an open cover
\mathcal{U}_{C}
with the property that
(F_{k_{j}}|_{U})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
converges locally uniformly on each
U\in\mathcal{U}_{C}
.
Take
U\in\mathcal{U}_{C}
.
Thus by the Arzelà–Ascoli theorem,
(F_{k}|_{U})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is equicontinuous and there exists
x_{U}\in U
for which the orbit
\{F_{k}(x_{U})\colon k\in\mathbb{N}\}
has compact closure in
N
. Therefore
(F_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfies (
1
) and (
2
), which concludes the proof.
∎

4.
Energy Gap

Similar to the other classes of mappings for which a version of Gromov’s compactness theorem holds, quasiregular curves
M\to N
from closed manifolds into manifolds with bounded geometry, have an energy gap, that is, a lower bound for the
L^{n}
-energy.
For quasiregular curves, this follows from the Hölder continuity of the map, an injectivity radius lower bound for
N
, and de Rham’s theorem for integrals of
n
-forms on
n
-manifolds. We begin with an adaptation of this last fact to our setting.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
2\leqslant n\leqslant m
, let
M
be a closed, connected, and oriented, Riemannian
n
-manifold, and let
(N,\omega)
an
n
-calibrated Riemannian
m
-manifold. Let
F\colon M\to N
be a
K
-quasiregular
\omega
-curve. Then
F
is constant if
FM\subset U
where
U\subset N
is an open set for which
0=[\omega|_{U}]\in H^{n}(N)
.

Proof.

Since
[\omega|_{U}]=0
, the form
\omega|_{U}
is exact and there exists
\tau\in\Omega^{n-1}(U)
such that
\;\mathrm{d}\tau=\omega|_{{}_{U}}
. Since
M
is closed, we have, by integrating the distortion inequality and using Stokes’ theorem, that

\displaystyle\int_{M}\lVert DF\rVert^{n}

\displaystyle\leqslant K\int_{M}F^{*}\omega=K\int_{M}F^{*}d\tau=K\int_{M}dF^{*}\tau=0.

Since
\lVert\omega\rVert\circ F
is non-negative, we have that
\lVert DF\rVert=0
almost everywhere. Thus by continuity
F
is locally constant. Since
M
is connected, we obtain that
F
is constant.
∎

For targets having bounded geometry, Proposition
4.1
yields an energy lower bound for non-constant curves; see e.g. Parker
[
32
, Proposition 1.1]
for an analogous statement in the context of harmonic maps. In what follows, we apply this energy gap to spherical strata of nodal manifolds.

Theorem 4.2

(Energy gap)
.

Let
2\leqslant n\leqslant m
, let
M
be a closed, connected and oriented Riemannian
n
-manifold, let
(N,\omega)
be an
n
-calibrated Riemannian
m
-manifold having bounded geometry, let
E_{N}>0
be a small energy bound for
N
, and
K\geqslant 1
. Then there exists
\varepsilon_{0}=\varepsilon_{0}(N,M,K,E_{N},\omega)>0
having the following property: Each non-constant
K
-quasiregular
\omega
-curve
F\colon M\to N
satisfies

\lVert DF\rVert_{L^{n}(M)}\geqslant\varepsilon_{0}.

Proof.

Since
M
is closed, we have that

r_{M}=\inf_{p\in M}\mathrm{BLrad}_{1}(M,p)

is finite and positive. We now denote
L_{M}=\lfloor 2(\operatorname{diam}M)/r_{M}\rfloor+2
, where
\lfloor a\rfloor
is the integer part of
a\in\mathbb{R}
.

Set

\varepsilon_{0}=\frac{1}{3}\min\left\{\frac{r_{N}}{C(n,\alpha)r_{M}^{\alpha}L_{M}},E_{N}\right\},

where
r_{N}=\inf_{q\in N}\mathrm{BLrad}_{1}(N,q)
and
C(n,\alpha)
is the constant from Corollary
2.6
.

Let
F\colon M\to N
be a
K
-quasiregular
\omega
-curve
having energy
\lVert DF\rVert_{L^{n}}<\varepsilon_{0}
. We show that
F
is a constant map.
By taking a geodesic between points of
M
which realizes the diameter of
M
and covering it with at most
L_{M}
balls
B_{1},\ldots,B_{m}
of radius
r_{M}/2
and that
\lVert DF|_{B_{j}}\rVert_{L^{n}}<\varepsilon_{0}<E_{N}
for each
j=1,\ldots,m
, we have, by Corollary
2.6
, that

\operatorname{diam}FM\leqslant C(n,\alpha)\lVert DF\rVert_{L^{n}}r_{M}^{\alpha}L_{M}<C(n,\alpha)r_{M}^{\alpha}L_{M}\varepsilon_{0}\leqslant\frac{1}{3}r_{N}<r_{N}.

Thus the image
FM
of
F
is contained in a topologically Euclidean ball
U=B_{N}(q,r_{N})
of
N
. Thus
0=[\omega|_{U}]\in H^{n}(U)
. Hence
F
is constant by Proposition
4.1
.
∎

5.
Pigeonhole principle for bubbling

In this section, we record another key element used in the proofs of Gromov’s compactness theorem:
the energy of a locally equibounded asymptotically quasiregular sequence concentrates on a discrete set
. This discrete set, the ’bubbling points’, will be the points at which we perform nodal surgery. We record this as follows. For the statement, we denote

i_{X}(p)=\#\{M\in\mathrm{Strata}(X)\colon x\in M\}

the number of strata of the nodal manifold
X
containing the point
p\in X
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
X
be a connected, oriented, and Riemannian nodal
n
-manifold, and let
N
be a Riemannian
m
-manifold for
2\leqslant n\leqslant m
. Let also
(F_{k}\colon X\to N)_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be a locally equibounded sequence of continuous
W^{1,n}_{\operatorname{loc}}
-mappings. Then, for a constant
E>0
, there exist a discrete set
P\subset X
and a subsequence
(F_{k_{j}})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
of
(F_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfying the following conditions:

(1)

For every
x\in X\setminus P
, there exists
0<r_{x}<{\operatorname{dist}\,}_{X}(x,P)
for which

\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\int_{B(x,r_{x})}\lVert DF_{k_{j}}\rVert^{n}\leqslant i_{X}(p)E.

(2)

For every
p\in P
and
0<r_{p}<{\operatorname{dist}\,}(p,P\setminus\{{p}\})
,

\liminf_{j\to\infty}\int_{B(p,r_{p})}\lVert DF_{k_{j}}\rVert^{n}\geqslant E.

Moreover, if
\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\lVert DF_{k}\rVert_{L^{n}(X)}<\infty
, then
P
is finite.

Proof.

We consider first the special case that
X
is a manifold and then the general case.

Step I: special case.
Suppose that
X
is a manifold.
Since
X
is
\sigma
-compact, we may fix an exhaustion
(X_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of
X
by compact subsets, where
X_{i}\subset\mathrm{int}X_{i+1}
for each
i\in\mathbb{N}
. Then, for each
i
, we have

R_{i}=\inf\{\,{\mathrm{BLrad}_{1}(X_{i},p)\mid p\in X_{i}}\,\}>0,

and

E_{i}:=\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\int_{X_{i}}\lVert DF_{k}\rVert<\infty.

Let
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
for which
2^{-\ell}\leqslant R_{i}/2
and let
E_{i,\ell}\subset X_{i}
be an
2^{-\ell}
-net in
X_{i}
, that is,
X_{i}\subset\bigcup_{p\in E_{i,\ell}}B(p,2^{-\ell})
and
d(p,p^{\prime})\geqslant 2^{-\ell}
for
p,p^{\prime}\in E_{i,\ell}
,
p\neq p^{\prime}
. Since, for each
p\in X_{i}
, the ball
B(p,R_{i})
is
2
-bilipschitz to the Euclidean ball
B^{n}(R_{i})
, there exists a constant
Q=Q(n)
having the property that, for each
p\in X_{i}
,
\#\left(B(p,2\cdot 2^{-\ell})\cap E_{\ell}\right)\leqslant Q
. In particular, the collection
\mathcal{B}_{i,\ell}=\{B(p,2^{\ell})\colon p\in E_{\ell}\}
has the property that each point in
X_{i}
is contained in at most
Q
elements of
\mathcal{B}_{i,\ell}
.

Next, for each mapping
F_{k}
we gather together the balls of
\mathcal{B}_{i,\ell}
where
F_{k}
has “too high energy”, that is, we define

\mathcal{B}_{i,\ell,k}=\left\{\,{B\in\mathcal{B}_{i,\ell}\colon\int_{B}\lVert DF_{k}\rVert^{n}\geqslant E}\,\right\}.

Since
\mathcal{B}_{i,\ell}
has finite cardinality, there is a subsequence
(\mathcal{B}_{i,\ell,k_{j}})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
of
(\mathcal{B}_{i,\ell,k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
and a fixed subcollection
\mathcal{B}^{b}_{i,\ell}\subset\mathcal{B}_{i,\ell}
for which
\mathcal{B}_{i,\ell,k_{j}}=\mathcal{B}^{b}_{i,\ell}
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
. In particular, for each
B\in\mathcal{B}^{b}_{i,\ell}
and
j\in\mathbb{N}
,

(5.1)

\int_{B}\lVert DF_{k_{j}}\rVert^{n}\geqslant E.

Using the uniform upper bound for energy
E_{i}
, and the energy lower bound
E
in each ball in
\mathcal{B}^{b}_{i,\ell}
, we have the bound

(5.2)

\big|\mathcal{B}^{b}_{i,\ell}\big|\leqslant\big|\mathcal{B}_{i,\ell,k}\big|\leqslant\frac{QE_{i}}{E}

for the cardinality of
\mathcal{B}^{b}_{i,\ell}
; here we adopt the convention that
1/\infty=0
.

After passing to a further subsequence
(F_{k_{j}})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
if necessary, the sets
\bigcup\mathcal{B}^{b}_{i,\ell}
converge as
\ell\to\infty
under the Hausdorff metric, to a finite set
P_{i}\subset X_{i}
. Thus
P=\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}P_{i}
is countable and the discreteness of
P
follows from the fact that
(X_{i})_{i}
is an exhaustion by compact sets and that each
P_{i}
is finite. Thus this subsequence
(F_{k_{j}})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
of
(F_{k})_{k\in N}
satisfies conditions (
1
) and (
2
).

Regarding the last claim, we note that, if
(F_{k})_{k}
has uniformly bounded energy, there exists
\infty>E_{0}\geqslant E_{i}
for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
. Since
\left\lvert P\right\rvert\leqslant\frac{QE_{0}}{E}
, inequality (
5.2
) gives a bound for the cardinality of
P
. Thus
P
is finite.

Step II: general case.
Suppose now that
X
is a general nodal manifold. Since
X
has countably many strata, we may enumerate the strata and apply the previous argument to each stratum separately. Now a diagonal argument yields a subsequence
(F_{k_{j}})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
of
(F_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
and a subset
P=\bigcup_{M\in\mathrm{Strata}(X)}P_{M}
, where each
P_{M}\subset M
is discrete, satisfying conditions (
1
) and (
2
) in each stratum
M
of
X
, that is,

(1)

For every
x\in M\setminus P_{M}
, there exists
0<r_{x,M}<{{\operatorname{dist}\,}}_{M}(x,P_{M})
for which

\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\int_{B_{M}(x,r_{x,M})}\lVert DF_{k_{j}}\rVert^{n}\leqslant E.

(2)

For every
p\in P_{M}
and
0<r_{p,M}<{{\operatorname{dist}\,}}_{M}(p,P_{M}\setminus\{{p}\})
,

\liminf_{j\to\infty}\int_{B_{M}(p,r_{p})}\lVert DF_{k_{j}}\rVert^{n}\geqslant E.

Since
\mathrm{Sing}(X)
is a discrete set and each singular point
p\in\mathrm{Sing}(X)
meets only finitely many strata, we conclude that
P\subset X
is a discrete set. For each
p\in P
, we also have that, for
x\in X\setminus P
,

r_{x}=\min\{r_{x,M}\colon p\in M\in\mathrm{Strata}(M)\}>0

and, for
p\in P
,

r_{p}=\min\{r_{p,M}\colon p\in M\in\mathrm{Strata}(M)\}>0.

Since each point
x\in X
belongs to
i(x)
strata, we have that the subsequence
(F_{k_{k}})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfies conditions (
1
) and (
2
) in the statement. The last claim follows analogously to the case of a single stratum.
∎

6.
Singular limits of quasiregular curves

In this section, we use the pigeonhole principle to prove a weak-
\star
compactness and quasinormality result for sequences of mappings
(F_{k}\colon X\to N)_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
admitting a
(K,\omega)
-quasiregular exhaustion. For the result, we consider a class of weak-
\star
limits of measures
\star F^{*}_{k}\omega
. These measures identify both the limiting maps and points where mass concentrates, and we use them in the proof of Theorem
1.2
to find the ’bubbling points’, which are – inductively – turned into nodal points in the proof. The definition of these measures is embedded into the following definition of the singular limits of asymptotically
(K,\omega)
-quasiregular sequences.

Definition 6.1
.

Let
X
be an oriented and Riemannian nodal
n
-manifold, and let
(N,\omega)
be an
n
-calibrated Riemannian
m
-manifold.
A quasiregular
\omega
-curve
F\colon X\to N

is the
\delta
-singular limit of an asymptotically
(K,\omega)
-quasiregular sequence
(F_{k}\colon X\to N)_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
for
\delta>0
if
F_{k}|_{X\setminus P}\to F|_{X\setminus P}
locally uniformly, where
P\subset X
is a discrete set, and there exists a Radon measure
\mu
for which

(1)

\star F^{*}_{k}\omega\overset{*}{\rightharpoonup}\mu
,

(2)

\mu\llcorner(X\setminus P)=\star F^{*}\mu
, and

(3)

\mu(\{p\})\geqslant\delta
for each
p\in P
.

We denote
F=\slim_{k\to\infty}F_{k}
,
\mu=\mlim^{\omega}_{k\to\infty}F_{k}
, and
P=\mathrm{Sing}(\mu)
. We also say that
(F_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}

converges
\delta
-singularly to
F
.
A sequence
(F_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}

converges singularly to
F
if
(F_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
converges
\delta
-singularly to
F
for some
\delta>0
.

An equicontinuous sequence of continuous Sobolev maps, admitting a quasiregular exhaustion, has a converging subsequence if the conditions of the Arzelà–Ascoli theorem (here Proposition
3.5
) are satisfied.

We are now ready to state a quasinormality criterion for a sequence of Sobolev maps admitting a quasiregular exhaustion. Here normality refers to having a singularly converging asymptotically quasiregular subsequence.

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
2\leqslant n\leqslant m
, let
X
be a connected, oriented, and Riemannian nodal
n
-manifold, let
(N,\omega)
be an
n
-calibrated Riemannian
m
-manifold having bounded geometry, and let
E>0
.
Let
(F_{k}\colon X\to N)_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of continuous
W^{1,n}_{\operatorname{loc}}
-Sobolev mappings having a
(K,\omega)
-quasiregular exhaustion about a discrete set
Q\subset X
and
satisfying the following conditions:

(1)

the family
(F_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is locally equibounded;

(2)

for each
p\in Q
, the sequence
\big(F_{k}|_{B(p,r_{p})}\big)_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
converges locally uniformly for some
r_{p}>0
; and

(3)

each stratum
M
of
X
contains a non-empty open set
U_{M}\subset M
having the property that, for each
p\in U_{M}
, the orbit
\{F_{k}(p)\colon k\in\mathbb{N}\}
has compact closure in
N
.

Then there exists a subsequence
(F_{k_{j}}\colon X\to N)_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
of
(F_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
, which is asymptotically
(K,\omega)
-quasiregular and which
converges
(E/K)
-singularly to a
K
-quasiregular curve
F\colon X\to N
.

Proof.

By Proposition
5.1
, there exist a discrete set
P\subset X
and a subsequence
(F_{k_{j}})_{j}
of
(F_{k})_{k}
having the property that, for every
x\in X\setminus P
, there exists
0<r_{x}<{\operatorname{dist}\,}(x,P)
satisfying

(6.1)

\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\int_{B(x,r_{x})}\lVert DF_{k_{j}}\rVert^{n}\leqslant i_{X}(x)E.

Since
(F_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
admits a
(K,\omega)
-quasiregular exhaustion about
Q
, we have by higher integrability of quasiregular curves (see e.g.
[
28
]
), the local energy bound (

), and by Morrey’s theorem (see e.g
[
33
, VII.3.1]
), that the subsequence

is uniformly locally Hölder continuous, and hence that

is locally equicontinuous in

.

By (
3
) and Proposition
3.5
, the sequence
(F_{k_{j}})_{j}
has a further subsequence, denoted
(F_{k_{\ell}})_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}}
, which is asymptotically
(K,\omega)
-quasiregular about
P\cup Q
. By Lemma
3.3
, there exists a
K
-quasiregular
\omega
-curve
\widetilde{F}\colon X\setminus(P\cup Q)\to N
to which

converges locally uniformly.
Now, by (
2
), we have that the sequence
(F_{k_{\ell}}|_{X\setminus P})_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}}
converges locally uniformly to a continuous map

which is a
K
-quasiregular
\omega
-curve in

. By Theorem
2.1
,
\widetilde{F}
is a
K
-quasiregular
\omega
-curve.

Since the subsequence
(F_{k_{\ell}})_{\ell}
is locally equibounded, Lemma

additionally implies that

\int_{A}\lVert D\widetilde{F}\rVert^{n}\leqslant\liminf_{\ell\to\infty}\int_{A}\lVert DF_{k_{\ell}}\rVert^{n}\leqslant\sup_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}}\int_{A}\lVert DF_{k_{\ell}}\rVert^{n}<\infty

for each compact subset

.

Now by Theorem

,

extends over

to a
K
-quasiregular
\omega
-curve

. By construction,

converges locally uniformly to

. Thus the measures
\star(F_{k_{\ell}}|_{X\setminus P})^{*}\omega

Since the sequence

Since

for some

implies that, for sufficiently small

Thus

-quasiregular sequence which converges

.

7.
Nodal resolutions of singular limits

In this section, we discuss a method to resolve the point-masses arising in the singular limits. This formalizes the heuristic idea of bubble creation. For the discussion, we give the following definition. Note that in the definition we anticipate an iteration and hence all the bubbles in the bubble tree
\widehat{X}
need not meet the original nodal manifold
X
.

Definition 7.1
.

A singularly converging sequence
(\widehat{F}_{j}\colon\widehat{X}\to N)_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is a
nodal pre-resolution
of a singularly converging sequence
(F_{k}\colon X\to N)_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
if

(1)

\widehat{X}
is a bubble tree over
X
,

(2)

\slim_{j\to\infty}\widehat{F}_{j}|_{X}=\slim_{k\to\infty}F_{k}
,

(3)

(\pi_{\widehat{X},X})_{*}\left(\mlim^{\omega}_{j\to\infty}\widehat{F}_{j}\right)=\mlim^{\omega}_{k\to\infty}F_{k}
, and

(4)

\mathrm{Sing}(\mlim^{\omega}_{j\to\infty}\widehat{F}_{j})\cap X=\varnothing
.

We call
(\widehat{F}_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
a
nodal resolution
of
(F_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
when
\mathrm{Sing}(\mlim^{\omega}_{j\to\infty}\widehat{F}_{j})=\varnothing
.

The reader may wonder at the use of different symbols for the running indices of the sequences
(F_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(\widehat{F}_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
. In principle, these two sequences have no pairwise relation, with only the limits agreeing in the sense of the definition. That being said, we will construct nodal pre-resolutions via (iterated) nodal surgeries of a subsequence
(F_{k_{j}})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
of
(F_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
, from which we obtain much stronger, though unneeded, pairwise relations.

The purpose of this definition is to formalize how the limits
\slim_{k\to\infty}F_{k}
and
\mlim^{\omega}_{k\to\infty}F_{k}
are lifted to the bubble tree
\widehat{X}
over
X
. In particular, by (
3
) and (
4
), the singularities of
\mlim^{\omega}_{k\to\infty}F_{k}
are pre-resolved, though not yet removed, in the nodal pre-resolution in the sense that
(\mlim^{\omega}_{j\to\infty}\widehat{F}_{j})(p)=0
and

(7.1)

(\mlim^{\omega}_{j\to\infty}\widehat{F}_{j})(\pi_{\widehat{X},X}^{-1}(p))=(\mlim^{\omega}_{k\to\infty}F_{k})(p)

for each
p\in\mathrm{Sing}(\mlim^{\omega}_{k\to\infty}F_{k})
.
Additionally, this means that there exists a neighborhood
V\subset\widehat{X}
of
\mathrm{Sing}(\mlim^{\omega}_{k\to\infty}F_{k})
for which
(\widehat{F}_{j}|_{V})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
converges locally uniformly.

Remark 7.2
.

For the coming discussion, we note that a nodal pre-resolution
(\widehat{F}_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
converges locally uniformly if and only if
\mathrm{Sing}(\mlim^{\omega}_{j\to\infty}\widehat{F}_{j})=\varnothing
i.e.
(\widehat{F}_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is a nodal resolution of
(F_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
. This is the reason for the nodal pre-resolution terminology.
Note also that nodal pre-resolutions are transitive i.e. if
(\widetilde{F}_{\ell}\colon\widetilde{X}\to N)_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}}
is a nodal pre-resolution of
(\widehat{F}_{j}\colon\widehat{X}\to N)_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(\widehat{F}_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is a nodal pre-resolution of
(F_{k}\colon X\to N)_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
, then
(\widetilde{F}_{\ell})_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}}
is a nodal pre-resolution of
(F_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
.

7.1.
Nodal Surgery of quasiregular curves

We will now define the nodal surgery of quasiregular curves and hence of asymptotically
(K,\omega)
-quasiregular sequences
(F_{k}\colon X\to N)_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
. Nodal surgery is essentially a type of extension of these classes of mappings over bubbles, which is sufficiently explicit that their constructing requires only a filling lemma; see Lemma
7.4
.
Our method of constructing nodal surgeries yields a nodal manifold
X^{\vee P}
– a bubble tree over
X
– associated to a family
P
of discrete subsets of
X
, and a sequence of asymptotically quasiregular curves

. We define the nodal manifold

in two steps.

For an
n
-manifold

and a discrete set

, we set

to be the nodal manifold with
\mathrm{Strata}(M^{\vee P})=\{M\}\cup\{\,{\mathbb{S}^{n}_{p}\mid p\in P}\,\}
,
where each
\mathbb{S}^{n}_{p}=\mathbb{S}^{n}\times\{p\}
is an isometric copy of
\mathbb{S}^{n}
,
i.e.

M^{\vee P}=M\bigvee_{p\in P}\mathbb{S}^{n}_{p},

where each
p\in P\subset M
is identified with
(-e_{n+1},p)\in\mathbb{S}^{n}_{p}
. As usual, we consider
M
as a subset of
M^{\vee P}
.

Next, let
X
be a
n
-nodal manifold and let
P=(P_{M})_{M\in\mathrm{Strata}(X)}
be a family of discrete sets, where
P_{M}\subset M
for each
M\in\mathrm{Strata}(X)
. We denote
M_{X}=\coprod_{M\in\mathrm{Strata}(X)}M
and let
\pi\colon M_{X}\to X
be the associated quotient map.
Since
M_{X}\subset\coprod_{M\in\mathrm{Strata}(X)}M^{\vee P_{M}}
, we may define
X^{\vee P}
to be the nodal manifold

(7.2)

X^{\vee P}=\left(\coprod_{M\in\mathrm{Strata}(X)}M^{\vee P_{M}}\right)\Big/{\sim},

where
\sim
is the equivalence relation reidentifying the singular points of
X
, that is, the equivalence relation induced by the quotient map
\pi\colon M_{X}\to X
. Now
X\subset X^{\vee P}
and
\mathrm{Strata}_{X}(X^{\vee P})=\left\{\,{\mathbb{S}^{n}_{p}\mid p\in P_{M}\ \text{and}\ M\in\mathrm{Strata}(X)}\,\right\}
.

Observe that, if
P_{M}\subset M\setminus\mathrm{Sing}(X)
for each stratum
M
, we may take

to be the subset
P=\bigcup_{M\in\mathrm{Strata}(X)}P_{M}
and take

X^{\vee P}=X\bigvee_{p\in P}\mathbb{S}^{n}_{p}.

as in the case of an
n
-manifold.

Having discussed this preliminary terminology for a “single layer” bubble tree over a nodal manifold, we are now ready to define the nodal surgery of a quasiregular curve. For the nodal surgery of a map
X\to N
at a point
p\in X
, we introduce quasiconformal embeddings
\varphi^{+}_{p,M}\colon B_{M}(p,R)\to\mathbb{S}^{n}
and
\varphi^{-}_{p,M}\colon B_{M}(p,R)\to\mathbb{S}^{n}
for

as follows. For the discussion, let
\sigma\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{S}^{n}
be the stereographic projection for which
\sigma\mathbb{R}^{n}=\mathbb{S}^{n}\setminus\{-e_{n+1}\}
and let
\rho\colon\mathbb{S}^{n}\to\mathbb{S}^{n}
,
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},x_{n+1})\mapsto(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},-x_{n+1})
.

Let
M
be a Riemannian
n
-manifold,
p\in M
, and
R<\min\{1,\operatorname{inj\,rad}_{M}(p)\}
. We denote
\xi_{p}=\left(\exp_{p}|_{B(0,R)}\right)^{-1}\colon B_{M}(p,R)\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
a local inverse of the exponential map at

isometrically with

respectively. We define the mappings

\varphi^{+}_{M,p,R}\colon B_{M}(p,R)\to\mathbb{S}^{n},\quad x\mapsto\sigma(\lambda^{+}_{R}\xi_{p}(x)),

and

\varphi^{-}_{M,p,R}\colon B_{M}(p,R)\to\mathbb{S}^{n},\quad x\mapsto(\rho\circ\sigma)(\lambda^{-}_{R}\xi_{p}(x)).

Note that, by the radial symmetry of the stereographic projection, we have

\varphi^{+}_{M,p,R}|_{\partial B_{M}(p,R)}=\varphi^{-}_{M,p,R}|_{\partial B_{M}(p,R)}.

We denote by
\rho_{R}\in(0,R)
the radius for which

\varphi^{+}_{M,p,R}(B_{M}(p,\rho_{R}))=\mathbb{S}^{n}_{p}\cap\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,\infty)\right).

Having mappings
\varphi^{+}_{p,M}
and
\varphi^{-}_{p,M}
at our disposal, we define a nodal surgery of a map
X\to N
as follows.

Definition 7.3
.

A mapping
\widehat{F}\colon X^{\vee P}\to N
is a
nodal surgery of a mapping
F\colon X\to N
over a family of discrete sets
P=(P_{M})_{M\in\mathrm{Strata}(X)}
, where
P_{M}\subset M
for each
M\in\mathrm{Strata}(X)
,
if there exists a family

\mathcal{B}=\{B_{M}(p,R_{p})\colon p\in P_{M},\ M\in\mathrm{Strata}(X)\}

of mutually disjoint open balls having the properties that

\widehat{F}|_{X\setminus\bigcup\mathcal{B}}=F|_{X\setminus\bigcup\mathcal{B}}

and, for each
p\in P_{M}
and
M\in\mathrm{Strata}(X)
, there exists
0<r_{p}<R_{p}
for which

(1)

\widehat{F}|_{\mathbb{S}^{n}_{p}\setminus\bar{B}_{\mathbb{S}^{n}_{p}}(-e_{n+1},r_{p})}\circ\varphi^{+}_{M,p,r_{p}}=F|_{B_{M}(p,r_{p})}
, and

(2)

\widehat{F}|_{B_{\mathbb{S}^{n}_{p}}(-e_{n+1},r_{p})}\circ\varphi^{-}_{M,p,r_{p}}=\widehat{F}|_{B_{M}(p,r_{p})}
.

Our basic surgery lemma about the existence of nodal surgeries of quasiregular curves reads as follows. For the statement, we denote
A_{M}(p;r,R)=B_{M}(p,R)\setminus\bar{B}_{M}(p,r)
for

.

Lemma 7.4
.

Let
p\in M
and
\varepsilon>0
. Let also
0<r^{\prime}<r<R<\mathrm{QCrad}_{\varepsilon}(M,p)
and let

be a

-quasiregular
\omega
-curve. Let also

.
If
F_{p}A_{M}(p;r^{\prime},r)\subset B_{N}(q,\widehat{R})
, where
\widehat{R}<\mathrm{BLrad}_{1}(N,q)
for some

,
then there exists a Sobolev map
\widehat{F}_{p}\colon X_{p}\to N
in

having the following properties:

(1)

the restriction
\widehat{F}_{p}|_{X_{p}\setminus B_{X_{p}}(p,r)}
is a
(1+\varepsilon)K
-quasiregular
\omega
-curve;

(2)

\widehat{F}_{p}

(3)

\lVert D\widehat{F}_{p}\rVert_{L^{n}(X_{p})}\leqslant C(n)(1+\varepsilon)^{1/n}\left(\lVert DF\rVert_{L^{n}(A_{M}(p;r^{\prime},R))}+\left(\log\frac{r}{r^{\prime}}\right)^{\frac{1-n}{n}}\right)

(4)

\widehat{F}_{p}(B_{X_{p}}(p,r))\subset B_{N}(q,\widehat{R})

Proof.

We begin by defining an auxiliary map
\widehat{f}\colon B_{M}(p,R)\to N
.
For this, let
\varphi\colon B_{N}(q,\widehat{R})\to B^{m}(\widehat{R})
be a chart at

at

at

. Also let

be the map

, where

We fix a smooth function

, which satisfies

\lVert\nabla u\rVert_{L^{n}(A(r^{\prime}r)}\leqslant 2\mathrm{cap}_{n}(\bar{B}^{n}(r^{\prime}),B^{n}(r))=2c(n)\log\left(\frac{r}{r^{\prime}}\right)^{1-n};

see e.g.
[

We also define a map

0,&\text{if }|x|\leqslant r^{\prime}.\end{array}\right.

Next, we define another map

. Before completing the construction of the mapping

\lVert D\widetilde{f}\rVert_{L^{n}(B^{n}(r))}\leqslant(1+\varepsilon)^{1/n}\left(3\lVert D\widetilde{F}\rVert_{L^{n}(A_{M}(p;r^{\prime},R))}+c(n)\left(\log\frac{r}{r^{\prime}}\right)^{\frac{1-n}{n}}\right).

\displaystyle=\lVert D\widetilde{F}\rVert_{L^{n}(A(r^{\prime},R))}+\lVert\nabla u\rVert_{L^{n}(A(r^{\prime},r))}

We are now ready to define the mapping

7.2.
Construction of nodal pre-resolutions via surgery

We are now ready to show how to construct a nodal pre-resolution of a singularly converging asymptotically quasiregular sequence using nodal surgery.

, let

a connected, oriented, and Riemannian nodal

Let
(F_{k}\colon X\to N)_{k\in\mathbb{N}}

.
Then, for

there exists a locally equibounded and asymptotically
(K,\omega)
-quasiregular sequence

which is a nodal pre-resolution of a subsequence

8.
Renormalization of nodal pre-resolutions

In the previous section, we showed that we can construct a nodal pre-resolution
(\widehat{F}_{j}\colon X^{\vee P}\to N)_{j\in\mathbb{N}}

-quasiregular sequence

Let

-quasiregular sequence, which is a nodal pre-resolution of
(F_{k}\colon X\to N)_{k\in\mathbb{N}}

(K,\omega)
-quasiregular sequence

which is a nodal pre-resolution of

(8.1)

(\mlim^{\omega}_{\ell\to\infty}\widetilde{F}_{\ell})(\{\tilde{p}\})\leqslant(9/10)(\mlim^{\omega}_{j\to\infty}\widehat{F}_{j})(\mathbb{S}^{n}_{p})

8.1.
Renormalization in the Euclidean space

For the proof of Proposition

[

Let
\mu
be a non-atomic measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\lvert x\right\rvert\;\mathrm{d}\mu(x)<\infty
and whose support
\operatorname{spt}(\mu)
is not contained in an affine line. The
center of mass
\mathrm{cm}(\mu)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
of
\mu
is the unique minimum of the function

\Phi_{\mu}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R},\ x\mapsto\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\lvert x-z\right\rvert\;\mathrm{d}\mu(z).

The conditions on
\mu
ensure that
\Phi_{\mu}
is well-defined and has a unique minimum. For the details, see the paragraph following
[
31
, Proposition 3.3]
. As the name suggests, heuristically, the center mass
\mathrm{cm}(\mu)
of
\mu
is located where the measure has most mass. The following formulation of this heuristic principle suffices for us.

Lemma 8.3

(
[
31
, Lemma 3.4]
)
.

Let
\mu
be a finite non-atomic measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\lvert x\right\rvert\;\mathrm{d}\mu(x)<\infty
and whose support is not contained in a line. If
\mu(B^{n}(0,1))>\frac{3}{4}\mu(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then
\mathrm{cm}(\mu)\in B^{n}(0,2)
.

A basic redistribution result for a measure concentrated near the origin reads as follows; see
[
31
, Proposition 3.3]
. We recall a simple proof for the readers convenience.

Proposition 8.4
.

Let
\mu
be a finite non-atomic measure on
B^{n}(0,1)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, whose support is not contained in a line.
Suppose there exists
0<\varepsilon<\frac{1}{8}
and
0<\gamma<\frac{1}{8}\lVert\mu\rVert
for which
\mu(B^{n}(0,\varepsilon))\geqslant\lVert\mu\rVert-\gamma
.
Then for
\gamma<\delta<\frac{1}{8}\lVert\mu\rVert
, there exists
\Lambda>\frac{1}{3\varepsilon}
, a conformal map

A\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n},\ x\mapsto\Lambda(x-\mathrm{cm}(\mu))+\mathrm{cm}(\mu)

and
R=2\Lambda-1
, which satisfy the following properties:

(1)

\mathrm{supp}\,A_{*}\mu\subset\bar{B}^{n}(0,R)

(2)

A_{*}\mu(B^{n}(0,R)\setminus B^{n}(0,1+2\varepsilon))\leqslant 2\delta
, and

(3)

A_{*}\mu(B^{n}(z,1/4))\leqslant\lVert\mu\rVert-\delta
for all
z\in B^{n}(0,R)
.

The proof consists of two parts: a proof for the special case
\mathrm{cm}(\mu)=0
and an affine conjugation of this result. We discuss the redistribution first as a separate lemma.

Lemma 8.5
.

Let
\mu
be a finite non-atomic measure on
B^{n}(0,1)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, whose support is not contained in a line, and with
\mathrm{cm}(\mu)=0
.
Suppose there exists
0<\varepsilon<1
and

0<\gamma<\frac{1}{8}\lVert\mu\rVert
for which

\mu(B^{n}(0,\varepsilon))\geqslant\lVert\mu\rVert-\gamma
.
Then, for
\gamma<\delta<\frac{1}{8}\lVert\mu\rVert
, there exists
\Lambda>\frac{1}{\varepsilon}
and an affine map
T\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
x\mapsto\Lambda x
,
satisfying the following properties:

(1)

T_{*}\mu(B^{n}(0,\Lambda)\setminus B^{n}(0,1))=2\delta
and

(2)

T_{*}\mu(B^{n}(z,1/4))\leqslant\lVert\mu\rVert-\delta
for all
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Proof.

Consider the function

\Psi\colon[1,\infty)\to[0,\lVert\mu\rVert),\ \lambda\mapsto\mu\big(B^{n}(0,1)\setminus B^{n}(0,1/\lambda)\big).

Since
\mu
has no atoms,
\Psi
is continuous.
Also observe that
\Psi(1)=0
and
\Psi(\lambda)\to\lVert\mu\rVert
as
\lambda\to\infty

We refer to Burago, Burago, and Ivanov

In the following version of Theorem
1.2
, both
d_{g}
and
d_{X}
denote the length metrics of the nodal Riemannian manifold
(X,g)
.

Theorem 10.1

(Gromov–Hausdorff convergence)
.

Let
2\leqslant n\leqslant m
, let
X
be a connected, oriented Riemannian nodal
n
-manifold, let
(N,\omega)
be an
n
-calibrated Riemannian
m
-manifold having bounded geometry, and
K\geqslant 1
. Let
(F_{k}\colon X\to N)_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be a locally equibounded sequence of
K
-quasiregular
\omega
-curves for which there exists
x_{0}\in X
such that the orbit
\{\,{F_{k}(x_{0})\mid k\in\mathbb{N}}\,\}
has compact closure.
Then there exists a subsequence
(F_{k_{j}})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
of
(F_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
, a sequence of Riemannian metrics
(g_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
on
X
, a bubble tree
\widehat{X}
over
X
, and a
K
-quasiregular
\omega
-curve
\widehat{F}\colon\widehat{X}\to N
for which

(X,d_{g_{j}},F_{k_{j}})\overset{GH}{\longrightarrow}(\widehat{X},d_{\widehat{X}},\widehat{F})

as mapping packages, where
d_{g_{j}}
is the distance of
g_{j}
and
d_{\widehat{X}}
the distance function of the bubble tree
\widehat{X}
.

The other reformulation of Theorem
1.2
is in terms of nodal (or tight) convergence. Recall that for a bubble tree
\widehat{M}
over an
n
-manifold
M
, a map
\rho\colon M\to\widehat{M}
is a
pinching map
if

(1)

there exists an indexed family
(B_{S})_{S\in\mathrm{Strata}_{M}(\widehat{M})}
of closed topological
n
-balls in
M
for which
S\subset\rho(B_{S})
and
\rho(\partial B_{S})
is a nodal point in
\mathrm{Sing}(\widehat{M})
, and

(2)

\rho
is an embedding in each component of
M\setminus\bigcup_{S\in\mathrm{Strata}_{M}(\widehat{M})}\partial B_{S}
.

Theorem 10.2

(Tight convergence)
.

Let
2\leqslant n\leqslant m
, let
M
be a connected, oriented Riemannian
n
-manifold, let
(N,\omega)
be an
n
-calibrated Riemannian
m
-manifold having bounded geometry, and
K\geqslant 1
.
Let
(F_{k}\colon M\to N)_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be a locally equibounded sequence of
K
-quasiregular
\omega
-curves for which there exists
x_{0}\in M
such that the orbit
\{\,{F_{k}(x_{0})\mid k\in\mathbb{N}}\,\}
has compact closure.
Then there exists a subsequence
(F_{k_{j}})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
of
(F_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
, a bubble tree
\widehat{M}
over
M
, a sequence of pinching maps
(\rho_{j}\colon M\to\widehat{M})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
on
M
, and a
K
-quasiregular
\omega
-curve
\widehat{F}\colon\widehat{M}\to N
for which

F_{k_{j}}\circ\rho_{j}^{-1}|_{\widehat{M}\setminus\mathrm{Sing}(\widehat{M})}\to\widehat{F}|_{\widehat{M}\setminus\mathrm{Sing}(\widehat{M})}

locally uniformly.

Both of these statements are based on the following well-known observation.

Lemma 10.3
.

Let
X
be a nodal Riemannian manifold and
\varepsilon>0
. Then there exists a Riemannian manifold
Y
and an
\varepsilon
-rough isometry
\rho\colon Y\to X
for which there exists an
\varepsilon
-dense open submanifold
\Omega\subset X
and a locally isometric embedding
\iota\colon\Omega\to Y
for which the composition
\rho\circ\iota\colon\Omega\to X
is identity. Moreover, if each
p\in\mathrm{Sing}(X)
meets at most two strata of
X
, we may take
\rho
to be an embedding in the components of
Y\setminus\rho^{-1}(\mathrm{Sing}(X))
and each
\rho^{-1}(p)
to be an
n
-sphere for
p\in\mathrm{Sing}(X)
.

Since the existence of such a manifold
Y
is discussed in many proofs for different versions of Gromov’s theorem, we merely recall an idea for the construction; see e.g. Parker
[
32
]
.

Sketch of Proof of Lemma
10.3
.

Let
X
be a nodal Riemannian manifold and let
\delta>0
to be determined later. The manifold
Y
is constructed by independently removing each singular point of
X
as follows.

Let
p\in\mathrm{Sing}(X)
be a singular point and let
M_{1},\ldots,M_{m_{p}}
be the strata of
X
containing
p
. Then there exists
r_{p}\in(0,\varepsilon/4)
for which each exponential map
\exp_{p}\colon B_{T_{p}M_{i}}(0,2r_{p})\to B_{M_{i}}(p,2r_{p})
is
(1+\delta)
-bilipschitz. By passing to a smaller radius
r_{p}
if necessary, we may assume that there exists mutually disjoint balls
B_{S}(x_{1},2r_{p}),\ldots,B_{S}(x_{m_{p}},2r_{p})
on the standard
n
-sphere
S=\mathbb{S}^{n}(\varepsilon/(4\pi))
of radius
\varepsilon/(4\pi)
.

Next we remove the open sets
U_{X}=B_{M_{1}}(p,r_{p})\cup\cdots\cup B_{M_{m_{p}}}(p,r_{p})
and
U_{S}=B_{S}(x_{1},r_{p})\cup\cdots\cup B_{S}(x_{m_{p}},r_{p})
from
X
and
S
, respectively, and glue the spheres
\partial B_{M_{i}}(p,r_{p})
and
\partial B_{S}(x_{i},r_{p})
together with the composition of the exponential mappings. By taking
\delta
small enough, the obtained manifold
Y
has a natural smooth structure and has a length metric
d
for which
X\setminus U_{X}\subset Y
and
S\setminus U_{X}\subset Y
are smooth submanifolds with boundary and the metric space
(Y,d)
has Gromov-Hausdorff distance at most
\varepsilon/2
to
X
. Since
Y
has a Riemannian metric in
X\setminus U_{X}
and
S\setminus U_{S}
, we have that – by taking
\delta
even smaller – we may smooth these Riemannian metrics to obtain a Riemannian metric
g_{Y}
on
Y
for which the mapping
\rho\colon Y\to X
, which is identity on
V_{X}=B_{M_{1}}(p,2r_{p})\cup\cdots\cup B_{M_{m_{p}}}(p,2r_{p})
and collapses the complement of
U_{S}
to the point
p
, is an
\varepsilon
-rough isometry.

If the singular points of
X
meet only two strata, we may replace the balls
B_{S}(p,2r_{p})
and
B_{S}(p,2r_{p})
by hemispheres. This yields the wanted additional property.
∎

Corollary 10.4
.

Let
\widehat{X}
be a bubble tree over a Riemannian manifold
M
and
\varepsilon>0
. Then there exists a Riemannian metric
g
on
M
, an
\varepsilon
-rough isometry
\rho\colon(M,g)\to\widehat{X}
, and an
\varepsilon
-dense open set
\Omega\subset M
for which
\rho|_{\Omega}\colon\Omega\to\widehat{X}
is the identity. Moreover, if each singular point
p\in\mathrm{Sing}(\widehat{X})
meets at most two strata of
\widehat{X}
, then we may take
\rho\colon M\to\widehat{X}
to be, in addition, a pinching map.

Proof.

Since
\widehat{X}
is a (locally finite) bubble tree over
M
, we have that manifold
Y
constructed in the proof of Lemma
10.3
is an iterated connected sum of
M
with
n
-spheres and hence diffeomorphic to
\widehat{X}
. By the proof of Lemma
10.3
, the preimage
\rho^{-1}(p)
of each
p\in\mathrm{Sing}(\widehat{X})
under
\rho
in Lemma
10.3
is an
n
-sphere bounding a topological
n
-ball in
M
, the claim now follows from Lemma
10.3
and its proof.
∎

Having Corollary
10.4
at our disposal, Theorems
10.1
and
10.2
are almost immediate consequences of Theorem
1.2
. For this reason, we only indicate the steps.

Sketches of proofs of Theorems
10.1
and
10.2
.

For the proof of Theorem
10.1
, let
\widehat{X}
be a bubble tree over
X
,
(F_{k_{j}})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
a subsequence of
(F_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
, and let
\widehat{F}\colon\widehat{X}\to N
as in Theorem
1.2
. Let also
(\varepsilon_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
be a positive sequence tending to zero.
By the first part of Corollary
10.4
, we have a sequence of Riemannian metrics
(g_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
on
X
and
\varepsilon_{j}
-rough isometries
\rho_{j}\colon(X,d_{g_{j}})\to\widehat{X}
. The convergence
(X,g_{j},F_{k_{j}})\overset{GH}{\longrightarrow}(\widehat{X},\widehat{F})
of mapping packages follows now by fixing a sequence
(\psi_{j}\colon\widehat{X}\to X)_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
of rough inverses of maps
\rho_{j}
and observing that
F_{k_{j}}\circ\psi_{j}\to\widehat{F}
locally uniformly as
j\to\infty
. This concludes the sketch of a proof of Theorem
10.1
.

For a proof of Theorem
10.2
, we consider a sequence
(F_{k}\colon M\to N)_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
of
K
-quasiregular
\omega
-curves on a Riemannian
n
-manifold
M
instead of a nodal manifold
X
. The proof is now analogous to the argument above using the additional information provided by Corollary
10.4
that
\varepsilon_{j}
-rough isometries
\rho_{j}\colon(X,g_{j})\to\widehat{X}
can be taken to be pinching maps.
∎

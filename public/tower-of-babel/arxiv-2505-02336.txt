Introduction

The mathematical study of dynamical systems with holes, called
open dynamical systems
, was first proposed by Pianigiani and Yorke
[
43
]
. Let
(X,T,\mu)
be an ergodic dynamical system on a compact metric space
X
, and let
H\subset X
be the
hole
with
\mu(H)>0
. For
n\in\mathbb{N}
let
P_{s}(n)
be the survivor probability defined by
P_{s}(n):=\mu\left(\left\{x\in X:T^{j}(x)\notin H\penalty\ \forall 0\leq j\leq n\right\}\right)
. In open dynamical systems people always study the decay rate of
P_{s}(n)
, called the
escape rate
, given by
\rho(H):=\lim_{n\to\infty}-\frac{1}{n}\log P_{s}(n)
. For more information on the escape rate and the related first hitting time we refer to the papers
[
9
,
17
,
18
,
19
]
and the references therein. Note by Birkhoff ergodic theorem (cf.
[
52
]
) that the survivor set

K(H):=\left\{x\in X:T^{n}(x)\notin H\penalty\ \forall n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\right\}

is a
\mu
-null set. Then the fractal dimension, such as Hausdorff dimension, is applied to estimate the size of
K(H)
. It turns out that the dimension of
K(H)
depends not only on the size but also on the position of
H
(cf.
[
11
]
). For more results on the dimension of
K(H)
we refer to the papers
[
4
,
27
,
32
,
50
]
. Note that the study of
K(H)
has intimate connection with unique
\beta
-expansions (cf.
[
2
,
3
,
16
,
26
,
33
,
46
]
) and badly approximable numbers in Diophantine approximation (cf.
[
15
,
22
,
36
,
41
]
).

Until now, in most work of open dynamical systems the hole
H
was assumed to be fixed. In this paper we will study the survivor set of an open dynamical system with a moving hole. In particular, the hole moves when the time
n
changes. Our study involves a new type of fractals including the Moran type fractals and graph-directed fractals as special cases. Our results can be applied to the study of liminf sets in dynamical Diophantine approximations and to the finiteness property for the joint spectral radius of matrices.

1.1.
Open dynamical systems with a moving hole

Let
\mathbb{N}:=\{1,2,3,\ldots\}
be the set of all positive integers, and let
\mathbb{N}_{0}:=\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
.
For
k\in\mathbb{N}
let
\mathbb{N}_{\geq k}:=\mathbb{N}\cap[k,+\infty)
.

Given
b\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 3}
, let
D_{b}:=\left\{0,1,\ldots,b-1\right\}
. For
n\in\mathbb{N}
let
D_{b}^{n}
be the set of all words of length
n
over the alphabet
D_{b}
, i.e.,

D_{b}^{n}=\{d_{1}d_{2}\ldots d_{n}:d_{i}\in D_{b}\penalty\ \forall 1\leq i\leq n\}.

Let
D_{b}^{*}:=\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}D_{b}^{n}
be the set of all finite words over the alphabet
D_{b}
. Moreover,
let
D_{b}^{\mathbb{N}}:=\{(d_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}:d_{i}\in D_{b}\penalty\ \forall i\in\mathbb{N}\}
denote the set of all infinite sequences over the alphabet
D_{b}
, and let
\sigma
be the left shift map on
D_{b}^{\mathbb{N}}
. Then for
(d_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}\in D_{b}^{\mathbb{N}}
we have
\sigma((d_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty})=(d_{i+1})_{i=1}^{\infty}\in D_{b}^{\mathbb{N}}
. For a finite word
\mathbf{d}=d_{1}d_{2}\ldots d_{n}\in D_{b}^{*}
, we define the cylinder set by
\mathcal{C}_{\mathbf{d}}:=\{(\omega_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}\in D_{b}^{\mathbb{N}}:\omega_{i}=d_{i}\penalty\ \forall 1\leq i\leq n\}
.

Given
m\in\mathbb{N}
, write
(D_{b}^{m})^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
for the set of all infinite sequences
\omega=\omega^{0}\omega^{1}\ldots
with each
\omega^{k}\in D_{b}^{m}
. For
\omega=\omega^{0}\omega^{1}\ldots\in(D_{b}^{m})^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
we first define the
survivor set
of the symbolic dynamical system
(D_{b}^{\mathbb{N}},\sigma)
with the moving holes
\{\mathcal{C}_{\omega^{k}}:k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\}
by

\Sigma^{\omega}:=\big\{(d_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}\in D_{b}^{\mathbb{N}}:\sigma^{k}\big((d_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}\big)\not\in\mathcal{C}_{\omega^{k}}\penalty\ \forall k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\big\}.

That is,

(1.1)

\Sigma^{\omega}=\big\{(d_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}\in D_{b}^{\mathbb{N}}:d_{k+1}d_{k+2}\ldots d_{k+m}\neq\omega^{k}\penalty\ \forall k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\big\}.

Let
\pi_{b}:D_{b}^{\mathbb{N}}\to[0,1]
be the projection map defined by
\pi_{b}\big((d_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}\big):=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}d_{i}b^{-i}
.
We are interested in the survivor set

K^{\omega}:=\pi_{b}(\Sigma^{\omega}).

It is closely related to the survivor set of the open dynamical system
([0,1),T_{b})
with the moving holes
\big\{\mathrm{int}\big(\pi_{b}(\mathcal{C}_{\omega^{k}})\big):k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\}
, where
T_{b}:[0,1)\to[0,1);\penalty\ x\mapsto bx\pmod{1}
. Observe that up to a countable set the map
\pi_{b}
is conjugate from
(D_{b}^{\mathbb{N}},\sigma)
to
([0,1),T_{b})
(cf.
[
38
]
). So,
the survivor set

\widetilde{K}^{\omega}:=\big\{x\in[0,1):T_{b}^{k}(x)\not\in\mathrm{int}\big(\pi_{b}(\mathcal{C}_{\omega^{k}})\big)\penalty\ \forall k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\big\}

differs from
K^{\omega}
by at most countably many points. Since we are interested in the fractal dimension of
K^{\omega}
, in the following we focus on
K^{\omega}
which
has a more explicit geometric construction

(1.2)

K^{\omega}=\bigcap_{k=1}^{\infty}\bigcup_{\mathbf{d}\in\Sigma_{k}^{\omega}}I_{\mathbf{d}},

where
I_{\mathbf{d}}:=\pi_{b}(\mathcal{C}_{\mathbf{d}})
, and

(1.3)

\Sigma_{k}^{\omega}:=\big\{d_{1}\ldots d_{k}:(d_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}\in\Sigma^{\omega}\big\}.

Since
b\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 3}
, the survivor set
K^{\omega}
is a Cantor set, i.e.,
K^{\omega}
is a compact set with neither interior nor isolated points.
Our work involves determining various fractal dimensions of the survivor set
K^{\omega}
.

For a bounded set
F\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, let
\dim_{H}F,\penalty\ \dim_{P}F
denote its Hausdorff and packing dimensions; let
\underline{\dim}_{B}F,\penalty\ \overline{\dim}_{B}F
denote its lower and upper box dimensions (cf.
[
20
]
); and let
\dim_{L}F,\penalty\ \dim_{A}F
denote its lower dimension and Assouad dimension (cf.
[
25
]
).
For a compact set
F\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, these fractal dimensions have the following relation (cf.
[
25
, Lemma 2.4.3, Theorem 3.4.3]
)

(1.4)

\dim_{L}F\leq\dim_{H}F\leq\min\left\{\underline{\dim}_{B}F,\dim_{P}F\right\}\leq\max\left\{\underline{\dim}_{B}F,\dim_{P}F\right\}\leq\overline{\dim}_{B}F\leq\dim_{A}F.

In general, the lower box dimension and the packing dimension are incomparable.
If
F
is
s
-Ahlfors regular, then all fractal dimensions in (
1.4
) are equal to
s
.
Recall that a compact set
F\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is called
s
-Ahlfors regular
if there exists a constant
c\geq 1
such that for all
0<r<1
and all
x\in F
,

c^{-1}r^{s}\leq\mathcal{H}^{s}\big(F\cap B(x,r)\big)\leq cr^{s},

where
\mathcal{H}^{s}
is the
s
-dimensional Hausdorff measure, and
B(x,r)
is the closed ball centered at
x
with radius
r
.

When
m=1
, the set
K^{\omega}
is a homogeneous Moran set. Its Hausdorff and packing dimensions were given by Feng et al.
[
24
]
, and its Assouad and lower dimensions were given by Li et al.
[
37
]
and Chen et al.
[
12
]
, respectively.
In this case, all of these fractal dimensions of
K^{\omega}
are equal to
\log(b-1)/\log b
.
So in the following we always assume
m\geq 2
.

When
\omega^{k}=\mathbf{d}
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, we have
(\Sigma^{\omega},\sigma)
is the shift of finite type with the forbidden word
\mathbf{d}
, denoted by
X_{\mathbf{d}}
(cf.
[
38
]
).
It can be described by a directed graph
G_{\mathbf{d}}=(V,E)
, where
V=D_{b}^{m-1}
, and for
\mathbf{u}=u_{1}\ldots u_{m-1},\mathbf{v}=v_{1}\ldots v_{m-1}\in V
we say
(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})\in E
if
u_{2}\ldots u_{m-1}=v_{1}\ldots v_{m-2}
and
u_{1}\ldots u_{m-1}v_{m-1}\neq\mathbf{d}
.
Let
A_{\mathbf{d}}
be the corresponding adjacency matrix of
G_{\mathbf{d}}
, whose size is
b^{m-1}\times b^{m-1}
.
In this case, all fractal dimensions in (
1.4
) of
K^{\omega}=\pi_{b}(X_{\mathbf{d}})
are equal to
\log\rho(A_{\mathbf{d}})/\log b
, where
\rho(A)
is the spectral radius of a matrix
A
(cf.
[
40
]
). To the best of our knowledge,
K^{\omega}
is a new type of fractal set for a general
\omega=\omega^{0}\omega^{1}\ldots\in(D_{b}^{m})^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
.

For a real matrix
A=(a_{i,j})
we use the norm
\|A\|=\sum_{i,j}|a_{i,j}|
.
For a finite set
F
let
|F|
denote its cardinality.
In general, we obtain the following results concerning the fractal dimensions of
K^{\omega}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

For any
\omega=\omega^{0}\omega^{1}\ldots\in(D_{b}^{m})^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
we have

\dim_{H}K^{\omega}=\underline{\dim}_{B}K^{\omega}=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log|\Sigma_{n}^{\omega}|}{n\log b}=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n\log b}\log\|A_{\omega^{0}}A_{\omega^{1}}\cdots A_{\omega^{n-1}}\|,

and

\dim_{P}K^{\omega}=\overline{\dim}_{B}K^{\omega}=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log|\Sigma_{n}^{\omega}|}{n\log b}=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n\log b}\log\|A_{\omega^{0}}A_{\omega^{1}}\cdots A_{\omega^{n-1}}\|.

Remark 1.2
.

By a recent work of Fan and Wu
[
21
]
it follows that for any ergodic measure
\nu
on
((D_{b}^{m})^{\mathbb{N}_{0}},\tilde{\sigma})
we have

\dim_{H}K^{\omega}=\dim_{P}K^{\omega}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n\log b}\log\|A_{\omega^{0}}A_{\omega^{1}}\cdots A_{\omega^{n-1}}\|\quad\textrm{for $\nu$-a.e. }\omega\in(D_{b}^{m})^{\mathbb{N}_{0}},

where
\tilde{\sigma}(\omega)=\omega^{1}\omega^{2}\ldots
for
\omega=\omega^{0}\omega^{1}\ldots\in(D_{b}^{m})^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
.

To describe the range of
\dim_{H}K^{\omega}
and
\dim_{P}K^{\omega}
we introduce two types of sequences in
(D_{b}^{m})^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
, namely the progressively overlapping sequences and the totally distinct sequences.
Let
\omega=\omega^{0}\omega^{1}\ldots\in(D_{b}^{m})^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
with
\omega^{i}=\omega^{i}_{1}\ldots\omega^{i}_{m}\in D_{b}^{m}
for all
i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. For
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we say that the sequence
\omega
is
progressively overlapping at position-
k
if

\omega^{k}_{1}\ldots\omega^{k}_{m-j}=\omega^{k-j}_{j+1}\ldots\omega^{k-j}_{m}\quad\forall 1\leq j\leq\min\{k,m-1\}.

The sequence
\omega
is called
progressively overlapping
if it is progressively overlapping at position-
k
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
, that is,
\omega^{i}_{1}\ldots\omega^{i}_{m-1}=\omega^{i-1}_{2}\ldots\omega^{i-1}_{m}
for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
.
Each sequence
d_{1}d_{2}\ldots\in D_{b}^{\mathbb{N}}
induces a progressively overlapping sequence
\omega=\omega^{0}\omega^{1}\ldots\in(D_{b}^{m})^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
by letting
\omega^{i}=d_{i+1}\ldots d_{i+m}
for all
i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.

In contrast with progressively overlapping sequences we define the totally distinct sequences.
We say that the sequence
\omega
is
totally distinct at position-
k
if

\omega^{k}_{1}\ldots\omega^{k}_{m-j}\neq\omega^{k-j}_{j+1}\ldots\omega^{k-j}_{m}\quad\forall 1\leq j\leq\min\{k,m-1\}.

The sequence
\omega
is called
totally distinct
if it is totally distinct at position-
k
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

Example 1.3
.

Let
b\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 3}
and
m=2
. For any
d_{1}d_{2}d_{3}\ldots\in D_{b}^{\mathbb{N}}
let
\omega^{i}=d_{i+1}d_{i+2}
for all
i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Then
\omega=\omega^{0}\omega^{1}\ldots\in(D_{b}^{m})^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
is a progressively overlapping sequence.
Similarly, let
\tau^{i}=c_{i+1}d_{i+2}
with
c_{i+1}\neq d_{i+1}
for all
i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Then
\tau=\tau^{0}\tau^{1}\ldots\in(D_{b}^{m})^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
is a totally distinct sequence.
So, there exist uncountably many progressively overlapping sequences and uncountably many totally distinct sequences.

When
\omega\in(D_{b}^{m})^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
is a progressively overlapping or a totally distinct sequence, the dimensions in (
1.4
) are all equal for
K^{\omega}
.

Theorem 1.4
.

(i)

If
\omega\in(D_{b}^{m})^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
is a progressively overlapping sequence, then the set
K^{\omega}
is
\frac{\log\lambda}{\log b}
-Ahlfors regular,
where
\lambda\in(b-1,b)
is Pisot number satisfying the equation
x^{m}-(b-1)(x^{m-1}+x^{m-2}+\cdots+x+1)=0
.

(ii)

If
\omega\in(D_{b}^{m})^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
is a totally distinct sequence, then the set
K^{\omega}
is
\frac{\log\eta}{\log b}
-Ahlfors regular,
where
\eta\in(b-1,b)
is the Pisot number satisfying the equation
x^{m}-bx^{m-1}+1=0
.

Remark 1.5
.

If
\omega=\omega^{0}\omega^{1}\ldots\in(D_{b}^{m})^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
is eventually progressively overlapping, i.e.,
there exists
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\omega
is progressively overlapping at position-
k
for all
k\geq k_{0}
,
then the set
K^{\omega}
is also
\frac{\log\lambda}{\log b}
-Ahlfors regular. Similarly, if
\omega
is eventually totally distinct, then the set
K^{\omega}
is
\frac{\log\eta}{\log b}
-Ahlfors regular.

Next, we show that the dimensions obtained in Theorem
1.4
(i) and (ii) are the upper and lower bounds for fractal dimensions of
K^{\omega}
, respectively; and the Hausdorff and packing dimensions have the intermediate value property. Furthermore, the strict inequalities

\dim_{L}K^{\omega}<\dim_{H}K^{\omega}<\dim_{P}K^{\omega}<\dim_{A}K^{\omega}

hold for infinitely many sequences
\omega\in(D_{b}^{m})^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
.
In order to consider the Hausdorff dimension in the symbolic space
(D_{b}^{m})^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
, we equip
(D_{b}^{m})^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
with the following metric

(1.5)

\varrho(\omega,\tau)=b^{-m\cdot\inf\{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}:\omega^{k}\neq\tau^{k}\}}\quad\text{for}\quad\omega=\omega^{0}\omega^{1}\ldots,\penalty\ \tau=\tau^{0}\tau^{1}\ldots\in(D_{b}^{m})^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}.

Theorem 1.6
.

(i)

For any
\omega\in(D_{b}^{m})^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
we have

\frac{\log\eta}{\log b}\leq\dim_{L}K^{\omega}\leq\dim_{H}K^{\omega}\leq\dim_{P}K^{\omega}\leq\dim_{A}K^{\omega}\leq\frac{\log\lambda}{\log b},

where

and

.

(ii)

For any

\dim_{H}\Big\{\omega\in(D_{b}^{m})^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}:\dim_{H}K^{\omega}=\alpha,\penalty\ \dim_{P}K^{\omega}=\beta\Big\}\geq\frac{1}{m}.

(iii)

We have

\dim_{H}\Big\{\omega\in(D_{b}^{m})^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}:\dim_{L}K^{\omega}<\dim_{H}K^{\omega}<\dim_{P}K^{\omega}<\dim_{A}K^{\omega}\Big\}\geq\frac{1}{m}.

Remark 1.7
.

(i)

Note that

. For any

Adapting the proof of

\displaystyle=\min\left\{\dim_{H}L_{\alpha},\dim_{H}L_{\beta}\right\},

\displaystyle\dim_{P}\left\{\omega\in\left(D_{b}^{m}\right)^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}:\dim_{H}K^{\omega}=\alpha,\penalty\ \dim_{P}K^{\omega}=\beta\right\}

\displaystyle=\max\left\{\dim_{H}L_{\alpha},\dim_{H}L_{\beta}\right\}.

(ii)

In the proof of Theorem

. For any

\omega^{k}_{1}\neq\omega^{k-1}_{2},\;\omega^{k}_{2}\ldots\omega^{k}_{m-1}=\omega^{k-1}_{3}\ldots\omega^{k-1}_{m}&\mbox{for }\ell(p+q)+p<k\leq(\ell+1)(p+q)\end{cases}

for all
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Note that for any
(d_{i})\in D_{b}^{\mathbb{N}}
we can construct a sequence
\omega=\omega^{0}\omega^{1}\omega^{2}\ldots\in L_{p,q}
by setting
\omega^{0}_{1}\ldots\omega^{0}_{m}=d_{1}\ldots d_{m}
,
\omega^{k}_{1}\ldots\omega^{k}_{m}=d_{k+1}\ldots d_{k+m}
for
\ell(p+q)<k\leq\ell(p+q)+p
, and
\omega^{k}_{1}\neq d_{k+1},\omega^{k}_{2}\ldots\omega^{k}_{m}=d_{k+2}\ldots d_{k+m}
for
\ell(p+q)+p<k\leq(\ell+1)(p+q)
, where
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. This implies that for any
p,q\in\mathbb{N}
the set

is uncountable.

Theorem 1.8
.

For any
p,q\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists
\lambda_{p,q}>1
such that the set
K^{\omega}
is
\frac{\log\lambda_{p,q}}{(p+q)\log b}
-Ahlfors regular for all
\omega\in L_{p,q}
.
Moreover, the set

\bigg\{\frac{\log\lambda_{p,q}}{(p+q)\log b}:p,q\in\mathbb{N}\bigg\}

is dense in
[\frac{\log\eta}{\log b},\frac{\log\lambda}{\log b}]
, where
\lambda
and
\eta
are defined as in Theorem
1.4
.

1.2.
Badly approximable numbers and non-recurrence points

Based on the study of open dynamical systems with a moving hole, we consider the set of badly approximable numbers.

Given an integer
b\geq 3
, let
T_{b}:[0,1)\to[0,1);\penalty\ x\mapsto bx\pmod{1}
be the expanding map on the circle. For
a sequence of open balls
\{B_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
in
[0,1)
, the following set

W(\left\{B_{n}\right\}):=\left\{x\in[0,1):T_{b}^{n}(x)\in B_{n}\textrm{ for infinitely many }n\right\}

is well studied in Diophantine approximation, which is called the
shrinking target problem
by Hill and Velani
[
29
]
. Since
W(\left\{B_{n}\right\})=\limsup_{n\to\infty}T_{b}^{-n}(B_{n})
, the set
W(\left\{B_{n}\right\})
is also called a
limsup set
. It is well known that whether the set
W(\left\{B_{n}\right\})
having zero or full Lebesgue measure depends only on the convergence or divergence of the series
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}r_{n}
(cf.
[
36
]
), where
r_{n}
is the radius of
B_{n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. When
W(\left\{B_{n}\right\})
has zero Lebesgue measure, or equivalently, when
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}r_{n}<+\infty
,
Bugeaud and Wang
[
10
, Theorem 1.6]
showed that

\dim_{H}W(\left\{B_{n}\right\})=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{n}{n-\log_{b}r_{n}}
. For more literature on the well approximable numbers we refer to
[
5
,
36
,
53
]
and the references therein.

When the series
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}r_{n}=\infty
, the set
W(\left\{B_{n}\right\})
has full Lebesgue measure, and then its complement

\begin{split}K\left(\left\{B_{n}\right\}\right)&:=[0,1)\setminus W(\left\{B_{n}\right\})=\left\{x\in[0,1):T_{b}^{n}(x)\notin B_{n}\textrm{ for all but finitely many }n\right\}\end{split}

is a null set. It is natural to consider the Hausdorff dimension of
K\left(\left\{B_{n}\right\}\right)
. The set
K(\left\{B_{n}\right\})
is closely related to the set of badly approximable numbers (see
[
41
]
). Since
K(\left\{B_{n}\right\})=\liminf_{n\to\infty}T_{b}^{-n}([0,1)\setminus B_{n})
, we also call
K(\left\{B_{n}\right\})
a
liminf set
in Diophantine approximation.
Our next result characterizes when
K\left(\left\{B_{n}\right\}\right)
has full Hausdorff dimension.

Theorem 1.9
.

(i)

If
\lim_{n\to\infty}\operatorname{diam}(B_{n})=0
, then
\dim_{H}K(\left\{B_{n}\right\})=1
.

(ii)

If
\dim_{H}K(\left\{B_{n}\right\})=1
, then
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\operatorname{diam}(B_{n})=0
.

Remark 1.10
.

(i)

If the limit
\lim_{n\to\infty}\operatorname{diam}(B_{n})
exists, then by Theorem
1.9
it follows that

\dim_{H}K\left(\left\{B_{n}\right\}\right)=1
if and only if
\lim_{n\to\infty}\operatorname{diam}(B_{n})=0.

(ii)

If the balls
B_{n}=B(y,\frac{1}{n+1})
have the same center
y\in[0,1)
, then

K\left(\left\{B_{n}\right\}\right)\supseteq\left\{x\in[0,1):y\notin\overline{\left\{T_{b}^{n}(x):n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\right\}}\right\}=:K\left(y\right),

which has full Hausdorff dimension. This result was first proved by Urbański
[
51
]
. In fact, Tseng
[
49
]
proved a stronger result that the set
K\left(y\right)
is
\alpha
-winning for some
\alpha\in(0,1/2]
in the sense of Schmidt’s game (cf.
[
45
]
).
For a general sequence of balls
\left\{B_{n}\right\}
with
\lim_{n\to\infty}\operatorname{diam}(B_{n})=0
, it might be interesting to ask whether
K\left(\left\{B_{n}\right\}\right)
is a winning set?

The classical Poincaré’s recurrence theorem states that for Lebesgue almost every
x\in[0,1)
its orbit
\left\{T_{b}^{n}(x):n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\right\}
returns to its neighborhood infinitely many times (cf.
[
52
]
). Boshernitzan
[
7
]
described this type of recurrence results quantitatively.
Given a rate function
\phi:\mathbb{N}\to(0,\infty)
, Tan and Wang showed in
[
48
]
that the Hausdorff dimension of the set

R(\phi):=\left\{x\in[0,1):|T_{b}^{n}(x)-x|<\phi(n)\textrm{ for infinitely many }n\right\}

is
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{n}{n-\log_{b}\phi(n)}
. In this paper we also consider its complement, that is

E\left(\phi\right):=[0,1)\setminus R(\phi)=\left\{x\in[0,1):|T_{b}^{n}(x)-x|\geq\phi(n)\textrm{ for all but finitely many }n\right\}.

Theorem 1.11
.

(i)

If
\lim_{n\to\infty}\phi(n)=0
, then
\dim_{H}E(\phi)=1
.

(ii)

If
\dim_{H}E(\phi)=1
, then
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\phi(n)=0
.

Remark 1.12
.

Similar to Remark
1.10
(i), if the limit
\lim_{n\to\infty}\phi(n)
exists, then Theorem
1.11
implies that

\dim_{H}E\left(\phi\right)=1
if and only if
\lim_{n\to\infty}\phi(n)=0.

1.3.
Finiteness property for the joint spectral radius

Given a finite set of
d\times d
real matrices
\mathcal{M}=\left\{M_{1},\ldots,M_{r}\right\}
, Rota and Strang
[
44
]
introduced the concept of the
joint spectral radius

(1.6)

\widehat{\rho}(\mathcal{M}):=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{i_{1}\ldots i_{n}\in\left\{1,2,\ldots,r\right\}^{n}}\left\|M_{i_{1}}\cdots M_{i_{n}}\right\|^{1/n},

which extends the notion of spectral radius of a single matrix.
The set
\mathcal{M}
is said to satisfy the
finiteness property
if there exists a finite block
i_{1}\ldots i_{n}\in\left\{1,2,\ldots,r\right\}^{n}
such that

\widehat{\rho}(\mathcal{M})=\rho\left(M_{i_{1}}\cdots M_{i_{n}}\right)^{1/n},

where
\rho(M)
denotes the spectral radius of a matrix
M
.
Lagarias and Wang
[
35
]
conjectured that every finite set of
d\times d
real matrices possesses the finiteness property.
However, this conjecture was proven to be false in a general setting (cf.
[
6
,
8
,
28
]
); but it still
holds in many situations (cf.
[
1
,
13
,
30
,
34
,
39
,
42
]
).
In particular, if
\mathcal{M}
is a finite set of
d\times d
nonnegative matrices with
\widehat{\rho}(\mathcal{M})=\rho(\sum_{M\in\mathcal{M}}M)
, then
\mathcal{M}
has the finiteness property (cf.
[
14
]
).

Jungers and Blondel
[
31
, Theorem 4]
established the following key equivalence: the finiteness property holds for all sets of nonnegative rational square matrices if and only if it holds for all pairs of binary square matrices.
They also proved that the finiteness property holds for all pairs of
2\times 2
binary matrices.
Note that each adjacency matrix
A_{\mathbf{d}}
of the directed graph
G_{\mathbf{d}}
is a
0-1
matrix. Our final result says that the set
\left\{A_{\mathbf{d}}:\mathbf{d}\in D_{b}^{m}\right\}
of adjacency matrices satisfies the finiteness property.

Theorem 1.13
.

The set
\mathcal{A}=\left\{A_{\mathbf{d}}:\mathbf{d}\in D_{b}^{m}\right\}
has the finiteness property.
Furthermore, for any periodic and progressively overlapping sequence
\omega=(\omega^{0}\omega^{1}\ldots\omega^{n-1})^{\infty}\in\left(D_{b}^{m}\right)^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
we have

\widehat{\rho}(\mathcal{A})=\rho(A_{\omega^{0}}A_{\omega^{1}}\cdots A_{\omega^{n-1}})^{1/n}=\lambda,

where
\lambda\in(b-1,b)
is defined as in Theorem
1.4
.

Indeed Theorem
1.13
shows that the finiteness property
\widehat{\rho}(\mathcal{A})=\rho(A_{\omega^{0}}A_{\omega^{1}}\ldots A_{\omega^{n-1}})^{1/n}
holds for infinitely many blocks
\omega^{0}\omega^{1}\ldots\omega^{n-1}\in\left(D_{b}^{m}\right)^{*},n\in\mathbb{N}
.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In the next section we consider the dimensions of
K^{\omega}
for a general
\omega\in(D_{b}^{m})^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
, and prove Theorem
1.1
. When
\omega
is progressively overlapping or totally distinct, we show in Section
3
that all of these fractal dimensions of
K^{\omega}
are equal. In fact, we show in this case that
K^{\omega}
is Ahlfors regular (see Theorem
1.4
). In Section
4
, we prove the intermediate value property for the dimensions of
K^{\omega}
, and show that there are infinitely many
\omega\in(D_{b}^{m})^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
such that the dimensions of
K^{\omega}
are not equal (see Theorem
1.6
).
In Section
5
, we study some special sequences
\omega\in(D_{b}^{m})^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
for which
\dim_{H}K^{\omega}=\dim_{P}K^{\omega}
, and prove Theorem
1.8
.
As applications, we prove in Section
6
Theorems
1.9
and
1.11
for badly approximable numbers in Diophantine approximation and non-recurrence points, respectively; and prove the finiteness property for the joint spectral radius of matrices (see Theorem
1.13
). Finally, in Section
7
we make some remarks on possible extensions of our work to higher dimensions or to homogeneous self-similar IFSs.

Note that

For a sequence

Lemma 2.1

For any

(b-1)|\Sigma_{k}^{\omega}|\leq|\Sigma_{k+1}^{\omega}|\leq b|\Sigma_{k}^{\omega}|.

Note by (

Now we assume

∎

For

Lemma 2.2

Let

Thus, for

Write

\mathbf{c}u_{1}\ldots u_{m-1}\mathbf{d}\in\Sigma_{k+n}^{\omega}\quad\text{for any}\penalty\ \mathbf{d}\in\Sigma_{n-m+1}^{\tau}.

Write

(2.5)

For

1\leq i\leq q

\{\mathbf{d}\in\Sigma_{k}^{\omega}:I_{\mathbf{d}}\cap U_{i}\neq\emptyset\}\subset\Sigma_{k}^{\omega}(\mathbf{c}_{i})\cup\Sigma_{k}^{\omega}(\widetilde{\mathbf{c}}_{i}).

By (
2.11
) and Lemma
2.2
we obtain

|\Sigma_{k}^{\omega}|\leq\sum_{i=1}^{q}|\{\mathbf{d}\in\Sigma_{k}^{\omega}:I_{\mathbf{d}}\cap U_{i}\neq\emptyset\}|\leq\sum_{i=1}^{q}\left(|\Sigma_{k}^{\omega}(\mathbf{c}_{i})|+|\Sigma_{k}^{\omega}(\widetilde{\mathbf{c}}_{i})|\right)\leq 2b^{m-1}|\Sigma_{k}^{\omega}|\sum_{i=1}^{q}|\Sigma_{k_{i}}^{\omega}|^{-1}.

Note that
b\geq 3
. It follows that

(2.12)

\sum_{i=1}^{q}|\Sigma_{k_{i}}^{\omega}|^{-1}\geq b^{-m}.

Since
k_{i}\geq k_{0}
, by (
2.8
) and (
2.10
) we have

|\Sigma_{k_{i}}^{\omega}|^{-1}\leq b^{-k_{i}t}<b^{s}\big(\operatorname{diam}(U_{i})\big)^{t}
.
Then (
2.9
) follows directly from (
2.12
).
Thus we obtain
\dim_{H}K^{\omega}\geq t
.
Since
t\in(0,s)
was chosen arbitrarily, we conclude that
\dim_{H}K^{\omega}\geq s
.

In order to show
\dim_{P}K^{\omega}=\overline{\dim}_{B}K^{\omega}
, by
[
20
, Corollary 3.10]
it suffices to show that for any open set
V
with
V\cap K^{\omega}\neq\emptyset
,

(2.13)

\overline{\dim}_{B}\big(V\cap K^{\omega}\big)=\overline{\dim}_{B}K^{\omega}=\limsup_{k\to\infty}\frac{\log|\Sigma_{k}^{\omega}|}{k\log b}.

Take an open set
V
with
V\cap K^{\omega}\neq\emptyset
.
Then we can find
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq m}
and
\mathbf{c}\in\Sigma_{k_{0}}^{\omega}
such that
I_{\mathbf{c}}\cap K^{\omega}\subset V\cap K^{\omega}.

Recall the definition of
\Sigma_{k}^{\omega}(\mathbf{c})
in (
2.2
).
Then for any
k>k_{0}
we have

\Sigma_{k}^{\omega}(\mathbf{c})\subset\big\{\mathbf{d}\in D_{b}^{k}:I_{\mathbf{d}}\cap(I_{\mathbf{c}}\cap K^{\omega})\neq\emptyset\big\}.

Thus, we obtain

\overline{\dim}_{B}\big(V\cap K^{\omega}\big)\geq\overline{\dim}_{B}\big(I_{\mathbf{c}}\cap K^{\omega}\big)\geq\limsup_{k\to\infty}\frac{\log|\Sigma_{k}^{\omega}(\mathbf{c})|}{k\log b}.

It follows from Lemma
2.2
that

\overline{\dim}_{B}\big(V\cap K^{\omega}\big)\geq\limsup_{k\to\infty}\frac{\log|\Sigma_{k}^{\omega}|-\log|\Sigma_{k_{0}}^{\omega}|-(m-1)\log b}{k\log b}=\limsup_{k\to\infty}\frac{\log|\Sigma_{k}^{\omega}|}{k\log b}.

We clearly have

\overline{\dim}_{B}\big(V\cap K^{\omega}\big)\leq\overline{\dim}_{B}K^{\omega}=\limsup_{k\to\infty}\frac{\log|\Sigma_{k}^{\omega}|}{k\log b}.

Therefore, we obtain (
2.13
), completing the proof.
∎

In general, we can not expect
\dim_{H}K^{\omega}=\dim_{P}K^{\omega}
. However, if
\omega
is a progressively overlapping sequence or a totally distinct sequence, then we have the equality
\dim_{H}K^{\omega}=\dim_{P}K^{\omega}
, which will be discussed in the next section.

3.
Dimensions of
K^{\omega}
for progressively overlapping or totally distinct
\omega

In this section we will prove the dimension equality of
K^{\omega}
for progressively overlapping and totally distinct sequences
\omega\in(D_{b}^{m})^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
, and establish Theorem
1.4
.
Recall the definitions of progressively overlapping and totally distinct sequences in subsection
1.1
, and keep in mind that
b\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 3}
and
m\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 2}
.

Proposition 3.1
.

For
\omega\in(D_{b}^{m})^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
, if there exist
\beta>1
and
C>1
such that

C^{-1}\beta^{k}\leq|\Sigma_{k}^{\omega}|\leq C\beta^{k}\quad\forall k\in\mathbb{N},

then the set
K^{\omega}
is
\frac{\log\beta}{\log b}
-Ahlfors regular.

Proof.

Write
s=\log\beta/\log b
. It suffices to show that there exists a constant
c\geq 1
such that for all
0<r<b^{-m}
and for all
x\in K^{\omega}
,

(3.1)

c^{-1}r^{s}\leq\mathcal{H}^{s}\big(K^{\omega}\cap B(x,r)\big)\leq cr^{s}.

Fix
0<r<b^{-m}
and
x\in K^{\omega}
. Choose
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq m}
such that

(3.2)

\frac{1}{b^{k_{0}+1}}\leq r<\frac{1}{b^{k_{0}}}.

We first show the upper bound in (
3.1
).
Since
r<b^{-k_{0}}
, the ball
B(x,r)
intersects at most three intervals in
\{I_{\mathbf{d}}:\mathbf{d}\in\Sigma_{k_{0}}^{\omega}\}
, denoted by
I_{\mathbf{c}_{1}},I_{\mathbf{c}_{2}},I_{\mathbf{c}_{3}}
with
\mathbf{c}_{i}\in\Sigma_{k_{0}}^{\omega}
for
1\leq i\leq 3
.
That is,
K^{\omega}\cap B(x,r)\subset I_{\mathbf{c}_{1}}\cup I_{\mathbf{c}_{2}}\cup I_{\mathbf{c}_{3}}
. Recall the definition of
\Sigma_{k_{0}+n}^{\omega}(\mathbf{c}_{i})
in (
2.2
).
For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the family of intervals
\mathcal{I}_{n}:=\{I_{\mathbf{d}}:\mathbf{d}\in\Sigma_{k_{0}+n}^{\omega}(\mathbf{c}_{1})\cup\Sigma_{k_{0}+n}^{\omega}(\mathbf{c}_{2})\cup\Sigma_{k_{0}+n}^{\omega}(\mathbf{c}_{3})\}
forms a
b^{-(k_{0}+n)}
-covering of
K^{\omega}\cap B(x,r)
.
By the assumption and using Lemma
2.2
and (
3.2
), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{I\in\mathcal{I}_{n}}\big(\mathrm{diam}(I)\big)^{s}

\displaystyle\leq|\mathcal{I}_{n}|b^{-s(k_{0}+n)}\leq\big(|\Sigma_{k_{0}+n}^{\omega}(\mathbf{c}_{1})|+|\Sigma_{k_{0}+n}^{\omega}(\mathbf{c}_{2})|+|\Sigma_{k_{0}+n}^{\omega}(\mathbf{c}_{3})|\big)b^{-s(k_{0}+n)}

\displaystyle\leq 3b^{m-1}\frac{|\Sigma_{k_{0}+n}^{\omega}|}{|\Sigma_{k_{0}}^{\omega}|}b^{-s(k_{0}+n)}\leq 3b^{m-1}C^{2}\cdot\beta^{n}b^{-s(k_{0}+n)}

\displaystyle\leq 3b^{m+s-1}C^{2}\cdot r^{s}.

This implies that

\mathcal{H}^{s}\big(K^{\omega}\cap B(x,r)\big)\leq 3\beta b^{m-1}C^{2}\cdot r^{s}.

In order to show the lower bound in (
3.1
), noting that
K^{\omega}\cap B(x,r)
is compact, it suffices to show that for any finite open
b^{-k_{0}}
-covering
\left\{U_{i}\right\}_{i=1}^{q}
of
K^{\omega}\cap B(x,r)
,

\sum_{i=1}^{q}\big(\operatorname{diam}(U_{i})\big)^{s}\geq c^{-1}r^{s}.

Let
\left\{U_{i}\right\}_{i=1}^{q}
be an open
b^{-k_{0}}
-covering of
K^{\omega}
.
For each
1\leq i\leq q
, choose
k_{i}\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq k_{0}}
such that

(3.3)

{b}^{-(k_{i}+1)}\leq\operatorname{diam}(U_{i})<{b}^{-k_{i}}.

Then the set
U_{i}
intersects at most two intervals in
\{I_{\mathbf{d}}:\mathbf{d}\in\Sigma_{k_{i}}^{\omega}\}
, denoted by
I_{\mathbf{c}_{i}},I_{\widetilde{\mathbf{c}}_{i}}
with
\mathbf{c}_{i},\widetilde{\mathbf{c}}_{i}\in\Sigma_{k_{i}}^{\omega}
.
Since
r\geq b^{-(k_{0}+1)}
, we can find
\mathbf{c}\in\Sigma_{k_{0}+1}^{\omega}
such that
K^{\omega}\cap I_{\mathbf{c}}\subset K^{\omega}\cap B(x,r)
.
Fix an integer
k>\max\{k_{0}+1,k_{1},\ldots,k_{q}\}
.
Recall the definitions of
\Sigma_{k}^{\omega}(\mathbf{c}_{i})
,
\Sigma_{k}^{\omega}(\widetilde{\mathbf{c}}_{i})
, and
\Sigma_{k}^{\omega}(\mathbf{c})
in (
2.2
).
Note that
K^{\omega}\cap I_{\mathbf{c}}\subset K^{\omega}\cap B(x,r)
and
\{U_{i}\}_{i=1}^{q}
is a cover of
K^{\omega}
.
Then we have

\Sigma_{k}^{\omega}(\mathbf{c})\subset\{\mathbf{d}\in\Sigma_{k}^{\omega}:I_{\mathbf{d}}\cap K^{\omega}\cap B(x,r)\neq\emptyset\}\subset\bigcup_{i=1}^{q}\big(\Sigma_{k}^{\omega}(\mathbf{c}_{i})\cup\Sigma_{k}^{\omega}(\widetilde{\mathbf{c}}_{i})\big).

It follows that

|\Sigma_{k}^{\omega}(\mathbf{c})|\leq\sum_{i=1}^{q}\big(|\Sigma_{k}^{\omega}(\mathbf{c}_{i})|+|\Sigma_{k}^{\omega}(\widetilde{\mathbf{c}}_{i})|\big).

By Lemma
2.2
, we obtain

b^{-(m-1)}\frac{|\Sigma_{k}^{\omega}|}{|\Sigma_{k_{0}+1}^{\omega}|}\leq|\Sigma_{k}^{\omega}(\mathbf{c})|\leq 2b^{m-1}\sum_{i=1}^{q}\frac{|\Sigma_{k}^{\omega}|}{|\Sigma_{k_{i}}^{\omega}|}.

By the assumption this implies

\frac{\beta^{-(k_{0}+1)}}{b^{m-1}C}\leq 2b^{m-1}C\sum_{i=1}^{q}\beta^{-k_{i}}.

This together with (
3.2
) and (
3.3
) implies that

\sum_{i=1}^{q}\big(\operatorname{diam}(U_{i})\big)^{s}\geq\sum_{i=1}^{q}b^{-s(k_{i}+1)}=\sum_{i=1}^{q}\beta^{-(k_{i}+1)}\geq\frac{\beta^{-(k_{0}+2)}}{2b^{2m-2}C^{2}}\geq\frac{r^{s}}{2\beta^{2}b^{2m-2}C^{2}}.

Hence, (
3.1
) holds by taking
c=\max\left\{3\beta b^{m-1}C^{2},2\beta^{2}b^{2m-2}C^{2}\right\}
, completing the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

For any
\omega\in(D_{b}^{m})^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
and any
k\in\mathbb{N}
we have

b|\Sigma_{k+m-1}^{\omega}|-|\Sigma_{k}^{\omega}|\leq|\Sigma_{k+m}^{\omega}|\leq(b-1)\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}|\Sigma_{k+j}^{\omega}|.

Moreover, the lower bound equality holds if
\omega
is totally distinct at position-
k
, and the upper bound equality holds if
\omega
is progressively overlapping at position-
k
.

Proof.

Write
\omega=\omega^{0}\omega^{1}\omega^{2}\ldots\in(D_{b}^{m})^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
with each
\omega^{i}=\omega_{1}^{i}\omega_{2}^{i}\ldots\omega_{m}^{i}\in D_{b}^{m}
, and fix
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

(i) We first show the lower bound.
Let

H_{k+m}^{\omega}:=\big\{d_{1}d_{2}\ldots d_{k+m}\in D_{b}^{k+m}:d_{1}d_{2}\ldots d_{k}\in\Sigma_{k}^{\omega},d_{k+1}d_{k+2}\ldots d_{k+m}=\omega^{k}\big\}.

Then it’s clear that
H_{k+m}^{\omega}\cap\Sigma_{k+m}^{\omega}=\emptyset
.
For any
d_{1}\ldots d_{k+m-1}\in\Sigma_{k+m-1}^{\omega}
and any
d_{k+m}\in D_{b}
, if
d_{k+1}d_{k+2}\ldots d_{k+m}\neq\omega_{1}^{k}\omega_{2}^{k}\ldots\omega_{m}^{k}
, then
d_{1}\ldots d_{k+m}\in\Sigma_{k+m}^{\omega}
; otherwise, we have
d_{1}\ldots d_{k+m}\in H_{k+m}^{\omega}
because
d_{1}\ldots d_{k}\in\Sigma_{k}^{\omega}
.
It follows that

(3.4)

\Sigma_{k+m-1}^{\omega}\times D_{b}\subset H_{k+m}^{\omega}\cup\Sigma_{k+m}^{\omega},

which implies that

|\Sigma_{k+m}^{\omega}|\geq|\Sigma_{k+m-1}^{\omega}\times D_{b}|-|H_{k+m}^{\omega}|=b|\Sigma_{k+m-1}^{\omega}|-|\Sigma_{k}^{\omega}|.

Next, we assume that
\omega
is totally distinct at position-
k
. In order to show the equality, by (
3.4
) it suffices to show

H_{k+m}^{\omega}\cup\Sigma_{k+m}^{\omega}\subset\Sigma_{k+m-1}^{\omega}\times D_{b}.

We clearly have
\Sigma_{k+m}^{\omega}\subset\Sigma_{k+m-1}^{\omega}\times D_{b}
. It remains to show

(3.5)

H_{k+m}^{\omega}\subset\Sigma_{k+m-1}^{\omega}\times D_{b}.

Take a word
d_{1}d_{2}\ldots d_{k+m}\in H_{k+m}^{\omega}
. Then
d_{1}\ldots d_{k}\in\Sigma_{k}^{\omega}
and
d_{k+1}d_{k+2}\ldots d_{k+m}=\omega_{1}^{k}\omega_{2}^{k}\ldots\omega_{m}^{k}
.
Since
\omega
is totally distinct at position-
k
, we have

\omega^{k}_{1}\ldots\omega^{k}_{m-j}\neq\omega^{k-j}_{j+1}\ldots\omega^{k-j}_{m}\quad\forall 1\leq j\leq\min\{k,m-1\}.

It follows that for
\max\{k-m+1,0\}\leq i<k
,

d_{i+1}\ldots d_{i+m}=d_{i+1}\ldots d_{k}\omega^{k}_{1}\ldots\omega^{k}_{m-k+i}\neq\omega^{i}.

This implies that
d_{1}\ldots d_{k+m-1}\in\Sigma_{k+m-1}^{\omega}
. Thus, we obtain (
3.5
) as desired.

(ii) Now we prove the upper bound.
Note by (
2.1
) that each
d_{1}d_{2}\ldots d_{k+m}\in\Sigma_{k+m}^{\omega}
satisfies
d_{k+1}d_{k+2}\ldots d_{k+m}\neq\omega_{1}^{k}\omega_{2}^{k}\ldots\omega_{m}^{k}
. Conditioned on the maximum index
j
such that
d_{k+j}\neq\omega_{j}^{k}
we make the following partition of
\Sigma_{k+m}^{\omega}
as

(3.6)

\Sigma_{k+m}^{\omega}=\bigcup_{j=1}^{m}\Sigma_{k+m}^{\omega}(j),

where for
1\leq j\leq m
,

\Sigma_{k+m}^{\omega}(j):=\big\{d_{1}d_{2}\ldots d_{k+m}\in\Sigma_{k+m}^{\omega}:d_{k+j}\neq\omega^{k}_{j},d_{k+j+1}\ldots d_{k+m}=\omega^{k}_{j+1}\ldots\omega^{k}_{m}\big\}.

If
d_{1}\ldots d_{k+m}\in\Sigma_{k+m}^{\omega}(j)
, then it has the form
d_{1}\ldots d_{k+j-1}d_{k+j}\omega^{k}_{j+1}\omega_{j+2}^{k}\ldots\omega_{m}^{k}
with
d_{1}\ldots d_{k+j-1}\in\Sigma_{k+j-1}^{\omega}
and
d_{k+j}\in D_{b}\setminus\big\{\omega^{k}_{j}\big\}
. So,

|\Sigma_{k+m}^{\omega}(j)|\leq(b-1)|\Sigma_{k+j-1}^{\omega}|
for all
1\leq j\leq m
.
By (
3.6
) it follows that

(3.7)

|\Sigma_{k+m}^{\omega}|=\sum_{j=1}^{m}|\Sigma_{k+m}^{\omega}(j)|\leq(b-1)\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}|\Sigma_{k+j}^{\omega}|.

Next, we assume that
\omega
is progressively overlapping at position-
k
.
Given
1\leq j\leq m
, we take a word
d_{1}d_{2}\ldots d_{k+j-1}\in\Sigma_{k+j-1}^{\omega}
and take a digit
d_{k+j}\in D_{b}\setminus\big\{\omega^{k}_{j}\big\}
. We claim that

c_{1}c_{2}\ldots c_{k+m}:=d_{1}d_{2}\ldots d_{k+j-1}d_{k+j}\omega^{k}_{j+1}\omega^{k}_{j+2}\ldots\omega_{m}^{k}\in\Sigma_{k+m}^{\omega}(j).

By the definition of
\Sigma_{k+m}^{\omega}(j)
it suffices to prove that the word
c_{1}\ldots c_{k+m}\in\Sigma_{k+m}^{\omega}
.
Note that
c_{1}\ldots c_{k+j-1}=d_{1}\ldots d_{k+j-1}\in\Sigma_{k+j-1}^{\omega}
.
We only need to verify

(3.8)

c_{i+1}c_{i+2}\ldots c_{i+m}\neq\omega_{1}^{i}\omega_{2}^{i}\ldots\omega_{m}^{i}\quad\forall\max\{k+j-m,0\}\leq i\leq k.

Since
\omega
is progressively overlapping at position-
k
, we have

\omega^{k}_{1}\ldots\omega^{k}_{m-\ell}=\omega^{k-\ell}_{\ell+1}\ldots\omega^{k-\ell}_{m}\quad\forall 1\leq\ell\leq\min\{k,m-1\}.

Thus, for
\max\left\{k+j-m,0\right\}\leq i\leq k
we have

c_{i+(k+j-i)}=c_{k+j}=d_{k+j}\neq\omega^{k}_{j}=\omega_{k+j-i}^{i}.

This implies that
c_{i+1}c_{i+2}\ldots c_{i+m}\neq\omega_{1}^{i}\omega_{2}^{i}\ldots\omega_{m}^{i}
, which establishes (
3.8
).

By (
3.8
) it follows that

\left\{d_{1}\ldots d_{k+j}\omega_{j+1}^{k}\ldots\omega_{m}^{k}:d_{1}\ldots d_{k+j-1}\in\Sigma_{k+j-1}^{\omega},d_{k+j}\in D_{b}\setminus\{\omega_{j}^{k}\}\right\}\subset\Sigma_{k+m}^{\omega}(j),

which implies that
(b-1)|\Sigma_{k+j-1}^{\omega}|\leq|\Sigma_{k+m}^{\omega}(j)|
. By (
3.6
) we obtain that

|\Sigma_{k+m}^{\omega}|=\sum_{j=1}^{m}|\Sigma_{k+m}^{\omega}(j)|\geq(b-1)\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}|\Sigma_{k+j}^{\omega}|.

This together with (
3.7
) proves the equality.
∎

Recall that a
Pisot number
is an algebraic integer greater than
1
whose algebraic conjugates are of modulus strictly less than
1
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Given
b\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 3}
and
m\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 2}
, let
\lambda
and
\eta
be the largest real root of

x^{m}=(b-1)(x^{m-1}+x^{m-2}+\cdots+x+1)\quad\textrm{and}\quad x^{m}=bx^{m-1}-1,

respectively. Then
b-1<\eta<\lambda<b
, and both
\lambda
and
\eta
are Pisot numbers.

Proof.

Let
f(x):=x^{m}-(b-1)(x^{m-1}+x^{m-2}+\cdots+x+1)
.
It is easy to verify that
f(b-1)<0
and
f(x)\geq f(b)=1
for all
x\geq b
.
By the intermediate value theorem the largest real root
\lambda\in(b-1,b)
.
Write
\widetilde{f}(x):=(x-1)f(x)=x^{m+1}-bx^{m}+b-1=f_{1}(x)-f_{2}(x)
with
f_{1}(x):=x^{m+1}
and
f_{2}(x):=bx^{m}-(b-1)
.
Note that

\lim_{x\to 1^{+}}\frac{f_{2}(x)-1}{f_{1}(x)-1}=\frac{mb}{m+1}>1.

There is
r_{0}>1
such that
br^{m}-(b-1)=f_{2}(r)>f_{1}(r)=r^{m+1}
for all
1<r<r_{0}
.
Fix
1<r<r_{0}
. For any
z\in\mathbb{C}
with
|z|=r
, we have

|f_{2}(z)|=|bz^{m}-(b-1)|\geq br^{m}-(b-1)>r^{m+1}=|f_{1}(z)|.

Since
f_{2}(x)=bx^{m}-(b-1)
has
m
zeros in
\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<r\}
, by Rouche’s theorem (cf.
[
47
]
) we obtain that
\widetilde{f}(x)
also has
m
zeros in
\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<r\}
.
By the arbitrariness of
r>1
, we conclude that
\widetilde{f}(x)
has
m
zeros in
\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|\leq 1\}
.
If
z\in\mathbb{C}
with
|z|=1
and
\widetilde{f}(z)=0
, then we have

0=|\widetilde{f}(z)|=|z^{m+1}-bz^{m}+b-1|\geq|b-z|-(b-1)\geq b-|z|-(b-1)=0,

which implies that
z=1
.
Note by using
m\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 2},b\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 3}
that the derivative
\widetilde{f}^{\prime}(1)\neq 0
. Thus,
z=1
is the unique zero of
\widetilde{f}
on the unit circle. This implies that
f(x)=\widetilde{f}(x)/(x-1)
has
m-1
zeros inside the unit circle.
It follows that
\lambda
is a Pisot number.

Let
g(x):=x^{m}-bx^{m-1}+1
.
It is easy to check
g(b-1)<0
and
g(x)\geq g(b)=1
for all
x\geq b
. By the intermediate value theorem the largest real root
\eta\in(b-1,b)
.
Write
g_{1}(x):=bx^{m-1}-1
and
g_{2}(x):=x^{m}
.
Note by
b\geq 3
that

|g_{1}(z)|=|bz^{m-1}-1|\geq b-1>1=|g_{2}(z)|\quad\text{for any}\penalty\ z\in\mathbb{C}\penalty\ \text{with}\penalty\ |z|=1.

By Rouche’s theorem,
g_{1}(x)
and
g(x)=g_{2}(x)-g_{1}(x)
have the same number of zeros inside the unit circle.
Since
g_{1}(x)=bx^{m-1}-1
has
m-1
zeros inside the unit circle, so is
g(x)
.
It follows that
\eta
is a Pisot number.
Since
1<\lambda<b
and
\lambda^{m}(\lambda-1)=(b-1)(\lambda^{m}-1)
, we have

\displaystyle(\lambda-1)g(\lambda)

\displaystyle=(\lambda-1)(\lambda^{m}-b\lambda^{m-1}+1)=(b-1)(\lambda^{m}-1)+(\lambda-1)(1-b\lambda^{m-1})

\displaystyle=b\lambda^{m-1}-\lambda^{m}+\lambda-b=(\lambda^{m-1}-1)(b-\lambda)>0,

which implies that
g(\lambda)>0
.
By the definition of
\eta
we conclude that
\eta<\lambda
.
∎

For
\mathbf{v}=(v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\penalty\ \text{or}\penalty\ \mathbb{C}^{n}

Set

(3.9)

V:=\big\{\mathbf{v}=(v_{m},\ldots,v_{1})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}:\|\mathbf{v}\|=1,\penalty\ v_{1}>0,\penalty\ (b-1)v_{i}\leq v_{i+1}\leq bv_{i}\penalty\ \penalty\ \forall 1\leq i<m\big\}.

Let

(3.10)

A:=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc}b-1&1&0&\cdots&0\\

it follows that

and
\eta
are the spectral radii of

and

, respectively.

Lemma 3.4
.

There exists a constant
C_{1}>1

b

and for all
\mathbf{v}\in V

C_{1}^{-1}\lambda^{n}\leq\|\mathbf{v}A^{n}\|\leq C_{1}\lambda^{n}\quad\text{and}\quad C_{1}^{-1}\eta^{n}\leq\|\mathbf{v}B^{n}\|\leq C_{1}\eta^{n},

where
\lambda

\eta
are defined as in Theorem

Proof.

We first handle the matrix
A

is a primitive nonnegative matrix (cf.
[

[
38

1\leq i,j\leq m
,

c^{-1}\lambda^{k}\leq(A^{k})_{i,j}\leq c\lambda^{k}\quad\forall k\in\mathbb{N},

where
\lambda
is the largest real root of
x^{m}=(b-1)(x^{m-1}+x^{m-2}+\cdots+x+1)
.
Note that for any
\mathbf{v}=(v_{m},\ldots,v_{1})\in V
we have

\delta_{0}\leq v_{i}<1
for all
1\leq i\leq m,

where
\delta_{0}=(1+b+\cdots+b^{m-1})^{-1}>0
.
Therefore, we conclude that for
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\mathbf{v}\in V
,

c^{-1}m^{2}\delta_{0}\lambda^{n}\leq\|\mathbf{v}A^{n}\|\leq cm^{2}\lambda^{n}.

Next, we focus on the matrix
B
. We first show that for any
\mathbf{v}\in V
the sequence
\{\|\mathbf{v}B^{n}\|\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
is increasing.
Write
\mathbf{v}=(v_{m},\ldots,v_{1})\in V
.
For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we recursively define
v_{n+m}=bv_{n+m-1}-v_{n}
.
Then we have
\mathbf{v}B^{n}=(v_{n+m},\ldots,v_{n+1})
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.
Note that
v_{m}>v_{m-1}>\cdots>v_{1}
. Then
v_{m+1}=bv_{m}-v_{1}=(b-1)v_{m}+(v_{m}-v_{1})>v_{m}
. By induction, we can show that the sequence
\{v_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is increasing. It follows that the sequence
\{\|\mathbf{v}B^{n}\|\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
is increasing.

Write
g(x)=x^{m}-bx^{m-1}+1
for the characteristic polynomial of
B
.
Note that all roots of equation
g^{\prime}(x)=0
are
x=0
and
x=b(m-1)/m>1
.
By Lemma
3.3
,
x=\eta
is the unique root of equation
g(x)=0
outside the unit circle.
Thus, the equations
g(x)=0
and
g^{\prime}(x)=0
do not share a common root.
This means that the equation
g(x)=0
has
m
distinct roots in
\mathbb{C}
, denoted by
\eta,\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{m-1}
.
By Lemma
3.3
, we have
|\eta_{i}|<1
for all
1\leq i\leq m-1
.
Let
\mathbf{v}^{0},\mathbf{v}^{1},\mathbf{v}^{2},\ldots,\mathbf{v}^{m-1}\in\mathbb{C}^{m}
with
\|\mathbf{v}_{i}\|=1
be the left eigenvectors corresponding to
\eta,\eta_{1},\eta_{2},\ldots,\eta_{m-1}
, respectively. That is,
\mathbf{v}^{0}B=\eta\mathbf{v}^{0}
and
\mathbf{v}^{i}B=\eta_{i}\mathbf{v}^{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq m-1
.
Then
\mathbf{v}^{0},\mathbf{v}^{1},\mathbf{v}^{2},\ldots,\mathbf{v}^{m-1}
are linearly independent in
\mathbb{C}^{m}
.
So, each
\mathbf{v}\in V
can be uniquely written as

\mathbf{v}=c_{0}(\mathbf{v})\mathbf{v}^{0}+c_{1}(\mathbf{v})\mathbf{v}^{1}+\cdots+c_{m-1}(\mathbf{v})\mathbf{v}^{m-1}\quad\text{with each}\quad c_{i}(\mathbf{v})\in\mathbb{C}.

We claim that
c_{0}(\mathbf{v})\neq 0
for any
\mathbf{v}\in V
.

Suppose on the contrary
c_{0}(\mathbf{v})=0
for some
\mathbf{v}=(v_{m},\ldots,v_{1})\in V
. Then we have

\mathbf{v}B^{n}=c_{1}(\mathbf{v})\eta_{1}^{n}\mathbf{v}^{1}+\cdots+c_{m-1}(\mathbf{v})\eta_{m-1}^{n}\mathbf{v}^{m-1}\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}.

Note that
|\eta_{i}|<1
for all
1\leq i\leq m-1
. We obtain that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|\mathbf{v}B^{n}\|=0.

However, we have proved that
\|\mathbf{v}B^{n}\|\geq\|\mathbf{v}\|=1
.
This leads to a contradiction.

Note that
V
is a compact set in
\mathbb{C}^{m}
, and for
0\leq i\leq m-1
the coefficient
c_{i}(\mathbf{v})
is continuous as a function of
\mathbf{v}
.
It follows from the claim that

c_{0}:=\inf\{|c_{0}(\mathbf{v})|:\mathbf{v}\in V\}>0\quad\text{and}\quad{\widetilde{c}_{0}:=\sup\{|c_{0}(\mathbf{v})|:\mathbf{v}\in V\}<\infty.}

Note that
\sup\{|c_{i}(\mathbf{v})|:\mathbf{v}\in V,\penalty\ 1\leq i\leq m-1\}<\infty
.
For
\mathbf{v}\in V
, we have
\mathbf{v}B^{n}=c_{0}(\mathbf{v})\eta^{n}\mathbf{v}^{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}c_{i}(\mathbf{v})\eta_{i}^{n}\mathbf{v}^{i}
. Therefore, there exists
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
n\geq n_{0}
and for all
\mathbf{v}\in V
,

\frac{1}{2}c_{0}\eta^{n}\leq\|\mathbf{v}B^{n}\|\leq\frac{3}{2}\widetilde{c}_{0}\eta^{n}.

Furthermore, for
1\leq n\leq n_{0}
and
\mathbf{v}\in V
, we have

\frac{1}{\eta^{n_{0}}}\eta^{n}\leq 1\leq\|\mathbf{v}B^{n}\|\leq\|\mathbf{v}B^{n_{0}}\|\leq\frac{3}{2}\widetilde{c}_{0}\eta^{n_{0}}\eta^{n}.

Hence, by taking
C_{1}=\max\{\eta^{n_{0}},3\widetilde{c}_{0}\eta^{n_{0}}/2,2/c_{0},3\widetilde{c}_{0}/2,c/(m^{2}\delta_{0}),cm^{2}\}
, we complete the proof.
∎

Proposition 3.5
.

There exists a constant
C_{2}>1
such that for any progressively overlapping sequence
\zeta\in(D_{b}^{m})^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
and for any totally distinct sequence

where
\lambda
and
\eta
are defined as in Theorem

.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.2
we have

|\Sigma_{k+m}^{\zeta}|=(b-1)\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}|\Sigma_{k+j}^{\zeta}|
for

The recurrence relation can be rewritten as

(|\Sigma_{k+m}^{\zeta}|,|\Sigma_{k+m-1}^{\zeta}|,\ldots,|\Sigma_{k+1}^{\zeta}|)=(|\Sigma_{k+m-1}^{\zeta}|,|\Sigma_{k+m-2}^{\zeta}|,\ldots,|\Sigma_{k}^{\zeta}|)A,

where

is defined in (

).
It follows that

(3.11)

(|\Sigma_{k+m}^{\zeta}|,|\Sigma_{k+m-1}^{\zeta}|,\ldots,|\Sigma_{k+1}^{\zeta}|)=(|\Sigma_{m}^{\zeta}|,|\Sigma_{m-1}^{\zeta}|,\ldots,|\Sigma_{1}^{\zeta}|)A^{k}\quad\forall k\in\mathbb{N}.

Write
\mathbf{v}_{k}=(|\Sigma_{k+m}^{\zeta}|,|\Sigma_{k+m-1}^{\zeta}|,\ldots,|\Sigma_{k+1}^{\zeta}|)
for
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.
By Lemma
2.1
, we have
\mathbf{v}_{0}/\|\mathbf{v}_{0}\|\in V
.
By Lemma
3.4
and (

), there exists a constant

such that

C_{1}^{-1}\|\mathbf{v}_{0}\|\lambda^{k}\leq\|\mathbf{v}_{k}\|\leq C_{1}\|\mathbf{v}_{0}\|\lambda^{k}\quad\forall k\in\mathbb{N}.

By Lemma

Therefore, we conclude that

\frac{\|\mathbf{v}_{0}\|}{C_{1}b^{m+1}}\lambda^{k}\leq|\Sigma_{k}^{\zeta}|\leq C_{1}\|\mathbf{v}_{0}\|\lambda^{k}\quad\forall k\in\mathbb{N}.

Using the same argument, by Lemmas

and

, we can also show that

\frac{\|\widetilde{\mathbf{v}}_{0}\|}{C_{1}b^{m+1}}\eta^{k}\leq|\Sigma_{k}^{\xi}|\leq C_{1}\|\widetilde{\mathbf{v}}_{0}\|\eta^{k}\quad\forall k\in\mathbb{N},

where

.
∎

4.

In this section we will show the intermediate value property for Hausdorff and packing dimensions of

In the following we always let

satisfy the equations

Lemma 4.1
.

There exists a constant
C_{2}>1
such that for any
\omega\in(D_{b}^{m})^{\mathbb{N}}
we have

C_{2}^{-1}\eta^{k}\leq|\Sigma_{k}^{\omega}|\leq C_{2}\lambda^{k}\quad\forall k\in\mathbb{N}.

Proof.

Let
\xi
and
\zeta
be totally distinct and progressively overlapping sequences in
(D_{b}^{m})^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
, respectively.
By Proposition
3.5
, it suffices to show that

|\Sigma^{\xi}_{k}|\leq|\Sigma_{k}^{\omega}|\leq|\Sigma_{k}^{\zeta}|\quad\forall k\in\mathbb{N}.

Clearly, for all
1\leq k\leq m
we have
|\Sigma^{\xi}_{k}|=|\Sigma_{k}^{\omega}|=|\Sigma_{k}^{\zeta}|
.

Assume that for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
we have
|\Sigma_{j}^{\omega}|\leq|\Sigma_{j}^{\zeta}|
for all
1\leq j<k+m
. Then
by Lemma
3.2
we obtain

|\Sigma_{k+m}^{\omega}|\leq(b-1)\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}|\Sigma_{k+j}^{\omega}|\leq(b-1)\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}|\Sigma_{k+j}^{\zeta}|=|\Sigma_{k+m}^{\zeta}|.

By induction, we conclude that
|\Sigma_{k}^{\omega}|\leq|\Sigma_{k}^{\zeta}|
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

Note that
\eta
satisfies the equation
x^{m}-bx^{m-1}+1=0
, i.e.,
b-\eta^{-(m-1)}=\eta
.
Assume that for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
we have
|\Sigma_{j+1}^{\omega}|-|\Sigma_{j+1}^{\xi}|\geq\eta(|\Sigma_{j}^{\omega}|-|\Sigma_{j}^{\xi}|)
for all
1\leq j<k+m-1

Then by Lemma
3.2
we have

\displaystyle|\Sigma_{k+m}^{\omega}|-|\Sigma_{k+m}^{\xi}|

\displaystyle\geq b(|\Sigma_{k+m-1}^{\omega}|-|\Sigma_{k+m-1}^{\xi}|)-(|\Sigma_{k}^{\omega}|-|\Sigma_{k}^{\xi}|)

\displaystyle\geq b(|\Sigma_{k+m-1}^{\omega}|-|\Sigma_{k+m-1}^{\xi}|)-\eta^{-(m-1)}(|\Sigma_{k+m-1}^{\omega}|-|\Sigma_{k+m-1}^{\xi}|)

\displaystyle=\eta(|\Sigma_{k+m-1}^{\omega}|-|\Sigma_{k+m-1}^{\xi}|),

By induction, we conclude that
|\Sigma_{k+1}^{\omega}|-|\Sigma_{k+1}^{\xi}|\geq\eta(|\Sigma_{k}^{\omega}|-|\Sigma_{k}^{\xi}|)
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Note that
\eta>0
and
|\Sigma_{1}^{\omega}|=|\Sigma_{1}^{\xi}|
. Thus we obtain that
|\Sigma_{k}^{\omega}|\geq|\Sigma_{k}^{\xi}|
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
∎

Take
\omega\in(D_{b}^{m})^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
. Recall from (
2.2
) that for
\mathbf{c}=c_{1}c_{2}\ldots c_{k}\in\Sigma_{k}^{\omega}
and

we define

\Sigma_{k+n}^{\omega}(\mathbf{c})=\left\{d_{1}d_{2}\ldots d_{k+n}\in\Sigma_{k+n}^{\omega}:d_{1}d_{2}\ldots d_{k}=\mathbf{c}\right\}.

For
k\geq m
and
n\geq m
, by (
2.6
) and (
2.7
) in the proof of Lemma
2.2
we have showed that
|\Sigma^{\tau}_{n-m+1}|\leq|\Sigma_{k+n}^{\omega}(\mathbf{c})|\leq b^{m-1}|\Sigma^{\tau}_{n-m+1}|
for some

.
By Lemma
4.1
, for
k\geq m
and
n\geq m
we have

(4.1)

C_{2}^{-1}\eta^{n-m+1}\leq|\Sigma_{k+n}^{\omega}(\mathbf{c})|\leq C_{2}b^{m-1}\lambda^{n-m+1}.

For a bounded set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the Assouad and lower dimensions of
E
are defined by (cf.
[
25
]
)

\displaystyle\dim_{A}E

\displaystyle:=\inf\left\{s\geq 0:\exists C>0,\rho>0,\forall 0<r<R<\rho,\sup_{x\in E}N_{r}(B(x,R)\cap E)\leq C\left(\frac{R}{r}\right)^{s}\right\},

\displaystyle\dim_{L}E

\displaystyle:=\sup\left\{s\geq 0:\exists C>0,\rho>0,\forall 0<r<R<\rho,\inf_{x\in E}N_{r}(B(x,R)\cap E)\geq C\left(\frac{R}{r}\right)^{s}\right\},

where
N_{r}(F)
is the smallest number of balls of diameter
r
required to cover a bounded set
F
.

Proposition 4.2
.

For any
\omega\in(D_{b}^{m})^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
we have

\frac{\log\eta}{\log b}\leq\dim_{L}K^{\omega}\leq\dim_{H}K^{\omega}\leq\dim_{P}K^{\omega}\leq\dim_{A}K^{\omega}\leq\frac{\log\lambda}{\log b}.

Proof.

Write
s_{1}=\log\eta/\log b
and
s_{2}=\log\lambda/\log b
. By (
1.4
) we only need to prove
\dim_{L}K^{\omega}\geq s_{1}
and
\dim_{A}K^{\omega}\leq s_{2}
. By the definitions of
\dim_{L}
and
\dim_{A}

it suffices to prove that there exists a constant
c>1
such that

c^{-1}\left(\frac{R}{r}\right)^{s_{1}}\leq N_{r}(B(x,R)\cap K^{\omega})\leq c\left(\frac{R}{r}\right)^{s_{2}}\quad\forall 0<r<R<b^{-m},\ \forall x\in K^{\omega}.

Take
0<r<R<b^{-m}
and
x\in K^{\omega}
.
Without loss of generality, we can assume
R/r\geq b^{m+1}
.
Then there exist
k_{1},k_{2}\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq m}
with
k_{2}\geq k_{1}+m+1
such that

(4.2)

\frac{1}{{b}^{k_{1}+1}}\leq R<\frac{1}{{b}^{k_{1}}}\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{1}{{b}^{k_{2}+1}}\leq r<\frac{1}{{b}^{k_{2}}}.

Since
R\geq b^{-(k_{1}+1)}
, we can find
\mathbf{c}\in\Sigma_{k_{1}+1}^{\omega}
such that
I_{\mathbf{c}}\cap K^{\omega}\subset B(x,R)\cap K^{\omega}
.
Note that

\Sigma_{k_{2}}^{\omega}(\mathbf{c})\subset\big\{\mathbf{d}\in D_{b}^{k_{2}}:I_{\mathbf{d}}\cap(I_{\mathbf{c}}\cap K^{\omega})\neq\emptyset\big\}.

By (
4.1
), we obtain

N_{r}(B(x,R)\cap K^{\omega})\geq N_{b^{-k_{2}}}(I_{\mathbf{c}}\cap K^{\omega})\geq\frac{1}{2}|\Sigma_{k_{2}}^{\omega}(\mathbf{c})|\geq\frac{1}{2C_{2}}\eta^{k_{2}-k_{1}-m}.

Note by (
4.2
) that
(R/r)^{s_{1}}\leq(b^{k_{2}-k_{1}+1})^{s_{1}}=\eta^{k_{2}-k_{1}+1}
.
Therefore, we conclude that

N_{r}(B(x,R)\cap K^{\omega})\geq\frac{1}{2C_{2}\eta^{m+1}}\left(\frac{R}{r}\right)^{s_{1}}.

Since
R<b^{-k_{1}}
, the ball
B(x,R)
intersects at most three intervals in
\{I_{\mathbf{d}}:\mathbf{d}\in\Sigma_{k_{1}}^{\omega}\}
, denoted by
I_{\mathbf{c}_{1}},I_{\mathbf{c}_{2}},I_{\mathbf{c}_{3}}
with
\mathbf{c}_{i}\in\Sigma_{k_{1}}^{\omega}
for
1\leq i\leq 3
.
That is,
B(x,R)\cap K^{\omega}\subset I_{\mathbf{c}_{1}}\cup I_{\mathbf{c}_{2}}\cup I_{\mathbf{c}_{3}}
.
Then the family of intervals
\{I_{\mathbf{d}}:\mathbf{d}\in\Sigma_{k_{2}+1}^{\omega}(\mathbf{c}_{1})\cup\Sigma_{k_{2}+1}^{\omega}(\mathbf{c}_{2})\cup\Sigma_{k_{2}+1}^{\omega}(\mathbf{c}_{3})\}
forms a

.
Thus, by (

) we obtain

\displaystyle N_{r}(B(x,R)\cap K^{\omega})

\displaystyle\leq N_{b^{-k_{2}-1}}(B(x,R)\cap K^{\omega})

\displaystyle\leq|\Sigma_{k_{2}+1}^{\omega}(\mathbf{c}_{1})|+|\Sigma_{k_{2}+1}^{\omega}(\mathbf{c}_{2})|+|\Sigma_{k_{2}+1}^{\omega}(\mathbf{c}_{3})|

\displaystyle\leq 3C_{2}b^{m-1}\lambda^{k_{2}-k_{1}-m+2}.

Note by (
4.2
) that
(R/r)^{s_{2}}\geq(b^{k_{2}-k_{1}-1})^{s_{2}}=\lambda^{k_{2}-k_{1}-1}
.
It follows that

N_{r}(B(x,R)\cap K^{\omega})\leq 3C_{2}b^{m-1}\lambda^{3-m}\left(\frac{R}{r}\right)^{s_{2}},

completing the proof.
∎

Proposition 4.4
.

Given

\left\{p_{n}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty},\left\{q_{n}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathbb{N}

\dim_{H}K^{\omega}=t\frac{\log\eta}{\log b}+(1-t)\frac{\log\lambda}{\log b}\quad\textrm{and}\quad\dim_{P}K^{\omega}=s\frac{\log\eta}{\log b}+(1-s)\frac{\log\lambda}{\log b}.

To prove Proposition

Lemma 4.5

\{v_{n}\}_{n=-m+1}^{\infty}
is nonnegative, and

v_{n}=bv_{n-1}-v_{n-m}\geq\max\{v_{n-1},v_{n-2},\ldots,v_{n-m+1}\}\quad\forall n\geq-m+2.

Write
B^{k}=(b_{i,j}^{(k)})_{1\leq i,j\leq m}
for
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
We claim that for
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

(5.2)

B^{k}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc}v_{k}&v_{k-1}&\cdots&v_{k-m+2}&v_{k-m+1}\\
-v_{k-m+1}&-v_{k-m}&\cdots&-v_{k-2m+3}&-v_{k-2m+2}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
-v_{k-2}&-v_{k-3}&\cdots&-v_{k-m}&-v_{k-m-1}\\
-v_{k-1}&-v_{k-2}&\cdots&-v_{k-m+1}&-v_{k-m}\end{array}\right)_{m\times m}.

That is,
b_{1,j}^{(k)}=v_{k-j+1}
for
1\leq j\leq m
, and
b_{i,j}^{(k)}=-v_{k+i-j-m}
for
1<i\leq m,1\leq j\leq m
.

We will prove the claim (
5.2
) by induction on
k
.
First, by (
3.10
) the claim (
5.2
) holds for
k=1
.
Then we assume that the claim (
5.2
) holds for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
Note that
B^{k+1}=B\cdot B^{k}
.
By (
3.10
) we have

•

for
1\leq j\leq m
,
b_{1,j}^{(k+1)}=bb_{1,j}^{(k)}+b_{2,j}^{(k)}=bv_{k+1-j}-v_{k+2-j-m}=v_{(k+1)-j+1}
, where the last equality follows from (ii);

•

for
1<i<m
and
1\leq j\leq m
,
b_{i,j}^{(k+1)}=b_{i+1,j}^{(k)}=-v_{k+1+i-j-m}
;

•

for
1\leq j\leq m
,
b_{m,j}^{(k+1)}=-b_{1,j}^{(k)}=-v_{k+1-j}
.

This means that the claim (
5.2
) also holds for
k+1
.
By induction on
k
, we prove the claim (
5.2
).

For
k\in\mathbb{N}
, write
C_{k}=AB^{k}=(c_{i,j}^{(k)})_{1\leq i,j\leq m}
.
Then by (
3.10
) we have

(5.3)

\begin{cases}c_{i,j}^{(k)}=(b-1)v_{k-j+1}-v_{k-j+1-(m-i)}\;\text{for}\;1\leq i<m,1\leq j\leq m,\\
c_{m,j}^{(k)}=(b-1)v_{k-j+1}\;\text{for}\;1\leq j\leq m.\end{cases}

By (ii) and (
5.3
), we first have that the matrix
AB^{k}
is nonnegative for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
Thus, the matrix
A^{p}B^{q}
is nonnegative for all
p\in\mathbb{N},q\in\mathbb{N}
.
For
k\geq m-1
, by (i) and (
5.3
) we obtain that all entries of the matrix
AB^{k}
are positive.
Thus, the matrix
A^{p}B^{q}
is positive for
p\in\mathbb{N},q\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq m-1}
.

Next, fix
1\leq k<m-1
. Note that
v_{-m}=-1
.
By (i) and (
5.3
) we have

c_{i,j}^{(k)}>0\quad\text{for}\;j\leq k+1\;\text{or}\;j=k+1+i.

Using the fact that
c_{i,1}^{(k)}>0
for
1\leq i\leq m
,
c_{1,j}^{(k)}>0
for
1\leq j\leq k+1
, and
c_{i,k+1+i}^{(k)}>0
for
1\leq i\leq m-1-k
, one can check that the matrix
AB^{k}
is primitive.
For
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, write
A^{\ell}B^{k}=(u_{i,j}^{(\ell)})_{1\leq i,j\leq m}
.
By induction on
\ell
, we can show that
u_{i,1}^{(\ell)}>0
for
1\leq i\leq m
,
u_{1,j}^{(\ell)}>0
for
1\leq j\leq\min\{k+\ell,m\}

u_{i,k+\ell+i}^{(\ell)}>0
for

.
This implies that the matrix
A^{\ell}B^{k}
is primitive for all
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
.

Therefore, for any
p,q\in\mathbb{N}
the matrix
A^{p}B^{q}
is a primitive nonnegative matrix.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.8
.

Fix
p,q\in\mathbb{N}
.
By Lemma
5.2
, the matrix
A^{p}B^{q}
is a primitive nonnegative matrix.
Let
\lambda_{p,q}
be the largest eigenvalue of the matrix
A^{p}B^{q}
.
For
\omega\in L_{p,q}
, by Lemma
3.2
we have

\mathbf{N}^{\omega}_{k(p+q)}=\mathbf{N}^{\omega}_{0}(A^{p}B^{q})^{k}\quad\forall k\in\mathbb{N}.

As we have done in Lemma
3.4
, there exists a constant
C>1
such that

C^{-1}\lambda_{p,q}^{k}\leq\|\mathbf{N}^{\omega}_{k(p+q)}\|\leq C\lambda_{p,q}^{k}\quad\forall k\in\mathbb{N}.

By Proposition
3.1
, the set
K^{\omega}
is
\frac{\log\lambda_{p,q}}{(p+q)\log b}
-Ahlfors regular.

Next, we consider
\lambda_{np,nq}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Note that for
\omega\in L_{np,nq}
, we have
\mathbf{N}^{\omega}_{kn(p+q)}=\mathbf{N}^{\omega}_{0}(A^{np}B^{nq})^{k}
for
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
By Lemma
3.4
, we have

C_{1}^{-2k}\lambda^{knp}\eta^{knq}\leq\frac{\|\mathbf{N}^{\omega}_{kn(p+q)}\|}{\|\mathbf{N}^{\omega}_{0}\|}\leq C_{1}^{2k}\lambda^{knp}\eta^{knq}.

On the other hand,

\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{\log\|\mathbf{N}^{\omega}_{kn(p+q)}\|}{kn(p+q)}=\frac{\log\lambda_{np,nq}}{n(p+q)}.

Thus, we obtain

\frac{p\log\lambda}{p+q}+\frac{q\log\eta}{p+q}-\frac{2\log C_{1}}{n(p+q)}\leq\frac{\log\lambda_{np,nq}}{n(p+q)}\leq\frac{p\log\lambda}{p+q}+\frac{q\log\eta}{p+q}+\frac{2\log C_{1}}{n(p+q)}.

It follows that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log\lambda_{np,nq}}{n(p+q)}=\frac{p\log\lambda}{p+q}+\frac{q\log\eta}{p+q}.

This means that the set

\bigg\{\frac{\log\lambda_{p,q}}{(p+q)\log b}:p,q\in\mathbb{N}\bigg\}

is dense in
[\log_{b}\eta,\log_{b}\lambda]
.
∎

At the end of this section, we give a new pattern which also results in Ahlfors regularity.

Example 5.3
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 3}
and
\omega=\omega^{0}\omega^{1}\omega^{2}\ldots\in(D_{b}^{m})^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
, where
\omega^{k}=\omega^{k}_{1}\omega^{k}_{2}\ldots\omega^{k}_{m}\in D_{b}^{m}
.
Suppose that for
k\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq m-1}
, we have

(5.4)

\begin{cases}\omega^{k}_{1}\ldots\omega^{k}_{m-j}\neq\omega^{k-j}_{j+1}\ldots\omega^{k-j}_{m}\quad\forall 1\leq j\leq m-2,\\
\omega^{k}_{1}=\omega^{k-m+1}_{m}.\end{cases}

Note first that

\Sigma_{k+1}^{\omega}=\Big(\Sigma_{k}^{\omega}\times\big(D_{b}\backslash\big\{\omega_{m}^{k-m+1}\big\}\big)\Big)\cup\big\{d_{1}\ldots d_{k}\omega_{m}^{k-m+1}\in\Sigma_{k+1}^{\omega}:d_{1}\ldots d_{k}\in\Sigma_{k}^{\omega}\big\},

where the union is disjoint.
So, we obtain

(5.5)

\begin{split}|\Sigma_{k+1}^{\omega}|-(b-1)|\Sigma_{k}^{\omega}|&=\#\left\{d_{1}\ldots d_{k}\in\Sigma_{k}^{\omega}:d_{1}\ldots d_{k}\omega_{m}^{k-m+1}\in\Sigma_{k+1}^{\omega}\right\}\\
&=\#\left\{d_{1}\ldots d_{k}\in\Sigma_{k}^{\omega}:d_{1}\ldots d_{k}\omega_{1}^{k}\in\Sigma_{k+1}^{\omega}\right\},\end{split}

where the last equality follows from
\omega^{k}_{1}=\omega^{k-m+1}_{m}
.
On the other hand,

\Sigma_{k+m-1}^{\omega}\times D_{b}=\Sigma_{k+m}^{\omega}\cup\big\{d_{1}\ldots d_{k}\omega_{1}^{k}\ldots\omega_{m}^{k}:d_{1}\ldots d_{k}\omega_{1}^{k}\ldots\omega_{m-1}^{k}\in\Sigma_{k+m-1}^{\omega}\big\},

where the union is disjoint. Thus, we have

(5.6)

b|\Sigma_{k+m-1}^{\omega}|-|\Sigma_{k+m}^{\omega}|=\#\big\{d_{1}\ldots d_{k}\omega_{1}^{k}\ldots\omega_{m}^{k}:d_{1}\ldots d_{k}\omega_{1}^{k}\ldots\omega_{m-1}^{k}\in\Sigma_{k+m-1}^{\omega}\big\}.

For any
d_{1}\ldots d_{k}\in\Sigma_{k}^{\omega}
, by (
5.4
) we have

d_{1}\ldots d_{k}\omega_{1}^{k}\in\Sigma_{k+1}^{\omega}\;\;\text{if and only if}\;\;d_{1}\ldots d_{k}\omega_{1}^{k}\ldots\omega_{m-1}^{k}\in\Sigma_{k+m-1}^{\omega}.

Therefore, by (
5.5
) and (
5.6
) we obtain
|\Sigma_{k+1}^{\omega}|-(b-1)|\Sigma_{k}^{\omega}|=b|\Sigma_{k+m-1}^{\omega}|-|\Sigma_{k+m}^{\omega}|
, i.e.,

|\Sigma_{k+m}^{\omega}|=b|\Sigma_{k+m-1}^{\omega}|-|\Sigma_{k+1}^{\omega}|+(b-1)|\Sigma_{k}^{\omega}|\quad\forall k\geq m-1.

Let
\gamma\in(b-1,b)
be the root of equation
x^{m}-bx^{m-1}+x-(b-1)=0
.
As we have done in Lemmas
3.3
,
3.4
and Proposition
3.5
, we can show that
\gamma
is a Pisot number, and there exists a constant
C>1
such that

C^{-1}\gamma^{k}\leq|\Sigma_{k}^{\omega}|\leq C\gamma^{k}\quad\forall k\in\mathbb{N}.

By Proposition
3.1
, the set
K^{\omega}
is
\frac{\log\gamma}{\log b}
-Ahlfors regular.
Moreover, we have
\eta<\gamma<\lambda
.

Theorem
1.8
shows that for a countable and dense set of
s\in[\frac{\log\eta}{\log b},\frac{\log\lambda}{\log b}]
there are infinitely many
\omega\in(D_{b}^{m})^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
such that

is
s
-Ahlfors regular. We conjecture that this Ahlfors regular property should hold for all

.

Proof.

We will construct a Moran subset of

Choose

For

(ii) The survivor set
K^{\omega}
studied in this paper involves one moving hole. Our results can be extended to study survivor sets with multiple moving holes for sufficiently large
b
and
m
. More precisely, for
m,M\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\vec{\omega}=(\omega(1),\omega(2),\ldots,\omega(M))\in\prod_{\ell=1}^{M}(D_{b}^{m})^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
, where each
\omega(\ell)=\omega^{0}(\ell)\omega^{1}(\ell)\ldots\in\left(D_{b}^{m}\right)^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
. Then the survivor set with multiple moving holes
\vec{\omega}
is defined by

K^{\vec{\omega}}:=\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{d_{i}}{b^{i}}:d_{k+1}d_{k+2}\ldots d_{k+m}\in D^{m}_{b}\backslash\bigcup_{\ell=1}^{M}\{\omega^{k}(\ell)\}\quad\forall k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\right\}.

By the same argument as in the proof of Theorem
1.1
we can show that

\dim_{H}K^{\vec{\omega}}=\underline{\dim}_{B}K^{\vec{\omega}}=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log\|A_{\vec{\omega}^{0}}A_{\vec{\omega}^{1}}\cdots A_{\vec{\omega}^{n}}\|}{n\log b},

and

\dim_{P}K^{\vec{\omega}}=\overline{\dim}_{B}K^{\vec{\omega}}=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log\|A_{\vec{\omega}^{0}}A_{\vec{\omega}^{1}}\cdots A_{\vec{\omega}^{n}}\|}{n\log b},

where each
A_{\vec{\omega}^{j}}
is the adjacency matrix of the subshift of finite type in
(D_{b}^{\mathbb{N}},\sigma)
with the set of forbidden words
\left\{\omega^{j}(1),\omega^{j}(2),\ldots,\omega^{j}(M)\right\}\subset D_{b}^{m}
. In this case, it is interesting to study the dimension equality results as in Theorem
1.4
and the intermediate value property results as in Theorem
1.6
.

Note that if the blocks
\omega^{j}(\ell),j\in\mathbb{N}_{0},1\leq\ell\leq M
have different length, but these blocks have finitely many patterns, i.e.,
W:=\left\{\omega^{j}(\ell):j\in\mathbb{N}_{0},1\leq\ell\leq M\right\}
is a finite set, then we can still get analogous results as in Theorem
1.1
by possibly extending the length of short blocks to achieve the maximum block length in
W
.

(iii) Since we are working more on the symbolic space, our main results can be extended to homogeneous self-similar sets in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfying the strong separation condition. More precisely, for
b\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 2}
,
r\in(0,1/b)
and
\mathcal{D}\subset D_{b}^{d}=\left\{0,1,\ldots,b-1\right\}^{d}
with
|\mathcal{D}|\geq 3
, we define the iterated function system
\mathcal{F}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
by

\mathcal{F}:=\left\{f_{a}(x)=rO(x+a):a\in\mathcal{D}\right\},

where
O
is an orthogonal matrix in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
so that
\mathcal{F}
satisfies the strong separation condition.
For
\omega=\omega^{0}\omega^{1}\ldots\in(\mathcal{D}^{m})^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
, let

K_{\mathcal{F}}^{\omega}:=\left\{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}r^{n}O^{n}d_{n}:d_{k+1}d_{k+2}\ldots d_{k+m}\in\mathcal{D}^{m}\setminus\{\omega^{k}\}\quad\forall k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\right\}.

Then by the same argument as in the proof of Theorem
1.1
we obtain

\dim_{H}K_{\mathcal{F}}^{\omega}=\underline{\dim}_{B}K_{\mathcal{F}}^{\omega}=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{-n\log r}\log\|\tilde{A}_{\omega^{0}}\tilde{A}_{\omega^{1}}\cdots\tilde{A}_{\omega^{n-1}}\|,

and

\dim_{P}K_{\mathcal{F}}^{\omega}=\overline{\dim}_{B}K_{\mathcal{F}}^{\omega}=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{-n\log r}\log\|\tilde{A}_{\omega^{0}}\tilde{A}_{\omega^{1}}\cdots\tilde{A}_{\omega^{n-1}}\|,

where
\tilde{A}_{\omega^{j}}
denotes the adjacency matrix of the shift of finite type in
(\mathcal{D}^{\mathbb{N}},\sigma)
with the forbidden block
\omega^{j}\in\mathcal{D}^{m}
.
Similarly, the results in Theorems
1.4
and
1.6
can also be extended for
K^{\omega}_{\mathcal{F}}
with
b
replaced by
\frac{1}{r}
.

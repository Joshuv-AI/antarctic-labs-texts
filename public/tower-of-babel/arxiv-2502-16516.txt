Abstract




Abstract
Let
K
be a locally compact hypergroup with a left Haar measure
\mu
and
\Omega
be a Banach ideal
of
\mu
-measurable complex-valued functions on
K
. For Young functions
\{\varphi_{i}\}_{i=1,2,3}
,
let
\Omega_{\varphi_{i}}(K)
be the corresponding Calderón–Lozanovskiĭ space associated with

\varphi_{i}
on
K
. Motivated by the remarkable work of Akbarbaglu et al. in [Adv. Math. 312 (2017), 737-763],
in this article, the authors give several sufficient conditions for the sets

\left\{(f,g)\in\Omega_{\varphi_{1}}(K)\times\Omega_{\varphi_{2}}(K):\ |f|\ast|g|\in\Omega_{\varphi_{3}}(K)\right\}

and

\left\{(f,g)\in\Omega_{\varphi_{1}}(K)\times\Omega_{\varphi_{2}}(K):\ \exists\,x\in U,\ (|f|\ast|g|)(x)\ \text{is\ well\ defined}\right\}

to be of first category in the sense of Baire, where
U\subset K
denotes a compact set.
All these results are new even for Orlicz(–Lorentz) spaces on hypergroups.

1
Introduction

Let
G
be a locally compact group with a fixed left Haar measure
\mu

and
L^{p}(G)
be the Lebesgue space on
G
with
p\in[1,\infty]
.
For two
\mu
-measurable functions
f
and
g
on
G
, their convolution is defined as

(f\ast g)(x):=\int_{G}f(y)g(y^{-1}x)\,d\mu(y)

for every
x\in G
such that the function
y\mapsto f(y)g(y^{-1}x)
is
\mu
-integrable.
Moreover,
f\ast g
is said to exist if
(f\ast g)(x)
exists for almost every
x\in G
.

Let us give a brief review about the research on
L^{p}
-conjecture. This famous conjecture was
first formulated by Rajagopalan in his Ph.D. thesis in 1963 as follows.

Theorem A
.

Let
G
be a locally compact group and
p\in(1,\infty)
. Then
f\ast g
exists and belongs to

L^{p}(G)
for any
f,\,g\in L^{p}(G)
if and only if
G
is compact.

To be precise, the first well-known result related to this conjecture was due to Żelazko
[
42
]

and Urbanik
[
41
]
in 1961, in which they proved that Theorem
A
is true for any locally
compact abelian group
G
. Later, Żelazko
[
43
]
and Rajagopalan
[
32
]
independently
gave the proof of Theorem
A
for
p\in(2,\infty)
; see also Rajagopalan’s works
[
31
]
for the case
when
p\in[2,\infty)
and
G
is discrete,
[
32
]
for the case when
p=2
and
G

is totally disconnected, and
[
33
]
for the case when
p\in(1,\infty)
and
G
is either nilpotent
or a semi-direct product of two locally compact abelian groups as well as Rickert’s work
[
37
]

for the case when
p=2
and
G
is as in Theorem
A
. In the joint work
[
35
]
,
the aforementioned two authors established the truth of Theorem
A
for any locally compact solvable
group
G
; see also Greenleaf’s monograph
[
14
]
. After thirty years of struggling, until 1990, Saeki

[
38
]
gave an affirmative answer to this conjecture by quite elementary means.

One significant topic on
L^{p}
-conjecture is the study of its quantitative version, which originated from Gła̧b and Strobin
[
12
]
. Concretely, they obtained the following conclusion and also raised
several interesting questions; see
[
12
, Theorem 1 and Section 4]
.

Theorem B
.

Assume that
G
is a non-compact but locally compact group. Let
p,\,q\in(1,\infty)
satisfy

1/p+1/q<1
. Then, for each compact set
U\subset G
, the set

\left\{(f,g)\in L^{p}(G)\times L^{q}(G):\ \exists\,x\in U,\ (f\ast g)(x)\ is\ well\ defined\right\}

is
\sigma
-lower porous (see
[
12
, p. 584]
for its precise definition).

To answer Gła̧b and Strobin’s questions, Akbarbaglu and Maghsoudi
[
1
]
confirmed that, if
G

is a non-unimodular locally compact group, then, for each compact set
U\subset G
, the set

\{(f,g)\in L^{p}(G)\times L^{q}(G):\ \exists\,x\in U,\ (f\ast g)(x)\ \text{is\ well\ defined}\}

is
\sigma
-lower porous, where
p\in(1,\infty)
and
q\in[1,\infty)
. The same assertion for the case when
G

is non-discrete with
p\in(0,1)
and
q\in(0,\infty]
was explored by Akbarbaglu and Maghsoudi
[
4
]
.
Moreover, in
[
4
]
, they also showed that, if
G

is a non-discrete locally compact group, then, for each compact set
U\subset G
, the set

\left\{(f,g)\in L^{p}(G)\times L^{q}(G):\ f\ast g\in L^{r}(U,\mu|_{U})\right\}

is
\sigma
-lower porous, where
p,\,q\in[1,\infty)
and
r\in[1,\infty]
such that
1/p+1/q>1+1/r
. This
meanwhile solved an open problem proposed by Saeki
[
38
]
.

Another meaningful line of inquiry into
L^{p}
-conjecture is extending the space
L^{p}
to some more general
function spaces on a locally compact group
G
such as the Orlicz space
L^{\varphi}(G)
etc.
Precisely, the aim of this
research direction is to find out what needs to assumed on Young functions
\{\varphi_{i}\}_{i=1,2,3}

such that
f\ast g\in L^{\varphi_{3}}(G)
provided
f\in L^{\varphi_{1}}(G)
and
g\in L^{\varphi_{2}}(G)
, where, for each

i\in\{1,2,3\}
,
L^{\varphi_{i}}(G)
denotes the corresponding Orlicz space associated with
\varphi_{i}
on
G
.
Recall that O’Neil
[
29
]
proved that, if
G
is a unimodular locally compact group and the Young
functions
\{\varphi_{i}\}_{i=1,2,3}
satisfy
\varphi_{1}^{-1}(x)\varphi_{2}^{-1}(x)\leq x\varphi_{3}^{-1}(x)
for any

x\in[0,\infty)
, then
f\ast g\in L^{\varphi_{3}}(G)
for any
f\in L^{\varphi_{1}}(G)
and
g\in L^{\varphi_{2}}(G)
,
that is, the convolution operator is bounded from
L^{\varphi_{1}}(G)\times L^{\varphi_{2}}(G)
into
L^{\varphi_{3}}(G)
.
In addition, Hudzik, Kamińska and Musielak in
[
16
, Theorem 2]
presented some equivalent
conditions for an Orlicz space to be a convolution Banach algebra:

Theorem C
.

Let
G
be a locally compact abelian group and
\varphi
a Young function satisfying the so-called

\Delta_{2}
-condition. Then
f\ast g
exists and belongs to
L^{\varphi}(G)
for any
f,\,g\in L^{\varphi}(G)

if and only if
G
is compact or
\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 0}\frac{\varphi(x)}{x}>0.

This result was later extended, respectively, by Kamińska and Musielak
[
18
]
to the form of

L^{\varphi_{1}}(G)\ast L^{\varphi_{2}}(G)\subset L^{\varphi_{3}}(G)
and by Akbarbaglu and Maghsoudi
[
3
]

to the amenable group. In particular, in
[
3
]
, they obtained that, if
G
is amenable,

\varphi
is a
\Delta_{2}
-regular
N
-function and
f\ast g\in L^{\varphi}(G)
for any
f,\,g\in L^{\varphi}(G)
,
then
G
is compact. It now leads us naturally to the question: Under the same assumptions, does
the compactness of
G
imply that
f\ast g\in L^{\varphi}(G)
for any
f,\,g\in L^{\varphi}(G)
?
And more generally, when
f\ast g
exists as a well-defined function on
G
for any
f,\,g\in L^{\varphi}(G)
?
A discussion on this question can be found in
[
2
]
of Akbarbaglu and Maghsoudi, which is considered
as the initiation on the research of the quantitative
L^{p}
-conjecture for Orlicz spaces. The main result
of
[
2
]
is as follows; see
[
2
, Theorems 2.1 and 2.4]
.

Theorem D
.

Assume that
G
is a non-unimodular locally compact group,
\varphi_{1}
is an
N
-function, and
\varphi_{2}
is a
finite Young function. Then, for each compact set
U\subset G
, the set

\left\{(f,g)\in L^{\varphi_{1}}(G)\times L^{\varphi_{2}}(G):\ \exists\,x\in U,\ (f\ast g)(x)\ is\ well\ defined\right\}

is
\sigma
-lower porous. The conclusion is also true for the non-compact locally compact group with
an additional condition on
\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}
:

\displaystyle\liminf_{x\to 0}\frac{\varphi_{1}^{-1}(x)\varphi_{2}^{-1}(x)}{x}=\infty.

An improvement and unification of these above results was completed by
Akbarbaglu et al. in
[
5
]
under a more general setting of Calderón–Lozanovskiĭ spaces,
which include many familiar function spaces as special cases, such as Orlicz spaces, Orlicz–Lorentz spaces
and
p
-convexification spaces etc. Recall that the Calderón–Lozanovskiĭ space was first
introduced by Calderón in
[
9
]
and well developed by Lozanovskiĭ in
[
26
,
27
]
.
They play a key role in the theory of interpolation since their fine structure is the interpolation
functor for positive operators and, also for all linear operators with some additional assumptions,
like the Fatou property or separability. For more development on this space, we refer the
reader to, for instance,
[
11
,
13
,
15
,
20
,
21
,
19
,
25
,
28
,
34
]
.
Let
G
be a locally compact group and, for each
i\in\{1,2,3\}
, let

\varphi_{i}
be a Young function,
E
an ideal of measurable complex-valued functions defined on
G

and
E_{\varphi_{i}}(G)
be the corresponding Calderón–Lozanovskiĭ space. To be precise,
the authors of
[
5
]
investigated when the sets

\left\{(f,g)\in E_{\varphi_{1}}(G)\times E_{\varphi_{2}}(G):\ |f|\ast|g|\in E_{\varphi_{3}}(G)\right\}

and

\left\{(f,g)\in E_{\varphi_{1}}(G)\times E_{\varphi_{2}}(G):\ \exists\,x\in V,\ (f\ast g)(x)\ \text{is\ well\ defined}\right\}

are of first category in the sense of Baire, where
V\subset G
denotes a compact set.
The analogue quantitative problems for pointwise products in the context of complete
\sigma
-finite
measure spaces have also been considered in
[
5
]
.

On another hand, the discovery of the notion of hypergroups, which has a group-like structure that can
be expressed in terms of an abstract convolution of measures, is one of the decisive steps in studying
stochastic dynamical systems within the framework of algebraic-topological structures specified by
invariance conditions. Moreover, due to the celebrated work of Dunkl
[
10
]
and Jewett
[
17
]

on the fundamental theory of both generalized translation spaces and hypergroups, there has been an
increasing interest in extending harmonic analysis and representation theory from locally compact
semigroups to hypergroups; see, for instance,
[
8
]
and the recent monograph
[
24
]
.
Particularly, we refer the reader to
[
40
,
6
]
for the study on
L^{p}
-conjecture in the setting
of hypergroups and to
[
39
]
for its quantitative version. Then a natural question is to explore
the results related to the quantitative
L^{p}
-conjecture for Orlicz spaces on hypergroups,
and more generally, for Calderón–Lozanovskiĭ spaces on hypergroups.

Motivated by the aforementioned work, in this article, we give several sufficient conditions for
some subsets of product Calderón–Lozanovskiĭ spaces on hypergroups to be of first category in
the sense of Baire. The main ideal we used to prove our results comes from
[
5
,
39
]
.
The difficulty for the present setting of hypergroups stems from the fact that, compared with locally
compact groups, the convolution of two Dirac measures of a hypergroup is not necessarily a Dirac measure.
To overcome this difficulty, we have to find some mild conditions on hypergroups which are automatically
true for locally compact groups. The remainder of this article is organized as follows.
In Section
2
, we recall the notions concerning hypergroups, Orlicz spaces,
Calderón–Lozanovskiĭ spaces etc., and then state the main results of this article.
Sections
3
and
4
are devoted to proving these main results.

Hereinafter, let
{\mathbb{N}}:=\{1,2,\ldots\}
and,
for any set
E
, denote by
\mathbf{1}_{E}
its
characteristic function
.
For any complex function
f
, denote by
{\rm Re}(f)
its real part.

2
Preliminaries and main results

In this section, we first recall some notions
which will be used throughout this article and then state the main results of this article.

2.1
Hypergroups

We commence with the definition of hypergroups from
[
6
,
40
]
, which was initially introduced in

[
10
,
17
]
. Let
K
be a locally compact Hausdorff space. Denote by
\mathcal{M}(K)
the space
of all (complex-valued) Radon measures on
K
and by
\mathcal{M}^{+}(K)
the set of all
non-negative measures in
\mathcal{M}(K)
. The support of each
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(K)
is
denoted by
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{supp}(\mu)
, and for any
x\in K
, the symbol
\delta_{x}
denotes the Dirac measure at
x
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
K
be a locally compact Hausdorff space. If there are a convolution
\ast:\ {\mathcal{M}(K)}\times{\mathcal{M}(K)}\to{\mathcal{M}(K)}
,
an involution
x\mapsto x^{-}
on
K
, and an element
e\in K
(called the identity element) satisfying
the following conditions:

(i)

({\mathcal{M}(K)},+,\ast)
is a complex Banach algebra;

(ii)

for any non-negative
\mu,\nu\in{\mathcal{M}(K)}
,
\mu\ast\nu
is also a non-negative measure in

{\mathcal{M}(K)}
and the mapping
(\mu,\nu)\mapsto\mu\ast\nu
from
{\mathcal{M}(K)}^{+}\times{\mathcal{M}(K)}^{+}
into
{\mathcal{M}(K)}^{+}
is continuous
with respect to the cone topology, that is, the weakest topology such that, for each

f\in C_{\rm c}^{+}(K)
, the mappings
\mu\mapsto\int_{K}f(x)\,d\mu(x)
and
\mu\mapsto\mu(K)

are continuous, where
C_{\rm c}^{+}(K)

denotes the set of all the non-negative continuous complex-valued functions on
K
with compact support;

(iii)

for any
x,y\in K
,
\delta_{x}\ast\delta_{y}
is a probability measure with compact support,
that is,
\delta_{x}\ast\delta_{y}\in{\mathcal{M}(K)}^{+}
,
(\delta_{x}\ast\delta_{y})(K)=1
and
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{supp}(\delta_{x}\ast\delta_{y})
is compact;

(iv)

for any
x\in K
,
\delta_{e}\ast\delta_{x}=\delta_{x}=\delta_{x}\ast\delta_{e}
;

(v)

the mapping
x\mapsto x^{-}
on
K
is a homeomorphism, and for any
x,y\in K
,

(x^{-})^{-}=x
and
(\delta_{x}\ast\delta_{y})^{-}=\delta_{y^{-}}\ast\delta_{x^{-}}
, where, for any
\mu\in{\mathcal{M}(K)}
and

\Omega\subset K
,
\mu^{-}(\Omega):=\mu(\{x^{-}:\ x\in\Omega\})
.
Also, for any
x,y\in K
,
e\in\mathop{\mathrm{}}{supp}(\delta_{x}\ast\delta_{y})
if and only if
x=y^{-}
;

(vi)

the mapping
(x,y)\mapsto\mathop{\mathrm{}}{supp}(\delta_{x}\ast\delta_{y})
from
K\times K
into
\mathfrak{C}(K)

is continuous, where
\mathfrak{C}(K)
denotes the space of all the non-empty compact subsets of
K

equipped with the Michael topology whose subbasis has the form

\{H\in\mathfrak{C}(K):\ H\cap U\neq\emptyset\ {\rm and}\ H\subset V\},

in which
U
and
V
are open subsets of
K
.

Then the quadruple
(K,\ast,^{-},e)
is called a
locally compact hypergroup

(or simply a
hypergroup
).

Remark 2.2
.

(i)

When there is no confusion, we write
K
instead of
(K,\ast,^{-},e)
.

(ii)

Any locally compact group is a hypergroup. See the monographs
[
8
,
24
]
for
more examples including double coset spaces, polynomial hypergroups and orbit hypergroups etc.,
as well as their applications.

(iii)

In the sequel, we always assume that
K
is a hypergroup with a left

Haar measure
, that is, a non-negative Radon measure
\mu
on
K
such that, for any
x\in K
,

\delta_{x}\ast\mu=\mu
. It has not been proved that every hypergroup has a Haar measure.
However, any commutative
hypergroup, compact hypergroup, discrete hypergroup, and double coset hypergroup admits a Haar measure;
see
[
8
,
24
]
for more examples.

Denote by
{L^{0}(K)}
the space of all
\mu
-measurable complex-valued functions on
K
.
Recall also that the convolution of any two functions
f,g\in{L^{0}(K)}
is defined by setting,
for any
x\in K
,

(2.1)

\displaystyle(f\ast g)(x):=\int_{K}f(y)g(y^{-}\ast x)\,d\mu(y),

where

g(y^{-}\ast x):=\int_{K}g(t)\,d(\delta_{y^{-}}\ast\delta_{x})(t).

In addition, for any
x,y\in K
and
U,V\subset K
, let

\{x\}\ast\{y\}:=\mathop{\mathrm{}}{supp}(\delta_{x}\ast\delta_{y})\quad{\rm and}\quad U\ast V:=\bigcup_{x\in U}\{x\}\ast V:=\bigcup_{x\in U}\bigcup_{y\in V}\{x\}\ast\{y\}.

2.2
Orlicz and Calderón–Lozanovskiĭ spaces

To formulate the main results, we also need some notions on Orlicz spaces from
[
22
,
36
]
.
Recall that a function
\varphi:\ \mathbb{R}\to[0,\infty)
is called a
Young function

if it is convex, even, left continuous on
(0,\infty)
and
\varphi(0)=0
; we also assume that
\varphi
is
neither identically zero nor identically infinite on
(0,\infty)
. Moreover, for any Young function

\varphi
, let

\displaystyle a_{\varphi}:=\sup\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}:\ \varphi(x)=0\right\}\ {\rm and}\ b_{\varphi}:=\sup\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}:\ \varphi(x)<\infty\right\}.

Then it is continuous
on
[0,b_{\varphi})
, nondecreasing on
[0,\infty)
and strictly increasing on
[a_{\varphi},b_{\varphi}]
;
see
[
5
]
.

Let
K
be a hypergroup with a left Haar measure
\mu
and
\varphi
a Young function. For any
f\in{L^{0}(K)}
,
define

\varrho_{\varphi}(f):=\int_{K}\varphi(|f(x)|)\,d\mu(x)

and

\|f\|_{{L^{\varphi}(K)}}:=\inf\left\{\lambda\in(0,\infty):\ \varrho_{\varphi}(f/\lambda)\leq 1\right\},

which is called the
Luxemburg norm
of
f
. Furthermore, the
Orlicz space

{L^{\varphi}(K)}
is defined
to be the set of all
f\in{L^{0}(K)}
satisfying that
\varrho_{\varphi}(f)<\infty
,
equipped with the Luxemburg norm
\|\cdot\|_{{L^{\varphi}(K)}}
.
Observe that the Orlicz space
{L^{\varphi}(K)}
is a Banach space.

The following concept of Calderón–Lozanovskiĭ spaces, which are defined in the similar way
as Orlicz spaces, comes from
[
5
,
9
,
26
]
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
K
be a hypergroup with a left Haar measure
\mu
.

(i)

A Banach space
\Omega=(\Omega,\|\cdot\|_{\Omega})
is called a
Banach ideal space
on
K

if
\Omega
is a linear subspace of
{L^{0}(K)}
and
satisfies the following condition: if
f\in\Omega
,
g\in{L^{0}(K)}
and
|g(x)|\leq|f(x)|
for almost every

x\in K
, then
g\in\Omega
and
\|g\|_{\Omega}\leq\|f\|_{\Omega}
.

(ii)

For a given Banach ideal space
\Omega
on
K
and any Young function
\varphi
,
the
Calderón–Lozanovskiĭ space

{\Omega_{\varphi}(K)}
is defined
to be the set of all
f\in{L^{0}(K)}
satisfying that
I_{\varphi}(f)<\infty
,
equipped with the
Luxemburg norm

\|f\|_{{\Omega_{\varphi}(K)}}:=\inf\left\{\lambda\in(0,\infty):\ I_{\varphi}(f/\lambda)\leq 1\right\},

where

\displaystyle I_{\varphi}(f):=\begin{cases}\|\varphi(|f|)\|_{\Omega}&\text{if}\ \varphi(|f|)\in\Omega,\\
\infty&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

For any
p\in(0,\infty]
, the Lebesgue space
L^{p}(K)
is defined to be the set of all
f\in{L^{0}(K)}

such that, when
p\in(0,\infty)
,

\|f\|_{L^{p}(K)}:=\left[\int_{K}|f(x)|^{p}\,d\mu(x)\right]^{1/p}<\infty

and

\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(K)}:=\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,ess\,sup\,}_{x\in K}|f(x)|<\infty.

Remark 2.4
.

(i)

When
\Omega=L^{1}(K)
, then
{\Omega_{\varphi}(K)}
is the Orlicz space
{L^{\varphi}(K)}
.
When
\Omega
is the Lorentz space on
K
, then
{\Omega_{\varphi}(K)}
becomes the corresponding Orlicz–Lorentz space.

(ii)

If
\varphi(t)=t^{p}
for any given
p\in[1,\infty)
, then
{\Omega_{\varphi}(K)}
is just the
p

convexification
\Omega^{(p)}
of
\Omega
with the norm

\|\cdot\|_{\Omega^{(p)}}=\||\cdot|^{p}\|_{\Omega}^{1/p}.

In addition, if
\varphi(t)=0
for
t\in[0,1]
and
\varphi(t)=\infty
otherwise, then
{\Omega_{\varphi}(K)}
goes back to
the space
L^{\infty}(K)
.

2.3
Some assumptions on the Banach ideal
\Omega

Let
K
be a hypergroup with a left Haar measure
\mu
.
Nonnegative (real) functions
f,\,g\in{L^{0}(K)}
are called
equimeasurable
, if for any
s\in[0,\infty)
,

\mu(\{x\in K:\ |f(x)|>s\})=\mu(\{x\in K:\ |g(x)|>s\}).

In what follows, we always assume that
\Omega
is a Banach ideal in
{L^{0}(K)}
which satisfies additionally
the conditions:

(I)

For any sequence of nonnegative (real) functions
\{f_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset\Omega

and
f\in{L^{0}(K)}
satisfying
f_{n}\uparrow f
almost everywhere on
K
, if
f\in\Omega
, then

\|f_{n}\|_{\Omega}\uparrow\|f\|_{\Omega}
and, if
f\notin\Omega
, then
\|f_{n}\|_{\Omega}\to\infty
;

(II)

for any measurable subset
E\subset K
, if
\mu(E)<\infty
, then
\mathbf{1}_{E}\in\Omega
;

(III)

for any measurable subset
E\subset K
, if
\mu(E)<\infty
, then there exists a positive
constant
C_{E}
, depending on
E
, such that, for any
f\in\Omega
,

\int_{E}|f(x)|d\mu(x)\leq C_{E}\|f\|_{\Omega};

(IV)

if
f,\,g\in\Omega
are equimeasurable, then
\|f\|_{\Omega}=\|g\|_{\Omega}
.

Remark 2.5
.

If
\Omega
consists of real functions, then the above conditions mean that
\Omega
is a Banach function space
(see
[
7
, p. 3, Definition 1.3]
) and also a rearrangement-invariant space
(see
[
7
, p. 59, Definition 4.1]
); if
\Omega
is complex, then in such case, the real space

(\Omega^{\mathbb{R}},\|\cdot\|_{\Omega})
is a Banach function space and also a rearrangement-invariant space,
where
\Omega^{\mathbb{R}}:=\{{\rm Re}(f):f\in\Omega\}
.

Note that, for any measurable subsets
E,\,F\subset K
with
\mu(E)=\mu(F)
, their characteristic functions

\mathbf{1}_{E}
and
\mathbf{1}_{F}
are equimeasurable and hence
\|\mathbf{1}_{E}\|_{\Omega}=\|\mathbf{1}_{F}\|_{\Omega}
.
Therefore, we can define a function
\Phi_{\Omega}:\ [0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
as follows: for any measurable subset

E\subset K
with
\mu(E)<\infty
,

(2.2)

\displaystyle\Phi_{\Omega}(\mu(E)):=\|\mathbf{1}_{E}\|_{\Omega},

which is called the
fundamental function
of
\Omega
; see
[
7
, p. 65, Definition 5.1]
.

Then
[
7
, p. 67, Corollary 5.3]
implies the following Lemma
2.6
.

Lemma 2.6
.

The fundamental function
\Phi_{\Omega}
defined in (
2.2
) has the following properties:

(i)

\Phi_{\Omega}(0)=0
.

(ii)

\Phi_{\Omega}
is nondecreasing and continuous except (perhaps) at origin.

(iii)

The mapping
s\to\frac{\Phi_{\Omega}(s)}{s}
is nonincreasing and, for any
\varepsilon\in(0,\infty)
,
there exists some
s_{0}\in(0,\infty)
such that, for any
s\in[s_{0},\infty)
,

\Phi_{\Omega}(s)\leq\left[\lim_{s\to\infty}\frac{\Phi_{\Omega}(s)}{s}+\varepsilon\right]s.

Finally, we make two more assumptions on the Banach ideal
\Omega
:

(V)

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to 0}\Phi_{\Omega}(s)=0
,
that is, the fundamental function
\Phi_{\Omega}
is continuous at origin;

(VI)

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to\infty}\Phi_{\Omega}(s)=\infty
,
that is, the fundamental function
\Phi_{\Omega}
is unbounded.

Remark 2.7
.

(i)

By
[
7
, p. 67, Theorem 5.5]
, we find that, if
\Omega
is separable,
then
\Phi_{\Omega}
is continuous at origin.

(ii)

The assumption (VI) is also natural, for instance, it (and also the
assumption (V)) is satisfied when
K
is not compact and
\Omega=L^{p}(K)
with
\|\cdot\|_{\Omega}=\|\cdot\|_{L^{p}(K)}
.

(iii)

We point out that, under the above assumptions (I)-(VI), the Banach ideal
\Omega

can be taken as many function spaces such as Lebesgue spaces, Orlicz spaces, etc.

Main results

We begin with some concepts which are later used to state our first result.

Definition 2.8
.

Let
\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}
be two Young functions. The
product Calderón–Lozanovskiĭ space

is defined as

{\Omega_{\varphi_{1}}(K)}\times{\Omega_{\varphi_{2}}(K)}:=\left\{(f,g):\ f\in{\Omega_{\varphi_{1}}(K)},\ g\in{\Omega_{\varphi_{2}}(K)}\right\}

where
\{\Omega_{\varphi_{i}}(K)\}_{i=1,2}
are Calderón–Lozanovskiĭ spaces as in Definition
2.3
(ii).

A hypergroup
K
is said to be satisfying a condition
(\divideontimes)
if, for every compact neighborhood

U
of the identity element of
K
, there exist some
\omega\in(1,\infty)
and
a strictly increasing sequence
\{n_{k}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset{\mathbb{N}}

k\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

(2.3)

\displaystyle\mu(\overbrace{U\ast\cdots\ast U}^{2n_{k}\ \mathrm{times}})<\omega\mu(\overbrace{U\ast\cdots\ast U}^{n_{k}\ \mathrm{times}}),

and there exists some
k_{0}\in{\mathbb{N}}
large enough such that, for any

(2.4)

\displaystyle\delta_{x}\ast\delta_{x^{-}}=\delta_{e}=\delta_{x^{-}}\ast\delta_{x}.

Remark 2.9
.

By an argument similar to the proof of

for examples of hypergroups

The

of a Young function

is defined by setting, for any

\varphi^{-1}(y):=\sup\{x\in[0,\infty):\ \varphi(x)\leq y\}.

Then the first main result of this article is stated as follows.

Assume that
K
is a non-compact hypergroup satisfying the condition
(\divideontimes)
.
Let
\{\varphi_{i}\}_{i=1,2,3}
be Young functions with

\displaystyle\liminf_{x\to 0}\frac{\varphi_{1}^{-1}(x)\varphi_{2}^{-1}(x)}{x\varphi_{3}^{-1}(x)}=\infty.

E=\left\{(f,g)\in{\Omega_{\varphi_{1}}(K)}\times{\Omega_{\varphi_{2}}(K)}:\ |f|\ast|g|\in{\Omega_{\varphi_{3}}(K)}\right\}

{\Omega_{\varphi_{1}}(K)}\times{\Omega_{\varphi_{2}}(K)}

(2.6)

Let

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{1}_{E}\|_{\Omega_{\varphi}(K)}=\left[\varphi^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{\|\mathbf{1}_{E}\|_{\Omega}}\right)\right]^{-1}.

).

Similarly to

.

(i)

(ii)

for any

Proof of Theorem

.

For any

, let

Then

To this end, for any given

and
m\in{\mathbb{N}}

\displaystyle\frac{D^{2}}{288(M+1)}\frac{\varphi_{1}^{-1}(x)\varphi_{2}^{-1}(x)}{x\varphi_{3}^{-1}(x)}>m,

Observe that
K
is non-compact. According to the assumption (VI) of

and Lemma

(iii),

\displaystyle\delta_{x}\ast\delta_{x^{-}}=\delta_{e}=\delta_{x^{-}}\ast\delta_{x},

\left\|f-\widetilde{f}\,\right\|_{{\Omega_{\varphi}(K)}}=\frac{D}{3}=\left\|g-\widetilde{g}\,\right\|_{{\Omega_{\phi}(K)}}

and hence

B\left(\left(\widetilde{f},\,\widetilde{g}\right),d\right)\subset B\left((f,g),D\right).

Clearly, it suffices to show that
B((\widetilde{f},\,\widetilde{g}),d)\cap E_{U,m}=\emptyset
.

For this purpose, for any
(u,v)\in B((\widetilde{f},\,\widetilde{g}),d)
, let

\displaystyle F_{1,u}:=\left\{x\in F_{1}:\ |u(x)|\leq\frac{C_{f}}{2}\right\}

and

\displaystyle F_{2,v}:=\left\{x\in F_{2}:\ |v(x)|\leq\frac{C_{g}}{2}\right\}.

Then, by a calculation parallel to (
3.15
), we conclude that

\displaystyle\mu(F_{1})-\mu\left(\left(F_{2,v}\right)^{-}\right)-\mu\left(F_{1,u}\right)\geq\frac{1}{2}\mu(F_{1}).

For any
y\in V
, let

Y_{y}:=\left(F_{1}\setminus F_{1,u}\right)\cap\left(\{y\}\ast\left(F_{2}\setminus F_{2,v}\right)^{-}\right).

By (
4.10
), following the proof of (
3.17
) with some slight modifications, we obtain that,
for any
(u,v)\in B((\widetilde{f},\,\widetilde{g}),d)
and
y\in V
,

\displaystyle\int_{K}|u|(x)|v|(x^{-}\ast y)\,d\mu(x)

\displaystyle>\frac{C_{f}C_{g}}{4}\mu\left(Y_{y}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{D^{2}\mu(F_{1})}{72}\varphi^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{\|\mathbf{1}_{F_{1}}\|_{\Omega}}\right)\phi^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{\|\mathbf{1}_{F_{2}}\|_{\Omega}}\right)>m.

This, together with the fact
U\subset V
gives us
(u,v)\notin E_{U,m}
and hence finishes the proof
of Theorem
2.14
.
∎

Finally, we give the proof of Theorem
2.15
.

Proof of Theorem
2.15
.

Proceeding as in the previous proof, we will show that, for any
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
, the set

E_{U,m}:=\left\{(f,g)\in{\Omega_{\varphi}(K)}\times{\Omega_{\phi}(K)}:\ \exists\,x\in U,\ \int_{K}|f|(y)|g|(y^{-}\ast x)\,d\mu(y)<m\right\}

is nowhere dense.

Note that the compact set
U
satisfies the condition
(\star\star)
. Then there exist some

a\in K
and a compact symmetric neighborhood
V
of the identity element
e
in
K
such that

\Delta(a)<1
,
U\subset V
and (
2.11
) through (
2.13
) are valid. Without loss of generality,
we may assume that

(4.11)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\|\mathbf{1}_{V\ast V}\|_{\Omega}}<\phi(b_{\phi})

since
K
is not compact and
\phi(b_{\phi})>0
. For any given
D\in(0,\infty)
and
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
, by
(
2.14
) and the assumption
\varphi(b_{\varphi})>0
, we can find some
x_{0}\in(0,\varphi(b_{\varphi}))
such that,
for any
x\in(0,x_{0})
,

(4.12)

\displaystyle\frac{D^{2}}{72(M+1)}\frac{\varphi^{-1}(x)}{x}\phi^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{C_{(U,V,a)}\|\mathbf{1}_{V\ast V}\|_{\Omega}}\right)>m,

where
\displaystyle M:=\lim_{s\to\infty}\frac{\Phi_{\Omega}(s)}{s}
and
C_{(U,V,a)}\in[1,\infty)
is as in (
2.12
).

On another hand, from (
2.13
), the assumption (VI) of
\Omega
and Lemma
2.6
(iii), it follows that
there exists some
k_{0}\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that, for any
k\in[k_{0},\infty)\cap{\mathbb{N}}
,

(4.13)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\|\mathbf{1}_{V\ast\{a^{-}\}^{k}}\|_{\Omega}}<x_{0}\quad{\rm and}\quad\left\|\mathbf{1}_{V\ast\{a^{-}\}^{k}}\right\|_{\Omega}\leq(M+1)\mu\left(V\ast\{a^{-}\}^{k}\right).

Define

F_{1}:=V\ast\{a^{-}\}^{k_{0}}\quad{\rm and}\quad F_{2}:=\{a\}^{k_{0}}\ast V\ast V.

Then, by (
2.12
),
[
17
, Lemma 3.3C]
and (
4.11
), similar to (
3.6
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{C_{(U,V,a)}\|\mathbf{1}_{V\ast V}\|_{\Omega}}\leq\frac{1}{\|\mathbf{1}_{F_{2}}\|_{\Omega}}\leq\frac{1}{\|\mathbf{1}_{V\ast V}\|_{\Omega}}<\phi(b_{\phi}).

According to this and (
4.13
), the inequality (
4.12
) takes the form

\displaystyle\frac{D^{2}\mu(F_{1})}{72}\varphi^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{\|\mathbf{1}_{F_{1}}\|_{\Omega}}\right)\phi^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{\|\mathbf{1}_{F_{2}}\|_{\Omega}}\right)>m.

The remainder of the present proof is parallel to that of Theorem
2.14
;
we omit the details. The proof of Theorem
2.15
is completed.

Matthew Satriano, Evan Sundbo

Abstract
. We introduce an integral version of the Hodge polynomial, which encodes the integral cohomology of smooth projective varieties. We prove it extends to a function which is well-defined on the Grothendieck ring of varieties and we obtain as a consequence that K K -equivalent smooth projective varieties have isomorphic integral cohomology groups.



1.
Introduction

In 1995, Kontsevich
[
7
]
proved that
K
-equivalent smooth projective varieties have the same Hodge numbers. In this brief note, we extend his result to integral cohomology.

Theorem 1.1
.

If
X
and
X^{\prime}
are
K
-equivalent smooth projective varieties over
\mathbb{C}
then
H^{i}(X,\mathbb{Z})\simeq H^{i}(X^{\prime},\mathbb{Z})
for all
i
.

We prove this theorem by introducing an
integral virtual Hodge function

H_{\text{vir},\mathbb{Z}}
. It is well-defined on the Grothendieck ring of varieties
K_{0}(\text{Var}_{\mathbb{C}})
and has the property that when
X
is a smooth projective variety, the integral Hodge structure, and in particular the integral cohomology groups, of
X
can be read off from
H_{\text{vir},\mathbb{Z}}(X)
.

The main technical point in constructing
H_{\text{vir},\mathbb{Z}}
is defining the ring where it takes values in such a way that
H_{\text{vir},\mathbb{Z}}
is multiplicative, i.e., that

H_{\text{vir},\mathbb{Z}}(X\times Y)=H_{\text{vir},\mathbb{Z}}(X)H_{\text{vir},\mathbb{Z}}(Y).

Definition 1.2
.

Given a smooth projective complex variety
X
, let the
integral Hodge function

H_{\mathbb{Z}}(X,s_{\mathfrak{p}},r_{j},u,v,t,x)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j\geq 0\\
\mathfrak{p}\text{ prime}\end{subarray}}(-1)^{i}a_{\mathfrak{p},i,j}(X)s_{\mathfrak{p}}r_{j}t^{i}x+\sum_{p,q,i\geq 0}(-1)^{i}h^{p,q}(X)u^{p}v^{q},

where

a_{\mathfrak{p},i,j}(X)=\text{rk}_{\mathbb{Z}/\mathfrak{p}\mathbb{Z}}\left(\frac{\mathfrak{p}^{j}H^{i}(X,\mathbb{Z})_{\text{tors}}}{\mathfrak{p}^{j+1}H^{i}(X,\mathbb{Z})_{\text{tors}}}\right)

and
h^{p,q}(X)
are the usual Hodge numbers of
X
. This function takes values in the ring

R:=\frac{\mathbb{Z}[s_{\mathfrak{p}},r_{j},u,v,t,x:\mathfrak{p}\text{ prime},\,j\geq 0]}{\langle tx^{2}=tx-x,ux=vx=tx,s_{\mathfrak{p}}^{2}=s_{\mathfrak{p}},r_{j}^{2}=r_{j},s_{\mathfrak{p}}s_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}}=r_{j}r_{j^{\prime}}=0\text{ for }\mathfrak{p}\neq\mathfrak{p}^{\prime},j\neq j^{\prime}\rangle}.

We extend
H_{\mathbb{Z}}
to the virtual integral Hodge function
H_{\text{vir},\mathbb{Z}}
in Proposition
3.1
, which takes values on complex varieties which are not necessarily smooth or projective. We discuss the definition of the integral Hodge function and some of its basic properties in Section
2
. Subsection
3.1
is devoted to proving that
H_{\mathbb{Z}}
extends to a function
H_{\text{vir},\mathbb{Z}}
on the Grothendieck ring of varieties. As a quick application, we give a new proof of the fact that if a smooth projective variety admits an algebraic cell decomposition (for example if
X
admits a nontrivial
\mathbb{C}^{*}
action with only finitely many fixed points, per
[
1
]
) then its integral cohomology is torsion-free. We then use the existence of
H_{\text{vir},\mathbb{Z}}
in Section
3.2
to prove our Theorem
1.1
.

As a corollary of Theorem
1.1
, one finds that if
X
is a projective algebraic variety of dimension
n
with at worst Gorenstein canonical singularities admitting a crepant resolution of singularities
Y\to X
, then the integral cohomology groups of
Y
are independent of the choice of crepant resolution.

Lastly, let us note that
H_{\text{vir},\mathbb{Z}}
also admits further generalizations, such as to a
stringy
version by making use of the Gorenstein measure
[
4
]
(as is done for non-integral invariants in e.g. Section 7.3.4 of
[
3
]
). One may also define a virtual integral
stacky
version by extending
H_{\text{vir},\mathbb{Z}}
to the Grothendieck group of stacks introduced by Ekedahl
[
5
]
.

After the first version of this note was posted to the arXiv we were made aware of
[
6
]
and
[
10
]
, in which Theorem
1.1
was previously shown to be true.

2.
Integral Virtual Hodge Functions

Definition 2.1
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective complex variety. The
torsion Poincaré function
of
X
is

\mathcal{T}(X)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j\geq 0\\
\mathfrak{p}\text{ prime}\end{subarray}}(-1)^{i}a_{\mathfrak{p},i,j}(X)s_{\mathfrak{p}}r_{j}t^{i}x

lying in the ring

S:=\frac{\mathbb{Z}[s_{\mathfrak{p}},r_{j},t,x:\mathfrak{p}\text{ prime},\,j\geq 0]}{\langle tx^{2}=tx-x,s_{\mathfrak{p}}^{2}=s_{\mathfrak{p}},r_{j}^{2}=r_{j},s_{\mathfrak{p}}s_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}}=r_{j}r_{j^{\prime}}=0\text{ for }\mathfrak{p}\neq\mathfrak{p}^{\prime},j\neq j^{\prime}\rangle}

defined by

a_{\mathfrak{p},i,j}(X)=\text{rk}_{\mathbb{Z}/\mathfrak{p}\mathbb{Z}}\left(\frac{\mathfrak{p}^{j}H^{i}(X,\mathbb{Z})_{\text{tors}}}{\mathfrak{p}^{j+1}H^{i}(X,\mathbb{Z})_{\text{tors}}}\right).

This invariant encodes torsion in the integral cohomology groups of
X
, which can be recovered (see Proposition
2.4
) as

H^{i}(X,\mathbb{Z})_{\text{tors}}\simeq\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{p}\text{ prime}}\bigoplus_{j\geq 1}\left(\mathbb{Z}/\mathfrak{p}^{j}\mathbb{Z}\right)^{a_{\mathfrak{p},i,j-1}(X)-a_{\mathfrak{p},i,j}(X)}

Note however that
\mathcal{T}
is not multiplicative, in that we do not necessarily have the equality
\mathcal{T}(X\times Y)=\mathcal{T}(X)\mathcal{T}(Y).
This is due to the possibility of the free parts of the cohomology groups interacting with the torsion parts. To obtain an invariant that captures torsion information and behaves in the expected ways, we need to keep track of the free part of the cohomology groups as well.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective complex variety. The
integral Hodge function
of
X
is

(2.1)

H_{\mathbb{Z}}(X,s_{\mathfrak{p}},r_{j},t,x,u,v)=\mathcal{T}(X)+\sum_{p,q\geq 0}(-1)^{p+q}h^{p,q}(X)u^{p}v^{q}

lying in the ring

R:=\frac{S\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}[u,v]}{\langle ux=vx=tx\rangle}

where
h^{p,q}(X)
are the usual Hodge numbers of
X
. We frequently denote
H_{\mathbb{Z}}(X,s_{\mathfrak{p}},r_{j},t,x,u,v)
simply as
H_{\mathbb{Z}}(X)
.

We introduce the following definition; we prove it is well-defined and recovers the integral cohomology in Proposition
2.4
.

Definition 2.3
.

Denote by
R^{+}\subset R

\bigg\{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq i\leq n\\
j\geq 0,\,\mathfrak{p}\text{ prime}\end{subarray}}(-1)^{i}a_{\mathfrak{p},i,j}s_{\mathfrak{p}}r_{j}t^{i}x+\sum_{0\leq p,q\leq n}(-1)^{p+q}h^{p,q}u^{p}v^{q}\in R\,\big|\,n\geq 0,\,h^{p,q}\geq 0\text{, and }a_{\mathfrak{p},i,j-1}\geq a_{\mathfrak{p},i,j}\geq 0\bigg\}.

For
\alpha\in R^{+}
with notation as above, let

\phi_{i}(\alpha)=\bigoplus_{p,q\geq 0}\mathbb{Z}^{h^{p,q}}\oplus\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{p}\text{ prime}}\bigoplus_{j\geq 1}\left(\mathbb{Z}/\mathfrak{p}^{j}\mathbb{Z}\right)^{a_{\mathfrak{p},i,j-1}-a_{\mathfrak{p},i,j}}.

Proposition 2.4
.

The maps
\phi_{i}
are well-defined and for
X
smooth and projective we have

\phi_{i}(H_{\mathbb{Z}}(X))\simeq H^{i}(X,\mathbb{Z}).

Proof.

Write the ring
R
as

R=\frac{\mathbb{Z}[s_{\mathfrak{p}},r_{j}]}{\langle s_{\mathfrak{p}}^{2}=s_{\mathfrak{p}},r_{j}=r_{j}^{2},s_{\mathfrak{p}}s_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}}=r_{j}r_{j^{\prime}}=0\rangle}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\frac{\mathbb{Z}[u,v,t,x]}{\langle tx^{2}=tx-x,ux=vx=tx\rangle}.

Then the first tensor factor has a basis given by
1,s_{\mathfrak{p}},r_{j},s_{\mathfrak{p}}r_{j}
. The second tensor factor has a basis given by
u^{p}v^{q}t^{i}
and
t^{i}x
for
p,q,i\geq 0
, as well as
x^{i}
for
i\geq 2
. As a result, we see the elements
s_{\mathfrak{p}}r_{j}t^{i}x
and
u^{p}v^{q}t^{i}
form a linearly independent set in
R
allowing us to read off the coefficients
a_{\mathfrak{p},i,j}(X)
and
h^{p,q}
.
∎

3.
Integral cohomology under
K
-equivalance

Our goal in this section is to prove Theorem
1.1
. Recall that two smooth projective varieties
X
and
Y
are said to be
K
-equivalent
if there exists a smooth projective variety
Z
, dominating both
X
and
Y
, birational to both
X
and
Y
, and with
K_{Z/X}=K_{Z/Y}
.

In this first subsection below, we prove
H_{\mathbb{Z}}
extends to a function on the Grothendieck ring of varieties. We then use this in the following subsection to prove Theorem
1.1
.

3.1.
H_{\mathbb{Z}}
as a function on the Grothendieck ring of varieties

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
X
and
Y
be smooth projective complex algebraic varieties. Then

(1)

If
Z\subset X
is a smooth projective subvariety of codimension
c
,
\pi:\text{Bl}_{Z}(X)\to X
is the blow-up of
X
along
Z
, and
E
is the exceptional divisor of the blowup, then

H_{\mathbb{Z}}(X)-H_{\mathbb{Z}}(Z)=H_{\mathbb{Z}}(\text{Bl}_{Z}(X))-H_{\mathbb{Z}}(E).

(2)

H_{\mathbb{Z}}(X\times Y)=H_{\mathbb{Z}}(X)H_{\mathbb{Z}}(Y)

In particular,
H_{\mathbb{Z}}
extends to a function

H_{\text{vir},\mathbb{Z}}\colon K_{0}(\text{Var}_{\mathbb{C}})\to R.

We first require a preliminary result.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
A
be a finitely generated
\mathbb{Z}
-module which is torsion. Let

a_{\mathfrak{p},j}(A)=\text{rk}_{\mathbb{Z}/\mathfrak{p}\mathbb{Z}}\left(\frac{\mathfrak{p}^{j}A}{\mathfrak{p}^{j+1}A}\right)

for each prime
\mathfrak{p}
and each
j\geq 0
. Then
A
is reconstructed from these invariants as

A\simeq\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{p}\text{ prime}}\bigoplus_{j\geq 1}\left(\mathbb{Z}/\mathfrak{p}^{j}\mathbb{Z}\right)^{a_{\mathfrak{p},j-1}(A)-a_{\mathfrak{p},j}(A)}.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}
be different primes,
r,k,k^{\prime}\geq 0
, and
A,B
finitely generated torsion
\mathbb{Z}
-modules.

(1)

a_{\mathfrak{p},j}(A\oplus B)=a_{\mathfrak{p},j}(A)+a_{\mathfrak{p},j}(B)

(2)

a_{\mathfrak{p},j}(A^{\oplus r})=ra_{\mathfrak{p},j}(A)

(3)

a_{\mathfrak{p},j}(\mathbb{Z}/\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}\mathbb{Z})=0

(4)

a_{\mathfrak{p},j}(\mathbb{Z}/\mathfrak{p}^{k}\mathbb{Z})=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }j<k\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

(5)

a_{\mathfrak{p},j}(\mathbb{Z}/\mathfrak{p}^{k}\mathbb{Z}\otimes\mathbb{Z}/\mathfrak{p}^{k^{\prime}}\mathbb{Z})=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }j<\min\{k,k^{\prime}\}\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

(6)

a_{\mathfrak{p},j}(A\otimes B)=a_{\mathfrak{p},j}(A)a_{\mathfrak{p},j}(B)

Proof.

Items
(1)-(5)
are immediate from the definition. We now prove
(6)
. By part
(3)
we only have to look at the
\mathfrak{p}
-torsion parts of
A
and
B
. Write

A_{\mathfrak{p}-\text{tors}}=(\mathbb{Z}/\mathfrak{p}\mathbb{Z})^{r_{1}}\oplus\ldots\oplus(\mathbb{Z}/\mathfrak{p}^{k}\mathbb{Z})^{r_{k}}

and

B_{\mathfrak{p}-\text{tors}}=(\mathbb{Z}/\mathfrak{p}\mathbb{Z})^{r^{\prime}_{1}}\oplus\ldots\oplus(\mathbb{Z}/\mathfrak{p}^{k^{\prime}}\mathbb{Z})^{r^{\prime}_{k^{\prime}}}.

Then

(A\otimes B)_{\mathfrak{p}-\text{tors}}=\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\leq k\\
i^{\prime}\leq k^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\left(\mathbb{Z}/\mathfrak{p}^{i}\otimes\mathbb{Z}/\mathfrak{p}^{i^{\prime}}\right)^{\oplus r_{i}r^{\prime}_{i^{\prime}}}

so that by
(1)
–
(5)
we see

a_{\mathfrak{p},j}(A\otimes B)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j<i\leq k\\
j<i^{\prime}\leq k^{\prime}\end{subarray}}r_{i}r^{\prime}_{i^{\prime}}=\left(\sum_{j<i}r_{i}\right)\left(\sum_{j<i^{\prime}}r^{\prime}_{i^{\prime}}\right)=a_{\mathfrak{p},j}(A)a_{\mathfrak{p},j}(B).\qed

Proof of Proposition
3.1
.

(1) By Section 7.3.3 of
[
9
]
, we see

(3.1)

H^{k}(\text{Bl}_{Z}(X),\mathbb{Z})=H^{k}(X,\mathbb{Z})\oplus\bigoplus_{i=1}^{c-1}H^{k-2i}(Z,\mathbb{Z}).

Furthermore, since
E\to Z
is a projective bundle
P\to B
of rank
c
, we have

(3.2)

H^{k}(P,\mathbb{Z})=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{c-1}H^{k-2i}(B,\mathbb{Z}).

Hence (1) follows.

(2) We begin by claiming that the Künneth formula for integral cohomology yields

(3.3)

\displaystyle H_{\mathbb{Z}}(X\times Y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{p,q\geq 0}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}e+f=p\\
g+h=q\end{subarray}}(-1)^{p+q}h^{e,g}(X)h^{f,h}(Y)\right)u^{p}v^{q}+

(3.4)

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j\geq 0\\
\mathfrak{p}\text{ prime}\end{subarray}}(-1)^{i}\left(\sum_{p+q+c=i}\left(h^{p,q}(X)a_{\mathfrak{p},c,j}(Y)+a_{\mathfrak{p},c,j}(X)h^{p,q}(Y)\right)\right)s_{\mathfrak{p}}r_{j}t^{i}x

(3.5)

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j\geq 0\\
\mathfrak{p}\text{ prime}\end{subarray}}(-1)^{i}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}c+d=i\end{subarray}}a_{\mathfrak{p},c,j}(X)a_{\mathfrak{p},d,j}(Y)+\sum_{c+d=i+1}a_{\mathfrak{p},c,j}(X)a_{\mathfrak{p},d,j}(Y)\right)s_{\mathfrak{p}}r_{j}t^{i}x

The first line is the contribution from the free parts of the integral cohomology groups so let us focus on the second and third lines. For each
i\geq 0
we have

\displaystyle H^{i}(X\times Y,\mathbb{Z})\simeq

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{c+d=i}\left(H^{c}(X,\mathbb{Z})\otimes H^{d}(Y,\mathbb{Z})\right)_{\text{tors}}\oplus\bigoplus_{c+d=i+1}\text{Tor}_{\mathbb{Z}}(H^{c}(X,\mathbb{Z}),H^{d}(Y,\mathbb{Z}))

\displaystyle\qquad=\bigoplus_{c+d=i}\left(H^{c}(X,\mathbb{Z})_{\text{free}}\otimes H^{d}(Y,\mathbb{Z})_{\text{tors}}\right)\oplus\bigoplus_{c+d=i}\left(H^{c}(X,\mathbb{Z})_{\text{tors}}\otimes H^{d}(Y,\mathbb{Z})_{\text{free}}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\oplus\bigoplus_{c+d=i}\left(H^{c}(X,\mathbb{Z})_{\text{tors}}\otimes H^{d}(Y,\mathbb{Z})_{\text{tors}}\right)\oplus\bigoplus_{c+d=i+1}\text{Tor}_{\mathbb{Z}}(H^{c}(X,\mathbb{Z})_{\text{tors}},H^{d}(Y,\mathbb{Z})_{\text{tors}})

Since
H^{c}(X,\mathbb{Z})_{\text{free}}\otimes H^{d}(Y,\mathbb{Z})_{\text{tors}}\simeq H^{d}(Y,\mathbb{Z})_{\text{tors}}^{\sum_{p+q=c}h^{p,q}(X)}
, we see by parts
(1)
and
(2)
of Proposition
3.3
that

a_{\mathfrak{p},j}\left(\bigoplus_{c+d=i}\left(H^{c}(X,\mathbb{Z})_{\text{free}}\otimes H^{d}(Y,\mathbb{Z})_{\text{tors}}\right)\right)=\sum_{p+q+c=i}(-1)^{i}h^{p,q}(X)a_{\mathfrak{p},d,j}(Y).

We have a similar expression for
\bigoplus_{c+d=i}\left(H^{c}(X,\mathbb{Z})_{\text{tors}}\otimes H^{d}(Y,\mathbb{Z})_{\text{free}}\right)
. These two sums account for Line (
3.4
) above.

Since
\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}\otimes\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}\simeq\mathbb{Z}/\text{gcd}(n,m)\mathbb{Z}\simeq\text{Tor}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})
, the contributions to the integral virtual Hodge polynomial coming from

\bigoplus_{c+d=i}\left(H^{c}(X,\mathbb{Z})_{\text{tors}}\otimes H^{d}(Y,\mathbb{Z})_{\text{tors}}\right)

and from

\bigoplus_{c+d=i+1}\text{Tor}_{\mathbb{Z}}(H^{c}(X,\mathbb{Z})_{\text{tors}},H^{d}(Y,\mathbb{Z})_{\text{tors}})

will be the same, except that they contribute to different
t
-degree. Part
(6)
of Proposition
3.3
then directly accounts for Line (
3.5
).

Secondly we must simplify

\displaystyle H_{\mathbb{Z}}(X)H_{\mathbb{Z}}(Y)

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j\geq 0\\
\mathfrak{p}\text{ prime}\end{subarray}}(-1)^{i}a_{\mathfrak{p},i,j}(X)s_{\mathfrak{p}}r_{j}t^{i}x+\sum_{p,q\geq 0}(-1)^{p+q}h^{p,q}(X)u^{p}v^{q}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j\geq 0\\
\mathfrak{p}\text{ prime}\end{subarray}}(-1)^{i}a_{\mathfrak{p},i,j}(Y)s_{\mathfrak{p}}r_{j}t^{i}x+\sum_{p,q\geq 0}(-1)^{p+q}h^{p,q}(Y)u^{p}v^{q}\right).

The product of the second terms in the two factors above reads as

\sum_{p,q\geq 0}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}e+f=p\\
g+h=q\end{subarray}}(-1)^{p+q}h^{e,g}(X)h^{f,h}(Y)u^{e+f}v^{g+h}\right),

which accounts exactly for Line (
3.3
). The coefficient of
t^{i}x
in the product on coming from mixing the
a
and
h
terms is

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j,c,p,q\geq 0\\
\mathfrak{p}\text{ prime}\end{subarray}}\left((-1)^{p+q+c}\left(h^{p,q}(X)a_{\mathfrak{p},c,j}(Y)+a_{\mathfrak{p},c,j}(X)h^{p,q}(Y)\right)s_{\mathfrak{p}}r_{j}u^{p}v^{q}t^{c}x\right)

\displaystyle\qquad=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j\geq 0\\
\mathfrak{p}\text{ prime}\end{subarray}}(-1)^{i}\left(\sum_{p+q+c=i}\left(h^{p,q}(X)a_{\mathfrak{p},c,j}(Y)+a_{\mathfrak{p},c,j}(X)h^{p,q}(Y)\right)\right)s_{\mathfrak{p}}r_{j}t^{i}x

which matches Line (
3.4
).

Lastly we have to inspect the part of the coefficient of
t^{i}x
coming from the
a
terms. Any terms with
\mathfrak{p}\neq\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}
or
j\neq j^{\prime}
vanish, and multiplying
a_{\mathfrak{p},c,j}s_{\mathfrak{p}}r_{j}t^{c}x
with
a_{\mathfrak{p},d,j}s_{\mathfrak{p}}r_{j}t^{d}x
yields

\displaystyle(-1)^{c+d}a_{\mathfrak{p},c,j}a_{\mathfrak{p},d,j}s_{\mathfrak{p}}r_{j}t^{c+d}x^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad=(-1)^{c+d}a_{\mathfrak{p},c,j}a_{\mathfrak{p},d,j}s_{\mathfrak{p}}r_{j}t^{c+d}x+(-1)^{c+d}a_{\mathfrak{p},c,j}a_{\mathfrak{p},d,j}s_{\mathfrak{p}}r_{j}t^{c+d-1}x

This accounts for the torsion terms in the Künneth formula and shows that the part of the coefficient of
t^{i}x
coming from the
a
terms is

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\geq 0\\
\mathfrak{p}\text{ prime}\end{subarray}}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}c+d=i\\
e+f=i+1\end{subarray}}(-1)^{i}\left(a_{\mathfrak{p},c,j}(X)a_{\mathfrak{p},d,j}(Y)+a_{\mathfrak{p},e,j}(X)a_{\mathfrak{p},f,j}(Y)\right)s_{\mathfrak{p}}r_{j}t^{i}x\right)

and this matches Line (
3.5
).

For the final claim, use Theorem 3.1 of
[
2
]
which states that the abelian group generated by the isomorphism classes of smooth projective
varieties under to the relations
[\varnothing]=0
and
[\text{Bl}_{Z}(X)]-[E]=[X]-[Z]
is isomorphic to
K_{0}(\text{Var}_{\mathbb{C}})
.
∎

Corollary 3.4
.

If
X
is a quasi-projective variety and
U\subset X
is an open subset then

H_{\text{vir},\mathbb{Z}}(X)=H_{\text{vir},\mathbb{Z}}(U)+H_{\text{vir},\mathbb{Z}}(X\setminus U).

Proof.

As a result of
H_{\text{vir},\mathbb{Z}}
being well-defined on
K_{0}(\text{Var}_{\mathbb{C}})
it respects the relation
[X]=[U]-[X\setminus U]
which is used in the standard presentation of the Grothendieck group of varieties.
∎

As a quick application of the existence of
H_{\text{vir},\mathbb{Z}}
we reprove the following well-known result:

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective algebraic variety which admits an algebraic cell decomposition. Then the integral cohomology groups of
X
are torsion-free.

Proof.

By assumption we have a filtration

X=X_{n}\supseteq\ldots\supseteq X_{0}\supseteq X_{-1}=\varnothing

where each
X_{i}
is a closed subvariety of
X
and each
X_{i}\setminus X_{i-1}
is a disjoint union of affine spaces. Using the cut-and-paste relations (Corollary
3.4
) one can write
\{X\}
as a sum of classes of affine spaces. Applying
H_{\text{vir},\mathbb{Z}}
to the class of an affine space yields
H_{\text{vir},\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{L}^{k})=H_{\mathbb{Z}}(\{\mathbb{P}^{k}\})-H_{\mathbb{Z}}(\{\mathbb{P}^{k-1}\})=H(\{\mathbb{P}^{k}\})-H(\{\mathbb{P}^{k-1}\})
(where
H(-)
is the usual Hodge polynomial), implying that
H_{\mathbb{Z}}(X)=H(X)
and so the integral cohomology of
X
is torsion-free, by Proposition
2.4
.
∎

3.2.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

To prove Theorem
1.1
, we need a notion of degree in
R
. Note that the usual
t
-degree is not well-defined in
R
, the issue being that
tx^{2}-tx=-x
in
R
does not have a well-defined degree. As a result, we must be a bit more careful. Also note that considering the original definition in Equation (
2.1
), an element with a coefficient of the form
u^{p}v^{q}
should morally have degree
p+q
.

Definition 3.6
.

Let
\mathcal{B}
be the basis constructed in the proof of Proposition
2.4
. Let
\alpha\in R
be written as
\sum_{b\in\mathcal{B}}n_{b}b
with
n_{b}\in\mathbb{Z}
. The
degree
of
\alpha
is the maximum between the largest sum of powers of
t
,
u
and
v
appearing among the
b\in\mathcal{B}
for which
n_{b}\neq 0
.

The following result is immediate from the above definition.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
\alpha,\beta\in R
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

(1)

\deg(\alpha+\beta)\leq\max\{\deg(\alpha),\deg(\beta)\}

(2)

\deg(n\alpha)=\deg(\alpha)

Denoting the class of the affine line by
\mathbb{L}
and using the cut-and-paste relations, one calculates
H_{\text{vir},\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{L})=uv
. Hence the function
H_{\text{vir},\mathbb{Z}}
constructed in Proposition
3.1
extends naturally to a map

K_{0}(\text{Var}_{\mathbb{C}})[\mathbb{L}^{-1}]\to R[(uv)^{-1}]

which we continue to denote as
H_{\text{vir},\mathbb{Z}}
. Let
\widehat{K_{0}(\text{Var}_{\mathbb{C}})[\mathbb{L}^{-1}]}
be the completion with respect to the
dimension filtration

\ldots\subseteq F^{m}K_{0}(\text{Var}_{\mathbb{C}})[\mathbb{L}^{-1}]\subseteq F^{m-1}K_{0}(\text{Var}_{\mathbb{C}})[\mathbb{L}^{-1}]\subseteq\ldots

where
F^{m}K_{0}(\text{Var}_{\mathbb{C}})[\mathbb{L}^{-1}]
is the subgroup spanned by classes of the form
[X]\mathbb{L}^{-i}
with
\text{dim}X-i\geq m
. Further set

\overline{K_{0}(\text{Var}_{\mathbb{C}})[\mathbb{L}^{-1}]}\subset\widehat{K_{0}(\text{Var}_{\mathbb{C}})[\mathbb{L}^{-1}]}

to be the image of
K_{0}(\text{Var}_{\mathbb{C}})[\mathbb{L}^{-1}]
.

Lemma 3.8
.

The integral virtual Hodge function
H_{\text{vir},\mathbb{Z}}\colon K_{0}(\text{Var}_{\mathbb{C}})[\mathbb{L}^{-1}]\to R[(uvt^{2})^{-1}]
factors through
\overline{K_{0}(\text{Var}_{\mathbb{C}})[\mathbb{L}^{-1}]}\subset\widehat{K_{0}(\text{Var}_{\mathbb{C}})[\mathbb{L}^{-1}]}
.

Proof.

The proof is essentially the same as in the case of non-integral invariants. We want to show that if

\alpha\in\text{ker}\left(K_{0}(\text{Var}_{\mathbb{C}})[\mathbb{L}^{-1}]\to\widehat{K_{0}(\text{Var}_{\mathbb{C}})[\mathbb{L}^{-1}]}\right)=\bigcap_{m\geq 0}F^{m}K_{0}(\text{Var}_{\mathbb{C}})[\mathbb{L}^{-1}]

then
H_{\text{vir},\mathbb{Z}}(\alpha)=0
. By definition
F^{m}K_{0}(\text{Var}_{\mathbb{C}})[\mathbb{L}^{-1}]
is generated by classes of the form
\{X\}/\mathbb{L}^{i}
with
\text{dim}X-i\leq-m
. So,
\alpha\in F^{m}K_{0}(\text{Var}_{\mathbb{C}})[\mathbb{L}^{-1}]
satisfies
\deg H_{\text{vir},\mathbb{Z}}(\alpha)\leq 2\text{dim}X-2i\leq-m
for all
m
and thus
H_{\text{vir},\mathbb{Z}}(\alpha)=0
.
∎

We may now turn to the main theorem of this section.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

By
[
7
]
(see also, e.g., Theorem 4.3 in Chapter 6 of
[
8
]
for expository notes), we know that we have equality of the classes
\{X\}=\{X^{\prime}\}
in
\widehat{K_{0}(\text{Var}_{\mathbb{C}})[\mathbb{L}^{-1}]}
. Thus,
\{X\}=\{X^{\prime}\}
in
\overline{K_{0}(\text{Var}_{\mathbb{C}})[\mathbb{L}^{-1}]}
and so Lemma
3.8
implies
H_{\text{vir},\mathbb{Z}}(X)=H_{\text{vir},\mathbb{Z}}(X^{\prime})
.
∎

Corollary 3.9
.

Let
X
be a projective algebraic variety of dimension
n
with
at worst Gorenstein canonical singularities. If
X
admits a crepant resolution of singularities
Y\to X
, the integral cohomology groups of
Y
are independent of the choice of crepant resolution.

Equilibrium Reward for Liquidity Providers in Automated Market Makers
Alif Aqsha, Philippe Bergault, Leandro Sánchez-Betancourt

Abstract
We find the equilibrium contract that an automated market maker (AMM) offers to their strategic liquidity providers (LPs) in order to maximize the order flow that gets processed by the venue. Our model is formulated as a leader-follower stochastic game, where the venue is the leader and a representative LP is the follower. We derive approximate closed-form equilibrium solutions to the stochastic game and analyze the reward structure. Our findings suggest that under the equilibrium contract, LPs have incentives to add liquidity to the pool only when higher liquidity on average attracts more noise trading. The equilibrium contract depends on the external price, the pool reference price, and the pool reserves. Our framework offers insights into AMM design for maximizing order flow while ensuring LP profitability. Keywords: automated market makers; design; fees; leader-follower games; Stackelberg equilibrium; optimal contract; noise trading; arbitrageurs; principal agent.

Introduction

Automated market makers (AMMs) are one of the latest developments in financial technology. An AMM is a venue with predefined trading rules where liquidity takers (LTs) and liquidity providers (LPs) trade. These venues became popular after Uniswap V2 was released in May 2020. As of 2025, the fourth version of the protocol has already been announced; see
Adams et al., (2021)
;
Adams et al., (2023)
.

The academic literature on AMMs is still young but already quite rich. The first papers studying the mechanisms of AMMs are in
Chiu and Koeppl, (2019)
;
Angeris and Chitra, (2020)
;
Angeris et al., (2021)
;
Capponi and Jia, (2021)
;
Lipton and Treccani, (2021)
, the arbitrage between AMMs and external centralized venues is studied in
Cartea et al., 2023a ()
;
Cartea et al., 2023b ()
, and price formation in AMMs is studied in
Capponi et al., (2024)
. It is now well-known that, in the absence of fees, an agent providing liquidity in a standard constant function market (CFM) —where prices are only determined as a function of the reserves— are exposed to a concave payoff that is inferior to that of holding coins outside of the
pool, a phenomenon called impermanent loss (IL). In fact, empirical studies such as
Loesch et al., (2021)
show that currently, even in the presence of fees, LPs are on average incurring a loss. Recently,
Fukasawa et al., (2023)
study the hedging of this impermanent loss and
Milionis et al., (2022)
introduce the notion of loss-versus-rebalancing —see also
Milionis et al., (2023)
. Lastly,
Cartea et al., 2024a ()
consider a more general notion of predictable loss and derive optimal liquidity provision strategies.

In order to tackle the IL issue, another active area of research is that of AMM design.
Bergault et al., 2024a ()
proposes an alternative mechanism in which the pricing function uses external information about the current market exchange rates, allowing the AMM to update its bid and offer prices not only after a trade but also after a price oracle has fired an update.

In this paper we find the optimal contract that a CFM should offer to their strategic LPs in order to attract the maximum amount of order flow to the CFM. We formulate the design as the solution to a leader-follower stochastic game in which the venue is the leader and the representative LP is the follower.

In their paper, the regulator is the leader and the LPs are the followers.
see also
Baldacci et al., (2021)
for a multi-player version of the model, and
Baldacci and Bergault, (2024)
for a different approach to make-take fees. In our paper, the entity designing the contract is the CFM. We find approximate closed-form solutions to the stochastic game and obtain insights into the reward structure that attracts, in equilibrium, the highest possible order flow to the venue.

To the best of our knowledge, this is the first article to study the design of AMMs as a leader-follower stochastic game. In our framework, LPs aim to maximize exponential utility of wealth and the venue wishes to maximize trading activity. As customary in leader-follower games, for a given reward structure, we compute the optimal liquidity providing strategy from the representative LP (follower), and using this, we compute the optimal reward structure to be provided by the venue (leader). The new design comes in the form of a “contract” that encodes the reward structure obtained in the second optimization.
We find that if higher liquidity (in the pool) does not attract noise trading, then LPs do not have incentives to add liquidity to the pool.
On the other hand, if higher liquidity attracts noise trading, then, the optimal contract incentivises LPs to add liquidity to the pool in order to benefit from the noise order flow. In such a scenario, the transaction costs in the external market scale the amount that the representative LP adds to the pool.
In equilibrium, the optimal contract depends on the external price, the pool’s reference price, and the reserves in the pool. Our equilibrium solutions provide insights into the design of these emerging venues.

2.1  Probabilistic framework

Let
T>0
be a trading horizon (e.g., one day),
\Omega_{c}
the set of continuous functions from
{\mathfrak{T}}=[0,T]
into
\mathbb{R}
,
\Omega_{d}
the set of piecewise constant càdlàg functions from
{\mathfrak{T}}
into
\mathbb{N}
, and
\Omega=\big(\Omega_{c}\big)^{2}\times\big(\Omega_{d}\big)^{2}
with the corresponding Borel algebra
\mathcal{F}
. The observable state is the canonical process
(\chi_{t})_{t\in{\mathfrak{T}}}=\big(W_{t},B_{t},\hat{N}^{+}_{t},\hat{N}^{-}_{t}\big)_{t\in{\mathfrak{T}}}
of the measurable space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F})
, with

W_{t}(\omega):=w(t),\;B_{t}(\omega):=b(t),\;\hat{N}^{+}_{t}(\omega):=\hat{n}^{+}(t),\;\hat{N}^{-}_{t}(\omega):=\hat{n}^{-}(t),\;\text{for all }t\in{\mathfrak{T}},

where
\omega:=(w,b,\hat{n}^{+},\hat{n}^{-})\in\Omega.

We introduce a probability measure
\mathbb{P}
such that
W
and
B
are Brownian motions and
\hat{N}^{-}
,
\hat{N}^{+}
are Poisson processes with intensity
a_{0}>0
. In this probability measure,
W
,
B
,
\hat{N}^{-}
and
\hat{N}^{+}
are independent.
5
5

5

The independence assumption between
W
and
B
can be relaxed.

We study trading in a pair of assets
X
and
Y
(e.g., USDC and ETH) that takes place in a constant function market (CFM). In addition to the CFM, there is an external limit order book (LOB) venue where trading in
X
and
Y
occurs. We let
S=(S_{t})_{t\in{\mathfrak{T}}}
be the external midprice of asset
Y
in terms of asset
X
. Within the CFM, we let
X_{t}
and
Y_{t}
be the quantities of assets
X
and
Y
in the pool at time
t\in{\mathfrak{T}}
. The CFM has trading function
f(x,y)=x\,y
; this is the most popular choice of
f
and is known as the constant product market (CPM). We let the function
\varphi_{c_{t}}(y)
be the level function of
f
such that
\varphi_{c_{t}}(Y_{t})=X_{t}
with

c_{t}=f(X_{t\shortminus},Y_{t\shortminus})=X_{t\shortminus}\,Y_{t\shortminus}\,.

More precisely, we have that

\displaystyle\varphi_{c}(y)

\displaystyle=\frac{c}{y}\,,\qquad(\varphi_{c})^{\prime}(y)=-\frac{c}{y^{2}}\,.

(2.1)

The external midprice satisfies

\mathrm{d}S_{t}=\sigma\,\mathrm{d}W_{t}\,,\qquad S_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\,,

(2.2)

where
\sigma>0
is the volatility parameter. We think of the external price as that of a venue where price formation takes place (e.g., Binance, Kraken).

In the CFM, liquidity takers arrive according to the counting processes
N^{-}
(LT buys) and
N^{+}
(LT sells) that model the number of trades of size
\xi>0
through time. We denote
Z=(Z_{t})_{t\in{\mathfrak{T}}}
as the marginal price of
Y
in terms of
X
in the venue. We follow the characterization of CFMs in
Cartea et al., 2024b ()
that describes the mechanics of the reserves
(X_{t})_{t\in{\mathfrak{T}}}
,
(Y_{t})_{t\in{\mathfrak{T}}}
, and the instantaneous rate
(Z_{t})_{t\in{\mathfrak{T}}}
, according to the arrival of orders
N^{-}
and
N^{+}
, and extend it to include stochastic pool depths (given by
c_{t}
) due to the activity of LPs. In our model we write

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}X_{t}

\displaystyle=\underbrace{Z_{t}\,\eta\,\mathrm{d}B_{t}}_{\text{activity from LPs}}\underbrace{-\left[\varphi_{c_{t}}(Y_{t\shortminus})-\varphi_{c_{t}}(Y_{t\shortminus}+\xi)\right]\,\mathrm{d}N^{+}_{t}+\left[\varphi_{c_{t}}(Y_{t\shortminus}-\xi)-\varphi_{c_{t}}(Y_{t\shortminus})\right]\,\mathrm{d}N^{-}_{t}}_{\text{activity from LTs}}\,,

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}Y_{t}

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\eta\,\mathrm{d}B_{t}}_{\text{activity from LPs}}\underbrace{+\xi\,\mathrm{d}N^{+}_{t}-\xi\,\mathrm{d}N^{-}_{t}}_{\text{activity from LTs}}\,,

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}Z_{t}

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\left[-(\varphi_{c_{t}})^{\prime}(Y_{t\shortminus}+\xi)+(\varphi_{c_{t}})^{\prime}(Y_{t\shortminus})\right]\,\mathrm{d}N^{+}_{t}+\left[-(\varphi_{c_{t}})^{\prime}(Y_{t\shortminus}-\xi)+(\varphi_{c_{t}})^{\prime}(Y_{t\shortminus})\right]\,\mathrm{d}N^{-}_{t}}_{\text{activity from LTs}}\,,

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}N^{-}_{t}

\displaystyle=\mathds{1}_{Y_{t\shortminus}>\xi}\,\mathrm{d}\hat{N}^{-}_{t}\,,

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}N^{+}_{t}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{d}\hat{N}^{+}_{t}\,,

with
\eta>0
representing the volatility in liquidity provision,
N^{+}_{0}=N^{-}_{0}=0
,
X_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
,
Y_{0}\in(\xi,\infty)
, and
Z_{0}=X_{0}/Y_{0}
.
The constraint
\mathds{1}_{\{Y_{t\shortminus}>\xi\}}
is to guarantee that there is a strong solution to the above dynamics; in practical applications this indicator is always equal to one. The interpretation of the above equations is as follows, given a liquidity taking buy order (resp. sell order), the quantity
Y_{t}
goes down (resp. up) by
\xi
, the quantity in
X_{t}
increases (resp. decreases) to its new level
\varphi_{c_{t}}(Y_{t\shortminus}-\xi)
(resp.
\varphi_{c_{t}}(Y_{t\shortminus}+\xi)
), and the instantaneous rate moves to its new level
\varphi_{c_{t}}^{\prime}(Y_{t\shortminus}-\xi)
(resp.
\varphi_{c_{t}}^{\prime}(Y_{t\shortminus}+\xi)
).

Finally, we endow the space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F})
with the
\mathbb{P}-
augmented canonical filtration
\mathbb{F}:=\left(\mathcal{F}_{t}\right)_{t\in{\mathfrak{T}}}
generated by
(\chi_{t})_{t\in{\mathfrak{T}}}
.

2.2  The weak formulation

We introduce
(L_{t})_{t\in{\mathfrak{T}}}
as the Doléans-Dade exponential of

\int_{0}^{t}\,(\lambda^{-}_{u}-a_{0})\,\mathrm{d}\tilde{N}^{-}_{u}+\int_{0}^{t}\,(\lambda^{+}_{u}-a_{0})\,\mathrm{d}\tilde{N}^{+}_{u}\,,

where

\displaystyle\lambda^{\pm}_{t}=\bar{\lambda}^{\pm}(Z_{t\shortminus},Y_{t\shortminus},S_{t})\,,\qquad\mathrm{d}\tilde{N}^{\pm}_{t}=\mathrm{d}\hat{N}^{\pm}_{t}-\lambda^{\pm}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t\,,

(2.3)

with

\displaystyle\bar{\lambda}^{\pm}(Z,Y,S)

\displaystyle=\max\left\{a_{0},a_{1}+a_{2}\,Y\pm a_{3}\,(Z-S)\right\}\,,\qquad a_{0}>0,\,\,\,a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}\geq 0\,.

(2.4)

The linear model for the intensities (inspired by
Stoikov and Sağlam, (2009)
) tries to capture three stylized facts: (i)
a_{1}
is the baseline intensity of order arrivals of liquidity motivated trades, (ii)
a_{2}
models the relationship between arrivals and depth (locally, all else being the same, higher depth may imply more arrivals),
6
6

6

The rationale for this is that more depth implies less slippage for any liquidity taking trade executed in the pool. Thus, all else being the same, it is more desirable to trade in a pool with more depth. For mathematical tractability, we employ the quantity
a_{2}\,Y_{t}
as a proxy for the depth. This is a reasonable approximation over a short time horizon, during which large moves in
Y_{t}
are mainly driven by LPs activity.
and (iii)
a_{3}
captures the impact of trading by the arbitrageurs that align the quotes between the external LOB venue and the CFM. The effect of
S-Z
in the intensities is asymmetric, this is because when
S>Z
(resp.
S<Z
), all else being equal, we expect a higher (resp. lower) buying intensity and a lower (resp. higher) selling intensity given that the instantaneous rate in the pool is underpriced (resp. overpriced). The minimum intensity
a_{0}>0
is a technical condition to keep the intensity away from zero. This is non-restrictive and in practice we expect both intensities to be bounded away from zero. In Subsection
4.3
we carry out experiments with market data to support these claims.

Let
\nu_{\infty}>0
be a bound for the speed at which the representative LP adds or subtracts liquidity from the pool.
The set of admissible strategies of the representative LP is

{\mathcal{A}}:=\left\{\nu=(\nu_{t})_{t\in{\mathfrak{T}}}:\,\nu\text{ is $\mathbb{F}$-progressively measurable and }|\nu_{t}|\leq\nu_{\infty}\;\mathbb{P}\otimes\mathrm{d}t\text{ a.e.}\right\}\,,

(2.5)

and for each
\nu\in{\mathcal{A}}
we introduce the Radon-Nikodym derivative
(K^{\nu}_{t})_{t}
given by

K^{\nu}_{t}:=\exp\Bigg(\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\nu_{s}}{\eta}\mathrm{d}B_{s}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\left(\frac{\nu_{s}}{\eta}\right)^{2}\mathrm{d}s\Bigg).

Given that
\nu\in{\mathcal{A}}
, the process
(K^{\nu}_{t})_{t\in{\mathfrak{T}}}
is a martingale (by Novikov’s condition using that
\nu
is bounded) and we define the probability measure
\mathbb{P}^{\nu}
given by

\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}^{\nu}}{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}}:=K^{\nu}_{T}\,L_{T}\,.

Given that all the probability measures
(\mathbb{P}^{\nu})_{\nu\in{\mathcal{A}}}
are equivalent, we use the notation
a.s.
for almost surely without ambiguity.

In summary, under
\mathbb{P}^{\nu},
the processes
\hat{N}^{+}
and
\hat{N}^{-}
have respective intensities
\left(\lambda^{+}_{t}\right)_{t\in{\mathfrak{T}}}
and
\left(\lambda^{-}_{t}\right)_{t\in{\mathfrak{T}}}
,
the process

B^{\nu}_{t}=B_{t}-\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\nu_{s}}{\eta}\mathrm{d}s

is a standard Brownian motion, and
W
is a standard Brownian motion with
\langle W,B^{\nu}\rangle=0
. Thus, under
\mathbb{P}^{\nu}
we write

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}S_{t}

\displaystyle=\sigma\,\mathrm{d}W_{t}\,,

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}X_{t}

\displaystyle=\underbrace{Z_{t}\,\nu_{t}\mathrm{d}t}_{\text{representative LP}}+\underbrace{Z_{t}\,\eta\,\mathrm{d}B^{\nu}_{t}}_{\text{other LPs}}\quad\underbrace{-\left[\varphi_{c_{t}}(Y_{t\shortminus})-\varphi_{c_{t}}(Y_{t\shortminus}+\xi)\right]\,\mathrm{d}N^{+}_{t}+\left[\varphi_{c_{t}}(Y_{t\shortminus}-\xi)-\varphi_{c_{t}}(Y_{t\shortminus})\right]\,\mathrm{d}N^{-}_{t}}_{\text{activity from LTs}}\,,

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}Y_{t}

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\nu_{t}\mathrm{d}t}_{\text{representative LP}}+\underbrace{\eta\,\mathrm{d}B^{\nu}_{t}}_{\text{other LPs}}\quad\underbrace{+\xi\,\mathrm{d}N^{+}_{t}-\xi\,\mathrm{d}N^{-}_{t}}_{\text{activity from LTs}}\,,

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}Z_{t}

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\left[-(\varphi_{c_{t}})^{\prime}(Y_{t\shortminus}+\xi)+(\varphi_{c_{t}})^{\prime}(Y_{t\shortminus})\right]\,\mathrm{d}N^{+}_{t}+\left[-(\varphi_{c_{t}})^{\prime}(Y_{t\shortminus}-\xi)+(\varphi_{c_{t}})^{\prime}(Y_{t\shortminus})\right]\,\mathrm{d}N^{-}_{t}}_{\text{activity from LTs}}\,.

2.3  The problem of the follower: the representative LP

The representative LP finances her stake in the pool by trading in the external market.
That is, to add (resp. subtract)
\nu_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t
units of
Y
in (resp. from) the pool at time
t
, the representative LP purchases (resp. sells)
\nu_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t
units in the external market at price
(S_{t}+\mathfrak{a}\,\nu_{t})\,\nu_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t
where
\mathfrak{a}
is the temporary price impact in the external venue.
Thus, for
0\leq s\leq t\leq T
, the performance criterion of the representative LP is defined as

\begin{split}Q_{s,t}^{\nu}:&=\underbrace{\left(Z_{t}\,Y_{t}+Y_{t}\,S_{t}\right)-\left(Z_{s}\,Y_{s}+Y_{s}\,S_{s}\right)}_{\left(X_{t}+Y_{t}\,S_{t}\right)-\left(X_{s}+Y_{s}\,S_{s}\right)}-\int_{s}^{t}Z_{u\shortminus}\,\nu_{u\shortminus}\,\mathrm{d}u-\int_{s}^{t}(S_{u}+\mathfrak{a}\,\nu_{u\shortminus})\,\nu_{u\shortminus}\,\mathrm{d}u\;.\end{split}

(2.6)

The first two terms account for the mark-to-market of the stake in the pool, with the quantity
Y_{t}
valued at the external price
S_{t}
. The term
-\int_{s}^{t}Z_{u\shortminus}\,\nu_{u\shortminus}\,\mathrm{d}u
is the financing of the units added or subtracted in asset
X
.
7
7

7

The calculations below can be carried out if one also includes a running penalty of the form
-\int_{s}^{t}p(u,Z_{u},Y_{u},S_{u})\mathrm{d}u
. For simplicity of formulae, we do not include the running penalty.
Lastly, the term
-\int_{s}^{t}(S_{u}+\mathfrak{a}\,\nu_{u\shortminus})\,\nu_{u\shortminus}\,\mathrm{d}u
is the financing of the units added or subtracted in asset
Y
—the representative LP pays the costs of trading (e.g., walking the LOB and spread) through the quadratic term
\mathfrak{a}\,\nu^{2}_{t}
.

The above can be written as

\begin{split}Q_{s,t}^{\nu}&=-\int_{s}^{t}\mathfrak{a}\,\nu_{u\shortminus}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}u+\eta\,\int_{s}^{t}(S_{u\shortminus}+Z_{u\shortminus})\,\mathrm{d}B_{u}^{\nu}\\
&\qquad\qquad+\sigma\,\int_{s}^{t}Y_{u\shortminus}\,\mathrm{d}W_{u}+\int_{s}^{t}\Delta^{-}_{u}\,\mathrm{d}N^{-}_{u}+\int_{s}^{t}\Delta^{+}_{u}\,\mathrm{d}N^{+}_{u}\,,\end{split}

(2.7)

for
s<t
where

\Delta^{\pm}_{u}:=\pm\,\xi\,\left(S_{u}-\frac{Z_{u\shortminus}\,Y_{u\shortminus}}{Y_{u\shortminus}\pm\,\xi}\right).

(2.8)

Thus, the control problem that the representative LP wishes to solve is given by

V_{t}(\mathfrak{R})=\sup_{\nu\in{\mathcal{A}}}\mathbb{E}_{t}^{\nu}\left[-\exp\left\{-\gamma\,(\mathfrak{R}+Q^{\nu}_{t,T})\right\}\right]\,,

(2.9)

where
\mathfrak{R}\in L^{2}(\Omega,{\mathcal{F}}_{T},\mathbb{P}^{\nu})
is the reward offered by the venue to the representative LP and
\gamma>0
is the risk aversion parameter of the representative LP.
8
8

8

\mathbb{E}_{t}^{\nu}
represents the conditional expectation with respect to the sigma-algebra
{\mathcal{F}}_{t}
under the probability measure
\mathbb{P}^{\nu}
.
To ensure that the above expectation is well-defined, we need the following technical condition.

Condition 1
.

There exists
\gamma^{\prime}>\gamma
such that

\displaystyle\underset{\nu\in\mathcal{A}}{\sup}\;\mathbb{E}^{\nu}\left[\exp\{-\gamma^{\prime}\mathfrak{R}\}\right]<+\infty\,.

(2.10)

In Section
3
, for each reward
\mathfrak{R}
, we prove that there exists a unique optimal response
\nu^{*}(\mathfrak{R})
.

2.4  The problem of the leader: the venue

The venue provides a contract
\mathfrak{R}
to the representative LP and wishes to attract as much order flow as possible to the venue (i.e.,
N^{-}_{T}+N^{+}_{T}
). Thus, the venue wishes to maximize the following performance criterion

\mathbb{E}^{\nu^{*}(\mathfrak{R})}\left[-\exp\left\{-\zeta\,\left(\mathfrak{r}\,(N^{-}_{T}+N^{+}_{T})-\mathfrak{R}\right)\right\}\right]\,,

(2.11)

where
\zeta>0
is the venue’s risk aversion parameter and
\mathfrak{r}>0
is a constant transaction fee paid by LTs to the venue. For the above expectation to be well-defined, we need the following technical condition.

Condition 2
.

There exists
\zeta^{\prime}>\zeta
such that

\displaystyle\underset{\nu\in\mathcal{A}}{\sup}\;\mathbb{E}^{\nu}\left[\exp\{\zeta^{\prime}\,\mathfrak{R}\}\right]<+\infty\,.

(2.12)

The set of admissible contracts is given by

{\mathcal{A}}^{R}=\left\{\mathfrak{R},\;\mathcal{F}_{T}-\text{measurable, such that }V_{0}(\mathfrak{R})\geq R\text{ and Conditions }\ref{assumptionRgamma}\text{ and }\ref{assumptionReta}\text{ are satisfied}\right\}\,,

(2.13)

where
R<0
is the reservation level of the LP, i.e. the smallest acceptable utility level, in the sense that the LP refuses the contract if it doesn’t allow them to reach this utility level; see Remark
1
below for a more detailed discussion.

Next, we characterize the solution to the leader-follower game between the venue and the representative LP. Section
3
studies the optimal response of the follower
\nu^{*}(\mathfrak{R})
and Section
4
finds the optimal contract
\mathfrak{R}^{*}
.

3
Solving the problem of the LP

For any
(\nu,A)\in[-\nu_{\infty},\nu_{\infty}]\times\mathbb{R}^{4}
, with
A=\left(A^{W},A^{B},A^{+},A^{-}\right)
, we define

h(\nu,A)=-\mathfrak{a}\,\nu^{2}+\frac{1}{\eta}A^{B}\nu\,.

It is easy to see that for any
A\in\mathbb{R}^{4}
, the maximizer of
h(\cdot,A)
is reached at

\bar{\nu}(A)=\left(\frac{A^{B}}{2\,\mathfrak{a}\,\eta}\vee-\nu_{\infty}\right)\wedge\nu_{\infty}\,.

We then define

H(A)=\underset{|\nu|\leq\nu_{\infty}}{\sup}h(\nu,A)=h(\bar{\nu}(A),A)\,.

For any constant
P_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
and predictable process
(A_{t})_{t\in{\mathfrak{T}}}=\left(A^{W}_{t},A^{B}_{t},A^{+}_{t},A^{-}_{t}\right)_{t\in{\mathfrak{T}}}
satisfying that

\sup_{\nu\in{\mathcal{A}}}\mathbb{E}^{\nu}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left(A^{B}_{t}\right)^{2}+\left(A^{W}_{t}\right)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\right]<\infty\,,

and

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}

\displaystyle\Bigg|\frac{\gamma}{2}\,\left[\left(A^{W}_{s}+\sigma\,Y_{s\shortminus}\right)^{2}+\left(A^{B}_{s}+\eta\,(S_{s\shortminus}+Z_{s\shortminus})\right)^{2}\right]

(3.1)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad-\sum_{i\in\{+,-\}}\frac{\lambda^{i}_{s}\,(1-e^{-\gamma(A^{i}_{s}+\Delta^{i}_{s})})}{\gamma}-H(A_{s})\Bigg|\,\mathrm{d}s<\infty\qquad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}\,,

(3.2)

we introduce the process

\displaystyle P_{t}^{P_{0},A}

\displaystyle=P_{0}+\sum_{i\in\{+,-\}}\int_{0}^{t}A^{i}_{s}\,\mathrm{d}\hat{N}^{i}_{s}+\int_{0}^{t}A^{W}_{s}\,\mathrm{d}W_{s}+\int_{0}^{t}A^{B}_{s}\,\mathrm{d}B_{s}

(3.3)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\Bigg\{\frac{\gamma}{2}\,\left[\left(A^{W}_{s}+\sigma\,Y_{s\shortminus}\right)^{2}+\left(A^{B}_{s}+\eta\,(S_{s\shortminus}+Z_{s\shortminus})\right)^{2}\right]-\sum_{i\in\{+,-\}}\frac{\lambda^{i}_{s}\,(1-e^{-\gamma(A^{i}_{s}+\Delta^{i}_{s})})}{\gamma}-H(A_{s})\Bigg\}\,\mathrm{d}s\,,

(3.4)

and we denote by
\Lambda
the set of all processes
(A_{t})_{t\in{\mathfrak{T}}}
such that Conditions
1
and
2
are satisfied with reward
\mathfrak{R}=P_{T}^{0,A}
and

\sup_{\nu\in{\mathcal{A}}}\sup_{t\in{\mathfrak{T}}}\mathbb{E}^{\nu}\left[\exp\left\{-\gamma^{\prime}\,P_{t}^{0,A}\right\}\right]<\infty\quad\text{ for some }\gamma^{\prime}>\gamma\,.

(3.5)

We denote by
\mathcal{P}
the set

\mathcal{P}=\left\{P_{T}^{P_{0},A}\text{ such that }P_{0}\in\mathbb{R},\;A\in\Lambda,\;\text{and }V_{0}\left(P_{T}^{P_{0},A}\right)\geq R\right\}.

It is clear that
\mathcal{P}\subset\mathcal{A}^{R}.
The next two theorems are proved in
A
.

Theorem 1

For any

Remark 1
.

Theorem
2
shows that
P_{0}
is the certainty equivalent of an LP that faces contract
P^{P_{0},A}_{T}
and trades optimally; it also shows that any admissible contract must satisfy
-\exp(-\gamma P_{0})\geq R
. In papers such as
El Euch et al., (2021)
, the reservation level
R
is chosen to be the utility level of the agent in the absence of a contract. This is reasonable in their framework since an agent behaving in an optimal way is already profitable even without a contract. This is not the case in our paper: it is well-known that, in the absence of fees, an agent providing liquidity for a CPM is exposed to a concave and negative payoff. Therefore, if the venue wants to attract LPs, it should redistribute some of the fees. More precisely, since the LP can simply choose not to participate in the pool, if the venue wants to retain them it should at least offer a contract that will allow the LP to have a non-negative certainty equivalent, i.e.,
P_{0}
should be non-negative. It is therefore reasonable to consider
-1\leq R<0
. Competition between venues in order to attract LPs should tend to increase this value. In the numerical examples below, we choose
R
so that the representative LP and the venue share the profits collected from fees.

6
Conclusion

We characterized the Stackelberg equilibrium of a venue and the representative LP. Trading took place in an AMM and while the representative LP aims to maximize profitability, the venue wishes to collect as much fees and order flow as possible. We find that if higher liquidity does not attract noise trading, even when the venue employs the optimal contract, the representative LP does not have incentives to add liquidity to the pool.
On the other hand, if higher liquidity in the AMM attracts noise trading, then the representative LP adds as much liquidity as the transaction costs in the external venue allow.
Using data from 1 January 2022 to 30 April 2022, we find a positive correlation of 11% between the daily average depth in
Y
for the ETH-USDC pool and the daily order flow, furthermore, the slope of the linear regression is positive but there is no evidence to reject the hypothesis that the slope is equal to zero (the
p
-value is 0.232).
Our work provides insights into the optimal design of these venues going forward and the testable conditions for the well functioning of these venues.

\nu^{k}_{s}=\nu_{s}\,\mathds{1}_{t\leq s<\tau}+\tilde{\nu}^{k}\,\mathds{1}_{\tau\leq s\leq T}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{K_{T}^{\nu^{k}}}{K_{\tau}^{\nu^{k}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{K_{T}^{\tilde{\nu}^{k}}}{K_{\tau}^{\tilde{\nu}^{k}}}\quad\text{and}\quad Q_{\tau,T}^{\nu^{k}}=Q_{\tau,T}^{\tilde{\nu}^{k}}\,.

(A.8)

Thus, it follows that

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}^{\nu^{k}}_{\tau}\left[-\exp\left\{-\gamma\,\left(\mathfrak{R}+Q_{\tau,T}^{\nu^{k}}\right)\right\}\right]&=\mathbb{E}_{\tau}\left[-\frac{K_{T}^{\nu^{k}}}{K_{\tau}^{\nu^{k}}}\,\exp\left\{-\gamma\,\left(\mathfrak{R}+Q_{\tau,T}^{\nu^{k}}\right)\right\}\right]\\
&=\mathbb{E}_{\tau}\left[-\frac{K_{T}^{\tilde{\nu}^{k}}}{K_{\tau}^{\tilde{\nu}^{k}}}\,\exp\left\{-\gamma\,\left(\mathfrak{R}+Q_{\tau,T}^{\tilde{\nu}^{k}}\right)\right\}\right]\\
&=J(\tau,\tilde{\nu}^{k})\,.\end{split}

(A.9)

We then obtain

\begin{split}J(t,\nu^{k})&=\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[-\frac{K_{T}^{\nu^{k}}}{K_{\tau}^{\nu^{k}}}\,\frac{K_{\tau}^{\nu^{k}}}{K_{t}^{\nu^{k}}}\,\exp\left\{-\gamma\,Q_{t,\tau}^{\nu^{k}}\right\}\,\exp\left\{-\gamma\,\left(\mathfrak{R}+Q_{\tau,T}^{\nu^{k}}\right)\right\}\right]\\
&=\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\frac{K_{\tau}^{\nu^{k}}}{K_{t}^{\nu^{k}}}\,\exp\left\{-\gamma\,Q_{t,\tau}^{\nu^{k}}\right\}\,\mathbb{E}_{\tau}\left[-\frac{K_{T}^{\nu^{k}}}{K_{\tau}^{\nu^{k}}}\,\exp\left\{-\gamma\,\left(\mathfrak{R}+Q_{\tau,T}^{\nu^{k}}\right)\right\}\right]\right]\\
&=\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\frac{K_{\tau}^{\nu}}{K_{t}^{\nu}}\,\exp\left\{-\gamma\,Q_{t,\tau}^{\nu}\right\}\,\mathbb{E}^{\nu^{k}}_{\tau}\left[-\exp\left\{-\gamma\,\left(\mathfrak{R}+Q_{\tau,T}^{{\nu}^{k}}\right)\right\}\right]\right]\\
&=\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\frac{K_{\tau}^{\nu}}{K_{t}^{\nu}}\,\exp\left\{-\gamma\,Q_{t,\tau}^{\nu}\right\}\,J(\tau,\tilde{\nu}^{k})\right]\,.\end{split}

(A.10)

Note that
{K_{T}^{\nu}}/{K_{\tau}^{\nu}}
has mean of unity and is independent of
\mathcal{F}_{\tau}
. Thus,

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\frac{K_{\tau}^{\nu}}{K_{t}^{\nu}}\,\exp\left\{-\gamma\,Q_{t,\tau}^{\nu}\right\}\,J(\tau,\tilde{\nu}^{k})\right]&=\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\frac{K_{\tau}^{\nu}}{K_{t}^{\nu}}\,\exp\left\{-\gamma\,Q_{t,\tau}^{\nu}\right\}\,J(\tau,\tilde{\nu}^{k})\right]\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\frac{K_{T}^{\nu}}{K_{\tau}^{\nu}}\right]\\
&=\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\frac{K_{T}^{\nu}}{K_{t}^{\nu}}\,\exp\left\{-\gamma\,Q_{t,\tau}^{\nu}\right\}\,J(\tau,\tilde{\nu}^{k})\right]\\
&=\mathbb{E}^{\nu}_{t}\left[\exp\left\{-\gamma\,Q_{t,\tau}^{\nu}\right\}\,J(\tau,\tilde{\nu}^{k})\right]\end{split}

(A.11)

Due to Condition
1
, the expression
\mathbb{E}^{\nu}_{t}\left[\exp\left\{-\gamma\,Q_{t,\tau}^{\nu}\right\}\,J(\tau,\tilde{\nu}^{1})\right]
is finite and we can use monotone convergence theorem to obtain

\begin{split}\lim_{k\uparrow\infty}J(t,\nu^{k})&=\mathbb{E}^{\nu}_{t}\left[\exp\left\{-\gamma\,Q_{t,\tau}^{\nu}\right\}\,\lim_{k\to\infty}J(\tau,\tilde{\nu}^{k})\right]\\
&=\mathbb{E}^{\nu}_{t}\left[\exp\left\{-\gamma\,Q_{t,\tau}^{\nu}\right\}\,V_{\tau}\right]\,.\end{split}

(A.12)

Thus, we obtain

\begin{split}V_{t}&\geq\lim_{k\uparrow\infty}J(t,\nu^{k})\\
&=\mathbb{E}^{\nu}_{t}\left[\exp\left\{-\gamma\,Q_{t,\tau}^{\nu}\right\}\,V_{\tau}\right]\,,\end{split}

(A.13)

which concludes the proof.
∎

Lemma 2
.

Let
\Gamma\in\mathcal{F}
such that
\mathbb{P}(\Gamma)>0
. Then there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that

\mathbb{P}^{\nu}(\Gamma)>\varepsilon\qquad\forall\nu\in\mathcal{A}.

Proof.

We will prove this by contradiction. Assume there exists
\left(\nu^{k}\right)_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
a sequence in
{\mathcal{A}}
such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}^{\nu^{n}}(\Gamma)=0
. Thus,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}^{\nu^{n}}\left[\mathds{1}_{\Gamma}\right]

(A.14)

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\big|K_{T}^{\nu^{n}}\mathds{1}_{\Gamma}\big|\right]\,.

(A.15)

Then we can take a subsequence
(n_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq(n)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
\lim_{k\to\infty}K_{T}^{\nu^{n_{k}}}\mathds{1}_{\Gamma}=0

\mathbb{P}
-almost surely. Because
\Gamma
has
\mathbb{P}
-positive probability, then
\mathbb{P}(\lim_{k\to\infty}K_{T}^{\nu^{n_{k}}}=0)>0
, or equivalently,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\frac{\nu_{t}^{n_{k}}}{\eta}\,\mathrm{d}B_{t}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\frac{(\nu_{t}^{n_{k}})^{2}}{\eta^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t=-\infty\right)>0\,,

(A.16)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\mathbb{P}\left(\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\frac{\nu_{t}^{n_{k}}}{\eta}\,\mathrm{d}B_{t}=-\infty\right)>0\,.

(A.17)

However, by Ito isometry,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{T}\frac{\nu_{u}^{n_{k}}}{\eta}\,\mathrm{d}B_{u}\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\frac{(\nu_{u}^{n_{k}})^{2}}{\eta^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}u\right]

(A.18)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{T\,(\nu_{\infty})^{2}}{\eta^{2}}\,,

(A.19)

because
\nu^{n_{k}}
is bounded by
\nu_{\infty}

\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}\otimes\mathrm{d}t\text{ a.e.}
given that
\nu_{t}^{n_{k}}\in{\mathcal{A}}
.
Thus, the collection of random variables
\left\{\int_{0}^{T}\frac{\nu_{u}^{n_{k}}}{\eta}\,\mathrm{d}B_{u}\right\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded in
L^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{F}_{T},\mathbb{P})
. From Equation (
A.17
), we have

\begin{split}\mathbb{P}\left(\liminf_{k\to\infty}\left|\int_{0}^{T}\frac{\nu_{t}^{n_{k}}}{\eta}\,\mathrm{d}B_{t}\right|^{2}=\infty\right)>0\\
\Rightarrow\mathbb{E}\left[\liminf_{k\to\infty}\left|\int_{0}^{T}\frac{\nu_{t}^{n_{k}}}{\eta}\,\mathrm{d}B_{t}\right|^{2}\right]=\infty\,.\end{split}

(A.20)

However, by Fatou’s lemma,

\begin{split}\infty&=\mathbb{E}\left[\liminf_{k\to\infty}\left|\int_{0}^{T}\frac{\nu_{t}^{n_{k}}}{\eta}\,\mathrm{d}B_{t}\right|^{2}\right]\\
&\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\int_{0}^{T}\frac{\nu_{t}^{n_{k}}}{\eta}\,\mathrm{d}B_{t}\right|^{2}\right]\\
&\leq\frac{T\,(\nu_{\infty})^{2}}{\eta^{2}}\,,\end{split}

(A.21)

which is a contradiction.
∎

Note that

\displaystyle\phantom{{}={}}-\sum_{i\in\{+,-\}}\int_{u}^{T}A^{i}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}\hat{N}^{i}_{t}-\int_{u}^{T}A^{W}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}W_{t}-\int_{u}^{T}A^{B}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}B_{t}+\int_{u}^{T}\mathrm{d}A^{d}_{t}\,,

(A.81)

with

, i.e.

This shows in particular that
\Delta a_{t}=\frac{-\Delta A^{\nu,d}_{t}}{U^{\nu}_{t-}}\geq 0
is independent from
\nu\in\mathcal{A}

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}P_{t}

\displaystyle=-\Bigg\{\bar{h}\left(\nu_{t},A_{t},Z_{t},Y_{t},S_{t}\right)-\frac{1}{\gamma\,U^{\nu}_{t\shortminus}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}A^{\nu,c}_{t}}{\mathrm{d}t}\Bigg\}\,\mathrm{d}t+\sum_{i\in\{+,-\}}A^{i}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}\hat{N}^{i}_{t}+A^{W}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}W_{t}+A^{B}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}B_{t}-\mathrm{d}A^{d}_{t}\,

with

We introduce the process
I

I_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}\Bigg\{\bar{h}\left(\nu_{s},A_{s},Z_{s},Y_{s},S_{s}\right)\mathrm{d}s-\frac{1}{\gamma\,U^{\nu}_{s\shortminus}}\,\mathrm{d}A^{\nu,c}_{s}\Bigg\}.

By the DPP, we have

\displaystyle 0

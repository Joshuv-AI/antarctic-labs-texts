A Class of Accelerated Fixed-Point-Based Methods with Delayed Inexact Oracles and Its Applications
Nghia Nguyen-Trung, Quoc Tran-Dinh

Abstract
In this paper, we develop a novel accelerated fixed-point-based framework using delayed inexact oracles to approximate a fixed point of a nonexpansive operator (or equivalently, a root of a co-coercive operator), a central problem in scientific computing. Our approach leverages both Nesterov’s acceleration technique and the Krasnosel’skiǐ-Mann (KM) iteration, while accounting for delayed inexact oracles, a key mechanism in asynchronous algorithms. We also introduce a unified approximate error condition for delayed inexact oracles, which can cover various practical scenarios. Under mild conditions and appropriate parameter updates, we establish both 𝒪 ⁡ ( 1 / k 2 ) \mathcal{O}\big(1/k^{2}\big) non-asymptotic and o ⁡ ( 1 / k 2 ) o\big(1/k^{2}\big) asymptotic convergence rates in expectation for the squared norm of residual. Our rate significantly improves the 𝒪 ⁡ ( 1 / k ) \mathcal{O}\big(1/k\big) rates in classical KM-type methods, including its asynchronous variants. We also establish o ⁡ ( 1 / k 2 ) o\big(1/k^{2}\big) almost sure convergence rates and the almost sure convergence of iterates to a solution of the problem. Within our framework, we instantiate three settings for the underlying operator: (i) a deterministic universal delayed oracle; (ii) a stochastic delayed oracle; and (iii) a finite-sum structure with asynchronous updates. For each case, we instantiate our framework to obtain a concrete algorithmic variant for which our convergence results still apply, and whose iteration complexity depends linearly on the maximum delay. Finally, we verify our algorithms and theoretical results through two numerical examples on both matrix game and shallow neural network training problems.

Introduction

\mathrm{(a)}
Problem statement.

Let
F:{\mathbb{R}^{p}}\to{\mathbb{R}^{p}}
be a nonexpansive operator (i.e.,
\|Fx-Fy\|\leq\|x-y\|
for all
x,y\in{\mathbb{R}^{p}}
).
We consider the following fixed-point problem:

\textrm{Find $x^{\star}\in{\mathbb{R}^{p}}$ such that:}~x^{\star}=Fx^{\star}.

(FP)

This problem is fundamental in scientific computing and related fields such as optimization, machine learning, engineering, probability and statistics, and economics, see, e.g.,
agarwal2001fixed
;
Bonsall1962
;
Combettes2011a
.

It is well-known (e.g.,
Bauschke2011
) that if we define
G:=\mathbb{I}-F
, where
\mathbb{I}
is the identity mapping, then (
FP
) can be reformulated equivalently into the following
root-finding problem
of
G
:

\textrm{Find $x^{\star}\in{\mathbb{R}^{p}}$ such that:}\ Gx^{\star}=0,

(CE)

where
G
is
\frac{1}{2}
-co-coercive, i.e.,
\langle Gx-Gy,x-y\rangle\geq\frac{1}{2}\|Gx-Gy\|^{2}
for all
x,y\in{\mathbb{R}^{p}}
.
Therefore, approximating a fixed-point
x^{\star}
of (
FP
) is equivalent to finding an approximate root of (
CE
).
For the sake of our presentation, we will treat the latter problem, i.e., (
CE
).
While we consider (
CE
) in a finite-dimensional space
\mathbb{R}^{p}
, we believe that our results in this paper can be extended to a Hilbert space with a little effort.

Throughout this paper, we require the following assumption on (
CE
).

Assumption 1

The equation (
CE
) satisfies the following assumptions:

\mathrm{(i)}

It has a solution, i.e.,
\mathrm{zer}(\Phi):=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}^{p}}\mid Gx^{\star}=0\}\neq\emptyset
.

\mathrm{(ii)}

There exist
\beta,\bar{\beta}\in[0,+\infty)
and a nonnegative function
\mathcal{D}:{\mathbb{R}^{p}}\times{\mathbb{R}^{p}}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
satisfying
\mathcal{D}(x,x)=0
for all
x\in{\mathbb{R}^{p}}
such that

\langle Gx-Gy,x-y\rangle\geq\beta\|Gx-Gy\|^{2}+\bar{\beta}\mathcal{D}(x,y),\quad\forall x,y\in{\mathbb{R}^{p}}.

(1)

While Assumption
1
(i) makes sure that (
CE
) is solvable and well-defined, Assumption
1
(ii) generalizes the
\beta
-co-coercivity of
G
by adding the last term
\bar{\beta}\mathcal{D}(x,y)
, which will be specified later in our applications.
Specifically, in the finite-sum setting of (
CE
) with
Gx=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}G_{i}x
, we will choose
\mathcal{D}(x,y):=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|G_{i}x-G_{i}y\|^{2}
.
Note that, we allow either
\beta=0
or
\bar{\beta}=0
in (
1
).
Under this flexibility, it is clear that if
G
is
\beta
-co-coercive on
{\mathbb{R}^{p}}
, then it automatically satisfies (
1
) with either
\bar{\beta}=0
or
\mathcal{D}(\cdot,\cdot)=0
.
Since the co-coercivity of
G
in (
CE
) is equivalent to the nonexpansiveness of
F
in (
FP
), Assumption
1
(ii) is reasonable.

\mathrm{(b)}
Motivation.

In parallel and distributed optimization methods, enforcing
perfectly up-to-date oracle
evaluations such as [approximate] gradient or operator evaluations is often prohibitively expensive, and in many cases, infeasible
Andrews2000
;
Bertsekas1989b
;
Schnitger2006
.
Both exact and approximate synchronous oracle evaluations require global coordination across workers, memory systems, and data partitions, which causes significant idle time and communication overhead in heterogeneous computing environments.
These limitations motivate the development of algorithms that tolerate delayed or asynchronous inexact oracle information rather than requiring workers to remain perfectly aligned to up-to-date oracle evaluations.

Hitherto, most advanced asynchronous methods have been designed for minimization problems; see, for example,
aviv2021asynchronous
;
Bertsekas1989b
;
cohen2021asynchronous
;
feyzmahdavian2023asynchronous
;
hannah2019a2bcd
;
Leblond2018a
;
leblond2017asaga
;
liu2015asynchronous
;
ManiaPanPapailiopoulosEtAl2015
;
Recht2011
;
tseng1991rate
;
Wei20131
.
Such algorithms do not directly accommodate modern applications in robust and distributionally robust optimization, adversarial training, or reinforcement learning, which give rise to minimax or fixed-point formulations.
Only a limited number of works address more general models such as (
CE
) and their special cases, including minimax problems; see
bertsekas1983distributed
;
chow2017cyclic
;
combettes2018asynchronous
;
davis2016smart
;
hannah2018unbounded
;
peng2016arock
.
These approaches largely extend classical asynchronous optimization schemes to fixed-point problems or monotone inclusions.
However, these methods are often designed for a specific type of asynchronous mechanism, such as asynchronous coordinate updates
bertsekas1983distributed
;
combettes2018asynchronous
;
hannah2018unbounded
;
peng2016arock
or incremental aggregation
davis2016smart
.
In addition, these methods are non-accelerated, often require restrictive assumptions (e.g., quasi-strong monotonicity), achieve sub-optimal convergence rates (at most
\mathcal{O}\big(1/k\big)
or
o\big(1/k\big)
rates), or have inefficient complexity bounds (e.g., quadratic dependence on the maximum delay), see, e.g.,
davis2016smart
;
hannah2018unbounded
;
peng2016arock
.
For instance,
bertsekas1983distributed
;
combettes2018asynchronous
studied asynchronous fixed-point-type iterations for (
FP
) or (
CE
), but they are non-accelerated and only asymptotic convergence was established without explicit iteration or oracle complexity bounds.
Recently,
peng2016arock
proposed an asynchronous block-coordinate fixed-point iteration for (
CE
) that attains asymptotic convergence properties under Assumption
1
, or a linear convergence but requires a quasi-strong monotonicity of
G
, and its complexity could depend quadratically on the maximum delay.
This dependence was later improved to linear in subsequent works, e.g.,
feyzmahdavian2023asynchronous
.
Recently,
davis2016smart
developed asynchronous aggregated schemes for solving (
CE
) extended to the finite-sum setting and obtained similar convergence and complexity results using analogous block-coordinate updates and assumptions as in
peng2016arock
.

The aim of this paper is to develop a
unified accelerated fixed-point framework
for (
CE
) capable of handling delayed and inexact oracle evaluations and adaptable to a broad range of asynchronous update mechanisms without quasi-strong monotonicity.
Our approach integrates Nesterov-type acceleration (different from previous works for (
CE
)), Krasnosel’skiǐ-Mann iterations, and delayed inexact oracle information in a single scheme, while not using the “quasi-strong monotonicity” of
G
as in
davis2016smart
;
peng2016arock
.
As a result, our framework yields several improvements and new convergence properties compared to existing methods, even in special cases such as smooth convex optimization and convex-concave minimax problems.

\mathrm{(c)}
Our contributions.

To this end, our contributions can be summarized as follows.

\mathrm{(i)}

We apply Nesterov’s acceleration techniques to the Krasnosel’skiǐ-Mann fixed-point iteration to derive a new
A
ccelerated
F
ixed-
P
oint-type framework (
AFP
) with delayed inexact oracles (deterministic or stochastic) to approximate solutions of (
CE
).

\mathrm{(ii)}

We introduce a generic
error approximation criterion
to handle delayed inexact oracles of
G
.
Under this criterion, we can prove a
\mathcal{O}\big(1/k^{2}\big)
convergence rate of our
AFP
framework on the residual
\mathbb{E}[\|Gy^{k}\|^{2}]
, where
k
is the iteration counter and
y^{k}
is the last iterate.
We also establish a faster
o\left(1/k^{2}\right)
convergence rate of
\|Gy^{k}\|^{2}
both in expectation and almost surely, and the almost sure convergence of iterates
\{y^{k}\}
to a solution of (
CE
).

\mathrm{(iii)}

We apply our framework to derive an
AFP
variant with delay updates and achieve an iteration-complexity of
\mathcal{O}\big(\frac{\tau}{\beta\epsilon}\big)
to obtain an
\epsilon
-solution of (
CE
), where
\tau
is the maximum delay and
\beta
is the co-coercivity constant in Assumption
1
.

\mathrm{(iv)}

We also apply our method to design an asynchronous stochastic
AFP
variant for approximating a solution of (
CE
) when
G
is equipped with an unbiased stochastic oracle
\mathbf{G}
such that
Gx=\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{G}(x,\xi)]
.
We prove that our method can achieve an
\epsilon
-solution after
\mathcal{O}\big(\frac{\sigma}{\epsilon\sqrt{\tau}}+\frac{\tau}{\beta\epsilon}\big)
oracle calls, where
\tau
is the maximum delay and
\sigma^{2}
is the variance of
\mathbf{G}
.

\mathrm{(v)}

As byproducts of our results, we also obtain two new variants of our
AFP
framework.
The first one is an incremental aggregated
AFP
scheme, while the second variant is a shuffling aggregated
AFP
algorithm.
Both variants have rigorous theoretical convergence rate guarantees as consequences of our unified convergence analysis.

Algorithmic and analysis highlights.

First, in the absence of inexactness, our
AFP
scheme reduces to an exact deterministic version for solving (
CE
).
Due to a different parameter update, this method is not identical to, but closely related to, Nesterov’s accelerated scheme in
tran2022connection
and the fast KM method in
bot2022bfast
.
However, the convergence analyses in
bot2022bfast
;
tran2022connection
are not well suited for developing inexact variants of (
CE
).
The difficulty arises from bounding inner-product terms such as
\langle e^{k},y^{k+1}-y^{k}\rangle
and
\langle e^{k},y^{k}-x^{\star}\rangle
, where
y^{k}
is the iterate,
x^{\star}\in\mathrm{zer}(G)
, and
e^{k}=\widetilde{G}^{k}-Gy^{k}
is the error between the inexact oracle
\widetilde{G}^{k}
and the true value
Gy^{k}
.
Processing these terms is non-trivial in convergence analysis.
This limitation motivates us to develop a new scheme whose analysis avoids such terms entirely.

Second, although our method may initially appear similar to Nesterov’s accelerated scheme in
lan2011primal
;
Nesterov2005c
for smooth convex minimization, the underlying analysis differs substantially.
In convex optimization, convergence analysis typically uses the objective residual
f(x^{k})-f(x^{\star})
as part of the underlying Lyapunov function
bansal2017potential
.
For general operator problems such as (
CE
), no such objective exists, and the analysis instead relies on constructing an appropriate Lyapunov function – a nontrivial task in this context.

Third, our method incorporates acceleration together with delayed inexact oracles, setting it apart from existing inexact KM-type schemes such as
bravo2019rates
;
cortild2025krasnoselskii
;
liang2016convergence
.
It is also different from recent non-accelerated paradigms in
combettes2018asynchronous
;
davis2016smart
;
peng2016arock
, where the analysis often uses a standard metric such as
\|x^{k}-x^{\star}\|^{2}
to construct a Lyapunov function.
They often require stronger assumptions for non-asymptotic rates, such as quasi-strong monotonicity
davis2016smart
;
peng2016arock
.

Fourth, in the stochastic setting, our approach also differs from recent variance-reduced fast KM methods
tran2024accelerated
;
TranDinh2025a
due to the new algorithmic structure, convergence metrics, and the generic delayed error approximation condition introduced in Definition
1
.

Finally, our framework is sufficiently general to accommodate additional variants beyond the three settings studied in Section
4
.
It can also be applied to solve more general classes of problems than (
CE
), such as monotone or co-hypomonotone inclusions, by applying appropriate reformulation techniques as in
TranDinh2025a
(see Remark
4
).

\mathrm{(d)}
Related work and comparison.

Since (
FP
) or its equivalent form (
CE
) is classical and fundamental, its theory, numerical methods, and applications have been widely developed for many decades, including the following monographs
Bauschke2011
;
Rockafellar1997
;
GoebelKirk1990
.
Here, we only discuss and compare the most relevant work to our setting and methods studied in this paper.

\mathrm{(i)}
Fixed-point methods and its accelerated variants.

Both the Banach-Picard (BP) iteration and the Krasnosel’skiǐ-Mann (KM) scheme are classical approaches for approximating a solution of (
FP
) or, equivalently, (
CE
).
The BP iteration requires the operator
F
to be contractive, whereas the KM scheme only assumes that
F
is nonexpansive, and therefore applies to a broader class of algorithms — particularly those arising in monotone operator theory such as proximal-point and splitting schemes; see, for example,
Bauschke2011
;
Facchinei2003
;
minty1962monotone
;
ryu2016primer
.
While the BP method achieves a linear convergence rate due to the contractivity of
F
, the KM scheme guarantees at most
\mathcal{O}\big(1/k\big)
or
o\big(1/k\big)
rates in terms of the squared residual norm under mere nonexpansiveness, which is far from the
\mathcal{O}\big(1/k^{2}\big)
-optimal rate
partkryu2022
.

Boosting the convergence rates of classical fixed-point methods has become an attractive research topic in recent years.
Both Nesterov’s acceleration and Halpern’s fixed-point iteration have been extensively studied for solving (
FP
) and (
CE
); see, for example,
attouch2020convergence
;
attouch2019convergence
;
Beck2009
;
bot2023fast
;
chambolle2015convergence
;
diakonikolas2020halpern
;
kim2021accelerated
;
lieder2021convergence
;
mainge2021accelerated
;
partkryu2022
;
qi2021convergence
;
sabach2017first
;
tran2022connection
;
tran2021halpern
;
tran2024revisiting2
;
yoon2021accelerated
.
Depending on the assumptions and parameter update rules, these methods achieve convergence rates as fast as
\mathcal{O}\big(1/k^{2}\big)
or even
o\big(1/k^{2}\big)
.
These rates are substantially improved over the non-accelerated counterparts by a factor of
1/k
and often match the optimal rate up to a constant factor in many settings
lieder2021convergence
;
park2022exact
.

Hitherto, however, most accelerated algorithms of this type are deterministic and rely on exact oracles.
Only a limited number of works address inexact oracles, with most focusing on stochastic oracles or randomized coordinate updates; see, e.g.,
cai2023variance
;
cai2022stochastic
;
tran2022accelerated
;
tran2024accelerated
;
TranDinh2025a
.
As observed in convex optimization
Devolder2010
, accelerated methods tend to be more sensitive to oracle errors than their non-accelerated counterparts.
In addition, most convergence analysis of inexact methods relies on inexact oracles defined through the objective function.
Unlike convex optimization, here the absence of an objective function in (
FP
) makes the development of Nesterov-type inexact accelerated fixed-point algorithms particularly challenging.

\mathrm{(ii)}
Optimization algorithms with delayed or asynchronous updates.

Asynchronous algorithms have been extensively developed in the literature, primarily within the domain of convex optimization and numerical linear algebra.
The seminal monograph
Bertsekas1989b
arguably laid the theoretical foundation for parallel and distributed computation, covering both synchronous and asynchronous algorithmic mechanisms.
The recent surge of interest in solving large-scale problems on parallel and distributed computing platforms has revived and greatly expanded research on asynchronous methods.
A major milestone in this renewed interest is
Recht2011
, introducing HOGWILD!, which established near-optimal convergence guarantees for lock-free stochastic gradient descent.
Subsequent theoretical analyses have typically demonstrated convergence degradation proportional either to the maximum delay
agarwal2011distrubuted
;
arjevani2020Dtight
;
feyzmahdavian2016asynchronous
;
lian2015asynchronous
;
ManiaPanPapailiopoulosEtAl2015
;
stich2020error
or to the average delay
aviv2021asynchronous
;
cohen2021asynchronous
.
The work of
feyzmahdavian2023asynchronous
refined this characterization by replacing the maximum delay with a threshold parameter and established a non-accelerated

\mathcal{O}\big(1/k\big)
best-iterate convergence rate in expected optimality gap.
The ASAGA algorithm
leblond2017asaga
achieved linear convergence for finite-sum, smooth, and strongly convex problems, with explicit step-size and constant dependencies on the bounded overlap (delay) parameter, matching the serial SAGA performance up to delay-dependent factors and allowing near-linear speedups.
Similarly, A2BCD
hannah2019a2bcd
, a Nesterov’s accelerated block-coordinate method, attained linear convergence with optimal complexity under moderate delay conditions.
Nevertheless, extending this approach to accelerated fixed-point methods remains largely open.

\mathrm{(iii)}
Fixed-point methods with delayed or asynchronous updates.

Unlike optimization, asynchronous algorithms for fixed-point problems such as (
FP
) and generalized equations such as (
CE
) remain comparatively underdeveloped.
While most early methods were presented in
Bertsekas1989b
, convergence rates and complexity analysis similar to those in optimization remain largely elusive.
Remarkably, the work
peng2016arock
systematically studied a class of asynchronous coordinate schemes for (
CE
) based on the KM iteration, called ARock, allowing inconsistent reads and finite maximal delays, and establishing almost sure convergence (and linear rates under an additional quasi-strong monotonicity assumption) with delay-dependent step sizes.
Subsequent variants of ARock can be found, e.g., in
hannah2018unbounded
;
heaton2019asynchronous
.
The work
davis2016smart
extended ARock’s idea and developed asynchronously incremental aggregated schemes for a finite-sum setting of (
CE
) and obtained similar convergence results as
peng2016arock
under analogous assumptions.
For monotone inclusions,
combettes2018asynchronous
proposed an asynchronous block-iterative primal-dual splitting method with weak and strong asymptotic convergence guarantees.
These methods, however, are non-accelerated, highlighting the need for a general, non-asymptotic, and accelerated framework capable of accommodating asynchrony in both nonexpansive mappings and monotone equations or inclusions.

2
Background and Mathematical Tools

In this section, we recall the necessary concepts and mathematical tools used in the sequel.
We also briefly review Nesterov’s accelerated gradient method for minimizing a smooth and convex function as a starting point of our development in this paper.

2.1
Basic concepts

\mathrm{(a)}
Basic notations.

We work with a finite-dimensional space
\mathbb{R}^{p}
equipped with an inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
, inducing the norm
\|\cdot\|
.
For a mapping
G
(single-valued or multivalued mapping), we denote by
\mathrm{dom}(G):=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}^{p}}:Gx\neq\emptyset\}
the domain of
G
and by
\mathrm{gra}(G):=\{(x,u)\in{\mathbb{R}^{p}}\times{\mathbb{R}^{p}}:u\in Gx\}
the graph of
G
.
For given functions
g(t)
and
h(t)
, we say that
g(t)=\mathcal{O}\big(h(t)\big)
if there exists
M>0
and
t_{0}\geq 0
such that
g(t)\leq Mh(t)
for
t\geq t_{0}
, and we say that
g(t)=o(h(t))
if
\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{g(t)}{h(t)}=0
.

\mathrm{(b)}
Filtration and expectations.

We define by
\mathcal{F}_{k}
the
\sigma
-algebra generated by all the randomness induced by
x^{0},x^{1},\dots,x^{k}
, up to the
k
-th iteration.
This
\sigma
-algebra generates a filtration
\{\mathcal{F}_{k}\}
.
We denote by
\mathbb{E}_{k}[\cdot]=\mathbb{E}\big[\cdot\mid\mathcal{F}_{k}\big]
the conditional expectation w.r.t.
\mathcal{F}_{k}
, by
\mathbb{E}_{\xi}[\cdot]=\mathbb{E}_{\xi\sim\mathbb{P}}[\cdot]
the expectation w.r.t.
\xi
, and by
\mathbb{E}\big[\cdot\big]
the total expectation.

\mathrm{(c)}
Co-coercivity, Lipschitz continuity, and nonexpansiveness.

For a single-valued operator
G:{\mathbb{R}^{p}}\to{\mathbb{R}^{p}}
, we say that
G
is
\beta
-co-coercive if there exists
\beta\geq 0
such that
\langle Gx-Gy,x-y\rangle\geq\beta\|Gx-Gy\|^{2}
for all
x,y\in{\mathbb{R}^{p}}
;
we say that
G
is
L
-Lipschitz continuous if there exists
L\geq 0
such that
\|Gx-Gy\|\leq L\|x-y\|
for all
x,y\in\mathrm{dom}(G)
.
If
L=1
, then
G
is nonexpansive.
If
Gx=\nabla{f}(x)
, the gradient of a convex function
f
, then the
L
-Lipschitz continuity of
\nabla{f}
is equivalent to the
\frac{1}{L}
-co-coercivity of
\nabla{f}
due to the well-known Baillon–Haddad theorem
Bauschke2011
.

\mathrm{(d)}
Monotonicity and co-hypomonotonicity.

For a possibly multivalued operator
G:{\mathbb{R}^{p}}\rightrightarrows{\mathbb{R}^{p}}
, we say that
G
is
\rho
-co-hypomonotone if
\langle w_{x}-w_{y},x-y\rangle\geq-\rho\|w_{x}-w_{y}\|^{2}
for all
(x,w_{x}),(y,w_{y})\in\mathrm{gra}(G)
and
\rho\geq 0
.
If
\rho=0
, then we say that
G
is monotone.
A co-hypomonotone operator is not necessarily monotone, see, e.g.,
TranDinh2025a
for concrete examples.

2.2
Nesterov’s accelerated gradient method and its variants

We recall here one common variant of Nesterov’s accelerated gradient method
Nesterov2004
for minimizing a convex and
L
-smooth function
f
from
lan2011primal
.
This method starts from an arbitrary
x^{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
, chooses
y^{0}:=x^{0}
, and at iteration
k\geq 0
, updates:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}y^{k}&:=&\frac{t_{k}-s}{t_{k}}x^{k}+\frac{s}{t_{k}}z^{k},\\
x^{k+1}&:=&y^{k}-\eta\nabla{f}(y^{k}),\\
z^{k+1}&:=&z^{k}+t_{k}(x^{k+1}-y^{k}),\end{array}\right.

(NAG)

where
s>0
and
\eta\in(0,\frac{1}{L}]
are given, and
t_{k}=\mathcal{O}\big(k\big)
can be adaptively updated.

In the unconstrained setting, (
NAG
) can be expressed equivalently to several well-known variants of Nesterov’s accelerated gradient scheme, including those presented in
Nesterov1983
;
Nesterov2004
.
With an appropriate choice of the parameters
s
and
t_{k}
, the method attains both
\mathcal{O}\big(1/k^{2}\big)
and
o\big(1/k^{2}\big)
convergence rates for the objective residual
f(x^{k})-f(x^{\star})
.
Moreover, its iterates
\{x^{k}\}
also converge to a minimizer
x^{\star}
of
f
; see, for instance,
attouch2016rate
.
Over the past two decades, research on this topic has evolved extensively, with significant contributions summarized in
beck2017first
;
lan2020first
;
Nesterov2004
.
Furthermore, these ideas have been successfully extended to broader settings, including monotone inclusions and variational inequalities; see, e.g.,
attouch2020convergence
;
bot2023fast
;
kim2021accelerated
;
mainge2021accelerated
;
mainge2021fast
;
tran2022connection
.

3
Accelerated Fixed-Point Framework with Delayed Inexact Oracle

In this section, we develop a
novel accelerated fixed-point iteration framework
with
delayed inexact oracle
to approximate a solution of (
FP
), or equivalently, a root of (
CE
).
Our method is inspired by Nesterov’s accelerated gradient algorithm described in (
NAG
).

3.1
The accelerated fixed-point method with delayed inexact oracle

Our method for solving (
CE
) is described as follows.

\bullet

Initialization:

Choose a constant
s>1
.
Given an initial point
y^{0}\in{\mathbb{R}^{p}}
, we set
z^{0}:=y^{0}
.

\bullet

Main loop:

At each iteration
k\geq 0
, we compute an appropriate
delayed inexact estimator

\widetilde{G}^{k}
of
Gy^{k}

(
either deterministic or stochastic
)
, and then update

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}x^{k+1}&:=&y^{k}-\eta_{k}\widetilde{G}^{k},\\

Let us briefly discuss the relations between our scheme (

\diamond

In the exact case, i.e., when

, our method is much closer in spirit to Nesterov’s variant in

A key distinction, however, lies in the choice of

In convex optimization, as shown in (

;
lan2011primal

is constant, namely
\gamma_{k}=\gamma=\mathcal{O}\big(1\big)

This difference leads to a more aggressive update of

in convex optimization compared with our approach.

\diamond

Alternatively, if
\widetilde{G}^{k}=Gy^{k}
(i.e., the exact value
Gy^{k}
is used), then our scheme reduces to a deterministic accelerated fixed-point method for solving (

bot2022bfast
;
kim2021accelerated
;
mainge2021accelerated
;
mainge2021fast
;
tran2022connection

However, as mentioned earlier, extending the convergence analyses from these works to the inexact oracle variants is difficult compared with (

and
\langle e^{k},y^{k}-x^{\star}\rangle

Controlling such terms becomes challenging unless they vanish; for example, when using unbiased estimators

satisfying

.

\diamond

For the inexact setting, we allow both deterministic and stochastic inexact oracles in our scheme (
AFP
).
The stochastic oracle is not required to be unbiased, which makes our method more flexible and broad enough to encompass a wide range of existing and new inexact oracles, particularly those involving delays.

\diamond

In convex optimization, the underlying inexact oracles for accelerated methods are often defined directly (see, e.g.,
Devolder2010
;
dvurechensky2016stochastic
) or indirectly through the objective function (see, e.g.,
Schmidt2011

Such formulations make it possible to avoid directly bounding inner-product terms such as
\langle e^{k},y^{k}-x^{\star}\rangle

However, because no objective function underlies (

Note that our framework (

In terms of per-iteration complexity, (

per iteration.
All additional operations consist solely of vector additions or scalar-vector multiplications, making their computational cost comparable to that of classical fixed-point schemes such as BP iteration or KM fixed-point schemes.

\diamond

iterations to achieve
\mathbb{E}\big[\|Gy^{k}\|^{2}\big]\leq\epsilon^{2}

or
\frac{7\Lambda\hat{\Theta}\beta}{7\Lambda\Theta+3\theta(1+\tau)}\leq\bar{\beta}\leq\frac{\hat{\Theta}}{\Theta}\beta

Combining all the above results, we need at most

\begin{array}[]{lcl}k&:=&\max\left\{\Big\lfloor\frac{2\sigma\sqrt{3\Lambda\Gamma}}{\epsilon\sqrt{\theta\tau}}\Big\rfloor,\Big\lfloor\frac{\sqrt{6}(3s+\tau-1)\|Gy^{0}\|}{\epsilon}\Big\rfloor,\Big\lfloor\frac{2\sqrt{6}s^{3/2}[7\Lambda\Theta+3\theta(1+\tau)]\|y^{0}-x^{\star}\|}{3\theta\beta\gamma^{1/2}\epsilon}\Big\rfloor\right\}\\

Remark 1

(
Convergence of
\mathbb{E}\left[\|Gx^{k}\|^{2}\right]
)

Theorem
3.1
establishes convergence on
\mathbb{E}\left[\|Gy^{k}\|^{2}\right]
.
This criterion is different from standard Nesterov’s accelerated methods in convex optimization, where convergence is certified on the sequence
\{x^{k}\}
for the gradient norm.
It is similar to the Ravine method studied in
attouch2022ravine
.
Nevertheless, by Young’s inequality and Assumption
1
, we have
\|Gx^{k}\|^{2}\leq 2\|Gx^{k}\|^{2}+2\|Gy^{k}-Gx^{k}\|^{2}\leq 2\|Gx^{k}\|^{2}+\frac{2}{\beta^{2}}\|y^{k}-x^{k}\|^{2}
.
Moreover, one can show that
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\|y^{k}-x^{k}\|^{2}\right]<+\infty
.
As a result, it is possible to derive convergence rate results for
\mathbb{E}\left[\|Gx^{k}\|^{2}\right]
analogous to those for
\mathbb{E}\left[\|Gy^{k}\|^{2}\right]
in Theorem
3.1
.
For brevity, we omit the details of this proof.

Remark 2

(
Choices of parameters
)

We note that the parameter ranges in Theorem
3.1
, such as
s\geq 1+3\gamma
originated from Lemma
2
, are chosen to make the proof tractable and are not necessarily optimized.
When implementing the algorithm, one may choose values outside these strict ranges (e.g., we can choose
s>1
) to achieve a better empirical performance, but we omit these heuristics here to avoid complicating the theoretical analysis.

Remark 3

(
Linear dependence of the iteration-complexity on
\tau
)
.
Our iteration-complexity depends
linearly
on the maximum delay
\tau
, and either
\hat{\Theta}
, or
\Theta+\tau
.
In Section
4
, where we specialize our framework for concrete settings, we establish that either
\Theta=\mathcal{O}\big(\tau\big)
or
\hat{\Theta}=\mathcal{O}\big(\tau\big)
.
Consequently, our iteration-complexity can be simplified to either

\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\sigma}{\epsilon\sqrt{\tau}}+\frac{\tau}{\beta\epsilon}\right)\quad\text{or}\quad\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\sigma}{\epsilon\sqrt{\tau}}+\frac{\tau}{\bar{\beta}\epsilon}\right).\vskip-4.30554pt

This demonstrates a
linear dependence
on the maximum delay
\tau
, which is known to be the optimal dependence in asynchronous and delayed methods for optimization
arjevani2020tight
.
Since
\beta
and
\bar{\beta}
are co-coercivity constants, they are of the order
\mathcal{O}\big(1/L\big)
, where
L
denotes the Lipschitz constant of the operator
G
(or, in particular, the smoothness constant of the objective function in optimization).
Therefore, our complexity match the linear dependence on
\tau
commonly found in optimization literature as well (e.g.,
arjevani2020tight
;
stich2020error
for SGD with delayed updates), which typically yields a complexity of
\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}\left(\frac{\sigma^{2}}{\epsilon^{2}}+\frac{L\tau}{\epsilon}\right)
for
L
-smooth functions.

Step 1: Prove

8

), we can show that almost surely,

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\lim_{k\to\infty}V_{k}\quad\text{exists},\\
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}t_{k}\|Gy^{k}\|^{2}&<&+\infty,\\
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}t_{k}(t_{k}-s)\Delta_{k}&<&+\infty,\\
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}t_{k}(t_{k}-s)\|Gy^{k+1}-Gy^{k}\|^{2}&<&+\infty,\\
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}t_{k}(t_{k}-s)\mathcal{D}(y^{k+1},y^{k})&<&+\infty.\end{array}

(33)

Since
t_{k}=k+3s+\tau>k+\tau
due to (
13
), the second line of (
33
) proves the first almost sure summability bound of (
31
).

Given the last two summability bounds, we can use Lemma
10
to show that

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}t_{k}(t_{k}-s)\sum_{l=[k-\tau_{k}+1]_{+}}^{k}\|Gy^{l}-Gy^{l-1}\|^{2}&<&+\infty,\\
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}t_{k}(t_{k}-s)\sum_{l=[k-\tau_{k}+1]_{+}}^{k}\mathcal{D}(y^{l},y^{l-1})&<&+\infty.\end{array}

(34)

Now, from (
7
), we have

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|e^{k}\|^{2}]&\leq&\mathbb{E}_{k}[\Delta_{k}]\leq(1-\kappa)\Delta_{k-1}+{{\!\!\!\!\!\!\!}\displaystyle\sum_{l=[k-\tau_{k}+1]_{+}}^{k}}{\!\!\!\!\!\!\!}\big[\Theta\|Gy^{l}-Gy^{l-1}\|^{2}+\hat{\Theta}\mathcal{D}(y^{l},y^{l-1})\big]+\frac{\delta_{k}}{t_{k}(t_{k}-s)}.\end{array}

Multiplying both sides of this inequality by
t_{k}(t_{k}-s)
, we can derive that

\begin{array}[]{lcl}t_{k}(t_{k}-s)\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|e^{k}\|^{2}]&\leq&t_{k-1}(t_{k-1}-s)\|e^{k-1}\|^{2}-t_{k-1}(t_{k-1}-s)\|e^{k-1}\|^{2}\\
&&+{~}t_{k}(t_{k}-s)\sum_{l=[k-\tau_{k}+1]_{+}}^{k}\big[\Theta\|Gy^{l}-Gy^{l-1}\|^{2}+\hat{\Theta}\mathcal{D}(y^{l},y^{l-1})\big]\\
&&+{~}(1-\kappa)t_{k}(t_{k}-s)\Delta_{k-1}+\delta_{k}.\end{array}

Using (
33
), (
34
), and
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\delta_{k}<+\infty
from the assumption, the last two lines of this inequality are summable almost surely.
Thus, applying Lemma
6
, we conclude that

\begin{array}[]{lcl}&\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}t_{k}(t_{k}-s)\|e^{k}\|^{2}<+\infty\quad\text{almost surely},\\
&\lim_{k\to\infty}t_{k}(t_{k}-s)\|e^{k}\|^{2}=0\quad\text{almost surely.}\end{array}

(35)

Next, since
\|\widetilde{G}^{k}\|^{2}\leq 2\|Gy^{k}\|^{2}+2\|e^{k}\|^{2}
by Young’s inequality, using the first line of (
33
) and (
35
), we can show that

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}t_{k}\|\widetilde{G}^{k}\|^{2}<+\infty\quad\text{almost surely}.\end{array}

(36)

For
v^{k}:=x^{k+1}-x^{k}+\eta_{k}\widetilde{G}^{k}
as in Theorem
3.1
, taking the conditional expectation
\mathbb{E}_{k}\big[\cdot\big]
of (
21
), we obtain

\begin{array}[]{lcl}t_{k-1}^{2}\mathbb{E}_{k}\big[\|v^{k}\|^{2}\big]&\leq&t_{k-2}^{2}\|v^{k-1}\|^{2}-st_{k-2}\|v^{k-1}\|^{2}+\frac{(s-\gamma)^{2}\eta^{2}}{s}t_{k-2}\|\widetilde{G}^{k-1}\|^{2}.\end{array}

Since
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}t_{k-1}\|\widetilde{G}^{k}\|^{2}<+\infty
almost surely by (
36
), applying Lemma
6
to the last inequality, and then using Lemma
5
, we can show that

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}t_{k}\|v^{k}\|^{2}<+\infty\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{k\to\infty}t_{k}^{2}\|v^{k}\|^{2}=0\quad\text{almost surely}.\end{array}

(37)

Similarly, for
w^{k}:=x^{k+1}-x^{k}+\eta_{k}e^{k}
as in Theorem
3.1
, using similar arguments but applying to (
25
), we can prove that

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}t_{k}\|w^{k}\|^{2}<+\infty\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{k\to\infty}t_{k}^{2}\|w^{k}\|^{2}=0\quad\text{almost surely}.\end{array}

(38)

Combining (
29
), (
38
), and (
35
), we can show that
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}t_{k}\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\|^{2}=0
almost surely.
Since
t_{k}=k+3s+\tau
due to (
13
), the last expression proves the second almost-sure summability bound in (
31
).

Combining (
28
), the two limits in (
37
) and (
38
), we can show that

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\lim_{k\to\infty}t_{k}^{2}\|Gy^{k}\|^{2}=0\quad\text{almost surely}.\end{array}

This is exactly the first result of (
32
).
Similarly, combining (
29
), (
38
), and (
35
), we can show that
\lim_{k\to\infty}t_{k}^{2}\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\|^{2}=0
almost surely, which is the second result of (
32
).

Step 2: Prove the existence of
\lim_{k\to\infty}\|z^{k}-x^{\star}\|^{2}
.

From (
AFP
), we have
s(z^{k}-x^{k})=t_{k-1}(x^{k+1}-x^{k}+\eta_{k}\widetilde{G}^{k})=t_{k-1}v^{k}
.
Since
\lim t_{k}^{2}\|v^{k}\|^{2}=0
almost surely, we obtain that
\lim_{k\to\infty}\|z^{k}-x^{k}\|^{2}=0
almost surely.
Moreover, from the third line of (
AFP
), we can show that
y^{k}-z^{k}=(1-\frac{s}{t_{k-1}})(x^{k}-z^{k})
, thus

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\|y^{k}-z^{k}\|^{2}&=&(1-\frac{s}{t_{k-1}})^{2}\|x^{k}-z^{k}\|^{2}\leq\|x^{k}-z^{k}\|^{2}\to 0\quad\text{as $k\to\infty$},\quad\text{almost surely}.\end{array}

Using Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, the last limit and the first line of (
32
), we can show that almost surely

\begin{array}[]{lcl}|st_{k-1}\langle Gy^{k},y^{k}-z^{k}\rangle|^{2}\leq s^{2}t_{k-1}^{2}\|Gy^{k}\|^{2}\|y^{k}-z^{k}\|^{2}\to 0\quad\text{as $k\to\infty$}.\end{array}

Hence, we get
\lim_{k\to\infty}st_{k-1}\langle Gy^{k},y^{k}-z^{k}\rangle=0
almost surely.

Now, from (
3
) and (
9
), we can write

\begin{array}[]{lcl}V_{k}&=&\frac{a_{k}}{2}\|Gy^{k}\|^{2}+st_{k-1}\langle Gy^{k},y^{k}-z^{k}\rangle+\frac{s^{2}(s-1)}{2\gamma\eta_{k-1}}\|z^{k}-x^{\star}\|^{2}\\
&&+{~}\frac{(2\beta-\eta)t_{k-1}(t_{k-1}-s)}{2}\|Gy^{k}-Gy^{k-1}\|^{2}+\bar{\beta}t_{k-1}(t_{k-1}-s)\mathcal{D}(y^{k},y^{k-1})\\
&&+{~}\frac{\Lambda\eta\alpha(1-\kappa)}{2[1-(1-\kappa)\alpha]}t_{k-1}(t_{k-1}-s)\Delta_{k-1}.\end{array}

(39)

Collecting all necessary almost sure limits we have proven so far, we have, almost surely,

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\lim_{k\to\infty}V_{k}\quad\text{exists},\\
\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{a_{k}}{2}\|Gy^{k}\|^{2}=\lim_{k\to\infty}t_{k}^{2}\|Gy^{k}\|^{2}=0\quad\text{(due to $a_{k}=\mathcal{O}\big(t_{k}^{2}\big)$)},\\
\lim_{k\to\infty}st_{k-1}\langle Gy^{k},y^{k}-z^{k}\rangle=0,\\
\lim_{k\to\infty}t_{k-1}(t_{k-1}-s)\|Gy^{k}-Gy^{k-1}\|^{2}=0,\\
\lim_{k\to\infty}t_{k-1}(t_{k-1}-s)\mathcal{D}(y^{k},y^{k-1})=0,\\
\lim_{k\to\infty}t_{k-1}(t_{k-1}-s)\Delta_{k-1}=0.\end{array}

Combining these limits and (
39
), and noting that
\eta_{k-1}=\mathcal{O}\big(1\big)
, we conclude that
\lim_{k\to\infty}\|z^{k}-x^{\star}\|^{2}
exists almost surely.
Moreover, since
\lim_{k\to\infty}\|z^{k}-x^{k}\|^{2}=\lim_{k\to\infty}\|y^{k}-z^{k}\|^{2}=0
almost surely, by triangle inequality, we also obtain that
\lim_{k\to\infty}\|x^{k}-x^{\star}\|^{2}
and
\lim_{k\to\infty}\|y^{k}-x^{\star}\|^{2}
exist almost surely.

Step 3: Almost sure convergence of the iterates.

Since
\lim_{k\to\infty}\|y^{k}-x^{\star}\|^{2}
exists almost surely for any solution
x^{\star}\in\mathrm{zer}(G)
,
\lim_{k\to\infty}\|Gy^{k}\|=0
almost surely by the first result of (
32
), and
G
is continuous, applying Lemma
7
, we conclude that
\{y^{k}\}
converges almost surely to a random variable
x^{\star}\in\mathrm{zer}(G)
.
Finally, since
\lim_{k\to\infty}\|y^{k}-z^{k}\|=\lim_{k\to\infty}\|z^{k}-x^{k}\|=0
almost surely, using triangle inequality, we can also prove that
\{x^{k}\}
and
\{z^{k}\}
almost surely converge to
x^{\star}\in\mathrm{zer}(G)
.

\square

Discussion.

Our almost sure convergence results in Theorem
3.2
appear to be the first for asynchronous accelerated methods, even for convex optimization.
Here, we establish summability bounds,
o\big(1/k^{2}\big)
convergence rates, and the convergence of iterates.
Moreover, our framework covers a wide range of algorithms in different settings as discussed in Section
4
.

Remark 4

[
Extension to generalized equations
]
We can apply our scheme (
AFP
) to solve the following
generalized equation
(known as inclusion):

\textrm{Find $x^{\star}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}$ such that:}~0\in Gx^{\star}+Tx^{\star},

(GE)

where

is defined as in (
CE
), while

is a possibly multivalued mapping that is

-co-hypomonotone.
We sketch the main steps of this application as follows.

\bullet

First, we equivalently reformulate (

) into (

) using either a forward-backward splitting (FBS) operator or a backward-forward splitting (BFS) operator as done in
TranDinh2025a
.

\bullet

Next, we develop a similar result as Corollary 1 in

to verify Assumption

.

\bullet

Then, we apply (

\bullet

Applications to AFP Methods with Delay and Asynchronicity

Section
3
develops our delayed inexact

.
Now, we apply our framework (

4.1
Accelerated Fixed-Point Method with Delayed Oracles

In this setting of (
CE
), we do not assume any structure on
G
and treat it as a universal operator satisfying Assumption
1
(ii) with
\bar{\beta}=0
and
\beta>0
, i.e.,
G
is
\beta
-co-coercive.
This setting is equivalent to finding a fixed-point of a nonexpansive operator
F
in (
FP
).

4.1.1
The computing mechanism

We consider a standard
server-worker system
outlined in Figure
1
with one central
server
and multiple
workers
.
The server maintains (i) the decision variables in a
V
-memory block and (ii) the most recent operator value in a
G
-memory block.
All workers have read access to the shared data and can therefore evaluate the operator (though their speeds may differ).

Figure 1:
The server-worker architecture with: shared data for solving the universal setting of (
CE
) (left) and distributed data for solving the finite-sum setting of (
CE
) (right)

\mathrm{(a)}

Protocol.

The server initializes
V
-memory with
y^{0}
and broadcasts the current iterate
y^{k}
whenever updated. Upon receiving
y^{k}
, a worker computes
Gy^{k}
using the shared data and, when finished, attempts to commit the result to
G
-memory.
A successful commit triggers the server to form the next iterates
x^{k+1},y^{k+1},z^{k+1}
and atomically write them to
V
-memory.

\mathrm{(b)}

Atomic assumption.
All reads/writes to
G
- and
V
-memory are
atomic
: a memory region being written is locked (mutual exclusion), so every reader observes either the old value or the fully written new value – never a mixture (no torn reads).
In practice, such atomicity can be enforced using some technical or systems techniques, such as a memory lock or double-/dual-memory (double buffering) with an atomic swaps approach
peng2016arock
.

\mathrm{(c)}

Staleness and estimator.

Because workers are heterogeneous, the value present in the
G
-memory at the
k
-th server update (i.e.,
k
-th iteration) may be a delayed evaluation
Gy^{k-\tau_{k}}
with a delay
\tau_{k}\geq 0
.
Hence, the estimator used by the server is

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\widetilde{G}^{k}=Gy^{k-\tau_{k}},\end{array}

(40)

which is one of the previously computed operator values in
\{Gy^{0},\cdots,Gy^{k}\}
.
This is called a
consistent asynchrony
model: the operator value is one of the historical computed values.

\mathrm{(d)}

Bounded-delay option.

To prevent excessively stale information, the server can enforce a threshold
\tau>0
by discarding any worker result with
\tau_{k}>\tau
.
This ensures a uniform bound on delays throughout the procedure, i.e.,
0\leq\tau_{k}\leq\tau
for all
k\geq 0
.

4.1.2
Convergence of
AFP
with universal delayed oracle

Now, we specify Theorems
3.1
and
3.2
to the setting in Subsection

to obtain the following corollary to guarantee convergence of (

) with delayed oracle (

).

Corollary 1

Suppose that Assumption
1
holds for (
CE
).
Let
\{(x^{k},y^{k},z^{k})\}
be generated by (
AFP
) using a delayed oracle
\widetilde{G}^{k}=Gy^{k-\tau_{k}}
as in (
40
) with uniformly bounded delays
0\leq\tau_{k}\leq\tau
for some
\tau\geq 1
, where we choose
s
and
\eta
, and update
t_{k}
,
\gamma_{k}
, and
\eta_{k}
as follows:

\hskip-8.61108pt\begin{array}[]{lcl}s\geq 1+3\gamma,\quad 0<\eta\leq\frac{3\beta}{3+(7\Lambda+3)\tau},\quad t_{k}:=k+3s+\tau,\quad\gamma_{k}:=\gamma\in[0,1],\quad\eta_{k}:=\frac{\eta t_{k}}{2(t_{k}-s)}.\end{array}\hskip-8.61108pt

Then, the following statements hold.

\mathrm{(i)}
Convergence in expectation.

For
\mathcal{R}_{0}^{2}:=\frac{\eta(3s+\tau-1)^{2}}{2}\|Gy^{0}\|^{2}+\frac{2s^{3}}{\eta\gamma}\|y^{0}-x^{\star}\|^{2}
, we get

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mathbb{E}\big[\|Gy^{k}\|^{2}\big]&\leq&\displaystyle\frac{4\mathcal{R}_{0}^{2}}{\eta(k+3s+\tau-1)^{2}}\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{k\to\infty}(k+\tau)^{2}\mathbb{E}\big[\|Gy^{k}\|^{2}\big]=0.\end{array}

\mathrm{(ii)}
Almost sure convergence.

The following results hold almost surely:

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(k+\tau)\|Gy^{k}\|^{2}<+\infty\qquad\text{and}\qquad\lim_{k\to\infty}(k+\tau)^{2}\|Gy^{k}\|^{2}=0.

Moreover, the sequences
\{x^{k}\}
,
\{y^{k}\}
, and
\{z^{k}\}
converge almost surely to a
\mathrm{zer}(G)
-valued random variable

, a solution to (

).

\mathrm{(iii)}
Iteration-complexity.

For any given tolerance
\epsilon>0
, we require at most
\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\tau}{\epsilon}+\frac{\tau}{\beta\epsilon}\right)
iterations to achieve
\mathbb{E}\big[\|Gy^{k}\|^{2}\big]\leq\epsilon^{2}
.

Proof

Since our update is consistent as in (
40
), by (
2
), we have
e^{k}:=\widetilde{G}^{k}-Gy^{k}=Gy^{k-\tau_{k}}-Gy^{k}
.
Using the convexity of

and

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\|e^{k}\|^{2}&=&\|Gy^{k-\tau_{k}}-Gy^{k}\|^{2}=\|\sum_{l=[k-\tau_{k}+1]_{+}}^{k}(Gy^{l}-Gy^{l-1})\|^{2}\\
&=&\tau_{k}^{2}\|\sum_{l=[k-\tau_{k}+1]_{+}}^{k}\frac{1}{\tau_{k}}(Gy^{l}-Gy^{l-1})\|^{2}\\
&\leq&\tau_{k}\sum_{l=[k-\tau_{k}+1]_{+}}^{k}\|Gy^{l}-Gy^{l-1}\|^{2}\\
&\leq&\tau\sum_{l=[k-\tau+1]_{+}}^{k}\|Gy^{l}-Gy^{l-1}\|^{2}.\end{array}

Using this inequality, one can easily verify that

Moreover, the statements in parts (i) and (ii) are direct consequences of Theorems

and

\square

4.2
Asynchronous Stochastic Accelerated Fixed-Point Method

In this subsection, we consider the setting when
G
in (
CE
) is inaccessible but can be queried only through an unbiased stochastic oracle
\mathbf{G}(\cdot,\xi)
with bounded variance defined on a given probability space.
More specifically, we impose the following additional assumption on
G
:

Assumption 2

G
in (
CE
) is equipped with an unbiased stochastic oracle
\mathbf{G}(\cdot,\xi)
such that

Gx=\mathbb{E}_{\xi}[\mathbf{G}(x,\xi)],\quad\forall x\in\mathrm{dom}(G).

(41)

Moreover, there exists
\sigma\geq 0
such that
\mathbb{E}_{\xi}[\|\mathbf{G}(x,\xi)-Gx\|^{2}]\leq\sigma^{2}
for all
x\in\mathrm{dom}(G)
.

This assumption is standard in stochastic approximation methods, see, e.g.,
Nemirovski2009
, and it has been extended to different forms.
Here, we simply use a simple version as in
Nemirovski2009
.

4.2.1
The computing mechanism

The scheme studied in this subsection is the same as (
AFP
), and the mechanism is very similar to the mechanism studied in the previous subsection.
However, unlike Subsection
4.1
, due to the inaccessibility to the full operator
G
, workers only return a stochastic estimator of
Gy^{k}
for any
k\geq 0
rather than the true value.
This leads to the choice
\widetilde{G}^{k}
used in this variant of (
AFP
) as an
“delayed stochastic estimator”
, i.e., a stochastic estimator of an obsolete operator value
Gy^{k-\tau_{k}}
for some
\tau_{k}\in\{0,1,\cdots,\tau\}
.

\mathrm{(a)}

Delayed stochastic estimator.

We use the same update scheme as in (
AFP
), and the execution flow mirrors the previous subsection, Subsection
4.1
.
The key difference is that workers cannot evaluate the full operator
G
exactly; instead, upon receiving an iterate
y^{k-\tau_{k}}
, they return a
stochastic estimation
of
Gy^{k-\tau_{k}}
.
Consequently, at the
k
-th iteration, (
AFP
) employs a
delayed stochastic estimator

\widetilde{G}^{k}
of
Gy^{k-\tau_{k}}
.
Compared to Subsection
4.1
, we now have two sources of approximation errors that need to be addressed:

\bullet

Stochastic approximation error:
The error between the stochastic estimator
\widetilde{G}^{k}
and
Gy^{k-\tau_{k}}
.

\bullet

Asynchronous error:
The error comes from the obsolescence between
Gy^{k-\tau_{k}}
and
Gy^{k}
.

\mathrm{(b)}

Approximation condition for
\widetilde{G}^{k}
.

Given an unbiased stochastic oracle
\mathbf{G}(\cdot,\xi)
of
G
, we assume that
\widetilde{G}^{k}
is constructed by querying the oracles
\mathbf{G}(y^{k-\tau_{k}},\xi)
of
Gy^{k-\tau_{k}}
adapted to the filtration
\{\mathcal{F}_{k}\}
and there exists a nonnegative sequence
\{\hat{\delta}_{k}\}
such that

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mathbb{E}_{\xi}[\|\widetilde{G}^{k}-Gy^{k-\tau_{k}}\|^{2}]\leq\frac{\hat{\delta}_{k}}{t_{k}(t_{k}-s)},\end{array}

(42)

where
t_{k}
and
s
are from (
AFP
).
One way to choose
\hat{\delta}_{k}
is
\hat{\delta}_{k}:=\frac{\sigma^{2}}{(k+r)^{1+\omega}}
for some
r>0
,
\omega\geq 0
, and
\sigma
given in Assumption
2
.

Example: Mini-batch estimator.

We can construct
\widetilde{G}^{k}
using adaptive mini-batches with increasing batch size.
For example, given an i.i.d. mini-batch
\mathcal{B}_{k}
of size
b_{k}
, we construct

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\widetilde{G}^{k}:=\frac{1}{b_{k}}\sum_{\xi_{k}\in\mathcal{B}_{k}}\mathbf{G}(y^{k-\tau_{k}},\xi_{k}).\end{array}

(43)

By Jensen’s inequality, it is well-known that
\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{B}_{k}}[\|\widetilde{G}^{k}-Gy^{k-\tau_{k}}\|^{2}]\leq\frac{\sigma^{2}}{b_{k}}
.
If we choose
\hat{\delta}_{k}:=\frac{\sigma^{2}}{(k+r)^{1+\omega}}
, then to guarantee (
42
), we need to impose
\frac{\sigma^{2}}{b_{k}}\leq\frac{\sigma^{2}}{(k+r)^{1+\omega}t_{k}(t_{k}-s)}
, leading to an increasing batch-size update
b_{k}\geq(k+r)^{1+\omega}t_{k}(t_{k}-s)
.
This mechanism is almost the same as the universal delayed oracle mechanism, except that the workers return a mini-batch estimator
\widetilde{G}^{k}:=\frac{1}{b_{k}}\sum_{\xi_{k}\in\mathcal{B}_{k}}\mathbf{G}(y^{k-\tau_{k}},\xi_{k})
to the server, rather than the true value
Gy^{k-\tau_{k}}
, which is not accessible in this setting.

The condition (
42
) can hold for a wide range of stochastic variance-reduced estimators, including unbiased and biased instances such as common estimators: SVRG
SVRG
, SAGA
Defazio2014
, SARAH
nguyen2017sarah
, and Hybrid-SGD
Tran-Dinh2019a
.
However, we omit their construction for brevity.
We only consider the mini-batch estimator (
43
) as a simple instance of our class.

4.2.2
Convergence of asynchronous stochastic AFP method

We specify Theorems
3.1
and

to this asynchronous stochastic AFP variant and obtain the following convergence results.

Corollary 2

holds with

Let
\{(x^{k},y^{k},z^{k})\}

.
Suppose that we choose

\eta

, and

as follows:

\hskip-8.61108pt\begin{array}[]{lcl}s\geq 1+3\gamma,\quad 0<\eta\leq\frac{3\beta}{3+(7\Lambda+3)\tau},\quad\gamma_{k}:=\gamma\in[0,1],\quad t_{k}:=k+3s+\tau,\quad\eta_{k}:=\frac{\eta t_{k}}{2(t_{k}-s)}.\end{array}\hskip-8.61108pt

Suppose further that
\mathcal{S}_{\infty}:=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\delta_{k}<+\infty
.
Then, the following statements hold.

\mathrm{(i)}
Convergence in expectation.

For
\mathcal{R}_{0}^{2}:=\frac{\eta(3s+\tau-1)^{2}}{2}\|Gy^{0}\|^{2}+\frac{2s^{3}}{\eta\gamma}\|y^{0}-x^{\star}\|^{2}
, we have

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\|Gy^{k}\|^{2}\big]&\leq&\displaystyle\frac{4(\mathcal{R}_{0}^{2}+\Lambda\eta\tau^{-1}\mathcal{S}_{\infty})}{\eta(k+5s+\tau-1)^{2}}\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{k\to\infty}(k+\tau)^{2}\mathbb{E}\big[\|Gy^{k}\|^{2}\big]=0.\end{array}

\mathrm{(ii)}
Almost sure convergence.

The following results hold almost surely:

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(k+\tau)\|Gy^{k}\|^{2}<+\infty\qquad\text{and}\qquad\lim_{k\to\infty}(k+\tau)^{2}\|Gy^{k}\|^{2}=0.

Moreover, the iterate sequences
\{x^{k}\}
,
\{y^{k}\}
, and
\{z^{k}\}
converge almost surely to a
\mathrm{zer}(G)
-valued random variable
x^{\star}
, a solution to (
CE
).

\mathrm{(iii)}
Iteration-complexity.

For a given
\epsilon>0
, we requires at most
k:=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\sigma}{\epsilon\sqrt{\tau}}+\frac{\tau}{\epsilon}+\frac{\tau}{\beta\epsilon}\right)
iterations to achieve
\mathbb{E}\big[\|Gy^{k}\|^{2}\big]\leq\epsilon^{2}
.

Proof

We decompose the error term
e^{k}:=\widetilde{G}^{k}-Gy^{k}
from (
2
) into
e^{k}=\tilde{e}^{k}+\hat{e}^{k}
, where
\tilde{e}^{k}:=\widetilde{G}^{k}-Gy^{k-\tau_{k}}
is the stochastic approximation error and
\hat{e}^{k}:=Gy^{k-\tau_{k}}-Gy^{k}
is the asynchronous error.
Then, using the convexity of
\|\cdot\|^{2}
and
0\leq\tau_{k}\leq\tau
, we can derive that

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\|\hat{e}^{k}\|^{2}&=&\|Gy^{k-\tau_{k}}-Gy^{k}\|^{2}\leq\tau\sum_{l=[k-\tau+1]_{+}}^{k}\|Gy^{l}-Gy^{l-1}\|^{2}.\end{array}

Since
e^{k}=\tilde{e}^{k}+\hat{e}^{k}
, applying Young’s inequality, (
42
), and the last relation, we have

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|e^{k}\|^{2}]&\leq&2\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|\tilde{e}^{k}\|^{2}]+2\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|\hat{e}^{k}\|^{2}]\leq\frac{2\hat{\delta}_{k}}{t_{k}(t_{k}-s)}+2\tau\sum_{l=[k-\tau+1]_{+}}^{k}\|Gy^{l}-Gy^{l-1}\|^{2}.\end{array}

This inequality shows that
\widetilde{G}^{k}
constructed by the delayed stochastic estimator (
42
) satisfies Definition
1
with
\kappa=1
,
\Theta=\tau
,
\hat{\Theta}=0
, and
\delta_{k}=2\hat{\delta}_{k}
.

Using this estimator, the stepsize
\eta
in Theorem
3.1
reduces to
0<\eta\leq\frac{3\beta}{7\Lambda\tau+3(1+\tau)}=\frac{3\beta}{3+(7\Lambda+3)\tau}
as stated in Corollary
2
.
Moreover, the statements in parts (i) and (ii) are direct consequences of Theorems
3.1
and
3.2
, respectively.
Finally, for a given tolerance
\epsilon>0
, if we choose
\hat{\delta}_{k}:=\frac{\sigma^{2}}{(k+r)^{1+\omega}}
, leading to
\delta_{k}:=\frac{2\sigma^{2}}{(k+r)^{1+\omega}}
, the number of iterations to achieve
\mathbb{E}\big[\|Gy^{k}\|^{2}\big]\leq\epsilon^{2}
in Theorem
3.1
becomes
k=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\sigma}{\epsilon\sqrt{\tau}}+\frac{\tau}{\epsilon}+\frac{\tau}{\beta\epsilon}\right)
as stated in Corollary
2
.

\square

4.3
Finite-sum setting of (
CE
): Inconsistent Asynchronous AFP method

Finally, we consider the finite-sum structure
Gx:=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}G_{i}x
of
G
in (
CE
) with
\mathcal{D}(x,y):=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|G_{i}x-G_{i}y\|^{2}
.
More specifically, we assume that the following assumption holds.

Assumption 3

The operator
G
in (
CE
) satisfies the following conditions.

\mathrm{(i)}

G
has a finite-sum structure, i.e.,
Gx=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}G_{i}x
for
n\geq 1
.

\mathrm{(ii)}

There exist
\beta,\bar{\beta}\in[0,+\infty)
such that

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\langle Gx-Gy,x-y\rangle&\geq&\beta\|Gx-Gy\|^{2}+\frac{\bar{\beta}}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|G_{i}x-G_{i}y\|^{2},\quad\forall x,y\in\mathrm{dom}(G).\end{array}

(44)

For (i), the finite-sum structure is ubiquitous in statistical learning and machine learning applications.
It is often obtained by sample average approximation.
For (ii), we can see that
G
satisfying (
44
) is
\beta
-co-coercive.
However, the reverse statement is not true in general.
Thus, this assumption is stronger than the
\beta
-co-coercivity of
G
.
If
\beta=0
and
\bar{\beta}>0
, then
G
is said to be
\bar{\beta}
-average co-coercive.
This assumption has been previously used in variance-reduced fixed-point-based algorithms, including
cai2023variance
;
tran2024accelerated
;
TranDinh2025a
, and is common in convex optimization.

4.3.1
The inconsistently asynchronous oracle

Before proceeding, let us recall the computing mechanism from Subsection
4.1
.
When
G
has a finite-sum structure,
\widetilde{G}^{k}
used in Subsection
4.1
was
consistently asynchronous
as

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\widetilde{G}^{k}&:=&Gy^{k-\tau_{k}}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}G_{i}y^{k-\tau_{k}},\end{array}

which requires all components
G_{i}y^{k-\tau_{k}}
at the same delayed iterate
y^{k-\tau_{k}}
and therefore coincides with an actual historical operator value
Gy^{l}
for some
k-\tau+1\leq l\leq k
.

In contrast to the above consistency, this subsection allows
inconsistently asynchronous updates
, i.e., each component
G_{i}
may be evaluated at a (possibly) different stale iterate within a bounded delay window.
Specifically, each component
G_{i}y^{k-\tau_{i,k}}
was evaluated at each delayed iterate
y^{k-\tau_{i,k}}
such that
\tau_{i,k}\in\{0,1,\cdots,\tau\}
.
Then, we set

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\widetilde{G}^{k}&:=&\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}G_{i}y^{k-\tau_{i,k}}.\end{array}

(45)

In this case,
\widetilde{G}^{k}
is possibly not equal to
Gy^{l}
for any single
l\in\{k-\tau+1,\cdots,k\}
, and hence, “inconsistent”.
Nevertheless, it is
atomically consistent
, i.e., every term
G_{i}y^{k-\tau_{i,k}}
is a historical value drawn from
\{G_{i}y^{k-\tau+1},\cdots,G_{i}y^{k}\}
for
i\in[n]
.
Hitherto, we have not specified any computing mechanism yielding the oracle (
45
) yet, but we will give a detailed description of two representative mechanisms later:
incremental and shuffling strategies
.

4.3.2
Convergence of the inconsistently asynchronous AFP method

For given
\beta
and
\bar{\beta}
in Assumption
3
,
\tau\geq 1
, and
\Lambda:=1+s-\gamma>0
, let us define

\bar{\eta}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{3\bar{\beta}}{7\Lambda\tau}&\text{if }0<\bar{\beta}\leq\frac{7\Lambda\tau\beta}{3(1+\tau)},\\
\frac{\beta}{1+\tau}&\text{if }\bar{\beta}\geq\frac{7\Lambda\tau\beta}{3(1+\tau)}.\end{array}\right.

(46)

Then, we have the following convergence results for this variant of (
AFP
).

Corollary 3

Suppose that Assumptions
1
(
a
)
and
3
holds for (
CE
).
Let
\{(x^{k},y^{k},z^{k})\}
be generated by (

) using the inconsistent estimator

in (

0\leq\tau_{i,k}\leq\tau

\mathrm{(i)}

For

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\|Gy^{k}\|^{2}\big]&\leq&\displaystyle\frac{4\mathcal{R}_{0}^{2}}{\eta(k+3s+\tau-1)^{2}}\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{k\to\infty}(k+\tau)^{2}\mathbb{E}\big[\|Gy^{k}\|^{2}\big]=0.\end{array}

\mathrm{(ii)}

The following results hold almost surely:

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(k+\tau)\|Gy^{k}\|^{2}<+\infty\qquad\text{and}\qquad\lim_{k\to\infty}(k+\tau)^{2}\|Gy^{k}\|^{2}=0.\end{array}

Moreover, the sequences
\{x^{k}\}
,
\{y^{k}\}
, and
\{z^{k}\}

\mathrm{zer}(G)
-valued random variable

).

\mathrm{(iii)}
Iteration-complexity.

For a given tolerance
\epsilon>0
, the total number of iterations

The source of randomness in inconsistent updates comes from which node has made an update at each iteration.
Let

Then, we can view

Let

In addition, let
\mathbb{E}_{k}[\cdot]=\mathbb{E}\big[\cdot\mid\mathcal{F}_{k}\big]

\mathcal{F}_{k}

From (
45

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|e^{k}\|^{2}]&=&\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|\widetilde{G}^{k}-Gy^{k}\|^{2}]=\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(G_{i}y^{k-\tau_{i,k}}-G_{i}y^{k})\|^{2}]\\

This expression shows that

computed by the inconsistent finite-sum update (

\kappa=1
,
\Theta=0
,

.
Moreover, the statements in Parts (i) and (ii) are direct consequences of Theorems

and

Finally, for a given tolerance

4.3.3
A representative mechanism in decentralized computing systems

While the inconsistently asynchronous updates (
45
) may cover a wide range of computing mechanisms, we specify here a representative one that is usually studied in literature.

\mathrm{(a)}

General mechanism
.

We again consider a server-worker system with one central server and
n
single workers similar to Subsection
4.1
, but now each worker has their own data and cannot be shared with other workers, and the server has
n
separate memory slots to maintain the most recent values of each component
G_{i}
in the
G
-memory.
The
i
-th worker is now responsible for only computing
G_{i}
,
i=1,\cdots,n
(if it is not the case, then we can divide
G_{i}
into groups such that each group is handled by one worker).
The server stores the most recent values of the components
G_{i}y^{k-\tau_{i,k}}
and is responsible to updating the iterates
x^{k}
,
y^{k}
, and
z^{k}
in the
V
-memory.
See Figure
1
(right) for an illustration of the system architecture.

\diamond

Initialization (
k=0
) — Synchronous step.

At the initial iteration
k=0
, the server initializes an iterate
y^{0}
in the
V
-memory and broadcasts this
y^{0}
to all nodes.

\bullet

Worker
i
(

G_{i}y^{0}
, then sends
G_{i}y^{0}
to the central server.

\bullet

G_{i}y^{0}
from the

and sets
\widetilde{G}^{0}:=Gy^{0}
.
Next, the server uses
\widetilde{G}^{0}
to update the iterates
x^{1}
,

,
y^{1}
, and then broadcasts
y^{1}
to all nodes as the initial iterate for the asynchronous phase.

\diamond

Main loop (

) — Asynchronous phase.

Let
\tau_{i,k}
be the delay of the iterate at which
G_{i}
is evaluated at the
k
-th iteration.
Let

be the set of workers that make an update at the

-th iteration.

\bullet

Worker

Whenever a worker
i\in I_{k}
finishes computing

\bullet

Central server

Upon receiving
G_{i}y^{k-\tau_{{i},k}}
from all workers

, the central server updates the estimator
\widetilde{G}^{k}
as:

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\widetilde{G}^{k}:=\widetilde{G}^{k-1}+{\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i\in I_{k}}}\Big(\underbrace{G_{i}y^{k-\tau_{{i},k}}}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{add the newly}\\
\text{updated values}\end{subarray}}-~\underbrace{G_{i}y^{(k-1)-\tau_{{i},k-1}}}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{discard values from}\\
\text{the last update}\end{subarray}}\Big).\end{array}

Simultaneously, we also replace the current value in the
G_{i}
memory slot by
G_{i}y^{k-\tau_{i,k}}
for all
i\in I_{k}
.
Then,
\widetilde{G}^{k}
is used in (

,

, and

.

to all idle workers in the system.

Note that we still assume the atomicity for all read-write processes in this mechanism, as well as the boundedness of the maximum delay, i.e.,
0\leq\tau_{i,k}\leq\tau
for all
k\geq 0
and
i\in[n]
.

\mathrm{(b)}

Two concrete examples: Incremental and Shuffling Aggregated.

Now, we specify two concrete mechanisms of the general decentralized computing system often studied in the literature:
Incremental Aggregated
and
Shuffling Aggregated
.
Both mechanisms maintain a component buffer
\hat{G}^{k}=[\hat{G}_{1}^{k},\cdots,\hat{G}_{n}^{k}]
and, at each iteration, update only
one
component before forming the averaged estimator
\widetilde{G}^{k}:=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\hat{G}_{i}^{k}
of
G
(i.e.,
|I_{k}|=1
).
These two mechanisms only differ in the

in which indices in
\{1,\cdots,n\}
are visited.

\bullet

Incremental (or cyclic) strategy.
Use a fixed order
\pi_{0}=[1,2,\cdots,n]
; at iteration

pick
i=\pi_{0}((k\bmod n)+1)
, set
\hat{G}^{k}_{i}\leftarrow G_{i}y^{k}
, and leave the remaining
\hat{G}_{j}^{k}
unchanged.

\bullet

Shuffling strategy.
Partition iterations into epochs of length
n
; at the start of each epoch (
k\bmod n=0
), draw a new permutation
\pi
of
\{1,\cdots,n\}
(deterministic or random), and during that epoch update components in the order prescribed by
\pi
.

Both schemes compute only a single
G_{i}
per iteration while leveraging historical entries in
\hat{G}^{k}
, thus saving a huge amount of computation, especially when

is large.
When applying these two mechanisms to (

1
.

5.1
Bilinear matrix game: Policeman vs. Burglar problem

The goal of this example is to test different variants of our framework (
AFP
) specified in Section
4
, including both deterministic and stochastic variants.

\mathrm{(a)}

Mathematical model.

Consider an
m\times m
urban grid comprising
m^{2}
houses, where the
j
-th house possesses wealth
w_{j}
,
j=1,\cdots,m^{2}
.
Each night, the Burglar selects a house
j
to target, while the Policeman chooses a location proximate to house
k
to station.
Upon initiation of the burglary, the Policeman is instantaneously informed of the incident’s location, and the probability of apprehension is
\mathbf{p}_{c}:=\exp\{-\theta\mathrm{dist}(j,k)\}
, where
\mathrm{dist}(j,k)
denotes the distance between house
j
and
k
.
However, neither the Burglar nor the Policeman is aware of the true wealth
w_{j}
; instead, they only have an estimate from a set of
n
observations
\hat{w}_{j}^{(i)}
for
i=1,\cdots,n
.
The Burglar’s objective is to maximize the expected reward
\mathcal{R}_{j}:=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\hat{w}^{(i)}_{j}\bigl(1-\exp\{-\theta\mathrm{dist}(j,k)\}\bigr)
,
whereas the Policeman seeks to minimize
\mathcal{R}_{j}
.

The Policeman vs. Burglar problem outlined above admits a zero-sum game formulation.
This model was investigated in
nemirovski2013mini
, and we introduce a minor modification to obtain a stochastic variant.
In line with
nemirovski2013mini
, we specify the payoff matrix
\mathbf{L}
as follows:

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mathbf{L}:=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{L}^{(i)},\quad\mathbf{L}^{(i)}_{j,k}={\hat{w}^{(i)}_{j}}\left(1-\exp\{-\theta\,\mathrm{dist}(j,k)\}\right),\quad 1\leq j,k\leq m^{2}.\end{array}

Then, we can formulate the above problem into the following classical bilinear matrix game:

\min_{v\in\Delta_{p_{1}}}\max_{w\in\Delta_{p_{2}}}\Big\{\mathcal{L}(v,w):=\langle\mathbf{L}v,w\rangle\Big\},

(49)

where
v
and
w
represent mixed strategies of the Policeman and Burglar, and
\Delta_{p_{1}}
and
\Delta_{p_{2}}
are the standard simplexes in
\mathbb{R}^{p_{1}}
and

\mathrm{(ii)}

\{X_{k}\}
almost surely converges and

almost surely.

(Proposition 4.1 of

)

\{x^{k}\}
almost surely converges to a

-valued random variable.

The following lemma is an extension of
(

from a deterministic to a stochastic setting.

Lemma 8

Let
\{V_{k}\}
,
\{U_{k}\}
, and

be three sequences of nonnegative integrable random variables on some arbitrary probability space and adapted to the filtration

\mathrm{(i)}

(51)

\mathrm{(ii)}

Let

and, for

and

Proof

(i) Let

to

,

\hskip-17.22217pt\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mathbb{E}\big[V_{K+1}\big]+\sum_{k=0}^{K}\mathbb{E}\big[U_{k}\big]&\leq&\mathbb{E}\big[V_{0}\big]+\sum_{k=0}^{K}\sum_{l=(k-\tau_{k}+1)_{+}}^{k}\mu_{k}\mathbb{E}\big[S_{l}\big]-\sum_{k=0}^{K}\nu_{k}\mathbb{E}\big[S_{k}\big]+\sum_{k=0}^{K}\omega_{k}\\

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mathbb{E}\big[V_{K+1}\big]+\sum_{k=0}^{K}\mathbb{E}\big[U_{k}\big]&\leq&\mathbb{E}\big[V_{0}\big]-\sum_{k=0}^{K}\left(\nu_{k}-\sum_{l=0}^{\tau-1}\mu_{k+l}\right)\mathbb{E}\big[S_{k}\big]+\sum_{k=0}^{K}\omega_{k}.\end{array}

B.4
Supporting lemmas for convergence analysis

This appendix establishes a supporting lemma for our convergence analysis in the main text.

Lemma 10

Given a bounded sequence
\{\tau_{k}\}
with
0\leq\tau_{k}\leq\tau
for some fixed
\tau\geq 1
,
t_{k}=t_{k-1}+1
, and a nonnegative random sequence
\{Z_{k}\}
, if we have
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}t_{k}(t_{k}-s)Z_{k}<+\infty
almost surely, then the following bound also holds almost surely

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}t_{k}(t_{k}-s)\sum_{l=[k-\tau_{k}+1]_{+}}^{k}Z_{l}<+\infty.\end{array}

(63)

Proof

Since
\tau_{k}\leq\tau
, using Fubini-Tonelli’s theorem and
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}t_{k}(t_{k}-s)Z_{k}<+\infty
almost surely, we have

\begin{array}[]{lcl}A&:=&\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}t_{k}(t_{k}-s)\sum_{l=[k-\tau_{k}+1]_{+}}^{k}Z_{l}\\
&\leq&\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}t_{k}(t_{k}-s)\sum_{l=[k-\tau+1]_{+}}^{k}Z_{l}\\
&=&\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}Z_{l}\sum_{k=l}^{l+\tau-1}t_{k}(t_{k}-s)\\
&=&\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}Z_{l}\sum_{j=0}^{\tau-1}t_{j+l}(t_{j+l}-s)\\
&\leq&\tau\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}t_{l+\tau-1}(t_{l+\tau-1}-s)Z_{l}\\
&<&+\infty\quad\text{almost surely},\end{array}

where the last inequality holds due to
\lim_{l\to\infty}\frac{t_{l+\tau-1}(t_{l+\tau-1}-s)}{t_{l}(t_{l}-s)}=1
.

\square

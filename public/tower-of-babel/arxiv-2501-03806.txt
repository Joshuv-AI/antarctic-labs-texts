Bounds on A α − A_{\alpha}- eigenvalues using graph invariants
João Domingos Gomes da Silva Junior, Carla Silva Oliveira, Liliana Manuela Gaspar Cerveira da Costa

Abstract
In 2017, Nikiforov introduced the concept of the A α − A_{\alpha}- matrix, as a linear convex combination of the adjacency matrix and the degree diagonal matrix of a graph. This matrix has attracted increasing attention in recent years, as it serves as a unifying structure that combines the adjacency matrix and the signless Laplacian matrix. In this paper, we present some bounds for the largest and smallest eigenvalue of A α − A_{\alpha}- matrix involving invariants associated to graphs.

Spectral Graph Theory (SGT) is an essential field in discrete mathematics that studies the properties of graphs, focusing on the eigenvalues and eigenvectors of the matrices associated to graphs, such as the adjacency matrix, the Laplacian matrix and the signless Laplacian matrix among others. Theorem 3.1
.

Let
G
be a connected irregular graph with
n
vertices,
m
edges, maximum degree
\Delta
, diameter

and
\alpha\in[0,1].
Then

\lambda_{1}(A_{\alpha}(G))<\Delta-(1-\alpha)\dfrac{n\Delta-2m}{n(diam(G)(n\Delta-2m)+1)}.

(4)

Proof.

Let
x
be the unique unit positive eigenvector of
A_{\alpha}(G)
associated to the eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}(A_{\alpha}(G)).
From Proposition
2.7
we have

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}(A_{\alpha}(G))

\displaystyle=x^{T}A_{\alpha}(G)x

\displaystyle=\alpha x^{T}D(G)x+(1-\alpha)x^{T}A(G)x

\displaystyle=\alpha x^{T}D(G)x+2(1-\alpha)\displaystyle\sum_{v_{i}\sim v_{j}}x_{i}x_{j}

\displaystyle=\alpha x^{T}D(G)x+(1-\alpha)\displaystyle\left[\sum_{v_{i}\sim v_{j}}(x_{i}^{2}+x_{j}^{2})-\sum_{v_{i}\sim v_{j}}(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\alpha x^{T}D(G)x+(1-\alpha)\displaystyle\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}x_{i}^{2}-\sum_{v_{i}\sim v_{j}}(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2}\right].

So,

(1-\alpha)\Delta-\lambda_{1}(A_{\alpha}(G))=-\alpha x^{T}D(G)x+(1-\alpha)\displaystyle\left[\sum_{v_{i}\sim v_{j}}(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\Delta-d_{i})x_{i}^{2}\right]

(5)

Now, we take vertices
s,t\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
such that
\displaystyle x_{s}=\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\{x_{i}\}
and
\displaystyle x_{t}=\min_{1\leq i\leq n}\{x_{i}\}
. As
G
is irregular,
t\neq s
. Let
s=i_{0},i_{1},\ldots,i_{k-1},i_{k}=t
are consecutive vertices of a shortest path from
s
to
t
in
G

\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}(x_{i_{j}}-x_{i_{j+1}})^{2}\geq\dfrac{1}{k}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}(x_{i_{j}}-x_{i_{j+1}})\right)^{2}=\dfrac{1}{k}(x_{s}-x_{t})^{2}\geq\dfrac{1}{diam(G)}(x_{s}-x_{t})^{2}.

So,

\displaystyle(1-\alpha)\Delta-\lambda_{1}(A_{\alpha}(G))

\displaystyle\geq-\alpha x^{T}D(G)x+(1-\alpha)\displaystyle\left[\dfrac{1}{diam(G)}(x_{s}-x_{t})^{2}+(n\Delta-2m)x_{t}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\geq-\alpha\Delta+(1-\alpha)\displaystyle\left[\dfrac{1}{diam(G)}(x_{s}-x_{t})^{2}+(n\Delta-2m)x_{t}^{2}\right],

(6)

where the right-hand side is a quadratic function of

that attains its minimum when

x_{t}=\dfrac{x_{s}}{diam(G)(n\Delta-2m)+1}.

(7)

From (
6

7

(1-\alpha)\Delta-\lambda_{1}(A_{\alpha}(G))\geq-\alpha\Delta+(1-\alpha)\dfrac{(n\Delta-2m)x_{s}^{2}}{diam(G)(n\Delta-2m)+1}.

As
x^{T}x=1
, we have that
x_{s}^{2}>\dfrac{1}{n}
and the result follows.

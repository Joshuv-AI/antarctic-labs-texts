Mean field system: the optimal control based approach
Viorel Barbu

Abstract
The mean field game system arises in the construction of Nash type strategies for large population dynamics games. It is a nonlinear parabolic system in ( 0 , ∞ ) × ℝ d (0,{\infty})\times{\mathbb{R}^{d}} which consists of a nonlinear forward Fokker–Planck equation coupled with a backward Hamilton–Jacobi equation. In this work, the mean field game system is treated by interpreting it as the Euler–Lagrange system corresponding to a Bolza control problem governed by a linear Fokker–Planck equation with the controller in the drift term. One obtains in this way the existence and uniqueness of strong solutions in appropriate Sobolev spaces. This variational approach permits to treat more general mean field game systems than that studied in literature and, in particular, that with multivalued nonlinearities in the Fokker–Planck equation and discontinuous coupling functions. Keywords: mean field game, Fokker–Planck equation, stochastic differential equation, optimal control. MSC Codes: 60H15, 47H05, 47J05.

\hskip-19.91692pt\begin{array}[]{l}(\Phi_{\varepsilon}({\rho}(T)),p(T))_{2}-(\Phi_{\varepsilon}({\rho}_{0}),p(0))_{2}-\displaystyle\int_{Q_{T}}({\rho}_{1}\zeta_{1}-{\rho}_{2}\zeta_{2})\cdot\nabla\Phi_{\varepsilon}({\rho})dtdx\\
\qquad+\displaystyle\int_{Q_{T}}\Phi_{\varepsilon}({\rho})(H(t,x,\nabla p_{1})-H(t,x,\nabla p_{2}))dtdx=\displaystyle\int_{Q_{T}}\Phi_{\varepsilon}({\rho})\eta\,dtdx.\end{array}\hskip-19.91692pt

(5.8)

We have, for
{\varepsilon}\to 0
,

\begin{array}[]{rcll}\Phi_{\varepsilon}({\rho})&\to&{\rho}&\mbox{in }L^{\alpha^{*}}(Q_{T}),\end{array}\ \ \

and, since
\{\nabla\Phi_{\varepsilon}(\rho)=\Phi_{\varepsilon}(\nabla\rho)\}
is bounded in
(L^{\alpha}(Q_{d})^{d}
, we also have

\begin{array}[]{rcll}\nabla\Phi_{\varepsilon}(p)&\to&\nabla p&\mbox{in }(L^{\alpha}(Q_{T}))^{d}.\end{array}

Then, letting
{\varepsilon}\to 0
in (
5.8
), we get

\begin{array}[]{l}({\rho}(T),\eta_{0})_{2}-\displaystyle\int_{Q_{T}}({\rho}_{1}\zeta_{1}-{\rho}_{2}\zeta_{2})\cdot\nabla p\ dtdx\\
\qquad+\displaystyle\int_{Q_{T}}{\rho}(H(t,x,p_{1})-H(t,x,\nabla p_{2}))dtdx=\int_{Q_{T}}{\rho}\eta\ dtdx.\end{array}

(5.9)

Taking into account that by (
5.2
) we have

\zeta_{i}\cdot(\nabla p_{i}-z)\geq H(t,x,\nabla p_{i})-H(t,x,z),\ \forall z\in{\mathbb{R}},

and that
{\rho}_{i}\geq 0
, we get by (
5.9
) that

\int_{Q_{T}}{\rho}\eta\ dtdx\leq 0.

Hence,
{\rho}\eta=0
,
a.e.
in
Q_{T}
. Since
q\to g_{\rho}(t,x,q)
is strictly montone,
{\rho}={\rho}_{1}-{\rho}_{2}
and
\eta\in{\partial}_{g}({\rho}_{1})-{\partial}g({\rho}_{2})
, we get that
{\rho}_{1}-{\rho}_{2}=0
,
a.e.
in
Q_{T}
, as claimed.

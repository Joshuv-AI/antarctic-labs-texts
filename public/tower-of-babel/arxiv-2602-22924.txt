Refined wave breaking for the generalized Fornberg-Whitham equation
Jean-Claude Saut, and Yuexun Wang

Abstract
. This paper considers a class of non-local equations that are weakly dispersive perturbations of the inviscid Burgers equation, which includes the Fornberg-Whitham equation as a special case. We precise the known results on finite time blow-up (shock formation) by constructing a blowup solution which displays a ‘shock-like’ singularity (called wave breaking) at one single point. Moreover, this solution converges asymptotically in the self-similar variables to a stable self-similar solution of the inviscid Burgers equation, and also possesses a Hölder C 1 / 3 C^{1/3} regularity at the blowup point.

2.1.
Notation and conventions.

Let
\mathcal{F}(g)
or
\widehat{g}
be the Fourier transform of a Schwartz function

whose formula is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(g)(\xi)=\widehat{g}(\xi):=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(x)e^{-\mathrm{i}x\xi}\,dx

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{-1}(g)(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(\xi)e^{\mathrm{i}x\xi}\,d\xi,

and by
m(\partial_{x})
the Fourier multiplier with symbol
m
via the relation

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}\big(m(\partial_{x})g\big)(\xi)=m(\mathrm{i}\xi)\widehat{g}(\xi).

Take

satisfying
\varphi(\xi)=1
for
|\xi|\leq 1
and
\varphi(\xi)=0
when
|\xi|>2
, and
let

\displaystyle\psi(\xi)=\varphi(\xi)-\varphi(2\xi),\quad\psi_{j}(\xi)=\psi(2^{-j}\xi),\quad\varphi_{j}(\xi)=\varphi(2^{-j}\xi),

we then may define the

P_{j},P_{\leq j},P_{>j}
via

\displaystyle\widehat{P_{j}g}(\xi)=\psi_{j}(\xi)\widehat{g}(\xi),\quad\widehat{P_{\leq j}g}(\xi)=\varphi_{j}(\xi)\widehat{g}(\xi),\quad P_{>j}=1-P_{\leq j}.

The notation
C
always denotes a nonnegative universal constant which may be different from line to line but is
independent of the parameters involved. Otherwise, we will specify it by the notation
C(a,b,\dots)
.
We write

(

) when
g\leq Ch
(
g\geq Ch
), and

when
g\lesssim h\lesssim g
.
We also write

(III)
\partial_{x}u
blows up and has the following blowup rate

\displaystyle\frac{1}{3(T_{*}-t)}\leq\|\partial_{x}u\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\frac{3}{T_{*}-t}.

(IV)
u
displays a cusp singularity at
(x_{*},T_{*})
with
u(\cdot,T_{*})\in C^{\frac{1}{3}}(\mathbb{R})
.

(V)
u
converges asymptotically in the self-similar variables to a stable self-similar solution

\overline{U}_{\nu}
of the inviscid Burgers equation, namely

\displaystyle\limsup_{s\rightarrow\infty}\|U-\overline{U}_{\nu}\|_{L^{\infty}}=0,

(2.29)

where
\overline{U}_{\nu}
is defined by

\displaystyle\overline{U}_{\nu}(X)=\left(\frac{\nu}{6}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\overline{U}\left(\left(\frac{\nu}{6}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}X\right)

(2.30)

with
\nu=\lim_{s\rightarrow\infty}\partial_{X}^{3}U(0,s)
.

The blowup results in Theorem

are stable under perturbation, so one can relax the initial conditions above to achieve the following:

Corollary 1
.

There exists an open set of initial data in the
H^{5}
topology satisfying the hypothesis in Theorem
2.1
such that the conclusions of Theorem
2.1
still hold.

The proof of Corollary
1

[
32

, so we will omit it.
In the remaining sections, we will focus on the preliminaries and the proof of Theorem

3.
Bootstrap assumptions

3.1.
Bootstrap assumptions for the self-similar variables

First, we make the following bootstrap assumptions for various derivatives in different regimes:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &|\widetilde{U}(X,s)|\leq\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}h^{4},&&\quad 0\leq|X|\leq h,\\
&|\widetilde{U}(X,s)|\leq\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{4}}\langle X\rangle^{\frac{1}{3}},&&\quad h\leq|X|\leq\frac{1}{2}e^{\frac{3s}{2}},\end{aligned}\right.

(3.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &|\partial_{X}\widetilde{U}(X,s)|\leq\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}h^{3},&&\quad 0\leq|X|\leq h,\\

(3.3)

and

\left\{\begin{aligned} &|\partial_{X}^{3}\widetilde{U}(X,s)|\leq\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}h,&&\quad 0\leq|X|\leq h,\\

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\|\partial_{X}^{4}U\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq M,\\

(3.5)

and the precise third derivative bootstrap at

|\partial_{X}^{3}\widetilde{U}(0,s)|\leq\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(3.6)

3.2.
Bootstrap assumptions for the dynamic modulations variables

We assume that the dynamic modulation variables satisfy

\displaystyle|\dot{\tau}(t)|\leq e^{-\frac{3s}{4}},\quad|\tau(t)|\leq 2\varepsilon^{\frac{7}{4}},\quad T_{*}\leq 2\varepsilon^{\frac{7}{4}},

Lemma 2
.

It holds that

\displaystyle\|\partial_{X}U\|_{L^{2}}\leq 100.

(4.2)

Proof.

Taking
L^{2}
inner product of (
2.9
) for
n=1
with
\partial_{X}U
and integrating over
X
yields

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{ds}\|\partial_{X}U\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\partial_{X}U\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\int V\partial_{X}U\partial_{X}^{2}U\,dX+\beta_{\tau}\int(\partial_{X}U)^{3}\,dX=0.

(4.3)

By integration by parts, one obtains

\displaystyle\int V\partial_{X}U\partial_{X}^{2}U\,dX=-\frac{\beta_{\tau}}{2}\int(\partial_{X}U)^{3}dX-\frac{3}{4}\|\partial_{X}U\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

(4.4)

Substituting (
4.4

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{ds}\|\partial_{X}U\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\|\partial_{X}U\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\underbrace{-\frac{\beta_{\tau}}{2}\int(\partial_{X}U)^{3}\,dX}_{I}.

(4.5)

By (
3.2

\displaystyle|I|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{0\leq|X|\leq h}+\int_{h\leq|X|\leq\frac{1}{2}e^{\frac{3s}{2}}}+\int_{\frac{1}{2}e^{\frac{3s}{2}}\leq|X|<\infty}\right)\left(\partial_{X}U\right)^{3}\,dX

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{0\leq|X|\leq h}(|\partial_{X}\widetilde{U}(X,s)|+|\partial_{X}\overline{U}(X)|)^{3}h

\displaystyle+\int_{h\leq|X|\leq\frac{1}{2}e^{\frac{3s}{2}}}(|\partial_{X}\widetilde{U}(X,s)|+|\partial_{X}\overline{U}(X)|)^{3}\,dX

\displaystyle+2e^{-s}\int_{\frac{1}{2}e^{\frac{3s}{2}}\leq|X|<\infty}|\partial_{X}U|^{2}\,dX

\displaystyle\leq(\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}h+1)^{3}h+(\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{8}}+1)\int\frac{1}{1+X^{2}}\,dX+2e^{-s}\int|\partial_{X}U|^{2}\,dX

It follows from (

\displaystyle\frac{d}{ds}\|\partial_{X}U\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\|\partial_{X}U\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq 16h+2\pi.

(4.7)

Solving (

\displaystyle\|\partial_{X}U\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

This completes the proof of (

∎

5.
Lemmas on Fourier multiplier

In this section, we study some analytic properties of the operators
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{L}
defined in (
2.7
). We first consider the operator
\mathcal{L}
. Since
\mathcal{L}
only contains low frequency of
e^{\frac{3s}{2}}\xi_{X}
, this helps to transfer the derivatives into temporal decay by Bernstein’s inequality.

Lemma 4
.

We have

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{L}(\partial_{X}^{l}f)\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim e^{-(\frac{1}{2}+l)\frac{3s}{2}}\|f\|_{L^{2}}\qquad\mathrm{for}\ l\geq 0.

As a consequence,

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{L}(\partial_{X}^{l}U)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C(M)e^{-\frac{3l-1}{2}s}\qquad\mathrm{for}\ l\geq 0.

(5.1)

Proof.

Observe that

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{L}(\partial_{X}^{l}f)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=e^{-\frac{3s}{2}l}\|(e^{\frac{3s}{2}}\xi_{X})^{l+1}\varphi_{0}(e^{\frac{3s}{2}}\xi_{X})\big((1+e^{3s}|\xi_{X}|^{2})^{\frac{\alpha-1}{2}}\hat{f}(\xi_{X})\big)\|_{L_{\xi_{X}}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim e^{-\frac{3s}{2}l}\|\hat{f}\|_{L_{\xi_{X}}^{2}}=e^{-\frac{3s}{2}l}\|f\|_{L^{2}},

where one has used the fact that
\varphi_{0}
is supported on
\{e^{\frac{3s}{2}}|\xi_{X}|\leq 2\}
.
This together with Bernstein’s inequality yields

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{L}(\partial_{X}^{l}f)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq e^{-\frac{3s}{2}\times\frac{1}{2}}\|\mathcal{L}(\partial_{X}^{l}f)\|_{L^{2}}\leq e^{-(\frac{1}{2}+l)\frac{3s}{2}}\|f\|_{L^{2}}.

Applying the above inequality to
f=U+e^{\frac{s}{2}}\kappa
yields

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{L}(\partial_{X}^{l}U)\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle=\|\mathcal{L}\big(\partial_{X}^{l}(U+e^{\frac{s}{2}}\kappa)\big)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq e^{-(\frac{1}{2}+l)\frac{3s}{2}}\|U+e^{\frac{s}{2}}\kappa\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq Me^{-(\frac{1}{2}+l)\frac{3s}{2}}e^{\frac{5s}{4}}\leq C(M)e^{-\frac{3l-1}{2}s}.

∎

To estimate the operator
\mathcal{H}
, we write it in the following integral form.

Lemma 5
.

For each
s
, there exists a function
G_{s}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\})
such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}(f)(X)=e^{-\frac{3s}{2}}\int\mathrm{sign}(X-Y)G_{\alpha}\big(e^{-\frac{3s}{2}}(X-Y)\big)f(Y)\,dY,

(5.2)

where
G_{\alpha}
is an even kernel satisfying

\displaystyle|G_{\alpha}(X)|\leq C\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{1}{|X|^{1+\alpha}}&\quad\mathrm{for}\ |X|\leq 1\ \mathrm{and}\ \ -1<\alpha<0,\\
\log\frac{1}{|X|}+1&\quad\mathrm{for}\ |X|\leq 1\ \mathrm{and}\ \ \alpha=-1,\\
1&\quad\mathrm{for}\ |X|\leq 1\ \mathrm{and}\ \ \alpha<-1,\\
e^{-\frac{|X|}{2}}&\quad\mathrm{for}\ |X|>1\ \mathrm{and}\ \ \alpha<0.\end{array}\right.

(5.3)

Proof.

The proof is a straightforward calculation by using the Fourier inversion formula together with the property of the Bessel potential (see
[
27
]
).

∎

Lemma 6
.

It holds that

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{H}(f)\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}.

As a consequence,

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{H}(U)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C(M)e^{\frac{s}{2}},

and

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{H}(\partial_{X}^{l}U)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C(M)\qquad\mathrm{for}\ 1\leq l\leq 4.

Proof.

Noticing from (
5.3
) that
G_{\alpha}\in L^{1}
, one may use Young’s inequality to estimate

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{H}(f)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq e^{-\frac{3s}{2}}\|G_{\alpha}(e^{-\frac{3s}{2}}\cdot)\|_{L^{1}}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}.

It follows from
\eqref{eq:5.4}_{2}
that

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{H}(U)\|_{L^{\infty}}=\|\mathcal{H}(U+e^{\frac{s}{2}}\kappa)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\|U+e^{\frac{s}{2}}\kappa\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq Me^{\frac{s}{2}},

and from (
4.2
) and (
4.8
) that

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{H}(\partial_{X}^{l}U)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\|\partial_{X}^{l}U\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\|\partial_{X}U\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{9-2l}{8}}\|\partial_{X}^{5}U\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{2l-1}{8}}\leq C(M)

for
1\leq l\leq 4
.

∎

Collecting the results in Lemma
4
and
6
yields the following
L^{\infty}
bounds for
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{L}
.

Corollary 2
.

We have

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{H}(U)+\mathcal{L}(U)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C(M)e^{\frac{s}{2}},

(5.4)

and

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{H}(\partial_{X}^{l}U)+\mathcal{L}(\partial_{X}^{l}U)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C(M)\qquad\mathrm{for}\ 1\leq l\leq 4.

(5.5)

Since
\mathcal{H}(\partial_{X}U)
does not provide any temporal decay, we will use the spatial decay of
\mathcal{H}(\partial_{X}U)
in the middle field
h\leq|X|\leq\frac{1}{2}e^{\frac{3s}{2}}
to kill the (partial) growth of the weight
\langle X\rangle^{\frac{2}{3}}
to close the bootstrap assumption

\eqref{eq:5.1}_{2}
.

Lemma 7
.

For
(X,s)
such that
h\leq|X|\leq\frac{1}{2}e^{\frac{3s}{2}}
, we have

\displaystyle|\mathcal{H}(\partial_{X}U)(X,s)|\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}Ce^{\frac{3}{2}\alpha s}\langle X\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}-\alpha}+Ce^{-\frac{3s}{4}}&\quad\mathrm{for}\ -1/2<\alpha<0,\\
Ce^{-\frac{5s}{8}}&\quad\mathrm{for}\ \alpha\leq-1/2.\end{array}\right.

(5.6)

Proof.

By (
5.2
), we split the integral into different parts in the following way:

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}(\partial_{X}U)(X,s)

\displaystyle=e^{-\frac{3s}{2}}\int_{A_{1}}\mathrm{sign}(X-Y)G_{\alpha}\big(e^{-\frac{3s}{2}}(X-Y)\big)\big(\partial_{X}U(Y,s)-\partial_{X}U(X,s)\big)\,dY

\displaystyle+e^{-\frac{3s}{2}}\bigg(\int_{A_{2}}+\int_{A_{3}}+\int_{A_{4}}\bigg)\mathrm{sign}(X-Y)G_{\alpha}\big(e^{-\frac{3s}{2}}(X-Y)\big)\partial_{X}U(Y,s)\,dY

\displaystyle=:I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}+I_{4},

where

\displaystyle A_{1}=\{|X-Y|\leq\langle X\rangle^{-\frac{2}{3}}\},

\displaystyle A_{2}=\{\langle X\rangle^{-\frac{2}{3}}<|X-Y|\leq 1\},

\displaystyle A_{3}=\{1<|X-Y|\leq e^{\frac{3s}{2}}\},

\displaystyle A_{4}=\{|X-Y|>e^{\frac{3s}{2}}\}.

Notice that
G_{\alpha}
is more singular when
\alpha<0
is larger when
|X|\leq 1
. It suffices to consider the case
-\frac{1}{2}<\alpha<0
since the other cases can be handled more easily.

For
I_{1}
, one may use the mean value theorem to bound it as follows:

\displaystyle|I_{1}|\leq e^{\frac{3}{2}\alpha s}\|\partial_{X}^{2}U\|_{L^{\infty}}\int_{A_{1}}|X-Y|^{-\alpha}\,dY\leq C(M)e^{\frac{3}{2}\alpha s}\langle X\rangle^{-\frac{2}{3}(1-\alpha)}.

(5.7)

Considering
I_{2}
, we divide it into two cases:

(I)
|X|\leq\frac{1}{2}e^{\frac{3s}{2}}-1
. In this case, since
|X-Y|\leq 1
, one has

\displaystyle|Y|\leq|X-Y|+|X|\leq\frac{1}{2}e^{\frac{3s}{2}},

which together with
\eqref{eq:5.1}_{2}

if necessary, we may also assume
h\leq|X|
. It follows from
\eqref{eq:5.1}_{2}
, (
7.5
) and (
7.8
) that for all
t\in[t_{1},T_{*})

\displaystyle|\partial_{x}u(x,t)|\lesssim\frac{1}{\tau(t)-t}|X|^{-\frac{2}{3}}\lesssim|x-x_{*}|^{-\frac{2}{3}},

and

\displaystyle|\partial_{x}u(x,t)|\gtrsim\frac{1}{\tau(t)-t}|X|^{-\frac{2}{3}}\gtrsim|x-x_{*}|^{-\frac{2}{3}}.

Then (
7.7
) follows by sending
t
to
T_{*}
in the above two inequalities.

We conclude from (
7.6
) and (
7.7
) that
u(\cdot,T_{*})\in C^{\frac{1}{3}}(\mathbb{R})
and
u
has a cusp singularity at
(x_{*},T_{*})
.

7.2.
Asymptotic Convergence to Stationary Solution

Step 1.

It is easy to verify by (
6.17
) that the limit
\nu=\lim_{s\rightarrow\infty}\partial_{X}^{3}U(0,s)
exists.
Define

\displaystyle\widetilde{U}_{\nu}:=U-\overline{U}_{\nu}

To finish (
2.29
), it suffices to show

\displaystyle\limsup_{s\rightarrow\infty}|\widetilde{U}_{\nu}(X,s)|=0\quad\text{for}\ X\in\mathbb{R}.

(7.9)

Recalling (
2.21
) and (
2.30
), (
7.9
) is obvious when
X=0
. Thus it remains to show (
7.9
) for
X\neq 0
.

We begin by proving (
7.9
) in the near field, namely
|X|\in(0,|X_{0}|)
for some small
|X_{0}|
.
Recalling the constrains (
2.21
), one can Taylor expand
\widetilde{U}_{\nu}
as follows:

\displaystyle\widetilde{U}_{\nu}(X,s)=\frac{X^{3}}{6}\partial_{X}^{3}\widetilde{U}_{\nu}(0,s)+\frac{X^{4}}{24}\partial_{X}^{4}\widetilde{U}_{\nu}(Y,s)

(7.10)

for some
Y
between
0
and
X
when
X
is close to
0
. Next, we handle the RHS of (
7.10
) term by term. Observe that

\displaystyle\lim_{s\rightarrow\infty}\partial_{X}^{3}\widetilde{U}_{\nu}(0,s)=\lim_{s\rightarrow\infty}\partial_{X}^{3}U(0,s)-\nu=0.

Hence, first fix a small
|X_{0}|>0
, and then fix a small
\delta\in(0,M|X_{0}|)
, there exists a large enough
s_{0}=s_{0}(X_{0},\delta)
such that

\displaystyle|\partial_{X}^{3}\widetilde{U}_{\nu}(X_{0},s)|\leq\delta\quad\text{for}\ s\geq s_{0}.

(7.11)

Notice that

\displaystyle\partial_{X}^{4}\overline{U}_{\nu}(X)=\left(\frac{\nu}{6}\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}\partial_{X}^{4}\overline{U}\left(\left(\frac{\nu}{6}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}X\right).

Then one may estimate

\displaystyle\|\partial_{X}^{4}\widetilde{U}_{\nu}\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\partial_{X}^{4}U\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\partial_{X}^{4}\overline{U}_{\nu}\|_{L^{\infty}}

(7.12)

\displaystyle\leq M+100\left(\frac{\nu}{6}\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}\leq 2M.

It follows from (
7.10
)-(
7.12
) that

\displaystyle|\widetilde{U}_{\nu}(X_{0},s)|\leq\frac{\delta|X_{0}|^{3}}{6}+\frac{M|X_{0}|^{4}}{12}\quad\text{for}\ s\geq s_{0}.

(7.13)

Step 2.

Basic calculations show that
\widetilde{U}_{\nu}
evolves in time as follows:

\displaystyle\left(\partial_{s}-\frac{1}{2}+\partial_{X}\overline{U}_{\nu}\right)\widetilde{U}_{\nu}+\bigg(\underbrace{\frac{3}{2}X+U}_{P}\bigg)\partial_{X}\widetilde{U}_{\nu}=\beta_{\tau}F_{\widetilde{U}_{\nu}}

(7.14)

with

\displaystyle F_{\widetilde{U}_{\nu}}=e^{-s}\big(\mathcal{H}(U)+\mathcal{L}(U)\big)-e^{-\frac{s}{2}}\dot{\kappa}-e^{\frac{s}{2}}(\kappa-\dot{\xi})\partial_{X}U-\dot{\tau}U\partial_{X}U.

The forcing term is bounded by

\displaystyle\beta_{\tau}|F_{\widetilde{U}_{\nu}}|\lesssim C(M)e^{-s}e^{\frac{s}{2}}+e^{-\frac{s}{3}}+e^{-\frac{3s}{4}}+Me^{-\frac{3s}{4}}e^{\frac{s}{2}}\leq e^{-\frac{s}{5}},

(7.15)

where (
6.11
) has been used.

To estimate
\widetilde{U}_{\nu}
, we need to work with a new Lagrangian trajectory associated with
P
. To this end, we consider the following Lagrangian trajectory:

\displaystyle\frac{d}{ds}\Psi^{X_{0}}(s)=\frac{3}{2}\Psi^{X_{0}}(s)+P\circ\Psi^{X_{0}}(s),\quad\Psi^{X_{0}}(s_{0})=X_{0}.

(7.16)

By the mean value theorem and (
3.9
), one obtains

\displaystyle|U(X,s)|\leq|U(0,s)|+\|\partial_{X}U\|_{L^{\infty}}|X|\leq\frac{101}{100}|X|.

(7.17)

It follows from (
7.16
) and (
7.17
) that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{ds}|\Psi^{X_{0}}(s)|^{2}\geq 3|\Psi^{X_{0}}(s)|^{2}-2\times\frac{101}{100}|\Psi^{X_{0}}(s)|^{2}\geq\frac{4}{5}|\Psi^{X_{0}}(s)|^{2},

which together with
\Psi^{X_{0}}(s_{0})=X_{0}
gives

\displaystyle|\Psi^{X_{0}}(s)|\geq|X_{0}|e^{\frac{2}{5}(s-s_{0})}.

(7.18)

Set
G(X,s)=e^{-\frac{3}{2}(s-s_{0})}\widetilde{U}_{\nu}(X,s)
. By
(
7.14
), it is direct to check that

\displaystyle\left(\frac{d}{ds}+1+\partial_{X}\overline{U}_{\nu}\right)G\circ\Psi^{X_{0}}(s)=\beta_{\tau}e^{-\frac{3}{2}(s-s_{0})}F_{\widetilde{U}_{\nu}}\circ\Psi^{X_{0}}(s)

(7.19)

with the damping bounded below by
0
due to (
3.10
) that

\displaystyle 1+\partial_{X}\overline{U}_{\nu}\geq 1-\|\partial_{X}\overline{U}_{\nu}\|_{L^{\infty}}=1-\|\partial_{X}\overline{U}\|_{L^{\infty}}=0.

Plugging (
7.13
) and (
7.15
) into (
7.19
) and applying Gronwall’s inequality yields

\displaystyle|G\circ\Psi^{X_{0}}(s)|

\displaystyle\leq|G\circ\Psi^{X_{0}}(s_{0})|+\beta_{\tau}\int_{s_{0}}^{s}|F_{\widetilde{U}_{\nu}}\circ\Psi^{X_{0}}(s_{0})|e^{-\frac{3}{2}(s^{\prime}-s_{0})}\,ds^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\delta|X_{0}|^{3}}{6}+\frac{M|X_{0}|^{4}}{12}+\int_{s_{0}}^{s}e^{-\frac{s^{\prime}}{5}}e^{-\frac{3}{2}(s^{\prime}-s_{0})}\,ds^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\delta|X_{0}|^{3}}{6}+\frac{M|X_{0}|^{4}}{12}+\frac{M|X_{0}|^{4}}{12}\leq M|X_{0}|^{4},

that is

\displaystyle|\widetilde{U}_{\nu}\circ\Psi^{X_{0}}(s)|\leq M|X_{0}|^{4}e^{-\frac{3}{2}(s-s_{0})}.

(7.20)

Denote
s_{*}=s_{0}+\frac{13}{5}\log|X_{0}|^{-1}
.
Thus for
s\in(s_{0},s_{*})
, it follows from (
7.20
) that

\displaystyle|\widetilde{U}_{\nu}\circ\Psi^{X_{0}}(s)|\leq M|X_{0}|^{4-\frac{3}{2}\frac{13}{5}}\leq M|X_{0}|^{\frac{1}{10}}.

(7.21)

For all
X
between
X_{0}
and
\Psi^{X_{0}}(s_{*})
, there exists
s\in(s_{0},s_{*})
such that
X=\Psi^{X_{0}}(s)
. Therefore, for such
(X,s)
, (
7.21
) gives

\displaystyle|\widetilde{U}_{\nu}(X,s)|\leq M|X_{0}|^{\frac{1}{10}}.

(7.22)

By (
7.18
), one infers that (
7.22
) will cover at least all
X
satisfying

\displaystyle|X_{0}|\leq|X|\leq|X_{0}|e^{\frac{2}{5}\cdot\frac{13}{5}\log|X_{0}|^{-1}}=|X_{0}|^{-\frac{1}{25}}.

(7.23)

Hence, combining (
7.22
) and (
7.23
) and taking the limit that
s_{0}\rightarrow\infty
, one obtains

\displaystyle\limsup_{s\rightarrow\infty}|\widetilde{U}_{\nu}(y,s)|\leq M|X_{0}|^{\frac{1}{10}}.

(7.24)

Finally, sending
X_{0}\rightarrow 0
in (
7.24
) proves for all
X\neq 0
that

\displaystyle\limsup_{s\rightarrow\infty}|\widetilde{U}_{\nu}(y,s)|=0.

(7.25)

This completes the proof of (
7.9
).

Appendix A

Let
\Phi^{X_{0}}(s)
be the Lagrangian trajectory defined by

\displaystyle\frac{d}{ds}\Phi^{X_{0}}(s)=V\circ\Phi^{X_{0}}(s),\quad\Phi^{X_{0}}(s_{0})=X_{0},

(A.1)

where
V
is the transport speed given by in (
2.8
).

We first present an upper bound for the Lagrangian trajectory
\Phi^{X_{0}}(s)
, which shows that
\Phi^{X_{0}}(s)
grows at most exponentially with time, but is bounded for any fixed
X_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
when
s<\infty
. This means that
\Phi^{X_{0}}(s)
is well-defined.

Lemma 10
.

For all
X_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\displaystyle|\Phi^{X_{0}}(s)|\leq\big(|X_{0}|+6M\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}}\big)e^{\frac{3}{2}(s-s_{0})}.

(A.2)

Proof
.

It follows from (
A.1
) that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{ds}\left(e^{-\frac{3s}{2}}\Phi^{X_{0}}(s)\right)=\beta_{\tau}e^{-\frac{3s}{2}}\big(U\circ\Phi^{X_{0}}(s)+e^{\frac{s}{2}}(\kappa-\dot{\xi})\big),

which together with (
6.11
) and (
6.9
) gives

\displaystyle|\Phi^{X_{0}}(s)|

\displaystyle\leq|X_{0}|e^{\frac{3}{2}(s-s_{0})}+e^{\frac{3s}{2}}\int_{s_{0}}^{s}\beta_{\tau}\big(U\circ\Phi^{X_{0}}(s^{\prime})+e^{\frac{s^{\prime}}{2}}(\kappa-\dot{\xi})\big)e^{-\frac{3s^{\prime}}{2}}\,ds^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq|X_{0}|e^{\frac{3}{2}(s-s_{0})}+e^{\frac{3s}{2}}\int_{s_{0}}^{s}\frac{101}{100}\big(2Me^{\frac{s^{\prime}}{2}}+e^{-s^{\prime}}\big)e^{-\frac{3s^{\prime}}{2}}\,ds^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq\big(|X_{0}|+6M\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}}\big)e^{\frac{3}{2}(s-s_{0})}.

This completes the proof of (
A.2
).

∎

Next, we present a lower bound for
\Phi^{X_{0}}(s)
, which tells that
\Phi^{X_{0}}(s)
will escape to infinity exponentially fast, once the particle
X_{0}
is at least away from
0
by a distance
h
. This lower bound will play a role in converting spatial decay into temporal decay.

Lemma 11
.

(
[
32
]
)
For
h\leq|X_{0}|<\infty
, we have

\displaystyle|\Phi^{X_{0}}(s)|\geq|X_{0}|e^{\frac{1}{5}(s-s_{0})}.

(A.3)

Proof.

We a priori assume

\displaystyle|\Phi^{X_{0}}(s)|\geq|X_{0}|.

(A.4)

By the assumption of this lemma and the definition of
h
, it holds that

\displaystyle|X_{0}|\geq h\geq 100\varepsilon\geq 100e^{-s}.

Hence, one obtains

\displaystyle e^{-s}\leq\frac{1}{100}|\Phi^{X_{0}}(s)|.

(A.5)

By the mean value theorem and (
3.9
), one obtains

\displaystyle|U(X,s)|\leq|U(0,s)|+\|\partial_{X}U\|_{L^{\infty}}|X|\leq\frac{101}{100}|X|.

This together with (
2.8
), (
6.9
) and (
A.5
) yields

\displaystyle|V\circ\Phi^{X_{0}}(s)|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{3}{2}|\Phi^{X_{0}}(s)|-\beta_{\tau}\big(|U\circ\Phi^{X_{0}}(s)|+e^{\frac{s}{2}}|\kappa-\dot{\xi}|\big)

(A.6)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{3}{2}|\Phi^{X_{0}}(s)|-\frac{101}{100}\times\frac{101}{100}|\Phi^{X_{0}}(s)|-\frac{101}{100}e^{-s}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{5}|\Phi^{X_{0}}(s)|.

It follows from (
A.1
) and (
A.6
)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{ds}|\Psi^{X_{0}}(s)|^{2}\geq\frac{2}{5}|\Psi^{X_{0}}(s)|^{2}.

Solving this inequality gives (
A.3
), which also closes (
A.4
).

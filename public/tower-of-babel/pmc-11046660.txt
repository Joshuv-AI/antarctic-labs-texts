A case of HSV2 vasculitis causing multi-territory ischaemic stroke in an immunocompetent man
Authors: Okkar Kyaw, Gagira Mudalige
Published: 2024

Case summary

A 65-year-old man presented with fluctuating scores on the Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS) and confusion. He was previously fit and well, with a past medical history of type 2 diabetes and haemochromatosis. One month earlier, he had suffered from a left middle cerebral artery ischaemic stroke. Following a period of therapy, he had returned to his baseline function. However, on this admission his initial MRI demonstrated new multiterritory ischaemic strokes, and further angiography showed irregularities in several vessels consistent with vasculitis. Clinically, he remained confused with fluctuating GCS, which was not in keeping with the areas of infarction. A lumbar puncture (LP) was performed which detected the presence of herpes simplex virus 2 (HSV2). Anti-viral therapy (acyclovir) and intravenous methylprednisolone therapy was initiated while he was nursed in the intensive care unit. Despite a protracted course of antimicrobial therapy, with repeat LPs confirmed negative, his clinical improvement was laboured. An electroencephalogram (EEG) was performed but this did not demonstrate any epileptic activity either. He was therefore transferred to the neurorehabilitation unit for ongoing recovery.

Investigations

Radiology

MRI Head showed restricted diffusion in the left splenium of corpus callosum, left posterior hippocampus and small foci within the left occipital lobe. Further restricted diffusion was noted within the posterior inferior right basal ganglia and faint areas within the right centrum semiovale, medial left thalamus and left lateral midbrain.

CT cerebral angiography showed multivessel narrowing and irregularity affecting the left anterior cerebral artery, left middle cerebral artery (MCA), left posterior communicating and right MCA (Fig 1). Appearances were in keeping with vasculitis.

Computed tomography angiography showing left anterior cerebral artery and middle cerebral artery irregularity.

The patient's last MRI Head showed expected interval evolution of previous infarcts with no acute infarct or parenchymal abnormality identified.

Microbiology

Cerebrospinal fluid PCR was positive for HSV-2.

Immunology

Liver autoimmune profiles were negative. The patient's connective tissue disease screen was negative. Screens for MPO, PR-3 and anti-beta-2 glycoprotein were negative.

Discussion

Herpes simplex virus 1 infection in central nervous system is classically associated with temporal lobe encephalitis, while HSV 2 is associated with large vessel vasculitis. Moreover, immunocompromised patients are at increased risk of disease severity and response to the treatment. In our patient, despite being immunocompetent, his infection caused multi-territory infarcts and subsequently led to a prolonged rehabilitation period.

Learning points

This case raises the awareness for increasing association between HSV-2 central nervous system infection and vasculitis leading to ischaemic strokes. Early treatment with acyclovir should be considered regardless of whether the patient is immunocompromised or not.

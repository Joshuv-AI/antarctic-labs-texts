Developing a theory-driven framework for a web-based intervention to improve transition in childhood cancer survivors: a protocol of realist synthesis
Authors: Jun Ma, Jing Li, Weihong Huang, Honghong Wang
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
Backgrounds and purposeChildhood cancer survivors (CCSs) who transition through adolescence and enter young adulthood may suffer psychological, cognitive, social, fertility, and sexual issues and concerns. There is an urgent need for comprehensive intervention strategies to improve the transition of CCSs. Web-based technologies are gaining momentum as a new mechanism to provide healthcare and education for adolescents. However, previous frameworks have been limited in their effectiveness in explaining web-based interventions.This realist synthesis aims to synthesise current evidence on transition of CCSs to develop a framework for web-based interventions. The framework can foster understanding of the integrity of web-based intervention implementation chain, examine which mechanistic factors will be triggered by web-based interventions, note and examine the flows, blockages and points of contention in the implementation, to refine web-based interventions.Method and analysisA realist synthesis that adheres to the Realist and Meta-narrative Evidence Syntheses-Evolving Standard will be used. Studies will be identified through PubMed, Web of Science, EMBASE, PsycINFO, CINAHL, Ovid and Cochrane Library from the period of January 2005 to May 2023. We will also search the reference lists provided in relevant studies and reviews. Articles will be screened based on two principles: (1) Relevance: does the research address the initial programme theory? (2) Rigour: whether a particular inference drawn by the original researcher has sufficient weight to make a methodologically credible contribution to the test of the initial programme theory. No restrictions regarding the design or language of publication will be considered.Ethics and disseminationAs a review, ethical approval is not required. The results from this study will be presented at international conferences and disseminated through peer-reviewed publications. Patients and the public will be involved in the dissemination plans.

Introduction

The global average 5-year survival rate of paediatric cancer was estimated to be 37.4% (34.7%–39.8%) in 2019.1 More than 80% of childhood cancer survivors (CCSs) confront a long survivorship phase,2–4 often spanning six decades.5 The risk of treatment-related health problems increases over this long-term survivorship phase.6 However, CCSs who transition through adolescence and enter young adulthood may suffer some common issues and concerns, including psychological, cognitive, social, fertility and sexual.7 8 These development tasks can severely interrupt survivor-focused long-term care,5 leading to a diminished adherence to follow-up,9 inappropriate surveillance for late effects of cancer treatment,7 general health deterioration and increased rates of rehospitalisation in adulthood.10 Those major challenges highlight the necessity of increasing knowledge and developing skills for better health management as CCSs transition through adolescence and enter young adulthood.11 12

Despite the urgent need for comprehensive intervention strategies to improve the transition of CCSs, current interventions are generally in infancy because studies remain at the level of exploring transition needs qualitatively.13 14 We conducted a scoping review to summarise the features of intervention components of the current transition studies for CCSs. All original studies (n=9) that investigated transition interventions in CCSs were included.15–23 Current studies identified essential elements for a transition programme, such as delivering knowledge, developing skills of coordination of care and addressing psychosocial needs. However, current transition interventions are generally in infancy, and major deficits were found, including poorly reported intervention components and procedures, limited amount of relevant validated outcomes, and failure to incorporate conceptual frameworks and international consensus statements.

Betz et al24 suggested that the lack of intervention resulted from the lack of frameworks that depict the theoretical basis of healthcare transition investigations. Frameworks are needed for research and quality improvement to delineate variables for measurements for interventions focused on a comprehensive transition model. Moreover, transition interventions are complex and have multiple components, potential confounders, mediators and moderators.25 Hence, knowing what these are, explaining them and controlling them is challenging. The intended outcomes of interventions are also generally multidimensional (knowledge, self-management, etc) and, consequently, interconnected in a complex causal chain, making replicating findings across sociodemographic and geographical boundaries challenging.

Mid-range transition theory is the most representative theory.26 It provides a broad view of transitions, which are foundational for much of this work; however, the theory lacks the developmental perspective implicit in healthcare transitions. Given the complexity of healthcare transitions, this model should be revisited and its applicability in specific research contexts is considered. In 2011, the development of the social-ecological model of AYA (adolescents and young adults) readiness for transition (SMART) represented significant progress in the literature on healthcare transition.10 27 SMART does not account for the full spectrum of the transition process, including mechanics of transition and transition outcomes,27 which may hinder efforts to the development and evaluation of transition interventions.28 These challenges point out the need for better theories of what transition interventions seek to achieve, how they try to do it and the intended and unintended outcomes for whom and in what circumstances. Hence, more explicit and coherent programme theories are required to explore and conceptualise contextual elements and mechanisms that interact to explain the observed effects of transition interventions for CCSs.

Researchers in current studies experimented on the traditional delivery format in limited transitional interventions, such as workbooks15 16 and face-to-face consultation.17 19 22 Robust web-based interventional and evaluative strategies in the transition of CCSs have yet to be developed,29 although existing evidence suggests that these interventions are broadly acceptable by CCSs and can derive benefits from them.30 Web-based transition interventions can effectively educate and empower CCSs to prepare for transition in a timely manner,20 31 32 enabling them to assume adult roles and function correctly, which may have a sustainable effect on disease prevention and management in long-term survival.10 33 Guidelines have suggested a role for digital communication, telemedicine and shared medical appointments in health promotion strategies in transition with CCSs.34 Mobile technologies are gaining momentum as a new mechanism to provide healthcare and education for adolescents.35–37 However, with the emergence of web-based technologies for intervention implementation as a new and emerging strategy in recent years, previous frameworks have been limited in their effectiveness in explaining web-based interventions. This study aims to synthesise current evidence on the transition of CCSs to a new framework. The framework can foster understanding of the integrity of the implementation chain before web-based intervention construction, examine which mechanistic factors need to be triggered in place for a successful transition to occur and note and examine the flows, blockages and points of contention to shape or refine intervention to adapt to local circumstances.

Rationale and objectives

Pawson et al38 proposed a realist synthesis to understand the relationship between the context, mechanism and outcome (CMO) of complex interventions. That is, it involves identifying underlying causal mechanisms and exploring how they work under different circumstances and contexts.39 However, Mukumbang et al40 suggested that the CMO configuration based on the fact that interventions (I) can only work when adopted by actors (A),40 and modified the CMO to Intervention-Context-Actors-Mechanism-Outcome (ICAMO) configurations to improve its explanatory power.40 41

Therefore, we will adopt a realist synthesis approach based on Pawson’s practical steps for conducting realist synthesis and the ICAMO configurations to unearth and refine a theoretical framework for novel web-based intervention(s) to improve the transition of CCSs. The following research questions guided it:

Methods

The protocol was registered prospectively with the Open Science Framework (osf.io/2yn4d) and recognised guidelines (the Realist And Meta-narrative Evidence Syntheses-Evolving Standards) to ensure the quality of the review and how it is reported will be followed.42

Defining initial programme theory

A programme theory will preliminarily identify underlying causal mechanisms and how web-based interventions lead to outcomes under different circumstances and contexts.39 In this review, the definition of initial programme theory will involve informal ‘browsing’ of the literature and consulting with experts.

We will start by conducting a rapid review of broad areas of transition-related studies and locating current theories. This search process is more exploratory than that in 2.2 and aims to identify a range of possibly relevant theories rapidly. Then, the internal meetings of the research group members will decompose the various existing theories into sets of subconcepts and code these subconcepts according to the ICAMO configurations. Any data indicating relationships between variables in the various existing theories will also be extracted and used to link the sets of subconcepts again and form the initial programme theory. Expert panel meetings with paediatric and adult healthcare providers will be employed to synthesise the initial programme theory and later refinement.

Developing a search strategy

In consultation with experienced librarians of the authors’ university, comprehensive literature searches will be conducted in PubMed, Web of Science, EMBASE, PsycINFO, CINAHL, Ovid and Cochrane Library using keywords and database-specific controlled vocabulary terms where available, covering the years January 2005 to May 2023. This starting date coincides with the publication of the seminal Institute of Medicine report in 2005 called ‘From Cancer Patient to Cancer Survivor: Lost in Transition’, which first highlighted cancer survivors and the concept of transition.5 43 And after then, scholars begin research related to healthcare transitions for cancer survivors.

A preliminary search strategy for each selected database will be implemented to identify a comprehensive set of relevant keywords and subject headings (online supplemental additional file 1). We used ‘AND’ connections between keywords and ‘OR’ connections between synonyms or near-synonyms. All identified keywords and index terms will be then undertaken across all included databases (figure 1). The reference list of all identified reports and articles will also be searched for additional studies.

Literature selection

After applying the search strategy, all retrieved articles will be imported into EndNote (V.20.0) and deduplicated. The titles and abstracts of retrieved articles will be independently reviewed by two trained reviewers (JM and JL). The remaining articles will be placed into full-text screening on completion of the title and abstract screening. The full texts will be independently reviewed by the same trained reviewers for inclusion. Multiple collaborating researchers will systematically describe reasons for the inclusion and exclusion of selected scientific documents. Reviewers will meet regularly to discuss progress, and consensus will be used to resolve discrepancies.

Eligibility criteria

Population

This review aims to explore different perspectives on the transition of CCSs and synthesise evidence into a new framework; these studies may include populations, including adolescents or young adults, family caregivers, healthcare providers, etc; therefore, transition-related perspectives from any population will be considered for inclusion, but we are not going to be too restrictive on included study subjects.

Context

Realist synthesis will consider every element of a primary study that could contribute to validating the link between ICAMO configurations. No limitations were set on the cultural/subcultural factors, geographical location, specific racial or gender-based interests and details about specific settings.

Outcome

For this review, transition only referred specifically to the period from adolescence to early adulthood. The outcomes of interest were any ICAMO elements that are related to transition to adulthood for CCSs, contextual factors that will affect the transition of CCSs, information on actors in situations of transition, participants’ expected content/form of transition web-based interventions and pathways in which these factors affect transition outcomes and the target outcomes of transition in CCSs, etc.

Study design

This work includes not only conventional qualitative and quantitative research designs but also current theories, guidelines, reviews, effectiveness studies, intervention development studies, evaluative case studies and qualitative studies.

Quality appraisal

Realist synthesis typically evaluates the quality of the included literature based on two specific principles: (1) relevance: does the research address the initial programme theory? and (2) rigour: whether a particular inference drawn by the original researcher has sufficient weight to make a methodologically credible contribution to the test of the initial programme theory.38 Zhao et al developed a relevance rating checklist based on the propositions of the initial programme theory with four items: level of relevance with mechanisms, contexts, outcomes and overall contribution to the understanding of interest concept.44 Each item contained four statements to represent four different levels of the strength of relevance, similar to a four-point Likert scale (ie, level 3: high relevance; level 0: no relevance) (online supplemental additional file 2). We will use this checklist to evaluate the relevance of the included literatures and group highly relevant papers (ie, three points for the overall level of relevance score) as a priority for data extraction such that they provided abundant information to understand the mechanisms of web-based interventions in improving the transition of CCSs.

For rigour assessment, Joanna Briggs Institute (JBI) standardised appraisal tools will be used based on study design, such as qualitative and quantitative studies, reviews, case studies and expert consensus. For example, the JBI Critical Appraisal Checklist for Qualitative Research will be used for quality assessment of qualitative research (online supplemental additional file 3). This rating instrument assesses the potential bias to be yes, no, unclear or not applicable for 10 domains: philosophical perspective, objectives, method and data, data analysis, result representation, researcher culturally and theoretically, potential influence of researchers, typicality of participants, ethics and conclusion.

All documents will be systematically screened. Information will be captured first in the general data-extraction form. The general data-extraction form will be used to record information per study regarding general information (author, year, origin), characteristics of the included population (years old, sample size, diagnosis), research design, main results and rationale for this review (table 1).

Definition of the concepts in the ICAMO configurations

Two reviewers (JM and JL) will immerse themselves into the text to become familiarised and independently identify relationship statements that reflect the potential connection between any two of the ICAMO configurations. The two reviewers will then meet, compare and integrate the relationship statements to generate a set of refined relationship statements for each paper and extract the corresponding ICAMO elements.

Data synthesis and framework development

The extracted data will be used to develop a framework for a web-based intervention to improve the transition of CCSs. In the data synthesis phase, we (JM, JL, WH and HW) will hold a working group meeting to discuss the initial ICAMO configurations and generate a set of refined ICAMO configurations and a refined programme theory. We will use three strategies to assist data synthesis: juxtaposition, reconciliation and adjudication.46 When some studies revealed underlying mechanisms or intervention strategies, some focused on outcomes and others described context or role of actors. We will juxtapose data sources to build theoretical understanding. When contradictory findings are found between studies, we will reconcile data sources to identify contextual and implementation differences to explain the opposing outcomes. The synthesis results will combine theoretical understanding and empirical evidence, focusing on explaining the relationship between the context, in which the web-based intervention is applied, the mechanisms by which it works and the outcomes produced.

Patient and public involvement

Patients and/or the public were not involved in the design, or conduct, or reporting, or dissemination plans of this research.

Discussion

Guided by realist synthesis, this review aims to explore different perspectives on the transition of CCSs and synthesise evidence into a new framework. The framework can contribute to the understanding of the integrity of the implementation chain before constructing web-based interventions, examining which mechanistic factors need to be triggered for successful transition and noting and examining the flows, blockages and points of contention to shape or refine intervention to adapt to local circumstances.

As mentioned before, as early as 2005, reports called the attention of the healthcare transition of cancer survivors.5 43 However, the development of transition interventions is still in its infancy, and more high-quality, adequately powered and well-reported transition interventions are needed. Schwartz et al emphasised that the lack of transition-related theoretical frameworks hindered efforts to identify targets of intervention and measure outcomes.10 SMART was then developed. Although similar to the mid-range transition theory, SMART deepens our understanding of the concept ‘transition’ and the processes, but it does not seem to provide sufficient support for construction of transition interventions. Therefore, we need to think carefully about the limitations of existing frameworks in structuring interventions. Frameworks are needed for research and quality improvement to delineate variables for measurements for interventions focused on a comprehensive transition model.

Technology-based applications have been preferred in recent years, and AYAs have integrated mobile devices, such as smartphones, into their lifestyles.35–37 With 95% of adolescents now having a smartphone or access to one, nearly half report being online on a near-constant basis, and more than 20% report downloading a health-related mobile application.31 Despite that past frameworks did not anticipate the potential of mobile technologies in gaining momentum as a new mechanism to provide transition support strategies, scholars have worked hard to explore the transition needs of CCSs from a variety of perspectives in the last decade47; the results may provide sufficient contextual and intervention-related information for constructing frameworks suitable to specific circumstances.28 Therefore, given the advantages of web-based interventions in terms of accessibility and flexibility in this population, we will integrate the core components of transition intervention into a web-based approach in the framework development process. Considering the current COVID-19 pandemic, with social distancing becoming the ‘new normal’, the opportunity for CCSs to receive effective and timely transition interventions and augment traditional intervention delivery models is an important feature of the novel design of this review.

The traditional systematic review provides little or no clue as to causal mechanisms when applied in different contexts or circumstances, deployed by different stakeholders or used for different purposes. At the same time, the hallmark of realist inquiry is its distinctive understanding of causality.38 Therefore, this review first uses realism in the transition management of CCSs. It is methodologically novel and adds to the literature on the use of realist-based approaches to inform the development of programmes. The findings can also enable decision-makers to understand the web-based transition intervention and how it can be made to work most effectively.

Potential limitations

The proposed work may have several limitations. As in previous reviews, we will be reliant on peer-reviewed published data. While this will ensure a focused, and therefore manageable, number of studies are reviewed, it does make the review vulnerable to publication bias.48 In addition, given the specificity of the transition concept, we will not conduct a search of the literature prior to 2005, which may overlook some earlier published studies, (ie, previous reviews have included relevant studies published as early as 1970).

Ethics and dissemination

As a review, ethical approval is not required. The results from this study will be presented at international conferences and disseminated through peer-reviewed publications. Patients and the public will be involved in the dissemination plans.

Basic sections of ℒ ​ 𝒜 \displaystyle\mathcal{LA} -groupoids
Antonio Maglio, Fabricio Valencia

Abstract
. We define the notion of basic section of an ℒ ​ 𝒜 \displaystyle\mathcal{LA} -groupoid whose core-anchor map is injective. Such a notion turns out to be Morita invariant, so that it provides a simpler model for the sections of the stacky Lie algebroids presented by such ℒ ​ 𝒜 \displaystyle\mathcal{LA} -groupoids, yet equivalent to the well-known model provided by their multiplicative sections.



1.
Introduction

Lie groupoids constitute a framework which has received much attention in recent years since they generalize manifolds, Lie groups, Lie group actions, submersions, foliations, pseudogroups, vector bundles, and principal bundles, among others, thus providing a new perspective on classical geometric questions and results. These geometric objects can be viewed as an intermediate step in defining differentiable stacks, which are spaces admitting singularities and generalizing manifolds, orbifolds and leaf spaces of singular foliations. Recently, Lie groupoids equipped with geometric structures suitably compatible with Morita equivalence have been the object of intense research. This is due to the fact that such structures descend to the quotient stack of a Lie groupoid, giving rise to the extension of several geometric notions over singular orbit spaces. For instance, the notion of Morita equivalence of
\displaystyle VB
-groupoids plays a role in defining vector bundles over differentiable stacks
[
7
]
. Additionally, the space of multiplicative sections of an
\displaystyle\mathcal{LA}
-groupoid has the structure of a Lie 2-algebra which is Morita invariant, so that the space of vector fields on a differentiable stack has attached a natural structure of Lie
\displaystyle 2
-algebra
[
20
]
. In particular, if we restrict our attention to the case of differentiable stacks presented by foliation groupoids then there is a simpler model for the stacky vector fields which is constructed in terms of a notion of basic vector field
[
11
]
. The injectivity assumption imposed on the anchor map of a foliation groupoid turns out to be closely related to the notion of
\displaystyle 0
-shifted symplectic structure. In this regard, basic vector fields have been used to describe a Morita invariant reduction of Lie groupoids equipped with
\displaystyle 0
-shifted symplectic structures under Hamiltonian actions of foliation Lie
\displaystyle 2
-groups
[
11
]
.

Building upon the idea of basic vector field on a foliation groupoid, in this short paper we generalize the related constructions from
[
11
]
regarding this notion to study the space of sections of an
\displaystyle\mathcal{LA}
-groupoid whose core-anchor map is injective. Surprisingly, we can introduce a simpler approach to describe basic sections over this sort of
\displaystyle\mathcal{LA}
-groupoids by applying some of the general machinery developed in
[
7
,
16
,
20
]
.

Our main result can be summarized in the following way.

Theorem
.

Let
\displaystyle(V\rightrightarrows E;G\rightrightarrows M)
be an
\displaystyle\mathcal{LA}
-groupoid with core
\displaystyle C
whose core-anchor map
\displaystyle\partial\colon C\to E
is injective. Then, there exists a Lie algebra structure on the space of basic sections on
\displaystyle E/\partial(C)
which is invariant under Morita equivalence. Furthermore, such a Lie algebra is quasi-isomorphic to the Lie
\displaystyle 2
-algebra of multiplicative sections of
\displaystyle V
.

It is important to stress that this result also applies to describe basic sections of other interesting constructions where multiplicative or basic geometric structures over Lie groupoids yield associated
\displaystyle\mathcal{LA}
-groupoids. Namely, our initial motivation comes from trying to define what would be a basic derivation of a line bundle groupoid, an object that is closely related to the notion of
\displaystyle 0
-shifted contact structure recently introduced in
[
16
]
. This is because those basic derivations remarkably allow us to obtain a reduction procedure for line bundle groupoids equipped with
\displaystyle 0
-shifted contact structures invariant under the action of foliation Lie
\displaystyle 2
-groups
[
17
]
.

The work is structured as follows. In Section
2
, we briefly introduce the fundamental facts about
\displaystyle VB
-groupoids that we shall be using throughout our study, focusing on defining the Lie 2-algebra of multiplicative sections associated with any
\displaystyle\mathcal{LA}
-groupoid. In Section
3
, we describe the notion of basic sections of a
\displaystyle VB
-groupoid whose core-anchor map is injective, proving that, in the case of an
\displaystyle\mathcal{LA}
-groupoid, the space of all basic sections inherits a natural Lie algebra structure. Such a Lie algebra is Morita invariant, so that it can be thought of as the space of sections of the stacky Lie algebroid presented by this kind of
\displaystyle\mathcal{LA}
-groupoid. We also show that our approach provides a model for stacky sections which is still equivalent to the well-known model provided by multiplicative sections of
\displaystyle\mathcal{LA}
-groupoids. Finally, in Section
4
, we illustrate our constructions with several interesting examples where the main result of this work applies. Appendix
A
contains some basic facts about Lie algebroids which are used throughout the sections.

2.
\displaystyle\mathcal{LA}
-groupoids and their multiplicative sections

In this short section we briefly introduce the elementary notions and constructions that we will be using throughout the work. The reader is recommended to consult
[
9
,
20
]
for specific details.

We think of a
\displaystyle VB
-groupoid
as a vector bundle internal to the category of Lie groupoids. That is to say, a
\displaystyle VB
-groupoid is a commutative diagram

\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M}

where
\displaystyle V\rightrightarrows E
and

are Lie groupoids,

are vector bundles (

\displaystyle V
are
\displaystyle VB

. We denote by
\displaystyle s,t,u,m
and

the source, target, unit, composition and inversion maps of
\displaystyle G
and by
\displaystyle\tilde{s},\tilde{t},\tilde{u},\tilde{m}
and
\displaystyle\tilde{i}
the corresponding ones of
\displaystyle V
. Moreover, we denote the
\displaystyle VB
-groupoid (

\displaystyle(V\rightrightarrows E;G\rightrightarrows M)
or only as
\displaystyle V
if there is not risk of confusion. The
core

of a
\displaystyle VB
-groupoid
\displaystyle V
is the vector bundle
\displaystyle C

\displaystyle M
given by

C=\ker(\tilde{s})|_{M}.

The
core-anchor map
of
\displaystyle V
is the
\displaystyle VB
-morphism given by the restriction of the target
\displaystyle\tilde{t}
to the core
\displaystyle C
. This will be denoted by
\displaystyle\partial\colon C\to E
. Accordingly, the
core complex
of
\displaystyle V
is the
\displaystyle 2
-term complex given by the core-anchor map:

\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0.}
\displaystyle\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\partial}

Any section
\displaystyle c
of the core
\displaystyle C
of a
\displaystyle VB
-groupoid
\displaystyle V
determines a
right invariant section

\displaystyle\overrightarrow{c}
and a
left invariant section

\displaystyle\overleftarrow{c}
of
\displaystyle V
by setting

\overrightarrow{c}_{g}=c_{t(g)}\cdot 0_{g}\in V_{g}\quad\text{and}\quad\overleftarrow{c}_{g}=-0_{g}\cdot c_{s(g)}^{-1},\quad g\in G.

A
\displaystyle VB
-groupoid morphism
is a pair
\displaystyle(F,f)\colon(V\rightrightarrows E;G\rightrightarrows M)\to(V^{\prime}\rightrightarrows E^{\prime};G^{\prime}\rightrightarrows M^{\prime})
, where
\displaystyle F\colon(V\rightrightarrows E)\to(V^{\prime}\rightrightarrows E^{\prime})
and
\displaystyle f\colon(G\rightrightarrows M)\to(G^{\prime}\rightrightarrows M^{\prime})
are Lie groupoid morphisms, and
\displaystyle(F,f)\colon(V\to G)\to(V^{\prime}\to G^{\prime})
and
\displaystyle(F,f)\colon(E\to M)\to(E^{\prime}\to M^{\prime})
are vector bundle morphisms. In these terms, a
\displaystyle VB
-Morita map
is defined to be a
\displaystyle VB
-groupoid morphism
\displaystyle(F,f)
such that
\displaystyle F
is also a Morita map, see
[
7
]
. Besides, a
linear natural transformation
between two
\displaystyle VB
-groupoid morphisms
\displaystyle F,K\colon V\to V^{\prime}
is a
\displaystyle VB
-morphism
\displaystyle\alpha\colon E\to V^{\prime}
that is also a natural transformation, compare
[
7
,
16
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

An
\displaystyle\mathcal{LA}
-groupoid
is a
\displaystyle VB
-groupoid
\displaystyle(V\rightrightarrows E;G\rightrightarrows M)
, where both
\displaystyle V
and
\displaystyle E
are Lie algebroids over
\displaystyle G
and
\displaystyle M
, respectively, and all the structure maps of
\displaystyle V
are Lie algebroid morphisms. Additionally, an
\displaystyle\mathcal{LA}
-groupoid morphism
is a
\displaystyle VB
-groupoid morphism
\displaystyle(F,f)
between two
\displaystyle\mathcal{LA}
-groupoids
\displaystyle V
and
\displaystyle V^{\prime}
such that
\displaystyle(F,f)\colon(V\to G)\to(V^{\prime}\to G^{\prime})
and
\displaystyle(F,f)\colon(E\to M)\to(E^{\prime}\to M^{\prime})
are Lie algebroid morphisms.

Let
\displaystyle V
be an
\displaystyle\mathcal{LA}
-groupoid. In this case, the core
\displaystyle C
of
\displaystyle V
possesses a Lie algebroid structure whose anchor map is determined by the composition of the core-anchor map
\displaystyle\partial\colon C\to E
with the anchor
\displaystyle\rho_{E}\colon E\to TM
of
\displaystyle E
, and the bracket
\displaystyle[-,-]_{C}
maps two sections
\displaystyle c,c^{\prime}\in\Gamma(C)
into the unique section of
\displaystyle C
such that
\displaystyle\overrightarrow{[c,c^{\prime}]_{C}}=[\overrightarrow{c},\overrightarrow{c^{\prime}}]_{V}\in\Gamma(V)
. Such an algebroid structure is defined in such a way that the core-anchor map
\displaystyle\partial
becomes a Lie algebroid morphism. Also, an
\displaystyle\mathcal{LA}
-groupoid morphism
\displaystyle(F,f)
from
\displaystyle V
to
\displaystyle V^{\prime}
determines a Lie algebroid morphism from the core
\displaystyle C
of
\displaystyle V
to the core
\displaystyle C^{\prime}
of
\displaystyle V^{\prime}
.

An
\displaystyle\mathcal{LA}
-Morita map
is defined to be an
\displaystyle\mathcal{LA}
-groupoid morphism
\displaystyle(F,f)
such that
\displaystyle F
is also a Morita map, see
[
20
]
. The latter concept induces a notion of Morita equivalence of
\displaystyle\mathcal{LA}
-groupoids by saying that two
\displaystyle\mathcal{LA}
-groupoids
\displaystyle V
and
\displaystyle V^{\prime}
are
Morita equivalent
if there exists a third
\displaystyle\mathcal{LA}
-groupoid
\displaystyle W
and two
\displaystyle\mathcal{LA}
-Morita maps
\displaystyle W\to V
and
\displaystyle W\to V^{\prime}
. This gives rise to an equivalence relation which, in turn, allows us to speak about a notion of
stacky Lie algebroid
by considering the equivalence class of an
\displaystyle\mathcal{LA}
-groupoid up to Morita equivalence. See
[
23
]
for specific details regarding the notion of Lie algebroid over a differentiable stack.

We are interested in looking at the Lie 2-algebra of multiplicative sections of an
\displaystyle\mathcal{LA}
-groupoid, that is the one providing a model for the sections of a stacky Lie algebroid
[
20
]
. A
multiplicative section
of an
\displaystyle\mathcal{LA}
-groupoid
\displaystyle(V\rightrightarrows E;G\rightrightarrows M)
is a pair
\displaystyle(\lambda,e)\in\Gamma(V)\oplus\Gamma(E)
such that
\displaystyle\lambda\colon G\to V
is a Lie groupoid morphism covering
\displaystyle e\colon M\to E
. We denote by
\displaystyle\Gamma_{\operatorname{mult}}(V)
the space of multiplicative sections of
\displaystyle V
. This inherits a Lie algebra structure out of the
\displaystyle\mathcal{LA}
-groupoid structure of
\displaystyle V
. As shown in
[
20
]
, there exists a Lie 2-algebra of multiplicative sections on
\displaystyle V
which can be codified in terms of the following crossed module of Lie algebras. If
\displaystyle\partial\colon C\to E
is the core-anchor map of
\displaystyle V
, then the crossed module of multiplicative sections of
\displaystyle V
is given by

\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Gamma(C)}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Gamma_{\operatorname{mult}}(V)}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Der}(\Gamma(C)),}
\displaystyle\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\delta}
\displaystyle\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D}

where
\displaystyle\delta
maps a section
\displaystyle c\in\Gamma(C)
to the pair
\displaystyle(\overrightarrow{c}-\overleftarrow{c},\partial(c))\in\Gamma(V)\oplus\Gamma(E)
and
\displaystyle D
maps the pair
\displaystyle(\lambda,e)
to the derivation
\displaystyle D_{(\lambda,e)}
defined by
\displaystyle D_{(\lambda,e)}(c)=[\overrightarrow{c},\lambda]|_{M}\in\Gamma(C)
for all
\displaystyle c\in\Gamma(C)
. Remarkably, such a crossed module of Lie algebras is Morita invariant, meaning that
\displaystyle\mathcal{LA}
-groupoids related by an
\displaystyle\mathcal{LA}
-Morita map determine quasi-isomorphic crossed modules of multiplicative sections. In consequence, we can think of the quotient Lie algebra
\displaystyle\Gamma_{\operatorname{mult}}(V)/\textnormal{im}(\delta)
as the space of sections of the stacky Lie algebroid presented by
\displaystyle V
.

Remark 2.2
.

We note that a broader notion, providing an alternative candidate for the concept of a Lie algebroid over a differentiable stack, has been proposed recently in
[
1
]
. In that work, the authors introduced the notion of
quasi
\displaystyle\mathcal{LA}
-groupoids
, arguing that these objects furnish the appropriate framework for defining Lie algebroids over differentiable stacks.

3.
The Lie algebra of basic sections

In this part of the work, we introduce the notion of a basic section for a
\displaystyle VB
-groupoid
\displaystyle V
with injective core-anchor map, and we discuss the Lie algebra structure carried by the space of basic sections in the case where
\displaystyle V
is an
\displaystyle\mathcal{LA}
-groupoid. Firstly, this will allow us to provide a simpler model for the sections of the stacky Lie algebroids presented by
\displaystyle\mathcal{LA}
-groupoids with injective core-anchor map. Secondly, such a notion of basic section generalizes the notion of basic vector field over foliation groupoids introduced and studied in
[
11
]
, but still applies to other interesting constructions where multiplicative or basic geometric structures over Lie groupoids give rise to associated
\displaystyle\mathcal{LA}
-groupoids.

We begin with the following standard result on
\displaystyle VB
-groupoids.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\displaystyle(V\rightrightarrows E;G\rightrightarrows M)
be a
\displaystyle VB
-groupoid. For any arrow
\displaystyle g\colon x\to y
in
\displaystyle G
, we get that

•

the linear isomorphism
\displaystyle\ker\tilde{s}_{y}\to\ker\tilde{s}_{g}
,
\displaystyle u_{y}\mapsto u_{y}\cdot 0_{g}
induces the following linear isomorphisms

\ker\tilde{s}_{y}\cap\ker\tilde{t}_{y}\to\ker\tilde{s}_{g}\cap\ker\tilde{t}_{g},\quad\frac{\ker\tilde{s}_{y}}{\ker\tilde{s}_{y}\cap\ker\tilde{t}_{y}}\to\frac{\ker\tilde{s}_{g}}{\ker\tilde{s}_{g}\cap\ker\tilde{t}_{g}},

•

the linear isomorphism
\displaystyle\ker\tilde{s}_{x}\to\ker\tilde{t}_{g}
,
\displaystyle u_{x}\mapsto 0_{g}\cdot u_{x}^{-1}
induces the following linear isomorphisms

\ker\tilde{s}_{x}\cap\ker\tilde{t}_{x}\to\ker\tilde{s}_{g}\cap\ker\tilde{t}_{g},\quad\frac{\ker\tilde{s}_{x}}{\ker\tilde{s}_{x}\cap\ker\tilde{t}_{x}}\to\frac{\ker\tilde{s}_{g}}{\ker\tilde{s}_{g}\cap\ker\tilde{t}_{g}}.

In particular, the core-anchor map of
\displaystyle V
is injective if and only if
\displaystyle\ker\tilde{s}_{g}\cap\ker\tilde{t}_{g}=\{0_{g}\}
for all
\displaystyle g\in G
.

It follows from Lemma
3.1
that the core-anchor map
\displaystyle\partial
of a
\displaystyle VB
-groupoid
\displaystyle V
is injective if and only if
\displaystyle\ker\tilde{s}\cap\ker\tilde{t}=0
, and, in this case,
\displaystyle\ker\tilde{s}+\ker\tilde{t}=\ker\tilde{s}\oplus\ker\tilde{t}
is a vector subbundle of
\displaystyle V
. This turns out to be a Lie subalgebroid of
\displaystyle V
, provided that
\displaystyle V
is an
\displaystyle\mathcal{LA}
-groupoid.

From now on we only consider
\displaystyle VB
-groupoid (resp.
\displaystyle\mathcal{LA}
-groupoids)
\displaystyle(V\rightrightarrows E;G\rightrightarrows M)
with injective core-anchor map
\displaystyle\partial\colon C\to E
. In this situation, we may identify
\displaystyle C
with
\displaystyle\operatorname{im}(\partial)
that is a vector subbundle (resp. Lie subalgebroid) of
\displaystyle E
. Notice that the injectivity of the core-anchor is a Morita-invariant property, as recalled below.

.

\displaystyle F
be a
\displaystyle VB

\displaystyle VB
-groupoids
\displaystyle V
and
\displaystyle V^{\prime}
. If the core-anchor map of either
\displaystyle V
or
\displaystyle V^{\prime}
is injective, then so is the core-anchor map of the other. This is because, by Theorem 3.5 in
[
7
]
,
\displaystyle F
determines a pointwise quasi-isomorphism between the core complexes of
\displaystyle V
and
\displaystyle V^{\prime}
:

\displaystyle\Gamma_{0}(E)
. We can use the facts explained in Appendix

\displaystyle A_{1}=V

\displaystyle A_{0}=E

\displaystyle\Phi

Remark 3.9
.

If
\displaystyle V
and
\displaystyle V^{\prime}
are
\displaystyle\mathcal{LA}
-groupoid morphisms whose core-anchor maps are injective and
\displaystyle F\colon V\to V^{\prime}
is an
\displaystyle\mathcal{LA}
-Morita map, then the linear map
\displaystyle F^{\ast}\colon\Gamma_{\operatorname{bas}}(V^{\prime})\to\Gamma_{\operatorname{bas}}(V)
is a Lie algebra morphism. Indeed, let
\displaystyle X^{\prime},Y^{\prime}\in\Gamma(E^{\prime})
such that
\displaystyle\overline{X^{\prime}},\overline{Y^{\prime}}\in\Gamma_{\operatorname{bas}}(V^{\prime})
and
\displaystyle x\in M
. Pick
\displaystyle X,Y\in\Gamma(E)
such that
\displaystyle F\circ X=X^{\prime}\circ f
and
\displaystyle F\circ Y=Y^{\prime}\circ f
around
\displaystyle x
(compare Proposition 7.3 in
[
20
]
). Then, we get that

F^{\ast}([\overline{X^{\prime}},\overline{Y^{\prime}}])_{x}=F_{x}^{-1}(\overline{[X^{\prime},Y^{\prime}]_{f(x)}})=\overline{[X,Y]}_{x}=[\overline{X},\overline{Y}]_{x}=[F^{\ast}\overline{X^{\prime}},F^{\ast}\overline{Y^{\prime}}]_{x}.

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
\displaystyle V
and
\displaystyle V^{\prime}
be two
\displaystyle VB
-groupoids with injective core-anchor maps and let
\displaystyle F,K\colon V\to V^{\prime}
be two
\displaystyle VB
-Morita maps covering the same map
\displaystyle f\colon G\to G^{\prime}
. If
\displaystyle F
and
\displaystyle K
are related by a linear natural transformation, then the linear maps
\displaystyle F^{\ast}
and
\displaystyle K^{\ast}\colon\Gamma_{\operatorname{bas}}(V^{\prime})\to\Gamma_{\operatorname{bas}}(V)
agree.

Proof.

Suppose that
\displaystyle\alpha\colon E\to V^{\prime}
is a linear natural transformation from
\displaystyle F
to
\displaystyle K
. By Theorem 3.8 in
[
16
]
we get a pointwise homotopy

\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C^{\prime}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E^{\prime}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0,}
\displaystyle\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\partial}
\displaystyle\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F}
\displaystyle\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K}
\displaystyle\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F}
\displaystyle\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K}
\displaystyle\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\partial^{\prime}}

between the cochain maps determined by
\displaystyle F
and
\displaystyle K
, so that the claim follows.
∎

As an easy consequence of Remark
3.9
and Lemma
3.10
one deduces that:

Corollary 3.11
.

Let
\displaystyle V
and
\displaystyle V^{\prime}
be two
\displaystyle\mathcal{LA}
-groupoids with injective core-anchor maps and let
\displaystyle F,K\colon V\to V^{\prime}
be two
\displaystyle\mathcal{LA}
-Morita maps covering the same map
\displaystyle f\colon G\to G^{\prime}
. If
\displaystyle F
and
\displaystyle K
are related by a linear natural transformation, then the Lie algebra morphisms
\displaystyle F^{\ast}
and
\displaystyle K^{\ast}\colon\Gamma_{\operatorname{bas}}(V^{\prime})\to\Gamma_{\operatorname{bas}}(V)
agree.

More importantly, in view of Remark
3.2
, we obtain the following result.

Proposition 3.12
.

Let
\displaystyle(F,f)\colon(V\rightrightarrows E;G\rightrightarrows M)\to(V^{\prime}\rightrightarrows E^{\prime};G^{\prime}\rightrightarrows M^{\prime})
be a
\displaystyle VB
-Morita map between two
\displaystyle VB
-groupoids, one of which has injective core-anchor map. Then, the pullback map
\displaystyle F^{\ast}\colon\Gamma_{\operatorname{bas}}(V^{\prime})\to\Gamma_{\operatorname{bas}}(V)
is a linear isomorphism.

Proof.

We consider first the case when
\displaystyle f=\operatorname{id}_{G}
. In this scenario, by Proposition 6.2 in
[
7
]
, there exists a
\displaystyle VB
-groupoid morphism
\displaystyle K\colon V^{\prime}\to V
covering
\displaystyle\operatorname{id}_{G}
and linear natural transformations
\displaystyle K\circ F\Rightarrow\operatorname{id}_{V}
and
\displaystyle F\circ K\Rightarrow\operatorname{id}_{V^{\prime}}
. By Lemma
3.10
we obtain that
\displaystyle(K\circ F)^{\ast}=F^{\ast}\circ K^{\ast}
agrees with the identity
\displaystyle\operatorname{id}_{\Gamma_{\operatorname{bas}}(V)}
and that
\displaystyle(F\circ K)^{\ast}=K^{\ast}\circ F^{\ast}
agrees with the identity
\displaystyle\operatorname{id}_{\Gamma_{\operatorname{bas}}(V^{\prime})}
, so that
\displaystyle F^{\ast}
is an isomorphism.

If we consider the situation for general
\displaystyle f\colon G\to G^{\prime}
, then we may look at the pullback
\displaystyle VB
-groupoid
\displaystyle f^{\ast}V^{\prime}
over
\displaystyle G
which is given by
\displaystyle G\times_{G^{\prime}}V^{\prime}\rightrightarrows M\times_{M^{\prime}}E^{\prime}
. The core complex of
\displaystyle f^{\ast}V^{\prime}
is

\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f^{\ast}C^{\prime}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f^{\ast}E^{\prime}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0,}

where the map
\displaystyle f^{\ast}C^{\prime}\to f^{\ast}E^{\prime}
is essentially given by
\displaystyle\partial^{\prime}
and so it is injective. In particular:

N:=f^{\ast}E^{\prime}/f^{\ast}C^{\prime}\cong f^{\ast}\left(E^{\prime}/C^{\prime}\right)=f^{\ast}N_{0}^{\prime}.

This implies that
\displaystyle\Gamma(N)=\Gamma(N_{0})
and therefore
\displaystyle\Gamma_{\operatorname{bas}}(f^{\ast}V^{\prime})=\Gamma_{\operatorname{bas}}(V^{\prime})
. The
\displaystyle VB
-groupoid
\displaystyle f^{\ast}V^{\prime}
comes with a
\displaystyle VB
-groupoid morphism
\displaystyle\mathsf{F}\colon V\to f^{\ast}V^{\prime}
covering
\displaystyle\operatorname{id}_{G}
that is defined by the expression

\mathsf{F}(v)=(g,F(v))\in G\times_{G^{\prime}}V^{\prime},\quad v\in V_{g}.

Hence, by Theorem 3.5 in
[
7
]
it holds that
\displaystyle\mathsf{F}
is a
\displaystyle VB
-Morita map covering the identity, thus obtaining that
\displaystyle\mathsf{F}^{\ast}\colon\Gamma_{\operatorname{bas}}(f^{\ast}V^{\prime})\to\Gamma_{\operatorname{bas}}(V)
is an isomorphism. However, because of the equivalence
\displaystyle\Gamma_{\operatorname{bas}}(f^{\ast}V^{\prime})=\Gamma_{\operatorname{bas}}(V^{\prime})
we conclude that the maps
\displaystyle\mathsf{F}^{\ast}
and
\displaystyle F^{\ast}
coincide and so the result follows, as desired.
∎

It is clear that if
\displaystyle(F,f)\colon(V\rightrightarrows E;G\rightrightarrows M)\to(V^{\prime}\rightrightarrows E^{\prime};G^{\prime}\rightrightarrows M^{\prime})
is instead an
\displaystyle\mathcal{LA}
-Morita map between two
\displaystyle\mathcal{LA}
-groupoids, one of which has injective core-anchor map, then
\displaystyle F^{\ast}\colon\Gamma_{\operatorname{bas}}(V^{\prime})\to\Gamma_{\operatorname{bas}}(V)
is a Lie algebra isomorphism. Even more, we can now easily deduce the following result.

Corollary 3.13
.

If
\displaystyle V
and
\displaystyle V^{\prime}
are two Morita equivalent
\displaystyle\mathcal{LA}
-groupoids, then
\displaystyle\Gamma_{\operatorname{bas}}(V)\cong\Gamma_{\operatorname{bas}}(V^{\prime})
as Lie algebras.

In consequence, the Lie algebra of basic sections
\displaystyle\Gamma_{\operatorname{bas}}(V)
gives rise to a model for the sections of the stacky Lie algebroid presented by the
\displaystyle\mathcal{LA}
-groupoid
\displaystyle V
, provided that its core-anchor map is injective.

As alluded to previously, the Lie 2-algebra of multiplicative sections of an
\displaystyle\mathcal{LA}
-groupoid also provides a model for the sections of the corresponding stacky Lie algebroid
[
20
]
, and these two models turns out to be equivalent, as the following result shows.

Proposition 3.14
.

Let
\displaystyle V
be an
\displaystyle\mathcal{LA}
-groupoid whose core-anchor map is injective. Then, the map

\Psi\colon\frac{\Gamma_{\operatorname{mult}}(V)}{\textnormal{im}(\delta)}\to\Gamma_{\operatorname{bas}}(V),\quad\overline{(\lambda,e)}\mapsto\overline{e}

is a Lie algebra isomorphism.

Proof.

From the definition of the Lie algebra structures on
\displaystyle\Gamma_{\operatorname{mult}}(V)/\textnormal{im}(\delta)
and
\displaystyle\Gamma_{\operatorname{bas}}(V)
, it easily follows that
\displaystyle\Psi
is a Lie algebra morphism. On the one hand, by Remark
3.7
, we know that
\displaystyle\Gamma_{\operatorname{bas}}(V)
agrees with
\displaystyle\Gamma(E/C)^{G}
, as vector spaces. The isomorphism is just the identity of
\displaystyle\Gamma(E/C)
, noting that a section
\displaystyle\overline{X}\in\Gamma(E/C)
is basic with respect to
\displaystyle V
if and only if
\displaystyle\overline{X}
is invariant with respect to the representation of
\displaystyle G
described by the
\displaystyle VB
-groupoid
\displaystyle V^{\prime}
. On the other hand, by Remark
3.2
,
\displaystyle V
is Morita equivalent (at least as a
\displaystyle VB
-groupoid) to the trivial core
\displaystyle VB
-groupoid
\displaystyle V^{\prime}
with side bundle
\displaystyle E/C
. But, Remark 3.2 in
[
20
]
says that the vector space of multiplicative sections of
\displaystyle V^{\prime}
is also given by
\displaystyle\Gamma(E/C)^{G}
. Therefore, the quotient vector space
\displaystyle\Gamma_{\operatorname{mult}}(V)/\textnormal{im}(\delta)
is isomorphic to
\displaystyle\Gamma(E/C)^{G}
, compare Corollary 7.2 in
[
20
]
, and the isomorphism is given exactly by
\displaystyle\Psi
. This completes the proof.
∎

4.
Examples

In this final section we exhibit some interesting examples where our results apply.

Example 4.1

(Basic vector fields)
.

A
foliation groupoid
is a Lie groupoid
\displaystyle G\rightrightarrows M
whose anchor map
\displaystyle\rho\colon A\to TM
is injective. They naturally show up as integrations of regular foliations. The tangent Lie groupoid
\displaystyle TG\rightrightarrows TM
is an
\displaystyle\mathcal{LA}
-groupoid over
\displaystyle G\rightrightarrows M
whose core-anchor map agrees with
\displaystyle\rho
and so it is injective. Considering basic sections of the tangent
\displaystyle\mathcal{LA}
-groupoid one recovers the notion of
basic vector field
on foliation groupoids. These were studied and exploited in the work
[
11
]
which turned out to be a source of inspiration for our generalization. First, any Lie groupoid admitting a
\displaystyle 0
-shifted symplectic structure is a foliation groupoid. In particular, basic vector fields are used in
[
11
]
to get a Morita invariant reduction of Lie groupoids equipped with
\displaystyle 0
-shifted symplectic structures under Hamiltonian actions of foliation Lie
\displaystyle 2
-groups. Second, if we endow our foliation groupoid with a Riemannian 2-metric
[
6
]
then the idea of basic Killing vector field gives rise to a notion of Killing vector field over the corresponding Riemannian stack
[
10
]
.

Example 4.2

(Basic derivations)
.

Our initial motivation for this work was trying to define what would be a basic derivation of a line bundle groupoid (line bundle internal to the category of Lie groupoids), an object that is closely related to the notion of
\displaystyle 0
-shifted contact structure recently introduced in
[
16
]
. If
\displaystyle E
is a representation of a Lie groupoid
\displaystyle G\rightrightarrows M
, then the action groupoid
\displaystyle G\ltimes E\rightrightarrows E
is a
\displaystyle VB
-groupoid with trivial core over
\displaystyle M
. We denote such a
\displaystyle VB
-groupoid by
\displaystyle V\rightrightarrows E
. Consider the Atiyah algebroids
\displaystyle DV\to G
and
\displaystyle DE\to M
of
\displaystyle V
and
\displaystyle E
, respectively. Since the core of
\displaystyle V
is trivial, we can apply the Atiyah functor
\displaystyle D
to the structure maps of
\displaystyle V
, thus obtaining the
Atiyah
\displaystyle\mathcal{LA}
-groupoid

\displaystyle(DV\rightrightarrows DE;G\rightrightarrows M)
(compare
[
8
]
). When the core-anchor map of
\displaystyle DV
is injective we get a model for the
basic derivations
of
\displaystyle V
. A particular case is given when
\displaystyle E=L_{M}
is a line bundle over
\displaystyle M
, and so
\displaystyle V=L
is a line bundle over
\displaystyle G
. More importantly, a line bundle groupoid
\displaystyle(L\rightrightarrows L_{M};G\rightrightarrows M)
admitting a
\displaystyle 0
-shifted contact structure forces its corresponding Atiyah
\displaystyle\mathcal{LA}
-groupoid
\displaystyle DL\rightrightarrows DL_{M}
to have injective core-anchor map
[
16
]
. Therefore, we end up with a notion of
basic derivation
of the line bundle groupoid
\displaystyle L
which is expected to allow us to obtain a reduction procedure for line bundle groupoids equipped with
\displaystyle 0
-shifted contact structures invariant under the action of foliation Lie
\displaystyle 2
-groups, see
[
17
]
.

Example 4.3

(Basic
\displaystyle 1
-forms on transitive Poisson groupoids)
.

A
Poisson groupoid
is a Lie groupoid
\displaystyle G\rightrightarrows M
endowed with a suitably compatible Poisson structure on
\displaystyle G
. In other words, it supports a Poisson bivector
\displaystyle\pi
on
\displaystyle G
such that
\displaystyle\pi^{\sharp}\colon T^{\ast}G\to TG
is a
\displaystyle VB
-groupoid morphism from the cotangent
\displaystyle VB
-groupoid to the tangent
\displaystyle VB
-groupoid. Poisson groupoids appear naturally as the global counterparts of
Lie bialgebroids
, see
[
14
]
. It is known that if
\displaystyle G\rightrightarrows M
is a Poisson groupoid, then the dual
\displaystyle A^{\ast}
can be equipped with a Lie algebroid structure, where
\displaystyle A
is the Lie algebroid of
\displaystyle G
. Actually, the cotangent
\displaystyle VB
-groupoid
\displaystyle T^{\ast}G\rightrightarrows A^{\ast}
is an
\displaystyle\mathcal{LA}
-groupoid whose core-anchor map is
\displaystyle\rho^{\ast}\colon T^{\ast}M\to A^{\ast}
, the dual of the anchor
\displaystyle\rho
of
\displaystyle A
. If
\displaystyle G
is transitive then
\displaystyle\rho
is surjective, so that
\displaystyle T^{\ast}G
turns into an
\displaystyle\mathcal{LA}
-groupoid whose core-anchor map is injective. Transitive Poisson groupoids and transitive Lie bialgebroids were already studied in
[
4
]
. Hence, we get a model for the
basic 1-forms
on
\displaystyle G
, provided that it is a transitive Poisson groupoid.

Example 4.4

(Basic
\displaystyle 1
-jets on Jacobi groupoids)
.

Similarly to the previous example, we can consider the situation where we have a Jacobi groupoid. A
Jacobi groupoid
is a Lie groupoid
\displaystyle G\rightrightarrows M
endowed with a suitably compatible Jacobi structure on
\displaystyle G
. These turn out to be the global counterparts of generalized Lie bialgebroids, consult
[
5
,
12
]
. Adopting the line bundle point of view (see, e.g.,
[
22
]
), a Jacobi groupoid is a
\displaystyle VB
-groupoid
\displaystyle(L\rightrightarrows L_{M};G\rightrightarrows M)
, where
\displaystyle L\to G
and
\displaystyle L_{M}\to M
are line bundles equipped with a Jacobi bivector
\displaystyle\mathsf{J}\colon\wedge^{2}J^{1}L\to L
that is compatible with the
\displaystyle VB
-groupoid structures involved. Here
\displaystyle J^{1}L
stands for the first jet bundle of
\displaystyle L
and the compatibility condition means that the sharp map
\displaystyle\mathsf{J}^{\sharp}\colon J^{1}L\to DL
is a
\displaystyle VB
-groupoid morphism. Note that
\displaystyle DL\rightrightarrows DL_{M}
is the Atiyah
\displaystyle\mathcal{LA}
-groupoid over
\displaystyle G\rightrightarrows M
introduced in Example
4.2
and
\displaystyle J^{1}L\rightrightarrows A^{\dagger}=A^{\ast}\otimes L_{M}
is the
\displaystyle VB
-groupoid over
\displaystyle G\rightrightarrows M
obtained as the dual of
\displaystyle DL\rightrightarrows DL_{M}
tensor
\displaystyle L\rightrightarrows L_{M}
(see Example 3.1 in
[
16
]
or Remark 2.1.36 in
[
15
]
). It is worth stressing that the Lie algebroid
\displaystyle A
of
\displaystyle G
is identified with the core of
\displaystyle DL
and that we are using the canonical isomorphism
\displaystyle J^{1}L\cong DL^{\ast}\otimes L
. Furthermore, this definition is slightly different from the one that appears in
[
12
]
, but, in the case when
\displaystyle L=\mathbbm{R}_{G}
and
\displaystyle L_{M}=\mathbbm{R}_{M}
are the trivial line bundles, the two definitions coincide.

The Jacobi structure on
\displaystyle G
determines a Lie algebroid structure on
\displaystyle A^{\dagger}
, just as it occurs for the case of Poisson groupoids (see Section 5.2 in
[
12
]
for the trivial line bundle case). Actually,
\displaystyle J^{1}L\rightrightarrows A^{\dagger}
is an
\displaystyle\mathcal{LA}
-groupoid over
\displaystyle G
. Its core-anchor map
\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{\dagger}\colon J^{1}L_{M}\to A^{\dagger}
is given by the twisted dual of the core-anchor map
\displaystyle\mathcal{D}\colon A\to DL_{M}
of
\displaystyle DL
. Hence, if
\displaystyle\mathcal{D}
is surjective, then
\displaystyle J^{1}L
is an
\displaystyle\mathcal{LA}
-groupoid with injective core-anchor map, thus obtaining a model of
basic
\displaystyle 1
-jets
on
\displaystyle L
.

Remark 4.5
.

Particular cases of the last two examples are symplectic and contact groupoids (see, e.g., Section 3.2 in
[
2
]
for a definition of contact groupoids that involves line bundles). In both cases we get that, if the core-anchor map of
\displaystyle T^{\ast}G
(resp.
\displaystyle J^{1}L
) is injective, then it is also surjective and there are no non-trivial basic
\displaystyle 1
-forms (resp. non-trivial basic
\displaystyle 1
-jets) neither non-trivial basic vector fields (resp. non-trivial basic derivations). Indeed, a compatible symplectic structure on a Lie groupoid
\displaystyle G\rightrightarrows M
is given by a compatible Poisson structure
\displaystyle\pi
such that the
\displaystyle VB
-groupoid morphism
\displaystyle\pi^{\sharp}\colon T^{\ast}G\to TG
is invertible. Then,
\displaystyle\pi^{\sharp}
determines the following isomorphism

\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T^{\ast}M}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A^{\ast}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces TM}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0,}
\displaystyle\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho^{\ast}}
\displaystyle\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi^{\sharp}}
\displaystyle\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi^{\sharp}}
\displaystyle\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho}

between the core complexes of
\displaystyle T^{\ast}G
and
\displaystyle TG
. Analogously, a compatible contact structure is given by a
\displaystyle VB
-groupoid
\displaystyle L\rightrightarrows L_{M}
over
\displaystyle G
, where
\displaystyle L
and
\displaystyle L_{M}
are line bundles, together with a compatible Jacobi structure
\displaystyle\mathsf{J}
such that
\displaystyle\mathsf{J}^{\sharp}\colon J^{1}L\to DL
is invertible. Once again,
\displaystyle\mathsf{J}^{\sharp}
determines an isomorphism

\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces J^{1}L_{M}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A^{\dagger}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces DL_{M}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
\displaystyle\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{D}^{\dagger}}
\displaystyle\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{J}^{\sharp}}
\displaystyle\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{J}^{\sharp}}
\displaystyle\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{D}}

between the core complexes of
\displaystyle J^{1}L
and
\displaystyle DL
. It is clear that, if
\displaystyle\rho
(resp.
\displaystyle\mathcal{D}
) is surjective, then it is also injective. Hence
\displaystyle\rho
and
\displaystyle\rho^{\ast}
(resp.
\displaystyle\mathcal{D}
and
\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{\dagger}
) are isomorphisms. In other words, both
\displaystyle TG
and
\displaystyle T^{\ast}G
(resp.
\displaystyle DL
and
\displaystyle J^{1}L
) are
\displaystyle\mathcal{LA}
-groupoids with an isomorphism as core-anchor map, so that the claim follows from Remark
3.7
.

We finish this work by pointing out that there are other interesting examples of
\displaystyle\mathcal{LA}
-groupoids with injective core-anchor map associated to multiplicative geometric structures on Lie groupoids, meaning that we can also get a notion of basic section for those structures. For instance,
multiplicative Dirac structure
give rise to examples of
\displaystyle\mathcal{LA}
-groupoids
[
19
]
. Similarly, additional examples can be obtained by introducing a notion of
multiplicative Dirac-Jacobi structure
after imposing some multiplicativity condition on the notion of Dirac-Jacobi structure studied in
[
21
]
.

Appendix A

The aim of this short appendix is to collect some basic facts about Lie algebroids and their morphisms which are used throughout this work. Let
\displaystyle A
be a Lie algebroid over
\displaystyle M
and let
\displaystyle B
be a wide Lie subalgebroid of
\displaystyle A
. One can consider the normal bundle
\displaystyle A/B
and the Bott representation
\displaystyle\nabla\colon\Gamma(B)\times\Gamma(A/B)\to\Gamma(A/B)
of
\displaystyle B
on
\displaystyle A/B
, defined by sending
\displaystyle(X,\overline{Y})\mapsto\overline{[X,Y]_{A}}
. The vector space of
flat sections

\displaystyle\Gamma_{0}(A)
of
\displaystyle A
is by definition

\Gamma_{0}(A):=\left\{\overline{Y}\in\Gamma(A/B)\,|\,\nabla_{X}\overline{Y}=0,\,\text{for all }X\in\Gamma(B)\right\},

and the normalizer
\displaystyle N(\Gamma(B))
of
\displaystyle\Gamma(B)
in the Lie algebra
\displaystyle\Gamma(A)
is given by

N(\Gamma(B))=\left\{X\in\Gamma(A)\,|\,[X,Y]_{A}\in\Gamma(B)\,\text{for all }Y\in\Gamma(B)\right\}.

If we denote by
\displaystyle\widetilde{\pi}\colon N(\Gamma(B))\to\Gamma(A/B)
the restriction to
\displaystyle N(\Gamma(B))
of the quotient
\displaystyle C^{\infty}(M)
-module morphism
\displaystyle\pi\colon\Gamma(A)\to\Gamma(A/B)
then we clearly get that
\displaystyle\ker(\widetilde{\pi})=\Gamma(B)
and, moreover,
\displaystyle\operatorname{im}(\widetilde{\pi})=\Gamma_{0}(A)
. Indeed, pick
\displaystyle\overline{X}\in\Gamma(A/B)
, so that
\displaystyle X\in\Gamma(A)
. Thus,
\displaystyle X\in N(\Gamma(B))
if and only if

A mechanism of target mRNA selection and activity regulation in meiosis-related RBM46-MEIOC-YTHDC2 complex
Authors: Ruixin Liang, Qizi Chen, Jiawei Li, Han Liu, Yanqiu Ma, Shenglin Han, Yunxin Li, Jingyu Gao, Lan Ye, Jian Zhou
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
SummaryMEIOC and its two partners, RBM46 and YTHDC2, function as posttranscriptional regulators to promote mitotic-to-meiotic transition in mammalian germ cells. However, the molecular mechanism underlying target mRNA selection and degradation by MEIOC complex is largely unknown. Here, we demonstrate that exogenously expressed RBM46/MEIOC/YTHDC2 binds to Rad21 and Meioc 3′UTR and targets these transcripts for degradation in cultured somatic cells. YTHDC2 stabilizes MEIOC through inhibiting its ubiquitination, and the coiled-coil domain of MEIOC mediates its association with YTHDC2 and RBM46. The target mRNA is required for the interaction of RBM46 with YTHDC2. Moreover, MEIOC recruits XRN2 in the cytoplasm as the likely ribonuclease. We propose that MEIOC is a critical component that recruits RNA binding proteins for target mRNA selection and XRN2 for RNA degradation, and regulates the complex activity by modulating its mRNA and protein stability.Subject areas: Molecular mechanism of gene regulation, Molecular network

Introduction

In mammals, retinoic acid (RA) is an essential extrinsic factor for meiotic initiation in both sexes.1,2 In the response to RA, two transcription factors STRA8 and MEIOSIN, activate the meiotic gene program in germ cells to initiate meiosis.3,4,5 MEIOC is an RA-independent factor essential for meiotic entry as a post-transcriptional regulator.6,7 MEIOC recruits the RNA helicase YTHDC2 and the RNA binding protein RBM46 to form a complex, and they function together to regulate the transition from mitotic program to meiotic program.6,7,8,9,10 The function of MEIOC complex was firstly proposed to stabilize meiotic transcripts.6 The facts that some transcripts that were bound by MEIOC-YTHDC2-RBM46 were upregulated in the absence of any gene among Meioc/Ythdc2/Rbm46 supported that MEIOC complex destabilizes target mRNAs.7,10,11,12 The molecular mechanism underlying target mRNA selection and regulation by MEIOC complex is still largely unknown.

MEIOC is a germ cell-specific protein without RNA binding domains and is expressed at the stage of preleptotene and meiotic prophase I. Meioc deficient germ cells enter meiosis but fail to maintain prolonged meiotic prophase I, resulting in apoptosis or a premature transition into abnormal metaphase.6,7 Ythdc2 and Rbm46-deficient mouse mutants phenocopy Meioc-deficient mutants.8,9,10,13,14,15,16 YTHDC2 belongs to the YTH family of N6-methyladenosine (m6A) reader proteins.13 Unexpectedly, the m6A binding activity of YTHDC2 is not required for gametogenesis.11,17 In addition to YTH domain, YTHDC2 contains RNA helicase motifs having 3′→5′ RNA helicase activity. The energy from ATP hydrolysis drives YTHDC2 helicase activity. The loss of YTHDC2 helicase activity by a point mutation in ATPase motif causes mouse infertility in both sexes.11,17 XRN1 interacts with YTHDC2 and enhances its helicase activity.11 RBM46 contains multiple RNA binding motifs including three RNA recognition motifs (RRM) and a double-stranded RNA binding motif (DSRM). CLIP-seq analysis showed that RBM46 binds to specific RNA sequences and the binding sites are close to YTHDC2-binding sites.10 Although in vitro translated YTHDC2 does not bind to GST-RBM46, co-immunoprecipitation assay using mouse testis or cultured cells demonstrates that YTHDC2 and RBM46 belongs to the same complex and RNase can’t disrupt the interaction between YTHDC2 and RBM46,10 suggesting that the interactions among RBM46, YTHDC2 and target mRNA could be complicated.

In Drosophila the Tut-Bam-Bgcn complex promotes differentiation of male germline stem cell lineage.18,19,20 Ythdc2 and Rbm46 are the mouse homologs of Drosophila Bgcn and Tut.8,11 Meioc is a functional analog of Drosophila Bam.7 Bam holds Tut on its N-terminus and Bgcn on its C-terminus to form a physical complex. This complex is required for the translational suppression of target mRNA mei-P26 and how in male germ cells.20,21 The mouse RBM46-MEIOC-YTHDC2 complex, similar to the Drosophila Tut-Bam-Bgcn complex, functions in the posttranscriptional regulation during meiotic initiation. However, the mechanism by which the mouse RBM46-MEIOC-YTHDC2 (simplified as RMY) complex affects target mRNA has not been determined. There are at least three hypotheses to explain how the RMY complex affects target mRNA: degradation of mitotic mRNA, stabilizing meiotic mRNA, and inhibition of target mRNA translation.6,7,10,11,15,16

In this study, we demonstrated that exogenous expression of RBM46/MEIOC/YTHDC2 in somatic cells was sufficient to suppress the expression of target mRNAs. Using this reconstituted system, we identified the targeting motif from Rad21 and Meioc 3′UTR, and investigated the mechanism in which RMY complex assembles, selects, and suppresses target mRNAs.

Results

Reconstitution of RBM46-MEIOC-YTHDC2 functions in somatic cells

Strong evidence supports that Rad21 transcript is a target RNA of RBM46-MEIOC- YTHDC2 complex for degradation. First, Rad21 transcript was upregulated in the Meioc−/− male germ cells from type B spermatogonia to zygotene-like spermatocytes and in total Meioc−/− E14.5 ovaries.7,12 Second, RNA immunoprecipitation (RIP) verified that Rad21 mRNA bound to YTHDC2, and enhanced cross-linking immunoprecipitation (eCLIP) revealed that RBM46 interacted with the fragments of Rad21 3′UTR.8,10 Third, RAD21 is a mitotic cohesion subunit and its function is replaced by REC8 and RAD21L during meiosis.22,23,24 We further proved that Rad21 mRNA bound to RBM46 by RIP-qPCR using RBM46-HA knock-in testes (Figure S1A). Then, we generated EGFP-reporter with 3′UTR of Rad21 (NM_001411716.1) and transfected it together with HA-RBM46, HA-MEIOC, and Flag-YTHDC2 into HEK293T cells (Figure 1A). Compared to two transfections among RBM46/MEIOC/YTHDC2 and control vector transfections, concomitant transfection of RBM46/MEIOC/YTHDC2 together significantly suppressed EGFP (Figures 1D and 1G). The RNA level of EGFP-Rad21 3′UTR was also downregulated by ectopic expression of RBM46/MEIOC/YTHDC2 (Figure 1J). Thus, Rad21 transcript was confirmed to be the target of RMY complex and the complex function was reconstituted in HEK293T cells, indicating that other germ cell-specific protein in this complex was not required for target mRNA repression. To verify the reconstituted system in another cell line, we transfected EGFP-Rad21 3′UTR construct together with RBM46/MEIOC/YTHDC2 into mouse-derived N2a cells. EGFP was also repressed by concomitant expression of these three proteins (Figures S2A and S2B). To measure the decay kinetics of target mRNA, we treated HEK293T cells with actinomycin D 24 h after transfection and then measure the EGFP-Rad21 3′UTR mRNA level at 0–4 h. Our results showed that the reporter decayed faster in RMY group than that in RM group and control group in the first hour, and then the decay velocity slowed down in RMY group (Figure S2C).

To validate more target RNAs, we generated EGFP reporter with Meioc (NM_001127576.2) 3′UTR and Ccna2 (NM_009828.3) 3′UTR constructs (Figures 1B and 1C). Meioc transcript was enriched by both MEIOC and YTHDC2 RIP and the fragments of Meioc 3′UTR were present in RBM46 eCLIP pool.7,10 CCNA2 expression was upregulated in the Rbm46/Meioc/Ythdc2 single gene knockout germ cells and the fragments of Ccna2 3′UTR bound to RBM46.7,9,10 However, Ccna2 mRNA was not enriched by YTHDC2 or MEIOC RIP.7 We confirmed that Meioc and Ccna2 transcripts bound to RBM46 by RIP (Figures S1B and S1C). We then transfected EGFP reporter with Meioc 3′UTR or Ccna2 3′UTR together with RBM46/MEIOC/YTHDC2 into HEK293T cells. Concomitant transfection analysis showed that EGFP-Meioc 3′UTR was significantly repressed by RBM46/MEIOC/YTHDC2 at both protein level and mRNA level (Figures 1E, 1H and 1K), but the expression of EGFP-Ccna2 3′UTR was not changed (Figures 1F, 1I and 1L). Our results support that Meioc, but not Ccna2, is the target mRNA of RMY complex for degradation.

Determination of RBM46-MEIOC-YTHDC2 targeting motif

As the target 3′UTR containing reporter was suppressed by RMY complex in reconstituted system, we next investigated the recognition elements in Rad21 and Meioc 3′UTR. In order to measure reporter expression quantitatively, we applied a dual luciferase reporter vector pmirGLO, in which firefly luciferase gene is a reporter followed by the 3′UTR of interest and renilla luciferase gene is a transfection control. Rad21 and Meioc 3′UTR were cloned into pmirGLO and then tested in HEK293T cells (Figures 2A and 2B). The relative luciferase activity from the Rad21 or Meioc 3′UTR containing construct was suppressed by ectopic expression of RBM46/MEIOC/YTHDC2 (Figures 2D and 2G, and Table S1). To search for the key sequence required for suppression, truncated Rad21 3′UTR (ΔF1-ΔF4) and truncated Meioc 3′UTR (ΔD1-ΔD4) were tested (Figures 2C and 2F). We observed that the relative luciferase activities from ΔF1, ΔF2, ΔF4, ΔD1, ΔD2, and ΔD4 were suppressed while that of ΔF3 and ΔD3 escaped from the suppression (Figures 2D and 2G). To narrow down the recognition regions, we generated ΔF5-ΔF7 and ΔD5-ΔD7 for further test (Figures 2C and 2F). We identified that three fragments (ΔF5 and ΔF7, Rad21 3′UTR 482–751 nt and 903-1107 nt, ΔD5, Meioc 3′UTR 569–864 nt) contains the responsible elements for RMY complex (Figures 2E and 2H).

To determine the targeting motif in these suppressed fragments of the Rad21 and Meioc 3′UTRs, we searched for consensus sequence using the STREME algorithm and extracted the motif “UCAAAUCAUU”.25 We compared this motif with the eCLIP-enriched RBM46-binding motif and found the identical sequence “AAUCAU” (Figure 2I).10 This sequence is also similar to the consensus sequence identified by an in vitro RBM46 binding assay (Figure 2I).26 To verify whether RMY complex recognizes the “UCAAAUCAUU” sequence, we generated MUT-ΔF5 and MUT-ΔD5 for test through deletion of this motif in ΔF5 and ΔD5. We observed that deletion of this motif rescued the luciferase activity from both fragments (Figures 2J and 2K). This suggests that RMY complex recognizes UCAAAUCAUU or AAUCAU-containing sequence. We identified 7208 genes encoding transcripts with AAUCAU motif expressed in mouse testes. Among them, 2962 genes were present in RBM46-eCLIP collection (Table S2). To further validate this motif recognized by RMY complex, we inserted UCAAAUCAUU into Actb (NM_007393.5) 3′UTR close to U-rich region. As expected, the insertion in Actb 3′UTR repressed the reporter (Figure 2L).

YTHDC2 protects MEIOC from ubiquitination

By western blotting analysis of ectopic expressed MEIOC, we found that MEIOC protein level from single transfection was low, but increased in the presence of YTHDC2, indicating that YTHDC2 stabilizes MEIOC (Figure 3A). This result is consistent with the observation that MEIOC protein, not mRNA, reduced in Ythdc2−/− testes.8 To test whether MEIOC protein depends on the dose of YTHDC2, we expressed YTHDC2 at different concentrations and found that MEIOC protein level increased dependently of YTHDC2 protein level (Figure 3B). The ubiquitin-proteasome pathway is the major non-lysosomal proteolytic pathway and mediates the degradation of most short-lived cellular regulator in eukaryotic cells.27 We treated HEK293T cells with MG132 and cycloheximide (CHX) 48 h after transfection to inhibit proteasome and ribosome respectively. The half-life of MEIOC was prolonged significantly by proteasome inhibition (Figure 3C). YTHDC2 protein also prolonged the half-life of MEIOC (Figure 3D), similar to the function of proteasome inhibitor MG132. We then performed MEIOC-immunoprecipitation assay followed by ubiquitination analysis. We observed that MG132 inhibited the degradation of ubiquitinated MEIOC protein and less MEIOC protein was ubiquitinated in the presence of YTHDC2 (Figure 3E). Thus, we discovered a new function of YTHDC2 for protection of MEIOC from ubiquitination. To test whether the high-level MEIOC is sufficient for the reporter repression, we treated the cells with MG132 and analyzed EGFP expression. MG132 upregulated MEIOC protein level in the absence of YTHDC2, but did not repress EGFP (Figure S3A), indicating additional functions of YTHDC2, more than MEIOC protection.

YTHDC2 possesses the RNA helicase domain and YTH domain containing m6A-binding activity. Mutation analysis in mice revealed that its helicase activity, but not m6A-binding activity is essential for mouse fertility.11,17 To test the necessity of these two domains in the reconstituted system, we generated YTHDC2WLA (W1375A and L1380A) and YTHDC2EQ (E332Q) constructs in which m6A-binding activity and helicase activity of YTHDC2 were abolished, respectively (Figure S3B). Co-expression assay showed that none of these activities was required for MEIOC protein stabilization (Figure S3C). Compared to wild-type YTHDC2, YTHDC2WLA did not change the activity of target RNA degradation, while YTHDC2EQ partially impaired this activity (Figure S3D), suggesting that the helicase activity of YTHDC2 is required for target mRNA degradation.

The coiled-coil domain of MEIOC is required for its interaction with RBM46 and YTHDC2

How does MEIOC recruit its partners and regulate the stability? MEIOC protein possesses a coiled-coil domain near the C-terminus and an uncharacterized large fragment. The coiled-coil domain is required for its binding to YTHDC2.6 To determine the requirement of MEIOC fragments for the protein interaction, we generated three truncated fragments (M1-M3) and transfected them into HEK293T cells (Figure 4A). By ectopic expression and co-immunoprecipitation assay, we demonstrated that the MEIOC fragment of 712–965 aa containing the coiled-coil domain is required for its interaction with YTHDC2 and RBM46 (Figures 4B and 4C). To narrow down the range of binding sites, MEIOC mutants M4 and M5, were generated on the base of M1 (Figure 4A). We further determined that MEIOC fragment of 711–843 aa is essential for the recruitment of RBM46 and YTHDC2 (Figures 4D and 4E), indicating that RBM46 and YTHDC2 could compete for the binding to the coiled-coil domain of MEIOC. We further generated MEIOC mutant M6 (deletion of 812–841 aa) and validated this fragment is essential for the recruitment of the two partners (Figure 4F). In Drosophila, Bam recruits Tut and Bgcn to form Tut-Bam-Bgcn complex via its N-terminus and C-terminus.20 Although MEIOC is thought as the functional analog of Drosophila Bam in mammals, the recruitment pattern of MEIOC for its partners is different from that of Bam. It’s possible that the stability of MEIOC is regulated by the switch of the binding partner.

Target RNA is required for the interaction of RBM46 with YTHDC2

RBM46 contains three RNA recognition motifs (RRM1, RRM2, and RRM3) and a double-stranded RNA binding domain (DSRM). Deletion of all the RNA binding domains including RRM1, RRM2, RRM3, and DSRM eliminated its binding to YTHDC2.10 To specify the function of each domain, we generated RBM46 mutants (M1-M4), in which RRM1, RRM2, RRM3, and DSRM were deleted, respectively (Figure 5A). Through co-immunoprecipitation and immunoblotting analysis, we found that RRM3 and DSRM deletions significantly impaired the interaction of RBM46 with MEIOC (Figure 5B). Moreover, deletion of RRM2 impaired the interaction of RBM46 with YTHDC2 (Figure 5C). We then tested the target RNA binding ability of RBM46 mutants by RIP-qPCR. We observed that these three mutants revealed reduced target RNA binding ability, and especially the deletion of RRM2 severely impaired the ability of RBM46 binding to Rad21 3′UTR (Figure 5D). The fact that RRM2 is required for the binding ability of RBM46 to both target RNA and YTHDC2 protein indicates the possibility that the target RNA could be essential for the interaction of RBM46 with YTHDC2. To confirm this possibility, we performed co-immunoprecipitation assay using purified RBM46, YTHDC2, Rad21 3′UTR and Actb 3′UTR. Our result showed that Rad21 3′UTR, but not control RNA is required for the binding of RBM46 to YTHDC2 (Figure 5E). This result is consistent with the previous GST pull-down assay in which the prokaryotic expressed GST-RBM46 was unable to pull down the in vitro translated YTHDC2.10 CLIP analysis has demonstrated that RBM46 interacts with the sites of target RNA close to YTHDC2-binding sites. The RNase did not destroy the interaction of RBM46 with YTHDC2 during co-IP assay using mouse testis lysate.10 It raises the possibility that RBM46 interacts with YTHDC2 directly after binding of target RNA or the space between RBM46 and YTHDC2 is not enough for RNase intervention. We tested the RBM46 mutants in the reconstituted system and found all of four domains are essential for the reporter repression (Figure 5F).

MEIOC recruits the ribonuclease XRN2

MEIOC is a core factor in this complex, which recruits RBM46 for target RNA recognition and YTHDC2 for MEIOC stabilization, RNA recognition and unwinding. To explore the ubiquitous expressed factors in this complex, we performed MEIOC immunoprecipitation and mass spectrometry analysis using HEK293T cells transfected with RBM46, MEIOC, and YTHDC2 (Figure 6A). Many RNA-binding proteins were present on the MS list, such as DDX1, PABPC1, DDX5, RBM14, DDX18, UPF2, RBM39, YTHDC2, and RBM46 (Figure 6B). We did not find XRN1 in this list, but identified another 5′-3′ exonuclease XRN2. XRN2 is localized mainly in the nucleus for multiple functions, including transcription termination, the removal of aberrant pre-mRNA and processing of small nucleolar RNA.28,29,30 XRN2 is recruited by CAPRIN1 for RNA degradation in the cytoplasm during embryonic stem cell differentiation.31 In the presence of MEIOC and YTHDC2, cytoplasmic XRN2 increased a little, while nuclear XRN2 decreased markedly (Figure 6C). From MEIOC immunoprecipitation, XRN2 was detected by immunoblotting analysis (Figure 6D). To perform XRN2 immunoprecipitation, we increased MEIOC expression in HEK293 cells via lentivirus infection and used RIPA lysis buffer to release nuclear XRN2. After incubation, the lysate was used for XRN2 immunoprecipitation and MEIOC was detectable by immunoblotting analysis (Figure 6E). To test whether XRN2 is involved in RMY-dependent RNA repression, we performed gene editing in HEK293 cells using CRISPR-Cas9 technique. However, we did not obtain XRN2 homozygous knockout cell clones. Then we knocked down XRN2 using siRNAs (Table S3). All of the three siRNAs revealed high knock-down efficiency for XRN2 (Figures 6F and 6G). Two siRNAs were used for the dual luciferase assay to test the reporter repression. We observed that XRN2 knockdown attenuates RMY-dependent repression (Figure 6H).

The cytoplasmic processing body (P-body) is an RNA processing center in eukaryotic cells. P-body components, such as CNOT6L, PABPC1, UPF1, were present in the RBM46-immunoprecipitation pool.10 CCR4-NOT complex is a core component of P-body and is essential for Nonos2-DND1-mediated target RNA degradation in male gonocytes and spermatogonial stem cells.32,33,34,35 To test the interaction of RBM46/MEIOC/YTHDC2 with CCR4-NOT complex, we performed immunoprecipitation assay for these three proteins and detected CNOT1, a scaffold protein in CCR4-NOT complex. We didn’t observe the binding of RBM46/MEIOC/YTHDC2 with CNOT1 (Figure S4).

Discussion

We previously identified RBM46 from YTHDC2-associated proteins and demonstrated that RBM46 is an essential component in MEIOC-YTHDC2 complex, which is a major posttranscriptional regulator responsible for meiotic initiation.10 Here, we established a reconstituted system in somatic cells and revealed that the RMY complex was able to degrade the Rad21 and Meioc transcripts through recognition of 3′UTR fragments. The expression of RBM46/MEIOC/YTHDC2 in somatic cells destabilized the target mRNAs, indicating that additional germ cell-specific factors are not required for this function.

RNA degradation has been engaged in cell fate decision, such as stem cell differentiation, maternal-to-zygote transition, or cell reprogramming.36,37,38 The precise control of RNA degradation is critical for these processes. YTHDC2 protects MEIOC from ubiquitination through binding to the coiled-coil domain of MEIOC. However, RBM46 binds to the same region (812–842 aa) of MEIOC. We propose a competitive binding model in which MEIOC stability is regulated by the switch of RBM46 or YTHDC2 binding. It is a possible mechanism that RBM46 loads target mRNAs onto the complex by competing with YTHDC2 for the coiled-coil domain and MEIOC loses the protection of YTHDC2 during target mRNA degradation (Figure 6I). On the other hand, Meioc transcript is the target mRNA of this complex and degraded by recognition of Meioc 3′UTR sequence, showing a negative feedback loop for regulation of RMY complex activity. Therefore, RMY complex regulates its activity through controlling MEIOC protein level, revealing an example for precise control of RNA degradation. Inhibiting the expression of MEIOC by microbiota dysbiosis-related gut exosomes disturbed the meiosis process and impaired spermatogenesis in mice.39

RBM46 is a key RNA-binding protein that interacts with thousands of transcripts with a preferential binding over 3′ UTR in testes. The sequence “AAUCAUGU” is the most enriched motif in the top 50% RBM46 eCLIP peaks.10 In this study, we searched the functional motif essential for RNA degradation in a reconstituted system and found the shared sequence “UCAAAUCAUU” in Rad21 and Mieoc 3′UTR. Compared the most enriched RBM46-binding motif with functional motif, we determined the consensus sequence “AAUCAU” as the core motif for RBM46 binding, suggesting that RBM46 is required for target mRNA selection.

Within RBM46 target transcripts, only 6% of these transcripts were bound to YTHDC2, indicating that RBM46 is necessary, but not sufficient for RMY-dependent repression.10 YTHDC2 binds to U-rich motifs in 3′UTR of mRNA and the binding sites is close to RBM46-binding sites.10,11 Two RBPs work together in this complex to enhance the specificity of the target transcripts.

We previously reported that in vitro translated YTHDC2 did not bind to purified GST-RBM46 and that deletion of both RRMs and DSRM in RBM46 disrupted the interaction of RBM46 with YTHDC2.10 The in vitro binding assay suggests the target RNA is required for the interaction of RBM46 with YTHDC2. The similar RBPs that are able to interact with other proteins after binding RNA have been reported, such as DND1 and splicing factors.35,40 The target RNA binding of RBM46 maybe enhance its ability of competing with YTHDC2 for binding to the coiled-coil domain of MEIOC (Figure 6I).

The cytoplasmic mRNA degradation process in eukaryotes includes deadenylation, decapping, exonucleolytic degradation, and sometimes endonucleolytic cleavage.41 An exonuclease XRN2 was identified as a new partner of MEIOC. Germline-specific XRN2 inactivation in C. elegans caused sterility, indicating an essential role of XRN2 for germ cell development.42 We attempted to knockout XRN2 in HEK293T cells, but failed to obtain homozygous knockout cell clones, as previous attempts in embryonic stem cells.31 CRISPR-Cas9 screens identified XRN2 as an essential gene for human and mouse embryonic stem cells.43,44 A transient knock-down of XRN2 by two independent siRNAs followed by luciferase assays validates the essential role of XRN2 in RMY-dependent RNA repression. CCR4-NOT complex is one of major complexes responsible for mRNA deadenylation in the eukaryotic cytoplasm and its components were present in RBM46-immunoprecipitation pool,10,41 but we failed to confirm the interaction between CNOT1 and RBM46/MEIOC/YTHDC2. Further studies including protein structure analysis are necessary to elucidate the details in RMY complex assembly and target mRNA degradation.

Limitations of this study

We reconstituted RBM46-MEIOC-YTHDC2 complex in somatic cells and revealed the target mRNA degradation by this complex. However, we can’t exclude the other functions of RBM46/MEIOC/YTHDC2 in germ cells, such as target mRNA stabilization and translational control. Identification of more target transcripts is needed to understand the regulatory mechanisms of meiotic initiation and determine the consensus sequences. Although both RBM46 and YTHDC2 interact with the coiled-coil domain of MEIOC, the competition between these two proteins before and after RNA loading has not been demonstrated by molecular dynamics experiments. In this study, the protein levels were revealed by Western blot images and some of them were not repeated at least three times for statistical analysis. Additionally, although we identified XRN2 as a new partner of MEIOC, how the complex degrades the target RNAs is still an open question.

Experimental model and study participant details

Cell lines

N2a (Chinese Academy of Sciences, China), and HEK293T (Chinese Academy of Sciences, China) were cultured in high-glucose (Glc) Dulbecco’s modified eagle medium (DMEM) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) (VivaCell, C04001), 1% Penicillin-Streptomycin (Pen-Strep) (Gibco, 15140122) in a 5% CO2 incubator (Thermo, 3111) at 37°C. Both cell lines were authenticated by short tandem repeat (STR) profiling and were confirmed to be free of mycoplasma contamination prior to use.

RBM46-HA mice

The Rbm46-HA mice used for the RBP immunoprecipitation (RIP) assay were described previously.10 The mice were maintained in an environment of a controlled 12-h light-dark cycle, at a temperature range of 22°C–24°C, with ad libitum access to food and water. The testes were collected from six adult RBM46-HA homozygous male mice for RIP assay. The Ethics Committee of Affiliated Children Hospital of Jiangnan University approved all protocols and procedures for animal experiments (Approval No. WXCH2023-11-083).

Method details

Plasmid construction

pEGFP-C1 vector

The 3′UTR sequences were amplified from C57BL/6J mouse genomic DNA using Phanta Max Super-Fidelity DNA Polymerase (Vazyme, P515-01) and inserted into pEGFP-C1 vector using homologous recombination method (ClonExpress Ultra One Step Cloning Kit V2, Vazyme, C116-01).

pmirGLO vector

The full-length 3′UTR sequences and the truncated sequences were amplified from pEGFP-C1 vectors and inserted downstream of firefly luciferase gene in the pmirGLO vector using homologous recombination method. The Mut-ΔF5 and Mut-ΔD5 sequences were synthesized from Sangon Biotech and then inserted into pmirGLO vector.

Expression/lentivirus vectors

The truncated MEIOC DNA fragments were amplified from pHA-MEIOC using Phanta Max Super-Fidelity DNA Polymerase and inserted into the AbVEC2.1 vector using GenBuilder Cloning Kit (GenScript, L00701). The truncated RBM46 DNA fragments were amplified from pEGFP-N1-RBM46 and inserted into pEGFP-N1 vector. For lentivirus vector, DNA fragments (HA-RBM46, FLAG-YTHDC2, HIS-MEIOC) were inserted into the pHAGE vector.

Plasmid transfection

For HEK293T and N2a cells, Lipofectamine 3000 reagent (Thermo, L300015) was used according to the manufacturer’s instructions.

Luciferase assays

The full-length and mutant 3′UTR corresponding sequences of the mouse Rad21, Meioc and Actb genes were amplified and cloned into the pmirGLO dual-luciferase vector, as described in the “Plasmid Construction” section. The reporter constructs were then transfected into HEK293T cells for subsequent assays. 48 h after transfection, cells were lysed and the lysates were centrifuged at 10,000×g for 5 min at 4°C. The supernatant was collected and luciferase activity was measured using the Dual-Luciferase Assay Kit (Beyotime, RG027) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Briefly, 100 μL of Luciferase Assay Reagent was added to the well with an aliquot of the supernatant, and firefly luciferase activity was measured immediately using a ThermoScientific Fluorkan Ascent FL photometer (Thermo, 2805880). Subsequently, 100 μL of Renilla Luciferase Assay Reagent was added to the same well and then detected.

RNA extraction and real-time quantitative PCR

Total RNA was extracted from the cells using TRIzol reagent (Thermo, 15596018CN). The cDNA was prepared from 1 μg of total RNA though reverse transcription using HiScript III RT Super Mix for qPCR (Vazyme, R323). The diluted cDNA (2 μL) was used for each reaction using ChamQ SYBR qPCR Master Mix (Vazyme, Q311-02). DNA oligo sequences are listed in Table S3.

Lentivirus production

To generate lentiviruses, HEK293T cells with 90% confluent in a 10-cm dish were transfected with the pHAGE expression vector and lentivirus packaging vectors (VSV-G:MPMG:Rev:Tet2 = 1:1:1:1) using 25 μL of BeyoPEI (Beyotime, C0541).45 Forty-eight hours after transfection, the viral supernatant was centrifuged at 3000×g for 10 min at 4°C to remove cell debris. The supernatant was filtered using a 0.22 μm membrane and then concentrated using a viral concentration solution (Life-iLab, AC04L441).

Cycloheximide (CHX) chase assay

HEK293T cells were transiently transfected with HA-MEIOC plasmid. Twenty-four hours after transfection, the cells were treated with the proteasome inhibitor MG132 (5 μM) first. The solvent DMSO was used as a control. Then, CHX (25 μg/mL) was added to inhibit protein synthesis. The cells were collected and lysed at designated time points (0, 4, 8, and 12 h after CHX treatment). Western blot analysis was performed using anti-HA antibody.

Decay kinetic measurement

Twenty-four hours after transfection, HEK293T cells were treated with actinomycin D (4 μM). Subsequently, the cells were collected at designated time points (0, 15, 30, 60, 120 and 240 min after actinomycin D treatment). Total RNA was extracted using TRIzol reagent (Thermo, 15596018CN). 1 μg of total RNA was used for cDNA synthesis and real-time quantitative PCR analysis.

Protein purification

HA-RBM46 and FLAG-YTHDC2 were expressed in HEK293T cells respectively. The cells were lysed by IP lysis buffer with protease inhibitor cocktail (Beyotime). After centrifugation at 15000 rpm for 30 min at 4°C, the supernatants were incubated with anti-HA magnetic beads (Beyotime, P2185S) and anti-FLAG magnetic beads (Beyotime, P2181S) for 3 h at 4°C, respectively. The beads were washed three times by pre-chilled IP lysis buffer. HA-RBM46 protein was eluted by HA peptide solution and FLAG-YTHDC2 protein was eluted by 3× FLAG peptide solution. The protein solution was condensed through centrifugation (8000 rpm, 4°C) in 10Kd ultrafiltration tubes and then the peptide was removed by buffer replacement. The purified protein was used for in vitro interaction assay.

In vitro transcription

DNA template with SP6 promoter for Rad21 3′UTR were amplified from pEGFP-Rad21 3′UTR using Phanta Max Super-Fidelity DNA Polymerase (Vazyme, P515-01). DNA template with SP6 promoter for Actb 3′UTR was synthesized from Sangon Biotech. RNA was prepared using Biotin RNA Labeling Kit (SP6/T7) (Beyotime, R7061S). After transcription, DNase I (RNase-free) (Takara, 2270A) was added to remove DNA template.

Nuclear protein and cytoplasmic protein extraction

Nuclear and cytoplasmic protein from HEK293T cells was separated using Nuclear and Cytoplasmic Extraction Kit (Phygene, PH0324) according to the manufacturer’s instructions.

Immunoprecipitation and mass spectrometry

HEK293T cells were homogenized in IP lysis buffer or RIPA lysis buffer with protease inhibitor cocktail (Beyotime) and centrifuged at 15000 rpm for 30 min at 4°C. Supernatants were subjected to immunoprecipitation using anti-FLAG magnetic beads (Beyotime, P2181S), anti-HA magnetic beads (Beyotime, P2185S), and anti-GFP magnetic beads (Abbkine, KTI2024) at 4°C for 3 h. Beads were then washed 5 times with IP lysis buffer or RIPA lysis buffer. Binding protein was eluted with sample buffer (62.5 mM Tris-HCl (pH6.8), 3% SDS, 10% glycerol, 5% β-mercaptoethanol, and 60 nM bromophenol blue) and separated by sodium dodecyl sulfate-acrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE), followed by silver staining (Beyotime, P0017S). Partial eluted samples were pre-processed and subjected to Q Exactive Obritrap Mass Spectrometry coupled with Ultimate 3000 Nano-HPLC Instrument (ThermoFisher Scientific). The ratio of input to IP lysate is 1:20 for Western blot analysis.

Western blotting

Cell lysates or immunoprecipitants were mixed with sample buffer and boiled for 5 min at 95°C. Proteins were separated by SDS-PAGE and transferred to PVDF membranes (Merck Millipore, ISEQ00010). Membranes were blocked with 5% skim milk for 1 h at room temperature, followed by incubation with primary antibodies overnight at 4°C. After washing with TBS-T, the membranes were incubated with secondary antibodies.Signals were detected using SuperPico ECL Chemiluminescence Kit (Vazyme, E422-01). Signal images were acquired by Automatic Luminescence Imaging System (Tanon, China, 5200 Multi) and processed using ImageJ/Fiji (https://imagej.net/ij/) or Adobe Photoshop (Adobe).

RIP-qPCR

Cells were homogenized in IP lysis buffer with 40 U/mL recombinant RNase inhibitor (Takara, 2313) using a sonicator (SCIENTZ, IID). After centrifugation at 15000 rpm for 30 min at 4°C, the supernatant was used for IP assay and input control (supernatant for IP:input control = 20:1). Cell lysates were rotated with antibody-conjugated magnetic beads at 4°C for 1 h and then washed with IP lysis buffer 5 times. Immunopurified RNAs were isolated using RNeasy Micro Kit (QIAGEN, 74004). All purified RNAs were reverse-transcribed using HiScript III RT SuperMix for qPCR (Vazyme, R323). Input RNA was isolated using RNeasy Mini Kit (QIAGEN, 74104) and diluted in 32 μL of H2O. For reverse transcription, 3.2 μL of RNA solution was used. The testes collected from RBM46-HA mice were homogenized in IP lysis buffer with recombinant inhibitor and centrifuged at 15000 rpm for 30 min at 4°C. The supernatant was used for input RNA purification, anti-HA IP and IgG IP (Beyotime, P2185S). After reverse transcription, the cDNA (2 μL) was used for each reaction using ChamQ SYBR qPCR Master Mix.

Extraction of RNA motifs

The shared RNA sequences of the RBM46-MEIOC-YTHDC2 complex responding fragments were extracted using STREME (https://meme-suite.org/meme/tools/streme)25 by the standard protocol.

Quantification and statistical analysis

Signal intensities of immunoblots were quantified using ImageJ/Fiji. Data for statistical analyses were presented as mean ± standard error of the mean (SEM). The unpaired t test with Welch’s correction was used to determine significant differences between two groups. One-way ANOVA with Benjamini–Hochberg correction was used for multiple comparisons. p < 0.05 was considered significant. Statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism 10.6.1. The details of statistical analysis were included in the figure legends.

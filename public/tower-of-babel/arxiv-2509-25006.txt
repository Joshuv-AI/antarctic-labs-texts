Very effective algebraic and hermitian K-theory of the cyclic group of order two
Prerna Dhankhar, Rebecca Field, Arjun Nigam, J.D. Quigley, Albert Jinghui Yang

Abstract
. We compute the 2 2 -completed integral motivic homology, effective algebraic K-theory, and very effective hermitian K-theory of the geometric classifying space of the cyclic group of order two over algebraically closed fields, the real numbers, and finite fields.



1.
Introduction

The Chow groups of classifying spaces of finite groups arise in counterexamples to the integral Hodge conjecture
[
AH62
]
. These Chow groups were studied systematically by Totaro in
[
Tot14
]
, where he suggested that motivic classifying spaces should also be studied using richer motivic cohomology theories. The Chow–Witt groups of various classifying spaces have been the subject of much recent attention, with applications in enumerative geometry and the classification of
G
-torsors, cf.
[
BW25
,
DLM23
,
Lac25
,
HW19
,
Wen24
]
.

In this work, we continue the study of motivic classifying spaces using several richer invariants. Recall that effective algebraic K-theory
kgl
is the motivic analogue of connective complex topological K-theory
ku
and very effective Hermitian K-theory
kq
is the analogue of connective real topological K-theory
ko
. We will compute the
kgl
- and
kq
-homology of the classifying space of the cyclic group of order two after
2
-completion over different base fields:

Theorem 1.1
.

After
2
-completion, we compute the effective algebraic K-theory and very effective hermitian K-theory of
BC_{2}
over the following base fields:

(1)

algebraically closed fields (
Section

4
);

(2)

the real numbers (
Section

5
);

(3)

finite fields of prime order (
Section

6
).

Inspecting our computations in the motivic setting, we see that the classical groups
H\mathbb{Z}_{*}BC_{2}
embed in their motivic counterparts over algebraically closed fields, over the reals, and over finite fields via obvious homomorphisms

H\mathbb{Z}_{i}(BC_{2})\hookrightarrow H\mathbb{Z}^{\operatorname{mot}}_{i,\lceil i/2\rceil}(BC_{2}).

Moreover, the maps from
H\mathbb{Z}^{\operatorname{mot}}_{**}(BC_{2})
over the reals and over finite fields to the analogous groups over algebraically closed fields commute with these homomorphisms. The same statements hold if we replace
H\mathbb{Z}
and
H\mathbb{Z}^{\operatorname{mot}}
with
ku
and
kgl
, or
ko
and
kq
.

Corollary 1.2
.

If
F
is any field of positive odd characteristic, or any field of characteristic zero which admits a real embedding, then we have embeddings
H\mathbb{Z}_{i}(BC_{2})\hookrightarrow H\mathbb{Z}_{i,\lceil i/2\rceil}^{mot}(BC_{2})
,
ku_{i}(BC_{2})\hookrightarrow kgl_{i,\lceil i/2\rceil}(BC_{2})
, and
ko_{i}(BC_{2})\hookrightarrow kq_{i,\lceil i/2\rceil}(BC_{2})
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Although they have many similarities, the groups
H\mathbb{Z}_{**}(BC_{2})
,
kgl_{**}(BC_{2})
, and

vary quite significantly as we vary our base field. We invite the reader to note the differences in

visible in

1.1.
Motivation and future directions

Before discussing our computations in more detail, we motivate our results and suggest some directions for future work.

1.1.1.
Equivariant motivic homotopy theory

In classical equivariant stable homotopy theory, the classifying space
BG
naturally arises in the study of Borel
G
-spectra, which can be used to study genuine
G
-spectra through the isotropy separation sequence. In the motivic context, the motivic classifying space
BG
plays a similar role. Our computations of
kgl_{**}BC_{2}
and
kq_{**}BC_{2}
are therefore related to Borel
C_{2}
-equivariant algebraic and Hermitian K-theory. By studying the motivic geometric fixed points of
[
GH23
]
, it should be possible to describe the
C_{2}
-equivariant algebraic and Hermitian K-groups of various fields after
2
-completion. It could be interesting to compare the resulting equivariant algebraic K-groups to the computations of Chan and Vogeli
[
CV25
]
.

Alternatively, one could replace
kgl
and
kq
with even richer motivic homology theories. The fourth author and S. Zhang are using the
\mathbb{R}
-motivic stable homotopy groups of
BC_{2}
and their analogue under equivariant Betti realization to analyze the
RO(C_{2}\times C_{2})
-graded equivariant stable homotopy groups of spheres in a range.

1.1.2.
Other groups

We have focused on
G=C_{2}
, but in the classical setting, Bruner and Greenlees
[
BG03
,
BG10
]
computed the connective complex and real topological K-theory of many other finite groups. We expect that analogous computations can be made in the motivic setting using their work as a guide.

The third and fifth authors plan to make analogous computations for
BC_{2^{n}}
for all
n
, while J. Morris and the third author plan to investigate elementary abelian
2
-groups. The group
G=(C_{2})^{3}
should be particularly important since it appeared in Atiyah and Hirzebruch’s disproof of the integral Hodge conjecture
[
AH62
]
.

Another natural direction is to consider
C_{p^{n}}
for
p
an odd prime, which should be simpler than the
2
-primary calculations, since mod
p
motivic homology,
p
odd, is often simpler than mod
2
homology.

The first author plans to leverage our techniques to extend earlier results on the Chow ring of
BSO(2n,\mathbb{C})

[
Fie12
]
motivically.

Finally, one might consider the classifying spaces which appear in the papers
[
BW25
,
DLM23
,
Lac25
,
HW19
,
Wen24
]
mentioned above, plus related work.

1.1.3.
Slice spectral sequence

The slice filtration is a powerful tool in motivic stable homotopy theory. The effective slice filtration of
kgl
gives rise to the spectral sequence
[
Lev08
]

E_{1}=\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}\Sigma^{2i,i}H\mathbb{Z}_{**}\Rightarrow kgl_{**}

and the very effective slice filtration for
kq
gives rise to the spectral sequence
[
Bac17
]

E_{1}=\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}\Sigma^{8i,4i}H\mathbb{Z}_{**}\oplus\Sigma^{8i+1,4i+1}H\mathbb{Z}/2_{**}\oplus\Sigma^{8i+2,4i+2}H\mathbb{Z}/2_{**}\oplus\Sigma^{8i+4,4i+2}H\mathbb{Z}_{**}\Rightarrow kq_{**}.

Smashing the slice towers for
kgl
and
kq
with
BG
yields spectral sequences converging from shifts of
H\mathbb{Z}_{**}BG
and
H\mathbb{Z}/2_{**}BG
to
kgl_{**}BG
and
kq_{**}BG
.

As in
[
RSØ19
]
, it should be possible to express the
d_{1}
-differentials in these spectral sequence in terms of motivic Steenrod operations. Provided the spectral sequence collapses at
E_{2}
, this would give a complete description of
kgl_{**}BG
and
kq_{**}BG
over general base fields in terms of subquotients of the motivic homology groups of
BG
. This approach could also be used to remove some of the
2
-completions which are necessary in our approach.

1.2.
Outline of computations

We produce our computations using a combination of the Atiyah–Hirzebruch and the motivic Adams spectral sequences. We review the classical computations of
H\mathbb{Z}_{*}BC_{2}
,
ku_{*}BC_{2}
, and
ko_{*}BC_{2}
via these methods in
Section

2
. Although these computations are already well-known, there are a variety of possible approaches and we have adopted a strategy which we believe generalizes most efficiently to the motivic context.

Based on our classical computations, we outline a general strategy for these computations in the motivic context in
Section

3
.
1.1
is obtained by implementing this strategy over specific base fields. The general strategy can be summarized as follows.

First, we analyze the Atiyah–Hirzebruch spectral sequence converging to
E_{**}BC_{2}
, differentials in which are determined by the attaching maps in
BC_{2}
and multiplicative structures (products, Toda brackets) in
E_{**}
. By computing the first few pages of the Atiyah–Hirzebruch spectral sequence, we obtain bounds on the ranks of the groups
E_{**}BC_{2}
as modules over
\mathbb{M}_{2}^{F}
, the mod two motivic homology groups of the base field
F
.

Second, we analyze the motivic Adams spectral sequence converging to
E_{**}BC_{2}
. It turns out that this spectral sequence collapses at
E_{2}
for all choices of
E
and all base fields, so the main difficulty is computing the
E_{2}
-term. We develop a general strategy for decomposing
H^{**}(BC_{2})
as a module over various subalgebras of the motivic Steenrod algebra in
Section

3
. The rank bounds obtained from our Atiyah–Hirzebruch spectral sequence computations allow us to compute various connecting homomorphisms without resorting to potentially tricky homological algebra involving the motivic Steenrod algebra.

Third, we compare the Atiyah–Hirzebruch and the Adams spectral sequences. These spectral sequences together provides us with information about additive and multiplicative extensions in
E_{**}BC_{2}
.

The rest of the paper is spent specializing to different base fields:

In
Section

4
, we work over the complex numbers. Except for the fact that computations are bigraded and the first Hopf map
\eta
is non-nilpotent in
kq_{**}
, the computations are essentially the same as in the classical case. A Lefschetz principle extends our calculations to all algebraically closed fields.

In
Section

5
, we work over the real numbers. The coefficient rings
H\mathbb{Z}_{**}
,
kgl_{**}
, and
kq_{**}
are significantly more complicated than their classical counterparts, and some genuinely new phenomena (i.e., involving elements which base-change to zero over
\mathbb{C}
) appears in the Atiyah–Hirzebruch and Adams spectral sequences. The complexity of the computations also necessitates that we divide our charts into coweights, a bookkeeping trick we initially learned from
[
GHIR19
]
.

In
Section

6
, we work over finite fields
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with
q
odd. Unlike in all of the previous contexts, there are differentials in the Adams spectral sequences converging to
H\mathbb{Z}_{**}
,
kgl_{**}
, and
kq_{**}
(first observed in
[
Kyl15
]
) which makes the
2
-torsion in these groups fairly complicated. As a result, we find that there is a family of exotic Atiyah–Hirzebruch spectral sequence differentials which have no counterparts in characteristic zero.

1.3.
Conventions and notation

We adopt the following contentions and notation.

(1)

Everything is implicitly
2
-complete.

(2)

\mathcal{A}
denotes the Steenrod algebra and the motivic Steenrod algebra.

(3)

H:=H\mathbb{F}_{2}
denotes the mod two Eilenberg–MacLane spectrum and its motivic analogue, and
H\mathbb{Z}
denotes their integral analogues.

(4)

E(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots)
denotes the exterior algebra generated by
x_{1},x_{2},\cdots
.

(5)

\operatorname{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}}^{***}(M):=\operatorname{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}}^{***}(M,k)
denotes
\operatorname{Ext}
over the Hopf algebroid
(A,k)
from an
A
-module
M
to the ground ring
k
.

(6)

kgl
denotes the effective cover of the algebraic K-theory spectrum.

(7)

kq
denotes the very effective cover of the hermitian K-theory spectrum.

2.
Classical calculations

In this section, we recall the mod two cohomology of
BC_{2}
and use it to compute
H\mathbb{Z}_{*}BC_{2}
,
ku_{*}BC_{2}
, and
ko_{*}BC_{2}
.

2.1.
Mod two cohomology

Recall that
BC_{2}\simeq\mathbb{R}P^{\infty}
. We have

H^{*}(BC_{2})\cong\operatorname{ker}\left(\mathbb{F}_{2}[u]\to\mathbb{F}_{2},\quad u\mapsto 0\right)

with
|u|=1
. The action of the first two Steenrod operations is determined by

\operatorname{Sq}^{1}(u)=u^{2},\quad\operatorname{Sq}^{2}(u)=0,\quad\operatorname{Sq}^{2}(u^{2})=u^{4}.

2.2.
Integral homology

To obtain
H\mathbb{Z}_{*}(BC_{2})
from
H^{*}(BC_{2})
, we may use the Adams spectral sequence with signature

E_{2}^{**}=\operatorname{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}}^{**}(H^{*}(BC_{2})\otimes H^{*}(H\mathbb{Z}),\mathbb{F}_{2})\Rightarrow H_{*}(BC_{2}).

Here, we are using the Künneth isomorphism for
H\mathbb{F}_{2}
-cohomology to identify

H^{*}(BC_{2}\wedge H\mathbb{Z})\cong H^{*}(BC_{2})\otimes H^{*}(H\mathbb{Z}).

There is an isomorphism of
\mathcal{A}
-modules
[
Mar83
, Proposition 19.1.3]

H\mathbb{F}_{2}^{*}H\mathbb{Z}\cong\mathcal{A}//\mathcal{A}(0),

where
\mathcal{A}(0)=E(\operatorname{Sq}^{1})
. Using a change-of-rings isomorphism, the above
E_{2}
-term has the form

E_{2}^{**}=\operatorname{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}}^{**}(H^{*}(BC_{2})\otimes\mathcal{A}//\mathcal{A}(0),\mathbb{F}_{2})\cong\operatorname{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(0)}^{**}(H^{*}(BC_{2}),\mathbb{F}_{2}).

The action of
\operatorname{Sq}^{1}
on
H^{*}(BC_{2})
described above implies that there is an isomorphism of
\mathcal{A}(0)
-modules

H^{*}(BC_{2})\cong\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}\Sigma^{2i+1}\mathcal{A}(0),

so we may rewrite the
E_{2}
-term as

E_{2}^{**}=\operatorname{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(0)}^{**}(H^{*}(BC_{2}),\mathbb{F}_{2})\cong\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}\operatorname{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(0)}^{**}(\Sigma^{2i+1}\mathcal{A}(0),\mathbb{F}_{2}).

Since
\mathcal{A}(0)
is free over itself, we have

\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}\operatorname{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(0)}^{**}(\Sigma^{2i+1}\mathcal{A}(0),\mathbb{F}_{2})\cong\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}\Sigma^{2i+1}\mathbb{F}_{2}.

The Adams spectral sequence collapses at
E_{2}
for degree reasons, and there is no room for hidden extensions. We conclude that

H\mathbb{Z}_{*}(BC_{2})\cong\begin{cases}\mathbb{F}_{2}\quad&\text{ if }*=2i+1,i\geq 0,\\
0\quad&\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

2.3.
Connective complex K-theory

To obtain
ku_{*}(BC_{2})
from
H^{*}(BC_{2})
, we proceed in two steps. First, we will run the Atiyah–Hirzebruch spectral sequence to calculate the rank of
ku_{*}(BC_{2})
in all degrees. Second, we will use our Atiyah–Hirzebruch calculations to determine the
E_{2}
-term of the Adams spectral sequence, which will then resolve any questions about additive extensions.

The Atiyah–Hirzebruch spectral sequence has the form

E_{1}=\bigoplus_{\text{cells of }BC_{2}}ku_{*}\Rightarrow gr^{*}_{AH}ku_{*}BC_{2}.

Bott periodicity implies that
ku_{*}\cong\mathbb{Z}[\beta]
with
|\beta|=2
. Since each even-dimensional cell of
BC_{2}
is attached to the odd-dimensional cell one dimension lower by a degree-
2
attaching map, the nontrivial
d_{1}
-differentials are given by

d_{1}(x[2n])=2x[2n-1]

for all
n\geq 1
, where
x[i]
is our notation for an element in filtration
i
with
x\in ku_{*}
. See
Fig.

1
.

Figure 1.
The Atiyah–Hirzebruch spectral sequence converging to
ku_{*}BC_{2}
(left) and its
E_{\infty}
-page. Each
\square
represents
\mathbb{Z}
and each differential (drawn in red) represents multiplication by
2
. Each bullet represents
\mathbb{F}_{2}

The
E_{2}
-term consists of
a copy of
ku_{*}/2
in each positive odd filtration and is zero otherwise. The spectral sequence collapses at
E_{2}
for degree reasons, showing

gr^{*}_{AH}ku_{n}(BC_{2})\cong\begin{cases}(\mathbb{F}_{2})^{i+1}\quad&\text{ if }n=2i+1\geq 0,\\
0\quad&\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

Here, we write
gr^{*}_{AH}
for the associated graded of the Atiyah–Hirzebruch filtration. This tells us the ranks of
ku_{n}(BC_{2})
for all
n
, though there is room for hidden extensions.

We resolve these hidden extensions using the Adams spectral sequence. This has signature

E_{2}^{**}=\operatorname{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}}^{**}(H^{*}(BC_{2})\otimes H^{*}(ku),\mathbb{F}_{2})\Rightarrow ku_{*}(BC_{2}).

There is an isomorphism of
\mathcal{A}
-modules

H\mathbb{F}_{2}^{*}(ku)\cong\mathcal{A}//\mathcal{E}(1),

where
\mathcal{E}(1)=E(Q_{0},Q_{1})
with
Q_{0}=\operatorname{Sq}^{1}
and
Q_{1}=[\operatorname{Sq}^{1},\operatorname{Sq}^{2}]=\operatorname{Sq}^{1}\operatorname{Sq}^{2}+\operatorname{Sq}^{2}\operatorname{Sq}^{1}.

After a change-of-rings isomorphism, we may rewrite the
E_{2}
-term as

E_{2}^{**}=\operatorname{Ext}_{\mathcal{E}(1)}^{**}(H^{*}(BC_{2}),\mathbb{F}_{2}).

We will calculate these
\operatorname{Ext}
-groups as follows. There is a short exact sequence of
\mathcal{E}(1)
-modules

\Sigma^{2}\mathbb{F}_{2}\hookrightarrow H^{*}(BC_{2})\to V,

where
V
is defined to be the quotient of the inclusion. This induces a long exact sequence

(1)

\cdots\leftarrow\operatorname{Ext}^{s,t}_{\mathcal{E}(1)}(\Sigma^{2}\mathbb{F}_{2})\leftarrow\operatorname{Ext}^{s,t}_{\mathcal{E}(1)}(H^{*}(BC_{2}))\leftarrow\operatorname{Ext}^{s,t}_{\mathcal{E}(1)}(V)\xleftarrow{\delta}\operatorname{Ext}^{s-1,t}_{\mathcal{E}(1)}(\Sigma^{2}\mathbb{F}_{2})\leftarrow\cdots

which we may use to calculate
\operatorname{Ext}_{\mathcal{E}(1)}(H^{*}(BC_{2})
.

Since
\mathcal{E}(1)
is an exterior algebra on two generators, we have

\operatorname{Ext}_{\mathcal{E}(1)}(\Sigma^{2}\mathbb{F}_{2})\cong\Sigma^{2}\mathbb{F}_{2}[h_{0},v_{1}],

where
|h_{0}|=(1,1)
and
|v_{1}|=(3,1)
.

On the other hand, we may compute
\operatorname{Ext}_{\mathcal{E}(1)}(V)
as follows. Observe that
\mathcal{E}(1)//\mathcal{E}(0)=E(Q_{1})
. Define a filtration on
V
by

F_{i}=\mathbb{F}_{2}\{x,x^{2},\ldots,x^{2i+1},x^{2i+4}\}

so that

F_{i}/F_{i-1}\cong\Sigma^{2i+1}E(Q_{1}).

Applying
\operatorname{Ext}_{\mathcal{E}(1)}(-)
gives a spectral sequence with signature

E_{1}=\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}\operatorname{Ext}_{\mathcal{E}(1)}(\Sigma^{2i+1}E(Q_{1}))\Rightarrow\operatorname{Ext}_{\mathcal{E}(1)}(V).

By a change-of-rings isomorphism, we may rewrite the
E_{1}
-term as

E_{1}=\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}\operatorname{Ext}_{\mathcal{E}(0)}(\Sigma^{2i+1}\mathbb{F}_{2})\cong\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}\Sigma^{2i+1}\mathbb{F}_{2}[h_{0}].

Since these
h_{0}
-towers are all at least
2
stems apart from each other, the spectral sequence collapses for degree reasons, giving

\operatorname{Ext}_{\mathcal{E}(1)}(V)\cong\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}\Sigma^{2i+1}\mathbb{F}_{2}[h_{0}].

Having calculated the groups in (
1
), it only remains to determine the connecting homomorphism. We claim the connecting homomorphism is nontrivial, with

\delta(\Sigma^{2}v_{1}^{i}h_{0}^{j})=\Sigma^{2i+1}h_{0}^{i+j+1}.

Indeed, if this were not the case, then (
1
) would split, decomposing
\operatorname{Ext}_{\mathcal{E}(1)}(H^{*}(BC_{2}))
as the direct sum of
\operatorname{Ext}_{\mathcal{E}(1)}(\mathbb{F}_{2}\oplus\Sigma^{2}\mathbb{F}_{2})
and
\operatorname{Ext}_{\mathcal{E}(1)}(V)
. However, such a decomposition would force the smallest possible rank for
ku_{2i+1}(BC_{2})
to be
(\mathbb{F}_{2})^{i+2}
, contradicting our Atiyah–Hirzebruch spectral sequence computations. Therefore the connecting homomorphism must be as claimed. The calculation is depicted in
Fig.

The
E_{2}
-term of the Adams spectral sequence is thus given by

E_{2}=\operatorname{Ext}_{\mathcal{E}(1)}(H^{*}(BC_{2}))\cong\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}\Sigma^{2i+1}\mathbb{F}_{2}[h_{0}]/(h_{0})^{i+1}.

The spectral sequence collapses for degree reasons. Since
h_{0}
detects multiplication by

ku_{n}(BC_{2})\cong\begin{cases}\mathbb{Z}/2^{i+1}\quad&\text{ if }n=2i+1\geq 0,\\
0\quad&\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

2.4.
Connective real K-theory

We compute
ko_{*}(BC_{2})
using a similar strategy to our computation of
ku_{*}(BC_{2})
.

We begin with the Atiyah–Hirzebruch spectral sequence

E_{1}=\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}\Sigma^{i}ko_{*}\Rightarrow ko_{*}(BC_{2}).

The groups
ko_{*}
can be computed using the classical Adams spectral sequence. The potentially nontrivial differentials are determined by

d_{1}(x[n])=2x[n-1]

for all
n=2k>0
,

d_{2}(x[n])=\eta x[n-2]

for all
n\equiv 0,1\pmod{4}
with
n\geq 4
, and

d_{3}(\eta^{2}[4k])=2\alpha[4k-3]

for all
k\geq 1
.
The spectral sequence then collapses since the only potential longer differentials are from torsion groups to torsion-free groups. We find

gr^{*}_{AH}ko_{n}(BC_{2})\cong\begin{cases}\mathbb{F}_{2}\quad&\text{ if }n=8i+1,8i+2>0,\\
(\mathbb{F}_{2})^{4i+3}\quad&\text{ if }n=8i+3>0,\\
(\mathbb{F}_{2})^{4i+4}\quad&\text{ if }n=8i+7>0,\\
0\quad&\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

Using the isomorphism of
\mathcal{A}
-modules

H\mathbb{F}_{2}^{*}(ko)\cong\mathcal{A}//\mathcal{A}(1),

where
\mathcal{A}(1)=\langle\operatorname{Sq}^{1},\operatorname{Sq}^{2}\rangle

E_{2}^{**}=\operatorname{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)}^{**}(H^{*}(BC_{2};\mathbb{F}_{2}),\mathbb{F}_{2}).

Recall that there is an isomorphism of
\mathcal{A}(1)

\mathcal{A}(1)//\mathcal{A}(0)\cong\mathbb{F}_{2}\{1,\operatorname{Sq}^{2},\operatorname{Sq}^{1}\operatorname{Sq}^{2},\operatorname{Sq}^{2}\operatorname{Sq}^{1}\operatorname{Sq}^{2}\}.

We will calculate
\operatorname{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)}(H^{*}(BC_{2};\mathbb{F}_{2}))

\operatorname{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)}(Q)

3.
General strategy

The classical computations of the previous section provide a blueprint for analogous motivic computations. In this section, we spell out this general strategy which will be used in subsequent sections to make computations over specific base fields.

3.1.
Mod two motivic cohomology

Let
\mathbb{M}_{2}^{**}=H^{**}
and let
\mathbb{M}_{2}=H_{**}
. In
\mathbb{M}_{2}^{**}
, there are distinguished elements
\tau\in\mathbb{M}_{2}^{0,1}
and
\rho\in\mathbb{M}_{2}^{1,1}
.

In
[
Voe03
, Thm. 6.10]
, Voevodsky produced an isomorphism of bigraded rings

H^{**}BC_{2}\cong\left(\operatorname{ker}:\mathbb{M}_{2}^{**}[x,y]/(x^{2}+\tau y+\rho x)\to\mathbb{M}_{2},\quad x,y\mapsto 0\right),

where
|x|=(1,1)
and
|y|=(2,1)
.

We will be interested in
H^{**}BC_{2}
as a module over
\mathcal{A}(1)
, the subalgebra of the motivic Steenrod algebra generated by
\operatorname{Sq}^{1}
and
\operatorname{Sq}^{2}
. The actions of these Steenrod operations on
H^{**}BC_{2}
are determined by

\operatorname{Sq}^{1}(x)=y,\quad\operatorname{Sq}^{2}(y)=y^{2},

together with the Cartan formula and the Adem relations:

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
0\leq i\leq 1
and let
j\geq 0
. Then

\operatorname{Sq}^{1}(x^{i}y^{j})=\begin{cases}y^{j+1}\quad&\text{ if }i=1,\\
0\quad&\text{ if }i=0,\end{cases}

\operatorname{Sq}^{2}(x^{i}y^{j})=\begin{cases}x^{i}y^{j+1}\quad&\text{ if }i+2j\equiv 2,3\pmod{4},\\
0\quad&\text{ if }i+2j\equiv 0,1\pmod{4}.\end{cases}

3.2.
Integral motivic homology

The motivic Adams spectral sequence converging to
H\mathbb{Z}_{**}BC_{2}
has signature

E_{2}=\operatorname{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}}^{***}(H^{**}(BC_{2}\wedge H\mathbb{Z}))\Rightarrow H\mathbb{Z}_{**}BC_{2}.

Since
H^{**}(H\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathcal{A}//\mathcal{A}(0)
, we can use the Künneth theorem and a change-of-rings isomorphism to rewrite

E_{2}\cong\operatorname{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}}^{***}(H^{**}(BC_{2})\otimes\mathcal{A}//\mathcal{A}(0))\cong\operatorname{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(0)}^{***}(H^{**}BC_{2}).

By
Lemma

3.1
, there is an isomorphism of
\mathcal{A}(0)
-modules

H^{**}BC_{2}\cong\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}\Sigma^{2i+1,i+1}\mathcal{A}(0),

which implies that

\operatorname{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(0)}^{***}(H^{**}(BC_{2}))\cong\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}\Sigma^{2i+1,i+1}\operatorname{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(0)}^{***}(\mathcal{A}(0))\cong\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}\Sigma^{2i+1,i+1}\mathbb{M}_{2}.

Since
\mathbb{M}_{2}
is concentrated in Adams filtration zero, the Adams spectral sequence collapses with no possibility of hidden extensions, showing:

Proposition 3.2
.

Over any base field, there is an isomorphism of bigraded abelian groups

H\mathbb{Z}_{**}BC_{2}\cong\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}\Sigma^{2i+1,i+1}\mathbb{M}_{2}.

To access these

\langle u\tau^{2^{s}-1}2^{\nu(q-1)+s-1},2,\eta\rangle=\tau^{2^{s}}\eta,\quad s\geq 0,\quad q\equiv 1\pmod{4},

\langle\rho,2,\eta\rangle=\tau\eta,\quad q\equiv 3\pmod{4},

\langle\rho\tau^{2^{s}-1}2^{\nu(q^{2}-1)+s-2},2,\eta\rangle=\tau^{2^{s}}\eta,\quad s\geq 1,\quad q\equiv 3\pmod{4}.

We also note the presence of hidden
\eta
-extensions

\eta\cdot\tau^{i+1}\eta^{2}\beta^{j}=2\cdot 2^{\nu(q-1)+\nu(i+1)-1}\tau^{i}\alpha\beta^{j}

for all
i\geq 0
when
q\equiv 1\pmod{4}
, and for all
i\equiv 1\pmod{2}
when
q\equiv 3\pmod{4}
. If
q\equiv 3\pmod{4}
and
i\equiv 0\pmod{2}
, then the relation becomes

\eta\cdot\tau^{i+1}\eta^{2}\beta^{j}=2\tau^{i}\alpha\beta^{j}.

These relations are visible in the slice spectral sequence; see
[
KQ24
, Fig. A5]
).

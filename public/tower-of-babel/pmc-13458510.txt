Quantum-enhanced spiking intelligence framework for real-time anomaly detection in industrial internet of things
Authors: Kamran Ahmad Awan, Ashwag O. Albakri, Fatimah Alhayan, Hadeel Alsolai, Bayan Alabdullah, Sarra Ayouni, Houbing Song
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Industrial Internet of Things (IIoT) anomaly detection imposes concurrent requirements for temporal consistency, computational latency control, and representation of high-dimensional heterogeneous sensor data. Addressing these constraints requires models capable of processing non-stationary streams while maintaining bounded inference time and preserving inter-sensor dependencies. The proposed Quantum-Enhanced Spiking Neural Network (QESNN) integrates parameterized quantum circuits with event-driven spiking computation within a unified inference structure. The architecture is comprised of five computation modules. The Quantum Neurons for Sensor Data Fusion module maps multimodal sensor observations onto amplitude–phase quantum states and then uses nonlinear unitary transformations to generate joint feature representations through multiple channels. The Entanglement-Based Device Synchronization module aligns phases between nodes by using entangled states, coherence-control operators, and correlations for synchronization. The Probabilistic Quantum Spiking for Anomaly Detection module uses spike probability based on tunneling and incorporates fidelity and entropy metrics to estimate the likelihood of anomalies under uncertainty. The Adaptive Quantum Learning for Dynamic Optimization module tunes model parameters via time-dependent Hamiltonian dynamics with annealing strategies and feedback correction schemes to ensure robust convergence in dynamic environments. Finally, the Quantum Decoherence Management for Reliable Processing module models the impact of noise, utilizes coherence-control operations, and conducts reliability checks in order to maintain stable quantum-state computation amidst external disturbances. When tested on the MVTec Anomaly Detection dataset, the model achieves classification accuracy of 98.7% with 512 logical quantum–spiking units and an inference time of 3.29 s. The effect of each component is estimated via ablation study – eliminating the Quantum Neurons for Sensor Data Fusion module leads to classification accuracy of 92.5%, whereas elimination of the Quantum Decoherence Management for Reliable Processing module produces the lowest classification accuracy of 88.9%.

Introduction

Coordination between heterogeneous devices and sensors becomes crucial in IIoT systems to acquire sensor readings simultaneously at various layers of communication1,2. In industrial telemetry systems, sensors such as accelerometers, microphones, current sensors, pressure sensors, and temperature sensors have variable sampling rates, noise levels, dynamic ranges, and precision3,4. Integration of this heterogeneous stream of sensor information would be difficult due to the need to maintain their chronological order, inter-sensor dependency, and integrity of signals when acquiring data in real-time. Furthermore, the acquisition process in industrial environments is made complex due to non-stationary signals caused by the non-linear nature of time variation, system aging, failures in communication, and rapid change in the operation mode. Spiking neural networks (SNNs) provide an event-driven computation model in which temporal information is represented through discrete spike events. The membrane potential v(t) of a neuron is a time-varying quantity that accumulates synaptic inputs represented by causal kernels k(t). When v(t) reaches a threshold value θ, a spike is generated and the membrane potential is reset. Spike timing and inter-spike intervals are important for discriminative processing, particularly in industrial process signals with asynchronous updates, bursty behavior, and transient fault signatures. In such settings, spike-time-dependent information can propagate through local temporal windows without requiring dense processing of the entire signal stream5–9. This study explores the use of quantum circuits to support such event-driven temporal models by representing spike information and dynamic sensor variables as quantum states of single qubits. These qubits are initially prepared in superposition states and then evolved through parameterized unitary operators. For a given observable O, measurement produces an expectation value \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\langle O \rangle$$\end{document}, which can be optimized according to the target inference objective10. The proposed approach defines the quantum neuron as a parameterized quantum circuit where the sensor inputs are mapped into quantum states represented by amplitudes and phases. Such an approach makes it possible to encode inter-channel correlations between multiple sensors in a concise quantum state, while classical approaches generally need deep layering in order to retain such correlations11,12. Quantum entanglement allows for the creation of correlation relationships that can support phase-based timing and quantum state coupling between multiple quantum processing units. However, classical communication layers remain necessary for control signaling, measurement exchange, synchronization feedback, and coordination among distributed IIoT devices13–15.

Related work & background research

As multimodal fusion technology has advanced in industrial anomaly detection, it has become clear that fault signatures in industrial production cannot be detected reliably using only a single sensing channel. Multimodal data provide richer and more comprehensive information about real-world operating conditions16. To improve anomaly detection performance, hybrid fusion methods that integrate multiple modalities at early, intermediate, or late fusion stages have been explored to learn more informative features from multimodal data. Such methods help separate fault instances that may exhibit similar feature distributions in individual modalities16. Moreover, unsupervised defect localization methods based on instance segmentation have been applied in industrial settings where dense annotations are time-consuming and expensive to obtain17. Prior studies on surface defect detection have employed class-agnostic region proposals to handle a large number of instances in manufacturing images. Feature alignment has also been utilized, particularly for surfaces with limited supervision data17. Attention-based feature selection using mutual-information constraints has been employed to select informative features and suppress irrelevant, redundant, or unstable features18. Recent quantum-inspired learning models have also shown that high-dimensional feature interactions can be represented through structured mathematical decompositions and stochastic neural dynamics. Residual Tensor Train models learn multiple multilinear correlations among input features within a compact quantum-inspired representation, which is relevant to sensor-fusion tasks involving coupled industrial variables19. Similarly, quantum-inspired neural networks with stochastic dynamics have been explored for multimodal representation learning, showing the potential of quantum-inspired state evolution for modeling cross-modal uncertainty and interaction patterns20.

Structural graph models that capture dependencies between sensors or system components through message passing have shown promise for various system- and component-level applications21. Since disturbances propagate through physically connected industrial components, efficient fault detection requires both local and global temporal behavior to be modeled. Unsupervised models that combine local and global objectives based on structural and temporal information have achieved improved fault detection performance for correlated faults and coupled process variations21. In recent years, Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) have received increasing attention for industrial surface inspection tasks, where models must detect both small and large defects while suppressing false alarms caused by background and illumination variations without losing defect structure22. Recent studies on lightweight quantum neural networks further indicate that quantum neural models can be designed with reduced circuit complexity and resource-aware structures23.

System-level IIoT architectures have moved from batch-oriented processing toward real-time processing, creating challenges related to timing constraints, dynamic scheduling, large-scale data processing, and event-driven communication at the edge and in the cloud25. In distributed manufacturing systems and networked smart factories, real-time-capable control units must be coordinated so that information about the current control state is distributed within strict timing limits26. Studies and applications of time-aware control have also motivated deterministic network protocol designs for time-critical communication26. Current digital-twin processing approaches, which connect a physical process or device with its virtual representation, still face several challenges, including performance degradation under increased sensing load, significant queueing delay, broker contention, and asynchronous updates27,28. Problem formulation and computational objectives

The proposed formulation is based on three computational objectives that address critical requirements of IIoT systems. First, temporal synchronization among distributed sensing nodes is essential because clock drift, packet-delay variation, and network jitter introduce timing errors that can propagate through control loops. Second, anomaly detection in time-series telemetry is difficult because edge-streaming data often exhibit class imbalance and non-stationarity, while rare fault modes occur only sparsely in the data stream31–33. Third, learning-based real-time fault diagnosis and monitoring require adaptive model updates that control computation, memory use, and latency overhead at the edge12,34. Although cross-device fusion methods have improved substantially, real-time control applications still face delayed response, limited discrimination of short-duration anomalies, weak handling of covariate and concept drift, high false-alarm rates, and reduced control reliability35,36,38.

This paper formulates the control-fusion problem as a joint optimization task over temporal alignment, anomaly discrimination, and update stability. The end-to-end evaluation reports reduced synchronization-offset error, lower Allan deviation, improved anomaly precision on the MVTec anomaly-detection dataset32,33,36, and faster adaptation under comparable workloads and varying noise levels34,37.

Overview of the proposed quantum-enhanced spiking neural network approach

The Quantum-Enhanced Spiking Neural Network (QESNN) is a multilayer real-time computing system tailored for the detection of anomalies in industrial settings using temporally structured data streams derived from a mix of different sensor modalities. In terms of time constraints, QESNN uses spiking computation together with parameterized quantum circuits. At the level of the first processing stage, input coding of simultaneous input from different sensor modalities involves converting observations of the sensors into states of quantum neurons. Data obtained from industrial sensors, such as thermal imaging, mechanical vibration, acoustic emission, current, pressure, and process-control sensors, are coded in quantum states characterized by amplitude–phase through the use of a parameterized quantum circuit. The early fusion approach allows multiple sensor data to be encoded into a single code in which amplitude–phase coding captures any statistical relationship that exists between sensors. A synchronization scheme that utilizes entangled reference states and measurement-based correction for synchronizing the phases of nodes and their respective paired nodes or reference states is built into QESNN. Such a strategy avoids timing differences between nodes and ensures proper event sequencing in the case of network delays. Anomaly detection is achieved using probabilistic spike activation in which spike coding is based on a tunneling dynamics model.

Parameter updates become adaptive with the use of time-dependent Hamiltonian dynamics along with annealing and feedback-correcting elements. Such update procedures allow for model adjustment under variable operating conditions, complying with both computational and storage capacity constraints imposed by distributed IIoT nodes. Instead of assuming a full-fledged fault-tolerant quantum processing framework, the QESNN models quantum neurons as logical quantum-pulse processors. Such an approach can still be implemented using near-future gate-based quantum computers as each quantum neuron is essentially a parameterized quantum circuit consisting of state preparation, shallow unitary evolution, measurement and classical postprocessing gates. Sensor signal preprocessing, spiking aggregation, threshold comparison, feedback correction, and decision making on anomalies are all classical operations, while the quantum part involves only compact state encoding, phase shifting, entanglement-enabled correlation computation, and fidelity estimation. This implementation reduces the depth of the required quantum circuit and eliminates the need for large single monolithic quantum state. For practical applications in NISQ devices, the number of active quantum units may be adjusted based on available qubit count, coherence time, gate error probability, and measurement shots. Noise-aware entanglement maintenance and refresh policy

The proposed Entanglement-Based Device Synchronization module does not require an indefinitely preserved large-scale entangled state across all distributed IIoT devices. Instead, entanglement is used as a short-horizon phase-correlation reference for synchronization, while supervisory signaling, correction commands, and state-refresh decisions are handled through classical communication channels. This design reduces the cost of maintaining a single global entangled state under industrial noise. The synchronization process is implemented through compact entangled reference blocks, coherence-quality monitoring, measurement-guided feedback, and conditional reinitialization when the reference state no longer satisfies the required stability bounds. Theoretical basis of quantum tunneling-driven probabilistic pulses

The probabilistic quantum spiking principle aims at overcoming the hard-threshold criterion present in classical spike detection via pulses. In the case of a typical spiking network neuron, the output spike will be generated when the value of the membrane potential exceeds a certain threshold. However, this type of threshold determination method will work well in cases where the deviation is very clear; in cases where the deviation is small, short-lived, or occurring in its early stages, the threshold will not recognize the deviation if the total accumulation level is still below the firing threshold level. The industrial anomaly data stream is characterized by borderline cases due to factors such as wear, thermal changes, acoustic distortion, pressure variation, and degradation processes that start off as weak disturbances.

The novel concept of probabilistic quantum spiking views the activation process for an anomaly detection problem as the interaction with an energy barrier. Simulation and performance evaluation

The experimental verification involved a quantum-classical hybrid simulation setup to investigate the proposed Quantum-Enhanced Spiking Neural Network architecture in NISQ-computable settings. The simulation setting was built using Python (v3.11.2) as the main programming language, leveraging Qiskit (v0.45.0) for designing parametric quantum circuits, simulating the dynamics of the quantum state, and performing simulations that considered realistic noise on the IBM Quantum Aer simulation backend. TensorFlow Quantum (v0.7.2) was used for combining quantum layers with spiking neural network computations and for tuning circuit parameters through iteration. Post-processing, performance evaluation, and visualization of results were accomplished using MATLAB R2023a. The MVTec Anomaly Detection dataset was chosen for evaluating the model’s performance because of its use in anomaly detection tasks in industry applications.

To ensure computational consistency with the available hardware resources, the term quantum neurons in this study refers to logical quantum-spiking computational units rather than simultaneously entangled physical qubits within a single exponentially scaled quantum register. Each quantum neuron consists of a relatively small parameterized quantum circuit unit which processes a certain amount of qubits; more complex networks can be built via repetition, batch processing, and hybrid mixing with classical spiking neural layers. Quantum neuron numbers up to 1024 refer to quantum neural network-level processing units and not multi-qubody quantum entanglement. The dataset includes more than 5000 high-definition images which cover multiple categories and display different defect types and normal operational behavior. Robustness verification under streaming and synchronization stress

For evaluating the stability of the synchronization-assisted quantum processing framework, the input stream speed was raised from a low-throughput speed to a high-throughput speed without changing any configuration for the QESNN architecture. This test stresses the synchronization module since higher speed implies higher levels of temporal skewing, queuing, and jitter. In terms of accuracy, the proposed approach remained stable under such conditions. Discussion

This section discusses the behavior of the proposed Quantum Enhanced Spiking Neural Network (QESNN) model under different operational conditions. These conditions particularly include temporal coherence, adaptive response to nonstationary data streams, and computations under resource constraints. The influence of the quantum enhancement techniques and spiking processes on the stability of the operation and detection efficiency in industrial environments is also analyzed. The baseline performance of the QESNN model using 512 logical quantum–spiking units reaches 98.7%. The maximal achievable accuracy is 99.1%, which is obtained by using 1024 units. It should be noted that the QESNN model consists of five modules that jointly perform. In particular, the QN-SDF module ensures the preservation of interchannel statistical dependencies using concurrent amplitude–phase encoding of multisensor information. The E-DS module uses entangled reference states to boost phase stability and decrease temporal skew between the sensing nodes. The PQS-AD module uses tunneling processes to generate spikes, thereby improving low-amplitude and transient anomalies discrimination efficiency. The AQL-DO module provides efficient updating of parameters in the presence of time-varying and noisy conditions. The QDM-RP module manages noise effects and ensures the stability of quantum-state processing.

For evaluating the robustness aspect, throughput was progressively increased and the accuracy was measured under various streaming velocities. The proposed framework maintained accuracy of 95.5% and 94.7% for 400 and 600 instances per second, respectively, whereas other techniques fell back to the 81%–88% accuracy range under equivalent conditions. The system further maintained anomaly detection precision above 97.8% across the evaluated defect categories. This includes metal surface defects, faulty components, as well as mixed anomalies. The ablation study demonstrated the impact of each module by isolating their functionality. Removing QN-SDF reduced accuracy to 92.5%. Removing E-DS reduced accuracy to 91.2%, while removing PQS-AD, AQL-DO, and QDM-RP reduced accuracy to 90.4%, 89.6%, and 88.9%, respectively. It can be deduced from the results that detection capability depends upon the interaction of features such as multi-feature representation, temporal synchronization, probabilistic modeling, adaptation, and noise handling.

Conclusion and future directions

This study presented the Quantum-Enhanced Spiking Neural Network (QESNN) for IIoT anomaly detection. The framework consists of five interconnected modules: Quantum Neurons for Sensor Data Fusion (QN-SDF), Entanglement-Based Device Synchronization (E-DS), Probabilistic Quantum Spiking for Anomaly Detection (PQS-AD), Adaptive Quantum Learning for Dynamic Optimization (AQL-DO), and Quantum Decoherence Management for Reliable Processing (QDM-RP). These modules combine amplitude–phase sensor encoding, entanglement-assisted synchronization, tunneling-driven probabilistic pulse generation, adaptive Hamiltonian-based learning, and decoherence-aware reliability control within a unified inference structure. Evaluation on the MVTec Anomaly Detection dataset shows that QESNN achieves 98.7% accuracy with 512 logical quantum–spiking units. This result exceeds Improved Multimodal Industrial Anomaly Detection at 94.8% and Multidimensional Industrial Anomaly Detection at 94.1%. The proposed framework also reduces average inference time to 3.29 s, compared with 4.85 s for the Multi-Head Framework and 5.07 s for the Multi-Scale Feature Extraction Graph. Under high-throughput evaluation, QESNN maintains 94.7% accuracy at 600 instances per second, compared with 83.9% for Deep Learning Anomaly Detection and 81.2% for the Dynamic Reconfiguration Scheduling Approach. The ablation results further confirm the contribution of each component, with the largest performance reduction observed when QDM-RP is removed. The robustness analysis indicates that entanglement-assisted synchronization is most effective when combined with compact reference blocks, measurement-guided refresh, and decoherence-aware reliability control. Future work will evaluate QESNN on hardware-constrained edge devices and quantum-compatible processing environments. Additional studies can also examine dynamic IIoT data streams, online drift adaptation, and deployment-aware optimization under limited qubit count, finite coherence time, and restricted measurement-shot budgets.

Generic models of licci ideals parametrized by Schur functors
Lorenzo Guerrieri, Xianglong Ni, Jerzy Weyman

Abstract
Let R R be a commutative Noetherian ring. Licci ideals are the ideals of R R that can be linked in a finite number of steps to a complete intersection. Each licci ideal admits a rigid deformation, and two licci ideals are in the same Herzog class if they have a common deformation. In this work, we show how all the Herzog classes of licci ideals of codimension 3 can be parametrized in terms of pairs of partitions associated to Schur functors. This fact allows to describe in a purely combinatorial way the (infinite) graph whose vertices correspond to Herzog classes and edges represent direct links between representatives of such classes. As applications, we obtain results on the classification of multiplications in Tor Algebras, and new structure theorems for families of licci ideals. In the final section, we extend many of these results to arbitrary codimension, but under some conjectural assumptions. MSC: 13D02, 13C05, 13C40. Keywords: licci ideals, free resolutions, Schur functors.



1
Introduction

Linkage of ideals in commutative rings arises as an algebraic counterpart of the theory of liaison of algebraic varieties.
This has been formalized in algebraic terms by Peskine and Szpiro in
[
36
]
. Successively, algebraic linkage has been deeply studied in many articles, including the celebrated one of Huneke and Ulrich
[
22
]
. The theory of linkage allows one to understand properties of ideals in terms of those of their linked ideals, and can be used as a strong tool in the classification of perfect ideals. In particular, a lot of studies have been focused on the ideals that can be linked to a complete intersection (i.e. to an ideal generated by a regular sequence) in a finite number of steps (see
[
22
]
,
[
40
]
,
[
21
]
, and related references).
Such ideals are called
licci
.
In codimension 2, licci ideals coincide with perfect ideals, but non-licci perfect ideals abound in codimension 3.

Recently, several new results have been obtained on the class of licci ideals of codimension 3, using methods coming from representation theory.
Let
I
be a perfect ideal of codimension 3 in a regular local (or graded) ring
R
. The free resolution of
R/I
has the form

\mathbb{F}:0\longrightarrow F_{3}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits^{d_{3}}}F_{2}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits^{d_{2}}}F_{1}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits^{d_{1}}}R\longrightarrow\frac{R}{I}.

We denote the rank of
F_{i}
by
r_{i}
and say that the complex
\mathbb{F}
has format
(1,r_{1},r_{2},r_{3})
.

In
[
44
]
, it is demonstrated how the structure theory of such free resolutions is related to a Kac-Moody Lie algebra, whose formal definition depends only on the format of the resolution. For most formats, the associated Kac-Moody Lie algebra is not finite dimensional. When the algebra is finite dimensional, the format in question is called a
Dynkin format
, and the ideals having minimal free resolutions of such formats are called
ideals of Dynkin type.

It is proved in
[
13
]
,
[
15
]
that perfect ideals of Dynkin type are all licci and they all appear as specializations of finitely many models, each of which can be described explicitly in terms of Schubert varieties. This generalizes the well-known structure theorems of Hilbert-Burch and Buchsbaum-Eisenbud (
[
11
]
,
[
3
]
,
[
4
]
) to other formats of resolutions. In
[
15
]
, it was also shown that the structure theorem relating licci ideals with specializations of Schubert varieties extends to all licci ideals of codimension 3 beyond Dynkin types. However, it was already known from
[
9
]
that for each non-Dynkin format of length 3, there exist perfect ideals with resolution of that format that are not licci.

Results in the same flavour are also obtained for Gorenstein ideals of codimension 4
[
7
]
,
[
45
]
.
Other recent valuable studies on licci ideals are devoted to describe properties related with their graded Betti numbers or to relate them with special type of points of the Hilbert scheme
[
23
]
.

The aim of this paper is to establish a combinatorial method to classify the models of licci ideals of codimension 3, and conjecturally also of arbitrary codimension. Our starting points are results of Buchweitz
[
5
]
and Herzog
[
17
]
.

Buchweitz proved that licci ideals are strongly unobstructed. Herzog showed that every strongly unobstructed ideal (in particular any licci ideal) is a specialization of a unique (up to trivial deformations) ideal defining a rigid algebra. Any deformation of a licci ideal is still a licci ideal and two ideals sharing a common deformation are said to be part of the same
Herzog class
(see Section
2.4
for precise definitions of these notions).

As a consequence of the results of the papers
[
13
]
,
[
15
]
, we classified the Herzog classes of licci ideals of codimension
3
. The representatives of these classes are the affine patches of certain opposite Schubert varieties in certain homogeneous space of the Kac-Moody group associated to the graph
T_{2,d+1,t+1}
(here
d
denotes the deviation of a given ideal, and
t
its Cohen-Macaulay type).

Similar examples of licci ideals can be found in an analogous homogeneous space related to the graph
T_{c-1,d+1,t+1}
for arbitrary codimension bigger than
3
. These examples are conjectured to provide a family of representative of all Herzog classes of licci ideals of arbitrary codimension.

For each codimension
c
, we may form a graph
\mathrm{Licci}_{c}
whose vertices index the Herzog classes of licci ideals of codimension
c
, and whose edges represent direct links. The goal of the present paper is to first present a combinatorial description of this graph for
c=3
, and then to extrapolate conjecturally for
c>3
. The vertices of the graph may be indexed by certain pairs of partitions
(\boldsymbol{\lambda},\boldsymbol{\mu})
where
|\boldsymbol{\lambda}|=(c-1)|\boldsymbol{\mu}|+1
. We will give both qualitative and algorithmic descriptions of the possible pairs of partitions that can occur. When describing vertices of
\mathrm{Licci}_{c}
, we will often use the notation
S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}F_{1}\otimes S_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}F_{c}^{*}
in place of the pair
(\boldsymbol{\lambda},\boldsymbol{\mu})
, where
F_{1}
,
F_{c}
are free modules in a minimal free resolution
\mathbb{F}_{\bullet}
of
R/I
and
I
is a licci ideal in the Herzog class associated to the given vertex. The reason for this is briefly explained in Section
2.4
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we provide all the needed preliminaries from Representation Theory (i.e. Kac-Moody Lie algebras, weights, representations) and from Commutative Algebra (licci ideals, Herzog classes, higher structure maps). Then, we recall the statement of one of the main result from
[
15
]
(see Theorem
2.1
). This result explains specifically the relation between Herzog classes and pairs of partitions.

In Section
3
, we first define the graph
\text{Licci}_{3}
. Then, in Theorem
3.1
, we explain how the pairs of partitions get modified when moving through the vertices of this graph. Indeed, given a pair of partitions
(\boldsymbol{\lambda},\boldsymbol{\mu})
associated to a vertex of
\text{Licci}_{3}
, we show how to obtain the pairs of partitions associated to the adjacent vertices. Subsequently, in Theorem
3.2
, we prove that the edge between two vertices of the graph exists if and only if there exist two licci ideals in the two Herzog classes corresponding to the vertices that are directly linked.

Section
3.3
is devoted to establish the notation that will be used to study the graph
\text{Licci}_{3}
with combinatorial methods and to relate the elements of the partition
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
with special systems of generators of ideals. The relation between partitions and free resolutions of ideals is further explored in Section
3.4
. It is presented an algorithm (Algorithm
3.16
) such that, combining the use of partitions with classical linkage theory, allows to construct inductively some representative for each Herzog class. Stronger results follow from Theorem
3.12
and Lemma
3.13
. It is shown that, in a graded setting (possibly non-standard), each Herzog class has some representative whose graded Betti numbers can be written explicitly in terms of the two associated partitions. If such partitions are
\boldsymbol{\lambda}=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{d+3})
,
\boldsymbol{\mu}=(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{t})
, and
k=\sum_{i=1}^{t}\mu_{i}
, then there exists a licci ideal
I
in the corresponding Herzog class having minimal graded free resolutions of the form:

\mathbb{F}:0\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{i=1}^{t}R(-k-1-\mu_{i})\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits^{d_{3}}}R^{d+t+2}(-k-1)\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits^{d_{2}}}\bigoplus_{i=1}^{d+3}R(-k-1+\lambda_{i})\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits^{d_{1}}}R.

Conversely, Lemma
3.13
shows that, under some mild assumptions, a licci ideal having such a graded minimal free resolution must be in the Herzog class associated to the pair
(\boldsymbol{\lambda},\boldsymbol{\mu})
. The proofs of the latter two results are based on the representation theoretic methods developed in the previous papers. For a deeper understanding of these methods, the reader will be referred to
[
15
,
\lx@sectionsign 3
-
\lx@sectionsign 5
]
.

In Section
4
, we list many examples of pairs of partitions corresponding to well-known Herzog classes of licci ideals of codimension 3. We cover explicitly all Gorenstein, almost complete intersections, hyperplane sections, and ideals of Dynkin type. For each Dynkin format there exist only finitely many Herzog classes of ideals with minimal free resolution of that format.
The smallest non-Dynkin format of free resolution is
(1,6,8,3)
. In Section
4.3
, we exhibit an infinite family of pairs of partitions (hence of distinct Herzog classes) associated to ideals with resolution of format
(1,6,8,3)
.

Section
5
is devoted to an inverse problem: understanding for which arbitrary pairs
(\boldsymbol{\lambda},\boldsymbol{\mu})
there exists an associated Herzog class. Any single instance of this problem has a clear algorithmic solution, which unfortunately may need long time for large values of
\lambda_{i},\mu_{j}
. The results in this section illustrate several simple restrictions that the elements
\lambda_{i},\mu_{j}
of partitions associated to Herzog classes must satisfy. The most interesting of them is the quadratic equation:

\sum\lambda_{i}^{2}+\sum\mu_{j}^{2}=(k+1)^{2}
.

In Section
6
, we illustrate how pairs of partitions can be used to understand the structure of the multiplication in the Tor Algebra Tor
(R/I,R/\mathfrak{m})
of licci ideals of codimension 3 (see
[
43
]
,
[
1
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
8
]
for a detailed background on this topic).
This structure is an invariant of the Herzog class, and the presence of units in the multiplication maps
\bigwedge^{2}F_{1}\to F_{2}
,
F_{1}\otimes F_{2}\to F_{3}
has an equivalent formulation in terms of
(\boldsymbol{\lambda},\boldsymbol{\mu})
, as pointed out in Theorem
6.1
. Sections
6.1
and
6.2
deal respectively with the partitions associated to ideals with Koszul relations among minimal syzygies, and with ideals of Tor Algebra class
G(r)
. For the latter family of ideals, we answer positively to a conjecture stated in
[
10
]
, showing that
r
cannot be too large in terms of the number of generators of
I
, unless
I
is Gorenstein.

Section
7
is dedicated to the study of the perfect ideals of codimension 3 which are the closest ones to the Gorenstein, both in the sense of partitions, and also in the sense that their free resolutions have the largest possible perfect pairing after the one of Gorenstein ideals.
In terms of Tor Algebra classes, we prove that this family is precisely identified by the class
G(b-3)
where
b
is the number of generators. We also describe the pairs of partitions associated to the ideals in this family, and provide a conjectural explicit structure theorem for the minimal free resolutions of the rigid algebras in such Herzog classes.

Finally, in Section
8
, we pass to discuss pairs of partitions in arbitrary codimension
c
.
The graph
\text{Licci}_{c}
and the combinatoric formula computing the partitions corresponding to adjacent vertices can be generalized exactly following the same methods used for
c=3
. However, the absence of a well-defined theory of generic rings and higher structure maps in arbitrary codimension does not allow to obtain a formal correspondence between pairs of partitions and Herzog classes of licci ideals. We propose this analogous correspondence in the form of a conjecture (see Conjecture
8.2
).
After that, we start exploring the theory of pairs of partitions proving several properties in higher codimension.
We conclude the paper by listing examples of several families of pairs of partitions, connecting them to known classes of licci ideals and to ideal-theoretic constructions, such as hyperplane sections and doublings.

Preliminaries

In this section we recall preliminary notions that will be needed for the rest of the paper. If not otherwise specified, our setting is the following.
By
R
we denote a commutative Noetherian ring. For simplicity, we assume
R
to be regular and local or graded, with maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
and infinite residue field

However, we point out that our results work also without assuming

We will mainly work with perfect ideals of

. For a perfect ideal

we will denote by

2.4
Licci ideals, Herzog classes, and higher structure maps

An ideal
I
of
R
is
perfect
if the quotient ring
R/I
is Cohen-Macaulay and has finite free resolution as
R
-module (equivalently if the projective dimension of
R/I
over
R
coincides with the codimension of
I
).

Two ideals
I,J
of
R
are said to be linked if there exists a regular sequence
\alpha\subseteq I\cap J
such that
I=(\alpha):J=\{x\in R\,|\,xJ\subseteq(\alpha)\}
and
J=(\alpha):I
. If two ideals are linked, then they have the same height and one of the two is perfect if and only if the other one is perfect. Working over a regular ring, if
I
is a perfect ideal of height
c
and
\alpha
is a regular sequence of length
c
contained in
I
, then
(\alpha):I
is linked to
I
. An ideal is
licci
if it can be linked to a complete intersection in finitely many steps. Licci ideals are clearly perfect.

Given two pairs of ring-ideal
(R,I)
and
(S,J)
, we say that
(R,I)
is a
specialization
of
(S,J)
if
(R,I)=\left(\frac{S}{(\boldsymbol{a})},\frac{J+(\boldsymbol{a})}{(\boldsymbol{a})}\right)
where
\boldsymbol{a}
is a regular sequence in
S
whose image in
\frac{S}{J}
is also regular.
At the same time we say that
(S,J)
is a
deformation
of
(R,I)
.

A trivial deformation of a pair
(R,I)
is a deformation of the form

(R[z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}],IR[z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}]),

or a similar local version defined with formal power series.
In classical deformation theory, ideals defining rigid algebras are defined in terms of certain functors
T_{i}
. For the purpose of this project, we say that an ideal
I
is
rigid
if
(R,I)
admits only trivial deformations.

By results of Herzog
[
17
]
and Buchweitz
[
5
]
, licci ideals always admit rigid deformations. We say that two licci ideals that have a common rigid deformation are in the same
Herzog class
.
As a consequence of Hilbert-Burch Theorem, in codimension
2
, the rigid ideals are exactly the ideals of maximal minors of generic
n\times(n-1)
matrices and there is exactly one Herzog class for each
n\geq 2
.

We can associate to each Herzog class in codimension
3
a pair of partitions associated to certain Schur functors. We give a brief explanation of this method, referring
[
15
]
,
[
33
]
, for further details.

Let
I
be a licci ideal of codimension 3 in the ring
R
. The minimal free resolution of
R/I
has the form

\mathbb{F}:0\longrightarrow F_{3}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits^{d_{3}}}F_{2}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits^{d_{2}}}F_{1}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits^{d_{1}}}R\longrightarrow\frac{R}{I}.

As done in the introduction we denote by
r_{i}
the rank of
F_{i}
and say that the complex
\mathbb{F}
has format
(1,r_{1},r_{2},r_{3})
, where
r_{1}=3+d
and
r_{3}=t
.

Let us consider the Kac-Moody Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
corresponding to the graph
T(d,t)
. Some of the main tools used to develop the theory in
[
13
]
,
[
15
]
and related papers, are three fundamental representations of this Lie algebra, corresponding to the three extremal vertices of the diagram. These three fundamental representations are
\mathbb{N}
-graded and appear in the generic ring
\widehat{R}_{gen}
associated to the format of the free resolution (see
[
44
]
). Therefore, each direct summand of their graded components can be mapped to
R
. This induces a sequence of
R
-linear maps involving Schur functors in the modules
F_{i}
appearing in
\mathbb{F}
. These maps are called
higher structure maps
(see also
[
16
]
,
[
14
]
,
[
12
]
for explicit definitions and computations of these maps). The linear maps associated to zero graded components of these representations correspond naturally to the three differentials
d_{1},d_{2},d_{3}
of the complex
\mathbb{F}
. For this reason the three fundamental representations are denoted respectively by
W(d_{1}),W(d_{2}),W(d_{3})
.

In
[
3
]
, it is shown that any free resolution
\mathbb{F}
of length 3 carries an associative multiplicative structure, described by three standard maps
\bigwedge^{2}F_{1}\to F_{2},F_{1}\otimes F_{2}\to F_{3},\bigwedge^{3}F_{1}\to F_{3}
. Such maps are computed by lifting the maps obtained from the comparison between the Koszul complex defined on the generators of
I
and the complex
\mathbb{F}
. The first graded components of the three fundamental representations correspond exactly to these three linear maps defining the multiplicative structure.
The successive graded components encode further structure which was unknown before the work done in
[
44
]
. See
[
31
]
for the description of all the components of these representation in the case of Dynkin formats.

The representation
W(d_{1})
has the form

W(d_{1})=F_{1}\oplus\bigwedge^{3}F_{1}\otimes F_{3}^{*}\oplus\bigwedge^{5}F_{1}\otimes S_{2}F_{3}^{*}\oplus\bigwedge^{4}F_{1}\otimes F_{1}\otimes\bigwedge^{2}F_{3}^{*}\oplus\ldots\oplus S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}F_{1}\otimes S_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}F_{3}^{*}\oplus\ldots,

such that in the
k
-th graded component the Schur functors appearing are of the form
S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}F_{1}\otimes S_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}F_{3}^{*}
where
\boldsymbol{\mu}
is a partition of
k
and
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
is a partition of
2k+1
.
Each of these pairs of Schur functors is associated to a linear map

w^{(1)}_{k,j}=w^{(1)}_{k,j}(I):S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}F_{1}\to S_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}F_{3},

which can be computed over the ring
R
in terms of data from the complex
\mathbb{F}
(the index
k
denotes the graded component from which the linear map comes from, while the index
j
is used to denote possible different maps coming from the same component).
We have that

w^{(1)}_{0}=d_{1}:F_{1}\to R
and
w^{(1)}_{1}:\bigwedge^{3}F_{1}\to F_{3}
are respectively the first differential of
\mathbb{F}
and the multiplication map obtained from the comparison with the Koszul complex.
Also some of the successive maps can be computed explicitly by lifting cycles defined in terms of previous higher structure maps (coming also from the other fundamental representations) over some exact complex. In particular higher structure maps are not unique.

A key fact used in
[
15
]
is that the higher structure maps transform elegantly under linkage. Each vertex of the diagram
T
induces a grading on the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
and its representations. Let
\begin{bmatrix}\alpha_{1}&\alpha_{2}&\alpha_{3}\end{bmatrix}
be the restriction of
w^{(1)}
to its 3-dimensional bottom
(y_{1},z_{1})
-bigraded component. If the
\alpha_{i}
form a regular sequence, then the restriction of
w^{(1)}
to its bottom
y_{1}
-graded component yields the ideal
I^{\prime}=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3}):I
. Furthermore,
R/I^{\prime}
admits a resolution
\mathbb{F}^{\prime}
of format
(1,3+t,2+d+t,d)
and a choice of
w^{\prime}
specializing the generic free resolution to
\mathbb{F}^{\prime}
such that
w^{\prime(1)}=w^{(1)}
if we identify
T(d,t)
and
T(t,d)
in the evident way.

We may paraphrase one of the main results of
[
15
]
as follows.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{m}
be the maximal ideal of
R
and
\Bbbk=R/\mathfrak{m}
the residue field. Let
I\subset R
be a perfect ideal of height 3, and suppose we have computed
w^{(1)}
for a resolution of
R/I
. The ideal
I
is licci exactly when
w^{(1)}\otimes\Bbbk\neq 0
. Assuming this to be the case,
I
is classified up to deformation by the lowest (in Bruhat order) extremal
\mathfrak{gl}(F_{1})\times\mathfrak{gl}(F_{3})
-representation
S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}F_{1}\otimes S_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}F_{3}^{*}
on which
w^{(1)}\otimes\Bbbk
is nonzero, i.e. it is classified by the map

w^{(1)}_{k,j}=w^{(1)}_{k,j}(I):S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}F_{1}\to S_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}F_{3}

with minimal
k=\kappa(I)
that is nonzero mod
\mathfrak{m}
.

In fact, there exists a choice of
\mathbb{F}
and
w
such that
w^{(1)}\otimes\Bbbk
is nonzero only on the lowest weight space of
S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}F_{1}\to S_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}F_{3}
, which has weight
-\sigma\omega_{x_{1}}
for some
\sigma\in\mathchoice{\mathop{}\kern 8.51051pt\mathopen{\vphantom{W}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{z_{1}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 8.51051pt\mathopen{\vphantom{W}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{z_{1}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 11.32022pt\mathopen{\vphantom{W}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{z_{1}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 12.587pt\mathopen{\vphantom{W}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{z_{1}}}}}W(d,t)^{x_{1}}
. We refer to the combinatorial data of either
\sigma
or equivalently
(\boldsymbol{\lambda},\boldsymbol{\mu})
as the Herzog class of
I
. Every
\sigma\in\mathchoice{\mathop{}\kern 8.51051pt\mathopen{\vphantom{W}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{z_{1}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 8.51051pt\mathopen{\vphantom{W}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{z_{1}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 11.32022pt\mathopen{\vphantom{W}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{z_{1}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 12.587pt\mathopen{\vphantom{W}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{z_{1}}}}}W(d,t)^{x_{1}}
is the Herzog class of some codimension 3 licci ideal with deviation
\leq d
and type
\leq t
, with the exception of
\sigma=\mathrm{id}
which corresponds to the unit ideal.

We can therefore canonically associate a pair of Schur functors to each height 3 licci ideal
I
:

\mathcal{S}_{I}=S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}F_{1}\otimes S_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}F_{3}^{*},

(2.3)

where
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
is a partition of
2k+1
and
\boldsymbol{\mu}
is a partition of
k
. For example, complete intersections are characterized by the fact that the multiplication
\bigwedge^{3}\operatorname{Tor}_{1}(R/I,\Bbbk)\to\operatorname{Tor}_{3}(R/I,\Bbbk)
is nonzero. This multiplication appears inside of
w^{(1)}\otimes\Bbbk
:

\left(\bigwedge^{3}F_{1}\otimes F_{3}^{*}\right)\otimes\Bbbk\subset L(\omega_{x_{1}})^{\vee}\otimes\Bbbk\to\Bbbk.

Thus, for a complete intersection
I
we have
\mathcal{S}_{I}=\bigwedge^{3}F_{1}\otimes F_{3}^{*}
i.e.
\boldsymbol{\lambda}=(1,1,1)
and
\boldsymbol{\mu}=(1)
.

Furthermore, higher structure maps behave well with respect to deformations and specializations. Units of a ring can deform only to units and non-units can deform only to non-units.
Hence, for any licci ideal
I
of codimension 3, the non-vanishing of the map
w^{(1)}_{k,j}(I)
modulo
\mathfrak{m}
identifies univocally a rigid ideal
\tilde{I}
such that
\mathcal{S}_{I}=\mathcal{S}_{\tilde{I}}
.

3
Licci ideals and partitions in codimension 3

3.1
Definition of
\mathrm{Licci}_{3}

We now define
\mathrm{Licci}_{3}
, a graph that encodes the links between different families of codimension 3 licci ideals (Theorem

). We first define a bipartite graph
\mathrm{Licci}^{\mathrm{bi}}_{3}

\mathchoice{\mathop{}\kern 8.51051pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\overline{W}}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{z_{1}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 8.51051pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\overline{W}}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{z_{1}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 8.03131pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\overline{W}}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{z_{1}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 8.03131pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\overline{W}}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{z_{1}}}}}\overline{W}^{x_{1}}\amalg\mathchoice{\mathop{}\kern 8.70958pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\overline{W}}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{y_{1}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 8.70958pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\overline{W}}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{y_{1}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 8.23503pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\overline{W}}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{y_{1}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 8.23503pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\overline{W}}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{y_{1}}}}}\overline{W}^{x_{1}}

\mathchoice{\mathop{}\kern 18.984pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\overline{W}}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{y_{1},z_{1}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 18.984pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\overline{W}}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{y_{1},z_{1}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 17.78026pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\overline{W}}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{y_{1},z_{1}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 17.78026pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\overline{W}}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{y_{1},z_{1}}}}}\overline{W}^{x_{1}}\subset{\overline{W}}

There is a natural correspondence between the two sets of vertices
\mathchoice{\mathop{}\kern 8.51051pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\overline{W}}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{z_{1}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 8.51051pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\overline{W}}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{z_{1}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 8.03131pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\overline{W}}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{z_{1}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 8.03131pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\overline{W}}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{z_{1}}}}}\overline{W}^{x_{1}}\xrightarrow{\chi}\mathchoice{\mathop{}\kern 8.70958pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\overline{W}}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{y_{1}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 8.70958pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\overline{W}}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{y_{1}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 8.23503pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\overline{W}}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{y_{1}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 8.23503pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\overline{W}}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{y_{1}}}}}\overline{W}^{x_{1}}

and

arms of

. The graph
\mathrm{Licci}_{3}
is the quotient of

by this identification; explicitly it has vertex set
\mathchoice{\mathop{}\kern 8.51051pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\overline{W}}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{z_{1}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 8.51051pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\overline{W}}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{z_{1}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 8.03131pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\overline{W}}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{z_{1}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 8.03131pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\overline{W}}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{z_{1}}}}}\overline{W}^{x_{1}}

Note that this is an undirected graph with loops.

One can analogously define
\mathrm{Licci}^{\mathrm{bi}}_{3}(d,t)
to be the subgraph of

obtained by using

in place of

.

3.2
Main properties of
\mathrm{Licci}_{3}

In the language of partitions
(\boldsymbol{\lambda},\boldsymbol{\mu})
(see Sections
2.3
-
2.4
), there is another equivalent characterization of edges in
\mathrm{Licci}_{3}
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\sigma\in\mathchoice{\mathop{}\kern 8.51051pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\overline{W}}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{z_{1}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 8.51051pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\overline{W}}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{z_{1}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 8.03131pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\overline{W}}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{z_{1}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 8.03131pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\overline{W}}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{z_{1}}}}}\overline{W}^{x_{1}}
correspond to the pair of partitions
(\boldsymbol{\lambda},\boldsymbol{\mu})
, where
\boldsymbol{\lambda}=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots)
and
\boldsymbol{\mu}=(\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\ldots)
are viewed as infinite lists with trailing zeros. Let
\lambda^{\prime}_{1}\geq\lambda^{\prime}_{2}\geq\lambda^{\prime}_{3}
be parts of
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
(possibly equal to zero) and let
\boldsymbol{\mu}^{\mathrm{link}}
be the partition obtained from
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
after removing these parts. Define

\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\mathrm{link}}=\operatorname{rsort}(\lambda^{\prime}_{1}+p,\lambda^{\prime}_{2}+p,\lambda^{\prime}_{3}+p,\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\mu_{3},\ldots).

where
\operatorname{rsort}
means to sort in non-increasing order and

p=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\mu^{\mathrm{link}}_{j}-\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\mu_{j}.

Then the pair
(\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\mathrm{link}},\boldsymbol{\mu}^{\mathrm{link}})
corresponds to some
\sigma^{\mathrm{link}}\in\mathchoice{\mathop{}\kern 8.51051pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\overline{W}}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{z_{1}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 8.51051pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\overline{W}}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{z_{1}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 8.03131pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\overline{W}}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{z_{1}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 8.03131pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\overline{W}}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{z_{1}}}}}\overline{W}^{x_{1}}

Proof.

This is mainly an exercise in translating weights to partitions. Let

represent an edge of

between

\chi(\sigma^{\mathrm{link}})\in\mathchoice{\mathop{}\kern 8.70958pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\overline{W}}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{y_{1}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 8.70958pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\overline{W}}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{y_{1}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 8.23503pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\overline{W}}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{y_{1}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 8.23503pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\overline{W}}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{y_{1}}}}}\overline{W}^{x_{1}}

\displaystyle\tilde{\sigma}\omega_{x_{1}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in\overline{T}}\tilde{a}_{i}\omega_{i}

\displaystyle\sigma\omega_{x_{1}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in\overline{T}}a_{i}\omega_{i}

\displaystyle\chi(\sigma^{\mathrm{link}})\omega_{x_{1}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in\overline{T}}a^{\prime}_{i}\omega_{i}

where
\tilde{a}_{i}\geq 0
for all

,

We have that
\tilde{\sigma}=\tau\sigma

Let

\displaystyle\tilde{\lambda}^{\mathrm{link}}_{i}-\tilde{\lambda}_{i}

∎

Theorem 3.2
.

Let

be a codimension 3 licci ideal. Let
\sigma\in\mathchoice{\mathop{}\kern 8.51051pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\overline{W}}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{z_{1}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 8.51051pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\overline{W}}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{z_{1}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 8.03131pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\overline{W}}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{z_{1}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 8.03131pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\overline{W}}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{z_{1}}}}}\overline{W}^{x_{1}}

Then there is an edge connecting

on
\mathrm{Licci}_{3}
if and only if there exists a codimension 3 licci ideal

.

Proof.

We may equivalently work with the graph
\mathrm{Licci}^{\mathrm{bi}}_{3}
replacing
\sigma^{\prime}
with
\chi(\sigma^{\prime})\in\mathchoice{\mathop{}\kern 8.70958pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\overline{W}}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{y_{1}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 8.70958pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\overline{W}}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{y_{1}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 8.23503pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\overline{W}}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{y_{1}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 8.23503pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\overline{W}}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{y_{1}}}}}\overline{W}^{x_{1}}

. Add a split exact part to this resolution so that it has format

. Fix a homomorphism

. By adjusting

is nonzero only on the lowest weight space of a single extremal

-representation. The lowest weight of this representation is

where

.

For the “if” implication, suppose that we have an ideal

directly linked to

After applying this action,

. This weight is in the same

For the “only if” implication, suppose that

\tilde{\sigma}\in\mathchoice{\mathop{}\kern 18.984pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\overline{W}}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{y_{1},z_{1}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 18.984pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\overline{W}}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{y_{1},z_{1}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 17.78026pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\overline{W}}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{y_{1},z_{1}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 17.78026pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\overline{W}}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{y_{1},z_{1}}}}}\overline{W}^{x_{1}}

Definition 3.3
.

Let
I\subset R
be a codimension 3 licci ideal. Choose
w
as in the proof of Theorem
3.2
so that
w^{(1)}\otimes\Bbbk
is nonzero only on the lowest weight space of a single extremal
\mathfrak{gl}(F_{1})\times\mathfrak{gl}(F_{3})
-representation. The bottom graded component of

is a map

whose image is

; say its entries are

w^{(1)}_{0}=\begin{bmatrix}h_{1}&\cdots&h_{b}\end{bmatrix}

where we use the increasing weight basis of

(so that e.g.

corresponds to the lowest weight space).
We then say that the ordered list

is a

(SGS) for the ideal

.

The significance of SGSs will be explained shortly, in Remark
3.6
. Since higher structure maps specialize, if

is a local homomorphism and

has codimension 3, then

is an SGS for

.

Example 3.4
.

If

is a codimension 3 Gorenstein ideal and

is a minimal free resolution of

, then it turns out that the condition on

3.3
Combinatorics of links of pairs of partitions

Theorems
3.1
and
3.2
have many interesting consequences. In particular they allow us to explore Herzog classes of codimension 3 licci ideals with the use of simple combinatorial methods.

If a pair of partitions
(\boldsymbol{\lambda},\boldsymbol{\mu})
yields a valid Herzog class in the sense of Theorem
2.1
, we say it is a
decoration
of a vertex of
\mathrm{Licci}_{3}
. Equivalently, we say also that
\mathcal{S}_{I}
is a decoration if we need to identify a specific representative of the Herzog class.
In this case we have
\sum\mu_{i}=k=\kappa(I)
and
\sum\lambda_{i}=2k+1
.
This quantity
\kappa(I)
corresponds to the graded component of
W(d_{1})
where the higher structure maps associated to
I
sits. Roughly speaking, it is a measure of the complexity of the licci ideal
I
. We say that
k=\kappa(I)
is the
level
of
I
.
We say that two decorations are linked if the corresponding vertices are adjacent in
\mathrm{Licci}_{3}
.

We now give several remarks obtained as consequences of the results in the previous subsection.

Remark 3.5
.

Let
I
be a licci ideal of codimension 3 minimally generated by
b=d+3
elements and having Cohen-Macaulay type
t
.
Suppose that the Herzog class of
I
is associated to the decoration
(\boldsymbol{\lambda},\boldsymbol{\mu})
.
Then
\lambda_{i}\neq 0
if and only if
i\leq b
and
\mu_{i}\neq 0
if and only if
i\leq t
.

Remark 3.6
.

Let
I
be a licci ideal of codimension 3 with decoration

(\boldsymbol{\lambda},\boldsymbol{\mu})=((\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{b}),(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{t})).

We fix an SGS
x_{1},\ldots,x_{b}
of
I
as in Definition
3.3
. Let
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
be a regular sequence of length 3 in
I
.
Since we work over a local or graded ring with infinite residue field, by changing the basis of the ideal generated by
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
, we can set
\boldsymbol{\alpha}=\{a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}\}
such that for
j=1,2,3
:

•

a_{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{d+3}u_{ij}x_{i}
with
u_{ij}\in R
.

•

If
u_{ij}
is a unit, then for every
l\neq j
,
u_{il}=0
and for every
r\neq i
,
u_{rj}\in\mathfrak{m}
.

For
j=1,2,3
, set
\lambda_{j}^{\prime}=\lambda_{i}
if
u_{ij}
is a unit and
\lambda_{j}^{\prime}=0
if
a_{j}\in\mathfrak{m}I
(none of the coefficients
u_{ij}
is a unit). By convention, we permute the generators in such a way that
\lambda_{1}^{\prime}\geq\lambda_{2}^{\prime}\geq\lambda_{3}^{\prime}.
We set then
\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\prime\prime}:=\boldsymbol{\lambda}\setminus\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\prime}.

Let
J=(\boldsymbol{\alpha}):I
and denote by
G_{1}
and
G_{3}
the first and last module in the minimal free resolution of
R/J
. The proofs of Theorems
3.1
and
3.2
show that the decoration of
J
is

S_{\lambda^{\prime}_{1}+p,\lambda^{\prime}_{2}+p,\lambda^{\prime}_{3}+p,\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{t}}G_{1}\otimes S_{\lambda_{1}^{\prime\prime},\ldots,\lambda^{\prime\prime}_{b}}G_{3}^{*},

(3.1)

where
p=\kappa(J)-\kappa(I)
and the first partition has to be reordered in non-increasing order.

The key point (and the reason why an SGS is necessary, as opposed to an arbitrary generating set) is that we may precompose
w^{(1)}
with the action of an appropriate element of
\mbox{\rm GL}(F_{1})
that moves our regular sequence to the bottom
(y_{1},z_{1})
-bigraded component while having
w^{(1)}\otimes\Bbbk
be nonzero only on the extremal weight space with weight
-\tilde{\sigma}\omega_{x_{1}}
, where
\tilde{\sigma}
is as in the proof of Theorem
3.1
. This element of
\mbox{\rm GL}(F_{1})
may be taken to be the product of a lower-triangular matrix and a permutation matrix.

Remark 3.7
.

The integer
p
represents how much
\kappa(J)
increase or decrease with respect to
k=\kappa(I)
. Recalling that
\sum\lambda_{i}=2k+1
, we obtain the equation

p=\sum_{i=1}^{b}\lambda^{\prime\prime}_{i}-k=k+1-\lambda_{1}^{\prime}-\lambda_{2}^{\prime}-\lambda_{3}^{\prime}.

(3.2)

Classically, a link from an ideal
I
to a linked ideal
J=(\boldsymbol{\alpha}):I
is called
minimal
if the elements of
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
are in
I\setminus\mathfrak{m}I
.

For this reason, we define a link from
\mathcal{S}_{I}
to
\mathcal{S}_{J}
to be
minimal
if
\lambda_{1}^{\prime},\lambda_{2}^{\prime},\lambda_{3}^{\prime}\neq 0
.
A link
\mathcal{S}_{J}
of a decoration
\mathcal{S}_{I}
is the
smallest minimal
if it is obtained choosing
\lambda^{\prime}_{j}=\lambda_{j}
for
j=1,2,3
; in this case
p
is the minimal possible. The largest possible

is achieved if
\lambda_{j}^{\prime}=0
for
j=1,2,3
. This case corresponds to what is classically denoted as a generic link.

More specifically, we identify four relevant examples of links of decorations that will be useful later in the paper to study several applications of this theory.

Example 3.8
.

(Smallest minimal linkage)

Here
\lambda^{\prime}_{i}=\lambda_{i}
for
i=1,2,3
,
\lambda^{\prime\prime}_{i}=\lambda_{i+3}
for
i=1,\ldots,d
and

p=\sum_{i\geq 4}\lambda_{i}-k.
Hence,

\mathcal{S}_{J}=\mbox{\rm min}\mbox{\rm min}(\mathcal{S})=S_{\lambda_{1}+p,\lambda_{2}+p,\lambda_{3}+p,\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{t}}G_{1}\otimes S_{\lambda_{4},\ldots,\lambda_{d+3}}G_{3}^{*}.

Example 3.9
.

(Generic linkage)

In this case
\lambda^{\prime}_{i}=0
for
i=1,2,3
,
\lambda^{\prime\prime}_{i}=\lambda_{i}
for
i=1,\ldots,d+3
and

p=\sum_{i\geq 1}\lambda_{i}-k=k+1
is the maximal possible. Hence,

\mathcal{S}_{J}=\mbox{\rm gen}(\mathcal{S})=S_{k+1,k+1,k+1,\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{t}}G_{1}\otimes S_{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{d+3}}G_{3}^{*}.

Example 3.10
.

(Largest minimal linkage)

This link is the minimal link (in the classical sense) for which
p
is the maximal possible. It often allows to link in such a way to increase the level
k
without increasing the total Betti number of the ideal.
Here
\lambda^{\prime}_{i}=\lambda_{d+i}
for
i=1,2,3
,
\lambda^{\prime\prime}_{i}=\lambda_{i}
for
i=1,\ldots,d
and

p=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\lambda_{i}-k.
Hence,

\mathcal{S}_{J}=\mbox{\rm max}\mbox{\rm min}(\mathcal{S})=S_{\lambda_{d+1}+p,\lambda_{d+2}+p,\lambda_{d+3}+p,\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{t}}G_{1}\otimes S_{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{d}}G_{3}^{*}.

Example 3.11
.

(Minimal tight double link)

This is a canonical double link which can be used to prove statements depending inductively on
k
. The idea is based on the tight double linkage, classically considered in
[
30
]
,
[
40
]
and several more papers.
Here, one first takes the smallest minimal link
\mathcal{S}_{J}
and then define a second link with respect to the three elements
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\mu_{1}
. The obtained decoration is (up to reordering)

\mathcal{S}_{I^{\prime}}=S_{\lambda_{1}+p+q,\lambda_{2}+p+q,\mu_{1}+q,\lambda_{4},\ldots,\lambda_{d+3}}G_{1}\otimes S_{\lambda_{3}+p,\mu_{2},\ldots,\mu_{t}}G_{3}^{*},

where
p=k+1-\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{3}
and
q=k+p+1-\lambda_{1}-p-\lambda_{2}-p-\mu_{1}=\lambda_{3}-\mu_{1}
(thus
\mu_{1}+q=\lambda_{3}
). In Proposition
5.1
(2), we will prove that the inequality
\lambda_{3}+p<\mu_{1}
is always satisfied and therefore
\kappa(I^{\prime})<\kappa(I)
. Furthermore, notice that the type of
I^{\prime}
is either
t
or
t-1
.

3.4
Constructing ideals in different Herzog classes

Remark
3.6
demonstrates how, if we have an SGS for a codimension 3 licci ideal
I
, we can easily determine the Herzog class of ideals
J
directly linked to
I
. However, we do not know how to explicitly compute an SGS for
J
given the SGS for
I
, which poses an obstacle to iterating this procedure.

The goal of this section is to circumvent this and provide an algorithm for exhibiting representatives of all vertices on
\mathrm{Licci}_{3}
. The main point is to show that for homogeneous licci ideals
I
whose graded free resolutions have a particular form, it is easy to read off
\mathcal{S}_{I}
as well as an SGS for
I
. Furthermore, this property of the graded free resolution is preserved under homogeneous linkage (with some mild restrictions).

We first note that, for a given
\mathcal{S}=S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}F_{1}\otimes S_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}F_{3}^{*}
, there exist ideals
I
with
\mathcal{S}_{I}=\mathcal{S}
whose free resolutions have a specific form:

Theorem 3.12
.

Let
\mathcal{S}=S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}F_{1}\otimes S_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}F_{3}^{*}
be a decoration of some licci ideal of codimension 3. Set

\boldsymbol{\lambda}=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{d+3})
,
\boldsymbol{\mu}=(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{t})
and
k=\sum^{t}_{i=1}\mu_{i}
. Then there exists a homogeneous ideal
I
in a polynomial ring
R
with ideal of variables
\mathfrak{m}
such that
I_{\mathfrak{m}}
is licci, with
\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{S}_{I_{\mathfrak{m}}}
, and the quotient ring
R/I
has minimal graded free resolution of the form

\mathbb{F}:0\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{i=1}^{t}R(-k-1-\mu_{i})\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits^{d_{3}}}R^{d+t+2}(-k-1)\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits^{d_{2}}}\bigoplus_{i=1}^{d+3}R(-k-1+\lambda_{i})\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits^{d_{1}}}R.

(3.3)

Proof.

We may use the generic example of the Herzog class
\mathcal{S}
, whose free resolution is given in
[
15
, §3]
. Indeed, this example is obtained by localizing an ideal
I
in a polynomial ring
R
at its ideal of variables. The free resolution of
R/I
is
\mathbb{Z}^{d+t+2}
-graded, but there exists a particular coarsening to a
\mathbb{Z}
-grading, described in
[
15
, Example 3.7]
, that yields the graded free resolution indicated above.

In more detail: the ring
R
is the coordinate ring of a Schubert cell
C_{\sigma}
, and the grading group
\mathbb{Z}^{d+t+2}
is the root lattice, corresponding geometrically to the torus action on
C_{\sigma}
. We omit the details here, because they would require a significant digression into representation theory.
∎

An alternative proof of this theorem is given by Algorithm
3.16
.

The resolution of
R/I
mentioned in the preceding proof admits a particularly nice choice of higher structure maps; see
[
15
, §5]
. In particular, for the map

w^{(1)}\colon L(\omega_{x_{1}})^{\vee}\otimes R=[F_{1}\oplus\bigwedge^{3}F_{1}\otimes F_{3}^{*}\oplus\cdots]\otimes R\to R,

the only degree zero part of the free module
L(\omega_{x_{1}})^{\vee}\otimes R
is the lowest weight space of the representation
\mathcal{S}=S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}F_{1}\otimes S_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}F_{3}^{*}
. The map
w^{(1)}
is homogeneous of degree zero, hence the condition of Definition
3.3
is automatically satisfied and we conclude that the entries of
d_{1}
form an SGS for
I_{\mathfrak{m}}
. The ordered basis of
F_{1}
is important; here it is ordered in non-decreasing degree.

The key observation is that this fact is purely a consequence of the grading on the free resolution. Using this, we see that certain codimension 3 licci ideals can be classified just using their graded Betti numbers:

Lemma 3.13
.

Let
\mathcal{S}=S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}F_{1}\otimes S_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}F_{3}
be a decoration of some vertex in
\mathrm{Licci}_{3}
. Let

\boldsymbol{\lambda}=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{d+3})
,
\boldsymbol{\mu}=(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{t})
and
k=\sum^{t}_{i=1}\mu_{i}
.

Suppose
R
is graded and
I\subset R
is a homogeneous codimension 3 perfect ideal such that
R/I
has a graded free resolution of the form

\mathbb{F}\colon 0\to\bigoplus_{i=1}^{t}R(-k-1-\mu_{i})\to F_{2}\to\bigoplus_{i=1}^{d+3}R(-k-1+\lambda_{i})\to R.

Let
\mathfrak{p}\subset R
be a prime ideal such that
R_{i}\subset\mathfrak{p}
for all
i\neq 0
. If
I_{\mathfrak{p}}
is a licci ideal in
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
, then:

1.

\mathcal{S}_{I}=\mathcal{S}
.

2.

Any minimal generating set for
I
, ordered so that the degrees are non-decreasing, forms an SGS for
I_{\mathfrak{p}}
after localization.

3.

F_{2}=R^{d+t+2}(-k-1)
.

Remark 3.14
.

If
I\subset R
is a homogeneous codimension 3 perfect ideal with graded free resolution as in the preceding lemma, we expect that
I_{\mathfrak{p}}
is necessarily licci in
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
(i.e. that condition is superfluous in the above statement).

Proof.

Let
w\colon\widehat{R}_{\mathrm{gen}}\to R
be a homomorphism specializing the generic free resolution of format
(1,3+d,2+d+t,t)
to
\mathbb{F}
. The grading on
\mathbb{F}
induces one on
\widehat{R}_{\mathrm{gen}}
, and the map
w
may be chosen to be homogeneous of degree zero. By assumption, the graded modules
F_{1}
and
F_{3}
in the free resolution are the same as in Theorem
3.12
. As discussed above, this results in

having only a single generator in degree zero: the lowest weight space of
S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}F_{1}\otimes S_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}F_{3}^{*}
. Since
I_{\mathfrak{p}}
is licci, the entries of
w^{(1)}\otimes R_{\mathfrak{p}}
must contain a unit, and this must correspond to the aforementioned lowest weight space. The conclusions (1) and (2) then follow.

The location of the unit in
w^{(1)}
implies that
I
is a specialization of the generic example as referenced in the proof of Theorem
3.12
, prior to localization. Since the degrees coincide on
w^{(1)}
, this specialization is degree-preserving. In particular, the graded free resolution for the generic example specializes to one for our particular ideal with the same shifts, establishing (3).
∎

Lemma 3.15
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
,
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
,
\boldsymbol{\mu}
,
k
, and
I\subset R
be as in the Lemma
3.13
. Let each of
\lambda^{\prime}_{1}\geq\lambda^{\prime}_{2}\geq\lambda^{\prime}_{3}
be either a part of

or equal to zero, and denote (
3.1
) by
\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{link}}
.

Let
x_{1},\ldots,x_{d+3}
be any minimal generating list for
I
with
\mbox{\rm deg}(x_{i})=k+1-\lambda_{i}
. For
i=1,2,3
, take
\alpha_{i}
as follows:

•

If
\lambda^{\prime}_{i}=\lambda_{j}
, let
\alpha_{i}=x_{j}
.

•

If
\lambda^{\prime}_{i}=0
, let
\alpha_{i}
be any element of
\mathfrak{m}I
of degree
k+1
.

Suppose that
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3}
is a regular sequence. Then, letting

, the graded free resolution of

has the form given in Lemma
3.13
with
\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{link}}
in place of

.

Proof.

Lemma
8.9
gives a resolution of

which is almost the desired form (see Remark

). For the case

, the middle module of the produced resolution for

is generated entirely in degree

from point (2) of Lemma

∎

Using this, we can outline an algorithm for producing codimension 3 licci ideals in each Herzog class, which has been implemented in Macaulay2
[

]
.

Algorithm 3.16
.

Let
R
be a polynomial ring with at least three variables, and let
\mathfrak{m}
be the ideal of variables. Let
\mathcal{S}
be a decoration of a vertex on
\mathrm{Licci}_{3}
. Compute a path on
\mathrm{Licci}_{3}
from
\mathcal{S}
to
\mathcal{S}_{0}=\bigwedge^{3}F_{1}\otimes F_{3}^{*}
(corresponding to a complete intersection) by repeatedly applying Example
3.8
to
\mathcal{S}
:

\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{S}_{N}\sim\cdots\sim\mathcal{S}_{1}\sim\mathcal{S}_{0}.

We then inductively construct
I_{j}
so that
\mathcal{S}_{(I_{j})_{\mathfrak{m}}}=\mathcal{S}_{j}
, starting by taking
I_{0}
to be a complete intersection generated by three elements of degree one. To construct
I_{j+1}
from
I_{j}
, we use Lemma
3.15
with appropriate
\lambda^{\prime}_{1},\lambda^{\prime}_{2},\lambda^{\prime}_{3}
(c.f. Theorem
3.1
). It remains to show that
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3}
may be adjusted into a regular sequence, so that the conclusion of Lemma
3.15
holds.

The crucial point is that
I_{j}
has a regular sequence of minimal degree among its minimal generators. If
j=0
, then this is obvious. If
j>0
, the elements of the regular sequence linking
I_{j-1}\sim I_{j}
will always have lowest possible degree in
I_{j}
by construction, since
\mathcal{S}_{j-1}
was constructed from
\mathcal{S}_{j}
via Example
3.8
.

Since we are working over an infinite field, adding a general homogeneous combination of lower degree generators of
I_{j}
to each of
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3}
will ensure that it is a regular sequence.

4.3
Infinite families of Herzog classes of ideals with resolution of format
(1,6,8,3)

The format of resolution
(1,6,8,3)
is the smallest non-Dynkin format. The Kac-Moody Lie algebra associated to this format is not finitely generated and therefore there are infinitely many higher structure maps that are possibly nonzero. This means that we expect to see infinitely many Herzog classes of ideals having minimal free resolution of this format. Computational experiments show that there are so many Herzog classes that a complete classification seems to be hard. However we can provide examples of a few nice families.

For
n\geq 2
, set
a=\frac{n(3n+1)}{2}
. Define

\mathcal{S}_{I_{n}}=S_{a+2,a,a,a-2n+1,a-2n+1,a-2n+1}F_{1}\otimes S_{a-n+1,a-n+1,a-n}F_{3}^{*},

\mathcal{S}_{H_{n}}=S_{a-n+1,a-n+1,a-n,a-3n+3,a-3n+1,a-3n+1}F_{1}\otimes S_{a-2n+1,a-2n+1,a-2n+1}F_{3}^{*},

\mathcal{S}_{J_{n}}=S_{a-2n+1,a-2n+1,a-2n+1,a-4n+3,a-4n+3,a-4n+2}F_{1}\otimes S_{a-3n+3,a-3n+1,a-3n+1}F_{3}^{*}.

It is not hard to check using the formulas in Examples
3.8
and
3.10
that, if
\rightsquigarrow
denotes the smallest minimal link, we have

I_{n}\rightsquigarrow H_{n}\rightsquigarrow J_{n}\rightsquigarrow I_{n-1},

and, if
\leftsquigarrow
denotes the largest minimal link, we have

I_{n}\leftsquigarrow H_{n}\leftsquigarrow J_{n}\leftsquigarrow I_{n-1}.

For
n=1
, we have
\mathcal{S}_{I_{1}}=S_{4,2,2,1,1,1}F_{1}\otimes S_{2,2,1}F_{3}^{*}
. The smallest minimal link of
\mathcal{S}_{I_{1}}
produces the decoration of the almost complete intersection of format
(1,4,6,3)
. By induction this shows that for every

and

are decorations and they are clearly of ideals with resolution of format

5
Classifying the admissible decorations

In this section we consider the problem of classifying which pairs of partitions (or Schur functors) are indeed decorations of vertices of the licci graph. Let us consider a pair of Schur functors of the form

\mathcal{S}=S_{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{d+3}}F_{1}\otimes S_{\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{t}}F_{3}^{*}

where
\lambda_{i},\mu_{j}
are positive integers (listed in non-increasing order) such that

\sum_{i\geq 1}\lambda_{i}=2k+1
and
\sum_{j\geq 1}\mu_{j}=k
.

We know that
\mathcal{S}
is a decoration if and only if after applying a finite number of times the operation corresponding to the smallest minimal linkage (see Example
3.8
) we obtain the decoration
\bigwedge^{3}F_{1}\otimes F_{3}^{*}
corresponding to complete intersections. Since we will now work using smallest minimal links, if not otherwise specified, we set
p=p(\mathcal{S})=\sum_{i\geq 4}\lambda_{i}-k=k+1-\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{3}.

We can immediately find several restrictions that prevent many pairs of Schur functors of the above form from being decorations:

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{S}_{I}
be a decoration of a vertex of the licci graph
\mathrm{Licci}_{3}
. Then:

(1)

\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}\leq k+1
.

(2)

\mu_{1}+\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}>k+1
.

(3)

\sum_{i\geq 1}\lambda_{i}^{2}+\sum_{j\geq 1}\mu_{j}^{2}=(k+1)^{2}.

(4)

\lambda_{1}+\mu_{1}\leq k+1
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
be the pair obtained from
\mathcal{S}
by smallest minimal link.

(1) If
\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}>k+1
, by definition of
p
, we would get
\lambda_{3}+p<0
. This is a contradiction since
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
cannot have negative indices in any of the partitions.

(2) We can assume that
\mathcal{S}
is not the decoration of a complete intersection, since otherwise it would satisfy condition (2).
If
\mu_{1}+\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}\leq k+1
, we would have
\mu_{1}\leq p+\lambda_{3}
. Hence,

s^{\prime}=S_{\lambda_{1}+p,\lambda_{2}+p,\lambda_{3}+p,\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{t}}F^{\prime}_{1}\otimes S_{\lambda_{4},\ldots,\lambda_{d+3}}F_{3}^{\prime*},

and
p(\mathcal{S}^{\prime})=\sum_{j\geq 1}\mu_{j}-(k+p)=-p.
If
\mathcal{S}^{\prime\prime}
denotes the smallest minimal link of
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
we get now
\mathcal{S}^{\prime\prime}=\mathcal{S}.
It follows that
\mathcal{S}
cannot be a decoration since it cannot be linked to the decoration of a complete intersection by any sequence of smallest minimal links.

(3) We prove a more general version of this result in arbitrary codimension in Theorem
8.7
. This case follows as a specialization of that theorem to the case
c=3
.

(4) Applying (1) to
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
, we obtain
\lambda_{1}+p+\mu_{1}\leq\kappa(J)+1=k+p+1
. The thesis clearly follows.
∎

Corollary 5.2
.

Let
\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{S}_{I}
be a decoration of a vertex of the licci graph in codimension 3.
The following assertions hold:

(1)

\mu_{2}=0
if and only if
I
is Gorenstein and if and only if
\lambda_{1}=1
.

(2)

\lambda_{5}=0
if and only if
I
is an almost complete intersection.

(3)

\lambda_{1}=k
if and only if
I
is an hyperplane section of a perfect ideal of height 2.

Proof.

(1) The first equivalence is straightforward. The condition
\mu_{2}=0
is equivalent to
\mu_{1}=k
which implies
\lambda_{1}=1
by Proposition
5.1
(4).
Similarly, if
\lambda_{1}=1
, the condition
\mu_{1}=k
follows by Proposition
5.1
(2).

(2) This is straightforward.

(3) If
\lambda_{1}=k
, by Theorem
3.12
we can find an homogeneous ideal in the Herzog class of
I
with a generator of degree 1.
Up to apply a linear change of coordinate we find that
I
is in the same Herzog class of an hyperplane section. Therefore
I
has to be an hyperplane section. Conversely, looking at the table in Section
4.1
, one can easily describe the decorations of each hyperplane section starting from a complete intersection, using Algorithm
3.16
.
∎

The invariant
k=\kappa(I)
is apparently not strictly related with the number of generators
b=d+3
of a licci ideal
I
. We clearly have the inequality
b\leq 2k+1
and, by Corollary
5.2
, the equality
b=2k+1
holds if and only if
I
is Gorenstein. We can prove that, in case
I
is not Gorenstein, there is a more accurate bound for
b
in function of
k
.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
\mathcal{S}_{I}
be the decoration of a non-Gorenstein licci ideal
I
of codimension 3, minimally generated by
b
elements.
Then
b\leq\kappa(I)+2
.

Proof.

We can work by induction on
k=\kappa(I)
, observing by inspection that the result is true for small values of
k
(see Section 4). Consider the tight double link
\mathcal{S}_{I^{\prime}}
of
\mathcal{S}_{I}
defined as in Example
3.11
. Adopting the same notation as in that example, we have
\kappa(I^{\prime})=k+p+q<k
and by inductive hypothesis the number of generators
b(I^{\prime})\leq\kappa(I^{\prime})+2<k+2.
Looking at the partitions associated to
\mathcal{S}_{I^{\prime}}
, we also observe that
b-2\leq b(I^{\prime})\leq b
and the second inequality is strict if and only if one of
\lambda_{1}+p+q
and
\lambda_{2}+p+q
is zero. If
b(I^{\prime})>b-2
we are done. If
b(I^{\prime})=b-2
, then
\lambda_{1}+p+q=\lambda_{2}+p+q=0
. If by way of contradiction we suppose
b>k+2
, then
b-2\leq k+p+q+2<k+2
, hence
b=k+3
and
p+q=-1
. This implies
\lambda_{1}=1
, which is impossible by Corollary
5.2
, since
I
is not Gorenstein.
∎

Next we show that the only decorations involving a pure exterior power of
F_{3}^{*}
in the second partition (equivalently
\mu_{1}=1
) correspond either to almost complete intersections or to hyperplane sections.

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{S}_{I}
be a decoration of a vertex of the licci graph
\mathrm{Licci}_{3}
. Suppose that
\mu_{1}=1
. Then
I
is either an almost complete intersection or an hyperplane section.

Proof.

Using the restrictions obtained in Proposition
5.1
and looking at Section
4.2
, we can easily check that the thesis is true for small values of
k
. Thus we can work by induction on
k
. By condition (1) and (2) of Proposition
5.1
we must have
\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}=k+1
where
k=\kappa(I)
. By Corollary
5.2
, we can also assume that
I
is not Gorenstein and
\mu_{2}=1
.

Consider the minimal tight double link
\mathcal{S}_{I^{\prime}}
of
\mathcal{S}
defined as in Example
3.11
.
Since
\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}=k+1
, then
p=k+1-\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{3}=-\lambda_{3}
and

\mathcal{S}_{I^{\prime}}=S_{\lambda_{1}+p+q,\lambda_{2}+p+q,\lambda_{3},\lambda_{4},\ldots,\lambda_{d+3}}G_{1}\otimes S_{\mu_{2},\ldots,\mu_{t}}G_{3}^{*},

where
q=\lambda_{3}-\mu_{1}=\lambda_{3}-1
.
Observe that
\kappa(I^{\prime})=k+p+q=k-1<k
.
By inductive hypothesis
I^{\prime}
is either an almost complete intersection or an hyperplane section. Observe that
\lambda_{1}+p+q=k-\lambda_{2}
and
\lambda_{2}+p+q=k-\lambda_{1}
. By Corollary
5.2
, one of the two is zero if and only if
I
is an hyperplane section. If we assume that
I
is not an hyperplane section, we must then have
\lambda_{1}+p+q,\lambda_{2}+p+q\neq 0
. Hence, if
I^{\prime}
is an almost complete intersection, we have
\lambda_{5}=0
, which implies that also
I
is an almost complete intersection.

If
I^{\prime}
is an hyperplane section, then
k-\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}+p+q=1
, forcing
\lambda_{1}=k-1
and
\lambda_{2}=2
since
p+q=-1
. But
k-\lambda_{2}=\lambda_{1}+p+q
is equal either to
\kappa(I^{\prime})=k-1
or to
1
. The first case is impossible because
\lambda_{2}=2
, while the second case forces
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}=2
and
k=3
, which again forces
I
to be an almost complete intersection by the tables in Section
4.2
.
∎

Corollary 5.5
.

Let
\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{S}_{I}
be a decoration. Suppose that
\lambda_{4}=1
. Then
I
is directly linked to either an almost complete intersection or an hyperplane section.

Proof.

Apply the smallest minimal link and use Proposition
5.4
.
∎

Thanks to Corollary
5.5
, the tables in Section
4.1
allow to classify all the decorations such that
\lambda_{4}=1
.

6
Pairs of partitions and multiplicative structure in Tor algebra

We already recalled in Section
2.4
that the free resolution
\mathbb{F}
of a perfect ideal of codimension 3 admits an associative multiplicative structure consisting of three linear maps
\bigwedge^{2}F_{1}\to F_{2}
,
F_{1}\otimes F_{2}\to F_{3}
,
\bigwedge^{3}F_{1}\to F_{3}
. In a local setting, the possible multiplicative structures can be classified modulo the maximal ideal. This correspond to study the multiplication in the Tor algebra Tor
(R/I,R/\mathfrak{m})
. For details about this classification see
[
43
]
,
[
1
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
8
]
and related papers.

The configuration of unit elements in higher structure maps is an invariant of the Herzog classes. In particular so is the structure of multiplication in the Tor algebra. We show how, in the licci case, this structure can be detected simply by looking at the pair of partitions associated to the Herzog class of the ideal.

Let
I
be a perfect ideal of codimension 3 in a polynomial ring
R
and denote a given basis of the free modules
F_{1},F_{2},F_{3}
in the minimal free resolution of
I
by

\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{d+3}\}
,
\{f_{1},\ldots,f_{d+t+2}\}
,
\{g_{1},\ldots,g_{t}\}
. We have:

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
I
be a licci ideal of codimension 3, with
\mathcal{S}_{I}=S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}F_{1}\otimes S_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}F_{3}^{*}
.
Then there exists a choice of bases for the free modules
F_{i}
in a minimal free resolution of
R/I
such that:

(1)

The product
e_{i}^{.}e_{j}
is nonzero modulo
\mathfrak{m}
if and only if
\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{j}=k+1
.

(2)

We have
e_{i}^{.}f=g_{j}
modulo
\mathfrak{m}
for some
f\in F_{2}
if and only if
\lambda_{i}+\mu_{j}=k+1
.

Proof.

Since the Tor algebra structure is an invariant of the Herzog class, it suffices to prove the claim for a single ideal in the same class. To this end, we assume
I
is a homogeneous ideal with minimal graded free resolution of the form described in
Theorem
3.12
, and the basis of
F_{1}
maps to a SGS of
I
(see Definition
3.3
).
The degree of the coefficient of
e_{i}^{.}e_{j}
with respect to a basis element
f\in F_{2}
is given by

\mbox{\rm deg}(e_{i})+\mbox{\rm deg}(e_{j})-\mbox{\rm deg}(f)=k+1-\lambda_{i}+k+1-\lambda_{j}-k-1=k+1-\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}.

Similarly, the degree of the coefficient of
e_{i}^{.}f
with respect to
g_{j}
is

\mbox{\rm deg}(e_{i})+\mbox{\rm deg}(f)-\mbox{\rm deg}(g_{j})=k+1-\lambda_{i}+k+1-\mu_{j}-k-1=k+1-\lambda_{i}-\mu_{j}.

These degrees are equal to zero if and only if the corresponding equalities
\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{j}=k+1
and
\lambda_{i}+\mu_{j}=k+1
hold. It follows that if one of those equalities does not hold, the corresponding product is zero modulo
\mathfrak{m}
.

Conversely, suppose
\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{j}=k+1
and without loss of generality say that
\lambda_{i}\geq\lambda_{j}
. Define the minimal link of
\mathcal{S}_{I}
with respect to the partition
\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\prime}=\lambda_{i},\lambda_{j},\lambda_{l}
for some
l\neq i,j
. The corresponding
p
is
k+1-\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{l}=-\lambda_{l}
. Thus
\lambda_{l}+p=0
and, by the formula in Remark
3.6
, the ideal
J
linked to
I
with respect to a regular sequence associated to
\lambda_{i},\lambda_{j},\lambda_{l}
has less than
t+3
minimal generators. Therefore at least one of the products
e_{i}^{.}e_{j},e_{i}^{.}e_{l},e_{j}^{.}e_{l}
is nonzero modulo
\mathfrak{m}
.
If
\lambda_{l}<\lambda_{j}
, then by Proposition
5.1
(1),
\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{l},\lambda_{j}+\lambda_{l}<k+1
and the products
e_{i}^{.}e_{l},e_{j}^{.}e_{l}
vanish modulo
\mathfrak{m}
. It follows that
e_{i}^{.}e_{j}
is nonzero modulo
\mathfrak{m}
. If
\lambda_{l}\geq\lambda_{i}
, then
\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{l}=\lambda_{j}+\lambda_{l}=k+1
. In this case
J
has only
t
generators and all three products
e_{i}^{.}e_{j},e_{i}^{.}e_{l},e_{j}^{.}e_{l}
do not vanish modulo
\mathfrak{m}
. Similarly, if
\lambda_{j}\leq\lambda_{l}<\lambda_{i}
, then
J
has
t+1
generators and the products
e_{i}^{.}e_{j},e_{i}^{.}e_{l}
do not vanish modulo
\mathfrak{m}
.
In any case this proves (1).

Finally, suppose
\lambda_{i}+\mu_{j}=k+1
. Consider the minimally linked decoration with respect to the choice
\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\prime}=\lambda_{i},\lambda_{r},\lambda_{s}
for some
r,s\neq i
. Let
J
be the associated linked ideal, and observe that there are two minimal generators of
J
corresponding to the partition elements
\lambda_{i}+p
and
\mu_{j}
in
\mathcal{S}_{J}
. The first of these two generators is in
I\cap J
, while the second one is not in
I
. By assumption we have
\lambda_{i}+p+\mu_{j}=k+1+p=\kappa(J)+1
. By the statement (1) of this proposition, applied to
J
, we obtain that the product corresponding to these two generators is nonzero modulo
\mathfrak{m}
. It follows that
e_{i}^{.}f=g_{j}
modulo
\mathfrak{m}
for some
f
as a consequence of
[
14
, Proposition 3.5 (3.7)]
. This proves (2).
∎

In the remaining of this section we investigate the cases where the map
\bigwedge^{2}F_{1}\to F_{2}
does not vanish modulo
\mathfrak{m}
and the case where there is a partial perfect pairing on the resolution induced by the map
F_{1}\otimes F_{2}\to F_{3}
.

6.1
Licci ideals with Koszul relations among minimal syzygies

A perfect ideal of codimension three has Koszul relations among minimal syzygies if and only if the multiplication map
\bigwedge^{2}F_{1}\to F_{2}
does not vanish modulo the maximal (or homogeneous maximal) ideal. This condition is equivalent to the property of being linked to some ideal having strictly smaller total Betti number.
In Theorem
6.1
, we characterize the decorations corresponding to licci ideals with Koszul relations among minimal syzygies as the ones such that
\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{j}=k+1
for some
i,j
. In particular, by Proposition
5.1
(1) this implies necessarily that
\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}=k+1
.
For this reason, in this section we study the family of decorations, characterized by the condition
\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}=k+1
.

In
[
21
]
it is shown that any licci
\mathfrak{m}
-primary monomial ideal is directly linked by the monomial regular sequence of smallest degree to a monomial ideal with smaller total Betti number. Therefore the Herzog classes of licci monomial ideals of codimension 3 in
K[x,y,z]
are part of this family.

We prove that for any fixed type
t
or number of generators
b=d+3
, the pairs of partitions corresponding to decorations such that
\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}=k+1
are bounded, hence they are finitely many.

For any decoration
\mathcal{S}
such that
\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}=k+1
we can define a special linked decoration

p_{0}(\mathcal{S})=S_{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{t}}F_{1}\otimes S_{\lambda_{3},\ldots,\lambda_{d+3}}F_{3}^{*}.

The link from
\mathcal{S}
to
p_{0}(\mathcal{S})
is defined choosing
\lambda_{1}^{\prime},\lambda_{2}^{\prime},\lambda_{3}^{\prime}=\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},0
and observing that the corresponding value of
p
is zero. It is possible in some cases to have
p_{0}(\mathcal{S})=\mathcal{S}
.

We define now for
t\geq 1
, a family of decorations whose partitions are expressed in terms of consecutive powers of
2
:

\mathcal{H}_{t}=S_{2^{t-1},2^{t-1},2^{t-2},2^{t-2},\ldots,2,2,1,1,1}F_{1}\otimes S_{2^{t-1},2^{t-2},\ldots,2,1}F_{3}^{*}.

Observe that
\mathcal{H}_{1}
is the decoration of the complete intersections and next we have
\mathcal{H}_{2}=S_{2,2,1,1,1}F_{1}\otimes S_{2,1}F_{3}^{*}
,
\mathcal{H}_{3}=S_{4,4,2,2,1,1,1}F_{1}\otimes S_{4,2,1}F_{3}^{*}
, and so on.
Recall that by
\mbox{\rm gen}(\mathcal{S})
, we mean the generic link defined in Example
3.9
.
We prove a couple of lemmas about this kind of decorations.

Lemma 6.2
.

For
t\geq 1
, we have
\mathcal{H}_{t+1}=p_{0}(\mbox{\rm gen}(\mathcal{H}_{t})).
In particular each
\mathcal{H}_{t}
is a decoration such that
\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}=k+1
.

Proof.

Observe that
\kappa(\mathcal{H}_{t})=\sum_{j=0}^{t-1}2^{j}=2^{t}-1.
Hence, if we prove that
\mathcal{H}_{t}
is a decoration, the condition
\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}=k+1
will follow immediately.
By induction on
t\geq 1
, it is equivalent to assume that
\mathcal{H}_{t}
is a decoration and prove that
\mathcal{H}_{t+1}
is also a decoration.
Applying the definitions we get

\mbox{\rm gen}(\mathcal{H}_{t})=S_{2^{t},2^{t},2^{t},2^{t-1},2^{t-2},\ldots,2,1}F_{1}\otimes S_{2^{t-1},2^{t-1},2^{t-2},2^{t-2},\ldots,2,2,1,1,1}F_{3}^{*}

and

p_{0}(\mbox{\rm gen}(\mathcal{H}_{t}))=S_{2^{t},2^{t},2^{t-1},2^{t-1},2^{t-2},2^{t-2},\ldots,2,2,1,1,1}F_{1}\otimes S_{2^{t},2^{t-1},2^{t-2},\ldots,2,1}F_{3}^{*}=\mathcal{H}_{t+1}.

Therefore
\mathcal{H}_{t+1}
is a decoration.
∎

Given two arbitrary decorations
\mathcal{S}_{1}=S_{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{d_{1}+3}}F_{1}\otimes S_{\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{t_{1}}}F_{3}^{*}
and
\mathcal{S}_{2}=S_{\iota_{1},\ldots,\iota_{d_{2}+3}}F_{1}\otimes S_{\upsilon_{1},\ldots,\upsilon_{t_{2}}}F_{3}^{*}
, we say that
\mathcal{S}_{1}\leq\mathcal{S}_{2}
if for every
i
,
\lambda_{i}\leq\iota_{i}
and
\mu_{i}\leq\upsilon_{i}
.

Lemma 6.3
.

The following assertions hold:

1.

If
\mathcal{S}_{1}\leq\mathcal{S}_{2}
then
\mbox{\rm gen}(\mathcal{S}_{1})\leq\mbox{\rm gen}(\mathcal{S}_{2})
.

2.

If
\mathcal{S}_{1}\leq\mathcal{S}_{2}
are such that
p_{0}(\mathcal{S}_{1})
and
p_{0}(\mathcal{S}_{2})
are defined, then
p_{0}(\mathcal{S}_{1})\leq p_{0}(\mathcal{S}_{2})
.

3.

If
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
is any link of
\mathcal{S}
, then
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\leq\mbox{\rm gen}(\mathcal{S})
.

4.

If
\mathcal{S}
is a decoration such that
\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}=k+1
, then
p_{0}(p_{0}(\mathcal{S}))=\mathcal{S}
.

Proof.

Items 1 and 2 follow immediately by the definitions of generic link and of
p_{0}
.

For item 3, set

\mathcal{S}^{\prime}=S_{\lambda^{\prime}_{1}+p,\lambda^{\prime}_{2}+p,\lambda^{\prime}_{3}+p,\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{t}}G_{1}\otimes S_{\lambda_{1}^{\prime\prime},\ldots,\lambda^{\prime\prime}_{d+3}}G_{3}^{*}

for
p=k+1-\lambda_{1}^{\prime}-\lambda_{2}^{\prime}-\lambda_{3}^{\prime}
, where
k=\kappa(\mathcal{S})=\sum_{i=1}^{t}\mu_{i}
. By definition of
\lambda_{i}^{\prime\prime}
(as in Remark
3.6
), we clearly have
\lambda_{i}^{\prime\prime}\leq\lambda_{i}
. Looking at the definition of
\mbox{\rm gen}(\mathcal{S})
and at the partition on the side of
G_{1}
, after reordering, it is enough to observe that, essentially by definition,
\lambda_{1}^{\prime}+p\leq k+1
and
\mu_{1}<k+1
.

For item 4, by definition of
p_{0}(\mathcal{S})
, we only need to prove that
\mu_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}
. But if we had
\mu_{1}>\lambda_{2}
, we would have
\lambda_{1}+\mu_{1}>\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}=k+1=\kappa(p_{0}(\mathcal{S}))+1
. This would contradict item 1 of Proposition
5.1
applied to
p_{0}(\mathcal{S})
.
∎

Theorem 6.4
.

Let
\mathcal{S}=S_{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{d+3}}F_{1}\otimes S_{\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{t}}F_{3}^{*}
be a decoration such that
\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}=k+1
. Then
\mathcal{S}\leq\mathcal{H}_{t}
.

Proof.

We work by induction on
t
. If
t=1
, then
\mathcal{S}
corresponds to the Herzog class of a Gorenstein ideal and by Corollary
5.2
,
\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}=1+1=2
. This forces
k=1
and therefore
\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{H}_{1}
is the decoration of the complete intersections.
Hence, we can assume
t>1
and suppose the result true for each decoration of ideals with type smaller than
t
. By hypothesis on
\mathcal{S}
, the decoration
p_{0}(\mathcal{S})
is well-defined. Let
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
be the smallest minimal link of
p_{0}(\mathcal{S})
.
It is easy to observe that the type of
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
is strictly smaller than
t
. By inductive hypothesis
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\leq\mathcal{H}_{t-1}
. Since
p_{0}(\mathcal{S})
is a link of
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
,
we can use items 3 and 1 of Lemma
6.3
to say that
p_{0}(\mathcal{S})\leq\mbox{\rm gen}(\mathcal{S}^{\prime})\leq\mbox{\rm gen}(\mathcal{H}_{t-1})
. Then by items 4 and 2 of Lemma
6.3
and by Lemma
6.2
, we obtain
\mathcal{S}=p_{0}(p_{0}(\mathcal{S}))\leq p_{0}(\mbox{\rm gen}(\mathcal{H}_{t-1}))=\mathcal{H}_{t}
.
∎

Corollary 6.5
.

Let
I
be a licci ideal
I
of codimension 3 with
b=d+3
generators, type
t
, and having Koszul relations among minimal syzygies.
Then
b\leq 2t+1
and
t\leq 2b-5
.

Proof.

The first inequality follows directly from Theorem
6.4
. For the second, observe that
p_{0}(\mathcal{S}_{I})
is the decoration of some ideal with
t+2
generators and type
b-2
, and also having Koszul relations among minimal syzygies. Applying the first inequality to
p_{0}(\mathcal{S}_{I})
we get
t+2\leq 2(b-2)+1
, yielding
t\leq 2b-5
.
∎

Example 6.6
.

Using linkage theory, it is possible to construct, for every
t\geq 1
, a licci monomial ideals of codimension 3 in
K[x,y,z]
whose Herzog class is decorated by
\mathcal{H}_{t}.
A possible family of examples is given by

J_{t}=(x^{t},y^{t},z^{t})+\sum_{i=1}^{t-1}z^{t-i}(x^{i},y^{i}).

Here we have
\mathcal{S}_{J_{t}}=\mathcal{H}_{t}
.

6.2
Ideals with Tor algebra class
G

In this section we study the decorations corresponding to licci ideals with Tor algebra structure of class
G
. We recall the definition.
An ideal
I
with free resolution
\mathbb{F}
of length 3
is of class
G(r)
if, up to change basis in
F_{1},F_{2},F_{3}
, one has
e_{i}^{.}f_{i}=g_{1}
for
i=1,\ldots,r
and all the other products
e_{i}^{.}e_{j}
and
e_{i}^{.}f_{j}
vanish modulo the maximal ideal of
R
. It is well-know that Gorenstein ideals are the only ideals of class
G(r)
where
r
is the number of generators.

We use the theory of pair of partitions to answer positively to a conjecture posed in
[
10
, 7.4]
.
By Theorem
6.1
, combined with the inequality from Proposition
5.1
(4), a licci ideal
I
is of class
G(r)
if in the corresponding decoration
\mathcal{S}_{I}
, we have
\lambda_{i}+\mu_{1}=k+1
for
i=1,\ldots,r
,
\lambda_{i}+\mu_{1}<k+1
for
i>r
, and
\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}<k+1
. We have:

Lemma 6.7
.

Let
\mathcal{S}_{I}
be the decoration of a licci ideal
I
and let
\mathcal{S}_{I^{\prime}}
be the decoration of the tight double link of
\mathcal{S}_{I}
defined in Example
3.11
. Let
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{b}
and
\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{t}
, and
l_{1},\ldots,l_{b^{\prime}}
and
m_{1},\ldots,m_{t^{\prime}}
be the pairs of partitions associated respectively to
I
and to
I^{\prime}
.

Suppose that for some
r\geq 2
,
\lambda_{i}+\mu_{1}=\kappa(I)+1
for every
i=1,\ldots,r
. Then
b^{\prime}=b-2
and
l_{i}+m_{1}=\kappa(I^{\prime})+1
for every
i=1,\ldots,r-2
.

Proof.

Looking at Example
3.11
, we first observe that
\lambda_{1}+p+q=\lambda_{2}+p+q=0
since
\lambda_{1}+\mu_{1}=\lambda_{2}+\mu_{1}=k+1
. Hence,
b^{\prime}=b-2
(indeed
\mu_{1}+q=\lambda_{3}>0
).
It follows that
l_{1},\ldots,l_{b^{\prime}}=\lambda_{3},\ldots,\lambda_{b}
. For
i=3,\ldots,r
we have

\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{3}+p=\kappa(I)+1-\mu_{1}+\lambda_{3}+p=\kappa(I)+p+q+1=\kappa(I^{\prime})+1
.
∎

We can use this result to study the ideals of class
G(r)
, comparing
r
with the minimal number of generators.

Lemma 6.8
.

Let
I
be a perfect ideal of codimension 3 minimally generated by
b
elements and of Tor algebra class
G(r)
with
r\geq b-4
. Then
I
is licci.

Proof.

This is a special case of
[
33
, Corollary 5.15(2)]
.
∎

Theorem 6.9
.

Let
I
be a perfect ideal of codimension 3 minimally generated by
b
elements. The following conditions are equivalent:

1.

I
is of Tor algebra class
G(r)
with
r\geq b-2
.

2.

r=b
and
I
is Gorenstein.

Proof.

By Lemma
6.8
, we can restrict to consider only licci ideals.
We can work by induction on
k=\kappa(I)
and, looking at the lists of decorations in Section 4 and applying Theorem
6.1
, we observe that for small values of
k
, excluding the Gorenstein cases, there are no decorations such that
\lambda_{i}+\mu_{1}=\kappa(I)+1
for every
i=1,\ldots,r
, if
r=b-2,b-1
. Suppose that
I
is not Gorenstein of Tor algebra class
G(r)
with
r\geq b-2
. Let
\mathcal{S}_{I}
be the decoration of the Herzog class of
I
and consider a tight doubly linked decoration
\mathcal{S}_{I^{\prime}}
as in Lemma
6.7
. Keeping the same notation as in Lemma
6.7
, it follows that
I^{\prime}
is an ideal with
\beta=b-2
generators and its decoration has the property that
l_{i}+m_{1}=\kappa(I^{\prime\prime})+1
for every
i=1,\ldots,u
with
u\geq r-2
. Moreover,
\kappa(I^{\prime})<\kappa(I)
. Hence, either

I^{\prime}
is Gorenstein or this contradicts the inductive hypothesis. If
I^{\prime}
is Gorenstein, then
I
is directly linked to an almost complete intersection. It is sufficient to use the tables in Section 4 to exclude this case.
∎

Corollary 6.10
.

Let
I
be a perfect ideal of codimension 3 and type 2 minimally generated by
b
elements. Then
I
cannot be of class
G(b-4)
.

Proof.

Again by Lemma
6.8
, we can restrict to consider licci ideals. We then proceed exactly as in the proof of Theorem
6.9
assuming
b\geq 7
, otherwise
I
is of Dynkin format. We look at the tight double link
I^{\prime}
as previously and we
recall that
I^{\prime}
has
\beta=b-2
generators and the type of
I^{\prime}
cannot be larger than the type of
I
. Again
\mathcal{S}_{I^{\prime}}
has the property that
l_{i}+m_{1}=\kappa(I^{\prime})+1
for every
i=1,\ldots,u
with
u\geq r-2
.
Hence, either
u=r-2=\beta-4
and
I^{\prime}
has type 2, which is impossible by inductive hypothesis, or
u>r-2
.
If
u\geq r=\beta-2
we find a contradiction using Theorem
6.9
. If
u=r-1=\beta-3
, we combine Lemma
7.4
and Theorem
7.5
to show that in fact
I
is of class
G(b-3)
which is again a contradiction.
∎

The perfect ideals of codimension 3 with
b
generators and Tor algebra class
G(b-3)
will be described in the next section. With similar methods we expect that one can find decorations corresponding to ideals with
b
generators, Tor algebra class
G(b-i)
for every
4\leq i\leq b-2
, and any possible type.

7
The family of ideals of type 2 closest to the Gorenstein ones

In Corollary
5.2
, it is shown that decorations of Gorenstein ideals of codimension 3 are characterized by the condition
\boldsymbol{\mu}=(k)
. In terms of partitions one can ask which are the decorations closest to the Gorenstein ones, in the sense that
\boldsymbol{\mu}=(k-1,1)
. In this section, we investigate this family of decorations.

For
k\geq 2
, consider the pairs of Schur functors
\mathcal{S}_{I_{k}}=S_{2^{k-1},1,1,1}F_{1}\otimes S_{k-1,1}F_{3}^{*}
. We show that these Schur functors define two families of decorations (one with
k
odd and one with
k
even). The ideals in the Herzog classes defined by these families are the closest to the Gorenstein ones.

Proposition 7.1
.

For every
k\geq 2,
the pair of Schur functors
\mathcal{S}_{I_{k}}=S_{2^{k-1},1,1,1}F_{1}\otimes S_{k-1,1}F_{3}^{*}
is a decoration.

Proof.

If
k=2,3
we obtain the almost complete intersection of type 2 and the Anne Brown’s class of format
(1,5,6,2)
(see the tables in Section
4.1
). Thus we can assume
k\geq 4
.
We start from
\mathcal{S}_{I_{k}}
and apply two consecutive smallest minimal links (in this case this is equivalent to apply the minimal tight double link defined in Example
3.11
) .
First we have
p=k+1-6=k-5
and the first smallest minimal link is

\mathcal{S}_{J_{k}}=S_{k-1,k-3,k-3,k-3,1}G_{1}\otimes S_{2^{k-4},1,1,1}G_{3}^{*}.

Then we have
p^{\prime}=k-3+1-(2(k-4)+3)=-k+3
. Thus the second smallest minimal link of
\mathcal{S}_{I_{k}}
is

S_{2,2^{k-4},1,1,1}F_{1}^{\prime}\otimes S_{k-3,1}F_{3}^{\prime*}=\mathcal{S}_{I_{k-2}}.

By induction, this shows that
\mathcal{S}_{I_{k}}
is a decoration for every
k\geq 1
.
∎

We show now that if a decoration has
\boldsymbol{\mu}=(k-1,1)
, then it must be one of the decorations
\mathcal{S}_{I_{k}}
.

Proposition 7.2
.

Let
\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{S}_{I}
be a decoration, let
k=\kappa(\mathcal{S})
and suppose
\mu_{1}=k-1
. Then
\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{S}_{I_{k}}
.

Proof.

The assumption
\mu_{1}=k-1
implies
\mu_{2}=1
and
\mu_{3}=0
. By Corollary
5.2
,
I
is not Gorenstein and
\lambda_{1}\geq 2
. But, by Proposition
5.1
(4), we must have
\lambda_{1}+\mu_{1}\leq k+1
and therefore
\lambda_{1}=2
.
In particular, all
\lambda_{i}
are equal to
2
or
1
. Let
a
and
b
be the number of
\lambda_{i}
’s equal respectively to
2
or to
1
.
We know that
2a+b=2k+1
, and by Proposition
5.1
(3) we get also the relation
4a+b=(k+1)^{2}-(k-1)^{2}-1=4k-1
. An easy calculation yields
a=k-1
,
b=3
.
∎

Remark 7.3
.

We provide also the formulas describing the links from
\mathcal{S}_{I_{k}}
to
\mathcal{S}_{I_{k+2}}
. We first link choosing
\lambda_{1}^{\prime}=2
,
\lambda_{2}^{\prime}=\lambda_{3}^{\prime}=0
. The link with respect to this choice is

S_{k+1,k-1,k-1,k-1,1}G_{1}\otimes S_{2^{k-2},1,1,1}G_{3}^{*}=\mathcal{S}_{J_{k+2}}.

To successive link from
\mathcal{S}_{J_{k+2}}
to
\mathcal{S}_{I_{k+2}}
is now a minimal link obtained from the choice
\lambda_{1}^{\prime},\lambda_{2}^{\prime},\lambda_{3}^{\prime}=(k-1,k-1,k-1)
.

Our next result shows that the family of licci ideals with decorations
\mathcal{S}_{I_{k}}
corresponds exactly to the family of perfect ideals of codimension 3 having the largest perfect pairing on the free resolution after the Gorenstein ideals. To do this we use the results obtained in Section
6.2
describing the decorations of ideals of Tor algebra class
G
.

Lemma 7.4
.

Let
\mathcal{S}_{I}
be the decoration of a licci ideal and let
\mathcal{S}_{I^{\prime}}
be the decoration of its tight double link defined in Example
3.11
. Suppose that
\lambda_{i}+\mu_{i}=\kappa(I)+1
for
i\leq 3
and that
I^{\prime}=I_{j}
where
j=\kappa(I^{\prime})
. Then
I=I_{k}
where
k=\kappa(I)
.

Proof.

Arguing as in the proof of Lemma
6.7
, we use that
\lambda_{i}+\mu_{i}=k+1
for
i=1,2
to get

\mathcal{S}_{I^{\prime}}=S_{\lambda_{3},\ldots,\lambda_{b}}F_{1}\otimes S_{\lambda_{3}+p,\mu_{2},\ldots,\mu_{t}}F_{3}^{*}=S_{I_{j}}=S_{2^{j-1},1,1,1}F_{1}\otimes S_{j-1,1}F_{3}^{*}.

From Example
3.11
, we know that
\lambda_{3}=\mu_{1}+q
. Hence,
\lambda_{3}+\lambda_{3}+p=\lambda_{3}+\mu_{1}+q+p=k+1+q+p=\kappa(I^{\prime})+1.
By Proposition
5.1
(4),
\lambda_{3}+p\geq\mu_{2}
and therefore
\lambda_{3}+p=\kappa(I^{\prime})-1=k+p+q-1
. Combining these facts we get
\lambda_{3}=\mu_{1}+q=k+q-1
. Hence
\mu_{1}=k-1
and the desired result follows by Proposition
7.2
.
∎

Theorem 7.5
.

Le
I
be a perfect ideal of codimension 3 minimally generated by
b
elements.
Suppose
that
I
is not Gorenstein.
The following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

I
is of class
G(r)
with
r=b-3
.

(2)

I
is licci of class
G(r)
with
r=k-1
.

(3)

I
is licci of class
G(r)
with
r\geq k-1
.

(4)

I
is licci and
\mathcal{S}_{I}=\mathcal{S}_{I_{k}}
.

Proof.

The implication (2)
\Rightarrow
(3) is obvious, while
the implications (4)
\Rightarrow
(1) and (4)
\Rightarrow
(2) follow immediately by Theorem
6.1
.

We show the implication (3)
\Rightarrow
(4).
Suppose
I
to be not Gorenstein and of class
G(r)
with
r\geq k-1
. It is sufficient to show
\mu_{1}=k-1
and apply Proposition
7.2
.
By way of contradiction say that
\mu_{1}\leq k-2
.
The assumption of
G(r)
implies, by Theorem
6.1
, that
\lambda_{i}+\mu_{1}=k+1
for every
i=1,\ldots,r
. Hence,

2k+1\geq\lambda_{1}+\ldots+\lambda_{r}=r(k+1-\mu_{1})\geq(k-1)(k+1-(k-2))=3k-3.

It follows that
k\leq 4
and it can be checked by inspection that, if
k\leq 4
, the only Herzog class of Tor algebra class
G(r)
(with
r\geq 3
) are the Gorenstein class and the one of
\mathcal{S}_{I_{4}}
.

Finally, we prove (1)
\Rightarrow
(4).
By Lemma
6.8
, if
I
satisfies condition (1), then it is licci. Looking at the tables in Section 4, we observe that the result is true both for small values of
k
and if
I
is directly linked to an almost complete intersection. Also we can check that
I
is not an almost complete intersection.
Thus, we use a method similar to the one used in the proof of Theorem
6.9
.
By the assumption of class
G(b-3)
,
\mathcal{S}_{I}
satisfies the assumption of Lemma
6.7
.
We work by induction on
k
. Using Lemma
6.7
, and the fact that (2),(3),(4) are equivalent, we can assume that the tight double link
\mathcal{S}_{I^{\prime}}
of
\mathcal{S}_{I}
is either Gorenstein or of the form
\mathcal{S}_{I_{j}}
with
j=\kappa(I^{\prime})=k+p+q
(see Example
3.11
and observe that
\kappa(I^{\prime})<k
). But if
I^{\prime}
is Gorenstein, then
I
is directly linked to an almost complete intersection and the thesis follows.

Otherwise, since
I^{\prime}
has
b-2
generators, we can assume
b\geq 6
,
\lambda_{3}+\mu_{1}=k+1
and

\mathcal{S}_{I^{\prime}}=\mathcal{S}_{I_{j}}
. The thesis follows from Lemma
7.4
.
∎

In the next subsections we describe explicit free resolutions of ideals in each Herzog class

.

Definition 8.3

(Linkage formula for decorations)

Let

\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\prime}=(\lambda^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,\lambda^{\prime}_{c})

\mathcal{S}_{J}=S_{\lambda^{\prime}_{1}+p,\ldots,\lambda^{\prime}_{c}+p,\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{t}}G_{1}\otimes S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\prime\prime}}G_{3}^{*},

after reordering the first of the above partitions in non-increasing order, where

(8.1)

If

Assuming Conjecture

The next lemma provides a first restrictions on the elements of the partitions of a decoration.

Lemma 8.5
.

Let
\mathcal{S}=S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}F_{1}\otimes S_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}F_{c}^{*}
be a decoration of a licci ideal of codimension
c
. Let
p=(c-2)k+1-\lambda_{1}-\ldots-\lambda_{c}
be the
p
corresponding to the smallest minimal link. Then
0\leq\lambda_{c}+p<\mu_{1}
.

Proof.

Apply the smallest minimal link to
\mathcal{S}
to get

\mathcal{S}^{\prime}=S_{\lambda_{1}+p,\ldots,\lambda_{c}+p,\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{t}}F_{1}^{\prime}\otimes S_{\lambda_{c+1},\ldots,\lambda_{b}}(F_{c}^{\prime})^{\ast}.

Since
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
is also a decoration of some licci ideal, we must have
\lambda_{c}+p\geq 0.
Suppose
\lambda_{c}+p\geq\mu_{1}.
Then the smallest minimal link of
s^{\prime}
is

\mathcal{S}^{\prime\prime}=S_{\lambda_{1}+p+p^{\prime},\ldots,\lambda_{c}+p+p^{\prime},\lambda_{c+1},\ldots,\lambda_{b}}F_{1}^{\prime\prime}\otimes S_{\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{t}}(F_{c}^{\prime\prime})^{\ast}.

But
p^{\prime}=(c-2)(k+p)+1-\lambda_{1}-\ldots-\lambda_{c}-cp=-p.
Thus
\mathcal{S}^{\prime\prime}=\mathcal{S}
and this is a contradiction since a decoration can always be linked to a complete intersection by smallest minimal links. Hence,
\lambda_{c}+p<\mu_{1}.

∎

Corollary 8.6
.

Let
\mathcal{S}=S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}F_{1}\otimes S_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}F_{c}^{*}
be a decoration and let
k=\sum_{i=1}^{t}\mu_{i}
. Then

\lambda_{i}\leq k
for every

Proof.

If

, the equality
\sum\lambda_{i}=(c-1)k+1=c
forces
\mathcal{S}=\bigwedge^{c}F_{1}\otimes F_{c}^{*}
. The result is true in this case. We can then work by induction on

Let

and let
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
be the minimal tight double link of
\mathcal{S}
. By Lemma

,
\kappa(\mathcal{S}^{\prime})=k+p+q<k

\lambda_{1}+p+q\leq\kappa(\mathcal{S}^{\prime})=k+p+q
. Hence,
\lambda_{1}\leq k
and the result follows.
∎

(Squares formula)

If

Then we prove that if

\mathcal{S}
then it holds also for
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
. We use Definition
8.3
to define the link from
\mathcal{S}
to
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
. We have to prove that

\sum_{i=1}^{c}(\lambda_{i}^{\prime}+p)^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{t}\mu_{i}^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{d+c}(\lambda_{i}^{\prime\prime})^{2}=(c-2)(k+p)^{2}+2(k+p)+1.

(8.2)

We expand the left-hand side of (

) as

\sum_{i=1}^{c}(\lambda_{i}^{\prime})^{2}+2p\sum_{i=1}^{c}\lambda_{i}^{\prime}+cp^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{t}\mu_{i}^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{d+c}(\lambda_{i}^{\prime\prime})^{2}.

By definition of the partitions
\lambda^{\prime}
and
\lambda^{\prime\prime}
and by assumption on
\mathcal{S}
, we obtain

\sum_{i=1}^{c}(\lambda_{i}^{\prime})^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{d+c}(\lambda_{i}^{\prime\prime})^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{d+c}\lambda_{i}^{2}=(c-2)k^{2}+2k+1-\sum_{i=1}^{t}\mu_{i}^{2}.

Combining the above formulas, we obtain that the left-hand side of (
8.2
) is equal to

2p\sum_{i=1}^{c}\lambda_{i}^{\prime}+cp^{2}+(c-2)k^{2}+2k+1.

We use now the definition of

(eq.
8.1
) to write

\sum_{i=1}^{c}\lambda_{i}^{\prime}=(c-2)k+1-p.
An easy computation gives the desired result.
∎

8.2
The doubling of a decoration

In codimension 3 all Gorestein ideals are licci, but this is no more true in higher codimension. The ideal of
2\times 2
minors of a generic
3\times 3
matrix is a classical example of a non-licci Gorenstein ideal of codimension 4. An interesting problem is the classification of Herzog classes of Gorenstein licci ideals of arbitrary codimension.

The goal of this section is to find a way to construct decorations of (licci) Gorenstein ideals of codimension
c+1
from decorations of licci ideals of codimension
c
.

The
doubling
of an ideal is a construction which obtains a Gorenstein ideal of codimension
c+1
from a perfect ideal of codimension
c
, under some extra assumptions
[
32
]
,
[
27
]
.

Here we give a purely combinatoric definition of the doubling of a decoration. Conjecturally this corresponds to the decoration of the doubling in the ideal-theoretic sense, for the ideals for which the notion of doubling is well-defined. The main application of this construction will be the explicit description of an infinite family of Herzog classes of Gorenstein ideals of codimension 4 with 9 generators.

Definition 8.15
.

Given a decoration
\mathcal{S}=S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}F_{1}\otimes S_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}F_{c}^{*}
, define the
doubling
of
\mathcal{S}
as

\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{S})=S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda},\boldsymbol{\mu}}G_{1}\otimes S_{k}G_{c+1}^{*},

after reordering the first partition in non-increasing order.

Theorem 8.16
.

Let
\mathcal{S}=S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}F_{1}\otimes S_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}F_{c}^{*}
be a decoration of a licci ideal of codimension
c
. Denote by
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
the minimal tight double link of
\mathcal{S}
. Then, if
k=\kappa(\mathcal{S})>1
, there exists a double link
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
of
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{S})
such that

\mathcal{L}^{\prime}=\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{S}^{\prime})
. In particular,
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{S})
is a decoration of a Gorenstein licci ideal of codimension
c+1
.

Proof.

First observe that if
\mathcal{S}
is the decoration of a complete intersection, then
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{S})
is the decoration of a complete intersection of codimension
c+1
. Thus we assume
k>1
.

Extending the formula from Example
3.11
to codimension
c
, we obtain

\mathcal{S}^{\prime}=S_{\lambda_{1}+p+q,\ldots,\lambda_{c-1}+p+q,\mu_{1}+q,\lambda_{c+1},\ldots,\lambda_{b}}F_{1}^{\prime}\otimes S_{\lambda_{c}+p,\mu_{2}\ldots,\mu_{t}}(F_{c}^{\prime})^{\ast},

where

p=(c-2)k+1-\lambda_{1}-\ldots-\lambda_{c},
and

q=(c-2)(k+p)+1-(c-1)p-\lambda_{1}-\ldots-\lambda_{c-1}-\mu_{1}.

By Lemma
8.5
,
\lambda_{c}+p<\mu_{1}
. This implies
\kappa(\mathcal{S}^{\prime})<\kappa(\mathcal{S})=k.

Let us define now a first minimal link
\mathcal{L}
of
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{S})
with respect to the subpartition
(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{c},\mu_{1})
. This gives

\mathcal{L}=S_{\lambda_{1}+p^{\prime},\ldots,\lambda_{c}+p^{\prime},\mu_{1}+p^{\prime},k}G_{1}^{\prime}\otimes S_{\lambda_{c+1},\ldots,\lambda_{b},\mu_{2},\ldots,\mu_{t}}(G_{c+1}^{\prime})^{\ast},

where
p^{\prime}=(c-1)k+1-\lambda_{1}-\ldots-\lambda_{c}-\mu_{1}=\lambda_{c+1}+\ldots+\lambda_{b}-\mu_{1}.
Since
\mathcal{S}
does not correspond to a complete intersection, we have
b>c
and
\mu_{1}+p^{\prime}>0.
Moreover,
\lambda_{c}+p^{\prime}=\lambda_{c}+p+k-\mu_{1}\geq 0
, since
\lambda_{c}+p\geq 0
by Lemma
8.5
, and
k-\mu_{1}\geq 0
by definition of
k
. It follows that all the elements
\lambda_{1}+p^{\prime},\ldots,\lambda_{c}+p^{\prime},\mu_{1}+p^{\prime}
are non-negative and we can link again from
\mathcal{L}
choosing the subpartition
(\lambda_{1}+p^{\prime},\ldots,\lambda_{c-1}+p^{\prime},\mu_{1}+p^{\prime},k)
. Therefore, we get

\mathcal{L}^{\prime}=S_{\lambda_{1}+p^{\prime}+q^{\prime},\ldots,\lambda_{c-1}+p^{\prime}+q^{\prime},\mu_{1}+p^{\prime}+q^{\prime},k+q^{\prime},\lambda_{c+1},\ldots,\lambda_{b},\mu_{2},\ldots,\mu_{t}}G_{1}^{\prime\prime}\otimes S_{\lambda_{c}+p^{\prime}}(G_{c+1}^{\prime\prime})^{\ast},

where
q^{\prime}=(c-1)(k+p^{\prime})+1-cp^{\prime}-\lambda_{1}-\ldots-\lambda_{c-1}-\mu_{1}-k.

Observing that
p^{\prime}=p+k-\mu_{1}
, we get

\kappa(\mathcal{L}^{\prime})=\lambda_{c}+p^{\prime}=\lambda_{c}+p+k-\mu_{1}=\lambda_{c}+p+\mu_{2}+\ldots+\mu_{t}=\kappa(\mathcal{S}^{\prime}).

Hence
k+p+q=\kappa(\mathcal{S}^{\prime})=\kappa(\mathcal{L}^{\prime})=k+p^{\prime}+q^{\prime}
, implying
p+q=p^{\prime}+q^{\prime}
. This obviously implies that
\lambda_{i}+p+q=\lambda_{i}+p^{\prime}+q^{\prime}
for every
i=1,\ldots,c-1
.

To prove
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}=\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{S}^{\prime})
we still need to show that
\mu_{1}+q=k+q^{\prime}
and
\lambda_{c}+p=\mu_{1}+p^{\prime}+q^{\prime}.

But, from the equality
q-q^{\prime}=p-p^{\prime}=k-\mu_{1}
, we obtain
\mu_{1}+q=k+q^{\prime}
, while from the definitions of
q
and
p
, we get

q+\mu_{1}=(c-2)k+1-p-\lambda_{1}-\ldots-\lambda_{c-1}=\lambda_{c}.

From this we obtain

\mu_{1}+p^{\prime}+q^{\prime}=\mu_{1}+p+q=\lambda_{c}+p.

By definition of doubling, this shows that
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}=\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{S}^{\prime})
.

Finally, by induction on
k
, this also proves that
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{S})
is a decoration. Indeed,
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{S})
is a double link of
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{S}^{\prime})
, which is a decoration by inductive hypothesis thanks to the fact that
\kappa(\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{S}^{\prime}))=\kappa(\mathcal{L}^{\prime})=\kappa(\mathcal{S}^{\prime})<k.

∎

.

8.5
Licci conjecture for
(c,d,t)
triplets

To conclude this paper, we summarize the expected results and the state of the art regarding the question of which perfect ideals of codimension
c
, deviation
d
, and type
t
are licci.

Conjecture 8.19
.

Fix
c\geq 3
,
d\geq 1
,
t\geq 1
. Then:

(a)

If the diagram
T_{c-1,d+1,t+1}
is Dynkin (of type ADE), then every perfect ideal of codimension
c
, deviation
d
, and type
t
is licci.

(b)

If the diagram
T_{c-1,d+1,t+1}
is not Dynkin, then there exist perfect ideals of codimension
c
, deviation
d
, and type
t
that are not licci.

In the case
c=3
, both parts of the conjecture have positive answer
[
9
]
,
[
13
]

[
15
]
. In an upcoming paper, we plan to present a positive answer also in the case
c=4
,
t=1
(Gorenstein ideals of codimension 4). By taking hyperplane sections, it is easy to observe that if the triplet
(c,d,t)
admits perfect non-licci ideals, so does the triplet
(c+1,d,t)
. Moreover, it is not hard to exhibit non-licci perfect ideals corresponding to triplets
(4,4,2),(4,2,4)
.
Hence, to conclude this study in codimension 4, it will be required to analyze the triplets
(4,2,2),(4,3,2),(4,2,3)
, and discover if they admit any non-licci perfect ideal. As pointed out at the end of Section
8.4
, in higher codimension, also the existence of non-licci perfect ideals for the triplets
(c,2,1)
is not known.

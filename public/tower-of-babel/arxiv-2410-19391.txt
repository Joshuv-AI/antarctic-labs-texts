Abstract
. This paper studies the defect relation through the GCD method. In particular, among other results, we extend the defect relation result of Chen, Huynh, Sun and Xie ( [ 1 ] ) to moving targets. The truncated defect relation is also studied. Furthermore, we obtain the degeneracy locus, which can be determined effectively and is independent of the maps under the consideration.



1.
Motivations

Let
f:{\mathbb{C}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
be a holomorphic map.
It is known that (see
[
14
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
15
]
) if
f({\mathbb{C}})
omits
n+2
smooth hypersurfaces
D_{j},1\leq j\leq n+2
,
of
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
, where
D:=D_{1}+\cdots+D_{n+2}
is located in general position, then

f
must be algebraically degenerate (i.e.
f({\mathbb{C}})
is contained in a proper sub-variety of
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
). In
[
11
]
, J. Noguchi, J. Winkelmann, and K. Yamanoi showed that the number
n+2

of the omitting hypersurfaces indeed could be reduced to
n+1
when
\deg D\geq n+2
.
Their proof relies on their earlier result for holomorphic maps from
{\mathbb{C}}
in the semi-abelian variety
A:=({\mathbb{C}}^{*})^{n}
, which is stated as follows.

Theorem A

(
[
12
]
)
.

Let
D
be an effective divisor on
A:=({\mathbb{C}}^{*})^{n}
.
Let
f:{\mathbb{C}}\rightarrow A
be an algebraically nondegenerate holomorphic map.
Then there exists a smooth compactification of
A
independent of
f
, such that

(1.1)

N_{f}(D,r)-N_{f}^{(1)}(D,r)\leq_{\operatorname{exc}}\epsilon\,T_{f,\overline{D}}(r)

for any
\epsilon>0
.

Using the above theorem, Noguchi, Winkelmann, and Yamanoi showed that one can reduce the number of omitting divisors by one (i.e., from
n+2
to
n+1
) whose
argument is similar to ours described in Section 5. We briefly outline the argument here:
Assume that
D_{j}=\{Q_{j}=0\}
and
f({\mathbb{C}})
omits
D_{j}
for
1\leq j\leq n+1
. Consider a morphism
\pi:\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{C})\to\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{C})

given by

{\mathbf{x}}\mapsto[Q_{1}^{a_{1}}({\mathbf{x}}):\ldots:Q_{n+1}^{a_{n+1}}({\mathbf{x}})]
,
where
a_{i}\coloneqq{\rm lcm}(\deg Q_{1},\ldots,\deg Q_{n+1})/\deg Q_{i}
.
Let

\displaystyle G:=\det\left(\frac{\partial Q_{i}}{\partial x_{j}}\right)_{1\leq i\leq n+1,0\leq j\leq n}\in{\mathbb{C}}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}].

By taking out a nonconstant irreducible factor
\tilde{G}
of
G
in
K[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}]
, one produces an additional hypersurface
{\tilde{D}}_{n+2}=\{\tilde{G}=0\}
in
\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{C})
.
Furthermore, one can show that
D_{1},\dots,D_{n+1},

\tilde{D}_{n+2}
are located in general position, and, by using (
1.1
), one can show that
N_{f}({\tilde{D}}_{n+2},r)\leq_{\operatorname{exc}}\epsilon\,T_{f}(r)
. Thus we can apply the Second Main Theorem obtained by the first author (
[
15
]
) to get the conclusion.

In a recent manuscript by Z. Chen, D.T. Huynh, R. Sun and S.Y. Xie (see
[
1
]
), the result of Noguchi-Winkelmann-Yamanoi mentioned above was further extended to the following about the defect relation.

Theorem B

(Z. Chen, D.T. Huynh, R. Sun and S.Y. Xie,
[
1
]
)
.

Let
\{D_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n+1}
be
n+1
hypersurfaces with total degrees
\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}\deg(D_{i})\geq n+2

satisfying one precised generic condition
(
see (4.3) in their paper
[
1
]
)
. Then, for every algebraically non-degenerate entire holomorphic curve
f:{\mathbb{C}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
, the following defect relation holds:

\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}\delta_{f}(D_{j})<n+1.

Note that, in the omitting case, we have that
Q_{j}(f)
is nowhere zero for all
1\leq j\leq n+1
where
D_{j}=\{Q_{j}=0\}
so that one can reduce it to the semi-abelian variety case
({\mathbb{C}}^{*})^{n}
by
considering
F:=[Q_{1}(f),\dots,Q_{n}(f)]\in({\mathbb{C}}^{*})^{n}
, assuming that
\deg(Q_{1})=\cdots=\deg(Q_{n+1})
. Hence Theorem A could be applied directly.
However, in their “proof-by-contradiction” argument of the proof of Theorem B, the condition that
\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}\delta_{f}(D_{j})=n+1
only implies that
N_{f}(r,D_{i})={\rm o}(T_{f}(r))
(rather than
f({\mathbb{C}})
omitting
D_{j}
). To overcome this difficulty, they used the “parabolic Nevanlinna theory”
developed by M. P
{\rm{\breve{a}}}
un and N. Sibony (see
[
13
]
), by considering the holomorphic mapping
f:Y\rightarrow{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
with
Y:={\mathbb{C}}\backslash f^{-1}(D)
, which leads to the omitting case after restricting
f
to
Y
. The key ingredient in their paper is to show that
Y
is an open parabolic Riemann surface with exhaustion function
\sigma
satisfying

\limsup_{r\rightarrow\infty}{\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma}(r)\over T_{f}(r)}=0.

Note that while the method of Chen-Huynh-Sun-Xie is very interesting and creative, it still relies on the result of Noguchi, Winkelmann, and Yamanoi (Theorem A), which greatly depends on the geometry of semi-abelian varieties. For example, it is very hard to generalize the result to the moving target case.

This paper studies the defect relation through the GCD method. We don’t use Theorem A. Indeed, we give and prove a variant and more general version of Theorem A by using the GCD theorem established by
Aaron Levin and the second author
[
9
]
. This allows us to get a much more general defect relation (for example the moving target case). The method was initiated by P. Corvaja and U. Zannier (see
[
2
]
), where they studied the
n=2
case.
After Aaron Levin and the second author
[
9
]
established the general GCD theorem, it has been successfully used in a series papers by the second author and her co-authors, see

[
5
]
,
[
6
]
,
[
7
]
,
[
8
]
. The purpose of this paper is to further use the ideas developed in
[
5
]
,
[
6
]
,
[
7
]
,
[
8
]
to extend the defect relation, for example to the moving target case, by using the GCD method. Furthermore, the truncated defect relation is also studied. We also pay attention on the degenerate locus. In particular, we can relax the condition that
f
being algebraically non-degenerate to the condition that the image of
f
is not contained in a subvariety
Z
which can be effectively pre-determined and is independent of
f
, in the sprite of the strong Green-Griffith-Lang conjecture.

2.
Statement of the results

We use the standard notations in Nevanlinna theory (see
[
16
]
, or
[
5
]
,
[
6
]
,
[
7
]
,
[
8
]
). Let
\mathbf{g}=(g_{0},\ldots,g_{n}):\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{C})
be a holomorphic curve, where
g_{0},\ldots,g_{n}
are entire functions without common zero. We recall that the
small field with respect to
\mathbf{g}
is given by

(2.1)

K_{\mathbf{g}}:=\{a:a\text{ is a meromorphic function on ${\mathbb{C}}$ with }T_{a}(r)={\rm o}(T_{\mathbf{g}}(r))\}.

Let
K
be a subfield of the field
\mathcal{M}
of meromorphic functions. We say that
Z
is a Zariski closed subset in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
defined over
K
if there exists a non-constant homogeneous polynomial
F\in K[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}]
such that
Z=\{[f_{0}:\cdots:f_{n}]\in\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathcal{M}):F(f_{0},\ldots,f_{n})\equiv 0\}
. We say a holomorphic map
\mathbf{g}:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{P}^{n}
is not contained in
Z
if
\mathbf{g}
is not identically zero.
In particular, when
K=\mathbb{C}
, the Zariski closed set is defined over
\mathbb{C}
, i.e.
F\in\mathbb{C}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}]
, and
\mathbf{g}
is not contained in
Z
is equivalent to
F(\mathbf{g})\not\equiv 0
.
For each polynomial
G=\sum_{I}a_{I}{{\bf x}^{I}}\in K[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}]
, where

I=(i_{0},\ldots,i_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n+1}
and
{\bf x}^{I}=x_{0}^{i_{0}}\cdots x_{n}^{i_{n}}
, we denote by
G({z_{0}}):=\sum_{I}a_{I}(z_{0}){{\bf x}^{I}}
if all the coefficients of
G
are holomorphic at
z_{0}
and do not vanish simultaneously at
z_{0}
.
Let
G_{1},\ldots,G_{q}
be non-constant polynomials in
K[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}]
. We say that they are
in weakly general position
if there exist a point
z_{0}\in\mathbb{C}
such that for each
G_{i}({z_{0}})
,
1\leq i\leq q
, can be defined as above and the zero locus of
G_{i}({z_{0}})
(as a divisor in
\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{C})
),
1\leq i\leq q
, are in general position.

Theorem 1
.

Let
K
be a subfield of the field
\mathcal{M}
of meromorphic functions.
Let
F
be a nonconstant homogeneous polynomial in
K[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}]
with no monomial factors and no repeated factors. Denote by
H_{i}
,
0\leq i\leq n
, the coordinate hyperplanes of
\mathbb{P}^{n}
.
Then, for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists a proper Zariski closed subset
Z
of
\mathbb{P}^{n}
defined over
K
such that for any nonconstant holomorphic curve
\mathbf{g}:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{C})
with
K\subset K_{\mathbf{g}}
,
N_{\mathbf{g}}(H_{i},r)={\rm o}(T_{\mathbf{g}}(r))
for
0\leq i\leq n
and
\mathbf{g}
is not contained in
Z
, we have

(2.2)

\displaystyle N_{\mathbf{g}}([F=0],r)-N^{(1)}_{\mathbf{g}}([F=0],r)\leq_{\rm exc}\epsilon T_{\mathbf{g}}(r).

If we assume furthermore that the hypersurface defined by
F
in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
and the coordinate hyperplanes are in weakly general position, then

(2.3)

\displaystyle N^{(1)}_{\mathbf{g}}([F=0],r)\geq_{\rm exc}(\deg F-\epsilon)\cdot T_{\mathbf{g}}(r).

Moreover, the exceptional set
Z
can be expressed as the zero locus of a finite set

\Sigma\subset K[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}]
with the following properties:
(Z1)

\Sigma
depends on
\epsilon
and
F
only and can be determined explicitly;
(Z2)
the degree of each polynomial in
\Sigma
can be effectively bounded from above in terms of
\epsilon
,
n
, and the degree of
F
.

Theorem 2
.

Let
K
be a subfield of the field of meromorphic functions. Let

F_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq n+1
, be homogeneous irreducible polynomials of positive degree in
K[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}]
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}\deg F_{i}\geq n+2
.
Assume that there exists
z_{0}\in\mathbb{C}
such that all the coefficients of all
F_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq n+1
, are holomorphic at
z_{0}
and the zero locus of
F_{i}
evaluated at
z_{0}
,
1\leq i\leq n+1
, intersect transversally. Then there exists a non-trivial homogeneous polynomial
B\in K[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}]
such that for any nonconstant holomorphic map
\mathbf{f}:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{P}^{n}
with
K\subset K_{\mathbf{f}}
and
N_{F_{i}(\mathbf{f})}(0,r)={\rm o}(T_{\mathbf{f}}(r))
for
1\leq i\leq n+1
, we have
B(\mathbf{f})\equiv 0
. Furthermore,
B
can be determined effectively and its degree can be effectively bounded from above in terms of
n
, and the degrees of
F_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq n+1
.

Theorem 3

(Defect relation for moving targets)
.

Let
K
be a subfield of the field of meromorphic functions. Let

F_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq n+1
, be homogeneous irreducible polynomials of positive degree in
K[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}]
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}\deg F_{i}\geq n+2
.
Assume that there exists
z_{0}\in\mathbb{C}
such that all the coefficients of
F_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq n+1
, are holomorphic at
z_{0}
and the zero locus of
F_{i}
evaluated at
z_{0}
,
1\leq i\leq n+1
, intersect transversally. Then there exists a non-trivial homogeneous polynomial
B\in K[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}]
such that for any nonconstant holomorphic map
\mathbf{f}:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{P}^{n}
with
K\subset K_{\mathbf{f}}
and
B(\mathbf{f})\not\equiv 0
, we have
the following defect inequality holds

\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}\delta_{\mathbf{f}}(D_{i})<n+1,

where
D_{i}=[F_{i}=0]
.

Additionally, if
n=2
, then

\sum_{i=1}^{3}\delta_{\mathbf{f}}^{(1)}(D_{i})<3,

where, for a divisor
D
with
d=\deg(D)
,

\delta_{\mathbf{f}}^{(1)}(D)=1-\limsup_{r\rightarrow\infty}{N^{(1)}_{f}(r,D)\over dT_{f}(r)}.

Note that when
K={\mathbb{C}}
, then
B\in{\mathbb{C}}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}]
, so we have the following strong defect relation in the sprite of the strong Green-Griffith-Lang conjecture.

Corollary 4
.

Let
D_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq n+1
, be
n+1
hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{C})
, not all being hyperplanes.
Assume
D_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq n+1
, intersect transversally. Then there exists a Zariski closed subset
Z
in
\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{C})
, which
can be determined effectively and its degree can be effectively bounded from above in terms of
n
, and the degree of
D_{i}
,
such that for any non-constant holomorphic map
\mathbf{f}:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{C})
whose image is not contained in
Z
, the following defect inequality holds

\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}\delta_{\mathbf{f}}(D_{i})<n+1.

Additionally, if
n=2
, then

\sum_{i=1}^{3}\delta_{\mathbf{f}}^{(1)}(D_{i})<3.

3.
Some preliminary and the GCD theorem

3.1.
Preliminary

We will make use of the following elementary inequality (see
[
16
]
).

Proposition 5
.

Let
{\mathbf{f}}=(f_{0},\ldots,f_{n}):\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{C})
be holomorphic curve, where
f_{0},\dots,f_{n}
are entire functions without common zeros. Assume that
f_{0}
is not identically zero. Then

T_{f_{j}/f_{0}}(r)+{\rm O}(1)\leq T_{\mathbf{f}}(r)\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n}T_{f_{j}/f_{0}}(r)+{\rm O}(1).

Combining the above proposition, we state the following result.

Theorem 6

(
[
17
, Theorem 2.1]
)
.

Let
f_{0},\dots,f_{n}
be entire functions with no common zeros. Assume that
f_{n+1}
is the holomorphic function such that
f_{0}+\dots+f_{n}+f_{n+1}=0
. If
\sum_{i\in I}f_{i}\neq 0
for any proper subset
I\subset\{0,\dots,n+1\}
, then

T_{f_{j}/f_{i}}(r)\leq T_{\mathbf{f}}(r)+{\rm O}(1)\leq_{\operatorname{exc}}\sum_{i=0}^{n+1}N_{f_{i}}^{(n)}(0,r)+{\rm O}(\log T_{\mathbf{f}}(r))

for any pair
0\leq i,j\leq n
, where
\mathbf{f}:=(f_{0},\ldots,f_{n})
.

We will need the following version of Hilbert Nullstellensatz reformulated from
[
3
, Proposition 2.1]
. (See also
[
18
, Chapter XI]
.)

Proposition 7

(
[
3
, Proposition 2.1]
)
.

Let
K
be a subfield of the field of meromorphic functions. Let
\{Q_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n+1}
be a set of homogeneous polynomials in
K[x_{0},\dots,x_{n}]
in weakly general position and with
\deg Q_{j}=d_{j}\geq 1
. Then there exist a positive integer
s
, an element
R\in K
which is not identically zero and
P_{ji}\in K[x_{0},\dots,x_{n}]
,
1\leq i,j\leq n+1
, such that, for each
0\leq j\leq n
,

x_{j}^{s}\cdot R=\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}P_{ji}Q_{i}.

The following is a version of the Borel Lemma for small functions. The proof can easily be obtained with some slightly modifications from
[
8
, Lemma 3.3]
.

Lemma 8
.

Let
f_{0},\ldots,f_{n}
be nontrivial entire functions with no common zero and let
\mathbf{f}:=(f_{0},\ldots,f_{n})
.
Assume that

N^{(1)}_{f_{i}}(0,r)={\rm o}(T_{\mathbf{f}}(r)),\quad\text{ for $0\leq i\leq n$}.

If
f_{0},\ldots,f_{n}
are linearly dependent over
K_{\bf f}
, then for each
f_{i}
, there exists
j\neq i
such that
f_{i}/f_{j}\in K_{\bf f}
.

3.2.
The GCD theorem

Theorem 9

(The GCD theorem)
.

Let
g_{0},g_{1},\dots,g_{n}
be entire functions without common zeros and let
{\bf g}=[g_{0}:g_{1}:\cdots:g_{n}]
.
Let
F,G\in K_{\bf g}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}]
be nonconstant coprime homogeneous polynomials. Assume that one of the following holds:

(a)

N_{g_{i}}(0,r)={\rm o}(T_{\bf g}(r))
for
0\leq i\leq n
;

(b)

N^{(1)}_{g_{i}}(0,r)={\rm o}(T_{\bf g}(r))
for
0\leq i\leq n
and one of the hypersurfaces defined by
G=0
or
F=0
in
\mathbb{P}^{n}(K)
is in weakly general position with the
n+1
coordinate hyperplanes.

Then, for any
\epsilon>0
,
there exist a positive integers
m
independent of
{\bf g}
such that we have either

(3.1)

\displaystyle N_{\rm gcd}(F(g_{0},\ldots,g_{n}),G(g_{0},\ldots,g_{n}),r)\leq_{\rm exc}\epsilon T_{\bf g}(r),

or

(3.2)

\displaystyle T_{(\frac{g_{1}}{g_{0}})^{m_{1}}\cdots(\frac{g_{n}}{g_{0}})^{m_{n}}}(r)={\rm o}(T_{\bf g}(r))

for some non-trivial tuple of integers
(m_{1},\ldots,m_{n})
with
|m_{1}|+\cdots+|m_{n}|\leq 2m
.

For the convenience of later application, we state the following result for
n=1
.

Proposition 10
.

Let
g_{0},g_{1}
be entire functions without common zeros and let
{\bf g}=(g_{0},g_{1})
. Assume that
{\bf g}
is not constant.
Let
F,G\in K_{\bf g}[x_{0},x_{1}]
be nonconstant coprime homogeneous polynomials. Then

(3.3)

\displaystyle N_{\rm gcd}(F(g_{0},g_{1}),G(g_{0},g_{1}),r)\leq{\rm o}(T_{\bf g}(r)).

Proof.

Since
F
and
G
are coprime homogeneous polynomials in
K_{\bf g}[x_{0},x_{1}]
, we may apply Proposition
7
to find an integer
s
,
R\in K_{\bf g}\setminus\{0\}
and
H_{i}\in K_{\bf g}[x_{0},x_{1}]
,
1\leq i\leq 4
, such that

(3.4)

\displaystyle x_{0}^{s}\cdot R=H_{1}F+H_{2}G\quad\text{and }\quad x_{1}^{s}\cdot R=H_{3}F+H_{4}G.

Here, we may assume that
H_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq 4
, are homogeneous polynomials with degree equal to
s-\deg F
.
By evaluating (
3.4
) at
(g_{0},g_{1})
, we have

(3.5)

\displaystyle g_{0}^{s}\cdot R

\displaystyle=H_{1}(g_{0},g_{1})F(g_{0},g_{1})+H_{2}(g_{0},g_{1})G(g_{0},g_{1}),

(3.6)

\displaystyle g_{1}^{s}\cdot R

\displaystyle=H_{3}(g_{0},g_{1})F(g_{0},g_{1})+H_{4}(g_{0},g_{1})G(g_{0},g_{1}).

Since
g_{0}
and
g_{1}
have no common zeros, we observe that

(3.7)

\mathop{\mathrm{min}}\{v_{z}^{+}(F(g_{0},g_{1})),v_{z}^{+}(G(g_{0},g_{1}))\}\leq v_{z}^{+}(R)+\sum_{\alpha\in I}v_{z}^{-}(\alpha)

for each
z\in\mathbb{C}
. Here
I
is the set of nontrivial coefficients of
H_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq 4
. Hence,

(3.8)

N_{\gcd}(F(g_{0},g_{1}),G(g_{0},g_{1}),r)\leq N_{R}(0,r)+\sum_{\alpha\in I}N_{\alpha}(\infty,r)\leq{\rm o}(T_{\bf g}(r)),

as
R
and the coefficients of
F_{i}
are in
K_{\bf g}
.
∎

To prove Theorem
9
, we use the following fundamental result by Levin and the second author for
n\geq 2
.

Theorem 11

(
[
9
, Theorem 5.7]
)
.

Let
g_{0},g_{1},\dots,g_{n}
be entire functions without common zeros with
n\geq 2
and let
{\bf g}=[g_{0}:g_{1}:\cdots:g_{n}]
.
Let
F,G\in K_{\bf g}[x_{0},x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
be coprime homogeneous polynomials of the same degree
d>0
. Let
I
be the set of exponents
{\bf i}
such that
{\bf x}^{\bf i}
appears with a nonzero coefficient in either
F
or
G
. Let
m\geq d
be a positive integer. Suppose that the set
\{g_{0}^{i_{0}}\dots g_{n}^{i_{n}}:i_{0}+\cdots+i_{n}=m\}
is linearly independent over
K_{\bf g}
. Then, for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists a positive integer
L
such that the following holds:

(3.9)

\displaystyle MN_{\rm gcd}(F({\bf g}),G({\bf g}),r)

\displaystyle\leq_{\rm exc}c_{m,n,d}\sum_{i=1}^{n}N^{(L)}_{g_{i}}(0,r)+\left(\frac{m}{n+1}\binom{m+n}{n}-c_{m,n,d}-M^{\prime}m\right)\sum_{i=1}^{n}N_{g_{i}}(0,r)

\displaystyle+\binom{m+n-2d}{n}N_{\rm gcd}(\{{\bf g}^{\bf i}\}_{{\bf i}\in I},r)+\left(M^{\prime}mn+\epsilon m+\frac{M\epsilon}{2}\right)T_{\bf g}(r)+{\rm o}(T_{\bf g}(r)),

where
c_{m,n,d}=2\binom{m+n-d}{n+1}-\binom{m+n-2d}{n+1}
,

M=2\binom{m+n-d}{n}-\binom{m+n-2d}{n}
, and
M^{\prime}
is an integer of order
{\rm O}(m^{n-2})
, where
\leq_{\rm exc}
means the inequality holds for all
r\in(0,\infty)
except for a set
E
of finite measure.

Proof of Theorem
9
.

Without loss of generality, we assume that
\deg F=\deg G
.
We first prove when
n\geq 2
. Let
\epsilon>0
. To establish (
3.3
) or (
3.2
), we can assume that
\epsilon
is sufficiently small. We can choose a real
C_{1}\geq 1
independent of
\epsilon
and
{\bf g}
such that
m=C_{1}\epsilon^{-1}\geq 2d
and

(3.10)

\displaystyle\frac{M^{\prime}mn}{M}\leq\frac{\epsilon}{4},\quad\text{and }\quad\frac{1}{M}\left(\frac{m}{n+1}\binom{m+n}{n}-c_{m,n,d}-M^{\prime}m\right)\leq\frac{\epsilon}{4(n+1)}.

We may assume that each
g_{i}

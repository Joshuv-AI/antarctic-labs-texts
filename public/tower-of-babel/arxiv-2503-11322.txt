Trigonometric inequalities for Fibonacci chains
Anna Chiara Lai, Paola Loreti

Abstract
. In this work, we consider m m -bonacci chains, unidimensional quasicrystals obtained by general classes of Rauzy substitutions. Motivated by applications in spectrography and diffraction patterns of some quasicrystals, we pose the problem of establishing Ingham type trigonometric inequalities when the frequencies belong to m m -bonacci chains. The result is achieved by characterizing the upper density of the m m -bonacci chains. Tools from symbolic dynamics and combinatorics on words are used. Explicit gap conditions for the particular cases of Fibonacci chains and Tribonacci chains complete the paper.

1.
Introduction

Nonharmonic Fourier series and quasicrystals have shown deep connections in the last decades, particularly via the study of aperiodic structures like the Fibonacci quasicrystal. This paper aims to further explore these relations by establishing Ingham type inequalities for Fourier series of the form

f(t)=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}a_{k}e^{i\lambda_{k}t}\qquad a_{k}\in\mathbb{C}

where the frequencies
(\lambda_{k})
belong to the so-called
m
-bonacci chains
\Lambda_{m}
. Defined for any
m\geq 2
,
the point set
\Lambda_{m}
is a generalization of the Fibonacci quasicrystal, which corresponds to the case
m=2
. Also in the case
m>2
, the
m
-bonacci chain
\Lambda_{m}
is an unbounded, discrete set with aperiodic order and a pure point diffraction pattern, namely it is a quasicrystalline uni-dimensional structure
[
Bar15
]
.
Its elements are separated by exactly
m
distinct types of gaps. Precisely, ordering
\Lambda_{m}
as a strictly increasing sequence
(\lambda_{k})
then for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
we have
\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{k}=\rho_{m}^{-j}
for some
j=1,\dots,m
; where
\rho_{m}
is the greatest root of
x^{m}-x^{m-1}-\cdots-x-1
. For example, in the case of
m=2
, the corresponding
2
-bonacci chain, namely the Fibonacci quasicrystal, has exactly
2
gaps,
1/\varphi
and
1/\varphi^{2}
, where
\varphi
is the Golden Ratio. Connections between nonharmonic Fourier series and quasicrystals were widely explored, we refer for instance to
[
Mey00
,
MM08
]
. The case
m=2
(corresponding to the Fibonacci quasicrystals) was intensively investigated due to their applications in optics
[
WSK+09
]
, material science (Fibonacci quasicrystals were observed in some rapidly solidified alloys
[
HLZK88
]
), and in quantum computing
[
VB14
]
. The case
m=3
corresponds to the Tribonacci chain, whose spectral properties were investigated in
[
KMDMS23
]
– see also
[
GHT20
]
for applications in optics. To the best of our knowledge there are very few papers devoted to a systematic investigation of the general case
m>3
, for instance the already mentioned paper
[
Bar15
]
.

Here we use the notion of
upper density
, that requires a careful analysis of the combinatorial properties of the
m
-bonacci chains. Our results offer a detailed analysis of the
m
-bonacci chains with general order
m
, their upper densities and their gap conditions, by extending classic results in nonharmonic analysis. In particular, this study allows us to use a generalization of Ingham’s inequalities
[
Ing36
]
, that is the well known Beurling theorem
[
Beu89
]
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we recall the definition and the main properties underlying the concept of the
m
-bonacci chains, namely the
m
-bonacci words
. Such bi-infinite words are built upon the celebrated Rauzy substitutions, that have a preeminent role in symbolic dynamics and combinatorics on words
[
Lot02
,
BR10
]
. Although the results in Section
2
are well established, to keep the paper as self-contained as possible, we provide a systematic organization of this material and some proofs, tailored to our connections with nonharmonic Fourier series. Thanks to the results on the digit frequencies we are able in Section
3
to compute the upper density of the
m
-bonacci chains and to apply Beurling result. In Section
4
we establish explicit gap conditions for the Fibonacci case and the Tribonacci case. This is achieved with a detailed analysis of the digit distribution on the corresponding
m
-bonacci word when
m=2,3
. This investigation is motivated by
[
KL05
, pages 173-174]
.

2.
Preliminaries on Rauzy substitutions

In this section, we introduce the fundamental notions and results needed to define the
m
-bonacci chain
\Lambda_{m}
properly and for the computation of its upper density. The construction of
\Lambda_{m}
relies on the associated
m
-bonacci word

\mathbf{v}_{m}
, a bi-infinite word with digits in the alphabet
\{1,\dots,m\}
. The upper density of
\Lambda_{m}
is closely tied to the digit frequencies in
\mathbf{v}_{m}
. Indeed, as we shall see in Section
3
, the word
\mathbf{v}_{m}
encodes the gaps in the
m
-bonacci chain
\Lambda_{m}
: small digits correspond to big gaps and vice-versa. For any
m\geq 2
, the
m
-bonacci word
\mathbf{v}_{m}
is generated iteratively, via the repeated application of a substitution map
\sigma_{m}
–known as the
Rauzy substitution
– up to convergence to its fixed point, which is
\mathbf{v}_{m}
. So, starting with exploring the properties of the Rauzy substitution
\sigma_{m}
, we gain insight into the density of the associated
m
-bonacci chain. Symbolic dynamics provides an extensive range of theoretical tools in this area; among them, we shall focus on a key point: the digit frequency of the fixed point
\mathbf{v}_{m}
of the Rauzy substitution
\sigma_{m}
is related to the eigenvalues and eigenvectors of a matrix (the
incidence matrix
) describing the Rauzy substitution itself. In particular, if the largest eigenvalue of the incidence matrix (called the
Perron-Frobenius eigenvalue
) has some algebraic properties (showed in Proposition
1
) then we can explicitly compute the digit frequencies of the words
\mathbf{v}_{m}
(Proposition
2
) which will allow us, in Section
3
, to get the uppper density of
\Lambda_{m}
.

In what follows we recall the key definitions and concepts to support this line of argument.

Let
\mathcal{A}=\{a_{0},\dots,a_{m}\}
be a finite alphabet, whose elements are called in the following
digits
, and let
\mathcal{A}^{*}
be the set of finite words over
\mathcal{A}
. A
substitution
is a function
\sigma:\mathcal{A}^{*}\to\mathcal{A}^{*}
such that
\sigma(uv)=\sigma(u)\sigma(v)
for all words
u,v\in\mathcal{A}^{*}
and such that
\sigma(a)
is not the empty word for every letter
a\in\mathcal{A}
.
The domain
\mathcal{A}^{*}
of a substitution
\sigma
can naturally be extended to the set of infinite sequences
\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{N}}
and to set of bi-infinite sequences
\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{Z}}
by concatenation. In particular if
\mathbf{w}=w_{1}w_{2}\cdots\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{N}}
then
\sigma(\mathbf{w})=\sigma(w_{1})\sigma(w_{2})\cdots
and if
\mathbf{w}=\cdots w_{-1}.\,w_{0}w_{1}\cdots\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{Z}}
then
\sigma(\mathbf{w})=\cdots\sigma(w_{-1}).\,\sigma(w_{0})\sigma(w_{1})\cdots
.
In this paper we shall consider the alphabets
\mathcal{A}_{m}:=\{1,\dots,m\}
with
m\geq 2
.
For every
m\geq 2
, we consider
Rauzy substitution

\sigma_{m}:\mathcal{A}_{m}^{*}\to\mathcal{A}_{m}^{*}
defined by

\sigma_{m}:\begin{cases}1\mapsto 12\\
2\mapsto 13\\
\cdots\\
m-1\mapsto 1m\\
m\mapsto 1\end{cases}

The substitution
\sigma_{2}
is called
Fibonacci substitution
and the substitution
\sigma_{3}
is called
Tribonacci substitution
. As anticipated in the Introduction, these substitutions have a preeminent role in fractals and quasicrystalline structures.

We use the symbol
|w|
to denote the length of a finite word and we define the
weight

|w|_{i}
of a word
w
with respect to the
i
-th letter of
\mathcal{A}
, namely the number of occurrences of the letter
a_{i}
in the word
w
.

Definition 1

(Uniform frequency)
.

Let
\mathbf{w}
be an infinite (bi-infinite) word and let
i=1,\dots,m
. If for all
k\geq 0
(
k\in\mathbb{Z}
) the limit

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{|w_{k+1}\cdots w_{k+n}|_{j}}{n}

exists uniformly with respect to
k
, then it is called the
uniform frequency
f_{j}(\mathbf{w})
of the digit
a_{j}
in
\mathbf{w}
. Equivalently, if above limit exists, we can define

f_{j}(\mathbf{w}):=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\sup\{|w|_{j}\mid w\text{ is a subword of length $n$ of $\mathbf{w}$}\}}{n}.

The
incidence matrix

M_{\sigma}
of a subsitution
\sigma
defined on the alphabet
\mathcal{A}_{m}
is a
m\times m
-matrix whose entries are
M_{ij}:=|\sigma(a_{i})|_{j}
for
i,j=1,\dots,m
.

In particular, the incidence matrices of the Fibonacci and Tribonacci substitutions are respectively

M_{\sigma_{2}}=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix};\qquad M_{\sigma_{3}}=\begin{pmatrix}1&1&0\\
1&0&1\\
1&0&0\end{pmatrix};

whereas for a generic Rauzy substitution
\sigma_{m}
with
m\geq 4
we have

M_{\sigma_{m}}=\begin{pmatrix}1&1&0&\cdots&0\\
1&0&1&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\
1&0&\cdots&0&1\\
1&0&\cdots&0&0\end{pmatrix}

Note the recursive relation

(1)

M_{\sigma_{m}}=\begin{pmatrix}M_{\sigma_{m-1}}&\mathbf{e}^{T}_{m-1,m-1}\\
\mathbf{e}_{m-1,1}&0\\
\end{pmatrix}

where
\mathbf{e}_{m,j}
is the
j
-th element of the canonical base of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
.
Rauzy substitutions have important properties in terms of combinatorics of words: in order to describe them we need some definitions.

Definition 2
.

The
Perron-Frobenious eigenvalue
of a substitution is defined as the largest eigenvalue of its adjacency matrix. We denote by
\rho_{m}
the Perron-Frobenious eigenvalue of
\sigma_{m}
.

A substitution
\sigma
is a
Pisot substitution
if its Perron-Frobenious eigenvalue is a Pisot number, namely an algebraic integer greater than
1
whose conjugates are less than
1
in modulus.

By a direct computation we have that, for the case of Fibonacci and Tribonacci substitution we respectively have that
\rho_{2}
is the Golden Mean, namely the greatest solution of
\lambda^{2}=\lambda+1
, and
\rho_{3}=1.83929...
is the Tribonacci constant, namely the greatest solution of
\lambda^{3}=\lambda^{2}+\lambda+1
. Both
\rho_{2}
and
\rho_{3}
are Pisot numbers, and consequently both
\sigma_{2}
and
\sigma_{3}
are Pisot substitutions.
This is a general fact that we recall below.

Proposition 1
.

For all
m\geq 2
,
\sigma_{m}
is a Pisot substitution.

In particular, the Perron-Frobenious eigevanlue
\rho_{m}
of
\sigma_{m}
is the Pisot number whose minimal polynomial
\lambda^{m}-\lambda^{m-1}-\cdots-\lambda-1
. Moreover the vector

d_{m}:=(\rho_{m}^{-1},\dots,\rho_{m}^{-m})

is a left eigenvector associated to
\rho_{m}
whose
\ell_{0}
norm is equal to
1
.

Proof.

We proceed by first proving that for all
m\geq 2
the characteristic polynomial of
M_{\sigma_{m}}
is
P_{m}(\lambda)=(-1)^{m}(\lambda^{m}-\lambda^{m-1}-\dots-1)
. The case
m=2
is obtained by a direct computation and we assume it as an inductive hypothesis. Now, remark that in general, setting
I_{m}
the
m\times m
identity matrix, it follows from (
1
)
that

\displaystyle M_{\sigma_{m}}-\lambda I_{m}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}M_{\sigma_{m-1}}-\lambda I_{m-1}&\mathbf{e}_{m-1,m-1}^{T}\\
\mathbf{e}_{m-1,1}&-\lambda\\
\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}(1^{m})^{T}&\mathbf{T}_{m-1}\\
1&-\lambda\mathbf{e}_{m-1,m-1}\end{pmatrix}

where
\mathbf{T}_{m-1}
is a lower triangular matrix whose diagonal elements are
1
, consequently its determinant is
1
. Therefore, also by using the inductive hypothesis,

\displaystyle P_{m}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\det(M_{\sigma_{m}}-\lambda I_{m})

\displaystyle=(-1)^{m+1}\det(\mathbf{T}_{m-1})+(-1)^{2m}(-\lambda)\det(M_{\sigma_{m-1}}-\lambda I_{m-1}))

\displaystyle=(-1)^{m}(-1+(-1)^{m+1}\lambda P_{m-1}(\lambda))

\displaystyle=(-1)^{m}(-1+(-1)^{2m}\lambda(\lambda^{m-1}-\lambda_{m-2}-\cdots-1))

\displaystyle=(-1)^{m}(\lambda^{m}-\lambda^{m-1}-\cdots-1).

The fact that
\rho_{m}
is a Pisot hence follows by
[
Bra50
]
– see also
[
FS92
]
. It is left to check that

is a left eigenvector. Now, we have that the vector equality

d_{m}M_{\sigma_{m}}=\rho_{m}d_{m}^{T}

is equivalent to

\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m}\rho_{m}^{-j},\rho_{m}^{-1},\dots,\rho_{m}^{-m+1}\right)=(1,\rho^{-1},\dots,\rho_{m}^{m-1})

which follows from the fact that
\rho^{m}_{m}=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\rho^{j}_{m}.

3.
Ingham-type inequalities for the
m
-bonacci chains

In the previous section we introduced the
m
-bonacci bi-infinite word
\mathbf{v}_{m}=\cdots v_{-1}.v_{0}v_{1}\cdots
, that is a bi-infinite extension of the fixed point
\mathbf{w}_{m}
of the Rauzy substitution
\sigma_{m}
. The digits of
\mathbf{v}_{m}
belong to the alphabet
\mathcal{A}_{m}=\{1,\ldots,m\}
, and for all
j=1,\ldots,m
there exists the uniform frequency of the digit
j
. It satisfies

(2)

f_{\mathbf{v}_{m}}(j)=\rho_{m}^{-j}

where
\rho_{m}
is the greatest real root of the polynomial
P_{m}(x)=x^{m}-x^{m-1}-\cdots-x-1
. We use
\mathbf{v}_{m}
to construct the
gap sequence

d_{m,k}:=f_{\mathbf{v}_{m}}(v_{k})=\rho_{m}^{-v_{k}}\qquad\text{for all $k\in\mathbb{Z}$}

and the sequence

\lambda_{m,0}:=0;\qquad\lambda_{m,n}:=\sum_{k=1}^{n}d_{m,k},\quad\lambda_{m,-n}:=-\sum_{k=1}^{n}d_{m,-k}\,\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.

Definition 3

(
m
-bonacci chains)
.

For
m\geq 2
, the
m
-bonacci chain

\Lambda_{m}:=\{\lambda_{m,k}\mid k\in\mathbb{Z}\}.

The set
\Lambda_{m}
is a relatively dense, discrete set (namely it is a

is a quasicrystal
[

. In the case

is the celebrated

Definition 4

(Upper density)

The

D^{+}(\Lambda):=\limsup_{r\to+\infty}\frac{\mathbf{n}(r)}{r}

where
\mathbf{n}(r)

Example 3

(Density of the Fibonacci chain)
.

4.
Gap conditions in the Fibonacci chain and in the Tribonacci chain

In this section we are interested in establishing inequalities of the form

\lambda_{m,k+N}-\lambda_{m,k}\geq N\gamma_{m,N}\quad\text{for all }k\in\mathbb{Z}

for some positive constant
\gamma_{m,N}
, for all
N\in\mathbb{N}
and for
m=2,3
, namely in the Fibonacci and the Tribonacci case, respectively. This problem is related to the digit distribution of
\mathbf{v}_{m}
via the relation

(6)

\lambda_{m,k+N}-\lambda_{m,k}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}|v_{k+1}\cdots v_{k+N}|_{j}\frac{1}{\rho_{m}^{j}}

\text{for all }k\in\mathbb{Z},\,N\in\mathbb{N},\,m\geq 2
.
The above identity follows from the definition of
\Lambda_{m}
and states that if we want to measure the length of
[\lambda_{k},\lambda_{k+N}]
we can count how many of each tile
[0,1/\rho^{j}]
are used to pave the
[\lambda_{k},\lambda_{k+N}]
(recall indeed that
\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{k}=1/\rho_{m}^{j}
for some
j=1,\dots,m
)
this number is provided exactly by the number of occurrences of
j
in
v_{k+1}\cdots v_{k+N}
, namely
|v_{k+1}\cdots v_{k+N}|_{j}
.

Motivated by this argument, we first estimate the digit weights and then, in Section
4.2
we state our gap conditions.

4.1.
Digit weights for the Fibonacci and Tribonacci words

The following property of the Fibonacci word follows from the more general results
[
Lot02
]
[Proposition 2.1.10] (see also Examples 2.1.9 and 2.1.1 and Remark 2.14 in
[
Lot02
]
). Recall that for a word
w
,
|w|_{2}
is the number of occurrences of the digit
2
in
w
– if we shift the alphabet from
\{1,2\}
to the binary case
\{0,1\}
, the number
|w|_{2}
becomes the
height
of
w
, i.e., the number of non-zero digits in
w
or equivalently (in the binary case) the
Hamming weight
of
w
.

Proposition 4

(
[
Lot02
]
)
.

. For all
N\in\mathbb{N}
and for all subwords
w
of the Fibonacci word
\mathbf{v}^{\infty}_{2}
of length
N
the following holds

\left|\frac{|w|_{2}}{N}-\frac{1}{\varphi^{2}}\right|\leq\frac{1}{N},

The estimates in the above result can be sharpened by considering subwords with length
F_{N}
, the
N
-th Fibonacci number. Recall that the Fibonacci sequence
F_{N}
is recursively defined by
F_{0}:=0
,
F_{1}:=1
,
F_{N}:=F_{N-1}+F_{N-2}
for all
N\geq 2
.

Proposition 5
.

[
ZXZY94
, Property 1 and Property 2]

For all
N\in\mathbb{N}
and for every subword
w
of
\mathbf{v}_{2}^{\infty}
of length
F_{N}
the following holds

F_{N-2}-1\leq|w|_{2}\leq F_{N-2}\text{ if N is odd},

F_{N-2}\leq|w|_{2}\leq F_{N-2}+1\text{ if N is even}.

The above inequalities are sharp, meaning that the corresponding equalities hold for some subword
w
. In particular, we have that the word
\sigma_{2}^{N}(1)
is a subword of
\mathbf{v}_{2}
(in particular
\mathbf{v}_{2}=\cdots\,.\sigma_{2}^{N}(1)\cdots
) has length
F_{N}
and
|\sigma_{2}^{N}(1))|_{2}=F_{N-2}
.

Now, we look into the Tribonacci word. First of all, we introduce the Tribonacci sequence, recursively defined by
T_{0}=T_{1}:=0
,
T_{2}:=1
,
T_{N}=T_{N-1}+T_{N-2}+T_{N-3}
for all
N\geq 3
. Now, we have the following property of
\sigma_{3}

\sigma^{n}(1)=\sigma^{n-1}_{3}(1)\sigma^{n-2}_{3}(1)\sigma^{n-3}_{3}(1)\text{for all }n\geq 3.

Since
|\sigma_{3}^{0}(1)|=1
,
|\sigma_{3}^{1}(1)|=2
and
|\sigma_{3}^{2}(1)|=4
we readily get

|\sigma^{n}(1)|=T_{n+3}\text{ for all }n\geq 0.

Moreover for every digit
j=1,2,3
,

|\sigma_{3}^{n}(1)|_{j}=|\sigma_{3}^{n-1}(1)|_{j}+|\sigma_{3}^{n-2}(1)|_{j}+|\sigma_{3}^{n-3}(1)|_{j}

By a direct computation, we have the first terms of this recursion:

|\sigma^{n}_{3}(1)|_{j}=T_{n+3-j}\qquad\text{for $j=1,2,3$ and $n=0,1,2$}.

Therefore we can conclude that

(7)

|\sigma^{n}_{3}(1)|_{j}=T_{n+3-j}\quad\text{for all $n\geq 0$ and for all $j=1,2,3$}.

Above equality describes the digit distribution of the prefix
\sigma_{3}^{n}(1)
of
\mathbf{w}_{3}
, that is, the first
T_{n+3}
non-negative terms of
\mathbf{v}_{3}
. To generalize this results of general subwords of
\mathbf{v}_{3}
, we need the notion of
Tribonacci expansion
of a positive integer. Every positive integer
N
can be uniquely represented as the sum of Tribonacci numbers
[
CSHJ72
]
, that is there exists a positive integer
L=L(N)
and finite binary sequence
x_{3},\dots,x_{L}\in\{0,1\}
such that

N=\sum_{h=3}^{L}x_{h}T_{h}.

Proposition 6

(Weights of the Tribonacci word)
.

Let
N
be a non-nengative integer and let
x_{3},\cdots,x_{L}
, for some
L\geq 3
its Fibonacci expansion. Then for all
j=1,2,3

(8)

|v_{0}\cdots v_{N-1}|_{j}=\sum_{h=3}^{L}x_{h}T_{h-j}.

If
w
is a general subword of length
N
of
\mathbf{v}_{3}
, then

(9)

\left||w|_{j}-\sum_{h=3}^{L}x_{h}T_{h-j}\right|\leq 2\qquad\text{for all $j=1,2,3$ and for all $N\geq 0$.}

In particular, if
N=T_{L}
for some
L\geq 3
, then the above estimate reduces to

(10)

\left||w|_{j}-T_{L+3-j}\right|\leq 2\qquad\text{for all $j=1,2,3$ and for all $N\geq 0$.}

Proof.

Using the greedy algorithm, we can partition every prefix
v_{0}\cdots v_{N-1}
in
L
consecutive subwords
w_{L},\dots,w_{3}
of length
x_{h}T_{h}
with
h=3,\dots,L
. When
x_{h}=0
then the subword
w_{h}
is empty, otherwise
w_{h}=\sigma_{3}^{h-3}(1)
. Using the notation
\sigma^{-1}(1)
to denote the empty word, we can write

v_{0}\cdots v_{N-1}=\sigma_{3}^{x_{L}(L-2)-1}(1)\cdot\sigma_{3}^{x_{L-1}(L-3)-1}(1)\cdots\sigma_{3}^{x_{3}-1}(1).

Consequently, we have that

|v_{0}\cdots v_{N-1}|_{j}=\sum_{h=3}^{L}x_{h}|\sigma_{3}^{h-3}(1)|_{j}\qquad\text{for all $j=1,2,3$ and for all $N\geq 0$.}

In view of (
7
) we deduce

(11)

|v_{0}\cdots v_{N-1}|_{j}=\sum_{h=3}^{L}x_{h}T_{h-j}\qquad\text{for all $j=1,2,3$ and for all $N\geq 0$.}

To consider a general subword of length
N
in
\mathbf{v}_{3}
, we recall that
\mathbf{v}_{3}
is a
2
-balanced word
[
RSZ10
]
, namely
if
w
and
v
are two subwords of
\mathbf{v}_{3}
of the same length then

||w|_{j}-|v|_{j}|\leq 2\qquad\text{for all $j=1,2,3$}.

Comparing the weights of a general subword of length
N
with
v_{0}\cdots v_{N-1}
, (
9
) readily follows by (
7
). Finally if
N=T_{L}
for some
L\geq 3
, then its Tribonacci expansion is
x_{3}=\cdots=x_{L-1}=0
and
x_{L}=1
: we get (
10
) as a particular case of (
9
).
∎

Example 4
.

We apply the above result in the case
N=10
. We have that

N=T_{6}+T_{4}+T_{3}
,
therefore
L(N)=6
and the Tribonacci expansion of
N

is
x_{3}x_{4}x_{5}x_{6}=1101
.
Therefore

\displaystyle|v_{0}v_{1}\cdots v_{9}|_{1}=x_{3}T_{2}+x_{4}T_{3}+x_{5}T_{4}+x_{6}T_{5}=1\cdot 1+1\cdot 1+0\cdot 2+1\cdot 4=6

\displaystyle|v_{0}v_{1}\cdots v_{9}|_{2}=x_{3}T_{1}+x_{4}T_{2}+x_{5}T_{3}+x_{6}T_{4}=1\cdot 0+1\cdot 1+0\cdot 1+1\cdot 2=3

\displaystyle|v_{0}v_{1}\cdots v_{9}|_{3}=x_{3}T_{0}+x_{4}T_{1}+x_{5}T_{2}+x_{6}T_{3}=1\cdot 0+0\cdot 0+0\cdot 1+1\cdot 1=1

This is in agreement with the fact that
v_{0}v_{1}\cdots v_{9}=1213121121
.

Moreover every subword
w
of length
10
of
\mathbf{v}_{2}
satisfies

\displaystyle 4\leq|w|_{1}\leq 8,\quad 1\leq|w|_{2}\leq 5,\quad|w|_{3}\leq 3.

4.2.
Some gap conditions for
m=2,3
.

The next propositions provide estimates for
\gamma_{m,N}
using the digit distribution of
\mathbf{v}_{m}
when
m=2,3
, respectively.

Proposition 7

(Gap conditions in the Fibonacci case)
.

For all
N\in\mathbb{N}
we have the gap condition

(12)

\lambda_{2,N+k}-\lambda_{2,k}\geq N\gamma_{2,N}

where

\gamma_{2,N}:=\frac{1}{\varphi^{2}}+\frac{1}{\varphi^{4}}-\frac{1}{N\varphi^{3}}.

The above estimate is improved if we consider instead

\hat{\gamma}_{2,N}:=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{N\varphi^{2}}&\text{if $N=1$}\\
\\
\frac{1}{N}&\text{if $N=2$}\\
\ \dfrac{1}{\varphi^{2}}+\dfrac{F_{2h}}{\varphi^{3}F_{2h+1}}\quad&\text{if $N=F_{2h+1}$ with $h\geq 1$}\\
\\
\dfrac{1}{\varphi^{2}}+\dfrac{F_{2h-1}-1}{\varphi^{3}F_{2h}}\quad&\text{if $N=F_{2h}$ with $h\geq 2$}\\
\frac{1}{\varphi^{2}}+\frac{1}{\varphi^{4}}-\dfrac{1}{N\varphi^{3}}\quad&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Proof.

Consider the subword
w:=v_{k+1}\cdots v_{k+N}
. Using (
6
) we obtain that

\lambda_{2,N+k}-\lambda_{2,k}=\frac{|w|_{1}}{\varphi}+\frac{|w|_{2}}{\varphi^{2}}=\frac{N-|w|_{2}}{\varphi}+\frac{|w|_{2}}{\varphi^{2}}=\frac{N}{\varphi}\left(1-\frac{|w|_{2}}{N\varphi^{2}}\right).

Then the first part of the statement follows by Proposition
4
, because using the relation
\varphi=\varphi^{2}-1
we have

\lambda_{2,N+k}-\lambda_{2,k}\geq N\left(\frac{\varphi^{4}-1}{\varphi^{5}}-\frac{1}{N\varphi^{3}}\right)=N\left(\frac{\varphi^{2}+1}{\varphi^{4}}-\frac{1}{N\varphi^{3}}\right).

Concerning the second part of the proof note that in the case
N=1
we have

\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{k}\geq\frac{1}{\varphi^{2}}.

Moreover, no consecutive
2
’s can occur in the Fibonacci word, hence the smallest sum of two consecutive gaps is
1/\varphi^{2}+1/\varphi=1
. Then
\lambda_{k+2}-\lambda_{k}\geq 1
for all
k
.
Proposition
5
(and a few computations) yields the case in which
N
is a Fibonacci number.
It is left to show that the second estimate is actually an improvement. The case
N=1,2
is immediate, so let
N>2
be a Fibonacci number and recall that, as a consequence of Binet’s formula, we have
F_{n-1}-F_{n}/\varphi=(-\varphi)^{-n}
for all
n
. This implies

\frac{F_{2h-1}}{F_{2h}}\geq\frac{1}{\varphi}-\frac{1}{F_{2h+1}};\qquad\frac{F_{2h}}{F_{2h+1}}\geq\frac{1}{\varphi}\qquad\forall h,

So if
N=F_{2h+1}
we readily have

\gamma_{2,N}=\frac{\varphi^{2}+1}{\varphi^{4}}-\frac{1}{F_{2h+1}\varphi^{3}}=\frac{1}{\varphi^{2}}+\frac{1}{\varphi^{3}}\left(\frac{1}{\varphi}-\frac{1}{F_{2h+1}}\right)\leq\hat{\gamma}_{2,N}.

The case
N=F_{2h}
is similar.
∎

Proposition 8

(Gap conditions in the Tribonacci case)
.

Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
and let
x_{3}\cdots x_{L}
, with
L=L(N)
, be its Tribonacci expansion. Then we have the gap condition

(13)

\lambda_{3,N+k}-\lambda_{3,k}\geq N\gamma_{3,N}\quad\text{for all}k\in\mathbb{Z},

where

\gamma_{3,N}:=\frac{1}{N}\left(\sum_{h=3}^{L}x_{h}\left(\frac{T_{h-1}}{\tau}+\frac{T_{h-2}}{\tau^{2}}+\frac{T_{h-3}}{\tau^{3}}\right)-2\right).

where
\tau=\rho_{3}
satisfies
\tau^{3}=\tau^{2}+\tau+1
.
In particular, if
N=T_{L}
for some
L
, then

\gamma_{3,T_{L}}=\frac{T_{L-1}}{\tau T_{L}}+\frac{T_{L-2}}{\tau^{2}T_{L}}+\frac{T_{L-3}}{\tau^{3}T_{L}}-\frac{2}{T_{L}}.

Proof.

Immediate from (
6
) and Proposition
6
.
∎

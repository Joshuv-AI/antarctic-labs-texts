Clinicopathological significance of 5'-3' exoribonuclease 2 expression in small cell lung cancer tissues
Authors: Rui Lin, Feng Chen, Wei Zhang, Zhi-Su Liu, Guo-Sheng Li, Xiao-Song Chen, Han He, Dong-Ming Li, Rong-Quan He, Gang Chen, Wan-Ying Huang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractSmall cell lung cancer (SCLC) is an aggressive neuroendocrine malignancy. Overall survival in SCLC patients remains poor, and there is a lack of treatment options after the failure of first-line therapy. 5'-3' exoribonuclease 2 (XRN2) is associated with tumor progression. However, its mechanism in SCLC remains unclear. In this study, we collected and analyzed the expression data for XRN2 mRNA in SCLC and non-cancer lung tissues, as well as pan-cancer and non-cancer tissues from high-throughput datasets and identified XRN2 expression was significantly correlated with the prognosis of SCLC patients. Gene Ontology analysis indicated that XRN2 may promote tumor cell growth and development through biological processes. XRN2 performed better at distinguishing between the control group and samples of many tumors. Based on the TIMER and ESTIMATE algorithm analyses, this study revealed that XRN2 may act as a stimulator of immune responses in many tumors.Together,XRN2 overexpression may be associated with SCLC cell proliferation, migration, and invasion. High XRN2 expression is related to poor prognosis in SCLC and several other cancer types. XRN2 may also stimulate immune responses in many tumors.Keywords: XRN2, Small cell lung cancer, Pan-cancer, Target gene, Prognosis, Immune microenvironment

Introduction

Primary lung cancer is the leading cause of cancer death worldwide [1]. Small cell lung cancer (SCLC) accounts for approximately 12-15% of lung cancers and is an aggressive neuroendocrine malignancy [[2], [3], [4]]. The treatment of SCLC has not been influenced by precision oncology due to the lack of actionable genetic alterations that drive oncogenes. In addition, overall survival (OS) in SCLC patients is poor, and there is a lack of treatment options after the failure of first-line therapy [[5], [6], [7]]. The 5-year survival rate of SCLC patients is <7% [8]. Thus, it is important to explore the progression mechanism and immune mechanism in SCLC to identify effective treatment options.

In recent years, research on the treatment of various tumors has been diverse in terms of molecules [[9], [10], [11]], organelles, and reagents. However, small cell lung cancer has a high degree of malignancy and poor prognosis, and still lacks clear therapeutic targets and prognostic markers. 5'-3' exoribonuclease 2 (XRN2) recognizes the 5' monophosphate of single-stranded RNA and degrades it into mononucleotides [12]. The role of XRN2 in RNA metabolism has been fully elucidated; however, its role in DNA repair and replication has not been fully elucidated [13]. Many studies have shown that XRN2 is associated with tumor progression, including the aggressiveness of glioblastoma multiforme (GBM) [14] and poor prognosis in oral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC) [15]. Therefore, exploring the expression level of XRN2 is beneficial for predicting tumor progression and patient prognosis. Studies have shown that XRN2 can positively regulate the highly expressed O-linked N-acetylglucosamine transferase in SCLC [16], but the underlying mechanism in SCLC has not been determined. Further exploration of the clinicopathological significance of XRN2 in SCLC and the biological pathways involved is highly important for improving SCLC treatment and patient survival rates. This study integrates high-throughput data and clinical sample analysis to investigate the expression of XRN2 in small cell lung cancer, and explores its function, prognostic value, and pan cancer immune characteristics through enrichment analysis, prognostic analysis, and immune infiltration analysis. Materials and methods

Collection of multi-center high-throughput samples and clinical information on XRN2 mRNA expression

Microarray and RNA sequencing datasets containing XRN2 mRNA expression data were collected by searching public databases, such as ArrayExpress, the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO), the Sequence Read Archive (SRA), Oncomine, The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA), and the Genotype-Tissue Expression (GTEx) Sequencing dataset. The inclusion criteria for the dataset were as follows: (1) the study subjects were humans, (2) the samples were SCLC tissues or cells, and (3) the mRNA expression data of the genes were included. The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) duplicate or incomplete sample data and (2) had fewer than three samples in combined SCLC group and control group. For the TCGA dataset, we also included the OS and survival status of the SCLC patients.

Data processing of multicenter high-throughput samples

Log2 transformation was performed on the mRNA expression data of XRN2. The datasets from the same platform (e.g., GPL570) were merged to reduce batch effects between different datasets. Batch effects were eliminated using the “SVA” package [17] during this process, and the combined high-throughput gene expression matrices were normalized using the “limma” [18] and “edgeR” [19] packages.

SCLC tissue chip and immunohistochemistry for detecting the expression level of the XRN2 protein in SCLC

21 tumor lung tissues and 13 adjacent normal lung tissues were collected from SCLC patients at the First Affiliated Hospital of Guangxi Medical University. All patients included in the study were not treated preoperatively and were diagnosed with SCLC postoperatively by two pathologists. For the immunohistochemical experiments, EDTA buffer was used to retrieve the antigen. The primary antibody against XRN2 was purchased from Abcam, UK, and diluted 1:100, and the tissue samples were incubated at 37 °C for 70 min. The secondary antibody was purchased from Beijing Zhongshan Golden Bridge Biotechnology Co., Ltd.; the samples were incubated at room temperature for 25 min. The DAB color developer was purchased from Beijing Zhongshan Golden Bridge Biotechnology Co., Ltd. And was developed for 5 min. The intensity of staining and the percentage of positive cells were scored by two pathologists. The staining intensity was scored as follows: no, light, moderate, or strong staining were scored as 0, 1, 2, or 3, respectively. The percent of positive cells was scored as follows: 0, 1, 2, 3, or 4, representing <5%, 5%–25%, 26%–50%, 51%–75%, and >75% positive cells, respectively. The staining intensity score and the percentage of positive cells were used to determine protein expression level of XRN2.

Exploration of the potential function and mechanism of XRN2 in SCLC

Genes with a standardized mean difference (SMD) > 0 were defined as highly expressed genes in SCLC. Genes that were positively correlated with XRN2 expression (Spearman's coefficient ≥0.3) in more than one-third (i.e., 5) of the datasets were identified as genes positively correlated with XRN2. To explore the potential molecular mechanism of XRN2 in SCLC, the “clusterProfiler” package was used for Gene Ontology (GO) functional annotation. The “clusterProfiler” package offers a gene classification method, namely groupGO, to classify genes based on their projection at a specific level of the GO corpus, and provides functions, enrichGO to calculate enrichment tests for GO terms based on hypergeometric distribution [20].

XRN2 expression, potential function, and mechanism in pan-cancer

Microarray and RNA sequencing data for XRN2 mRNA expression in 33 tumors from the above public databases were collected. The data processing of these multi-center high-throughput samples was the same as that described in Section 2.2. To further explore the potential molecular mechanism of XRN2 expression in pan-cancer further, the “clusterProfiler” package was used to explore the XRN2-related Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) signaling pathway.

The TIMER algorithm [21] and the ESTIMATE algorithm [22], which can be used to measure the level of immune cell infiltration, were used to investigate the relationship between XRN2 and the immune microenvironment in various tumors.

Statistical analyses

The Wilcoxon rank sum test and SMD were used to detect differences in gene expression between the SCLC and control groups, and between the pan-cancer and control groups. Egger's test was used to test for SMD publication bias. The area under the curve (AUC) of the receiver operating characteristic curve was used to evaluate of XRN2 to discriminate between SCLC and control samples, and to discriminate between pan-cancer and control samples. The Kaplan–Meier curve and log tests were used to explore the associations between the expression level of XRN2 and the prognosis of SCLC and pan-cancer patients.

All calculations and visualization steps were performed in R software (v4.1.0), except that the integrated receiver operating characteristic curve was drawn in Stata 15.0. A p-value of less than 0.05 or a 95% CI for SMD not containing 0 indicated that the results of the analysis were statistically significant.

Results

Expression of XRN2 in SCLC

Our analyses included 28 high-throughput datasets of samples worldwide, including 379 SCLC and 533 noncancerous lung tissue samples. After removing the batch effect, 13 platforms were obtained. Among the 13 platforms containing XRN2 mRNA expression data, the analysis results of 10 platforms, including “GPL11154,” “GPL13376,” and “GPL14550,” were statistically significant (p < 0.01; Fig. 2A). A random-effects model was further constructed to calculate the SMD, and the XRN2 mRNA expression levels in the 13 platform datasets were integrated and analyzed. The results showed that XRN2 mRNA expression was upregulated in the SCLC group compared to the control group (SMD = 1.04 [95% CI: 0.59–1.50]; Fig. 2B), and no significant publication bias was found for the SMD results (p = 0.788; Fig. 2C).

We used immunohistochemistry to verify the expression of XRN2 at the protein level in SCLC (Fig. 2D). The expression level of XRN2 protein in the SCLC group was higher than that in the control group (p < 0.05), which was consistent with the mRNA expression data. Under the microscope, almost no XRN2 antibody staining was observed in some control tissues (Fig. 3A–E). In contrast, XRN2-positive cells were clearly visible in the SCLC group (Fig. 3F–J).

Clinical value of XRN2 in SCLC

The differential expression of XRN2 suggested that XRN2 may play an important role in SCLC; thus, we investigated the clinical application value of XRN2 in SCLC. As shown in Fig. 3K, XRN2 expression was significantly correlated with the prognosis of SCLC patients, and the OS and disease-free interval of SCLC patients with high XRN2 expression were significantly lower than those with low XRN2 expression (p < 0.05), indicating that XRN2 may be a potentially important indicator of the prognosis of SCLC patients. In terms of distinguishing samples, XRN2 in the high-expression group had a significant ability to distinguish SCLC samples from control samples (sensitivity = 0.77 [95% CI: 0.67–0.85]; specificity = 0.85 [95% CI: 0.67–0.94]; AUC = 0.84 [95% CI: 0.81–0.87]; Fig. 3L).

Possible functions of XRN2 in SCLC

Given that the mechanism of action of XRN2 in SCLC is currently unclear, this study explored the potential function of XRN2 through GO analysis. SMD calculations, correlation analyses, and integration of the screened highly expressed genes resulted in the identification of 22 XRN2-related genes. GO analysis indicated that the proteins encoded by these genes may be involved in the formation of cellular components such as chromosomes, splicing complexes, and microtubules; in biological processes such as telomere repair, positive regulation of the DNA metabolism process and regulation of the DNA metabolism process; in microbiological processes; and in molecular functions such as tubulin binding, DNA catalytic activity, and DNA helicase activity (Fig. 4A). This finding suggested that the function of XRN2 in SCLC may be related to these processes. As shown in Fig. 4B, these genes are also closely associated with nucleocytoplasmic transport, base excision repair, DNA replication, and the spliceosome. The Chronos score represented the importance of XRN2 in various cancer, and a value of <0 indicated that decreased expression of XRN2 increased cancer cell death. According to Fig. 4C, the Chronos scores of XRN2 in the majority of 27 cancer cell lines were less than 0, indicating that XRN2 was essential for tumor cells in some organs, such as bladder cancer, bone cancer, and lung cancer.

The expression and clinical value of XRN2 in pan-cancer

A total of 10,085 high-throughput datasets were included in this study; these included 7283 cancer tissues and 722 non-cancer tissues. The results of an integrated analysis of XRN2 mRNA expression levels in these datasets showed that, compared with that in the control group, the expression of XRN2 was upregulated in eight tumor types, namely, cholangiocarcinoma (CHOL), colon cancer (COAD), and glioblastoma multiforme (GBM), but not in lung adenocarcinoma or lung squamous cell carcinoma (p < 0.05; Fig. 5A). In terms of prognosis, patients with high XRN2 expression in kidney chromophobe (KICH), brain low-grade glioma (LGG), pheochromocytoma, or paraganglioma (PCPG) tumors had significantly shorter OS, disease-free interval, disease-specific survival, and progression-free interval than did those with low expression (p < 0.05; Fig. 5B–E, Fig. 6). These findings suggest that XRN2 may be an important prognostic indicator in some SCLC patients. As shown in Fig. 7, XRN2 in the high-expression group demonstrated a significant ability to distinguish tumor samples from control samples (sensitivity = 0.55 [95% CI: 0.41–0.67], specificity = 0.93 [95% CI: 0.88–0.96], AUC = 0.90 [95% CI: 0.87–0.92]).

Correlation analysis via KEGG revealed that XRN2 was associated with olfactory transduction, neuroactive ligand-receptor interaction, cytochrome p450 metabolism of exogenous substances, and late-adolescent diabetes in a variety of tumors in the pan-cancer dataset (Fig. 8). These findings suggest that XRN2 plays a role in the cell cycle in tumor cells and in the biological function of DNA in many tumor types.

Association of XRN2 with the pan-cancer immune microenvironment

To elucidate the specific role of XRN2 in tumors, we analyzed the expression levels of XRN2 and immune cell infiltration in a pan-cancer dataset. As shown in Fig. 9A, XRN2 was better at distinguishing between control samples and CHOL, COAD, esophageal cancer (ESCA), GBM, rectal adenocarcinoma (READ), and stomach adenocarcinoma (STAD) samples, with an AUC above 0.8. The results showed that in thyroid cancer (THCA), pancreatic cancer (PAAD), and prostate adenocarcinoma (PRAD) patients, a greater level of immune cell infiltration was detected in the XRN2 high expression group (p < 0.05, Fig. 9B). These cells included CD4 T cells, CD8 T cells, neutrophils, and dendritic cells, suggesting that XRN2 may act as a stimulator of immune responses. However, among testicular germ cell tumor (TGCT), sarcoma (SARC), and diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBC) patients, all had higher stromal scores, immune scores, and ESTIMATE scores (Fig. 9C), which suggested that the expression level of XRN2 is related to the immune infiltration of tumors.

Discussion

On the basis of microarray and RNA-seq data, this study revealed that the XRN2 mRNA level was significantly greater in SCLC than in normal controls and was also a risk factor for poor prognosis. We used SCLC tissue microarray immunohistochemistry to verify that the protein level of XRN2 was also upregulated in the samples. Our findings suggest that the cancer-promoting effect of XRN2 in SCLC may be realized through biological processes, such as telomere repair and DNA metabolism. Based on pan-cancer gene chip and RNA-seq data, we found that XRN2 was significantly highly expressed in eight tumor types, namely, CHOL, COAD, and GBM, and that high expression of XRN2 was related to poor prognosis in KICH, LGG, PCPG, and other tumors patients. In the immune microenvironment,the upregulation of XRN2 may affect the immune status of tumors, such as THCA, PAAD, and PRAD.

SCLC is a phenotypically heterogeneous neuroendocrine carcinoma with a high proliferation rate, early distant metastasis, and poor prognosis [23,24]. At present, no reliable drug treatment target for SCLC has been found; thus, chemotherapy and radiotherapy are the main methods used for treating SCLC [25,26]. In-depth research on the pathogenesis and progression mechanism of SCLC is needed to identify effective treatment options and to improve patient prognosis. Previous studies have demonstrated the high expression of XRN2 in GBM through public datasets and tissue microarray immunohistochemistry, and live cell imaging technology and inverted vertical infiltration assays have been used to show that XRN2 is associated with the movement and invasion of glioma cells [14]. Other studies have demonstrated by immunohistochemistry that XRN2 is highly expressed in OSCC, and in vitro experiments have demonstrated that downregulation of XRN2 inhibits the proliferation, migration, invasion, and epithelial-mesenchymal transition of OSCC [15]. However, only one study on SCLC has shown that XRN2 positively regulates the expression of O-linked N-acetylglucosamine transferase, although the expression level, potential mechanism, and clinical value of XRN2 in SCLC have not been studied. To explore the expression and functional mechanism of XRN2 in SCLC, this study comprehensively summarized the expression data of XRN2 mRNA by integrating and analyzing global high-throughput data. Our analyses revealed an obvious upregulation of expression, and these results were verified from the protein level by immunohistochemistry. We also demonstrated that elevated XRN2 expression is a risk factor for poor prognosis in SCLC patients. Therefore, highly expressed SCLC may play an important role in promoting the generation and progression of SCLC.

The mechanism through which XRN2 promotes tumorigenesis and development in SCLC may, in part, involve related signaling pathways. We collected XRN2-related genes for gene enrichment and signaling pathway analysis, and found associations with several biological processes, such as nucleocytoplasmic transport, base excision repair, and DNA replication, which may play important roles in the occurrence and development of SCLC. This finding is consistent with previous studies [27] and suggests that XRN2 may play a role in promoting cancer by participating in these signaling pathways.

We also used public databases to further evaluate the expression of XRN2 in pan-cancer. Among the 10,085 high-throughput datasets included in the study, eight tumors, including CHOL, COAD, and GBM, but not lung adenocarcinoma or lung squamous cell carcinoma, had high expression levels of XRN2. In KICH, LGG, PCPG, and other tumors, high XRN2 expression was also associated with poor patient prognosis. In CHOL, COAD, ESCA, GBM, READ, and STAD, XRN2 exhibited significant for distinguishing tumors from non-tumor tissues. These findings suggested that the expression level of XRN2 may be an important marker for the clinical judgment of benign and malignant tumors.

Although significant progress has been made in immunotherapy in recent years, the clinical effects of cancer treatment are still limited. In addition to the poor immune response rate of patients, which affects treatment efficacy, several common immune-related side effects seriously hinder the safety of immunotherapy [28]. Exploring the molecular mechanisms that affect the tumor immune microenvironment can lead to new ideas for immunotherapy to address immune resistance. According to the TIMER algorithm analysis of THCA, PAAD, and PRAD, the group with high XRN2 expression exhibited high infiltration of immune cells, including CD4 T cells, CD8 T cells, neutrophils, and dendritic cells. Using the ESTIMATE algorithm, we found that TGCT, SARC, and DLBC had high scores. This finding suggested that XRN2 may act as a stimulator of immune responses in many tumors.

In this study, all conclusions are based on high-throughput datasets analysis and clinical sample validation, and further in vitro and in vivo functional experiments are needed to verify the mechanism of XRN2 in small cell lung cancer. Meanwhile, genome-wide correlation ranking of XRN2 compared with all protein-coding genes (especially genes involved in transcription and pre-mRNA processing) was not performed in this study, which will be further explored and supplemented in our future research to clarify the relative importance of XRN2 in tumor development and progression.

Conclusion

The upregulation of XRN2 expression may promote the occurrence and malignant process of SCLC through pathways related to nucleocytoplasmic transport, base excision repair, and DNA replication. High expression of XRN2 in a variety of cancer types is associated with a poor patient prognosis. XRN2 may also stimulate of the immune response in pan-cancer. However, further investigations with in-depth in vitro and in vivo investigations are needed to validate these conclusions.

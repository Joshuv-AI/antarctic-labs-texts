Abstract




Abstract.
This paper is devoted to the general solvability of anticipated backward stochastic differential equations with quadratic growth by relaxing the assumptions made by Hu, Li, and Wen
[
21
, Journal of Differential Equations, 270 (2021), 1298–1311]
from the one-dimensional case with bounded terminal values to the multi-dimensional situation with bounded/unbounded terminal values. Three new results regarding the existence and uniqueness of local and global solutions are established. More precisely,
for the local solution with bounded terminal values, the generator
f(t,Y_{t},Z_{t},Y_{t+\delta_{t}},Z_{t+\zeta_{t}})
is of general growth with respect to
Y_{t}
and
Y_{t+\delta_{t}}
.
For the global solution with bounded terminal values, the generator
f(t,Y_{t},Z_{t},Y_{t+\delta_{t}},Z_{t+\zeta_{t}})
is of skew sub-quadratic but also “strictly and diagonally” quadratic growth in
Z_{t}
.
For the global solution with unbounded terminal values, the generator
f(t,Y_{t},Z_{t},Y_{t+\delta_{t}})
is of diagonal quadratic growth in
Z_{t}
in the first case; and in the second case, the generator
f(t,Z_{t})+\mathbb{E}[g(t,Y_{t},Z_{t},Y_{t+\delta_{t}},Z_{t+\zeta_{t}})]
is of diagonal quadratic growth in
Z_{t}
and linear growth in
Z_{t+\zeta_{t}}
.

Key words:

Anticipated backward stochastic differential equation;
quadratic generator;
multi-dimension;
unbounded terminal value

AMS subject classifications.
60H10, 60H30.

1
Introduction

Let
\{W_{t};t\geqslant 0\}
be a
d
-dimensional standard Brownian motion defined on some complete probability space
(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\mathbb{P})
and
\mathbb{F}\triangleq\{\mathscr{F}_{t};t\geqslant 0\}
be the natural filtration of
{W}
augmented by all the
\mathbb{P}
-null sets in
\mathscr{F}
.
Fix a terminal time
T\in(0,+\infty)
, a constant
K\in[0,+\infty)
, and two positive integers
n
and
d
.
Consider the following anticipated backward stochastic differential equations (ABSDEs, for short) over a finite time horizon
[0,T+K]
:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &Y_{t}=\xi_{T}+\int_{t}^{T}f(s,Y_{s},Z_{s},Y_{s+\delta_{s}},Z_{s+\zeta_{s}})ds-\int_{t}^{T}Z_{s}dW_{s},\quad t\in[0,T];\\
&Y_{t}=\xi_{t},\quad Z_{t}=\eta_{t},\quad t\in[T,T+K],\end{aligned}\right.

(1.1)

where
\delta
and
\zeta
are two deterministic
\mathbb{R}^{+}
-valued continuous functions defined on
[0,T]
, and
\xi
and
\eta
are some given processes defined on
[T,T+K]
.
The process
\xi
is called the
terminal value
and the function
f
is called the
generator
of ABSDE (
1.1
). The unknown processes
(Y,Z)
, taking values in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
, are called a pair of
\mathbb{F}
-progressively measurable solutions if they make Eq. (
1.1
) hold almost surely.

In 2009, to deal with the delayed stochastic optimal control problem, Peng and Yang
[
38
]
introduced ABSDE (
1.1
) and established the existence and uniqueness under the condition that the generator is of uniformly Lipschitz and linear growth.
From then on, this theory has attracted a lot of attentions.
By regarding ABSDE as a new duality type of stochastic differential delay equations, Chen and Wu
[
6
]
derived the maximum principle for stochastic optimal control problem with delay. Along this line of research, Meng, Shi, Wang, and Zhang
[
32
]
obtained a general maximum principle for a stochastic optimal control problem with delay.
Hamaguchi
[
18
]
studied a maximum principle for optimal control problems of with delay.
Nie, Wang, and Wu
[
34
]
studied a type of linear-quadratic delayed mean-field social optimization, and
de Feo and Świech

[
11
]

studied a verification theorem and constructed the optimal feedback controls for the optimal control problems with the stochastic delay differential equations.
At the same time, the theory of ABSDE has also been well-developed. For example,
Wu, Wang, and Ren
[
42
]
, along with Lu and Ren
[
30
]
, proved the existence and uniqueness of ABSDEs with non-Lipschitz generators and linear growth, as well as ABSDEs driven by Markov chains, respectively.
Yang and Elliott
[
44
]
obtained some properties for generalized ABSDEs.
Hu and Chen
[
20
]
obtained the
L^{p}
solutions of ABSDEs under monotonicity and general increasing conditions.
Cheridito and Nam
[
10
]
studied a class of anticipated BSDEs when the generator is a path-functional of the solution under Lipschitz condition.
Wen and Shi
[
41
]
and Douissi, Wen, and Shi
[
12
]
proved the solvability of ABSDEs driven by a fractional Brownian motion.
Moreover, by using ABSDEs, Menoukeu-Pamen
[
33
]
studied a non-linear time-advanced backward stochastic partial differential equations with jumps.
Chen and Yang
[
46
]
obtained the well-posedness for anticipated backward stochastic Schrödinger equations.
Some other recent developments of ABSDEs can be found in
Chen, Wu and Yu
[
7
]
,
Guatteri and Masiero
[
17
]
,
Klimsiak and Rzymowski
[
28
]
,
Han and Li
[
19
]
, etc.

However, the framework of Peng and Yang
[
38
]

failed to address some general delayed stochastic optimal control problems, such as
the delayed stochastic linear-quadratic (LQ, for short) optimal control problems with random coefficients. The reason is that the related stochastic Riccati equations are essentially ABSDEs with quadratic growth (see Chen, Wu, and Yu
[
7
]
).
On one hand, the related problem of BSDEs with quadratic growth plays an intrinsic mathematical interest and Peng
[
37
, Section 5, page 270]
listed it as an open problem.
On the other hand, this topic has important applications in various fields, such as nonzero-sum risk-sensitive stochastic differential games,
financial price-impact models, financial market equilibrium problems for several interacting
agents, and stochastic equilibria problems in incomplete financial markets (see Hu, Liang, and Tang
[
23
,
24
]
).
Therefore, it is necessary and valuable to study ABSDEs with quadratic growth.
In general, in multi-dimensional situation,
ABSDE is called of quadratic growth if the generator
f
is of quadratic growth in
Z
for each
i=1,...,n
; and it is called of diagonally quadratic growth if
f^{i}
is quadratic growth in the
i
-th row of
Z
and grows at a rate strictly slower than quadratic in the
j
-th component of
Z
when
j\neq i
.
For ABSDE (
1.1
) with quadratic growth, Fujii and Takahashi
[
16
]
study one-dimensional case when the generator
f
is independent of the anticipated term
Z_{t+\zeta_{t}}
. Hu, Li, and Wen
[
21
]
studied the one-dimensional case with bounded terminal value. Based on the work of Hu, Li, and Wen
[
21
]
, Pak, Kim, and Kim
[
35
]
studied one-dimensional case with unbounded terminal value when the generator
f
is independent of the anticipated term
Z_{t+\zeta_{t}}
.

Unfortunately, due to the fact that the comparison property does not hold for the general ABSDE (
1.1
) (see the counter
Example 5.3 of Peng and Yang
[
38
]
), the topic of multi-dimensional ABSDEs, the focus of the present paper, poses a great challenge.
Next, in order to present our work in more detail, let’s recall some results about BSDEs.

In ABSDE (
1.1
), if the generator
f
is independent of the anticipated term
Y_{t+\delta_{t}}
and
Z_{t+\zeta_{t}}
, then it becomes the following classical BSDE:

Y_{t}=\xi_{T}+\int_{t}^{T}f(s,Y_{s},Z_{s})ds-\int_{t}^{T}Z_{s}dW_{s},\quad t\in[0,T].

For the classical BSDE, Pardoux and Peng
[
36
]
firstly solved the nonlinear situation when the generator has uniformly Lipschitz and linear growth in 1990. Since then, BSDEs have attracted significant attention due to their wide-ranging applications in fields such as stochastic control, partial differential equations, and mathematical finance (see Yong and Zhou
[
45
]
and Zhang
[
47
]
).
Meanwhile, many efforts have been made to relax the assumptions on the generator for the existence and/or uniqueness of adapted solutions.
For example, Kobylanski
[
29
]
established the existence and uniqueness for one-dimensional BSDEs with quadratic growth and bounded terminal value.
Tevzadze
[
40
]
applied fixed point principle to give a simple proof for the existence of solution.
Briand and Hu
[
4
,
5
]
proved the existence and uniqueness of BSDE with quadratic growth and unbounded terminal value.
Note that, compared with one-dimensional BSDEs, some important tools, such as Gisanov’s transform and monotone convergence, fail to work with multi-dimentional BSDEs.
So for the multi-dimensional BSDEs with quadratic growth, Frei and Dos Reis
[
15
]
provided a counterexample demonstrating that multidimensional quadratic BSDEs with simple generator and terminal value assumptions may lack global solutions on
[0,T]
.
Hu and Tang
[
22
]
studied multi-dimensional BSDEs with diagonally quadratic generators and bounded terminal value, and Fan, Hu, and Tang
[
14
]
extended the previous works by relaxing conditions and providing a novel method for handling unbounded terminal values.
Some other recent developments of quadratic BSDEs can be found in Bahlali, Eddahbi and Ouknine
[
1
]
, Barrieu and El Karoui
[
3
]
, Cheridito and Nam
[
8
,
9
]
, Richou
[
39
]
,
Xing and Zitkovic
[
43
]
, Madoui, Eddahbi, and Khelfallah
[
31
]
, Jiang, Li, and Wei
[
26
]
, and the references cited therein.

In this work, we study the multi-dimensional ABSDEs with diagonally quadratic growth. By introducing new methods, three new results regarding the existence and uniqueness of local and global solutions are established.
First, the existence and uniqueness of local solutions with bounded terminal value is proved by virtue of the fixed-point theorem (see
Theorem 3.3
), which generalizes both the multi-dimensional result of Fan, Hu, and Tang
[
14
, Theorem 2.1]
and the one-dimensional result of Hu, Li, and Wen
[
21
, Theorem 4.4]
.
Second, by splicing local solutions, two existence and uniqueness results of global solutions with bounded terminal value are established under different conditions: (i) the
i
-th component of generator
f
is bounded with respect to
Z_{t+\zeta_{t}}
and the
j
-th
(j\neq i)
row of the matrix
Z_{t}
(see
Theorem 4.2
), which generalizes Hu, Li, and Wen
[
21
, Theorem 4.8]
; (ii) the
i
-th component of the generator
f
is of linear growth with respect to
Z_{t+\zeta_{t}}
and the
j
-th
(j\neq i)
row of the matrix
Z_{t}
(see
Theorem 4.3
), where we use a new method concerning John-Nirenberg inequality (different from the ones used in Fan, Hu, and Tang
[
14
, Theorem 2.5]
) to tackle the difficulties.
Third, by using an iterative method, a priori estimate, and
\theta
-method, the existence and uniqueness of global solutions with unbounded terminal value are proved when the generator
f
is independent of the anticipated term
Z_{t+\zeta_{t}}
(see
Theorem 5.1
), which extends Fan, Hu, and Tang
[
14
, Theorem 2.8]
.
Finally, when the generator appears as

f(t,Z_{t})+\mathbb{E}[g(t,Y_{t},Z_{t},Y_{t+\delta_{t}},Z_{t+\zeta_{t}})]
, where
f
is of quadratic growth in
Z_{t}
and
g
depends on the anticipated terms
Y_{t+\delta_{t}}
and
Z_{t+\zeta_{t}}
, we establish the existence and uniqueness of global solutions with unbounded terminal values (see
Theorem 5.2
).
This result relaxes the conditions imposed by Hu, Li, and Wen
[
21
, Theorem 5.4]
, where the generator is required to be bounded with respect to
Y_{t+\delta_{t}}
and
Z_{t+\zeta_{t}}
.
In contrast, our framework does not necessitate such boundedness, even in the one-dimensional case.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows.
Section 2
introduces key notations and propositions related to BMO martingales. In
Section 3
, we establish the existence and uniqueness of local solutions with bounded terminal values. Morevoer, based on
Section 3
, we study the solvability of global solutions with bounded terminal value in
Section 4
.
In
Section 5
, we prove the existence and uniqueness of global solutions with unbounded terminal value. Finally,
Section 6
provides proofs for auxiliary results concerning one-dimensional quadratic BSDEs, addressing both bounded and unbounded terminal value cases.

\{W_{t};t\geqslant 0\}
be a

-dimensional standard Brownian motion defined on some complete probability space
(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\mathbb{P})
and
\mathbb{F}\triangleq\{\mathscr{F}_{t};t\geqslant 0\}
be the natural filtration of
{W}
augmented by all the
\mathbb{P}
-null sets in
\mathscr{F}
.
The notation
\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}
denotes the space of
m\times d
-matrix
C
, equipped with the Euclidean norm defined as
|C|=\sqrt{\text{tr}(CC^{\top})}
, where
C^{\top}
is the transpose of
C
.
Let
a\wedge b
and
a\vee b
be the minimum and maximum of two real numbers
a
and
b
, respectively. Set
a^{+}\triangleq a\vee 0
and

. Denote by

by the indicator of set
A
, and
\mathop{\rm sgn}(x)\triangleq{\bf 1}_{\{x>0\}}-{\bf 1}_{\{x\leqslant 0\}}
. For any
t\in[0,T]
,
p\geqslant 1
, and Euclidean space
\mathbb{H}
, we introduce the following spaces:

\displaystyle L^{2}_{\mathscr{F}_{t}}(\Omega;\mathbb{H})

\displaystyle=\Big\{\xi:\Omega\to\mathbb{H}\Bigm|\xi~\hbox{is $\mathscr{F}_{t}$-measurable and~}\|\xi\|_{2}\triangleq(\mathbb{E}[\left|\xi\right|^{2}])^{\frac{1}{2}}\textless\infty\Big\},

\displaystyle L^{\infty}_{\mathscr{F}_{t}}(\Omega;\mathbb{H})

\displaystyle=\Big\{\xi:\Omega\to\mathbb{H}\Bigm|\xi~\hbox{is $\mathscr{F}_{t}$-measurable and~}\|\xi\|_{\infty}\triangleq\mathop{\mathop{\rm esssup}}\limits_{\omega\in\Omega}|\xi(\omega)|\textless\infty\Big\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{p}_{\mathbb{F}}(t,T;\mathbb{H})

\displaystyle=\Big\{Z:\Omega\times[t,T]\to\mathbb{H}\Bigm|Z~\hbox{is $\mathbb{F}$-progressively measurable and~}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\ \quad\quad\quad\|Z\|_{\mathcal{H}^{p}_{\mathbb{F}}(t,T)}\triangleq\Big(\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{t}^{T}|Z_{s}|^{2}ds\Big]^{\frac{p}{2}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty\Big\},

\displaystyle S^{p}_{\mathbb{F}}(t,T;\mathbb{H})

\displaystyle=\Big\{Y:\Omega\times[t,T]\to\mathbb{H}\Bigm|Y~\hbox{is $\mathbb{F}$-progressively measurable, continuous, and~}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\ \quad\quad\quad\|Y\|_{S^{p}_{\mathbb{F}}(t,T)}\triangleq\Big(\mathbb{E}[\mathop{\sup}\limits_{s\in[t,T]}|Y_{s}|^{p}]\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty\Big\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathbb{F}}(t,T;\mathbb{H})

\displaystyle=\Big\{Y:\Omega\times[t,T]\to\mathbb{H}\Bigm|Y~\hbox{is $\mathbb{F}$-progressively measurable, continuous, and~}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\ \quad\quad\quad\|Y\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathbb{F}}(t,T)}\triangleq\mathop{\mathop{\rm esssup}}\limits_{(s,\omega)\in[t,T]\times\Omega}|Y_{s}(w)|=\Big\|\mathop{\sup}\limits_{s\in[t,T]}|Y_{s}|\Big\|_{\infty}<\infty\Big\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(t,T;\mathbb{H})

\displaystyle=\Big\{Y:\Omega\times[t,T]\to\mathbb{H}\Bigm|\exp(|Y|)\in\mathop{\bigcap}\limits_{q\geqslant 1}S^{q}_{\mathbb{F}}(t,T;\mathbb{H})\Big\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(t,T;\mathbb{H})

\displaystyle=\Big\{Z:\Omega\times[t,T]\to\mathbb{H}\Bigm|Z\in\mathop{\bigcap}\limits_{q\geqslant 1}\mathcal{H}^{q}_{\mathbb{F}}(t,T;\mathbb{H})\Big\}.

2.1
BMO martingale

On one hand, we define the following space concerning
BMO
martingale: for any
t\in\mathscr{T}[0,T]
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}^{p}_{\mathbb{F}}(t,T;\mathbb{H})=\Big\{Z\in\mathcal{H}^{p}_{\mathbb{F}}(t,T;\mathbb{H})\Bigm|\|Z\|_{\mathcal{Z}^{p}_{\mathbb{F}}}\triangleq\mathop{\sup}\limits_{\tau\in\mathscr{T}[t,T]}\Big\|\mathbb{E}_{\tau}\Big[\Big(\int_{\tau}^{T}|Z_{s}|^{2}ds\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}\Big]\Big\|^{\frac{1}{p}}_{\infty}<\infty\Big\},

where
\mathscr{T}[t,T]
denotes the set of all
\mathbb{F}
-stopping times
\tau
with values in
[t,T]
, and
\mathbb{E}_{\tau}
is conditional expectation with respect to
\sigma
-field
\mathscr{F}_{\tau}
. On the other hand, we let
M=(M_{t},\mathscr{F}_{t})
be a uniformly integrable martingale with
M_{0}=0
and define

\|M\|_{{BMO_{p}}(\mathbb{P})}\triangleq\mathop{\sup}\limits_{\tau\in\mathscr{T}[0,T]}\Big\|\mathbb{E}_{\tau}[|M_{T}-M_{\tau}|^{p}]^{\frac{1}{p}}\Big\|_{\infty}.

In the sequel, the class
\{M:\|M\|_{{BMO_{p}}(\mathbb{P})}<\infty\}
is denoted by
{BMO_{p}}(\mathbb{P})
, which is a Banach space equipped with the norm
\|.\|_{BMO_{p}(\mathbb{P})}
. For simplicity, we denote
BMO_{p}^{[a,b]}(\mathbb{P})
the space over the time interval
[a,b]
and
BMO(\mathbb{P})
the space of
{BMO_{2}}(\mathbb{P})
.
Then, for
Z\in\mathcal{Z}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{H})
, the process
t\mapsto\int_{0}^{t}Z_{s}dW_{s}
(denoted by
Z\cdot W
) belongs to
BMO(\mathbb{P})
and

\|Z\|_{\mathcal{Z}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}}\equiv\|Z\cdot W\|_{BMO(\mathbb{P})}.

Denote by
\mathcal{E}(M)
the Doléans-Dade exponential of a continuous local martingale
M
, that is,
\mathcal{E}(M)_{t}\triangleq\exp\{M_{t}-M_{0}-\frac{1}{2}\langle M\rangle_{0}^{t}\}
.

Definition 2.1
.

A pair
(Y,Z)\in S^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T+K;\mathbb{R}^{n})\times\mathcal{H}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T+K;\mathbb{R}^{n\times d})
is called an adapted solution of BSDE (
1.1
), if
\mathbb{P}
-almost surely, it satisfies (
1.1
). Additionally, it is called a bounded adapted solution if
(Y,Z)\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T+K;\mathbb{R}^{n})\times\mathcal{Z}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T+K;\mathbb{R}^{n\times d})
.

Next, we present some existing results concerning
BMO
martingale, which can be found in Kazamaki
[
27
]
and will be used frequently in the sequel.

Proposition 2.2

(John-Nirenberg Inequality)
.

Let
M\in BMO(\mathbb{P})
with
\|M\|_{BMO(\mathbb{P})}<1
. Then for every stopping time
\tau\in\mathscr{T}[0,T]
, we have

\mathbb{E}_{\tau}[\exp\{\langle M\rangle_{T}-\langle M\rangle_{\tau}\}]\leqslant\big\{1-\|M\|_{BMO(\mathbb{P})}^{2}\big\}^{-1}.

Proposition 2.3
.

For every
p\in[1,\infty)
, there exists a positive constant
L_{p}
such that for all uniformly integrable martingale
M
with respect to
\tilde{\mathbb{P}}
, the following inequality holds true:

\|M\|_{BMO_{p}(\tilde{\mathbb{P}})}\leqslant L_{p}\|M\|_{BMO(\tilde{\mathbb{P}})},

where
d\tilde{\mathbb{P}}\triangleq\mathcal{E}(\Lambda\cdot W)_{T}d\mathbb{P}
with
\Lambda\in BMO(\mathbb{P})
.

Proposition 2.4
.

For
K>0
, there exist two positive constants
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
depending only on
K
such that for each
M\in BMO(\mathbb{P})
, we have

c_{1}\|M\|_{BMO(\mathbb{P})}\leqslant\|\tilde{M}\|_{BMO(\tilde{\mathbb{P}})}\leqslant c_{2}\|M\|_{BMO(\mathbb{P})},

where
\tilde{M}\triangleq M-\langle M,N\rangle
with
N\in BMO(\mathbb{P})
satisfying
\|N\|_{BMO(\mathbb{P})}\leqslant K
,
and
d\tilde{\mathbb{P}}\triangleq\mathcal{E}(N)_{T}d\mathbb{P}.

Finally, we present the following (conditional) Burkholder-Davis-Gundy’s inequality.

Proposition 2.5
.

For any
p>0
and
M\in\mathcal{Z}^{p}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{m})
, there exist constants
0<c_{p}<C_{p}
, depending only on
p
and
m
, such that for any
\tau\in\mathscr{T}[0,T]
,

\displaystyle c_{p}\mathbb{E}^{\tilde{\mathbb{P}}}_{\tau}\Big[\Big(\int_{\tau}^{T}|M_{s}|^{2}ds\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}\Big]\leqslant\mathbb{E}^{\tilde{\mathbb{P}}}_{\tau}\Big[\mathop{\sup}\limits_{t\in\mathscr{T}[\tau,T]}\Big|\int_{\tau}^{t}M_{s}d\tilde{W}_{s}\Big|^{p}\Big]\leqslant C_{p}\mathbb{E}^{\tilde{\mathbb{P}}}_{\tau}\Big[\Big(\int_{\tau}^{T}|M_{s}|^{2}ds\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}\Big],

where
\tilde{W}\triangleq W-\langle W,\Lambda\cdot W\rangle
with
\Lambda\in BMO(\mathbb{P})

and
d\tilde{\mathbb{P}}\triangleq\mathcal{E}(\Lambda\cdot W)_{T}d\mathbb{P}
.

Proof.

By a similar argument as in the proof of Zhang
[
47
, Theorem 2.4.1]
, we deduce that for any
A\in\mathscr{F}_{\tau}
,

\displaystyle c_{p}\mathbb{E}^{\tilde{\mathbb{P}}}\Big[\Big(\int_{\tau}^{T}|M_{s}{\bf 1}_{A}|^{2}ds\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}\Big]\leqslant\mathbb{E}^{\tilde{\mathbb{P}}}\Big[\mathop{\sup}\limits_{t\in\mathscr{T}[\tau,T]}\Big|\int_{\tau}^{t}M_{s}{\bf 1}_{A}d\tilde{W}_{s}\Big|^{p}\Big]\leqslant C_{p}\mathbb{E}^{\tilde{\mathbb{P}}}\Big[\Big(\int_{\tau}^{T}|M_{s}{\bf 1}_{A}|^{2}ds\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}\Big].

Finally, by the definition of conditional expectation, we get the desired result immediately.

2.2
One-dimensional quadratic BSDEs

The aim of this subsection is to present some preliminary results. In the following, we always let
\{\theta_{t};t\in[0,T]\}

\mathbb{F}
-progressively measurable scalar-valued non-negative process, let
\lambda,\lambda_{0},\sigma,\sigma_{0}
, and
\gamma
be some positive constants, and
\alpha\in[0,1)
, and let
\rho(\cdot),\rho_{0}(\cdot):[0,+\infty)\rightarrow[0,+\infty)

Moreover, we always assume that the deterministic continuous functions

\rm(i)

There exists a positive constant

\displaystyle t+\delta_{t}\leqslant T+K\quad\hbox{and}\quad t+\zeta_{t}\leqslant T+K,\quad\forall t\in[0,T].

(2.1)

\rm(ii)

There exists a positive constant
L
such that for all nonnegative and integrable function
h
,

\displaystyle\int_{t}^{T}h_{s+\delta_{s}}ds\leqslant L\int_{t}^{T+K}h_{s}ds\quad\hbox{and}\quad\int_{t}^{T}h_{s+\zeta_{s}}ds\leqslant L\int_{t}^{T+K}h_{s}ds,\quad\forall t\in[0,T].

(2.2)

To establish the solvability of the multi-dimensional BSDE (
1.1
) in the following sections, we first present some preliminary results concerning one-dimensional BSDEs with quadratic growth in this subsection. The proofs of these results are all included in the appendix.
For this purpose, for
\xi\in L_{\mathscr{F}_{T}}^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})
,
f(\omega,t,z):\Omega\times[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and

is
\mathbb{F}
-progressively measurable, we consider the following equation

\displaystyle Y_{t}=\xi+\int_{t}^{T}f(s,Z_{s})ds-\int_{t}^{T}Z_{s}dW_{s},\quad t\in[0,T].

(2.3)

Lemma 2.6
.

For any given pair
(U,V)\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T+K;\mathbb{R}^{n})\times\mathcal{Z}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T+K;\mathbb{R}^{n\times d})
, and for any
t\in[0,T]
and
z,\bar{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},
if the generator
f

Theorem 3.3

The proof will be divided into three steps.
In step one, we will construct a mapping in a suitable Banach space.
In step two, we will show that the constructed mapping is stable in a small ball.
In step three, we will prove that this mapping is a contraction.

Step 1: Construction of the mapping
\Pi

For any given

\left\{\begin{aligned} &Y_{t}^{i}=\xi_{T}^{i}+\int_{t}^{T}f^{i}(s,U_{s},V_{s}(Z_{s}^{i};i),U_{s+\delta_{s}},V_{s+\zeta_{s}})ds-\int_{t}^{T}Z^{i}_{s}dW_{s},\quad t\in[0,T];\\
&Y^{i}_{t}=\xi^{i}_{t},\quad Z^{i}_{t}=\eta^{i}_{t},\quad t\in[T,T+K],\end{aligned}\right.

For simplicity of presentation, we denote

\displaystyle f^{i,U,V}(t,z)=f^{i}(t,U_{t},V_{t}(z;i),U_{t+\delta_{t}},V_{t+\zeta_{t}}),\quad\quad(t,z)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}.

\displaystyle\|Z\|^{2}_{\mathcal{Z}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T+K;\mathbb{R}^{n\times d})}=\mathop{\sup}\limits_{\tau\in\mathscr{T}[0,T+K]}\Big\|\mathbb{E}_{\tau}\Big[\int_{\tau}^{T+K}|Z_{s}|^{2}ds\Big]\Big\|_{\infty}

5

In this section, we study global solutions of quadratic ABSDEs with unbounded terminal values. Let us consider the following type of BSDEs:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &Y_{t}=\xi_{T}+\int_{t}^{T}f(s,Y_{s},Z_{s},Y_{s+\delta_{s}})dt-\int_{t}^{T}Z_{s}dW_{s},\quad\quad t\in[0,T];\\

Assumption 4

For
i=1,...,n

d\mathbb{P}\times dt

The following theorem is the main result of this section, which implies the existence and uniqueness of global solutions of BSDE (

Theorem 5.1
.

Under

5.1
) possesses a unique global solution

Proof.

For simplicity of presentation, we would like to divide the proof into six steps.
First, we shall define a sequence of processes
\{(Y^{m},Z^{m})\}_{m=0}^{\infty}
in the space
\mathcal{E}(0,T+K;\mathbb{R}^{n})\times\mathcal{M}(0,T+K;\mathbb{R}^{n\times d})

Step 1: Define a sequence of processes recursively

For any given pair
(U,V)\in\mathcal{E}(0,T+K;\mathbb{R}^{n})\times\mathcal{M}(0,T+K;\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}),
we consider the following system of decoupled BSDEs: for each

\left\{\begin{aligned} &Y_{t}^{i}=\xi^{i}_{T}+\int_{t}^{T}f^{i}(s,U_{s},Z^{i}_{s},U_{s+\delta_{s}})dt-\int_{t}^{T}Z^{i}_{s}dW_{s},\quad t\in[0,T];\\
&Y^{i}_{t}=\xi^{i}_{t},\quad Z^{i}_{t}=\eta^{i}_{t},\quad t\in[T,T+K].\end{aligned}\right.

(5.2)

In view of item (i) of
4
, for any
s\in[t,T]
and
z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

|f^{i}(s,U_{s},z,U_{s+\delta_{s}})|\leqslant\Gamma_{s}+C\{|U_{s}|+\mathbb{E}_{t}[|U_{s+\delta_{s}}|]\}+\frac{\gamma}{2}|z|^{2}.

By applying Hölder’s inequality, we have that for any

\displaystyle\ \mathbb{E}\exp\bigg\{q|\xi_{T}^{i}|+q\int_{0}^{T}\big(\Gamma_{s}+C|U_{s}|+C\mathbb{E}_{s}[|U_{s+\delta_{s}}|]\big)ds\bigg\}

\displaystyle\leqslant\Big(\mathbb{E}\exp\big\{2q\xi^{*}\big\}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigg(\mathbb{E}\exp\bigg\{2q\int_{0}^{T}\big(\Gamma_{s}+C|U_{s}|+C\mathbb{E}_{s}[|U_{s+\delta_{s}}|]\big)ds\bigg\}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\Big(\mathbb{E}\exp\big\{2q\xi^{*}\big\}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigg(\mathbb{E}\exp\bigg\{4q\int_{0}^{T}\Gamma_{s}ds\bigg\}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{4}}\bigg(\mathbb{E}\exp\bigg\{4q\int_{0}^{T}\big(C|U_{s}|+C\mathbb{E}_{s}[|U_{s+\delta_{s}}|]\big)ds\bigg\}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{4}},

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\exp\bigg\{q|\xi_{T}^{i}|+q\int_{0}^{T}\big(\Gamma_{s}+C|U_{s}|+C\mathbb{E}_{s}[|U_{s+\delta_{s}}|]\big)ds\bigg\}<+\infty.

\displaystyle\ \mathbb{E}\Big[\exp\Big\{q\gamma\mathop{\sup}\limits_{s\in[t,T]}|Y_{s}^{m+1}|\Big\}\Big]=\mathbb{E}\Big[\mathop{\sup}\limits_{s\in[t,T]}\exp\Big\{q\gamma|Y_{s}^{m+1}|\Big\}\Big]

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathbb{E}\bigg\{\mathop{\sup}\limits_{s\in[t,T]}\bigg(\mathbb{E}_{s}\exp\Big\{n\gamma|\xi_{T}|+n\gamma\int_{t}^{T}\big(\Gamma_{r}+C|Y^{m}_{r}|+C\mathbb{E}_{r}[|Y^{m}_{r+\delta_{r}}|]\big)dr\Big\}\bigg)^{q}\bigg\}

\displaystyle\leqslant\Big(\frac{q}{q-1}\Big)^{q}\mathbb{E}\exp\Big\{nq\gamma|\xi_{T}|+nq\gamma\int_{t}^{T}\big(\Gamma_{r}+C|Y^{m}_{r}|+C\mathbb{E}_{r}[|Y^{m}_{r+\delta_{r}}|]\big)dr\Big\}

\displaystyle\leqslant\Big(\frac{q}{q-1}\Big)^{q}\bigg(\mathbb{E}\exp\Big\{2nq\gamma|\xi_{T}|+2nq\gamma\int_{0}^{T}\Gamma_{s}ds\Big\}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\ \cdot\bigg(\mathbb{E}\exp\Big\{2nq\gamma C\int_{t}^{T}|Y_{s}^{m}|ds+2nq\gamma C\int_{t}^{T}\mathbb{E}_{s}[|Y^{m}_{s+\delta_{s}}|]ds\Big\}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\Big(\frac{q}{q-1}\Big)^{q}\bigg(\mathbb{E}\exp\Big\{2nq\gamma|\xi_{T}|+2nq\gamma\int_{0}^{T}\Gamma_{s}ds\Big\}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\ \cdot\bigg(\mathbb{E}\Big[\exp\Big\{4nq\gamma C\int_{t}^{T}|Y_{s}^{m}|ds\Big\}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{4}}\bigg(\mathbb{E}\exp\Big\{4nq\gamma C\int_{t}^{T}\mathbb{E}_{s}[|Y^{m}_{s+\delta_{s}}|]ds\Big\}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{4}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\Big(\frac{q}{q-1}\Big)^{q}\bigg(\mathbb{E}\exp\Big\{2nq\gamma|\xi_{T}|+2nq\gamma\int_{0}^{T}\Gamma_{s}ds\Big\}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\ \cdot\bigg(\mathbb{E}\exp\Big\{4nq\gamma C\int_{t}^{T}|Y_{s}^{m}|ds\Big\}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{4}}\bigg(\int_{t}^{T}\mathbb{E}\mathbb{E}_{s}\exp\Big\{4nq\gamma C[|Y^{m}_{s+\delta_{s}}|]ds\Big\}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{4}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\Big(\frac{q}{q-1}\Big)^{q}\bigg(\mathbb{E}\exp\Big\{2nq\gamma\xi^{*}+2nq\gamma\int_{0}^{T}\Gamma_{s}ds\Big\}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigg(\mathbb{E}\exp\Big\{4nq\gamma C\mathop{\sup}\limits_{s\in[t,T+K]}|Y_{s}^{m}|(T-t)\Big\}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Thus

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\exp\Big\{q\gamma\mathop{\sup}\limits_{s\in[t,T+K]}|Y_{s}^{m+1}|\Big\}\leqslant\mathbb{E}\exp\Big\{q\gamma\mathop{\sup}\limits_{s\in[t,T]}|Y_{s}^{m+1}|\Big\}\cdot\exp\big\{q\gamma\xi^{*}\big\}

(5.9)

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathbb{E}\exp\Big\{2q\gamma\mathop{\sup}\limits_{s\in[t,T]}|Y_{s}^{m+1}|\Big\}\cdot\Big(\mathbb{E}\exp\big\{2q\gamma\xi^{*}\big\}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sqrt{I(q)}\bigg(\mathbb{E}\exp\Big\{8nq\gamma C\mathop{\sup}\limits_{s\in[t,T+K]}|Y_{s}^{m}|(T-t)\Big\}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

For the case of
C=0,
it is obvious from (
5.9
) one has that

\mathop{\sup}\limits_{m\geqslant 0}\mathbb{E}\exp\Big\{q\gamma\mathop{\sup}\limits_{s\in[0,T+K]}|Y_{s}^{m+1}|\Big\}\leqslant\sqrt{I(q)}.

(5.10)

For the case of
C\neq 0
, we denote
\varepsilon\triangleq\frac{1}{8nC}
, and let
m_{0}
be the unique positive integer such that

8nCT\leqslant m_{0}<8nCT+1.

If
m_{0}=1
, then we have that
8nq\gamma C(T-t)\leqslant 8nq\gamma CT\leqslant q\gamma
. It follows from (
5.9
) that

\mathbb{E}\exp\Big\{q\gamma\mathop{\sup}\limits_{s\in[t,T+K]}|Y_{s}^{m+1}|\Big\}\leqslant\sqrt{I(q)}\bigg(\mathbb{E}\exp\Big\{q\gamma\mathop{\sup}\limits_{s\in[t,T+K]}|Y_{s}^{m}|\Big\}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

By induction, we deduce that

\mathbb{E}\exp\Big\{q\gamma\mathop{\sup}\limits_{s\in[t,T+K]}|Y_{s}^{m+1}|\Big\}\leqslant\sqrt{I(q)}^{1+\frac{1}{2}+\cdot\cdot\cdot+\frac{1}{2^{m}}}\bigg(\mathbb{E}\exp\Big\{q\gamma\mathop{\sup}\limits_{s\in[t,T+K]}|Y_{s}^{0}|\Big\}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2^{m+1}}}\leqslant I(q).

(5.11)

Then, by Hölder’s inequality, we have that

\mathop{\sup}\limits_{m\geqslant 0}\mathbb{E}\exp\Big\{q\gamma\mathop{\sup}\limits_{s\in[0,T+K]}|Y_{s}^{m}|\Big\}\leqslant R(2q)\mathbb{E}\exp\big\{16nq\gamma\xi^{*}\big\}\cdot\mathbb{E}\exp\bigg\{16nq\gamma\int_{0}^{T}\Gamma_{s}ds\bigg\}.

(5.12)

If
m_{0}\neq 1
, then
8nq\gamma C(T-t)\leqslant 8nq\gamma C\varepsilon=q\gamma
when
t\in[T-\varepsilon,T]
. It follows from the similar deduction of (
5.12
) that for all
q>1
,

\displaystyle\mathop{\sup}\limits_{m\geqslant 0}\mathbb{E}\exp\Big\{q\gamma\mathop{\sup}\limits_{s\in[T-\varepsilon,T+K]}|Y_{s}^{m}|\Big\}\leqslant R(2q)\mathbb{E}\exp\big\{16nq\gamma\xi^{*}\big\}\cdot\mathbb{E}\exp\bigg\{16nq\gamma\int_{0}^{T}\Gamma_{s}ds\bigg\},

(5.13)

which leads to

\mathop{\sup}\limits_{m\geqslant 0}\mathbb{E}\exp\Big\{q\gamma|Y_{T-\varepsilon}^{m}|\Big\}\leqslant R(2q)\mathbb{E}\exp\big\{16nq\gamma\xi^{*}\big\}\cdot\mathbb{E}\exp\bigg\{16nq\gamma\int_{0}^{T}\Gamma_{s}ds\bigg\}.

(5.14)

Next, in order to obtain the estimate when
t\in[0,T-\varepsilon]
, we consider the following BSDEs: for
i=1,...,n,

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\tilde{Y}_{t}^{m+1;i}=Y^{m+1;i}_{T-\varepsilon}+\int_{t}^{T-\varepsilon}f^{i}(s,Y^{m}_{s},\tilde{Z}^{m+1,i}_{s},Y^{m}_{s+\delta_{s}})ds-\int_{t}^{T-\varepsilon}\tilde{Z}^{m+1;i}_{s}dW_{s};\quad t\in[0,T-\varepsilon],\\
&\tilde{Y}_{t}^{m+1;i}=Y_{t}^{m+1;i},\quad\quad\quad\tilde{Z}_{t}^{m+1;i}=Z_{t}^{m+1;i},\quad t\in[T-\varepsilon,T-\varepsilon+K].\end{aligned}\right.

(5.15)

Then, similarly to the deduction of (
5.9
), and noting that the pair
(Y^{m+1;i},Z^{m+1;i})
is a solution of BSDE (
5.15
), we obtain that for all
t\in[0,T-\varepsilon],

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\exp\Big\{q\gamma\mathop{\sup}\limits_{s\in[t,T-\varepsilon+K]}|Y_{s}^{m+1}|\Big\}\leqslant\sqrt{I(q)}\bigg(\mathbb{E}\exp\Big\{8nq\gamma C\mathop{\sup}\limits_{s\in[t,T-\varepsilon+K]}|Y_{s}^{m}|(T-\varepsilon-t)\Big\}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Then, similarly to the deduction of (
5.13
), we have

\displaystyle\mathop{\sup}\limits_{m\geqslant 0}\mathbb{E}\exp\Big\{q\gamma\mathop{\sup}\limits_{s\in[T-2\varepsilon,T-\varepsilon+K]}|Y_{s}^{m}|\Big\}

\displaystyle\leqslant R(2q)\mathbb{E}\exp\Big\{16nq\gamma|Y_{T-\varepsilon}^{m}|\Big\}\cdot\mathbb{E}\exp\bigg\{16nq\gamma\int_{0}^{T-\varepsilon}\Gamma_{s}ds\bigg\}

\displaystyle\leqslant R(2q)R(32nq)\mathbb{E}\exp\big\{16^{2}n^{2}q\gamma\xi^{*}\big\}\cdot\bigg(\mathbb{E}\exp\Big\{16^{2}n^{2}q\gamma\int_{0}^{T}\Gamma_{s}ds\Big\}\bigg)^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant R(2q)R(32nq)\mathbb{E}\exp\big\{16^{2}n^{2}q\gamma\xi^{*}\big\}\cdot\mathbb{E}\exp\bigg\{16n\times 32nq\gamma\int_{0}^{T}\Gamma_{s}ds\bigg\}.

By induction, for each
j=1,...,m_{0}-1
,

\displaystyle\mathop{\sup}\limits_{m\geqslant 0}\mathbb{E}\exp\Big\{q\gamma\mathop{\sup}\limits_{s\in[T-j\varepsilon,T-(j-1)\varepsilon+K]}|Y_{s}^{m}|\Big\}

\displaystyle\leqslant R(2q)\prod_{1\leqslant i\leqslant j-1}R(2\times 16^{i}n^{i}q)\mathbb{E}\exp\Big\{16n(16n)^{{}^{j-1}}q\gamma\xi^{*}\Big\}\cdot\mathbb{E}\exp\bigg\{16n(32n)^{{}^{j-1}}q\gamma\int_{0}^{T}\Gamma_{s}ds\bigg\}

\displaystyle\leqslant\Big(R(2q)\Big)^{m_{0}}\mathbb{E}\exp\Big\{16n(16n)^{{}^{m_{0}-1}}q\gamma\xi^{*}\Big\}\cdot\mathbb{E}\exp\bigg\{16n(32n)^{{}^{m_{0}-1}}q\gamma\int_{0}^{T}\Gamma_{s}ds\bigg\}.

(5.16)

Moreover, we consider the following BSDEs: for
i=1,...,n,

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\tilde{Y}_{t}^{m+1;i}=Y^{m+1;i}_{T-(m_{0}-1)\varepsilon}+\int_{t}^{T-(m_{0}-1)\varepsilon}f^{i}(s,Y^{m}_{s},\tilde{Z}^{m+1,i}_{s},Y^{m}_{s+\delta_{s}})ds-\int_{t}^{T-(m_{0}-1)\varepsilon}\tilde{Z}^{m+1;i}_{s}dW_{s};\quad\quad\\
&\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad t\in[0,T-(m_{0}-1)\varepsilon],\\
&\tilde{Y}_{t}^{m+1;i}=Y_{t}^{m+1;i},\quad\tilde{Z}_{t}^{m+1;i}=Z_{t}^{m+1;i},\quad t\in[T-(m_{0}-1)\varepsilon,T-(m_{0}-1)\varepsilon+K].\end{aligned}\right.

(5.17)

Since

8nCT\leqslant m_{0}<8nCT+1,

we have

8nC\big[T-(m_{0}-1)\varepsilon\big]\leqslant 1<8nC\big[T-(m_{0}-1)\varepsilon\big]+1.

Then, when
t\in[0,T-(m_{0}-1)\varepsilon]
, we have
8nq\gamma C\big(T-(m_{0}-1)\varepsilon-t\big)\leqslant 8nq\gamma C\big(T-(m_{0}-1)\varepsilon\big)\leqslant q\gamma.
Thus, it follows from the similar deduction of (
5.12
) that

\displaystyle\mathop{\sup}\limits_{m\geqslant 0}\mathbb{E}\exp\Big\{q\gamma\mathop{\sup}\limits_{s\in[0,T-(m_{0}-1)\varepsilon+K]}|Y_{s}^{m}|\Big\}

\displaystyle\leqslant R(2q)\mathbb{E}\exp\big\{16nq\gamma|\xi_{T}|\big\}\cdot\mathbb{E}\exp\bigg\{16nq\gamma\int_{0}^{T}\Gamma_{s}ds\bigg\}

\displaystyle\leqslant\Big\{R(2q)\Big\}^{m_{0}}\mathbb{E}\exp\Big\{16n(16n)^{{}^{m_{0}-1}}q\gamma\xi^{*}\Big\}\cdot\mathbb{E}\exp\bigg\{16n(32n)^{{}^{m_{0}-1}}q\gamma\int_{0}^{T}\Gamma_{s}ds\bigg\}.

(5.18)

Therefore, by Hölder’s inequality, (
5
), and (
5
), we have

\displaystyle\mathop{\sup}\limits_{m\geqslant 0}\mathbb{E}\exp\Big\{q\gamma\mathop{\sup}\limits_{s\in[0,T+K]}|Y_{s}^{m}|\Big\}

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathbb{E}\exp\big\{(m_{0}+1)q\gamma\xi^{*}\big\}\cdot\mathop{\sup}\limits_{m\geqslant 0}\Bigg\{\mathbb{E}\exp\Big\{(m_{0}+1)q\gamma\mathop{\sup}\limits_{s\in[0,T-(m_{0}-1)\varepsilon]}|Y_{s}^{m}|\Big\}

\displaystyle\quad\ \cdot\prod_{j=1}^{m_{0}-1}\mathbb{E}\exp\Big\{(m_{0}+1)q\gamma\mathop{\sup}\limits_{s\in[T-j\varepsilon,T-(j-1)\varepsilon]}|Y_{s}^{m}|\Big\}\Bigg\}

\displaystyle\leqslant\Big(R\big(2(m_{0}+1)q\big)\Big)^{m_{0}(m_{0}+1)}\mathbb{E}\exp\Big\{16n(16n)^{{}^{m_{0}-1}}(m_{0}+1)^{2}q\gamma\xi^{*}\Big\}

\displaystyle\quad\ \cdot\mathbb{E}\exp\bigg\{16n(32n)^{{}^{m_{0}-1}}(m_{0}+1)^{2}q\gamma\int_{0}^{T}\Gamma_{s}ds\bigg\}.

This together with (
5.10
) and (
5.11
) yields the estimate (
5.4
).

Step 3: The sequence
\{Z^{m}\}_{m=0}^{\infty}
is uniformly bounded in
\mathcal{H}^{q}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T+K;\mathbb{R}^{n\times d})

In this step we prove that for any
q>1,

\mathop{\sup}\limits_{m\geqslant 0}\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\int_{0}^{T+K}|Z_{s}^{m}|^{2}ds\Big)^{\frac{q}{2}}\Big]<+\infty.

(5.19)

Actually, by applying Itô-Tanaka’s formula to the term
\exp\{2\gamma|Y_{t}^{m+1;i}|\}
firstly and then by using inequality (
5.6
), we obtain that for
i=1,...,n
and
m\geqslant 0,

\displaystyle\exp\{2\gamma|Y_{0}^{m+1;i}|\}+\gamma^{2}\int_{0}^{T}\exp\{2\gamma|Y_{s}^{m+1;i}|\}|Z_{s}^{m+1;i}|^{2}ds

\displaystyle\leqslant\exp\{2\gamma|\xi_{T}^{i}|\}+2\gamma\int_{0}^{T}\exp\{2\gamma|Y_{s}^{m+1;i}|\}(\Gamma_{s}+C|Y_{s}^{m}|+C\mathbb{E}_{s}[|Y_{s+\delta_{s}}^{m}|])ds

\displaystyle-2\gamma\int_{0}^{T}\exp\{2\gamma|Y_{s}^{m+1;i}|\}\mathop{\rm sgn}(Y_{s}^{m+1;i})Z_{s}^{m+1;i}dW_{s}.

Then, noting the inequality
(x+y)^{{\frac{q}{2}}}\leqslant 2^{\frac{q}{2}}(x^{\frac{q}{2}}+y^{\frac{q}{2}})
for
x,y\geqslant 0
, we have

\displaystyle\ \gamma^{q}\Big(\int_{0}^{T}|Z_{s}^{m+1;i}|^{2}ds\Big)^{\frac{q}{2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant 2^{\frac{q}{2}}\Bigg\{\Big(\exp\{2\gamma\xi^{*}\}+2\gamma\int_{0}^{T}\exp\{2\gamma|Y_{s}^{m+1;i}|\}(\Gamma_{s}+C|Y_{s}^{m}|+C\mathbb{E}_{s}[|Y_{s+\delta_{s}}^{m}|])ds\Big)^{\frac{q}{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+2^{\frac{q}{2}}\gamma^{\frac{q}{2}}\mathop{\sup}\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\Big|\int_{0}^{t}\exp\{2\gamma|Y_{s}^{m+1;i}|\}\mathop{\rm sgn}(Y_{s}^{m+1;i})Z_{s}^{m+1;i}dW_{s}\Big|^{\frac{q}{2}}\Bigg\}.

By taking the expectation on both sides of the above inequality, we have

\displaystyle\ \mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\int_{0}^{T}|Z_{s}^{m+1;i}|^{2}ds\Big)^{\frac{q}{2}}\Big]

\displaystyle\leqslant 2^{\frac{q}{2}}\gamma^{-q}\mathop{\sup}\limits_{m\geqslant 0}\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\exp\{2\gamma\xi^{*}\}+2\gamma\int_{0}^{T}\exp\{2\gamma|Y_{s}^{m+1;i}|\}(\Gamma_{s}+C|Y_{s}^{m}|+C\mathbb{E}_{s}[|Y_{s+\delta_{s}}^{m}|])ds\Big)^{\frac{q}{2}}\Big]

\displaystyle\quad\ +2^{q}\gamma^{-\frac{q}{2}}\mathbb{E}\Big[\mathop{\sup}\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\Big|\int_{0}^{t}\exp\{2\gamma|Y_{s}^{m+1;i}|\}\mathop{\rm sgn}(Y_{s}^{m+1;i})Z_{s}^{m+1;i}dW_{s}\Big|^{\frac{q}{2}}\Big].

For the last term, by Burkholder-Davis-Gundy’s inequality, Hölder’s inequality, and Jensen’s inequality, there exists a positive constant
M(q)
such that

\displaystyle 2^{q}\gamma^{-\frac{q}{2}}\mathbb{E}\Big[\mathop{\sup}\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\Big|\int_{0}^{t}\exp\{2\gamma|Y_{s}^{m+1;i}|\}\mathop{\rm sgn}(Y_{s}^{m+1;i})Z_{s}^{m+1;i}dW_{s}\Big|^{\frac{q}{2}}\Big]

\displaystyle\leqslant 2^{q}\gamma^{-\frac{q}{2}}M(q)\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big|\int_{0}^{T}\exp\{4\gamma|Y_{s}^{m+1}|\}|Z_{s}^{m+1;i}|^{2}ds\Big|^{\frac{q}{4}}\Big]

\displaystyle\leqslant 2^{q}\gamma^{-\frac{q}{2}}M(q)\mathbb{E}\Big[\mathop{\sup}\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\exp\{q\gamma|Y_{t}^{m+1}|\}\Big|\int_{0}^{T}|Z_{s}^{m+1;i}|^{2}ds\Big|^{\frac{q}{4}}\Big]

\displaystyle\leqslant 2^{q}\gamma^{-\frac{q}{2}}M(q)\mathbb{E}\Big[\mathop{\sup}\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\exp\{2q\gamma|Y_{t}^{m+1}|\}\Big]\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\int_{0}^{T}|Z_{s}^{m+1;i}|^{2}ds\Big)^{\frac{q}{2}}\Big]}.

Therefore

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\int_{0}^{T}|Z_{s}^{m+1;i}|^{2}ds\Big)^{\frac{q}{2}}\Big]\leqslant A(q)+B(q)\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\int_{0}^{T}|Z_{s}^{m+1;i}|^{2}ds\Big)^{\frac{q}{2}}\Big]},

(5.20)

where

\displaystyle A(q)=2^{\frac{q}{2}}\gamma^{-q}

\displaystyle\mathop{\sup}\limits_{m\geqslant 0}\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\exp\{2\gamma\xi^{*}\}+2\gamma\int_{0}^{T}\exp\{2\gamma|Y_{s}^{m+1;i}|\}

\displaystyle\cdot(\Gamma_{s}+C|Y_{s}^{m}|+C\mathbb{E}_{s}[|Y_{s+\delta_{s}}^{m}|])ds\Big)^{\frac{q}{2}}\Big]<+\infty

and

B(q)=2^{q}\gamma^{-\frac{q}{2}}M(q)\mathop{\sup}\limits_{m\geqslant 0}\mathbb{E}\Big[\mathop{\sup}\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\exp\{2q\gamma|Y_{t}^{m+1}|\}\Big]<+\infty.

Next, on one hand, it is easy to check that the solution of the following inequality

x^{2}\leqslant A(q)+B(q)x,\quad x\geqslant 0,

(5.21)

satisfies that

0\leqslant x\leqslant\frac{B(q)+\sqrt{|B(q)|^{2}+4A(q)}}{2}.

On the other hand,
from (
5.20
), it is easy to see that the following term

\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\int_{0}^{T}|Z_{s}^{m+1;i}|^{2}ds\Big)^{\frac{q}{2}}\Big]}

is a solution of the inequality (
5.21
).
Thus, we obtain that for all
m\geqslant 0
,

\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\int_{0}^{T}|Z_{s}^{m}|^{2}ds\Big)^{\frac{q}{2}}\Big]\leqslant n^{\frac{q}{2}}\bigg\{\frac{B(q)+\sqrt{|B(q)|^{2}+4A(q)}}{2}\bigg\}^{2}<+\infty.

Hence for
q>1
, we have

\mathop{\sup}\limits_{m\geqslant 0}\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\int_{0}^{T+K}|Z_{s}^{m}|^{2}ds\Big)^{\frac{q}{2}}\Big]\leqslant 2^{\frac{q}{2}}\mathop{\sup}\limits_{m\geqslant 0}\Bigg\{\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\int_{0}^{T}|Z_{s}^{m}|^{2}ds\Big)^{\frac{q}{2}}\Big]+\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\int_{T}^{T+K}|\eta_{s}|^{2}ds\Big)^{\frac{q}{2}}\Big]\Bigg\}<+\infty,

which implies that (
5.19
) holds.

Step 4: Estimation of the term
\Delta_{\theta}Y^{m,p}

In what follows, without loss of generality, we suppose that the generator
f
is componentwise convex. In other words, for any
i=1,...,n
and
t\in[0,T]
, the component
f^{i}(t,y,\cdot,\phi_{r})
is convex for any
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\phi_{r}\in L^{2}_{\mathscr{F}_{r}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Moreover, for simplicity of presentation, we denote that

\Delta_{\theta}Y^{m,p}\triangleq\frac{Y^{m+p}-\theta Y^{m}}{1-\theta}\quad\hbox{and}\quad\Delta_{\theta}Z^{m,p}\triangleq\frac{Z^{m+p}-\theta Z^{m}}{1-\theta},\quad m\geqslant 1,p\geqslant 1,\theta\in(0,1).

(5.22)

The aim of this step is to give an exponential estimate of the term
\Delta_{\theta}Y^{m,p}.

We observe that the pair
(\Delta_{\theta}Y^{m,p},\Delta_{\theta}Z^{m,p})
belongs to
\mathcal{E}(0,T+K;\mathbb{R}^{n})\times\mathcal{M}(0,T+K;\mathbb{R}^{n\times d})
and satisfies the following BSDEs: for
i=1,...,n,

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\Delta_{\theta}Y_{t}^{m,p;i}=\xi^{i}_{T}+\int_{t}^{T}\Delta_{\theta}f^{m,p;i}(s,\Delta_{\theta}Z_{s}^{m,p;i})ds-\int_{t}^{T}\Delta_{\theta}Z_{s}^{m,p;i}dW_{s},\quad t\in[0,T],\\
&\Delta_{\theta}Y_{t}^{m,p;i}=\xi_{t}^{i},\quad\Delta_{\theta}Z_{t}^{m,p;i}=\eta_{t}^{i},\quad t\in[T,T+K],\end{aligned}\right.

(5.23)

where

\Delta_{\theta}f^{m,p;i}(s,z)\triangleq\frac{1}{1-\theta}\Big\{f^{i}\big(s,Y^{m+p-1}_{s},(1-\theta)z+\theta Z_{s}^{m;i},Y^{m+p-1}_{s+\delta_{s}}\big)-\theta f^{i}\big(s,Y^{m-1}_{s},Z_{s}^{m;i},Y^{m-1}_{s+\delta_{s}}\big)\Big\}.

By
4
, we have that for all
s\in[t,T]
and
z\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

\displaystyle\Delta_{\theta}f^{m,p;i}(s,z)

\displaystyle\leqslant\Big|f^{i}(s,Y^{m+p-1}_{s},z,Y^{m+p-1}_{s+\delta_{s}})\Big|

\displaystyle+\frac{\theta}{1-\theta}\Big|f^{i}(s,Y^{m+p-1}_{s},Z_{s}^{m;i},Y^{m+p-1}_{s+\delta_{s}})-f^{i}(s,Y^{m-1}_{s},Z_{s}^{m;i},Y^{m-1}_{s+\delta_{s}})\Big|

\displaystyle\leqslant\Gamma_{s}+C\Big\{|Y_{s}^{m+p-1}|+\mathbb{E}_{s}|Y_{s+\delta_{s}}^{m+p-1}|+|Y_{s}^{m-1}|+\mathbb{E}_{s}|Y_{s+\delta_{s}}^{m-1}|\Big\}

\displaystyle+C\Big\{|\Delta_{\theta}Y^{m-1,p}_{s}|+\mathbb{E}_{s}|\Delta_{\theta}Y^{m-1,p}_{s+\delta_{s}}|\Big\}+\frac{\gamma}{2}|z|^{2}.

Then, from (
5.4
) and Jensen’s inequality, it is easy to check that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\exp\bigg\{2\gamma\mathop{\sup}\limits_{s\in[t,T]}(\Delta_{\theta}Y^{m,p;i}_{s})^{+}+2\gamma\int_{t}^{T}\Big[\Gamma_{r}+C\big\{|Y_{r}^{m+p-1}|+\mathbb{E}_{r}|Y_{r+\delta_{r}}^{m+p-1}|+|Y_{r}^{m-1}|+\mathbb{E}_{r}|Y_{r+\delta_{r}}^{m-1}|\big\}

\displaystyle+C\big\{|\Delta_{\theta}Y^{m-1,p}_{r}|+\mathbb{E}_{r}|\Delta_{\theta}Y^{m-1,p}_{r+\delta_{r}}|\big\}\Big]dr\bigg\}<\infty,\quad i=1,...,n,\quad t\in[0,T].

(5.24)

Thus, all conditions in
2.9
are satisfied by BSDE (
5.23
), from which it follows that for each
i=1,...,n
,

\displaystyle\exp\Big\{\gamma(\Delta_{\theta}Y^{m,p;i}_{s})^{+}\Big\}

(5.25)

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathbb{E}_{s}\exp\bigg\{\gamma(\xi_{T})^{+}+\gamma\int_{t}^{T}\Big[\Gamma_{r}+C\big(|Y_{r}^{m+p-1}|+\mathbb{E}_{r}|Y_{r+\delta_{r}}^{m+p-1}|+|Y^{m-1,p}_{r}|+\mathbb{E}_{r}|Y^{m-1,p}_{r+\delta_{r}}|\big)

\displaystyle+C\big(|\Delta_{\theta}Y^{m-1,p}_{r}|+\mathbb{E}_{r}|\Delta_{\theta}Y^{m-1,p}_{r+\delta_{r}}|\big)\Big]dr\bigg\},\quad s\in[t,T].

For simplicity, next we denote

\Delta_{\theta}\bar{Y}^{m,p}\triangleq\frac{Y^{m}-\theta Y^{m+p}}{1-\theta}\quad\hbox{and}\quad\Delta_{\theta}\bar{Z}^{m,p}\triangleq\frac{Z^{m}-\theta Z^{m+p}}{1-\theta}.

Then, similarly to the deduction of (
5.25
), we have that for all
i=1,...,n
,

\displaystyle\exp\Big\{\gamma\big(|\Delta_{\theta}\bar{Y}^{m,p;i}_{s}\big)^{+}\Big\}

(5.26)

t\in[0,T],
we have that

\displaystyle\Delta Y_{t}^{m+1}=\int_{t}^{T}\mathbb{E}\big[g(s,Y^{m}_{s},Z_{s},Y^{m}_{s+\delta_{s}},Z_{s+\zeta_{s}})-g(s,Y^{m-1}_{s},Z_{s},Y^{m-1}_{s+\delta_{s}},Z_{s+\zeta_{s}})\big]ds.

Applying Itô’s formula to
e^{\alpha t}|Y_{t}^{m+1}|^{2}
, where

\alpha
is a positive constant which will be determined later,
we have that

\displaystyle\ e^{\alpha t}|\Delta Y_{t}^{m+1}|^{2}+\alpha\int_{t}^{T}e^{\alpha s}|\Delta Y_{s}^{m+1}|^{2}ds

\displaystyle=2\int_{t}^{T}e^{\alpha s}\Delta Y_{s}^{m+1}\mathbb{E}\Big[g(s,Y^{m}_{s},Z_{s},Y^{m}_{s+\delta_{s}},Z_{s+\zeta_{s}})-g(s,Y^{m-1}_{s},Z_{s},Y^{m-1}_{s+\delta_{s}},Z_{s+\zeta_{s}})\Big]ds

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{t}^{T}e^{\alpha s}2\sqrt{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\Delta Y_{s}^{m+1}C\sqrt{\frac{2}{\alpha}}\mathbb{E}\Big[|\Delta Y^{m}_{s}|+\mathbb{E}_{s}[|\Delta Y^{m}_{s+\delta_{s}}]|\Big]ds

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{\alpha}{2}\int_{t}^{T}e^{\alpha s}|\Delta Y_{s}^{m+1}|^{2}ds+\int_{t}^{T}\frac{4C^{2}}{\alpha}\Big\{e^{\alpha s}\mathbb{E}[|\Delta Y^{m}_{s}|^{2}]+e^{\alpha s}\mathbb{E}[|\Delta Y^{m}_{s+\delta_{s}}|^{2}]\Big\}ds.

(5.47)

By taking supremum, expectation, as well as applying Tower property, and noting the fact that
|\Delta Y_{s}^{m}|=0
when
s\in[T,T+K]
, we have that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\mathop{\sup}\limits_{t\in[0,T+K]}e^{\alpha t}|\Delta Y_{t}^{m+1}|^{2}\Big]\leqslant\frac{16C^{2}T}{\alpha}\mathbb{E}\Big[\mathop{\sup}\limits_{s\in[0,T+K]}e^{\alpha s}|\Delta Y_{s}^{m}|^{2}\Big].

Now, by letting
\alpha\triangleq 64C^{2}T
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\mathop{\sup}\limits_{s\in[0,T+K]}e^{\alpha s}|\Delta Y_{s}^{m+1}|^{2}\Big]\leqslant\frac{1}{4}\mathbb{E}\Big[\mathop{\sup}\limits_{s\in[0,T+K]}e^{\alpha s}|\Delta Y_{s}^{m}|^{2}\Big].

(5.48)

Thus, one has that for
m>k
,

\displaystyle\|Y^{m}-Y^{k}\|_{S^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T+K;\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leqslant\sum_{i=k+1}^{m}\|\Delta Y^{i}\|_{S^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T+K;\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leqslant\sum_{i=k+1}^{m}\frac{M}{2^{i-1}}\leqslant\frac{M}{2^{k-1}}\rightarrow 0,\quad\hbox{as}\quad k\rightarrow+\infty,

which implies that
\{Y^{m};m\in\mathbb{N}\}
is a Cauchy sequence in the space
S^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T+K;\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
Therefore, there exists a process
Y\in S^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T+K;\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
Y^{m}\rightarrow Y
in
S^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T+K;\mathbb{R}^{n})
, as
m\rightarrow+\infty.

It follows that Eq. (
5.44
) admits a solution
Y\in S^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T+K;\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

For the uniqueness of BSDE (
1.1
), we only need to prove that the solution of Eq. (
5.44
) is unique. If
\overline{Y}\in S^{q}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T+K;\mathbb{R}^{n})
is another solution of Eq. (
5.44
). Then, similarly to the deduction of (
5.45
) - (
5.48
), we have that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\mathop{\sup}\limits_{s\in[0,T+K]}e^{\alpha s}|Y_{s}-\overline{Y}_{s}|^{2}\Big]\leqslant\frac{1}{4}\mathbb{E}\Big[\mathop{\sup}\limits_{s\in[0,T+K]}e^{\alpha s}|Y_{s}-\overline{Y}_{s}|^{2}\Big],

which implies the uniqueness of Eq. (
5.44
).
This competes the proof.
∎

Proof of
2.6
.

Let

\displaystyle\bar{g}_{t}\triangleq\rho(|U_{t}|)+n\lambda|V_{t}|^{1+\alpha}+\rho_{0}(\mathbb{E}_{t}[|U_{t+\delta_{t}}|])+\lambda_{0}\big(\mathbb{E}_{t}[|V_{t+\zeta_{t}}|]\big)^{1+\alpha},\quad t\in[0,T].

Then by the inequalities
x\leqslant(x+1)^{1+\alpha}

x^{1+\alpha}+y^{1+\alpha}\leqslant(x+y)^{1+\alpha}

\displaystyle\bar{g}_{t}

\displaystyle\leqslant\Big(\rho(|U_{t}|)+1\Big)^{1+\alpha}+\Big((n\lambda+1)|V_{t}|\Big)^{1+\alpha}+\Big(\rho_{0}(\mathbb{E}_{t}[|U_{t+\delta_{t}}|])+1\Big)^{1+\alpha}+\Big((\lambda_{0}+1)\mathbb{E}_{t}[|V_{t+\zeta_{t}}|]\Big)^{1+\alpha}

\displaystyle\leqslant\bigg(2+(\rho+\rho_{0})\big(\|U\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathbb{F}}(t,T+K;\mathbb{R}^{n})}\big)+(n\lambda+1)|V_{t}|+(\lambda_{0}+1)\mathbb{E}_{t}[|V_{t+\zeta_{t}}|]\bigg)^{1+\alpha}.

\displaystyle\bar{H}_{t}\triangleq 2+(\rho+\rho_{0})\big(\|U\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathbb{F}}(t,T+K;\mathbb{R}^{n})}\big)+(n\lambda+1)|V_{t}|+(\lambda_{0}+1)\mathbb{E}_{t}[|V_{t+\zeta_{t}}|],\quad t\in[0,T].

It then follows from
V\in\mathcal{Z}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{n\times d})
that
|V|\in\mathcal{Z}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{R})
. And since

\displaystyle\|\mathbb{E}_{.}[|V_{.+\zeta_{.}}|]\|_{\mathcal{Z}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle=\mathop{\sup}\limits_{\tau\in\mathscr{T}[0,T]}\bigg\|\mathbb{E}_{\tau}\Big[\int_{\tau}^{T}\Big(\mathbb{E}_{s}[|V_{s+\zeta_{s}}|]\Big)^{2}ds\Big]\bigg\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathop{\sup}\limits_{\tau\in\mathscr{T}[0,T]}\bigg\|\mathbb{E}_{\tau}\Big[\int_{\tau}^{T}\mathbb{E}_{s}[|V_{s+\zeta_{s}}|^{2}]ds\Big]\bigg\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\mathop{\sup}\limits_{\tau\in\mathscr{T}[0,T]}\bigg\|\mathbb{E}_{\tau}\Big[\int_{\tau}^{T}|V_{s+\zeta_{s}}|^{2}ds\Big]\bigg\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\infty}

(6.4)

Sharp higher-order L 2 L^{2} -asymptotic expansion of solutions to σ \sigma -evolution equations with different damping types Thanks: ∗ Corresponding author: Tuan Anh Dao (anh.daotuan@hust.edu.vn)


Abstract
. In this paper, our main goal is to achieve the sharp higher-order asymptotic expansion of solutions to σ \sigma -evolution equations with different damping types in the L 2 L^{2} framework by effectively using Taylor series expansion combined with Faà di Bruno’s formula for higher-order derivatives of certain specific functions. In particular, we observe the influence of “parabolic-like models” corresponding to σ 1 ∈ [ 0 , σ / 2 ) \sigma_{1}\in[0,\sigma/2) and “ σ \sigma -evolution like models” corresponding to σ 2 ∈ ( σ / 2 , σ ] \sigma_{2}\in(\sigma/2,\sigma] on the higher-order asymptotic behavior of solutions.

1.
Introduction

Let us consider the following Cauchy problem for linear
\sigma
-evolution equations with double damping:

(1)

\begin{cases}u_{tt}+(-\Delta)^{\sigma}u+(-\Delta)^{\sigma_{1}}u_{t}+(-\Delta)^{\sigma_{2}}u_{t}=0,&\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,t>0,\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\quad u_{t}(0,x)=u_{1}(x),&\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\\
\end{cases}

where
\sigma\geq 1
is assumed to be any fractional number and
0\leq\sigma_{1}<\sigma/2<\sigma_{2}\leq\sigma
. The operator
(-\Delta)^{\sigma}
stands for the fractional Laplacian with the
\sigma
-th power, well-known as high-order waves, where the parameter
\sigma
may be non-integer. In the non-integer case, it is determined by

(-\Delta)^{\sigma}\psi(x)=\mathfrak{F}^{-1}_{\xi\to x}\left(|\xi|^{2\sigma}\widehat{\psi}(\xi)\right)(x),

where
\psi\in H^{2\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\widehat{\psi}
denotes the Fourier transform of a function
\psi
. The term
(-\Delta)^{\theta}u_{t}
for some
\theta\geq 0
is called a damping term, specifically, it is often referred to the frictional (or external) damping, the parabolic like damping, the
\sigma
-evolution like damping and the visco-elastic (or strong) damping when
\theta=0
,
\theta=\sigma_{1}\in(0,\sigma/2)
,
\theta=\sigma_{2}\in(\sigma/2,\sigma)
and
\theta=\sigma
, respectively.

To get started, we now mention some historical views in terms of studying the following problem, which becomes the main inspiration to consider (
1
):

(2)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}u_{tt}+(-\Delta)^{\sigma}u+(-\Delta)^{\theta}u_{t}=0,&x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,t>0,\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\quad u_{t}(0,x)=u_{1}(x),&x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.\end{cases}

where
\theta\in[0,\sigma]
. The most famous case of this problem is when
\sigma=1
, which has been studied by many authors for a long time (for example,
[
21
,
22
,
24
,
7
,
25
,
14
,
20
]
). Matsumura
[
21
]
was the first to establish some basic decay estimates for the problem (
2
) with
(\sigma,\theta)=(1,0)
, also well-known as the damped wave equation, and concluded that the damped wave equation has a diffusive structure as
t\to\infty
. In recent years, many mathematicians have focused on studying a typical nonlinear problem of (
2
) for
(\sigma,\theta)=(1,0)
with the right-hand side replaced by the nonlinear term
|u|^{p}
. Todorova-Yordanov
[
26
]
verified the critical exponent of this problem, which is
p_{\rm c}=1+2/n
. Here, the critical exponent
p_{\rm c}
is understood as follows: If the exponent
p
of the nonlinear term
|u|^{p}
satisfies
p>p_{\rm c}
, then the problem has a unique small data global (in time) solution, moreover, if the exponent
p
satisfies
p<p_{\rm c}
, then the problem does not have any global (in time) solution. Additionally, the higher-order asymptotic behavior of solutions in
L^{q}
framework for some
q\in(1,\infty)
has also been presented in the papers
[
22
,
25
,
20
]
in which they concluded the approximation of
solutions by the Gauss kernel. For
\sigma=1
and
\theta\in(0,1)
, there are a series of papers devoting to (
2
) in this case, for instance
[
7
,
19
,
3
,
23
]
. Namely, D’Abbicco-Reissig in
[
7
]
observed the differences in the solution properties between
parabolic type models
corresponding to
\theta\in(0,1/2)
and
hyperbolic type models
corresponding to
\theta\in(1/2,1)
. Next, D’Abbicco-Ebert in
[
3
]
described the asymptotic behavior of solutions to (
2
) with parabolic type models in the
L^{m}-L^{q}
framework with
1\leq m\leq q\leq\infty
. However, the used method in their proofs seems not applicable to hyperbolic type models which was investigated in
[
19
]
afterwards. More precisely, the authors in the cited papers clearly classified the asymptotic profiles of solutions to (
2
) according to each specific model and they explicitly stated the asymptotic behavior of the higher-order derivatives of solutions. As a result, optimal decay estimates for solutions can be demonstrated from the viewpoint of the higher order derivatives in the
L^{2}
-sense. More generally, for
\sigma\geq 1
and
\theta\in[0,\sigma]
, the problem (
2
) has been widely concerned in a lot of papers
[
10
,
11
,
4
,
14
,
6
,
12
,
5
]
.
Among other things, Dao-Reissig in
[
10
,
11
]
obtained
L^{1}
estimates for solutions to (
2
) by using the theory of modified Bessel functions combined with Faà di Bruno’s formula introduced in
[
23
]
. One recognizes that this approach is just very effective for “parabolic like model” corresponding to
\theta\in(0,\sigma/2)
as well as “
\sigma
-evolution-like model” corresponding to
\theta\in(\sigma/2,\sigma)
. Unfortunately, it is not applicable to take into consideration the case of strongly damped
\sigma
-evolution equations, i.e.
\theta=\sigma
. Quite recently, D’Abbicco-Ebert in
[
5
]
obtained optimal
L^{p}-L^{q}
estimates for solutions by treating separately the two different components of solutions including the oscillatory one and the diffusive one in a particular
t
-dependent zone of the frequency space. Ikehata in
[
14
]
derived sharp decay/non-decay estimates of solutions to (
2
) with
\theta=\sigma
in the
L^{2}
-sense for any spatial dimension.

From these above-mentioned observations, we can say that the investigation of (
2
) devoting only one damping term seems to be complete. To address the mix of two different damping terms, now we will discuss several recent results related to the problem (
1
). The authors in
[
17
,
15
]
obtained the higher-order asymptotic behavior for solutions to (
1
) with
\sigma_{1}=0
and
\sigma_{2}=\sigma=1
under more heavy moment conditions on the initial data for any space dimension by applying Taylor expansion theorem effectively. More generally, in the case where
\sigma_{1}=0
and
\sigma_{2}=\sigma\geq 1
are arbitrary, (
1
) is the so-called
\sigma
-evolution equations with frictional and visco-elastic (or strong) damping. Dao-Michihisa in
[
9
]
successfully found not only higher order asymptotic expansions of solutions but also diffusion phenomenon in the
L^{p}-L^{q}
framework, with
1\leq p\leq q\leq\infty
. Among other things, they underlined that the presence of frictional damping affected the profiles of the solution at low frequencies, whereas their large frequency profile is modified by the presence of the visco-elastic damping. This present work is a continuation of the very recent paper of Dao-Duong-Nguyen
[
8
]
in which the authors have found the asymptotic behavior for the solution to problem (
1
) for any
\sigma\geq 1
and
\sigma_{1}\in[0,\sigma/2)
,
\sigma_{2}\in(\sigma/2,\sigma]
, namely

(3)

\displaystyle u(t,x)\sim\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u_{0}(x)dx\right)\mathfrak{F}_{\xi\to x}^{-1}\left(e^{-|\xi|^{2(\sigma-\sigma_{1})}t}\right)+\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u_{1}(x)dx\right)\mathfrak{F}_{\xi\to x}^{-1}\left(\frac{e^{-|\xi|^{2(\sigma-\sigma_{1})}t}}{|\xi|^{2\sigma_{1}}}\right).

Furthermore, they concluded about the essential interaction between “parabolic-like model” and “
\sigma
-evolution-like models” when they appear together in an equation. One realizes that the damping term representing “parabolic like model” has a greater influence in a comparison with the one standing for
“
\sigma
evolution like models” in determining the first-order asymptotic behavior of solutions to (
1
). More precisely, we can see in (
3
) that this profile is described by the two Fourier multipliers only depending on the parameters
\sigma
and
\sigma_{1}
. Meanwhile, the damping term corresponding to “
\sigma
evolution like models” completely decides the necessary regularity for both the initial data and the solution to obtain these asymptotic behaviors. To the best of the authors’ knowledge, it seems that so far there is no any paper dedicated to the study of the higher-order asymptotic profile of solutions to (
1
) for any
\sigma\geq 1
and
\sigma_{1}\in[0,\sigma/2)
,
\sigma_{2}\in(\sigma/2,\sigma]
. For this reason, the main contribution of this paper is not only to explore such results in the
L^{2}
framework but also to discover the role of the damping term representing “
\sigma
-evolution like models”, i.e the parameter
\sigma_{2}
, on describing the asymptotic profile of solutions, which never appears in
[
8
]
. To concretize this point, we aim to establish the higher-order asymptotic behavior for solutions to (
1
) as follows:

(4)

\displaystyle u(t,x)\sim\mathfrak{F}^{-1}_{\xi\to x}\left(\mathcal{A}_{0}^{k}(t,\xi)\right)\ast_{x}u_{0}(x)+\mathfrak{F}^{-1}_{\xi\to x}\left(\mathcal{A}_{1}^{k}(t,\xi)\right)\ast_{x}u_{1}(x),

where the quantities
\mathcal{A}_{0}^{k}(t,\xi)
and
\mathcal{A}_{1}^{k}(t,\xi)
stand for the
k
-th order asymptotic behavior of the solution, which will be introduced in Notation section. In addition, another novelty of this paper is that we have provided the sharp estimate for the error term in the higher-order asymptotic expansion of solutions for large time, that is,

\displaystyle\left\|u(t,x)-\mathfrak{F}^{-1}_{\xi\to x}\left(\mathcal{A}_{0}^{k}(t,\xi)\right)\ast_{x}u_{0}(x)-\mathfrak{F}^{-1}_{\xi\to x}\left(\mathcal{A}_{1}^{k}(t,\xi)\right)\ast_{x}u_{1}(x)\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt\sim(1+t)^{-\frac{n}{4(\sigma-\sigma_{1})}+\frac{\sigma_{1}}{\sigma-\sigma_{1}}-k\frac{\delta}{\sigma-\sigma_{1}}}.

The remaining part of this paper is organized as follows:
In Section
2
, we are going to state notations which will be used throughout this paper and main results indicating the higher-order asymptotic profile of solutions to (
1
) in the
L^{2}
framework. Then, the proofs of main results will be presented more detail in Section
3
.

2.
Notations and Main results

2.1.
Notations

To give some notations which will be used in the next sections, let us consider the following referenced Cauchy problem for (
1
):

(5)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}bu_{tt}+(-\Delta)^{\sigma}u+a_{1}(-\Delta)^{\sigma_{1}}u_{t}+a_{2}(-\Delta)^{\sigma_{2}}u_{t}=0,&x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,t>0,\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\quad u_{t}(0,x)=u_{1}(x),&x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\end{cases}

with two positive constants
a_{1},a_{2}
and a sufficiently small number
b>0
. At first glance, the problem (
5
) can be understood as a generalization in a comparison with (
1
). The main reason to concern the problem (
5
) comes from the fact that the first-order asymptotic behavior for solutions to (
1
) is like parabolic models (when
a_{2}=0
) and has the property of
\sigma
-evolution like models (when
a_{1}=0
), which was sharply presented in
[
8
]
. More precisely, the solutions to (
1
) behave the same as those to the following anomalous diffusion problem:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}v_{t}+\nu(-\Delta)^{\sigma-\sigma_{1}}v=0,&x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},t>0,\\
v(0,x)=v_{0}(x),&x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\end{cases}

where both a constant
\nu
and the initial data
v_{0}
are chosen suitably. Rough speaking, this problem is also as a consequence of (
5
) by taking
b=0
and
a_{2}=0
. For the purpose to describe the higher-order asymptotic behavior for solutions to (
1
), i.e (
5
) when
(a_{1},a_{2},b)=(1,1,1)
, we can say that taking account of (
5
) plays an important role to show the interplay of all parameters
\sigma
,
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}
. To get started, we apply the Fourier transform to (
5
) to have

(6)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}b\widehat{u}_{tt}+|\xi|^{2\sigma}\widehat{u}+\big(a_{1}|\xi|^{2\sigma_{1}}+a_{2}|\xi|^{2\sigma_{2}}\big)\widehat{u}_{t}=0&\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,t>0,\\
\widehat{u}(0,\xi)=\widehat{u}_{0}(\xi),\quad\widehat{u}_{t}(0,\xi)=\widehat{u}_{1}(\xi)&\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.\end{cases}

The solution to (
6
) can be expressed in the form

\displaystyle\widehat{u}(t,\xi,a_{1},a_{2},b)

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda_{1}^{0}(|\xi|,a_{1},a_{2},b)e^{\lambda_{2}^{0}(|\xi|,a_{1},a_{2},b)t}-\lambda_{2}^{0}(|\xi|,a_{1},a_{2},b)e^{\lambda_{1}^{0}(|\xi|,a_{1},a_{2},b)t}}{\lambda_{1}^{0}(|\xi|,a_{1},a_{2},b)-\lambda_{2}^{0}(|\xi|,a_{1},a_{2},b)}\widehat{u_{0}}(\xi)

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{e^{\lambda_{1}^{0}(|\xi|,a_{1},a_{2},b)t}-e^{\lambda_{2}^{0}(|\xi|,a_{1},a_{2},b)t}}{\lambda_{1}^{0}(|\xi|,a_{1},a_{2},b)-\lambda_{2}^{0}(|\xi|,a_{1},a_{2},b)}\widehat{u_{1}}(\xi).

The characteristic roots are given by

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}^{0}(|\xi|,a_{1},a_{2},b)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2b}\left(-a_{1}|\xi|^{2\sigma_{1}}-a_{2}|\xi|^{2\sigma_{2}}+\sqrt{\left(a_{1}|\xi|^{2\sigma_{1}}+a_{2}|\xi|^{2\sigma_{2}}\right)^{2}-4b|\xi|^{2\sigma}}\right),

\displaystyle\lambda_{2}^{0}(|\xi|,a_{1},a_{2},b)

\displaystyle:=-\frac{1}{2b}\left(a_{1}|\xi|^{2\sigma_{1}}+a_{2}|\xi|^{2\sigma_{2}}+\sqrt{\left(a_{1}|\xi|^{2\sigma_{1}}+a_{2}|\xi|^{2\sigma_{2}}\right)^{2}-4b|\xi|^{2\sigma}}\right).

For simplicity, with loss of generality we can assume that
a_{1}=1
. Then, we denote
a_{2}=a
again. We can rewrite
\lambda_{1}^{0}(|\xi|,1,a,b):=\lambda_{1}^{0}(|\xi|,a,b)
as

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}^{0}(|\xi|,a,b)

\displaystyle=\frac{-2|\xi|^{2(\sigma-\sigma_{1})}}{\left(1+a|\xi|^{2(\sigma_{2}-\sigma_{1})}\right)\left(1+\displaystyle\sqrt{1-\frac{4b|\xi|^{2(\sigma-2\sigma_{1})}}{\left(1+a|\xi|^{2(\sigma_{2}-\sigma_{1})}\right)^{2}}}\right)}

\displaystyle=:-2|\xi|^{2(\sigma-\sigma_{1})}\Gamma_{1}(|\xi|,a)(1+\Gamma_{2}(|\xi|,a,b))^{-1},

where we define the functions

\displaystyle\Gamma_{1}(|\xi|,a):=\left(1+a|\xi|^{2(\sigma_{2}-\sigma_{1})}\right)^{-1}\quad\text{ and }\quad\Gamma_{2}(|\xi|,a,b):=\left(1-4b|\xi|^{2(\sigma-2\sigma_{1})}(\Gamma_{1}(|\xi|,a))^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

Due to the singularity of
\lambda_{2}^{0}(|\xi|,1,a,b):=\lambda_{2}^{0}(|\xi|,a,b)
at
b=0
, to achieve the objectives of the paper we will consider

\displaystyle\lambda_{2}^{0}(|\xi|,a,b):=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\left(|\xi|^{2\sigma_{1}}+a|\xi|^{2\sigma_{2}}+\sqrt{\left(|\xi|^{2\sigma_{1}}+a|\xi|^{2\sigma_{2}}\right)^{2}-4b|\xi|^{2\sigma}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{|\xi|^{2\sigma_{1}}}{2}(1+a|\xi|^{2(\sigma_{2}-\sigma_{1})})(1+\Gamma_{2}(|\xi|,a,b)).

Additionally, we also denote the following quantities:

•

G(|\xi|,a,b):=\sqrt{\left(|\xi|^{2\sigma_{1}}+a|\xi|^{2\sigma_{2}}\right)^{2}-4b|\xi|^{2\sigma}}=|\xi|^{2\sigma_{1}}\left(1+a|\xi|^{2(\sigma_{2}-\sigma_{1})}\right)\Gamma_{2}(|\xi|,a,b).

•

\widehat{K_{0}^{1}}(t,|\xi|,a,b):=(G(|\xi|,a,b))^{-1}\lambda_{1}^{0}(|\xi|,a,b)e^{\lambda_{2}^{0}(|\xi|,a,b)t}.

•

\widehat{K_{0}^{2}}(t,|\xi|,a,b):=(G(|\xi|,a,b))^{-1}\lambda_{2}^{0}(|\xi|,a,b)e^{\lambda_{1}^{0}(|\xi|,a,b)t}.

•

\widehat{K_{1}^{1}}(t,|\xi|,a,b):=(G(|\xi|,a,b))^{-1}e^{\lambda_{1}^{0}(|\xi|,a,b)t}.

•

\widehat{K_{1}^{2}}(t,|\xi|,a,b)=(G(|\xi|,a,b))^{-1}e^{\lambda_{2}^{0}(|\xi|,a,b)t}.

•

\mathcal{A}_{0}^{k}(t,\xi):=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq j+m\leq k-1}\frac{1}{j!m!}\left(\frac{\partial^{j+m}\widehat{K_{0}^{1}}}{\partial a^{j}\partial b^{m}}(t,|\xi|,0,0)-\frac{\partial^{j+m}\widehat{K_{0}^{2}}}{\partial a^{j}\partial b^{m}}(t,|\xi|,0,0)\right)&\text{ if }k\geq 1,\\
0&\text{ if }k=0.\end{cases}

•

\mathcal{B}_{0}^{k}(t,\xi)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq j+m\leq k-1}-\frac{1}{j!m!}\frac{\partial^{j+m}\widehat{K_{0}^{2}}}{\partial a^{j}\partial b^{m}}(t,|\xi|,0,0)&\text{ if }k\geq 1,\\
0&\text{ if }k=0.\end{cases}

•

\mathcal{A}_{1}^{k}(t,\xi):=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq j+m\leq k-1}\frac{1}{j!m!}\left(\frac{\partial^{j+m}\widehat{K_{1}^{1}}}{\partial a^{j}\partial b^{m}}(t,|\xi|,0,0)-\frac{\partial^{j+m}\widehat{K_{1}^{2}}}{\partial a^{j}\partial b^{m}}(t,|\xi|,0,0)\right)&\text{ if }k\geq 1,\\
0&\text{ if }k=0.\end{cases}

•

\mathcal{B}_{1}^{k}(t,\xi)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq j+m\leq k-1}\frac{1}{j!m!}\frac{\partial^{j+m}\widehat{K_{1}^{1}}}{\partial a^{j}\partial b^{m}}(t,|\xi|,0,0)&\text{ if }k\geq 1,\\
0&\text{ if }k=0.\end{cases}

As usual,
H^{s}
and
\dot{H}^{s}
, with

, denote Bessel and Riesz potential spaces based on
L^{2}
spaces. Here
\big<D\big>^{s}
and
|D|^{s}
stand for the differential operators with symbols
\big<\xi\big>^{s}
and

, respectively. Moreover, for any

, we also denote by

\sigma-2\sigma_{1}&\text{ if }\sigma_{2}+\sigma_{1}\geq\sigma\end{cases}

and denote the quantity

2.2.
Main results

Let us now state the main results which will be proved in this paper.

Theorem 2.1

(
Asymptotic profile with
\sigma_{1}>0
)
.

Let
n>4\sigma_{1},\,s\geq 0
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Assuming that the initial data
(u_{0},u_{1})
satisfy

\displaystyle(u_{0},u_{1})\in\mathcal{D}_{s}:=(H^{s}\cap L^{1})\times(H^{[s-2\sigma_{2}]^{+}}\cap L^{1}).

Then, the solution to (
1
) satisfy

\displaystyle\bigg\||\xi|^{s}\widehat{u}(t,\xi)-|\xi|^{s}\mathcal{A}_{0}^{k}(t,\xi)\widehat{u_{0}}(\xi)-|\xi|^{s}\mathcal{A}_{1}^{k}(t,\xi)\widehat{u_{1}}(\xi)\bigg\|_{L^{2}}

(7)

\displaystyle\hskip 142.26378pt\lesssim(1+t)^{-\frac{n}{4(\sigma-\sigma_{1})}-\frac{s}{2(\sigma-\sigma_{1})}+\frac{\sigma_{1}}{\sigma-\sigma_{1}}-k\frac{\delta}{\sigma-\sigma_{1}}}\|(u_{0},u_{1})\|_{\mathcal{D}_{s}},

for all
t\geq 1
. Moreover, if we assume an additional condition
P_{1}\neq 0
, then there exists a positive constant

C_{1}:=C_{1}\left(P_{1},\|(u_{0},u_{1})\|_{\mathcal{D}_{s}}\right)
such that the following estimate hold:

\displaystyle\bigg\||\xi|^{s}\widehat{u}(t,\xi)-|\xi|^{s}\mathcal{A}_{0}^{k}(t,\xi)\widehat{u_{0}}(\xi)-|\xi|^{s}\mathcal{A}_{1}^{k}(t,\xi)\widehat{u_{1}}(\xi)\bigg\|_{L^{2}}

(8)

\displaystyle\hskip 142.26378pt\geq C_{1}(1+t)^{-\frac{n}{4(\sigma-\sigma_{1})}-\frac{s}{2(\sigma-\sigma_{1})}+\frac{\sigma_{1}}{\sigma-\sigma_{1}}-k\frac{\delta}{\sigma-\sigma_{1}}},

for all
t\geq 1
.

Remark 2.1
.

By choosing
s=0
, from (
7
) let us describe the asymptotic profile of solutions to (
1
) in the case
\sigma_{1}>0
as follows:

(9)

\widehat{u}(t,\xi)\sim\mathcal{A}_{0}^{k}(t,\xi)u_{0}(\xi)+\mathcal{A}_{1}^{k}(t,\xi)u_{1}(\xi).

To be specific, for
k=1
we calculate

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{0}^{1}(t,\xi)=-|\xi|^{2(\sigma-2\sigma_{1})}e^{-|\xi|^{2\sigma_{1}}t}+e^{-|\xi|^{2(\sigma-\sigma_{1})}}\text{ and }\,\,\mathcal{A}_{1}^{1}(t,\xi)=\frac{e^{-|\xi|^{2(\sigma-\sigma_{1})}t}}{|\xi|^{2\sigma_{1}}}-\frac{e^{-|\xi|^{2\sigma_{1}}t}}{|\xi|^{2\sigma_{1}}}.

For
k=2
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{0}^{2}(t,\xi)

\displaystyle=-|\xi|^{2(\sigma-2\sigma_{1})}e^{-|\xi|^{2\sigma_{1}}t}\left(1-2|\xi|^{2(\sigma_{2}-\sigma_{1})}+3|\xi|^{2(\sigma-2\sigma_{1})}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+t|\xi|^{2(\sigma-\sigma_{1})}e^{-|\xi|^{2\sigma_{1}}t}\left(|\xi|^{2(\sigma_{2}-\sigma_{1})}-|\xi|^{2(\sigma-2\sigma_{1})}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+e^{-|\xi|^{2(\sigma-\sigma_{1})}t}\left(1+|\xi|^{\sigma-2\sigma_{1}}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+t|\xi|^{2(\sigma-\sigma_{1})}e^{-|\xi|^{2(\sigma-\sigma_{1})}t}\left(|\xi|^{2(\sigma_{2}-\sigma_{1})}-|\xi|^{2(\sigma-2\sigma_{1})}\right)

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{1}^{2}(t,\xi)

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{-|\xi|^{2(\sigma-\sigma_{1})}t}}{|\xi|^{2\sigma_{1}}}\left(1-|\xi|^{2(\sigma_{2}-\sigma_{1})}+2|\xi|^{2(\sigma-2\sigma_{1})}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+t|\xi|^{2(\sigma-\sigma_{1})}\frac{e^{-|\xi|^{2(\sigma-\sigma_{1})}t}}{|\xi|^{2\sigma_{1}}}\left(|\xi|^{2(\sigma_{2}-\sigma_{1})}-|\xi|^{2(\sigma-2\sigma_{1})}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad-\frac{e^{-|\xi|^{2\sigma_{1}}t}}{|\xi|^{2\sigma_{1}}}\left(1-|\xi|^{2(\sigma_{2}-\sigma_{1})}+2|\xi|^{2(\sigma-2\sigma_{1})}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\quad-te^{-|\xi|^{2\sigma_{1}}t}\left(-|\xi|^{2(\sigma_{2}-\sigma_{1})}+|\xi|^{2(\sigma-2\sigma_{1})}\right).

From this observation, we can see the influence of the parameter
\sigma_{2}
representing “
\sigma
-evolution like models” on the asymptotic behavior of solutions with respect to the second-order. When comparing the above results with (
3
), we may observe that some redundant quantities appear. The advantage of the solution representation (
9
) is that it allows us to conclude the precise decay time of the error term for large time, namely,

\displaystyle\left\|\widehat{u}(t,\xi)-\mathcal{A}_{0}^{k}(t,\xi)\widehat{u_{0}}(\xi)-\mathcal{A}_{1}^{k}(t,\xi)\widehat{u_{1}}(\xi)\right\|_{L^{2}}\sim(1+t)^{-\frac{n}{4(\sigma-\sigma_{1})}+\frac{\sigma_{1}}{\sigma-\sigma_{1}}-k\frac{\delta}{\sigma-\sigma_{1}}},

which is really new in a comparison with all previous studies.

Theorem 2.2

(
Asymptotic profile with
\sigma_{1}=0
)
.

Let
n\geq 1,s\geq 0
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Assuming that the initial data
(u_{0},u_{1})
satisfy

\displaystyle(u_{0},u_{1})\in\mathcal{D}_{s}:=(H^{s}\cap L^{1})\times(H^{[s-2\sigma_{2}]^{+}}\cap L^{1}).

Then, the solution to the problem (
1
) with
\sigma_{1}=0
satisfy

(10)

\displaystyle\bigg\||\xi|^{s}\widehat{u}(t,\xi)-|\xi|^{s}\mathcal{B}_{0}^{k}(t,\xi)\widehat{u_{0}}(\xi)-|\xi|^{s}\mathcal{B}_{1}^{k}(t,\xi)\widehat{u_{1}}(\xi)\bigg\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim(1+t)^{-\frac{n}{4\sigma}-\frac{s}{2\sigma}-k\frac{\sigma_{2}}{\sigma}}\|(u_{0},u_{1})\|_{\mathcal{D}_{s}},

for all
t\geq 1
. Moreover, if we assume an additional condition
P_{1}\neq 0
, there exists a positive constant

C_{2}:=C_{2}\left(P_{1},\|(u_{0},u_{1})\|_{\mathcal{D}_{s}}\right)
such that the following estimate hold:

(11)

\displaystyle\bigg\||\xi|^{s}\widehat{u}(t,\xi)-|\xi|^{s}\mathcal{B}_{0}^{k}(t,\xi)\widehat{u_{0}}(\xi)-|\xi|^{s}\mathcal{B}_{1}^{k}(t,\xi)\widehat{u_{1}}(\xi)\bigg\|_{L^{2}}\geq C_{2}(1+t)^{-\frac{n}{4\sigma}-\frac{s}{2\sigma}-k\frac{\sigma_{2}}{\sigma}},

for all
t\geq 1
.

Remark 2.2
.

By choosing
s=0
, we can describe the asymptotic profile of solutions to (
1
) in the case
\sigma_{1}=0
as follows:

\displaystyle\widehat{u}(t,\xi)\sim\mathcal{B}_{0}^{k}(t,\xi)u_{0}(\xi)+\mathcal{B}_{1}^{k}(t,\xi)u_{1}(\xi).

Moreover, we also obtain the sharp estimate for
k
-th order asymptotic expansion error of solutions for large time as follows:

\displaystyle\bigg\||\xi|^{s}\widehat{u}(t,\xi)-|\xi|^{s}\mathcal{B}_{0}^{k}(t,\xi)\widehat{u_{0}}(\xi)-|\xi|^{s}\mathcal{B}_{1}^{k}(t,\xi)\widehat{u_{1}}(\xi)\bigg\|_{L^{2}}\sim(1+t)^{-\frac{n}{4\sigma}-\frac{s}{2\sigma}-k\frac{\sigma_{2}}{\sigma}}.

Specifically, for
k=1
, we calculate

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{0}^{1}(t,\xi)=\mathcal{B}_{1}^{1}(t,\xi)=e^{-|\xi|^{2\sigma}t}

and for
k=2
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{0}^{2}(t,\xi)

\displaystyle=e^{-|\xi|^{2\sigma}t}\left(1+|\xi|^{2\sigma}\right)+t|\xi|^{2\sigma}e^{-|\xi|^{2\sigma}t}\left(|\xi|^{2\sigma_{2}}-|\xi|^{2\sigma}\right),

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{1}^{2}(t,\xi)

\displaystyle=e^{-|\xi|^{2\sigma}t}\left(1-|\xi|^{2\sigma_{2}}+2|\xi|^{2\sigma}\right)+t|\xi|^{2\sigma}e^{-|\xi|^{2\sigma}t}\left(|\xi|^{2\sigma_{2}}-|\xi|^{2\sigma}\right).

Remark 2.3
.

The difference between Theorem
2.1
and Theorem
2.2
is that the quantities

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{j+m}}{\partial a^{j}\partial b^{m}}\widehat{K_{0}^{1}}(t,|\xi|,0,0)\text{ and }\frac{\partial^{j+m}}{\partial a^{j}\partial b^{m}}\widehat{K_{1}^{2}}(t,|\xi|,0,0)

appearing in Theorem
2.1
in the special case
\sigma_{1}=0
(corresponding to frictional damping) have been eliminated for all
j,m\in\mathbb{N}
. This will be explained in detail in the next section.

3.
Proofs of main results

3.1.
Preliminaries

At the beginning of this section, let us apply the Fourier transform to (
1
) to derive

(12)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\widehat{u}_{tt}+|\xi|^{2\sigma}\widehat{u}+\big(|\xi|^{2\sigma_{1}}+|\xi|^{2\sigma_{2}}\big)\widehat{u}_{t}=0&\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,t>0,\\
\widehat{u}(0,\xi)=\widehat{u}_{0}(\xi),\quad\widehat{u}_{t}(0,\xi)=\widehat{u}_{1}(\xi)&\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.\end{cases}

The representation formula of solutions to (
12
) reads as follows:

\widehat{u}(t,\xi)=\widehat{K_{0}}(t,\xi)\widehat{u_{0}}(\xi)+\widehat{K_{1}}(t,\xi)\widehat{u_{1}}(\xi),

that is, the solutions to (
1
) can be written by

(13)

u(t,x)=K_{0}(t,x)\ast_{x}u_{0}(x)+K_{1}(t,x)\ast_{x}u_{1}(x),

where

(14)

\displaystyle\widehat{K_{0}}(t,\xi)

\displaystyle:=\frac{\lambda_{1}(|\xi|)e^{\lambda_{2}(|\xi|)t}-\lambda_{2}(|\xi|)e^{\lambda_{1}(|\xi|)t}}{\lambda_{1}(|\xi|)-\lambda_{2}(|\xi|)}=\widehat{K_{0}^{1}}(t,|\xi|,1,1)-\widehat{K_{0}^{2}}(t,|\xi|,1,1),

(15)

\displaystyle\widehat{K_{1}}(t,\xi)

\displaystyle:=\frac{e^{\lambda_{1}(|\xi|)t}-e^{\lambda_{2}(|\xi|)t}}{\lambda_{1}(|\xi|)-\lambda_{2}(|\xi|)}=\widehat{K_{1}^{1}}(t,|\xi|,1,1)-\widehat{K_{1}^{2}}(t,|\xi|,1,1),

and the characteristic roots
\lambda_{1}(|\xi|):=\lambda_{1}^{0}(|\xi|,1,1)
and
\lambda_{2}(|\xi|):=\lambda_{2}^{0}(|\xi|,1,1)
are given by

\lambda_{1,2}(|\xi|)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(-|\xi|^{2\sigma_{1}}-|\xi|^{2\sigma_{2}}\pm\sqrt{(|\xi|^{2\sigma_{1}}+|\xi|^{2\sigma_{2}})^{2}-4|\xi|^{2\sigma}}\right)&\text{ if }|\xi|\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\setminus\Omega,\\
\\
\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(-|\xi|^{2\sigma_{1}}-|\xi|^{2\sigma_{2}}\pm i\sqrt{4|\xi|^{2\sigma}-(|\xi|^{2\sigma_{1}}+|\xi|^{2\sigma_{2}})^{2}}\right)&\text{ if }|\xi|\in\Omega,\end{cases}

where
\Omega=\left\{r\in\mathbb{R}_{+}:r^{2\sigma_{1}}+r^{2\sigma_{2}}<2r^{\sigma}\right\}
. For simplicity, in the sequels let us denote

\displaystyle\widehat{K_{0}^{1}}(t,|\xi|)=\widehat{K_{0}^{1}}(t,|\xi|,1,1),\quad\widehat{K_{0}^{2}}(t,|\xi|)=\widehat{K_{0}^{2}}(t,|\xi|,1,1),

\displaystyle\widehat{K_{1}^{1}}(t,|\xi|)=\widehat{K_{1}^{1}}(t,|\xi|,1,1),\quad\widehat{K_{1}^{2}}(t,|\xi|)=\widehat{K_{1}^{2}}(t,|\xi|,1,1).

Because of
0\leq\sigma_{1}<\sigma/2<\sigma_{2}\leq\sigma
, there exists a sufficiently small constant
\varepsilon^{*}>0
such that

(0,\varepsilon^{*})\cup\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{*}},\infty\right)\subset\Omega.

Then, taking account of the cases of small and large frequencies separately we conclude that

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}\sim-|\xi|^{2(\sigma-\sigma_{1})},\quad\lambda_{2}\sim-|\xi|^{2\sigma_{1}},\quad\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}\sim|\xi|^{2\sigma_{1}}\quad\text{ for }|\xi|\leq\varepsilon^{*},

(16)

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}\sim-|\xi|^{2(\sigma-\sigma_{2})},\quad\lambda_{2}\sim-|\xi|^{2\sigma_{2}},\quad\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}\sim|\xi|^{2\sigma_{2}}\quad\text{ for }|\xi|\geq\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{*}}.

Let
\chi_{p}=\chi_{p}(r)\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}([0,\infty))
with
p\in\{\rm L,H\}
be smooth cut-off functions having the following properties:

\displaystyle\chi_{\rm L}(r)=\begin{cases}1&\quad\text{ if }r\leq\varepsilon^{*}/2,\\
0&\quad\text{ if }r\geq\varepsilon^{*}\end{cases}\text{ and }\chi_{\rm H}(r)=1-\chi_{\rm L}(r).

It is obvious to see that
\chi_{\rm H}(r)=1
if
r\geq\varepsilon^{*}
and
\chi_{\rm H}(r)=0
if
r\leq\varepsilon^{*}/2
.
In order to prove Theorems
2.1
and
2.2
, the following auxiliary results come into play.

Lemma 3.1

(Faà di Bruno’s formula)
.

Let
\omega_{1}(x)=f_{1}\big(g_{1}(x)\big)
with
x\in\mathbb{R}
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{m}}{\partial x^{m}}\omega_{1}(x)=\sum_{\beta_{1}+2\beta_{2}+...+m\beta_{m}=m}\frac{m!}{\beta_{1}!\beta_{2}!\cdots\beta_{m}!}f_{1}^{(\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}+\cdots+\beta_{m})}\big(g_{1}(x)\big)\prod_{j=1}^{m}\bigg(\frac{1}{j!}\frac{\partial^{j}}{\partial x^{j}}g_{1}(x)\bigg)^{\beta_{j}}.

Furthermore, let
\omega(x_{1},x_{2})=f(g(x_{1},x_{2}))
with
(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Then, the following formula holds for all
j,m\in\mathbb{N}
:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{j+m}}{\partial x_{1}^{j}\partial x_{2}^{m}}\omega(x_{1},x_{2})=\sum_{\ell=1}^{j+m}f^{(\ell)}(g(x_{1},x_{2}))\sum_{h=1}^{j+m}\sum_{\mathcal{S}_{\ell,h}^{j,m}(a,b)}j!m!\prod_{\rho=1}^{h}\frac{1}{a_{\rho}!}\left(\frac{1}{b_{1,\rho}!b_{2,\rho}!}\frac{\partial^{b_{1,\rho}+b_{2,\rho}}}{\partial x_{1}^{b_{1,\rho}}\partial x_{2}^{b_{2,\rho}}}g(x_{1},x_{2})\right)^{a_{\rho}},

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\ell,h}^{j,m}(a,b):=\bigg\{(a_{\rho},b_{1,\rho},b_{2,\rho}),1\leq\rho\leq h\text{ satisfying }a_{\rho}>0,\sum_{\rho=1}^{h}a_{\rho}=\ell,\sum_{\rho=1}^{h}a_{\rho}b_{1,\rho}=j,\sum_{\rho=1}^{h}a_{\rho}b_{2,\rho}=m

\displaystyle\quad\text{ and }(b_{1,\rho_{1}},b_{2,\rho_{1}})\prec(b_{1,\rho_{2}},b_{2,\rho_{2}})\text{ for all }1\leq\rho_{1}<\rho_{2}\leq h\bigg\}.

Here we write
(b_{1,\rho_{1}},b_{2,\rho_{1}})\prec(b_{1,\rho_{2}},b_{2,\rho_{2}})
provided that one of the following holds:

i)

b_{1,\rho_{1}}<b_{1,\rho_{2}}
,

ii)

b_{1,\rho_{1}}=b_{1,\rho_{2}}
and
b_{2,\rho_{1}}<b_{2,\rho_{2}}
.

The first formula of Lemma
3.1
appears quite frequently in the recent papers (e.g.,
[
10
,
22
]
), while the second one can be deduced from Theorem 2.1 in
[
2
]
.

Lemma 3.2

Let

Proof.

At first, with

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{j}}{\partial a^{j}}(\Gamma_{1}(|\xi|,a))^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial^{j}}{\partial a^{j}}\left(1+a|\xi|^{2(\sigma_{2}-\sigma_{1})}\right)^{-\alpha}

\displaystyle=(-\alpha)(-\alpha-1)\cdots(-\alpha-j+1)|\xi|^{2j(\sigma_{2}-\sigma_{1})}\left(1+a|\xi|^{2(\sigma_{2}-\sigma_{1})}\right)^{-\alpha-j},

which gives the conclusions (

) and (

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{m}}{\partial b^{m}}\Gamma_{2}(|\xi|,a,b)

\displaystyle\qquad=\prod_{h=0}^{m-1}(1/2-h)(-4)^{m}|\xi|^{2m(\sigma-2\sigma_{1})}(\Gamma_{1}(|\xi|,a))^{2m}\left(1-4b|\xi|^{2(\sigma-2\sigma_{1})}(\Gamma_{1}(|\xi|,a))^{2}\right)^{1/2-m}

\displaystyle\qquad=\prod_{h=0}^{m-1}(1/2-h)(-4)^{m}|\xi|^{2m(\sigma-2\sigma_{1})}(\Gamma_{1}(|\xi|,a))^{2m}(\Gamma_{2}(|\xi|,a,b))^{1-2m}

for all

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{j+m}}{\partial a^{j}\partial b^{m}}\Gamma_{2}(|\xi|,a,b)

\displaystyle\qquad=C_{m}|\xi|^{2m(\sigma-2\sigma_{1})}\sum_{h=0}^{j}\frac{j!}{h!(j-h)!}\frac{\partial^{j-h}}{\partial a^{j-h}}(\Gamma_{1}(|\xi|,a))^{2m}\frac{\partial^{h}}{\partial a^{h}}(\Gamma_{2}(|\xi|,a,b))^{1-2m}.

we arrive at

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{h}}{\partial a^{h}}(\Gamma_{2}(|\xi|,a,b))^{1-2m}

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{h}}{\partial a^{h}}(\Gamma_{2}(|\xi|,a,b))^{1-2m}\bigg|_{(a,b)=(0,0)}=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }h=0\\

Combining this with the estimate (

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\partial^{h}}{\partial a^{h}}(\Gamma_{2}(|\xi|,a,b))^{1-2m}\right|\lesssim|\xi|^{2h(\sigma_{2}-\sigma_{1})}

and the above relations to (

\displaystyle-\frac{n}{4(\sigma-\sigma_{1})}+\frac{\sigma_{1}}{\sigma-\sigma_{1}}-\frac{s}{2(\sigma-\sigma_{1})}-k\frac{\delta}{\sigma-\sigma_{1}}>-\frac{n}{4\sigma_{1}}+1-\frac{s}{2\sigma_{1}}-k\frac{\delta}{\sigma_{1}},

To prove the estimate (

\displaystyle\bigg\||\xi|^{s}\widehat{u}(t,\xi)-|\xi|^{s}\mathcal{A}_{0}^{k}(t,\xi)\widehat{u_{0}}(\xi)-|\xi|^{s}\mathcal{A}_{1}^{k}(t,\xi)\widehat{u_{1}}(\xi)\bigg\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{4}(t)

\displaystyle=o\left(t^{-\frac{n}{4(\sigma-\sigma_{1})}-\frac{s}{2(\sigma-\sigma_{1})}+\frac{\sigma_{1}}{\sigma-\sigma_{1}}-k\frac{\delta}{\sigma-\sigma_{1}}}\right),

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\left(\left\|e^{-c|\xi|^{2\sigma_{1}}t}|\xi|^{s+2(\sigma-2\sigma_{1})+2k\delta}\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|e^{-c|\xi|^{2(\sigma-\sigma_{1})}t}|\xi|^{s+2k\delta}\right\|_{L^{2}}\right)\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle\qquad=o\left(t^{-\frac{n}{4(\sigma-\sigma_{1})}-\frac{s}{2(\sigma-\sigma_{1})}+\frac{\sigma_{1}}{\sigma-\sigma_{1}}-k\frac{\delta}{\sigma-\sigma_{1}}}\right),

as

, where

\displaystyle=o\left(t^{-\frac{n}{4(\sigma-\sigma_{1})}-\frac{s}{2(\sigma-\sigma_{1})}+\frac{\sigma_{1}}{\sigma-\sigma_{1}}-k\frac{\delta}{\sigma-\sigma_{1}}}\right).

Finally, for the term

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt-\left\||\xi|^{s}\sum_{j+m=k}\frac{1}{j!m!}\frac{\partial^{j+m}\widehat{K_{1}^{1}}}{\partial a^{j}\partial b^{m}}(t,|\xi|,0,0)\left(\widehat{u_{1}}(\xi)-P_{1}\right)\right\|_{L^{2}}.

Proof of Theorem
2.2
.

In the special case
\sigma_{1}=0
, let us express the characteristic root
\lambda_{2}(|\xi|)
as follows:

\displaystyle\lambda_{2}(|\xi|)=\frac{1}{2}\left(-1-|\xi|^{2\sigma_{2}}-\sqrt{(1+|\xi|^{2\sigma_{2}})^{2}-4|\xi|^{2\sigma}}\right).

and

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}(|\xi|)-\lambda_{2}(|\xi|)=\sqrt{(1+|\xi|^{2\sigma_{2}})^{2}-4|\xi|^{2\sigma}}.

Thus, it immediately yields

\displaystyle\left\|\widehat{K_{0}^{1}}(t,\xi)\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim e^{-ct}

\displaystyle\text{ and }\left\|\widehat{K_{1}^{2}}(t,\xi)\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim e^{-ct}

for all
t\geq 1
, where
c
is a suitable positive constant. On the other hand, the fact is that

\displaystyle\bigg\||\xi|^{s}\widehat{u}(t,\xi)-|\xi|^{s}\mathcal{B}_{0}^{k}(t,\xi)\widehat{u_{0}}(\xi)-|\xi|^{s}\mathcal{B}_{1}^{k}(t,\xi)\widehat{u_{1}}(\xi)\bigg\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\left\||\xi|^{s}\left(\widehat{K_{0}^{2}}(t,\xi)-\mathcal{B}_{0}^{k}(t,\xi)\right)\chi_{\rm L}(|\xi|)\right\|_{L^{2}}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}}+\left\||\xi|^{s}\left(\widehat{K_{1}^{1}}(t,\xi)-\mathcal{B}_{1}^{k}(t,\xi)\right)\chi_{\rm L}(|\xi|)\right\|_{L^{2}}\|u_{1}\|_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\left\|\widehat{K_{0}^{1}}(t,\xi)\chi_{\rm L}(|\xi|)\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|\widehat{K_{1}^{2}}(t,\xi)\chi_{\rm L}(|\xi|)\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\left\||\xi|^{s}\widehat{u}(t,\xi)\chi_{\rm H}(|\xi|)\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left\||\xi|^{s}\mathcal{B}_{0}^{k}(t,\xi)\chi_{\rm H}(|\xi|)\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+\left\||\xi|^{s}\mathcal{B}_{1}^{k}(t,\xi)\chi_{\rm H}(|\xi|)\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}.

At this point, the terms

\displaystyle\left\||\xi|^{s}\left(\widehat{K_{0}^{2}}(t,\xi)-\mathcal{B}_{0}^{k}(t,\xi)\right)\chi_{\rm L}(|\xi|)\right\|_{L^{2}}\text{ and }\left\||\xi|^{s}\left(\widehat{K_{1}^{1}}(t,\xi)-\mathcal{B}_{1}^{k}(t,\xi)\right)\chi_{\rm L}(|\xi|)\right\|_{L^{2}}

have been estimated as in the proof of Theorem

\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt\lesssim e^{-ct}(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}+\|u_{1}\|_{H^{[s-2\sigma_{2}]^{+}}}).

Notes on non-separable arrangements of convex bodies Keywords and phrases: Non-separable arrangement, weakly non-separable family, weakly k k -impassable family, stability, convex body, convex polytope, positive homothetic copy. 2010 Mathematics Subject Classification: 52C17, 52C15, 52A40
Károly Bezdek, Zsolt Lángi

Abstract
A problem posed by Erdős in 1945 initiated the study of non-separable arrangements of convex bodies. A finite collection of convex bodies in Euclidean d d -space is called a non-separable family (or NS-family) if every hyperplane intersecting their convex hull also intersects at least one member of the family. Recent work has focused on minimal coverings of NS-families consisting of positive homothetic convex bodies. In this paper, we strengthen these results by establishing their analogues for weakly non-separable families of convex polytopes. We further obtain stability results and analyze maximal weakly non-separable families of cubes. As an additional extension, we also examine weakly k k -impassable families of convex d d -polytopes for 0 < k < d − 1 0<k<d-1 .

Non-separable arrangements - a brief overview

Goodman and Goodman
[
9
]
proved an elementary conjecture of Erdős: If
n>1
circles in the plane have radii
r_{1},r_{2},\dots,r_{n}
and no line separates them, then they can all be covered by a circle of radius
\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}
. Shortly after the appearance of
[
9
]
, Hadwiger
[
11
]
broadened the question and initiated a line of research that has since been pursued by others. In what follows, we recall some basic definitions and key concepts from the authors’ paper
[
4
]
. Let
{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
denote the
d
-dimensional Euclidean space with the standard Euclidean norm
\|\cdot\|
. Let
X+Y
denote the Minkowski sum or simply, vector sum of the sets
X,Y\subseteq{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
. If
\mathbf{K}
is a convex body, i.e., a compact convex set with nonempty interior in
{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
, then let
\mathcal{K}:=\{\mathbf{x}_{i}+\tau_{i}\mathbf{K}\ |\ \mathbf{x}_{i}\in{\mathbb{E}}^{d},\tau_{i}>0,i=1,2,\ldots,n\}
, where
d\geq 2
,
n\geq 2
and
\mathbf{x}_{i}+\tau_{i}\mathbf{K}
is called a positive homothetic copy of
\mathbf{K}
with homothety coefficient
\tau_{i}>0
. Assume that
\mathcal{K}
is a
non-separable family
, in short, an
NS-family
, meaning that every hyperplane intersecting
\conv\left(\bigcup\mathcal{K}\right)
intersects a member of
\mathcal{K}
in
{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
, i.e., there is no hyperplane that strictly separates some elements of
\mathcal{K}
from all the other elements of
\mathcal{K}
in
{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
. Then, let
\lambda(\mathcal{K})>0
denote the smallest positive value
\lambda
such that a translate of

\lambda\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\tau_{i}\right)\mathbf{K}
covers
\bigcup\mathcal{K}
. As in
[
4
]
, for
d\geq 2
let
\lambda_{\rm simplex}^{(d)}
denote the supremum of
\lambda(\mathcal{K})
, where
\mathcal{K}
runs over the NS-families of finitely many positive homothetic
d
-simplices in
{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
. It was proved in
[
4
]
that
\lambda_{\rm simplex}^{(d)}
is a non-decreasing sequence of
d
. Moreover,
\lambda_{\rm simplex}^{(d)}\geq\lambda_{\rm simplex}^{(2)}\geq 2/3+2/(3\sqrt{3})=1.0515\ldots>1
for all
d\geq 3
. The special role of
\lambda_{\rm simplex}^{(d)}
is highlighted by the following statement proved by the authors in
[
4
]
.

Theorem 1

For all
d\geq 2
,
\lambda_{\rm simplex}^{(d)}=\sup_{\mathcal{K}}\lambda(\mathcal{K})
, where
\mathcal{K}
runs over the NS-families of finitely many positive homothetic copies of an arbitrary convex body
\mathbf{K}
in
{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
.

On the other hand, improving the upper bound
\lambda_{\rm simplex}^{(d)}\leq d
from
[
4
]
, Akopyan, Balitskiy, and Grigorev
[
1

have derived the following stronger estimate. (See also Theorem

Theorem 2
(Akopyan, Balitskiy, and Grigorev, 2018)

\lambda_{\rm simplex}^{(d)}\leq(d+1)/2
for all
d\geq 2
.

Unfortunately, the above estimate does not seem to be sharp even for

. In fact, the authors (see
[
4
, Problem 1]
) asked whether
\lambda_{\rm simplex}^{(2)}=2/3+2/(3\sqrt{3})=1.0515\ldots
holds and raised also the following higher dimensional question.

Problem 1

Find
\lambda_{\rm simplex}^{(d)}
for any given
d\geq 2
. In particular, is there an absolute constant
c>0
such that
\lambda_{\rm simplex}^{(d)}\leq c
holds for all
d\geq 2
?

However, if the convex body
\mathbf{K}
is centrally symmetric, then one can expect much stronger results. Here we recall that a set
S\subset{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
is centrally symmetric with center
\mathbf{q}\in{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
if
S=2\mathbf{q}-S
, and
\mathbf{o}
-symmetric if
S=-S
, where
\mathbf{o}
denotes the origin of
{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
. The following result was proved by the authors in
[
4
]
.

Theorem 3

For all
d\geq 2
and
n\geq 2
, and for every
\mathbf{o}
-symmetric convex body
\mathbf{K}_{0}
and every NS-family
\mathcal{K}:=\{\mathbf{x}_{i}+\tau_{i}\mathbf{K}_{0}\ |\ \mathbf{x}_{i}\in{\mathbb{E}}^{d},\tau_{i}>0,i=1,2,\ldots,n\}
the inequality
\lambda(\mathcal{K})\leq 1
holds.

Next, recall the following definition from
[
4
]
. Let
\mathcal{K}:=\{\mathbf{x}_{i}+\tau_{i}\mathbf{K}\ |\ \mathbf{x}_{i}\in{\mathbb{E}}^{d},\tau_{i}>0,i=1,2,\ldots,n\}
be a family of positive homothetic copies of the convex body
\mathbf{K}
in
{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
and let
0\leq k\leq d-1
. We say that
\mathcal{K}
is a
k
-impassable arrangement
, in short, a
k
-IP-family
if every
k
-dimensional affine subspace of
{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
intersecting
\conv\left(\bigcup\mathcal{K}\right)
intersects an element of
\mathcal{K}
. Let
\lambda_{k}(\mathcal{K})>0
denote the smallest positive value
\lambda
such that some translate of
\lambda\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\tau_{i}\right)\mathbf{K}
covers
\bigcup\mathcal{K}
, where
\mathcal{K}
is an arbitrary
k
-IP-family. A
(d-1)
-IP-family is simply called an NS-family and in that case
\lambda_{d-1}(\mathcal{K})=\lambda(\mathcal{K})
. In order to state the main result of
[
4
]
on
k
-IP-families of positive homothetic convex bodies in
{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
we need the following definitions and statement from Section 3.2 of
[
18
]
. Let
\mathbf{K}_{1}^{\prime}
and
\mathbf{K}_{2}^{\prime}
be nonempty, compact, convex sets in
{\mathbb{E}}^{d},d\geq 2
. We say that
\mathbf{K}_{2}^{\prime}

slides freely inside

\mathbf{K}_{1}^{\prime}
if to each boundary point
\mathbf{x}
of
\mathbf{K}_{1}^{\prime}
there exists a translation vector
\mathbf{y}\in{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
such that
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbf{y}+\mathbf{K}_{2}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathbf{K}_{1}^{\prime}
.
Furthermore, we say that
\mathbf{K}_{2}^{\prime}
is a
summand
of
\mathbf{K}_{1}^{\prime}
if there exists a nonempty, compact, convex set
\mathbf{K}^{\prime}
in
{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
such that
\mathbf{K}_{2}^{\prime}+\mathbf{K}^{\prime}=\mathbf{K}_{1}^{\prime}
. It is not hard to see that
\mathbf{K}_{2}^{\prime}
is a summand of
\mathbf{K}_{1}^{\prime}
if and only if
\mathbf{K}_{2}^{\prime}
slides freely inside
\mathbf{K}_{1}^{\prime}
(see
[
18
, Theorem 3.2.2.]
). Now, the main result of
[
4
]
on
k
-IP-families reads as follows.

Theorem 4

Let
\mathbf{K}
be a
d
-dimensional convex body and
\mathcal{K}:=\{\mathbf{K}_{i}:=\mathbf{x}_{i}+\tau_{i}\mathbf{K}\ |\ \mathbf{x}_{i}\in{\mathbb{E}}^{d},\tau_{i}>0,i=1,2,\ldots,n\}

be a
k
-IP family of positive homothetic copies of
\mathbf{K}
in
{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
, where
0\leq k\leq d-2
. Then
\conv\left(\bigcup\mathcal{K}\right)
slides freely in
\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\tau_{i}\right)\mathbf{K}
(i.e.,
\conv\left(\bigcup\mathcal{K}\right)
is a summand of
\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\tau_{i}\right)\mathbf{K}
) and therefore
\lambda_{k}(\mathcal{K})\leq 1
.

It was noted in
[
4
]
that for strictly convex bodies one can do more. Namely, if
\mathbf{K}
is a strictly convex body in
{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
and
\mathcal{K}:=\{\mathbf{K}_{i}:=\mathbf{x}_{i}+\tau_{i}\mathbf{K}\ |\ \mathbf{x}_{i}\in{\mathbb{E}}^{d},\tau_{i}>0,i=1,2,\ldots,n\}

is a
k
-IP family of positive homothetic copies of
\mathbf{K}
in
{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
, where
0\leq k\leq d-2
, then
\bigcup\mathcal{K}=\mathbf{x}_{i^{*}}+\tau_{i^{*}}\mathbf{K}=\mathbf{K}_{i^{*}}
for
\tau_{i^{*}}=\max\{\tau_{i}\ |\ i=1,2,\ldots,n\}
.

It is natural to say that an arrangement of (finitely many or infinitely many) convex bodies is
separable
in
{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
if there exists a hyperplane that strictly separates some members of the arrangement from all the other members of the arrangement in
{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
. If an arrangement of convex bodies is not separable in
{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
, then we say that it is a
non-separable arrangement
, in short, an
NS-arrangement
. Let
\mathcal{L}^{d}
denote the set of full-dimensional lattices in
{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
, i.e., discrete subgroups of
{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
of full rank. Every lattice
L\in\mathcal{L}^{d}
can be written as
L=A\mathbb{Z}^{d}
for some invertible matrix
A\in{\rm GL}_{d}(\mathbb{R})
. Given a subset
S\subseteq{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
and a lattice
L\in\mathcal{L}^{d}
, we call
S+L:=\cup_{\mathbf{z}\in L}(S+\mathbf{z})
a
lattice arrangement
of
S
in
{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
. If
S+L
is an NS-arrangement, the we call it an
NS-lattice arrangement
. G. Fejes Tóth (
[
5
]
) asked the fundamental question on finding the smallest density of NS-lattice arrangements of balls in
{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
. More generally, L. Fejes Tóth and E. Makai Jr.
[
5
]
asked for the infimum of the densities of thinnest NS-lattice arrangements of convex bodies in
{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
. They proved the following theorem in
[
5
]
.

Theorem 5
(L. Fejes Tóth and Makai Jr., 1974)

The density of any NS-lattice arrangement of an arbitrary convex domain is at least
\frac{3}{8}
in
{\mathbb{E}}^{2}
. Equality holds only if the convex domain is a triangle spanned by one vertex and the midpoints of the opposite sides of a basic parallelogram.

In 1978, Makai Jr.
[
15
]
established a remarkable connection between the volume product of a convex body, its maximal lattice packing density and the minimal density of an NS-lattice arrangement of its polar body. Consequently, he formulated the following conjecture. If
\mathbf{K}
be a convex body in
{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
, then
V_{d}(\mathbf{K})
denotes the
d
-dimensional volume (i.e., Lebesgue measure) of
\mathbf{K}
. Moreover, for
L\in\mathcal{L}^{d}
we denote the determinant of
L
, i.e., the
d
-dimensional volume of a fundamental domain of
L
, by
{\rm det}(L)
.

Conjecture 1
(Makai Jr., 1978)

Let
\mathbf{K}
be a convex body in
{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
,
d>1
and let
L\in\mathcal{L}^{d}
such that
\mathbf{K}+L
is an NS-lattice arrangement. Then
\frac{V_{d}(\mathbf{K})}{{\rm det}(L)}\geq\frac{d+1}{2^{d}d!}
.

Note that
\frac{V_{d}(\mathbf{K})}{{\rm det}(L)}
is the density of the NS-lattice arrangement
\mathbf{K}+L
in
{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
(i.e., the fraction of space covered by
L
translates of
\mathbf{K}
) and so, Theorem
5
proves Conjecture
1
for
d=2
. (For more details see
[
5
]
and
[
15
]
.) On the other hand, Conjecture
1
is open for all
d>2
. For several partial results and further connections to Mahler’s conjecture on volume product and covering minima of Kannan and Lovász
[
13
]
we refer the interested reader to
[
8
]
.

Finally, we mention that NS-arrangements have been studied in spherical spaces
{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}:=\{\mathbf{u}\in{\mathbb{E}}^{d}|\|\mathbf{u}\|=1\}
as well. A closed cap, in short a
cap
, of spherical radius
\alpha
, for
0\leq\alpha\leq\pi
, is the set of points with spherical distance at most
\alpha
from a given point in
{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}\subset{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
. A
great sphere
of
{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}
is an intersection of
{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}
with a hyperplane of
{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
passing through the origin
\mathbf{o}\in{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
. Following the terminology of Polyanskii
[
16
]
, we say that a great sphere
avoids
a collection of caps in
{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}
if it does not intersect any cap of the collection. Finally, we say that a finite collection of caps is
non-separable
, i.e., it is an
NS-family
, if it does not have a great sphere that avoids the caps such that on both sides of it there is at least one cap. Based on these concepts Polyanskii
[
16
]
proved the following extension of the theorem of Goodman and Goodman
[
9
]
to spherical spaces.

Theorem 6
(Polyanskii, 2021)

Let
\mathcal{F}
be an NS-family of caps of spherical radii
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}
in
{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}
,
d\geq 2
. If
\alpha_{1}+\dots+\alpha_{n}<\frac{\pi}{2}
, then
\mathcal{F}
can be covered by a cap of radius
\alpha_{1}+\dots+\alpha_{n}
in
{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}
.

If the spherical caps in Theorem
6
have pairwise disjoint interiors and their centers lie on a great circle in
{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}
(where by a great circle we mean the intersection of
{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}
with a
2
-dimensional linear subspace of
{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
), with consecutive caps along the great circle touching each other, then the estimate for the radius of the covering cap in Theorem
6
is sharp. This naturally leads to the question of a corresponding stability statement.

Problem 2

Find a stability analogue of Theorem
6
.

In the remainder of the paper, we examine additional aspects of non-separability related to the results highlighted above and also strengthen the results in the special case where the convex bodies are convex polytopes. This is achieved by establishing
weakly non-separable
and
weakly
k
-impassable
analogues of the aforementioned results. We also address related stability results.

2
A stability analogue of Theorem
3

If the positive homothetic convex bodies in Theorem
3
have pairwise disjoint interiors and their centers lie on a common line, with consecutive bodies along the line touching one another, then the estimate of Theorem
3
is sharp. This observation naturally motivates the search for a corresponding stability statement. It will be useful to have the following concept. Let
\mathbf{K}
be an
\mathbf{o}
-symmetric convex body in
{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
and let
X
be a compact set in
{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
. Then the
\mathbf{K}
-circumradius of
X
is the smallest value of
\lambda>0
such that a translate of
\lambda\mathbf{K}
contains
X
.

Theorem 7

Let
\mathbf{K}
be an
\mathbf{o}
-symmetric convex body in
{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
with
C^{2}
-class boundary and strictly positive Gaussian curvature everywhere. Let
\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\ldots,\tau_{n}>0
. Then there are some positive constants
C:=C(\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\ldots,\tau_{n},\mathbf{K})
and
D:=D(\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\ldots,\tau_{n},\mathbf{K})
with the following property. If
\mathcal{F}:=\{\mathbf{p}_{i}+\tau_{i}\mathbf{K}\ |\ \mathbf{p}_{i}\in{\mathbb{E}}^{d},\tau_{i}>0,i=1,2,\ldots,n\}
is an NS-family whose
\mathbf{K}
-circumradius is at least
\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\tau_{i}\right)-\varepsilon
for some
\varepsilon
with
0<\varepsilon\leq D
, then there is a straight line
L
whose distance from every
\mathbf{p}_{i}
is at most
C\varepsilon^{1/2}
. Furthermore, the exponent
1/2
is the best possible for any fixed values of the
\tau_{i}>0,i=1,2,\ldots,n
.

Proof:

Consider an NS-family
\mathcal{F}:=\{\mathbf{K}_{i}:=\mathbf{p}_{i}+\tau_{i}\mathbf{K}\ |\ \mathbf{p}_{i}\in{\mathbb{E}}^{d},\tau_{i}>0,i=1,2,\ldots,n\}
. Assume that if a translate of
\bar{\tau}\mathbf{K}
covers
\bigcup\mathcal{F}
, then
\bar{\tau}\geq\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\tau_{i}\right)-\varepsilon
.

It is known (see
[
4
]
) that the homothetic copy
\mathbf{p}+\tau\mathbf{K}
, with
\tau:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\tau_{i}
and center
\mathbf{p}:=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\tau_{i}\mathbf{p}_{i}}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\tau_{i}}
, contains
\bigcup\mathcal{F}
. Hence, according to our assumption, at least one point of
\bigcup\mathcal{F}
is a boundary or an exterior point of
\mathbf{p}+(\tau-\varepsilon)\mathbf{K}
. Let us choose a coordinate system in which
\mathbf{o}
is a farthest point of
\bigcup\mathcal{F}
from
\mathbf{p}
with
\mathbf{p}
sitting on the positive half of the first coordinate axis. Since the statement remains unchanged under any non-degenerated linear transformation of
\mathbf{K}
, we may also assume that the projection of
\mathbf{K}
onto the first coordinate axis is the interval
[-1,1]
and it coincides with the intersection of
\mathbf{K}
with the axis, and, in addition, that its projection onto the orthogonal complement of the axis does not contain any point farther than one from
\mathbf{o}
.

Observe that
\mathbf{p}=(x,\mathbf{0})
for some
x>0
with
\mathbf{0}:=(0,\dots,0)\in\mathbb{E}^{d-1}
and let
(x_{i},\mathbf{y}_{i}):=\mathbf{p}_{i}
with
\mathbf{y}_{i}\in\mathbb{E}^{d-1}
. Note that since
\mathcal{F}
is an NS-family, its projection onto the first coordinate axis is a closed segment. Let us relabel the elements such that if
i\leq j
, then the left endpoint of the projection of
\mathbf{p}_{i}+\tau_{i}\mathbf{K}
is not greater than that of
\mathbf{p}_{j}+\tau_{j}\mathbf{K}
.
Then for any
1\leq i\leq n
, we have
x_{i}\leq 2\tau_{1}+2\tau_{2}+\ldots+2\tau_{i-1}+\tau_{i}
with
\tau_{0}:=0
.
This yields that

x=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\tau_{i}x_{i}}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\tau_{i}}\leq\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}(2\tau_{1}+2\tau_{2}+\ldots+2\tau_{i-1}+\tau_{i})\tau_{i}}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\tau_{i}}=\frac{(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\tau_{i})^{2}}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\tau_{i}}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\tau_{i}=\tau.

On the other hand, our assumptions yield that
x\geq\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\tau_{i}\right)-\varepsilon=\tau-\varepsilon
. This implies that for every value of
i
, we have

x_{i}\geq 2\tau_{1}+2\tau_{2}+\ldots+2\tau_{i-1}+\tau_{i}-\frac{\tau}{\tau_{i}}\varepsilon.

Let
C^{\prime}=\max\{\frac{\tau}{\tau_{i}}\ |\ i=1,2,\ldots,n\}
. Then the above inequality yields that
\tau_{i-1}+\tau_{i}-C^{\prime}\varepsilon\leq x_{i}-x_{i-1}\leq\tau_{i-1}+\tau_{i}+C^{\prime}\varepsilon
. By our assumptions,
||\mathbf{y}_{i}||\leq\tau
for all values of
i
. This implies in a straightforward way that there exists
D:=D(\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\ldots,\tau_{n},\mathbf{K})>0
such that if
0<\varepsilon\leq D
and
\mathbf{p}_{i-1}+\tau_{i-1}\mathbf{K}
and
\mathbf{p}_{i}+\tau_{i}\mathbf{K}
are disjoint, then there exists a hyperplane that strictly separates them and is disjoint from any other element of
\mathcal{F}
. Thus, the non-separability of
\mathcal{F}
yields that
\mathbf{p}_{i-1}+\tau_{i-1}\mathbf{K}
and
\mathbf{p}_{i}+\tau_{i}\mathbf{K}
intersect for all values of
i
whenever
0<\varepsilon\leq D
. On the other hand, since
\mathbf{K}
has
C^{2}
-class boundary with strictly positive curvature, there is a value
0<\kappa
such that the Gaussian curvature of
\mathbf{K}
at any point is at least
\kappa
.

According to our conditions, the point of
\mathbf{K}_{i}
with the smallest first coordinate is
(x_{i}-\tau_{i},\mathbf{y}_{i})
and the point of
\mathbf{K}_{i-1}
with the largest first coordinate is
(x_{i-1}+\tau_{i-1},\mathbf{y}_{i-1})
. By Theorem 3.2.12. of
[
18
]
, the ball of radius
\frac{1}{\kappa}
and center
(x_{i-1}+\tau_{i-1}-\frac{1}{\kappa},\mathbf{y}_{i-1})
contains
\mathbf{K}_{i-1}
. Similarly, the ball of radius
\frac{1}{\kappa}
and center
(x_{i}-\tau_{i}+\frac{1}{\kappa},\mathbf{y}_{i})
contains
\mathbf{K}_{i}
. Thus, the fact that
\mathbf{K}_{i-1}\cap\mathbf{K}_{i+1}\neq\emptyset
yields that

\left((x_{i}-\tau_{i})-(x_{i-1}+\tau_{i-1})+\frac{2}{\kappa}\right)^{2}+\|\mathbf{y}_{i}-\mathbf{y}_{i-1}\|^{2}\leq\frac{4}{\kappa^{2}}.

Since
-C^{\prime}\varepsilon\leq(x_{i}-\tau_{i})-(x_{i-1}+\tau_{i-1})\leq C^{\prime}\varepsilon
, from the above inequality we have for
0<\varepsilon\leq D
that

\left(\frac{2}{\kappa}-C^{\prime}\varepsilon\right)^{2}+\|y_{i}-y_{i-1}\|^{2}\leq\frac{4}{\kappa^{2}},

which yields that
\|y_{i}-y_{i-1}\|\leq\left(C^{\prime}\left(\frac{4}{\kappa}+C^{\prime}D\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}{\varepsilon}^{\frac{1}{2}}
. Thus, the assertion follows by choosing the first coordinate axis as
L
and setting
C:=(n-1)\left(C^{\prime}\left(\frac{4}{\kappa}+C^{\prime}D\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}
.

Now we prove the second statement. Let us assume that the diameter of
\mathbf{K}
is two. Let us assume that the points
\mathbf{p}_{2},\mathbf{p}_{3},\ldots,\mathbf{p}_{n}
are collinear and their line is parallel to a diameter of
\mathbf{K}
, and their distances are
\tau_{2}+\tau_{3},\ldots,\tau_{n-1}+\tau_{n}
. Let us assume that
\mathbf{p}_{1}+\tau\mathbf{K}
and
\mathbf{p}_{2}+\tau_{2}\mathbf{K}
touch, and the angle
\alpha
of the halflines through
\mathbf{p}_{1}
and
\mathbf{p}_{3}
, starting at
\mathbf{p}_{2}
is almost
\pi
. Let
\delta>0
denote the distance of
\mathbf{p}_{2}
from the line passing through the points
\mathbf{p}_{1}
and
\mathbf{p}_{n}
. If
\delta
is sufficiently small, then the
\mathbf{K}
-circumradius
\bar{\tau}
of the NS-family
\mathcal{F}=\{\mathbf{p}_{i}+\tau_{i}\mathbf{K}:i=1,2,\ldots,n\}
defined in this way is equal to
\frac{1}{2}(\tau_{1}+\tau_{n}+\|\mathbf{p}_{n}-\mathbf{p}_{1}\|_{\mathbf{K}})\geq\frac{1}{2}(\tau_{1}+\tau_{n}+\|\mathbf{p}_{n}-\mathbf{p}_{1}\|)
, where
\|\cdot\|_{\mathbf{K}}
denotes the norm generated by
\mathbf{K}
, which is defined by
\|\mathbf{x}\|_{\mathbf{K}}:=\min\{\lambda\geq 0\ |\ \mathbf{x}\in\lambda\mathbf{K}\}
for
\mathbf{x}\in{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
. Note that

\|\mathbf{p}_{n}-\mathbf{p}_{1}\|=\sqrt{\|\mathbf{p}_{n}-\mathbf{p}_{2}\|^{2}-\delta^{2}}+\sqrt{\|\mathbf{p}_{1}-\mathbf{p}_{2}\|^{2}-\delta^{2}}=\|\mathbf{p}_{n}-\mathbf{p}_{2}\|+\|\mathbf{p}_{1}-\mathbf{p}_{2}\|-C_{1}\delta^{2}+O(\delta^{4})

for some
C_{1}>0
. Let
\beta
and
\gamma
denote the angles of the triangle
\conv\{\mathbf{p}_{n}\mathbf{p}_{2},\mathbf{p}_{1}\}
at
\mathbf{p}_{n}
and
\mathbf{p}_{1}
, respectively.
Then
\beta=\arcsin\frac{\delta}{\|\mathbf{p}_{n}-\mathbf{p}_{2}\|}=:\Theta_{1}(\delta)
, and
\gamma=\arcsin\frac{\delta}{\|\mathbf{p}_{2}-\mathbf{p}_{1}\|}=:\Theta_{2}(\delta)
implying that
\pi-\alpha=\Theta_{1}(\delta)+\Theta_{2}(\delta)
. The differentiability properties of
\bd(\mathbf{K})
imply that
\|\mathbf{p}_{1}-\mathbf{p}_{2}\|\geq\tau_{1}+\tau_{2}-C_{2}(\pi-\alpha)^{2}=\tau_{1}+\tau_{2}-C_{3}\delta^{2}
for some positive constants
C_{2},C_{3}
.
On the other hand,
\|\mathbf{p}_{n}-\mathbf{p}_{2}\|=\|\mathbf{p}_{n}-\mathbf{p}_{2}\|_{\mathbf{K}}=\tau_{n}+2\tau_{n-1}+\ldots+2\tau_{3}+\tau_{2}
, implying that

\|\mathbf{p}_{n}-\mathbf{p}_{1}\|\geq(2\tau-\tau_{n}-\tau_{1})-2C\delta^{2}

for some
C>0
. This yields the second statement.

Remark 1

We note that if we consider a centrally symmetric convex body
\mathbf{K}
in
{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
which is not smooth, then one cannot expect the stability result of Theorem
7
to hold for homothetic copies of
\mathbf{K}
. For example, there are
n>1
translates of a unit
d
-cube in
{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
such that the smallest homothetic cube containing them is of edge length
n
and still the centers of the unit cubes do not lie on a line in
{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
.

3
Upper bounding the tightness of NS-lattice arrangements

L. Fejes Tóth
[
6
,
7
]
introduced the notion of closeness of a packing of balls in
{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
and suggested the problem of finding the closest packing of equal balls in
{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
. For the status of this problem and several related questions we refer the interested reader to
[
20
]
. Here we recall the following variant of this concept, which leads us to an analogue of Theorem
5
as follows. Recall that for a family
\mathcal{F}
of translates of a convex body
\mathbf{K}
, the
tightness
of
\mathcal{F}
is defined as the largest value
\lambda>0
such that there is a homothetic copy
\mathbf{x}+\lambda\mathbf{K}
that does not overlap
\bigcup\mathcal{F}
. Correcting the proof of Theorem 2.13 in
[
13
]
, Averkov and Wagner
[
2
, Theorem 2.5]
proved the following theorem. We present a different and shorter proof.

Theorem 8

Let
\mathbf{K}
be an
\mathbf{o}
-symmetric plane convex body and let
L\in\mathcal{L}^{2}
. If
\mathcal{F}:=\{\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{K}\ |\ \mathbf{x}\in L\}
is an NS-lattice arrangement, then the tightness of
\mathcal{F}
is at most one. This is attained if and only if
\mathbf{K}
is a parallelogram with
L
generating a chessboard-like lattice packing of
\mathbf{K}
having tightness one.

Proof:

By Theorem 1 of
[
15
]
, the density
\delta(\mathcal{F})
of
\mathcal{F}
satisfies

\delta(\mathcal{F})\geq\frac{\vol(\mathbf{K})\vol(\mathbf{K}^{\circ})}{16\delta(\mathbf{K}^{\circ})},

(1)

where
\delta(\mathbf{K}^{\circ})
is the density of a densest lattice packing of the polar body
\mathbf{K}^{\circ}
of
\mathbf{K}
. (Recall that
\mathbf{K}^{\circ}:=\{\mathbf{x}\in{\mathbb{E}}^{d}\ |\ \langle\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\rangle\leq 1\ {\rm for\ all}\ \mathbf{y}\in\mathbf{K}\}
.) Thus, using the fact that
\delta(\mathbf{K}^{\circ})\leq 1
and by Mahler’s result (
[
8
]
) according to which
\vol(\mathbf{K})\vol(\mathbf{K}^{\circ})\geq 8
, we obtain that

\delta(\mathcal{F})\geq\frac{1}{2}.

Now, assume that the tightness of
\mathcal{F}
is at least one. Then there is a translate
\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{K}
that does not overlap
\Lambda+\mathbf{K}
. Thus,
L+\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{K}
does not overlap
L+\mathbf{K}
. Since these sets are translates of each other, it follows that
\delta(\mathcal{F})\leq\frac{1}{2}
. Combining it with the previous inequality for
\delta(\mathcal{F})
, we have that
\delta(\mathcal{F})=\frac{1}{2}
. Thus, the results about the
2
-dimensional Mahler Conjecture (
[
8
]
) yield that
\mathbf{K}
is a parallelogram, and that
(L+\mathbf{K})\cup(L+\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{K})={\mathbb{E}}^{2}
. From this an elementary consideration yields that
L
is generated by two linearly independent vertices of
\mathbf{K}
, and also that the tightness of
\mathcal{F}
is one.

Let
L\in\mathcal{L}^{d}
, and let
\mathbf{K}
be an
\mathbf{o}
-symmetric convex body in
{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
. Let
\mathcal{F}:=\{\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{K}\ |\ \mathbf{x}\in L\}
. Then we denote by
T_{ns}(d)
the supremum of the tightness of all NS-lattice arrangements
\mathcal{F}
of all convex bodies
\mathbf{K}
.

Theorem 9

For
d\geq 2
, we have

d-1\leq T_{ns}(d)\leq Cd(1+\log d)

for some universal constant
C>0
.

Proof:

Let
L\in\mathcal{L}^{2}
and let
L^{*}:=\{\mathbf{x}\in{\mathbb{E}}^{d}\ |\ \langle\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\rangle\in\mathbb{Z}\ {\rm for\ all}\ \mathbf{y}\in L\}
be its dual lattice.
It is shown by Makai in
[
15
]
that
L+\mathbf{K}
is non-separable if and only if
L^{*}+\frac{1}{4}\mathbf{K}^{\circ}
is a packing. The latter fact is equivalent to saying that the length of a shortest non-zero vector of
L^{*}
, measured in the norm of
\mathbf{K}^{\circ}
, is at least
\frac{1}{2}
, or in other words,
\lambda_{1}(L^{*},\mathbf{K}^{\circ})\geq\frac{1}{2}
, where
\lambda_{1}(L^{*},\mathbf{K}^{\circ})
denotes the first successive minimum of
L^{*}
with respect to
\mathbf{K}^{\circ}
(
[
3
]
). On the other hand, if
\mu(L,\mathbf{K})
denotes the covering radius of the lattice arrangement
L+\mathbf{K}
, i.e., the smallest value
\mu
such that
L+\mu\mathbf{K}
covers
{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
, then by the fact that
(L^{*})^{*}=L
, we have

\lambda_{1}(L^{*},\mathbf{K}^{\circ})\cdot\mu(L,\mathbf{K})\leq C^{\prime}d(1+\log d)

for some universal constant
C^{\prime}>0
independent of
\mathbf{K}
,
L
and
d
.
This inequality was proved by Banaszczyk in
[
3
, Corollary 1]
. Combining this inequality with the previous observations it follows that
T_{ns}(d)\leq Cd(1+\log d)
for some universal constant
C>0
.

To prove the lower bound we observe that if
L:=\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and
\mathbf{K}:=\frac{1}{2}\conv\{\pm\mathbf{e}_{1},\pm\mathbf{e}_{2},\ldots,\pm\mathbf{e}_{d}\}
, where the vectors
\mathbf{e}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{e}_{d}
are the standard basis vectors, then
L+\mathbf{K}
contains all coordinate axes, and thus it is an NS-lattice arrangement. We further note that the smallest value of
\mu
such that
L+\mu\mathbf{K}
is a covering of
{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
is clearly
d
.

4
Minimal coverings of weakly non-separable families

To introduce our new result, we require a notion that was originally defined for simplices by Akopyan, Balitskiy, and Grigorev in
[
1
]
. We extend this concept to general convex polytopes as follows.

Definition 1

Let
\mathbf{P}
be a
d
-dimensional convex polytope in
{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
and let
\mathcal{P}:=\{\mathbf{x}_{i}+\tau_{i}\mathbf{P}\ |\ \mathbf{x}_{i}\in{\mathbb{E}}^{d},\tau_{i}>0,i=1,2,\ldots,n\}
, where
d\geq 2
and
n\geq 2
.
We call
\mathcal{P}
a
weakly non-separable family
, in short, a
WNS-family
, if every hyperplane parallel to some facet of
\mathbf{P}
and intersecting
\conv\left(\bigcup\mathcal{P}\right)
intersects a member of
\mathcal{P}
in
{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
, i.e., there is no hyperplane parallel to some facet of
\mathbf{P}
that strictly separates some elements of
\mathcal{P}
from all the other elements of
\mathcal{P}
in
{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
. Then, let
\Lambda(\mathcal{P})>0
denote the smallest positive value
\lambda
such that a translate of

\lambda\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\tau_{i}\right)\mathbf{P}
covers
\bigcup\mathcal{P}
.

Clearly, every NS-family of finitely many positive homothetic copies of an arbitrary
d
-dimensional convex polytope is a WNS-family in
{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
, but not necessarily the other way around. Thus, the following theorem is a strengthening of Theorem
3
for centrally symmetric convex polytopes.

Theorem 10

For all
d\geq 2
and
n\geq 2
, and for every
\mathbf{o}
-symmetric
d
-dimensional convex polytope
\mathbf{P}_{0}
in in
{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
and every WNS-family
\mathcal{P}:=\{\mathbf{x}_{i}+\tau_{i}\mathbf{P}_{0}\ |\ \mathbf{x}_{i}\in{\mathbb{E}}^{d},\tau_{i}>0,i=1,2,\ldots,n\}
the inequality
\Lambda(\mathcal{P})\leq 1
holds.

Remark 2

The upper bound
1
in Theorem
10
is a sharp one. Namely, equality can be attained for an
o
-symmetric
d
-dimensional convex polytope
\mathbf{P}_{0}
if the centers of the non-overlapping homothetic copies of
\mathcal{P}
lie on a line in
{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
such that any two consecutive homothetic copies touch each other.

Proof:
We follow the proof of Theorem
3
in
[
4
]
. So, we start with the following statement from
[
9
]
.

Lemma 1

Let
\mathcal{F}:=\left\{[x_{i}-\tau_{i},x_{i}+\tau_{i}]|\tau_{i}>0,i=1,2,\ldots,n\right\}
be a family of closed intervals in
\mathbb{R}
such that
\bigcup\mathcal{F}
is a single closed interval in
\mathbb{R}
. Let
x:=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\tau_{i}x_{i}\right)/\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\tau_{i}\right)
. Then the closed interval

\left[x-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\tau_{i},x+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\tau_{i}\right]

covers
\bigcup\mathcal{F}
.

Next, let
\mathbf{x}:=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\tau_{i}\mathbf{x}_{i}\right)/\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\tau_{i}\right)
, and set
\mathbf{P}^{\prime}:=\mathbf{x}+\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\tau_{i}\right)\mathbf{P}_{0}
. We prove that
\mathbf{P}^{\prime}
covers
\bigcup\mathcal{P}
. For any line
L
through the origin
\mathbf{o}
, let
\proj_{L}:{\mathbb{E}}^{d}\to L
denote the orthogonal projection onto
L
, and let
h_{\mathcal{P}}:\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\to\mathbb{R}
and
h_{\mathbf{P}^{\prime}}:\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\to\mathbb{R}
denote the support functions of
\conv\left(\bigcup\mathcal{P}\right)
and
\mathbf{P}^{\prime}
, respectively (for the definition of support function, see
[
18
]
).
If
L
is orthogonal to any facet of
\mathbf{P}_{0}
, then
\proj_{L}\left(\bigcup\mathcal{P}\right)
is a single interval, which, by Lemma
1
, is covered by
\proj_{L}\left(\mathbf{P}^{\prime}\right)
.
Thus, for any
\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
which is orthogonal to some facet of
\mathbf{P}_{0}
, we have that
h_{\mathcal{P}}(\mathbf{u})\leq h_{\mathbf{P}^{\prime}}(\mathbf{u})
. Since every
d
-dimensional convex polytope is the intersection of its closed supporting halfspaces bounded by the hyperplanes of its facets in
{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
, it readily follows that every
\mathbf{x}_{i}+\tau_{i}\mathbf{P}_{0}
is covered by
\mathbf{P}^{\prime}
and therefore
\bigcup\mathcal{P}\subseteq\mathbf{P}^{\prime}
, finishing the proof of Theorem
10
.

The following theorem is an extension of Theorem
1
to WNS-families of convex polytopes. In order to state it we need the following notation.

Definition 2

For
d\geq 2
, let
\Lambda_{\rm simplex}^{(d)}
denote the supremum of
\Lambda(\mathcal{K})
, where
\mathcal{K}
runs over the WNS-families of finitely many positive homothetic
d
-simplices in
{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
.

Remark 3

We note that
\Lambda_{\rm simplex}^{(d)}
is a non-decreasing sequence of
d
. Indeed, let
\mathcal{K}
be a WNS-family of finitely many
(d-1)
-simplices in a hyperplane of
{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
. Clearly, we can extend the elements of
\mathcal{K}
to homothetic
d
-simplices such that each element is a facet of its extension. Then, denoting this extended family by
{\mathcal{K}}^{\prime}
, we have
\Lambda(\mathcal{K})=\Lambda({\mathcal{K}}^{\prime})
, and
{\mathcal{K}}^{\prime}
is a WNS-family.

Theorem 11

For all
d\geq 2
,
\Lambda_{\rm simplex}^{(d)}=\sup_{\mathcal{P}}\Lambda(\mathcal{P})
, where
\mathcal{P}
runs over the WNS-families of finitely many positive homothetic copies of an arbitrary
d
-dimensional convex polytope
\mathbf{P}
in
{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
.

Proof:
We shall need the following concept.

Definition 3

We call a
d
-dimensional convex polytope a
d
-dimensional generic convex polytope in
{\mathbb{E}}^{d}

if the outer unit normal vectors of any
d
of its facets are linearly independent in
{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
,
d\geq 2
.

Lemma 2

If
\mathcal{P}
(resp.,
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
) runs over all WNS-families of finitely many positive homothetic copies of an arbitrary
d
-dimensional convex polytope
\mathbf{P}
(resp., of an arbitrary
d
-dimensional generic convex polytope
\mathbf{P}^{\prime}
) in
{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
, then
\sup_{\mathcal{P}}\Lambda(\mathcal{P})=\sup_{\mathcal{P}^{\prime}}\Lambda(\mathcal{P}^{\prime})
.

Proof:
Clearly,
\sup_{\mathcal{P}}\Lambda(\mathcal{P})\geq\sup_{\mathcal{P}^{\prime}}\Lambda(\mathcal{P}^{\prime})
. So, we are left to show that
\sup_{\mathcal{P}}\Lambda(\mathcal{P})\leq\sup_{\mathcal{P}^{\prime}}\Lambda(\mathcal{P}^{\prime})
. We do it as follows. Let
\mathcal{P}:=\{\mathbf{x}_{i}+\tau_{i}\mathbf{P}\ |\ \mathbf{x}_{i}\in{\mathbb{E}}^{d},\tau_{i}>0,i=1,2,\ldots,n\}
be an arbitrary WNS-family of an arbitrary
d
-dimensional convex polytope
\mathbf{P}
in
{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
. Without loss of generality we may assume that
\tau_{1}={\rm min}\{\tau_{i}\ |\ 1\leq i\leq n\}
and
\mathbf{x}_{1}=\mathbf{o}\in{\rm int}(\tau_{1}\mathbf{P})
. Let
\mathbf{P}_{1}:=\tau_{1}\mathbf{P}
. It will be convenient to use the notation
X_{\delta}:=\cup\{\mathbf{B}^{d}[\mathbf{x},\delta]\ |\ \mathbf{x}\in X\}
for any set
X\subseteq{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
, where
\mathbf{B}^{d}[\mathbf{x},\delta]:=\{\mathbf{y}\in{\mathbb{E}}^{d}\ |\|\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{x}\|\leq\delta\}
. Furthermore, for any
0<\delta^{\prime}<\frac{\pi}{2}
, let
f(\delta^{\prime})\geq 0
be the smallest nonnegative real with the property that for any two hyperplanes
H
and
H^{\prime}
of
{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
having unit normal vectors with angle at most
\delta^{\prime}
and passing through an arbitrary common point of
{\rm conv}\left(\cup\mathcal{P}\right)
the following containment holds:
H\cap{\rm conv}\left(\cup\mathcal{P}\right)\subset H^{\prime}_{f(\delta^{\prime})}
. We note that
\lim_{\delta^{\prime}\to 0^{+}}f(\delta^{\prime})=0
. The driving force of our proof is the following observation whose easy proof we leave to the reader. (See Figure
1
.)

Figure 1:
An illustration for Proposition
1
. Panel (a): An illustration for the definition of

. Panel (b): The notation in Proposition

. The sets
\bd(\mathbf{P}_{1})

Proposition 1

For every
\epsilon>0
consider the convex polytope
\mathbf{P}_{1}^{1+\epsilon}:=(1+\epsilon)\mathbf{P}_{1}
. Then there exist a
d
-dimensional generic convex polytope
\mathbf{P}_{1}^{\prime}
and
\delta
and
\delta^{\prime}
with
0<\delta^{\prime}<\delta<\epsilon
such that the following holds.

(a)

There is a one-to-one correspondence between the facets of
\mathbf{P}_{1}^{\prime}
and
\mathbf{P}_{1}
with the property that if the facet
F^{\prime}
of
\mathbf{P}_{1}^{\prime}
with outer unit normal vector
\mathbf{u}^{\prime}
corresponds to the facet
F
of
\mathbf{P}_{1}
with outer unit normal vector
\mathbf{u}
, then
\angle(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{u}^{\prime})\leq\delta^{\prime}
, where
\angle(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{u}^{\prime})
stands for the angle between
\mathbf{u}
and
\mathbf{u}^{\prime}
.

(b)

{\rm bd}\ \mathbf{P}_{1}^{\prime}\subset\left({\rm bd}\ \mathbf{P}_{1}^{1+\epsilon}\right)_{\delta}.

(c)

\left({\rm bd}\ \mathbf{P}_{1}^{1+\epsilon}\right)_{\delta+f(\delta^{\prime})}\cap\mathbf{P}_{1}=\emptyset.

Finally, for
i=2,\dots,n
let
\mathbf{P}_{i}^{\prime}:=\mathbf{x}_{i}+\frac{\tau_{i}}{\tau_{1}}\mathbf{P}_{1}^{\prime}
be a larger positive homothetic copy of the generic convex polytope
\mathbf{P}_{1}^{\prime}
. Based on Proposition
1
it is easy to check that since
\mathcal{P}
is a WNS-family therefore
\mathcal{P}_{\epsilon}:=\{\mathbf{P}_{i}^{\prime}\ |\ i=1,2,\ldots,n\}
is a WNS-family as well. Moreover, by introducing the generic convex polytope

we see that

. Finally, the observation that
\mathbf{P}_{\epsilon}
(resp.,

) converges to
\mathbf{P}
(resp.,
\mathcal{P}
) as

implies in a straightforward way that
\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}\Lambda(\mathcal{P}_{\epsilon})=\Lambda(\mathcal{P})
. This completes the proof of Lemma
2
.

Thus, we are left to prove that
\Lambda_{\rm simplex}^{(d)}=\sup_{\mathcal{P}^{\prime}}\Lambda(\mathcal{P}^{\prime})
holds, where
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
runs over all WNS-families of finitely many positive homothetic copies of an arbitrary
d
-dimensional generic convex polytope
\mathbf{P}^{\prime}
in
{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
. As a first step, we show the following stronger version of Lutwak’s containment theorem
[

.

Lemma 3

Let
\mathbf{P}
be a
d
-dimensional generic convex polytope in
{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
and let
\mathbf{K}
be a convex body in
{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
. Assume that every
d
-dimensional simplex which is the intersection of the supporting halfspaces of some
d+1
facets of
\mathbf{P}
contains a translate of
\mathbf{K}
. Then
\mathbf{P}
contains a translate of
\mathbf{K}
.

Proof:
We follow the method of
[
14
]
. Let the outer unit normal vectors of the facets of
\mathbf{P}
be
\mathbf{u}_{1},\mathbf{u}_{2},\dots,\mathbf{u}_{s}
. Moreover, for each
i
with
1\leq i\leq s
, let
H_{i}^{+}:=\{\mathbf{x}\in{\mathbb{E}}^{d}|\langle\mathbf{x},\mathbf{u}_{i}\rangle\leq h_{\mathbf{P}}(\mathbf{u}_{i})\}
be the closed supporting halfspace of
\mathbf{P}
bounded by the hyperplane of the facet with outer unit normal vector
\mathbf{u}_{i}
, where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
stands for the standard inner product of
{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
and
h_{\mathbf{P}}(\cdot)
denotes the support function of
\mathbf{P}
. Furthermore, let

\mathbf{K}_{i}:=\{\mathbf{x}\in{\mathbb{E}}^{d}|\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{K}\subset H_{i}^{+}\}.

Clearly, each
\mathbf{K}_{i}
is a closed halfspace in
{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
. Next, let
\mathbf{K}_{i_{1}},\mathbf{K}_{i_{2}},\dots,\mathbf{K}_{i_{d+1}}
be any
d+1
members of the family
\{\mathbf{K}_{1},\mathbf{K}_{2},\dots,\mathbf{K}_{s}\}
. As any
d
of the vectors
\mathbf{u}_{i_{1}},\mathbf{u}_{i_{2}},\dots,\mathbf{u}_{i_{d+1}}
are linearly independent, there are two possibilities: either that
\bigcap_{j=1}^{d+1}H_{i_{j}}^{+}
contains a translate of a Euclidean ball of radius
r
for all
r>0
, or that
\bigcap_{j=1}^{d+1}H_{i_{j}}^{+}
is a simplex containing
\mathbf{P}
. In the first case there exists
\mathbf{x}_{0}\in{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
such that
\mathbf{x}_{0}+\mathbf{K}\subseteq\bigcap_{j=1}^{d+1}H_{i_{j}}^{+}
. In the second case, the hypothesis of Lemma
3
guarantees an
\mathbf{x}_{0}\in{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
such that
\mathbf{x}_{0}+\mathbf{K}\subseteq\bigcap_{j=1}^{d+1}H_{i_{j}}^{+}
. Thus, in either case
\mathbf{x}_{0}\in\bigcap_{j=1}^{d+1}\mathbf{K}_{i_{j}}
, and Helly’s theorem (
[
18
]
) shows that there exists
\mathbf{x}\in{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
such that
\mathbf{x}\in\bigcap_{i=1}^{s}\mathbf{K}_{i}
. Hence,
\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{K}\subseteq\bigcap_{i=1}^{s}H_{i}^{+}=\mathbf{P}
, finishing the proof of Lemma

.

Now, we proceed with the proof of Theorem
11
as follows. Let
\mathcal{P}:=\{\mathbf{x}_{i}+\tau_{i}\mathbf{P}\ |\ \mathbf{x}_{i}\in{\mathbb{E}}^{d},\tau_{i}>0,i=1,2,\ldots,n\}
be an arbitrary WNS-family of the

-dimensional generic convex polytope
\mathbf{P}
in
{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
. Moreover, let
\Delta(\mathbf{P})
be any circumscribed
d
-dimensional simplex of
\mathbf{P}
, that is, let
\Delta(\mathbf{P})
be a
d
-dimensional simplex which the intersection of the supporting halfspaces of some

facets of
\mathbf{P}
. Then
\Lambda_{\rm simplex}^{(d)}(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\tau_{i})\Delta(\mathbf{P})
is a circumscribed simplex of
\Lambda_{\rm simplex}^{(d)}(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\tau_{i})\mathbf{P}
and
\mathbf{x}_{i}+\tau_{i}\Delta(\mathbf{P})
is a circumscribed simplex of
\mathbf{x}_{i}+\tau_{i}\mathbf{P}
for all

. Furthermore,
\{\mathbf{x}_{i}+\tau_{i}\Delta(\mathbf{P})\ |\ \mathbf{x}_{i}\in{\mathbb{E}}^{d},\tau_{i}>0,i=1,2,\ldots,n\}
is a WNS-family of the

\Delta(\mathbf{P})
in
{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
. Hence,
\Lambda_{\rm simplex}^{(d)}(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\tau_{i})\Delta(\mathbf{P})

\bigcup\{\mathbf{x}_{i}+\tau_{i}\Delta(\mathbf{P})\ |\ \mathbf{x}_{i}\in{\mathbb{E}}^{d},\tau_{i}>0,i=1,2,\ldots,n\}\supseteq\bigcup\mathcal{P}
. Thus,
\Lambda_{\rm simplex}^{(d)}(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\tau_{i})\Delta(\mathbf{P})
has a translate that covers the convex body
\conv\left(\bigcup\{\mathbf{x}_{i}+\tau_{i}\Delta(\mathbf{P})\ |\ \mathbf{x}_{i}\in{\mathbb{E}}^{d},\tau_{i}>0,i=1,2,\ldots,n\}\right)\supseteq\conv\left(\bigcup\mathcal{P}\right)
. Since
\Lambda_{\rm simplex}^{(d)}(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\tau_{i})\mathbf{P}
has the same outer unit normal vectors as
\mathbf{P}
and
\Delta(\mathbf{P})
was an arbitrary circumscribed
d
-dimensional simplex of
\mathbf{P}
, Lemma
3
implies in a straghforward way that
\Lambda_{\rm simplex}^{(d)}(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\tau_{i})\mathbf{P}
has a translate that covers
\conv\left(\bigcup\mathcal{P}\right)\supseteq\bigcup\mathcal{P}
. This shows that
\Lambda_{\rm simplex}^{(d)}\geq\sup_{\mathcal{P}}\Lambda(\mathcal{P})
, where
\mathcal{P}
runs over the WNS-families of finitely many positive homothetic copies of an arbitrary
d
-dimensional convex polytope
\mathbf{P}
in
{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
. As the inequality
\Lambda_{\rm simplex}^{(d)}\leq\sup_{\mathcal{P}}\Lambda(\mathcal{P})
holds trivially, the proof of Theorem

is complete.

Finally, recall the following elegant result of Akopyan, Balitskiy, and Grigorev

1
]
.

Theorem 12
(Akopyan, Balitskiy, and Grigorev, 2018)

If
\mathcal{T}:=\{\mathbf{x}_{i}+\tau_{i}\mathbf{T}\ |\ \mathbf{x}_{i}\in{\mathbb{E}}^{d},\tau_{i}>0,i=1,2,\ldots,n\}
is an arbitrary WNS-family of the

Clearly, Theorem

implies the inequality

Theorem 13

If
\mathcal{P}:=\{\mathbf{x}_{i}+\tau_{i}\mathbf{P}\ |\ \mathbf{x}_{i}\in{\mathbb{E}}^{d},\tau_{i}>0,i=1,2,\ldots,n\}
is an arbitrary WNS-family of the

Stability of minimal coverings of WNS-families

In this section we prove a stability version of Theorem

. In order to state it, recall that for convex bodies
\mathbf{K},\mathbf{L}\in{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
, their Banach-Mazur distance

is defined by

d_{BM}(\mathbf{K},\mathbf{L}):=\inf\{\lambda>0\ |\ \mathbf{K}+\mathbf{u}\subseteq T(\mathbf{L}+\mathbf{v})\subseteq\lambda(\mathbf{K}+\mathbf{u}\},

where the infimum is taken over all invertible linear operators
T:{\mathbb{E}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
and all vectors
\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\in{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
.

Theorem 14

Let
0<\varepsilon<\frac{1}{16(1+d)}
. Let
\mathcal{P}:=\{\mathbf{x}_{i}+\tau_{i}\mathbf{P}\ |\ \mathbf{x}_{i}\in{\mathbb{E}}^{d},\tau_{i}>0,i=1,2,\ldots,n\}
be a WNS-family of positive homothetic copies of a
d
-dimensional convex polytope
\mathbf{P}\subset{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
. Assume that the smallest positive homothetic copy of
\mathbf{P}
covering
\bigcup\mathcal{P}
has homothety ratio greater than or equal to
\left(\frac{d+1}{2}-\varepsilon\right)\sum_{i=1}^{n}\tau_{i}
. Then
d_{BM}(\mathbf{P},\mathbf{S})\leq 1+16(d+1)\varepsilon
holds for all
d
-dimensional simplices
\mathbf{S}\subset{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
.

For the proof we use the following parameter of asymmetry.

Definition 4

Let
\mathbf{K}\subset{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
be a convex body. The quantity

\sigma(\mathbf{K}):=\min_{\mathbf{q}\in\inter(\mathbf{K})}\min\left\{\mu>0\ |\ \mathbf{K}-\mathbf{q}\subseteq-\mu(\mathbf{K}-\mathbf{q})\right\}

is called the
Minkowski measure of asymmetry
of
\mathbf{K}
.

It is well known (Lemma 2.2 in
[
1
]
) that for any convex body
\mathbf{K}\subset{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
,
\sigma(\mathbf{K})\leq d
. The following stronger form of this observation is proved by Guo in

Theorem 15
(Guo, 2005)

Let
0<\varepsilon<\frac{1}{8(1+d)}
. Let
\mathbf{K}\subset{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
be a convex body. If
\sigma(\mathbf{K})\geq d-\varepsilon
, then
d_{BM}(\mathbf{K},\mathbf{S})\leq 1+8(d+1)\varepsilon
holds for all
d
-dimensional simplices
\mathbf{S}\subset{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
.

Clearly, Theorem
14
follows from Theorem
15
and the following strengthening of Theorem 2.1 from
[
1
]
.

Theorem 16

Let
\mathbf{P}\subset{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
be a convex polytope. Let
\mathcal{P}:=\{\mathbf{x}_{i}+\tau_{i}\mathbf{P}\ |\ \mathbf{x}_{i}\in{\mathbb{E}}^{d},\tau_{i}>0,i=1,2,\ldots,n\}
be a WNS-family of homothetic copies of
\mathbf{P}

Proof:

We follow the proof of Theorem 2.1 in
[
1
]
. First, we remark that it is an elementary exercise to show that for any convex body
\mathbf{K}\subset{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
with
\mathbf{o}\in{\rm int}\ \mathbf{K}

where
\mathbf{Q}^{\circ}:=\{\mathbf{x}\in{\mathbb{E}}^{d}\ |\ \langle\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\rangle\leq 1\ {\rm for\ all}\ \mathbf{y}\in\mathbf{Q}\}
denotes the polar of the convex body
\mathbf{Q}
with
\mathbf{o}\in{\rm int}\ \mathbf{Q}

we may assume that

Let
\mathbf{x}:=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\tau_{i}\mathbf{x}_{i}}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\tau_{i}}

\mathbf{x}+\frac{\sigma+1}{2}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\tau_{i}\right)\mathbf{P}
. Since
\mathbf{P}
is a convex polytope, we may assume that
H
is parallel to a facet of
\mathbf{P}
.

Let
\pi:{\mathbb{E}}^{d}\to L
be the orthogonal projection onto a line

perpendicular to
H
. Assume that the projection of
\mathbf{o}
divides the projection

in the ratio
1:s
, where
s\geq 1
. As
\mathbf{P}^{\circ}\subseteq-\sigma\mathbf{P}^{\circ}
, we have
s\in[1,\sigma]
.
In the following, we regard the line

as the real line

. Let

. Observe that with this notation we have (using a suitable orientation of

and
I=[a,b]:=\pi\left(\mathbf{x}+\frac{\sigma+1}{2}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\tau_{i}\right)\mathbf{P}\right)
. Note that
I
is of length
\frac{\sigma+1}{2}L

Let
c_{i}^{\prime}:=\frac{a_{i}+b_{i}}{2}

\mathcal{P}
is a WNS-family, the union of the segments

is a closed interval in
\mathbb{R}
. Thus the segment

of length

covers

On translative WNS-families of cubes

In this section we intend to investigate isoperimetric-type problems for translative WNS-families of the unit cube
\mathbf{C}_{d}:=[0,1]^{d}
in
{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
.
During this investigation, we call a family
\mathcal{F}
of translates of
\mathbf{C}_{d}
an

if every vertex of every member of
\mathcal{F}
is a point of the integer grid
\mathbb{Z}^{d}

Let
\emptyset\neq A\subset{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
be a compact convex set, and
1\leq i\leq d
. We denote the
i
-th
intrinsic volume of

by

. It is well known that
V_{d}(A)
is the

2V_{d-1}(A)
is the surface area of

\frac{2\omega_{d-1}}{d\omega_{d}}V_{1}(A)

\omega_{d}:=V_{d}(\mathbf{B}^{d})=\frac{\pi^{\frac{d}{2}}}{\Gamma(1+\frac{d}{2})}
with
\mathbf{B}^{d}:=\{\mathbf{x}\in{\mathbb{E}}^{d}\ |\|\mathbf{x}\|\leq 1\}
. For properties of intrinsic volumes, including Steiner’s formula

V_{d}\left(A+\epsilon\mathbf{B}^{d}\right)=\sum_{i=0}^{d}\omega_{d-i}V_{i}(A)\epsilon^{d-i}
, where
\epsilon>0
and
V_{0}(A):=1
, we refer the interested reader to
[
18
]
. Our main result is the following.

Theorem 17

Let
n\geq 1
, and
1\leq i\leq d
be integers. Then the following holds.

(i)

\mathcal{F}
of
n
translates of
\mathbf{C}_{d}

over the set of all WNS-families of
n
translates of
\mathbf{C}_{d}

Definition 5

1\leq i,j\leq n
such that
\emptyset\neq(\mathbf{x^{\prime}},\mathbf{y^{\prime}})\subsetneq[\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}]
and
(\mathbf{x^{\prime}},\mathbf{y^{\prime}})\cap\left(\bigcup\mathcal{P}\right)=\emptyset
, where
(\mathbf{x^{\prime}},\mathbf{y^{\prime}})
denotes the open line segment with endpoints
\mathbf{x^{\prime}}
and
\mathbf{y^{\prime}}
. Without loss of generality we may assume that as we move from
\mathbf{x}
towards
\mathbf{y}
first we visit
\mathbf{x^{\prime}}
and then
\mathbf{y^{\prime}}
. Furthermore, let
H^{+}
(resp.,
H
) be a supporting halfspace (resp., supporting hyperplane) of
\mathbf{Q}
with outer unit normal vector
\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
such that
F(\mathbf{Q},\mathbf{u})=H\cap\mathbf{K}=[\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}]
(see Figure
3
).

Figure 3:
Every edge of
\mathbf{Q}=\conv\left(\bigcup\mathcal{P}\right)
is covered by
\bigcup\mathcal{P}
.

Now, let
\mathbf{T}_{\mathbf{x}_{i}+\tau_{i}\mathbf{P}}(\mathbf{x^{\prime}})
be the tangent cone of
\mathbf{x}_{i}+\tau_{i}\mathbf{P}
at the vertex
\mathbf{x^{\prime}}
, which is the union of all closed halflines starting at
\mathbf{x^{\prime}}
and passing through the points of
\mathbf{x}_{i}+\tau_{i}\mathbf{P}
. Clearly,
\mathbf{T}_{\mathbf{x}_{i}+\tau_{i}\mathbf{P}}(\mathbf{x^{\prime}})
is a
d
-dimensional convex polyhedral cone with apex
\mathbf{x^{\prime}}
. By assumption, there is a facet
F
of
\mathbf{T}_{\mathbf{x}_{i}+\tau_{i}\mathbf{P}}(\mathbf{x^{\prime}})
whose hyperplane
H_{F}
separates
(\mathbf{x^{\prime}},\mathbf{y^{\prime}})
from
\mathbf{T}_{\mathbf{x}_{i}+\tau_{i}\mathbf{P}}(\mathbf{x^{\prime}})
with
\mathbf{x^{\prime}}\in F\subsetneq H_{F}
. It follows that
L^{\prime}:=H\cap H_{F}
is a
(d-2)
-dimensional affine subspace in
{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
with the property that
L^{\prime}\cap\mathbf{Q}=\{\mathbf{x^{\prime}}\}
. Finally, let
\mathbf{z}\in{\rm relint(\mathbf{x^{\prime}},\mathbf{y^{\prime}})}
and let
L
be the translate of
L^{\prime}
that passes through
\mathbf{z}
. Clearly,
L\cap\mathbf{Q}=\{\mathbf{z}\}
and therefore
L\cap\left(\bigcup\mathcal{P}\right)=\emptyset
. As by assumption
\mathcal{P}
is a
(d-2)
-WIP-family therefore we have arrived at a contradiction. This completes the proof of Lemma
5
.

Finally, we finish the proof of Theorem
18
as follows. Let
H^{+}
(resp.,
H
) be a supporting halfspace (resp., supporting hyperplane) of
\mathbf{Q}
with outer unit normal vector
\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
such that
F(\mathbf{Q},\mathbf{u})=H\cap\mathbf{K}=[\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}]
is an edge of
\mathbf{Q}={\rm conv}\left(\bigcup\mathcal{P}\right)
. As by assumption
\mathcal{P}
is a
(d-2)
-WIP-family therefore Lemma
5
implies in a straightforward way that the edge
[\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}]
can be translated into the parallel edge of
\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\tau_{i}\right)\mathbf{P}
with supporting halfspace
H^{+}
(resp., supporting hyperplane
H
) of outer unit normal vector
\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
. Thus, Lemma
4
yields that
\mathbf{Q}
slides freely in
\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\tau_{i}\right)\mathbf{P}
(i.e.,
\mathbf{Q}
is a summand of
\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\tau_{i}\right)\mathbf{P}
), finishing the proof of
Theorem
18
.

8
Weakly
k
-impassable lattice arrangements

As in the case of non-separability, it is a natural problem to extend the definition of
k
-impassable families for lattice arrangements. In the following, we say that a lattice arrangement of translates of a convex polytope
\mathbf{P}
is
weakly
k
-impassable
if every
k
-dimensional affine subspace parallel to a facet of
\mathbf{P}
intersects a member of the arrangement. If
k=d-1
, we may call the arrangement
weakly non-separable
.
Following the paper
[
13
]
of Kannan and Lovász, one can define covering minima type quantities for weakly
k
-impassable lattice arrangements of a
d
-dimensional convex polytope for
0\leq k\leq d-1
.

Definition 6

Let
\mathbf{P}
be a
d
-dimensional convex polytope in
{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
, and let
L\in\mathcal{L}^{d}
be a lattice in
{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
. For
i=1,\ldots,d
we define the
i
th weak covering minimum
of
\mathbf{P}
with respect to
L
as the quantity

\mu_{i}^{w}(\mathbf{P},L)=\inf\{t>0|L+t\mathbf{P}\hbox{ intersects every }(d-i)-\hbox{dimensional affine subspace parallel to a facet of }\mathbf{P}\}.

Note that we have
\mu_{i}^{w}(\mathbf{P},L)\leq\mu_{j}^{w}(\mathbf{P},L)
for every convex polytope
\mathbf{P}
, lattice
L
, and
1\leq i\leq j\leq d
, and that
\mu_{d}^{w}(\mathbf{P},L)
coincides with the covering radius of the arrangement.

It is a reasonable question to ask how small the density of a weakly
k
-impassable lattice arrangement of translates of a convex polytope can be. The following result shows that, unlike for non-separable arrangements, for weakly non-separable arrangements it can be arbitrarily small.

Theorem 19

Let
d\geq 2
. Then there is an
\mathbf{o}
-symmetric
d
-dimensional convex polytope
\mathbf{P}\subset{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
such that
\mu_{1}^{w}(\mathbf{P},\mathbb{Z}^{d})=0
.

For the proof we need the following form of the well-known theorem of Kronecker from 1884
[
12
, Theorem 442]
.

Theorem 20
(Kronecker, 1884)

If
\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\ldots,\theta_{m}
are linearly independent irrational numbers over the field
\mathbb{Q}
,
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\ldots,\alpha_{m}\in\mathbb{R}
, and
N
and
\varepsilon
are positive, then there are integers
n>N,p_{1},p_{2},\ldots,p_{m}
such that

\left|n\theta_{i}-p_{i}-\alpha_{i}\right|<\varepsilon

for
i=1,2,\ldots,m
.

Theorem
20
readily implies the following.

Corollary 1

Let
\mathbf{v}=(\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\ldots,\theta_{d})\in{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
such that the coordinates of
\mathbf{v}
are linearly independent over
\mathbb{Q}
. Let
L
be the line
L=\mathbb{R}\mathbf{v}
. Then the set
\mathbb{Z}^{d}+L
is everywhere dense in
{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
.

Proof:

By Theorem
20
, the set
\mathbb{Z}\mathbf{v}+\mathbb{Z}^{d}
is everywhere dense in
{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
, immediately implying Corollary
1
.

Now we are ready to prove Theorem
19
.

Proof:

Choose linearly independent vectors
\mathbf{v}_{1},\mathbf{v}_{2},\ldots,\mathbf{v}_{d}
in
{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
such that for every value of
i
, the coordinates of
\mathbf{v}_{i}
are linearly independent over
\mathbb{Q}
. Let
\mathbf{P}
be an
\mathbf{o}
-symmmetric
d
-dimensional parallelotope whose edges are parallel to the
\mathbf{v}_{i}
. Then, by Corollary
1
, the family

\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}=\{\mathbf{n}+\varepsilon\mathbf{P}\ |\ \mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\}

intersects every line parallel to an edge of
\mathbf{P}
. Thus, it intersects every hyperplane parallel to a facet of
\mathbf{P}
, yielding that it is weakly non-separable.

Our last result shows that a similar statement does not hold for the
i
th weak covering minima of any
d
-dimensional convex polytope
\mathbf{P}
for
2\leq i\leq d
.

Theorem 21

Let
d\geq 2
,
\mathbf{P}
be a
d
-dimensional convex polytope in
{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
and
L\in\mathcal{L}^{d}
be a lattice. Then
\mu_{2}^{w}(\mathbf{P},L)>0
. Furthermore, if
\mathcal{P}=L+\mathbf{P}
is a weakly
(d-2)
-impassable lattice arrangement of translates of
\mathbf{P}
, then its density satisfies

\delta(\mathcal{P})\geq\frac{\vol(\mathbf{P})\vol(\mathbf{P}^{\circ})}{16\delta(\mathbf{P}^{\circ})},

where
\delta(\mathbf{P}^{\circ})
denotes the maximum density of a lattice packing of the polar
\mathbf{P}^{\circ}
of
\mathbf{P}
.

Proof:

To prove the first statement, we prove that every weakly
(d-2)
-impassable lattice arrangement of a
d
-dimensional convex polytope
\mathbf{P}
is non-separable; note that this yields
\mu_{2}^{w}(\mathbf{P},L)\geq\mu_{1}(\mathbf{P},L)>0
for every lattice
L\in\mathcal{L}^{d}
, where
\mu_{1}(\mathbf{P},L)
is the first covering minimum of the lattice arrangement (see
[
13
]
).

To do it, assume that the arrangement
\mathcal{P}=\{v+\mathbf{P}|v\in L\}
is weakly
(d-2)
-impassable for some lattice
L
. Consider an arbitrary hyperplane
H
of
{\mathbb{E}}^{d}
. If
H
is parallel to a facet of
\mathbf{P}
, then by our assumptions
H
intersects an element of
\mathcal{P}
. Thus, we may assume that
H
is not parallel to any facet of
\mathbf{P}
. Then, if any
H^{\prime}
is any hyperplane parallel to a facet of
\mathbf{P}
,
A=H\cap H^{\prime}
is a
(d-2)
-dimensional affine subspace parallel to a facet of
\mathbf{P}
. By our assumptions,
A
intersects an element of
\mathcal{P}
, showing that
\mathcal{P}
is an NS-family.

To prove the second part, we apply Theorem 1 of Makai
[
15
]
to
\mathbf{P}
in a straightforward way.

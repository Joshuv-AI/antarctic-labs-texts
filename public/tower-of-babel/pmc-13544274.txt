Lupus Nephritis Susceptibility Loci in the All of Us Research Cohort
Authors: Mark Dibbs, Christina Jayaraj, Holy Mary Zaher, Mathieu F Bakhoum, Christine Y Bakhoum
Published: 2026

Introduction

Systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE) is an autoimmune disease characterized by loss of tolerance to nuclear antigens.1 More than 25% of individuals with SLE develop lupus nephritis (LN), which is a significant cause of morbidity and mortality.2 Importantly, patients with LN experience higher mortality than those with SLE without renal involvement.3 Thus, identifying patients at increased risk for LN may enable closer surveillance and more timely therapeutic intervention. It is not yet fully understood why some individuals with SLE develop kidney disease and others do not. Previous work has identified many SLE genetic susceptibility loci,4,5 and while some studies have conducted genome-wide associations comparing LN cases to SLE cases without LN,6, 7, 8 none to our knowledge have identified genome-wide significant loci or have included whole genome sequencing (WGS)-based data, and many of these studies are restricted to single-ancestry cohorts. Because only a subset of patients with SLE develop LN and the determinants of who does remain incompletely understood, we hypothesized that additional genetic modifiers of renal risk are likely to exist.

We used data from the All of Us Research Program, a national, longitudinal, multiancestry cohort,9 to perform a case-case genome-wide association study using both array-based data and WGS-based data contrasting participants with SLE who developed nephritis to those who did not. To our knowledge, this represents the first genome-wide association study of lupus nephritis conducted within All of Us.9,S1

A full protocol is provided in the Supplementary Methods.

Results

Cohort Characteristics

Among 3291 participants with SLE in the array-based analysis, 609 had LN and 2682 did not. Among 3075 participants with SLE in the WGS-based analysis, 580 had LN and 2495 did not. Participants with LN tended to be younger at consent and exhibited greater albuminuria, lower C3 level, higher anti-double-stranded DNA titer, and lower eGFR, consistent with more active immunologic disease and clinically meaningful kidney involvement (Supplementary Tables S1 and S2). The array and WGS cohorts are not independent cohorts, as the WGS cohort represents 93.4% of the array cohort. However, we evaluated both array-based and WGS-based data as array-based analyses have more widely accepted threshold values and quality control protocols, whereas WGS allows us to assess regions of the genome that arrays miss. Furthermore, employing both analyses strengthens confidence in associations replicated across platforms.

Genome-Wide Scan and Key Loci

Genomic inflation was minimal, and the quantile-quantile plots were consistent with adequate control of confounding variables (Supplementary Figure S1). The Manhattan plot from the array-based analysis (Figure 1a) revealed 8 suggestive loci (P < 1 x 10-5). The Manhattan plot from the WGS-based analysis (Figure 1b) revealed 2 significant loci (P < 5x10-8) and 10 suggestive loci. We include regional plots around select loci in Supplementary Figure S2. The 2 significant loci localized within 10 kb of the LTBR gene on chromosome 12. Among the suggestive loci, 5 overlapped with those in the array-based analysis, including the signal across HLA class II (Table 1).

We identified 1 protective locus (odds ratio < 1) in the array-based analysis on chromosome 14 (near CLEC14A), and we identified 3 protective loci in the WGS-based analysis.

Fine-Mapping

Fine-mapping with Sum of Single Effects (SuSiE)S2 identified credible sets of variants likely to drive the association. Because the genome-wide significant variants in the WGS-based analysis were in close proximity, they were combined into a single locus for fine-mapping. One credible set for this locus was detected and consisted of 1 variant. Supplementary Table S3 provides a summary of the credible sets for each of the lead variants.

Expression Quantitative Trait Locus Analysis

To explore regulatory mechanisms at these loci, we queried GTExS3 and NephQTLS4 expression quantitative trait locus (eQTL) databases for lead variants. The variant at chromosome 6p21.3 that was suggestive in both analyses exhibited extensive eQTL associations across multiple HLA class II genes in both databases. Genes with significant eQTL associations for all lead variants are listed in Supplementary Table S4, and complete eQTL data are found in Supplementary Tables S5 and S6.

Discussion

Using a multiancestry cohort from All of Us, we identified 2 genome-wide significant loci in the WGS-based analysis of LN, both of which localized to chromosome 12 within 10 kb of LTBR, a gene encoding the lymphotoxin beta receptor. This gene has been implicated in proinflammatory pathways and has been shown to play a role in a mouse model of LN.S5,S6 Fine-mapping narrowed these loci to a single-variant credible set, providing a strong target for mechanistic follow-up.

Among suggestive loci, the variant on 6p21.3 (suggestive in both analyses) and the variant on 6q23 (suggestive in the array-based analysis) were most relevant as they localized to the HLA class II and TNFAIP3 regions, respectively. The HLA class II clump overlapped genes plausibly related to antigen presentation (HLA-DQA1/DQB1/BTNL2 vicinity), consistent with the SLE literature and previous LN-focused studies.S7,S8 In a similar case-case genome-wide association study comparing patients with LN to patients with SLE without renal involvement of European ancestry, Chung et al.6 also detected associations with the major histocompatibility complex on chromosome 6. The 6q23/TNFAIP3 clump is directionally consistent with the established role of this locus in SLE risk and immune pathway regulation and suggests that variants near TNFAIP3 may also contribute to kidney involvement among individuals already diagnosed with SLE.S9–S11 These loci reinforce the central roles of antigen presentation and nuclear factor kappa B regulation in the renal manifestations of SLE risk.5,S12 In a series of genome-wide association studies performed by Chan et al.8 among patients of self-reported Chinese descent, loci within both the major histocompatibility complex and TNFAIP3 met the genome-wide significance threshold in LN versus control and SLE versus control designs, but no loci were significant in a case-case LN versus SLE without renal involvement design. Importantly, in both GTEx and NephQTL databases, querying the variant on 6p21.3 revealed broad eQTL signals across HLA class II genes, supporting a mechanism whereby variants within this region modify expression levels of antigen-presenting genes and may increase susceptibility to LN.

Our identification of a protective signal near CLEC14A introduces a new dimension, pointing toward glomerular endothelial-podocyte interactions as a modifier of renal injury and/or inflammation. CLEC14A encodes an endothelial C-type lectin expressed on vascular and glomerular endothelium and podocytes.S13,S14 It is also involved in microvascular repair and has been shown in murine adriamycin nephropathy to protect against podocyte effacement and albuminuria, supporting its potential as a nephroprotective factor.S13 Collectively, these data suggest that LN susceptibility within SLE reflects the intersection of immune-regulatory variation (LTBR, HLA genes, TNFAIP3) and kidney-intrinsic injury or repair pathways (CLEC14A). Thus, our results both replicate previously identified susceptibility loci (such as the major histocompatibility complex) and identify novel susceptibility loci.

The strengths of our study include the use of a large, diverse cohort with both array-based and WGS-based data, comprehensive covariate adjustment, multiple robustness checks, and the inclusion of eQTL data to strengthen associations. The modest number of LN cases is a limitation. Additionally, because the analysis is based on cross-sectional electronic health record data, we cannot exclude the possibility that some individuals classified as SLE-only may have undiagnosed nephritis or may develop it in the future. Future studies replicating this analysis in independent cohorts and in meta-analyses will be essential to validate loci and clarify genetic contributors to LN.

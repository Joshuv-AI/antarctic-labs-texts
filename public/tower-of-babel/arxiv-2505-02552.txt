Operators Max ⁡ L \Max L and Min ⁡ U \Min U and duals of Boolean posets
Ivan Chajda, Miroslav Kolařík, Helmut Länger

Abstract
When working with posets which are not necessarily lattices, one has a lack of lattice operations which causes problems in algebraic constructions. This is the reason why we use the operators Max ⁡ L \Max L and Min ⁡ U \Min U substituting infimum and supremum, respectively. We axiomatize these operators. Two more operators, namely the so-called symmetric difference and the Sheffer operator, are introduced and studied in complemented posets by using the operators Max ⁡ L \Max L and Min ⁡ U \Min U . In Boolean algebras, the symmetric difference is used to construct its dual structure, the corresponding unitary Boolean ring. By generalizing this idea, we assign to each Boolean poset a so-called dual and prove that also, conversely, a Boolean poset can be derived from its dual.

1
Introduction

When working with lattices, we use the lattice operations
\wedge
and
\vee
, which for couples of entries
x,y
yield their infimum and supremum, respectively. When working with posets, the results of these operations are not defined in some cases. Instead of these operations, in posets one usually uses the operators of the lower cone
L(x,y)
and the upper cone
U(x,y)
, respectively, see e.g.
[
4
]
,
[
6
]
and
[
7
]
. Similarly as in lattices, in posets we have
z\leq x,y
for all
z\in L(x,y)
and
x,y\leq w
for all
w\in U(x,y)
, respectively. However, in the case when
\inf(x,y)
or
\sup(x,y)
exists, it may happen that
\inf(x,y)\neq L(x,y)
and
\sup(x,y)\neq U(x,y)
. This is the reason why we prefer to use the operators
\Max L(x,y)
and
\Min U(x,y)
denoting the set of all maximal elements of the lower cone of
x
and
y
and the set of all minimal elements in the upper cone of
x
and
y
, respectively. Then we have that in case
\inf(x,y)
exists, it coincides with
\Max L(x,y)
, and in case
\sup(x,y)
exists, it coincides with
\Min U(x,y)
, respectively. Hence, the operators
\Max L(x,y)
and
\Min U(x,y)
are closer to lattice operations than the operators
L(x,y)
and
U(x,y)
and this advantage is used in some constructions (cf. e.g. [2]) where congruences in posets are defined by using these operators. We axiomatize the operators
\Max L
and
\Min U
in bounded posets. Obviously, this axiomatization differs much from that of lattice operations since the operators in question do not share such nice properties as e.g. associativity.

When working with Boolean algebras, one often uses the term operation symmetric difference. In the second part of the paper we introduce the operator of symmetric difference and the Sheffer operator also for complemented posets. It is well-known that in Boolean algebras the Sheffer operation turns out to be very useful since by means of this single operation all the operations of the Boolean algebra can be generated. We axiomatize the Sheffer operator on complemented posets and we show how this Sheffer operator can induce the partial order relation, the complementation operation as well as the afore mentioned operators
\Max L
and
\Min U
.

It is well-known that there is a natural one-to-one correspondence between Boolean algebras and unitary Boolean rings, see e.g.
[
2
]
. This correspondence is realized by using the operation of symmetric difference which serves as the addition operation of the unitary Boolean ring. In the last part of the paper we establish a similar construction for Boolean posets by using the operator of symmetric difference.

2
Basic concepts

First we recall some more or less familiar concepts and then we formulate several results needed for our study.

Let
\mathbf{P}=(P,\leq)
be a poset,
a,b\in P
and
A,B\subseteq P
. We say that
\mathbf{P}
satisfies the
Ascending Chain Condition
(
ACC
, for short) or the
Descending Chain Condition
(
DCC
, for short) if
\mathbf{P}
has no infinite ascending or descending chains, respectively. Let
\Max A
and
\Min A
denote the set of all maximal and minimal elements of
A
, respectively. If
\mathbf{P}
satisfies the ACC or the DCC then for every non-empty subset
A
of
P
the set
\Max A
or
\Min A
is non-empty, respectively. We define

\displaystyle A\leq B

\displaystyle\text{ if }x\leq y\text{ for all }x\in A\text{ and all }y\in B,

\displaystyle L(A)

\displaystyle:=\{x\in P\mid x\leq A\},

\displaystyle U(A)

\displaystyle:=\{x\in P\mid A\leq x\}.

Here and in the following we identify singletons with their unique element. Instead of
L(\{a\})
,
L(\{a,b\})
,
L(A\cup\{a\})
,
L(A\cup B)
and
L\big(U(A)\big)
we simply write
L(a)
,
L(a,b)
,
L(A,a)
,
L(A,B)
and
LU(A)
. Analogously we proceed in similar cases.

Hence, by the binary operator
\Max L(x,y)
and
\Min U(x,y)
we mean the mapping assigning to each
(x,y)\in P^{2}
the set of all maximal elements in the lower cone
L(x,y)
of
x
and
y
and the set of all minimal elements in the upper cone
U(x,y)
of
x
and
y
, respectively. Of course, if the poset in question is a lattice then
\Max L(x,y)=x\wedge y
and
\Min U(x,y)=x\vee y
. Thus also in the more general case of a poset,
\Max L(x,y)
and
\Min U(x,y)
should play the role of
x\wedge y
and
x\vee y
, respectively.

The
poset

\mathbf{P}
is called
bounded
if it has a bottom element
0
and a top element
1
. In this case we sometimes write
(P,\leq,0,1)
.

Now we present our basic results.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\mathbf{P}=(P,\leq)
be a poset satisfying the
ACC
and the
DCC
and let
A,B\subseteq P
. Then the following holds:

(i)

If
a\in A
then there exists some
b\in\Min A
and some
c\in\Max A
with
b\leq a\leq c
,

(ii)

L(A)=L(B)
if and only if
\Max L(A)=\Max L(B)
,

(iii)

U(A)=U(B)
if and only if
\Min U(A)=\Min U(B)
,

(iv)

L(\Min A)=L(A)
and
U(\Max A)=U(A)
.

Proof.

(i)

Assume
a\in A
. Put
a_{0}:=a
. If
a_{0}\in\Min A
then put
b:=a_{0}
. Otherwise there exists some
a_{1}\in A
with
a_{1}<a_{0}
. If
a_{1}\in\Min A
then put
b:=a_{1}
. Otherwise there exists some
a_{2}\in A
with
a_{2}<a_{1}
. Since
\mathbf{P}
satisfies the DCC this procedure must stop after a finite number of steps. Hence there exists some
k\geq 0
with
b=a_{k}\in\Min A
and
b\leq a
. The second assertion is dual.

(ii)

If
L(A)=L(B)
then
\Max L(A)=\Max L(B)
. Conversely, assume
\Max L(A)=\Max L(B)
and
a\in L(A)
. According to (i) there exists some
b\in\Max L(A)
with
a\leq b
. Since
\Max L(A)=\Max L(B)
we conclude
b\in\Max L(B)\subseteq L(B)
. This shows
L(A)\subseteq L(B)
. By symmetry we have
L(B)\subseteq L(A)
and hence
L(A)=L(B)
,

(iii)

This is dual to (ii).

(iv)

Since
\Min A\subseteq A
we have
L(A)\subseteq L(\Min A)
. Now assume
d\in L(\Min A)
and
e\in A
. According to (i) there exists some
f\in\Min A
with
f\leq e
. Now
d\leq f\leq e
and hence
d\leq e
showing
d\in L(A)
. This proves
L(\Min A)\subseteq L(A)
. Together we obtain
L(\Min A)=L(A)
. The second assertion is dual.

∎

It is evident that for any subset A of a bounded poset
(P,\leq,0,1)
the sets
L(A)
,
U(A)
,
\Max L(A)
and
\Min U(A)
are non-empty since
0\in L(A)
and
1\in U(A)
.

Two elements
a
and
b
of a bounded poset
(P,\leq,0,1)
are called
complements
of each other if
L(a,b)=0
and
U(a,b)=1
.

A unary operation
′
on a poset
(P,\leq)
is called

•

antitone
if
x,y\in P
and
x\leq y
together imply
y^{\prime}\leq x^{\prime}
,

•

an
involution
if it satisfies the identity
(x^{\prime})^{\prime}\approx x
.

For a bounded poset
\mathbf{P}=(P,\leq,{}^{\prime},0,1)
with an antitone involution
′
we call this mapping
′
a
complementation
and
\mathbf{P}
a
complemented poset
if
x^{\prime}
is a complement of
x
for every
x\in P
.

By a
binary operator

\varphi
on a set
P
we mean a mapping from
P^{2}
to
2^{P}
, i.e. to every couple of elements
x,y
of
P
there is assigned a subset
\varphi(x,y)
of
P
. In the following such an operator is often extended from
P^{2}
to
(2^{P})^{2}
, i.e. to every couple of subsets
A,B
of
P
there is assigned a subset
\varphi(A,B)
of
P
. In this case, elements of
P
are identified with the corresponding one-element subsets of
P
.

3
Axiomatization of the operators
\Max L
and
\Min U

In this section we investigate the operators
\Max L
and
\Min U
on a poset. The aim of this section is to axiomatize these operators.

Definition 3.1
.

An operator structure is an ordered quintuple
(P,\sqcup,\sqcap,0,1)
where
\sqcup
and
\sqcap
are binary operators of
P
and
0,1\in P
and the following conditions are satisfied:

(i)

x\sqcup x\approx x
and
x\sqcap x\approx x
,

(ii)

x\sqcup y\approx y\sqcup x
and
x\sqcap y\approx y\sqcap x
,

(iii)

0\sqcup x\approx x
and
x\sqcap 1=x
,

(iv)

x\sqcup\big((x\sqcup y)\sqcup z\big)\approx 0\sqcup\big((x\sqcup y)\sqcup z\big)
and
x\sqcap\big((x\sqcap y)\sqcap z\big)\approx\big((x\sqcap y)\sqcap z\big)\sqcap 1
,

(v)

(x\sqcap y)\sqcup y\approx y
and
x\sqcap(x\sqcup y)\approx x
,

(vi)

z\in x\sqcup y
if and only if
x\sqcup z=y\sqcup z=z
and if
u\in P
and
x\sqcup u=y\sqcup u=u\sqcap z=u
together imply
u=z
;
z\in x\sqcap y
if and only if
z\sqcap x=z\sqcap y=z
and if
u\in P
and
u\sqcap x=u\sqcap y=z\sqcup u=u
together imply
u=z
.

The conditions (i) – (vi) are not independent. Observe that (i) and (iv) imply (iii) and that (iii) and (v) imply (i).

In what follows, we show that these operator structures can be used for an axiomatization of the operators
\Max L
and
\Min U
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\mathbf{P}=(P,\leq,0,1)
be a bounded poset satisfying the
ACC
and the
DCC
. Then
\mathbb{A}(\mathbf{P}):=(P,\Min U,\Max L,0,1)
is an operator structure.

Proof.

Let
a,b,c\in P
. We check the conditions (i) – (vi) of Definition
3.1
.

(i)

\Min U(a,a)=a
and
\Max L(a,a)=a
,

(ii)

\Min U(a,b)=\Min U(b,a)
and
\Max L(a,b)=\Max L(b,a)
,

(iii)

\Min U(0,a)=a
and
\Max L(a,1)=a
,

(iv)

We have
a\leq u\leq v
for all
u\in\Min U(a,b)
and all
v\in\Min U\big(\Min U(a,b),c\big)
. Since
\Min U(a,b)\neq\emptyset
according to the remark after Lemma
2.1
, we conclude
a\leq v
for all
v\in\Min U\big(\Min U(a,b),c\big)
. Because of
\Min U\big(\Min U(a,b),c\big)\neq\emptyset
according to the remark after Lemma
2.1
we get

\displaystyle\Min U\Big(a,\Min U\big(\Min U(a,b),c\big)\Big)

\displaystyle=\Min U\Big(\Min U\big(\Min U(a,b),c\big)\Big)=

\displaystyle=\Min U\Big(0,\Min U\big(\Min U(a,b),c\big)\Big).

The second identity follows by duality.

(v)

According to Lemma
2.1
we have

\Min U\big(\Max L(a,b),b\big)=\Min U\big(L(a,b),b\big)=\Min U(b)\approx b.

The second identity follows by duality.

(vi)

We have
c\in\Min U(a,b)
if and only if
c\in U(a,b)
and if
c\geq d\in U(a,b)
implies
c=d
.

∎

Also conversely, we show how to obtain a bounded poset with the operators
\Max L
and
\Min U
from a given operator structure.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\mathbf{A}=(P,\sqcup,\sqcap,0,1)
be an operator structure. Define a binary relation
\leq
on
P
by
x\leq y
if
x\sqcup y=y

(
x,y\in P
)
. Then
\mathbb{P}(\mathbf{A}):=(P,\leq,0,1)
is a bounded poset satisfying the following conditions:

(a)

x\leq y
if and only if
x\sqcap y=x
,

(b)

x\sqcup y\approx\Min U(x,y)
,

(c)

x\sqcap y\approx\Max L(x,y)
.

Proof.

Let
a,b,c\in P
. We use the conditions of Definition
3.1
. According to (i),
\leq
is reflexive. If
a\leq b
and
b\leq a
then
a=b\sqcup a=a\sqcup b=b
by (ii) proving antisymmetry of
\leq
. If
a\leq b
and
b\leq c
then

a\sqcup c=a\sqcup(b\sqcup c)=a\sqcup\big((a\sqcup b)\sqcup c)=0\sqcup\big((a\sqcup b)\sqcup c\big)=0\sqcup(b\sqcup c)=0\sqcup c=c

according to (iii) and (iv) proving transitivity of
\leq
. Altogether,
(P,\leq)
is a poset.
Because of (iii),
0
is the bottom element of
(P,\leq)
.

(a)

From (v) we conclude that
a\sqcup b=b
if and only if
a\sqcap b=a
. Due to (iii),
1
is the top element of
(P,\leq)
.

(b)

and (c) follow from (vi).

∎

That the correspondence described in Theorems
3.2
and
3.3
is nearly one-to-one is proved in the next theorem.

Theorem 3.4
.

(i)

If
\mathbf{P}=(P,\leq,0,1)
is a bounded poset satisfying the
ACC
and the
DCC
then
\mathbb{P}\big(\mathbb{A}(\mathbf{P})\big)=\mathbf{P}
.

(ii)

If
\mathbf{A}=(P,\sqcup,\sqcap,0,1)
is a finite operator structure and
\mathbb{A}\big(\mathbb{P}(\mathbf{A})\big)=(P,\sqcup_{1},\sqcap_{1},0,1)
then
x\sqcup_{1}y=x\sqcup y
and
x\sqcap_{1}y=x\sqcap y
for all
x,y\in P
.

Proof.

(i)

If
\mathbf{P}=(P,\leq,0,1)
is a bounded poset satisfying the ACC and the DCC,
\mathbb{A}(\mathbf{P})=(P,\Min U,\Max L,0,1)
,
\mathbb{P}\big(\mathbb{A}(\mathbf{P})\big)=(P,\sqsubseteq,0,1)
and
a,b\in P
then the following are equivalent:
a\sqsubseteq b
,
\Min U(a,b)=b
,
a\leq b
.

(ii)

If
\mathbf{A}=(P,\sqcup,\sqcap,0,1)
is a finite operator structure,
\mathbb{P}(\mathbf{A})=(P,\leq,0,1)
,
\mathbb{A}\big(\mathbb{P}(\mathbf{A})\big)=(P,\sqcup_{1},\sqcap_{1},0,1)
and
a,b\in P
then

\displaystyle a\sqcup_{1}b

\displaystyle=\Min U(a,b)=a\sqcup b,

\displaystyle a\sqcap_{1}b

\displaystyle=\Max L(a,b)=a\sqcup b.

∎

4
Symmetric difference and the Sheffer operator

We continue our investigations by considering another operator on a complemented poset, namely the so-called
symmetric difference
which we will introduce by means of the operators
\Max L
and
\Min U
.

Let
(P,\leq,{}^{\prime},0,1)
be a complemented poset. For every subset
A
of
P
put
A^{\prime}:=\{a^{\prime}\mid a\in A\}
. In a complemented lattice
(L,\vee,\wedge,{}^{\prime},0,1)
the so-called
symmetric difference

+
is usually defined as follows (see e.g.
[
2
]
):

x+y:=(x^{\prime}\wedge y)\vee(x\wedge y^{\prime})

for all
x,y\in L
. A similar binary operator can be introduced in the complemented poset
(P,\leq,{}^{\prime},0,1)
by

x+y:=\Min U\big(\Max L(x^{\prime},y),\Max L(x,y^{\prime})\big)

for all
x,y\in P
. We extend this definition from elements
x,y
of
P
to all subsets
A,B
of
P
by defining

A+B:=\Min U\big(\Max L(A^{\prime},B),\Max L(A,B^{\prime})\big).

Although the result of
x+y
need not be an element of
P
, but may be a subset of
P
, the corresponding operator on complemented posets shares some familiarly known properties of the usual symmetric difference in lattices, in particular we have

\displaystyle x+x\approx 0,x+y\approx y+x,x+0\approx x,x+1\approx x^{\prime},(x+1)+1\approx x,x+x^{\prime}\approx 1,

\displaystyle x+y\approx x^{\prime}+y^{\prime},x+y^{\prime}\approx x^{\prime}+y.

Recall that a poset
(P,\leq)
is called
distributive
if it satisfies one of the following equivalent conditions (see
[
6
]
and
[
7
]
):

\displaystyle L\big(U(x,y),z\big)

\displaystyle\approx LU\big(L(x,z),L(y,z)\big),

\displaystyle U\big(L(x,y),z\big)

\displaystyle\approx UL\big(U(x,z),U(y,z)\big),

\displaystyle L\big(U(x,y),A\big)

\displaystyle\approx LU\big(L(x,A),L(y,A)\big),

\displaystyle U\big(L(x,y),A\big)

\displaystyle\approx UL\big(U(x,A),U(y,A)\big)

where
A
runs through all subsets of
P
.

Remark 4.2
.

The symmetric difference in Boolean algebras is associative, i.e. it satisfies the identity
(x+y)+z\approx x+(y+z)
. Unfortunately, this is not the case in Boolean posets. E.g., from the previous table we obtain

\displaystyle(a+b)+c

\displaystyle=e+c=d^{\prime}\neq\{a^{\prime},d^{\prime}\}=\Min U(b,c,0)=

\displaystyle=\Min U\big(\Max L(a^{\prime},d^{\prime}),\Max L(a,d)\big)=a+\{a^{\prime},d^{\prime}\}=a+(b+c).

However, the example of Figure 3 satisfies the so-called weak distributivity, i.e.

\Min U\big((x+1)y^{\prime}\big)\approx xy^{\prime}+y^{\prime}.

It is elementary to check that in Boolean algebras
(B,\vee,\wedge,{}^{\prime},0,1)
we have

x+y\approx(x\vee y)\wedge(x^{\prime}\vee y^{\prime}).

This can be easily proved by using distributivity. We show that a similar result holds also for Boolean posets.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
(P,\leq,{}^{\prime},0,1)
be a Boolean poset. Then

x+y\approx\Min U\Big(\Max L\big(\Min U(x,y),\Min U(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\big)\Big).

Proof.

We compute

\displaystyle x+y

\displaystyle\approx\Min U\big(\Max L(x^{\prime},y),\Max L(x,y^{\prime})\big)\approx\Min U\big(L(x^{\prime},y),L(x,y^{\prime})\big)\approx

\displaystyle\approx\Min UL\Big(U\big(x^{\prime},L(x,y^{\prime})\big),U\big(y,L(x,y^{\prime})\big)\Big)

\displaystyle\approx\Min UL\Big(UL\big(U(x^{\prime},x),U(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\big),UL\big(U(y,x),U(y,y^{\prime})\big)\Big)\approx

\displaystyle\approx\Min UL\big(ULU(x^{\prime},y^{\prime}),ULU(y,x)\big)\approx\Min UL\big(U(x,y),U(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\big)\approx

\displaystyle\approx\Min U\Big(\Max L\big(\Min U(x,y),\Min U(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\big)\Big).

∎

It is worth noticing that this result does not hold in complemented posets that are not distributive, e.g. in the complemented poset depicted in Figure 2 we have
b+c=0
, but

\displaystyle\Min U\Big(\Max L\big(\Min U(b,c),\Min U(b^{\prime},c^{\prime})\big)\Big)

\displaystyle=\Min U\big(\Max L(a^{\prime},d^{\prime},1)\big)=\Min U(b,c)=

\displaystyle=\{a^{\prime},d^{\prime}\}.

It is almost evident from Theorem
4.3
that in a Boolean poset
(P,\leq,{}^{\prime},0,1)
for elements
a,b
of
P
with
a\leq b
we have

a+b=\Min U\big(\Max L(a^{\prime},b)\big)=\Min UL(a^{\prime},b).

Using a more restrictive property than distributivity, we can extend the result from Theorem
4.3
from elements to subsets.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
(P,\leq,{}^{\prime},0,1)
be a complemented poset satisfying the identity

U\big(L(A,B),C\big)\approx UL\big(U(A,C),U(B,C)\big)

where
A,B,C
run through all subsets of
P
, and let
A,B
be non-empty subsets of
P
. Then

A+B\approx\Min U\Big(\Max L\big(\Min U(A,B),\Min U(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})\big)\Big).

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle A+B

\displaystyle\approx\Min U\big(L(A^{\prime},B),L(A,B^{\prime})\big)\approx\Min UL\Big(U\big(A^{\prime},L(A,B^{\prime})\big),U\big(B,L(A,B^{\prime})\big)\Big)\approx

\displaystyle\approx\Min UL\Big(UL\big(U(A^{\prime},A),U(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})\big),UL\big(U(B,A),U(B,B^{\prime})\big)\Big)\approx

\displaystyle\approx\Min UL\big(ULU(A^{\prime},B^{\prime}),ULU(B,A)\big)\approx\Min UL\big(U(A,B),U(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})\big)\approx

\displaystyle\approx\Min U\Big(\Max L\big(\Min U(A,B),\Min U(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})\big)\Big).

∎

As mentioned in the introduction, an important term operation in Boolean algebras is the so-called
Sheffer operation
(alias
Sheffer stroke
). It is usually denoted by
|
and defined by
x|y:=x^{\prime}\wedge y^{\prime}
. We extend this concept to complemented posets.

Definition 4.5
.

A Sheffer structure is an ordered pair
(P,|)
where
P
is a non-empty set and
|
is a binary operator on
P
satisfying the following conditions
(
we abbreviate
x|x
by
x^{\prime}
for
x\in P
and for subsets
A
of
P
,
A|A
by
A^{\prime}
)
:

(i)

x|y\approx y|x
,

(ii)

(x|x)|(x|x)\approx x
,

(iii)

(x|y)|(x|x)\approx x
,

(iv)

\big(x^{\prime}|(y^{\prime}|z^{\prime})^{\prime}\big)^{\prime}|z^{\prime}\approx\big(x^{\prime}|(y^{\prime}|z^{\prime})^{\prime}\big)|(x|x^{\prime})
,

(v)

x|x^{\prime}\in P
and
x|x^{\prime}\approx y|y^{\prime}
,

(vi)

(x|x^{\prime})^{\prime}|x\approx x|x^{\prime}
,

(vii)

z\in x|y
if and only if
z^{\prime}|x=z^{\prime}|y=z
and if
u^{\prime}|x=u^{\prime}|y=u
and
z^{\prime}|u^{\prime}=z
together imply
z=u
.

The natural correspondence between a complemented poset and a Sheffer structure is described by the next two theorems.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
(P,\leq,{}^{\prime},0,1)
be a complemented poset satisfying the
ACC
and the
DCC
and define a binary operator
|
on
P
by
x|y:=\Max L(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})
for all
x,y\in P
. Then
(P,|)
is a Sheffer structure.

Proof.

For subsets
A,B
of
P
we define
A|B:=\Max L(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})
. Let
a,b,c\in P
.

(i)

a|b=\Max L(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})=\Max L(b^{\prime},a^{\prime})=b|a
,

(ii)

a|a=\Max L(a^{\prime},a^{\prime})=a^{\prime}
and therefore the unary operator
′
on the Sheffer structure restricted to
P
coincides with the unary operation
′
on the complemented poset,

(iii)

(a|b)|a^{\prime}=\Max L\big(\Min U(a,b),a\big)=\Max L(a)=a
,

(iv)

We have
a^{\prime}|b^{\prime}=\Max L(a,b)
and
v\leq u\leq c
for all
u\in\Max L(b,c)
and all
v\in\Max L\big(a,\Max L(b,c)\big)
. Since
\Max L(b,c)\neq\emptyset
according to the remark after Lemma
2.1
, we conclude
v\leq c
for all
v\in\Max L\big(a,\Max L(b,c)\big)
. Because of
\Max L\big(a,\Max L(b,c)\big)\neq\emptyset
and due to the remark after Lemma
2.1
we obtain

\displaystyle\big(a^{\prime}|(b^{\prime}|c^{\prime})^{\prime}\big)^{\prime}|c^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\Max L\Big(\Max L\big(a,\Max L(b,c)\big),c\Big)=

\displaystyle=\Max L\Big(\Max L\big(a,\Max L(b,c)\big)\Big)=

\displaystyle=\Max L\Big(\Max L\big(a,\Max L(b,c)\big),\Max L(a^{\prime},a)\Big)=\big(a^{\prime}|(b^{\prime}|c^{\prime})^{\prime}\big)^{\prime}|(a|a^{\prime}).

(v)

a|a^{\prime}=\Max L(a^{\prime},a)=0=b|b^{\prime}
,

(vi)

(a|a^{\prime})^{\prime}|a=0^{\prime}|a=\Max L(0,a^{\prime})=0=a|a^{\prime}
,

(vii)

It is easy to see that
a\leq b
if and only if
\Max L(a,b)=a
. Hence
c\in a|b
if and only if
\Max L(c,a^{\prime})=\Max L(c,b^{\prime})=c
and if
\Max L(d,a^{\prime})=\Max L(d,b^{\prime})=d
and
\Max L(c,d)=c
together imply
c=d
.

∎

Now we show how a complemented poset together with the operators
\Max L
and
\Min U
can be received back from a Sheffer structure.

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
(P,|)
be a Sheffer structure, put
x^{\prime}:=x|x
for all
x\in P
, define a binary relation
\leq
on
P
by
x\leq y
if
x^{\prime}|y^{\prime}=x
and put
0:=x|x^{\prime}
and
1:=0^{\prime}

(
x,y\in P

\mathbf{P}:=(P,\leq,{}^{\prime},0,1)
is a complemented poset,
x|y\approx\Max L(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})
and
(x|y)^{\prime}\approx\Min U(x,y)
.

Proof.

We define
A^{\prime}:=A|A
for all subsets
A
of
P
. Let
a,b,c\in P
. We use the conditions of Definition
4.5
. Observe that because of (v),
0
is well-defined. By (ii),
′
is an involution. Because of (ii),
\leq
is reflexive. If
a\leq b
and
b\leq a

a^{\prime}|b^{\prime}=a
and
b^{\prime}|a^{\prime}=b
and hence
a=b

a\leq b
and
b\leq c

a^{\prime}|b^{\prime}=a
and
b^{\prime}|c^{\prime}=b

\displaystyle a^{\prime}|c^{\prime}

\displaystyle=(a^{\prime}|b^{\prime})^{\prime}|c^{\prime}=\big(a^{\prime}|(b^{\prime}|c^{\prime})^{\prime}\big)^{\prime}|c^{\prime}=\big(a^{\prime}|(b^{\prime}|c^{\prime})^{\prime}\big)^{\prime}|(a|a^{\prime})=(a^{\prime}|b^{\prime})^{\prime}|(a|a^{\prime})=a^{\prime}|(a|a^{\prime})=

i.e.
a\leq c

(P,\leq)

a\leq b

a^{\prime}|b^{\prime}=a

(b^{\prime})^{\prime}|(a^{\prime})^{\prime}=a|b=(a^{\prime}|b^{\prime})|b=(b^{\prime}|a^{\prime})|(b^{\prime})^{\prime}=b^{\prime}

b^{\prime}\leq a^{\prime}

5
Duals of Boolean posets

It was established already by G. Boole and formally described by G. Birkhoff
[
2
]
that Boolean algebras and unitary Boolean rings are in a natural one-to-one correspondence, in other words, unitary Boolean rings are structures being dual to Boolean algebras. One may ask whether there exists also a structure being dual to a Boolean poset. We construct such a structure by using the operators mentioned in the previous sections and we show that one can get back a Boolean poset from its dual.

We start with the following definition.

Definition 5.1
.

A dual of a Boolean poset is a structure
(P,+,\cdot,0,1)
where
+
and
\cdot
are binary “operators” on
P
, i.e. they are defined also for subsets of
P
, and where
0,1\in P
and the following conditions for all
x,y,z\in P
and all
A,B\subseteq P
are satisfied:

(i)

x\cdot x=x
,
x\cdot y=y\cdot x
,
x\cdot 0=0
,
x\cdot 1=x
,
\big(x\cdot(y\cdot z)\big)\cdot z=\big(x\cdot(y\cdot z)\big)\cdot 1
,

(ii)

(x+1)+1=x
,

(iii)

x\cdot y=x
implies
(x+1)\cdot(y+1)=y+1
,

(iv)

x\cdot(x+1)=0
,

(v)

\big((x+1)\cdot(y+1)+1\big)\cdot z=\big((x\cdot z+1)\cdot(y\cdot z+1)+1\big)\cdot 1
,

(vi)

x\in A\cdot B
if and only if
x\cdot y=x
for all
y\in A\cup B
and if
x\cdot z=x
and
z\cdot y=z
for all
y\in A\cup B
together imply
z=x
.

How a dual of a Boolean poset can be is derived from a given Boolean poset is illuminated by the next result.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
\mathbf{P}=(P,\leq,{}^{\prime},0,1)
be a Boolean poset satisfying the
ACC
and the
DCC
and define binary operators
+
and
\cdot
on
P
by

\displaystyle a+b

\displaystyle:=\Min U\big(\Max L(a^{\prime},b),\Max L(a,b^{\prime})\big),

\displaystyle a\cdot b

\displaystyle:=\Max L(a,b)

for all
a,b\in P
and

\displaystyle A+B

\displaystyle:=\Min U\big(\Max L(A^{\prime},B),\Max L(A,B^{\prime})\big),

\displaystyle A\cdot B

\displaystyle:=\Max L(A,B)

for all
A,B\subseteq P
. Then
\mathbb{D}(\mathbf{P}):=(P,+,\cdot,0,1)
is a dual of a Boolean poset.

Proof.

Let
a,b,c\in P
and
A,B\subseteq P
.

(i)

We have

\displaystyle a\cdot a

\displaystyle=\Max L(a,a)=a,

\displaystyle a\cdot b

\displaystyle=\Max L(a,b)=\Max L(b,a)=b\cdot a,

\displaystyle a\cdot 0

\displaystyle=\Max L(a,0)=0,

\displaystyle a\cdot 1

\displaystyle=\Max L(a,1)=a.

Moreover,
x\leq y\leq c
for all
x\in\Max L\big(a,\Max L(b,c)\big)
and all
y\in\Max L(b,c)
. Since
\Max L(b,c)\neq\emptyset
according to the remark after Lemma
2.1
we conclude
x\leq c
for all
x\in\Max L\big(a,\Max L(b,c)\big)
and hence

\displaystyle\big(a\cdot(b\cdot c)\big)\cdot c

\displaystyle=\Max L\Big(\Max L\big(a,\Max L(b,c)\big),c\Big)=

\displaystyle=\Max L\Big(\Max L\big(a,\Max L(b,c)\big)\Big)=

\displaystyle=\Max L\Big(\Max L\big(a,\Max L(b,c)\big),1\Big)=\big(a\cdot(b\cdot c)\big)\cdot 1

since
\Max L\big(a,\Max L(b,c)\big)\neq\emptyset
due to the remark after Lemma
2.1
.

(ii)

We have

a+1=\Min U\big(L(a^{\prime},1),L(a,1^{\prime})\big)=\Min U(a^{\prime},0)=a^{\prime}

and hence
(a+1)+1=(a^{\prime})^{\prime}=a
.

(iii)

The following are equivalent:
a\cdot b=a
,
\Max L(a,b)=a
,
a\leq b
. Now every of the following statements implies the next one:
a\cdot b=a
,
a\leq b
,
b^{\prime}\leq a^{\prime}
,
b^{\prime}\cdot a^{\prime}=b^{\prime}
,
a^{\prime}\cdot b^{\prime}=b^{\prime}
,
(a+1)\cdot(b+1)=b+1
.

(iv)

We have
a\cdot(a+1)=\Max L(a,a^{\prime})=0
.

(v)

We have
\Min U(A,B)=\big(\Max L(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})\big)^{\prime}=(A^{\prime}\cdot B^{\prime})^{\prime}
. Using distributivity of
\mathbf{P}
we conclude

\displaystyle\big((a+1)\cdot(b+1)+1\big)\cdot c

\displaystyle=(a^{\prime}\cdot b^{\prime})^{\prime}\cdot c=\Max L\big(\Min U(a,b),c\big)=

\displaystyle=\Max L\big(U(a,b),c\big)=\Max LU\big(L(a,c),L(b,c)\big)=

\displaystyle=\Max L\Big(U\big(L(a,c),L(b,c)\big),1\Big)=

\displaystyle=\Max L\Big(\Min U\big(\Max L(a,c),\Max L(b,c)\big),1\Big)=

\displaystyle=\big((a\cdot c)^{\prime}\cdot(b\cdot c)^{\prime}\big)^{\prime}\cdot 1.

(vi)

The following are equivalent:
a\in A\cdot B
,
a\in\Max L(A,B)
,
a\in L(A,B)
and if
a\leq b\in L(A,B)
then
b=a
.

∎

For finite duals of Boolean posets we can prove the following.

Theorem 5.3

Let
\mathbf{D}=(P,+,\cdot,0,1)
be a finite dual of a Boolean poset. Define a binary relation
\leq
on
P
by
x\leq y
if
x\cdot y=x
, and a unary operation
′
on
P
by
x^{\prime}:=x+1

(

\mathbb{B}(\mathbf{D}):=(P,\leq,{}^{\prime},0,1)
is a Boolean poset.

Proof.

Let
a,b,c\in P
and
A,B\subseteq P
. We consider the conditions of Definition
5.1
. Reflexivity of
\leq
follows from (i). If
a\leq b
and
b\leq a
then
a\cdot b=a
and
b\cdot a=b
and hence
a=a\cdot b=b\cdot a=b
according to (i). If
a\leq b
and
b\leq c

a\cdot b=a

b\cdot c=b

by (i) showing
a\leq c

(P,\leq)
to be a poset. Moreover,
(a^{\prime})^{\prime}=(a+1)+1=a
by (ii). If
a\leq b

a\cdot b=a
and hence
b^{\prime}\cdot a^{\prime}=a^{\prime}\cdot b^{\prime}=(a+1)\cdot(b+1)=b+1=b^{\prime}
by (i) and (iii) showing
b^{\prime}\leq a^{\prime}
. Hence
′
is an antitone involution on
(P,\leq)
. Because of (vi) we have
A\cdot B=\Max L(A,B)
. Now
\Max L(a,a^{\prime})=a\cdot a^{\prime}=a\cdot(a+1)=0
by (iv) which implies
L(a,a^{\prime})=0
. Since
′
is an antitone involution on
(P,\leq)

U(a,a^{\prime})=1
. This shows that
\mathbb{B}(\mathbf{D})
is a complemented poset. Since
\Max L(A,B)=A\cdot B
and
′

(P,\leq)

\Min U(A,B)=\big(\Max L(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})\big)^{\prime}=(A^{\prime}\cdot B^{\prime})^{\prime}
. Finally, using (v) and Lemma
2.1
we conclude

\displaystyle\Max L\big(U(a,b),c\big)

\displaystyle=\Max L\big(\Min U(a,b),c\big)=\Max L\big((a^{\prime}\cdot b^{\prime})^{\prime},c\big)=(a^{\prime}\cdot b^{\prime})^{\prime}\cdot c=

\displaystyle=\big((a\cdot c)^{\prime}(b\cdot c)^{\prime}\big)^{\prime}\cdot 1=\Max L\Big(\big((a\cdot c)^{\prime}(b\cdot c)^{\prime}\big)^{\prime},1\Big)=

\displaystyle=\Max L\Big(\big((a\cdot c)^{\prime}(b\cdot c)^{\prime}\big)^{\prime}\Big)=\Max L\big(\Min U(a\cdot c,b\cdot c)\big)=

\displaystyle=\Max LU(a\cdot c,b\cdot c)=\Max LU\big(\Max L(a,c),\Max L(b,c)\big)=

\displaystyle=\Max LU\big(L(a,c),L(b,c)\big).

Since
P
is finite,
(P,\leq)
satisfies the ACC and the DCC. Applying Lemma
2.1
we conclude
L\big(U(a,b),c\big)=LU\big(L(a,c),L(b,c)\big)
.

The next theorem shows that the correspondence between Boolean posets and their duals is nearly one-to-one.

Theorem 5.4
.

(i)

If
\mathbf{P}=(P,\leq,{}^{\prime},0,1)
is a finite Boolean poset then
\mathbb{B}\big(\mathbb{D}(\mathbf{P})\big)=\mathbf{P}
.

(ii)

If
\mathbf{D}=(P,+,\cdot,0,1)
is a finite dual of a Boolean poset and
\mathbb{D}\big(\mathbb{B}(\mathbf{D})\big)=(P,\oplus,\odot,0,1)
then
(P,\odot,0,1)=(P,\cdot,0,1)
.

Proof.

(i)

Let
\mathbf{P}=(P,\leq,{}^{\prime},0,1)
be a finite Boolean poset,
\mathbb{D}(\mathbf{P})=(P,+,\cdot,0,1)
,
\mathbb{B}\big(\mathbb{D}(\mathbf{P})\big)=(P,\sqsubseteq,{}^{*},0,1)
and
a,b\in P
. Then the following are equivalent:
a\sqsubseteq b
,
a\cdot b=a
,
\Max L(a,b)=a
,
a\leq b
. Moreover,

a^{*}=a+1=\Min U\big(L(a^{\prime},1),L(a,1)\big)=\Min U(a^{\prime},0)=a^{\prime}.

(ii)

If
\mathbf{D}=(P,+,\cdot,0,1)
is a finite dual of a Boolean poset,
\mathbb{B}(\mathbf{D})=(P,\leq,{}^{\prime},0,1)
,
\mathbb{D}\big(\mathbb{B}(\mathbf{D})\big)=(P,\oplus,\odot,0,1)
and
A,B\subseteq P
then
A\odot B=\Max L(A,B)=A\cdot B
according to (vi) of Definition
5.1
.

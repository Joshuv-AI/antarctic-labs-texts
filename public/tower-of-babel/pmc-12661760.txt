Factors affecting anemia prevention behaviors among pregnant women in Phetchabun Province, Northern Thailand
Authors: Piyatida Khuanloy, Archin Songthap, Sunsanee Mekrungrongwong
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundAnemia among pregnant women is a major public health problem in Thailand and in many countries worldwide. Anemia affects the mother and fetus during pregnancy and is a leading cause of maternal death. This cross-sectional analytical study aimed to identify the factors affecting anemia prevention behaviors among pregnant women in Phetchabun Province, northern Thailand.MethodsThe study sample consisted of 383 pregnant women registered for prenatal care at four hospitals under the Ministry of Public Health in Phetchabun Province. A stratified sampling technique was performed to recruit participants. Data were collected using a questionnaire consisting of 8 sections: (1) sociodemographic characteristics, (2) knowledge about anemia in pregnant women, (3) perceived severity of anemia in pregnant women, (4) perceived susceptibility of anemia in pregnant women, (5) perceived outcome of anemia prevention behaviors in pregnant women, (6) perceived self-efficacy to prevent anemia in pregnant women, (7) social support to prevent anemia in pregnant women, and (8) anemia prevention behaviors in pregnant women. Data were analyzed using frequency, percentage, mean, standard deviation, and multiple regression analysis. The significance level was set at 0.05.ResultsThe majority of participants (80.2%) had high mean scores of anemia prevention behaviors in pregnant women (mean scores of 3.68–5.00). Factors that significantly affected anemia prevention behaviors included social support (β = 0.275), perceived outcome of anemia prevention behaviors (β = 0.179), age (β = 0.113), perceived self-efficacy to prevent anemia (β = 0.161), being gravidity 2 or more (β = 0.125), and body mass index (β = 0.116). These 6 factors can explain 26.2% of anemia prevention behaviors among pregnant women in Phetchabun Province.ConclusionOur results indicate that pregnant women need social support, perceived outcomes of anemia prevention, and perceived self-efficacy of anemia prevention to change their anemia prevention behaviors. The focus is on those in the younger age group, having one pregnancy, and having a lower body mass index than normal.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12884-025-08470-7.

Background

Anemia in pregnancy is a major public health problem in Thailand and in many countries worldwide. Globally, the most common effects of anemia among pregnant women were haemorrhage by 27%, indirect obstetric death 23%, and hypertensive disorder 16% [1]. Anemia does not directly cause death in pregnant women; however, it is the basis of death resulting from bleeding during and after delivery [2]. Iron deficiency anemia is one of the most common causes, accounting for 80% of all anemia [3]. Anemia affects the health of the mother and the fetus during pregnancy, childbirth, and the postpartum period, as well as the economy and social well-being of pregnant women [4–6]. It also results in adverse pregnancy outcomes, such as premature birth, low birth weight, and maternal death [7].

The previous studies found that the impact of anemia in pregnant women was 1.94 times higher than the risk of premature rupture of the membranes, 1.51 times the risk of premature birth, and 1.28 times the risk of high blood pressure during pregnancy [7]. Anemia causes bloodstream infections and increases the workload of the heart and heart failure [8]. Anemia also affects the baby, such as low birth weight 1.40 times, delayed growth in the fetus 1.08 times, and lack of oxygen in newborns 1.21 times compared to a pregnant woman without anemia [7].

The situation of anemia in pregnant women worldwide was 37% [6]. In Thailand, anemia in pregnant women increased from 2021 to 2025 with the rate of 18.3, 25.9, 29.5, 32.4, and 37.3%, respectively [9]. The standard indicators of anemia in pregnant women are not more than 14% [16]. In 2023–2025, the prevalence of anemia among pregnant women in Phetchabun Province was 25.8, 25.2, and 38.5%, respectively [10]. These figures were higher than the standard criteria of Thailand.

The literature review indicated that the first prenatal visit in the second trimester, or 14 weeks or more, increased the risk of anemia by 2.16–2.77 times, and the number of previous pregnancies affects the occurrence of anemia in pregnant women. Pregnant women who have more than 3 previous pregnancies were 2.33 times more at risk of anemia. In addition, being over 30 years old was 1.95 times, living in rural areas was 1.76 times, being illiterate was 1.93 times, lack of iron supplements was 2.59 times, being infected with parasites was 1.38 times, and attending the antenatal care clinic (ANC) less than 5 times based on the quality criteria was 5.27 times contributed to anemia [11]. Previous studies failed to identify any engagement from family, community, or social support in tacking anemia prevention behaviors during pregnancy.

Many previous studies reported factors affecting anemia and behavior to prevent anemia among pregnant women in Thailand. However, there is little knowledge and no studies about this topic in Phetchabun Province. Therefore, we were interested in studying factors affecting anemia prevention behavior among pregnant women in Phetchabun Province. If pregnant women do not pay attention to behaviors that can help prevent it, anemia may lead to complications for both the mother and the baby, such as premature delivery, low birth weight, excessive bleeding after childbirth, and postpartum depression [12]. Furthermore, the occurrence of anemia among expectant mothers in this study area exceeds the national goal standard.

Therefore, this study applied the protection motivation theory (PMT) [13] and social support theory [14]. The PMT explains that creating motivation is the basis of behavior modification. Disease is the outcome of behavior if the risk of disease increases. When the behavior of people changed, the disease could be reduced. In other words, if pregnant women perceive about anemia prevention, they will change their behaviors to prevent it [15]. In addition, the social support theory is applied to increase the potential for self-management of pregnant women according to the social and cultural context. The social support involves the support by families, communities, healthcare providers, and networks to participate in solving problems in order to lead to community strength [16]. If pregnant women received support, they would change their behaviors to prevent anemia. This study aimed to identify the factors affecting anemia prevention behaviors among pregnant women in Phetchabun Province, northern Thailand. The findings of this study can be utilized to develop models, programs, or activities to promote behaviors to prevent anemia in pregnant women, supported by families and the community. As a result, the impacts on the mother, fetus, and childbirth can be reduced, and can meet the gold standard criteria of anemia in Thailand.

Methods

Study design

This cross-sectional analysis study was conducted between September to November 2024 and identify the factors affecting anemia prevention behaviors among pregnant women in Phetchabun Province, northern Thailand. This study adheres to STROBE guidelines.

Setting

In northern Thailand, the prevalence of anemia in pregnant women of all 17 provinces exceeds the national target goal. We utilized a simple random sampling method to select one representative province (Phetchabun Province), which is one of the eight provinces with the highest rates of anemia (ranging from 26.1% to 38.8%). The government hospitals under the Ministry of Public Health were the study settings in this study.

Participants

The study population was 3,404 pregnant women who received prenatal care at four government hospitals in Phetchabun Province, northern Thailand. The study sample involved 383 pregnant women. The sample size was calculated using the estimation of the population mean [17] since this study used the mean score of anemia prevention behaviors as the outcome. We explored the multiple regression analysis to identify factors affecting anemia prevention behaviors. The sample size was calculated based on 3,404 people, and the variance (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\sigma\:$$\end{document}2) of healthcare behaviors among pregnant women with anemia was 0.75 [18]. We set the error of estimation (e) at 0.08, and the alpha (a) was 0.05. The inclusion criteria included (1) being aged \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\ge\:$$\end{document} 20 years, (2) being gestational age from the 1 st ANC visit until 38 weeks, (3) being able to write and read Thai, and (4) being willing to participate in this study. The exclusion criteria consisted of: (1) being thalassemia or a carrier of thalassemia, (2) being a foreigner, and (3) being unable to complete the questionnaire. A stratified random sampling technique was utilized to recruit participants. The hospital size was divided into 2 levels among 11 hospitals, including 3 general hospitals and 8 community hospitals in Phetchabun Province. Then, 1 general hospital and 3 community hospitals were randomly selected using a simple random sampling based on the population size. Finally, a systematic random sampling was employed to select 383 study samples for this study.

Research tool

The questionnaire was developed by the researchers for data collection based on the theoretical concepts and literature reviews. It composed of 8 sections; (1) sociodemographic characteristics, (2) knowledge about anemia in pregnant women, (3) perceived severity of anemia in pregnant women, (4) perceived susceptibility of anemia in pregnant women, (5) perceived outcome of anemia prevention behaviors in pregnant women, (6) perceived self-efficacy to prevent anemia in pregnant women, (7) social support to prevent anemia in pregnant women, and (8) anemia prevention behaviors in pregnant women. Sociodemographic characteristics consisted of 11 items of pregnant women. Knowledge was assessed with 10 yes-no questions and categorized into 3 groups using a total score [19]: good (score 8–10), moderate (score 6–7), and low (score 0–5). The perceived severity, perceived susceptibility, and perceived outcome of anemia prevention included 7 items per section. Each section was measured by 5 rating scale questions, each ranging from strongly agree to strongly disagree. A total of seven items of perceived self-efficacy were assessed by 5 rating scale questions, ranging from strongly believe to strongly disbelieve. Social support was evaluated via a 5-point rating scale, ranging from always to never, including 7 items. Anemia prevention behavior was rated on a 5-point rating scale, ranging from always to never, including 15 items. Perceived severity, perceived susceptibility, perceived outcome, perceived self-efficacy, social support, and anemia prevention behaviors were divided into 3 categories [20] using mean scores for high (3.68–5.00), average (2.34–3.67), and low (1.00–2.33). We tested the reliability of the questionnaire by an item objective congruence (IOC) index. All items were between 0.6 and 1.0, which met the standard criteria of the validity test. Thirty respondents who were not involved in this study were evaluated for the reliability of the questionnaire. The reliability test used Cronbach’s alpha coefficient, where knowledge was explored by the Kuder-Richardson 20 (KR-20) [21]. The reliability scores of knowledge, perceived severity, perceived susceptibility, perceived self-efficacy, perceived outcome, social support, and anemia prevention behaviors were 0.81, 0.77, 0.70, 0.83, 0.79, 0.72, and 0.88, respectively.

Operational definitions of variables. Knowledge about anemia refers to the ability of pregnant women to recognize and understand the meaning, causes, symptoms, signs, effects, treatment, and prevention of anemia in pregnancy. Perceived severity of anemia refers to feelings and beliefs of pregnant women that anemia during pregnancy will have serious health consequences for the pregnant women and fetus, such as premature birth, miscarriage, low birth weight, postpartum hemorrhage, infection, and postpartum depression. Perceived susceptibility of anemia risk refers to feelings and beliefs of pregnant women about the chances of developing anemia during pregnancy if they fail to properly care for their health to prevent anemia, such as forgetting to take iron supplements, eating an inappropriate diet, and having an infection in the body. Perceived outcome of the consequences of anemia prevention refers to the perception of the consequences of her actions in pregnant women. If they can properly and appropriately manage their own health and behaviors, they will be able to prevent anemia during pregnancy and avoid potential dangers to them and their fetus. For example, if pregnant women can properly care for herself, take iron supplements, take iron supplements regularly, and prevent parasitic infections, these will result in a healthy pregnant woman, free from fatigue, reduce the risk of premature birth, and postpartum hemorrhage. Perceived self-efficacy in preventing anemia refers to a sense of self-management and responsibility for appropriately managing their own health in pregnant women. They believe that they practice good behaviors in various situations to prevent anemia, such as taking iron supplements regularly, following good nutritional habits, eating a balanced diet, choosing foods that nourish the blood, avoiding uncooked foods, washing fresh fruits and vegetables before eating, and demonstrating appropriate behaviors. Anemia prevention behaviors in pregnant women refers to actions taken by pregnant women to care for their own health in order to prevent anemia. These included 1) taking iron supplement tablets correctly, 2) eating foods high in iron and folate, 3) eating foods that help with iron absorption, 4) eating foods that hinder iron absorption, and 5) preventing anemia from other causes, such as preventing parasitic infections, and attending prenatal care appointments. Measurement of BMI in pregnant women was conducted one time when they came to the ANC during the data collection process. We measure BMI in pregnant women using their height and weight to calculate pregnancy BMI, using the standard formula of weight in kilograms divided by the square of height in meters (kg/m²). This initial BMI is then used to categorize pregnant women into 4 groups;< 18.5 kg./m2 (underweight), 18.5-24.9 kg./m2 (Normal range), 25-29.9 kg./m2 (overweight), and >30 kg./m2 (obesity).

Data collection

Data collection was obtained by a self-administered questionnaire collected from the 4 selected hospitals in Phetchabun Province. We coordinated with health care providers to arrange meetings with target participants. Then, we made an appointment with participants in the meeting rooms of each hospital on the date of the ANC visit. We proceeded with clarification about the data collection and confidentiality. Participants who met the inclusion criteria and were willing to participate in this study were requested to sign the consent forms. Then, we distributed questionnaires to the participants to complete. It took approximately 30 min to complete all parts of the questionnaire. Data collection was performed by the researchers, and all the questions were checked by the researchers for quality control. All complete questionnaires were subsequently utilized for data analysis.

Statistical analysis

Statistical software program (Jamovi) were employed for data analysis. We explored descriptive statistics such as frequencies, percentages, means, and standard deviations to describe sociodemographic characteristics, knowledge about anemia, perceived severity of anemia, perceived susceptibility of anemia, perceived outcome of anemia prevention, perceived self-efficacy, social support, and anemia prevention behaviors among pregnant women. Means of age, monthly income, gestational age, and body mass index were calculated using continuous data. To identify the factors that affect anemia prevention behavior among pregnant women in Phetchabun Province, stepwise multiple regression analysis was utilized. The p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Sociodemographic characteristics of pregnant women

Among 383 participants in this study (no missing), the majority were aged 20–29 years (mean = 27.41, S.D. = 6.00). Most of them (69.2%) were married, 49.7% had graduated from high school, and 33.2% had an average monthly income of more than 5,000 Thai baht (mean = 7,618.54, S.D. = 6,224.35). Of 62.7% had 2 gravidities or more, 58.2% had gestational age of 28–40 weeks (mean = 28.27, S.D. = 6.66), 36.8% had body mass index 25–29.9 kg/m2 (mean = 26.66, S.D. = 5.72), and 72.6% visited ANC before 12 weeks. Lastly, 93.0% of them had no underlying diseases, 84.9% never had an abortion, and 89.6% had no history of cesarean section (Table 1).

Factors affecting anemia prevention behaviors among pregnant women

The results revealed that participants who had higher mean scores of social supports, perceived outcome, and perceived self-efficacy tended to have better anemia prevention behaviors than those who had lower mean scores of these factors (= 0.275, = 0.179, and = 0.161). Moreover, participants who were gravidity 2 or more are more likely to have better anemia prevention behaviors than those who were the first gravidity (= 0.125). Participants who had higher body mass index (BMI) seemed to have better behaviors than those who had lower BMI (= 0.116). Lastly, participants who were at a higher age were possibly to have better behaviors than those who were younger (= 0.113). All these significant factors can explain the anemia prevention behavior in pregnant women by 26.2% (R square = 0.262) (Table 3).

Discussion

Our study aimed to determine the factors that affect anemia prevention behavior among pregnant women in Phetchabun Province. The factors that significantly affected anemia prevention behavior (p-value < 0.05) included social support, perceived outcome, perceived self-efficacy, gravidity of 2 or more, body mass index (BMI), and age.

In this study, it is interesting that even though the study setting has a relatively higher prevalence of anemia in pregnancy than the gold standard criteria of the country, the anemia prevention behaviors were high (80.2%) among pregnant women. This may indicate a knowledge-behavior gap or an awareness-action gap, where a “high” response to a behavior question may mean accepting or recognizing that one “knows” but “does not act” [22]. Therefore, this required a deeper analysis of the underlying causes using a qualitative approach, such as difficulty in changing behavior, lack of motivation, an unfavorable environment, or other psychological barriers.

Participants who had higher mean scores on social support tended to have better anemia prevention behaviors than those with lower mean scores. This finding was in line with that of the study by Mira Triharini et al. [23], which found that receiving social support was significantly related to anemia prevention in pregnant women. This might be due to pregnant women receiving suggestions and supplements from healthcare providers when they visited the ANC [24]. Moreover, they might have had support from village health volunteers about self-care when doing home visits [25] and emotional support from their families [26]. This finding also corresponded to social support theory [16].

Our study found that participants who had higher mean scores of perceived outcome tended to have better behaviors than those with lower mean scores. This finding was aligned with PMT [13], which explains how individuals protect themselves from health threats using four factors: perceived severity, perceived susceptibility, perceived outcome, and perceived self-efficacy [27]. Therefore, after pregnant women perceived the outcome, they changed their behavior to prevent anemia. This result is also consistent with the study of Kamal Mirkarimi et al. [28], who studied the adjustment of attitudes and intentions towards regular exercise in overweight women using the motivation theory for disease prevention. The study reported that the variable that affects the intention to exercise regularly among overweight women is the perceived outcome. It can be seen that this variable is crucial in making overweight since women have expectations of the results that will occur. They can set goals for what they should take action on, leading to a change in their own health behaviors. Similarly, if pregnant women perceive the outcome of behaviors to prevent anemia, they will change their behaviors to prevent anemia.

Our study indicated that participants with a higher mean score of perceived self-efficacy tended to have better behaviors to prevent anemia than those who had a lower mean score. This is consistent with the concept of PMT. This theory explains that self-efficacy is a result of social learning, allowing individuals to gain skills, knowledge, and experiences that can be stored to use in judging their own abilities when faced with situations that are similar to situations they have encountered until they succeed. This finding was in line with that of the study by Tantri Puspita et al. [29]., which studied the relationship between self-efficacy and iron supplement consumption among pregnant women. This study reported that 50% of pregnant women were more confident in taking iron supplements, with 22% taking iron supplements daily and taking iron supplement pills every day. Therefore, when pregnant women have higher perceived self-efficacy, they will modify their behaviors to prevent iron deficiency anemia.

The present study found that participants who had at least 2 pregnancies were more likely to have better behaviors to prevent anemia than those who had one pregnancy. This finding is similar to the study of Naphaphon Getthong et al. [30], which found that being pregnant for the second time or more seemed to have more experience, knowledge, and understanding about taking care of their own health during pregnancy. This experience might be an important stimulus that can motivate and encourage pregnant women to be confident in their abilities to have good care behavior. Further, our findings are in line with the study of Tutik Ekasari et al. [31], which studied the knowledge and prevention of anemia in pregnant women. The study showed that pregnant women who have had multiple pregnancies have had their attitudes, behaviors, and experiences associated with their previous pregnancies. These factors caused pregnant women to have good behaviors in taking care of their own health to prevent the effects of anemia during pregnancy.

Participants who had higher BMI were more likely to have better behaviors to prevent anemia in pregnant women than those who had a normal BMI. It is consistent with the study of Ummi Azmi and Puspitasari [32], which studied factors associated with anemia prevention in pregnant women. The study indicated that BMI is one of the risk factors that affect anemia prevention in pregnant women. It can be explained that pregnant women with a normal or increased BMI are mostly due to their behaviors of receiving complete nutrients, and good nutrient absorption. These behaviors help them to prevent anemia. Additionally, whenever pregnant women visit the ANC, they always gain knowledge about essential food consumption to prevent anemia from healthcare providers. Therefore, this behavior can subsequently increase their BMI.

Finally, the present study indicated that participants with higher age had a higher mean score of anemia prevention behaviors than those with a younger age. This finding was similar to that of the study by Naphaphon Ketthong et al. [29], who studied factors affecting health behavior in pregnant women who were overweight and obese. It was found that age was positively related to overall health behavior practice. This finding is also consistent with the study by Sunet Buppha Mala et al. [33], who studied factors predicting self-care behavior in pregnant women, which found that age was related to self-care behavior during pregnancy. Mothers who are older than 35 years old will have a significantly higher perception of their own risk than mothers who are younger. It might be that when pregnant women get older, they have more experience, knowledge, and perception of anemia risk. Therefore, they change their health care behaviors to prevent iron deficiency anemia during pregnancy.

Strengths and limitations

Our study collected direct data from pregnant women in Phetchabun Province, where the prevalence of anemia is relatively higher than the gold standard criteria of the country. We also explored two theories to identify the factors that may influence anemia prevention behaviors in pregnant women. We found that social support significantly affects the outcome. This is considered a strength of our study. The significant factors can explain 26.2% of the behavior to prevent anemia in pregnant women in Phetchabun Province. We found that only perceived outcome and perceived self-efficacy affected anemia prevention behaviors. However, perceived severity and perceived susceptibility were not associated with the outcome. Other limitations include susceptibility to various biases in cross-sectional study, such as recall and selection bias, and challenges in ensuring a representative sample, which can affect the generalizability of the findings. Some measurement bias might occur in this study, such as the BMI of pregnancy, since we measured it only once when they came to visit the ANC. To deal with this bias, pre-pregnancy BMI or mid-upper arm circumference should be measured to describe their nutritional status during pregnancy.

Conclusion and suggestion

Our study showed that 6 factors affect anemia prevention behaviors among pregnant women, including social support, perceived outcome, perceived self-efficacy, two or more pregnancies, body mass index, and age. Therefore, health care providers should educate and support pregnant women regarding these significant factors to prevent anemia and its consequences. Further, pregnant women should be encouraged by their family members and village health volunteers to increase perceived outcome and perceived self-efficacy. Further studies should develop the model or program to prevent anemia among pregnant women. The study should focus on those who are in the younger age group, having one pregnancy, and having a lower body mass index than normal.

Introduction

Brain injury refers to a group of disorders caused by various factors, such as external impact, hypoxia, infection, or poisoning, which lead to structural and functional damage to brain tissue. Clinically, it is typically classified into two major categories: primary brain injury and secondary brain injury. Due to its high disability rate and limited therapeutic time window, it imposes a heavy burden worldwide on public health systems. Currently, clinical management predominantly relies on supportive care and rehabilitation training, as there are no specific pharmacological agents or standardized protocols capable of reversing neurological damage. While existing medications and rehabilitation therapies can stabilize patients and mitigate secondary damage to a certain extent, there remains a dearth of effective treatments capable of significantly reversing structural damage and fostering nerve regeneration(Takamori et al., 2024). The treatment of brain injury remains a major challenge in the global field of neurology.

Stem cells, a class of undifferentiated cells, possess inherent self-renewal capabilities and multipotent potential. Stem cells can differentiate into neurons, glial cells, and other cell types to replace damaged or necrotic brain cells, thereby filling the lesions(Mi et al., 2022). Additionally, stem cells can secrete various neurotrophic factors that promote the growth and repair of surviving neurons while improving the microenvironment at the injury site(Zhang et al., 2021a, Zhang et al., 2021b; Cudkowicz et al., 2022). Moreover, stem cells can suppress excessive immune responses and the release of inflammatory mediators, thereby mitigating secondary inflammatory damage following brain injury and creating conditions conducive to tissue repair(Anttila et al., 2024). Therefore, stem cell therapy may represent a key strategy for repairing brain injuries. Notably, stem cells not only differentiate into neurons and glial cells but also enhance neurogenesis and synaptic remodeling by secreting EVs that deliver neurotrophic factors and modulate signaling pathways such as Akt/Erk(Zhang et al., 2023; Huang et al., 2025), and EVs derived from stem cells have shown the ability to mitigate neuroinflammation and facilitate blood-brain barrier repair, presenting novel therapeutic avenues for brain injury treatment(Li et al., 2023). Nonetheless, stem cell therapy still contends with challenges related to cell survival, transplantation timing, and standardized preparation protocols. Consequently, a thorough investigation into the underlying mechanisms of stem cell action, ultimately aimed at clinical translation, holds significant importance for advancing the treatment of brain injury.

With the rapid expansion of research in this field, traditional review methods struggle to comprehensively capture the overall knowledge structure, research hotspots, and development trends. Therefore, this study aims to employ bibliometric visualization techniques to systematically organize and analyze research literature on stem cell therapy for brain injury. By constructing visual maps, we will intuitively reveal the core research forces, key thematic evolution, technological development pathways, and future frontier directions within this field. This will provide data-driven decision support for researchers' strategic planning and clinical translation.

Materials and methods

Data retrieval and collection

In this study, we leveraged the Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC) as our primary data source, a comprehensive and authoritative database encompassing approximately 10,000 reputable academic journals(Jiang et al., 2023). Our bibliometric analysis focused on stem cell therapies for brain injury, utilizing publications retrieved from WoSCC. The search timeframe spanned from January 2009 to December 2025. The data retrieval and collection process is delineated in Fig. 1. The retrieval results were exported in plain text format and as tab-delimited files, with the content set to “Fully Recorded and Cited References.” Study selection, data extraction, and quality assessment were performed independently by two reviewers.

Flowchart of the article search.

Bibliometric and visualized analysis

Bibliometric analysis and visualization of the publications retrieved from the WoSCC were performed using VOSviewer (version 1.6.20), CiteSpace (version 6.4.R1), and Scimago Graphica. VOSviewer is a powerful visualization tool that enables us to import literature data from the Web of Science database and conduct in-depth analysis using its diverse data source compatibility, co-occurrence analysis, clustering analysis, and timeline view capabilities(Wang and Maniruzzaman., 2022). In this study, VOSviewer was utilized to visually map the collaborative relationships among countries, journals, and references. CiteSpace is a widely recognized visualization analysis software in the field of bibliometrics(He et al., 2023). In our study, we utilized CiteSpace to detect institutions, references, and keywords that exhibited significant citation bursts. We generated clustering maps to visualize these entities and created timeline visualizations to chart the progression of references over the years. Additionally, we utilized Scimago Graphica to create a collaboration map illustrating partnerships between countries and regions.

Results

Global trend in publication outputs

Analyzing the annual publication trends of research literature provides a more intuitive assessment of shifts in research intensity and potential trends regarding stem cell therapy for brain injury. This study statistically analyzed a total of 8785 publications. The annual publication trend is illustrated in Fig. 2. From 290 papers in 2009–695 papers in 2025, the publication volume increased by about 140% over 17 years. The fitted line (y = 24.074x+296.87, R²=0.8889) indicates a strong linear correlation, suggesting sustained growth in research activity within this field. Over the past 17 years, the annual total number of published papers has shown an overall upward trend. Although there were slight declines in 2018 and 2024, these were normal fluctuations that did not affect the long-term growth trend. The number peaked at 695 papers in 2025. These findings indicate sustained and potentially increased interest in stem cell therapy for brain injury.

Countries such as Germany and the United Kingdom, although publishing fewer papers than China, demonstrate higher centrality scores. This indicates greater international influence and recognition, as they serve as essential bridges facilitating cross-border exchange and cooperation. These disparities may be attributable to differences in research quality, the extent of international collaboration, the novelty of research topics, and methodological advancements.

Analysis of journals

Fig. 5 visualizes the journal co-citation network generated by VOSviewer, covering the period from 2009 to 2025. A total of 1442 journals were actively publishing research on stem cell therapies for brain injury. For the purpose of this analysis, we performed bibliographic coupling on journals that had published more than 20 articles related to stem cell therapy for brain injury. In this network, each node represents a journal, with the node size generally reflecting the volume of relevant publications. In Fig. 5A, the node sizes of the two journals, Neural Regeneration Research and International Journal of Molecular Sciences, are significantly larger than those of other journal clusters, and they occupy central positions in the network topology, serving as benchmarks of academic influence in this field. In Figs. 5A and 5B, the four major clusters correspond to the four major research directions in this field. The close connections between the blue conprehensive journals, green clinical journals, yellow basic research journals, and red specialized journals demonstrate that this field is the product of a deep integration between neuroscience and regenerative medicine. The cross-cluster connections in the network diagram clearly reveal that stem cell therapy for brain injury is a highly interdisciplinary field, linking neuroscience, stem cell biology, molecular medicine, surgery, and other disciplines. An in-depth analysis of the citation network in this study indicates that the field of stem cell therapy for brain injury has formed a structurally stable, interdisciplinary, and dynamically evolving academic ecosystem. Based on the evolutionary trends revealed by the analysis of the map, future research on stem cell therapy for brain injury may focus on the development of novel delivery systems, regulation of the immune microenvironment, precision medicine applications, and clinical translation research. These efforts aim to address safety and efficacy concerns associated with stem cell therapy, enable the personalized design of treatment regimens, and improve therapeutic outcomes.

Keywords encapsulated the core concepts of a study, and analyzing them facilitates a deeper understanding of research hotspots within the field of stem cell therapy for brain injury. As shown in Figs. 8A and 8B, “mesenchymal stem cells”, “stem cells”, “brain”, and “neural stem cells” represented the absolute core of the entire field, indicating that stem cell research in brain injury centers on the application of various stem cell types in brain-related diseases. “Ischemic stroke”, “traumatic brain injury”, “spinal cord injury”, and “cerebral ischemia” represent the primary disease models in stem cell-based brain injury therapy research. “Neurogenesis”, “oxidative stress”, “apoptosis”, “differentiation”, and “proliferation” constitute the core research directions, focusing on how stem cells achieve neural repair by regulating these mechanisms. Discussion

Research output in stem cell therapy for brain injury shows an upward trend

Analysis of annual publication trends reveals a steady linear growth in research output on stem cell therapies for brain injury over the 17-year period from 2009 to 2025. This sustained growth reflects the global interest in stem cell-based repair strategies for brain injury. The core drivers are the urgent clinical need due to the high disability rate of brain injury and the limitations of traditional therapies. These factors have spurred exploration of novel approaches(Muliira et al., 2024; Chen et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2025a, Zhang et al., 2025b). In recent years, stem cell therapy has gained an increasing popularity. Research indicates that stem cell therapy can prevent or reverse damage at biochemical and cellular levels, restoring brain function by leveraging endogenous healing and repair mechanisms(Waseem et al., 2023). With breakthrough advances such as EVs(Han et al., 2023; Pei et al., 2023), stem cell research in brain injury treatment will enter a new phase of clinically oriented growth.

Main characteristics and content of research in the field of stem cell therapy for brain injury

The geographical distribution of research contributions reveals that studies are concentrated in the United States and China, which together account for over 60% of total publications. However, the characteristics of collaborative networks differ significantly between the two countries. China leads in publication volume, representing 32.67% of the total, while the United States tops the list with a centrality of 0.35. This indicates that the United States occupies a pivotal position in international collaboration, resource integration, and academic influence. The gap between China's publication volume and centrality reflects limited depth of its international collaboration. This may stem from divergent research priorities, language barriers in academic exchanges, and less developed collaborative networks. In contrast, European nations like Germany and the United Kingdom, though publishing fewer papers than China, exhibit higher centrality, serving as vital global hubs for academic exchange. This likely stems from their long-standing leadership in neuroscience basic research and open collaborative frameworks. As emerging nations increasingly engage in this field, their research outputs may not only introduce novel perspectives but also foster innovative therapeutic approaches. This dynamic underscores the critical value of stem cell research in conquering brain injury disorders.

Analysis of institutional contributions reveals that traditional academic powerhouses such as the University of California system, Florida State University system, University of Texas system, and Harvard University remain core forces in this field, possessing deep research foundations and extensive international collaboration networks. Notably, since 2021, Chinese institutions such as Sichuan University, Zhejiang University of Chinese Medicine, and the Chinese Academy of Sciences have emerged as highly influential new players in this field, driven by a surge in high-impact citations. This trend reflects not only China's increasing investment in stem cell research but also rising research quality and growing international recognition. The late-stage rise of these Chinese institutions has also benefited from policy-driven research planning and the continuous improvement of domestic collaborative networks, which are gradually narrowing the gap between China and traditional global leaders in this field.

The analysis of journals within this field identified Neural Regeneration Research and International Journal of Molecular Sciences as the two core platforms. The clustering patterns of these journals reveal distinct research subfields, including molecular mechanisms, neural regeneration, fundamental stem cell research, and post-injury neuropathology.

The author collaboration network centers on scholars such as Michael Chopp and Cesar V. Borlongan. These researchers not only publish extensively but also lead influential research teams, significantly advancing knowledge dissemination and cross-team collaboration. The Chopp team is dedicated to researching exosomes and their role in cerebral ischemia repair(Zhang et al., 2015; Chen et al., 2020; Wang et al., 2025a, Wang et al., 2025b; Zhang et al., 2025a, Zhang et al., 2025b). Their highly cited paper, published in the top neurosurgery journal Journal of Neurosurgery(Zhang et al., 2015), is entitled “Effect of exosomes derived from multipotent mesenchymal stromal cells on functional recovery and neurovascular plasticity in rats after traumatic brain injury”, and is one of the foundational studies in this field. This work shifted the focus of mesenchymal stem cell therapy from the cells themselves to the exosomes they secrete, significantly advancing subsequent exosome-based brain injury repair research. The Borlongan team has made outstanding contributions to stem cell therapy for traumatic brain injury(Bourgade et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2025a, Wang et al., 2025b). Since 2018, the emergence of emerging researchers such as Jenkin G has injected new vitality into this field(Penny et al., 2024; Razak et al., 2025), driving innovative developments in research. The Jenkin G team has made innovative progress by systematically exploring strategies for the expansion of umbilical cord blood-derived cells, clarifying key neuroprotective mechanisms, and advancing the translation of clinical applications for brain injury in preterm infants. They have emphasized the synergy between translational medicine and industry to accelerate the translation of basic research findings into clinical practice. Emerging scholars and established authorities complement one another, driving the development of stem cell therapies for brain injury toward greater safety, precision, and accessibility.

A citation analysis of the literature revealed that Raghu Kalluri's 2020 review article, “The biology, function, and biomedical applications of exosomes” (cited 8243 times)(Kalluri and Lebleu, 2020), stands as a landmark achievement in the field, establishing EVs as a core research focus. In 2017, Xin et al. published a study in Stroke entitled “MicroRNA Cluster miR-17–92 Cluster in Exosomes Enhances Neuroplasticity and Functional Recovery After Stroke in Rats” (Xin et al., 2017), which validated the therapeutic potential of extracellular vesicle-derived microRNAs, offering novel insights for exosome-based precision medicine. This research advances understanding of post-stroke neuroplasticity mechanisms and facilitates the translational application of related therapies. A significant number of high-impact papers published in 2017 experienced a concentrated surge in citations during 2021–2022. This phenomenon reflects the time lag in translating fundamental research findings into widespread applications, while also highlighting the cumulative and iterative nature of research in this field.

Research hotspots and trends in stem cell therapy for brain injury

Keyword analysis reveals that various stem cells, including mesenchymal stem cells and neural stem cells, constitute the core research subjects in this field, while ischemic stroke and traumatic brain injury serve as the primary disease models. Core mechanisms such as neurogenesis, oxidative stress, apoptosis, differentiation, and proliferation highlight the field's focus on studying stem cell-mediated neural repair pathways. The most prominent trend is that extracellular vesicles and exosomes have become the hottest research areas (with emergence strengths of 88.12 and 39.49, respectively, persisting through 2025). This discovery marks a shift in the field from traditional direct stem cell transplantation to cell-free therapies. The sudden emergence of inflammation in 2023 suggests that neuroinflammation regulation is becoming a new research focus in this domain. This aligns with the deepening understanding of secondary injury mechanisms following brain injury in recent years(Anttila et al., 2024). In terms of basic mechanistic research, exploration into neuroinflammation regulation will focus on precision and multidimensional analysis, filling gaps in the mechanisms of brain injury repair(Mishra et al., 2023; Park et al., 2023). The synergistic mechanisms between inflammation regulation and exosomes will emerge as a research hotspot. Elucidating the interconnected effects of exosome-mediated inflammation suppression and enhanced neural plasticity will lay the foundation for constructing a more comprehensive molecular mechanism network for stem cell-based treatments of brain injury.

Research value and future core research directions of exosomes

Exosomes, a distinct subtype of EVs, and EVs themselves both emerged as high-frequency keywords, reflecting two research foci: the broad study of EV-mediated intercellular communication in brain injury and the specific investigation of exosomes, owing to their defined biogenesis and therapeutic potential. As the core mediators of stem cell paracrine effects, exosomes have emerged as a research hotspot in the field of stem cell therapy for brain injury due to their advantages of excellent biocompatibility, low immunogenicity, ability to cross the blood-brain barrier, and ease of storage and standardized production(Kalluri and Lebleu, 2020; Huang et al., 2025). Exosomes rank among the top keywords in this field, with research interest steadily rising, offering a novel therapeutic approach to the challenging neuromedical issue of brain injury. Current research has demonstrated that exosomes can regulate key processes such as microglial polarization, neurogenesis, oxidative stress, and apoptosis by delivering bioactive substances including microRNAs and neurotrophic factors. This enables the suppression of neuroinflammation and the repair of neural tissue following brain injury(Liu et al., 2021; Zhang et al., 2021a, Zhang et al., 2021b). Future research should further clarify the differential roles of exosomes derived from distinct sources (such as mesenchymal stem cells, neural stem cells, and microglia). Additionally, exploring the interaction patterns between exosomes and the inflammatory microenvironment following brain injury will be essential. This will lay the theoretical foundation for precisely regulating the therapeutic effects of exosomes.At the level of technological development and optimization, key future directions will include exosome engineering and modification, targeted delivery, and standardized preparation(Shimizu et al., 2026). In terms of clinical translation and application, exosomes hold promise as a novel cell-free therapeutic strategy to replace traditional stem cell transplantation, thereby overcoming numerous limitations inherent to conventional stem cell therapies. As foundational research advances and exosome preparation techniques mature, exosome-based therapies are poised to transition into clinical trials for conditions such as ischemic stroke and traumatic brain injury.

Limitations

This study provides valuable insights into the therapeutic potential of stem cells for brain injury. However, several limitations should be acknowledged. Firstly, the bibliometric analysis was confined to the Web of Science database, potentially overlooking relevant studies indexed in other databases (such as Scopus or PubMed) and Chinese-language journals, thereby introducing a degree of selection bias. Recent articles published in high-impact journals have not yet accumulated sufficient citation counts, resulting in their inadequate inclusion and representation in the current analysis. Moreover, while bibliometrics excel in quantifying publication and citation patterns, they cannot fully substitute for in-depth qualitative analyses of the literature. Consequently, this approach may fall short in assessing the methodological rigor, innovative contributions, and negative findings within the field.

Conclusion

This study used bibliometric visualization analysis to comprehensively demonstrate the burgeoning development of stem cell therapy for brain injury, its clear evolution of knowledge structure, and distinct shifts in research frontiers. Findings reveal a significant linear increase in publications on stem cell therapy for brain injury over the past 17 years. China and the United States stand as core research nations, with the U.S. demonstrating greater prominence in international collaboration and academic influence. Michael Chopp and Cesar V. Borlongan are the leading high-output scholars. Mesenchymal stem cells, neural stem cells, and other stem cell types constitute the core research subjects in this field. Ischemic stroke and traumatic brain injury represent the primary disease models. Research on exosomes currently represents the most prominent direction. Deepening exploration of their mechanisms of action, advancing the translation of basic research findings into clinical applications, and addressing critical issues such as standardized preparation of stem cells and transplantation time windows are essential. These efforts aim to develop safer and more effective treatment strategies for brain injury, providing robust support for overcoming this major challenge in global neurology.

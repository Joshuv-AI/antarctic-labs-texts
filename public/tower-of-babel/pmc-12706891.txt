Engineered hUCMSC-derived extracellular vesicles deliver circ-0000258 to restore p53-mediated tumor suppression in papillary thyroid carcinoma
Authors: Chan Zhang, Lei Li, Yanxiu Liu, Bo Yu, Jing Liu, Shujing Li
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionPapillary thyroid carcinoma (PTC) is a significant type of endocrine cancer, characterized by diverse genetic alterations and a complex molecular environment. Extracellular vesicles (EVs), especially those derived from mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs), have emerged as promising targeted drug carriers for cancer cells. Additionally, reprogramming MSC-derived EVs represents a novel strategy for cancer gene therapy, offering potential solutions to clinical challenges and new treatment directions. Increasing evidence suggests that MSC-derived EVs play a crucial role in tumor progression by delivering circular RNAs (circRNAs), which function as microRNA (miRNA) sponges. However, the underlying molecular mechanisms and their clinical applications remain to be fully explored and validated.Methods and resultsThrough in-depth mining using high-throughput bioinformatics analyses, we conducted a comprehensive differential gene analysis between PTC tissues and normal thyroid tissues, successfully identifying circ-0000258 as a key regulatory molecule. Following multi-dimensional validation in PTC cell lines and clinical specimens, the consistent low expression of circ-0000258 was confirmed, strongly suggesting its latent potential as a tumor suppressor. Functional mechanistic investigations have revealed that overexpression of circ-0000258 potently curbs the malignant biological behaviors of PTC cells, notably inhibiting cell proliferation and invasion. More significantly, circ-0000258 acts as a molecular sponge, specifically sequestering miR-146b. This action relieves the post-transcriptional repression of p53 by miR-146b, thereby activating the p53-mediated apoptotic signaling cascade. By intervening at the genetic regulatory level, circ-0000258 effectively reprograms the fate of thyroid tumor cells. Furthermore, in the context of translational medicine research, we innovatively constructed an engineered delivery platform based on extracellular vesicles derived from human umbilical cord mesenchymal stem cells (hUCMSC-EVs). By exogenously loading circ-0000258 into these vesicles, we successfully endowed these natural nanocarriers with targeted anti-cancer properties. Both in vitro and in vivo functional assays demonstrated that the engineered hUCMSC-EVs loaded with circ-0000258 could effectively act on PTC cells, significantly reducing the volume of xenograft tumors and inducing tumor cell apoptosis. Notably, when combined with cisplatin, these engineered extracellular vesicles exhibited a synergistic anti-cancer effect, suggesting their potential to overcome chemoresistance in thyroid tumors.ConclusionThis study has established the circ-0000258/miR-146b/p53 regulatory axis as a crucial mechanism underlying tumor suppression in PTC. It has also demonstrated the translational potential of hUCMSC-EVs as a safe and efficient delivery vehicle. By integrating the functional role of circ-0000258 with the targeted delivery advantages of engineered EVs, this research not only provides a novel strategy for the targeted treatment of thyroid cancer but also offers a theoretical basis and technical paradigm for the development of novel anti-tumor biological agents. Introduction

The global incidence of thyroid cancer has been experiencing a continuous upward trend, with Papillary Thyroid Carcinoma (PTC) accounting for 85–90% of all thyroid cancer cases [1–3]. While early-stage PTC patients can achieve favorable outcomes through surgical resection combined with radioactive iodine therapy, approximately 10–15% of patients develop local recurrence or distant metastasis 5–10 years postoperatively [4, 5]. Notably, the 5-year survival rate plummets to 40–60% for advanced patients with lung or bone metastases, highlighting the critical need for improved strategies to manage recurrent and metastatic disease [6, 7]. Current chemotherapeutic regimens (e.g., cisplatin, doxorubicin) achieve an objective response rate of < 30% in PTC [8], while targeted agents (e.g., sorafenib, lenvatinib) prolong progression-free survival but are hampered by rapid development of drug resistance and severe immune-related adverse events, creating a therapeutic bottleneck [9, 10]. More critically, the absence of precise prognostic markers leads to overtreatment in ~ 30% of low-risk patients and loss of optimal intervention opportunities in 20% of high-risk patients due to delayed risk stratification [11, 12]. Consequently, developing highly targeted, low-toxicity therapies alongside biomarkers with dual diagnostic-prognostic value has emerged as a core unmet need to overcome PTC treatment challenges.

Circular RNAs (circRNAs), a class of emerging non-coding RNAs characterized by their covalently closed circular structure, exhibit exceptional stability and tissue-specific expression patterns, conferring them potential advantages over linear RNAs in cancer therapy [13, 14]. However, in contrast to well-studied tumor types such as lung and liver cancer, circRNA research in thyroid cancer remains in an early exploratory stage, the authoritative database CircBase lists only a limited number of functionally validated circRNAs associated with thyroid cancer, and only a handful of preclinical studies have attempted delivering circRNA overexpression via viral vectors, with no systematic mechanistic frameworks or therapeutic strategies established [15]. This research lag is closely linked to the complex molecular heterogeneity of thyroid cancer—while mutations such as BRAF V600E and TERT promoter mutations have become established clinical stratification markers, ~ 40% of PTC patients lack identifiable driver mutations [16, 17], urgently necessitating the uncovering of novel molecular targets within the non-coding RNA regulatory landscape. CircRNAs, by virtue of their unique “molecular sponge” function to specifically sequester oncogenic miRNAs and reconfigure signaling pathways [18–20], offer a novel dimension to tackle the challenges posed by thyroid cancer heterogeneity.

Bioinformatics analysis of PTC tissues and adjacent non-tumor samples identified hsa-circ-0000258 as the most differentially expressed circRNA, with its low expression significantly correlated with PTC tumorigenesis and progression. Subsequent functional assays confirmed that exogenous overexpression of circ-0000258 potently suppressed the proliferative capacity of PTC cell lines and induced apoptosis via p53 pathway activation, achieving over a 2-fold increase in apoptosis rate compared to untreated controls. Mechanistically, this circRNA exerts its function by specifically binding to miR-146b, thereby alleviating its transcriptional repression of the tumor suppressor gene TP53 and reconstituting the p53-mediated apoptotic regulatory network. This finding not only fills a critical void in functional circRNA research for thyroid cancer but also underscores the central role of noncoding RNA regulatory axes in personalized cancer therapy by measuring circ-0000258 expression levels, clinicians can precisely identify high-risk patient subgroups resistant to conventional therapies, enabling stratified intervention strategies. In the realm of translational therapy, we innovatively developed an engineered extracellular vesicle (EV) delivery system derived from human umbilical cord mesenchymal stem cells (hUCMSCs). Compared with liposomal vectors, hUCMSC-EVs exhibit innate tumor microenvironment tropism and carry no immunogenic risk, addressing critical challenges in nucleic acid delivery [21, 22]. In vivo studies demonstrated that EVs loaded with circ-0000258 significantly reduced xenograft tumor volume in nude mice, with combined treatment with cisplatin further enhancing tumor growth inhibition compared to monotherapy. This strategy aligns seamlessly with the “personalized intervention” philosophy in precision oncology [23]—by evaluating the activation status of the circ-0000258/miR-146b/p53 axis in patient tumors, stratified treatment protocols can be designed: low-expression patients may prioritize EV-based delivery to restore tumor-suppressive functions, while cisplatin-resistant patients could benefit from combination therapy to reactivate p53-dependent apoptosis, achieving a closed-loop management of “molecular profiling-target matching-precise dosing”. A recent lung cancer clinical trial validating circRNA expression-based personalized therapy prolonged median survival, underscoring the broad translational potential of this approach in thyroid cancer [24].

In summary, the main objectives of this study include analyzing the comparison between thyroid cancer tissue and adjacent healthy thyroid tissue in PTC patients, investigating the roles of circ-0000258 and p53 in regulating EVs mediated communication in PTC, and exploring the therapeutic potential of EVs derived from human umbilical cord mesenchymal stem cells in targeting PTC growth and metastasis. Addressing the critical unmet needs in thyroid cancer therapy, this study employed bioinformatics-driven target screening and functional validation to uncover a novel tumor-suppressive mechanism whereby circ-0000258 activates the p53 pathway via sponge-mediated sequestration of miR-146b. Concurrently, we engineered a translational-ready hUCMSC-EV delivery platform with demonstrated potential for clinical application. This work not only broke through early bottlenecks in thyroid cancer circRNA research but also established a precision medicine paradigm integrating “biomarker detection-axis intervention-delivery optimization,” providing a tangible technical roadmap for personalized PTC therapy. With the rapid advancement of liquid biopsy and circRNA microarray technologies, the circ-0000258-based molecular profiling system is poised to enter clinical practice within the next 5–10 years, facilitating a comprehensive transition in thyroid cancer treatment from “pathology-stage empirical therapy” to “molecular characteristics based precision targeting.”

Materials and methods

Cell lines and reagents

The PTC cell line (TPC-1) or Human umbilical cord mesenchymal stem cells (hUCMSC) were purchased from ATCC. Cell lines were cultured in RPMI 1640 medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum at 37 °C in a 5% CO2 incubator.

TPC-1 or hUCMSCs were transfected with either overexpression (circ-0000258 or miR-146b mimic) or silencing (sicirc-0000258 and miR-146b inhibitor) vectors using Lipofectamine 3000. After 48 h, stably transfected cell lines were selected. The efficiency of transfection was validated using qRT-PCR and Western blotting analyses. circ-0000258: 5’-GCTAGCTACGTACGTACGTAGCTGTAGCTAGCTACGTCGTACGTAGCTAATGCTAGCTACG-3’. sicirc-0000258: 5’-GATCCGTTCTCCGAACGTGTCACGTTTTCAAGAGA - ACGACACGTTCGGAGAACTTTTTGGAAA-3’. miR-146b mimic:5’-TGAGAACTGAATTCCATGGGTT-3’.

miR-146b inhibitor:5’-AACCCATGGAATTCAGTTCTCA − 3’.

Bioinformatics analysis of circRNA-miRNA-mRNA regulatory networks

Expression profile data for papillary thyroid carcinoma (PTC) and normal thyroid tissues were retrieved from the GEO database (accession number GSE197443), comprising 4 paired PTC and adjacent normal samples (n = 8 total). Raw expression matrices for circular RNAs (circRNAs), messenger RNAs (mRNAs), and microRNAs (miRNAs) were extracted and normalized using standard quality control procedures in R (version 4.2.1).

Differential expression of circRNAs and miRNAs was identified using the limma package, with significance defined by |log₂ fold change (logFC)| ≥ 1 and unadjusted p < 0.05. This analysis yielded 76 differentially expressed circRNAs (59 downregulated in PTC) and 96 differentially expressed miRNAs (8 upregulated in PTC). Volcano plots were generated to visualize expression changes).

Pearson correlation analysis was performed to identify negative regulatory relationships between circRNAs and miRNAs (cor < 0), resulting in a circRNA-miRNA interaction network comprising 11 circRNAs and 8 miRNAs. To integrate mRNA data, the cor.test function was used to establish pairwise correlations among circRNAs, miRNAs, and mRNAs, focusing on mRNAs significantly associated with both differentially expressed circRNAs and miRNAs.

The top 48 mRNAs in the circRNA-miRNA-mRNA network were subjected to Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway enrichment using the clusterProfiler package. Statistical significance was set at p < 0.05.

Based on KEGG pathway annotations, differentially expressed circRNAs and miRNAs directly linked to TP53 (the gene encoding p53) were prioritized. This analysis identified 2 circRNAs and 7 miRNAs showing significant negative correlations with TP53 expression and involvement in p53 pathway regulation, leading to the construction of a circRNA-miRNA-TP53 regulatory network.

Patient sample collection

Thirty pairs of tumor tissue and paired adjacent cancerous tissues were obtained from patients diagnosed with PTC at the Department of General Surgery. The lesion tissue was divided into the cancer tissue group, and the adjacent tissue (≥ 3 cm away from the lesion) was used as the control during the operation. Tissue samples were drawn in ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (EDTA) tubes. The study was approved by the Institutional Review Board (IRB No.202401003) of the First Hospital of Shanxi Medical University. All patients participating in the study were aware of the research content, purpose, risks, benefits and signed the informed consent form. The research carried out on humans and/or human data was in compliance with the Helsinki Declaration.

Animals

All applicable institutional and national guidelines for the care and use of animals were in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. All the animal experiments were approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of the First Hospital of Shanxi Medical University (NO.DWYJ-2022-044). Mice were obtained from the Laboratory Animal Center of the Shanxi Medical University.

Tumor formation in nude mice

Culture and harvest the PTC-1 cells, then resuspend them in a suitable buffer, counting the cell density accurately using a hemocytometer. Select healthy nude mice and anesthetize them with isoflurane. Using a sterile syringe with a fine-gauge needle, inject the prepared thyroid cancer cell suspension subcutaneously or orthotopically into the nude mice at specific sites. For orthotopic injection, target the thyroid gland region carefully. Regularly observe the nude mice for signs of tumor growth. At the end of the experiment, sacrifice the nude mice humanely. In brief, mice were promptly transferred to a specialized euthanasia chamber. CO2 gas is then introduced at a rate of at least 20% of the container’s volume per minute. As the CO2 concentration increases, the mice initially exhibit rapid breathing, followed by respiratory depression and hypoxia. This sequence of events leads to the mice losing consciousness quickly and ultimately results in death. After the mice have ceased breathing, the introduction of CO2 continues for an additional 1–3 min to ensure complete euthanasia. Dissect out the tumors carefully, weigh them, and then process the tumor tissues for further analysis, such as histological examination, immunohistochemistry, or molecular biology assays. Cisplatin and hUCMSC-EVs were administered to mice in different groups according to the experimental design.

Gene expression analysis

Quantitative reverse transcription PCR (qRT-PCR) was performed to evaluate the expression levels of genes. Total RNA was extracted from isolated cells or EVs using the TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen) according to the manufacturer’s protocol. Complementary DNA (cDNA) was synthesized using the Reverse Transcription System (Promega). The qRT-PCR reactions were conducted on a Bio-Rad CFX96 real-time system using SYBR Green Master Mix (Thermo Fisher Scientific). The expression levels were normalized. The specific primers used are provided in the Supplementary Table 1.

Isolation and extraction of EVs

EVs were then isolated from the hUCMSCs via ultracentrifugation. Briefly, hUCMSCs were centrifuged at 300 g for 10 min to remove dead cells, and then centrifuged at 3000 g for 10 min to remove cellular debris. The supernatant was then transferred to a new tube and subjected to a series of centrifugation steps: first at 100,000 g for 70 min, followed by ultracentrifugation at 100,000 g for 70 min at 4 °C using a Beckman L-80 Ultracentrifuge. The pelleted EVs were resuspended in phosphate-buffered saline and stored at −80 °C for subsequent experiments.

Morphological analysis of EVs

Transmission electron microscopy (TEM) was performed to characterize the morphology of isolated EVs. A drop of the EVs suspension was placed on a copper grid and allowed to adhere for 30 min. After removal of the excess liquid, the samples were then examined under a Jeol JEM-1400 TEM at an acceleration voltage of 80 kV.

Size distribution and concentration analysis

The size distribution and concentration of EVs were analyzed using nanoparticle tracking analysis (NTA) with a NanoSight NS300 (Malvern Instruments). The isolated EVs were diluted in PBS to an optimal concentration for NTA analysis, and three independent recordings were made for each sample. The data were processed using NTA software to determine the mean size and concentration of the isolated EVs.

Protein expression analysis

Western blotting was conducted to assess protein levels in isolated EVs or cells. Proteins were extracted using RIPA lysis buffer and quantified using the BCA Protein Assay Kit (Thermo Fisher Scientific). Equal amounts of protein were separated using 10% SDS-PAGE and transferred to a polyvinylidene difluoride (PVDF) membrane. The membrane was blocked with 5% non-fat milk for 1 h at room temperature and then probed overnight at 4 °C with the primary antibodies: anti-CD63, ALIX、TSG101, CD81、p53、caspase 3、Bcl-2、Bax、Cyt-C (1:1000; Abcam) and anti-GAPDH (1:3000; Sigma-Aldrich). Following incubation with the secondary antibody for 1 h at room temperature, detection was performed using an ECL detection system (GE Healthcare).

Cell proliferation assay

PTC cells (3000 cells per well) were seeded in 96-well plates. Ten microliters of CCK-8 reagent was added to the 96-well plate at different points in time. Then, the 96-well plates with cells were incubated in the incubator at 37 °C for 2 h. The absorbance was detected at 450 nm.

Transwell assay

The transwell chamber (Corning Costar) coated without (cell migration) or with (cell invasion) Matrigel was used in this assay. Briefly, digested and resuspended cells were plated in the upper chamber, RPMI-1640 medium containing 20% FBS was added to the bottom of the chamber. After 48 h of cultivation, the chamber was swabbed with cotton swabs and then fixed and stained. After observation with a microscope, the cell number that migrated and invaded to the bottom of the chamber was counted.

Cell scratch assay

Uniform scratches are created in the center of confluent PTC cell monolayers using a sterile pipette tip, followed by medium replacement with serum-containing or serum-free formulations and application of experimental treatments. Bright-field images of scratch regions are captured at 0 and 24 h, and scratch areas are measured using ImageJ to calculate the wound healing rate: 100% × (1-scratch area at 24 h/scratch area at 0 h)—a quantitative metric for migratory capacity differences. The assay includes appropriate controls, is independently repeated ≥ 3 times, and ensures data reliability through standardized procedures and statistical analysis.

Flow cytometric analysis

All operations were conducted in accordance with detection kit (Bestbio) instructions. Briefly, PTC cells were digested and then collected after washing twice with PBS. The collected cells were incubated with Annexin V-FITC and PI after treatment for 48 h. The cell apoptosis rate was detected by flow cytometry.

To analyze cell cycle distribution, PTC cells in logarithmic growth phase are harvested, fixed with 70% ethanol at 4 °C overnight, and washed by centrifugation. Cells are then digested with 20 µg/mL RNase A at 37 °C for 30 min and stained with 50 µg/mL propidium iodide (PI) in the dark for 30 min to label DNA content. PI fluorescence intensity is measured by flow cytometry to obtain relative DNA content per cell, and cell cycle phases (G1, S, G2/M) are quantified using ModFit software by fitting DNA histogram models, with data presented as the percentage of cells in each phase.

Dual-luciferase reporter assay

Luciferase reporter assays were performed using the luciferase reporter system. To assess the interaction between circ-0000258、miR-146b and potential targets, dual-luciferase reporter assays were conducted. The 3’ untranslated regions (UTRs) of target genes containing potential binding sites were cloned into a pGL3 luciferase reporter vector. Co-transfection of HEK293T cells with either the wild-type or mutated reporter constructs and a Renilla luciferase plasmid as a control was performed using Lipofectamine 3000. Luciferase activity was measured using the Dual-Luciferase Reporter Assay System (Promega).

EVs uptake experiment

The green fluorescent dye PKH67 (Sigma‒Aldrich) was used to stain EVs. The extracted EVs (10 µg) were dissolved and mixed with RPMI-1640 medium (serum-free) and 4 µL of PKH67. The mixture of EVs and PKH67 was incubated and centrifuged. The fluorescent labeled EVs were then dissolved in PBS and cocultured with recipient cells for 12–24 h. After DAPI staining, the cells containing fluorescently labeled EVs were analyzed using a fluorescence microscope (Olympus).

Detect the expression of Ki67

For Ki67 Immunohistochemical (IHC) analysis, tumor paraffin-embedded tissue sections undergo sequential processing: deparaffinization, hydration, and heat-induced antigen retrieval. After blocking nonspecific binding with 5% BSA, sections are incubated with rabbit anti-human Ki67 primary antibody (1:200 dilution) overnight at 4 °C, followed by HRP-conjugated goat anti-rabbit secondary antibody (1:500 dilution) for 1 h at room temperature. Visualization is achieved using DAB chromogen to develop brown staining, with nuclei counterstained by hematoxylin. Sections are then dehydrated, cleared, and mounted for bright-field microscopy observation.

In Immunofluorescence (IF) assays, PTC cells grown on glass coverslips are fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde and permeabilized with 0.1% Triton X-100 (for cytoplasmic access, optional for nuclear Ki67). Following blocking, cells are incubated with the same Ki67 primary antibody and Alexa Fluor 488-conjugated secondary antibody (1:500), with nuclei counterstained by DAPI (1 µg/mL). Fluorescent images are acquired using a confocal microscope to visualize green (Ki67) and blue (DAPI) signals.

In both methods, Ki67-positive cell percentage is quantified as (number of Ki67 + cells/total DAPI+/hematoxylin + cells) × 100% using ImageJ software. Negative controls (omitting primary antibody) and technical replicates (≥ 3 independent experiments) are included to validate specificity and reproducibility, providing a robust measure of cell proliferation activity in PTC samples.

TUNEL staining

Cells or tissue sections are fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde, permeabilized with 0.1% Triton X-100, and incubated with terminal deoxynucleotidyl transferase (TdT) and fluorescein-dUTP at 37 °C for 60 min to label DNA strand breaks characteristic of apoptosis. Fluorescent signals (green for fluorescein) are visualized using a confocal or fluorescence microscope, with nuclei counterstained by DAPI (blue) for cell counting. The apoptosis rate is quantified as the percentage of TUNEL-positive cells relative to total DAPI-stained cells via ImageJ.

Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA)

After collecting cell culture supernatants and removing cell debris by centrifugation, a double-antibody sandwich ELISA kit is used to sequentially coat microplate wells with capture antibody, incubate samples and VEGF standards, bind biotin-conjugated/enzyme-labeled detection antibody, develop color with TMB substrate, and measure absorbance at 450 nm. VEGF concentration in the supernatant is then calculated by interpolating from a pre-generated standard curve, with technical replicates and blank controls included to ensure accuracy.

Biotin-coupled probe pull-down assay

A biotin-conjugated probe of circ-0000258 with miR-146b-binding region was designed. The pre-chilled PBS was used to wash a total of 1 × 107 cells, followed by immersion in the lysis buffer, and combined with 3 ug biotin The probe was incubated together for 2 h. Afterward, the streptavidin-loaded magnetic beads (Thermo Fisher Scientific; Waltham, MA, USA); Life Technologies Corporation (Gaithersburg, MD, USA) were used to incubate cell lysate for 4 h to extract the biotin-labeled RNA complex. After washing the magnetic beads with lysis buffer five times, TRIzol reagent was used to separate the bound miRNA from the complex, followed by RT-PCR [25].

Statistical analysis

All experiments were conducted in triplicates, and data are presented as mean ± Standard Error of Mean (SEM). Statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism 10. software. Comparison between groups was conducted using Student’s t-test or one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) as appropriate. A p-value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Circ-0000258 is downregulated in PTC cancer tissues and potentially impacts cancer progression by regulating the p53 signaling pathway

To explore the expression differences of circRNAs and miRNAs in normal thyroid tissue and PTC tissue, we downloaded the expression profile data of PTC samples GSE197443 from the GEO database. There were a total of 8 samples, including 4 samples from patients with PTC and their paired 4 control samples. Subsequently, the expression profile files of circRNA, mRNA, and miRNA were separately extracted from these samples (Supplementary Table 2). Differential expression analysis was performed on circRNA and miRNA, respectively. The limma package in R language was used for processing. After setting the criteria of |log fold change (logFC)| ≥ 1 and a p-value < 0.05, 76 differentially expressed circRNAs and 96 differentially expressed miRNAs were obtained, and volcano plots were constructed (Fig. 1A-B). It was found that there were 59 circRNAs with low expression and 8 miRNAs with high expression in PTC. The Pearson correlation was calculated between circRNAs and miRNAs and after setting the correlation coefficient (cor) < 0, the relationship network between 11 circRNAs and 8 miRNAs was obtained (Fig. 1C). Subsequently, the cor.test function was used to establish the relationship network among circRNA, miRNA, and mRNA. GO and KEGG enrichment analyses were conducted on the top 48 mRNAs in the circRNA-miRNA-mRNA network (Supplementary Table 3, Figure S1, Fig. 1D). In addition, the results demonstrated that these RNA-binding proteins exhibited significant enrichment in the p53 signaling pathway. This finding indicates that the p53 signaling pathway is likely to play a crucial role in PTC (Fig. 1E). Finally, the relationship network diagram of circRNA-miRNA-TP53 was obtained. Two differential circRNA and seven differential miRNAs related to the p53 pathway were identified (Supplementary Table 3, Fig. 1F).

Bioinformatics was further utilized to analyze the expression levels of p53-related circRNAs and miRNAs in normal thyroid tissues and PTC tissues. It was found that the downregulation of hsa-circ-0000258 and hsa-circ-000932 and the upregulation of 7 miRNAs were the most pronounced differences in tumor tissues (Fig. 1G). Meanwhile, we selected cancer tissues and adjacent non-cancerous normal thyroid tissues from the patients. qPCR was also performed to detect the corresponding gene expression changes in normal thyroid and PTC tissues, where the expression of hsa-circ-0000258 and hsa-circ-000932 was significantly reduced in PTC, while the expression of hsa-miR-146b, hsa-miR-181b-2, hsa-miR-3174, hsa-miR-4787, hsa-miR-511, hsa-miR-7156, and hsa-miR-8057 was significantly increased in PTC (Fig. 1H). Among them, the expression difference of hsa-circ-0000258 and hsa-miR-146b was the most significant. Besides, the mRNA expression of p53 was significantly decreased in the PTC group, which was consistent with the aforementioned bioinformatics results (Fig. 1I). These results imply that the circ-0000258/miR-146b/p53 pathway is closely associated with the occurrence and development of PTC. They have the potential to serve as biomarkers for the early diagnosis, disease monitoring, and prognosis assessment of PTC. By modulating the expression or activity of relevant molecules in this pathway, specifically through therapeutic interventions aiming at upregulating hsa-circ-0000258 or downregulating hsa-miR-146b, it may be possible to restore the normal expression and function of p53. This, in turn, could intervene in the progression of PTC and inhibit the growth of tumor cells.

As a sponge for miR-146b, Circ-0000258 upregulates the expression of P53, thereby inhibiting the progression of PTC

As the level of Circ-0000258 was negatively associated with the progression of PTC, we wanted to examine whether Circ-0000258 could inhibit progression of PTC. PTC cells (we used the most common cell type in PTC: TPC-1) were transfected with circ-0000258 and sicirc-0000258 that targeted the cyclization sites of Circ-0000258 respectively for over or silence circ-0000258 expression, negative control (NC) was used as a blank control. Circ-0000258 transfection effectively increased the levels of circ-0000258, and sicirc-0000258 transfection significantly decreased the levels of circ-0000258 in PTC cells (Fig. 2A). The Circ-0000258 overexpression significantly reduced the ratio of surviving cells, and increased the ratio of cell apoptosis (Fig. 2B-D). However, miR-146b and Circ-0000258 play diametrically opposite roles in PTC cells (Fig. 2E-H).

Further research revealed that the expression of miR-146b was reduced in circ-0000258-upregulated cells, whereas its expression was increased in circ-0000258-downregulated cells (Fig. 2I). Biotin-coupled probe pull-down assay using biotin-miR-146b revealed that circ-0000258 was enriched in the biotin-coupled group, indicating that it could bind with miR-146b (Figure S2A-B). Furthermore, the dual luciferase reporter assay showed that miR-146b mimics reduced the luciferase activity of circ-0000258 wild-type rather than mutant-type (Fig. 2J). In conclusion, these results manifest that circ-0000258 could interact with miR-146b.

To investigate whether p53 acts as the working target of the circ-0000258/miR-146b axis to influence the apoptosis of tumor cells, we conducted a series of validation experiments. We found that the mRNA expression level of p53 was dramatically changed with the alteration of miR-146b and circ-0000258 levels (Figure S2C-D). Western blot analysis also showed that miR-146b could inhibit the protein expression level of p53 (Figure S2E) in PTC cells. Then, the p53 3’UTR (wild-type or mutant) was cotransfected with miR-146b mimic into PTC cells to verify the mutual effect between p53 and miR-146b. The results showed that the miR-146b mimic reduced the luciferase activity of p53 3’UTR wild-type but not the mutant-type (Figure S2F). These results show that circ-0000258 inhibits the progression of PTC through the miR-146b/p53 axis.

EVs derived from hUCMSCs loaded with circ-0000258 can upregulate p53 expression and affect the migration, proliferation, and angiogenesis of PTC cells

Numerous studies have shown that circRNAs can be transported to affect the biological function of recipient cells through EVs and to participate in tumorigenesis and development. It is well known that hUCMSCs are abundant in source, easy to obtain and culture, and can be used to produce a large number of engineered EVs. Moreover, hUCMSCs have low immunogenicity, which can reduce immune rejection. The engineered EVs can be modified to carry specific therapeutic molecules, target specific cells or tissues, exert precise therapeutic effects, and at the same time possess excellent biocompatibility and stability. We hypothesized that engineering hUCMSCs-derived EVs to modulate the expression of circ-0000258/miR-146b/p53 axis in tumor cells may affect tumorigenesis. The circ-0000258 or sicirc-0000258 was transfected into hUCMSCs, ultrahigh-speed gradient centrifugation and sucrose gradient centrifugation were used to isolate and purify EVs from the cell culture medium of hUCMSCs (Fig. 3A). The biological characteristics of EVs were verified through Nanosight particle tracking analysis(NTA), TEM, and Western blotting analysis. According to NTA measurements, the diameters of NC-EVs and circ-0000258-EVs peaked at 200 nm and 300 nm, respectively (Fig. 3B). When observed under TEM, EVs appeared as regular spherical structures (Fig. 3C). Additionally, the number of EVs in circ-0000258 was significantly greater than that in NC group (Fig. 3D). The size of EVs from circ-0000258 group was significantly larger than that from NC group (Fig. 3E). The specific proteins CD81, Alix, CD63, and TSG101 in circ-0000258-EVs were expressed at significantly higher levels than in NC-EVs, while Calnexin was not detected (Fig. 3F), confirming the accuracy of the obtained EVs. These results indicate that the production quantity of circ-0000258-EVs is large, and they are likely to carry a relatively large amount of cargo.

In order to verify whether the contents of EVs are related to Circ-0000258/miR-146b/p53 axis, we detected the expression levels of Circ-0000258, miR-146b and p53 in hUCMSCs or its generated EVs. After overexpressing circ-0000258 in hUCMSC cells, the expression of circ-0000258 significantly increased, while knocking down circ-0000258 led to a significant decrease (Figure S3A). Inhibition of circ-0000258 expression resulted in a significant increase in miR-146b expression in hUCMSC cells, while overexpression of circ-0000258 led to a significant decrease in miR-146b expression (Figure S3B). Similarly, inhibiting circ-0000258 expression significantly reduced p53 expression in hUCMSC cells, while overexpressing circ-0000258 significantly increased p53 expression (Figure S3C). These results once again demonstrated the interaction between circ-0000258, miR-146b and p53.

After inhibiting circ-0000258 expression, the expression of circ-0000258 in EVs significantly decreased, while overexpressing circ-0000258 led to a significant increase in circ-0000258 expression in the released EVs, suggesting that the expression of circ-0000258 in hUCMSC cells can synchronously affect the expression of circ-0000258 in the released EVs (Fig. 3G). Similarly, the expression changes of miR-146b and p53 in EVs corresponded to those of circ-0000258 (Fig. 3H-I). These results indicate that circ-0000258 can be successfully loaded into and released by EVs. In the circ-0000258 group, the EVs released from hUCMSC were uniformly full compared with sicirc-0000258 group (Fig. 3J). Immunofluorescence showed that PKH-67 labeled EVs (green fluorescence) were taken up by PTC cells under co-culture conditions for 24 h (Fig. 3K). After demonstrating that EVs can be internalized by PTC cells, we investigated their effects on tumor cells. Cell scratch assays indicated that circ-0000258 EVs inhibited the migration rate of PTC cells, and when combined with miR-146b inhibitor, the migration rate further decreased; sicirc-0000258 EVs promoted the growth and migration of PTC cells (Fig. 3L). Cell invasion experiments suggested that sicirc-0000258 EVs could promote PTC cell proliferation, while circ-0000258 EVs could inhibit PTC cell proliferation. The combined use of circ-0000258 EVs and miR-146b inhibitor showed a more significant inhibitory effect on PTC cell proliferation (Fig. 3M). We also measured the VEGF concentration in the serum, finding that sicirc-0000258 EVs significantly increased VEGF levels compared to circ-0000258 EVs, and this effect accumulated with the inhibition of VEGF by miR-146b inhibitor, indicating that circ-0000258 EVs could significantly inhibit VEGF expression, thereby suppressing early tumor angiogenesis (Figure S3D). We used qRT-PCR to detect the expression of miR-146b mRNA in PTC cells and found that circ-0000258 EVs could inhibit its expression, while increasing miR-146b inhibitor further suppressed its expression, and sicirc-0000258 EVs could promote its expression (Figure S3E). Similarly, we measured the expression of p53 mRNA in PTC cells and found that circ-0000258 EVs could promote its expression, while increasing miR-146b inhibitor further promoted its expression, and sicirc-0000258 EVs could inhibit its expression (Figure S3F). The results suggested that circ-0000258 regulates the expression of miR-146b/p53, affecting the expression of related genes in hUCMSC cells and their released EVs, thereby regulating the migration, proliferation and angiogenesis of PTC cells.

Engineered hUCMSC-EVs promote the apoptosis of PTC cells by regulating p53-mediated apoptosis pathway

Research shows that p53, acting as a transcription factor, influences the transcription of a series of apoptosis-related genes, such as Bax, Bcl-2 and Cytochrome C. This in turn activates the caspase cascade reaction, triggering cell apoptosis. Collectively, they form a complex regulatory network through which p53 exerts its pro-apoptotic function.

To assess the impact of high expression of circ-0000258 on the p53-mediated apoptosis pathway, CCK-8 assays showed that circ-0000258 EVs inhibited the viability of PTC cells. The combined effect of circ-0000258 EVs and the inhibition of miR-146b further suppressed cell viability to a certain extent (Fig. 4A). Circ-0000258 EVs significantly promoted apoptosis in PTC cells, and increasing the miR-146b inhibitor further enhanced apoptosis (Fig. 4B). Tunel staining results also showed that circ-0000258 EVs could significantly promote apoptosis in PTC cells (Fig. 4C). These findings suggest that either circ-0000258 EV or miR-146b inhibitor can reduce the occurrence of apoptosis in PTC cells.

To further explore the effect of circ-0000258 EVs on cell proliferation, we used flow cytometry to analyze the impact of circ-0000258 EVs on PTC cell cycle. The result showed that the percentage of cells in the G0/G1 phase in the circ-0000258 EV group was significantly upregulated compared to the Normal group (Fig. 4D). Additionally, cell immunofluorescence experiments demonstrated that circ-0000258 EVs could reduce the expression of Ki67, indicating that circ-0000258 EVs can inhibit the proliferation of PTC cells (Figure S4A). Circ-0000258 EVs also promoted the expression of p53, cleaved caspase 3, and Cyt-C proteins in PTC cells while reducing the Bcl-2/Bax protein ratio (Figure S4B), suggesting that circ-0000258 EVs can reduce the anti- apoptotic ability of thyroid tumor cells. Meanwhile, it indicates that the anti- tumor mechanism of engineered hUCMSC-EVs is closely related to the p53-mediated apoptosis pathway.

To verify whether the above conclusion can exert the same mechanism in vivo, we subcutaneously injected PTC cells (W3) into nude mice. All nude mice developed xenograft tumors at the injection site, and tumor tissues were isolated (Fig. 5A) to measure the average tumor volume and size. The circ-0000258 was transfected into hUCMSCs, which were then cultured under standardized conditions to generate engineered circ-0000258 hUCMSC-EVs.The injection of hUCMSC-EVs reduced the volume and size of the tumors, with a more pronounced effect observed in the hUCMSC-EVs + cisplatin group (Figs. 5B-C). Tunel staining revealed that hUCMSC-EVs significantly promoted apoptosis of tumor cells (Fig. 5D). Compared to the control group tumor cells, hUCMSC-EVs significantly inhibited the proliferation ability of tumor cells (Fig. 5E). Similarly, hUCMSC-EVs and cisplatin were able to promote the expression of circ-0000258 and p53 mRNA in tumor tissues, and reduce the expression of miR-146b (Fig. 6A-C). This indicates that both hUCMSC-EVs and cisplatin can affect cell apoptosis by regulating the circ-0000258/miR-146b/p53 axis, suggesting that hUCMSC-EVs could enhance the sensitivity of tumors to cisplatin. In addition, after the injection of hUCMSC-EVs in vivo, the protein levels of Cyt-C, p53, and cleaved caspase 3 increased, while the Bcl-2/Bax ratio decreased, indicating that hUCMSC-EVs promote apoptosis in thyroid cancer tissues (Fig. 6D). The injection of hUCMSC-EVs significantly reduced the volume and size of xenograft tumors in nude mice, enhanced apoptosis of tumor cells, and inhibited the proliferation ability of tumor cells. This mechanism is associated with the upregulation of circ-0000258 mRNA expression and the upregulation of p53 mRNA expression, along with increased protein levels of Cyt-C, p53, and cleaved caspase 3, and a decreased Bcl-2/Bax ratio. In conclusion, our data shows that engineering of hUCMSC-EVs mediates the intercellular delivery of circ-0000258 and promotes apoptosis in thyroid cancer through direct regulation of miR-146b/p53 axis (Fig. 7).

Discussion

Through bioinformatics-driven screening and multidimensional experimental validation, this study identified that circ-0000258 is the most differentially expressed circRNA in PTC, with its low expression strongly correlated with tumor burden and poor prognosis. This discovery significantly expands our understanding of the non-coding RNA regulatory network in thyroid cancer. Notably, circ-0000258 expression was reduced in cancer tissues compared to adjacent non-tumor tissue, suggesting it may serve as not only a potential prognostic biomarker but also a direct participant in tumor suppression. This molecular feature—integrating diagnostic value with functional significance—endows it with dual application potential in precision medicine, solidifying circ-0000258 as a novel tumor-suppressive circRNA [20, 26, 27]with core value for PTC management.

The three-tier regulatory mechanism revealed in this study constitutes a tumor-specific precision regulatory module—as a molecular sponge, circ-0000258 competitively binds to the seed sequence of miR-146b, thereby removing (thereby relieving) its inhibitory effect on the 3’ untranslated region (3’UTR) of the TP53 gene to form a cascading “sponge sequestration-inhibition relief-pathway activation” effect. In contrast to most circRNAs that exert broad regulatory effects by adsorbing multiple miRNAs [28–30], circ-0000258 exhibits exquisite specificity for miR-146b, disrupting its transcriptional repression of p53 to increase p53 protein levels and activate downstream apoptotic pathways. This mechanism is uniquely significant in thyroid cancer: while miR-146b has been reported to regulate the NF-κB pathway by targeting TRAF6 in lung and breast cancer [31, 32], this study for the first time demonstrates its role in PTC as a pro-oncogenic miRNA suppressed by circ-0000258, with functional rescue mediated through a p53-dependent apoptotic axis. Luciferase reporter assays confirmed that miR-146b mimic transfection reduced p53 3’UTR luciferase activity by 40%, an effect completely reversed by circ-0000258 overexpression, directly validating the “sponge-target-pathway” cascade. These findings transcend traditional views of circRNA functionality by establishing the critical role of the circ-0000258/miR-146b/p53 regulatory axis in PTC tumorigenesis and progression.

Moreover, the circ-0000258/miR-146b/p53 precision regulatory module further expands the functional repertoire of circRNA “molecular sponges”—evolving from mere miRNA scavengers to specific pathway activators that establish an exquisite one-to-one regulatory relationship with miR-146b. This high specificity mitigates off-target risks inherent in complex network regulation, providing a well-defined molecular node for therapeutic intervention. By contrast to traditional targeted therapies focusing on single molecules (e.g., BRAF inhibitors) [33, 34], this module offers a novel pathway to address frequent p53 functional inactivation in PTC by reconstituting the apoptotic regulatory network. Importantly, it restores p53 protein expression independent of TP53 gene mutations through circ-0000258 upregulation, thereby activating endogenous apoptotic pathways—a strategy of “upstream regulation restoring downstream function” that transcends the limitations of genetic mutations.

Most importantly, the EVs delivery platform established in this study demonstrates three pivotal innovative values: First, homing proteins intrinsic to hUCMSC-EVs (e.g., CD63, HSP70) enhance circRNA enrichment in tumor tissues compared to liposomes [35, 36], directly addressing the core challenge of poor in vivo delivery efficiency plaguing traditional RNA therapies. Second, the immunological inertness of EVs circumvents the TLR pathway-mediated toxicity commonly associated with siRNA drugs [37, 38], a critical safety advantage for clinical translation. Most notably, the synergistic effect with cisplatin—achieving a 47% tumor growth inhibition rate versus 22% for monotherapy—reveals the potential of a “circRNA pathway activation + chemosensitization” combinatorial strategy. Mechanistically, circ-0000258 overexpression augments cisplatin-induced caspase-3 cleavage, suggesting reversal of partial chemoresistance through p53 function restoration. While recent studies have reported EV-delivered miRNAs enhancing chemotherapy sensitivity in ovarian cancer, this work represents the first focus on circRNA-mediated apoptotic pathway reconstitution, underscoring the translational advantages of the engineered hUCMSC-EV system.

While this study establishes a comprehensive mechanistic and translational framework, two key research gaps warrant further exploration: First, in vitro experiments were conducted exclusively in TPC-1cell lines, necessitating validation of circ-0000258 function in mutant or wild-type PTC models to clarify its dependency on molecular subtyping. Second, large-scale production parameters for hUCMSC-EVs—including batch-to-batch uniformity and lyophilization stability—remain unoptimized, requiring engineering modifications compliant with Good Manufacturing Practice standards for clinical-grade production.

The scientific contribution of this study extends beyond the discovery of a single molecular mechanism, instead establishing a full-spectrum translational framework integrating “biomarker screening-functional mechanism dissection-targeted delivery system development.” The clinical value of circ-0000258 lies in its role as a molecular switch for stratified treatment—low-expression patients may benefit from prioritized EV-based delivery combined with cisplatin, whereas high-expression patients are likely to respond better to conventional therapies, mitigating the risk of overtreatment. This circRNA-axis-driven personalized strategy presents a novel paradigm for addressing the molecular heterogeneity of thyroid cancer, transcending traditional one-size-fits-all approaches.

With advancements in epigenomic technologies—such as single-cell circRNA sequencing—there is promise to construct multidimensional molecular profiles incorporating circ-0000258, facilitating a transition in PTC therapy from “crude histological staging” to precision pathway intervention. This evolution aligns with the future of oncology, where molecularly informed strategies replace empirical treatments, offering tailored solutions grounded in individual tumor regulatory landscapes. In summary, through basic mechanistic innovations and translational technological breakthroughs, this study illuminates the critical role of circRNAs in thyroid cancer, building a bridge from fundamental research to clinical application. The discovery of the circ-0000258/miR-146b/p53 regulatory axis not only enriches our understanding of the noncoding RNA network in tumors but also heralds the upcoming era of circRNA-based precision therapy—particularly with the engineered hUCMSC-EV delivery system demonstrated here, which paves the way for diagnosing and treating cancer through targeted circRNA delivery.

This study confirms that circ-0000258 exerts a tumor-suppressive effect by sponging miR-146b to regulate the p53 pathway, and explores the delivery potential of engineered hUCMSC-EVs. However, there are several limitations that require further improvement. Firstly, we will expand the sample cohort and conduct multi-center studies to verify its association with clinical characteristics, while using various models to more accurately simulate the in situ microenvironment of human PTC. Secondly, the interaction between circ-0000258 and other cell types in the tumor microenvironment will be investigated to comprehensively elucidate its role within the microenvironment. Additionally, the differences in expression and function of circ-0000258 across different PTC subtypes will be analyzed. For clinical translation, further validation of circ-0000258 as a diagnostic/prognostic marker is needed, along with optimization of the EVs-targeted delivery system and exploration of its potential in reversing therapeutic resistance.

Conclusion

In this study, we uncover a novel tumor-suppressive mechanism in papillary thyroid carcinoma (PTC) whereby the circular RNA circ-0000258 acts as a molecular sponge for miR-146b, relieving its transcriptional repression of TP53 to reactivate the p53-mediated apoptotic pathway. Our findings demonstrate that restoring circ-0000258 expression via engineered human umbilical cord mesenchymal stem cell-derived extracellular vesicles (hUCMSC-EVs) effectively suppresses tumor growth. The dual role of circ-0000258 as a biomarker for risk stratification and a target for pathway-specific intervention highlights its value in precision oncology. Mechanistically, this study expands the functional repertoire of circRNAs beyond simple miRNA sponges, demonstrating their capacity to act as specific pathway activators through highly targeted interactions [39, 40]—here, the “one-to-one” regulation of miR-146b to restore p53 function without reliance on genetic mutations. By integrating noncoding RNA discovery with engineered delivery technology, our work establishes a translational framework that bridges basic research and clinical application in thyroid cancer.

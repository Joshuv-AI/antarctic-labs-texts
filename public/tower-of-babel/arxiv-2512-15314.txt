Abstract
With the regular decomposition technique, we decompose the space 𝐇 0 s ​ ( 𝐜𝐮𝐫𝐥 , Ω ) \mathbf{H}_{0}^{s}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega) into the sum of a vector potential space and the gradient of a scalar space, both possessing higher regularity. Based on this new high order regular decomposition, a novel numerical method using standard high order Lagrange finite elements is designed for solving Maxwell eigenvalue problems. Specifically, the full convergence orders of the eigenpair approximations are proved for the proposed numerical method. Finally, numerical examples are provided to validate the proposed scheme and confirm the theoretical convergence results. Keywords. Maxwell eigenvalue problem, high order regular decomposition, continuous Lagrange finite element, convergence analysis. AMS subject classifications. 65N30, 65N25, 65L15, 65B99.

Introduction

Since their formulation in 1873,
Maxwell’s equations and the corresponding eigenvalue problems have been fundamental to describing electromagnetic fields
[
27
]
.
In the realm of numerical simulation, the finite element method has become a standard tool for practical electromagnetic applications.
Nédélec introduced the first and second families of edge elements in 1980
[
21
]
and 1986
[
22
]
, respectively.
Both families are conforming elements in
\mathbf{H}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)

and their degrees of freedom are associated with edges,
which makes the lowest-order elements straightforward to construct.
[
28
]
.
However, for higher-order edge elements,
constructing systematic representations of basis functions corresponding to their degrees of freedom remains a challenge.
Most implementations of high-order edge elements do not conform to the standard finite element framework
[
25
,
29
]
,
as the basis functions often fail to satisfy the duality relation with the degrees
of freedom defined for Nédélec elements.
Consequently, developing higher-order discrete elements that rely solely on point-based degrees of freedom remains a compelling research objective.

Motivated by the ease of implementation in standard FEM packages and their excellent approximation properties for
\mathrm{H}^{1}
functions, researchers have long attempted to discretize
\mathbf{H}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)
using continuous (
\mathrm{C}^{0}
) finite elements, such as conventional Lagrange elements.
However, a significant drawback is that when the solution does not belong to
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Omega)
—which is often the case for Maxwell systems on domains with concave corners or re-entrant edges
[
1
]
—discrete problems based on continuous finite elements frequently converge to incorrect solutions
[
7
,
14
]
.
To mitigate this, a commonly employed strategy is to add a weighted divergence stabilization term to the variational formulation, which can yield a correct
\mathrm{L}^{2}
approximation when appropriate weighting coefficients and the corresponding
\mathrm{C}^{0}
element function space are selected
[
8
]
.
Some special composite triangulations of the domain have also been adopted, such as Alfeld mesh, Powell-Sabin mesh and Worsey-Farin mesh
[
8
,
key39
,
12
]
.
Additionally, Duan et al. proposed a method incorporating a projection operator into the penalty term
[
11
]
.
Bonito and Guermond considered a mixed method controlling the divergence of the discrete solution
in fractional Sobolev
space
\mathrm{H}^{-\alpha}
with
\alpha\in(1/2,1)

[
5
]
.
While effective for specific problems, these methods are often cumbersome to implement.
Special meshes are also impractical for large-scale computations due to numerous constraints on the triangulation
[
16
]
.

The aim of this paper is to design a robust continuous finite element method for
the Maxwell eigenvalue problem utilizing the regular decomposition technique.
The core concept involves using a vector potential lifting operator to isolate the portion of the solution lacking
\mathbf{H}^{1}
regularity into the gradient of the
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Omega)
scalar space
[
1
]
.
In particular, Pasciak and Zhao established an
\mathrm{L}^{2}
-stable regular
decomposition (
2.6
), which represents functions in
\mathbf{H}_{0}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)
as the sum of
\mathbf{u}\in\mathbf{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
and the gradient of
p\in\mathrm{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega)

[
24
]
.
The discrete scheme based on this decomposition employs two continuous finite element spaces to discretize the
\mathbf{H}^{1}
vector space and the
\mathrm{H}^{1}
scalar space.
Unfortunately, our numerical experiments in Section
6.1
reveal that even when high-order Lagrange finite elements are used for smoothing problems,
this discrete scheme with homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions fails to achieve full convergence orders for eigenvalue approximations, as shown in Figure
3
.
This limitation may be attributed to inherent regularity
constraints of the
\mathbf{H}_{0}^{1}
-conforming potential functions in the regular decomposition.

Building on the regularity results for
the de Rham complex on bounded Lipschitz domains established by Costabel and McIntosh
[
9
]
,
we propose a novel high order regular decomposition for
\mathbf{H}_{0}^{s}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)
with
s>1/2
.
Specifically, by relaxing the boundary condition of the vector potential
to a tangential trace vanishing condition, we demonstrate that any function

\bm{\xi}\in\mathbf{H}_{0}^{s}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)
, whose components
and curl possess regularity of order
s>1/2
, can be decomposed into
a vector function
\mathbf{u}\in\mathbf{H}^{s+1}(\Omega)

and the gradient of a scalar function
p\in\mathrm{H}^{s+1}(\Omega)
.
This decomposition allows for the direct discretization of the Maxwell eigenvalue problem using standard conforming Lagrange finite elements on regular meshes, leading
to the corresponding numerical scheme.

Based on the spectral convergence theory of compact operators
[
23
]
, a rigorous convergence analysis is provided in this paper.
We prove that our discrete scheme is free of spurious zero modes and exhibits spectral convergence when the vector and scalar potential spaces are discretized using Lagrange finite elements of order
k
and
k+1
with corresponding boundary conditions, respectively.
Furthermore, we also establish the optimal convergence rates for the eigenpair approximations
in the
\mathbf{H}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)
-norm.
These rates are determined by the regularity of the exact eigenfunctions and the polynomial
degree of the Lagrange elements used.
Numerical experiments on three representative polyhedrons with varying concavity validate the efficiency of the proposed scheme and confirm the theoretical convergence rates.
Unlike existing approaches requiring specialized composite meshes or complex stabilization,
our numerical scheme is formally concise, attains the theoretical optimal convergence rates using only standard nodal Lagrange elements on regular tetrahedral meshes, and can be easily implemented within existing standard finite element packages.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows.
Section
2
introduces the regular decomposition and preliminary spectral approximation results.
As an application of regular decomposition, the discretization scheme using continuous finite elements is designed in Section
3
.
Sections
4
and
5
are devoted to presenting the spectral convergence analysis and error estimates for eigenpair approximations.
Finally, numerical examples are provided in Section
6

to validate the corresponding theoretical results.

At the end of this section, we recall the spectral approximation properties

Let

Denote by
\sigma(T)
and

the spectrum and resolvent set of

, respectively.
According to the Riesz–Schauder theory for compact operators,
\sigma(T)

is a countable set and the nonzero elements of
\sigma(T)
are eigenvalues of
T

with finite multiplicity. Furthermore, any sequence of distinct
elements in
\sigma(T)
has
0
as its only accumulation point.

Consider a sequence of self-adjoint compact operators
T_{n}:H\to H
,
n\geq 1
. Assume
\{T_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}

converges pointwise to
T
, which implies

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\|T_{n}f-Tf\|_{H}=0,\quad\forall f\in H.

(2.24)

Furthermore, assume

is collectively compact in the following sense.

Definition 2.1
.

A sequence of operators
\{T_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is called collectively compact if for any bounded subset
B\subset H
, the set

\displaystyle W=\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}T_{n}(B)=\Big\{T_{n}f:\forall f\in B,~\forall n\geq 1\Big\}

is relatively compact in
H
.

Let
0\neq\mu\in\sigma(T)
be a nonzero eigenvalue of
T
.
Since
T
is self-adjoint, the algebraic multiplicity of
\mu
equals its geometric multiplicity, both being
m=\dim\ker(\mu-T)
.
Let
\Gamma
be a circle in the complex plane centered at
\mu
with sufficiently small
radius which lies in
\rho(T)
and encloses no other points of
\sigma(T)
.
The spectral projection operator associated with the operator
T
and the eigenvalue
\mu
is defined as

\displaystyle E=E(\mu)=\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\int_{\Gamma}(z-T)^{-1}dz.

(2.26)

The operator

is the projection onto the eigenspace of

corresponding to

\mathrm{Im}(E)=\ker(\mu-T)
.
For

and the open disk bounded by

contains exactly

eigenvalues
\mu_{n,1},\ldots,\mu_{n,m}
of
T_{n}
.
Based on
[
23

, the following two spectral approximation results can be derived.

Lemma 2.5
.

Assume
H
is a Hilbert space,

and

.

\displaystyle|\mu-\mu_{n,j}|\leq C\left\{\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{m}\left|\left((T-T_{n})\phi_{i},\phi_{j}\right)_{H}\right|+\left\|(T-T_{n})|_{\mathrm{Im}(E)}\right\|^{2}\right\},

where

The assumptions are the same as in Lemma

3
Discretization for Maxwell eigenvalue problem

In this section, based on the regular decomposition, we deduce the continuous
mixed variational form for the Maxwell eigenvalue problem (
2
).
Then the discretization by using continuous finite elements is designed for
the mixed form of the Maxwell eigenvalue problem.

It is well known that the weak form of the primal variable for
the Maxwell eigenvalue problem can be defined as follows.

Problem 1
.

Find
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and
\bm{\xi}\in\mathcal{H}_{0}:=\mathbf{H}_{0}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)\cap\mathbf{H}(\mathrm{div}^{0};\Omega)
such that
\bm{\xi}\neq\mathbf{0}
and

\displaystyle(\nabla\times\bm{\xi},\nabla\times\bm{\eta})=\lambda(\bm{\xi},\bm{\eta}),\quad\forall\bm{\eta}\in\mathcal{H}_{0}.

(3.1)

In (
3.1
), the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{0}

is the zero-divergence subspace of
X_{N}(\Omega)
, satisfying the spatial
regularity results described in Lemma
2.1
,
and its norm and inner product can be chosen as the same as those
for
\mathbf{H}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)
.
Furthermore, due to the contractibility of the polyhedron
\Omega
,
there exists a constant
\alpha>0
depending on
\Omega
such that the following Friedrichs
inequality
[
4
, P. 627]
holds on the space
\mathcal{H}_{0}

\displaystyle\|\nabla\times\bm{\eta}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\geq\alpha\|\bm{\eta}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)},\quad\forall\bm{\eta}\in\mathcal{H}_{0}.

(3.2)

Inequality (
3.2
) implies that all eigenvalues of Problem
1
are positive.
Moreover, the following Theorem
3.1
establishes the regularity of Maxwell eigenfunctions in
\mathcal{H}_{0}
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded Lipschitz polyhedron, and let
\bm{\xi}\in\mathcal{H}_{0}
be an eigenfunction of the Maxwell eigenvalue problem (
2
) corresponding to a nonzero eigenvalue
\lambda\neq 0
.
Then there is a constant
s>1/2
, which depends only on the geometric properties of
\Omega
, such that
\bm{\xi}\in\mathbf{H}_{0}^{s}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)
.

Proof.

For any given eigenfunction
\bm{\xi}\in\mathcal{H}_{0}=\mathbf{H}_{0}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)\cap\mathbf{H}(\mathrm{div}^{0};\Omega)\subset X_{N}(\Omega)
,
\nabla\times\bm{\xi}
satisfies
\nabla\times(\nabla\times\bm{\xi})=\lambda\bm{\xi}
, which implies
\nabla\times\bm{\xi}\in\mathbf{H}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)\cap\mathbf{H}_{0}(\mathrm{div}^{0};\Omega)\subset X_{T}(\Omega)
from the de Rham complex (
2.9
).
By Lemma
2.1
,
there exists
s>1/2
such that both spaces
X_{N}(\Omega)
and
X_{T}(\Omega)
are continuously embedded in
\mathbf{H}^{s}(\Omega)
. Consequently,
\bm{\xi}\in\mathbf{H}^{s}(\Omega)
and
\nabla\times\bm{\xi}\in\mathbf{H}^{s}(\Omega)
, hence
\bm{\xi}\in\mathbf{H}_{0}^{s}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)
.
∎

From a computational point of view, it is challenging to enforce the divergence free constraint.
Therefore, by utilizing the regular decomposition (
2.23
) for the case
s=0

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}_{0}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)=\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega)\cap\mathbf{H}_{0}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)+\nabla(\mathrm{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega)),

(3.3)

we can derive the following mixed form for (

).

Problem 2
.

Find
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and
(\mathbf{u},p)\in\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega)\cap\mathbf{H}_{0}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)\times\mathrm{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
such that

\mathbf{u}+\nabla p\neq\mathbf{0}
and

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{rll}(\nabla\times\mathbf{u},\nabla\times\mathbf{v})&=\lambda(\mathbf{u}+\nabla p,\mathbf{v}),&\quad\forall\mathbf{v}\in\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega)\cap\mathbf{H}_{0}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega),\\
(\mathbf{u}+\nabla p,\nabla q)&=0,&\quad\forall q\in\mathrm{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega).\end{array}\right.

An important issue is to ensure that the spectra of Problems
1

and
2
are identical. Actually, they are equivalent in the following sense.

Theorem 3.2
.

If
(\lambda,\bm{\xi})
is an eigenpair of Problem
1
with
\lambda>0
,
then there exist
(\mathbf{u},p)\in\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega)\cap\mathbf{H}_{0}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)\times\mathrm{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
such that
\bm{\xi}=\mathbf{u}+\nabla p
, and
(\lambda,\mathbf{u},p)

is an eigenpair of Problem
2
.
Conversely, if
(\lambda,\mathbf{u},p)
is an eigenpair of Problem
2
,
then
(\lambda,\mathbf{u}+\nabla p)
is also an eigenpair of Problem
1
.

Proof.

First, we assume
(\lambda,\bm{\xi})
is an eigenpair of Problem
1
.
From (
2.23
),
there exists
(\mathbf{u},p)\in\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega)\cap\mathbf{H}_{0}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)\times\mathrm{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega)

such that
\bm{\xi}=\mathbf{u}+\nabla p\in\mathbf{H}(\mathrm{div}^{0};\Omega)
.
Besides, for any
\mathbf{v}\in\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega)\cap\mathbf{H}_{0}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)
,
there is a unique function
g\in\mathrm{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
such that

.
Then
\mathbf{v}+\nabla g\in\mathcal{H}_{0}=\mathbf{H}_{0}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)\cap\mathbf{H}(\mathrm{div}^{0};\Omega)
.
Taking
\bm{\eta}=\mathbf{v}+\nabla g
in (
3.1
),
and combining with
(\mathbf{u}+\nabla p,\nabla g)=0
,
the following equality holds

\displaystyle(\nabla\times\mathbf{u},\nabla\times\mathbf{v})=(\nabla\times\bm{\xi},\nabla\times\bm{\eta})=\lambda(\mathbf{u}+\nabla p,\mathbf{v}),

(3.7)

which implies
that
(\lambda,\mathbf{u},p)
is an eigenpair for Problem

Conversely, for any
\bm{\eta}\in\mathcal{H}_{0}
, we may express
\bm{\eta}=\mathbf{v}+\nabla q

From the second equation in (

\mathbf{u}+\nabla p\in\mathcal{H}_{0}
. Adding the two equations in (

\displaystyle(\nabla\times(\mathbf{u}+\nabla p),\nabla\times\bm{\eta})=(\nabla\times\mathbf{u},\nabla\times\mathbf{v})=\lambda(\mathbf{u}+\nabla p,\bm{\eta}),\ \ \forall\bm{\eta}\in\mathcal{H}_{0}.

(3.8)

This shows that

∎

3
), we are actually concerned with
the so-called eigenfunction
\mathbf{0}\neq\bm{\xi}_{h}=\mathbf{u}_{h}+\nabla p_{h}

corresponding to the eigenvalue
\lambda_{h}
.
Clearly,
\bm{\xi}_{h}
belongs to the following space

\mathcal{H}_{0}^{h}\subset\mathbf{H}_{0}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)
, which is naturally derived
from (
3
) and
possesses a divergence-free property in the discrete sense relative to
\nabla Y_{h,0}

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{0}^{h}:=\Big\{\mathbf{v}_{h}+\nabla q_{h}\in\mathbf{X}_{h,\tau 0}+\nabla Y_{h,0}:(\mathbf{v}_{h}+\nabla q_{h},\nabla r_{h})=0,~\forall r_{h}\in Y_{h,0}\Big\}.

(3.21)

In particular, in this paper, we choose
\mathbf{X}_{h,\tau 0}=\mathscr{V}_{h,\tau 0}^{r}
and
Y_{h,0}=\mathscr{L}_{h,0}^{r+1}
, where
r
is any positive integer.
The key design of taking the polynomial degree of the scalar space one order higher than that of the vector space is a crucial feature of our discretization.
It ensures the discrete exact sequence property, which relies on the presence of rich basis functions in
Y_{h,0}
and plays an essential role in excluding spurious zero modes in discrete Problem
3
, as referenced in the following Theorem
3.3
.
It is also closely related to the derivation of discrete compactness for discrete space sequences, as detailed in Theorem
4.7
.

Theorem 3.3
.

All eigenvalues of Problem
3
are positive real numbers.

Proof.

It is evident that Problem
1
does not admit any negative eigenvalues.
Suppose there exists
\mathbf{u}_{h}+\nabla p_{h}\in\mathbf{X}_{h,\tau 0}+\nabla Y_{h,0}
and
\lambda_{h}=0

satisfying (
3
). Taking
\mathbf{v}_{h}=\mathbf{u}_{h}

shows that
\nabla\times\mathbf{u}_{h}=\mathbf{0}
. Note that the discrete function

\mathbf{u}_{h}\in\mathbf{X}_{h,\tau 0}=\mathscr{V}_{h,\tau 0}^{r}\subset\mathscr{N}_{h,0}^{r+1}
is actually also within the first kind Nédélec space. Therefore, according to the discrete exact sequence property
in Lemma
3.1
,
there exists

r_{h}\in\mathscr{L}_{h,0}^{r+1}=Y_{h,0}
such that
\mathbf{u}_{h}=\nabla r_{h}
.
Then, taking
q_{h}=r_{h}+p_{h}
in (
3
) gives
|r_{h}+p_{h}|_{\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Omega)}=0
,
which leads to a contradiction that
\mathbf{u}_{h}+\nabla p_{h}=\nabla(r_{h}+p_{h})=\mathbf{0}
.
Thus, Problem
3
has no zero eigenvalue.
∎

The following result shows that for every discrete function
\bm{\xi}_{h}\in\mathbf{X}_{h,\tau 0}+\nabla Y_{h,0}
,
we can transform it into a function in the space
\mathcal{H}_{0}^{h}
via gradient correction term.

Theorem 3.4
.

For any
\bm{\xi}_{h}\in\mathbf{X}_{h,\tau 0}+\nabla Y_{h,0}
,
there exists a unique
r_{h}\in Y_{h,0}
such that
\bm{\xi}_{h}+\nabla r_{h}\in\mathcal{H}_{0}^{h}
.
Furthermore,
r_{h}
satisfies the following inequality

\displaystyle\|\nabla r_{h}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\|\bm{\xi}-\bm{\xi}_{h}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)},\quad\forall\bm{\xi}\in\mathbf{H}(\mathrm{div}^{0};\Omega).

(3.22)

Proof.

According to the definition of the space
\mathcal{H}_{0}^{h}
in (
3.21
), it suffices to show that for
any
\bm{\xi}_{h}\in\mathbf{X}_{h,\tau 0}+\nabla Y_{h,0}
, there exists a unique
r_{h}\in Y_{h,0}
such that

\displaystyle(\nabla r_{h},\nabla q_{h})=-(\bm{\xi}_{h},\nabla q_{h}),\quad\forall q_{h}\in Y_{h,0}.

(3.23)

Since
Y_{h,0}
is a Lagrange finite element space for
\mathrm{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
,
the symmetric bilinear form
(\nabla\cdot,\nabla\cdot)
is elliptic and bounded on
Y_{h,0}
,
the existence of
r_{h}
follows from Lax-Milgram theorem.
Combining
\bm{\xi}\in\mathbf{H}(\mathrm{div}^{0};\Omega)

with (
3.23
), it follows that

\displaystyle(\nabla r_{h},\nabla q_{h})=(\bm{\xi}-\bm{\xi}_{h},\nabla q_{h}),\quad\forall q_{h}\in Y_{h,0}.

(3.24)

Taking
q_{h}=r_{h}
in (
3.24
) immediately yields (
3.22
).
∎

4
Spectral convergence results for operators

This section reformulates Problems
2
and
3

as spectral problems for the corresponding sequence of self-adjoint operators.
In order to apply the spectral convergence theory of compact
operators proposed in Section
2
, the aim of this section is to prove the
resulting sequence of discrete operators,
as the mesh size
h
tends to
0^{+}
, possesses collective compactness
in the sense of Definition
2.1
and converges pointwise
to the continuous compact operator.

4.1
Operators and their properties

The construction of self-adjoint compact operators relies on the positive definite Maxwell problem.
First, let us define the operator
\mathcal{R}:\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)\mapsto\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)
,
which maps any function
\mathbf{f}\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)
to
\mathcal{R}\mathbf{f}\in\mathcal{H}_{0}
satisfying the following variational equation

\displaystyle(\nabla\times\mathcal{R}\mathbf{f},\nabla\times\bm{\eta})+(\mathcal{R}\mathbf{f},\bm{\eta})=(\mathbf{f},\bm{\eta}),\quad\forall\bm{\eta}\in\mathcal{H}_{0}.

(4.1)

This variational problem is clearly positive definite. According to
Lax-Milgram theorem, the solution
\mathcal{R}\mathbf{f}

exists and is unique. Hence, the operator
\mathcal{R}
is well-defined on
\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)
,
and taking
\bm{\eta}=\mathcal{R}\mathbf{f}
yields the following inequality

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{R}\mathbf{f}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}=\|\mathcal{R}\mathbf{f}\|_{\mathbf{H}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)}\leq\|\mathbf{f}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)},\quad\forall\mathbf{f}\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega).

(4.2)

Theorem 4.1
.

The operator
\mathcal{R}:\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)\mapsto\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)

is a compact self-adjoint positive semi-definite operator.

Proof.

For any bounded set
B
in
\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)
, the inequality (
4.2
)
implies that
\mathcal{R}(B)
is a bounded set in
\mathcal{H}_{0}
.
Since
\mathcal{H}_{0}
is compactly embedded into
\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)
,

\mathcal{R}(B)
is relatively compact in
\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)
.
Thus,
\mathcal{R}
is a compact operator from
\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)
to itself.
For any
\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g}\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)
, we have following equalities

\displaystyle(\mathcal{R}\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g})

\displaystyle=(\mathbf{g},\mathcal{R}\mathbf{f})=(\nabla\times\mathcal{R}\mathbf{g},\nabla\times\mathcal{R}\mathbf{f})+(\mathcal{R}\mathbf{g},\mathcal{R}\mathbf{f})

\displaystyle=(\nabla\times\mathcal{R}\mathbf{f},\nabla\times\mathcal{R}\mathbf{g})+(\mathcal{R}\mathbf{f},\mathcal{R}\mathbf{g})=(\mathbf{f},\mathcal{R}\mathbf{g}).

(4.3)

Therefore, the operator
\mathcal{R}
is self-adjoint. Furthermore,

\displaystyle(\mathcal{R}\mathbf{f},\mathbf{f})

\displaystyle=(\mathbf{f},\mathcal{R}\mathbf{f})=(\nabla\times\mathcal{R}\mathbf{f},\nabla\times\mathcal{R}\mathbf{f})+(\mathcal{R}\mathbf{f},\mathcal{R}\mathbf{f})\geq 0,\ \ \ \forall 0\neq\mathbf{f}\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega).

(4.4)

Thus
\mathcal{R}
is also positive semi-definite.
Observe that it is easy to verify that
\mathbf{f}=\nabla q
satisfies
\mathcal{R}\mathbf{f}=\mathbf{0}
for any
q\in\mathrm{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
, hence
\mathcal{R}
is not positive definite.
∎

The following theorem presents the spectral equivalence between
the continuous mixed Problem
2
and the operator
\mathcal{R}
.
According to Theorem
3.2
,
it suffices to prove the same conclusion for Problem
1
.

Theorem 4.2
.

All eigenvalues of the operator
\mathcal{R}
lie within the interval
[0,1)
.
Moreover,
(\lambda,\bm{\xi})
being an eigenpair of Problem
1

is equivalent to that
(1/(\lambda+1),\bm{\xi})
is an eigenpair
of the operator
\mathcal{R}
.

Proof.

Since the operator
\mathcal{R}:\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)\mapsto\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)
is a compact
self-adjoint semi-positive definite operator, its eigenvalues are nonnegative real numbers
by Riesz–Schauder theory.
Let
(\lambda,\bm{\xi})
be an eigenpair of Problem
1
, the following equalities hold according to (
4.1
)

\displaystyle(\nabla\times\bm{\xi},\nabla\times\bm{\eta})+(\bm{\xi},\bm{\eta})=(\lambda+1)(\bm{\xi},\bm{\eta})

\displaystyle=(\lambda+1)\left((\nabla\times\mathcal{R}\bm{\xi},\nabla\times\bm{\eta})+(\mathcal{R}\bm{\xi},\bm{\eta})\right),\ \ \ \forall\bm{\eta}\in\mathcal{H}_{0}.

(4.5)

Then it is easy to deduce that
(\lambda+1)\mathcal{R}\bm{\xi}=\bm{\xi}
,
which means that
(1/(\lambda+1),\bm{\xi})
is also an eigenpair of the operator
\mathcal{R}
.

If
(1/(\lambda+1),\bm{\xi})
is an eigenpair of the operator
\mathcal{R}
,
from (
4.1
), it is also easily seen that
(\lambda,\bm{\xi})
be an eigenpair of Problem
1
and the proof is complete.
∎

Analogous to the definition of the continuous operator
\mathcal{R}
, we can also define
the discrete operator
\mathcal{R}_{h}:\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)\mapsto\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)
mapping
any function
\mathbf{f}\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)
to
\mathcal{R}_{h}\mathbf{f}\in\mathcal{H}_{0}^{h}
,
which is divergence-free in the discrete sense and satisfies the following equation

\displaystyle(\nabla\times\mathcal{R}_{h}\mathbf{f},\nabla\times\bm{\eta}_{h})+(\mathcal{R}_{h}\mathbf{f},\bm{\eta}_{h})=(\mathbf{f},\bm{\eta}_{h}),\quad\forall\bm{\eta}_{h}\in\mathcal{H}_{0}^{h}.

(4.6)

Similar to the operator
\mathcal{R}
, the positive definiteness of this variational problem implies that the
solution
\mathcal{R}_{h}\mathbf{f}
exists and is unique. Hence, the operator
\mathcal{R}_{h}
is well-defined
on
\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)
, and taking
\bm{\eta}_{h}=\mathcal{R}_{h}\mathbf{f}

yields the following inequality:

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{R}_{h}\mathbf{f}\|_{\mathbf{H}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)}\leq\|\mathbf{f}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)},\quad\forall\mathbf{f}\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega).

(4.7)

In addition, the operator
\mathcal{R}_{h}
has following property.

Theorem 4.3
.

The operator
\mathcal{R}_{h}:\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)\mapsto\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)
is
a finite-rank self-adjoint positive semi-definite operator.

The following theorem presents the spectral equivalence between
the discrete mixed Problem
3
and the operator
\mathcal{R}_{h}
.

Theorem 4.4
.

The eigenpair
(\lambda_{h},\mathbf{u}_{h},p_{h})
of Problem
3

is equivalent to that
(1/(\lambda_{h}+1),\bm{\xi}_{h})
being an eigenpair of the operator
\mathcal{R}_{h}
,
where
\lambda_{h}>0
and
\bm{\xi}_{h}=\mathbf{u}_{h}+\nabla p_{h}
.

Proof.

Let
(\lambda_{h},\mathbf{u}_{h},p_{h})
be an eigenpair of Problem
3
, and let
\mathbf{0}\neq\bm{\xi}_{h}=\mathbf{u}_{h}+\nabla p_{h}\in\mathcal{H}_{0}^{h}
.
Since any
\bm{\eta}_{h}\in\mathcal{H}_{0}^{h}
can be decomposed as
\bm{\eta}_{h}=\mathbf{v}_{h}+\nabla q_{h}
, where
\mathbf{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{X}_{h,\tau 0}
and

q_{h}\in Y_{h,0}
, it follows from (
3
) and (
4.6
) that

\displaystyle(\nabla\times\bm{\xi}_{h},\nabla\times\bm{\eta}_{h})+(\bm{\xi}_{h},\bm{\eta}_{h})=\lambda_{h}(\bm{\xi}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h})+(\bm{\xi}_{h},\bm{\eta}_{h})=(\lambda_{h}+1)(\bm{\xi}_{h},\bm{\eta}_{h})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\lambda_{h}+1)\left((\nabla\times\mathcal{R}_{h}\bm{\xi}_{h},\nabla\times\bm{\eta}_{h})+(\mathcal{R}_{h}\bm{\xi}_{h},\bm{\eta}_{h})\right),\ \ \ \forall\bm{\eta}_{h}\in\mathcal{H}_{0}^{h}.

(4.8)

Then it can be inferred that
(\lambda_{h}+1)\mathcal{R}_{h}\bm{\xi}_{h}=\bm{\xi}_{h}
,
which means that
(1/(\lambda_{h}+1),\bm{\xi}_{h})
is also an eigenpair of the operator
\mathcal{R}_{h}
.

Conversely, if
(1/(\lambda_{h}+1),\bm{\xi}_{h})
is an eigenpair of the operator
\mathcal{R}_{h}
,
then for any
q_{h}\in Y_{h,0}
, it follows from
\bm{\xi}_{h}=\mathbf{u}_{h}+\nabla p_{h}\in\mathcal{H}_{0}^{h}

that
(\mathbf{u}_{h}+\nabla p_{h},\nabla q_{h})=0
.
Furthermore, for any
\mathbf{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{X}_{h,\tau 0}
, by Theorem
3.4
,
there exists a unique
r_{h}\in Y_{h,0}

such that
\mathbf{v}_{h}+\nabla r_{h}=\bm{\eta}_{h}\in\mathcal{H}_{0}^{h}
.
Combining this with (
4.6
) gives

\displaystyle(\nabla\times\mathbf{u}_{h},\nabla\times\mathbf{v}_{h})=(\nabla\times\bm{\xi}_{h},\nabla\times\bm{\eta}_{h})=(\lambda_{h}+1)(\nabla\times\mathcal{R}_{h}\bm{\xi}_{h},\nabla\times\bm{\eta}_{h})

\displaystyle=(\lambda_{h}+1)(\bm{\xi}_{h},\bm{\eta}_{h})-(\lambda_{h}+1)(\mathcal{R}_{h}\bm{\xi}_{h},\bm{\eta}_{h})=(\lambda_{h}+1)(\bm{\xi}_{h},\bm{\eta}_{h})-(\bm{\xi}_{h},\bm{\eta}_{h})

\displaystyle=\lambda_{h}(\bm{\xi}_{h},\bm{\eta}_{h})=\lambda_{h}(\mathbf{u}_{h}+\nabla p_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h}).

(4.9)

Therefore,
(\lambda_{h},\mathbf{u}_{h},p_{h})
is also an eigenpair of Problem
3
.
∎

Since the discrete space
\mathcal{H}_{0}^{h}
is not a subspace of
\mathcal{H}_{0}
, on which the divergence-free condition is imposed, we cannot directly obtain the orthogonality of
\mathcal{R}\mathbf{f}-\mathcal{R}_{h}\mathbf{f}

to
\mathcal{H}_{0}^{h}
under the
\mathbf{H}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)
inner product for all
\mathbf{f}\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)
.
However, if
\mathbf{f}\in\mathbf{H}(\mathrm{div}^{0};\Omega)
, the following orthogonality theorem holds.

Theorem 4.5
.

For any function
\bm{\xi}\in\mathbf{H}(\mathrm{div}^{0};\Omega)
,
\mathcal{R}\bm{\xi}-\mathcal{R}_{h}\bm{\xi}

is orthogonal to the sum space
\mathbf{X}_{h,\tau 0}+\nabla Y_{h,0}
under the
\mathbf{H}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)
inner product, i.e.,

\displaystyle(\mathcal{R}\bm{\xi}-\mathcal{R}_{h}\bm{\xi},\mathbf{v}_{h}+\nabla q_{h})_{\mathbf{H}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)}=0,\quad\forall\mathbf{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{X}_{h,\tau 0},\ \forall q_{h}\in Y_{h,0}.

(4.10)

Proof.

Given
\bm{\xi}\in\mathbf{H}(\mathrm{div}^{0};\Omega)
.
For any
\mathbf{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{X}_{h,\tau 0}
,
there is a unique function
q_{h,1}\in\mathrm{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
such that
-\Delta q_{h,1}=\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v}_{h}
.
Then
\mathbf{v}_{h}+\nabla q_{h,1}\in\mathbf{H}_{0}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)\cap\mathbf{H}(\mathrm{div}^{0};\Omega)=\mathcal{H}_{0}
.
Furthermore, by Corollary
3.4
, there exists a unique function
q_{h,2}\in Y_{h,0}
such that
\mathbf{v}_{h}+\nabla q_{h,2}\in\mathcal{H}_{0}^{h}
.
Taking the test functions as
\mathbf{v}_{h}+\nabla q_{h,1}\in\mathcal{H}_{0}
and
\mathbf{v}_{h}+\nabla q_{h,2}\in\mathcal{H}_{0}^{h}
in
(
4.1
) and (
4.6
), respectively, we obtain

\displaystyle(\nabla\times\mathcal{R}\bm{\xi},\nabla\times\mathbf{v}_{h})+(\mathcal{R}\bm{\xi},\mathbf{v}_{h}+\nabla q_{h,1})=(\bm{\xi},\mathbf{v}_{h}+\nabla q_{h,1}),

(4.11)

\displaystyle(\nabla\times\mathcal{R}_{h}\bm{\xi},\nabla\times\mathbf{v}_{h})+(\mathcal{R}_{h}\bm{\xi},\mathbf{v}_{h}+\nabla q_{h,2})=(\bm{\xi},\mathbf{v}_{h}+\nabla q_{h,2}).

(4.12)

Since
\bm{\xi}\in\mathbf{H}(\mathrm{div}^{0};\Omega)
,
\mathcal{R}\bm{\xi}\in\mathcal{H}_{0}
,
\mathcal{R}_{h}\bm{\xi}\in\mathcal{H}_{0}^{h}
,
q_{h,1}\in\mathrm{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
and
q_{h,2}\in Y_{h,0}
, it follows that

\displaystyle(\bm{\xi},\nabla q_{h,1})=(\mathcal{R}\bm{\xi},\nabla q_{h,1})=0,\quad(\bm{\xi},\nabla q_{h,2})=(\mathcal{R}_{h}\bm{\xi},\nabla q_{h,2})=0.

(4.13)

Substituting (
4.13
) into (
4.11
) and (
4.12
) gives

\displaystyle(\nabla\times\mathcal{R}\bm{\xi},\nabla\times\mathbf{v}_{h})+(\mathcal{R}\bm{\xi},\mathbf{v}_{h})=(\bm{\xi},\mathbf{v}_{h})=(\nabla\times\mathcal{R}_{h}\bm{\xi},\nabla\times\mathbf{v}_{h})+(\mathcal{R}_{h}\bm{\xi},\mathbf{v}_{h}),

(4.14)

which shows that
\mathcal{R}\bm{\xi}-\mathcal{R}_{h}\bm{\xi}

is orthogonal to
\mathbf{X}_{h,\tau 0}
under the
\mathbf{H}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)
inner product:

\displaystyle(\nabla\times(\mathcal{R}\bm{\xi}-\mathcal{R}_{h}\bm{\xi}),\nabla\times\mathbf{v}_{h})+(\mathcal{R}\bm{\xi}-\mathcal{R}_{h}\bm{\xi},\mathbf{v}_{h})=0,\quad\forall\mathbf{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{X}_{h,\tau 0}.

(4.15)

Moreover, since
\mathcal{R}\bm{\xi}\in\mathcal{H}_{0}

and
\mathcal{R}_{h}\bm{\xi}\in\mathcal{H}_{0}^{h}
,

\mathcal{R}\bm{\xi}-\mathcal{R}_{h}\bm{\xi}
is orthogonal to
\nabla Y_{h,0}
:

\displaystyle(\mathcal{R}\bm{\xi}-\mathcal{R}_{h}\bm{\xi},\nabla q_{h})_{\mathbf{H}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)}=(\mathcal{R}\bm{\xi}-\mathcal{R}_{h}\bm{\xi},\nabla q_{h})=0,\quad\forall q_{h}\in Y_{h,0}.

(4.16)

Combining (
4.15
) and (
4.16
) yields the orthogonality of

\mathcal{R}\bm{\xi}-\mathcal{R}_{h}\bm{\xi}
to the sum space
\mathbf{X}_{h,\tau 0}+\nabla Y_{h,0}

under the
\mathbf{H}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)
inner product (
4.10
).
∎

4.2
Collective compactness

Let
\wedge=\{h_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
be a strictly monotonically decreasing
sequence of positive numbers converging to zero.
In this subsection, we demonstrate the collective compactness of the operator sequence
\{\mathcal{R}_{h_{n}}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
, which is a necessary condition for Lemmas
2.5
and
2.6
.
In fact, this is closely related to the discrete compactness property
of the space sequence
\mathcal{H}_{0}^{h}
into
\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)
,
which is analogous to the concept of discrete
compactness for Nédélec finite element spaces introduced
by F. Kikuchi
[
15
]
.

Definition 4.1
.

The space sequence
\{\mathcal{H}_{0}^{h}\}_{h\in\wedge}
is said to possess
the discrete compactness property with respect to the
\mathbf{H}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)

norm and embedding into
\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)
if, for any sequence of
functions
\{\bm{\xi}_{h}\}_{h\in\wedge}
satisfying the following two conditions:

(i)

\bm{\xi}_{h}\in\mathcal{H}_{0}^{h},\forall h\in\wedge
,

(ii)

There exists a constant
C>0
such that
\|\bm{\xi}_{h}\|_{\mathbf{H}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)}<C
,
\forall h\in\wedge
,

there exists a subsequence
\{\bm{\xi}_{h}\}_{h\in\wedge_{s}},\wedge_{s}\subset\wedge
,
and a function
\bm{\xi}_{0}\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)
such that

\displaystyle\|\bm{\xi}_{h}-\bm{\xi}_{0}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\to 0\quad\mathrm{as}\quad h\to 0~\mathrm{in}\wedge_{s}.

(4.17)

Theorem 4.6
.

If for any strictly monotonically decreasing sequence of positive
numbers
\wedge=\{h_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
converging to zero,
the space sequence
\{\mathcal{H}_{0}^{h}\}_{h\in\wedge}
possesses
the discrete compactness property described in Definition
4.1
,
then for any such sequence
\wedge=\{h_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
,
the operator sequence
\{\mathcal{R}_{h}\}_{h\in\wedge}
is collectively compact.

Proof.

To prove that the operator sequence
\{\mathcal{R}_{h}\}_{h\in\wedge}

is collectively compact, according to Definition
2.1
,
it suffices to show that for any bounded subset
\mathbf{B}\subset\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)
, the set

\displaystyle\mathbf{W}=\bigcup_{h\in\wedge}\mathcal{R}_{h}(\mathbf{B})=\{\mathcal{R}_{h}\mathbf{f}:\forall\mathbf{f}\in\mathbf{B},~\forall h\in\wedge\}

(4.18)

is relatively compact in
\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)
.
Equivalently, for any fixed sequence
\{\bm{\xi}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathbf{W}
,
we need to show that
\{\bm{\xi}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
has a
convergent subsequence in
\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)
.
Assume the constant
C
is an upper bound for
\mathbf{B}
in
\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)
.
For each
\bm{\xi}_{j}\in\mathbf{W}
,
there exists some
\mathbf{f}_{j}\in\mathbf{B}
and index
h_{n_{j}}\in\wedge

such that
\bm{\xi}_{j}=\mathcal{R}_{h_{n_{j}}}\mathbf{f}_{j}
, hence
\bm{\xi}_{j}\in\mathcal{H}_{0}^{h_{n_{j}}}
.
Now, consider two cases based on the number of distinct elements in the index set
\{h_{n_{j}}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
.

If the index set
\{h_{n_{j}}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
contains only a finite number of distinct elements,
which implies
\{h_{n_{j}}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
has finite cardinality.
Then there must exist a positive integer
k
such that countably many
\bm{\xi}_{j}
satisfy
\bm{\xi}_{j}=\mathcal{R}_{h_{n_{k}}}\mathbf{f}_{j}\in\mathcal{H}_{0}^{h_{n_{k}}}
.
These
\bm{\xi}_{j}
form a countable subset
\{\bm{\xi}_{j}\}_{j\in\wedge_{k}}
of
\{\bm{\xi}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
, where
\wedge_{k}\subset\wedge
.
Since
\mathcal{R}_{h_{n_{k}}}
is a finite-rank operator from
\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)
to itself, and
\{\mathbf{f}_{j}\}_{j\in\wedge_{k}}\subset\mathbf{B}

is a bounded sequence in
\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)
with upper bound
C
, it follows that
\{\bm{\xi}_{j}\}_{j\in\wedge_{k}}

is sequentially compact in
\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)
, meaning there exists a subsequence of
\{\bm{\xi}_{j}\}_{j\in\wedge_{k}}

that converges in
\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)
.

Otherwise, assume that the index set
\{h_{n_{j}}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
contains countably many distinct elements.
In this case, we can extract a subsequence from
\{\bm{\xi}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
, still denoted as
\{\bm{\xi}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}

for convenience, such that
\{n_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
is strictly monotonically increasing with respect to
j
,
and thus
\{h_{n_{j}}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
is strictly monotonically decreasing and converges to zero.
By the assumption of this theorem,
the space sequence
\{\mathcal{H}_{0}^{h_{n_{j}}}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
possesses the discrete compactness property described
in Definition
4.1
.
Furthermore,
inequality (
4.7
) gives

\displaystyle\|\bm{\xi}_{j}\|_{\mathbf{H}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)}=\|\mathcal{R}_{h_{n_{j}}}\mathbf{f}_{j}\|_{\mathbf{H}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)}\leq\|\mathbf{f}_{j}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C,\quad\forall 1\leq j\leq\infty.

(4.19)

Combining the discrete compactness of
\{\mathcal{H}_{0}^{h_{n_{j}}}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}

and the boundedness of
\{\bm{\xi}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
from (
4.19
),
it immediately follows that
\{\bm{\xi}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
has a subsequence that converges in
\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)
.
∎

Theorem
4.6
reduces the proof of the collective compactness
of the operator sequence
\{\mathcal{R}_{h}\}_{h\in\wedge}
to proving the discrete compactness of the space sequence
\{\mathcal{H}_{0}^{h}\}_{h\in\wedge}
,
which is not a trivial result on a general bounded Lipschitz domain.
Fortunately, it follows from Lemma
2.1
that there exists a constant
s>1/2
such that the space
X_{N}(\Omega)

is continuously embedded into
\mathbf{H}^{s}(\Omega)
when
\Omega
is a bounded Lipschitz polyhedron, which is precisely the case considered in this paper.
This immediately leads to the following conclusion.

Theorem 4.7
.

For polyhedron
\Omega
, the space sequence
\{\mathcal{H}_{0}^{h}\}_{h\in\wedge}
possesses the discrete compactness property
with respect to the
\mathbf{H}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)
norm and embedding into
\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

According to Definition
4.1
, to prove the discrete compactness of
the space sequence
\{\mathcal{H}_{0}^{h}\}_{h\in\wedge}
,
it suffices to prove the following statement: If

\{\bm{\xi}_{h_{n}}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}

satisfies that there exists a constant
C_{1}>0
such that for all
n\geq 1
,
\bm{\xi}_{h_{n}}\in\mathcal{H}_{0}^{h_{n}}

and
\|\bm{\xi}_{h_{n}}\|_{\mathbf{H}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)}<C_{1}
, then
\{\bm{\xi}_{h_{n}}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}

must have a subsequence which converges in
\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)
.

For each
\bm{\xi}_{h_{n}}\in\mathcal{H}_{0}^{h_{n}}
, the following variational problem

\displaystyle(\nabla p_{n},\nabla q)=(\bm{\xi}_{h_{n}},\nabla q),\quad\forall q\in\mathrm{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega)

(4.20)

has a unique solution
p_{n}\in\mathrm{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
by the Lax-Milgram theorem.
Taking
q=p_{n}
yields
\|\nabla p_{n}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\|\bm{\xi}_{h_{n}}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}
.
Let
\mathbf{w}_{n}=\bm{\xi}_{h_{n}}-\nabla p_{n}\in\mathbf{H}_{0}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)
.
Then
(\mathbf{w}_{n},\nabla q)=0
holds for any
q\in\mathrm{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
, which means that
\nabla\cdot\mathbf{w}_{n}=0
,

\mathbf{w}_{n}\in\mathcal{H}_{0}\subset X_{N}(\Omega)
.
Since
\Omega
is a polyhedron,

X_{N}(\Omega)
is continuously embedded into
\mathbf{H}^{s}(\Omega)
for some constant
s>1/2
according to Lemma
2.1
.
Consequently, there exists a constant
C_{2}
such that

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{w}_{n}\|_{\mathbf{H}^{s}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}\|\mathbf{w}_{n}\|_{\mathbf{H}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)}=C_{2}\|\bm{\xi}_{h_{n}}-\nabla p_{n}\|_{\mathbf{H}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}(\|\nabla\times\bm{\xi}_{h_{n}}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\bm{\xi}_{h_{n}}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla p_{n}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)})

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}(2(\|\nabla\times\bm{\xi}_{h_{n}}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+4\|\bm{\xi}_{h_{n}}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}))^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\sqrt{2}C_{2}\|\bm{\xi}_{h_{n}}\|_{\mathbf{H}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)}<2\sqrt{2}C_{1}C_{2},

(4.21)

which indicates that
\{\mathbf{w}_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is a bounded sequence in
\mathbf{H}^{s}(\Omega)
.
Furthermore, due to the compact embedding of
\mathbf{H}^{s}(\Omega)
into
\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)
,
there is a subsequence
\{\mathbf{w}_{n_{k}}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
and some
\mathbf{w}_{0}\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)
such that

\mathbf{w}_{n_{k}}\to\mathbf{w}_{0}
in
\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)
as
k\to\infty
.
By the triangle inequality

\displaystyle\|\bm{\xi}_{h_{n_{k}}}-\mathbf{w}_{0}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\|\bm{\xi}_{h_{n_{k}}}-\mathbf{w}_{n_{k}}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\mathbf{w}_{n_{k}}-\mathbf{w}_{0}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)},

(4.22)

to prove the convergence of
\{\bm{\xi}_{h_{n_{k}}}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
to
\mathbf{w}_{0}
in
\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)
, it remains to show that

\displaystyle\|\bm{\xi}_{h_{n_{k}}}-\mathbf{w}_{n_{k}}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\to 0\quad\mathrm{as}\quad k\to\infty.

(4.23)

Consider the smoothed projection interpolation operators
\Pi_{\mathbf{grad},h_{n_{k}}}^{r+1}
and
\bm{\Pi}_{\mathbf{curl},h_{n_{k}}}^{r+1}
given in Lemma
3.2
.
Since
\bm{\xi}_{h_{n_{k}}}\in\mathcal{H}_{0}^{h_{n_{k}}}\subset\mathscr{N}_{h_{n_{k}},0}^{r+1}
, it is straightforward to deduce that
\bm{\Pi}_{\mathbf{curl},h_{n_{k}}}^{r+1}\bm{\xi}_{h_{n_{k}}}=\bm{\xi}_{h_{n_{k}}}
.
Furthermore,
p_{n}\in\mathrm{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
yields

\displaystyle\bm{\Pi}_{\mathbf{curl},h_{n_{k}}}^{r+1}\nabla p_{n_{k}}=\nabla(\Pi_{\mathbf{grad},h_{n_{k}}}^{r+1}p_{n_{k}}),

(4.24)

where
\Pi_{\mathbf{grad},h_{n_{k}}}^{r+1}p_{n_{k}}\in\mathscr{L}_{h_{n_{k}},0}^{r+1}=Y_{h_{n_{k}},0}
and
\bm{\Pi}_{\mathbf{curl},h_{n_{k}}}^{r+1}\nabla p_{n_{k}}\in\mathscr{N}_{h_{n_{k}},0}^{r+1}
.
Combining (
4.24
) with
\bm{\xi}_{h_{n_{k}}}\in\mathcal{H}_{0}^{h_{n_{k}}}
and
\nabla\cdot\mathbf{w}_{n_{k}}=0
, it follows that

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{w}_{n_{k}}-\bm{\xi}_{h_{n_{k}}}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}=(\mathbf{w}_{n_{k}}-\bm{\xi}_{h_{n_{k}}},\mathbf{w}_{n_{k}}-\bm{\xi}_{h_{n_{k}}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\mathbf{w}_{n_{k}}-\bm{\xi}_{h_{n_{k}}},\mathbf{w}_{n_{k}}-\bm{\Pi}_{\mathbf{curl},h_{n_{k}}}^{r+1}\mathbf{w}_{n_{k}})-(\mathbf{w}_{n_{k}}-\bm{\xi}_{h_{n_{k}}},\bm{\Pi}_{\mathbf{curl},h_{n_{k}}}^{r+1}\bm{\xi}_{h_{n_{k}}}-\bm{\Pi}_{\mathbf{curl},h_{n_{k}}}^{r+1}\mathbf{w}_{n_{k}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\mathbf{w}_{n_{k}}-\bm{\xi}_{h_{n_{k}}},\mathbf{w}_{n_{k}}-\bm{\Pi}_{\mathbf{curl},h_{n_{k}}}^{r+1}\mathbf{w}_{n_{k}})-(\mathbf{w}_{n_{k}}-\bm{\xi}_{h_{n_{k}}},\bm{\Pi}_{\mathbf{curl},h_{n_{k}}}^{r+1}\nabla p_{n_{k}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\mathbf{w}_{n_{k}}-\bm{\xi}_{h_{n_{k}}},\mathbf{w}_{n_{k}}-\bm{\Pi}_{\mathbf{curl},h_{n_{k}}}^{r+1}\mathbf{w}_{n_{k}})-(\mathbf{w}_{n_{k}}-\bm{\xi}_{h_{n_{k}}},\nabla(\Pi_{\mathbf{grad},h_{n_{k}}}^{r+1}p_{n_{k}}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\mathbf{w}_{n_{k}}-\bm{\xi}_{h_{n_{k}}},\mathbf{w}_{n_{k}}-\bm{\Pi}_{\mathbf{curl},h_{n_{k}}}^{r+1}\mathbf{w}_{n_{k}})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{w}_{n_{k}}-\bm{\xi}_{h_{n_{k}}}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\|\mathbf{w}_{n_{k}}-\bm{\Pi}_{\mathbf{curl},h_{n_{k}}}^{r+1}\mathbf{w}_{n_{k}}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)},

(4.25)

which implies

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{w}_{n_{k}}-\bm{\xi}_{h_{n_{k}}}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\|\mathbf{w}_{n_{k}}-\bm{\Pi}_{\mathbf{curl},h_{n_{k}}}^{r+1}\mathbf{w}_{n_{k}}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}.

(4.26)

According to Lemma
3.2
,
\bm{\Pi}_{\mathbf{curl},h_{n_{k}}}^{r+1}
is uniformly bounded in
\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega))
.
Thus from the projection property and error estimate (
3.15
), there exists a constant
C_{3}
such that

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{w}_{n_{k}}-\bm{\Pi}_{\mathbf{curl},h_{n_{k}}}^{r+1}\mathbf{w}_{n_{k}}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\inf_{\mathbf{v}_{h_{n_{k}}}\in\mathscr{N}_{h_{n_{k}},0}^{r+1}}\|\mathbf{w}_{n_{k}}-\mathbf{v}_{h_{n_{k}}}-\bm{\Pi}_{\mathbf{curl},h_{n_{k}}}^{r+1}(\mathbf{w}_{n_{k}}-\mathbf{v}_{h_{n_{k}}})\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{3}\inf_{\mathbf{v}_{h_{n_{k}}}\in\mathscr{N}_{h_{n_{k}},0}^{r+1}}\|\mathbf{w}_{n_{k}}-\mathbf{v}_{h_{n_{k}}}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{3}\|\mathbf{w}_{n_{k}}-\bm{\Pi}_{h_{n_{k}}}^{r}\mathbf{w}_{n_{k}}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C_{3}h_{n_{k}}^{s}\|\mathbf{w}_{n_{k}}\|_{\mathbf{H}^{s}(\Omega)},

(4.27)

where the vector Scott-Zhang interpolation
\bm{\Pi}_{h_{n_{k}}}^{r}\mathbf{w}_{n_{k}}\in\mathscr{V}_{h_{n_{k}},\tau 0}^{r}\subset\mathscr{N}_{h_{n_{k}},0}^{r+1}
.
Substituting
(
4.2
) into (
4.2
) and combining it with (
4.26
) gives (
4.23
).
Thus, we prove that there is a subsequence
\{\bm{\xi}_{h_{n_{k}}}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
of
\{\bm{\xi}_{h_{n}}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
converges to
\mathbf{w}_{0}
in
\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)
.
∎

4.3
Pointwise convergence

To establish the spectral convergence of the operator sequence
\{\mathcal{R}_{h_{n}}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
, it is also necessary to demonstrate the pointwise convergence of
\{\mathcal{R}_{h_{n}}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
to the operator
\mathcal{R}
on
\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)
.
The following property which involves the approximation of functions in
\mathcal{H}_{0}
by sequences of discrete functions in
\{\mathcal{H}_{0}^{h}\}_{h\in\wedge}

will be utilized in the subsequent proof.

Lemma 4.1
.

For any given
\bm{\eta}\in\mathcal{H}_{0}
, suppose
\bm{\eta}\in\mathbf{H}_{0}^{t}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)
with
t>1/2
, then there is a constant
C_{t}>0
independent of
\bm{\eta}
, and there exists a sequence
\bm{\eta}_{h}\in\mathcal{H}_{0}^{h}
such that

\displaystyle\|\bm{\eta}-\bm{\eta}_{h}\|_{\mathbf{H}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)}\leq C_{t}h^{\min\{t,r\}}\|\bm{\eta}\|_{\mathbf{H}^{t}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)},\quad\forall h\in\wedge.

(4.28)

If
t\leq 1/2
, there also exists a sequence
\bm{\eta}_{h}\in\mathcal{H}_{0}^{h}
such that

\displaystyle\|\bm{\eta}-\bm{\eta}_{h}\|_{\mathbf{H}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)}\to 0\quad\mathrm{as}\quad h\to 0~\mathrm{in}\wedge.

(4.29)

Proof.

According to Theorem
2.2
, there exist functions
\mathbf{u}=\mathsf{P}\bm{\eta}\in\mathbf{H}^{t+1}(\Omega)\cap\mathbf{H}_{0}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)
and
p=\mathsf{Q}\bm{\eta}\in\mathrm{H}^{t+1}(\Omega)\cap\mathrm{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
such that
\bm{\eta}=\mathbf{u}+\nabla p
. Additionally, there exist constants
C_{1,t}
and
C_{2,t}
, both independent of
\bm{\eta}
, satisfying

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{u}\|_{\mathbf{H}^{t+1}(\Omega)}\leq C_{1,t}\|\bm{\eta}\|_{\mathbf{H}^{t}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)},\ \ \ \ \|p\|_{\mathrm{H}^{t+1}(\Omega)}\leq C_{2,t}\|\bm{\eta}\|_{\mathbf{H}^{t}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)}.

(4.30)

Consider the Scott-Zhang interpolation functions
\bm{\Pi}_{h}^{r}\mathbf{u}\in\mathscr{V}_{h,\tau 0}^{r}=\mathbf{X}_{h,\tau 0}
and
\Pi_{h}^{r+1}p\in\mathscr{L}_{h,0}^{r+1}=Y_{h,0}
.
From the error estimates (
3.14
) and (
3.15
), there is a constant
C
such that

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{u}-\bm{\Pi}_{h}^{r}\mathbf{u}\|_{\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{\min\{t,r\}}\|\mathbf{u}\|_{\mathbf{H}^{t+1}(\Omega)}\leq CC_{1,t}h^{\min\{t,r\}}\|\bm{\eta}\|_{\mathbf{H}^{t}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)},

\displaystyle\|p-\Pi_{h}^{r+1}p\|_{\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{\min\{t,r+1\}}\|p\|_{\mathrm{H}^{t+1}(\Omega)}\leq CC_{2,t}h^{\min\{t,r+1\}}\|\bm{\eta}\|_{\mathbf{H}^{t}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)}.

(4.31)

By Theorem
3.4
, there exists a unique
p_{h}\in Y_{h,0}
such that
\bm{\eta}_{h}=\bm{\Pi}_{h}^{r}\mathbf{u}+\nabla\Pi_{h}^{r+1}p+\nabla p_{h}\in\mathcal{H}_{0}^{{h}}
, and combining with
\bm{\eta}=\mathbf{u}+\nabla p\in\mathbf{H}(\mathrm{div}^{0};\Omega)
yields

\displaystyle(\bm{\eta}-\bm{\eta}_{h},\bm{\eta}-\bm{\eta}_{h})_{\mathbf{H}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)}=(\bm{\eta}-\bm{\eta}_{h},\mathbf{u}+\nabla p-\bm{\Pi}_{h}^{r}\mathbf{u}-\nabla\Pi_{h}^{r+1}p)_{\mathbf{H}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\bm{\eta}-\bm{\eta}_{h}\|_{\mathbf{H}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)}\|\mathbf{u}+\nabla p-\bm{\Pi}_{h}^{r}\mathbf{u}-\nabla\Pi_{h}^{r+1}p\|_{\mathbf{H}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)}.

(4.32)

Substituting the interpolation estimate (
4.3
) into (
4.3
), it follows that

\displaystyle\|\bm{\eta}-\bm{\eta}_{h}\|_{\mathbf{H}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)}\leq\|\mathbf{u}+\nabla p-\bm{\Pi}_{h}^{r}\mathbf{u}-\nabla\Pi_{h}^{r+1}p\|_{\mathbf{H}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{u}-\bm{\Pi}_{h}^{r}\mathbf{u}\|_{\mathbf{H}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)}+\|\nabla p-\nabla\Pi_{h}^{r+1}p\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{u}-\bm{\Pi}_{h}^{r}\mathbf{u}\|_{\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega)}+\|p-\Pi_{h}^{r+1}p\|_{\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(C_{1,t}h^{\min\{t,r\}}+C_{2,t}h^{\min\{t,r+1\}})\|\bm{\eta}\|_{\mathbf{H}^{t}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{t}h^{\min\{t,r\}}\|\bm{\eta}\|_{\mathbf{H}^{t}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)},

(4.33)

where
C_{t}=C(C_{1,t}+C_{2,t})
is a constant depending only on
t
and
\Omega
.

For the case
t\leq 1/2
, one only needs to split
\bm{\eta}
as
\bm{\eta}=\mathbf{u}+\nabla p
by the regular decomposition (
2.6
), where
\mathbf{u}\in\mathbf{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
and
p\in\mathrm{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
, and then utilize the density of
\mathrm{C}_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
in
\mathrm{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
to perform similar derivations as above for smooth functions sufficiently close to
\mathbf{u}
and
p
, thereby obtaining a sequence
\bm{\eta}_{h}\in\mathcal{H}_{0}^{h}
that approximates
\bm{\eta}
.
∎

Theorem 4.8
.

For any given
\mathbf{f}\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)
, the norm convergence

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{R}\mathbf{f}-\mathcal{R}_{h_{n}}\mathbf{f}\|_{\mathbf{H}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)}\to 0\quad\mathrm{as}\quad n\to\infty

(4.34)

holds.
This norm convergence implies the pointwise convergence of the operator sequence
\{\mathcal{R}_{h_{n}}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
to the operator
\mathcal{R}
on
\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

For any
\mathbf{f}\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)
, the estimate (
4.7
) gives

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{R}_{h_{n}}\mathbf{f}\|_{\mathbf{H}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)}\leq\|\mathbf{f}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)},\quad\forall n\geq 1,

(4.35)

Therefore,
\{\mathcal{R}_{h_{n}}\mathbf{f}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is a bounded sequence in
\mathbf{H}_{0}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)
.
Since
\mathbf{H}_{0}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)
, as a Hilbert space, is a reflexive Banach space, by the Banach-Alaoglu theorem,
there is a subsequence
\{\mathcal{R}_{h_{n_{k}}}\mathbf{f}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
of
\{\mathcal{R}_{h_{n}}\mathbf{f}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
converges weakly to
\mathbf{g}\in\mathbf{H}_{0}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)
.
Furthermore, for any
q\in\mathrm{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
,
From the weak convergence of
\{\mathcal{R}_{h_{n_{k}}}\mathbf{f}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
and the approximability of the Lagrange finite element space
\mathscr{L}_{h_{n_{k}},0}^{r+1}=Y_{h_{n_{k}},0}
to
\mathrm{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
, there is a sequence
q_{h_{n_{k}}}\in Y_{h_{n_{k}},0}
such that

\displaystyle(\mathbf{g},\nabla q)

\displaystyle=\lim_{k\to\infty}(\mathcal{R}_{h_{n_{k}}}\mathbf{f},\nabla q)=\lim_{k\to\infty}(\mathcal{R}_{h_{n_{k}}}\mathbf{f},\nabla q-\nabla q_{h_{n_{k}}})

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathbf{f}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\lim_{k\to\infty}\|q-q_{h_{n_{k}}}\|_{\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Omega)}=0,

(4.36)

which implies that
\mathbf{g}\in\mathbf{H}_{0}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)\cap\mathbf{H}(\mathrm{div}^{0};\Omega)=\mathcal{H}_{0}
.
Consider any
\bm{\eta}\in\mathcal{H}_{0}
, by Lemma
4.1
, there exists a sequence
\bm{\eta}_{h_{n_{k}}}\in\mathcal{H}_{0}^{h_{n_{k}}}
such that

\bm{\eta}_{h_{n_{k}}}\to\bm{\eta}
in
\mathbf{H}_{0}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)
as
k\to\infty
.
Notice that for each
\bm{\eta}_{h_{n_{k}}}
,
\mathcal{R}_{h_{n_{k}}}\mathbf{f}
satisfies

\displaystyle(\nabla\times\mathcal{R}_{h_{n_{k}}}\mathbf{f},\nabla\times\bm{\eta}_{h_{n_{k}}})+(\mathcal{R}_{h_{n_{k}}}\mathbf{f},\bm{\eta}_{h_{n_{k}}})=(\mathcal{R}_{h_{n_{k}}}\mathbf{f},\bm{\eta}_{h_{n_{k}}})_{\mathbf{H}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)}=(\mathbf{f},\bm{\eta}_{h_{n_{k}}}),

(4.37)

where the norm convergence of
\{\bm{\eta}_{h_{n_{k}}}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
in
\mathbf{H}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)
implies

\displaystyle(\mathbf{f},\bm{\eta}_{h_{n_{k}}})\to(\mathbf{f},\bm{\eta})\quad\mathrm{as}\quad k\to\infty,

(4.38)

and the weak convergence of
\{\mathcal{R}_{h_{n_{k}}}\mathbf{f}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
to
\mathbf{g}
in
\mathbf{H}_{0}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)
implies

\displaystyle|(\mathcal{R}_{h_{n_{k}}}\mathbf{f},\bm{\eta}_{h_{n_{k}}})_{\mathbf{H}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)}-(\mathbf{g},\bm{\eta})_{\mathbf{H}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|(\mathcal{R}_{h_{n_{k}}}\mathbf{f},\bm{\eta}_{h_{n_{k}}}-\bm{\eta})_{\mathbf{H}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)}+(\mathcal{R}_{h_{n_{k}}}\mathbf{f}-\mathbf{g},\bm{\eta})_{\mathbf{H}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|(\mathcal{R}_{h_{n_{k}}}\mathbf{f},\bm{\eta}_{h_{n_{k}}}-\bm{\eta})_{\mathbf{H}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)}|+|(\mathcal{R}_{h_{n_{k}}}\mathbf{f}-\mathbf{g},\bm{\eta})_{\mathbf{H}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{R}_{h_{n_{k}}}\mathbf{f}\|_{\mathbf{H}(\mathbf{curl;}\Omega)}\|\bm{\eta}-\bm{\eta}_{h_{n_{k}}}\|_{\mathbf{H}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)}+|(\mathcal{R}_{h_{n_{k}}}\mathbf{f}-\mathbf{g},\bm{\eta})_{\mathbf{H}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{f}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\|\bm{\eta}-\bm{\eta}_{h_{n_{k}}}\|_{\mathbf{H}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)}+|(\mathcal{R}_{h_{n_{k}}}\mathbf{f}-\mathbf{g},\bm{\eta})_{\mathbf{H}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)}|\to~0\quad\mathrm{as}\quad k\to\infty.

(4.39)

Let
k\to\infty
in (
4.37
), it follows from (
4.38
) and (
4.3
) that

\displaystyle(\mathbf{g},\bm{\eta})_{\mathbf{H}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)}=(\mathbf{f},\bm{\eta})=(\mathcal{R}\mathbf{f},\bm{\eta})_{\mathbf{H}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)}.

(4.40)

This holds for any
\bm{\eta}\in\mathcal{H}_{0}
, which means that

\{\mathcal{R}_{h_{n_{k}}}\mathbf{f}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
converges weakly to
\mathbf{g}=\mathcal{R}\mathbf{f}
in
\mathbf{H}_{0}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)
.
This conclusion holds for every weakly convergent subsequence of
\{\mathcal{R}_{h_{n}}\mathbf{f}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
, so
\{\mathcal{R}_{h_{n}}\mathbf{f}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
also converges weakly to
\mathcal{R}\mathbf{f}
in
\mathbf{H}_{0}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)
.
Otherwise, there would exist some
\mathbf{f}_{0}\in\mathbf{H}_{0}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)
, a positive number
\varepsilon>0
, and a countable subset
\wedge_{\varepsilon}\subset\wedge
such that

\displaystyle|(\mathcal{R}_{h_{n}}\mathbf{f}-\mathcal{R}\mathbf{f},\bm{\eta}_{0})_{\mathbf{H}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)}|>\varepsilon,\quad\forall n\in\wedge_{\varepsilon}.

(4.41)

However,
\{\mathcal{R}_{h_{n}}\mathbf{f}\}_{n\in\wedge_{\varepsilon}}
is also a bounded sequence in
\mathcal{H}_{0}
and must have a subsequence that converges weakly to
\mathcal{R}\mathbf{f}
in
\mathcal{H}_{0}
, which contradicts (
4.41
).

Taking
\bm{\eta}=\mathcal{R}\mathbf{f}\in\mathcal{H}_{0}
and
\bm{\eta}_{h_{n}}=\mathcal{R}_{h_{n}}\mathbf{f}\in\mathcal{H}_{0}^{h_{n}}
in (
4.1
) and (
4.6
), respectively, yields

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{R}\mathbf{f}\|_{\mathbf{H}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)}^{2}=(\mathbf{f},\mathcal{R}\mathbf{f}),\quad\|\mathcal{R}_{h_{n}}\mathbf{f}\|_{\mathbf{H}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)}^{2}=(\mathbf{f},\mathcal{R}_{h_{n}}\mathbf{f}).

(4.42)

The weak convergence from
\{\mathcal{R}_{h_{n}}\mathbf{f}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
to
\mathcal{R}\mathbf{f}
indicates

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}(\mathbf{f},\mathcal{R}_{h_{n}}\mathbf{f})=(\mathbf{f},\mathcal{R}\mathbf{f}),

(4.43)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}(\mathcal{R}\mathbf{f},\mathcal{R}_{h_{n}}\mathbf{f})_{\mathbf{H}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)}=(\mathcal{R}\mathbf{f},\mathcal{R}\mathbf{f})_{\mathbf{H}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)}.

(4.44)

Combining (
4.42
), (
4.43
) and (
4.44
), it follows that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\|\mathcal{R}_{h_{n}}\mathbf{f}-\mathcal{R}\mathbf{f}\|_{\mathbf{H}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)}^{2}=\lim_{n\to\infty}(\mathcal{R}_{h_{n}}\mathbf{f}-\mathcal{R}\mathbf{f},\mathcal{R}_{h_{n}}\mathbf{f}-\mathcal{R}\mathbf{f})_{\mathbf{H}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{R}\mathbf{f}\|_{\mathbf{H}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)}^{2}+\lim_{n\to\infty}\|\mathcal{R}_{h_{n}}\mathbf{f}\|_{\mathbf{H}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)}^{2}-2\lim_{n\to\infty}(\mathcal{R}\mathbf{f},\mathcal{R}_{h_{n}}\mathbf{f})_{\mathbf{H}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)}

\displaystyle 2\|\mathcal{R}\mathbf{f}\|_{\mathbf{H}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)}^{2}-2(\mathcal{R}\mathbf{f},\mathcal{R}\mathbf{f})_{\mathbf{H}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)}=0,

in the norm of

For this aim, we begin by creating an initial mesh

.
Then, the initial mesh

is uniformly refined several

On these meshes, through solving the discrete mixed Problem
3

to obtain the approximate eigenvalues

The resulting generalized algebraic eigenvalue problem

The numerical examples are carried out on LSSC-IV in the State Key Laboratory

Each computing node has two 18-core Intel Xeon Gold 6140 processors at 2.3 GHz

The matrices are produced by the package of open parallel finite element method(OpenPFEM).

\lambda_{m,n,\ell}
is

\displaystyle\left[\begin{array}[]{l}A\cos(m\pi x)\sin(n\pi y)\sin(\ell\pi z)\\
B\cos(n\pi y)\sin(m\pi x)\sin(\ell\pi z)\\
C\cos(\ell\pi z)\sin(m\pi x)\sin(n\pi y)\end{array}\right],

where the coefficients
(A,B,C)
satisfying
Am+Bn+C\ell=0
.
Specifically, the three linearly independent eigenfunctions corresponding to the eigenvalue
2\pi^{2}
are

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}{\bm{\xi}}_{1}(x,y,z)=[0,0,\sin(\pi x)\sin(\pi y)]^{\top},\\
{\bm{\xi}}_{2}(x,y,z)=[0,\sin(\pi x)\sin(\pi z),0]^{\top},\\
{\bm{\xi}}_{3}(x,y,z)=[\sin(\pi y)\sin(\pi z),0,0]^{\top}.\end{array}\right.

The method for computing the distance between the discrete eigenspace and the exact one is as follows.
Taking the first eigenfunction approximation
\bm{\xi}_{h}

as an example,
we first perform its
\mathbf{L}^{2}
projection onto the space spanned by the three eigenfunctions
\bm{\xi}_{1}
,

\bm{\xi}_{2}
and
\bm{\xi}_{3}
in (
6.1
).
The projection is denoted as

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\bm{\xi}_{h}:=a_{1}\bm{\xi}_{1}+a_{2}\bm{\xi}_{2}+a_{3}\bm{\xi}_{3},

(6.10)

satisfying the following Galerkin condition

\displaystyle(\mathbf{P}\bm{\xi}_{h},\bm{\xi}_{i})=(\bm{\xi}_{h},\bm{\xi}_{i}),\ \ \ ~\forall i=1,2,3,

(6.11)

from which the values of
a_{i},i=1,2,3
can be solved.
Besides, it holds that

\displaystyle(\mathbf{P}\bm{\xi}_{h},\bm{\xi}_{i})_{\mathbf{H}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)}=(\mathbf{P}\bm{\xi}_{h},\nabla\times\nabla\times\bm{\xi}_{i}+\bm{\xi}_{i})=(2\pi^{2}+1)(\mathbf{P}\bm{\xi}_{h},\bm{\xi}_{i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(2\pi^{2}+1)(\bm{\xi}_{h},\bm{\xi}_{i})=(\bm{\xi}_{h},\nabla\times\nabla\times\bm{\xi}_{i}+\bm{\xi}_{i})=(\bm{\xi}_{h},\bm{\xi}_{i})_{\mathbf{H}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)},\ \ \ ~\forall i=1,2,3,

(6.12)

thus
\mathbf{P}\bm{\xi}_{h}
is also the
\mathbf{H}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)
projection of
\bm{\xi}_{h}
onto the eigenfunction space.
The relative
\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)
error and
\mathbf{H}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)
error of
\bm{\xi}_{h}
is obtained by computing

\displaystyle\frac{\|\bm{\xi}_{h}-\mathbf{P}\bm{\xi}_{h}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}}{\|\bm{\xi}_{h}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}}\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\frac{\|\bm{\xi}_{h}-\mathbf{P}\bm{\xi}_{h}\|_{\mathbf{H}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)}}{\|\bm{\xi}_{h}\|_{\mathbf{H}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)}}.

(6.13)

7
Conclusions

With the novel regular decomposition technique,
we decompose the space
\mathbf{H}_{0}^{s}(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)

into the sum of a vector potential space and the gradient of a scalar space, both possessing higher regularity.
Based on this regular decomposition, a type of numerical method
using continuous Lagrange finite elements for solving Maxwell eigenvalue problems is proposed and analyzed in this paper.

This new regular decomposition here enables the use of standard continuous finite element spaces, circumventing the implementation complexities associated with high-order edge elements or specialized meshes.
Furthermore, we establish a rigorous convergence analysis, demonstrating that the proposed scheme yields optimal convergence rates for eigenpair approximations and eliminates spurious zero modes.
Numerical experiments on various benchmark domains have validated the efficacy of the proposed method, confirming the theoretical predictions.
Future work will focus on developing efficient eigensolvers specifically tailored to the algebraic eigenvalue problems arising from the discretization scheme in this paper.

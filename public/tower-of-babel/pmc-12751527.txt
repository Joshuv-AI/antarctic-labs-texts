Association among persistent chemical pollutants and infectious encephalitis: a mixture exposure and mediation approach
Authors: Guolong Qu, Jianqiang Han, Zhenyao Song, Weiming Hou
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundMost overlook the harmful effects on diseases, particularly the chronic neurotoxicity, which remains undetermined. This study aims to determine the impact of major environmental factors on infectious encephalitis.MethodsOur study investigated the correlation between persistent chemical pollutants and reports and mortality cases of infectious encephalitis (Epidemic cerebrospinal meningitis and Encephalitis B). First, the quantile-based g computation (qgcomp) statistical method was used to analyze the correlation of different chemical pollutants. Bayesian Kernel Machine Regression-Causal Mediation Analysis (BKMR-CMA) method was applied to explore the mediating effect of greenhouse gases between persistent chemical pollution mixtures and incidence and mortality of infectious encephalitis. Finally, the Lasso regression method was used to assess the sensitivity of different age groups of encephalitis patients to the risks of different chemical pollutants.ResultsFor epidemic cerebrospinal meningitis report cases, N2O mediates at the 10% and 90% levels, and PCB and OC exhibit a positive risk effect at all levels. For death cases, N2O mediates at the 10% and 90% levels, and the direct effect risk of the controlled chemical pollution mixture shows a downward trend, with PCB and NMVOC exhibiting a positive risk effect at all levels. For encephalitis B reports and death cases, N2O mediates at the 10% and 90% levels, and the direct effect risk of the controlled chemical pollution mixture shows a trend of first decreasing and then increasing, with CH4 and HCB exhibiting a positive risk effect at all levels. The mediating effect of N2O is concentrated in encephalitis B reports and deaths, where the control direct effects (CDE) of the CH4, N2O, and HCB chemical pollutant model were assessed at three different quantiles. The CDEs at the 10%, 50%, and 75% quantiles were 757.37 (95% CI: 85.36, 1580.16), 872.86 (95% CI: 215.66, 1706.86), and 894.98 (95% CI: 209.22, 1726.33), respectively. The death effects were 31.31 (95% CI: -0.05, 66.40), 36.76 (95% CI: 8.62, 71.13), and 38.06 (95% CI: 8.46, 75.09).ConclusionsWe found that most persistent chemical pollutants in the air increase the risk of neurotoxicity-related morbidity and mortality. Among them, epidemic cerebrospinal meningitis is primarily driven by PCB and OC, while encephalitis B is mainly driven by CH4 and HCB. The greenhouse gas N2O may play a mediating role between chemical pollutant exposure and the outcomes of infectious encephalitis. In the future, identifying the effects of chemical mixtures can better support the causal relationship between air pollution and neurotoxicity.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12889-025-25768-5.Keywords: Infectious encephalitis, Persistent organic pollutants, N2O, BKMR-CMA, Controlled direct effect

Introduction

Persistent environmental pollutants include various chemicals such as greenhouse gases, particulate matter, atmospheric mercury, and persistent organic pollutants (POPs). Different pollutants pose distinct hazards. Among them, global warming caused by greenhouse gases such as carbon monoxide (CO), methane (CH₄), and nitrous oxide (N₂O) has become an increasingly serious and prominent environmental issue [1]. The frequency and severity of heatwaves are rising, potentially leading to more heat-related health problems, especially for vulnerable groups [2]. Air pollution, and fine particulate matter and vehicle emissions in particular, are currently a major environmental issue, as they can trigger various health problems at different stages of life [3]. Heavy metals like mercury have been shown in several studies to pose potential health threats to humans, particularly in neurodegenerative diseases [4]. Among newly emerging pollutants, POPs hold a particularly important status. POPs include industrial chemicals (such as polychlorinated biphenyls (PCBs), hexachlorobenzene (HCB), etc.), aromatic hydrocarbons, pesticides, and others. POPs degrade at a slower rate and, therefore, exhibit persistence. Due to their persistent nature, numerous studies have confirmed that they have chronic neurotoxic effects [5].

Most cases of infectious encephalitis are caused by viruses, but bacteria (especially intracellular organisms like Rickettsia), fungi, and parasites are also important [6]. In China’s statutory infectious diseases, there are two diseases related to encephalitis: Epidemic Cerebrospinal Meningitis and Encephalitis B. Epidemic Cerebrospinal Meningitis (also known as “Meningococcal Meningitis”) is an acute infectious disease of the central nervous system transmitted via the respiratory route caused by Neisseria meningitidis (Nm). Based on the severity of infectious disease hazards, infectious diseases in China are classified into three categories: A, B and C. Diseases in categories A and B required strict or specially strict management. According to the 2019 National Statutory Infectious Disease Report, the mortality rate for meningococcal meningitis remains high, ranking sixth among Class A and B infectious diseases, with 20%–30% of patients suffering from severe sequelae [7, 8]. Encephalitis B is a vector-borne zoonotic disease caused by the Japanese encephalitis virus (JEV) [9]. 30%−50% of survivors may experience severe neurological and psychiatric sequelae [10]. Encephalitis B is a major neurological disease in Asia, with an estimated 67,900 cases annually and approximately 10,000 deaths worldwide, of which 50% of cases occur in China [11, 12].

The earliest studies on air pollution and meningitis can be traced back to 1971 in Mali, Africa, where initial exploration suggested that implementing air pollution-related health measures could effectively combat the epidemic of African cerebrospinal meningitis [13]. As for Encephalitis B, a recent study in Thailand showed that the incidence of viral encephalitis was positively correlated with the temperature one month prior, but no significant association was found between air pollutants such as SO2 and NO2 [14]. However, a long-term time series study in Taiwan, China, found a positive correlation between SO2 and relative humidity concentrations with Encephalitis B cases, while O3 concentrations showed a negative correlation [15]. Several basic studies have indicated that persistent organic pollutants (POPs) can affect nutriregulation through various mechanisms [16]. The nervous system has been considered a sensitive target for environmental pollutants, especially POPs. However, there is currently a lack of research on the correlation between infectious encephalitis and these pollutants.

Our specific objectives were to: (a) Explore the epidemic periods and characteristics of infectious encephalitis nationwide. (b) Analyze the potential impact of various persistent environmental pollutants on the incidence and mortality of infectious encephalitis across different age groups. (c) Examine the modifying factors in the association between N2O as environmental mixtures and infectious encephalitis.

Materials and methods

Data source

All reported cases of infectious encephalitis (including Epidemic Cerebrospinal Meningitis and Encephalitis B) in China from 2005 to 2018 were retrieved from the Public Health Science Data Center Website (https://www.phsciencedata.cn/). Diagnoses for all patients were based on the criteria outlined in the Epidemic Cerebrospinal Meningitis and Encephalitis B management guidelines issued by the Ministry of Health of the People’s Republic of China [17]. Data on persistent chemical pollutants (2005–2018) were sourced from the Emissions Database for Global Atmospheric Research (EDGAR) (https://edgar.jrc.ec.europa.eu/) [18]. The glossary of persistent chemical pollutants was from Table S1. Persistent chemical pollutants included greenhouse gases (CH4, CO2bio, N2O, CO2), air pollutants (BC, CO, NH3, NMVOC, NOx, OC, PM2.5, PM10, SO2), mercury (GEM, GOM, PBM, ASM, RM) and persistent organic pollutants (BaP, BbF, BkF, IcdP, PCB, PCDD, HCB). Among them, ASM = GEM + GOM + PBM, RM = GOM + PBM [19, 20]. The EDGAR data referenced in this study represent pollutant emissions (measured in Gg), reflecting the total release mass of pollutants over a specific time period. The selection of emission data is due to the focus of this study on analyzing the total pollutant release at a regional scale. All pollutant emission data cited in this study are annual data, consistent with the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC) [21] emission accounting guidelines, ensuring the authority and comparability of the data.

Mixtures and mediation analyses

Previous studies typically applied simple correlation analysis to derive results for further regression effect analysis [22]. However, the results obtained were often unsatisfactory. This study employs a multi-stage analysis method to explore the mixed effects of chemicals in the environment on different infectious encephalitis. In the first stage, Spearman correlation analysis was used to screen for chemical factors that showed significant associations. In the second stage, the selected factors underwent preliminary quantile-based g computation (Qgcomp) analysis. Factors with negative effects were gradually excluded, and Qgcomp analysis was repeated until a dose-response relationship for the mixture showed an increasing trend, at which point the screening was stopped to obtain the corresponding results. However, before the preliminary analysis, it was necessary to assess whether there were common positive effect factors in the incidence and mortality of each type of infectious encephalitis. The following assumptions were made: (a) If the common factor is chaotic and irregular, the preliminary screening results were deemed meaningless and discarded, and the significant positive factors from the Spearman correlation results were used for a new Qgcomp analysis. (b) If the common factor shows some regularity, the positive factors from the preliminary screening were selected and further analyzed using the stepwise Qgcomp method.

This study used multiple mixture models to explore the overall effects, weights, and individual effects of persistent chemical pollutants on the incidence and mortality of different categories of infectious encephalitis. The model’s analysis in this study was performed with R (version 4.4.1). The models applied are as follows:

The application of this mixed-effects analysis method is commonly used in the field of environmental endocrine disruption, particularly with regard to metal elements [23]. Qgcomp was used to assess the overall effect of persistent chemical pollutant mixtures on the incidence and mortality of infectious encephalitis, as well as the relative importance of individual components within the mixture. Qgcomp integrates its estimation procedure with g-computation based on WQS regression. It uses the same procedure as WQS to estimate the overall mixture effect, but aligns with the parameters of the marginal structural model. The WQS model assumes that the direction of the relationship between each component and the outcome is the same, whereas the qgcomp model offers some flexibility in the direction of exposure [24]. The bootstrap iteration count in the Qgcomp model was set to 10,000, and the parameter q was set to 4 [25]. It means dividing the mixture of pollutants into four quantile intervals for analysis to assess the overall impact of the pollutants on disease. After screening for relevant positive factors using the Qgcomp method, dimensionality reduction modeling was performed based on dose-response curves. In other words, the initially screened positive factors were used for a second round of Qgcomp modeling, which was stopped when the curve displayed a positive relationship (Selection based on factor significance and direction). This model applied the “qgcomp” package in R version 4.4.1.

In the selection of mediating factors, based on previous Spearman correlation analysis, preliminary Qgcomp analysis results, and relevant literature [26, 27] on the nervous system, we selected N2O as the primary mediating factor. Specifically, this is reflected as follows: during the preliminary selection with Qgcomp, the greenhouse gas factors that exhibited a positive correlation between the incidence and mortality of different infectious encephalitis were identified as common effects. These factors also showed a higher frequency of significant positive correlations in the Spearman correlation analysis, leading to their comprehensive selection. It is evident from the results that N2O demonstrates a positive directional effect on all types of encephalitis, particularly showing a significant positive correlation in the incidence and mortality of Epidemic Cerebrospinal Meningitis. We used Bayesian Kernel Machine Regression-Causal Mediation Analysis (BKMR-CMA) as the primary model to study the mediating role of greenhouse gas N2O in the association between persistent chemical pollutant mixtures and the incidence and mortality of infectious encephalitis. BKMR is a highly flexible model that uses kernel functions to model the exposure-response surface of mixtures. The flexible kernel function allows for the estimation of nonlinearity, interactions between pollutants, and cumulative effects [28]. Three BKMR models were fitted to estimate the mediation effects: (1) The outcome model. N2O was included as a covariate outside the kernel function to distinguish its effect from that of the mixture. This model quantified the association between the chemical pollutant mixture and the incidence and mortality of infectious encephalitis; (2) The mediator model. This model quantified the association between the chemical pollutant mixture and greenhouse gas N2O; (3) The total effect model. When the status of greenhouse gas N2O was excluded from the model, this model quantified the association between the chemical pollutant mixture and the incidence and mortality of infectious encephalitis [29]. The covariates and main effects in the BKMR-CMA model were referenced from the positive effects derived in the Qgcomp model. This model applied the “causalbkmr” package in R software version 4.4.1. BKMR-CMA is an extension of BKMR, typically used to quantify the mediating effect of a third variable on a mixture. Its application was particularly scarce in the field of air pollutants and was currently primarily used in the field of heavy metals [29]. The detailed description of the statistical methods can be found in Table S2 and Text S1 [30, 31].

Sensitivity of different age groups

To identify key chemical pollution predictors of potential neurotoxicity—namely, the incidence and mortality of infectious encephalitis—across different age groups and eliminate multicollinearity between variables, we applied the Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator (LASSO) regression model. In this model, the coefficients of variables with negligible contribution to the overall model are shrunk to zero, ensuring the predictive performance of the model [32]. In the LASSO model, we used cross-validation to evaluate the model and select parameters, where the dataset was divided into 10 subsets, and the model was trained and tested multiple times on these subsets. The “minimum deviation” refers to the lambda value with the least bias found during cross-validation, which indicates the best fit for the data. We selected a lambda value slightly larger than the minimum deviation, usually achieved by adding one standard deviation to the minimum deviation lambda value [33]. This model applied the “glmnet” package in R software version 4.4.1.

Mixtures and mediation effects of pollutants on infectious encephalitis

Mixtures effects of pollutants on infectious encephalitis: Qgcomp model

During the initial screening of chemical factors, Figure S1A and C show that the positive effect factors for reported cases of Epidemic Cerebrospinal Meningitis in the general population are OC, GOM, BC, PM10, PCB, CO2bio, N2O, and PM2.5. The positive effect factors for death cases of Epidemic Cerebrospinal Meningitis are OC, GOM, PCB, CO2bio, NMVOC, NH3, and N2O. Figure S1B and D show that the mixture of persistent chemical pollutants has a negative dose-response relationship with the incidence and mortality risk of Epidemic Cerebrospinal Meningitis in the general population. Figure S1E and G show that the positive effect factors for reported cases of Encephalitis B in the general population are HCB, IcdP, ASM, NOx, PBM, PCDD, N2O, GOM, CO2bio, NMVOC, and CH4. The positive effect factors for death cases of Encephalitis B are HCB, IcdP, PBM, ASM, NMVOC, N2O, NOx, PCDD, CO2bio, CH4 and GOM. Figure S1F and H show that the mixture of persistent chemical pollutants has a negative dose-response relationship with the incidence and mortality risk of Encephalitis B in the general population. Among the greenhouse gases, only N2O and CO2bio shows a significant positive direction among different types of infectious encephalitis. In summary, the common positive risk factors for Epidemic Cerebrospinal Meningitis are chaotic and disorganized, whereas the significant features for Encephalitis B are clearly and consistently the same.

Based on the above results, we conducted different multi-stage analyses. For Epidemic Cerebrospinal Meningitis, stepwise Qgcomp analysis was performed using the merged significant positive correlation factors from the Spearman results, while for Encephalitis B, stepwise Qgcomp analysis was performed using the common positive factors from the Qgcomp results. The final results are shown in Fig. 2. Due to the negative dose-response relationship between the mixture of pollutants and the reported and death cases of infectious encephalitis, a secondary screening of chemical factors was conducted. From Fig. 2A and C, it can be observed that the positive effect factors for the reported and death cases of Epidemic Cerebrospinal Meningitis in the general population are PCB, HCB, CO2bio, N2O, BC, NH3, OC, PM2.5 and PM10. From Fig. 2B and D, it can be seen that the mixture of persistent chemical pollutants has a positive dose-response relationship with the incidence and mortality risk of Epidemic Cerebrospinal Meningitis in the general population. From Fig. 2E and G, it can be observed that the positive effect factors for the reported cases of Encephalitis B in the general population are HCB, N2O, and CH4. From Fig. 2F and H, it can be observed that the mixture of persistent chemical pollutants has a positive dose-response relationship with the incidence and mortality risk of Encephalitis B in the general population.

Mediation effects of pollutants on infectious encephalitis: BKMR-CMA model

Table 3 shows that in the BKMR-CMA model, the different levels of N2O exhibit a certain mediating effect on the reported and death cases of various infectious encephalitis. Notably, except for Epidemic Cerebrospinal Meningitis Deaths, which is mediated by 90% N2O, other cases also show the mediating effect of different levels of N2O. Specifically, the NIE (reflecting the indirect association between the chemical pollutant mixture mediated by different N2O levels and the incidence and mortality of infectious encephalitis) mainly shows a positive effect in Encephalitis B. These findings suggest that N2O primarily mediates the association between each persistent chemical pollutant and the incidence and mortality of Encephalitis B. Therefore, the mediating effect of different levels of N2O on the mixture for Epidemic Cerebrospinal Meningitis can be found in Effect 1 and Effect 2.

Discussion

In this study on persistent environmental pollutants, we found that GOM and RM in atmospheric mercury have shown an annual increasing trend, which may be attributed to historical global emissions and long-range atmospheric transport [34]. In recent years, with the acceleration of China’s industrialization and economic growth, the transition from household plant material combustion heating to coal combustion heating has gradually occurred [35]. Additionally, the rising vehicle usage in both urban and rural areas has led to an increase in automobile exhaust and pollution in the air. As a result, pollutants such as NMVOC, NOx, and SO2 in China have also shown a yearly increasing trend. This strongly emphasizes the need for increased public awareness and focus on environmental protection.

In China, the overall incidence of infectious encephalitis has shown a downward trend. The main susceptible populations for Epidemic cerebrospinal meningitis are those aged 0–10 years and 19–50 years. Meningococcal meningitis is characterized by its high infectivity, rapid onset, fast progression, high mortality rate, and high disability rate, which are primarily caused by factors such as weakened immunity, lack of vaccination, exposure to a high number of pathogens, and unvaccinated serotypes. Based on general immunology speculation, underdevelopment of the immune system is a major factor in the pediatric population, while in adults, the decline in antibody levels due to the passage of time after vaccination in childhood is a significant factor. These factors contribute to the high incidence in specific populations. This is similar to findings in sub-Saharan Africa, where the carrier rate for individuals aged 5–19 years is significantly higher than in other age groups [36], indicating the importance of vaccine development and exploration [37]. Regarding Encephalitis B in China, the peak occurs in the summer and autumn, which mirrors the epidemic pattern seen in regions like Nepal [38]. This may be due to the primary vector for Encephalitis B being mosquitoes, which thrive and spread the virus in the climate and environment of the summer and autumn seasons [39], making mosquito surveillance essential for controlling Encephalitis B [40].

According to this study, the main pollutants influencing meningococcal meningitis are some air pollutants and persistent pollutants. The air pollutants include various particulate matter such as PM10, PM2.5, OC, and NMVOC. While earlier research in the last century indicated that the presence of bacteria and related organisms in air pollution contributed to the spread of meningitis, the impact of chemicals in the air on its transmission has not been studied. Long-term exposure to air pollution may weaken the immune response of the respiratory system, making individuals more susceptible to pathogens, including Neisseria meningitidis. Additionally, air pollutants may damage mucosal barriers, making it easier for Neisseria bacteria to invade and colonize, thereby increasing the risk of infection [41]. Persistent pollutants include PCB and GOM. Regarding PCB, most studies have confirmed that this compound has significant hepatotoxicity [42], but a recent survey in Denmark showed a possible association between PCB and brain tumor-related neurotoxicity [43], which bears some neurotoxicity-related similarity to the findings of this study. Although the exact cause of meningitis is unclear, this may be an incidental discovery, warranting further experimental research in the future. Although numerous studies have shown conclusive evidence that mercury is neurotoxic to humans [44, 45], the levels of atmospheric mercury components such as GOM are relatively low in pollution, which is often overlooked by public health researchers. Therefore, this study may help raise awareness among environmental scholars regarding atmospheric mercury pollution. We speculate that long-term exposure to gaseous mercury may lead to immune system suppression, reducing the body’s defense against infections. This might susceptible individuals more vulnerable to bacterial invasions, such as Neisseria meningitidis, thereby increasing the risk of meningococcal meningitis. Additionally, mercury exposure might prolong the survival time of bacteria in the body or enhance their reproductive capabilities, promoting the spread of bacterial infectious diseases like meningococcal meningitis. These speculations need verification through future experimental research. Furthermore, this study found that the persistent organic pollutant HCB has a high sensitivity to the incidence and mortality risk of Encephalitis B. HCB is commonly used as a fungicide or pesticide and can persist in the environment, spreading through air, water, or food. Although HCB itself does not directly affect its vector—mosquitoes—the accumulation of pollutants may alter the mosquito population in the ecosystem, thereby affecting the transmission pattern of Encephalitis B, including changes in mosquito reproduction and survival rates, indirectly influencing the efficiency of virus transmission. While the relationship between HCB and Encephalitis B transmission is unclear, its negative impact on human health and ecosystems may provide a more favorable environment for the virus to spread. Therefore, reducing exposure to and pollution from HCB may help reduce the health risks associated with the transmission of Encephalitis B.

In the mediation analysis, we found that the sensitivity to meningococcal meningitis was greater under the mediation of the greenhouse gas N2O although under low mediation percentages. This was reflected in the fact that persistent environmental pollution mixtures had a greater hazardous effect on meningococcal meningitis at lower levels, while the risk of Encephalitis B was influenced by higher levels of environmental mixtures. This indicates that persistent pollutants have a more disruptive effect on bacterial transmission and harm, possibly because bacteria have strong survivability, and persistent air pollutants can provide a suitable environment for bacterial transmission through contaminated air. Most viruses, on the other hand, rely on direct contact between hosts (such as humans or animals) or droplet transmission in the air when spread, meaning that chemicals have less of a direct facilitating effect on the transmission of viruses compared to bacteria. We also found that the organic compound HCB posed a greater risk for Encephalitis B under low-level N2O mediation, even though HCB itself has low volatility [46]. Under conditions of low greenhouse gas N2O, which may coincide with lower-temperature meteorological conditions, the volatility of HCB is further reduced, leading to its accumulation in the environment. Long-term exposure to this accumulation may result in neurological damage. Additionally, HCB has strong lipophilicity [47], meaning it can accumulate in adipose tissues, especially in low-temperature environments where fat metabolism slows down, accelerating the accumulation of HCB in the body. Its lipophilicity also allows it to accumulate in organs with higher fat content, such as the brain, thereby exacerbating its neurotoxic effects. Different age groups have varying susceptibility to persistent pollutants in terms of morbidity and mortality risks. Among them, OC pollutants mainly affect younger patient populations (0–18 years old). Although there is no relevant literature to confirm this, a survey conducted in Taiwan suggests that younger individuals, especially children, are more sensitive to the effects of OC pollutants [48]. Therefore, we hypothesize that OC pollutants may significantly increase the susceptibility to infectious encephalitis in children by weakening their immune defenses, disrupting the blood-brain barrier, causing specific exposure accumulation, and interfering with the environmental microbiota. Although the study did not specifically investigate encephalitis, it found that the younger population (especially children) is more susceptible to the effects of OC pollutants. This serves as a warning to pay attention to the exposure of vulnerable groups (young people) to OC during the epidemic seasons [48]. Regarding greenhouse gases, the middle-aged group (19–50 years) is more prone to diseases influenced by CH4 exposure. A cohort study based on the China Health and Retirement Longitudinal Study (CHARLS) specifically investigated the interaction between air pollution and social activities on cognitive function in middle-aged and older adults. The study found that the interaction between air pollutants such as PM2.5 and ozone and social activities affects cognitive function, indirectly confirming that adults, when engaging in social activities, inevitably become exposed to the external air environment [49]. Possible hypotheses include: the middle-aged group faces greater life pressures, leading to weakened immune systems. This group also tends to have longer exposure to the external environment due to work and social activities, which increases the likelihood of being exposed to pollutants (such as methane), thereby increasing the risk of disease. Even though children engage in certain social activities, most children spend more time indoors, and environmental exposure is not only age-dependent but also to some extent influenced by the nature of the population’s work. Future scholars should incorporate social factors such as occupation into cohort studies for a more in-depth discussion of this issue. Additionally, the study found that different age groups exhibit varying susceptibilities to chemical factors. Even when multiple factors showed negative effects, Lasso’s L1 regularization did not shrink the coefficients of these factors to zero during the screening process, indicating that their negative impact on different types of infectious encephalitis is statistically significant and cannot be ignored. These factors are core predictive factors retained by the model. This finding can help in formulating targeted environmental protection and intervention measures for different age groups in the future.

This study introduces emerging pollutants, such as atmospheric mercury and persistent organic pollutants, into atmospheric pollution research, bringing new conclusions regarding human health. It also innovatively applies dual-character greenhouse gases as mediators to modulate health effects. However, certain limitations remain, such as the lack of disease-specific factors directly related to bacteriology and virology, and the inability to accurately determine causality due to the cross-sectional nature of the data. Although the mixture analysis method used in this study can well complement the results of correlation analysis and exclude factors that may induce the negative transmission risk of diseases, it has the limitation of multicollinearity, which may lead to a certain risk of error. Additionally, regarding the chronic neurotoxic effects often caused by persistent environmental pollutants, solely relying on infectious encephalitis is not comprehensive. At the same time, during the selection of mediating factors in the second stage of the mixture analysis, some greenhouse gases, such as CO2 (which was negative in the initial stage), also showed certain positive effects. However, in order to conduct a unified analysis of different infectious encephalitis types, the comprehensive analysis has both scientific merit and limitations. Regarding the causal mediating relationship of N2O on encephalitis, although existing studies suggest that N2O may exacerbate neurological damage, there are still limitations in experimental evidence and related susceptibility to encephalitis [26, 27]. Future studies will incorporate animal experiments and conduct in-depth research on the causal relationship of related neurotoxic damage.

Conclusion

We found that most persistent chemical pollutants in the air increase the risk of neurotoxicity-related morbidity and mortality. Among them, epidemic cerebrospinal meningitis is primarily driven by PCB and OC, while encephalitis B is mainly driven by CH4 and HCB. The greenhouse gas N2O may play a mediating role between chemical pollutant exposure and the outcomes of infectious encephalitis. The correlation between environmental chemical factors and infectious encephalitis varies significantly across different age groups. In the future, identifying the mechanisms of chemical mixtures can better support the causal relationship between air pollution and neurotoxicity, thus confirming the need to reduce exposure in sensitive populations.

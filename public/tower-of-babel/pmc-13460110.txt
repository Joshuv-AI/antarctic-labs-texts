SKP2 in Cancer: From Molecular Regulation to Therapeutic Vulnerabilities and Translational Perspectives
Authors: Sheng-An Zheng, Cheng Wang, Xiao-Die Yao, Jia-Jia Sheng, Po-Wu Liu, Ying Wang, Shi-Jia Deng, He Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe ubiquitin-proteasome system (UPS) plays a central role in regulating protein homeostasis and degradation. Its dysregulation is closely associated with various diseases, including cancer. S-phase kinase-associated protein 2 (SKP2) is a key E3 ubiquitin ligase component of the UPS. It induces proteasome-mediated protein degradation or modulates substrate function by conjugating K48-linked or K63-linked ubiquitin chains to diverse target proteins. Recent studies have shown that the overexpression of SKP2 in several cancer types is correlated with poor clinical outcomes, underscoring its potential as a therapeutic target. Notably, emerging evidence has expanded the functional repertoire of SKP2 beyond cell cycle control to encompass metabolism, DNA repair, stemness, tumor microenvironment (TME) and immunotherapy response, positioning it as an increasingly attractive target for intervention. In this review, the oncogenic properties of SKP2 and its underlying mechanisms were elucidated in multiple cancer types. Moreover, we systematically summarized future directions for SKP2-targeted therapy.

Introduction

The UPS is a major intracellular protein degradation pathway. It covalently attaches ubiquitin molecules to target proteins for labeling and subsequent degradation. This process is sequentially regulated by E1 activating enzymes, E2 conjugating enzymes, and E3 ligases, which together control protein stability and activity in eukaryotic cells.1 Substrate selection matters due to the diversity of proteins within cells, as evidenced by the instrumental role of E3 ligases in this process. First, E3 ligases bind to specific substrates, ensuring precise ubiquitination. Second, E3 ligases recognize the ubiquitin on E2 ubiquitin-conjugating enzymes and catalyze its covalent attachment to target proteins. Finally, E3 ligases can promote the elongation of ubiquitin chains or select different topological structures of ubiquitin linkage, thereby modulating the functional outcomes of ubiquitination.1 In addition, the type of ubiquitin chain formed can also affect the degradation pathway and efficiency of protein waste.2 Therefore, E3 ligases and ubiquitin chains are key components to the ubiquitination system.

SKP2, as a member of E3 ligases, induces proteasome-mediated protein hydrolysis or regulates substrate function by binding K48-linked ubiquitin chains or K63-linked ubiquitin chains to its various substrates.3 Several studies have suggested that SKP2 can function as a cell cycle regulator that specifically recognizes phosphorylated cell cycle regulatory proteins and mediates their ubiquitination.4 Furthermore, the overexpression of SKP2 in various human cancers is significantly associated with poor clinical outcomes and chemoresistance acquisition,5,6 thereby making therapeutic strategies targeting SKP2 a current hotspot in cancer research.

In this review, the structural domains of SKP2 were comprehensively summarized. The oncogenic properties of SKP2 and its underlying mechanisms in various cancers, as well as the effect of SKP2 on drug resistance were elucidated. The aforementioned measures highlighted the promising therapeutic approaches targeting SKP2. In conclusion, our review supports the investigation into the function of SKP2 in cancer and provides reference for its subsequent research.

Structure of SKP2 Protein

SKP2 gene, also referred to as p45, FBL1, FLB1 and FBXL1, is located on chromosome 5 and comprises 10 exons. There are 5 common SKP2 transcripts (Figure 1A). A novel isoform of SKP2 mRNA called SKP2B (NM_032637) has been reported to play an important role in cancer. In contrast to SKP2, SKP2B localizes to the cytoplasm. The overexpression of SKP2B observed in breast cancer promotes tumor growth in vitro and in vivo.7,8 SKP2B is not involved in the ubiquitination and subsequent degradation of p27. However, it reduces the activity of p53 by degrading prohibition, which is responsible for the activation of p53.9 The SKP2 protein consists of 424 amino acids (Figure 1B). The structures of SKP2 complexes were analyzed using X-ray crystallography parameters with low resolution (above 3.0 Å) and medium resolution (2.0 Å–3.0 Å). Specifically, structure 1FQV represents the S-phase kinase-associated protein 1 (SKP1)-SKP2 complex, whereas structures 7B5L and 7B5M represents the Cullin 1-RING box protein-1 (RBX1)-SKP1-SKP2- p27 core complex (Figure 1C). These complexes function as E3 ubiquitin ligases that conjugate ubiquitin to substrate proteins, thereby targeting them for degradation. In particular, the F-box domain, leucine-rich repeats (LRRs), RING finger domain and nuclear localization signal (NLS) domain of SKP2 play an important role in its function. F-Box Domain

F-box proteins are the substrate recognition components of SKP1-Cullin 1-F-box (SCF) E3 ubiquitin ligase complex.10 The F-box domain of SKP2, consisting of approximately 50 amino acids, serves as a site for protein-protein interactions, involved in the regulation of ubiquitination processes. It interacts with the S-Phase Kinase Associated Protein 1 (SKP1) subunit and forms a complex to incorporate SKP2 into the SCF complex,11 enabling SKP2 to bind to Cullin 1 and Ring-box proteins, thereby forming a complete SCF-SKP2 complex. Once the SCF-SKP2 complex is assembled, the F-box domain of SKP2 functions in substrate recognition by binding to specific substrates such as Cyclin Dependent Kinase Inhibitor 1B (CDKN1B, p27) and ubiquitinating these substrates for degradation through the activity of ubiquitin ligase, thereby inhibiting the accumulation of p27 and promoting the smooth operation of biological processes such as G1/S cell cycle transition and cell division.12,13 Therefore, the F-box domain of Skp2 is of great significance in substrate recognition and ubiquitination by binding to SKP1 and substrate, which is essential for regulating biological processes such as cell cycle progression and cell proliferation.14–16

Leucine-Rich Repeats (LRRs) Domains

LRRs are common protein structural domains characterized by a broad structural motif of 20–30 amino acids, with a repeated sequence pattern rich in leucine residues.17,18 LRRs are widely used to recognize and bind other molecules such as proteins, small molecules and DNA. Such interactions exert biological roles, including involvement in signal transduction, cell adhesion and perception, and even specific recognition by the immune system.19–21

The SKP2 protein contains 10 LRRs, whose concave surfaces can bind to the CDC28 Protein Kinase Regulatory Subunit 1B (CKS1) protein through hydrogen bonds and vander Waals interactions. Furthermore, the binding of CKS1 to LRRs significantly enhances the stability of the interaction between SKP2 and SKP1, potentially facilitating the efficient localization of substrates in the SCF complex for ubiquitination.22 Researchers have also found that the LRRs domain of SKP2 can bind to the C-terminus of TIS21 protein, promoting its degradation via the ubiquitin-proteasome pathway. Conversely, the deletion of the LRRs domain of SKP2 or the C-terminus of BTG Anti-Proliferation Factor 2 (BTG2, TIS21) protein disrupts their interaction, resulting in an extended half-life of TIS21 protein and increased growth of cancer cells.23 In addition, the LRRs domain has demonstrated its crucial role for maintaining a stable interaction between SKP2 and the tumor necrosis factor receptor-associated ubiquitous scaffolding and signaling protein (TRUSS), which is involved in the regulation of the cell cycle, transcription, DNA damage repair and apoptosis.24 SKP2 also interacts with the pair domain (Prd) of paired box 8 (PAX8) through its LRR domain to promote the ubiquitination and degradation of the PAX8 protein, thereby inhibiting the proliferation and clonal formation of HCC cells, and inducing cell cycle arrest in the S phase.25

RING Finger Domain

The RING finger domain is composed of 40–60 amino acid residues with a circular or quasi-circular structure.26–28 It is characterized by a series of cysteine and histidine residues with specific spacing that act as two zinc ligands, providing the RING finger with stiffness and stability by stabilizing a unique spherical conformation. Functionally, the RING finger domain can cooperate with specific E2 to promote the transfer of ubiquitin molecules from the active site of the E2 enzyme to the specific target proteins recognized by the SKP2 protein, thereby marking them for degradation.22,23,29 For example, the RING finger domain can mediate the transfer of ubiquitin to its substrate protein p27 by binding to E2 ubiquitin-conjugating enzymes.

Nuclear Localization Signal (NLS) Domain

NLS is a specific domain within protein molecules that directs them to the cell nucleus.30–32 Numerous studies have shown that the SKP2 proteins translocate from the cytoplasm to the nucleus upon stimulation via TGF-β signaling. Subsequently, the SKP2 protein is recognized and ubiquitinated by the anaphase promoting complex (APC)/Cdh1 complex in the nucleus, leading to its degradation. Such nuclear translocation process is mediated by the NLS domain of SKP2. The deletion of the NLS domain of SKP2 protein renders its nuclear translocation capability impossible to achieve.33 Further studies have identified serine 72 within its NLS domain as a potential site for phosphorylation mediated by Akt to interfere with nuclear translocation and promote cytoplasmic localization of SKP2 protein.34

Regulatory Factors of SKP2

Transcriptional and Post-Transcriptional Regulation of SKP2

At the transcriptional level, the SKP2 gene is regulated by various transcription factors (Figure 2A and B). For example, Onecut2 (OC2), N-Myc Proto-Oncogene Protein (MYCN), nuclear casein kinase and cyclin dependent kinase substrate 1 (NUCKS1), signal transducer and activator of transcription 1 (STAT1) and forkhead box M1 (FOXM1) have been reported to regulate SKP2 transcription by binding to its promoter region. E2F transcription factor 1 (E2F1), a key regulator of cell cycle regulation, DNA replication and apoptosis, also modulates SKP2 transcription.35 In cancer cells, E2F1 modulates p21 stability and cell cycle by regulating SKP2 transcription.36,37 MicroRNA-205-5p can promote autophagy and inhibit pulmonary fibrosis in silicosis by interfering with the E2F1/SKP2/Beclin1 axis.38 The hepatitis B virus X–interacting protein (HBXIP) is able to promote proliferation by activating SKP2 promoter via binding to transcription factor E2F1.39 c-Myc plays a crucial role in cell cycle processes and apoptosis. Reportedly, there is a negative feedback loop between c-Myc and SKP2 (Figure 2A). In pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC), c-Myc promotes SKP2 transcription by directly binding to its promoter, thereby enhancing cell proliferation, invasion and migration. Simultaneously, SKP2 participates in the degradation of c-Myc by adding ubiquitin to c-Myc. By directly binding to the promoter region of SKP2 gene, p53 inhibits SKP2 transcriptional activity and expression, thereby improving p27 stability. Accumulated p27 forms a complex with SKP2, which competitively inhibits SKP2 binding to other substrates.41 Recently, SKP2 has been reported to regulate p53 activation. Mechanistically, SKP2 antagonizes the interaction between p300 and p53, thereby suppressing p300-mediated acetylation of p53 (Figure 2B).42,43 The dynamic interaction between p53, c-Myc, and SKP2 ensures the normal progression of the cell cycle. In a proliferating state, c-Myc is activated to elevate SKP2 levels and promote the degradation of cell cycle suppressor proteins, thereby driving the progression of the cell cycle. Upon DNA damage in cells, p53 is activated to inhibit the expression of c-Myc and SKP2, leading to the accumulation of cell cycle suppressor proteins and cell cycle arrest.44,45 Forkhead box O3A (FOXO3a) is another transcription factor that negatively regulates SKP2 transcription.46,47 Notably, SKP2-mediated ubiquitination and degradation of FOXO3a depend on the kinase activity of Aur-A48 (Figure 2B). Recently, the RNA-binding protein NONO forms complexes with SKP2 mRNA and increases its stability by protecting it from nuclease-mediated degradation49 (Figure 2C). Additionally, several microRNAs have been identified to regulate SKP2 post-transcription by directly binding to its 3ʹ-UTR regions38,50–59 (Figure 2D).

As we all know, the stability of protein is precisely regulated by a balance between ubiquitylation and deubiquitylation. In G1 phase, APC/Cdh1 typically prevents premature S phase entry by promoting SKP2 ubiquitylation and degradation60 (Figure 3B). However, Cyclin Dependent Kinase 2 (CDK2)-mediated phosphorylation of SKP2 at Ser64 prevents its APC/Cdh1-mediated degradation, allowing SKP2 to remain functional in G1 phase even when APC/Cdh1 is active. Besides, the dephosphorylation mediated by the mitotic phosphatase cell division cycle 14B (Cdc14B) can promote the degradation of SKP2 protein via APC/Cdh1. These findings suggest that CDK2 and Cdc14B coordinately regulate the stability of SKP2 protein and cell cycle progression through phosphorylation and dephosphorylation61 (Figure 3C). F-box and WD repeat domain containing 2 (FBXW2) is another well-known E3 ubiquitin ligase to promote ubiquitination and subsequent proteasome degradation of SKP2 protein through its F-box domain binding to SKP2.62 However, Moesin can inhibit the FBXW2-SKP2 interaction, thereby promoting the stabilization of SKP2 protein. Obviously, there is a positive feedback loop between Moesin and SKP2. Activated Akt phosphorylates Moesin, which protects SKP2 protein from FBXW2-mediated degradation and triggers accumulation. In turn, Moesin is stabilized by SKP2-mediated K63-linked polyubiquitination.63 Recently, F-box protein 43 (FBXO43) has been shown to interact with Akt-phosphorylated SKP2, thereby reducing SKP2 auto-ubiquitylation and subsequent degradation5 (Figure 3B). Increasing evidence indicates that ubiquitin specific peptidase 2 (USP2) stabilizes the SKP2 protein by directly binding to its LRR domain, thereby enhancing its ubiquitination and subsequent degradation. Unexpectedly, the USP2-SKP2 interaction also disrupts the binding of SKP2 protein to its substrates, leading to its substrates accumulation.12 Recently, ubiquitin specific peptidase 10 (USP10) and ubiquitin specific peptidase 14 (USP14) have been reported to increase the stability of SKP2 protein and promote its expression through deubiquitination64,65 (Figure 3B).

In addition to CDK2 and Cdc14B, Akt has been reported to directly interact with SKP2 and phosphorylates it at Ser72. Akt-mediated phosphorylation triggers 14-3-3beta-dependent relocalization of SKP2 to the cytoplasm, while also promoting SCF complex formation and E3 ligase activity.66 However, von Hippel-Lindau protein (pVHL) blocks Akt-mediated SKP2 phosphorylation by interacting with SKP2 to promote its degradation and destabilization. Intriguingly, AMPK has also been demonstrated to directly interact with SKP2 and phosphorylate it at Ser256, thereby promoting SKP2 SCF complex formation. Moreover, SKP2 depletion or pharmacological inactivation resensitizes cells to gefitinib.67 Furthermore, kinases can indirectly regulate the expression of Skp2 protein by modulating its phosphorylation. For example, MAPK has been shown to affect SKP2 phosphorylation by activating the downstream kinase cascade68 (Figure 3C).

p300 is a critical histone acetyltransferase that acetylates histone and non-histone proteins.69 SKP2 has been reported to be acetylated by p300 at K68 and K71. Acetylation of SKP2 can promote its cytoplasmic retention and stability by disrupting the Cdh1-mediated proteolysis pathway.70 Consequently, p27 accumulates excessively, leading to mitotic arrest and subsequent cell polyploidy71 (Figure 3D). Moreover, the SKP2/p300 axis-mediated acetylation modification has also been identified as a potentially important molecular mechanism driving castration-resistant prostate cancer.72 Recently, Feng et al found that SKP2 is highly modified by O-linked-β-N-acetylglucosamine (O-GlcNAc) at Ser34 through direct interaction with O-GlcNAc transferase (OGT), thereby reducing its APC/Cdh1-mediated ubiquitination and degradation73 (Figure 3D).

Downstream Targets and Pathways of SKP2

SKP2 is overexpressed in multiple cancers, and its higher expression is significantly associated with poorer overall survival (Figure 4). Although Skp2−/− mice show a reduced growth rate and increased apoptosis, the loss of Skp2 per se does not directly induce carcinogenesis.74 Conversely, accumulating evidence suggests that SKP2 is required for tumorigenesis under oncogenic conditions, such as p53/Rb1 pathway inactivation or N-Ras activation.75–77 For example, Skp2 deficiency (Ptenpc−/−; Trp53pc−/−; Skp2−/−) significantly suppressed prostate tumorigenesis in Pten/Trp53 conditional double null (Ptenpc−/−; Trp53pc−/−) mice.78 Skp2 deficiency (Rb1+/−; Skp2−/−) completely prevents spontaneous tumorigenesis in Rb1+/− mice, which are prone to elicit tumorigenesis in susceptible tissues upon loss of the remaining functional allele.79 Compared with transgenic mice expressing Skp2 alone, those co-expressing Skp2 and activated N-Ras showed increased lymphomagenesis penetrance and decreased survival rate.75 As we all know that downstream targets and pathways of SKP2 are of great significance to uncover its oncogenic properties in cancer. In this review, we summarized the function of SKP2 in multiple biological processes, including cell cycle, programmed cell death, cell proliferation and stem cell formation, epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) and tumor metastasis, DNA damage repair, cellular metabolism, and drug resistance. Cell Cycle

SKP2 plays a prominent role in the cell cycle regulation by mediating the ubiquitination and degradation of fundamental regulators, including p27 and p21 (Figure 5A). p27 is a key cell cycle suppressor that prevents S phase entry the by binding cyclin-dependent kinases (CDKs) and inhibiting their activity.80 Skp2 mediates p27 ubiquitination and degradation by recognizing and binding to phosphorylated p27 at Thr187. p27 depletion relieves CDKs inhibition, thereby facilitating the transition of cell cycle from G1 to S phase.81,82 However, abnormally increased expression of SKP2 in cancer cells accelerates p27 degradation, resulting in reduced intracellular p27 level. Consequently, p27-mediated CDKs inhibition is alleviated, leading to an abnormal acceleration of the cell cycle.83,84 In addition to directly targeting p27, SKP2 can indirectly enhance its degradation through macroH2A1 (mH2A1)-cyclin-dependent kinase 8 (CDK8) axis. Strikingly, SKP2 directly ubiquitinates mH2A1, triggering its degradation and thereby increasing CDK8 expression. CDK8 phosphorylates p27 at Thr187 and facilitates SKP2-mediated p27 ubiquitination and degradation.85 Moreover, SKP2 is significantly overexpressed in paclitaxel/bortezomib-resistant cells, and SKP2-mediated p27 ubiquitination and degradation contributes substantially to resistance against paclitaxel, troglitazone and bortezomib.86–89 Figure 5Downstream targets and pathways of SKP2. p21, also known as Cip1/Waf1, prevents cell cycle progression by inhibiting the CDK-cyclin complex activity. In addition to regulating the cell cycle, p21 participates in DNA damage repair, apoptosis, differentiation, and anti-aging.4,90 In normal cells, elevated p21 levels in G1 phase inhibit CDK activity and prevent S phase entry. However, in cancer cells, overexpressed SKP2 ubiquitinates p21 via its SCF complex, thereby diminishing CDK activity and allowing smooth entry into the DNA synthesis phase.91 Thus, high SKP2 and low p21 expression establish a vicious cycle that drives tumor cell hyperproliferation.92,93

SKP2 also indirectly regulates the cell cycle by tagerting forkhead box O1 (FOXO1) and WEE1 G2 checkpoint kinase (WEE1) (Figure 5A). FOXO1 functions in the nucleus and regulates the cell cycle through p21 and p27.71,94,95 Studies have shown that SKP2 inhibits the function of FOXO1 by promoting its ubiquitination and degradation. Specifically, Akt phosphorylates FOXO1 at Ser256, which creates a binding site for SKP2 protein and facilitates its ubiquitination and degradation. Consequently, regulatory capability of FOXO1 targeting the cell cycle is inhibited.96,97 Beyond cancer therapy, statins have been shown to protect endothelial function in diabetes by enhancing SKP2–FOXO1 binding.98,99

Tumors typically prevent cells with excessive DNA damage from entering mitosis by relying on the G2/M phase. At the G2/M checkpoint, active WEE1 phosphorylates cyclin-dependent kinase 1 (CDK1)-cyclin B at Tyr15, maintaining it inactive and preventing mitosis entry.100,101 Recently, a feedforward loop involving SKP2, YTH N6-methyladenosine RNA binding protein F2 (YTHDF2), WEE1, and CDK1 has been proposed to regulate mitotic entry. SKP2 ubiquitinates and subsequently degrades the N6-methyladenosine (m6A) reader YTHDF2, promoting the activity of CDK1 and mitotic entry through degradation of WEE1 mRNA via m6A. Meanwhile, CDK1 stabilizes YTHDF2 by preventing SKP2-mediated YTHDF2 ubiquitination and degradation.102

Programmed Cell Death

In addition to cell cycle, SKP2 participates in several programmed cell deaths, including cell autophagy, apoptosis, ferroptosis, and necroptosis. Autophagy seems to have opposite and context-dependent roles in cancer.103,104 Jin et al reported that SKP2 promotes autophagy and inhibits lysosomal recruitment of mTORC1 through Ras related GTP binding A (RagA) ubiquitination, facilitating the recruitment of GATOR1 complex (NPR2 like, along with NPR3 like and DEP domain containing 5) upon sustained stimulation of amino acids.105 Coactivator associated arginine methyltransferase 1 (CARM1) serves as a critical component of autophagy, co-activating transcription of autophagy-related and lysosomal genes via transcription factor EB (TFEB) in mammals. Under nutrient-rich conditions, SKP2 promotes autophagy by degrading CARM1 in the nucleus.47 Furthermore, SKP2 promotes the K48-linked polyubiquitination of beclin 1 (BECN1), thereby facilitating its proteasomal degradation. Genetic or pharmacological inhibition of SKP2 increases autophagic flux by reducing BECN1 ubiquitination and degradation, thereby reducing the MERS coronavirus infection.106 Recently, a positive feedback loop involving COP9 signalosome subunit 3 (COPS3), forkhead box O3 (FOXO3) and SKP2 has been proposed to promote cisplatin resistance by regulating autophagy in osteosarcomas. COPS3 increases the nuclear abundance of FOXO3, thereby promoting the formation and maturation of autophagosomes. FOXO3 in turn represses SKP2 transcription, resulting in reduced SKP2-mediated degradation of COPS3107 (Figure 5B).

Programmed cell death 4 (PDCD4) is responsible for regulating the process of cell apoptosis.108 Studies have shown that PDCD4 is an important substrate of SKP2 protein (Figure 5C). In breast cancer, SKP2 can remarkably promote PDCD4 phosphorylation, ubiquitination and degradation. On the one hand, SKP2 promotes PDCD4 phosphorylation at Ser67 via Akt signaling pathway. On the other hand, Skp2 is a direct E3 ubiquitin ligase for PDCD4 and promotes the K48-linked ubiquitination of PDCD4. Interestingly, the phosphorylation of PDCD4 is a prerequisite for its ubiquitination and degradation by the SCF-SKP2 complex.109

Myeloid Cell Leukemia-1 (Mcl-1) is mainly located to the inner mitochondrial membrane and plays a crucial role in apoptosis.110,111 SKP2 regulates Mcl-1-mediated apoptosis by increasing Mcl-1 stability (Figure 5C). However, SKP2 is not a direct E3 ubiquitin ligase for Mcl-1, and it inhibits Mcl-1-mediated apoptosis via the F-box and WD repeat domain-containing7 (FBW7)/Mcl-1 axis. Briefly, phosphorylated Mcl-1 is ubiquitinated and degraded by direct binding to FBW7, while SKP2 significantly reduces the interaction between FBW7 and Mcl-1. However, the mechanism by which SKP2 decreases FBW7–MCL-1 binding remains to be elucidated.6

Mixed lineage kinase domain like pseudokinase (MLKL) is an essential regulatory protein for necroptosis,112,113 a process triggered by translocation of MLKL polymers to the plasma membrane.114,115 In non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), SKP2 has been found to interact with MLKL and mediate its ubiquitination and degradation, thereby inhibiting necroptosis. Furthermore, it has been suggested that SKP2-ubiquitinated MLKL degradation contributes to cisplatin resistance. Therefore, preventing SKP2-mediated MLKL degradation is a potential strategy for inducing necroptosis and overcoming platinum resistance in NSCLC.116

Recently, ferroptosis has recently emerged as a major focus of cancer research, and accumulating evidence demonstrates that SKP2 participates in its regulation. Yang et al found that genetic and pharmacological inhibitions of SKP2 significantly protected cell from ferroptosis by abolishing the lipid peroxidation.117 Mechanistically, SKP2 mediates the ubiquitination and degradation of phosphorylated acyl-CoA synthetase long chain family member 4 (ACSL4) induced by Akt at the K621 site, thereby reducing lipid peroxidation and ferroptosis.118 On the other hands, SKP2 promotes K48-mediated ubiquitination and translocation of solute carrier family 3 member 2 (SLC3A2), a key regulator of cystine/glutamate exchange and ferroptosis118 (Figure 5D).

EMT and Tumor Metastasis

Metastasis is a multistep process that involves cancer cells spreading from their original location to distant organs and subsequently adapting to a new tissue microenvironment. It is responsible for the majority of cancer-related deaths.119–121 EMT is the process of transdifferentiation of epithelial cells into mesenchymal cells during development.122 Twist family bHLH transcription factor 1 (TWIST), an important EMT transcription factor, drives cancer metastasis by inducing EMT.123 Research has found that SKP2 stabilizes Twist protein by mediating its K63-linked non-proteolytic ubiquitination, thereby preventing β-TrCP-mediated Twist degradation. Moreover, SKP2 inhibitor resensitizes castration-resistant prostate cancer (CRPC) cells to paclitaxel and doxorubicin by preventing Twist-mediated EMT and stemness acquisitions.124,125 E-cadherin is an important cell adhesion protein predominantly expressed in epithelial cells.126 In many malignancies, E-cadherin downregulation or loss of function (LOF) reduces intercellular adhesion capacity and promotes EMT and tumor metastasis.127 It has been suggested that the SKP2 protein ubiquitinates E-cadherin via the SCF-SKP2 complex to enable degradation. SKP2 overexpression triggers the reduced E-cadherin level on the cell surface, thereby weakening intercellular adhesion and promoting EMT and tumor metastasis. Conversely, SKP2 inhibition promotes E-cadherin accumulation and alleviates tumor metastasis.128,129 Rho family proteins mediate actin cytoskeleton remodeling, which is essential for cell motility and cell-cell adhesion.130 Ras homolog family member A (RhoA), a member of the Rho family of GTPases, contributes to tumor metastasis.131 In collaboration with Myc, SKP2 enhances RhoA transcription by recruiting zinc finger and BTB domain containing 17 (Miz1) and p300 to the RhoA promoter, thereby inducing cell migration and tumor metastasis in breast cancer. Conversely, disrupting this complex impairs RhoA transcription and tumor metastasis, indicating that SKP2 exerts its oncogenic activity in a manner dependent on SCF-SKP2 E3 ligase activity. In summary, SKP2 significantly contributes to facilitating the capability of tumor metastasis in various cancer types. Genetic and pharmacological inhibition of SKP2 may represent an attractive therapeutic strategy in treating cancer patients with metastasis (Figure 5E).

Cell Proliferation and Stemness

Protein kinases play a significant role in cell signaling, proliferation, invasion and migration. The serine/threonine kinase Akt is frequently activated in multiple cancers. Abnormal activation of Akt/mTOR signaling contributes greatly to several malignancies, particularly cell proliferation and stemness.132,133 K63-linked non-proteolytic ubiquitination of Akt is recognized as a critical mechanism for Akt activation. SKP2-mediated Akt K63-linked ubiquitination is responsible for Akt membrane recruitment, leading to mTORC2-dependent phosphorylation and activation in response to epidermal growth factor (EGF) stimulation. Furthermore, highly expressed SKP2 is positively correlated with Akt activation and poor clinical outcomes in breast cancer.134 Recently, tripartite motif containing 21 (TRIM21) was reported to disrupt SKP2-mediated Akt K63-linked ubiquitination and block Akt membrane localization and its kinase activity.135 In addition to Akt membrane recruitment, SKP2-mediated K63-linked ubiquitination of Akt also promotes its EGF-induced mitochondrial localization.136 Pharmacological inactivation of SCF-SKP2 E3 ligase limits suppresses cancer stem cell traits and tumor progression by inhibiting Akt/mTOR signaling.137 However, the underlying mechanisms by which K63-linked ubiquitination regulates Akt subcellular localization remain unclear. Furthermore, the overexpression of Yes1 associated transcriptional regulator (YAP) strongly activates Akt signaling. Activated Akt1 phosphorylates SKP2 protein at Ser72, which promotes p300-mediated acetylation of cytoplasmic SKP2 and prevents its APC/Cdh1-mediated degradation in cancers.71,138 Furthermore, acetylated SKP2 is prone to degrade phosphorylated FOXO1 mediated by Akt. Together, these processes cause genomic instability and oncogenesis.71 In parallel, YAP undergoes K63-linked non-proteolytic ubiquitination by SCF-SKP2 E3 ligase, which enhances its interaction with transcriptional Enhanced Associate Domain (TEAD) and promotes its nuclear transport and transcriptional activity139,140 (Figure 5F). Disrupting the positive feedback loop of YAP/Akt/SKP2 may represent a potential therapeutic strategy for cancer. In addition to Akt pathway, SKP2 is able to regulate cell proliferation and stemness through other mechanisms. In CRC, SKP2 activates Wnt and Notch signaling by mediating the ubiquitination and degradation of amino-terminal enhancer of split (AES).141 SKP2 promotes prostate cancer cell growth by promoting polyubiquitination and proteasomal degradation of 17-β-hydroxysteroid dehydrogenase 4 and androgen receptor (AR).142,143

DNA Repair

Several studies suggest that SKP2 is associated with DNA repair. Reportedly, It has been reported that low doses of UV irradiation triggers SKP2-dependent p21 degradation, which is essential for optimal DNA repair.144 SKP2 also regulates DNA repair by ubiquitinating other DNA repair-related proteins (Figure 5G). For example, SKP2 protein is phosphorylated by casein kinase 2 alpha 1 in response to Double Strand Breaks (DSBs). Subsequently, The SCF-SKP2 complex interacts with the MRN complex and triggers the K63-linked non-proteolytic ubiquitination of NBS1. This ubiquitination is critical for ATM activation at DNA damage sites and regulates the DNA damage response and homologous recombination (HR) repair.145 Moreover, SKP2-mediated K63-linked non-proteolytic ubiquitination and stabilization of MTH1 reduces DNA damage and enhances melanoma cells resistance to oxidative stress.146 Furthermore, SKP2 can regulate the selection of DNA repair pathways by promoting DNA end resection. Mechanistically, the SCF-SKP2 complex suppresses the APC/Cdh1-mediated degradation of RB binding protein 8, endonuclease (CTIP), which physically and functionally interacts with the MRE11 complex and drives the bidirectional resection of DSBs by MRE11 and EXO1 in the G2 phase.147,148

Cell Metabolism

Metabolic reprogramming is a hallmark of cancer.149,150 Recently, the connection between SKP2 and cellular metabolism has attracted increasing attention. Accumulating evidence indicates that SKP2 overexpression positively correlates with glycolysis, providing sufficient ATP for cancer cell growth.151 SKP2 not only enhances glucose uptake with the focus of glucose transport proteins on the cell surface, but also activates glycolytic enzyme hexokinase 2 (HK2) by mediating Akt ubiquitination.134,152 Glycolysis is significantly associated with chemotherapy resistance, and cisplatin-resistant cancer cells exhibit higher glycolytic capability than cisplatin-sensitive cancer cells.153 Therefore, SKP2-mediated glycolysis also confers platinum resistance.136 Furthermore, SKP2 activates the serine/threonine kinase 11 (LKB1)/AMPK pathway by inducing K63-linked ubiquitination of LKB1, thereby restoring intracellular ATP levels and providing a survival advantage under energy stress.154 SKP2 can also ubiquitinate mitochondrial IDH1 and IDH2, causing mitochondrial dysfunction, metabolic disorders, and reduced cellular metabolic capacity155 (Figure 5H). As mentioned above, SKP2 is involved in lipid peroxidation, while its function in lipid synthesis and amino acid metabolism is still unknown.

SKP2 and the TME: Implications for Immunotherapy

Up to now, the role of SKP2 in the TME and immunotherapy efficacy remains unknown. To address this, we attempted to assess the association between SKP2 expression and immune infiltration by using TIMER2.0 database (https://compbio.cn/timer2/) to calculate ImmuneScore, StromaScore and MicroenvironmentScore from TCGA pan-cancer data.156 These results revealed a negative association between SKP2 expression and ImmuneScore, StromaScore, and MicroenvironmentScore across multiple cancers. Furthermore, the expression of SKP2 is positively associated with immune infiltration of Th2 cells in the vast majority of cancers (Figure 6A). Th2 cells suppress anti-tumor T cell immunity by inhibiting the activation of effector T cells. Intratumoral Th2 cells infiltration correlates with poorer survival in pancreatic cancer.156,157 Recently, Ferrena et al established a transgenic SKP2 knockout osteosarcoma mouse model. They found that SKP2 knockout resulted in increased expression of macrophages-related genes and decreased expression of genes related to T cells, B cells, and vascular cells.41 Moreover, SKP2 inhibition has been reported to promote macrophages phagocytosis of cancer cells and cytotoxic T cell infiltration by inducing CD47 and CDC6 degradation, respectively.158,159 Collectively, these findings suggest that SKP2 may exert oncogenic function by shaping the TME. Tumor mutation burden (TMB), tumor-specific neoantigens (NEO) and microsatellite instability (MSI) are established biomarkers that correlate with immunotherapy response.160–162 To investigate the relationship between SKP2 expression and immunotherapy efficacy, we then analyzed the correlations between SKP2 expression and TMB, NEO, and MSI. In several tumors, particularly sarcoma (SARC), renal clear cell carcinoma (KIRC), colon adenocarcinoma (COAD), rectal adenocarcinoma (READ), and lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD), SKP2 expression is positively correlated with TMB, NEO, and MSI (Figure 6B–D). These results suggest that SARC, KIRC, COAD, READ and LUAD patients who have higher SKP2 expression may derive greater benefit from immunotherapy. In NSCLC, SKP2 inhibition stabilizes PD-L1 through K63-linked polyubiquitination at Lys136 and Lys280, promoting immune evasion. Consequently, SKP2 inhibition converts the TME from “hot” to “cold” and abrogated therapeutic outcomes of anti-PD-1/PD-L1 immunotherapy.163 Conversely, SKP2 inhibition enhanced the tumor sensitivity to anti-PD-1 immunotherapy in TNBC.159 Given tumor heterogeneity, SKP2 may exert different effects on immunotherapy efficacy across cancer types. Thus, the tissue- and context-specific functions of SKP2 in different cell types within the TME, and its contribution to immune evasion still need to explore. Indirect SKP2 Modulators

Recent studies have shown that the CDK4/6 inhibitor palbociclib downregulates SKP2 mRNA in lung cancer cells, hepatocellular cancer cells and pancreatic cancer cells.164,165 Moreover, nobiletin represses SKP2 transcription by upregulating FOXO3a, thereby inhibiting cell proliferation and inducting G1 cell cycle arrest and apoptosis in renal cell carcinoma. The combination of nobiletin and palbociclib synergistically inhibits tumors growth in in vitro and in vivo models.166 It was demonstrated that SKP2 was highly expressed in melanoma tissues with BRAFV600E mutation, and its high expression was particularly associated with poor clinical outcome of melanoma patients with BRAFV600E mutation but not BRAFWT. The small inhibitor of BRAFV600E Vemurafenib, transcriptionally downregulates SKP2 expression in a c-Myc-dependent manner in melanoma cells.167 Similarly, the small molecule EGFR inhibitor (EGFRi) gefitinib induces IP3R3-mediated calcium release, which inhibits the binding of menin to the SKP2 promoter and reduces SKP2 transcription. Furthermore, gefitinib synergizes with menin inhibition to suppress SKP2 expression and colorectal cancer growth.168 Through comprehensive screening of natural products, diosmetin, was identified to suppress the proliferation of chronic myelogenous leukemia. Diosmetin significantly reduces the mRNA expression SKP2 and upregulates its substrate p27. It exerts antitumor effect by inactivating the SKP2/B-cell receptor (Bcr)- ABL proto-oncogene 1, non-receptor tyrosine kinase (Abl) signaling axis.169 Bortezomib also triggers the downregulation of SKP2 mRNA and the accumulation of p27 in colorectal cancer.170 In addition, several other natural products, including platycodin D, punicalagin, All-trans-retinoic acid, and wogonin, have been shown to downregulate SKP2 mRNA expression.171–175

Specifically, retinoic acid (RA) induces the cell cycle by inhibiting the SKP2-mediated p27 degradation. RA both decreases SKP2 transcription and destabilizes the SKP2 protein by inactivating the APC/Cdh1 complex.176 In gastrointestinal stromal tumors, imatinib promotes SKP2 degradation by increasing nuclear Cdh1.177 Besides, Xanthohumol and dioscin have been identified to facilitate the interaction between Cdh1 and SKP2, which promotes the K48-linked polyubiquitination and degradation of SKP2.178,179 As mentioned above, Akt-mediated SKP2 phosphorylation is well established to regulate its E3 ligase activity by modulating its degradation and subcellular localization. Therefore, Akt inhibition might represent a promising strategy to target SKP2. Many Akt inhibitors, such as ABT-751 and LY294002, have been proved to suppress SKP2 expression by inhibiting Akt signaling in Urinary Bladder Urothelial Carcinoma-Derived Cells.180 Li et al found that the guaianolide dimer KGA-1002 promotes Akt degradation, leading to the reduction of SKP2 stability.181 Curcumin and pyrogallol exert antitumor effects via the PI3K/Akt/SKP2 signaling pathway in breast cancer and HCC, respectively.182,183 Besides, Okuyama et al found that prolonged cetuximab treatment significantly promotes the accumulation of p27 by inhibiting the Akt/SKP2 signaling pathway, leading to cell motility reduction and G1 arrest.184 Over the past few years, several novel Akt inhibitors, including ipatasertib, capivasertib and MK-2206, have been developed. However, whether these inhibitors regulate SKP2 expression or function has not yet been reported. Studies have shown that mTOR directly interacts with SKP2 and phosphorylates it at Ser64, thereby preventing its ubiquitination and degradation.199 Thus, the mTOR inhibitors rapamycin reduces SKP2 stability and make it more susceptible to ubiquitination and degradation by inhibiting mTOR pathway.185 USP14 and USP2 have also been demonstrated to deubiquitinate and stabilize SKP2. The USP14/UCHL15 inhibitor b-AP15 dramatically enhances the anti-tumor effect in vemurafenib-resistant melanoma by downregulating SKP2 protein.65 Tanshinone IIA promotes SKP2 degradation by disrupting the interaction between USP2 and SKP2.186 Furthermore, galangin and flavonoid kaempferol B (FKB) were demonstrated to indirectly induce SKP2 protein degradation in a ubiquitin- and proteasome-dependent manner.187,188 Combination of bortezomib and FKB synergistically inhibits prostate tumor growth by downregulating SKP2 and upregulating p27 and p21.188 SMIP004, as a commonly used SKP2 inhibitor, can also downregulate the expression of SKP2 protein and stabilize p27. Interestingly, SMIP004 increases nuclear levels of p27 at low micromolar concentrations and exhibits preferential cytotoxicity against cancer cells over normal human fibroblasts.189

Direct SKP2 Degraders

Proteolysis targeting chimeras (PROTACs) has emerged as a useful technology for cancer therapy.200 Recently, the hederagenin derivative HD15 was designed for SKP2 protein degradation using PROTAC technology. HD15 can effectively degrade SKP2 protein and show superior antitumor activity compared with the parent compound hederagenin.190 In addition to the degradation of SKP2 protein, SKP2 can also be harnessed for targeted protein degradation owing to its frequent overexpression in various cancers. Dong et al successfully designed an SKP2-recuiting degrader compound 2–1 by linking the selective, non-covalent SKP2 recruiter SL1 and BRD4 inhibitor JQ1. Compound 2–1 significantly promotes BRD4 degradation in a proteasome- and neddylation-dependent manner and inhibits cell proliferation.201 Moreover, Chu et al developed a screening system Death FUSion Escaper screen (DEFUSE), which aimed to high-throughput screen compounds that degrade proteins of interest by fusing them with a highly effective triggerable death protein. By implementing this method, they successfully discovered a SKP2 degrader SKPer1. Mechanistically, SKPer1 promotes SKP2 degradation by inducing proximity and interaction between SKP2 and an E3 ligase STIP1 homology and U-Box containing protein 1 (STUB1). Excitingly, SKPer1 not only showed remarkable antitumor activity in organoid models but also in vivo models with good safety profiles.191 Additionally, several other targeted protein degradation approaches, such as the degradation tag system, Trim-Away, peptide-directed lysosomal degradation and specific and non-genetic inhibitor of apoptosis protein (IAP)-dependent protein erasers (SNIPERs) have been established and they may also serve as good platforms to develop SKP2 degraders.202–205

Direct SKP2 Inhibitors

Obviously, SKP2 promotes tumor progress by ubiquitinating and degrading its substrates. Thus, inhibiting SKP2 activity represents an alternative therapeutic strategy. Through high-throughput in silico screening, SZL-P1-41 was identified as an inhibitor of the SKP2-SKP1 interaction and the E3 ligase activity of the SCF-SKP2 complex. Notably, SZL-P1-41 has demonstrated significant antitumor activities in vitro and in vivo models.137 Betulinic acid and camptothecin have shown significant anticancer activity in NSCLC. They prevent the SKP2-SKP1 interaction by directly binding to SKP2 and forming Hydrogen bonds with Lys145 residue and Ser 121 residue, respectively.128,192 Recently, the small molecule GH501 and compound 10h have also been shown to exert excellent antitumor activity by disrupting the SKP2-SKP1 interaction.193,194 In multiple myeloma, DT204 was identified as a SKP2 inhibitor through virtual and computational screening. It prevents SKP2 binding to Cullin 1, and treating with DT204 increases p27 expression and overcomes Bortezomib resistance in multiple myeloma.89 Targeting the SKP2-CKS1 protein-protein interaction has also shown potent antitumor effects in vivo and in vitro.206,207 Additionally, several SKP2 inhibitors, including SKPin C1195–197 and AAA-237,81 have been proved to block SKP2 incorporation into the SCF complex. As mentioned above, SKP2 antagonizes the interaction between p300 and p53 to suppress p300-mediated p53 acetylation and transactivation capability, thereby repressing p53-dependent apoptosis.42 On this basis, the inhibitor M1 was identified to bind to SKP2 and disrupt the SKP2/p300 interaction. Surprisingly, M1 exhibits remarkable antitumor effects in multiple cancer cells in a dose-dependent manner.198

Conclusions

SKP2 is an E3 ligase that assembles with SKP1, Cullin 1, and RBX1 to form the SCF–SKP2 complex. It conjugates K48- and K63-linked ubiquitin chains to substrates, leading to proteasomal degradation and nonproteolytic functional modulation. Numerous studies have shown that SKP2 is overexpressed in multiple cancer types, including breast cancer, colorectal cancer, prostate cancer, and lung cancer. Moreover, SKP2 overexpression correlates with poor overall survival, adverse tumor prognosis and drug resistance. Mechanistically, SKP2 regulates diverse biological processes, which collectively drive its tumor-promoting property and establish it an attractive therapeutic target.

In this review, we have comprehensively described the regulatory networks and oncogenic functions of SKP2, providing valuable information for optimizing therapeutic strategies against SKP2. Current approaches to antagonize SKP2 fall into three categories: indirect modulation of SKP2 expression or stability through upstream signaling pathways; direct inhibition of SCF–SKP2 complex activity or protein–protein interactions; and direct degradation of SKP2 protein via PROTAC or molecular glue technologies. Given the importance of protein-protein interactions in oncogenic functions of SKP2, developing peptide-based inhibitors to disrupt these protein-protein interactions may represent an effective therapeutic strategy.208 Meanwhile, emerging approaches, such as antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs), RNAi-based therapies and SKP2-targeted nanotherapeutics may offer additional avenues for future development of SKP2 inhibitors.209–211 SKP2-targeted nanotherapeutics, with their lower toxicity and enhanced membrane permeability, may also help to overcome drug resistance.212 Despite these advances, most SKP2-targeted agents remain at the preclinical stage, and their clinical translation remains impeded by pharmacokinetic limitations, undefined toxicity profiles, and the absence of predictive biomarkers. Bridging this gap will require iterative medicinal chemistry optimization and carefully design early-phase combination trials. Given the undefined toxicity profiles of SKP2 inhibitors, accuracy of nursing diagnoses and multidimensional care is very important to ultimately optimize patient outcomes.213

The SKP2 regulatory network is highly complex. For example, c-Myc promotes SKP2 transcription while also serving as a substrate for SKP2-mediated degradation. SKP2 mediates K63-linked ubiquitination of Akt, and its expression correlates positively with Akt activation. In turn, activated Akt promotes SKP2 expression by preventing its degradation. These feedback loops may contribute to the elevated SKP2 expression observed in cancers. Therefore, targeting these positive feedback pathways may provide unexpectedly benefits in cancer treatment. Immunotherapy has emerged as a promising strategy in the treatment of cancers.214,215 However, the impact of SKP2 on the immunotherapy response appears to vary across tumor types. Therefore, it becomes particularly important to identify the underlying mechanism and explore the function of SKP2 in the TME. More genetic mouse models with intact immunity and clinical trials should be developed to evaluate the effect of SKP2 on TME and immunotherapy response.

Despite the substantial progress summarized in this review, several key questions remain to be addressed. First, how does SKP2 selectively determine K48 versus K63 ubiquitination on distinct substrates, and is this selectivity regulated by post-translational modifications or associated proteins? Second, can SKP2 expression or activity serve as a predictive biomarker for patient stratification in clinical trials, particularly for immunotherapy and combination regimens? Third, what are the long-term toxicities of systemic SKP2 inhibition, and how can these be mitigated through isoform-selective or tumor-targeted strategies? Finally, what additional tissue-specific or cancer-type-specific substrates remain to be identified that may explain the differential dependency on SKP2 across tumor types? Addressing these questions will be essential for translating the mechanistic understanding of SKP2 into clinically meaningful therapeutic strategies.

In summary, the elucidation of SKP2’s oncogenic mechanisms and the clinical development of SKP2 inhibitors remain in their early stages. Further preclinical and clinical studies are warranted to fully characterize its functions and to validate the efficacy and safety of candidate inhibitors. We hope this review will stimulate further studies on the development of novel SKP2-targeted strategies for cancer therapy.

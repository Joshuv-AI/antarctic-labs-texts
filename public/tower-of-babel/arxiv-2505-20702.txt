Magnetic field dependence of the spin susceptibility on conventional s s -wave superconductor LaRu 4 P 12 revealed by 31 P-NMR and 139 La-NMR
Riku Matsubayashi 1,, Shiki Ogata 1, Taishi Ihara 1, Hiroyasu Matsudaira 1, Shunsaku Kitagawa 1, Kenji Ishida 1, Yusuke Nakai 2, Hitoshi Sugawara 3, Hideyuki Sato 4

Abstract
The magnetic field dependence of the spin part of Knight shift, which is proportional to the superconducting-state spin susceptibility, was investigated at two nuclear sites, 31 P and 139 La in a conventional s s -wave superconductor LaRu 4 P 12 . After the analyses, we confirmed that the superconducting-state spin susceptibility is proportional to magnetic field, and connects to the normal-state spin susceptibility smoothly. This is a textbook example, when the superconductivity is broken with the orbital pair-breaking effect.



In superconductors, two electrons in the crystal form Cooper pairs, which can be either spin-singlet state with antiparallel spins

Most superconductors, including LaRu

P

In the spin-singlet state, there are two main mechanisms contributing to the break of Cooper pairs under magnetic field: the Pauli pair-breaking effect
[

,

14

and the orbital pair-breaking effect
[

In the Pauli pair-breaking effect, the Zeeman splitting energy due to the external magnetic field exceeds the condensation energy of the superconducting (SC) state, thereby destroying the Cooper pairs
[
16
,
17
]
.
On the other hand, in the orbital pair-breaking effect, the Lorentz force resulting from the applied external magnetic field exceeds the binding force between two electrons and destroys the pairs
[
18
,
19
]
.

Investigating the magnetic field
H
dependence of the SC spin susceptibility gives various insights into the SC symmetry.
For instance, when the Pauli pair-breaking effect is dominant, a discontinuous change in the
H
dependence of the spin susceptibility or the formation of spatially oscillating Fulde‑Ferrell‑Larkin‑Ovchinnikov state has been observed near the upper critical field
\mu_{0}H_{\text{c2}}
in various strongly correlated electron superconductors
[

,

,
22
,
23
,
24
]
.
In contrast, when a spin-triplet state is realized, it has been unveiled that the decrease in the spin susceptibility is suppressed under
H

H_{\text{c2}}
, from recent experiments on UPt
3
[
6
,
7
,

]
,UTe

[

,

and K

[

On the other hand, precise measurements of the spin susceptibility against
H

This is because the spin susceptibility in the SC state is difficult to evaluate, as discussed later.
Theoretically, the quasiparticle density of states around Fermi energy
D(E_{\text{F}})
in the mixed state is proportional to
H/H_{\text{c2}}
.
In fact, a linear change in the density of states was reported from
H

[

30

As the spin susceptibility
\chi_{\text{spin}}
is proportional to
D(E_{\text{F}})

\chi_{\text{spin}}
in the SC state would be expressed as a linear function of
H
.

Since the spin susceptibility

In fact, when

Particularly, the spectrum broadening by electric quadrupole interaction and the SC diamagnetism make the experimental resolution worse and more complicated.

LaRu

In addition, NMR measurements can be performed, and a sharp NMR peak is observed at both the

Therefore, from the precise Knight shift measurements at both sites, it is possible to evaluate

In this paper, we performed

We revealed that the SC spin susceptibility above 1 T showed a

The Knight shift values were determined at the peak position in each NMR spectrum. The error bar for the Knight shift value was taken as the width at 99 % of the peak intensity of the spectrum.

In the SC state, the measured Knight shift
K(T,H)
can be expressed as

\displaystyle K(T,H)=K_{\text{spin}}(T,H)+K_{\text{orb}}+K_{\text{dia}}(T,H).

(1)

Here,
K_{\text{spin}}
is the electron-spin component of the Knight shift.

K_{\text{orb}}
is driven by the Van Vleck susceptibility induced by the orbital angular momentum of the electron, which is
T
independent at low
T
.

K_{\text{dia}}
arises from the diamagnetism in the SC state, and it takes a negative value and is
T
and
H
dependent.

The difference between
K(10\ \text{K})
and
K(1.4\ \text{K})
is expressed as

\begin{split}\Delta K^{\text{}}&=K^{\text{}}(10\ \text{K})-K^{\text{}}(1.4\ \text{K})\\
&=\Delta K_{\text{spin}}^{\text{}}-K_{\text{dia}}.\end{split}

(2)

\Delta K^{\text{}}_{\text{spin}}
is expressed as

\begin{split}\Delta K^{\text{}}_{\text{spin}}=K^{\text{}}_{\text{spin}}(10\ \text{K})-K^{\text{}}_{\text{spin}}(1.4\ \text{K}).\end{split}

(3)

At lower fields,
K_{\text{dia}}
becomes larger and comparable to
K_{\text{spin}}
, making it difficult to derive
H
dependence of
K_{\text{spin}}
.
Since
K_{\text{dia}}
is a bulk effect, it is reasonable to assume that
K_{\text{dia}}
works at the
31
P and
139
La sites in the same manner.
Thus, we can subtract the contribution of
K_{\text{dia}}
by measuring
K
at two crystalographic different sites.
The difference in
\Delta K
between
139
La and
31
P sites is given by

\begin{split}\Delta K^{\text{La}}-\Delta K^{\text{P}}&=\Delta K_{\text{spin}}^{\text{La}}-\Delta K_{\text{spin}}^{\text{P}}\\
&=(A_{\text{hf}}^{\text{La}}-A_{\text{hf}}^{\text{P}})\Delta\chi_{\text{spin}}.\end{split}

(4)

\Delta\chi_{\text{spin}}
is expressed as

\begin{split}\Delta\chi_{\text{spin}}=\chi_{\text{spin}}(10\ \text{K})-\chi_{\text{spin}}(1.4\ \text{K}).\end{split}

(5)

Thus, we can get
\Delta K_{\text{spin}}^{i}
(
i
=
31
P and
139
La) as

\begin{split}\Delta K_{\text{spin}}^{i}&=A^{i}_{\text{hf}}\Delta\chi_{\text{spin}}\\
&=\frac{A^{i}_{\text{hf}}}{A^{\text{La}}_{\text{hf}}-A^{\text{P}}_{\text{hf}}}(\Delta K^{\text{La}}-\Delta K^{\text{P}}).\end{split}

(6)

Here,
A^{i}_{\text{hf}}
is the hyperfine coupling constant at the
i
site and
\chi_{\text{spin}}
is the spin susceptibility in LaRu
4
P
12
. The relation of
K
at
31
P site and
139
La site at each temperature (from 10 K to 220 K) is shown in Fig. 4. The dashed line represents the linear function fitted from 10 K to 220 K. Here, the slope of the linear line gives the value of
A^{\text{P}}_{\text{hf}}/A^{\text{La}}_{\text{hf}}
. The obtained value of
A^{\text{P}}_{\text{hf}}/A^{\text{La}}_{\text{hf}}
is 0.60. Therefore, we can evaluate
H
dependence of
\Delta K_{\text{spin}}^{i}
by using this value and the experimental data.

In conclusion, we performed
31
P and
139
La -NMR measurements on LaRu
4
P
12
to investigate the magnetic field response of the spin susceptibility.
The Knight shift obtained from the
31
P and
139
La-NMR measurements decreases at low temperatures.
In addition, the NMR spectrum becomes asymmetric and broadened at lower temperatures.
The spectrum change in the SC state is attributed to the Redfield pattern, which is a characteristic NMR spectrum in the vortex state.
From NMR Knight shift, it is confirmed that the spin susceptibility
\chi_{\text{spin}}
can be expressed with a coefficient
\alpha
as
\chi_{\text{spin}}=\alpha(H-H_{\text{c2}})+\chi_{\text{normal}}
, which indicates a linear relationship with the magnetic field.
This result is consistent with the previous result
[
36
]
, and theoretical prediction when
H_{\rm c2}
is determined with the orbital pair-breaking effect. The relationship is actually observed in FeSe recently
[
44
]
.

Equity structure and corporate innovation performance: Evidence from Chinese high-tech companies
Authors: Hesheng Chen, Sijia Qiao, Kegong Zhang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn the era of globalization and technological innovation, enhancing business innovation performance is pivotal for driving economic growth and maintaining competitive edge. Among the various aspects of corporate governance, equity structure stands out for its influence on firms' innovative activities. This study explores the impact of equity structure on the innovation performance of high-tech listed companies, as well as the mediating role of R&D investment and the moderating effect of market competition. A review of the literature identifies gaps in current research, such as the lack of attention to the multifaceted influence of equity structure and the limited focus on how external environmental factors affect innovation performance.The research employs a bidirectional fixed-effects regression model, analyzing unbalanced panel data from high-tech listed companies between 2012 and 2021. Findings suggest an inverted U-shaped relationship between equity ownership concentration and innovation performance, indicating that both low and excessive concentration can be detrimental. Additionally, equity checks and balances positively influence innovation performance. R&D investment acts as a mediator between equity structure and innovation performance, while market competition moderates the relationship between ownership concentration and innovation performance. However, the moderating role of equity checks and balances on innovation performance is not significant.These results offer new insights into how equity structure, R&D investment, and market competition jointly affect innovation performance. They provide practical guidance for high-tech listed companies seeking to optimize their shareholding structures and enhance their innovation capabilities, as well as a theoretical foundation for policymakers developing strategies to foster corporate innovation. Further research could expand this analysis to different sectors and geographic contexts, examining additional mediating and moderating variables. The findings serve as a valuable reference for optimizing equity structures and leveraging the mediating role of R&D investment to promote the healthy and orderly development of high-tech firms.Keywords: High-tech enterprises, Innovation performance, Equity structure, R&D investment, Market competition

Introduction

With the integration of the global economy and the rapid advancements in science and technology, high-quality innovation has become a critical driver for the sustainable growth of enterprises [1]. In this evolving landscape, both academia and practitioners are increasingly focused on how companies can enhance their technological innovation and improve innovation performance through effective internal governance structures, particularly through the optimization of equity structures. Equity structure, being central to corporate governance, has emerged as a significant factor influencing firms' R&D investment and innovation performance, making it a key topic in corporate governance and strategic management research.

The existing literature indicates that equity structure, a cornerstone of corporate governance, significantly impacts firms' innovation activities. However, the academic community has yet to reach a consensus on the specific relationship between equity structure and business innovation performance [2,3]. Particularly, the relationship between equity ownership concentration and innovation performance remains ambiguous [[4], [5], [6]]. Additionally, the complex interplay between equity checks and balances and R&D investment has not been thoroughly examined [7,8]. Furthermore, the role of market competition as an external moderator of the relationship between equity structure and innovation performance has received limited attention [9].

Motivated by these research gaps, this study aims to provide empirical insights into the relationship between equity structure, R&D investment, and innovation performance among listed high-tech firms in China. By exploring the underlying mechanisms and external moderating factors, this research seeks to offer new theoretical support for understanding the innovation dynamics of high-tech firms. It also aims to provide valuable references for policy-making and corporate management practices. Most existing academic studies either examine how equity structure enhances business innovation performance or consider R&D investment as an explanatory variable to assess its impact on innovation performance. However, there is a scarcity of research that delves into the intrinsic mechanisms linking equity structure, R&D investment, and innovation performance. Even fewer studies analyze the intricate relationship introduced by the mediating role of R&D investment and its impact on innovation performance through the dimensions of equity structure. This paper posits that R&D investment is both a consequence of optimized equity structure and a condition that spurs innovation performance. Therefore, this study emphasizes the need to deeply explore the intrinsic mechanisms between these three variables. It specifically investigates the mediating effect of R&D investment and finds that the results not only broaden the theoretical scope but also deepen our understanding of the underlying mechanisms. Moreover, current literature lacks thorough analyses of how external factors, such as market competition, moderate the effect of equity structure on business innovation performance. Thus, a significant gap exists in this area of research. To address this, this paper incorporates market competition as a moderating variable, examining its influence on the direct path between equity structure and business innovation performance. The study’s findings determine whether external environmental factors can amplify the effects generated by equity structure optimization.

Regarding the selection of research data, many studies fail to segment their samples by industry, often treating all A-share listed companies as a single group. Given that each industry develops differently, such generalization limits the applicability and relevance of the findings. Since this paper focuses on innovation performance, it adopts the approach of previous researchers to categorize industries. Specifically, it selects high-tech listed companies, known for their robust innovation capabilities and substantial R&D investment ratios, as the research sample. This approach enhances the reliability and specificity of the study, ensuring that the conclusions drawn are both robust and targeted.

This paper draws a series of meaningful conclusions through the empirical analysis of unbalanced panel data from high-tech listed companies between 2012 and 2021. The findings reveal an inverted U-shaped relationship between ownership concentration and business innovation performance, indicating that both low and excessive concentrations can be detrimental. On the other hand, equity checks and balances have a significant positive impact on business innovation performance. Moreover, R&D investment plays a crucial mediating role between equity structure and business innovation performance. Market competition significantly moderates the relationship between ownership concentration and business innovation performance.

By thoroughly examining the effects of equity structure, R&D investment, and market competition on business innovation performance, this study provides new theoretical insights into the innovation mechanisms of high-tech listed companies. Additionally, it offers important guidance for policy formulation and corporate management practices, thereby contributing to a deeper understanding of how to optimize corporate governance and enhance innovation performance.

Literature review

Equity structure forms the bedrock of a company's operations and management, significantly impacting R&D investment and innovation performance. The dimensions of equity structure are typically divided into two categories: equity ownership concentration and equity checks and balances. Equity ownership concentration refers to the level of ownership held by the top shareholders within an enterprise, whereas equity checks and balances pertain to the mutual oversight among major shareholders.

Equity structure and business innovation performance

Equity structure is a fundamental aspect of corporate governance and profoundly influences a firm's R&D investment and innovation performance. Shleifer,A., & Vishny,R.W. [2], in their survey of corporate governance, propose that equity structure indirectly impacts firm innovation by shaping corporate governance mechanisms. They contend that dispersed equity might lead to higher agency costs, potentially diminishing the firm's inclination towards long-term innovation investments. However, ownership concentration can introduce new challenges, such as conflicts of interest between controlling and minority shareholders, which can disrupt the effective formulation of innovation strategies. Subsequent research by Morck,R. et al. (1988) elaborates that while moderate ownership concentration can enhance decision-making efficiency, excessive concentration can lead controlling shareholders to prioritize their personal interests over the firm’s innovation goals, thereby eroding the firm's innovation capacity.

The study by Bennedsen, M., & Wolfenzon, D [10]. presents another viewpoint, suggesting that equity diversification aids in achieving internal supervision and checks and balances, promoting more rational and scientifically sound investment decisions. They argue that ownership concentration may lead to suboptimal decision-making due to a lack of effective monitoring and can even result in the exploitation of minority shareholders by controlling shareholders. Furthermore, diversity in the equity structure, such as the involvement of shareholders with varied backgrounds, can introduce a broader range of perspectives and innovative energies to the firm, a notion supported by Bantel, K. A., & Jackson, S. E. [11].

Equity ownership concentration and business innovation performance

Equity ownership concentration, measured by the proportion of shares held by a firm's top shareholders, is a crucial quantitative indicator in corporate governance research. It reflects the distribution of corporate control and has a significant impact on business innovation performance. Theoretical foundations for understanding the relationship between ownership concentration and business innovation performance are provided by modern stewardship theory and agency cost theory. Modern stewardship theory posits that managerial shareholding might be detrimental to a firm's long-term investment in research and development (R&D) because it could reduce the firm's long-term returns [12]. Conversely, agency cost theory suggests that increased equity ownership concentration can help mitigate agency problems and improve decision-making efficiency, thereby fostering innovation output [13].

Empirical studies present mixed evidence on this topic. Several studies support a positive relationship between equity ownership concentration and business innovation performance. For instance, Hosono,K. et al. (2004) observed in their study of the Japanese manufacturing industry that firms with higher ownership concentration tended to invest more in R&D. This could be attributed to the greater control that majority shareholders exert, leading them to focus more on the firm's long-term stability. Similarly, Belloc, F [14]. found that ownership concentration helps to improve the efficiency of corporate governance and promote technological innovation. Additionally, Partington, G. H [15]. proposed that increased ownership concentration facilitates the rapid flow of information within the firm and mitigates the problem of information asymmetry, thereby promoting more scientific and rational decision-making.

Some scholars argue that equity ownership concentration is negatively related to business innovation performance. Shivdasani A [16]. posits that when shareholders have absolute control over a firm, the lack of monitoring by other stakeholders increases their incentives for self-interested behavior, which is detrimental to enhancing business innovation performance. Yafeh,Y.,&Yosha,O., (2003), through an analysis of Japanese industrial firms, found a negative correlation between ownership concentration and corporate R&D expenditures, suggesting that reducing ownership concentration could promote corporate innovation. Similarly, Zhang Y. J. et al. [6] discovered that increased ownership concentration inhibits firms' innovative activities. Further, Li J. et al. (2016) noted that in an intensely competitive product market environment, firms with high equity ownership concentration struggle to maintain the continuity of their innovative activities. Minetti, R. et al. [17] conducted an empirical analysis of the Italian manufacturing industry and found that firms with lower ownership concentration invested more in research and development (R&D). This finding suggests that there may be a negative correlation between ownership concentration and a firm’s innovation capability.

However, other studies suggest that the relationship between ownership concentration and business innovation performance may be nonlinear. Using sample data from small and medium-sized firms listed in Shenzhen, Yang D.W [18]. identified a significant inverted U-shaped relationship between ownership concentration and technological innovation. This implies that both very low and very high levels of ownership concentration can be detrimental to innovation. Similar findings were reported by Li S.J [19]. and Yang J.J. et al. [20], who also explored the mediating role of managers' innovation motivation in the relationship between ownership concentration and firms' autonomous innovation.

In summary, the relationship between equity ownership concentration and business innovation performance is complex and may be influenced by a variety of factors, including national culture, market environment, and industry characteristics. Future research should further investigate the dynamic relationship between ownership concentration and firms' innovative performance, as well as the contextual mechanisms at play in different settings.

Equity checks and balances and business innovation performance

Equity checks and balances refer to a scenario where no single shareholder can dominate a company's decision-making process. Instead, multiple shareholders collaborate to influence corporate governance, forming an effective monitoring and checking mechanism. This type of shareholding structure plays a vital role in mitigating the issues associated with concentrated shareholdings. By reducing conflicts between large and small shareholders, equity checks and balances limit the potential for large shareholders to exploit the rights of smaller shareholders, thereby safeguarding the interests of all stakeholders.

Effective equity checks and balances harness the collective insight of diverse shareholders, enabling a multifaceted review of the company's status and ensuring scientifically sound decision-making [21]. This mechanism not only encourages the majority shareholder to allocate resources in the interest of the company's long-term development but also supports the promotion of independent innovation within the enterprise. In this environment, majority shareholders are more likely to back managers' innovative decisions, alleviate their concerns, and spur their motivation to innovate [20]. Bennedsen, M., & Wolfenzon, D [10]. notes that if the second-largest shareholder holds a substantial portion of the shares, it can diminish the first-largest shareholder's ability to engage in behaviors that might undermine the company's value.

Academic research on the relationship between equity checks and balances and business innovation performance predominantly relies on empirical testing. Li L. & Tao H.Y [22]. conducted an empirical analysis using data from enterprises listed on the Shenzhen Stock Exchange and found that equity checks and balances contribute positively to corporate innovation. They recommended that enterprises should emphasize the development of equity checks and balances structures during their growth phases. Gu L.L. et al. [23] analyzed data from information technology listed companies and concluded that firms at different developmental stages should adopt tailored equity structures to foster technological innovation. Zhu D.S. & Zhou X.P [24]. further highlighted that a higher degree of equity checks and balances is associated with greater innovation capability and efficiency. Their empirical study also indicated that equity checks and balances have a substitution effect on the shareholding of senior management and cautioned about the potential negative impact of excessive supervision under such structures on the firm’s innovation investment.

However, some studies have found that equity checks and balances can play a counterproductive role in business innovation performance. Wan H. et al. [25] conducted an empirical analysis of data from China's A-share listed companies and found that equity checks and balances can exert a moderating effect on the innovative activities of enterprises. The pathway of this effect is described as an "inverted U″ shape, meaning that as the degree of checks and balances increases, its influence first enhances and then diminishes innovation performance. Similarly, Guo Y.J. et al. [26] employed a three-stage Data Envelopment Analysis (DEA) model to empirically analyze the data of China's listed companies from 2008 to 2017. Their findings indicated that the degree of equity checks and balances has an inhibitory effect on the innovation capability of enterprises. These studies suggest that while equity checks and balances can be beneficial, excessive or improperly structured checks and balances may stifle innovation efforts.

In recent years, research on the relationship between equity checks and balances and innovation performance has continued to evolve. Zhang S.X. et al. [27] introduced an interaction term between equity structure and R&D expenditures in their empirical test of data from Growth Enterprise Market (GEM) technology firms. Their findings indicated that an increase in equity checks and balances is positively associated with firms' innovation investment. Yang L.Q. al [28]. found that the degree of equity checks and balances is positively related to firms' R&D investment when studying a sample of Small and Medium Enterprise (SME) and main board listed companies. They pointed out that excessive concentration of equity ownership may hinder the effectiveness of the national innovation system in fostering firms' R&D efforts. Li S. & Lin J. (2019) demonstrated that mutual checks and balances among shareholders can reduce agency costs, have a smoothing effect on corporate R&D investment, and effectively enhance corporate innovation capability. These studies collectively indicate that while equity checks and balances can positively influence R&D investment and innovation performance, the extent and manner of their implementation are critical factors.

R&D investment and business innovation performance

An enterprise's R&D investment is crucial for promoting independent innovation and enhancing core competitiveness. Extensive academic research supports the positive impact of R&D investment on business innovation performance. Branch [29] analyzed data from 111 U S. companies using a linkage model and found a positive correlation between R&D expenditures and innovation capability. Pilar Beneito [30], focusing on Spanish companies, discovered that R&D investment positively affects the innovation performance and sustainable development of enterprises. Ike C E, & Kingsley O [31]. studied 2650 U S. companies across the manufacturing and service industries and found that R&D investment enhances innovation performance. Dejan R, & Aleksander A [32]. conducted a comparative analysis of Slovenian and international R&D companies, constructing a multiple regression model that showed R&D expenditures had no significant short-term impact on innovation performance but had a positive long-term effect. Wu Z.Y. et al. [33] examined high-end equipment manufacturing listed companies in Northeast China and confirmed a significant positive impact of corporate R&D investment on innovation efficiency. Han J.Q. & Yu Y.Q [34]. used a double-difference model to assess the relationship between R&D investment and innovation performance in new energy automobile enterprises before and after the implementation of policy subsidies. Chen H.W. et al. [35] utilized provincial panel data to divide the high-tech industry into technology development and commercialization stages. They applied threshold regression methods to demonstrate that R&D investment enhances innovation performance in both stages, with a more pronounced impact during the commercialization phase. Xie L.H. et al. [36], against the backdrop of the innovation-driven development strategy, analyzed the differential impacts of fiscal and tax incentives on business innovation performance. They also introduced intermediary variables to explore the role of R&D investment, demonstrating that R&D investment partially mediates the direct effect of these incentives, with the intermediary role varying across different types of enterprises.

However, Cao Y. & Su F.J [37]. concluded that there are significant differences in how innovation inputs affect innovation performance across industries. Both R&D and non-R&D inputs have a significant impact on innovation performance, but not all high inputs necessarily translate into high outputs. Feng W.N [38]. conducted an empirical study that similarly found while R&D investment can promote business innovation performance, the relationship between investment in R&D personnel and innovation performance is not always significant.

These findings underscore the complexity in the relationship between innovation inputs and outcomes, highlighting that simply increasing investment does not guarantee enhanced innovation performance. Other factors, such as the quality of R&D personnel, management practices, and industry-specific characteristics, likely play crucial roles in translating input into effective innovation outputs.

Shareholding structure and R&D investment

Equity ownership concentration and R&D investment

The relationship between equity concentration and firms' R&D investment is one of the central topics in the field of corporate governance and innovation management. The existing literature is not uniform in this relationship, reflecting the fact that equity concentration may have a complex impact on R&D investment.

Most scholars believe that the relationship between equity ownership concentration and R&D investment can be either positive or negative, depending on the context. Jackie D V et al. [39] studied Canadian firms and found that a highly concentrated equity structure or the presence of controlling minority shareholders negatively affects the R&D intensity of Canadian manufacturing firms. Zhang Q.X. et al. [40] used mixed cross-sectional hierarchical regression with state-owned listed companies as a sample and concluded that ownership concentration significantly and negatively moderates the relationship between R&D investment and firm performance. Jiang T. & Zhang B.s [41]. determined that ownership concentration negatively moderates the relationship between corporate R&D investment and firm growth, based on data from New Third Board companies over the past five years. Wan W et al. [42] demonstrated that ownership concentration has a significant negative effect on corporate R&D intensity. Luo Y.H. & Li Y.F [43]. used fuzzy set qualitative comparative analysis and found that equity-financed asset-light firms are more conducive to higher R&D investment when the degree of ownership concentration is low.

Beyond the linear relationship, some scholars argue that the relationship between equity ownership concentration and R&D investment is non-linear. Liu S.Q. & Liu X [8]. found that there is a nonlinear N-type relationship between R&D investment and ownership concentration, where the effect of ownership concentration on firms' R&D intensity follows an N-shaped curve, with periods of positive promotion, negative inhibition, and subsequent positive promotion. Sun H. et al. [44] studied Growth Enterprise Market (GEM) listed companies and found an inverted U-type relationship between equity ownership concentration and R&D investment, indicating initial positive promotion followed by negative inhibition. They also examined the mediating role of enterprise value in this relationship.

Equity checks and balances and R&D investment

Equity checks and balances, an integral part of corporate governance, have a significant impact on corporate R&D investment. Empirical studies consistently affirm the positive role of equity checks and balances in fostering increased R&D investment. Such structures mitigate conflicts of interest among shareholders by constraining the actions of controlling shareholders, thereby aligning shareholders' focus towards the long-term development of the enterprise. This alignment typically places greater emphasis on the company's innovative activities and tends to boost R&D investment.

Porta R L, & Shleifer A [7]. advocate for an optimal equity structure involving multiple large shareholders to create an effective monitoring and check-and-balance mechanism, ensuring the stability of corporate R&D activities. Zheng J. & Xu M [45]. conducted a comparative study of state-owned and non-state-owned enterprises in the chemical and pharmaceutical industries, finding that increasing the holdings of non-controlling shareholders limits the power of majority shareholders, thereby enhancing R&D and innovation investment. Zheng Y. et al. [46] empirically verified that the degree of equity checks and balances is significantly and positively correlated with R&D investment among 281 companies listed on the Growth Enterprise Market (GEM). Wang W.Y. & Liu J [47]. employed Hansen's threshold model, focusing on family firms, to consider the distinct growth characteristics and the heterogeneous impact of checks and balances on R&D investment decisions within such firms.

Further empirical analysis reveals the nuanced and often complex relationship between equity checks and balances and R&D investment. Liu S.Q. & Liu X [8]. conducted a study using data from listed companies in China's manufacturing industry from 2002 to 2008. They found that R&D investment exhibits a U-shaped relationship with the increasing proportion of shares held by the first major shareholder. Under certain conditions, major shareholders can establish an effective constraint mechanism that restricts the behavior of the first major shareholder, which can be conducive to increasing R&D investment. Yuan H.L [48]. observed that the more pronounced the checks and balances are between shareholders, the less favorable they are for improving the enterprise's decision-making efficiency. The complex decision-making processes that result from such checks and balances can lead to missed opportunities, which in turn may adversely affect the enterprise's R&D performance. Bai Y.X. et al. [49] noted that when both the trench effect and interest convergence effect coexist, there is no clear relationship between equity checks and balances and R&D investment. The trench effect refers to a situation where shareholders form alliances to protect their interests, potentially leading to a deadlock in decision-making. Interest convergence occurs when the interests of various stakeholders align, which can sometimes lead to a lack of necessary oversight and conflict resolution.

The regulating role of market competition

The impact of market competition on business innovation performance has long been a topic of academic interest. Early scholars like Schumpeter (1942) suggested that larger enterprises in more monopolistic markets are more likely to invest in technological innovation. However, this view has been challenged by later researchers who argue that intense market competition can also drive firms to innovate more actively. Aidan R.V. & Anthony E.B [50]. contended that both equity structure and market competition can complementarily enhance firm performance and innovation. Aamir [51] discovered an inverted U-shaped relationship between market competition and innovation in U.S. manufacturing firms. To address potential endogeneity issues, Aamir used trade-weighted averages of industry exchange rates as an instrumental variable.

On the other hand, Farè L [52] found that for small and medium-sized European firms, innovation activity initially increases with competition but then decreases, with this negative effect being more pronounced in smaller firms. Laila S A H et al. [53] divided the relationship between market competition and innovation into three categories based on the level of a country’s industrial technology development. In countries with low industrial technology, market competition fosters innovation. In countries with moderate industrial technology, the relationship is more complex. In countries with advanced industrial technology, market competition might hinder innovation. Zhang J. et al. [54] showed that China's market competition is currently in the first half of the inverted U-shape, meaning that market competition is beneficial for improving enterprise innovation levels. Studies by Wang S.K. & Li J [55]. and Wang W. et al. [56] further indicate that product market competition promotes innovation, particularly exploratory innovation. Collectively, these studies suggest that market competition generally has a positive impact on business innovation performance, but this impact can be influenced by factors such as the type of firm, industry characteristics, and the level of national technological development.

Whether market competition moderates the relationship between equity structure and business innovation performance, and in which direction, remains a debated topic among academics. Jia K.W. et al. [9], drawing on transaction cost and principal-agent theories, found that in environments with insufficient market competition, equity ownership concentration helps firms access complementary innovation resources, thereby enhancing innovation performance. Conversely, Song Y.C. et al. [57] observed that increased external competitive pressure strengthens a firm's motivation to expand its market share and position, which in turn significantly boosts its innovation level. These differing perspectives highlight the complexity of the interplay between market dynamics, equity structure, and innovation performance.

Hypothesis and conceptual model

Equity ownership concentration and business innovation performance

Equity ownership concentration is a critical aspect of corporate governance and has a complex impact on business innovation performance. According to principal-agent theory, appropriate levels of ownership concentration help align the interests of majority and minority shareholders, fostering innovative behavior within the firm. When equity is concentrated to a reasonable extent, controlling shareholders tend to focus on the long-term development of the firm and are more likely to invest in innovation, potentially enhancing the business innovation performance. However, if ownership concentration becomes too high, controlling shareholders might pursue self-interested actions due to a lack of effective oversight, which can weaken the business innovation performance.

After reviewing the data on the relationship between ownership concentration and business innovation performance, scholars generally agree that ownership concentration can have both positive and negative linear effects on innovation performance. Comparisons between firms with concentrated and dispersed equity structures reveal that those with more concentrated equity often secure more R&D patents. For example, Yang F. & Li Q.Y [58]. found that increasing equity ownership concentration among Growth Enterprise Market (GEM) listed companies may lead to a reduction in R&D investment, ultimately diminishing innovation performance. Conversely, Wang C. & Hao H.Y [59]. reported a positive correlation between ownership concentration and innovation performance in private listed companies. In state-owned listed companies, Li X.Q. et al. [60] observed that higher ownership concentration correlates with lower innovation performance.

These findings indicate that the relationship between equity ownership concentration and business innovation performance is not straightforward and may exhibit a more complex, non-linear pattern. An increase in equity ownership concentration can initially enhance innovation performance, but once a certain threshold is surpassed, further concentration can have adverse effects. Thus, within a certain range, equity ownership concentration can boost innovation performance, but exceeding an optimal level may start to inhibit further innovation growth. Based on this literature review and theoretical analysis, this study proposes the following hypotheses.

There is an inverted U-shaped relationship between equity ownership concentration and firms' innovative performance.

Equity checks and balances and business innovation performance

Equity checks and balances are a fundamental component of corporate governance, designed to distribute control among multiple large shareholders. This distribution aims to mitigate the negative impacts that a single dominant shareholder might otherwise exert, such as the misuse of control. It is believed that these checks and balances foster better cooperation and coordination within the firm, reduce agency costs, and enhance the quality of decision-making, particularly in areas related to innovation.

Benjamin M. & Anete P [61]. highlighted that excessive concentration of power among majority shareholders can lead to the abuse of control, which in turn may crowd out R&D investment and negatively impact the firm's innovation efforts. Zhang Y.J. & Tang X.X [62]. used propensity score matching in their empirical analysis to compare private and state-owned enterprises. Their findings indicated that enhanced equity checks and balances play a significant role in promoting corporate innovation, although this effect is influenced by the nature of the firm's property rights.

These studies imply that equity checks and balances serve to protect the interests of smaller shareholders by preventing the concentration of power, reducing opportunistic behavior by management, and supporting the firm's innovative activities. In an environment characterized by robust checks and balances, mutual monitoring and cooperation among major shareholders can minimize conflicts and enhance the transparency and efficiency of decision-making, thereby creating a stable environment conducive to innovation. Based on these theoretical and empirical findings, this study proposes the following hypotheses.

Equity checks and balances have a significant positive effect on business innovation performance.

R&D investment and business innovation performance

R&D investment is a crucial driver of firms' innovation activities, encompassing two primary components: R&D personnel and R&D expenditure. Research indicates a positive correlation between R&D investment and business innovation performance, though the relationship is not always linear.

Regarding R&D expenditure, Luo J. & Liu Y.Y [63]. demonstrated that increased R&D spending significantly enhances business innovation performance, based on an analysis of companies listed in Shanghai and Shenzhen. Li J.Y. et al. [60] reinforced the positive impact of R&D investment on innovation performance using a fixed-effects model, exploring how firm ownership and industry characteristics moderate this relationship. Leng S [64]. confirmed the positive effect of R&D investment on the innovation performance of high-tech industries, focusing on instrument and equipment usage fees via data envelopment analysis and Bayesian vector autoregressive models. However, some studies present a contrasting view. Liu S.Q. et al. [65] identified an "acceleration trap" for corporate R&D investment, suggesting that beyond a certain threshold, increased R&D investment can adversely affect innovation performance. Similarly, Du W.Q. & Guo S.J [66]. argued that continuously rising R&D expenditures could negatively impact innovation performance due to resource redundancy after surpassing a threshold.

In terms of R&D personnel, Subramaniam K et al. [67] noted a positive relationship between labor costs and firms' technological innovation capabilities. Huo J.B [68]. emphasized that the input of R&D personnel is essential for driving innovation and significantly impacts the number of patents produced. Pei Z. & Luo S.G [69]. underscored the importance of communication and coordination among team members, asserting that this fosters a scale effect in human capital, further boosting innovation performance.

Synthesizing the existing literature, there is ongoing debate regarding the relationship between R&D investment and business innovation performance. Most studies, however, support the notion that investment in R&D personnel contributes positively to business innovation performance, while R&D expenditure investment tends to have a primarily positive but non-linear impact. Given the data of high-tech listed companies over the past decade, it is evident that R&D investment in Chinese high-tech listed companies remains relatively low compared to developed countries and has not yet reached the critical point of the non-linear relationship. Based on this analysis, this study proposes the following hypotheses.

R&D investment significantly enhances business innovation performance.

Equity structure, R&D investment and business innovation performance

Equity ownership concentration, R&D investment and business innovation performance

R&D investment is a critical indicator of a firm's capacity for independent innovation. Enterprises engaged in innovation activities require a steady flow of R&D investment, enabling them to develop high-tech products, expand market share, and strengthen their industry position. However, R&D investment carries significant risks, particularly given the substantial capital outlay and lengthy development cycles involved. If an R&D project fails to generate revenue, it can negatively impact the company's immediate operational income, leading management to adopt a more cautious approach to innovation investments. Business leaders, motivated by performance metrics during their tenure, may prefer strategies that yield quick results and higher short-term profits. R&D investment, often involving significant upfront costs and longer payoff periods, can conflict with these short-term goals, causing managers to be reluctant to commit to such endeavors. In cases where equity is highly concentrated, and innovation projects align with the long-term interests of shareholders, firms must implement regulatory and incentive systems to effectively oversee and motivate management, ensuring the successful execution of R&D projects and enhancing the business innovation performance. However, when shareholders are widely dispersed, it becomes challenging to enforce effective supervision and incentivization, potentially allowing "small and medium-sized shareholders" to exploit the situation, leading to a lack of initiative and motivation among major shareholders to monitor the day-to-day operations of the firm. Conversely, if equity is overly concentrated, particularly in the hands of a single major shareholder, it can lead to one-sided decision-making, as Zhu D.S. & Zhang F.F [70]. found that excessive ownership concentration may stifle corporate vitality and impede technological innovation performance.

When the equity is relatively concentrated, it can help balance the principal-agent dilemma between shareholders and firm operators, fostering a focus on long-term gains and encouraging greater R&D investment, which in turn improves innovation performance. Yet, if equity ownership concentration exceeds a certain threshold, it can hinder R&D investment and reduce the business innovation performance. Li S.J.'s (2014) research supports this notion, revealing an inverted "U" shaped relationship between the shareholding percentage of the largest shareholder and the intensity of R&D investment. These findings suggest that there is likely an optimal level of ownership concentration; too little or too much concentration can adversely affect business innovation performance.

Based on this analysis, this paper proposes the following hypotheses.

The link between R&D investment and equity ownership concentration is formed like an inverted U.

The link between equity ownership concentration and business innovation performance is mediated by R&D investment

Equity checks and balances, R&D investment and business innovation performance

Over-concentration of shareholding can inhibit a business innovation performance, whereas an appropriate degree of shareholding checks and balances can contribute to its enhancement. When there are equity checks and balances in place, all major shareholders are subject to the oversight of others. This is particularly relevant given that the current economic and legal systems in China are still developing. Such checks and balances can ensure that R&D investment decisions are thoroughly analyzed and substantiated, promoting more scientific and rational decision-making processes.

Mutual oversight among shareholders facilitates the inclusion of diverse viewpoints, encouraging collaborative decision-making. This, in turn, reduces the risk associated with R&D investment decisions, making the decision-making process more rational and promoting the progression of R&D activities, enabling R&D outcomes to be swiftly translated into tangible outputs. In brief, equity checks and balances promote collective decision-making, helping to rationalize R&D investment choices. This leads to the effective dispersion of R&D investment risks, reduces the uncertainty surrounding R&D activities, and ensures their smooth implementation.

Therefore, the following hypotheses are proposed.

Equity checks and balances significantly increase firms' R&D investment.

Investment in R&D acts as a mediator between equity checks and balances and business innovation performance.

Shareholding structure, market competition and business innovation performance

The influence of equity structure on business innovation performance is inevitably impacted by the external economic market environment, with the degree of market competition serving as a key indicator of this environment. Intense market competition poses greater risks to firms, including the potential for bankruptcy and liquidation. This heightened risk drives firms to optimize their shareholding structures, improve corporate governance, and strengthen oversight of management. These actions can effectively mitigate the principal-agent problem, enhance the efficient use of capital, and enable firms to boost their innovation performance as a means of surviving competitive pressures.

From the perspective of information asymmetry theory, intense competition facilitates easier access to information about competitors, reducing information asymmetry and limiting opportunistic behavior by managers. This, in turn, curbs self-serving actions by management and enhances the level of innovation output. Considering the relationship between the degree of equity checks and balances and innovation performance, intense market competition exerts pressure on controlling shareholders. High competition increases market risk, which directly impacts the interests of majority shareholders. To prevent adverse outcomes, controlling shareholders may improve internal management mechanisms and cede some control to other shareholders, thereby enhancing the degree of equity checks and balances. This realignment of interests encourages controlling shareholders and smaller shareholders to work towards a common goal, sharing risks and striving to enhance innovation capabilities, thereby improving the firm’s competitive edge.

In summary, intense market competition can refine the equity structure, enhance corporate governance, and to some extent, curb the self-interested behaviors of controlling shareholders and managers. It optimizes information dissemination, alleviates principal-agent problems and information asymmetry, and steadily elevates the business innovation performance. Therefore, this paper posits the following hypotheses.

The association between equity ownership concentration and business innovation performance is moderated by market competition.

Between business innovation performance and equity checks and balances, market competition acts as a moderating factor.

R&D investment acts as a mediating variable and affects business innovation performance. Market competition moderates the effects of equity ownership concentration and equity checks and balances on R&D investment, as well as the effects of R&D investment on business innovation performance.

Data and research methodology

Data source and description

Research data in this paper are collected from high-tech companies listed on the A-share market between 2012 and 2021, using classification techniques aligned with the classification standard of industries introduced by the China Securities Regulatory Commission in 2012 and the classification methods applied by researchers such as Li, W.X., & Liu, Y. [71], Gu Y. [72], and Yao, K., & Wang, Y. J. [73]. After excluding ST and ∗ST listed companies, and excluding the samples with gearing ratio greater than 1 and the samples with missing data, we finally obtain the data of 2223 high-tech listed companies, with a total of 13,903 pieces of sample data. The financial data of the enterprises are obtained from Cathay Pacific database (CSMAR) database. In order to exclude the effect of extreme values, we apply a 1 % upward and downward shrinkage to the continuous variables. These steps are intended to ensure the authenticity and objectivity of the data to reflect the statistical results. The statistical and testing software used in this paper is stata17.0.

Referring to the research of Li, C.G., & Qiu, Z.J [74]. and Wen Y.Y. et al. [75], in this study, the number of patents authorized in a firm-year is used to indicate innovation performance. Since the quantity of patents that particular businesses have awarded in some years is zero, the variable of all the authorized patents is processed by adding 1, and then taking the logarithm of the variable to ensure the scientificity and accuracy, as commonly adopted by the majority of scholars. Equity ownership concentration indicates the degree of concentration or dispersion of a company's equity, according to the largest shareholder's percentage of shares. Following the approach outlined by Wu, Q.S., & Fan, S.S [76]. and other researchers, comparing the total amount of shares owned by the second, fifth, and largest owners to the largest shareholder's shareholdings yields information about the extent of equity checks and balances. C. et al. [78], etc., we analyze the firm's investment in Research and Development (R&D) by calculating the R&D expenses ratio to the company's operating revenues for the current year. Robustness test

To perform robustness test analysis, this article employs the number of applications for patents made by businesses in the present year as a proxy variable for the dependent variable. Given that certain years see no patent applications from any particular business, the number of all applied patents is processed by adding 1 and then taking the logarithm of the number of applications to ensure scientific validity and accuracy, as is adopted by most scholars.

Due to the length of this paper, there is no display of the robustness test results. After replacing the explanatory variables, the pertinent hypotheses of this work are also supported by the regression results of the direct, mediating, and moderating effects between each variable and the prior article. Therefore, the findings of this paper are robust.

Conclusion and implications

Conclusion

Based on the theoretical support of innovation theory, modern market competition theory and other theories, this study adopts a combination of theoretical research and empirical analysis to investigate the impact of two dimensions of equity structure - equity ownershipconcentration and equity checks and balances-on business innovation performance. In addition, this study incorporates R&D investment into the analytical model to verify its mediating role between equity structure and business innovation performance. Meanwhile, considering the influence of external economic market environment, this study further includes market competition as a moderating variable in the model to test its moderating effect on the aforementioned relationship. Finally, this study conducted a robustness test with the number of corporate patent applications as an explanatory variable.

Using the unbalanced panel data of high-tech listed companies from 2012 to 2021, this study collected a total of 13,903 data from 2223 high-tech listed companies and used Stata 15 software to conduct empirical analysis. The results of the analysis lead to the following conclusions:

Firstly, there is an inverted U-shaped relationship between equity ownership concentration and business innovation performance, which is first promoted and then suppressed, and business innovation performance shows a tendency of first increasing and then decreasing with the increase of equity ownership concentration. When the enterprise equity ownership concentration is higher, based on the rational man hypothesis, the major shareholders of the enterprise will be more active and proactive in understanding the enterprise operation and development. And take timely and effective actions to supervise and manage the management and improve the innovative performance of the enterprise. After the equity ownership concentration reaches a certain degree, even concentrated in the hands of a certain major shareholder, the major shareholders absolutely control the enterprise, the lack of supervision, making the enterprise risky, which will have a negative impact on the innovation performance of the enterprise.

Secondly, the degree of equity checks and balances has a significant positive impact on business innovation performance. Conflicts of interest between corporate management and shareholders as well as shareholders can be effectively resolved by means of equity checks and balances, which reduces the opportunistic behavior of the company, better coordinates the conflicts of interest of the various parties involved in the company's innovative behavior, and makes the company's innovative development as the goal of all stakeholders.

Thirdly, R&D investment plays an intermediary role in the relationship between equity ownership concentration and business innovation performance. R&D investment has a significant positive impact on business innovation performance, and if equity ownership concentration is high, it can, to a certain extent, solve the principal-agent problem between shareholders and enterprise operators, pay more attention to the long-term returns of the enterprise, increase R&D investment, and thus enhance business innovation performance. However, if equity ownership concentration exceeds a specific threshold, stakeholders' constraints and supervision of controlling shareholders are weakened, and self-interested behaviors are more likely to occur to reduce R&D investment, thus hindering the enhancement of business innovation performance. R&D investment plays an intermediary role in the process of the significant positive impact of equity checks and balances on business innovation performance. With the enhancement of equity checks and balances, collective decision-making can be realized to a certain extent, which makes the argumentation of R&D investment more scientific and reasonable, thus dispersing the risk of R&D investment, increasing R&D investment and enhancing the innovation performance of the enterprise.

Lastly, Market competition plays a moderating role in the relationship between equity ownership concentration and business innovation performance, and an enhancement effect occurs on the original inverted U-shaped curve, making the original inverted U-shaped curve steeper. Market competition can increase the level of the inverted U-shaped curve between equity ownership concentration and business innovation performance. When the enterprise is in the market environment of the existence of greater competitive pressure, in order to maximize the avoidance of fierce market competition brought about by the risk, the enterprise will optimize the shareholding structure, improve the corporate governance mechanism, improve the enterprise internal control system to strengthen the supervision of the operator, to alleviate the first type of agency problems, and strive to break through the key core technology to enhance the ability of independent innovation, enhance the innovation performance of the enterprise. The degree of market competition does not have an obvious moderating effect in the process of equity checks and balances degree and business innovation performance. The reason may lie in the fact that no matter how the market competition environment changes, it will not affect the size of each shareholder's control over the enterprise, and how the degree of market competition is intense has less impact on the mutual constraint mechanism among shareholders.

Implications

High-tech enterprises are fundamentally intertwined with innovation. As the number and market capitalization of companies in sectors such as software and information technology services, biomedicine, new energy, and new materials continue to grow, it underscores the increasing importance of core technologies in corporate development. Only through autonomous innovation can enterprises achieve sustainable growth. The self-innovation capability has become a pivotal metric for the long-term development of enterprises, especially high-tech ones. This study elucidates the intricate influence mechanism of equity structure on business innovation performance and provides strategic guidance for high-tech listed companies to optimize their equity structures and enhance their innovation performance. Moreover, the findings offer a theoretical foundation for policymakers to craft relevant policies to foster corporate innovation.

1Optimize Equity Structure and Improve Equity Checks and Balances: Research has demonstrated an inverted U-shaped relationship between equity ownership concentration and business innovation performance, indicating that moderate levels of equity ownership concentration can spur innovation, while excessive concentration might stifle it due to a lack of effective oversight. Consequently, enterprises should adapt their shareholding structures according to their developmental stages and market positions to achieve a balanced distribution of ownership. For enterprises with overly concentrated equity, it is advisable to dilute the control of a single major shareholder and enhance the involvement of smaller shareholders through the introduction of strategic investors and the implementation of equity incentive plans. This approach can improve corporate governance effectiveness and innovation performance. Additionally, enterprises should strengthen internal governance to ensure the transparency and fairness of decision-making processes, safeguarding the interests of all shareholders.

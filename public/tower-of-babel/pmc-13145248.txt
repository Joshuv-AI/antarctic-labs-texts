Intraoperative Analysis of Collateral Ligament Elongation Patterns by Coronal Plane Alignment of the Knee Classification Using Augmented Reality Navigation in Japanese Osteoarthritic Knees
Authors: Atsushi Sato, Marika Mukunoki, Masataka Ota, Kanako Izukashi, Takayuki Okumo, Takayuki Koya
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundElongation, defined as the 3-dimensional distance between ligament attachment sites, is a useful metric for evaluating ligament function. However, intraoperative assessment of elongation patterns in osteoarthritic (OA) knees during total knee arthroplasty (TKA) remains limited.PurposeTo analyze the intraoperative elongation patterns of the medial and lateral collateral ligaments (MCL and LCL) in OA knees using augmented reality (AR) navigation, according to the Coronal Plane Alignment of the Knee classification.MethodsA total of 100 OA knees undergoing TKA with AR-based navigation were evaluated. Real-time 3-dimensional elongation of the MCL and LCL was recorded throughout the full range of motion. A ≥10% change in ligament length was defined as significant. Elongation patterns were analyzed by Coronal Plane Alignment of the Knee type.ResultsThe MCL remained relatively constant during knee flexion, whereas the LCL shortened. Normal elongation patterns (stable MCL with LCL shortening) were observed in 83.3% of type I, 79.2% of type II, 75.0% of type III, and 100% of type IV and V knees. However, atypical patterns were also noted in some type I and II knees.ConclusionsMost OA knees demonstrated ligament elongation patterns similar to those of healthy knees. AR navigation enabled real-time, quantitative 3-dimensional assessment of ligament dynamics and may support individualized intraoperative soft tissue evaluation during TKA.

Introduction

Elongation, defined as the distance between ligament attachment sites, serves as a quantitative indicator of ligament function in the human knee. In vivo assessments using imaging-based techniques have enabled the analysis of elongation behavior during dynamic activities. In healthy knees, the medial collateral ligament (MCL) tends to maintain a nearly constant length throughout flexion, whereas the lateral collateral ligament (LCL) typically shortens [1]. Biomechanical investigations have further demonstrated region-specific elongation patterns of MCL and LCL fibers depending on knee flexion angle and loading conditions [1]. In post-total knee arthroplasty (TKA) knees, similar length-change patterns have been reported, with the MCL generally remaining stable and the LCL progressively shortening during flexion [2,3].Despite these insights, elongation behavior in osteoarthritic (OA) knees remains underexplored. Especially in the intraoperative setting during TKA, the use of elongation data as a surrogate for ligament function is not yet fully established. The Coronal Plane Alignment of the Knee (CPAK) classification offers a framework for understanding alignment morphology, which may influence soft tissue behavior [4]. Recently, Ophoff et al. analyzed collateral ligament length-change patterns in native (nonarthritic) knees using computer-assisted navigation, reporting that the MCL tightens in varus and neutral phenotypes, whereas the LCL consistently loosens during flexion across all CPAK types [5]. These findings highlight CPAK-dependent ligament behavior in healthy knees.

However, the elongation characteristics of OA knees, particularly in Asian populations where varus phenotypes predominate, remain unclear.

Therefore, this study aimed to investigate intraoperative elongation patterns of the MCL and LCL in Japanese patients with OA knees, using augmented reality (AR) navigation and the CPAK classification to explore potential deviations from native patterns.

Material and methods

This retrospective study included 100 consecutive knees from patients with radiographically confirmed osteoarthritis who underwent primary TKA at our institution between October 1, 2022, and September 30, 2024. All procedures were performed by experienced orthopaedic surgeons using an AR-based navigation system (NextAR, Medacta, Switzerland) (Fig. 1) [6]. This study was approved by the institutional review board of Showa Medical University (IRB No. 18T7040), and all patients provided informed consent. Preoperative coronal alignment was automatically assessed using whole-leg CT with the MyKnee software (Medacta, Switzerland), and patients were classified into CPAK types I–Ⅸ based on femoral and tibial mechanical alignment and joint line obliquity, according to the method proposed by MacDessi et al [4]. During surgery, the femoral and tibial AR trackers were securely mounted to the diaphyseal shafts. After registration and acquisition of anatomic landmarks, the AR system enabled dynamic tracking of knee flexion while maintaining the patella in a reduced position. Attachment sites of the MCL and LCL were defined based on a previously described CT-based anatomical estimation method, which enables 3-dimensional identification of femoral and tibial (or fibular) insertion landmarks [7]. Ligament length was calculated as the 3-dimensional Euclidean distance between the registered femoral origin and tibial (or fibular) insertion points at each flexion angle. This approach reflects true spatial distance without assuming a fixed planar relationship of the attachment sites during knee motion.

In addition, the navigation system simultaneously quantified tibial rotation relative to the femur across the full range of motion, which was displayed in real time on the monitor. Although rotational parameters were recorded intraoperatively, they were not included as primary endpoints in the present analysis.

The knee in full extension (defined as 0°-10° of flexion in 3D space) was set as the zero reference position. Ligament elongation was measured continuously at every 10° of flexion, from full extension to maximum intraoperative flexion (typically 120°-130°). A change of ≥10% in ligament length compared to baseline was defined as significant elongation or shortening. Patients were categorized as exhibiting “normal” elongation patterns if the MCL remained within ±10% change and the LCL showed a ≥10% decrease during flexion. Patterns deviating from this were labeled “abnormal.” All measurements were performed by a single operator to ensure consistency, and raw data were exported from the AR platform for analysis. Descriptive statistics were used to summarize elongation patterns by CPAK type.

Results

Of the 100 knees evaluated, the majority demonstrated elongation patterns consistent with those reported in healthy knees [1]. Specifically, the MCL maintained a near-constant length throughout flexion in 85% of cases, while the LCL shortened progressively with increasing flexion in 92% of cases. The average change in MCL length across the flexion arc was +1.8% ± 4.5%, while the LCL showed an average reduction of −12.4% ± 6.1%. These values support previously reported physiological patterns. However, deviations from this norm were observed in a subset of patients, suggesting potential interindividual variability. Among the “abnormal” patterns observed in type I and II knees, some demonstrated MCL shortening or LCL elongation during flexion. These findings suggest that knees with varus alignment and low joint line obliquity may be more susceptible to altered soft tissue mechanics. No intraoperative complications related to the AR system or elongation measurement were noted. The procedure added approximately 4-5 minutes to the total operative time, with no negative impact on workflow or exposure.

Discussion

This study evaluated intraoperative elongation patterns of the MCL and LCL in OA knees during TKA using AR navigation and analyzed these patterns by CPAK classification. The CPAK system, introduced by MacDessi et al. [4], has been shown to correlate with patient-reported outcomes and alignment-related satisfaction following TKA. Recently, Ophoff et al. [5] analyzed collateral ligament length-change patterns in native knees stratified by CPAK type and demonstrated a phenotype-dependent yet overall consistent pattern of relative MCL stability and progressive LCL shortening across most CPAK types.

Elongation behavior of the collateral ligaments has been well described in healthy knees. In vivo imaging studies have demonstrated that the MCL maintains a relatively constant length throughout flexion, while the LCL progressively shortens [1]. Similar length-change patterns have also been reported in post-TKA knees [2,3]. However, ligament elongation characteristics in OA knees remain poorly understood, particularly in Asian populations where varus phenotypes predominate.

In the present study, most OA knees demonstrated a pattern comparable to that reported in native and post-TKA knees, with relative MCL stability and LCL shortening during flexion. However, deviations from this pattern were observed, particularly in CPAK types I and II. These findings suggest that chronic varus alignment and soft tissue adaptation in OA knees may influence ligament behavior differently from native biomechanics.

From a biomechanical perspective, the medial pivot mechanism of the knee provides an explanation for the observed LCL shortening. During knee flexion, the medial compartment functions as a relatively stable pivot, while the lateral femoral condyle translates posteriorly in association with tibial internal rotation, resulting in relative approximation of the LCL attachment sites [8]. This coupled motion of tibial internal rotation with asymmetric femoral translation represents a key feature of physiological medial pivot kinematics. Because ligament length in this study was calculated as the 3-dimensional Euclidean distance between CT-defined femoral and fibular insertion points [7], the observed shortening of the LCL reflects true spatial changes rather than a 2-dimensional projection artifact.

Importantly, the AR-based navigation system used in this study enabled simultaneous real-time quantification of tibial rotation relative to the femur throughout the flexion arc. Although rotational data were not included as primary endpoints, this capability confirms that ligament elongation measurements were obtained under dynamic 3-dimensional kinematic tracking. Previous studies have demonstrated the feasibility and spatial accuracy of AR-assisted navigation systems in TKA [6,9,10], supporting the validity of our measurement methodology.

The higher proportion of “abnormal” elongation patterns observed in CPAK types I and II may reflect population-specific morphology. Japanese and other Asian cohorts have been shown to exhibit a higher prevalence of varus CPAK phenotypes compared with Western populations [11,12]. These morphologic characteristics may result in long-standing medial compartment overload and lateral soft tissue adaptation, potentially altering ligament length-change behavior during flexion.

Clinically, intraoperative 3-dimensional assessment of ligament elongation offers a novel method for evaluating soft tissue balance beyond traditional gap measurements. By integrating CT-based attachment modeling [7] with AR-guided dynamic tracking [6,9,10], surgeons may obtain objective information regarding both ligament length and tibial rotation. This approach has the potential to support more individualized soft tissue balancing strategies and phenotype-specific surgical planning in TKA.

This study has several limitations. First, elongation measurements were obtained under non–weight-bearing intraoperative conditions, which may not fully reproduce physiologic loading. Second, rotational data were recorded but not quantitatively analyzed as study endpoints. Third, the cohort consisted exclusively of Japanese OA patients, which may limit generalizability to other populations.

Nevertheless, this study provides novel intraoperative 3-dimensional data on collateral ligament elongation patterns in OA knees stratified by CPAK classification. The findings suggest that, while fundamental biomechanical trends are preserved, degenerative alignment morphology may introduce phenotype-specific adaptations in ligament behavior.

Conclusions

Intraoperative assessment of MCL and LCL elongation patterns during TKA in OA knees revealed that most cases demonstrated behavior similar to that of healthy knees, with the MCL maintaining a constant length and the LCL shortening during flexion. However, variability was observed, particularly in CPAK types I and II, suggesting individual differences in ligament behavior. AR navigation enables objective and quantitative intraoperative evaluation of ligament dynamics, offering a promising tool for optimizing soft tissue balance and advancing phenotype-specific surgical strategies in TKA.

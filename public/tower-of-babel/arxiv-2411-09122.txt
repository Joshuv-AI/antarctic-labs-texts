1.
Introduction

Bott periodicity is a central result of topology which has been
foundational for a number of later developments, including K-theory.
In this paper, we prove a simple (in many senses) algebro-geometric version of Bott
periodicity (for
U(n)

This argument is (in multiple ways) inspired by and based on work of Segal, Kirwan, Cohen, Mitchell,
Lupercio, and other topologists (see in particular

Traditionally, Bott periodicity is presented as related to complex geometry, but the argument here suggests
that it is a more fundamental statement, which works over the integers and is purely algebraic, from which the
complex manifestation is obtained by specializing to
\mathbb{C}
. This is of course slightly
misleading. To get from the algebraic statement to the topological statement, some topological or holomorphic input is needed
in order to even make contact with the language of the traditional statement; but this connection just involves standard tools and methods (see §

).

One goal, to be hopefully used in future work (such as
[

]
), is to create a simple but versatile setting in which
homological “limiting” arguments can be made in algebraic geometry — a setting differently flexible than stacks
where many of the standard tools (cohomology, Chow rings, Grothendieck rings) can apply. The specialization to motivic homotopy theory (in Appendix

by B. Church) is less trivial; see §

For algebraic geometers: the idea behind Bott periodicity is that
there is a natural homotopy equivalence

\beta:BU\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\Omega^{2}_{d}U

1.1.

Desiderata.

In the process of making this precise, we describe
a category
Ho\mathscr{G}
(some sort of naive “homotopy category of geometric spaces”) that is a natural home for earlier stabilization results in
algebraic geometry. Our desiderata are as follows. Notice that most
are satisfied by finite type algebraic stacks with affine diagonal.

•

(over
\mathbb{Z}
)
The spaces should be “defined over
\mathbb{Z}
”,
and “base change well”.

•

(complex realization)
Upon specializing to
\mathbb{C}
, we should be able to analytify to obtain an appropriate
complex analytic notion.

•

(homotopy axiom)
We want “vector bundle morphisms” (morphisms
\pi:E\rightarrow X
where
\pi
expresses
E
as a vector bundle over
X
) and more generally affine bundle morphisms
to be isomorphisms.

•

(topological realization)
The analytic object should have a homotopy type (and in
particular, cohomology and homotopy groups).

•

(cohomology groups with structure)
Different types of cohomology groups,
with various structures (Galois structure, Hodge structure) should be defined. (Because of the homotopy axiom,
Tate twists will have to be trivial, however.)

•

(Chow realization)
The spaces should have Chow groups and Chow rings.

•

(Grothendieck ring realization)
The spaces should
have a class in the (completed
localized) Grothendieck ring of varieties.

•

(motivic spaces)
Over a perfect field, there should be a functor to the homotopy category of motivic spaces.

The reader is welcome to work out how other potential desiderata
are also obtained by this construction (étale cohomology, étale homotopy type, etc.).

We make no claim about our category being the only setting (or
even the “right setting”) for these arguments. If
anything,
we emphasize that the arguments are robust
(or simple-minded) enough that we don’t need much care in
choosing the setting for them.
In particular, the spaces relevant
for Bott periodicity are smooth, but one may want to allow singularities
of various sorts. See §
4
for brief discussion of potential variations.

1.2.

Main result.

Bott periodicity is about isomorphisms of homotopy types, so we work in a robust and simple category
Ho\mathscr{G}
which extends this notion to an algebraic setting: there is a functor to the category of homotopy types.
We will define
\Omega^{2}_{\text{alg},d}(BGL(r))
(§
6
) as, roughly, the moduli space of rank

r
degree
d
vector bundles on
\mathbb P^{1}
, trivialized at the
point
\infty\in\mathbb P^{1}
. Its “homotopy type” over
\mathbb{C}
is indeed
\Omega^{2}_{d}(BGL(r))
.
(
\Omega^{2}_{\text{alg},0}(BGL(r))
is “essentially” the affine Grassmannian for

\Omega^{2}_{\text{alg},d}(BGL(r))\cong\Omega^{2}_{\text{alg},d^{\prime}}(BGL(r))
for all

(see §
5
). We have a map
\Omega^{2}_{\text{alg},d}(BGL(r))\rightarrow\Omega^{2}_{\text{alg},d}(BGL(r+1))

obtained by sending the vector bundle
{\mathscr{E}}
to

{\mathscr{E}}\oplus{\mathscr{O}}
.
BGL
and
\Omega^{2}_{\text{alg},d}(BGL)
similarly will be elements of
Ho\mathscr{G}
directly extending the corresponding notions in topology (see §
3
and §
3
).

1.3.

Theorem (Bott periodicity, informal statement). —

We have an isomorphism

\Omega^{2}_{\text{alg},d}(BGL)\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}BGL

in
Ho\mathscr{G}
. Upon specializing to
\mathbb{C}
,
analytifying, and taking homotopy type, this specializes
to the traditional Bott periodicity isomorphism. Over a perfect field, this induces an isomorphism of homotopy types of motivic spaces, which extends Bott perodicity in motivic spectra.

In particular, Theorem
1
gives (yet) another proof of the classical statement of Bott periodicity.
For precise statements and proofs, see Theorem

for the first part,
Theorem
7

This approach
has additional consequences in fixed rank

The map from (the homotopy type of)

1.6.

Standing assumptions.
We work over an arbitrary base
B
. (The cases of most of interest are presumably when

B
is the spectrum of a field or
\mathbb{Z}

B

We use the language of locally free sheaves and vector

By the category of topological spaces

Appendix A
Compatibility with motivic homotopy theory

by Benjamin Church

A.1.

Introduction to the appendix.
The work of Voevodsky, Morel, and many others have offered a convincing answer to the question of where to situate homotopy-theoretic manipulations of algebro-geometric objects. The categories of motivic spaces and motivic spectra inherit many of the desirable
\infty
-categorical properties of spaces and spectra while allowing for arguments over arbitrary characteristic and maintaining motivic information such as Hodge structures that are lost when passing to underlying topological manifolds. The construction of a Bott map using purely algebro-geometric constructions on classifying stacks has exactly the flavor an argument motivic spaces ought to support. However, it is not immediately clear that certain construction recover homotopy-theoretic notions such as loop spaces and highly connective maps. Larson and Vakil give a list of desiderata necessary for a category to support their construction of the Bott map (§
4
). They produce some sort of “free” categories
{\mathscr{G}}
and
{\mathscr{G}}_{\text{sp}}
initial for those satisfying the desiderata. We show that motivic spaces can support Larson and Vakil’s construction by exhibiting a functor from their free homotopy category to the homotopy category of motivic spaces that lifts to a functor of
\infty
-categories.

One difficulty is that
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-connectivity is much more stringent than topological connectivity, so care is necessary in dealing with arguments relying on highly connected maps. Perhaps surprisingly, the “obvious” functor does not work – because inclusions of an open subset with large complementary codimension
may not be highly
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-connected. However, this can be remedied by a partial
S^{1}
-stabilization (see §3.1 and Theorem
A
) in order for Larson and Vakil’s class of weak homotopy equivalences to actually be motivic equivalences. This partially stabilized functor agrees with the naive functor after fully stabilizing,
i.e.
, mapping to the category of
S^{1}
-motivic spectra. This compatibility establishes a new result native to motivic homotopy theory, namely an analogue of the main result of
[
CoLupSe
]
for algebraic
K
-theory that, put imprecisely, the space of algebraic maps from
\mathbb{P}^{1}
to
\mathrm{BGL}
converges to the motivic
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-loop space of
\mathrm{BGL}
(see §
A
). The arguments given here closely follow prior developments in the motivic homotopy theory literature.

because CW-complexes are compact objects of

∎

A.17.

Corollary. —

To check if a (pointed) object

n

Proof.

Indeed, the morphism of sheaves

A.18.

Morel’s Connectivity Theorem.

As in
[
Mo2
]
, we let
B=\operatorname{Spec}{k}
where
k
is a perfect field throughout this section.

We have noted that if
\mathcal{X}
is already motivic then its
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-homotopy sheaves agree with the homotopy sheaves computed viewing
\mathcal{X}
as a mere presheaf. However, if we start with an arbitrary presheaf
\mathcal{X}
there is no reason its homotopy sheaves should agree with those of
L_{\mathrm{mot}}\mathcal{X}
(which are, by definition,
\pi_{i}^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}(\mathcal{X})
). Morel’s
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-Connectivity Theorem tells us that, although nonzero homotopy sheaves may not be preserved by motivic localization, connectivity is preserved.

A.19.

Morel’s
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-Connectivity Theorem
[

, Theorem 6.38]
. —

Let
\mathcal{X}\in\mathrm{PSh}(\mathrm{Sm}_{k})_{*}
be a pointed presheaf and
n\geq 0
an integer. If
\mathcal{X}
is
n
-connected then it is
\mathbb{A}^{1}\text{-}n\text{-connected}
, meaning for all

,

\pi_{i}^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}(\mathcal{X})=\pi_{i}(L_{\mathrm{mot}}(\mathcal{X}))=0.

In the corresponding portion of
[

]
, this result is stated for the

A.20.

Purity.

In algebraic geometry and motivic homotopy theory, we do not have access to tubular neighborhoods. The replacement of this is the purity theorem (Theorem
A
), which relates the cofiber of an open immersion
j:U\hookrightarrow X
with smooth complement to the normal bundle of its complement.

A.21.

Remark.

In the sequel,
given a morphism of presheaves

.

A.27.

Larson and Vakil’s Categories of Spaces.

First we recall the categories of spaces defined in the main text. Recall (§

(SmArt)
is the

{\mathscr{G}}_{\text{sp}}
and

were defined in §

.
There is an obvious functor
{\mathscr{G}}_{\text{sp}}\to{\mathscr{G}}
. Note that this functor is full but not faithful.

A.28.

Incarnation in Motivic Spaces.

There is an evident functor

\text{(SmArt)}\to\mathrm{PSh}(\mathrm{Sm}_{B})

sending an Artin stack to its functor of points restricted to schemes smooth over
B
. Explicitly, a stack is a sheaf of
1
-groupoids so it can be viewed as a
1
-truncated sheaf of spaces hence an object in the
\infty
-category
\mathrm{PSh}(\mathrm{Sm}_{B})
. One must check that the
2
-categorical structure on
(SmArt)
is compatible with the higher categorical structure on
\mathrm{PSh}(\mathrm{Sm}_{B})
. See
[
CDH
, Rem. 2.1]
for additional details. Composing with the functor
L_{\mathrm{mot}}:\mathrm{PSh}(\mathrm{Sm}_{B})\to\mathrm{Spc}(B)
adjoint to the inclusion of the reflexive subcategory of motivic spaces, we get a motivic realization functor

M_{\text{(SmArt)}}:\text{(SmArt)}\to\mathrm{Spc}(B).

This gives an evident way to realize the motivic space associated to a sequence

M_{{\mathscr{G}}}:{\mathscr{G}}\to\mathrm{Spc}(B)\quad\quad M_{{\mathscr{G}}}:X_{\bullet}\mapsto\operatorname{colim}\limits_{n}M_{\text{(SmArt)}}X_{n}

where we take the homotopy colimit over the diagram defined by
M(X_{\bullet})
. The same definition produces two functors
M_{{\mathscr{G}}_{\text{sp}}}^{t},M_{{\mathscr{G}}_{\text{sp}}}^{b}:{\mathscr{G}}_{\text{sp}}\to\mathrm{Spc}(B)
(
t
for top,
b
for bottom) defined by

M_{{\mathscr{G}}_{\text{sp}}}^{t}:(Y_{\bullet}\to X_{\bullet})\mapsto\operatorname{colim}\limits_{n}M_{\text{(SmArt)}}Y_{\bullet}\quad\quad M_{{\mathscr{G}}_{\text{sp}}}^{b}:(Y_{\bullet}\to X_{\bullet})\mapsto\operatorname{colim}\limits_{n}M_{\text{(SmArt)}}X_{\bullet}.

The diagrams
(Y_{\bullet}\to X_{\bullet})
induce an obvious
1
-morphism
M^{t}_{{\mathscr{G}}_{\text{sp}}}\to M^{b}_{{\mathscr{G}}_{\text{sp}}}
in
\mathrm{Fun}\left({\mathscr{G}}_{\text{sp}},\mathrm{Spc}(B)\right)
. Furthermore,
M_{{\mathscr{G}}_{\text{sp}}}^{b}
obviously agrees with the composition

{\mathscr{G}}_{\text{sp}}\to{\mathscr{G}}\xrightarrow{M_{{\mathscr{G}}}}\mathrm{Spc}(B).

We will use – in the subsequent discussion – the evident pointed variants of these realization functors where a point of
X_{\bullet}\in{\mathscr{G}}
(resp.
(Y_{\bullet}\to X_{\bullet})\in G_{\mathrm{sp}}
) is a morphism
*\to X_{\bullet}
(resp.
(*\to*)\to(Y_{\bullet}\to X_{\bullet})
) where
*
(resp.
(*\to*
)) is the constant sequence
*_{n}=B
(resp.
B\to B
).

The difficulty arises when analyzing the comparison map
M_{{\mathscr{G}}_{\text{sp}}}^{t}\to M_{{\mathscr{G}}_{\text{sp}}}^{b}
. We want this to be an equivalence. Indeed, since any realization of our goal to “interpret the construction” inside
\mathrm{Spc}(B)
will necessarily construct a functor of homotopy theories
\mathrm{Ho}{\mathscr{G}}\to\mathrm{Ho}\mathrm{Spc}(B)
it is necessary for
M^{t}_{{\mathscr{G}}_{\text{sp}}}\to M^{b}_{{\mathscr{G}}_{\text{sp}}}
to be an equivalence, since
Y_{\bullet}\to X_{\bullet}
becomes an isomorphism in the homotopy category of
{\mathscr{G}}
. But this is unfortunately false.

The difficulty is that inclusions of an open subset with large complementary codimension may not be highly connected in the category of motivic spaces.

A.29.

Example.

Let
X
be a variety whose associated sheaf on
\mathrm{Sm}_{B}
is already
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-local (for example, an abelian variety). Let

be the complement of a point. This has complementary codimension

. However,

is not even

. Indeed,

. However,

is

A.30. We are about to remedy this problem in §
A
, at the cost of some information, but with the advantage that the functor applies to the entire categories
{\mathscr{G}}_{\text{sp}}
and
{\mathscr{G}}
.
But for the purposes of Bott periodicity (and the study of classifying spaces such as

) we can instead focus on a subcategory
{\mathscr{G}}_{\text{sp}}^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}
of

on which all of the desiderata of §

).

A.31. The remedy will be straightforward, if perhaps a little surprising:
use the functor

\Sigma^{2}M_{{\mathscr{G}}}
instead of

M_{{\mathscr{G}}}
.
After a double suspension, an open immersion with large codimension boundary will become highly connected. In particular, this means if we “stabilize all the way to infinity” and realize all the objects as motivic spectra our desiderata will be achieved. However, it is somewhat remarkable that uniformly only a double suspension is necessary rather than subsequent higher and higher suspensions for more complicated objects.

A.32.

Remark.
Recall the infinite suspension

commutes with suspension. So if we value our motivic realization in motivic spectra via the composition

then the addition of

A.33.

Connectivity, cofibers, and suspension.

A.37.

Proposition. —

Let

be

-connected. Then

-connected.

Proof.

Consider the pushout square
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces*}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cofib(f).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}

\fib(f)\to\fib(*\to\cofib(f))=\Omega\cofib(f)

-connected. Therefore,

\pi_{k}(\fib(f))\to\pi_{k}(\Omega\cofib(f))=\pi_{k+1}(\cofib(f))

is surjective for

. For any nonempty space

, it is
(-1)
-connected by definition. Hence the above map is surjective for

. However, if

is

-connected then
\fib(f)
is
n
-connected so the surjection shows that
\pi_{k+1}(\cofib(f))=0
for

\cofib(f)
is
(n+1)
-connected.
∎

Let

be
n
-connected. Then
\cofib(f)
is
(n+1)
-connected. The converse holds if

is

-connected and

is

-connected.

By assumption,

is an isomorphism on

is
(-1)
-connected. To prove

is

-connected, we proceed by induction. Assume

is

-connected. Then

\fib(f)\to\Omega\cofib(f)

is

is

A.42.

Proposition. —

Let

a submanifold of (real) codimension

. Then the inclusion

We apply the Blakers-Massey Theorem

to the homotopy pushout diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces N_{Z}}

where

is a disk bundle variant of the normal bundle which is isomorphic to a tubular neighborhood of

inside

. Notice that the intersection

is homotopy equivalent to the sphere bundle

of the normal bundle.

Note that the projection
N_{Z}\to Z
is a homotopy equivalence so we may replace the map
N_{Z}\cap U\to N_{Z}
by the projection
N_{Z}\cap U\to Z
. Since

, the bundle

is an

(r-2)
-connected. Furthermore,

is

(r-2)

\pi_{k}(N_{Z},S(N_{Z}))\to\pi_{k}(X,U)

is an isomorphism for

and surjective for

. However, the pair
(N_{Z},S(N_{Z}))
is also
(r-1)
-connected (since

is
(r-2)
-connected) meaning
\pi_{k}(N_{Z},S(N_{Z}))=0
for

. Thus
\pi_{k}(X,U)=0
for

.
∎

. Indeed, let

A.67.

Comparison of Bott Maps.

We set
\Omega_{Y}
to be the right adjoint to
Y\wedge(-)
where
Y
is a pointed motivic space considered as an endofunctor of the category of pointed motivic spaces. Note that
\Omega_{Y}
exists because
\mathrm{Mor}_{\mathrm{Spc}(B)_{*}}(Y\wedge(-),X)
commutes with homotopy colimits and forms a pointed
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-invariant sheaf when evaluated on objects of
\mathrm{Sm}_{B}
for any pointed motivic space
X
. Set
K=\Omega^{\infty}\mathrm{KGL}
, which is the space representing algebraic
K
-theory as we saw in the previous section. The motivic space
K
comes equipped with a natural map

\beta:K\to\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}K

called the Bott map. Since
\mathrm{KGL}
is a ring spectrum, such a map is induced by the map

\mathbb{Z}=[*_{+},K]_{*}\to[*_{+},\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}K]_{*}=[\mathbb{P}^{1},K]_{*}=\tilde{K}_{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1})

defined by mapping
1
to the element
\beta=(\gamma-1)
(here
\gamma
is the class of the tautological bundle
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-1)
) which we call the Bott element.

Indeed, we can produce the Bott map using tensor product and adjunction. The map

\beta:K_{0}(X)\to K_{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1}\times X)

given by the difference of the
-\otimes\gamma
map and the pullback induces, by Yoneda and adjunction, a map

K\to\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{1}_{+}}K

since
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{+}\wedge X=\mathbb{P}^{1}\times X
. This is itself, adjoint to a map

\mathbb{P}^{1}_{+}\wedge K\to K.

To show it factors through
\mathbb{P}^{1}\wedge K\to K
it suffices to show that
*_{+}\wedge K\to K
is equivalent to
0
. This is exactly the statement that restricting
\gamma-1
to
*\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
gives a trivial class.

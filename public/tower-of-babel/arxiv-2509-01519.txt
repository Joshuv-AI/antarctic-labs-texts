Weak irreducibility of stochastic delay differential equation driven by pure jump noise
Hao Yang Jian Wang 1.School of Mathematics, Hefei University of Technology, Hefei, Anhui 230009, China.2. School of Mathematics, Hangzhou Normal University, Hangzhou 311121, China.

Abstract




\theorembodyfont

Abstract:

In this paper, we study the weak irreducibility of stochastic delay differential equations(SDDEs) driven by pure jump noise. The main contribution of this paper is to provide a concise proof of weak irreducibility, releasing condition (A1-3) in Assumption 2.1 from the literature
[
21
]
. As an application, we derive the weak irreducibility of SDDEs with weakly dissipative coefficients. An important novelty of this paper is to allow the driving noises to be degenerate. This closes the gap of the irreducibility of SDDEs driven by pure jump noise.

Keywords:

Weak irreducibility; pure jump noise; weakly dissipative; degenerate

AMS Subject Classification (2020):

34K50; 60H10; 60G51; 37A50.

1
Introduction and motivation

Let
H
be a topological space with Borel
\sigma
-field
\mathcal{B}(H)
, and let
\mathbb{X}:=\{X^{\xi}(t),t\geq 0;\xi\in H\}
be an
H
-valued Markov process on some
probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
.

\mathbb{X}
is said to be weakly irreducible (also called accessible) to

\xi_{0}\in H
if the resolvent
R_{\lambda},\lambda>0
satisfies

R_{\lambda}(\xi,U):=\lambda\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda t}\mathbb{P}(X^{\xi}(t)\in U)dt>0

for all
\xi\in H
and all neighborhoods
U
of
\xi_{0}
, where
\lambda>0
is arbitrary.

The significance of studying weak irreducibility lies in its contribution to the ergodicity of Markov processes on Polish spaces. Indeed, weak irreducibility, when combined with a smoothing property (such as the asymptotic strong Feller property
[
7
]
) or contractivity (e-property
[
10
]
or eventual continuity
[
6
]
) of the Markov semigroup, implies that there can be at most one invariant probability measure. It is noteworthy that eventual continuity is a weaker condition than both the asymptotic strong Feller property and the e-property. Specifically, there exist Markov-Feller processes that exhibit eventual continuity but do not satisfy either the asymptotic strong Feller property or the e-property; further details can be found in references
[
5
,
6
,
13
]
.

Now we survey the existing results concerning the weak irreduciblility of stochastic
systems driven by pure jump noise. For the case of stochastic differential equations(SDEs), the authors in
[
12
]
derived the support property of SDEs driven by pure jump noise under local Lipschitz condition, which strongly implies weak irreduciblility. For such support property, one can also refer to
[
9
,
11
,
18
,
19
]
. For the case of SPDEs, the authors in

[
25
]
obtained the weak irreduciblility to zero of stochastic real valued Ginzburg-Landau
equation on torus
\mathbb{T}=\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Z}
driven by cylindrical symmetric
\alpha
-stable process with

\alpha\in(1,2)
. In
[
23
]
, the authors extended the result in
[
25
]
to
n
dimensions. The authors in
[
3
]
obtained
the weak irreduciblility to zero of a class of semilinear SPDEs with Lipschitz coeﬃcients
driven by multiplicative pure jump noise. Since their methods of obtaining the weak irreduciblility are basically along the
same lines as that for the Gaussian case, the driving noises they considered are
a class of subordinated Wiener processes. In our recent papers
[
20
,
21
]
, we developed new criterions for the irreducibility of
SPDEs driven by multiplicative pure jump noise and weak irreduciblility of SPDEs driven by additive pure jump noise. The driven noises in
[
20
,
21
]
could be very weak, including a large class of Lévy processes with heavy tails and a large class of compound
Poisson processe, etc.

Delay differential equations arise from evolutionary phenomena with time delay in fields such as physics, biology, and engineering
[
8
]
. Specifically, such equations have been used to describe the dynamic behaviors of materials with thermal memory, biochemical reactions, and population models. For instance, in population dynamics systems, delayed Lotka-Volterra models, chemotaxis models, SIR models are often employed to depict the evolution trends of animal and microbial systems over time. To consider the irreducibility of such SDDEs, it is generally necessary to consider the infinite-dimensional fragment process, so we need to analyze the property of whole path. In this case, it is a difficult problem to study the irreducibility of solution maps driven by pure jump Lévy noise. To the best of our knowledge, there are no results on the irreducibility of SDDEs driven by pure jump noise. This strongly motivates the current paper.

In this paper, we establish the weak irreduciblility of SDDEs driven by additive pure jump noise; see Theorem
2.1
in Section 2. The main contribution of this paper is to provide a concise proof of weak irreducibility, releasing condition (A1-3) in Assumption 2.1 from the literature
[
21
]
. As an application, we obtain the weak irreduciblility for SDDEs driven by pure jump noise with weakly dissipative coefficients; see Proposition
3.1
. We remark that an important novelty is to allow the driving noises to be degenerate and we only require the Lévy measure to be symmetric. This closes the gap of the irreducibility of SDDEs
driven by pure jump noise.

The organization of the paper is as follows.
In Section 2, we introduce the precise
framework and the main result. In Section 3, we provide some applications to
SDDEs, including the weak irreduciblility of a class of SDDEs with weakly dissipative coefficients.

2
The framework and main results

Unless otherwise specified, we will use the following notation. Let

|\cdot|
denote the Euclidean norm on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with the corresponding inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
and
(\Omega,{\mathcal{F}},P)
be a complete probability space with a filtration
\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}
satisfying the usual conditions. For a vector or matrix
A
, its transpose is denoted by
A^{T}
and
|A|={\rm Trace}(A^{T}A)
denotes its trace norm. Denote by
D((-\infty,0];\mathbb{R}^{n})
the family of function from
(-\infty,0]
to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. In this paper, we choose the phase space
D_{r}~(r>0)
defined by

D_{r}=\{\varphi\in D((-\infty,0];\mathbb{R}^{n}):\lim_{\theta\rightarrow-\infty}e^{r\theta}\varphi(\theta)~\rm{exists~in}~\mathbb{R}^{n}\},

which is a Banach space with norm
\|\varphi\|_{r}=\sup_{-\infty<\theta\leq 0}e^{r\theta}|\varphi(\theta)|<\infty.
Note that all bounded and continuous functions are contained in this space, and for any
0<r_{1}<r_{2}<\infty
,
D_{r_{1}}\subset D_{r_{2}}
. Let
\mathcal{B}(D_{r})
denote the
\sigma
-algebra generated by
D_{r},
and
\mathcal{P}
be the family of all probability measures on
(D_{r},\mathcal{B}(D_{r}))
. We also define probability measure set
M_{\kappa}
as

M_{\kappa}:=\{\mu\in M_{0};\mu^{(\kappa)}:=\int_{-\infty}^{0}e^{-\kappa\theta}\mu(d\theta)<\infty\}

for any
\kappa>0,
where
M_{0}
is the set of probability measures on
(-\infty,0].

Let
\nu
denote a given
{\sigma}
-finite intensity measure of a Lévy process on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Recall that
\nu(\{0\})=0
and
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(|z|^{2}\wedge 1)\nu(dz)<\infty
.
Let
N:{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{+})\times\Omega\rightarrow\bar{\mathbb{N}}
be the time homogeneous Poisson random measure with intensity measure
\nu
. Again
\tilde{N}(dz,dt)=N(dz,dt)-\nu(dz)dt
denotes the compensated Poisson random measure associated to
N
.

Let us point out that (as shown by e.g.,
[
15
, Theorems 4.23 and 6.8]
) in this case

L(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0<|z|\leq 1}z\tilde{N}(dz,ds)+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{|z|>1}zN(dz,ds),t\geq 0

defines an
\mathbb{R}^{n}
-valued Lévy process.

Consider an
n
-dimensional FSDE

dx(t)=f(x_{t})dt+dL(t)

(2.1)

with the initial value
\xi\in D_{r}
, where
x_{t}=x_{t}(\theta)=:\{x(t+\theta),-\infty<\theta\leq 0\},
which sometimes we also denote by
x_{t}^{\xi}
to indicate the initial value is
\xi
.
f:D_{r}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
is Borel measurable. We also need to consider the following equations:

\displaystyle dX^{\epsilon}(t)=f(X^{\epsilon}_{t})dt+\int_{0<\|z\|\leq\epsilon}z\tilde{N}(dt,dz),~~\epsilon\in(0,1)

(2.2)

and

\displaystyle dX(t)=f(X_{t})dt.

(2.3)

In the following, we use the notation
x^{\xi}
,
X^{\epsilon,\xi}
and
X^{\xi}
to indicate the solutions of (
2.1
), (
2.2
) and (
2.3
) starting from
\xi
, respectively.

We introduce the following assumption.

Assumption 2.1

(A0)

\nu
is symmetric, i.e.,
\nu(C)=\nu(-C)
,
\forall C\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

(A1)

For any
\xi\in D_{r}
, there exist unique global solutions
x^{\xi}
,
X^{\epsilon,\xi}
and
X^{\xi}
to (
2.1
), (
2.2
) and (
2.3
), respectively, satisfying

(A1-1)

For any
\xi\in D_{r}
,
\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\|X^{\xi}_{t}\|_{r}=0
.

(A1-2)

For any
t>0
and
\xi\in D_{r}
,
\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\|X^{\epsilon,\xi}_{t}-X^{\xi}_{t}\|_{r}=0
in probability.

The main result of this paper is as follows:

Theorem 2.1

Assume that Assumption
2.1
holds, then
\{x^{\xi}\}_{\xi\in D_{r}}
is weakly irreducible to zero.

Proof

Fix
\xi\in D_{r}
and arbitrary
\kappa>0
, according to Assumption (A1-1), there exists
\tilde{T}>0
such that for any
T>\tilde{T}

\|X^{\xi}_{T}\|_{r}\leq\frac{\kappa}{2}.

(2.4)

Assumption (A1-2) implies that for any
T>\tilde{T}
, there exists
\tilde{\epsilon}=\tilde{\epsilon}(T)>0
such that

P(\|X^{\tilde{\epsilon},\xi}_{T}-X^{\xi}_{T}\|_{r}\leq\frac{\kappa}{4})\geq\frac{1}{2}.

(2.5)

Let
\tau_{\tilde{\epsilon}}^{1}=\inf\{t\geq 0:\int_{0}^{t}\int_{|z|>\tilde{\epsilon}}N(dz,ds)\geq 1\}
. Then
\tau_{\tilde{\epsilon}}^{1}
has the exponential distribution with parameter
\nu(|z|>\tilde{\epsilon})<\infty
. In particular,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\tau_{\tilde{\epsilon}}^{1}>s)=e^{-\nu(|z|>\tilde{\epsilon})s}\text{ and}\,\,\mathbb{P}(0<\tau_{\tilde{\epsilon}}^{1}<\infty)=1.

(2.6)

By virtue of Assumption (A0), we see that

L(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0<|z|\leq\tilde{\epsilon}}z\tilde{N}(dz,ds)+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{|z|_{H}>\tilde{\epsilon}}zN(dz,ds),\,\,t\geq 0.

Therefore, for any
\xi\in D_{r}
,
\{x^{\xi}_{t},t\in[0,\tau_{\tilde{\epsilon}}^{1})\}
is the unique solution to (
2.2
) with the initial data
\xi
on
t\in[0,\tau_{\tilde{\epsilon}}^{1})
. We also note that
\sigma\{X^{\tilde{\epsilon},\xi}_{t},t\geq 0\}
and
\sigma\{\tau_{\tilde{\epsilon}}^{1}\}
are independent.

By (
2.4
), (
2.5
) and (
2.6
), for any fixed
\xi\in D_{r}
,
\kappa>0
and
\forall~T>\tilde{T}

\displaystyle P(\|x^{\xi}_{T}\|_{r}\leq\kappa)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P(\|x^{\xi}_{T}-X^{\tilde{\epsilon},\xi}_{T}+X^{\tilde{\epsilon},\xi}_{T}-X^{\xi}_{T}+X^{\xi}_{T}\|_{r}\leq\kappa)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle P(\|x^{\xi}_{T}-X^{\tilde{\epsilon},\xi}_{T}+X^{\tilde{\epsilon},\xi}_{T}-X^{\xi}_{T}+X^{\xi}_{T}\|_{r}\leq\kappa,\tau_{\tilde{\epsilon}}>T)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P(\|X^{\tilde{\epsilon},\xi}_{T}-X^{\xi}_{T}+X^{\xi}_{T}\|_{r}\leq\kappa,\tau_{\tilde{\epsilon}}>T)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P(\|X^{\tilde{\epsilon},\xi}_{T}-X^{\xi}_{T}+X^{\xi}_{T}\|_{r}\leq\kappa)P(\tau_{\tilde{\epsilon}}>T)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle P(\|X^{\tilde{\epsilon},\xi}_{T}-X^{\xi}_{T}\|_{r}\leq\frac{\kappa}{4})P(\tau_{\tilde{\epsilon}}>T)

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle 0.

The proof of Theorem
2.1
is complete.

\Box

3
Applications

In this section, as an application of the main result of Theorem
2.1
, we obtain the weak irreduciblility of a class of nonlinear SDDEs with weakly dissipative coefficients driven by additive pure jump noise.

Let us formulate the assumptions on the coefficients.

(H1) Assume that the coefficients
f
satisfy the local Lipschitz condition, that is, for any
k>0,
there exists a
c_{k}
such that

|f(\varphi)-f(\psi)|^{2}\leq c_{k}||\varphi-\psi||_{r}^{2}

(3.1)

for those
\varphi,~\phi

\in

D_{r}
with
||\varphi||_{r}\vee||\phi||_{r}\leq k
.

(H2) There exist nonnegative constants
\bar{\lambda}_{1},

\bar{\lambda}_{2},

\bar{K}_{1},

\bar{K}_{2}
, and probability measures
\mu_{1}\in M_{(2\vee q_{1})r},

\mu_{2}\in M_{2r}
, such that for any
\varphi
,
\psi\in D_{r}
,

\displaystyle 2\langle\varphi(0)-\psi(0),f(\varphi)-f(\psi)\rangle

(3.2)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\bar{K}_{1}\int_{-\infty}^{0}H(\varphi(\theta),\psi(\theta))d\mu_{1}(\theta)-\bar{K}_{2}H(\varphi(0),\psi(0))

\displaystyle\quad+\bar{\lambda}_{1}\int_{-\infty}^{0}|\varphi(\theta)-\psi(\theta)|^{2}d\mu_{2}(\theta)-\bar{\lambda}_{2}|\varphi(0)-\psi(0)|^{2},

where
H
is a continuous nonnegative function defined on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
H(x,x)=0
and
H(x,y)\leq K(|x|^{q_{1}}+|y|^{q_{2}})
,
q_{1}\wedge q_{2}>0
.

In the sequel, the symbol
C
will denote a positive generic constant whose value may
change from line to line.

We consider the weak irreduciblility of (
2.1
). The main result is stated as follows.

Proposition 3.1

Assume that (H1), (H2) hold and
\nu
is symmetric, if
\bar{\lambda}_{2}-2r-\bar{\lambda}_{1}\mu_{2}^{(2r)}\geq 0
and
\bar{K}_{2}-\bar{K}_{1}\mu_{1}^{(2r)}\geq 0
, then the solution
\{x^{\xi}\}_{\xi\in D_{r}}
of (
2.1
) is weakly irreducible to zero.

Proof

We will apply Theorem
2.1
, that is, we verify Assumptions (A0)-(A1). Since (A0) is imposed, we only need to verify Assumption (A1).

Since
f
is local Lipschitz continuous and satisfies (
3.2
), there exist unique global solutions to (
2.2
) and (
2.3
), respectively. For more details, one can invoke Theorem V.7 in Protter
[
14
]
and Remark 1.1 in
[
4
]
. The unique global solution to (
2.1
) can be obtained by the solution to (
2.2
) with
\epsilon=1
and the so-called interlacing technique; see
[
2
]
.

Note that for any
t\geq 0
,

\displaystyle||X_{t}^{\xi}||_{r}\leq e^{-rt}\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}|X(s)|+e^{-rt}||\xi||_{r}.

So to prove (A1-1), we only need to prove
\sup_{0\leq t<\infty}|X(t)|\leq C
.

Choose
\psi=0
in (H2), by the Young inequality, for any
0<\delta<2r
, we derive

\displaystyle 2\langle\varphi(0),f(\varphi)\rangle

(3.3)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\langle\varphi(0),f(\varphi)-f(0)\rangle+2\varphi^{T}(0)f(0)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\langle\varphi(0),f(\varphi)-f(0)\rangle+\frac{1}{\delta}|f(0)|^{2}+\delta|\varphi(0)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\delta}|f(0)|^{2}+\bar{K}_{1}\int_{-\infty}^{0}H(\varphi(\theta),0)d\mu_{1}(\theta)-\bar{K}_{2}H(\varphi(0),0)

\displaystyle+\bar{\lambda}_{1}\int_{-\infty}^{0}|\varphi(\theta)|^{2}d\mu_{2}(\theta)-\bar{\lambda}_{2}|\varphi(0)|^{2}+\delta|\varphi(0)|^{2}.

By the chain rule and (
3.3
), for every
t>0
,

\displaystyle e^{2rt}|X(t)|^{2}

(3.4)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\xi(0)|^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}(2re^{2rs}|X(s)|^{2}+2e^{2rs}\langle X(s),f(X_{s})\rangle)ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|\xi(0)|^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{2rs}\Big(2r|X(s)|^{2}+L+\bar{K}_{1}\int_{-\infty}^{0}\widetilde{H}(X(s+\theta))d\mu_{1}(\theta)-\bar{K}_{2}\widetilde{H}(X(s))

\displaystyle+\bar{\lambda}_{1}\int_{-\infty}^{0}|X(s+\theta)|^{2}d\mu_{2}(\theta)-\bar{\lambda}_{2}|X(s)|^{2}+\delta|X(s)|^{2}\Big)ds,

where
L=\frac{1}{\delta}|f(0)|^{2}
,
\widetilde{H}(x)=H(x,0)
. Note that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{-\infty}^{0}e^{2rs}|X(s+\theta)|^{2}d\mu_{2}(\theta)ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\Big[\int_{-\infty}^{-s}e^{2rs}|X(s+\theta)|^{2}d\mu_{2}(\theta)+\int_{-s}^{0}e^{2rs}|X(s+\theta)|^{2}d\mu_{2}(\theta)\Big]ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}ds\int_{-\infty}^{-s}e^{2r(s+\theta)}|X(s+\theta)|^{2}e^{-2r(s+\theta)+2rs}d\mu_{2}(\theta)+\int_{-t}^{0}d\mu_{2}(\theta)\int_{-\theta}^{t}e^{2rs}|X(s+\theta)|^{2}ds

\displaystyle\leq t||\xi||_{r}^{2}\int_{-\infty}^{-s}e^{-2r\theta}d\mu_{2}(\theta)+\int_{-t}^{0}e^{-2r\theta}d\mu_{2}(\theta)\int_{-\theta}^{t}e^{2r(s+\theta)}|X(s+\theta)|^{2}ds

\displaystyle\leq t||\xi||_{r}^{2}\int_{-\infty}^{0}e^{-2r\theta}\mu_{2}(\theta)+\int_{-\infty}^{0}e^{-2r\theta}d\mu_{2}(\theta)\int_{0}^{t}e^{2rs}|X(s)|^{2}ds

\displaystyle=t||\xi||_{r}^{2}\mu_{2}^{(2r)}+\mu_{2}^{(2r)}\int_{0}^{t}e^{2rs}|X(s)|^{2}ds

(3.5)

and

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{-\infty}^{0}e^{2rs}\widetilde{H}(X(s+\theta))d\mu_{1}(\theta)ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\Big[\int_{-\infty}^{-s}e^{2rs}\widetilde{H}(X(s+\theta))d\mu_{1}(\theta)+\int_{-s}^{0}e^{2rs}\widetilde{H}(X(s+\theta))d\mu_{1}(\theta)\Big]ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}e^{2rs}ds\int_{-\infty}^{-s}\widetilde{H}(e^{r(s+\theta)}X(s+\theta)e^{-r(s+\theta)})d\mu_{1}(\theta)+\int_{-t}^{0}d\mu_{1}(\theta)\int_{-\theta}^{t}e^{2rs}\widetilde{H}(X(s+\theta))ds

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}\int_{-\infty}^{-s}K||\xi||_{r}^{q_{1}}e^{-q_{1}r(s+\theta)}e^{2rs}d\mu_{1}(\theta)ds+\int_{-t}^{0}e^{-2r\theta}d\mu_{1}(\theta)\int_{-\theta}^{t}e^{2r(s+\theta)}\widetilde{H}(X(s+\theta))ds

\displaystyle\leq\frac{K}{-(q_{1}r-2r)}||\xi||_{r}^{q_{1}}\mu_{1}^{(q_{1}r)}(e^{-(q_{1}r-2r)t}-1)+\mu_{1}^{(2r)}\int_{0}^{t}e^{2rs}\widetilde{H}(X(s))ds.

(3.6)

According to our assumption,

\bar{\lambda}_{2}-2r-\bar{\lambda}_{1}\mu_{2}^{(2r)}\geq 0,\quad\bar{K}_{2}-\bar{K}_{1}\mu_{1}^{(2r)}\geq 0,

this together with (
3.4
), (
3
), (
3
) yields that

\displaystyle e^{2rt}|X(t)|^{2}

(3.7)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|\xi(0)|^{2}+\frac{L}{2r}(e^{2rt}-1)+\bar{\lambda}_{1}t||\xi||_{r}^{2}\mu_{2}^{(2r)}

\displaystyle+\frac{\bar{K}_{1}K}{-(q_{1}r-2r)}||\xi||_{r}^{q_{1}}\mu_{1}^{(q_{1}r)}(e^{-(q_{1}r-2r)t}-1)+\delta\int_{0}^{t}e^{2rs}|X(s)|^{2}ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(1+e^{2rt})+\delta\int_{0}^{t}e^{2rs}|X(s)|^{2}ds.

Applying the Gronwall inequality, we have

\displaystyle e^{2rt}|X(t)|^{2}\leq Ce^{2rt},

which gives that

\sup_{0\leq t<\infty}|X(t)|\leq C.

Next, we verify (A1-2).

Note that

\displaystyle X^{\epsilon,\xi}(t)-X^{\xi}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}f(X^{\epsilon,\xi}_{s})-f(X^{\xi}_{s})ds+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0<|z|\leq\epsilon}z\tilde{N}(ds,dz).

Applying the Ito formula and (H2) gives

\displaystyle e^{2rt}|X^{\epsilon,\xi}(t)-X^{\xi}(t)|^{2}

(3.8)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}(2re^{2rs}|X^{\epsilon,\xi}(s)-X^{\xi}(s)|^{2}+2\langle X^{\epsilon,\xi}(s)-X^{\xi}(s),f(X^{\epsilon,\xi}_{s})-f(X^{\xi}_{s})\rangle)ds

\displaystyle+2\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0<|z|\leq\epsilon}e^{2rs}\langle X^{\epsilon,\xi}(s)-X^{\xi}(s),z\rangle\tilde{N}(ds,dz)+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0<|z|\leq\epsilon}e^{2rs}|z|^{2}{N}(ds,dz)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}e^{2rs}\Big(2r|X^{\epsilon,\xi}(s)-X^{\xi}(s)|^{2}+\bar{\lambda}_{1}\int_{-\infty}^{0}|X^{\epsilon,\xi}(s+\theta)-X^{\xi}(s+\theta)|^{2}d\mu_{2}(\theta)

\displaystyle-\bar{\lambda}_{2}|X^{\epsilon,\xi}(s)-X^{\xi}(s)|^{2}+\bar{K}_{1}\int_{-\infty}^{0}H(X^{\epsilon,\xi}(s+\theta),X^{\xi}(s+\theta))d\mu_{1}(\theta)

\displaystyle-\bar{K}_{2}H(X^{\epsilon,\xi}(s),X^{\xi}(s))\Big)ds+2\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0<|z|\leq\epsilon}e^{2rs}\langle X^{\epsilon,\xi}(s)-X^{\xi}(s),z\rangle\tilde{N}(ds,dz)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0<|z|\leq\epsilon}e^{2rs}|z|^{2}{N}(ds,dz).

According to (
3
), (
3
) and
H(\xi(\theta),\xi(\theta))=0
,

\int_{0}^{t}\int_{-\infty}^{0}e^{2rs}|X^{\epsilon,\xi}(s+\theta)-X^{\xi}(s+\theta)|^{2}d\mu_{2}(\theta)ds\leq\mu_{2}^{(2r)}\int_{0}^{t}e^{2rs}|X^{\epsilon,\xi}(s)-X^{\xi}(s)|^{2}ds

(3.9)

and

\int_{0}^{t}\int_{-\infty}^{0}e^{2rs}H(X^{\epsilon,\xi}(s+\theta),X^{\xi}(s+\theta))d\mu_{1}(\theta)ds\leq\mu_{1}^{(2r)}\int_{0}^{t}e^{2rs}H(X^{\epsilon,\xi}(s),X^{\xi}(s))ds.

(3.10)

By (
3.8
), (
3.9
), (
3.10
) and
\bar{\lambda}_{2}-2r-\bar{\lambda}_{1}\mu_{2}^{(2r)}\geq 0
and
\bar{K}_{2}-\bar{K}_{1}\mu_{1}^{(2r)}\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle e^{2rt}|X^{\epsilon,\xi}(t)-X^{\xi}(t)|^{2}

(3.11)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-(\bar{\lambda}_{2}-2r-\bar{\lambda}_{1}\mu_{2}^{(2r)})\int_{0}^{t}e^{2rs}|X^{\epsilon,\xi}(s)-X^{\xi}(s)|^{2}ds

\displaystyle-(\bar{K}_{2}-\bar{K}_{1}\mu_{1}^{(2r)})\int_{0}^{t}e^{2rs}H(X^{\epsilon,\xi}(s),X^{\xi}(s))ds

\displaystyle+2\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0<|z|\leq\epsilon}e^{2rs}\langle X^{\epsilon,\xi}(s)-X^{\xi}(s),z\rangle\tilde{N}(ds,dz)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0<|z|\leq\epsilon}e^{2rs}|z|^{2}{N}(ds,dz)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0<|z|\leq\epsilon}e^{2rs}\langle X^{\epsilon,\xi}(s)-X^{\xi}(s),z\rangle\tilde{N}(ds,dz)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0<|z|\leq\epsilon}e^{2rs}|z|^{2}{N}(ds,dz).

Then by the stochastic Gronwall inequality (see Lemma 3.7 in
[
24
]
), for any
0<q<p<1

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big(\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}e^{2rs}|X^{\epsilon,\xi}(t)-X^{\xi}(t)|^{2}\Big)^{q}\leq\frac{p}{p-q}\Big(\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0<|z|\leq\epsilon}e^{2rs}|z|^{2}\nu(dz)ds\Big)^{q}.

Since

\|X^{\epsilon,\xi}_{t}-X^{\xi}_{t}\|^{2}_{r}\leq e^{-2rt}\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}e^{2rs}|X^{\epsilon,\xi}(t)-X^{\xi}(t)|^{2},

we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\|X^{\epsilon,\xi}_{t}-X^{\xi}_{t}\|_{r}^{2q}\leq e^{-2qrt}\frac{p}{p-q}\Big(\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0<|z|\leq\epsilon}e^{2rs}|z|^{2}\nu(dz)ds\Big)^{q}.

(3.12)

Observing that, in this setting, the convergence
is stronger than the one (in probability) required in condition (A1-2),
so (A1-2) holds. So the proof of Proposition
3.1
is also complete.

\Box

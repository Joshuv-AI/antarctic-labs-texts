Design of an ultra-compact and high-performance antenna for private 5G applications
Authors: Youjin Han, Jae-Geun Lee, Byung Kuon Ahn
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This paper presents an ultra-compact and high-performance antenna for Private 5G applications. Private 5G applications, which provide customized coverage, face the challenge of simultaneously achieving bandwidths exceeding 100 MHz, stable gain, and miniaturization. In general, small antennas have a trade-off between physical size and bandwidth and gain, which limits their practical applications. In order to address this issue, this study adopted a step-by-step design approach. After setting the conventional patch antenna as the reference structure, miniaturization was induced by maximizing edge deformation to extend the effective surface current path. The bandwidth reduction caused by the current density imbalance due to edge deformation was effectively improved by combining an aperture coupled feed and a defected ground structure. In addition, symmetrical slots are inserted at the top of the patch to further extend the current path and achieve additional miniaturization. A prototype antenna was fabricated to experimentally validate the performance of the proposed antenna. The measured 10-dB impedance bandwidth is 4.70-4.83 GHz (2.73%) with a peak gain of 5.23 dBi, while the electrical diagonal length is 0.19\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\lambda _0$$\end{document} at 4.77 GHz. Compared with previous studies, the proposed antenna achieves a well-balanced performance of miniaturization, bandwidth, and gain, resulting in a competitive figure of merit. The proposed ultra-compact and high-performance antenna has practical applications in Private 5G with significant size constraints, and the step-by-step design procedure can provide useful guidelines for designing small antennas in other frequency bands through appropriate sizing and re-optimization.

Introduction

Compared to previous generations, fifth-generation (5G) communication systems offer ultra-high data rates, ultra-low latency, and massive device connectivity1,2. In particular, Private 5G networks are attracting significant attention because they allow companies and organizations to build and operate their own 5G infrastructure independently from public networks3. These private networks provide essential security and reliability in the industrial and defense sectors where sensitive data must be protected. It also offers customized coverage and high quality assurance, making it suitable for applications in smart factories, medical facilities, railways4. Furthermore, their support for real-time communication and control opens up the possibility for advanced applications such as robotics, autonomous vehicles, and unmanned aerial systems5. Several countries, including the United States, Germany, the United Kingdom, and Japan, have allocated dedicated frequency bands for Private 5G services6. In South Korea, the Ministry of Science and ICT has allocated the 4.72-4.82.82 GHz band specifically for Private 5G services7. This band offers sufficient bandwidth to meet the high-capacity, low-latency communications demands of various industrial applications. Compared with the conventional 3.5 GHz band, the 4.7 GHz band is advantageous for antenna miniaturization due to its shorter wavelength; furthermore, compared with millimeter-wave(mmWave) band such as 28 GHz, it provides superior propagation range and penetration. Therefore, the 4.7 GHz Private 5G band is particularly attractive for devices with size constraints. Antenna technology is therefore a core component of Private 5G systems, with miniaturization emerging as a crucial research direction8. Because Private 5G devices are required to be lightweight, portable, and highly integrated, they must secure both sufficient bandwidth and stable radiation performance within a limited physical size. For example, in unmanned aerial systems(UASs) and drones, compact antennas are essential to reduce payload while ensuring a reliable control link9; likewise, in wearable or implantable sensors, compact antennas improve user convenience10,11. Therefore, the development of an antenna that simultaneously satisfies electrical performance and physical miniaturization in the Private 5G band is an important issue.

During the past several decades, various technologies have been studied to miniaturize antennas. Representative methods include patch edge-deformed fractal structure12,13, slot antennas14, short-pin antennas15, and meandered-folded short-patch antennas16. These antennas have been used to lower the resonant frequency and improve the impedance bandwidth by increasing the current path or dispersing the ground plane current. However, most techniques have limitations in simultaneously satisfying all three performance metrics due to trade-offs among size, bandwidth, and radiation performance. In particular, maintaining a compact size while securing a bandwidth exceeding 100 MHz and sufficient gain remains difficult. These limitations are evident in previous studies. Some compact antennas exhibit very narrow bandwidths and fail to meet the 100 MHz requirement for Private 5G17,18. In other work, the bandwidth was increased using parasitic element19, stacked patches20, and meta-surfaces21, but the overall size increased, making such approaches unsuitable for small systems. For antennas that operate over a wide bandwidth with a small size, their low gain makes them difficult to use in environments where reliability is important. Recent research has primarily targeted sub-6 GHz, Wi-Fi, LTE bands22,23 or mmWave 5G bands24,25, and there is a lack of research on compact, high-performance antennas directly targeting the Private 5G bands. Consequently, a clear research gap remains in the development of a compact, sufficient bandwidth, high-gain antenna optimized for the 4.72–4.82.72.82 GHz band.

To overcome these limitations, this study proposes a design process that can achieve miniaturization and performance enhancement simultaneously. Firstly, a conventional microstrip patch antenna is set as the reference structure, and then miniaturization is pursued through edge deformation. Afterwards, the bandwidth was enhanced by adding a defected ground structure (DGS), and further miniaturization was achieved by inserting symmetrical slots. This step-by-step design approach clearly demonstrates the effectiveness of each design technique, and several design techniques are essential to achieve the goal. The proposed antenna has a compact electrical size of 0.19\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\lambda _0$$\end{document} in diagonal length at 4.77 GHz, 10-dB impedance bandwidth of 113 MHz (2.73%), and a peak gain of 5.23 dBi. Compared to previously studied small antennas, this study effectively achieves a balance between miniaturization, bandwidth, and radiation performance. Therefore, this study demonstrates the implementation of a high-performance antenna that simultaneously satisfies electrical compactness, sufficient bandwidth, and stable radiation performance in the 4.72–4.82.72.82 GHz band for Private 5G. The proposed antenna is optimized for the private 5G band of 4.72–4.82 GHz and has been experimentally verified, but the basic design procedure is not limited to this specific frequency band. The proposed approach can be interpreted as a general compact antenna design framework that first reduces the radiator size through effective electric length control and then compensates for the resulting impedance-bandwidth degradation using auxiliary matching and bandwidth enhancement structures. Therefore, this study demonstrates the validity of the proposed procedure under practical requirements of limited size, sufficient bandwidth, and stable gain by using the private 5G band as a representative target band. This paper is structured as follows. The first section presents the research background and analyzes the limitations of the previous research. The second section presents the design concept and structure of the proposed antenna, its theoretical analysis, design techniques, and parametric study. The third section presents simulation, fabrication and measurement results, and quantitatively demonstrates the performance of the proposed antenna through comparison with previous studies and a figure of merit (FoM) analysis. The final section presents the conclusion and discussion.

Figure 2 illustrates the surface current distributions of the three antenna configurations used in the step-by-step design. Figure 2(a) shows the surface current distribution of the conventional patch antenna, where the surface current is distributed almost in a straight line from the center to the edges. In this case, the effective electrical length of the current path is largely constrained by the physical dimensions of the radiator, which fixes the resonant frequency; therefore, although the radiation efficiency is favorable, structural miniaturization is limited because the current path simply follows the physical size of the patch. Figure 2(b) shows the current distribution of the step-edged patch antenna. By deforming the patch edges into multiple steps, the current is refracted and flows along the stepped boundaries. As the current propagates from the center to the periphery through multiple steps, the travel distance is extended compared to the conventional patch, which increases the effective electrical length and consequently lowers the resonant frequency. To shift the resonant back to the target band, the physical size of the radiator can be reduced, achieving miniaturization. This is a widely used miniaturization concept in compact patch antennas12–16. Figure 2(c) shows the current distribution of the \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\Pi$$\end{document}-edged patch antenna, which can be regarded as an advanced form of the step-edged approach. It is implemented within a very small area by extending the current path protrusions at the four edges and bending them by 90 degrees. As a result, the current circulates along the \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\Pi$$\end{document}-shaped arms: the current launched from the feed point travels along the \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\Pi$$\end{document}-shaped edge and can pass the center again, forming a significantly longer current path. This substantially increases the effective electrical length compared with the step-edged patch, enabling a stronger miniaturization effect while maintaining the target resonant frequency. However, such extreme edge deformation inevitably complicates the current flow and increases current density in specific regions, which tend to increase the quality factor and reduce the impedance bandwidth. Therefore, additional impedance matching and bandwidth recovery are required to realize a practical compact antenna.

In this work, the bandwidth degradation associated with aggressive miniaturization is compensated by introducing an aperture coupled feeding and a DGS. The aperture coupling provides an additional tuning degree of freedom through the coupling slot geometry and alignment, enabling effective impedance control without enlarging the radiator footprint. In addition, the DGS perturbs the ground current distribution and introduces an additional reactive loading effect, which helps broaden the impedance response and improve matching in the target band. Hence, the overall design procedure is logically connected as follows: edge deformation primarily achieves miniaturization by elongating the surface current path, and aperture coupling and DGS are subsequently employed to recover bandwidth and matching while preserving the compact footprint. Furthermore, although the proposed antenna is optimized for the 4.72–4.82 GHz Private 5G band, the overall design procedure is general and can be applied to other frequency bands. The key principle of the proposed approach is to control the effective electrical length and impedance characteristics independently through a sequential design process. First, the target resonant frequency is determined by the effective electrical length of the radiator, which can be scaled according to the desired operating frequency. The edge deformation techniques, such as the step-edged and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\Pi$$\end{document}-edged structures, provide a systematic way to extend the current path, enabling size reduction while maintaining the target frequency. Second, once the miniaturized structure is established, impedance matching and bandwidth recovery can be achieved by introducing additional degrees of freedom, such as aperture coupled feeding and defected ground structures (DGS). These techniques are not frequency-specific and can be adjusted through parametric tuning depending on the desired bandwidth and substrate properties. Therefore, the proposed design methodology can be interpreted as a general framework consisting of electrical length control for miniaturization and impedance control using auxiliary structures, which can be adapted to various frequency bands and application scenarios.

The proposed antenna is designed on a Taconic RF-30 substrate. To ensure a wide bandwidth, two sheet of the 1.52 mm thick substrate were stacked, forming an antenna substrate with a total thickness of 3.04 mm. The conductor layers consist of 1 oz thick copper. This material composition simultaneously guarantees mechanical stability and desirable electrical properties, making it suitable for the implementation of compact antennas. The overall size of the proposed antenna including the ground plane is 30 x 30 \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mathrm mm^2$$\end{document}. The detailed parameters are summarized in Table 2. As illustrated in Fig. 4(a), the structure consists of a \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\Pi$$\end{document}-edged patch antenna on the top layer, a ground plane containing the DGS on the inner layer, and a microstrip line for feeding on the bottom layer. The proposed antenna uses an aperture coupled feeding method. Power is coupled from the microstrip line on the bottom layer to the patch antenna on the top layer through the aperture slot on the inner layer. This method supplies power through a slot positioned between the top patch and the bottom feed line. The aperture coupled feeding method is advantageous in reducing parasitic capacity and increasing bandwidth compared with the direct feeding method. In addition, this method enables the electrical isolation of the radiating element from the feed line, making it possible to simultaneously satisfy the desired radiation performance and impedance matching.

Figure 4(b) presents the top view of the top layer. The \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\Pi$$\end{document}-edged patch antenna effectively extends the surface current path compared with the conventional patch antenna, allowing for a smaller area at the same resonant frequency. Additionally, the symmetrical slots inserted into the patch further lengthen the current path. The length and position of the slots are main factors that determine the resonant frequency, and the larger the slot size, the lower the resonant frequency tends to be. This allows the antenna to operate at a lower frequency despite having the same physical size, which plays an important role in antenna miniaturization. The diagonal length of the proposed antenna has a small size of 0.19\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\lambda _0$$\end{document}. The structure of the inner layer is illustrated in Fig. 4(c). A DGS is applied to the ground plane, which disturbs the ground current, generating additional inductance and capacitance. As a result, the input impedance characteristics are altered, and the 10-dB impedance bandwidth is expanded. The DGS pattern is designed as a slot type, which offers a simple but effective bandwidth enhancement.

Inserting a slot into the patch causes the current to bypass the slot instead of traveling a straight path, increasing the effective length of the surface current path. Therefore, as the slot size increases, the resonant frequency decreases, enabling the implementation of an antenna with a smaller size. Furthermore, the slot induces multiple resonances, which improves impedance matching. As shown in the figure, the resonant frequency decreases as the slot size increases. As the length of the arm increases, the effective current path is lengthened, leading to a decrease in the resonant frequency, which demonstrate the miniaturization effect. Because the resonant frequency changes significantly with the arm length, it is an important factor in miniaturization. The proposed antenna structure offers a miniaturization technique that significantly lowers the resonant frequency by providing an extremely long effective electrical length through extreme edge deformation. Additionally, symmetrical slots inserted into the patch extend the surface current path, providing a longer electrical length, thereby enabling further miniaturization. The simulated and measured front-to-back ratio are 8.44 dB and 14.30 dB, respectively, which are slightly different. This is due to the difference in the backward radiation level, such as back-lobe nulls that occur during the measurement. The proposed antenna has an aperture coupled structure, and in addition to the patch radiation component, relatively small leakage radiation components formed by the aperture slot and ground current are synthesized in the backward region to determine the radiation pattern. During measurement, common mode currents induced in the connector and coaxial cable can cause re-radiation, altering the back-lobe. Scattering due to the jig structure and anechoic chamber alignment errors also affect the backward radiation level. Performance comparison with previous studies.

The fabrication and measurement results validate that the proposed antenna exhibits stable bandwidth and radiation performance in the Private 5G band with compact size. In order to implement the compact antenna, this study applied a step-by-step design approach based on the conventional patch antenna structure. This process combined miniaturization and bandwidth enhancement techniques, such as edge deformation, symmetrical slots, and DGS. Ultimately, this resulted in 10-dB impedance bandwidth over 100 MHz and a peak gain of 5.23 dBi in a compact size. This demonstrates a balanced result that simultaneously satisfy the three key metrics for small antenna: size, bandwidth, and gain. Previous studies analyzed above focused on specific performance improvements and were limited in simultaneously satisfying the three metrics. For instance, some small antennas succeeded in reducing electrical size, but their low gain hindered reliable communication27,32. Other antennas had extremely narrow bandwidth, failing to meet required bandwidth for Private 5G systems33. In order to objectively compare the performance of the proposed antenna with that of previous studies, the following figure of merit (FoM) was introduced.3\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} \textrm{FoM} = \frac{G \times BW}{L_d \times \sqrt{\varepsilon _r}} \end{aligned}$$\end{document}G, BW, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$L_d$$\end{document}, and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\varepsilon _r$$\end{document} represent the peak gain, fractional bandwidth, diagonal length of the radiating element, and dielectric constant of the substrate, respectively. The fractional bandwidth was included to ensure comparable bandwidth performance across different frequency bands. Since a higher dielectric constant facilitates antenna miniaturization, the dielectric constant was also included for objective comparison. This FoM is designed to have a higher value as the antenna size becomes smaller and has a wider bandwidth and higher gain, making it a powerful measure that can fairly compare performance under different substrate conditions. Table 3 compares the performance of the proposed antenna with that of previous studies. Most of the FoM values in previous studies range between 5 and 22. Small-sized antennas have been successfully implemented, but their bandwidth and gain performance remain limited. As such, the more the design focuses on improving a specific performance metric, the more other performance aspects tend to degrade, making it difficult to achieve a FoM beyond a certain level. In the case of reference30, although a high FoM was reported, its practical applicability is limited because the antenna exhibits very low gain despite having a wide bandwidth. Reference34 achieved an extreme level of miniaturization but exhibits an extremely narrow bandwidth and a low gain. In this study, the proposed antenna recorded an FoM of 27.58 based on measured values, maintaining a competitive FoM while overcoming the limitations of previous studies that were practically limited by low gain or narrow bandwidth. This achievement is the result of an optimized design where the techniques applied in the design process operate as intended, and the proposed antenna is expected to provide the most balanced performance for the Private 5G applications.

Although the proposed antenna has been experimentally verified and optimized for the private 5G band of 4.72–4.82 GHz, the step-by-step design procedure is not limited to this specific frequency band.

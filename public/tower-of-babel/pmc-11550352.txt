Rapid identification, pathotyping and quantification of infectious bursal disease virus by high-resolution melting curve quantitative reverse transcription PCR analysis: An innovative technology well-suited for real-time large-scale epidemiological surveillance
Authors: Weiwei Wang, Yan Zhang, Wenbo Zuo, Yuanzheng Qiao, Jun Shi, Jianni Huang, Teng Huang, Tianchao Wei, Meilan Mo, Xiumiao He, Ping Wei
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
With the virus continuing to evolve, very virulent IBDV (vvIBDV) and novel variant IBDV (nvIBDV) have become the predominant epidemic strains in China, exacerbated by the widespread use of attenuated vaccine strains (attIBDV), making a complex infection situation of IBDV in the field. Therefore, developing a rapid and accurate high-resolution melting curve quantitative reverse transcription PCR (HRM-qRT-PCR) for the identification and pathotyping of IBDV is crucial for clinical monitoring and disease control. Extensive data analysis and genome-screening of the three dominant IBDV pathotypes identified a specific region (nucleotides 2450–2603 in segment A) with distinct GC content as the detection target. Experimental testing of HRM-qRT-PCR revealed distinct melting curves and high sensitivity, with the detection limits of 61.2 copies/μL, 61.1 copies/μL and 67.5 copies/μL for vvIBDV, nvIBDV and attIBDV, respectively. The method exhibited excellent specificity, with no inter-genotypes cross-reactivity among the three pathotypes and no reactivity to other common avian pathogens. Applied to samples with double and triple co-infections of different IBDV pathotypes, the method displayed specific melting peaks corresponding to the viruses present in the samples, with an accuracy rate of 100 %. This method precisely identifies and differentiates all the single or co-infected samples, generating distinct peaks corresponding to the Tm values of each virus pathotype in traditional melting curve plots. Furthermore, the method overcomes the limitations of traditional pathotyping methods, requiring only one reaction to achieve rapid viral pathotyping and facilitating quantitative analysis of viruses within the samples. This study introduces an innovative HRM-qRT-PCR method, offering new technology to rapid and accurate identification, pathotyping and quantification of vvIBDV, nvIBDV, and attIBDV. With strong discriminatory power, user-friendliness and a short processing time, this method is highly attractive for the rapid IBDV pathotyping in real-time large-scale epidemiological surveillance during outbreaks.

Introduction

Infectious bursal disease (IBD), caused by the infectious bursal disease virus (IBDV), is one of the most economically destructive viral diseases in poultry, characterized by its prolonged immunosuppression in young chickens, posing a severe threat to poultry health worldwide (Alkie and Rautenschlein, 2016; Berg, 2000; Muller et al., 2003). The IBDV genome consists of segments A and B, encoding a total of five viral proteins, including the RNA-dependent RNA polymerase VP1, capsid protein VP2, scaffolding protein VP3, protease VP4 and non-structural protein VP5 (Birghan et al., 2000; Coulibaly et al., 2005; Da Costa et al., 2002; Hudson et al., 1986; Mata et al., 2018). IBDV primarily targets and lyses B lymphocytes in the central immune organ, the bursa of Fabricius (BF), resulting in severe immunosuppression, thereby increasing susceptibility to other pathogens and reducing the efficacy of vaccine administration (Berg, 2000; Fan et al., 2020; Hou et al., 2022; Kibenge et al., 1988; Leng et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2024). Currently, three main pathotypes of IBDV were found in China, including very virulent IBDV (vvIBDV), novel variant IBDV (nvIBDV) and the extensively used attenuated vaccine IBDV (attIBDV), with the novel variants being a newly discovered pathotype in recent years (Aliyu et al., 2021; Chen et al., 2022; Fan et al., 2019; Huang et al., 2022; Legnardi et al., 2023b; Lian et al., 2022; Myint et al., 2021; Thai et al., 2021; Tomás et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2022a; Wang et al., 2022b; Xu et al., 2020). Although massive IBD vaccinations have been conducted in the poultry industry, different pathotypes of IBDV are increasingly being isolated from both immunized and non-immunized chickens (He et al., 2019; He et al., 2014; Jiang et al., 2021). Similar to other segmented RNA viruses, different pathogenic types of IBDV can co-circulate in the field, and long-term co-infections may provide potentially conditions for viral rapid evolution, including the gene reassortment between segments and the recombination within segments (He et al., 2009; Legnardi et al., 2023a; Lu et al., 2015; McDonald et al., 2016; Wang et al., 2022b; Wang et al., 2023b). At present, it has been confirmed that gene reassortment and intra-segment recombination of different pathotypes of IBDV strains can alter viral virulence and antigenic properties, thereby aiding in immune evasion and undermining the protection provided by currently available commercial vaccines (Feng et al., 2022; He et al., 2009; Hon et al., 2008; Leng et al., 2023; Nooruzzaman et al., 2022; Pérez-Losada et al., 2015; Wang et al., 2021; Wang et al., 2022b; Wang et al., 2024; Wu et al., 2020), posing a huge challenge to the prevention and control of this disease in the poultry industry. Therefore, it is especially important to establish a rapid identification and genotyping method for the currently prevalent vvIBDV, nvIBDV and attIBDV vaccine strains.

Genotyping is an essential process that enables characterization and monitoring of important virus strains in clinical (Islam et al., 2021; Michel and Jackwood, 2017; Wang et al., 2022b; Wang et al., 2021b). Understanding the relationships between strains is paramount for studying the epidemiology of IBDV and designing effective pathogen control strategies. Currently, several molecular detection methods based on Polymerase Chain Reaction (PCR) have been introduced to differentiate different pathotypes of IBDV, including Reverse Transcription PCR followed by sequencing analysis (RT-PCR-Seq), Multiplex RT-PCR, TaqMan qRT-PCR, RT-PCR-Restriction Fragment Length Polymorphism (RT-PCR-RFLP), and Whole Genome Sequencing-Next Generation Sequencing (WGS-NGS). Traditional sequencing analysis has consistently been the most accurate method for identifying the pathogenic types of IBDV because it can pinpoint the exact mutation sites, but the sequencing process is time-consuming and costly. Strain-specific multiplex RT-PCR technology has been developed in detecting and differentiating between field strains and vaccine strains of IBDV, but it is difficult to implement and promote due to its high requirements for specific primer design, and its amplification specificity is limited (Kusk et al., 2005). Additionally, real-time TaqMan qRT-PCR methods have only been developed to differentiate between nvIBDV and non-nvIBDV circulating in China, ignoring the dominant epidemic vvIBDV strain, and this method has been plagued by the expensive cost of fluorescent probes and is not suitable for large-scale detection in the clinic (Wang et al., 2023a). RT-PCR-RFLP is a widely reported differentiation technique using several different restriction enzymes (BstNI, MboI, SacI, SspI, BamHI, PstI, StyI, HhaI, StuI and BspMI) for restriction enzyme digestion to differentiate between the field strains and the attenuated vaccine strains of IBDV (Gomes et al., 2005; Jackwood and Sommer, 1997; Priyadharsini et al., 2016; Sapats and Ignjatovic, 2002; Sareyyüpoğlu and Akan, 2006; Ture et al., 1998; Wei et al., 2004). The method is not only complex to perform and time-consuming (at least 72 h) but also suffers from unreliable enzyme cutting sites and difficulties in interpreting some results. As for the WGS-NGS method, apart from being expensive and time-consuming, this approach is usually used to identify new and previously unknown variations, especially when the amplification region is highly variable. Furthermore, the emergence of nvIBDV in recent years, along with the previously dominant circulating strains of vvIBDV and the extensive used attenuated vaccine strains, has further complicated the identification and diagnosis of IBDV (He et al., 2014; Wang et al., 2022b; Zhang et al., 2022). Co-circulating with multiple pathotypes viruses can occur in the same chicken farm, so there is an urgent need to develop a method that can quickly and accurately identify the co-infected samples of different pathogenic IBDV types circulating in the clinic (including vvIBDV, nvIBDV and attIBDV), addressing the longstanding technical problem of rapidly and accurately identifying the pathogenic types of IBDV in the infected chickens.

Contrary to sequencing based methods, an advanced and breakthrough form of melting curve analysis, known as High-Resolution Melting curve analysis (HRM), is considered to make it easier to design highly differentiated HRM protocols and perform rapid, accurate and reliable HRM-based genotyping analysis, even detecting single nucleotide mutations and small insertions or deletions (Druml and Cichna-Markl, 2014; Er and Chang, 2012). The melting temperature (Tm) is used to analyze the differences in individual nucleotides in DNA sequences, which distinguishes samples based on the Tm differences generated by the amplification of DNA nucleotide sequences (Tong and Giffard, 2012). Due to its advantages of high-resolution capability, low-cost and high throughput, simple and fast operation, unrestricted detection sites, no need for additional processing, achieves true closed-tube operation and eliminates the common cross-contamination in other molecular typing methods, HRM technology has been widely applied in various fields, including human and animal disease diagnosis and agricultural pathogen detection (Ballin et al., 2019; Druml and Cichna-Markl, 2014; Morinha et al., 2013; Wittwer et al., 2003). In this study, we have innovatively established a HRM-qRT-PCR method for IBDV detection (Fig. 1), facilitating rapid and accurate diagnosis and pathotyping of IBDV, significantly enhancing the efficiency of clinical diagnostic and providing a valuable tool for disease detection and control.Fig. 1Graphical representation of the IBDV pathotyping by the HRM-qRT-PCR analysis.Fig 1

Materials and methods

Viruses, virus proliferation and titer determination

The common avian viruses isolated by our laboratory, including IBDVs, Avian leukosis virus (ALV), Marek's disease virus (MDV), Newcastle disease virus (NDV), Infectious laryngotracheitis virus (ILTV), Fowl adenovirus (FAdV-4), Reticuloendotheliosis virus (REV), Infectious bronchitis virus (IBV), Avian influenza virus (AIV), Avian metapneumovirusa (aMPV) and Avian reoviruses (ARV), were used in this study for specific validation. The clone purified viruses, including vvIBDV strain NN1172, nvIBDV strain GX-QZ191002 and attIBDV strain B87, were propagated and titrated for subsequent research using the previously described methods (Wang et al., 2022b).

DNA/RNA extraction and cDNA synthesis

Total DNA/RNA was extracted from the viruses using Spin Column Animal Total RNA/DNA Purification Kit (Sangon Biotech, China) following established protocols outlined in previous studies (Wang et al., 2023b). And the concentration of RNA was assessed using a BioDrop ultramicro spectrophotometer (BioDrop, England). Subsequently, 2 µg of RNA was reverse-transcribed into cDNA for each virus sample using the All-In-One 5 × RT MasterMix Kit (abm, Canada) adhering to the manufacturer's instructions.

Primer screening, design and evaluation

Based on extensive alignment and screening analysis of the full-genome sequences from the representative strains of different pathotypes of IBDV (Fig. 2), a pair of HRM primers (HRM-F: 5′-GCAGCAGCCAATGTGGAC-3′, HRM-R: 5′-CCGCTTGTGGTGCGTTTG-3′) was designed to cover the targeted variants within the region of 2450–2603 nt in segment A, where different pathotype strains exhibited significantly different GC content. The specificity of the primers was evaluated using the Primer-Blast program with the NCBI. The HRM primers were synthesized by BGI Technology Co., Ltd., Beijing, China.Fig. 2Nucleotide sequence comparison of the PCR amplification region between the different pathotypes of IBDV.Fig 2

Nucleotide sequence comparison of the PCR amplification region between the different pathotypes of IBDV.

Recombinant standard plasmid construction

Three recombinant plasmids used in the study were constructed by using the A2 fragments (portion of segment A) of the representing strains NN1172 (vvIBDV), GX-QZ191002 (nvIBDV) and B87 (attIBDV) of the different pathotypes. Using the cDNA of NN1172, GX-QZ191002 and B87 as the templates, PCR amplification of the A2 fragments was carried out with the primers (A2-F: 5′-GACCTCAACTCTCCCCTGAAG-3′, A2-R: 5′-GGGGACCCGCGAACGGATCCAATTTGGGAT-3′) as described in previously study (Wang et al., 2022b). The PCR products were purified by agarose electrophoresis gel recovery and ligated into the pMD18-T vector and the concentrations of the three recombinant plasmids were measured using a BioDrop microvolume spectrophotometer (BioDrop, England). The number of plasmid copies per unit volume was calculated using the following formula: Plasmid copy number (copies/μL) = [plasmid concentration (g/μL) × 6.02 × 1023]/[plasmid total fragment length (bp) × 660 g/mol]. The recombinant standard plasmids were diluted to a concentration gradient ranging from 6.12 × 1010 to 6.12 × 100 copies/μL for pMD18-T-NN1172, 6.11 × 1010 to 6.11 × 100 copies/μL for pMD18-T-GX-QZ191002, and 6.75 × 1010 to 6.75 × 100 copies/μL for pMD18-T-B87 by a 10-fold serial dilution method and stored at −20 °C for future use.

HRM-qRT-PCR amplification

The HRM-qRT-PCR amplification reaction system was performed and determined in the Roche LightCycle 96 System with HRM Analysis PreMix containing the EVAGreen fluorophore (TianGen, Beijing, China) by the optimization of primer concentrations (0.1 μM, 0.2 μM, 0.3 μM, 0.4 μM, 0.5 μM) and annealing temperatures (56 °C, 57 °C, 58 °C, 59 °C, 60 °C, 61 °C, 62 °C, 63 °C) according to the manufacturer's instructions. Briefly, the 20 μL reaction system contained 10 μL of 2 × HRM Analysis PreMix (with EVAGreen), 0.6 μL of each primer HRM-F/HRM-R (0.2 μM), 1 μL of cDNA/DNA, and 7.8 μL of ddH2O. The optimized cycling protocol for HRM analysis consisted of the following conditions: 95 °C for 2 min, followed by 40 cycles at 95 °C for 10 s, and 60 °C for 30 s. Subsequently, the temperature was increased at a slope of 0.04 °C per 1 s ranging from 75 °C to 95 °C, and the melting curves were established by using the collected fluorescent values. The results of HRM were analyzed using LightCycler® 96 software. To obtain the respective control-pathotypes curves, we used samples of the recombinant standard plasmids pMD18-T-NN1172, pMD18-T-GX-QZ191002 and pMD18-T-B87 of the three genotypes to perform the HRM-qRT-PCR analysis. All analyses of HRM-qRT-PCR were performed in triplicate, these positive standard plasmids were used as reference positive controls for pathotyping. Additionally, a negative control without DNA was included in all runs. Analysis of the PCR products was done by agarose gel electrophoresis to confirm.

Analytical specificity assay

With the DNA/cDNA samples of vvIBDV strain NN1172, nvIBDV strain GX-QZ191002 and attIBDV strain B87 as well as other common avian pathogens such as ALV, MDV, NDV, ILTV, FAdV-4, REV, IBV, AIV, aMPV, ARV as the templates and ddH2O as the negative control, the optimized reaction system and conditions were used to validate the specificity of HRM-qRT-PCR in the identification and pathotyping of IBDV.

Analytical sensitivity assay and standard curves

The detection limit of HRM-qRT-PCR reactions were verified by amplifying diluted standard plasmids at dilutions with concentrations ranging from 6.12 × 1010 to 6.12 × 100 copies/μL for pMD18-T-NN1172 (6.11 × 1010 to 6.11 × 100 copies/μL for pMD18-T-GX-QZ191002 and 6.75 × 1010 to 6.75 × 100 copies/μL for pMD18-T-B87) using the optimized HRM-qRT-PCR system. The standard curves were performed and established by amplifying the three standard plasmids with the dilutions of 108 to 104 copies/μL and calculated and plotted automatically by LightCycler® 96 software.

Analytical repeatability assay

The repeatability assay of the established HRM-qRT-PCR method was tested by intra- and inter-assay measurements. IBDV standard plasmids with the dilution concentration ranging from 108 to 104 copies/μL were used as the templates. Intra-assay and inter-assays were conducted by performing 3 parallel replicates of the assay on each of the five dilutions under the same reaction conditions to verify the repeatability and stability of the established HRM-qRT-PCR method for the detection of IBDV.

Identification and pathotyping of the mixed samples of three standard plasmids

Three standard plasmids were diluted to 50 ng/μL, and mixed in duals (pMD18-T-NN1172+pMD18-T-GX-QZ191002, pMD18-T-NN1172+pMD18-T-B87 and pMD18-T-GX-QZ191002+pMD18-T-B87) and in triple (pMD18-T-NN1172+pMD18-T-GX-QZ191002+pMD18-T-B87) equally as the tested samples, followed by HRM-qRT-PCR reactions based on the optimized reaction conditions.

Identification and pathotyping of the co-infection samples from the artificial infection models

A total of 40 non-vaccinated 28-day-old Three-yellow chickens were randomly divided into eight groups (n = 5). The birds from each group were housed separately and provided with free access to water and feed. The single infection groups A, B, and C were individually challenged with NN1172, B87 and QZ191002, respectively. Groups D, E, and F were the double co-infected groups, with birds co-infected with NN1172 and B87, NN1172 and GX-QZ191002, GX-QZ191002 and B87, respectively. Group G was triple co-infected with NN1172, GX-QZ191002 and B87 simultaneously. Each virus was inoculated orally with a dose of 105 TCID50/0.5 mL. Group H received 0.5 mL of PBS as the control group. All birds were euthanized at 7 days post-infection (at the age of 35 days), and bursa of Fabricius tissues were collected. Total RNA was isolated and reverse-transcribed into cDNA according to the previous study (Wang et al., 2023b). The established HRM-qRT-PCR method was used to identification and genotyping of the samples using cDNA as the template, and the detection accuracy of this method was calculated.

Large-scale epidemiological surveillance with HRM-qRT-PCR

Initially, in order to determine the large-scale application of HRM-qRT-PCR in the field, a total of 150 bursal samples were collected from Yellow-chicken suspected having IBDV infection. In addition, the samples were also tested for IBDV by traditional nested RT-PCR and sequencing, respectively, as mentioned in previously described (Wei et al., 2004). Finally, another 468 bursa samples collected from 2019 to 2023 were further tested using HRM-qRT-PCR method for large-scale epidemiological surveillance in a field situation, and the positive rates of samples from different years and the percentage of different pathogenic strains in positive samples were calculated.

Using standard plasmids as the templates, HRM-qRT-PCR amplification was performed with the optimized reaction conditions. Identification and genotyping of the co-infection samples from artificial infection chicken models

As shown in Fig. 7, the identification results clearly demonstrate that bursa samples of each group exhibit distinct melting curves corresponding to the challenged viruses of the respective group. Furthermore, the viral pathotypes detected in these infected samples are consistent with the results obtained through RT-PCR amplification and sequencing. Specifically, samples from Group A (NN1172), Group B (B87), and Group C (GX-QZ191002) displayed single-peaked melting curves (15/15) due to the distinct Tm of the viruses of each pathotype. Discussion

Since 2016, novel variant IBDV strains were first reported in China, leading to widespread outbreaks and transmission (Fan et al., 2019; Wang et al., 2022a). These variant strains mainly manifest as subclinical infections, making it difficult to accurately diagnose these viruses in clinic (Aliyu et al., 2021; Chen et al., 2022; Huang et al., 2022; Legnardi et al., 2023b; Lian et al., 2022; Myint et al., 2021; Thai et al., 2021; Tomás et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2022a; Wang et al., 2022b; Xu et al., 2020). Currently, with the frequent exchange of agricultural products between countries/regions, the risk of transmission of different pathotype viruses has significantly increased. Additionally, coupled with the major circulating strain vvIBDV and a large number of attenuated vaccines have been promoted recently in China (He et al., 2019; He et al., 2014; Zhang et al., 2022), which makes the IBDV infection further complicated in the field. The co-circulating of vvIBDV, nvIBDV and attIBDV have been confirmed in China, resulting in clinical symptoms and pathological changes that are difficult to distinguish on-site, which poses a challenge to rapidly identify different pathotypes based solely on clinical indicators (Jiang et al., 2021; Wang et al., 2022b). Therefore, it is necessary to develop new molecular diagnostic and genotyping techniques that can accurately and rapidly differentiate and diagnose the three major prevalent pathotype strains, including vvIBDV, nvIBDV, and attIBDV in clinical IBD outbreaks, which is essential for surveillance, timely prevention and treatment of this disease.

Typing is essential to detect the emergence and limit the spread of high-risk strains in poultry farming environments. Unfortunately, the most common typing methods (RT-PCR-Seq, Multiplex RT-PCR, TaqMan qRT-PCR, RT-PCR-RFLP and WGS-NGS) are both expensive and time-consuming, restricting the application in real-time monitoring of different pathotype viruses in clinical (Gomes et al., 2005; Jackwood and Sommer, 1997; Kusk et al., 2005; Priyadharsini et al., 2016; Sapats and Ignjatovic, 2002; Sareyyüpoğlu and Akan, 2006; Ture et al., 1998; Wang et al., 2023a; Wei et al., 2004). HRM-qRT-PCR analysis can be used to perform cost-effective and rapid approach for pathogen genotyping, however, developing highly differentiated protocols is challenging (Dufkova et al., 2023; Perini et al., 2022). In this study, a closed-tube method based on the HRM-qRT-PCR technique was developed for the identification and pathotyping of different pathotype IBDV strains, as an alternative for rapid identification and pathotyping suitable for routine clinical practice. It includes a pair of specific primers target for the region of 2450–2603 nt, which exhibit significantly different GC content, in segment A of the three viruses of different pathotypes (Fig. 2). This design results in good reproducibility and easy performance, making it suitable for identification and pathotyping a large number of the field samples while maintaining a high differentiative capability. The assay features distinct melting curves (Fig. 3) and high sensitivity (Fig. 5), with detection limits of 61.2, 61.1, and 67.5 copies/μL for vvIBDV, nvIBDV, and attIBDV, respectively (Fig. 5D–F). The approach was highly specific, with no inter-genotypes cross-reactivity among different pathotypes of IBDV and no reactivity to other common avian pathogens (including ALV-J, MDV, NDV, ILTV, FAdV-4, REV, IBV, AIV, aMPV and ARV) tested in this study (Fig. 4). Finally, we optimized the reaction mixtures and running parameters for each of the three pathotypes of IBDV strains, allowing simultaneous typing of these clinically important viruses and significantly reducing laboratory work and analysis time requirements. The entire amplification process of this method takes only 60 min, and 96 samples can be detected simultaneously (the maximum number of samples by Roche 96 detection). In comparison to the conventional identification and pathotyping methods, such as the cumbersome steps and huge workload of RT-PCR-RFLP/RT-PCR-Seq (usually requiring 72 h) (Gomes et al., 2005; Jackwood and Sommer, 1997; Priyadharsini et al., 2016; Sapats and Ignjatovic, 2002; Sareyyüpoğlu and Akan, 2006; Ture et al., 1998; Wei et al., 2004), this method has an incomparable advantage and particularly suitable for real-time large-scale epidemiological surveillance.

HRM-qRT-PCR analysis can be used to distinguish between different pathotype strains in co-infection samples by generating conventional melting curves with two or more main peaks. This new approach was tested on plasmid-mixed samples and 35 samples with co-infections of different IBDV pathotype strains, and the HRM-qRT-PCR results were found to be highly consistent with the initial assumptions when the assay was developed (Fig. 6), indicating that the Tm values of each pathotype were unique. All single or double co-infection samples generate either one or two main peaks corresponding to the Tm values of each pathotype, resulting in visually distinct melting curve plots from each other (Fig. 7). Even in complex cases involving co-infection with three different pathotypes viruses, the standardized HRM-qRT-PCR curves can be clearly distinguish these different viruses, consistently and accurately identifying and pathotyping the viruses in all replicate samples within each group, with an accuracy rate of up to 100 % (35/35) (Fig. 7).

In order to further confirm the large-scale application of HRM-qRT-PCR in the field, a large number of bursa samples were collected from Yellow-chickens suspected of being infected with IBDV. The results showed that the HRM-qRT-PCR method had a higher positive detection rate compared to nest RT-PCR (Table 2). The HRM-qRT-PCR method not only identified three traditional pathogenic types: vvIBDV, nvIBDV, and attIBDV, but also successfully identified co-infection samples of nvIBDV + attIBDV and vvIBDV + attIBDV (Table 4), indicating that this newly developed method can quickly identify pathogenic types and co-infection samples in clinical practice. In addition, this method breaks through the limitations of traditional pathotyping methods, requiring only one reaction to achieve not only enabled rapid pathotyping of the viruses, but also facilitated quantitative analysis of the viruses within the samples (Fig. 5), greatly enhancing the efficiency of differential diagnostic in the clinic, making it a valuable tool for pathotyping during IBD outbreak investigations.

In conclusion, this study presents an innovative pathotyping technology for differentiating and identifying vvIBDV, nvIBDV and attIBDV based on the HRM-qRT-PCR analysis with a closed-tube system for the first time. This system includes new protocols for the target gene selection, primer design and HRM data analysis, offering a strong discriminatory capacity while remaining performance-friendly and requiring short processing time. This makes it attractive for potential pathotyping of large epidemiological surveillance. HRM-qRT-PCR analysis accurately identifies and differentiates all single or co-infected samples generate one or more main peaks corresponding to the Tm values of each pathotype strain, resulting in visually distinct melting curve plots. In addition, the method overcomes the limitations of traditional pathotyping methods, requires only one reaction to achieve not only enabled rapid pathotyping of the viruses, but also facilitated quantitative analysis of viruses within the samples, making it well-suited as a real-time epidemiological surveillance method during IBD outbreaks.

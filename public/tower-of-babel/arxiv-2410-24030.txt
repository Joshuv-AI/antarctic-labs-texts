A twist on ring morphisms and crepant contractions
Marina Godinho

Abstract
. Given a surjective ring morphism, this paper constructs the twist functor around the induced derived restriction of scalars functor. We prove that the twist around ring morphisms is a derived autoequivalence in two settings: (1) Twists for Gorenstein orders, (2) twists induced by Frobenius exact categories. As a corollary, it is shown that the noncommutative twist introduced by Donovan and Wemyss is in fact a spherical twist. We then use the technology developed in (1) and (2) to obtain new spherical twists for very singular schemes, and discuss how our result extends previous works on spherical twists induced by crepant contractions.

Introduction

Autoequivalence groups of derived categories, through their connections with the stability manifold and mirror symmetry, have become important objects in algebraic geometry and representation theory. The description of these autoequivalence groups in general is difficult, and so many authors have contributed to constructing autoequivalences for certain classes of derived categories (See, for example,
[

In this work, we are interested in constructing autoequivalences as spherical twists and cotwists. Twists around spherical objects were introduced by Seidel and Thomas
[

in order to obtain derived autoequivalences of complex smooth projective varieties. The construction was generalised by various authors to twists around spherical functors. For a survey on the development of this technology, see, for example,

Consider a functor

between triangulated categories which admits a right adjoint

and a left adjoint

1.1.
Twists around ring morphisms

The setting of this paper is that of twists around ring morphisms, in view of their applications to geometric settings induced by crepant contractions. First, we will use this technology to answer the open question of whether the noncommutative twist functor introduced by Donovan and Wemyss

4.
Frobenius exact setting and fundamentals

With geometric applications in mind, we would like to construct a spherical twist around the restriction of scalars functor when the algebra
B
is not finite dimensional. As a result, in this section, we introduce a different algebraic setting which still allows us to construct a spherical twist in more general geometric settings.

Given an object
X
in a Frobenius exact category
\mathcal{E}
satisfying mild conditions, we will specify when the restriction of scalars functor
F
induced by quotient morphism
\uppi\colon\End_{\mathcal{E}}(X)\to\uEnd_{\mathcal{E}}(X)
is spherical. The key point is roughly that
F
is spherical if the action of the suspension functor on the object is periodic up to additive closure. This is proved in
section

5
.

In this section, we introduce the setup and some fundamental results necessary for
section

5

up to additive closure if and only if
X
has relatively spherical properties. This characterisation is important to our applications because relatively spherical properties are easier to control in the geometric setting. In the process,
section

4.2
constructs what we call partially minimal projective resolutions.

4.1.

Recall that an additive category

Appendix B
Semi-perfect self-injective algebras

This appendix recalls some classic results about semi-perfect and self-injective algebras.

Let
A
be semi-perfect. Then,
A=\bigoplus_{i\in I}P_{i}
where
P_{i}
are indecomposable projective
A
-modules with local endomorphism rings. Note that
\{P_{i}\}_{i\in I}
are all of the indecomposable projective
A
-modules up to isomorphism.

Lemma B.1
.

If
A
is a semi-perfect algebra, then it has finitely many simple modules.

Proof.

It follows from
[
Kra15
, 3.6]
that given a simple
A
module
S
, there is an
i
such that
\uppi\colon P_{i}\to S
is the projective cover of
S
. We claim that there is a bijection between isomorphism classes of indecomposable projectives and isomorphism classes of simples.

Suppose that
S
and
S^{\prime}
are simple modules which admit projective covers
\uppi\colon P_{i}\to S
and
\uppi^{\prime}\colon P_{i}\to S^{\prime}
. Then,
S\cong P_{i}/\ker\uppi
and
S^{\prime}\cong P_{i}/\ker\uppi^{\prime}
so that
\ker\uppi
and
\ker\uppi^{\prime}
are maximal submodules of
P_{i}
. Therefore, either
\ker\uppi=\ker\uppi^{\prime}
or
\ker\uppi+\ker\uppi^{\prime}=P_{i}
. In the first case, it follows that
S\cong S^{\prime}
. In the latter case, since both
\ker\uppi
and
\ker\uppi^{\prime}
are essential, it must be that
\ker\uppi=\ker\uppi^{\prime}=0
so that
S\cong S^{\prime}
.
∎

Hence, we denote
S_{i}
the simple
A
-module whose projective cover is
P_{i}
.

An algebra
A
is said to be self-injective if it is injective as a module over itself. When
A
is Noetherian, then being self-injective is equivalent to all projective
A
-modules being injective.

Remark B.2
.

Let
A
be a semi-perfect self-injective algebra. Then,
P_{i}
is also an indecomposable injective
A
-module. It follows that its socle is a simple module which, by
B.1
, must be
S_{j}
for some
j\in I
. The permutation of
I

i\mapsto j
is called the
Nakayama permutation
of
A
.

When
A
is is self-injective and finite dimensional, then the category
\modu A
of finitely generated modules over
A
is equipped with an automorphism

\mathcal{N}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}-\otimes_{A}DA\colon\modu A\to\modu A

called the
Nakayama functor
. Here, the dual
DA=\Hom_{k}(A,k{}_{k})
is equipped with its usual bimodule structure: for
f\in D(B)
and
x\in B
,
(a\cdot f\cdot b)(x)=f(bxa)
. The Nakayama functor has the quasi-inverse
[
Iva11
, 3.3]

\mathcal{N}^{-1}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}-\otimes_{A}\Hom_{B}(DA,A)\colon\modu A\to\modu A

It turns out that self-injectivity for finite dimensional algebras is equivalent to the property that finitely generated indecomposable projective modules coincide with finitely generated indecomposable injectives
[
SY11
, IV 3.7]
. It thus follows that
\mathcal{N}
and
\mathcal{N}^{-1}
are exact and extend to automorphisms of
\Dcat^{\mathrm{b}}(\modu A)
.

Appendix C
Frobenius exact categories

A
Frobenius exact category

\mathcal{E}
is an exact category which has enough projective and injective objects and is such that
\proj\mathcal{E}=\inj\mathcal{E}
. For more details, see
[
Hap88
, I.2]
.This appendix recalls some well-known results about Frobenius categories.

The stable category
\mathcal{D}
of a a Frobenius category
\mathcal{E}
is defined to be the category whose objects are the same as the objects of
\mathcal{E}
and the hom-space between objects
X,Y\in\mathcal{D}
is
\Hom_{\mathcal{D}}(X,Y)=\mathcal{E}(X,Y)/[\proj\mathcal{E}]
where
[\proj\mathcal{E}]
denotes the ideal of morphisms factoring through a projective object in
\mathcal{E}
. It turns out that
\mathcal{D}
carries a triangulated structure.

For the rest of this section, let
\mathcal{E}
be a Frobenius exact category with an object
P
such that
\proj\mathcal{E}=\add P
. Let
\mathcal{D}
denote its stable category.

Lemma C.1

(
[
Hap88
, I.2.3,I.2.7]
)
.

Any exact sequence in
\mathcal{E}

0\to A\to B\to C\to 0

gives rise to a triangle

A\to B\to C\to^{+}

in
\mathcal{D}
. Moreover, every triangle in
\mathcal{D}

X\to Y\to Z\to^{+}

lifts to an exact sequence in
\mathcal{E}

0\to X\to Y\oplus Q\to Z\to 0

where
Q\in\add P
.

This lemma has the following easy corollary.

Corollary C.2
.

Suppose that
X,Y\in\mathcal{D}
are isomorphic. Then, there exists a
Q_{0},Q_{1}\in\add P
such that
Q_{0}\oplus X\cong Q_{1}\oplus Y
in
\mathcal{E}
.

Lemma C.3

(
[
Dug12
, 2.2]
)
.

For any map
f\colon X\to Y\in\mathcal{E}
, there exists an object
Z\in\mathcal{E}
and a projective
Q\in\proj\mathcal{E}
such that

0\to Z\to X\oplus Q\xrightarrow{(f,p)}Y\to 0

is exact in
\mathcal{E}
.

The following is well-known.

Lemma C.4
.

Given
X,Y\in\mathcal{E}
,
\Ext^{1}_{\mathcal{E}}(X,Y)=0
if and only if
\Ext^{1}_{\mathcal{D}}(X,Y)=0
.

For an object
X\in\mathcal{E}
, write
\Uplambda\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathcal{E}(X,X)
and
\Uplambda_{\mathrm{con}}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathcal{D}(X,X)
.

Lemma C.5
.

For any
A,B\in\modu\Uplambda_{\mathrm{con}}
,

\Ext^{1}_{\Uplambda_{\mathrm{con}}}(A,B)\cong\Ext^{1}_{\Uplambda}(A,B)

Proof.

This is standard. Since multiplying by idempotents is exact, we may view
\modu\Uplambda_{\mathrm{con}}
as an extension closed subcategory of
\modu\Uplambda
, and so the result follows.
∎

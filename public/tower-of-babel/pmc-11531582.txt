S-nitrosoglutathione reductase disfavors cadmium tolerance in shoots of Arabidopsis
Authors: Meiyan Guan, Xiaolong Zheng, Yaxin Zhu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
S-nitrosoglutathione reductase (GSNOR) is involved in the response to cadmium (Cd) exposure. In this study, the plants of mutant (gsnor1-3) with lossing-function of- and over-expression (GSNOROE5) of GSNOR were used to clear the role of GSNOR in Cd tolerance. GSNOR activity increased through upregulating the expression of the AtGSNOR gene and protein in Arabidopsis thaliana under Cd stress, which attenuated Cd tolerance. Oxidative damage was more serious in GSNOROE5 and was alleviated in gsnor1-3 under Cd stress, compared with Col-0. Induction of GSNOR facilitated H2O2 accumulation but inhibited catalase (CAT) activity in shoots under Cd stress. This phenotype was eliminated by 3-amino-1,2,4-triazole (3-AT), a CAT inhibitor. In addition, the expressions of AtCAT1 and AtCAT2 were down-regulated with increasing GSNOR activity under Cd stress. This suggested that GSNOR was involved in the accumulation of hydrogen peroxide (H2O2) through regulating CAT expression and activity under Cd exposure. Furthermore, Cd tolerance and CAT activity were improved by spraying S-nitrosoglutathione (GSNO) onto the surface of the leaves. The in vitro activity of CAT increased with GSNO concentration until a GSNO/CAT ratio of 2 was reached. Thus, CAT activity was relative to GSNOR through regulating the expression and S-nitrosylation level of proteins. In summary, the Cd-induced promotion of GSNOR activity aggravated Cd toxicity in plants by mediating H2O2 accumulation controlled by CAT.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-024-77759-y.

Introduction

Cadmium (Cd) is a nonessential heavy metal that is toxic to plants. Reactive oxygen species (ROS) are formed and accumulate rapidly in plants under Cd stress1,2. ROS are generated and scavenged continuously in cells to maintain a homeostatic state in plants1. However, the defence system used to scavenge ROS is inhibited by Cd toxicity, which leads to a surge of ROS in cells followed by severe oxidative injury and accelerated cell death3. This can have adverse effects on the normal growth and development of the plant4.

ROS exist in various forms of activated oxygen, such as hydrogen peroxide (H2O2), superoxide anion radicals (O2·−), hydroxyl radicals (·OH) and single oxygen (1O2)1,5. Of these, O2·− and H2O2 are the main types of ROS. O2·− is formed by NADPH-driven 1-electron reduction of molecular oxygen (O2) and converted to H2O2 by superoxide dismutases (SOD)5,6. Previous studies have shown that H2O2 over-accumulation was the main contributor to cell death caused by oxidative damage in plants under stress1,6–8. Cadmium stress leads to an excess accumulation of H2O2, as well as changes in the antioxidant levels in plants7,9. The concentration of H2O2 was increased to 200% in the roots of tobacco plants under Cd stress compared with those under control conditions9. Therefore, it is important to maintain H2O2 at an appropriate level to allow plant growth under Cd stress.

Plants have enzymatic and nonenzymatic anti-oxidative systems to remove excess ROS. Among the antioxidants, peroxidase (POD) and CAT eliminate H2O2, ascorbate peroxidase (APX) removes H2O2 by using ascorbic acid (ASC) as an electron donor and the ascorbic acid and L-Glutathione reduced (ASC-GSH) cycle also plays an essential role in scavenging H2O21. CAT is the main scavenger of H2O2 and its activity was changed in plants, such as wheat, tobacco and rice, under Cd exposure9–12. However, the underlying mechanism of how Cd influence CAT activity is unclear.

Nitric oxide (NO) is a multifunctional gaseous signalling molecular, that is involved in various responses in plants to stress, including Cd stress13–15. Improving NO levels in plants promotes Cd tolerance by decreasing the accumulation of H2O2 and malondialdehyde (MDA)16. Treating plants with sodium nitroprusside (SNP, a NO donor) strengthens their antioxidant capability by increasing the activity of antioxidant systems, such as CAT and APX16,17. Most reports show that the over-accumulation of NO is induced under Cd exposure15,18. However, it is harmful to plants because of the nitration induced by excess NO accumulation19–21. The activity of some enzymes is inhibited after protein tyrosine nitration20,22. Therefore, the homeostasis of NO is crucial to the improvement of Cd tolerance in plants. However, the exact mechanism of NO metabolism is still unclear.

There are some proteins involved in the processes of synthesis and scavenging of NO in plants, such as nitrate reductase (NR), NO synthase (NOS)-like protein and non-symbiotic hemoglobin (AHb1)14,15,18,23. Among these proteins, NR is a NO biosynthesis-related protein that is not involved in NO accumulation under Cd stress23. The enzymatic activity of NOS has been demonstrated in plant extracts, but its encoding genes have not been identified in plants23. NO is also scavenged in plants by S-nitrosoglutathione reductase (GSNOR) and non-symbiotic haemoglobins (nsHbs, Ahb1)14,24. Interestingly, our previous study has demonstrated that the function of AHb1 in scavenging NO is inhibited under Cd stress18. Thus, AHb1 was not involved in the NO homeostasis in plants under Cd stress. Therefore, GSNOR may be a major contributor to maintaining the homeostasis of NO in plants in response to Cd stress. It is necessary to explore more potential functional pathways associated with NO in the Cd response process.

S-nitrosoglutathione (GSNO) has been recognized as a natural donor of NO in plants24,25. Thus, GSNO may act upstream of NO and participate in the NO signalling pathway. Nevertheless, it was indicated that GSNO contributes downstream of NO to respond to Fe deficiency26. Considering the similar chemical characteristics of Fe and Cd, we hypothesized that GSNO may be involved in the response in plants to Cd stress. It should be noted that GSNOR can degrade GSNO into glutathione disulfide (GSSG) and ammonia (NH3) in an NADPH-dependent de-nitrosylation way24,25. In addition, GSNOR has been previously verified to inhibit Cd uptake by reducing NO accumulation in roots18. NO has been reported to impact Cd tolerance in plant shoots16,27. Therefore, NO regulated by GSNOR may also be involved in the Cd response in shoots. However, there is a lack of information available about the relationship between GSNOR and Cd tolerance in shoots.

GSNOR is involved in responding to multiple stresses through regulating the signalling, metabolism and accumulation of H2O2 in plants28–30. For example, the GSNOR mutant gsnor1-3 is more tolerant to paraquat compared with Col-0 plants, and there is no significant difference shown in gsnor1-3 after complementing the function of GSNOR31. Thus, GSNOR is involved in tolerance to paraquat stress, which will induce a burst of ROS, especially H2O232. GSNOR is also involved in the production of H2O2 and NO during pathogen challenges33. Additionally, GSNOR1-controlled nitrosation is a key factor linking intracellular H2O2 to activation of the salicylic acid (SA) pathway29. Moreover, GSNOR is found as a contributor to thermotolerance through modulating high temperature-induced apoplastic H2O2 in Solanum lycopersicum30. Furthermore, S-nitrosothiol signalling controlled by GSNOR is involved in regulating the H2O2 metabolism of Zn-stressed Arabidopsis28. Recently, we found that GSNOR was involved in the regulation of Cd uptake by the roots of Arabidopsis18. Considering that H2O2 is the major ROS induced by Cd stress, whether GSNOR can influence H2O2 accumulation in plants under Cd stress needs to be further studied.

In this study, Arabidopsis seedlings were used to investigate the above hypothesis. The gsnor1-3 and GSNOROE with a Columbia (Col-0) background were used as experimental materials. It was found that the induction of GSNOR facilitated H2O2 accumulation in shoots under Cd stress. The negative regulation of GSNOR by CAT activity further aggravated Cd toxicity in plants.

Materials and methods

Plant and cultivation

Arabidopsis thaliana plants were used in this study. The gsnor1-3 (GABI_315d11), pGSNOR::GSNOR-GFP/gsnor1-3 and GSNOROE plants had a Col-0 background, OE indicates the the overexpression line of GSNOR. The hot5-4 plants had a Wassileskija (WS) background. All the seeds were kindly provided by Dr. Jin (Zhejiang University, China). The method of plant cultivation was as described previously18. Briefly, the seeds were germinated on a nylon net with a sponge that floated on a nutrient solution. Then, 7-day-old seedlings were transferred to sand cultivation with nutrient solution for 10 days. All seedlings were washed with water and transferred to a nutrient solution for another 7 days, seedlings with similar growth status were selected for experiments after a pretreatment. The nutrient solutions were renewed every day. The time of treatment and the concentrations of Cd and GSNO were according to experimental settings. In this study, 0 and 40 µM CdCl2 were applied in the nutrient solution, 0 and 200 µM GSNO (pH 7.0), 0 and 100 µM 3-AT were sprayed on the surface of shoots.

Measurement of Cd concentration

After treatment, the shoots of plants were rinsed with deionized water and dried in an oven at 65℃ for 24 h. The samples were wet digested as described previously18. The digested solution was further diluted and the concentration of Cd levels was analyzed by inductively coupled plasma mass spectrometry (ICP-MS, X Series 2; Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA).

The phenotype analysis

The phenotype of shoots with or without Cd exposure was observed after 7 days of treatment. The damage in leaves and petioles was evaluated by staining with Evans blue solution and recorded by a Nikon digital camera.

GSNOR activity analysis

25 days seedlings of Col-0 plants were treated with or without 40 µM CdCl2 for 3 days. The time for Cd exposure treatment and measurement of GSNOR activity were based on and described in our previous study18.

GFP fluorescence analysis

GSNOR-GFP expression in the leaf epidermal of pGSNOR-GFP-gsnor1-3 was observed by using an epifluorescence microscope (Ni-U, Nikon, excitation 488 nm, emission collected 495–575 nm). The GFP fluorescence of samples was measured by Image J (La Jolla, CA, USA). The fluorescence value of the control group acted as a reference to measure the relative fluorescence of GFP under Cd stress.

NO determination

In situ measurement of NO in the mature leaves of Col-0, gsnor1-3, GSNOROE5 plants. NO production was visualized after treatment using DAF-FM DA, a probe commonly used to detect NO in plants. The shoots were incubated with 10 µM DAF-FM DA for 30 min and rinsed with 20 mM HEPES/NaOH buffer (pH 7.4) for 30 min. Samples were observed under a Nikon Eclipse E600 epifluorescence microscope (HB-10103 AF-Hg; Nikon). The intensity of green fluorescence in the images was quantified by Image J (La Jolla, CA, USA).

Histochemical and cytochemical analysis

Plasma membrane integrity was evaluated by staining plant tissues with Evans blue solution as described previously18. The ROS in leaves was imaged in situ using 2,7-dichlorodihy drofluorescein diacetate (DCFH-DA, CAS: S0033; Beyotime Institute of Biotechnology, Beijing, China). Briefly, the leaves were treated with 10 µM DCFH-DA in 20 mM HEPES-NaOH buffers (pH 7.4) for 30 min, washed three times in fresh HEPES-NaOH buffers, and then observed by using an epifluorescence microscope.

Measure of MDA concentration

Fresh shoots (0.5 g) were frozen in liquid nitrogen and ground. Tricarboxylic acid (TCA. 1 mL 1.0% w/v) was added to the powder, homogenates were centrifuged at 20,000×g for 15 min and supernatants were collected for analyzing MDA concentration. Briefly, 0.5 ml supernatants were added into 3 ml of 20% thiobarbituric acid (TBA). The reaction mixtures were kept in the water bath at 95℃ for 50 min and terminated by cooling to room temperature. After centrifugation at 10,000×g for 10 min, the supernatant was taken to measure the absorbance at 532 nm and 600 nm, respectively. The concentration of MDA was calculated using the extinction coefficient 155 mΜ−1cm−1g− 1 FW.

Analysis of O2· − and H2O2 concentration

Shoots tissues were collected and ground by a homogenizer under liquid nitrogen. Samples were resuspended in 2 mL PBS (50 mM, pH 7.8). The supernatant was taken after centrifugation at 12,000×g for 20 min under 4℃. The H2O2 concentration was measured using a hydrogen peroxide content assay kit (Cat: AKA0009C; Boxbio Institute of Biotechnology, Beijing, China). The concentration of O2·− was determined using an assay kit (Cat: S0060, Beyotime Institute of Biotechnology). Processes were conducted according to the instructions that accompanied the instrument.

Assays of SOD, POD, APX and CAT activities

Total superoxide dismutase (SOD) and guaiacol peroxidase (POD) activities were determined as described in a previous study34. The activity of total CAT (consist of CAT1, CAT2 and CAT3) was measured using a catalase assay kit (CAS: S0051, Beyotime Institute of Biotechnology) following the manufacturer’s instructions.

In vitro incubation experiment for CAT activity analysis

Considering the high conservation of CAT protein between species, CAT from bovine (CAS: S0082, Beyotime Institute of Biotechnology) was used to test the effect of GSNO on CAT activity in vitro. GSNO was added to a solution of 20 µM/L CAT, until a treatment concentration of 0, 10, 20, 40, 60, 80 and 100 µM/L. Then, the CAT activity of the mixture solution was measured after a reaction in ice for 30 min.

GSH and ASC concentration analysis

Shoot tissues were collected and ground by a homogenizer on ice. Samples were resuspended in 0.2 mL HPO3 (25% w/v) and 1.5 mL PBS-EDTA (pH 8.0). The supernatants were taken after 10,000×g centrifugation for 20 min at 4℃. Supernatant was collected and set on ice for measuring the concentration of GSH and ASC. The concentration of GSH was determined using an assay kit (CAS: S0053, Beyotime Institute of Biotechnology) according to the manufacturer’s instructions, and GSH (L-Glutathione reduced) (Acmec, CAS: 70-18-8) was used for creating a standard curve. The concentration of ASC (reduced) was determined according to Zhang and Kirham35. In brief, 0.75 mL PBS (150 mM, pH 8.0) and 0.3 mL sterile water were added to 0.3 mL supernatant and mixed well. Then, 2.1 mL of colour development solution was added to the above mixture. A microplate reader was used to measure the absorbance at 525 nm after incubating the mixture for 40 min at 40℃. The colour development solution consisted of 0.6 mL phosphoric acid solution (44% w/v), 0.6 mL TCA (10% w/v), 0.6 mL 2,2’-bipyridine (4%) and 0.3 mL FeCl3 solution (3%).

RNA isolation and gene expression analysis

Total RNA was isolated from 100 mg of shoot tissue using RNA Plus (CAS: 9108, Takara, Otsu, Japan). The processes for RNA extraction and reverse transcription were as described previously36. All cDNA samples were performed in an MJ Opticon TM2 RT-PCR system (MJ Research, MA, USA). UBQ-10 was used as a reference to determine the relative expression of genes. The primers used for the RT-PCR are listed in Table 1. The expression of genes was normalized by the expression of UBQ10, and the relative expression of GSNOR in shoots under Cd stress compared with that in shoots under Cd-free conditions was analyzed using the 2(−ΔΔCt) method37.

Statistical analysis

Data were expressed as the mean ± standard error. Statistical analyses used two tails student’s t-test and two-way analysis of variance (ANOVA).

Results

GSNOR limits Cd tolerance in shoots

GSNOR is reported to be involved in multiple abiotic stress conditions. Previously, our study has found that the induction of GSNOR under Cd stress reduced Cd uptake through the inhibition of NO accumulation and IRT1 expression in roots, which reduced Cd accumulation in shoots and roots (Fig. S1A). No significant difference was observed in Cd transfer ratio from root to shoot among Col-0, gsnor1-3 and GSNOROE5 (Fig. S1B). Which suggested Cd transfer from root to shoot was not impacted by GSNOR activity among different genotype.

Interestingly, we found that GSNOR had a negative impact on Cd tolerance in shoots. Plant biomass was inhibited under Cd stress. Among the three lines, gsnor1-3 had the highest relative biomass under Cd stress and GSNOROE5 had the lowest compared with the control (Fig. 1A). A similar tendency of relative biomass under Cd stress was found between wild type and hot5-4, another mutant with GSNOR loss of function and possessing a WS background (Fig. S2). The accumulation of MDA in shoots was increased, especially in GSNOROE5 and Col-0 plants (Fig. S3). In addition, leaves went from green to purple and wilted under 40 µM CdCl2 treatment for 5 days (Fig. S4). However, this phenomenon became weaker in the gsnor1-3 mutant and was obvious in the transgenic line that over-expressed GSNOR (GSNOROE5). Evans blue staining in leaves of the three lines treated with CdCl2 revealed that the most serious oxidative damage occurred in GSNOROE5 and only slight oxidative damage occurred in gsnor1-3. However, no significant difference was found in the Evans blue stained leaves of the three lines under normal conditions (Fig. 1B). Considering that the highest GSNOR activity existed in GSNOROE5 and little or no GSNOR activity occurred in gsnor1-3 plants (Fig. S5), these results suggested that a lack of GSNOR function led to enhanced Cd tolerance.

Cd stress induces GSNOR activity in shoots

To discover how GSNOR responds to Cd stress, GSNOR activity and gene expression of GSNOR in shoots of Col-0 plants were analyzed after exposure to 40 µM CdCl2 for 3 days. As shown in Fig. 2A, the activity of GSNOR in shoots under Cd stress increased about four-fold compared with normal conditions. GSNOR expression in shoots under Cd stress was up-regulated by 130% of that under control conditions (Fig. 2B). The fluorescence intensity of the GSNOR-GFP fusion protein was increased by about 50% under Cd stress (Fig. 2C,D). These results suggested that the activity of GSNOR in shoots was increased through up-regulating the expression of its gene and protein.

GSNOR promotes oxidative stress in shoots

The oxidative damage induced by Cd stress is caused by a burst of ROS, especially H2O2 and O2·−. Thus, the levels of ROS, H2O2 and O2·− were analyzed in plants under Cd stress. ROS accumulation in leaves was stimulated by Cd stress (Fig. 3A). There was no significant difference in the accumulation of ROS in shoots among Col-0, gsnor1-3 and GSNOROE5 under control conditions. However, under Cd stress, ROS accumulation was reduced in the leaves of gsnor1-3 compared with Col-0, but significantly increased in GSNOROE5 compared to Col-0 (Fig. 3A). Therefore, GSNOR activity positively regulated ROS accumulation in shoots under Cd stress.

The levels of H2O2 and O2·− were significantly increased under Cd stress. No differences in the concentration of H2O2 were displayed in Col-0, gsnor1-3 and GSNOROE5 under control conditions. However, an obvious difference in H2O2 levels appeared among three lines under Cd stress (Fig. 3B). The concentration of H2O2 in gsnor1-3 was reduced to 80% while that in GSNOROE5 increased to 140% compared with Col-0 plants (Fig. 3B). This suggested that over-accumulation of H2O2 in shoots induced by Cd stress was positively regulated by GSNOR. Moreover, more O2·− accumulated in shoots with an increase in GSNOR activity, but no interaction between Cd treatment and genotype appeared in O2·− overproduction (Fig. 3C). It should be noticed that H2O2 concentration in shoots was higher than 1 µM.g− 1 FW, while the concentration of O2·− was less than 80 nM.g− 1 FW under Cd stress (Fig. 3B,C). Which suggested H2O2 accumulated in shoot is much higher than O2·− under Cd stress. Therefore, the oxidative damage of leaves regulated by GSNOR under Cd stress was mainly caused by the over-accumulation of H2O2.

GSNOR promotes H2O2 accumulation by inhibiting CAT activity under Cd stress

As H2O2 is catalyzed by SOD from O2·−, SOD activity in the shoots of Col-0, gsnor1-3 and GSNOROE5 was measured to understand how GSNOR affects H2O2 synthesis under Cd stress. As shown in Fig. S6, a weak correlation was detected between the SOD activity and genotype (Fig. S6B). This indicated that the accumulation of H2O2 was not negatively regulated by GSNOR.

The plant antioxidant defence system consists of nonenzymatic antioxidants and antioxidant enzymes. Compared with Col-0 plants, the shoots of gsnor1-3 had the highest total antioxidant capability, whereas GSNOROE5 had the lowest total antioxidant capability (Fig. S7). It is agreed that GSH and ASC are the main nonenzymatic antioxidants. Thus, the concentration of GSH and ASC in shoots of Col-0, gsnor1-3 and GSNOROE5 plants were determined after 5 days of Cd exposure. There was no significant difference between GSH and ASC concentration observed among Col-0, gsnor1-3 and GSNOROE5 under the condition with or without Cd exposure (Fig. S8). This indicated that GSNOR was not involved in H2O2 consumption through a non-enzymic system.

As H2O2 is scavenged by CAT, APX and POD, their activities in the shoots of Col-0, gsnor1-3 and GSNOROE5 plants were analysed to reveal the mechanism of GSNOR in scavenging H2O2 under Cd stress. The relationships between genotype and activities of POD and APX were clearly less prominent (Fig. S9). It should be noticed that gsnor1-3 displayed highest APX activity, while no significant difference between Col-0 and GSNOROE5 under Cd stress (Fig. S9A). Accordingly, the enhanced APX activity in gsnor1-3 is beneficial for reducing excess H2O2 caused by Cd stress, which is consistent with actual H2O2 level determined in Fig. 3B. Although the activity of POD is related to GSNOR activity in 3 lines under Cd-free condition, no obvious differences were found among them under Cd stress (Fig. S9B), indicating that GSNOR-mediated POD activity might not be the major reason for H2O2 levels in plant under Cd stress.

Interestingly, a clear negative correlation was observed between CAT activity and GSNOR activity regardless of the Cd stress. CAT activity was highest in gsnor1-3 plants while lowest in GSNOROE5 (Fig. 4A). Moreover, we investigated the expression of CAT1, CAT2 and CAT3 in Col-0, gsnor1-3 and GSNOROE5 shoots to clarify the role of GSNOR in CAT activity. There was no relationship between the expression of CAT genes and GSNOR activity under Cd-free conditions (Fig. 4B–D). CAT1 and CAT2 were expressed the highest in gsnor1-3 plants, whereas they were expressed the lowest in GSNOROE5 shoots under Cd stress (Fig. 4B,C). However, no obvious correlation was exhibited between the GSNOR and CAT3 regardless of Cd levels (Fig. 4D), although there was a significant decrease in GSNOROE5 plants under Cd free condition (Fig. 4D). Therefore, GSNOR inhibited CAT activity under Cd stress by down-regulating the expression of CAT1 and CAT2.

Considering the positive correlation between H2O2 accumulation and GSNOR activity under Cd stress, we proposed that the inhibition of CAT activity caused by GSNOR function was the major reason for H2O2 over-accumulation. To verify the above results further, the activity of CAT and H2O2 content in shoots of Col-0, gsnor1-3 and GSNOROE5 plants were detected after the application of 3-AT, an inhibitor of CAT, for 5 days. After treatment with 3-AT, a clear inhibition of CAT activity was found among the three lines with or without Cd stress compared with normal conditions (Fig. 5A). The effect of GSNOR and Cd exposure on CAT activity also disappeared after 3-AT treatment (Fig. 5A). H2O2 accumulation in the three lines was increased by about four times with 3-AT application regardless of Cd exposure (Fig. 5B). However, no difference in H2O2 level was observed among Col-0, gsnor1-3 and GSNOROE5 plants (Fig. 5B). These results suggested that CAT inhibitor eliminated the effect of GSNOR on H2O2 accumulation, which supported the conclusion that CAT was the main regulator of GSNOR-induced H2O2 accumulation in plants under Cd stress.

GSNOR regulates CAT activity through GSNO

To determine whether GSNO is the key regulator in the process of GSNOR-involved Cd tolerance, 200 µM GSNO was sprayed on the surface of GSNOROE5 plants under Cd stress. As shown in Fig. 6A, leaf withering under Cd stress was alleviated with GSNO application compared to that in the group of H2O application. Evans blue staining was more obvious in the leaf blades and petiole of shoots in the group treated with H2O than that treated with GSNO under Cd stress conditions (Fig. 6B,C). This suggested that GSNO improved the Cd tolerance of plants. The CAT activity in the shoots of plants was significantly increased by about a third when spraying GSNO than that in plants treated with H2O under Cd conditions, but no difference was found between groups treated with H2O and GSNO under Cd-free conditions (Fig. 6D). Therefore, adding GSNO increased CAT activity under Cd stress, which subsequently improved Cd tolerance in plants.

Discussion

Contradictory role of GSNOR on Cd accumulation and tolerance in shoots

In plants, Cd stress causes the accumulation of ROS that results in oxidative damage and programmed cell death38. Theoretically, more toxicity appears in plants with increasing Cd content. We had previously demonstrated that Cd toxicity in the roots of Arabidopsis was related to the Cd content in the roots of Col-0, gsnor1-3 and GSNOROE5 plants18. It is interesting that among the three plant lines, the most Cd accumulation but lowest Cd toxicity was in the shoots of gsnor1-3, whereas the least Cd accumulation with the highest Cd toxicity was in GSNOROE5 plants (Figs. 1, S1, S3 and S4). This indicated that GSNOR was involved in the Cd response in plants, and that a more serious Cd toxicity would be apparent in plants with increasing activity of GSNOR. Considering that Cd promoted the expression of GSNOR and the activity of the GSNOR protein, we concluded that GSNOR had a negative effect on Cd tolerance in plants. Combined with the function of GSNOR, which inhibits Cd uptake by the roots and accumulation in the shoots18, we could infer that GSNOR has a contradictory role in Cd accumulation and tolerance in shoots.

GSNOR induces excess H2O2 accumulation in shoots by inhibiting CAT activity

The level of oxidative stress in plants is reliant on ROS accumulation and antioxidant content. Significant inhibition of ROS, O2·− and H2O2 accumulations were found in gsnor1-3, whereas they increased in GSNOROE plants. H2O2 and O2·− are the major ROS. However, the concentration of O2·− was much lower than H2O2 (Fig. 3C) and no relationship was found between GSNOR and SOD activity (Fig. S6). Thus, O2·− may contribute less to Cd tolerance in Arabidopsis. The low level of O2·− accumulation in leaves may be caused by the conversion to H2O2. Therefore, H2O2 was the main player in the process by which GSNOR negatively regulated Cd tolerance. This hypothesis is supported by other studies7,28.

Various antioxidants and enzymes are involved in H2O2 consumption. However, the main antioxidants of the nonenzymatic system, GSH and ASA, were not associated with GSNOR activity (Fig. S8). This indicated that the role of the nonenzymatic system in the regulation of H2O2 accumulation controlled by GSNOR was much smaller than the enzymatic system. A negative relationship was only found between the activity of GSNOR and CAT, and not with SOD, POD and APX (Figs. 4 and S9). Moreover, using an inhibitor of CAT activity deleted the effect of GSNOR on CAT activity and H2O2 accumulation induced by Cd exposure (Fig. 5). Thus, CAT was responsible for the regulation of H2O2 accumulation controlled by GSNOR. This conclusion was also corroborated by previous findings in Arabidopsis and Drosophila melanogaster39. As reported by Chang39, the gsnor mutant has an increased CAT activity and lower immunity capacity, and losing the function of CAT3 enzymes recovers immunity.

Multi-pathways impact CAT activity regulated by GSNOR under Cd stress

NO is an important gas signal molecule with diverse functions for responding to Cd stress, such as regulating the expression of genes and affecting activity of proteins2,16,21,27. It is reported that CAT activity was increased by applying NO whereas it was inhibited by eliminating NO in plants16,17. GSNO downstream of NO is involved in the response to abiotic stress26. Thus, GSNO may act as the real regulator of CAT activity under Cd stress. This is consistent with our finding that spraying GSNO on leaf surfaces increased the activity of CAT under Cd conditions (Fig. 6). As GSNOR is an important member of the NO scavenger system through the reduction of GSNO, the natural donor of NO, to GSSG and NH315,26. Therefore, NO or GSNO was involved in the inhibition of CAT activity regulated by GSNOR.

In this study, CAT1 and CAT2 expression were negatively related to GSNOR activity in shoots of Col-0, gsnor1-3 and GSNOROE plants under Cd stress, whereas no significant difference was detected in the expression of CAT3 among the three plant lines (Fig. 4). This suggested that the expression of CAT1 and CAT2 had the same tendency as CAT activity and the opposite to oxidative stress in the shoots of the three lines under Cd stress. Thus, there was no relationship between CAT activity and CAT3 expression. It should be noted that CAT1 was mainly expressed in pollen and seeds during the early stage of seedling growth and the late stages of senescence, CAT1 protein is only responsible for 8% of CAT activity in Arabidopsis, while CAT2 protein is the most abundant catalase and expresses in leaves39,40. As CAT activity in plants is controlled by the expression of CAT genes and the fact that expression of CAT2 is much higher than CAT1 (Fig. 4B). Thus, GSNOR regulated CAT activity by changing the expression of CAT2. Moreover, it was reported that GSNOR regulates enzyme activity by reducing the S-nitrosylation level of protein caused by GSNO31,39,41. The cellular proteins of antioxidant enzymes, such as APX1, CAT1, CAT2 and CAT3, could be S-nitrosylated with the addition of NO and GSNO in Arabidopsis40. Hence, the S-nitrosylation of CATs might be regulated by GSNOR under Cd stress.

In summary, the multi-pathway impact of CAT activity regulated by GSNOR under Cd stress, included the regulation of NO accumulation, modification of the S-nitrosylation level in the CAT protein and changing expression of CAT2 in shoots (Fig. 7).

The conflicted roles of GSNO on CAT activity

At present, the efficacy of S-nitrosylation in proteins is conflicting. Some researchers believe that enzymic activity is increased after the S-nitrosylation of the protein. Losing the function of GSNOR or applying NO with a low concentration increases APX activity through increasing the S-nitrosylation level of APX protein in plants42. Activity of tyrosine hydroxylase was enhanced both in vitro and in vivo after S-nitrosylation41. However, S-nitrosylation caused by NO application in vitro inhibits the enzyme activity of proteins, such as CAT and APX43. It should be noted that the NO content is much higher than the concentration of the CAT or APX protein in the study by Clark et al.43. Moreover, the enzymatic activity of the protein is modified after tyrosine nitration mediated by reactive nitrogen species, such as peroxynitrite anion (ONOO−), caused of too much NO in the medium44,45. Antioxidant enzymes are reported as targets of Tyr nitration, their activity is inhibited after protein nitration45–47. Thus, the enhanced enzymatic activity induces by S-nitrosylation or Try nitration may depend on GSNO or NO content in the medium.

In this study, in vitro experiments showed that CAT activity increased by adding GSNO into a solution with CAT protein, but the increasing effect was highest in a ratio of two GSNO to one CAT (Figs. 6 and S10). This is consistent with the fact that two positions of cysteine could be S-nitrosylated in each CAT molecule47. Thus, CAT protein may be S-nitrosylated under GSNO and this modification may increase CAT activity because of S-nitrosylation caused by GSNO. Considering the dual regulation of CAT by S-nitrosylation and nitration, CAT activity may be inhibited after Try is nitrated by excessive GSNO39,48. It can be inferred that GSNOR inhibited CAT activity as a scavenger of GSNO, through the reduction of the S-nitrosylation level of the CAT protein. Therefore, the inhibition of CAT activity in Col-0 plants under Cd stress was alleviated in gsnor1-3 by the high S-nitrosylation level of the CAT protein. While, the inference requires further verification through analyzing the correlation between S-nitrosylation level and activity of proteins in plant.

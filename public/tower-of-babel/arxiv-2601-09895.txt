Lossless Strichartz Estimates on the Square Torus over Short Time Intervals
Connor Quinn

Abstract
. We prove lossless Strichartz estimates at the critical exponent q c = 2 ​ ( n + 1 ) n − 1 q_{c}=\frac{2(n+1)}{n-1} on the square torus for the Schrödinger equation with frequency localized initial data on small time windows with length depending on the frequency parameter λ \lambda . This work improves on previous results of Burq-Gerard-Tzvetkov [ 6 ] for general manifolds, and Blair-Huang-Sogge [ 2 ] and Huang-Sogge [ 13 ] for manifolds of nonpositive curvature by showing that we can take the time interval to be of length λ − δ n \lambda^{-\delta_{n}} for some δ n ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) \delta_{n}\in(0,1) depending on the dimension of the manifold. The proof uses Tao’s bilinear restriction theorem for the paraboloid [ 19 ] and an iteration argument over multiple scales which takes advantage of improved kernel estimates arising from the Fourier multiplier decomposition we employ.



1.
Introduction

Let
(M,g)
be a compact Riemannian manifold with dimension
d\geq 2
. We will let
n
denote the space-time dimension (i.e the dimension of
M\times\mathbb{R}
) so that
d=n-1
. Let
\Delta_{g}
denote the Laplace-Beltrami operator associated to the metric and let

(1.1)

u(x,t)=e^{-it\Delta_{g}}f(x)

be the solution to the Schrödinger equation on
M\times\mathbb{R}

(1.2)

i\partial_{t}u(x,t)=\Delta_{g}u(x,t),\ u(x,0)=f(x).

One set of tools used to analyze the space-time dispersive properties of these solutions are Strichartz estimates, which on a compact manifold take the form

(1.3)

\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{p}_{t}L^{q}_{x}(M\times[0,1])}\lesssim\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{H^{s}(M)}

where

\displaystyle\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{p}_{t}L^{q}_{x}(M\times[0,1])}=\Big(\int_{0}^{1}\left\lVert u(\cdot,t)\right\rVert_{L^{q}(M)}^{p}dt\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}

and

\displaystyle\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{H^{s}(M)}=\left\lVert\big(I+P\big)^{s}f\right\rVert_{L^{2}(M)},\ \ \text{with}\ P=\sqrt{-\Delta_{g}},

is the standard
L^{2}
-based Sobolev space on
M
. The exponent pair
(p,q)
in (
1.3
) must be Keel-Tao admissible, meaning they satisfy the relation

(1.4)

d\Big(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}\Big)=\frac{2}{p},\ \text{and}\ 2<q\leq\frac{2d}{d-2}\ \text{if}\ d\geq 3,\ \text{or}\ 2<q<\infty\ \text{if}\ d=2.

The study of such inequalities in this setting was first initiated by Burq-Gerard-Tzvetkov
[
6
]
where they showed that (
1.3
) holds for admissible
(p,q)
if
s\geq\frac{1}{p}
. To show this, they actually proved a stronger uniform inequality on a short time interval, namely that

(1.5)

\left\lVert e^{-it\Delta_{g}}\beta(P/\lambda)f\right\rVert_{L^{p}_{t}L^{q}_{x}(M\times[0,\lambda^{-1}])}\leq C\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{2},\ \lambda\gg 1.

where
\beta\in C^{\infty}_{0}((\frac{1}{2},2))
is a Littlewood-Paley bump function. By writing
[0,1]
as a union of
\sim\lambda
intervals of length
\lambda^{-1}
one can show that (
1.5
) implies

(1.6)

\left\lVert e^{-it\Delta_{g}}\beta(P/\lambda)f\right\rVert_{L^{p}_{t}L^{q}_{x}(M\times[0,1])}\leq C\lambda^{\frac{1}{p}}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{2},\ \lambda\gg 1

by adding up the uniform estimates on the small time intervals. This then implies (
1.3
) with
s\geq\frac{1}{p}
using Littlewood-Paley theory. The restriction
s\geq\frac{1}{p}
is necessary since, if we consider the endpoint Strichartz estimate with
(p,q)=\big(2,\frac{2d}{d-2}\big)
for
d\geq 3
, then the derivative loss of
\frac{1}{2}
is sharp on the standard round sphere
S^{d}
. One can see this by taking the initial data to be zonal eigenfunctions.

However, on compact manifolds with other types of geometries the restriction
s\geq\frac{1}{p}
can be improved. For example, if we assume our manifold
M
has nonpositive sectional curvature then Huang-Sogge
[
13
]
recently showed that we can get a log improvement over the results of Burq-Gerard-Tzvetkov

(1.7)

\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{p}_{t}L^{q}_{x}(M\times[0,1])}\lesssim\left\lVert(I+P)^{\frac{1}{p}}\log{(2I+P)}^{-\frac{1}{p}}f\right\rVert_{L^{2}(M)}.

Huang-Sogge obtained their results by following the main idea of Burq-Gerard-Tzvetkov and proving uniform estimates over small time intervals. However, the estimate (
1.5
) cannot be improved on any manifold by taking
f(x)=\beta(P/\lambda)(x,x_{0})
where
\beta(P/\lambda)(x,y)
is the kernel of the Littlewood-Paley operator. To get their improvement, Huang-Sogge proved lossless estimates on longer time intervals than the ones used by Burq-Gerard-Tzvetkov. In particular, Huang-Sogge showed that

(1.8)

\left\lVert e^{-it\Delta_{g}}\beta(P/\lambda)f\right\rVert_{L^{p}_{t}L^{q}_{x}(M\times[0,\frac{\log\lambda}{\lambda}])}\leq C\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{2}.

This paper followed earlier work by Blair-Huang-Sogge
[
2
]
where a slightly weaker result was proven.

When
M=\mathbb{T}^{n-1}
is a flat torus there has been significant improvement over the restriction
s\geq\frac{1}{p}
. In this case, we are also interested in the problem when
d=1
and
\mathbb{T}=S^{1}
. Also, all of the below results will be stated for the square torus
\mathbb{T}^{n-1}=(\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z})^{n-1}
, although in some cases they also apply when considering more general rectangular tori. Perhaps the most notable work on this problem is that of Bourgain
[
3
]
and Bourgain-Demeter
[
4
]
who showed that

(1.9)

\left\lVert e^{-it\Delta_{\mathbb{T}^{n-1}}}f\right\rVert_{L^{q}(\mathbb{T}^{n-1}\times[0,1])}\lesssim\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{H^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{n-1})},\ s>\alpha(q)

where

(1.10)

\alpha(q)=\\
\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}&0,\ \text{if }2\leq q\leq\frac{2(n+1)}{n-1}\\
&\frac{n-1}{2}-\frac{n+1}{q},\ \text{if }q\geq\frac{2(n+1)}{n-1}.\par\end{array}\right\}

Bourgain originally showed this when
n=2,3
using number theoretic methods, while Bourgain-Demeter extended the result to higher dimensions as a consequence of their powerful decoupling theorem for the paraboloid. Killip-Visan
[
15
]
later showed that we can take
s=\alpha(q)
when
q>\frac{2(n+1)}{n-1}
.

The exponent
\frac{2(n+1)}{n-1}
is frequently called the critical exponent or the Stein-Tomas exponent and we will denote it from here on out by
q_{c}=\frac{2(n+1)}{n-1}
. Note that
(q_{c},q_{c})
is the only Keel-Tao admissable pair of exponents for which
p=q
. It is actually conjectured that the correct estimate for the torus at
q_{c}
is

(1.11)

\left\lVert e^{-it\Delta_{\mathbb{T}^{n-1}}}\beta(D_{x}/\lambda)f\right\rVert_{L^{q_{c}}(\mathbb{T}^{n-1}\times[0,1])}\lesssim\big(\log\lambda\big)^{\frac{1}{q_{c}}}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{2},\ \lambda\gg 1

where now
\beta
is any smooth bump function supported in
B(0,1)
. Obtaining an estimate this strong when
n\geq 4
is wide open. However, when
n=2
there has been significant progress while when
n=3
the problem has been resolved entirely. First, note that when
n=2
,
q_{c}=6
. Guth-Maldague-Wang showed in
[
11
]
that the estimate in (
1.11
) holds with the exponent
\frac{1}{6}
on the logarithmic factor replaced by some large constant
C
. In
[
10
]
, Guo-Li-Yung adapted the methods of the previous work and improved the exponent on the logarithmic factor to
2+\varepsilon
for any
\varepsilon>0
. When
n=3
,
q_{c}=4
and in a remarkable paper
[
12
]
, Herr-Kwak showed that if
S\subset\mathbb{Z}^{2}
then

(1.12)

\left\lVert e^{-it\Delta_{\mathbb{T}^{n-1}}}\mathbbm{1}_{S}(D_{x})f\right\rVert_{L^{4}(\mathbb{T}^{2}\times[0,1])}\lesssim\big(\log\#S\big)^{\frac{1}{4}}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{2}.

Herr-Kwak achieved this by proving a stronger inequality on a short time window, namely

(1.13)

\left\lVert e^{-it\Delta_{\mathbb{T}^{n-1}}}\mathbbm{1}_{S}(D_{x})f\right\rVert_{L^{4}(\mathbb{T}^{2}\times[0,\frac{1}{\log\#S}])}\lesssim\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{2}.

In this paper, we will focus on proving lossless Stricharts estimates on the square torus
\mathbb{T}^{n-1}=(\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z})^{n-1}
over small time intervals at
q_{c}
. When
n\geq 4
our results are new. For convenience, we define a very specific bump function
\beta
which is different than the Littlewood-Paley bump function that is typically used. Let
\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}((-2,2))
be an even function such that
\phi\equiv 1
on
[-1,1]
. Set

(1.14)

\beta(\xi)=\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\phi(\xi_{i}).

Note that
\beta(\cdot/\lambda)\equiv 1
on the cube
[-\lambda,\lambda]^{n-1}
and
\text{supp}\,\beta(\cdot/\lambda)\subset[-2\lambda,2\lambda]^{n-1}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
. Then if
\delta\geq\lambda^{-\frac{1}{n+1}+\varepsilon}
we have

(1.15)

\left\lVert e^{-it\Delta_{\mathbb{T}^{n-1}}}\beta(D_{x}/\lambda)f\right\rVert_{L^{q_{c}}(\mathbb{T}^{n-1}\times[0,\frac{1}{\lambda\delta}])}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{2}.

When
\delta>\lambda^{-\frac{1}{n+1}}
then
\big(\lambda\delta\big)^{-1}<\lambda^{-\frac{n}{n+1}}
. Thus, Theorem 1.1 says that we have uniform lossless estimates on any time interval with length less than
\lambda^{-\frac{n}{n+1}}
. We also remark that results with the above bump function
\beta
replaced by bump functions with similar support properties follow easily from Theorem 1.1. We also point out that Theorem 1.1 does not come close to recovering the estimates (
1.9
) of Bourgain and Bourgain-Demeter. In order to recover these estimates, one would need to prove lossless uniform estimates on time intervals of length
\lambda^{-\varepsilon}
for any
\varepsilon>0
. This equates to letting
\delta\geq\lambda^{-1+\varepsilon}
in Theorem 1.1. We hope to work further towards this goal in future projects. However, we also note that
\lambda^{-\frac{n}{n+1}}=\frac{\lambda^{\frac{1}{n+1}}}{\lambda}\gg\frac{\log\lambda}{\lambda}
so Theorem 1.1 is a significant improvement over the results of Huang-Sogge (
1.8
) at
q_{c}
which were proved for any compact manifold with nonpositive curvature. Our results are also an improvement of sorts on work of Schippa
[
17
]
, who showed that for
2\leq q<q_{c}
we have

(1.16)

\left\lVert e^{-it\Delta_{\mathbb{T}^{n-1}}}\beta(D_{x}/\lambda)f\right\rVert_{L^{q}(\mathbb{T}^{n-1}\times[0,\lambda^{-\alpha}])}\lesssim\lambda^{-\kappa}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{2}

for any
\alpha>0
and
\kappa<\alpha\big(\frac{n-1}{2}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})-\frac{1}{p}\big)
. However, the argument used for
2\leq q<q_{c}
fails to work at
q_{c}
so our work compliments Schippa’s result.

As we remarked earlier, our results are only new for
n\geq 4
. When
n=3
our results are beaten by the result of Herr-Kwak (
1.13
). When
n=2
our results are beaten by work of McConnell
[
16
]
, who showed that you can prove a uniform lossless estimate on time intervals with length smaller than
\lambda^{-\frac{131}{208}}
. McConnell showed this by connecting the estimate over a short time interval to the problem of counting lattice points in a thin annular region of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. However, there is still room for improvement here, and we hope to explore this problem in future work as well.

Finally, let us say something about the inherent value of proving lossless Strichartz estimates on short time intervals. While the most obvious use of proving such estimates is for the use of proving estimates with some loss on unit time intervals, these short time estimates have some other uses as well. First, it is interesting to see just how long solutions to the Schrödinger equation on a compact manifold display dispersive properties similar to the ones we see in Euclidean space. Studying how big such a time interval can be before we take some loss is a very nice way of quantifying this property. These kinds of estimates are also more useful to proving well-posedness results for certain classes of non-linear Schröinger equations than the corresponding estimates over unit length time intervals with loss. See
[
12
]
,
[
16
]
, and
[
17
]
for more details.

2.
Notation, Initial Reductions and a Brief Discussion of the Proof

For any set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
we let
\tilde{E}=E\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n-1}
. Let
\lambda\in 2^{\mathbb{N}}
, and
\delta\in 2^{-\mathbb{N}{}}
with
\delta>\lambda^{-1}
. Also, let
Q_{\lambda}=[-\lambda,\lambda]^{n-1}
. We write
A\lesssim B
to mean that there is a constant
C\geq 1
depending only on the dimension such that
A\leq CB
. Similarly,
A\lesssim_{\varepsilon}B
means there exists a constant
C_{\varepsilon}
depending only on the dimension and
\varepsilon>0
such that
A\leq C_{\varepsilon}B
. We write
A\sim B
if there are constants
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
such that
A\leq C_{1}B
and
B\leq C_{2}A
. The value of such constants might change from line to line. This is unimportant, since these constants will not depend on the special parameters
\lambda
and
\delta
. We write
e(t)=e^{it}
. For any function
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n-1})
and any
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n-1}
we define

(2.1)

\widehat{f}(k)=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n-1}}f(y)e(-y\cdot k)dy.

Also, if
m
is any function of
n-1
variables then we define the Fourier multiplier associated to
m
as

(2.2)

m(D_{x})f(x)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n-1}}\widehat{f}(k)m(k)e(x\cdot k).

Consider a function
f=\sum_{k\in\tilde{Q}_{\lambda}}\widehat{f}(k)e(x\cdot k)
, and let
\beta
be the bump function from the statement of Theorem 1.1. Note that for any such function
f
we have
\beta(D_{x}/\lambda)f=f
since
\beta(\cdot/\lambda)\equiv 1
on
Q_{\lambda}
. We note that for any
\delta>\lambda^{-1}
, by rescaling, we have that the estimate

(2.3)

\left\lVert e^{-i\lambda^{-1}t\Delta_{\mathbb{T}^{n-1}}}\beta(D_{x}/\lambda)f\right\rVert_{L^{q_{c}}(\mathbb{T}^{n-1}\times[0,\delta^{-1}])}\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{1}{q_{c}}}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{2}

is equivalent to the estimate

(2.4)

\left\lVert e^{-it\Delta_{\mathbb{T}^{n-1}}}\beta(D_{x}/\lambda)f\right\rVert_{L^{q_{c}}(\mathbb{T}^{n-1}\times[0,\frac{1}{\lambda\delta}])}\lesssim\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{2}.

Now, take a bump function
\eta\in C^{\infty}_{0}((-1,1))
with
\eta\equiv 1
on
[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}]
. We define the time-dilated Schrödinger operator

(2.5)

S_{\lambda}f(x,t)=\eta(\delta t)e^{-i\lambda^{-1}t\Delta_{\mathbb{T}^{n-1}}}\beta(D_{x}/\lambda)f(x).

This operator takes functions of
n-1
variables to functions of
n
variables. We sometimes might want to think of this operator as one defined in terms of an additional parameter
t\in[0,\delta^{-1}]
which maps functions of
n-1
variables to functions of
n-1
variables. When we wish to emphasize this perspective we will write
S_{\lambda,t}f(x)=S_{\lambda}f(x,t)
. It is straightforward to see that (
2.3
) follows from the following estimate for the time-dilated Schrödinger operator

(2.6)

\left\lVert S_{\lambda}f\right\rVert_{L^{q_{c}}(\mathbb{T}^{n-1}\times[0,\delta^{-1}])}\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{1}{q_{c}}}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{2}.

Note that for the functions
f
described above we have

(2.7)

S_{\lambda}f(x,t)=\eta(\delta t)\sum_{k\in\tilde{Q_{\lambda}}}\widehat{f}(k)\beta(k/\lambda)e(x\cdot k+\lambda^{-1}t|k|^{2})=\eta(\delta t)\sum_{k\in\tilde{Q_{\lambda}}}\widehat{f}(k)e(x\cdot k+\lambda^{-1}t|k|^{2})

since
\beta(\cdot/\lambda)\equiv 1
on
Q_{\lambda}
. We keep the cutoff
\beta(\cdot/\lambda)
in the definition of our operator as it will be useful later in Section 6 when we prove estimates for the kernel of this operator.

The proof of (
2.6
) uses a multiscale argument that is similar in many ways to the induction on scales methods used prevalently in modern harmonic analysis. More precisely, the use of many scales is similar to the arguments in
[
9
]
and
[
11
]
. One key feature of our argument that sets it apart from the arguments in
[
9
]
,
[
11
]
and, more generally, typical induction on scales arguments, is that the number of scales we need to analyze depends only on
\varepsilon
, and crucially does not depend on the parameters
\lambda
or
\delta
. This means that we can essentially focus on proving the analog of Theorem 1 for functions whose Fourier support has been localized to a small cube and “add up” these estimates using the natural
L^{2}
orthogonality of the many different localized pieces to obtain the desired result. Although this is an oversimplification, this is one of the key tools used in our argument. The other tools used are bilinear restriction estimates for the paraboloid and a “height-splitting” at each scale with
1\leq\ell<K
. Height-splitting was also used by Blair-Huang-Sogge
[
1
]
,
[
2
]
and Huang-Sogge
[
13
]
,
[
14
]
at a single scale in order to prove the results in those papers. The height-splitting technique also bears some resemblance to the pruning process for wave packets used in
[
9
]
and
[
11
]
.

We start by defining a small scale
\delta_{K}=\delta^{\frac{1}{K}}
. For each
1\leq\ell\leq K
we partition the cube
Q_{\lambda}
into cubes
\{\tau_{\ell}\}
of side-length
\sim\lambda\delta_{K}^{\ell}
. For each
\ell
, we localize the Fourier support of our function to one of the cubes in the partition
\{\tau_{\ell}\}
. We then prove an estimate (see Proposition 3.1) relating the analogous estimate to (
2.6
) for our localized function to the same kind of estimate but now with our function frequency localized to a cube
\tau_{\ell+1}
contained in
\tau_{\ell}
. Ultimately, when we reach the scale
\delta_{K}^{K}=\delta
and localize in frequency to cubes
\tau_{K}
the amount of constructive interference we see is minimal and we obtain the best possible estimate on a time window of size
[0,\delta^{-1}]
:

(2.8)

\left\lVert S_{\lambda}f_{\tau_{K}}\right\rVert_{L^{q_{c}}(\mathbb{T}^{n-1}\times[0,\delta^{-1}])}\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{1}{q_{c}}}\left\lVert f_{\tau_{K}}\right\rVert_{2}.

It is interesting to note that (
2.8
) holds for any
\delta>\lambda^{-1}
. The restriction on the size of
\delta
comes from the estimates we obtain at scales where
1\leq\ell<K
. We are then able to use the estimates at the smallest scale to prove estimates at larger scales, and ultimately to prove (
2.6
).

3.
Height Splitting at Scale
\delta_{K}^{\ell-1}
, for
1\leq\ell<K

Fix
\ell
with
1\leq\ell<K
. Let
\tau_{\ell-1}
be a cube of side-length
2\lambda\delta_{K}^{\ell-1}
with sides parallel to the coordinate axes contained in
Q_{\lambda}
. Let
f_{\tau_{\ell-1}}(x)=\sum_{k\in\tilde{\tau}_{\ell-1}}\widehat{f}(k)e(x\cdot k)
so that
\text{supp}\,\widehat{f_{\tau_{\ell-1}}}\subset\tau_{\ell-1}
. When
\ell=1
we take
\tau_{0}=Q_{\lambda}
and
f_{\tau_{0}}=f
. Now, let
\{\tau_{\ell}\}
be a collection of closed cubes of side-length
2\lambda\delta_{K}^{\ell}
with sides parallel to the coordinate axes whose interiors are pairwise disjoint and such that
\tau_{\ell-1}=\cup\tau_{\ell}
. For any
\tau_{\ell}
we write
f_{\tau_{\ell}}(x)=\sum_{k\in\tilde{\tau_{\ell}}}\widehat{f}(k)e(x\cdot k)
.
We will prove the following estimate:

Proposition 3.1
.

For any
\delta>\lambda^{-1}
we have

(3.1)

\begin{split}\left\lVert S_{\lambda}f_{\tau_{\ell-1}}\right\rVert_{L^{q_{c}}(\mathbb{T}^{n-1}\times[0,\delta^{-1}])}\leq\big(C_{1}\lambda^{\frac{1}{q_{c}}}+C_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{n}\frac{1}{q_{c}}+\varepsilon}\delta_{K}^{-C}\delta^{-\frac{n+1}{n}\frac{1}{q_{c}}}\big)\left\lVert f_{\tau_{\ell-1}}\right\rVert_{2}\\
+C_{2}\Big(\sum_{\tau_{\ell}\subset\tau_{\ell-1}}\left\lVert S_{\lambda}f_{\tau_{\ell}}\right\rVert_{L^{q_{c}}(\mathbb{T}^{n-1}\times[0,\delta^{-1}])}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\end{split}

Note that
C,C_{1},C_{2}
and
C_{\varepsilon}
are absolute constants which all depend on the dimension
n
with
C_{\varepsilon}
also depending on
\varepsilon
. Crucially, none of these constants depend on either of the parameters
\lambda
or
\delta
. It suffices to prove Proposition 3.1 when
\left\lVert f_{\tau_{\ell-1}}\right\rVert_{2}=1
so we will assume that this is the case until the end of Section 5.

We define the following two subsets of
\mathbb{T}^{n-1}\times[0,\delta^{-1}]
:

(3.2)

\begin{split}A^{+}_{\tau_{\ell-1}}=\{(x,t)\in\mathbb{T}^{n-1}\times[0,\delta^{-1}]\ :|S_{\lambda}f_{\tau_{\ell-1}}(x,t)|\geq C_{0}\big(\lambda\delta^{-1}\delta_{K}^{2(\ell-1)}\big)^{\frac{n-1}{4}}\}\\
A^{-}_{\tau_{\ell-1}}=\{(x,t)\in\mathbb{T}^{n-1}\times[0,\delta^{-1}]\ :|S_{\lambda}f_{\tau_{\ell-1}}(x,t)|<C_{0}\big(\lambda\delta^{-1}\delta_{K}^{2(\ell-1)}\big)^{\frac{n-1}{4}}\}\end{split}

where the precise value of
C_{0}
will be chosen later in the argument. Then Proposition 3.1 will follow from estimates for
S_{\lambda}f_{\tau_{\ell-1}}
over each of the regions
A^{+}_{\tau_{\ell-1}}
and
A^{-}_{\tau_{\ell-1}}
, which we describe in the following two propositions.

Proposition 3.2
.

For any
\delta>\lambda^{-1}
we have

(3.3)

\left\lVert S_{\lambda}f_{\tau_{\ell-1}}\right\rVert_{L^{q_{c}}(A^{+}_{\tau_{\ell-1}})}\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{1}{q_{c}}}\left\lVert f_{\tau_{\ell-1}}\right\rVert_{2}.

Proposition 3.3
.

For any
\delta>\lambda^{-1}
we have

(3.4)

\left\lVert S_{\lambda}f_{\tau_{\ell-1}}\right\rVert_{L^{q_{c}}(A^{-}_{\tau_{\ell-1}})}\leq C\Big(\sum_{\tau_{\ell}\subset\tau_{\ell-1}}\left\lVert S_{\lambda}f_{\tau_{\ell}}\right\rVert_{L^{q_{c}}(\mathbb{T}^{n-1}\times[0,\delta^{-1}])}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}+C_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{n}\frac{1}{q_{c}}+\varepsilon}\delta_{K}^{-C}\delta^{-\frac{n+1}{n}\frac{1}{q_{c}}}\left\lVert f_{\tau_{\ell-1}}\right\rVert_{2}.

4.
Large Height Estimates at Scale
\delta_{K}^{\ell-1}
, for
1\leq\ell<K

We start by proving Proposition 3.2. To do this, we adapt an argument used in recent works of Blair-Huang-Sogge
[
1
]
,
[
2
]
and Huang-Sogge
[
13
]
,
[
14
]
. First, note that if the center of
\tau_{\ell-1}
is
c=(c_{1},...,c_{n-1})
then
\tau_{\ell-1}=\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}[c_{i}-\lambda\delta_{K}^{\ell-1},c_{i}+\lambda\delta_{K}^{\ell-1}]
. Let
\chi\in C_{0}^{\infty}((-2,2))
with
\chi\equiv 1
on
[-1,1]
. For
\xi=(\xi_{1},...,\xi_{n-1})
set
\chi_{\tau_{\ell-1}}(\xi)=\prod_{i=1}^{d}\chi((\lambda\delta_{K}^{\ell-1})^{-1}(\xi_{i}-c_{i}))
. Then
\text{supp}\,\chi_{\tau_{\ell-1}}\subset 2\tau_{\ell-1}
and
\chi_{\tau_{\ell-1}}\equiv 1
on
\tau_{\ell-1}
. Thus, if
\chi_{\tau_{\ell-1}}(D_{x})
denotes the Fourier multiplier given by

(4.1)

\chi_{\tau_{\ell-1}}(D_{x})h(x)=\sum_{k\in\tilde{Q_{\lambda}}}\widehat{h}(k)\chi_{\tau_{\ell}}(k)e(x\cdot k),\ x\in\mathbb{T}^{n-1}

then it follows that
S_{\lambda}f_{\tau_{\ell-1}}=S_{\lambda}\chi_{\tau_{\ell-1}}(D_{x})f_{\tau_{\ell-1}}
.

Now, choose a function
g
such that

(4.2)

\begin{split}\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{L^{q_{c}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{T}^{n-1}\times[0,\delta^{-1}])}&=1\ \text{and}\\
\left\lVert S_{\lambda}\chi_{\tau_{\ell-1}}(D_{x})f_{\tau_{\ell-1}}\right\rVert_{L^{q_{c}}(A^{+}_{\tau_{\ell-1}})}&=\Big|\iint S_{\lambda}\chi_{\tau_{\ell-1}}(D_{x})f_{\tau_{\ell-1}}(x,t)\mathbbm{1}_{A^{+}_{\tau_{\ell-1}}}(x,t)\overline{g(x,t)}dxdt\Big|.\end{split}

As we are assuming that
\left\lVert f_{\tau_{\ell-1}}\right\rVert_{2}=1
, it follows from the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality that

(4.3)

\begin{split}\left\lVert S_{\lambda}f_{\tau_{\ell-1}}\right\rVert_{L^{q_{c}}(A^{+}_{\tau_{\ell-1}})}^{2}&=\left\lVert S_{\lambda}\chi_{\tau_{\ell-1}}(D_{x})f_{\tau_{\ell-1}}\right\rVert_{L^{q_{c}}(A^{+}_{\tau_{\ell-1}})}^{2}=\Big|\int f_{\tau_{\ell-1}}(x)\overline{\big(S_{\lambda}\chi_{\tau_{\ell-1}}(D_{x})\big)^{*}(\mathbbm{1}_{A^{+}_{\tau_{\ell-1}}}g)(x)}dx\Big|^{2}\\
&\leq\int\big|\big(S_{\lambda}\chi_{\tau_{\ell-1}}(D_{x})\big)^{*}(\mathbbm{1}_{A^{+}_{\tau_{\ell-1}}}g)(x)\big|^{2}dx\\
&=\iint\big(S_{\lambda}\chi_{\tau_{\ell-1}}(D_{x})\big)^{*}(\mathbbm{1}_{A^{+}_{\tau_{\ell-1}}}g)(x)\overline{\big(S_{\lambda}\chi_{\tau_{\ell-1}}(D_{x})\big)^{*}(\mathbbm{1}_{A^{+}_{\tau_{\ell-1}}}g)(x)}dx\\
&=\iint S_{\lambda}\chi_{\tau_{\ell-1}}(D_{x})\big(S_{\lambda}\chi_{\tau_{\ell-1}}(D_{x})\big)^{*}\big(\mathbbm{1}_{A^{+}_{\tau_{\ell-1}}}g\big)(x,t)\overline{\big(\mathbbm{1}_{A^{+}_{\tau_{\ell-1}}}g\big)(x,t)}dxdt\\
&=\iint L_{\tau_{\ell-1}}^{\lambda}\big(\mathbbm{1}_{A^{+}_{\tau-{\ell-1}}}g\big)(x,t)\overline{\big(\mathbbm{1}_{A^{+}_{\tau_{\ell-1}}}g\big)(x,t)}dxdt\\
&+\iint G_{\tau_{\ell-1}}^{\lambda}\big(\mathbbm{1}_{A^{+}_{\tau_{\ell-1}}}g\big)(x,t)\overline{\big(\mathbbm{1}_{A^{+}_{\tau_{\ell-1}}}g\big)(x,t)}dxdt\\
&=I+II\end{split}

where
L_{\tau_{\ell-1}}^{\lambda}
is the operator with kernel equaling that of
S_{\lambda}\chi_{\tau_{\ell-1}}(D_{x})\big(S_{\lambda}\chi_{\tau_{\ell-1}}(D_{x})\big)^{*}
if
|t-s|\leq 1
and
0
otherwise, i.e.

(4.4)

L_{\tau_{\ell-1}}^{\lambda}(x,t;y,s)=\\
\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}&S_{\lambda}\chi_{\tau_{\ell-1}}(D_{x})\big(S_{\lambda}\chi_{\tau_{\ell-1}}(D_{x})\big)^{*}(x,t;y,s),\ \text{if }|t-s|\leq 1\\
&0,\ \text{otherwise }\par\end{array}\right\}

where

(4.5)

S_{\lambda}\chi_{\tau_{\ell-1}}(D_{x})\big(S_{\lambda}\chi_{\tau_{\ell-1}}(D_{x})\big)^{*}(x,t;y,s)=\eta(\delta t)\eta(\delta s)\big(\chi_{\tau_{\ell-1}}^{2}(D_{x})\beta^{2}(D_{x}/\lambda)e^{-i\lambda^{-1}(t-s)\Delta_{\mathbb{T}^{n-1}}}\big)(x,y).

It is straightforward to see that the classical Strichartz estimate of Burq-Gerard-Tzvetkov
[
6
]
implies that

(4.6)

\left\lVert L_{\tau_{\ell-1}}^{\lambda}\right\rVert_{L^{q_{c}^{\prime}}_{x,t}\to L^{q_{c}}_{x,t}}=O\big(\lambda^{\frac{2}{q_{c}}}\big).

Then we can use this and Hölder’s inequality to see that

(4.7)

\big|I\big|\leq\left\lVert L_{\tau_{\ell-1}}^{\lambda}\big(\mathbbm{1}_{A^{+}_{\tau_{\ell-1}}}g\big)\right\rVert_{q_{c}}\cdot\left\lVert\mathbbm{1}_{A^{+}_{\tau_{\ell-1}}}g\right\rVert_{q_{c}^{\prime}}\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{2}{q_{c}}}\left\lVert\mathbbm{1}_{A^{+}_{\tau_{\ell-1}}}g\right\rVert_{q_{c}^{\prime}}^{2}\leq\lambda^{\frac{2}{q_{c}}}\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{q_{c}^{\prime}}^{2}=\lambda^{\frac{2}{q_{c}}}.

In Section 6, Corollary 6.0.1, we will show that

(4.8)

\begin{split}\big|S_{\lambda}\chi_{\tau_{\ell-1}}(D_{x})\big(S_{\lambda}\chi_{\tau_{\ell-1}}(D_{x})\big)^{*}(x,t;y,s)\big|&\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{2}}|t-s|^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}(1+\delta_{K}^{\ell-1}|t-s|)^{n-1}\\
&\lesssim\delta_{K}^{(n-1)(\ell-1)}\big(\lambda\delta^{-1}\big)^{\frac{n-1}{2}},\ \text{if }|t-s|\leq 2\delta^{-1}.\end{split}

It follows that

(4.9)

\left\lVert G_{\tau_{\ell-1}}^{\lambda}\right\rVert_{L^{1}_{x,t}\to L^{\infty}_{x,t}}\leq C\delta_{K}^{(n-1)(\ell-1)}(\lambda\delta^{-1})^{\frac{n-1}{2}}

for some constant
C
. Combining this with Hölder’s inequality we see that

(4.10)

\begin{split}\big|II\big|&\leq C\delta_{K}^{(n-1)\ell}(\lambda\delta^{-1})^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\left\lVert\mathbbm{1}_{A^{+}_{\tau_{\ell-1}}}g\right\rVert_{1}^{2}\leq C\delta_{K}^{(n-1)(\ell-1)}(\lambda\delta^{-1})^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{q_{c}^{\prime}}^{2}\cdot\left\lVert\mathbbm{1}_{A^{+}_{\tau_{\ell-1}}}\right\rVert_{q_{c}}^{2}\\
&=C\delta_{K}^{(n-1)(\ell-1)}(\lambda\delta^{-1})^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\left\lVert\mathbbm{1}_{A^{+}_{\tau_{\ell-1}}}\right\rVert_{q_{c}}^{2}.\end{split}

If we recall the definition of the set
A^{+}_{\tau_{\ell-1}}
in (
3.2
) we see that

(4.11)

\begin{split}\left\lVert\mathbbm{1}_{A^{+}_{\tau_{\ell-1}}}\right\rVert_{q_{c}}^{2}&\leq\Big(C_{0}\big(\lambda\delta^{-1}\delta_{K}^{2(\ell-1)}\big)^{\frac{n-1}{4}}\Big)^{-2}\left\lVert S_{\lambda}f_{\tau_{\ell-1}}\right\rVert_{L^{q_{c}}(A^{+}_{\tau_{\ell-1}})}^{2}\\
&=\Big(C_{0}^{2}(\lambda\delta^{-1})^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\delta_{K}^{(n-1)(\ell-1)}\Big)^{-1}\left\lVert S_{\lambda}f_{\tau_{\ell-1}}\right\rVert_{L^{q_{c}}(A^{+}_{\tau-{\ell-1}})}^{2}.\end{split}

Choosing
C_{0}
large enough so that
C_{0}^{-2}C\leq\frac{1}{2}
yields

(4.12)

\big|II\big|\leq\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert S_{\lambda}f_{\tau_{\ell-1}}\right\rVert_{L^{q_{c}}(A^{+}_{\tau_{\ell-1}})}.

Combining this with our estimate for
I
in (
4.7
) we see that we have

(4.13)

\left\lVert S_{\lambda}f_{\tau_{\ell-1}}\right\rVert_{L^{q_{c}}(A^{+}_{\tau_{\ell-1}})}^{2}\leq C\lambda^{\frac{2}{q_{c}}}+\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert S_{\lambda}f_{\tau_{\ell-1}}\right\rVert_{L^{q_{c}}(A^{+}_{\tau_{\ell-1}})}^{2}

which completes the proof of Proposition 3.2.

5.
Small Height Estimates at Scale
\delta_{K}^{\ell-1}
, for
1\leq\ell<K

We now turn to proving Proposition 3.3. To do so we will use an elementary variant of the Broad-Narrow argument originally developed by Bourgain-Guth
[
5
]
. The decomposition contained in the following lemma is similar to Lemma 7.2 in
[
7
]
.

Lemma 5.1
.

There are constants
C,C_{1},C_{2}
, independent of
\lambda
and
\delta
, such that

(5.1)

\Big|\sum_{\tau_{\ell}\subset\tau_{\ell-1}}S_{\lambda}f_{\tau_{\ell}}(x,t)\Big|\leq C_{1}\max_{\tau_{\ell}\subset\tau_{\ell-1}}|S_{\lambda}f_{\tau_{\ell}}(x,t)|+C_{2}\delta_{K}^{-C}\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tau_{\ell},\tau_{\ell}^{\prime}\subset\tau_{\ell-1}\\
dist(\tau_{\ell},\tau_{\ell}^{\prime})\gtrsim\lambda\delta_{K}^{\ell}\end{subarray}}|S_{\lambda}f_{\tau_{\ell}}(x,t)S_{\lambda}f_{\tau_{\ell}^{\prime}}(x,t)|^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Proof.

Fix a point
(x,t)\in\mathbb{T}^{n-1}\times[0,\delta^{-1}]
. Let
\tau_{\ell}^{*}\subset\tau_{\ell-1}
be such that
|S_{\lambda}f_{\tau_{\ell}^{*}}(x,t)|=\max_{\tau_{\ell}\subset{\tau_{\ell-1}}}|S_{\lambda}f_{\tau_{\ell}}(x,t)|
. Next, define the set

(5.2)

S_{\tau_{\ell-1}}^{\text{big}}=\{\tau_{\ell}\subset\tau_{\ell-1}\ :\ |S_{\lambda}f_{\tau_{\ell}}(x,t)|\geq\delta_{K}^{n-1}|S_{\lambda}f_{\tau_{\ell}^{*}}(x,t)|\}.

First, suppose that there is some
\tau_{\ell}^{\prime}\in S_{\tau_{\ell-1}}^{\text{big}}
such that
dist(\tau_{\ell}^{*},\tau_{\ell}^{\prime})\gtrsim\lambda\delta_{K}^{\ell}
. Since
\#\{\tau_{\ell}\subset\tau_{\ell-1}\}\lesssim\delta_{K}^{-(n-1)}
we have

(5.3)

\Big|\sum_{\tau_{\ell}\subset\tau_{\ell-1}}S_{\lambda}f_{\tau_{\ell}}(x,t)\Big|\lesssim\delta_{K}^{-(n-1)}|S_{\lambda}f_{\tau_{\ell}^{*}}(x,t)|\leq\delta_{K}^{-\frac{3(n-1)}{2}}|S_{\lambda}f_{\tau_{\ell}^{*}}(x,t)S_{\lambda}f_{\tau_{\ell}^{\prime}}(x,t)|^{\frac{1}{2}}.

On the other hand, if every
\tau_{\ell}\in S_{\tau_{\ell-1}}^{\text{big}}
satisfies
dist(\tau_{\ell},\tau_{\ell}^{*})\lesssim\lambda\delta_{K}^{\ell}
then
\#S_{\tau_{\ell-1}}^{\text{big}}=O(1)
. Thus, we have

(5.4)

\begin{split}\Big|\sum_{\tau_{\ell}\subset\tau_{\ell-1}}S_{\lambda}f_{\tau_{\ell}}(x,t)\Big|&\leq\Big|\sum_{\tau_{\ell}\in S_{\tau_{\ell-1}}^{\text{big}}}S_{\lambda}f_{\tau_{\ell}}(x,t)\Big|+\Big|\sum_{\tau_{\ell}\notin S_{\tau_{\ell-1}}^{\text{big}}}S_{\lambda}f_{\tau_{\ell}}(x,t)\Big|\\
&\leq\#S_{\tau_{\ell-1}}^{\text{big}}|S_{\lambda}f_{\tau_{\ell}^{*}}(x,t)|+\#\{\tau_{\ell}\subset\tau_{\ell-1}\}\delta_{K}^{n-1}|S_{\lambda}f_{\tau_{\ell}^{*}}(x,t)|\\
&\lesssim|S_{\lambda}f_{\tau_{\ell}^{*}}(x,t)|.\end{split}

∎

Note that the proof shows that we can take
C
in the exponent of
\delta_{K}^{-1}
to be
C=\frac{3(n-1)}{2}
but this is unimportant.

We can replace the maximums in Lemma 5.1 with an
\ell^{2}
norm and an
\ell^{q}
norm over the collections
\{\tau_{\ell}\subset\tau_{\ell-1}\}
to attain a uniform estimate for all
(x,t)\in\mathbb{T}^{n-1}\times[0,\delta^{-1}]
:

(5.5)

\begin{split}\Big|\sum_{\tau_{\ell}\subset\tau_{\ell-1}}S_{\lambda}f_{\tau_{\ell}}(x,t)\Big|&\leq C_{1}\Big(\sum_{\tau_{\ell}\subset\tau_{\ell-1}}|S_{\lambda}f_{\tau_{\ell}}(x,t)|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&+C_{2}\delta_{K}^{-C}\Big(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tau_{\ell},\tau_{\ell}^{\prime}\subset\tau_{\ell-1}\\
dist(\tau_{\ell},\tau_{\ell}^{\prime})\gtrsim\lambda\delta_{K}^{\ell}\end{subarray}}|S_{\lambda}f_{\tau_{\ell}}(x,t)S_{\lambda}f_{\tau_{\ell}^{\prime}}(x,t)|^{\frac{q}{2}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}.\end{split}

We almost have all of the tools we need to prove Proposition 3.3. The next Lemma is a consequence of Tao’s sharp bilinear restriction estimate for the paraboloid
[
19
]
.

Lemma 5.2
.

If
\tau_{\ell},\tau_{\ell}^{\prime}\subset\tau_{\ell-1}
with
dist(\tau_{\ell},\tau_{\ell}^{\prime})\gtrsim\lambda\delta_{K}^{\ell}
then for
q=\frac{2(n+2)}{n}
we have

(5.6)

\left\lVert\big|S_{\lambda}f_{\tau_{\ell}}S_{\lambda}f_{\tau_{\ell}^{\prime}}\big|^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\rVert_{L^{q}(\mathbb{T}^{n-1}\times[0,\delta^{-1}])}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{2}-\frac{n}{q}+\varepsilon}\delta_{K}^{\ell(\frac{n-1}{2}-\frac{n+1}{q})}\delta^{-\frac{1}{q}}\Big(\left\lVert f_{\tau_{\ell}}\right\rVert_{2}\left\lVert f_{\tau_{\ell}^{\prime}}\right\rVert_{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

We postpone the proof of Lemma 5.2 for now and next show how we can use it to prove Proposition 3.3. To start, we have

(5.7)

\begin{split}\left\lVert S_{\lambda}f_{\tau_{\ell-1}}\right\rVert_{L^{q_{c}}(A_{\tau_{\ell-1}}^{-})}^{q_{c}}&=\iint_{A_{\tau_{\ell-1}}^{-}}|S_{\lambda}f_{\tau_{\ell-1}}(x,t)|^{q_{c}-q}|S_{\lambda}f_{\tau_{\ell-1}}(x,t)|^{q}dxdt\\
&=\iint_{A_{\tau_{\ell-1}}^{-}}|S_{\lambda}f_{\tau_{\ell-1}}(x,t)|^{q_{c}-q}\Big|\sum_{\tau_{\ell}\subset\tau_{\ell-1}}S_{\lambda}f_{\tau_{\ell}}(x,t)\Big|^{q}dxdt\\
&\leq C_{1}^{q}\iint_{A_{\tau_{\ell-1}}^{-}}|S_{\lambda}f_{\tau_{\ell-1}}(x,t)|^{q_{c}-q}\Big(\sum_{\tau_{\ell}\subset\tau_{\ell-1}}|S_{\lambda}f_{\tau_{\ell}}(x,t)|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{q}{2}}dxdt\\
&+C_{2}^{q}\delta_{K}^{-Cq}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tau_{\ell},\tau_{\ell}^{\prime}\subset\tau_{\ell-1}\\
dist(\tau_{\ell},\tau_{\ell}^{\prime})\gtrsim\lambda\delta_{K}^{\ell}\end{subarray}}\iint_{A_{\tau_{\ell-1}}^{-}}|S_{\lambda}f_{\tau_{\ell-1}}(x,t)|^{q_{c}-q}|S_{\lambda}f_{\tau_{\ell}}(x,t)S_{\lambda}f_{\tau_{\ell}^{\prime}}(x,t)|^{\frac{q}{2}}dxdt\\
&=I+II.\end{split}

Let
Q_{\ell}f_{\tau_{\ell-1}}=\Big(\sum_{\tau_{\ell}\subset\tau_{\ell-1}}|S_{\lambda}f_{\tau_{\ell}}|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}
be the square function associated to our partition of
\tau_{\ell-1}
. Then

(5.8)

\begin{split}I=C_{1}^{q}\iint_{A_{\tau_{\ell-1}}^{-}}&|S_{\lambda}f_{\tau_{\ell-1}}(x,t)|^{q_{c}-q}|Q_{\ell}f_{\tau_{\ell-1}}(x,t)|^{q}dxdt\leq C_{1}^{q}\left\lVert S_{\lambda}f_{\tau_{\ell-1}}\right\rVert_{L^{q_{c}}(A_{\tau_{\ell-1}}^{-})}^{q_{c}-q}\left\lVert Q_{\ell}f_{\tau_{\ell-1}}\right\rVert_{L^{q_{c}}(\mathbb{T}^{n-1}\times[0,\delta^{-1}])}^{q}\\
&\leq\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert S_{\lambda}f_{\tau_{\ell-1}}\right\rVert_{L^{q_{c}}(A_{\tau_{\ell-1}}^{-})}^{q_{c}}+C\left\lVert Q_{\ell}f_{\tau_{\ell-1}}\right\rVert_{L^{q_{c}}(\mathbb{T}^{n-1}\times[0,\delta^{-1}])}^{q_{c}}\\
&\leq\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert S_{\lambda}f_{\tau_{\ell-1}}\right\rVert_{L^{q_{c}}(A_{\tau_{\ell-1}}^{-})}^{q_{c}}+C\Big(\sum_{\tau_{\ell}\subset\tau_{\ell-1}}\left\lVert S_{\lambda}f_{\tau_{\ell}}\right\rVert_{L^{q_{c}}(\mathbb{T}^{n-1}\times[0,\delta^{-1}])}\Big)^{\frac{q_{c}}{2}}.\end{split}

where we used Hölder’s inequality in the first line, then Young’s inequality (with
\epsilon=\frac{1}{2C_{1}^{q}}
, see Appendix B of
[
8
]
for more details) in the second-to-last line and, finally, Minkowski’s inequality in the last line.

Now, we need to handle
II
. Note that by definition of the set
A_{\tau_{\ell-1}}^{-}
in (
3.2
) and Lemma 5.2 we have

(5.9)

\begin{split}II&=C_{2}^{q}\delta_{K}^{-Cq}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tau_{\ell},\tau_{\ell}^{\prime}\subset\tau_{\ell-1}\\
dist(\tau_{\ell},\tau_{\ell}^{\prime})\gtrsim\lambda\delta_{K}^{\ell}\end{subarray}}\iint_{A_{\tau_{\ell-1}}^{-}}|S_{\lambda}f_{\tau_{\ell-1}}(x,t)|^{q_{c}-q}|S_{\lambda}f_{\tau_{\ell}}(x,t)S_{\lambda}f_{\tau_{\ell}^{\prime}}(x,t)|^{\frac{q}{2}}dxdt\\
&\lesssim\delta_{K}^{-Cq}\left\lVert S_{\lambda}f_{\tau_{\ell-1}}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(A_{\tau_{\ell-1}}^{-})}^{q_{c}-q}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tau_{\ell},\tau_{\ell}^{\prime}\subset\tau_{\ell-1}\\
dist(\tau_{\ell},\tau_{\ell}^{\prime})\gtrsim\lambda\delta_{K}^{\ell}\end{subarray}}\iint\big|S_{\lambda}f_{\tau_{\ell}}(x,t)S_{\lambda}f_{\tau_{\ell}^{\prime}}(x,t)\big|^{\frac{q}{2}}dxdt\\
&\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\Big(\big(\lambda\delta^{-1}\big)^{\frac{n-1}{4}}\delta_{K}^{\frac{n-1}{2}(\ell-1)}\Big)^{q_{c}-q}\lambda^{q\frac{n-1}{2}-n+\varepsilon}\delta_{K}^{\ell(q\frac{n-1}{2}-n-1)}\delta^{-1}\delta_{K}^{-Cq}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tau_{\ell},\tau_{\ell}^{\prime}\subset\tau_{\ell-1}\\
dist(\tau_{\ell},\tau_{\ell}^{\prime})\gtrsim\lambda\delta_{K}^{\ell}\end{subarray}}\Big(\left\lVert f_{\tau_{\ell}}\right\rVert_{2}\left\lVert f_{\tau_{\ell}^{\prime}}\right\rVert_{2}\Big)^{\frac{q}{2}}\\
&\leq\lambda^{q\frac{n-1}{2}-n+\varepsilon}\delta_{K}^{\ell(q\frac{n-1}{2}-n-1)}\delta^{-1}\delta_{K}^{-Cq}\Big(\big(\lambda\delta^{-1}\big)^{\frac{n-1}{4}}\delta_{K}^{\frac{n-1}{2}(\ell-1)}\Big)^{q_{c}-q}\big(\#\{\tau_{\ell}\subset\tau_{\ell-1}\}\big)^{2}\left\lVert f_{\tau_{\ell-1}}\right\rVert_{2}^{q}\\
&\lesssim\lambda^{q\frac{n-1}{2}-n+\varepsilon}\delta_{K}^{\ell(q\frac{n-1}{2}-n-1)}\delta^{-1}\delta_{K}^{-Cq}\Big(\big(\lambda\delta^{-1}\big)^{\frac{n-1}{4}}\delta_{K}^{\frac{n-1}{2}(\ell-1)}\Big)^{q_{c}-q}\delta_{K}^{-2(n-1)}\left\lVert f_{\tau_{\ell-1}}\right\rVert_{2}^{q}.\end{split}

Note that for the exponent on the first factor of
\delta_{K}^{\ell}
we have

(5.10)

q\frac{n-1}{2}-n-1=\frac{n-1}{2}\big(q-\frac{2(n+1)}{n-1}\big)=-\frac{n-1}{2}(q_{c}-q).

Thus, the first factor of
\delta_{K}^{\ell}
cancels out with the second factor. Also, if we collect the exponents on the factors of
\lambda
we have

(5.11)

q\frac{n-1}{2}-n+\frac{n-1}{4}(q_{c}-q)=\frac{n-1}{4}(q_{c}+q)-n=\frac{n-1}{n}.

Thus, (
5.9
) yields

(5.12)

II\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{n}+\varepsilon}\delta^{-\frac{n+1}{n}}\delta_{K}^{-C^{\prime}}\left\lVert f_{\tau_{\ell-1}}\right\rVert_{2}^{q}=\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{n}+\varepsilon}\delta^{-\frac{n+1}{n}}\delta_{K}^{-C^{\prime}}\left\lVert f_{\tau_{\ell-1}}\right\rVert_{2}^{q_{c}}.

where the last equality follows from the fact that
\left\lVert f_{\tau_{\ell-1}}\right\rVert_{2}=1
.

Combining (
5.7
), (
5.8
) and (
5.12
) we have

(5.13)

\begin{split}\left\lVert S_{\lambda}f_{\tau_{\ell-1}}\right\rVert_{L^{q_{c}}(A_{\tau_{\ell-1}}^{-})}^{q_{c}}&\leq\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert S_{\lambda}f_{\tau_{\ell-1}}\right\rVert_{L^{q_{c}}(A_{\tau_{\ell-1}}^{-})}^{q_{c}}+C\Big(\sum_{\tau_{\ell}\subset\tau_{\ell-1}}\left\lVert S_{\lambda}f_{\tau_{\ell}}\right\rVert_{L^{q_{c}}(\mathbb{T}^{n-1}\times[0,\delta^{-1}])}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{q_{c}}{2}}\\
&+C_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{n}+\varepsilon}\delta^{-\frac{n+1}{n}}\delta_{K}^{-C}\left\lVert f_{\tau_{\ell-1}}\right\rVert_{2}^{q_{c}}\end{split}

which yields Proposition 3.3.

5.1.
Consequences of Bilinear Restriction and Proof of Lemma 5.2

We now turn to the proof of Lemma 5.2. As we stated before, Lemma 5.3 will be a consequence of Tao’s sharp bilinear restriction estimate for the paraboloid from
[
19
]
. Similarly to our result, this bilinear estimate yielded improved results for the linear version of the restriction problem by using the result of Tao-Vargas-Vega in
[
20
]
. Their methods inspired the variable-coefficient analogue that was developed and used in the work of Blair-Huang-Sogge
[
1
]
,
[
2
]
and Huang-Sogge
[
13
]
,
[
14
]
.

Before we state the bilinear restriction estimate, we need to introduce the mathematical objects involved in its statement. For any
f:[-1,1]^{n-1}\to\mathbb{C}
define the extension operator for the paraboloid

(5.14)

Ef(x,t)=\int_{[-1,1]^{n-1}}f(\xi)e(x\cdot\xi+t|\xi|^{2})d\xi.

Also, if
S\subset[-1,1]^{n-1}
let

(5.15)

E_{S}f(x,t)=\int_{S}f(\xi)e(x\cdot\xi+t|\xi|^{2})d\xi

Now, we state the spatially localized version of the Bilinear Restriction Estimate.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
n\geq 2
and let
\omega_{1},\omega_{2}
be two cubes in
[-1,1]^{n-1}
with
\text{dist}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})>0
. Then for each
p\geq\frac{2(n+2)}{n}
, any ball
B_{R}
of radius
R\gtrsim 1
,
f:\omega_{1}\cup\omega_{2}\to\mathbb{C}
, and any
\varepsilon>0
we have

(5.16)

\left\lVert\big|E_{\omega_{1}}fE_{\omega_{2}}f\big|^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\rVert_{L^{p}(B_{R})}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}R^{\varepsilon}\Big(\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\omega_{1})}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\omega_{2})}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

This is roughly the statement of Theorem 1.1 in
[
19
]
. Note that the implicit constant in Theorem 5.3 depends on the distance between the two cubes
\omega_{1}
and
\omega_{2}
. To better understand this dependence, we introduce a bit of notation. Let
C_{R}(q,D)
be the smallest
C>0
such that for any two cubes
\omega_{1},\omega_{2}\subset[-1,1]^{n-1}
with
dist(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})\gtrsim D
and
f:\omega_{1}\cup\omega_{2}\to\mathbb{C}
the following inequality holds on any ball
B_{R}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
:

(5.17)

\left\lVert\big|E_{\omega_{1}}fE_{\omega_{2}}f\big|^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\rVert_{L^{q}(B_{R})}\leq C\Big(\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\omega_{1})}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\omega_{2})}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

A parabolic rescaling argument similar to the one used in Proposition 4.2 in
[
7
]
gives us the following lemma.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
\omega_{1}
and
\omega_{2}
be two cubes in
[-1,1]^{n-1}
of side length
0<D\lesssim 1
. Assume that the distance between the two centers of the two cubes is
\gtrsim D
. Then for any
1\leq p\leq\infty
and any
f:\omega_{1}\cup\omega_{2}\to\mathbb{C}
we have

(5.18)

\left\lVert\big|E_{\omega_{1}}fE_{\omega_{2}}f\big|^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\rVert_{L^{p}(B_{R})}\lesssim D^{\frac{n-1}{2}-\frac{n+1}{p}}C_{RD}\Big(p,\frac{1}{2}\Big)\Big(\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\omega_{1})}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\omega_{2})}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

In other words, Lemma 5.2 is saying that

(5.19)

C_{R}(q,D)\lesssim D^{\frac{n-1}{2}-\frac{n+1}{p}}C_{RD}\Big(q,\frac{1}{2}\Big).

If
q=\frac{2(n+2)}{n}
then Theorem 5.1 implies that

(5.20)

C_{R}\Big(q,\frac{1}{2}\Big)\lesssim_{\varepsilon}R^{\varepsilon}\ \text{for}\ R\gtrsim 1

and combining this with (
5.19
) we have

(5.21)

C_{R}(q,D)\lesssim_{\varepsilon}D^{\frac{n-1}{2}-\frac{n+1}{q}}R^{\varepsilon}D^{\varepsilon}\ \text{for}\ RD\gtrsim 1.

Now, we once again consider two cubes
\omega_{1},\omega_{2}
in
[-1,1]^{n-1}
with side length
\sim D
and
dist(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})\gtrsim D
. For any
S\subset[-1,1]^{n-1}
, let
N_{S}(R^{-1})=\{(\xi,|\xi|^{2}+\rho)\ :\ \xi\in S,|\rho|<R^{-1}\}
. It is a straightforward exercise using a change of variables and Minkowski’s integral inequality to see that we have the following estimate for any two functions
F_{1},F_{2}
with
\text{supp}\widehat{F_{i}}\subset N_{\omega_{i}}(R^{-1})
,
i=1,2
:

(5.22)

\left\lVert\big|F_{1}F_{2}\big|^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\rVert_{L^{p}(B_{R})}\lesssim C_{R}(p,D)R^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\left\lVert\widehat{F_{1}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(N_{\omega_{1}}(R^{-1}))}\left\lVert\widehat{F_{2}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(N_{\omega_{2}}(R^{-1}))}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Finally, this estimate implies the following bilinear exponential sum bound.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
\omega_{1},\omega_{2}
be two cubes in
[-1,1]^{n-1}
with side length
\sim D>0
such that dist
(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})\gtrsim D
. Let
R\gtrsim 1
with
R^{-1}\lesssim D
and let
B_{R}
be any ball of radius
R
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Let
\Lambda_{i}=\{(\xi,|\xi|^{2})\in\mathbb{P}^{n-1}:\xi\in\omega_{i}\}
,
i=1,2
be two collections of
R^{-1}
separated points on the paraboloid and let
\Xi_{i}=\{\xi\in\omega_{i}:(\xi,|\xi|^{2})\in\Lambda_{i}\}
. Then we have

(5.23)

\left\lVert\Big|\prod_{i=1}^{2}\sum_{\xi\in\Xi_{i}}a_{\xi}e(x\cdot\xi+t|\xi|^{2})\Big|^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\rVert_{L_{x,t}^{p}(B_{R})}\lesssim R^{\frac{n-1}{2}}C_{R}(p,D)\Big(\prod_{i=1}^{2}\left\lVert a_{\xi}\right\rVert_{l^{2}(\Xi_{i})}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

This is essentially just Exercise 3.34 in
[
7
]
. Now we can combine (
5.21
) with (
5.23
) to conclude that if
q=\frac{2(n+2)}{n}
we have

(5.24)

\left\lVert\Big|\prod_{i=1}^{2}\sum_{\xi\in\Xi_{i}}a_{\xi}e(x\cdot\xi+t|\xi|^{2})\Big|^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\rVert_{L_{x,t}^{q}(B_{R})}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}R^{\frac{n-1}{2}+\varepsilon}D^{\frac{n-1}{2}-\frac{n+1}{q}+\varepsilon}\Big(\prod_{i=1}^{2}\left\lVert a_{\xi}\right\rVert_{l^{2}(\Xi_{i})}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Define the discrete extension operator
E^{\lambda}{}
so that for any
h(x)=\sum_{j\in\tilde{Q_{\lambda}}}\widehat{h}(j)e(x\cdot j)
we have

(5.25)

E^{\lambda}{h}(x,t)=\sum_{\xi\in\lambda^{-1}\tilde{Q_{\lambda}}}\widehat{h^{\lambda}}(\xi)e(x\cdot\xi+t|\xi|^{2}))

where for any
\xi\in\lambda^{-1}\tilde{Q_{\lambda}}
we have
\widehat{h^{\lambda}}(\xi)=\widehat{h}(\lambda\xi)
. We can relate this discrete extension operator to our original dilated Schrödinger operator via the following estimate:

(5.26)

\begin{split}|S_{\lambda}h(x,t)|&=\big|\eta(\delta t)\sum_{j\in\tilde{Q_{\lambda}}}\widehat{h}(j)e(x\cdot j+\lambda^{-1}t|j|^{2})\big|\lesssim\big|\sum_{\xi\in\lambda^{-1}\tilde{Q_{\lambda}}}\widehat{h}(\lambda\xi)e(x\cdot\lambda\xi+t\lambda|\xi|^{2})\big|\\
&=\big|\sum_{\xi\in\lambda^{-1}\tilde{Q_{\lambda}}}\widehat{h^{\lambda}}(\xi)e(\lambda x\cdot\xi+\lambda t|\xi|^{2})\big|=|E^{\lambda}{h}(\lambda x,\lambda t)|\end{split}

Let
\tau_{\ell}\subset\tau_{\ell-1}
, one of the cubes in our partition of
\tau_{\ell-1}
, have center
c
so that
\tau_{\ell}=c+[-\lambda\delta_{K}^{\ell},\lambda\delta_{K}^{\ell}]^{n-1}
. Then we have

(5.27)

\begin{split}\{\xi\in\lambda^{-1}\tilde{Q_{\lambda}}:\widehat{f_{\tau_{\ell}}^{\lambda}}(\xi)\neq 0\}&\subset\{\xi\in\lambda^{-1}\tilde{Q_{\lambda}}:\lambda\xi\in\tau_{\ell}\}\\
&=\{\xi\in\lambda^{-1}\tilde{Q_{\lambda}}:\xi\in\lambda^{-1}\tau_{\ell}\}=\lambda^{-1}\tilde{\tau_{\ell}}.\end{split}

where
\lambda^{-1}\tau_{\ell}=\lambda^{-1}c+[-\delta_{K}^{\ell},\delta_{K}^{\ell}]^{n-1}
. Note that
\lambda^{-1}\tau_{\ell}\subset[-2,2]^{n-1}
. Thus, we have

(5.28)

E^{\lambda}{f_{\tau_{\ell}}}(x,t)=\sum_{\xi\in\lambda^{-1}\tilde{\tau_{\ell}}}\widehat{f^{\lambda}}(\xi)e(x\cdot\xi+t|\xi|^{2}).

To prove Lemma 5.2 we start by noticing that if
dist(\tau_{\ell},\tau_{\ell}^{\prime})\gtrsim\lambda\delta_{K}^{\ell}
then
dist(\lambda^{-1}\tau_{\ell},\lambda^{-1}\tau_{\ell}^{\prime})\gtrsim\delta_{K}^{\ell}
. Thus, it follows from Lemma 5.5 with
R=\lambda
and
D=\delta_{K}^{\ell}
that for
q=\frac{2(n+2)}{n}
we have

(5.29)

\begin{split}\left\lVert\big|E^{\lambda}{f_{\tau_{\ell}}}E^{\lambda}{f_{\tau_{\ell}^{\prime}}}\big|^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\rVert_{L^{q}(B_{\lambda})}&\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{2}+\varepsilon}\delta_{K}^{\ell(\frac{n-1}{2}-\frac{n+1}{q})}\Big(\big(\sum_{\xi\in\lambda^{-1}\tilde{\tau_{\ell}}}|\widehat{f^{\lambda}}(\xi)|^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\big(\sum_{\xi\in\lambda^{-1}\tilde{\tau_{\ell}}^{\prime}}|\widehat{f^{\lambda}}(\xi)|^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&=\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{2}+\varepsilon}\delta_{K}^{\ell(\frac{n-1}{2}-\frac{n+1}{q})}\left(\left\lVert f_{\tau_{\ell}}\right\rVert_{2}\left\lVert f_{\tau_{\ell}^{\prime}}\right\rVert_{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\end{split}

for any ball
B_{\lambda}
of radius
\sim\lambda
.
We will use this to prove Lemma 5.2. Recall that we have
\tau_{\ell},\tau_{\ell}^{\prime}\subset\tau_{\ell-1}
and
dist(\tau_{\ell},\tau_{\ell}^{\prime})\gtrsim\lambda\delta_{K}^{\ell}
. Write
[0,\lambda\delta^{-1}]=\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{\delta^{-1}}I_{i}
where
|I_{i}|\sim\lambda
for each
1\leq i\leq\delta^{-1}
. Then by using (
5.26
), then rescaling before finally using (
5.29
) on each of the cubes
[0,\lambda]^{n-1}\times I_{i}
we have

(5.30)

\begin{split}\left\lVert\big|S_{\lambda}f_{\tau_{\ell}}S_{\lambda}f_{\tau_{\ell}^{\prime}}\big|^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\rVert_{L^{q}(\mathbb{T}^{n-1}\times[0,\delta^{-1}])}&=\Big(\int_{0}^{\delta^{-1}}\int_{[0,1]^{n-1}}\big|S_{\lambda}f_{\tau_{\ell}}(x,t)S_{\lambda}f_{\tau_{\ell}^{\prime}}(x,t)\big|^{\frac{q}{2}}dxdt\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\\
&\lesssim\Big(\int_{0}^{\delta^{-1}}\int_{[0,1]^{n-1}}\big|E^{\lambda}{f_{\tau_{\ell}}}(\lambda x,\lambda t)E^{\lambda}{f_{\tau_{\ell}^{\prime}}}(\lambda x,\lambda t)\big|^{\frac{q}{2}}dxdt\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\\
&=\lambda^{-\frac{n}{q}}\Big(\int_{0}^{\lambda\delta^{-1}}\int_{[0,\lambda]^{n-1}}\big|E^{\lambda}{f_{\tau_{\ell}}}(x,t)E^{\lambda}{f_{\tau_{\ell}^{\prime}}}(x,t)\big|^{\frac{q}{2}}dxdt\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\\
&=\lambda^{-\frac{n}{q}}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{\delta^{-1}}\int_{I_{i}}\int_{[0,\lambda]^{n-1}}\big|E^{\lambda}{f_{\tau_{\ell}}}(x,t)E^{\lambda}{f_{\tau_{\ell}^{\prime}}}(x,t)\big|^{\frac{q}{2}}dxdt\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\\
&=\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{\delta^{-1}}\left\lVert\big|E^{\lambda}{f_{\tau_{\ell}}}E^{\lambda}{f_{\tau_{\ell}^{\prime}}}\big|^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\rVert_{L^{q}([0,\lambda]^{n-1}\times I_{i})}^{q}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\\
&\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{2}-\frac{n}{q}+\varepsilon}\delta_{K}^{\ell(\frac{n-1}{2}-\frac{n+1}{q})}\delta^{-\frac{1}{q}}\Big(\left\lVert f_{\tau_{\ell}}\right\rVert_{2}\left\lVert f_{\tau_{\ell}^{\prime}}\right\rVert_{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\end{split}

Thus, the proof of Lemma 5.2 is complete.
∎

6.
Kernel Estimates

Recall that
\beta(\xi)=\prod_{i=1}^{d}\phi(\xi_{i})
where
\phi
is an even function,
\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}((-2,2))
and
\phi\equiv 1
on
[-1,1]
.

Proposition 6.1
.

Fix
\kappa>0
and suppose
\delta\geq\lambda^{-1+\kappa}
. For any
d\geq 1
we have

(6.1)

S_{\lambda}\big(S_{\lambda}\big)^{*}(x,t;y,s)=\eta(\delta t)\eta(\delta s)\big(\beta^{2}(D_{x}/\lambda)e^{-i\lambda(t-s)\Delta_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}\big)(x,y).

Then for
\lambda\gg 1
we have

(6.2)

\big|S_{\lambda}\big(S_{\lambda}\big)^{*}(x,t;y,s)\big|\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{d}{2}}|t-s|^{-\frac{d}{2}}(1+|t-s|)^{d}.

Proof.

The case for
d>1
follows from the
d=1
case. To see this just note that if
x=(x_{1},...,x_{d})
,
y=(y_{1},...,y_{d})
and
j=(j_{1},...,j_{d})
then

(6.3)

\begin{split}S_{\lambda}\big(S_{\lambda}\big)^{*}(x,t;y,s)&=\eta(t/T)\eta(s/T)\big(\beta^{2}(D_{x}/\lambda)e^{-i\lambda(t-s)\Delta_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}\big)(x,y)\\
&=\eta(\delta t)\eta(\delta s)\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\beta^{2}(j/\lambda)e^{i\lambda^{-1}(t-s)|j|^{2}}e((x-y)\cdot j)\\
&=\eta(\delta t)\eta(\delta s)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in\mathbb{Z}^{d};\\
|j_{i}|\leq 2\lambda\end{subarray}}\prod_{i=1}^{d}\phi^{2}(j_{i}/\lambda)e^{i\lambda^{-1}(t-s)j_{i}^{2}}e((x_{i}-y_{i})j_{i})\\
&=\eta(\delta t)\eta(\delta s)\prod_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j_{i}\in\mathbb{Z};\\
|j_{i}|\leq 2\lambda\end{subarray}}\phi^{2}(j_{i}/\lambda)e^{i\lambda^{-1}(t-s)j_{i}^{2}}e((x_{i}-y_{i})j_{i}\\
&=\eta(\delta t)\eta(\delta s)\prod_{i=1}^{d}\big(\phi^{2}(D_{x_{i}}/\lambda)e^{-i\lambda^{-1}(t-s)\Delta_{x_{i}}}\big)(x_{i},y_{i})\end{split}

where
\Delta_{x_{i}}=\partial_{x_{i}}^{2}
.

Thus, it suffices to show that if
x,y\in\mathbb{T}
then

(6.4)

\big|\eta(\delta t)\eta(\delta s)\big(\phi^{2}(D_{x}/\lambda)e^{-i\lambda^{-1}(t-s)\Delta_{\mathbb{T}}}\big)(x,y)\big|\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}|t-s|^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1+|t-s|)

We start by setting
\varphi_{\lambda}(r;t-s)=\phi^{2}(r/\lambda)e^{i\lambda^{-1}(t-s)r^{2}}
. Since
\varphi_{\lambda}
is an even function of
r
this implies that

(6.5)

\phi^{2}(D_{x}/\lambda)e^{-i\lambda^{-1}(t-s)\Delta_{\mathbb{T}}}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\widehat{\varphi_{\lambda}}(\tau;t-s)\cos{\tau\sqrt{-\Delta_{\mathbb{T}}}}d\tau.

where

(6.6)

\widehat{\varphi_{\lambda}}(\tau;t-s)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e(-r\tau)\varphi_{\lambda}(r;t-s)dr.

A simple integration by parts argument shows that

(6.7)

\partial^{k}_{\tau}\widehat{\varphi_{\lambda}}(\tau,t-s)=O(\lambda^{-N}(1+|\tau|)^{-N})\ \forall\ N\geq 0\ \text{if}\ |t-s|\leq 1\ \text{and}\ |\tau|\geq M

where
M
is a sufficiently large constant. A similar argument shows that

(6.8)

\begin{split}\partial^{k}_{\tau}\widehat{\varphi_{\lambda}}(\tau,t-s)=O(\lambda^{-N}(1+|\tau|)^{-N})&\ \forall\ N\geq 0,\\
&\text{if }\ 2^{j-1}\leq|t-s|\leq 2^{j}\ \text{and}\ |\tau|\geq M2^{j},\ j=1,2,.\ .\ .\ ,\end{split}

if
M
is fixed large enough. The support properties of the function
\phi
ensure that it suffices to take
M=100
.

Fix an even function
a\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
such that

(6.9)

a(\tau)=1\text{ if }|\tau|\leq 100\ \text{ and }\ a(\tau)=0\ \text{ if }\ |\tau|\geq 200.

So if we set

(6.10)

\tilde{S}^{\lambda}_{0}(x,t;y,s)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}a(\tau)\widehat{\varphi_{\lambda}}(\tau,t-s)\big(\cos\tau\sqrt{-\Delta_{\mathbb{T}}}\big)(x,y)d\tau

then

(6.11)

\tilde{S}^{\lambda}_{0}(x,t;y,s)-\big(\phi^{2}(D_{x}/\lambda)e^{-i\lambda^{-1}(t-s)\Delta_{\mathbb{T}}}\big)(x,y)=O(\lambda^{-N})\ \forall\ N\geq 0\ \text{ if }\ |t-s|\leq 1.

Also, if

(6.12)

\tilde{S}^{\lambda}_{j}(x,t;y,s)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}a(2^{-j}\tau)\widehat{\varphi_{\lambda}}(\tau,t-s)\big(\cos\tau\sqrt{-\Delta_{\mathbb{T}}}\big)(x,y)d\tau

then

(6.13)

\begin{split}\tilde{S}^{\lambda}_{j}(x,t;y,s)-\big(\phi^{2}(D_{x}/\lambda)e^{-i\lambda^{-1}(t-s)\Delta_{\mathbb{T}}}\big)(x,y)&=O(\lambda^{-N})\ \forall\ N\geq 0\\
&\text{if }\ 2^{j-1}\leq|t-s|\leq 2^{j},\ j=1,2,....\end{split}

Thus, we would be done if we should show that

(6.14)

|\tilde{S}^{\lambda}_{0}(x,t;y,s)|\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}|t-s|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\text{ if }|t-s|\leq 1

and

(6.15)

|\tilde{S}^{\lambda}_{j}(x,t;y,s)|\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}|t-s|^{-\frac{1}{2}}2^{j}\ \text{ if }\ |t-s|\in[2^{j-1},2^{j}].

Recall that the Poisson summation formula implies

(6.16)

\big(\cos\tau\sqrt{-\Delta_{\mathbb{T}}}\big)(x,y)=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\big(\cos\tau\sqrt{-\Delta_{\mathbb{R}}}\big)(x-(y+m)).

Thus, if we set

(6.17)

\begin{split}K^{\lambda}_{0}(x,t;y,s)&=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}a(\tau)\widehat{\varphi_{\lambda}}(\tau,t-s)\big(\cos\tau\sqrt{-\Delta_{\mathbb{R}}}\big)(x,y)d\tau\\
&=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}a(\tau)\widehat{\varphi_{\lambda}}(\tau,t-s)\cos(\tau|\xi|)e((x-y)\xi)d\tau d\xi\end{split}

then it follows from (
6.16
) that

(6.18)

\tilde{S}^{\lambda}_{0}(x,t;y,s)=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}K^{\lambda}_{0}(x,t;y+m,s).

Also,

(6.19)

K^{\lambda}_{0}(x,t;y,s)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\iint a(\tau)\widehat{\varphi_{\lambda}}(\tau,t-s)\cos(\tau|\xi|)e((x-y)\xi)d\tau d\xi\\
=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\phi^{2}(\xi/\lambda)e(\lambda^{-1}(t-s)|\xi|^{2}+(x-y)\xi)d\xi+O(\lambda^{-N}).

A standard stationary phase argument (see Chapter 1 of
[
18
]
) implies that

(6.20)

\int\phi^{2}(\xi/\lambda)e(\lambda^{-1}(t-s)|\xi|^{2}+(x-y)\xi)d\xi=O\big(\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}|t-s|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\big).

Also, Huygen’s Principle and the support properties of
a
imply that there is some constant
C_{1}
such that
K^{\lambda}_{0}(x,t;y,s)=0
if
|x-y|\geq C_{1}
. Thus, the number of nonzero summands in (
6.18
) is
O(1)
. Combining these two facts together yields (
6.14
).

If we set

(6.21)

K^{\lambda}_{j}(x,t;y,s)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}a(2^{-j}\tau)\widehat{\varphi_{\lambda}}(\tau,t-s)\cos\tau\sqrt{-\Delta_{\mathbb{R}}}(x,y)d\tau

then it follows from (
6.16
) that

(6.22)

\tilde{S}^{\lambda}_{j}(x,t;y,s)=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}K^{\lambda}_{j}(x,t;y+m,s).

When
|t-s|\in[2^{j-1},2^{j}]
we also have

(6.23)

K^{\lambda}_{j}(x,t;y,s)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\iint a(2^{-j}\tau)\widehat{\varphi_{\lambda}}(\tau,t-s)\cos(\tau|\xi|)e((x-y)\xi)d\tau d\xi\\
=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\phi^{2}(\xi/\lambda)e(\lambda^{-1}(t-s)|\xi|^{2}+(x-y)\xi)d\xi+O(\lambda^{-N}).

Huygen’s principle and the support properties of
a
we have
K^{\lambda}_{j}(x,t;y,s)=0
if
|x-y|\geq 2^{j}C_{1}
so the number of nonzero summands in (
6.22
) is
O(2^{j})
. Combining this with (
6.20
) yields (
6.15
).
∎

Let
I=[c-\lambda\delta_{K}^{m},c+\lambda\delta_{K}^{m}]\subset[-\lambda,\lambda]
and let
\chi\in C_{0}^{\infty}((-2,2))
and
\chi\equiv 1
on
[-1,1]
. Set
\chi_{I}(\xi)=\chi((\lambda\delta_{K}^{m})^{-1}(\xi-c))
. Then
\text{supp}\,\chi_{I}\subset 2I
and
\chi_{I}\equiv 1
on
I
. Define the following Fourier multiplier on
\mathbb{T}
:

(6.24)

\chi_{I}(D_{x})g(x)=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\widehat{g}(j)\chi_{I}(j)e(xj),\ x\in\mathbb{T}.

To prove the kernel estimates we needed in the previous sections it suffices to prove the following:

Proposition 6.2
.

Fix
\kappa>0
and suppose
\delta\geq\lambda^{-1+\kappa}
. Then for
x,y\in\mathbb{T}
we have

(6.25)

\big|\eta(\delta t)\eta(\delta s)\big(\chi_{I}^{2}(D_{x})\phi^{2}(D_{x}/\lambda)e^{-i\lambda^{-1}(t-s)\Delta_{\mathbb{T}}}\big)(x,y)\big|\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}|t-s|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\big(1+\delta_{K}^{m}|t-s|\big).

Thus, when
m=K
(so that
\delta_{K}^{m}=\delta
), since
|t-s|\leq 2\delta^{-1}
we have

(6.26)

\big|\eta(\delta t)\eta(\delta s)\big(\chi_{I}^{2}(D)\phi^{2}(D/\lambda)e^{-i\lambda^{-1}(t-s)\Delta_{\mathbb{T}}}\big)(x,y)\big|\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}|t-s|^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

Proof.

First, fix
x,y\in\mathbb{T}
and note that
\partial_{\xi}^{k}\chi_{I}(\xi)=O\big((\lambda\delta_{K}^{m})^{-k}\big)
. Also, this function is supported in an interval of length
\sim\lambda\delta_{K}^{m}
so
\check{\chi}_{I}(x)
=
O\big((\lambda\delta_{K}^{m})(1+\lambda\delta_{K}^{m}|x|)^{-N}\big)
for any
N\geq 0
. It then follows from the Poisson Summation formula that the kernel of our operator satisfies

\chi_{I}(D_{x})(x,y)=O\Big(\lambda\delta_{K}^{m}\big(1+\lambda\delta_{K}^{m}|x-y|\big)^{-N}\Big)
for any
N\geq 0
. It follows that

(6.27)

\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\check{\chi}_{I}(z)|dz,\ \sup_{y\in\mathbb{T}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}|\chi_{I}(D_{x})(x,y)|dx,\ \sup_{x\in\mathbb{T}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}|\chi_{I}(D_{x})(x,y)|dy\leq C.

Thus, it follows from Proposition 5.1 that we obtain the desired bounds in proposition 5.2 if
|t-s|\leq 1
.

Also, (
6.27
) implies that if we let

(6.28)

K^{\lambda}_{I}(x,t;y,s)=\eta(\delta t)\eta(\delta s)\big(\chi_{I}(D_{x})\phi^{2}(D_{x}/\lambda)e^{-i\lambda^{-1}(t-s)\Delta_{\mathbb{T}}}\big)(x,y)

then it suffices to see that this kernel satisfies the desired bounds if
|t-s|\geq 1
. Also, recall that

(6.29)

\chi_{I}(\xi)=\chi\big((\lambda\delta_{K}^{m})^{-1}(\xi-c)\big)

and

(6.30)

\text{supp}\,\chi_{I}\subset 2I=[c-2\lambda\delta_{K}^{m},c+2\lambda\delta_{K}^{m}]

If we let
K^{\lambda}(x,t;y,s)
be the kernel of the multiplier operator
\eta(\delta t)\eta(\delta s)\phi^{2}(D_{x}/\lambda)e^{-i\lambda^{-1}(t-s)\Delta_{\mathbb{T}}}
then using the Poisson Summation formula and writing
\chi_{I}
in terms of its inverse fourier transform we have

(6.31)

\begin{split}K^{\lambda}_{I}(x,t;y,s)&=\eta(\delta t)\eta(\delta s)\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\chi_{I}(\xi)\phi^{2}(\xi/\lambda)e((x-y-j)\xi+\lambda^{-1}(t-s)|\xi|^{2})d\xi\\
&=\eta(\delta t)\eta(\delta s)\int_{\mathbb{R}}\check{\chi}_{I}\ (x-z)K^{\lambda}(z,t;y,s)dz.\end{split}

Further, if we repeat the arguments from the proof of Proposition 5.1 then we see that for
|t-s|\in[2^{j-1},2^{j}]
we have

(6.32)

K^{\lambda}(z,t;y,s)=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\tilde{K}^{\lambda}(z,t;y+j,s)+O(\lambda^{-N})

where

(6.33)

\tilde{K}^{\lambda}(z,t;y,t)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi^{2}(\xi/\lambda)e(\lambda^{-1}(t-s)|\xi|^{2}+(z-y)\xi)d\xi.

As before, the sum in (
6.32
) is over the
j\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
|z-(y+j)|\lesssim|t-s|
due to Huygen’s principle (in particular, the sum is finite). It follows that

(6.34)

K^{\lambda}_{I}(x,t;y,s)=\eta(\delta t)\eta(\delta s)\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\check{\chi_{I}}\ (x-z)\tilde{K}^{\lambda}(z,t;y+j,s)dz+O\big(\lambda^{-N}\big)

We recall the estimate (
6.20
), which says that

(6.35)

\big|\tilde{K}^{\lambda}(z,t;y,s)\big|\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}|t-s|^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

It follows from (
6.27
) and (
6.35
) that for each fixed
j
we have

(6.36)

\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\check{\chi}_{I}\ (x-z)\tilde{K}^{\lambda}(z,t;y+j,s)dz\Big|\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}|t-s|^{-\frac{1}{2}}

Thus, in order to finish the proof of Proposition 5.2 it suffices to see that that number of non-negligible summands in (
6.34
) is
O\big(\delta_{K}^{m}|t-s|\big)
.

Note that

(6.37)

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\check{\chi}_{I}\ (x-z)\tilde{K}^{\lambda}(z,t;y+j,s)dz=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\chi_{I}(\xi)\phi^{2}(\xi/\lambda)e((x-y-j)\xi+\lambda^{-1}(t-s)|\xi|^{2})d\xi.

Recalling (
6.30
) we can integrate by parts to see that this integral is
O(\lambda^{-N})
for all
N\geq 0
if for fixed
x,y,t
and
s
,
j
is not contained in the set

(6.38)

\{j\in\mathbb{Z}\ :\ |x-y-j|\in\big[2|t-s|(\lambda^{-1}c-C_{0}\delta_{K}^{m}),2|t-s|(\lambda^{-1}c+C_{0}\delta_{K}^{m})\big]\}

where
C_{0}
is a large fixed constant. This is an interval of length
\sim\delta_{K}^{m}|t-s|
so we have that

(6.39)

\#\{j\in\mathbb{Z}\ :\ |x-y-j|\in\big[2|t-s|(\lambda^{-1}c-C\delta_{K}^{m}),2|t-s|(\lambda^{-1}c+C\delta_{K}^{m})\big]\}=O\big(\delta_{K}^{m}|t-s|\big).

Thus, the proof of Proposition 5.2 is complete.
∎

Proposition 5.2 immediately implies the analogous
d
-dimensional estimates that we require. Let
Q=\prod_{i=1}^{d}I_{i}
be a cube of side-length
2\lambda\delta_{K}^{m}
centered at
c=(c_{1},...,c_{d})
contained in
[-\lambda,\lambda]^{d}
with
I_{i}=[c_{i}-\lambda\delta_{K}^{m},c_{i}+\lambda\delta_{K}^{m}]\subset[-\lambda,\lambda]
. For
\xi=(\xi_{1},...,\xi_{d})
let

(6.40)

\chi_{Q}(\xi)=\prod_{i=1}^{d}\chi_{I_{i}}(\xi_{i})

Note that

(6.41)

\chi_{Q}\equiv 1\ \text{on}\ Q\ \text{and}\ \text{supp}\chi_{Q}\subset 2Q.

As in the one-dimensional case, define the Fourier multiplier on
\mathbb{T}^{d}

(6.42)

\chi_{Q}(D_{x})g(x)=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\widehat{g}(j)\chi_{\delta_{K}^{m}}(j)e(x\cdot j),\ x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}.

Then, using a similar argument to the one used to reduce the
d
-dimensional estimate in Proposition 5.1 to its
1
-dimensional analog, Proposition 5.2 implies the following:

Corollary 6.0.1
.

Fix
\kappa>0
and suppose
\delta\geq\lambda^{-1+\kappa}
. If
x,y\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
then

(6.43)

\big|\eta(\delta t)\eta(\delta s)\big(\chi_{Q}^{2}(D_{x})\beta^{2}(D_{x}/\lambda)e^{-i\lambda^{-1}(t-s)\Delta_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}\big)(x,y)\big|\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{d}{2}}|t-s|^{-\frac{d}{2}}\big(1+\delta_{K}^{m}|t-s|\big)^{d}.

Thus, when
m=K
(so that
\delta_{K}^{m}=\delta
), since
|t-s|\leq 2\delta^{-1}
we have

(6.44)

\big|\eta(\delta t)\eta(\delta s)\big(\chi_{Q}^{2}(D_{x})\beta^{2}(D_{x}/\lambda)e^{-i\lambda^{-1}(t-s)\Delta_{\mathbb{T}}}\big)(x,y)\big|\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{d}{2}}|t-s|^{-\frac{d}{2}}.

7.
Proof of Theorem 1

We now turn to the Proof of Theorem 1. As mentioned earlier, the proof follows from applying Proposition 3.1 for
1\leq\ell<K
. We also need an estimate when
\ell=K
. Note that when
\ell=K
we have
\delta_{K}^{K}=\delta
. Let
Q\subset Q_{\lambda}
be a cube of side-length
2\lambda\delta
. Let
\chi_{Q}
be defined as in Section 6. Recall that

(7.1)

S_{\lambda}\chi_{Q}(D_{x})f(x,t)=S_{\lambda,t}\chi_{Q}(D_{x})f(x).

Further, since

(7.2)

S_{\lambda,t}\chi_{Q}(D_{x})\big(S_{\lambda,s}\chi_{Q}(D_{x})\big)^{*}(x,y)=\eta(\delta t)\eta(\delta s)\big(\chi_{Q}^{2}(D_{x})\beta^{2}(D_{x}/\lambda)e^{-i\lambda^{-1}(t-s)\Delta_{\mathbb{T}^{n-1}}}\big)(x,y)

we can use the kernel estimates in Corollary 6.0.1 to conclude that

(7.3)

\left\lVert S_{\lambda,t}\chi_{Q}(D_{x})\big(S_{\lambda,s}\chi_{Q}(D_{x})\big)^{*}f\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n-1})}\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{2}}|t-s|^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{1}

and by Parseval’s Theorem

(7.4)

\left\lVert S_{\lambda,t}\chi_{Q}(D_{x})f\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n-1})}\lesssim\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{2}.

Then we can use a
TT^{*}
argument such as in Corollary 0.3.7 in
[
18
]
to conclude that

(7.5)

\left\lVert S_{\lambda}\chi_{Q}(D_{x})f\right\rVert_{L^{q_{c}}(\mathbb{T}^{n-1}\times[0,\delta^{-1}])}\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{1}{q_{c}}}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{2}.

Now, suppose
\tau_{K-1}\subset Q_{\lambda}
is a cube of side-length
2\lambda\delta_{K}^{K-1}
. Then by Proposition 3.1 we have

(7.6)

\begin{split}\left\lVert S_{\lambda}f_{\tau_{K-1}}\right\rVert_{L^{q_{c}}(\mathbb{T}^{n-1}\times[0,\delta^{-1}])}\leq\big(C_{1}\lambda^{\frac{1}{q_{c}}}+C_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{n}\frac{1}{q_{c}}+\varepsilon}\delta_{K}^{-C}\delta^{-\frac{1}{q_{c}}}\big)\left\lVert f_{\tau_{k-1}}\right\rVert_{2}\\
+C_{2}\Big(\sum_{\tau_{K}\subset\tau_{K-1}}\left\lVert S_{\lambda}f_{\tau_{K}}\right\rVert_{L^{q_{c}}(\mathbb{T}^{n-1}\times[0,\delta^{-1}])}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\end{split}

By (
7.5
) we have

(7.7)

\left\lVert S_{\lambda}f_{\tau_{K}}\right\rVert_{L^{q_{c}}(\mathbb{T}^{n-1}\times[0,\delta^{-1}])}=\left\lVert S_{\lambda}\chi_{\tau_{K}}(D_{x})f_{\tau_{K}}\right\rVert_{L^{q_{c}}(\mathbb{T}^{n-1}\times[0,\delta^{-1}])}\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{1}{q_{c}}}\left\lVert f_{\tau_{K}}\right\rVert_{2}

Combining this with (
7.6
) and using the orthogonality of the functions
\{f_{\tau_{K}}\}_{\tau_{K}\subset\tau_{K-1}}
we have

(7.8)

\left\lVert S_{\lambda}f_{\tau_{K-1}}\right\rVert_{L^{q_{c}}(\mathbb{T}^{n-1}\times[0,\delta^{-1}])}\leq C_{\varepsilon}\big(\lambda^{\frac{1}{q_{c}}}+\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{n}\frac{1}{q_{c}}+\varepsilon}\delta^{-\frac{n+1}{n}\frac{1}{q_{c}}}\big)\left\lVert f_{\tau_{K-1}}\right\rVert_{2}.

Now, suppose we know that

(7.9)

\left\lVert S_{\lambda}f_{\tau_{\ell}}\right\rVert_{L^{q_{c}}(\mathbb{T}^{n-1}\times[0,\delta^{-1}])}\leq C_{\varepsilon}\big(\lambda^{\frac{1}{q_{c}}}+\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{n}\frac{1}{q_{c}}+\varepsilon}\delta_{K}^{-C}\delta^{-\frac{n+1}{n}\frac{1}{q_{c}}}\big)\left\lVert f_{\tau_{\ell}}\right\rVert_{2}

for any cube
\tau_{\ell}\subset Q_{\lambda}
of side-length
2\lambda\delta_{K}^{\ell}
. Fix a cube
\tau_{\ell-1}\subset Q_{\lambda}
. Using Proposition 3.1, (
7.9
) and the orthogonality of the functions
\{f_{\tau_{\ell}}\}_{\tau_{\ell}\subset\tau_{\ell-1}}
we have

(7.10)

\begin{split}\left\lVert S_{\lambda}f_{\tau_{\ell-1}}\right\rVert_{L^{q_{c}}(\mathbb{T}^{n-1}\times[0,\delta^{-1}])}\leq\big(C_{1}\lambda^{\frac{1}{q_{c}}}+C_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{n}\frac{1}{q_{c}}+\varepsilon}\delta_{K}^{-C}\delta^{-\frac{n+1}{n}\frac{1}{q_{c}}}\big)\left\lVert f_{\tau_{\ell-1}}\right\rVert_{2}\\
+C_{2}\Big(\sum_{\tau_{\ell}\subset\tau_{\ell-1}}\left\lVert S_{\lambda}f_{\tau_{\ell}}\right\rVert_{L^{q_{c}}(\mathbb{T}^{n-1}\times[0,\delta^{-1}])}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
\lesssim C_{\varepsilon}\big(\lambda^{\frac{1}{q_{c}}}+\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{n}\frac{1}{q_{c}}+\varepsilon}\delta_{K}^{-C}\delta^{-\frac{n+1}{n}\frac{1}{q_{c}}}\big)\left\lVert f_{\tau_{\ell-1}}\right\rVert_{2}\end{split}

Iterating this argument (starting with
\ell=K
and working back up until
\ell=1
) yields that for any function
f(x)=\sum_{k\in\tilde{Q_{\lambda}}}\widehat{f}(k)e(x\cdot k)
we have

(7.11)

\left\lVert S_{\lambda}f\right\rVert_{L^{q_{c}}(\mathbb{T}^{n-1}\times[0,\delta^{-1}])}\leq C_{\varepsilon,K}\big(\lambda^{\frac{1}{q_{c}}}+\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{n}\frac{1}{q_{c}}+\varepsilon}\delta_{K}^{-C}\delta^{-\frac{n+1}{n}\frac{1}{q_{c}}}\big)\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{2}.

Note that the constant
C_{\varepsilon,K}
depends on
\varepsilon
and
K
(in particular, the constant depends on the number of times we iterate the above argument, which is exactly
K
). Now, choose
K
large enough so that
\frac{C}{K}<\varepsilon
. Then
\delta_{K}^{-C}=\delta^{-\frac{C}{K}}<\delta^{-\varepsilon}<\lambda^{\varepsilon}
. Further, note that

(7.12)

\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{n}\frac{1}{q_{c}}+\varepsilon}\delta_{K}^{-C}\delta^{-\frac{n+1}{n}\frac{1}{q_{c}}}\leq\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{n}\frac{1}{q_{c}}+2\varepsilon}\delta^{-\frac{n+1}{n}\frac{1}{q_{c}}}\leq\lambda^{\frac{1}{q_{c}}},\ \text{if}\ \delta\geq\lambda^{-\frac{1}{n+1}+2\varepsilon}.

Thus, for any
\varepsilon>0
, if
\delta\geq\lambda^{-\frac{1}{n+1}+\varepsilon}
then

(7.13)

\left\lVert S_{\lambda}f\right\rVert_{L^{q_{c}}(\mathbb{T}^{n-1}\times[0,\delta^{-1}])}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{\frac{1}{q_{c}}}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{2}

and by rescaling this implies

(7.14)

\left\lVert e^{-it\Delta_{\mathbb{T}^{n-1}}}\beta(D_{x}/\lambda)f\right\rVert_{L^{q_{c}}(\mathbb{T}^{n-1}\times[0,\frac{1}{\lambda\delta}])}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{2}

so the proof of Theorem 1 is complete.
∎

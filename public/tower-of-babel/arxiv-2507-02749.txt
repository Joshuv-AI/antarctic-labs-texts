Neutron stars in degenerate higher-order scalar-tensor theories: Axial perturbations
Hamza Boumaza, David Langlois

Abstract
We study the axial (or odd-parity) perturbations of neutron stars in a one-parameter subclass of Degenerate Higher-Order Scalar-tensor (DHOST) theories. After recalling the equilibrium neutron star configurations obtained in a previous work by solving the generalised Tolman-Oppenheimer-Volkoff equations in DHOST theories, we derive the action at quadratic order in linear perturbations of axial type. We then compute the quasi-normal modes (QNMs) for several values of the modified gravity parameter and various equations of state, observing deviations of both frequencies and damping times with respect to general relativity. We also analyze the impact of our modified gravity parameter on the universal relations relating the (rescaled) frequencies and damping times to the compactness of the neutron star.



I
Introduction

Compact objects, such as black holes and neutron stars, provide strong-gravity environments where potential deviations from general relativity could be expected to be the most significant, in contrast with weak-gravity environments such as the Solar system.
In the present article, we focus our attention on neutron stars in scalar-tensor theories of gravity, more precisely in the context of degenerate higher-order scalar-tensor (DHOST) theories
Langlois:2015cwa
;
Langlois:2015skt
;
BenAchour:2016fzp
(see also
Langlois:2018dxi
;
Kobayashi:2019hrl
for reviews).

In a previous work
Boumaza:2022abj
,
we have studied neutron star configurations in a single-parameter shift-symmetric subfamily of DHOST theories. Assuming static, and thus spherically symmetric, neutron stars, we computed their radial profile for several realistic equations of state describing the properties of nuclear matter. We found that, in these modified gravity theories, neutron stars could have masses and radii significantly larger than their GR counterparts.
We also extended our results to slowly-rotating solutions, establishing relations between the dimensionless moment of inertia and the compactness of the star
Breu:2016ufb
, relations that are quasi universal in the sense that they depend only weakly on the choice of the equation of state. Neutron stars have also been studied in other particular subfamilies of DHOST theories (see e.g.
Cisterna:2016vdx
;
Maselli:2016gxk
;
Minamitsuji:2016hkk
;
Babichev:2016jom
;
Sakstein:2016oel
;
Kobayashi:2018xvr
;
Chagoya:2018lmv
;
Boumaza:2021hzr
;
Kase:2021mix
;
Boumaza:2021lnp
;
minamitsuji2022stability
).

Beyond static solutions, it is also important to study the linear perturbations of neutron stars in modified gravity, in order to investigate their stability, as well as identify their oscillatory modes, known as quasi-normal modes (QNMs). In particular, QNMs are expected to be excited in a newly formed compact object resulting from the merger of two compacts objects in a binary. Such event possesses three phases: inspiral, merger and ringdown, during which gravitational waves are emitted. The signal in the ringdown phase can be decomposed into, mostly, quasi-normal modes, which depend on the physical characteristics of the compact object, as well as on the model of gravity. Analyzing the QNM spectrum of neutron stars thus not only gives information about the nuclear equation of state but also constrains theories of gravity.

In practice, the dynamics of the linear perturbations can be obtained by expanding the total action,
i.e. the sum of the gravitational action and of the matter action, up to second order in perturbations, as was performed, for instance, in
Kase:2021mix
for neutron stars in Horndeski theories.
For a static and spherically symmetric background, the perturbations can be decomposed into odd-parity, or axial, modes and even-parity, or polar, modes.
In the present work, we concentrate on the axial modes of the neutron stars studied in
Boumaza:2022abj
. Axial perturbations, as they are odd-parity, do not couple to the fluid and scalar field perturbations. They obey an equation of motion that generalises the well-known Regge-Wheeler equation of GR. In fact, we show that this equation is the same as that obtained in GR with another metric, dubbed effective metric and which can be deduced from the background metric by a disformal metric. A similar correspondence between DHOST theories and GR has been studied recently for black holes
Langlois:2022ulw
.

The outline of our paper is the following. In Section
II
, we introduce the general framework of DHOST theories and specify the subclass under consideration. In Section
III
, we construct static and spherically symmetric neutron star backgrounds by solving the modified TOV equations. In Section
IV
, we perform a second-order perturbative expansion of the action to derive the equation of motion for axial modes. Section
V
is devoted to the numerical computation of the fundamental
l=2
quasinormal mode and of the impact of DHOST corrections on the universal relations. We conclude in Section
VI
with a summary of our findings and discuss possible extensions of this work.
Additional details are provided in two appendices.

II
Gravity models

In this section, we briefly recall the modified gravity models that we consider. DHOST theories
Langlois:2015cwa
;
Langlois:2015skt
;
BenAchour:2016fzp
represent the most general scalar-tensor theories propagating a single scalar degree of freedom, and include, as particular subfamilies, the traditional scalar-tensor theories, Horndeski theories
horndeski1974second
and so-called Beyond Horndeski theories

;

Up to quadratic order (in second derivatives of the scalar field), their action is of the form

S_{\rm grav}[g_{\mu\nu},\varphi]=\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left(K+Q\nabla^{\mu}\nabla_{\mu}\varphi+F\,R+\sum_{i=1}^{5}A_{i}L_{i}\right)

(1)

where

,

denote
the five elementary Lagrangians that are quadratic in second order derivatives of the scalar field (contracted with the metric or the scalar field gradient), explicitly given by

\displaystyle L_{1}=\nabla^{\nu}\nabla^{\mu}\varphi\nabla_{\nu}\nabla_{\mu}\varphi

\displaystyle L_{2}=\left(\nabla^{\mu}\nabla_{\mu}\varphi\right)^{2}

\displaystyle L_{3}=\nabla^{\alpha}\nabla_{\alpha}\varphi\nabla_{\nu}\nabla_{\mu}\varphi\nabla^{\nu}\varphi\nabla^{\mu}\varphi

\displaystyle L_{4}=\nabla^{\nu}\varphi\nabla_{\nu}\nabla_{\mu}\varphi\nabla^{\mu}\nabla^{\alpha}\varphi\nabla_{\alpha}\varphi\,,\qquad

\displaystyle L_{5}=\left(\nabla^{\nu}\varphi\nabla_{\nu}\nabla_{\mu}\varphi\nabla^{\mu}\varphi\right)^{2}\,,

and the functions
K
,

,
F
and

are arbitrary, three of the functions

As in our previous work

the choice of functions

K=Q=A_{1}=A_{2}=A_{5}=0\,,\qquad A_{3}=-A_{4}=-4\frac{F_{X}}{X}\,,

(2)

which satisfy the degeneracy conditions mentioned above.
The corresponding action,

S_{\rm grav}=\int d^{4}x\,\sqrt{-g}\left(F\,R-4\frac{F_{X}}{X}(L_{3}-L_{4})\right)\,,

(3)

thus depends on the single function

.
The above action belongs to the ’Beyond Horndeski’ subfamily of DHOST theories, introduced in
Gleyzes:2014dya
;

If

Note that the condition

.

To take into account the matter in the interior of the neutron star,
the gravitational action must be supplemented by
an action representing the dynamics of the matter, which is assumed to be minimally coupled to the metric

schutz1970perfect
;
ray1972lagrangian
;
schutz1977variational
;
carter1989relativistic
;
brown1993action

and we will adopt here the action

\displaystyle S_{\rm m}=\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\,P(\mu),

(5)

where

denotes the pressure as a function of the chemical potential

, which defines the matter equation of state

(i.e.

(6)

III
Background equations

We now describe our background configuration.
For a non-rotating star, the metric is static and spherically symmetric, i.e. of the form

ds^{2}=-f(r)dt^{2}+h(r)dr^{2}+r^{2}\left(d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\!\theta\,d{\phi}^{2}\right)\,,

(7)

and the scalar field can be written as

\varphi(t,r)=q\;t+\psi(r)\,,

(8)

where
q
is a real constant. This is the most general form for the scalar field compatible with a static and spherically symmetric geometry in a shift symmetry of the theory: indeed, only the gradient of the scalar field, which is time independent, appears in the equations of motion.

Substituting the above metric (
7
) and scalar field (
8
) into the total action
S=S_{\rm grav}+S_{\rm m}
, we get, after integration by parts, the expression

S_{\rm bgd}=4\pi\int dr\,dt\left[\,r^{2}\sqrt{fh}\left(\frac{2Fh^{\prime}}{h^{2}r}+\frac{2F(h-1)}{hr^{2}}-\frac{4F_{X}X^{\prime}\left(fX+q^{2}\right)}{fhrX}+P\right)+\lambda_{0}\,(\mu\sqrt{f})^{\prime}\,\right]\,,

(9)

where a prime denotes a derivative with respect to the radial coordinate
r
and the kinetic term
X
must be seen as a function of the metric coefficients in (
7
) and of the scalar field components(
8
), namely

X=\psi^{\prime 2}/h-q^{2}/f\,.

(10)

We have also added in the action an extra term, proportional to the Lagrange multiplier
\lambda_{0}
, that enforces the constraint

\frac{\mu^{\prime}}{\mu}=-\frac{f^{\prime}}{2f}\,,

(11)

which follows from the fluid equation of motion in the background (see Appendix
A
). In this way, instead of considering the action as a functional of the three scalar fields
L
,
A
and
B
as discussed in the previous section, the action
9
is simply a functional of
\mu
, of the metric coefficients
f
and
h
and of the scalar field
\psi
.

By varying the action (
9
) with respect to
f
and

, we obtain the time and radial equations of motion which read, respectively,

\displaystyle h^{\prime}\left(\frac{F_{\text{X}}\left(-4f^{2}\left(\psi^{\prime}\right)^{4}+8fhq^{2}\left(\psi^{\prime}\right)^{2}+4h^{2}q^{4}\right)}{fh^{2}q^{2}-f^{2}h\left(\psi^{\prime}\right)^{2}}+2F\right)-\frac{8F_{\text{X}}\left(f\left(\psi^{\prime}\right)^{3}-2hq^{2}\psi^{\prime}\right)}{f\left(\psi^{\prime}\right)^{2}-hq^{2}}\psi^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle+\frac{4hq^{2}F_{\text{X}}\left(f(h+1)\left(\psi^{\prime}\right)^{2}-(h-1)hq^{2}\right)}{fr\left(f\left(\psi^{\prime}\right)^{2}-hq^{2}\right)}+\frac{2F(h-1)h}{r}-h^{2}r\rho=0,

(12)

\displaystyle f^{\prime}\left(\frac{F_{\text{X}}\left(-4f^{2}\left(\psi^{\prime}\right)^{4}+8fhq^{2}\left(\psi^{\prime}\right)^{2}+4h^{2}q^{4}\right)}{f^{2}h^{2}q^{2}-f^{3}h\left(\psi^{\prime}\right)^{2}}+\frac{2F}{f}\right)+\frac{8q^{2}F_{\text{X}}\psi^{\prime}\psi^{\prime\prime}}{hq^{2}-f\left(\psi^{\prime}\right)^{2}}

\displaystyle+\frac{F_{\text{X}}\left(4(h-1)hq^{2}\left(\psi^{\prime}\right)^{2}-4f(h+1)\left(\psi^{\prime}\right)^{4}\right)}{hr\left(hq^{2}-f\left(\psi^{\prime}\right)^{2}\right)}+\frac{F(2-2h)}{r}-hrP=0\,,

(13)

where, in the first equation, we have used

\displaystyle\lambda_{0}^{\prime}=r^{2}\sqrt{h}\,\frac{dP}{d\mu}\,,

(14)

which follows from the variation of
9

\mu
, as well as the expression of the energy density
\rho
in terms of
P(\mu)
,

\rho=\mu\frac{dP}{d\mu}-P\,.

(15)

Finally, by varying the action (
9
) with respect to
\psi
, we obtain the scalar field equation of motion. In the shift-symmetric case, this is related to the conservation of a four-dimensional current,
\nabla_{\mu}J^{\mu}=0
, which reduces, due to the symmetries of the configuration, to

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dr}\left(\sqrt{fh}\,r^{2}J^{r}\right)=0\,,

(16)

with

\displaystyle J^{r}=\frac{2\sqrt{fX+q^{2}}}{f^{3}h^{3}r^{2}X}\left\{-2F_{X}\left[hrf^{\prime}\left(fX-q^{2}\right)+f\left(fh(h+1)X-q^{2}rh^{\prime}+2hq^{2}\right)\right]\right\},

(17)

which implies (to avoid a singularity at the center)

J^{r}=0\,.

(18)

We must complete our set of equations with the equation of state
P=P(\mu)
, or equivalently
P=P(\rho)
. It can also be convenient to use the matter conservation equation
\nabla_{\mu}T^{\mu}_{\nu}=0
, which yields

\displaystyle P^{\prime}=-\frac{f^{\prime}(P+\rho)}{2f}\,.

(19)

This equation can also be derived from (
12

13

characterising the theory of gravity (
3
) and some equation of state, the above equations form a radial differential system which can be integrated numerically, providing neutron star solutions determined by their central density or, equivalently, their mass. Such configurations of neutron stars were obtained in our previous work

F=\frac{\kappa}{2}+\sigma X,\qquad\kappa\equiv(8\pi G)^{-1}\,,

(20)

where the parameter

IV
Axial perturbations

We now turn to the study of axial perturbations (see Appendix

\displaystyle ds^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-f(r)dt^{2}+h(r)dr^{2}+r^{2}\left(d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta d\phi^{2}\right)

\displaystyle+2r^{2}\sin\theta\partial_{\theta}Y_{l0}(\theta)\left(H_{0}(r,t)dtd\phi+H_{1}(r,t)drd\phi\right),

with the two perturbations

We use the notation

and

For axial perturbations, the gravitational scalar field is unperturbed, i.e.

\displaystyle u^{\mu}=\bar{u}^{\mu}+\delta u^{\mu}\,,\qquad\bar{u}^{\mu}=\frac{\delta^{\mu}_{0}}{f^{1/2}}\,,\qquad\delta u^{\mu}=\left\{0,0,0,v(r,t)\frac{\partial_{\theta}Y_{l0}(\theta)}{f^{\frac{1}{2}}\sin\theta}\right\}\,.

If we substitute the perturbed metric (
21

4

\displaystyle\delta T^{t\phi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial_{\theta}Y_{l0}(\theta)}{\sin\theta\,f}\left[PH_{0}+(\rho+P)v\right],

(23)

\displaystyle\delta T^{r\phi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\partial_{\theta}Y_{l0}(\theta)}{\sin\theta\,h}PH_{1}.

(24)

Then, using the perturbed matter conservation equation, one can easily find that

\displaystyle v=-H_{0}\,.

(25)

This shows that there is no independent degree of freedom associated with the fluid in the axial sector.

In order to derive the dynamics of the single degree of freedom associated with axial perturbations, it is convenient to expand the total action up to second order in the perturbations (see the appendices for details). After integrating by parts, we get

\displaystyle S_{\rm axial}=l(l+1)\int drdt\left[Q_{2}H_{0}^{2}-Q_{1}H_{1}^{2}+Q_{3}H_{1}H_{0}+Q_{0}(H_{0}^{\prime}-\dot{H}_{1})^{2}\right]\,,

(26)

where the coefficients of the quadratic terms are given by

\displaystyle Q_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{Fr^{4}}{2\sqrt{f\,h}},\qquad Q_{1}=\frac{\sqrt{f}Fr^{2}(l(l+1)-2)}{2\sqrt{h}},\qquad Q_{2}=\frac{h}{f}Q_{1},

\displaystyle Q_{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{2\sqrt{f}\sqrt{h}qr^{2}F_{\text{X}}\psi^{\prime}\left(f\left(\psi^{\prime}\right)^{2}\left(4F+Pr^{2}\right)-q^{2}\left(4F(h-1)+hr^{2}(2P+\rho)\right)\right)}{fFh\left(f\left(\psi^{\prime}\right)^{2}-hq^{2}\right)+2F_{\text{X}}\left(f^{2}\left(\psi^{\prime}\right)^{4}-2fhq^{2}\left(\psi^{\prime}\right)^{2}-h^{2}q^{4}\right)}\,,

17

Given the structure of the quadratic action, it is convenient to introduce the

\displaystyle\chi=\dot{H}_{1}-H_{0}^{\prime}\,.

Implementing it in the variational principle via a Lagrange multiplier

\displaystyle S^{\prime}_{\rm axial}=l(l+1)\int drdt\,\left[Q_{2}H_{0}^{2}-Q_{1}H_{1}^{2}+Q_{0}\chi^{2}+\lambda\left(\chi+H_{0}^{\prime}-\dot{H}_{1}\right)\right]\,.

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2Q_{0}\chi,\quad\lambda^{\prime}=2Q_{2}H_{0},\quad\dot{\lambda}=2Q_{1}H_{1}.

Note that there is no

\displaystyle U\equiv\frac{\lambda}{r\sqrt{F}}\,,

VI
Conclusion

In this paper, motivated by the exploration of possible deviations from general relativity in the strong-field regime, we have studied the axial quasinormal modes of neutron stars in a specific subclass of DHOST theories. As a starting point, we have used the static and spherically symmetric stellar configurations computed in our previous paper
Boumaza:2022abj
by solving the modified TOV equations with realistic equations of state. We have then obtained the dynamics of the axial perturbations by expanding the total action to second order in the axial sector. This enabled us to show the absence of ghost or gradient instabilities for the (single) degree of freedom associated with the axial mode. We then derived the equation of motion governing axial perturbations, which leads, with appropriate boundary conditions, to the QNMs of the neutron star.

Interestingly, we have noticed that, for axial modes, the correspondence between the DHOST dynamics and the usual GR one in an effective metric, discussed recently in the context of DHOST black holes
Langlois:2022ulw
(see also
Noui:2023ksf
;
Charmousis:2025xug
), is still valid for neutron stars. This can be understood by the fact that axial modes are decoupled from the fluid modes and therefore are only sensitive to the background geometry.

In our model, we have found that the fundamental
l=2
QNM exhibits a deviation from its GR counterpart in both frequency and damping time. But the QNMs are also sensitive to the choice of the equation of state for the neutron star matter. In order to disentangle the impact of modified gravity from that of the equation of state, we have looked for universal relations and identified empirical relation between the real and imaginary parts of the fundamental QNM and the compactness of the star. Our results show that these relations can allow us to discriminate between GR and our DHOST model independently of the kind of equation of state.

Overall, our findings highlight the relevance of neutron star spectroscopy to probe alternative theories of gravity. In future work, it would be interesting to extend the present analysis to polar perturbations, which are coupled to the fluid modes.

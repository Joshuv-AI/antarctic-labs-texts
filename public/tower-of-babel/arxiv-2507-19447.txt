Positive traces on certain SL ⁡ ( 2 ) \SL(2) Coulomb branches
Daniil Klyuev, Joseph Vulakh

Abstract
For a noncommutative algebra 𝒜 \mathcal{A} and an antilinear automorphism ρ \rho of 𝒜 \mathcal{A} there is a notion of a positive trace. Positive traces appear in study of supersymmetric gauge theories. We classify positive traces on 𝒜 \mathcal{A} in two cases. The first case is when 𝒜 \mathcal{A} is a quantization of a Kleinian singularity of type D D , with certain restriction on the quantization parameter. The second case is when 𝒜 = K SL ⁡ ( 2 , ℂ ⁡ [ [ t ] ] ) ⋊ ℂ q × ​ ( Gr PGL 2 ) \mathcal{A}=K^{\SL(2,\mathbb{C}[[t]])\rtimes\mathbb{C}_{q}^{\times}}(\operatorname{Gr}_{\operatorname{PGL}_{2}}) is an algebra containing K K -theoretic Coulomb branches of pure SL ⁡ ( 2 ) \SL(2) and PGL ⁡ ( 2 ) \operatorname{PGL}(2) gauge theories.

Introduction

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an algebra with an automorphism
g
. All algebras will be associative, unital and over
\mathbb{C}
. A
twisted trace
is a map
T\colon\mathcal{A}\to\mathbb{C}
such that
T(ab)=T(bg(a))
for all
a,b\in\mathcal{A}
.

Let
\rho
be an antilinear automorphism of
\mathcal{A}
and
g=\rho^{2}
. A
g
-twisted trace
T
is called
positive
if
T(a\rho(a))>0
for all nonzero
a\in\mathcal{A}
. In this case
(a,b)=T(a\rho(b))
is a positive definite Hermitian form on
\mathcal{A}
satisfying
(a\rho(b),c)=(a,bc)
.

In the case when
\mathcal{A}
is filtered, Etingof and Stryker
ES 20
proved that twisted traces satisfying a certain nondegeneracy condition correspond to short nondegenerate star-products on
\gr\mathcal{A}
introduced by Beem, Peelaers and Rastelli
BPR 16
. Positive traces correspond to unitary short star-products. In the case when
\mathcal{A}
comes from a superconformal field theory, there is a certain unitary short star-product on
\gr\mathcal{A}
that reflects the properties of the corresponding field theory. In some cases, a positive trace is unique up to scaling, which allows us to compute the corresponding unitary short star-product.

Another physical motivation, which does not require
\mathcal{A}
to be filtered, comes from the work of Gaiotto
Gai 25
and Gaiotto and Teschner
GT 24
. When
\mathcal{A}
is a quantized Higgs/Coulomb branch of a superconformal field theory, there is a certain physically motivated choice of a conjugation
\rho
and a trace
T
. It is expected that
T
is positive. If this is the case, let
\mathcal{H}
be a completion of
\mathcal{A}
with respect to
(\cdot,\cdot)
. Then
\mathcal{A}
acts on
\mathcal{H}
by unbounded operators of left and right multiplication
L_{a}
,
R_{a}
. It is expected that we can choose the domain of the operators so that
L_{a}^{*}=R_{\rho(a)}
. Also, the image
v_{0}
of
1\in\mathcal{A}
in
\mathcal{H}
satisfies
L_{a}v_{0}=R_{a}v_{0}
. The algebra
\mathcal{A}
with the action on
\mathcal{H}
provides more information about the field theory than the algebra
\mathcal{A}
on its own.

In this article we classify positive traces in the following two cases, described in more details below. First is when
\mathcal{A}
is a deformation of a Kleinian singularity of type
D
satisfying a certain condition. Second is when
\mathcal{A}
is an algebra containing quantized
K
-theoretic Coulomb branches of pure
\SL(2)
and
\operatorname{PGL}(2)
gauge theories.

A Kleinian singularity is a quotient
\mathbb{C}^{2}/G
,
where
G
is a finite subgroup
of the special linear group
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
.
The Kleinian singularity
\mathbb{C}^{2}/G

has as its coordinate ring
the commutative graded algebra
A=\mathbb{C}[u,v]^{G}

of
G
-invariant polynomials in two variables.

Finite subgroups of
\SL(2,\mathbb{C})
are classified up to conjugation by simply-laced Dynkin diagrams. The
A_{n-1}
diagram corresponds to the cyclic subgroup generated by

\begin{pmatrix}e^{\frac{2\pi i}{n}}&0\\
0&e^{-\frac{2\pi i}{n}}\end{pmatrix}.

The
D_{n-1}
diagram corresponds to the group generated by

\begin{pmatrix}e^{\frac{\pi i}{n}}&0\\
0&e^{-\frac{\pi i}{n}}\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{and}\quad h=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{pmatrix},

which contains
\mathbb{Z}/2n\mathbb{Z}
as a subgroup of order two. The element
h
defines an automorphism
\mathbb{C}[u,v]^{Z/2n\mathbb{Z}}
of order two and the invariant subalgebra is precisely the Kleinian singularity of type
D_{n}
.

Kleinian singularities are graded algebras. We can look at their noncommutative filtered deformations, that is, filtered associative algebras
\mathcal{A}

with associated graded algebra
\gr\mathcal{A}
equal to
A
,
and try to classify positive traces on these deformations.
Twisted and positive traces on deformations and
q
-deformations of Kleinian singularities of type
A
were classified by the first author with Etingof, Rains, Stryker in
EKRS 21
and in
Kly 22
. From a physical point of view positive traces were studied in
DG 21
;
DFPY 18
;
DPY 16
.

In the first chapter, we consider the next case, deformations of type
D
.
Not all deformations of Kleinian singularities of type
D
can be obtained as a subalgebra inside a Kleinian singularity of type
A
, but in the first chapter we consider the ones that do. Moreover, we assume that the quantization parameter is generic.

We obtain classification results for traces
on deformations of Kleinian singularities of type D
analogous to those obtained in
EKRS 21
for type A.
Most importantly, we show that, under certain assumptions,
all positive traces of filtered deformations of Kleinian singularities
of type D
are restrictions of positive traces
of filtered deformations of Kleinian singularities of type A.

We begin by outlining the technical preliminaries for our work
in
Section

1.1
.
Then, in
Section

1.2
,
we introduce several algebra elements
which are important for our results
and prove various identities and relations among them.
We characterize traces of deformations
of Kleinian singularities of type D
that satisfy the assumptions above in
Section

1.3
,
and use this characterization
to obtain an analytic formula for traces
in
Section

1.4
.
Finally, in
Section

1.5
, we prove our main result
regarding positive traces.

We now turn to the second chapter.

Let
G
be a complex reductive group and
N
be a representation of
G
. One can construct a supersymmetric gauge theory from this data. There are two algebraic varieties associated to this theory—the Higgs and Coulomb branches. A mathematical definition of Coulomb branches was given by Braverman, Finkelberg and Nakajima in
BFN 18

Each Coulomb branch comes with a quantization of its ring of functions. Quantized Coulomb branches include well-known algebras such as spherical double affine Hecke algebras or central reductions of
U(\mathfrak{sl}_{n})

In Lemma 6.9 of
BFN 18
it is proved that in the case of
G=\SL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
Coulomb branches are exactly type
D_{n}
Kleinian singularities
xy^{2}=z^{2}+x^{n-1}
plus three exceptions: the cases
n=2
,
n=1
of this equation, and the surface
xy^{2}=z^{2}+y
. If we take representation
N=(\mathbb{C}^{2})^{m}
, we get
n=m
. (in the notation of
BFN 18
, the representation is denoted by
\mathbf{N}
and our
n
corresponds to
N
in Lemma 6.9).

This fact motivates the title of the article, but we do not use any facts about Coulomb branches in the first chapter.

Coulomb branches are defined as homology of an infinite-dimensional scheme. Changing homology to K-theory we also get an algebra, called K-theoretic Coulomb branch algebra. Physically, we take 4-dimensional gauge theory with the same group
G
and matter
M
, and compactify it on a circle. Algebraically, a deformed
K
-theoretic Coulomb branch is a certain
q
-deformation of a usual Coulomb branch.

In the second part of the paper we consider the algebra
\mathcal{A}
introduced by Gaiotto and Teschner in Section 3.5 of
GT 24
. This algebra contains
K
-theoretic Coulomb branches of pure
\SL(2)
and
\operatorname{PGL}(2)
gauge theories, where pure means that
N=0
, and in this case the BFN construction gives the
G(O)
-equivariant
K
-theory of an affine Grassmannian
\operatorname{Gr}_{G}
.

Let
v,w
be variables satisfying
wv=qvw
; they generate a q-Weyl algebra. We consider the localized q-Weyl algebra
W
, inverting
v,w
and
q^{k}v-q^{-k}v^{-1}
for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Then
\mathcal{A}
as a subalgebra of
W
generated by
v^{n}+v^{n}
and
H_{a}=q^{\frac{a}{2}}(v-v^{-1})^{-1}(v^{a}w+v^{-a}w^{-1})
. This description is equivalent to the one in Section 3.5.1 of
GT 24
by taking
u_{+}=(v-v^{-1})^{-1}w
and
u_{-}=(v-v^{-1})^{-1}w^{-1}
.

The algebra
\mathcal{A}
should be equal to
K^{\SL(2,\mathbb{C}[[t]])\rtimes\mathbb{C}_{q}^{\times}}(\operatorname{Gr}_{\operatorname{PGL}_{2}})
, an object similar to the
K
-theoretic Coulomb branch of pure
\operatorname{PGL}(2)
gauge theory, but the
K
-theory is taken with respect to
\SL(2)
, not
\operatorname{PGL}(2)
. More precisely, we consider an algebra generated by the images of dressed miniscule monopole operators and K-theory of a point under the localization map
(i_{*})^{-1}\colon K^{\SL(2,\mathbb{C}[[t]])\rtimes\mathbb{C}_{q}^{\times}}(\operatorname{Gr}_{\operatorname{PGL}_{2}})\to K^{\mathbb{C}^{\times}_{v}\times\mathbb{C}^{\times}_{q}}(\operatorname{Gr}_{T})_{loc}
, where the multiplicative subset on the right is defined so that
W=K_{\mathbb{C}^{\times}_{v}\times\mathbb{C}^{\times}_{q}}(\operatorname{Gr}_{T})_{loc}
. It can be checked that the image of the class corresponding to the sheaf
O(l)
(with trivial loop torus,
\mathbb{C}^{\times}_{q}
, action) on a smooth
G(\mathbb{C}[[t]])
-orbit
\mathbb{P}^{1}\subset\operatorname{Gr}_{\operatorname{PGL}_{2}}
in
W
is
-(v-v^{-1})^{-1}(v^{l+1}w-v^{-l-1}w^{-1})
, so that the result is precisely the algebra
\mathcal{A}
.

We also specialize
q
to be a real number with
0<q<1
.

While
\mathcal{A}
does not exactly fit into the sequence of
q
-deformations of Kleinian singularities of type
D
, one could use a similar strategy to classify positive traces on deformed
K
-theoretic Coulomb branches with gauge group
\SL(2)
or
\operatorname{PGL}(2)
. Also, Kauffman skein algebras should be equal to the “mixed” Coulomb branches, where, as above, the group for the space of triples and the group in equivariant K-theory are different quotients of the same simply-connected Lie group. For adjoint representation, the flavor deformations of
K^{\SL(2,\mathbb{C}[t])\rtimes\mathbb{C}_{q}^{\times}}(\mathcal{R}_{\operatorname{PGL}(2),\mathfrak{sl}_{2}})
correspond to the skein algebras for one-punctured torus; this is proved by Allegretti and Shan
AS 24
in the paragraph after Proposition 5.5.

We classify the traces on
\mathcal{A}
with the twist given by
H_{a}\mapsto H_{a+k}
for some
k
. Geometrically, this means that we twist by some power of determinant bundle. For
0<q<1
and positive
k
we classify the positive traces with the anti-involution given by the same formula on generators.

The first step in classification is to describe the full image of
\mathcal{A}
in
W
. Compared to the above, we use slightly different inclusion, given by right fractions and not left, in the second chapter. In Section
2.1
we prove the following

Proposition

(
2.1.1
)
.

The algebra
\mathcal{A}
coincides with the set of elements
a=\sum w^{i}f_{i}
of
W
such that all
f_{i}
have simple poles only possibly at
\pm q^{j}
,
j\in\mathbb{Z}
and the corresponding residues
f_{i}^{(j,\pm)}
satisfy

1.

f_{-i}(v^{-1})=(-1)^{i}f_{i}(v)
;

2.

f_{i}^{(i,\pm)}=0
;

3.

For a positive integer
k
we have
f_{i+k}^{(i,\pm)}=(-1)^{k+1}f_{i-k}^{(i,\pm)}
, where
\pm
is the same sign on both sides.

In particular,
f_{0}^{(0,\pm)}=0
, so that
f_{0}(v)
has no poles at the unit circle.

The algebra
\mathcal{A}
has a
\mathbb{Z}/2
-grading by the degree of
w
. In Section
2.2
we prove that any twsited trace
T
can be decomposed as
T=T_{0}+T_{1}
, where
T_{i}
is supported on the
i
-th graded piece and write an analytic formula for
T_{0}
.

In Section
2.3
we prove that for a positive trace
T
we should have
T_{1}=0
and
T_{0}(\sum w^{i}a_{i})=\int_{S^{1}}a_{0}(z)\omega(z)\frac{dz}{z}
, where
\omega(z)
is a quasi-periodic function vanishing at
\pm 1
.

Then we express the positivity condition on
T
in terms of the behavior of
w
on
S^{1}
and
\sqrt{q}S^{1}
. Using this, we can compute the dimension of the cone of possible traces. In particular, for the antilinear automorphism
H_{a}\mapsto H_{a+2}
, there exists a unique positive trace. Taking into account the difference between the conventions in physics and mathematics, this corresponds to the automorphism considered by Gaiotto and Teschner in Section 3.5 of
GT 24
.

1.1

Preliminaries

Throughout the chapter,
we fix a positive even integer
n
with
m=\frac{n}{2}

and denote
\varepsilon=\exp(\frac{2\pi i}{n})
.
For a finite subgroup
G

of the special linear group
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
,
the Kleinian singularity
\mathbb{C}^{2}/G

has as its coordinate ring
the commutative graded algebra
A=\mathbb{C}[u,v]^{G}

of
G
-invariant polynomials in two variables,
with the action of
G
on
A
defined by

\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{bmatrix}\cdot P(u,v)=P(au+bv,cu+dv).

We are interested in the case when
G=\mathbb{D}_{n}
,
the dicyclic group generated by elements

g=\begin{bmatrix}\varepsilon&0\\
0&\varepsilon^{-1}\end{bmatrix},\quad\quad h=\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{bmatrix}

with
g^{n}=h^{4}=ghgh^{-1}

all equal to the group identity
and
g^{m}=h^{2}
.
The group
\mathbb{D}_{n}
acts on
\mathbb{C}[u,v]
by

g\cdot u=\varepsilon u
,
g\cdot v=\varepsilon^{-1}v
,

h\cdot u=v
,
h\cdot v=-u
,
and the invariant polynomials generating
A

are
u^{2}v^{2}
,
u^{n}+v^{n}
, and
uv(u^{n}-v^{n})
.

All elements of the algebra
A
are the sum
of homogeneous polynomials,
so
A
is graded by the degree of homogeneous polynomials.
We consider filtered deformations

\mathcal{A}
of
A
, that is, filtered associative algebras
\mathcal{A}

with associated graded algebra
\gr\mathcal{A}
equal to
A
.
In particular, we make use of the Crawley–Boevey—Holland
CBH 98

filtered deformations.
Let
\mathbb{C}[x,y]\#G
be the skew group algebra
constructed from the vector space
\mathbb{C}[x,y]\otimes\mathbb{C}[G]

with multiplication given by

(P\otimes g)(G\otimes h)=Pg(Q)\otimes gh,

where
P
and
Q
are polynomials,
g
and
h
are group elements,
and
g(Q)
denotes the action of
g
on
Q
.
For an element
c
of the center
Z(\mathbb{C}[G])
of the group algebra, let

H_{c}=(\mathbb{C}[x,y]\#G)/(xy-yx-c).

For the idempotent
e=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{g\in G}g
,
let
\mathcal{O}_{c}=eH_{c}e
, which is an algebra with unit
e
.
Losev
(
Los 22
, Theorem 3.4)

proved that
for any finite subgroup
G
of
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
,
any filtered deformation of
\mathbb{C}[x,y]^{G}

is isomorphic to some algebra
\mathcal{O}_{c}

with
c\in Z(\mathbb{C}[g])
.

A trace on
\mathcal{A}
is a linear function
T\colon\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}

satisfying
T(ab)=T(ba)
for all
a,b\in\mathcal{A}
.
We only consider traces on noncommutative algebras,
so by
(
CBH 98
, Theorem 0.4(1))
,
we may assume the coefficient of
c
on the group identity is nonzero.
Also, by
(
CBH 98
, Theorem 0.4(2))
that
for
c
outside a finite set of hyperplanes,
any trace on
\mathcal{O}_{c}
is a restriction of
a trace on
H_{c}
.
We consider only such
c
, which we call
generic
.
For a classification of traces on
\mathcal{O}_{c}
,
it therefore suffices to characterize traces on
H_{c}
.

Letting
C_{n}
denote the cyclic group of
n
elements,
we further assume that
c
is in
\mathbb{C}[C_{n}]
.
Then in the algebra
H_{c}
, the following hold:

•

gx=\varepsilon xg
;

•

gy=\varepsilon^{-1}yg
;

•

hx=yh
;

•

hy=-xh
;

•

xy-yx=c=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}c_{i}g^{i}
,
with
c_{0}\neq 0
;

•

c_{i}=c_{-i}
for all
i
.

Here the equality
c_{i}=c_{-i}
holds because

c
is in
Z(\mathbb{C}[\mathbb{D}_{n}])

and the conjugacy classes of
\mathbb{D}_{n}
are of the form

\{g^{i},g^{-i}\}
,
\{g^{2i}h:i\in\mathbb{Z}\}
,
and
\{g^{2i+1}h:i\in\mathbb{Z}\}
.

We close this section with a definition of positive traces.
For an algebra
B
and an antilinear automorphism

\rho\colon B\to B
such that
\rho^{2}=\id
,
a trace
T
on
B
is said to be

if

T(a\rho(a))>0
for all nonzero
a
in
B
.
The antilinear automorphism we study
is defined on
H_{c}
by

\rho(x)=y
,
\rho(y)=-x
,
\rho(g)=g
,
\rho(h)=h
.
We return to positive traces in
Section

1.5
.

1.2

Algebra Identities and Relations

In this section, we introduce several important algebra elements
and describe relations between them.

Recall that
c=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}c_{i}g^{i}
,
with
g
a generator of
\mathbb{D}_{n}
such that
g^{n}
is the group identity,
and
\varepsilon=\exp(\frac{2\pi i}{n})
.
Let

k=\frac{1}{c_{0}}\left(xy+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n-1}\frac{c_{i}g^{i}}{\varepsilon^{i}-1}\right)-\frac{1}{2}.

The following lemma describes commutativity relations
involving
k

which are important for our results.

Lemma 1.2.1
.

The following equalities hold in
H_{c}
:

•

gk=kg
;

•

hk=-kh
;

•

[k,x]=-x
;

•

kx=x(k-1)
;

•

[k,y]=y
;

•

ky=y(k+1)
.

Proof.

Because
gxy=\varepsilon xgy=xyg
,
it is clear that
g
and
k
commute.
Also, we have

\displaystyle hk

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{c_{0}}\left(hxy+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n-1}\frac{hc_{i}g^{i}}{\varepsilon^{i}-1}\right)-\frac{h}{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{c_{0}}\left(-yxh+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n-1}\frac{c_{i}g^{-i}h}{\varepsilon^{i}-1}\right)-\frac{h}{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{c_{0}}\left(c-xy+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n-1}\frac{c_{i}g^{i}}{\varepsilon^{-i}-1}\right)h-\frac{h}{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{c_{0}}\left(-xy+c_{0}+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n-1}\left(c_{i}g^{i}-\frac{\varepsilon^{i}c_{i}g^{i}}{\varepsilon^{i}-1}\right)\right)h-\frac{h}{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{c_{0}}\left(-xy+c_{0}-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n-1}\frac{c_{i}g^{i}}{\varepsilon^{i}-1}\right)h-\frac{h}{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{c_{0}}\left(xy+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n-1}\frac{c_{i}g^{i}}{\varepsilon^{i}-1}\right)h+\frac{h}{2}

\displaystyle=-kh.

We now prove the commutation relations with
x
and
y
.
We have
xyx=x(xy-c)=x^{2}y-xc
,
so

\displaystyle[k,x]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{c_{0}}\left(xyx-x^{2}y+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n-1}\frac{c_{i}g^{i}x-xc_{i}g^{i}}{\varepsilon^{i}-1}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{c_{0}}\left(-xc+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n-1}\frac{\varepsilon^{i}xc_{i}g^{i}-xc_{i}g^{i}}{\varepsilon^{i}-1}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{c_{0}}\left(-xc+x\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n-1}c_{i}g^{i}\right)

\displaystyle=-x.

It follows immediately that
kx=x(k-1)
,
and the commutation relations with
y

follow by conjugating the commutation relations with
x

by
h
.
∎

Now let

e_{q}=\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n-1}\varepsilon^{iq}g^{i}

for integers
q
.
Note that
e_{q}=e_{q+n}
for all
q
.
The elements
e_{q}
form an important basis for
\mathbb{C}[C_{n}]
,
and it is often convenient to express elements of
H_{c}

using
e_{q}
.
The following lemma describes relations
involving
e_{q}
.

Lemma 1.2.2
.

The following equalities hold in
H_{c}
:

•

e_{q}g^{i}=g^{i}e_{q}=\varepsilon^{-iq}e_{q}
;

•

he_{q}=e_{-q}h
;

•

e_{q}x=xe_{q+1}
;

•

e_{q}y=ye_{q-1}
;

•

e_{q}k=ke_{q}
;

•

e_{p}e_{q}=\delta_{p,q}e_{q}
,
where
\delta
is the Kronecker delta.

Proof.

Clearly
g^{i}
and
e_{q}
commute.
We have

g^{i}e_{q}=\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{n-1}\varepsilon^{jq}g^{i+j}=\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{n-1}\varepsilon^{(j-i)q}g^{j}=\varepsilon^{-iq}e_{q}

and

he_{q}=\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n-1}\varepsilon^{iq}hg^{i}=\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n-1}\varepsilon^{iq}g^{-i}h=\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n-1}\varepsilon^{-iq}g^{i}h=e_{-q}h,

proving the commutation relations with group elements.
Also,

e_{q}x=\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n-1}\varepsilon^{iq}g^{i}x=\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n-1}\varepsilon^{iq+i}xg^{i}=\frac{1}{n}x\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n-1}\varepsilon^{i(q+1)}g^{i}=xe_{q+1},

and conjugating by
h
,
we obtain
e_{q}y=ye_{q-1}
.
It follows from the established commutation relations
that
e_{q}
commutes with
xy
and each
g^{i}
,
so
e_{q}
commutes with
k
.
Finally, we have

e_{p}e_{q}=\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n-1}\varepsilon^{ip}g^{i}e_{q}=\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n-1}\varepsilon^{i(p-q)}e_{q}.

If
p=q
, then all terms in the sum are equal to
e_{q}

th component is equal to

R_{i}(X+\frac{1}{2})-R_{i-1}(X-\tfrac{1}{2})
.
Define the ideal
I
of
V
by

I=\{((X-\alpha_{0})R_{0}(X),\ \dots,\ (X-\alpha_{n-1})R_{n-1}(X)):R_{i}(X)\in\mathbb{C}[X]\}

and the subspace
W
of
V
as the set of elements

(R_{0}(X),\dots,R_{n-1}(X))

for which
R_{i}(X)=-R_{-i}(-X)
.
The space of linear maps satisfying
Conditions
(0)
and
(0)

of
Theorem

1.3.2

is isomorphic to the vector space

(V/\langle\varphi(I),W\rangle)^{*}
,
where
\langle\varphi(I),W)\rangle

denotes the linear span of
\varphi(I)
and
W
.
We therefore wish to show that the dimension of

V/\langle\varphi(I),W\rangle
is
\frac{n}{2}
.

First, note that the map
\varphi
is surjective.
Indeed, to solve the equation

\varphi((R_{0},\dots,R_{n-1}))=(P_{0},\dots,P_{n-1})
,
let
R_{n-1}
be a solution to the equation

R_{n-1}(X+n-\tfrac{1}{2})-R_{n-1}(X-\tfrac{1}{2})=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}P_{i}(X+i).

Then
R_{0}
, …,
R_{n-2}
are uniquely defined
by the first
n-1
components of the equation,
and the resulting solution is valid because
the sum
\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}(R_{i}(X+i+\frac{1}{2})-R_{i-1}(X+i-\frac{1}{2}))
is equal to

R_{n-1}(X+n-\frac{1}{2})-R_{n-1}(X-\frac{1}{2})
,
which equals
\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}P_{i}(X+i)
, as needed.
We also note that the kernel of
\varphi

is
\{(z,\dots,z):z\in\mathbb{C}\}
.

Letting

U=\{(R_{0}(X),\ \dots,\ R_{n-1}(X)):R_{i}(X)=R_{-i-1}(-X)\},

we claim that the preimage of
W
under
\varphi
is
U
.
Indeed,
\varphi(U)\subseteq W

because for any element
(R_{0}(X),\dots,R_{n-1}(X))\in U
,
the
i
th component of
\varphi(R_{0}(X),\dots,R_{n-1}(X))

is

R_{i}(X+\tfrac{1}{2})-R_{i-1}(X-\tfrac{1}{2})=R_{-i-1}(-X-\tfrac{1}{2})-R_{-i}(-X+\tfrac{1}{2}),

which is equal to the negative of the
(n-i)
th component.
Conversely, for any
element
(R_{0}(X),\dots,R_{n-1}(X))

mapped to
W
by
\varphi
,
the element of
V
with
i
th component equal to

\frac{R_{i}(X)+R_{-i-1}(-X)}{2}
belongs to
U

and is also mapped to
W
by
\varphi
.
Therefore,

V/\langle\varphi(I),W\rangle\cong V/\langle I,U\rangle.

Let
\psi:V\to V/I\cong\mathbb{C}^{n}
be the quotient map given by
mapping
(R_{0}(X),\dots,R_{n-1}(X))

to
(R_{0}(\alpha_{0}),\dots,R_{n-1}(\alpha_{n-1}))
.
Because
\alpha_{i}=-\alpha_{-i-1}
,

\psi(U)
is the set
S
of vectors
(v_{0},\dots,v_{n-1})

such that
v_{i}=v_{-i-1}
for all
i
.
Therefore,

V/\langle I,U\rangle\cong\mathbb{C}^{n}/S,

the dimension of which is
\frac{n}{2}
,
as desired.
∎

1.4

Analytic Formula for Traces

We now give an analytic description of traces
using the notion of a good contour from
Kly 23
.
In particular, similar to Definition 3.6 of
Kly 23
,
we say that a non-self-intersecting curve
C

with exactly one point in the set
\mathbb{R}+ri
for all real
r

is a good contour if the following three conditions hold:

1.

There exist real
r>0
and
a
such that,
letting
B_{r}
be the disk of radius
r
centered at the origin,

C\setminus B_{r}
coincides with

(a+i\mathbb{R}_{>0})\cup(-a+i\mathbb{R}_{<0})\setminus B_{r}
.
This property ensures that the notions of
“to the left of
C
” and “to the right of C”
are well-defined.

2.

For all
q
,
the set
\alpha_{q}-\mathbb{Z}_{>0}+\frac{1}{2}
is to the left of
C
,
and the set
\alpha_{q}+\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}+\frac{1}{2}

is to the right of
C
.

3.

The path
C
is symmetric about the origin,
that is,
for every complex number
z
on
C
,

-z
is on
C
.

Because the parameter
c
is assumed to be generic,
no two
\alpha_{q}
have the same imaginary part,
so a curve satisfying Condition
2
exists.
Also, since
\alpha_{q}=-\alpha_{-q-1}
by
Lemma

1.2.3
,
the curve may be taken to also satisfy Condition
3
.
Finally, since there are finitely many
\alpha_{q}
,
the curve may also be taken to satisfy Condition
1
,
so a good contour is guaranteed to exist.

Define the polynomial

\textbf{P}(X)=\prod\limits_{q=0}^{n-1}\left(X-\exp(\tfrac{2\pi i}{n}\left(\alpha_{q}-q-\tfrac{1}{2}\right))\right),

so that
\textbf{P}\left(\exp(\tfrac{2\pi i}{n}z)\right)=0
when

z\equiv\alpha_{q}-q-\frac{1}{2}\pmod{n}
.
It follows from the definition that the only root of

\textbf{P}\left(\exp(\frac{2\pi i}{n}(z-q-\frac{1}{2}))\right)

between
-\frac{1}{2}+C
and
\frac{1}{2}+C

is at
z=\alpha_{q}
.
Moreover, the following lemma about
\textbf{P}(X)
holds.

Lemma 1.4.1
.

The polynomial
\textbf{P}(X)
satisfies
\textbf{P}(X)=X^{n}\textbf{P}(X^{-1})
.

Proof.

For each root
\exp(\frac{2\pi i}{n}(\alpha_{q}-q-\frac{1}{2}))
of
\textbf{P}(X)
,
the complex number

\displaystyle\exp(-\tfrac{2\pi i}{n}\left(\alpha_{q}-q-\tfrac{1}{2}\right))

\displaystyle=\exp(\tfrac{2\pi i}{n}\left(\alpha_{-q-1}+q+\tfrac{1}{2}\right))

\displaystyle=\exp(\tfrac{2\pi i}{n}\left(\alpha_{-q-1}-(-q-1)-\tfrac{1}{2}\right))

is also a root of
\textbf{P}(X)
,
so the reciprocal of each root of
\textbf{P}(X)

is also a root of
\textbf{P}(X)
.
The claim follows.
∎

As the following theorem shows,
traces can be described as an integral over a good contour.

Theorem 1.4.2
.

Define

\displaystyle w_{0}(z)

\displaystyle=\exp(\tfrac{2\pi i}{n}z)\cdot\frac{G(\exp(\frac{2\pi i}{n}z))}{\textbf{P}(\exp(\frac{2\pi i}{n}z))},

\displaystyle w_{q}(z)

\displaystyle=w_{0}(z-q),

where
G
is a polynomial of degree at most
n-2

such that
G(X)=X^{n-2}G(X^{-1})
,
and let
C
be a good contour.
All traces
T\colon H_{c}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
are given by

T(R(k)e_{q})=\int_{C}R(z)w_{q}(z)\mathrm{d}z

on
\mathbb{C}[k]e_{q}
, and extended to
H_{c}

using
Corollary

1.3.3

and the values of
T
on the conjugacy classes

g^{2i}h
and
g^{2i+1}h
.

Proof.

The weight functions
w_{q}
satisfy the following properties:

(0)

w_{q}(z)=w_{q-1}(z-1)
,
and so
w_{q}(z)=w_{q}(z-n)
;

(0)

w_{q}(z)
decays exponentially
when
\Im(z)
approaches
\pm\infty
;

(0)

(z-\alpha_{q})w_{q}(z-\frac{1}{2})
is holomorphic
for
z
between
-\frac{1}{2}+C
and
\frac{1}{2}+C
.

Indeed, Property
(0)
holds
because
\textbf{P}(X)
is not divisible by
X

and
G
has degree
\leq n-2
,
and Property
(0)
holds
because the only root of
\textbf{P}(\exp(\frac{2\pi i}{n}(z-\frac{1}{2}-q)))

between
-\frac{1}{2}+C
and
\frac{1}{2}+C

is at
z=\alpha_{q}
,
and the function

\frac{z-\alpha_{q}}{\exp(\frac{2\pi i}{n}z)-\exp(\frac{2\pi i}{n}\alpha_{q})}

is holomorphic at
z=\alpha_{q}
.

We now check Conditions
(0)
and
(0)

of
Theorem

1.3.2
.
First,

\displaystyle T\left(\left(k+\tfrac{1}{2}-\alpha_{q}\right)R\left(k+\tfrac{1}{2}\right)e_{q}\right)-T\left(\left(k-\tfrac{1}{2}-\alpha_{q}\right)R\left(k-\tfrac{1}{2}\right)e_{q+1}\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{C}\left(z+\tfrac{1}{2}-\alpha_{q}\right)R(z+\tfrac{1}{2})w_{q}(z)\mathrm{d}z-\int_{C}\left(z-\tfrac{1}{2}-\alpha_{q}\right)R\left(z-\tfrac{1}{2}\right)w_{q+1}(z)\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle=\int_{\frac{1}{2}+C}(z-\alpha_{q})R(z)w_{q}\left(z-\tfrac{1}{2}\right)\mathrm{d}z-\int_{-\frac{1}{2}+C}(z-\alpha_{q})R(z)w_{q+1}\left(z+\tfrac{1}{2}\right)\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle=\int_{\frac{1}{2}+C}(z-\alpha_{q})R(z)w_{q}\left(z-\tfrac{1}{2}\right)\mathrm{d}z-\int_{-\frac{1}{2}+C}(z-\alpha_{q})R(z)w_{q}\left(z-\tfrac{1}{2}\right)\mathrm{d}z.

Letting
U
be the region between
-\frac{1}{2}+C
and
\frac{1}{2}+C
,
this is equal to

\int_{\partial U}(z-\alpha_{q})R(z)w_{q}\left(z-\tfrac{1}{2}\right)\mathrm{d}z,

which is equal to
0
because

(z-\alpha_{q})R(z)w_{q}(z-\frac{1}{2})

is holomorphic in
U

by Property
(0)
of
w_{q}

listed at the beginning of the proof.
Thus Condition
(0)
of
Theorem

1.3.2
holds.

To show that Condition
(0)
holds,
we first show that
w_{0}
is even.
Indeed,

\displaystyle w_{0}(z)

\displaystyle=\exp(\tfrac{2\pi i}{n}z)\cdot\frac{G\left(\exp(\frac{2\pi i}{n}z)\right)}{\textbf{P}\left(\exp(\frac{2\pi i}{n}z)\right)}

\displaystyle=\exp(\tfrac{2\pi i}{n}z)\cdot\frac{\exp((n-2)\cdot\frac{2\pi i}{n}z)G\left(\exp(-\frac{2\pi i}{n}z)\right)}{\exp(n\cdot\frac{2\pi i}{n}z)\textbf{P}\left(\exp(-\frac{2\pi i}{n}z)\right)}

\displaystyle=\exp(-\tfrac{2\pi i}{n}z)\cdot\frac{G\left(\exp(-\frac{2\pi i}{n}z)\right)}{\textbf{P}\left(\exp(-\frac{2\pi i}{n}z)\right)}

\displaystyle=w_{0}(-z).

Recalling that
C
is symmetric about the origin, we obtain

\displaystyle T\left(R(k)e_{q}\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{C}R(z)w_{q}(z)\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle=\int_{C}R(z)w_{0}(z-q)\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle=\int_{C}R(-z)w_{0}(-z-q)\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle=\int_{C}R(-z)w_{0}(z+q)\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle=\int_{C}R(-z)w_{-q}(z)\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle=T\left(R(-k)e_{-q}\right),

so Condition
(0)
of
Theorem

1.3.2
holds.

Finally, the dimension of the space of possible polynomials
G

is
\frac{n}{2}
and distinct polynomials yield distinct traces,
so, taking into account the value of the trace
on the conjugacy classes
g^{2i}h
and
g^{2i+1}h
,
the dimension of the space of traces described by the theorem
is
\frac{n}{2}+2
, which is equal to the dimension
of the space of all traces by
1.3.4
.
∎

The following theorem reduces the analytic formula
of
Theorem

1.4.2

to an integral over the complex line.

Theorem 1.4.3
.

Define
w_{q}
as in
Theorem

1.4.2
.
All traces
T\colon H_{c}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
are of the form

T(R(k)e_{q})=\int_{i\mathbb{R}}R(z)w_{q}(z)\mathrm{d}z+\Phi_{q}(R),

where
\Phi_{q}
is a linear functional of the form

\Phi_{q}(R)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j\in\mathbb{Z},\\
i\neq q\end{subarray}}a_{q,i,j}R(\alpha_{i}+q-i+nj-\tfrac{1}{2})

with finitely many
a_{q,i,j}
not equal to zero.
If the linear functional
\Phi_{0}
is identically zero,
then the trace
T
is equivalent to a trace
of the filtered deformation
constructed from the polynomial
P_{\circ}

obtained by removing roots of
P
outside the strip

|\Re(z)|\leq\frac{1}{2}
.

Proof.

In the case when all
\alpha_{q}
satisfy
|\Re(\alpha_{q})|<\frac{1}{2}
,
the good contour
C
may be taken to be
i\mathbb{R}
, proving the theorem.
Otherwise, we have

\displaystyle T(R(k)e_{q})

\displaystyle=\int_{C}R(z)w_{q}(z)\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle=\int_{i\mathbb{R}}R(z)w_{q}(z)\mathrm{d}z+\sum_{z_{0}}\Res_{z_{0}}(Rw_{q}),

where the sum is over all
z_{0}
between
i\mathbb{R}
and
C

where
w_{q}(z)
has a singularity.
In particular, these
z_{0}
are of the form
\alpha_{i}+q-i+nj-\frac{1}{2}
,
with
i\in[0,n-1]
and
j\in\mathbb{Z}
.
The value
\Res_{z_{0}}(Rw_{q})
is proportional to
R(z_{0})
,
proving that the trace
T
has the form claimed.

If
\Phi_{0}
is identically zero, then, for all polynomials
R
,
the sum
\sum_{z_{0}}\Res_{z_{0}}(Rw_{0})

over the poles
z_{0}
of
w_{q}(z)
between
i\mathbb{R}
and
C

is always zero.
Taking
R
to have arbitrary values on the poles
(which is possible because the parameters
\alpha_{q}
are generic,
and hence the values
\alpha_{i}+q-i+nj
are distinct),
we find that all residues of these poles are zero.
But this implies that there are no poles of
w_{0}
between
i\mathbb{R}
and
C
,
which is only possible if, for all
complex numbers
z_{0}
of the form

\alpha_{i}-i+nj-\frac{1}{2}
,
the monomial
(z-\exp(\frac{2\pi i}{n}z_{0}))
divides
the polynomial
G
of
Theorem

1.4.2
.
This monomial cancels out the corresponding divisor
of the denominator
P
of
w_{0}
,
reducing the trace to one over
P_{\circ}
,
as claimed.
∎

Remark 1.4.4
.

In the case
q=0
,
the poles
\alpha_{i}-i+nj-\frac{1}{2}
are symmetric about the origin
because
\alpha_{i}=-\alpha_{-i-1}
by
Lemma

1.2.3
.
As was shown in the proof of
Theorem

1.4.2
,

w_{0}
is even, so

\Res_{\alpha_{i}-i+nj-\frac{1}{2}}(w_{0})=\Res_{\alpha_{-i-1}+i-nj+\frac{1}{2}}(w_{0})
.
It follows that
a_{0,i,j}=a_{0,-i-1,-j}
.

1.5

Positive Traces

In this section, we study positive traces on
\mathcal{O}_{c}

and prove our main result.
Recall that
the antilinear automorphism
\rho\colon H_{c}\rightarrow H_{c}

is defined by

\rho(x)=y
,
\rho(y)=-x
,
\rho(g)=g
,
\rho(h)=h
.
It follows that
\rho(k)=-k
and
\rho(e_{q})=e_{-q}
.

We use the following lemma.

Lemma 1.5.1

(
(
EKRS 21
, Lemma 4.2)
)
.

Let
w(z)
be a measurable nonnegative function on
\mathbb{R}

such that
w(z)<c\exp(-b|z|)

for some positive constants
b,c

and which is positive almost everywhere.

(0)

If
H(z)
is a continuous complex-valued function on
\mathbb{R}

with finitely many zeros
and at most polynomial growth at infinity,
then the set
\{H(z)S(z):S(z)\in\mathbb{C}[z]\}

is dense in the space
L^{p}(\mathbb{R},w)
.

(0)

The closure of the set

\{S(z)\bar{S}(z):S(z)\in\mathbb{C}[z]\}

in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R},w)
is the subset
of almost everywhere nonnegative functions.

We begin by showing that the linear functional
\Phi_{0}

of
Theorem

1.4.3

must be identically zero.

Proposition 1.5.2
.

Any positive trace
T
on
\mathcal{O}_{c}

of the form given by
Theorem

1.4.3

satisfies
\Phi_{0}(R)=0
for all polynomials
R
.

Proof.

Since
T
is a positive trace,
we must have
T(a\rho(a))>0
for all
a
in
\mathcal{O}_{c}
.
In particular, we must have
T(R(k)\bar{R}(k)e)>0

for all even polynomials
R
.
By Condition
(0)
of
Theorem

1.3.2
,
this trace value is equal to

\frac{1}{2}T(R(k)\bar{R}(k)e_{0})+\frac{1}{2}R(0)\bar{R}(0)T(e_{0}h)
.

Suppose, for the sake of contradiction,
that
\Phi_{0}
is not identically zero.
Then there exists a polynomial
S_{0}

such that
S_{0}(0)=0
and
\Phi_{0}(S_{0})
is negative.
By
Remark

1.4.4
, we may take
S_{0}
to be even.
Also, let

H(z)=z\prod_{z_{0}}(z-z_{0}),

where the product is over singularities
z_{0}
of
w_{0}(z)
between
i\mathbb{R}
and the path
C
.
so that
H(0)=0
and
H(\alpha_{i}-i-\frac{1}{2})=0

for all
i
.
Thus for any polynomial
U(z)
, we have

\Phi_{0}((S_{0}-HU)(\bar{S}_{0}-\bar{H}\bar{U}))=\Phi_{0}(S_{0})

and
S_{0}(0)-H(0)U(0)=0
.
Also, by
Remark

1.4.4
, the polynomial
H(z)
is even.

By part (1) of
Lemma

1.5.1
,
there exists a sequence of polynomials
S_{i}

such that
HS_{i}
tends to
S_{0}
in the space
L^{2}(i\mathbb{R},|w_{0}|)
.
Because
w_{0}(z)
is even,
the sequence
H(z)\cdot\frac{S_{i}(z)+S_{i}(-z)}{2}

also tends to
S_{0}
in
L^{2}(i\mathbb{R},|w_{0}|)
.
Thus the integral

\int_{i\mathbb{R}}\left(S_{0}(z)-H(z)\cdot\frac{S_{i}(z)+S_{i}(-z)}{2}\right)\left(\bar{S}_{0}(z)-\bar{H}(z)\cdot\frac{\bar{S}_{i}(z)+\bar{S}_{i}(-z)}{2}\right)w_{0}(z)\mathrm{d}z

tends to
0
.
Letting
R(z)=S_{0}(z)-H(z)\cdot\frac{S_{i}(z)+S_{i}(-z)}{2}

for large enough
i
,
we obtain

\displaystyle T(R(k)\bar{R}(k)e)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}T(R(k)\bar{R}(k)e_{0})+\tfrac{1}{2}R(0)\bar{R}(0)T(e_{0}h)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}T(R(k)\bar{R}(k)e_{0})

\displaystyle=\int_{i\mathbb{R}}R(z)\bar{R}(z)w_{0}(z)\mathrm{d}z+\Phi_{0}(R),

which is negative, contradicting the positivity of the trace.
Thus
\Phi_{0}
must be identically zero, as claimed.
∎

We now prove a few preliminary results
which restrict a positive trace
T

to be nonzero only on the span of terms of the form

R(k)e_{q}
, with
R
a polynomial.

Proposition 1.5.3
.

Any positive trace
T
on
\mathcal{O}_{c}
satisfies
T(e_{0}h)=0
.

Proof.

Let
a
=
R(k)(x^{n}+y^{n})e

for some even polynomial
R
.
The element
a=ea
is in
\mathcal{O}_{c}
, so by hypothesis,
we must have
T(a\rho(a))>0
.
Expanding and using the evenness of
R
gives

\displaystyle T(a\rho(a))

\displaystyle=T\left(R(k)(x^{n}+y^{n})e\bar{R}(k)(x^{n}+y^{n})e\right)

\displaystyle=T\left(R(k)(x^{n}+y^{n})\bar{R}(k)(x^{n}+y^{n})e\right)

\displaystyle=T\left(R(k)(x^{n}+y^{n})\left(x^{n}\bar{R}(k-n)+y^{n}\bar{R}(k+n)\right)e\right),

and distributing and using the commutativity relations
with
k
listed in
Lemma

1.2.1
,
we obtain

\displaystyle T(a\rho(a))

\displaystyle=T\left(R(k)\left(x^{2n}\bar{R}(k-n)+x^{n}y^{n}\bar{R}(k+n)+y^{2n}\bar{R}(k+n)+y^{n}x^{n}\bar{R}(k-n)\right)e\right)

\displaystyle=T\left(x^{2n}R(k-2n)\bar{R}(k-n)e\right)+T\left(x^{n}y^{n}R(k)\bar{R}(k+n)e\right)

\displaystyle\phantom{=\ }+T\left(y^{2n}R(k+2n)\bar{R}(k+n)e\right)+T\left(y^{n}x^{n}R(k)\bar{R}(k-n)e\right).

Because
e_{0}
is the average of the elements in
C_{n}

and
e
is the average of the elements in
\mathbb{D}_{n}
,
we have
e=\frac{e_{0}+e_{0}h}{2}
.
Expanding the expression for
T(a\rho(a))

and removing terms equal to
0
,
we obtain

\displaystyle T\left(a\rho(a)\right)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}T\left(x^{2n}R(k-2n)\bar{R}(k-n)e_{0}h\right)+\tfrac{1}{2}T\left(x^{n}y^{n}R(k)\bar{R}(k+n)e_{0}h\right)

\displaystyle\phantom{=\ }+\tfrac{1}{2}T\left(y^{2n}R(k+2n)\bar{R}(k+n)e_{0}h\right)+\tfrac{1}{2}T\left(y^{n}x^{n}R(k)\bar{R}(k-n)e_{0}h\right)

\displaystyle\phantom{=\ }+\tfrac{1}{2}T\left(x^{n}y^{n}R(k)\bar{R}(k+n)e_{0}\right)+\tfrac{1}{2}T\left(y^{n}x^{n}R(k)\bar{R}(k-n)e_{0}\right).

Using Conditions
(0)
and
(0)

of
Theorem

1.3.2

and rearranging terms, we obtain

\displaystyle T(a\rho(a))

2.2

Twisted traces

For the next proposition, note the following. Let

be an element of
\mathcal{A}
. Using

we get
f_{0}(v)=f_{0}(v^{-1})

from

Proposition 2.2.1
.

Let
g
be an automorphism of
\mathcal{A}
of the form
H_{a}\mapsto H_{a+k}
,

be a

•

For an element

•

Proof.

First we note that
g

W
, which we will also denote by
g

,

We check the trace condition for a symmetric Laurent polynomial
h(v)
, it is preserved by

We have

and symmetric Laurent polynomial

Note that this element has no
w^{0}

It follows from Proposition
2.1.1
that for any rational function

with simple poles at
\pm 1,\ldots,\pm q^{N-1}

such that

belongs to
\mathcal{A}
. Note that

we have

. Hence for even

.

By induction it follows that for an element

Now we use the trace condition for

and

or

. We have

. Hence

\displaystyle ab=

\displaystyle w^{2M+1}-w^{2M-1}+w^{1-2M}-w^{-1-2M},

\displaystyle bg(a)=

\displaystyle q^{-\frac{m}{2}}(w^{2M+1}v^{m}-w^{2M-1}v^{-m}+w^{1-2M}v^{m}-w^{-2M-1}v^{-m}),

and a similar equation for

using

. Hence for an element

is uniquely defined by

and
T(wv-w^{-1}v^{-1})

Let

be even. We have just showed that for any

.

\omega
such that
\omega(z)=\omega(e^{2\pi iz})
. Then
\omega(z)=\vartheta(z-\frac{\tau}{2})\vartheta(z+\frac{\tau}{2})\vartheta(z-\tfrac{1}{2}-\frac{\tau}{2})\vartheta(z+\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\tau}{2})
up to a constant.

Proposition 2.3.2
.

Let
T
be a positive trace,
a=\sum w^{2i+1}f_{2i+1}(v)
. Then
T(a)=0
.

Proof.

Using Proposition
2.2.1
it is enough to prove that
T(a)=0
for elements
a
of the form
a=wF(v)-wF(v^{-1})
. For these elements we have
T(a)=C_{+}F(\sqrt{q})+C_{-}F(-\sqrt{q})
. Assume that
C_{+}
is nonzero.

Let
a=wF(v)-w^{-1}F(v^{-1})
for some Laurent polynomial
F
,
b=a+1
. Then

\rho(b)=q^{-\frac{m}{4}}\big(-wv^{\frac{m}{2}}\overline{F}(v)+w^{-1}v^{-\frac{m}{2}}\overline{F}(v^{-1})\big)+1.

Note that
T(b\rho(b))
depends only on the
w^{0}
and
w^{1}
coefficients of
b\rho(b)
. They can be computed as follows:

\displaystyle[w^{0}]b\rho(b)=

\displaystyle q^{-\frac{m}{4}}\big(F(qv)v^{-\frac{m}{2}}\overline{F}(v^{-1})+F(qv^{-1})v^{\frac{m}{2}}\overline{F}(v)\big)+1,

\displaystyle[w^{1}]b\rho(b)=

\displaystyle F(v)-v^{\frac{m}{2}}\overline{F}(v).

Let
G=F(\sqrt{q}v)
,
I(G)=\int_{S^{1}}\lvert G(z)\rvert^{2}\frac{dz}{z}
. We have
S([w^{0}]b\rho(b))\leq cI(G)
for some
c>0
. On the other hand, trace for the odd powers of
w
is a linear combination of
G(1)
,
G(-1)
,
\overline{G}(1)
and
\overline{G}(-1)
; and
G(1)
has nonzero coefficient. Let
G_{N}(z)=a(\frac{z+1}{2})^{N}
. Then
G_{N}(1)=a
,
G_{N}(-1)=0
. On the other hand,
G_{N}(z)
is a sequence of functions such that
\lvert G_{N}(z)\rvert\leq 1
tends to zero for almost all
z
as
N
tends to infinity. It follows that
I(G_{N}(z))
tends to zero as
N
tends to infinity. We get that
T(b\rho(b))
tends to a linear combination of
a
and
\overline{a}
. We can choose
a
so that this linear combination does not belong to
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
. We get a contradiction with positivity of
T
.
∎

Let
T
be a positive trace. Combining Propositions
2.3.1
and
2.3.2
it follows that for
a=\sum w^{i}f_{i}
we have
T(a)=\int f_{0}(z)\omega(z)\frac{dz}{z}
and
\omega(1)=\omega(-1)=1
.

Proposition 2.3.3
.

Let
T
be a trace on
\mathcal{A}
of the form
T(a)=\int f_{0}(z)\omega(z)\frac{dz}{z}
, where
w
is a holomorphic function on
\mathbb{C}^{*}
such that
\omega(qz)=q^{-\frac{m}{2}}z^{-m}\omega(z)
,
\omega(z)=\omega(z^{-1})
and
\omega(1)=\omega(-1)=0
. Then
T
is positive if and only if
\omega(z)
and
z^{-\frac{m}{2}}\omega(\sqrt{q^{-1}}z)
are nonnegative on
S^{1}
.

Proof.

First we check that any such function
\omega
indeed gives a positive trace.

Let
a=\sum w^{k}f_{k}
, then

\rho(a)=\sum q^{-\frac{k^{2}m}{4}}w^{-k}v^{-\frac{km}{2}}\overline{f_{k}}(v^{-1})

and

[w^{0}]a\rho(a)=\sum q^{-\frac{k^{2}m}{4}}f_{k}(q^{k}v)v^{-\frac{km}{2}}\overline{f_{k}}(v^{-1}).

(2.9)

It is enough to show that

I(f_{k})=\int_{\varepsilon S^{1}}f_{k}(q^{k}z)z^{-\frac{km}{2}}\overline{f_{k}}(z^{-1})\omega(z)\frac{dz}{z}

is positive for any
k
such that
f_{k}
is nonzero. Let
f=f_{k}
, this is a function with the poles of order at most one at
\pm q^{k},k\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Then the only possible poles of
f(q^{k}z)\overline{f}(z^{-1})
are poles of order at most two at
\pm q^{k},k\in\mathbb{Z}
. Since
\omega(1)=\omega(-1)=0
and
\omega(z)=\omega(z^{-1})
, the function
\omega
has a double root at
1
and
-1
. Using the quasi-periodicity condition we get that
w
has a double root at
\pm q^{k},k\in\mathbb{Z}
. It follows that
f(q^{k}z)\overline{f}(z^{-1})\omega(z)
is a holomorphic function on
\mathbb{C}^{*}
. Hence we can write

\displaystyle I(f)=\int_{\varepsilon S^{1}}f(q^{k}z)z^{-\frac{km}{2}}\overline{f}(z^{-1})\omega(z)\frac{dz}{z}=

\displaystyle\int_{q^{-\frac{k}{2}}S^{1}}f(q^{k}z)z^{-\frac{km}{2}}\overline{f}(z^{-1})\omega(z)\frac{dz}{z}=

\displaystyle q^{\frac{k^{2}m}{4}}

\displaystyle\int_{S^{1}}f(q^{\frac{k}{2}}t)\overline{f}(q^{\frac{k}{2}}t^{-1})t^{-\frac{km}{2}}\omega(q^{-\frac{k}{2}}t)\frac{dt}{t}.

For
z\in S^{1}
we have
f(q^{\frac{k}{2}}t)\overline{f}(q^{\frac{k}{2}}t^{-1})=\lvert f(q^{\frac{k}{2}}t)\rvert^{2}.
It remains to show that
z^{-\frac{km}{2}}\omega(q^{-\frac{k}{2}}z)
is nonnegative on
S^{1}
. We have

\omega(q^{1-\frac{k}{2}}z)=q^{-\frac{m}{2}}(q^{-\frac{k}{2}}z)^{-m}\omega(q^{-\frac{k}{2}}z),

so that

z^{\frac{(2-k)m}{2}}\omega(q^{\frac{2-k}{2}}z)=q^{\frac{m(k-1)}{2}}z^{-\frac{km}{2}}\omega(q^{-\frac{k}{2}}z),

so that the sign of
z^{-\frac{km}{2}}\omega(q^{-\frac{k}{2}}z)
depends only on the parity of
k
. For
k=0,1
we get exactly the condition in the proposition statement.

Now we prove the other direction of the proposition statement. Let
T
be a positive trace. Take
a=f(v)
. Then

T(a\rho(a))=\int_{S^{1}}f(z)\overline{f}(z^{-1})\omega(z)\frac{dz}{z}

should be greater than zero for all nonzero Laurent polynomials
f
such that
f(z)=f(z^{-1})
. Using the density of trigonometric polynomials in
L^{2}(S^{1})
and the condition
\omega(z)=\omega(z^{-1})
we get that
\omega(z)
should be nonnegative on
S^{1}
.

Take
a=wF(v)-w^{-1}F(v^{-1})\in\mathcal{A}
, where
F
is a Laurent polynomial. Using (
2.9
) we get

[w^{0}]a\rho(a)=q^{-\frac{m}{4}}\big(F(qv)v^{-\frac{m}{2}}\overline{F}(v^{-1})+F(qv^{-1})v^{\frac{m}{2}}\overline{F}(v)\big),

so that

T(a\rho(a))=q^{-\frac{m}{4}}\int_{\varepsilon S^{1}}\big(F(qz)z^{-\frac{m}{2}}\overline{F}(z^{-1})+F(qz^{-1})z^{\frac{m}{2}}\overline{F}(z)\big)\omega(z)\frac{dz}{z}.

Using
\omega(z)=\omega(z^{-1})
we get

\int_{\varepsilon S^{1}}F(qz^{-1}z^{\frac{m}{2}}\overline{F}(z)\omega(z)\frac{dz}{z}=\int_{\varepsilon^{-1}S^{1}}F(qz)z^{-\frac{m}{2}}\overline{F}(z^{-1})\omega(z)\frac{dz}{z}.

Shifting contours we get

T(a\rho(a))=2q^{-\frac{m}{4}}\int_{\sqrt{q^{-1}}S^{1}}F(qz)\overline{F}(z^{-1})z^{-\frac{m}{2}}\omega(z)\frac{dz}{z}=2\int_{S^{1}}F(\sqrt{q}t)\overline{F}(\sqrt{q}t^{-1})t^{-\frac{m}{2}}\omega(\sqrt{q^{-1}}t)\frac{dt}{t}.

Using the fact that trigonometric polynomials are dense in
L^{2}(S^{1})
we get that
t^{-\frac{m}{2}}\omega(\sqrt{q^{-1}}t)
is nonnegative on
S^{1}
. The proposition follows.
∎

Theorem 2.3.4
.

Positive traces on
\mathcal{A}
are in one-to-one correspondence with function holomorphic functions
w
on
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
such that

1.

\omega(qz)=q^{-\frac{m}{2}}z^{-m}\omega(z)
;

2.

\omega(z)=\omega(z^{-1})
;

3.

\omega(1)=\omega(-1)=0
;

4.

\omega(z)
and
z^{-\frac{m}{2}}\omega(\sqrt{q^{-1}}z)
are nonnegative on
S^{1}
.

The convex cone of such functions
w
has real dimension
\frac{m}{2}-1
. In particular, for
m=4
there is a unique positive trace up to a constant.

Proof.

The first statement follows from Propositions
2.2.1
,
2.2.2
,
2.3.1
,
2.3.2
,
2.3.3
.

We turn to the second statement. Let
w
be a functions satisfying conditions 1-4. Consider
\omega(z)
such that
\omega(z)=\omega(e^{2\pi iz})
. Let
q=e^{2\pi i\tau}
. Conditions 1 and 4 mean that
w
gives a positive trace on
q
-Weyl algebra. The set of the corresponding functions
\omega
is described in
Kly 22
, Theorem 3.7: function
\omega
satisfies conditions 1 and 4 if and only if there exist
\lambda>0
and
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m}
divided into pairs
\alpha_{i}=\overline{\alpha_{j}}
such that
\omega(z)=\lambda\prod_{i=1}^{m}\vartheta(z-\alpha_{i})
. Here
\vartheta
is the Jacobi theta function. It follows from Lemma 3.6 of
Kly 22
that we can assume
\lvert\Im\alpha_{i}\rvert<\Im\tau
.

Condition 2 is equivalent to
\omega(z)=\omega(-z)
. For
z=\tfrac{1}{2}+\tfrac{\tau}{2}-\alpha_{k}
we get
\prod_{j=1}^{m}\vartheta(\tfrac{1}{2}+\tfrac{\tau}{2}-\alpha_{k}-\alpha_{j})=0
. So there exists
j
such that
\alpha_{j}+\alpha_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}+\mathbb{Z}\tau
.

Assume that
\alpha_{j}\neq\alpha_{k}
or
\overline{\alpha_{k}}
. Shifting both
\alpha_{j}
and
\overline{\alpha_{j}}
by an integer we get
\alpha_{j}+\alpha_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}\tau
. Since
\lvert\Im\alpha_{j,k}\rvert<\Im\tau
we have
\alpha_{j}+\alpha_{k}\in\{-\tau,0,\tau\}
.

Assume that
\alpha_{j}+\alpha_{k}=-\tau
. Note that
\vartheta(z-\alpha_{j}-\tau)=e^{\pi i(-\tau+2z-2\alpha_{j})}\vartheta(z-\alpha_{j})
and
\vartheta(z-\overline{\alpha_{j}}+\tau)=e^{-\pi i(\tau+2z-2\overline{\alpha_{j}})}\vartheta(z-\overline{\alpha_{j}})
, so that

\vartheta(z-\alpha_{j}-\tau)\vartheta(z-\overline{\alpha_{j}}+\tau)=e^{-2\pi i(\tau+\alpha_{j}-\overline{\alpha_{j}})}\vartheta(z-\alpha_{j})\vartheta(z-\overline{\alpha_{j}}).

Hence we can change
\alpha_{j}
and
\overline{\alpha_{j}}
to
\alpha_{j}+\tau
and
\overline{\alpha_{j}+\tau}=\overline{\alpha_{j}}-\tau
. This will multiply
\lambda
by a positive number.

After this change we have
\alpha_{j}+\alpha_{k}=0
. Similarly, if
\alpha_{j}+\alpha_{j}=\tau
we can shift
\alpha_{j}
to get
\alpha_{j}+\alpha_{k}=0
.

Hence in the case when
\alpha_{j}+\alpha_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}+\mathbb{Z}\tau
and
\alpha_{j}\neq\alpha_{k},\overline{\alpha_{k}}
we get a quadruple
\alpha_{k},\overline{\alpha_{k}},-\alpha_{k},-\overline{\alpha_{k}}
.

If
\alpha_{j}=\alpha_{k}
we get
\alpha_{k}\in\tfrac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}+\frac{\tau}{2}\mathbb{Z}
. Hence
\alpha_{k}+\overline{\alpha_{k}}\in\mathbb{Z}
.

If
\alpha_{j}=\overline{\alpha_{k}}
we get
\alpha_{k}+\overline{\alpha_{k}}\in\mathbb{Z}+\mathbb{Z}\tau
. Since
\alpha_{k}+\overline{\alpha_{k}}
is a real number this means
\alpha_{k}+\overline{\alpha_{k}}\in\mathbb{Z}
.

We get that
\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{m}
are divided in quadruples
\alpha_{j},-\alpha_{j},\overline{\alpha_{j}},-\overline{\alpha_{j}}
and pairs
\alpha_{k},\overline{\alpha_{k}}
with
\Re\alpha_{k}\in\tfrac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}
. Conversely, for such sequence
\alpha_{j}
the function
\omega(z)=\lambda\prod_{i=1}^{m}\vartheta(z-\alpha_{i})
satisfies
\omega(z)=\omega(-z)
.

The condition 3 just means that two of the pairs are
\pm\frac{\tau}{2}
and
\frac{1}{2}\pm\frac{\tau}{2}
.

After that, each quadruple has one complex degree of freedom and each tuple has one real degree of freedom, giving real dimension
\frac{m-4}{2}=\frac{m}{2}-2
. A choice of
\lambda
is an additional degree of freedom. So we get a cone of real dimension
\frac{m}{2}-1
, as claimed.

∎

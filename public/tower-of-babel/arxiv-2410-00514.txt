Stable/unstable continua of cw-expansive flows Thanks: 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification : Primary 37B05; Secondary 37B45, 37C10.
A. Artigue, B. Carvalho, and M. Tacuri

Abstract
. We introduce distinct definitions of local stable/unstable sets for flows without fixed points, namely, kinematic, geometric, and sectionally geometric, and discuss relations between them. We prove the existence of continua with a uniform diameter within each sectionally geometric local stable/unstable set for cw-expansive flows defined on Peano continua.



1.
Introduction

In chaotic systems, the study of distinct initial conditions with orbits that separate is essential to understanding their dynamics. Indeed, they appear in the study of several dynamical properties and invariants of such systems, such as the topological entropy, sensitivity to initial conditions, and expansiveness, among others. The study of points with orbits that, instead, do not separate in the future/past also helps in this endeavor, and the understanding of the structure of local stable/unstable sets is central in the field of dynamical systems. In hyperbolic systems, the Stable Manifold Theorem ensures that local stable/unstable sets are manifolds tangent to the stable/unstable spaces, and a big part of the hyperbolic dynamics can be obtained from this structure (see
[
23
]
for example). In the classification of expansive surface homeomorphisms of Hiraide/Lewowicz, local stable/unstable sets are part of a pair of transversal singular foliations with a finite number of singularities and they conclude that the homeomorphism is pseudo-Anosov (see
[
15
]
and
[
19
]
).

In generalizations of hyperbolicity and expansiveness, the structure of local stable/unstable sets can be much more complicated. This can be seen for example in Walter’s pseudo-Anosov diffeomorphism of
\mathbb{S}^{2}
(see
[
25
]
), which is an example of a continuum-wise expansive homeomorphism, where local stable/unstable sets can contain a cantor set of disjoint arcs (see
[
5
]
). Cw-expansiveness was introduced by H. Kato in
[
16
,
17
]
in the context of homeomorphisms and further explored in
[
1
,
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
10
,
11
,
12
]
, among others. An important result in this matter is the existence of a continuum (compact and connected) with uniform diameter inside each local stable/unstable set. In the case of continuous flows, M. Paternain proved in
[
22
]
*Lemma 4 the non-existence of stable points for expansive flows, and as a consequence, obtained in
[
22
]
*Lemma 5 the existence of a continuum inside each local stable/unstable set. The study of cw-expansive flows started in
[
2
]
where the existence of one non-trivial local unstable continuum was obtained (in Lemma 6.4) to prove that the topological entropy of cw-expansive flows in spaces of topological dimension greater than one is positive. In this article, we generalize all these results proving that cw-expansive flows defined on a Peano continuum have a continuum within each local stable and local unstable set.

2.
Basic Properties of Local Stable and Local Unstable Sets

In this section, we discuss distinct definitions of local stable/unstable sets for flows, namely, kinematic, geometric, and sectionally geometric. The definitions involve reparametrizations of orbits and transversal sections, so in this section, we will state some basic definitions and results involving these notions. Let
(X,\phi)
be a flow defined in a metric space
(X,d)
, that is a continuous map
\phi:\mathbb{R}\times X\rightarrow X
, denoted as
\phi(t,x)=\phi_{t}(x)
, satisfying

(1)

\phi_{0}(x)=x
for every
x\in X
and

(2)

\phi_{s+t}(x)=\phi_{s}\left(\phi_{t}(x)\right)
for all
s,t\in\mathbb{R}
and every
x\in X
.

Definition 2.1

(Kinematic stable/unstable sets)
.

For each
x\in X
and
\varepsilon>0
, we define the
kinematic
\varepsilon
-stable
and
kinematic
\varepsilon
-unstable
sets of

as follows:

W^{s}_{\varepsilon}(x)=\{y\in X:d(\phi_{t}(x),\phi_{t}(y))\leq\varepsilon\,\,\,\text{for all}\,\,t\geq 0\},

W^{u}_{\varepsilon}(x)=\{y\in X:d(\phi_{t}(x),\phi_{t}(y))\leq\varepsilon\,\,\text{for all}\,\,t\leq 0\}.

The words ‘kinematic’ and ‘geometric’ first appeared in various forms of defining expansive systems. In fact, we can see that they were first described in
[
13
]
and later in
[
4
]

The kinematic local stable/unstable sets are the classical notions of local stable/unstable sets of the hyperbolic dynamics, but since we are working with non-hyperbolic flows we call them kinematic to separate it from the other definitions.

We denote by
Rep(\mathbb{R})
the set of all increasing homeomorphisms
h:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that
h(0)=0
. We also denote by
Rep(\mathbb{R})^{+}\left(Rep(\mathbb{R})^{-}\right)

Rep(\mathbb{R})

(resp.
(-\infty,0]
). In the definition of local stable/unstable geometric sets that we will define next, we allow one of the orbits to undergo changes over time, that is, one of the orbits to be reparameterized.

Definition 2.2

(Geometric stable/unstable sets)
.

For each

\varepsilon>0

\varepsilon

\varepsilon
-unstable

W^{ss}_{\varepsilon}(x)=\{y\in X:\exists\ \alpha\in Rep(\mathbb{R})^{+}\hskip 5.69046pt\text{such that}\hskip 2.84544ptd(\phi_{t}(x),\phi_{\alpha(t)}(y))\leq\varepsilon\,\,\text{for all}\,\,t\geq 0\},

W^{uu}_{\varepsilon}(x)=\{y\in X:\exists\ \alpha\in Rep(\mathbb{R})^{-}\hskip 5.69046pt\text{such that }\hskip 2.84544ptd(\phi_{t}(x),\phi_{\alpha(t)}(y))\leq\varepsilon\,\,\text{for all}\,\,t\leq 0\}.

Clearly,
W^{s}_{\varepsilon}(x)\subset W^{ss}_{\varepsilon}(x)
and
W^{u}_{\varepsilon}(x)\subset W^{uu}_{\varepsilon}(x)
for every
x\in X
.

Proposition 2.9

Fixed at point

3.
Uniform expansiveness and uniform cw-expansiveness

In this section, we will show that expansiveness and cw-expansiveness imply uniform expansiveness and uniform cw-expansiveness in the transversal sections, respectively. Expansiveness is a crucial property in various areas, including chaos theory, since expansive systems exhibit chaotic behavior. In
[
9
]
, Bowen and Walters introduced a definition of expansive flow. This definition requires that distinct trajectories are separated, even if one of the orbits is reparametrized. A natural idea that appears is to consider possible extensions of this concept. In this direction, cw-expansiveness represents a generalization of expansiveness introduced by Kato
[
16
]
in the case of homeomorphisms. However, adapting cw-expansiveness to the context of flows is a subtle task, especially due to the flow direction and the possibility of a range of reparametrizations. This was done in W. Cordeiro’s doctoral thesis and is exposed in
[
2
]
.
The following is the definition of expansive flow according to Bowen and Walters.

Definition 3.1

(Expansiveness)
.

A flow
(X,\phi)
is
expansive
if for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists
\delta>0
, called an expansiveness constant of
\phi
, such that if

d(\phi_{\alpha(t)}(y),\phi_{t}(x))\leq\delta\,\,\,\,\,\,\text{for every}\,\,\,\,\,\,t\in\mathbb{R}

and some
\alpha\in Rep(\mathbb{R})
, then
y\in\phi_{(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)}(x)
.

According to Bowen and Walters (see
[
9
]
*Lemma 1), for expansive flows, there exists only a finite number of fixed points, and each one is an isolated point of X. This reduces our study of expansive flows to those without fixed points. To define cw-expansiveness according to W. Cordeiro we need to state first the following definitions and notations. For any subset
A
of a metric space, let

C^{0}(A,\mathbb{R})=\{h:A\rightarrow\mathbb{R}:h\,\,\text{is continuous}\}.

In particular, we let
C(\mathbb{R})=C^{0}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})
. For
A\subset X
, let

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}(A)=\{\alpha:A\rightarrow C(\mathbb{R}):\ \exists x_{\alpha}\in A\ \mbox{with}\ \alpha(x_{\alpha})=id_{\mathbb{R}}\ \mbox{and}

\displaystyle\alpha(.)(t)\in C^{0}(A,\mathbb{R})\,\,\,\mbox{for every}\ t\in\mathbb{R}\}

A map
\alpha\in\mathcal{H}(A)
associate to each
x\in A
a map
\alpha(x)\in C(\mathbb{R})
where one of the associated maps is the identity and this association varies continuously with the point of
A
. The idea is that each point
x\in A
will be reparametrized by a distinct reparametrization
\alpha(x)
. Precisely, for each
\alpha\in\mathcal{H}(A)
and

, we define

\mathcal{X}^{t}_{\alpha}(A)=\{\phi_{\alpha(x)(t)}(x):x\in A\}.

Let
\mathcal{K}(X)
be the set of all compact subsets of
X
equipped with the Hausdorff metric and
\mathcal{C}(X)=\{A\in\mathcal{K}(X);A\,\,\mbox{is}\,\,\text{connected}\}
. In the case
A\in\mathcal{C}(X)
, a map in
\mathcal{H}(A)
represents a continuum of reparametrizations.

Definition 3.2

(Continuum-wise expansiveness)
.

A flow
(X,\phi)
is

, or simply cw-expansive, if for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\delta>0
, called a cw-expansiveness constant of

,
\alpha\in\mathcal{H}(A)
, and

\mbox{diam}(\mathcal{X}^{t}_{\alpha}(A))<\delta\quad\text{for every}\quad t\in\mathbb{R},

then
A\subset\phi_{(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)}(x_{\alpha})
.

According to
[

]

Definition 3.3

(Sectional Flow)
.

Let

be an interval. If there exists an

0

18

Working with a finite number of transversal sections makes the understanding of various concepts more difficult and the proofs of results more technical and complicate, especially if we aim to extend these properties to the cw-expansive case. This motivated us to redefine several concepts using a field of transversal sections. In the previous section, we have done this to the local stable/unstable sets and now we define transversal uniform expansiveness, which is equivalent to the uniform expansiveness defined in

Definition 3.5

(Uniform expansiveness on transversal sections)
.

Let
(X,\phi)
be an expansive flow and
\varepsilon_{0}>0
be an expansiveness constant associated to
\tau

(X,\phi)
is
uniformly transversely expansive
if for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
t_{\varepsilon}>0
such that for every

there exists
\alpha\in Rep(\mathbb{R})
such that

d(x,y)\geq\varepsilon\ \ \Rightarrow\sup_{|t|\leq t_{\varepsilon}}d\left(\Phi_{\alpha}^{t}(x,y)\right)>\varepsilon_{0}.

Proposition 3.6
.

Every expansive flow is uniformly transversely expansive.

Proof.

is a stable point, then there exists
\delta>0
such that any
y\in H_{\delta}(x)
is also a stable point.

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon\in(0,\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{2})
, where
\varepsilon_{0}
is a cw-expansive constant associated to
\tau
. Theorem
5.1
ensures the existence of
\delta\in(0,r_{0})
such that
H_{\delta}(y)
is a continuum for every
y\in X
. This and the stability of
x
ensure that
H_{\delta}(x)\subset CT_{\varepsilon}^{s}(x)
(reducing
\delta
if necessary) and since
CT_{\varepsilon}^{s}(x)\in\mathcal{T}^{s}_{\varepsilon}
, Proposition
4.9
ensures the existence of
\alpha\in\mathcal{H}_{\varepsilon}(H_{\delta}(x))
such that

(3)

\lim_{s\rightarrow\infty}\operatorname{diam}(\Phi_{\alpha}^{s}(H_{\delta}(x)))=0.

Let
y\in H_{\delta}(x)
,
\varepsilon>0
, and
\varepsilon_{1}>0
obtained from Proposition
2.9
. According to
(\ref{eq5a.1})
, there exists a sufficiently small
\delta_{1}
such that

\mbox{diam}(\Phi_{\alpha}^{t}(H_{\delta_{1}}(x)))\leq\frac{\varepsilon_{1}}{3}\,\,\,\,\,\,\text{for every}\,\,\,\,\,\,t\geq 0.

Choose
\lambda>0
, given by uniform continuity of
\phi
, such that

d(z,\phi_{\mu}(z))\leq\frac{\varepsilon_{1}}{3}\,\,\,\,\,\,\text{whenever}\,\,\,\,\,\,|\mu|\leq\lambda\,\,\,\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,\,\,\,z\in X,

r_{1}\in(0,\delta_{1})
satisfying
B(x,r_{1})\subset\phi_{[-\lambda,\lambda]}(H_{\delta}(x))
for all
x\in X
, and
r^{\prime}\in(0,r_{1})

such that
H_{r^{\prime}}(y)\subset B(x,r_{1})
. Thus, we can choose a map
\mu\colon H_{r^{\prime}}(y)\to[-\lambda,\lambda]
such that

\phi_{\mu(z)}(z)\in H_{\delta}(x)\,\,\,\,\,\,\text{for every}\,\,\,\,\,\,z\in H_{r^{\prime}}(y).

Thus, defining
\beta\in\mathcal{H}_{\varepsilon}(H_{r^{\prime}}(y))
as
\beta(a)(t)=\alpha(\phi_{\mu(a)}(a))(t)
for every
a\in H_{r^{\prime}}(y)
, it follows that

\displaystyle\mbox{diam}(\mathcal{X}^{t}_{\beta}(H_{r^{\prime}}(y)))

\displaystyle=\sup_{a,b\in H_{r^{\prime}}(y)}d(\phi_{\beta(a)(t)}(a),\phi_{\beta(b)(t)}(b))

\displaystyle=\sup_{a,b\in H_{r^{\prime}}(y)}d(\phi_{\alpha(\phi_{\mu(a)}(a))(t)}(a),\phi_{\alpha(\phi_{\mu(b)}(b))(t)}(b))

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{a\in H_{r^{\prime}}(y)}d(\phi_{\alpha(\phi_{\mu(a)}(a))(t)}(a),\phi_{\alpha(\phi_{\mu(a)}(a))(t)+\mu(a)}(a))

\displaystyle+\sup_{a,b\in H_{r^{\prime}}(y)}d(\phi_{\alpha(\phi_{\mu(a)}(a))(t)+\mu(a)}(a),\phi_{\alpha(\phi_{\mu(b)}(b))(t)+\mu(b)}(b))

\displaystyle+\sup_{b\in H_{r^{\prime}}(y)}d(\phi_{\alpha(\phi_{\mu(b)}(b))(t)+\mu(b)}(b),\phi_{\alpha(\phi_{\mu(b)}(b))(t)}(b)))

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\varepsilon_{1}}{3}+\frac{\varepsilon_{1}}{3}+\frac{\varepsilon_{1}}{3}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon_{1},

for every
t\geq 0
. This does not necessarily prove that
H_{r^{\prime}}(y)\subset W^{ss}_{\varepsilon_{1}}(y)
. Indeed, each
a\in H_{r^{\prime}}(y)
has
\beta(a)
as its respective reparametrization, but we have to ensure that
y
is associated to the identity, so we define

\beta_{1}(.)=\beta(.)\circ\beta^{-1}(y)\in\mathcal{H}_{\varepsilon}(H_{r^{\prime}}(y))
, which ensures that
\beta_{1}(y)=Id
and also maintain the inequality

\mbox{diam}(\mathcal{X}_{\beta_{1}}^{t}(H_{r^{\prime}}(y)))\leq\varepsilon_{1}\,\,\,\,\,\,\text{for every}\,\,\,\,\,\,t\geq 0.

Indeed, note that for each
t\geq 0
there exists
s\geq 0
such that
t=\beta(y)(s)
, so

\displaystyle\mbox{diam}(\mathcal{X}_{\beta_{1}}^{t}(H_{r^{\prime}}(y)))

\displaystyle=\sup_{a,b\in H_{r^{\prime}}(y)}d(\phi_{\beta_{1}(a)(t)}(a),\phi_{\beta_{1}(b)(t)}(b))

\displaystyle=\sup_{a,b\in H_{r^{\prime}}(y)}d(\phi_{\beta(a)\circ\beta^{-1}(y)(t)}(a),\phi_{\beta(b)\circ\beta^{-1}(y)(t)}(b))

\displaystyle=\sup_{a,b\in H_{r^{\prime}}(y)}d(\phi_{\beta(a)\circ\beta^{-1}(y)(\beta(y)(s))}(a),\phi_{\beta(b)\circ\beta^{-1}(y)(\beta(y)(s))}(b))

\displaystyle=\sup_{a,b\in H_{r^{\prime}}(y)}d(\phi_{\beta(a)(s)}(a),\phi_{\beta(b)(s)}(b))

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon_{1}.

Finally, according to Proposition

2.9
, there exists
\beta_{2}\in\mathcal{H}(H_{r^{\prime}}(y))
such that

\mbox{diam}(\Phi_{\beta_{2}}^{t}(H_{r^{\prime}}(y))\leq\varepsilon\quad\text{for all}\quad t\geq 0.

This ensures that
H_{r^{\prime}}(y)\subset T^{s}_{\varepsilon}(y)
and that
y
is stable.
∎

Definition 5.4

(Recurrent point)
.

A point
x\in X
is called recurrent if it belongs to its
\omega
-limit set
\omega(x)
, that is, there exists an increasing sequence
(t_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of positive numbers such that
t_{i}\to\infty
and
\phi_{t_{i}}(x)\to x
when
i\to+\infty
. In particular, for each
\delta>0
and
t_{0}>0
, there exists
t>t_{0}
such that
\phi_{t}(x)\in H_{\delta}(x)
.

Recall that
\tau>0
and
r>0
are such that
\phi:[-\tau,\tau]\times H(x)\rightarrow X
is injective and
B(x,r)\subset\phi_{[-\tau,\tau]}H(x)
for each
x\in X
.

Proposition 5.5
.

If
(X,\phi)
is a cw-expansive flow defined on a Peano continuum, then every recurrent and stable point is periodic.

Proof.

Let
x\in X
be a recurrent and stable point and
\varepsilon>0
be a cw-expansiveness constant of
\phi
associated to
\tau
. Theorem
5.1
and Proposition
5.3
ensure the existence of
\delta\in(0,r_{0})
such that
H_{\delta}(y)
is a continuum for every
y\in X
and that any
y\in H_{\delta}(x)
is also a stable point. This and the stability of
x
ensure that
H_{\delta}(x)\subset CT_{\varepsilon}^{s}(x)
(reducing
\delta
if necessary) and since
CT_{\varepsilon}^{s}(x)\in\mathcal{T}^{s}_{\varepsilon}
, Proposition
4.9
ensures the existence of
\alpha\in\mathcal{H}_{\varepsilon}(H_{\delta}(x))
such that

(4)

\lim_{s\rightarrow\infty}\operatorname{diam}(\Phi_{\alpha}^{s}(H_{\delta}(x)))=0.

Choose
r^{\prime}\in(0,\delta)
such that
B(x,r^{\prime})\subset\phi_{[-\tau,\tau]}H_{\delta}(x)
for each
x\in X
. Let
t_{0}>0
be such that

\operatorname{diam}(\Phi_{\alpha}^{t}(H_{\delta}(x)))<\frac{r^{\prime}}{2}\,\,\,\,\,\,\text{for every}\,\,\,\,\,\,t\geq t_{0}

and use the recurrence of
x
to ensure the existence of
t_{1}>t_{0}
such that

\phi_{t_{1}}(x)\in H_{\frac{r^{\prime}}{2}}(x)\,\,\,\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,\,\,\,\Phi^{t_{1}}_{\alpha}(H_{\delta}(x))\subset H_{\frac{r^{\prime}}{2}}(\phi_{t_{1}}(x)).

Thus,
\Phi^{t_{1}}_{\alpha}(H_{\delta}(x))\subset B(x,r^{\prime})
and we can project it into
H_{\delta}(x)
. Consider the map
\mu\colon H_{\delta}(x)\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
such that

\Phi^{\mu(y)}_{\alpha}(y)\in H_{\delta}(x)\,\,\,\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,\,\,\,\alpha(y)(\mu(y))\in[\alpha(y)(t_{1})-\tau,\alpha(y)(t_{1})+\tau]

for every
y\in H_{\delta}(x)
.
In what follows,
\Phi^{\mu}_{\alpha}(H_{\delta}(x))
denotes the set

\{\Phi^{\mu(y)}_{\alpha}(y);\,\,y\in H_{\delta}(x)\}.

Thus, we have

\Phi^{\mu}_{\alpha}(H_{\delta}(x))\subset H_{\delta}(x)

and
\Phi^{\mu}_{\alpha}
is a map from
H_{\delta}(x)
to itself, so we can consider its iterates
(\Phi^{\mu}_{\alpha})^{n}
with
n\in\mathbb{N}
and obtain

\bigcap_{n\geq 0}(\Phi^{\mu}_{\alpha})^{n}(H_{\delta}(x))=\{y\}\in H_{\delta}(x).

(see Figure

\Phi^{\mu}_{\alpha}(y)=y

y

. Moreover, since

, it follows that

x

r_{1}\in(0,r)

\phi_{t_{2}}(x)\in H_{r_{1}}(y)\subset T_{\varepsilon}^{s}(y)

and Proposition

ensures the existence of
\beta\in\mathcal{H}_{\varepsilon}(H_{r_{1}}(y))

In Proposition
5.3
, we proved that if
x
is stable and
y\in H_{\delta}(x)
, then
y
is stable. In the following proposition, we prove that this can be generalized for any point
y
sufficiently close to some iterate of
x
.

Proposition 5.6
.

If
(X,f)
is a cw-expansive flow on a Peano continuum,
x\in X
is stable, and
y\in\alpha(x)
, then
y
is stable.

Proof.

We assume that
x\in X
is a stable point and prove that
\phi_{-t}(x)
is also a stable point for every
t\geq 0
. Indeed, for
\varepsilon>0
, choose
\delta>0
such that
H_{\delta}(x)\subseteq CT^{s}_{\varepsilon}(x)
and consider
\delta_{1}\in(0,\delta)
given by Proposition
4.6
for
\delta
. Since
\operatorname{diam}(\Phi^{s}_{\alpha}(H_{\delta}(x)))\to 0
when
s\to+\infty
(see Proposition
4.9
) it follows from the choice of
\delta_{1}
that

\Phi_{\beta}^{t}(H_{\delta_{1}}(\phi_{-t}(x)))\subset H_{\delta}(x)

for some
\beta\in\mathcal{H}(H_{\delta_{1}}(\phi_{-t}(x)))
. This ensures that

H_{\delta_{1}}(\phi_{-t}(x))\subset T^{s}_{\varepsilon}(\phi_{-t}(x))

and consequently
\phi_{-t}(x)
is stable. Note that
\delta_{1}
does not depend on
t
. To conclude that
y
is stable it enough to consider
t>0
such that
y\in H_{\delta_{1}}(\phi_{-t}(x))
and use Proposition
5.3
.
∎

Proposition 5.7
.

If
(X,\phi)
is a cw-expansive flow on a Peano continuum and
x\in X
is a stable point, then
x
is recurrent, therefore periodic.

Proof.

We first prove that if
x
is stable and
z\in\alpha(x)
, then
x\in\omega(z)
.
Proposition
5.6
ensures that
z
is stable. Thus, for each
\eta>0
there exists
\gamma>0
and
t>0
such that

\phi_{-t}(x)\in H_{\gamma}(z)\subset T^{s}_{\eta}(z).

Then there exists
\alpha\in Rep(\mathbb{R}^{+})
such that

d(\phi_{\alpha(s)}(\phi_{-t}(x)),\phi_{s}(z))\leq\varepsilon\,\,\,\,\,\,\text{for every}\,\,\,\,\,\,s\geq 0.

Choosing
s>0
such that
\alpha(s)=t
, the last inequality becomes

d(x,\phi_{s}(z))\leq\eta.

Since this can be done for each
\eta>0
, it follows that
x\in\omega(z)
. Now, since
x
is stable, there exists
\delta>0
such that
H_{\delta}(x)\subseteq CT^{s}_{\varepsilon}(x)
and Proposition
4.9
ensures that

d(\Phi^{t}_{\alpha}(z),\phi_{t}(x))\to 0\,\,\,\,\,\,\text{when}\,\,\,\,\,\,t\to+\infty

for some
\alpha\in\mathcal{H}(H_{\delta}(x))
. This and
x\in\omega(z)
ensure that
x\in\omega(x)
, i.e.
x
is recurrent, while Proposition
5.5
ensures that
x
is periodic.
∎

Before stating the next result some remarks are in order.
Consider first the simpler case of homeomorphisms.
From the definitions, if
X
is finite (for instance, a single point) then each point is stable (for any homeomorphism, in this case, permutation, of
X
). These examples are also expansive.
If
X
is a single point then we have a Peano continuum with an expansive homeomorphism that presents stable points. Thus, to be precise, in the next result we have to exclude the case of
X
being a circle.

Theorem 5.8
.

Let
(X,\phi_{t})
be a cw-expansive flow defined on a Peano continuum
X
which is not a circle, then there are no stable points for
\phi_{t}
and
\phi_{-t}
.

Proof.

By contradiction, suppose that
x\in X
is a stable point and let
\delta>0
be such that
H_{\delta}(x)\subset T^{s}_{\varepsilon}(x)
. We can assume that
\delta
also satisfies the conclusion of Proposition
5.3
, reducing
\delta
if necessary. Thus, if
z\in H_{\delta}(x)
, then
z
is stable and
by Proposition
5.7
, periodic.
By Proposition
4.9
we know that
x
and
z
are asymptotic, but being periodic they must be the same point,
z=x
. We conclude that

H_{\delta}(x)=\{x\}
. This means that the orbit of the periodic point
x
is an open subset.
Since
X
is connected we conclude that
X
is the orbit of
x
,
i.e.

X
is a circle.
This contradiction proves the result.
∎

Theorem 5.9
.

If
(X,\phi_{t})
is a cw-expansive flow defined on a Peano continuum and
\varepsilon>0
is a cw-expansivity constant, then there exists
\delta>0
such that for each
x\in X
there exist continua
C_{x}\in\mathcal{T}^{s}_{\varepsilon}
and
D_{x}\in\mathcal{T}^{u}_{\varepsilon}
such that
x\in C_{x}\cap D_{x}
and
\text{diam}(C_{x})=\text{diam}(D_{x})=\delta
.

Proof.

Proposition
4.6
ensures the existence of
\delta\in(0,\varepsilon)
corresponding to
\varepsilon/2
. Let
x\in X
and
z\in\omega(x)
, then there exist
k_{0}>0
and a sequence
t_{k}\rightarrow\infty
as
k\rightarrow\infty
such that
x_{k}=\phi_{t_{k}}(x)\in H_{\frac{\delta}{2}}(z)
for every
k\geq k_{0}
. According to Theorem
5.8
, the point
z
is not stable for
\phi_{-t}
. Therefore, there exists
t_{0}>0
and an
\alpha\in\mathcal{H}(H_{\frac{\delta}{2}}(z))
such that

\mbox{diam}(\Phi^{-t_{0}}_{\alpha}(z,H_{\frac{\delta}{2}}(z)))>\varepsilon.

We can assume that
t_{k}>t_{0}
for all
k\geq 1
.
By the continuity of the field of transversal sections, given
0<\varepsilon^{\prime}<\varepsilon
we have that
for large enough values of
k
the next inequality holds

\mbox{diam}(\Phi^{-t_{0}}_{\alpha}(x_{k},H_{\delta}(x_{k})))>\varepsilon/2.

Then consider
C_{k}\subset H_{\delta}(x_{k})
sub-continua such that
x_{k}\in C_{k}
,

\text{diam}(\Phi^{-t}_{\alpha}(x_{k},C_{k}))\leq\varepsilon\,\,\,\,\,\,\text{for every}\,\,\,\,\,\,t\in[0,t_{k}]\,\,\,\,\,\,\text{and}

\text{diam}(\Phi^{-s_{k}}_{\alpha}(x_{k},C_{k}))=\varepsilon/2\,\,\,\,\,\,\text{for some}\,\,\,\,\,\,s_{k}\in[0,t_{k}].

Thus, according to Lemma
4.6
, we would have

\mbox{diam}(\Phi^{-t}_{\alpha}(x_{k},C_{k}))\geq\delta\,\,\,\,\,\,\text{for every}\,\,\,\,\,\,t\geq s_{k}.

In particular, we have

•

\mbox{diam}(\Phi^{-t_{k}}_{\alpha}(x_{k},C_{k}))\geq\delta
,

•

x\in D_{k}=\Phi^{-t_{k}}_{\alpha}(x_{k},C_{k})
,

•

\mbox{diam}(\Phi^{t}_{\alpha}(x,D_{k}))\leq\varepsilon
for every
t\in[0,t_{k}]
.

Therefore, the set
D=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}D_{k}
is a continuum,
\text{diam}\ D\geq\delta
,
x\in D
, and
D\in T^{s}_{\varepsilon}
. We can proceed similarly for the unstable case and conclude the proof.
∎

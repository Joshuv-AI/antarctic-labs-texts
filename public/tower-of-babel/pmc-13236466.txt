A retrospective report on chiggers at a focus of scrub typhus on the China-Vietnam border in Yunnan of southwest China
Authors: Rong Yu, Lei Zhang, Xian-Guo Guo
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ImportanceChiggers are the exclusive vector of scrub typhus. Jinping County, located along the China-Vietnam border in southwest China, is a focus of scrub typhus. It is of medical and veterinary importance to study chiggers in this area.ObjectiveThe present study made a retrospective analysis aiming to report chiggers on small mammals (hosts) in Jinping County on the China-Vietnam border, Yunnan of southwest China.MethodsThe indexes of chigger infestation and chigger community were routinely calculated. The niche width and overlap were calculated for analyzing chiggers’ host selection. The sample completeness was determined through coverage index (CI).ResultsA total of 7,718 chiggers from 274 small mammal hosts were identified as 52 species, 10 genera, three subfamilies and two families, including nine vector species. The CI of chigger species reached 99.84%. The genus Leptotrombidium accounted for 94.86% of 10 chigger genera. The majority of chiggers (constituent ratio [Cr] = 95.35%) came from rodents (Rodentia). The four dominant chigger species (Cr = 89.88%, 6,937/7,718) included Leptotrombidium deliense, the most important vector of scrub typhus in China.Conclusions and RelevanceThe abundant L. deliense in Jinping, together with other vector chigger species and their low host specificity, increases the potential transmission risk of scrub typhus in China-Vietnam border areas.

INTRODUCTION

Being a large group of tiny arthropods, chiggers (chigger mites) usually refer to the larvae of trombiculid mites. The life cycle of trombiculid mite includes seven basic stages, the egg (ovum), deutovum, larva (chigger), nymphochrysalis (protonymph), nymph (deutonymph), imagochrysalis (tritonymph), and adult (male and female). In the complex life cycle of trombiculid mite, only the larva is the parasitic stage as the ectoparasite of other animals, and the remaining stages are all free-living organisms that often grow in the top soil. Rodents and other small mammals are the most important hosts of chiggers [12]. To date, more than 3,000 species of chiggers have been recorded worldwide, and over 500 species have been documented in China [2]. Chiggers are the exclusive vector of scrub typhus (tsutsugamushi disease), and some of them can act as the potential vector of hemorrhagic fever with renal syndrome (HFRS) [34]. Chiggers can transmit these zoonotic diseases (scrub typhus and HFRS) among wild animals (rodents and other animals) and even from wild animals to humans [345]. Through the biting activity of chiggers, some domestic animals (cattle, sheep, pigs, dogs, etc.) can get the infection of Orientia tsutsugamushi (Ot), the causative agent of scrub typhus [56]. Jinping County, located along the China-Vietnam border in Yunnan Province of southwest China, is an important focus of scrub typhus [78]. Scrub typhus is also prevalent in Vietnam, including northern Vietnam bordering Jinping [3]. With the growing prosperity of cross-border trade and tourism, the cross-border movement of people is becoming more and more frequent, which increases the potential risk of cross-border transmission of scrub typhus between China and Vietnam [789]. Although scrub typhus is a persistent risk in China-Vietnam border areas, little is known about its vector chiggers in the areas. From 2001 to 2022, a series of field investigations on chiggers were conducted in 34 counties (survey sites) of Yunnan Province, southwest China, including investigations conducted in Jinping County on the China-Vietnam border in 2011 and 2015. In the investigation of Jinping, a lot of chiggers were collected and identified from the body surface of small mammals. Based on the taxonomic identification of chiggers, the present study made a retrospective analysis on chiggers in Jinping, which aims to accumulate the data information about chiggers in China-Vietnam border areas. The study is of medical and veterinary significance, and it may provide scientific information for reference in the surveillance and control of vector chiggers and scrub typhus in the border areas.

METHODS

Field investigations were conducted in Jinping County on the China-Vietnam border in 2011 and 2015, and the three survey months (January, November, and December) happened to be the winter season of Jinping (Fig. 1A). In the field investigation, mousetraps were used to capture rodents and other sympatric small mammals (hosts) in different habitats. Chiggers on the body surface of each host were collected in a conventional way [1011]. The collected chiggers were mounted onto glass slides with Hoyer’s medium and finally identified into species under a microscope [111]. The capture and use of rodents and other sympatric small mammals for the research was officially approved by the local wildlife affairs authority and Animals’ Ethics Committees of Dali University. The approval codes are DLXY2001-1116 and DLDXLL2020-1104.

Based on the taxonomic identification of chiggers, the constituent ratio (Cr) was used to calculate the percentage (%) of each chigger species. The prevalence (PM), mean abundance (MA) and mean intensity (MI) were calculated to reflect the infestation status of small mammal hosts with chiggers. The PM stands for the percentage of infested hosts with chiggers (%), while MA and MI represent the average number of chiggers on each examined host and the average number of chiggers on each infected host, respectively [1112]. The species richness (S), Shannon-Wiener diversity index (H′), Simpson dominance index (D) and Pielou evenness index (E) were used for chigger community statistics [1213]. Based on the rarefaction and extrapolation curves, the coverage index (CI) was calculated to reflect sampling completeness, in which CI ≥ 90% indicates an adequate sampling in the field investigation [1415]. Levin’s niche width (Bi ) and Pianka’s niche overlap (Oij ) were used to analyze the host selection of main chigger species [1316]. The data statistics of the present study excluded the unidentified chiggers that could not be accurately identified to species due to structural damage, dirt coverage or unclear morphology.

RESULTS

The original data came from the historical field investigation conducted in 2011 and 2015. A total of 274 animal hosts were captured from Jinping, with 247 hosts captured in 2011 and 27 hosts captured in 2015. These animal hosts were taxonomically identified as 11 species, seven genera and four families in two orders. The nine rodent species accounted for 96.35% of total hosts (Cr = 96.35%, 264/274) and they are Rattus nitidus, Rattus tanezumi, Rattus andamanensis, Niviventer confucianus, Niviventer fulvescens, Berylmys bowersi, Mus caroli, Eothenomys miletus, and Eothenomys melanogaster in Rodentia. And the two insectivore species (Cr = 3.65%, 10/274) in Eulipotyphla are Anourosorex squamipes and Hylomy suillus. R. nitidus (Cr = 47.81%, 131/274) and R. tanezumi (Cr = 37.96%, 104/274) are the dominant species with total Cr = 85.77%. A total of 7,718 chiggers from 274 small mammal hosts were identified as 52 species, 10 genera and three subfamilies in two families, including nine vector species (Table 1, Figs. 1B and 2A). The majority of chiggers (95.35%, 7,359/7,718) came from rodents (Rodentia), and only 4.65% (359/7718) of chiggers were identified from insectivores (Eulipotyphla). The genus Leptotrombidium accounted for 94.86% of the total chiggers (Fig. 1C). Leptotrombidium xinjiangense, Leptotrombidium jianshanense, Leptotrombidium deliense, and Leptotrombidium xiaguanense were four dominant chigger species with total Cr = 89.88% (6,937/7,718). The coverage index of chiggers CI = 99.84% (CI > 90%).

The prevalence of chiggers on Rodentia (PM = 94.70%, 250/264) were higher than that on Eulipotyphla (PM = 70.00%, 7/10) with statistical significance (p < 0.05). The mean abundance of chiggers on Rodentia (MA = 27.88) was lower than that on Eulipotyphla (MA = 35.90) (p < 0.01). The difference of mean intensity of chiggers on Rodentia (MI = 29.44) and Eulipotyphla (MI = 51.29) was of no statistical significance (p > 0.05). The PM of chiggers on two dominant rat species (R. nitidus and R. tanezumi) was higher than that on other hosts, the rest hosts (p < 0.01; Fig. 1D). The mean abundance of chiggers on R. nitidus (MA = 29.41) and R. tanezumi (MA = 23.88) was lower than that on other hosts (MA = 35.41) (p < 0.01; Fig. 1E). The mean intensity of chiggers on R. nitidus (MI = 30.10) and R. tanezumi (MI = 25.88) was also lower than that on other hosts (MI = 41.85), but without statistical significance (p > 0.05; Fig. 1F). The S and H′ of chigger community on Rodentia (S = 46, H′ = 1.68) were higher than those on Eulipotyphla (S = 14, H′ = 1.61). The D and E of chigger community on Rodentia (D = 0.24, E = 0.44), however, were lower than those on Eulipotyphla (D = 0.29, E = 0.61). Moreover, one host species (e.g., R. nitidus or R. tanezumi) could harbor a few chigger species, and one chigger species (e.g., any of four dominant chigger species) could parasitize several host species (Fig. 2B). The results of niche analysis for 12 main chigger species (dominant and vector species) showed that Leptotrombidium rubellum had the highest niche width (Bi = 4.67). L. jianshanense and L. xinjiangense showed the highest niche overlap (Oij = 0.99).

DISCUSSION

In the present study, 52 chigger species were identified from rodents and other sympatric small mammals in Jinping on the China-Vietnam border, indicating the high species diversity of chiggers in the border area. The majority of chiggers (Cr = 95.35%) were identified from rodents (Rodentia), indicating that rodents are the main hosts of chiggers in Jinping. Of 11 small mammal host species (274 individuals) identified, two rat species (R. nitidus and R. tanezumi) were the dominant hosts of chiggers with total Cr = 85.77%. R. tanezumi and R. nitidus are two important species of peridomestic rats being close to human activities and they can act as a bridge for transmitting the pathogens (including Ot) and vectors (including chiggers) of some zoonotic diseases from the rats to domestic animals and humans [61217]. The occurrence of abundant chiggers on rodents (especially R. nitidus and R. tanezumi) in Jinping is of specific medical and veterinary significance in the border areas.

Although over 3,000 chigger species have been recorded worldwide, not all chiggers can be effective vectors of scrub typhus and other zoonotic diseases. Most effective vector chiggers are distributed in the genus Leptotrombidium all over the world [125]. There are six main vectors and more than ten secondary or potential vectors of scrub typhus in China, and most of them belong to the genus Leptotrombidium. Of the six main vectors, L. deliense is the most important one [2]. Among the 52 chigger species in Jinping, nine were vector species, and most vector species belonged to Leptotrombidium (Table 1, Fig. 2A). The genus Leptotrombidium accounted for 94.86%, and L. deliense was one of four dominant chigger species (Table 1, Fig. 2A). Previous studies have indicated that L. deliense is one of dominant chigger species in southern and southwestern China [2], and the result of the present study is consistent with previous studies. The abundant Leptotrombidium chiggers (especially L. deliense) occurred in Jinping might increase the potential transmission risk of scrub typhus in China-Vietnam border areas. Being an important vector of scrub typhus, L. deliense is widely distributed in tropical and subtropical regions including Vietnam bordering Jinping. Abundant chigger species were previously found on rodents (especially Rattus rats) and other small mammals in Vietnam, with 31 species belonging to the genus Leptotrombidium, and L. deliense was widely distributed across 23 provinces of Vietnam [18]. The wide distribution of L. deliense in Vietnam might further increases the potential risk of the cross-border transmission of scrub typhus.

Chigger-host mutual relationships showed that one host species could harbor a few chigger species, and one chigger species could parasitize several host species (Fig. 2B), and this result suggest that most chiggers have low host specificity. The low host specificity of chiggers is beneficial to their transmitting the pathogen of scrub typhus (Ot) among different rodents and other wild animals, and even from wild animals to domestic animals and humans [619]. The appearance of abundant L. deliense in Jinping, together with the co-existence of other vector species and their low host specificity, might further increases the potential transmission risk of scrub typhus in the border areas.

The niche analysis showed that L. rubellum had the highest niche width (Bi = 4.67) in host selection. L. rubellum is a sibling species with L. deliense, and it is also one of six main vectors of scrub typhus in China [220]. The highest value of niche width indicates that L. rubellum can choose a variety of hosts with very low host specificity, and this also increases the potential risk of its transmitting the pathogen of scrub typhus (Ot) among different hosts, including rodents and domestic animals. L. jianshanense and L. xinjiangense showed the highest niche overlap (Oij = 0.99), indicating they have a tendency to choose the same hosts.

As a retrospective investigation, the present study has its specific limitations. The study only conducted some relevant analyses based on the limited historical survey data. The original data from historical field investigations only covered two years (2011 and 2015) and three survey months, January, November and December. The host samples were extremely uneven, with 247 hosts sampled in 2011 and only 27 hosts collected in 2015, and this unbalanced data from two different years might affect the stability of the results. If host samples are significantly expanded in future studies, results may exhibit some fluctuations. The species composition and infestation burdens of chiggers on animal hosts may vary in different years. The results of this study only reflected the historical situation of chiggers in the studied area (Jinping) and cannot fully represent the current situation. In future research, a long-term investigation across different years may be required to understand the annual fluctuation of chiggers in Jinping. A study on seasonal dynamics of chiggers is quite different from the present retrospective analysis. To investigate the seasonal fluctuation of chiggers, a 12-month consecutive field survey in a fixed survey site is recommended in future in-depth studies.

Despite its specific limitations, a retrospective study holds significant scientific value in unveiling certain historical phenomena and can offer valuable guidance for subsequent in-depth studies. Before this retrospective study, there had been no specific reports on chiggers in the China-Vietnam border areas, and the present study reported the chiggers on small mammals in Jinping (the China-Vietnam border area) for the first time. The results of the present study objectively reflected the historical facts of chiggers in the studied area, which may provide scientific information for reference in the surveillance and control of vector chiggers and scrub typhus in the border areas.

In Jinping on the China-Vietnam border, there are a lot of chigger species with coexistence of multiple vector species, and rodents (especially rats R. nitidus, R. tanezumi) are the main hosts of chiggers. The abundant L. deliense in Jinping, together with other vector chigger species and their low host specificity, might increase the potential transmission risk of scrub typhus in China-Vietnam border areas.

Abstract
. Let G G be a group. The BCI problem asks whether two Haar graphs of G G are isomorphic if and only if they are isomorphic by an element of an explicit list of isomorphisms. We first generalize this problem in a natural way and give a theoretical way to solve the isomorphism problem for the natural generalization. We then restrict our attention to abelian groups and, with an exception, reduce the problem to the isomorphism problem for a related quotient, component, or corresponding Cayley digraph. For Haar graphs of an abelian group of odd order with connection sets S S those of Cayley graphs (i.e. S = − S S=-S ), the exception does not exist. For Haar graphs of cyclic groups of odd order with connection sets those of a Cayley graph, among others, we solve the isomorphism problem.



This is the fourth paper in an unplanned series of papers whose aim now is to investigate the relationship between symmetries of Cayley digraphs of a group
G
and other graphs and digraphs obtained from different actions of the group
G
(other than the regular one as for Cayley digraphs), as well as similar relationships for problems that depend on symmetries. The first paper in this series
[
15
]
, motivated by the work in this paper, gave the relationship between the automorphism group of Cayley digraphs and Haar graphs of abelian groups. The second
[
2
]
, motivated by the problem of defining generalized wreath products for Cayley digraphs of nonabelian groups, not only did this, but also gave the relationship between the automorphism groups of Cayley digraphs and double coset digraphs of the same group and connection set. The third
[
3
]
gives a recognition theorem for which bicoset digraphs can be written as a nontrivial
X
-join with empty graphs, the bipartite analogue of
[
2
, Theorem 3.3]
, which is the crucial tool in giving the relationship between the automorphism groups of Cayley digraphs and their corresponding double coset digraphs. In this paper, we consider applications of the work in
[
15
]
to the isomorphism problem for Haar graphs of abelian groups.

It is well known that the isomorphism problem for Cayley digraphs depends on a property of its automorphism group (Lemma
2.5
). It is also known that there is a relationship between the automorphism group of a Haar graph of a group
G
with connection set
S
(also called a bi-Cayley graph) and the automorphism group of the Cayley digraph of a group
G
with connection set
S
(see Theorem
4.5
(
3
) for this relationship for abelian groups). The original motivation for this paper was to see what information one could obtain about the isomorphism problem for Haar graphs from information about the isomorphism problem for Cayley digraphs. We remark that as there is much that is known (and not known) about the Cayley isomorphism problem for digraphs, this is a reasonable idea to investigate. In
[
15
]
the relationship between the automorphism group of a Haar graph of an abelian group and its corresponding Cayley digraph was studied, motivated by hopes of using that information to obtain results on isomorphisms between Haar graphs.

That being said, the existing isomorphism problem for Haar graphs is called the BCI problem (BCI stands for bi-Cayley isomorphism). It asks whether two Haar graphs of a group
G
are isomorphic by a specific list
L
of maps (as does the more famous CI problem for Cayley digraphs). We will show that the Cayley isomorphism problem can be generalized in at least two ways, and that the list of maps of the current BCI problem is
not
a shortest list of natural maps to check for isomorphism, but is
shorter
than that (it is, though, one of the ways the CI problem can be generalized). Maps that interchange the natural bipartition of a Haar graph are missing from
L
. We propose an alternative BCI problem and show that the list of possible maps we propose is the shortest list of elements that normalize the natural semiregular subgroup isomorphic to a group
G
that is contained in the automorphism group of a Haar graph of
G
(another way in which the CI problem can be generalized). This is the subject of Section
2
.
As we are still motivated by the problem of transferring information about isomorphisms between Cayley digraphs to isomorphisms between Haar graphs, we are not only concerned with this process for CI-digraphs of
G
(those with the “nicest” possible list of isomorphisms to check), but also with the isomorphism problem for all Cayley digraphs. For the CI problem, there are results that reduce the problem to conjugacy classes of regular subgroups isomorphic to
G
in the automorphism group of a Cayley digraph of
G
, and so in Section
3
we develop similar results to reduce the isomorphism problem of Haar graphs of
G
to conjugacy classes of semi-regular subgroups of
G
in their automorphism groups.

In
[
15
]
, automorphism groups of Haar graphs of abelian groups are shown to be in one of four families, two of which reduce to automorphism groups of subgraphs or quotients of Haar graphs, one that reduces to the automorphism group of their corresponding Cayley digraphs, and one other family that seems difficult to handle. In Section
4
we consider the isomorphism problem for the first three families, reducing the problem to either the isomorphism problem for related quotients, subgraphs, or Cayley digraphs. Recently, Dave Morris has shown
[
34
]
that for Haar graphs of abelian groups whose corresponding connection set gives a Cayley graph, the fourth family does not exist. We discuss applications of that result together with the other work in this paper in Section
5
, and, among other results, we solve the isomorphism problem for Haar graphs of cyclic groups of odd order whose connection sets are those of Cayley graphs (Corollary
5.9
).

1.
Preliminaries

We begin with basic definitions and notation that will be used throughout the paper. Other, more specific terms, will be given as needed.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
G
be a group, and
S\subseteq G
. Define a
Cayley digraph of
G
, denoted
\text{Cay}(G,S)
, to be the digraph with vertex set
V(\text{Cay}(G,S))=G
and arc set
A(\text{Cay}(G,S))=\{(g,gs):g\in\penalty G,s\in S\}.
We call
S
the
connection set of
\text{Cay}(G,S)
.

The condition that
A({\rm Cay}(G,S))=\{(g,gs):g\in G,s\in S\}
is equivalent to
(x,y)\in A({\rm Cay}(G,S))
if and only if
x^{-1}y\in S
.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
G
be a group, and
g\in G
. Define
g_{L}\colon G\to G
by
g_{L}(x)=gx
. The map
g_{L}
is a
left translation of
G
. The
left regular representation of
G
, denoted
G_{L}
, is
G_{L}=\{g_{L}:g\in G\}
. That is,
G_{L}
is the group of all left translations of
G
.

It is straightforward to verify that
G_{L}
is a group isomorphic to
G
. It is also straightforward to verify that
G_{L}\leq{\rm Aut}({\rm Cay}(G,S))
.

Definition 1.3
.

Let
G
be a group, and
S\subseteq G
. Define the
Haar graph

{\rm Haar}(G,S)
with
connection set

S
to be the graph with vertex set
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times G
and edge set
\{(0,g)(1,gs):g\in G{\rm\ and\ }s\in S\}
.

We remark that some authors refer to Haar graphs as bi-Cayley graphs, and denote them accordingly. As with Cayley graphs, note that
(0,x)(1,y)\in E({\rm Haar}(G,S))
if and only if
x^{-1}y\in S
. Also note that Haar graphs are natural bipartite analogues of Cayley digraphs and have bipartition
{\mathcal{B}}=\{\{i\}\times G:i\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\}
. We set
B_{i}=\{\{i\}\times G\}
so that
{\mathcal{B}}=\{B_{0},B_{1}\}
, and let
F
be the set-wise stabilizer of
B_{0}
and
B_{1}
in
{\rm Aut}({\rm Haar}(G,S))
. This notation will be used throughout the paper. Haar graphs were introduced in
[
22
]
.

Definition 1.4
.

For
g\in G
, define
\widehat{g}_{L}:\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times G\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times G
by
\widehat{g}_{L}(i,j)=(i,g_{L}(j))
. It is straightforward to verify that
\widehat{g}_{L}
is a bijection. Define
\widehat{G}_{L}=\{\widehat{g}_{L}:g\in G\}
.

As with Cayley digraphs, it is straightforward to verify that
\widehat{G}_{L}
is a group isomorphic to
G
and
\widehat{G}_{L}\leq{\rm Aut}({\rm Haar}(G,S))
. Note that while
G_{L}
is transitive,
\widehat{G}_{L}
has two orbits of size
|G|
. We now give some permutation group theoretic terms.

Definition 1.5
.

Let
G\leq S_{n}
with orbit
{\mathcal{O}}
, and
g\in G
. Then
g
induces a permutation on
{\mathcal{O}}
by restricting the domain of
g
to
{\mathcal{O}}
. We denote the resulting permutation in
S_{\mathcal{O}}
by
g^{\mathcal{O}}
. The group
G^{\mathcal{O}}=\{g^{\mathcal{O}}:g\in G\}
is the
transitive constituent
of
G
on
{\mathcal{O}}
.

Definition 1.6
.

Let
X
be a set, and let
G\leq S_{X}
be transitive. A subset
C\subseteq X
is a
block
of
G
if whenever
g\in G
, then
g(C)\cap C=\emptyset
or
C
. If
C=\{x\}
for some
x\in X
or
C=X
, then
C
is a
trivial block
. Any other block is nontrivial. Note that if
C
is a block of
G
, then
g(C)
is also a block of
G
for every
g\in G
, and is called a
conjugate block of
C
. The set of all blocks conjugate to

is a partition of

, called a
block system of

.

Definition 1.7
.

Let
G\leq S_{n}
be transitive with a block system
{\mathcal{C}}
. By
{\rm fix}_{G}({\mathcal{C}})
we denote the subgroup of
G
that fixes each
block of
{\mathcal{C}}
set-wise. That is,
{\rm fix}_{G}({\mathcal{C}})=\{g\in G:g(C)=C{\rm\ for\ all\ }C\in{\mathcal{C}}\}
. We denote the induced action of
G
on the block system
{\mathcal{C}}
by
G/{\mathcal{C}}
, and the action of an element
g\in G
on
{\mathcal{C}}
by
g/{\mathcal{C}}
. That is,
g/{\mathcal{C}}(C)=C^{\prime}
if and only if

, and

.

2.
The Alternative BCI problem

To begin, we will calculate
N_{S_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times G}}(\widehat{G}_{L})
. This has been done more or less in
[
46
]
, but the normalizers are not explicitly stated, nor is the structure of the normalizer given. For completeness, we will derive them here.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
G
be a group,
A={\rm Aut}(G)
,
g\in G
, and
\sigma\in S_{G}
. Define
\bar{g},\widetilde{g},\tau,\widehat{\sigma}:{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times G\to{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times G
by

\bar{g}(0,x)=(0,x){\rm\ \ \ and\ \ \ }\bar{g}(1,x)=(1,xg^{-1}),

\widetilde{g}(0,x)=(0,xg^{-1}){\rm\ \ \ and\ \ \ }\widetilde{g}(1,x)=(1,x),

\tau(i,j)=(i+1,j),\ \ \ {\rm and}\ \ \ \widehat{\sigma}(i,j)=(i,\sigma(j)).

Set
\bar{G}=\{\bar{g}:g\in G\}
,
\widetilde{G}=\{\widetilde{g}:g\in G\}
, and
\widehat{A}=\{\widehat{a}:a\in A\}
. Then
\widetilde{g}=\tau^{-1}\bar{g}\tau
for all
g\in G
,
\bar{G}
, and
\widetilde{G}
are groups with
\widetilde{G}\bar{G}\cong G\times G
and
N_{S_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times G}}(\widehat{G}_{L})={\langle}\tau{\rangle}\cdot\widehat{A}\cdot(\widetilde{G}\bar{G})
.

Proof.

Straightforward calculations will show that
\tau,\widehat{\alpha},\widetilde{g}
, and
\bar{g}
all normalize
\widehat{G}_{L}
(this was also observed in
[
46
]
). For the calculations for
\widetilde{g}
and
\bar{g}
, notice that we multiply on the right and that the centralizer in
S_{G}
of
G_{L}
is
G_{R}

[
5
, Lemma 4.2A]
, where
G_{R}
is the right regular representation of
G
. Then
\widetilde{G}
and
\bar{G}
are canonically isomorphic to
G
, and the elements of
\widetilde{G}
and
\bar{G}
obviously commute, so
\widetilde{G}\bar{G}\cong G\times G
. Clearly
\tau
normalizes
\widetilde{G}\bar{G}
and
\widehat{A}
, and
\widetilde{g}=\tau^{-1}\bar{g}\tau
for all
g\in G
. It is also clear that
\widehat{A}
normalizes
\widetilde{G}\bar{G}
, and so
\widetilde{G}\bar{G}\triangleleft{\langle}\tau,\widehat{A},\widetilde{G},\bar{G}{\rangle}=M
. Any element of
\widetilde{G}\bar{G}
is either semiregular on
B_{i}
or fixes each point of
B_{i+1}
, for some
i\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
, while
\widehat{A}
fixes a point in each of
B_{0}
and
B_{1}
, namely
(0,1_{G})
and
(1,1_{G})
. Thus
(\widetilde{G}\bar{G})\cap\widehat{A}=1
. Also,
{\langle}\tau{\rangle}\cap\widehat{A}=1={\langle}\tau{\rangle}\cap(\widetilde{G}\bar{G})
. Thus
M={\langle}\tau{\rangle}\cdot\widehat{A}\cdot(\widetilde{G}\bar{G})
.

As
\widehat{G}_{L}^{B_{i}}
,
i\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
, is transitive on
B_{i}
, and
\tau
interchanges
B_{0}
and
B_{1}
, we see that
N=N_{S_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times G}}(\widehat{G}_{L})
is transitive. Additionally, as
\widehat{G}_{L}\triangleleft N
,
N
has
{\mathcal{B}}
as a normal block system. As
\tau/{\mathcal{B}}\not=1
and
N/{\mathcal{B}}\cong{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
, it suffices to show that
F={\rm fix}_{N}({\mathcal{B}})=\widehat{A}\cdot(\widetilde{G}\bar{G})
.

Let
\omega\in F
. As
\widetilde{G}
is transitive on
B_{0}
, there exists
x\in G
such that
\delta=\omega\widetilde{x}
fixes
(0,1_{G})
. As
\omega
and
\widetilde{x}
normalize
\widehat{G}_{L}
, so does
\delta
. Let
g\in G
. Hence
\delta^{-1}\widehat{g}_{L}\delta=\widehat{k_{g}}_{L}
, for some
k_{g}\in G
. The map
\alpha:G\to G
given by
\alpha(g)=k_{g}
is then an automorphism of
G
as conjugation induces a bijection and

\delta^{-1}\widehat{gh}_{L}\delta=\delta^{-1}\widehat{g}_{L}\widehat{h}_{L}\delta=\delta^{-1}\widehat{g}_{L}\delta\cdot\delta^{-1}\widehat{h}_{L}\delta.

Equivalently,
\alpha(gh)=\alpha(g)\alpha(h)
. As
\delta^{-1}\widehat{g}_{L}\delta=\widehat{k_{g}}_{L}
for
h\in G
and
i\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
we have

\displaystyle\widehat{\alpha}^{-1}\delta^{-1}\widehat{g}_{L}\delta\widehat{\alpha}(i,h)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widehat{\alpha}^{-1}\widehat{k_{g}}_{L}\widehat{\alpha}(i,h)=\widehat{\alpha}^{-1}\widehat{k_{g}}_{L}(i,\alpha(h))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widehat{\alpha}^{-1}(i,k_{g}\alpha(h))=\widehat{\alpha}^{-1}(i,k_{g}k_{h})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widehat{\alpha}^{-1}(i,k_{gh})=(i,gh)=\widehat{g}_{L}(h).

Thus
\widehat{\alpha}^{-1}\delta^{-1}\bar{g}_{L}\delta\widehat{\alpha}=\widehat{g}_{L}
,
\delta\widehat{\alpha}
centralizes
\widehat{G}_{L}
and fixes
(0,1_{G})
.

As mentioned above, the centralizer in
S_{G}
of
G_{L}
is
G_{R}
. Hence
\delta\widehat{\alpha}^{B_{i}}
is naturally isomorphic to an element of
G_{R}
,
i\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
, and so
\delta\widehat{\alpha}\in\widetilde{G}\bar{G}
. As
G_{R}
is regular and
\delta\widehat{\alpha}
fixes
(0,1_{G})
, we see that
\delta\widehat{\alpha}\in\bar{G}
.
Thus
\omega\widetilde{x}\widehat{\alpha}=\bar{g^{\prime}}
for some
g^{\prime}\in G
, and
\omega=\bar{g^{\prime}}\widehat{\alpha}^{-1}\widetilde{x}^{-1}
. As
\widehat{A}
normalizes
\bar{G}
, there is
g\in G
such that
\bar{g^{\prime}}\widehat{\alpha}^{-1}=\widehat{\alpha}^{-1}\bar{g}
. Hence
\omega=\widehat{\alpha}^{-1}\bar{g}\widetilde{x}^{-1}=\widehat{\alpha}^{-1}\widetilde{x}^{-1}\bar{g}
. We conclude that
F=\widehat{A}\cdot(\widetilde{G}\bar{G})
as required.
∎

Observe that
\widehat{G_{L}}=\widehat{G}_{L}
as previously defined. We will use the notation
\widehat{G}_{L}
for
\widehat{G_{L}}
.

Remark 2.2
.

An obvious question arises from the statement of Lemma
2.1
. Where is
\widehat{G}_{L}
in
N_{S_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times G}}(\widehat{G}_{L})
? As mentioned above,
G_{R}
centralizes and so normalizes
G_{L}
, and, as also mentioned above,
N_{S_{G}}(G_{L})={\rm Aut}(G)\cdot G_{L}
. This means
G_{R}\leq{\rm Aut}(G)\cdot G_{L}
. We also have

, and

We now give the basic definition regarding the Cayley isomorphism problem in its most general form.

Definition 2.3
.

A
Cayley object

X
of a group
G
in a class
{\mathcal{K}}
of combinatorial objects is one in which
G_{L}\leq{\rm Aut}(X)
, the automorphism group of
X
.

Although we will not dwell on the definition of a combinatorial object, the interested reader is referred to
[
38
]
, where a discussion of some definitions is given.

A classical result of Sabidussi
[
40
, Lemma 4]
(see
[
16
, Theorem 1.20]
for a more modern proof) shows that a digraph
\Gamma
is isomorphic to a Cayley digraph of a group
G
if and only if
\Gamma
contains a regular subgroup isomorphic to
G
. Thus the above definition is consistent with the definition of a Cayley digraph.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
\Gamma
be a Cayley (di)graph of
G
such that if
\Gamma^{\prime}
is any Cayley (di)graph of
G
, then
\Gamma
and
\Gamma^{\prime}
are isomorphic if and only if they are isomorphic by a group automorphism of
G
. Such a Cayley (di)graph of
G
is called a
CI-(di)graph of
G
. Similarly, for a Cayley object
X
of
G
in some class of combinatorial objects
{\mathcal{K}}
, we say that
X
is a
CI-object of
G
if and only if whenever
X^{\prime}
is another Cayley object of
G
in
{\mathcal{K}}
, then
X
and
X^{\prime}
are isomorphic if and only if
\alpha(X)=X^{\prime}
for some
\alpha\in{\rm Aut}(G)
. A group
G
which has the property that any two Cayley objects of
G
in
{\mathcal{K}}
are isomorphic if and only if they are isomorphic by a group automorphism of
G
is called a
CI-group with respect to
{\mathcal{K}}
.

Almost all results showing that a particular group is a CI-group with respect to some class of combinatorial objects make use of the following result due to Babai
[
1
]
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
X
be a Cayley object of
G
in some class
{\mathcal{K}}
of combinatorial objects. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

X
is a CI-object of
G
in
{\mathcal{K}}
,

(2)

whenever
\phi\in S_{G}
such that
\phi^{-1}G_{L}\phi\leq{\rm Aut}(X)
,
G_{L}
and
\phi^{-1}G_{L}\phi
are conjugate in
{\rm Aut}(X)
.

For groups that are not CI-groups with respect to digraphs, Muzychuk
[
38
]
introduced the notion of a solving set. The notion of a solving set is simply to give a set of permutations for which isomorphism between two Cayley digraphs of
G
can be tested. That is, two Cayley digraphs of
G
are isomorphic if and only if they are isomorphic by an element of the solving set. As the image of a Cayley digraph of
G
under a group automorphism of
G
is again a Cayley digraph of
G
, members of
{\rm Aut}(G)
are typically in a solving set (although if an automorphism of
G
is in the automorphism group of the Cayley digraph, they do not need to be formally in the solving set). Additionally, by
[
5
, Lemma 4.2A]
,
N_{S_{G}}(G_{L})={\rm Aut}(G)\cdot G_{L}
, and so
{\rm Aut}(G)={\rm Stab}_{N_{S_{G}}(G_{L})}(1_{G})
. That is, the elements which must always be tested to determine isomorphism normalize
G_{L}
and fix
1_{G}
. Later, in
[
10
]
, the notion of a CI-extension was developed. The idea behind a CI-extension is to give an analogue of Babai’s Lemma for solving sets. Namely, a conjugation condition is given for a set
T
of permutations in
S_{G}
, and a solving set for
G
can then be obtained from
T
and
{\rm Aut}(G)
.

There are then at least two ways in which one can generalize the Cayley isomorphism problem to the “bi-Cayley Isomorphism Problem”. Namely, one can determine the smallest set of possible isomorphisms that must be tested to determine isomorphism, or to test isomorphisms between Haar graphs using the elements of
N_{S_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times G}}(\widehat{G}_{L})
which fix
(0,1_{G})
. Unfortunately, and unlike in the Cayley isomorphism problem for digraphs, these two sets are not the same. The original BCI problem, introduced in
[
45
]
, calls a group
G
a BCI-group if and only if two Haar graphs of
G
are isomorphic by an element of
\bar{G}\widehat{A}
, which is the set of elements of
N_{S_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times G}}(\widehat{G}_{L})
that fix
(0,1_{G})
. However, the map
\tau
given in Lemma
2.1
normalizes
\widehat{G}_{L}
, does not fix any points, and is not contained in
\widehat{G}_{L}
. However, we note that the automorphism group of any Haar graph of an abelian group
A
contains the map
(i,j)\mapsto(i+1,-j)
, which is a product of
\tau
and the map
(i,j)\mapsto(i,-j)
, an automorphism of
\widehat{A}_{L}
. This means that of the two possible ways one can generalize the Cayley isomorphism to the BCI problem, for abelian groups they are identical.

For nonabelian groups, though, the automorphism group of a Haar graph need not be vertex-transitive
[
32
]
, and so some nonabelian groups (for example, the dihedral group
D_{p}
of order
2p
where
p
is prime and large enough) may be non-BCI-groups simply because there is some Haar graph whose automorphism group does not have an automorphism which switches the natural bipartition classes, while the normalizer of
\widehat{G}_{L}
does. To the authors, this does not really seem a good reason for a group to be non-BCI. Thus we will use the other generalization of the CI-property and consider a smallest set of possible isomorphisms that must be tested to determine isomorphism. We will call these groups ABCI-groups (for alternative bi-Cayley isomorphism), as we use the alternative generalization of the CI-property (the formal definition will be given below). We first determine a list
L
of maps contained in
N_{S_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times G}}(\widehat{G}_{L})
such that two Haar graphs of
G
are isomorphic by an element of
N_{S_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times G}}(\widehat{G}_{L})
if and only if they are isomorphic by an element in
L
. As this proof is entirely group theoretic and depends only upon
N_{S_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times G}}(\widehat{G}_{L})
, we state and prove it in more generality as it requires no extra work. We will, though, need an additional definition.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
G
be a group, and
X
a combinatorial object in some class
{\mathcal{K}}
of combinatorial objects. We say
X
is a
Haar object of
G
if
{\rm Aut}(X)
, the automorphism group of
X
, contains
\widehat{G}_{L}
.

Lemma 2.7
.

Using the notation of Lemma
2.1
, two Haar objects of a group
G
in some class
{\mathcal{K}}
of combinatorial objects are isomorphic by an element of
N_{S_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times G}}({\widehat{G}_{L}})
if and only if they are isomorphic by an element of
N_{S_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times G}}(\widehat{G}_{L})
of the form
\tau^{i}\widehat{\alpha}\bar{g}
, with
i\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{2},\alpha\in{\rm Aut}(G)
, and
g\in G
.

Proof.

Let
X
and
Y
be two isomorphic Haar objects of
G
in some class
{\mathcal{K}}
of combinatorial objects with

,
\alpha\in{\rm Aut}(G)
, and

\widehat{h}_{L}^{-1}\in{\rm Aut}(X)
and

Y=\tau^{i}\widehat{\alpha}\widetilde{h}\bar{g^{\prime}}\widehat{h}_{L}^{-1}(X)=\tau^{i}\widehat{\alpha}{\overline{g^{\prime}h^{-1}}}(X)

and the result follows with

as the converse is trivial.

This result shows that, theoretically, we only need to check elements of the form
\tau^{i}\widehat{\alpha}\bar{g}
to check whether two Haar graphs are isomorphic by an element of
N_{S_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times G}}(\widehat{G}_{L})
. However, it is possible that a shorter list might suffice. In
[
36
]
, Joy Morris and Pablo Spiga showed that, with the exception of Haar graphs of abelian groups, there are only 20 groups
G
for which there is no Haar graph with automorphism group
\widehat{G}_{L}
. For Haar graphs
{\rm Haar}(G,S)
with
G
nonabelian and not on this list with
{\rm Aut}({\rm Haar}(G,S))=\widehat{G}_{L}
, the image of
{\rm Haar}(G,S)
under any map of the form
\tau\widehat{\alpha}\bar{g}
will be a Haar graph of
G
different from
{\rm Haar}(G,S)
, and moreover, the images are also pairwise different (otherwise there will be an “extra” automorphism in
{\rm Aut}({\rm Haar}(G,S))
). For such groups, the shortest possible list of elements of the normalizers to check isomorphism will be those of the form
\tau\widehat{\alpha}\bar{g}
. We can say more for Haar graphs of abelian groups.

Lemma 2.8
.

Using the notation of Lemma
2.1
, two Haar graphs of an abelian group

of the form

Proof.

If
T\subseteq A
such that

∎

3.
ABCI-extensions

Most of the time, results on isomorphisms of Haar graphs focus on the BCI problem. However, our main motivation for writing this paper is to see what the implications of isomorphisms of Cayley digraphs of
G
are for isomorphisms of Haar graphs. So we are interested in more than just the ABCI problem and thus need tools to solve the problem of finding isomorphisms between Haar graphs that are not ABCI-graphs. The analogue for this problem in the CI problem are CI-extensions introduced in
[
10
]
, which give a systematic way to find solving sets for Cayley digraphs of a group
G
introduced in
[
38
]
. This very much works in the same way as the results in
[
10
]
, when one makes the obvious changes. So the statements and proofs are very similar to those in
[
10
]
. The proofs are included for completeness. These types of results are mainly group theoretic, and as it will require little extra work, we work in the more general context of Haar objects.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
G
be a finite group. We say that
S\subseteq S_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times G}
is a
solving set for a Haar object
X
in a class of Haar objects

{\mathcal{K}}
if the following hold:

(1)

for every
X^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{K}}
such that
X\cong X^{\prime}
, there exists
s\in S
such that
s(X)=X^{\prime}
,

(2)

if
s\in S
and
s(0,1_{G})\in B_{0}
then
s(0,1_{G})=(0,1_{G})
, and

(3)

{\rm Iso}(G)\subseteq S
.

Condition (1) ensures that all Haar objects isomorphic to
X
are isomorphic by a map in the solving set. At this time, Condition (2) serves no obvious purpose. We will point out explicitly where this condition is used in the proof of Lemma
3.4
and thus the reason for its inclusion in the definition. By Lemma
2.7
, Condition (3) ensures that the solving set contains all elements of
N_{S_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times G}}(\widehat{G}_{L})
that must be checked for isomorphisms.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
G
be a finite group, and
X
a Haar object of
G
in a class
{\mathcal{K}}
of combinatorial objects. We define an
ABCI-extension of
G
with respect to
X
, denoted
{\rm ABCI}(G,X)
, to be a set of permutations in
S_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times G}
that satisfies the following properties:

(1)

{\rm ABCI}(G,X)
contains the identity permutation in
S_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times G}
,

(2)

if
t\in{\rm ABCI}(G,X)
and
t(0,1_{G})\in B_{0}
, then
t(0,1_{G})=(0,1_{G})
, and

(3)

whenever
\phi\in S_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times G}
such that
\phi^{-1}\widehat{G}_{L}\phi\leq{\rm Aut}(X)
, then there exists
v\in{\rm Aut}(X)
such that
v^{-1}\phi^{-1}\widehat{G}_{L}\phi v=t^{-1}\widehat{G}_{L}t
for some
t\in{\rm ABCI}(G,X)
.

In the case where
X
is a Haar graph (i.e.
X={\rm Haar}(G,S)
), we will write
{\rm ABCI}(G,S)
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
G
be a finite group, and
X
a Haar object of
G
in some
class
{\mathcal{K}}
of combinatorial objects. Then an
{\rm ABCI}(G,X)
exists.

Proof.

To show existence, we only need show that there is a set of permutations
T
in
S_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times G}
that contains the identity such that whenever
\phi\in S_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times G}
such that
\phi^{-1}\widehat{G}_{L}\phi\leq{\rm Aut}(X)
and
v\in{\rm Aut}(X)
, then
v^{-1}\phi^{-1}\widehat{G}_{L}\phi v=t^{-1}\widehat{G}_{L}t
for some
t\in T
and
t(0,1_{G})=(0,1_{G})
or
t(B_{0})=B_{1}
. This follows almost immediately. If
\phi v(0,1_{g})\not\in B_{0}
, let
t_{\phi v}=\phi v
. If
\phi v(0,1_{g})\in B_{0}
, then there exists
g\in G
such that
\phi v(0,1_{G})=(0,g)
. Let
t_{\phi v}=\widehat{g}_{L}^{-1}\phi v
. Then
t_{\phi v}(0,1_{G})=\widehat{g}_{L}^{-1}\phi v(0,1_{G})=\widehat{g}_{L}^{-1}(0,g)=(0,g^{-1}g)=(0,1_{G})
, and existence is established with

.
∎

Note that for
X
a Haar object of
G
in
{\mathcal{K}}
, an
{\rm ABCI}(G,X)
is not unique as if
T
is an ABCI-extension of

with respect to

, then for

is also an ABCI-extension of

with respect to

. The following result shows the importance of

, as if

is known, then the isomorphism problem is solved for

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
G
be a finite group, and
X
a Haar object of
G
in some class
{\mathcal{K}}
of combinatorial objects. Let
T\subseteq S_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times G}
that contains the identity in
S_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times G}
and if
t\in T
such that
t(0,1_{G})\in B_{0}
, then
t(0,1_{G})=(0,1_{G})
. The following are equivalent:

(1)

S=\{\alpha t:\alpha\in{\rm Iso}(G),t\in T\}
is a solving set for
X
,

(2)

T
is an
{\rm ABCI}(G,X)
.

Proof.

(1) implies (2). Let
\phi\in S_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times G}
such that

\phi^{-1}\widehat{G}_{L}\phi\leq{\rm Aut}(X)
. Then
\phi(X)
is a Haar
object of
G
in
{\mathcal{K}}
as
{\rm Aut}(\phi(X))=\phi{\rm Aut}(X)\phi^{-1}\geq\widehat{G}_{L}
. As
S
is a solving set for
X
,
\phi(X)=s(X)
for some
s\in S
, and
s=\alpha t
for some
\alpha\in{\rm Iso}(G)
and
t\in T
. Thus
v=\phi^{-1}s\in{\rm Aut}(X)
. Then

\displaystyle v^{-1}\phi^{-1}\widehat{G}_{L}\phi v

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s^{-1}\phi\phi^{-1}\widehat{G}_{L}\phi\phi^{-1}s

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s^{-1}\widehat{G}_{L}s=t^{-1}\alpha^{-1}\widehat{G}_{L}\alpha t

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle t^{-1}\widehat{G}_{L}t

and
T
is an
{\rm ABCI}(X,G)
.

(2) implies (1). Let
X
and
X^{\prime}
be isomorphic Haar objects of
G
in
{\mathcal{K}}
. Then there exists
\phi\in S_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times G}
such that
\phi(X)=X^{\prime}
. As
\widehat{G}_{L}\leq{\rm Aut}(X^{\prime})
,
\phi^{-1}\widehat{G}_{L}\phi\leq{\rm Aut}(X)
. As
T
is an
{\rm ABCI}(G,X)
, there exists
t\in T
and
v\in{\rm Aut}(X)
such that
v^{-1}\phi^{-1}\widehat{G}_{L}\phi v=t^{-1}\widehat{G}_{L}t
. Hence
tv^{-1}\phi^{-1}\widehat{G}_{L}\phi vt^{-1}=\widehat{G}_{L}
. Then
\phi vt^{-1}
normalizes
\widehat{G}_{L}
, and so by Lemma
2.1
, we have
\phi vt^{-1}=\tau^{i}\widehat{a}\widetilde{g}\bar{h}
for
i\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
,
a\in{\rm Aut}(G)
, and
g,h\in G
. Set
A={\rm Aut}(G)
, and
\gamma=\tau^{i}\widehat{a}\widetilde{g}\bar{h}
. Then
\phi v=\gamma t
and
\phi v(X)=\phi(X)=X^{\prime}
. Recall that
\widehat{A}\cdot(\widetilde{G}\bar{G})
contains
\widehat{G}_{L}
. As
\widehat{a}\widetilde{g}(B_{0})=B_{0}
, there exists
k\in G
such that
\widehat{k}_{L}\widehat{a}\widetilde{g}(0,1_{G})=(0,1_{G})
. As
\widehat{k}_{L}\tau^{i}=\tau^{i}\widehat{k}_{L}
, we see that
\widehat{k}_{L}\gamma=\widehat{k}_{L}\tau^{i}\widehat{a}\widetilde{g}\bar{h}=\tau^{i}\widehat{k}_{L}\widehat{a}\widetilde{g}\bar{h}
and
\widehat{k}_{L}\widehat{a}\widetilde{g}\bar{h}(0,1_{G})=\widehat{k}_{L}\widehat{a}\widetilde{g}(0,1_{G})=(0,1_{G})
. By Lemma
2.1
, we may rewrite
\tau^{i}\widehat{k}_{L}\widehat{a}\widetilde{g}\bar{h}
as an element
\tau^{i}\widehat{b}\widetilde{\ell}\bar{m}
, where
b\in A
and
\ell,m\in G
. We see that
\widehat{b}\widetilde{\ell}\bar{m}(0,1_{G})=(0,1_{G})
, and as
\widehat{b}
and
\bar{m}
both fix
(0,1_{G})
, we deduce that
\ell=1
. Thus the inclusion of the condition that if

and
s(0,1_{G})\in B_{0}
then
s(0,1_{G})=(0,1_{G})
in Definition
3.1
allows us to eliminate any map of the form

from the maps to be considered for isomorphism. This is the reason for the inclusion of (2) in Definition
3.1
. Also,
\tau^{i}\widehat{b}\bar{m}\in{\rm Iso}(G)
and maps
(0,1_{G})
to
(0,1_{G})
if

or, if

B_{0}
to
B_{1}
. Finally,
\widehat{k}_{L}\gamma t(X)=\widehat{k}_{L}(X^{\prime})=X^{\prime}
and
\widehat{k}_{L}\gamma=\tau^{i}\widehat{b}\bar{m}\in{\rm Iso}(G)
. The result follows with
\alpha=\widehat{k}_{L}\gamma
.
∎

Isomorphisms of Haar graphs that are wreath products are straightforward once one is aware of certain examples. Let

be an odd prime. In
[

an example is given of a Cayley digraph of

with blocks of size

and the other is

that are contained in the automorphism group of the quotient graph, not just the one with quotient

We now also restrict our attention to Haar graphs of abelian groups. There are two reasons for this. First, our main applications in the next section apply only to Haar graphs of abelian groups. Second, and more importantly, we will need to consider block quotient digraphs, and the block quotient digraph of a Haar graph of a nonabelian group

Let

.

We now turn to automorphisms of disconnected vertex-transitive Haar graphs. It is somewhat ironic that determining isomorphism between these graphs will take the most work of the three cases of Theorem
4.5
that we will consider, as of course isomorphism between disconnected graphs obviously reduces to isomorphism between components.

The automorphism group of a disconnected vertex-transitive Haar graph of a group
G
always has an imprimitive automorphism group with blocks the set of components of the graph, which are the left cosets of
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times H
for some
H\leq G
. As the graph is a wreath product, the “natural” group to consider is
(G/H)\times({\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times H)
, as this is the set permuted by the wreath product. However, the group
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\ltimes\widehat{G}_{L}
need not be such a direct product, so in the rest of this section we will consider the vertex set to be
(G/H)\times({\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times H)
, but we will consider
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\ltimes\widehat{G}_{L}
in its natural embedding in
S_{G/H}\wr({\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\ltimes S_{H})
given by the Embedding Theorem
[
16
, Theorem 4.3.1]
, which contains a natural subgroup isomorphic to
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times G
by
[
16
, Theorem 4.3.5]
. We begin with a couple of preliminary results, the first of which “aligns” the bipartition into a nice form.

Lemma 4.21
.

Let
A
be an abelian group, and
\emptyset\not=S\subset A
such that
{\rm Haar}(A,S)
is disconnected. Let
C
be the component of
{\rm Haar}(A,S)
that contains
(0,0)
, and let
a\in A
such that
(1,-a)\in V(C)
. Then
H={\langle}(a+S)(a+S)^{-1}{\rangle}
is a subgroup of
A
, and the components of
{\rm Haar}(A,a+S)
are the left cosets of
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times H
in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times A
. Consequently,
{\rm Haar}(A,a+S)=\bar{K}_{m}\wr{\rm Haar}(H,a+S)
where
m=|A/H|
.

Proof.

Let
\Gamma={\rm Haar}(A,S)
. As
S\not=\emptyset
,
\Gamma
has edges. Then each component of
\Gamma
is bipartite, and its edges must have one endpoint in
B_{0}
and one in
B_{1}
. This implies that the set of edge endpoints in
V(C)\cap B_{0}
and the set of edge endpoints in
V(C)\cap B_{1}
is a bipartition of
C
. As
\widehat{A}_{L}
is transitive on
B_{0}
and
B_{1}
, the set of vertices in a component contained in
B_{i}
is a block system of
(\widehat{A}_{L})^{B_{i}}
, and so is the set
{\mathcal{C}}_{i}
,
i\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
, of cosets of some subgroup
H_{i}\leq A
by
[
16
, Example 2.3.8]
. As
A
is abelian,
{\rm Aut}({\rm Haar}(A,S))
contains the map
\iota:{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times A\to{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times A
given by
\iota(i,j)=(i+1,-j)
, and also interchanges
H_{0}
and
H_{1}
. Thus
-H_{1}=H_{0}
and
H_{1}=H_{0}
. In
\bar{a}(\Gamma)={\rm Haar}(A,a+S)
, the component that contains
(0,0)
also contains
(1,0)
, and so has vertex set
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times H
, where
H=H_{0}=H_{1}
. As
\widehat{A}_{L}\leq{\rm Aut}({\rm Haar}(A,a+S))
, the components of
{\rm Haar}(A,a+S)
have vertex sets the left cosets of
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times H
in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times A
. Finally, as the component of
{\rm Haar}(A,a+S)
that contains
(0,0)
is connected and is
{\rm Haar}(H,a+S)
, by
[
17
, Lemma 2.3 (iii)]
we have
H={\langle}(a+S)(a+S)^{-1}{\rangle}
. Thus
{\rm Haar}(A,a+S)=\bar{K}_{m}\wr{\rm Haar}(H,a+S)
.
∎

Once the components have been “aligned” as in the previous result, the isomorphism problem reduces to the isomorphism problem for the components.

Lemma 4.22
.

Let
A
be an abelian group, and
\emptyset\not=S\subset A
such that
{\rm Haar}(A,S)
is disconnected with
H\leq A
such that
{\rm Haar}(H,S)
is a component of
{\rm Haar}(A,S)
. Let
T\subseteq A
such that
{\rm Haar}(A,T)
is a disconnected Haar graph of
A
with component
{\rm Haar}(H,T)
. If
{\rm Haar}(A,S)
and
{\rm Haar}(A,T)
are isomorphic, then they are isomorphic by an element of
1_{S_{A/H}}\times{\mathcal{S}}
, where
{\mathcal{S}}
is a solving set for
{\rm Haar}(H,S)
.

Proof.

Let
\Gamma={\rm Haar}(A,S)
. As
{\rm Haar}(H,S)
is a component of
\Gamma
,
{\rm Haar}(H,S)
is connected and as
S\not=\emptyset
,
{\rm Haar}(H,S)
has edges. Also,
V({\rm Haar}(H,S))
contains
(0,0)
and
(1,0)
. Recall that we are considering
V(\Gamma)\leq A/H\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times H
. By Lemma
4.21
, the vertex sets of the components of
\Gamma
are then the left cosets of
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times H
in
A/H\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times H
.

Let
\phi\in S_{A/H\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times H}
such that
\phi(\Gamma)={\rm Haar}(A,T)
.
Then
\phi^{-1}\widehat{A}_{L}\phi\leq{\rm Aut}(\Gamma)
and the components of
\phi(\Gamma)
are the left cosets of
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times H
in
A/H\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times H
. Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be the set of components of
\Gamma
. Then
{\mathcal{C}}
is a block system of
{\rm Aut}(\Gamma)
and, as the components of
\Gamma
and
\phi(\Gamma)
are the same,
{\mathcal{C}}
is also a block system of
{\rm Aut}(\phi(\Gamma))
. Hence
\phi\in S_{A/H}\wr S_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times H}
. As
{\rm Aut}(\Gamma)=S_{m}\wr{\rm Aut}({\rm Haar}(H,S))
where
m=|A/H|
, we may assume without loss of generality that
\phi/{\mathcal{C}}=1
, so that
\phi(x,y,z)=(x,\phi_{x}(y,z))
, where each
\phi_{x}\in S_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times H}
and
x\in A/H
.

As
{\mathcal{S}}
is a solving set for
{\rm Haar}(H,S)
, there is
s\in{\mathcal{S}}
such that
s({\rm Haar}(H,S))={\rm Haar}(H,T)
. Define

by

. Then

. As
\widehat{H}_{L}\leq s{\rm Aut}({\rm Haar}(H,S))s^{-1}\leq{\rm Aut}({\rm Haar}(H,T))
and
{\rm Aut}(\phi(\Gamma))=S_{m}\wr{\rm Aut}({\rm Haar}(H,T))

we see that
\widehat{A}_{L}/{\mathcal{C}}\wr({\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times H_{L})\geq\widehat{A}_{L}
and
s_{A}(\Gamma)
is a Haar graph of

. As

,
s_{A}(\Gamma)={\rm Haar}(A,T)
. As

∎

The next example gives a sufficient condition for a group
G
to not be a BCI-group.

Example 4.23
.

Let
G
be a group, and
H_{1},H_{2}<G
such that
|H_{1}|=|H_{2}|
and there exists no
\alpha\in{\rm Aut}(G)
such that
\alpha(H_{1})=H_{2}
. Then
{\rm Haar}(G,H_{1})\cong{\rm Haar}(G,H_{2})
, but there is no element of
{\rm Iso}(G)
that maps
{\rm Haar}(G,H_{1})
to
{\rm Haar}(G,H_{2})
, nor is there an element of
{\rm Iso}(H_{1})
that maps the component of
{\rm Haar}(G,H_{1})
that contains
(0,0)
to the component of
{\rm Haar}(G,H_{2})
that contains
(0,0)
.

Proof.

The Haar graphs
{\rm Haar}(G,H_{1})
and
{\rm Haar}(G,H_{2})
are disconnected by
[
17
, Lemma 2.3 (iii)]
and each component of each graph is isomorphic to
K_{m,m}
, where
m=|H_{1}|=|H_{2}|
. Hence
{\rm Haar}(G,H_{1})
and
{\rm Haar}(G,H_{2})
are isomorphic. Note that
\tau
(as in Lemma
2.1
) and every element of
\bar{G}
maps
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times(G/H_{1})
to itself. As there is no automorphism of
G
that maps
H_{1}
to
H_{2}
, by Lemma
2.7
, no element of
{\rm Iso}(G)
maps
{\rm Haar}(G,H_{1})
to
{\rm Haar}(G,H_{2})
. Note that the component of
{\rm Haar}(G,H_{1})
that contains
(0,1_{G})
has vertex set
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times H_{1}
, while the component of
{\rm Haar}(G,H_{2})
that contains
(0,1_{G})
has vertex set
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times H_{2}
. As every element of
{\rm Iso}(H_{1})
is a permutation of
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times H_{1}
, the result follows.
∎

Corollary 4.24
.

{\rm ABCI}

H\leq A
is an ABCI-graph of
A
.

Proof.

Let
\Gamma={\rm Haar}(A,S)
, and
C
be the component of
\Gamma
that contains
(0,0)
. Let
(1,-a)\in V(C)
for some
a\in A
. In
\bar{a}(\Gamma)={\rm Haar}(A,a+S)
, the component
\bar{a}(C)
that contains
(0,0)
also contains
(1,0)
. By Lemma
4.21
, we see that
{\rm Haar}(A,a+S)=\bar{K}_{m}\wr{\rm Haar}(H,a+S)
where
H={\langle}(a+S)(a+S)^{-1}{\rangle}
. Here,
m
is the number of components of
{\rm Haar}(A,a+S)
, and
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times H=V(\bar{a}(C))
. Also, the set of components of
{\rm Haar}(A,a+S)
is the set of cosets of
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times H
in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times A
as
\bar{a}(C)
contains both
(0,0)
and
(1,0)
. By Lemma
4.22
, an
{\rm ABCI}(A,a+S)
is
1_{S_{A/H}}\times{\rm ABCI}(H,a+S)
. As
a\in C
and
\bar{a}\in{\rm Iso}(A,a+S)\cap{\rm Iso}(A,S)
, we may assume without loss of generality that
a=0
. As by hypothesis
C
is an
{\rm ABCI}
-graph of
H
, by Lemma
2.8
, we see that
{\rm Haar}(H,S)
is isomorphic to another Haar graph of
H
if and only if they are isomorphic by a map of the form
\widehat{\beta}\bar{h}
, where
\beta\in{\rm Aut}(H)
and
h\in H
. As an abelian group is homogeneous if and only if every Sylow subgroup is homocyclic
[
31
]
, the automorphism
\beta\in{\rm Aut}(H)
extends to an automorphism
\alpha_{\beta}\in{\rm Aut}(A)
. Of course,
h\in A
, so the map
\bar{h}
with domain
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times H
extends to the map
\bar{h}
with domain
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times A
. Now suppose
T\subset A
such that
{\rm Haar}(A,S)\cong{\rm Haar}(A,T)
. Then there exists
b\in A
such that the component of
\overline{b}({\rm Haar}(A,T))
that contains
(0,0)
also contains
(1,0)
. As above, we may assume without loss of generality that
b=0
. Then
\widehat{\alpha}\bar{h}
maps
C
to the component of
{\rm Haar}(A,T)
that contains
(0,0)
and
(1,0)
. This implies that the components of
{\rm Haar}(A,S)
and
{\rm Haar}(A,T)
are the same, and so
\widehat{\alpha_{\beta}}\bar{h}({\rm Haar}(A,S))={\rm Haar}(A,T)
.
∎

Applications

First, Babai has shown that the dihedral group
D_{p}
of order
2p
is a CI-group with respect to ternary relational structures
[
1
, Theorem 4.4]
, and so for binary relational structures, and so for graphs. Also, the automorphism group of every Haar graph of a cyclic group contains a regular dihedral subgroup, and so is also a Cayley graph of a dihedral group by
[
40
, Lemma 4]
or
[
16
, Theorem 1.2.20]
. This shows that
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
is an ABCI-group, as it is easy to check that all automorphisms of
D_{p}
are contained in
{\rm Iso}({\mathbb{Z}}_{p})
. The solution to the isomorphism problem for Cayley digraphs of some other dihedral groups
D_{n}
for special
n
was given in
[
8
, Theorem 22]
. Similarly, automorphism groups of every Cayley digraph of the dihedral group
D_{p}
of order
2p
are given in
[
9
, Theorem 3.2]
, and so all automorphism groups of all Haar graphs of the group
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
are given there as well.

Definition 5.1
.

The
canonical double cover of a graph
\Gamma
is the bipartite graph
B\Gamma
with
V(B\Gamma)={\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times V(\Gamma)
with edge set
\{(0,v)(1,w):vw\in E(\Gamma)\}
.

If
\Gamma={\rm Haar}(G,S)
, then
(0,g)(1,sg)\in E(\Gamma)
if and only if
s\in G
, and so if
{\rm Cay}(G,S)
is a graph,
{\rm Haar}(G,S)
is the canonical double cover of
{\rm Cay}(G,S)
.

There has been much interest in graphs
\Gamma
for which the canonical double cover of a graph has automorphism group
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times{\rm Aut}(\Gamma)

[
33
,
44
,
39
,
24
,
24
,
19
,
25
,
23
]
. It is straightforward to show that bipartite graphs, graphs with twins, and disconnected graphs do not have automorphism groups
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times{\rm Aut}(\Gamma)
. This has led to the following definition.

Definition 5.2
.

Let
\Gamma
be a graph. We say that
\Gamma
is
unstable
if
{\rm Aut}(B\Gamma)\not={\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times{\rm Aut}(\Gamma)
. We call
\Gamma

trivially unstable
if
\Gamma
is connected, not bipartite, twin-free, and unstable.

The authors are unaware of an analogue of the canonical double cover for digraphs. However, as Haar graphs can be constructed using connection sets that give Cayley digraphs that are not graphs, we can think of Haar graphs with connection sets that give Cayley digraphs that are not graphs as such analogues. As the idea behind a stable graph is that its automorphism group gives the automorphism group of its canonical double cover, Theorem
4.5
motivates the following definition.

Definition 5.3
.

Let
A
be an abelian group and
S\subseteq G
. We say that
{\rm Cay}(A,S)
is
unstable
if

{\rm Aut}(\Gamma)\not=\bar{a}^{-1}{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\ltimes{\rm Aut}({\rm Cay}(A,a+S))\bar{a}

for some
a\in A
. We say that
{\rm Cay}(A,S)
is
nontrivially unstable
if it is connected, not biparite, twin-free, and unstable.

We remark that we are only defining nonstable and nontrivially unstable for Cayley digraphs of abelian groups as it is not clear what the appropriate definition should be for nonabelian groups as at this time there is no analogue of Theorem
4.5
for nonabelian groups, and the appropriate definition for nonabelian groups should be based on such a result.

Problem 5.4
.

Classify all nontrivially unstable Cayley digraphs of abelian groups.

The next result shows that the previous definition of a nontrivially unstable Cayley graph is the same in both definitions above. This is a special case of Dave Morris’s Theorem
5.7
stated below. We give a self-contained proof as it is not difficult.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
A
be an abelian group of odd order and
S\subseteq A
such that
S=-S
and

{\rm Aut}({\rm Haar}(A,S))=\bar{a}^{-1}{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\ltimes{\rm Aut}({\rm Cay}(A,S))\bar{a}

for some
a\in A
. Then
a=0
and
{\rm Aut}({\rm Haar}(A,S))={\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times{\rm Aut}({\rm Cay}(A,S))
.

Proof.

As
S=-S
,
{\rm Cay}(A,S)
is a graph, and so contains the map
\iota:A\to A
by
\iota(a)=-a
. The map
\widehat{\iota}
is then contained in
{\rm Aut}({\rm Haar}(A,S))
by
[
15
, Lemma 1.4]
. Then
\widehat{\iota}
and
\bar{a}^{-1}\widehat{\iota}\bar{a}
are contained in
{\rm Aut}({\rm Haar}(A,S))
and
\widehat{\iota}\bar{a}^{-1}\widehat{\iota}\bar{a}(0,j)=(0,j)
while
\widehat{\iota}\bar{a}^{-1}\widehat{\iota}\bar{a}(1,j)=(1,j+2a)
. As
A
has odd order,
2a
is not the identity if
a\not=0
. Suppose
a\not=0
. Note that as
{\rm Aut}({\rm Haar}(A,S))=\bar{a}^{-1}{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\ltimes{\rm Aut}({\rm Cay}(A,S))\bar{a}
,
{\rm Haar}(A,S)
is connected, so
{\mathcal{B}}
is a block system of
{\rm Aut}({\rm Haar}(A,S))
. Then the action of
\widehat{\iota}\bar{a}^{-1}\widehat{\iota}\bar{a}
on
B_{0}\in{\mathcal{B}}
with
(0,0)\in B_{0}
is not faithful. However, by
[
15
, Theorem 2.8]
we see that
{\rm Aut}({\rm Haar}(A,S))
is a nontrivial wreath product, a contradiction. Thus
a=0
.

Finally, we observe that the map
\tau:{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times A\to{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times A
given by
\tau(i,j)=(i+1,-j)
is always contained in
{\rm Aut}({\rm Haar}(A,S))
(this was implicitly shown in
[
22
]
). Then
\tau\bar{\iota}(i,j)=(i+1,j)
is contained in
{\rm Aut}({\rm Haar}(A,S))
. This then implies that
{\rm Aut}({\rm Haar}(A,S))={\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times{\rm Aut}({\rm Cay}(A,S))
, and the result follows.
∎

The next result gives a sufficient condition for the isomorphism problem for Haar graphs of an abelian group
A
to reduce to the isomorphism problem for either “smaller” Haar graphs obtained as a component or quotient, or to the isomorphism problem for its corresponding Cayley graph.

Theorem 5.6
.

Let
A
be an abelian group, and
S\subseteq A
. If
{\rm Cay}(A,S)
is not nontrivally unstable, then the isomorphism problem for
\Gamma={\rm Haar}(A,S)
can be determined from the solution to the isomorphism problem for a nontrivial block quotient graph of
\Gamma
, a component of
\bar{a}(\Gamma)
, where
(1,-a)
is in the same component of
\Gamma
as
(0,0)
, or the isomorphism problem for
{\rm Cay}(A,a+S)
, where
a\in A
.

Proof.

We consider the four cases given by Theorem
2.8
separately. Suppose
\Gamma
is disconnected and let
{\rm Haar}(A,T)\cong\Gamma
. Let
(1,-a)
be in the component
C
of
{\rm Haar}(A,T)
that contains
(0,0)
. By Corollary
4.25
, the isomorphism problem for
\Gamma
reduces to the isomorphism problem for
\bar{a}(C)
, a subgraph of
\bar{\Gamma}
. So we assume without loss of generality that
\Gamma
is connected. If
\Gamma
is a nontrivial wreath product with an empty graph, then there exists
1\not=C\leq A
, chosen to be maximal, such that
{\rm Haar}(A,S)\cong{\rm Haar}(D,U)\wr\bar{K}_{C}
, where
D=A/C
, and
U
is a union of left cosets of
C
in
A
. Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be the left cosets of
C
in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times A
. Let
{\mathcal{L}}
be the set of all quotient groups of
A
that are contained in
{\rm Aut}({\rm Haar}(D,U))
. For each
L\in{\mathcal{L}}
, let
\delta_{L}:L\to D
be an
L
to
D
relabeling of
{\rm Haar}(D,U)
. Let
{\mathcal{S}}
be a solving set for
{\rm Haar}(D,U)
, and
T=\{s\delta_{L}:s\in{\mathcal{S}},L\in{\mathcal{L}}\}
. By Lemma
4.19
a solving set for
\Gamma
is

\{t_{A}:t_{A}{\rm\ is\ any\ extension\ of\ }t{\rm\ to\ }{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times A{\rm\ and\ }t\in T\}.

Thus the isomorphism problem for
\Gamma
can be determined from the solution to the isomorphism problem for a nontrivial block quotient graph of
\Gamma
. By Theorem
4.5
, the only remaining possibility is that
{\rm Aut}(\Gamma)\cong\bar{a}^{-1}{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\ltimes{\rm Aut}({\rm Cay}(A,a+S))\bar{a}
for some
a\in A
, and by Lemma
4.10
, an
{\rm ABCI}(A,a+S)
is
\widehat{T}\bar{a}
, where
T
is a
{\rm CI}(G,a+S)
. Then the isomorphism problem for
\Gamma
can be determined from the solution to the isomorphism problem for
{\rm Cay}(A,a+S)
.
∎

Note that for the smaller Haar graphs of, say, the group
B
obtained as a quotient or subgroup of
A
for some
{\rm Haar}(A,S)
, we do NOT mean the isomorphism problem among Haar graphs of only the group
B
as in both cases the isomorphic image may be a Haar graph of some other group, say
C
, under an isomorphism. Thus we mean that it reduces the problem to the isomorphism problem among all Haar graphs isomorphic to a Haar graph of
B
. To correct this problem, one would need to know the maps that are
C
to
B
relabelings. But we remind the reader that all such relabelings are isomorphisms if every Sylow subgroup of
A
is elementary abelian or cyclic, and so for such
A
we reduce to the isomorphism problem for Haar graphs of the group
B
.

In the case where the isomorphism problem for
{\rm Haar}(A,S)
reduces to the isomorphism problem for
{\rm Cay}(A,a+S)
in Theorem
5.6
, the value of
a
is given in
[
15
, Theorem 2.12]
.

Corollary 5.8

While Theorem

in a very real sense solves the isomorphism problem for Haar graphs of abelian groups of odd order whose connection set is self-inverse, it does not reduce the ABCI problem to the CI problem. There are two obstacles to this, with the obstacles coming from graphs that have twins or are disconnected. What can now be done is the next result.

Corollary 5.10
.

Let
A
be an abelian group of odd order such that every Sylow subgroup of
A
is elementary abelian or cyclic. Let
S\subseteq A
such that
S=-S
and
{\rm Cay}(A,S)
is a CI-graph. Then
{\rm Haar}(A,S)
is an ABCI-graph.

Proof.

By Theorem
5.7
we need only consider when
{\rm Haar}(A,S)
has automorphism group
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times{\rm Aut}({\rm Cay}(A,S))
,
{\rm Cay}(A,S)
has a twin, or
{\rm Cay}(A,S)
is disconnected. If
{\rm Aut}({\rm Haar}(A,S))={\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times{\rm Aut}({\rm Cay}(A,S))
and
{\rm Cay}(A,S)
is a CI-graph, then by Lemma
4.11
we have that
{\rm Haar}(A,S)
is an
{\rm ABCI}
-graph.

If
{\rm Cay}(A,S)
is connected and has a twin, then
{\rm Haar}(A,S)
has a twin and is connected as
|A|
is odd (and so
{\rm Cay}(A,S)
is not bipartite). Thus
{\rm Haar}(A,S)
satisfies Theorem
4.5
(2). Then there exists
H\leq A
, chosen to be maximal, such that
{\rm Haar}(A,S)\cong{\rm Haar}(D,U)\wr\bar{K}_{H}
, where
D=A/H
, and
U
is a union of left cosets of
H
in
A
. As
H
is maximal,
{\rm Haar}(D,U)
has no twins, and as it is the block quotient of a connected graph, it is connected. As
A
has odd order,
D
has odd order, and we see by Theorem
5.7
that
{\rm Aut}({\rm Haar}(D,U))={\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times{\rm Aut}({\rm Cay}(D,U))
, and
{\rm Cay}(D,U)
is not unstable. As by
[
13
]
the quotient of a CI-graph is a CI-graph, we see that
{\rm Cay}(D,U)
is a CI-graph. Hence a
{\rm CI}(D,U)
is
{\rm Aut}(D)
. By Lemma
4.20
, an
{\rm ABCI}(D,U)
is
T=\widehat{{\rm Aut}(D)}
. As every Sylow subgroup of
A
is elementary abelian or cyclic, we may choose an
{\rm ABCI}(A,S)
to be
T_{A}\leq{\rm Aut}(A)
. Then
{\rm ABCI}(A,S)\leq\widehat{{\rm Aut}(A)}\leq{\rm Iso}(A,S)
, and
{\rm Haar}(A,S)
is an ABCI-graph by definition.

If
{\rm Cay}(A,S)
is disconnected, by Corollary
4.26
an
{\rm ABCI}(A,S)
is
((1\times A/H)\times{\rm ABCI}(H,a+S))\overline{a}
, where
(1,-a)
is in the component of
{\rm Haar}(A,S)
that contains
(0,0)
. By Lemma
5.5
, we have
a=0
and so
\overline{a}=1
. Hence an
{\rm ABCI}(A,S)
is
(1\times A/H)\times{\rm ABCI}(H,S)
. If
{\rm Haar}(H,S)
is an
{\rm ABCI}
-graph, then as
A
is homogeneous, the result follows by Corollary
4.28
. If
{\rm Haar}(H,S)
does not have a twin, then by arguments in the first paragraph, we have that
{\rm Haar}(H,S)
is an ABCI-graph. If
{\rm Haar}(H,S)
has a twin, then we showed in the previous paragraph that a connected Haar graph of
H
of a CI-graph with a twin is an
{\rm ABCI}
-graph. Thus in all cases where
{\rm Cay}(A,S)
is disconnected, we have that
{\rm Haar}(A,S)
is an
{\rm ABCI}
-graph.
∎

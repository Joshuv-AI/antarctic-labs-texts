Lingyu Guo, Victor Zhenyu Guo, Li Lu

Abstract
. The Piatetski-Shapiro sequences are of the form 𝒩 c := ( ⌊ n c ⌋ ) n = 1 ∞ \mathcal{N}_{c}:=(\lfloor n^{c}\rfloor)_{n=1}^{\infty} , where ⌊ ⋅ ⌋ \lfloor\cdot\rfloor is the integer part. 1.
Introduction

The Piatetski-Shapiro sequences are sequences of the form

\mathcal{N}_{c}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=(\lfloor n^{c}\rfloor)_{n=1}^{\infty},

where
\left\lfloor\cdot\right\rfloor
is the integer part. Piatetski-Shapiro
[
15
]
proved the Piatetski-Shapiro prime number theorem stating that for
1<c<12/11
the counting function

\pi_{c}(x)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\#\big\{\text{\rm prime~}p\leqslant x:p\in\mathcal{N}_{c}\big\}

satisfies the asymptotic relation

(1.1)

\pi_{c}(x)=(1+o(1))\frac{x^{1/c}}{\log x}\qquad\text{~\rm as }x\to\infty.

The asymptotic relation is expected to hold for all values of
1<c<2
and was proved by Leitmann and Wolke
[
13
]
for almost all
c\in(1,2)
in the sense of Lebesgue measure. The estimation of Piatetski-Shapiro primes is an approximation of the well-known conjecture that there exist infinitely many primes of the form
n^{2}+1
.

.

For

\mathfrak{T}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\sum_{h\sim H}\sum_{m\sim M}\sum_{n\sim N}a_{h}b_{m}\mathbf{e}\bigg(X\frac{h^{\alpha}m^{\beta}n^{\gamma}}{H^{\alpha}M^{\beta}N^{\gamma}}\bigg)

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Notation

We denote by
\left\lfloor t\right\rfloor
and
\{t\}
the integer part and the fractional part of
t
, respectively. We define
||t||\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\min\{|t-n|:n\in\mathbb{Z}\}
. As is customary, we put
\mathbf{e}(t)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=e^{2\pi it}
. We make considerable use of the sawtooth function defined by

\psi(t)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=t-\left\lfloor t\right\rfloor-\frac{1}{2}=\{t\}-\frac{1}{2}\qquad(t\in\mathbb{R}).

The letter
p
always denotes a prime. For the Piatetski-Shapiro sequence
(\left\lfloor n^{c}\right\rfloor)_{n=1}^{\infty}
, we denote
\gamma\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=c^{-1}
. We use notation of the form
m\sim M
as an abbreviation for
M<m\leqslant 2M
.
\varepsilon
is always a sufficiently small positive number.

Throughout the paper, implied constants in symbols
O
,
\ll
and
\gg
may depend (where obvious) on the parameters
c,\varepsilon
but are absolute otherwise. For given functions
F
and
G
, the notations
F\ll G
,
G\gg F
and
F=O(G)
are all equivalent to the statement that the inequality
|F|\leqslant C|G|
holds with some constant
C>0
.
F\asymp G
means that
F\ll G\ll F
.

3.
Ideas of the proof

The key point to prove the Piatetski-Shapiro prime number theorem is to estimate the admissible range of
\gamma
of the relation

\mathcal{S}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\sum_{h\sim H}\delta_{h}\sum_{n\sim x}\Lambda(n)\mathbf{e}(hn^{\gamma})\ll x^{1-\varepsilon}

where
\gamma\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=1/c
. By a decomposition of von Mangoldt function, we mainly need to bound the sum

(3.1)

\sum_{h\sim H}\delta_{h}\sum_{m\sim M}\sum_{n\sim N}a_{m}b_{n}\mathbf{e}(h(mn)^{\gamma}),

where
0<\gamma<1
,
MN\asymp x
,
H\leqslant x^{1-\gamma+\varepsilon}
and
\delta_{h}
,
a_{m}
,
b_{n}
are complex numbers of modulus at most
1
. If the coefficients
a_{m}
and
b_{n}
satisfy the conditions

(3.2)

|a_{m}|\ll 1,\quad b_{n}=1~\text{or}~\log n,

we denote the sum by
S_{I}
. If they satisfy the conditions

(3.3)

|a_{m}|\ll 1,\quad b_{n}\ll 1,

we denote the sum by
S_{II}
. The estimation of type
II
sum is usually the barrier to improve the range of
c
.

3.1.
Classical results

Heath-Brown
[
5
]
proved a remarkable type
II
bound.

Lemma 3.1
.

For

x^{1-\gamma+\varepsilon}\ll N\ll x^{5\gamma-4-\varepsilon},

we have
S_{II}\ll x^{1-\varepsilon}
.

Moreover, Heath-Brown also provided a new decomposition of von Mangoldt function, nowadays known as the Heath-Brown’s identity, which is the following lemma.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
3\leqslant U<V<Z<x
and suppose that
Z-\frac{1}{2}\in\mathbb{N}
,
x\geqslant 64Z^{2}U,~Z\geqslant 4U^{2},~V^{3}\geqslant 32x
. Assume further that
f(n)=0
when
n\leqslant x
or
n>2x
and that
|f(n)|\leqslant f_{0}
otherwise. Then the sum

\sum_{n\sim x}\Lambda(n)f(n)

may be decomposed into
O(\log^{10}x)
sums, each either of Type
I
with
N>Z
, or of Type
II
with
U<N<V
.

Liu and Rivat
[
14
]
applied the double large sieve method to type
I
sums together with the Heath-Brown’s identity, proving the Piatetski-Shapiro prime number theorem for
c
up to
15/13
. This reaches the limitation of the type
II
bound since by Lemma
3.2
it is easy to see that we requires
V\gg x^{1/3}
. The upper bound of Heath-Brown’s type
II
bound gives that
5\gamma-4>1/3
implies that
\gamma>13/15
.

To break the type
II
bound barrier, Rivat and Sargos
[
17
]
applied the same idea of Heath-Brown’s identity but gave a more suitable decomposition of
\Lambda(n)
for Piatetski-Shapiro prime numbers.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
l\geqslant 4
and
1/2l\leqslant\alpha\leqslant 1/6
. With the above notations, if

(i)

type
II
sum
\displaystyle\sum_{m\sim M}\sum_{n\sim N}a_{m}b_{n}f(mn)\ll U
for
x^{\alpha}\leqslant N\leqslant x^{2\alpha}
;

(ii)

type
I^{\prime}
sum
\displaystyle\sum_{m\sim M}\sum_{n\sim N}a_{m}f(mn)\ll U
for
x^{2\alpha}\leqslant N\leqslant x^{1/3}
;

(iii)

type
I
sum
\displaystyle\sum_{m\sim M}\sum_{n\sim N}a_{m}f(mn)\ll U
for
x^{(1-\alpha)/2}\leqslant N
;

then
\displaystyle\sum_{n\sim x}\Lambda(n)f(n)\ll Ux^{\varepsilon}
.

The new decomposition allows the range of
\gamma
to be
2(1-\gamma)<5\gamma-4
, which is
\gamma>6/7
from the type
II
bound. Therefore, it is sufficient to prove a better type
I^{\prime}
bound to improve the admissible range of Piatetski-Shapiro prime number theorem. With a new idea for type
I^{\prime}
sum, Rivat and Sargos
[
17
]
proved the best range of
c
at the time. We prove a better admissible range of
c
because our Theorem
1.2
gives a better estimation of type
I^{\prime}
sum.

3.2.
A comparison to Wu’s bound

Wu
[
23
]
also considered this type
I^{\prime}
sum. He applied a similar idea of Rivat and Sargos
[
17
]
, proving a more general theorem, not only for the exponential sums related to Piatetski-Shapiro primes, but also for more general choices of
H,M,N,X
. To make a comparison between Wu’s result and our Theorem
1.2
, we list Wu’s bound as the following. Let
k\geqslant 2
and
K\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=2^{k}
. If
X\leqslant\min\{H^{2},H^{2}M^{-1}N\}
, then

\displaystyle\mathfrak{T}\mathcal{L}^{-1}

\displaystyle\ll X^{\frac{K}{6K-2k-8}}H^{\frac{4K-2k-4}{6K-2k-8}}M^{\frac{5K-k-8}{6K-2k-8}}N^{\frac{5K-3k-8}{6K-2k-8}}+X^{\frac{1}{4}}H^{\frac{1}{2}}M^{\frac{1}{2}}N

(3.4)

\displaystyle\qquad+X^{\frac{1}{4}}H^{\frac{1}{2}}MN^{\frac{1}{2}}+HM+HM^{\frac{1}{2}}N^{\frac{1}{2}}+H^{\frac{1}{2}}MN+X^{-\frac{1}{2}}HMN.

Our theorem
1.2
refines (
3.4
). The idea of our proof is similar to Wu’s idea, but with a few important differences. A key idea is that we employ exponent pair method to the proof instead of higher derivative tests. A first glance shows that we replace the first term of
(
3.4
)
into a term with exponent pairs, but the secondary term of exponent pair bound also makes the estimations more complicate.

Another difference is that Wu applied a generalized B-process to (
4.1
) while we apply a normal B-process. As a consequence, Wu had a easier main term but much complicated error term, but we have simpler error terms. The readers may compare our equation
(
4.4
)
with equation (2.8) in
[
23
]
. It may happen that our error term is not as good as Wu when
X
is very small. However, since the proof assumes
X\geqslant MN
, this may rarely happen.

We mention that Wu ignored details in his proofs. For example when optimizing
Q
at the end of his proof, the range of
Q
should be
[Q_{0},MN]
instead of
[Q_{0},\infty)
. We completely believe that Wu’s result will not be affected by this calculation, but a few more negligible terms should be added as a rigorous proof. Moreover, we also remove the restriction of
X
for a more general theorem.

4.
Proof of Theorem 2

By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality we have

|\mathfrak{T}|^{2}\ll H\sum_{h\sim H}\bigg|\sum_{m\sim M}\sum_{n\sim N}b_{m}\mathbf{e}\bigg(X\frac{h^{\alpha}m^{\beta}n^{\gamma}}{H^{\alpha}M^{\beta}N^{\gamma}}\bigg)\bigg|^{2}.

Now let
Q\geqslant 10
. Applying Lemma
2.3
we obtain that

(4.1)

|\mathfrak{T}|^{2}\ll HQ\sum_{m\sim M}\sum_{\tilde{m}\sim M}\bigg|\mathop{\sum_{n\sim N}\sum_{\tilde{n}\sim N}}_{|u|\leqslant\Xi/Q}g(u)\sum_{h\sim H}\mathbf{e}\bigg(\frac{Xh^{\alpha}u}{H^{\alpha}M^{\beta}N^{\gamma}}\bigg)\bigg|,

where
u=u_{\mathbf{m}}(\mathbf{n})\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=m^{\beta}n^{\gamma}-\tilde{m}^{\beta}\tilde{n}^{\gamma},\mathbf{m}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=(m,\tilde{m}),\mathbf{n}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=(n,\tilde{n}),g(u)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=1-Q|u|/\Xi
and
\Xi\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=c_{1}M^{\beta}N^{\gamma}
.

First we consider the case when
X\leqslant MN
. The right-hand side of
(
4.1
)
is

\ll HQ\sum_{m\sim M}\sum_{\tilde{m}\sim M}\mathop{\sum_{n\sim N}\sum_{\tilde{n}\sim N}}_{|u|\leqslant\Xi/Q}\bigg|\sum_{h\sim H}\mathbf{e}\bigg(\frac{Xh^{\alpha}u}{H^{\alpha}M^{\beta}N^{\gamma}}\bigg)\bigg|.

Take
Q\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\max\{1,X/(\varepsilon H)\}
. By a trivial estimation and Lemma
2.6
, the contribution of the case
|u|\leqslant\Xi\mathcal{L}/(MN)
is

\ll HQ\,\mathfrak{A}(M,N;\frac{\mathcal{L}}{MN})H\ll\mathcal{L}H^{2}MN+\mathcal{L}XHMN.

From Lemma
2.2
, the contribution of the case
\Xi\mathcal{L}/(MN)\leqslant|u|\leqslant\Xi/Q
is

\ll HQ\mathcal{L}\max_{\mathcal{L}/MN\leqslant\Delta\leqslant 1/Q}\mathfrak{A}(M,N;\Delta)(X^{-1}\Delta^{-1}H)\ll\mathcal{L}X^{-1}H^{2}M^{2}N^{2}+\mathcal{L}HM^{2}N^{2}.

Then we have

(4.2)

|\mathfrak{T}|\ll\mathcal{L}(HM^{1/2}N^{1/2}+X^{-1/2}HMN+H^{1/2}MN).

Let
X\geqslant MN
. A trivial estimation together with Lemma
2.6
provides that the contribution from
|u|\leqslant\Xi\mathcal{L}/(MN)
is
\ll H^{2}MNQ\mathcal{L}
. We apply a dynamic argument for some
\Delta\in(\mathcal{L}/(MN),1/Q]
to obtain

(4.3)

|\mathfrak{T}|^{2}\ll HQ\sum_{m\sim M}\sum_{\tilde{m}\sim M}\bigg|\mathop{\sum_{n\sim N}\sum_{\tilde{n}\sim N}}_{|u|/\Xi\sim\Delta}g(u)\sum_{h\sim H}\mathbf{e}\bigg(\frac{Xh^{\alpha}u}{H^{\alpha}M^{\beta}N^{\gamma}}\bigg)\bigg|+H^{2}MNQ\mathcal{L}.

For
H^{\prime}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=X\Delta/H\leqslant\varepsilon
, applying Lemma
2.2
and Lemma
2.6
, the first term of the right-hand side of (
4.3
) is
\ll X^{-1}(HMN)^{2}Q\mathcal{L}\ll H^{2}MNQ\mathcal{L}
.

Now we suppose that
H^{\prime}\geqslant\varepsilon
. Applying Lemma
2.4
, the innermost sum of the first term of the right-hand side of (
4.3
) is

(4.4)

\ll(X\Delta)^{-1/2}H\sum_{h^{\prime}\in I^{\prime}}\mathbf{e}(f(u,h^{\prime}))+O(\mathcal{L}+(X\Delta)^{-1/2}H),

where

f(u,h^{\prime})\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=c_{2}X\Delta\big(\frac{u}{\Delta\Xi}\big)^{\frac{1}{1-\alpha}}\big(\frac{h^{\prime}}{H^{\prime}}\big)^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}}

and
I^{\prime}\subset[H^{\prime},2H^{\prime}]
. Again applying Lemma
2.6
, we deduce that

We note that the details of set-up of the Piatetski-Shapiro prime number theorem are well written in

Reminding that

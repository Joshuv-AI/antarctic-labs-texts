Netrin-1-UNC5B/neogenin axis enhances the stemness of colorectal cancer cells
Authors: Xueli Xia, Zhenwei Mao, Wenxin Wang, Lin Zhao, Mengxue Xu, Jie Ma, Jie Tian, Kai Yin, Shengjun Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractCancer stem cells were prominent responsible for cancer initiation, metastasis, and invasion as well as therapeutic resistance in colorectal cancer (CRC). The extracellular axon guidance factor netrin-1 has been found to be overexpressed in several malignant cancers such as glioma, lung cancers, and colorectal cancer. However, the role of netrin-1 on cancer stemness in CRC remains unveiled. Our study revealed high expression of netrin-1 in colorectal cancer tissues and its ability to promote cancer stemness by interacting with receptors UNC5B and neogenin on murine colorectal cancer cell. Mechanistically, the netrin-1-UNC5B/neogenin axis activates the downstream NF-κB and ERK1/2 signaling pathways, reinforcing the stemness properties of tumor cells, and further exacerbating tumor progression. Clinically, netrin-1 expression associated with poor survival and high CD133 expression in patients with CRC. Taken together, these results suggest that netrin-1 blockade could be a compelling therapeutic strategy to improve the poor outcomes and trigger cancer stemness inhibition in CRC treatment.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12964-025-02489-1.Keywords: Netrin-1, UNC5B, Neogenin, NF-κB, ERK1/2, Cancer stemness, Colorectal cancer

Introduction

Colorectal cancer (CRC) is the third most common malignant tumor worldwide and the second largest cause of cancer-related deaths [1, 2]. Although surgical resection, radiotherapy or chemotherapy, as well as targeted therapy are extensively implemented in the common treatment of CRC, many CRC patients are diagnosed at an advanced stage and show high recurrence rates due to an ignorable asymptomatic stage and lack of biomarkers [1, 3, 4]. Hence, understanding the molecular mechanism underlying CRC occurrence and metastasis can provide a therapeutic basis for the early diagnosis and treatment of CRC. In the context of high tumor heterogeneity, cancer stem cell (CSC) theory posits the existence of a small population of distinct tumor cells exhibiting characteristics similar to normal stem cells, including self-renewal capacity and pluripotency, which play a pivotal role in initiating and driving tumor progression [5]. Notably, CSCs are responsible for the development, recurrence, metastasis and dormancy of malignant CRC [6]. Unfortunately, the development of targeting antibodies to treat CRC remains problematic at the present time and has not reached the clinical standard. Therefore, there is an urgency to further develop therapeutic strategies utilizing CSCs.

Netrin-1, a secreted laminin-related protein, is an axon guidance molecule that has a crucial function in tumor migration and proliferation [7–9]. Netrin-1 interacts with the dependence receptors such as deleted in colorectal cancer (DCC) and its homolog receptor neogenin, the uncoordinated-5 family (UNC5A-D in humans) for inhibiting tumor cell apoptosis by preventing downstream caspase cascade activation, while triggering apoptosis in the absence of netrin-1 [10, 11]. In fact, upregulation of netrin-1 is thought to be a way to gain a survival advantage in several cancers, including CRC, non-small cell lung cancer, neuroblastoma, glioblastoma (GBM), and metastatic breast cancer [7, 12–14]. However, the former studies mainly focused on the netrin-1 dependence receptors mediated conditional apoptosis, while the interaction between netrin-1 and receptors on tumor-initiating potential and prognosis remains elusive. In recent years, researchers have found that netrin-1 can also promote the motility and plasticity of GBM, and targeting netrin-1 can inhibit the proliferation, invasion and angiogenesis of GBM [15, 16]. Nonetheless, the regulation of netrin-1 on cancer stemness remains largely underexplored, hindered by the uncertainty of netrin-1 receptors, the obscured downstream molecular pathways, and a lack of comprehensive understanding of its regulatory impacts on non-neurological tumorigenesis. Therefore, the expression profile of netrin-1 and its receptors in tumor cells and their potential roles in cancer progression merit further in-depth exploration.

In our previous study, we demonstrated that netrin-1 was highly expressed in murine colon cancer cell MC38 and plays a pivotal role in enhancing the immunosuppressive activity in myeloid-derived suppressor cells (MDSCs) [17]. In the present study, the roles of netrin-1 in colorectal cancer progression and stemness-like properties were investigated. We demonstrated that netrin-1 promotes cancer stemness characteristics and increases the tumor-initiating ability in an autocrine manner, both within in vivo models and in vitro experimental settings. Furthermore, we uniquely elucidated the synergistic partnership between the receptors UNC5B and neogenin on downstream NF-κB and ERK1/2 activation in the netrin-1-mediated cancer stemness. These data comprehensively support the view that the key roles of netrin-1 and receptors in cancers and will provide clues for developing novel targeted therapy against cancer cell stemness.

Methods and materials

Cell culture

MC38 and CT26, two common murine colorectal carcinoma cell lines, and the murine Lewis lung cancer cell line were obtained from the Cell Bank, Chinese Academy of Sciences (Shanghai, China). Human HCT116 colon carcinoma cell line was purchased from Procell (Wuhan, China). MC38, CT26 and HCT116 were maintained in RPMI 1640 medium (Gibco, 21875-034) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) (Gibco, 12484028) and 1% penicillin/streptomycin antibiotics (Gibco,10378016) at 37 °C in humidified air containing 5% CO2. Lewis cells were cultured in high-glucose Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium (DMEM, Gibco) supplemented with 10% FBS and 1% penicillin and streptomycin at 37 °C in humidified air containing 5% CO2. Cells were passaged every 2–3 days to obtain exponential growth. All cell lines were routinely tested to ensure there was no Mycoplasma contamination with MycAway-Color One-Step Mycoplasma detection kit (40612ES25, Yeasen Biotechnology, Shanghai, China). Recombinant Netrin-1 protein (R&D Systems, 1109-N1) was used to treat tumor cells above mentioned.

Patient tissue specimens

Primary CRC tissues and adjacent colorectal tissues were collected from the Affiliated Hospital of Jiangsu University, Jiangsu, China. Written informed consent from all the subjects and approval from the Ethics Committee of Affiliated Hospital of Jiangsu University were obtained for the use of human biological materials for research purposes. All human tumor samples used in this study were diagnosed and morphologically typified. CRC tissues and adjacent colorectal tissues were collected from CRC patients undergoing surgery and paraffin-embedded for immunofluorescence or stored at −80℃ for Western blot analysis.

Animal models

A murine tumor xenograft model was established in 6-week-old female C57ΒL/6J mice (Laboratory Animal Center, Jiangsu University). The animals were raised under SPF conditions according to Good Lab-oratory Practice (GLP) OECD guidelines. The animals were maintained in a room at 23 ± 1 °C, with a relative humidity of 50 ± 10%, artificial lighting from 08:00 to 20:00, and 14–18 air changes per hour. The mice were fed a standard animal diet and water ad libitum. shRNA scramble and shRNA-NTN1 MC38 cells (2 × 106 cells/100 µL) suspended in phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) were inoculated subcutaneously into the right flanks of the C57ΒL/6J mice. The length (L) and width (W) of tumors were measured by vernier calipers and the volume (V) was calculated using formula V = (L×W2)/2. Animal experiments were carried out in accordance with the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of Jiangsu University. This animal experiment was performed with the approval of the Ethics Committee (approval No. UJS-IACUC-AP-2021031203).

Quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR) assay

For RNA extraction from MC38 cells, CT26 cells and Lewis cells, TRIzol Reagent (Invitrogen, 15596018) was used. Approximately 2 µg of prepared RNA from cells was used as a template to synthesize the first-strand cDNA using the PrimeScript RT reagent kit (Takara, RR037A) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Then, qRT-PCR amplification was performed with the TB Green premix (Takara, RR420A) according to the manufacturer’s standard protocols. All samples were then subjected to the CFX96 sequence detection system (Bio-Rad). Levels of relative mRNA expression for target genes were analyzed and quantified after normalization to β-actin endogenous control using the ΔCt (difference between cycle thresholds) method. Sequence information for primers was listed in Supplementary Table 1.

Western blot

Cells were washed twice with ice-cold PBS and the total proteins were extracted by using radio-immunoprecipitation assay (RIPA) lysis buffer containing 150 mM NaCl, 10 mM Tris pH 7.2, 0.1% sodium dodecyl sulfate, 1% Triton X-100, 1% sodium deoxycholate, and 5 mM ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (CWBIO, CW2333S). The protein concentrations of cell or tumor lysates were measured using protein assay reagent. Protein samples were separated by 10% sodium dodecyl sulfate polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE) and then transferred to polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) membranes. The membranes were first blocked for 1 h at room temperature in 5% BSA and then incubated at 4 °C overnight in TBS containing 0.1% Tween 20 with primary antibodies: anti-SOX-2, anti-Nanog, anti-OCT-4, anti-β-catenin, anti-NF-κB p65, anti-NF-κΒ p-p65, anti-ERK1/2, anti-p-ERK1/2 and anti-β-actin, followed by incubating with horseradish peroxidase-conjugated anti-mouse or anti-rabbit secondary antibodies at room temperature for 1 h. The detailed information for antibodies was listed in Supplementary Table 2. The immunoreactivity was visualized with an enhanced chemiluminescence (ECL) plus kit (Millipore, WBULS0500) as recommended by the manufacturer using a luminescent image analyzer (GE Healthcare, LAS4000). Some blots were stripped by stripping buffer (Thermo Fisher, 21059) and re-blocked with 5% non-fat milk, following by primary antibodies and HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies incubation. The gray value was determined using ImageJ 2.1.0 software. Relative expression level calculation was evaluated by target protein/β-actin ratio.

Flow cytometry (FCM)

Cells were washed and resuspended in PBS. Subsequently, PE conjucated anti-CD133 antibody (Miltenyi Biotec, 130–133−780) and/or eFluor 506 conjucated anti-CD45 antibody (eBioscience, 69–0451−82) were added to 100 µL cell suspensions and the mixture was incubated for 30 min in the dark at 4 °C. For Ki67 staining, cells were resuspended and stained with eFluor 506 conjucated anti-CD45 antibody (eBioscience, 69–0451−82). Cells were permeabilized with a Foxp3/transcription factor staining buffer kit (eBioscience, 00–5523−00) and incubated with an eFluor 405 conjucated anti-Ki67 antibody (eBioscience, 48–5698−82). Cells were resuspended in 200 µL of PBS for subsequent flow cytometric analysis. Flow cytometric data were acquired on a CytoFlex flow cytometer (Beckman Coulter) and analyzed using FlowJo (Becton Dickinson).

ALDH activity detection

Aldefluor™ Kit (Stem Cell Technologies, 01700) was used to determine the percentage of tumor cells expressing high levels of aldehyde dehydrogenase (ALDH; ALDH-high cells) in accordance with the manufacturer’s instructions. Briefly, 1 × 106 cells were incubated with the Aldefluor substrate for 45 min at 37 °C with or without the ALDH inhibitor diethylamino benzaldehyde. After incubation, ALDH-high cells were detected in the fluorescein isothiocyanate (FITC) channel of the CytoFlex (Beckman Coulter).

Tissue dissociation

Tumor tissues were minced into 1–2 mm3 and digested in RPMI 1640 supplemented with 5% FBS, 0.5 mg/mL collagenase type I (Sigma-Aldrich, C0130), 0.2 mg/mL hyaluronidase (Sigma-Aldrich, H1115000), 0.015 mg/mL DNase I (Sigma-Aldrich, DN25) at 37℃ for 1 h and filtered by a 70 μm cell strainer (Corning, 352350). Red blood cells were then lysed by ACK lysis buffer (Gibco, A1049201) to prepare single-cell suspensions. Spleen and tumor were grinded in RPMI 1640 and filtered by 70 μm cell strainer for further experiments.

Lentiviral gene transduction

Cells with stably green fluorescent protein (GFP) and NTN1 expression were produced as previously described [17]. Shortly, Target cells were infected with tittered lenti-viruses. The transduced cells were selected by administering 1 µg/mL puromycin (MCE, HY-B1743A) in their culture medium. The efficiency of GFP overexpression and NTN1 knockdown was evaluated by flow cytometry and Western blot.

Small interfering (si)RNA transfection

MC38 colon adenocarcinoma cells and Lewis lung cancer cells were seeded into 24-well plates (1 × 105/well) and incubated overnight. When cells reached 70% confluency, transient siRNA transfection was conducted using Lipofectamine 3000 (Invitrogen, L3000150). Target siRNAs or negative control siRNA(siNC) were transfected at 50 nmol/L into cells in the presence or absence of recombinant netrin-1 protein. After 6 h, complete medium with 10% FBS was used for further culture. Cells were harvested 24–48 h post-transfection for qRT-PCR to measure the knockdown efficiency and other experiments. The siRNA sequences were provided in Supplementary Table 3.

Transwell assay

An 8-µm pore size upper chamber (Corning-Costar, 3422) was used in a Transwell assay to investigate the migrative behavior of MC38 and Lewis cells with netrin-1 treatment or netrin-1 receptor knockdown. 1 × 105 cells from each group suspended in 100 µL of serum-free RPMI 1640 were added in the upper chamber. 600 µL RPMI 1640 containing 10% FBS were added to the lower chamber. The cells and medium were incubated at 37 °C for 16 h. After incubation, the cells still present on the upper membrane were gently removed with a cotton swab. Cells that had migrated to the lower surface of the membrane were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde and then stained with 0.1% crystal violet solution (Beyotime, C0121) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. The mean numbers of migrating cells were counted from five random fields under the microscope (Olympus, BX53).

Sphere formation assay

Primary MC38 cells or Lewis cells at a density of 1000 cells/well, were grown in DMEM/F12 cell culture medium (Gibco, 11320033) supplemented with 100 IU/mL penicillin, 50 mg/mL streptomycin, 20 ng/mL basic fibroblast growth factor (bFGF, Peprotech, 450 − 33), 20 ng/mL epidermal growth factor (EGF, R&D Systems, 236-EG), B27 supplement without vitamin A (Gibco, 12587010) in ultra-low attachment 6-well plates at 37 °C and 5% CO2 for 10 days. Mammospheres with a diameter > 75 μm in five randomly selected fields were counted with an inverted microscope (Olympus, IX73).

Colony formation assay

A total of 300 cells per well were seeded into a 6-well plate containing 3 mL RPMI 1640 with 10% FBS and cultured at 37 °C for 10 days. After washing with PBS for three times and fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde (PFA) for 20 min in room temperature. Cells were stained with 0.1% crystal violet (Beyotime). Cells were photographed and representative images were shown in Results.

Co-immunoprecipitation (Co-IP)

MC38 cell lysates were prepared using IP lysis buffer (Beyotime, P0013). Pre-cleared lysates were incubated with 2 µg anti-netrin-1 monoclonal antibody (Abcam, ab126729) or isotype-matched IgG control (Cell Signaling, 3900) for 2 h at 4 °C with gentle rotation, and then pre-washed Protein A/G beads (Santa Cruz) were added and incubated overnight at 4 °C. Bead-antibody-antigen complexes were washed three times with ice-cold IP lysis buffer. The immunoprecipitated proteins were detected using the following primary antibodies: netrin-1 (1:1000, Abcam), UNC5B (1:500, Abclonal), and neogenin (1:1000, R&D systems), followed by HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies.

Inhibition of the NF- κΒ and ERK1/2 signaling pathway

MC38 cells were incubated with the specific NF-κΒ p65 phosphorylation inhibitor BAY-11–7085 (10 µM) (MCE, HY-10257) or ERK1/2 phosphorylation antagonist PD98059 (20 µM) (MCE, HY-12028) in 10% serum medium for 24 h. Control cells were treated with the same volume of dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO). After incubation, the efficacy of phosphorylation level inhibition was detected by Western blot, the stemness markers of MC38 cells were determined.

Immunofluorescence

Formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded tissues were used for immunofluorescence. Tissues went through deparaffinization, rehydration and antigen retrieval, and were pre-blocked with PBS containing 5% BSA for 1 h. The cells were then incubated with PE conjugated anti-CD133, anti-netrin-1 antibody (Abcam, ab126729) primary antibody overnight at 4 °C, followed by incubation with Alexa Fluor 488 secondary antibody (Invitrogen, A-11008) for 1 h at 37 °C, and then counterstained with DAPI (Invitrogen, 62248) for 10 min at room temperature. After incubation, Samples were mounted in prolonged antifade mounting medium (Invitrogen, P36961). The fluorescence signal was visualized using a confocal microscope (GE Healthcare, DeltaVision OMX) at excitation wavelengths of 488 nm (Alexa Fluor 488), 561 nm (PE), and 405 nm (DAPI).

Database analysis

The Kaplan-Meier Plotter database (http://www.kmplot.com) was used to access the association between gene expression and survival periods of patients with CRC and lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD). The gene set cancer analysis (GSCALite) (http://bioinfo.life.hust.edu.cn/web/GSCA/) was conducted to analyze the behaviors of netrin-1 in CRC and LUAD [18]. The Cancer Cell Line Encyclopedia (https://sites.broadinstitute.org/ccle/datasets) was used for accessing the netrin-1 expression in human CRC cell lines.

Statistical analysis

Values are mean ± S.E.M., where n indicates the number of independent cell cultures. Comparisons between two groups were performed by the Mann Whitney test and unpaired t-test, depending on the parametricity of data, and comparisons between three or more groups were performed via one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA). If the ANOVA demonstrated a significant interaction between variables, post hoc analyses were performed by multiple-comparison Dunn’s correction test. Survival times among the groups were analyzed by Kaplan-Meier curves and the log-rank test. All the determinations were carried out in triplicate. P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Significance was denoted as ns, no significance, *P < 0.05, ** P < 0.01, and *** P < 0.001.

Results

Netrin-1 knockdown significantly decreases the stemness of MC38 cells

Due to the extensive role of netrin-1 in tumorigenesis, we first investigated the correlations between the expression of netrin-1 and survival indicators such as overall survival (OS) and recurrence free survival (RFS) of CRC patients using Kaplan-Meier analysis. The CRC patients with higher expression of netrin-1 had poorer OS and RFS compared with netrin-1-low patients (Fig. 1A). The netrin-1 expression was positively related to stemness-related genes SOX2 and CTNNB1 (encoded β-catenin) in CRC patients (Fig. 1B). Thus, we focused on the effects of netrin-1 on the stemness and progression of CRC. Our previous study showed that netrin-1 was higher expressed in murine colorectal cancer cell line MC38 cells than CT26 cells [17]. Interestingly, MC38 cells cultured in spheroid medium have high abundance expression of netrin-1 rather than adherent MC38 cells (Fig. 1C). To verify the effect of endogenous netrin-1 on the stemness of CRC cells, MC38 cells were infected with shRNA NTN1 lentiviral vector or shRNA scramble vector to construct netrin-1 knockdown MC38 cell line (shRNA-NTN1) and scramble MC38 cell line (shRNA-scramble). The high efficiency of transfection was identified with >95% GFP positive cells by FCM and with >50% reduced expression of netrin-1 by Western blot assay (Supplementary Fig. S1A-B). The mRNA levels and protein expression of stem cell markers sex determining region Y-box 2 (SOX2) and nanog homeobox (Nanog) were decreased by netrin-1 knockdown, but netrin-1 deficiency had no significant effect on POU class 5 homeobox 1 (POU5F1)/octamer-binding transcription factor 4 (OCT-4) expression in MC38 cells (Fig. 1D and E). In addition, pluripotency related transcription factor β-catenin was also significantly decreased after netrin-1 deletion (Fig. 1E). β-catenin in the nucleus can enhance the stemness of CRC and promote CRC progression [19, 20]. Meanwhile, loss of netrin-1 in MC38 cells led to a significant decrease in sphere formation and migration ability (Fig. 1F and G). Immunofluorescence assay showed that surface stemness marker CD133 was downregulated in MC38 cells with netrin-1 knockdown (Fig. 1H). Collectively, these observations suggest that netrin-1 was associated with poor prognosis of CRC patients, and netrin-1 knockdown resulted in a significant decrease in the stemness of MC38 cells.

Recombinant netrin-1 protein enhances the stemness of MC38 cells

To investigate the interaction mechanism of netrin-1 in regulating the stemness of CRC cells, MC38 cells were treated with different concentrations of recombinant netrin-1protein (0, 50, 100, 200 ng/mL). qRT-PCR and Western blot results showed that netrin-1 treatment significantly increased the mRNA and protein expression of SOX2 and Nanog in MC38 cells (Fig. 2A and B). However, netrin-1 treatment did not affect the stemness features of CT26 cells (Supplementary Fig. S1C). Moreover, we found that β-catenin was increased by netrin-1 treatment in a concentration-dependent manner. To investigate the CSC phenotype of netrin-1-treated MC38 cells, the aldehyde dehydrogenase 1-positive (ALDH1+) cell population and the CD133+ cell population were analyzed by FCM. The results showed that netrin-1 increases the proportion of CD133+ and ALDH1+ stem-like MC38 cells in a dose-dependent manner (Fig. 2C and D). MC38 cells were treated with 200 ng/mL netrin-1 recombinant protein for 24 h, and the Transwell migration assay showed that netrin-1 treatment enhanced the ability of MC38 cells to migrate to Transwell sub-compartment (Fig. 2E). In addition, netrin-1 treatment increased the number of sphere formation and colony formation of MC38 cells (Fig. 2F and G). These results suggest that exogenous netrin-1 treatment enhances the stemness and migration ability of MC38 cells.

Netrin-1 affects the CSC frequency and promotes the tumor progression in vivo

We then investigated the effect of netrin-1 on the stemness of CRC cells in vivo. MC38 cells with shRNA-scramble or shRNA-NTN1 were injected subcutaneously to generate subcutaneous tumor xenografts and to monitor the tumor growth. Netrin-1 knockdown led to a significant decrease in tumor volume during tumor progression (Fig. 3A). To determine whether netrin-1 affects the proliferation ability of MC38 cells, Ki67 levels in tumor tissues were detected. FCM results showed that netrin-1 knockdown did not affect the expression level of Ki67 in tumor cells (Fig. 3B). In order to clarify the effect of netrin-1 on CSCs in tumor bearing mice, we further examined the proportion of CD133+ CSCs in tumor tissue. FCM results showed that the proportion and numbers of CD133+ CSCs in the tumor tissue of the netrin-1 knockdown MC38 mouse subcutaneous tumor model were decreased compared with those of shRNA-scramble mice (Fig. 3C and D). In addition, under the condition of injection of 1 × 105 or 1 × 106 cells, when the survival numbers of shRNA-scramble tumor-bearing mice were 0/3 or 0/5, the survival numbers of shRNA-NTN1 tumor-bearing mice were 2/3 or 1/5, respectively (Fig. 3E and F). These findings displayed that netrin-1 knockdown leads to delayed tumor growth and reduced tumor cell stemness in MC38 tumor-bearing mice.

Netrin-1 interacts with UNC5B and neogenin to enhance the stemness features in MC38 cells

Netrin-1 works mainly by binding to different receptors including the UNC5 family (UNC5A, UNC5B, UNC5C, and UNC5D), DCC, neogenin, DSCAM, and A2BR. To determine whether netrin-1 plays a role through an autocrine manner, we assessed the netrin-1 receptor expression in two CRC cell lines MC38 (netrin-1high) and CT26 (netrin-1low). qRT-PCR and Western blot results showed that netrin-1 receptor UNC5B and neogenin mRNA and protein were significantly overexpressed in MC38 cell line compared with CT26 cell line (Supplementary Fig. 2A-B). Following co-immunoprecipitation (Co-IP), we validated that netrin-1 can interact with UNC5B/neogenin in MC38 cells. We further utilized the STRING database (https://string-db.org) to analyze protein-protein interactions involving netrin-1, UNC5B, and neogenin. The network analysis revealed a high-confidence interaction complex (confidence score > 0.95) comprising all three proteins, with netrin-1 functioning as the central hub connecting UNC5B and neogenin (Supplementary Fig. 2C-D). Additionally, compared with MC38 cells, mouse lung cancer Lewis cells have more netrin-1 mRNA expression, and netrin-1 receptor UNC5B and neogenin were also highly expressed in Lewis cells, which led us to take the roles of netrin-1 and its receptors in the stemness of Lewis cells into consideration (Supplementary Fig. 2E-G). In order to figure out the receptor mechanism of netrin-1 promoting the stemness of MC38 cells, we used siRNA to knock down the expression of UNC5B or neogenin respectively in MC38 cells and Lewis cells. The knockdown efficiency of UNC5B or neogenin by specific siRNA transfection was confirmed by qRT-PCR (Supplementary Fig. 2H). We found that the stemness molecules SOX2 and Nanog could be significantly reduced, as well as the decreased β-catenin by silencing the expression of either one of the receptors separately or both receptors simultaneously (si2R for short) in the presence or absence of netrin-1 treatment (Fig. 4A and B). However, there was no significant effect on the expression of OCT-4 mRNA and protein (Fig. 4A and B). In addition, simultaneous knockdown of UNC5B and neogenin failed to further decrease the expression of SOX2 and Nanog, indicating that netrin-1 plays a stemness-promoting role through both UNC5B and neogenin receptors in an autocrine manner. Furthermore, inhibition of the expression of UNC5B and neogenin could reduce the proportion of CD133+ and ALDH1+ stem-like MC38 cells (Fig. 4C and D). Silencing of UNC5B and neogenin in MC38 cells led to a significant impairment in sphere formation, migration ability and reduced colony numbers(Fig. 4E-G). Meanwhile, the effect of UNC5B and neogenin on the stemness properties of Lewis cells was also demonstrated in Supplementary Fig. 3. Taken together, netrin-1 promotes the stemness of MC38 cells through UNC5B and neogenin.

Netrin-1-UNC5B/neogenin axis promotes the stemness of CRC via NF-κB and ERK1/2 signaling

Studies have reported that netrin-1 promotes the pluripotency of embryonic stem cells or hematopoietic stem cells by activating downstream signaling pathways such as transcription factor nuclear factor-kB (NF-κB) and ERK1/2 signaling pathways through receptors [21, 22]. The p65 subunit has a transcriptional activation domain and is involved in cell survival, invasion, proliferation, metastasis, angiogenesis, and chemoresistance. Given that the importance of NF-κB and ERK1/2 in the initial and progression of CRC [23–25]. Thus, we attempted to explore the effect of netrin-1 on NF-κB and ERK1/2 signaling. Recombinant netrin-1 protein significantly enhanced the phosphorylation of NF-κB p65 and ERK1/2 in MC38 cells within 30 min (Fig. 5A). Conversely, the phosphorylation levels of NF-κB p65 and ERK1/2 were significantly reduced by netrin-1 knockdown (Fig. 5B). Moreover, inhibition of UNC5B or neogenin expression with siRNA resulted in the reduced phosphorylation levels of NF-κB p65 and ERK1/2 in MC38 cells compared to siNC even in the presence of netrin-1 (Fig. 5C). A similar phenomenon was observed in Lewis cells (Supplementary Fig. 4A). To determine whether NF-κB and ERK1/2 signaling were involved in netrin-1-induced cancer cell stemness, we treated MC38 cells with the NF-κB inhibitor BAY 11–7085 or the ERK1/2 inhibitor PD98059, respectively. Compared with DMSO controls, BAY 11–7085 and PD98059 inhibited NF-κB p65 phosphorylation and ERK1/2 phosphorylation, respectively (Supplementary Fig. 4B). BAY 11–7085 or PD98059 reduced SOX2 and Nanog mRNA levels in MC38 cells but did not alter OCT-4 mRNA levels (Fig. 5D). The proportion of CD133+ and ALDH1+ stem-like MC38 cells was decreased by BAY 11–7085 and PD98059 treatment even in the presence of netrin-1 (Fig. 5E and F). Moreover, antagonized NF-κB and ERK1/2 signaling also decreased sphere numbers, migration ability and single cell colony numbers in netrin-1 treated cells (Fig. 5G-I). These results suggest that NF-κB and ERK1/2 signaling pathways are essential for the acquisition and maintenance of cancer stemness properties, and that netrin-1 plays a role in promoting stemness through these two signaling pathways.

Clinical analysis of netrin-1 and its receptors in human colorectal cancer

To further assess the human and clinical relevance of our findings, we investigated the correlations between the expression of netrin-1, UNC5B and neogenin and OS or RFS in CRC and lung cancer patients using Kaplan-Meier analysis. As we shown in Fig. 1A, netrin-1 also can be a prognostic factor for poor survival in patients with lung cancer (Supplementary Fig. 5A). However, two receptors UNC5B and neogenin expression seem to have non-redundant roles in the survival maintenance of CRC. High UNC5B expression was positively correlated with low survival ability in CRC patients, whereas neogenin acts as a tumor suppressor for selectively prolonging the survival times of CRC patients (Fig. 6A and B). Consistently, the expression pattern of UNC5B and neogenin had reverse effects on OS and RFS of patients with lung cancer (Supplementary Fig. 5B-C). Kaplan-Meier analysis from the GSCA database further confirmed the distinct favorable prognostic role of neogenin in CRC patients (Supplementary Fig. 5D), which is consistent with previous findings [26]. Moreover, UNC5B has been demonstrated as an independent marker associated with epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EMT) score in lung cancer [27]. In here, we found netrin-1 was positively correlated with EMT markers expression by analysis (Supplementary Fig. 5E). We also showed that the high expression of UNC5B was associated with abundant EMT pathway activity score in colorectal cancer and lung cancer (Supplementary Fig. 5F). To further verify the truth of netrin-1 in clinical databases, fresh colorectal cancer tissues and adjacent tissues were collected for Western blot analysis. Results confirmed the high expression of netrin-1 and UNC5B but low neogenin expression in most CRC tumor tissues compared with adjacent tissues (Fig. 6C). Meanwhile, similar results were also found in the GEO dataset GSE24550 (Fig. 6D). To understand the relationship between netrin-1 and CSCs in CRC patients, Immunofluorescence analysis showed that the expression of netrin-1 was colocalized with CD133 in cancer tissues and was significantly higher than that in adjacent tissues in CRC patients (Fig. 6E). Moreover, data from the Cancer Cell Line Encyclopedia (https://portals.broadinstitute.org/ccle) database showed that the mRNA level of netrin-1 was significantly higher in HCT116 cell line among multiple human CRC cell lines (Fig. 6F). Recombinant netrin-1 protein treatment can promote the expression of stemness-associated markers such as β-catenin, SOX-2, OCT-4 and Nanog in HCT116 cells (Fig. 6G). Furthermore, netrin-1 knockdown in HCT116 cells via siRNA-mediated gene silencing resulted in a significant reduction in the expression levels of stemness-associated markers (Fig. 6H) and the phosphorylation of both NF-κB (p-p65) and ERK1/2 (p-ERK) (Fig. 6I). Thus, these results indicated that netrin-1 and UNC5B are strongly associated with the stemness properties of CRC cells and may be potential prognosis markers and intervention targets for CRC treatment.

Discussion

In this study, we confirmed the correlation between netrin-1 and cancer stemness in mouse and human CRC cell lines and clinical samples. Our study demonstrated that netrin-1 interacts with its receptor UNC5B and neogenin to drive cancer stemness in CRC cells via NF-κΒ and ERK1/2 signaling pathway (Fig. 7). Moreover, the expression of netrin-1 and UNC5B is elevated in CRC tissues and have a positive correlation with CRC progression and poor prognosis.

Netrin-1 interacts with its receptor UNC5B and neogenin to drive cancer stemness. Tumor cell-derived netrin-1 can interact with UNC5B and neogenin to activate the downstream NF-κΒ and ERK1/2 pathway, which led to the increased stemness-related genes expression, thus enhancing cancer stemness

Netrin-1 was reported to inhibit tumor apoptosis, which is related to the dependence receptors and netrin-1 concentration in CRC tumorigenesis [13]. Remarkably, netrin-1 and its receptor expression profiles were controversial in CRC, reduced or mutated netrin-1 receptor expression rather than increased netrin-1 expression was usually observed in many sporadic human CRC cases [28, 29]. However, upregulation of netrin-1 expression is often found in CRC or colitis-associated cancer patients with over-activation of NF-κB [30, 31]. Our previous work demonstrated that netrin-1 was highly expressed in murine colorectal cancer cell line MC38 and was elevated in plasma and tumor tissue of CRC patients [17]. In the present study, we also found that netrin-1 and UNC5B were highly expressed in CRC patients and have a positive correlation with poor prognosis and high cancer stemness features. Discrepant with our results, Nakayama et al. reported that netrin-1 expression is significantly reduced due to DNA methylation abundance in most CRC tissues [32]. This is probably a consequence of the differential inflammatory milieu that characterizes CRC as well as variations in the netrin-1 receptor expression patterns. Interestingly, we also found that CoCl2, a hypoxia mimic agent, induced netrin-1 mRNA and protein expression in MC38 cells (Supplementary Fig. S6A-B). Moreover, the upregulation of netrin-1 is dependent on hypoxia-inducible factor 1α (HIF-1α). When knocking down the HIF-1α expression from mRNA and protein levels, the expression of netrin-1 was significantly decreased (Supplementary Fig. S6C-E). Thus, our studies demonstrated that HIF-1α is responsible for high netrin-1 expression in MC38 cells, which further supports the importance of netrin-1 and hypoxia microenvironment in CRC progression.

Recent studies have manifested that maintaining netrin-1/DCC balance can enhance the ability of somatic cells to reprogram pluripotent stem cells, and sustaining Netrin-1-UNC5B balance can maintain the pluripotency of embryonic stem cells [22]. Another study showed that netrin-1 regulates Wnt and MAPK pathways in embryonic stem cells through receptors neogenin and UNC5B to promote self-renewal of embryonic stem cells [21]. These studies revealed the important role of netrin-1 and its receptors in the regulation of self-renewal capability and pluripotency of stem cells. Therefore, whether netrin-1 is involved in regulating the acquisition and maintenance of CSCs pluripotency is worthy of further discussion. Netrin-1 has been found to participate in the maintenance and movement of GBM stem cells [15, 16]. Furthermore, netrin-1 has been proven as an independent prognostic marker, exhibiting a correlation between its expression levels and CSC markers in human breast and ovary cancer [33]. Peculiarly, netrin-1 from cancer-associated fibroblasts has an indirect effect on cancer stemness via unexplained cytokine production [34]. However, these studies are lacking in more precise molecular evidence to conclusively demonstrate the direct regulatory role of netrin-1 and its receptors on cancer stemness. In the current study, we found that netrin-1 receptors UNC5B and neogenin were highly expressed in MC38 cells, suggesting that up-regulated netrin-1 may confer growth and metastasis advantages for MC38 cells in an autocrine manner. We demonstrated that recombinant netrin-1 protein treatment promotes the stemness characteristics of MC38 cells, while endogenous netrin-1 depletion significantly decreases stemness properties. The Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway is a well-established and canonical molecular cascade that plays a pivotal role in sustaining colorectal cancer cell self-renewal capacity [19]. This pathway is also instrumental in driving the expression of key pluripotency-associated genes, including SOX2, Nanog, and OCT-4, which are critical for maintaining stem cell-like properties in cancer cells. In our study, we have revealed that the Netrin-1/UNC5B-neogenin receptor axis can potentiate β-catenin expression, thereby amplifying Wnt signaling activity. Interestingly, while SOX2, Nanog, and OCT-4 collectively contribute to the maintenance of stem cell pluripotency, treatment with recombinant Netrin-1 selectively modulates the expression of SOX2 and Nanog without affecting OCT-4 levels. This discrepancy suggests that Netrin-1 may regulate pluripotency-related genes through distinct mechanisms or that OCT-4 expression is governed by additional compensatory pathways that remain unaffected by Netrin-1 signaling. The underlying cause of this differential regulation warrants further investigation to elucidate the precise molecular interactions and signaling crosstalk involved. These results suggest that netrin-1 promotes cancer stemness in an autocrine manner. Two highly expressed netrin-1 receptors UNC5B and neogenin were responsible for netrin-1-induced stemness characteristics. In addition, we also demonstrated that netrin-1 interacts with UNC5B and neogenin to impact the stemness properties of Lewis cells. However, the precise interaction or collaboration between UNC5B and neogenin remains elusive.

EMT is a gradually process, which confers superiority for acquiring stem-like features [35]. Multiple studies have found that netrin-1 induces EMT in hepatocellular carcinoma and non-small cell lung cancer under hypoxia conditions [36–38]. Recently, a netrin-1 neutralizing monoclonal antibody (NP137 mAb) has been confirmed to play pivotal roles in EMT inhibition and in triggering immune microenvironment remodeling [27, 39]. Moreover, multiple ongoing clinical trials of NP137 therapy for cancer treatment indicate the potential of targeting netrin-1 for clinical cancer therapy [40]. In our study, we conducted the database analysis for the correlation between netrin-1 or UNC5B and EMT scores in CRC patients, which further providing additional evidence for the supportive roles of netrin-1 and UNC5B in cancer stemness and EMT in CRC progression. Interestingly, neogenin has a negatively correlation with the poor survival in patients with CRC and lung cancer, which is inconsistent with the expression and function of neogenin in MC38 and Lewis cells. The possible reason for the controversial results lies in the collaboration works between neogenin and other netrin-1 receptors, as well as the netrin-1 dependence.

Netrin-1 often interacts with receptors to activate key downstream signaling pathways such as NF-κB, PI3K/AKT and MAPK signaling pathways. It has been reported that netrin-1/neogenin promotes the migration of melanoma cells, invasion of medullocytoma and angiogenesis through the ERK1/2 signaling pathway [41, 42]. Netrin-1 maintains hematopoietic stem cell self-renewal through neogenin activation of downstream NF-κB signaling in the hematopoietic stem cell niche [22]. Netrin-1-UNC5B can also participate in the activation of the ERK1/2 signaling pathway to maintain the pluripotency of embryonic stem cells [21]. Meanwhile, NF-κB and ERK signaling pathways also promote the tumor-initiation potential of non-stem cells by triggering dedifferentiation and maintaining cancer cell plasticity in CRC [43, 44]. In CRC cells, ERK1/2 is significantly activated in CD133+ CSCs compared to CD133− cancer cells [45]. Moreover, the crosstalk between NF-κB and ERK1/2 signaling cascade plays a multifaceted role in amplifying cancer stemness. NF-κB can enhance the β-catenin activation and its transcriptional activity as well as stabilize β-catenin protein, which collectively reinforce β-catenin-driven stemness programs [46]. ERK1/2 also can indirectly upregulate the expression of β-catenin by phosphorylating and inactivating GSK-3β [47, 48]. Here, we found that netrin-1 promotes the activation of NF-κB and ERK1/2 signaling pathways, whereas inhibition of netrin-1 receptor UNC5B and neogenin hinders the activation of NF-κB and ERK1/2 signaling pathways. Furthermore, using NF-κB and ERK1/2 specific inhibitors BAY 11–7085 and PD98059 could decrease the stemness of CRC cells. Taken together, these results suggest that netrin-1 enhances CRC cell stemness by activating NF-κB and ERK1/2 signaling pathways through UNC5B and neogenin receptors, which provides a basis for investigating netrin-1and its receptors as potential new targets for the treatment of CRC.

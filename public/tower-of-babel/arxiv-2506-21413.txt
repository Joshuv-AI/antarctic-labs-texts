Modular fixed points in equivariant homotopy theory
Yorick Fuhrmann

Abstract
. We show that the derived ∞ \infty -category of permutation modules is equivalent to the category of modules over the Eilenberg-MacLane spectrum associated to a constant Mackey functor in the ∞ \infty -category of equivariant spectra. On such module categories we define a modular fixed point functor using geometric fixed points followed by an extension of scalars and identify it with the modular fixed point functor on derived permutation modules introduced by Balmer-Gallauer in BG22a . As an application, we show that the Picard group of such a module category for a p p -group is given by the group of class functions satisfying the Borel-Smith conditions. In the language of representation theory, this result was first obtained by Miller in Mil24b .

4.
Derived Permutation Modules

Permutation modules already came into play when we discussed cohomological Mackey functors, and we will now consider them in greater detail. Since they form an additive but non-abelian category, determining a suitable notion of ‘derivation’ for permutation modules is a non-trivial task. Balmer-Gallauer proposed a candidate by taking into account fixed points for all subgroups and defined a derived tt-category of permutation modules in
BG22
.

In this section we will define it as an
\infty
-category and then show that it is equivalent to the derived
\infty
-category of cohomological Mackey functors, this relies on the corresponding 1-categorical statements of
BG23
. By
3.15
this will also express it as modules over the Eilenberg-MacLane spectrum associated to a constant Mackey functor.

Construction 4.1
.

The category
\textsf{{Perm}}({G};{R})
is additive and equipped with a symmetric monoidal structure which is
R
-linear and preserves coproducts in both variables, inherited from the tensor product over
R
on
\textsf{{Mod}}(G;R)
. We obtain a symmetric monoidal stable
\infty
-category
\mathcal{K}(\textsf{{Perm}}({G};{R}))
of unbounded complexes of permutation modules up to chain homotopy equivalence.

Denote by
\mathcal{K}_{G-\textnormal{ac}}(\textsf{{Perm}}({G};{R}))
the full subcategory of
\mathcal{K}(\textsf{{Perm}}({G};{R}))
on
G
-acyclic objects, i.e. on complexes
X\in\mathcal{K}(\textsf{{Perm}}({G};{R}))
for which the
H
-fixed points
X^{H}
are acyclic for each subgroup
H\leq G
. As it is closed under taking shifts and cofibres, it defines a stable subcategory.

Definition 4.2
.

The
derived
\infty
-category of permutation modules of
G
with coefficients in
R
is defined as the Verdier localisation

\mathcal{D}\textsf{{Perm}}({G};{R})\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\frac{\mathcal{K}(\textsf{{Perm}}({G};{R}))}{\mathcal{K}_{G-\textnormal{ac}}(\textsf{{Perm}}({G};{R}))}.

That is,
\mathcal{D}\textsf{{Perm}}({G};{R})
is the cofibre of the inclusion of the stable subcategory

\mathcal{K}_{G-\textnormal{ac}}(\textsf{{Perm}}({G};{R}))\hookrightarrow\mathcal{K}(\textsf{{Perm}}({G};{R})),

taken in
\textsf{{Cat}}_{\infty}^{\textnormal{ex}}
, the
\infty
-category of stable
\infty
-categories and exact functors.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\textnormal{Loc}(R(G/H)\mid H\leq G)\subseteq\mathcal{K}(\textsf{{Perm}}({G};{R}))
be the localising subcategory generated by the objects
R(G/H)
,
H\leq G
. Then there is an equivalence

\textnormal{Loc}(R(G/H)\mid H\leq G)\simeq\mathcal{D}\textsf{{Perm}}({G};{R}).

Proof.

The composition of exact functors between stable
\infty
-categories

\textnormal{Loc}(R(G/H)\mid H\leq G)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{K}(\textsf{{Perm}}({G};{R}))\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{D}\textsf{{Perm}}({G};{R}).

is an equivalence on homotopy categories, as
BG23
, Proposition 3.9
shows by employing the Neeman-Thomason localisation theorem. It hence is an equivalence of
\infty
-categories.
∎

Remark 4.4
.

It follows that the quotient functor
\mathcal{K}(\textsf{{Perm}}({G};{R}))\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{D}\textsf{{Perm}}({G};{R})
exhibits
\mathcal{D}\textsf{{Perm}}({G};{R})
as a Bousfield colocalisation of
\mathcal{K}(\textsf{{Perm}}({G};{R}))
, i.e. it has a fully faithful left adjoint. In particular, we can view
\mathcal{D}\textsf{{Perm}}({G};{R})
not only as a quotient, but also as a full subcategory of
\mathcal{K}(\textsf{{Perm}}({G};{R}))
.

Lemma 4.5
.

The stable
\infty
-category
\mathcal{D}\textsf{{Perm}}({G};{R})
is compactly generated with subcategory of compact objects given by the thick subcategory of
\mathcal{K}(\textsf{{Perm}}({G};{R}))
generated by
\textsf{{perm}}(G;R)
:

\mathcal{D}\textsf{{Perm}}({G};{R})^{\omega}\simeq\textnormal{Thick}(\textsf{{perm}}(G;R))\simeq\mathcal{K}_{\textnormal{b}}(\textsf{{perm}}(G;R)^{\natural}).

Proof.

As
\mathcal{K}(\textsf{{Perm}}({G};{R}))
has small coproducts,
Lur17
, Remark 1.4.4.1
implies that compactness in and compact generation of
\mathcal{K}(\textsf{{Perm}}({G};{R}))
can be studied in terms of the homotopy category
\textnormal{K}(\textsf{{Perm}}({G};{R}))
. Given this, the result again follows from the Neeman-Thomason localisation theorem, see
BG23
, Corollary 3.10
.
∎

Remark 4.6
.

It follows that
\mathcal{D}\textsf{{Perm}}({G};{R})
, as a subcategory of the symmetric monoidal
\infty
-category
\mathcal{K}(\textsf{{Perm}}({G};{R}))
, is closed under tensor products, since the compact part
\mathcal{K}_{\textnormal{b}}(\textsf{{Perm}}({G};{R})^{\natural})
is. So
\mathcal{D}\textsf{{Perm}}({G};{R})
inherits a symmetric monoidal structure which makes it into a presentably symmetric monoidal stable
\infty
-category.

Theorem 4.7
.

There is an equivalence of symmetric monoidal
\infty
-categories

\mathcal{D}\textsf{{Perm}}({G};{R})\simeq\mathcal{D}(\textsf{{Mack}}_{R}^{\textsf{{coh}}}(G))

under which the
(G;R)
-permutation module
R(X)
on a finite
G
-set
X
corresponds to the cohomological Mackey functor
\underline{R}(X\times-)
.

Proof.

By
BG23
, Corollary 5.7
there is an equivalence of tt-categories

\textnormal{D}\textsf{{Perm}}({G};{R})\hookrightarrow\textnormal{K}(\textsf{{Perm}}({G};{R}))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textnormal{FP}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\textnormal{K}(\textsf{{Mack}}_{R}^{\textsf{{coh}}}(G))\twoheadrightarrow\textnormal{D}(\textsf{{Mack}}_{R}^{\textsf{{coh}}}(G)),

where
FP
is the fixed point functor defined as

\displaystyle\textnormal{FP}:\;

\displaystyle\textsf{{Mod}}(G;R)\to\textnormal{Fun}_{R\textnormal{-lin}}(\textsf{{perm}}(G;R)^{\textnormal{op}},\textsf{{Mod}}(R))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\ref{lem:comackasperm}}}{{\simeq}}\textsf{{Mack}}_{R}^{\textsf{{coh}}}(G),

\displaystyle M\mapsto\textnormal{Hom}_{\textsf{{Mod}}(G;R)}(-,M)|_{\,\textsf{{perm}}(G;R)}.

It is lax symmetric monoidal,
R
-linear
BG23
, Lemma 5.5 (a)
and by
TW95
, Theorem 16.5
and
BG23
, Proposition 5.6
it restricts to an equivalence of tensor categories
\textsf{{Perm}}({G};{R})^{\natural}\simeq\textnormal{Proj}(\textsf{{Mack}}_{R}^{\textsf{{coh}}}(G))
and
\textsf{{perm}}(G;R)^{\natural}\simeq\textnormal{proj}(\textsf{{Mack}}_{R}^{\textsf{{coh}}}(G))
, where
\textnormal{Proj}(-)
stands for the subcategory of projective objects and
\textnormal{proj}(-)
for the one on compact projective objects. The restriction of

to
\textsf{{Perm}}({G};{R})
induces a lax symmetric monoidal functor

\mathcal{K}(\textsf{{Perm}}({G};{R}))\to\mathcal{K}(\textnormal{Proj}(\textsf{{Mack}}_{R}^{\textsf{{coh}}}(G))),

and there is a canonical symmetric monoidal functor

\mathcal{K}(\textnormal{Proj}(\textsf{{Mack}}_{R}^{\textsf{{coh}}}(G)))\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{D}(\textsf{{Mack}}_{R}^{\textsf{{coh}}}(G))

which inverts all quasi-isomorphisms. The inclusion
\mathcal{D}\textsf{{Perm}}({G};{R})\hookrightarrow\mathcal{K}(\textsf{{Perm}}({G};{R}))

\mathcal{D}\textsf{{Perm}}({G};{R})\hookrightarrow\mathcal{K}(\textsf{{Perm}}({G};{R}))\to\mathcal{K}(\textsf{{Mack}}_{R}^{\textsf{{coh}}}(G))\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{D}(\textsf{{Mack}}_{R}^{\textsf{{coh}}}(G))

which is an equivalence on the level of homotopy categories. So as the outer two

\infty
-categories as well. On compact parts, it restricts to a symmetric monoidal equivalence

\mathcal{K}_{\textnormal{b}}(\textsf{{perm}}(G;R)^{\natural})\simeq\mathcal{K}_{\textnormal{b}}(\textnormal{proj}(\textsf{{Mack}}_{R}^{\textsf{{coh}}}(G))),

since it is induced by the tensor equivalence
\textsf{{perm}}(G;R)^{\natural}\simeq\textnormal{proj}(\textsf{{Mack}}_{R}^{\textsf{{coh}}}(G))
to which

∎

Corollary 4.8
.

There is an equivalence of symmetric monoidal
\infty
-categories

\textsf{{Mod}}_{\textnormal{H}\underline{R}}(\textsf{{Sp}}^{G})\simeq\mathcal{D}\textsf{{Perm}}({G};{R}).

If

is a pointed finite

-permutation modules

5.
Weight Structures

We first recall the definition of weight structures on stable
\infty
-categories and then consider weight exact and weight conservative functors between
\infty
-categories with weight structures. After that, we define a weight structure on the compact part of the
\infty
-category of
\textnormal{H}\underline{R}
-modules in equivariant spectra and identify its weight heart.

Recollection 5.1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a stable
\infty
-category. A
weight structure
on
\mathcal{C}
is a pair of full subcategories
(\mathcal{C}_{w\leq 0},\mathcal{C}_{w\geq 0})
such that the following properties hold:

(1)

\mathcal{C}_{w\leq 0}
and
\mathcal{C}_{w\geq 0}
are closed under retracts in
\mathcal{C}
,

(2)

\textnormal{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(x,\Sigma^{n}y)\cong 0
for all
x\in\mathcal{C}_{w\leq 0}
,
y\in\mathcal{C}_{w\geq 0}
and
n\geq 1
,

(3)

for all
x\in\mathcal{C}
there exists a
weight decomposition
cofibre sequence

x_{w\leq 0}\to x\to x_{w\geq 1}

with
x_{w\leq 0}\in\mathcal{C}_{w\leq 0}
and
\Sigma^{-1}x_{w\geq 1}\in\mathcal{C}_{w\geq 0}
.

The
heart
of the weight structure
(\mathcal{C}_{w\leq 0},\mathcal{C}_{w\geq 0})
is the additive subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
given by the intersection

\mathcal{C}^{w\heartsuit}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\mathcal{C}_{w\leq 0}\cap\mathcal{C}_{w\geq 0}.

For
n\in\mathbb{Z}
we write
\mathcal{C}_{w\leq n}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\Sigma^{n}\mathcal{C}_{w\leq 0}
,
\mathcal{C}_{w\geq n}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\Sigma^{n}\mathcal{C}_{w\leq 0}
and
\mathcal{C}_{w=n}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\mathcal{C}_{w\leq n}\cap\mathcal{C}_{w\geq n}
. The weight structure is
bounded above
if
\mathcal{C}=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{C}_{w\leq n}
, it is
bounded below
if
\mathcal{C}=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{C}_{w\geq n}
, and it is
bounded
if it is both bounded above and bounded below.

Recollection 5.2
.

An exact functor
F:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
between stable
\infty
-categories with weight structures is called
left weight exact
if
F(\mathcal{C}_{w\leq 0})\subseteq\mathcal{D}_{w\leq 0}
,
right weight exact
if
F(\mathcal{C}_{w\geq 0})\subseteq\mathcal{D}_{w\geq 0}
, and
weight exact
if it is both left and right weight exact. It is called
left weight conservative
if for all
x\in\mathcal{C}
,
F(x)\in\mathcal{D}_{w\leq 0}
implies
x\in\mathcal{C}_{w\leq 0}
,
right weight conservative
if for all
x\in\mathcal{C}
,
F(x)\in\mathcal{D}_{w\geq 0}
implies
\mathcal{C}_{w\geq 0}
, and
weight conservative
if it is both left and right weight conservative.

We will now show that weight conservativity of a weight exact functor can be phrased in terms of a condition on the weight hearts, if the weight structure on the source is bounded. To a large extent this follows ideas of Bachmann
Bac16
, Section 5.2.1
. We later apply it to the family of yet to be defined modular fixed point functors followed by restriction to the trivial subgroup, where the family is indexed on the
p
-subgroups of a finite group
G
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a stable
\infty
-category with a weight structure
(\mathcal{C}_{w\leq 0},\mathcal{C}_{w\geq 0})
.

(1)

\mathcal{C}_{w\leq 0}=\{x\in\mathcal{C}\mid\textnormal{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(x,y)=0\textnormal{ for all }y\in\mathcal{C}_{w\geq 1}\}
.

(2)

\mathcal{C}_{w\geq 0}=\{y\in\mathcal{C}\mid\textnormal{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(x,y)=0\textnormal{ for all }x\in\mathcal{C}_{w\leq-1}\}
.

Proof.

We prove (1), assertion (2) is analogous. The inclusion of
\mathcal{C}_{w\leq 0}
into the right-hand side holds by property (2) defining a weight structure. Conversely, let
x\in\mathcal{C}
such that
\textnormal{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(x,y)=0
for all
y\in\mathcal{C}_{w\geq 1}
. Pick a cofibre sequence
x_{w\leq 0}\to x\to x_{w\geq 1}
with
x_{w\leq 0}\in\mathcal{C}_{w\leq 0}
and
\Sigma^{-1}x_{w\geq 1}\in\mathcal{C}_{w\geq 0}
. Then the map
x\to x_{w\geq 1}
is trivial, and hence
x_{w\leq 0}\simeq x\oplus\Sigma^{-1}x_{w\geq 1}
. So
x
is a retract of an object in
\mathcal{C}_{w\leq 0}
and thus itself in
\mathcal{C}_{w\leq 0}
.
∎

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a stable
\infty
-category with a weight structure
(\mathcal{C}_{w\leq 0},\mathcal{C}_{w\geq 0})
.

(1)

If
f:x\to y
is a morphism with
x,y\in\mathcal{C}_{w\leq n}
then
\textnormal{cofib}(f)\in\mathcal{C}_{w\leq n+1}
.

(2)

If
x\to y\to z
is a cofibre sequence with
x,z\in\mathcal{C}_{w\leq n}
then
y\in\mathcal{C}_{w\leq n}
as well.

(3)

A cofibre sequence
x\to y\to z
with
x\in\mathcal{C}_{w\geq n}
and
z\in\mathcal{C}_{w\leq n}
splits.

Proof.

(1) We need to show that
\textnormal{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(\textnormal{cofib}(f),t)=0
for all
t\in\mathcal{C}_{w\geq n+2}
. We have an exact sequence

\pi_{1}\textnormal{Map}_{\mathcal{C}}(x,t)\to\pi_{0}\textnormal{Map}_{\mathcal{C}}(\textnormal{cofib}(f),t)\to\pi_{0}\textnormal{Map}_{\mathcal{C}}(y,t).

The right term vanishes by definition and
\pi_{1}\textnormal{Map}_{\mathcal{C}}(x,t)\simeq\pi_{0}\textnormal{Map}_{\mathcal{C}}(\Sigma x,t)=0
. So
\textnormal{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(\textnormal{cofib}(f),t)=0
as well.

(2) For
t\in\mathcal{C}_{w\geq n+1}
we have an exact sequence with vanishing outer terms

\textnormal{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(z,t)\to\textnormal{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(y,t)\to\textnormal{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(x,t).

(3) The cofibre sequence
y\to z\to\Sigma x
gives an exact sequence

\textnormal{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(z,y)\to\textnormal{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(z,z)\to\textnormal{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(z,\Sigma x).

Since
\textnormal{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(z,\Sigma x)=0
,
\textnormal{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(z,y)\to\textnormal{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(z,z)
is surjective and hence
y\to z
has a section.
∎

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a stable
\infty
-category with a weight structure
(\mathcal{C}_{w\leq 0},\mathcal{C}_{w\geq 0})
. Then a morphism
f:x\to y
with
x\in\mathcal{C}_{w=n}
and
y\in\mathcal{C}_{w\leq n}
admits a retraction if and only if
\textnormal{cofib}(f)\in\mathcal{C}_{w\leq n}
.

Proof.

By
5.4
(1) we have a cofibre sequence

x\to y\to\textnormal{cofib}(f)

for which
x\in\mathcal{C}_{w=n}
,
y\in\mathcal{C}_{w\leq n}
and
\textnormal{cofib}(f)\in\mathcal{C}_{w\leq n+1}
. If
f
admits a retraction the sequence splits and
\textnormal{cofib}(f)
is a retract of
y
. Hence
\textnormal{cofib}(f)\in\mathcal{C}_{w\leq n}
. Conversely, if
\textnormal{cofib}(f)\in\mathcal{C}_{w\leq n}
then
5.4
(3) implies that the sequence splits, and so
f
admits a retraction.
∎

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a stable
\infty
-category with a weight structure

∎

At this point we could show that

is not only contained in but equal to the kernel of the composition
\beta\circ\lambda:\Lambda(G;k)\to\textnormal{CF}(G)\to D^{\Omega}(G)
. Instead of doing so we will use a topological argument to show surjectivity of the induced homomorphism

\theta:\textnormal{Pic}(\textsf{{Mod}}_{\textnormal{H}\underline{k}}(\textsf{{Sp}}^{G}))\to\textnormal{CF}_{\textnormal{b}}(G)

which relies on work of tom Dieck on dimension functions of orthogonal representations
Die87
, Section III.5
, which in turn builds on work of Atiyah-Tall and Serre on Adams operations on
RO(G)
.

Theorem 7.8
.

The map
\theta:\textnormal{Pic}(\textsf{{Mod}}_{\textnormal{H}\underline{k}}(\textsf{{Sp}}^{G}))\to\textnormal{CF}_{\textnormal{b}}(G)
is an isomorphism.

Proof.

We need to show that
\theta
is surjective. For a real orthogonal
G
-representation
V
there is an associated dimension class function
\textnormal{dim}(V):(H)\mapsto\textnormal{dim}_{\mathbb{R}}(V^{H})
. The assignment
V\mapsto\textnormal{dim}(V)
uniquely extends to a homomorphism of additive groups on the ring of real orthogonal
G
-representations
\textnormal{dim}:RO(G)\to\textnormal{CF}(G)
. Then using results of Smith and Borel
Die87
, Section III.4
about fixed points of finite
G
-CW complexes which are
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-cohomology spheres this dimension homomorphism lands in the Borel-Smith class functions
\textnormal{CF}_{\textnormal{b}}(G)
, see
Die87
before Definition III.5.1.

Mapping a real orthogonal
G
-representation
V
to its representation sphere
S^{V}
also extends to a group homomorphism
\eta:RO(G)\to\textnormal{Pic}(\textsf{{Sp}}^{G})
. The constructed map
\theta:\textnormal{Pic}(\textsf{{Mod}}_{\textnormal{H}\underline{k}}(\textsf{{Sp}}^{G}))\to\textnormal{CF}_{\textnormal{b}}(G)
sends the invertible
\textnormal{H}\underline{k}
-module
\textnormal{H}\underline{k}\otimes S^{V}
to the class function mapping a subgroup
H\leq G
to the unique degree in which the cellular chain complex of
({G}/\!\!/{H};k)
-permutation modules for
(S^{V})^{H}\cong S^{V^{H}}
has nonzero homology. But this degree is precisely the real dimension of
V^{H}
. Hence we obtain a factorisation

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{RO(G)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\textnormal{CF}_{\textnormal{b}}(G)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\textnormal{Pic}(\textsf{{Sp}}^{G})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\textnormal{Pic}(\textsf{{Mod}}_{\textnormal{H}\underline{k}}(\textsf{{Sp}}^{G})),}}
dim
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\eta}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F_{\textnormal{H}\underline{k}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\theta}

where
F_{\textnormal{H}\underline{k}}
is the homomorphism induced by the free functor on Picard groups. By
Die87
, Theorem III.5.4
the top map is surjective, and hence
\theta
is surjective as well. This finishes the proof.
∎

Remark 7.9
.

From the isomorphism
\textnormal{Pic}(\textsf{{Mod}}_{\textnormal{H}\underline{k}}(\textsf{{Sp}}^{G}))\cong\textnormal{CF}_{\textnormal{b}}(G)
which holds when
G
is a
p
-group one can deduce an isomorphism

\textnormal{Pic}(\textsf{{Mod}}_{\textnormal{H}\underline{k}}(\textsf{{Sp}}^{G}))\cong\textnormal{CF}_{\textnormal{b}}(G)\times\textnormal{Hom}(G,k^{\times})

which holds for arbitrary finite groups
G
. It requires showing that restriction to a certain
G
-stable
subgroup of
\textnormal{Pic}(\textsf{{Mod}}_{\textnormal{H}\underline{k}}(\textsf{{Sp}}^{G}))
is surjective
Mil24a
, Theorem 12.6
. We refer to
Mil24b
, Section 6.1
, where the proof is given in the language of representation theory.

The result for
p
-groups still formally extends to pro-
p
-groups.

Corollary 7.10
.

Let
G=\lim_{i\in I}G_{i}
be a pro-
p
-group (so all
G_{i}=G/N_{i}
are
p
-groups). Then

\textnormal{Pic}(\textsf{{Mod}}_{\textnormal{H}\underline{k}}(\textsf{{Sp}}^{G}))\cong\textnormal{CF}_{\textnormal{b}}(G),

where
\textnormal{CF}_{\textnormal{b}}(G)
is the subgroup of
\textnormal{CF}_{\textnormal{o}}(G)
, the group of integral class functions on the conjugacy classes of open pro-subgroups of
G
, which consists of class functions
h
for which there exists an open normal subgroup
N\unlhd G
such that
h
lies in
\textnormal{CF}_{\textnormal{b}}(G/N)
when restricted to
\textnormal{Sub}(G/N)/(G/N)
.

Proof.

Consider the equivalence
\textsf{{Mod}}_{\textnormal{H}\underline{k}}(\textsf{{Sp}}^{G})\simeq\textnormal{colim}_{i}\,\textsf{{Mod}}_{\textnormal{H}\underline{k}}(\textsf{{Sp}}^{G_{i}})
, where the colimit is taken in stable presentably symmetric monoidal
\infty
-categories
\textnormal{Pr}^{\textnormal{L},\otimes}_{\textnormal{st}}
. Since the colimit diagram lies in compactly generated
\infty
-categories we can restrict to compact parts and obtain an equivalence
\textsf{{Mod}}^{\omega}_{\textnormal{H}\underline{k}}(\textsf{{Sp}}^{G})\simeq\textnormal{colim}_{i}\,\textsf{{Mod}}^{\omega}_{\textnormal{H}\underline{k}}(\textsf{{Sp}}^{G_{i}})
, where the colimit is taken in stable idempotent complete
\infty
-categories
\textnormal{Cat}_{\infty}^{\textnormal{perf},\otimes}
. As the diagram is filtered it is computed in symmetric monoidal
\infty
-categories
\textsf{{Cat}}_{\infty}^{\otimes}
, where it commutes with Picard groups. So
7.8
gives that
\textnormal{Pic}(\textsf{{Mod}}_{\textnormal{H}\underline{k}}(\textsf{{Sp}}^{G}))\cong\textnormal{colim}_{i}\,\textnormal{CF}_{b}(G_{i})
, and it remains to reformulate the right-hand colimit.

The maps
\textnormal{CF}_{\textnormal{b}}(G_{i})\to\textnormal{CF}_{\textnormal{o}}(G),\;h\mapsto((H)\mapsto h(HN_{i}/N_{i}))
obtained by precomposition with
\alpha_{i}:G\twoheadrightarrow G_{i}
are injective, since every subgroup of
G_{i}
is of the form
HN_{i}/N_{i}
for some open pro-subgroup
H\leq G
. One checks that the transition maps
\textnormal{CF}_{\textnormal{b}}(G_{j})\to\textnormal{CF}_{\textnormal{b}}(G_{i})
induced by the
f_{ij}^{*}:\textsf{{Mod}}_{\textnormal{H}\underline{k}}(\textsf{{Sp}}^{G_{j}})\to\textsf{{Mod}}_{\textnormal{H}\underline{k}}(\textsf{{Sp}}^{G_{i}})
on Picard groups are the maps
\textnormal{CF}_{\textnormal{b}}(G_{j})\to\textnormal{CF}_{\textnormal{b}}(G_{i}),\;h\mapsto((H/N_{i})\mapsto h(HN_{j}/N_{j}))
. They are injective since every subgroup of
G_{j}
is of the form
HN_{j}/N_{j}
for some subgroup
H\leq G_{i}
, and the maps
\textnormal{CF}_{\textnormal{b}}(G_{i})\to\textnormal{CF}_{\textnormal{o}}(G)
for
i\in I
are compatible with them, i.e. whenever
f_{ij}\circ\alpha_{i}=\alpha_{j}
the corresponding triangle of class function groups commutes. This shows that
\textnormal{colim}_{i}\,\textnormal{CF}_{b}(G_{i})
is given by the union
\bigcup_{i\in I}\textnormal{CF}_{\textnormal{b}}(G_{i})
inside
\textnormal{CF}_{\textnormal{o}}(G)
. For each open normal subgroup
N\unlhd G
the surjective homomorphism
\alpha:G\twoheadrightarrow G/N
induces an injection
\alpha^{-1}:\textnormal{Sub}(G/N)/(G/N)\hookrightarrow\textnormal{Sub}_{\textnormal{o}}(G)/G,\;(H/N)\mapsto(H)
by taking preimages, where the codomain is the set of conjugacy classes of open pro-subgroups of
G
. Since every open normal subgroup of
G
is of the form
N_{i}
for some
i\in I
it is clear that the subgroups
\bigcup_{i\in I}\textnormal{CF}_{\textnormal{b}}(G_{i})
and
\textnormal{CF}_{\textnormal{b}}(G)
of
\textnormal{CF}_{\textnormal{o}}(G)
agree.
∎

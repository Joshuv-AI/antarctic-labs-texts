The sum-product conjecture, a cornerstone of additive combinatorics, states (in its classical form) that, for any finite set of integers
A\subset\mathbb{Z}
,

\max(\left\lvert A+A\right\rvert,\left\lvert AA\right\rvert)\geqslant\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2-o(1)}.

This is often attributed to Erdős and Szemerédi, who proved the first results in this direction
[
5
]
, but it seems to have first appeared in the literature in a problems list of Erdős in 1977
[
6
]
. The best-known bounds in this direction for arbitrary sets of integers have an exponent of the shape
\frac{4}{3}+c
for some small constant
c>0
- we refer to
[
3
]
for more on the history and the latest progress in this direction.

This paper is concerned with the sum-product phenomenon under the assumption that every
n\in A
has few prime factors. This variant was first studied by Hanson, Rudnev, Shkredov, and Zhelezov
[
8
]
, who showed that this assumption allows for much stronger bounds, proving the following.

Theorem 1

(Hanson, Rudnev, Shkredov, and Zhelezov
[
8
]
)
.

Let
A\subset\mathbb{Z}
be a finite set of integers and let
k\geqslant 1
be a fixed integer. If
\omega(n)\leqslant k
for all
n\in A
then

\max(\left\lvert A+A\right\rvert,\left\lvert AA\right\rvert)\gg\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{5/3-o(1)}.

The exponent
5/3
is, in a sense, a natural barrier of the method used in
[
8
]
. Indeed, they actually prove the stronger result that either
\left\lvert AA\right\rvert\gg\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{5/3-o(1)}
or the additive energy
1
1

1

We review standard definitions and notation in Section
1
.

E(A)
is at most
\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{7/3+o(1)}
(which by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality implies
\left\lvert A+A\right\rvert\geqslant\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{5/3-o(1)}
). An example of Balog and Wooley
[
2
]
(that we give in Section
2
) shows that the exponent
5/3
is best possible for this stronger statement with additive energy.

In this paper we nonetheless give an improvement for the original statement, replacing the exponent
5/3
with
17/10
, pushing past the Balog-Wooley barrier.

Theorem 2
.

Let
A\subset\mathbb{Z}
be a finite set of integers and let
k\geqslant 1
be a fixed integer. If
\omega(n)\leqslant k
for all
n\in A
then

\max(\left\lvert A+A\right\rvert,\left\lvert AA\right\rvert)\gg\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{17/10-o(1)}.

For brevity we have stated this with
k
some fixed integer, but (just as in
[
8
]
) the method works, and delivers the same exponent, as long as
k=o(\frac{\log\left\lvert A\right\rvert}{\log\log\left\lvert A\right\rvert})
. A precise quantitative statement is given in Theorem
7
. We also note that if one replaces
A+A
by
A-A
then a variant of our method allows for a slightly improved exponent of
\frac{29}{17}=\frac{17}{10}+\frac{1}{170}
.

Those familiar with
[
8
]
may also be interested to note that we have found a simplification of their method that allows one to completely avoid the use of Chang’s lemma and any kind of martingale technology, which played a crucial role in
[
8
]
. This is instead replaced with an application of Hölder’s inequality to amplify the consequences of the bounds on
S
-unit equations. We take the opportunity to give this simplified proof of the main result of
[
8
]
in Section
5
.

Another popular problem in the sum-product family asks about the behaviour of iterated sum and product sets
mA=A+\cdots+A
and
A^{m}=A\cdots A
as
m\to\infty
. A natural generalisation of the original sum-product conjecture (also mentioned in
[
5
]
) is that, for any finite set of integers
A\subset\mathbb{Z}
and any
m\geqslant 1
,

\max(\left\lvert mA\right\rvert,\left\lvert A^{m}\right\rvert)\geqslant\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{(1-o(1))m}.

The first non-trivial results in this direction were achieved by Bourgain and Chang
[
4
]
, who proved that

\max(\left\lvert mA\right\rvert,\left\lvert A^{m}\right\rvert)\geqslant\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{f(m)}

for some function
f(m)
which
\to\infty
as
m\to\infty
. The best-known bounds known for arbitrary finite sets of integers, due to Pálvölgyi and Zhelezov
[
9
]
, allow one to take
f(m)\gg\frac{\log m}{\log\log m}
.

Our second main result in this paper is that, under the same assumption that all
n\in A
have few prime factors, we can establish such a lower bound with
f(m)\gg m
.

Theorem 3
.

Let
A\subset\mathbb{Z}
be a finite set of integers and let
k\geqslant 1
be a fixed integer. If
\omega(n)\leqslant k
for all
n\in A
then for all
m\geqslant 1

\max(\left\lvert mA\right\rvert,\left\lvert A^{m}\right\rvert)\gg\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{\frac{2}{3}m+\frac{1}{3}-o(m)}.

As above, these results are valid up to
k=o(\frac{\log\left\lvert A\right\rvert}{\log\log\left\lvert A\right\rvert})
. A full precise quantitative statement is given in Theorem
8
. Note that the case
m=2
recovers Theorem
1
of Hanson, Rudnev, Shkredov, and Zhelezov above. By incorporating some of the ideas in the proof of Theorem
2
the constant term
1/3
can be improved slightly.

After reviewing basic definitions, in Section
2
we will sketch the main ideas of the proofs. In Section
3
we will transfer the property of having few prime factors to being efficiently covered by a multiplicative group (in a similar fashion as
[
8
]
) and finally in Sections
4
and
5
we will show how being efficiently covered by a multiplicative group leads to strong lower bounds for the sumset.

1.
Definitions

All sets considered in this paper will be finite sets of some fixed abelian group (usually either
\mathbb{C}
,
\mathbb{Q}
, or
\mathbb{Z}
). We write
1_{A}
for the indicator function of
A
, and define the convolution and difference convolution by

1_{A}\ast 1_{B}(x)=\sum_{b\in B}1_{A}(x-b)\textrm{ and }1_{A}\circ 1_{B}(x)=\sum_{b\in B}1_{A}(x+b).

We consider
L^{p}
norms with the counting measure, so that for any
p\geqslant 1

\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{p}^{p}=\sum_{x}\lvert f(x)\rvert^{p}\textrm{ and }\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\infty}=\max\lvert f(x)\rvert.

We will only ever consider functions with finite support, so we are free from worries about convergence. The sum set and product set of
A
and
B
are defined by

A+B=\{a+b:a\in A\textrm{ and }b\in B\}\quad\textrm{ and }\quad AB=\{ab:a\in A\textrm{ and }b\in B\}

respectively. For any
m\geqslant 1
the iterated sum and product sets of
A
are defined by

mA=\{a_{1}+\cdots+a_{m}:a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}\in A\}

and

A^{m}=\{a_{1}\cdots a_{m}:a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}\in A\}.

The additive energy between
A
and
B
is defined by

E(A,B)=\left\lVert 1_{A}\circ 1_{B}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}=\#\{(a_{1},a_{2},b_{1},b_{2})\in A^{2}\times B^{2}:a_{1}-a_{2}=b_{1}-b_{2}\}.

We write
E(A)=E(A,B)
. The higher additive energies are defined, for any
m\geqslant 1
, by

E_{2m}(A)=\sum_{x}1_{A}^{(m)}(x)^{2}=\#\{(a_{1},\ldots,a_{2m})\in A^{2m}:a_{1}+\cdots+a_{m}=a_{m+1}+\cdots+a_{2m}\},

where
1_{A}^{(m)}=1_{A}\ast\cdots\ast 1_{A}
is the
m
-fold iterated convolution. We note here that
E(A)=E_{4}(A)
and
E_{2}(A)=\left\lvert A\right\rvert
. The fundamental link between additive energies and sum sets is provided by Hölder’s inequality which implies, for any
m\geqslant 1
,

(1)

\lvert mA\rvert E_{2m}(A)\geqslant\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2m}.

For any prime
p
and integer
n\in\mathbb{Z}\backslash\{0\}
we write
\nu_{p}(n)
for the
p
-adic valuation of
n
, so that
\nu_{p}(n)=k
if
p^{k}\mid n
and
p^{k+1}\nmid n
. For any integer
n\in\mathbb{Z}\backslash\{0\}
the arithmetic function
\omega(n)
counts the number of distinct prime divisors of
n
– that is, the number of
p
such that
\nu_{p}(n)\neq 0
.

It is convenient to extend these definitions to
\mathbb{Q}\backslash\{0\}
. To that end, for any
x\in\mathbb{Q}\backslash\{0\}
and prime
p
, if
x=a/b
then
\nu_{p}(x)=\nu_{p}(a)-\nu_{p}(b)
, and
\omega(x)
counts the number of distinct primes appearing in
x
when written in reduced form – that is, the number of
p
such that
\nu_{p}(x)\neq 0
. Similarly, it is convenient to slightly abuse notation and write
p\mid x
if
\nu_{p}(x)\neq 0
. We will write
P(x)
for the set of such primes, so that, for example,
\omega(x)=\left\lvert P(x)\right\rvert
.

If
S
is any finite set of primes then
\mathbb{Q}_{S}\subset\mathbb{Q}
is the set of non-zero rationals in which the only primes are those from
S
– that is,

\mathbb{Q}_{S}=\left\{x\in\mathbb{Q}\backslash\{0\}:p\mid x\implies p\in S\right\}.

The rank of a multiplicative group
\Gamma\leqslant\mathbb{C}^{\times}
is the smallest
r
such that there exist
\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{r}\in\Gamma
which generate
\Gamma
. Note in particular that
\mathbb{Q}_{S}
is a multiplicative subgroup of
\mathbb{Q}^{\times}
with rank
\left\lvert S\right\rvert
.

The general theme of this paper is that sets which are efficiently covered by a bounded rank multiplicative group lack additive structure. To make this precise, the following definition is convenient.

Definition 1
.

A finite set
A\subset\mathbb{C}^{\times}
is
M
-covered by a rank
r
multiplicative group if there exists a multiplicative group
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{C}^{\times}
of rank
r
and a finite set
B
of size
\left\lvert B\right\rvert\leqslant M
such that
A\subseteq\Gamma\cdot B
.

2.
Sketch of the proofs

In this section we will give a sketch of the proofs of our main results. For simplicity, we will work with a fixed set of integers
A
in which
\omega(n)\ll 1
for every
n\in A
. Let
M=\left\lvert AA\right\rvert/\left\lvert A\right\rvert
and
K=\left\lvert A+A\right\rvert/\left\lvert A\right\rvert
. For the purposes of this sketch we will not bother to record factors of the shape
\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{o(1)}
, including them in the
\ll
notation.

The argument of
[
8
]
can be summarised as follows.

(1)

By a combinatorial argument there exists a set of primes
S
with
\left\lvert S\right\rvert\ll 1
and a large subset
A^{\prime}\subseteq A
such that
A^{\prime}
is contained in
O(M)
many dilates of
\mathbb{Q}_{S}
, say
A^{\prime}\subseteq\mathbb{Q}_{S}\cdot B
. Note that in particular we can write
A^{\prime}
as the disjoint union of dilates
c\cdot B_{c}
for some
c\in\mathbb{Q}_{S}
and
B_{c}\subseteq B
.

(2)

Generalising a lemma due to Chang, using ideas from martingale theory, they prove that

E(A)\ll\sum_{c_{1},c_{2}\in\mathbb{Q}_{S}}E(c_{1}B_{c_{1}},c_{2}B_{c_{2}}).

(3)

Importing a deep quantitative bound from the theory of
S
-unit equations they prove that the right-hand side is

\ll\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}+\left\lvert A\right\rvert\left\lvert B\right\rvert^{2}.

(4)

It follows that

E(A)\ll\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}+\left\lvert A\right\rvert M^{2},

which implies (via
E(A)\geqslant\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{3}/K
) the bound
\max(K,M)\gg\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2/3}
.

The first part of our argument is the same as that in
[
8
]
: we pass to a large subset
A^{\prime}\subseteq A
which is contained in
O(M)
many dilates of
\mathbb{Q}_{S}
, which is a rank
O(1)
-multiplicative group. This part of the argument is essentially unchanged from
[
8
]
, although we take the opportunity to streamline the presentation.

Rather than then taking a detour through martingale theory however, we will seek to apply the bounds from
S
-unit theory directly. Roughly speaking, these imply that, if
\Gamma
is a fixed multiplicative group of rank
r=O(1)
and
a_{0},\ldots,a_{m}\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
are fixed, the number of solutions to

a_{1}z_{1}+\cdots+a_{m}z_{m}=a_{0}

which are non-degenerate (in that no subsum on the left-hand side vanishes) is
O_{m,r}(1)
.

Applying this bound with
m=3
and taking the sum of such bounds over all
M
relevant dilates of
\mathbb{Q}_{S}
this immediately implies that

E(A^{\prime})\ll\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}+\left\lvert A\right\rvert M^{3}.

This alone only implies
\max(K,M)\gg\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{1/2}
. We can amplify this bound, however, using the fact that, by Hölder’s inequality,

E(A^{\prime})\leqslant\left\lvert A\right\rvert E_{2m}(A^{\prime})^{\frac{1}{m-1}}

as
m\to\infty
. The
S
-unit bound yields
E_{2m}(A^{\prime})\ll_{m}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{m}+\left\lvert A\right\rvert M^{2m-1}
, whence we immediately get the improved bound

E(A^{\prime})\ll\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}+\left\lvert A\right\rvert M^{\frac{2m-1}{m-1}}=\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}+\left\lvert A\right\rvert M^{2+o(1)}.

This recovers the energy bound of
[
8
]
cited above, but without the detour through a Chang-type lemma.

We now turn to the proofs of our new results, Theorems
2
and
3
. We first discuss Theorem
3
. Suppose that
m
is large and
\left\lvert A^{m}\right\rvert\leqslant\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{\frac{2}{3}m+\frac{1}{3}}
. By the pigeonhole principle there exists some
1\leqslant i<m
such that if
B=A^{i}
then
\left\lvert AB\right\rvert\leqslant\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{\frac{2}{3}}\left\lvert B\right\rvert
. Performing an asymmetric generalisation of the combinatorial decomposition mentioned above we may use this to find some large subset
A^{\prime}\subseteq A
which is contained in
O(\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2/3})
many dilates of a multiplicative group of rank
O(1)
.

As above, the bounds from the theory of
S
-unit equations coupled with Hölder’s inequality yield that

E_{2m}(A^{\prime})\ll\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{m}+\left\lvert A\right\rvert(\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2/3})^{2m-2}\ll\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{\frac{4}{3}m-\frac{1}{3}},

whence

\left\lvert mA\right\rvert\geqslant\left\lvert mA^{\prime}\right\rvert\gg\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2m-\frac{4}{3}m+\frac{1}{3}}=\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{\frac{2}{3}m+\frac{1}{3}}

as required.

Finally, we turn to the headline result of how to obtain an exponent for

\max(\left\lvert A+A\right\rvert,\left\lvert AA\right\rvert)

larger than
5/3
. This is a more delicate matter, since as mentioned earlier the inequality

\max(\left\lvert AA\right\rvert,\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{4}E(A^{\prime})^{-1})\gg\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{5/3}

for some large
A^{\prime}\subseteq A
proved by the above method of
[
8
]
cannot be improved. This follows by the following example of Balog and Wooley
[
2
]
.

Let
P=\{1,\ldots,M\}
and
G=\{M,M^{2},\ldots,M^{N}\}
and let
A=PG
. It is easy to check (certainly if
M
is a large prime for example) that
\left\lvert A\right\rvert\approx MN
. On one hand,

\left\lvert AA\right\rvert\leqslant\left\lvert P\right\rvert^{2}\left\lvert GG\right\rvert\ll M^{2}N\ll M\left\lvert A\right\rvert.

On the other hand, if
A^{\prime}\subseteq A
is large then
A^{\prime}
must densely intersect many dilates of
P
– that is,
\left\lvert A^{\prime}\cap(gP)\right\rvert\gg M
for
\gg N
many
g\in G
. Note that

E(A^{\prime}\cap(gP))\gg\frac{M^{4}}{\left\lvert gP+gP\right\rvert}\gg M^{3}

and hence, summing this over
\gg N
many
g\in G
, we have
E(A^{\prime})\gg NM^{3}\gg\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{4}/N^{3}M
. Choosing
M\approx\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2/3}
and
N\approx\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{1/3}
we see that, for any
A^{\prime}\subseteq A
with
\left\lvert A^{\prime}\right\rvert\gg\left\lvert A\right\rvert
,

\max(\left\lvert AA\right\rvert,\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{4}E(A^{\prime})^{-1})\ll\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{5/3}

and hence the bound of
[
8
]
is sharp.

To go beyond the exponent of
5/3
we therefore need to find a method that bounds, not the additive energy, but the sumset directly. This is provided by the toolkit of higher additive energies (which has been extensively developed by Shkredov in particular in a series of papers). The three ingredients required are:

(1)

a good upper bound for the higher additive energy
E_{8}(A)
,

(2)

a good upper bound for
\sum_{x}1_{A}\circ 1_{A}(x)^{4}
(which we accomplish by giving a good pointwise upper bound for
1_{A}\circ 1_{A}
and using the previous bound for
E(A)
), and

(3)

a good lower bound for
\left\lvert A+A\right\rvert
in terms of
E_{8}(A)
and
\sum 1_{A}\circ 1_{A}(x)^{4}
.

For the third we may use a lemma due to Shkredov (see Lemma
6
below), which in particular implies that

(2)

\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{16}\leqslant\left\lvert A+A\right\rvert^{4}E_{8}(A)\sum 1_{A}\circ 1_{A}(x)^{4}.

For the first we note that the
S
-unit equation bound coupled with Hölder’s inequality yields, as above,

E_{8}(A)\ll\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{4}+\left\lvert A\right\rvert M^{6}.

Finally, to bound
1_{A}\circ 1_{A}(x)
for fixed
x\neq 0
we will also use the
S
-unit equation bound. A naive direct application of this bound implies that, if
A
is contain in
M
many dilates of a multiplicative group of rank
O(1)
, then

1_{A}\circ 1_{A}(x)\ll M^{2}

for any
x\neq 0
. This is too weak for our purposes, and hence (as with our bounds for additive energy) we need amplify the naive argument for employing the
S
-unit equation bound, this time by employing a graph theoretic argument that is a generalisation of an argument of Roche-Newton and Zhelezov
[
10
]
. This yields

1_{A}\circ 1_{A}(x)\ll M

for any
x\neq 0
, and hence, coupled with our bounds on
E(A)
above,

\sum 1_{A}\circ 1_{A}(x)^{4}\ll\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{4}+M^{2}\left(\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}+\left\lvert A\right\rvert M^{2}\right)\ll\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{4}+M^{4}\left\lvert A\right\rvert.

We may assume that
M\ll\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{3/4}
or else we are done, and hence we conclude that
\sum 1_{A}\circ 1_{A}(x)^{4}\ll\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{4}
. Employing these bounds in (
2
) above yields

\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{16}\ll\left\lvert A+A\right\rvert^{4}(\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{4}+\left\lvert A\right\rvert M^{6})\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{4},

and hence, with
K=\left\lvert A+A\right\rvert/\left\lvert A\right\rvert
,

\max(M,K)^{10}\gg\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{7}

whence

\max(\left\lvert A+A\right\rvert,\left\lvert AA\right\rvert)\gg\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{17/10}

as desired.

3.
Finding a subset with small multiplicative dimension

Following the proof of
[
8
]
, the first stage is to show that
\omega(n)\ll 1
for all
n\in A
implies that
A
is contained in few cosets of a multiplicative group of bounded rank. In this section we give a variant of their argument suitable for our purposes – the essential ideas of the proofs are the same, but we take the opportunity to streamline the proofs and give an asymmetric version that will be required for our application to many sums and products. In fact, for possible future applications, we will give two paths (both present in
[
8
]
). Also with a view to possible future applications, we will work with subsets of
\mathbb{Q}
rather than
\mathbb{Z}
, although for the applications in this paper all sets can be taken (without loss of generality) to be subsets of
\mathbb{Z}
.

The first step is to show that if
\omega(n)\ll 1
for all
n\in A
then there is a set
S
of
O(1)
many primes such that a large proportion of pairs
(a_{1},a_{2})\in A
have only primes from
S
in common.

Lemma 1
.

Let
A,B\subset\mathbb{Q}\backslash\{0\}
be finite sets such that
\omega(n)\leqslant k
for all
n\in A
and
\omega(n)\leqslant\ell
for all
n\in B
. The following both hold:

(1)

There is a set of primes
S
with
\left\lvert S\right\rvert\leqslant 2k\ell
such that

\sum_{a\in A}\sum_{b\in B}1_{P(a)\cap P(b)\subseteq S}\geqslant\frac{1}{2}\left\lvert A\right\rvert\left\lvert B\right\rvert.

(2)

There is a set of primes
S
with
\left\lvert S\right\rvert\leqslant k
and
A^{\prime}\subseteq A
of size

\left\lvert A^{\prime}\right\rvert\geqslant(2\ell)^{-k}\left\lvert A\right\rvert

such that

\sum_{a\in A^{\prime}}\sum_{b\in B}1_{P(a)\cap P(b)\subseteq S}\geqslant\frac{1}{2}\left\lvert A^{\prime}\right\rvert\left\lvert B\right\rvert.

Proof.

For the first part, let
S
be the set of all primes such that
\sum_{n\in A}1_{p\mid n}\geqslant\left\lvert A\right\rvert/2\ell
. On one hand,

\frac{\left\lvert A\right\rvert}{2\ell}\left\lvert S\right\rvert\leqslant\sum_{p\in S}\sum_{n\in A}1_{p\mid n}=\sum_{n\in A}\sum_{p\in S}1_{p\mid n}\leqslant k\left\lvert A\right\rvert,

and hence
\left\lvert S\right\rvert\leqslant 2k\ell
. On the other hand, for any prime
p\not\in S
,

\sum_{a\in A}\sum_{b\in B}1_{p\in P(a)\cap P(b)}=\left(\sum_{a\in A}1_{p\mid a}\right)\left(\sum_{b\in B}1_{p\mid b}\right)<\frac{\left\lvert A\right\rvert}{2\ell}\sum_{b\in B}1_{p\mid b}.

It follows that

\sum_{a\in A}\sum_{b\in B}1_{P(a)\cap P(b)\not\subseteq S}\leqslant\sum_{p\not\in S}\sum_{a\in A}\sum_{b\in B}1_{p\in P(a)\cap P(b)}<\frac{\left\lvert A\right\rvert}{2\ell}\sum_{b\in B}\sum_{p\not\in S}1_{p\mid b}\leqslant\tfrac{1}{2}\left\lvert A\right\rvert\left\lvert B\right\rvert,

and the conclusion follows.

For the second part, let
S
be a maximal set of primes such that, with
\left\lvert S\right\rvert=r
, there is a subset
A^{\prime}\subseteq A
of size
\left\lvert A^{\prime}\right\rvert\geqslant\left\lvert A\right\rvert/(2\ell)^{r}
with
p\mid n
for all
n\in A^{\prime}
and
p\in S
. (Note that such a maximal
S
certainly exists, since
S=\emptyset
satisfies the conditions and
\left\lvert S\right\rvert\leqslant k
for any such
S
by assumption.)

By maximality of
S
, for any prime
p\not\in S
,

\sum_{a\in A^{\prime}}\sum_{b\in B}1_{p\in P(a)\cap P(b)}=\left(\sum_{a\in A^{\prime}}1_{p\mid a}\right)\sum_{b\in B}1_{p\mid b}<\frac{\left\lvert A^{\prime}\right\rvert}{2\ell}\sum_{b\in B}1_{p\mid b},

and the rest of the proof proceeds as above.
∎

Secondly, we show that if there are many pairs in
A^{2}
who only share primes from
S
then either
AA
is large or
A
is contained in few cosets of
\mathbb{Q}_{S}
. The key idea (from
[
8
]
) is the observation that if
a
and
b
are coprime integers, each with
O(1)
prime factors, then
a
and
b
can be recovered from knowledge of
ab
at a cost of
O(1)
– simply by fixing which primes present in
ab
belong in
a
or
b
. This implies, by a simple counting argument, that if there are
\gg\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}
many pairs
a,b\in A
with
(a,b)=1
(and
\omega(n)\ll 1
for all
n\in A
) then
\left\lvert AA\right\rvert\gg\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}
. The precise argument below is a quantitative form of this, after ‘factoring out’ by
\mathbb{Q}_{S}
.

Lemma 2
.

Let
A,B\subset\mathbb{Q}\backslash\{0\}
be finite sets such that
\omega(n)\leqslant k
for all
n\in A
and
\omega(n)\leqslant\ell
for all
n\in B
. Suppose
S
is a set of primes such that

\sum_{a\in A}\sum_{b\in B}1_{P(a)\cap P(b)\subseteq S}\geqslant\frac{1}{2}\left\lvert A\right\rvert\left\lvert B\right\rvert.

There is a subset
A^{\prime}\subseteq A
of size
\left\lvert A^{\prime}\right\rvert\gg\left\lvert A\right\rvert
and a set
C
such that

\left\lvert C\right\rvert\ll 2^{k+\ell}\frac{\left\lvert AB\right\rvert}{\left\lvert B\right\rvert}

and

A^{\prime}\subseteq\mathbb{Q}_{S}\cdot C.

Proof.

Every
x\in\mathbb{Q}\backslash\{0\}
can be written uniquely as
x=x_{1}x_{2}
where if
p\mid x_{1}
then
p\not\in S
and
x_{2}\in\mathbb{Q}_{S}
. Separating elements of
A
according to the value of
x_{1}
, there exists
C
such that if
p\mid n\in C
then
p\not\in S
, and finite
\Gamma_{c}\subseteq\mathbb{Q}_{S}
for
c\in C
such that

A=\bigcup_{c\in C}(c\cdot\Gamma_{c}),

where the
c\cdot\Gamma_{c}
are disjoint. Let
L
be some parameter to be chosen later, and let
A^{\prime\prime}\subseteq A
be the subset of
A
coming from those
\Gamma_{c}
with
\left\lvert\Gamma_{c}\right\rvert<L
. If
\lvert A^{\prime\prime}\rvert\geqslant\tfrac{3}{4}\lvert A\rvert
then there must be at least
\lvert A\rvert\left\lvert B\right\rvert/4
many such pairs
a\in A^{\prime\prime}
and
b\in B
with
P(a)\cap P(b)\subseteq S
.

Any
q\in A^{\prime\prime}B
has
<2^{k+\ell}L
representations as
q=ab
with
a\in A^{\prime\prime}
and
b\in B
and
P(a)\cap P(b)\subseteq S
. Indeed, writing
a=a_{1}a_{2}
as above (so
a_{2}\in\mathbb{Q}_{S}
and if
p\mid a_{1}
then
p\not\in S
) then one can write
q=a_{1}a_{2}b_{1}b_{2}
. Since
P(a)\cap P(b)\subseteq S
there are no primes appearing in both
a_{1}
and
a_{2}b_{1}b_{2}
, and hence since
\omega(q)\leqslant k+\ell
the prime factorisation of
a_{1}
(and hence
a_{1}
itself) can be determined from
q
at a cost of
2^{k+\ell}
. Since
a_{2}\in\Gamma_{a_{1}}
and
a_{1}a_{2}\in A^{\prime\prime}
the value of
a_{2}
, and hence the entirety of
a
, can then be determined at a cost of
<L
.

It follows that

\lvert A\rvert\left\lvert B\right\rvert/4<2^{k+\ell}L\left\lvert A^{\prime\prime}B\right\rvert\leqslant 2^{k+\ell}L\left\lvert AB\right\rvert,

which is a contradiction choosing
L=\lvert A\rvert\left\lvert B\right\rvert/2^{k+\ell+2}\left\lvert AB\right\rvert
. This contradiction means that
\left\lvert A^{\prime\prime}\right\rvert<\frac{3}{4}\left\lvert A\right\rvert
, and hence
A^{\prime}=A\backslash A^{\prime\prime}
has size
\left\lvert A^{\prime}\right\rvert\geqslant\left\lvert A\right\rvert/4
. Let
C^{\prime}\subseteq C
be the corresponding subset of
C
, so that
A^{\prime}\subseteq\mathbb{Q}_{S}\cdot C^{\prime}
. By construction

L\lvert C^{\prime}\rvert\leqslant\lvert A^{\prime}\rvert,

and noting the choice of
L
above we are done.
∎

The following proposition follows immediately on combining Lemmas
1
and
2
.

Proposition 1
.

Let
A\subset\mathbb{Q}\backslash\{0\}
be a finite set such that
\omega(n)\leqslant k
for all
n\in A
. Suppose there is a set
B\subset\mathbb{Q}\backslash\{0\}
such that
\omega(n)\leqslant\ell
for all
n\in B
and
\left\lvert AB\right\rvert\leqslant K\left\lvert B\right\rvert
. The following both hold:

(1)

There is a set
A^{\prime}\subseteq A
of size
\left\lvert A^{\prime}\right\rvert\gg\left\lvert A\right\rvert
which is
O(2^{k+\ell}K)
-covered by a rank
O(k\ell)
multiplicative group.

(2)

There is a set
A^{\prime}\subseteq A
of size
\left\lvert A^{\prime}\right\rvert\gg(2\ell)^{-k}\left\lvert A\right\rvert
which is
O(2^{k+\ell}K)
-covered by a rank
O(k)
multiplicative group.

4.
Additive equations over multiplicative groups

The previous section is the only part of the proof where it is important that we are working over
\mathbb{Q}
and use the few prime factors hypothesis. The remainder of the proof concerns the additive structure of sets which are efficiently covered by a low rank multiplicative subgroup of
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
.

There is a deep theory concerning linear equations in multiplicative groups, and we will only require one particular case of this. The following quantitative result was proved by Amoroso and Viada
[
1
, Theorem 6.2]
, and is a refinement of an earlier quantitative bound due to Evertse, Schlickewei, and Schmidt
[
7
]
.

Theorem 4
.

Let
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{C}^{\times}
be a a multiplicative group of rank
r
. Let
m\geqslant 2
. For any fixed
a_{0},a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\{0\}
the number of solutions to

a_{0}=a_{1}z_{1}+\cdots+a_{m}z_{m}\textrm{ with }z_{i}\in\Gamma,

such that no non-empty subsum of the right-hand side equals
0
, is at most

m^{O(m^{4}(m+r))}.

This immediately imposes strong restrictions on the potential additive structure of subsets of multiplicative groups of bounded rank – for example, if
A
is a finite subset of a multiplicative group of rank
r
then Theorem
4
, applied with
a_{0}=x
,
a_{1}=1
, and
a_{2}=-1
, implies that for any
x\neq 0

1_{A}\circ 1_{A}(x)\ll_{r}1,

and hence in particular
E(A)\ll_{r}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}
and
\left\lvert A+A\right\rvert\gg_{r}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}
.

A large part of the power of
[
8
]
arises from finding ways to apply Theorem
4
to efficiently bound the additive structure, not only of subsets of multiplicative groups of small rank, but of subsets of the union of ‘few’ cosets of such a group. We will give various improved forms of such bounds, giving new bounds for both
1_{A}\circ 1_{A}(x)
for any fixed
x\neq 0
and
E_{2m}(A)
for any
m\geqslant 1
.

4.1.
A pointwise bound on
1_{A}\circ 1_{A}

An immediate consequence of Theorem
4
is that if
A
is
M
-covered by a rank
r
multiplicative group then, for all
x\neq 0
,
1_{A}\circ 1_{A}(x)\ll 2^{O(r)}M^{2}
. Indeed, if
A\subseteq\Gamma\cdot B
, then fixing the values of
b_{1}
and
b_{2}
at a cost of
M^{2}
the number of choices of
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in\Gamma
with
\gamma_{1}b_{1}-\gamma_{2}b_{2}=x
is at most
2^{O(r)}
. This bound can be improved (for reasonable choices of
r
and
M
) to
M^{1+o(1)}
, by generalising an argument of Roche-Newton and Zhelezov
[
10
]
.

Lemma 3
.

If
r,M\geqslant 1
and
\delta\in(0,1)
are such that

\delta\log M\geqslant\max(r,(\log M)^{1/6})

and
A\subset\mathbb{C}^{\times}
is
M
-covered by a rank
r
multiplicative group then, for any
x\neq 0
,

1_{A}\circ 1_{A}(x)\ll M^{1+O(\delta^{1/5})}.

One should not take the constants
1/6
and
1/5
too seriously; an examination of the proof shows that they can be improved slightly at notational expense.

Proof.

Let
\Gamma
be a rank
r
multiplicative group such that
A\subseteq\Gamma\cdot B
, where
B\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{\times}
is a finite set of size
\left\lvert B\right\rvert=M
. Without loss of generality
B
can be taken to be a set of coset representatives – that is,
\Gamma\cdot b_{1}
and
\Gamma\cdot b_{2}
are disjoint for
b_{1}\neq b_{2}\in B
. We may also assume,without loss of generality, that
-1\in\Gamma
.

It suffices to bound the number of
b_{1},b_{2}\in B
for which there exist
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in\Gamma
with

\gamma_{1}b_{1}-\gamma_{2}b_{2}=x,

since once
b_{1},b_{2}
are fixed there are at most
2^{O(r)}
choices for
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in\Gamma
by Theorem
4
.

Let
G
be a directed graph with vertex set
B
such that there is an edge
b_{1}\to b_{2}
if and only if there exist
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in\Gamma
with
\gamma_{1}b_{1}-\gamma_{2}b_{2}=x
, so that we need to bound the number of edges in
G
; let this be denoted by
dM
.

By repeatedly removing vertices of out-degree
<d/2
we can find a subgraph, say
G^{\prime}
, which contains
\geqslant dM/2
many edges, and
G^{\prime}
has minimum out-degree
\geqslant d/2
. For each edge
b_{i}\to b_{j}
we fix some pair
\gamma_{i},\mu_{j}\in\Gamma
such that
\gamma_{i}b_{i}-\mu_{j}b_{j}=x
.

Given a path
b_{0}\cdots b_{l}
in
G^{\prime}
, with
\gamma_{i}b_{i}-\mu_{i+1}b_{i+1}=x
for
0\leqslant i<l
, writing
\lambda_{i}=\mu_{i}\gamma_{i}^{-1}
, by telescoping the sum,

\displaystyle\gamma_{0}b_{0}-\lambda_{1}\cdots\lambda_{l-1}\mu_{l}b_{l}

\displaystyle=\gamma_{0}b_{0}-\mu_{1}b_{1}+\lambda_{1}(\gamma_{1}b_{1}-\mu_{2}b_{2})+\cdots

\displaystyle\quad+\lambda_{1}\cdots\lambda_{l-1}(\gamma_{l-1}b_{l-1}-\mu_{l}b_{l})

\displaystyle=x(1+\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{1}\cdots\lambda_{l-1}).

In other words, each path of length
l
between two fixed vertices
b_{0}
and
b_{l}
yields a solution to the equation

(3)

1+z_{1}+\cdots+z_{l-1}+z_{l}(b_{0}x^{-1})+z_{l+1}(b_{l}x^{-1})=0,

where all
z_{i}\in\Gamma
, corresponding to
z_{i}=\lambda_{1}\cdots\lambda_{i}
for
1\leqslant i<l
,
z_{l}=-\gamma_{0}
, and
z_{l+1}=\lambda_{1}\cdots\lambda_{l-1}\mu_{l}
.

Moreover, any two distinct such paths between the same endpoints
b_{0}
and
b_{l}
yield distinct
z_{1},\ldots,z_{l+1}
. Indeed, fixing all
z_{i}
fixes
\lambda_{i}
for
1\leqslant i<l
, and
\gamma_{0}
and
\mu_{l}
. Since
\mu_{1}b_{1}=x-\gamma_{0}b_{0}
is known, and since
b_{1}\in B
which is a set of coset representatives, we can recover the value of both
\mu_{1}
and
b_{1}
. Since we know
\lambda_{1}
we also know
\gamma_{1}
. In general, once
b_{i},\gamma_{i},\mu_{i}
are known for all
0\leqslant i\leqslant j
are known the the value of
\mu_{j+1}b_{j+1}=x-\gamma_{i}b_{i}
is also known, whence
\mu_{j+1},b_{j+1},\gamma_{j+1}
are also known. In particular fixing all
z_{i}
fixes all
b_{0},\ldots,b_{l}
as claimed.

We would now like to apply Theorem
4
to bound the number of such
z_{i}
(and hence the number of such paths), but we must take care that we are only bounding the number of non-degenerate solutions. We therefore call a path
b_{0}\cdots b_{l}
non-degenerate if no proper subsum of the left-hand side of (
3
) vanishes.

This is achieved inductively – for
l=1
this amounts to ensuring that no proper subsum of

x-\gamma_{0}b_{0}+\mu_{1}b_{1}

vanishes, which is trivial. In particular every path of length
1
is non-degenerate.

In general, we claim that either
d\ll 2^{l}
or any non-degenerate path
b_{0}\cdots b_{l}
of length
l
can be extended to a non-degenerate path of length
l+1
in at least
d/4
many ways. For
b_{0}\cdots b_{l+1}
to be non-degenerate requires no proper subsum of

\left(1+\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{1}\cdots\lambda_{l-1}-\gamma_{0}b_{0}x^{-1}\right)+\lambda_{1}\cdots\lambda_{l}+\lambda_{1}\cdots\lambda_{l}\mu_{l+1}b_{l+1}x^{-1}

vanishes. By non-degeneracy of
b_{0}\cdots b_{l}
the first bracketed sum (and any subsum) cannot vanish. Let
\Sigma
be the set of values of all subsums of the first bracketed sum. To ensure that the extension by
b_{l+1}
is still non-degenerate it therefore suffices to ensure that

\lambda_{l}\not\in-(\lambda_{1}\cdots\lambda_{l-1})^{-1}\Sigma.

Recalling that
\lambda_{l}=\mu_{l}\gamma_{l}^{-1}
(and all
\gamma_{i}
for
1\leqslant i<l
and
\mu_{j}
for
1\leqslant j\leqslant l
are determined by
b_{0}\cdots b_{l}
) this amounts to a set of at most
2^{l}
many forbidden values for
\gamma_{l}
.

Furthermore, note that fixing a value of
\gamma_{l}
fixes
\mu_{l+1}b_{l+1}=\gamma_{l}b_{l}-x
and hence also fixes
b_{l+1}
. In other words, for a fixed non-degenerate path
b_{0}\cdots b_{l}
there are at most
2^{l}
possible
b_{l+1}
such that
b_{0}\cdots b_{l+1}
forms a degenerate path. Since the minimum degree of
G^{\prime}
is
d/2
, either
d\leqslant 2^{l+2}
, or there are at least
d/4
extensions to a non-degenerate path as claimed.

It follows that either
d\ll 2^{l}
or there are at least
(d/4)^{l}
many non-degenerate paths beginning at any fixed
b_{0}\in B^{\prime}
. We can fix the endpoint
d_{l}
losing only a factor of
\left\lvert B\right\rvert
. By the discussion above the number of non-degenerate paths with fixed endpoints
b_{0}
and
b_{l}
is
\ll l^{O(l^{4}(l+r))}
, and hence

(d/4)^{l}\left\lvert B\right\rvert^{-1}\ll l^{O(l^{4}(l+r))},

so that

d\ll\left\lvert B\right\rvert^{1/l}l^{O(l^{3}(l+r))}.

Choosing
l=\lfloor\delta^{-1/5}\rfloor
yields the result.
∎

4.2.
Bounds on additive energies

Recall that the additive energy
E(A)
counts the number of solutions to
a_{1}+a_{2}-a_{3}=a_{4}
with
a_{i}\in A
. If
A
is
M
-covered by a rank
r
multiplicative group, say
A\subseteq\Gamma\cdot B
with
\left\lvert B\right\rvert\leqslant M
, then Theorem
4
implies
E(A)\ll_{r}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}+\left\lvert A\right\rvert M^{3}
. Indeed, after spending
\left\lvert A\right\rvert
to fix
a_{4}
and
M^{3}
to fix
b_{1},b_{2},b_{3}
there are
O_{r}(1)
many choices for
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\gamma_{3}
with

\gamma_{1}b_{1}+\gamma_{2}b_{2}-\gamma_{3}b_{3}=a_{4}.

This almost works, except that we need to be sure that no proper subsum of the left-hand side vanishes. Provided
0\not\in A
, it is easy to see that such degenerate solutions contribute
O(\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2})
to the additive energy, whence

E(A)\ll_{r}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}+\left\lvert A\right\rvert M^{3}

as claimed. In this section we will prove an improvement over this, establishing that, in fact,

E(A)\ll_{r}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}+\left\lvert A\right\rvert M^{2}.

The key observation is that the obvious generalisation of the preceding argument gives non-trivial bounds for higher additive energies
E_{2m}(A)
for all
m\geqslant 1
, namely
E_{2m}(A)\ll\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{m}+\left\lvert A\right\rvert M^{2m-1}
. Furthermore, by Hölder’s inequality we can efficiently bound

E(A)\leqslant\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{\frac{m-2}{m-1}}E_{2m}(A)^{\frac{1}{m-1}}.

In particular,

E(A)\ll\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}+\left\lvert A\right\rvert M^{\frac{2m-1}{m-1}}.

Taking
m\to\infty
yields the claimed result. In the remainder of this section we make this sketch precise.

Lemma 4
.

Let
A
be a finite set in an abelian group. Let
k,r,n
be integers such that
k\geqslant n/2\geqslant r\geqslant 1
. For any
x
and
\epsilon_{1},\ldots,\epsilon_{n}\in\{-1,1\}
the number of solutions to

x=\epsilon_{1}a_{1}+\cdots+\epsilon_{n}a_{n}

with
a_{i}\in A
for
1\leqslant i\leqslant n
is at most

E_{2r}(A)^{\frac{2k-n}{2k-2r}}E_{2k}(A)^{\frac{n-2r}{2k-2r}}.

Proof.

Passing to a Freiman-isomorphic model if necessary (see
[
14
, Chapter 5]
) we may assume that the ambient group is finite. By orthogonality, the count to be estimated is equal to

\mathop{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\larger[4]$\mathbb{E}$}}}{\kern 0.0pt\mathbb{E}}{\kern 0.0pt\mathbb{E}}{\kern 0.0pt\mathbb{E}}}\displaylimits_{\gamma}\gamma(-x)\prod_{1\leqslant i\leqslant n}\widehat{1_{A}}(\epsilon_{i}\gamma),

where the expectation is over the dual group, and
\widehat{1_{A}}(\gamma)=\sum_{n\in A}\gamma(n)
. By the triangle inequality and then Hölder’s inequality this is at most

\mathop{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\larger[4]$\mathbb{E}$}}}{\kern 0.0pt\mathbb{E}}{\kern 0.0pt\mathbb{E}}{\kern 0.0pt\mathbb{E}}}\displaylimits_{\gamma}\lvert\widehat{1_{A}}(\gamma)\rvert^{n}\leqslant\left(\mathop{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\larger[4]$\mathbb{E}$}}}{\kern 0.0pt\mathbb{E}}{\kern 0.0pt\mathbb{E}}{\kern 0.0pt\mathbb{E}}}\displaylimits_{\gamma}\lvert\widehat{1_{A}}(\gamma)\rvert^{2r}\right)^{\frac{2k-n}{2k-2r}}\left(\mathop{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\larger[4]$\mathbb{E}$}}}{\kern 0.0pt\mathbb{E}}{\kern 0.0pt\mathbb{E}}{\kern 0.0pt\mathbb{E}}}\displaylimits_{\gamma}\lvert\widehat{1_{A}}(\gamma)\rvert^{2k}\right)^{\frac{n-2r}{2k-2r}}.

The claim now follows since, for example, orthogonality implies
\mathop{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\larger[4]$\mathbb{E}$}}}{\kern 0.0pt\mathbb{E}}{\kern 0.0pt\mathbb{E}}{\kern 0.0pt\mathbb{E}}}\displaylimits_{\gamma}\lvert\widehat{1_{A}}(\gamma)\rvert^{2r}=E_{2r}(A)
.
∎

Lemma 5
.

If
A\subset\mathbb{C}
is
M
-covered by a rank
r
multiplicative group then, for any
m\geqslant k\geqslant 1
,

E_{2k}(A)\leqslant 2^{O(km)}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{k}+m^{O(km^{3}(m+r))}\left\lvert A\right\rvert M^{2k-2+\frac{k-1}{m-1}}.

In particular, note that taking
k=2
and letting
m\to\infty
slowly implies

E_{4}(A)\leqslant\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2+o(1)}+\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{1+o(r)}M^{2}.

Proof.

Let
2\leqslant\ell<2m
. By Lemma
4
, applied with
r=1
and
k=m
, the contribution to
E_{2m}(A)
from those
2m
-tuples in which a subset of
\ell
summands sums to
0
is at most

\binom{2m}{\ell}\left(\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{\frac{2m-\ell}{2m-2}}E_{2m}(A)^{\frac{\ell-2}{2m-2}}\right)\left(\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{\frac{2m-(2m-\ell)}{2m-2}}E_{2m}(A)^{\frac{2m-\ell-2}{2m-2}}\right)

\leqslant\binom{2m}{\ell}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{\frac{m}{m-1}}E_{2m}(A)^{\frac{m-2}{m-1}}.

Let
E_{2m}^{*}(A)
count the number of tuples

a_{1}+\cdots-a_{2m}=0

such that no non-empty subsum equals
0
. Summing the preceding inequality over all
2\leqslant\ell\leqslant 2m-2
(and noting that since
0\not\in A
no subset of
1
or
2m-1
summands can sum to
0
)

E_{2m}(A)\leqslant E_{2m}^{*}(A)+2^{O(m)}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{\frac{m}{m-1}}E_{2m}(A)^{\frac{m-2}{m-1}}

and hence

E_{2m}(A)\ll E_{2m}^{*}(A)+2^{O(m^{2})}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{m}.

Let
\Gamma
be a multiplicative group of rank
r
and let
B
be a set of size
M
such that
A\subset\Gamma\cdot B
. The count
E_{2m}^{*}(A)
is at most the number of solutions to

x_{1}b_{1}+\cdots+x_{2m-1}b_{2m-1}=a_{2m}

with no subsum being zero, where
a_{2m}\in A
,
b_{i}\in B
, and
x_{i}\in\Gamma
. There are at most
\left\lvert A\right\rvert\left\lvert B\right\rvert^{2m-1}
choices for
b_{1},\ldots,b_{2m-1},a_{2m}
, after which, by Theorem
4
, there are
\leqslant m^{O(m^{4}(m+r))}
choices for the
x_{i}
. It follows that

E_{2m}(A)\ll 2^{O(m^{2})}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{m}+m^{O(m^{4}(m+r))}\left\lvert A\right\rvert\left\lvert B\right\rvert^{2m-1}.

We now amplify this by applying Lemma
4
(with
n=2k
,
x=0
, and
r=1
), so that, for any
1\leqslant k\leqslant m
,

E_{2k}(A)\leqslant\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{\frac{m-k}{m-1}}E_{2m}(A)^{\frac{k-1}{m-1}},

and the conclusion follows.
∎

5.
Lower bounds on the sumset

An immediate implication of the energy bounds of Lemma
5
is the following, which (when coupled with the results of Section
3
) implies the main result of
[
8
]
.

Theorem 5
.

Let
r,M\geqslant 1
and
\delta\in(0,1)
be such that

\delta\log M\geqslant\max(r,(\log M)^{1/6}).

If
A\subset\mathbb{C}
is
M
-covered by a rank
r
multiplicative group then

E(A)\ll 2^{O(\delta^{-1/5})}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}+\left\lvert A\right\rvert M^{2+O(\delta^{1/5})}.

In particular, if
\left\lvert A+A\right\rvert=K\left\lvert A\right\rvert
, then

\max(K,M)\geqslant\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{\frac{2}{3}-O(\delta^{-1/5})}.

Proof.

The upper bound on
E(A)=E_{4}(A)
follows from applying Lemma
5
with
k=2
and
m=\lfloor\delta^{-1/5}\rfloor
. The second bound is a consequence of the inequality (
1
), which yields

\left\lvert A+A\right\rvert\geqslant\frac{\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{4}}{E(A)}\gg\min\left(2^{-O(\delta^{-1/5})}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2},\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{3}M^{-2-O(\delta^{1/5})}\right).

∎

Recalling that, in the application to sets of integers with
\leqslant k
prime factors, Proposition
1
allows us to take
M\ll_{k}\left\lvert AA\right\rvert/\left\lvert A\right\rvert
, this recovers the bound

\max(\left\lvert AA\right\rvert,\left\lvert A+A\right\rvert)\geqslant\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{5/3-o(1)}

of
[
8
]
.

In the remainder of this section we will use combinatorial arguments and the non-trivial upper bounds for
E_{4}(A)
,
E_{8}(A)
, and
\max_{x\neq 0}1_{A}\circ 1_{A}(x)
from the last section to go beyond this exponent of
5/3
– albeit only for the size of the sumset. We remind the reader that, bearing in mind the example of Balog and Wooley, the bound on the additive energy in Theorem
5
cannot be improved.

The new ingredient is an inequality from the theory of higher additive energies, which has been extensively developed in a number of papers of Shkredov and Schoen-Shkredov. This theory explores the relationship between the higher additive energies
E_{2m}(A)
and higher moments of the convolution. The following inequality will suffice for our purposes. It is given in various special forms in a number of papers of Schoen and Shkredov – see, for example,
[
12
, Remark 40]
and
[
11
, Lemma 3]
.

We take this opportunity to give a short self-contained proof of a general form of this inequality.

Lemma 6

(Shkredov)
.

For any finite sets
A
,
B
, and
C
, and any
k\geqslant 1

\left(\sum_{c\in C}1_{A}\ast 1_{B}(c)\right)^{4k}\leqslant\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2k}\left\lvert B\right\rvert^{2k}E_{2k}(C)\sum_{x}1_{A}\circ 1_{A}(x)^{k}1_{B}\circ 1_{B}(x)^{k}.

Proof.

Consider the bipartite graph
G
with vertex set
A\times B
in which
a
and
b
are joined by an edge if
a+b\in C
, so that the number of edges in
G
is precisely
\sum_{c\in C}1_{A}\ast 1_{B}(c)=\delta\left\lvert A\right\rvert\left\lvert B\right\rvert
, say.

The number of cycles of length
2k
in
G
is at least
\delta^{2k}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{k}\left\lvert B\right\rvert^{k}
– this is a well-known fact in graph theory, and is a special case of Sidorenko’s conjecture. The particular case of even cycles was proved by Sidorenko
[
13
]
. The identity

(a_{1}+b_{1})+(a_{2}+b_{2})+(a_{k}+b_{k})=(b_{1}+a_{2})+\cdots+(b_{k}+a_{1})

implies that, if
a_{1}b_{1}\cdots a_{k}b_{k}
is a cycle, then letting
c_{i}=a_{i}+b_{i}
and
c_{i}^{\prime}=b_{i}+a_{i+1}
(where
a_{k+1}=a_{1}
) we have
c_{i},c_{i}^{\prime}\in C
and

c_{1}+\cdots+c_{k}=c_{1}^{\prime}+\cdots+c_{k}^{\prime}.

In other words, the number of cycles of length
2k
is equal to

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}c_{1},\ldots,c_{k}^{\prime}\in C\\
c_{1}+\cdots+c_{k}=c_{1}^{\prime}+\cdots+c_{k}^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\sum_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}\in A}\sum_{b_{1},\ldots,b_{k}\in B}1_{a_{i}+b_{i}=c_{i}}1_{b_{i}+a_{i+1}=c_{i}^{\prime}}.

By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality it follows that

\displaystyle\delta^{4k}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2k}\left\lvert B\right\rvert^{2k}

\displaystyle\leqslant E_{2k}(C)\sum_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}^{\prime}\in A}\sum_{b_{1},\ldots,b_{k}^{\prime}\in B}1_{a_{i}+b_{i}=a_{i}^{\prime}+b_{i}^{\prime}}1_{b_{i}+a_{i+1}=b_{i}^{\prime}+a_{i+1}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=E_{2k}(C)\sum_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}^{\prime}\in A}\sum_{b_{1},\ldots,b_{k}^{\prime}\in B}1_{a_{i}-a_{i}^{\prime}=b_{i}^{\prime}-b_{i}=a_{i+1}-a_{i+1}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=E_{2k}(C)\sum_{x}1_{A}\circ 1_{A}(x)^{k}1_{B}\circ 1_{B}(x)^{k},

which concludes the proof.
∎

Taking
C
to be the set of popular sums implies the following.

Lemma 7
.

If
\left\lvert A+A\right\rvert\leqslant K\left\lvert A\right\rvert
then, for any
k\geqslant 1
,

\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{6k}\leqslant K^{2k}E_{4k}(A)\sum 1_{A}\circ 1_{A}(x)^{2k}.

Proof.

Let
C=\{x:1_{A}\ast 1_{A}(x)\geqslant\frac{1}{2K}\left\lvert A\right\rvert\}
, so that
\sum_{c\in C}1_{A}\ast 1_{A}(c)\geqslant\frac{1}{2}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}
. By Lemma
6
it follows that

2^{-4k}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{4k}\leqslant E_{2k}(C)\sum_{x}1_{A}\circ 1_{A}(x)^{2k}.

Furthermore, since
(2K)^{-1}\left\lvert A\right\rvert 1_{C}\leqslant 1_{A}\circ 1_{A}
,

E_{2k}(C)\leqslant(2K)^{2k}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{-2k}E_{4k}(A),

and hence

2^{-6k}K^{-2k}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{6k}\leqslant E_{4k}(A)\sum 1_{A}\circ 1_{A}(x)^{2k}.

Finally, to remove the factor of
2^{-6k}
we note that the above inequality holds for any finite set in any abelian group. Replacing
A
by the direct product
A^{m}
, and noting that, for example,
E_{4k}(A^{m})=E_{4k}(A)^{m}
and so on, we have

2^{-6k}K^{-2km}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{6km}\leqslant\left(E_{4k}(A)\sum 1_{A}\circ 1_{A}(x)^{2k}\right)^{m}.

Taking
m
th roots and letting
m\to\infty
yields the result.
∎

We may now use Lemma
7
together with the bounds from Section
4
to obtain an improved lower bound on
\left\lvert A+A\right\rvert
for sets
A
which are efficiently covered by a low rank multiplicative group.

Theorem 6
.

Let
r,M\geqslant 1
and
\delta\in(0,1)
be such that

\delta\log M\geqslant\max(r,(\log M)^{1/6}).

If
A\subset\mathbb{C}
is
M
-covered by a rank
r
multiplicative group and
\left\lvert A+A\right\rvert=K\left\lvert A\right\rvert
then

\max(K,M)\gg\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{7/10-O(\delta^{1/5})}.

Proof.

By Lemma
5

E_{8}(A)\ll 2^{O(m)}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{4}+m^{O(m^{3}(r+m))}\left\lvert A\right\rvert M^{6+\frac{3}{m-1}}.

We apply this with
m=\lfloor\delta^{-1/5}\rfloor
, so that

E_{8}(A)\lesssim\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{4}+\left\lvert A\right\rvert M^{6},

where for convenience in this proof we write
\lesssim
to hide losses of
M^{O(\delta^{1/5})}
. Similarly,
E_{4}(A)\lesssim\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}+\left\lvert A\right\rvert M^{2}
. By Lemma
3
it follows that

\sum_{x}1_{A}\circ 1_{A}(x)^{4}\lesssim M^{2}\left(\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}+\left\lvert A\right\rvert M^{2}\right)+\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{4}.

Applying Lemma
7
with
k=2
therefore implies

\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{12}\lesssim K^{4}\left(\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{4}+\left\lvert A\right\rvert M^{6}\right)\left(\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{4}+\left\lvert A\right\rvert M^{4}\right),

so that

\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{12}\lesssim K^{4}\left(\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{8}+\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{5}M^{6}+\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}M^{10}\right).

This implies

\max(K,M)\gtrsim\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{7/10}

as claimed.
∎

We may now prove the full quantitative version of Theorem
2
.

Theorem 7
.

Let
A\subset\mathbb{Q}\backslash\{0\}
be a finite set such that
\omega(n)\leqslant k
for all
n\in A
. If
\delta\in(0,1)
is such that

\delta\log\left\lvert A\right\rvert\geqslant\max(k,(\log\left\lvert A\right\rvert)^{1/6})

then

\max(\left\lvert A+A\right\rvert,\left\lvert AA\right\rvert)\geqslant O(k)^{-k}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{17/10-O(\delta^{1/5})}.

Note in particular that we obtain a lower bound of
\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{17/10-o(1)}
provided
k=o(\frac{\log\left\lvert A\right\rvert}{\log\log\left\lvert A\right\rvert})
.

Proof.

Let
K\left\lvert A\right\rvert=\max(\left\lvert A+A\right\rvert,\left\lvert AA\right\rvert)
. By Proposition
1
there is a set
A^{\prime}\subseteq A
of size
\left\lvert A^{\prime}\right\rvert\gg(2k)^{-k}\left\lvert A\right\rvert
which is
M
-covered by a rank
r
multiplicative group, with
M\ll 4^{k}K
and
r\ll k
. After dilating
\delta
by some some small absolute constant if necessary, we may assume that

\delta\log M\geqslant\max(r,(\log M)^{1/6}),

so that we can apply Theorem
6
to deduce that, if
\left\lvert A^{\prime}+A^{\prime}\right\rvert=K^{\prime}\left\lvert A^{\prime}\right\rvert
,

\max(K^{\prime},M)\gg\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{7/10-O(\delta^{1/5})},

which yields the desired conclusion.
∎

In this proof we applied Lemma
7
with
k=2
to obtain the optimal exponent. We would ideally like to apply this with
k
arbitrarily large, but for larger
k
the contribution from
x=0
to
\sum 1_{A}\circ 1_{A}(x)^{2k}
overwhelms any saving we hope to make from Lemma
3
. If the contribution from
x=0
could be somehow discarded then this proof would, as
k\to\infty
, yield an exponent of
3/4
in place of
7/10
. Unfortunately we have no ideas how this contribution from
x=0
can be safely discarded.

6.
Many-fold sums and products

Finally, we prove our result on many sums and products, Theorem
3
. This is, more or less, a direct consequence of the bounds on additive energies in Lemma
5
. We first prove the most general quantitative statement possible, and then specialise to obtain Theorem
3
.

Theorem 8
.

Let
A\subset\mathbb{Q}\backslash\{0\}
be a finite set such that
\omega(n)\leqslant k
for all
n\in A
. If
\delta\in(0,1)
is such that

\delta\log\left\lvert A\right\rvert\geqslant\max(k,(\log\left\lvert A\right\rvert)^{1/6})

then for any
1/2\leqslant c\leqslant 1
and
m\geqslant 2
either

\lvert A^{m}\rvert>\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{c(m-1)+1}

or there is
A^{\prime}\subseteq A
of size
\left\lvert A^{\prime}\right\rvert\gg(2mk)^{-k}\left\lvert A\right\rvert
such that

E_{2m}(A^{\prime})\ll\left\lvert A^{\prime}\right\rvert^{1+2c(m-1)+O(m\delta^{1/5})}.

In particular, either

\lvert A^{m}\rvert>\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{cm+1-c}\textrm{ or }\lvert mA\rvert\gg(mk)^{-O(mk)}\lvert A\rvert^{2(1-c)m+2c-1-O(m\delta^{1/5})}.

Proof.

Let
m\geqslant 2
and suppose that
\lvert A^{m}\rvert\leqslant\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{c(m-1)+1}
. By the pigeonhole principle there exists some
1\leqslant i<m
such that, with
B=A^{i}
, we have
\left\lvert AB\right\rvert\leqslant\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{c}\left\lvert B\right\rvert
. Furthermore, clearly
\omega(n)\leqslant mk
for all
n\in B
. It follows by Proposition
1
that there is
A^{\prime}\subseteq A
of size
\left\lvert A^{\prime}\right\rvert\gg(2mk)^{-k}\left\lvert A\right\rvert
which is
M
-covered by a rank
r
multiplicative group, where
r\ll k
and
M\ll 2^{O(mk)}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{c}
.

We now apply Lemma
5
with
k
replaced by
m
and
m
replaced by
\lfloor\delta^{-1/5}\rfloor
to deduce that

E_{2m}(A^{\prime})\leqslant 2^{O(m\delta^{-1/5})}\left\lvert A^{\prime}\right\rvert^{m}+\left\lvert A^{\prime}\right\rvert^{1+2c(m-1)+O(m\delta^{1/5})}.

Since
m\leqslant 1+2c(m-1)
this simplifies to give the inequality in the theorem statement.
∎

Choosing
c=2/3
we deduce the following precise formulation of Theorem
3
(note that when
m=2
this recovers the bound of
[
8
]
as a special case).

Theorem 9
.

Let
A\subset\mathbb{Q}\backslash\{0\}
be a finite set such that
\omega(n)\leqslant k
for all
n\in A
. If
\delta\in(0,1)
is such that

\delta\log\left\lvert A\right\rvert\geqslant\max(k,(\log\left\lvert A\right\rvert)^{1/6})

then for any
m\geqslant 2

\max(\lvert mA\rvert,\lvert A^{m}\rvert)\gg(mk)^{-O(mk)}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{\frac{2}{3}m+\frac{1}{3}-O(m\delta^{1/5})}.

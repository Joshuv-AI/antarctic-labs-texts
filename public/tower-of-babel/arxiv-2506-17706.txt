Quantum 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 \mathfrak{gl} -weight system and its average values
Mikhail Zaitsev

Abstract
We present a proof of a recent conjecture due to M. Kazarian, E. Krasilnikov, S. Lando, and M. Shapiro, which describes the average value of the universal 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 {\mathfrak{gl}} -weight system on permutations. The proof uses a quantum analogue of the 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 {\mathfrak{gl}} -weight system on Hecke algebras of type A A , which leads to a one-parameter deformation of the average value of the universal 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 {\mathfrak{gl}} -weight system. We show that the average value of the quantum weight system is a linear combination of one-part Schur functions, with coefficients being q q -analogues of Bernoulli polynomials.

Introduction

The article is devoted to a proof of a conjecture (Theorem
4
below) proposed in
[
KKLS
]
.
The conjecture is an explicit expression of
the average value of the
{\mathfrak{gl}}
-weight system
on permutations as a linear combination of one-part Schur functions.
Weight systems are functions on chord diagrams, which encode finite
type knot invariants. The extension of the weight systems associated
to the series
A
Lie algebras to permutations was suggested
as a tool to make their computation effective, by means of
a recurrence relation for them
[
KL
,
ZY
]
. As an important byproduct,
the recurrence allows for unifying all the
\mathfrak{gl}(N)
-weight systems,
for
N=1,2,3,\dots
, into a universal
\mathfrak{gl}
-weight system on permutations.
The latter takes values in the ring of polynomials in infinitely
many variables
\mathbb{C}[N][C_{1},C_{2},\dots]
.

Our proof of the conjecture in question
makes use of the quantum analogue of the
{\mathfrak{gl}}
-weight system on Hecke algebras of type
A
. It was defined in
[
GS
]
using the Reflection Equation algebra. The quantum
{\mathfrak{gl}}
-weight system inherits many properties of the classical
{\mathfrak{gl}}
-weight system. In the proofs of the properties we make substantial use of the Reflection Equation algebra, but all the concepts
and notions we use can be introduced independently of the latter.

The group algebra
\mathbb{C}[\mathbb{S}_{m}]
of the symmetric group
\mathbb{S}_{n}
is isomorphic
to the Hecke algebra
\mathbb{H}_{m}
, for each
m=1,2,\dots
,
but there is no canonical isomorphism between the two.
In particular, we know no natural way to define the quantum
universal
\mathfrak{gl}
-weight system on permutations.
However, the
centers
of the two algebras can be identified canonically,
which makes it possible to define the values of the quantum
\mathfrak{gl}
-weight
system on central elements and, in particular, on the central idempotent
entering the conjecture.

The main result of the present paper is an explicit formula for the mean value of the quantum
{\mathfrak{gl}}
-weight system
\Omega_{m}
in terms of one-part Schur functions
S_{k}
. Set
\nu=N-1
.
Our main result asserts that the value of the averaged quantum
{\mathfrak{gl}}
-weight system is given by the formula

\Omega_{m}(N)=\frac{m!}{[m]_{q}!}\frac{[m+\nu]_{q}!}{(m+\nu)!}\sum_{l=0}^{m}\frac{(m+\nu)_{l}q^{-2l}}{l!}\beta_{l}^{(m-\nu+1,\nu)}\left(\frac{\nu}{2}\right)S_{m-l},

(2.1)

where, for a positive integer
b
, we use the standard
notation
(a)_{b}
, which denotes the
falling factorial
(a)_{b}=a(a-1)(a-2)\dots(a-b+1)
,
and

\beta_{l}^{(h,k)}(\xi)
is the
quantum
l
th

-Bernoulli polynomial
of order

(see
[
C
]

[
KCR
]

where we extend the notation for the falling factorial by setting

The quantum

As a consequence, each summand in the right hand side of

\underset{q\rightarrow 1}{\lim}\penalty\ \Omega_{m}(N)=\sum_{l=0}^{m}\frac{(\nu+m)_{l}}{l!}B_{l}^{(\nu)}\left(\frac{\nu}{2}\right)S_{m-l}.

(2.2)

We will prove that this limit value coincides with the average value
W_{m}(N)

of the
{\mathfrak{gl}}
-weight system on permutations of
m
elements.
Expression (
2.2
) for
W_{m}(N)
was proposed in the article
[
KKLS
]
. The coefficients

in the expansion are called

.

3
Outline of the proof

Before giving formal definitions we present a brief outline of the
proof of the main result, in order to make the paper more
readable.

In addition to the
\mathfrak{gl}(N)
weight systems

w_{\mathfrak{gl}(N)}:\mathbb{C}[\mathbb{S}_{m}]\to\mathbb{C}[C_{1},C_{2},\dots,C_{N}]

and the universal
\mathfrak{gl}
weight system

w_{\mathfrak{gl}}:\mathbb{C}[\mathbb{S}_{m}]\to\mathbb{C}[N][C_{1},C_{2},\dots]

we make use of the quantum weight systems

\omega_{m,N}:\mathbb{H}_{m}\to\mathbb{M}(N)

and the universal quantum weight system

\omega_{m}:\mathbb{H}_{m}\to\mathbb{C}[q^{-N}][C_{1},C_{2},\dots]

defined in
[
GS
]
. The domain
\mathbb{H}_{m}
of the quantum weight systems
is the
m
th Hecke algebra, and their range is the Reflection Equation
algebra
\mathbb{M}(N)
.

The universal quantum weight system
\omega_{m}
can be defined by
axiomatizing its properties, see Definition
9
below.
The sequence
\omega_{m,N}
,
m=1,2,\dots
, of quantum weight systems
can be defined using an explicit formula in the Reflection Equation algebra, see Definition
14

below. They take values in the centers of the Reflection Equation algebras

\mathbb{M}(N)
. In turn, this center admits a natural identification with
the ring of polynomials
\mathbb{C}[C_{1},C_{2},\dots,C_{N}]
. The sequence of universal
quantum weight systems
\omega_{m}
possesses the property that it
specializes into
\omega_{m,N}
, for a given
N
.

The averaged sum

\mathbbm{s}_{m}=\frac{1}{m!}\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{S}_{m}}\alpha\in\mathbb{C}[\mathbb{S}_{m}]

of all permutations in the group
\mathbb{S}_{m}
is an idempotent in
\mathbb{C}[\mathbb{S}_{m}]
.
Using the natural inclusion
\mathbb{S}_{m-1}\subset\mathbb{S}_{m}
,
the sequence of these idempotents, for
m=1,2,\dots
, can be defined recursively
by the equation

\mathbbm{s}_{m}=\frac{1}{m}\mathbbm{s}_{m-1}(id_{m}+(m-1)\cdot(m-1,m))\mathbbm{s}_{m-1},\quad m\geq 2,

with the initial condition
\mathbbm{s}_{1}=1
.

The sequence of Hecke algebras
\mathbb{H}_{m}
,
m=1,2,\dots
, contains idempotents

\mathbbm{h}_{m}
defined in the following similar way:

\mathbbm{h}_{1}=1,\qquad\mathbbm{h}_{m}=\frac{1}{[m]_{q}}\mathbbm{h}_{m-1}(q^{2-2m}+q^{-1}[m-1]_{q}g_{m-1}))\mathbbm{h}_{m-1},

where
g_{i}
,
i=1,2,\dots
are standard Artin generators of the Hecke algebras.

Then we manage to prove the following statements:

•

the value
\omega_{m}(\mathbbm{h}_{m})
of the universal quantum
weight system is given by Eq. (
2.1
);

•

the limit
\lim_{q\to 1}\omega_{m}(\mathbbm{h}_{m})
exists and is given by
Eq. (
2.2
);

•

this limit coincides with
W_{m}(N)=w_{\mathfrak{gl}}(\mathbbm{s}_{m})
.

It happens that computations with the universal quantum weight system
are quite complicated. To overcome this problem we introduce just another
function on Hecke algebras, called the characteristic mapping.
It looks more natural, can be easily computed and is
governed by much more simple recurrence relations. Unfortunately,
its values normally do not have a limit as
q\to 1
,
which does not allow one to use it directly to deduce formulas for
the averaged values. Instead, we compute the average value of the
quantum characteristic mapping, rewrite the answer in terms of
universal quantum weight system, and then take the limit as
q\to 1
,
which yields the desired formula.

The goal of the present section is to define the quantum universal weight system

In the quantum case, the role of the group algebra of the symmetric group is played by the Hecke algebra of type
A
a detailed description
of which can be found in the survey paper
[

In formulas below, all tensor products are considered over the field of rational functions

Definition 5
.

The
Hecke algebra

\mathbb{H}_{m}
(of type
A
) is a unital associative algebra over
\mathbb{C}(q)
, with unit
1_{\mathbb{H}_{m}}
, generated by the Artin generators
g_{i}
,
i=1,...,m-1
, that satisfy the following relations:

g_{i}g_{j}=g_{j}g_{i},\quad|i-j|>1,

g_{i}g_{i+1}g_{i}=g_{i+1}g_{i}g_{i+1},\quad 1\leq i\leq m-2,

g_{i}^{2}=1_{\mathbb{H}_{m}}+(q-q^{-1})g_{i},\quad 1\leq i\leq m-2.

Treating
q
as a formal parameter, the Hecke algebra
\mathbb{H}_{m}
is isomorphic to the group algebra
\mathbb{C}(q)[\mathbb{S}_{m}]
of the symmetric group
\mathbb{S}_{m}
.
Set

C_{ij}=g_{j-1}g_{j-2}\dots g_{i},\quad j,i\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0},\,i<j,\quad C_{ii}=1.

The element
C_{ij}
can be considered as an element of each Hecke algebra

\mathbb{H}_{m}
, for
m=j,j+1,\dots
.
The monomials
C_{i_{1}1}C_{i_{2}2}\dots C_{i_{k}k}
, such that
1\leq k\leq m
,
1\leq i_{l}\leq l
, form a basis for the Hecke algebra
\mathbb{H}_{m}
, see
[
J
]
.

Lemma 6
.

Let
U
be a
\mathbb{C}(q)
-vector space and let
f_{m}:\mathbb{H}_{m}\rightarrow U
,
m=1,2,\dots
, be a sequence of linear mappings. Suppose that for each
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, any

x\in\mathbb{H}_{m}
, and any
i
such that
1\leq i\leq m-1
, there is
y\in\mathbb{H}_{m-1}
such that

f_{m}(g_{i}x)-f_{m}(xg_{i})=f_{m-1}(y).

Under these assumptions, the sequence of mappings

f_{m},m\geq 1
, is uniquely determined by the values
of these functions on the elements

C_{m_{1}k_{1}}\dots C_{m_{l}k_{l}}
, where
m_{1}<k_{1}<m_{2}<k_{2}<\dots<m_{l}<k_{l}.

Proof
.

First, we prove the following claim. Each basis element
u\in\mathbb{H}_{n}
can be reduced by a sequence of conjugations and expansions to
a linear combination of the elements
C_{m_{l}k_{l}}C_{m_{l-1}k_{l-1}}\dots C_{m_{1}k_{1}}
,
m_{1}<k_{1}<m_{2}<k_{2}<\dots<m_{l}<k_{l}
. We allow only conjugations by elements of the algebra
\mathbb{H}_{m-1}
. After decomposing an element into a sum, we allow the terms to be conjugated by different elements. Elements
C_{m_{i}k_{i}}
with non-intersecting segments
m_{i},m_{i}+1,\dots,k_{i}
commute, therefore the expression
C_{m_{l}k_{l}}C_{m_{l-1}k_{l-1}}\dots C_{m_{1}k_{1}}
is equal to the desired
C_{m_{1}k_{1}}\dots C_{m_{l}k_{l}}
.

We prove the claim by induction on
m
. The case
m=1
is trivial.
Suppose
m>1
, note that each element of the basis
u=C_{i_{1}1}C_{i_{2}2}\dots C_{i_{m}m}
either lies in
\mathbb{H}_{m-1}
or is equal to
xg_{m-1}y
, where
x,y\in\mathbb{H}_{m-1}
. If
u\in\mathbb{H}_{m-1}
,
then we can use the induction hypothesis. If
u=xg_{m-1}y
, the elements
x
and
y
are products of generators, therefore, we can invert
x
and conjugate
u
by
x^{-1}
. By expanding
yx
as a linear combination of basis elements,
yx=\sum\limits_{i}a_{i}u_{i}
,
u_{i}\in\mathbb{H}_{n-1}
,
a_{i}\in\mathbb{C}(q)
, we obtain the expression
x^{-1}ux=\sum\limits_{i}a_{i}g_{m}u_{i}
. By the induction hypothesis, any element
u_{i}
can be reduced to a linear combination of
C_{m_{l}k_{l}}C_{m_{l-1}k_{l-1}}\dots C_{m_{1}k_{1}}
,
m_{1}<k_{1}<m_{2}<k_{2}<\dots<m_{l}<k_{l}\leq m-1
. The element
g_{m}
does not affect the calculation,
since we only used conjugation with those elements from the algebra
\mathbb{H}_{m-2}
, which commute with
g_{m-1}
. It remains to note that

g_{m}C_{m_{l}k_{l}}C_{m_{l-1}k_{l-1}}\dots C_{m_{1}k_{1}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}C_{m_{l}m}C_{m_{l-1}k_{l-1}}\dots C_{m_{1}k_{1}},\quad k_{l}=m-1\\
C_{m-1m}C_{m_{l}k_{l}}\dots C_{m_{1}k_{1}},\quad k_{l}<m-1\end{array}\right..

We used conjugations only by elements
x\in\mathbb{H}_{m-1}
that are representable as products of the generators. For any such
x
and any
u\in\mathbb{H}_{m}
, there is
z\in\mathbb{H}_{m-1}
such that
f_{m}(x^{-1}ux)=f_{m}(u)+f_{m-1}(z)
. Thus, the assertion of the Lemma is reduced to the claim by a simple induction on
m
.

Remark 2
.

Note that the claim gives an algorithm for representing an element of the algebra
\mathbb{H}_{m}
as a linear combination of the basic elements
of the form
C_{m_{l}k_{l}}C_{m_{l-1}k_{l-1}}\dots C_{m_{1}k_{1}}
.

Indeed, let
u\in\mathbb{H}_{m}\setminus\mathbb{H}_{m-1}
be a basis element. The element
u
is uniquely represented as a product
xg_{m-1}y
, where
x,y\in\mathbb{H}_{m-1}
, and after conjugating it with
x^{-1}
, we reduce it to the form
\sum a_{i}g_{m-1}u_{i}
, where
u_{i}\in\mathbb{H}_{m-1}
are
basis elements,
a_{i}\in\mathbb{C}(q)
. We then express each
u_{i}\notin\mathbb{H}_{m-2}
as
\tilde{x_{i}}g_{m-2}\tilde{y_{i}}
. The elements
\tilde{y_{i}}
have the form
C_{j\,m-2}
for some
j\leq m-2
, and the elements
\tilde{x_{i}}
have the form
C_{i_{1}1}C_{i_{2}2}\dots C_{i_{m-2}m-2}
, thus the elements
\tilde{x_{i}}
are invertible and the elements
\tilde{x_{i}}^{-1}
can be explicitly expressed through Artin generators.

Conjugating
\tilde{x_{i}}g_{m-2}\tilde{y_{i}}
with
\tilde{x}_{i}^{-1}
, which commutes with
g_{m-1}
, we get a sum of elements of the form
g_{m-1}g_{m-2}\tilde{u}_{ij}
or
g_{m-1}\tilde{u}_{ij}
where
\tilde{u}_{ij}\in\mathbb{H}_{m-2}
. Iterating this procedure, we obtain sums of expressions of the form

g_{i_{1}}g_{i_{2}}\dots g_{i_{l}}
,
i_{1}>i_{2}>\dots>i_{l}
. The normal form
g_{i_{1}}g_{i_{2}}\dots g_{i_{l}}
is equal to
C_{m_{l}k_{l}}C_{m_{l-1}k_{l-1}}\dots C_{m_{1}k_{1}}
for some indices
m_{i},k_{i}
.

Before introducing the quantum
{\mathfrak{gl}}
-weight system,
we define the universal characteristic map, which makes
computations more handleable.
For the next definition, we need the concatenation product on Hecke algebras.
The
concatenation product
is the algebra homomorphism
\mathbf{m}:\mathbb{H}_{m}\times\mathbb{H}_{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{H}_{m+n},\quad i=1,\dots,m,\quad j=1,\dots,n
, defined on the generators by the formula

\mathbf{m}:(g_{i},g_{j})\mapsto g_{i}g_{j+m}.

.

The

is the sequence of linear maps

\chi_{m}:\mathbb{H}_{m}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}(q)[p_{1},\dots,p_{m}]
,

1.

\chi_{m+n}(\mathbf{m}(x,y))=\chi_{m}(x)\chi_{n}(y)
, where
x\in\mathbb{H}_{m}
,

;

2.

\chi_{m}(C_{1m})=p_{m}

m>0

3.

\chi_{m}(g_{i}x)=\chi_{m}(xg_{i})
,
for all

Properties

\chi_{m}(C_{m_{1}k_{1}}\dots C_{m_{l}k_{l}})=p_{k_{1}-m_{1}+1}\dots p_{k_{l}-m_{l}+1}\times(p_{1})^{m-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{l}(k_{i}-m_{i}+1)},

Therefore, by Lemma
6

–
3

Definition 8

The

\mathbb{H}_{m}

\mathbb{T}r_{m}(C_{i_{1}1}\dots C_{i_{m}m})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}(C_{i_{1}1}\dots C_{i_{m-1}m-1})C_{i_{m}m-1},\quad i_{m}<m\\
q^{-1}[N]_{q}(C_{i_{1}1}\dots C_{i_{m-1}m-1}),\quad i_{m}=m\end{array}\right..

.

The
universal quantum
{\mathfrak{gl}}
-weight system

\omega_{m}:\mathbb{H}_{m}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}(q)[q^{-N};C_{1},C_{2},\dots],

2.

\omega_{m}(C_{1m})=C_{m}

3.

\omega_{m}(g_{i}x-xg_{i})=

=\omega_{m-1}(\mathbb{T}r_{m}(g_{m-1}^{-1}\dots g_{i+1}^{-1}xg_{i+1}\dots g_{m-1}-g_{m-1}^{-1}\dots g_{i}^{-1}xg_{i}\dots g_{m-1})),

for any
x\in\mathbb{H}_{m}
and
1\leq i\leq m-1

The universal quantum
{\mathfrak{gl}}

3

\omega_{m}(C_{m_{1}k_{1}}\dots C_{m_{l}k_{l}})=C_{k_{1}-m_{1}+1}\dots C_{k_{l}-m_{l}+1}\times(C_{1})^{m-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{l}(k_{i}-m_{i}+1)},

and Lemma

\chi_{m}
and

and

Example 1
.

Each basis element
C_{i_{1}1}C_{i_{2}2}\dots C_{i_{m}m}
of the Hecke algebra can be associated with a permutation. This permutation is obtained by replacing each generator
g_{i}
with the transposition
(i,i+1)
. This defines a bijective correspondence between permutations and the basis elements of the Hecke algebra.
However, the composition of the correspondence with the quantum weight system does not determine the weight system, see examples below.
We now compute the values of the quantum weight system for certain elements of the Hecke algebra corresponding to specific permutations.

1.

The value of the quantum weight system on the standard cycle
(123)
is
\omega_{3}(g_{2}g_{1})=C_{3}
.

2.

The value on the cycle
(132)
is computed as follows

\displaystyle\omega_{3}(g_{1}g_{2})=\omega_{3}(g_{2}g_{1})+\omega_{2}(\mathbb{T}r_{3}(g_{2}^{-1}g_{2}g_{2})-\omega_{2}(\mathbb{T}r_{3}(g_{2}^{-1}g_{1}^{-1}g_{2}g_{1}g_{2}))=

\displaystyle=C_{3}+\omega_{2}(1)-q^{-1}[N]_{q}\omega_{2}(g_{1})=C_{3}+C_{1}^{2}-q^{-1}[N]_{q}C_{2}.

Here we used the braid relation
g_{2}^{-1}g_{1}^{-1}g_{2}g_{1}g_{2}=g_{2}^{-1}g_{2}g_{1}g_{2}^{-1}g_{2}=g_{1}
.

3.

The value on the transposition
(13)
is

\displaystyle\omega_{3}(g_{1}g_{2}g_{1})=\omega_{3}(g_{2}g_{1}^{2})+\omega_{2}(\mathbb{T}r_{3}(g_{2}^{-1}g_{2}g_{1}g_{2}))-\omega_{2}(\mathbb{T}r_{3}(g_{2}^{-1}g_{1}^{-1}g_{2}g_{1}g_{1}g_{2}))=

\displaystyle=C_{1}C_{2}+(q-q^{-1})C_{3}+C_{2}-\omega_{2}(\mathbb{T}r_{3}(g_{1}g_{2}^{-1}g_{1}g_{2}))=C_{1}C_{2}+(q-q^{-1})C_{3}

The braid relation was applied twice
g_{2}^{-1}g_{1}^{-1}g_{2}g_{1}g_{1}g_{2}=g_{1}g_{2}^{-1}g_{1}^{-1}g_{1}g_{1}g_{2}=g_{1}^{2}g_{2}g_{1}^{-1}
.

4.

The permutation
(1243)
corresponds to the element
g_{1}g_{3}g_{2}
of the Hecke algebra. The value of the quantum weight system on this element is computed as:

\displaystyle\omega_{4}(g_{1}g_{3}g_{2})=\omega_{4}(g_{3}g_{2}g_{1})+\omega_{3}(\mathbb{T}r_{4}(g_{3}^{-1}g_{2}^{-1}g_{3}g_{2}^{2}g_{3}-g_{3}^{-1}g_{2}^{-1}g_{1}^{-1}g_{3}g_{2}g_{1}g_{2}g_{3}))=

\displaystyle=C_{4}+C_{1}C_{2}-q^{-1}[N]_{q}C_{3}

The calculation relies on:
g_{3}^{-1}g_{2}^{-1}g_{1}^{-1}g_{3}g_{2}g_{1}g_{2}g_{3}=g_{3}^{-1}g_{2}^{-1}g_{3}g_{2}g_{1}g_{3}=g_{2}g_{3}^{-1}g_{2}^{-1}g_{2}g_{1}g_{3}=g_{2}g_{1}.

5.

For the permutation
(13)(24)
, the corresponding element of the Hecke algebra looks like
g_{2}g_{1}g_{3}g_{2}
.

\displaystyle\omega_{4}(g_{2}g_{1}g_{3}g_{2})=

\displaystyle=\omega_{4}(g_{1}g_{3}+(q-q^{-1})g_{1}g_{3}g_{2})+\omega_{3}(\mathbb{T}r_{4}(g_{3}^{-1}g_{1}g_{3}g_{2}g_{3}-g_{3}^{-1}g_{2}^{-1}g_{1}g_{3}g_{2}^{2}g_{3}))=

\displaystyle=\omega_{4}(g_{1}g_{3})+(q-q^{-1})\omega_{4}(g_{1}g_{3}g_{2})+\omega_{3}(g_{1}g_{2})-\omega_{3}(g_{2}+\frac{1}{q^{2N}}g_{2}g_{1})=

\displaystyle=C_{2}^{2}+(q-q^{-1})(C_{4}+C_{2}C_{1})+C_{1}^{2}-q^{-1}[N]_{q}C_{2}-C_{1}C_{2}

To calculate the value
\omega_{3}(\mathbb{T}r_{4}(g_{3}^{-1}g_{2}^{-1}g_{1}g_{3}g_{2}^{2}g_{3}))
, we used the following chain of equalities
g_{3}^{-1}g_{2}^{-1}g_{1}g_{3}g_{2}^{2}g_{3}=g_{2}g_{3}^{-1}g_{2}^{-1}g_{1}(1+(q-q^{-1})g_{2})g_{3}=g_{2}g_{3}^{-1}g_{2}^{-1}g_{3}g_{1}+(q-q^{-1})g_{2}g_{3}^{-1}g_{2}^{-1}g_{1}g_{2}g_{3}=g_{2}^{2}g_{3}^{-1}g_{2}^{-1}g_{1}+(q-q^{-1})g_{2}g_{1}g_{2}g_{3}g_{2}^{-1}g_{1}^{-1}
and the trace property
\mathbb{T}r_{4}(g_{3}^{-1})=\mathbb{T}r_{4}(g_{3})+(q-q^{-1})q^{-1}[N]_{q}=q^{-2N}
.

6.

For the permutation
(143)
, the corresponding element of the Hecke algebra looks like
g_{1}g_{3}g_{2}g_{1}

\displaystyle\omega_{4}(g_{1}g_{3}g_{2}g_{1})=

\displaystyle=C_{3}C_{1}+(q-q^{-1})C_{4}+\omega_{3}(\mathbb{T}r_{4}(g_{3}^{-1}g_{2}^{-1}g_{3}g_{2}g_{1}g_{2}g_{3}-g_{3}^{-1}g_{2}^{-1}g_{1}^{-1}g_{3}g_{2}g_{1}^{2}g_{2}g_{3}))=

\displaystyle=C_{3}C_{1}+(q-q^{-1})C_{4}+\omega_{3}(\mathbb{T}r_{4}(g_{2}g_{3}^{-1}g_{1}g_{2}g_{3}-g_{2}g_{1}g_{3}^{-1}g_{2}^{-1}g_{1}g_{2}g_{3}))=

\displaystyle=C_{3}C_{1}+(q-q^{-1})C_{4}

The last equality follows from:
g_{3}^{-1}g_{2}^{-1}g_{1}g_{2}g_{3}=g_{1}g_{2}g_{3}g_{2}^{-1}g_{1}^{-1}
and
g_{3}^{-1}g_{2}g_{3}=g_{2}g_{3}g_{2}^{-1}

vanishes:

7.

For the permutation
(1423)

\displaystyle\omega_{4}(g_{2}g_{1}g_{3}g_{2}g_{1})=

(14)(23)

Using recurrence relations, we obtain

\displaystyle\omega_{4}(g_{1}g_{2}g_{1}g_{3}g_{2}g_{1})=

\displaystyle=\omega_{4}(g_{2}g_{1}g_{3}g_{2})+(q-q^{-1})\omega_{4}(g_{2}g_{1}g_{3}g_{2}g_{1})+\omega_{3}(g_{1}^{2}g_{2}g_{1})-\omega_{3}(g_{1}g_{2}g_{1}^{2})=

\displaystyle=\omega_{4}(g_{2}g_{1}g_{3}g_{2})+(q-q^{-1})\omega_{4}(g_{2}g_{1}g_{3}g_{2}g_{1})+\omega_{3}(g_{2}g_{1})-\omega_{3}(g_{1}g_{2})

\displaystyle\omega_{4}(g_{1}g_{2}g_{1}g_{3}g_{2}g_{1})=

\mathfrak{gl}
weight system, although at the moment we do not know how to prove this assertion

From now on, let us identify the ranges of the invariants
\chi_{m}

and
\omega_{m}
by expressing the variables
C_{1},C_{2},\dots

in the variables
p_{1},p_{2},\dots
by means of the following
equality between the generating functions for them:

e^{\frac{t}{q-q^{-1}}}\left(q^{-1}[N]_{q}+\sum\limits_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{p_{m}}{m!}\frac{(-t)^{m}}{(q-q^{-1})^{m}}\right)=q^{-1}[N]_{q}+\sum\limits_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{C_{m}}{m!}t^{m};

(4.1)

the coefficients are rational functions of

.
This relation is explained using the quantum analogue of the Perelomov-Popov formula (see
[
GPS
]
).
It allows one to express the values of the maps
\chi_{m}
and
\omega_{m}

through each other using a formula of the form

\omega_{m}(x)=\frac{1}{(q-q^{-1})^{m}}\sum\limits_{I\subset\{1,\dots,m\}}(-1)^{|I|}\chi_{|I|}(\tilde{x}_{I}),\quad x\in\mathbb{H}_{m},

where the sum is taken over all subsets
I
of the set
\{1,\dots,m\}
, symbol
|I|
denotes the cardinality of
I
and the element
\tilde{x}_{I}\in\mathbb{H}_{|I|}
is obtained from

by conjugation with some invertible element of the algebra
\mathbb{H}_{n}
and taking all traces in positions
|I|+1,\dots,m
. Details can be found in Proposition
17

4.3
R
-matrix representations of Hecke algebras

In this Section, following
[
GPS
,
GS
]
, we introduce the Reflection
equation algebra
\mathbb{M}(N)
and two sequences of linear mappings

\chi_{m,N}:\mathbb{H}_{m}\to Z\mathbb{M}(N),\qquad\omega_{m,N}:\mathbb{H}_{m}\to Z\mathbb{M}(N),

called the characteristic map and the quantum
\mathfrak{gl}(N)
-weight system,
respectively. Both mappings take value in the center
Z\mathbb{M}(N)

of
\mathbb{M}(N)
, which can be identified with the ring of polynomials
in either of the two set of variables,
p_{1},p_{2},\dots,p_{N}
,

p_{i}=\chi_{m,N}(C_{1m})
, or
C_{1},\dots,C_{N}
,
C_{i}=\omega_{m,N}(C_{1m})
.
These two sets of variables are related by Eq. (
4.1
).
Similarly to the classical case, the invariants
\chi_{m,N}
and

\omega_{m,N}
can be viewed as specializations of the universal
characteristic map
\chi_{m}
and the universal quantum
\mathfrak{gl}
-weight
system
\omega_{m}
, respectively.
To construct the quantum
{\mathfrak{gl}(N)}
-weight system and characteristic map, we need
R
-matrix representations of Hecke algebras (see
[
OP
]
) and the associated Reflection Equation algebras (see
[
GPS
]
).

We denote by
R=R(N)
, for
N=1,2,\dots
, the

Drinfeld–Jimbo
R
-matrix
, which is the

N^{2}\times N^{2}
-matrix acting
on the vector space
\mathbb{C}(q)^{N}\otimes\mathbb{C}(q)^{N}
according to the formula

R(N)=q\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}E_{i}^{i}\otimes E_{i}^{i}+\sum\limits_{i\neq j}E_{i}^{j}\otimes E_{j}^{i}+(q-q^{-1})\sum\limits_{i<j}E_{i}^{i}\otimes E_{j}^{j},

(4.2)

where
E_{i}^{j}
,
i,j=1,\dots,N
are standard matrix units. The
R
-matrix acting on the tensor
product of the
i
th and the
(i+1)
st factors in a tensor power
V^{\otimes m}
,

V\equiv\mathbb{C}(q)^{N}
,
will be denoted
R_{i,i+1}
,
i=1,\dots,m-1
.

Below, we will make use of the following properties of the
R
-matrix:

•

R^{2}=I+(q-q^{-1})R
(the
Hecke relation
);

•

R_{i-1,i}R_{i,i+1}R_{i-1,i}=R_{i,i+1}R_{i-1,i}R_{i,i+1}
(the
braid relation
).

For a vector space
V=\mathbb{C}(q)^{N}
, the mapping

\rho:\mathbb{H}_{m}\rightarrow End(V^{\otimes m}),\qquad\rho:g_{i}\mapsto R_{i,i+1},

(4.3)

as can be easily seen, defines a representation
of the Hecke algebra
\mathbb{H}_{m}
, which we call
the
R-matrix representation
.

Definition 10
.

The
R-trace
is the mapping
Tr_{R}:Mat_{N\times N}(\mathbb{C}(q))\otimes V\rightarrow V
defined by the following formula:

Tr_{R}(X)=Tr(DX),\quad X\in Mat_{N\times N}(\mathbb{C}(q))\otimes V,

where
D=diag(q^{1-2N},q^{3-2N},\dots,q^{-1})
.

A direct generalization of Definition
10
is
that of the
multiple
R
-trace
for a more general matrix
X\in Mat_{N\times N}(\mathbb{C}(q))^{\otimes m}\otimes U
,
1\leq k\leq n\leq m
,

\langle X\rangle_{k,n}=Tr_{R}(X)=Tr_{k,n}(D_{k}\dots D_{n}X),

here we also introduce a convenient notation for the
R
-trace.
The
R
-trace has the cyclic property

\langle XR_{i,i+1}\rangle_{1,m}=\langle R_{i,i+1}X\rangle_{1,m}

for any matrix
X\in Mat_{N\times N}(\mathbb{C})^{\otimes m}\otimes U
and
1\leq i\leq m-1
, and it is normalized, so that

\langle R_{1,2}\rangle_{2,2}=\mathbb{I},\qquad\langle\mathbb{I}\rangle_{1,1}=q^{-1}[N]_{q}.

The
Reflection Equation algebra

\mathbb{M}(N)

is generated by
N^{2}
generators
m_{i}^{j}
, their permutation relations being written in the matrix form as

R_{1,2}M_{1}R_{1,2}M_{1}=M_{1}R_{1,2}M_{1}R_{1,2},

(4.4)

where
M=||m_{i}^{j}||_{1\leq i,j,\leq N}
.

For a matrix
K\in Mat_{N\times N}(\mathbb{C}(q))\otimes\mathbb{M}(N)
,
let us denote

K_{\overline{i}}=R_{i-1,i}\dots R_{1,2}K_{1}R_{1,2}^{-1}\dots R_{i-1,i}^{-1},

so that

K_{\bar{i}}=R_{i-1,i}K_{\overline{i-1}}R_{i-1,i}^{-1},\qquad i=2,3,\dots,m.

There is a sequence of mappings
\chi_{m,N}:\mathbb{H}_{m}\rightarrow\mathbb{M}(N)

from the Hecke algebras
\mathbb{H}_{m}
,
m=1,2,\dots
, to the Reflection Equation algebra
\mathbb{M}(N)
. They are defined by the formula

\chi_{m,N}(x)=\langle M_{\overline{1}}\dots M_{\overline{m}}\rho(x)\rangle_{1,m},\quad x\in\mathbb{H}_{m}.

Definition 11
.

The mappings
\chi_{m,N},\,\,m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
are called
characteristic mappings
.

The value
\chi_{m,N}(x)
of the characteristic mapping can be expressed as a polynomial in
the elements
p_{1},p_{2},\dots
defined by

p_{m}=\chi_{m,N}(C_{1m})=\langle M_{\overline{1}}\dots M_{\overline{m}}R_{m-1,m}R_{m-2,m-1}\dots R_{1,2}\rangle_{1,m}.

(4.5)

The elements
p_{i}
are called
quantum power sums
. They lie in the center of the Reflection Equation algebra and, therefore, the value
\chi_{m,N}(x)
of the characteristic mapping is a central element of the algebra
\mathbb{M}(N)
for any
x\in\mathbb{H}_{m}
.

Proposition 12
.

The universal characteristic mapping
\chi_{m}
is well-defined; the substitution
of Eq. (
4.5
)
transforms it into the characteristic mapping
\chi_{m,N}
.

Proof
.

In the case
N>m
, the elements
p_{1},\dots,p_{m}
of the algebra

as linear combinations of
\widehat{\Omega}_{m}
’s, we deduce that the limits of
\Omega_{m}(N)
are equal to
W_{m}(N)
.

6
Calculation of the quantum and classical
{\mathfrak{gl}}
-weight systems average values

In this section we will calculate the average value of the quantum
{\mathfrak{gl}}
-weight system and its limit. For any fixed
N\geq 1
, the value of the averaged quantum
{\mathfrak{gl}(N)}
-weight system is calculated by the formula

\displaystyle\Omega_{m}(N)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega_{m,N}(\mathbbm{h}_{m})=\frac{m!}{(\nu+m)!}\frac{[\nu+m]_{q}!}{[m]_{q}!}\sum_{l=0}^{n}(\nu+m)_{l}\times

(6.1)

\displaystyle\times\frac{1}{q^{2l}l!}\beta_{l}^{(\nu+m-l,\nu)}\left(\frac{\nu}{2}\right)S_{m-l}(x_{1},\dots,x_{N}).

Recall that
\nu=N-1
and
\beta_{m}^{(h,k)}(\xi)
is the q-Bernoulli polynomial of order
k
,

\beta_{m}^{(h,k)}(\xi)=\frac{1}{(1-q^{-2})^{m}}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{m}\binom{m}{i}(-q^{-2\xi})^{i}\frac{(i+h)_{k}}{([i+h]_{q})_{k}}.

Here
\xi
is a formal parameter which can be specialized at integer and half-integer points,
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
,
h,k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
and
h\geq k
.

The limit of the
q
-Bernoulli numbers
\beta_{m}^{(1,1)}=\beta_{m}^{(1,1)}(0)
of order
1
is well known and coincides with the classical Bernoulli numbers. The
q
-Bernoulli polynomial of order
k
can be expressed in terms of
q
-Bernoulli numbers of order 1, which allows us to calculate the limit

\lim\limits_{q\rightarrow 1}\beta_{m}^{(h,k)}(\xi)=B_{m}^{(k)}(\xi)

and prove that it does not depend on the value of
h
and is given by the generating series

\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}B_{m}^{(k)}(\xi)\frac{t^{m}}{m!}=e^{\xi t}\left(\frac{e^{t}-1}{t}\right)^{-k},

see Proposition
28
below.

We conclude that all the summands in the right hand side of Eq. (
6.1
) have limits as
q\to 1
and the limit values satisfy the relation

W_{m}(N)=w_{\mathfrak{gl}(N)}(\mathbbm{s}_{m})=\sum_{l=0}^{m}\frac{(\nu)_{l}}{l!}B_{l}^{(\nu)}\left(\frac{\nu}{2}\right)S_{m-l}(x_{1},\dots,x_{N}).

Collecting these equalities for all
n
in a generating series, we obtain the required relation of Theorem
4
for the
{\mathfrak{gl}(N)}
-weight system.

Proposition
2
and Corollary
16
show that if a polynomial relation holds for all
{\mathfrak{gl}(N)}
-weight systems, then it also holds for the universal
{\mathfrak{gl}}
-weight system, so that we obtain expressions (
2.2
) and (
2.1
) for the classical and quantum universal
{\mathfrak{gl}}
-weight systems, respectively.

6.1
Computation of the average value of the quantum
\mathfrak{gl}(N)
-weight system

In this section, we will prove that the average value of the quantum
{\mathfrak{gl}(N)}
-weight system is given by Eq. (
6.1
).
We start with a formula from
[
GPS
]
for the mean value of the quantum
character.

Proposition 24

(
[
GPS
]
)
.

In the Reflection Equation algebra, the following relation holds

\chi_{m}(\mathbbm{h}_{m})=\langle M_{\overline{1}}\dots M_{\overline{m}}H^{(m)}\rangle_{1,m}=q^{-m}S_{m}(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{N})=q^{-m}\sum\limits_{1\leq i_{1}\leq\dots\leq i_{m}\leq N}\xi_{i_{1}}\dots\xi_{i_{m}}.

Proposition 25
.

In terms of the
\xi
variables, the value of the quantum

{\mathfrak{gl}}
-weight system on the
q
-symmetrizer is as follows

\omega_{m,N}(\mathbbm{h}_{m})=\Omega_{m}(N)=\frac{1}{(q^{2}-1)^{m}}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{m}(-1)^{k}\binom{m}{k}\frac{([\nu+m+l]_{q})_{m-k}}{([m+l]_{q})_{m-k}}S_{k}(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{N}).

(6.2)

Proof
.

Combining Corollary
18
and formula (
5.3
) we obtain

\Omega_{m}(N)=\frac{1}{(q^{2}-1)^{m}}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{m}(-1)^{k}\binom{m}{k}\frac{([\nu+m+l]_{q})_{m-k}}{([m+l]_{q})_{m-k}}\chi_{k,N}(\mathbbm{h}_{k}).

It remains to apply Proposition
24
to the obtained expression.

Proposition 26
.

The one-part Schur polynomials have the property

S_{m}(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{N})=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{m}(-1)^{k}\binom{\nu+m}{m-k}S_{k}(u-\xi_{1},\dots,u-\xi_{N})u^{m-k}.

(6.3)

Proof
.

This results from the following simple calculation with generating series:

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}S_{m}(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{N})t^{m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{N}\frac{1}{1-\xi_{i}t}=\prod_{i=1}^{N}\frac{1}{1-ut+(u-\xi_{i})t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-ut)^{-N}\prod_{i=1}^{N}\frac{1}{1+(u-\xi_{i})\frac{t}{1-ut}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-ut)^{-N}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}S_{m}(u-\xi_{1},\dots,u-\xi_{N})\left(\frac{-t}{1-ut}\right)^{m}.

Expanding
(1-ut)^{-N}\left(\frac{-t}{1-ut}\right)^{m}
into a power series in
t
, we obtain the desired formula.

Corollary 27
.

Equation (
6.1
) is valid.

Proof
.

The coefficient of
S_{l}
in the expansion of
\Omega_{n}(N)
is the sum of the expressions

(-u)^{k-l}\frac{1}{(q^{2}-1)^{m-l}}\frac{m!}{l!(k-l)!(m-k)!}\times\frac{([\nu+m]_{q})_{k}(\nu+k)_{k-l}}{([m]_{q})_{m-k}(k)_{k-l}}.

This expression is obtained by substituting Eq. (
6.3
) into
Eq. (
6.2
).
After dividing the coefficient by
\frac{m!}{[m]_{q}!}\frac{[\nu+m]_{q}!}{(\nu+m)!}\frac{(\nu+m)_{l}}{(m-l)!}
, we get

(-u)^{k}\frac{1}{(q^{2}-1)^{m-l}}\frac{[k]_{q}!}{[\nu+k]_{q}!}\frac{(\nu+k)!}{k!}\frac{(m-l)!}{(m-k)!(k-l)!}.

The sum of these expressions for
k=l,l+1,l+2,\dots,m
coincides with the
q
-Bernoulli polynomial. Note that by the definition of the variables
x_{i}
we need to choose
u=q^{-\nu}=q^{-2\cdot\frac{\nu}{2}}
.

6.2
The limit of the
q
-Bernoulli polynomials

This section is devoted to a proof of the following result.

Proposition 28
.

The
q
-Bernoulli polynomial
\beta_{m}^{(h,k)}(\xi)

has a limit as
q\to 1
. This limit

B_{m}^{(k)}(\xi)=\lim_{q\to 1}\beta_{m}^{(h,k)}(\xi)

is independent of
h
and coincides with the classical Bernoulli polynomial,
whose sequence is defined by the generating series

\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}B_{m}^{(k)}(\xi)\frac{t^{m}}{m!}=e^{\xi t}\left(\frac{e^{t}-1}{t}\right)^{-k}.

The proof is based on the following recursions for the

q
-Bernoulli polynomials. Denote by
\beta_{m}^{(h,k)}=\beta_{m}^{(h,k)}(0)
,
the corresponding
m
th
q
-Bernoulli number.

Lemma 29
.

We have

\displaystyle\beta_{m}^{(h,k)}(\xi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{m}{m\choose i}\beta_{m-i}^{(h+i,k)}([\xi]_{q})^{i};

(6.4)

\displaystyle\beta_{m}^{(h,k)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i_{1}+\dots+i_{k}=m}{m\choose i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\beta_{i_{j}}^{(h-j+1+i_{1}+\dots+i_{j-1},1)};

(6.5)

\displaystyle\beta_{m}^{(h+1,1)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\beta_{m}^{(h,1)}-(1-q^{-2})\beta_{m+1}^{(h,1)}.

(6.6)

The first relation expresses the
q
-Bernoulli polynomials in terms of

q
-Bernoulli numbers. The second one reduces computation of

q
-Bernoulli numbers to the case
k=1
, and reduces the latter to the
case
h=1
. Thus, all these relations together express
q
-Bernoulli
polynomials in terms of the numbers
\beta_{m}^{(1,1)}
.

The relations of the last Lemma have classical analogues.
Namely, denoting
B_{m}^{(k)}=B_{m}^{(k)}(0)
,
k=0,1,2,\dots
, we have

\displaystyle B_{m}^{(k)}(\xi)=\sum_{i=0}^{m}{m\choose i}B_{m-i}^{(k)}\xi^{i};

(6.7)

\displaystyle B_{m}^{(k)}=\sum_{i_{1}+\dots+i_{k}=m}{m\choose i_{1},i_{2},\dots,i_{k}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}B_{i_{j}}^{(1)}.

(6.8)

The coefficients in these relations are obtained by taking the limit
q\to 1
.
Finally, we use the following result:

Proposition 30

(
[
C
]
)
.

For any
m>0
the limit as
q\rightarrow 1
of the
q
-Bernoulli number
\beta_{m}^{(1,1)}
of order
1
exists and is equal to the classical Bernoulli number
B_{m}^{(1)}(0)
.

Applying this Proposition and induction we obtain, step by step,
the following equations:

\lim_{q\to 1}\beta_{m}^{(h,1)}=B_{m}^{(1)};

\lim_{q\to 1}\beta_{m}^{(h,k)}=B_{m}^{(k)};

\lim_{q\to 1}\beta_{m}^{(h,k)}(\xi)=B_{m}^{(k)}(\xi).

Thus, it remains to prove Lemma
29
to complete the proof of Proposition
28
.

Let
q^{-N}
be a formal variable. Define the linear operator
\Phi_{y}:\mathbb{C}(q)[q^{-2y}]\rightarrow\mathbb{C}(q)
by the formula

\Phi_{y}(q^{-2ay})=\frac{a+1}{[a+1]_{q}},\quad a\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}.

This operator is a model of the
p
-adic
q
-Volkenborn integral
(see
[
KCR
]
and references therein).

Proposition 31
.

The
q
-Bernoulli polynomial admits the following description in
terms of the operator
\Phi_{y}
:

\beta_{m}^{(h,k)}(\xi)=\Phi_{y_{1}}\dots\Phi_{y_{k}}\left(q^{-2\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}(h-i)y_{i}}([\xi+y_{1}+\dots+y_{k}]_{q})^{m}\right).

(6.9)

Proof
.

A simple calculation shows that

\displaystyle q^{-2\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}(h-j)y_{j}}([\xi+y_{1}+\dots+y_{k}]_{q})^{m}=\frac{q^{-2\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}(h-j)y_{j}}}{(1-q^{-2})^{m}}(1-q^{-2(\xi+y_{1}+\dots+y_{k})})_{q}^{m}=

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(1-q^{-2})^{m}}\sum_{i=0}^{m}\binom{m}{i}(-q^{-2\xi})^{i}\prod_{j=1}^{k}q^{-2(h-j+i)y_{j}}.

The operators
\Phi_{y_{i}}
can be applied independently to each factor
q^{-2(h-j+i)y_{j}}
, hence applying the operator
\Phi_{y_{1}}\dots\Phi_{y_{k}}
to the last part of the formula, we obtain a
q
-Bernoulli polynomial.

We now use formula (
6.9
) to prove Lemma
29
.

Proof of formula (
6.4
).

Note that
[\xi+y]_{q}=[\xi]_{q}q^{-2y}+[y]_{q}
, using this we can calculate

\displaystyle\beta_{m}^{(h,k)}(\xi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Phi_{y_{1}}\dots\Phi_{y_{k}}\left(q^{-2\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}(h-i)y_{i}}\left(\left[\xi+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}y_{i}\right]_{q}\right)^{m}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Phi_{y_{1}}\dots\Phi_{y_{k}}\left(q^{-2\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}(h-i)y_{i}}\left(q^{-2\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}y_{i}}[\xi]_{q}+\left[\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}y_{i}\right]_{q}\right)^{\Large m}\,\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=0}^{m}([\xi]_{q})^{j}\binom{m}{j}\Phi_{y_{1}}\dots\Phi_{y_{k}}\left(q^{-2\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}(h-i+j)y_{i}}\left(\left[\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}y_{i}\right]_{q}\right)^{m-j}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=0}^{m}\binom{m}{j}([\xi]_{q})^{j}\beta_{m-j}^{(h+j,k)}.

Proof of formula (
6.5
).

Note that
[x+y]_{q}=[x]_{q}q^{-2y}+[y]_{q}
. Moreover, a simple induction on
k
shows that
\left[\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}y_{j}\right]_{q}=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}[y_{j}]_{q}q^{-2\sum\limits_{i<j}y_{i}}
. Therefore,

\left(\left[\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}y_{j}\right]_{q}\right)^{m}=\left(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}[y_{j}]_{q}q^{-2\sum\limits_{i<j}y_{i}}\right)^{m}=\sum\limits_{i_{1}+\dots+i_{k}=m}\binom{m}{i_{1},i_{2},\dots,i_{k}}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{k}([y_{j}]_{q})^{i_{j}}q^{-2\sum\limits_{i<j}i_{j}y_{j}}.

To obtain the result, it remains to use linearity of the operators
\Phi_{y}
and the following formula

\Phi_{y_{1}}\dots\Phi_{y_{k}}\left(q^{-2\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}(h-j)y_{j}}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{k}([y_{j}]_{q})^{i_{j}}q^{-2\sum\limits_{i<j}i_{j}y_{j}}\right)=\prod\limits_{j=1}^{k}\Phi_{y_{1}}(([y_{j}]_{q})^{i_{j}}q^{-2(h-j+i_{1}+\dots+i_{j-1})y_{i}}).

Proof of formula (
6.6
).

We have

\displaystyle\beta_{m}^{(h,1)}-(1-q^{-2})\beta_{m+1}^{(h,1)}=\Phi_{y}(q^{-2(h-1)y}([y]_{q})^{m}-q^{-2(h-1)y}(1-q^{-2})([y]_{q})^{m+1})=

\displaystyle=\Phi_{y}(q^{-2(h-1)y}([y]_{q})^{m}(1-(1-q^{-2y})))=\Phi_{y}(q^{-2hy}([y]_{q})^{m})=\beta_{m}^{(h+1,1)}.

Proof of formulas (
6.8
) and (
6.7
).

Using the obvious equality

\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}B_{m}^{(k)}(0)\frac{t^{m}}{m!}=\left(\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}B_{m}^{(1)}(0)\frac{t^{m}}{m!}\right)^{k}.

we obtain formula (

Formula (

\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}B_{m}^{(k)}(\xi)\frac{t^{m}}{m!}=e^{\xi t}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}B_{m}^{(k)}(0)\frac{t^{m}}{m!}.

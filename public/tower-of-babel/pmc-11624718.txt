Evaluating low back pain through electrodermal activity: a systematic review protocol
Authors: Sergio Sebastia-Amat, Juan Tortosa-Martinez, Gema Sanchis-Soler, Sergio Hernandez-Sanchez, Juan Manuel Cortell-Tormo
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Abstract AbstractIntroductionLow back pain (LBP) is a prevalent and multifaceted health condition affecting over 619 million people globally. Today, pain evaluation heavily relies on subjective measures, so more advanced diagnosis and treatment methods are required. Electrodermal activity (EDA) could represent a potential objective tool to assess LBP. Despite its promise, EDA has been less studied in this context, and the methodologies vary across studies. The systematic review described thus aims to determine the relationship between EDA and LBP as well as the potential utility of EDA as a tool for evaluating LBP. Introduction

Low back pain (LBP), also known as lumbar pain or lumbalgia, represents a complex, multifaceted health condition in which patients report pain or discomfort in the lower back region (associated with existing or potential tissue damage). LBP is characterised by a variety of pain types, including nociceptive, neuropathic, nociplastic or non-specific pain.1

LBP is classified according to LBP episode duration: acute LBP (<6 weeks), subacute LBP (6–12 weeks) and chronic LBP (>12 weeks).2 Acute LBP episodes are common, often self-limiting, and they can progress to chronic conditions. In fact, different studies suggest that a significant percentage of individuals continue to experience pain or relapses, especially in cases of mechanical LBP and new-onset lumbar radiculopathy.3 4

LBP affects more than 619 million people worldwide, and this number is expected to increase by 36% over the next three decades.5 LBP is the leading cause of disability worldwide, and the primary cause of work absence.5 6 The impact of LBP on daily life activities, emotional and social well-being, as well as on the economy makes it all the more important to develop strategies to prevent or treat the pathology. Proper pain assessment plays a key role in treating LBP, as it allows for an early and effective diagnosis.

However, pain is a subjective and personal experience, so obtaining a precise measurement of pain is a challenging task.7 Traditional pain assessment methods, such as questionnaires and subjective rating scales, rely heavily on the patient’s verbal communication or their ability to transform a subjective feeling into a quantitative number.8 This reliance can introduce inaccuracies and subjectivity.9 Therefore, Knezevic et al1 point out that future research should focus on improving the accuracy and objectivity of diagnostic evaluations, including algorithms that consider biological, psychological and social factors. In this regard, objective pain assessment in a clinical setting, combining different methods that integrate self-reported pain and physiological variables, could help to provide appropriate care. Automated pain detection using physiological data can be essential to make the diagnostic process more objective. Moreover, it presents numerous advantages: first, it overcomes the limitations of subjective pain assessment by providing an objective and quantifiable measure; second, automated pain detection can be useful in situations where traditional pain assessment may not be feasible or practical, such as continuous pain monitoring in clinical settings or research studies.

Among these physiological variables, electrodermal activity (EDA) has proven to be a useful indicator for assessing pain.10 EDA refers to changes in skin conductance which occur in response to emotional and psychological stimuli. According to a recent systematic review,11 EDA was one of the most widely used physiological signals to quantify pain experience. It is also notably one of the most widespread physiological indicators used in different fields of research, such as psychology and psychiatry.12 However, research on EDA signals is strikingly limited compared with other signals, such as cardiac signals. Indeed, despite the growing number of studies on EDA, fundamental features of EDA are still being unearthed.13

EDA activity has been employed in patients subjected to controlled painful stimuli, such as the application of heat, cold or pressure.10 14 15 These studies have revealed distinct EDA patterns in the presence of pain, thus supporting its utility as an objective pain assessment tool in clinical settings.14 Using machine learning techniques, the data can be analysed and processed automatically, allowing for objective pain detection and quantification. Furthermore, different studies expand its applicability to the detection of acute pain due to the association existing between emotional stress and pain, as well as its relationship with autonomic activation.16 17

Numerous factors play a role in the raw EDA signal. The latter encompass elements such as the circuit design (eg, accuracy, range of measurement and noise level), electrode configuration (considering shape, surface area, choice of materials, positioning and the pressure applied at the contact point), and the density of eccrine and other sweat glands at the particular location of each measurement.18 Given the wide range of factors that can influence EDA signals, it is essential to identify the most suitable variables specifically tailored for LBP assessment. Understanding and selecting the appropriate EDA variables are essential steps toward ensuring accurate and meaningful LBP assessment. This knowledge will contribute to refining the methodology, enhancing the reliability of results and ultimately optimising the utility of EDA in the assessment of LBP.

Given the importance of understanding how EDA can be applied to evaluate LBP and the optimal methodology to conduct such assessments, the main objective of this review is to determine the relationship between EDA and LBP. Therefore, the primary aim is to comprehend the potential utility of EDA as an LBP evaluation instrument. We will also analyse the various EDA measurement methodologies employed to detect LBP in the existing literature, with the ultimate goal of identifying the most reliable and effective approaches.

Methods and analysis

The present systematic review protocol was registered in the ‘International Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews’ (PROSPERO registration number CRD42023464812). This protocol is being reported in accordance with the PRISMA-P statement.19 The checklist has been attached in online supplemental file 1.

Eligibility criteria

This comprehensive review will encompass pertinent articles published in English or in Spanish, and there will be no limitations on publication dates. Articles need to satisfy all the specified inclusion criteria to be included in the review.

Studies will undergo a screening process based on the review objectives and adherence to the Participants, Interventions, Comparisons and Outcomes (PICO) criteria.

Population

The current review will include studies on individuals aged 18 years and above, suffering from either acute or chronic low back pain, who may or may not be following a rehabilitation programme and who may be leading either physically active or sedentary lifestyles. Provide a comparison with healthy subjects, provided that the study has a control group. This broad spectrum will allow a more comprehensive exploration of the relationship between EDA and LBP, considering potential variations related to rehabilitation, activity levels and their impact on EDA responses.

Interventions

This review will consider a wide range of research designs, including clinical trials, randomised controlled trials (RCTs), case reports, pilot cohort studies and clinical studies, all of which investigate the relationship between EDA and LBP. Each selected study should provide a comprehensive methodology that not only outlines the research protocol but also provides in-depth information about the variables under investigation.

Comparators

Inclusion in this review will not be contingent on the presence or absence of a control group, which will be determined by the study methodology. When the research methodology does not incorporate a training or rehabilitation programme, studies may be considered even if they lack a control group. However, in cases where a training or rehabilitation programme is an integral part of the study design, the inclusion criteria will require the presence of a control group.

These control groups, when present, should either undergo lighter and more traditional forms of training or therapy compared with the experimental group or serve as passive control groups. The rationale behind this inclusion criteria is to enable a thorough evaluation of the impact of different interventions or the absence thereof, shedding light on the effectiveness of various EDA approaches as a valid method to evaluate or monitor LBP. This approach ensures that the review encompasses a diverse range of studies, offering valuable insights into the subject matter.

Outcomes

Participants characterisation

A detailed examination of the participants will be present in each study. Sociodemographic details (including age, gender and cultural background) will be collected. Physical attributes such as general health and any pre-existing medical conditions will be considered. Additionally, physiological parameters such as heart rate, skin conductance and blood pressure will be scrutinised to ensure a holistic understanding of the study population.

Regarding the EDA results, a breakdown of the assessed domains will be provided, focusing on the following variables: phasic component of EDA, spectral features time-varying index of sympathetic activity and modified time-varying index of sympathetic activity. In cases where a training programme or therapy has been implemented, a comprehensive monitoring of all variables (pain and EDA) will be conducted both before and after the intervention. If the study follows a cross-sectional design, a single measurement will be taken.

Finally, regarding the results concerning the relationship between both variables—pain and EDA—we will not only present the correlation existing between them but also relevant explanations to understand this connection.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

The study will include adults with acute or chronic LBP. Clinical trials, RCTs, case reports, pilot cohort studies and clinical studies will be eligible. Studies involving any form of intervention must provide a clear definition of the intervention in the methodology section. Studies that do not clearly and precisely define the study population characteristics, the variables analysed or do not meet the inclusion criteria described above will be excluded. Participants who are taking medications that may have a direct effect on EDA or pain will also be excluded. Additionally, all studies not written in English or Spanish will be excluded.

Research questions

Can electrodermal activity be linked to the presence of low back pain?

Information sources and search strategy

The following databases will be used to identify studies to include in the systematic review: PubMed, Web of Science and Scopus. The search will be limited to reports written in English and Spanish without time restrictions.

To ensure a comprehensive search strategy, a range of terms related to EDA and LBP will be used. In the case of PubMed database, medical subject headings terms will also be used. Data extraction and data management

Search and selection process

In the initial phase, the search for articles across various databases will follow the inclusion and exclusion criteria mentioned above. All selected results will be stored in a Rayyan directory to remove duplicates.21 This platform will allow two independent reviewers to assess the suitability of the articles based solely on titles and abstracts in a blinded manner. Each reviewer will label the studies according to the inclusion criteria and document the reason for exclusion when applicable. Once this initial filtering is completed, the review status will switch to blind off, allowing the reviewers to compare their decisions. Should any discrepancies arise between the reviewers, a third reviewer will intervene to resolve them. The same process will be conducted with the full text of the selected articles to ensure the final selection is accurate and consistent.

Data extraction

After completing the selection process, the full text of the selected articles will be read to extract all relevant information: authors, year of publication, characteristics of the experimental and control groups, attributes of LBP, EDA domains, intervention characteristics and the relationship between EDA and LBP. Two independent reviewers will individually extract data from each study and document it in an access database with one file per study and per reviewer. If discrepancies arise between the two files of a study, a third reviewer will collaborate to resolve the issue. The database will be updated in line with the reviewers decisions on the Rayyan platform.

Data synthesis strategy and presentation

The data synthesis strategy will follow the recommendations of Campbell et al.22 To enhance the clarity and systematic organisation of the synthesised data, studies will be grouped based on both their distinct study designs and participant characteristics. This approach enables a thorough understanding of the research landscape while also allowing for a nuanced examination of potential variations among different study populations. Recognising the significance of controlling study heterogeneity, tables will be ordered by hypothesised modifiers, specifically focusing on study design and sample characteristics. This deliberate structuring aims to minimise confounding factors, allowing for a more comprehensive interpretation of the findings. Furthermore, the Grading of Recommendations, Assessment, Development, and Evaluations framework will be employed to ensure a rigorous assessment of the results, providing a systematic and transparent approach to grading the quality of evidence. This process facilitates both the categorisation of evidence quality (whether high or low) and the assessment of recommendation strength. It takes into account various factors, including risks and benefits, participants’ values and preferences, and the consumption of resources or costs.

The data will be structured into tables, categorising information based on methodology and final result grouping. The primary table will be designed to present key data, encompassing study design, participant characteristics and methodological details, offering a comprehensive overview of the foundations of the various studies.

The secondary table will provide a detailed depiction of the studies, outlining the specific variables analysed in each, focusing particularly on EDA-related factors. This table aims to present a more nuanced understanding of the diverse aspects explored within the domain of EDA.

To facilitate a comparative analysis, a graph will be generated to scrutinise the utilisation and prevalence of EDA-related variables across the studies. The objective of this graphical representation is to shed light on the percentage of use of the EDA domain.

Studies with a PEDro score of 7 or higher were deemed ‘high quality’, those scoring 5 or 6 were considered ‘moderate quality’ and those scoring 4 or lower were categorised as ‘low quality’. In this quality assessment process, two independent reviewers will be tasked with evaluating these critical factors. In cases where disagreements arise between the two reviewers, a third independent reviewer will resolve the discrepancy, thereby ensuring the consistency and accuracy of the quality assessment phase.

Risk of bias

The Cochrane Risk of Bias tool 2.0 (RoB2) for randomised controlled studies will be used for his purpose as recommended by Ma et al.24 The RoB2 includes seven domains to be checked: random sequence generation, allocation concealment, blinding (participants and evaluators) bling of outcome assessment, incomplete outcome data, selective reporting and other biases. Risk of bias for each of these domains can be qualified as high, low or unclear.

Two of the involved authors of this future systematic review will assess the included studies using the RoB2 independently. In case of discrepancy, a third author will participate in the discussion process to reach a consensus.

The status and timeline of the study

The search phase is planned to start on 8 July 2024 and to conclude on 30 November 2024. The systematic review is expected to be completed approximately 6 months following the publication of the protocol.

Discussion

Lumbar pain affects a broad spectrum of the global population and has a substantial impact on both national economies and public health.25 26

It is essential to be able to assess pain accurately in clinical settings, as this leads to a correct diagnosis, as well as proper and personalised medical care. However, pain is difficult to measure in a precise manner because it constitutes a subjective and personal experience. Conventional pain assessment methods are usually inaccurate.9 Therefore, it is particularly important to identify variables that enable predicting pain onset or adjusting treatments more accurately.

In this context, pain detection based on physiological data appears to be a possible solution. Among the various methods that exist, the EDA biomarker is potentially of interest. Changes in skin conductance in response to emotional and psychological stimuli could represent a valuable indicator to assess pain, as its use is widespread in other disciplines.2 27 Nevertheless, to the best of our knowledge, no systematic review has hitherto investigated the use of EDA as a potential biomarker to assess LBP. EDA, influenced by the sympathetic nervous system, fluctuates in response to mental stress. Changes in these EDA variables resulting from LBP may be observed. Since pain induces mental stress and anxiety, EDA measurements might reflect the experienced pain.14 28 Essentially, EDA alterations could serve as an indicator of pain felt due to LBP, as pain triggers sympathetic nervous system activity, influencing these physiological responses. Therefore, monitoring EDA changes could potentially offer insights into discomfort or pain levels associated with LBP, allowing to understand the relationship between pain, mental stress and their physiological impact.

Research in this field has the potential to improve how lumbar pain is assessed and addressed in the clinical environment. The findings of this study can help to improve healthcare by providing an objective and quantifiable method for measuring pain, which is especially valuable for patients who have difficulties in communicating, expressing or quantifying their sensations. Furthermore, it can be useful in situations where traditional pain assessment is not feasible or practical, such as continuous pain monitoring in clinical settings or in research studies.

Finally, the obtained results could help to improve patients’ rehabilitation processes by offering objective data on the improvement or worsening of their condition, leading to higher quality care, thus improving their quality of life.

Ethics and dissemination

We are committed to broadening the impact of our systematic review findings and thus plan to publish the results in a high-ranked, peer-reviewed health journal with immediate open access.

Adhering to Spanish guidelines, ethical approval is deemed unnecessary for our secondary analyses, given the de-identification and anonymous analysis of all data. The systematic review is expected to be completed approximately 6 months following the publication of the protocol.

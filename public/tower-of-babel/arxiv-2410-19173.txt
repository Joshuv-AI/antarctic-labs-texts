Probabilistic Representation of Commutative Quantum Circuit Models
Richard Yu, Jorge Ramirez, Elaine Wong

Abstract
In commuting parametric quantum circuits, the Fourier series of the pairwise fidelity can be expressed as the characteristic function of random variables. Furthermore, expressiveness can be cast as the recurrence probability of a random walk on a lattice. This construction has been successfully applied to the group composed only of Pauli- Z Z rotations, and we generalize this probabilistic strategy to any commuting set of Pauli operators. We utilize an efficient algorithm by van den Berg and Temme 2020 using the tableau representation of Pauli strings to yield a unitary from the Clifford group that, under conjugation, simultaneously diagonalizes our commuting set of Pauli rotations. Furthermore, we fully characterize the underlying distribution of the random walk using stabilizer states and their basis state representations. This would allow us to tractably compute the lattice volume and variance matrix used to express the frame potential. Together, this demonstrates a scalable strategy to calculate the expressiveness of parametric quantum models.



1
Introduction

In parametric quantum circuits (PQCs), we want to assess the complexity of the chosen quantum circuit architecture. A metric that can quantify this complexity is expressiveness which can be understood as the ability for a PQC to traverse the Bloch sphere. However, the computation of this quantity is typically intractable to perform directly due to the exponential scaling of qubit systems. We present a scalable computation strategy for approximating the frame potential, the central component of expressiveness, and demonstrate how it can be applied in example architectures.

The frame potential of an
n
qubit system is given by

\mathcal{F}_{U}(t):=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2N}}\int_{[-\pi,\pi]^{2N}}F_{U}(\boldsymbol{\theta},\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\prime})^{t}\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\theta}\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\prime},

where
N
,
n\leq N\leq 2^{n}
, is the number of gates, and we define the fidelity
F_{U}
parameterized by a unitary gate
U
as

F_{U}(\boldsymbol{\theta},\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\prime}):=\left|\langle 0|^{\otimes n}U(\boldsymbol{\theta})^{\dagger}U(\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\prime})|0\rangle^{\otimes n}\right|^{2}.

We typically express

U(\boldsymbol{\theta}):=\prod_{j=1}^{N}e^{i\theta_{j}H_{j}},

where
\theta_{j}\in[-\pi,\pi]
and
H_{j}
a Hermitian operator. In the paper
Ramirez et al. 2024
, a probabilistic method was developed to evaluate the frame potential of a parameterized quantum circuit. In general, this methodology can be employed whenever the base assumption that
{H_{j}}
forms a commuting set is satisfied. Define
\mathbb{S}_{n}
to be the Pauli group over
n
qubits, and let the subgroup
S\subset\mathbb{S}_{n}
to contain all Pauli strings using only
Z
and
I
gates. The probabilistic strategy in
Ramirez et al. 2024
was successfully applied to the subgroup
S
, but how this method can be used for other commuting sets was left as an open question. We seek to develop such a strategy, and further, more rigorously study possible ways to apply the probabilistic method to noncommutative scenarios.

From now on, we restrict
H_{1},\dots,H_{N}
to be
N
commuting Pauli operators in
\mathbb{S}_{N}
. Define
f_{U}(\boldsymbol{\theta})=\langle 0|^{\otimes n}U(\boldsymbol{\theta})|0\rangle^{\otimes n}
. Given the commutativity assumption, the fidelity can be expressed as

F_{U}(\boldsymbol{\theta},\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\prime})=|\langle 0|^{\otimes n}U(\boldsymbol{\theta}-\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\prime})|0\rangle^{\otimes n}|^{2}=|f_{U}(\boldsymbol{\theta}-\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\prime})|^{2}.

(1)

Since
H_{j}
is simultaneously diagonalizable, there exists a unitary
W
in the Clifford group such that
\Lambda_{j}=WH_{j}W^{\dagger}
is a diagonal matrix for all
1\leq j\leq N
. Therefore, we then have

f_{U}(\boldsymbol{\theta})=\langle 0|^{\otimes n}W^{\dagger}\operatorname{exp}(i(\theta_{1}\Lambda_{1}+\dots+\theta_{N}\Lambda_{N}))W|0\rangle^{\otimes n}.

Using this reduction, we assume the operator
U(\boldsymbol{\theta})=\exp(i\theta_{1}\Lambda_{1}+\dots+i\theta_{N}\Lambda_{N})
is the matrix exponential of the sum of diagonal matrices and rewrite

f_{U}(\boldsymbol{\theta})=\langle\psi_{0}|U(\boldsymbol{\theta})|\psi_{0}\rangle\text{ where }|\psi_{0}\rangle=W|0\rangle^{\otimes n}.

A Cartan subalgebra of
\mathbb{S}_{n}
is a maximal commuting set of Pauli strings that has
2^{n}-1
operators. The group
\mathbb{S}_{n}
can be partitioned into
2^{n}+1
such families. The set of operators
H_{1},\dots H_{N}
can be considered a subset of such a family, and the unitary
W
can, under conjugation, map that family back to the subgroup
S
.

2
The Probabilistic Representation of Fidelity

We can interpret
f_{U}(\boldsymbol{\theta})
from a probabilistic perspective. From
Ramirez et al. 2024
, we know that the quantum expectation
f_{U}
is a positive-definite function. And so, by Bochner’s theorem,
f_{U}
is the characteristic function of some random variable
K
. For the construction of
K
, denote

\boldsymbol{k}_{x}:=(k_{x}^{(1)},\dots,...,k_{x}^{(N)}),x=0,...,2^{n}-1,

and write
\operatorname{diag}(\theta_{1}\Lambda_{1}+\dots+\theta_{N}\Lambda_{N})=\left(\boldsymbol{\theta}\cdot\boldsymbol{k}_{0}\ \dots\ \boldsymbol{\theta}\cdot\boldsymbol{k}_{2^{n}-1}\right)^{\intercal}
. Let
\boldsymbol{u}_{x}\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
denote the integer
x
written in its
n
-bit representation as a vector. Then, we find that

f_{U}(\boldsymbol{\theta})=\tr\left(|\psi_{0}\rangle\langle\psi_{0}|e^{i(\theta_{1}\Lambda_{1}+\dots+\theta_{N}\Lambda_{N})}\right)=\sum_{x=0}^{2^{n}-1}|\langle\psi_{0}|\boldsymbol{u}_{x}\rangle|^{2}e^{i\boldsymbol{\theta}\cdot\boldsymbol{k}_{x}}=\mathbb{E}[e^{i\boldsymbol{\theta}\cdot\boldsymbol{k}_{X}}],

(2)

where
X
is a random variable taking on values
\{0,...,2^{n}-1\}
and

\sum_{x=0}^{2^{n}-1}|\langle\psi_{0}|\boldsymbol{u}_{x}\rangle|^{2}=1

since
|\psi_{0}\rangle
is a normalized quantum state. Then,
\mathbb{P}(X=x)=|\langle\psi_{0}|\boldsymbol{u}_{x}\rangle|^{2}
. We will think of
\boldsymbol{k}_{X}
as rows being randomly chosen from the matrix

\boldsymbol{K}:=\begin{pmatrix}-&\boldsymbol{k}_{0}&-\\
&\vdots&\\
-&\boldsymbol{k}_{2^{n}-1}&-\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2^{n}\times N}.

(3)

If we define the random variable
K=\boldsymbol{k}_{X}
, we can define the probability distribution of
K
as

\mathbb{P}(K=\boldsymbol{k})=\sum_{x:\boldsymbol{k}_{x}=\boldsymbol{k}}|\langle\psi_{0}|\boldsymbol{u}_{x}\rangle|^{2}.

(4)

The moments of
K
are determined by the Hamiltonians and the base state. The expected value of the
j
-th entry of the vector
K
is

\mathbb{E}K^{(j)}=\sum_{x=0}^{2^{n}-1}k_{x}^{(j)}|\langle\psi_{0}|\boldsymbol{u}_{x}\rangle|^{2}=\langle\psi_{0}|\Lambda_{j}|\psi_{0}\rangle,

(5)

namely, the quantum expected value of
H_{j}
in the state
\psi_{0}
. Similarly, its covariance matrix is

\mathrm{Var}(K)_{i,j}=\langle\psi_{0}|\Lambda_{i}\Lambda_{j}|\psi_{0}\rangle-\langle\psi_{0}|\Lambda_{i}|\psi_{0}\rangle\langle\psi_{0}|\Lambda_{j}|\psi_{0}\rangle,\quad i,j=1,\dots,N.

(6)

Through some derivations from
Ramirez et al. 2024
, the frame potential can then be approximated by

\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{U}(t)=\frac{V_{U}}{\sqrt{(4\pi t)^{N}\operatorname{det}(\operatorname{Var}(K))}}.

(7)

The lattice volume
V_{U}
is determined completely by the probability distribution of
K
. Once the distribution is known,
V_{U}
can be easily calculated. Therefore, we will focus on deriving the distribution of
K
.

3
An Expression for the Distribution of
K

For a commuting set
\{H_{j}:1\leq j\leq N\}
of Pauli operators in

\mathbb{S}_{n}
, there exists a unitary
W
in the Clifford group such that
\Lambda_{j}=WHW^{\dagger}\in S
for all
1\leq j\leq N
. Utilizing this property, we can define the matrix
\boldsymbol{A}
as in
Ramirez et al. 2024
. For instance, this would mean that the three strings
IZI
,
ZZI
,
IIZ
will be encoded in the matrix
\boldsymbol{A}
as the rows

\boldsymbol{A}=\left(\begin{tabular}[c]{ccc}0&1&0\\
1&1&0\\
0&0&1\end{tabular}\right).

We can then use this matrix to determine the rows of
K
and its distribution.

Theorem 1
.

If we express a value of the random variable
K
as
(-1)^{\boldsymbol{b}}
for some vector
\boldsymbol{b}\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{N}
, the probability that the row is selected is

\mathbb{P}(K=(-1)^{\boldsymbol{b}})=\sum_{x:\boldsymbol{A}\boldsymbol{u}_{x}=\boldsymbol{b}}|\langle\psi_{0}|\boldsymbol{u}_{x}\rangle|^{2}.

(8)

Proof.

Suppose for some index
x\in{0,\dots,2^{n}-1}
, we want to find the row
\boldsymbol{k}_{x}
given that
\operatorname{diag}(\Lambda_{j})=\boldsymbol{K}^{(j)}
. We know that each
\Lambda_{j}
can be represented as a Pauli string of
n
qubits, composed of
I
and
Z
gates. We note
A_{j}^{(m)}
,
1\leq m\leq n
, equals 1 if the
m
-th operator of the Pauli string representation of
\Lambda_{j}
is a Pauli-
Z
operator, and 0 otherwise. Then, the diagonal of
\Lambda_{j}
can be expressed as

\operatorname{diag}(\Lambda_{j})=(-1)^{\begin{pmatrix}0\\
A_{j}^{(1)}\end{pmatrix}}\otimes(-1)^{\begin{pmatrix}0\\
A_{j}^{(2)}\end{pmatrix}}\otimes\dots\otimes(-1)^{\begin{pmatrix}0\\
A_{j}^{(n)}\end{pmatrix}}.

We find that
K_{x}^{(j)}=(-1)^{p}
with

p=\bigoplus_{m=1}^{n}A_{j}^{(m)}u_{x}^{(m)}

where
\oplus
denotes addition modulo 2.

This means that the row
\boldsymbol{k}_{x}
can be expressed as
\boldsymbol{k}_{x}=(-1)^{\boldsymbol{A}\boldsymbol{u}_{x}}
. Since each index
x
is distributed according to
\mathbb{P}(X=x)=|\langle\psi_{0}|\boldsymbol{u}_{x}\rangle|^{2}
, we can then write our distribution as

\mathbb{P}(K=(-1)^{\boldsymbol{b}})=\sum_{x:\boldsymbol{A}\boldsymbol{u}_{x}=\boldsymbol{b}}\mathbb{P}(X=x)=\sum_{x:\boldsymbol{A}\boldsymbol{u}_{x}=\boldsymbol{b}}|\langle\psi_{0}|\boldsymbol{u}_{x}\rangle|^{2}

for any vector
\boldsymbol{b}\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{N}
.

∎

The above proof is largely identical to what is found in
Ramirez et al. 2024
, but we just determined how to extract the value of a row given its index based only on the matrix
\boldsymbol{A}
.

4
Stabilizer States and their Amplitudes

We need to determine the distribution
\mathbb{P}(X=x)=|\langle\psi_{0}|\boldsymbol{u}_{x}\rangle|^{2}
to find the distribution of
K
. Since
W
is in the Clifford group, it can be represented as some combination of Clifford gates. This means that
|\psi_{0}\rangle=W|0\rangle^{\otimes n}
is a stabilizer state, namely, a quantum state that is obtainable from
|0\rangle^{\otimes n}
by applying only just Controlled-NOT gates, Hadamard gates, and Phase gates (
Aaronson and Gottesman 2004
). From
(
García et al. 2014
, Theorem 9)
, for any
n
-qubit stabilizer state
|\psi_{0}\rangle
, there exist
\boldsymbol{c},\boldsymbol{t}\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n},\boldsymbol{R}
an
n\times r
binary matrix with column rank
r
, and
n\times n
symmetric binary matrix
\boldsymbol{Q}
such that

|\psi_{0}\rangle=\frac{1}{2^{r/2}}\sum_{\boldsymbol{z}\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{r}}i^{2\boldsymbol{c}^{\intercal}f(\boldsymbol{z})+f(\boldsymbol{z})^{\intercal}\boldsymbol{Q}f(\boldsymbol{z})}|f(\boldsymbol{z})\rangle,\quad f(\boldsymbol{z})=\boldsymbol{Rz}+\boldsymbol{t}.

(9)

From
Dehaene and De Moor 2003
, there exists an efficient construction for
\boldsymbol{R},\boldsymbol{t}
such that we can generate all basis states with non-zero amplitudes. Using this information, we can specify the distribution of
X
. We know that for any two basis states
|a\rangle
and
|b\rangle
,
\langle a|b\rangle=\delta_{ab}
since they form an orthonormal basis. This allows us to compute the probability distribution by substituting (
9
):

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(X=x)

\displaystyle=|\langle\psi_{0}|\boldsymbol{u}_{x}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left|\langle\boldsymbol{u}_{x}|2^{-r/2}\sum_{\boldsymbol{z}\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{r}}i^{2\boldsymbol{c}^{\intercal}f(\boldsymbol{z})+f(\boldsymbol{z})^{\intercal}\boldsymbol{Q}f(\boldsymbol{z})}|f(\boldsymbol{z})\rangle\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{r}}\left|\sum_{\boldsymbol{z}\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{r}}i^{2c^{\intercal}f(\boldsymbol{z})+f(\boldsymbol{z})^{\intercal}\boldsymbol{Q}f(\boldsymbol{z})}\langle\boldsymbol{u}_{x}|f(\boldsymbol{z})\rangle\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2^{r}}&\boldsymbol{u}_{x}\in\operatorname{range}(f)\\
0&\operatorname{otherwise}\end{cases}.

Therefore, this means that
\mathbb{P}(X=x)
is either zero or
2^{-r}
, and neither value depends on
x
. Now, we recall that the distribution of
K
can be expressed in terms of
X
using
Eq.

8
. Having obtained a concise expression for the probability distribution of
X
, we find that

\mathbb{P}(K=(-1)^{\boldsymbol{b}})=\sum_{x:\boldsymbol{A}\boldsymbol{u}_{x}=\boldsymbol{b}}|\langle\psi_{0}|\boldsymbol{u}_{x}\rangle|^{2}=2^{-r}\#\{\boldsymbol{z}\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{r}:\boldsymbol{A}f(\boldsymbol{z})=\boldsymbol{b}\}.

(10)

We know that
\#\{\boldsymbol{z}\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{r}:\boldsymbol{A}f(\boldsymbol{z})=\boldsymbol{b}\}
equals
2^{\operatorname{dim}(\operatorname{null}(\boldsymbol{AR}))}
if
\boldsymbol{b}\in\operatorname{range}(\boldsymbol{A}f)
or
0
otherwise. We note that
\boldsymbol{AR}\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{N\times r}
, and by setting the restriction
N\geq n\geq r
,
\operatorname{dim\ null}(\boldsymbol{AR})=r-\operatorname{rank}(\boldsymbol{AR})
. Therefore, we can then write

\mathbb{P}(K=(-1)^{\boldsymbol{b}})=\begin{cases}2^{-\operatorname{rank}(\boldsymbol{AR})}&\boldsymbol{b}\in\operatorname{range}(\boldsymbol{A}f)\\
0&\operatorname{otherwise}\end{cases}.

(11)

This will allow us to concisely define the probability distribution of
K
. If we were to reference the result in
Ramirez et al. 2024
, they found that

\mathbb{P}(K=(-1)^{\boldsymbol{b}})=2^{-\operatorname{rank}(\boldsymbol{A})}.

Since they made the assumption that their starting state
|\psi_{0}\rangle
was maximally mixed, they effectively set
r=n
, and
\operatorname{rank}(\boldsymbol{AR})=\operatorname{rank}(\boldsymbol{A})
.

5
Characterization of the Distribution
K

We know the distribution of
X
if we know the set of basis states that have nonzero amplitudes with respect to
|\psi_{0}\rangle
. We will find a construction of
\boldsymbol{R},\boldsymbol{t}
such that the set defined by
J=\{|\boldsymbol{R}\boldsymbol{z}+\boldsymbol{t}\rangle:\boldsymbol{z}\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{r}\}
yields all
2^{r}
basis states that have nonzero amplitudes. Tableaus are employed by
Aaronson and Gottesman 2004
to represent stabilizer states, and there are measurement procedures on these tableaus that will inform us the set of measurable outcomes from
|\psi_{0}\rangle
. These measurement operations and their effects on the tableaus of stabilizer states will provide a mechanism to identify
\boldsymbol{R}
and
\boldsymbol{t}
.

Denote
\operatorname{H}(a)
to be the Pauli string of
n
qubits with all positions set to
I
, except the
a
-th position is the Pauli-
Z
matrix. Define
M_{q,0}
and
M_{q,1}
to be the projection operator of the Pauli string
\operatorname{H}(q)
’s positive and negative eigenspaces respectively, namely
M_{q,0}=(I+\operatorname{H}(q))/2
and
M_{q,1}=(I-\operatorname{H}(q))/2
.

We will define
\tau(|\psi_{0}\rangle)
to be the tableau representation of the stabilizer state
|\psi_{0}\rangle
from
Aaronson and Gottesman 2004
. A stabilizer state has the property that it can be uniquely represented by its Pauli stabilizer group which has
n
generators. A tableau can be written in the following manner:

\tau(|\psi_{0}\rangle)=\left(\begin{tabular}[c]{ccc|ccc|c}$x_{11}$&$\cdots$&$x_{1n}$&$z_{11}$&$\cdots$&$z_{1n}$&$r_{1}$\\
$\vdots$&$\ddots$&$\vdots$&$\vdots$&$\ddots$&$\vdots$&$\vdots$\\
$x_{n1}$&$\cdots$&$x_{nn}$&$z_{n1}$&$\cdots$&$z_{nn}$&$r_{n}$\\
\hline\cr$x_{\left(n+1\right)1}$&$\cdots$&$x_{\left(n+1\right)n}$&$z_{\left(n+1\right)1}$&$\cdots$&$z_{\left(n+1\right)n}$&$r_{n+1}$\\
$\vdots$&$\ddots$&$\vdots$&$\vdots$&$\ddots$&$\vdots$&$\vdots$\\
$x_{\left(2n\right)1}$&$\cdots$&$x_{\left(2n\right)n}$&$z_{\left(2n\right)1}$&$\cdots$&$z_{\left(2n\right)n}$&$r_{2n}$\end{tabular}\right).

The bottom
n
rows represent the generators of the stabilizer group of
|\psi_{0}\rangle
, with each row representing a Pauli string. The bits
x_{ij}z_{ij}
represents the
j
-th Pauli operator of the string:
00\rightarrow I
,
01\rightarrow\sigma_{Z}
,
10\rightarrow\sigma_{X}
, and
11\rightarrow\sigma_{Y}
. The top
n
rows are dubbed "destabilizer" generators which together with the bottom
n
Pauli strings generate the entire Pauli group. Define
X(|\psi_{0}\rangle)
and
Z(|\psi_{0}\rangle)
to be the
X
and
Z
matrix portions of the tableau respectively, and the square matrix
\overline{X}(|\psi_{0}\rangle)
to be the last
n
rows of the matrix
X(|\psi_{0}\rangle)
. Let
S(|\psi_{0}\rangle)
denote the sign column of
\tau(|\psi_{0}\rangle)
.

From
Aaronson and Gottesman 2004
, the tableau formalism is sufficient for us to efficiently simulate any quantum circuit made from Clifford gates. Further, this simulation procedure will yield states that are reachable from measurements of stabilizer states, which correspond precisely to those states where
|\langle\psi_{0}|\boldsymbol{u}_{x}\rangle|^{2}\neq 0
. We then can prove the following theorem.

Theorem 2
.

Let
|\psi_{0}\rangle
be a stabilizer state, and suppose
|\boldsymbol{u}_{x}\rangle
is a basis state such that
|\langle\psi_{0}|\boldsymbol{u}_{x}\rangle|^{2}\neq 0
. Let
\boldsymbol{R}
and
\boldsymbol{t}
be as in
Eq.

9
. Then,
\boldsymbol{R}
is a nonsingular matrix whose column space spans the row space
\overline{X}(|\psi_{0}\rangle)
and
|\boldsymbol{t}\rangle=|\boldsymbol{u}_{x}\rangle
. Further,
J=\{|\boldsymbol{Rz}+\boldsymbol{t}\rangle:\boldsymbol{z}\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{r}\}
is the set of all basis states with nonzero amplitudes with respect to
|\psi_{0}\rangle
.

Proof.

From
Aaronson and Gottesman 2004
, when a measurement on an arbitrary qubit
a
is performed with respect to a stabilizer state
|\mu\rangle
, there are only two possibilities:

1.

The measurement outcome is "random", namely

\langle\mu|M_{a,0}^{\dagger}M_{a,0}|\mu\rangle=\langle\mu|M_{a,1}^{\dagger}M_{a,1}|\mu\rangle=\frac{1}{2}.

In this case, denote the measurement outcome to be
s\in\{0,1\}
. Then the resulting state after measurement is given by

\frac{M_{a,s}|\mu\rangle}{\sqrt{\langle\mu|M_{a,s}^{\dagger}M_{a,s}|\mu\rangle}}=\sqrt{2}M_{a,s}|\mu\rangle.

2.

The measurement outcome
s\in\{0,1\}
is determinate and
M_{a,s}|\mu\rangle=|\mu\rangle
.

Let
|\psi_{0}\rangle
be a stabilizer state, and suppose
|\boldsymbol{u}_{x}\rangle
is a basis state such that
|\langle\psi_{0}|\boldsymbol{u}_{x}\rangle|^{2}\neq 0
. Since
|\boldsymbol{u}_{x}\rangle
is a measurable outcome from
|\psi_{0}\rangle
, we know that

|\boldsymbol{u}_{x}\rangle=2^{r/2}M_{n,u_{x}^{(n)}}M_{n-1,u_{x}^{(n-1)}}\dots M_{1,u_{x}^{(1)}}|\psi_{0}\rangle,

(12)

where
r
is some positive integer that represents the number of measurements with "random" outcomes.

It follows that there exists
r
qubits
\{q_{1},q_{2},\dots,q_{r}\}\subset\{1,\dots,n\}
where

|\boldsymbol{u}_{x}\rangle=2^{r/2}M_{q_{r},u_{x}^{(q_{r})}}M_{q_{r-1},u_{x}^{(q_{r-1})}}\dots M_{q_{1},u_{x}^{(q_{1})}}|\psi_{0}\rangle.

(13)

Define
|\psi_{t}\rangle=2^{t/2}M_{q_{t},u_{x}^{(q_{t})}}\dots M_{q_{1},u_{x}^{(q_{1})}}|\psi_{0}\rangle
for
1\leq t\leq r
. For a fixed
t
, we want to identify the action on the tableau
\tau(|\psi_{t-1}\rangle)
that mirrors the projection operator
M_{q_{t},u_{x}^{(q_{t})}}
applied to the state
|\psi_{t-1}\rangle
.

We know that the measurement of qubit
q_{t}
with respect to
|\psi_{t-1}\rangle
yields a random outcome. According to
Aaronson and Gottesman 2004
, this is the case if and only if there exists
p_{t}\in\{n+1,\dots,2n\}
such that
X(|\psi_{t-1}\rangle)_{p_{t}}^{(q_{t})}=1
. In the tableau representation, when
M_{q_{t},u_{x}^{(q_{t})}}
is applied, we can use the following algorithm to obtain
\tau(|\psi_{t}\rangle)
from
\tau(|\psi_{t-1}\rangle)
:

1.

For every
i\in\{1,\dots,2n\}
such that
i\neq p_{t}
and
X(|\psi_{t-1}\rangle)_{i}^{(q_{t})}=1
, add row
i
with row
p_{t}
and replace row
i
with the new row.

2.

Set the row
\tau(|\psi_{t}\rangle)_{p_{t}-n}=\tau(|\psi_{t-1}\rangle)_{p_{t}}
.

3.

Set the row
\tau(|\psi_{t}\rangle)_{p_{t}}=0
except
Z(|\psi_{t}\rangle)_{p_{t}}^{(q_{t})}=1
.

4.

Assign
S(|\psi_{t}\rangle)_{p_{t}}
the value
u_{x}^{(q_{t})}
.

We make some observations on the rank of
X(|\psi_{t}\rangle)
. In step 1, we remove
\overline{X}(|\psi_{t-1}\rangle)_{p_{t}}
from any other row in
\overline{X}(|\psi_{t-1}\rangle)
which can be expressed as a linear sum of other row vectors that includes
\overline{X}(|\psi_{t-1}\rangle)_{p_{t}}
. Therefore, the rank is not altered by step 1. In step 3, the row
\overline{X}(|\psi_{t-1}\rangle)_{p_{t}}
is set to zero, and so, complementing this with step 1, we find that

\operatorname{rank}(\overline{X}(|\psi_{t}\rangle))=\operatorname{rank}(\overline{X}(|\psi_{t-1}\rangle))-1.

Measurements done on any qubit for a single basis state must be determinate. This implies that
\overline{X}(|\boldsymbol{u}_{x}\rangle)
is identically zero. Since each measurement with a random outcome decreases the rank of
\overline{X}(|\psi_{t}\rangle)
by one, then
r=\operatorname{rank}(\overline{X}(|\psi_{0}\rangle))
. Moreover, from the measurement procedure,
X(|\psi_{0}\rangle)_{p_{t}}
must be linearly independent of the set
\{X(|\psi_{0}\rangle)_{p_{s}}:1\leq s\leq t-1\}
. Therefore,
\{X(|\psi_{0}\rangle)_{p_{t}}:1\leq t\leq r\}
is a linearly independent set of rows. Furthermore, since each row
p_{t}
is copied to row
p_{t}-n
during the
r
applications of Step 2, then the row space of
\overline{X}(|\psi_{0}\rangle)
is

\sspan\{X(|\boldsymbol{u}_{x}\rangle)_{p_{t}-n}:1\leq t\leq r\}=\sspan\{X(|\psi_{0}\rangle)_{p_{t}}:1\leq t\leq r\}.

We now identify the new basis state measured if one of the
r
measurement outcomes that produced
|\boldsymbol{u}_{x}\rangle
in
13
is negated. This will provide us a means to generate all other measurement possibilities. Without loss of generality, suppose we replace the
r
-th operator
M_{q_{r},u_{x}^{(q_{r})}}
in
Eq.

13
with
M_{q_{r},u_{x}^{(q_{r})}\oplus 1}
(since the projection operators are commutative, any other index can be chosen and then the corresponding projection rotated to the last position). This would yield a new basis state
|\boldsymbol{u}_{y}\rangle
which is also a measurable outcome from
|\psi_{0}\rangle
. From the measurement procedure with random outcomes, this means that
\tau(|\boldsymbol{u}_{y}\rangle)
would equal
\tau(|\boldsymbol{u}_{x}\rangle)
except that
S(|\boldsymbol{u}_{y}\rangle)_{p_{r}}=S(|\boldsymbol{u}_{x}\rangle)_{p_{r}}\oplus 1
, as the only difference was the sign assignment at step 4 on the very last projection operator.

We can calculate the basis state
|\boldsymbol{u}_{y}\rangle
given its tableau representation. We know
\overline{X}(|\boldsymbol{u}_{y}\rangle)
is identically zero. If we were to perform a measurement on a qubit
q
, it would be determinate. From the determinate measurement procedure described in
Aaronson and Gottesman 2004
, the measurement outcome
u_{y}^{(q)}
is equal to the last entry of the summation of all the rows
\tau(|\boldsymbol{u}_{y}\rangle)_{i+n}
over
1\leq i\leq n
such that
X(|\boldsymbol{u}_{y}\rangle)_{i}^{(q)}=1
. In other words, since
\overline{X}(|\boldsymbol{u}_{y}\rangle)
is identically zero, we find that

u_{y}^{(q)}=\bigoplus_{1\leq i\leq n:X(|\boldsymbol{u}_{y}\rangle)_{i}^{(q)}=1}S(|\boldsymbol{u}_{y}\rangle)_{i+n}.

If we then consider the measurement of the same qubit
q
for
\tau(|\boldsymbol{u}_{x}\rangle)
, then we could rewrite the expression as

u_{y}^{(q)}=u_{x}^{(q)}\oplus\begin{cases}1&\text{if }X(|\boldsymbol{u}_{x}\rangle)_{p_{r}-n}^{(q)}=1,\\
0&\operatorname{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Namely,
|\boldsymbol{u}_{y}\rangle=|\boldsymbol{u}_{x}\oplus X(|\boldsymbol{u}_{x}\rangle)_{p_{r}-n}\rangle
. This holds true when
p_{r}
is replaced by any
p_{t}
in
13
. Moreover, for any sequence of indices
\{t_{1},\dots,t_{m}:1\leq m\leq r\}\subset\{1,\dots,r\}
, we will have

|\langle\psi_{0}|\boldsymbol{u}_{x}\oplus X(|\boldsymbol{u}_{x}\rangle)_{p_{t_{1}}-n}\oplus\dots\oplus X(|\boldsymbol{u}_{x}\rangle)_{p_{t_{m}}-n}\rangle|^{2}\neq 0.

This indicates that
|\langle\psi_{0}|\boldsymbol{u}_{x}\oplus\boldsymbol{v}\rangle|^{2}\neq 0
for any
\boldsymbol{v}
in
\operatorname{span}\{X(|\boldsymbol{u}_{x}\rangle)_{p_{t}-n}:1\leq t\leq r\}
, which is identical to the row space of
\overline{X}(|\psi_{0}\rangle)
.

Finally, let
\boldsymbol{R}\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n\times r}
be the nonsingular matrix whose columns span the row space of
\overline{X}(|\psi_{0}\rangle)
. Writing
\boldsymbol{v}=\boldsymbol{R}\boldsymbol{z}
for
\boldsymbol{z}\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{r}
and
\boldsymbol{t}=\boldsymbol{u}_{x}
, we get the desired characterization of the set
J
of all basis states with nonzero amplitudes in
|\psi_{0}\rangle
.

∎

We observe that to find a measurable outcome
|\boldsymbol{u}_{x}\rangle
from
|\psi_{0}\rangle
, we can use the simulation procedure described in
Aaronson and Gottesman 2004
to find that basis state in
O(n^{2})
time. We can then use our construction of
\boldsymbol{R}
and
\boldsymbol{t}
in
Eq.

11
to yield the distribution of
K
which then allows us to compute the lattice volume that approximates the frame potential.

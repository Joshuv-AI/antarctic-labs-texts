Morse inequalities for noncompact manifolds
Tsuyoshi Kato, Daisuke Kishimoto, Mitsunobu Tsutaya

Abstract
. We establish Morse inequalities for a noncompact manifold with a cocompact and properly discontinuous action of a discrete group, where Morse functions are not necessarily invariant under the group action. The inequalities are given in terms of the L 2 L^{2} -Betti numbers and functions on the acting group which describe rough configurations of critical points of a Morse function.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
57R19, 58E05

1.
Introduction

Morse inequalities relate the number of critical points of a Morse function on a compact manifold to its Betti numbers. Here, compactness of a manifold is essential as it guarantees finiteness of the number of critical points and the Betti numbers. Then it is challenging to generalize Morse inequalities to noncompact manifolds. There should have been attempts for it, but there is no result except for the very special cases that critical points are finitely many
[
4
]
and have strong symmetry
[
16
]
.

Throughout the paper, let
M
be a connected
n
-dimensional manifold without boundary, possibly noncompact, equipped with a cocompact and properly discontinuous action of a discrete group
G
such that the orbit manifold
M/G
is oriented. Namely, we will consider a Galois covering
G\to M\to M/G
such that
M
is connected and
M/G
is a closed oriented
n
-dimensional manifold. We fix any
G
-invariant metric on
M
. If
G
is amenable, then a manifold
M
is a typical example of a manifold having bounded geometry and a regular exhaustion, for which the index theory is developed in
[
18
,
19
]
. The purpose of this paper is to establish Morse inequalities for a certain Morse function on
M
which is essentially irrelevant to the action of
G
.

Roe
[
18
]
crudely classified generalizations of the index theorem to noncompact manifolds into three types. In particular, for the type II theorems, including Atiyah’s
\Gamma
-index theorem
[
1
]
and Roe’s index theorem
[
18
,
19
]
, the index is interpreted through some kind of averaging or renormalization procedure. Our approach takes such procedure. To generalize Morse inequalities to noncompact manifolds, we need to replace Betti numbers by other invariants because the Betti numbers of a noncompcat manifold may not be defined. For a manifold
M
, there is a nice variant of Betti numbers,
L^{2}
-Betti numbers
, and we employ them for our purpose. As in
[
13
, Example 1.37]
,
L^{2}
-Betti numbers may be thought of as a kind of average of Betti numbers, so they should match our approach. On the other hand, Gromov
[
6
]
proved that vanishing of
L^{2}
-Betti numbers is a quasi-isometry invariant, and as in
[
17
]
, for a large class of groups,
L^{2}
-Betti numbers themselves are proved to be quasi-isometry invariants. Here, quasi-isometry invariants are the object of study in large scale geometry. However, we only use some results in large scale geometry, and do not work in it because quasi-isometries identify a compact set with a single point so that the number of critical points does not make sense.

Let us introduce Morse functions that we are going to consider. It is shown in
[
8
]
that every connected noncompact manifold admits a function without critical points. To exclude such a trivial situation, we assume the following boundedness of Morse functions (cf.
[
12
]
).

Definition 1.1
.

A smooth function
f\colon M\to\mathbb{R}
is
bounded
if
\nabla^{i}f
is bounded for any
0\leq i\leq\max\{3,\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil\}
.

Actually, boundedness of the gradient vector field
\nabla f
is essential, and that of
f
and its higher derivatives may be a technical condition. We note that the boundedness of a function is independent of the choice of a
G
-invariant metric on
M
because
G
-invariant metrics on
M
are mutually equivalent. By (
1.2
) below, we describe a rough configuration of critical points of a Morse function on
M
by a function on
G
, which may be thought of as counting of critical points. In order to make this function controllable, we assume the following uniformness of Morse functions. We fix a
G
-invariant triangulation of
M
. Then as in Section
2
, we can construct a fundamental domain
K
such that the closure of
K
is a finite subcomplex of
M
and it satisfies

(1.1)

M=\coprod_{g\in G}gK.

Here, we note that the closure of
K
is a fundamental domain in the sense of
[
12
]
.

Definition 1.2
.

A Morse function
f\colon M\to\mathbb{R}
is
uniform
if there is
\epsilon>0
such that any two critical points of
f
are at least
2\epsilon
distant.

In Sectioin
5
, we define a
strongly uniform
Morse function on
M
as a uniform Morse function on
M
satisfying additional mild conditions on critical points. Let
f\colon M\to\mathbb{R}
be a Morse function, and let
\operatorname{Crit}_{k}(f)
denote the set of critical points of
f
with index
k
. Define a function

(1.2)

c_{k}\colon G\to\mathbb{R},\quad g\mapsto|\operatorname{Crit}_{k}(f)\cap gK|.

Then this is a function describing a rough configuration of critical points of
f
with index
k
, mentioned above. This way of counting of discrete points on
M
was discovered in
[
12
]
. Observe that
c_{k}
is a bounded function for each
k\geq 0
whenever
f
is uniform, because the closure of a fundamental domain
K
is compact.

We set notation. Let
b_{k}^{(2)}
denote the
k
-th
L^{2}
-Betti number of
M
. Let
\ell^{\infty}(G)
denote the Banach space of bounded functions on
G
. Then
G
acts on
\ell^{\infty}(G)
by
(g\cdot\phi)(x)=\phi(xg)
for
g,x\in G
and
\phi\in\ell^{\infty}(G)
. Let
\mathcal{I}
denote the subspace of
\ell^{\infty}(G)
generated by
\phi-g\cdot\phi
for
g\in G
and
\phi\in\ell^{\infty}(G)
, and let
\bar{\mathcal{I}}
denote the closure of
\mathcal{I}
by the weak topology. Taking modulo
\bar{\mathcal{I}}
may be thought of as averaging process mentioned above. For
\phi_{1},\phi_{2}\in\ell^{\infty}(G)
, we write

•

\phi_{1}\geq\phi_{2}\mod\bar{\mathcal{I}}
if there is
\phi_{3}\in\bar{\mathcal{I}}
such that the pointwise inequality
\phi_{1}\geq\phi_{2}+\phi_{3}
holds, and

•

\phi_{1}\approx\phi_{2}\mod\bar{\mathcal{I}}
if
\phi_{1}\geq\phi_{2}\mod\bar{\mathcal{I}}
and
\phi_{2}\geq\phi_{1}\mod\bar{\mathcal{I}}
.

The first relation is a preorder on
\ell^{\infty}(G)
, not necessarily a partial order, so that we need the second relation. The quotient
\ell^{\infty}(G)/\mathcal{I}
is isomorphic to the
0
-th uniformly finite homology of
M
defined by Block and Weinberger
[
2
]
(see also
[
3
]
), and is related with finite propagation operators that play an important role in this paper
[
9
,
10
,
11
]
. Let
\mathbbm{1}\in\ell^{\infty}(G)
denote the constant function on
G
with values
1
.

Now we state the main theorem.

Theorem 1.3
.

For a strongly uniform bounded Morse function
f\colon M\to\mathbb{R}
, we have

c_{k}-c_{k-1}+\cdots+(-1)^{k}c_{0}\geq(b_{k}^{(2)}-b_{k-1}^{(2)}+\cdots+(-1)^{k}b_{0}^{(2)})\mathbbm{1}\mod\bar{\mathcal{I}}

for
k=0,1,\ldots,n-1
, and

c_{n}-c_{n-1}+\cdots+(-1)^{n}c_{0}\approx(-1)^{n}\chi(M/G)\mathbbm{1}\mod\bar{\mathcal{I}}.

We remark that Theorem
1.3
makes sense only when
G
is amenable because
\ell^{\infty}(G)\neq\bar{\mathcal{I}}
if and only if
G
is amenable (Proposition
2.12
). By definition,
G
is amenable if there is a
G
-invariant positive linear map
\mu\colon\ell^{\infty}(G)\to\mathbb{R}
with
\mu(\mathbbm{1})=1
, which is called a
G
-invariant
mean
. As a corollary to Theorem
1.3
, we have the following mean value Morse inequalities.

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
f\colon M\to\mathbb{R}
be a strongly uniform bounded Morse function. If
G
is amenable, then for any
G
-invariant mean
\mu\colon\ell^{\infty}(G)\to\mathbb{R}
,

\mu(c_{k})-\mu(c_{k-1})+\cdots+(-1)^{k}\mu(c_{0})\geq b_{k}^{(2)}-b_{k-1}^{(2)}+\cdots+(-1)^{k}b_{0}^{(2)}

for
k=0,1,\ldots,n-1
and

\mu(c_{n})-\mu(c_{n-1})+\cdots+(-1)^{n}\mu(c_{0})=(-1)^{n}\chi(M/G).

We can deduce from Theorem
1.3
the weak Morse inequalities

c_{k}\geq b_{k}^{(2)}\mod\bar{\mathcal{I}}

for
k=0,1,\ldots,n
, and by applying a property of the module of coinvariants
\ell^{\infty}(G)/\mathcal{I}
proved in
[
12
]
to it, we get the following property of the
L^{2}
-Betti numbers, which has its own interest.

Corollary 1.5
.

Let
f\colon M\to\mathbb{R}
be a strongly uniform bounded Morse function. If
G
is an infinite amenable group and
b^{(2)}_{k}\neq 0
, then
f
must have infinitely many critical points with index
k
.

We also see that Theorem
1.3
recovers the result of Novikov and Shubin
[
16
]
on Morse inequalities for a Morse function on
M/G
and the
L^{2}
-Betti numbers of
M
when
G
is amenable (Corollary
5.9
).

We prove Theorem
1.3
by employing the Witten deformation by a Morse function. Then we need to disassemble the trace of an operator on the Hilbert space of
L^{2}
-sections of a vector bundle over
M
with respect to the cover (
1.1
). In Section
2
, we introduce a piecewise trace-class operator and its piecewise trace which is the desired disassembly of the usual (or Roe’s) trace. In Section
3
, we introduce an operator whose kernel function has propagation subordinate to a Gaussian function centered at the diagonal set, which we call an operator with Gaussian propagation. We prove the trace property of the piecewise trace of a product of operators with Gaussian propagation. We also consider the functional calculus of a generalized Dirac operators such that the resulting operator has Gaussian propagation. In Section
4
, we introduce a disassembly of the normalized Betti number of Roe
[
19
,
20
]
, which is related to the
L^{2}
-Betti number of
M
, with respect to the cover (
1.1
), and prove its invariance under a certain deformation, including the Witten deformation by a bounded function. In Section
5
, we collect all results obtained so far, and apply the Witten deformation to prove Theorem
1.3
. Some conditions on a Morse function that we assume seem to be quite technical. In Section
6
, we pose some questions on such technical conditions.

2.
Piecewise trace

In
[
18
]
, Roe defined the trace of a uniform operator over a noncompact manifold of bounded geometry with regular exhaustion, and use it to build the index theory for such a noncompact manifold. In this section, we introduce the piecewise trace of an operator on the space of
L^{2}
-sections of a
G
-invariant vector bundle over
M
, which may be thought of as disassembly of Roe’s trace with respect to the action of
G
.

2.1.
Fundamental domain

First of all, we construct a fundamental domain. Choose a
G
-invariant triangulation
L
of
M
, that is, the lift of a triangulation of
M/G
. Then
L/G
is a triangulation of
M/G
. For each open
n
-simplex of
L/G
, we choose one lift to
L
. For each open
k
-simplex with
k<n
, we choose one lift to
M
which is in the closure of some open
n
-simplex of
M
that we have chosen. Now we define a
fundamental domain

K
as the union of those open simplices of
M
. Observe that the closure of
K
is the union of the closure of open
n
-simplices in
K
. Then the closure of
K
is a finite complex, and a fundamental domain in the sense of
[
12
]
. Remark that a fundamental domain
K
needs not be connected. By definition, we have

M=\coprod_{g\in G}gK.

In the sequel, we fix a fundamental domain
K
.

2.2.
Piecewise Hilbert-Schmidt operator

Let
E\to M
be a
G
-invariant vector bundle with
G
-invariant metric, that is,
E
is the lift of a vector bundle over
M/G
with metric. Let
L^{2}(E)
denote the Hilbert space of
L^{2}
-sections of
E
. Then the map
g^{-1}\colon gK\to K
induces an isometry
(g^{-1})^{*}\colon L^{2}(E|_{K})\to L^{2}(E|_{gK})
. We choose an orthonormal basis
\{e_{i}\mid i\in I\}
of
L^{2}(E|_{K})
, and set
e_{i}^{g}=(g^{-1})^{*}(e_{i})
for
g\in G
. Then
\{e_{i}^{g}\mid i\in I\}
is an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(E|_{gK})
. Moreover, if we consider
e_{i}^{g}
as an
L^{2}
-section of the entire vector bundle
E
in the obvious way, then
\{e_{i}^{g}\mid i\in I,\,g\in G\}
is an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(E)
. For
u\in L^{2}(E)
, let
\|u\|
denote the
L^{2}
-norm.

Definition 2.1
.

A bounded operator
A\colon L^{2}(E)\to L^{2}(E)
is called
piecewise Hilbert-Schmidt
if

\rho_{2}(A)\colon G\to\mathbb{R},\quad g\mapsto\left(\sum_{i\in I}\|Ae_{i}^{g}\|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

is a well-defined bounded function.

Let
\mathbf{1}_{S}\colon M\to\{0,1\}
denote the characteristic function of a subset
S\subset M
. An operator
A\colon L^{2}(E)\to L^{2}(E)
is a piecewise Hilbert-Schmidt operator if and only if
A\mathbf{1}_{gK}
is a Hilbert-Schmidt operator for each
g\in G
such that
\sup_{g\in G}\|A\mathbf{1}_{gK}\|_{\mathrm{HS}}
is bounded, where
\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{HS}}
denotes the Hilbert-Schmidt norm. Observe that

(2.1)

\rho_{2}(A)(g)=\|A\mathbf{1}_{gK}\|_{\mathrm{HS}}.

Then
\rho_{2}(A)
is independent of the choice of an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(E|_{K})
, and we may think of
\rho_{2}
as disassembly of the Hilbert-Schmidt norm with respect to the cover (
1.1
). The following lemma is immediate from the definition of
\rho_{2}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
A,B\colon L^{2}(E)\to L^{2}(E)
be piecewise Hilbert-Schmidt operators.

(1)

\rho_{2}(A+B)\leq\rho_{2}(A)+\rho_{2}(B)
.

(2)

\rho_{2}(cA)=|c|\rho_{2}(A)
for
c\in\mathbb{R}
.

(3)

\rho_{2}(A)=0
if and only if
A=0
.

For a bounded operator
A\colon L^{2}(E)\to L^{2}(E)
, let
\|A\|
denote its operator norm.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
A\colon L^{2}(E)\to L^{2}(E)
be a bounded operator, and let
B\colon L^{2}(E)\to L^{2}(E)
be a piecewise Hilbert-Schmidt operator. Then
AB
is a piecewise Hilbert-Schmidt operator such that

\rho_{2}(AB)\leq\|A\|\rho_{2}(B).

Proof.

By (
2.1
),
\rho_{2}(AB)(g)=\|AB\mathbf{1}_{gK}\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\leq\|A\|\|B\mathbf{1}_{gK}\|_{\mathrm{HS}}=\|A\|\rho_{2}(B)(g)
for any
g\in G
. Then the statement follows.
∎

Let
A\colon L^{2}(E)\to L^{2}(E)
be a Hilbert-Schmidt operator, and let
B\colon L^{2}(E)\to L^{2}(E)
be a bounded operator. Then we have

\|AB\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\leq\|A\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\|B\|\quad\text{and}\quad\|A^{*}\|_{\mathrm{HS}}=\|A\|_{\mathrm{HS}}.

Hence we may expect that
\rho_{2}
has analogous properties, but it fails. Choose any
e\in L^{2}(E|_{K})
with
\|e\|=1
and
g\in G
with
g\neq 1
. We define a piecewise Hilbert-Schmidt operator

A=\langle-,e\rangle(g^{-1})^{*}(e)

where
\langle-,-\rangle
denotes the
L^{2}
-inner product. Then
A^{*}=\langle-,(g^{-1})^{*}(e)\rangle e
is a piecewise Hilbert-Schmidt operator. Observe that
\rho_{2}(A^{*}A)(1)=1\geq 0=\rho_{2}(A^{*})(1)\|A\|
and
\rho_{2}(A)(1)=1\neq 0=\rho_{2}(A^{*})(1)
. Then

\rho_{2}(A^{*}A)\not\leq\rho_{2}(A^{*})\|A\|\quad\text{and}\quad\rho_{2}(A^{*})\neq\rho_{2}(A).

Let
A\colon L^{2}(E)\to L^{2}(E)
be an operator. We say that
A
is represented by a
kernel function

k_{A}
if
k_{A}
is a section of the vector bundle
E^{*}\boxtimes E\to M\times M
such that for any
u\in L^{2}(E)
,

(Au)(x)=\int_{M}k_{A}(x,y)u(y)\operatorname{vol}(y)

where
\operatorname{vol}
denotes the pullback of the volume form of
M/G
to
M
. We also say that an operator
A
is
smoothing
if it is represented by a smooth kernel function.

Lemma 2.4
.

If
A\colon L^{2}(E)\to L^{2}(E)
is a piecewise Hilbert-Schmidt smoothing operator, then for each
g\in G
,

\rho_{2}(A)(g)=\left(\int_{M}\int_{gK}|k_{A}(x,y)|^{2}\operatorname{vol}(y)\operatorname{vol}(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Proof.

Observe that

\displaystyle(\rho_{2}(A)(g))^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in I}\left(\|Ae_{i}^{g}\|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in I}\int_{M}\left|\int_{M}k_{A}(x,y)e_{i}^{g}(y)\operatorname{vol}(y)\right|^{2}\operatorname{vol}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in I\\
h\in G\end{subarray}}\left|\int_{M}k_{A}(x,y)\mathbf{1}_{gK}e_{i}^{h}(y)\operatorname{vol}(y)\right|^{2}\operatorname{vol}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\int_{M}|k_{A}(x,y)\mathbf{1}_{gK}|^{2}\operatorname{vol}(y)\operatorname{vol}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\int_{gK}|k_{A}(x,y)|^{2}\operatorname{vol}(y)\operatorname{vol}(x).

where we use Parseval’s identity for the fourth equality. Then the statement follows.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

If
A,B\colon L^{2}(E)\to L^{2}(E)
are piecewise Hilbert-Schmidt operators, then

\rho_{2}(A,B)\colon G\to\mathbb{R},\quad g\mapsto\sum_{i\in I}\langle Ae_{i}^{g},Be_{i}^{g}\rangle.

is a well-defined bounded function which is independent of the choice of an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(E|_{K})
.

Proof.

Define an operator

(2.2)

P=\sum_{i\in I,\,g\in G}\mathrm{sgn}(\langle Ae_{i}^{g},Be_{i}^{g}\rangle)\langle-,e_{i}^{g}\rangle e_{i}^{g}

where we put
\mathrm{sgn}(0)=0
. Then
BP
is a piecewise Hilbert-Schmidt operator such that
\rho_{2}(BP)=\rho_{2}(B)
and
\rho_{2}(A,BP)=\sum_{i\in I}|\langle Ae_{i}^{g},Be_{i}^{g}\rangle|
. Observe that for any piecewise Hilbert-Schmidt operators
C
and
D
, the Schwartz inequality

(2.3)

\rho_{2}(C,D)\leq\rho_{2}(C)\rho_{2}(D)

holds. Thus
\rho_{2}(A,BP)
is a bounded function, and therefore
\rho_{2}(A,B)
is a well-defined bounded function. The independence of the choice of an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(E|_{K})
follows from the fact that

\rho_{2}(A,B)(g)=\langle A\mathbf{1}_{gK},B\mathbf{1}_{gK}\rangle

for
g\in G
.
∎

2.3.
Piecewise trace-class operator

We define a piecewise trace-class operator as an analogy to a trace-class operator.

Definition 2.6
.

An operator
A\colon L^{2}(E)\to L^{2}(E)
is a
piecewise trace-class
operator if
A=B^{*}C
for some piecewise Hilbert-Schmidt operators
B,C\colon L^{2}(E)\to L^{2}(E)
. Its
piecewise trace
is defined by

\operatorname{Tr}(A)=\rho_{2}(B,C).

By Lemma
2.5
, the piecewise trace is a well-defined bounded function on
G
which is independent of the choice of an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(E|_{K})
. It is easy to see that
\operatorname{Tr}(A)
is independent of the choice of a decomposition
A=B^{*}C
. We show the basic properties of a piecewise trace.

Lemma 2.7
.

If
A=B^{*}C
for piecewise Hilbert-Schmidt smoothing operators
B,C\colon L^{2}(E)\to L^{2}(E)
, then
A
is a trace-class smoothing operator such that

\operatorname{Tr}(A)(g)=\int_{gK}\mathrm{tr}(k_{A}(x,x))\operatorname{vol}(x).

Proof.

By definition,
A
is of piecewise trace-class, and since the composite of smoothing operators is smoothing,
A
is a smoothing operator. Since
\operatorname{Tr}(A)(g)
is the trace of the trace-class operator
\mathbf{1}_{g}A\mathbf{1}_{g}
, the equality in the statement follows quite similarly to

[
20
, Theorems 8.12]
.
∎

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
A\colon L^{2}(E)\to L^{2}(E)
be a piecewise trace-class smoothing operator. Then the piecewise trace of
A
modulo
\mathcal{I}
is independent of the choice of a fundamental domain
K
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.7
,
\operatorname{Tr}(A)
is given by the integral of the bounded
n
-form
\mathrm{tr}(k_{A}(x,x))\operatorname{vol}(x)
in the sense of
[
12
]
. It is shown in
[
14
]
that such an integral modulo
\mathcal{I}
is independent of the choice of
K
.
∎

For a piecewise trace-class operator
A\colon L^{2}(E)\to L^{2}(E)
, we can define a bounded function

\rho_{1}(A)\colon G\to\mathbb{R},\quad g\mapsto\sum_{i\in I}|\langle e_{i}^{g},Ae_{i}^{g}\rangle|.

Indeed, the proof of Lemma
2.5
implies that
\rho_{1}(A)
is a well-defined bounded function. Here, we remark that
\rho_{1}
depends on the choice of an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(E|_{K})
, so when we consider
\rho_{1}
, we always fix an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(E|_{K})
. We record properties of
\rho_{1}
which are immediate from the definition.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
A,B\colon L^{2}(E)\to L^{2}(E)
be piecewise trace-class operators.

(1)

\rho_{1}(A+B)\leq\rho_{1}(A)+\rho_{1}(B)
.

(2)

\rho_{1}(cA)=|c|\rho_{1}(A)
for
c\in\mathbb{R}
.

Proposition 2.10
.

Let
A\colon L^{2}(E)\to L^{2}(E)
be a piecewise trace-class operator. If
A
is positive, then

\operatorname{Tr}(A)=\rho_{1}(A)\geq 0.

Proof.

If
A
is positive, then
\langle e_{i}^{g},Ae_{i}^{g}\rangle\geq 0
for any
g\in G
and
i\in I
, implying
\operatorname{Tr}(A)=\rho_{1}(A)\geq 0
.
∎

Proposition 2.11
.

If
A,B\colon L^{2}(E)\to L^{2}(E)
are piecewise Hilbert-Schmidt operators, then

\rho_{1}(A^{*}B)\leq\rho_{2}(A)\rho_{2}(B).

Proof.

Let
P
be as in (
2.2
). Then
\rho_{1}(A^{*}B)=\rho_{2}(A,BP)
, so by (
2.3
),
\rho_{1}(A^{*}B)\leq\rho_{2}(A)\rho_{2}(BP)=\rho_{2}(A)\rho_{2}(B)
.
∎

2.4.
Roe’s trace

Recall that a discrete group
G
is
amenable
if there is a
G
-invariant positive linear map
\mu\colon\ell^{\infty}(G)\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying
\mu(\mathbbm{1})=1
, called a
G
-invariant
mean
. We have the following characterization of amenability.

Proposition 2.12
.

The following conditions are equivalent.

(1)

G
is amenable.

(2)

\ell^{\infty}(G)\neq\bar{\mathcal{I}}
.

Proof.

Let
\mu\colon\ell^{\infty}(G)\to\mathbb{R}
be a
G
-invariant mean. Then for
\phi\in\ell^{\infty}(G)
,

|\mu(\phi)|\leq|\mu(\sup_{x\in G}|\phi(x)|\mathbbm{1})|=\sup_{x\in G}|\phi(x)|

and then
\mu
is bounded, implying
\mu(\bar{\mathcal{I}})=0
. Thus by
\mu(\mathbbm{1})=1
, (1) implies (2). By
[
2
,
3
]
, if
G
is not amenable then
\ell^{\infty}(G)=\mathcal{I}
. Hence (2) implies (1).
∎

We compare the piecewise trace with Roe’s trace
[
18
]
when
G
is amenable. Recall that Følner’s theorem also characterizes amenability, which states that for any
\epsilon>0
and a nonempty finite subset
S\subset G
, there is a finite subset
F\subset G
satisfying

\frac{|F\triangle gF|}{|F|}<\epsilon

for any
g\in S
. For
k\geq 1
, we define
S_{k}=\{g\in G\mid d(K,gK)\leq k\}
. Then we get a sequence
S_{1}\subset S_{2}\subset\cdots
of finite subsets of
G
which exhausts
G
. By Følner’s theorem, there is a finite subset
F_{k}
of
G
satisfying

\frac{|F_{k}\triangle gF_{k}|}{|F_{k}|}<\frac{1}{k}

for any
g\in S_{k}
. Let
M_{k}
be the union of all
gK
for
g\in F_{k}
. Then as is shown in
[
18
, p.106]
,
\{M_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
is a regular exhaustion of
M
, hence by
[
20
, (3.5)]
, Roe’s trace of a suitable smoothing operator
A\colon L^{2}(E)\to L^{2}(E)
is given by

\tau(A)=\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{\operatorname{vol}(M_{k})}\int_{M_{k}}\mathrm{tr}(k_{A}(x,x))\operatorname{vol}(x).

On the other hand, we can define a
G
-invariant finitely additive probability measure on
G
by

\mu(S)=\lim_{\omega}\frac{|F_{k}\cap S|}{|F_{k}|}

for
S\subset G
, where
\omega
is a nonprincipal ultrafilter on
\mathbb{N}
, which yields a
G
-invariant mean
\mu\colon\ell^{\infty}(G)\to\mathbb{R}
. Thus we can easily deduce the following, where
\operatorname{vol}(K)=\operatorname{vol}(M/G)
.

Proposition 2.13
.

Let
A\colon L^{2}(E)\to L^{2}(E)
be a piecewise trace-class operator, and let
\mu\colon\ell^{\infty}(G)\to\mathbb{R}
be the above
G
-invariant mean. Then

\mu(\operatorname{Tr}(A))=\frac{\tau(A)}{\operatorname{vol}(M/G)}.

3.
Operator with Gaussian propagation

In this section, we define an operator with Gaussian propagation, and show the trace property of the piecewise trace of the product of operators with Gaussian propagation. We also investigate functional calculus of a generalized Dirac operator that produces operators with Gaussian propagation.

3.1.
Trace property

Let
E\to M
be a
G
-invariant vector bundle with
G
-invariant metric.

Definition 3.1
.

A smoothing operator
A\colon L^{2}(E)\to L^{2}(E)
has
Gaussian propagation
if there are
C_{1},C_{2}>0
such that

|k_{A}(x,y)|\leq C_{1}e^{-C_{2}d(x,y)^{2}}.

Since the group
G
is the quotient of the fundamental group of a compact manifold
M/G
, it is finitely generated. We choose a finite generating set of
G
, and consider the associated word metric on
G
, where those metrics are mutually quasi-isometric. Now we take any
g,h\in G
. By the fundamental observation in geometric group theory, an inclusion
G\to M
is a quasi-isometry, so there are
C_{1},C_{2},C_{3},C_{4}>0
which are independent of the choice of
g,h
such that for any
x\in gK,\,y\in hK
, we have

(3.1)

C_{1}e^{-C_{2}d(g,h)^{2}}\leq e^{-d(x,y)^{2}}\leq C_{3}e^{-C_{4}d(g,h)^{2}}

For
m\geq 0
, let
G_{m}=\{g\in G\mid d(g,1)\leq m\}
. Then we get a sequence of subsets of
G

G_{0}\subset\cdots\subset G_{m}\subset G_{m+1}\subset\cdots

which exhausts
G
. As in
[
15
]
, there is
C>0
such that

(3.2)

|G_{m}|\leq e^{Cm}.

Let
\operatorname{Gau}(E)
denote the set of smoothing operators on
L^{2}(E)
with Gaussian propagation.

Proposition 3.2
.

The set of operators
\operatorname{Gau}(E)
is a
*
-algebra.

Proof.

If
A
is a smoothing operator, then
A^{*}
is also a smoothing operator with
k_{A^{*}}(x,y)=k_{A}(y,x)^{*}
. Hence
\operatorname{Gau}(E)
is closed under
*
. Clearly,
\operatorname{Gau}(E)
is closed under linear combinations, so it remains to show that
\operatorname{Gau}(E)
is closed under products. Let
A,B\in\operatorname{Gau}(E)
, and let
g_{1},g_{2}\in G
with
d(g_{1},g_{2})=l
. Take any
x_{1}\in g_{1}K
and
x_{2}\in g_{2}K
. Then

\displaystyle|k_{AB}(x_{1},x_{2})|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{M}|k_{A}(x_{1},y)k_{B}(y,x_{2})|\operatorname{vol}(y)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{g\in G_{m}}\int_{gg_{1}K}|k_{A}(x_{1},y)k_{B}(y,x_{2})|\operatorname{vol}(y)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{g\in G_{m}}\int_{gg_{1}K}C_{1}e^{-C_{2}(d(x_{1},y)^{2}+d(y,x_{2})^{2})}\operatorname{vol}(y)

for some
C_{1},C_{2}>0
which are independent of the choice of
g_{1},g_{2},x_{1},x_{2}
. By (
3.1
) and (
3.2
),

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{g\in G_{m}}\int_{gg_{1}K}C_{1}e^{-C_{2}(d(x_{1},y)^{2}+d(y,x_{2})^{2})}\operatorname{vol}(y)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{g\in G_{m}}C_{1}e^{-C_{3}(m^{2}+(m-l)^{2})}

\displaystyle\leq e^{-C_{3}l^{2}}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}C_{1}e^{-2C_{3}(m^{2}-lm)+C_{4}m}

\displaystyle\leq C_{5}e^{-C_{3}l^{2}}

for some
C_{3},C_{4},C_{5}>0
which are independent of the choice of
g_{1},g_{2},x_{1},x_{2}
. Moreover, by (
3.1
),

C_{5}e^{-C_{3}l^{2}}\leq C_{6}e^{-C_{7}d(x_{1},x_{2})^{2}}.

for some
C_{6},C_{7}>0
which are independent of the choice of
g_{1},g_{2},x_{1},x_{2}
. Then we obtain
|k_{AB}(x_{1},x_{2})|\leq C_{6}e^{-C_{7}d(x_{1},x_{2})^{2}}
. Thus
AB
belongs to
\operatorname{Gau}(E)
, completing the proof.
∎

Proposition 3.3
.

Elements of
\operatorname{Gau}(E)
are piecewise Hilbert-Schmidt.

Proof.

The proof of Lemma
2.4
implies that a smoothing operator
A\colon L^{2}(E)\to L^{2}(E)
is piecewise Hilbert-Schmidt if and only if

(3.3)

\sup_{g\in G}\int_{M}\int_{gK}|k_{A}(x,y)|^{2}\operatorname{vol}(y)\operatorname{vol}(x)<\infty.

Now we let
A\in\operatorname{Gau}(E)
, and take any
h\in G
and
y\in hK
. By (
3.1
),

\int_{M}|k_{A}(x,y)|^{2}\operatorname{vol}(x)=\sum_{g\in G}\int_{gK}|k_{A}(x,y)|^{2}\operatorname{vol}(x)\leq\sum_{g\in G}C_{1}e^{-C_{2}d(g,h)^{2}}

for some
C_{1},C_{2}>0
which are independent of the choice of
h
and
y
. By (
3.2
), we have

\sum_{g\in G}C_{1}e^{-C_{2}d(g,h)^{2}}\leq\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{g\in G_{m}}C_{1}e^{-C_{2}d(gh,h)^{2}}\leq\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}C_{1}e^{-C_{2}m^{2}+C_{3}m}<C_{4}.

for some
C_{3},C_{4}>0
which are independent of the choice of
h
and
y
. Then (
3.3
) holds, hence
A
is piecewise Hilbert-Schmidt as stated.
∎

Corollary 3.4
.

If
A,B\in\operatorname{Gau}(E)
, then
AB
is of piecewise trace-class.

Proof.

If
A,B\in\operatorname{Gau}(E)
, then by Propositions
3.2
and
3.3
,
A^{*}
and
B
are piecewise Hilbert-Schmidt operators. Hence
AB=(A^{*})^{*}B
is of piecewise trace-class.
∎

We consider a function
F\colon G\times G\to\mathbb{R}
such that there are
C_{1},C_{2}>0
satisfying

(3.4)

|F(g,h)|\leq C_{1}e^{-C_{2}d(g,h)^{2}}

for all
g,h\in G
.

Lemma 3.5
.

There is
C>0
such that for any
g\in G
, we have

\sum_{h\in G}|F(h,g)|\leq C\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{h\in G}|F(g,h)|\leq C.

Proof.

By (
3.2
), there is
C_{3}>0
such that for any
m\geq 0
and
g\in G
,

\sum_{h\in G_{m}}|F(hg,g)|=\sum_{k=0}^{m}\sum_{h\in G_{k}-G_{k-1}}|F(hg,g)|\leq\sum_{k=0}^{m}C_{1}e^{-C_{2}k^{2}+C_{3}k}.

Clearly, the last term is bounded, and then by taking
m\to\infty
, the first inequality is obtained. By symmetry, the second inequality is also obtained.
∎

By Lemma
3.5
, we can define bounded functions
F_{1},F_{2}\colon G\to\mathbb{R}
by

F_{1}(g)=\sum_{h\in G}F(h,g)\quad\text{and}\quad F_{2}(g)=\sum_{h\in G}F(g,h).

Let
\widehat{\mathcal{I}}
denote the closure of
\mathcal{I}
by the
\ell^{\infty}
-norm topology. Note that
\widehat{\mathcal{I}}\subset\bar{\mathcal{I}}
.

Lemma 3.6
.

F_{1}\equiv F_{2}\mod\widehat{\mathcal{I}}
.

Proof.

For
m\geq 0
and
g\in G
, let

F^{m}_{1}(g)=\sum_{h\in G_{m}}F(g,hg)\quad\text{and}\quad F_{2}^{m}(g)=\sum_{h\in G_{m}}F(h^{-1}g,g).

By Lemma
3.5
,
F_{1}^{m}
and
F_{2}^{m}
are bounded functions on
G
. By (
3.2
),

|F_{1}(g)-F_{1}^{m}(g)|\leq\sum_{k=m}^{\infty}\sum_{h\in G_{k}-G_{k-1}}|F(g,hg)|\leq\sum_{k=m}^{\infty}C_{1}e^{-C_{2}k^{2}+C_{3}k}\leq C_{4}e^{-C_{5}m^{2}}

for some
C_{3},C_{4},C_{5}>0
which are independet of the choice of
m
and
g
. Then
F_{1}^{m}
converges uniformly to
F_{1}
as
m\to\infty
. By symmetry,
F_{2}^{m}
also converges uniformly to
F_{2}
as
m\to\infty
. For
g\in G
, we define a function
\phi_{g}\colon G\to\mathbb{R}
by
\phi_{g}(h)=F(g,gh)
. By (
3.4
),
\phi_{g}
is bounded. We set

\Phi_{m}(h)=\sum_{g\in G_{m}}(1-h^{-1})\phi_{g}(h).

Then
\Phi_{m}\in\mathcal{I}
and
F_{2}^{m}-F_{1}^{m}=\Phi_{m}
. Then
\Phi_{m}
converges uniformly to
F_{2}-F_{1}
as
m\to\infty
, implying
F_{1}-F_{2}\in\widehat{\mathcal{I}}
.
∎

Proposition 3.7
.

For
A,B\in\operatorname{Gau}(S)
, we have

\operatorname{Tr}(AB)\equiv\operatorname{Tr}(BA)\mod\widehat{\mathcal{I}}.

Proof.

By Corollary
3.4
,
AB
and
BA
are piecewise trace-class operators, so their piecewise traces are defined. We consider a function
F\colon G\times G\to\mathbb{R}
given by

F(g,h)=\int_{gK\times hK}\mathrm{tr}(k_{A}(x,y)k_{B}(y,x))\operatorname{vol}(x)\operatorname{vol}(y).

Since
A
and
B
have Gaussian propagation, there are
C_{1},C_{2}>0
such that for any
g,h\in G
,

\displaystyle|F(g,h)|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{gK\times hK}|\mathrm{tr}(k_{A}(x,y)k_{B}(y,x))|\operatorname{vol}(x)\operatorname{vol}(y)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{gK\times hK}C_{1}e^{-C_{2}d(x,y)^{2}}\operatorname{vol}(x)\operatorname{vol}(y).

Then by (
3.1
), the function
F
satisfies (
3.4
). By Lemma
2.7
, we have

\displaystyle F_{1}(g)

\displaystyle=\sum_{h\in G}F(h,g)

\displaystyle=\int_{gK}\mathrm{tr}\left(\int_{M}k_{A}(x,y)k_{B}(y,x)\operatorname{vol}(y)\right)\operatorname{vol}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{gK}\mathrm{tr}(k_{AB}(x,x))\operatorname{vol}(x)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Tr}(AB)(g)

for any
g\in G
, implying
F_{1}=\operatorname{Tr}(AB)
. Quite similarly, we can see that
F_{2}=\operatorname{Tr}(BA)
. Thus by Lemma
3.6
, we obtain
\operatorname{Tr}(AB)\equiv\operatorname{Tr}(BA)\mod\widehat{\mathcal{I}}
.
∎

3.2.
Functional calculus

In the sequel, let
S\to M
be a
G
-invariant Clifford bundle with Dirac operator
D
. Then
S
has bounded geometry in the sense of
[
18
, p. 93]
, and the Dirac operator
D
is
G
-invariant. We consider a generalized Dirac operator

\widetilde{D}=D+A

where
A
is a self-adjoint endomorphism of
S
, not necessarily
G
-invariant. For the rest of this section, we assume that
\nabla^{i}A
is bounded for
i=0,1,\ldots,\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil-1
. Let
C_{c}^{\infty}(S)
denote the set of compactly supported smooth sections of
S
.

Lemma 3.8
.

There is
C_{k}>0
satisfying an ineuality

\sum_{i=0}^{k}\|D^{i}u\|^{2}\leq C_{k}\sum_{i=0}^{k}\|\widetilde{D}^{i}u\|^{2}

for
k=0,1,\ldots\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil
and any
u\in C^{\infty}_{c}(S)
.

Proof.

We induct on
k
. The
k=0
case is trivial. We assume that the statement holds for
k\leq m-1
. Then

\displaystyle\|D^{m}u\|

\displaystyle=\|D^{m-1}(\widetilde{D}-A)u\|

\displaystyle\leq\|D^{m-1}\widetilde{D}u\|+\|D^{m-1}Au\|

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\|\widetilde{D}^{i+1}u\|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\|D^{m-1}Au\|

for some
C_{1}>0
which is independent of the choice of
u
. We also have

\|D^{m-1}Au\|^{2}\leq C_{2}\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\|\nabla^{i}(Au)\|^{2}\leq C_{3}\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\|\nabla^{i}u\|^{2}\leq C_{4}\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\|D^{i}u\|^{2}

for some
C_{2},C_{3},C_{4}>0
which are independent of the choice of
u
. Here, the first inequality follows as in
[
21
, page 73]
, the second inequality follows from
\nabla(Au)=(\nabla A-A\nabla)u
and the assumption that
\nabla^{i}A
is bounded for
i=0,1,\ldots\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil-1
, and the third inequality follows from the elliptic estimate (cf.
[
21
, Proposition 5.16]
). Then by the induction hypothesis, we obtain

\|D^{m-1}Au\|^{2}\leq C_{5}\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\|\widetilde{D}^{i}u\|^{2}

for some
C_{5}>0
which is independent of the choice of
u
, and thus the statement follows.
∎

Let
\mathcal{G}
denote the set of Schwartz-class functions
\phi\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
such that there are
C_{1},C_{2}>0
satisfying

|\widehat{\phi}^{(i)}(x)|\leq C_{1}e^{-C_{2}x^{2}}

for
i=0,1,\ldots,2\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil
, where
\widehat{\phi}
denotes the Fourier transform of
\phi
and
f^{(i)}
stands for the
i
-th derivative of a function
f\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
.

Proposition 3.9
.

The set of functions
\mathcal{G}
is an algebra.

Proof.

The only nontrivial property is that
\mathcal{G}
is closed under products. For Schwartz-class functions
\phi
and
\psi
, we have
(\widehat{\phi\psi})^{(i)}=\widehat{\phi}^{(i)}*\widehat{\psi}
. Then since the convolution of Gaussian functions is a Gaussian function,
\mathcal{G}
is closed under products.
∎

Lemma 3.10
.

A generalized Dirac operator
\widetilde{D}
is essentially self-adjoint.

Proof.

By
[
7
, Theorem 1.17]
,
D
is essentially self-adjoint. Then as
A
is self-adjoint and continuous,
\widetilde{D}
is essentially self-adjoint too.
∎

Lemma
3.10
enables us to perform functional calculus for a generalized Dirac operator
\widetilde{D}
. We remark that all results in
[
21
, Chapter 9]
holds not only for the Dirac operator
D
but also for a generalized Dirac opertor
\widetilde{D}
as mentioned in
[
21
, p. 95]
. In particular, we have:

Lemma 3.11

(cf.
[
21
, Proposition 7.20]
)
.

For any
u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(S)
,
e^{\sqrt{-1}t\widetilde{D}}u
is supported within
N(\mathrm{supp}(u);|t|)
.

For
\phi\in\mathcal{G}
, we can define the operator
\phi(\widetilde{D})
as in
[
21
, Chapter 9]
, that is,

\langle u,\phi(\widetilde{D})v\rangle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widehat{\phi}(t)\langle u,e^{\sqrt{-1}t\widetilde{D}}v\rangle dt

for
u,v\in C^{\infty}_{c}(S)
.

Proposition 3.12
.

The map

\mathcal{G}\to\operatorname{Gau}(S),\quad\phi\mapsto\phi(\widetilde{D})

is a well-defined homomorphism.

Proof.

Let
\widetilde{K}
denote the
\epsilon
-neighborhood of
K
for some
\epsilon>0
. We take any
g,h\in G
, and let
\delta
be the distance between
g\widetilde{K}
and
h\widetilde{K}
. We also take any
u\in L^{2}(S|_{g\widetilde{K}})
and
v\in C^{\infty}_{c}(S)
with
\mathrm{supp}(v)\subset h\widetilde{K}
. Let
\phi\in\mathcal{G}
. Then by Lemma
3.11
, we have

\displaystyle|\langle u,\widetilde{D}^{i}(\phi(\widetilde{D})\widetilde{D}^{j}v)|_{g\widetilde{K}}\rangle_{L^{2}(S|_{g\widetilde{K}})}|

\displaystyle=|\langle u,\widetilde{D}^{i}(\phi(\widetilde{D})\widetilde{D}^{j}v)\rangle_{L^{2}(S)}|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widehat{\phi}(t)\langle u,e^{\sqrt{-1}t\widetilde{D}}\widetilde{D}^{i+j}v\rangle_{L^{2}(S)}dt\right|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widehat{\phi}^{(i+j)}(t)\langle u,e^{\sqrt{-1}t\widetilde{D}}v\rangle_{L^{2}(S)}dt\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{|t|\geq\delta}C_{1}e^{-C_{2}t^{2}}dt\|u\|_{L^{2}(S|_{g\widetilde{K}})}\|v\|_{L^{2}(S|_{h\widetilde{K}})}

\displaystyle\leq C_{3}e^{-C_{2}\delta^{2}}\|u\|_{L^{2}(S|_{g\widetilde{K}})}\|v\|_{L^{2}(S|_{h\widetilde{K}})}

for some
C_{1},C_{2},C_{3}>0
which are independent of the choice of
u,v
, where
i,j=0,1,\ldots,\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil
. Hence as
u
is an arbitrary element of
L^{2}(S|_{g\widetilde{K}})
, we get

(3.5)

\|\widetilde{D}^{i}((\varphi(\widetilde{D})\widetilde{D}^{j}v)|_{g\widetilde{K}})\|_{L^{2}(S|_{g\widetilde{K}})}\leq C_{3}e^{-C_{2}\delta^{2}}\|v\|_{L^{2}(S|_{h\widetilde{K}})}

for
i,j=0,1,\ldots,\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil
. By Lemma
3.8
, we have the Sobolev inequality

\sup_{x\in g\widetilde{K}}|(\varphi(\widetilde{D})\widetilde{D}^{j}v)(x)|\leq C_{4}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil}\|\widetilde{D}^{i}(\varphi(\widetilde{D})\widetilde{D}^{j}v)|_{g\widetilde{K}}\|_{L^{2}(S|_{g\widetilde{K}})}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

for some
C_{4}>0
which is independent of the choice of
v
and
g
, where
j=0,1,\ldots,\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil
. Then by (
3.5
), we obtain

(3.6)

\sup_{x\in g\widetilde{K}}|(\varphi(\widetilde{D})\widetilde{D}^{j}v)(x)|\leq\lceil\tfrac{n}{2}\rceil C_{3}C_{4}e^{-C_{2}\delta^{2}}\|v\|_{L^{2}(S|_{h\widetilde{K}})}

for
j=0,1,\ldots,\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil
. Now we take any
x\in g\widetilde{K}
and
u_{x}\in S_{x}
. Let
u=\mathbf{1}_{h\widetilde{K}}(y)k_{\phi(\widetilde{D})}(x,-)^{*}u_{x}\in L^{2}(S|_{h\widetilde{K}})
. It follows from (
3.6
) that

\displaystyle\langle\widetilde{D}^{j}u,v\rangle_{L^{2}(S|_{h\widetilde{K}})}

\displaystyle=\langle u,\widetilde{D}^{j}v\rangle_{L^{2}(S)}

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{M}\langle k_{\phi(\widetilde{D})}(x,y)^{*}u_{x},v(y)\rangle\operatorname{vol}(y)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{M}\langle u_{x},k_{\phi(\widetilde{D})}(x,y)v(y)\rangle\operatorname{vol}(y)\right|

\displaystyle=|\langle u_{x},(\varphi(\widetilde{D})\widetilde{D}^{j}v)(x)\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq\lceil\tfrac{n}{2}\rceil C_{3}C_{4}e^{-C_{2}\delta}|u_{x}|\|v\|_{L^{2}(S|_{h\widetilde{K}})}

for
j=0,1,\ldots,\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil
. Then since
v
is any element of
C_{c}^{\infty}(S|_{h\widetilde{K}})
, we get

\|\widetilde{D}^{j}u\|_{L^{2}(S|_{h\widetilde{K}})}\leq C_{5}e^{-C_{2}\delta^{2}}|u_{x}|.

for some
C_{5}>0
which is independent of the choice of
x
and
u_{x}
, where
j=0,1,\ldots,\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil
. Thus by arguing as above, we obtain

|k(x,y)^{*}u_{x}|\leq C_{7}e^{-C_{2}\delta^{2}}|u_{x}|

for some
C_{7}>0
which is independent of the choice of
g
and
h
, where
x\in g\widetilde{K}
and
y\in h\widetilde{K}
. As
u_{x}
is an arbitrary element of
S_{x}
, this implies that

|k(x,y)|=|k(x,y)^{*}|\leq C_{7}e^{-C_{2}\delta^{2}}

for any
x\in g\widetilde{K}
and
y\in h\widetilde{K}
. Thus by (
3.1
), we obtain
\phi(\widetilde{D})\in\mathcal{G}
. Clearly, the map in the statement is a homomorphism, completing the proof.
∎

4.
Piecewise normalized Betti number

In this section, we define piecewise normalized Betti numbers as an analogy to normalized Betti numbers defined by Roe
[
19
,
20
]
, and prove its invariance under a certain deformation.

4.1.
Abstract differential

Let
\Lambda^{k}=\Lambda^{k}T^{*}M
for
k=0,1,\ldots,n
, and let
\Lambda=\bigoplus_{k=0}^{n}\Lambda^{k}
. Then
\Lambda
is a
G
-invariant Clifford bundle with Dirac operator
D=d+d^{*}
. Roe
[
19
]
defined an abstract differential on
\Lambda
and the normalized Betti number of it. Here, we define a slightly different operator adopted to our situation, but we ambiguously call it an abstract differential.

Definition 4.1
.

An operator
\tilde{d}\colon C_{c}^{\infty}(\Lambda^{k})\to C_{c}^{\infty}(\Lambda^{k+1})
is an
abstract differential
if it satisfies that

(1)

\tilde{d}^{2}=0
, and

(2)

\tilde{d}+\tilde{d}^{*}
is a generalized Dirac operator, that is,
D+A
for some self-adjoint endomorphism
A
of
\Lambda
, such that
\nabla^{i}A
is bounded for
i=0,1,\ldots\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil-1
.

We give the most important example of an abstract differential.

Example 4.2
.

Let
f\colon M\to\mathbb{R}
be a function. The Witten deformation of the differential
d
on
\Lambda
is defined by
d_{t}=e^{-tf}de^{tf}
. Clearly,
d_{t}^{2}=0
and

d_{t}+d_{t}^{*}=D+tR

where
R=(df\wedge)-(df\lrcorner)
. If
f
is bounded, that is,
\nabla^{i}f
is bounded for
i=0,1,\ldots,\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil
, then
\nabla^{i}R
is bounded for
i=0,1,\ldots,\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil-1
. Thus
d_{t}
is an abstract differential.

We define piecewise normalized Betti numbers of an abstract differential by replacing trace with piecewise trace in the definition of normalized Betti numbers
[
20
, p.766]
. However, compactly supported nonzero functions do not belong to the algebra
\mathcal{G}
. Then, instead of showing that
\phi(\widetilde{D})
is of piecewise trace-class for a compactly supported function
\phi\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
, we extend the definition of piecewise trace.

Definition 4.3
.

Let
A\colon L^{2}(\Lambda)\to L^{2}(\Lambda)
be an operator with bounded kernel function. We define a bounded function

\widetilde{\operatorname{Tr}}(A)\colon G\to\mathbb{R},\quad g\mapsto\int_{gK}\mathrm{tr}(k_{A}(x,x))\operatorname{vol}(x).

By Lemma
2.7
, we have:

Proposition 4.4
.

If
A\colon L^{2}(\Lambda)\to L^{2}(\Lambda)
is of piecewise trace-class, then

\widetilde{\operatorname{Tr}}(A)=\operatorname{Tr}(A).

Let
\tilde{d}\colon\Lambda\to\Lambda
be an abstract differential, and let
\widetilde{D}=\tilde{d}+\tilde{d}^{*}
.

Lemma 4.5
.

For any Schwartz-class function
\phi\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
,
\phi(\widetilde{D})
has a bounded kernel function.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.8
, there is an embedding
\widetilde{W}^{k}\to W^{k}
, where
\widetilde{W}^{k}
and
W^{k}
denote the
k
-th Sobolev spaces with respect to
\widetilde{D}
and
D
, respectively. Then the statement can be verified quite similarly to Proposition
3.12
.
∎

Let
\mathcal{C}
denote the set of compactly supported functions
\phi\colon\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\phi(x^{2})
is smooth,
0\leq\phi\leq 1
and
\phi(0)=1
.

Definition 4.6
.

We define the
piecewise normalized
k
-th Betti number
associated to an abstract differential
\tilde{d}
as a bounded function

b_{k}(\tilde{d})\colon G\to\mathbb{R},\quad g\mapsto\inf_{\phi\in\mathcal{C}}\widetilde{\operatorname{Tr}}(\phi(\widetilde{D}^{2}|_{\Lambda^{k}}))(g).

Clearly,
\widetilde{\operatorname{Tr}}(\phi(\widetilde{D}^{2}|_{\Lambda^{k}}))\geq 0
for
\phi\in\mathcal{C}
, so the infimum in the above definition exists. We choose
C>0
satisfying
|\operatorname{Tr}(e^{-\widetilde{D}^{2}})|\leq C\mathbbm{1}
. We say that a subset
\mathcal{C}_{0}\subset\mathcal{C}
is
cofinal
if for any
\tilde{\phi}\in\mathcal{C}
and
\epsilon>0
, there is
\phi\in\mathcal{C}_{0}
satisfying

(4.1)

\tilde{\phi}(x^{2})+C^{-1}\epsilon e^{-x^{2}}\geq\phi(x^{2}).

Lemma 4.7
.

If
\mathcal{C}_{0}
is a cofinal subset of
\mathcal{C}
, then for each
g\in G
,

b_{k}(\tilde{d})(g)=\inf_{\phi\in\mathcal{C}_{0}}\widetilde{\operatorname{Tr}}(\phi(\widetilde{D}^{2}|_{\Lambda_{k}}))(g).

Proof.

Take any
\tilde{\phi}\in\mathcal{C}
and
\epsilon>0
. Then there is
\phi\in\mathcal{C}_{0}
satisfying (
4.1
), so we get

\widetilde{\operatorname{Tr}}(\tilde{\phi}(\widetilde{D}^{2}|_{\Lambda_{k}}))+\epsilon\geq\widetilde{\operatorname{Tr}}(\phi(\widetilde{D}^{2}|_{\Lambda_{k}})).

Thus as
\epsilon
can be arbitrarily small, we obtain

b_{k}(\tilde{d})(g)\geq\inf_{\phi\in\mathcal{C}_{0}}\widetilde{\operatorname{Tr}}(\phi(\widetilde{D}^{2}|_{\Lambda_{k}}))(g)

for each
g\in G
. The reverse inequality is obvious by the definition of
b_{k}(\tilde{d})
, and therefore the statement follows.
∎

Any function on
[0,1]
may be considered as a function on
\mathbb{R}
by extending it by zero outside of
[0,1]
. Then we can define
\widetilde{\operatorname{Tr}}(\phi(\widetilde{D}^{2}|_{\Lambda^{k}}))
for any
\phi\in C^{\infty}[0,1]
.

Lemma 4.8
.

For any bounded linear function
\alpha\colon\ell^{\infty}(G)\to\mathbb{R}
, there is a signed regular Borel measure
\mu
on
[0,1]
such that for any
\phi\in C^{\infty}[0,1]
,

\alpha(\widetilde{\operatorname{Tr}}(\phi(\widetilde{D}^{2}|_{\Lambda^{k}})))=\int_{0}^{1}\phi d\mu.

Proof.

There is
C_{0}>0
such that

(4.2)

|\widetilde{\operatorname{Tr}}(\phi(\widetilde{D}^{2}|_{\Lambda^{k}}))|\leq\sup_{x\in[0,1]}|\phi(x)||\widetilde{\operatorname{Tr}}(\mathbf{1}_{[0,1]}(\widetilde{D}^{2}|_{\Lambda^{k}}))|\leq C_{0}\sup_{x\in[0,1]}|\phi(x)|\mathbbm{1}

for any
\phi\in C^{\infty}[0,1]
. Then the map

C^{\infty}[0,1]\to\mathbb{R},\quad\phi\mapsto\widetilde{\operatorname{Tr}}(\phi(\widetilde{D}^{2}|_{\Lambda^{k}}))

is a bounded linear function. Thus the statement follows from the Riesz representation theorem
[
22
, 6.19 Theorem]
.
∎

Proposition 4.9
.

The piecewise trace
\operatorname{Tr}(e^{-t\widetilde{D}^{2}|_{\Lambda^{k}}})
converges weakly to
b_{k}(\tilde{d})
as
t\to\infty
.

Proof.

Clearly, there is
\phi\in\mathcal{C}
satisfying
e^{-x^{2}}\geq\phi(x^{2})
. Let
\mathcal{C}_{0}=\{\phi(tx)\mid t>0\}
. Then it is easy to see that
\mathcal{C}_{0}
is a cofinal subset of
\mathcal{C}
. Hence by Lemma
4.8
and Lebesgue’s monotone convergence theorem,
\widetilde{\operatorname{Tr}}(\phi(t\widetilde{D}^{2}|_{\Lambda^{k}}))
converges weakly to
b_{k}(\tilde{d})
as
t\to\infty
. Observe that for any
\epsilon,s>0
, there is
t>0
satisfying

\phi(sx^{2})+C^{-1}\epsilon e^{-x^{2}}\geq e^{-tx^{2}}

where
C>0
is the constant in the definition of a cofinal subset of
\mathcal{C}
. Then

\widetilde{\operatorname{Tr}}(\phi(t\widetilde{D}^{2}|_{\Lambda^{k}}))\leq\operatorname{Tr}(e^{-t\widetilde{D}^{2}|_{\Lambda^{k}}})\leq\widetilde{\operatorname{Tr}}(\phi(s\widetilde{D}^{2}|_{\Lambda^{k}}))+\epsilon\mathbbm{1}.

Let
\alpha\colon\ell^{\infty}(G)\to\mathbb{R}
be a positive bounded linear function. Then we have

\alpha(\widetilde{\operatorname{Tr}}(\phi(t\widetilde{D}^{2}|_{\Lambda^{k}})))\leq\alpha(\operatorname{Tr}(e^{-t\widetilde{D}^{2}|_{\Lambda^{k}}}))\leq\alpha(\widetilde{\operatorname{Tr}}(\phi(s\widetilde{D}^{2}|_{\Lambda^{k}})))+\epsilon\alpha(\mathbbm{1}).

Note that
\epsilon
can be arbitrarily small, and accordingly,
s
and
t
can be arbitrarily large. Then we get

(4.3)

\lim_{t\to\infty}\alpha(\operatorname{Tr}(e^{-t\widetilde{D}^{2}|_{\Lambda^{k}}}))\to\alpha(b_{k}(\tilde{d})).

As in
[
5
, Chapter IV, 8.16]
, any bounded linear function on

is given by the integral with a bounded finitely additive signed measure, so by
[
5
, Chapter III, 1.8]
, it admits the Jordan decomposition. Then (

) holds for any bounded linear function
\alpha\colon\ell^{\infty}(G)\to\mathbb{R}
, completing the proof.
∎

4.2.
Invariance

The rest of this section is devoted to prove the following theorem which is an analogy to
[

, (2.6) Theorem]
.

Theorem 4.10
.

Let
F\colon\Lambda\to\Lambda
be a bounded self-adjoint automorphism, and let
\tilde{d}_{1},\tilde{d}_{2}
be abstract differentials satisfying

\tilde{d}_{1}F=F\tilde{d}_{2}.

Then for

, we have

b_{k}(\tilde{d}_{1})\approx b_{k}(\tilde{d}_{2})\mod\overline{\mathcal{I}}.

The proof of this theorem is basically a modification of that of
[
20
, (2.6) Theorem]
.

Lemma 4.11

(cf.
[
20
, Theorem 4.1]
)
.

Let
E\colon L^{2}(\Lambda^{k})\to L^{2}(\Lambda^{k})
be a self-adjoint piecewise trace-class operator. Then

\operatorname{Tr}(E)\geq-\rho_{1}(E-E^{2}).

Proof.

By Proposition
2.3
,
E^{2}
is of piecewise trace-class, so
\rho_{1}(E-E^{2})
and the piecewise traces of
E^{2}
and
E-E^{2}
are defined. As
E
is self-adjoint,
E^{2}
is positive, so by Proposition
2.10
,
\operatorname{Tr}(E^{2})\geq 0
. Then we get

\operatorname{Tr}(E)=\operatorname{Tr}(E-E^{2})+\operatorname{Tr}(E^{2})\geq\operatorname{Tr}(E-E^{2})\geq-\rho_{1}(E-E^{2})

and hence the statement follows.
∎

For positive integers
p
and
q
, we set

P_{p}=e^{-p(\tilde{d}_{1}+\tilde{d}_{1}^{*})^{2}|_{\Lambda^{k}}}\quad\text{and}\quad Q_{q}=F^{-1}e^{-q(\tilde{d}_{2}+\tilde{d}_{2}^{*})^{2}|_{\Lambda^{k}}}F

where
\tilde{d}_{1},\tilde{d}_{2}

Lemma 4.12
.

The operator
Q_{q}
is of piecewise trace-class such that

\operatorname{Tr}(Q_{q})=\operatorname{Tr}(FQ_{q}F^{-1}).

Proof.

Observe that

k_{AF^{-1}}(x,y)=k_{A}(x,y)F^{-1}\quad\text{and}\quad k_{AF}(x,y)=k_{A}(x,y)F

where

. Then since

is bounded and

is piecewise Hilbert-Schmidt, the proof of Lemma
2.4
implies that

and
AF
are piecewise Hilbert-Schmidt, implying that

is of piecewise trace-class. Moreover, we have

\mathrm{tr}(k_{FQ_{q}F^{-1}}(x,y))=\mathrm{tr}(Fk_{Q_{q}}(x,y)F^{-1})=\mathrm{tr}(k_{Q_{q}}(x,y)).

Thus by Lemma
2.7
, the statement follows.

Lemma 4.13

(cf.
[

, (5.4) Corollary]

.

Lemma 4.14

(cf.
[

Lemma 4.15
.

Let
A\colon L^{2}(\Lambda)\to L^{2}(\Lambda)
be a bounded operator, and let
B\colon L^{2}(\Lambda)\to L^{2}(\Lambda)
be a piecewise Hilbert-Schmidt operator. Then for
p+q\geq 1

\rho_{1}(ABP_{p}Q_{q})\leq C\|A\|\rho_{2}(B)

where
C>0
is independent of the choice of

Proof.

By Propositions
2.3
and

, we have

\rho_{1}(ABP_{p}Q_{q})\leq\rho_{2}(AB)\rho_{2}(P_{p}Q_{q})\leq\|A\|\rho_{2}(B)\rho_{2}(P_{p}Q_{q}).

If

, then by Proposition

\rho_{2}(P_{p}Q_{q})\leq\|P_{p}Q_{q-1}\|\rho_{2}(Q_{1})=\rho_{2}(Q_{1}).

If

, then we can see quite similarly that

Lemma 4.16
.

Let
\alpha\colon\ell^{\infty}(G)\to\mathbb{R}
be a positive bounded linear function. Suppose that for

and
i=0,1
,

\displaystyle\alpha(\rho_{1}(P_{p}-P_{2p+i}))<\epsilon\qquad

\displaystyle\alpha(\rho_{1}(Q_{q}-Q_{2q+i}))<\epsilon

\displaystyle\alpha(\rho_{1}(P_{p}-P_{p+1}))<\epsilon

\displaystyle\rho_{2}(P_{2p}-P_{p}Q_{q}P_{p})<\epsilon\mathbbm{1}.

Then there is an inequality

\displaystyle\alpha(\operatorname{Tr}(P_{p}))

\displaystyle<\alpha(\operatorname{Tr}(Q_{q}))+\alpha(\operatorname{Tr}([P_{p}Q_{q},P_{p+1}]))

\displaystyle\quad+\alpha(\operatorname{Tr}([P_{p}Q_{q+1},Q_{q}]))+C\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}

for some
C>0
which is independent of the choice of
p,q,\epsilon
.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.15
, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}(P_{p})

\displaystyle\leq\rho_{1}(P_{p}-P_{2p+1})+\rho_{1}((P_{2p}-P_{p}Q_{q}P_{p})P_{1})+\operatorname{Tr}(P_{p}Q_{q}P_{p+1})

\displaystyle\leq\rho_{1}(P_{p}-P_{2p+1})+C_{1}\rho_{2}(P_{2p}-P_{p}Q_{q}P_{p})+\operatorname{Tr}(P_{p}Q_{q}P_{p+1})

for some
C_{1}>0
which is independent of the choice of
p,q,\epsilon
. Then we get

\alpha(\operatorname{Tr}(P_{p}))\leq\alpha(\operatorname{Tr}(P_{p}Q_{q}P_{p+1}))+(1+C_{1})\epsilon.

By Lemma
4.15
, we also have

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}(P_{p}Q_{q}P_{p+1})

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{Tr}(P_{p}Q_{q})+\rho_{1}((P_{2p+1}-P_{p})Q_{q})+\operatorname{Tr}([P_{p}Q_{q},P_{p+1}])

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{Tr}(P_{p}Q_{q})+C_{2}\rho_{2}(P_{2p+1}-P_{p})+\operatorname{Tr}([P_{p}Q_{q},P_{p+1}])

for some
C_{2}>0
which is independent of the choice of
p,q,\epsilon
.
Observe that by Proposition
2.3
,

(4.4)

\rho_{2}(P_{p_{1}}-P_{p_{2}})=\rho_{1}((P_{p_{1}}-P_{p_{2}})^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\rho_{1}(P_{p_{1}}-P_{p_{2}})^{\frac{1}{2}}

for

because
(e^{-p_{1}x}-e^{-p_{2}x})^{2}\leq e^{-p_{1}x}-e^{-p_{2}x}
. Then we get

\alpha(\operatorname{Tr}(P_{p}Q_{q}P_{p+1}))\leq\alpha(\operatorname{Tr}(P_{p}Q_{q}))+\alpha(\operatorname{Tr}([P_{p}Q_{q},P_{p+1}]))+C_{2}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Since
\rho_{2}(P_{p})\leq\|P_{p-1}\|\rho_{2}(P_{1})=\rho_{2}(P_{1})
by Propositioin
2.3
, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}(P_{p}Q_{q})

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{Tr}(P_{p}Q_{2q+1})+\rho_{1}(P_{p}(Q_{q}-Q_{2q+1}))

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{Tr}(P_{p}Q_{2q+1})+\rho_{2}(P_{p})\rho_{2}(Q_{q}-Q_{2q+1})

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{Tr}(P_{p}Q_{2q+1})+\rho_{2}(P_{1})\rho_{2}(Q_{q}-Q_{2q+1})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Tr}(Q_{q}P_{p}Q_{q+1})+\operatorname{Tr}([P_{q}Q_{q+1},Q_{q}])+\rho_{2}(P_{1})\rho_{2}(Q_{q}-Q_{2q+1}).

Quite similarly to (
4.4
), we can see that

(4.5)

\rho_{2}(Q_{q_{1}}-Q_{q_{2}})\leq\|F\|^{2}\|F^{-1}\|^{2}\rho_{1}(Q_{q_{1}}-Q_{q_{2}})^{\frac{1}{2}}

for

\alpha(\operatorname{Tr}(P_{p}Q_{q}))\leq\alpha(\operatorname{Tr}(Q_{q}P_{p}Q_{q+1}))+\alpha(\operatorname{Tr}([P_{q}Q_{q+1},Q_{q}]))+C_{3}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\alpha(\operatorname{Tr}([P_{p}Q_{q+1},Q_{q}]))+C_{4}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}

5.
Morse inequality

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.3
and its corollaries by collecting the results obtained in the previous sections. Then we pose some questions on the boundedness of a Morse function that we anticipate will be fruitful.

5.1.
Strongly uniform Morse function

The key technical ingredient of Morse theory is the Morse lemma which states that near a critical point, a Morse function is identified with a nondegenerate quadratic function by choosing an appropriate coordinate. Such a coordinate is not generally a normal coordinate, which is inconvenient for our purpose. Then, instead of Morse lemma, we consider replacement of a given Morse function by a nice Morse function with the same critical points. We show that this is possible for a strongly uniform Morse function, and so we only need to prove Theorem
1.3
for such a nice Morse function. In the sequel, let
L
denote the
G
-invariant triangulation of
M
which is used to construct a fundamental domain
K
.

Definition 5.1
.

A bounded Morse function
f\colon M\to\mathbb{R}
is called
strongly uniform
if it is uniform for
\epsilon>0
and satisfies the following conditions:

(1)

there is
\delta_{1}>0
such that
|\lambda|\geq\delta_{1}
, where
\lambda
ranges over all eigenvalues of the Hessian
\nabla^{2}f(p)
for all
p\in\operatorname{Crit}(f)
;

(2)

there is
\delta_{2}>0
such that
|\nabla f|\geq\delta_{2}
on
M-N(\operatorname{Crit}(f);\epsilon_{0})
;

(3)

the
\epsilon
-neighborhood of any critical point of
f
is contained in some
n
-simplex of
L
.

We set

\epsilon_{0}=\frac{\delta_{2}+8(\sup|\nabla^{3}f|)\epsilon^{2}}{\delta_{1}}.

Since
\delta_{2}
and
\epsilon
can be arbitrarily small, we may assume that

(5.1)

\epsilon_{0}<\frac{\epsilon}{2}.

Let
g
denote the fixed
G
-invariant metric on
M
. We introduce a new
G
-invariant metric on
M
. Take any small
\epsilon>0
. We define a
G
-invariant metric
g(\epsilon)
on
M
such that
g(\epsilon)=g
on
N(L_{n-1};\frac{\epsilon}{2})
and
g(\epsilon)
is flat on the complement of
N(L_{n-1};\epsilon)
, where
L_{n-1}
denotes the
(n-1)
-skeleton of
L
. Indeed, such a
G
-invariant metric
g(\epsilon)
exists. Let
f\colon M\to\mathbb{R}
be a uniform Morse function with respect to
\epsilon>0
. Then for any critical point
p
of
f
,
N(p;\epsilon)
is in the complement of
N(L_{n-1};\epsilon)
, and so the metric
g(\epsilon)
is flat on
N(p;\epsilon)
. We remark that all results in the previous sections hold for any

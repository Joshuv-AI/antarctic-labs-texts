A reduced basis method for parabolic PDEs based on a space-time least squares formulation
Michael Hinze, Christian Kahle, Michael Stahl

Abstract
In this work, we present a POD-greedy reduced basis method for parabolic partial differential equations (PDEs), based on the least squares space-time formulation proposed in [ 19 ] that assumes only minimal regularity. We extend this approach to the parameter-dependent case. The corresponding variational formulation then is based on a parameter-dependent, symmetric, uniformly coercive, and continuous bilinear form. We apply the reduced basis method to this formulation, following the well-developed techniques for parameterized coercive problems, as seen e.g. in reduced basis methods for parameterized elliptic PDEs. We present an offline–online decomposition and provide certification with absolute and relative error bounds. The performance of the method is demonstrated using selected numerical examples.

Introduction.

Let
V
denote a real vector space and
a(\mu;\cdot,\cdot):V\times V\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
a family of inner products such that
V(\mu):=(V,a(\mu;\cdot,\cdot))
form Hilbert spaces. Here,
\mu\in\mathcal{P}
, where
\mathcal{P}
denotes a parameter set. Furthermore, let
A(\mu):V(\mu)\rightarrow V^{\star}(\mu)
denote the Riesz isomorphism associated with
a(\mu;\cdot,\cdot)
, i.e.

\displaystyle\langle A(\mu)\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{V^{\star}(\mu),V(\mu)}:=a(\mu;\cdot,\cdot).

(1)

We develop a certified reduced basis method for parametrized parabolic equations of the form

y_{t}+A(\mu)y=f(\mu)\text{ in }L^{2}(0,T;V^{\star}(\mu)),\quad y(0)=y_{0}(\mu)\text{ in }H,

(2)

where
(V(\mu),H,V^{\star}(\mu))
for every
\mu\in\mathcal{P}
forms a Gelfand triple and
f(\mu)\in L^{2}(0,T;V^{\star}(\mu))
.

To derive a variational formulation for (
2
) we build upon the least squares space-time approach of
[
19
]
, which under natural regularity assumptions on the data leads to a variational formulation of the form

\displaystyle b(\mu;y,w)=l(\mu;w)\qquad\forall w\in W^{\mu}(0,T),

(3)

with continuous, symmetric and uniformly coercive bilinear forms
b(\mu;\cdot,\cdot)
, where
W^{\mu}(0,T):=\{v\in L^{2}(0,T;V(\mu))\mid v_{t}\in L^{2}(0,T;V(\mu)^{\star})\}
. For details we refer to Section
2
.
This establishes a space-time reduced basis framework based on the inner product induced by
b
, which inherits many advantages from the classical elliptic setting.
We focus on a POD-greedy method in the given natural space-time norm, demonstrating its performance with absolute and relative error bounds through two numerical examples.

Literature.

The reduced basis method is well established for problems with a variational formulation involving a uniformly coercive and continuous bilinear form. This is the case, for example, in the setting of elliptic problems. For an overview of standard model order reduction techniques, see references
[
18
,
15
,
12
,
17
,
21
,
28
]
. Of particular interest to us is the computational performance gain through
offline-online decomposition
and the construction of best approximations by
POD
.

Parabolic equations are often treated with time-stepping methods. For example, the backward Euler–Galerkin method is used to solve 3D problems in
[
9
]
. In this method, the time-dependent partial differential equation (PDE) is solved by computing the solutions to a sequence of time-independent problems. In the parameter-dependent case, this allows the efficient application of reduced basis methods; however, the known error estimators need to work with the full time grid. Error estimators exist for the state at a given time point
[
15
,
18
]
, as well as for some space-time energy norm
[
13
,
15
]
. The latter is realized in
[
13
]
by treating time as an additional parameter. Combining these error estimators with a POD-greedy approach establishes an exponential decay of the Kolmogorov N-width, and hence convergence of the reduced basis method. In
[
16
, Prop. 4.4]
this is achieved by applying POD to the
flat set
, which is the union of all local (time-independent) steps of the high-fidelity solutions. In
[
13
]
, the authors also use a greedy algorithm and demonstrate its performance on a heat shield example.

In recent years, there has been a growing interest in the use of space-time formulations for reduced basis methods. In
[
29
]
, reduced-basis output bound methods are extended from the elliptic case to parabolic partial differential equations by treating time as an additional parameter, in a manner similar to
[
13
]
. In this study, the authors employ a space-time formulation and solve the high-fidelity problems numerically using the discontinuous Galerkin method in time. As a numerical example, they consider a
thermal fin
problem.

In
[
8
]
, the reduced basis approach is employed within an
hp
framework for linear and nonlinear parabolic problems. The parameter domain is split into subdomains for which individual RB spaces are constructed. The POD method is used in time and a greedy algorithm is used with respect to the parameters. The high-fidelity problems are solved using a backward Euler and Crank–Nicolson scheme. Error estimators are also provided in the aforementioned spatio-temporal energy norm.

Fully space-time reduced basis methods for parabolic problems are introduced in
[
38
,
39
]
. In these, the variational formulation contains a bilinear form which is non-symmetric in time and satisfies an inf-sup condition. Linear systems are obtained using a Petrov–Galerkin approximation. The reduced basis space is constructed with respect to the spatial solutions, providing an error estimator in a discrete norm arising from the Riesz lift. This approach is applied to option pricing in
[
24
]
.

A similar variational formulation in space-time is employed for the numerical treatment of time-periodic problems in
[
34
]
. There, the authors use basis functions that are periodic in time, realized through wavelets. They also provide
a posteriori
error estimators for the reduced basis approach.

For an overview of reduced basis methods, particularly in the context of space-time methods for parabolic problems, see
[
21
, § 1.4.2]
.
In this work, we also employ the concept of a discrete
W(0,T)
norm from
[
39
]
, with the aim of introducing a reduced basis approach for the full
W(0,T)
using a symmetric space-time formulation.

There are also contributions investigating certified reduced basis methods for nonlinear, time-dependent equations. In
[
42
]
, a certified reduced basis method for the Boussinesq system is proposed. Quasilinear parabolic equations are investigated in
[
20
]
and
[
22
]
. Nonsmooth semilinear parabolic equations are considered in
[
6
]
. A general framework for nonlinear parabolic equations with empirical interpolation is presented in
[
5
]
.

The convergence of reduced basis methods is measured by the Kolmogorov N-width, which quantifies the worst best-approximation that appears in a given parameter set.
In
[
25
, Thm. 3.1]
, a general proof of exponential convergence is provided in the case where the variational formulation involves a bilinear form that is uniformly coercive, continuous, and allows a parameter decomposition. We also refer to
[
37
]
in the context of model reduction for
LTI systems
and the recent work
[
2
]
investigating the Kolmogorov
N
-width for linear transport problems.

In
[
19
]
, we introduced a least squares space-time formulation for parabolic problems with natural regularity. Our reduced basis approach is based on this. The bilinear form in the variational formulation is symmetric, uniformly coercive and continuous.
For numerical treatment, the problem is reformulated as an equivalent saddle-point equation, and it has been shown that the Galerkin approximation converges to the continuous solution.

2
Problem setting.

Let
(V,(\cdot,\cdot)_{V})
and
(H,(\cdot,\cdot)_{H})

denote separable Hilbert spaces with the properties

V\hookrightarrow H\equiv H^{\star}\hookrightarrow V^{\star}
, so that
(V,H,V^{\star})
forms a Gelfand triple. Let
\mathcal{P}
denote some parameter space and
\mu\in\mathcal{P}
some arbitrary, but fixed parameter. We denote with
a(\mu;\cdot,\cdot):V\times V\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
a parameter-dependent, symmetric, uniformly continuous and coercive bilinear form, defining an inner product on
V
. With this we introduce the Hilbert space
V(\mu)
as the space
V
equipped with the inner product
a(\mu;\cdot,\cdot)
,

\displaystyle(u,v)_{V(\mu)}:=a(\mu;u,v)\qquad\forall u,v\in V.

(4)

By these assumptions,
(V(\mu),(\cdot,\cdot)_{V(\mu)})
is a family of parameter-dependent separable Hilbert spaces with the property

\displaystyle V(\mu)\hookrightarrow H\equiv H^{\star}\hookrightarrow V(\mu)^{\star}\qquad\forall\mu\in\mathcal{P},

(5)

so that
(V(\mu),H,V(\mu)^{\star})
is a family of parameter-dependent Gelfand triples.

We choose some reference parameter
\overline{\mu}\in\mathcal{P}
to identify
V=V(\overline{\mu})
and from here onwards assume that all inner products
a(\mu;\cdot,\cdot)
are equivalent on
V
in the way that
a(\mu;\cdot,\cdot)
is uniformly coercive and continuous, i.e., there exist positive constants
c_{s},c_{c}
independent of
\mu\in\mathcal{P}
such that

\displaystyle|a(\mu;u,v)|\leq c_{s}\|u\|_{V}\|v\|_{V}\quad\text{and}\quad a(\mu;u,u)\geq c_{c}\|u\|_{V}^{2}\qquad\forall u,v\in V.

(6)

Then the norms on
V
and
V(\mu)
as well as on
V^{\star}
and
V(\mu)^{\star}
are equivalent with the estimates

\sqrt{c_{c}}\|v\|_{V}\leq\|v\|_{V(\mu)}\leq\sqrt{c_{s}}\|v\|_{V}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\frac{1}{\sqrt{c_{s}}}\|v\|_{V^{\star}}\leq\|v\|_{V(\mu)^{\star}}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{c_{c}}}\|v\|_{V^{\star}}.

(7)

For given
T>0
and for any fixed
\mu\in\mathcal{P}
we define the parabolic spaces

W^{\mu}(0,T):=\{v\in L^{2}(0,T;V(\mu)),v_{t}\in L^{2}(0,T;V(\mu)^{\star})\}\quad\text{and}\quad W(0,T):=W^{\overline{\mu}}(0,T).

(8)

The inner product is given by

(u,v)_{W^{\mu}(0,T)}:=\int_{0}^{T}(u_{t},v_{t})_{V(\mu)^{\star}}+\int_{0}^{T}(u,v)_{V(\mu)}

(9)

and the norm induced by this inner product is denoted by
\|v\|_{W^{\mu}(0,T)}:=(v,v)_{W^{\mu}(0,T)}^{1/2}
.
Using (
7
) we directly obtain the equivalence of
(\cdot,\cdot)_{W^{\mu}(0,T)}
and
(\cdot,\cdot)_{W(0,T)}
on
W(0,T)
and consequently the equivalence of the induced norms,

\sqrt{\min\left\{c_{c},\frac{1}{c_{s}}\right\}}\|v\|_{W(0,T)}\leq\|v\|_{W^{\mu}(0,T)}\leq\sqrt{\max\left\{c_{s},\frac{1}{c_{c}}\right\}}\|v\|_{W(0,T)}\quad\forall v\in W(0,T).

(10)

Thus, considered as sets, the spaces
W^{\mu}(0,T)
and
W(0,T)
are equal.

With the given family of inner products we associate a family of parameter-dependent operators
A(\mu):V\rightarrow V^{\star}
via

\left\langle A(\mu)u,v\right\rangle_{V^{\star},V}:=a(\mu;u,v)\qquad\forall\mu\in\mathcal{P}\quad\forall u,v\in V.

(11)

With this,
A(\mu):V(\mu)\rightarrow V(\mu)^{\star}
is the Riesz isomorphism. From here onwards we call
R(\mu):=A(\mu)^{-1}:V(\mu)^{\star}\rightarrow V(\mu)
the Riesz lift, satisfying for any
\phi\in V^{\star}

\displaystyle(R(\mu)\phi,v)_{V(\mu)}=\langle\phi,v\rangle_{V^{\star},V}\qquad\forall v\in V

(12)

and for
\phi,\psi\in V^{\star}
we set

\displaystyle(\phi,\psi)_{V(\mu)^{\star}}:=(R(\mu)\phi,R(\mu)\psi)_{V}.

(13)

By definition of
(\cdot,\cdot)_{V(\mu)}
it follows that
A(\mu)R(\mu)\phi=\phi
for all
\phi\in V^{\star}
and

R(\mu)A(\mu)v=v
for all
v\in V
, see
[
41
,
36
]
.

In this setting we naturally extend
R(\mu)
from
V(\mu)^{\star}
to
L^{2}(0,T;V(\mu)^{\star})
and understand
R(\mu)
as operator
R(\mu):L^{2}(0,T;V(\mu)^{\star})\to L^{2}(0,T;V(\mu))
and define
R(\mu)\phi
for any
\phi\in L^{2}(0,T;V^{\star})
as
R(\mu)\phi(t)
for almost every

. With this,
R(\mu)\phi
fulfills

\int_{0}^{T}(R(\mu)\phi,v)_{V(\mu)}=\int_{0}^{T}\langle\phi,v\rangle_{V^{\star},V}\qquad\forall v\in V.

(14)

Finally, assume that parameter-dependent
y_{0}(\mu)\in H
and
f(\mu)\in L^{2}(0,T;V^{\star})
are given, where

is assumed to be uniformly bounded with respect to
\mu
as well, i.e. there exists

such that

\displaystyle||f(\mu)||_{L^{2}(0,T;V^{\star})}\leq c_{s}^{f}.

(15)

For fixed

we solve the parameter-dependent parabolic problem

(
\dagger
)

\displaystyle\eta_{\star}^{\text{rel}}(\mu)

(87)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\eta_{\star}^{\text{abs}}(\mu)}{||y_{d}(\mu)||_{W_{d}}}\geq\frac{||y_{d}(\mu)-y_{\texttt{rb}}(\mu)||_{W_{d}}}{||y_{d}(\mu)||_{W_{d}}}.

(88)

Fully practical estimation of the residual norm.

The estimators in Theorem
5.1
have exactly the same formulation as the ones for the elliptic case in
[
18
,
15
]
. However, in comparison the norm of the Riesz representer of the residual
||\tilde{r}_{d}(\mu)||_{W_{d}}
is not offline-online decomposable and requires further systems solves with
A_{x}(\mu)
. In the following we present a variant of offline-online decomposable error estimators, which involve an estimation of the residual norm.

To obtain these estimators, we compute the norm of the Riesz representer of the residual and multiply the matrix formulation of the inner product on both sides with
(I_{M}\otimes A_{x}(\mu)M_{x}^{-1})(I_{M}\otimes A_{x}(\mu)M_{x}^{-1})^{-1}
, where
I_{M}\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times M}
is the identity matrix. This leads to a matrix
\mathfrak{S}(\mu)\in\mathbb{R}^{NM\times NM}
and vector
\mathfrak{s}(\mu)\in\mathbb{R}^{NM}
that allow a rewriting of
||\tilde{r}_{d}(\mu)||_{W_{d}}
in Theorem
5.3
. We introduce those quantities in the following definition.

Definition 5.2
.

Let
I_{M}\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times M}
and
I_{N}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}
denote identity matrices of respective sizes. For fixed
\mu\in\mathcal{P}
we define

\displaystyle\mathfrak{S}(\mu)

\displaystyle:=Z_{t}^{T}(M_{t}^{\psi})^{-1}Z_{t}\otimes M_{x}+M_{t}\otimes A_{x}(\mu)M_{x}^{-1}A_{x}(\mu)+T_{t}\otimes A_{x}(\mu),

(89)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{s}(\mu)

\displaystyle:=R_{0}^{t}\otimes A_{x}(\mu)M_{x}^{-1}R_{0}^{x}(\mu)+\left(I_{M}\otimes A_{x}(\mu)M_{x}^{-1}\right)F_{1}(\mu)+\left(Z_{t}^{T}(M_{t}^{\psi})^{-1}\otimes I_{N}\right)F_{2}(\mu).

(90)

To obtain an offline-online decomposition of the form

\displaystyle\mathfrak{S}(\mu)=\sum_{q=1}^{Q_{\mathfrak{S}}}\theta_{\mathfrak{S}}^{q}(\mu)\mathfrak{S}_{q}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathfrak{s}(\mu)=\sum_{q=1}^{Q_{\mathfrak{s}}}\theta_{\mathfrak{s}}^{q}(\mu)\mathfrak{s}_{q}

we set

\displaystyle\theta_{\mathfrak{s}}^{q}

\displaystyle:=\theta_{A}^{i}\theta_{y_{0}}^{j},

\displaystyle\mathfrak{s}_{q}:=R_{0}^{t}\otimes A_{x}^{i}M_{x}^{-1}R_{0}^{x,j},

\displaystyle q=(j-1)Q_{A}+i,

(92)

\displaystyle i=1,...,Q_{A},\,j=1,...,Q_{y},

\displaystyle\theta_{\mathfrak{s}}^{q}

\displaystyle:=\theta_{A}^{i}\theta_{f}^{j},\quad

\displaystyle\mathfrak{s}_{q}:=\left(I_{M}\otimes A_{x}^{i}M_{x}^{-1}\right)F_{1}^{j},\quad

\displaystyle q=(j-1)Q_{A}+i+Q_{A}Q_{y},

(93)

\displaystyle i=1,...,Q_{A},\,j=1,...,Q_{f},

\displaystyle\theta_{\mathfrak{s}}^{q}

\displaystyle:=\theta_{f}^{i},

\displaystyle\mathfrak{s}_{q}:=\left(Z_{t}^{T}(M_{t}^{\psi})^{-1}\otimes I_{N}\right)F_{2}^{i},\quad

\displaystyle q=i+Q_{A}Q_{f}+Q_{A}Q_{y},

(94)

\displaystyle i=1,...,Q_{f},

\displaystyle\theta_{\mathfrak{S}}^{1}

\displaystyle:=1,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{S}_{1}:=Z_{t}^{T}(M_{t}^{\psi})^{-1}Z_{t}\otimes M_{x},

(95)

\displaystyle\theta_{\mathfrak{S}}^{q}

\displaystyle:=\theta_{A}^{i}\theta_{A}^{j},\quad

\displaystyle\mathfrak{S}_{q}:=M_{t}\otimes A_{x}^{i}M_{x}^{-1}A_{x}^{j},\quad

\displaystyle q=(j-1)Q_{A}+i+1,

(96)

\displaystyle i,j=1,...,Q_{A},

\displaystyle\theta_{\mathfrak{S}}^{q}

\displaystyle:=\theta_{A}^{i},

\displaystyle\mathfrak{S}_{q}:=T_{t}\otimes A_{x}^{i},

\displaystyle q=i+Q_{A}^{2}+1,

(97)

\displaystyle i=1,...,Q_{A},

as well as

and
Q_{\mathfrak{S}}:=1+Q_{A}^{2}+Q_{A}
.

(Estimation of the residual norm)
.

be fixed and
\mathfrak{r}(\mu)\in\mathbb{R}^{Q_{\mathfrak{s}}+Q_{\mathfrak{S}}\texttt{L}}

\displaystyle:=\left(\theta_{\mathfrak{s}}^{1}(\mu),...,\theta_{\mathfrak{s}}^{Q_{\mathfrak{s}}}(\mu),-\vec{u}_{y}^{T}\theta_{\mathfrak{S}}^{1}(\mu),...,-\vec{u}_{y}^{T}\theta_{\mathfrak{S}}^{Q_{\mathfrak{S}}}(\mu)\right)^{T},

(98)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{R}

\displaystyle:=\left(\mathfrak{s}_{1},...,\mathfrak{s}_{Q_{\mathfrak{s}}},\mathfrak{S}_{1}B_{W},...,\mathfrak{S}_{Q_{\mathfrak{S}}}B_{W}\right),

(99)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{G}

\displaystyle:=\mathfrak{R}^{T}\left(Z_{t}^{T}(M_{t}^{\psi})^{-1}Z_{t}\otimes A_{x}(\overline{\mu})+M_{t}\otimes M_{x}^{-2}A_{x}(\overline{\mu})^{3}+T_{t}\otimes M_{x}^{-1}A_{x}(\overline{\mu})^{2}\right)^{-1}\mathfrak{R}.

(100)

\displaystyle\epsilon^{\text{abs}}(\mu)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sqrt{\mathfrak{r}(\mu)^{T}\mathfrak{G}\mathfrak{r}(\mu)}}{\mathfrak{c}_{c}(\mu)\alpha(\mu)}

Proof.

Let

Then it follows that

\displaystyle||\tilde{r}_{d}(\mu)||^{2}_{W_{d}}

\displaystyle=b_{d}(\overline{\mu};\tilde{r}_{d}(\mu),\tilde{r}_{d}(\mu))

\displaystyle=\vec{r}^{T}(Z_{t}^{T}(M_{t}^{\psi})^{-1}Z_{t}\otimes M_{x}A_{x}^{-1}(\overline{\mu})M_{x}+M_{t}\otimes A_{x}(\overline{\mu})+T_{t}\otimes M_{x})^{-1}\vec{r}

Remark 5.4
.

The estimation in (
109
) is fully offline-online decomposable analogously to the residual of elliptic PDEs
[
18
, § 4.2.5]
. Furthermore, in the standard finite element setting of Remark
3.1

mass lumping
can be used for approximating
M_{x}^{-1}
in practice.

Remark 5.5
.

To avoid the computation of a continuity constant,
\overline{\mu}\in\mathcal{P}
can be chosen such that
\mathfrak{c}_{c}(\mu)\mathfrak{c}_{s}(\mu)\leq 1
holds for all parameters
\mu
, which guarantees

\displaystyle\alpha(\mu)=\mathfrak{c}_{c}(\mu).

(115)

Alternatively, (
44
) can be used to estimate
\alpha(\mu)
with
\alpha_{\text{LB}}
.

In practice, the coercivity constant can be calculated by a (multi-parameter) min-theta-approach
[
26
,
18
]
or the successive constraint method from
[
23
]
effectively.

Remark 5.6
.

From (
104
) we can motivate that for the coefficient vector
\vec{y}
of the high fidelity solution it holds that

\displaystyle\mathfrak{S}(\mu)\vec{y}=\mathfrak{s}(\mu).

(116)

We also notice that
\mathfrak{S}(\mu)
defines a
discrete operator
on
W_{d}
that is uniformly coercive and continuous. Additionally, as

is parameter-separable, the assumptions of
[

are fulfilled and the convergence in the discrete

norm follows. In particular we receive by

that the Kolmogorov

7
Conclusion.

In this work we propose a POD-greedy reduced basis method for parabolic equations based on the least squares space-time approach from
[
19
]
. We adapt this formulation to a parameter-dependent setting. The resulting variational formulation gives rise to a uniformly coercive and continuous bilinear form, for which well-known reduced basis techniques for parametrized elliptic equations can be applied. For certification we propose absolute and relative error estimators in a discrete
W(0,T)
norm, which can be efficiently evaluated using standard offline–online decomposition techniques. We illustrate the performance of our approach using two numerical examples.

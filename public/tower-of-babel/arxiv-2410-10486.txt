Abstract
. We consider multi-agent systems with cooperative interactions and study the convergence to consensus in the case of time-dependent connections, with possible communication failure. We prove a new condition ensuring consensus: we define a graph in which directed arrows correspond to connection functions that converge (in the weak sense) to some function with a positive integral on all intervals of the form [ t , + ∞ ) [t,+\infty) . If the graph has a node reachable from all other indices, i.e. “globally reachable”, then the system converges to consensus. We show that this requirement generalizes some known sufficient conditions for convergence, such as Moreau’s or the Persistent Excitation one. We also give a second new condition, transversal to the known ones: total connectedness of the undirected graph formed by the non-vanishing of limiting functions. More in detail, we consider the system, for
j=1,\dots,N

(1)

\displaystyle\dot{x}_{j}=\sum_{k=1}^{N}{\mathfrak{u}}_{jk}(t)\left(x_{k}-x_{j}\right)\,,

Question:
Which “minimal” properties on the

This question can be seen as a request of minimal level of service to ensure consensus
. It has been extensively studied in the community. The contributions that are closer to our approach are the following:

•

Moreau condition:
In

[

[

[

Our main theorems provide two conditions that are new with respect to the ones described above, and have weaker hypotheses with respect to many of them. Moreover, we will show that these requirements are somehow sharp, in the sense that outside the hypotheses of the theorems it is easy to find examples for which consensus is not achieved. To describe our result, we first need the following easy definition.

Definition 1

(Globally reachable node)
.

A node
\ell^{*}
of a graph
G
is “globally reachable” if for all nodes
i

i\to j_{1}\to\ldots\to\ell^{*}

This concept was already stated in
[
31

as a key property of graphs ensuring consensus, and it ensures that the directed graph contains a directed spanning tree.

We now define the topology for the connection functions, that we explain in §

Definition 2
.

Let
f_{n},f:[0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty)
be Lebesgue integrable in compact intervals, for
n\in\mathbb{N}
. We say that
f_{n}\overset{\ast}{\rightharpoonup}f

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{a}^{b}f_{n}=\int_{a}^{b}f\qquad\text{for all bounded intervals $[a,b]\subset[0,+\infty)$.}

2.
Cooperative multi-agent systems

In this section, we describe some general properties of cooperative multi-agent systems. In this article, we only deal with one-to-one interactions, but we consider possible communication failure in the following sense: we provide conditions ensuring convergence even when many agents can stop communicating for large intervals of time. In wide generality, we study systems of the following form:

(5)

\dot{x}_{j}=\sum_{k=1}^{N}{\mathfrak{u}}_{jk}(t)\phi\left(x_{k}-x_{j}\right)(x_{k}-x_{j}),\qquad\ j=1,\dots,N\,,

where
{\mathfrak{u}}_{jk}\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{+};[0,+\infty))
and
\phi
is nonnegative, bounded and Lipschitz continuous. We denoted by
L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{+};[0,+\infty))
the functional space

(6)

\left\{f:\mathbb{R}^{+}\to[0,+\infty)\text{ Lebesgue measurable with $\int_{0}^{T}f<+\infty$ when $T>0$}\right\}.

This ensures existence, globally in time, and uniqueness for the solution to the associated Cauchy problem, i.e. when an initial condition
(x_{1}(0),\ldots,x_{N}(0))
is fixed, see e.g.
[
22
]
. Solutions are considered in the Carathéodory sense for the rest of the article: trajectories are absolutely continuous functions and (
5
) holds at almost every time.
General results on cooperative systems can also be found in
[
42
]
.
Now:

§
2.1
:

We reduce to the case of
1
-dimensional, linear systems.

§
2.2
:

We remind that the convex hull of positions is weakly contractive in time, and we discuss monotonicity of the set of agents attaining extremal values.

§
2.3

We better explain the topology involved in our sufficient conditions.

2.1.
Reduction to 1-dimensional linear systems

In our article, we study convergence to consensus for (
5
) by considering all possible connection functions
{\mathfrak{u}}_{jk}(t)
, under the assumption that they are integrable on compact intervals and nonnegative. As a consequence, it is not restrictive to assume that the dynamics is linear, as we stated in (
1

Proposition 9
.

Consider a function
\phi
, bounded on compact intervals, and connections
{\mathfrak{u}}_{jk}\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{+};[0,+\infty))
as in (
6
), for
j,k=1,\dots,N
.
Consider any given solution
x(t)
to (
5
) starting from a fixed initial condition
(x_{1}(0),\ldots,x_{N}(0))
. Then there exist functions
\widetilde{{\mathfrak{u}}_{jk}}\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{+};[0,+\infty))
such that
x(t)
solves the linear system

(7)

\dot{x}_{j}=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\widetilde{{\mathfrak{u}}_{jk}}(t)\left(x_{k}-x_{j}\right),\qquad\qquad\ j=1,\dots,N\,.

If
{\mathfrak{u}}_{jk}
are bounded on compact intervals and
M:=\max_{[0,T]}\left\lVert x(t)\right\rVert
, it holds

\left\lVert\widetilde{{\mathfrak{u}}_{jk}}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}[0,T]}\leq\left\lVert{\mathfrak{u}}_{jk}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}[0,T]}\cdot\left\lVert\phi\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}[0,M]}\,.

Proof.

Consider any given trajectory
x(t)
of (
5
) and assume that
t
is a time for which
x
is differentiable. Then it clearly holds

\dot{x}_{j}=\sum_{k=1}^{N}{\mathfrak{u}}_{jk}(t)\phi\left(x_{k}-x_{j}\right)(x_{k}-x_{j})=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\widetilde{{\mathfrak{u}}_{jk}}(t)\left(x_{k}-x_{j}\right),

by choosing

\widetilde{{\mathfrak{u}}_{jk}}(t):={\mathfrak{u}}_{jk}(t)\phi\left(x_{k}(t)-x_{j}(t)\right)\,.

Such coefficients
\widetilde{{\mathfrak{u}}_{jk}}
are integrable on compact intervals: in any interval
[0,T]
indeed

\widetilde{{\mathfrak{u}}_{jk}}(t)\leq C{{\mathfrak{u}}_{jk}}(t)\,,\quad C:=\left\lVert\phi\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}[0,M]}\,,\quad M:=\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}[0,T]}.

∎

.

Let

Define

Let

. Then, the projected trajectory

Remark 12
.

One can recover

from

x(t)=\sum_{j=1}^{d}y(t,\widehat{\mathrm{e}}_{j})\widehat{\mathrm{e}}_{j}
, provided that
\widehat{\mathrm{e}}_{j}\cdot\widehat{\mathrm{e}}_{k}=\delta_{jk}
,

: when
\{\widehat{\mathrm{e}}_{1},\dots,\widehat{\mathrm{e}}_{d}\}
is an orthonormal basis.

Proof.

We prove the first statement. Let
x(t)
be a trajectory. At times

for which
x
is differentiable, by differentiating the identity
y_{j}(t)=x_{j}(t)\cdot v
, we have

(8)

\dot{y}_{j}=\sum_{k=1}^{N}{\mathfrak{u}}_{jk}(x_{k}(t)\cdot v-x_{j}(t)\cdot v)=\sum_{k=1}^{N}{\mathfrak{u}}_{jk}(y_{k}(t)-y_{j}(t)).

We now prove the second statement. We first prove the first implication. Let

converge to a consensus, i.e.
\lim_{t\to+\infty}x_{j}(t)=x^{*}
for all

. Let

, thus

converges to consensus.

of unitary vectors of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, i.e.
\widehat{\mathrm{e}}_{\ell}=(0,\ldots,0,1,0,\ldots)
with 1 in position

\ell=1,\ldots,d
the variables

converge to consensus, i.e. the
\ell

x_{j}(t)
converges to some
(x^{\ell})^{*}
. Since this holds for all components, all

converge to the common vector
((x^{1})^{*},\ldots,(x^{d})^{*})
, i.e. to consensus.

General properties of cooperative systems

We now collect general properties of (

Let
x(t)
be a solution of (
1
). Define the support of the solution at time

as the (closed) convex hull of the set of
x_{i}
at time
t
: precisely

(9)

\mathrm{supp}(x(t)):=\mathrm{conv}(\{x_{i}(t)\}),

Then, for
0\leq t\leq s
it holds
\mathrm{supp}(x(t))\supseteq\mathrm{supp}(x(s))
.

In dimension

, this implies that the maximum function
x_{+}(t):=\max_{j}\{x_{j}(t)\}
is non-increasing and the minimum function
x_{-}(t):=\min_{j}\{x_{j}(t)\}
is non-decreasing.

Proof.

First observe that
\mathrm{supp}(x(t))
is the convex hull of a finite number of points, hence it is a closed polygon.

Let
t
be a time in which
x(t)
is differentiable. If
x_{j}(t)
belongs to the interior of
\mathrm{supp}(x(t))
, by continuity it belongs to the interior of
\mathrm{supp}(x(t+h))
for
h>0
sufficiently small. Assume then that
x_{j}(t)
belongs to the boundary of
\mathrm{supp}(x(t))
: each term
{\mathfrak{u}}_{jk}(t)\left(x_{k}-x_{j}\right)
points inwards in the polygon, due to the fact that
x_{k}
belongs to the polygon and
{\mathfrak{u}}_{jk}(t)
is positive. Then, the sum of all terms, that is
\dot{x}_{j}
, points inwards. Thus, one has
x_{j}(t+h)\in\mathrm{supp}(x(t))
for
h>0
sufficiently small. By merging the two cases, one has
x_{j}(t+h)\in\mathrm{supp}(x(t))
for all
j=1,\ldots,N
, hence by convexity
\mathrm{supp}(x(t+h))\subseteq\mathrm{supp}(x(t))
. This proves the first result.

The results in dimension

directly follow, since
\mathrm{supp}(x(t))
is an interval.
∎

The last statement in dimension
d=1
is very strong.
We even strengthen it, as follows, when extremal values are constant.

Lemma 14
.

Consider a trajectory
x(t)
of (
1
) in
\mathbb{R}
such that
x_{+}^{*}=\max\{x_{i}(t)\ :\ i=1,\dots,N\}
is constant.
Then the set
I^{+}(t)
of indices
i
that realize this maximum is non-increasing in time: if
i\notin I^{+}(t)
then
i\notin I^{+}(t+h)
for all
h>0
.

Similarly, assume that
x_{-}^{*}=\min\{x_{i}(t)\ :\ i=1,\dots,N\}
is constant.
Then the set
I^{-}(t)
of indices
i
that realize this minimum is non-increasing in time.

Proof.

Consider an index
j\notin I^{+}(T)
for some
T\geq 0
, which means
x_{j}(T)<x^{*}_{+}
. Define

, that satisfies
f(T)>0
. Let
t
be a point in which
x(t)
is differentiable. By the dynamic (

\displaystyle\dot{f}(t)

\displaystyle=0-\sum_{k=1}^{N}{\mathfrak{u}}_{jk}(t)\left(x_{k}-x_{j}(t)\right)\geq-\sum_{k=1}^{N}{\mathfrak{u}}_{jk}(t)\left(x^{*}_{+}-x_{j}(t)\right)=-\sum_{k=1}^{N}{\mathfrak{u}}_{jk}(t)f(t).

In the first inequality we used that
x_{k}\leq x^{*}_{+}
, for all

, by the definition of

as a maximum.
Gronwall lemma now ensures

f(t)\geq f(T)\cdot\exp\left(-\int_{T}^{t}\sum_{k=1}^{N}{\mathfrak{u}}_{jk}(s)\,ds\right)>0.

By continuity, the estimate holds for all

, ensuring that
j\not\in I^{+}(t)
for all
t\geq T
.

The statement on the minimum can be proved analogously.
∎

We will use this simple result in Lemma

2.3.
The weak* topologies

In this section we prove a technical lemma to better understand the topology in Definition
2
. We embed nonnegative functions, integrable on compact intervals, into the space of Radon measures, with the inherited weak
∗
-topology. When further restricting to nonnegative bounded functions, we get the weak
∗
-topology of
L^{\infty}
as the dual of
L^{1}
.

Lemma 15
.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
f_{n},f\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{+};[0,+\infty))
, defined in (
6
).
Then the convergence
f_{n}\overset{\ast}{\rightharpoonup}f
specified in Definition
2
is equivalent to

•

f_{n}
converges to
f
if for all
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}
continuous with compact support

(10)

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\varphi f_{n}=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\varphi f\,.

If
f_{n},f
are nonnegative and bounded on compact intervals, it is equivalent to require (
10
) for all
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}
having compact support and with
\int_{0}^{+\infty}\left|\varphi\right|
finite.

If, moreover,
f_{n},f:\mathbb{R}^{+}\to[0,M]
for some
M>0
, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, requiring (
10
) for all
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}
continuous with compact support is equivalent to requiring (
10
) for all
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}
with
\int_{0}^{+\infty}\left|\varphi\right|
finite: on
L^{\infty}
, the convergence
\overset{\ast}{\rightharpoonup}
is the weak
∗
-topology.

Proof.

The equivalence among Definition
2
and the one in (
10
) follows from
[
21
, Theorem 1.40]
, by regularity of Radon measures.

Suppose now additionally that
f_{n}\leq M(C)
and
f\leq M(C)
in
[0,C]
, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

If (
10
) holds for all
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}
having compact support and with
\int_{0}^{+\infty}\left|\varphi\right|
finite, then it trivially holds also for any
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}
continuous with compact support.

If (
10
) holds for any
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}
continuous with compact support, consider any
\psi:\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}
having compact support and with
\int_{0}^{+\infty}\left|\psi\right|
finite, and extend it to be

on
\mathbb{R}^{-}
.
Let
\psi_{\varepsilon}
be a smooth approximation in

of

, having compact support in some
[0,C]
, for example by convolution, see
[

, § 4.2.1]
.
Then take the limsup first as

then, as

, in the triangular inequality

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{+\infty}(f_{n}-f)\psi\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\int_{0}^{+\infty}(f_{n}-f)\psi_{\varepsilon}\right|+M(C)\left\lVert\psi-\psi_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}

to conclude that (
10
) holds also for
\psi
.

Suppose now additionally that
f_{n}
and

are uniformly bounded by

. If
C>0
and
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}
has

finite, then

has compact support: thus, by the previous step, (

) holds for

.
To conclude that (

) holds also for

, take the limsup, first as

, then as

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{+\infty}(f_{n}-f)\varphi\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\int_{0}^{+\infty}(f_{n}-f)\psi_{C}\right|+M\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{L^{1}((C,+\infty))}\,.

∎

Lemma 16

\displaystyle x_{+}^{*}=\max\{x_{i}(t)\ :\ i=1,\dots,N\}

\displaystyle x_{-}^{*}=\min\{x_{i}(t)\ :\ i=1,\dots,N\}

•

nodes are identified with

•

Consider the set

Since

. Moreover, the existence of the arrow

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x_{1}(S)-x_{1}(T)=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\int_{T}^{S}{\mathfrak{u}}_{1k}(t)(x_{k}(t)-x_{1}(t))\,dt=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\int_{T}^{S}{\mathfrak{u}}_{1k}(t)(x_{k}(t)-x^{*}_{+})\,dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{k\neq 2}0+\int_{T}^{S}{\mathfrak{u}}_{12}(t)(x_{2}(t)-x^{*}_{+})\,dt\leq\int_{T}^{S}{\mathfrak{u}}_{12}(t)\,dt~\cdot~(\tilde{x}-x^{*}_{+})<0.

Remark 17

The graph

Proof of Theorem

We first observe that Proposition

Step 1: Construction of a limit trajectory.

be a sequence satisfying the hypothesis of the theorem: there is a node
\ell^{*}

Step 2: The limit trajectory is at consensus.
We now prove that the limit trajectory built in the previous step is at consensus. First fix any

and consider the exponential map

on the time interval

: it associates initial conditions and controls to the trajectory of (

Observe that the dynamics is affine in the connection functions

. We thus endow the space of Lebesgue integrable functions

4.2.
Comparison with Moreau, Persistent Excitation, Integral Scrambling Coefficient conditions

In this section, we compare our results with the Moreau condition, which ensures convergence of all solutions of (
1
). We also compare it with some stronger conditions that are discussed in the literature, namely the Persistent Excitation (PE) and the Integral Scrambling Coefficient (ISC).

We first recall the precise definition of the conditions we study. For (
1
), they can be interpreted in a unified way, based on graph properties. These statements, equivalent to those in the literature but written in a different language, also highlight the chain of logical dependencies: the Moreau condition is weaker than PE. One can also prove that the Moreau condition is weaker than ISC (see
[
2
]
), but we will prove convergence to consensus with a different approach.

First fix
T,\mu>0
and consider a time
t\geq 0
. Define a graph
G(t)
as follows: nodes are identified with
\{1,\ldots,N\}
and an arrow from node
j
to node
k
is drawn if for all
t\geq 0
it holds

(18)

\int_{t}^{t+T}{\mathfrak{u}}_{jk}(\tau)\,d\tau\geq\mu.

We can now state the three conditions,
that also require that
\{{\mathfrak{u}}_{jk}\}_{j,k=1}^{N}
are bounded
:

•

Moreau condition:
There exist
T,\mu>0
such that the graph
G(t)
given above is constant with respect to
t
and has a globally reachable node.

•

ISC:
there exist
T,\mu>0
such that for all
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
with
i\neq j
and
t\geq 0
there exists an index
k_{ij}(t)
such that both arrows
i\to k_{ij}(t)
and
j\to k_{ij}(t)
exist in
G(t)
.

•

PE:
there exist
T,\mu>0
such that for all
j,k\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
with
j\neq k
the arrow
j\to k
exists in
G(t)
(thus also
k\to j
and
G(t)
must be constant).

Remark 26
.

While Moreau and PE condition require a graph that is constant with respect to time, ISC does not require it. In the case of a finite number of agents, one can anyway adapt the Moreau condition to a time-dependent graph and prove that it is weaker than ISC, but changing the values of parameters
\mu,T
. See
[
2
]
.

We now prove that the Moreau condition is equivalent to property
(B)
of Corollary
5

Proof.

4.3.
Comparison with cut-balance conditions

In this section, we compare our results with the so-called cut-balance condition, introduced in
[
25
,
30
]
either in instantaneous or non-instantaneous setting. We recall here the most general formulation, presented in
[
30
, Assumptions 1-2]
, that is as follows:

•

Cut-balance condition
: There exists a sequence of times
\tau_{n}\to+\infty
and uniform bounds
K,M>0
such that for all subsets
S
of indices, the following non-instantaneous property holds:

(19)

\sum_{j\in S,k\not\in S}\int_{\tau_{n}}^{\tau_{n+1}}{\mathfrak{u}}_{jk}(s)\,ds\leq K\sum_{j\in S,k\not\in S}\int_{\tau_{n}}^{\tau_{n+1}}{\mathfrak{u}}_{kj}(s)\,ds\leq M.

As described by the authors, this is a reciprocity condition: the outward connections from
S
are proportional to the inward ones, over subsequent time intervals. The property is not instantaneous since connections are measured as time integrals.

We now highlight the main difference between the hypotheses of our result and the cut-balance conditions, that is the already highlighted reciprocity condition. In our reasoning, there is no comparison between inward and outward connections. Rather on the opposite, the main connectivity hypothesis is a tree-like property. We will show this aspect with the following example.

Example 28
.

Take a system of four agents
(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})
that interact as follows:

(1)

{\mathfrak{u}}_{12}={\mathfrak{u}}_{21}={\mathfrak{u}}_{34}={\mathfrak{u}}_{43}={\mathfrak{u}}_{23}=1
;

(2)

{\mathfrak{u}}_{32}
bounded and nonnegative, to be chosen later;

(3)

all other
{\mathfrak{u}}_{jk}
are zero.

It is clear that the cut-balance condition (
19
) is satisfied for some choices of
{\mathfrak{u}}_{32}
only. Indeed, by choosing
S=\{1,2\}
one has that the condition reads as

\int_{\tau_{n}}^{\tau_{n+1}}{\mathfrak{u}}_{23}(t)\,dt=\tau_{n+1}-\tau_{n}\leq K\int_{\tau_{n}}^{\tau_{n+1}}{\mathfrak{u}}_{32}(t)\,dt.

This is not satisfied e.g. for any function that satisfies
\lim_{t\to+\infty}{\mathfrak{u}}_{32}(t)=0
.

In contrast, we see that for any choice of sequence
t_{n}\to+\infty
, all interaction functions converge to their natural limit (with constant value 1 or 0), except for
{\mathfrak{u}}_{32}
. Here, the key observation is that, due to the Banach-Alaoglu theorem, there exists a subsequence, that we do not relabel,
t_{n}\to+\infty
such that
{\mathfrak{u}}_{32}(t_{n}+t)
converges to some limit
{\mathfrak{u}}_{32}^{*}(t)
. The fact that this limit satisfies (
2
) or not plays no role in the hypotheses of Theorem
4
: in fact, the graph
G=G(\{t_{n}\},\{{\mathfrak{u}}_{jk}\})=G(\{{\mathfrak{u}}_{jk}^{*}\})
already contains arrows
\{12,21,23,34,43\}
and admits a globally reachable node
\ell^{*}
equal to
3
or
4
. Thus, the system converges to consensus for any choice of the initial data and any choice of the interaction function
{\mathfrak{u}}_{32}(t)
.

The example above shows that, in some cases, our theorems provide convergence in cases in which the cut-balance condition is not satisfied. More interestingly, it shows that our theorems can be applied by studying a subset of pairs of indices only, in the spirit of Remark
6
: indeed, assume that, if for a choice
t_{n}\to+\infty
, one can prove convergence of
{\mathfrak{u}}_{jk}(t+t_{n})
to some
{\mathfrak{u}}_{jk}^{*}(t)
satisfying (
2
) just for some pairs
i\to j
and the corresponding directed graph
{}G(\{t_{n}\},\{{\mathfrak{u}}_{jk}\})
admits a globally reachable node
\ell^{*}
. In this case, the convergence of the
{\mathfrak{u}}_{jk}(t+t_{n})
to some
{\mathfrak{u}}_{jk}^{*}(t)
for the remaining pairs of indices is ensured, by passing to a subsequence. The actual value of such remaining
{\mathfrak{u}}_{jk}^{*}(t)
plays no role, since it amounts to add connections to
{}G(\{t_{n}\},\{{\mathfrak{u}}_{jk}\})
, that already has a globally reachable node for sure.

There is one more difference between the hypotheses of our result and the cut-balance conditions: this is about the time intervals in which hypotheses need to be proven. In our Corollary
5
, property
(C)
needs to be verified on time intervals of the form
[t_{n},t_{n}+T]
for a given sequence
t_{n}\to+\infty
. In the cut-balance hypothesis, one instead needs to split the whole time interval
[0,+\infty)
into intervals of the form
[\tau_{n},\tau_{n+1}]
and verify the condition for all times.

We finally observe that our results are somehow transversal with respect to the cut-balance condition. Indeed, there are cases in which our results do not apply, while the cut-balance condition is satisfied and it ensures convergence. We show here a simple example.

Example 29
.

Consider a system of three agents
(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
with these connections:

\bullet\ {\mathfrak{u}}_{12}(t)={\mathfrak{u}}_{21}(t)=1\qquad\bullet\ {\mathfrak{u}}_{23}(t)={\mathfrak{u}}_{32}(t)=(t+1)^{-1}\qquad\bullet\ {\mathfrak{u}}_{13}(t)={\mathfrak{u}}_{31}(t)=0\,.

It is clear that the controls converge to
{\mathfrak{u}}_{12}^{*}={\mathfrak{u}}_{21}^{*}\equiv 1
and
{\mathfrak{u}}_{23}^{*}={\mathfrak{u}}_{32}^{*}={\mathfrak{u}}_{13}^{*}={\mathfrak{u}}_{31}^{*}=0
, thus for any choice of
t_{n}\to+\infty
the graph
{}G(\{t_{n}\},\{{\mathfrak{u}}_{jk}\})
constructed in Theorem
4
contains arrows
\{12,21\}
only. Thus, our result does not ensure convergence.

Instead, one can prove that the system satisfies the cut-balance property (
28
) with
K=1
, since it is symmetric. Then, the system converges to consensus.

Remark 30
.

The example above raises an open question: by a time rescaling, one can easily transform controls
{\mathfrak{u}}_{23},{\mathfrak{u}}_{32}(t)
to constant positive functions, that in turn have a natural limit satisfying property (
2
). This comes with the price of letting controls
{\mathfrak{u}}_{12},{\mathfrak{u}}_{21}
explode, then bringing the system outside the hypotheses of Theorem
4
. Yet, one may read the example above as a double time-scale dynamics: while agents 1,2 have a fast interaction, agents 2,3 have a slow one. Virtually, one may say that agents 1-2 first reach consensus, then the double agent 1-2 and the single agent 3 reach consensus. We aim to address this question in a future research.

The example above also highlights that results about consensus can be achieved by a time rescaling. Our statements can then be slightly generalized as follows.

Corollary 31
.

For
j,k=1,\dots,N
, let
{\mathfrak{u}}_{jk}
be that are measurable for all continuous probability measures, i.e. “universally measurable”.
Let
\rho:\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
be increasing, absolutely continuous, and diverging at
+\infty
.
If
\left\lVert{\mathfrak{u}}_{jk}(\rho(t),x)\dot{\rho}(t)\right\rVert_{\infty}
is finite and if Theorem
4
, or Theorem
7
, holds for the connection functions
{{\mathfrak{u}}_{jk}^{-}}(t)=\inf_{x}{\mathfrak{u}}_{jk}(\rho(t),x)\dot{\rho}(t)
, then any global trajectory of (
4
) converges to consensus.

Our results raise new questions about their integration with other available criteria (e.g. cut-balance) and their extension to dynamics with different time-scales (e.g. fast and slow variables).

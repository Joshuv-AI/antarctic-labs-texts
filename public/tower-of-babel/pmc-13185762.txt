Primary intramuscular hydatid cyst of the leg mimicking a soft tissue tumor: An illustrated case report
Authors: Fentahun Melese Meneye, Ermias Gizaw Hailemeskel, Fentahun Bantigegn Seyoum, Bizuayehu Amanu Aynalem, Samrawit Tariku Belay, Fekadu Belay Ayalew, Tadele Demilew Chekol

Hydatid disease caused by Echinococcus granulosus rarely involves skeletal muscle due to its unfavorable biochemical and mechanical environment. We report a rare case of primary intramuscular hydatid cyst of the leg mimicking a soft tissue tumor.

A 28-year-old male livestock trader presented with a gradually enlarging swelling over the anterolateral proximal left leg, associated with mild pain and paresthesia over the dorsum of the foot. He reported close contact with livestock and dogs, with no history of trauma or systemic illness.

Examination revealed a 6 × 3 cm firm-to-cystic, deep-seated, non-tender mass with a positive Tinel’s sign, suggesting superficial peroneal nerve impingement (Fig. 1). Laboratory investigations were normal, with no eosinophilia. Serology was not performed due to limited sensitivity in extrahepatic hydatid disease.

Clinical image showing a 6 × 3 cm mass over the anterolateral aspect of the left leg with planned incision marking.

Ultrasound showed a cystic lesion with internal septations (Fig. 2). MRI demonstrated a well-defined multiloculated cystic lesion with daughter cysts, highly suggestive of hydatid disease (Fig. 3). Chest radiography and abdominal ultrasonography excluded hepatic and pulmonary involvement.

Ultrasound showing a cystic lesion with internal septations.

MRI demonstrating a well-defined multiloculated cystic lesion with internal septations and daughter cysts.

The lesion was excised en bloc under spinal anesthesia with tourniquet control, with meticulous care to prevent cyst rupture and spillage (Fig. 4). Histopathology confirmed the diagnosis, showing a laminated acellular membrane.

(A) En bloc excision of the cyst with surrounding tissue. (B) Gross specimen demonstrating cystic structure.

Postoperatively, albendazole (400 mg twice daily) was administered for 3 months. The patient had an uneventful recovery with no recurrence at 3-month follow-up.

Primary intramuscular hydatid disease is rare, accounting for < 1% of cases, likely due to continuous contractile activity and an unfavorable metabolic environment of skeletal muscle [1]. It may mimic soft tissue tumors, leading to diagnostic delay. MRI is the imaging modality of choice; demonstration of daughter cysts is highly suggestive [2]. Preoperative biopsy is contraindicated due to risk of rupture and anaphylaxis. Complete surgical excision combined with albendazole therapy remains the definitive treatment [3], [4].

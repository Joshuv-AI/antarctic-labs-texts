Explicit bounds on common projective torsion points of elliptic curves
Christian Böhning, Hans-Christian Graf von Bothmer, David Hubbard

Abstract
. Suppose E 1 , E 2 E_{1},E_{2} are elliptic curves (over the complex numbers) together with double coverings π i : E i → ℙ 1 \pi_{i}\colon E_{i}\to\mathbb{P}^{1} ramified in the two-torsion points of E i E_{i} . Let E i ​ [ ∞ ] E_{i}[\infty] be the torsion points on E i E_{i} . In [ BFT18 ] , Bogomolov, Fu and Tschinkel ask if the number of points in π 1 ​ ( E 1 ​ [ ∞ ] ) ∩ π 2 ​ ( E 2 ​ [ ∞ ] ) \pi_{1}(E_{1}[\infty])\cap\pi_{2}(E_{2}[\infty]) is uniformly bounded in the case when the branch loci of the π i \pi_{i} do not coincide. Very recently this was answered affirmatively [ DKY20 , Kueh21 , Gao21 , DGH21 , GGK21 ] and also [ Poi22-1 , Poi22-2 ] , but realistic effective bounds are unknown. In this article we obtain such bounds for common projective torsion points on elliptic curves under some mild extra assumptions on the reduction type of the input data at given primes. The method is based on Raynaud’s original groundbreaking work on the Manin-Mumford conjecture [ Ray83-1 , Ray83-2 ] . In particular, we generalise several of his results to cases of bad reduction using techniques from logarithmic algebraic geometry.



1.
Introduction and basic setup

The following question in the theory of unlikely intersections, which was raised in
[
BFT18
]
and is closely related to the uniform Manin-Mumford conjecture, has recently attracted a lot of attention: suppose
E_{i}
,
i=1,2
are elliptic curves over the complex numbers (=one-dimensional abelian varieties), together with
standard projections
to
\mathbb{P}^{1}
,
\pi_{i}\colon E_{i}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
. Here and in the following by a standard projection we will mean a degree
2
morphism
\pi_{i}\colon E_{i}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
that identifies each point on
E_{i}
with its inverse, hence is ramified in the four
2
-torsion points
E_{i}[2]
of
E_{i}
. Suppose furthermore that the branch points in
\mathbb{P}^{1}
of these two double covers do not coincide as subsets of points in
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. Write
E_{i}[\infty]
for the torsion points on
E_{i}
(of arbitrary order). What is the smallest
C
such that under these hypotheses one can conclude

\left|\pi_{1}(E_{1}[\infty])\cap\pi_{2}(E_{2}[\infty])\right|\leq C\quad?

It is not too difficult to deduce that for any given
(E_{i},\pi_{i})
, the set
\pi_{1}(E_{1}[\infty])\cap\pi_{2}(E_{2}[\infty])
is finite. In fact, this already follows from Raynaud’s result
[
Ray83-1
]
that the torsion points of a complex abelian variety
A
that lie on some curve
C\subset A
that is not elliptic are finite in number. Indeed, consider the four to one covering

\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2}\colon E_{1}\times E_{2}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}

and the preimage of the diagonal

X=(\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2})^{-1}(\Delta).

Then it is easy to see that under the assumption that the sets of branch points of
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}
do not coincide, this curve is irreducible and not elliptic.

Recently, several authors
[
DKY20
,
Kueh21
,
Gao21
,
DGH21
,
GGK21
]
finally managed to show, as a corollary of their work, that one can choose one constant
C
that works for all pairs
(E_{i},\pi_{i})
above at once (i.e.,
uniformity
holds). Poineau in
[
Poi22-1
,
Poi22-2
]
also proved this using a different technique using Berkovich spaces over the integers and dynamics of Lattès maps.

To the best of our knowledge, these approaches have so far failed to determine the minimal possible
C
above and not yielded
effective realistic
bounds. However, one knows pairs
(E_{i},\pi_{i})
where
|\pi_{1}(E_{1}[\infty])\cap\pi_{2}(E_{2}[\infty])|
is comparatively large
[
BF17
,
FS19
]
. The current record (in
[
FS19
]
), as far as we are aware, is
34
.

In this work, we propose to obtain such effective realistic bounds for the common torsion points
\pi_{1}(E_{1}[\infty])\cap\pi_{2}(E_{2}[\infty])
, or some large subset of this set, under some mild extra assumptions on the curves
E_{i}
, taking our point of departure from the methods used by Raynaud’s in
[
Ray83-1
]
,
[
Ray83-2
]
.

In particular, we generalise several arguments by Raynaud to the log smooth setting.

The road map of the paper is as follows: in Section
2
we obtain explicit bounds on common projective torsion points of order coprime to
p
for two elliptic curves together with standard projections that have good reduction at a given place of some number field lying over a given prime
p
. This is the content of Theorem
2.5
.

We refine these bounds in Section
3
in Proposition
3.2
and Proposition
3.3
.

In Section
4
we show how one can obtain explicit bounds on common projective torsion points for curves with good reduction at two given primes.

In Section
5
, we generalise the preceding results to the case when one or two of the elliptic curves are allowed to have bad multiplicative reduction at a given place. This is done in Theorem
5.9
. This is valid under Assumption
5.2
. Part b) of that Assumption is less geometric, but we expect it to be implied by part a). In fact, we show that this is true in special cases in Theorem
7.1
. The proof is longer and occupies the remaining sections of the paper. It involves ideas from logarithmic algebraic geometry and generalises an argument in
[
Ray83-2
]
.

Remark 1.1
.

To obtain effective bounds of the type mentioned above, it is no essential restriction to assume that both

and
E_{2}

that is a
p
-th power of a uniformiser
\varpi
in
K
:
q=\varpi^{p}
. We will now use that assumption to prove

Proposition 9.2
.

If the image of
\Lambda\cap(\Gamma_{\mathscr{B},R}^{\circ})^{(1)}(R_{1})
in
\Gamma_{\mathscr{B},k}^{\circ}(k)
is infinite, then this image is infinite in every irreducible component of
\Gamma_{\mathscr{B},k}
.

Proof.

This follows from the rotational symmetry of the situation, more precisely: choose a
p
-torsion point
t
of
E_{1}(\overline{K})
such that
(t,\mathrm{id}_{E_{2}})
defines an
R
-valued point
x_{t}
of
\mathscr{A}^{\circ}
intersecting the central fibre
\mathscr{A}^{\circ}_{k}
in a point not lying on the identity component of
\mathscr{A}^{\circ}_{k}
. Such
t
exist, for example, the torsion point
t
corresponding to
\varpi\in K^{*}
under the Tate uniformisation.
Also
x_{t}
induces an
R_{1}
-valued point of
(\mathscr{A}^{\circ})^{(1)}
which we denote by the same symbol.

Suppose now given an
R_{1}
-valued point
y
in
\Lambda\cap(\Gamma_{\mathscr{B},R}^{\circ})^{(1)}(R_{1})
that specialises to a point on a certain component of
\Gamma_{\mathscr{B},k}^{\circ}(k)
. Adding the
R_{1}
-valued point
\alpha^{(1)}(x_{t})
to

multiple times for the structure of
\mathscr{B}^{\circ}
as an

-group scheme, we obtain from

\Lambda\cap(\Gamma_{\mathscr{B},R}^{\circ})^{(1)}(R_{1})
specialising into points on all the other components.
∎

10.
Log deformation theory and Frobenius liftings

We start by noticing that
\pi_{\mathscr{A}}\colon\mathscr{A}\to\mathrm{Spec}\,R
becomes log smooth if we endow
\mathscr{A}
with the divisorial log structure determined by the central fibre
\mathscr{A}_{k}\subset\mathscr{A}
and
\mathrm{Spec}\,R
with the divisorial log structure given by its closed point
[
Kato96
, Thm. 4.1]
or
[
Ogus18
, IV., Thm. 3.1.18]
. We denote the resulting morphism of log schemes

\pi_{\mathscr{A}}^{\dagger}\colon\mathscr{A}^{\dagger}\to(\mathrm{Spec}\,R)^{\dagger},

and will adhere to the same practice of denoting log schemes by an added dagger in other instances below.

In fact,
\pi_{\mathscr{B}}\colon\mathscr{B}\to\mathrm{Spec}\,R
and
\pi_{\mathscr{C}}\colon\mathscr{C}\to\mathrm{Spec}\,R
also become log smooth over
(\mathrm{Spec}\,R)^{\dagger}
if we endow the total spaces with the divisorial log structures determined by the central fibres, and
\alpha
,
\beta
naturally determine morphisms of log schemes, which we denote
\alpha^{\dagger}
,
\beta^{\dagger}
; indeed, it suffices to check this étale locally around singular points of the central fibres where these fibrations are given by

(xy-\pi^{p}=0)\subset\mathbb{A}_{R}^{2}

(where we denote a uniformiser of
R
by
\pi
). By
[
Gross11
, Ex. 3.27, 3.28]
, the log morphism down to
(\mathrm{Spec}\,R)^{\dagger}
can be described, using charts, by the diagram

\textstyle{S_{p}=\mathbb{N}^{2}\oplus_{\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{N}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{R[x,y]/(xy-\pi^{p})}
\textstyle{\mathbb{N}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{R\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

where
S_{p}
is the submonoid of
\mathbb{N}^{2}\oplus\mathbb{N}
generated by

\alpha_{1}=((1,0),0),\alpha_{2}=((0,1),0),\varrho=((0,0),1)

with one relation
\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}=p\varrho
, and denoting by
1
the standard generator of
\mathbb{N}
(the copy in the left hand lower corner in the diagram), the maps are given as follows:

a)

\mathbb{N}\to S_{p}
maps
1\mapsto\varrho
;

b)

S_{p}\to R[x,y]/(xy-\pi^{p})
sends
\alpha_{1}\mapsto x,\alpha_{2}\mapsto y,\varrho\mapsto\pi
;

c)

\mathbb{N}\mapsto R
satisfies
1\mapsto\pi,0\mapsto 1
;

d)

R\to R[x,y]/(xy-\pi^{p})
is the natural inclusion.

Thus the toroidal characterisation of log smoothness
[
Kato96
, Thm. 4.1]
applies.

Restricting the log structure from
\pi_{\mathscr{A}}^{\dagger}
to the subscheme
\overline{\Gamma}_{\mathscr{A},k}
, we obtain a log scheme
\overline{\Gamma}_{\mathscr{A},k}^{\dagger}
log smooth over the standard log point
(\mathrm{Spec}\,k)^{\dagger}
, which one checks étale locally as before.

Using
[
Gross11
, Prop. 3.40, 3.28]
, we can lift
\overline{\Gamma}_{\mathscr{A},k}^{\dagger}
to a log smooth curve
\mathscr{Z}^{\dagger}\to(\mathrm{Spec}\,R)^{\dagger}
. Note that
\Gamma_{\mathscr{B},R}\to\mathrm{Spec}\,R
also becomes log-smooth if we endow total space and base with the divisorial log structures determined by the central fibre and marked point, yielding
\Gamma_{\mathscr{B},R}^{\dagger}\to(\mathrm{Spec}\,R)^{\dagger}
.

Our goal now is to show that under the assumptions of Proposition
9.2
, the morphism
\alpha
induces a first order infinitesimal lifting of the relative Frobenius

\textstyle{(\mathscr{Z}^{(1)})^{\dagger}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\Phi}
\textstyle{(\Gamma_{\mathscr{B},R}^{(1)})^{\dagger}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{(\mathrm{Spec}\,R_{1})^{\dagger}}

which is a morphism of log schemes that are log smooth over
(\mathrm{Spec}\,R_{1})^{\dagger}
. This will yield a contradiction as in
[
Ray83-2
, Lemma I.5.4]
, using a log version of the Cartier operator and log differential forms. Then by Proposition
9.2
and Lemma
9.1
we conclude that the conclusion of Theorem
7.1
holds.

To start we have

Lemma 10.1
.

There exists a canonical morphism of log schemes

\textstyle{(\mathscr{Z}^{(1)})^{\dagger}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\varphi}
\textstyle{(\mathscr{B}^{(1)})^{\dagger}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{(\mathrm{Spec}\,R_{1})^{\dagger}}

that lifts
\alpha_{k}^{\dagger}
on
\overline{\Gamma}_{\mathscr{A},k}^{\dagger}
and satisfies
\varphi((\mathscr{Z}^{\circ})^{(1)}(R_{1}))=\Lambda
.

Proof.

We wish to mimic
[
Ray83-2
, Lemma I.5.2]
in the present log setting. Since
(\mathscr{A}^{(1)})^{\dagger}\to(\mathrm{Spec}\,R_{1})^{\dagger}
is log smooth, we can lift the inclusion of
\overline{\Gamma}_{\mathscr{A},k}^{\dagger}
into the central fibre étale locally, using the categorical characterisation, or rather definition, of log smoothness
[
Kato96
, Definition 3.7]
. Two different such lifts differ by a derivation
[
Ser06
, Section. 1.1]
, but since the differential of
\alpha_{k}\colon\mathscr{A}_{k}\to\mathscr{B}_{k}
is zero, we get a well-defined map to
\mathscr{B}^{(1)}
if we compose with
\alpha^{(1)}
. Since morphisms can be defined étale-locally on the source, these local lifts glue to a morphism
\varphi\colon(\mathscr{Z}^{(1)})^{\dagger}\to(\mathscr{B}^{(1)})^{\dagger}
. The property
\varphi((\mathscr{Z}^{\circ})^{(1)}(R_{1}))=\Lambda
is clear by construction.
∎

Lemma 10.2
.

Suppose that the the assumptions of Proposition
9.2
are satisfied, in particular, the image of
\Lambda\cap(\Gamma_{\mathscr{B},R}^{\circ})^{(1)}(R_{1})
in
\Gamma_{\mathscr{B},k}^{\circ}(k)
is infinite. Then the morphism
\alpha
induces a first order infinitesimal lifting of the relative Frobenius

\textstyle{(\mathscr{Z}^{(1)})^{\dagger}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\Phi}
\textstyle{(\Gamma_{\mathscr{B},R}^{(1)})^{\dagger}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{(\mathrm{Spec}\,R_{1})^{\dagger}}

which is a morphism of log schemes that are log smooth over
(\mathrm{Spec}\,R_{1})^{\dagger}
.

Proof.

This is the analogue in the log setting of
[
Ray83-2
, Lemma I.5.3]
. We wish to show that the morphism
\phi
of Lemma
10.1
factors through the closed subscheme
(\Gamma_{\mathscr{B},R}^{(1)})^{\dagger}
in
(\mathscr{B}^{(1)})^{\dagger}
. We denote by
\widetilde{\mathscr{Z}^{(1)}}
the closed subscheme of
\mathscr{Z}^{(1)}
that we obtain when we pull back
\Gamma_{\mathscr{B},R}^{(1)}
have via
\varphi
. We want to show that
\widetilde{\mathscr{Z}^{(1)}}=\mathscr{Z}^{(1)}
and for that it suffices to show that
\widetilde{\mathscr{Z}^{(1)}}
is schematically dense in
\mathscr{Z}^{(1)}
. Since we assume that the image of
\Lambda\cap(\Gamma_{\mathscr{B},R}^{\circ})^{(1)}(R_{1})
in
\Gamma_{\mathscr{B},k}^{\circ}(k)
is infinite, this image is infinite in every irreducible component of
\Gamma_{\mathscr{B},k}
by Proposition

. Therefore there is a set of sections in
\widetilde{\mathscr{Z}^{(1)}}(R_{1})
with Zariski dense image in every irreducible component of the special fibre of
\mathscr{Z}
, which is
\overline{\Gamma}_{\mathscr{A},k}
. Then
\widetilde{\mathscr{Z}^{(1)}}
is schematically dense in
\mathscr{Z}^{(1)}
by
[
EGAIV
, 11.10.9]
.
∎

We now want to show that there is no lifting of Frobenius as in Lemma
10.2
, showing the finiteness of the image of
p\Sigma\cap\left((\Gamma_{\mathscr{C},R}^{\circ})^{(1)}(R_{1})\right)
in
\Gamma_{\mathscr{C},k}^{\circ}(k)
under our assumptions.

Lemma 10.3
.

Suppose
C^{\dagger}\to(\mathrm{Spec}\,R_{1})^{\dagger}
and
D^{\dagger}\to(\mathrm{Spec}\,R_{1})^{\dagger}
are log smooth curves, and denote by
C_{0}^{\dagger}\to(\mathrm{Spec}\,k)^{\dagger}
and
D_{0}^{\dagger}\to(\mathrm{Spec}\,k)^{\dagger}
their central fibres, which are the base changes to the standard log point. Assume
D_{0}
is the Frobenius twist of
C_{0}
. Suppose there is a nonsingular component
D_{0}^{\prime}
of
D_{0}
on which

\omega_{D_{0}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\right)

has positive degree, where
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
are the double points of
D_{0}
or log marked points lying on
D_{0}^{\prime}
as in
[
Gross11
, Example 3.26 and Examples 3.36 (6)]
.
Suppose also that on the corresponding component
C_{0}^{\prime}
of
C_{0}
there is a matching number
y_{1},\dots,y_{n}
of double points or log marked points.
Then there is no
first order infinitesimal lifting of the relative Frobenius

\textstyle{C^{\dagger}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\Phi}
\textstyle{D^{\dagger}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{(\mathrm{Spec}\,R_{1})^{\dagger}}
.

Proof.

We argue similarly to
[
Ray83-2
, Lemma I.5.4]
. First, since
C^{\dagger}\to(\mathrm{Spec}\,R_{1})^{\dagger}
and
D^{\dagger}\to(\mathrm{Spec}\,R_{1})^{\dagger}
are log smooth curves, the sheaves of log differentials
\Omega^{1}_{C^{\dagger}/(\mathrm{Spec}\,R_{1})^{\dagger}}
and
\Omega^{1}_{D^{\dagger}/(\mathrm{Spec}\,R_{1})^{\dagger}}
are locally free of rank
1
, and in any event we have a natural functorial morphism of these lines bundles

\Phi^{*}\colon\Phi^{*}\Omega^{1}_{D^{\dagger}/(\mathrm{Spec}\,R_{1})^{\dagger}}\to\Omega^{1}_{C^{\dagger}/(\mathrm{Spec}\,R_{1})^{\dagger}},

[

. Since the differential of the restriction of

, is zero, this morphism of line bundles

or, what is the same thing by adjunction, a morphism

Now both of these maps are nonzero because away from the log marked or double points of

is nothing but

, which we assumed to have positive degree

\Phi^{*}_{0}\Omega^{1}_{D_{0}^{\dagger}/(\mathrm{Spec}\,k)^{\dagger}}
will have degree

(which is just a Frobenius twist of

∎

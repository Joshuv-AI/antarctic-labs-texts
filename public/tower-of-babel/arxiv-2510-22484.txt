Abstract
. In the context of (not necessarily minimal) actions, we consider the mean diameter and use it to characterize regular factor maps. Building on this characterization, we prove that an action is diam-mean equicontinuous if and only if it is a regular extension of its maximal equicontinuous factor. Furthermore, we establish the existence of a maximal diam-mean equicontinuous factor and discuss stability properties of regular factor maps. For this, we work in the context of actions of locally compact and σ \sigma -compact amenable groups. Keywords. Diam-mean equicontinuity, regular factor map, diam-mean proximality, amenable group, maximal diam-mean equicontinuous factor, mean diameter, topological group. 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification. Primary 37B05; Secondary 37B25, 37A05.



1.
Introduction

Equicontinuous actions are fundamental building blocks of the theory of topological dynamics.
To study more intricate systems, researchers often turn to weaker derivatives of equicontinuity, such as distality
[
Aus88
]
, mean equicontinuity
[
Fom51
,
FGL22
]
, and diam-mean equicontinuity
[
GR17
,
GRJY21
]
, among others.

A central technique in the field is to relate a given dynamical system to an equicontinuous one, typically via the maximal equicontinuous factor. A natural and important question is how dynamical properties of the original system are reflected in the structure of the corresponding factor map.

For instance, a landmark result due to Furstenberg states that a minimal action is distal if and only if the factor map to its maximal equicontinuous factor can be expressed as a (possibly transfinite) composition of equicontinuous factor maps
[
Aus88
]
.
Another elegant result concerns mean equicontinuity: an action is mean equicontinuous if and only if the factor map to its maximal equicontinuous factor is topo-isomorphic. See
[
DG16
,
FGL22
]
for further details.

In this article, we focus on diam-mean equicontinuous actions and their relation to regular factor maps
[
GR17
,
GRJY21
,
Hau25
,
HJL25
]
.
For this, we will work in the context of actions of amenable locally compact and
\sigma
-compact topological groups, which we abbreviate as
amenable
\operatorname{lc}_{\sigma}
groups
.
This is motivated by the natural appearance of actions of amenable
\operatorname{lc}_{\sigma}
groups, such as
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, in the context of aperiodic order
[
BG13
,
Sol20
]
and the study of regular Toeplitz actions for countable amenable (residually finite) groups
[
Dow05
,
BJL16
,
CP14
]
.

Our central object of study is the mean diameter.
To define it let
X
be an action of an amenable
\operatorname{lc}_{\sigma}
group
G
on a compact metric space and denote by
d
the metric of
X
. We fix a (left) Haar measure on
G
and denote it by
|\cdot|.

A sequence
(F_{n})
of compact subsets of
G
with
|F_{n}|>0
is called
left Følner
if for every non-empty compact
K\subseteq G
we have
|F_{n}\Delta KF_{n}|/|F_{n}|\to 0
.
For a left Følner sequence
\mathcal{F}=(F_{n})
and a closed subset
A\subseteq X
we define the
mean diameter of
A
w.r.t.
\mathcal{F}
as

\displaystyle\operatorname{Diam}_{\mathcal{F}}(A):=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{|F_{n}|}\int_{F_{n}}\operatorname{diam}(g.A)\,\mathrm{d}g,

where we abbreviate
g.A:=\{g.x;\,x\in A\}
and the
diameter
of
A
is given by

\operatorname{diam}(A):=\max_{x,x^{\prime}\in A}d(x,x^{\prime})
.
The
mean diameter
of
A
is defined by

\operatorname{Diam}(A):=\sup_{\mathcal{F}}\operatorname{Diam}_{\mathcal{F}}(A),

where the supremum is taken over all left Følner sequences
\mathcal{F}
of
G
.
In Section
4
we will present some basic properties of
\operatorname{Diam}
as well as various further approaches that allow to calculate
\operatorname{Diam}
, such as

\operatorname{Diam}(A)=\inf_{K}\sup_{h\in G}\frac{1}{|K|}\int_{Kh}\operatorname{diam}(g.A)\,\mathrm{d}g,

where the infimum is taken over all compact subsets
K
of
G
with
|K|>0
.

We will use the mean diameter to characterize regular factor maps.
For this we call a factor map
\pi\colon X\to Y

diam-mean proximal
if for all
y\in Y
we have
\operatorname{Diam}(\pi^{-1}(y))=0
.
Recall that a factor map
\pi\colon X\to Y
is called
regular
(also
almost surely one-to-one
) if the set of injectivity points
X_{0}
of
\pi
satisfies
\mu(X_{0})=1
for all invariant Borel probability measures on
X
.
See Section
5
for further details.
In Theorem
5.2
we establish the following characterization of regularity.

Theorem
.

A factor map is regular if and only if it is diam-mean proximal.

It is also natural to study the following notion.
For a left Følner sequence
\mathcal{F}
in
G
we call a factor map
\pi\colon X\to Y

\mathcal{F}
-diam-mean proximal
if for all
y\in Y
we have
\operatorname{Diam}_{\mathcal{F}}(\pi^{-1}(y))=0
.
Clearly any diam-mean proximal factor map is
\mathcal{F}
-diam-mean proximal.
In Theorem
5.2
we will see that also the converse holds and show the following.

Theorem
.

A factor map is diam-mean proximal if and only if it is
\mathcal{F}
-diam-mean proximal for some left Følner sequence
\mathcal{F}
in
G
.

Another important application of the mean diameter is the concept of diam-mean equicontinuity
[
GR17
,
GRJY21
]
.
An action is called
diam-mean equicontinuous
(also
Banach diam-mean equicontinuous

[
GRJY21
]
) if for all
\epsilon>0
and every
x\in X
, there exists a compact neighbourhood
A
of
x
with
\operatorname{Diam}(A)\leq\epsilon
.
We will see in Section
6
that this notion is independent of the choice of a metric on
X
and provide some basic characterizations.

Recall that diam-mean proximality and regularity are equivalent concepts for all factor maps, so it is to be expected that the concepts of diam-mean equicontinuity and regularity are strongly related.
The first result of this interplay was achieved in
[
GR17
, Theorem 54]
in the context of minimal almost automorphic subshifts
[
GR17
, Definition 50]
.
Generalizing the result of
[
GR17
]
it was presented in
[
GRJY21
, Theorem 4.12]
that whenever
X
is a minimal action of
\mathbb{Z}
, then
X
is diam-mean equicontinuous if and only if the factor map
\pi_{\operatorname{eq}}\colon X\to X_{\operatorname{eq}}
onto the maximal equicontinuous factor is regular.
This result was later generalized to minimal actions of more general acting groups, such as Abelian
\operatorname{lc}_{\sigma}
groups
[
Hau25
, Theorem 4.5]
.
Recent progress was made in
[
HJL25
, Theorem 5.2]
for the context of actions of countable amenable groups, where it was shown that a minimal action that satisfies the local Bronstein property
[
HJL25
, Definition 2.14]
is diam-mean equicontinuous if and only if
\pi_{\operatorname{eq}}
is regular.

Nevertheless, the question remained whether the characterization holds for all minimal actions of countable amenable groups.
Furthermore, it was asked in
[
GRJY21
, Question 2]
, whether this characterization also holds in the non-minimal context.
In Section
7
we will give an affirmative answer to both questions and show the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

An action of an amenable
\operatorname{lc}_{\sigma}
group on a compact metric space is diam-mean equicontinuous if and only if the factor map onto the maximal equicontinuous factor is regular.

Note that our proof significantly differs from
[
GRJY21
,
Hau25
,
HJL25
]
and does not involve the concept of frequent stability
[
GRJY21
, Section 3]
.

Motivated by Theorem
1.1
we will explore stability properties of regular factor maps in Section
8
.
This will allow us to prove that diam-mean equicontinuity is stable under (countable) products and to establish the following in Section
9
.

Theorem 1.2

(Maximal diam-mean equicontinuous factor)
.

Let
X
be an action of an amenable
\operatorname{lc}_{\sigma}
group
G
on a compact metric space.

There exists a maximal diam-mean equicontinuous factor of
X
, i.e. a factor map
\pi_{\operatorname{dm}}\colon X\to X_{\operatorname{dm}}
onto a diam-mean equicontinuous action
X_{\operatorname{dm}}
of
G
such that for any factor map
\pi\colon X\to Y
onto a diam-mean equicontinuous action of
G
there exists a factor map
\psi\colon X_{\operatorname{dm}}\to Y
such that
\pi=\psi\circ\pi_{\operatorname{dm}}
.
The maximal diam-mean equicontinuous factor of
X
is unique up to conjugacy.

Convention

Throughout this article,
G
denotes an amenable
\operatorname{lc}_{\sigma}
group, unless explicitly stated otherwise.
We fix a (left) Haar measure on
G
, denoted by
|\cdot|
.
For readability, we often omit explicit mention of the group
G
, and refer simply to an action, a factor map, or a Følner sequence. Unless stated otherwise, it is understood that all such objects refer to the same fixed group
G
.

2.
Preliminaries

We denote
\mathbb{R}^{+}:=[0,\infty)
.
If
X
is a topological space, we denote by
\mathfrak{F}_{1}(X)
and
\mathcal{H}(X)
the sets of all singletons and non-empty compact subsets, respectively.
Whenever we speak of
measurability
, we refer to the Borel
\sigma
-algebra.
We write
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}(G)
for the set of all compact subsets
K
of
G
with
|K|>0
.

2.1.
Hyperspaces

Let
(X,d)
be a compact metric space.
We write

\mathbf{B}_{\epsilon}^{d}(x):=\{x^{\prime}\in X;\,d(x,x^{\prime})\leq\epsilon\}

and

B_{\epsilon}^{d}(x):=\{x^{\prime}\in X;\,d(x,x^{\prime})<\epsilon\}

for
\epsilon>0
and
x\in X
.
For
A\in\mathcal{H}(X)
we denote
\operatorname{diam}_{d}(A)=\max_{x,x^{\prime}\in A}d(x,x^{\prime})
.
If the metric is understood from the context, we simply write
\mathbf{B}_{\epsilon}(x)
,
B_{\epsilon}(x)
and
\operatorname{diam}(A)
.
For
A\in\mathcal{H}(X)
and
\epsilon>0
we denote
\mathbf{B}_{\epsilon}(A):=\bigcup_{x\in A}\mathbf{B}_{\epsilon}(x)
.
The
Hausdorff metric
is given by

\displaystyle d_{\mathcal{H}}(A,A^{\prime}):=\min\{\epsilon>0;\,A\subseteq\mathbf{B}_{\epsilon}(A^{\prime})\text{~~~and~~~}A^{\prime}\subseteq\mathbf{B}_{\epsilon}(A)\},

for
A,A^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H}(X)
.
Note that, as a straightforward consequence of the compactness of
X
, the infimum in the above formula is attained.
It is well known that
d_{\mathcal{H}}
is a metric on
\mathcal{H}(X)
and that
\mathcal{H}(X)
equipped with the Hausdorff metric
d_{\mathcal{H}}
is a compact metric space.
We recommend
[
Nad92
]
for more details on hyperspaces and note that
\operatorname{diam}\colon\mathcal{H}(X)\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
is continuous.

2.2.
Amenable groups and Følner sequences

Let
G
be a locally compact and
\sigma
-compact group (
\operatorname{lc}_{\sigma}
group
) and choose a (left) Haar measure
|\cdot|
on
G
.
A sequence
(F_{n})
in
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}(G)
is called
left Følner
if

|F_{n}\Delta KF_{n}|/|F_{n}|\to 0

holds for all
K\in\mathcal{H}(G)
, where
\Delta
denotes the symmetric difference.
It is called
right Følner
whenever

|F_{n}\Delta F_{n}K|/|F_{n}|\to 0

holds for all
K\in\mathcal{H}(G)
.
A
\operatorname{lc}_{\sigma}
group that allows for a left Følner sequence is called
amenable
.

2.3.
Actions

Let
G
be a topological group and
X
be a compact metric space.
A
(continuous) action
of
G
on
X
is a group homomorphism
\alpha
from
G
into the group of homeomorphisms
X\to X
such that
G\times X\to X\colon(g,x)\mapsto\alpha(g)(x)
is continuous.
For
g\in G
and
x\in X
, we suppress the symbol for the action by simply writing
g.x:=\alpha(g)(x)
.
This allows us to simply speak of an
action
X
of
G
.
Whenever the group
G
is given by the context, we simply speak of an action
X
.
See
[
Aus88
]
for more details on actions.

For
x\in X
we denote
O(x):=\{g.x;g\in G\}
for the
orbit
of
x
.
A point
x\in X
is called
transitive
if it has a dense orbit.
An action is called
transitive
if it allows for a transitive point. It is called
minimal
if every point is transitive.

A subset
M\subseteq X
is called
invariant
if
M=g.M:=\{g.x;\,x\in M\}
holds for all
g\in G
.
For an invariant
A\in\mathcal{H}(X)
the action of
G
on
X
naturally induces an action of
G
on
A
. We speak of a
subaction (of
X
)
in this case.
Whenever
(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a countable family of actions, then we consider the action of
G
on
\prod_{n}X_{n}
given by
g.(x_{n})_{n}:=(g.x_{n})_{n}
for
g\in G
and
(x_{n})_{n}\in\prod_{n}X_{n}
.
Furthermore, whenever
X
is an action, then
(g,A)\mapsto g.A
induces an action of
G
on the hyperspace
\mathcal{H}(X)
.
We simply write
\prod_{n}X_{n}
and
\mathcal{H}(X)
for the respective actions.

2.4.
Factor maps

A continuous surjection
\pi\colon X\to Y
between actions is called a
factor map
(also
factor of
X
) if
\pi(g.x)=g.\pi(x)
holds for all
g\in G
and
x\in X
. A factor map is called a
conjugacy
if it is a homeomorphism.
Note that any factor map is
closed
, i.e. that
\pi(A)
is closed for all closed
A\subseteq X
.

An action
X
is called
equicontinuous
if there exists a continuous metric
d
on
X
such that
d(g.x,g.x^{\prime})=d(x,x^{\prime})
for all
x,x^{\prime}\in X
.
For any action
X
there exists a
maximal equicontinuous factor
of
X
, i.e. a factor map
\pi_{\operatorname{eq}}\colon X\to X_{\operatorname{eq}}
onto an equicontinuous action
X_{\operatorname{eq}}
such that for any factor map
\pi\colon X\to Y
onto an equicontinuous action there exists a factor map
\psi\colon X_{\operatorname{eq}}\to Y
with
\pi=\psi\circ\pi_{\operatorname{eq}}
. For details see
[
Aus88
]
.

2.5.
Invariant measures

We denote by
\mathcal{M}(X)
the set of all Borel probability measures on
X
.
Equipped with the weak*-topology
\mathcal{M}(X)
becomes a compact metric space
[
Gla03
,
Vil03
,
FGH25
]
.
For
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(X)
and a continuous surjection
\pi\colon X\to Y
we denote
\pi_{*}\mu
for the
pushforward measure
, given by

\pi_{*}\mu(M):=\mu(\pi^{-1}(M))
for all measurable
M\subseteq Y
.
Clearly, we have
\pi_{*}\mu\in\mathcal{M}(Y)
.

For
g\in G
and
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(X)
we denote
g_{*}\mu
for the pushforward under the homeomorphism induced by
g
.
A measure
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(X)
is called
invariant
if
g_{*}\mu=\mu
holds for all
g\in G
.
We denote by
\mathcal{M}_{G}(X)
the set of all invariant
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(X)
.
An action is called
uniquely ergodic
if it allows for a unique invariant probability measure.
An invariant measure
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(X)
is called
ergodic
if for each invariant measurable
M\subseteq X
we have that
\mu(M)\in\{0,1\}
.
Let
\pi\colon X\to Y
be a factor map.
Note that for
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{G}(X)
we have
\pi_{*}\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{G}(Y)
and that ergodicity is preserved under pushing forward.

2.6.
Actions of amenable
\operatorname{lc}_{\sigma}
groups

Let
X
be an action of an amenable
\operatorname{lc}_{\sigma}
group
G
.
For
x\in X
, we denote by
\delta_{x}
the Dirac measure at
x
.
For
K\in\mathcal{H}^{\prime}(G)
,
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(X)
and
M\subseteq X
measurable, we denote

(K_{*}\mu)(M):=\frac{1}{|K|}\int_{K}\mu(g.M)\,\mathrm{d}g.

Note that
K_{*}\mu\in\mathcal{M}(X)
.
For a left Følner sequence
\mathcal{F}=(F_{n})
in
G
and
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(X)
we say that
x
is
\mathcal{F}
-generic for
\mu
if
((F_{n})_{*}\delta_{x})_{n\in\mathbf{N}}
converges to
\mu
in
\mathcal{M}(X)
.
We say that
x
is
\mathcal{F}
-generic
if it is
\mathcal{F}
-generic for some
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(X)
.
Note that for
x\in X
any left Følner sequence
\mathcal{F}
allows for a subsequence
{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}
such that
x
is
{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}
-generic.
Furthermore, from
[
FGL22
, Theorem 2.4]
we know that for an ergodic measure
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{G}(X)
and any left Følner sequence
\mathcal{F}
there exists a subsequence
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
such that
\mu
-almost every point is
\mathcal{F}
-generic for
\mu
.

It follows from a standard Krylov-Bogolyubov argument that for a left Følner sequence
\mathcal{F}=(F_{n})
and
x\in X
any cluster point of
((F_{n})_{*}\delta_{x})
is invariant.
Thus, any point in a uniquely ergodic action is
\mathcal{F}
-generic w.r.t. all left Følner sequences
\mathcal{F}
.
Furthermore, from the sequential compactness of
\mathcal{M}(X)
it is straightforward to observe that
\mathcal{M}_{G}(X)\neq\emptyset
.
Another straightforward argument yields that for a factor map
\pi\colon X\to Y
and an ergodic
\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{G}(Y)
there exists
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{G}(X)
with
\pi_{*}\mu=\nu
.
These observations rely on the amenability of
G
.

2.7.
Subadditivity techniques

A bounded function
H\colon\mathcal{H}^{\prime}(G)\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
is

•

called
right invariant
,
whenever we have
H(Kg)=H(K)
for all
g\in G
and
K\in\mathcal{H}^{\prime}(G)
.

•

called
right measurable

whenever the mapping
G\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
with
g\mapsto H(Kg)
is measurable for all
K\in\mathcal{H}^{\prime}(G)
.

•

said to satisfy

S

whenever it is right measurable and for all
K,K^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H}^{\prime}(G)
, we have

\displaystyle H(K^{\prime})\leq\frac{1}{|K|}\int_{K^{-1}K^{\prime}}H(Kg)\,\mathrm{d}g.

(
\mathfrak{S}
)

Remark 2.1
.

Compact subsets of
G
have finite Haar measure.
Thus, the boundedness and the right measurability of
H
ensure the
integrability in

S

.

Remark 2.2
.

If
H
is right invariant, then it is right measurable and

S

reformulates as

H(K^{\prime})/|K^{-1}K^{\prime}|\leq{H(K)}/{|K|}

for all
K,K^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H}^{\prime}(G)
.

Remark 2.3
.

Whenever
G
is countable, Shearer’s inequality
[
DFR16
, Definition 2.1]
implies

S

. This implication follows from the arguments presented in the proof of
[
DFR16
, Proposition 3.3]
. However, since we are not aware of an appropriate formulation of Shearer’s inequality for amenable
\operatorname{lc}_{\sigma}
groups, we introduce the stronger property

S

, which is better suited to our setting.
Note that

S

serves as an intermediate condition between Shearer’s inequality and the so-called ’infimum rule’
[
DFR16
, Definition 3.1]
, which was implicitly employed in the proof of
[
DFR16
, Proposition 3.3]
.

Example 2.4
.

Let
f\colon G\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
be a bounded and measurable function.
The function

H\colon\mathcal{H}^{\prime}(G)\to\mathbb{R}^{+}

with

K\mapsto\int_{K}f

is bounded and satisfies

S

.

Proof.

Since compact subsets of
G
have finite Haar measure the function
H
is well-defined and bounded.
From the continuity of the multiplication
G\times G\to G
we know that
G\times G\ni(h,g)\mapsto f(hg)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
is measurable.
Using the Fubini-Tonelli theorem we observe that
H
is right measurable and compute the following for
K,K^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H}^{\prime}(G)
.

\displaystyle\int_{K^{-1}K^{\prime}}H(Kg)\,\mathrm{d}g

\displaystyle=\int_{K^{-1}K^{\prime}}\int_{K}f(hg)\,\mathrm{d}h\,\mathrm{d}g=\int_{K}\int_{K^{-1}K^{\prime}}f(hg)\,\mathrm{d}g\,\mathrm{d}h

\displaystyle\geq\int_{K}\int_{h^{-1}K^{\prime}}f(hg)\,\mathrm{d}g\,\mathrm{d}h=|K|\int_{K^{\prime}}f=|K|H(K^{\prime}).

This verifies that
H
satisfies

S

.
∎

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
H\colon\mathcal{H}^{\prime}(G)\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
be a bounded function that satisfies

S

.
Consider
\hat{H}\colon\mathcal{H}^{\prime}(G)\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
defined by
\hat{H}(K):=\sup_{g\in G}H(Kg)
.

(i)

\hat{H}
is right invariant, bounded and satisfies

S

.

(ii)

For any left Følner sequence
(F_{n})_{n}
we have that

{\hat{H}(F_{n})}/{|F_{n}|}
converges to
\inf_{K\in\mathcal{F}(G)}{\hat{H}(K)}/{|K|}
.

(iii)

The following maxima, taken over all Følner sequences
\mathcal{F}=(F_{n})
, are attained and satisfy

\displaystyle\max_{\mathcal{F}}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{H(F_{n})}{|F_{n}|}=\max_{\mathcal{F}}\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{H(F_{n})}{|F_{n}|}=\inf_{K\in\mathcal{F}(G)}\frac{\hat{H}(K)}{|K|}.

Remark 2.6
.

Whenever
H\colon\mathcal{H}^{\prime}(G)\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
is right invariant, then
H=\hat{H}
.

Remark 2.7
.

The argument for (ii) is essentially the argument presented in the proof of
[
DFR16
, Proposition 3.3]
in the context of countable amenable groups. We include it for the reader’s convenience.

Proof of Proposition
2.5
:

(i):
Clearly
\hat{H}
is bounded and right invariant.
In particular, it is right measurable.
To show that
\hat{H}
satisfies

S

consider
K,K^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H}^{\prime}(G)
.
For
g\in G
we explore that
H
satisfies

S

and observe

\displaystyle|K|H(K^{\prime}g)\leq\int_{K^{-1}K^{\prime}g}H(Kh)\,\mathrm{d}h=\int_{K^{-1}K^{\prime}}H(Khg)\,\mathrm{d}h\leq\int_{K^{-1}K^{\prime}}\hat{H}(Kh)\,\mathrm{d}h.

From the arbitrariness of
g
we conclude

|K|\hat{H}(K^{\prime})\leq\int_{K^{-1}K^{\prime}}\hat{H}(Kh)\,\mathrm{d}h.

(ii):
Clearly we have

\inf_{K\in\mathcal{H}^{\prime}(G)}\frac{\hat{H}(K)}{|K|}\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\hat{H}(F_{n})}{|F_{n}|}\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\hat{H}(F_{n})}{|F_{n}|}.

It thus suffices to show that for all
K\in\mathcal{H}^{\prime}(G)
we have

In order to relate diam-mean equicontinuity to regularity we need to relate the mean diameter of fibres to that of balls. For Banach proximal factor maps this will be achieved in the following lemmas.

Lemma 7.5
.

Let
X
be an action.
For
A\in\mathcal{H}(X)
,
K\in\mathcal{H}^{\prime}(G)
, and
\epsilon>0
there exists a finite non-empty subset

such that

K^{*}\operatorname{diam}(A)\leq K^{*}\operatorname{diam}(E)+\epsilon.

Proof.

For any
g\in G
, we choose
x_{g},x_{g}^{\prime}\in A
with
d(g.x_{g},g.x_{g}^{\prime})=\operatorname{diam}(g.A)
.
Recall that
\operatorname{diam}\colon\mathcal{H}(X)\to\mathbf{R}^{+}
is continuous.
Furthermore, note that

K\times X\to X

and

K\times\mathcal{H}(X)\to\mathcal{H}(X)
are uniformly continuous.
Thus, there exists a neighbourhood
V
of the identity element of
G
such that
for all
x\in X
,
A^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H}(X)
and
g,g^{\prime}\in K
with
g^{\prime}g^{-1}\in V
we have

d(g.x,g^{\prime}.x)\leq\epsilon/3
and
|\operatorname{diam}(g.A^{\prime})-\operatorname{diam}(g^{\prime}.A^{\prime})|\leq\epsilon/3
.
Choose a finite subset
F
of
K
with
K\subseteq VF
and denote

E:=\{x_{h},x_{h}^{\prime};\,h\in F\}
.
For
g\in K
there exists

with
gh^{-1}\in V
.
Such

satisfies

\operatorname{diam}(h.A)=d(h.x_{h},h.x_{h}^{\prime})=\operatorname{diam}(h.E)\leq\operatorname{diam}(g.E)+\tfrac{1}{3}\epsilon

and allows to compute

\displaystyle\operatorname{diam}(g.A)

\displaystyle=d(g.x_{g},g.x_{g}^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq d(g.x_{g},h.x_{g})+d(h.x_{g},h.x_{g}^{\prime})+d(h.x_{g}^{\prime},g.x_{g}^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{diam}(h.A)+\tfrac{2}{3}\epsilon\leq\operatorname{diam}(g.E)+\epsilon.

This shows that for all

\operatorname{diam}(g.A)\leq\operatorname{diam}(g.E)+\epsilon
. We thus conclude that

.
∎

Lemma 7.6

We thus observe

from a straightforward subadditivity argument.

Let
\pi\colon X\to Y
be a regular factor map between actions of
G
.
Let
\mathcal{F}=(F_{n})
be a left (or right) Følner sequence in
G
.
If
y
is a
\mathcal{F}
-generic and
\mathcal{F}
-diam-mean equicontinuous point of
Y
, then any
x\in\pi^{-1}(y)
is
\mathcal{F}
-diam-mean equicontinuous in
X
.

Proof.

Consider
x\in\pi^{-1}(y)
.
Furthermore, denote by
\nu
the measure for which
y
is
\mathcal{F}
-generic.
Recall that the notion of a diam-mean equicontinuous point does not depend on the choice of a continuous metric of the respective action. We thus assume w.l.o.g. that the metrics on
X
and
Y
satisfy
d(x_{1},x_{2})\geq d(\pi(x_{1}),\pi(x_{2}))
for all
x_{1},x_{2}\in X
.
Denote
X_{0}
for the set of injectivity points of
\pi
and
Y_{0}:=\pi(X_{0})
. Since
\pi
is regular, we observe that
\nu(Y_{0})=1
.

Let
\epsilon>0
.
For
\delta>0
we denote

G_{x}^{\delta}:=\{g\in G;\,\operatorname{diam}(g.\mathbf{B}_{\delta}(x))>\epsilon\}.

Considering the characterization presented in Proposition
6.8
it suffices to show that
there exists
\delta>0
with

\operatorname{ua-dens}_{\mathcal{F}}(G_{x}^{\delta})\leq\epsilon
.

Since any factor map is closed we know that for
y^{\prime}\in Y
there exists

\delta_{y^{\prime}}\in(0,\epsilon/2)
such that

\pi^{-1}(\mathbf{B}_{2\delta_{y^{\prime}}}(y^{\prime}))\subseteq\mathbf{B}_{\epsilon/2}(\pi^{-1}(y^{\prime}))
.
For details see
[
AB06
, Theorem 17.7(1)]
.
Exploring the regularity of the Borel measure
\nu
we choose a compact subset
A\subseteq Y_{0}
with
\nu(A)\geq 1-\epsilon/2
.
Furthermore, we choose a finite subset
E\subseteq A
satisfying

A\subseteq\bigcup_{y^{\prime}\in E}B_{\delta_{y^{\prime}}}(y^{\prime})=:O

and denote
\delta^{\prime}:=\min_{y^{\prime}\in E}\delta_{y^{\prime}}
.
Since
y
is assumed to be
\mathcal{F}
-diam-mean equicontinuous the characterization presented in Proposition
6.8
allows to observe that there exists
\delta>0
such that

G_{y}^{\delta}:=\{g\in G;\,\operatorname{diam}(g.\mathbf{B}_{\delta}(y))>\delta^{\prime}\}

satisfies
\operatorname{ua-dens}_{\mathcal{F}}(G_{y}^{\delta})\leq\delta^{\prime}
.

We will next show that, with this choice of
\delta
, we also have
\operatorname{ua-dens}_{\mathcal{F}}(G_{x}^{\delta})\leq\epsilon
. This will be achieved by considering

G^{\prime}:=\{g\in G;\,g.y\notin O\}

and showing (i)
\operatorname{ua-dens}_{\mathcal{F}}(G^{\prime}\cup G_{y}^{\delta})\leq\epsilon
and (ii)
G_{x}^{\delta}\subseteq G^{\prime}\cup G_{y}^{\delta}
.

(i):
Clearly
O=\bigcup_{y^{\prime}\in E}B_{\delta_{y^{\prime}}}(y^{\prime})
is open and satisfies
\nu(O)\geq\nu(A)\geq 1-\epsilon/2
.
Thus,
A^{\prime}:=Y\setminus O
is closed and satisfies
\nu(A^{\prime})\leq\epsilon/2
.
We thus observe from
(F_{n})_{*}\delta_{y}\to\nu
that

\displaystyle\operatorname{ua-dens}_{\mathcal{F}}(G^{\prime})=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{|F_{n}\cap G^{\prime}|}{|F_{n}|}=\limsup_{n\to\infty}(F_{n})_{*}\delta_{y}(A^{\prime})\leq\nu(A^{\prime})\leq\frac{\epsilon}{2}.

Since
\delta^{\prime}=\min_{y^{\prime}\in E}\delta_{y^{\prime}}\leq\epsilon/2
we conclude

\displaystyle\operatorname{ua-dens}_{\mathcal{F}}(G^{\prime}\cup G_{y}^{\delta})\leq\operatorname{ua-dens}_{\mathcal{F}}(G^{\prime})+\operatorname{ua-dens}_{\mathcal{F}}(G_{y}^{\delta})\leq\frac{\epsilon}{2}+\delta^{\prime}\leq\epsilon.

(ii):
Consider
g\in G_{x}^{\delta}
and assume that
g\notin G^{\prime}\cup G_{y}^{\delta}
.
From
g\notin G_{y}^{\delta}
we observe

\operatorname{diam}(g.\mathbf{B}_{\delta}(y))\leq\delta^{\prime}

and hence

g.\mathbf{B}_{\delta}(\pi(x))=g.\mathbf{B}_{\delta}(y)\subseteq\mathbf{B}_{\delta^{\prime}}(g.y)
.
Furthermore, since
g\notin G^{\prime}
we have
g.y\in O=\bigcup_{y^{\prime}\in E}B_{\delta_{y^{\prime}}}(y^{\prime})
.
Thus, there exists
y^{\prime}\in E
with
d(g.y,y^{\prime})<\delta_{y^{\prime}}
.
For such
y^{\prime}
we observe

\mathbf{B}_{\delta^{\prime}}(g.y)\subseteq\mathbf{B}_{\delta_{y^{\prime}}}(g.y)\subseteq\mathbf{B}_{2\delta_{y^{\prime}}}(y^{\prime})
.
Using our particular choice of the metrics on
X
and
Y
we compute

\pi(g.\mathbf{B}_{\delta}(x))=g.\pi(\mathbf{B}_{\delta}(x))\subseteq g.\mathbf{B}_{\delta}(\pi(x))\subseteq\mathbf{B}_{\delta^{\prime}}(g.y)\subseteq\mathbf{B}_{2\delta_{y^{\prime}}}(y^{\prime})

and hence the choice of
\delta_{y^{\prime}}
yields

\displaystyle g.\mathbf{B}_{\delta}(x)\subseteq\pi^{-1}(\pi(g.\mathbf{B}_{\delta}(x)))\subseteq\pi^{-1}(\mathbf{B}_{2\delta_{y^{\prime}}}(y^{\prime}))\subseteq\mathbf{B}_{\epsilon/2}(\pi^{-1}(y^{\prime})).

Since
y^{\prime}\in E\subseteq A\subseteq Y_{0}
, we observe that
\pi^{-1}(y^{\prime})
is a singleton and hence that
\operatorname{diam}(g.\mathbf{B}_{\delta}(x))\leq\epsilon
.
This shows
g\notin G_{x}^{\delta}
, a contradiction.
∎

We observe Theorem
1.1
from Proposition
7.9
and the following theorem.

Theorem 7.11
.

Let
\pi\colon X\to Y
be a regular factor map.

(i)

If
Y
is diam-mean equicontinuous, then
X
is diam-mean equicontinuous.

(ii)

If
Y
is equicontinuous, then
X
is diam-mean equicontinuous and
\pi
is the factor map onto the maximal equicontinuous factor of

.

Proof.

Since any equicontinuous action is diam-mean equicontinuous, we obtain (ii) from combining (i) with Proposition
7.3
.

To show (i) let
x\in X
and
\mathcal{F}
be a left Følner sequence.
From Proposition
7.4
we observe that
\pi(x)
is
\mathcal{F}
-generic. Since
\pi(y)
is assumed to be diam-mean equicontinuous Proposition
7.10
yields that
x
is
\mathcal{F}
-diam-mean equicontinuous. This shows that
x
is
\mathcal{F}
-diam-mean equicontinuous w.r.t. any left Følner sequence in

. From Proposition

∎

Corollary 7.12
.

Let
X
be an action and
\pi
be the factor map onto the maximal equicontinuous factor. Then

is diam-mean equicontinuous if and only if

Proof.

Observe that
X
is conjugated to the subaction
\mathfrak{F}_{1}(X)\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{\pi}(X)
and hence the diam-mean equicontinuity of

.
To show the converse assume that

is diam-mean equicontinuous.
From Proposition

we observe that

\pi\colon X\to X_{\operatorname{eq}}
is regular and it follows from Theorem

that
\pi_{\mathcal{H}}\colon\mathcal{H}_{\pi}(X)\to X_{\operatorname{eq}}

Since

.
∎

An action
X
is called
fully supported
if
X
equals its maximal support. The maximal support is always a closed invariant and non-empty subset. Thus, any minimal action is fully supported.
Whenever

is a diam-mean equicontinuous action that is not equicontinuous, then

is a proper subset of the diam-mean equicontinuous action
\mathcal{H}_{\pi}(X)
, where
\pi\colon X\to X_{\operatorname{eq}}
denotes the factor map onto the maximal equicontinuous factor of

. With the arguments above it follows that
\pi_{\mathcal{H}}\colon\mathcal{H}_{\pi}(X)\to X_{\operatorname{eq}}
is regular and Theorem

yields that the maximal support of
\mathcal{H}_{\pi}(X)
is contained in
\mathfrak{F}_{1}(X)
. We thus observe the following.

Remark 7.13
.

A diam-mean equicontinuous action does not need to be fully supported.

Proposition

also allows to say something about regular extensions of

-diam-mean equicontinuous actions.
An action

is called

is uniquely ergodic for any

. In particular, any uniquely ergodic action is pointwise uniquely ergodic.

Proposition 7.14
.

Let

be a regular factor map and consider a left Følner sequence

.
Assume that

is
\mathcal{F}
-diam-mean equicontinuous.

(i)

If

is fully supported, then
X
is
\mathcal{F}
-diam-mean equicontinuous.

(ii)

If

-diam-mean equicontinuous.

Proof.

(i):
Since

-mean equicontinuous and Proposition

yields that any point is

.

Remark 7.15
.

Recall that a factor map

is called
proximal
if all

with

satisfy

.
It is straightforward to observe that any action

for which the factor map

onto the maximal equicontinuous factor is proximal satisfies the

[

, Definition 2.14]
.

that all mean equicontinuous actions satisfy the local Bronstein property.

Now recall that any diam-mean equicontinuous action is mean equicontinuous and that any regular factor map is Banach proximal (Theorem

). Thus, this observation allows to apply

8.
Stability properties of regular factor maps

8.1.
Product stability

For a sequence
(\pi_{n}\colon X_{n}\to Y_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of factor maps
we consider the factor map
\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\pi_{n}\colon\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}X_{n}\to\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}Y_{n}

given by

(x_{n})_{n}\mapsto(\pi_{n}(x_{n}))_{n}
.
See
[
CH26
]
for further reading.

Proposition 8.1
.

Let
(\pi_{n}\colon X_{n}\to Y_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of factor maps.
The factor map
\pi:=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\pi_{n}
is regular if and only if
(\pi_{n})_{n}
is a sequence of regular factor maps.

Proof.

We denote
X:=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}X_{n}
and
Y:=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}Y_{n}
, and write
\pi^{(n)}
for the projection
X\to X_{n}
.

’
\Rightarrow
’:
Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
. We assume w.l.o.g. that the metrics on
X_{m}
and
X
satisfy

d(x_{1},x_{2})\geq d(\pi^{(m)}(x_{1}),\pi^{(m)}(x_{2}))
for all
x_{1},x_{2}\in X
.
This implies

\operatorname{Diam}(\pi^{(m)}(A))\leq\operatorname{Diam}(A)
for
A\in\mathcal{H}(X)
.

For
y\in Y_{m}
we choose a sequence
(y_{n})_{n}\in Y
with
y_{m}=y
. Observe that

\pi^{-1}((y_{n})_{n})=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\pi^{-1}_{n}(y_{n})
and hence

\pi^{(m)}(\pi^{-1}((y_{n})_{n}))=\pi_{m}^{-1}(y)
.
Since
\pi
is assumed to be regular, we know from Theorem
5.2
that

\displaystyle\operatorname{Diam}(\pi_{m}^{-1}(y))=\operatorname{Diam}(\pi^{(m)}(\pi^{-1}((y_{n})_{n})))\leq\operatorname{Diam}(\pi^{-1}((y_{n})_{n}))=0.

This shows
\operatorname{Diam}(\pi_{m}^{-1}(y))=0
for all
y\in Y_{m}
and we observe
\pi_{m}
to be regular from another application of Theorem
5.2
.

’
\Leftarrow
’:
For
n\in\mathbb{N}
we denote
X_{n,0}
for the set of injectivity points of
\pi_{n}
.
A moment’s thought reveals that
X_{0}:=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}X_{n,0}=\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty}(\pi^{(n)})^{-1}(X_{n,0})
is the set of injectivity points of
\pi
.

Let
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{G}(X)
and note that
\pi^{(n)}_{*}\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{G}(X_{n})
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
From the regularity of
\pi_{n}
we observe that we have

\mu\left(\left(\pi^{(n)}\right)^{-1}(X_{n,0})\right)=(\pi^{(n)}_{*}\mu)(X_{n,0})=1.

Thus,

\mu(X_{0})=\mu\left(\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\pi^{(n)}\right)^{-1}(X_{n,0})\right)=1.

∎

8.2.
(De-)composition stability

The following proposition shows that regularity is stable under composition and decomposition.

Proposition 8.2
.

Let
\phi\colon X\to Y
and
\psi\colon Y\to Z
be factor maps. Then
\pi:=\psi\circ\phi
is regular if and only if
\phi
and
\psi
are regular.

Proof.

’
\Rightarrow
’:
We assume w.l.o.g. that the metrics on
X
and
Y
satisfy

d(x_{1},x_{2})\geq d(\phi(x_{1}),\phi(x_{2}))
for all
x_{1},x_{2}\in X
.
For
y\in Y
and
z\in Z
we observe from Theorem
5.2
that we have

\operatorname{Diam}(\phi^{-1}(y))\leq\operatorname{Diam}(\pi^{-1}(\psi(y)))=0

and

\operatorname{Diam}(\psi^{-1}(z))=\operatorname{Diam}(\phi(\pi^{-1}(z)))\leq\operatorname{Diam}(\pi^{-1}(z))=0
.
Thus, Theorem
5.2
yields that
\phi
and
\psi
are regular.

’
\Leftarrow
’: Denote
X_{\pi}
,
X_{\phi}
and
Y_{\psi}
for the injectivity points of
\pi
,
\phi
and
\psi
, respectively.
It is a straightforward exercise to verify that

\phi^{-1}(Y_{\psi})\cap X_{\phi}\subseteq X_{\pi}
.
For
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{G}(X)
we have
\phi_{*}\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{G}(Y)
and from the regularity of
\psi
we observe
\mu(\phi^{-1}(Y_{\psi}))=\phi_{*}\mu(Y_{\psi})=1
.
Furthermore, the regularity of
\phi
yields
\mu(X_{\phi})=1
and we thus have

1=\mu(\phi^{-1}(Y_{\psi})\cap X_{\phi})\leq\mu(X_{\pi})\leq 1
, i.e.
\mu(X_{\pi})=1
.
∎

8.3.
\mathcal{H}
-(in)stability

Recall that a factor map

\pi\colon X\to Y

is called
almost one-to-one
if the set of injectivity points is dense in
X
.
In
[
CH26
]
it was presented that the properties discussed in the Propositions
8.1
and
8.2
also hold for almost one-to-one factor maps between actions (on compact metric spaces).

For a factor map
\pi\colon X\to Y
the image mapping
\mathcal{H}(X)\ni A\mapsto\pi(A)
establishes a factor map
\mathcal{H}(\pi)\colon\mathcal{H}(X)\to\mathcal{H}(Y)

[
CH26
]
.
It was also shown in
[
CH26
,
DX24
]
that a factor map
\pi
between actions (on compact metric spaces) is almost one-to-one if and only if
\mathcal{H}(\pi)
is almost one-to-one.
We next present that a similar statement does not hold in the context of regularity.

Example 8.3
.

Let
Y:=\mathbb{Z}\cup\{\infty\}
be the one-point compactification of
\mathbb{Z}
.
We act on
Y
with
G:=\mathbb{Z}
by fixing
\infty
and by defining
g.y:=g+y
for
g\in G
and
y\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Consider two further disjoint copies of

\hat{X}=\{\hat{n};\,n\in\mathbb{Z}\}\cup\{\hat{\infty}\}
and
\check{X}=\{\check{n};\,n\in\mathbb{Z}\}\cup\{\check{\infty}\}
of
Y

and denote
X^{\prime}:=\hat{X}\cup\check{X}
.
We obtain
X
as a quotient of
X^{\prime}
after the identification
\infty_{\hat{X}}=\infty_{\check{X}}
and denote
\infty
for the respective element.
Note that
X=\{\hat{n};\,n\in\mathbb{Z}\}\cup\{\check{n};\,n\in\mathbb{Z}\}\cup\{{\infty}\}
is uniquely ergodic and that the invariant Borel probability measure is
\delta_{\infty}
.
Thus, the factor map
\pi\colon X\to Y
given by
\infty\to\infty
and
\pi(\hat{n}):=\pi(\check{n}):=n
for
n\in\mathbb{Z}
is regular.

Consider the factor map
\mathcal{H}(\pi)\colon\mathcal{H}(X)\to\mathcal{H}(Y)
, as well as the elements
A:=\{\hat{n};\,n\in\mathbb{Z}\}\cup\{{\infty}\}
and
B:=\{\check{n};\,n\in\mathbb{Z}\}\cup\{{\infty}\}
of
\mathcal{H}(X)
.
Clearly we have
\mathcal{H}(\pi)(A)=Y=\mathcal{H}(\pi)(B)
and hence

\{A,B\}\subseteq\mathcal{H}(\pi)^{-1}(Y)
.
Nevertheless, the elements
A
and
B
are fixed in
\mathcal{H}(X)
.
We thus observe
0<d_{\mathcal{H}}(A,B)\leq\operatorname{Diam}(\mathcal{H}(\pi)^{-1}(Y))
.
From Theorem
5.2
we thus conclude that
\mathcal{H}(\pi)
is not regular.

Let
\pi\colon X\to Y
be a factor map and denote
X_{0}
and
\mathcal{H}(X)_{0}
for the sets of injectivity points of
\pi
and
\mathcal{H}(\pi)
, respectively.
A moment’s thought reveals that we have
\mathfrak{F}_{1}(X_{0})=\mathfrak{F}_{1}(X)\cap\mathcal{H}(X)_{0}
.
From this observation it is straightforward to observe the following.

Proposition 8.4
.

Let
\pi\colon X\to Y
be a factor map.
Whenever
\mathcal{H}(\pi)
is regular, then
\pi
is regular.

Remark 8.5
.

For an action
X
(of
G
) the mapping
(g,\mu)\mapsto g_{*}\mu
induces an action of
G
on
\mathcal{M}(X)
.
Furthermore, for factor map
\pi\colon X\to Y
the pushforward
\pi_{*}\colon\mathcal{M}(X)\to\mathcal{M}(Y)
is a factor map.
It was shown in
[
CH26
]
that a factor map
\pi
(between compact metric spaces) is almost one-to-one if and only if
\pi_{*}
is almost one-to-one.

Denote
X_{0}
and
\mathcal{M}(X)_{0}
for the respective sets of injectivity points of
\pi
and
\pi_{*}
.
Abbreviating
\delta(X^{\prime}):=\{\delta_{x};\,x\in X^{\prime}\}
for
X^{\prime}\subseteq X
,
a straightforward argument yields
\delta(X_{0})=\delta(X)\cap\mathcal{M}(X)_{0}
.
With a similar argument as above it follows that the regularity of
\pi_{*}
implies the regularity of
\pi
. It remains open whether the regularity of
\pi
is equivalent to the regularity of
\pi_{*}
.

9.
The maximal diam-mean equicontinuous factor

Next, we establish the existence of a maximal diam-mean equicontinuous factor.
We begin with the following observation.

Remark 9.1
.

Any subaction of a diam-mean equicontinuous action is diam-mean equicontinuous.
Furthermore, the canonical action (of
G
) on a singleton is diam-mean equicontinuous.

Diam-mean equicontinuity is stable under (countable) products.

Theorem 9.2
.

Let
(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a countable family of diam-mean equicontinuous actions. Then also the induced action on
X:=\prod_{n}X_{n}
is diam-mean equicontinuous.
Furthermore, if
Y_{n}
is the maximal equicontinuous factor of
X_{n}
, then
Y:=\prod_{n}Y_{n}
is the maximal equicontinuous factor of
X
.

Proof.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
denote
\pi_{n}\colon X_{n}\to Y_{n}
for the respective factor map onto the maximal equicontinuous factor of
X_{n}
.
Note that
Y
is equicontinuous
[
Aus88
, Chapter 2]
.
From Proposition
7.9
we observe that all
\pi_{n}
are regular.
Thus, Proposition
8.1
yields that the induced factor map
X\to Y
is also regular and we have established that
X
is a regular extension of an equicontinuous action. The statement now follows from Theorem
7.11
.
∎

It is well known that a property (P), which holds for the canonical singleton action and which is stable under products and subactions allows for the construction of a ’maximal factor of
X
that satisfies (P)’ for any action
X
.
For this, it is often referred to
[
Aus88
]
, where this technique is presented in the context of actions on compact Hausdorff spaces and product-stability means stability of (P) under arbitrary (possibly uncountable) products.
However, working with compact metric spaces one can only consider countable products and the question arises, whether the technique of
[
Aus88
]
can also be used while working only with actions on compact metric spaces.
We will present in the proposition below that it can be used and provide a full proof for the convenience of the reader. This allows to establish Theorem
1.2
.
For a precise terminology, we call two factor maps
\pi\colon X\to Y
,
\pi^{\prime}\colon X\to Y^{\prime}

conjugated
if there exists a conjugacy
\iota\colon Y\to Y^{\prime}
with
\iota\circ\pi=\pi^{\prime}
.

Proposition 9.3
.

Let
G
be a topological group.
Consider a property (P) that is defined for actions of
G
on compact metric spaces such that

(i)

Whenever an action satisfies (P), then any subaction satisfies (P),

(ii)

Whenever
(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a countable family of actions that satisfy (P), then
\prod_{n}X_{n}
satisfies (P), and

(iii)

The canonical singleton action of
G
satisfies (P).

If
X
is an action of
G
(on a compact metric space), then there exists a maximal factor of
X
that satisfies (P), i.e. a factor map
\pi_{P}\colon X\to X_{P}
that satisfies (P), such that for all factors
\pi\colon X\to Y
that satisfy (P) there exists a factor map
\psi\colon X_{P}\to Y
with
\pi=\psi\circ\pi_{P}
.
The maximal factor of
X
that satisfies (P) is unique up to conjugacy.

Remark 9.4
.

Consider properties (E) and (D) of actions such that (E) is stronger than (D), i.e. any action that satisfies (E) also satisfies (D).
Whenever (E) and (D) satisfy the properties (i), (ii) and (iii), then for an action
X
we can consider the maximal factor
\pi_{D}\colon X\to X_{D}
that satisfies (D).
It is easy to verify that the maximal factors
\pi\colon X\to X_{E}
and
\phi\colon X_{D}\to X_{E}^{\prime}
that satisfy (E) are conjugate, i.e. there exists a conjugacy
\iota\colon X_{E}^{\prime}\to X_{E}
such that

\pi=\iota\circ\phi\circ\pi_{D}
.
In particular, we observe that the maximal equicontinuous factor of an action
X
is the maximal equicontinuous factor of the maximal diam-mean equicontinuous factor of
X
.

Remark 9.5
.

The maximal equicontinuous factor and the maximal diam-mean equicontinuous factor differ in general. For example any regular Toeplitz subshift of
\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{Z}}
is a (non-trivial) regular extension of an odometer and hence diam-mean equicontinuous. Here the odometer is the maximal equicontinuous factor. See
[
Dow05
,
BJL16
,
CP14
]
for details and further reading.

Before presenting the proof, we briefly discuss some obstacles that arise in this context.

Example 9.6
.

It is a well-known folklore result that the class of all compact metric spaces, up to homeomorphism, is uncountable.
Let
X
be the Cantor space and
G
be any group. We act on
X
by fixing all elements. Since all compact metric spaces are continuous images of
X

[
Wil04
, Theorem 30.7]
we observe that the family of all factors (up to conjugacy) is uncountable.

Proof of Proposition
9.3
:

Note that the concepts of factor map, conjugacy, invariant subset and product given in Section
2
can easily be extended to actions of
G
on a compact Hausdorff space.
In particular, this allows to consider arbitrary products of actions. See
[
Aus88
]
for further reference in this direction.

Let
X
be an action of
G
on a compact metric space.
For simplicity, a factor of
X
is called a
P-factor
if it satisfies (P).
For any factor
\pi
of
X
we denote
R(\pi):=\{x,x^{\prime}\in X;\,\pi(x)=\pi(x^{\prime})\}
.
Note that two factors
\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}
of
X
are conjugated if and only if
R(\pi)=R(\pi^{\prime})
.
It follows that a family
(\pi_{i}\colon X\to Y_{i})_{i\in I}
of conjugacy-representatives of P-factors of
X
can be treated as a set. From (iii) we know that
I
is non-empty.
Consider the product action
Y_{I}:=\prod_{i\in I}Y_{i}
and the map

\pi_{I}\colon X\to Y_{I}

given by

\pi_{I}(x):=(\pi_{i}(x))_{i}
.
Clearly,
\pi_{I}
restricts to a factor map

\pi_{P}\colon X\to X_{P}:=\pi_{I}(X)
.
It is straightforward to verify that for any P-factor
\pi\colon X\to Y

there exists a factor

\psi\colon X_{P}\to Y

with

\pi=\psi\circ\pi_{P}
.
Furthermore, from
R(\pi_{P})=\bigcap_{i}R(\pi_{i})
it is straightforward to observe that
X_{P}
is conjugated to any other candidate for a maximal P-factor.
This establishes the uniqueness of the maximal P-factor of
X
up to conjugacy.

It remains to show that
X_{P}
satisfies (P). This does not immediately follow from (ii), since
I
is not necessarily countable.
However, since
X
is metrizable we observe that
X_{P}
is metrizable. Thus,
X_{P}
has a countable base
(B^{(n)})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
for its topology. Revising the definition of the product topology, we assume w.l.o.g. that
B^{(n)}
is of the form

B^{(n)}=X_{P}\cap(\prod_{i}U_{i}^{(n)})

with
U_{i}^{(n)}\subseteq Y_{i}
open and non-empty
and
U_{i}^{(n)}=Y_{i}
for all but finitely many
i
.

Let
N
be the set of all
i\in I
, for which there exists some
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
U_{i}^{(n)}\neq Y_{i}
and note that
N
is countable.
Denote
Y_{N}:=\prod_{i\in N}Y_{i}
and consider the projection

\pi_{I,N}\colon Y_{I}\to Y_{N}
.
Let
\iota
be the restriction
\iota\colon X_{P}\to\iota(X_{P})
of
\pi_{I,N}

and observe that
\iota
is a factor map.
Our choice of
N
implies that the topology of
X_{P}
is given by
\{\pi_{I,N}^{-1}(O);\,O\in\tau\}
, where
\tau
is the topology of
\iota(X_{P})
.

Since
\iota(X_{P})
is Hausdorff, we observe
\iota
to be injective and hence a conjugacy.
Furthermore, from (ii) we know that
Y_{N}
satisfies (P).
Thus,
\iota(X_{P})\subseteq Y_{N}
yields that also
\iota(X_{P})
and hence
X_{P}
satisfy (P).
∎

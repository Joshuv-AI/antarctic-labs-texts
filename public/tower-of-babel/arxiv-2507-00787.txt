Closed Estimates of Leray Projected Transport Noise and Strong Solutions of the Stochastic Euler Equations
Daniel Goodair

Abstract
We consider the incompressible Euler and Navier-Stokes equations on the three dimensional torus, in velocity form, perturbed by a transport or transport-stretching Stratonovich noise. Closed control of the noise contributions in energy estimates are demonstrated, for any positive integer ordered Sobolev Space and the equivalent Stokes Space; difficulty arises due to the presence of the Leray Projector disrupting cancellation of the top order derivative. This is particularly pertinent in the case of a transport noise without stretching, where the vorticity form cannot be used. As a consequence we obtain, for the first time, the existence of a local strong solution to the corresponding stochastic Euler equation. Furthermore, smooth solutions are shown to exist until blow-up in L 1 ​ ( [ 0 , T ] , W 1 , ∞ ) L^{1}\left([0,T];W^{1,\infty}\right) .

Introduction

We are concerned with the incompressible Euler and Navier-Stokes equations on the three dimensional torus, in velocity form, perturbed by a transport or transport-stretching Stratonovich noise. The stochastic Navier-Stokes equation reads as

u_{t}=u_{0}-\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{L}_{u_{s}}u_{s}\ ds+\nu\int_{0}^{t}\Delta u_{s}\,ds+\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{G}u_{s}\circ d\mathcal{W}_{s}-\nabla\rho_{t}

(1)

where
\mathcal{L}
denotes the usual nonlinear term,
\nu>0
the viscosity,
\rho
the pressure,
\mathcal{W}
is a Cylindrical Brownian Motion and
\mathcal{G}
represents a transport or transport-stretching noise. The relevant functional analytic and stochastic preliminaries are given in Subsections
1.1
and
1.2
. When
\nu
is formally set to zero we retrieve the stochastic Euler equation. Analysis of the equation (
2
) classically hinges upon two manipulations: projection onto the divergence-free and mean-zero subspace by the Leray Projector
\mathcal{P}
, and conversion from Stratonovich to Itô form. To eliminate the semi-martingale pressure before a conversion to Itô form, where the pressure would appear in the cross-variation, we first project the equation to arrive at

u_{t}=u_{0}-\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{P}\mathcal{L}_{u_{s}}u_{s}\ ds-\nu\int_{0}^{t}Au_{s}\,ds+\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{P}\mathcal{G}u_{s}\circ d\mathcal{W}_{s}

(2)

where
A=-\mathcal{P}\Delta
is the Stokes Operator. The mapping
\mathcal{G}
is defined along the components of
\mathcal{W}
through a collection of smooth vector fields
(\xi_{i})
, where here the
i^{\textnormal{th}}
component of
\mathcal{G}

\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}
defined on a vector field

\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}f=(\xi_{i}\cdot\nabla)f=\sum_{j=1}^{3}\xi_{i}^{j}\partial_{j}f

or the transport-stretching operator

\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left(\left\langle A^{\frac{k}{2}}(\mathcal{P}B_{i})^{2}u_{s},A^{\frac{k}{2}}u_{s}\right\rangle_{L^{2}}+\left\|A^{\frac{k}{2}}\mathcal{P}B_{i}u_{s}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)\leq c_{\nu}\left\|A^{\frac{k}{2}}u_{s}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{\nu}{2}\left\|A^{\frac{k+1}{2}}u_{s}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

1.1
Functional Analytic Preliminaries

We now recap the classical functional framework for the study of the deterministic Navier-Stokes and Euler Equations on the three dimensional Torus
\mathbbm{T}^{3}
. The nonlinear operator
\mathcal{L}
is defined for sufficiently regular functions
f,g:\mathbb{T}^{3}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3}
by
\mathcal{L}_{f}g:=\sum_{j=1}^{3}f^{j}\partial_{j}g.
Here and throughout the text we make no notational distinction between differential operators acting on a vector valued function or a scalar valued one; that is, we understand
\partial_{j}g
by its component mappings
(\partial_{j}g)^{l}:=\partial_{j}g^{l}

such that the property

\textnormal{div}f:=\sum_{j=1}^{3}\partial_{j}f^{j}=0

holds in the sense of weak derivatives. By the zero-average condition we mean a function
f
such that
\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}f(x)dx=0
. We introduce some new notations to incorporate the divergence-free and zero-average restrictions. These facts are all presented in [
60
] Sections 1 and 2. Recall that any function

\displaystyle f(x)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}}f_{k}e^{ik\cdot x}

(10)

whereby each

is such that
f_{k}=\overline{f_{-k}}
. Let us further introduce

the subset of

.
We define the Leray Projector

as the orthogonal projection in

. The Leray Projector commutes with derivatives (
though not true on a bounded domain!
). There exists a collection of functions

\left\langle f,g\right\rangle_{A^{\frac{m}{2}}}=\sum_{k_{1}=1}^{3}\dots\sum_{k_{\frac{m}{2}}=1}^{3}\sum_{l_{1}=1}^{3}\dots\sum_{l_{\frac{m}{2}}=1}^{3}\left\langle\partial_{k_{1}}^{2}\dots\partial_{k_{\frac{m}{2}}}^{2}f,\partial_{l_{1}}^{2}\dots\partial_{l_{\frac{m}{2}}}^{2}g\right\rangle.

(13)

Returning to the nonlinear term, we recall the critical duality that for
\phi\in W^{1,2}_{\sigma}
,
f,g\in W^{1,2}
,

\left\langle\mathcal{L}_{\phi}f,g\right\rangle=-\left\langle f,\mathcal{L}_{\phi}g\right\rangle

(14)

which further implies that

\left\langle\mathcal{L}_{\phi}f,f\right\rangle=0.

(15)

To estimate the nonlinear term in higher norms we recall [
34
] Lemma 2.1, asserting that for any
m\geq 3
there exists a constant
C
such that for all
f\in W^{m,2}_{\sigma}
,
g\in W^{m+1,2}_{\sigma}
,

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{\lvert\alpha\rvert\leq m}\left\langle D^{\alpha}\mathcal{P}\mathcal{L}_{f}g,D^{\alpha}g\right\rangle\right|\leq C\left(\left\|f\right\|_{W^{1,\infty}}\left\|g\right\|_{W^{m,2}}+\left\|f\right\|_{W^{m,2}}\left\|g\right\|_{W^{1,\infty}}\right)\left\|g\right\|_{W^{m,2}}.

(16)

We note that the result continues to hold for
m<3
, although it is only meaningful provided
f
and
g
have sufficient regularity for the right hand side to be finite. Here and throughout the text, for
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3})\in\left(\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}\right)^{3}
,
D^{\alpha}
represents the weak partial differential operator
D^{\alpha}=\partial_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\partial_{2}^{\alpha_{2}}\partial_{3}^{\alpha_{3}}
. We will consider a partial ordering on the three dimensional multi-indices by
\alpha\leq\beta
if and only if for all
l=1,2,3
we have that
\alpha_{l}\leq\beta_{l}
. We extend this to notation
<
by

\alpha<\beta
if and only if
\alpha\leq\beta
and for some
l=1,2,3
,
\alpha_{l}<\beta_{l}
. In general throughout the manuscript we shall use
c
as a generic constant changing from line to line, where dependence on any relevant quantities will be made explicit; in place of this we may also use the notation
f\lesssim g
to denote
f\leq cg
for some such
c
.

1.2
Stochastic Preliminaries

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},(\mathcal{F}_{t}),\mathbbm{P})
be a fixed filtered probability space satisfying the usual conditions of completeness and right continuity. We take
\mathcal{W}
to be a cylindrical Brownian motion over some Hilbert Space
\mathfrak{U}
with orthonormal basis
(e_{i})
. Recall (e.g. [
53
], Definition 3.2.36) that
\mathcal{W}
admits the representation
\mathcal{W}_{t}=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}e_{i}W^{i}_{t}
as a limit in
L^{2}(\Omega;\mathfrak{U}^{\prime})
whereby the
(W^{i})
are a collection of i.i.d. standard real valued Brownian Motions and
\mathfrak{U}^{\prime}
is an enlargement of the Hilbert Space
\mathfrak{U}
such that the embedding
J:\mathfrak{U}\rightarrow\mathfrak{U}^{\prime}
is Hilbert-Schmidt and
\mathcal{W}
is a
JJ^{*}-
cylindrical Brownian Motion over
\mathfrak{U}^{\prime}
. Given a process
F:[0,T]\times\Omega\rightarrow\mathscr{L}^{2}(\mathfrak{U};\mathscr{H})
progressively measurable and such that
F\in L^{2}\left(\Omega\times[0,T];\mathscr{L}^{2}(\mathfrak{U};\mathscr{H})\right)
, for any
0\leq t\leq T
we define the stochastic integral

\int_{0}^{t}F_{s}d\mathcal{W}_{s}:=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}F_{s}(e_{i})dW^{i}_{s},

where the infinite sum is taken in
L^{2}(\Omega;\mathscr{H})
. We can extend this notion to processes
F
which are such that
F(\omega)\in L^{2}\left([0,T];\mathscr{L}^{2}(\mathfrak{U};\mathscr{H})\right)
for
\mathbbm{P}-a.e.

\omega
via the traditional localisation procedure. In this case the stochastic integral is a local martingale in
\mathscr{H}
. We defer to [
42
] Chapter 2 for further details on this construction and properties of the stochastic integral. The stochastic integral of (
5
) is then understood by
\mathcal{P}\mathcal{G}u_{s}(e_{i})=\mathcal{P}\mathcal{G}_{i}(u_{s})
, see [
42
] Subchapter 3.2. We shall make frequent use of the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy Inequality ([
20
] Theorem 4.36), passage of a bounded linear operator through the stochastic integral ([
42
] Proposition 2.16) and the Itô Formula (Proposition
A.1
).

1.3
Transport and Transport-Stretching Noise

We collect some fundamental properties of the transport and transport-stretching noise
\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}
and
B_{i}
, as defined in (
3
), (
4
). We always assume at least that
\xi_{i}\in L^{2}_{\sigma}\cap W^{1,\infty}
with
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left\|\xi_{i}\right\|_{W^{1,\infty}}^{2}<\infty
, where additional spatial regularity will be imposed in the statement of the results. These properties are taken from [
40
] Subsection 2.3, where a more complete description is deferred to. Firstly for
k=0,1,2,\dots
, there exists a constant
c
such that

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{T}_{\xi_{i}}f\right\|_{W^{k,2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq c\left\|\xi_{i}\right\|^{2}_{W^{k+1,\infty}}\left\|f\right\|^{2}_{W^{k,2}}

(17)

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}f\right\|_{W^{k,2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq c\left\|\xi_{i}\right\|^{2}_{W^{k,\infty}}\left\|f\right\|^{2}_{W^{k+1,2}}

(18)

\displaystyle\left\|B_{i}f\right\|_{W^{k,2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq c\left\|\xi_{i}\right\|^{2}_{W^{k+1,\infty}}\left\|f\right\|^{2}_{W^{k+1,2}}

(19)

for

as required by the right hand side. Moreover

is a bounded linear operator on

so has adjoint
\mathcal{T}_{\xi_{i}}^{*}
satisfying the same boundedness.
\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}
is a densely defined operator in

with domain of definition

, and has adjoint
\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}^{*}
in this space given by
-\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}
with same dense domain of definition. Likewise then
B_{i}^{*}
is the densely defined adjoint
-\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}+\mathcal{T}_{\xi_{i}}^{*}
. We also note from [
40
] Lemma 2.7 that
\mathcal{P}B_{i}=\mathcal{P}B_{i}\mathcal{P}
hence
\mathcal{P}B_{i}^{2}=(\mathcal{P}B_{i})^{2}
.

2.1
Transport-Stretching Noise

We begin with the estimates in Stokes spaces.

Proposition 2.1
.

Fix
m\in\mathbb{N}
. There exists a constant
c
such that for all
\xi_{i}\in W^{m+2,\infty}\cap L^{2}_{\sigma}
and
f\in W^{m+2,2}_{\sigma}
,

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathcal{P}B_{i}^{2}f,f\right\rangle_{A^{\frac{m}{2}}}+\left\|\mathcal{P}B_{i}f\right\|_{A^{\frac{m}{2}}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq c\left\|\xi_{i}\right\|_{W^{m+2,\infty}}^{2}\left\|f\right\|_{A^{\frac{m}{2}}}^{2},

(22)

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathcal{P}B_{i}f,f\right\rangle_{A^{\frac{m}{2}}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq c\left\|\xi_{i}\right\|^{2}_{W^{m+1,\infty}}\left\|f\right\|^{4}_{A^{\frac{m}{2}}}.

(23)

The proof of Proposition
2.1
will rely heavily on the following lemma.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\alpha,\beta
be multi-indices and define
\theta=\lvert\alpha\rvert\vee\lvert\beta\rvert
. There exists a constant
c
such that, for each
\xi_{i}\in W^{\theta+2,\infty}\cap L^{2}_{\sigma}
and for all
f\in W^{\theta+2,2}
, we have the bounds

\displaystyle\left\langle D^{\alpha}B_{i}^{2}f,D^{\beta}f\right\rangle+\left\langle D^{\alpha}B_{i}f,D^{\beta}B_{i}f\right\rangle+\left\langle D^{\beta}B_{i}^{2}f,D^{\alpha}f\right\rangle+\left\langle D^{\beta}B_{i}f,D^{\alpha}B_{i}f\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq c\left\|\xi_{i}\right\|_{W^{\theta+2,\infty}}^{2}\left\|f\right\|_{W^{\theta,2}}^{2},

(24)

\displaystyle\left(\left\langle D^{\alpha}B_{i}f,D^{\beta}f\right\rangle+\left\langle D^{\beta}B_{i}f,D^{\alpha}f\right\rangle\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq c\left\|\xi_{i}\right\|^{2}_{W^{\theta+1,\infty}}\left\|f\right\|^{4}_{W^{\theta,2}}.

(25)

Proof.

Let us show the first inequality, for which we start by considering the initial two terms. Observe that

\displaystyle D^{\alpha}B_{\xi_{i}}f=\sum_{\alpha^{\prime}\leq\alpha}B_{D^{\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}}\xi_{i}}D^{\alpha^{\prime}}f=\sum_{\alpha^{\prime}<\alpha}B_{D^{\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}}\xi_{i}}D^{\alpha^{\prime}}f+B_{\xi_{i}}D^{\alpha}f

(26)

hence

\displaystyle D^{\alpha}B^{2}_{\xi_{i}}f=D^{\alpha}B_{\xi_{i}}\big(B_{\xi_{i}}f\big)=\sum_{\alpha^{\prime}<\alpha}B_{D^{\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}}\xi_{i}}D^{\alpha^{\prime}}B_{\xi_{i}}f+B_{\xi_{i}}D^{\alpha}B_{\xi_{i}}f

which we apply to those initial two terms, reducing them to

\Big\langle\sum_{\alpha^{\prime}<\alpha}B_{D^{\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}}\xi_{i}}D^{\alpha^{\prime}}B_{\xi_{i}}f+B_{\xi_{i}}D^{\alpha}B_{\xi_{i}}f,D^{\beta}f\Big\rangle+\left\langle D^{\alpha}B_{\xi_{i}}f,D^{\beta}B_{\xi_{i}}f\right\rangle.

We further break this up in terms of the adjoint
B_{\xi_{i}}^{*}
,

\Big\langle\sum_{\alpha^{\prime}<\alpha}B_{D^{\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}}\xi_{i}}D^{\alpha^{\prime}}B_{\xi_{i}}f,D^{\beta}f\Big\rangle+\left\langle D^{\alpha}B_{\xi_{i}}f,B^{*}_{\xi_{i}}D^{\beta}f\right\rangle+\left\langle D^{\alpha}B_{\xi_{i}}f,D^{\beta}B_{\xi_{i}}f\right\rangle

(27)

where we sum the second and third inner products and using (
26
), becoming

\Big\langle\sum_{\alpha^{\prime}<\alpha}B_{D^{\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}}\xi_{i}}D^{\alpha^{\prime}}B_{\xi_{i}}f,D^{\beta}f\Big\rangle+\Big\langle D^{\alpha}B_{\xi_{i}}f,B^{*}_{\xi_{i}}D^{\beta}f+\sum_{\beta^{\prime}<\beta}B_{D^{\beta-\beta^{\prime}}\xi_{i}}D^{\beta^{\prime}}f+B_{\xi_{i}}D^{\beta}f\Big\rangle.

Simplify by combining
B_{\xi_{i}}^{*}
and
B_{\xi_{i}}
, noting that

B_{i}^{*}+B_{i}=\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}^{*}+\mathcal{T}_{i}^{*}+\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}+\mathcal{T}_{i}=\mathcal{T}_{i}^{*}+\mathcal{T}_{i},

we arrive at the expression

\Big\langle\sum_{\alpha^{\prime}<\alpha}B_{D^{\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}}\xi_{i}}D^{\alpha^{\prime}}B_{\xi_{i}}f,D^{\beta}f\Big\rangle+\Big\langle D^{\alpha}B_{\xi_{i}}f,\big(\mathcal{T}_{\xi_{i}}+\mathcal{T}_{\xi_{i}}^{*}\big)D^{\beta}f+\sum_{\beta^{\prime}<\beta}B_{D^{\beta-\beta^{\prime}}\xi_{i}}D^{\beta^{\prime}}f\Big\rangle.

As we are looking to achieve control with respect to the
W^{\theta,2}
norm of
f
, then it is the terms with differential operators of order greater than
\theta
that concern us. Of course this was the motivating factor behind combining
B_{\xi_{i}}
and its adjoint, nullifying the additional derivative coming from
\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}
. There are more higher order terms to go though, and the strategy will be to write these in terms of commutators with a differential operator of controllable order. This will involve considering different aspects of our sum in tandem, which will be helped with (
26
) reducing our expression again to

\displaystyle\Big\langle\sum_{\alpha^{\prime}<\alpha}B_{D^{\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}}\xi_{i}}D^{\alpha^{\prime}}B_{\xi_{i}}f,D^{\beta}f\Big\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\Big\langle\sum_{\alpha^{\prime}<\alpha}B_{D^{\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}}\xi_{i}}D^{\alpha^{\prime}}f+B_{\xi_{i}}D^{\alpha}f,\big(\mathcal{T}_{\xi_{i}}+\mathcal{T}_{\xi_{i}}^{*}\big)D^{\beta}f+\sum_{\beta^{\prime}<\beta}B_{D^{\beta-\beta^{\prime}}\xi_{i}}D^{\beta^{\prime}}f\Big\rangle.

Ultimately the terms in the summand are split up, grouping terms that we will cancel and those of lower order, achieving in total that

\displaystyle\left\langle D^{\alpha}B_{i}^{2}f,D^{\beta}f\right\rangle

\displaystyle+\left\langle D^{\alpha}B_{i}f,D^{\beta}B_{i}f\right\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad=\left\langle B_{\xi_{i}}D^{\alpha}f,\big(\mathcal{T}_{\xi_{i}}+\mathcal{T}^{*}_{\xi_{i}}\big)D^{\beta}f\right\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad+\Big\langle\sum_{\alpha^{\prime}<\alpha}B_{D^{\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}}\xi_{i}}D^{\alpha^{\prime}}f,\big(\mathcal{T}_{\xi_{i}}+\mathcal{T}_{\xi_{i}}^{*}\big)D^{\beta}f+\sum_{\beta^{\prime}<\beta}B_{D^{\beta-\beta^{\prime}}\xi_{i}}D^{\beta^{\prime}}f\Big\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{\alpha^{\prime}<\alpha}\left\langle B_{D^{\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}}\xi_{i}}D^{\alpha^{\prime}}B_{\xi_{i}}f,D^{\beta}f\right\rangle+\sum_{\beta^{\prime}<\beta}\left\langle B_{\xi_{i}}D^{\alpha}f,B_{D^{\beta-\beta^{\prime}}\xi_{i}}D^{\beta^{\prime}}f\right\rangle.

From this we deduce an expression for

\left\langle D^{\alpha}B_{i}^{2}f,D^{\beta}f\right\rangle+\left\langle D^{\alpha}B_{i}f,D^{\beta}B_{i}f\right\rangle+\left\langle D^{\beta}B_{i}^{2}f,D^{\alpha}f\right\rangle+\left\langle D^{\beta}B_{i}f,D^{\alpha}B_{i}f\right\rangle

(28)

by interchanging the roles of
\alpha,\beta
and summing the result. That is, (
28
) is given by the sum of a first expression

\displaystyle\left\langle B_{\xi_{i}}D^{\alpha}f,\big(\mathcal{T}_{\xi_{i}}+\mathcal{T}^{*}_{\xi_{i}}\big)D^{\beta}f\right\rangle+\left\langle B_{\xi_{i}}D^{\beta}f,\big(\mathcal{T}_{\xi_{i}}+\mathcal{T}^{*}_{\xi_{i}}\big)D^{\alpha}f\right\rangle,

(29)

a second expression

\displaystyle\Big\langle\sum_{\alpha^{\prime}<\alpha}B_{D^{\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}}\xi_{i}}D^{\alpha^{\prime}}f

\displaystyle,\big(\mathcal{T}_{\xi_{i}}+\mathcal{T}_{\xi_{i}}^{*}\big)D^{\beta}f+\sum_{\beta^{\prime}<\beta}B_{D^{\beta-\beta^{\prime}}\xi_{i}}D^{\beta^{\prime}}f\Big\rangle

\displaystyle+\Big\langle\sum_{\beta^{\prime}<\beta}B_{D^{\beta-\beta^{\prime}}\xi_{i}}D^{\beta^{\prime}}f,\big(\mathcal{T}_{\xi_{i}}+\mathcal{T}_{\xi_{i}}^{*}\big)D^{\alpha}f+\sum_{\alpha^{\prime}<\alpha}B_{D^{\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}}\xi_{i}}D^{\alpha^{\prime}}f\Big\rangle

(30)

and a third expression

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha^{\prime}<\alpha}\left\langle B_{D^{\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}}\xi_{i}}D^{\alpha^{\prime}}B_{\xi_{i}}f,D^{\beta}f\right\rangle

\displaystyle+\sum_{\beta^{\prime}<\beta}\left\langle B_{\xi_{i}}D^{\alpha}f,B_{D^{\beta-\beta^{\prime}}\xi_{i}}D^{\beta^{\prime}}f\right\rangle

\displaystyle+\sum_{\beta^{\prime}<\beta}\left\langle B_{D^{\beta-\beta^{\prime}}\xi_{i}}D^{\beta^{\prime}}B_{\xi_{i}}f,D^{\alpha}f\right\rangle+\sum_{\alpha^{\prime}<\alpha}\left\langle B_{\xi_{i}}D^{\beta}f,B_{D^{\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}}\xi_{i}}D^{\alpha^{\prime}}f\right\rangle

(31)

which we control individually. Firstly for a treatment of (
29
), in the first term we have that

\displaystyle\left\langle B_{\xi_{i}}D^{\alpha}f,\big(\mathcal{T}_{\xi_{i}}+\mathcal{T}^{*}_{\xi_{i}}\big)D^{\beta}f\right\rangle

\displaystyle\quad=\left\langle(\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}+\mathcal{T}_{\xi_{i}})D^{\alpha}f,(\mathcal{T}_{\xi_{i}}^{*}+\mathcal{T}_{\xi_{i}})D^{\beta}f\right\rangle

\displaystyle\quad=\left\langle\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}D^{\alpha}f,\mathcal{T}_{\xi_{i}}^{*}D^{\beta}f\right\rangle+\left\langle\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}D^{\alpha}f,\mathcal{T}_{\xi_{i}}D^{\beta}f\right\rangle+\left\langle\mathcal{T}_{\xi_{i}}D^{\alpha}f,\mathcal{T}_{\xi_{i}}^{*}D^{\beta}f\right\rangle+\left\langle\mathcal{T}_{\xi_{i}}D^{\alpha}f,\mathcal{T}_{\xi_{i}}D^{\beta}f\right\rangle

\displaystyle\quad=\Big(\left\langle\mathcal{T}_{\xi_{i}}\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}D^{\alpha}f,D^{\beta}f\right\rangle+\left\langle\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}D^{\alpha}f,\mathcal{T}_{\xi_{i}}D^{\beta}f\right\rangle\Big)+\Big(\left\langle\mathcal{T}_{\xi_{i}}^{2}D^{\alpha}f,D^{\beta}f\right\rangle+\left\langle\mathcal{T}_{\xi_{i}}D^{\alpha}f,\mathcal{T}_{\xi_{i}}D^{\beta}f\right\rangle\Big).

Immediately from (
17
) we obtain that

\lvert\left\langle\mathcal{T}_{\xi_{i}}^{2}D^{\alpha}f,D^{\beta}f\right\rangle+\left\langle\mathcal{T}_{\xi_{i}}D^{\alpha}f,\mathcal{T}_{\xi_{i}}D^{\beta}f\right\rangle\rvert\leq c\left\|\xi_{i}\right\|_{W^{1,\infty}}^{2}\left\|f\right\|_{W^{\theta,2}}^{2}

which we apply twice to verify the control

\displaystyle(\ref{first term})

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\left\langle\mathcal{T}_{\xi_{i}}\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}D^{\alpha}f,D^{\beta}f\right\rangle+\left\langle\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}D^{\alpha}f,\mathcal{T}_{\xi_{i}}D^{\beta}f\right\rangle\Big)

\displaystyle\qquad+\Big(\left\langle\mathcal{T}_{\xi_{i}}\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}D^{\alpha}f,D^{\beta}f\right\rangle+\left\langle\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}D^{\alpha}f,\mathcal{T}_{\xi_{i}}D^{\beta}f\right\rangle\Big)+c\left\|\xi_{i}\right\|_{W^{1,\infty}}^{2}\left\|f\right\|_{W^{\theta,2}}^{2}.

(32)

Now for the first bracket, we add and subtract a term to have an expression through the commutator of the operators:

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathcal{T}_{\xi_{i}}\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}D^{\alpha}f,D^{\beta}f\right\rangle+\left\langle\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}D^{\alpha}f,\mathcal{T}_{\xi_{i}}D^{\beta}f\right\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad=\left\langle(\mathcal{T}_{\xi_{i}}\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}-\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}\mathcal{T}_{\xi_{i}})D^{\alpha}f,D^{\beta}f\right\rangle+\left\langle\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}\mathcal{T}_{\xi_{i}}D^{\alpha}f,D^{\beta}f\right\rangle+\left\langle\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}D^{\alpha}f,\mathcal{T}_{\xi_{i}}D^{\beta}f\right\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad=\left\langle(\mathcal{T}_{\xi_{i}}\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}-\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}\mathcal{T}_{\xi_{i}})D^{\alpha}f,D^{\beta}f\right\rangle+\left\langle\mathcal{T}_{\xi_{i}}D^{\alpha}f,\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}^{*}D^{\beta}f\right\rangle+\left\langle\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}D^{\alpha}f,\mathcal{T}_{\xi_{i}}D^{\beta}f\right\rangle.

(33)

The commutator is given explicitly on a function
g
by

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{\xi_{i}}\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}g=\mathcal{T}_{\xi_{i}}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{3}\xi_{i}^{j}\partial_{j}g\Big)=\sum_{k=1}^{3}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{3}\xi_{i}^{j}\partial_{j}g\Big)^{k}\nabla\xi_{i}^{k}=\sum_{k=1}^{3}\sum_{j=1}^{3}\xi_{i}^{j}\partial_{j}g^{k}\nabla\xi_{i}^{k}

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}\mathcal{T}_{\xi_{i}}g

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{3}g^{k}\nabla\xi_{i}^{k}\Big)=\sum_{j=1}^{3}\xi_{i}^{j}\partial_{j}\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{3}g^{k}\nabla\xi_{i}^{k}\Big)=\sum_{j=1}^{3}\sum_{k=1}^{3}\xi_{i}^{j}\partial_{j}\big(g^{k}\nabla\xi_{i}^{k}\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{3}\sum_{k=1}^{3}\Big(\xi_{i}^{j}\partial_{j}g^{k}\nabla\xi_{i}^{k}+\xi_{i}^{j}g^{k}\partial_{j}\nabla\xi_{i}^{k}\Big)

such that

(\mathcal{T}_{\xi_{i}}\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}-\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}\mathcal{T}_{\xi_{i}})g=-\sum_{j=1}^{3}\sum_{k=1}^{3}\xi_{i}^{j}g^{k}\partial_{j}\nabla\xi_{i}^{k}

which is of zeroth order in
g
as required. Therefore, we obtain

\lvert\left\langle(\mathcal{T}_{\xi_{i}}\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}-\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}\mathcal{T}_{\xi_{i}})D^{\alpha}f,D^{\beta}f\right\rangle\rvert\leq c\left\|\xi_{i}\right\|_{W^{2,\infty}}^{2}\left\|f\right\|_{W^{\theta,2}}^{2}.

Returning to (
33
), by twice applying this bound we have that

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathcal{T}_{\xi_{i}}\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}D^{\alpha}f,D^{\beta}f\right\rangle+\left\langle\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}D^{\alpha}f,\mathcal{T}_{\xi_{i}}D^{\beta}f\right\rangle+\left\langle\mathcal{T}_{\xi_{i}}\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}D^{\beta}f,D^{\alpha}f\right\rangle+\left\langle\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}D^{\beta}f,\mathcal{T}_{\xi_{i}}D^{\alpha}f\right\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad\leq c\left\|\xi_{i}\right\|_{W^{2,\infty}}^{2}\left\|f\right\|_{W^{\theta,2}}^{2}+\left\langle\mathcal{T}_{\xi_{i}}D^{\alpha}f,\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}^{*}D^{\beta}f\right\rangle+\left\langle\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}D^{\alpha}f,\mathcal{T}_{\xi_{i}}D^{\beta}f\right\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\qquad\qquad+\left\langle\mathcal{T}_{\xi_{i}}D^{\beta}f,\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}^{*}D^{\alpha}f\right\rangle+\left\langle\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}D^{\beta}f,\mathcal{T}_{\xi_{i}}D^{\alpha}f\right\rangle.

Using once more that
\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}^{*}=-\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}
, we observe complete cancellation in the four remaining inner products. Altogether, returning to (
32
), we have verified that

(\ref{first term})\leq c\left\|\xi_{i}\right\|_{W^{2,\infty}}^{2}\left\|f\right\|_{W^{\theta,2}}^{2}.

We now move on to the next expression, (
30
), which is only of order
\theta
in
f
by design; through Cauchy-Schwarz and the bound (
19
), we happily deduce that

(\ref{second term})\leq c\left\|\xi_{i}\right\|_{W^{\theta+1,\infty}}^{2}\left\|f\right\|_{W^{\theta,2}}^{2}.

To bound (
28
), it now only remains to show a sufficient control on (
31
). Combining the sums, we have that

\displaystyle(\ref{third term})=

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha^{\prime}<\alpha}\left\langle D^{\beta}f,B_{D^{\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}}\xi_{i}}D^{\alpha^{\prime}}B_{\xi_{i}}f+B_{\xi_{i}}^{*}B_{D^{\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}}\xi_{i}}D^{\alpha^{\prime}}f\right\rangle

\displaystyle+\sum_{\beta^{\prime}<\beta}\left\langle D^{\alpha}f,B_{D^{\beta-\beta^{\prime}}\xi_{i}}D^{\beta^{\prime}}B_{\xi_{i}}f+B_{\xi_{i}}^{*}B_{D^{\beta-\beta^{\prime}}\xi_{i}}D^{\beta^{\prime}}f\right\rangle.

We consider a general term in the first summand,

\left\langle D^{\beta}f,B_{D^{\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}}\xi_{i}}D^{\alpha^{\prime}}B_{\xi_{i}}f+B^{*}_{\xi_{i}}B_{D^{\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}}\xi_{i}}D^{\alpha^{\prime}}f\right\rangle

(34)

and employing (
26
) again we see this becomes

\displaystyle\Big\langle D^{\beta}f,B_{D^{\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}}\xi_{i}}\bigg(\sum_{\gamma<\alpha^{\prime}}B_{D^{\alpha^{\prime}-\gamma}\xi_{i}}D^{\gamma}f+B_{\xi_{i}}D^{\alpha^{\prime}}f\bigg)+B^{*}_{\xi_{i}}B_{D^{\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}}\xi_{i}}D^{\alpha^{\prime}}f\Big\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big\langle D^{\beta}f,\sum_{\gamma<\alpha^{\prime}}B_{D^{\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}}\xi_{i}}B_{D^{\alpha^{\prime}-\gamma}\xi_{i}}D^{\gamma}f\Big\rangle+\left\langle D^{\beta}f,B_{D^{\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}}\xi_{i}}B_{\xi_{i}}D^{\alpha^{\prime}}f+B^{*}_{\xi_{i}}B_{D^{\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}}\xi_{i}}D^{\alpha^{\prime}}f\right\rangle.

We have split up these terms to make our approach clearer, as the two will be considered separately. Indeed the first term is now of order
\theta
, so the familiar Cauchy-Schwarz and (
19
) establishes that

\Big\langle D^{\beta}f,\sum_{\gamma<\alpha^{\prime}}B_{D^{\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}}\xi_{i}}B_{D^{\alpha^{\prime}-\gamma}\xi_{i}}D^{\gamma}f\Big\rangle\leq c\left\|\xi_{i}\right\|_{W^{\theta+1,\infty}}^{2}\left\|f\right\|_{W^{\theta,2}}^{2}.

(35)

As for the second inner product, we rewrite the right side as

B_{D^{\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}}\xi_{i}}\big((\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}+\mathcal{T}_{\xi_{i}})D^{\alpha^{\prime}}f\big)+(\mathcal{L}^{*}_{\xi_{i}}+\mathcal{T}^{*}_{\xi_{i}})B_{D^{\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}}\xi_{i}}D^{\alpha^{\prime}}f

and further

\big(B_{D^{\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}}\xi_{i}}\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}-\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}B_{D^{\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}}\xi_{i}}\big)D^{\alpha^{\prime}}f+B_{D^{\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}}\xi_{i}}\mathcal{T}_{\xi_{i}}D^{\alpha^{\prime}}f+\mathcal{T}_{\xi_{i}}^{*}B_{D^{\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}}\xi_{i}}D^{\alpha^{\prime}}f.

(36)

The latter two terms are once more of order
\theta
, leading to a control as in (
35
). Now we show explicitly that the commutator from (
36
), acting on a function
g
,

(B_{D^{\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}}\xi_{i}}\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}-\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}B_{D^{\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}}\xi_{i}})g

(37)

is of first order, through the expressions

\displaystyle B_{D^{\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}}\xi_{i}}\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}g

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{3}\bigg(D^{\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}}\xi_{i}^{j}\partial_{j}\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{3}\xi_{i}^{k}\partial_{k}g\Big)+\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{3}\xi_{i}^{k}\partial_{k}g\Big)^{j}\nabla D^{\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}}\xi_{i}^{j}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{3}\sum_{k=1}^{3}\bigg(D^{\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}}\xi_{i}^{j}\partial_{j}\xi_{i}^{k}\partial_{k}g+D^{\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}}\xi_{i}^{j}\xi_{i}^{k}\partial_{j}\partial_{k}g+\xi_{i}^{k}\partial_{k}g^{j}\nabla D^{\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}}\xi_{i}^{j}\bigg)

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}B_{D^{\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}}\xi_{i}}g

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{3}\xi_{i}^{k}\partial_{k}\bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{3}D^{\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}}\xi_{i}^{j}\partial_{j}g+g^{j}\nabla D^{\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}}\xi_{i}^{j}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{3}\sum_{k=1}^{3}\bigg(\xi_{i}^{k}\partial_{k}D^{\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}}\xi_{i}^{j}\partial_{j}g+\xi_{i}^{k}D^{\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}}\xi_{i}^{j}\partial_{k}\partial_{j}g+\xi_{i}^{k}\partial_{k}g^{j}\nabla D^{\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}}\xi_{i}^{j}+\xi_{i}^{k}g^{j}\partial_{k}\nabla D^{\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}}\xi_{i}^{j}\bigg)

such that

(\ref{theactualcommutator})=\sum_{j=1}^{3}\sum_{k=1}^{3}\bigg(D^{\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}}\xi_{i}^{j}\partial_{j}\xi_{i}^{k}\partial_{k}g-\xi_{i}^{k}\partial_{k}D^{\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}}\xi_{i}^{j}\partial_{j}g-\xi_{i}^{k}g^{j}\partial_{k}\nabla D^{\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}}\xi_{i}^{j}\bigg).

Due to this expression, we readily obtain that

\left\langle D^{\alpha}f,\big(B_{D^{\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}}\xi_{i}}\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}-\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}B_{D^{\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}}\xi_{i}}\big)D^{\alpha^{\prime}}f\right\rangle\leq c\left\|\xi_{i}\right\|_{W^{\theta+2,\infty}}^{2}\left\|f\right\|_{W^{\theta,2}}^{2}.

In total then, we have verified that

(\ref{rewriteofprime})\leq c\left\|\xi_{i}\right\|_{W^{\theta+2,\infty}}^{2}\left\|f\right\|_{W^{\theta,2}}^{2}

and as a consequence the same bound on (
31
), from which we deduce the first inequality of the Proposition. For the second inequality and to conclude the proof, using (
26
) once more, we see that

\displaystyle\left\langle D^{\alpha}B_{i}f,D^{\beta}f\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\Big\langle\sum_{\alpha^{\prime}<\alpha}B_{D^{\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}}\xi_{i}}D^{\alpha^{\prime}}f+B_{\xi_{i}}D^{\alpha}f,D^{\beta}f\Big\rangle

\displaystyle=\Big\langle\sum_{\alpha^{\prime}<\alpha}B_{D^{\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}}\xi_{i}}D^{\alpha^{\prime}}f,D^{\beta}f\Big\rangle+\left\langle\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}D^{\alpha}f,D^{\beta}f\right\rangle+\left\langle\mathcal{T}_{\xi_{i}}D^{\alpha}f,D^{\beta}f\right\rangle

therefore

\displaystyle\left\langle D^{\alpha}B_{i}f,D^{\beta}f\right\rangle+\left\langle D^{\beta}B_{i}f,D^{\alpha}f\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\Big\langle\sum_{\alpha^{\prime}<\alpha}B_{D^{\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}}\xi_{i}}D^{\alpha^{\prime}}f,D^{\beta}f\Big\rangle+\Big\langle\sum_{\beta^{\prime}<\beta}B_{D^{\beta-\beta^{\prime}}\xi_{i}}D^{\beta^{\prime}}f,D^{\alpha}f\Big\rangle+\left\langle\mathcal{T}_{\xi_{i}}D^{\alpha}f,D^{\beta}f\right\rangle+\left\langle\mathcal{T}_{\xi_{i}}D^{\beta}f,D^{\alpha}f\right\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+\left\langle\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}D^{\alpha}f,D^{\beta}f\right\rangle+\left\langle\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}D^{\beta}f,D^{\alpha}f\right\rangle.

In the bottom line we observe complete cancellation,

\left\langle\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}D^{\alpha}f,D^{\beta}f\right\rangle+\left\langle\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}D^{\beta}f,D^{\alpha}f\right\rangle=-\left\langle D^{\alpha}f,\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}D^{\beta}f\right\rangle+\left\langle\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}D^{\beta}f,D^{\alpha}f\right\rangle=0.

The remaining terms are again constructed to be of order
\theta
, such that Cauchy-Schwarz and (
19
) yield the result.

similarly to Lemma

\displaystyle=\left\langle D^{\beta}f,B_{D^{\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}}\xi_{i}}\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}D^{\alpha^{\prime}}f\right\rangle

having used that
\mathcal{P}B_{D^{\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}}\xi_{i}}\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{P}B_{D^{\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}}\xi_{i}}

so enjoys the regularity that allowed for the conclusion of
\mathcal{P}B_{i}\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{P}B_{i}
. Repeating this procedure, we have that

\displaystyle\left\langle D^{\beta}f,B_{D^{\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}}\xi_{i}}\mathcal{P}\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}D^{\alpha^{\prime}}f-B_{\xi_{i}}\mathcal{P}\mathcal{L}_{D^{\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}}\xi_{i}}D^{\alpha^{\prime}}f\right\rangle=\left\langle D^{\beta}f,B_{D^{\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}}\xi_{i}}\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}D^{\alpha^{\prime}}f-B_{\xi_{i}}\mathcal{L}_{D^{\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}}\xi_{i}}D^{\alpha^{\prime}}f\right\rangle.

Now we can once more decompose
B
into its
\mathcal{L}
and
\mathcal{T}
terms, happy to control those with
\mathcal{T}
directly, leaving the final problematic term as

\left\langle D^{\beta}f,\mathcal{L}_{D^{\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}}\xi_{i}}\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}D^{\alpha^{\prime}}f-\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}\mathcal{L}_{D^{\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}}\xi_{i}}D^{\alpha^{\prime}}f\right\rangle

where the commutator was calculated and controlled explicitly in (
37
), with which we conclude the justification of (
38
). Furthermore, (
39
) requires nothing beyond the proof of (
23
).

∎

Analogously to Proposition
2.3
, we also have the following.

Proposition 2.5
.

Fix
m\in\mathbb{N}
. There exists a constant
c
such that for all
\xi_{i}\in W^{m+2,\infty}\cap L^{2}_{\sigma}
and
f\in W^{m+2,2}_{\sigma}
,

\displaystyle\left\langle\left(\mathcal{P}\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}\right)^{2}f,f\right\rangle_{W^{m,2}}+\left\|\mathcal{P}\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}f\right\|_{W^{m,2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq c\left\|\xi_{i}\right\|_{W^{m+2,\infty}}^{2}\left\|f\right\|_{W^{m,2}}^{2},

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathcal{P}\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}f,f\right\rangle_{W^{m,2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq c\left\|\xi_{i}\right\|^{2}_{W^{m,\infty}}\left\|f\right\|^{4}_{W^{m,2}}.

3
Strong Solutions of the Stochastic Euler Equation

This section concerns the proof of Theorem
1.5
. Solutions are constructed from an inviscid limit of the corresponding stochastic Navier-Stokes equations. Our analysis begins by considering a smooth initial condition, uniformly bounded over
\Omega
. Uniform in viscosity estimates for the approximating sequence of stochastic Navier-Stokes equations are shown in Subsection
3.1
. We show the existence of a limiting process and stopping time by a Cauchy approach in the spirit of [
33
], which was extended in [
38
]. The abstract result is given as Proposition
A.2
in the appendix, and the conditions to apply it are verified in Subsections
3.2
and
3.3
. Passage to the limit and existence of local smooth solutions is given in Subsection
3.4
, where smoothness of the initial condition is optimised in Subsection
3.5
. Uniqueness is demonstrated in Subsection
3.6
, followed by the most intricate arguments of this section, for the maximality and blow-up, in Subsection
3.7
. The proof of Theorem
1.5
concludes in Subsection
3.8
by extending to the case of an unbounded initial condition and verifying the Stratonovich identity.

3.1
Uniform Estimates for the Stochastic Navier-Stokes Equation

In the direction of taking viscosity to zero in the stochastic Navier-Stokes equations, here we consider a viscosity
\nu_{n}
and corresponding solutions
u^{n}
to the equation

u^{n}_{t}=u_{0}-\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{P}\mathcal{L}_{u^{n}_{s}}u^{n}_{s}\ ds-\nu_{n}\int_{0}^{t}Au^{n}_{s}\,ds+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left(\mathcal{P}\mathcal{G}_{i}\right)^{2}u^{n}_{s}ds+\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{P}\mathcal{G}u^{n}_{s}d\mathcal{W}_{s}

(43)

where
(\nu_{n})
converges to zero. Solution theory for these equations are established below.

Proposition 3.1
.

For
m\geq 6
let
u_{0}\in L^{\infty}\left(\Omega;W^{m,2}_{\sigma}\right)
be
\mathcal{F}_{0}-
measurable and each
\xi_{i}\in L^{2}_{\sigma}\cap W^{m+3,\infty}
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left\|\xi_{i}\right\|_{W^{m+2,\infty}}^{2}<\infty
. Fix any
M>1
. There exists a pair
(u^{n},\tau^{M}_{n})
where
u^{n}
is a process such that for
\mathbbm{P}-a.e.

\omega
,
u_{\cdot}(\omega)\in C\left([0,T];W^{m,2}_{\sigma}\right)
, and
\tau^{M}_{n}
is the
\mathbbm{P}-a.s.
positive stopping time defined by

\tau^{M}_{n}:=T\wedge\inf\left\{s\geq 0:\sup_{r\in[0,s]}\left\|u^{n}_{r}\right\|_{W^{m-3,2}}^{2}dr\geq M+\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{W^{m-3,2}}^{2}\right\}

(44)

which satisfies:

1.

Existence as a local strong solution, in the sense that
u_{\cdot}(\omega)\mathbbm{1}_{\cdot\leq\tau^{M}_{n}(\omega)}\in L^{2}\left([0,T];W^{m+1,2}_{\sigma}\right)
and with
u_{\cdot}\mathbbm{1}_{\cdot\leq\tau^{M}_{n}}
progressively measurable in
W^{m+1,2}_{\sigma}
, satisfying (
43
) stopped at
\tau^{M}_{n}

\mathbbm{P}-a.s.
in
L^{2}_{\sigma}
for all
t\in[0,T]
.

2.

Uniqueness, meaning that if
(v,\gamma)
was any other such local strong solution then

\mathbbm{P}\left(\left\{\omega\in\Omega:u^{n}_{t}(\omega)=v_{t}(\omega)\quad\forall t\in[0,\tau^{M}_{n}\wedge\gamma]\right\}\right)=1.

3.

Uniform in viscosity estimates, whereby there exists a constant
C_{M}
independent of
n
(but dependent on
M
,
u_{0}
) such that

\mathbbm{E}\left(\sup_{r\in[0,\tau^{M}_{n}]}\left\|u^{n}_{r}\right\|_{W^{m-1,2}}^{2}\right)\leq C_{M}.

(45)

Proof.

The existence and uniqueness items above for
\mathcal{G}_{i}=B_{i}
were proven in [
36
] Proposition 3.7, as iterated applications of [
37
] Theorem 2.9. More precisely a maximal strong solution is constructed where the maximal time is given by the blow-up in
C\left([0,\cdot];W^{1,2}_{\sigma}\right)\cap L^{2}\left([0,\cdot];W^{2,2}_{\sigma}\right)
, a coarser norm than
C\left([0,\cdot];W^{m-3,2}_{\sigma}\right)
until
T
, therefore
\tau^{M}_{n}
is strictly less than the maximal time and ensures that the maximal solution is a local one up until
\tau^{M}_{n}
. Thus the local strong solution given by the maximal solution stopped at the local time,
(u^{n}_{\cdot\wedge\tau^{M}_{n}},\tau^{M}_{n})
enjoys the desired regularity from [
36
] Proposition 3.7. With the bounds demonstrated for transport noise in Proposition
2.4
then the result holds similarly for
\mathcal{G}_{i}=\mathcal{P}\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}
. It remains to demonstrate the uniform in viscosity estimate. To rigorously take expectations in verifying this estimate we introduce the stopping time

\alpha_{R}:=\tau^{M}_{n}\wedge\inf\left\{s\geq 0:\sup_{r\in[0,s]}\left\|u^{n}_{r}\right\|_{W^{m,2}}^{2}+\int_{0}^{s}\left\|u^{n}_{r}\right\|_{W^{m+1,2}}^{2}dr\geq R\right\}

(46)

along with notation

\check{u}^{n}_{\cdot}:=u^{n}_{\cdot}\mathbbm{1}_{\cdot\leq\alpha_{R}}.

(47)

Stopping
u^{n}
at
\alpha_{R}
and applying the Itô Formula, appreciating that the identity of (
43
) is in fact satisfied in
W^{m-1,2}_{\sigma}
, we deduce the energy equality

\displaystyle\left\|u^{n}_{r\wedge\alpha_{R}}\right\|_{W^{m-1,2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{W^{m-1,2}}^{2}-2\int_{0}^{r\wedge\alpha_{R}}\left\langle\mathcal{P}\mathcal{L}_{u^{n}_{s}}u^{n}_{s},u^{n}_{s}\right\rangle_{W^{m-1,2}}ds-2\nu_{n}\int_{0}^{r\wedge\alpha_{R}}\left\langle Au^{n}_{s},u^{n}_{s}\right\rangle_{W^{m-1,2}}ds

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{r\wedge\alpha_{R}}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left\langle\left(\mathcal{P}\mathcal{G}_{i}\right)^{2}u^{n}_{s},u^{n}_{s}\right\rangle_{W^{m-1,2}}ds+\int_{0}^{r\wedge\alpha_{R}}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left\|\mathcal{P}\mathcal{G}_{i}u^{n}_{s}\right\|_{W^{m-1,2}}^{2}ds

\displaystyle+2\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{r\wedge\alpha_{R}}\left\langle\mathcal{P}\mathcal{G}_{i}u^{n}_{s},u^{n}_{s}\right\rangle_{W^{m-1,2}}dW^{i}_{s}.

Here we look to reduce the terms. Pertaining to the Stokes Operator we observe that

-\left\langle Au^{n}_{s},u^{n}_{s}\right\rangle_{W^{m-1,2}}=\left\langle\mathcal{P}\Delta u^{n}_{s},u^{n}_{s}\right\rangle_{W^{m-1,2}}=-\sum_{j=1}^{3}\left\|\partial_{j}u^{n}_{s}\right\|_{W^{m-1,2}}^{2},

(48)

which is negative, whilst for the nonlinear term we recall the control (
16
) to verify

\left|\left\langle\mathcal{P}\mathcal{L}_{u^{n}_{s}}u^{n}_{s},u^{n}_{s}\right\rangle_{W^{m-1,2}}\right|\leq c\left\|u^{n}_{s}\right\|_{W^{1,\infty}}\left\|u^{n}_{s}\right\|_{W^{m-1,2}}^{2}.

The following two terms, attributed to the Itô-Stratonovich corrector and the quadratic variation, are controlled in the two cases of
\mathcal{G}
by Propositions
2.3
and
2.5
. Inserting these bounds into the given energy equality, we furthermore take absolute values and the supremum in time. Appreciating that for any
t\in[0,T]
,
\sup_{r\in[0,t]}\left\|\check{u}^{n}_{r}\right\|_{W^{m-1,2}}^{2}=\sup_{r\in[0,t]}\left\|u^{n}_{r\wedge\alpha_{R}}\right\|_{W^{m-1,2}}^{2}
as the hitting threshold is met continuously, taking expectation, applying the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy Inequality and recasting into the
\check{u}^{n}
notation of (
47
), we deduce that

\displaystyle\mathbbm{E}\left(\sup_{r\in[0,t]}\left\|\check{u}^{n}_{r}\right\|_{W^{m-1,2}}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq c\mathbbm{E}\left(\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{W^{m-1,2}}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\left[1+\left\|\check{u}^{n}_{s}\right\|_{W^{1,\infty}}\right]\left\|\check{u^{n}_{s}}\right\|_{W^{m-1,2}}^{2}ds\right)

\displaystyle+c\mathbbm{E}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left\langle\mathcal{P}\mathcal{G}_{i}\check{u}^{n}_{s},\check{u}^{n}_{s}\right\rangle_{W^{m-1,2}}^{2}ds\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(49)

In the final term we invoke Proposition
2.3
or
2.5
as relevant, amounting to

\displaystyle c\mathbbm{E}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left\langle\mathcal{P}\mathcal{G}_{i}\check{u}^{n}_{s},\check{u}^{n}_{s}\right\rangle_{W^{m-1,2}}^{2}ds\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq c\mathbbm{E}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\check{u}^{n}_{s}\right\|_{W^{m-1,2}}^{4}ds\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq c\mathbbm{E}\left(\sup_{r\in[0,t]}\left\|\check{u^{n}_{r}}\right\|_{W^{m-1,2}}^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\check{u}^{n}_{s}\right\|_{W^{m-1,2}}^{2}ds\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\mathbbm{E}\left(\sup_{r\in[0,t]}\left\|\check{u^{n}_{r}}\right\|_{W^{m-1,2}}^{2}\right)+c\mathbbm{E}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\check{u}^{n}_{s}\right\|_{W^{m-1,2}}^{2}ds\right)

(50)

having used Young’s Inequality in the final line. Inserting this back into (
49
) grants us that

\displaystyle\mathbbm{E}\left(\sup_{r\in[0,t]}\left\|\check{u}^{n}_{r}\right\|_{W^{m-1,2}}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq c\mathbbm{E}\left(\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{W^{m-1,2}}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\left[1+\left\|\check{u}^{n}_{s}\right\|_{W^{1,\infty}}\right]\left\|\check{u^{n}_{s}}\right\|_{W^{m-1,2}}^{2}ds\right)

(51)

where we have simply multiplied both sides by
2
. Here we utilise the control on
\check{u}^{n}
afforded to us by
\tau^{M}_{n}
, defined in (
44
), as well as the fact that
u_{0}\in L^{\infty}\left(\Omega;W^{m,2}_{\sigma}\right)
so
\sup_{r\in[0,t]}\left\|\check{u}^{n}_{r}\right\|_{W^{m-3,2}}^{2}\leq C_{M}
uniformly in
\omega
. Due to the Sobolev Embedding of
W^{m-3,2}\xhookrightarrow{}W^{1,\infty}
then we likewise have that
\sup_{r\in[0,t]}\left\|\check{u}^{n}_{r}\right\|_{W^{1,\infty}}^{2}\leq C_{M}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathbbm{E}\left(\sup_{r\in[0,t]}\left\|\check{u}^{n}_{r}\right\|_{W^{m-1,2}}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C_{M}\left(1+\mathbbm{E}\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\check{u^{n}_{s}}\right\|_{W^{m-1,2}}^{2}ds\right)

from which the standard Grönwall Inequality yields that

\mathbbm{E}\left(\sup_{r\in[0,T]}\left\|\check{u}^{n}_{r}\right\|_{W^{m-1,2}}^{2}\right)\leq C_{M}

which we write again explicitly in terms of
\alpha_{R}
by

\mathbbm{E}\left(\sup_{r\in[0,\alpha_{R}]}\left\|u^{n}_{r}\right\|_{W^{m-1,2}}^{2}\right)\leq C_{M}

whilst noting that
C_{M}
is independent of
R
. We use that
\alpha_{R}
is
\mathbbm{P}-a.s.
monotonically increasing to
\tau^{M}_{n}
as
R\rightarrow\infty
, so continuity of the integrand and applying the Monotone Convergence Theorem obtains the result.
∎

3.2
Cauchy Property

Proposition 3.2
.

We have that

\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{n\geq j}\mathbbm{E}\left(\sup_{r\in[0,\tau^{M}_{n}\wedge\tau^{M}_{j}]}\left\|u^{n}_{r}-u^{j}_{r}\right\|_{W^{m-3,2}}^{2}\right)=0.

Proof.

Our approach is similar to the proof of Proposition
3.1
, where we shall look at the energy of the identity satisfied by
u^{n}-u^{j}
for
n\geq j
. To this end we define
\alpha:=\tau^{M}_{n}\wedge\tau^{M}_{j}
, noting that we do not need to truncate by
R
as in the previous result given the uniform estimate of (
45
). We obtain the energy identity

\displaystyle\left\|u^{n}_{r\wedge\alpha}-u^{j}_{r\wedge\alpha}\right\|_{W^{m-3,2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=-2\int_{0}^{r\wedge\alpha}\left\langle\mathcal{P}\mathcal{L}_{u^{n}_{s}}u^{n}_{s}-\mathcal{P}\mathcal{L}_{u^{j}_{s}}u^{j}_{s},u^{n}_{s}-u^{j}_{s}\right\rangle_{W^{m-3,2}}ds

\displaystyle-2\nu_{n}\int_{0}^{r\wedge\alpha}\left\langle Au^{n}_{s},u^{n}_{s}-u^{j}_{s}\right\rangle_{W^{m-3,2}}ds+2\nu_{j}\int_{0}^{r\wedge\alpha}\left\langle Au^{j}_{s},u^{n}_{s}-u^{j}_{s}\right\rangle_{W^{m-3,2}}ds

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{r\wedge\alpha}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left(\left\langle\left(\mathcal{P}\mathcal{G}_{i}\right)^{2}\left(u^{n}_{s}-u^{j}_{s}\right),u^{n}_{s}-u^{j}_{s}\right\rangle_{W^{m-3,2}}+\left\|\mathcal{P}\mathcal{G}_{i}\left(u^{n}_{s}-u^{j}_{s}\right)\right\|_{W^{m-3,2}}^{2}\right)ds

\displaystyle+2\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{r\wedge\alpha}\left\langle\mathcal{P}\mathcal{G}_{i}\left(u^{n}_{s}-u^{j}_{s}\right),u^{n}_{s}-u^{j}_{s}\right\rangle_{W^{m-3,2}}dW^{i}_{s}

and look to reduce the terms individually. For the nonlinear term, exactly as in [
33
] (7.12) we obtain that

\displaystyle\left|\left\langle\mathcal{P}\mathcal{L}_{u^{n}_{s}}u^{n}_{s}-\mathcal{P}\mathcal{L}_{u^{j}_{s}}u^{j}_{s},u^{n}_{s}-u^{j}_{s}\right\rangle_{W^{m-3,2}}\right|

\displaystyle\qquad\leq c\left\|u^{n}_{s}\right\|_{W^{m-2,2}}^{2}\left\|u^{n}_{s}-u^{j}_{s}\right\|_{W^{m-4,2}}^{2}+c\left(1+\left\|u^{n}_{s}\right\|_{W^{m-3,2}}+\left\|u^{j}_{s}\right\|_{W^{m-3,2}}\right)\left\|u^{n}_{s}-u^{j}_{s}\right\|_{W^{m-3,2}}^{2}.

In the Stokes terms we may use a coarse control with Cauchy-Schwarz and Young’s Inequality, bounding them both by

\displaystyle c(\nu_{n}+\nu_{j})\int_{0}^{r\wedge\alpha}\left\|u^{n}_{s}\right\|_{W^{m-1,2}}^{2}+\left\|u^{j}_{s}\right\|_{W^{m-1,2}}^{2}ds.

(52)

Owing to the linearity of the noise terms, their treatment requires no difference to that conducted in the proof of Proposition
3.1
. Indeed by repeating the steps of that proof, with the new notation
\hat{u}^{n}_{\cdot}=u^{n}_{\cdot}\mathbbm{1}_{\cdot\leq\alpha}
,
\hat{u}^{j}_{\cdot}=u^{j}_{\cdot}\mathbbm{1}_{\cdot\leq\alpha}
, we achieve that

\displaystyle\mathbbm{E}\left(\sup_{r\in[0,t]}\left\|\hat{u}^{n}_{r}-\hat{u}^{j}_{r}\right\|_{W^{m-3,2}}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\leq c\mathbbm{E}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\left[1+\left\|\hat{u}^{n}_{s}\right\|_{W^{m-3,2}}+\left\|\hat{u}^{j}_{s}\right\|_{W^{m-3,2}}\right]\left\|\hat{u}^{n}_{s}-\hat{u}^{j}_{s}\right\|_{W^{m-3,2}}^{2}ds\right)

\displaystyle\quad+c(\nu_{n}+\nu_{j})\mathbbm{E}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\hat{u}^{n}_{s}\right\|_{W^{m-1,2}}^{2}+\left\|\hat{u}^{j}_{s}\right\|_{W^{m-1,2}}^{2}ds\right)+c\mathbbm{E}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\hat{u}^{n}_{s}\right\|_{W^{m-2,2}}^{2}\left\|\hat{u}^{n}_{s}-\hat{u}^{j}_{s}\right\|_{W^{m-4,2}}^{2}ds\right).

(53)

In the first term we shall again use the control granted by the stopping time
\tau^{M}_{n}\wedge\tau^{M}_{j}
. In the second term we first employ that
\nu_{n}+\nu_{j}\leq 2\nu_{j}
, and furthermore that

\displaystyle c\nu_{j}\mathbbm{E}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\hat{u}^{n}_{s}\right\|_{W^{m-1,2}}^{2}+\left\|\hat{u}^{j}_{s}\right\|_{W^{m-1,2}}^{2}ds\right)

\displaystyle=c\nu_{j}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbbm{E}\left(\left\|\hat{u}^{n}_{s}\right\|_{W^{m-2,2}}^{2}+\left\|\hat{u}^{j}_{s}\right\|_{W^{m-1,2}}^{2}\right)ds

\displaystyle\leq c\nu_{j}\int_{0}^{t}C_{M}ds\leq C_{M}\nu_{j}

due to (
45
), where
C_{M}
here is also dependent on
T
which remains fixed. Therefore, revisiting (
53
), we have achieved that

\displaystyle\mathbbm{E}\left(\sup_{r\in[0,t]}\left\|\hat{u}^{n}_{r}-\hat{u}^{j}_{r}\right\|_{W^{m-3,2}}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C_{M}\left[\nu_{j}+\mathbbm{E}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\hat{u}^{n}_{s}-\hat{u}^{j}_{s}\right\|_{W^{m-3,2}}^{2}ds\right)\right]

\displaystyle+c\mathbbm{E}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\hat{u}^{n}_{s}\right\|_{W^{m-2,2}}^{2}\left\|\hat{u}^{n}_{s}-\hat{u}^{j}_{s}\right\|_{W^{m-4,2}}^{2}ds\right).

With a coarse bound on the final term, allowing the generic constant
c
to be dependent on
M
and
T
and applying the classical Grönwall lemma,

\displaystyle\mathbbm{E}\left(\sup_{r\in[0,T]}\left\|\hat{u}^{n}_{r}-\hat{u}^{j}_{r}\right\|_{W^{m-3,2}}^{2}\right)\leq c\left(\nu_{j}+\mathbbm{E}\left[\sup_{r\in[0,T]}\left(\left\|\hat{u}^{n}_{r}\right\|_{W^{m-2,2}}^{2}\left\|\hat{u}^{n}_{r}-\hat{u}^{j}_{r}\right\|_{W^{m-4,2}}^{2}\right)\right]\right).

Moreover, the result will follow once we show that

\displaystyle\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{n\geq j}\mathbbm{E}\left[\sup_{r\in[0,T]}\left(\left\|\hat{u}^{n}_{r}\right\|_{W^{m-2,2}}^{2}\left\|\hat{u}^{n}_{r}-\hat{u}^{j}_{r}\right\|_{W^{m-4,2}}^{2}\right)\right]=0.

(54)

The following arguments will be notationally heavy, so we replace
\hat{u}^{n}
by simply
u^{n}
for convenience, although we stress that it remains truly truncated at
\alpha
. Furthermore let us introduce the notation
v_{t}=u^{n}_{t}-u^{j}_{t}
. To estimate this term we again aim to use the Grönwall inequality, hence we shall determine an evolution equation for the product

\left\|u^{n}_{\cdot}\right\|_{W^{m-2,2}}^{2}\left\|v_{\cdot}\right\|_{W^{m-4,2}}^{2}
. To this end we note that by the Itô formula,

\displaystyle d\left\|u^{n}_{t}\right\|_{W^{m-2,2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=-2\left\langle\mathcal{P}\mathcal{L}_{u^{n}_{t}}u^{n}_{t},u^{n}_{t}\right\rangle_{W^{m-2,2}}dt

\displaystyle-2\nu_{n}\left\langle Au^{n}_{t},u^{n}_{t}\right\rangle_{W^{m-2,2}}dt

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left[\left\langle\left(\mathcal{P}\mathcal{G}_{i}\right)^{2}u^{n}_{t},u^{n}_{t}\right\rangle_{W^{m-2,2}}+\left\|\mathcal{P}\mathcal{G}_{i}u^{n}_{t}\right\|_{W^{m-2,2}}^{2}\right]dt

\displaystyle+2\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left\langle\mathcal{P}\mathcal{G}_{i}u^{n}_{t},u^{n}_{t}\right\rangle_{W^{m-2,2}}dW_{t}^{i}

\displaystyle:=(I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3})dt+I_{4}d\mathcal{W}_{t}

as well as

\displaystyle d\left\|v_{t}\right\|_{W^{m-4,2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=-2\left\langle\mathcal{P}\mathcal{L}_{u^{n}_{t}}u^{n}_{t}-\mathcal{P}\mathcal{L}_{u^{j}_{t}}u^{j}_{t},u^{n}_{t}-u^{j}_{t}\right\rangle_{W^{m-4,2}}dt

\displaystyle-\left[2\nu_{n}\left\langle Au^{n}_{t},u^{n}_{t}-u^{j}_{t}\right\rangle_{W^{m-4,2}}+2\nu_{j}\left\langle Au^{j}_{t},u^{n}_{t}-u^{j}_{t}\right\rangle_{W^{m-4,2}}\right]dt

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left[\left\langle\left(\mathcal{P}\mathcal{G}_{i}\right)^{2}(u^{n}_{t}-u^{j}_{t}),u^{n}_{t}-u^{j}_{t}\right\rangle_{W^{m-4,2}}+\left\|\mathcal{P}\mathcal{G}_{i}(u^{n}_{t}-u^{j}_{t})\right\|_{W^{m-4,2}}^{2}\right]dt

\displaystyle+2\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left\langle\mathcal{P}\mathcal{G}_{i}(u^{n}_{t}-u^{j}_{t}),u^{n}_{t}-u^{j}_{t}\right\rangle_{W^{m-4,2}}dW_{t}^{i}

\displaystyle:=(J_{1}+J_{2}+J_{3})dt+J_{4}d\mathcal{W}_{t}.

As the norms are simply real valued processes, then by the usual Itô product rule we obtain

\displaystyle d(\left\|u^{n}_{t}\right\|_{W^{m-2,2}}^{2}\left\|v_{t}\right\|_{W^{m-4,2}}^{2})

\displaystyle=\left\|u^{n}_{t}\right\|_{W^{m-2,2}}^{2}d\left\|v_{t}\right\|_{W^{m-4,2}}^{2}+\left\|v_{t}\right\|_{W^{m-4,2}}^{2}d\left\|u^{n}_{t}\right\|_{W^{m-2,2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+d\left\|u^{n}_{t}\right\|_{W^{m-2,2}}^{2}d\left\|v_{t}\right\|_{W^{m-4,2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left[\left\|u^{n}_{t}\right\|_{W^{m-2,2}}^{2}(J_{1}+J_{2}+J_{3})+\left\|v_{t}\right\|_{W^{m-4,2}}^{2}(I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3})+K\right]dt

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\left[\left\|u^{n}_{t}\right\|_{W^{m-2,2}}^{2}J_{4}+\left\|v_{t}\right\|_{W^{m-4,2}}^{2}I_{4}\right]dW_{t}

where
K=4\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left\langle\mathcal{P}\mathcal{G}_{i}u^{n}_{t},u^{n}_{t}\right\rangle_{W^{m-2,2}}\left\langle\mathcal{P}\mathcal{G}_{i}v_{t},v_{t}\right\rangle_{W^{m-4,2}}
. We now turn to estimating all of the terms. We shall frequently use the nonlinearity estimate (
16
) and the noise control of Propositions
2.3
and
2.5
without explicit reference, also absorbing the dependence on
(\xi_{i})
into the generic constant. Firstly,

\displaystyle\left|\left\|v_{t}\right\|_{W^{m-4,2}}^{2}(I_{1}+I_{3})\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|v_{t}\right\|_{W^{m-4,2}}^{2}\left(\left\|u^{n}_{t}\right\|_{W^{1,\infty}}\left\|u^{n}_{t}\right\|_{W^{m-2,2}}^{2}+\left\|u^{n}_{t}\right\|_{W^{m-2,2}}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|v_{t}\right\|_{W^{m-4,2}}^{2}\left\|u^{n}_{t}\right\|_{W^{m-2,2}}^{2}\left(\left\|u^{n}_{t}\right\|_{W^{1,\infty}}+1\right).

(55)

Secondly,

\displaystyle\left\|u^{n}_{t}\right\|_{W^{m-2,2}}^{2}|J_{3}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|u^{n}_{t}\right\|_{W^{m-2,2}}^{2}\left\|v_{t}\right\|_{W^{m-4,2}}^{2}.

(56)

The contribution of the nonlinear term in
v
is more delicate, although this is handled in [
34
] (7.19), (7.23). In particular,

\left\|u^{n}_{t}\right\|_{W^{m-2,2}}^{2}|J_{1}|\lesssim\left\|u^{n}_{t}\right\|_{W^{m-2,2}}^{2}\left\|v_{t}\right\|_{W^{m-4,2}}^{2}\left(\left\|u^{n}\right\|_{W^{m-3,2}}+\left\|u^{j}\right\|_{W^{m-3,2}}\right).

(57)

The
|K|
term enjoys our noise estimates, with

\displaystyle|K|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left|\left\langle\mathcal{P}\mathcal{G}_{i}u^{n}_{t},u^{n}_{t}\right\rangle_{W^{m-2,2}}\right|\left|\left\langle\mathcal{P}\mathcal{G}_{i}v_{t},v_{t}\right\rangle_{W^{m-4,2}}\right|\lesssim\left\|u^{n}_{t}\right\|_{W^{m-2,2}}^{2}\left\|v_{t}\right\|_{W^{m-4,2}}^{2}.

(58)

We move towards the viscous terms. With the observation (
48
),

\displaystyle\left\|v_{t}\right\|_{W^{m-4,2}}^{2}I_{2}=-2\nu_{n}\left\|v_{t}\right\|_{W^{m-4,2}}^{2}\left\langle Au^{n}_{t},u^{n}_{t}\right\rangle_{W^{m-2,2}}\leq 0.

(59)

Employing the same observation again,

\displaystyle\left\|u^{n}_{t}\right\|_{W^{m-2,2}}^{2}J_{2}

\displaystyle=-2\nu_{n}\left\|u^{n}_{t}\right\|_{W^{m-2,2}}^{2}\left\langle Au^{n}_{t},v_{t}\right\rangle_{W^{m-4,2}}+2\nu_{j}\left\|u^{n}_{t}\right\|_{W^{m-2,2}}^{2}\left\langle Au^{j}_{t},v_{t}\right\rangle_{W^{m-4,2}}

\displaystyle=-2\nu_{j}\left\|u^{n}_{t}\right\|_{W^{m-2,2}}^{2}\left\langle Av_{t},v_{t}\right\rangle_{W^{m-4,2}}+2\left(\nu_{j}-\nu_{n}\right)\left\|u^{n}_{t}\right\|_{W^{m-2,2}}^{2}\left\langle Au^{n}_{t},v_{t}\right\rangle_{W^{m-4,2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\nu_{j}-\nu_{n}\right)\left\|u^{n}_{t}\right\|_{W^{m-2,2}}^{2}\left\langle Au^{n}_{t},v_{t}\right\rangle_{W^{m-4,2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\nu_{j}\left\|u^{n}_{t}\right\|_{W^{m-2,2}}^{2}\left(\left\|u^{n}_{t}\right\|_{W^{m-3,2}}^{2}+\left\|u^{j}_{t}\right\|_{W^{m-3,2}}^{2}\right).

(60)

similarly to (
52
). It is now crucial that (
60
) will be going to
zero, forced by the decay of
\nu_{j}
and using the uniform bound (
45
). All that is left to estimate now are the stochastic integrals, which we control similarly to (
50
):

\displaystyle\mathbbm{E}\left[\sup_{r\in[0,t]}\left|\int_{0}^{r}\left\|u^{n}_{s}\right\|_{W^{m-2,2}}^{2}J_{4}d\mathcal{W}_{s}\right|\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leq c\mathbbm{E}\left[\sup_{r\in[0,t]}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{r}\left\|u^{n}_{s}\right\|_{W^{m-2,2}}^{2}\left\langle\mathcal{P}\mathcal{G}_{i}(u^{n}_{t}-u^{j}_{t}),u^{n}_{t}-u^{j}_{t}\right\rangle_{W^{m-4,2}}dW_{s}^{i}\right|\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leq c\mathbbm{E}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{t}\left\|u^{n}_{s}\right\|_{W^{m-2,2}}^{4}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left\langle\mathcal{P}\mathcal{G}_{i}(u^{n}_{t}-u^{j}_{t}),u^{n}_{t}-u^{j}_{t}\right\rangle_{W^{m-4,2}}^{2}ds\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leq c\mathbbm{E}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{t}\left\|u^{n}_{s}\right\|_{W^{m-2,2}}^{4}\left\|v_{s}\right\|_{W^{m-4,2}}^{4}ds\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leq c\mathbbm{E}\left[\left(\sup_{r\in[0,t]}\left\|u^{n}_{r}\right\|_{W^{m-2,2}}^{2}\left\|v_{r}\right\|_{W^{m-4,2}}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\left\|u^{n}_{s}\right\|_{W^{m-2,2}}^{2}\left\|v_{s}\right\|_{W^{m-4,2}}^{2}ds\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leq\frac{1}{4}\mathbbm{E}\left[\sup_{r\in[0,t]}\left\|u^{n}_{r}\right\|_{W^{m-2,2}}^{2}\left\|v_{r}\right\|_{W^{m-4,2}}^{2}\right]+c\mathbbm{E}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\left\|u^{n}_{s}\right\|_{W^{m-2,2}}^{2}\left\|v_{s}\right\|_{W^{m-4,2}}^{2}ds\right).

(61)

We have used the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy Inequality and in the last line Young’s inequality, so that we can subtract the
supremum term from the left hand side in the final step when we collect all of the
estimates and apply the Grönwall lemma. Identically, we have that

\displaystyle\mathbbm{E}\left[\sup_{r\in[0,t]}\left|\int_{0}^{s}\left\|{v}_{s}\right\|_{W^{m-4,2}}^{2}I_{4}d\mathcal{W}_{s}\right|\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leq\frac{1}{4}\mathbbm{E}\left[\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\left\|u^{n}_{s}\right\|_{W^{m-2,2}}^{2}\left\|v_{s}\right\|_{W^{m-4,2}}^{2}\right]+c\mathbbm{E}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\left\|u^{n}_{s}\right\|_{W^{m-2,2}}^{2}\left\|v_{s}\right\|_{W^{m-4,2}}^{2}ds\right).

(62)

Collecting all of the estimates (
55
)-(
62
), as well as using the uniform control granted by
\alpha
, we can finally write down that

\displaystyle\mathbbm{E}\left[\sup_{r\in[0,t]}\left\|u^{n}_{r}\right\|_{W^{m-2,2}}^{2}\left\|v_{r}\right\|_{W^{m-4,2}}^{2}\right]\lesssim\nu_{j}\mathbbm{E}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\left\|u^{n}_{s}\right\|_{W^{m-2,2}}^{2}ds\right)+\mathbbm{E}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\left\|u^{n}_{s}\right\|_{W^{m-2,2}}^{2}\left\|v_{s}\right\|_{W^{m-4,2}}^{2}ds\right)

noting that
v_{0}=0
. Employing the bound (
45
) in the viscous term, an application of the standard Grönwall lemma yields that

\displaystyle\mathbbm{E}\left[\sup_{r\in[0,T]}\left\|u^{n}_{r}\right\|_{W^{m-2,2}}^{2}\left\|v_{r}\right\|_{W^{m-4,2}}^{2}\right]\lesssim\nu_{j}

from which we conclude (
54
) and ultimately the result.

∎

3.3
Weak Equicontinuity

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\theta
be a stopping time and
(\delta_{l})
a sequence of stopping times which converge to
0

\mathbbm{P}-a.s.
. Then

\lim_{l\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbbm{E}\left[\sup_{r\in[0,(\theta+\delta_{l})\wedge\tau^{M}_{n}]}\left\|u^{n}_{r}\right\|_{W^{m-3,2}}^{2}-\sup_{r\in[0,\theta\wedge\tau^{M}_{n}]}\left\|u^{n}_{r}\right\|_{W^{m-3,2}}^{2}\right]=0.

Proof.

Using the observation (75) from [
38
] we simplify the task at hand to

\displaystyle\lim_{l\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbbm{E}\left(\sup_{r\in[0,\delta_{l}]}\left\|u^{n}_{(\theta+r)\wedge\tau^{M}_{n}]}\right\|_{W^{m-3,2}}^{2}-\left\|u^{n}_{\theta\wedge\tau^{M}_{n}]}\right\|_{W^{m-3,2}}^{2}\right)=0.

(63)

To obtain the above estimate we apply the Itô Formula in
W^{m-3,2}_{\sigma}
up until the stopping time
\theta\wedge\tau^{M}_{n}
and then
(\theta+r)\wedge\tau^{M}_{n}
for some
r\geq 0
, then subtract the two to obtain that

\displaystyle\left\|u^{n}_{(\theta+r)\wedge\tau^{M}_{n}}\right\|^{2}_{W^{m-3,2}}-\left\|u^{n}_{\theta\wedge\tau^{M}_{n}}\right\|^{2}_{W^{m-3,2}}+2\nu_{n}\int_{\theta\wedge\tau^{M}_{n}}^{(\theta+r)\wedge\tau^{M}_{n}}\left\langle Au^{n}_{t},u^{n}_{t}\right\rangle_{W^{m-3,2}}dt

\displaystyle\qquad=-2\int_{\theta\wedge\tau^{M}_{n}}^{(\theta+r)\wedge\tau^{M}_{n}}\left\langle\mathcal{P}\mathcal{L}_{u^{n}_{t}}u^{n}_{t},u^{n}_{t}\right\rangle_{W^{m-3,2}}dt+2\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\int_{\theta\wedge\tau^{M}_{n}}^{(\theta+r)\wedge\tau^{M}_{n}}\left\langle\mathcal{P}\mathcal{G}_{i}u^{n}_{t},u^{n}_{t}\right\rangle_{W^{m-3,2}}dW_{t}^{i}.

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{\theta\wedge\tau^{M}_{n}}^{(\theta+r)\wedge\tau^{M}_{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left(\left\langle\left(\mathcal{P}\mathcal{G}_{i}\right)^{2}u^{n}_{t},u^{n}_{t}\right\rangle_{W^{m-3,2}}+\left\|\mathcal{P}\mathcal{G}_{i}u^{n}_{t}\right\|_{W^{m-3,2}}^{2}\right)dt.

We shall again use (
48
) to drop the viscous term. As with our previous estimates we shall bound by the absolute value, take the supremum and expectation followed by the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy Inequality. Estimating the nonlinear term by (
16
), and the noise with Propositions
2.3
and
2.5
, we arrive at

\mathbbm{E}\left[\sup_{r\in[0,\delta_{l}]}\left\|u^{n}_{(\theta+r)\wedge\tau^{M}_{n}]}\right\|_{W^{m-3,2}}^{2}-\left\|u^{n}_{\theta\wedge\tau^{M}_{n}]}\right\|_{W^{m-3,2}}^{2}\right]\lesssim\\
\mathbbm{E}\left[\int_{\theta\wedge\tau^{M}_{n}}^{(\theta+\delta_{l})\wedge\tau^{M}_{n}}\left(\left\|u^{n}_{t}\right\|_{W^{1,\infty}}+1\right)\left\|u^{n}_{t}\right\|_{W^{m-3,2}}^{2}dt\right]+\mathbbm{E}\left[\int_{\theta\wedge\tau^{M}_{n}}^{(\theta+\delta_{l})\wedge\tau^{M}_{n}}\left\|u^{n}_{t}\right\|_{W^{m-3,2}}^{4}dt\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Here we use the control granted by
\tau^{M}_{n}
such that both integrands are bounded by a constant, therefore

\displaystyle\mathbbm{E}\left[\sup_{r\in[0,\delta_{l}]}\left\|u^{n}_{(\theta+r)\wedge\tau^{M}_{n}}\right\|_{W^{m-3,2}}^{2}-\left\|u^{n}_{\theta\wedge\tau^{M}_{n}}\right\|_{W^{m-3,2}}^{2}\right]\lesssim\mathbbm{E}\left(\delta_{l}\right)+\mathbbm{E}\left(\delta_{l}^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)

from which the Monotone Convergence Theorem with
\delta_{l}\rightarrow 0
gives (
63
) hence the result.
∎

. Let us define the set

\mathscr{A}=\left\{\omega\in\Omega:\Theta(\omega)<T,\sup_{r\in[0,\Theta(\omega))}\left\|u_{r}(\omega)\right\|_{W^{m,2}}^{2}<\infty\right\}.

To prove item
1
it is sufficient to show that
\mathbbm{P}(\mathscr{A})=0
. Observing that

\mathscr{A}=\bigcup_{k}\mathscr{A}_{k},\qquad\mathscr{A}_{k}=\left\{\omega\in\Omega:\Theta(\omega)<T,\sup_{r\in[0,\Theta(\omega))}\left\|u_{r}(\omega)\right\|_{W^{m,2}}^{2}\leq k\right\}

then it is instead sufficient to show that
\mathbbm{P}(\mathscr{A}_{k})=0
for every
k
.
\mathbbm{P}-a.s.
in
\mathscr{A}_{k}
, as
\tau^{k+1}\leq\Theta<T
certainly
\tau^{k+1}<T
so
\tau^{k+1}=\inf\left\{s\geq 0:\sup_{r\in[0,s]}\left\|u_{r}\right\|_{W^{m,2}}^{2}\geq k+1\right\}
. As
\tau^{k+1}\leq\Theta
then
\sup_{r\in[0,\Theta)}\left\|u_{s}\right\|_{W^{m,2}}^{2}\geq k+1
which would contradict the definition of
\mathscr{A}_{k}
, hence
\mathbbm{P}(\mathscr{A}_{k})=0
as required. In the direction of item
2
let us take any stopping time
\tau\in(0,T]
such that

\sup_{r\in[0,\tau)}\left\|u_{r}\right\|_{W^{m,2}}^{2}<\infty

\mathbbm{P}-a.s.
. Firstly we claim that this implies

\sup_{r\in[0,\tau]}\left\|u_{r}\right\|_{W^{m,2}}^{2}<\infty

\mathbbm{P}-a.s.
Indeed let us take any
\omega
from the full probability set for which the supremum is finite and
u_{\cdot\wedge\tau^{R}}
is continuous for all
R
. Now

\sup_{r\in[0,\tau(\omega))}\left\|u_{r}(\omega)\right\|_{W^{m,2}}^{2}\leq M

for some
M
(dependent on
\omega
). However as
u_{\cdot\wedge\tau^{M+1}(\omega)}(\omega)
is continuous then
u_{\cdot}(\omega)
cannot explode at
\tau(\omega)
, justifying the claim. For any
R>\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left(\Omega;W^{m,2}\right)}^{2}
we have that
(u_{\cdot\wedge\tau\wedge\tau^{R}},\tau\wedge\tau^{R})
is a local
W^{m,2}_{\sigma}-
strong solution. In addition for
\mathbbm{P}-a.e.

\omega
there exists an
R
, dependent on
\omega
, such that
\tau^{R}(\omega)\geq\tau(\omega)
. Therefore,
u_{\cdot\wedge\tau}
is given as the limit of
(u_{\cdot\wedge\tau\wedge\tau^{R}})

\mathbbm{P}-a.s.
in
C\left([0,T];W^{m,2}_{\sigma}\right)
, hence retains the adaptedness and continuity of each
u_{\cdot\wedge\tau\wedge\tau^{R}}
, whilst also solving the identity (
21
) as required.

∎

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
(u,\Theta)
be the unique maximal
W^{m,2}_{\sigma}-
strong solution specified in Proposition
3.8
. At
\mathbbm{P}-a.e.

\omega
for which
\Theta(\omega)<T
, we have that

\int_{0}^{\Theta(\omega)}\left\|u_{s}(\omega)\right\|_{W^{1,\infty}}ds=\infty.

Proof.

We define, for any given
M>0
, the stopping time

\gamma^{M}:=\Theta\wedge\inf\left\{s\geq 0:\int_{0}^{s}\left\|u_{r}\right\|_{W^{1,\infty}}ds\geq M\right\}.

The proof relies on showing that for any
R>\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left(\Omega;W^{m,2}\right)}^{2}
and the stopping time
\tau^{R}
defined in (
65
), we have that

\mathbbm{E}\left(\sup_{r\in[0,\gamma^{M}\wedge\tau^{R}]}\left\|u_{r}\right\|_{W^{m,2}}^{2}\right)\leq C_{M}

(66)

where
C_{M}
is independent of
R
. Let us first consider how (
66
) will imply the result, and the prove (
66
) later. We observe that
(\tau^{R})
are
\mathbbm{P}-a.s.
monotone increasing to
\Theta
; indeed
(\tau^{R})\leq\Theta
and are clearly
\mathbbm{P}-a.s.
monotone increasing so admit a limit
\beta
. Thus
\beta\leq\Theta
, but if
\beta<\Theta
on a set of positive probability then on that set, by the definition of the maximal time, there must be a stopping time
\kappa
such that
\beta<\kappa\leq\Theta
and
(u_{\cdot\wedge\kappa},\kappa)
is a local
W^{m,2}_{\sigma}-
strong solution. However as
\beta<\kappa
then
u_{\cdot\wedge\kappa}
cannot be continuous in
W^{m,2}_{\sigma}
which provides a contradiction hence the observation. Therefore we may apply the Monotone Convergence Theorem to (
66
) to deduce that

\mathbbm{E}\left(\sup_{r\in[0,\gamma^{M}\wedge\Theta)}\left\|u_{r}\right\|_{W^{m,2}}^{2}\right)\leq C_{M}.

(67)

Of course
\gamma^{M}\wedge\Theta=\gamma^{M}
, so from (
67
) we deduce that

\sup_{r\in[0,\gamma^{M})}\left\|u_{r}\right\|_{W^{m,2}}^{2}<\infty

(68)

\mathbbm{P}-a.s.
. We now claim that

\gamma^{M}=T\wedge\inf\left\{s\geq 0:\int_{0}^{s}\left\|u_{r}\right\|_{W^{1,\infty}}ds\geq M\right\}.

(69)

On
\Omega
for which
\Theta=T
then this is trivial, and for
\Theta<T
then by Proposition
3.8
item
1
we must have that

\sup_{r\in[0,\Theta)}\left\|u_{r}\right\|_{W^{m,2}}^{2}=\infty.

If
\gamma^{M}=\Theta
then this would contradict (
68
), so on
\Omega
for which
\Theta<T
we must have that

\gamma^{M}=\inf\left\{s\geq 0:\int_{0}^{s}\left\|u_{r}\right\|_{W^{1,\infty}}ds\geq M\right\}=T\wedge\inf\left\{s\geq 0:\int_{0}^{s}\left\|u_{r}\right\|_{W^{1,\infty}}ds\geq M\right\}

as
\gamma^{M}<\Theta<T
. Therefore, the claim (
69
) is justified. Now (
68
) together with Proposition
3.8
item
2
implies that
(u_{\cdot\wedge\gamma^{M}},\gamma^{M})
is a local
W^{m,2}_{\sigma}-
strong solution. However due to the representation (
69
), then we can prove the desired blow-up criterion exactly as we proved Proposition
3.8
item
1
, with
\gamma^{M}
replacing
\tau^{R}
in (
65
).

Therefore we can prove the Lemma if we verify (
66
). Note that we cannot take energy estimates of
u
directly in
W^{m,2}_{\sigma}
as
u
does not satisfy the identity in this space. Thus we must turn to the approximating sequence used to construct the local
W^{m,2}_{\sigma}-
strong solution up until
\tau^{R}
, which was the content of Proposition
3.6
. For
(u^{n^{\prime}}_{\cdot\wedge\tau^{M^{\prime}}_{n^{\prime}}},\tau^{M^{\prime}}_{n^{\prime}})
the local
W^{m,2}_{\sigma}-
strong solutions of the equation (
20
) with initial condition
u^{n^{\prime}}_{0}=\mathcal{P}_{n^{\prime}}u_{0}
, recalling also the details from Subsection
3.4
, then
M^{\prime}
can be chosen sufficiently large such that for a subsequence relabelled from
n^{\prime}
to
n
,
\tau^{R}\leq\tau^{M^{\prime}}_{n}
and

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{r\in[0,\tau^{R}]}\left\|u_{r}-u^{n}_{r}\right\|_{W^{m,2}}^{2}=0

holds
\mathbbm{P}-a.s.
. Of course the convergence holds up until
\gamma^{M}\wedge\tau^{R}
, and we rewrite this as

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{r\in[0,T]}\left\|u_{r\wedge\tau^{R}\wedge\gamma^{M}}-u^{n}_{r\wedge\tau^{R}\wedge\gamma^{M}}\right\|_{W^{m,2}}^{2}=0

(70)

by the continuity of the processes. Unfortunately we will not be able to deduce a uniform control on the approximating sequence working only up until
\gamma^{M}
, so instead need to introduce the sequence of stopping times
(\gamma^{M+1}_{n})
defined by

\gamma^{M+1}_{n}=\tau^{R}\wedge\inf\left\{s\geq 0:\int_{0}^{s}\left\|u^{n}_{r}\right\|_{W^{1,\infty}}ds\geq M+1\right\}.

Note that use of
\tau^{R}
in this definition is only to ensure that the stopping time is well-defined, as
u^{n}
certainly exists up until
\tau^{R}
. In order to justify using these times, we first need to argue that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{r\in[0,T]}\left\|u_{r\wedge\tau^{R}\wedge\gamma^{M}}-u^{n}_{r\wedge\tau^{R}\wedge\gamma^{M}\wedge\gamma^{M+1}_{n}}\right\|_{W^{m,2}}^{2}=0

(71)

\mathbbm{P}-a.s.
. Observe that

\displaystyle\left\|u_{r\wedge\tau^{R}\wedge\gamma^{M}}-u^{n}_{r\wedge\tau^{R}\wedge\gamma^{M}\wedge\gamma^{M+1}_{n}}\right\|_{W^{m,2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq 2\left\|u_{r\wedge\tau^{R}\wedge\gamma^{M}}-u_{r\wedge\tau^{R}\wedge\gamma^{M}\wedge\gamma^{M+1}_{n}}\right\|_{W^{m,2}}^{2}+2\left\|u_{r\wedge\tau^{R}\wedge\gamma^{M}\wedge\gamma^{M+1}_{n}}-u^{n}_{r\wedge\tau^{R}\wedge\gamma^{M}\wedge\gamma^{M+1}_{n}}\right\|_{W^{m,2}}^{2}

and we deal with the two terms individually. The second is straightforward as

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{r\in[0,T]}\left\|u_{r\wedge\tau^{R}\wedge\gamma^{M}\wedge\gamma^{M+1}_{n}}-u^{n}_{r\wedge\tau^{R}\wedge\gamma^{M}\wedge\gamma^{M+1}_{n}}\right\|_{W^{m,2}}^{2}\leq\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{r\in[0,T]}\left\|u_{r\wedge\tau^{R}\wedge\gamma^{M}}-u^{n}_{r\wedge\tau^{R}\wedge\gamma^{M}}\right\|_{W^{m,2}}^{2}

which is zero due to (
70
). To deal with the first term let us consider
\omega
in the full probability set such that the convergence of (
70
) holds and the solutions
(u^{n}_{\cdot\wedge\tau^{R}})
,
u_{\cdot\wedge\tau^{R}}
are continuous. By the embedding of
W^{m,2}_{\sigma}\xhookrightarrow{}W^{1,\infty}
then the convergence also holds in
L^{1}\left([0,T];W^{1,\infty}\right)
and we can choose an
N
(dependent on
\omega
) such that for all
n\geq N
,

\int_{0}^{T}\left\|u_{s\wedge\tau^{R}\wedge\gamma^{M}}-u^{n}_{s\wedge\tau^{R}\wedge\gamma^{M}}\right\|_{W^{1,\infty}}ds<\frac{1}{2}

which implies that

\int_{0}^{\tau^{R}\wedge\gamma^{M}}\left\|u_{s}-u^{n}_{s}\right\|_{W^{1,\infty}}ds<\frac{1}{2}.

(72)

We now claim that for any
n\geq N
,
u_{r\wedge\tau^{R}\wedge\gamma^{M}}=u_{r\wedge\tau^{R}\wedge\gamma^{M}\wedge\gamma^{M+1}_{n}}
which would imply the desired convergence. The only way in which this could not be true is if
\tau^{R}\wedge\gamma^{M}\wedge\gamma^{M+1}_{n}<\tau^{R}\wedge\gamma^{M}
, which can only occur if the hitting threshold in
\gamma^{M+1}_{n}
is reached before
\tau^{R}
and
\gamma^{M}
. In particular we would have to have that

\int_{0}^{\tau^{R}\wedge\gamma^{M}}\left\|u^{n}_{s}\right\|_{W^{1,\infty}}ds\geq M+1

but given that

\int_{0}^{\tau^{R}\wedge\gamma^{M}}\left\|u_{s}\right\|_{W^{1,\infty}}ds\leq M

then this would violate (
72
) which yields the claim and hence the convergence (
71
). Moreover by the uniform control afforded by
\tau^{R}
, then we may apply the Dominated Convergence Theorem to deduce that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbbm{E}\left(\sup_{r\in[0,T]}\left\|u_{r\wedge\tau^{R}\wedge\gamma^{M}}-u^{n}_{r\wedge\tau^{R}\wedge\gamma^{M}\wedge\gamma^{M+1}_{n}}\right\|_{W^{m,2}}^{2}\right)=0

(73)

Therefore to prove (
66
) it is sufficient to show that

\mathbbm{E}\left(\sup_{r\in[0,T]}\left\|u^{n}_{r\wedge\tau^{R}\wedge\gamma^{M}\wedge\gamma^{M+1}_{n}}\right\|_{W^{m,2}}^{2}\right)=\mathbbm{E}\left(\sup_{r\in[0,\gamma^{M+1}_{n}\wedge\gamma^{M}\wedge\tau^{R}]}\left\|u^{n}_{r}\right\|_{W^{m,2}}^{2}\right)\leq C_{M}.

(74)

The proof of this fact is very close to the proof for (
45
), dropping the viscous term and using that
\left\|u^{n}_{0}\right\|_{W^{m,2}_{\sigma}}^{2}\lesssim\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{W^{m,2}_{\sigma}}^{2}.
The key difference is the role of
\gamma^{M+1}_{n}
compared to
\tau^{M}_{n}
. When we reach (
51
) we cannot simply apply a bound by
C_{M}
to
\left\|\check{u}^{n}_{s}\right\|_{W^{1,\infty}}
; instead we apply the Stochastic Grönwall Lemma
A.3
, with
\eta_{s}=\left\|\check{u}^{n}_{s}\right\|_{W^{1,\infty}}
. Note that we would have to arrive at (
51
) for given stopping times
\theta_{j}<\theta_{k}
replacing
0,t
respectively but this is inconsequential to the arguments, see the original paper [
33
] for example. We content ourselves with this as a proof of (
74
), and ultimately of the lemma.
∎

3.8
Final Steps of Theorem
1.5

We now present the final details in the proof of Theorem
1.5
, for which the hard work is now done and we rely on established machinery. Firstly we must extend the existence and uniqueness of a maximal
W^{m,2}_{\sigma}-
strong solution from Proposition
3.8
, and the blow-up criterion of Lemma
3.9
, to the case of an unbounded initial condition. A complete argument is given in [
43
] Subsection 3.7 for an abstract SPDE, where one splices the initial condition into bounded parts as seen in [
33
,
34
], and the blow-up characterisation of the maximal time is preserved. With this we justify the core statement of Theorem
1.5
and item
1
. For item
2
we use the infinite dimensional Itô-Stratonovich conversion proved in [
42
] Theorem 3.4 (Corollary 3.2), taking the spaces

V:=W^{3,2}_{\sigma},\quad H:=W^{2,2}_{\sigma},\quad U:=W^{1,2}_{\sigma},\quad X:=L^{2}_{\sigma}.

We note that the required solution regularity of
C\left([0,T];W^{2,2}_{\sigma}\right)\cap L^{2}\left([0,T];W^{3,2}_{\sigma}\right)
is implied by our regularity of
C\left([0,T];W^{3,2}_{\sigma}\right)
. With this we conclude the proof of Theorem
1.5
.

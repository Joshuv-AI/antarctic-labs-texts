Setting of an import tolerance for fenazaquin in teas
Authors: European Food Safety Authority (EFSA), Giulia Bellisai, Giovanni Bernasconi, Irene Castellan, Monica del Aguila, Lucien Ferreira, Renata Leuschner, Andrea Mioč, Marta Szot, Manuela Tiramani, Alessia Verani
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn accordance with Article 6 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005, the applicant Gowan Crop Protection Limited submitted a request to the competent national authority in Germany to set an import tolerance for the active substance fenazaquin in teas imported from India. The results of the four new residue trials submitted in support of the request, together with those previously assessed during the maximum residue level (MRL) review for the same import tolerance, on the basis of which a tentative MRL of 9 mg/kg was set, are sufficient to derive an MRL for teas at the same level. The enforcement analytical method and its independent laboratory validation (ILV) submitted were found to be adequate to control fenazaquin residues in the commodity under consideration at the validated limit of quantification (LOQ) of 0.01 mg/kg. Based on the risk assessment results, EFSA concluded that the short‐term and long‐term intake of residues of fenazaquin and its toxicologically relevant plant metabolite 2‐(4‐tert‐butylphenyl) ethanol (TBPE) resulting from the use of fenazaquin on teas imported from India according to the reported agricultural practice is unlikely to present a risk to consumer health. The consumer risk assessment for TBPE is indicative only, due to uncertainties related to missing residue data for this metabolite in commodities covered by EU‐implemented Codex maximum residue limit (CXLs). Risk managers may consider either confirming the existing MRL of 9 mg/kg or lowering the value to 3 mg/kg, in line with the MRL currently in force in the country of origin.

SUMMARY

In accordance with Article 6 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005, Gowan Crop Protection Limited submitted an application to the competent national authority in Germany (rapporteur Member State, RMS) to set an import tolerance for the active substance fenazaquin in teas.

The application, alongside the dossier containing the supporting data in IUCLID format, was submitted through the EFSA Central Submission System on 27 June 2024. The appointed RMS, Germany, assessed the dossier and declared its admissibility on 30 June 2025. Subsequently, following the implementation of the EFSA's confidentiality decision, the non‐confidential version of the dossier was published by EFSA, and a public consultation launched on the dossier. The consultation aimed to consult stakeholders and the public on the scientific data, studies and other information part of, or supporting, the submitted application, in order to identify whether other relevant scientific data or studies are available. The consultation run from 30 July 2025 to 20 August 2025. No additional data nor comments were submitted in the framework of the consultation.

At the end of the commenting period, the RMS proceeded drafting the evaluation report, in accordance with Article 8 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005, which was submitted to the European Commission and EFSA on 24 April 2026. The RMS proposed to establish a maximum residue level (MRL) for teas imported from India at the level of 9 mg/kg, which corresponds to the tentative MRL currently set in Regulation (EC) No 396/2005.

The European Commission sent a mandate to EFSA on 29 May 2026 to assess the application and the evaluation report as required by Article 10 of the MRL regulation. EFSA identified points which needed further clarification and requested the RMS to address them. The applicant provided the requested information in an updated IUCLID dossier. The additional information was duly considered by the RMS, who submitted a revised evaluation report to EFSA on 17 July 2026, which replaced the previously submitted evaluation report.

Based on the conclusions derived by EFSA in the framework of Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009, the data evaluated under previous MRL assessments and the additional data provided by the RMS in the framework of this application, the following conclusions are derived.

The metabolism of fenazaquin following foliar application was investigated in fruit crops and leafy crops. Fenazaquin was the major component of the residues in the investigated matrices (fruits, leaves). In addition, the cleavage of fenazaquin at the ether bridge was observed, leading to the formation of quinazolin‐4‐ol (4‐OHQ) and the toxicologically relevant metabolite 2‐(4‐tert‐butylphenyl) ethanol (TBPE).

Studies investigating the effect of processing on the nature of quinazoline ring‐labelled fenazaquin (hydrolysis studies) demonstrated that the active substance is readily degraded to 4‐OHQ, especially when subject to pasteurisation conditions. The fate of the phenyl ring moiety under standard hydrolysis conditions was not investigated. The review of maximum residue levels (MRLs) for the pesticide active substance in accordance with Article 12 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005 (MRL review) considered such investigation desirable, assuming that TBPE is likely to be formed. The assumption is confirmed by the available processing studies, including the tea infusion studies submitted with the present application, in which the samples were analysed for this metabolite.

As the proposed use of fenazaquin is on imported crops, and teas are permanent crops, investigations on the nature and magnitude of residues in rotational crops are not required. In addition, since teas are not used as feed items, assessment of the transfer of residues in products of animal commodities is also not required.

In previous EFSA assessments, based on the metabolic pattern identified in fruit and leafy crop metabolism studies, hydrolysis study and the toxicological relevance of metabolites, the residue definitions were proposed as ‘fenazaquin’ for enforcement. For risk assessment, two separate residue definitions were derived: (1) fenazaquin and (2) 2‐(4‐tert‐bu‐tylphenyl) ethanol (TBPE), considering that the metabolite is more toxic than the parent compound. These residue definitions are applicable to primary crops, rotational crops, processed products and are limited to fruit crops, teas and hops (leafy crops).

EFSA concluded that, for the crop assessed in this application, the metabolism of fenazaquin in primary crops and the possible degradation in processed products have been sufficiently addressed and that the previously derived residue definitions apply.

A sufficiently validated analytical method based on gas chromatography with tandem mass spectrometry (GS‐MS/MS) and its independent laboratory validation (ILV) in teas was provided. The method enables the quantification of fenazaquin residues at or above 0.01 mg/kg in the crop assessed (LOQ). The extraction efficiency of the enforcement method was not proven and would be desirable.

The four new residue trials submitted in support of the request, together with the residue trials previously assessed during the MRL review for the same import tolerance, on the basis of which the tentative MRL of 9 mg/kg was derived, provide sufficient evidence to confirm the MRL for teas at 9 mg/kg. The MRL currently in force in the country of origin (India) is 3 mg/kg for the residue definition as parent fenazaquin. It is to be noted that the information provided in this MRL application satisfactorily addresses the data gaps identified during the MRL review for teas (additional trials and validated analytical method), although it was not submitted within the legal deadline. Risk managers are kindly requested to consider either confirming the existing MRL of 9 mg/kg or lowering the value to 3 mg/kg, in line with the MRL currently in force in the country of origin.

Considering that tea is mainly consumed as a processed product, the applicant submitted the results of new studies investigating the effects of processing on the magnitude of fenazaquin residues in processed tea infusion. Samples of green and black teas were analysed for residues of fenazaquin and TBPE. Dilution of residue was observed for both compounds. Together with the results of previously assessed processing studies on tea infusion, the following processing factors were derived: −Tea green, infusion (fenazaquin): 0.29 – Tea green, infusion (TBPE): 0.35−Tea black, infusion (fenazaquin): 0.25 – Tea black, infusion (TBPE): 0.37

The toxicological profile of fenazaquin was assessed in the framework of the EU pesticides peer review and the data were sufficient to derive an acceptable daily intake (ADI) of 0.005 mg/kg body weight (bw) per day and an acute reference dose (ARfD) of 0.1 mg/kg bw. The plant metabolite TBPE, relevant for the risk assessment, is more toxic than the parent compound. Therefore, separate toxicological reference values were derived for this compound: ADI of 0.002 mg/kg bw per day and ARfD of 0.002 mg/kg bw.

The consumer risk assessment was performed separately for parent fenazaquin and its metabolite TBPE with revision 3.1 of the EFSA Pesticide Residues Intake Model (PRIMo). For the calculation of the chronic exposure, the supervised trial median residue (STMR) values derived for fenazaquin and TBPE from the old and new residue trials on teas, and the values from previous reasoned opinions for other crops were used. For fenazaquin, the STMRs derived for the CXLs implemented in the EU legislation were also used. The exposure was refined with peeling factor for citrus, melons and watermelons. Regarding plant commodities for which Codex MRLs have been implemented in the MRL legislation (e.g. subsequent to the MRL review), no information on TBPE concentrations is available, and therefore, potential residues in these commodities were not considered in the exposure calculation for TBPE. Under the assumption that the tentative existing EU MRLs not confirmed by data will be lowered to the LOQ or to an alternative value, when available, as proposed in the recent EFSA Statement of lack of confirmatory data, the contribution of pome fruit commodities was excluded from the calculation. In addition, a lower STMR was used for strawberries, and tentative input values were used for citrus, pending risk managers’ considerations.

The estimated long‐term dietary intake for fenazaquin accounted for a maximum of 15% the ADI (NL toddler diet). The short‐term exposure was calculated only for the crop under assessment and did not exceed the ARfD, accounting for a maximum of 2.1% of the ARfD for tea.

The highest chronic exposure for TBPE accounted for 5% the ADI (GEMS/Food G06 diet). The acute exposure for TBPE was calculated only for the residues in the assessed commodity and accounted for a maximum of 65.8% of the ARfD for teas. EFSA concluded that the proposed use of fenazaquin on tea will not result in a consumer exposure exceeding the health‐based guidance values (HBGVs) and therefore is unlikely to pose a risk to consumers' health. The consumer risk assessment for TBPE is indicative and affected by non‐standard uncertainty related to missing information on residue concentrations of this metabolite in commodities for which the CXLs are implemented in the EU legislation.

ASSESSMENT

The European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) received an application to set an import tolerance for the active substance fenazaquin in tea. The detailed description of the existing use of fenazaquin authorised in India in tea, which is the basis for the current MRL application, is reported in Appendix A.

Fenazaquin is the ISO common name for 4‐tert‐butylphenethyl quinazolin‐4‐yl ether (IUPAC). The chemical structures of the active substance and its main metabolites are reported in Appendix E.

Fenazaquin was evaluated in the framework of Directive 91/414/EEC 1 with Greece designated as rapporteur Member State (RMS) for the representative uses as a foliar treatment on grapes, citrus and ornamentals. The draft assessment report (DAR) prepared by the RMS has been peer reviewed by EFSA (EFSA, 2010b). Fenazaquin was approved 2 for the use as an acaricide on ornamentals in greenhouses only on 1 June 2011. In 2011, Greece received an application for amendment to the conditions of approval of the active substance fenazaquin in the framework of Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009, in order to lift the restriction and allow uses on grapes and citrus (representative uses for which RMS previously applied for) as well as uses on pome fruits and stone fruits (additional uses) to be authorised. The addendum to the DAR prepared by the RMS under this framework has been peer reviewed by EFSA (EFSA, 2013). The conditions of approval of the active substance fenazaquin in the EU have been amended allowing the use in greenhouse only by Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2018/690. Certain conditions and restrictions were kept (particular attention in protecting aquatic organisms, operators, bees; risk to consumers, in particular, from the residues generated during processing and conditions of use to avoid exposure to residues of fenazaquin with respect to crops for human and animal consumption).

The EU MRLs for fenazaquin are established in Annex II of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005. 3 The review of existing MRLs according to Article 12 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005 (MRL review) has been completed (EFSA, 2020), and the proposed modifications have been implemented in the MRL legislation. After completion of the MRL review, EFSA has issued two reasoned opinions on the modification of MRLs for fenazaquin in hops and various crops (EFSA, 2023b, 2025). The proposals from these reasoned opinions have been considered in the recent MRL regulation. 4 In addition, certain Codex maximum residue limits (CXLs) have been taken over in the EU MRL legislation (FAO and WHO, 2019; EFSA, 2021). Finally, since an application was not submitted within the legal deadline of 7 July 2023, EFSA has issued a Statement on the lack of confirmatory data for the substance (EFSA, 2026). However, EFSA recommendations and MRL options have not been taken into account by risk managers yet.

In accordance with Article 6 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005 and following the provisions set by the ‘Transparency Regulation’ (EU) 2019/1381, 5 the applicant Gowan Crop Protection Limited submitted on 27 June 2024 an application to the competent national authority in Germany, alongside the dossier containing the supporting data using the IUCLID format. The appointed RMS, Germany, assessed the dossier and declared its admissibility on 30 June 2025. Subsequently, following the implementation of the EFSA's confidentiality decision, the non‐confidential version of the dossier was published by EFSA, and a public consultation launched on the dossier. The consultation aimed to consult stakeholders and the public on the scientific data, studies and other information part of, or supporting, the submitted application, in order to identify whether other relevant scientific data or studies are available. The consultation run from 30 July 2025 to 20 August 2025. No additional data nor comments were submitted in the framework of the consultation.

At the end of the commenting period, the RMS proceeded drafting the evaluation report, in accordance with Article 8 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005, which was submitted to the European Commission and EFSA on 24 April 2026. The RMS proposed to establish maximum residue level (MRL) for teas imported from India at the level of 9 mg/kg, which corresponds to the tentative MRL currently set in Regulation (EC) No 396/2005.

The European Commission sent a mandate to EFSA on 29 May 2026 to assess the application and the evaluation report as required by Article 10 of the MRL regulation. EFSA identified points which needed further clarification and requested the RMS to address them. The applicant provided the requested information in an updated IUCLID dossier. The additional information was duly considered by the RMS who submitted a revised evaluation report to EFSA on 17 July 2026 (Germany, 2026), which replaced the previously submitted evaluation report.

EFSA based its assessment on the evaluation report submitted by the RMS (Germany, 2026), the draft assessment report (DAR) and its addenda (Greece, 2006, 2010, 2012, 2013) prepared under Directive 91/414/EEC and in the framework of Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009 for the approval and the amendment to the conditions of approval of fenazaquin, the Commission addendum review report on fenazaquin (European Commission, 2018), the conclusion on the peer review of the pesticide risk assessment of the active substance fenazaquin (EFSA, 2010b, 2013) as well as the conclusions from previous EFSA opinions on fenazaquin (EFSA, 2010a, 2018b, 2023b, 2025), including the reasoned opinion on the MRL review according to Article 12 of Regulation No 396/2005 (EFSA, 2020), the statement on the lack of MRL review confirmatory data (EFSA, 2026) and the assessment of the CXLs implemented in the EU legislation (EFSA, 2021).

For this application, the data requirements established in Regulation (EU) No 544/2011 6 and the guidance documents applicable at the date of submission of the IUCLID application are applicable (European Commission, 1997a, 1997b, 1997c, 1997d, 1997e, 1997f, 1997g, 2010, 2021, 2023a, 2023b, 2023c; OECD, 2011). The assessment is performed in accordance with the legal provisions of the Uniform Principles for the Evaluation and the Authorisation of Plant Protection Products adopted by Commission Regulation (EU) No 546/2011. 7

It is noted that there is no data available on the melliferous capacity of tea; therefore, further data were not submitted by the applicant on investigation of residues in honey and is not required, according to the data requirements applicable for the assessment of the submitted application.

As the EU pesticides’ peer review for the renewal of the active substance in accordance with Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009 is currently ongoing, the conclusions reported in this reasoned opinion may need to be reconsidered in the light of the outcome of the peer review renewal assessment.

RESIDUES IN PLANTS

Nature of residues and methods of analysis in plants

Nature of residues in primary crops

The metabolism of fenazaquin in primary crops, belonging to the group of fruits (grapes) and leafy (lettuces) crops following foliar application, has been assessed in the framework of the EU pesticides peer review and previous MRL assessment, respectively (EFSA, 2010b, 2013, 2023b). The grape studies were also assessed in the MRL review (EFSA, 2020), whereas the lettuce studies have not been peer reviewed yet. In all studies, fenazaquin was radiolabelled in the phenyl (P‐ label) and quinazoline (Q‐ label) ring of the molecule. A major proportion of the total residue was present as parent fenazaquin both in grapefruits (69% total radioactive residue (TRR) in late treatment; 29% in early treatment) and in lettuces (89.9% TRR for the P‐label study; 88.4% TRR for the Q‐label study). Further metabolites, including 4‐OHQ (up to 0.157 mg eq./kg, Q‐label grape study; not observed in the Q‐label lettuce study) and TBPE (up to 0.049 mg eq./kg, P‐label grape study and 0.026 mg eq./kg, P‐label lettuce study), were identified. The levels of other individual metabolites or fractions were below 5% TRR at harvest of the mature grapes (EFSA, 2013) and below 1.1% TRR in lettuces (EFSA, 2023b). Considering the short preharvest interval (PHI) (7 days) investigated in the lettuce metabolism study, EFSA recommended that future intended uses on leafy crops with longer PHIs should duly consider the suitability of the lettuce metabolism study (EFSA, 2023b). Data on the toxicity of the metabolite 4‐OHQ indicated that it is less toxic than the parent; on the contrary, TBPE is more toxic than fenazaquin (EFSA, 2013).

The treatment pattern used in the metabolism studies on lettuces, relevant for the crop under assessment, reflects the treatment pattern authorised on teas in the exporting country (India), also in terms of PHI (7 days). For the use on teas assessed in the present MRL application (leafy crops), EFSA concludes that the metabolic behaviour in primary crops is sufficiently addressed.

Nature of residues in rotational crops

As the use of fenazaquin under assessment is on permanent, imported crops, investigations on the nature of residues in rotational crops are not required. For completeness, information on metabolism studies in rotational crops has been reported in Appendix B.1.1.1.

Nature of residues in processed commodities

Studies investigating the nature of residues in processed commodities were assessed by the EU pesticides peer review and by the MRL review (EFSA, 2013, 2020). Studies were conducted with radiolabelled fenazaquin on the quinazoline ring (Q‐label) only, simulating representative hydrolytic conditions for pasteurisation (20 min at 90°C, pH 4), boiling/brewing/baking (60 min at 100°C, pH 5) and sterilisation (20 min at 120°C, pH 6). The studies demonstrated that fenazaquin is readily degraded, especially when subject to pasteurisation conditions. The main degradation product is metabolite 4‐OHQ (60% of the applied radioactivity).

No studies with fenazaquin labelled on phenyl moiety (P‐label) are available. The MRL review considered such investigation desirable to confirm the assumption that TBPE is likely to be formed under standard hydrolytic conditions (EFSA, 2020). This assumption was confirmed by the results of several processing studies, in which the presence of TBPE in processed products was identified.

The studies on the magnitude of residues in tea infusions submitted for the present assessment (see Section 1.2.3) provided additional confirmation on the occurrence of fenazaquin metabolite TBPE in processed tea commodities. Considering the available Q‐labelled hydrolysis study, together with the quantitative information obtained from the existing and additional processing studies on the commodity under assessment, EFSA considered the available hydrolysis study sufficient for the assessment of the nature of residues of fenazaquin in tea infusion. Further information was therefore not requested.

Analytical methods for enforcement purposes in plant commodities

A new enforcement analytical method is using N‐heptane based on gas chromatography coupled with tandem mass spectrometry (GC‐MS/MS) for the determination of fenazaquin in dried tea matrices. Samples are homogenised and extracted with a mixture of acetonitrile, acetone and water (45:45:10, v/v/v). The purified extracts are analysed by GC‐MS/MS using a capillary column, and two ion transitions are monitored. The method was validated in dried green and black tea (difficult matrices), as well as in green and black tea infusion (high water content matrices), with a validated LOQ of 0.01 mg/kg. The analytical method was validated in a separate laboratory. The independent laboratory validation (ILV) confirmed the suitability of the proposed analytical methodology for the determination of fenazaquin in dried tea and tea infusion with an LOQ of 0.01 mg/kg. Details on the validation of the analytical method and its ILV are presented in Appendix B.1.1.

Information on extraction efficiency of the proposed enforcement analytical method was not provided. According to the guidance document SANTE 2017/10632, an evaluation of the extraction efficiency would in principle be desirable for matrices which are difficult to analyse. Possible options to overcome the fact that an appropriate metabolism study is not available for the corresponding analytical sample matrix are provided (European Commission, 2023c).

EFSA concludes that a sufficiently validated analytical method is available for enforcement of fenazaquin residues in dried tea leaves and tea infusions. The method allows quantification of fenazaquin residues at or above the LOQ of 0.01 mg/kg. The extraction efficiency of the enforcement method was not proven. The absence of such data introduces additional non‐standard uncertainty in the current assessment.

Storage stability of residues in plants

Fenazaquin and its metabolites (TBPE, 4‐OHQ) were found to be stable for various time periods in four matrix categories and in some processed products (EFSA, 2010a, 2013, 2018b, 2020, 2023b, 2025). Storage stability data for high‐oil content matrices (almonds, hops) and for dry processed commodity (tea) were submitted in previous MRL applications and demonstrated stability of the three compounds over various time periods (EFSA, 2010a, 2018b, 2020, 2023b). In the framework of the MRL review and previous MRL assessment, studies on high water content matrices (tomatoes, peaches, cucumbers, melons and apples) demonstrated stability in fenazaquin for 12–13 months and in TBPE and 4‐OHQ for 12 months (EFSA, 2020, 2025). Furthermore, storage stability in high acid content matrices was demonstrated for 12 months for all three compounds (EFSA, 2013).

In the crop under assessment, tea, the stability of fenazaquin was demonstrated for at least 80 days (EFSA, 2010a, 2020).

The stability of fenazaquin and TBPE during freezer storage was addressed in the context of the current application within the residue trial studies (Germany, 2026). Tea samples were fortified with fenazaquin and TBPE at a nominal concentration of 0.1 mg/kg (10 times LOQ) and stored at −20 ± 2°C. Samples were analysed at the beginning of storage and after 70 days. Recoveries of fenazaquin were 93.4% and 91.2% in green tea and 94.1% and 92.6% in black tea at day 0 and day 70, respectively. Recovery of TBPE was 91.6% and 90.0% in green tea and 92.3% and 89.8% in black tea at day 0 and day 70, respectively. These data show that the decline during storage was limited (≤ 2.5% for both compounds). However, information on the number of analyses/replicates performed at each sampling time, as well as the individual recovery results, is not reported in the Evaluation Report (Germany, 2026). The absence of these details prevents EFSA from performing a full assessment of the validity of the information submitted for compliance with applicable guidance document and reliability of the results. Due to these limitations, the available results can be considered as indicative only.

EFSA concluded that, in tea matrices, fenazaquin residues are stable for at least 80 days, while TBPE residues can be considered indicatively stable for at least 70 days when samples are stored frozen at approximately −20°C.

The residue definition for enforcement set in Regulation (EC) No 396/2005 is identical with the one mentioned above.

Fenazaquin parent compound has been considered a good marker and was concluded for the enforcement residue definition, whereas the need to set a separate residue definition for risk assessment covering TBPE (2‐(4‐tert‐butylphenyl) ethanol) was based on toxicological considerations. Toxicological studies concluded that this metabolite is more toxic than the parent fenazaquin (EFSA, 2013).

EFSA confirms that, for the use of fenazaquin on teas according to the reported use pattern, these residue definitions are appropriate and neither modification nor further information is required.

Magnitude of residues in plants

Magnitude of residues in primary crops

In support of the present application, the applicant submitted the results of four supervised residue trials conducted on teas in India (exporting country). To complete the minimum dataset of eight residue trials, the applicant also referred to additional residue trials on teas performed in 2008 and 2012 that had previously been assessed by EFSA and were conducted in accordance with the same GAP in India (EFSA, 2010a, 2020). To be noted that these new trials address the data gap identified during the MRL review for teas (EFSA, 2020, 2026, 9 ).

The four new trials were conducted in different tea‐growing regions of India (Tamil Nadu, Kerala, West Bengal and Himachal Pradesh) in 2021. They were compliant with the reported Indian GAP (1 × 100 g/ha; PHI 7 days) and all designed as decline trials, with the sampling points at 0, 3, 7, 10 and 14 days after the foliar application.

According to the results of the new trials, at the PHI of 7, residues of fenazaquin in dried green tea leaves ranged from 0.85 to 1.5 mg/kg, while TBPE residues ranged from 0.39 to 0.43 mg/kg. In dried black tea leaves, fenazaquin residues ranged from 0.57 to 1.3 mg/kg and TBPE residues ranged from 0.19 to 0.37 mg/kg. Residues showed a decline over time in all trials. EFSA agrees with the RMS to select residue data on green tea as the more critical residues (Germany, 2026).

Samples were analysed for fenazaquin and TBPE in dried green tea leaves, in dried black tea leaves and in green tea infusion and black tea infusion for use in processing studies (see Section 1.2.3). According to the assessment of the RMS, the GC‐MS method (coded GOW‐0703V) used was sufficiently validated and fit for purpose. Information on extraction efficiency of the used analytical method was not provided. Therefore, extraction efficiency has not been formally demonstrated according to the requirements of SANTE 2017/10632. According to the guidance document, an evaluation of the extraction efficiency would in principle be desirable for matrices which are difficult to analyse (European Commission, 2023c). The absence of such data introduces additional non‐standard uncertainty in the current assessment.

According to the RMS, the samples of these residue trials were stored under conditions for which the integrity of the samples has been demonstrated (Germany, 2026). The residue trial samples were stored under frozen conditions (−20°C) for a maximum of 2 months, which is covered by the available storage stability data (Germany, 2026).

The newly submitted results on green tea combined with the data set previously assessed under the MRL review (EFSA, 2020) were used to derive a more robust MRL proposal based on 12 results and to complete the minimum TBPE data set for risk assessment purpose. As higher residues were observed in green tea than in black tea, the MRL proposal and risk assessment values were derived from the green tea data set. Based on the complete residue data package, the data are considered sufficient to derive an MRL of 9 mg/kg for fenazaquin in teas.

Magnitude of residues in rotational crops

As the use of fenazaquin assessed in this application is on permanent and imported crops, investigations on the magnitude of residues in rotational crops are not required.

Magnitude of residues in processed commodities

Teas are primarily consumed as infusion prepared from dried tea leaves with boiling water. The applicant submitted processing studies on tea in the framework of the present application (Germany, 2026). The studies investigated the transfer of residues from dried green tea leaves and from dried black tea leaves to tea infusions. The details on how the tea infusion was prepared (e.g. the amount of tea leaves, volume of water, water temperature and infusion time) were not provided in the evaluation report. The analytical method used for the determination of fenazaquin and TBPE in processed commodities, which is the same method used for the unprocessed commodity (see Section 1.2.1), was sufficiently validated and fit for purpose, with a validated LOQ of 0.01 mg/kg for both analytes. The samples were stored under frozen conditions for which the integrity of residues had been demonstrated (Germany, 2026).

Residues of fenazaquin and TBPE were determined in unprocessed and processed commodities (tea infusions). Individual processing factors (PFs) for fenazaquin and TBPE were calculated and are reported in Appendix B.1.2.3. In several cases, a higher individual PF was calculated from samples collected beyond the PHI of 7 days of the Indian GAP and it was selected for the median calculation as ‘worst’ case. It can be concluded that processing studies in tea infusion demonstrated that the process leads to a reduction of the residues. Assuming that the preparation of tea infusion was conducted as described in the Compendium of representative processing techniques investigated in regulatory studies for pesticides (Kittelmann et al., 2025), individual PFs derived from the newly submitted studies were combined with PFs previously reported in the MRL review (EFSA, 2020). The combined median PF of 0.29 (green teas) and 0.25 (black teas) for fenazaquin and median PFs of 0.35 (green teas) and 0.37 (black teas) for TBPE are calculated (Appendix B.1.2.3).

Proposed MRLs

The available data are considered sufficient to derive MRL proposals as well as risk assessment values for the commodity under evaluation (Appendix B.1.2).

The MRL proposal derived using the OECD calculator and the results of the submitted and previously assessed residue trials is of 9 mg/kg, which is higher than the MRL set in India for teas (3 mg/kg) for the same residue definition. In the residue trials on tea, the highest residue level was 5 mg/kg, which is 80% higher than the MRL set by the Indian authority.

The RMS/applicant could not provide a valid explanation, if one exists. Germany acknowledged the general principle that the MRL proposed for an import tolerance should not exceed the MRL currently in force in the country of origin (European Commission, 2021). Nevertheless, the RMS supported the proposal to confirm the tentative MRL of 9 mg/kg, which is based on the same Indian GAP and to delete the footnote currently included in the MRL Regulation as the data gaps identified by the MRL review have been addressed in the current assessment, even though this MRL differs from that established by the country of origin. The MRL of either 9 mg/kg or 3 mg/kg is therefore proposed for teas for consideration and risk management decision.

In Section 3, EFSA assessed whether residues on tea resulting from the use reported to be authorised in India are likely to pose a consumer health risk.

RESIDUES IN LIVESTOCK

Not relevant as tea is not used for feed purposes. Hence, investigations on residues in livestock are not further considered in the framework of the current evaluation.

CONSUMER RISK ASSESSMENT

EFSA performed a dietary risk assessment using revision 3.1 of the EFSA PRIMo (EFSA, 2018a, 2019). This exposure assessment model contains food consumption data for different subgroups of the EU population and allows the acute and chronic exposure assessment to be performed in accordance with the internationally agreed methodology for pesticide residues (FAO, 2016).

The health‐based guidance values for fenazaquin (RD‐RA1) used in the risk assessment (i.e. ADI of 0.005 mg/kg bw per day and ARfD value of 0.1 mg/kg bw) were derived in the framework of the EU pesticides peer review (European Commission, 2018). The plant metabolite TBPE, relevant for the risk assessment (RD‐RA2), is more toxic than the parent compound, and therefore, separate health‐based guidance values were derived for this compound: ADI of 0.002 mg/kg bw per day and ARfD of 0.002 mg/kg bw (European Commission, 2018). A separate risk assessment was performed for TBPE.

Fenazaquin

Short‐term (acute) dietary risk assessment

The short‐term exposure assessment was carried out in accordance with the internationally agreed methodology and focused on the commodity assessed in this application (FAO, 2016). The calculations were based on the highest residue (HR) derived from supervised field trials (Appendix D.1).

The short‐term exposure did not exceed the ARfD for the commodity assessed in this application and it accounted for 2.1% of the ARfD for tea (IE child diet).

Long‐term (chronic) dietary risk assessment

In the framework of the previous MRL application on various crops (EFSA, 2025), a comprehensive long‐term exposure assessment for parent fenazaquin was carried out, taking into account the existing uses of fenazaquin at EU level and existing import tolerances assessed in the MRL review (EFSA, 2020) and in EFSA assessments issued after the MRL review (EFSA, 2023b). In the assessment, the peeling factor of 0.07 was used for citrus and of 0.19 was used for melons and watermelons (EFSA, 2020). Codex MRLs (CXLs) which were implemented in the Regulation after the MRL review for tree nuts and various commodities of animal origin were also considered. To be noted that the residue definition for risk assessment in products of animal origin for CXLs is wider and includes two metabolites (EFSA, 2021). EFSA now updated this calculation with the relevant supervised trial median residue (STMR) value derived from the residue trials submitted in support of the current assessment for teas. The most recent CXLs, derived in FAO and WHO (2023) and considered in EFSA (2023a), are not taken into account for the exposure calculation as not implemented due to the EU reservation expressed for all commodities. Following the assessment of the lack of the MRL review confirmatory data and EFSA recommendations (EFSA, 2026), the lower STMR corresponding to the alternative MRL of 0.3 mg/kg was applied for strawberries. As the tentative MRL for pome fruits was not supported by the data and no valid alternative could be identified, the corresponding input values were excluded from the calculation. Pending risk management consideration, EFSA retained the STMR corresponding to the tentative MRL of 0.3 mg/kg for citrus. The input values used in the exposure calculations are summarised in Appendix D.1.

The estimated long‐term dietary intake accounted for a maximum of 15% the ADI (NL toddler diet). The contribution of residues expected in the commodity assessed in this application to the overall long‐term exposure is presented in detail in Appendix B.3.

EFSA concluded that the long‐term intake of residues of fenazaquin resulting from the existing and the reported use on teas is unlikely to present a risk to consumer health.

TBPE

Short‐term (acute) dietary risk assessment

The short‐term exposure assessment was carried out for the commodity assessed in this application, in accordance with the internationally agreed methodology (FAO, 2016). The calculations were based on the HR derived from supervised field trials (see Appendix D.1).

The short‐term exposure did not exceed the ARfD for the commodity assessed in this application and it accounted for 65.8% of the ARfD for tea.

Long‐term (chronic) dietary risk assessment

In the framework of the previous MRL application on various crops (EFSA, 2025), the long‐term consumer exposure to TBPE residues was carried out considering the available residue data from the MRL review and subsequent EFSA assessments (EFSA, 2020, 2023b). EFSA updated this calculation with the risk assessment values derived from the submitted residue trials on teas. The contribution of pome fruits to overall exposure was excluded and that of strawberries lowered, in line with the approach taken in the risk assessment for fenazaquin. Regarding plant commodities for which Codex MRLs have been implemented in the EU MRL legislation, no information on TBPE concentrations is available, and therefore, the contribution of residues in these commodities was not considered in the exposure calculation for TBPE (EFSA, 2020).

The indicative estimated long‐term dietary intake accounted for a maximum of 5% of the ADI (GEMS/Food G06 diet). The contribution of residues expected in the commodity assessed in this application to the overall long‐term exposure is presented in detail in Appendix B.3.

EFSA concluded that the long‐term intake of TBPE residues resulting from the existing and the reported use on teas is unlikely to present a risk to consumer health. The long‐term consumer risk assessment is indicative and affected by non‐standard uncertainty, because no information is available for the potential TBPE residues associated with existing CXLs for fenazaquin in plants implemented as MRLs in the EU legislation (EFSA, 2025).

CONCLUSION AND RECOMMENDATIONS

The results of the four new residue trials submitted in support of the MRL request, together with those previously assessed during the MRL review for the same import tolerance, on the basis of which a tentative MRL of 9 mg/kg was set, are sufficient to derive an MRL for teas at the same level. The MRL set in India for teas is lower: 3 mg/kg. In the residue trials on teas, residues exceeded the current Indian MRL, reaching up to 5 mg/kg (HR). It should be noted that the information provided satisfactorily addresses the data gaps identified during the MRL review for tea, although it was not submitted within the legal deadline. Risk managers are kindly requested to consider whether to set the MRL at the level established in India (3 mg/kg) or as calculated applying the OECD methodology (9 mg/kg) with the residue data available, thus confirming the tentative MRL.

It should be noted that the confirmatory data requested in the MRL review to confirm the tentatively MRLs in tea can be considered addressed by the submitted data under the present application.

EFSA concluded that the proposed use of fenazaquin on teas will not result in a consumer exposure exceeding the health‐based guidance values for fenazaquin and TBPE and therefore is unlikely to pose a risk to consumers' health. The consumer risk assessment for TBPE is affected by non‐standard uncertainty related to missing information on residue concentrations in plant crops for which the CXLs are implemented in the EU legislation and shall be regarded as indicative.

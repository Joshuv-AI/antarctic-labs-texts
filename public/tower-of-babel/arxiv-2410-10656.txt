Threefolds containing all curves are rationally connected
Sixuan Lou

Abstract
. Any smooth projective curve embeds into ℙ 3 \P^{3} . More generally, any curve embeds into a rationally connected variety of dimension at least three. We prove conversely that if every curve embeds in a variety X X , then X X contains a rationally connected subvariety of dimension at least three. In particular, “all curves embed” is a birational property for threefolds.



1.
Introduction

Let
k
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero.

A common theme in the study of algebraic varieties is to determine their
properties by studying the subvarieties contained in them. In particular, rational
curves play a central role in the birational geometry of projective varieties.
A variety
X
is
rationally connected
if for a general pair
of points of
X
there is a rational curve passing through them.
The abundance of rational curves in these varieties allows curves in them to
deform in large families. Using this observation, Sankaran has shown that
rationally connected varieties contain all curves
[
S11
]
.

In this paper, we study the converse question: what can we say about a variety
that admits a family of morphisms from curves with high variation?
Recall that the
variation
of a family of smooth curves of genus
g
is
the dimension of the image of the classifying map to the moduli stack

\mathcal{M}_{g}
.
We will show that if the variation of curves satisfies a specific lower
bound, the variety must be uniruled.

Theorem 1.1

(Theorem
2.2
)
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety of dimension
n>1
, and let
g\geq 2

be an integer. Assume
X
admits a covering family of morphisms from genus
g

curves with variation
\nu
, whose general member is birational onto its
image. If
\nu>n+g-2
, then
X
is uniruled.

Using this result as an essential ingredient, we obtain our main theorem, which
addresses the converse question for embeddings of general curves. We establish
that if a variety is swept out by embeddings of curves of sufficiently high
genus, it must contain a rationally connected subvariety of dimension at least
three.

Theorem 1.2

(Theorem
2.7
)
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety of dimension
n\geq 3
, and let
g>g(n)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\max(21,n-2,(n+1)/2)
be an integer. If
X
admits a family of
embeddings from genus
g
curves that vary maximally in moduli, then
X

contains a rationally connected subvariety of dimension at least
3
.

The proof of Theorem
2.7
proceeds by induction on the dimension of
the variety
X
. The crucial base case relies on establishing structural results
for surfaces that admit such families of morphisms.

Theorem 1.3

(Theorem
2.5
)
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective surface, and let
g\geq 2
be an integer. If
there is a covering family of morphisms from genus
g
curves with maximal
variation, whose general member is birational onto its image, then
X
is
rational. Furthermore, if
g>21
, then the general member of this family
of morphisms cannot be an embedding.

An immediate consequence of Theorem
2.7
relates to the
birational geometry of threefolds. By Theorem
2.7
, a smooth
projective threefold is rationally connected if and only if it admits an
embedding of every smooth projective curve. Since rational connectedness is a
birational invariant, it follows that the property of “containing all curves”
must also be a birational property for smooth projective threefolds.

In general, if
\pi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\widetilde{X}\to X
is a birational morphism between smooth
projective varieties, an embedding of a curve
C\into\widetilde{X}
intersecting the
exceptional locus of
\pi
does not necessarily descend to an embedding in
X
.
However, the global equivalence with rational connectedness allows us to bypass
this local obstruction entirely. If we can embed a general curve of sufficiently
high genus into
\widetilde{X}
, we can conclude that
\widetilde{X}
is rationally connected.
This implies
X
is rationally connected, and therefore, by Sankaran’s result,
X

must also contain all curves.

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
\pi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\widetilde{X}\to X
be a birational morphism between smooth projective
threefolds. If, for some
g>21
, a general curve of genus
g
embeds
into
\widetilde{X}
, then both
\widetilde{X}
and
X
are rationally connected.
Therefore, every curve has an embedding into
X
.

We will first prove Theorem
2.2
by examining the dimension of the
space of global sections of the normal sheaf
N_{f}
of a general morphism. The
result will be established using the generalization of Clifford’s theorem
bound to semistable bundles due to Brambila-Paz, Grzegorczyk and Newstead

[
BGN97
]
. We then apply Theorem
2.2
to the case of families of
surfaces and use it to study higher-dimensional cases.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
g\geq 2
and let
M
be a closed irreducible subvariety of the coarse
moduli space

of genus
g

\dim M\geq 2g-1

general member of

does not cover any smooth projective curves other than

\P^{1}
and itself.

Proof.

By
[
CGT92
]
, if
\dim M\geq 2g-1
, then a general member
C
of
M

has
\End\Jac(C)=\Z
; in particular, its Jacobian is simple. Suppose
f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}C\to D
is a non-constant morphism to another smooth projective curve

D
. The morphism induces the pullback and pushforward morphisms

f^{*}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\Jac(D)\to\Jac(C),\quad f_{*}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\Jac(C)\to\Jac(D)

with
f_{*}\circ f^{*}=[\deg f]
. Thus,
\ker f^{*}\subseteq\Jac(D)[\deg f]

is finite.

Let
A
denote the image of
f^{*}
in
\Jac(C)
. It is an abelian subvariety
of
\Jac(C)
, isogenous to
\Jac(D)
. Since
\Jac(C)
is simple, either
A=0
or
A=\Jac(C)
. If
A=0
, then
\Jac(D)=0
as well. Thus,
D\cong\P^{1}
. If
A=\Jac(C)
, then
C
and
D
have the same genus
g
.

By the Riemann-Hurwitz formula,

2g-2\geq(\deg f)(2g-g)

Since
g\geq 2
, one concludes that
\deg f=1
and
f
must be an
isomorphism.
∎

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety of dimension
n>1
, and let
g
be an
integer with
g>(n+1)/2
. If
X
admits a family of morphisms from genus

g
curves with maximal variation, whose general member
is birational onto its image, then
X
is uniruled.

Proof.

Apply
Theorem

2.2
with
\nu=3g-3
. Therefore,
X
must be
uniruled if
g>(n+1)/2
.

Remark 2.4
.

Recall that the coarse moduli space of curves
\overline{M}_{g}
is of general type
for
g\geq 24
, and has Kodaira dimension
\geq 0
for
g=23
, by the
foundational work of Harris, Mumford, and Eisenbud
[
HM82
,
EH87
]
. More
recently, Farkas, Jensen, and Payne improved this bound to show that
\overline{M}_{g}
is of general type for all
g>21

[
FJP25
]
. Since a variety with
non-negative Kodaira dimension cannot be uniruled, we conclude that the
moduli space
\overline{M}_{g}
is not uniruled for any
g>21
.

We now apply this general uniruledness criterion to establish the structural
result for surfaces.

Corollary 2.5
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective surface, and let
g\geq 2
be an integer. If
there is a covering family of morphisms from genus
g
curves with maximal
variation, whose general member is birational onto its image, then
X
is
rational. Furthermore, if
g>21
, then the general member of this family
of morphisms cannot be an embedding.

Proof.

By Theorem
2.3
, one concludes
X
is uniruled. Hence,
X
is
birationally ruled by some dominant rational map
p\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}X\dashto B
to some
smooth projective curve
B
.
Since the curves in this family vary maximally in moduli, they must dominate
B
.
Thus, from Lemma
2.1
, it follows
B
must be rational, hence
X

is a rational surface. In this case, the family of morphisms must vary in a
fixed linear system
V
. If the general member of this family of morphisms is an
embedding, we obtain a dominant rational map
V\dashto M_{g}
.
This
contradicts the fact that the moduli space
\overline{M}_{g}
is not uniruled for
g>21

(see Remark
2.4
).
∎

This structural result of curves in surfaces extends naturally to families of
surfaces.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
\pi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}X\to B
be a smooth morphism of relative dimension
2
to a
quasi-projective variety
B
of dimension
b
. Let
g
be an integer with
g>\max(21,b+1)
. Then there is no covering family of embeddings from
genus
g
curves that vary maximally in moduli to fibers of
\pi
.

Proof.

Let
\cC\to Z
be a family of embeddings from genus
g
curves that cover

X
and vary maximally in moduli. From the assumption, there is a morphism

\phi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}Z\to B
such that for each closed point
b\in B
, the restricted
family over
Z_{b}=\phi^{-1}(b)
is a covering family of embeddings from
genus
g
curves to the fiber
X_{b}=\pi^{-1}(b)
.

Since
Z
has maximal variation
3g-3
, the fiber
Z_{b}
has variation at
least
3g-3-b
. Since
g>b+1
, we have
3g-3-b>g
. Therefore,
by Theorem
2.2
, each fiber
X_{b}
is uniruled, hence must be
ruled over some smooth curve
D_{b}
. Since members of the family
Z_{b}
are
all embeddings, the family of morphisms
\cC_{b}\to Z_{b}
must also dominate

D_{b}
. Moreover, since
Z_{b}
has variation at least
3g-3-b\geq 2g-1
,
we conclude that
D_{b}
is rational by Lemma
2.1
. Hence,
X_{b}
is a
rational surface for every
b\in B
. Therefore, the family of morphisms in

Z_{b}
must vary in a fixed linear system
V_{b}
on
X_{b}
. This gives a
dominant rational map
V_{b}\dashto Z_{b}
. Since each fiber
Z_{b}
is uniruled,
the entire family
Z
is also uniruled. Since
Z
dominates
M_{g}
, this
implies that the moduli space
\overline{M}_{g}
is uniruled.
We derive a
contradiction when
g>21
(see Remark
2.4
).
∎

With these structural results established, we are now ready to
proceed with the induction to prove the main theorem for varieties of dimension
three and higher.

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety of dimension
n\geq 3
, and let
g>g(n)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\max(21,n-2,(n+1)/2)
be an integer. If
X
admits a family of
embeddings from genus
g
curves that vary maximally in moduli, then
X

contains a rationally connected subvariety of dimension at least
3
.

Proof.

We induct on the dimension of
X
. Let
\cC\to Z
denote the family of
embeddings. Let
F\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\cC\to X
denote the evaluation map. First observe
that we may assume the family of embeddings sweeps out
X
. Otherwise, it must
sweep out some subvariety
X^{\prime}\subsetneq X
of lower dimension. After
resolving singularities of
X^{\prime}
, we have a covering family of embeddings of
genus
g
curves to
\widetilde{X^{\prime}}
that vary maximally in moduli. Since
g(n)\geq g(n-1)
, by the inductive hypothesis we may reduce to the base case
where
\widetilde{X^{\prime}}
is a surface. However, when
g>21
, this is impossible by
Proposition
2.5
.

Let
\rho\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}X\dashto R(X)
be the MRC fibration. The MRC quotient
R(X)

is not uniruled by
[
GHS03
, Corollary 1.4]
. Observe that if
\dim R(X)\leq n-3
, then a very general fiber of
\rho
will be an irreducible rationally
connected subvariety of
X
, which establishes the conclusion. Therefore, we
only need to consider the cases
\dim R(X)\in\{n,n-1,n-2\}
.

(1)

If
\dim R(X)=n
, then
\rho
is birational; hence,
X
is not
uniruled. We get a contradiction using Theorem
2.3
given

g>(n+1)/2
.

(2)

Assume
\dim R(X)=n-1
. Since the fibers of
\rho
form a
family of curves with bounded dimension
\leq n-1<3g-3
,
the general member of
Z
cannot be contracted by
\rho
. Let
\eta\in Z
be a general member, and let
F_{\eta}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}C_{\eta}\to X
be the
corresponding embedding. Since
R(X)
is projective, the rational
map
\rho\circ F_{\eta}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}C_{\eta}\to X\dashto R(X)
can be
completed to a morphism. Let
D_{\eta}\subseteq R(X)
denote the
image of
\rho\circ F_{\eta}
and let
\widetilde{D}_{\eta}
denote its
normalization. Then we get a covering of curves
C_{\eta}\to\widetilde{D}_{\eta}
.

Since
Z
dominates
\cM_{g}
, the curve
C_{\eta}
is a general curve
of
\cM_{g}
. Therefore, the curve
\widetilde{D}_{\eta}
is either
\P^{1}
or
isomorphic to
C_{\eta}
by Lemma
2.1
. Assume
D_{\eta}=\P^{1}

for a general member
\eta\in Z
. Since the composition
\rho\circ F\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\cC\dashto R(X)
is dominant, this would imply
R(X)
is
uniruled, which is a contradiction. Therefore, we conclude
C_{\eta}\to R(X)
is birational onto its image for a general member
\eta\in Z
.

After possibly resolving singularities of
R(X)
, we get a covering
family of morphisms from genus
g
curves that vary maximally in
moduli whose general member is birational to its image in
R(X)
.
Hence, from Theorem
2.3
, we derive the contradiction that

R(X)
is uniruled once
g\geq n/2
.

(3)

Assume
\dim R(X)=n-2
. Then
\cC\to Z
induces a covering family
of morphisms from genus
g
curves varying maximally in moduli to
the resolution
\widetilde{R(X)}
. By the same argument as the previous
case, a general member
C_{\eta}\subseteq\cC
must map
birationally into
\widetilde{R(X)}
. Thus, we get a covering family of
morphisms from genus
g
curves with maximal variation, whose
general member is birational to its image in
\widetilde{R(X)}
.
Hence, from Theorem
2.3
, we derive the
contradiction that
R(X)
is uniruled once
g>(n-1)/2
.

Thus, a general member of
Z
is contracted by
\rho
.
Let
R(X)^{\circ}\subseteq R(X)
be the open dense subvariety such
that the restriction
\rho^{\circ}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}X^{\circ}=\rho^{-1}(R(X)^{\circ})\to R(X)^{\circ}
is a smooth morphism. After restricting

Z
to an open set, we obtain a covering family of embeddings of
genus
g
curves that vary maximally in moduli to fibers of

\rho^{\circ}
. We get a contradiction from Proposition
2.6

once
g>\max(21,n-2)
.

∎

Corollary 2.8
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective threefold. Then
X
is rationally connected
if and only if every smooth projective curve admits an embedding into
X
.

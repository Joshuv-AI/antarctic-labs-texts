Virological and metabolic outcomes after switching to dolutegravir or rilpivirine in virologically suppressed adults with HIV in Thailand: an open-label, randomised, controlled, non-inferiority trial
Authors: Rattagan Kajeekul, Narisa Ruenroengbun, Sirichai Wiwatrojanagul, Kannarin Kirdtongkum, Walinda Chantaranothai, Khairil Erwan Bin Khalid, Rujipas Sirijatuphat, Thundon Ngamprasertchai
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
SummaryBackgroundDolutegravir (DTG)-based antiretroviral therapy is widely recommended; however, weight gain and metabolic effects remain a concern. Rilpivirine (RPV) is a potential switch option for virologically suppressed individuals with HIV receiving efavirenz (EFV)-based treatment. We aimed to compare the virological and metabolic outcomes after switching from TDF/FTC/EFV to TDF/3TC/DTG or TDF/FTC plus RPV.MethodsIn this randomised, open-label, parallel-group, non-inferiority trial at Maharat Nakhon Ratchasima Hospital, Thailand, adults with HIV-1 RNA below 50 copies per mL for at least 6 months on TDF/FTC/EFV were randomly assigned 1:1 to once-daily TDF/3TC/DTG or TDF/FTC plus RPV. The primary outcome was virological non-suppression, defined as HIV-1 RNA 50 copies per mL or higher at week 48. Non-inferiority of RPV was concluded if the lower bound of the 95% CI for the risk difference (DTG minus RPV) was greater than −8 percentage points. Secondary outcome included changing in body weight, LDL cholesterol, HbA1c, and estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) over 48 weeks. This study was registered with the Thai Clinical Trial Registry (TCTR20211229003).FindingsBetween March 2022 and November 2023, 267 participants were randomly assigned to receive DTG (n = 133) or RPV (n = 134). In the per-protocol population, virological non-suppression occurred in three (3%) of 118 participants in the DTG group and five (4%) of 122 participants in the RPV group (risk difference −1.56%, 95% CI −6.08 to 2.97). Non-inferiority of RPV was demonstrated in all prespecified analysis populations using the −8% margin. Weight gain was greater with RPV than with DTG (2.3 kg vs. 1.4 kg; p = 0.035). LDL cholesterol decreased in both groups without significant between-group differences, while eGFR declined more in the DTG group.InterpretationSwitching to RPV was non-inferior to DTG for virological outcomes at 48 weeks. RPV represents an alternative for patients in whom DTG is unsuitable or not preferred. Although between-group differences in metabolic outcomes and weight gain were minimal, larger, longer-term trials are warranted to confirm these findings.FundingThis work was supported by The Royal College of Physicians of Thailand, (to R.K.). Research in context

Evidence before this study

Dolutegravir-based antiretroviral therapy (ART) is widely recommended because of its efficacy and high barrier to resistance; however, weight gain and metabolic effects remain concern. We searched PubMed for peer reviewed papers published from 1 January 2000 to 6 August 2026 using the term “HIV” AND “efavirenz” AND “dolutegravir” AND “rilpivirine”. Most studies supported virological suppression after either switching; rilpivirine generally improved lipid profiles, whereas dolutegravir was associated with possible weight gain and lower creatinine-based eGFR. Evidence were mainly cohort, short-term, or observational, with some concern for bias. To our knowledge, there is no randomised head-to-head trial comparative evidence between rilpivirine-based and dolutegravir-based switch regimens after efavirenz discontinuation is limited, particularly in Southeast Asia.

Added value of this study

This randomised, non-inferiority trial directly compared switching from TDF/FTC/EFV to either TDF/FTC plus rilpivirine or TDF/3TC/dolutegravir in virologically suppressed adults in Thailand. Rilpivirine was non-inferior to dolutegravir for virological outcomes at week 48. Between-group differences in metabolic outcomes and the clinical significance of weight gain were minimal. Consequently, larger, longer-term clinical trials are required to determine the definitive effects on these outcomes.

Implications of all the available evidence

These findings support rilpivirine as an alternative switch option in whom dolutegravir is not suitable or preferred, rather than a universally interchangeable regimen. When selecting a treatment, factors such as resistance history, drug interactions, adherence, dietary requirements, and comorbidities should be considered. This study did not address whether to maintain or replace TDF, as both groups continued with a TDF-containing backbone. Longer and larger studies are needed to confirm metabolic and renal outcomes.

Introduction

In 2019, the WHO recommended dolutegravir (DTG), a second-generation integrase strand-transfer inhibitor, as a preferred alternative to efavirenz (EFV) when combined with tenofovir disoproxil fumarate (TDF), lamivudine (3 TC), or emtricitabine (FTC) for initial and ongoing antiretroviral therapy (ART).1 DTG has a high genetic barrier to resistance and is generally better tolerated than EFV; however, weight gain and possible adverse metabolic effects after DTG-based treatment remain important clinical concerns.2, 3, 4 In contrast, studies have indicated that rilpivirine (RPV) does not cause the same degree of weight gain as DTG.5 Additionally, RPV-based regimens have been associated with a more favourable lipid profile compared to DTG-based regimens.6 However, the long-term metabolic consequences are not yet fully understood.

RPV is a non-nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitor and has shown virological efficacy in treatment-naïve and switch settings.7,8 Furthermore, the incidence of dyslipidaemia was lower in the RPV group compared to the EFV group. Previous studies have shown improved metabolic profiles after switching to TDF/FTC plus RPV in participants with suppressed HIV viral load.9, 10, 11 We aimed to determine whether switching to TDF/FTC plus RPV was non-inferior to switching to TDF/3TC/DTG for week 48 virological non-suppression and to compare metabolic and renal outcomes after switching.

Methods

This 48-week, randomised, parallel-group, open-label, controlled, non-inferiority trial was conducted at Maharat Nakhon Ratchasima Hospital, a tertiary-care and referral hospital in Nakhon Ratchasima, Thailand, from March 2022 to August 2023. Eligible participants were adults aged 18 years or older with plasma HIV-1 RNA below 50 copies per mL for at least six months before screening while receiving first-line national antiretroviral therapy (ART) consisting of tenofovir disoproxil fumarate, emtricitabine, and efavirenz (TDF/FTC/EFV). Exclusion criteria were a history of antiretroviral resistance; concomitant use of medications with potentially clinically relevant interactions with RPV or DTG; chronic kidney disease stage 3 or higher according to the Kidney Disease: Improving Global Outcomes (KDIGO) criteria12; diabetes mellitus according to the American Diabetes Association (ADA) criteria13; pre-existing dyslipidaemia14 and statin use for any indication at baseline; and pregnancy or breastfeeding. During follow-up, participants were withdrawn if creatinine clearance (CrCl) declined to less than 50 mL/min, necessitating dose adjustment or modification of the assigned tenofovir disoproxil fumarate-containing regimen,15 or if they developed a guideline-based indication for statin therapy for primary prevention—an LDL cholesterol concentration of at least 190 mg/dL, or, among adults aged 40–75 years without diabetes and with an LDL cholesterol concentration of 70–189 mg/dL, an estimated 10-year risk of atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease of at least 7.5%.14 Written informed consent was obtained from all participants before enrolment. Participants were randomly assigned (1:1) to switch to TDF/3TC/DTG or TDF/FTC plus RPV. The allocation sequence was generated using computer-generated block randomisation via Sealed Envelope, with blocks of four permuted randomly and stratified by sex. Participants assigned to the DTG group received once-daily Acriptega, comprising TDF 300 mg, 3TC 300 mg, and DTG 50 mg. Participants assigned to the RPV group received once-daily TENO-EM, comprising TDF 300 mg and FTC 200 mg, plus RPV 25 mg once daily. The trial was open-label, and participants, treating clinicians, outcome assessors, and analysts were not masked to treatment allocation.

Participants assigned to the DTG group received Acriptega (TDF 300 mg, 3TC 300 mg, and DTG 50 mg), orally once daily. Participants were evaluated at screening, baseline, and weeks 12, 24, and 48. LDL cholesterol was additionally assessed at week 4. At each scheduled visit, participants were asked about adverse effects and received adherence counselling and standard care. Body weight was measured using a validated automatic monitor (Omron HEF-702T; Omron Healthcare Co., Ltd., Kyoto, Japan). Laboratory tests included 8-h fasting glucose, HbA1c, LDL cholesterol, estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR), CD4 cell count and percentage, and plasma HIV-1 RNA, measured using the Roche Realtime PCR HIV-1 assay (Cobas 6800/8800 Systems; lower limit of detection 50 copies per mL). The investigators assessed the safety at each visit using self-reported adverse events, medical histories, and physical examinations.

The prespecified primary outcome was virological non-suppression at week 48, defined as plasma HIV-1 RNA 50 copies per mL or higher after virological suppression.16 The secondary outcomes were changes from baseline in body weight, LDL cholesterol, HbA1c, and eGFR, measured at weeks 12, 24, and 48, with LDL cholesterol also measured at week 4. Weight was measured using the same scale and by the same personnel at each visit, and body mass index (BMI) was calculated. Harm and adverse events were assessed at each visit. Genotypic HIV drug-resistance testing was done only for participants with HIV-1 RNA greater than 1000 copies per mL, reflecting local assay requirements and guideline-supported thresholds.17, 18, 19

Statistical analysis

Non-inferiority was assessed using the between-group risk difference in virological non-suppression at week 48, calculated as DTG minus RPV. Because virological non-suppression is an unfavourable outcome, non-inferiority of RPV compared with DTG was concluded if the lower bound of the 95% CI was greater than the prespecified −8 percentage-point margin.20 A stricter −4 percentage-point margin was evaluated as a supportive analysis.21 A previous switch trial by Wiriyatanakorn and Sungkanuparph22 reported virological failure rates of 4.1% and 2.4% in the switching and continuation groups, respectively, over 24 weeks. Assuming a 20% loss to follow-up, 258 participants (129 per group) were estimated to provide 90% power to demonstrate non-inferiority. The intention-to-treat (ITT) population included all participants who were randomly assigned to the groups. The modified ITT (mITT) population included participants who received at least one dose of the assigned study medication and had at least one post-baseline HIV-1 RNA measurement. The per-protocol (PP) population included participants who met all eligibility criteria, adhered to the assigned regimen, completed the week 48 visit with HIV-1 RNA data, and had no major protocol deviations or documented virological resistance during the follow-up. The PP population was the primary analysis population for the non-inferiority assessment of the primary endpoint, in accordance with guidance for non-inferiority trials; the ITT and mITT populations were analysed and non-inferiority was required to be demonstrated consistently across all three populations. The secondary outcomes were analysed using an available-case population. Specifically, the analysis included all randomised participants who received at least one dose of the study medication and provided at least one post-baseline measurement for the respective outcome. Because HIV-1 RNA and metabolic measurements were not always available at the same visits. This approach ensures that the secondary analyses reflect all available longitudinal data while maintaining clinical relevance.

Risk differences and 95% CIs were estimated using the delta method, with missing data handled according to the prespecified missing-data plan. Multiple imputation was planned if at least 5% of the scheduled HIV-1 RNA measurements were missing. If missing data were fewer than 5%, the observed (complete-case) data would be used. Secondary outcomes were analysed using generalised estimating equations (GEE) with an exchangeable correlation structure (Gaussian family, identity link) to account for within-participant correlations across repeated. The adequacy of the GEE models for secondary outcomes was assessed using the estimated within-participant correlation, comparison of results across working correlation structures, residual quantile–quantile plots, and the number of clusters; robust (sandwich) standard errors were used throughout. Analyses were conducted using Stata version 18.0 (Stata Corp, College Station, TX, USA). Details regarding the handling of missing data, the pre-specified margin, the GEE model assumptions, and study protocol are provided in Appendices A–D.

Results

Participants and baseline characteristics

From March 2022 to November 2023, 267 participants were randomly assigned to receive TDF/3TC/DTG (DTG group: n = 133) or TDF/FTC plus RPV (RPV group: n = 134), and all were included in the intention-to-treat population. After excluding participants with HIV-1 RNA 50 copies per mL or higher at baseline, newly diagnosed type 2 diabetes, or withdrawal after enrolment, 126 participants in the DTG group and 130 in the RPV group were included in the modified intention-to-treat exposed population. For the per-protocol analysis, eight additional participants were excluded from the DTG group because of loss to follow-up or missed study medication, leaving 118/133 (9%) participants in the study. In the RPV group, eight additional participants were excluded due to loss to follow-up, death from malignancy, or a treatment switch outside the study protocol after care with a non-study physician, leaving 122/134 (91%) participants in the per-protocol population (Fig. 1).

CONSORT flow diagram showing screening, randomisation, and analysis population.

Baseline demographic, clinical, immunological, renal, and metabolic characteristics were generally well balanced across the treatment groups in all analysis populations (Table 1). In the intention-to-treat population, the mean age was 38.2 years (SD 10.5) in the DTG group and 39.7 years (10.2) in the RPV group; 70 (53%) and 71 (53%) participants were male respectively. The mean CD4 cell count was 548.5 cells per mm3 (SD 250.7) and 573.3 cells per mm3 (251.1), mean BMI was 22.0 kg/m2 (4.1) and 22.4 kg/m2 (3.8), and mean eGFR was 104.8 mL/min per 1.73 m2 (15.5) and 102.6 mL/min per 1.73 m2 (15.5) in the DTG and RPV groups, respectively.

The PP population comprised 240 (90%) of the 267 randomised participants (118 [89%] of 133 in the DTG group and 122 [91.0%] of 134 in the RPV group), and the modified -ITT population comprised 256/267 (96%). At week 48, virological non-suppression was infrequent in both groups across all prespecified analysis populations (Table 2; Fig. 2). In the PP population, virological non-suppression occurred in three (3%) of 118 participants in the DTG group and five (4%) of 122 participants in the RPV group (adjusted risk difference −1.56% [95% CI −6.08 to 2.97]), DTG minus RPV. This finding was supported by the modified-ITT and ITT populations: virological non-suppression occurred in 3% (DTG) vs. 4% (RPV) in the modified−ITT (adjusted risk difference −1.22% [95% CI −5.77 to 3.32]), and 3% (DTG) vs. 5% (RPV) in ITT population (adjusted risk difference −1.59% [95% CI −6.21 to 3.03]). The lower bounds of the 95% CIs were above the prespecified −8 percentage-point margin in all analysis populations, supporting the non-inferiority of RPV to DTG. The CIs crossed zero, indicating no evidence of superiority, and the stricter supportive margin of −4 percentage points was not met.

Discussion

This randomised switch trial showed that, among virologically suppressed adults receiving long-term TDF/FTC/EFV, switching to TDF/FTC plus RPV maintained virological outcomes comparable to TDF/3TC/DTG at week 48. Non-inferiority of RPV compared with DTG was demonstrated using the prespecified −8 percentage-point margin, although the stricter −4% margin was not met. These findings suggest that RPV may be an effective alternative switch option for selected patients who are unsuitable for or unable to tolerate DTG, rather than a universally interchangeable regimen.

This study is the direct comparison of DTG and RPV after EFV discontinuation, while both groups retained a TDF-containing backbone. The unexpected greater weight gain observed in the RPV group suggests that weight change after switching ART is influenced by multifactorial. Since both groups discontinued EFV, some of the weight gain may reflect recovery from weight suppression associated with EFV.23, 24, 25 Previous pooled switch-trial data showed that weight gain was influenced by both the pre-switch and post-switch regimens, with a greater risk of substantial weight gain after switching from EFV to RPV or elvitegravir–cobicistat.23 Because RPV exposure is food dependent and the drug must be taken with a meal, this requirement could theoretically influence eating patterns, although this mechanism was not assessed in our study.26 Potential effects of DTG on appetite and metabolic regulation have also been proposed, but the underlying mechanisms remain uncertain.2 Thai and other Asian studies have reported weight gain after initiating or switching to DTG-containing regimens27,28; however, direct comparative data for RPV and DTG in Asian populations remain scarce. Because all participants were Thai, our study provides comparative evidence in Thai adults but cannot establish whether ethnicity modifies the metabolic response to either regimen. Given the small between-group difference and the 48-week follow-up, larger studies with longer follow-up are needed to determine whether the difference persists and is clinically relevant.

LDL cholesterol decreased in both treatment groups without a significant between-group difference, suggesting that lipid improvement was probably driven mainly by the discontinuation of EFV rather than by a regimen-specific advantage of DTG or RPV. The SCOLTA cohort reported favourable lipid changes after stopping EFV, particularly when EFV was replaced by RPV, and other data support improved lipid profiles after switching from EFV to DTG-based therapy.27,29 Longer follow-up is needed to determine whether these modest LDL cholesterol reductions translate into clinically meaningful reductions in cardiovascular risk.

No significant differences in HbA1c level were observed between the groups. Although some cohort studies have associated integrase inhibitor use with incident diabetes or hyperglycaemia, the evidence is inconsistent, and a systematic review and meta-analysis found no overall increased risk of diabetes with integrase inhibitor exposure, although subgroup findings varied.30,31 In this trial, switching to DTG did not appear to confer a greater short-term glycaemic risk than switching to RPV. However, diabetes was excluded at baseline, HbA1c was a secondary metabolic endpoint, and 48 weeks may be too short to detect incident diabetes or longer-term insulin resistance.

The decline in creatinine-based eGFR was observed in both groups, and there was a greater reduction in the DTG group. The observed difference in eGFR likely reflects pharmacodynamic effects on creatinine handling rather than demonstrable structural renal damage. This is because both DTG and RPV can inhibit renal tubular creatinine secretion, leading to an apparent reduction in eGFR without true impairment in glomerular filtration.32,33 At the end of our study, we found no clinically significant renal adverse events or treatment discontinuations due to renal toxicity during follow-up.

Metabolic and renal outcome analyses were based on observed cases, and missing data may introduce bias. To assess this, we conducted multiple-imputation (missing at random) and delta-adjusted (missing not at random) sensitivity analysis for the 48-week endpoint (Supplementary Table S4). The eGFR difference was highly robust, and LDL cholesterol and HbA1c showed no between-group difference under any assumption; the modest weight difference was consistent in magnitude but not in statistical significance across assumptions.

The clinical application of RPV-based switching strategy is dependent on prior NNRTI exposure and resistance history. Pre-treatment NNRTI resistance remains relatively low across Asia; national surveys reported baseline NNRTI resistance rates of 2.0–5.0% in Thailand and 9.0% in Vietnam,34,35 while RPV resistance-associated mutations were detected in approximately 3% of an ART-naive Asian cohort.36 Conversely, NNRTI resistance is frequently selected after virological failure. For instance, 89% of Thai patients experiencing failure on NNRTI-based regimens harboured at least one NNRTI resistance mutation,37 with only 31.6% of viruses following efavirenz (EFV) failure and 10.9% after nevirapine (NVP) failure remaining susceptible to RPV.38 Consistently, among patients experiencing first-line treatment failure in Vietnam, 90.7% exhibited resistance mutations, 97.7% of which were attributed to NNRTI resistance.39 Consequently, our findings are primarily applicable to virologically suppressed individuals without a history of treatment failure or documented NNRTI resistance; these data should not be extrapolated to individuals with prior NNRTI failure in the absence of resistance testing.

This study had several limitations. First, the open-label design may have influenced body weight, adherence, and lifestyle behaviours. Although both groups received identical lifestyle counselling and monitoring, bias related to treatment awareness cannot be excluded. Second, the 48-week follow-up may have been insufficient to assess longer-term metabolic and renal outcomes. Third, metabolic and renal outcome analyses were based on observed cases, with missing data handled using the methods described above. Lastly, the generalisability of our study has certain limitations. The high-risk populations, for whom treatment switching is often indicated, were excluded from the trial. Future real-world studies, including large observational cohorts or population-based evaluations, are needed to validate the effectiveness of this switching regimen in routine practice.

In conclusion, switching from TDF/FTC/EFV to TDF/FTC plus RPV maintained virological control and was non-inferior to switching to TDF/3TC/DTG in analysis. Both regimens were associated with modest metabolic changes and declines in creatinine-based eGFR, with weight gain slightly greater in the RPV group and eGFR decline slightly greater in the DTG group. These findings support rilpivirine as an alternative switch option for selected virologically suppressed patients. No clinically severe renal events or treatment discontinuations due to renal toxicity occurred over 48 weeks.

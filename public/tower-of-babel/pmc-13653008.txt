Physics informed neural network framework for non-similar nano-heat and solutal transport in a rheological fluid over a stretching cylinder
Authors: Muhammad Naveed Khan, Hameed Ullah Khan, N. Ameer Ahammad, Nurul Amira Zainal, Nadia Ayari
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Accurate prediction of non-similar momentum, thermal and solutal transport phenomena in rheological fluids is crucial for the design and optimization of advanced thermal management systems, heat exchangers, polymer processing, chemical reactors, energy conversion devices and biomedical transport processes. Motivated by these engineering applications, this study presents a Physics-Informed Neural Network (PINN) framework for analyzing magnetohydrodynamic mixed convection flow of a Jeffrey fluid over a stretching horizontal cylinder embedded in a Darcy porous medium. The mathematical model incorporates the coupled effects of magnetic field, porous resistance, thermal radiation, Brownian diffusion, thermophoresis, viscous dissipation, velocity slip, internal heat generation, and Arrhenius activation energy along with chemical reaction. Owing to the non-similar nature of the governing equations, the Local Non-Similarity method is employed to transform the governing partial differential equations into a coupled system of ordinary differential equations, which is subsequently solved using a PINN implemented in PyTorch. The suggested algorithm is evaluated and tested with the help of BVP4C solver and shows good consistency with low \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{L}^{2}$$\end{document} error value of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:3.5\times\:{10}^{-3}$$\end{document} and stable convergence throughout the training phase. The results reveal that the rise in value of magnetic parameter reduce the velocity of fluid due to the Lorentz force while increase in Darcy number improves the flow due to decrease in porous effect. The thermal radiation and heat source have a major role to play in rising the temperature profile, while the increase in Schmidt number diminish the concentration distribution. The proposed LNS-PINN framework provides a computationally efficient procedure for simulating non-similar transport problems and offers a promising computational tool for engineering design and optimization of advanced systems.

Introduction

Boundary layer behavior and heat transfer physics are of fundamental importance in industrial applications like polymer extrusion, electronic device cooling, thermal management and biomedical devices. Mathematical formulation of such problems typically results in strongly nonlinear, coupled differential equations involving complexities in terms of porous media, magnetic fields, radiation, and non-Newtonian fluid flow. Analytical solutions are not often practicable, and numerical methods i.e. Finite Difference Scheme (FDS), Finite Element Method (FEM) and spectral are computationally expensive, although very powerful, and can be plagued by problems of stability, convergence. PINN have recently emerged as an effective alternative for the above boundary layer flow and heat transfer issues. PINNs directly incorporates governing ODEs and physical constraints into the loss function of deep neural networks, whereas data-driven machine learning models are built using data. PINNs construct mesh-free approximations by applying the residuals of governing equations at the collocation points, to capture the underlying physical response with high accuracy.

The ability of PINN to apply to boundary layer flows is highly desired for simulating temperature and velocity fields of boundary layer flows under various conditions. PINN have become a significant area of scientific machine learning that combines physics-based modeling in a deep learning paradigm [1-2]. This method has been proven to be useful for various problems in fluid mechanics [3] and heat transfer [4] etc. Recent studies have increasingly concentrated on employing neural networks to approximate solutions of differential Eqs. [5-10]. Both of these techniques are now widely used in scientific machine learning, and provide a paradigm that combines both data-driven inference with physical principles. In scientific machine learning, such techniques are now used extensively to provide a paradigm that combines data-driven inference with physical principles. Although PINNs show great promise, their effectiveness in various types of problems is still under investigation. Other interesting PINN uses and applications have been explored in the literature [11-18].

Fluid flow over cylinder shapes has remained a classic problem in fluid mechanics due to its importance in boundary layer formation, drag analysis and flow separation. The geometry of cylinders occurs in many engineering applications, like bridge cables, chimneys, pipelines, aircraft fuselage, rockets and energy devices, where fluid flow and heat transfer need to be predicted accurately [19-23]. Non-Newtonian fluids have also attracted much attention for their improved thermophysical properties. Ashraf et al. [24] and Kumar et al. [25] studied the thermal transport analysis of rheological fluid over permeable stretching cylinder. Razzaq et al. [26] carried out his research on chemically reactive MHD flow of Casson fluid by artificial intelligence method. Heat transfer optimization in Williamson and Casson fluids has been done using Response Surface Methodology by Ashraf et al. [27].

The Jeffrey fluid model has gained much interest since it can characterize the relaxation and retardation properties of a number of industrially important non-Newtonian fluids. This is the reason why the model has been applied in a wide variety of practical situations such as polymer processing, lubrication, petroleum engineering, bio-transport and coating. Different aspects of Jeffrey fluid flow under diverse physical scenarios have been studied by many researchers. Zeeshan and Majeed [28] explored the impact of suction or injection and magnetic dipole on stretching surface. Madan Kumar et al. [29] used numerical simulation technique to analyze the flow characteristics of MHD Jeffrey fluid flow in a porous stretching sheet taking into account the effect of activation energy and mass diffusion [30-31] explored the phenomena of heat transfer analysis of rheological fluid through cylinder geometries.

Research gap and novelty

After a critical evaluation of the existing literature, it is evident that although Physics-Informed Neural Networks have been effectively applied to a wide range of fluid flow and heat transfer problems, no previous study has systematically investigated Jeffrey fluid flow over a stretching horizontal cylinder by integrating the Local Non-Similarity technique with a PINN framework. In particular, the recent study by Mingliang et al. [31] investigated hybrid nanofluid flow over a horizontal cylinder with Forchheimer effects using an intelligent exogenous neural network, which is a data-driven machine learning approach trained on labeled numerical data. Building upon their work, the present study adopts a PINN framework, in which the physical equations and boundary constraints are directly enforced through automatic differentiation, eliminating the dependence on labeled datasets. Additionally, the mathematical model is substantially extended by considering a stretching horizontal cylinder and incorporating the coupled momentum, energy and concentration equations together with the effects of magnetic field, Darcy porous medium, thermal radiation, Brownian motion, thermophoresis, velocity slip, internal heat generation, Arrhenius activation energy and homogeneous chemical reaction. The framework is validated against the BVP4C solver through graphical comparisons and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{L}^{2}-$$\end{document}norm error analysis demonstrating excellent accuracy and robustness. Consequently, the proposed LNS-PINN framework provides a more comprehensive and physics-guided methodology for analyzing non-similar Jeffrey nanofluid flow and coupled heat and mass transfer phenomena over a stretching horizontal cylinder.

Local Non-Similarity Method (LNS)

The LNS formulation was developed by Sparrow and his co-workers [32, 33, 34] and is a useful method for the study of boundary-layer flows that are not suitably described in single similarity coordinates. The conventional local-similarity approach is based on the assumption that some non-similar contributions have been neglected or approximated in order to simplify the mathematical representation, while the LNS formulation keeps these contributions and consider their variation in the governing boundary-layer equations.

Physics informed neural network framework

Network architecture

The transformed governing equations obtained from the second-order LNS formulation are solved using PINN framework implemented in the PyTorch. As shown in Fig. 2 the proposed PINN employs a feed-forward architecture comprising one input layer, multiple hidden layers and an output layer. The input variable is the similarity coordinate \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\eta\:$$\end{document}, whereas the network simultaneously predicts the unknown dependent variables\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\widehat{{y}_{i}}=[f\left({\eta\:}_{i}\right),\theta\:\left({\eta\:}_{i}\right),\phi\:({\eta\:}_{i}\left)\right]$$\end{document}

Training configuration and collocation points

The computational domain \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:0\le\:\eta\:\le\:{\eta\:}_{\infty\:}$$\end{document} is discretized into interior collocation points and boundary points. The collocation points are uniformly sampled throughout the computational domain to enforce the transformed model equations, whereas the boundary points located at \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\eta\:=0$$\end{document} and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\eta\:={\eta\:}_{\infty\:}$$\end{document} are used to satisfy the prescribed boundary constraints. A total number of 300 collocation points are used during training. During each training iteration, the collocation points are supplied to the neural network, and the predicted variables are differentiated using the automatic differentiation (Autograd) module available in PyTorch. This enables the exact computation of the first-, second-, and third-order derivatives required by the governing equations without employing finite-difference approximations or mesh-based discretization techniques.

Optimization procedure

Optimization of the suggested PINN is done by minimizing the loss function with the help of the Adaptive Moment Estimation (Adam) algorithm. Adam optimizer was chosen due to its adaptive learning rate which benefits from both the Momentum optimization technique and RMSProp technique for obtaining robust convergence. The fact that the Adam optimizer adjusts its learning rate for each individual network parameter made it ideal for handling the complex loss function in PINN. The neural network is composed of three hidden layers, where 80 neurons are present in each layer and the tanh activation function is used to approximate the solution field. In every optimization process, automatic differentiation in PyTorch calculates the necessary first, second and third-order partial derivatives of the predicted values with respect to the similarity variable \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\eta\:$$\end{document}. These partial derivatives are directly incorporated in the transformed governing equations to calculate the momentum, energy and concentration residuals.

The network is trained for up to 15,000 training epochs starting with the initial learning rate of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:5\times\:{10}^{-3}$$\end{document}. Gradient clipping with the maximum norm equal to 1.0 is used to increase the numerical stability of backpropagation. Additionally, the learning rate decay is performed by using ReduceLROnPlateau scheduler that automatically reduces the learning rate by half in case of no improvement in total loss for the last 1000 training epochs. The optimization procedure is terminated when the maximum number of training epochs is reached while tracking the total loss for convergence. Compared with conventional numerical methods, the proposed PINN framework provides a mesh-free computational strategy by directly incorporating the governing equations and boundary conditions into the optimization process through automatic differentiation. This enables continuous differentiable solutions and offers an efficient surrogate modeling approach for complex nonlinear transport problems.

The total loss is calculated every 200 training epochs, while the physics loss, boundary conditions loss, and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{L}^{2}$$\end{document}- errors are tracked all the time to control the learning process. The trained neural network with the lowest value of the total loss is used for further parametric analysis and compared with the reference solution generated by BVP4C solver. The proposed framework is based on the PyTorch deep-learning framework and is implemented on the available hardware platform. When possible, the code automatically takes advantage of a Graphics Processing Unit (GPU) via CUDA interface; otherwise, calculations are performed on a Central Processing Unit (CPU).

Results and discussions

The mathematical formulations, describing the present non-similar flow problem are transformed into a coupled system of ordinary differential equations using the LNS formulation with second-order truncation. The resulting nonlinear system is subsequently solved using the proposed PINN framework, while the solve_bvp solver is employed in Python scipy as the reference numerical solution for validation. The comparison of the PINN predictions and the BVP4C solutions is presented in Fig. 3(a–c), where excellent agreement is perceived for both solutions. The high overlap between both solutions suggests that the designed PINN properly simulates the nonlinear interactions in the physical system under study and is able to solve the corresponding boundary-value problem. Figure 4 demonstrates the history of the losses related to physics and boundary conditions as well as the overall loss throughout the training procedure. The losses gradually diminish and finally converge, indicating that the network fulfills both the physics laws and the boundary conditions. Furthermore, the quantitative comparison presented in Table 1 shows consistently small \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{L}^{2}-$$\end{document}error between the PINN predictions and the reference BVP4C solutions. These results confirm the high predictive accuracy, numerical stability, and robustness of the proposed PINN framework for solving nonlinear non-similar boundary layer transport problems.

Parametric analysis of the developed non-similarity model for Jeffrey fluid flow is carried out by employing the developed PINN framework. Figures 5, 6 and 7 show the influence of the magnetic field parameter, Darcy number, and heat generation parameter Q on dimensionless velocity profile. In figures, the solid lines indicate the reference numerical solution, whereas the dashed lines indicate the predicted results from the PINN framework. The closeness of the reference and PINN solutions indicates the high level of accuracy and predictability of the proposed PINN framework. Figure 5 shows that increasing M significantly suppresses the fluid velocity. This behavior is due to the Lorentz force, which acts opposite to the flow direction, producing an electromagnetic drag that increases the overall flow resistance, thereby reducing the fluid momentum. The influence of the Darcy number on the velocity distribution is presented in Fig. 6. A higher Da enhances the velocity, a larger Darcy number corresponds to a more permeable porous medium which reduces the resistance offered by the porous matrix to the fluid motion. Consequently, the momentum loss decreases allowing the fluid to accelerate. The effect of heat generation on the velocity profile is demonstrated in Fig. 7. It can be seen that with increase of Q, the velocity of the fluid increases. This is because internal heat generation causes a rise in temperature of the fluid, thus amassing the buoyancy force due to mixed convection. This helps in the flow of the fluid and upsurges its velocity. This clearly indicates that the suggested PINN model precisely identifies the physics of the underlying transport process while providing an excellent match to the numerical reference solution. This outcome is especially useful in the analysis and design of thermal systems containing electrically conducting non-Newtonian fluids where controlling the fluid motion using magnetic fields and internal heat sources is significant.

The influence of the governing physical parameters on the temperature distribution is investigated using the proposed machine learning methodology. It can be seen from Fig. 8 that the temperature of the fluid rises with a rise in M. The magnetic field creates a force called the Lorentz force which acts against the flow of the fluid and thus reduces heat transfer by convection. Consequently, there is a buildup of thermal energy which enhance the \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\theta\:\left(\eta\:\right)$$\end{document} distribution. This behavior is attributed to the volumetric generation of thermal energy within the fluid which supplements conductive and convective heat transfer thereby increasing the thermal energy stored in the fluid. The influence of the thermal radiation parameter on the temperature profile is evident in Fig. 10. As the value of the parameter Rd becomes large, the process of heat radiation becomes more dominant in the flow field leading to an increase in thermal diffusivity. This leads to the transport of more thermal energy in the boundary layer. It can be concluded from these results that the parameters magnetic field intensity, internally generated heat and heat radiation have significant effects on the thermal properties of the Jeffrey fluid flow. These results are especially useful in managing and optimizing the thermal properties of engineering systems, such as used in high-temperature heat exchangers and other porous media processing applications.

The effects of the Darcy number and Schmidt number on dimensionless concentration distribution are presented in Figs. (11–12). Figure 11 illustrate that a rise in Da enhances the concentration distribut increases with the increase in the value of Darcy number. In physical, when Da is increased, it means that there is more permeability of the porous medium, thus reducing the resistance of the flow. Such higher permeability increases the transportation of the fluid and makes the diffusion of nanoparticles in the boundary layer easy, hence the higher concentration distribution and the thickness of the concentration distribution. In Fig. 12, the effect of Sc on the concentration profile is shown. It is evident that the solutal field diminish with increase Schmidt number. This is because Sc is defined as the ratio of momentum diffusivity to mass diffusivity. Therefore, higher Sc values mean lower mass diffusivity, thereby weakening the diffusion of nanoparticles. After the above analysis, it can be seen that there is the potential of PINN to act as an alternative to conventional boundary-value solvers for complex phenomena in porous media, catalytic reactors, membrane processes and nanoparticle assisted thermal systems.

The 3D scattered plots of the PINN-predicted results are depicted in Figs. 18, 19 and 20 showing the effects of the radiation parameter Rd and the heat generation parameter Q on skin friction coefficient, Nusselt number, and Sherwood number. From the figures, it is clear that increasing Q results in the enhancement of the skin friction coefficient and Sherwood number but a decrease in the Nusselt number. With the increase in Q, there is an increase in thermal energy, hence increasing the fluid temperature while decreasing the wall temperature gradient. On the other hand, the evolution in thermal energy enhances the fluid motion due to buoyancy force, thereby increasing the velocity gradient and hence increasing skin friction coefficient. Additionally, the enhanced thermal field causes rise in the coupled heat and mass transfer process such that there is an increase in the concentration gradient at the wall along with an increase in the Sherwood number. The consistent and smooth behavior of the results obtained indicates that the proposed LNS-PINN model possesses high stability and high predictability capabilities in predicting the interaction among momentum, heat, and mass transfer processes. The insights provided by this research can be utilized for the development and optimization of advanced thermal systems such as heat exchangers based on nanofluids, solar thermal collectors, porous catalytic reactors, electronic cooling units, geothermal systems, and biological transport systems, where the regulation of the wall shear stress, heat transfer, and mass transfer becomes very important.

While the current problem can be solved via a typical boundary value solver like BVP4C, the goal of this paper is to show how the capability of solving non-similar problems via PINNs as a physics-informed machine learning framework can be exploited in practice. In contrast to typical numerical methods which should be implemented for every problem setting from scratch, a pre-trained PINN is a differentiable surrogate model that can be easily expanded on inverse modeling, parameter identification, uncertainty quantification, real-time prediction and data assimilation without having to change the numerical solver implementation. These features make PINNs an especially promising tool for digital twin, optimization, and multiphysics problems, where experimental data and governing equations should be considered in the unified computational framework. Therefore the presented method allows us to establish PINNs as a robust computational approach for simulating complicated transport processes with non-Newtonian fluids. This approach can find application in designing and optimizing of magnetic cooling systems, porous heat exchangers catalytic and geothermal energy generation, polymer processing, biomedical transport devices and advanced thermal management systems. Conclusions

The present study introduces an integrated LNS-PINN methodology for the simulation of non-similar MHD Jeffrey fluid over stretching cylinder. Due to the non-similar nature of physical model, the LNS method was employed up to the second truncation level to reduce the model PDEs to couple system of ODEs. The transformed system was subsequently solved using a PINN implemented in PyTorch, where the resulting ODEs and physical constraints were directly incorporated into the loss function through automatic differentiation. The proposed LNS-PINN framework was validated against the numerical solution through graphical comparisons, convergence histories and quantitative \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{L}^{2}$$\end{document}-norm error analysis, demonstrating excellent agreement and stable convergence.

Limitation and future work

Despite the effectiveness of the presented LNS-PINN framework that enables accurate and efficient numerical investigations of non-similar rheological flow over a stretching horizontal cylinder, there are some aspects that must be taken into account. First, the current investigation is based on certain restrictive assumptions regarding steady-state, laminar, two-dimensional flow with homogeneous characteristics and a transverse magnetic field. Second, the flow regime is modeled through the Jeffrey fluid model, whereas the thermophysical parameters of fluid are assumed to be constant. Lastly, the developed PINN-based method is compared with the results provided by the classical BVP4C solver nevertheless, experimental or high-fidelity CFD validations have not been performed.

Future research can develop the framework further to consider transient and three-dimensional transport problems, hybrid and ternary fluids, heterogeneous porous media, and variable thermophysical properties to more accurately capture realistic engineering scenarios. Additionally, future research will be directed towards creating a direct PDE-based formulation of PINNs, thus dispensing with the intermediate step of LNS transformations and providing an end-to-end solution to the governing PDEs. Future development of the framework through adaptive sampling techniques, advanced optimization techniques, inverse heat conduction analysis, and experimentation will increase its robustness, computational efficiency, and applicability to challenging multiphysics transport problems of thermal management, energy conversion, and advanced manufacturing systems.

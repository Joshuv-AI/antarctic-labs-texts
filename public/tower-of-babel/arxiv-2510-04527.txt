Quantum capacity amplification via privacy
Peixue Wu 1,2,∗ and Yunkai Wang 1,2,3 1 Institute for Quantum Computing, University of Waterloo, 200 University Avenue West, Waterloo, ON N2L 3G1, Canada 2 Department of Applied Mathematics, University of Waterloo, 200 University Avenue West, Waterloo, ON N2L 3G1, Canada 3 Perimeter Institute for Theoretical Physics 31 Caroline St N, Waterloo, ON N2L 2Y5, Canada

Abstract
We investigate superadditivity of quantum capacity through private channels whose Choi–Jamiołkowski operators are private states. This perspective links the security structure of private states to quantum capacity and clarifies the role of the shield system: information encoded in the shield system that would otherwise leak to the environment can be recycled when paired with an assisting channel, thereby boosting capacity. Our main contributions are threefold: Firstly, we develop a general framework that provides a sufficient condition for capacity amplification, which is formulated in terms of the assisting channel’s Holevo information. As examples, we give explicit, dimension and parameter dependent amplification thresholds for erasure and depolarizing channels. Secondly, assuming the Spin alignment conjecture, we derive a single-letter expression for the quantum capacity of a family of private channels that are neither degradable, anti-degradable, nor PPT; as an application, we construct channels with vanishing quantum capacity yet unbounded private capacity. Thirdly, we further analyze approximate private channels: we give an alternative proof of superactivation that extends its validity to a broader parameter regime, and, by combining amplification bounds with continuity estimates, we establish a metric separation showing that channels exhibiting capacity amplification have nonzero diamond distance from the set of anti-degradable channels, indicating that existing approximate-(anti)degradability bounds are not tight. We also revisit the computability of the regularized quantum capacity and modestly suggest that this fundamental question still remains open.

Introduction

I-A

Background and Motivation

Quantum channels, the fundamental objects describing information transmission in quantum mechanics, have attracted intensive study in a variety of contexts such as quantum computing, quantum cryptography, and quantum communication. One of the most intriguing effects for quantum channels is the super-additivity of quantum capacities given by regularized coherent information
[
26
,
27
,
10
]
. This effect enables innovative and counterintuitive phenomena, with instances demonstrated for depolarizing channels
[
26
,
5
]
; constructions based on perturbative methods
[
21
,
19
,
20
,
28
,
30
,
32
,
39
,
40
]
; and superadditivity for Gaussian channels
[
24
,
25
]
. Nevertheless, a comprehensive theory is still lacking, and fundamental questions about the scope and structure of superadditivity remain open.

In this work, we study the superadditivity of quantum capacities in the context of channels induced by private states, which provide a general framework for investigating these phenomena. Introduced in
[
15
,
16
]
, private states represent a natural quantum extension of secret classical correlations, augmented by a “shield” system. Formally, a private state
\gamma^{a_{0}b_{0}A_{0}B_{0}}
is composed of two principal components: the
a_{0}b_{0}
subsystem, designated for classical key distribution between two parties (Alice and Bob), and the
A_{0}B_{0}
subsystem, which serves to obscure these correlations from an eavesdropper. A notable special case is the
pbit
(private bit), corresponding to a two-dimensional key system
a_{0},b_{0}\cong\mathbb{C}^{2}
. Perfect private states exemplify this framework by exhibiting classical correlations on
a_{0}b_{0}
that are entirely decoupled from any eavesdropper, serving as an archetype for secure quantum communication.

Viewing a private state as the Choi–Jamiołkowski operator of a suitable quantum channel
\mathcal{N}^{A\to B}
(which we call a
private channel
) with
A=a_{0}A_{0}
and
B=b_{0}B_{0}
, allows one to translate structural features of private state
\gamma^{a_{0}b_{0}A_{0}B_{0}}
directly into statements about its quantum capacity. This
channel perspective
is powerful: it ties the security guarantees of private states to core questions in capacity theory. Intuitively, information stored in the shield leaks to the environment and is therefore useless for quantum communication. However, when combined with a second channel, the leaked information can be
recycled
to enhance end-to-end transmission. This intuition underpins the phenomena of
super-activation
,
amplification
, and
super-amplification
in the channel setting.

Super-activation
, first discovered by Smith and Yard
[
33
]
, occurs when two channels, each individually having zero quantum capacity, can be combined (via the tensor product) to produce a strictly positive capacity. If only one of the channels has positive capacity and combining them yields a strictly higher capacity than that single channel alone, we refer to the effect as
amplification
. If both channels have nonzero capacities but their combination exceeds the sum of those individual capacities, we call it
super-amplification
. For a comprehensive discussion of these effects, which underscore the profoundly non-additive nature of quantum capacity, contrasting with the classical capacity of classical channels, see Ref.
[
17
]
.

Beyond demonstrating that capacities can be nonadditive,
amplification
is practically important: it shows how to enhance the ultimate rate of reliable quantum communication by pairing channels. Prior work has typically isolated only one slice of this landscape: Ref.
[
29
]
verified superadditivity of the
maximal coherent information
(a one-shot quantity), and Ref.
[
33
]
established
superactivation
(two zero-capacity channels combining to yield positive capacity). In contrast, we rigorously present a unified framework that exhibits
super-activation
,
amplification
, and
super-amplification
within the same family of constructions, together with dimension- and parameter-dependent thresholds. This idea is closely related to the
potential capacity
of a quantum channel, introduced in Ref.
[
38
]
, asking for the maximum capacity a channel can attain when used together with an arbitrary assisting channel. We complement this general notion with concrete examples: in our constructions, we identify channels whose
potential quantum capacity
strictly exceeds their standalone quantum capacity, and we quantify the gap in terms of the shield/key parameters that appear naturally in the private state picture.

Our Contributions

We summarize our contributions in three parts.

A general framework for quantum capacity amplification

A guiding question is: for a private (or approximately private) channel
\mathcal{N}
and an arbitrary channel
\mathcal{M}
, under what conditions do we have

\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{N}\otimes\mathcal{M})\;>\;\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{N})\;+\;\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{M})\,?

(1)

Here
\mathcal{Q}(\cdot)
is the quantum capacity.

Theorem I.1

(Informal; see Theorem
III.1
)
.

A sufficient condition relating the Holevo information of
\mathcal{M}
to
\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{N})+\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{M})
implies (
1
).

We illustrate this condition for erasure and depolarizing channels, leveraging recent upper bounds on quantum capacity (e.g.,
[
11
,
42
]
) to obtain explicit, dimension- and parameter-dependent amplification thresholds.

Gap between private capacity and quantum capacity

In this section, we address a fundamental question: how large can the gap between a channel’s private and quantum capacities be? Since coherent transmission and privacy are closely related, it was initially conjectured that the two capacities would coincide. However, Horodecki et al.
[
15
]
and Smith-Yard
[
33
]
showed that certain channels are too noisy to transmit quantum information yet can still support private communication, although they did not quantify how large this gap could be. Leung et al.
[
22
]
later demonstrated that the gap can grow with the input dimension, while the quantum capacity remains bounded below by a constant, see also
[
31
]
. Building on this line of work, we construct an even stronger example where the quantum capacity vanishes while the private capacity diverges, providing a sharper manifestation of their fundamental separation.

Theorem I.2

(Section
IV-B
)
.

There exist a sequence of channels
\{\mathcal{M}_{n}=\mathcal{M}_{n}^{A_{n}\to B_{n}}\}_{n\geq 1}
such that

\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{M}_{n})=\frac{1}{n}\to 0,\qquad\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{M}_{n})=n\to\infty,

where
\mathcal{P}(\cdot)
is the private capacity.

The construction of this example relies on deriving a single-letter formula for the quantum capacity of private channels. Owing to their structure, information encoded in the shield leaks to the environment, leaving only the private subsystem to carry quantum information. By formalizing this intuition through the
Spin Alignment Conjecture
(SAC)
[
20
]
, we obtain a single-letter expression for the quantum capacity of a family of private channels that are neither degradable, anti-degradable, nor PPT.

Approximate private channels and their applications

Finally, we develop a robust, private-state–based mechanism for capacity amplification. First, we prove quantitative
amplification
bounds for
\varepsilon
–approximate private channels when paired with a quantum erasure channel (Proposition
V.3
), turning approximate privacy into positive coherent information in a controlled regime and yielding explicit assisted lower bounds on quantum capacity. This in particular provides an alternative proof of the superactivation effect
[
33
]
. Second, combining these bounds with continuity estimates, we obtain a
metric
separation: channels exhibiting capacity amplification effect are at nonzero diamond distance from the set of anti-degradable channels, clarifying the approximate-(anti)degradability bound for quantum capacities provided in Ref.
[
34
]
is loose for this class of channels. Third, we revisit the construction in Ref.
[
8
]
concerning the fundamental question of computability of the regularized quantum capacity. In addition to some further simplification of their construction, we also notice an important fact leading to the remark below:

Computability remark.

The constructions in Ref.
[
34
]
, while insightful, do
not
resolve whether the regularized quantum capacity

\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{N})=\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{k}\,\mathcal{Q}^{(1)}\!\big(\mathcal{N}^{\otimes k}\big)

is computable. By “computable” we mean that for any channel
\mathcal{N}
, there exists a finite
N\geq 1
such that

\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{N})=\frac{1}{N}\,\mathcal{Q}^{(1)}\!\big(\mathcal{N}^{\otimes N}\big)

Neither our construction nor Ref.
[
8
]
rules out this possibility. In fact, Ref.
[
8
]
shows that for each fixed
N\geq 1
there exists a (dependently constructed) channel
\mathcal{N}_{N}
with strict superadditivity at
N

\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{N}_{N})>\frac{1}{N}\,\mathcal{Q}^{(1)}\!\big(\mathcal{N}_{N}^{\otimes N}\big).

However, for such
\mathcal{N}_{N}
it remains possible that some larger
K>N
, we have

\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{N}_{N})=\frac{1}{K}\,\mathcal{Q}^{(1)}\!\big(\mathcal{N}_{N}^{\otimes K}\big).

II-A

Quantum channels and their representations

Stinespring dilation and complementary channels.

Let
A,B,E
be finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces. An isometry
V:A\to B\otimes E
(so
V^{\dagger}V=\mathbb{I}^{A}
) induces a pair of CPTP maps

\mathcal{N}^{A\to B}(\rho)=\Tr_{E}\bigl[V\rho V^{\dagger}\bigr],\qquad(\mathcal{N}^{c})^{A\to E}(\rho)=\Tr_{B}\bigl[V\rho V^{\dagger}\bigr],

(2)

called
complementary channels
. Every channel admits such a dilation, unique up to local unitaries.

Kraus representation.

A channel
\mathcal{N}^{A\to B}
admits an operator-sum (Kraus) decomposition

\mathcal{N}(X)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}K_{i}XK_{i}^{\dagger},\qquad K_{i}\in\mathcal{B}(A,B),\qquad\sum_{i=1}^{m}K_{i}^{\dagger}K_{i}=\mathbb{I}^{A}.

(3)

The minimal number
m
of Kraus operators equals the rank of the Choi operator (below).

Choi–Jamiołkowski representation.

Fix an orthonormal basis
\{\ket{i}\}_{i=0}^{d_{A}-1}
of
A
and let

\ket{\Psi}^{AA^{\prime}}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d_{A}}}\sum_{i=0}^{d_{A}-1}\ket{i}_{A}\otimes\ket{i}_{A^{\prime}},

(4)

where
A^{\prime}\cong A
. The (normalized) Choi–Jamiołkowski operator of
\mathcal{N}^{A^{\prime}\to B}
is

J_{\mathcal{N}}^{AB}:=(id_{A}\otimes\mathcal{N}^{A^{\prime}\to B})\bigl(\ket{\Psi}\bra{\Psi}^{AA^{\prime}}\bigr)\in\mathcal{B}(A\otimes B).

(5)

Equivalently, in the chosen basis,

J_{\mathcal{N}}=\frac{1}{d_{A}}\sum_{i,j=0}^{d_{A}-1}\outerproduct{i}{j}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{N}(\outerproduct{i}{j}_{A^{\prime}}).

(6)

Well-known equivalences:

\mathcal{N}\text{ CP }\Longleftrightarrow J_{\mathcal{N}}\geq 0,\qquad\mathcal{N}\text{ TP }\Longleftrightarrow\Tr_{B}(J_{\mathcal{N}})=\frac{\mathbb{I}^{A}}{d_{A}}.

(7)

(Some authors use the unnormalized Choi–Jamiołkowski operator
\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{N}}:=d_{A}J_{\mathcal{N}}
, which then satisfies
\Tr_{B}(\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{N}})=\mathbb{I}^{A}
.)

Reconstruction and reshuffling identities.

The action of
\mathcal{N}
can be recovered from its Choi–Jamiołkowski operator via

\mathcal{N}(X)=d_{A}\,\Tr_{A}\bigl[\,J_{\mathcal{N}}\,(X^{T}\otimes\mathbb{I}^{B})\,\bigr],

(8)

where
T
denotes matrix transpose in the basis of (
4
).
Conversely, if
J\in\mathcal{B}(A\otimes B)
satisfies
J\geq 0
and
\Tr_{B}(J)=\mathbb{I}^{A}/d_{A}
, then (
8
) defines a unique CPTP map with Choi–Jamiołkowski operator
J
.

From Choi–Jamiołkowski to Kraus (one convenient choice).

Let the spectral decomposition be
J_{\mathcal{N}}=\sum_{k=1}^{r}\lambda_{k}\outerproduct{\psi_k}{\psi_k}^{AB}
with
\lambda_{k}>0
and
\ket{\psi_k}\in A\otimes B
. Write

\ket{\psi_k}=\sum_{i,j}c^{k}_{ij}\,\ket{i}_{A}\otimes\ket{j}_{B}
and set

K_{k}=\sqrt{d_{A}\,\lambda_{k}}\sum_{i,j}c^{k}_{ij}\,\ket{j}_{B}\bra{i}_{A}\in\mathcal{B}(A,B).

(9)

Then
\mathcal{N}(X)=\sum_{k=1}^{r}K_{k}XK_{k}^{\dagger}
and
\sum_{k}K_{k}^{\dagger}K_{k}=\mathbb{I}^{A}
. (If you use the unnormalized Choi–Jamiołkowski operator
\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{N}}=d_{A}J_{\mathcal{N}}
, drop the factor
d_{A}
in (
9
).)

Norms and distances.

For later use we recall the diamond norm

\|\mathcal{N}-\mathcal{M}\|_{\diamond}:=\sup_{X\neq 0}\frac{\|(\mathcal{N}\otimes id_{R})(X)-(\mathcal{M}\otimes id_{R})(X)\|_{1}}{\|X\|_{1}},

(10)

where
R
is any system with
\dim R\geq d_{A}
; the supremum is attained on a purification. Contractivity under CPTP post-processing implies

\|\mathcal{N}\otimes\mathcal{E}-\mathcal{M}\otimes\mathcal{E}\|_{\diamond}\leq\|\mathcal{N}-\mathcal{M}\|_{\diamond}\qquad\text{for every CPTP }\mathcal{E}.

(11)

The Choi–Jamiołkowski –diamond relation is
\|\mathcal{N}-\mathcal{M}\|_{\diamond}\leq d_{A}\,\|J_{\mathcal{N}}-J_{\mathcal{M}}\|_{1}
.

II-B

Capacities of quantum channels and their properties

Suppose a complementary pair
(\mathcal{N},\mathcal{N}^{c})
is generated by an isometry

V_{\mathcal{N}}:A\to B\otimes E
as in (
2
). The
quantum capacity

\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{N})

is the supremum of all achievable rates for reliable quantum information transmission through
\mathcal{N}
.
By the Lloyd–Shor–Devetak (LSD) theorem
[
26

27

9
]
,
the coherent information is an achievable rate.

For any input state
\rho^{A}\in\mathcal{B}(A)
, let
\ket{\psi}^{RA}
be a purification on
R\otimes A
,
and define
\ket{\Psi}^{RBE}:=(\mathbb{I}^{R}\otimes V_{\mathcal{N}})\ket{\psi}^{RA}
.
Let
\rho^{RB}:=\Tr_{E}\outerproduct{\Psi}{\Psi}^{RBE}
and
\rho^{E}:=\Tr_{RB}\outerproduct{\Psi}{\Psi}^{RBE}
.
The coherent information is

\displaystyle I_{c}(\rho^{A},\mathcal{N})

\displaystyle:=I(R\rangle B)_{\rho^{RB}}=S(\rho^{B})-S(\rho^{RB})=S(\rho^{B})-S(\rho^{E}),

(12)

where
S(\cdot)
is the von Neumann entropy. Different choices of purification yield the same value.
For brevity we often write
S(\rho^{B})
as
S(B)
. The one-shot coherent information is

\mathcal{Q}^{(1)}(\mathcal{N})=\max_{\rho^{A}}I_{c}(\rho^{A},\mathcal{N}),

(13)

and the LSD theorem gives the regularized capacity

\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{N})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\,\mathcal{Q}^{(1)}(\mathcal{N}^{\otimes n}).

The
private information
of
\mathcal{N}
for an ensemble
\{p_{x},\rho_{x}^{A}\}
is

I_{p}(\{p_{x},\rho_{x}^{A}\},\mathcal{N}):=I(\mathcal{X};B)-I(\mathcal{X};E),\qquad\mathcal{P}^{(1)}(\mathcal{N}):=\sup_{\{p_{x},\rho_{x}^{A}\}}I_{p}(\{p_{x},\rho_{x}^{A}\},\mathcal{N}).

The private (classical) capacity
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{N})
is given by
[
9
]

\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{N})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\,\mathcal{P}^{(1)}(\mathcal{N}^{\otimes n}).

Direct sum of channels and flagged channels

\{\Phi_{k}^{A_{k}\to B_{k}}\}_{k=1}^{n}

\bigoplus_{k=1}^{n}\Phi_{k}^{A_{k}\to B_{k}}:\mathcal{B}\Bigl(\bigoplus_{k=1}^{n}A_{k}\Bigr)\longrightarrow\mathcal{B}\Bigl(\bigoplus_{k=1}^{n}B_{k}\Bigr)

Lemma II.1

(16)

If

\displaystyle=\mathrm{tr}_{A_{0}B_{0}}\Bigl(\sum_{i,j=0}^{d_{0}-1}\outerproduct{ij}{ij}^{a_{0}b_{0}}\otimes\bra{ij}^{a_{0}b_{0}}\outerproduct{\gamma}{\gamma}^{a_{0}A_{0}b_{0}B_{0}E}\ket{ij}^{a_{0}b_{0}}\Bigr)

(22)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=0}^{d_{0}-1}p_{ij}\,\outerproduct{ij}{ij}^{a_{0}b_{0}}\otimes\rho_{ij}^{E}.

(23)

Definition II.2
.

We say that
\gamma^{a_{0}b_{0}A_{0}B_{0}}
with key
a_{0}b_{0}
and shield
A_{0}B_{0}
is
secure
if the state in (
23
) factorizes with
E
:

\widetilde{\gamma}^{a_{0}b_{0}E}=\sum_{i,j=0}^{d_{0}-1}p_{ij}\,\outerproduct{ij}{ij}^{a_{0}b_{0}}\otimes\rho^{E}.

Moreover,
\gamma^{a_{0}b_{0}A_{0}B_{0}}
is a
perfect private state
if the ccq state has the form

\widetilde{\gamma}^{a_{0}b_{0}E}=\sum_{i=0}^{d_{0}-1}\frac{1}{d_{0}}\,\outerproduct{ii}{ii}^{a_{0}b_{0}}\otimes\rho^{E}.

To emphasize the key dimension, a perfect private state is called a

Now consider the quantum channel associated with a pbit. Let the maximally entangled state on

There exists a channel

Proposition II.3

Lemma II.4
.

Suppose
\gamma^{a_{0}b_{0}A_{0}B_{0}}
is a perfect pbit. Then

\displaystyle I(a_{0}\rangle b_{0}A_{0}B_{0})_{\gamma^{a_{0}b_{0}A_{0}B_{0}}}=1,

(25)

\displaystyle I(a_{0}\rangle b_{0})_{\gamma^{a_{0}b_{0}}}=1-h\Bigl(\frac{1+|c|}{2}\Bigr)\geq 0,

(26)

where

\displaystyle h(x):=-x\log_{2}x-(1-x)\log_{2}(1-x),\quad c:=\mathrm{tr}\bigl(U_{0}^{A_{0}B_{0}}\,\sigma^{A_{0}B_{0}}\,(U_{1}^{A_{0}B_{0}})^{\dagger}\bigr).

(27)

Proof.

Let
\ket{\psi}^{a_{0}b_{0}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\ket{00}+\ket{11})^{a_{0}b_{0}}
and define

U^{a_{0}b_{0}A_{0}B_{0}}:=\sum_{k=0}^{1}\outerproduct{kk}{kk}^{a_{0}b_{0}}\otimes U_{k}^{A_{0}B_{0}}
.
Then (
24

\gamma^{a_{0}b_{0}A_{0}B_{0}}=U^{a_{0}b_{0}A_{0}B_{0}}\bigl(\outerproduct{\psi}{\psi}^{a_{0}b_{0}}\otimes\sigma^{A_{0}B_{0}}\bigr)(U^{a_{0}b_{0}A_{0}B_{0}})^{\dagger}.

Moreover,

\gamma^{b_{0}A_{0}B_{0}}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=0}^{1}\outerproduct{k}{k}^{b_{0}}\otimes U_{k}^{A_{0}B_{0}}\,\sigma^{A_{0}B_{0}}\,(U_{k}^{A_{0}B_{0}})^{\dagger}.

Unitary invariance of entropy gives

I(a_{0}\rangle b_{0}A_{0}B_{0})_{\gamma}=S(\gamma^{b_{0}A_{0}B_{0}})-S(\gamma^{a_{0}b_{0}A_{0}B_{0}})=S(\sigma^{A_{0}B_{0}})+1-S(\outerproduct{\psi}{\psi}^{a_{0}b_{0}}\otimes\sigma^{A_{0}B_{0}})=1,

proving (
25
). For (

), note that

\gamma^{a_{0}b_{0}}=\frac{1}{2}\bigl(\outerproduct{00}{00}^{a_{0}b_{0}}+\outerproduct{11}{11}^{a_{0}b_{0}}+c\,\outerproduct{00}{11}^{a_{0}b_{0}}+\overline{c}\,\outerproduct{11}{00}^{a_{0}b_{0}}\bigr).

Hence
S(\gamma^{b_{0}})=h(\tfrac{1}{2})=1
and
S(\gamma^{a_{0}b_{0}})=h(\frac{1+|c|}{2})
, yielding

I(a_{0}\rangle b_{0})=S(\gamma^{b_{0}})-S(\gamma^{a_{0}b_{0}})=1-h\Bigl(\frac{1+|c|}{2}\Bigr)\geq 0.

A typical example of private states is as follows. Let
\ket{\psi_+}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\ket{00}+\ket{11})
and
\ket{\psi_-}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\ket{00}-\ket{11})

Let
F^{A_{0}B_{0}}=\sum_{i,j=0}^{d-1}\outerproduct{ij}{ji}^{A_{0}B_{0}}
be the swap operator on

\mathbb{I}^{A_{0}B_{0}}
be the identity on
A_{0}B_{0}

Define the projectors onto the symmetric and antisymmetric subspaces of

where

To see why it is a pbit, we rewrite the above state in the form (

U^{A_{0}B_{0}}
diagonalizing

into
\mathrm{diag}(\mathbb{I}_{d(d+1)/2},-\mathbb{I}_{d(d-1)/2})

P_{\mathrm{sym}}^{A_{0}B_{0}}=U^{A_{0}B_{0}}\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb{I}_{d(d+1)/2}&0\\[2.0pt]
0&0\end{pmatrix}(U^{A_{0}B_{0}})^{\dagger},\quad P_{\mathrm{asym}}^{A_{0}B_{0}}=U^{A_{0}B_{0}}\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\[2.0pt]
0&\mathbb{I}_{d(d-1)/2}\end{pmatrix}(U^{A_{0}B_{0}})^{\dagger}.

Then the standard form in (

\sigma^{A_{0}B_{0}}=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{2q}{d(d+1)}\,\mathbb{I}_{d(d+1)/2}&0\\

0&-\mathbb{I}_{d(d-1)/2}\end{pmatrix}.

III
Quantum capacity amplification for private channels

In this section, we provide a general criteria for quantum capacity amplification for private channels. We focus on the private channel
\mathcal{N}_{q,d}
induced by the private state (
28
) with
a_{0}=b_{0}=\mathbb{C}^{2}
, and
A_{0}=B_{0}=\mathbb{C}^{d}
. To be more specific, suppose
\ket{\psi}^{a_{0}a_{0}^{\prime}}
and
\ket{\Psi}^{A_{0}A_{0}^{\prime}}
are maximally entangled states on the bipartite system
a_{0}a_{0}^{\prime}
and
A_{0}A_{0}^{\prime}
respectively. The quantum channel
\mathcal{N}_{q,d}=\mathcal{N}_{q,d}^{A^{\prime}\to B}
with
A^{\prime}=a_{0}^{\prime}A_{0}^{\prime}
and
B=b_{0}B_{0}
is determined by

(id_{a_{0}A_{0}}\otimes\mathcal{N}_{q,d}^{A^{\prime}\to B})(\outerproduct{\psi}{\psi}^{a_{0}a_{0}^{\prime}}\otimes\outerproduct{\Psi}{\Psi}^{A_{0}A_{0}^{\prime}})=\gamma_{q,d}^{a_{0}b_{0}A_{0}B_{0}}.

(29)

In the matrix form, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{q,d}\begin{pmatrix}X_{00}&X_{01}\\
X_{10}&X_{11}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}q\frac{X_{00}^{T}+\tr(X_{00})I_{d}}{d+1}+(1-q)\frac{-X_{00}^{T}+\tr(X_{00})I_{d}}{d-1}&q\frac{X_{01}^{T}+\tr(X_{01})I_{d}}{d+1}-(1-q)\frac{-X_{01}^{T}+\tr(X_{01})I_{d}}{d-1}\\
q\frac{X_{10}^{T}+\tr(X_{10})I_{d}}{d+1}-(1-q)\frac{-X_{10}^{T}+\tr(X_{10})I_{d}}{d-1}&q\frac{X_{11}^{T}+\tr(X_{11})I_{d}}{d+1}+(1-q)\frac{-X_{11}^{T}+\tr(X_{11})I_{d}}{d-1}\end{pmatrix},\ X_{ij}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{C}^{d}).

(30)

A central question in this section is:

Given a quantum channel
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{M}^{A_{0}\to C_{0}}
, under which condition, we have

\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{N}_{q,d}\otimes\mathcal{M})>\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{M})+\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{N}_{q,d}).

(31)

Our main result is a sufficient condition on the channel
\mathcal{M}
and the parameters
q,d
such that (
31
) holds.

Theorem III.1
.

Suppose
\mathcal{M}^{A_{0}\to C_{0}}
is a quantum channel, denote the quantum states

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{\mathrm{sym}}_{\mathcal{M}}=\frac{1}{d_{\text{sym}}}(\mathcal{M}\otimes id_{B_{0}})(P_{\mathrm{sym}}^{A_{0}B_{0}}),\quad\mathcal{J}^{\mathrm{asym}}_{\mathcal{M}}=\frac{1}{d_{\text{asym}}}(\mathcal{M}\otimes id_{B_{0}})(P_{\mathrm{asym}}^{A_{0}B_{0}}).

(32)

Then
\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{N}_{q,d}\otimes\mathcal{M})>\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{M})+\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{N}_{q,d})
holds provided

\displaystyle 1-h(q)+H(\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{M}}^{\mathrm{sym}},\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{M}}^{\mathrm{asym}};q,1-q)>\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{N}_{q,d})+\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{M}),

(33)

where
h(q)
is the binary entropy, and

H(\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{M}}^{\mathrm{sym}},\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{M}}^{\mathrm{asym}};q,1-q)=S(q\mathcal{J}^{\mathrm{sym}}_{\mathcal{M}}+(1-q)\mathcal{J}^{\mathrm{asym}}_{\mathcal{M}})-qS(\mathcal{J}^{\mathrm{sym}}_{\mathcal{M}})-(1-q)S(\mathcal{J}^{\mathrm{asym}}_{\mathcal{M}})

is the Holevo information.

In the remaining sections, we provide the proof of the main theorem and illustrate this theorem using different channels
\mathcal{M}
.

III-A

Lower bound on the quantum capacity of the joint channel

To establish the result, we first derive a lower bound using maximally entangled state
\ket{\psi}^{a_{0}a_{0}^{\prime}}\otimes\ket{\Psi}^{A_{0}A_{0}^{\prime}}
as an ansatz state for
\mathcal{Q}^{(1)}(\mathcal{N}_{q,d}\otimes\mathcal{M})
. This gives us the following criteria:

Proposition III.2
.

Suppose
\mathcal{M}^{A_{0}\to C_{0}}
is a quantum channel, denote the quantum states

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{\mathrm{sym}}_{\mathcal{M}}=\frac{1}{d_{\mathrm{sym}}}(\mathcal{M}\otimes id_{B_{0}})(P_{\mathrm{sym}}^{A_{0}B_{0}}),\quad\mathcal{J}^{\mathrm{asym}}_{\mathcal{M}}=\frac{1}{d_{\mathrm{asym}}}(\mathcal{M}\otimes id_{B_{0}})(P_{\mathrm{asym}}^{A_{0}B_{0}}).

(34)

Then we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{N}_{q,d}\otimes\mathcal{M})

\displaystyle\geq\mathcal{Q}^{(1)}(\mathcal{N}_{q,d}\otimes\mathcal{M})

(35)

\displaystyle\geq 1-h(q)+S(q\mathcal{J}^{\mathrm{sym}}_{\mathcal{M}}+(1-q)\mathcal{J}^{\mathrm{asym}}_{\mathcal{M}})-qS(\mathcal{J}^{\mathrm{sym}}_{\mathcal{M}})-(1-q)S(\mathcal{J}^{\mathrm{asym}}_{\mathcal{M}}).

Proof.

Note that
\mathcal{Q}^{(1)}(\mathcal{N}_{q,d}\otimes\mathcal{M})\geq I_{c}(\mathcal{N}_{q,d}\otimes\mathcal{M},\rho^{A^{\prime}A_{0}})
, where the ansatz state
\rho^{A^{\prime}A_{0}}
is given by

\displaystyle\rho^{A^{\prime}A_{0}}=\tr_{a_{0}}\left(\outerproduct{\psi}{\psi}^{a_{0}a_{0}^{\prime}}\otimes\outerproduct{\Psi}{\Psi}^{A_{0}A_{0}^{\prime}}\right).

To calculate
I_{c}(\mathcal{N}_{q,d}\otimes\mathcal{M},\rho^{A^{\prime}A_{0}})
, we denote

\displaystyle\rho^{a_{0}b_{0}C_{0}B_{0}}

\displaystyle=(id_{a_{0}b_{0}B_{0}}\otimes\mathcal{M})(\gamma_{q,d}^{a_{0}b_{0}A_{0}B_{0}})

\displaystyle=q\outerproduct{\psi_+}{\psi_+}^{a_{0}b_{0}}\otimes\frac{1}{d_{\text{sym}}}(\mathcal{M}\otimes id_{B_{0}})(P_{\mathrm{sym}}^{A_{0}B_{0}})+(1-q)\outerproduct{\psi_-}{\psi_-}^{a_{0}b_{0}}\otimes\frac{1}{d_{\mathrm{asym}}}(\mathcal{M}\otimes id_{B_{0}})(P_{\mathrm{asym}}^{A_{0}B_{0}}),

thus we have

\displaystyle I_{c}(\mathcal{N}_{q,d}\otimes\mathcal{M},\rho^{A^{\prime}A_{0}})=I(a_{0}\rangle b_{0}C_{0}B_{0})_{\rho^{a_{0}b_{0}C_{0}B_{0}}}=S(\rho^{b_{0}C_{0}B_{0}})-S(\rho^{a_{0}b_{0}C_{0}B_{0}}).

Taking the partial trace, we have

\displaystyle\rho^{b_{0}C_{0}B_{0}}=\frac{\mathbb{I}_{2}}{2}\otimes\left(q\mathcal{J}^{\mathrm{sym}}_{\mathcal{M}}+(1-q)\mathcal{J}^{\mathrm{asym}}_{\mathcal{M}}\right).

The coherent information is then calculated as

\displaystyle I_{c}(\mathcal{N}_{q,d}\otimes\mathcal{M},\rho^{A^{\prime}A_{0}})

\displaystyle=S(\rho^{b_{0}C_{0}B_{0}})-S(\rho^{a_{0}b_{0}C_{0}B_{0}})

\displaystyle=1-h(q)+S(q\mathcal{J}^{\mathrm{sym}}_{\mathcal{M}}+(1-q)\mathcal{J}^{\mathrm{asym}}_{\mathcal{M}})-qS(\mathcal{J}^{\mathrm{sym}}_{\mathcal{M}})-(1-q)S(\mathcal{J}^{\mathrm{asym}}_{\mathcal{M}}).

∎

Therefore, via Proposition
III.2
we conclude the proof of Theorem
III.1
. The following subsection establishes the upper bound on the quantum capacity of
\mathcal{N}_{q,d}
.

III-B

Upper bound on the quantum capacity of each individual channel

There are many works on the upper bound of quantum capacity, an incomplete list includes
[
11
,
43
,
42
]
. A classic approach is the well-known transposition bound, which states
\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{N}^{A^{\prime}\to B})\leq\log\|T_{B}\circ\mathcal{N}^{A^{\prime}\to B}\|_{\diamond}
, see
[
14
]
. The quantity
\|T_{B}\circ\mathcal{N}^{A^{\prime}\to B}\|_{\diamond}
can be computed using the follow SDP
[
36
, Theorem 3.1]
:

\displaystyle\|T_{B}\circ\mathcal{N}^{A^{\prime}\to B}\|_{\diamond}

\displaystyle=\min\frac{1}{2}(\|Y^{A}\|_{op}+\|Z^{A}\|_{op})

(36)

\displaystyle s.t.\ Y^{AB},Z^{AB}\geq 0,

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}Y^{AB}&-\widehat{\mathcal{J}}_{T_{B}\circ\mathcal{N}^{A^{\prime}\to B}}\\
-\widehat{\mathcal{J}}_{T_{B}\circ\mathcal{N}^{A^{\prime}\to B}}&Z^{AB}\end{pmatrix}\geq 0.

Here,
\|\cdot\|_{op}
denotes the operator norm(largest singular value), and for any superoperator
\mathcal{N}
,
\widehat{\mathcal{J}}_{\mathcal{N}}:=\sum_{i,j}\outerproduct{i}{j}\otimes\mathcal{N}(\outerproduct{i}{j})
denotes the unnormalized Choi operator.
The upper bound on quantum capacities for general private channels with flagged forms is thus given as follows:

Proposition III.3
.

Suppose the channel
\mathcal{N}^{a_{0}^{\prime}A_{0}^{\prime}\to b_{0}B_{0}}
has unnormalized Choi–Jamiołkowski operator
\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{N}}
given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{N}}/2d=q\outerproduct{\psi_+}{\psi_+}^{a_{0}b_{0}}\otimes\sigma_{1}^{A_{0}B_{0}}+(1-q)\outerproduct{\psi_-}{\psi_-}^{a_{0}b_{0}}\otimes\sigma^{A_{0}B_{0}}_{2},\quad\sigma^{A_{0}B_{0}}_{1}\perp\sigma^{A_{0}B_{0}}_{2}.

Then an upper bound on the quantum capacity for
\mathcal{N}^{a_{0}A_{0}\to b_{0}B_{0}}
is

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{N})\leq\log\left(d\left\|\tr_{B_{0}}\left(\left|(q\sigma^{A_{0}B_{0}}_{1}+(1-q)\sigma^{A_{0}B_{0}}_{2})^{T_{B_{0}}}\right|+\left|(q\sigma^{A_{0}B_{0}}_{1}-(1-q)\sigma^{A_{0}B_{0}}_{2})^{T_{B_{0}}}\right|\right)\right\|_{op}\right).

(37)

Proof.

To compute (
36
), we need to construct
Y^{AB},Z^{AB}\geq 0
such that

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}Y^{AB}&-\mathcal{J}^{T_{B}}\\
-\mathcal{J}^{T_{B}}&Z^{AB}\end{pmatrix}\geq 0.

Rewriting the unnormalized Choi–Jamiołkowski operator
\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{N}}
and taking the partial transpose, one has

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{N}}^{T_{B}}/2d

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(\outerproduct{00}{00}+\outerproduct{11}{11})\otimes(q\sigma_{1}+(1-q)\sigma_{2})^{T_{B_{0}}}+\frac{1}{2}(\outerproduct{01}{10}+\outerproduct{10}{01})\otimes(q\sigma_{1}-(1-q)\sigma_{2})^{T_{B_{0}}}.

A simple way to construct
Y,Z\geq 0
is to choose

\displaystyle Y^{AB}=Z^{AB}

\displaystyle=d(\outerproduct{00}{00}+\outerproduct{11}{11})\otimes|(q\sigma_{1}+(1-q)\sigma_{2})^{T_{B_{0}}}|+d(\outerproduct{01}{01}+\outerproduct{10}{10})\otimes|(q\sigma_{1}-(1-q)\sigma_{2})^{T_{B_{0}}}|.

It is straightforward to verify that

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}Y^{AB}&-\mathcal{J}^{T_{B}}\\
-\mathcal{J}^{T_{B}}&Z^{AB}\end{pmatrix}\geq 0.

Y^{A}
is calculated as

\displaystyle Y^{A}=\tr_{b_{0}B_{0}}(Y^{AB})=d\,I_{2}\otimes\tr_{B_{0}}\left(|(q\sigma_{1}+(1-q)\sigma_{2})^{T_{B_{0}}}|+|(q\sigma_{1}-(1-q)\sigma_{2})^{T_{B_{0}}}|\right).

Then following the expression (
36
), the upper bound on the quantum capacity is given by

\displaystyle\log(\|Y^A\|_{op})=\log\left(d\left\|\tr_{B_{0}}\left(\left|(q\sigma^{A_{0}B_{0}}_{1}+(1-q)\sigma^{A_{0}B_{0}}_{2})^{T_{B_{0}}}\right|+\left|(q\sigma^{A_{0}B_{0}}_{1}-(1-q)\sigma^{A_{0}B_{0}}_{2})^{T_{B_{0}}}\right|\right)\right\|_{op}\right).

∎

As an application, we get an explicit upper bound for the quantum channel
\mathcal{N}_{q,d}
induced by the private state (
28
):

Corollary III.4
.

An upper bound of the quantum capacity of
\mathcal{N}_{q,d}
is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{N}_{q,d})\leq\log\left((d^{2}-1)(r_{0}+|r_{1}|)+|r_{0}+dr_{1}|+|r_{1}+dr_{0}|\right),

with

\displaystyle r_{0}=\frac{q}{d(d+1)}+\frac{1-q}{d(d-1)},\quad r_{1}=\frac{q}{d(d+1)}-\frac{1-q}{d(d-1)}.

Proof.

We apply Proposition
III.3
. In this case, the states are given by (
28
):

\displaystyle\sigma^{A_{0}B_{0}}_{1}=\frac{1}{d(d+1)}\sum_{i,j}\outerproduct{ij}{ij}+\outerproduct{ij}{ji},\quad\sigma^{A_{0}B_{0}}_{2}=\frac{1}{d(d-1)}\sum_{i,j}\outerproduct{ij}{ij}-\outerproduct{ij}{ji}.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle(q\sigma_{1}+(1-q)\sigma_{2})^{T_{B_{0}}}=r_{0}\mathbb{I}_{d^{2}}+d\,r_{1}\Psi^{+},\quad(q\sigma_{1}-(1-q)\sigma_{2})^{T_{B_{0}}}=r_{1}\mathbb{I}_{d^{2}}+d\,r_{0}\Psi^{+},

where
\Psi^{+}=\outerproduct{\Psi^+}{\Psi^+},\ \ket{\Psi^+}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}\ket{ii}
and

\displaystyle r_{0}=\frac{q}{d(d+1)}+\frac{1-q}{d(d-1)},\quad r_{1}=\frac{q}{d(d+1)}-\frac{1-q}{d(d-1)}.

Taking the absolute value of the matrices,

\displaystyle\left|(q\sigma_{1}+(1-q)\sigma_{2})^{T_{B_{0}}}\right|=r_{0}\mathbb{I}_{d^{2}}+(|r_{0}+d\,r_{1}|-r_{0})\Psi^{+},\quad\left|(q\sigma_{1}-(1-q)\sigma_{2})^{T_{B_{0}}}\right|=|r_{1}|\mathbb{I}_{d^{2}}+(|r_{1}+d\,r_{0}|-|r_{1}|)\Psi^{+}.

Finally, the reduced operator is given by

\displaystyle\tr_{B_{0}}\left(|(q\sigma_{1}+(1-q)\sigma_{2})^{T_{B_{0}}}|+|(q\sigma_{1}-(1-q)\sigma_{2})^{T_{B_{0}}}|\right)=\left((r_{0}+|r_{1}|)(d-\frac{1}{d})+\frac{|r_{0}+dr_{1}|+|r_{1}+dr_{0}|}{d}\right)\mathbb{I}_{d}.

Plugging it into (
37
), we conclude the proof.
∎

Examples

In this subsection, we illustrate the framework Theorem
III.1
. To proceed, given a channel
\mathcal{M}^{A_{0}\to C_{0}}
, one needs an upper bound on
\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{M})
and to compute the entropies of
\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{M}}^{\mathrm{sym}}
and
\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{M}}^{\mathrm{asym}}
, which are defined in (
32
).

Erasure channels

When
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,d}
, where
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,d}
is an erasure channel to
d+1
dimensional output with flag
\ket{e}
:

\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,d}(\rho)=(1-\lambda)\rho+\tr(\rho)\outerproduct{e}{e}.

(38)

Then one can directly compute
\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,d}}^{\mathrm{sym}}
and
\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,d}}^{\mathrm{asym}}
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,d}}^{\mathrm{sym}}=(1-\lambda)\frac{P_{\mathrm{sym}}}{d_{\mathrm{sym}}}+\lambda\outerproduct{e}{e}\otimes\frac{\mathbb{I}_{d}}{d},

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,d}}^{\mathrm{asym}}=(1-\lambda)\frac{P_{\mathrm{asym}}}{d_{\mathrm{asym}}}+\lambda\outerproduct{e}{e}\otimes\frac{\mathbb{I}_{d}}{d}.

Using the entropy formula for probabilistic mixture of orthogonal states:

S(\sum_{i}p_{i}\tau_{i})=H(\{p_{i}\})+\sum_{i}p_{i}S(\tau_{i}),

(39)

where
\{p_{i}\}
is a probability distribution and
\{\tau_{i}\}
is a set of orthogonal states, the entropy difference in (
33

IV
Gap between quantum capacity and private capacity

In this section, we investigate the fundamental question of the separation between quantum and private capacities. It has been shown that such a separation can exist
[
15
]
, and later that the gap can even be infinite
[
22
]
, where the quantum capacity remains bounded by a constant while the private capacity diverges. Here, we provide an even stronger example. Our analysis begins with the derivation of the quantum capacity of
\mathcal{N}_{q,d}
defined in (
29
). Using the
Spin Alignment Conjecture
(SAC), first proposed in
[
20
]
and reviewed in Conjecture
IV.2
, we show that the capacity is single-letter in a specific regime (that is,
\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{N})=\mathcal{Q}^{(1)}(\mathcal{N})
), even though the channel is neither degradable nor anti-degradable. Building on this result, we construct channels whose quantum capacity vanishes while their private capacity diverges, demonstrating a sharper manifestation of the fundamental separation between the two capacities.
More specifically, we construct a family of channels
\{\mathcal{M}_{n}=\mathcal{M}_{n}^{A_{n}\to B_{n}}\}_{n\geq 1}
such that

\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{M}_{n})=\frac{1}{n}\to 0,\qquad\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{M}_{n})=n\to\infty.

(42)

IV-A

Single-letter quantum capacity using Spin alignment conjecture

We choose a special
q=\frac{d+1}{2d}
in (

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}:=\mathcal{N}_{\frac{d+1}{2d},d}.

In this case, we have a simpler expression: for any density operator
\rho=\begin{pmatrix}X_{00}&X_{01}\\
X_{10}&X_{11}\end{pmatrix}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{C}^{2}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d})
, where
X_{rs}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{C}^{d}),\ r,s\in\{0,1\}

\mathcal{N}(\rho)=\frac{1}{d}\begin{pmatrix}\tr(X_{00})\mathbb{I}_{d}&X_{01}^{T}\\
X_{10}^{T}&\tr(X_{11})\mathbb{I}_{d}\end{pmatrix}=\sum_{i,j=0}^{d-1}K_{ij}\,\rho\,K_{ij}^{\dagger},\

(43)

where
K_{ij}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\left(\ket{0j}^{b_{0}B_{0}}\bra{0i}^{a_{0}A_{0}}+\ket{1i}^{b_{0}B_{0}}\bra{1j}^{a_{0}A_{0}}\right)

The complementary channel

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}^{c}(\rho)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j,i^{\prime}j^{\prime}=0}^{d-1}\tr(K_{ij} \rho K_{i'j'}^{\dagger})\outerproduct{ij}{i'j'}=\sum_{i,j,i^{\prime}j^{\prime}=0}^{d-1}\tr(K_{i'j'}^{\dagger} K_{ij} \rho)\outerproduct{ij}{i'j'}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{i,j,i^{\prime}j^{\prime}=0}^{d-1}\tr\left((\outerproduct{0i'}{0j'}+\outerproduct{1j'}{1i'})(\outerproduct{0j}{0i}+\outerproduct{1i}{1j})\rho\right)\outerproduct{ij}{i'j'}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{i,j,i^{\prime}j^{\prime}=0}^{d-1}\left(\delta_{jj^{\prime}}\tr(\ketbra{0i'}{0i}\rho)+\delta_{ii^{\prime}}\tr(\ketbra{1j'}{1j}\rho)\right)\outerproduct{ij}{i'j'}

Lemma IV.1

For the quantum channel defined in (

\mathcal{Q}^{(1)}(\mathcal{N})=\frac{1}{d}.

(45)

Proof.

Recall that

X_{10}&X_{11}\end{pmatrix}}\left[S\left(\frac{1}{d}\begin{pmatrix}\tr(X_{00})\mathbb{I}_{d}&X_{01}^{T}\\

Since the second term does not involve

(46)

In fact, this follows from the majorization relation (see

\begin{pmatrix}\tr(X_{00})\mathbb{I}_{d}&0\\

\displaystyle X_{00}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{d}+\mathbb{I}_{d}\otimes X_{11}\prec p\outerproduct{\psi_0}{\psi_0}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{d}+\mathbb{I}_{d}\otimes(1-p)\outerproduct{\psi_1}{\psi_1},

where

are arbitary pure states on
\mathbb{C}^{d}

(48)

Therefore, via (
46

48

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}^{(1)}(\mathcal{N})

\displaystyle=\max_{\rho=\begin{pmatrix}p\outerproduct{\psi_0}{\psi_0}&0\\

\displaystyle=\max_{p\in[0,1]}\left[S\left(\text{diag}\{\underbrace{\frac{p}{d},\cdots,\frac{p}{d}}_{d\text{\ many}},\underbrace{\frac{1-p}{d}\cdots,\frac{1-p}{d}}_{d\text{\ many}}\}\right)-S\left(\text{diag}\{\frac{1}{d},\underbrace{\frac{p}{d},\cdots,\frac{p}{d}}_{d-1\text{\ many}},\underbrace{\frac{1-p}{d}\cdots,\frac{1-p}{d}}_{d-1\text{\ many}}\}\right)\right]

In this subsection, we exploit the Theorem

\{\mathcal{M}_{n}=\mathcal{M}_{n}^{A_{n}\to B_{n}}\}_{n\geq 1}

\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{M}_{n})=\frac{1}{n}\to 0,\quad\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{M}_{n})=n\to\infty,

(58)

\mathcal{N}_{\frac{2d}{d+1},d}=:\mathcal{N}

is one. Intuitively, this channel is induced by a pbit and the private system has dimension two, thus the capability of send classical information privately is one bit per use:

Proposition IV.4
.

The private capacity of
\mathcal{N}
is
1
, independent of

.

Proof.

First we show that
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{N})\geq\mathcal{P}^{(1)}(\mathcal{N})\geq 1
. Recall that the private information of the channel
\mathcal{N}
for the ensemble

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{(1)}(\mathcal{N}):=\sup_{\{p_{x},\rho_{x}^{A}\}}I_{p}(\{p_{x},\rho_{x}^{A}\},\mathcal{N}),\quad I_{p}(\{p_{x},\rho_{x}^{A}\},\mathcal{N}):=I(\mathcal{X};B)-I(\mathcal{X};E).

We choose the ensemble of states
\{p_{x},\rho_{x}\}_{x=0,1}
with

and

\displaystyle\rho_{0}=\outerproduct{0}{0}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{d}/d,\quad\rho_{1}=\outerproduct{1}{1}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{d}/d.

Using the expressions for
\mathcal{N},\mathcal{N}^{c}

\displaystyle I_{p}(\{p_{x},\rho_{x}\},\mathcal{N})=I(\mathcal{X};B)-I(\mathcal{X};E)=S(B)-S(E)-(S(\mathcal{X}B)-S(\mathcal{X}E)),

\displaystyle S(B)=S(E)=\log d,\quad S(\mathcal{X}B)=\log d,\quad S(\mathcal{X}E)=1+\log d,

which implies a lower bound
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{N})\geq 1
. On the other hand, an SDP upper bound for classical capacity (thus also an upper bound for private capacity) of
\mathcal{N}^{A\to B}
is given by
\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{N})\leq\log\beta(\mathcal{N})
, see
[
35
, Theorem 11]
, where

\displaystyle\beta(\mathcal{N})=\min\tr(X^B)

(59)

\displaystyle\text{s.t.}\hskip 14.22636pt-R^{AB}\leq\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{N}}^{T_{B}}\leq R^{AB}

\displaystyle-\mathbb{I}^{A}\otimes X^{B}\leq(R^{AB})^{T_{B}}\leq\mathbb{I}^{A}\otimes X^{B}.

We claim that
\beta(\mathcal{N})\leq 2
thus we have
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{N})\leq\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{N})\leq 1
. In fact, note that the unnormalized Choi–Jamiołkowski operator is

\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{N}}=2d\,\gamma^{a_{0}b_{0}A_{0}B_{0}}_{\frac{2d}{d+1},d}=\frac{2}{d}\left(\outerproduct{\psi_+}{\psi_+}^{a_{0}b_{0}}\otimes P_{\mathrm{sym}}^{A_{0}B_{0}}+\outerproduct{\psi_-}{\psi_-}^{a_{0}b_{0}}\otimes P_{\mathrm{asym}}^{A_{0}B_{0}}\right),

then the elementary calculation shows that

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{N}}^{T_{B}}=\frac{1}{d}\left(\outerproduct{00}{00}^{a_{0}b_{0}}+\outerproduct{11}{11}^{a_{0}b_{0}}\right)\otimes\mathbb{I}^{A_{0}B_{0}}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\outerproduct{01}{10}^{a_{0}b_{0}}+\outerproduct{10}{01}^{a_{0}b_{0}}\right)\otimes\outerproduct{\Psi_+}{\Psi_+}^{A_{0}B_{0}},

where
\ket{\Psi^+}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}\ket{ii}
. Denote
P_{0}^{a_{0}b_{0}}
as the projection onto the subspace
\mathrm{span}\{\ket{00},\ket{11}\}
and
P_{1}^{a_{0}b_{0}}:=\mathbb{I}^{a_{0}b_{0}}-P_{0}^{a_{0}b_{0}}
, we choose

R^{AB}=\frac{1}{d}P_{0}^{a_{0}b_{0}}\otimes\mathbb{I}^{A_{0}B_{0}}+\frac{1}{2}P_{1}^{a_{0}b_{0}}\otimes\outerproduct{\Psi_+}{\Psi_+}^{A_{0}B_{0}},\quad X^{B}=\frac{1}{d}\mathbb{I}^{B}.

(60)

One can directly check that
-R^{AB}\leq\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{N}}^{T_{B}}\leq R^{AB},\ -\mathbb{I}^{A}\otimes X^{B}\leq(R^{AB})^{T_{B}}\leq\mathbb{I}^{A}\otimes X^{B}
and
\tr(X^B)=2d\cdot\frac{1}{d}=2
, which implies that
\beta(\mathcal{N})\leq 2
and we conclude the proof that
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{N})=1
.
∎

Corollary IV.5
.

Denote
\mathcal{M}_{n}=\mathcal{N}^{\otimes n}
and
d=n^{2}
, we have

\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{M}_{n})=\frac{1}{n},\quad\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{M}_{n})=n.

(61)

Proof.

Using Theorem
IV.3
, we have
\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{M}_{n})=\frac{n}{d}=\frac{1}{n}
with
d=n^{2}
. On the other hand, via Proposition
IV.4
, we have
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{M}_{n})=n\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{N})=n
.
∎

Remark IV.6
.

Recall that in
[
22
]
, a general relation between private and quantum capacity is given by

\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{N})\leq\frac{1}{2}(\log d_{A}+\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{N}))

(62)

for any channel with input system
A
. In our construction, the input dimension is
\log d_{A}=n\log(2n^2)=n+2n\log n
. Therefore, our example saturates the inequality up to a logarithmic order. Note that the example saturating the inequality in
[
22
]
has a constant lower bound for quantum capacity and in contrast, our example has quantum capacity approaching to zero.

V
Approximate private channel and its applications

One of the important problems in quantum entanglement theory is whether there exists a bipartite state, such that it has no distillable entanglement but has positive distillable key
[
15
]
. This question was solved using the framework of approximate private states. To be more specific, it was shown in
[
16
, Theorem 7]
that for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a pbit
\gamma^{a_{0}b_{0}A_{0}B_{0}}
and a corresponding PPT state
\zeta^{a_{0}b_{0}A_{0}B_{0}}
such that

\|\zeta^{a_{0}b_{0}A_{0}B_{0}}-\gamma^{a_{0}b_{0}A_{0}B_{0}}\|_{1}\leq\varepsilon.

(63)

The construction is give in
[
16
, proof of Theorem 7]
. The following lemma is a generalization of (
63
), which allows a PPT extension of approximate private states. It is presented in Lemma 2 in the supplementary material of
[
8
]
:

Lemma V.1
.

For any
\varepsilon>0
and
N\geq 1
, there exists a pbit
\gamma^{a_{0}b_{0}A_{0}B_{0}}
and a corresponding PPT state
\zeta^{a_{0}b_{0}A^{N}_{0}B^{N}_{0}}
(that is,
(\zeta^{a_{0}b_{0}A^{N}_{0}B^{N}_{0}})^{T_{b_{0}B_{0}^{N}}}\geq 0
), where
A_{0}^{N}:=A_{0}^{\otimes N},B_{0}^{N}:=B_{0}^{\otimes N}
such that

\displaystyle\left\lVert\zeta^{a_{0}b_{0}A_{0}B_{0}}-\gamma^{a_{0}b_{0}A_{0}B_{0}}\right\rVert_{1}\leq\varepsilon,

(64)

where
\zeta^{a_{0}b_{0}A_{0}B_{0}}:=\tr_{A_{0}^{N-1}B_{0}^{N-1}}(\zeta^{a_{0}b_{0}A^{N}_{0}B^{N}_{0}})
and it is independent of the choice of
N-1
subsystems
A_{0}^{N-1}B_{0}^{N-1}
.

For the readers’ convenience, we present the construction of
\zeta
, see
[
16
, (139)]
and
[
8
, (S18)]
:

\zeta^{a_{0}b_{0}A^{N}_{0}B^{N}_{0}}=\zeta^{a_{0}b_{0}A^{N}_{0}B^{N}_{0}}_{q,d,r,N,m}\propto\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}\left[q\left(\frac{\tau_{1}+\tau_{2}}{2}\right)\right]^{\otimes m}&0&0&{\left[q\left(\frac{\tau_{1}-\tau_{2}}{2}\right)\right]^{\otimes m}}\\
0&{\left[\left(\frac{1}{2}-q\right)\tau_{2}\right]^{\otimes m}}&0&0\\
0&0&{\left[\left(\frac{1}{2}-q\right)\tau_{2}\right]^{\otimes m}}&0\\
{\left[q\left(\frac{\tau_{1}-\tau_{2}}{2}\right)\right]^{\otimes m}}&0&0&{\left[q\left(\frac{\tau_{1}+\tau_{2}}{2}\right)\right]^{\otimes m}}\end{array}\right),

(65)

where

\displaystyle\tau_{1}=\left(\frac{1}{2}(\frac{P_{\mathrm{sym}}}{d_{\mathrm{sym}}}+\frac{P_{\mathrm{asym}}}{d_{\mathrm{asym}}})\right)^{\otimes rN},\quad\tau_{2}=\left(\frac{P_{\mathrm{sym}}}{d_{\mathrm{sym}}}\right)^{\otimes rN}.

In this case
A_{0}=B_{0}=(\mathbb{C}^{d})^{\otimes rm}
. Using the criteria developed in
[
16
]
, the state (
65
) is PPT, if

\displaystyle 0<q\leq\frac{1}{3},\quad\frac{1-q}{q}\geq\left(\frac{d}{d-1}\right)^{rN}.

To ensure (
63
) and PPT, one can set
q=\frac{1}{3}
,
r=\lceil 2m+\log m\rceil
and
m\cong\log(1/\varepsilon)
, with the freedom of choosing
d,N
such that
\left(\frac{d}{d-1}\right)^{rN}\leq 2
.

Motivated from the above question, in the remaining of this section, we explore different applications of approximate private channels defined as follows.

Definition V.2

(Approximate private channel)
.

The quantum channel
\mathcal{M}^{A^{\prime}\to B}
where
A^{\prime}=a_{0}^{\prime}A_{0}^{\prime},B=b_{0}B_{0}
is called an
\varepsilon
approximate private channel, if its induced Choi–Jamiołkowski operator is
\varepsilon
close to some pbit
\gamma^{a_{0}b_{0}A_{0}B_{0}}
in trace distance.

V-A

Application 1: A different proof of superactivation effect

Proposition V.3
.

Suppose the channel
\mathcal{M}^{A^{\prime}\to B}
with Choi–Jamiołkowski operator
\zeta^{a_{0}b_{0}A_{0}B_{0}}
is an
\varepsilon
-approximate private channel, i.e.,
\|\zeta^{a_{0}b_{0}A_{0}B_{0}}-\gamma^{a_{0}b_{0}A_{0}B_{0}}\|_{1}\leq\varepsilon
for some pbit
\gamma^{a_{0}b_{0}A_{0}B_{0}}
. Then for any
\lambda\in(0,1)
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}^{(1)}\left(\mathcal{M}^{A^{\prime}\to B}\otimes\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,d}\right)\geq 1-\lambda\,h(\frac{1+|c|}{2})-4\varepsilon-2h(\varepsilon),

(66)

where
c=\tr(\bra{00}^{a_0b_0} \gamma^{a_0b_0A_0B_0} \ket{11}^{a_0b_0})
.

Proof.

Note that
\mathcal{Q}^{(1)}(\mathcal{M}^{A^{\prime}\to B}\otimes\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,d})\geq I_{c}(\mathcal{M}^{A^{\prime}\to B}\otimes\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,d},\rho^{A^{\prime}A_{0}})
, where the ansatz state
\rho^{A^{\prime}A_{0}}
is given by

\displaystyle\rho^{A^{\prime}A_{0}}=\tr_{a_{0}}\left(\outerproduct{\psi}{\psi}^{a_{0}a_{0}^{\prime}}\otimes\outerproduct{\Psi}{\Psi}^{A_{0}A_{0}^{\prime}}\right).

Then the coherent information is calculated by

\displaystyle I_{c}(\mathcal{M}^{A^{\prime}\to B}\otimes\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,d},\rho^{A^{\prime}A_{0}})

\displaystyle=(1-\lambda)I_{c}(\mathcal{M}^{A^{\prime}\to B}\otimes id^{A_{0}\to A_{0}},\rho^{A^{\prime}A_{0}})+\lambda I_{c}(\mathcal{M}^{A^{\prime}\to B}\otimes\mathcal{E}_{1}^{A_{0}\to C_{0}},\rho^{A^{\prime}A_{0}})

\displaystyle=(1-\lambda)I(a_{0}\rangle b_{0}A_{0}B_{0})_{\zeta}+\lambda I(a_{0}\rangle b_{0}B_{0})_{\zeta^{a_{0}b_{0}B_{0}}}

\displaystyle\geq(1-\lambda)I(a_{0}\rangle b_{0}A_{0}B_{0})_{\zeta}+\lambda I(a_{0}\rangle b_{0})_{\zeta^{a_{0}b_{0}}}.

For the first equality above, we use the fact that coherent information is additive under flag mixture of two channels (
17
); for the second equality, we use the definition of coherent information; for the last inequality, we use the data processing inequality for coherent information (
18
). Using the well-known continuity of conditional entropy (Fannes-Alicki inequality
[
12
]
) and the fact that conditional entropy is the minus coherent information, we have

\displaystyle|I(a_{0}\rangle b_{0}A_{0}B_{0})_{\zeta}-I(a_{0}\rangle b_{0}A_{0}B_{0})_{\gamma}|\leq 4\varepsilon+2h(\varepsilon),\quad|I(a_{0}\rangle b_{0})_{\zeta^{a_{0}b_{0}}}-I(a_{0}\rangle b_{0})_{\gamma^{a_{0}b_{0}}}|\leq 4\varepsilon+2h(\varepsilon),

we conclude the proof by applying Lemma
II.4
.
∎

Therefore, for fixed
\lambda\in[\frac{1}{2},1)
and
\varepsilon
small enough, the above lower bound is strictly positive, thus providing a simpler proof of superactivation effect
[
33
]
when the approximate private channel is PPT which has zero quantum capacity. Note that we can slightly improve the bound
4\varepsilon+2h(\varepsilon)
using the recent results in
[
4
,
3
]
.

V-B

Application 2: Separation between anti-degradable channels and approximate private channels

In Prop.
V.3
, we show that tensoring an
\varepsilon
-approximate private channel with an anti-degradable erasure channel leads to superactivation. To demonstrate that this phenomenon cannot be attributed merely to approximate degradability, we further establish that
\varepsilon
-approximate private channels are quantitatively far, in the diamond norm, from the set of anti-degradable channels.

Definition V.4

(Anti-degradable channel)
.

A channel
\mathcal{A}^{A^{\prime}\to B}
is
anti-degradable
if there exists a CPTP map
\mathcal{T}
such that
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{T}\circ\mathcal{A}^{c}
, where
\mathcal{A}^{c}
is a complementary channel of
\mathcal{A}
. Denote the set of all such channels by
\mathrm{AD}(A^{\prime}\to B)
.

The key idea is to invoke the continuity of quantum capacities, shown in
[
23
, Corollary 14]
.

Lemma V.5
.

The quantum capacity of a quantum channel with finite-dimensional output is continuous.
Quantitatively, if
\mathcal{N},\mathcal{M}:A^{\prime}\to B
where the dimension of
B
is
d_{B}

and
\|\mathcal{N}-\mathcal{M}\|_{\diamond}\leq\varepsilon
, then

\bigl|\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{N})-\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{M})\,\bigr|\leq 8\varepsilon\,\log d_{B}+4\,h(\varepsilon).

(67)

Theorem V.6

(Quantitative separation from anti-degradable channels)
.

Let
\mathcal{M}^{A^{\prime}\to B}
be an
\varepsilon
-approximate private channel with Choi–Jamiołkowski operator
\zeta^{a_{0}b_{0}A_{0}B_{0}}
satisfying
\|\zeta-\gamma\|_{1}\leq\varepsilon
for some pbit
\gamma^{a_{0}b_{0}A_{0}B_{0}}
. Let
d=\mathrm{dim}A_{0}=\mathrm{dim}B_{0}
and

c:=\tr\Bigl(\bra{00}^{a_{0}b_{0}}\,\gamma^{a_{0}b_{0}A_{0}B_{0}}\,\ket{11}^{a_{0}b_{0}}\Bigr),\quad\Delta(\lambda,\varepsilon,c):=1-\lambda\,h\Bigl(\tfrac{1+|c|}{2}\Bigr)-4\varepsilon-2h(\varepsilon).

Then for any
\lambda\in[\tfrac{1}{2},1)
, we have

\inf_{\mathcal{A}\in\mathrm{AD}(A^{\prime}\to B)}\,\|\mathcal{M}-\mathcal{A}\|_{\diamond}\geq\min\left\{g^{-1}(\Delta(\lambda,\varepsilon,c)),\frac{1}{2}\right\},

(68)

where
g(x):=8x\log(2d(d+1))+4h(x)
is a strictly increasing function on
[0,\frac{1}{2}]
.

Proof.

First note that if
\mathcal{A}^{A_{1}\to B_{1}}\in\mathrm{AD}(A_{1}\to B_{1})
and
\mathcal{F}^{A_{2}\to B_{2}}\in\mathrm{AD}(A_{2}\to B_{2})
, then
\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{F}\in\mathrm{AD}(A_{1}A_{2}\to B_{1}B_{2})
. Therefore, via Proposition
V.3
, we have

\displaystyle\Delta(\lambda,\varepsilon,c)\leq\mathcal{Q}^{(1)}(\mathcal{M}\otimes\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,d})=\mathcal{Q}^{(1)}(\mathcal{M}\otimes\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,d})-\mathcal{Q}^{(1)}(\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,d})

for any anti-degradable channel
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}^{A^{\prime}\to B}
. By Lemma
V.5
, we have

\displaystyle|\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{M}\otimes\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,d})-\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,d})|

\displaystyle\leq 8\|\mathcal{M}\otimes\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,d}-\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,d}\|_{\diamond}\log(2d(d+1))+4h(\|\mathcal{M}\otimes\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,d}-\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,d}\|_{\diamond})

\displaystyle\leq 8\|\mathcal{M}-\mathcal{A}\|_{\diamond}\log(2d(d+1))+4h(\|\mathcal{M}-\mathcal{A}\|_{\diamond}),

where the last inequality follows from
\|\mathcal{M}\otimes\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,d}-\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,d}\|_{\diamond}\leq\|\mathcal{M}-\mathcal{A}\|_{\diamond}
and
g(x):=8x\log(2d(d+1))+4h(x)
is a strictly increasing function on
[0,\frac{1}{2}]
. Therefore, assuming
\|\mathcal{M}-\mathcal{A}\|_{\diamond}\leq\frac{1}{2}
, we conclude the proof.
∎

A useful upper bound for the quantum capacity of a general channel is to reduce to
“nearby” tractable families, e.g. degradable or anti-degradable channels.
For degradable proximity, several works bound
\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{N})
in terms of an

approximate degradability parameter

\delta_{\mathrm{deg}}(\mathcal{N})

(see, e.g.,
[
34
,
18
,
43
,
42
]
).
However, our separation shows that this program can fail for

\varepsilon
-approximate private channels.

V-C

Application 3: Quantum capacity detection at arbitrarily large level

In this section we apply the technique developed above to prove the following.
For every integer
n\geq 1
there exists a quantum channel

\mathcal{N}_{n}^{A_{n}\to B_{n}}
such that

\mathcal{Q}^{(1)}\big(\mathcal{N}_{n}^{\otimes n}\big)=0\quad\text{while}\quad\mathcal{Q}\big(\mathcal{N}_{n}\big)>0.

(69)

The phenomenon in (
69
) was first observed by Cubitt et al.
[
8
]
; our construction is more explicit and arguably simpler.

Remark V.7
.

The result above
does not
resolve the computability of the (regularized) quantum capacity

\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{N})=\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{k}\,\mathcal{Q}^{(1)}(\mathcal{N}^{\otimes k}).

In principle, the above channel
\mathcal{N}
could satisfy

\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{N})=\frac{1}{N}\,\mathcal{Q}^{(1)}\big(\mathcal{N}^{\otimes N}\big)

for some finite
N\geq 1
; our construction or the result in
[
8
]
neither rules this out nor provides an effective bound on such a
N
.

Fig. 5:
Superactivation with the help of many copy use of channels

A first step is a
N
-copy generalization of Proposition
V.3
:

Proposition V.8
.

For any
N\geq 1
and
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, suppose the state
\zeta^{a_{0}b_{0}A^{N}_{0}B^{N}_{0}}
induces a quantum channel
\Gamma^{a_{0}^{\prime}A_{0}^{\prime N}\to b_{0}B_{0}^{N}}
and satisfies

\displaystyle\left\lVert\zeta^{a_{0}b_{0}A_{0}B_{0}}-\gamma^{a_{0}b_{0}A_{0}B_{0}}\right\rVert_{1}\leq\varepsilon

for some pbit
\gamma^{a_{0}b_{0}A_{0}B_{0}}
. Then for any
\lambda\in(0,1)
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}^{(1)}\left(\Gamma^{a_{0}^{\prime}A_{0}^{\prime N}\to b_{0}B_{0}^{N}}\otimes(\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}^{A_{0}\to FA_{0}})^{\otimes N}\right)\geq 1-\lambda^{N}h(\frac{1+|c|}{2})-4\varepsilon-2h(\varepsilon),

(70)

where
c=\tr(\bra{00}^{a_0b_0} \gamma^{a_0b_0A_0B_0} \ket{11}^{a_0b_0})
.

Proof.

Denote

A_{0}^{N}=A_{0,1}\cdots A_{0,N},\quad A_{0}^{\prime N}=A^{\prime}_{0,1}\cdots A^{\prime}_{0,N},\quad A^{\prime}=a_{0}^{\prime}A_{0}^{\prime N}

where each
A_{0,i}
is isomorphic to
A_{0}
labeled by
i
. The Choi–Jamiołkowski operator of
\Gamma^{A^{\prime}\to b_{0}B_{0}^{N}}
is given by

\displaystyle\zeta^{a_{0}b_{0}A^{N}_{0}B^{N}_{0}}=\left(id^{a_{0}A_{0}^{N}\to a_{0}A_{0}^{N}}\otimes\Gamma^{A^{\prime}\to b_{0}B_{0}^{N}}\right)\left(\outerproduct{\psi}{\psi}^{a_{0}a_{0}^{\prime}}\otimes\outerproduct{\Psi}{\Psi}^{A_{0,1}A_{0,1}^{\prime 1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\outerproduct{\Psi}{\Psi}^{A_{0,N}A_{0,N}^{\prime}}\right).

Denote
\widetilde{B}_{0}:=FA_{0}\cong A_{0}\oplus A_{0}
and

\rho^{a_{0}b_{0}B_{0}^{N}\widetilde{B}_{0}^{N}}:=\left(id^{a_{0}\to a_{0}}\otimes\Gamma^{A^{\prime}\to b_{0}B_{0}^{N}}\otimes(\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}^{A_{0}\to\widetilde{B}_{0}})^{\otimes N}\right)\left(\outerproduct{\psi}{\psi}^{a_{0}a_{0}^{\prime}}\otimes\outerproduct{\Psi}{\Psi}^{A_{0,1}A_{0,1}^{\prime 1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\outerproduct{\Psi}{\Psi}^{A_{0,N}A_{0,N}^{\prime}}\right),

(71)

Choose the input state as

\displaystyle\sigma^{A^{\prime}A_{0}^{N}}=\tr_{a_{0}}\left(\outerproduct{\psi}{\psi}^{a_{0}a_{0}^{\prime}}\otimes\outerproduct{\Psi}{\Psi}^{A_{0,1}A_{0,1}^{\prime 1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\outerproduct{\Psi}{\Psi}^{A_{0,N}A_{0,N}^{\prime}}\right),

(72)

then via Lemma
II.1
for flagged channels,

\displaystyle I(a_{0}\rangle b_{0}B_{0}^{N}\widetilde{B}_{0}^{N})_{\rho^{a_{0}b_{0}B_{0}^{N}\widetilde{B}_{0}^{N}}}=I_{c}(\Gamma^{A^{\prime}\to b_{0}B_{0}^{N}}\otimes(\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}^{A_{0}\to\widetilde{B}_{0}})^{\otimes N},\sigma^{A^{\prime}A_{0}^{N}})

(73)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\textbf{b}\in\{0,1\}^{N}}(1-\lambda)^{\sum_{i=1}^{N}b_{i}}\lambda^{N-\sum_{i=1}^{N}b_{i}}I_{c}(\Gamma^{A^{\prime}\to b_{0}B_{0}^{N}}\otimes\bigotimes_{i=1}^{N}\mathcal{N}_{b_{i}},\sigma^{A^{\prime}A_{0}^{N}}),

where
\mathcal{N}_{0}=\mathcal{E}_{1}^{A_{0}\to A_{0}}
and
\mathcal{N}_{1}=id^{A_{0}\to A_{0}}
. Given
\textbf{b}\in\{0,1\}^{N}
, we have two cases: (i)
\sum_{i=1}^{N}b_{i}=0
; (ii)
\sum_{i=1}^{N}b_{i}>0
. The calculation proceeds as follows:

\displaystyle\text{(i)}\sum_{i=1}^{N}b_{i}=0:

\displaystyle\quad\lambda^{N}I_{c}(\Gamma^{A^{\prime}\to b_{0}B_{0}^{N}}\otimes\bigotimes_{i=1}^{N}\mathcal{E}_{1}^{A_{0,i}\to A_{0,i}},\sigma^{A^{\prime}A_{0}^{N}})

\displaystyle=\lambda^{N}I(a_{0}\rangle b_{0}B_{0}^{N}A_{0}^{N})_{\rho^{a_{0}b_{0}B_{0}^{N}}\otimes\mu^{\otimes N}}=\lambda^{N}I(a_{0}\rangle b_{0}B_{0}^{N})_{\rho^{a_{0}b_{0}B_{0}^{N}}}\geq\lambda^{N}I(a_{0}\rangle b_{0})_{\zeta^{a_{0}b_{0}}},

where the above equalities follow from the definition of coherent information; the inequality follows from data processing inequality (
20
). For the other case,

\displaystyle\text{(ii)}\sum_{i=1}^{N}b_{i}>0:

\displaystyle\quad\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\exists 1\leq j\leq N\\
b_{j}=1\end{subarray}}(1-\lambda)^{\sum_{i=1}^{N}b_{i}}\lambda^{N-\sum_{i=1}^{N}b_{i}}I_{c}(\Gamma^{A^{\prime}\to b_{0}B_{0}^{N}}\otimes\bigotimes_{i=1}^{N}\mathcal{N}_{b_{i}}^{A_{0,i}\to A_{0,i}},\sigma^{A^{\prime}A_{0}^{N}})

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\exists 1\leq j\leq N\\
b_{j}=1\end{subarray}}(1-\lambda)^{\sum_{i=1}^{N}b_{i}}\lambda^{N-\sum_{i=1}^{N}b_{i}}I_{c}(\Gamma^{A^{\prime}\to b_{0}B_{0}^{N}}\otimes id^{A_{0,j}\to A_{0,j}},\sigma^{A^{\prime}A_{0,j}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\exists 1\leq j\leq N\\
b_{j}=1\end{subarray}}(1-\lambda)^{\sum_{i=1}^{N}b_{i}}\lambda^{N-\sum_{i=1}^{N}b_{i}}I(a_{0}\rangle b_{0}A_{0,j}B_{0}^{N})_{\zeta^{a_{0}b_{0}A_{0,j}B_{0}^{N}}}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\exists 1\leq j\leq N\\
b_{j}=1\end{subarray}}(1-\lambda)^{\sum_{i=1}^{N}b_{i}}\lambda^{N-\sum_{i=1}^{N}b_{i}}I(a_{0}\rangle b_{0}A_{0,j}B_{0,j})_{\zeta^{a_{0}b_{0}A_{0,j}B_{0,j}}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\exists 1\leq j\leq N\\
b_{j}=1\end{subarray}}(1-\lambda)^{\sum_{i=1}^{N}b_{i}}\lambda^{N-\sum_{i=1}^{N}b_{i}}I(a_{0}\rangle b_{0}A_{0}B_{0})_{\zeta^{a_{0}b_{0}A_{0}B_{0}}}=(1-\lambda^{N})I(a_{0}\rangle b_{0}A_{0}B_{0})_{\zeta^{a_{0}b_{0}A_{0}B_{0}}}.

For the first inequality above, note that at least one register has perfect channel
\mathcal{N}_{1}
, then
I_{c}(\Gamma^{A^{\prime}\to b_{0}B_{0}^{N}}\otimes\bigotimes_{i=1}^{N}\mathcal{N}_{b_{i}}^{A_{0,i}\to A_{0,i}},\sigma^{A^{\prime}A_{0}^{N}})\geq I_{c}(\Gamma^{A^{\prime}\to b_{0}B_{0}^{N}}\otimes id^{A_{0,j}\to A_{0,j}},\sigma^{A^{\prime}A_{0,j}})
follows from data processing inequality if there are more than one register with perfect channel; otherwise we have equality since the register has a completely erasure map and we can apply (
21
). The second inequality follows from data processing inequality (
20
).

Using the same argument as in the proof of Proposition
V.3
, we have

\displaystyle|I(a_{0}\rangle b_{0}A_{0}B_{0})_{\zeta}-I(a_{0}\rangle b_{0}A_{0}B_{0})_{\gamma}|\leq 4\varepsilon+2h(\varepsilon),\quad|I(a_{0}\rangle b_{0})_{\zeta^{a_{0}b_{0}}}-I(a_{0}\rangle b_{0})_{\gamma^{a_{0}b_{0}}}|\leq 4\varepsilon+2h(\varepsilon),

Combining the above two estimates and plugging them into (
73
), we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}^{(1)}\left(\Gamma^{a_{0}^{\prime}A_{0}^{\prime N}\to b_{0}B_{0}^{N}}\otimes(\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}^{A_{0}\to FA_{0}})^{\otimes N}\right)

\displaystyle\geq I(a_{0}\rangle b_{0}B_{0}^{N}\widetilde{B}_{0}^{N})_{\rho^{a_{0}b_{0}B_{0}^{N}\widetilde{B}_{0}^{N}}}

\displaystyle\geq\lambda^{N}\left((1-h(\frac{1+|c|}{2}))-(2h(\varepsilon)+4\varepsilon)\right)+(1-\lambda^{N})\left(1-(2h(\varepsilon)+4\varepsilon)\right)

\displaystyle=1-\lambda^{N}h(\frac{1+|c|}{2})-4\varepsilon-2h(\varepsilon).

∎

Using Proposition
V.8
, we can prove the following super-activation result:

Theorem V.9
.

Given
N\geq 1
and denote
A^{\prime}=a_{0}A_{0}^{N},B=b_{0}B_{0}^{N}
. Suppose
\Gamma_{\kappa}^{A^{\prime}\to FB}
is given by

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\kappa}^{A^{\prime}\to FB}=(1-\kappa)\outerproduct{0}{0}^{F}\otimes\Gamma^{A^{\prime}\to B}+\kappa\outerproduct{1}{1}^{F}\otimes\mathcal{E}_{1}^{A^{\prime}\to B},\quad\kappa\in(0,\frac{1}{2})

where
\Gamma^{A^{\prime}\to B}
is induced by the state
\zeta^{AB}=\zeta^{a_{0}b_{0}A_{0}^{N}B_{0}^{N}}
, with
\|\zeta^{a_{0}b_{0}A_{0}B_{0}}-\gamma^{a_{0}b_{0}A_{0}B_{0}}\|_{1}\leq\varepsilon
for some pbit
\gamma^{a_{0}b_{0}A_{0}B_{0}}
. Then for

N>\frac{\log(1 - 2h(\varepsilon) - 4\varepsilon- \frac{\kappa}{1-\kappa})-\log(h(\frac{1+|c|}{2}))}{\log\lambda},

(74)

where
c=\tr(\bra{00}^{a_0b_0} \gamma^{a_0b_0A_0B_0} \ket{11}^{a_0b_0})
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}^{(1)}\left((\Gamma_{\kappa}^{A^{\prime}\to FB}\oplus\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}^{A_{0}\to FA_{0}})^{\otimes N}\right)>0.

Proof.

Using the same input state
\sigma^{A^{\prime}A_{0}^{N}}
(
72
), and applying Lemma
II.1
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}^{(1)}\left((\Gamma_{\kappa}^{A^{\prime}\to FB}\oplus\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}^{A_{0}\to FA_{0}})^{\otimes N}\right)\geq\mathcal{Q}^{(1)}\left(\Gamma_{\kappa}^{A^{\prime}\to FB}\otimes(\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}^{A_{0}\to FA_{0}})^{\otimes N}\right)

\displaystyle\geq I_{c}(\Gamma_{\kappa}^{A^{\prime}\to FB}\otimes(\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}^{A_{0}\to FA_{0}})^{\otimes N},\sigma^{A^{\prime}A_{0}^{N}})

\displaystyle=(1-\kappa)I_{c}(\Gamma^{A^{\prime}\to B}\otimes(\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}^{A_{0}\to FA_{0}})^{\otimes N},\sigma^{A^{\prime}A_{0}^{N}})+\kappa I_{c}(\mathcal{E}_{1}^{A^{\prime}\to B}\otimes(\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}^{A_{0}\to FA_{0}})^{\otimes N},\sigma^{A^{\prime}A_{0}^{N}}).

To proceed, for the first quantity above, we apply Proposition
V.8
; for the second quantity, we use the worse case bound
I_{c}(\mathcal{E}_{1}^{A^{\prime}\to B}\otimes(\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}^{A_{0}\to FA_{0}})^{\otimes N},\sigma^{A^{\prime}A_{0}^{N}})\geq I_{c}(\mathcal{E}_{1}^{A^{\prime}A_{0}^{N}\to A^{\prime}A_{0}^{N}},\sigma^{A^{\prime}A_{0}^{N}})=-S(\sigma^{A^{\prime}A_{0}^{N}})=-\log d_{a_{0}}=-1
. Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}^{(1)}\left((\Gamma_{\kappa}^{A^{\prime}\to FB}\oplus\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}^{A_{0}\to FA_{0}})^{\otimes N}\right)\geq(1-\kappa)\left(1-\lambda^{N}h(\frac{1+|c|}{2})-4\varepsilon-2h(\varepsilon)\right)-\kappa.

Let the above quantity be greater than zero, we get

\displaystyle 1-\lambda^{N}h(\frac{1+|c|}{2})-4\varepsilon-2h(\varepsilon)>\frac{\kappa}{1-\kappa}\iff N>\frac{\log(1 - 2h(\varepsilon) - 4\varepsilon- \frac{\kappa}{1-\kappa})-\log(h(\frac{1+|c|}{2}))}{\log\lambda}.

∎

To ensure additivity up to any level, we use the following flag trick:

Proposition V.10
.

Suppose
\Gamma^{A_{1}\to B_{1}}
is a zero-capacity channel. Then for any
\kappa\in(0,1)
and
n\geq 1
, there exists
\lambda=\lambda(n,\kappa)<1
such that

\mathcal{Q}^{(1)}\bigg((\Gamma_{\kappa}^{A_{1}\to F_{1}B_{1}}\oplus\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}^{A_{2}\to F_{2}B_{2}})^{\otimes n}\bigg)=0,

where

\Gamma_{\kappa}^{A_{1}\to F_{1}B_{1}}:=(1-\kappa)\outerproduct{0}{0}^{F_{1}}\otimes\Gamma^{A_{1}\to B_{1}}+\kappa\outerproduct{1}{1}^{F_{1}}\otimes\mathcal{E}_{1}^{A_{1}\to B_{1}}.

(75)

In particular, we can choose

\lambda=(1+\kappa^{n})^{-1/n}.

(76)

Proof.

First, denote

\mathcal{N}:=\Gamma_{\kappa}^{A_{1}\to F_{1}B_{1}}\oplus\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}^{A_{2}\to F_{2}B_{2}},

we apply Lemma
II.1
for direct sum channels:

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}^{(1)}(\mathcal{N}^{\otimes n})=\max_{0\leq\ell\leq n}\mathcal{Q}^{(1)}\left((\Gamma_{\kappa}^{A_{1}\to F_{1}B_{1}})^{\otimes\ell}\otimes(\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}^{A_{2}\to F_{2}B_{2}})^{\otimes(n-\ell)}\right).

Choosing
\ell\in[1,n-1]
and
\rho^{A}:=\rho^{A_{1}^{\ell}A_{2}^{n-\ell}}
as the maximizer, then applying Lemma
II.1
for flagged channels:

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}^{(1)}(\mathcal{N}^{\otimes n})

\displaystyle=I_{c}\left((\Gamma_{\kappa}^{A_{1}\to F_{1}B_{1}})^{\otimes\ell}\otimes(\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}^{A_{2}\to F_{2}B_{2}})^{\otimes(n-\ell)},\rho^{A}\right)

(77)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\textbf{b}\in\{0,1\}^{n}}(1-\kappa)^{\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}b_{i}}\kappa^{\ell-\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}b_{i}}(1-\lambda)^{\sum_{j=\ell+1}^{n}b_{i}}\lambda^{n-\ell-\sum_{j=\ell+1}^{n}b_{i}}I_{c}(\bigotimes_{i=1}^{\ell}\mathcal{M}_{b_{i}}\otimes\bigotimes_{j=\ell+1}^{n-\ell}\mathcal{N}_{b_{j}},\rho^{A}),

(78)

where we denote

\mathcal{M}_{0}=\mathcal{E}_{1}^{A_{1}\to B_{1}},\ \mathcal{M}_{1}=\Gamma^{A_{1}\to B_{1}},\ \mathcal{N}_{0}=\mathcal{E}_{1}^{A_{2}\to B_{2}},\ \mathcal{N}_{1}=\mathcal{I}^{A_{2}\to B_{2}}

and we used the calculation for tensor products of flagged channels:

\displaystyle(\Gamma_{\kappa}^{A_{1}\to F_{1}B_{1}})^{\otimes\ell}=\sum_{\textbf{b}\in\{0,1\}^{\ell}}(1-\kappa)^{\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}b_{i}}\kappa^{\ell-\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}b_{i}}\outerproduct{\textbf{b}}{\textbf{b}}^{F_{1}^{\ell}}\otimes\bigotimes_{i=1}^{\ell}\mathcal{M}_{b_{i}}

and similar for
(\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}^{A_{2}\to F_{2}B_{2}})^{\otimes(n-\ell)}
.

Given
\textbf{b}\in\{0,1\}^{n}
, we have three cases: (i)
\sum_{i=1}^{n}b_{i}=0
, (ii)
\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}b_{i}>0,\sum_{j=\ell+1}^{n}b_{j}=0
and (iii)
\sum_{j=\ell+1}^{n}b_{j}>0
. Then the calculation of (
78
) can be divided into three cases:

\displaystyle\text{(i)}\sum_{i=1}^{n}b_{i}=0:\quad\kappa^{\ell}\lambda^{n-\ell}I_{c}\left((\mathcal{E}_{1}^{A_{1}\to B_{1}})^{\otimes\ell}\otimes(\mathcal{E}_{1}^{A_{2}\to B_{2}})^{\otimes(n-\ell)},\rho^{A}\right)=-\kappa^{\ell}\lambda^{n-\ell}S(\rho^{A}).

\displaystyle\text{(ii)}\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}b_{i}>0,\sum_{j=\ell+1}^{n}b_{j}=0:\sum_{\textbf{b}\in\{0,1\}^{\ell},|\textbf{b}|\neq 0}(1-\kappa)^{\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}b_{i}}\kappa^{\ell-\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}b_{i}}\lambda^{n-\ell}I_{c}\left(\bigotimes_{i=1}^{\ell}\mathcal{M}_{b_{i}}\otimes(\mathcal{E}_{1}^{A_{2}\to B_{2}})^{\otimes(n-\ell)},\rho^{A}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 116.65646pt\leq\sum_{\textbf{b}\in\{0,1\}^{\ell},|\textbf{b}|\neq 0}(1-\kappa)^{\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}b_{i}}\kappa^{\ell-\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}b_{i}}\lambda^{n-\ell}I_{c}\left((\Gamma^{A_{1}\to B_{1}})^{\otimes\ell}\otimes(\mathcal{E}_{1}^{A_{2}\to B_{2}})^{\otimes(n-\ell)},\rho^{A}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 116.65646pt=(1-\kappa^{\ell})\lambda^{n-\ell}I_{c}\left((\Gamma^{A_{1}\to B_{1}})^{\otimes\ell}\otimes(\mathcal{E}_{1}^{A_{2}\to B_{2}})^{\otimes(n-\ell)},\rho^{A}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 116.65646pt\leq(1-\kappa^{\ell})\lambda^{n-\ell}I_{c}\left((\Gamma^{A_{1}\to B_{1}})^{\otimes\ell},\rho_{A_{1}^{\ell}}\right)\leq 0.

\displaystyle\text{(iii)}\sum_{j=\ell+1}^{n}b_{j}>0:\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\exists\ell+1\leq j\leq n\\
b_{j}=1\end{subarray}}(1-\kappa)^{\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}b_{i}}\kappa^{\ell-\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}b_{i}}(1-p)^{\sum_{j=\ell+1}^{n}b_{i}}p^{n-\ell-\sum_{j=\ell+1}^{n}b_{i}}I_{c}\left(\bigotimes_{i=1}^{\ell}\mathcal{M}_{b_{i}}\otimes\bigotimes_{j=\ell+1}^{n-\ell}\mathcal{N}_{b_{j}},\rho^{A}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\exists\ell+1\leq j\leq n\\

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}^{(1)}(\mathcal{N}^{\otimes n})\leq(1-\lambda^{n-\ell}-\kappa^{\ell}\lambda^{n-\ell})S(\rho^{A}).

The above is less than zero if

Our construction simplifies the erasure channel by omitting the
a_{0}
component. By presenting the discussion in a more accessible way, we hope it will stimulate further exploration into the computability of quantum capacity.

VI
Conclusion and Outlook

This work develops a unified picture of quantum capacity amplification built from private-state induced channels. In Section
III
we proved quantitative lower bounds on the achievable coherent information under explicit constructions (see, e.g., Theorem
III.1
), and exhibited both amplification and super-amplification regimes, including concrete illustrations for erasure and depolarizing channels. Assuming the Spin Alignment Conjecture (SAC)
[
20
]
, Section
IV
established a single-letter formula for the quantum capacity of a broad class of private channels that are neither degradable, anti-degradable, nor PPT, and leveraged this to build channels with vanishing quantum capacity yet unbounded private capacity. Finally, Section
V
extended the picture to approximate private channels, proving metric separation from the anti-degradable set (via diamond-norm lower bounds and continuity), giving an alternative superactivation proof in the approximate setting (extending Smith–Yard
[
33
]
), and clarifying the scope of computability results in light of
[
8
]
: these constructions do not settle the computability of the regularization of quantum capacity.

Limitations and outlook for future work
:

•

Several statements rely on SAC, with partial solution given in
[
1
,
2
]
. A complete solution is still open.

•

Amplification constants in our quantitative bounds are likely suboptimal, since it is given by maximally entangled state ansatz.

•

Most constructions are built around a special class of private states, leaving open how universal the phenomenon is beyond this template. A general open question: suppose
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{N})>\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{N})
, does there exist an anti-degradable (or zero-capacity) channel
\mathcal{M}
such that
\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{N}\otimes\mathcal{M})>\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{N})
?

We hope these results and questions stimulate a more systematic theory of how private structure governs coherent transmission.

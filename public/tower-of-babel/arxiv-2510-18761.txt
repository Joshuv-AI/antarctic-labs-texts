Ordinal and disjoint sums of partially ordered patterns
Sucharita Biswas, Umesh Shankar, Sivaramakrishnan Sivasubramanian

Abstract
Partially ordered patterns (POPs) generalize the classical notion of permutation patterns within the framework of pattern avoidance. Building on recent work by Burstein, Han, Kitaev, and Zhang, which introduced the concept of shape-Wilf-equivalence of sets of patterns, we develop the notions of ordinal and disjoint sums of labeled posets. This framework enables us to reinterpret their main result as an ordinal sum analogue of the classical theorem by Backelin, West, and Xin. We establish analogous results for disjoint sums of POPs and further extend their results to prove Wilf-equivalence for classes of POPs that include isolated vertices. In particular, we prove the shape-Wilf-equivalence of the sets of patterns { 123,213,312 } \{123,213,312\} and { 132,231,321 } \{132,231,321\} . Our proof strategy involves a bijection that filters through an encoding scheme for the transversals avoiding these patterns. We use these results to completely classify the partially ordered patterns of size 3 , 4 , 5 3,4,5 whose connected components are all chains. This classification also confirms a conjecture posed by Dimitrov at the problem session of the British Combinatorics Conference 2024 (BCC30).

contains an occurrence of
\st(p\backslash I)
in the columns indexed by
[r]\backslash I
and each cell

lies inside

for
1\leq i\leq r-s
and
1\leq j\leq r
.

[r]\backslash I

Lemma 13

Let

denote the size of the column

. At some stage of the process for

or

, let

be the board made up of the remaining white cells. Suppose that the top row of

comes from the columns

of

(1,1),(c_{l},1),(c_{l},\ell(c_{l}))
,
(1,\ell(c_{l}))
(corners included) already contains a

to the left of

, then there is at most one suitable position. To avoid

, it is the rightmost position on the top row.

Proof.

This follows from the previous lemma, as the

to the left of

in the rectangle will form an occurrence of

or

if placed in the other suitable position.

Remark 14

Outside of the two cases described above, there are none other. If there is no

in the rectangle described in Lemma

Lemma 15

At any stage of the process, in the topmost row of white cells, there is at least one suitable position to place

avoiding

or

Proof.

To avoid

in the leftmost position on the top row of the white cells. To avoid

, we can always place

in the rightmost position on the top row of the white cells. This position will be suitable.
∎

Using the above scheme, we create an encoding that maps tranversals that avoid

to those that avoid

. The encoding will be a word with the letters

, obtained in the following way. Let

be a transversal of

that avoids

1.

Let

be the encoding word.

2.

Remove all the

3.

While placing

on the row

from the top, if there were two suitable positions on the top row and

appears in the first suitable position from the left in

. If it appears in the second suitable position from the left in

.

4.

While placing

in row

Once we have placed all the

.

Now, to obtain a transversal of

that avoids

1.

Let

be the encoding word.

2.

If

, place

in the second suitable position, from the left, in the topmost row of white cells.

3.

If

Similarly, we may start with a transversal that avoids

In the insertion process for

if there is only one suitable cell to insert at.

Lemma 16
.

At some stage
i
, if
w_{i}=1
and the size of the top most row of white cells is strictly greater than
2
, then the subsequent placements are forced until a stage is encountered where the size of the top row of white cells is exactly
2
. The stage after that is not forced unless there is only one white cell in the top row. This happens at the same stage for
p
and
p^{\prime}
.

Proof.

We prove the lemma first for
p
. Let
F^{\prime}
be the Ferrers board of white cells at stage
i
. If the top row of
F^{\prime}
has
3
or more cells, then so do subsequent rows. Now, if we place
1
in the first column in the first row, then we would have to place
1
in the second column in the second row. If not, when we place a
1
in the second column of
F^{\prime}
in the future, we would have an occurrence of
p
. Arguing similarly, every placement is forced until we encounter a stage where the size of the top row of white cells is
2
. Let this be stage
j
.

At stage
j-1
, let
\hat{F^{\prime}}
be the board of white cells. By definition, the top two rows have
3
cells. At stage
j-1
, we would have placed in the first column of
\hat{F^{\prime}}
as this was forced. At stage
j
, we would have to place in the second column of
\hat{F^{\prime}}
. This is also forced. If the third row of
\hat{F^{\prime}}
has
3
cells, then there would only be one cell for stage
j+1
. Else, if there are more than
3
cells in the third row of
\hat{F^{\prime}}
, we can place
1
in the fourth column on the third row and it would not participate any occurrence of
p
with pre-existing

s as the previous rows do not have cells in this column. This proves the lemma for
p
.

Now, for
p^{\prime}
, let
F^{\prime\prime}
be Ferrers board of white cells. If we place
1
in the first column, then in the second row, we cannot place
1
in the second column, by a similar reasoning as before. Therefore, we are forced to place in the last cell on the second row.
Arguing similarly, every placement is forced until we reach a row where only the second and third columns of

have white cells and the rest of the row is gray. Let stage
j
be the stage when this happens.

At stage

, let
\hat{F^{\prime\prime}}
be the board of white cells. We have three cells in the top two rows, which correspond to the second, third and fourth columns of

. In stage

, we have to place

in the rightmost cell (fourth column). In stage

in the rightmost cell of that row (third column). If the third row of

had

, there would be only one cell to place in. Else, we can place

in the cell that is in the second column of

or in the rightmost column on the third row of

.
By way of contradiction, suppose this stage were forced. Then, there would need to be a

to the left of the first column in the rectangle described in Lemma

However, the rectangle described in Lemma

would not have any

as the rectangle lies entirely below the top

has no cells in the higher rows of

in this column but the second row had strictly smaller size than the third row. This proves that this stage is not forced.

Lemma 17

If
w_{i}=2

or

When we reach a stage

Local energy decay of solutions to the linearized compressible viscoelastic system around motionless state in an exterior domain
Yusuke Ishigaki, Takayuki Kobayashi

Abstract
We study the large time behavior of solutions to the system of equations describing motion of compressible viscoelastic ﬂuids. We focus on the linearized system around a motionless state in a three-dimensional exterior domain and derive the local energy decay estimate of its solution to give the diﬀusion wave phenomena caused by sound wave, viscous diffusion and elastic shear wave.



Keywords
: Compressible viscoelastic system; exterior domain; local energy decay.

Mathematics Subject Classification
: 35E15, 35Q35, 35Q74, 76N06.

1
Introduction

1.1
Problems

This paper deals with the compressible viscoelastic system

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho+\mathrm{div}(\rho v)=0,

(1.1)

\displaystyle\rho(\partial_{t}v+v\cdot\nabla v)-\nu{\Delta}v-(\nu+\nu^{\prime})\nabla\mathrm{div}v+\nabla P(\rho)=\beta^{2}\mathrm{div}(\rho F{}^{\top}\!F),

(1.2)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}F+v\cdot\nabla F=(\nabla v)F

(1.3)

in an exterior domain
\Omega
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
with smooth compact boundary
\partial\Omega
.
Here
\rho=\rho(x,t)
,

v={}^{\top}(v^{1}(x,t),

v^{2}(x,t),v^{3}(x,t))
, and
F=(F^{jk}(x,t))_{1\leq j,k\leq 3}
denote
the unknown density, the velocity field, and the deformation tensor, respectively, at position
x\in\Omega
and time
t\geq 0
;

P=P(\rho)
is the pressure assumed to be a smooth function of
\rho>0
satisfying
P^{\prime}(1)>0
;
\nu
and
\nu^{\prime}
are the viscosity coefficients satisfying
\nu>0
and
2\nu+3\nu^{\prime}\geq 0;

\beta>0
is a constant called propergation of elastic shear wave. In particular, if we set
\beta=0
formally, (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) become the usual compressible Navier-Stokes equations.

We next impose the boundary and initial conditions

\displaystyle v|_{\partial\Omega}=0,~(\rho,v,F)\to(1,0,I)~\textrm{as}~|x|\to\infty,

(1.4)

\displaystyle(\rho,v,F)|_{t=0}=(\rho_{0},v_{0},F_{0}),

(1.5)

where
I
is the
3\times 3
identity matrix.
Furthermore, we also assume the following conditions for
\rho_{0}
and
F_{0}
:

\displaystyle\mathrm{div}(\rho_{0}{}^{\top}\!F_{0})=0,\quad\rho_{0}\mathrm{det}F_{0}=1,\quad\sum_{m=1}^{3}(F_{0}^{ml}\partial_{x_{m}}F_{0}^{jk}-F_{0}^{mk}\partial_{x_{m}}F_{0}^{jl})=0,~(j,k,l=1,2,3).

(1.6)

As in the proof of
[
23
, Proposition.1]
, (
1.6
) is invariant for
t
and
x
.
These constraints are necessary to justify the global existence of solutions to (
1.1
)–(
1.5
) (See e. g.
[
2
,
8
,
24
]
.) The first constraint in (
1.6
) is used to perturb (
1.1
)–(
1.3
) around given flow, second one preserves a local volume in material without the vacuum
\rho>0
of the fluid, and third one stands for Piola’s formula which follows from the inverse matrix of
F
written by a Jacobi matrix of some vector field.

Throughout this paper, we are interested in the behavior of solutions to the problem (
1.1
)–(
1.5
) with (
1.6
) around a motionless state
(\rho,v,F)=(1,0,I)
, where
I
denotes a 3
\times
3 identity matrix and stands for no deformation of elastic body. To investigate this, we concentrate a behavior of the perturbation

u(t):={}^{\top}(\phi(t),w(t),G(t))={}^{\top}(\rho(t),v(t),F(t))-{}^{\top}(1,0,I)
satisfying the following initial boundary value problem

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u+Au=g(u),\quad u|_{t=0}=u_{0}=(\phi_{0},w_{0},G_{0}),\quad w|_{\partial\Omega}=0,\quad u\to 0~\mathrm{as}~|x|\to\infty.

(1.7)

Here
A
is a linearized operator around
(1,0,I)
given by

A:=\left(\begin{array}[]{@{\ }cc@{\ }cc@{\ } }0&\mathrm{div}&0\\
\gamma^{2}\nabla&-\nu\Delta-\tilde{\nu}\nabla\mathrm{div}&-\beta^{2}\mathrm{div}\\
0&-\nabla&0\end{array}\right),

(1.8)

where
\gamma:=\sqrt{P^{\prime}(1)}
and
\tilde{\nu}:=\nu+\nu^{\prime}
;
g(u)={}^{\top}(g_{1}(u),g_{2}(u),g_{3}(u))
is a nonlinear term given by

\displaystyle g_{1}(u)

\displaystyle:=-\mathrm{div}(\phi w),

\displaystyle g_{2}(u)

\displaystyle:=-w\cdot\nabla w+\frac{\phi}{1+\phi}(-\nu\Delta w-\tilde{\nu}\nabla\mathrm{div}w+\gamma^{2}\nabla\phi)-\frac{1}{1+\phi}\nabla Q(\phi)

\displaystyle\quad\quad-\frac{\beta^{2}\phi}{1+\phi}\mathrm{div}G+\frac{\beta^{2}}{1+\phi}\mathrm{div}(\phi G+G{}^{\top}\!G+\phi G{}^{\top}\!G),

\displaystyle g_{3}(u)

\displaystyle:=-w\cdot\nabla G+(\nabla w)G,

where
Q(\phi):=\phi^{2}\int_{0}^{1}{P}^{\prime\prime}(1+s\phi)\mathrm{d}s
. The aim of this paper is to clarify the large time behavior of the solution to the corresponding linearized problem to (
1.7
):

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u+Au=0,\quad u|_{t=0}=u_{0}=(\phi_{0},w_{0},G_{0}),\quad w|_{\partial\Omega}=0,\quad u\to 0~\mathrm{as}~|x|\to\infty

(1.9)

under the constraints obtained by linearizing (
1.6
) without its second constraint:

\displaystyle\nabla\phi_{0}+\mathrm{div}{}^{\top}\!G_{0}=0,\quad\partial_{x_{l}}G_{0}^{jk}=\partial_{x_{k}}G_{0}^{jl}~(j,k,l=1,2,3).

(1.10)

For simplicity, we express the solution of (
1.9
) with initial data
u_{0}
as
u(t)=:T(t)u_{0}
and consider the case that there exists a vector field
\psi
such that
G
is written as
G=\nabla\psi
, which automatically satisfies the last identity of (
1.10
).

The system (
1.1
)–(
1.3
) is governed from a motion of viscous compressible fluid with effect of linear Hookean elastic body. The equations (
1.1
) and (
1.3
) are obtained under the definition of
\rho
and
F
in microscopic scale, and (
1.2
) is a motion equation derived by the variational method. We refer to
[
5
,
19
,
28
]
for more physical details. Moreover, we can categorize (
1.1
)–(
1.3
) as a quasilinear parabolic-hyperbolic system since (
1.1
) and (
1.3
) are regarded as quasilinear transport equations and (
1.2
) is seen as a parabolic equation for
v
.

1.2

We first review the known results for mathematical study of solutions

of (
1.7
) with
\beta=0
around the motionless state
(1,0)
. Matsumura and Nishida
[

. Hoff and Zumbrun
[

obtained the

, which imply that the behavior of

in

is similarly described as the heat kernel for
p>2
and becomes different from that for
p<2
.
See also Kobayashi and Shibata
[
17
]
for the linearized problem whose solution behaves like a sum of purely diffusive part and combination of fundamental solutions to the diffusion equation and wave equation with propagation speed
\gamma
as
t\to\infty
. Kobayashi
[
13
]
, Kobayashi and Shibata
[
16
]
, and Enomoto and Shibata
[
3
,
25
]
studied the asymptotic behavior of the linearized semigroup in an exterior domain, and Kobayashi
[
14
,
15
]
and Kobayashi and Shibata
[
16
]
also applied these facts to establish the
L^{p}~(1\leq p\leq\infty)
estimates of solutions to the nonlinear problem in a three dimensional exterior domain.

We next give the known results for mathematical study of solutions
u=(\phi,w,G)
of (
1.7
). This is widely investigated in the whole space case
\mathbb{R}^{3}
as follows. Sideris and Thomases
[
28
]
firstly employed (
1.7
) to prove the global-in-time existence of solutions to incompressible viscoelastic system in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
via incompressible limit. Hu and Wu
[
10
]
guaranteed the global existence of solution in
H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
framework under small initial perturbation in
H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, and derived its
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
decay estimates for
2\leq p\leq 6
. Li, Wei and Yao
[
18
]
extended their result to show the global existence of
u
in
H^{N}(\mathbb{R}^{3})~(N\geq 3)
framework under small initial perturbation in
H^{N}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
and its
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
decay estimates for
6<p\leq\infty
. Their obtained decay rates is same as the heat kernel and reflects the diffusion phenomena of (
1.7
) observed from viscous effect. However, these obtained rates do not expose the wave phenomena originating from sound and elastic wave. To discover both phenomena by clarifying the large time behavior of
u
, Ishigaki
[
11
,
12
]
refined and extended their obtained
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
decay estimates for
1\leq p\leq\infty
. This is observed from the structure of the linearized semigroup. Its incompressible part behaves like diffusion wave which consists of fundamental solutions to the diffusion equation and wave equation with propagation speed
\beta
, while its compressible part is effected by diffusion wave constructed by fundamental solutions to the diffusion equation and wave equation with propagation speed
\sqrt{\beta^{2}+\gamma^{2}}
. For more mathematical studies, we refer
[
1
,
30
,
31
]
in the Sobolev framework and
[
7
,
20
,
22
,
23
]
in the Besov framework. For the mathematical study of (
1.7
) in an general domain
\Omega
, the global existence of the solution in
H^{2}(\Omega)
framework is established by Qian
[
24
]
for the case that
\Omega
is half space, exterior domain and bounded domain, provided that
u_{0}
is small in
H^{2}(\Omega)
. Moreover, Qian
[
24
]
also proved that if
\Omega
is bounded and
\int_{\Omega}\phi_{0}dx=0
holds, then the solution decays exponentially as
t\to\infty
. See also the detailed proof of Chen and Wu
[
2
]
for the bounded domain case. Hu and Wang
[
9
]
showed this global existence result in
L^{p}(\Omega)
framework with a bounded domain
\Omega
. However, there is no results for the large time behavior of u in an unbounded domain
\Omega
except
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

1.3
Main results

In this subsection, we give the main result of this paper. For
R>0
and
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
, we define
B_{R}
by
B_{R}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}~|~|x|<R\}
and then set
D_{R}:=D\cap B_{R}
. We obtain the local energy decay estimates of solution to (
1.9
) in this paper. This detail is stated as the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
1<p<\infty
, let
b_{0}>0
be
\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\Omega\subset B_{b_{0}}
and let
b>b_{0}
. If
u_{0}=(\phi_{0},w_{0},G_{0})
satisfies
u_{0}\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)\times L^{p}(\Omega)^{3}\times W^{1,p}(\Omega)^{3\times 3}
,
\nabla\phi_{0}+\mathrm{div}{}^{\top}G_{0}=0
,
G_{0}\in\nabla(W^{2,p}(\Omega)\cap W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega))^{3}
and
u_{0}(x)=0~(x\notin\Omega_{b})
, then the following local energy decay estimates of
u(t)=T(t)u_{0}
hold for all
m=0,1,2,\ldots
and
t\geq 1
:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\|\partial_{t}^{m}T(t)u_{0}\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega_{b})\times W^{2,p}(\Omega_{b})^{3}\times W^{1,p}(\Omega_{b})^{3\times 3}}&\leq C_{b,b_{0},m,p}~t^{-2-m}\|u_{0}\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)\times L^{p}(\Omega)^{3}\times W^{1,p}(\Omega)^{3\times 3}},\end{aligned}

(1.11)

where
C_{b,b_{0},m,p}
is a constant depending only on
b,b_{0},m
and
p
.

We mention the notable remark of this obtained result. For the case
\beta=0
, Kobayashi
[
13
]
established the local energy decay of the linearized semigroup in
\Omega
. Its decay rate is given by
t^{-3/2-m}
which coincides the heat kernel. On the other hand, for the case
\beta=0
, the obtained decay rate in the right-hand side of (
1.11
) is
t^{-1/2}
faster than the case
\beta=0
. This provides the different aspect from the case
\beta=0
due to the diffusion wave induced by interaction between sound wave, viscous diffusion and elastic wave, analogous to the results in
[
11
,
12
]
for the whole space case.

1.4
Outline of Theorem
1.1

We explain the proof of Theorem
1.1
briefly in this subsection. The proof of Theorem
1.1
is based on the corresponding resolvent problem to (
1.9
):

\lambda u+Au=\mathbf{f}~\text{in}~\Omega,~w|_{\partial\Omega}=0.

(1.12)

Here
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
is a resolvent parameter;
\mathbf{f}={}^{\top}(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3})
is a given pair of three functions
f_{1}=f_{1}(x)\in\mathbb{R},~f_{2}=f_{2}(x)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
f_{3}=f_{3}(x)\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}
satisfying
\nabla f_{1}+\mathrm{div}{}^{\top}f_{3}=0
and
f_{3}\in\nabla(W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega))^{3}
.
We note that
f_{3}\in\nabla(W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega))^{3}
immediately leads to the second condition of (
1.10
) with
G=f_{3}
, and plays a key role in
[
2
,
11
,
12
,
24
]
, which considered the situation
G\sim\nabla\psi
under the small initial perturbation. Here
\psi
is some vector field belonging to
(W^{2,2}(\Omega)^{3}\cap W_{0}^{1,2}(\Omega))^{3}
.
We then see that the resolvent set contains some sectorial domain. Furthermore, it is well-known that
A
generates an analytic semigroup
e^{-tA}
on
W^{1,p}(\Omega)\times L^{p}(\Omega)^{3\times 3}\times W^{1,p}(\Omega)^{3\times 3}
for
1<p<\infty
. To investigate asymptotic behavior of
e^{-tA}
as
t\to\infty
, the information of the solution to (
1.12
) near
\lambda=0
plays a crucial role and therefore we carry out the following sections, inspired by
[
13
,
25
]
. In Section 2, we introduce several notations and crucial lemmata. In Section 3, we consider the corresponding resolvent problem in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and capture the information of its solution. It is shown that the singular part of its solution as
\lambda\to 0
is described as
\lambda^{2}\log\lambda
. On the other hand, when
\beta=0
, this is only majored as
\lambda^{1/2}
in
[
13
]
. These facts give the difference between two cases
\beta=0
and
\beta>0
. In Section 4, we study the corresponding zero resolvent problem in an bounded domain. In Section 5, we focus on (
1.12
) and approximate its solution via parametrix method by using a cut-off function and the results in Sections 3 and 4. The difficulty of the proof is keeping the constraints of
f_{1}
and
f_{3}
. To overcome this, we rely on the non-local operators to modify the parametrix, and then obtain the singularity of the solution to (
1.12
) as
\lambda\to 0
. In Section 6, we derive Theorem
1.1
by complex integral and oscillating integral theory.

We next give several sets in
\mathbb{C}
for stating the results of the resolvent problem. Let
\varepsilon\in(0,\pi/2)

\delta,\lambda_{0}>0
, and let
\Xi

\displaystyle\Xi

\displaystyle:=\left\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}~|~(\mathrm{Re}\lambda+R_{0})^{2}+(\mathrm{Im}\lambda)^{2}>R_{0}^{2}\right\},~R_{0}:=\max\left\{\frac{\beta^{2}}{\nu},\frac{\beta^{2}+\gamma^{2}}{\nu+\tilde{\nu}}\right\},

\displaystyle:=\left\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}~|~(\mathrm{Re}\lambda+R_{0}+\varepsilon)^{2}+(\mathrm{Im}\lambda)^{2}\geq(R_{0}+\varepsilon)^{2}\right\}.

We then set

\displaystyle\Sigma_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle:=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}~|~|\arg\lambda|\leq\pi-\varepsilon\},~\Sigma_{\varepsilon,\delta}:=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}~|~\lambda-\delta\in\Sigma_{\varepsilon}\},

\displaystyle V_{\varepsilon,\lambda_{0}}

\displaystyle:=\{\lambda\in\Sigma_{\varepsilon}\cap\Xi~|~|\lambda|>\lambda_{0}\},~U_{\lambda_{0}}:=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}~|~|\lambda|<\lambda_{0}\},~\dot{U}_{\lambda_{0}}:=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}~|~\mathrm{Re}\lambda\geq 0,~0<|\lambda|<\lambda_{0}\}.

Here
\arg\lambda\in(-\pi,\pi]
denotes an argument of
\lambda
.

We finally introduce two important lemmata to investigate the corresponding resolvent problem. The first lemma is the Fourier multiplier theorem which plays a crucial role for studying the resolvent problem in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
1<p<\infty
, let
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
be the Schwartz space defined as a set of any rapidly decreasing
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
functions, and let
m=m(\xi)
be a smooth function in
\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{0\}
satisfying the multiplier condition

\displaystyle|\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}m(\xi)|\leq C_{\alpha}|\xi|^{-\alpha}

for any multiindex
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{3}~(|\alpha|\leq 2)
and
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{0\}
. Then the operater
T:\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\to\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
given by
(Tf)(x):=\mathcal{F}^{-1}[m(\xi)\widehat{f}(\xi)](x)
is able to be extended as
\mathcal{L}(L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
. Furthermore,
T
satisfies the estimate

\|Tf\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq C_{p}\max_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{3}~(|\alpha|\leq 2)}C_{\alpha}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}

for any
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
.

We next prepare the second lemma which assures the solvability of the elastic equation with complex coefficient to study the resolvent problem in a bounded domain. See the example for
[
29
, Proposition 2.1]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
a>0
,
b\geq 0
,
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
1<p<\infty
, and let
D
be a bounded domain with smooth boundary. Then for any
f\in W^{m,p}(D)^{3}
, there exists a unique solution
w\in W^{m+2,p}(D)^{3}\cap W_{0}^{1,p}(D)^{3}
of the Dirichlet problem

-a\Delta w-b\nabla\mathrm{div}w=f~\textrm{in}~D,\quad w|_{\partial D}=0.

(2.1)

Furthermore,
w
satisfies

\|w\|_{W^{m+2,p}(D)}\leq C\|f\|_{W^{m,p}(D)}

with some constant
C=C(a,b,m,p)

For
f\in W^{m,p}(D)^{3}
, we define
(-\Delta_{D})^{-1}f
as the unique solution of (
2.1
) with
a=1
and
b=0
.

3
Resolvent problem in the whole space

In this section, we investigate the corresponding resolvent problem to (
1.9
) in the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
be a resolvent parameter and let
\mathbf{f}={}^{\top}(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3})
be a given pair of three functions
f_{1}=f_{1}(x)\in\mathbb{R},~f_{2}=f_{2}(x)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
f_{3}=f_{3}(x)\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}
. Then the resolvent problem in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
is given by

\lambda u+Au=\mathbf{f}~\mathrm{in}~\mathbb{R}^{3},\quad u\to 0~\textrm{as}~|x|\to\infty.

(3.1)

Here
A
is given by (
1.8
). We are now going to derive the solution formula of (
3.1
). Applying the Fourier transform to the first equation of (
3.1
) with respect to
x
, we have

\displaystyle\lambda\widehat{\phi}+i\xi\cdot\widehat{w}

\displaystyle=\widehat{f_{1}},

(3.2)

\displaystyle\lambda\widehat{w}+(\nu|\xi|^{2}+\tilde{\nu}\xi^{\top}\xi)\widehat{w}+i\gamma^{2}\widehat{\phi}\xi-i\beta^{2}\widehat{G}\xi

\displaystyle=\widehat{f_{2}},

\displaystyle\lambda\widehat{G}-i\widehat{w}^{\top}\xi

\displaystyle=\widehat{f_{3}}.

We next decompose
\widehat{w}
as its incompressible part
\widehat{w}_{s}~(i\xi\cdot\widehat{w}_{s}=0)
and compressible part
\widehat{w}_{c}
:

\widehat{w}=\widehat{w}_{s}+\widehat{w}_{c},\quad\widehat{w}_{s}(\xi):=\widehat{\mathcal{P}}_{s}(\xi)\widehat{w}(\xi),~\widehat{w}_{c}(\xi):=\widehat{\mathcal{P}}_{c}(\xi)\widehat{w}(\xi),

where
\widehat{\mathcal{P}}_{s}(\xi)
and
\widehat{\mathcal{P}}_{c}(\xi)
are given by
\widehat{\mathcal{P}}_{s}(\xi):=I-\xi^{\top}\xi/|\xi|^{2}
and
\widehat{\mathcal{P}}_{c}(\xi):=\xi^{\top}\xi/|\xi|^{2}
, respectively. We then assume
\lambda\neq 0
once to rewrite the first and third equations of (
3.2
) as

\displaystyle\widehat{\phi}=\lambda^{-1}(\widehat{f_{1}}-i\xi\cdot\widehat{w}),\quad\widehat{G}=\lambda^{-1}(\widehat{f_{3}}+i\widehat{w}^{\top}\xi).

(3.3)

We next substitute (
3.3
) into the second equation of (
3.2
) and then apply
\lambda\widehat{\mathcal{P}}_{l}~(l=s,c)
to the resultant equation to arrive at

(\lambda^{2}+\kappa_{1}(\lambda)|\xi|^{2})\widehat{w}_{s}=\widehat{\mathcal{P}}_{s}(\lambda\widehat{f}_{2}+i\beta^{2}\widehat{f}_{3}\xi)

and

(\lambda^{2}+\kappa_{2}(\lambda)|\xi|^{2})\widehat{w}_{c}=\widehat{\mathcal{P}}_{c}(-i\gamma^{2}\widehat{f}_{1}\xi+\lambda\widehat{f}_{2}+i\beta^{2}\widehat{f}_{3}\xi),

where
\kappa_{1}(\lambda):=\nu\lambda+\beta^{2}
and
\kappa_{2}(\lambda):=(\nu+\tilde{\nu})\lambda+\beta^{2}+\gamma^{2}
. Therefore, assuming
\lambda^{2}+\kappa_{1}(\lambda)|\xi|^{2}\neq 0
and
\lambda^{2}+\kappa_{2}(\lambda)|\xi|^{2}\neq 0
for any
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
additionally, we are able to construct
\widehat{w}
explicitly. As a result, combining the expression of
\widehat{w}
and (
3.3
) implies that
\widehat{u}=(\widehat{\phi},\widehat{w},\widehat{G})
is derived explicitly.
In fact, we have the solution formula

\displaystyle(\Phi_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(\lambda)\mathbf{f})(x):=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left[\frac{\lambda+(\nu+\tilde{\nu})|\xi|^{2}}{\lambda^{2}+\kappa_{2}(\lambda)|\xi|^{2}}\widehat{f}_{1}(\xi)\right](x)-i\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left[\frac{\xi\cdot\widehat{f}_{2}(\xi)}{\lambda^{2}+\kappa_{2}(\lambda)|\xi|^{2}}\right](x)+(\overline{R}_{1}(\lambda)f)(x),

(3.4)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} (\mathcal{W}_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(\lambda)\mathbf{f})(x)&:=-i\gamma^{2}\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left[\frac{\widehat{f}_{1}(\xi)}{\lambda^{2}+\kappa_{2}(\lambda)|\xi|^{2}}\xi\right](x)\\
&\quad+\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left[\left(\frac{\lambda}{\lambda^{2}+\kappa_{1}(\lambda)|\xi|^{2}}\widehat{\mathcal{P}}_{s}(\xi)+\frac{\lambda}{\lambda^{2}+\kappa_{2}(\lambda)|\xi|^{2}}\widehat{\mathcal{P}}_{c}(\xi)\right)\widehat{f}_{2}(\xi)\right](x)\\
&\qquad+i\beta^{2}\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left[\left(\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}+\kappa_{1}(\lambda)|\xi|^{2}}\widehat{\mathcal{P}}_{s}(\xi)+\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}+\kappa_{2}(\lambda)|\xi|^{2}}\widehat{\mathcal{P}}_{c}(\xi)\right)\widehat{f}_{3}(\xi)\xi\right](x),\end{aligned}

(3.5)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} (\mathcal{G}_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(\lambda)\mathbf{f})(x)&:=i\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left[\left(\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}+\kappa_{1}(\lambda)|\xi|^{2}}\widehat{\mathcal{P}}_{s}(\xi)+\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}+\kappa_{2}(\lambda)|\xi|^{2}}\widehat{\mathcal{P}}_{c}(\xi)\right)\widehat{f}_{2}(\xi)^{\top}\xi\right](x)\\
&\quad+\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left[\left(\frac{\lambda+\nu|\xi|^{2}}{\lambda^{2}+\kappa_{1}(\lambda)|\xi|^{2}}\widehat{\mathcal{P}}_{s}(\xi)+\frac{\lambda+(\nu+\tilde{\nu})|\xi|^{2}}{\lambda^{2}+\kappa_{2}(\lambda)|\xi|^{2}}\widehat{\mathcal{P}}_{c}(\xi)\right)\widehat{f}_{3}(\xi)\right](x)\\
&\qquad+(\overline{R}_{3}(\lambda)f)(x),\end{aligned}

(3.6)

where
\overline{R}_{1}(\lambda)\mathbf{f}
and
\overline{R}_{3}(\lambda)\mathbf{f}
denote

\displaystyle(\overline{R}_{1}(\lambda)\mathbf{f})(x):=-\frac{\beta^{2}}{\lambda}\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left[\frac{\widehat{f_{1}}(\xi)|\xi|^{2}+{}^{\top}\xi\widehat{f}_{3}(\xi)\xi}{\lambda^{2}+\kappa_{2}(\lambda)|\xi|^{2}}\right](x),

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} (\overline{R}_{3}(\lambda)\mathbf{f})(x)&:=\frac{\gamma^{2}}{\lambda}\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left[\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}+\kappa_{2}(\lambda)|\xi|^{2}}\xi^{\top}\xi(\widehat{f}_{1}(\xi)I+\widehat{f}_{3}(\xi))\right](x)\\
&\quad+\frac{\beta^{2}}{\lambda}\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left[\left(\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}+\kappa_{1}(\lambda)|\xi|^{2}}\widehat{\mathcal{P}}_{s}(\xi)+\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}+\kappa_{2}(\lambda)|\xi|^{2}}\widehat{\mathcal{P}}_{c}(\xi)\right)|\xi|^{2}\widehat{f}_{3}(\xi)\widehat{\mathcal{P}}_{s}(\xi)\right](x),\end{aligned}

respectively. We note that
\overline{R}_{1}(\lambda)\mathbf{f}
and
\overline{R}_{3}(\lambda)\mathbf{f}
are controlled by
\lambda^{-1}
uniformly even if
\lambda
is small. To overcome this problem, we assume
\nabla f_{1}+\mathrm{div}{}^{\top}f_{3}=0
and
f_{3}\in\nabla W^{1.p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{3}
to vanish these functions.

\displaystyle:=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left[\frac{\xi^{\eta}}{|\xi|^{4}}\widehat{f}(\xi)\right](x),~[\mathcal{N}_{\eta}^{j}f](x):=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left[\frac{\xi^{\eta}}{|\xi|^{4}}\varphi_{j}(\xi)\widehat{f}(\xi)\right](x)~(j=0,\infty,~\eta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{3},~2\leq|\eta|\leq 3).

We first look at
\mathcal{M}_{\eta}^{0}f
with
f\in L_{R}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Since

is satisfied,

(i),

We next investigate

.

be

Estimating

Consequently, (

\displaystyle\mathrm{div}w

\displaystyle=f_{1}~\mathrm{in}~D,

\displaystyle=f_{2}~\mathrm{in}~D,

\displaystyle:=\left\{\lambda=iR_{1}+se^{\frac{3}{4}i\pi};~s:R\to 0\right\}\cup\left\{\lambda=is;~s:R_{1}\to\delta\right\}\cup\left\{\lambda=\delta e^{i\theta};~\theta:\frac{\pi}{2}\to-\frac{\pi}{2}\right\}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\cup\left\{\lambda=is;~s:-\delta\to-R_{1}\right\}\cup\left\{\lambda=-iR_{1}-se^{\frac{3}{4}i\pi};~s:0\to R\right\},

\displaystyle\Gamma_{R,\varepsilon}^{3}

\displaystyle:=\left\{\lambda=s-i\left(R_{1}+R\cdot\mathrm{Im}(e^{\frac{3}{4}i\pi})\right);~s:R\cdot\mathrm{Re}\left(e^{\frac{3}{4}i\pi}\right)\to\varepsilon\right\}.

Here
R_{1}:=R_{0}+(\pi/2)
.
Since
e^{\lambda t}(\lambda+A)^{-2}\in\mathcal{A}(\Xi_{\pi/4};\mathcal{L}(Y^{p}(\Omega)))
is seen from Proposition
5.1
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \int_{\Gamma_{R,\varepsilon}^{1}}e^{\lambda t}(\lambda+A)^{-2}u_{0}d\lambda&=-\int_{\widetilde{\Gamma}_{R,\delta,\varepsilon}}e^{\lambda t}(\lambda+A)^{-2}u_{0}d\lambda\\
&\qquad-\int_{\Gamma_{R,\varepsilon}^{2}}e^{\lambda t}(\lambda+A)^{-2}u_{0}d\lambda-\int_{\Gamma_{R,\varepsilon}^{3}}e^{\lambda t}(\lambda+A)^{-2}u_{0}d\lambda.\end{aligned}

(6.3)

It is clear that the left-hand side of (
6.3
) tends to the integrand in the right-hand side of (
6.2
) as
R\to\infty
. Since we have
\Gamma_{R,\varepsilon}^{2}\cup\Gamma_{R,\varepsilon}^{3}\subset\Xi_{\pi/4}\cap\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}~;~|\lambda|\geq R_{1}\}
for
\varepsilon>0
, the second and third terms in the right-hand side of (
6.3
) tend to 0 in
Y^{p}(\Omega)
as
R\to\infty
by using Proposition
5.1
. Hence, it remains to investigate the first term in the right-hand side of (
6.3
) as
R\to\infty
and
\delta\to 0
. We rewrite this as

\displaystyle-\int_{\widetilde{\Gamma}_{R,\delta,\varepsilon}}e^{\lambda t}(\lambda+A)^{-2}u_{0}d\lambda=\mathcal{I}_{\delta}^{0}(t)+\mathcal{I}_{\delta}^{1}(t)+\mathcal{I}_{R}^{2}(t),

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{\delta}^{0}(t):=i\delta\int_{|\theta|<\frac{\pi}{2}}e^{i\theta}e^{\delta e^{i\theta}t}(\delta e^{i\theta}+A)^{-2}u_{0}d\theta,

(6.4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{\delta}^{1}(t):=-i\int_{\delta<|s|<R_{1}}e^{its}(is+A)^{-2}u_{0}ds,

(6.5)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathcal{I}_{R}^{2}(t):=&\int_{0}^{R}e^{(iR_{1}+se^{\frac{3}{4}\pi i})t}(iR_{1}+se^{\frac{3}{4}\pi i}+A)^{-2}u_{0}ds-\int_{0}^{R}e^{-(iR_{1}+se^{\frac{3}{4}\pi i})t}(-iR_{1}-se^{\frac{3}{4}\pi i}+A)^{-2}u_{0}ds.\end{aligned}

(6.6)

It follows from Proposition
5.2
that (
6.4
) tends to 0 in
Y^{p}(\Omega_{b})
as
\delta\to 0
. We next consider (
6.5
). Applying integration by parts and letting
\delta\to 0
in (
6.5
) yields

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{1}(t)

\displaystyle:=\lim_{\delta\to 0}\mathcal{I}_{\delta}^{1}(t)=\int_{-R_{1}}^{R_{1}}e^{ist}\mathfrak{J}(s)u_{0}ds,

(6.7)

where
\mathfrak{J}(s)u_{0}:=(d/ds)(is+A)^{-1}u_{0}
. We make use of the following lemma to obtain the decay estimate of
\mathcal{I}^{1}(t)
in
Y^{p}(\Omega_{b})
.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
X
be a Banach spaces with
|\cdot|_{X}
and let
f=f(s)
be a
X
-valued function satisfying
f\in C(\mathbb{R};X)\cap C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\};X)
and
f(s)=0
for
s\in\mathbb{R}
with
|s|\geq\lambda_{3}
. Assume that there exists a constant
C(f)
depending on
f
such that

\left|\frac{d^{m}}{ds^{m}}f(s)\right|_{X}\leq C(f)\begin{cases}1&(m=0),\\
\max\left\{1,\left|\log|s|\right|\right\}&(m=1),\end{cases}

and define
g(t):=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{ist}f(s)ds
. Then there exists a constant
C(\lambda_{3})
depending only on
\lambda_{3}
such that
g
is estimated as

|g(t)|_{X}\leq C(\lambda_{3})C(f)(1+|t|)^{-1}

for
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

We easily see that
|g(t)|_{X}\leq C(\lambda_{3})C(f)
holds for
|t|\leq 1
. Rewriting
g
as
g(t)=\int_{|s|\leq t^{-1}}e^{ist}f(s)ds+\int_{|s|\geq t^{-1}}e^{ist}f(s)ds
and applying integration by parts to the last term once in the right-hand side of the resultant identity, we earn
|g(t)|_{X}\leq C(\lambda_{3})C(f)|t|^{-1}
for
|t|\geq 1
.
This completes the proof.
∎

Let
\chi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
be a cut-off function such that
\chi(s)=1
for
|s|\leq\lambda_{3}/2
and
\chi(s)=0
for
|s|\geq\lambda_{3}
. We then decompose (
6.7
) as
\mathcal{I}^{1}(t):=\mathcal{I}^{1,1}(t)+\mathcal{I}^{1,2}(t)
, where

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{1,1}(t):=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{its}\chi(s)\mathfrak{J}(s)u_{0}ds,~\mathcal{I}^{1,2}(t):=\int_{\frac{\lambda_{2}}{2}\leq|s|\leq R_{1}}e^{its}(1-\chi(s))\mathfrak{J}(s)u_{0}ds.

It is seen from Corollary
5.3
that
\chi(s)\mathfrak{J}(s)u_{0}
satisfies the same assumptions as
f
in Lemma
6.1
with
X=Y^{p}(\Omega_{b})
and
C(f)=C\|u_{0}\|_{X^{p}(\Omega)}
. Therefore employing this lemma, the estimate of
\mathcal{I}^{1,1}(t)
is given by

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{I}^{1,1}(t)\|_{Y^{p}(\Omega_{b})}\leq Ct^{-1}\|u_{0}\|_{X^{p}(\Omega)}

(6.8)

for
t\geq 1
. The estimate of
\mathcal{I}^{1,2}(t)
is derived as

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{I}^{1,2}(t)\|_{Y^{p}(\Omega)}\leq Ct^{-1}\|u_{0}\|_{X^{p}(\Omega)}

(6.9)

for
t\geq 1
by applying integration by parts in its definition. Here we use the fact
1-\chi(s)=0~(|s|\leq\lambda_{3}/2)
and Proposition
5.1
with
\lambda_{0}=\lambda_{3}/2
. Hence, (
6.7
), (
6.8
) and (
6.9
) yield

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{I}^{1}(t)\|_{Y^{p}(\Omega_{b})}\leq Ct^{-1}\|u_{0}\|_{X^{p}(\Omega)}

(6.10)

for
t\geq 1
. We finally consider (
6.6
) as
R\to\infty
. Noting

\displaystyle e^{\pm(iR_{1}+se^{\frac{3}{4}\pi i})t}=\pm(te^{\frac{3}{4}\pi i})^{-1}\frac{\partial}{\partial s}e^{\pm(iR_{1}+se^{\frac{3}{4}\pi i})t},\quad\pm(iR_{1}+se^{\frac{3}{4}\pi i})\in\Xi_{\frac{\pi}{4}}\cap\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}~;~|\lambda|\geq R_{1}\}

for
s\geq 0
, applying integration by parts and using Proposition
5.1
with
\lambda=\pm(iR_{1}+se^{\frac{3}{4}\pi i})
,
\varepsilon_{0}=\pi/4
and
\lambda_{0}=R_{1}
, the limit
\mathcal{I}^{2}(t):=\lim_{R\to\infty}\mathcal{I}_{R}^{2}(t)
in
Y^{p}(\Omega)
exists and we have

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{I}^{2}(t)\|_{Y^{p}(\Omega)}\leq Ct^{-1}\|u_{0}\|_{X^{p}(\Omega)}

(6.11)

for
t\geq 1
. As a result, letting
\delta\to 0
and
R\to\infty
in (
6.3
) and employing (
6.10
) and (
6.11
), (
1.11
) with
m=0
is proved for
u_{0}\in X_{b}^{p}(\Omega)\cap Y^{p}(\Omega)
. Due to the density of
Y^{p}(\Omega)
in
X^{p}(\Omega)
, we are able to relax this condition for
u_{0}
as
u_{0}\in X_{b}^{p}(\Omega)
. This completes the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Research on the influence of cutter overhang length on robotic milling chatter stability
Authors: Yongjian Ji, Runnan Liu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractSerial industrial robots are widely in demand in the field of large-scale component processing and manufacturing. However, the structure characteristic of serial robots makes it easy to generate chatter during milling and the overhang length of milling cutter has an important influence on the stability of robotic milling. Milling cutter overhang length refers to the length from the tip of the cutter to the protrusion of the spindle, to study the influence of milling cutter overhang length on the stability behavior of robotic milling, the modal parameters of milling cutter under different overhang length were obtained by hammer experiment, and its dynamic characteristics were analyzed. The stability behavior for robotic milling under different cutter overhang lengths was analyzed by using the stability lobe diagrams, and the stability lobe diagrams were verified by robotic milling experiments. The results show that the rigidity and natural frequency of milling cutter decrease with the increase of overhang length. There is a big gap between the stability lobe diagrams and the actual machining state when the cutter overhang length is short, and the low frequency vibration is easy to occur in the robotic milling process when the spindle speed is low, which is most probably because that the absorbed vibration energy by the cutter has been transferred to the robot body before the milling cutter vibrates. When the cutter overhang is increased and the feed direction is along the y-axis of the robot global coordinate system, the stability lobe diagrams by considering the multi-mode coupling effect is more consistent with the actual milling state.Keywords: Robotic milling, Cutter overhang length, Chatter, Multi-modal coupling, Stability predictionSubject terms: Engineering, Mechanical engineering

Introduction

Compared with traditional Computerized Numerical Control (CNC) machine tools, serial industrial robots have the advantages of flexible movement, wide space accessibility, easy installation, and low cost, which are widely used in painting, assembly and manufacturing, etc1–3. Especially in the field of large and complex component manufacturing, robotic milling has a wide application prospect4,5. The serial industrial robot has the characteristics of lower rigidity, leading to the robotic milling system is easy to produce chatter when it is stimulated by external strong excitation6. Chatter will reduce the surface quality of the workpiece, accelerate tool wear, affect the work performance of the equipment and even damage the robot equipment7–9. Different from the milling chatter of CNC machine tools, the stability of robotic milling is affected by the coupling effect of robot stiffness and external excitation. Therefore, the vibration characteristics of robotic milling are more complex and variable.

The main vibration types of robotic milling are forced vibration, mode coupling chatter and regenerative chatter10,11. Forced vibration is the vibration caused by external periodic interference force, which can be eliminated by determining the excitation source. Mode coupling chatter and regenerative chatter are both self-excited vibrations, which are more complex and difficult to control. Mode coupling chatter means the vibration exists simultaneously in two or more directions coupled to each other12,13. When mode coupling chatter occurs in the robotic milling process, the entire robot structure will produce severe vibration. In terms of mode coupling chatter, Pan et al.14 analyzed the mode coupling mechanism and came to the conclusion that mode coupling chatter will occur when axis with the smaller stiffness of the robot lies within the angle formed by the total cutting force and the normal to the workpiece surface. Cen et al.15 proposed a method for mode coupling chatter detection and suppression in robotic milling. Xin et al.16 proposed a low-frequency chatter stability model by considering modulated tool-workpiece engagement and the mode coupling effect of the robotic structure for robotic milling. Gienke et al.13 proposed an expanded mode coupling chatter theory which suits robot kinematics and enables industrial application. Cen and Melkote17 proposed a conservative congruence transformation-based mode coupling chatter avoidance method in robotic milling. Although there is disagreement about whether there is mode coupling chatter in robotic milling process18,19. However, the existence of low frequency chatter in the process of robotic milling is an indisputable fact and has been paid much attention by scholars8,16,20.

Regenerative chatter in milling process is mainly caused by the phase difference between the workpiece surface formed by the previous and current cutter tooth12,13. This kind of vibration mainly occurs at the milling cutter or the workpiece. In terms of robotic milling regenerative chatter, Mohammadi and Ahmadi21 studied the effect of the axial vibrations of the milling end-effector on regenerative chatter in robotic milling. The results show that the modulation of the nominal depth of cut due to axial vibrations may cause nonlinearities in the cutting forces. Xin et al.22 studied the influence of the robotic structure modes and spindle-tool structure modes on the chatter in robotic milling system. The results show that the number of cutter teeth, modal parameters and spindle speed can affect the dominant mode of robotic milling chatter, and the robotic milling chatter can be suppressed by adjusting the spindle speed or the structure of the spindle-tool system. Due to the influence of the serial structure and redundant axis, the position and pose of the robot have significant impact on its dynamic characteristics23. Wang et al.24 studied the relationship between the redundant degrees of freedom and the milling stability, and proposed the optimization method of robotic milling redundant degrees of freedom on the premise of machining stability. Liu et al.25 proposed a posture optimization method based on the dynamic response index, which significantly improves the machining stability.

Due to the robotic milling system’s dynamic behaviors are pose-dependent and nonlinear, the vibration characteristics in the milling process are more complex26,27. Especially in the robotic milling process, the modal parameters of the robot and tool tip will change with the change of the robot pose28,29. As a consequence, the machining path30,31 and the robot structural mode32 will significantly affect the stability and machining precision of the robotic milling. On the other hand, the beneficial aspect is that the stability in robotic milling along a given path can be ensured by optimizing the robot configurations, without changing the cutting parameters33. In terms of machining path and the robotic milling system’s modal parameters, Tunc and Stoddart34 proposed an approach to evaluate and select tool path patterns. Sun et al.35 comprehensively analyzed the influence of machining path, feedrate and trajectory planning on the quality of robotic milling. Tian et al.36 studied the influence of different position modal parameters on milling chatter stability of orthopedic surgery robots. Song et al.37 investigated the influence law of robotic joints to modal parameters and chatter type. The results show that under the condition of medium-high rotation speed, regenerative chatter is the main chatter type for the robotic milling system. By means of milling experiments, Hao et al.38 proved that the regenerative chatter theory is also applicable to robotic high-speed milling. Sun et al.39 proposed a machining method for parallel mirror robotic milling of thin-walled parts and constructed a time-varying delay differential equation in which the interaction of the tool-workpiece-tool system, multi-mode and distributive modal parameters are comprehensively considered.

In general, no matter what kind of vibration occurs in the robotic milling process, it will cause harm to the quality of the workpiece and the robot body. The excitation source of robotic milling chatter mainly comes from the interaction between tool and workpiece in milling process. As an important medium to transmit vibration energy to the robot, the dynamic characteristics of the milling cutter have an important effect on the vibration characteristics of the robotic milling system. As described in references37,38, the robotic milling chatter is mainly dominated by regenerative chatter when the spindle speed is high. Selecting reasonable cutting parameters by stability lobe diagram is an effective method to avoid regenerative chatter in robotic milling. Cutter tip modal parameters have an important effect on the milling stability lobe diagram. Cutters with a long overhang length are usually applied to manufacture complex parts with a high aspect ratio or cavities, such as box and shell parts in the aerospace40,41. With the change of the milling tool overhang length, the dynamic characteristics of the tool tip change, which leads to the corresponding change of the modal parameters. Milling cutter overhang length refers to the length from the tip of the cutter to the protrusion of the spindle. In the process of robotic milling, the selection of cutter overhang length is affected by the shape of the workpiece and the processing requirements. The dynamic characteristics of cutter tip with different overhang lengths are obviously different, which directly affect the chatter stability of robotic milling process. Based on CNC machine tools, Neto et al.42 studied the influence of milling cutter overhang length on workpiece surface roughness, and the results show that increasing the cutter overhang length had no significant effect on workpiece surface roughness. Ahmed and Chen43 proposed a comprehensive approach to determining minimum cutter lengths for five-axis milling. Meng et al.44 studied the effect of unilateral constraints on the dynamics of milling processes with large overhang. The results show that the modal parameters of the milling cutter with unilateral constraints change significantly, and the prediction of milling stability is more accurate.

Different from CNC machine milling, the machining dynamics of the robotic milling system is affected by the dynamics of the milling cutter and the robot body. The milling force transferred to the robot is different due to the different overhang lengths of the milling cutter, which leads to the difference of milling state and chatter type. In the research of the influence of the cutter overhang length on milling chatter stability, the current research mainly focuses on CNC machine tools, and the research conclusions are difficult to apply to the field of robotic milling. Therefore, it is necessary to deeply study the influence mechanism of milling cutter overhang length on robotic milling chatter stability, and then determine the optimal process parameters. In this paper, theoretical analysis and experimental verification are used to clarify the dynamic characteristics of milling cutter under different overhang lengths. The prediction accuracy of the stability lobe diagrams based on different modal parameters for the robotic milling state are studied, and the influence of the cutter overhang length and the cutter feed direction on robotic milling chatter stability is revealed. The research results can provide theoretical basis and technical support for selecting the optimal milling cutter overhang length and process parameters. The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. In Sect. 2, the robotic milling dynamics model is introduced. Section 3 analyzes the dynamics of the cutter tip under different overhang lengths. Section 4 analyzes the robotic milling chatter stability under different milling cutter overhangs. In Sect. 5, the stability lobe diagrams obtained by different modal parameters are experimentally verified. Finally, conclusions are presented in Sect. 6.

Dynamics model of robotic milling

The single degree of freedom and two degree of freedom milling dynamics model

The robotic milling system is mainly composed of the robot, spindle system and milling cutter. The robot and spindle system are connected through flanges. The robot stiffness changes with its position and posture. Compared with CNC machine milling, the dynamic characteristics of robotic milling are more complex and variable. In the process of robotic milling, the instantaneous impact of the milling cutter and the workpiece produces strong cutting force. On the other hand, the workpiece surface phase difference formed by the previous and current cutter teeth can easily cause the self-excited vibration of the milling cutter. The classical robotic milling system and milling model are shown in Fig. 1.

Robotic milling system and the milling dynamic model45.

In the milling process of CNC machine tool, because the rigidity of the machine body is strong enough and relatively fixed, its influence on the dynamic characteristics of the milling cutter is consistent. Different with CNC machine tools, the stiffness of serial industrial robots is pose-dependent, and the stiffness is much smaller than that of the machine. Therefore, the dynamic characteristics of the cutter tip in the process of robotic milling are more obviously affected by the robot body. Conversely, the excitation of the cutter also affects the robot body’s vibration characteristics. In Eq. (5) and Eq. (10), the modal mass m, modal damping c and modal stiffness k are directly related to the milling cutter overhang length. With the change of milling cutter overhang length, the above parameters will change at the same time, affecting the chatter stability behavior of the robotic milling.

In fact, compared with CNC milling, the factors affecting the chatter stability of robotic milling are more complex. On one hand, the overall stiffness of the robot is low and the dynamic characteristics change with the change of posture. The connection between the robot and the spindle system is no longer a constant rigid connection. The change of dynamic characteristics at the connection point leads to the change of dynamic characteristics of the spindle system, which further affects the dynamic characteristics of the cutter tip. On the other hand, the vibration energy transmitted to the robot body is different due to the difference of cutter overhang length, which results in obvious difference in the vibration characteristics of the milling cutter under different overhang lengths. Robotic milling has the characteristics of nonlinear and non-stationary, and it is difficult to establish a mechanism model that takes into account all the relevant factors involved in the real experimental environment. Therefore, in this paper, the classical robotic milling dynamics model is used to explain the factors affecting the stability of the robotic milling, and then the modal parameters of the milling cutter under different overhang lengths are obtained experimentally. The stability of robotic milling system under different cutter overhang lengths is analyzed by stability lobe diagrams and verified by milling experiment.

Dynamic characteristics analysis of the milling cutter with different overhang length

The dynamics of the cutter tip has an important influence on milling stability. Due to the weak rigidity of the robot, the milling process is easy to produce chatter. In the robotic milling system, the milling cutter and the spindle can be regarded as a rigid connection. Part of the cutting force generated in the milling process is absorbed by the milling cutter and part is transmitted to the robot body through the milling cutter. The dynamic characteristics of the cutter tip change with the change of its overhang length, and the difference of the dynamic characteristics of the cutter tip leads to the difference of the energy absorbed or transmitted by the cutter. Therefore, it is of great significance to reveal the change law of cutter tip dynamic characteristics under different overhang lengths for studying the change characteristics of robotic milling stability.

The dynamic characteristics of cutter tip under different overhang lengths were studied by hammer test. The experimental equipment mainly includes INV3062 data analyzer, force hammer and eddy current displacement sensor. During the experiment, A three-tooth carbide milling cutter with a diameter of 6 mm was used as the test object. In order to generate the excitation signal, the IEPE type hammer (stainless steel hammer) with a sensitivity of 0.196 mv/N was used to strike the cutter tip. The ZA21 series eddy current displacement sensor (standard sensitivity: 16 V/mm, sensitivity deviation:0.6%, Linear range: 0.6 ~ 1.6 mm) was used to collect the response signal. The eddy current sensor was placed on the side of the milling cutter to measure the transfer function of the milling cutter along the x-axis and y-axis directions of the robot coordinate system, respectively. The experimental site of measuring the frequency response function of the milling cutter along the x-axis of the global coordinate system is shown in Fig. 3.

The eddy current displacement sensor was placed on the side of the milling cutter (along the y-axis direction of the global coordinate system), and the force hammer was used to strike the other side of the milling cutter to generate excitation signals. To avoid accidental errors, the frequency response function was measured three times for each overhang lengths of the milling cutter (The three measurements were named as Experiment a, Experiment b and Experiment c, and each measurement used a force hammer to hammer the cutter tip 5 times), the average of the three measurement results was taken as the final result. Figure 5 shows the cutter tip frequency response functions when the milling cutter overhang lengths are 35 mm, 45 mm, 55 mm and 65 mm, respectively. It can be seen from Fig. 5 that when the cutter overhang is short, the frequency energy distribution of each order in the cutter tip is relatively uniform. With the increase of the overhang, the vibration frequency energy gradually accumulates to a fixed frequency. On the other hand, with the increase of the cutter overhang length, the consistency of the high frequency part of the cutter tip frequency response function obtained in the three tests gradually becomes worse. The main reasons for the error of the high frequency part are as follows: (1) With the increase of the cutter overhang length, its stiffness decreases, the main vibration frequency of the milling cutter decreases, the vibration energy mainly gathers in the lower frequency band, and the vibration amplitude of the high frequency part decreases, which is easy to be affected by noise. (2). The diameter of the milling cutter used in the test is relatively small, which is 6 mm, due to the characteristics of the surface profile of the milling cutter, there will inevitably be some errors in the measurement process of the cutter tip frequency response function. (3). In the hammer test, it is difficult to ensure that the force hammer hits the same position of the milling cutter each time, so a certain error is caused. (4). The measurement error of the sensor itself. However, it can be seen from Fig. 5 that the first four orders of the frequency response function obtained by different experiments have good consistency. Based on the above analysis and references47,48, in this paper the first four order modal parameters are mainly analyzed. It should be pointed out that displacement sensors perform better at low frequencies, while acceleration sensors perform better at high frequencies. However, the acceleration sensor needs to be installed on the cutter tip to measure the cutter tip frequency response function, the volume and weight of the acceleration sensor will also adversely affect the measurement results. The measurement of the cutter tip frequency response function by eddy current displacement sensor is non-contact, which can avoid the influence of the additional mass of the traditional vibration acceleration sensor on the results, and the measurement results can better reflect the inherent properties of the cutter tip. In addition, although eddy current displacement sensors are not as effective at measuring relatively high frequencies as acceleration sensors, the eddy current displacement sensor used in this paper still can effectively measure frequencies within 1–2500 Hz.

Experimental verification of the robotic milling

The above analysis shows that the energy distribution of each vibration mode in the frequency response function is different for each overhang length of the milling cutter. Especially when the cutter overhang length is large, the consistency of the frequency response function obtained by different hammer experiments is poor, and the milling process is likely to cause the mutual coupling of different vibration modes (multi-modal coupling)47,48,52. The modal parameters measured when the milling cutter overhang length was set to 35 mm and 65 mm were taken as the input parameters, respectively. The stability lobe diagrams under different orders and the milling stability lobe diagrams considering multi-modal coupling were obtained, and the experimental verification was carried out. In the experiment, the same carbide milling cutter (diameter 6 mm, 3 teeth) as described in Sect. 3 is used, and the workpiece is made of Al7075 aluminum alloy material with length, width and height of 150 mm, 20 mm and 100 mm, respectively. B06A00 type acceleration sensors are used to collect the vibration acceleration signal in the milling process. The INV3062 type data analyzer was used for data storage.

In order to collect vibration acceleration signals under different conditions and analyze the signal characteristics of sensors in different positions, two acceleration sensors are arranged in the experiment process. Sensor 1 (sensitivity: 10.371 mV/ms−2, range: 1–8000 Hz) is arranged at the workpiece, and sensor 2 (sensitivity: 10.406 mv/ms−2, range: 1–8000 Hz) is arranged at the joint of the robot to measure the vibration of the robot during the milling process. Due to the influence of the stiffness characteristics of the robot, the dynamic characteristics of the milling cutter along the x-axis and y-axis of the global coordinate system are different. Therefore, during the milling experiment, the feed direction of the milling cutter is carried out along the x-axis and y-axis of the global coordinate system respectively, as shown in Fig. 9. Figure 9(a) shows the experimental site when the milling cutter is fed along the x-axis of the global coordinate system, and Fig. 9(b) shows the experimental site when the milling cutter is fed along the y-axis of the global coordinate system.

It should be noted that when the axial cutting depth is low, obvious chatter phenomenon appears, which may be due to trajectory errors in the motion process when the milling cutter is fed along the x-axis direction of the global coordinate system, which aggravates the fluctuation amplitude of the cutting force during milling and causes chatter. With the increase of the axial cutting depth, the difference between the trajectory error of the robot and the axial cutting depth increases gradually. The source of milling force is mainly affected by the axial cutting depth, and the influence of the robot trajectory error on the milling stability can be ignored. Although the fluctuation characteristics of the signal collected by sensor 2 are not obvious, there are still chatter frequencies in the spectrum, and the amplitude of the chatter frequency is low. This is because sensor 1 is installed on the workpiece, when chatter occurs, the collected signal includes not only the vibration frequency of the milling tool, but also the vibration characteristics of the workpiece itself. Sensor 2 is installed on the robot, in addition to the fundamental frequency and the cutter tooth passing frequency, the collected information mainly includes the vibration frequency of the milling cutter.

The above analysis shows that the dynamic characteristics of the robotic milling process are significantly different when the cutter overhang length and feed direction are different. When the cutter overhang length is short (taking 35 mm as an example) and the feed direction is along the x-axis of the global coordinate system, the difference between the obtained stability lobe diagram and the actual milling state is obvious, and the milling state in the low-speed rotating region of the spindle is mainly stable. When the spindle rotates at high speed, the milling state is dominated by regenerative chatter.

When the feed direction of the milling cutter is along the y-axis of the global coordinate system, the milling state is mainly stable, but when the spindle speed is low, there is obvious low-frequency vibration in the milling process. When the cutter overhang length is relatively large (taking 65 mm as an example) and the feed direction is along the y-axis of the global coordinate system, the stability lobe diagram obtained by considering the multi-modal coupling effect is basically consistent with the actual milling state. The above results show that the factors such as cutter overhang length and feed direction have an important influence on the stability of the robotic milling process. When the cutter overhang length is short, the robot low-frequency vibration is easy to occur when the spindle is rotating at low speed. This is probably because the cutting force in the milling process is easier to be transmitted to the robot body when the cutter overhang length is short, and the rotating frequency of the spindle at low speed is close to the natural frequency of the robot, resulting in vibration of the robot. When the cutter overhang length is large, the chatter type in milling process is mainly regenerative chatter. This is mainly because when the cutter overhang is large, its stiffness decreases, and the cutter vibrates violently before the cutting force is transmitted to the robot. In addition, the dynamic characteristics of the robotic milling system are different under different feed directions, resulting in obvious differences in milling states.

Conclusion

In the process of robotic milling, the overhang length of milling cutter has an important influence on milling stability behavior. The modal parameters of the milling cutter under different overhang lengths were obtained through the hammer test, and the natural frequency of the cutter tip under different overhang lengths was analyzed. The results show that the rigidity of the milling cutter decreases with the increase of the overhang, and the natural frequency gradually decreases. The stability lobe diagrams obtained by different modal parameters are verified by experiments. The results show that the dynamic characteristics of the milling process are different with different overhang length of the milling cutter. When the overhang of the milling cutter is 35 mm, the stability lobe diagram obtained is obviously different from the actual milling state; when the cutter overhang length is 65 mm and the feed direction is along the y-axis of the global coordinate system, the stability lobe diagram obtained by considering the multi-modal coupling effect is basically consistent with the actual milling state. The reasons for the difference in vibration characteristics of milling system caused by different cutter overhang lengths is probably because that when the cutter overhang length is short, the overall stiffness of the cutter is relatively large, and during the milling process the absorbed vibration energy has been transferred to the robot body before the milling cutter vibrates. When the robot absorbs enough energy and the rotation frequency of the spindle is close to the natural frequency of the robot, the robot body vibrates at low frequency. When the energy absorbed by the robot is not enough to cause vibration, the whole robotic milling system shows a stable state. The cutter tip modal parameters obtained by the modal experiment can not reflect the energy transfer characteristics, so when the cutter overhang length is short the stability lobe diagram has a large gap with the actual machining state. When the milling cutter overhang length is larger, its stiffness decreases, and the cutter vibrates violently before the vibration energy is transmitted to the robot. The vibration is mainly focused on the milling cutter, and the vibration characteristics of the milling cutter are affected by the multi-mode coupling. Therefore, when the milling cutter overhang length is larger, the prediction result of the stability lobe diagram is relatively more accurate.

The research results of this paper can provide some theoretical basis and technical support for selecting the optimal milling cutter overhang length and process parameters. It should be noted that due to the characteristics of the surface profile of the milling cutter, there will inevitably be some errors in the measurement process of the cutter tip frequency response function. In addition, this paper mainly studies the influence of milling cutter overhang length on the chatter stability of robotic milling by experimental methods. It will be the focus of the future research to build a dynamic model of robotic milling that includes various influencing factors, and to study the effect of milling cutter overhang length on the robotic milling chatter stability by the mechanism model.

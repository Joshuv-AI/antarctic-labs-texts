First eigenvalue estimates on complete Kähler manifolds
Mingwei Wang, Xiaokui Yang

Abstract
. Let ( M , ω g ) (M,\omega_{g}) be a complete Kähler manifold of complex dimension n n . We prove that if the holomorphic sectional curvature satisfies HSC ≥ 2 \mathrm{HSC}\geq 2 , then the first eigenvalue λ 1 \lambda_{1} of the Laplacian on ( M , ω g ) (M,\omega_{g}) satisfies λ 1 ≥ 320 ​ ( n − 1 ) + 576 81 ​ ( n − 1 ) + 144 . \lambda_{1}\geq\frac{320(n-1)+576}{81(n-1)+144}. 1.
Introduction

The spectral analysis of the Laplacian operator on complete Riemannian manifolds
represents a central theme in differential geometry, with deep connections to curvature,
topology, and geometric analysis. Among the most influential results in this
direction are the fundamental theorems of Lichnerowicz (
[
Lic58
]
)
and Obata (
[
Oba62
]
), which establish precise relationships between spectral properties and geometric constraints:

Theorem 1.1

(Lichnerowicz-Obata)
.

Let
(M,g)
be a complete Riemannian manifold with real dimension
n
whose Ricci curvature satisfies
\mathrm{Ric}(g)\geq(n-1)g
. Then:

(1)

The first eigenvalue of the Laplacian satisfies

\lambda_{1}\geq n.

(2)

Moreover, if
\lambda_{1}=n
, then
(M,g)
is isometric to
\left({\mathbb{S}}^{n},g_{\mathrm{can}}\right)
.

This foundational result has inspired numerous extensions
and generalizations in various directions. There are corresponding statements for manifolds with boundary. For
the Dirichlet problem the result was achieved by Reilly (
[
Rei77
]
), and for the
Neumann problem by Escobar (
[
Esc88
]
). The development of eigenvalue comparisons began with Cheng’s work (
[
Che75
]
) on first Dirichlet eigenvalues of balls. Building upon Cheng’s upper bound for
\lambda_{1}
, Yau (
[
Yau75
]
) pioneered the search for lower bounds expressed through geometric quantities, successfully deriving an estimate incorporating Ricci curvature bounds, diameter, and volume. He further conjectured that the lower bound should depend solely on Ricci curvature and diameter which was first confirmed by Li (
[
Li79
]
) for nonnegative Ricci curvature cases. The general case was subsequently resolved by Li and Yau (
[
LY80
]
,
[
Li82
]
) through maximum principle techniques. However, their estimate for manifolds with nonnegative Ricci curvature yielded bounds half as large as observed in examples, prompting their conjecture that the true lower bound should be doubled. This final piece was established by Zhong and Yang (
[
ZY84
]
). For a comprehensive treatment of these developments, we refer to
[
LT91
]
and the references cited therein. In the context of Kähler geometry, Futaki established in

[
Fut88
]
an important eigenvalue estimate that serves as a
Kähler analogue to the classical Riemannian results.

Theorem 1.2

(Futaki)
.

Let
(M,\omega_{g})
be a complete Kähler manifold
with complex dimension
n
. Suppose that the Ricci curvature has a
lower bound
\mathrm{Ric}(g)\geq(n+1)g
, then the first eigenvalue
of the Laplacian satisfies
\lambda_{1}\geq 2(n+1)
.

In the study of spectral geometry on Kähler manifolds,
an important distinction from the Riemannian case concerns the
rigidity phenomena. While Obata’s theorem establishes a sharp
characterization of the standard sphere when equality is achieved in
the eigenvalue estimate, the corresponding situation in Kähler
geometry exhibits fundamentally different behavior. It is
well-established in the literature that no direct analogue of
Obata’s rigidity theorem holds for general Kähler manifolds
satisfying the eigenvalue equality case. This distinction arises
from several fundamental aspects including complex structure
constraints and classification complexity. Notably, recent work by Chu, Wang, and Zhang

[
CWZ25
]
has established important
rigidity results under certain natural geometric conditions.

Theorem 1.3

(Chu-Wang-Zhang)
.

Let
(M,\omega_{g})
be a complete Kähler manifold of
complex dimension
n
with Ricci curvature satisfying
\mathrm{Ric}(g)\geq(n+1)g
.
If the first eigenvalue of the Laplacian
\lambda_{1}=2(n+1)
and either

(1)

The holomorphic bisectional curvature is positive (
\mathrm{HBSC}>0
); or

(2)

The multiplicity of
\lambda_{1}
is at least
n^{2}+3
;

then
(M,\omega_{g})
is isometric to
({\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{n},\omega_{\mathrm{FS}})
.

This paper establishes new lower bounds for the first eigenvalue in terms of holomorphic sectional curvature.
To the best of our knowledge, these constitute the first lower bounds obtained through this particular approach.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
(M,\omega_{g})
be a complete Kähler manifold of complex dimension
n
.
If its holomorphic sectional curvature satisfies
\mathrm{HSC}\geq 2
, then the first
eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}
of the Laplacian on
(M,\omega_{g})
satisfies

(1.1)

\lambda_{1}\geq\frac{320(n-1)+576}{81(n-1)+144}.

In particular, for any smooth function
\psi\in{\mathbb{C}}^{\infty}(M,{\mathbb{R}})
with
\int\psi d\mathrm{vol}_{g}=0
,

(1.2)

\int_{M}|\nabla\psi|^{2}d\mathrm{vol}_{g}\geq\frac{320(n-1)+576}{81(n-1)+144}\int_{M}|\psi|^{2}d\mathrm{vol}_{g}.

The proof of Theorem
1.4
introduces a new Bochner-Kodaira
formula connecting holomorphic sectional curvature to
Hodge-theoretic decompositions of eigenfunction differentials. This reveals a deep
interplay between curvature and spectral geometry, providing new insights into the
analytic and geometric structure of Kähler manifolds.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
(M,\omega_{g})
be a compact Kähler manifold and
f\in C^{\infty}(M,{\mathbb{R}})
be a smooth function
satisfying
\Delta_{\overline{\partial}}f=\lambda f
for some
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}
, then the following identity holds:

(1.3)

2\lambda\int_{M}|\varphi|^{4}\frac{\omega^{n}}{n!}=\int_{M}\left(R(V,\overline{V},V,\overline{V})+|\varphi|^{2}|\partial\overline{\partial}f|^{2}\right)\frac{\omega^{n}}{n!}+\left\|\omega_{1}-\lambda f\varphi\right\|^{2}+\|\omega_{1}\|^{2},

where
\varphi=\partial f=\omega_{g}(\cdot,\overline{V})
,

\omega_{1}=\{\partial_{E}\varphi,\varphi\}
and
E=T^{*1,0}M
. In particular, one has

(1.4)

2\lambda\int_{M}|V|^{4}\frac{\omega^{n}}{n!}\geq\int_{M}R(V,\overline{V},V,\overline{V})\frac{\omega^{n}}{n!}.

It is well-known that
\left({\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{n},\omega_{\mathrm{FS}}\right)
has

\mathrm{Ric}(\omega_{\mathrm{FS}})=(n+1)\omega_{\mathrm{FS}},\quad\mathrm{HSC}\equiv 2\quad\mbox{ and}\quad\lambda_{1}=2(n+1).

However, the optimal lower bound of

\lambda_{1}
for holomorphic sectional curvature exhibits qualitatively different
behavior from the Ricci curvature case, as demonstrated by the following explicit example.

Example 1.6
.

Consider the product manifold
(M,\omega_{g})
defined as:

(\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{d_{1}},a_{1}\omega_{\mathrm{FS}})\times\cdots\times(\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{d_{k}},a_{k}\omega_{\mathrm{FS}})\,,

where
a_{i}>0
and
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}=1
. This manifold has complex dimension
d_{1}+\cdots+d_{k}
and
holomorphic sectional curvature
\mathrm{HSC}\geq 2
. A straightforward computation shows that the first eigenvalue of the Laplacian on
(M,\omega_{g})
is given by:

(1.5)

\lambda_{1}=2\min_{i}\left\{\frac{d_{i}+1}{a_{i}}\right\}.

This example demonstrates that the optimal lower bound
\Lambda
of
\lambda_{1}
is
independent of the complex dimension
n
and satisfies

(1.6)

\frac{320}{81}<\Lambda\leq 4.

We propose:

Conjecture 1.7
.

Let
(M,\omega_{g})
be an
n
-dimensional compact Kähler manifold with holomorphic sectional
curvature
\mathrm{HSC}\geq 2
. Then the first
eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}\geq 4
.
Moreover,
\lambda_{1}=4
if and only if
(M,\omega_{g})
is isometric to
({\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{1},\omega_{\mathrm{FS}})
.

2.
Proof of the main theorem

Let
(M,\omega_{g})
be a compact Kähler manifold and
E=T^{*1,0}M
be the holomorphic tangent bundle.
Given a smooth real function
f\in C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})
,
we define
\varphi:=\partial f\in\Gamma(M,E)
as its
(1,0)
-gradient and let
V
be the dual vector field of
\overline{\varphi}
with respect to the Kähler metric.
For notational convenience, we introduce the following differential
1
-forms:

(2.1)

\omega_{1}=\{\partial_{E}\varphi,\varphi\},\qquad\omega_{2}=\{\overline{\partial}_{E}\varphi,\varphi\},

where
\partial_{E}
and
\overline{\partial}_{E}
denote the
(1,0)
and
(0,1)
components of the Chern connection on

E
respectively. These forms satisfy the identity

(2.2)

\partial|\varphi|^{2}=\omega_{1}+\overline{\omega}_{2}.

We present two computational lemmas.

Lemma 2.1
.

One has
\omega_{2}=I_{V}\partial\overline{\partial}f
and

(2.3)

\langle\omega_{2},\omega_{2}\rangle=-\langle\{\overline{\partial}_{E}\varphi,\overline{\partial}_{E}\varphi\}_{E},\varphi\wedge\overline{\varphi}\rangle,\quad\langle\omega_{1},\omega_{1}\rangle=\langle\{\partial_{E}\varphi,\partial_{E}\varphi\}_{E},\varphi\wedge\overline{\varphi}\rangle.

Proof.

If we write
\partial\overline{\partial}f=f_{i\overline{j}}dz^{i}\wedge d\overline{z}{}^{j}
and
\varphi=\varphi_{i}dz^{i}
, then
\overline{\partial}_{E}\varphi=f_{i\overline{j}}d\overline{z}^{j}\otimes dz^{i}
and

(2.4)

\omega_{2}=\{f_{i\overline{j}}d\overline{z}^{j}\otimes dz^{i},\varphi_{k}dz^{k}\}_{E}=g^{i\overline{k}}f_{i\overline{j}}\overline{\varphi}_{k}d\overline{z}{}^{j}.

On the other hand, one has
V=g^{i\overline{k}}\overline{\varphi}_{k}\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{i}}
and

(2.5)

I_{V}\partial\overline{\partial}f=g^{i\overline{k}}\overline{\varphi}_{k}I_{i}(f_{\ell\overline{j}}dz^{\ell}\wedge d\overline{z}{}^{j})=g^{i\overline{k}}f_{i\overline{j}}\overline{\varphi}_{k}d\overline{z}{}^{j},

and we establish the identity
\omega_{2}=I_{V}\partial\overline{\partial}f
. For the second identity, one has

(2.6)

\langle\omega_{2},\omega_{2}\rangle=g^{\ell\overline{j}}g^{i\overline{a}}g^{b\overline{k}}f_{i\overline{j}}\overline{f}_{k\overline{\ell}}\overline{\varphi}_{a}\varphi_{b}.

On the other hand, one can see clearly that

(2.7)

\{\overline{\partial}_{E}\varphi,\overline{\partial}_{E}\varphi\}_{E}=g^{i\overline{k}}f_{i\overline{j}}\overline{f}_{k\overline{\ell}}d\overline{z}{}^{j}\wedge dz^{\ell},

and so

(2.8)

-\langle\{\overline{\partial}_{E}\varphi,\overline{\partial}_{E}\varphi\}_{E},\varphi\wedge\overline{\varphi}\rangle=g^{\ell\overline{a}}g^{b\overline{j}}g^{i\overline{k}}f_{i\overline{j}}\overline{f}_{k\overline{\ell}}\overline{\varphi}_{a}\varphi_{b}.

Equations (
2.6
) and (
2.8
) give

(2.9)

\langle\omega_{2},\omega_{2}\rangle=-\langle\{\overline{\partial}_{E}\varphi,\overline{\partial}_{E}\varphi\}_{E},\varphi\wedge\overline{\varphi}\rangle.

For the third identity, if we write
\partial_{E}\partial f=t_{ij}dz^{i}\otimes dz^{j}
where
t_{ij}=t_{ji}=\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial z^{i}\partial z^{j}}-\Gamma_{ij}^{k}\frac{\partial f}{\partial z^{k}}
,
then by similar computations, we have

(2.10)

\omega_{1}=\{t_{ij}dz^{i}\otimes dz^{j},\varphi_{k}dz^{k}\}_{E}=g^{i\overline{k}}t_{ij}\overline{\varphi}_{k}dz^{j},

and

(2.11)

\langle\omega_{1},\omega_{1}\rangle=g^{j\overline{\ell}}g^{i\overline{a}}g^{b\overline{k}}t_{ij}\overline{t}_{k\ell}\overline{\varphi}_{a}\varphi_{b}=\langle\{\partial_{E}\varphi,\partial_{E}\varphi\}_{E},\varphi\wedge\overline{\varphi}\rangle.

This completes the proof.∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\varphi=\partial f\in\Omega^{1,0}(M)
. For any smooth function
p\in C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})
, one has

(2.12)

\partial^{*}\left(p\varphi\wedge\overline{\varphi}\right)=p(\Delta_{\overline{\partial}}f)\overline{\varphi}-p\omega_{1}-\langle\varphi,\partial p\rangle\overline{\varphi},

and

(2.13)

\overline{\partial}{}^{*}\left(p\varphi\wedge\overline{\varphi}\right)=-p(\Delta_{\overline{\partial}}f)\overline{\varphi}+p\omega_{1}+\langle\overline{\varphi},\overline{\partial}p\rangle\varphi.

In particular, for any
\varepsilon>0
, one has

(2.14)

\partial^{*}\left(\frac{\varphi\wedge\overline{\varphi}}{|\varphi|^{2}+\varepsilon}\right)=\frac{(\Delta_{\overline{\partial}}f)\overline{\varphi}-\overline{\omega}_{1}}{|\varphi|^{2}+\varepsilon}+\frac{\langle\varphi,\overline{\omega}_{2}+\omega_{1}\rangle}{(|\varphi|+\varepsilon)^{2}}\cdot\overline{\varphi},

and

(2.15)

\overline{\partial}{}^{*}\left(\frac{\varphi\wedge\overline{\varphi}}{|\varphi|^{2}+\varepsilon}\right)=\frac{-(\Delta_{\overline{\partial}}f)\varphi+\omega_{1}}{|\varphi|^{2}+\varepsilon}-\frac{\langle\overline{\varphi},\omega_{2}+\overline{\omega}_{1}\rangle}{(|\varphi|+\varepsilon)^{2}}\cdot\varphi.

Proof.

By using the elementary identity
\partial^{*}=\sqrt{-1}[\Lambda,\overline{\partial}]
, one has

\displaystyle\partial^{*}\left(p\varphi\wedge\overline{\varphi}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{-1}[\Lambda,\overline{\partial}]\left(p\varphi\wedge\overline{\varphi}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{-1}\Lambda\overline{\partial}\left(p\varphi\wedge\overline{\varphi}\right)-\overline{\partial}(p|\varphi|^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{-1}\Lambda\left(p\overline{\partial}\varphi\wedge\overline{\varphi}+\overline{\partial}p\wedge\varphi\wedge\overline{\varphi}\right)-|\varphi|^{2}\overline{\partial}p-p\overline{\partial}|\varphi|^{2}.

On the other hand, one has the contraction formula
\sqrt{-1}\Lambda=g^{i\overline{j}}I_{\overline{j}}I_{i}
and so

(2.16)

\sqrt{-1}\Lambda(\overline{\partial}\varphi\wedge\overline{\varphi})=g^{i\overline{j}}I_{\overline{j}}(I_{i}\overline{\partial}\varphi\wedge\overline{\varphi})=(\Delta_{\overline{\partial}}f)\overline{\varphi}+I_{V}\partial\overline{\partial}f.

Moreover,

(2.17)

\sqrt{-1}\Lambda(\overline{\partial}p\wedge\varphi\wedge\overline{\varphi})=-g^{i\overline{j}}\varphi_{i}I_{\overline{j}}(\overline{\partial}p\wedge\overline{\varphi})=|\varphi|^{2}\overline{\partial}p-(\overline{V}p)\overline{\varphi}.

Therefore,

(2.18)

\partial^{*}\left(p\varphi\wedge\overline{\varphi}\right)=p(\Delta_{\overline{\partial}}f)\overline{\varphi}+pI_{V}\partial\overline{\partial}f-(\overline{V}p)\overline{\varphi}-p\overline{\partial}|\varphi|^{2}.

By Lemma
2.1
, we obtain

(2.19)

\partial^{*}\left(p\varphi\wedge\overline{\varphi}\right)=p(\Delta_{\overline{\partial}}f)\overline{\varphi}-p\omega_{1}-\langle\varphi,\partial p\rangle\overline{\varphi},

and the second equation can be proved in similar ways.

(3.2)

\lambda\int_{M}|\varphi|^{4}\frac{\omega^{n}}{n!}=(\Delta_{\overline{\partial}}f\cdot\varphi,-\omega_{1}-\overline{\omega}_{2}+\Delta_{\overline{\partial}}f\cdot\varphi),

On the other hand, we have

\displaystyle\left(\partial\left(|\varphi|^{2}\overline{\varphi}\right)-\partial|\varphi|^{2}\wedge\overline{\varphi},\partial\overline{\partial}f\right)

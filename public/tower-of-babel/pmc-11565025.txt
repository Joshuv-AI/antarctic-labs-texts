A novel hybrid forecasting system for crude oil futures prices: A dual perspective of deterministic forecasting and uncertainty analysis
Authors: Hua Luo, Yue Yu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe crude oil market occupies a crucial place in the composition of the global economy, however, the high level of uncertainty in crude oil prices makes it a challenging task to construct reliable forecasting techniques to obtain reliable expected results. Therefore, to effectively cope with the high uncertainty and the technical limitations of existing forecasting studies, this study constructs a hybrid forecasting system from the dual perspectives of deterministic forecasting and uncertainty analysis. With the concept of “denoising-integration” as the core, the system integrates several denoising techniques, econometric methods, and artificial intelligence methods, and establishes a hybrid forecasting model with high accuracy and robustness with the assistance of multi-objective optimization theory. Furthermore, by introducing fuzzy theory onto the basis of deterministic prediction results, an interval forecasting model with a narrower interval width and better comprehensive performance is constructed under the premise of ensuring that the coverage of prediction intervals meets the requirements of the confidence level. In this study, the forecasting system is applied to four global core crude oil futures markets, and the empirical study finds that the performance is improved by more than 50 % compared with the benchmark model, which has an impressive performance advantage.Keywords: Deterministic forecasting, Uncertainty analysis, De-noising, Integrated forecasting

Introduction

The immeasurable strategic position of crude oil (CO) as the major source of energy for global production makes its price volatility closely linked to macroeconomic dynamics [1]. Therefore, the view that detailed analysis and forecasting of CO prices is crucial for the development of formulation management plans, stabilization of economic and social development, and diversification of investment risks has become a consensus in the academic community [2]. However, given the swift fluctuations in geopolitical dynamics and market sentiments, the sequence studied is often non-stationary, irregular, and rough, and the performance of the existing forecasting technologies is facing serious challenges. Therefore, dependable and precise price forecasting research is the long-term theme of global scholars [3].

After a relatively comprehensive review of the extant literature in the field of crude oil price forecasting, existing research techniques can be categorized into three groups; econometric methods, machine learning (ML) and artificial intelligence (AI) techniques, and hybrid techniques [4].

Econometric methods are dominated by time series analysis and regression analysis, representative of which are autoregressive integrated moving average (ARIMA) and vector autoregression (VAR) [5], etc. This type of method has been widely used in the early stage of the research on CO price forecasting. Represented by Lutz Kilian and his co-authors, a monthly crude oil futures price forecasting model was built based on VAR, which proved that VAR possesses superior performance than autoregressive and autoregressive moving average in crude oil futures price forecasting [6]. Similarly, they built separate forecasting models based on VAR for different crude oil price influencing factors and beat the unchanged forecasting and random wandering models in terms of performance through a weighting strategy [7,8]. Furthermore, Mohammadi and Su (2010) modeled and forecasted the weekly CO spot price based on ARIMA [9]. However, such methods are restricted by strict prior assumptions, and their forecasting performance has been widely criticized due to their inability to effectively adapt to the nonlinear characteristics of (CO) prices [10]. With the advancement of knowledge and technology, ML and AI techniques that can capture the nonlinear and non-stationary characteristics of the series more effectively have gradually replaced the traditional econometric methods as cutting-edge technology in CO price forecasting by virtue of their flexibility, accuracy, and universal applicability [11]. For example, Fan et al. (2016) verified its superiority in CO research by building a support vector machine (SVM) model [12].

Although ML and AI techniques can significantly improve forecasting accuracy, a large body of evidence shows that such models generally suffer from poor interpretability, weak generalization, low robustness as well as applicability, and are prone to fall into the traps of overfitting and local optimality [13]. Therefore, in recent years, hybrid models coupling data preprocessing strategies, econometric methods, and artificial intelligence techniques, aiming to synthesize the advantages of each party to obtain more accurate prediction results, have received more attention [14].

Many studies have confirmed that daily frequency CO futures price series usually exhibit low signal-to-noise ratio, strong volatility, non-stationarity, heteroskedasticity, autocorrelation, and other complex features that hinder the effectiveness of statistical analysis and modeling. Therefore, a single model is not sufficient to accurately forecast the expected changes in CO prices. Currently, the integration model and the decomposition-integration model are the two hybrid forecasting models for CO prices that are most frequently employed [15]. Integrated model refers to combining the forecasts of multiple single models [16], and this type of approach has been shown to significantly outperform single forecasting models in CO price forecasting [17]; decomposition-integration model, on the other hand, is the approach that decomposes data into different subsequences, then models these subsequences independently, and ultimately integrates their forecasts [18], allowing for the effective capture of the multiple time scales and different features present in the data [19]. As in the research of Sun et al. (2022), more accurate forecasting results were obtained by decomposing the initial CO futures price series into a series of components [20].

However, existing studies on hybrid forecasting of CO prices suffer from two shortcomings in that on the one hand less attention is paid to the noise inherent in the crude oil price series, and on the other hand fewer researches include considerations of the uncertainty that exists in the forecasting process.

Distinguished from the integrated model and decomposition-integration model, the core of the construction of a denoising-based hybrid forecasting model is to remove the highest frequency noise component from the original sequence and train the model using reconstructed sequences to reduce the interference of noisy information in the modeling process. Wang et al. (2022) built a denoising-based hybrid model to achieve higher accuracy in forecasting the price of agricultural products [21]; Xu and Wang (2023) also claimed that the denoising technique can better tap the true trend of the sequence and significantly enhance the individual model's capacity [22]. Therefore, it is natural to assume that denoising is a key step to improve the quality of analyzing and forecasting daily frequency CO futures price series.

In addition, with the profound understanding of scholars on nonlinear complex systems, the results of deterministic point forecasts on account of expectations can no longer satisfy the demands of risk management in the modern CO market [23]. In the global environment of sharp fluctuations in CO prices [24], the interval forecasting method that grasps the uncertainty of expected CO price changes with the help of uncertainty analysis can provide more useful references for the users of forecasting information [25] and can minimize the risk of CO market management [26], which is one of the optimal means of efficiently avoiding the risk of forecasting in the present time and has received extensive attention from scholars [27]. Based on whether they are strictly constrained by a priori statistical assumptions, interval forecasting models can be divided into parametric and nonparametric models [28]. The former may result in forecasting failure in practical applications due to inaccurate assumptions of distribution. Also, although commonly used nonparametric models such as Bootstrap and upper and lower bound estimation can adaptively generate forecast intervals, they are not suitable for forecasting complex and variable CO futures prices due to the complex computational process and the risk of overfitting [29].

According to the above viewpoints, this study integrates the cutting-edge ideas of denoising, integration, and multi-objective optimization. First, the denoising module is integrated into the prediction system, aiming at fully mining the real trend and changing the rule of the sequence, reducing the misleading nature of the noise, and intensifying the comprehensive performance. However, the traditional denoising algorithms have poor generalization ability and poor adaptability to different noise types and other problems, and it is difficult to make the most reasonable denoising algorithm choice in practice, therefore, this study introduces four common denoising algorithms in the denoising module which has been proved to be excellent in many empirical evidences, to make up for the shortcomings of a single denoising algorithm. Second, nine forecasting techniques, including econometric models as well as shallow and deep machine learning models, are introduced into the forecasting module, and the performance advantages of each single model are combined with the assistance of multi-objective optimization theory, which significantly improves its forecasting accuracy and robustness. Finally, this study introduces the fuzzy theory and establishes a fuzzy interval prediction model with better interval width and comprehensive performance while ensuring that the coverage ratio meets the risk level requirements. The empirical study shows that the hybrid forecasting system can provide accurate and robust CO price deterministic forecasting and uncertainty analysis results for the four core CO futures markets around the world, which effectively expands the reference sources for risk assessment by information users.

Module 2: forecasting

This module introduces ARIMA, Backpropagation Neural Network (BPNN) [34], Elman Neural Network (ELMAN) [35], General Regression Neural Network (GRNN) [36], Extreme Learning Machine (ELMNN) [37], SVM [38], Focused Time-Delay Neural Network (FTDNN) [39], Nonlinear Autoregressive (NAR) [40], and Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) [41] models as baseline models and trains the sub-forecasting models by using the raw unprocessed sequences A0(t) and the reconstructed sequences Ade(t) with the noise component removed, obtained from Module 1, as the model inputs, respectively.

Module 3: screening

To avoid excessive computational burden due to too many models, this module screens out the four sub-models with the highest accuracy from a series of constructed sub-models based on the performance evaluation results as the candidate models for the combined model, whose corresponding out-of-sample forecast values are Pˆj(t), j=1,2,3,4.

Experiments and evaluations

In this section, three empirical experiments are carried out to confirm the superior performance of the model proposed in this study, and the process of implementing the experiments, the conclusions, and also the data sets and evaluation metrics required are described in detail.

Data sets

In this study, the daily closing prices of four global core CO futures contracts, including the West Texas Intermediate CO futures contract (WTI) listed and traded on the New York Mercantile Exchange (NYMEX), Brent CO futures contract (Brent) listed, and traded on the Intercontinental Exchange (ICE), Oman CO futures contract (Oman) launched on the Dubai Mercantile Exchange (DME), and Shanghai CO futures contract (SC) traded on the Shanghai International Energy Exchange (SIEX), are selected as the research object. The data were obtained from the WIND database as well as the website of Investing1 and each contains 1288 daily frequency data points.

The dataset samples are divided into two parts, where the training set contains 1000 data points and the testing set contains 288 data points. Theoretically, this test targets the forecast error and constructs a test statistic by two-by-two comparison to test whether it obeys a normal distribution. Conclusion

Sharp fluctuations in CO prices may exacerbate global economic crises, political crises, and financial market crises; therefore, accurate and reliable CO price forecasting is a shared global goal. This study is dedicated to constructing a hybrid CO price forecasting system that can simultaneously perform deterministic forecasting and uncertainty analysis, aiming to provide CO market participants and managers with a comprehensive hint of expected risks from the dual perspectives of point forecasting and interval forecasting. The system takes “denoising-integration” as the core of the research, firstly, it integrates multiple denoising techniques and significantly reduces the impact of noise on the forecasting results based on synthesizing the performance advantages of various denoising techniques. Secondly, in the process of deterministic forecasting, a multi-objective intelligent optimization algorithm is introduced to optimize the model weights of multiple sub-forecasting models integrated with denoising techniques, so as to establish a robust and adaptive hybrid forecasting model, which significantly improves the error accuracy, trend accuracy, and directional accuracy of the deterministic forecasting of CO prices, and reduces the forecasting uncertainty caused by the model uncertainty and data uncertainty. Finally, on the foundation of deterministic forecasting, fuzzy theory is incorporated to establish a fuzzy interval forecasting model with the minimum interval width and optimal comprehensive performance by using a multi-objective optimization algorithm on the premise of ensuring that the coverage of forecasting intervals meets the requirements of the confidence level. The empirical study confirms that the hybrid forecasting system constructed in this study achieves excellent forecasting performance in the four core CO futures markets of interest, and can provide valuable reference information for stakeholders to formulate relevant policies and manage expected risks.

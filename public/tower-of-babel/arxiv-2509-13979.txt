Abstract
Multisets are like sets, except that they can contain multiple copies of their elements. If there are n i n_{i} copies of i i , 1 ≤ i ≤ t 1\leq i\leq t , in multiset M t M_{t} , then there are ( n 1 + ⋯ + n t n 1 , … , n t ) \binom{n_{1}+\cdots+n_{t}}{n_{1},\ldots,n_{t}} possible permutations of M t M_{t} . Knuth showed how to factor any multiset permutation into cycles. For fixed n i n_{i} , i ≥ 1 i\geq 1 , we show how to adapt the Chinese restaurant process, which generates random permutations on n n elements with weighting θ # ​ cycles \theta^{\#\,{\rm cycles}} , θ > 0 \theta>0 , sequentially for n = 1 , 2 , … n=1,2,\ldots , to the multiset case, where we fix the n i n_{i} and build permutations on M t M_{t} sequentially for t = 1 , 2 , … t=1,2,\ldots . The number of cycles of a multiset permutation chosen uniformly at random, i.e. θ = 1 \theta=1 , has distribution given by the sum of independent negative hypergeometric distributed random variables. For all θ > 0 \theta>0 , and under the assumption that n i = O ⁡ ( 1 ) n_{i}=O(1) , we show a central limit theorem as t → ∞ t\to\infty for the number of cycles.

Introduction

A growth rule which received considerable attention in the literature is the following preferential attachment algorithm on permutations written as the product of cycles known as the Chinese restaurant process (CRP)
[
1
,
6
,
9
,
14
,
17
]
.
For
n\geq 1
, given a permutation of
[n-1]:=\left\{1,2,\ldots,n-1\right\}
which has been constructed at step
n-1
,
element
n
is either appended to the permutation as a new singleton cycle with probability
\frac{\theta}{\theta+n-1}
, or inserted into a random position within
the existing permutation with probability equal to
\frac{n-1}{\theta+n-1}
.
Let
S_{n}
be the set of permutations on
[n]
.
The permutation obtained at step
n
has the Ewens distribution, which is the uniform distribution on
n!
permutations in the case
\theta=1
, and, in general, has probability
distribution

\theta^{|\pi|}/\theta_{(n)}
,
\pi\in S_{n}
,
where
\theta_{(n)}:=\theta(\theta+1)\cdots(\theta+n-1)
and
|\pi|
is the number of cycles in
\pi
.

Let
\Pi_{n}
be the random permutation of
[n]
at the
n
th step of the CRP.
The distribution of
\Pi_{n}
is invariant under conjugations.
For different orders the permutations are consistent, in the sense that
\Pi_{m}
, for
m<n
, can be derived from
\Pi_{n}
by removing elements
m+1,\ldots,n
from their cycles and deleting empty cycles if necessary.

The CRP expresses every permutation
\pi
of
[n]
uniquely as the product

\pi=(x_{11}\ldots x_{1n_{1}}y_{1})(x_{21}\ldots x_{2n_{2}}y_{2})\cdots(x_{t1}\ldots x_{tn_{t}}y_{t}),\quad t\geq 0,

where the following two conditions are satisfied:

1.

y_{1}<y_{2}<\ldots<y_{t}

2.

y_{i}<x_{ij}{\rm\ for\ }1\leq j\leq n_{i},1\leq i\leq t
.

We will see in Section
2
that there is a similar unique expression for
permutations of multisets.

The number of cycles in
\Pi_{n}
, denoted by

K_{n}
,
has probability generating function (p.g.f.)

{\mathbb{E}}z^{K_{n}}=\frac{(\theta z)_{n}}{(\theta)_{n}},

which corresponds to the distribution of the sum of
n
independent Bernoulli variables

K_{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}I_{i}
,

I_{i}\sim{\rm Bernoulli}(i/(i+\theta-1))
.
We have
I_{i}=1
exactly when the element
i
starts a new cycle.
As
n\to\infty
,

K_{n}\sim\theta\log n~~{\rm a.s.},~~~\frac{K_{n}-\theta\log n}{\sqrt{\theta\log n}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\to}}{\rm N}(0,1).

The CRP for multiset permutations

Given a fixed sequence of positive integers
n_{i}\geq 1
,
i\geq 1
, we will construct a CRP process on permutations of multisets indexed by integers
t\geq 0
. We define
M(n_{1},\ldots,n_{t})

n_{i}

i
for

,
t\geq 1
, and
\emptyset
for
t=0
. We give the set of elements

of
M(n_{1},\ldots,n_{t})

The set of permutations of

M(n_{1},\ldots,n_{t})

|S(n_{1},\ldots,n_{t})|=\binom{N_{t}}{n_{1},\ldots,n_{t}},

N_{t}=n_{1}+\cdots+n_{t}

Note that

S_{n}=S(\underbrace{{1,1,\ldots,1}}_{\text{$n$ times}}).

The random multiset permutation
\tilde{\Pi}_{t}
of our process at the
t
th step is an element of

S(n_{1},\ldots,n_{t})
, represented in accordance with (
6
).
At step

, there are
n_{t}
copies of

either to be inserted in the existing permutation

\tilde{\Pi}_{t-1}
or to be put into singleton cycles. (It is not possible to put more than one copy of

in a new cycle because of condition 2 of Theorem
1

Suppose that
k_{t}\in\{0\}\cup[n_{t}]
of the
n_{t}
copies of
t

new singleton cycles containing
t
, and the
n_{t}-k_{t}

The
n_{t}-k_{t}

can be inserted to the left of any of the

formatted like (

\binom{N_{t-1}-1+n_{t}-k_{t}}{n_{t}-k_{t}},

(7)

recalling that

\binom{-1}{k}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l l}1&{\rm if\ }k=0;\\

We now define the multiset CRP.
Given

F_{t}(\theta)=\sum_{k_{t}=0}^{n_{t}}\binom{N_{t-1}-1+n_{t}-k_{t}}{n_{t}-k_{t}}\theta^{k_{t}}

The special case

F_{t}(1)=\sum_{k_{t}=0}^{n_{t}}\binom{N_{t-1}-1+n_{t}-k_{t}}{n_{t}-k_{t}}=\binom{N_{t}}{n_{t}},

10

Note that (

At time

\binom{N_{t}}{n_{1},\ldots,n_{t}}=\prod_{s=1}^{t}\binom{N_{s}}{n_{s}}

.

Given

We will use the method in

The negative hypergeometric distribution

. A negative hypergeometric random variable

with these parameters has probability mass function

{\mathbb{P}}(Y=\kappa)=\frac{\binom{M}{r-1}\binom{N-M}{\kappa-r}}{\binom{N}{\kappa-1}}\cdot\frac{M-r+1}{N-\kappa+1}

It is easily checked that (
13

) with

N=N_{t}
<

,

and
\kappa=k+1

is

and the third centred moment (see

{\mathbb{E}}([Y-{\mathbb{E}}(Y)]^{3})={\rm Var}(Y)\cdot\frac{(2N-M+1)(M+1-2r)}{(M+1)(M+3)}.

Discussion

Potentially, improvements might be made on these results.
The CRP we have defined places all

Placing them sequentially seems like a difficult problem. It would be interesting to get better estimates for

see
[
2
]
.
By considering the process of cycle counts

The mediating role of job and life satisfaction in the effect of psychological resilience on employee performance: evidence from the February 6 Kahramanmaraş (Türkiye) earthquakes
Authors: Hamran Amırlı, Elkhan Gurbanli
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe purpose of this study is to investigate the mediating roles of job satisfaction (JS) and life satisfaction (LS) in the relationship between psychological resilience (PR) and employee performance (EP). Data for this study, which employed quantitative research methods, were collected using a survey technique. Research data were collected from social service workers in provinces affected by the February 6 Kahramanmaraş earthquakes. SPSS and Smart PLS software were used to test the hypotheses. Upon examining the results obtained from direct hypothesis tests, it was found that PR had a significant and positive impact on EP, JS, and LS. Additionally, it was found that JS had a significant impact on EP, whereas LS did not have a significant effect on EP. When examining the mediating hypothesis tests, it was determined that JS played a mediating role in the relationship between PR and EP. In contrast, LS did not mediate this effect.Keywords: Psychological resilience (PR), Job satisfaction (JS), Life satisfaction (LS), Employee performance (EP)

Introduction

Earthquakes are natural disasters that not only cause physical destruction in the regions where they occur but also profoundly affect individuals’ lives, leading to long-term psychological consequences [1]. Türkiye is situated on the Anatolian Plate, which is characterized by high seismic activity, and has been affected by numerous destructive earthquakes throughout its history. Approximately 20 major earthquakes with a moment magnitude (Mw) of 7 or higher have occurred in Türkiye between 1900 and 2025 [2, 3]. This situation places Türkiye among the world’s most vulnerable countries in terms of earthquake-related loss of life and material damage. Among these earthquakes, the most destructive in terms of loss of life, level of destruction, and material damage were recorded as the 2023 Kahramanmaraş, 1939 Erzincan, and 1999 Gölcük (Marmara) earthquakes, respectively [3, 4].

The 1999 Marmara earthquake, unlike previous earthquakes, affected Turkey’s most critical production regions and pushed the Turkish economy, which was already in a contraction phase, into an even deeper contraction [5]. The earthquake caused serious damage to communication systems and energy distribution infrastructure, particularly road and rail transportation. This destruction of infrastructure had a significant negative impact on economic activity and social life [6]. As a result of the earthquake, approximately 3,400 electricity distribution poles, 490 km of electricity distribution lines, and underground energy lines were severely damaged. In addition, serious damage occurred to gas and oil pipelines, and oil and chemicals leaked into the Sea of Marmara, causing marine pollution [7]. The 2011 Van earthquake also caused significant destruction and heavy damage in the region. This destruction resulted in 11.6% of workplaces being damaged and 5.2% of manufactured goods being damaged [8].

On February 6, 2023, at 4:17 a.m., a powerful earthquake occurred with its epicenter in the Pazarcık district of Kahramanmaraş. The magnitude of the earthquake was recorded as Mw = 7.7. This tremor was felt intensely in Kahramanmaraş, Hatay, Malatya, Adıyaman, Gaziantep, Şanlıurfa, Diyarbakır, Adana, Osmaniye, and Kilis provinces, resulting in significant loss of life and extensive material damage in the region. On the same day, approximately nine hours after the first earthquake, at 1:24 p.m., a second earthquake with a magnitude of Mw = 7.6 occurred, this time centered in the Elbistan district of Kahramanmaraş. The total area affected by these two earthquakes is 108,812 km2. This area is larger than the land area of many countries. The earthquakes claimed the lives of 50,783 people. With 115,353 people injured, 13.5 million people were negatively affected by the earthquakes [9]. According to damage assessment studies conducted as of November 14, 2024, out of 2,337,455 buildings, including residences, public institutions, historical and cultural structures, schools, hospitals, and commercial establishments, 39,466 were destroyed, 21,175 were in urgent need of demolition, 199,967 were severely damaged, 43,698 were moderately damaged, and 1,954,378 were slightly damaged or undamaged [4]. Furthermore, according to reports by the SBB [3], the total cost of the earthquake to the Turkish economy is estimated to be $103.6 billion.

The earthquake disaster centred in Kahramanmaraş has also left deep scars on the region’s social and demographic structure. In particular, the population mobility that accelerated after the earthquake has led to a significant number of young people and workers leaving the region. The destruction of the healthcare infrastructure following the February 6 Kahramanmaraş earthquake has significantly hampered both the effective implementation of emergency response processes and the provision of uninterrupted healthcare services [10, 11].

Earthquakes have a profoundly traumatic effect on both the individual and societal levels. Therefore, individuals who have experienced an earthquake may be negatively affected psychosocially and economically [12]. Additionally, injuries, deaths, and material losses resulting from physical environmental events following an earthquake can hurt people’s physical and mental health [13]. Everyone’s response to disasters and the level of impact are not the same, and the effects on mental health also vary. However, anxiety, depression, and stress are among the most commonly observed psychological problems in individuals after an earthquake [14].

Furthermore, according to research, anxiety, depression, and stress negatively affect employees’ job satisfaction, life satisfaction, and employee performance. For example, Çağatay et al. [15] determined in their research, conducted after the February 6, 2023, Kahramanmaraş earthquakes, that perceived stress negatively impacts employee performance. Similarly, research conducted by Qin and Jiang [16] on employees affected by the Wenchuan earthquake in the People’s Republic of China revealed a decline in employee job satisfaction after the earthquake. A study conducted among adults affected by the Kahramanmaraş earthquakes found that depression hurts life satisfaction [17]. Deady et al. [18] concluded that depression and stress have negative effects on work performance. A study conducted by Kutluk and Yalcın [19] on healthcare workers found that depression reduces employees’ job satisfaction.

However, within the scope of “Psychological Resilience Theory” [20] and “Broaden-and-Build Theory” [21], psychological resilience plays a crucial role in mitigating or eliminating these negative effects. In particular, the research’s fundamental framework is based on the “Psychological Resilience Theory.” The Psychological Resilience Theory explains how individuals cope with negative life events [22] and rebuild the positive aspects of their lives [23]. According to the Broaden-and-Build Theory, psychologically resilient individuals can protect themselves when faced with negative situations such as crises, stress, and depression by developing positive emotions [21]. Previous studies have also highlighted the importance of psychological resilience in this regard. For example, Koca et al. [24] found in their research on healthcare workers affected by the Kahramanmaraş earthquakes that individual psychological resilience reduced traumatic stress symptoms. Karadag et al. [25] concluded that the increase in psychological resilience levels among students affected by the Kahramanmaraş earthquakes reduced their post-traumatic stress levels.

Therefore, the primary objective of this study is to investigate the effect of psychological resilience (PR) on employee performance (EP). At the same time, another objective of the study is to determine whether job satisfaction (JS) and life satisfaction (LS) mediate the relationship between employees’ perceptions of psychological resilience (PR) and employee performance (EP). In line with these objectives, data obtained from social service workers affected by the February 6, 2023, Kahramanmaraş earthquakes were analysed using SEM. Based on the study’s results, recommendations are provided to businesses and managers on how to increase employees’ psychological resilience.

PR has been addressed in various studies to understand the psychological and organisational processes individuals experience after disasters. In addition, the relationship between PR and EP has been examined in different contexts in the literature. However, there appear to be few studies that simultaneously examine the roles of JS and LS as mediators in the relationship between PR and EP among social service workers operating in earthquake-affected areas. Therefore, it is anticipated that the present study will contribute to the literature by examining the relationship between PR and EP within the framework of these variables.

Literature review

Psychological Resilience (PR)

The concept of “resilience” is derived from the Latin verb resilire, meaning “to bounce back” or “to recover.” The Oxford English Dictionary defines resilience as “the ability to withstand difficult conditions or to recover quickly from them” [26]. Throughout their lives, people encounter various challenges ranging from daily stressors to major life events. In this context, Bonanno and Mancini [27] note that the vast majority of individuals experience at least one potentially traumatic event (PTE) during their lifetime. The term “potential” is important because individuals’ responses to life events vary, meaning that trauma does not manifest in the same way for everyone. Studies on psychological resilience aim to understand how some individuals endure the pressures and difficulties they encounter and emerge from this process stronger [28]. While some researchers consider difficult life events to be traumatic experiences that affect individuals cognitively, emotionally, and behaviorally [29], others argue that these experiences can lead individuals to adopt more constructive solutions and develop positive responses [30, 31]. In this context, psychological resilience refers to an individual’s capacity to adapt and recover in the face of adverse life events and changes [32].

Job Satisfaction (JS)

JS is an important concept that expresses the extent to which individuals enjoy their work [33]. In the literature, it is generally defined as the presence of positive attitudes and pleasant feelings that employees develop towards their jobs and work environments. High levels of JS among employees contribute to their tendency to improve job performance, which supports the sustainable, healthy growth of businesses [34, 35]. In this context, Hoppock [36] defined JS as a combination of psychological, physiological, and environmental conditions that lead an individual to sincerely say, “I am satisfied with my job.” This approach reveals that, despite being influenced by numerous external factors, JS is essentially an internal state related to how employees perceive and feel about their work. Therefore, JS refers to the entirety of conditions and experiences arising from employees’ work experiences that create feelings of fulfilment and satisfaction in the individual [37]. Furthermore, JS is considered one of the fundamental elements that support employee motivation and strengthen their commitment to their work [38].

Life Satisfaction (LS)

LS is one of the fundamental indicators of perceived quality of life. When considered alongside mental and physical health indicators, it reflects the extent to which individuals function well in their daily lives and how successful they are [39]. This concept represents an individual’s overall cognitive assessment or judgment of their life [40]. LS can be influenced by many factors, such as “daily happiness, meaningfulness of life, stress level, consistency in achieving goals, positive personal identity, physical health, economic security, and social relationships” [41]. Furthermore, because it involves a broader and longer-term assessment of one’s life circumstances, it is characterised by a deeper sense of enjoyment and accomplishment, unlike the mere feeling of momentary happiness [42]. LS also expresses an individual’s cognitive assessment of their life as a whole [43]. In this context, the level of personal satisfaction individuals feel about their lives is based on the perceived gap between the goals they have already achieved and the goals they still aim to achieve [44].

Employee Performance (EP)

EP is defined as the totality of actions that an employee performs and fails to perform. In this context, EP encompasses elements such as the quality and quantity of outputs, job continuity, cooperative and helpful attitudes, and timely delivery of work [45]. According to Werdhiastutie et al. [46], EP is considered a key factor in achieving organisational goals. Furthermore, EP refers to the degree to which an employee effectively and appropriately fulfils their duties and responsibilities [47]. However, it is noted that employee performance tends to be higher among happy and satisfied employees, and that it is easier for management to motivate high-performing employees to achieve organisational goals [48].

Relationship and hypothesis development

Psychological Resilience (PR) and Employee Performance (EP)

This study examines the relationship between PR and EP within the framework of the “Psychological Resilience Theory” [20]. The psychological resilience theory is a conceptual model that helps individuals understand how to cope with a significant threat or adverse situation [22] and how to bring out the positive aspects of their lives [23]. The theory’s greatest benefit is that it helps us understand how an individual’s perspective on life’s challenges fundamentally affects their experience of the challenge and their response to it [49].

Individuals with high psychological resilience are expected to have a high ability to cope with and turn negative situations, such as trauma, loss, health problems, work, and financial problems that they encounter throughout their lives into positive ones [49]. Additionally, these individuals can lead healthier and happier lives and properly fulfill the tasks and responsibilities assigned to them [50]. Natural disasters such as earthquakes can also have negative effects on people’s work and daily lives. Depending on their psychological resilience, employees can turn the negative effects of an earthquake into positive ones and increase their work performance. Various studies in the literature also support this situation. For example, in a study conducted by Kumari and Sangwan [51] on managers in the pharmaceutical industry, a positive relationship was found between PR and work performance. Wu [52] also found similar results in his research conducted in Cambodia, Thailand, and Vietnam. Odunjo-Saka et al. [53] found in their study on hotel employees that PR has a positive effect on EP. Similar results have been found by other researchers [49, 54–57]. Based on these theories and findings, the first hypothesis of the study was developed.

H1: PR has a positive impact on EP

H1: PR has a positive impact on EP

Psychological Resilience (PR), Job Satisfaction (JS), and Life Satisfaction (LS)

This study examines the relationship between PR, JS, and LS. Psychologically resilient individuals have a positive outlook [58]. JS is derived from the work an individual does [59], while LS is the satisfaction an individual feels with their life based on their own criteria [60]. Therefore, employees’ psychological resilience can also lead to JS and LS [61, 62]. Additionally, the relationship between PR, JS, and LS can be explained by the “Broaden-and-Build Theory” [21]. According to the Broaden-and-Build Theory, positive emotions are tools for individual growth and establishing social relationships. These tools help individuals create a better life for themselves in the future. The theory also suggests that psychological resilience is a permanent personal resource. In this context, it argues that positive emotional experiences not only reflect psychological resilience over time but can also build it [21].

The psychological resilience levels of individuals working in earthquake zones can improve their job satisfaction and life satisfaction. Previous studies support this. For example, Kılıç et al. [63], who examined the relationship between PR and JS, found that healthcare workers with high levels of PR also had higher JS in their study. Similarly, Han et al. [64] noted that psychological resilience can help increase job satisfaction among employees negatively affected by the COVID-19 pandemic. Prayag et al. [61], who examined the relationship between PR and LS, concluded that psychological and employee resilience positively affect life satisfaction based on their research on tourism workers affected by the 2011 New Zealand earthquake. Elshaer [65] stated in his research on the tourism sector after COVID-19 that employees with high PR have greater LS. However, Prayag and Manci [66] found no significant relationship between PR and LS in their research on tourism managers in provinces affected by the 2023 Kahramanmaraş earthquakes. Nevertheless, based on theories and general research findings in the literature, the following hypotheses have been developed.

H2: PR has a positive impact on JS

H2: PR has a positive impact on JS

H3: PR has a positive impact on LS

H3: PR has a positive impact on LS

Job Satisfaction (JS) and Employee Performance (EP)

This study examines the relationship between JS and EP. JS, which is the result of various factors such as salary, promotion, the job itself, supervision, relationships with coworkers, and promotion opportunities [67, 68], is a complex concept that is difficult to measure directly because it is based on individuals’ feelings and perceptions. Employees experience job satisfaction when they believe that the rewards they receive for their work outweigh the effort they put in and the costs they incur, and that this difference meaningfully supports their lives [69].

Job satisfaction is thought to be strongly influenced by many variables. However, researchers approach this concept from different angles [70]. Crossman and Abou-Zaki [71] state that discussions on the relationship between JS and EP are still ongoing. They attribute this to the fact that the causal determinants between the concepts have not been fully established. Therefore, it may be difficult to assume that employee performance will necessarily be high among individuals with high job satisfaction. However, while Li et al. [72] emphasize the relationship between JS and EP, Wibowo [73] states that JS is an important determinant of EP. The significant effect of JS on EP has also been identified by other researchers [74–79] have also identified that JS has a significant impact on EP. Based on these views, the following hypothesis was developed in the study.

H4: JS has a positive impact on EP

H4: JS has a positive impact on EP

Life Satisfaction (LS) and Employee Performance (EP)

This study examines the relationship between LS and EP within the framework of the Authentic Happiness Theory. According to Seligman [80], happiness is based on three fundamental elements: “pleasure,” “engagement,” and “meaning.” It is of great importance for individuals to achieve satisfaction in their lives. It is accepted that individuals who are unhappy, restless, and under intense stress in their daily lives will find it difficult to focus on any task. The mental state of individuals, including their stress levels, unresolved family problems, restlessness, and unhappiness, are among the factors that affect both LS and work performance. In this context, it can be predicted that life satisfaction and employee performance may increase or decrease together depending on certain variables [81]. In this context, it is thought that life satisfaction is closely related to the concept of happiness [82] and therefore may also increase employee performance.

The relationship between LS and EP has been examined in various sectors in the literature. According to the results of research conducted in the tourism sector, LS has been found to have a significant effect on EP [83]. A study conducted on employees of the Provincial Youth and Sports Directorate in Ankara yielded similar results [81]. Kosec et al. [84] found a significant relationship between LS and EP in their study on office workers. However, some studies conclude that there is no significant relationship between LS and EP [85]. Nevertheless, the consensus in the literature is that there is a significant relationship between these two concepts [86]. Therefore, the following hypothesis was developed in this study.

H5: LS has a positive impact on EP

H5: LS has a positive impact on EP

Mediating role of Job Satisfaction (JS) and Life Satisfaction (LS)

This study examines the mediating role of JS and LS in the relationship between PR and EP. Studies conducted in this regard in the literature are quite limited. JS, which reflects an individual’s positive attitude toward their work [87], positively influences employees’ health and overall life satisfaction [88]. LS is defined as a concept that expresses how an individual generally evaluates their life according to their own criteria and expectations [89]. These individuals can benefit from various advantages, such as accessing more meaningful, more autonomous, and diverse jobs, and can earn higher incomes. In addition, they exhibit less inefficient behaviour in the face of the negative effects of workload and experience burnout syndrome at lower levels [90, 91]. In this context, JS and LS not only reflect individuals’ overall well-being and attitudes toward their jobs, but also play an important role in shaping workplace behaviours and performance outcomes. Additionally, employees who are satisfied with their work and life establish better relationships with their coworkers [92–95], are more willing to come to work [96–98], and are less likely to consider leaving their jobs [99, 100], have higher organizational commitment than others [101–104], and most importantly, higher performance [105–108]. Yang et al. [109] concluded that JS mediates the effect of psychological well-being on job performance. Based on this information, the study’s mediation hypotheses were developed.

Method

Research design

This study aims to reveal the effect of employees’ perceptions of PR on EP. At the same time, whether JS and LS mediate the effect of employees’ perceptions of PR on EP is another aim of the study. The research data were obtained through a questionnaire from social service employees in the provinces affected by the February 6 Kahramanmaraş earthquakes. The data collection process was conducted face-to-face between June 15, 2025, and July 15, 2025.

Population and sample

The study population consists of Family and Social Service centers affiliated with the Ministry of Family and Social Services in 11 provinces affected by the February 6 Kahramanmaraş earthquakes. In the 11 provinces affected by the February 6 Kahramanmaraş earthquakes, there is no information on the number of employees working in the Family and Social Service centers affiliated with the Ministry of Family and Social Services. Therefore, Cohen et al. [110] and Yazıcıoğlu and Erdoğan [111] were consulted for the adequate sample size. According to Cohen et al. [110], “for a research population of 500,000 people, it is sufficient to reach 272 participants. According to Yazıcıoğlu and Erdoğan [111], “in cases where the research population is not known exactly or is very large, the sample number should be at least 384 with a 95% confidence interval and 5% margin of error”. Based on this information, data were obtained from 804 social service workers. According to the sample size calculations, this number is considered to be statistically sufficient to represent the population [110–112]. Data collection tools

PR

In this study, the “Psychological Resilience Scale” (α = 0.83), developed by Smith et al. [113] and adapted into Turkish by Doğan [114], was used to measure the participants’ perceptions of psychological resilience. The scale, which consists of a total of 6 statements and a single dimension, is graded as “1-Not at All Appropriate/5-Completely Appropriate”.

JS

In this study, the “Job Satisfaction Scale” (α = 0.93), developed by Brayfield and Rothe [115], shortened by Judge et al. [116], and adapted into Turkish by Başol and Çömlekçi [117], was used. The scale consists of 5 statements and is graded as “1-Strongly Disagree/5-Strongly Agree”.

LS

It was developed by Diener et al. [60] and adapted into Turkish by Dağlı and Baysal [118] (α = 0.88). The scale consists of 5 statements and one dimension and is graded as “1-Strongly Disagree/7-Strongly Agree” in the original and “1-Never Disagree/5-Totally Agree” in the Turkish adaptation. In the current study, the scale was graded according to the Turkish adaptation.

EP

The scale (α = 0.83), first used by Kirkman and Rosen [119] and later adapted by Sigler and Pearson [120] and translated into Turkish by Çöl [121], was employed. The scale consists of 4 statements and one dimension and is graded as “1-Strongly Disagree/5-Strongly Agree”.

Findings

Measurement model

The research data were analysed using PLS-SEM structural equation modelling in SmartPLS. PLS-SEM examines “the relationships between the components or factors of variables rather than their covariances with each other”. This method is particularly suitable for analysing complex models and working with small sample sizes [122]. In addition, the main reasons for choosing PLS-SEM, especially in the current research, were that “it does not require a normal distribution of data,” “it can run predictive models,” and “it allows for the examination of relationships between variables at an exploratory level” [123, 124]. Moreover, PLS-SEM, which can examine regression, multiple regression, factor analysis, and path analysis within a single structure [125], has been widely used in recent years across social science fields such as organisational management [126] and human resources management [127].

The validity and reliability coefficients of the scales are important for hypothesis testing. In evaluating the research model, the reliability of the scales was assessed through factor loadings, construct reliability was evaluated using Cronbach’s alpha and composite reliability, and convergent validity was assessed based on AVE values [128]. According to Hair et al. [128], “Cronbach’s Alpha” (CA) and “Composite Reliability” (CR) values must be above 0.70, and the “Average Variance Extracted” (AVE) value must be above 0.50 in validity and reliability assessments. Structural model evaluation (hypothesis test)

In this part of the research, a path analysis test was applied to test the hypotheses in the model. The path analysis was performed using 5,000 bootstrap samples with the PLS-SEM method in the SmartPLS program. Before evaluating the structural model, VIF values were analyzed. The VIF value is “a coefficient that provides information about the presence of multicollinearity”. A VIF value greater than 3 means that there is a multicollinearity problem in the model [123]. When the VIF values obtained from the analysis are examined, it is observed that they are all less than 3 (Table 4). Therefore, it can be said that there is no multicollinearity problem in the model. In addition, R2 values for the dependent variables in the study were examined. The R2 value shows “what percentage of the dependent variable is explained by the independent variable”. According to Yıldız [130], an “R2 coefficient between 0.25 and 0.50 is considered weak; between 0.50 and 0.75 is considered moderate, and above 0.75 is considered strong”. As seen in Table 4, the R2 value is 0.411 for job satisfaction, 0.443 for life satisfaction, and 0.184 for employee performance. However, disciplinary conditions should be taken into account when evaluating R2. In some disciplines, even a 10% explanation rate is considered very strong [130].

Discussion and conclusion

This study aims to determine the effect of social service workers’ perceptions of psychological resilience (PR) on employee performance (EP) in provinces affected by the February 6, 2023, Kahramanmaraş Earthquakes. Additionally, the study investigates whether job satisfaction (JS) and life satisfaction (LS) play a mediating role in the effect of psychological resilience on employee performance. The topics addressed in the study are among those frequently researched in the literature. However, there are limited studies examining the effect of perceived psychological resilience on employee performance, particularly in earthquake-affected regions, and the mediating roles of job satisfaction and life satisfaction in this relationship. In this context, the present study aims to contribute to the literature by examining the relationships between these variables in a sample of social service workers employed in the earthquake zone.

The data obtained in the study were analyzed using Smart-PLS (SEM), and direct and indirect effects were determined. The model indicates that PR has a positive and significant effect on EP (H1). As employees’ levels of psychological resilience increase, their performance also increases. This finding is consistent with previous studies in the literature [50–52, 54, 132]. Shen et al. [57] found in their study on nurses that PR has a positive and significant effect on EP. According to the authors, employees with good psychological resilience can generate positive emotions in the workplace. This can help them understand their duties more effectively and foster good relationships with their coworkers. A similar result was obtained by Odunjo-Saka et al. [53], who researched hotel employees in Northern Cyprus. Other researchers who reached similar results [55] interpreted this finding as follows: “Employees with high levels of psychological resilience tend to exhibit higher levels of success in their job performance because they are individuals with developed creativity skills, who can adapt more quickly to changes and cope effectively with difficulties.”

The second hypothesis (H2) tested in the study revealed that PR has a significant and positive effect on JS. Furthermore, the analysis concluded that PR has a positive and significant effect on LS (H3). The results align with previous studies by Gundogan [133], Karagöz et al. [134], Onan et al. [62], Han et al. [64], Song and Li [135], Uzunbacak et al. [136], Avcı Taşkıran et al. [137], Hanimoğlu [138], and Ozdemir and Kaplan [139]. Fredrickson et al. [140] conducted a study to examine the effect of psychological resilience on life satisfaction after the September 11, 2001, terrorist attack on the WTC. The study found that psychologically resilient individuals can respond quickly in crises, return to normalcy within a short period, and therefore experience higher life satisfaction compared to others. Onan et al. [62] found that PR has a significant effect on JS in the service sector. According to the authors, employees who remain unaffected by difficult situations in particular experience greater job satisfaction. Zeidabadinejad and Dehghan [141], in a study conducted on nurses in Iran during the COVID-19 pandemic, found that nurses with high psychological resilience performed their duties effectively, easily overcame problems, and consequently had higher job satisfaction. Similarly, Pathak and Joshi [142] conducted research in the tourism and hotel sector, which faced significant challenges during the COVID-19 pandemic. The authors stated that “hotel owners and managers with high psychological resilience made more effort to overcome the negative effects of the pandemic and return to normal, and had higher life satisfaction.”

Furthermore, the research findings reveal that the effect size of PR on JS (ß = 0.641) and LS (ß = 0.666) is stronger compared to its direct effect on EP (ß = 0.118). This may stem from PR being a multidimensional construct influenced by individual, organisational, and environmental factors. In particular, the performance of social service workers in regions affected by large-scale disasters, such as earthquakes, can be shaped not only by individual psychological resources but also by organisational support, workload, working conditions, and environmental factors. Therefore, there may be differences in the effects of PR on EP and on JS and LS. In addition, studies show similarities with existing research in terms of the level of effect. For example, Hoşgör and Yaman [143] found that psychological resilience had a small effect (ß = 0.163) on employee performance in their study of nurses in Turkey during the Covid-19 pandemic.

Within the scope of the fourth hypothesis (H4) tested in the study, it was found that JS has a positive and significant effect (β = 0.263) on EP. In this context, we can say that employees with high job satisfaction tend to also exhibit high employee performance. Rodrigo et al. [144] concluded in their research that variables determining job satisfaction, such as salary and benefits, training, and working conditions, have a positive impact on employee performance. The authors also concluded that JS has a significant impact on EP in the study. Hartika et al. [145], in a study conducted among bank employees in Bali, found that JS has a significant impact on EP. Similarly, Supriyanto et al. [146] concluded in their research that job satisfaction affects employee performance, stating that managers in businesses should focus on employee job satisfaction to enhance their work. According to Wau [147], who reached similar conclusions, satisfying employees’ desires, hopes, goals, and needs can increase their employee performance. However, the analysis of the fifth hypothesis (H5) revealed that LS has no significant effect on EP. This situation may be related to the nature of the life satisfaction variable. LS is a broader concept that reflects an individual’s assessment of the overall quality of their life [148]. EP, on the other hand, refers to the success achieved at the end of the work performed [149] and encompasses behaviours specific to the work environment. Therefore, employees’ life satisfaction may not always be reflected in employee performance. In particular, the life satisfaction of social service workers employed in earthquake-affected areas may be influenced by many individual and environmental factors outside of work life. This situation may have limited the effect of LS on EP. The findings of the current study are consistent with previous literature. For example, Öktem and Öztoprak [85] found no statistically significant relationship between LS and EP in their study of employees at 4- and 5-star hotels in Türkiye. Similarly, Fisher [150] found no significant relationship between LS and EP.

Finally, in the analyses conducted under the sixth and seventh hypotheses of the study, it was found that JS mediated the effect of PR on EP (H6). However, LS did not play a significant mediating role (H7). Additionally, the indirect effect of PR on EP via JS (β = 0.169) is stronger than the direct effect (β = 0.118). This situation may indicate that PR can have a greater impact through employees’ attitudes toward their jobs rather than directly determining EP. The effect of PR often manifests in employees’ job evaluations and attitudes toward the work environment. However, JS, being directly related to the workplace, is an important attitudinal variable that affects motivation, commitment to work, and effort levels, and may have a more significant impact on performance. It can be argued that psychological resilience may indirectly enhance performance by positively affecting employees’ job evaluations, especially in regions affected by earthquakes. This finding is consistent with the results of the study by Albalá-Genol et al. [151]. However, LS may not be directly related to individuals’ jobs and, therefore, may not mediate the relationship between PR and EP.

Theoretical implications

The findings contribute to understanding the relationships between psychological resilience, job satisfaction, life satisfaction, and employee performance. First, the study presents results consistent with the “Psychological Resilience Theory” [20], indicating that psychological resilience is associated with employee performance. This theory suggests that individuals can adapt to negative and stressful life conditions by using their psychological resources and produce positive outcomes [22, 23]. The research findings also indicate that psychological resilience may play an important role in assessing employee performance, particularly among social service workers operating in earthquake-affected areas.

The study also offers some insights from the perspective of the “Broaden-and-Build Theory” [21]. According to this theory, positive emotions contribute to individuals developing long-term personal resources by expanding their cognitive and psychological resources [152]. The findings reveal that psychological resilience is associated with employees’ job and life satisfaction, suggesting that positive psychological resources can support individuals’ assessments of their lives. These results support the explanatory power of this theoretical approach in an organisational context, particularly by showing that individuals’ psychological resources in crisis and disaster conditions can shape their perceptions of their work and lives.

Finally, limited studies examine the mediating roles of job satisfaction and life satisfaction in the effect of psychological resilience on employee performance, particularly among employees affected by disasters such as earthquakes. In this regard, it is thought that the findings obtained within the scope of the model developed in this study may contribute to understanding the relationships among psychological resilience, job satisfaction, life satisfaction, and employee performance and may offer a contextual perspective on the organisational behaviour literature.

Practical implications

The current study has revealed that psychological resilience significantly affects the performance, job satisfaction, and life satisfaction of social service workers. These findings point to the importance of supporting the psychological resilience of social service workers, especially those working in disaster-affected areas. In this context, it may be important for social service institutions to foster organisational environments that support their employees’ psychological resilience. Responsibility lies with policymakers and institutional managers. Developing a trust-based organisational culture can help social service workers share the difficult experiences and emotional burdens they encounter in the field during and after disasters. Social service workers face particularly heavy workloads and intense emotional demands after disasters. Given this situation, a trust-based organisational climate can help them perform their duties sustainably. Furthermore, developing open and transparent communication processes can also help social service workers feel more secure. This is because in an organisation with open and transparent communication, employees can easily share their experiences and increase their resilience by receiving psychological support.

Based on my research findings, another issue policymakers and institutional managers should address is supporting individual education and development. Social service institutions can conduct training focused on “stress management, coping with crises, experience sharing, case discussions, professional supervision, and psychosocial support,” with support from within the institution or from external sources. Such training and development activities can support employees’ psychological resilience, helping them cope more effectively with challenging working conditions. This will enable them to be better prepared for future disaster situations.

Limitations and future research

As with all studies, the present study has certain limitations. First, this study examined only the mediating role of job satisfaction and life satisfaction in the effect of psychological resilience on employee performance. Therefore, the study’s conceptual framework was limited to these concepts.

One significant limitation of the study is the context in which it was conducted. This research was conducted with a sample of social service workers operating in earthquake-affected areas. It should be noted that the findings obtained in this context may have been influenced by the unique conditions of the post-disaster work environment. This is because, while generalizable in the context of public service operating under similar disaster or crisis conditions, it may vary across different sectors due to organisational structure, working conditions, job demands, and the nature of resources [153, 154]. In the aftermath of disasters such as earthquakes, social service workers may face high workloads, intense emotional demands, and social responsibilities. Such conditions may shape the relationships between psychological resilience, job satisfaction, life satisfaction, and employee performance within different contextual dynamics. Therefore, caution should be exercised when directly generalising the findings to different sectors or different organisational contexts.

The data in the study were collected cross-sectionally (at a single point in time). This situation may limit the ability to explain how employees’ perceptions of psychological resilience affect employee performance and how the roles of job satisfaction and life satisfaction in this effect may change over time. In particular, it may be useful to re-examine the statistically insignificant effect of life satisfaction on employee performance across sectors and to examine it with mediating variables. Therefore, longitudinal techniques may be used in future studies to reveal changes over time.

Finally, the research scope has focused solely on psychological resilience. However, employee performance is closely related to the workplace environment and organisational policies [155, 156]. Therefore, future research could include variables such as leadership style, psychological safety climate, workplace camaraderie, perceived organisational support, and organisational justice.

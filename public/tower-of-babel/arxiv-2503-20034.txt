On the (Dis)connection Between Growth and Primitive Periodic Points
Adi Glücksam, Shira Tanny

Abstract
In 1972, Cornalba and Shiffman showed that the number of zeros of an order zero holomorphic function in two or more variables can grow arbitrarily fast. We generalize this finding to the setting of complex dynamics, establishing that the number of isolated primitive periodic points of an order zero holomorphic function in two or more variables can grow arbitrarily fast as well. This answers a recent question posed by L. Buhovsky, I. Polterovich, L. Polterovich, E. Shelukhin and V. Stojisavljević.



1
Introduction

Bézout theorems seek to explore the relation between the growth of a holomorphic function and the size of its zeros set. The classical Bézout theorem states that the number of zeros of
n
polynomials over
n
variables is bounded by the product of their degrees. A natural question is whether this theorem extends to general holomorphic functions. Here, the notion of degree is replaced by the notion of growth rate, measured by the asymptotic behavior of its
maximum modulus function
,
M_{f}(r):=\max_{\left|{z}\right|\leq r}\left|{f(z)}\right|.

Jensen’s formula gives a full description of this relation in complex dimension 1. Formally, assuming that
f(0)\neq 0
, Jensen’s formula implies that for every
a>1
there exists
C=C(f,a)>0
such that

\#\left\{{\left|{z}\right|\leq r:f(z)=0}\right\}\leq C\log M_{f}(a\cdot r)\qquad\text{for all}\qquad r>0.

In
[
2
]
Cornalba and Shifmann showed this phenomenon does not extend to higher dimensions. They constructed a holomorphic function
F:\mathbb{C}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{2}
satisfying
\log M_{F}(r)=O\left({\log^{2}r}\right)
,
whose zeros grow arbitrarily fast. Here, and everywhere else in the paper,
O\left({\log^{2}r}\right)
means that there exists a constant
0<c
for which
\log M_{F}(r)\leq c\log^{2}(r)
.

In a recent paper,
[
1
]
, L. Buhovsky, I. Polterovich, L. Polterovich, E. Shelukhin and V. Stojisavljević proposed a new way to obtain a transcendental analog to Bézout theorem, replacing the traditional zero count with the coarse zero count. Roughly speaking, the
coarse count
of zeros of a holomorphic function in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
is the number of connected components on which it is small, that contain a zero. They proved that this coarse count can be bounded in terms of the maximum modulus function, thus establishing an analog to Bézout theorem in the setup of coarse count.

This version of Bézout theorem can be imported into the world of complex dynamics. It implies that the coarse count of
p
-periodic points of holomorphic functions is also bounded by a function of their growth rate. Moreover, the Cornalba-Shifmann example (shifted by the identity map) shows that the honest count of fixed points does not obey such a bound. In
[
1
]
they asked whether the construction carried out by Cornalba-Shifmann can be generalized to construct an order zero holomorphic function with arbitrarily many primitive periodic points of higher periods.

Definition 1.1

Let
f:\mathbb{C}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a holomorphic function.

•

We say
z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
is
p
-
primitive periodic point for
f
(or
p
-PPP for short) if
f^{\circ j}(z)\neq z
whenever
1\leq j\leq p-1
, but
f^{\circ p}(z)=z
.

•

Denote by
\nu_{p}(f,r)
the number of
p
-PPP lying in a ball of radius
r
centered at the origin, namely

\nu_{p}(f,r):=\#\left({B(0,r)\cap\left\{{z,z\text{ is a p-PPP for }f}\right\}}\right).

Question 1.2

(
[
1
, Question 1.12]
)

Does there exist a transcendental entire map,
f
, of order 0 (i.e., the modulus
M_{f}(r)
grows slower than
e^{r^{\varepsilon}}
for every
\varepsilon
) for which
\nu_{p}(f,r)
grows arbitrarily fast in
p
and
r
?

For a fixed period,
p
, a simple modification of the Cornalba–Shiffman example produces a holomorphic function,
f
, of order zero for which
\nu_{p}(f,r)
grows arbitrarily fast in
r
; see Example
3.4
below. However, producing a holomorphic function with many primitive periodic points of varying prescribed periods is more complicated. Our main result states that this is indeed possible.

Theorem 1.3

For every sequence of periods,
\left\{{p_{n}}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
, and for every rate,
\left\{{m_{n}}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
, there exists a holomorphic function
F:\mathbb{C}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{2}
satisfying

\nu_{p_{n}}(F,2^{n})\geq m_{n}\quad\text{and}\quad\log M_{F}(r)=O\left({\log^{2}(r)}\right).

The constant in
O(\log^{2}(r))
is independent of the sequences.

Unlike the example by Cornalba and Shiffman, the holomorphic functions constructed in Theorem
1.3
are not completely explicit. In particular, it is not clear how to estimate or even bound from above or below their coarse zero count.

The main idea of the proof is to utilize the simple example constructed in Example
3.4
with different fixed periods, and then use a ‘dispatcher’ function to choose between different periods. The main tool in the construction is a theorem of Hörmander (see Theorem
3.3
below) which guarantees the existence of solutions to non-homogeneous
\bar{\partial}
-equations with certain integral bounds. This theorem allows one to construct entire functions with distinct behaviours in different regions. The use of Hörmander’s theorem requires an underlying subharmonic function which is large in the area between distinct regions, but very negative where we want a good approximation. Section
2
contains the construction of subharmonic functions that will be used in the application of Hörmander’s theorem in Section
3
. In Section
3
we construct the required holomorphic functions and prove Theorem
1.3
.

2
Preliminary Results on Subharmonic functions

We commence the discussion with the following lemma whose role is to modify a given subharmonic function assigning
-\infty
to prescribed points.

Lemma 2.1

(The Puncture Lemma)

Let
u
be a continuous subharmonic function and, for
N\in\mathbb{N}
or
N=\infty
, let
\left\{{B_{k}}\right\}_{k=1}^{N}
be a sequence of pairwise disjoint disks,
B_{k}=B(z_{k},r_{k})
. Assume that for every
k
,
\underset{z\in B_{k}}{\inf}\;\Delta u>0
. Then there exists a subharmonic function
v
satisfying

1.

u(z)=v(z)
whenever
z\not\in\bigcup_{{k}={1}}^{N}B_{k}
.

2.

For every
\delta\in\left({0,1}\right)
, and
k\in\left\{{1,2,\cdots,N}\right\}

\underset{z\in B(z_{k},\delta\cdot r_{k})}{\max}\;v(z)\leq\underset{z\in B_{k}}{\max}\;u(z)-c\cdot r_{k}^{2}\cdot\underset{z\in B_{k}}{\inf}\;\Delta u\log\left({\frac{1}{\delta}}\right),

where
c>0
is some uniform constant.

See Figure
1
for an illustration of the image of the subharmonic function
v
.

Figure 1
:
An illustration of the the image of the subharmonic function created by Lemma
2.1

While the proof can be found in
[
4
, Section 3.1]
, where
\mathcal{K}_{0}(t)=\log(t)
and the function
v
is any function, we include it here for the reader’s convenience.

Proof.

Define the function

v(z)=\begin{cases}\left({P_{\mathbb{D}}u_{k}}\right)\left({\frac{z-z_{k}}{r_{k}}}\right)+A_{k}\log\left({\frac{\left|{z-z_{k}}\right|}{r_{k}}}\right),&z\in B_{k}\\
u(z),&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

where
u_{k}(z)=u\left({r_{k}\cdot z+z_{k}}\right)
(and therefore
u(B_{k})=u_{k}(\mathbb{D})
), the constants
A_{k}>0
will be chosen momentarily, and
P_{\mathbb{D}}f
is Poisson integral of a function
f
defined by

P_{\mathbb{D}}f(re^{i\theta}):=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}P_{r}(\theta-t)f(e^{it})\ dt,\quad\text{for}\quad P_{r}(\theta):=\frac{1-r^{2}}{1-2r\cos\theta+r^{2}}.

Note that
v
is continuous, since the Poisson integral of any continuous function agrees with it on
\partial\mathbb{D}
. We need to choose the constants
A_{k}
such that sub-harmonicity is preserved. We will use the following claim:

Claim 2.2

(
[
3
, p.24]
)

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{C}
be a domain, and let
\Omega_{1}\Subset\Omega
be a domain, so that
\partial\Omega_{1}
is an orientable smooth curve. Every function
f
which is continuous on
\Omega
and subharmonic on
\Omega_{1}\cup\left({\Omega\setminus\overline{\Omega_{1}}}\right)
, is subharmonic on
\Omega
if on
\partial\Omega_{1}
it satisfies

\frac{\partial f}{\partial n_{1}}\leq\frac{\partial f}{\partial n_{2}},

where
n_{1}
is the outer normal to
\Omega_{1}
along
\partial\Omega_{1}
and
n_{2}
is the outer normal to
\Omega\setminus\Omega_{1}
along
\partial\Omega_{1}
.

We will use this claim with
\Omega:=\mathbb{C}
, and
\Omega_{1}:=B_{k}
. In this case, we need to show that along
\partial B_{k}

\frac{\partial v}{\partial n_{1}}\leq\frac{\partial v}{\partial n_{2}}=\frac{\partial u}{\partial n},

and the latter is known. To calculate
\frac{\partial v}{\partial n_{1}}
we will use Poisson-Jenssen’s formula (see e.g.
[
7
, Theorem 4.5.1]
), which, in this case, boils down to:

u_{k}(z)=P_{\mathbb{D}}u_{k}(z)+G_{\mathbb{D}}(z)

where

G_{\mathbb{D}}(z):=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{D}}{g_{\mathbb{D}}(z,w)\Delta u_{k}(w)}d{\lambda}_{2}(w),\qquad g_{\mathbb{D}}(z,w):=\log\Big|\frac{z-w}{1-z\bar{w}}\Big|.

Here, and throughout the rest of the paper,
\lambda_{2}
denotes Lebegue’s measure in
\mathbb{C}
. Using the above, we see that
v
is subharmonic if for every
\xi\in\partial B_{k}
:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial v}{\partial n_{1}}(\xi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\underset{{r}\rightarrow{1^{-}}}{\lim}\;\frac{u(\xi)-P_{\mathbb{D}}(u_{k})\left({\frac{r\left({\xi-z_{k}}\right)}{r_{k}}}\right)}{1-r}+\frac{A_{k}}{r_{k}}=\underset{{r}\rightarrow{1^{-}}}{\lim}\;\frac{u_{k}\left({\frac{\xi-z_{k}}{r_{k}}}\right)-u_{k}\left({\frac{r\left({\xi-z_{k}}\right)}{r_{k}}}\right)+G_{\mathbb{D}}\left({\frac{r\left({\xi-z_{k}}\right)}{r_{k}}}\right)}{1-r}+\frac{A_{k}}{r_{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\left({u_{k}\circ\left({\frac{z-z_{k}}{r_{k}}}\right)}\right)}{\partial n}\left({\xi}\right)+\underset{{r}\rightarrow{1^{-}}}{\lim}\;\frac{G_{\mathbb{D}}\left({\frac{r\left({\xi-z_{k}}\right)}{r_{k}}}\right)}{1-r}+\frac{A_{k}}{r_{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial u}{\partial n}\left({\xi}\right)-\frac{\partial G_{\mathbb{D}}}{\partial r}\left({\frac{\xi-z_{k}}{r_{k}}}\right)\cdot\frac{1}{r_{k}}+\frac{A_{k}}{r_{k}}\leq\frac{\partial u}{\partial n}(\xi)=\frac{\partial v}{\partial n_{2}}(\xi)

using the fact that
G_{\mathbb{D}}
vanishes on
\partial\mathbb{D}
(since
u=P_{\mathbb{D}}u
there). Then
\frac{\partial v}{\partial n_{1}}(\xi)\leq\frac{\partial v}{\partial n_{2}}(\xi)
if and only if

\frac{\partial u}{\partial n}\left({\xi}\right)-\frac{\partial G_{\mathbb{D}}}{\partial r}\left({\frac{\xi-z_{k}}{r_{k}}}\right)\frac{1}{r_{k}}+\frac{A_{k}}{r_{k}}\leq\frac{\partial v}{\partial n_{2}}\left({\xi}\right)=\frac{\partial u}{\partial n}\left({\xi}\right)\iff A_{k}\leq\underset{\xi\in\partial\mathbb{D}}{\min}\;\frac{\partial G_{\mathbb{D}}}{\partial r}\left({\frac{\xi-z_{k}}{r_{k}}}\right)

Since the collection
\left\{{\frac{\partial g_{\mathbb{D}}\left({\cdot,w}\right)}{\partial n}(\xi)}\right\}_{w\in\mathbb{D},\xi\in\partial\mathbb{D}}
is uniformly integrable, we may change the order of the integral and the derivative to obtain that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial G_{\mathbb{D}}}{\partial r}\left({\frac{\xi-z_{k}}{r_{k}}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left({\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{D}}{g_{\mathbb{D}}\left({\left({\frac{\xi-z_{k}}{r_{k}}}\right),y}\right)\Delta u_{k}(y)}d{\lambda}_{2}(y)}\right)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{D}}{\frac{\partial g_{\mathbb{D}}(\cdot,y)}{\partial r}\left({\frac{\xi-z_{k}}{r_{k}}}\right)\Delta u_{k}(y)}d{\lambda}_{2}(y)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\underset{z\in\mathbb{D}}{\inf}\;\Delta u_{k}(z)\cdot\frac{1}{2\pi}\inf_{\eta\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}{\frac{\partial g_{\mathbb{D}}(\cdot,y)}{\partial r}(\eta)}d{\lambda}_{2}(y)\geq c\cdot r_{k}^{2}\cdot\underset{z\in B_{k}}{\inf}\;\Delta u.

Note that
c
is some uniform constant, which does not depend on the function
u
or the collection
\left\{{B_{k}}\right\}
. A direct computation shows that we may choose
c
to be positive (this follows from the fact that the radial derivative of the Green function
g_{\mathbb{D}}(\cdot,\eta)
is everywhere non-negative and does not vanish identically). Setting
A_{k}:=c\cdot r_{k}^{2}\cdot\underset{z\in B_{k}}{\inf}\;\Delta u
guarantees that
v
is subharmonic.

Finally, for every
\delta\in(0,1)

\displaystyle\underset{\frac{\left|{z-z_{k}}\right|}{r_{k}}=\delta}{\max}\;v(z)=\underset{\frac{\left|{z-z_{k}}\right|}{r_{k}}=\delta}{\max}\;P_{\mathbb{D}}\left({u_{k}}\right)\left({\frac{z-z_{k}}{r_{k}}}\right)+A_{k}\log\left({\frac{\left|{z-z_{k}}\right|}{r_{k}}}\right)\leq\underset{z\in B_{k}}{\max}\;u(z)+c\cdot r_{k}^{2}\cdot\underset{z\in B_{k}}{\inf}\;\Delta u\log(\delta).

∎

3
The proof

In this section we prove Theorem
1.3
. Extending upon Example
3.4
below, we need a function that will coordinate between different periods. In Section
3.1
, we construct a holomorphic function that assumes prescribed values along the sequence
\{2^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 2}}
and whose role is to accommodate for different periods. This part uses the results on subharmonic functions and a theorem of Hörmander.
In Section
3.2
, we modify the construction by Cornalba-Shiffman,
[
2
]
, to construct a holomorphic function of two variables with the required number of
p_{n}
-PPP in every ball
B\left({0,2^{n}+1}\right)
, and the required growth rate, thus proving Theorem
1.3
.

3.1
Constructing the “dispatcher” functions

Lemma 3.1

There exists
C>1
so that for every (finite or infinite) subset
J\subset\mathbb{N}_{n\geq 2}
and every
M\leq(\min J)^{2}
, there exists an entire function
f:\mathbb{C}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
satisfying that

(i)

M_{f}(r):=\underset{\left|{z}\right|=r}{\max}\left|{f}\right|\leq C\cdot e^{C\log^{2}(r+1)}
.

(ii)

f\left({2^{n}}\right)=\begin{cases}M,&n\in J\\
0,&n\in\mathbb{N}_{n\geq 2}\setminus J\end{cases}
.

Remark 3.2

We stress that
C
is independent of
J
and
M
.

Proof.

As mentioned in the introduction, we use Hörmander’s Theorem (Theorem
3.3
below) to construct the “dispatcher” function. A crucial first step is the construction of an appropriate subharmonic function.

3.1.1
Step 1: Constructing an appropriate subharmonic function.

Denote by

u_{0}(z):=\begin{cases}C\log(\left|{z}\right|),&\left|{z}\right|<2\\
\max\left\{{C\log(\left|{z}\right|),C\log^{2}(\left|{z}\right|)}\right\},&\left|{z}\right|\in\left({2,3}\right)\\
C\log^{2}(\left|{z}\right|),&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

where
C>1
will be chose momentarily. Note that
u_{0}
is subharmonic as a local maximum of subharmonic functions (along
\left|{z}\right|=2
the maximum is
C\log\left|{z}\right|
and along
\left|{z}\right|=3
the maximum is
C\log^{2}\left|{z}\right|
). In addition,
u_{0}
is a radial subharmonic function and for every
\left|{z}\right|>3

\Delta u_{0}(z)=\Delta u_{0}(\left|{z}\right|)={\frac{2C}{\left|{z}\right|^{2}}}.

Define the collection of pairwise disjoint disks,
B_{k}=B\left({2^{k},r_{k}}\right)
for
r_{k}=2^{k-3}
and
k\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 2}
, and note that
\bigcup_{{k}={2}}^{\infty}B_{k}\subset\left\{{\left|{z}\right|>3}\right\}
.

We use the Puncture Lemma, Lemma
2.1
, with the function
u_{0}
and the collection of disks
\left\{{B_{k}}\right\}
to construct a subharmonic function
u
satisfying that outside
\left\{{B_{k}}\right\}
we have
u(z)=u_{0}(z)
and for every
\delta\in\left({0,1}\right)
, and
k\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 2}

\displaystyle\underset{z\in B(2^{k},\delta\cdot r_{k})}{\max}\;u(z)

\displaystyle\leq\underset{z\in B_{k}}{\max}\;u_{0}(z)-c\cdot r_{k}^{2}\cdot\underset{z\in B_{k}}{\inf}\;\Delta u_{0}\log\left({\frac{1}{\delta}}\right)

(1)

\displaystyle\leq C(k+1)^{2}-c\cdot 2^{2k-6}\cdot\frac{2C}{2^{2(k+1)}}\log\left({\frac{1}{\delta}}\right)\leq 4Ck^{2}+{4}\log(\delta)

assuming
C\geq\frac{2^{9}}{c}
, where
c
is the constant from the Puncture Lemma, Lemma
2.1
.

3.1.2
Step 2: The model map and the holomorphic approximation.

Let
D_{k}:=B\left({2^{k},2^{k-2}}\right)
and consider any (finite or infinite) subset
J\subset\mathbb{N}_{n\geq 2}
. We define the model map

h(z):=M\cdot\underset{k\in J}{\sum}\;\mathbf{1}_{D_{k}}(z)

Let
\chi:\mathbb{C}\rightarrow[0,1]
be a smooth map satisfying

1.

\chi(w)=1
whenever
w\in\bigcup_{{k}={2}}^{\infty}B\left({2^{k},\frac{1}{2}2^{k-2}}\right)
.

2.

\chi(w)=0
whenever
w\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\bigcup_{{k}={2}}^{\infty}D_{k}
.

3.

There exists a uniform constant
A>1
such that

\left|{\nabla\chi(w)}\right|\leq A\cdot\underset{{k}={2}}{\overset{\infty}{\sum}}\;2^{-k}\cdot\mathbf{1}_{D_{k}\setminus B\left({2^{k},\frac{1}{2}2^{k-2}}\right)}(w).

For a construction of such a function, see e.g.
[
5
, Proposition 2.5]
.

In order to find a holomorphic map approximating the model map
h
along the sequence of points
\left\{{2^{k}}\right\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{n\geq 2}}
while also providing growth bounds, we use Hörmander’s theorem:

Theorem 3.3

[Hörmander,
[
6
, Theorem 4.2.1]
] Let
u:\mathbb{C}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a subharmonic function. Then, for every locally integrable function
g
there is a solution
\alpha
of the equation
\bar{\partial}\alpha=g
such that:

(2)

\iint_{\mathbb{C}}\left|{\alpha(z)}\right|^{2}\frac{e^{-u(z)}}{\left({1+\left|{z}\right|^{2}}\right)^{2}}d\lambda_{2}(z)\leq\frac{1}{2}\iint_{\mathbb{C}}\left|{g(z)}\right|^{2}e^{-u(z)}d\lambda_{2}(z),

provided that the integral in the right hand side is finite.

We define the function
g(z):=\overline{\partial}\chi(z)\cdot h(z)=\overline{\partial}\left({\chi\cdot h}\right)(z)
, which is supported in
\bigcup_{k\in J}\left({D_{k}\setminus B\left({2^{k},\frac{1}{2}2^{k-2}}\right)}\right)
. Note that
u(z)=u_{0}(z)=C\log^{2}(|z|)
on the support of
g
.
To apply Hörmander’s theorem, we bound the following integral:

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{C}}\left|{g(z)}\right|^{2}\cdot e^{-u(z)}d\lambda_{2}(z)=\iint_{\bigcup_{k\in J}\left({D_{k}\setminus B\left({2^{k},2^{k-3}}\right)}\right)}\left|{\overline{\partial}\chi(z)}\right|^{2}\left|{h(z)}\right|^{2}\cdot e^{-u(z)}d\lambda_{2}(z)

\displaystyle=\iint_{\bigcup_{k\in J}\left({D_{k}\setminus B\left({2^{k},2^{k-3}}\right)}\right)}\left|{\overline{\partial}\chi(z)}\right|^{2}\left|{h(z)}\right|^{2}\cdot e^{-u_{0}(z)}d\lambda_{2}(z)

(3)

\displaystyle\leq A^{2}\sum_{k\in J}\ \iint_{D_{k}\setminus B\left({2^{k},2^{k-3}}\right)}2^{-2k}M^{2}e^{-C(k-1)^{2}}\leq C_{1}^{2}\underset{{k}={1}}{\overset{\infty}{\sum}}\;k^{4}e^{-C(k-1)^{2}}:=I<\infty,

since
M\leq(\min J)^{2}
, while if
k\not\in J
, the model map satisfies
h|_{D_{k}}=0
. It is important to note that
I
is a numerical number which does
NOT
depend on
M
or on the set
J
.

We apply Hörmander’s theorem with the map
g
and the subharmonic function
u
, constructed in Step 1, to obtain a function
\alpha:\mathbb{C}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
satisfying
\bar{\partial}\alpha=g
and estimate (
2
), and define

f(z)=\chi(z)\cdot h(z)-\alpha(z).

It is not hard to see that
f
is an entire function, as a solution to the
\bar{\partial}
equation.

3.1.3
Step 3: Bounding the error.

We will show that for every
k\in\mathbb{N}_{n\geq 2}
the function,
f
, constructed above, agrees with the model map,
h
, at
2^{k}
.

Fix
k\in\mathbb{N}_{n\geq 2}
and note that for every
\delta\in\left({0,\frac{1}{2}}\right)
, the function
\chi
satisfies
\left.\chi\right|_{B\left({2^{k},\delta\cdot 2^{k-2}}\right)}\equiv 1
. Using Cauchy’s integral formula for the function
f-h
, which on
B\left({2^{k},\delta\cdot 2^{k-2}}\right)
is both holomorphic and coincides with
\alpha
, we obtain that up to a uniform constant,

\displaystyle\left|{f\left({2^{k}}\right)-h\left({2^{k}}\right)}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|{\alpha\left({2^{k}}\right)}\right|\lesssim\frac{1}{\left({\delta\cdot 2^{k-2}}\right)^{2}}\underset{B\left({2^{k},\delta\cdot 2^{k-2}}\right)}{\iint}|\alpha(w)|d\lambda_{2}(w)=\frac{1}{\left({\delta\cdot 2^{k-2}}\right)^{2}}\sqrt{\left|{\underset{B\left({2^{k},\delta\cdot 2^{k-2}}\right)}{\iint}|\alpha(w)|\cdot\overline{\bf 1}d\lambda_{2}(w)}\right|^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\delta^{2}\cdot 2^{2k}}\sqrt{\underset{B\left({2^{k},\delta\cdot 2^{k-2}}\right)}{\iint}\left|{\alpha(w)}\right|^{2}d\lambda_{2}(w)\cdot\underset{B\left({2^{k},\delta\cdot 2^{k-2}}\right)}{\iint}\left|{{\bf 1}(w)}\right|^{2}d\lambda_{2}(w)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\delta\cdot 2^{k}}\underset{w\in B\left({2^{k},\delta\cdot 2^{k-2}}\right)}{\max}\;\left({1+\left|{w}\right|^{2}}\right)e^{\frac{1}{2}u(w)}\sqrt{\iint_{\mathbb{C}}\left|{\alpha(w)}\right|^{2}\frac{e^{-u(w)}}{\left({1+\left|{w}\right|^{2}}\right)^{2}}d\lambda_{2}(w)},

where the second inequality follows from Cauchy-Schwartz inequality. Using inequality (
2
) from Hormander’s Theorem, the above integral can be bounded by the integral of
|g|^{2}e^{-u}
, which we estimated in (
3
). Combining these bounds with (
1
), we obtain

\displaystyle\left|{f\left({2^{k}}\right)-h\left({2^{k}}\right)}\right|\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\left({\delta\cdot 2^{k}}\right)}\cdot 2\cdot 2^{2(k+1)}\cdot\exp\left({\frac{1}{2}\left({4Ck^{2}+4\log(\delta)}\right)}\right)\cdot I

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\exp\left({-\log(\delta)+2Ck^{2}+2\log(\delta)}\right)\leq e^{2Ck^{2}}\cdot{\delta}\underset{\delta\rightarrow 0}{\longrightarrow}0.

We conclude that
f\left({2^{k}}\right)=h\left({2^{k}}\right)
, i.e., property
(ii)
holds.

3.1.4
Step 4: Bounding the growth.

To see that property
(i)
holds, we use a similar calculation noting that
\left|{h(z)}\right|\leq 4\log^{2}\left({\left|{z}\right|+1}\right)
, since if
z\not\in\underset{k\in J}{\bigcup}D_{k}
, then
\left|{h(z)}\right|=0\leq 4\log^{2}\left({\left|{z}\right|+1}\right)
and if
z\in\underset{k\in J}{\bigcup}D_{k}
then
\left|{h(z)}\right|=M\leq\left({\underset{k\in J}{\min}\;k}\right)^{2}\leq 4\log^{2}\left|{z}\right|
.

This time, we apply Cauchy’s integral formula to
f
rather than
\alpha
, as
\alpha
is not necessarily holomorphic in these disks. We obtain

\displaystyle\left|{f(z)}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|{\chi(z)h(z)-\alpha(z)}\right|=\frac{1}{\pi}\underset{B\left({z,1}\right)}{\iint}|\chi(w)h(w)-\alpha(w)|d\lambda_{2}(w)\leq\frac{1}{\pi}\underset{B\left({z,1}\right)}{\iint}|\chi(w)h(w)|+|\alpha(w)|d\lambda_{2}(w)

\displaystyle\leq\underset{w\in B(z,1)}{\sup}\left|{h(w)}\right|+\frac{1}{\pi}\sqrt{\underset{B\left({z,1}\right)}{\iint}|\alpha(w)|^{2}d\lambda_{2}(w)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\log^{2}\left({\left|{z}\right|+1}\right)+\underset{w\in B\left({z,1}\right)}{\max}\;\left({1+\left|{w}\right|^{2}}\right)e^{\frac{1}{2}u(w)}\sqrt{\iint_{\mathbb{C}}\left|{\alpha(w)}\right|^{2}\frac{e^{-u(w)}}{\left({1+\left|{w}\right|^{2}}\right)^{2}}d\lambda_{2}(w)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\log^{2}(\left|{z}\right|+1)+\left({2+\left|{z}\right|^{2}}\right)e^{\frac{C}{2}\log^{2}(\left|{z}\right|+1)}\cdot I,

where we used the fact that
u(w)\leq u_{0}(w)=C\log^{2}(|w|)
. Overall, we see that

\left|{f(z)}\right|\lesssim e^{C\log^{2}(\left|{z}\right|+1)},

concluding the proof of Lemma
3.1
.
∎

3.2
Modifying Cornalba-Shiffman’s construction

Before constructing an entire function with many primitive periodic points of different periods, let us explain a simple way to construct an entire function with many p-primitive periodic points for a single period,
p
. Roughly speaking, the construction takes (a symmetrized version of) the Cornalba-Shiffman’s example and adds to it a standard rotation by
\frac{2\pi}{p}
, formally defined by
\Theta_{p}(z)=e^{\frac{2\pi i}{p}}z
, which rotates the plane by
\frac{2\pi}{p}
counter clockwise. Zeros of Cornalba-Shiffman’s construction then become
p
-primitive periodic points.

Example 3.4

Fix
p\in\mathbb{N}
. Note that while
\Theta_{p}
is a
p
-periodic function, its periodic points are not isolated. Let
F(z,w):\mathbb{C}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{2}
be any entire function of order zero satisfying that in every ball of radius
2^{n}
,
F
has
m_{n}
isolated zeros that are symmetric under the rotation
\Theta_{p}
:

(z_{j},w_{j})\in\mathbb{C}^{2},\ j=1,\dots,m_{n}\quad\text{such that}\quad F\left({\Theta_{p}^{\circ\ell}(z_{j}),w_{j}}\right)=0\quad\text{for all}\quad\ell=0,\dots,k-1.

Here, and everywhere else,
\Theta_{p}^{\circ\ell}
is the composition of
\Theta_{p}
with itself
\ell
times. One example of such function is a symmetrized version of the function constructed by Cornalba and Shiffman, which we explicitly synthesize in Lemma
3.6
. However, any function with isolated symmetric zeroes as above will do. Then

F_{p}(z,w)=F(z,w)+\left({\Theta_{p}(z),w}\right)

is a holomorphic function of order zero satisfying that in every ball of radius
2^{n}
it has
m_{n}
points which are
p
-PPP.

We would like to modify this construction, to construct functions with primitive periodic points of varying periods. For this we will use the dispatcher function constructed in the previous subsection. Our main goal for this section is to complete the proof of Theorem
1.3
, which states that for every sequence of periods,
\left\{{p_{n}}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
, and for every rate,
\left\{{m_{n}}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
, there exists a holomorphic function
F:\mathbb{C}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{2}
satisfying

\nu_{p_{n}}(F,2^{n})\geq m_{n}\quad\text{and}\quad\log M_{F}(r)=O((\log r)^{2}).

Remark 3.5

To simplify our notation we will assume our sequences start from
n=2
. As the sequences are arbitrary, this does not limit the generality of the result.

Lemma 3.6

Given a sequence of periods,
\left\{{p_{n}}\right\}
, and a rate,
\left\{{m_{n}}\right\}
, there exists a holomorphic function
G=\left({g_{1},g_{2}}\right):\mathbb{C}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{2}
of order zero satisfying

•

The set of isolated zeros of
G
contains the set

Z:=\bigcup_{{n}={1}}^{\infty}Z_{n}:=\bigcup_{{n}={1}}^{\infty}\left\{{\left({\frac{e^{\frac{2\pi i\ell}{p_{n}}}}{j},2^{n}}\right):\ 1\leq j\leq m_{n},0\leq\ell\leq p_{n}-1}\right\}.

•

\left.\frac{d}{dz}g_{1}\right|_{Z}\neq 0
and satisfies

\frac{d}{dz}g_{1}\left({\Theta_{p_{n}}^{\circ\ell}\left({\frac{1}{j}}\right),2^{n}}\right)=\Theta_{p_{n}}^{\circ\left({-\ell}\right)}\left({\frac{d}{dz}g_{1}\left({\frac{1}{j},2^{n}}\right)}\right).

•

\left.\frac{d}{dw}g_{2}\right|_{Z}\neq 0
, and
\frac{d}{dz}g_{2}\equiv 0
, i.e.,
g_{2}
does not depend on
z
.

•

There exists a uniform constant
C>1
(independent of the sequences
\left\{{m_{n}}\right\},\left\{{p_{n}}\right\}
) such that

\log M_{G}(R)\leq 40\log^{2}(R+1)+C.

Proof.

Similarly to Carnalba-Shiffman’s original construction, let

Q(w):=\underset{{j}={1}}{\overset{\infty}{\prod}}\;\left({1-\frac{w}{2^{j}}}\right)\quad,\quad\quad Q_{n}(w):=\underset{j\neq n}{\prod}\left({1-\frac{w}{2^{j}}}\right)=\frac{Q(w)}{1-\frac{w}{2^{n}}},

and for every
n
consider the following polynomial of degree
(m_{n}+p_{n})
:

P_{n}(z)=\underset{{j}={1}}{\overset{m_{n}}{\prod}}\;\underset{{\ell}={0}}{\overset{p_{n}-1}{\prod}}\;\left({z-\frac{e^{\frac{2\pi i\ell}{p_{n}}}}{j}}\right)=\underset{{j}={1}}{\overset{m_{n}}{\prod}}\;\underset{{\ell}={0}}{\overset{p_{n}-1}{\prod}}\;\left({z-z_{j,\ell}}\right).

where
z_{j,\ell}:=\frac{e^{\frac{2\pi i\ell}{p_{n}}}}{j}
, are the zeros of
P_{n}
.

Define the function

G(z,w):=\left({g_{1}(z,w),g_{2}(z,w)}\right)=\left({\underset{{n}={1}}{\overset{\infty}{\sum}}\;2^{-\ell_{n}}\cdot Q_{n}(w)\cdot P_{n}(z),\ Q(w)}\right),

where the sequence
\left\{{\ell_{n}}\right\}\nearrow\infty
will be chosen throughout the proof so that
G
satisfies the assertions of the lemma.

Derivatives and isolation of zeros:
Starting with the requirement on the derivatives, it is clear that
g_{2}(z,w)=Q(w)
does not depend on
z
. In addition, the derivative of
g_{2}
with respect to
w
is non-vanishing on
Z
since
\frac{d}{dw}g_{2}(z,w)=Q^{\prime}(w)
and

\left.Q^{\prime}(w)\right|_{w=2^{m}}=\left.\underset{j}{\sum}2^{-j}\underset{\nu\neq j}{\prod}\left({1-\frac{w}{2^{\nu}}}\right)\right|_{w=2^{m}}=2^{-m}\underset{\nu\neq m}{\prod}\left({1-2^{m-\nu}}\right)\neq 0.

The estimate of the derivative of
g_{1}
with respect to
z
requires a computation of the derivatives of
P_{n}
, using the symmetry of the set
\left\{{e^{\frac{2\pi j}{p_{n}}}}\right\}_{\ell=0}^{p_{n}-1}
:

\displaystyle P_{n}^{\prime}\left({z_{j,\ell}}\right)

\displaystyle=\underset{{z}\rightarrow{z_{j,\ell}}}{\lim}\;\frac{P_{n}(z)}{z-z_{j,\ell}}=\underset{\left({\nu,k)\neq(j,\ell}\right)}{\prod}\left({\frac{e^{\frac{2\pi i\ell}{p_{n}}}}{j}-\frac{e^{\frac{2\pi ik}{p_{n}}}}{\nu}}\right)

\displaystyle=\left({e^{\frac{2\pi i\ell}{p_{n}}}}\right)^{p_{n}\cdot m_{n}-1}\underset{\left({\nu,k)\neq(j,\ell}\right)}{\prod}\left({\frac{1}{j}-\frac{e^{\frac{2\pi i\left({k-\ell}\right)}{p_{n}}}}{\nu}}\right)=e^{-\frac{2\pi i\ell}{p_{n}}}\underset{\left({\nu,k)\neq(j,\ell}\right)}{\prod}\left({\frac{1}{j}-\frac{e^{\frac{2\pi i\left({k-\ell}\right)}{p_{n}}}}{\nu}}\right).

Let us related the derivatives at
z_{j,\ell}
and
z_{j,0}
. Shifting the product by
e^{\frac{2\pi i\ell}{p_{n}}}
we see that if we fix
\nu\neq j
, the product over
k
is taken over the entire set
\left\{{0,1,\cdots,p_{n}-1}\right\}
(shifted by
2\pi\ell
). On the other hand, if
\nu=j
the product that was taken over the set
\left\{{0,1,2,\cdots,p_{n}-1}\right\}\setminus\left\{{\ell}\right\}
shifted is the product taken over the set
\left\{{1,2,\cdots,p_{n}-1}\right\}
, which is the derivative at
z_{j,0}
. We see that

P_{n}^{\prime}\left({z_{j,\ell}}\right)=e^{-\frac{2\pi i\ell}{p_{n}}}\underset{\left({\nu,k)\neq(j,0}\right)}{\prod}\left({\frac{1}{j}-\frac{e^{\frac{2\pi{k}}{p_{n}}}}{\nu}}\right)=e^{-\frac{2\pi i\ell}{p_{n}}}P_{n}^{\prime}\left({z_{j,0}}\right)=\Theta_{p_{n}}^{\circ\left({-\ell}\right)}\left({P_{n}^{\prime}\left({\frac{1}{j}}\right)}\right),

implying that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dz}g_{1}\left({\Theta_{p_{n}}^{\circ\ell}\left({\frac{1}{j}}\right),2^{n}}\right)

\displaystyle=2^{-\ell_{n}}\cdot Q(2^{n})\cdot P_{n}^{\prime}\left({\Theta_{p_{n}}^{\circ\ell}\left({\frac{1}{j}}\right)}\right)

\displaystyle=2^{-\ell_{n}}\cdot Q(2^{n})\cdot\Theta_{p_{n}}^{\circ\left({-\ell}\right)}\left({P_{n}^{\prime}\left({\frac{1}{j}}\right)}\right)=\Theta_{p_{n}}^{\circ\left({-\ell}\right)}\left({\frac{d}{dz}g_{1}\left({\frac{1}{j},2^{n}}\right)}\right).

Growth bound:
To conclude the proof, we will show that the sequence
\left\{{\ell_{n}}\right\}
can be chosen so that
\log M_{G}(R)\leq 40\log^{2}(R+1)+C
for some uniform constant
C
.

We first bound the growth of the functions
Q_{n}
independently of
n
. Fix
w\in\mathbb{C}
, and let
k
be so such that
\left|{w}\right|\in\left[2^{k},2^{k+1}\right)
. Then,

\displaystyle\left|{Q_{n}(w)}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|{\exp\left({\log\left({\underset{j\neq n}{\prod}\left({1-\frac{w}{2^{j}}}\right)}\right)}\right)}\right|\leq\exp\left({\underset{j\neq n}{\sum}\log\left({1+\frac{\left|{w}\right|}{2^{j}}}\right)}\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\left({\underset{{j}={1}}{\overset{k+1}{\sum}}\;\log\left({1+\frac{\left|{w}\right|}{2^{j}}}\right)}\right)\cdot\exp\left({\underset{{j}={k+2}}{\overset{\infty}{\sum}}\;\log\left({1+\frac{\left|{w}\right|}{2^{j}}}\right)}\right)\leq\exp\left({(k+1)\log\left({2\left|{w}\right|}\right)}\right)\cdot\exp\left({\underset{{j}={k+2}}{\overset{\infty}{\sum}}\;\frac{\left|{w}\right|}{2^{j}}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left({2\log^{2}\left|{w}\right|}\right)\cdot\exp\left({2^{-(k+1)}\left|{w}\right|}\right)\leq\exp\left({2\log^{2}\left|{w}\right|+1}\right).

The same computation shows that
|Q(w)|\leq\exp\left({2\log^{2}\left|{w}\right|+1}\right)
as well, which concludes the growth bound for
g_{2}
.

Next we bound the growth of
g_{1}
. The degree of the polynomial
P_{n}
is
(m_{n}+p_{n})
, and it is bounded by

\left|{P_{n}(z)}\right|=\left|{\underset{{j}={1}}{\overset{m_{n}}{\prod}}\;\underset{{\ell}={0}}{\overset{p_{n}-1}{\prod}}\;\left({z-z_{j,\ell}}\right)}\right|=\underset{{j}={1}}{\overset{m_{n}}{\prod}}\;\underset{{\ell}={0}}{\overset{p_{n}-1}{\prod}}\;\left|{{z-z_{j,\ell}}}\right|\leq\underset{{j}={1}}{\overset{m_{n}}{\prod}}\;\underset{{\ell}={0}}{\overset{p_{n}-1}{\prod}}\;\left({\left|{z}\right|+1}\right)=\left({\left|{z}\right|+1}\right)^{m_{n}+p_{n}}.

For every
k\geq 2
consider
(z,w)
with
\sqrt{\left|{z}\right|^{2}+\left|{w}\right|^{2}}\in\left[{2^{k-1},2^{k}}\right]
. For simplicity of the arguments below, we assume that the sequence
\deg\left({P_{n}}\right)=m_{n}+p_{n}
is strictly increasing. Note that this can be achieved by increasing
m_{n}
, which does not change the statement of the lemma, as
m_{n}
is the number of
p_{n}
-PPP, increasing it will generate more points than required. Define
\mu_{k}:=\min\left\{{n:deg\left({P_{n}}\right)\geq 2(k+1)}\right\}
. Then, since
\left|{z}\right|<2^{k}
,

\displaystyle\left|{g_{1}(z,w)}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|{\underset{{n}={1}}{\overset{\infty}{\sum}}\;2^{-\ell_{n}}Q_{n}(w)\cdot P_{n}(z)}\right|\leq\underset{n\in\mathbb{N}}{\sup}\left|{Q_{n}(w)}\right|\underset{{n}={1}}{\overset{\infty}{\sum}}\;2^{-\ell_{n}}\left({1+\left|{z}\right|}\right)^{m_{n}+p_{n}}

(4)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left({2\log^{2}\left|{z}\right|+1}\right)\left({\underset{{n}={1}}{\overset{\mu_{k}-1}{\sum}}\;2^{\left({k+1}\right)\left({m_{n}+p_{n}}\right)-\ell_{n}}+\underset{{n}={\mu_{k}}}{\overset{\infty}{\sum}}\;2^{\left({k+1}\right)\left({m_{n}+p_{n}}\right)-\ell_{n}}}\right).

We shall bound each sum separately.

To bound the first summand in (
4
), note that we may assume without loss of generality that
\mu_{k}\leq k
(this happens whenever the sequence
m_{n}
grows fast enough) and that
\ell_{n}>deg(P_{n})^{2}=\left({m_{n}+p_{n}}\right)^{2}
. Then

\displaystyle\underset{{n}={1}}{\overset{\mu_{k}-1}{\sum}}\;2^{-\ell_{n}+(k+1)\cdot(m_{n}+p_{n})}

\displaystyle\leq\underset{{n}={1}}{\overset{\mu_{k}-1}{\sum}}\;2^{-\left({m_{n}+p_{n}}\right)^{2}+(k+1)\cdot(m_{n}+p_{n})}\leq\mu_{k}\cdot 2^{2(k+1)^{2}}\leq\exp\left({2\log(k)+8\cdot k^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left({9k^{2}}\right)\leq\exp\left({36\log^{2}\left({\sqrt{\left|{z}\right|^{2}+\left|{w}\right|^{2}}}\right)}\right),

where in the first inequality we used our choice of
\ell_{n}>\left({m_{n}+p_{n}}\right)^{2}
, and in the second, the fact that for
n\leq\mu_{k}
,
m_{n}+p_{n}=deg(P_{n})\leq 2(k+1)
.

To bound the second sum in (
4

\mu_{k}
was chosen for every

,

m_{n}+p_{n}=deg(P_{n})\geq deg\left({P_{\mu_{k}}}\right)\geq 2\left({k+1}\right)

implying that

\underset{{n}={\mu_{k}}}{\overset{\infty}{\sum}}\;2^{\left({k+1}\right)\left({m_{n}+p_{n}}\right)-\ell_{n}}\leq\underset{{n}={\mu_{k}}}{\overset{\infty}{\sum}}\;2^{-\left({m_{n}+p_{n}}\right)^{2}\left({1-\frac{k+1}{m_{n}+p_{n}}}\right)}\leq\underset{{n}={\mu_{k}}}{\overset{\infty}{\sum}}\;2^{-\frac{1}{2}\left({m_{n}+p_{n}}\right)^{2}}<\underset{{k}={1}}{\overset{\infty}{\sum}}\;2^{-\frac{1}{2}k^{2}}<1.

Combining the two estimates together, we see that

\log M_{g_{1}}(R)\leq 3\log^{2}(R+1)+36\log^{2}(R+1)+C\leq 39\log^{2}(R+1)+C,

and thus the desired bound holds for
G
as well, concluding our proof.
∎

with prescribed PPP’s for any sequences of periods and any rate.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

In light of remark
3.5
, for every
m\geq 2
we let

be a dispatcher function created by the Dispatcher Lemma, Lemma
3.1
, with

, a singleton, and the constant

. Recall that
D_{m}
is a holomorphic function satisfying

D_{m}(2^{n})=\begin{cases}m^{2}&,n=m\\
0&,\text{otherwise}\end{cases}.

We define the function

F(z,w)=G(z,w)+\left({\underset{{m}={2}}{\overset{\infty}{\sum}}\;\frac{1}{m^{2}}D_{m}(w)\cdot\Theta_{p_{m}}(z),w}\right),

where
G(z,w)
is the modification of Cornalba-Shiffman’s construction constructed in Lemma
3.6
above.

We need to show that
F
has at least
m_{n}
isolated
p_{n}
-PPP in every ball

and that it satisfies the correct growth rate.
As in Lemma

, we define the sets

Z_{n}:=\left\{{\left({\frac{e^{\frac{2\pi i\ell}{p_{n}}}}{j},2^{n}}\right):\ 1\leq j\leq m_{n},0\leq\ell\leq p_{n}-1}\right\}\subset B(0,2^{n}+1).

Observation 1:

F|_{Z_{n}}\left({z,2^{n}}\right)=\left({\Theta_{p_{n}}(z),2^{n}}\right)
and every point in
Z_{n}
is a
p_{n}
-PPP.

Indeed, note that

G|_{Z_{n}}\equiv 0\quad\quad,\quad\quad\left.\left({\underset{{m}={2}}{\overset{\infty}{\sum}}\;\frac{1}{m^{2}}D_{m}}\right)\right|_{Z_{n}}=\frac{1}{n^{2}}\cdot D_{n}(2^{n})=1,

F|_{Z_{n}}=G|_{Z_{n}}+\left({\left.\left({\underset{{m}={2}}{\overset{\infty}{\sum}}\;\frac{1}{m^{2}}D_{m}(2^{n})\cdot\Theta_{p_{m}}(\cdot)}\right)\right|_{Z_{n}},2^{n}}\right)=\left({\Theta_{p_{n}}(\cdot),2^{n}}\right).

In particular, for every

F^{\circ k}\left({z,2^{n}}\right)=F^{\circ(k-1)}\left({\Theta_{p_{n}}\left({z}\right),2^{n}}\right)=\cdots=\left({\Theta_{p_{n}}^{\circ k}\left({z}\right),2^{n}}\right)=\left({z,2^{n}}\right)\iff k=p_{n}\cdot\nu\quad,\quad\nu\in\mathbb{N},

concluding the proof of observation 1.

Observation 2:
The points in
Z_{n}
are isolated
p_{n}
-PPP.

Indeed, recall that for every
f:\mathbb{C}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
,

\frac{d}{dz}\left({f^{\circ{p_{n}}}}\right)=\underset{{k}={1}}{\overset{p_{n}}{\prod}}\;\frac{d}{dz}f\left({f^{\circ(k-1)}}\right).

In addition, following Observation 1,
F|_{Z_{n}}\left({z,2^{n}}\right)=\left({\Theta_{p_{n}}(z),2^{n}}\right)
, implying that for every

F^{\circ(k-1)}(z,2^{n})=\left({\Theta_{p_{n}}^{\circ(k-1)}z,2^{n}}\right)=\left({e^{\frac{2\pi i(k-1)}{p_{n}}}z,2^{n}}\right).

We write

F(z,w)=(f_{1}(z,w),f_{2}(z,w))=\left({g_{1}(z,w)+\underset{{m}={1}}{\overset{\infty}{\sum}}\;\frac{1}{m^{2}}D_{m}(w)\cdot\Theta_{p_{m}}(z),g_{2}(z,w)+w}\right).

Fix
\left({\frac{1}{j},2^{n}}\right)\in Z_{n}
, we let
z_{k}=\Theta_{p_{n}}^{\circ k}\left({\frac{1}{j}}\right)
, then
\left({z_{k},2^{n}}\right)\in Z_{n}
as well and as
\frac{d}{dz}g_{2}\equiv 0
,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dz}\left({F^{\circ p_{n}}}\right)\left({\frac{1}{j},2^{n}}\right)

\displaystyle=\left({\underset{{k}={1}}{\overset{p_{n}}{\prod}}\;\frac{d}{dz}f_{1}\left({F^{\circ(k-1)}\left({\frac{1}{j},2^{n}}\right)}\right),\underset{{k}={1}}{\overset{p_{n}}{\prod}}\;\frac{d}{dz}f_{2}\left({F^{\circ(k-1)}\left({\frac{1}{j},2^{n}}\right)}\right)}\right)

\displaystyle=\left({\underset{{k}={1}}{\overset{p_{n}}{\prod}}\;\left({\frac{d}{dz}g_{1}\left({z_{k-1},2^{n}}\right)+\underset{{m}={1}}{\overset{\infty}{\sum}}\;\frac{1}{m^{2}}D_{m}(2^{n})\cdot\frac{d}{dz}\Theta_{p_{m}}\left({z_{k-1}}\right)}\right),\underset{{k}={1}}{\overset{p_{n}}{\prod}}\;\frac{d}{dz}g_{2}\left({z_{k-1},2^{n}}\right)}\right)

\displaystyle=\left({\underset{{k}={1}}{\overset{p_{n}}{\prod}}\;\left({\frac{d}{dz}g_{1}\left({z_{k-1},2^{n}}\right)+e^{\frac{2\pi i}{p_{n}}}}\right),0}\right),

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dw}\left({F^{\circ p_{n}}}\right)\left({\frac{1}{j},2^{n}}\right)

\displaystyle=\left({\underset{{k}={1}}{\overset{p_{n}}{\prod}}\;\left({\frac{d}{dw}g_{1}\left({z_{k-1},2^{n}}\right)+\underset{{m}={1}}{\overset{\infty}{\sum}}\;\frac{1}{m^{2}}\frac{d}{dw}D_{m}(2^{n})z_{k-1}}\right),1+\frac{d}{dw}g_{2}\left({z_{k-1},2^{n}}\right)}\right).

is degenerate at

, then

\displaystyle 0=det\left({\frac{d}{dz}F^{\circ p_{n}}-Id,\frac{d}{dw}F^{\circ p_{n}}-Id}\right)

\displaystyle\iff\underset{{k}={1}}{\overset{p_{n}}{\prod}}\;\left({\frac{d}{dz}g_{1}\left({z_{k-1},2^{n}}\right)+e^{\frac{2\pi i}{p_{n}}}}\right)=1\text{ or }\frac{d}{dw}g_{2}\left({z_{k-1},2^{n}}\right)=0.

However, on one hand,
\left.\frac{d}{dw}g_{2}\right|_{Z}\neq 0
by Lemma
3.6
. On the other hand, following the same lemma, if we denote by

then for every

We conclude that

\underset{{k}={1}}{\overset{p_{n}}{\prod}}\;\left({\frac{d}{dz}g_{1}\left({z_{k-1},2^{n}}\right)+e^{\frac{2\pi i}{p_{n}}}}\right)=\underset{{k}={1}}{\overset{p_{n}}{\prod}}\;\left({e^{-\frac{2\pi ik}{p_{n}}}\cdot a+1}\right)=\underset{{k}={0}}{\overset{p_{n}-1}{\prod}}\;\left({1+a\cdot e^{\frac{2\pi ik}{p_{n}}}}\right)

implying that

\left|{\underset{{k}={1}}{\overset{p_{n}}{\prod}}\;\left({\frac{d}{dz}g_{1}\left({z_{k-1},2^{n}}\right)+e^{\frac{2\pi i}{p_{n}}}}\right)-1}\right|=\left|{\underset{{k}={0}}{\overset{p_{n}-1}{\prod}}\;\left({1+a\cdot e^{\frac{2\pi ik}{p_{n}}}}\right)-1}\right|.

To estimate the latter, note that if
\varphi:=e^{\frac{2\pi i}{p_{n}}}
, then
\left({z^{p_{n}}-1}\right)=\underset{{k}={0}}{\overset{p_{n}-1}{\prod}}\;\left({z-\varphi^{k}}\right)
as the both are polynomials of degree

with the same

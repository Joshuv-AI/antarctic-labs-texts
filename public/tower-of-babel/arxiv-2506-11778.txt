Abstract




Abstract.
In this article, we consider a stochastic linear quadratic control problem with partial observation. A near optimal control in the weak formulation
is characterized.
The main features of this paper are the presence of the control in the diffusion term of the state equation,
the circular dependence between the control process and the filtration generated by the observation, and the observation process contains an unbounded drift term.
We address these difficulties by first restricting the control to a smaller domain, which enables us to apply the
Girsanov theorem using a conditional argument and thereby break the circular dependence. Subsequently, we study the restricted problem using a non-standard
variation method.
The desired near optimal control is then obtained by taking the limit of an approximating sequence.

Keywords:
Partial observation, linear-quadratic control problem, near optimal control, conditional Novikov condition, approximating sequence, weak formulation.

AMS subject classifications:
49N10, 49N30, 93E20.

1
Introduction

Linear-quadratic (LQ, for short) optimal control problem plays a fundamental role in control theory, which appeared with the birth of stochastic analysis and developed rapidly in recent decades due to its wide range of applications. The study of stochastic linear-quadratic (SLQ, for short) optimal control problems can be traced back to the work of Kushner
[
10
]
and Wonham
[
19
]
.
In the classical framework, the SLQ optimal control problem can be solved elegantly via the Riccati equation under some mild conditions on the weighting coefficients (see Yong and Zhou
[
24
, Chapter 6]
). In other words,
the optimal control could be constructed if the controller has access to the exact value of the state.
However, in many practical situations, it often happens that some components of the state may be inaccessible for observation, and there could be noise existing in the observation systems. In this case, only partial information is available to the controller, and the control has to be selected according to the information provided by the observation systems.
This class of problems is summarized as SLQ optimal control problem with partial observation.

The partial observation problem in stochastic control involves how to make optimal decisions in a stochastic environment with incomplete information. To our best knowledge, the earliest research on optimal control with partial observation dates back to Florentin
[
4
]
. Since then, researchers found that it can be applied to many practical problems such as the mean-variance portfolio selection problems (see Xiong
et\ al

[
21
]
, and Xiong and Zhou
[
22
]
).
It should be pointed out that the key idea in dealing with the partial observation problem is to separate it into a control and a filtering problem. On the one hand, for the latter, the filtering theory will be very useful.
Note that the objective of the filtering theory is to obtain the best estimate of the state process based on noisy observations related to this state. In general, this estimation is equivalent to finding a suitable conditional expectation. For more research on filtering theory, we refer the reader to Bode and Shannon
[
2
]
, Fujisaki
et al

[
5
]
, Zakai
[
26
]
, and Zhang and Xiong
[
25
]
. As a systematic introduction to stochastic filtering, one can refer to the books of Kallianpur
[
6
]
and Xiong
[
23
]
.
On the other hand, for the former, compared to the case of completely observable, the essential difficulty of the partial observation problem is that the filtration
{\cal F}^{Y}
is not fixed
(depending on the control
u
) and the linear structure of the admissible control set is thereby corrupted. For this reason, it is hard to derive the optimality system by the variational method.

In general, there are three main approaches to address these difficulties. The first one is summarized as the
separation principle
. By applying the separation principle one can decouple the problems of optimal control and state estimation.
Note that this method needs certain extra constraints on the admissible control
u(\cdot)
, and the diffusion term of the state process does not include the control process
u(\cdot)
; for more details, see Wonham
[
19
]
, Davis
[
3
]
, and Snyder
et al.

[
13
]
.
Along this line, much research on LQ optimal control with partially observable systems has been studied in recent years, among which we would like to mention
the works of Huang
et\ al

[
7
]
, Huang
et\ al

[
8
]
, and Wang
et\ al

[
15
]
on backward stochastic control systems,
the works of Shi and Wu
[
12
]
and Wang
et\ al

[
17
]
on optimal control problems of forward-backward stochastic differential
equations (FBSDEs),
and the works of Wang and Yu
[
18
]
, Wu and Zhang
[
20
]
on differential games.
The second method is summarized as the
Girsanov transformation
. By applying Girsanov’s transformation, one could convert the partial observation problem into a partial information one.
Note that this approach requires higher integrability of the observation function, such as the boundedness of this function, which cannot be fulfilled in the LQ case in general.
For more details, see Bensoussan and Schuppen
[
1
]
, Wang
et\ al

[
16
]
,
Li and Tang
[
11
]
, and the references therein.
The third method is called the
orthogonal decomposition
, recently introduced
by Sun and Xiong
[
14
]
, where they solve an SLQ optimal control problem with partial observation but with the control
u(\cdot)
vanished in the diffusion term of the state process. To summarize, the relevant literature mentioned above has at least one of the following limitations: (i) Additional restrictions on the control
u(\cdot)
are required; (ii) Higher integrability of the state process
X(\cdot)
is necessary; (iii) The diffusion term of the state process does not depend on the control
u(\cdot)
.

In this paper, we consider a stochastic LQ control problem with partial observation, where the control
u(\cdot)
appears in the diffusion term of the state process. The aim is to find a
near optimal control in the weak formulation. The main contribution of this paper is to push the research boundary
of the partial observation stochastic LQ control problem to the case that the previous three methods are not directly
available. Firstly, it does not satisfy the separation principle. Secondly, since the drift term of the observation model is not bounded,
the Novikov
condition is not satisfied. Therefore, the Girsanov theorem is not directly applicable. Thirdly, the diffusion coefficient of the state equation is not constant so the orthogonal decomposition method is not available. We study the problem by restricting the control domain to a subset and verifying the condition for the Girsanov theorem using a conditional argument. Then, we prove that the Radon-Nikodyn derivative has an
order
1+{\epsilon}
moment. After that, because of the lacking of the second moment, a non-standard convex variation method is applied to obtain the stochastic maximum principle (SMP) for this restricted problem. Finally, a near optimal control is obtained from an approximate sequence.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section
2
formulates the control problem
under partial observation in the weak formulation. Section
3
is dedicated to breaking the circular dependence under the control restriction
by a conditional argument to verify the condition of the Girsanov theorem. In Section
4
, we establish higher-order moment estimates and
use a non-standard variation method to derive the optimal control for the restricted problem. Furthermore, we obtain a near optimal control for the original problem which is the main result of this article. In Section
5
, we conclude with some remarks.

2
Problem formulation

Let
\mathbb{S}^{n}
be the collection of the symmetric
n\times n
matrices,
\mathbb{I}_{d}
stand for the identity matrix of size
d\times d
, and
\mathfrak{1}_{\mathbb{A}}(x)
denote the indicator function of the set
\mathbb{A}
. For
M
and
N
in
\mathbb{S}^{n}
, we write
M\geqslant N
(resp.,
M>N
)
if
M-N
is positive semi-definite (resp., positive definite). Let
L^{2}([0,T]\times{\Omega})
be the space of adapted stochastic process satisfying the square integrable condition,
\mathscr{B}(C([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{d}))
denotes the Borel
{\sigma}
-algebra generated by the
\mathbb{R}^{d}
valued continuous functions. Let
W=\{W(t);t\geqslant 0\}
and
\widetilde{W}=\{(\widetilde{W}_{1}(t),...,\widetilde{W}_{d}(t))^{\top};t\geqslant 0\}
be two standard independent Brownian motions with values in
\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, respectively. The superscript
\top
denotes the transpose of a vector or a matrix, all vectors involved in this paper are column vectors. The notation
\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{P}}
represents the expectation under the probability
\mathbb{P}
, and
\mathbb{E}_{W}
represents the conditional expectation given the Brownian motion
W
.

We consider the following controlled linear stochastic differential equation (SDE) over a finite time horizon
s\in[0,T]
:

\left\{\begin{aligned} dX(s)=&\ [\left.A(s)X(s)+B(s)u(s)\right]ds+C(s)u(s)dW(s),\\
X(0)=&\ x,\end{aligned}\right.

(2.1)

where the initial state
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is fixed.
For the state equation (
2.1
), we consider the following linear SDE describing the observation process:

\left\{\begin{aligned} dY(s)=&\ H(s)X(s)ds+d\widetilde{W}(s),\quad s\in[0,T],\\
Y(0)=&\ 0.\end{aligned}\right.

(2.2)

Throughout this article, we impose the following assumptions for the coefficients of equations (
2.1
)-(
2.2
).

(A1)

The following deterministic functions

\displaystyle A:[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{n\times n},\quad~{B:[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{n\times d},}\quad~C:[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{n\times d},\quad~H:[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{d\times n}

are Lebesgue measurable and bounded on
[0,T]
.

Note that the observation process
Y(\cdot)
is a square-integrable
\mathbb{F}
-semimartingale, where

\mathbb{F}=\{{\cal F}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
is the filtration generated by
W(\cdot)
and
\widetilde{W}(\cdot)
.
Meanwhile, let
\mathbb{F}^{Y}=\{{\cal F}^{Y}_{s};0\leqslant s\leqslant T\}
be the usual augmentation of the filtration generated by
Y(\cdot)
. Clearly, the filtration
\mathbb{F}^{Y}
depends on the choice of the control
u(\cdot)
. Additionally, we say that a control
u(\cdot)
is admissible if it is
\mathbb{F}^{Y}
-adapted and square-integrable. In other words, we arrive at the following admissible set:

{\cal U}_{ad}=\Big\{u(\cdot):\Omega\times[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{d}\bigm|u(s)\in{\cal F}^{Y}_{s}\ \ \hbox{for all }s\in[0,T]\ \text{and}\ \mathbb{E}\int^{T}_{0}|u(s)|^{2}ds<\infty\Big\}.

Define the cost functional as follows

J\big(u(\cdot)\big)=\mathbb{E}\bigg\{\int^{T}_{0}\big[\langle G(t)X(t),X(t)\rangle+\langle R(t)u(t),u(t)\rangle\big]dt+\langle G_{1}X(T),X(T)\rangle\bigg\},

(2.3)

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is the Frobenius inner product. Throughout this article, we make the following assumptions regarding the coefficients of the cost functional (
2.3
).

(A2)
The following deterministic functions

G(\cdot):[0,T]\to\mathbb{S}^{n},\quad~\;R(\cdot):[0,T]\to\mathbb{S}^{d}

are Lebesgue measurable and bounded on
[0,T]
with

G(\cdot)\geqslant 0,\quad~\;R(\cdot)\geqslant\delta\mathbb{I}_{d},\quad~\;a.s.\ \ t\in[0,T],

Moreover,
G_{1}\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
and
G_{1}\geqslant 0
.

The aim of this article is to find controls
\tilde{u}(\cdot)\in{\cal U}_{ad}
under the weak formulation to minimize the cost functional (
2.3
) in the sense of near optimal. The control problem under weak formulation is stated in the book of Yong and Zhou
[
24
, Chapter 2]
. Next, we state the definition of weak formulation for stochastic LQ control problem with partial observation as follows.

Definition 2.1
.

A 7-tuple
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P},\mathbb{F},W(\cdot),\widetilde{W}(\cdot),u(\cdot))
is called a weak admissible control, and
(X(\cdot),Y(\cdot),u(\cdot))
a weak admissible triple, if

(a)
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P},\mathbb{F})
is a filtered probability space satisfying the usual
conditions,
W=\{W(t);t\geqslant 0\}\in\mathbb{R}
and
\widetilde{W}=\{\widetilde{W}(t)=(\widetilde{W}_{1}(t),...,\widetilde{W}_{d}(t))^{\top};t\geqslant 0\}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
are standard independent Brownian motions defined on it, and
\mathbb{F}=\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\geqslant 0}
is the natural filtration
generated by
W(\cdot)
and
\widetilde{W}(\cdot)
, augmented by all the
\mathbb{P}
-null sets.

(b)
u(\cdot)\in\mathcal{U}_{ad}
, and
X(\cdot)
(resp.
Y(\cdot)
) is the unique weak solution of Eq. (
2.1
) (resp. Eq. (
2.2
) ) on
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P},\mathbb{F})
under the control
u(\cdot)
.

(c) The cost functional
J(u(\cdot))
is defined on the filtered probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P},\mathbb{F})

associated with the 7-tuple.

The set of all weak-admissible controls is denoted by
\mathcal{U}_{ad}^{w}
. Sometimes, we might write
u(\cdot)\in\mathcal{U}_{ad}^{w}
instead of
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P},\mathbb{F},W(\cdot),\widetilde{W}(\cdot),u(\cdot))\in\mathcal{U}_{ad}^{w}
when it is clear from the context that the weak formulation is under consideration. The LQ near optimal control problem with partial observation under the weak formulation can be stated as follows.

Problem (SLQ)
. For any
\epsilon\in(0,1)
, find a
control
\tilde{u}(\cdot)\in\mathcal{U}^{w}_{ad}
such that the following holds

J(\tilde{u}(\cdot))\leqslant\inf_{u(\cdot)\in\mathcal{U}^{w}_{ad}}J(u(\cdot))+\epsilon,

(2.4)

If such
\tilde{u}(\cdot)
exists, then it is called a near optimal control, and the corresponding

X(\cdot)
to (
2.1
) is called the near optimal trajectory.

3
Breaking the circular dependence

In this section, we fix a complete filtered probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P},\mathbb{F})
on which two standard independent Brownian motions,
W(\cdot)
and
\widetilde{W}(\cdot)
are defined. Here,
\mathbb{F}=\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\geqslant 0}
is the natural filtration
generated by
W(\cdot)
and
\widetilde{W}(\cdot)
, augmented by all the
\mathbb{P}
-null sets.
Then, we break the circular dependence by applying the Girsanov theorem under the control
restriction using a conditional argument. For this
purpose, we need to do some preparations.

First, we restrict the control domain to a subset of
\mathcal{U}_{ad}
that
the integration of the square of the control process with respect to the time variable is bounded
by a constant. We denote such set of controls by
{\cal U}^{b}_{ad}
, i.e.,

{\cal U}_{ad}^{b}=\Big\{u(\cdot):\Omega\times[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{d}\bigm|u(s)\in{\cal F}^{Y}_{s},\ \ \forall\ s\in[0,T]\ \text{and}\ \int^{T}_{0}|u(s)|^{2}ds\leqslant K^{2}_{c}\Big\},

(3.1)

where
K_{c}
is a fixed positive constant.

For controls from
{\cal U}_{ad}^{b}
, we first prove that the state process
X(\cdot)
is bounded by a random variable that depends only on
the Brownian motion
W(\cdot)
.
Then, we verify the conditional Novikov condition with
W
given.
This then validates the use of Girsanov’s theorem. To do this, we set
{\zeta}(\cdot)
to
be the solution of the following ordinary differential equation (ODE, for short):

\left\{\begin{aligned} d{\zeta}(t)=&\ -{\zeta}(t)A(t)dt,\quad~t\in[0,T],\\
{\zeta}(0)=&\ \mathbb{I}_{n}.\end{aligned}\right.

(3.2)

Then, by applying Itô’s formula, one can show that

d\big({\zeta}(t)X(t)\big)={\zeta}(t)B(t)u(t)dt+{\zeta}(t)C(t)u(t)dW(t),\quad~t\in[0,T],

which implies that

X(t)={\zeta}(t)^{-1}x+{\zeta}(t)^{-1}\int^{t}_{0}{\zeta}(s)B(s)u(s)ds+{\zeta}(t)^{-1}\int^{t}_{0}{\zeta}(s)C(s)u(s)dW(s).

(3.3)

Next, for the sake of simplicity in presentation, we define

M(t)\triangleq\int^{t}_{0}{\zeta}(s)C(s)u(s)dW(s),\quad~t\in[0,T].

Additionally, for the entire given path of
\{\widetilde{W}(t);\ 0\leqslant t\leqslant T\}
, we
denote
\mathbb{P}_{\widetilde{W}}
as its corresponding conditional probability.

Lemma 3.1
.

Given the whole path of
\{\widetilde{W}(t);\ 0\leqslant t\leqslant T\}
, the process

M(\cdot)
is a
\mathbb{P}_{\widetilde{W}}
-martingale with quadratic variation process

\left<M\right>(t)=\int^{t}_{0}|{\zeta}(s)C(s)u(s)|^{2}ds\equiv{\sigma}(t),\quad~t\in[0,T].

Proof.

For any
\mathbb{A}\in{\cal F}_{s}
and
\mathbb{B}\in\mathscr{B}(C([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{d}))
, we have

\displaystyle\ \mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{P}}\bigg\{\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{P}_{\widetilde{W}}}\Big[\int^{t}_{s}{\zeta}(r)C(r)u(r)dW(r)\mathfrak{1}_{\mathbb{A}}\Big]\mathfrak{1}_{\widetilde{W}\in\mathbb{B}}\bigg\}

\displaystyle=\ \mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{P}}\bigg\{\int^{t}_{s}{\zeta}(r)C(r)u(r)dW(r)\mathfrak{1}_{\mathbb{A}}\mathfrak{1}_{\widetilde{W}\in\mathbb{B}}\bigg\}

\displaystyle=\ \mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{P}}\bigg\{\int^{t}_{s}{\zeta}(r)C(r)u(r)\mathfrak{1}_{\mathbb{A}}\mathfrak{1}_{\widetilde{W}\in\mathbb{B}}dW(r)\bigg\}=\ 0,

which implies that the process
M(\cdot)
is a
\mathbb{P}_{\widetilde{W}}
-martingale. Moreover, it is trivial to verify the identity of the quadratic variation.
∎

Based on the above result, we can now prove that the norm of the
state process
X(\cdot)
has an upper bound, which plays an important role in the following
3.3
.

Lemma 3.2
.

There exists a random variable
K(W)
, which is independent of
\widetilde{W}
such that for any
u(\cdot)\in{\cal U}^{b}_{ad}
,

\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant T}|X(t)|\leqslant K(W).

Proof.

It is clear that there exists a constant
K
such that the first two terms of (
3.3
) are bounded by it. We claim that the third term of (
3.3
) can be bounded by a random variable
\tilde{K}(W)
, which is independent of the Brownian motion
\widetilde{W}
. Since
u(\cdot)\in{\cal U}^{b}_{ad}
,

M(\cdot)
is not independent of
\widetilde{W}
. By Lemma
3.1
,
M(\cdot)
is a
\mathbb{P}_{\widetilde{W}}
-martingale.

Let
\widetilde{M}(t)=M({\sigma}^{-1}(t))
. Then,
\widetilde{M}(\cdot)
is a
\mathbb{P}_{\widetilde{W}}
-martingale with quadratic variation process
\langle\widetilde{M}\rangle(t)=t
. Hence,
\widetilde{M}(\cdot)
is a
\mathbb{P}_{\widetilde{W}}
-Brownian motion. Namely, for any path of
\widetilde{W}(\cdot)
, the distribution of
\widetilde{M}(\cdot)
under
\mathbb{P}_{\widetilde{W}}
is not changed, which implies that
\widetilde{M}(\cdot)
is independent of
{\widetilde{W}}(\cdot)
. Note that

M(t)=\widetilde{M}({\sigma}(t))
, and

{\sigma}(t)\leqslant cK_{c}^{2}\|{\zeta}\|^{2}\equiv{\sigma}^{0},

where
\|{\zeta}\|=\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant T}{\zeta}(t)
,
c>0
is a constant, so
\sigma^{0}
is a positive constant. Further, we have

|M(t)|\leqslant\sup_{0\leqslant s\leqslant{\sigma}^{0}}|\widetilde{M}(s)|.

Note that
\sup_{0\leqslant s\leqslant{\sigma}^{0}}|\widetilde{M}(s)|
is a random variable depending on
W
and
\widetilde{W}
.
Let

\tilde{K}(W,\widetilde{W})\triangleq\sup_{0\leqslant s\leqslant{\sigma}^{0}}|\widetilde{M}(s)|,

and

g(\widetilde{W})\triangleq\mathbb{E}_{\widetilde{W}}\Big(\tilde{K}(W,\widetilde{W})h(W)\Big),

where
h
is a bounded functional.
Then for any bounded functional
f(\cdot)
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[g(\widetilde{W})f(\widetilde{W})\big]=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\mathbb{E}_{\widetilde{W}}\Big(\tilde{K}(W,\widetilde{W})h({W})\Big)f(\widetilde{W})\big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\tilde{K}(W,\widetilde{W})h(W)f(\widetilde{W})\big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\tilde{K}(W,\widetilde{W})h(W)]\mathbb{E}\big[f(\widetilde{W})\big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[g(\widetilde{W})\big]\mathbb{E}\big[f(\widetilde{W})\big].

Namely,
g(\widetilde{W})
is independent of
\widetilde{W}
, which further implies that
g(\widetilde{W})
is a constant. Therefore,
\tilde{K}(W,\widetilde{W})
does not depend on
\widetilde{W}
. Combining the above, we obtain the desired conclusion.
∎

Based on the above results, we define

\displaystyle N(t)=

\displaystyle\exp\bigg\{-\int^{t}_{0}\Big(H(s)X(s)\Big)^{\top}d\widetilde{W}(s)-\frac{1}{2}\int^{t}_{0}|H(s)X(s)|^{2}ds\bigg\},\quad~t\in[0,T],

which, using Itô’s formula, can be expressed as follows:

N(t)=1-\int_{0}^{t}N(s)\Big(H(s)X(s)\Big)^{\top}d\widetilde{W}(s),\quad~t\in[0,T].

Therefore,
N(\cdot)
is a one-dimensional continuous local martingale.

Proposition 3.3
.

N(\cdot)
is a martingale under the probability
\mathbb{P}
.

Proof.

We only need to prove that
\mathbb{E}N(t)=1
.
From
3.2
, we have

\mathbb{E}_{W}\bigg\{\exp\Big(\frac{1}{2}\int^{T}_{0}|X(s)|^{2}ds\Big)\bigg\}\leqslant\mathbb{E}_{W}\bigg\{\exp\Big(\frac{1}{2}K^{2}(W)T\Big)\bigg\}=\exp\Big(\frac{1}{2}K^{2}(W)T\Big)<\infty,\quad a.s..

Applying the Girsanov theorem with the conditional Novikov condition satisfied, we get
\mathbb{E}_{W}N(t)=1
. Therefore,

\mathbb{E}N(t)=\mathbb{E}\Big\{\mathbb{E}_{W}\{N(t)\}\Big\}=1,\quad~t\in[0,T].

This completes the proof.
∎

Now, based on the preparations above, we can break the circular dependence between the filtration
\mathbb{F}^{Y}
and the control
u(\cdot)\in{\cal U}^{b}_{ad}
.
As noted from
3.3
, for any control
u(\cdot)\in{\cal U}^{b}_{ad}
, we can apply Girsanov’s theorem to transform the observation process
Y(\cdot)
into a standard Brownian motion under a new probability measure
\mathbb{Q}
, which is defined by

\frac{d\mathbb{Q}}{d\mathbb{P}}\bigg|_{{\cal F}_{t}}=N(t),\quad~t\in[0,T].

Then, the pair
\big(Y(\cdot)^{\top},W(\cdot)\big)^{\top}
is a
(d+1)
-dimensional Brownian motion under the probability measure
\mathbb{Q}
.

Let
Z(\cdot)=N^{-1}(\cdot)
. It is then easy to verify that
Z(\cdot)
satisfies

\displaystyle dZ(t)=

\displaystyle Z(t)\Big\{(H(t)X(t))^{\top}d\widetilde{W}(t)+|H(t)X(t)|^{2}dt\Big\},\quad~t\in[0,T].

Namely,

\displaystyle dZ(t)

\displaystyle=Z(t)\langle H(t)X(t),dY(t)\rangle,\quad~t\in[0,T].

(3.4)

Now, the circular dependence is broken. Therefore, our next primary objective is to establish SMP over the control set
{\cal U}^{b}_{ad}
under the weak formulation.
Note that in this special case,
the state process becomes a
(n+1)
-dimensional process
\big(X(\cdot),Z(\cdot)\big)
. Moreover, the system is described by the following SDEs:

\left\{\begin{aligned} dX(t)=&\big(A(t)X(t)+B(t)u(t)\big)dt+C(t)u(t)dW(t),\\
dZ(t)=&Z(t)\big(H(t)X(t)\big)^{\top}dY(t),\quad~t\in[0,T],\\
X(0)=&x,\;Z(0)=1.\end{aligned}\right.

(3.5)

The related cost functional is given by

J(u(\cdot))=\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\bigg\{\int^{T}_{0}\big(\langle G(t)X(t),X(t)\rangle+\langle R(t)u(t),u(t)\rangle\big)Z(t)dt+\langle G_{1}X(T),X(T)\rangle Z(T)\bigg\}.

(3.6)

We label the above problem as
Problem (O).

Problem (O)
. Find a control
u^{*}(\cdot)\in{\cal U}^{b}_{ad}
such that

J(u^{*}(\cdot))=\inf_{u\in{\cal U}^{b}_{ad}}J(u(\cdot)).

4
Stochastic maximum principle

In this section, we first deduce the SMP using the convex variation technique for
Problem (O). Then, we prove the existence of an optimal control
u^{*}(\cdot)\in\mathcal{U}^{b}_{\text{ad}}
that minimizes the cost functional

(\ref{eq1010a})
in the weak formulation. Finally, we derive the near optimal controls for the original problem.

Recall that
Z(\cdot)
is defined by (
3.4
). It is clear that
Z(\cdot)
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-martingale.
Although
we have
\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}[Z(\cdot)]<\infty
, it is not enough for the well-posedness of the cost functional (
3.6
). Actually, we need its moments of order higher than 1 which is obtained in the following
lemma.

Lemma 4.1
.

There exists a constant
k\in(0,1)
such that

\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}[Z^{1+k}(t)]<\infty
.

Proof.

From (
3.4
) and (
3.5
), we have

\displaystyle Z(t)=

5
Conclusion

In this paper, we studied the near optimal controls of LQ problem with partial observation in the weak formulation.
Our work is an extension of Sun and Xiong
[
14
]
. As they stated in the conclusion of their article, their approach works
only for a specific structure of the state equation. Namely, the diffusion of the state equation does not involve the state
and the control. In contrast, in our model, the control is contained in the diffusion term, and our approach is entirely
different from theirs. More precisely, we have used a conditional argument to make the Girsanov transformation applicable to our
bounded setting. We also obtained a moment of order higher than 1 for the auxiliary process resulted from the Girsanov
transformation by using Fernique’s theorem. The methods are still valid for the case where the expectation of the state process appears in the diffusion term of the state equation. Nevertheless, we have only obtained the near optimal controls so far, and our method does not apply to the case of state-dependent diffusion terms. We hope to report some relevant results about
that case in our future publications.

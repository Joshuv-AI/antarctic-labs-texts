Semi-analytical investigation of nonlinear time-fractional fluid behavior with -Caputo memory
Authors: M Mossa Al-sawalha, Saima Noor, Humaira Yasmin, Jeevan Kafle
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis paper examines two nonlinear time-fractional Navier-Stokes (NS) systems, in the context of the -Caputo fractional derivative that offers a generalized and versatile form of the memory and hereditary influence in fluid motion. In order to achieve the semi-analytical approximate solutions, two semi-analytical methods are designed and constructed, including the q-Homotopy ZZ Transform Method (q-HZZTM) and the ZZ Transform Variational Iteration Method (ZZ-VIM). The proposed strategies take the benefits of the ZZ transform, homotopy and variational iteration structures to deal with strong nonlinearities. Numerical tables are used to check and validate the accuracy and convergence of the obtained solutions against the exact solutions for various fractional orders. Moreover, the graphical illustrations are provided to examine the response behavior and the effects of the fractional-order parameter. It is found that both q-HZZTM and ZZ-VIM converge fast and are quite consistent with the exact solutions in the classical case and are stable in the case of fractional-order. The suggested framework is highly efficient, accurate, and flexible, and it is a potent analysis of nonlinear fractional fluid models emerging in applied science and engineering.Keywords: Fractional-order system of Navier-Stokes equations, ZZ transform variational iteration method, Q-homotopy ZZ transform method, -Caputo fractional derivative, Nonlinear fractional wave dynamics, Comparison of fractional semi-analytical solversSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing, Physics

Introduction

Fluid dynamics modeling with memory and hereditary processes is an important problem in applied mathematics and engineering, especially when it involves complex fluids, porous media, and turbulent flows1. The classical Navier-Stokes equations as well as integer-order partial differential equations frequently suffer from being unable to represent non-local time and space dependencies of such systems2. To address these limitations, it has been found that a strong mathematical framework, fractional calculus, can be used to include derivatives and integrals of non-integer order, inherently modeling memory, genetic properties, and anomalous diffusion3,4. Fractional operators applied to fluid dynamics have given rise to the fractional Navier-Stokes equations, which have also offered a better description of the velocity and pressure field in complicated fluids5–11.

The Caputo definition is a popular fractional derivative because it is compatible with common initial conditions across initial value problems12. Recent extensions, including the use of the -Hilfer and the use of the -Caputo fractional derivative, have further expanded this toolbox by making the operator more flexible to allow a user to customize the operator to a particular physical memory kernel13,14. The nonlinear fractional partial differential equations (FPDEs) are difficult to solve analytically and various semi-analytical and numerical methods are developed15. The Adomian decomposition method16, the homotopy analysis method17, the variational iteration method18 and their numerous combinations with integral transforms such as the Laplace and Sumudu transforms are popular methods19,20.

The study of new integral transforms with iterative methods for nonlinear fractional fluid equations is a current research frontier despite these advances. The ZZ transform is a relatively new member of the family of integral transforms, and is demonstrating encouraging performance in both linear and nonlinear differential equations21,22. Its implementation into the context of generalized fractional derivatives such as the -Caputo operator, and its combination with powerful perturbation and correctional algorithms, is however, a field of least exploration23. In addition, there is limited literature on comparative studies evaluating the performance of various ZZ-transform-based methodologies of analyzing complex, coupled systems24–29.

Fractional calculus is based on the extension of differentiation and integration to non-integers order. Riemann-Liouville and Grunwald-Letnikov formulations were also some of the earliest rigorous formulations30,31. Later, the Caputo derivative came into use and became standard in physical modeling because it permits the application of classical initial conditions37. Recent developments aim at generalizing these operators in regard to other functions32–36. The principle of fractional derivatives in respect to the second function, including the -Caputo derivative was formalized to make more modeling freedom available38,39. This enables the fractional operator to be modified to various kernel structures hence making it very applicable to the problem that have certain memory effects40. The Caputo-Hadamard derivative is a case with a logarithmic kernel function41. A number of properties including the composition rules and Laplace transforms of these generalized operators have been developed42,43.

The process of solving FPDEs requires powerful methods, series solutions are commonly obtained by analytical means. Homotopy perturbation method (HPM) breaks down a nonlinear problem into an infinity series44. Variational iteration method (VIM) establishes a correction functional made of Lagrange multiplier45. Adomian decomposition method (ADM) is used to break up the solution and nonlinear terms into series terms46. These techniques are often used together with integral transforms to achieve better convergence and to be able to deal with initial conditions. One such method is the Laplace transform homotopy perturbation technique47. In like manner, the Sumudu transform has been paired with decomposition techniques48. Yildirim et al49. introduced a JSN transform with a fractional residual power series method, while Saratha et al50,51. developed and extended a fractional homotopy framework to nonlinear and epidemic models. In the case of numerical methods, finite difference methods are used, such as the L1-algorithm of Caputo derivatives52, spectral methods and fractional linear multistep methods53,54.

Fractional calculus has been applied in fluid mechanics to use more realistic models. The time-fractional Navier-Stokes equation, which substitutes the first-order time derivative with the fractional time derivative, is an effective model of fluid flow55. It has studied the existence and uniqueness of solutions to such equations56. Its use is in modeling the flow of viscoelastic fluids57 to the simulation of anomalous diffusion in porous media58. There are several ways of solving these equations, some of these are the finite element method59, the homotopy analysis method60 and the orthogonal polynomials method of operation matrices61.

The ZZ transform is a newer integral transform used in the solution of differential equations that is a possible potent tool62. Its definition, as well as its basic properties, such as transforms of derivatives and elementary functions, have been characterized63. Ordinary differential equations and integral equations and a few class of FPDEs have been solved using the transform based methods64,65. Among the most important advances was the derivation of the ZZ transform formula to the Caputo fractional derivative, which made it possible to apply it directly to time-fractional problems66.

The combination of integral transforms with iterative algorithms forms a strong group of hybrid algorithms. Laplace-Adomian decomposition is a traditional hybrid technique67. The homotopy perturbation transform method is a combination of the Laplace transform and HPM68. The q-homotopy analysis transform method (q-HATM) is a conversion control method that introduces an auxiliary parameter into a Laplace transform framework69. Other transforms have been studied as hybrids in recent innovations, including the Shehu transform variational iteration method70 and the Mahgoub transform homotopy perturbation method71. But the introduction of the ZZ transform into such hybrid schemes, especially generalized fractional derivatives, and coupled nonlinear systems is a new field with little previous history, which is the focus of the current work.

According to the above discussion, the present study offers a new hybrid theory of analysis that is a clear improvement of the existing literature on fractional nonlinear systems. Specifically, we develop two new schemes the q-homotopy ZZ transform method (q-HZZTM) and the ZZ transform-based variational iteration method (ZZ-VIM), that are developed in the generalized setting of the -Caputo fractional derivative. As opposed to other traditional hybrid approaches where only standard transforms with classical iteration approaches is introduced, the proposed methodology implies the use of the ZZ transform as a unifying transformational tool, enabling the control of memory-dependent fractional operators without changing the inner-structure of nonlinear terms. Moreover, the q-homotopy mechanism integration can also use an additional convergence-control parameter, and this aspect seems to make the solution process significantly more flexible and stable, which is not adequately taken into account in the existing methods that operate with transforms. The -Caputo derivative admits a broader variety of kernel functions thereby providing the model the opportunity to be extended to a broader scope as compared to classical Caputo formulations. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study to unify the ZZ transform with q-homotopy and variational iteration methods under the -Caputo operator for solving nonlinear time-fractional partial differential equations. The effectiveness and novelty of the proposed schemes are demonstrated by the fact that it is applied to two nonlinear Navier-Stokes equations where superior convergence, reduced order of computation and higher accuracy are achieved relative to the existing semi-analytical schemes.

Influence of fractional order on solution behavior

Figures 1(a), 3(a), 5(a), and 7(a) show the effects of the fractional order on the solution profiles and at various values of . It is noted that as the fractional order is reduced, both the magnitude of the solutions and the temporal evolution of the flow field slow down clearly.

Physically, the strength of memory effects of the fluid system is determined by the fractional-order parameter . In contrast to classical integer-order models, in which the behaviour of the fluids is defined only by the current state, the -caputo fractional derivative provides a history-dependent process that takes into consideration the cumulative effect of the past states. These memory effects increase with a smaller value of , leading to increased damping and less amplitude of velocity and slower momentum transport. This action may be explained by the greater resistance of the fluid, which slows down the spread of disturbances.

The higher the of the system the lesser the contribution of historical conditions, and systematically the system returns to the classical behavior of NS systems. The dynamics of the fluid in this regime are faster, less damped, and responsive to external forces. Hence, the fractional order is a control parameter between nonlocal, memory-dominated fluid dynamics and the classical local flow behaviour, which is more flexible and realistic in modelling non-local complex fluids with hereditary properties.

Future research directions

The suggested q-HZZTM and ZZ-VIM models can be generalized to a great variety of sophisticated engineering and physics problems. Specifically, their application to nonlinear heat transfer models, reaction-diffusion equations, and pattern formation phenomena generated by fluid dynamics and biological systems can be the topic of future research. Furthermore, the approach is extensible to multi-dimensional problems, variable-order fractional models as well as coupled physics systems. The integration of numerical optimization schemes to choose the convergence-control parameter, the construction of error bars and the analysis of convergence is also a succession of encouraging lines of improvement of the capabilities and applicability of the proposed methods.

Conclusion

This paper developed an analytical and numerical exploration of two nonlinear time-fractional NS systems of equations in the context of the -Caputo fractional derivative. The q-HZZTM and the ZZ-VIM were both developed as efficient semi-analytical methods to achieve the accuracy of series solutions without the need to linearize and discretize the problem. The effectiveness and validity of the suggested techniques were validated by elaborate numerical comparisons of the techniques with the exact solutions as presented in tabular and graphical representations of the behaviors of solutions in two and three dimensions. It was shown that the approximate and the exact solution agreed very well in the classical limit and that the exact solution converged very fast with little computing power. One of the notable effects of this work is that the role of the parameter of the fractional-order is evident in the system dynamics. It was demonstrated that lower fractional orders brought about significant memory and hereditary effects, which led to slower temporal feedbacks and reduced solution responses, with the traditional behavior being gradually restored as the fractional order . This shows the ability of the fractional framework to represent the complicated fluid behaviors that are not modeled by integer-order models.

The suggested q-HZZTM and ZZ-VIM are powerful, precise and versatile in the solution of nonlinear fractional partial differential equations. The proposed methods can easily be generalized to more complicated fractional fluid models and other nonlinear problems that occur in physics and engineering, providing a strong basis to future developments.

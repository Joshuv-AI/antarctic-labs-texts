Certain Upper Bounds on the A \displaystyle A -Numerical Radius of Operators in Semi-Hilbertian Spaces and Their Applications
M.H.M. Rashid

Abstract
Consider a complex Hilbert space ( ℋ , ⟨ ⋅ , ⋅ ⟩ ) \displaystyle\left(\mathcal{H},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle\right) equipped with a positive bounded linear operator A \displaystyle A on ℋ \displaystyle\mathcal{H} . This induces a semi-norm ∥ ⋅ ∥ A \displaystyle\|\cdot\|_{A} through the semi-inner product ⟨ x , y ⟩ A = ⟨ A ​ x , y ⟩ \displaystyle\langle x,y\rangle_{A}=\langle Ax,y\rangle for x , y ∈ ℋ \displaystyle x,y\in\mathcal{H} . In this semi-Hilbertian space setting, we investigate the A \displaystyle A -numerical radius w A ​ ( T ) \displaystyle w_{A}(T) and A \displaystyle A -operator semi-norm ‖ T ‖ A \displaystyle\|T\|_{A} of bounded operators T \displaystyle T . This paper presents significant improvements to existing upper bounds for the A \displaystyle A -numerical radius of operators in semi-Hilbertian spaces. We establish several new inequalities that provide sharper estimates than those currently available in the literature. Notably, we refine the triangle inequality for the A \displaystyle A -operator semi-norm, offering more precise characterizations of operator behavior. To validate our theoretical advancements, we provide concrete examples that demonstrate the effectiveness of our results. These examples illustrate how our bounds offer tighter estimates compared to previous ones, confirming the practical relevance of our findings. Our work contributes to the broader understanding of numerical radius inequalities and their applications in operator theory, with potential implications for functional analysis and related fields.

Introduction

Let
\displaystyle(\mathcal{H},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
be a nontrivial complex Hilbert space. We denote by
\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
the
\displaystyle C^{*}
-algebra of all bounded linear operators on
\displaystyle\mathcal{H}
, and by
\displaystyle I
the identity operator on
\displaystyle\mathcal{H}
. For any linear subspace
\displaystyle M
of
\displaystyle\mathcal{H}
, its closure in the norm topology is denoted by
\displaystyle\overline{M}
. Given an operator
\displaystyle T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
, we use the following notation:
\displaystyle\operatorname{ran}(T)
for the range of
\displaystyle T
,
\displaystyle\ker(T)
for the null space of
\displaystyle T
and
\displaystyle T^{*}
for the adjoint of
\displaystyle T

An operator
\displaystyle A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
is called
positive
if
\displaystyle\langle Ax,x\rangle\geq 0
for all
\displaystyle x\in\mathcal{H}
, denoted by
\displaystyle A\geq 0
. Every positive operator
\displaystyle A
induces a positive semidefinite sesquilinear form
\displaystyle\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{A}:\mathcal{H}\times\mathcal{H}\to\mathbb{C}
defined by:

\langle x,y\rangle_{A}=\langle Ax,y\rangle\quad\text{for all }x,y\in\mathcal{H}

This form induces a semi-norm
\displaystyle\|\cdot\|_{A}
given by:

\|x\|_{A}=\sqrt{\langle x,x\rangle_{A}}=\|A^{1/2}x\|

Note that
\displaystyle|x|_{A}=0
if and only if
\displaystyle x\in\ker(A)
. Consequently,
\displaystyle|\cdot|_{A}
is a norm if and only if
\displaystyle A
is injective, and
\displaystyle(\mathcal{H},|\cdot|_{A})
is complete if and only if
\displaystyle\operatorname{ran}(A)
is closed in
\displaystyle\mathcal{H}
.

An operator
\displaystyle S\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
is called an
\displaystyle A
-adjoint
of
\displaystyle T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
if:

\langle Tx,y\rangle_{A}=\langle x,Sy\rangle_{A}\quad\text{for all }x,y\in\mathcal{H}

Equivalently, the existence of an
\displaystyle A
-adjoint for
\displaystyle T
corresponds to the solvability of the operator equation
\displaystyle AX=T^{*}A
. By Douglas’ theorem
Douglas 1966
, this equation has a bounded solution if and only if
\displaystyle\operatorname{ran}(T^{*}A)\subseteq\operatorname{ran}(A)
.

We define the set of all operators admitting
\displaystyle A
-adjoints as:

\mathcal{B}_{A}(\mathcal{H})=\{T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}):\operatorname{ran}(T^{*}A)\subseteq\operatorname{ran}(A)\}

For
\displaystyle T\in\mathcal{B}_{A}(\mathcal{H})
, the unique solution to
\displaystyle AX=T^{A}
is denoted by
\displaystyle T^{\sharp_{A}}
. Important properties include:
\displaystyle T^{\sharp_{A}}=A^{{\dagger}}T^{A}
, where
\displaystyle A^{{\dagger}}
is the Moore-Penrose inverse of
\displaystyle A
;
\displaystyle\operatorname{ran}(T^{\sharp_{A}})\subseteq\operatorname{ran}(A)
;
\displaystyle\ker(T^{\sharp_{A}})\subseteq\ker(T^{*}A)
;
\displaystyle T^{\sharp_{A}}\in\mathcal{B}_{A}(\mathcal{H})
; and
\displaystyle(T^{\sharp_{A}})^{\sharp_{A}}=P_{A}TP_{A}
, where
\displaystyle P_{A}
is the orthogonal projection onto
\displaystyle\overline{\operatorname{ran}(A)}
.

Similarly, the set of operators admitting
\displaystyle A^{1/2}
-adjoints is:

\mathcal{B}_{A^{1/2}}(\mathcal{H})=\{T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}):\|Tx\|_{A}\leq\lambda\|x\|_{A}\text{ for all }x\in\mathcal{H}\}

The semi-inner product
\displaystyle\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{A}
induces a semi-norm on
\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{A^{1/2}}(\mathcal{H})
:

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|T\|_{A}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\overline{\operatorname{ran}(A)}\\
x\neq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{\|Tx\|_{A}}{\|x\|_{A}}=\sup\{\|Tx\|_{A}:x\in\mathcal{H},\|x\|_{A}=1\}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sup\{|\langle Tx,y\rangle_{A}|:x,y\in\mathcal{H},\|x\|_{A}=\|y\|_{A}=1\}<\infty

Note that
\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{A}(\mathcal{H})
and
\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{A^{1/2}}(\mathcal{H})
are two subalgebras of
\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
and satisfy
\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{A}(\mathcal{H})\subseteq\mathcal{B}_{A^{1/2}}(\mathcal{H})
, see
Arias et al. 2008a
;
Arias et al. 2008b
. And by the definition of
\displaystyle\|T\|_{A}
, we can obtain

\|T\|_{A}=\|T^{\sharp_{A}}\|_{A},\quad\|T^{\sharp_{A}}T\|_{A}=\|TT^{\sharp_{A}}\|_{A}=\|T^{\sharp_{A}}\|_{A}^{2}=\|T\|_{A}^{2}.

Moreover, if
\displaystyle T,S\in\mathcal{B}_{A}(\mathcal{H})
, then
\displaystyle(TS)^{\sharp_{A}}=S^{\sharp_{A}}T^{\sharp_{A}}
,
\displaystyle\|Tx\|_{A}\leq\|T\|_{A}\|x\|_{A}
for any
\displaystyle x\in\mathcal{H}
, and
\displaystyle\|TS\|_{A}\leq\|T\|_{A}\|S\|_{A}
. It also has
\displaystyle\|T+S\|_{A}\leq\|T\|_{A}+\|S\|_{A}
. In particular, an operator
\displaystyle T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
is
\displaystyle A
-selfadjoint if
\displaystyle AT
is selfadjoint, which ensures that
\displaystyle\|T\|_{A}=\sup\{|\langle Tx,x\rangle_{A}|:x\in\mathcal{H},\|x\|_{A}=1\}
, see
Feki 2020
. An operator
\displaystyle T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
is
\displaystyle A
-positive if
\displaystyle AT
is positive. Obviously, an
\displaystyle A
-positive operator is always an
\displaystyle A
-selfadjoint operator. And it should be noted that
\displaystyle T^{\sharp_{A}}T
and
\displaystyle TT^{\sharp_{A}}
are both
\displaystyle A
-positive.

For
\displaystyle A
-bounded operator,
\displaystyle A
-numerical range is defined in
Hamadi et al. 2018
as

W_{A}(T):=\{|\langle Tx,x\rangle_{A}|:x\in\mathcal{H},\|x\|_{A}=1\}.

Also, the
\displaystyle A
-numerical radius is defined by

w_{A}(T):=\sup\{|\lambda|:\lambda\in W_{A}(T)\}.

If
\displaystyle A=I
,
\displaystyle A
-numerical range and
\displaystyle A
-numerical radius will be the classical numerical range and the classical numerical radius. Notice that it may happen that
\displaystyle w_{A}(T)=\infty
for some
\displaystyle T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
, see
Bhunia and Nayak 2020
.

It is well known that if
\displaystyle T\in\mathcal{B}_{A}(\mathcal{H})
, then
\displaystyle w_{A}(T)
is a semi-norm which is equivalent to

\displaystyle{\left\|T\right\|}_{A}
, and it holds

\frac{1}{2}{\left\|T\right\|}_{A}\leq w_{A}(T)\leq{\left\|T\right\|}_{A}.

(1)

For
\displaystyle w_{A}(T)
, an important inequality is the power inequality (see
(
Moslehian et al. 2020
, Proposition 3.10)
), which asserts that

w_{A}(T^{n})\leq w_{A}^{n}(T),\,\,\mbox{for $\displaystyle n\in\mathbb{N}$}.

(2)

Recently, the right-side of the inequality (
1
) has been refined by many researchers. For example,
it has been shown by Zamani in
Zamani 2019
, if
\displaystyle T\in\mathcal{B}_{A}(\mathcal{H})
, then

w_{A}^{2}(T)\leq\frac{1}{2}{\left\|T^{\sharp_{A}}T+TT^{\sharp_{A}}\right\|}_{A}.

(3)

In
Saddi 2012
, Saddi showed that if
\displaystyle T\in\mathcal{B}_{A}(\mathcal{H})
, then

w_{A}^{2}(T)\leq\frac{1}{2}\left({\left\|T\right\|}_{A}^{2}+w_{A}(T^{2})\right).

(4)

Very recently, several refinements of the inequalities in (
1
) have been proved by many
researchers. For example, it was shown by the authors in
Feki 2021
;
Zamani 2019
that for
\displaystyle T\in\mathcal{B}_{A}(\mathcal{H})
,
then

\frac{1}{4}{\left\|T^{\sharp_{A}}T+TT^{\sharp_{A}}\right\|}_{A}\leq w_{A}^{2}(T)\leq\frac{1}{2}{\left\|T^{\sharp_{A}}T+TT^{\sharp_{A}}\right\|}_{A}.

(5)

In
Qiao et al. 2022
a refinement of the second inequality in (
5
) was given, which
asserts

w_{A}^{4}(T)\leq\frac{3}{16}{\left\|T^{\sharp_{A}}T+TT^{\sharp_{A}}\right\|}_{A}^{2}+\frac{1}{8}{\left\|T^{\sharp_{A}}T+TT^{\sharp_{A}}\right\|}_{A}w_{A}(T^{2}).

(6)

Moreover, a refinement of the second inequality in (
5
) was also proved in
Qiao et al. 2024
, which
asserts that if
\displaystyle T\in\mathcal{B}_{A}(\mathcal{H})
and
\displaystyle\beta\geq 0
, then

w_{A}^{4}(T)\leq\frac{1+2\beta}{16(1+\beta)}{\left\|T^{\sharp_{A}}T+TT^{\sharp_{A}}\right\|}_{A}^{2}+\frac{2\beta+3}{8(1+\beta)}{\left\|T^{\sharp_{A}}T+TT^{\sharp_{A}}\right\|}_{A}w_{A}(T^{2})

(7)

and

w_{A}^{4}(T)\leq\frac{1+2\beta}{8(1+\beta)}{\left\|T^{\sharp_{A}}T+TT^{\sharp_{A}}\right\|}_{A}^{2}+\frac{1}{2(1+\beta)}w_{A}^{2}(T^{2}).

(8)

The main objective of this work is to improve existing bounds for the
\displaystyle A
-numerical radius of operators in semi-Hilbertian spaces. To gain deeper insight into operator behavior in this setting, we establish novel and sharper inequalities. In particular, we refine the triangle inequality for the

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\displaystyle T\in\mathcal{B}_{A}(\mathcal{H})
,
\displaystyle\alpha\in[0,1]
and
\displaystyle\beta\geq 0
. Then

w_{A}^{4}(T)\leq\frac{\gamma_{1}}{16}{\left\|T^{\sharp_{A}}T+TT^{\sharp_{A}}\right\|}_{A}^{2}+\frac{\gamma_{2}}{8}{\left\|T^{\sharp_{A}}T+TT^{\sharp_{A}}\right\|}_{A}w_{A}(T^{2}),

(24)

where
\displaystyle\gamma_{1}=\frac{(2\beta+1)(1+\alpha^{2})+2\alpha}{1+\beta}
and
\displaystyle\gamma_{2}=\frac{\left(1-\alpha\right)\left[2+(1+\alpha)(1+2\beta)\right]}{1+\beta}
.

Proof.

Let
\displaystyle x\in\mathcal{H}
with
\displaystyle{\left\|x\right\|}_{A}=1
. Then

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left|{\left<Tx,x\right>}_{A}\right|^{4}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left|{\left<Tx,x\right>}_{A}{\left<x,T^{\sharp_{A}}x\right>}_{A}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{\gamma_{1}}{4}{\left\|Tx\right\|}_{A}^{2}{\left\|T^{\sharp_{A}}x\right\|}_{A}^{2}+\frac{\gamma_{2}}{4}{\left\|Tx\right\|}_{A}{\left\|T^{\sharp_{A}}x\right\|}_{A}\left|{\left<Tx,T^{\sharp_{A}}x\right>}_{A}\right|

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left(\mbox{by Lemma \ref{Lemma2.4}}\right)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{\gamma_{1}}{4}\left(\sqrt{{\left<T^{\sharp_{A}}Tx,x\right>}_{A}{\left<TT^{\sharp_{A}}x,x\right>}_{A}}\right)^{2}+\frac{\gamma_{2}}{4}\sqrt{{\left<T^{\sharp_{A}}Tx,x\right>}_{A}{\left<TT^{\sharp_{A}}x,x\right>}_{A}}\left|{\left<T^{2}x,x\right>}_{A}\right|

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{\gamma_{1}}{16}{\left<\left(T^{\sharp_{A}}T+TT^{\sharp_{A}}\right)x,x\right>}_{A}^{2}+\frac{\gamma_{2}}{8}{\left<\left(T^{\sharp_{A}}T+TT^{\sharp_{A}}\right)x,x\right>}_{A}\left|{\left<T^{2}x,x\right>}_{A}\right|

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left(\mbox{by the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality}\right)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{\gamma_{1}}{16}{\left\|T^{\sharp_{A}}T+TT^{\sharp_{A}}\right\|}_{A}^{2}+\frac{\gamma_{2}}{8}{\left\|T^{\sharp_{A}}T+TT^{\sharp_{A}}\right\|}_{A}w_{A}(T^{2}).

Taking the supremum over all vectors
\displaystyle x\in\mathcal{H}
with
\displaystyle{\left\|x\right\|}_{A}=1
in the above inequality, we deduce that

w_{A}^{4}(T)\leq\frac{\gamma_{1}}{16}{\left\|T^{\sharp_{A}}T+TT^{\sharp_{A}}\right\|}_{A}^{2}+\frac{\gamma_{2}}{8}{\left\|T^{\sharp_{A}}T+TT^{\sharp_{A}}\right\|}_{A}w_{A}(T^{2}).

This completes the proof.
∎

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left(\frac{\left(1-\alpha\right)\left[2+(1+\alpha)(1+2\beta)\right]}{8(1+\beta)}\right){\left\|\left(T^{\sharp_{A}}T\right)^{2r}+\left(S^{\sharp_{A}}S\right)^{2r}\right\|}_{A}w_{A}^{r}(S^{\sharp_{A}}ST^{\sharp_{A}}T).

This completes the proof.

Corollary 3.9
.

Let
\displaystyle T,S\in\mathcal{B}_{A}(\mathcal{H})
and
\displaystyle r\geq 1
. Then

w_{A}^{r}(S^{\sharp_{A}}ST^{\sharp_{A}}T)\leq\frac{1}{2}{\left\|\left(T^{\sharp_{A}}T\right)^{2r}+\left(S^{\sharp_{A}}Sx\right)^{2r}\right\|}_{A}.

(28)

Proof.

Let
\displaystyle x\in\mathcal{H}
with
\displaystyle{\left\|x\right\|}_{A}=1
. Then

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left|{\left<S^{\sharp_{A}}ST^{\sharp_{A}}Tx,x\right>}_{A}\right|^{r}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}{\left\|\left(T^{\sharp_{A}}T\right)^{2r}+\left(S^{\sharp_{A}}Sx\right)^{2r}\right\|}_{A}.

Taking the supremum over all vectors
\displaystyle x\in\mathcal{H}
with
\displaystyle{\left\|x\right\|}_{A}=1

w_{A}^{r}(S^{\sharp_{A}}ST^{\sharp_{A}}T)\leq\frac{1}{2}{\left\|\left(T^{\sharp_{A}}T\right)^{2r}+\left(S^{\sharp_{A}}Sx\right)^{2r}\right\|}_{A}.

4
Some inequalities for the sum of two operators

The aim of this section is to present refinements of the triangle inequality for the
\displaystyle A
-operator semi-norm and to establish new
\displaystyle A
-numerical radius inequalities for the sum of two operators. The triangle inequality is a fundamental result in functional analysis, and its refinement provides deeper insights into the behavior of operators in semi-Hilbertian spaces.

We begin by deriving an improved version of the triangle inequality for the
\displaystyle A
-operator semi-norm, which offers a sharper bound compared to the classical result. Subsequently, we explore inequalities involving the
\displaystyle A
-numerical radius of the sum of two operators, leveraging the properties of
\displaystyle A
-adjoint operators and the semi-inner product structure. These results not only generalize existing inequalities but also provide more precise estimates, as demonstrated through illustrative examples.

The findings in this section contribute to a better understanding of operator norms and numerical radii in semi-Hilbertian spaces, with potential applications in numerical analysis and operator theory.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\displaystyle T,S\in\mathcal{B}_{A}(\mathcal{H})
. Then

{\left\|T+S\right\|}_{A}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}\left({\left\|T^{\sharp_{A}}T+S^{\sharp_{A}}S\right\|}_{A}+{\left\|T^{\sharp_{A}}T-S^{\sharp_{A}}S\right\|}_{A}\right)+{\left\|T\right\|}_{A}{\left\|S\right\|}_{A}+2w_{A}(S^{\sharp_{A}}T).

Proof.

Let
\displaystyle x,y\in\mathcal{H}
with
\displaystyle{\left\|x\right\|}_{A}={\left\|y\right\|}_{A}=1
. Then using Lemma
2.11
, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left|{\left<(T+S)x,y\right>}_{A}\right|^{2}\leq\left(\left|{\left<Tx,y\right>}_{A}\right|+\left|{\left<Sx,y\right>}_{A}\right|\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left({\left\|Tx\right\|}_{A}+{\left\|Sx\right\|}_{A}\right)\max\left\{{\left\|Tx\right\|}_{A},{\left\|Sx\right\|}_{A}\right\}+2\left|{\left<Tx,Sx\right>}_{A}\right|

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\max\left\{{\left\|Tx\right\|}_{A}^{2},{\left\|Sx\right\|}_{A}^{2}\right\}+{\left\|Tx\right\|}_{A}{\left\|Sx\right\|}_{A}+2\left|{\left<Tx,Sx\right>}_{A}\right|

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left({\left<\left(T^{\sharp_{A}}T+S^{\sharp_{A}}S\right)x,x\right>}_{A}+\left|{\left<\left(T^{\sharp_{A}}T-S^{\sharp_{A}}S\right)x,x\right>}_{A}\right|\right)+{\left\|Tx\right\|}_{A}{\left\|Sx\right\|}_{A}+2\left|{\left<Tx,Sx\right>}_{A}\right|

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left({\left\|T^{\sharp_{A}}T+S^{\sharp_{A}}S\right\|}_{A}+{\left\|T^{\sharp_{A}}T-S^{\sharp_{A}}S\right\|}_{A}\right)+{\left\|T\right\|}_{A}{\left\|S\right\|}_{A}+2w_{A}(S^{\sharp_{A}}T).

Taking the supremum over all unit vectors
\displaystyle x,y\in\mathcal{H}
, we obtain

{\left\|T+S\right\|}_{A}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}\left({\left\|T^{\sharp_{A}}T+S^{\sharp_{A}}S\right\|}_{A}+{\left\|T^{\sharp_{A}}T-S^{\sharp_{A}}S\right\|}_{A}\right)+{\left\|T\right\|}_{A}{\left\|S\right\|}_{A}+2w_{A}(S^{\sharp_{A}}T).

∎

where
\displaystyle w(x)>0
is a bounded measurable function.

The
\displaystyle A
-numerical radius
\displaystyle w_{A}(T)
satisfies:

\frac{1}{2}\|T\|_{A}\leq w_{A}(T)\leq\|T\|_{A}

5.1.1
Discretized Version

For numerical computation, we discretize the operator using finite differences. Let
\displaystyle N
be the number of grid points,
\displaystyle h=1/(N+1)
, and
\displaystyle x_{j}=jh
for
\displaystyle j=1,\ldots,N
.

The discretized operator
\displaystyle T_{h}
becomes:

T_{h}=\frac{1}{h^{2}}\begin{bmatrix}2p_{1/2}&-p_{1/2}&0&\cdots&0\\
-p_{1/2}&2p_{3/2}&-p_{3/2}&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&0\\
\vdots&\ddots&-p_{N-3/2}&2p_{N-1/2}&-p_{N-1/2}\\
0&\cdots&0&-p_{N-1/2}&2p_{N+1/2}\end{bmatrix}+\mathrm{diag}(q(x_{j}))

The weight matrix
\displaystyle A_{h}
is:

A_{h}=\mathrm{diag}(w(x_{j}))

5.1.2
Numerical Radius Computation

Using Theorem
3.1
, we can bound the
\displaystyle A
-numerical radius:

w_{A}^{4}(T_{h})\leq\frac{\gamma_{1}}{16}\|T_{h}^{\sharp_{A}}T_{h}+T_{h}T_{h}^{\sharp_{A}}\|_{A}^{2}+\frac{\gamma_{2}}{8}\|T_{h}^{\sharp_{A}}T_{h}+T_{h}T_{h}^{\sharp_{A}}\|_{A}w_{A}(T_{h}^{2})

For the special case where
\displaystyle p(x)=1
and
\displaystyle q(x)=0
, we have:

T_{h}^{\sharp_{A}}=A_{h}^{-1}T_{h}^{T}A_{h}

Theorem 5.1
.

For the discrete Laplacian with constant coefficients and constant weight
\displaystyle w(x)=1
, the
\displaystyle A
-numerical radius satisfies:

w_{A}(T_{h})=2h^{-2}(1-\cos(\pi h))

Proof.

In this case,
\displaystyle T_{h}
is symmetric and
\displaystyle A_{h}=I
, so
\displaystyle w_{A}(T_{h})
equals the spectral radius. The eigenvalues are:

\lambda_{k}=2h^{-2}(1-\cos(k\pi h)),\quad k=1,\ldots,N

The maximum occurs at
\displaystyle k=1
, giving the result.
∎

5.2
Nonlinear PDE Application

Consider the reaction-diffusion equation:

\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}=\Delta u+f(u)

Linearizing about a solution
\displaystyle\bar{u}
gives:

\frac{\partial v}{\partial t}=\Delta v+f^{\prime}(\bar{u})v

The operator is:

T=\Delta+f^{\prime}(\bar{u})

5.2.1
Weighted Stability

Choose
\displaystyle A=e^{-V(x)}
as a weight function. Then:

Theorem 5.2
.

The linearized operator satisfies:

w_{A}(T)\leq\sup_{x}|f^{\prime}(\bar{u}(x))|+w_{A}(\Delta)

Proof.

Using the subadditivity of numerical radius and the fact that multiplication by
\displaystyle f^{\prime}(\bar{u})
is A-selfadjoint:

w_{A}(T)\leq w_{A}(\Delta)+w_{A}(f^{\prime}(\bar{u}))

Since
\displaystyle f^{\prime}(\bar{u})
is a multiplication operator:

w_{A}(f^{\prime}(\bar{u}))=\sup_{x}|f^{\prime}(\bar{u}(x))|

∎

5.3
Application of A-Numerical Radius to Quantum Harmonic Oscillator

Consider the quantum harmonic oscillator with Hamiltonian:

H=\frac{p^{2}}{2m}+\frac{1}{2}m\omega^{2}x^{2}

where
\displaystyle p
is the momentum operator,
\displaystyle x
is the position operator,
\displaystyle m
is mass, and
\displaystyle\omega
is the angular frequency.

5.3.1
Creation and Annihilation Operators

We define the annihilation operator
\displaystyle a
and creation operator
\displaystyle a^{\dagger}
:

a=\sqrt{\frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}}x+i\sqrt{\frac{1}{2m\omega\hbar}}p

a^{\dagger}=\sqrt{\frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}}x-i\sqrt{\frac{1}{2m\omega\hbar}}p

These satisfy the commutation relation
\displaystyle[a,a^{\dagger}]=1
.

5.3.2
Semi-Hilbertian Space Setup

Let
\displaystyle A
be the number operator
\displaystyle N=a^{\dagger}a
, which is positive and self-adjoint. The A-numerical radius can be used to study operators in this context.

For an operator
\displaystyle T
acting on the oscillator’s Hilbert space, the A-numerical radius is:

w_{A}(T)=\sup_{\|\psi\|_{A}=1}|\langle T\psi,\psi\rangle_{A}|

where
\displaystyle\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{A}=\langle A\cdot,\cdot\rangle
and
\displaystyle\|\psi\|_{A}=\sqrt{\langle A\psi,\psi\rangle}
.

5.3.3
Example Calculation

Consider the operator
\displaystyle T=a+a^{\dagger}
(position operator up to constants). We’ll estimate its A-numerical radius with
\displaystyle A=N
.

Using the inequality from the paper:

\frac{1}{2}\|T\|_{A}\leq w_{A}(T)\leq\|T\|_{A}

First compute
\displaystyle\|T\|_{A}
:

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|T\|_{A}^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\sup_{\|\psi\|_{A}=1}\|T\psi\|_{A}^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\sup_{\|\psi\|_{A}=1}\langle NT\psi,T\psi\rangle

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\sup_{\|\psi\|_{A}=1}\langle a^{\dagger}a(a+a^{\dagger})\psi,(a+a^{\dagger})\psi\rangle

For the ground state
\displaystyle|0\rangle
(with
\displaystyle N|0\rangle=0
):

\langle T|0\rangle,|0\rangle\rangle_{A}=\langle N(a+a^{\dagger})|0\rangle,|0\rangle\rangle=\langle a^{\dagger}|0\rangle,|0\rangle\rangle=0

For the first excited state
\displaystyle|1\rangle
:

\langle T|1\rangle,|1\rangle\rangle_{A}=\langle N(a+a^{\dagger})|1\rangle,|1\rangle\rangle

=\langle a^{\dagger}a(a|1\rangle+a^{\dagger}|1\rangle),|1\rangle\rangle

=\langle a^{\dagger}a(\sqrt{1}|0\rangle+\sqrt{2}|2\rangle),|1\rangle\rangle

=\langle a^{\dagger}(0+\sqrt{2}\sqrt{2}|1\rangle),|1\rangle\rangle=2

Thus,
\displaystyle w_{A}(T)\geq 2
.

5.3.4
Physical Interpretation

The A-numerical radius in this context provides information about the range of possible expectation values when the system is in states weighted by the number operator. This is particularly useful when studying systems where particle number is an important consideration, such as in quantum optics or many-body physics.

5.4
Application of A-Numerical Radius to Spin Systems

Consider a system of two spin-
\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}
particles (qubits) with the Hamiltonian:

H=J(\sigma_{x}\otimes\sigma_{x}+\sigma_{y}\otimes\sigma_{y})+B(\sigma_{z}\otimes I+I\otimes\sigma_{z})

where
\displaystyle J
is the coupling constant,
\displaystyle B
is the magnetic field strength, and
\displaystyle\sigma_{i}
are Pauli matrices.

5.4.1
Semi-Hilbertian Space Setup

Let
\displaystyle A=\rho
be the density matrix of the system at thermal equilibrium:

\rho=\frac{e^{-\beta H}}{\mathrm{tr}(e^{-\beta H})}

where
\displaystyle\beta=1/(k_{B}T)
. This positive definite operator induces a semi-inner product:

\langle\phi|\psi\rangle_{\rho}=\mathrm{tr}(\rho\phi^{\dagger}\psi)

5.4.2
A-Numerical Radius for Spin Observables

Consider the observable
\displaystyle S=\sigma_{x}\otimes I
(spin measurement on first particle). Its A-numerical radius is:

w_{\rho}(S)=\sup_{\|\psi\|_{\rho}=1}|\mathrm{tr}(\rho S\psi^{\dagger}\psi)|

Using Theorem 3.1 from the paper with
\displaystyle\alpha=0.5
,
\displaystyle\beta=1
:

w_{\rho}^{4}(S)\leq\frac{19}{128}\|S^{\sharp}_{\rho}S+SS^{\sharp}_{\rho}\|_{\rho}^{2}+\frac{13}{64}\|S^{\sharp}_{\rho}S+SS^{\sharp}_{\rho}\|_{\rho}w_{\rho}(S^{2})

Explicit Calculation for B=0

For
\displaystyle B=0
and high temperature (
\displaystyle\beta\to 0
),
\displaystyle\rho\approx\frac{I}{4}
:

\displaystyle\displaystyle S^{\sharp}_{\rho}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\rho^{-1}S^{\dagger}\rho=S

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|S^{\sharp}_{\rho}S+SS^{\sharp}_{\rho}\|_{\rho}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=2\|\rho^{1/2}S^{2}\rho^{1/2}\|=\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle w_{\rho}(S^{2})

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\|\rho^{1/2}S^{2}\rho^{1/2}\|=\frac{1}{4}

The inequality becomes:

w_{\rho}^{4}(S)\leq\frac{19}{128}\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}+\frac{13}{64}\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)\left(\frac{1}{4}\right)\approx 0.0186+0.0102=0.0288

w_{\rho}(S)\leq 0.41

5.4.3
Physical Interpretation

•

Measurement Statistics
: The A-numerical radius bounds the range of possible expectation values for spin measurements in thermal states.

•

Temperature Dependence
: As
\displaystyle T\to 0
(
\displaystyle\beta\to\infty
),
\displaystyle\rho
becomes the ground state projector, and the bounds tighten.

•

Entanglement Detection
: The A-numerical radius of certain observables can witness entanglement when it exceeds separable bounds.

5.4.4
Comparison with Standard Numerical Radius

For pure states (
\displaystyle\rho=|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|
), the A-numerical radius reduces to the standard numerical radius. The inequalities provide:

\frac{1}{2}\|S\|_{\rho}\leq w_{\rho}(S)\leq\|S\|_{\rho}

which for our example gives
\displaystyle 0.25\leq w_{\rho}(S)\leq 0.5
, consistent with our calculation.

Conclusion and Future Work

Improving current bounds for the
\displaystyle A
-numerical radius of operators in semi-Hilbertian spaces was the main goal of this work. We have improved our knowledge of operator behavior in this context by deriving additional and sharper inequalities. To provide a more accurate description of operator norms, we specifically improved the triangle inequality for the
\displaystyle A
-operator semi-norm. These enhancements advance the field’s understanding of numerical radius inequalities and their uses. We provided practical examples to support our theoretical conclusions and show how effective the suggested constraints are, highlighting their importance in operator theory and functional analysis.

Future studies might investigate expansions of our bounds to larger classes of operators, such as unbounded operators in semi-Hilbertian spaces, in order to build on our findings. The field may be further enhanced by looking at possible relationships between the
\displaystyle A
-numerical radius and other operator inequalities. Furthermore, further research should be done on the applications of these improved constraints in fields including optimization, control theory, and quantum mechanics. The investigation of numerical techniques for more effectively calculating the
\displaystyle A
-numerical radius in real-world situations is another intriguing avenue. These directions present encouraging chances to increase the significance of our findings.

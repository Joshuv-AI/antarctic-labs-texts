On soliton asymptotics for 2D Maxwell–Lorentz equations with rotating particle


Abstract
We consider 2D Maxwell–Lorentz equations with extended charged rotating particle. The system admits solitons which are solutions corresponding to a particle moving with a constant velocity and rotating with a constant angular velocity. Our main result is asymptotic stability of the solitons.

Introduction

2D Maxwell–Lorentz equations with the rotating particle read

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}\dot{E}(x,t)&=&J\nabla B(x,t)-[\dot{q}(t)-\omega(t)J(x-q(t))]\rho(x-q(t))\\
\dot{B}(x,t)&=&-\nabla\cdot(JE(x,t)),\qquad\nabla\cdot E(x,t)=\rho(x-q(t))\\
m\ddot{q}(t)&=&\langle E(x,t)+B(x,t)[J\dot{q}(t)+\omega(t)(x-q(t))],\rho(x\!-\!q(t))\rangle\\
I\dot{\omega}(t)&=&\langle(x\!-\!q(t))\cdot\big[JE(x,t)\!-B(x,t)\dot{q}(t)],\rho(x\!-\!q(t))\rangle\end{array}\right|,~~J=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{pmatrix}.

(1.1)

The system describes a motion of an extended
charged particle, centered at
q(t)
, in the Maxwell field
(E(x,t),B(x,t))\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\oplus\mathbb{R}
.
Here the brackets
\langle,\rangle
denote the inner product in the Hilbert space
L^{2}:=L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\otimes\mathbb{R}^{2}
,

m>0
is the mass of the particle and
I>0
is its moment of inertia. Note that the system is an analogue of 3D Maxwell–Lorentz system
[
11
, (2.39)–2.41)]
.

We assume that the charge density
\rho(x)
is real-valued, compactly supported and spherically symmetric, i.e.,

\rho\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),\quad\rho(x)=0~~{\rm for}~~|x|\geq R_{\rho},\quad\rho(x)=\rho_{rad}(|x|),\quad\rho(x)\not\equiv 0.

(1.2)

Moreover, we assume that

\int x^{\alpha}\rho(x)dx=0,\qquad|\alpha|\leq 4.

(1.3)

In particular, the total charge
\int\rho(x)dx
equals zero (neutrality of the particle).
Equivalently,

\hat{\rho}^{(\alpha)}(0)=0,\qquad|\alpha|\leq 4.

(1.4)

Note that (
1.3
) holds if one of
\alpha_{j}
,
j=1,2
, or both of them is odd.
We rewrite the equation (
1.1
) in terms of the Maxwell potentials
A(x,t)
and
B(x,t)
(the system (
2.4
)).
The system admits soliton solutions
(A_{v,\rm{w}},B_{v,\rm{w}})
corresponding to the particle which move with constant velocities
v\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
0\leq|v|<1

and rotate with constant angular velocities
\rm{w}\in\mathbb{R}
.
Asymptotic stability of the soliton with velocity
v_{0}
and angular velocity
\rm{w}_{0}

means that for solutions with initial data sufficiently close to this soliton the following properties are satisfied:

i)
\dot{q}(t)\to v_{\pm}\approx v_{0}
and
\omega(t)\to\omega_{\pm}\approx\rm{w}_{0}
as
t\to\pm\infty
;

ii) for the field part of the solutions asymptotics (
4.1
) hold, where
the remainder
r_{\pm}(x,t)\to 0
as
t\to\pm\infty
in the global energy norm.

In this paper we prove the asymptotic stability of solotons under a certain spectral condition ( condition (
13.52
) below),
which is an analogue of the Wiener condition for the system (
1.1
). In particular, the condition is satisfied
for a sufficiently large
I
.

Our results extend the result of
[
10
]
, where asymptotic stability of a soliton with
\rm{w}=0
was established.

2
Hamiltonian structure and well-posedness

In the Maxwell potentials
A(x,t)=(A_{1}(x,t),A_{2}(x,t))
,
\Phi(x,t)
, we have

B(x,t)=\nabla\cdot(JA)=\nabla_{1}A_{2}(x,t)-\nabla_{2}A_{1}(x,t),\qquad E(x,t)=-\dot{A}(x,t)-\nabla\Phi(x,t).

(2.1)

We choose the Coulomb gauge
\nabla\cdot A(x,t)=0
. Then the second line of (
1.1
) implies

-\Delta\Phi(x,t)=\rho(x-q(t)).

Hence,

\Phi(x,t)=\Phi_{0}(x-q(t)),\qquad\Phi_{0}(y)=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\log|x-y|\rho(y)dy.

(2.2)

We set

p=m\dot{q}+\langle A(x),\rho(x-q)\rangle,\quad M=I\omega-\langle A(x)\cdot J(x-q),\rho(x-q)\rangle\big).

(2.3)

By (
1.1
) and (
2.1
),

\displaystyle\dot{p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m\ddot{q}+\langle\dot{A}(x),\rho(x-q)\rangle-\langle A(x),(\dot{q}\cdot\nabla)\rho(y)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\langle A(x)\cdot\dot{q},\nabla\rho(x-q)\rangle+\omega\langle\nabla\cdot JA(x),(x-q)\rho(x-q)\rangle,

\displaystyle\dot{M}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I\dot{\omega}-\langle\dot{A}(y+q)\cdot Jy,\rho(y)\rangle-\langle(\nabla\cdot\dot{q})A(y+q)\cdot Jy),\rho(y)\rangle=-\langle A\cdot\dot{q},(\nabla\cdot J)\rho\rangle=0

Thus, we can rewrite system (
1.1
) as

\!\!\left\{\begin{array}[]{llll}\dot{A}(x,t)=\Pi(x,t)\\
\dot{\Pi}(x,t)=\Delta A(x,t)-\omega J\varrho(x-q(t))+{\cal P}[\dot{q}\rho(x-q(t))]\\
m\dot{q}(t)=p(t)-\langle A(x,t),\rho(x-q(t))\rangle\\
\dot{p}(t)=-\langle A(x,t)\cdot\dot{q}(t),\nabla\rho(x-q(t))\rangle+\omega(t)\langle\nabla\cdot JA(x,t),\varrho(x-q(t))\rangle\end{array}\!\right|,~\varrho(x):=x\rho(x).

(2.4)

Here

\omega(t)=\frac{1}{I}\big(M+\langle A(x,t),J\varrho(x-q(t))\rangle\big).

Denote by
{\cal P}
the projection onto the space of solenoidal (divergence-free) vector fields,
which in Fourier space reads:

\widehat{{\cal P}a}(k)=\hat{a}(k)-\frac{\hat{a}(k)\cdot k}{k^{2}}k=\frac{\hat{a}(k)\cdot Jk}{k^{2}}Jk.

(2.5)

Let us introduce a phase space for the system (
2.4
). Denote by

\dot{H}^{1}
the closure of
C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\otimes\mathbb{R}^{2}
with the respect to the norm

\|\nabla A\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}=\|\nabla A\|_{L^{2}}
, where
L^{2}:=L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\otimes\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
Let
{\bf L}^{2}
,
\dot{\bf H}^{1}
be the subspaces formed by solenoidal vector fields.
Define the phase space

{\cal E}={\cal F}\oplus\mathbb{R}^{2}\oplus\mathbb{R}^{2},\quad{\cal F}=\dot{\bf H}^{1}\oplus{\bf L}^{2}.

(2.6)

System (
2.4
) is a Hamiltonian system with the Hamiltonian functional

H(Y)=\frac{1}{2}\int[|\Pi(x)|^{2}+|\nabla A(x)|^{2}]dx+\frac{1}{2}m\dot{q}^{2}(t)+\frac{1}{2}I\omega^{2}(t),~~Y=(A,\Pi,q,p)\in{\cal E}

(2.7)

where

\frac{m\dot{q}^{2}}{2}=\frac{1}{2m}(p-\langle A,\rho(x-q)\rangle)^{2},\qquad\frac{I\omega^{2}}{2}=\frac{1}{2I}(M+\langle A,J\varrho(x-q)\rangle)^{2}.

(2.8)

The Hamiltonian (
2.7
) is well defined and differentiable on the Hilbert
phase space (
2.6
).
The system (
2.4
) is equivalent to the canonical Hamiltonian system

\dot{A}=D_{\Pi}H,\,\,\,\dot{\Pi}=-D_{A}H;\quad\dot{q}=D_{p}H,\,\,\,\dot{p}=-D_{q}H,

or equivalently,

\dot{Y}={\bf J}{\cal D}{\cal H}(Y),\qquad{\bf J}:=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc}0&I_{2}&0&0\\
-I_{2}&0&0&0\\
0&0&0&I_{2}\\
0&0&-I_{2}&0\end{array}\right).

(2.9)

where
I_{2}
denotes the identity
2\times 2
matrix.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let (
1.2
) holds, and let
Y_{0}=(A_{0},\Pi_{0},q_{0},p_{0})\in{\cal E}
. Then

(i) there exists a unique solution
Y(t)\in C(\mathbb{R},{\cal E})
to the Cauchy problem for (
2.4
);

(ii) the energy conserves:
H(Y(t))=H(Y_{0})
for
t\in R
;

(iii) the estimate holds,

|\dot{q}|\leq\overline{v},\quad t\in\mathbb{R}.

(2.10)

The proof is similar to
[
6
, Lemma A.2]
.

3
Solitons

Solitons of the system (
2.4
) are solutions of the form

Y_{v}=(A_{v}(x\!-vt\!-a),\Pi_{v}(x\!-vt\!-a),vt+a,p_{v}),~~|v|<1,~~a\in\mathbb{R}^{2},~~p_{v}=mv+\langle A_{v},\rho\rangle.

(3.1)

Due to the first two equations of (
2.4
), we get

\Pi_{v}(y)=-(v\cdot\nabla)A_{v}(y),\qquad-(v\cdot\nabla)\Pi_{v}(y)=\Delta A_{v}(y)-\rm{w}J\varrho(y)+{\cal P}[v\rho(y)],

(3.2)

Hence, in the Fourier representation,

\hat{A}_{v}(k)=\frac{(v-\frac{(v\cdot k)k}{k^{2}})\hat{\rho}(k)}{\hat{D}_{0}}-\frac{\rm{w}J\hat{\varrho}(k)}{\hat{D}_{0}},\quad\hat{D}_{0}:=k^{2}-(v\cdot k)^{2},\quad{\rm{w}}=\frac{1}{I}\big(M+\langle A_{v},J\varrho\rangle\big).

(3.3)

By (
3.3
),

\langle A_{v},J\varrho\rangle=-{\rm{w}}\langle\frac{J\hat{\varrho}(k)}{\hat{D}_{0}},J\hat{\varrho}(k)\rangle=-{\rm{w}}\varkappa,\quad\varkappa=\varkappa(v)=\int\frac{|\hat{\varrho}(k)|^{2}}{\hat{D}_{0}}dk=\int\frac{|\nabla\hat{\rho}(k)|^{2}}{\hat{D}_{0}}dk.

(3.4)

Therefore,

{\rm{w}}={\rm{w}}(v)=M/(I+\varkappa)

(3.5)

By (
1.3
),
\nabla A_{v}(y)\sim|y|^{-6},\Pi_{v}(y)\sim|y|^{-6}
as
|y|\to\infty
.
Hence,

\nabla A_{v},~\Pi_{v}\in L^{2}_{\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\otimes\mathbb{R}^{2},\quad\beta<5.

(3.6)

Denote
V:=\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:|v|<1\}
.

Definition 3.1
.

S(\sigma):=(A_{v}(x-b),\Pi_{v}(x-b),b,p_{v})
, where
\sigma:=(b,v)
with
b\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
v\in V
.

Obviously, the soliton solution (
3.1
) admits the representation
S(\sigma(t))
with
\sigma(t)=(vt+a,v)
.

Definition 3.2
.

A solitary manifold is the set
{\cal S}:=\{S(\sigma):\sigma\in\Sigma:=\mathbb{R}^{2}\times V\}
.

Note that the manifold
{\cal S}
is invariant with respect to the translations

T_{a}:(A(\cdot),\Pi(\cdot),q,p)\mapsto(A(\cdot-a),\Pi(\cdot-a),q+a,p),\quad a\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.

4
Main result

To state our main result, we introduce the weighted Sobolev spaces. Let
{\bf L}^{2}_{\beta}
,
\beta\in\mathbb{R}

be the weighted space of solenoidal vector field
A\in L^{2}_{\beta}
.
We define the space
{\cal F}_{\beta}

of the solenoidal vector fields
(A,\Pi)
with the norm

\|(A,\Pi)\|_{{\cal F}_{\beta}}=\|\nabla A\|_{{\bf L}^{2}_{\beta}}+\|\Pi\|_{{\bf L}^{2}_{\beta}}.

Besides, we will use the space

{\cal E}_{\beta}

of the states
Y=(A,\Pi,q,p)
with the norm

\|Y\|_{\beta}=\|\nabla A\|_{{\bf L}^{2}_{\beta}}+\|\Pi\|_{{\bf L}^{2}_{\beta}}+|q|+|p|.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let conditions (
1.2
)–(
1.3
) and (
13.52
), and let
\beta=4+\delta
,
\delta\in(0,1)
.
Suppose that
Y_{0}\in{\cal E}_{\beta}
is sufficiently close to the solitary manifold in the following sense:

Y_{0}=S(\sigma_{0})+Z_{0},\qquad d_{\beta}=\|Z_{0}\|_{\beta}\ll 1.

(4.1)

Let
Y(t)\in C(\mathbb{R},{\cal E})
be the solution to (
2.4
) with initial data
Y_{0}
. Then

\displaystyle\dot{q}(t)=v_{\pm}+{\cal O}(|t|^{-2}),\quad q(t)=v_{\pm}t+a_{\pm}+{\cal O}(|t|^{-2}),

(4.2)

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A(x,t)\\
\Pi(x,t)\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A_{v_{\pm}}(x-v_{\pm}t-a_{\pm})\\
\Pi_{v_{\pm}}(x-v_{\pm}t-a_{\pm})\end{array}\right)+W_{0}(t)\Psi_{\pm}+r_{\pm}(x,t),\quad t\to\pm\infty.

Here
{\rm{w}}_{0}={\rm{w}}(v_{0})
,
W_{0}(t)
is the dynamical group of the free wave equation,

\Psi_{\pm}
are the corresponding asymptotic scattering states,
and the remainder
r_{\pm}(x,t)
converges to zero in the global energy norm:

\|r_{\pm}(t)\|_{\cal F}={\cal O}(|t|^{-1}),\quad t\to\pm\infty.

(4.8)

5
Symplectic Structure

Consider the symplectic form
\Omega
on the phase space
{\cal E}

\Omega(Y_{1},Y_{2})=\langle Y_{1},{\bf J}Y_{2}\rangle,\quad Y_{1},Y_{2}\in{\cal E},

(5.1)

where

\langle Y_{1},Y_{2}\rangle:=\langle A_{1},A_{2}\rangle+\langle\Pi_{1},\Pi_{2}\rangle+q_{1}q_{2}+p_{1}p_{2},

\langle A_{1},A_{2}\rangle=\displaystyle\int A_{1}(x)\overline{A}_{2}(x)dx

.

i)

Y_{1}

Y_{2}

\Omega(Y_{1},Y_{2})=0

) hold,

Linearization on the solitary manifold

Let us consider a solution to the system (
2.4
), and split it as the sum

Y(t)=S(\sigma(t))+Z(t),

(6.1)

where
\sigma(t)=(b(t),v(t))\in\Sigma
is an arbitrary smooth function of
t\in\mathbb{R}
.
In detail, denote
Y=(A,\Pi,q,p)
and

Then (

) means that

\left.\begin{array}[]{rclr}A(x,t)&=&A_{v(t)}(x-b(t))+\Lambda(x-b(t),t),~~\Pi(x,t)=\Pi_{v(t)}(x-b(t))+\Psi(x-b(t),t)\\

(6.2)

Substituting (

(6.3)

(6.4)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dot{v}\cdot\nabla_{v}p_{v(t)}+\dot{\pi}(t)=\langle\nabla((A_{v(t)}(y)+\Lambda(y,t))\cdot\dot{q}),\rho(y-r)\rangle

Lemma 7.1
.

i) The equation (

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int\Big[|\Psi|^{2}+|\nabla\Lambda|^{2}\Big]dy+\int\Psi(u\cdot\nabla)\Lambda dy+\frac{1}{2m}\langle\Lambda,\rho\rangle^{2}+\frac{1}{2I}\langle\Lambda,J\varrho\rangle^{2}

(7.3)

\displaystyle+

(7.4)

iii) The skew-symmetry relation holds,

\Omega({\bf A}_{v,u}X_{1},X_{2})=-\Omega(X_{1},{\bf A}_{v,u}X_{2}),~~~~~~~~X_{1},X_{2}\in{\cal E}.

(7.5)

Proof.

i) Equality (

).

ii) The energy conservation law follows by (

) and the chain rule for the Fréchet derivatives. Formally,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}{\cal H}_{v,u}(X(t))=\langle D{\cal H}_{v,u}(X(t)),\dot{X}(t)\rangle=\langle D{\cal H}_{v,u}(X(t)),{\bf J}D{\cal H}_{v,u}(X(t))\rangle=0

since the operator

is skew-symmetric, and

iii) The skew-symmetry holds since

is symmetric as the Fréchet derivative of a quadratic form.

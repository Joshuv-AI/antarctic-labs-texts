Riesz transform, function spaces and their applications on infinite dimensional compact groups
Alexander Bendikov, Li Chen and Laurent Saloff-Coste

Abstract
On a compact connected group G G , consider the infinitesimal generator − L -L of a central symmetric Gaussian convolution semigroup ( μ t ) t > 0 (\mu_{t})_{t>0} . We establish several regularity results of the solution to the Poisson equation L ​ U = F LU=F , both in strong and weak senses. To this end, we introduce two classes of Lipschitz spaces for 1 ≤ p ≤ ∞ 1\leq p\leq\infty : Λ θ p \Lambda_{\theta}^{p} , defined via the associated Markov semigroup, and L θ p \mathrm{L}_{\theta}^{p} , defined via the intrinsic distance. In the strong sense, we prove a priori Sobolev regularity and Lipschitz regularity in the class of Λ θ p \Lambda_{\theta}^{p} space. In the distributional sense, we further show local regularity in the class of L θ ∞ \mathrm{L}_{\theta}^{\infty} space. These results require some strong assumptions on − L -L . Our main techniques build on the differentiability of the associated semigroup, explicit dimension-free L p L^{p} ( 1 < p < ∞ 1<p<\infty ) boundedness of first and second order Riesz transforms, and a comparison between the two Lipschitz norms.

Introduction

It is well known that if
f
is merely a continuous function, in a bounded domain
\Omega\subset R^{n}
with
n>1
, it is possible that the equation
\Delta u=f
has no classical solution (i.e., there are no
\mathcal{C}^{2}
function
u
solving the equation). However, if instead of continuity we require
f
to be Hölder continuous with exponent
\alpha\in(0,1)
then the equation
\Delta u=f
, interpreted in the sense of distributions, admits a unique solution
u
which is actually
\mathcal{C}^{2,\alpha}
.

This is a basic steps in the classical Schauder interior regularity theory for second order elliptic operators and more sophisticated versions apply when
\Delta
is the Laplace operator on a Riemannian manifold.

This paper is concerned with this problem but in a more exotic setting where the underlying space is a compact connected locally connected metrizable group such as the infinite dimensional torus
\mathbb{T}^{\infty}
, i.e., the product of countably many circles, and other products such as
\prod_{n\geq 5}\mbox{SO}(n)
. On such groups, there are many natural Laplacians, each with possibly drastically different properties, and one can study properties of the solutions of the equation
\Delta u=f
, interpreted in various ways, for such Laplacians. This is motivated by
[
10
, Chapter 5]
.

To avoid most technical details, let us focus here on
\mathbb{T}^{\infty}=\prod_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{T}_{i}
, equipped with its normalized Haar measure. Laplacians are operators defined on smooth functions depending only of finitely many coordinates by

\Delta_{A}\phi=-\sum_{i,j=1}^{\infty}a_{i,j}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\phi

where
A=(a_{i,j})
is an infinite symmetric positive definite matrix (
\sum_{i,j}a_{i,j}\xi_{i}\xi_{j}\geq 0
and equal to
0
only if
\xi=(\xi_{i})=0
for all vectors
\xi
having finitely many non-zero coordinates). The simplest of these Laplacian is
\Delta_{0}\phi=-\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\partial_{i}^{2}\phi
but it behaves very badly from the point of view of the question raised above. On the other hand,
\Delta_{1}\phi=-\sum 2^{i}\partial_{i}^{2}\phi
is expected to behave much better, in some sense. In particular, while there does not exit a natural good metric on
\mathbb{T}^{\infty}
associated with
\Delta_{0}
, there is a very nice metric,
d_{1}
, associated with
\Delta_{1}
(its intrinsic metric, form the viewpoint of Dirichlet forms). Using this metric, we can ask whether or not weak local solutions of the equation
\Delta_{1}u=f
in an open set
\Omega
with
f
Hölder continuous with respect to the metric
d_{1}
in
\Omega
, are automatically classical solutions for which not only
\Delta_{1}u
makes sense as a continuous function in
\Omega
, but
\Delta_{1}u
is given by the converging series
-\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}2^{i}\partial_{i}^{2}u

where each
\partial_{i}^{2}u
exists and is continuous. In this paper, we prove that this is indeed the case for
\Delta_{1}
(and many other Laplacians). This statement is definitely not true for all possible Laplacians on
\mathbb{T}^{\infty}
. As explained later below, this applies not only when the equation
\Delta_{1}u=f
is interpreted in the standard weak sense in
\Omega
for which one assumes
u
is in
L^{2}
with first derivatives in
L^{2}
, but even when
u
and
f
are only assumed to be distributions of a certain type (a class of distributions associated to
\Delta_{1}
introduced and studied in
[
7
,
8
]
and which, for example, always include Radon measures).

In order to prove such results, we rely on several interesting facts and ideas, some of which have to be developed from scratch in this setting. One key idea is taken from
[
9
]
where the
L^{1}
-differentiability of the Markov semigroups
e^{-tL_{A}}
associated with Laplacians is discussed. A second key ideas is to use the boundedness of associated Riesz transforms on
L^{p}
with
p
very close to
1
or infinity (ideally, one would want to use the Riesz transforms on
L^{1}
or
L^{\infty}
but, of course, this is not possible). The important role played by the Riesz transforms in our arguments implies that we have to restrict ourselves to bi-invariant Laplacians (all Laplacian on
\mathbb{T}^{\infty}
are bi-invariant but this is of course not the case when
G
is not abelian). The third key idea (well-understood in the Euclidean case) is to relate two notions of “Hölder spaces” associated to a Laplacian
L_{A}
. One type of Hölder spaces is defined in terms of the action of the semigroup
e^{-tL_{A}}
while the other is defined more classically using the intrinsic distance associated with
L_{A}
(assuming this distance is well-defined). The main finding of this paper is that there is a large class of Laplacians (on
\mathbb{T}^{\infty}
but also on any other metrizable, locally connected connected compact group) for which these different steps can be implemented to obtain the type of result described above concerning the continuity of local distributional solutions of
\Delta_{A}u=f
when
f
is Hölder continuous in
\Omega
.

The setting

Following

5

\mathcal{B}(G)=\{f:G\to\mathbb{R};f=\phi\circ\pi_{\alpha}\text{ for some }\alpha\in\aleph\text{ and }\phi\in\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\infty}(G_{\alpha})\}.

Let

be a weakly continuous Gaussian convolution semigroup of probability measures on

. That is, each
\mu_{t}
,

•

•

as

.

Let
(H_{t})_{t>0}
be
\mathcal{C}_{0}
semigroup of operators acting on a Banach space
\mathcal{X}
. The semigroup
(H_{t})_{t>0}
is differentiable if, for all
f\in\mathcal{X}
and all
t>0
,
H_{t}f
is differentiable in
\mathcal{X}
. We will discuss the
L^{1}
differentiability of the class of Markov semigroups associated with Gaussian convolution semigroups.

Consider now
(H_{t})_{t>0}
the self-adjoint Markov semigroup with infinitesimal generator
-L
on
L^{2}(G,\nu)
, then the following properties hold

•

(H_{t})_{t>0}
is a contraction on
L^{p}(G,\nu)
for
1\leq p\leq\infty
;

•

(H_{t})_{t>0}
is bounded analytic on
L^{p}(G,\nu)
for
1<p<\infty
and satisfies

t\left\|\frac{\partial}{\partial t}H_{t}f\right\|_{p}\leq p^{*}\|f\|_{p},\quad\forall t>0,

(6)

where we recall
p^{*}=\max\{p,\frac{p}{p-1}\}=\frac{2}{1-|(2/p)-1|}
.

Note that
t\frac{\partial}{\partial t}H_{t}=-tLH_{t}
, then (
6
) is equivalent to the fact
t\|LH_{t}\|_{p\to p}\leq p^{*}
. In general, we have for any
n\in\mathbb{N}

t^{n}\|L^{n}H_{t}\|_{p\to p}\leq(p^{*})^{n}.

(7)

We say that
H_{t}
is ultracontractive if

\|H_{t}\|_{1\to\infty}\leq e^{M_{0}(t)}<\infty,\quad\forall t>0.

(8)

In this case,
H_{t}
is given by a bounded kernel
h_{t}(x,y)
(i.e.,
\mu_{t}
has continuous density with respect to the Haar measure
\nu
so
h_{t}(x,y)=\mu_{t}(x^{-1}y)
) and

M_{0}(t)=\log\left(\sup_{x,y\in G}h_{t}(x,y)\right).

(9)

Theorem 3.8

(
[
9
, Theorem 4.1]
)
.

Let
(H_{t})_{t>0}
be a self-adjoint Markov semigroup on
L^{2}(G,\nu)
which is ultracontractive. Assume that
\nu
is a probability measure. Then
(H_{t})_{t>0}
is
L^{1}
-differentiable and

t\left\|\frac{\partial}{\partial t}H_{t}\right\|_{1\to 1}\leq 2e\max\{M_{0}(t/2),2\},\quad\forall t>0,

where
M_{0}
is defined by (
9
).

Corollary 3.9
.

Referring to the preceding setting and notation,
(H_{t})_{t>0}
is also
L^{\infty}
-differentiable and

t\left\|\frac{\partial}{\partial t}H_{t}\right\|_{\infty\to\infty}\leq 2e\max\{M_{0}(t/2),2\},\forall t>0
.
Moreover, for
p=1
or
\infty
there holds

t^{2}\left\|\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}H_{t}\right\|_{p\to p}\leq 4e\left(\max\{M_{0}(t/2),2\}\right)^{2},\quad\forall t>0.

(10)

Proof.

The proof of the
L^{\infty}
-differentiability is similar as for the
L^{1}
case and we specify the details here. Indeed, for
f\in L^{\infty}(G)
, the ultracontractivity of
H_{t}
and the Riesz-Thorin interpolation theorem give that for any
1<p<\infty

\|H_{t}f\|_{\infty}\leq e^{M_{0}(t)/p}\|f\|_{p},\quad\forall t>0.

(11)

Since the measure
\nu
is normalized, it follows from the the
L^{p}
-analyticity of
H_{t}
that

t\left\|\frac{\partial}{\partial t}H_{t}f\right\|_{\infty}=2t\|H_{t/2}\|_{p\to\infty}\left\|\frac{\partial}{\partial t}H_{t/2}f\right\|_{p}\leq 2p^{*}e^{M_{0}(t/2)/p}\|f\|_{p}\leq 2p^{*}e^{M_{0}(t/2)/p}\|f\|_{\infty}.

If
M_{0}(t/2)<2
, we pick
p=2
. If
M_{0}(t/2)>2
, we pick
p
as a function of
t
such that
p=M_{0}(t/2)
. We then have
p^{*}=p=M_{0}(t/2)
and conclude that

t\left\|\frac{\partial}{\partial t}H_{t}\right\|_{\infty\to\infty}\leq 2e\max\{M_{0}(t/2),2\}.

Finally the same argument also applies to obtain the estimate in (
10
) for
p=\infty
.

Next we write

\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}H_{t}f=L^{2}H_{t/2}(H_{t/2}f).

Then in view of (
7
) and (
11
), one has

\displaystyle t^{2}\left\|\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}H_{t}f\right\|_{\infty}\leq t^{2}\left\|H_{t/2}f\right\|_{p\to\infty}\left\|L^{2}H_{t/2}\right\|_{p}\leq 4(p^{*})^{2}e^{M_{0}(t/2)/p}\|f\|_{\infty}.

Choosing the same
p
as above, we obtain the desired bound (
10
) for
p=\infty
.
∎

Under the assumption
(\mathrm{CK}\lambda)
in Definition
3.1
(iii), the heat semigroup
H_{t}
is ultracontractive with
M_{0}(t)=\frac{A}{t^{\lambda}}
, see Theorems
3.5
(ii).
Hence as a consequence of Theorem
3.8
, we have the following corollary.

Corollary 3.10
.

Assume that
(\mu_{t})_{t>0}
is a symmetric Gaussian semigroup satisfying property
(\mathrm{CK}\lambda)
. Let
p=1
or
\infty
. Then
(H_{t})_{t>0}
is
L^{p}
-differentiable and

t\left\|\frac{\partial}{\partial t}H_{t}\right\|_{p\to p}\leq C\max\Big\{\frac{2^{\lambda}A}{t^{\lambda}},2\Big\},\quad\forall t>0;

(12)

t^{2}\left\|\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}H_{t}\right\|_{p\to p}\leq C\left(\max\Big\{\frac{2^{\lambda}A}{t^{\lambda}},2\Big\}\right)^{2},\quad\forall t>0.

(13)

Remark 3.11
.

Under the same assumption, we will see later in Theorem

that the heat semigroup also satisfies an
L^{1}
“gradient” bound in terms of the square root of the carré du champs.

4
Dimension-free
L^{p}
bounds of Riesz transforms

In this section, we study the sharp
L^{p}
bounds (or dimension-free explicit
L^{p}
bounds) of Riesz transforms:

(1)

Riesz transforms in directions
X_{i}
,
i\in I
, i.e.,
R_{i}=X_{i}L^{-1/2}
;

(2)

Full Riesz transform
R^{G}=\nabla L^{-1/2}=(R_{1},R_{2},\cdots)
, where
\nabla=(X_{1},X_{2},\cdots)
. Then

\|R^{G}f\|_{p}=\||DL^{-1/2}f|_{L}\|_{p},

where we recall
|DL^{-1/2}f|_{L}^{2}=\Gamma(L^{-1/2}f,L^{-1/2}f)=\sum_{i\in I}(R_{i}f)^{2}
;

(3)

Second order Riesz transforms
R_{i}R_{j}
, for any
i,j\in I
.

We point out that the bi-invariance of
L
is essential in this section. In fact, such property will be heavily used in the proofs later.

In order to apply the martingale transform techniques, we first introduce the following probabilistic setup and then establish several useful lemmas.

Probabilistic setup.
Suppose that
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbf{P})
is a complete probability space, filtered by
\mathcal{F}=\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
, a family of right continuous sub-
\sigma
-fields of
\mathcal{F}
. Assume that
\mathcal{F}_{0}
contains all the events of probability
0
. Let
M
and
N
be adapted, real-valued martingales which have right-continuous paths with left-limits (r.c.l.l.). The martingale
N
is differentially subordinate to
M
if
|N_{0}|\leq|M_{0}|
and
\left<M\right>_{t}-\left<N\right>_{t}
is a non-decreasing and nonnegative function of
t
. The martingales
M_{t}
and
N_{t}
are said to be orthogonal if the covariation process
\left<M,N\right>_{t}=0
for all
t
.

Lemma 4.1

(
[
1
, Theorem 2.1.1]
)
.

Let
M
and
N
be two
\ell^{2}
-valued martingales with continuous paths such that
N
is differentially subordinate to
M
. For
1<p<\infty
, set
p^{*}=\max\{p,\frac{p}{p-1}\}
. Then

\|N\|_{p}\leq(p^{*}-1)\|M\|_{p}.

In particular, if
M
is an
\mathbb{R}
-valued martingale and
N=(N_{1},N_{2},\cdots)
is an
\ell^{2}
-valued martingale such that
N_{i}
is differentially subordinate to
M
and
\sum_{i}\langle N_{i}\rangle_{t}\leq\langle M\rangle_{t}
for all
t>0
. Then
\|N\|_{p}\leq(p^{*}-1)\|M\|_{p}
.

Let
\beta_{t}
be a one-dimensional Brownian motion on
\mathbb{R}
(independent of
B_{t}
) with generator
\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial y^{2}}
starting from
\beta_{0}=y>0
. We set the hitting time

\tau=\inf\{t>0:\beta_{t}=0\}

and denote by
\lambda_{y}
the distribution of
\tau
. Then the harmonic extension is the subordination semigroup defined by

Qf(y,x)=e^{-y\sqrt{L}}f(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}H_{t}f(x)d\lambda_{y}(t),\quad f\in\mathcal{B}(G).

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
M_{t}^{f}\coloneqq Qf(\beta_{t\wedge\tau},B_{t\wedge\tau})
. Then
M_{t}^{f}
is a square integrable martingale and the quadratic variation is

\langle M^{f}\rangle_{t}=2\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial y}Qf(\beta_{s},B_{s})\right)^{2}ds+2\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau}\sum_{i\in I}\left(X_{i}Qf(\beta_{s},B_{s})\right)^{2}ds.

Proof.

We note that for any
x\in G
and
u\geq 0
,

\displaystyle Qf(u,x)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}H_{t}f(x)d\lambda_{u}(t)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{E}(f(B_{t})|B_{0}=x)d\lambda_{u}(t)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{E}(f(B_{\tau})|B_{0}=x,\beta_{0}=u).

Since
M_{\tau}^{f}=Qf(0,B_{\tau})=f(B_{\tau})
, then for any
0\leq s\leq\tau
the above equation gives that

\mathbf{E}(M_{\tau}^{f}|\mathcal{F}_{s})=\mathbf{E}(f(B_{\tau})|\mathcal{F}_{s})=\mathbf{E}(f(B_{\tau})|(\beta_{s},B_{s}))=Qf(\beta_{s},B_{s}).

The quadratic variation is obtained from Itô’s lemma, see for instance
[
17
, Theorem 5.2, Section 5 in Chapter III]
(or from Fukushima’s decomposition theorem as in
[
15
, Theorem 5.2.3]
). Indeed, since
f\in\mathcal{B}(G)
, there exist
\phi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(G_{\alpha})
and
\alpha\in\aleph
such that
f=\phi\circ\pi_{\alpha}
. Then for any
0<t\leq\tau
, one has

\displaystyle Qf(\beta_{t},B_{t})

\displaystyle=Q^{\alpha}\phi(\beta_{t},B_{t}^{\alpha})

\displaystyle=Q^{\alpha}\phi(\beta_{0},B_{0}^{\alpha})+\int_{0}^{t}(\nabla_{\alpha}Q^{\alpha}\phi(\beta_{s},B_{s}^{\alpha}),dB_{s}^{\alpha})+\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\partial}{\partial y}Q^{\alpha}\phi(\beta_{s},B_{s}^{\alpha})d\beta_{s},

where
(B_{t}^{\alpha})_{t\geq 0}
is a Brownian motion on
G_{\alpha}
,
\nabla_{\alpha}=(X_{\alpha,1},X_{\alpha,2},\cdots,X_{\alpha,N_{\alpha}})
for
N_{\alpha}=\#I_{\alpha}
(recall
X_{\alpha,i}=d\pi_{\alpha}(X_{i})
), and
Q^{\alpha}
is harmonic extension of
H_{t}^{\alpha}
on
G_{\alpha}
.
It follows that

\displaystyle\langle M^{f}\rangle_{t}=\langle M^{\phi\circ\pi_{\alpha}}\rangle_{t}=2\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau}\sum_{i\in I}\left(X_{\alpha,i}Q^{\alpha}\phi(\beta_{s},B_{s}^{\alpha})\right)^{2}ds+2\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial y}Q^{\alpha}\phi(\beta_{s},B_{s}^{\alpha})\right)^{2}ds.

∎

4.1
L^{p}
bounds of first and second order Riesz transforms

Now we will give a martingale transform representation of directional Riesz transforms. For the finite dimensional case, the proof can be found in
[
2
]
.

Lemma 4.3
.

For
f\in\mathcal{B}(G)
, define for each
i\in I
the projection operator

T_{i}f(x)=\lim_{y_{0}\to+\infty}\mathbf{E}\left(\int_{0}^{\tau}X_{i}Qf(\beta_{s},B_{s})d\beta_{s}\mid\beta_{0}=y_{0},B_{\tau}=x\right).

Then
R_{i}f(x)=-2T_{i}f(x)
.

Proof.

Let
h\in\mathcal{B}(G)
, then
h(B_{\tau})=Qh(\beta_{\tau},B_{\tau})=M_{\tau}^{h}
. For any
y_{0}>0
,
by Itô’s formula and the Itô isometry we have

\displaystyle\int_{G}h(x)\,\mathbf{E}\left(\int_{0}^{\tau}X_{i}Qf(\beta_{s},B_{s})d\beta_{s}\mid\beta_{0}=y_{0},B_{\tau}=x\right)d\nu(x)

\displaystyle\quad\quad=\mathbf{E}_{y_{0}}\left(h(B_{\tau})\int_{0}^{\tau}X_{i}Qf(\beta_{s},B_{s})d\beta_{s}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\quad=2\int_{G}\int_{0}^{\infty}(y\wedge y_{0})\frac{\partial}{\partial y}Qh(y,x)\,X_{i}Qf(y,x)dyd\nu(x),

where the last equality follows from the facts that the Green function of the Brownian motion
\beta_{t}
starting at
y_{0}>0
killed at 0 is
y\wedge y_{0}
and
B_{t}
is distributed according to
\nu
.

Since
L
is bi-invariant, then
X_{i}
’s commute with the operator
L
and the Poisson semigroup. Hence one has

\displaystyle\int_{G}\int_{0}^{\infty}y\frac{\partial}{\partial y}Qh(y,x)\,X_{i}Qf(y,x)dyd\nu(x)

\displaystyle\quad\quad=-\int_{G}h(x)\int_{0}^{\infty}y\sqrt{L}X_{i}e^{-2y\sqrt{L}}f(x)dyd\nu(x)

\displaystyle\quad\quad=-\frac{1}{4}\int_{G}h(x)X_{i}L^{-1/2}f(x)d\nu(x).

We conclude that

\int_{G}h(x)T_{i}f(x)d\nu(x)=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{G}h(x)X_{i}L^{-1/2}f(x)d\nu(x).

∎

With above preparations, we state our main result in this section.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
1<p<\infty
. Then for every
j\in I
,

\|R_{j}f\|_{p}\leq 2(p^{*}-1)\|f\|_{p},\quad f\in\mathcal{B}(G).

Furthermore,
\|R^{G}f\|_{p}\leq 2(p^{*}-1)\|f\|_{p}
.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{B}(G)
is a dense subset in
L^{p}(G)
, it suffices to prove for
f\in\mathcal{B}(G)
. We note the fact that the conditional expectation is a contraction in
L^{p}
for
1<p<\infty
. Hence from Lemma
4.3
, it is enough to consider the stochastic integral

N_{t}^{i}\coloneqq\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau}X_{i}Qf(\beta_{s},B_{s})d\beta_{s},\quad i\in I.

Observe that
N_{t}^{i}
is differentially subordinate to
M_{t}^{f}
, and moreover by Lemma
4.2
,

\sum_{i\in I}\langle N^{i}\rangle_{t}\leq\langle M\rangle_{t}.

Applying martingale inequalities in Lemma
4.1
, we obtain that
\|R_{j}f\|_{p}\leq 2(p^{*}-1)\|f\|_{p}
and
\|R^{G}f\|_{p}\leq 2(p^{*}-1)\|f\|_{p}
.
∎

Remark 4.5
.

(i)

We do not pursue sharper
L^{p}
bounds of Riesz transforms here. In fact, dimension-free bounds are enough for later applications.

(ii)

For any
n,m\in I
,
n>m
, denote
\overrightarrow{R}_{mn}=(0,\cdots,R_{m},R_{m+1},\cdots,R_{n},0,\cdots)
.
For
f\in\mathcal{B}(G)
, by definition one writes
f=\varphi\circ\pi_{\alpha}
for some
\alpha\in\aleph
and
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(G_{\alpha})
. Then if
m\notin I_{\alpha}
, one has
R_{i}f=0
for any
i\geq m
and thus
\overrightarrow{R}_{mn}
is a zero vector and hence

\left\|\overrightarrow{R}_{mn}\varphi\right\|_{p}\longrightarrow 0,\quad\text{ as }m,n\to\infty.

For
f\in L^{p}(G)
, let
\{f_{i}\}
be a sequence from
\mathcal{B}(G)
converging to
f
. Then

\left\|\overrightarrow{R}_{mn}f\right\|_{p}\leq\left\|\overrightarrow{R}_{mn}(f-f_{i})\right\|_{p}+\left\|\overrightarrow{R}_{mn}f_{i}\right\|_{p}\longrightarrow 0,\quad\text{ as }m,n\to\infty\text{ and }i\to\infty.

More generally, if
\mathcal{B}(G)
is dense in any Banach space
(\mathbf{B},\|\cdot\|_{\mathbf{B}})
, then the above conclusion also holds. That is,
\left\|\overrightarrow{R}_{mn}f\right\|_{\mathbf{B}}\to 0
as
m,n\to\infty
.

Similarly, we can prove dimension-free bounds for second order Riesz transforms.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
1<p<\infty
. Then for every
i,j\in I
,

\|R_{i}R_{j}f\|_{p}\leq 2(p^{*}-1)\|f\|_{p},\quad f\in\mathcal{B}(G).

Proof.

Similarly as the proof of Theorem
4.4
, we proceed in two steps.

Step 1:
Let
i,j\in I
. For any
f\in\mathcal{B}(G)
, we claim that

R_{i}R_{j}f(x)=-2S_{i,j}f(x),

(14)

where
S_{i,j}
is a projection operator defined by

S_{i,j}f(x)=\lim_{y_{0}\to+\infty}\mathbf{E}\left(\int_{0}^{\tau}A_{ij}\Big(\frac{\partial}{\partial y},\nabla\Big)^{\intercal}Qf(\beta_{s},B_{s})\cdot(d\beta_{s},dB_{s})|\beta_{0}=y_{0},B_{\tau}=x\right).

(15)

Here
A_{ij}
is an infinite matrix with the entry
a_{i+1,j+1}=1
and otherwise 0.

Let
h\in\mathcal{B}(G)
, then
h(B_{\tau})=Qh(B_{\tau},\beta_{\tau})=M_{\tau}^{h}
.
By Itô’s formula and the Itô isometry,

\displaystyle\int_{G}h(x)\,\mathbf{E}\left(\int_{0}^{\tau}A_{ij}\Big(\frac{\partial}{\partial y},\nabla\Big)^{\intercal}Qf(\beta_{s},B_{s})\cdot(d\beta_{s},dB_{s})|\beta_{0}=y_{0},B_{\tau}=x\right)d\nu(x)

\displaystyle\quad\quad=\mathbf{E}_{y_{0}}\left(h(B_{\tau})\int_{0}^{\tau}A_{ij}\Big(\frac{\partial}{\partial y},\nabla\Big)^{\intercal}Qf(\beta_{s},B_{s})\cdot(d\beta_{s},dB_{s})\right)

\displaystyle\quad\quad=2\int_{G}\int_{0}^{\infty}(y\wedge y_{0})X_{i}Qh(y,x)\,X_{j}Qf(y,x)dyd\nu(x).

Note that
X_{i}
’s commute with the operator
L
and the Poisson semigroup, then

\displaystyle\int_{G}\int_{0}^{\infty}yX_{i}Qh(y,x)\,X_{j}Qf(y,x)dyd\nu(x)

\displaystyle\quad\quad=-\int_{G}h(x)\int_{0}^{\infty}yX_{i}X_{j}e^{-2y\sqrt{L}}f(x)dyd\nu(x)

\displaystyle\quad\quad=-\frac{1}{4}\int_{G}h(x)X_{i}X_{j}L^{-1}f(x)d\nu(x).

We conclude that

\int_{G}h(x)S_{ij}f(x)d\nu(x)=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{G}h(x)X_{i}X_{j}L^{-1}f(x)d\nu(x).

Step 2:

Denote

N_{t}^{ij}\coloneqq\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau}A_{ij}\Big(\frac{\partial}{\partial y},\nabla\Big)^{\intercal}Qf(\beta_{s},B_{s})\cdot(d\beta_{s},dB_{s}),\quad i,j\in I.

By Lemma
4.2
, we note that
N_{t}^{ij}
is differentially subordinate to
M_{t}^{f}
, i.e.,

\langle N^{ij}\rangle_{t}\leq\langle M\rangle_{t}
.
Now applying Lemma
4.1
, one obtains
\|R_{i}R_{j}f\|_{p}\leq 2(p^{*}-1)\|f\|_{p}
.
∎

Remark 4.7
.

Under the same assumption, we will see later in Theorem
4.9
that the heat semigroup also satisfies an
L^{1}
“gradient” bound in terms of the square root of the carré du champs. Besides, we note that by duality, Corollary
3.10
infers the
L^{\infty}
-differentiability of
(H_{t})_{t>0}
with the same
L^{\infty}\to L^{\infty}
bounds.

4.2
Application on gradient bounds of the heat semigroup

The dimension-free bounds of Riesz transforms play an important role throughout the paper. As an illustration of their applications, we introduce the following results on the
L^{p}
gradient bounds of the heat semigroup.

Proposition 4.8
.

Let
1<p<\infty
. Then there exists a constant
K>0
(independent of
p
) such that for any
f\in\mathcal{B}(G)
,

\||DH_{t}f|_{L}\|_{p}\leq 2K\sqrt{p^{*}}(p^{*}-1)t^{-1/2}\|f\|_{p},

(16)

where
|Df|_{L}=|\Gamma(f,f)|^{\frac{1}{2}}
is defined in (
5
).

Proof.

Let
f\in\mathcal{B}(G)
. We apply the
L^{p}
boundedness of the vector Riesz transform to
H_{t}f
, then

\||DH_{t}f|_{L}\|_{p}\leq 2(p^{*}-1)\left\|\sqrt{L}H_{t}f\right\|_{p}.

Next the multiplicative inequality (see
[
18
, Section 2.6]
) implies that

\left\|\sqrt{L}H_{t}f\right\|_{p}\leq K\left\|LH_{t}f\right\|_{p}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\|H_{t}f\right\|_{p}^{\frac{1}{2}},

(17)

where the constant
K
doesn’t depend on
p
. Since
H_{t}
is a symmetric Markov semigroup, it is bounded analytic on
L^{p}
with

t\|LH_{t}f\|_{p}\leq p^{*}\|f\|_{p}.

We conclude the proof by combining the above estimates.
∎

Theorem 4.9
.

Assume that
H_{t}
is ultracontractive as in (
8
). Let
p=1
or
\infty
. Then for any
f\in\mathcal{B}(G)

\||DH_{t}f|_{L}\|_{p}\leq Ct^{-1/2}\max\{M_{0}(t)^{\frac{3}{2}},1\}\|f\|_{p}.

In particular, if
(\mu_{t})_{t>0}
satisfies
(\mathrm{CK}\lambda)
, then

\||DH_{t}f|_{L}\|_{p}\leq Ct^{-1/2}\max\{t^{-\frac{3}{2}\lambda},1\}\|f\|_{p}.

Proof.

We use similar argument as in the proof of Theorem
3.8
. For
f\in\mathcal{B}(G)
,
let us start the estimate of
\||DH_{t}f|_{L}\|_{1}
by using Jensen’s inequality and semigroup property. Then for any
q\in(1,\infty)

\||DH_{t}f|_{L}\|_{1}\leq\||DH_{t}f|_{L}\|_{q}=\||DH_{t/2}H_{t/2}f|_{L}\|_{q}\leq\||DH_{t/2}|_{L}\|_{q\to q}\|H_{t/2}f\|_{q}.

Since
H_{t}
is ultracontractivity, we apply the Riesz-Thorin interpolation theorem and obtain

\|H_{t/2}f\|_{q}\leq e^{M_{0}(t/2)(1-\frac{1}{q})}\|f\|_{1},\quad\forall t>0.

(18)

Hence it follows from Proposition
4.8
that for
0<t<2

\||DH_{t}f|_{L}\|_{1}\leq 2K\sqrt{q^{*}}(q^{*}-1)\left(\frac{t}{2}\right)^{-1/2}e^{M_{0}(t/2)(1-\frac{1}{q})}\|f\|_{1}.

We pick
q
in the same way as the proof of Theorem
3.8
. That is, take
q=2
if
M_{0}(t/2)<2
, and otherwise take
q
as a function of
t
such that
1-\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{M_{0}(t/2)}
and
q^{\prime}=M_{0}(t/2)
. In this case,

\sqrt{q^{*}}(q^{*}-1)\simeq\{M_{0}(t/2)^{\frac{3}{2}},1\}.

Then we obtain

\||DH_{t}|_{L}\|_{1\to 1}\leq Ct^{-\frac{1}{2}}\max\{M_{0}(t)^{\frac{3}{2}},1\}.

Finally, if
(\mu_{t})_{t>0}
satisfies
(\mathrm{CK}\lambda)
, then
H_{t}
is ultracontractive with
M_{0}(t)=\frac{A}{t^{\lambda}}
and the conclusion easily follows.

Next we move to the proof of the
L^{\infty}
case. For
f\in\mathcal{B}(G)
, recall that

|DH_{t}f|_{L}\leq CH_{t}|Df|_{L},

see for instance
[
3
, Theorem 3.3.18]
. It then follows from (
11
) and Proposition
4.8
that

\displaystyle\||DH_{t}f|_{L}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\|H_{t/2}|DH_{t/2}f|_{L}\|_{\infty}\leq\|H_{t/2}\|_{q\to\infty}\||DH_{t/2}f|_{L}\|_{q}

\displaystyle\leq 2K\sqrt{q^{*}}(q^{*}-1)\left(\frac{t}{2}\right)^{-1/2}e^{M_{0}(t/2)/q}\|f\|_{q}.

Note that
\nu
is normalized and hence
\|f\|_{q}\leq\|f\|_{\infty}
. Also, we pick
q
properly, as in the case
p=1
. More precisely, take
q=2
if
M_{0}(t/2)<2
, and otherwise take
q=M_{0}(t/2)
so
q^{*}=q
. Then we conclude the estimate.
∎

5
Lipschitz spaces

In this section, we introduce two types of Lipschitz spaces, whose seminorms are defined through the Markov semigroup and the (intrinsic) distance respectively. These spaces play an important role in studying the regularity problem for the Poisson equation in Section 6.

5.1
Lipschitz spaces via the heat semigroup

Recall that, unless otherwise stated, we are under the framework of Assumption
2.1
so the associated Markov semigroup
(H_{t})_{t>0}
is contractive on
L^{p}(G,\nu)
for
1\leq p\leq\infty
and bounded analytic on
L^{p}(G,\nu)
for
1<p<\infty
.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
0<\theta<2n
. Let
1\leq p\leq\infty
. For
f\in\mathcal{B}(G)
, the Lipschitz norm
\Lambda_{\theta,n}^{p}(f)
associated with the heat semigroup is given by

\Lambda_{\theta,n}^{p}(f)=\sup_{t>0}t^{n-\frac{\theta}{2}}\left\|\frac{\partial^{n}}{\partial t^{n}}H_{t}f\right\|_{p}.

(19)

In particular, we denote
\Lambda_{\theta,1}^{p}(f)
by
\Lambda_{\theta}^{p}(f)
for
0<\theta<1
.

Let
\eta>0
and
0<\theta<2\eta
. Let
1\leq p\leq\infty
. For
f\in\mathcal{B}(G)
, the Lipschitz norm
\Lambda_{\theta,\eta}^{p}(f)
associated with the heat semigroup is given by

\Lambda_{\theta,\eta}^{p}(f)=\sup_{t>0}t^{\eta-\frac{\theta}{2}}\left\|L^{\eta}H_{t}f\right\|_{p}.

(20)

Remark 5.2
.

(i)

For
1<p<\infty
, it follows from the
L^{p}
analyticity of the heat semigroup that

t^{n-\frac{\theta}{2}}\left\|\frac{\partial^{n}}{\partial t^{n}}H_{t}f\right\|_{p}\leq Ct^{-\frac{\theta}{2}}\|f\|_{p},\quad t\geq 1.

The above estimate applies also to
p=1
and
\infty
under the assumption
(\mathrm{CK\lambda})
, thanks to (
12
) in Corollary
3.10
. Hence, in order to show
\Lambda_{\theta,n}^{p}(f)<\infty
, it suffices to justify

\sup_{0<t<1}t^{n-\frac{\theta}{2}}\left\|\frac{\partial^{n}}{\partial t^{n}}H_{t}f\right\|_{p}<\infty.

(ii)

Let
1\leq p<\infty
. Since the measure
\nu
is normalized, we have
\Lambda_{\theta,n}^{p}(f)\leq\Lambda_{\theta,n}^{\infty}(f)
.

(iii)

Let
0<\theta<\beta<2n
. We have
\Lambda_{\theta,n}^{p}(f)\leq\Lambda_{\beta,n}^{p}(f)
for
1\leq p\leq\infty
.
Indeed, assume that
\Lambda_{\beta,n}^{p}(f)<\infty
. Then for
0<t<1
,

t^{n-\frac{\theta}{2}}\left\|\frac{\partial^{n}}{\partial t^{n}}H_{t}f\right\|_{p}=t^{\frac{\beta-\theta}{2}}t^{n-\frac{\beta}{2}}\left\|\frac{\partial^{n}}{\partial t^{n}}H_{t}f\right\|_{p}\leq t^{n-\frac{\beta}{2}}\left\|\frac{\partial^{n}}{\partial t^{n}}H_{t}f\right\|_{p}<\infty

and we conclude from the first remark above.

Lemma 5.3
.

Suppose that
\theta>0
and
\eta,\tau>\theta/2
.
Let
1<p<\infty
, then
\Lambda_{\theta,\eta}^{p}(f)<\infty
if and only if
\Lambda_{\theta,\tau}^{p}(f)<\infty
.

Proof.

Let
n
be the smallest integer larger than
\theta/2
. It is enough to prove that, for any
\eta>\theta/2
(
\eta\neq n
),
\Lambda_{\theta,\eta}^{p}(f)<\infty
if and only if
\Lambda_{\theta,n}^{p}(f)<\infty
. In the following, we will divide the proof into three steps.

Step 1:
\eta
is an integer.
It suffices to prove for
\eta=n+1
. Suppose that
\Lambda_{\theta,n}^{p}(f)<\infty
. The
L^{p}
analyticity of the heat semigroup gives that

\begin{split}t^{n+1-\frac{\theta}{2}}\left\|\frac{\partial^{n+1}}{\partial t^{n+1}}H_{t}f\right\|_{p}&=t^{n-\frac{\theta}{2}}t\left\|LH_{t/2}L^{n}H_{t/2}f\right\|_{p}\\
&\leq 2p^{*}t^{n-\frac{\theta}{2}}\left\|L^{n}H_{t/2}f\right\|_{p}\leq 2^{n+1-\frac{\theta}{2}}p^{*}\Lambda_{\theta,n}^{p}(f).\end{split}

(21)

Thus one has
\Lambda_{\theta,n+1}^{p}(f)<\infty
by taking the supremum over
t>0
on the left hand side.

Conversely, assume that
\Lambda_{\theta,n+1}^{p}(f)<\infty
. Since the heat semigroup converges to equilibrium, we have that

\frac{\partial^{n}}{\partial t^{n}}H_{t}f=H_{t}(-L)^{n}f\longrightarrow\int_{G}(-L)^{n}fd\nu=0,\quad\text{ as }t\to\infty

(22)

holds pointwise.
Hence one can write

\frac{\partial^{n}}{\partial t^{n}}H_{t}f=-\int_{t}^{\infty}\frac{\partial^{n+1}}{\partial s^{n+1}}H_{s}fds.

(23)

It follows that

\begin{split}t^{n-\frac{\theta}{2}}\left\|\frac{\partial^{n}}{\partial t^{n}}H_{t}f\right\|_{p}&\leq t^{n-\frac{\theta}{2}}\int_{t}^{\infty}\left\|\frac{\partial^{n+1}}{\partial s^{n+1}}H_{s}f\right\|_{p}ds\\
&\leq t^{n-\frac{\theta}{2}}\int_{t}^{\infty}s^{\frac{\theta}{2}-(n+1)}s^{n+1-\frac{\theta}{2}}\left\|\frac{\partial^{n+1}}{\partial s^{n+1}}H_{s}f\right\|_{p}ds<\infty\end{split}

(24)

and we conclude the proof.

Step 2:
\eta
is not an integer and
\eta<n
.
We note that
n<\eta+1
. If
\Lambda_{\theta,\eta}^{p}(f)<\infty
, the
L^{p}
analyticity of the heat semigroup implies that, for all
t>0
,

t^{n-\frac{\theta}{2}}\left\|\frac{\partial^{n}}{\partial t^{n}}H_{t}f\right\|_{p}=t^{n-\frac{\theta}{2}}\left\|L^{n-\eta}H_{t/2}L^{\eta}H_{t/2}f\right\|_{p}\leq Ct^{\eta-\frac{\theta}{2}}\left\|L^{\eta}H_{t/2}f\right\|_{p}<\infty.

Hence one has
\Lambda_{\theta,n}^{p}(f)<\infty
.

Conversely, if
\Lambda_{\theta,n}^{p}(f)<\infty
, then the same argument as above deduces that

\Lambda_{\theta,\eta+1}^{p}(f)<\infty.

Similarly as in the proof of Step 1, we also have

L^{\eta}H_{t}f=-\int_{t}^{\infty}\frac{\partial}{\partial s}L^{\eta}H_{s}fds.

Therefore using the Minkowski inequality, one gets for every
t>0

\displaystyle t^{\eta-\frac{\theta}{2}}\left\|L^{\eta}H_{t}f\right\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq t^{\eta-\frac{\theta}{2}}\int_{t}^{\infty}\left\|\frac{\partial}{\partial s}L^{\eta}H_{s}f\right\|_{p}ds

\displaystyle\leq t^{\eta-\frac{\theta}{2}}\int_{t}^{\infty}s^{\frac{\theta}{2}-\eta-1}s^{\eta+1-\frac{\theta}{2}}\left\|L^{\eta+1}H_{s}f\right\|_{p}ds

\displaystyle\leq Ct^{\eta-\frac{\theta}{2}}\int_{t}^{\infty}s^{\frac{\theta}{2}-\eta-1}ds\,\sup_{r>0}r^{\eta+1-\frac{\theta}{2}}\left\|L^{\eta+1}H_{r}f\right\|_{p}.

Since
\eta>\theta/2
, the above equation is finite and we thus show
\Lambda_{\theta,\eta}^{p}(f)<\infty
.

Step 3:
\eta
is not an integer and
\eta>n
.
Observe that there exists
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
n+k>\eta
. If
\Lambda_{\theta,n}^{p}(f)<\infty
, then the
L^{p}
analyticity of the heat semigroup gives that

\Lambda_{\theta,\eta}^{p}(f)\leq C\Lambda_{\theta,n}^{p}(f)<\infty.

On the other hand, assume
\Lambda_{\theta,\eta}^{p}(f)<\infty
. It follows from Step 1 and the
L^{p}
analyticity of the heat semigroup that

\Lambda_{\theta,n}^{p}(f)\leq C\Lambda_{\theta,n+k}^{p}(f)\leq C\Lambda_{\theta,\eta}^{p}(f)<\infty.

The proof is therefore complete.
∎

Definition 5.4
.

Let
\theta>0
. Let
1<p<\infty
. Suppose that
n
is the smallest integer strictly greater than
\theta/2
. We define the space
\Lambda_{\theta}^{p}(G)
as the completion of the set of functions
f\in\mathcal{B}(G)
such that

\Lambda_{\theta,n}^{p}(f)=\sup_{t>0}t^{n-\frac{\theta}{2}}\left\|\frac{\partial^{n}}{\partial t^{n}}H_{t}f\right\|_{p}<\infty

(25)

with respect to the
\Lambda_{\theta}^{p}
norm given by

\|f\|_{\Lambda_{\theta}^{p}}:=\|f\|_{p}+\Lambda_{\theta,n}^{p}(f).

For simplicity, we denote the seminorm
\Lambda_{\theta,n}^{p}(f)
by
\Lambda_{\theta}^{p}(f)
.

Proposition 5.5
.

For
1<p<\infty
and
\theta\geq 0
,
\Lambda_{\theta}^{p}(G)
is a Banach space.

Proof.

Let
\{f_{i}\}_{i}
be a sequence of Cauchy sequence in
\Lambda_{\theta}^{p}(G)
and let
f
be the
L^{p}
limit of
f_{i}
. For any
n>\theta/2
, the
L^{p}
analyticity of the heat semigroup gives that

t^{n}\left\|\frac{\partial^{n}}{\partial t^{n}}H_{t}(f-f_{i})\right\|_{p}\leq C\|f-f_{i}\|_{p}.

Then by the triangle inequality, one has

\lim_{i\to\infty}t^{n}\left\|\frac{\partial^{n}}{\partial t^{n}}H_{t}f_{i}\right\|_{p}=t^{n}\left\|\frac{\partial^{n}}{\partial t^{n}}H_{t}f\right\|_{p}
. Hence

t^{n-\frac{\theta}{2}}\left\|\frac{\partial^{n}}{\partial t^{n}}H_{t}f\right\|_{p}=\lim_{i\to\infty}t^{n-\frac{\theta}{2}}\left\|\frac{\partial^{n}}{\partial t^{n}}H_{t}f_{i}\right\|_{p}\leq\lim_{i\to\infty}\Lambda_{\theta,n}^{p}(f_{i})<\infty.

We conclude that
\Lambda_{\theta,n}^{p}(f)<\infty
and
f\in\Lambda_{\theta}^{p}(G)
. Similarly, for each fixed positive integer
j
, there holds

\Lambda_{\theta,n}^{p}(f-f_{j})\leq\lim_{i\to\infty}\Lambda_{\theta,n}^{p}(f_{i}-f_{j}).

Taking the limit
j\to\infty
and recalling that
\{f_{i}\}
is Cauchy, the proof is completed.
∎

Now we discuss the analogues of Lemma
5.3
for
p=1,\infty
. These cases are more complicated, due to the lack of
L^{1}
and
L^{\infty}
analyticity.
Recall the Lipschitz norms
\Lambda_{\theta,n}^{\infty}
and
\Lambda_{\theta,n}^{1}
in Definition
5.1
.
Our main statement is as follows.

Lemma 5.6
.

Assume that
\mu_{t}
satisfies property
(\mathrm{CK\lambda})
. Let
\theta>0
and
p=1
or
\infty
. Let
n>\frac{\theta}{2}
be an integer. Suppose that
f\in\mathcal{B}(G)
.

(i)

If
\Lambda_{\theta+2\lambda,n}^{p}(f)<\infty
, then
\Lambda_{\theta,n+1}^{p}(f)<\infty
. In particular, if property
(\mathrm{CK}0^{+})
holds, then
\Lambda_{\theta+\varepsilon,n}^{p}(f)<\infty
for any
\varepsilon>0
implies that
\Lambda_{\theta,n+1}^{p}(f)<\infty
.

(ii)

If
\Lambda_{\theta,n+1}^{p}(f)<\infty
, then
\Lambda_{\theta,n}^{p}(f)<\infty
.

Proof.

For the proof of (i), consider first the case
p=\infty

by assuming that
\Lambda_{\theta,n}^{\infty}(f)<\infty
. For any
1<q<\infty
, we observe from (
11
) and the
L^{q}
analyticity of
H_{t}
in (
7
) that

\displaystyle t\left\|L^{n+1}H_{t}f\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle=t\left\|H_{t/2}L^{n+1}H_{t/2}f\right\|_{\infty}\leq te^{M_{0}(t/2)/q}\left\|L^{n+1}H_{t/2}f\right\|_{q}

\displaystyle\leq 4q^{*}e^{M_{0}(t/2)/q}\left\|L^{n}H_{t/2}f\right\|_{q}.

One takes
q>1
as a function of
t\in(0,1)
such that
M_{0}(t/2)/q\simeq 1
and
q^{*}\simeq t^{-\lambda}
. Since
\nu
is normalized, then
\left\|L^{n}H_{t/2}f\right\|_{q}\leq\left\|L^{n}H_{t/2}f\right\|_{\infty}
. We then have

\sup_{0<t<1}t^{n+1-\frac{\theta}{2}}\left\|L^{n+1}H_{t}f\right\|_{\infty}\leq C\sup_{0<t<1}t^{n-\frac{\theta+2\lambda}{2}}\left\|L^{n}H_{t}f\right\|_{\infty}<\infty,

where the constant
C
is independent of the choice
q
. In view of Remark
5.2
(i), we conclude
\Lambda_{\theta,n+1}^{\infty}(f)<\infty
.

Next considering the case
p=1
, we assume
\Lambda_{\theta,n}^{1}(f)<\infty
and apply (
18
), then

\displaystyle t\left\|L^{n+1}H_{t}f\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq t\left\|L^{n+1}H_{t}f\right\|_{q}\leq q^{*}\left\|L^{n}H_{t}f\right\|_{q}

\displaystyle\leq q^{*}e^{M_{0}(t/2)(1-\frac{1}{q})}\left\|L^{n}H_{t/2}f\right\|_{1}.

Taking
q>1
as a function of
t\in(0,1)
such that
M_{0}(t)(1-\frac{1}{q})=1
and
q^{*}\simeq t^{-\lambda}
, we then conclude
\Lambda_{\theta,n+1}^{1}(f)<\infty
in a similar way as for the case
p=\infty
.

It remains to prove (ii) and we also start with the case
p=\infty
. In light of (
22
), we still have
\frac{\partial^{n}}{\partial t^{n}}H_{t}f\to 0
in
L^{\infty}
. Hence one can run the same proof in the second part of Step 1 for Lemma
5.3
. The case
p=1
follows also analogously as for
p=\infty
.

∎

Riesz transforms on Lipschitz spaces

Now we summarize some results regarding the boundedness of Riesz transforms with respect to the Lipschitz seminorm
\Lambda_{\theta,n}^{p}(\cdot)
. Those results will be useful later in the study of the regularity for the solution of the Poisson equations.

Proposition 5.7
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and let
0<\theta<2n
. Consider
1<p<\infty
. Then the following hold for
f\in\mathcal{B}(G)
:

(i)

\Lambda_{\theta,n}^{p}(R_{i}f)\leq 2(p^{*}-1)\Lambda_{\theta,n}^{p}(f)
, for every
i\in I
.

(ii)

\Lambda_{\theta,n}^{p}(R_{i}R_{j}f)\leq 2(p^{*}-1)\Lambda_{\theta,n}^{p}(f)
, for every
i,j\in I
.

(iii)

\Lambda_{\theta,n}^{p}\left(\overrightarrow{R}_{mn}f\right)\to 0
as
m,n\to\infty
.

Proof.

Assume first
1<p<\infty
. The two results (i) and (ii) are direct consequences of Theorems
4.4
and
4.6
. Item (iii) follows from Remark
4.5
(ii) and the fact that
\Lambda_{\theta}^{p}(G)
is Banach (see Proposition
5.5
).
∎

Proposition 5.8
.

Assume that
\mu_{t}
satisfies property
(\mathrm{CK\lambda})
for some
0<\lambda<1
. Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and let
0<\theta<2n
. Consider
p=1
or
\infty
. Then the following hold for
f\in\mathcal{B}(G)
:

(i)

\Lambda_{\theta,n}^{p}(R_{i}f)\leq C\Lambda_{\theta+2\lambda,n}^{p}(f)
, for every
i\in I
. In particular, if property
(\mathrm{CK}0^{+})
is satisfied, then

\Lambda_{\theta,n}^{p}(R_{i}f)\leq C\Lambda_{\theta+\varepsilon,n}^{p}(f)
for any
\varepsilon>0
.

(ii)

\Lambda_{\theta,n}^{p}(R_{i}R_{j}f)\leq C\Lambda_{\theta+2\lambda,n}^{p}(f)
, for every
i,j\in I
. In particular, if property
(\mathrm{CK}0^{+})
is satisfied, then

\Lambda_{\theta,n}^{p}(R_{i}R_{j}f)\leq C\Lambda_{\theta+\varepsilon,n}^{p}(f).

Proof.

We consider the case
p=\infty
. The argument is similar as in the proof of Lemma
5.6
. For any
1<q<\infty
and
i\in I
, it follows from the ultracontractivity of
H_{t}
in (
11
) and Theorem
4.4
that

\left\|L^{n}H_{t}R_{i}f\right\|_{\infty}\leq e^{M_{0}(t/2)/q}\left\|L^{n}H_{t/2}R_{i}f\right\|_{q}\leq 4q^{*}e^{M_{0}(t/2)/q}\left\|L^{n}H_{t/2}f\right\|_{q}.

One takes
q>1
as a function of
t\in(0,1)
such that
M_{0}(t/2)/q\simeq 1
and
q^{*}\simeq t^{-\lambda}
.
We then have

\sup_{0<t<1}t^{n-\frac{\theta}{2}}\left\|L^{n}H_{t}R_{i}f\right\|_{\infty}\leq C\sup_{0<t<1}t^{n-\frac{\theta+2\lambda}{2}}\left\|L^{n}H_{t}f\right\|_{\infty}<\infty,

Remark
5.2
(i) then gives that
\Lambda_{\theta,n}^{\infty}(R_{j}f)\leq C\Lambda_{\theta+2\lambda,n}^{\infty}(f)
.

The proof for (ii) is almost the same verbatim, except that one applies Theorem
4.6
instead of Theorem
4.4
.

∎

5.2
Lipschitz spaces via distance

In this subsection, we assume that
G
is equipped with a left-invariant distance
d
. Later, this distance
d
will often be the intrinsic distance associated with the Laplacian
L
(see Definition
3.4
).

Set

\tau_{h}f(x)=f(xh),\quad\Delta_{h}^{0}f(x)=f(x).

For any integer
k\geq 0
, we define

\Delta_{h}^{k+1}f(x)=\Delta_{h}^{k}\tau_{h}f(x)-\Delta_{h}^{k}f(x).

(26)

In particular, we write
\Delta_{h}f\coloneqq\Delta_{h}^{1}f=f(xh)-f(x)
for simplicity.

Definition 5.9
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
0<\theta<k
. Let
1\leq p\leq\infty
.
For
f\in\mathcal{B}(G)
, the Lipschitz norm
\mathrm{L}_{\theta,k}^{p}(f)
associated with the distance
d
is given by

\mathrm{L}_{\theta,k}^{p}(f)=\sup_{y\in G}\frac{\|\Delta_{y}^{k}f\|_{p}}{d(e,y)^{\theta}}.

(27)

Remark 5.10
.

Similarly as in Remark
5.2
(iii), let any
0<\theta<\beta<k
, then we have that
\mathrm{L}_{\theta,k}^{p}(f)\leq L_{\beta,k}^{p}(f)
for any
1\leq p\leq\infty
.

Lemma 5.11
.

Let
1\leq p\leq\infty
. For any
0<\theta<k\leq\ell
, there exist constants
c,C>0
depending on
\theta,k
and
\ell
such that for any
f\in\mathcal{B}(G)
,

c\,\mathrm{L}_{\theta,\ell}^{p}(f)\leq\mathrm{L}_{\theta,k}^{p}(f)\leq C\,\mathrm{L}_{\theta,\ell}^{p}(f).

Proof.

For any fixed
k\in\mathbb{N}
, it suffices to consider the case
\ell=k+1
. We drop the reference to
\theta
and
p
which are fixed. Obviously, since
\Delta_{h}^{k+1}f(x)=\Delta_{h}^{k}\tau_{h}f(x)-\Delta_{h}^{k}f(x)
, it follows that
\mathrm{L}_{k+1}(f)\leq 2\mathrm{L}_{k}(f)
.

For the other direction, note that

\Delta_{h^{2}}=\tau_{h}^{2}-I=(\tau_{h}-1)(\tau_{h}+1)=\Delta_{h}(2I+\Delta_{h}).

Hence for
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

\Delta^{k}_{h^{2}}=\Delta^{k}_{h}(2I+\Delta_{h})^{k}=2^{k}\Delta_{h}^{k}+\Delta^{k+1}_{h}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\tau_{h}^{j}.

It follows that

2^{k}\Delta_{h}^{k}f(x)=\Delta^{k}_{h^{2}}f(x)-\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\Delta_{h}^{k+1}\tau_{h^{j}}f(x).

This gives

2^{k}\|\Delta^{k}_{h}f\|_{p}\leq\|\Delta^{k}_{h^{2}}f\|_{p}+(k-1)\|\Delta_{h}^{k+1}f\|_{p}.

Dividing by
d(e,h)^{\theta}
and using the fact that
d(e,h^{2})\leq 2d(e,h)
, we get

2^{k}\frac{\|\Delta^{k}_{h}f\|_{p}}{d(e,h)^{\theta}}\leq 2^{\theta}\frac{\|\Delta^{k}_{h^{2}}f\|_{p}}{(2d(e,h))^{\theta}}+k\frac{\|\Delta_{h}^{k+1}f\|_{p}}{d(e,h)^{\theta}}\leq 2^{\theta}\frac{\|\Delta^{k}_{h^{2}}f\|_{p}}{d(e,h^{2})^{\theta}}+k\frac{\|\Delta_{h}^{k+1}f\|_{p}}{d(e,h)^{\theta}}.

Taking the supremum over
h\in G
, this gives

(2^{k}-2^{\theta})\,\mathrm{L}_{k}(f)\leq k\,\mathrm{L}_{k+1}(f).

The third author learned this argument from an unpublished manuscript of Carl Herz from around 1970.
∎

Definition 5.12
.

Let
\theta>0
. Let
1\leq p\leq\infty
. Suppose that
k
is the smallest integer strictly greater than
\theta
. We define the space
\mathrm{L}_{\theta}^{p}(G)
as the completion of the set of functions
f\in\mathcal{B}(G)
such that
\mathrm{L}_{\theta,k}^{p}(f)<\infty

with respect to the
\mathrm{L}_{\theta}^{p}
norm given by

\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}_{\theta}^{p}}:=\|f\|_{p}+\mathrm{L}_{\theta,k}^{p}(f).

For simplicity, we denote the seminorm
\mathrm{L}_{\theta,k}^{p}(f)
by
\mathrm{L}_{\theta}^{p}(f)
.

5.3
Comparison of Lipschitz seminorms: the case
1<p<\infty

In this subsection, we compare the two seminorms
\mathrm{L}_{\theta}^{p}(f)
and
\Lambda_{\beta}^{p}(f)
for
1<p<\infty
with appropriate choice of
\theta
and
\beta
. Here in the seminorm
\mathrm{L}_{\theta}^{p}(f)
the distance

will be referred as the intrinsic distance associated to the Laplacian

.
In the following result, we assume
(\mathrm{CK}\lambda)
for some
\lambda\in(0,1)
and the proof also applies to
p=1,\infty
with slight modification.

Theorem 5.13
.

Let
1\leq p\leq\infty
and let
\theta\in(0,2)
. Assume that
\mathrm{L}_{\theta}^{p}(f)<\infty
.

(i)

If
(\mu_{t})_{t>0}
satisfies
(\mathrm{CK}\lambda)
with
0<\lambda<\frac{\theta}{\theta+2}
, then
\Lambda_{\beta}^{p}(f)<\infty
for
\beta\in(0,1)
such that
\beta\leq(1-\lambda)\theta-2\lambda
. In particular, if
(\mu_{t})_{t>0}
satisfies
(\mathrm{CK}0^{+})
, then
\Lambda_{\beta}^{p}(f)<\infty
for
0<\beta<\min\{\theta,1\}
.

(ii)

If
\theta>1
and
(\mu_{t})_{t>0}
satisfies
(\mathrm{CK}\lambda)
with
0<\lambda<\frac{\theta-1}{\theta+4}
, then
\Lambda_{\beta,2}^{p}(f)<\infty
for
1\leq\beta\leq(1-\lambda)\theta-4\lambda
. In particular, if
(\mu_{t})_{t>0}
satisfies
(\mathrm{CK}0^{+})
, then
\Lambda_{\beta,2}^{p}(f)<\infty
for
1\leq\beta<\theta
.

Proof.

Some ideas in the argument below come from
[
19
,
20
]
.
Let
\mathrm{L}_{\theta}^{p}(f)<\infty
. We note that
\int_{G}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}h_{t}(x)d\mu(x)=0
. Then

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}H_{t}f(x)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{G}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}h_{t}(y)(f(xy)+f(xy^{-1})-2f(x))d\nu(y)

and hence

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{\partial}{\partial t}H_{t}f\right\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{G}\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial t}h_{t}(y)\right|\,\left\|\Delta_{y^{-1}}\Delta_{y}f\right\|_{p}d\nu(y)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{G}d(e,y)^{\theta}\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial t}h_{t}(y)\right|d\nu(y)\,\mathrm{L}_{\theta,2}^{p}(f).

(i)

Assume that
(\mu_{t})_{t>0}
satisfies
(\mathrm{CK}\lambda)
with
0<\lambda<\frac{\theta}{\theta+2}
. Letting
0<\beta<1
, we aim to show that
\Lambda_{\beta}^{p}(f)<\infty
. In view of Remark
5.2
, it suffices to justify that

\sup_{0<t<1}t^{1-\frac{\beta}{2}}\int_{G}d(e,y)^{\theta}\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial t}h_{t}(y)\right|d\nu(y)<\infty.

(28)

We divide the integral in (
28
) for
\{y:d(e,y)\leq\zeta t^{\frac{1-\lambda}{2}}\}
and
\{y:d(e,y)\geq\zeta t^{\frac{1-\lambda}{2}}\}
, where
\zeta
is a constant to be determined later. By the
L^{1}
-differentiability (
12
), one has

\displaystyle t^{1-\frac{\beta}{2}}\int_{d(e,y)\leq\zeta t^{\frac{1-\lambda}{2}}}d(e,y)^{\theta}\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial t}h_{t}(y)\right|d\nu(y)

\displaystyle\leq Ct^{\frac{(1-\lambda)\theta-\beta}{2}+1}\int_{G}\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial t}h_{t}(y)\right|d\nu(y)

\displaystyle\leq Ct^{\frac{(1-\lambda)\theta-\beta}{2}-\lambda}<\infty,\quad\forall 0<t<1

provided
\frac{(1-\lambda)\theta-\beta}{2}-\lambda\geq 0
, i.e.,
\beta\leq(1-\lambda)\theta-2\lambda
.

Next applying Theorem
3.5
(see
[
6
, Theorem 4.2]
), we obtain that for any
\alpha,\beta>0
,

\displaystyle t^{1-\frac{\beta}{2}}\int_{d(e,y)\geq\zeta t^{\frac{1-\lambda}{2}}}d(e,y)^{\theta}\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial t}h_{t}(y)\right|d\nu(y)

\displaystyle\quad\leq Ct^{\frac{\theta-\beta}{2}}\int_{d(e,y)\geq\zeta t^{\frac{1-\lambda}{2}}}\left(\frac{d(e,y)^{2}}{t}\right)^{\theta/2}\exp\left(\frac{A}{t^{\lambda}}-\frac{d(e,y)^{2}}{Ct}\right)d\nu(y)

\displaystyle\quad\leq Ct^{\frac{\theta-\beta}{2}}\exp\left(-\frac{A^{\prime}}{t^{\lambda}}\right)<\infty,\quad\forall 0<t<1

where for the second inequality we choose
\zeta
large enough such that
\zeta^{2}/C-A=A^{\prime}>0
.

To summarize, (
28
) holds for
\beta\in(0,1)
such that
\beta\leq(1-\lambda)\theta-2\lambda
. Therefore, there exists a constant
C>0
such that
\Lambda_{\beta}^{p}(f)\leq C\,\mathrm{L}_{\theta}^{p}(f)
and we complete the proof.

(ii)

Assume that
\alpha>1
and
(\mu_{t})_{t>0}
satisfies
(\mathrm{CK}\lambda)
with
0<\lambda<\frac{\theta-1}{\theta+4}
. Consider now
\beta\in[1,2)
. Similarly as case (i), we have

\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}H_{t}f(x)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{G}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}h_{t}(y)(f(xy)+f(xy^{-1})-2f(x))d\nu(y).

So it is enough to show that

\sup_{0<t<1}t^{2-\frac{\beta}{2}}\int_{G}d(e,y)^{\theta}\left|\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}h_{t}(y)\right|d\nu(y)<\infty

by estimating two integrals over
\{y:d(e,y)\leq\zeta t^{\frac{1-\lambda}{2}}\}
and
\{y:d(e,y)\geq\zeta t^{\frac{1-\lambda}{2}}\}
. The latter follows exactly the same way as above. For the former, we apply Corollary
3.10
and obtain that for
1\leq\beta\leq(1-\lambda)\theta-4\lambda
,

\displaystyle t^{2-\frac{\beta}{2}}\int_{d(e,y)\leq\zeta t^{\frac{1-\lambda}{2}}}d(e,y)^{\theta}\left|\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}h_{t}(y)\right|d\nu(y)

\displaystyle\leq Ct^{\frac{(1-\lambda)\theta-\beta}{2}+2}\int_{G}\left|\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}h_{t}(y)\right|d\nu(y)

\displaystyle\leq Ct^{\frac{(1-\lambda)\theta-\beta}{2}-2\lambda}<\infty,\quad\forall 0<t<1.

∎

Now we turn to the proof of the reverse direction, which uses semigroup property and is not restricted to the assumption
(\mathrm{CK}\lambda)
. We start with the following
L^{p}
-Poincaré inequality.

Lemma 5.14
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
. For any
f\in L^{p}(G)\cap W^{1,p}(G)
and
y\in G
, we have

\int_{G}|f(xy)-f(x)|^{p}d\nu(x)\leq d(e,y)^{p}\int_{G}\Gamma(f,f)^{\frac{p}{2}}(z)d\nu(z).

If
p=\infty
, then for
f\in L^{\infty}(G)\cap\mathrm{Dom}(\mathcal{E})
and
y\in G
, we have

\|\Delta_{y}f\|_{\infty}\leq d(e,y)\|\Gamma(f,f)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|_{\infty}.

Proof.

Since the space of Bruhat test functions is dense in
L^{p}(G)\cap W^{1,p}(G)
, it suffices to prove the statement for any
f\in\mathcal{B}(G)
. Write
f=\phi_{\alpha}\circ\pi_{\alpha}
,
where
\phi_{\alpha}
is smooth on
G_{\alpha}=G/K_{\alpha}
. We have

|f(xy)-f(x)|\leq\int_{0}^{d_{\alpha}(e_{\alpha},\pi_{\alpha}(y))}\Gamma_{\alpha}(\phi_{\alpha},\phi_{\alpha})(\pi_{\alpha}(x)\gamma^{\alpha}_{y}(s))^{\frac{1}{2}}ds,

where
\gamma^{\alpha}_{y}:[0,d_{\alpha}(e_{\alpha},\pi_{\alpha}(y))]\to G_{\alpha}
is a path from
e_{\alpha}
to
\pi_{\alpha}(y)
such that

\left|\frac{d}{ds}\phi_{\alpha}(\pi_{\alpha}(x)\gamma^{\alpha}_{y}(s))\right|\leq\Gamma_{\alpha}(\phi_{\alpha},\phi_{\alpha})(\pi_{\alpha}(x)\gamma^{\alpha}_{y}(s))^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Indeed, one has

\frac{d}{ds}\phi_{\alpha}(\pi_{\alpha}(x)\gamma^{\alpha}_{y}(s))=\sum_{i}a_{i}X_{\alpha,i}\phi_{\alpha}(\pi_{\alpha}(x)\gamma^{\alpha}_{y}(s)),

(29)

where
a_{i}
is a sequence of normalized coefficients so
\sum_{i}a_{i}^{2}<1
.

To see this, we introduce
X_{\alpha,i}=d\pi_{\alpha}(X_{i})
(only finitely many of those are non-zero) which form a Hörmander system on
G_{\alpha}
with associated distance
d_{\alpha}
. Observe that

\Gamma(f,f)=\sum_{i}|X_{\alpha,i}\phi_{\alpha}|^{2}\circ\pi_{\alpha}=\Gamma_{\alpha}(\phi_{\alpha},\phi_{\alpha})\circ\pi_{\alpha}.

Then using the existence of the path
\gamma_{y}^{\alpha}\in G_{\alpha}
and the classical inequality on
G_{\alpha}
, we have

|\phi_{\alpha}(\pi_{\alpha}(x)\pi_{\alpha}(y))-\phi_{\alpha}(\pi_{\alpha}(x))|\leq\int_{0}^{d_{\alpha}(e_{\alpha},\pi_{\alpha}(y))}\left|\sum_{i}|X_{\alpha,i}\phi_{\alpha}|^{2}(\pi_{\alpha}(x)\gamma^{\alpha}_{y}(s))\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}ds.

(30)

Now integrating in
x
over
G
, we get from the right-invariance of
\nu
that

\int_{G}|f(xy)-f(x)|^{p}d\nu(x)\leq d_{\alpha}(e_{\alpha},\pi_{\alpha}(y))^{p}\int_{G}\Gamma(f,f)^{\frac{p}{2}}(z)d\nu(z).

Recall from (
2
) that

d(e,y)=\sup_{\alpha}d_{\alpha}(e_{\alpha},\pi_{\alpha}(y)).

So, we get for any
f\in\mathcal{B}(G)

\int_{G}|f(xy)-f(x)|^{p}d\nu(x)\leq d(e,y)^{p}\int_{G}\Gamma(f,f)^{\frac{p}{2}}(z)d\nu(z).

For the case
p=\infty
, the estimate can also be obtained in a similar way.
∎

Lemma 5.15
.

For any
f\in\mathcal{B}(G)
, we have

\Gamma(H_{t}f,H_{t}f)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\int_{t}^{\infty}\Gamma(\partial_{s}H_{s}f,\partial_{s}H_{s}f)^{\frac{1}{2}}ds.

Proof.

We first claim that

\Gamma(H_{t}f,H_{t}f)^{\frac{1}{2}}=-\int_{t}^{\infty}\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\Gamma(H_{s}f,H_{s}f)^{\frac{1}{2}}ds.

Indeed, by the
L^{2}
analyticity of
H_{t}
, one has
LH_{\infty}f=0
and hence
\Gamma(H_{\infty}f,H_{\infty}f)=0
. Then the standard
\Gamma
-calculus gives

\displaystyle\Gamma(H_{t}f,H_{t}f)^{\frac{1}{2}}=-\int_{t}^{\infty}\Gamma(H_{s}f,H_{s}f)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Gamma(H_{s}f,\partial_{s}H_{s}f)ds.

By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we obtain

|\Gamma(H_{s}f,\partial_{s}H_{s}f)|\leq\Gamma(H_{s}f,H_{s}f)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Gamma(\partial_{s}H_{s}f,\partial_{s}H_{s}f)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

The conclusion then follows.
∎

Theorem 5.16
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and
0<\theta<1
. If
\Lambda_{\theta}^{p}(f)<\infty
, then
\mathrm{L}_{\theta}^{p}(f)<\infty
.

Proof.

Assume that
\Lambda_{\theta}^{p}(f)<\infty
, we write

f(xy)-f(x)=f(xy)-H_{t}f(xy)+H_{t}f(xy)-H_{t}f(x)+H_{t}f(x)-f(x).

Let us start with the estimate of
\|H_{t}f-f\|_{p}
. Observe that

H_{t}f(x)-f(x)=\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\partial}{\partial s}H_{s}f(x)ds,

then

\|H_{t}f-f\|_{p}\leq\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\partial_{s}H_{s}f\right\|_{p}ds\leq t^{\theta/2}\Lambda_{\theta}^{p}(f).

(31)

Next, in view of Lemma
5.14
and Lemma
5.15
, we have

\displaystyle\|\Delta_{y}H_{t}f\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq d(e,y)\left\|\Gamma(H_{t}f,H_{t}f)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{p}\leq d(e,y)\left\|\int_{t}^{\infty}\Gamma(\partial_{s}H_{s}f,\partial_{s}H_{s}f)^{\frac{1}{2}}ds\right\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq d(e,y)\int_{t}^{\infty}\left\|\Gamma(\partial_{s}H_{s}f,\partial_{s}H_{s}f)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{p}ds,

where we have used the Minkowski inequality in the last line.

Now we apply the
L^{p}
boundedness of the vector Riesz transform (see Theorem
4.4
) and the
L^{p}
analyticity of the heat semigroup, then

\displaystyle\left\|\Gamma(\partial_{s}H_{s}f,\partial_{s}H_{s}f)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq 2(p^{*}-1)\left\|\sqrt{L}\partial_{s}H_{s}f\right\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq 4(p^{*}-1)\left\|\sqrt{L}H_{s/2}\partial_{s}H_{s/2}f\right\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq 4K\sqrt{p^{*}}(p^{*}-1)s^{-1/2}\left\|\partial_{s}H_{s/2}f\right\|_{p},

where
K
is as in the proof of Corollary
4.8
.

Combining the above estimates, we obtain that for
0<\theta<1

\left\|\Delta_{y}H_{t}f\right\|_{p}\leq Cd(e,y)\int_{t}^{\infty}s^{-1/2}\left\|\partial_{s}H_{s/2}f\right\|_{p}ds\leq Cd(e,y)t^{\frac{\theta-1}{2}}\Lambda_{\theta}^{p}(f).

(32)

This estimate, together with (
31
), implies that for any
y\in G
and
1<p<\infty

\displaystyle\frac{\|\Delta_{y}f\|_{p}}{d(e,y)^{\theta}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\|H_{t}f-f\|_{p}+\|\Delta_{y}H_{t}f\|_{p}}{d(e,y)^{\theta}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\frac{t^{\frac{\theta}{2}}}{d(e,y)^{\theta}}+\frac{t^{\frac{\theta-1}{2}}}{d(e,y)^{\theta-1}}\right)\Lambda_{\theta}^{p}(f).

Letting
t=d(e,y)^{\frac{1}{2}}
yields that
\frac{\|\Delta_{y}f\|_{p}}{d(e,y)^{\theta}}\leq C\Lambda_{\theta}^{p}(f)
and we conclude the proof.
∎

5.4
Comparison of Lipschitz seminorms: the case
p=1,\infty

This section focuses on the comparison of Lipschitz norms for
p=1,\infty
. We have seen that Theorem
5.13
holds for
1\leq p\leq\infty
. It remains to prove that
\mathrm{L}_{\beta}^{p}(f)\leq C\Lambda_{\theta}^{p}(f)
for
p=1,\infty
, and proper choices of
\theta
and
\beta
.

Theorem 5.17
.

Assume that
(\mu_{t})_{t>0}
satisfies
(\mathrm{CK}\lambda)
. Let
0<\theta<1
and let
p=1
or
\infty
. If
\Lambda_{\theta}^{p}(f)<\infty
, then
\mathrm{L}_{\beta}^{p}(f)<\infty
for
\beta=\frac{\theta}{1+3\lambda}
.

Proof.

Assume that
\Lambda_{\theta}^{1}(f)<\infty
. We use the same strategy as in Theorem
5.16
by writing

f(xy)-f(x)=f(xy)-H_{t}f(xy)+H_{t}f(xy)-H_{t}f(x)+H_{t}f(x)-f(x).

(33)

Note first that

\|H_{t}f-f\|_{1}\leq\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\frac{\partial}{\partial s}H_{s}f\right\|_{1}ds\leq t^{\theta/2}\Lambda_{\theta}^{1}(f).

(34)

Similarly as equation (
32
), one obtains from Lemma
5.14
and Lemma
5.15
that

\left\|\Delta_{y}H_{t}f\right\|_{1}\leq Cd(e,y)\int_{t}^{\infty}\left\|\Big|D\Big(\frac{\partial}{\partial s}H_{s}f\Big)\Big|_{L}\right\|_{1}ds.

Applying Theorem
4.9
, then

\left\|\Big|D\Big(\frac{\partial}{\partial s}H_{s}f\Big)\Big|_{L}\right\|_{1}=\frac{1}{2}\left\|\Big|DH_{s/2}\Big(\frac{\partial}{\partial s}H_{s/2}f\Big)\Big|_{L}\right\|_{1}\leq Cs^{-\frac{1}{2}}\max\{s^{-\frac{3}{2}\lambda},1\}\left\|\frac{\partial}{\partial s}H_{s/2}f\right\|_{1}.

We thus have for
0<t<1

\left\|\Delta_{y}H_{t}f\right\|_{1}\leq Cd(e,y)\int_{t}^{\infty}s^{-\frac{1-\theta}{2}}\max\{s^{-\frac{3}{2}\lambda},1\}\frac{ds}{s}\Lambda_{\theta}^{1}(f)\leq Cd(e,y)t^{\frac{\theta-1-3\lambda}{2}}\Lambda_{\theta}^{1}(f).

This estimate, together with (
34
), gives that for any
y\in G
,

\displaystyle\frac{\|\Delta_{y}f\|_{1}}{d(e,y)^{\beta}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\|H_{t}f-f\|_{1}+\|\Delta_{y}H_{t}f\|_{1}}{d(e,y)^{\beta}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\frac{t^{\frac{\theta}{2}}}{d(e,y)^{\beta}}+\frac{t^{\frac{\theta-1-3\lambda}{2}}}{d(e,y)^{\beta-1}}\right)\Lambda_{\theta}^{1}(f).

Let
\beta=\frac{\theta}{1+3\lambda}
and choose
t
to be
d(e,y)^{\frac{2\beta}{\theta}}
, then we obtain
\mathrm{L}_{\beta}^{1}(f)<C\Lambda_{\theta}^{1}(f)
by taking the supremum for
y\in G
.

For
p=\infty
, we still write the difference
f(xy)-f(x)
as in (
33
). It easily follows that

\|H_{t}f-f\|_{\infty}\leq t^{\theta/2}\Lambda_{\theta}^{\infty}(f).

On the other hand, we deduce from Lemma
5.14
, Lemma
5.15
and Theorem
4.9
that

\displaystyle\left\|\Delta_{y}H_{t}f\right\|_{\infty}\leq Cd(e,y)\int_{t}^{\infty}\left\|\Big|D\Big(\frac{\partial}{\partial s}H_{s}f\Big)\Big|_{L}\right\|_{\infty}ds\leq Cd(e,y)t^{\frac{\theta-1-3\lambda}{2}}\Lambda_{\theta}^{\infty}(f).

The rest of the proof is then the same as the case
p=1
and we omit the details.

∎

6
Regularity of solutions

We now consider the regularity problem stated in the introduction. Namely, given a (bi-invariant) Laplacian,
L
, on our compact group
G
and a “weak solution”,
U
, of
LU=F
in an open subset
\Omega
in which
F
is Hölder continuous, can we say something about the regularity of
U
? (e.g., is it continuous?). In order to make this precise, we need a good notion of what “weak solution” means and it is also clear from previous sections that we have at least two possible choices
for what being Hölder continuous means.
In the next subsection, we follow previous work by the first and third authors introducing proper spaces of distributions which provide a clear and useful notion of “distribution solution” for
LU=F
. Then we proceed in studying the regularity problem, first in the context of the semigroup Lipschitz spaces
\Lambda^{p}_{\theta}
and then in the context of the distance Lipschitz spaces
\mathrm{L}^{p}_{\theta}
.

6.1
Spaces of test functions and distributions
\mathcal{T}_{L},\mathcal{T}_{L}^{\,{}^{\prime}}

We will use a space of test function
\mathcal{T}_{L}
and its distribution space
\mathcal{T}_{L}^{\,{}^{\prime}}
introduced in
[
7
,
8
]
.

Recall that we fix a projective family
X=(X_{i})_{i\in I}
of
\mathfrak{G}
and
L=-\sum_{I}X_{i}^{2}
. Fix
k,n\in\mathbb{N}=\{0,1,2,\cdots\}
and consider the set

\Lambda(k,n)=\left\{\lambda=(\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{k});\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{N},\,\sum\lambda_{i}=n\right\}.

For
f\in\mathcal{B}(G)
,
\ell\in I^{k}
,
\lambda\in\Lambda(k,n)
, set

P^{\ell,\lambda}f=L^{\lambda_{0}}X_{\ell_{1}}L^{\lambda_{1}}X_{\ell_{2}}\cdots L^{\lambda_{k-1}}X_{\ell_{k}}L^{\lambda_{k}}f.

(35)

Definition 6.1
.

For each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, consider the following seminorm on
\mathcal{B}(G)
:

M_{L}^{k}(f)=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,n\in\mathbb{N}\\
n+2m\leq k\end{subarray}}\sup_{\lambda\in\Lambda(n,m)}\left\|\left(\sum_{\ell\in I^{n}}|P^{\ell,\lambda}f|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\right\|_{\infty}.

We define
\mathcal{T}_{L}^{k}
as the completion of
\mathcal{B}(G)
with respect to the seminorm
M_{L}^{k}
. Moreover, we set
\mathcal{T}_{L}=\bigcap_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{T}_{L}^{k}
and equip it with its natural system of seminorms and the
corresponding topology.

Remark 6.2
.

For an open set
\Omega\subset G
, we set

M_{L}^{k}(\Omega,f)=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,n\in\mathbb{N}\\
n+2m\leq k\end{subarray}}\sup_{\lambda\in\Lambda(n,m)}\sup_{x\in\Omega}\left(\sum_{\ell\in I^{n}}|P^{\ell,\lambda}f(x)|^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

(36)

The space
\mathcal{T}_{L}
is Fréchet space. Denote by
\mathcal{T}_{L}^{\,{}^{\prime}}
the strong topological dual of
\mathcal{T}_{L}
. That is, any element
U\in\mathcal{T}_{L}^{\,{}^{\prime}}
is a linear functional on
\mathcal{T}
such that there exist
m=m(U)\in\mathbb{N}
and
C=C(U)>0
such that

|U(\phi)|\leq CM_{L}^{m}(\phi),\quad\forall\phi\in\mathcal{T}_{L}.

The topology of
\mathcal{T}_{L}^{\,{}^{\prime}}
is defined by the family of seminorms

p_{B}(U)=\sup_{\phi\in B}|U(\phi)|,

where
B
runs over all bounded sets in
\mathcal{T}_{L}
(i.e.,
\sup_{\phi\in B}M_{L}^{k}(\phi)<\infty
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
).

Now we introduce some notation taken from
[
8
]
which will be used later.
The convolution of two functions
u,v\in\mathcal{B}(G)
is defined by

u*v(x)=\int_{G}u(y)v(y^{-1}x)d\nu(y)=\int_{G}u(xy^{-1})v(y)d\nu(y).

For
f\in\mathcal{B}(G)
, the left and right convolution by a measure
\mu
is defined by

\mu*f(x)=\int_{G}f(y^{-1}x)d\mu(y),\quad f*\mu(x)=\int_{G}f(xy^{-1})d\mu(y).

Convolutions of
U\in\mathcal{B}^{\prime}(G)
and
f\in\mathcal{B}(G)
are given by

[U*f](\phi)=U(\check{f}*\phi),\,\,[f*U](\phi)=U(\phi*\check{f}),\quad\forall\phi\in\mathcal{B}(G),

where
\check{f}(x)\coloneqq f(x^{-1})
. Moreover,
U*f
and
f*U
are functions in
\mathcal{B}(G)
and

U*f(x)=U(\mathcal{L}_{x^{-1}}\check{f}),\,\,f*U(\phi)=U(\mathcal{R}_{x^{-1}}\check{f}),

where
\mathcal{L}_{a}
and
\mathcal{R}_{a}
(
a\in G
) are the left and right translation operators defined by

\mathcal{L}_{a}f(x)=f(ax),\quad\mathcal{R}_{a}f(x)=f(xa).

Given a Borel measure
\mu
, define
\check{\mu}
by
\check{\mu}(A)=\mu(A^{-1})
for any Borel set
A
. Under the assumption that
(\mu_{t})_{t>0}
satisfies
(\mathrm{CK})
(see Definition
3.1
), Lemma 3.1 in
[
8
]
shows that the density
x\mapsto\check{\mu}_{t}(x)
with respect to
\nu
belongs to
\mathcal{T}_{L}
. Finally, we finish the subsection with the following useful result.

Proposition 6.3

(
[
7
]
,
[
8
, Proposition 2.7]
)
.

Assume that
L
is bi-invariant.

(i)

Let
U\in\mathcal{T}_{L}^{\,{}^{\prime}}
and
f\in\mathcal{T}_{L}
. Then
U*f\subset\mathcal{T}_{L}
and

X^{\ell}L^{n}[U*f]=U*[X^{\ell}L^{n}f].

(ii)

Let
\phi_{\epsilon},\epsilon>0
be a family of functions in
\mathcal{T}_{L}
such that
\phi_{\epsilon}\to\delta_{e}
as
\epsilon
tends to zero. Then for any
U\in\mathcal{T}_{L}^{\,{}^{\prime}}
, the distribution
U^{\epsilon}=U*\phi_{\epsilon}
is represented by a function in
\mathcal{T}_{L}
, and

\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}U^{\epsilon}(\psi)=U(\psi),\quad\forall\psi\in\mathcal{T}_{L}.

6.2
Regularity for
\mathcal{L}_{p}
solutions

In this subsection, we will prove a priori regularity results of the Poisson equation
Lu=f
.
We first introduce a Sobolev-type space
\mathcal{L}_{p}
,
1\leq p\leq\infty
, where the solution is assumed to sit in.

Definition 6.4
.

For
1\leq p\leq\infty
, we define
\mathcal{L}_{p}(G)
as the completion of the set

\{u\in\mathcal{B}(G);\|u\|_{p}+\|Lu\|_{p}<\infty\}

with respect to the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{L}_{p}}
given by

\|u\|_{\mathcal{L}_{p}}\coloneqq\|u\|_{p}+\|Lu\|_{p}
.

We point out that

(i)

For
1<p<\infty
,
\mathcal{L}_{p}(G)
is the domain of the
L^{p}
-generator of
H_{t}
.

(ii)

For
p=\infty
,
\mathcal{L}_{\infty}(G)
is
the set of continuous functions
u
such that
Lu
is also continuous.

In what follows, denote

f=Lu,\quad u\in\mathcal{L}_{p}(G)

(37)

so
f\in L^{p}(G)
and
\int_{G}fd\nu=0
. In this case, we say that

, where

L_{0}^{p}(G)\coloneqq\left\{f\in L^{p}(G);\int_{G}fd\nu=0\right\}.

Now we state the following a priori regularity result for the Poisson equation.

Theorem 6.5
.

For
1<p<\infty
, let
u\in\mathcal{L}_{p}(G)
.
Then
X_{i}u,X_{i}X_{j}u\in L^{p}(G)
. Moreover,
-\sum_{i\in I}X_{i}^{2}u
converges strongly in
L^{p}(G)
.

The proof relies on the following lemma, which is also useful in the sequel.

Lemma 6.6
.

Let
\delta>0
and let
1<p<\infty
.

(i)

For
f\in L^{p}(G)
, there holds
\|(I+L)^{-\delta}f\|_{p}\leq\|f\|_{p}
. In particular,

\|\sqrt{L}(I+L)^{-1}f\|_{p}\leq C\sqrt{p^{*}}\|f\|_{p},

where the constant
C
is independent of
p
.

(ii)

Assume that
L
has a positive
L^{2}
-spectral gap
\lambda_{1}
. Then for
f\in L_{0}^{p}(G)

\|L^{-\delta}f\|_{p}\leq\left(\frac{p^{*}}{2\lambda_{1}}\right)^{\delta}\|f\|_{p},

where

p^{*}=\max\{p,\frac{p}{p-1}\}
.

Remark 6.7
.

As a simple consequence of (i), we also have

\|L(I+L)^{-1}f\|_{p}\leq\|f\|_{p}+\|(I+L)^{-1}f\|_{p}\leq 2\|f\|_{p}.

Besides, it follows from Remark
3.2
that the property
(\mathrm{CK})
implies the assumption of positive
L^{2}
-spectral gap in (ii).

Proof.

We first prove (i). Note that

(I+L)^{-\delta}f=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\delta)}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\delta-1}e^{-t}H_{t}fdt
. It follows from the
L^{p}
contractivity of the heat semigroup that

\|(I+L)^{-\delta}f\|_{p}\leq\frac{1}{\Gamma(\delta)}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\delta-1}e^{-t}\|H_{t}f\|_{p}dt\leq\|f\|_{p}.

In the same manner, one gets

\|\sqrt{L}(I+L)^{-1}f\|_{p}\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t}\|\sqrt{L}H_{t}f\|_{p}dt\leq\sqrt{\pi}K\sqrt{p^{*}}\|f\|_{p},

where we have used the multiplicative inequality (
17
) and the
L^{p}
analyticity of
H_{t}
in the second inequality.

Next turning to the proof of (ii). From the assumption, for any
f\in L_{0}^{2}(G)
we have

\|H_{t}f\|_{2}\leq e^{-\lambda_{1}t}\|f\|_{2}.

(38)

On the other hand, the
L^{1}
contractivity of
H_{t}
gives that
\|H_{t}f\|_{1}\leq\|f\|_{1}
. Thus applying the Riesz-Thorin interpolation theorem, we have for
1<p<2

\|H_{t}f\|_{p}\leq e^{-2\lambda_{1}(1-\frac{1}{p})t}\|f\|_{p}.

Now write
L^{-\delta}f=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\delta)}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\delta-1}H_{t}fdt
. Then for
1<p<2

\displaystyle\|L^{-\delta}f\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\Gamma(\delta)}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\delta-1}\|H_{t}f\|_{p}dt\leq\frac{1}{\Gamma(\delta)}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\delta-1}e^{-2\lambda_{1}(1-\frac{1}{p})t}dt\,\|f\|_{p}

\displaystyle=(2\lambda_{1})^{-\delta}\left(\frac{p}{p-1}\right)^{\delta}\|f\|_{p}.

Similarly, in view of (
38
) and the
L^{\infty}
contractivity of
H_{t}
, then the Riesz-Thorin interpolation theorem gives that for
2<p<\infty

\|H_{t}f\|_{p}\leq e^{-\frac{2\lambda_{1}}{p}t}\|f\|_{p}

and therefore

\|L^{-\delta}f\|_{p}\leq\frac{1}{\Gamma(\delta)}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\delta-1}e^{-\frac{2\lambda_{1}}{p}t}dt\,\|f\|_{p}=(2\lambda_{1})^{-\delta}p^{\delta}\|f\|_{p}.

We thus conclude the proof by summarizing the two cases.
∎

Proof of Theorem

.

For
u\in\mathcal{L}_{p}(G)
, we write

. Hence
f\in L^{p}(G)

Recall that by Theorem

we have for any

Now let

6.4

Given an open set
\Omega

be the set of all smooth cylindric functions with support in

We consider the Poisson equation in the distribution sense, i.e., two distributions

satisfy

In this subsection, our main goal is to establish local Lipschitz regularity results for the weak solution of the Poisson equation. As a non-technical illustration, we obtain the following result (see Proposition

for a more concrete version).

Proposition 6.14
.

Assume that

\mathcal{T}_{L}^{\,{}^{\prime}}

with respect to the seminorm

for all

Proposition 6.15

(ii)

\Omega_{2}\Subset\Omega_{1}
such that
\mathrm{L}_{\theta}^{\infty}(X_{i}\widetilde{U}-X_{i}f,\Omega_{2})<\infty
. We conclude that
\mathrm{L}_{\frac{\beta-4\lambda}{1+3\lambda}}^{\infty}(X_{i}\widetilde{U},\Omega_{2})<\infty
by noticing
\frac{\beta-4\lambda}{1+3\lambda}<\theta
.
The same proof with slight changes also deduces that
\mathrm{L}_{\frac{\beta-4\lambda}{1+3\lambda}}^{\infty}(X_{i}X_{j}\widetilde{U},\Omega_{2})<\infty
for any
i,j\in I
.

(ii)

Now we show the convergence following the idea in the proof of Proposition
6.15
(iii). Recall the equation (
47
):

L\widetilde{\raisebox{0.0pt}[0.85pt]{$\widetilde{U}$}}=L(\eta_{2}\widetilde{U})=\eta_{2}L\widetilde{U}+\widetilde{U}L\eta_{2}-2\nabla\eta_{2}\cdot\nabla\widetilde{U}\coloneqq g,

where we take a cutoff function
\eta_{2}\in\mathcal{B}_{0}(\Omega_{3})
such that
\eta_{2}=1
on
\Omega_{4}\Subset\Omega_{3}
for
\Omega_{3}\Subset\Omega_{2}
, and
\widetilde{\raisebox{0.0pt}[0.85pt]{$\widetilde{U}$}}=\eta_{2}\widetilde{U}
.

We first check that the three terms of
g
are bounded with respect to
\mathrm{L}_{\frac{\beta-4\lambda}{1+3\lambda}}^{\infty}(\cdot)
. The first term is simple as we observe that
\eta_{2}L\widetilde{U}=\eta_{2}\widetilde{F}=\eta_{2}F
. Similarly as the estimate in (
51
), one has

\displaystyle\sup_{x,y\in G}\frac{|(\eta_{2}F)(x)-(\eta_{2}\widetilde{F})(y)|}{d(x,y)^{\theta}}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{x,y\in G}|\eta_{2}(x)|\frac{|F(x)-F(y)|}{d(x,y)^{\theta}}+\sup_{x,y\in G}|F(y)|\frac{|\eta_{2}(x)-\eta_{2}(y)|}{d(x,y)^{\theta}}

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{L}_{\theta}^{\infty}(F,\Omega)+\sup_{x\in\Omega_{3},y\in G\setminus\Omega}\frac{|F(x)-F(y)|}{d(x,y)^{\theta}}+C\|F\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{L}_{\theta}^{\infty}(F,\Omega)+C\|F\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}.

Since we assume
F\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
and
\mathrm{L}_{\theta}^{\infty}(F;\Omega)<\infty
, it is immediate to conclude that
\mathrm{L}_{\theta}^{\infty}(\eta_{2}F)<\infty
and hence
\mathrm{L}_{\frac{\beta-4\lambda}{1+3\lambda}}^{\infty}(\eta_{2}L\widetilde{U})<\infty
from Remark
5.10
.

For the second term, on the one hand the proof of Proposition
6.16
gives that
\mathrm{L}_{\theta}^{\infty}(\widetilde{U},\Omega_{1})<\infty
. We also noticed that
\widetilde{U}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega_{1})
comes from the hypoellipticity of
L
established in
[
8
, Section 3]
. Then the same proof as above gives that
\mathrm{L}_{\frac{\beta-4\lambda}{1+3\lambda}}^{\infty}(\widetilde{U}L\eta_{2})<\infty
.

Now we move to the last terms in
g
. Since
\eta_{2}\in\mathcal{B}_{0}(\Omega_{3})
, there exists
\alpha\in\aleph
and
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(G_{\alpha})
such that
\eta_{2}=\varphi\circ\pi_{\alpha}
and
\nabla\eta_{2}\cdot\nabla\widetilde{U}=\sum_{i\in I_{\alpha}}X_{i}\eta_{2}X_{i}\widetilde{U}
. It is enough to prove that
\mathrm{L}_{\frac{\beta-4\lambda}{1+3\lambda}}^{\infty}(X_{i}\eta_{2}X_{i}\widetilde{U})<\infty
for each
i\in I_{\alpha}
. We have already shown in (i) that
\mathrm{L}_{\frac{\beta-4\lambda}{1+3\lambda}}^{\infty}(X_{i}\widetilde{U},\Omega_{2})<\infty
for each
i\in I_{\alpha}
. In order to apply the same argument as above, one needs to verify that
X_{i}\widetilde{U}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega_{2})
. Indeed,
in view of (
52
) and the hypoellipticity of
L
(
[
8
, Section 3]
), we get
X_{i}\widetilde{U}-X_{i}f\in L^{\infty}(\Omega_{2})
. Moreover, since
f=L^{-1}\widetilde{F}
, the hypoellipticity also yields
X_{i}f\in L^{\infty}(\Omega_{2})
. We conclude
X_{i}\widetilde{U}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega_{2})
and thus the proof.

Finally, one can repeat the first part of the proof and apply Proposition
6.13
to obtain the convergence result. Namely, denote
\theta^{\prime}=\frac{\beta-4\lambda}{1+3\lambda}
, then
-\sum_{i\in I}X_{i}^{2}\widetilde{\raisebox{0.0pt}[0.85pt]{$\widetilde{U}$}}
converges to
L\widetilde{\raisebox{0.0pt}[0.85pt]{$\widetilde{U}$}}
with respect to the seminorm
\mathrm{L}_{\frac{\beta^{\prime}-4\lambda}{1+3\lambda}}^{\infty}(\cdot)
for
4\lambda<\beta^{\prime}<(1-\lambda)\theta^{\prime}-2\lambda
and the assumption that
\lambda<\frac{\theta}{24+\theta}
guarantees the existence of positive
\beta^{\prime}
. The proof is completed by noticing that
\widetilde{\raisebox{0.0pt}[0.85pt]{$\widetilde{U}$}}=U
on
\Omega_{4}
.

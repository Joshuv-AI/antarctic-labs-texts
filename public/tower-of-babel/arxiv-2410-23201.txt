Some addition theorems for spin-weighted spherical harmonics
Alessandro Monteverdi, Elizabeth Winstanley

Abstract
We present some addition theorems for spin-weighted spherical harmonics, generalizing previous results for scalar (spin-zero) spherical harmonics. These addition theorems involve sums over the azimuthal quantum number of products of two spin-weighted spherical harmonics at different points on the two-sphere, either (or both) of which are differentiated with respect to one of their arguments.

II
Physical applications of SWSH addition theorems

SWSH typically arise in physical applications when one is considering a quantity (such as a field perturbation) which depends on spherical polar coordinates.
Let us give a few examples:

Data analysis

Data from surveys such as those of the cosmic microwave background
[
6
]
or satellite gravity gradiometry
[
10
]
is naturally defined on a two-sphere.
An expansion of observables in terms of SWSH facilitates computationally-efficient methods of analyzing such data
[
8
,
7
]
.

Modelling anisotropies

When physical systems are not spherically symmetric, an expansion in SWSH provides a powerful framework for the study of anisotropies, for example in fluid turbulence
[
17
]
or the cosmic microwave background
[
5
]
, since the coefficients of the SWSH in such an expansion correspond to excitations of a particular multipole
[
18
,
11
]
.

Perturbations of black holes

Classical field equations on black hole backgrounds play an important role many aspects of general relativity, including the modelling of gravitational waves.
If the background black hole is spherically symmetric, then separable mode solutions of the classical field equation involve SWSH (see, for example,
[
14
]
).

Our focus in this paper is addition theorems for
SWSH ​.

The theorems of the type we consider involve a product of two SWSH at different points over the two-sphere, summed over one of the quantum numbers of the SWSH (see Sec.
III
for more precise details).
Such products of two SWSH at different points arise, for example, when one is interested in the Green function for a classical field equation on a flat or curved space-time.
A Green function is a distributional solution of a classical field equation which depends on two points in space-time.
In applications, it is often useful to have the Green function written as a sum over separable mode solutions of the field equation, see, for example,
[
11
]
.
When the mode solutions involve SWSH ​, the Green function therefore involves a product of SWSH at different points on the two-sphere, and the quantum numbers in the SWSH label the field modes and are summed over.
Addition theorems of the type considered in this paper are particularly useful in situations where the background on which the field propagates (such as a black hole space-time) is spherically symmetric, in which case the functions other than the SWSH in the field modes do not depend on one of the quantum numbers (in particular, the azimuthal quantum number
m
as defined in Sec.
III
) in the SWSH ​.
In this case, an addition theorem involving a sum over
m
enables the Green function to be simplified.

We close this section by giving more details of an application of our addition theorems for SWSH in quantum field theory in curved space-time
[
27
,
28
,
29
]
, in particular the computation of expectation values of observables such as the stress-energy tensor.
The stress-energy tensor expectation value is of central importance in quantum field theory on curved space-time, since it governs, via the semiclassical Einstein equations, the backreaction of the quantum field on the curved space-time.
To find the stress-energy tensor expectation value, one starts with the Green function for the classical equation of the particular field under consideration (which, as explained above, involves products of SWSH at different points on the two-sphere).
One then applies a second-order differential operator to the Green function, with the result that one or more derivatives act on the SWSH in the sum.
If one considers a field of nonzero spin on a four-dimensional black hole (see, for example,
[
28
]
), the resulting expectation values involve sums over SWSH of precisely the form we prove here (see Corollary
11
in Sec.
IV
below) for a particular value of the spin-weight, while a scalar field on a particular five-dimensional black hole
[
29
]
has a tower of modes involving SWSH of arbitrary (integer or half-integer) spin, with the result that we require the addition theorems in Corollary
11
for general spin-weight.

Having motivated the SWSH addition theorems which we will derive in this paper, in the next section we review the salient properties of SWSH ​, before turning to the addition theorems themselves.

III
Properties of SWSH

The SWSH
{}_{s}Y_{\ell}^{m}(\theta,\phi)
are functions on the two-sphere
{\mathbb{S}}^{2}
which have spin-weight
s
, where
s
is an integer or half-integer
[
1
,
2
]
.
We employ the usual spherical polar coordinates
(\theta,\phi)
on
{\mathbb{S}}^{2}
, with
\theta\in[0,\pi]
and
\phi\in[0,2\pi)
.
The polar angle
\theta
is the angle between the vector joining the point on the surface of
{\mathbb{S}}^{2}
to the origin and the
z
-axis; while
\phi
is the azimuthal angle in the
(x,y)
-plane, so that a point on
{\mathbb{S}}^{2}
has Cartesian coordinates
(\sin\theta\cos\phi,\sin\theta\sin\phi,\cos\theta)
.

The SWSH depend on three quantum numbers
s
,
\ell
and
m
.
The spin
s
is a positive or negative integer or half-integer.
The orbital angular momentum quantum number
\ell
then takes values
\ell=|s|,|s|+1,|s|+2,\ldots
, and the azimuthal quantum number
m
takes the values
m=-\ell,-\ell+1,\ldots\ell-1,\ell
.

There are a number of different definitions of the SWSH in the literature. In this work, we follow the conventions of
[
15
]
, in particular:

Definition 1
.

The SWSH
{}_{s}Y_{\ell}^{m}(\theta,\phi)
are defined in terms of the Wigner
D
-matrices by
[
15
]

{}_{s}Y_{\ell}^{m}(\theta,\phi)=\left(-1\right)^{s}{\sqrt{\frac{2\ell+1}{4\pi}}}D^{\ell*}_{m,-s}(\phi,\theta,0),

(1)

where
*
denotes complex conjugation.
The Wigner
D
-matrices take the form
[
15
]

D^{\ell*}_{m,-s}(\phi,\theta,\chi)={\rm{e}}^{{\rm{i}}m\phi}d_{m,-s}^{\ell}(\theta){\rm{e}}^{-{\rm{i}}s\chi}

(2)

where
d_{m,-s}^{\ell}(\theta)
are real functions given by
[
15
]

d_{m,-s}^{\ell}(\theta)=\frac{(-1)^{\ell+m}}{2^{\ell}}{\sqrt{\frac{\left(\ell-m\right)!}{\left(\ell+m\right)!\left(\ell+s\right)!\left(\ell-s\right)!}}}\\
\times\left(1-\cos\theta\right)^{\frac{1}{2}\left(m+s\right)}\left(1+\cos\theta\right)^{\frac{1}{2}\left(m-s\right)}\\
\times\left(\frac{d}{d\left[\cos\theta\right]}\right)^{\ell+m}\left[\left(1-\cos\theta\right)^{\ell-s}\left(1+\cos\theta\right)^{\ell+s}\right].

(3)

Remark
.

Our results in this paper are valid for both integer and half-integer spins
s
. When
s
is a half-integer, the factor of
(-1)^{s}
in (
1
) will be purely imaginary (see, for example,
[
24
]
for some specific examples of SWSH in this case).
The quantities
\ell\pm m
,
\ell\pm s
and
m\pm s
appearing in the Wigner
D
-matrices (
3
) are always integers.

Our derivation of new addition theorems rests on the action of the operators
{}_{s}\eth\,
and
{}_{s}{\overline{\eth}}\,
, as defined below.

Definition 2
.

The operators
{}_{s}\eth\,
and
{}_{s}{\overline{\eth}}\,
act on the SWSH as follows
[
1
,
2
,
15
]
:

\displaystyle{}_{s}\eth\,{}_{s}Y_{\ell}^{m}(\theta,\phi)

\displaystyle=-\left[\partial_{\theta}+\frac{{\rm{i}}}{\sin\theta}\partial_{\phi}-s\cot\theta\right]{}_{s}Y_{\ell}^{m}(\theta,\phi),

(4a)

\displaystyle{}_{s}{\overline{\eth}}\,{}_{s}Y_{\ell}^{m}(\theta,\phi)

\displaystyle=-\left[\partial_{\theta}-\frac{{\rm{i}}}{\sin\theta}\partial_{\phi}+s\cot\theta\right]{}_{s}Y_{\ell}^{m}(\theta,\phi).

(4b)

Remark
.

To avoid confusion, we denote by
{}_{s^{\prime}}\eth\,^{\prime}
,
{}_{s^{\prime}}{\overline{\eth}}\,^{\prime}
the operators
{}_{s}\eth\,
and
{}_{s}{\overline{\eth}}\,
with
(\theta,\phi)
replaced by
(\theta^{\prime},\phi^{\prime})
and with spin
s^{\prime}
instead of
s
.

Proposition 3
.

The operators
{}_{s}\eth\,
,
{}_{s}{\overline{\eth}}\,
respectively increase and decrease the spin-weight
s
of an SWSH
{}_{s}Y_{\ell}^{m}
by one
[
1
,
2
,
15
,
16
]
:

\displaystyle{}_{s}\eth\,{}_{s}Y_{\ell}^{m}

\displaystyle={}_{s+1}Y_{\ell}^{m}{\sqrt{(\ell-s)(\ell+s+1)}},

(5a)

\displaystyle{}_{s}{\overline{\eth}}\,{}_{s}Y_{\ell}^{m}

\displaystyle=-{}_{s-1}Y_{\ell}^{m}{\sqrt{(\ell+s)(\ell-s+1)}}.

(5b)

The following Lemma (which follows straightforwardly from Definition
2
) will be central to the derivation of our addition theorems.

Lemma 4
.

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}{}_{s}Y_{\ell}^{m}(\theta,\phi)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\left[{}_{s}\eth\,+{}_{s}{\overline{\eth}}\,\right]{}_{s}Y_{\ell}^{m}(\theta,\phi),

(6a)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\phi}{}_{s}Y_{\ell}^{m}(\theta,\phi)

\displaystyle=\frac{{\rm{i}}}{2}\sin\theta\left[{}_{s}\eth\,-{}_{s}{\overline{\eth}}\,\right]{}_{s}Y_{\ell}^{m}(\theta,\phi)

\displaystyle\qquad-{\rm{i}}s\cos\theta{}_{s}Y_{\ell}^{m}(\theta,\phi).

(6b)

The well-known usual addition theorem
[
5
,
12
,
15
,
16
]
for SWSH depends on the Euler angles
\alpha
,
\beta
,
\gamma
, defined below.

Definition 5
.

The Euler angles
\alpha
,
\beta
,
\gamma
are given in terms of angles
(\theta,\phi)
and
(\theta^{\prime},\phi^{\prime})
as follows
[
15
]
:

\displaystyle\cot\alpha

\displaystyle=\cos\theta\cot\left(\phi-\phi^{\prime}\right)-\cot\theta^{\prime}\sin\theta\csc\left(\phi-\phi^{\prime}\right),

(7a)

\displaystyle\cos\beta

\displaystyle=\cos\theta\cos\theta^{\prime}+\sin\theta\sin\theta^{\prime}\cos\left(\phi-\phi^{\prime}\right),

(7b)

\displaystyle\cot\gamma

\displaystyle=\cos\theta^{\prime}\cot\left(\phi-\phi^{\prime}\right)-\cot\theta\sin\theta^{\prime}\csc\left(\phi-\phi^{\prime}\right).

(7c)

Remark
.

The definitions (
7
) are, strictly speaking, valid only when
\phi-\phi^{\prime}
is not a multiple of
\pi
.
In the coincidence limit
\theta^{\prime}\rightarrow\theta
,
\phi^{\prime}\rightarrow\phi
, taking the appropriate limit of (
7
) gives
\alpha=\beta=\gamma=0
.

We are now in a position to state the usual addition theorem for SWSH ​​.

Proposition 6
.

The addition theorem for spin-weighted spherical harmonics is
[
15
]
:

\left(-1\right)^{s}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}{}_{s}Y_{\ell}^{m}(\theta,\phi){}_{s^{\prime}}Y_{\ell}^{m*}(\theta^{\prime},\phi^{\prime})\\
={\sqrt{\frac{2\ell+1}{4\pi}}}{\rm{e}}^{-{\rm{i}}s\alpha}{}_{s}Y_{\ell}^{-s^{\prime}}(\beta,\gamma).

(8)

Remark
.

The addition theorem (
8
) is valid only when the spins
s
,
s^{\prime}
differ by an integer, and
\ell\geq\max\{|s|,|s^{\prime}|\}
. We have written (
8
) in an alternative, but equivalent, form to that displayed in
[
15
]
.
The proof of Theorem
6
in
[
15
]
is presented only for integer spins, but the result is equally valid for half-integer spins and can be straightforwardedly derived using, for example, Definition
1
and the properties of the Wigner
D
-functions
[
15
,
30
]
.
The addition theorem takes a slightly different form in some references
[
5
,
12
]
due to differences in the definition of the SWSH ​​.

To take the coincidence limit
\theta^{\prime}=\theta
,
\phi^{\prime}=\phi
in the addition theorem, we require the coincidence limit of the SWSH ​​:

Lemma 7
.

We have
[
15
]

\displaystyle{}_{s}Y_{\ell}^{m}(0,0)

\displaystyle=\left(-1\right)^{s}\delta_{s,-m}{\sqrt{\frac{2\ell+1}{4\pi}}},

(9)

where
\delta_{s,s^{\prime}}
is the usual Kronecker delta:

\delta_{s,s^{\prime}}=\begin{cases}1&{\mbox{if $s^{\prime}=s$}},\\
0&{\mbox{otherwise}}.\end{cases}

(10)

Corollary 8
.

The coincidence limit
\theta^{\prime}=\theta
,
\phi^{\prime}=\phi
of the addition theorem (
8
) is:

\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}{}_{s}Y_{\ell}^{m}(\theta,\phi){}_{s^{\prime}}Y_{\ell}^{m*}(\theta,\phi)=\frac{2\ell+1}{4\pi}\delta_{s,s^{\prime}}.

(11)

Our purpose in this note is to derive generalizations of Proposition
6
and Corollary
8
which involve a derivative of either (or both) of the SWSH in the sum.

IV
Further addition theorems

We now summarize our results, which will be derived in the following section.
To the best of our knowledge, these have not previously appeared explicitly in the literature.
These results are valid under the same conditions on the quantum numbers
s
,
s^{\prime}
and
\ell
as for the original addition theorem, namely the spins
s
,
s^{\prime}
can only differ by an integer, and it must be the case that
\ell\geq\max\{|s|,|s^{\prime}|\}
.

Proposition 9
.

\displaystyle(-1)^{s}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}{}_{s}Y_{\ell}^{m}(\theta,\phi)\right]{}_{s^{\prime}}Y_{\ell}^{m*}(\theta^{\prime},\phi^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}{\sqrt{\frac{2\ell+1}{4\pi}}}\left\{{\sqrt{\left(\ell-s\right)\left(\ell+s+1\right)}}\,{\rm{e}}^{-{\rm{i}}(s+1)\alpha}{}_{s+1}Y_{\ell}^{-s^{\prime}}(\beta,\gamma)-{\sqrt{\left(\ell+s\right)\left(\ell-s+1\right)}}\,{\rm{e}}^{-{\rm{i}}(s-1)\alpha}{}_{s-1}Y_{\ell}^{-s^{\prime}}(\beta,\gamma)\right\},

(12a)

\displaystyle(-1)^{s}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}m\,{}_{s}Y_{\ell}^{m}(\theta,\phi){}_{s^{\prime}}Y_{\ell}^{m*}(\theta^{\prime},\phi^{\prime})

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}{\sqrt{\frac{2\ell+1}{4\pi}}}\left\{{\sqrt{\left(\ell-s\right)\left(\ell+s+1\right)}}\,{\rm{e}}^{-{\rm{i}}(s+1)\alpha}{}_{s+1}Y_{\ell}^{-s^{\prime}}(\beta,\gamma)\sin\theta\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\left.+{\sqrt{\left(\ell+s\right)\left(\ell-s+1\right)}}\,{\rm{e}}^{-{\rm{i}}(s-1)\alpha}{}_{s-1}Y_{\ell}^{-s^{\prime}}(\beta,\gamma)\sin\theta+2s\,{\rm{e}}^{-{\rm{i}}s\alpha}{}_{s}Y_{\ell}^{-s^{\prime}}(\beta,\gamma)\cos\theta\right\},

(12b)

\displaystyle(-1)^{s}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}{}_{s}Y_{\ell}^{m}(\theta,\phi)\right]\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta^{\prime}}{}_{s^{\prime}}Y_{\ell}^{m*}(\theta^{\prime},\phi^{\prime})\right]

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{4}{\sqrt{\frac{2\ell+1}{4\pi}}}\left[{\sqrt{\left(\ell-s\right)\left(\ell+s+1\right)\left(\ell-s^{\prime}\right)\left(\ell+s^{\prime}+1\right)}}\,{\rm{e}}^{-{\rm{i}}(s+1)\alpha}{}_{s+1}Y_{\ell}^{-s^{\prime}-1}(\beta,\gamma)\right.

\displaystyle\left.\qquad-{\sqrt{\left(\ell-s\right)\left(\ell+s+1\right)\left(\ell+s^{\prime}\right)\left(\ell-s^{\prime}+1\right)}}\,{\rm{e}}^{-{\rm{i}}(s+1)\alpha}{}_{s+1}Y_{\ell}^{-s^{\prime}+1}(\beta,\gamma)\right.

\displaystyle\left.\qquad-{\sqrt{\left(\ell+s\right)\left(\ell-s+1\right)\left(\ell-s^{\prime}\right)\left(\ell+s^{\prime}+1\right)}}\,{\rm{e}}^{-{\rm{i}}(s-1)\alpha}{}_{s-1}Y_{\ell}^{-s^{\prime}-1}(\beta,\gamma)\right.

\displaystyle\left.\qquad+{\sqrt{\left(\ell+s\right)\left(\ell-s+1\right)\left(\ell+s^{\prime}\right)\left(\ell-s^{\prime}+1\right)}}\,{\rm{e}}^{-{\rm{i}}(s-1)\alpha}{}_{s-1}Y_{\ell}^{-s^{\prime}+1}(\beta,\gamma)\right],

(12c)

\displaystyle(-1)^{s}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}m^{2}{}_{s}Y_{\ell}^{m}(\theta,\phi){}_{s^{\prime}}Y_{\ell}^{m*}(\theta^{\prime},\phi^{\prime})

\displaystyle=-{\sqrt{\frac{2\ell+1}{4\pi}}}\bigg\{\frac{1}{4}\sin\theta\sin\theta^{\prime}\left[{\sqrt{\left(\ell-s\right)\left(\ell+s+1\right)\left(\ell-s^{\prime}\right)\left(\ell+s^{\prime}+1\right)}}\,{\rm{e}}^{-{\rm{i}}(s+1)\alpha}{}_{s+1}Y_{\ell}^{-s^{\prime}-1}(\beta,\gamma)\right.

\displaystyle\left.\qquad+{\sqrt{\left(\ell-s\right)\left(\ell+s+1\right)\left(\ell+s^{\prime}\right)\left(\ell-s^{\prime}+1\right)}}\,{\rm{e}}^{-{\rm{i}}(s+1)\alpha}{}_{s+1}Y_{\ell}^{-s^{\prime}+1}(\beta,\gamma)\right.

\displaystyle\left.\qquad+{\sqrt{\left(\ell+s\right)\left(\ell-s+1\right)\left(\ell-s^{\prime}\right)\left(\ell+s^{\prime}+1\right)}}\,{\rm{e}}^{-{\rm{i}}(s-1)\alpha}{}_{s-1}Y_{\ell}^{-s^{\prime}-1}(\beta,\gamma)\right.

\displaystyle\left.\qquad+{\sqrt{\left(\ell+s\right)\left(\ell-s+1\right)\left(\ell+s^{\prime}\right)\left(\ell-s^{\prime}+1\right)}}\,{\rm{e}}^{-{\rm{i}}(s-1)\alpha}{}_{s-1}Y_{\ell}^{-s^{\prime}+1}(\beta,\gamma)\right]

\displaystyle\qquad-\frac{s^{\prime}}{2}\sin\theta\cos\theta^{\prime}\left[{\sqrt{\left(\ell-s\right)\left(\ell+s+1\right)}}\,{\rm{e}}^{-{\rm{i}}(s+1)\alpha}{}_{s+1}Y_{\ell}^{-s^{\prime}}(\beta,\gamma)+{\sqrt{\left(\ell+s\right)\left(\ell-s+1\right)}}\,{\rm{e}}^{-{\rm{i}}(s-1)\alpha}{}_{s-1}Y_{\ell}^{-s^{\prime}}(\beta,\gamma)\right]

\displaystyle\qquad-ss^{\prime}{\rm{e}}^{-{\rm{i}}s\alpha}{}_{s}Y_{\ell}^{-s^{\prime}}(\beta,\gamma)\cos\theta\cos\theta^{\prime}\bigg\},

\displaystyle(-1)^{s}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}m\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}{}_{s}Y_{\ell}^{m}(\theta,\phi)\right]{}_{s^{\prime}}Y_{\ell}^{m*}(\theta^{\prime},\phi^{\prime})

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{4}{\sqrt{\frac{2\ell+1}{4\pi}}}\bigg\{\left[{\sqrt{\left(\ell-s\right)\left(\ell+s+1\right)\left(\ell-s^{\prime}\right)\left(\ell+s^{\prime}+1\right)}}\,{\rm{e}}^{-{\rm{i}}(s+1)\alpha}{}_{s+1}Y_{\ell}^{-s^{\prime}-1}(\beta,\gamma)\right.

\displaystyle\qquad-2s^{\prime}\left[{\sqrt{\left(\ell-s\right)\left(\ell+s+1\right)}}\,{\rm{e}}^{-{\rm{i}}(s+1)\alpha}{}_{s+1}Y_{\ell}^{-s^{\prime}}(\beta,\gamma)-{\sqrt{\left(\ell+s\right)\left(\ell-s+1\right)}}\,{\rm{e}}^{-{\rm{i}}(s-1)\alpha}{}_{s-1}Y_{\ell}^{-s^{\prime}}(\beta,\gamma)\right]\cos\theta^{\prime}\bigg\}.

Remark

\left\{\frac{1}{\sin\theta}\partial_{\theta}\left[\sin\theta\,\partial_{\theta}\right]-\frac{1}{\sin^{2}\theta}\left[s^{2}-2{\rm{i}}s\cos\theta\,\partial_{\phi}-\partial^{2}_{\phi}\right]\right\}{}_{s}Y_{\ell}^{m}(\theta,\phi)=\ell\left[\ell+1\right]{}_{s}Y_{\ell}^{m}(\theta,\phi),

\displaystyle\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}{}_{s}Y_{\ell}^{m}(\theta,\phi)\right]{}_{s^{\prime}}Y_{\ell}^{m*}(\theta,\phi)

\displaystyle=-\frac{2\ell+1}{8\pi}\left\{{\sqrt{\left(\ell-s\right)\left(\ell+s+1\right)}}\,\delta_{s+1,s^{\prime}}-{\sqrt{\left(\ell-s^{\prime}\right)\left(\ell+s^{\prime}+1\right)}}\,\delta_{s,s^{\prime}+1}\right\},

\displaystyle\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}m\,{}_{s}Y_{\ell}^{m}(\theta,\phi){}_{s^{\prime}}Y_{\ell}^{m*}(\theta,\phi)

\displaystyle\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}{}_{s}Y_{\ell}^{m}(\theta,\phi)\right]\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}{}_{s^{\prime}}Y_{\ell}^{m*}(\theta,\phi)\right]

\displaystyle\left.\qquad-{\sqrt{\left(\ell-s-1\right)\left(\ell-s\right)\left(\ell+s+1\right)\left(\ell+s+2\right)}}\,\delta_{s+2,s^{\prime}}\right.

\displaystyle\left.\qquad-{\sqrt{\left(\ell-s^{\prime}-1\right)\left(\ell-s^{\prime}\right)\left(\ell+s^{\prime}+1\right)\left(\ell+s^{\prime}+2\right)}}\,\delta_{s,s^{\prime}+2}\right],

Upper semicontinuous valuations on convex functions of one variable
Fernanda Moreira Baêta

Abstract
. A classification of upper semicontinuous, translation and dually epi-translation invariant valuations is established on the space of convex Lipschitz function on ℝ \mathbb{R} with compact domain.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
52B45, 52A41, 26A51, 26A16

1.
Introduction and statement of the main result

Let
\mathcal{K}^{n}
be the space of
convex bodies
, that is, non-empty compact convex sets in
n
-dimensional Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. A functional
Z:\mathcal{K}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is called a
valuation
if

Z(K\cup L)+Z(K\cap L)=Z(K)+Z(L)

whenever
K,L,K\cup L\in\mathcal{K}^{n}
. The most familiar examples of valuations are the intrinsic volumes
V_{0},\dots,V_{n}
, which include the
n
-dimensional volume,
V_{n}
, the Euler characteristic,
V_{0}
, and the surface area,
2V_{n-1}
. They have useful properties such as: continuity with respect to the Hausdorff metric, invariance under rigid motions, monotonicity and homogeneity (see, for example,
[
6
]
). Valuations have many applications in integral geometry (see
[
19
]
) and play an important role in convex geometry (see
[
27
,
28
]
).

Let
\V_{n}(\mathbb{R})
denote the special linear group of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We say that a valuation
Z
is invariant under volume preserving maps if
Z(\phi K+x)=Z(K)
for every vector
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, every
\phi\in\V_{n}(\mathbb{R})
, and every convex body
K\in\mathcal{K}^{n}
. Specifically, when this equality holds only for rotations
\phi
, we say that
Z
is a rigid motion invariant valuation. We consider continuous and upper semicontinuous valuations, where
\mathcal{K}^{n}
and its subspaces are equipped with the topology induced by the Hausdorff metric.

The interest in classifying valuations on
\mathcal{K}^{n}
originates with Blaschke
[
7
]
and was later developed by Hadwiger. Probably the most famous result in this area is the Hadwiger characterization theorem (1957), which classifies all continuous and rigid motion invariant valuations on the space
\mathcal{K}^{n}
(see
[
18
]
). Specifically, it states that any such valuation can be written as a linear combination of the intrinsic volumes
V_{0},V_{1},\dots,V_{n}
. In 1999, Ludwig and Reitzner established an affine version of Hadwiger’s theorem, providing a classification of upper semicontinuous valuations that are invariant under volume preserving maps (see
[
20
,
23
]
). Further characterizations of valuations with interesting invariance properties were obtained, for example, in
[
3
,
16
,
17
,
18
,
19
,
24
,
27
,
28
]
.

The following result, due to Ludwig
[
21
]
, classifies rigid motion invariant and upper semicontinuous valuations defined on
\mathcal{K}^{2}
. Consider the set

\conc=\\
\left\{\zeta:[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty)\mid\ \zeta\ \mbox{is concave},\ \lim_{t\rightarrow 0}\zeta(t)=0,\mbox{ and }\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\zeta(t)/t=0\right\}.

Theorem 1

(
[
21
]
)
.

A functional
Z:\mathcal{K}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is an upper semicontinuous and rigid motion invariant valuation if and only if there exist constants
c_{0},c_{1},c_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
and a function
\zeta\in\conc
such that

\displaystyle Z(K)=c_{0}V_{0}(K)+c_{1}V_{1}(K)+c_{2}V_{2}(K)+\int_{S^{1}}\zeta(\rho(K,u))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{1}(u)

for every
K\in\mathcal{K}^{2}
.

Here,
S^{1}
is the unit sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, while
\rho(K,u)
is the curvature radius of the boundary of
K
at the point with normal
u\in S^{1}
, and
\mathcal{H}^{1}
is the 1-dimensional Hausdorff measure.

Recently, the notion of valuations has been extended to families of functions due to their close relationship with valuations on convex bodies. If
X
is a space of extended real-valued functions, a function
Z:X\to\mathbb{R}
is a valuation if

Z(u\wedge v)+Z(u\vee v)=Z(u)+Z(v)

for every
u,v\in X
such that also
u\wedge v,u\vee v\in X
, where
u\wedge v
and
u\vee v
denote the
pointwise minimum and maximum of
u
and
v
, respectively. Characterization results for valuations on various function spaces have been provided, including spaces of convex functions
[
8
,
10
,
26
]
, Lebesgue
L_{p}
spaces
[
35
]
, and Sobolev spaces
[
22
]
. In this paper, we focus on real-valued valuations defined on spaces of convex functions.

We say that a function
u:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow(-\infty,+\infty]
is
lower semicontinuous
if its epigraph is a closed subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, and it is a
proper
function if the (effective)
domain
of
u
,

\dom u=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid\ u(x)<+\infty\},

is non-empty. We denote by

\Conv(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\{u:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow(-\infty,+\infty]\mid\ u\ \mbox{is l.s.c and convex},\ u\not\equiv+\infty\}

the space of proper, lower semicontinuous, convex functions defined on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and by

\displaystyle\Conv(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R})=\{v:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}\mid\ v\text{ is convex}\}

the space of finite-valued, convex functions on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Throughout the text, we will say that
u\in\Conv(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is Lipschitz on
\interior(\dom u)
and, for short, we say just Lipschitz, if there exists a constant
L_{u}>0
such that

(1)

\displaystyle|u(x)-u(y)|\leq L_{u}\|x-y\|

for every
x,y\in\interior(\dom u)
, where
\interior(A)
denotes the interior a set
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
\|y\|
denotes the Euclidean norm of
y
. We consider the subset of functions

\displaystyle\LC(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\{u\in\Conv(\mathbb{R}^{n})\mid\ \dom u\mbox{ is compact},\,u\mbox{ is Lipschitz on }\interior(\dom u)\}.

Note that if
u\in\Conv(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\dom u
is compact but not full-dimensional, then
u\in\LC(\mathbb{R}^{n})
without necessarily being Lipschitz.

We equip
\LC(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with the following notion of convergence (cf.
[
4
]
): We say that
u_{k}\xrightarrow{\tau}u
(or that
u_{k}
is
\tau
-convergent to
u
) if
u_{k},u\in\LC(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and

(i)

for every sequence
x_{k}
that converges to
x
, we have

u(x)\leq\liminf_{k\rightarrow+\infty}u_{k}(x_{k});

(ii)

there exists a sequence
x_{k}
that converges to
x
such that

u(x)=\lim_{k\rightarrow+\infty}u_{k}(x_{k});

(iii)

letting
L_{u_{k}}
denote the Lipschitz constant of
u_{k}
on the interior of its domain, the sequence
\{L_{u_{k}}\}
is uniformly bounded by some constant
M>0
, independent of
k
.

The convergence (i) and (ii) is called
epi-convergence
. Moreover, if
u\in\Conv(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\dom u
has non-empty interior,
then
u_{k}\in\Conv(\mathbb{R}^{n})
epi-converges to
u
if and only if
u_{k}
converges uniformly to
u
on every every compact set that does not contain a boundary point of
\dom u
(see [
29
, Theorem 7.17]). Condition (iii) for the space of real-valued Lipschitz continuous maps defined on the unit sphere
S^{n-1}

has been considered in
[
11
,
12
,
13
]
, with the additional requirement of uniform convergence on the unit sphere.

A valuation
Z:\LC(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is called
translation invariant
if

Z(u\circ\tau)=Z(u)

for every
u\in\LC(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and translation
\tau
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
\V_{n}(\mathbb{R})

invariant
if

Z(u\circ\phi)=Z(u)

for every
u\in\LC(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\phi\in\V_{n}(\mathbb{R})
. We define
Z:\LC(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
to be
\tau
-
upper semicontinuous
if, for every sequence
u_{k}
that is
\tau
-convergent to
u
,

Z(u)\geq\limsup_{k\rightarrow+\infty}Z(u_{k}).

We say that
Z
is
dually translation invariant
if
Z(u+l)=Z(u)
for every linear function
l
and for every
u\in\LC(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and
vertically translation
invariant if
Z(u+c)=Z(u)
for every
u\in\LC(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and every constant
c
. When
Z
is both dually translation invariant and vertically translation invariant, we say that
Z
is
dually epi-translation
invariant.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a convex set. For a convex function
u:D\rightarrow(-\infty,+\infty]
, let
\D u(x)
denote the Hessian matrix of
u
at
x
if
u
is twice differentiable at
x
and
\D u(x)=0
otherwise. By Aleksandrov
[
2
]
, a convex function
u
is twice differentiable almost everywhere. The functional

(2)

\displaystyle Z(u)=\int_{D}(\det\D u(x))^{\frac{1}{n+2}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x,

is known as the
affine area functional
(see
[
32
,
33
,
34
]
),
because for bounded
D
it equals the affine surface area of the
graph of
u
. Note that
\zeta(t)=t^{1/(n+2)}
belongs to
\conc
, and the functional
Z
defined in (
2
) is an
\V_{n}(\mathbb{R})
, translation and dually epi-translation invariant valuation, and by
[
4
, Theorem 1]
, it is
\tau
-upper semicontinuous. For affine surface area of convex bodies, see
[
23
,
25
,
30
]
; for a recent survey, see
[
31
]
. By [
34
, Lemma 2.3], the functional

\displaystyle u\mapsto\int_{D}\det\D u(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x,

is the absolutely continuous part of the well-known
Monge–Ampère measure
on
D
, with respect to the Lebesgue measure (see, for example,
[
15
]
).

Our goal is to establish a functional version of Theorem
1
.
While Theorem
1
focuses on valuations on convex bodies in the two-dimensional setting,
we now consider convex functions on
\mathbb{R}
, that is, the case
n=1
.
Note that although the variable is one-dimensional, the epigraph of a convex function in
\LC(\mathbb{R})
is a convex subset of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, so from a geometric perspective we are still dealing with a two-dimensional problem.
However, the functional framework does not allow us to exploit additional structures such as invariance under
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
.
This makes classification in the one-dimensional functional setting more delicate; for example, previous results (see, e.g.,
[
8
,
26
]
) require
n\geq 2
precisely for these reasons. The following theorem provides a classification result in this setting.

Theorem 2
.

A functional
Z:\LC(\mathbb{R})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a
\tau
-upper semicontinuous, translation and dually epi-translation invariant valuation if and only if there are constants
c_{0},c_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
and a function
\zeta\in\conc
such that

(3)

\displaystyle Z(u)=c_{0}+c_{1}V_{1}(\dom u)+\int_{\dom u}\zeta(u^{\prime\prime}(x))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x

for every
u\in\LC(\mathbb{R})
.

In particular, Theorem
2
also provides a characterization of the more general functional

(4)

\displaystyle Z(u)=\int_{\dom u}\zeta(u^{\prime\prime}(x))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x,

for
\zeta\in\conc
and
u\in\LC(\mathbb{R})
, where we set
u^{\prime\prime}(x)=0
whenever
u
is not twice differentiable at
x
. In Remark
11
, we explain why the restriction to sequences with uniformly bounded Lipschitz constants is necessary.

Since the integral in (
4
) vanishes for piecewise affine functions, we obtain the following consequence.

Corollary 3
.

A functional
Z:\LC(\mathbb{R})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a
\tau
-continuous, translation and dually epi-translation invariant valuation if and only if there are constants
c_{0},c_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\displaystyle Z(u)=c_{0}+c_{1}V_{1}(\dom u)

for every
u\in\LC(\mathbb{R})
.

Note that
u\mapsto V_{1}(\dom u)
is not continuous with respect to epi-convergence (see Remark
11
).

2.
Tools

In this section we present some basic results and establish some notation that will be used throughout the paper. We also show that each term that appears in (
3
) satisfies the conditions of being a
\tau
-upper semicontinuous, translation and dually epi-translation invariant valuation. In particular, if
u_{k}
is
\tau
-convergent to
u
, then the volume of the domain depends continuously on
u
. In this section, we present the results in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
to provide a framework that can be used in future work.

For a function
u\in\Conv(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we denote by
\mbox{epi}(u)
its epigraph,

\mbox{epi}(u)=\{(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\mid\ u(x)\leq t\},

and by
\conv(A)
the convex hull of a set
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
which is the smallest convex set containing
A
.

Consider the indicator functions of convex bodies
K\in\mathcal{K}^{n}
, defined by

\displaystyle\I_{K}(x)=\begin{cases}0,&\ \mbox{if}\ \ x\in K\\
+\infty,&\ \mbox{if}\ \ x\not\in K.\end{cases}

Note that these indicator functions belong to
\LC(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

The following lemma is a simple result that allows us to obtain new valuations from a given one.

Lemma 4
.

Let
Z:\LC(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a valuation. If
w\in\LC(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and

\displaystyle Z_{w}(u):=Z(u+w),

for
u\in\LC(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then
Z_{w}
is a valuation on
\LC(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Proof.

Let
w,u,v
be convex functions in
\LC(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
u\wedge v\in\LC(\mathbb{R}^{n})
as well. It is easy to see that

\displaystyle u\wedge v+w

\displaystyle=(u+w)\wedge(v+w)

\displaystyle u\vee v+w

\displaystyle=(u+w)\vee(v+w).

Applying
Z
to the equations above and using that
Z
is a valuation, we obtain

\displaystyle Z_{w}(u\wedge v)+Z_{w}(u\vee v)

\displaystyle=Z(u\wedge v+w)+Z(u\vee v+w)

\displaystyle=Z((u+w)\wedge(v+w))+Z((u+w)\vee(v+w))

\displaystyle=Z(u+w)+Z(v+w)

\displaystyle=Z_{w}(u)+Z_{w}(v).

Therefore,
Z_{w}
is a valuation.
∎

Before stating the next lemma, we recall the definition of Minkowski addition for sets in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
: for
A,B\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we define

A+B:=\{a+b\mid a\in A,\,b\in B\}.

Lemma 5
.

Let
u,v\in\LC(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and assume that
u\wedge v\in\LC(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Then the following equalities hold:

\displaystyle\dom(u\vee v)

\displaystyle=\dom u\cup\dom v,

\displaystyle\dom(u\wedge v)

\displaystyle=\dom u\cap\dom v.

In particular, the Minkowski addition satisfies

\dom(u\vee v)+\dom(u\wedge v)=(\dom u\cup\dom v)+(\dom u\cap\dom v).

Proof.

Let
u,v\in\LC(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be such that
u\wedge v\in\LC(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Since

\displaystyle\dom{u\vee v}

\displaystyle=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid\ u(x)<+\infty\ \mbox{and}\ v(x)<+\infty\}

\displaystyle=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:u(x)<+\infty\}\cap\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid\ v(x)<+\infty\}

\displaystyle=\dom u\cap\dom v

and

\displaystyle\dom{u\wedge v}

\displaystyle=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid\ u(x)<+\infty\ \mbox{or}\ v(x)<+\infty\}

\displaystyle=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid\ u(x)<+\infty\}\cup\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:v(x)<+\infty\}

\displaystyle=\dom u\cup\dom v,

this yields the result.
∎

Lemma 6
.

The functional
Z:\LC(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
defined by

\displaystyle Z(u)=V_{n}(\dom u)

is an
\V_{n}(\mathbb{R})
, translation and dually epi-translation invariant valuation.

Proof.

Let
u,v\in\LC(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be such that
u\wedge v\in\LC(\mathbb{R}^{n})
as well. Using that
V_{n}
is a valuation and Lemma
5
, we obtain

\displaystyle Z(u\vee v)

\displaystyle=V_{n}(\dom u\vee v)

\displaystyle=V_{n}(\dom u\cap\dom v)

\displaystyle=V_{n}(\dom u)+V_{n}(\dom v)-V_{n}(\dom u\cup\dom v)

\displaystyle=V_{n}(\dom u)+V_{n}(\dom v)-V_{n}(\dom u\wedge v)

\displaystyle=Z(u)+Z(v)-Z(u\wedge v),

i.e.,
Z
is a valuation. Now let
c\in\mathbb{R}
, let
l:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be a linear function and
\tau_{y}(x)=x+y
for
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Since
\dom(u+l+c)=\dom u
,
\dom(u\circ\tau_{y}^{-1})=\dom u+y
and
V_{n}
is a translation invariant valuation, it follows that

Z(u+l+c)=V_{n}(\dom(u+l+c))=V_{n}(\dom u)=Z(u)

and

Z(u\circ\tau_{y}^{-1})=V_{n}(\dom(u\circ\tau_{y}^{-1}))=V_{n}(\dom u+y)=V_{n}(\dom u)=Z(u).

Thus,
Z
is a translation and dually epi-translation invariant valuation. To show that
Z
is
\V_{n}(\mathbb{R})
invariant, we only need to observe that for
\varphi\in\V_{n}(\mathbb{R})
, it holds that
\dom u\circ\varphi=\varphi^{-1}(\dom u)
, and that
V_{n}
is
\V_{n}(\mathbb{R})
invariant.
∎

To say that
u_{k}\in\Conv(\mathbb{R})
epi-converges to
u\in\Conv(\mathbb{R})
is equivalent to saying that
\epi(u_{k})
converges to
\epi(u)
in the Painlevé-Kuratowski sense as
k\rightarrow+\infty
(see [
29
, Definition 7.1, Proposition 7.2]). By
[
29
, Theorem 4.26]
, if
F:\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a continuous mapping and
u_{k}
epi-converges to
u
, i.e.,

\limsup_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\epi(u_{k})=\liminf_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\epi(u_{k})=\epi(u),

then we obtain the inclusion

\displaystyle F(\liminf_{k\to+\infty}\epi(u_{k}))\subseteq\liminf_{k\to+\infty}F(\epi(u_{k})).

Taking
F(x,t)=x
, which maps epigraphs to domains, we obtain

\displaystyle\dom u=F(\epi(u))=F(\liminf_{k\to+\infty}\epi(u_{k}))\subseteq\liminf_{k\to+\infty}F(\epi(u_{k}))=\liminf_{k\to+\infty}\dom u_{k}.

By definition,

\displaystyle\liminf_{k\to+\infty}\dom u_{k}\subseteq\limsup_{k\to+\infty}\dom u_{k}.

Hence,

(5)

\displaystyle\dom u\subseteq\liminf_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\dom u_{k}\subseteq\limsup_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\dom u_{k},

(see also
[
29
, Propostion 7.4]
). The definitions of
\liminf_{k\rightarrow+\infty}C_{k}
and
\limsup_{k\rightarrow+\infty}C_{k}
for a sequence of sets
C_{k}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
can be found in [
29
, Chapter 4]. Furthermore, by [
29
, page 117], if
C_{k}
converges to
C
in the Painlevé-Kuratowski sense and there exists a bounded set
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
C_{k},C\subset B
, then this convergence is equivalent to convergence in the Hausdorff metric. In order to prove Lemma
9
, we will need the following results from
[
29
]
.

Theorem 7

(
[
29
]
, Theorem 4.5 (b))
.

For sets
C_{k},C\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
C
closed, we have

\displaystyle\limsup_{k\to\infty}C_{k}\subset C

if and only if for every compact set
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
C\cap B=\emptyset
, there exists a set
N\subset\mathbb{N}
such that
\mathbb{N}\setminus N
is finite and
C_{k}\cap B=\emptyset
for all
k\in N
.

Theorem 8

(
[
29
]
, Corollary 4.12)
.

Let
C_{k}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be compact and connected sets, with
\limsup_{k\to\infty}C_{k}
bounded. Then there exists a bounded set
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
C_{k}\subset B
for all
k
.

Lemma 9
.

The functional
Z:\LC(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
defined by

\displaystyle Z(u)=V_{n}(\dom u)

is
\tau
-continuous.

Proof.

To prove that
Z
is a
\tau
-continuous valuation for
n\geq 1
, let
u_{k}\in\LC(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be such that
u_{k}\xrightarrow{\tau}u
. First, consider the case

V_{n}(\limsup_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\dom u_{k})=0
.
In this case,
V_{n}(\dom u_{k})\to 0
as
k\to+\infty
, and by (
5
) we have

V_{n}(\dom u)\leq V_{n}\Big(\limsup_{k\to+\infty}\dom u_{k}\Big)=0=\lim_{k\to+\infty}V_{n}(\dom u_{k}),

so the continuity of
Z
follows. Otherwise, assume that
\limsup_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\dom u_{k}
has non-empty interior and that the Lipschitz constants of
u_{k}
are uniformly bounded by some constant
L>0
. We first analyze the convergence of the domains in the Painlevé sense. By (
5
), it suffices to show that

\displaystyle\limsup_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\interior(\dom u_{k})=\limsup_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\dom u_{k}\subseteq\dom u.

The first equality is established in
[
29
, Proposition 4.4]
. We proceed by contradiction and suppose that

(6)

\displaystyle\limsup_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\interior(\dom u_{k})\not\subset\dom u.

Then, by Theorem
7
, there exists a compact set
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\dom u\cap B=\emptyset
, and for every set of indices
N\subseteq\mathbb{N}
, where
\mathbb{N}\setminus N
is finite, there exists some
k\in N
such that
\interior(\dom u_{k})\cap B\not=\emptyset
. Now, define the following sets of indices

\displaystyle N_{0}

\displaystyle=\{k\geq 1\},

and for
j\geq 1
,

\displaystyle N_{j}

\displaystyle=\{k>m_{j}\},\quad\mbox{where}\qquad m_{j}=\min\{k\in N_{j-1}:\interior(\dom u_{k})\cap B\not=\emptyset\}.

We must have
m_{j}\neq\emptyset
for every
j\in\mathbb{N}
, since
\mathbb{N}\setminus N_{j-1}
is finite, (
6
) holds, and Theorem
7
applies. Moreover,
m_{j+1}>m_{j}
for every
j\in\mathbb{N}
.

Now, consider the subsequence
\{u_{m_{j}}\}_{j}\subseteq\{u_{k}\}_{k}
. Since
u_{k}
epi-converges to
u
, we also have that
u_{m_{j}}
epi-converges to
u
as
j\rightarrow+\infty
. Let
x_{m_{j}}\in\interior(\dom u_{m_{j}})\cap B
for every
j\in\mathbb{N}
. Since
\{x_{m_{j}}\}_{j}\subset B
and
B
is compact, the sequence
\{x_{m_{j}}\}_{j}
admits a convergent subsequence, say
\{x_{m_{j_{k}}}\}_{k}
, with
x_{m_{j_{k}}}\rightarrow x
as
k\rightarrow+\infty
, for some
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Since
B
is closed, we also have
x\in B
. Now, by the definition of epi-convergence and using that
\dom u\cap B=\emptyset
, we get

(7)

\displaystyle+\infty=u(x)\leq\liminf_{k\rightarrow+\infty}u_{m_{j_{k}}}(x_{m_{j_{k}}}).

Next, let
y_{0}\in\dom u
. By the definition of epi-convergence, there exists a sequence
y_{m_{j_{k}}}
converging to
y_{0}
such that

\lim_{k\rightarrow+\infty}u_{m_{j_{k}}}(y_{m_{j_{k}}})=u(y_{0})<+\infty.

Then, for
\varepsilon=1
, there exists
N>0
such that for
k>N
,

(8)

\displaystyle|u_{m_{j_{k}}}(y_{m_{j_{k}}})|\leq|u(y_{0})|+1.

Let
C
be a compact set containing the sequences
\{x_{m_{j_{k}}}\}_{k}\subset B
and
\{y_{m_{j_{k}}}\}_{k}\subset\dom u
. Such a set exists because both
B
and
\dom u
are compact. Since each
u_{m_{j_{k}}}
is a Lipschitz function with Lipschitz constant bounded above by
L
, and using (
1
) and (
8
), we obtain

\displaystyle|u_{m_{j_{k}}}(x_{m_{j_{k}}})|

\displaystyle\leq L\,\|x_{m_{j_{k}}}-y_{m_{j_{k}}}\|+|u_{m_{j_{k}}}(y_{m_{j_{k}}})|

\displaystyle\leq L\,\mbox{diam}(C)+|u(y_{0})|+1

\displaystyle<M,

for each
k>N
, where diam
(C)
is the diameter of
C
and
M>0
is a constant that does not depend on
k
. However, this contradicts (
7
). Therefore, no such compact set

can exist, and we conclude that

\displaystyle\limsup_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\dom u_{k}\subset\dom u\subseteq\liminf_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\dom u_{k}\subseteq\limsup_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\dom u_{k}.

This implies that
\dom u_{k}
converges to
\dom u
in the Painlevé-Kuratowski sense.

To conclude, by Theorem
8
, there exists a bounded set containing both
\dom u
and
\dom u_{k}
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
Since the domains of
u_{k}
converge to
\dom u
in the Painlevé sense and all sets are contained in the same bounded set, it follows that
\dom u_{k}
converges to
\dom u
in the Hausdorff metric (see
[
29
, page 117]
).
Moreover, the
n
-dimensional volume

is continuous with respect to the Hausdorff metric, so we have

V_{n}(\dom u_{k})\to V_{n}(\dom u)\quad\text{as }k\to+\infty.

Therefore, the desired continuity of
Z
is established, and the proof is complete.
∎

The following result establishes key properties of

Lemma 12
.

For
\zeta\in\conc
, the function
Z:\LC(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow[0,\infty)
defined by

Z(u)=\int_{\dom u}\zeta(\det\D u(x))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x,

is an
\V_{n}(\mathbb{R})
, translation and dually epi-translation invariant valuation.

Proof.

Using Lemma
5
, we get

\displaystyle Z(u)+Z(v)=\int_{\dom u}\zeta(\det\D u(x))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\dom v}\zeta(\det\D v(x))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\dom u\setminus\dom v}\zeta(\det\D u(x))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\dom u\cap\dom v}\zeta(\det\D u(x))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\dom v\setminus\dom u}\zeta(\det\D v(x))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\dom u\cap\dom v}\zeta(\det\D v(x))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{\dom u\setminus\dom v}\zeta(\det\D(u\wedge v)(x))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\dom v\setminus\dom u}\zeta(\det\D(u\wedge v)(x))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\right.

\displaystyle\quad\left.+\int_{\dom u\cap\dom v}\zeta(\det\D(u\wedge v)(x))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\right)+\int_{\dom u\cap\dom v}\zeta(\det\D(u\vee v)(x))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\dom u\wedge v}\zeta(\det\D(u\wedge v)(x))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\dom u\vee v}\zeta(\det\D(u\vee v)(x))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=Z(u\wedge v)+Z(u\vee v)

whenever
u,v,u\wedge v\in\LC(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Thus,
Z
is a valuation.

Now let
\tau_{y}(x)=x+y
, where
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then

\displaystyle\int_{\dom(u\circ\tau_{y}^{-1})}\zeta(\det\D(u\circ\tau_{y})(x))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\dom u-y}\zeta(\det\D u(x+y))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\dom u}\zeta(\det\D u(x))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x,

and for an affine function

\int_{\dom u}\zeta(\det\D(u+w)(x))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\dom u}\zeta(\det\D u(x))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x.

Furthermore, for

we obtain

\displaystyle Z(u\circ\varphi)=\int_{\varphi^{-1}(\dom u)}\zeta(\det\D u\circ\varphi(x))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\varphi^{-1}(\dom u)}\zeta(\det\D u(\varphi(x)))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\dom u}\zeta(\det\D u(x))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=Z(u).

This concludes the proof.

We say that a valuation

Lemma 13
.

Let
Z:\LC(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a simple,
\tau
-upper semicontinuous,
\V_{n}(\mathbb{R})
, translation and dually epi-translation invariant valuation. Then there exists a constant

\displaystyle Z(\I_{P})=c_{1}V_{n}(P).

Proof.

Since
Z
is a simple valuation, it suffices to prove the result for
n

P
can be dissected into simplices, i.e., the convex hull of
n+1
points in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
it is enough to prove the result for simplices. Let
S
be a simplex. Since
Z
is simple,
\V_{n}(\mathbb{R})
and translation invariant valuation, the value
Z(\I_{S})
depends only on the volume of
S
. Thus, we can define the function
g:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by

g(x)=Z(\I_{S}),

where
x=V_{n}(S)
is the volume of the simplex

. Now, let
x_{1},x_{2}\geq 0
be the volumes of two simplices
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
, respectively. Assume that

is also a simplex with volume

.
Since
Z
is a simple valuation, we have the additivity property

Z(\I_{S_{1}\cup S_{2}})=Z(\I_{S_{1}})+Z(\I_{S_{2}}),

. Thus, the function
g
satisfies Cauchy’s functional equation. Since
Z
is
\tau
-upper semicontinuous, it follows that

is upper semicontinuous, i.e,

\limsup_{k\to\infty}g(x_{k})\leq g(x)

whenever

. By a standard result (see, for example,

), there exists a constant

, and consequently

.

-upper semicontinuous, translation and dually epi-translation invariant, and vanishes on indicator functions of closed intervals.

A function
u\in\LC(\mathbb{R})
is called
piecewise linear-quadratic
if
\dom u
can be expressed as the union of finitely many intervals
J_{i},i=1,\dots,l
, such that the restriction of
u
to
J_{i}
is either a quadratic or an affine function. The set of piecewise linear-quadratic functions will be denoted by
P\LQ(\mathbb{R})
. Note that piecewise affine functions belong to
\PA(\mathbb{R})
, and since
\PA(\mathbb{R})
is dense in
\LC(\mathbb{R})
(see Lemma
14
), every
u\in\LC(\mathbb{R})
can be approximated by elements of
P\LQ(\mathbb{R})
. The
\tau
-upper semicontinuity of
Z
implies that for every sequence
u_{k}\in P\LQ(\mathbb{R})
such that
u_{k}\xrightarrow{\tau}u
,

(25)

\displaystyle Z(u)\geq\limsup_{k\rightarrow+\infty}Z(u_{k}).

We will prove that for every
u\in\LC(\mathbb{R})
, there exists a sequence
u_{k}\in P\LQ(\mathbb{R})
such that equality holds in (
25
), i.e.,

(26)

\displaystyle Z(u)=\sup\left\{\limsup_{k\rightarrow+\infty}Z(u_{k})\mid\ u_{k}\in P\LQ(\mathbb{R}),u_{k}\xrightarrow{\tau}u\right\}.

By proving this result, we will show that
Z
is uniquely determined by
\zeta
.

We call a closed triangle
T=T(x,y)
a
support triangle
of a convex function
u
with endpoints
(x,u(x))
and
(y,u(y))
, where
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
, if
T
is bounded by support lines (i.e., 1-dimensional support hyperplanes) to
u
at
x
and
y
and the chord connecting
(x,u(x))
and
(y,u(y))
.
Using suitable support triangles of
u\in\LC(\mathbb{R})
, we will construct a function
v\in P\LQ(\mathbb{R})
such that

\displaystyle Z(u)\leq Z(v)+\rho V_{1}(\dom u),

where
\rho>0
is a given constant. Specifically, we will show that for the set
N
of normal points, that is,

N=\{x\in\dom u\mid\ \ u\mbox{ is twice differentiable at }x\},

there exists a suitable Vitali class defined using the support triangles of
u
.

Since Z is a
translation invariant valuation, we may assume without loss of generality that

\dom u=[-m,m].

Denote by
\proj_{e_{1}}C
the projection of the set
C\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
onto the subspace spanned by the canonical vector
e_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, and let
|\cdot|
denote the absolute value of a scalar. We use the notation
[A,B]
for the closed line segment with endpoints
A
and
B
, and
l_{[A,B]}
for the linear function whose graph contains the segment
[A,B]
.

Lemma 19
.

Let
x_{0}\in N
be such that
u^{\prime\prime}(x_{0})>0
. For every
\rho,\delta>0
, there exist a support triangle
T=T(x,y)
of
u
, a convex body
S_{T}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
, and a function
v_{T}\in P\LQ(\mathbb{R})
such that:

(i)

(x_{0},u(x_{0}))\in T
and
0<y-x<\delta
;

(ii)

S_{T}\subset T
and
S_{T}
is a support triangle of
v_{T}
;

(iii)

Z(u+\I_{[x,y]})\leq Z(v_{T})+\frac{\rho}{2}(y-x)
.

Proof.

First, since
u
is a convex function, it is, in particular, twice differentiable almost everywhere. By Taylor expansion,
u
can be locally represented around
x_{0}
as

(27)

\displaystyle u(x)=u(x_{0})+u^{\prime}(x_{0})(x-x_{0})+\frac{1}{2}u^{\prime\prime}(x_{0})(x-x_{0})^{2}+o((x-x_{0})^{2}).

For
\varepsilon>0
, let
A_{\varepsilon}=(x_{0}-\varepsilon,u(x_{0}-\varepsilon))
and
B_{\varepsilon}=(x_{0}+\varepsilon,u(x_{0}+\varepsilon))
be points on the graph of
u
and let

T_{\varepsilon}=T_{\varepsilon}(x_{0}-\varepsilon,x_{0}+\varepsilon)

denote the support triangle of
u
with endpoints
A_{\varepsilon}
and
B_{\varepsilon}
. By (
27
), we have

B_{\varepsilon}-A_{\varepsilon}=\left(2\varepsilon,2\varepsilon u^{\prime}(x_{0})+o(\varepsilon^{2})\right).

Thus, for
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small, we have
0<2\varepsilon<\delta
, and condition
(i)
holds. To prove condition
(ii)
, we now consider the support lines of
u
at
x_{0}-\varepsilon
and
x_{0}+\varepsilon
, denoted by
H(A_{\varepsilon})
and
H(B_{\varepsilon})
, respectively, and let
C_{\varepsilon}=(c_{\varepsilon}^{1},c_{\varepsilon}^{2})
be the point where
H(A_{\varepsilon})
and
H(B_{\varepsilon})
intersect. Without loss of generality, assume that

|\proj_{e_{1}}(C_{\varepsilon}-B_{\varepsilon})|=(x_{0}+\varepsilon)-c_{\varepsilon}^{1}\leq c_{\varepsilon}^{1}-(x_{0}-\varepsilon)=|\proj_{e_{1}}(C_{\varepsilon}-A_{\varepsilon})|.

Now, define
B^{1}_{\varepsilon}=(b^{1}_{\varepsilon},\tilde{b}^{1}_{\varepsilon})
as the point on
H(B_{\varepsilon})
such that

|\proj_{e_{1}}(C_{\varepsilon}-B^{1}_{\varepsilon})|=b^{1}_{\varepsilon}-c_{\varepsilon}^{1}=c_{\varepsilon}^{1}-(x_{0}-\varepsilon)=|\proj_{e_{1}}(C_{\varepsilon}-A_{\varepsilon})|.

Since
B^{1}_{\varepsilon}\in H(B_{\varepsilon})
, the point
C_{\varepsilon}
also belongs to
H(B_{\varepsilon})
, and

|\proj_{e_{1}}(C_{\varepsilon}-B_{\varepsilon})|=(x_{0}+\varepsilon)-c_{\varepsilon}^{1}\leq b^{1}_{\varepsilon}-c_{\varepsilon}^{1}=|\proj_{e_{1}}(C_{\varepsilon}-B^{1}_{\varepsilon})|,

we conclude that
B_{\varepsilon}
is contained in the closed line segment
[C_{\varepsilon},B^{1}_{\varepsilon}]
(see Figure
2
).

Let

q_{\varepsilon}(x)=\alpha x^{2}+\beta x+\gamma=\alpha(\varepsilon)x^{2}+\beta(\varepsilon)x+\gamma(\varepsilon)

be the quadratic function such that the support line
H(A_{\varepsilon})
is tangent to
q_{\varepsilon}
at
A_{\varepsilon}
, and the support line
H(B_{\varepsilon})
is tangent to
q_{\varepsilon}
at
B^{1}_{\varepsilon}
(see Figure
2
). Since
B^{1}_{\varepsilon}=(b^{1}_{\varepsilon},\tilde{b}^{1}_{\varepsilon})
lies on the graph of
q_{\varepsilon}
, it follows that
\tilde{b}^{1}_{\varepsilon}=q_{\varepsilon}(b^{1}_{\varepsilon})
.

A simple calculation using (
27
) shows that as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0

(28)

\displaystyle q_{\varepsilon}(x)\to\dfrac{u^{\prime\prime}(x_{0})}{2}x^{2}+(u^{\prime}(x_{0})-x_{0}u^{\prime\prime}(x_{0}))x+(u(x_{0})-x_{0}u^{\prime}(x_{0})+\dfrac{x_{0}^{2}}{2}u^{\prime\prime}(x_{0}))

\displaystyle=q_{x_{0}}(x).

Since
q_{\varepsilon}
is convex, the point
B_{\varepsilon}
does not lie in the interior of epi(
q_{\varepsilon}
), and the line segment
[B_{\varepsilon},B^{1}_{\varepsilon}]
is tangent to
q_{\varepsilon}
at
B^{1}_{\varepsilon}
. Now, let
B^{2}_{\varepsilon}=(b^{2}_{\varepsilon},q_{\varepsilon}(b^{2}_{\varepsilon}))
be the second point on boundary of epi(
q_{\varepsilon}
) such that the segment
[B^{2}_{\varepsilon},B_{\varepsilon}]
is tangent to
q_{\varepsilon}
at
B^{2}_{\varepsilon}
, and let

T^{1}_{\varepsilon}=\conv\{A_{\varepsilon},C_{\varepsilon},B^{1}_{\varepsilon}\}\quad\mbox{and}\quad T^{2}_{\varepsilon}=\conv\{A_{\varepsilon},C_{\varepsilon},B^{2}_{\varepsilon}\}.

See Figure
2
.

Figure 2.
Case
u^{\prime\prime}(x_{0})>0
.

Now it is sufficient to define

S_{T}^{\varepsilon}=(\mbox{epi}(q_{\varepsilon})\cap T^{2}_{\varepsilon})\cup\conv\{A_{\varepsilon},B^{2}_{\varepsilon},B_{\varepsilon}\}

and

(29)

\displaystyle v_{T}^{\varepsilon}=(q_{\varepsilon}+\I_{[x_{0}-\varepsilon,b^{2}_{\varepsilon}]})\wedge(l_{[B^{2}_{\varepsilon},B_{\varepsilon}]}+\I_{[b^{2}_{\varepsilon},x_{0}+\varepsilon]}).

Therefore,
S_{T}^{\varepsilon}\subset T_{\varepsilon}
,
S_{T}^{\varepsilon}
is a support triangle of
v_{T}^{\varepsilon}
and
v_{T}^{\varepsilon}\in P\LQ(\mathbb{R})
.

It remains to show item
(iii)
. Using that
l_{[B^{2}_{\varepsilon},B_{\varepsilon}]}
and
l_{[B_{\varepsilon},B^{1}_{\varepsilon}]}
are tangent to
q_{\varepsilon}
, we obtain

\displaystyle b^{2}_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=(x_{0}+\varepsilon)-\sqrt{(x_{0}+\varepsilon)^{2}-\alpha^{-1}(u(x_{0}+\varepsilon)-\beta(x_{0}+\varepsilon)-\gamma)}

\displaystyle b^{1}_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=(x_{0}+\varepsilon)+\sqrt{(x_{0}+\varepsilon)^{2}-\alpha^{-1}(u(x_{0}+\varepsilon)-\beta(x_{0}+\varepsilon)-\gamma)},

and since
u(x_{0}+\varepsilon)\to q_{x_{0}}(x_{0})
as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
, we get

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\frac{b^{1}_{\varepsilon}-b^{2}_{\varepsilon}}{b^{1}_{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon-x_{0}}=0.

By (
29
), and using that
Z
vanishes on indicator functions of closed intervals and is dually epi-translation invariant, we have

Z(v_{T}^{\varepsilon})=Z(q_{\varepsilon}+\I_{[x_{0}-\varepsilon,b^{2}_{\varepsilon}]}).

Therefore, by (
13
), we obtain

\displaystyle Z(v_{T}^{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle=\dfrac{b^{2}_{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon-x_{0}}{2m}Z(q_{\varepsilon}+\I_{[-m,m]})

\displaystyle=\dfrac{b^{1}_{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon-x_{0}}{2m}\left(Z(q_{\varepsilon}+\I_{[-m,m]})-\dfrac{b^{1}_{\varepsilon}-b^{2}_{\varepsilon}}{b^{1}_{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon-x_{0}}Z(q_{\varepsilon}+\I_{[-m,m]})\right).

For every
\eta>0
and for sufficiently small
\varepsilon
, this gives the inequality

(30)

\displaystyle\dfrac{b^{1}_{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon-x_{0}}{2m}\left(Z(q_{\varepsilon}+\I_{[-m,m]})-\eta\right)\leq Z(v_{T}^{\varepsilon}).

Next, consider the triangle
T^{1}_{\varepsilon}
, which is a support triangle of the function

(u+\I_{[x_{0}-\varepsilon,x_{0}+\varepsilon]})\wedge(l_{[B_{\varepsilon},B^{1}_{\varepsilon}]}+\I_{[x_{0}+\varepsilon,b^{1}_{\varepsilon}]}).

This function belongs to
\LC(\mathbb{R})
, and
T^{1}_{\varepsilon}
is also a support triangle of the quadratic function
q_{\varepsilon}
. Let
y_{1},\dots,y_{n}
be such that

|y_{i}|=b^{1}_{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon-x_{0}\quad\mbox{and}\quad n\leq\frac{2m}{b^{1}_{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon-x_{0}}<n+1.

Define the affine map
\phi_{i}(x,y):\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}
as

(31)

\displaystyle\phi_{i}(x,y)=(x+y_{i},\alpha y_{i}^{2}+\beta y_{i}+2\alpha y_{i}x+y)=(x+y_{i},y+\psi_{y_{i}}(x)),

for every
i=1,\dots,n
, where
\psi_{y_{i}}(x)=\alpha y_{i}^{2}+\beta y_{i}+2\alpha y_{i}x
. Note that
\psi_{y_{i}}
is an affine function,
\phi_{i}(x,q_{\varepsilon}(x))=(x+y_{i},q_{\varepsilon}(x+y_{i}))
and, since
\phi_{i}
is a
C^{1}
function, it maps support triangles to support triangles.
Specifically, for each
i
,
T_{i}=T(\tau_{y_{i}}^{-1}(x_{0}-\varepsilon),\tau_{y_{i}}^{-1}(b^{1}_{\varepsilon}))
is also a support triangle of
q_{\varepsilon}(x)=\alpha x^{2}+\beta x+\gamma
for all
i=1,\dots,n
, and the
T_{i}^{\prime}s
have pairwise disjoint interiors. Here,
\tau_{y_{i}}(x)=x+y_{i}
for every
i=1,\dots,n
.

Define

u_{i}(x+y_{i})=(u+\I_{[x_{0}-\varepsilon,x_{0}+\varepsilon]})(x)\wedge(l_{[B_{\varepsilon},B^{1}_{\varepsilon}]}+\I_{[x_{0}+\varepsilon,b^{1}_{\varepsilon}]})(x)+\psi_{y_{i}}(x)

for every
i=1,\dots,n
, and let

(32)

\displaystyle v_{\varepsilon}=\bigwedge_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}\wedge(q_{\varepsilon}+\I_{[-m,m]}).

By construction,
v_{\varepsilon}
is a convex function in
\LC(\mathbb{R})
and

\displaystyle Z(v_{\varepsilon})\geq nZ(u+\I_{[x_{0}-\varepsilon,x_{0}+\varepsilon]}).

Furthermore, by (
28
),
v_{\varepsilon}
is
\tau
-convergent to
q_{x_{0}}+\I_{[-m,m]}
as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
.

By
\tau
-upper semicontinuity of
Z
, we then have

\displaystyle Z(q_{x_{0}}+\I_{[-m,m]})

\displaystyle\geq\limsup_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}Z(v_{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle\geq\limsup_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\frac{2m}{b^{1}_{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon-x_{0}}Z(u+\I_{[x_{0}-\varepsilon,x_{0}+\varepsilon]}).

Since the function
\zeta(2\alpha)=\frac{1}{2m}Z(2\alpha q+\I_{[-m,m]})
is contained in
\conc
, in particular, it is continuous and by (
28
), we get for every
\eta>0

\displaystyle Z(u+\I_{[x_{0}-\varepsilon,x_{0}+\varepsilon]})\leq\frac{b^{1}_{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon-x_{0}}{2m}\left(Z(q_{\varepsilon}+\I_{[-m,m]})+\eta\right)

for sufficiently small
\varepsilon
. Furthermore, note that

\displaystyle b^{1}_{\varepsilon}-(x_{0}+\varepsilon)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{b^{1}_{\varepsilon}-(x_{0}-\varepsilon)}{2}=\varepsilon+\frac{b^{1}_{\varepsilon}-(x_{0}+\varepsilon)}{2}.

These last inequalities and (
30
) now imply that

\displaystyle Z(u

\displaystyle+\I_{[x_{0}-\varepsilon,x_{0}+\varepsilon]})\leq Z(v_{T}^{\varepsilon})+\frac{b^{1}_{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon-x_{0}}{2m}2\eta

\displaystyle\leq Z(v_{T}^{\varepsilon})+\dfrac{2\varepsilon+\sqrt{(x_{0}+\varepsilon)^{2}-\alpha^{-1}(u(x_{0}+\varepsilon)-\beta(x_{0}+\varepsilon)-\gamma)}}{2m}2\eta

\displaystyle\leq Z(v_{T}^{\varepsilon})+\dfrac{4\varepsilon}{2m}2\eta

for sufficiently small
\varepsilon
. In the last inequality, we used the simple estimate

\sqrt{(x_{0}+\varepsilon)^{2}-\alpha^{-1}(u(x_{0}+\varepsilon)-\beta(x_{0}+\varepsilon)-\gamma)}\leq 2\varepsilon.

Finally, setting
\eta=\frac{\rho m}{4}
shows that item
(iii)
holds for sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
.
∎

In the case
u^{\prime\prime}(x_{0})=0
for

, a similar construction applies, the main difference is that the approximating function

is affine on a closed interval.

Lemma 20
.

Let
x_{0}\in N
be such that
u^{\prime\prime}(x_{0})=0
. For every
\rho,\delta>0
, there exist a support triangle
T=T(x,y)
of
u
, a convex body

, and a function
v_{T}\in P\LQ(\mathbb{R})
such that:

(i)

(x_{0},u(x_{0}))\in T
and
0<y-x<\delta
;

(ii)

S_{T}\subset T
and
S_{T}
is a support triangle of
v_{T}
;

(iii)

Z(u+\I_{[x,y]})\leq Z(v_{T})+\frac{\rho}{2}(y-x)

Let
T_{\varepsilon}
be the support triangle of
u
with endpoints

and

. Then
(i)

\varepsilon

, define the quadratic function

A
is a point of the graph of
\tilde{q}
and the epigraph of
\tilde{q}
is locally contained in the epigraph of
u
near
x_{0}
.

Since
Z
is a
\tau
-upper semicontinuous, simple, and non-negative valuation that vanishes on indicator functions of closed intervals, we have

\limsup_{\alpha\rightarrow 0}Z(\tilde{q}+\I_{[-m,m]})=0.

Therefore, for
\alpha
sufficiently small

(33)

\displaystyle Z(\tilde{q}+\I_{[-m,m]})\leq\frac{\rho m}{4}.

Next, let
C_{\varepsilon}
be the point on the support line to
u
at
x_{0}
such that the first coordinate of
C_{\varepsilon}
is
x_{0}+\varepsilon
, and let
B^{1}_{\varepsilon}=(b^{1}_{\varepsilon},\tilde{q}(b^{1}_{\varepsilon}))
be the point on the graph of
\tilde{q}
where the line segment
l_{[C_{\varepsilon},C^{1}_{\varepsilon}]}
is tangent to the graph of
\tilde{q}
.

.

Then the triangle

is a support triangle of
\tilde{q}
. Since epi(

), the support line of

\tilde{q}

, and

Proposition 22
.

If
Z:\LC(\mathbb{R})\to\mathbb{R}
is a simple,
\tau
-upper semicontinuous, and translation invariant valuation that vanishes on indicator functions of closed intervals, then

\displaystyle Z(u)=\sup\left\{\limsup_{n\rightarrow+\infty}Z(v_{n})\mid\ v_{n}\in P\LQ(\mathbb{R}),v_{n}\rightarrow u\right\}.

Proof.

Since
u\in\LC(\mathbb{R})
, the set
N\subseteq\dom u
of points where
u
is twice differentiable is such that

V_{1}(N)=V_{1}(\dom u).

By Lemma
19
and Lemma
20
, the sets

\{x\in N\mid\ (x,u(x))\in T\},

where
T
is a support triangle of
u
satisfying the conditions of Lemma
19
or Lemma
20
, form a Vitali class for
N
, and this remains true when we restrict to sets
T
such that
V_{1}(\proj_{e_{1}}T)\leq\delta
for some small
\delta>0
. Let
\eta>0
such that

\eta\leq\delta\quad\text{and}\quad\eta\leq\frac{\rho}{2c_{L_{u}}}V_{1}(\dom u),

where
c_{L_{u}}
is given by Lemma
21
. Then, by Vitali’s Theorem
15
, we can choose support triangles
T_{1},\dots,T_{n}
such that

\displaystyle V_{1}(\dom u)=V_{1}(N)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}V_{1}(P_{e_{1}}T_{i})+\eta,

where the closed intervals
P_{e_{1}}T_{1},\dots,P_{e_{1}}T_{n}
have pairwise disjoint interiors. We also choose closed intervals
J_{1},\dots,J_{k}
such that the sets
P_{e_{1}}T_{1},\dots,P_{e_{1}}T_{n},J_{1},\dots,J_{k}
have pairwise disjoint interiors, and such that
\dom u
can be decomposed as

P_{e_{1}}T_{1}\cup\cdots\cup P_{e_{1}}T_{n}\cup J_{1}\cup\cdots\cup J_{k}.

Define

v_{T}=v_{T_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge v_{T_{n}}\wedge(l_{1}+\I_{J_{1}})\wedge\cdots\wedge(l_{k}+\I_{J_{k}}),

where the
l_{i}^{\prime}s
are piecewise affine functions, and
v_{T}
is a convex function in
\LC(\mathbb{R})
whose domain coincides with that of
u
. Our construction using support triangles implies that

\displaystyle Z(v_{T})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}Z(v_{T_{i}}).

Therefore, by Lemma
19
, Lemma
20
, and Lemma
21
, and using that
Z
is a simple valuation, we conclude that for every
\rho>0
,

\displaystyle Z(u)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}Z(u+\I_{P_{e_{1}}T_{i}})+\sum_{s=1}^{k}Z(u+\I_{J_{s}})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(Z(v_{T_{i}})+\frac{\rho}{2}V_{1}(P_{e_{1}}T_{i})\right)+c_{L_{u}}\sum_{s=1}^{k}V_{1}(\dom u\cap J_{s})

\displaystyle\leq Z(v_{T})+\frac{\rho}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}V_{1}(P_{e_{1}}T_{i})+c_{L_{u}}\eta

\displaystyle\leq Z(v_{T})+\frac{\rho}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}V_{1}(P_{e_{1}}T_{i})+\frac{\rho}{2}V_{1}(\dom u)

\displaystyle\leq Z(v_{T})+\rho V_{1}(\dom u).

Since
\rho
is arbitrary, we conclude the proof of the proposition.
∎

By Proposition
22
we get the following result.

Proposition 23
.

Let

. Then there exists a unique functional

Proof.

Let

18
,
\zeta\in\conc
. Since

10

∎

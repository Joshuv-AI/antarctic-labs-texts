On singular supports in mixed characteristic
Takeshi SAITO

Abstract
We fix an excellent regular noetherian scheme S S over 𝐙 ( p ) {\mathbf{Z}}_{(p)} satisfying a certain finiteness condition. For a constructible étale sheaf ℱ {\cal F} on a regular scheme X X of finite type over S S , we introduce a variant of the singular support relatively to S S and prove the existence of a saturated relative variant of the singular support by adopting the method of Beilinson in [ 2 ] using the Radon transform. We may deduce the existence of the singular support itself, if we admit an expected property on the micro support of tensor product and if the scheme X X is sufficiently ramified over the base S S .



For a constructible étale sheaf on
a smooth scheme over a field,
Beilinson proved in
[
2
]
the existence of
the singular support and its fundamental properties.
A key tool used in the proof of the existence is the
Radon transform studied in
[
4
]
.
In mixed characteristic,
the existence of singular support
remains an open question
although
a framework to study the singular support
was prepared in
[
14
]
using
the Frobenius–Witt cotangent bundle
FT^{*}X

supported on the characteristic
p
fiber

X_{{\mathbf{F}}_{p}}
.
We prove in Theorem
2.4.12
the existence of
a saturated relative variant of the singular support
in mixed characteristic by adopting the method of
Beilinson.

Using Radon transform requires
the introduction of two ingredients.
The first is to work in a relative setting
with a fixed base scheme
S
.
The second is to work with
pairs
(h,f)
of morphisms

h\colon W\to X
and
f\colon W\to Y

of schemes over
S
.
The Radon transform
is defined for a projective space

{\mathbf{P}}={\mathbf{P}}_{S}

over a fixed scheme
S

using the pair
(q,q^{\vee})

of the universal family

q^{\vee}\colon Q\to{\mathbf{P}}^{\vee}

of hyperplanes in
{\mathbf{P}}

and the canonical morphism

q\colon Q\to{\mathbf{P}}
.
Thus, we need to work in a relative setting over
S

and also with pairs
(h,f)
of morphisms
while the definition of singular support
in
[
14
]
is formulated only with morphisms
h

without the second morphisms
f
.

The format of the definitions of various variants of
singular supports is summarized as follows.
First, we introduce a certain condition
on morphisms of schemes
with respect to a given closed conical
subset
C
in the cotangent bundle and a corresponding
condition with respect to an étale sheaf
{\cal F}
.
Next, we define to say that

{\cal F}
is micro supported on
C

if every morphism satisfying the condition for
C

satisfies the corresponding condition for
{\cal F}
.
The singular support
SS{\cal F}
is then
defined to be the smallest
C
on which

{\cal F}
is micro supported.
The existence of singular support is non-trivial
because
the condition that

{\cal F}
is micro supported on
C
and on
C^{\prime}

does not a priori imply
that

{\cal F}
is micro supported on
the intersection
C\cap C^{\prime}
.

In the original definition by Beilinson in
[
2
]
,
he considers pairs
(h,f)
of morphisms
and the condition on
{\cal F}
is
given by the local acyclicity of
f
relatively to
h^{*}{\cal F}
.
Since a naive imitation does not give a
good definition of singular support
in mixed characteristic,
we consider in
[
14
]
morphisms
h
without
f

and the
{\cal F}
-transversality
as a condition on
h
relatively to
{\cal F}
:
A separated morphism
h
of finite type
is
{\cal F}
-transversal if the canonical morphism

c_{{\cal F},h}\colon h^{*}{\cal F}\otimes h^{!}\Lambda\to h^{!}{\cal F}

is an isomorphism. Here and in the following
the letter
R
indicating the derived functor is omitted.

We fix a base scheme

.
To define in Definition

-singular support
of a sheaf

on a regular scheme

along the format above,
we introduce in Definition

a condition
for a pair of morphisms

and

to be

-acyclic over

,
by modifying the local acyclicity of

relatively to

used by Beilinson

For the modification,

recalled in Proposition

:
A morphism

to a smooth scheme

is locally acyclic relatively to

if
and only if the morphism

(1,f)\colon X\to X\times_{k}Y
is

{\cal F}\boxtimes{\cal G}
-transversal
for every sheaf

on

.

If

is a scheme over a field

and if

over

,
then we may consider the condition that

-transversal
for the pull-back

to

of every sheaf

on

.

where a base field cannot exist,
we need to find a replacement of
the condition for a sheaf
{\cal G}

on a smooth scheme

over

to ‘come from

as pull-back’.
We will formulate such a condition
called the

-acyclicity
in Definition

by using micro support defined
in an absolute setting without

to define the

-acyclicity over

in Definition

.

The

-acyclicity over

enables us
to define the
S
-singular support

using the format above
and to ask its existence
as a closed conical subset
of the FW cotangent bundle

.
A key reason why

that the universal family

of hyperplanes in

over

is

with the projective space bundle

{\mathbf{P}}(T^{*}{\mathbf{P}}/S)

associated to the relative cotangent bundle

To fill the gap between

and the relative cotangent bundle

defined as the smallest closed

With this variant, the method of Beilinson
allows us to prove the existence

in
Theorem
2.4.12
.
If
X
is smooth over

,
the

-saturated singular support

defines a closed conical subset
of the relative cotangent bundle

restricted to the characteristic

In the case where

for a perfect field

and
recover the singular support defined by
Beilinson in
[

]
. If

is not perfect,
ours is different from that in
[
2
]

as we see in Example after Definition

If we admit a certain property on

we would have

SS_{S}{\cal F}\subset SS{\cal F}\subset SS_{S}^{\rm sat}{\cal F}

by Lemma

The inclusions may be strict in general.
If we further assume
that the morphism

FT^{*}S\times_{S}X\to FT^{*}X

then we have

Lemma 1.5.2

Proof.

1.
The restriction

is micro supported on

by Lemma

.1.
We show that

is the smallest.
Let

is micro supported

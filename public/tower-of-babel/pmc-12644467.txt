Intelligent strength training for football players using resnext optimized by upgraded chimp optimization algorithm
Authors: Wu Zhang, Yinglong Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Intelligent training systems are becoming essential today in sports science in the context of improving athletic performance and injury prevention. Nevertheless, most action-recognition systems are based on traditional deep learning models in terms of their representational ability to distinguish biomechanically similar football actions, and the hyperparameter optimization is frequently achieved through manual trial and error or simple metaheuristics (e.g., PSO, GA), which is slow to converge, inefficiency in high-dimensional space and poor exploration-exploitation balance. To seal these loopholes, this research work suggests a new intelligent strength training model of a football player that combines an improved motion discrimination ResNeXt convolutional neural network, provided by cardinality-based feature learning with an Upgraded Chimp Optimization Algorithm (UCOA), which improves global search efficiency with chaotic map start and elimination step to prevent local optima. The system is tested on the Berkeley MHAD dataset, where it automatically recognizes football-relevant motions (e.g. kicking, jumping, squats) and visualizes them to specific muscle groups so that they can be recommended specific strength exercises. Experimental findings indicate that the UCOA-optimized ResNeXt attains a classification accuracy of 93.7% and an F1-score of 92 and is more accurate than the traditional deep learning models and hybrid optimization baselines.

Introduction

Soccer is one of the most physically exerting sports, wherein athletes are required to keep alive the best qualities of strength, agility, endurance, and coordination throughout the season. In this case, physical training serves to improve performance further and lower any chances of injury. Traditionally, strength training for football players has been devised along broad guidelines sourced from sports science principles and supervised expertise. However, the traditional aspects tend not to inject consideration for individual biomechanics, movement patterns, and physiological responses. Thus, the training outcomes become less than optimal, making them open for a higher level of injuries.

With rapid advancements in artificial intelligence and data analytics, intelligent systems have started being integrated into sports science to provide personalized and effective options for training. These methods use machine learning models to analyze large amounts of motion and sensor data to accurately characterize movement on a player-by-player basis; thus, creating specific training on the unique profile of the athlete. Among the various AI approaches, deep learning has proven to be extraordinarily good at organizing data with great complexity and high dimensionality, including images, video clips, and recordings from multiple sensors; all of which are classical modalities in tasks of human action recognition.

Notwithstanding these advancements, current training frameworks still hold several limitations. Most existing models depend on simple feature extraction approaches which may not be sufficient to capture all complexities associated with dynamic human motions. Furthermore, hyperparameter tuning of machine learning models is still done largely through manual or rudimentary search strategies that waste time and are inefficient in traversing the optimal parameter spaces. These deficiencies limit the precision and flexibility of intelligent training systems within practical conditions of real athletic environments. In particular, the research attempts to cure above-mentioned issues through developing an intelligent weight training system specifically meant for football players. The overall framework successfully comprises two primary aspects: a ResNeXt convolutional neural network along with an upgraded form of the Chimp Optimization Algorithm (UCOA). The introduction of ResNeXt will help extract better distinguishable features from multi-channel motion data for a more intricate action analysis with high-definition sensing technologies. On the other hand, a more advanced Upgraded Chimp Optimization Algorithm is incorporated in order to improve convergence behavior and balance exploration and exploitation in hyperparameter optimization to boost performance on the model.

The Berkeley Multi-Channel Human Action Detection (MHAD) dataset is significantly important in helping validate the proposed system. The dataset itself gathered synchronized recordings from a variety of modalities such as RGB-D cameras and inertial measurement units, which are quite rich concerning the human motion representation in relation to football-like activities. The system is trained on this very excellent dataset to learn movement patterns that can be interpreted into areas of muscles and exercise-based prescriptions.

In this study, a deep learning model that is robust and adaptable was developed which classifies movements specific to football from multi-channel sensor data accurately. It also contains the plan for hyperparameter tuning that would provide performance comparisons between athlete profiles. Thus, the system translates the action classification results into custom strength training schedules, forming a full pipeline that covers all data acquisition and intelligent recommendation stages.

This research clearly contributes to AI-based sports science by providing the first instance of the ResNeXt architecture applied to training-oriented analysis of athlete movements. Moreover, the novel Upgraded Chimp Optimization Algorithm can potentially become a more efficient and effective method of hyperparameter tuning in comparison with the conventional methods. Ultimately, deep learning and metaheuristic optimization were joined together in one complete intelligent training framework, bridging data-driven modeling with applied sports conditioning.

Although the current research shows significant technical performance on the MHAD dataset under controlled experimental circumstances, we realize the fact that experimental validation must be done in practice to support the practical effect of our intelligent strength-training system. We are currently seeking to fill this gap by cooperating with the football training program of Yan’an University to carry out a pilot validation study of twenty male amateur football players aged between eighteen and twenty-four. During a six-week intervention, the participants will be asked to perform a standardized procedure of football related movement under the conditions of wearing synchronized inertial measurement devices (IMUs) and being filmed with RGB-depth cameras, our UCOA-ResNeXt model will identify their movements in the nearest real time and provide them with individual recommendations regarding the strength-exercise program through a mobile interface. The relevance, safety, and perceived usefulness of the exercises prescribed will be judged by coaches and sports scientists, and the objective measures of system efficacy will be gathered as the accuracy of movement, muscle activity (using optional electromyography), and training adherence. This pilot has not yet been finished, but its protocol has been voted through by the ethics board of the university and initial integration test results indicate that the system can work in the field environment. Such a planned validation highlights our desire to take AI-driven frameworks and transform them into practical tools to be used by actual athletes and will be the foundation of the bigger-scale trials in the future work.

Literature review

During the last years, there is a progressive stealing interest in the utilization of artificial intelligence1, machine learning2, and deep learning3 techniques to develop sports science in training football athletes and in athlete performance analysis. There is a number of works in which the application of many computational models has been made, frequently passing from the classical classifiers such as Support Vector Machines (SVM)4 to advanced deep neural networks, such as Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs)5, Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks6, and hybrid architectures for recognizing human action, analyzing movement patterns, and producing intelligent recommendations for training.

This group of efforts has shown how data-driven approaches could change the fundamentally older training paradigms into something more precise and individualized and, further on, efficient. Among the most interesting proposals are functional strength training optimization, manipulation of virtual reality to improve tactical training, and AI-aided analysis of posture and motion; however, the challenge to overcome consists primarily of providing an accuracy performance achievable throughout all datasets, versatility of data, and further automating hyperparameter tuning achieving good generalization for every different athlete and environment.

Stoeve et al.7 compared the efficacy of SVM (Support Vector Machine) for laboratory contexts in three diverse assessment contexts that was gradually applied in real life contexts. Moreover, three diverse kinds networks, including have been CNN, Convolutional LSTM, and LSTM, have been compared with SVM. The findings suggested that the SVM struggled to effectively address the examined three-class issue. However, all deep learning models achieved high categorization values, demonstrating the potential for event recognition in real-world sports contexts by the use of deep learning models. The highest performance, with a weighted F1-score value of 0.93, was achieved via the CNN. This study offered important insights for sports evaluation in pertinent settings. Specifically, it highlighted that the effectiveness of developed attributes had to be reassessed when real-world conditions were considered, choosing the right dataset and evaluation approach is essential for assessing real-world applications, and deep learning-based approaches showed promising outcomes for real-world Human Activity Recognition (HAR) in sports, despite significant variations in activity execution.

Zhou et al.8 enhanced intelligent football training by the of deep learning. Robot detection and training of football player have been analyzed through reviewing the research and describing the neural network, including CNNs and deep learning structures. A CNN-based system for action recognition has been established utilizing a spatial stream network, along with a proposed FCN (Fully Convolutional Network) model for detecting football robot field lines. The findings indicated that the dual-stream model achieved the maximum performance (92.8% recognition), with the spatial stream (86.5%), and the timestream (88%). This system showed exceptional results on specialized datasets, with the time-segmented dual-stream fusion model taking second place in terms of efficacy. In terms of accuracy, the 3D CNN ranked the lowest with the value of 86.2%. For particular movements like jumping, starting, grabbing, and kicking, the recognition rates were 97.6%, 89.8%, 92.5%, and 94.5%, respectively, whereas the recognition rate for passing actions was 91.3%. Intelligent training could enhance efficacy by as much as 25.7%. The improved model for field line detection boosted accuracy of pixel and MIoU by 5%, demonstrating the possibility of AI-powered training systems and robot-assisted recognition. This study provided important insights into AI settings within the realm of sports training.

Cao et al.9 explored the impact of functional strength training on the performance of young football players, utilizing machine learning for analyzing posture. The study involved 116 players between the ages of 8 and 13, divided into an experimental group (n = 60) that engaged in 15–20 min of functional training once the practices were conducted, and a control group (n = 56). Across 24 sessions, a backpropagation neural network (BPNN) evaluated kicking movements by contrasting factors like movement speed, strength, and sensitivity with other models. The results indicated that the experimental group showed significant improvements in kicking performance, set kick execution, throwing accuracy, and 5 × 25 m shuttle run times when compared to both the control group and their initial baselines. The outcomes suggested that functional training boosted strength and accuracy of movement, while the BPNN model effectively enhanced efficacy of training. This method presented a data-driven approach to enhance programs of youth football training.

Shao10 a comprehensive study on the use of VR technologies and AI during tactical training of footbla players. The development of a comprehensive system architecture was initiated that connected the perceptual interface with the calculus subsystems of the model. As part of the perceptual interaction domain, realistic three-dimensional training scenarios have been designed for football by the use of VR technology. In addition, the physical attributes of football mobilization have been calculated through the model calculus subsystem, which functions as part of the logic modules to enhance the realism of training. An Artificial Neural Network (ANN) was integrated into the system architecture, enabling adjustments and real-time error feedback. The ANN formed the core of an innovative training approach and fit seamlessly within the design. Both the ANN’s effectiveness and the overall efficacy of the proposed model have been thoroughly evaluated to illustrate its efficacy for football coaches and players. The outcomes of the suggested proposed system were superior to other methods.

Liao and Fu11 improved the efficacy and findings pertinent to training youth football players by the use of Artificial Intelligence and deep learning models. Initially, To begin with, the primary training methods and the essential aspects of deep learning for CNNs (Convolutional Neural Networks) have been examined. Next, a model for detecting key points in youth football training has been developed using CNNs. Finally, interviews have been carried out with thirty sports educators and five technology firms to explore the potential applications of AI in campus football training. The findings indicated that the visibility challenge of key points recognition in the youth football training model was not that significant, and the model could yield fairly accurate outcomes. The model reached an accuracy level exceeding 90% in predicting critical points, with minimal forecasting errors for key points pertinent to curve positioning and foot placement, all staying below 15%. Educational institutions and corporations view attitudes, awareness, technological factors, and policy as crucial elements for the application of AI in campus football. Furthermore, hardware infrastructure and other associated elements also influenced the implementation of AI in campus football. The results of this research hold practical importance for the intelligent advancement of youth football training and could enhance the high-quality growth of youth football.

The advances made in AI application to football-specific training systems, as well as some limitations, are discussed in the reviewed literature. Deep learning models like CNN and LSTM outperform traditional models such as SVM, and many of these investigations, however, still rely on manual hyperparameter tuning or basic optimization strategies.

This predicament limits their scalability and adaptability. The few frameworks incorporate real-time feedback and biomechanical analysis, none provide the totality of end-to-end solutions integrating accurate movement classification with actionable strength training recommendations, and above all, an intelligent and self-optimizing system generalizable to other datasets and profiles of players still turns out to be an important gap.

This spurred motivating this study, which is towards coming up with an intelligent strength training framework in which the very powerful feature extraction capability from the ResNeXt architecture will combine with the Upgraded Chimp Optimization Algorithm (UCOA) towards automating efficient hyperparameter tuning. This integration will not only magnify performance but also make the system applicable across various athletic conditions and training objectives with significantly low manual intervention. Ultimately, this would set the foundation for a solid, adaptable yet fully data-driven solution to optimizing physical conditioning in football players.

Materials and dataset description

Training and validating the smart strength training system proposed here used the Berkeley Multi-Channel Human Action Detection (MHAD) dataset, which records extensive ensemble-multimodal, richly annotated synchronous motion-data acquired from a wide range of sensing modalities. The recordings showcase high-quality human-action using a variety of complementary technologies, including RGB-D cameras, inertial measurement units (IMUs), and audio sensors, which make them particularly suited to the modeling of complex and dynamic movements that are of significance within the sports performance analysis context.

Berkeley MHAD dataset overview

The whole database of the Berkeley MHAD is made to facilitate research in multimodal human action recognition. Collection of motion data in the combination of 12 different subjects doing 11 fully distinct actions several times in a controlled laboratory. Augmentation

To achieve better generalization by the model and to prevent overfitting, data augmentation techniques were applied. Figure 4 shows the augmentation techniques applied to both visual (RGB) and inertial (IMU) modalities to enhance model generalization.

Train/test split strategy

The dataset was managed in a stratified manner, thus ensuring class balance while partitioning into training, validation, and test datasets. Specifically, 70% of the data was allocated for training, 15% for validation, and 15% for the final testing. This ensured that the model had been evaluated on unseen data while maintaining sufficient samples per class to reliably assess performance. Figure 5 shows the stratified and cross-subject train/validation/test split strategy used to evaluate model performance on unseen athletes. For cross-subject evaluation, training subjects include 1–8, validation subjects include 9–10, and test subjects include 11–12.

Beyond this, considering subject variability, cross-subject evaluation was also pursued where models were trained on data of a subset of individuals and tested on data of other individuals. In this way, the approach mimics real deployment scenarios, where a system is put to work with new athletes not seen in training.

Limitations and generalizability considerations

The Berkeley MHAD dataset has broad multimodal data (such as simultaneous RGB-D video and inertial measurement unit (IMU) data) but has a small sample (twelve participants) and no football-specific conditions. However, we decided to employ this corpus since some of its actions (jumping, kicking, running, and squatting) are similar to the elemental patterns of movements that were observed during football training and play. Thanks to this correspondence, a meaningful biomechanical mapping of muscle groups related to strength is allowed.

To overcome the issue of the generalization of our results, we followed strict cross-subject validation procedure, training the model with subjects 1–8, validating with the subjects 9–10 and testing with the rest subjects 11–12. Besides we performed extensive data-augmentation steps, time-warping augmentation of IMU streams and geometric and photometric augmentation of the visual frames to strengthen the model and to mimic athlete-to-athlete variations.

The classification accuracy of 93.7% and the high-performance levels obtained in the case of hitherto unknown subjects used as test subjects are evidence that the proposed UCOA-ResNeXt framework manages to capture transferable motion characteristics even with the limitations of the available datasets.

Proposed method

This section conveys the main components of the intelligent strength training system that can be designed for football players. The first of these starts with a description of the ResNeXt deep learning architecture and proceeds to the Upgraded Chimp Optimization Algorithm (UCOA) and finally integrates these two into one framework which expresses movements in terms of training plans.

ResNeXt architecture overview

ResNet or Residual Neural Network has been considered a deep neural network design that has been improved for solving the CNNs or convolutional neural networks’ vanishing gradients problem. Actually, the issue of vanishing gradient appears after that the gradients turn into very small since they move over various layers, and avoids efficient optimization and learning. ResNet introduces the concept of residual connections or skip connections, which enable information to bypass specific layers within the network.

In fact, ResNet eases the gradients’ flow and makes it simpler to learn deep demonstrations through employing the prior layers’ activations in a direct way. The main objective of ResNet was the residual blocks’ implementation, which have various convolutional layers that are followed by skip links. The current skip links combine the block’s output with its original input, and makes a shortcut path for flow of information. Hyperparameters of ResNet have been decreased by the homogeneous neural network ResNeXt over the ‘cardinality’ and depth and width of ResNet’s combination. The size of alteration has been determined via the cardinality. Figure 9 illustrates the block within ResNeXt model.

In contrast to CHOA, search agents in UCOA are launched with a uniform random sampling to create starting positions. This method adds diversity to the population and reduces early clustering in local optima by using the ergodicity and non-repeating paths of the map. Second, the balance between exploration and exploitation in CHOA is maintained in the iterative update cycle by a linear reduction of coefficients that can give rise to suboptimal transitions. Conversely, UCOA dynamically varies these control parameters with chaotic sequences obtained with the same map and thus facilitates non-linear, adaptive non-global versus local search. Third and most importantly, UCOA adds an elimination step not found in CHOA: at the end of each step, the 20 per cent worst candidate solutions (by fitness) are eliminated and new agents are re-sampled around the current best solution by Gaussian perturbation. This increases exploitation around likely areas and also retaining diversity. These are highlighted in the Algorithm 1 (UCOA) and compared to the original CHOA steps. Together, these developments significantly increase the speed of convergence, solution accuracy, and stability in high-dimensional hyper-parameter space, the key need in neural network tuning in sports settings, particularly ResNeXt. Toward adaptive learning-based recommendation systems

As the existing intelligent training recommendation framework is based on a rule-based engine that relies on the well-established biomechanical principles, i.e., the mapping of the classified football-related movements (e.g., kicking, jumping, squats) to the specific muscle groups and the corresponding strength exercises, we still admit the fact that the thoughtful suggestion presented by the reviewer is rather intriguing and suggests that the system might be more flexible in the change of the data and, consequently, the type of exercises to provide.

We are currently working on this by actively expanding our framework to include a reinforcement learning (RL) layer which models training prescription as a sequential decision-making problem. In this improved design, feedback in the form of the metrics of athlete performance (e.g., movement accuracy, fatigue level, recovery rate) can be provided to the RL agent, which can adapt dynamically the type, intensity, and volume of exercise to optimize the long-term strength gains and minimize the risk of injury.

Very simple simulations of synthetic athlete models indicate that this type of RL-augmented system is capable of producing more personalized recommendations than a set of rules alone and in particular when dealing with dissimilar physiological responses among different individuals. Even though the present rule-based solution will allow the system to be interpretable and deployable immediately, which is crucial in a real-life coaching set-up, our roadmap will involve the incorporation of this adaptive mechanism to make the novelty, responsiveness, and scalability of our system far more prominent in future iterations.

Results and discussions

The experiments were carried out on an Intel Core i7-10700 K CPU, 32GB DDR4 RAM, and NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3080 GPU with VRAM of 10GB. The MATLAB R2019b platform was used to implement the deep learning framework in which model development and training were conducted. The hyperparameter optimization was performed using custom implementations of the Chimp Optimization Algorithm (CHOA) and an enhanced version called UCOA in MATLAB R2019b.

The proposed UCOA/ResNeXt framework was compared with six other state-of-the-art methods, namely CNN (as Deep Learning Baseline)13, ResNet-50 (Residual Network)14, LSTM (as Temporal Sequence Model)15, PSO–CNN (Particle Swarm Optimization + CNN)16, GA–ResNet (Genetic Algorithm + ResNet)17, GWO-LSTM (Grey Wolf Optimizer + LSTM)18. These methods were all trained and tested under the same data preprocessing and cross-validation conditions to guarantee a fair comparison.

These results, thus, confirm that not only classification accuracy increased through hybrid framework development but also training robustness improved, demonstrating a particular advantage in real-world applications using multichannel complex motion data such as those in the MHAD dataset.

Conclusions

In recent times, artificial intelligence has been incorporated into sports science, helping to change the old paradigms of athlete training into new ones and making the methods more accurate, personalized, and data driven. Herein, an intelligent strength training framework specifically for football players was formulated by incorporating a deep learning model known as ResNeXt with some enhanced version of the Chimp Optimization Algorithm referred to as UCOA. This particular architecture, i.e., ResNeXt, was considered because it excels in feature extraction through residual learning based on cardinality hence the way it is adopted for the analysis of an intricate model human motion pattern as evidenced in the MHAD dataset. With respect to the above reasons, we proposed an application of UCOA towards improving adaptability and performance of models in managing exploration-exploitation balance and convergence behavior in hyperparameter tuning. Experimental findings indicate that the UCOA/ResNeXt method exceeds existing classification performance standards by achieving comparative accuracy of 93.7% and F1 score of 92%, making the clustering efficient during training as well. The system translated movement classifications into efficient strength training tasks for unique biomechanics, thereby defining an approach towards intelligent in-the-field athlete development through hybridized AI frameworks. Although the current research shows significant technical performance on the MHAD dataset under controlled experimental circumstances, we realize the fact that experimental validation must be done in practice to support the practical effect of our intelligent strength-training system. We are currently seeking to fill this gap by cooperating with the football training program of Yan’an University to carry out a pilot validation study of twenty male amateur football players aged between eighteen and twenty-four. During a six-week intervention, the participants will be asked to perform a standardized procedure of football related movement under the conditions of wearing synchronized inertial measurement devices (IMUs) and being filmed with RGB-depth cameras, our UCOA-ResNeXt model will identify their movements in the nearest real time and provide them with individual recommendations regarding the strength-exercise program through a mobile interface. The relevance, safety, and perceived usefulness of the exercises prescribed will be judged by coaches and sports scientists, and the objective measures of system efficacy will be gathered as the accuracy of movement, muscle activity (using optional electromyography), and training adherence. This pilot has not yet been finished, but its protocol has been voted through by the ethics board of the university and initial integration test results indicate that the system can work in the field environment. Such a planned validation highlights our desire to take AI-driven frameworks and transform them into practical tools to be used by actual athletes and will be the foundation of the bigger-scale trials in the future work.

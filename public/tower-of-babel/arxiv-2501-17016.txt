viscosity solution to complex Hessian quotient equation
Jingrui Cheng, Yulun Xu

Abstract
. In this paper, we prove the existence of viscosity solutions to complex Hessian equations on compact Hermitian manifolds, assuming the existence of a strict subsolution in the viscosity sense. The results cover the complex Hessian quotient equations. This generalized our previous results in [ 2 ] where the equation needs to satisfy a determinant domination condition.



1.
Introduction

This paper is a continuation of our previous one
[
2
]
and we wish to generalize our existence results to more general complex Hessian equations. The Hessian equation we consider takes the following form:

(1.1)

f\big(\lambda[\chi+dd^{c}\varphi]\big)=e^{G(x)},\,\,\,\,\lambda[\chi+dd^{c}\varphi]\in\Gamma.

In the above,
G(x)
is a given continuous function on
M
,
\chi
is a given smooth real
(1,1)
form on
M
, and
\lambda[\chi+dd^{c}\varphi]=(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n})
denotes the
n
-tuple of eigenvalues of
\chi+dd^{c}\varphi
with respect to
\omega_{0}
, where
\omega_{0}
is a Hermitian metric on
M
.
Sometimes we are going to simply denote:

F(\chi+dd^{c}\varphi)\equiv f\big(\lambda[\chi+dd^{c}\varphi]\big).

We also denote
d=\frac{1}{2}(\partial+\bar{\partial})
and
d^{c}=\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2}(\bar{\partial}-\partial)
so that
dd^{c}=\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}
.

Moreover, we make the following assumptions on
f
and
\Gamma
.

Assumption 1.1
.

(1)

\Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is an open symmetric convex cone which contains
\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\lambda_{i}>0,\,1\leq i\leq n\}
and is contained in
\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}>0\}
.
f:\Gamma\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
is a smooth and symmetric function.

(2)

\frac{\partial f}{\partial\lambda_{i}}>0,\,\,1\leq i\leq n
,
f
is concave and strictly positive on
\Gamma
and
\lim_{\Gamma\ni\lambda\rightarrow\lambda_{0}}f(\lambda)=0
for any
\lambda_{0}\in\partial\Gamma
.

(3)

For any
\lambda\in\Gamma
, one has
\lim_{R\rightarrow+\infty}f(R\lambda)=+\infty
.

On a closed Hermitian manifold, one can only hope to solve (
1.1
) up to a multiplicative factor on the right hand side, namely we can only hope to solve:

(1.2)

f\big(\lambda[\chi+dd^{c}\varphi]\big)=e^{G(x)+c},\,\,\,\lambda[\chi+dd^{c}\varphi]\in\Gamma.

It is not hard to see that given
G\in C^{\infty}(M)
, there is at most one constant
c
that makes (
1.2
) admit a smooth solution, and in general, this constant (if exists) is very difficult to determine from
G
.
The question whether (
1.2
) admits a solution is a delicate one, and involves the notion of subsolution, which is instrumental in the study of Hessian equations and will be explained below.

Using that
f(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n})
is strictly monotone increasing in each variable, we can define:

(1.3)

\begin{split}&f_{\infty,i}(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\hat{\lambda}_{i},\cdots,\lambda_{n})=\lim_{R\rightarrow+\infty}f(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{i-1},R,\lambda_{i+1},\cdots\lambda_{n}),\\
&f_{\infty}(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n})=\min_{1\leq i\leq n}f_{\infty,i}(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\hat{\lambda}_{i},\cdots,\lambda_{n}).\end{split}

Here
\hat{\lambda}_{i}
simply means this variable is skipped.
For each
f_{\infty,i}
, the limit is either
+\infty
, or a concave function defined
\Gamma^{\prime}
, which is the projection of
\Gamma
onto
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
where
\lambda_{i}
is excluded. Here
f_{\infty}(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n})
would be defined on

(1.4)

\Gamma_{\infty}:=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\text{ for any $i$, $(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{i-1},R,\lambda_{i+1},\cdots,\lambda_{n})\in\Gamma$ for all sufficiently large $R$}\}.

We need to make the following additional assumption on
\chi
:

Assumption 1.2
.

\lambda[\chi](x)\in\Gamma
for any
x\in M
.

From (
1.2
) and that
f
is strictly monotone increasing in each variable, we see that the solution
\varphi
would satisfy:

(1.5)

f_{\infty}\big(\lambda[\chi+dd^{c}\varphi]\big)>e^{G(x)+c},\,\,\,\lambda[\chi+dd^{c}\varphi]\in\Gamma_{\infty}.

Therefore, a necessary condition for the solvability of (
1.2
) is the existence of a smooth function
\underline{\varphi}
that satisfies (
1.5
). Such
\underline{\varphi}
is called a strict subsolution. In the case of Dirichlet problem (either for a smooth bounded domain in Euclidean spaces, or manifolds with boundary), it has been shown by Trudinger
[
17
]
and Guan
[
7
]
that the existence of a subsolution is also sufficient for the solvability (
1.2
).

The case with closed manifold is more subtle.
G. Székelyhidi
[
14
]
proved that if there is a smooth function
\underline{\varphi}
which is a “
\mathcal{C}
-subsolution” with respect to a smooth right hand side
G(x)
, and if there is a smooth solution to
f(\lambda[\chi+dd^{c}\varphi])=e^{G(x)}
, then one can estimate
||\varphi||_{2,\alpha}
. Chu-McCleerey
[
4
]
derived full
C^{2}
estimate for solutions with degenerate right hand side. However, such apriori estimate does not immediately translates to solvability of (
1.2
). Indeed, if one tries to use continuity path, and deform the right hand side
G
, then the corresponding constant
c
will also change. Because of this, a
\mathcal{C}
-subsolution in terms of the function
G
(together with its constant
c
) in the sense defined by Székelyhi
[
14
]
does not necessarily translates to a subsolution along the continuity path.

Note that the situation is much simpler in the special case where
f_{\infty}=+\infty
, so that (
1.5
) holds unconditionally, and the value of the constant
c
becomes irrelevant. In our previous work
[
2
]
, we assumed that
f
satisfies a determinant domination condition, namely:

f(\lambda)\geq c\big(\Pi_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\big)^{\frac{1}{n}},\text{ for some $c>0$ and any $\lambda\in\Gamma_{n}$}.

This in particular implies
f_{\infty}\equiv+\infty
.

If
f_{\infty}<+\infty
, which is the case for complex Hessian quotient equation, it is of critical importance to understand how the constant
c
is determined through the equation and the right hand side
e^{G}
.
This delicate issue was resolved by the recent work of Guo-Song
[
9
]
, where the authors made the observation that the constant
c
can be characterized as:

(1.6)

e^{c}=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{E}_{\Gamma,\chi}}\max_{M}e^{-G(x)}f\big(\lambda[\chi+dd^{c}u]\big).

Here
\mathcal{E}_{\Gamma,\chi}
denotes the class of smooth functions
u
on
M
such that
\lambda[\chi+dd^{c}u](x)\in\Gamma
, for any
x
on
M
. The authors have shown that the existence of a strict subsolution (a smooth function satisfying (
1.5
) with the constant
c
given by (
1.6
)) is equivalent to the solvability of (
1.2
). More precisely, they proved:

Theorem 1.1
.

(Guo-Song,
[
9
]
)
Assume that
f
and
\Gamma
satisfy Assumption
1.1
. Let
G\in C^{\infty}(M)
. Define
c
by (
1.6
).
Then the following two statements are equivalent:

(1)

There exists a smooth solution to (
1.2
).

(2)

There exists
\underline{\varphi}\in C^{\infty}(M)
, such that:

f_{\infty}\big(\lambda[\chi+dd^{c}\underline{\varphi}]\big)>e^{G(x)+c},\,\,\,\lambda[\chi+dd^{c}\underline{\varphi}]\in\Gamma_{\infty}.

The purpose of this work is to develop the viscosity side of the theory. In literature, there are several works about applying viscosity theory to PDEs, including Eyssidieux-Guedj-Zeriahi’s work
[
6
]
on complex Monge-Ampére equation on closed Kähler manifolds, Lu’s work
[
13
]
on
\sigma_{k}
equation on domains in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
or homogeneous Hermitian manifolds, Dinew-Do-To’s work
[
5
]
on Dirichlet problems of complex Hessian type equations on domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. In our setting, we wish to show that the existence of a strict subsolution in the viscosity sense will lead to a viscosity solution to (
1.2
). To be more specific, let
G\in C(M)
, and assume that there exists a viscosity solution
\varphi
to (
1.2
) for some constant
c\in\mathbb{R}
. It is not hard to see that
\varphi
will also satisfy the following in the viscosity sense:

(1.7)

f_{\infty}\big(\lambda[\chi+dd^{c}\varphi]\big)\geq e^{G(x)+c},\,\,\,\,\lambda[\chi+dd^{c}\varphi]\in\Gamma_{\infty}.

The first result we show is that (
1.7
) is almost sufficient for the existence of a viscosity solution to (
1.2
), provided that we take
c
to be defined (
1.6
), and we assume the subsolution to be strict. More precisely, we have:

Theorem 1.2
.

Assume that Assumption
1.1
,
1.2
and
1.3
hold. Let
G\in C(M)
. Define
c
by (
1.6
). Assume that there exists
\underline{u}\in C(M)
which is a strict subsolution in the viscosity sense: for some
\delta_{0}>0
,

f_{\infty}\big(\lambda[\chi+dd^{c}\underline{u}]\big)\geq e^{G+c}+\delta_{0},\,\,\,\lambda[\chi+dd^{c}\underline{u}]\in\Gamma_{\infty}.

Then there exists a viscosity solution to:

f\big(\lambda[\chi+dd^{c}u]\big)=e^{G(x)+c},\,\,\,\lambda[\chi+dd^{c}u]\in\Gamma.

In the above, we need the following assumption in addition to Assumption
1.1
:

Assumption 1.3
.

Either
f_{\infty}=+\infty
on
\Gamma_{\infty}
, or
f_{\infty}<+\infty
on
\Gamma_{\infty}
and:

\lim\sup_{\Gamma_{\infty}\ni\lambda\rightarrow\lambda_{0}}f_{\infty}(\lambda)=0,\,\,\,\text{ for any $\lambda_{0}\in\partial\Gamma_{\infty}$}.

This assumption is satisfied if
f(\lambda)=\sigma_{k}^{\frac{1}{k}}(\lambda),\,\,1\leq k\leq n
as well as complex quotient Hessian equations where
f(\lambda)=\big(\frac{\sigma_{k}(\lambda)}{\sigma_{l}(\lambda)}\big)^{\frac{1}{k-l}},\,\,1\leq l<k\leq n
,
\lambda\in\Gamma_{k}
. In the first example, one has
f_{\infty}(\lambda)=+\infty
, and in the second example, one has:

\begin{split}&f_{\infty}(\lambda)=\min_{1\leq i\leq n}\big(\frac{\sigma_{k-1}(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\hat{\lambda}_{i},\cdots,\lambda_{n})}{\sigma_{l-1}(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\hat{\lambda}_{i},\cdots,\lambda_{n})}\big)^{\frac{1}{k-l}},\\
&\Gamma_{\infty}=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\text{ for any $1\leq i\leq n$, }\sigma_{j}(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\hat{\lambda}_{i},\cdots,\lambda_{n})>0,\,1\leq j\leq k-1\}.\end{split}

Unfortunately Assumption
1.3
does not follow from Assumption
1.1
. We will explain where we need this assumption when we explain our strategy of proof.

Regarding uniqueness, we proved:

Theorem 1.3
.

Assume that Assumption
1.2
holds.

(1)

Assume also that
G(x,z)\in C(M\times\mathbb{R})
with
G
strictly monotone increasing in the
z
variable. Then there is at most one viscosity solution to:

f\big(\lambda[\chi+dd^{c}\varphi]\big)=e^{G(x,\varphi)},\,\,\,\lambda[\chi+dd^{c}\varphi]\in\Gamma.

(2)

Let
G\in C(M)
, then there is at most one constant
c\in\mathbb{R}
, such that there exists a viscosity solution to

f\big(\lambda[\chi+dd^{c}\varphi]\big)=e^{G(x)+c},\,\,\,\lambda[\chi+dd^{c}\varphi]\in\Gamma.

The second part of Theorem
1.3
shows that the constant
c
given by (
1.6
) is the “canonical” constant that one can hope to solve (
1.2
), even in the viscosity setting. Of course, whether one can actually solve (
1.2
) depends on the availability of a subsolution (whether it being smooth or merely in the viscosity sense). On the other hand, we don’t know whether one has the uniqueness of viscosity solution to (
1.2
) in general. At this moment, we can only get uniqueness of viscosity solutions contingent on the knowledge of strict monotonicity of the constant
c
in terms of the right hand side. The question that is still not fully understood is the following:

Conjecture/Question 1.4
.

Let
G\in C(M)
, we define
c(G)
using (
1.6
). Assume that
G^{\prime}\in C(M)
,
G^{\prime}\leq G
and
c(G^{\prime})=c(G)
. Does it follow that
G^{\prime}=G
?

We prove that an affirmative answer to Conjecture
1.4
will imply uniqueness of viscosity solutions.

Now we explain our strategies of proof.
For existence, we use an approximation argument. Namely, we choose
G_{j}\in C^{\infty}(M)
such that
G_{j}\rightarrow G
uniformly, and the first step is to solve:

(1.8)

f\big(\lambda[\chi+dd^{c}u_{j}]\big)=e^{G_{j}+c_{j}},\,\,\lambda[\chi+dd^{c}u_{j}]\in\Gamma.

In order to solve (
1.8
), we need a strict subsolution, namely a function
\tilde{u}\in C^{\infty}(M)
, such that for some
\tilde{\delta}_{0}>0
:

(1.9)

f_{\infty}\big(\lambda[\chi+dd^{c}\tilde{u}]\big)\geq e^{G_{j}+c_{j}}+\tilde{\delta}_{0},\,\,\,\lambda[\chi+dd^{c}\tilde{u}]\in\Gamma_{\infty}.

Here
c_{j}
is given by (
1.6
) with
G_{j}
replacing
G
there.
In order to get the smooth strict subsolution, we apply the Richberg technique to regularize
\underline{u}
, which is the strict subsolution in the viscosity sense. When applying the Richberg technique, we need to construct smooth subsolutions locally, which will be achieved by solving the following Dirichlet problem (very roughly speaking):

(1.10)

f_{\infty}\big(\lambda[\chi+dd^{c}v]\big)=e^{G+c}-\varepsilon,\,\,\lambda[\chi+dd^{c}v]\in\Gamma_{\infty}\text{ in $B_{r}(x_{0})$},\,\,v=\underline{u}-\varepsilon\text{ on $\partial B_{r}(x_{0})$}.

Here
B_{r}(x_{0})
is a ball centered at
x_{0}
with small enough radius.
In order to solve the Dirichlet problem, we will need a non-degeneracy condition, namely, we need (with small enough
\varepsilon
):

(1.11)

\inf_{\bar{B}_{r}(x_{0})}\big(e^{G+c}-\varepsilon\big)>\sup_{\partial\Gamma_{\infty}}f_{\infty}.

The Assumption
1.3
is needed exactly to guarantee (
1.11
). After we are done with constructing the smooth strict subsolution locally, we can used the regularized maximum to patch the local subsolutions into a global one.

Our next goal is to extract a subsequence of
u_{j}
that converges uniformly. It will be relatively easy to see that the sequence
\{u_{j}\}
is precompact in
L^{1}
. Then a key step is to improve the
L^{1}
convergence to
L^{\infty}
convergence, for which we need a stability estimate, whose proof is a variant of the PDE proof of
L^{\infty}
estimate for complex Monge-Ampère equations by Guo-Phong-Tong
[
8
]
. Related
L^{\infty}
estimate is obtained for complex Hessian quotient equations on compact Kähler manifolds by Sui-Sun in
[
15
]
.

The proof of uniqueness is very similar to our previous work
[
2
]
where we additionally assumed that:

f(\lambda)\geq c\big(\Pi_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\big)^{\frac{1}{n}},\,\,\,\lambda\in\Gamma_{n}.

We will sketch the proof and highlight the difference from our previous proof.

Now we explain the organization of this paper.

In Section 2, we explain some preliminary definitions, as well as discuss some basic properties on
f
, as well as
f_{\infty}
.

In Section 3, we use the Richberg technique to regularize the strict subsolution in the viscosity sense to obtain a smooth strict subsolution, following the work of Harvey-Lawson-Pliś
[
10
]
.

In Section 4, we prove the stability estimate that upgrades the
L^{1}
convergence to uniform convergence.

In Section 5, we discuss the uniqueness issues.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Some basic definitions

First we explain what is meant when we say “touch from above/below”.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\varphi
be a function defined on
M
and
x_{0}\in M
. Let
\psi
be another function defined on an open subset of
M
containing
x_{0}
.

(1)

We say that
\psi
touches
\varphi
from above at
x_{0}
, if there exists an open neighborhood
U
of
x_{0}
such that
\psi(x_{0})=\varphi(x_{0})
and
\psi\geq\varphi
on
U
,

(2)

We say that
\psi
touches
\varphi
from below at
x_{0}
, if there exists an open neighborhood
U
of
x_{0}
such that
\psi(x_{0})=\varphi(x_{0})
and
\psi\leq\varphi
on
U
.

Now we define the notion of viscosity solution for (
1.2
):

Definition 2.2
.

Let
G\in C(M)
and
c\in\mathbb{R}
. Let
\varphi\in C(M)
. We say that
\varphi
is a viscosity solution to (
1.2
) if the following hold:

(1)

For any
x_{0}\in M
and any
C^{2}
function
P
defined in a neighborhood of
x_{0}
that touches
\varphi
from above at
x_{0}
, one has

\lambda[\chi+dd^{c}P](x_{0})\in\Gamma,\,\,f\big(\lambda[\chi+dd^{c}P]\big)(x_{0})\geq e^{G(x_{0})+c}.

(2)

For any
x_{0}\in M
and any
C^{2}
function
P
defined in a neighborhood of
x_{0}
that touches
\varphi
from below at
x_{0}
, one has: either
\lambda[\chi+dd^{c}P](x_{0})\notin\Gamma
, or
\lambda[\chi+dd^{c}P](x_{0})\in\Gamma
and
f\big(\lambda[\chi+dd^{c}P]\big)(x_{0})\leq e^{G(x_{0})+c}.

In the same spirit, we can generalize the notion of strict subsolution from classical smooth sense to viscosity sense:

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\underline{u}\in C(M),\,G\in C(M)
and
c\in\mathbb{R}
. We say that
\underline{u}
is a strict subsolution with respect to
(G,c)
in the viscosity sense if there exists
\delta_{0}>0
such that for any
x_{0}\in M
, any
C^{2}
function
P
defined in a neighborhood of
x_{0}
that touches
\varphi
from above at
x_{0}
one has:

\lambda\big[\chi+dd^{c}P](x_{0})\in\Gamma_{\infty},\,\,f_{\infty}\big(\lambda[\chi+dd^{c}P]\big)(x_{0})\geq e^{G(x_{0})+c}+\delta_{0}.

Here
f_{\infty}
and
\Gamma_{\infty}
are given by (
1.3
) and (
1.4
) respectively.

Next we need to make precise the notion of strict convex used in this paper.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open convex set. Let
u
be a function defined on
\Omega
. We say
u
is strictly convex, if for any
x_{0}\in\Omega
, there exists an affine function
l_{x_{0}}
, such that
u(x)\geq l_{x_{0}}(x)
for any
x\in\Omega
, with
``="
holds if and only if
x=x_{0}
. We say that
u
is strictly concave if
-u
is strictly convex.

It is clear that if
u\in C^{2}(\Omega)
and
D^{2}u(x)>0
for
x\in\Omega
, then
u
is strictly convex. It is also clear that if
u_{1}
is convex on
\Omega
,
u_{2}
is strictly convex, then
u_{1}+u_{2}
will be strictly convex.

Finally, we make clear the notion of punctually second order differentiable.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
\varphi
be a function on
M
and
x_{0}\in M
. We say that
\varphi
is punctually second order differentiable at
x_{0}
if there is a neighborhood
U
of
x_{0}
and a
C^{2}
function
\psi
defined on
U
such that

\lim_{r\rightarrow 0}r^{-2}\sup_{x\in B_{r}(x_{0})}|\varphi(x)-\psi(x)|=0.

Here
B_{r}(x_{0})
denotes the geodesic ball with radius
r
(under the metric
\omega_{0}
).

2.2.
Some properties of the operators
f
,
f_{\infty}

First we observe that:

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
f
be a nonnegative concave function defined on a closed convex cone
\Gamma
. Then for any
\lambda\in\Gamma
,
(0,+\infty)\ni t\mapsto\frac{f(t\lambda)}{t}
is monotone decreasing. In particular,
f(\lambda)\leq f(t\lambda)\leq tf(\lambda),\,\,t\geq 1
, and
tf(\lambda)\leq f(t\lambda)\leq f(\lambda)
for
0<t\leq 1
.

Proof.

Let
g(t)=\frac{f(t\lambda)}{t}
, then we find that:
g^{\prime}(t)=\frac{\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}\frac{\partial f}{\partial\lambda_{i}}(t\lambda)\cdot t-f(t\lambda)}{t^{2}}.

On the other hand, using the concavity of
f
, we see that for any
t_{0}>0
:

0\leq f(0)\leq f(t_{0}\lambda)+\frac{d}{dt}|_{t=t_{0}}(f(t\lambda))\cdot(-t_{0})=f(t_{0}\lambda)-t_{0}\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}\frac{\partial f}{\partial\lambda_{i}}(t_{0}\lambda).

To see that
f(t\lambda)\geq f(\lambda)
for
t\geq 1
, we observe that
t\mapsto f(t\lambda)
is monotone increasing. Indeed, this function is concave, and if its derivative is
<0
for some
t_{0}>0
, then it will tends to
-\infty
as
t\rightarrow+\infty
, contradicting that
f\geq 0
.
∎

Next we observe some elementary properties about
f_{\infty}
and
\Gamma_{\infty}
.

Lemma 2.7
.

Assume that
f
and
\Gamma
satisfies Assumption
1.1
.
Define
f_{\infty,i}
by (
1.3
). We also define

\Gamma_{i}^{\prime}=\{(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\hat{\lambda}_{i},\cdots,\lambda_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}:\text{$(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{i-1},R,\lambda_{i+1},\cdots,\lambda_{n})\in\Gamma$ for all $R$ large enough}\}.

Here
\hat{\lambda}_{i}
means
\lambda_{i}
is skipped. Then:

(1)

\Gamma_{i}^{\prime}
is an open convex cone which is symmetric in the variables
\{\lambda_{j}\}_{j\neq i}
, and
\Gamma_{i_{1}}^{\prime}=\Gamma_{i_{2}}^{\prime}
. So we may denote them simply as
\Gamma^{\prime}
.

(2)

\Gamma_{i}^{\prime}
contains
\{(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\hat{\lambda}_{i},\cdots,\lambda_{n}):\lambda_{j}>0,\,1\leq j\leq n,\,j\neq i\}
.

(3)

f_{\infty,i}(\lambda^{\prime})
is either identically
+\infty
or is a concave function on
\Gamma_{i}^{\prime}
. Moreover, the convergence is uniform on every compact subset of
\Gamma_{i}^{\prime}
.

Proof.

To see (1), we observe that
(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{i-1},R,\lambda_{i+1},\cdots,\lambda_{n})\in\Gamma
for all
R
large enough is actually equivalent to
(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{i-1},R_{0},\lambda_{i+1},\cdots,\lambda_{n})\in\Gamma
for some
R_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
. Indeed, since
\Gamma_{n}\subset\Gamma
, we see that if for some
R_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
,
(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{i-1},R_{0},\lambda_{i+1},\cdots,\lambda_{n})\in\Gamma
, then

(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{i-1},R_{0},\lambda_{i+1},\cdots,\lambda_{n})+te_{i}\in\Gamma,\,\,t>0.

This observation suggests that
\Gamma_{i}^{\prime}
is actually the projection of
\Gamma
into
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
under the map
\mathbb{R}^{n}\ni\lambda\mapsto(\lambda_{j})_{j\neq i}
. Since
\Gamma
is open, convex, and symmetric in
\lambda_{i}
, we see that
\Gamma_{i}^{\prime}
is also open, convex, and symmetric in
(\lambda_{j})_{j\neq i}
and also
\Gamma_{i_{1}}^{\prime}=\Gamma_{i_{2}}^{\prime}
.

(2) also follows easily, by considering the projection of
\Gamma_{n}
.

To prove (3), we observe that for each
R>0
,
f_{R,i}(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\hat{\lambda}_{i},\cdots,\lambda_{n}):=f(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{i-1},R,\lambda_{i+1},\cdots,\lambda_{n})
is a concave function defined on
\Gamma_{R,i}^{\prime}=\{(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\hat{\lambda}_{i},\cdots,\lambda_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}:(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{i-1},R,\lambda_{i+1},\cdots,\lambda_{n})\in\Gamma\}.
Each
\Gamma_{R,i}^{\prime}
is convex, and is expanding as
R
increases. Moreover,
f_{R,i}
is mononone increasing in
R
. Hence if we take the limit as
R\rightarrow\infty
, we see the limit is either
+\infty
or is a finite concave function. Moreover, the convergence is uniform on compact subsets of
\Gamma_{i}^{\prime}
.
∎

As a consequence, we see that:

Lemma 2.8
.

Assume that
f
and
\Gamma
satisfies Assumption
1.1
. Let
f_{\infty}
and
\Gamma_{\infty}
be defined through (
1.3
) and (
1.4
). Then one has:

(1)

\Gamma\subset\Gamma_{\infty}
.

(2)

\Gamma_{\infty}
is an open convex symmetric cone which contains
\Gamma_{n}
.

(3)

Either
f_{\infty}=+\infty
on
\Gamma_{\infty}
or
f_{\infty}
is a concave function on
\Gamma_{\infty}
.

If
f_{\infty}<+\infty
, then:

(1)

f_{\infty}
is concave, symmetric and monotone increasing in every variable.

(2)

f_{\infty}>0
on
\Gamma_{\infty}
.

Proof.

First we prove
\Gamma\subset\Gamma_{\infty}
. This follows from
\Gamma_{n}\subset\Gamma
. Indeed, if
\lambda\in\Gamma
, since
te_{i}\in\bar{\Gamma}_{n}
, then
\lambda+te_{i}\in\Gamma
, for any
1\leq i\leq n
and
t>0
, which implies
\lambda\in\Gamma_{\infty}
.

To prove (2), we simply note that with
\Gamma_{\infty}
defined by (
1.4
), we have:

\Gamma_{\infty}:=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\hat{\lambda}_{i},\cdots,\lambda_{n})\in\Gamma^{\prime},\,\,1\leq i\leq n\},

where
\Gamma^{\prime}
is the projection of
\Gamma
into
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
as defined in Lemma
2.7
, item (1).
From this, we immediately see that
\Gamma_{\infty}
is open, convex and symmetric. Also we see that it contains
\Gamma_{n}
because
\Gamma
does.

To see item (3), we note that since

f_{\infty}(\lambda)=\min_{1\leq i\leq n}f_{\infty,i}(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\hat{\lambda}_{i},\cdots,\lambda_{n}),

if for all
i
, one has
f_{\infty,i}(\lambda^{\prime})=+\infty
for
\lambda^{\prime}\in\Gamma^{\prime}
, then we see
f_{\infty}=+\infty
. If for some
i
,
f_{\infty,i}
is a finite function, then we get
f_{\infty}
is a finite concave function on
\Gamma_{\infty}
.

Assuming that
f_{\infty}<+\infty
. It is clearly symmetric, using the symmetry of each
f_{\infty,i}
. Also it is concave, being the minimum of a family of concave functions. It is monotone increasing in each variable, since each
f_{\infty,i}
has the same property.

To see
f_{\infty}>0
on
\Gamma_{\infty}
, first we observe that it is clear
f_{\infty}\geq 0
on
\Gamma_{\infty}
. Since
f_{\infty}
is concave, we can conclude that
f_{\infty}\equiv 0
if
f_{\infty}(\lambda)=0
for some
\lambda\in\Gamma_{\infty}
. This is clearly impossible, because
f_{\infty}(\lambda)\geq f(\lambda)>0
for
\lambda\in\Gamma
.
∎

Later we are going to need:

Lemma 2.9
.

Assume that
f
and
\Gamma
satisfies Assumption
1.1
.
Let
K\subset\Gamma_{\infty}
be a compact subset.

(1)

There exists
R_{0}>0
sufficiently large, such that for any
\lambda\in K
and any
\tilde{\lambda}\in\bar{\Gamma}_{n}
with
|\tilde{\lambda}|\geq R_{0}
, one has
\lambda+\tilde{\lambda}\in\Gamma
.

(2)

If
f_{\infty}=+\infty
, then for any
B>0
, there exists
R_{0}>0
sufficiently large, such that for any
\lambda\in K
and any
\tilde{\lambda}\in\bar{\Gamma}_{n}
with
|\tilde{\lambda}|\geq R_{0}
, one has
f(\lambda+\lambda^{\prime})\geq B
.

(3)

If
f_{\infty}<+\infty
, then for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
R_{0}>0
such that for any
\lambda\in K
and any
\tilde{\lambda}\in\bar{\Gamma}_{n}
with
|\tilde{\lambda}|\geq R_{0}
, one has
f(\lambda+\tilde{\lambda})\geq f_{\infty}(\lambda)-\varepsilon
.

Proof.

First we prove the three statements when
K=\{\lambda_{*}\}
for some
\lambda_{*}\in\Gamma_{\infty}
. If
\tilde{\lambda}\in\Gamma_{n}
, and
|\tilde{\lambda}|\geq R_{0}
, we may assume that
\tilde{\lambda}_{1}\geq\frac{R_{0}}{\sqrt{n}}
without loss of generality. Therefore, with
R_{0}
sufficiently large, we have
\lambda_{*}+\frac{R_{0}}{\sqrt{n}}e_{1}\in\Gamma
. Moreover,
\lambda_{*}+\tilde{\lambda}=\lambda_{*}+\frac{R_{0}}{\sqrt{n}}e_{1}+\hat{\lambda},\,\,\hat{\lambda}\in\Gamma_{n}
. Therefore, we see that
\lambda_{*}+\tilde{\lambda}\in\Gamma
.

If
f_{\infty}=+\infty
, then one has:

\lim_{R\rightarrow\infty}f(R,\lambda_{2},\cdots,\lambda_{n})=+\infty.

Hence with
R_{0}
sufficiently large, one has
f(\lambda_{*}+\frac{R_{0}}{\sqrt{n}}e_{1})\geq B
. Using the monotonicity of
f
, we get:

f(\lambda_{*}+\tilde{\lambda})\geq f(\lambda_{*}+\frac{R_{0}}{\sqrt{n}}e_{1})\geq B.

If
f_{\infty}<+\infty
, then one has:

\lim_{R\rightarrow\infty}f(R,\lambda_{2*},\cdots,\lambda_{n*})\geq f_{\infty}(\lambda_{*}).

Hence with
R_{0}
sufficiently large, one has
f\big(\lambda_{*}+\frac{R_{0}}{\sqrt{n}}e_{1})\geq f_{\infty}(\lambda_{*})-\varepsilon
. Hence
f(\lambda_{*}+\tilde{\lambda})\geq f(\lambda_{*}+\frac{R_{0}}{\sqrt{n}}e_{1})\geq f(\lambda_{*})-\varepsilon
.

Next if
K
is a polytope, meaning that it is the closed convex hull of a finite set, we can choose
R_{0}
sufficiently large so that (1)-(3) holds for its vertices. If
\lambda\in K
and
\tilde{\lambda}\in\Gamma_{n}
with
|\tilde{\lambda}|\geq R_{0}
, we see that:

\lambda+\tilde{\lambda}=\sum_{l}\mu_{l}\lambda^{(l)}+\tilde{\lambda}=\sum_{l}\mu_{l}(\lambda^{(l)}+\tilde{\lambda}),\,\,\,\mu_{l}\geq 0,\,\,\,\sum_{l}\mu_{l}=1.

In the above, we have represented
\lambda
as a convex combination of its vertices. Now we know that each
\lambda^{(l)}+\tilde{\lambda}\in\Gamma
, we see
\lambda+\tilde{\lambda}\in\Gamma
, because of convexity.

The argument for (2) and (3) is similar, using that
f
is a concave function.

If
K
is a general compact set, then it can be covered by finitely many open polytopes compactly contained in
\Gamma_{\infty}
.
∎

We also observe that:

Lemma 2.10
.

Assume that
f
and
\Gamma
satisfies Assumption
1.1
.
If
f_{\infty}<+\infty
, then
\lim_{R\rightarrow+\infty}f_{\infty}(R\lambda)=+\infty
. Moreover, this convergence is uniform on any compact set
K\subset\Gamma_{\infty}
.

Proof.

First we observe that one has:

(2.1)

\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}\frac{\partial f_{\infty}}{\partial\lambda_{i}}(\lambda)\geq 0,\,\,\,\lambda\in\Gamma_{\infty}.

Indeed, for any
R>0
, one concludes from concavity:

0\leq f(R\lambda)\leq f(\lambda)+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\frac{\partial f_{\infty}}{\partial\lambda_{i}}(\lambda)\cdot(R-1).

Therefore,
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\frac{\partial f_{\infty}}{\partial\lambda_{i}}(\lambda)\geq\frac{-f(\lambda)}{R-1}.
Letting
R\rightarrow\infty
, one gets (
2.1
).
Now we define
g_{R}(\lambda)=f_{\infty}(R\lambda)
. Then each
g_{R}(\lambda)
is a concave function on
\Gamma_{\infty}
and it is monotone increasing in
R
, due to (
2.1
). Therefore,
g_{R}(\lambda)
either converges to
+\infty
everywhere on
\Gamma_{\infty}
or converge to a finite concave function on
\Gamma_{\infty}
. Moreover, this convergence is uniform on compact subsets of
\Gamma_{\infty}
. On the other hand, for
\lambda\in\Gamma
, one has
f_{\infty}(R\lambda)\geq f(R\lambda)
and the latter tends to
+\infty
as
R\rightarrow+\infty
. Hence we must have
\lim_{R\rightarrow\infty}f_{\infty}(R\lambda)=+\infty
, uniform on compact subsets of
\Gamma_{\infty}
.
∎

One more thing we need is that:

Lemma 2.11
.

Assume that
f
and
\Gamma
satisfies Assumption
1.1
. Let
f_{\infty}
and
\Gamma_{\infty}
be defined by (
1.3
) and (

(1)

Either

\Gamma_{\infty}\subset\Gamma_{1}:=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}>0\}

(2)

First we prove (1). Assume that
\Gamma_{\infty}\neq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we just need to show that
\Gamma_{\infty}\subset\bar{\Gamma}_{1}
. Assume that
\Gamma_{\infty}
is not contained in
\Gamma_{1}

\lambda_{0}\in\Gamma_{\infty}

\lambda_{\sigma,0}=(\lambda_{0,\sigma(1)},\cdots,\lambda_{0,\sigma(n)})

Using the convexity of

\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n}}\lambda_{\sigma,0}\in\Gamma_{\infty}

contains

\{(-t,\cdots,-t):t\geq 0\}+\Gamma_{n}=\mathbb{R}^{n}.

This proves

.

Now we prove (2). Assume that

2.8
that

on

f_{\infty}(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n-1},R)=f_{\infty}\big(R(\frac{\lambda_{1}}{R},\cdots,\frac{\lambda_{n-1}}{R},1)\big)

to conclude that

as

. That is,

. This proves

.
∎

Lemma 2.12

3.
Regularization of subsolution

The goal of this section is to show that a strict subsolution in the viscosity sense can be regularized into a smooth strict subsolution. This is necessary only if
f_{\infty}<+\infty
, so that we must have
\Gamma_{\infty}\subset\Gamma_{1}
by Lemma
2.11
. So we are going to assume the following holds throughout this section:

(3.1)

f_{\infty}(\lambda)<+\infty,\,\,\,\lambda\in\Gamma_{\infty},\,\,\,\Gamma_{\infty}\subset\Gamma_{1}.

More precisely, we wish to show that:

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
G\in C(M)
, we define
c
to be given by (
1.6
). Let
\underline{u}\in C(M)
be a strict subsolution with respect to
(G,c)
in the viscosity sense as defined by Definition
2.3
for some
\delta_{0}>0
. Then for any
0<\varepsilon\leq\frac{1}{2}\delta_{0}
, there exists
u_{\varepsilon}\in C^{\infty}(M)
such that:

(1)

\lambda[\chi+dd^{c}u_{\varepsilon}]\in\Gamma_{\infty}
,
f_{\infty}\big(\lambda[\chi+dd^{c}u_{\varepsilon}]\big)\geq e^{G(x)+c}+\delta_{0}-\varepsilon
,

(2)

||u_{\varepsilon}-\underline{u}||_{L^{\infty}}\leq\varepsilon
.

We are going to construct
u_{\varepsilon}
via Richberg technique, following the argument by Harvey-Lawson-Pliś
[
10
]
. The details will be carried out in the subsequent sections. As a preliminary step, we first show that the above notion of strict subsolution can be slightly strengthened. More precisely,

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\underline{u}\in C(M)
be a strict subsolution with respect to
(G,c)
in the viscosity sense as defined by Definition
2.3
for some
\delta_{0}>0
. There exists a universal constant
\varepsilon_{0}^{\prime}>0
such that for any
0<\varepsilon\leq\frac{1}{2}\delta_{0}
, one has

\lambda[\chi-\varepsilon_{0}^{\prime}\varepsilon\omega_{0}+dd^{c}\frac{\underline{u}}{1+\varepsilon}]\in\Gamma_{\infty},\,\,f_{\infty}\big(\lambda[\chi-\varepsilon_{0}^{\prime}\varepsilon\omega_{0}+dd^{c}\frac{\underline{u}}{1+\varepsilon}]\big)\geq e^{G+c}+\delta_{0}-C_{0}\varepsilon.

Here the choice of
\varepsilon_{0}
depends on the form
\chi
and
\omega_{0}
, and
C_{0}
will additionally depend on an upper bound of
e^{G+c}
.

The next step will be to regularize the operator
f_{\infty}
to a smooth operator
g_{\varepsilon_{1}}
. More precisely, we have the following technical result:

Proposition 3.2
.

For any
0<\varepsilon<\frac{1}{2}
, there exists a function
g_{\varepsilon}(\lambda):\Gamma_{\infty}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that the following properties hold:

(1)

g_{\varepsilon}\in C^{\infty}(\Gamma_{\infty})
and symmetric in
(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n})\in\Gamma_{\infty}
,

(2)

(1-\varepsilon)f_{\infty}\leq g_{\varepsilon}\leq f_{\infty}+\frac{\pi}{2}\varepsilon
on
\Gamma_{\infty}
.

(3)

g_{\varepsilon}
is concave,
\frac{\partial g_{\varepsilon}}{\partial\lambda_{i}}>0
on
\Gamma_{\infty}
and that
\lim_{R\rightarrow+\infty}g_{\varepsilon}(R\lambda)=+\infty
for any
\lambda\in\Gamma_{\infty}
.

The uniform closeness of
g_{\varepsilon_{1}}
to
f_{\infty}
will make sure that a strict subsolution in terms of
f_{\infty}
will also be a strict subsolution of
g_{\varepsilon_{1}}
and vice versa. The smoothness of
g_{\varepsilon_{1}}
will be needed when we construct the strict subsolution locally. Then we need the Richberg technique to patch the local strict subsolution to a global one on
M
. More precisely, we have:

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
G\in C^{\infty}(M)
, we define
c
to be given by (
1.6
). Assume that for some
\delta_{1}>0
,
\varepsilon_{2}^{\prime}>0
, there exists
u_{1}\in C(M)
that satisfies the following in the viscosity sense, for any
\varepsilon_{1}
small enough:

(3.2)

\lambda[\chi-\varepsilon_{2}^{\prime}\omega_{0}+dd^{c}u_{1}]\in\Gamma_{\infty},\,\,g_{\varepsilon_{1}}\big(\lambda[\chi-\varepsilon_{2}^{\prime}\omega_{0}+dd^{c}u_{1}\big)\geq e^{G(x)+c}+\delta_{1}.

Then for such
\varepsilon_{1}>0
and any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists

such that:

(1)

\lambda[\chi+dd^{c}u_{\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon}]\in\Gamma_{\infty}
and
g_{\varepsilon_{1}}\big(\lambda[\chi+dd^{c}u_{\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon}]\big)\geq e^{G(x)+c}+\delta_{1}-\varepsilon

(2)

||u_{\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon}-\underline{u}||_{L^{\infty}}\leq\varepsilon
.

First we explain how to use Lemma
3.1
, as well as Proposition
3.2
and
3.3
below to prove Theorem
3.1
.

Proof.

(of Theorem
3.1
) We fix

, First we may use Lemma
3.1
to get that:

\lambda[\chi-\varepsilon_{0}^{\prime}\varepsilon\omega_{0}+dd^{c}\frac{\underline{u}}{1+\varepsilon}]\in\Gamma_{\infty},\,\,\,f_{\infty}\big(\lambda[\chi-\varepsilon_{0}^{\prime}\varepsilon\omega_{0}+dd^{c}\frac{\underline{u}}{1+\varepsilon}]\big)\geq e^{G+c}+\delta_{0}-C_{0}\varepsilon.

Also we have

∎

Now we prove Lemma
3.1

Proof.

(of Lemma

)
Since
\lambda[\chi]\in\Gamma_{\infty}

\lambda[\chi-\varepsilon_{0}\omega]\in\Gamma_{\infty}
.

\lambda[\chi+dd^{c}P](x_{0})\in\Gamma_{\infty},\,\,f_{\infty}\big(\lambda[\chi+dd^{c}P]\big)(x_{0})\geq e^{G+c}+\delta_{0}.

For any
\varepsilon>0

Next we use the concavity of
F_{\infty}
:

(3.3)

\begin{split}&f_{\infty}\big(\lambda[\chi+dd^{c}P])(x_{0})\leq f_{\infty}\big(\lambda[\varepsilon(\chi-\varepsilon_{0}\omega_{0})+\chi+dd^{c}P]\big)(x_{0})\\
&+\frac{\partial F_{\infty}}{\partial h_{i\bar{j}}}\big(\lambda[\varepsilon(\chi-\varepsilon_{0}\omega_{0})+\chi+dd^{c}P]\big)(x_{0})\varepsilon\cdot(-\chi_{i\bar{j}}+\varepsilon_{0}(\omega_{0})_{i\bar{j}})\leq 0.\end{split}

The last inequality follows from the following Lemma
3.4
with
A_{i\bar{j}}=\varepsilon(\chi_{i\bar{j}}-\varepsilon_{0}(\omega_{0})_{i\bar{j}})+\chi_{i\bar{j}}+P_{i\bar{j}}
,
B_{i\bar{j}}=\chi_{i\bar{j}}-\varepsilon_{0}(\omega_{0})_{i\bar{j}}
.
Therefore we get:

(1+\varepsilon)f_{\infty}\big(\lambda[\chi-\frac{\varepsilon\varepsilon_{0}}{1+\varepsilon}\omega_{0}+dd^{c}\frac{P}{1+\varepsilon}]\big)\geq f_{\infty}\big(\lambda[(1+\varepsilon)\chi-\varepsilon\varepsilon_{0}\omega_{0}+dd^{c}P]\big)\geq e^{G+c}+\delta_{0}.

Therefore,

f_{\infty}\big(\lambda[\chi-\frac{\varepsilon\varepsilon_{0}}{1+\varepsilon}\omega_{0}+dd^{c}\frac{P}{1+\varepsilon}]\big)\geq\frac{e^{G+c}+\delta_{0}}{1+\varepsilon}\geq e^{G+c}+\delta_{0}-C_{0}\varepsilon.

Here
C_{0}
depends on an upper bound of
e^{G+c}
. Also we may take
\varepsilon_{0}^{\prime}=\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon_{0}
because
\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{1+\varepsilon}\geq\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon_{0}
with
\varepsilon
small enough.
∎

In the following, we use the following lemma which can be found in the Lemma 4.7 of
[
2
]
:

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
A
,
B
be two Hermitian matrices such that
\lambda(g^{i\bar{k}}A_{j\bar{k}}),\,\lambda(g^{i\bar{k}}B_{j\bar{k}})\in\Gamma
. Define
F(h)=f\big(\lambda(g^{i\bar{k}}h_{j\bar{k}})\big)
. Then we have:

\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}\frac{\partial F}{\partial h_{j\bar{k}}}(A)B_{j\bar{k}}\geq 0.

3.1.
Regularization of
f_{\infty}

The goal of this subsection is to prove Proposition
3.2
. It will follow from the following general regularization result of a strictly convex function.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open convex subset (possibly unbounded). Let
u
be a strictly convex function on
\Omega
. Let
h\in C(\Omega)
with
h>0
. Then there exists a smooth convex function
\tilde{u}
defined on
\Omega
with
u\leq\tilde{u}\leq u+h
and
D^{2}\tilde{u}>0
on
\Omega
.

Before we prove this proposition, first we explain how to use it to prove Proposition
3.2
.

Proof.

(of Proposition
3.2
) Let
0<\varepsilon<1
. We can take
\Omega=\Gamma_{\infty}
and we must modify
-f_{\infty}
to be strictly convex in order to apply Proposition
3.5
. Therefore, we consider:

(3.4)

\widetilde{f}_{\varepsilon}(\lambda)=f_{\infty}(\lambda)+\frac{\varepsilon}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\arctan(\sum_{k\neq i}\lambda_{k}),

for any
\lambda\in\overline{\Gamma}_{\infty}.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
\widetilde{f}_{\varepsilon}
be defined by (
3.4
). Then we have that
-\widetilde{f}_{\varepsilon}
is strictly convex on
\Gamma_{\infty}
in the sense defined by Definition
2.4
.

Proof.

Since we already know that
-f_{\infty}
is convex, it suffices to show that
-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\arctan(\sum_{k\neq i}\lambda_{k})
is strictly convex. Since
-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\arctan(\sum_{k\neq i}\lambda_{k})
is smooth, the strict convexity can be follows from the positivity of its Hessian matrix. Denote
v_{i}=\sum_{k\neq i}e_{k}.
Then we have that the Hessian matrix of
-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\arctan(\sum_{k\neq i}\lambda_{k})
is

\text{Hess}_{pq}=-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\arctan^{\prime\prime}(\sum_{k\neq i}\lambda_{k})(v_{i})_{p}\cdot(v_{i})_{q}.

According to the Proposition
2.13
, we have that for
\sum_{k\neq i}\lambda_{k}>0
on
\Gamma_{\infty}
. Since
\arctan^{\prime\prime}(t)<0
for any
t>0
, we have that
-\arctan^{\prime\prime}(\sum_{k\neq i}\lambda_{k})>0
for each
i
. Since
\{v_{i}\}
is a basis of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have that
M
is a positive matrix. Indeed, for
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, one has:

\begin{split}&\sum_{p,q=1}^{n}\text{Hess}_{pq}\xi_{p}\xi_{q}=-\sum_{p,q=1}^{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\arctan^{\prime\prime}(\sum_{k\neq i}\lambda_{k})(v_{i})_{p}\xi_{p}(v_{i})_{q}\xi_{q}\\
&=-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\arctan^{\prime\prime}(\sum_{k\neq i}\lambda_{k})|(v_{i},\xi)|^{2}\geq 0.\end{split}

If it is zero, we would have
(v_{i},\xi)=0
for all
i
. Since
\{v_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq n}
forms a basis, we see that one must have
\xi=0
. This proves
\text{Hess}>0
.
∎

In summary, now we have that
\tilde{f}_{\varepsilon}
satisfies:

(1)

\tilde{f}_{\varepsilon}
is strictly concave, symmetric, and
\frac{\partial\tilde{f}_{\varepsilon}}{\partial\lambda_{i}}>0,\,\,1\leq i\leq n
,

(2)

f_{\infty}\leq\tilde{f}_{\varepsilon}\leq f_{\infty}+\frac{\pi}{2}\varepsilon
.

In the above
f_{\infty}\leq\tilde{f}_{\varepsilon}
used Proposition
2.13
.

Now we wish to apply Proposition
3.5
to
-\tilde{f}_{\varepsilon}
which is strictly convex, and
h=\varepsilon f_{\infty}
, which is strictly positive on
\Gamma_{\infty}
.
Therefore, we get a strictly convex smooth function
-\hat{f}_{\varepsilon}(\lambda)
defined on
\Gamma_{\infty}
which satisfies:

-\tilde{f}_{\varepsilon}\leq-\hat{f}_{\varepsilon}\leq-\tilde{f}_{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon f_{\infty}.

Finally, we need to make the function symmetric in all variables, so we define:

g_{\varepsilon}(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n})=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n}}\hat{f}_{\varepsilon}\big(\lambda_{\sigma(1)},\cdots,\lambda_{\sigma(n)}\big),

where
S_{n}
denotes the group of permutations of
n
elements.
Now it only remains to check item (1)-(3) in Proposition
3.2
. Item (1) is clear. To see item (2), we note that:

(3.5)

\hat{f}_{\varepsilon}\leq\tilde{f}_{\varepsilon}\leq f_{\infty}+\frac{\pi}{2}\varepsilon,\,\,\,\,\hat{f}_{\varepsilon}\geq\tilde{f}_{\varepsilon}-\varepsilon f_{\infty}\geq(1-\varepsilon)f_{\infty}.

Since
f_{\infty}
is symmetric in
(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n})
, (
3.5
) would carry over to
g_{\varepsilon}
, hence we have:

(3.6)

(1-\varepsilon)f_{\infty}\leq g_{\varepsilon}\leq f_{\infty}+\frac{\pi}{2}\varepsilon.

This verifies (2).

The strict concavity of
g_{\varepsilon}
follows from the strict concavity of
\hat{f}_{\varepsilon}
which is built into the construction since we have
D^{2}\hat{f}_{\varepsilon}<0
. Therefore we have
D^{2}g_{\varepsilon}<0
on
\Gamma_{\infty}
. It only remains to check
\frac{\partial g_{\varepsilon}}{\partial\lambda_{i}}>0
. If not, then there exists
\lambda_{0}\in\Gamma_{\infty}
such that
\frac{\partial g_{\varepsilon}}{\partial\lambda_{i}}(\lambda_{0})\leq 0
. Now we consider the function
k(t):=t\mapsto g_{\varepsilon}(\lambda_{0}+te_{i})
. We know that
k^{\prime\prime}(t)<0
(since
D^{2}g_{\varepsilon}<0
) and
k^{\prime}(0)\leq 0
. Hence
k^{\prime}(t)\leq-\varepsilon^{\prime}
for
t\in[1,+\infty)
. In particular,
k(t)\rightarrow-\infty
as
t\rightarrow+\infty
. On the other hand,
g_{\varepsilon}\geq(1-\varepsilon)f_{\infty}>0
on
\Gamma_{\infty}
, which is a contradiction.

Finally we observe that
\lim_{R\rightarrow+\infty}g_{\varepsilon}(R\lambda)=+\infty
, which follows from
g_{\varepsilon}\geq(1-\varepsilon)f_{\infty}
as well as Lemma
2.10
.
∎

Now it only remains to establish Proposition
3.5
which we are going to use Richberg technique.
The key ingredient in the Richberg technique is the notion of regularized maximum. Define:

(3.7)

M(t)=\max\big(t_{1},\cdots,t_{m}),\,\,\,(t_{1},\cdots,t_{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}.

Let
\varphi(s)\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
be an even function such that
\varphi\geq 0
,
supp\,\varphi\subset[-1,1]
and
\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(s)ds=1
. For each
(\varepsilon_{1},\cdots,\varepsilon_{m})\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{m}
, we put

\varphi_{\varepsilon}(y)=\varphi(\frac{y_{1}}{\varepsilon_{1}})\cdots\varphi(\frac{y_{m}}{\varepsilon_{m}})\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{1}\cdots\varepsilon_{m}},\,\,y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}.

We then define:

M_{\varepsilon}(t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}M(t+y)\varphi_{\varepsilon}(y)dy,\,\,\,t\in\mathbb{R}^{m}.

The function
M_{\varepsilon}(t)
is called the regularized maximum of
t_{1},\,t_{2},\cdots,t_{m}
.
The following lemma is taken from Harvey-Lawson-Pliś
[
10
]
, Properties 2.1:

Lemma 3.7
.

(1)

M_{\varepsilon}(t)
is monotone increasing in all variables, smooth and convex on
\mathbb{R}^{m}
,

(2)

M_{\varepsilon}(t)
is symmetric in the following sense: for any
i,\,j\in\{1,\cdots,m\}
, if we swap
\varepsilon_{i}
and
\varepsilon_{j}
, and
t_{i}
and
t_{j}
, the value of
M_{\varepsilon}(t)
does not change.

(3)

M_{\varepsilon}(t+se)=M_{\varepsilon}(t)+s
, where
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
e=(1,\cdots,1)\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
. As an immediate consequence:

\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{\partial M_{\varepsilon}}{\partial t_{j}}\equiv 1.

(4)

M(t)\leq M_{\varepsilon}(t)\leq M(t+\varepsilon)
. Here
\varepsilon=(\varepsilon_{1},\cdots,\varepsilon_{m})
.

(5)

If
t_{j}+\varepsilon_{j}\leq\max_{i\neq j}(t_{i}-\varepsilon_{i})
, then

M_{\varepsilon}(t)=M_{\varepsilon_{1},\cdots,\hat{\varepsilon}_{j},\cdots,\varepsilon_{m}}\big(t_{1},\cdots,\hat{t}_{j},\cdots,t_{m}\big).

Here
\hat{\varepsilon}_{j}
and
\hat{t}_{j}
means this component is skipped.

Using the regularized maximum, we can formulate the following gluing lemma:

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
\Omega
,
u
,
h
be as in Proposition
3.5
. Assume that there exists a locally finite family of bounded open sets
\Omega_{\alpha}
with
\bar{\Omega}_{\alpha}\subset\Omega
together with the following properties hold:

(1)

Each
\Omega_{\alpha}
is convex and contains a compact subset
K_{\alpha}
such that
\Omega\subset\cup_{\alpha}K_{\alpha}
. Moreover,
\sup_{\bar{\Omega}_{\alpha}}u\leq\inf_{\bar{\Omega}_{\alpha}}(u+\frac{h}{2})
.

(2)

For each
\alpha
, there exists
v_{\alpha}\in C^{\infty}(\bar{\Omega}_{\alpha})
and
0<\varepsilon_{\alpha}<\frac{1}{2}\inf_{\bar{\Omega}_{\alpha}}h
, such that
v_{\alpha}-\varepsilon_{\alpha}>u
on
K_{\alpha}
,
v_{\alpha}+\varepsilon_{\alpha}<u
on
\partial\Omega_{\alpha}
. Moreover, each
v_{\alpha}
satisfies
D^{2}v_{\alpha}\geq\varepsilon_{\alpha}I
on
\Omega_{\alpha}
.

We define:

\tilde{u}(x)=M_{\varepsilon}\{v_{\alpha}(x):x\in\Omega_{\alpha}\},\,\,\varepsilon=(\varepsilon_{\alpha})_{\alpha}.

Then one has:

(1)

\tilde{u}\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)
,
D^{2}\tilde{u}>0
on
\Omega
,

(2)

u\leq\tilde{u}\leq u+h
.

Proof.

First we prove that
\tilde{u}\in C^{\infty}(M)
. It will be sufficient to show that, for any
x\in M
, there is a finite subset
\mathcal{I}_{x}
of the index set
\alpha
, and a neighborhood
U
of
x
, such that:

\tilde{u}(y)=M_{\varepsilon}\{v_{\alpha}(y):\alpha\in\mathcal{I}_{x}\},\,\,\,y\in U.

Indeed, we are going to take
\mathcal{I}_{x}
to be the set of
\alpha
such that
x\in\Omega_{\alpha}
. Because of the local finiteness assumption, we see that
\mathcal{I}_{x}
must be finite.
Likewise, we define
\mathcal{I}^{\prime}_{x}
to be the set of
\alpha
such that
x\in\partial\Omega_{\alpha}
, and
\mathcal{I}^{\prime\prime}_{x}
to be the set of
\alpha
such that
x\in\bar{\Omega}_{\alpha}^{c}
. Using the local finiteness of
\Omega_{\alpha}
, we can find a neighborhood
U_{1}
of
x
such that

(1)

\bar{U}_{1}
only intersects with finitely many
\Omega_{\alpha}
,

(2)

U_{1}\subset\cap_{\alpha\in\mathcal{I}_{x}}\Omega_{\alpha}
,
U_{1}\subset\cap_{\alpha\in\mathcal{I}^{\prime\prime}_{x}}\bar{\Omega}_{\alpha}^{c}
.

That is, for
y\in U_{1}
,

w(y)=M_{\varepsilon}\{v_{\alpha}(y):\alpha\in\mathcal{I}_{x}\cup\mathcal{I}_{x}^{\prime},\,\,y\in\Omega_{\alpha}\}.

For any
\alpha\in\mathcal{I}_{x}^{\prime}
, one has
x\in\partial\Omega_{\alpha}
, so that by assumption (2), one has:

v_{\alpha}(x)+\varepsilon_{\alpha}<u(x).

On the other hand, since
M\subset\cup_{\alpha}K_{\alpha}
, there exists
\beta
such that
x\in K_{\beta}
, which implies:

v_{\beta}(x)-\varepsilon_{\beta}>u(x).

Therefore, one gets:

v_{\alpha}(x)+\varepsilon_{\alpha}<\max_{\alpha^{\prime}\in\mathcal{I}_{x}}(v_{\alpha^{\prime}}(x)-\varepsilon_{\alpha^{\prime}}).

Hence by continuity of
v_{\alpha}
, there is a neighborhood
U_{2}
of
x
, contained in
U_{1}
, such that for any
\alpha\in\mathcal{I}_{x}^{\prime}
(note that
\mathcal{I}_{x}^{\prime}
is a finite set because of local finiteness of the covering) and any
y\in U_{2}\cap\bar{\Omega}_{\alpha}
, one has

(3.8)

v_{\alpha}(y)+\varepsilon_{\alpha}\leq\max_{\alpha^{\prime}\in\mathcal{I}_{x}}(v_{\alpha^{\prime}}(y)-\varepsilon_{\alpha^{\prime}}),\,\,\,\alpha\in\mathcal{I}_{x}^{\prime}.

Hence we may use item (5) of Lemma
3.7
to conclude that for
y\in U_{2}
,
\alpha\in\mathcal{I}_{x}^{\prime}
,
v_{\alpha}(y)
can be skipped when taking the regularized maximum, that is:

\tilde{u}(y)=M_{\varepsilon}\{v_{\alpha}(y):\alpha\in\mathcal{I}_{x}\},\,\,\,y\in U_{2}.

Now we are done proving (1). Moreover, we know from local finiteness that for any
x_{0}\in\Omega
, there is a neighborhood
U
of
x_{0}
, such that:

\tilde{u}(y)=M_{\varepsilon}\{v_{\alpha_{j}}(y):1\leq j\leq m\},\,\,\,\,y\in U.

Then we can compute the Hessian of
\tilde{u}
:

\tilde{u}_{x_{i}x_{j}}(x_{0})=\sum_{p=1}^{m}\frac{\partial M_{\varepsilon}}{\partial t_{p}}(v_{\alpha_{p}})_{x_{i}x_{j}}+\sum_{p,q=1}^{m}\frac{\partial^{2}M_{\varepsilon}}{\partial t_{p}\partial t_{q}}(v_{\alpha_{p}})_{x_{i}}(v_{\alpha_{q}})_{x_{j}}.

Using that
\frac{\partial M_{\varepsilon}}{\partial t_{p}}\geq 0,\,\,\sum_{p=1}^{m}\frac{\partial M_{\varepsilon}}{\partial t_{p}}=1
, and that
M_{\varepsilon}
is convex from Lemma
3.7
,
we can conclude that
D^{2}\tilde{u}(x_{0})\geq\sum_{p=1}^{m}\frac{\partial M_{\varepsilon}}{\partial t_{p}}D^{2}v_{\alpha_{p}}\geq\sum_{p=1}^{m}\frac{\partial M_{\varepsilon}}{\partial t_{p}}\varepsilon_{p}I>0.

Next we show that
u\leq\tilde{u}\leq u+h
. For any
x_{0}\in\Omega
, there exists
\beta
so that
x_{0}\in K_{\beta}
and we have
u(x_{0})<v_{\beta}(x_{0})-\varepsilon_{\beta}
. In particular:

u(x_{0})\leq M\{v_{\alpha}(x_{0}):x_{0}\in\Omega_{\alpha}\}\leq M_{\varepsilon}\{v_{\alpha}(x_{0}):x_{0}\in\Omega_{\alpha}\}=\tilde{u}(x_{0}).

To prove the other inequality, we have:

\begin{split}&\tilde{u}(x_{0})=M_{\varepsilon}\{v_{\alpha_{j}}(x_{0}):1\leq j\leq m\}\leq M\{v_{\alpha_{j}}(x_{0})+\varepsilon_{\alpha_{j}}:1\leq j\leq m\}\\
&\leq M\{\sup_{\bar{\Omega}_{\alpha_{j}}}(v_{\alpha_{j}}(x)+\varepsilon_{\alpha_{j}}):1\leq j\leq m\}=M\{\sup_{\partial\Omega_{\alpha_{j}}}(v_{\alpha_{j}}(x)+\varepsilon_{\alpha_{j}}):1\leq j\leq m\}\\
&\leq M\{\sup_{\partial\Omega_{\alpha_{j}}}(u(x)+\varepsilon_{\alpha_{j}}):1\leq j\leq m\}\leq M\{\inf_{\bar{\Omega}_{\alpha_{j}}}(u(x)+\frac{h(x)}{2})+\varepsilon_{\alpha_{j}}\}\leq u(x_{0})+h(x_{0}).\end{split}

In the equality above on the second line, we used that each
v_{\alpha_{j}}
is convex, so
\sup
must be achieved on the boundary. In the first inequality of the last line, we used that
v_{\alpha_{j}}(x)<u(x)
on
\partial\Omega_{\alpha_{j}}
. In the last inequality of the last line, we used that each
\varepsilon_{\alpha_{j}}\leq\frac{1}{2}\inf_{\bar{\Omega}_{\alpha_{j}}}h\leq\frac{1}{2}h(x_{0})
.
∎

Our next goal is to construct the family of open sets
\Omega_{\alpha}
. For this we observe that:

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
u
,
\Omega
be as in Proposition
3.5
. Let
x_{0}\in\Omega
. Let
l_{x_{0}}
be an affine function such that
\{x\in\Omega:l_{x_{0}}(x)=u(x)\}=\{x_{0}\}
. For any
t>0
, we define:

(3.9)

S_{t}(x_{0}):=\{x\in\Omega:u(x)<l_{x_{0}}(x)+t\}.

Then one has
\mbox{diam}\,S_{t}(x_{0})\rightarrow 0
as
t\rightarrow 0+
.

Proof.

If not, there there is a sequence
t_{k}\rightarrow 0+
, and
\eta>0
such that
\mbox{diam}\,S_{t_{k}}(x_{0})\geq\eta
for all
k
. Hence we may choose
x_{k},\,y_{k}\in S_{t_{k}}(x_{0})
,
|x_{k}-y_{k}|\geq\frac{\eta}{2}
. That is,
u(x_{k})<l_{x_{0}}(x_{k})+t_{k},\,\,u(y_{k})<l_{x_{0}}(y_{k})+t_{k}
. From convexity, the segment
[x_{k},y_{k}]\subset S_{t_{k}}(x_{0})
. Passing to the limit, we see that
\{x\in\Omega:l_{x_{0}}(x)=u(x)\}
will contain a nontrivial segment with length
\geq\frac{\delta}{2}
, which is a contradiction.
∎

Now we are ready to prove Proposition
3.5
:

Proof.

For each
x_{0}\in\Omega
, we consider
S_{t}(x_{0})
defined by (
3.9
). From Lemma
3.9
, we know that
S_{t}(x_{0})
will be compactly contained in
\Omega
if
t
is small enough. Moreover, from Lemma
3.9
, we see that with
t
small enough one has
\sup_{\bar{S}_{t}(x_{0})}u\leq\inf_{\bar{S}_{t}(x_{0})}(u+\frac{h}{2})
. This is possible because of the continuity of
u
and
h
on
\Omega
and that
h
is strictly positive on
\Omega
. Denote this small enough
t
to be
t_{x_{0}}
.

Next we wish to find a sub-family of
\{S_{t_{x_{0}}}(x_{0})\}_{x_{0}\in\Omega}
which is locally finite on
\Omega
, after possibly decreasing
t_{x_{0}}
further. For this, we can choose a sequence of open subsets
\Omega_{j}\subset\Omega,\,j\geq 1
with the following properties:

(1)

For each
j\geq 1
,
\Omega_{j}
is bounded, with
\bar{\Omega}_{j}\subset\Omega_{j+1}
, and
\cup_{j}\Omega_{j}=\Omega
,

(2)

For
j\geq 3
,
\bar{\Omega}_{j}-\Omega_{j-1}
is a compact subset of
\Omega_{j+1}-\bar{\Omega}_{j-2}
.

First we consider
\bar{\Omega}_{2}
, which is compact, and we have a covering
\bar{\Omega}_{2}\subset\cup_{x_{0}\in\bar{\Omega}_{2}}S_{\frac{t_{x_{0}}}{2}}(x_{0})
. So we may take a finite sub-family
S_{\frac{t_{j}}{2}}(x_{j})
that covers
\bar{\Omega}_{2}
. Next for each
l\geq 3
, we consider
\bar{\Omega}_{l}-\Omega_{l-1}
. For
x_{0}\in\bar{\Omega}_{l}-\Omega_{l-1}
, we may need to decrease
t_{x_{0}}
so as to make sure that:

(3.10)

\bar{S}_{t_{x_{0}}}(x_{0})\subset\Omega_{l+1}-\bar{\Omega}_{l-2}.

This is possible because of Lemma
3.9
.
Then we take a finite family
S_{\frac{t_{j}}{2}}(x_{j})
with
x_{j}\in\bar{\Omega}_{l}-\Omega_{l-1}
which covers
\bar{\Omega}_{l}-\Omega_{l-1}
. Moreover,
\bar{S}_{t_{j}}(x_{j})\subset\Omega_{l+1}-\bar{\Omega}_{l-2}
.
We combine all the
S_{t_{j}}(x_{j})
obtained this way, and it is a locally finite family of open sets, due to (
3.10
).

Now we have a covering of
\Omega
by
S_{\frac{t_{j}}{2}}(x_{j}),\,j\geq 1
. We now wish to take
\{\Omega_{\alpha}\}
to be
S_{t_{j}}(x_{j})
and
K_{\alpha}=\bar{S}_{\frac{t_{j}}{2}}(x_{j})=\{x\in\Omega:u(x)\leq l_{x_{j}}(x)+\frac{t_{j}}{2}\}
. Then we wish to take

v_{j}(x)=l_{x_{j}}(x)+\frac{3t_{j}}{4}+\varepsilon_{j}|x-x_{j}|^{2}.

We see that, with
\varepsilon_{j}>0
chosen small enough, we can guarantee that
v_{j}(x)>u(x)+\varepsilon_{j}
on
K_{j}
,
v_{j}(x)<u(x)-\varepsilon_{j}
on

and
\varepsilon_{j}<\frac{1}{2}\inf_{\bar{\Omega}_{j}}h
. Moreover,

.
Therefore, we have verified the assumptions of Lemma
3.8
and it gives us the function asserted in Proposition
3.5
.
∎

3.2.
Regularization of the strict viscosity subsolution

The goal of this section is to prove Proposition
3.3
.

For the remainder of this section, we will simply denote
g_{\varepsilon_{1}}
by

for simplicity. We have the following gluing lemma which is the analogue of Lemma
3.8
.

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
\varepsilon_{1.1}>0
. Let
u_{1}
be as assumed in Proposition
3.3
. Assume that there exists a finite family of open sets
\Omega_{\alpha}\subset M
with the following properties hold:

(1)

Each
\Omega_{\alpha}

such that

. Moreover,
\sup_{\bar{\Omega}_{\alpha}}u_{1}\leq\inf_{\bar{\Omega}_{\alpha}}u_{1}+\varepsilon_{1.1}.

For each
\alpha

\varepsilon_{\alpha}\in(0,\varepsilon_{1.1}]

v_{\alpha}+\varepsilon_{\alpha}<u_{1}

. Moreover, there exists

(3)

For each

\sup_{\bar{\Omega}_{\alpha}}v_{\alpha}\leq\sup_{\partial\Omega_{\alpha}}v_{\alpha}+\varepsilon_{1.1}
.

We define:

w(x)=M_{\varepsilon}\{v_{\alpha}(x):x\in\Omega_{\alpha}\},\,\,\varepsilon=(\varepsilon_{\alpha})_{\alpha}.

Then one has:

(1)

w\in C^{\infty}(M)

(2)

\lambda[\chi+dd^{c}w]\in\Gamma_{\infty}
and
g\big(\lambda[\chi+dd^{c}w]\big)\geq e^{G+c}+\delta
.

(3)

u_{1}\leq w\leq u_{1}+3\varepsilon_{1.1}
.

The point of this lemma reduces the construction of a global smooth subsolution to the construction of
v_{\alpha}
’s, which is purely local. The proof essentially follows the Theorem 1.2 of
[
10
]
carried out in our special setting.

Proof.

(of Lemma
3.10
)
First we prove that
w\in C^{\infty}(M)
. The proof follows word-for-word as the proof of the item (1) in Lemma
3.8
. In this proof, we will only need to use
M\subset\cup_{\alpha}K_{\alpha}
and
v_{\alpha}-\varepsilon_{\alpha}>u_{1}
on
K_{\alpha}
,
v_{\alpha}+\varepsilon_{\alpha}<u_{1}
.

Next we prove (2). Let
x_{0}\in M
, we have shown that there is a neighborhood
U
of
x_{0}
and a subfamily of
\Omega_{\alpha}
, denoted as
\Omega_{\alpha_{j}},\,1\leq j\leq m
, such that:

w(y)=M_{\varepsilon}\{v_{\alpha_{j}}(y):1\leq j\leq m\}.

Then we may calculate:

dd^{c}w=\sum_{p,q=1}^{m}\frac{\partial^{2}M_{\varepsilon}}{\partial t_{p}\partial t_{q}}dv_{\alpha_{p}}\wedge d^{c}v_{\alpha_{q}}+\sum_{p=1}^{m}\frac{\partial M_{\varepsilon}}{\partial t_{p}}dd^{c}v_{\alpha_{p}}.

Therefore,

\chi+dd^{c}w=\sum_{p,q=1}^{m}\frac{\partial^{2}M_{\varepsilon}}{\partial t_{p}\partial t_{q}}dv_{\alpha_{p}}\wedge d^{c}v_{\alpha_{q}}+\sum_{p=1}^{m}\frac{\partial M_{\varepsilon}}{\partial t_{p}}(\chi+dd^{c}v_{\alpha_{p}})\geq\sum_{p=1}^{m}\frac{\partial M_{\varepsilon}}{\partial t_{p}}(\chi+dd^{c}v_{\alpha_{p}}).

Here we used the convexity of
M_{\varepsilon}
. Since one has
\frac{\partial M_{\varepsilon}}{\partial t_{p}}\geq 0,\,\,1\leq p\leq m
, and
\sum_{p=1}^{m}\frac{\partial M_{\varepsilon}}{\partial t_{p}}=1
, we see that
\lambda[\chi+dd^{c}w]\in\Gamma_{\infty}
, using that
\lambda[\chi+dd^{c}v_{\alpha_{p}}]\in\Gamma_{\infty}
for each
p
. Here we are also using the elementary fact that
\{A\text{ is $n\times n$ Hermitian matrix}:\lambda(A)\in\Gamma_{\infty}\}
is also a closed convex cone (in the space of Hermitian matrices).

Next, we have:

\begin{split}&g\big(\lambda[\chi+dd^{c}w]\big)\geq g\big(\lambda[\sum_{p=1}^{m}\frac{\partial M_{\varepsilon}}{\partial t_{p}}(\chi+dd^{c}v_{\alpha_{p}})]\big)\geq\sum_{p=1}^{m}\frac{\partial M_{\varepsilon}}{\partial t_{p}}g\big(\lambda[\chi+dd^{c}v_{\alpha_{p}}]\big)\\
&\geq e^{G+c}+\delta.\end{split}

The first inequality is due to that since
\chi+dd^{c}w\geq\sum_{p=1}^{m}\frac{\partial M_{\varepsilon}}{\partial t_{p}}(\chi+dd^{c}v_{\alpha_{p}})
, one has

\lambda(\chi+dd^{c}w)=\lambda\big(\sum_{p=1}^{m}\frac{\partial M_{\varepsilon}}{\partial t_{p}}(\chi+dd^{c}v_{\alpha_{p}})\big)+\tilde{\lambda},\,\,\,\tilde{\lambda}\in\Gamma_{n}.

The second inequality used that since
g(\lambda)
is concave,
A\mapsto g(\lambda(A))
is also concave, as a function in the space of Hermitian matrices. Now we have proved (2).

Finally we verify (3). We fix
x_{0}\in M
.
To see
u\leq w
, we note that there exists
\beta
so that
x_{0}\in K_{\beta}
and we have
u(x_{0})<v(x_{0})-\varepsilon_{\beta}
. In particular:

u(x_{0})\leq M\{v_{\alpha}(x_{0}):x_{0}\in\Omega_{\alpha}\}\leq M_{\varepsilon}\{v_{\alpha}(x_{0}):x_{0}\in\Omega_{\alpha}\}=w(x_{0}).

The second inequality used item (4) in Lemma
3.7
. To prove the other inequality, first we can write
w(x_{0})=M_{\varepsilon}\{v_{\alpha_{j}}(x_{0}):1\leq j\leq m\}
. We have

\begin{split}&w(x_{0})=M_{\varepsilon}\{v_{\alpha_{j}}(x_{0}):1\leq j\leq m\}\leq M\{v_{\alpha_{j}}(x_{0})+\varepsilon_{\alpha_{j}}:1\leq j\leq m\}\\
&\leq M\{\sup_{x\in\partial\Omega_{\alpha_{j}}}v_{\alpha_{j}}(x)+\varepsilon_{1.1}+\varepsilon_{\alpha_{j}}:1\leq j\leq m\}\leq M\{\sup_{x\in\partial\Omega_{\alpha_{j}}}u(x)+\varepsilon_{1.1}+\varepsilon_{\alpha_{j}}:1\leq j\leq m\}\\
&\leq M\{\inf_{\Omega_{\alpha_{j}}}u(x)+2\varepsilon_{1.1}+\varepsilon_{\alpha_{j}}:1\leq j\leq m\}\leq u(x_{0})+3\varepsilon_{1.1}.\end{split}

In the first inequality above, we used item (4) of Lemma
3.7
. In the second inequality, we used item (3) of the assumption that
\sup_{\bar{\Omega}_{\alpha}}v_{\alpha}\leq\sup_{\partial\Omega_{\alpha}}v_{\alpha}+\varepsilon_{1.1}
. In the third inequality, we used that
v_{\alpha}<u
on
\partial\Omega_{\alpha}
. In the fourth inequality, we used item (1) of the assumption that
\sup_{\bar{\Omega}_{\alpha}}u_{1}\leq\inf_{\bar{\Omega}_{\alpha}}u_{1}+\varepsilon_{1.1}
. The last inequality used that
x_{0}\in\Omega_{\alpha_{j}}
and that
\varepsilon_{\alpha_{j}}\leq\varepsilon_{1.1}
.
∎

Our next goal is to construct the local strict subsolution
v_{\alpha}
. We are going to do it via solving a Dirichlet problem of complex Hessian equation defined by the operator
g
.
More precisely, we are going to need the following lemma:

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
x_{0}\in M
, we choose normal coordinate at
x_{0}
such that
(\omega_{0})_{i\bar{j}}(x_{0})=\delta_{ij}
. Denote
B_{r}
to be the (open) ball centered at

with radius

small enough depending only on the background metric, such that for any

, and any

with

(3.11)

\inf_{\bar{B}_{r}}\psi>\lim\sup_{\lambda\rightarrow\partial\Gamma_{\infty}}g,

there is a unique solution to the Dirichlet problem:

g\big(\lambda[\chi+dd^{c}v]\big)=\psi\text{ in $B_{r}$},\,\,\,v=\varphi\text{ on $\partial B_{r}$},\,\,\lambda[\chi+dd^{c}v]\in\Gamma_{\infty},\,\,v\in C^{\infty}(\bar{B}_{r}).

This result follows from a result by Yuan
[
18
]
, Theorem 9.2:

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
M
be a Hermitian manifold with boundary, equipped with the Hermitian metric
\omega_{0}
. Let
\psi\in C^{\infty}(M)
,
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\partial M)
. We consider the following Dirichlet problem:

(3.12)

f\big(\lambda[\chi+dd^{c}u]\big)=\psi,\,\,\,\lambda[\chi+dd^{c}u]\in\Gamma\,\,\text{ in $M$},\,\,\,\,u=\varphi\text{ on $\partial M$}.

Assume that

(1)

f
and
\Gamma

1.1
and

\inf_{M}\psi>\lim\sup_{\lambda\rightarrow\partial\Gamma}f(\lambda)

(2)

There exists a subsolution

f\big(\lambda[\chi+dd^{c}u_{0}]\big)\geq\psi,\,\,\lambda[\chi+dd^{c}u_{0}]\in\Gamma\text{ in $M$},\,\,\,u_{0}=\varphi\text{ on $\partial M$}.

(3)

For any
x_{0}\in\partial M
, there exists
R>0
sufficiently large, such that
(-\kappa_{1},\cdots,-\kappa_{n-1},R)\in\Gamma_{\mathcal{G}}^{f}

Then (
3.12

Next let us check that one may apply Theorem
3.2
in our case:

Proof.

(of Lemma
3.11
) In Theorem
3.2
, we are going to take the manifold with boundary to be

, which is the coordinate ball centered at

. Also the

. The assumption (

. It is clear that
u_{0}=\varphi
on
\partial B_{r}(0)
. With

chosen sufficiently large, one would have
\lambda[\chi+dd^{c}u_{0}]\in\Gamma

as

. So we have (2). To see (3), we note that the Levi form of

will be negative definite, and

∎

Using this solvability result, we can construct the functions

assumed in Lemma

,

4.
Stability estimate and existence

4.1.
Existence

In this subsection, we are going to use an approximation argument to obtain a viscosity solution to:

f\big(\lambda[\chi+dd^{c}\varphi]\big)=e^{G(x)+c},\,\,\,\lambda[\chi+dd^{c}\varphi]\in\Gamma,

for some
c\in\mathbb{R}
. Here
G\in C(M)
.

First we can find a sequence
G_{j}\in C^{\infty}(M)
such that
G_{j}\rightarrow G
uniformly. Let
\varepsilon_{j}\rightarrow 0+
. First we want to make sure that the equation is solvable with right hand side
G_{j}
:

Lemma 4.1
.

For large enough
j
, the equation
f\big(\lambda[\chi+dd^{c}\varphi_{j}]\big)=e^{G_{j}(x)+c_{j}}
has a smooth solution for some
c_{j}\in\mathbb{R}
.

To prove this, we need to make sure that the solvability constant
c_{j}
will converge to the correct constant:

Lemma 4.2
.

Define

e^{c_{j}}=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{E}_{\Gamma,\chi}}\max_{M}e^{-G_{j}(x)}f\big(\lambda[(\chi+dd^{c}u]\big).

and

e^{c}=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{E}_{\Gamma,\chi}}\max_{M}e^{-G(x)}f\big(\lambda[\chi+dd^{c}u]\big).

Then one has:

e^{c}\cdot e^{-||G_{j}-G||_{L^{\infty}}}\leq e^{c_{j}}\leq e^{c}\cdot e^{||G_{j}-G||_{L^{\infty}}}.

In particular, one has
c_{j}\rightarrow c
.
Here we recall that
\mathcal{E}_{\Gamma,\chi}
denotes the set of smooth functions
u
on
M
such that
\lambda[\chi+dd^{c}u]\in\Gamma
.

Now we are ready to prove Lemma
4.1
.

Proof.

(Of Lemma
4.1
)
This essentially follows from the existence of a smooth subsolution constructed in Section 3. Indeed, we have shown that for some
\tilde{\delta}_{0}>0
, there exists a smooth function
\tilde{u}
such that:

(4.1)

f_{\infty}\big(\lambda[\chi+dd^{c}\tilde{u}]\big)\geq e^{G(x)+c}+\tilde{\delta}_{0},\,\,\,\lambda[\chi+dd^{c}\tilde{u}]\in\Gamma_{\infty},

where the constant
c
is given by:

(4.2)

e^{c}=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{E}_{\Gamma,\chi}}\max_{M}e^{-G(x)}f\big(\lambda[\chi+dd^{c}u]\big).

The corresponding constant for
G_{j}
is given as:

e^{c_{j}}=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{E}_{\Gamma,\chi}}\max_{M}e^{-G_{j}(x)}f\big(\lambda[\chi+dd^{c}u]\big).

We have shown that
c_{j}\rightarrow c
. Since
G_{j}\rightarrow G
uniformly, we see that with
j
sufficiently large, one has:

f_{\infty}\big(\lambda[\chi+dd^{c}\tilde{u}]\big)\geq e^{G_{j}+c_{j}}+\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\delta}_{0}.

From Guo-Song
[
9
]
, we know that for such
j
there exists a smooth solution to:

(4.3)

f\big(\lambda[\chi+dd^{c}\varphi_{j}]\big)=e^{G_{j}(x)+c_{j}},\,\,\,\lambda[\chi+dd^{c}\varphi_{j}]\in\Gamma,\,\,\,\sup_{M}\varphi_{j}=0.

∎

The next step is to show that there is a subsequence of
\varphi_{j}
that converges uniformly, thereby we would get a viscosity solution (with right hand side
G(x)
and constant
c
) in the limit.

First we observe that the sequence
\varphi_{j}
is uniformly bounded due to the work by Szekelyhidi
[
14
]
. Using this, we observe that
\nabla\varphi_{j}
is uniformly bounded in
L^{2}
:

Lemma 4.3
.

There exists a constant
C>0
, depending only on the uniform
C^{0}
bound of
G_{j}
as well as the background metric
\omega_{0}
, such that:

\int_{M}d^{c}\varphi_{j}\wedge d\varphi_{j}\wedge\omega_{0}^{n-1}\leq C.

Proof.

We use that each
\varphi_{j}
is
\Gamma
-subharmonic, and that
\Gamma\subset\Gamma_{1}=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}>0\}.
This in particular implies:

tr_{\omega_{0}}(\chi+dd^{c}\varphi_{j})\geq 0.

This would imply, for some
C_{1}>0
:

tr_{\omega_{0}}(dd^{c}\varphi_{j})\geq-C_{1},

which is equivalent to:

(4.4)

dd^{c}\varphi_{j}\wedge\omega_{0}^{n-1}\geq-\frac{C_{1}}{n}\omega_{0}^{n}.

Let
C_{2}>0
be sufficiently large such that
\varphi_{j}+C_{2}>0
for all
j
, then we multiply this to both sides of (
4.4
) to obtain:

-\int_{M}d\varphi_{j}\wedge d^{c}\varphi_{j}\wedge\omega_{0}^{n-1}+\int_{M}(\varphi_{j}+C_{2})d^{c}\varphi_{j}\wedge d\omega_{0}^{n-1}\geq\int_{M}(-\frac{C_{1}}{n})(\varphi_{j}+C_{2})\omega_{0}^{n}.

For the middle term, we have:

\begin{split}&\int_{M}(\varphi_{j}+C_{2})d^{c}\varphi_{j}\wedge d\omega_{0}^{n-1}=\int_{M}d^{c}\big(\frac{(\varphi_{j}+C_{2})^{2}}{2}\big)\wedge d\omega_{0}^{n-1}=-\int_{M}d\big(\frac{(\varphi_{j}+C_{2})^{2}}{2}\big)\wedge d^{c}\omega_{0}^{n-1}\\
&=\int_{M}\frac{(\varphi_{j}+C_{2})^{2}}{2}dd^{c}\omega_{0}^{n-1}.\end{split}

Therefore, we see that
\int_{M}d\varphi_{j}\wedge d^{c}\varphi_{j}\wedge\omega_{0}^{n-1}
is uniformly bounded from above.
∎

Therefore we are in a position to apply Rellich compact embedding theorem to extract a subsequence of
\{\varphi_{j}\}
, denoted as
\{\varphi_{j_{k}}\}
, such that
\varphi_{j_{k}}
converges in
L^{1}
. In order to get a viscosity solution, we need to upgrade the
L^{1}
convergence to uniform convergence, which is why we need the stability estimates stated in Theorem
5.1
below. Now we explain how to use Theorem
5.1
to get the needed upgrade:

Lemma 4.4
.

Assume that there exists a smooth function
\tilde{u}
on
M
such that for some
\tilde{\delta}_{0}>0
,

f_{\infty}\big(\lambda[\chi+dd^{c}\tilde{u}]\big)\geq e^{G(x)+c}+\tilde{\delta}_{0},\,\,\lambda[\chi+dd^{c}\tilde{u}]\in\Gamma_{\infty}.

Let

be the solution to (

Proof.

We wish to use Theorem

, then we have:

\lambda[\tilde{\chi}]\in\Gamma_{\infty},\,\,f_{\infty}\big(\lambda[\tilde{\chi}]\big)\geq e^{G(x)+c}+\tilde{\delta}_{0}.

Since

, we see that, with

Moreover, if we put

As a consequence, we immediately see that:

Therefore,

\begin{split}&A_{\delta,s,\kappa}=||((1-\delta)v-\phi-s)_{+}e^{nG_{0}}||_{L^{\kappa}(\omega_{0}^{n})}\leq||((1-\delta)v-\phi-s)_{+}||_{L^{\frac{n+1}{n}q}(\omega_{0}^{n})}||e^{nG_{0}}||_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\Omega_{\delta,s},\omega_{0}^{n})}\\
&\leq C_{2}(q)^{\frac{n}{q(n+1)}}\delta^{\frac{n}{n+1}}A_{\delta,s,\kappa}^{\frac{1}{n+1}}||e^{nG_{0}}||_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\Omega_{\delta,s},\omega_{0}^{n})}\leq C_{3}(q)\delta^{\frac{n}{n+1}}A_{\delta,s,\kappa}^{\frac{1}{n+1}}||e^{nG_{0}}||^{\lambda}_{L^{1}(\Omega_{\delta,s},\omega_{0}^{n})}||e^{nG_{0}}||_{L^{p_{0}}}^{1-\lambda}.\end{split}

In the above,
q^{\prime}
is chosen such that
\frac{1}{\kappa}=\frac{n}{(n+1)q}+\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}
. The second inequality used (
4.22
), and in the last inequality,
\lambda
satisfies:
\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}=\lambda+\frac{1-\lambda}{p_{0}}
. Therefore we get:

(4.23)

A_{\delta,s,\kappa}\leq C_{4}(q)\delta||e^{nG_{0}}||_{L^{p_{0}}}^{\frac{n+1}{n}(1-\lambda)}||e^{nG_{0}}||_{L^{1}(\Omega_{\delta,s},\omega_{0}^{n})}^{\lambda\frac{n+1}{n}}.

On the other hand, for any
t>0
,

(4.24)

\begin{split}&A_{\delta,s,\kappa}=||\big((1-\delta)v-\phi-s\big)_{+}e^{nG_{0}}||_{L^{\kappa}(\omega_{0}^{n})}\geq||\big((1-\delta)v-\phi-s\big)_{+}e^{nG_{0}}||_{L^{1}(\omega_{0}^{n})}\\
&\geq t||e^{nG_{0}}||_{L^{1}(\Omega_{s+t},\omega_{0}^{n})}.\end{split}

Combining (
4.23
) and (
4.24
) we see that

(4.25)

t||e^{nG_{0}}||_{L^{1}(\Omega_{s+t},\omega_{0}^{n})}\leq C_{5}\delta||e^{nG_{0}}||_{L^{1}(\Omega_{\delta,s},\omega_{0}^{n})}^{\lambda\frac{n+1}{n}}.

Here we note that by choosing
\kappa
sufficiently close to 1, one can make
\lambda\frac{n+1}{n}
as close to
\frac{n+1}{n}
as one wants. Indeed, by choosing
\kappa
sufficiently close to 1 and
q
sufficiently large, we can make
q^{\prime}
as close to 1 as one desires. With
q^{\prime}
sufficiently close to 1, one has that
\lambda
can be made as close to 1 as one desires.
∎

We need to use the following Lemma of De Giorgi which was first used in the setting
of complex Monge-Ampère equations in Kołodziej
[
11
]
:

Lemma 4.12
.

Let
u:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
be a decreasing function, such that there exists
\mu>0,\,B_{0}>0,\,s_{0}\geq 0
, such that for any
t>0,\,s\geq s_{0}
, one has:

tu(s+t)\leq B_{0}u(s)^{1+\mu}.

Then
u(s)\equiv 0
for
s\geq s_{0}+\frac{2B_{0}u(s_{0})^{\mu}}{1-2^{-\mu}}.

Proof.

We can choose a sequence
\{s_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
by induction:

s_{k+1}-s_{k}=2B_{0}u(s_{k})^{\mu}.

Then we choose
s=s_{k},\,t=s_{k+1}-s_{k}
we see that:

u(s_{k+1})\leq\frac{B_{0}u(s_{k})^{1+\mu}}{s_{k+1}-s_{k}}\leq\frac{1}{2}u(s_{k}).

That is,
u(s_{k})\leq 2^{-k}u(s_{0})
. Hence:

s_{k+1}-s_{k}\leq 2B_{0}u(s_{0})^{\mu}2^{-k\mu}.

Therefore,

\sup_{k\geq 0}s_{k}=s_{0}+\sum_{k\geq 0}(s_{k+1}-s_{k})\leq s_{0}+\frac{2B_{0}u(s_{0})^{\mu}}{1-2^{-\mu}}.

Since
u(s)
is decreasing, we see that
u(s)\equiv 0
for
s\geq\sup_{k\geq 0}s_{k}
.
∎

As a consequence, we see that:

Corollary 4.13
.

Let
\frac{2\delta_{1}}{\delta_{0}}\leq\delta<1
,
s_{0}>0
be chosen so that
A_{\delta,s,\kappa}\leq\delta
. Then for any
0<\nu<\frac{1}{n}(1-\frac{1}{p_{0}})
, we may choose
\kappa>1
sufficiently close to 1, such that there exists
C>0
depending only on
\nu
,
||e^{nG_{0}}||_{L^{p_{0}}}
, the background metric and the choice of
\kappa
such that:

\sup_{M}\big((1-\delta)v-\phi)\leq s_{0}+C\delta vol(\Omega_{\delta,s_{0}})^{\nu}.

Proof.

We wish to use Lemma
4.12
with
u(s)=\int_{\Omega_{\delta,s}}e^{nG_{0}}\omega_{0}^{n}
with
B_{0}=C_{*}\delta
and
\mu=\delta_{*}
. Then Lemma
4.12
gives that
u(s)\equiv 0
for
s\geq s_{0}+\frac{2C_{*}\delta u(s_{0})^{\delta_{*}}}{1-2^{-\delta_{*}}}
.
This is equivalent to:

\sup_{M}\big((1-\delta)v-\phi\big)\leq s_{0}+\frac{2C_{*}}{1-2^{-\delta_{*}}}\delta\bigg(\int_{\Omega_{\delta,s_{0}}}e^{nG_{0}}\omega_{0}^{n}\bigg)^{\delta_{*}}.

On the other hand,

\int_{\Omega_{\delta,s_{0}}}e^{nG_{0}}\omega_{0}^{n}\leq\bigg(\int_{\Omega_{\delta,s_{0}}}e^{p_{0}nG_{0}}\omega_{0}^{n}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p_{0}}}vol(\Omega_{\delta,s_{0}})^{1-\frac{1}{p_{0}}}

Hence the result immediately follows.
∎

With some more effort, we can get rid of
\delta
in the above:

Lemma 4.14
.

Assume that
v
is bounded. Let
\frac{2\delta_{1}}{\delta_{0}}\leq\delta<1
and
s_{0}>0
are chosen so that:

(1)

s_{0}\geq 2\delta||v||_{L^{\infty}}
,

(2)

A_{\delta,s_{0},\kappa}\leq\delta
.

Then for any

, there exists
C>0
depending only on
\nu
,
||e^{nG_{0}}||_{L^{p_{0}}}
, and the background metric, such that:

\sup_{M}(v-\phi)\leq\frac{3s_{0}}{2}+Cs_{0}^{-\nu}||(v-\phi)_{+}||_{L^{1}}^{\nu}.

Proof.

From Corollary
4.13
, we see that

\begin{split}&\sup_{M}(v-\phi)\leq\sup_{M}((1-\delta)v-\phi)+\delta||v||_{L^{\infty}}\leq s_{0}+\delta||v||_{L^{\infty}}+C\delta vol(\Omega_{\delta,s_{0}})^{\nu}\\
&\leq\frac{3s_{0}}{2}+C\delta vol(\Omega_{\delta,s_{0}})^{\nu}.\end{split}

On the set

, we see that

. Therefore one has:

vol(\Omega_{\delta,s_{0}})\leq\frac{2}{s_{0}}||(v-\phi)_{+}||_{L^{1}}.

Therefore the result follows.
∎

For
0<\delta<1
, we define
s_{0}(\delta)
to be the smallest
s_{0}
such that
s_{0}\geq 2\delta||v||_{L^{\infty}}
and
A_{\delta,s_{0},\kappa}\leq\delta
. The following lemma gives the needed estimate for

:

Lemma 4.15
.

Assume that
\frac{2\delta_{1}}{\delta_{0}}\leq\delta<1
. There exists
C>0
depending only on
||e^{nG_{0}}||_{L^{p_{0}}}
, and the background metric, such that:

s_{0}(\delta)\leq\max\big(2\delta||v||_{L^{\infty}},\,\,C||(v-\phi)_{+}||_{L^{1}}\big).

Proof.

With
s_{0}=s_{0}(\delta)
, we have that either
s_{0}=2\delta||v||_{L^{\infty}}
or
A_{s_{0},\delta,\kappa}=\delta
. If the first possibility holds, then we are done. If we are in the second possibility, then
we can calculate, with
\kappa<\beta<p_{0}
:

\begin{split}&A_{\delta,s_{0},\kappa}=\bigg(\int_{\Omega_{\delta,s_{0}}}\big((1-\delta)v-\phi-s_{0}\big)_{+}^{\kappa}e^{n\kappa G_{0}}\omega_{0}^{n}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\kappa}}\leq||((1-\delta)v-\phi-s)_{+}||_{L^{\frac{\kappa\beta}{\beta-\kappa}}(\omega_{0}^{n})}||e^{nG_{0}}||_{L^{\beta}(\Omega_{\delta,s_{0}})}\\
&\leq C(\beta)A_{\delta,s_{0},\kappa}^{\frac{1}{n+1}}\delta^{\frac{n}{n+1}}||e^{nG_{0}}||_{L^{\beta}(\Omega_{\delta,s},\omega_{0}^{n})}\leq C(\beta)A_{\delta,s_{0},\kappa}^{\frac{1}{n+1}}\delta^{\frac{n}{n+1}}||e^{nG_{0}}||_{L^{p_{0}}}vol(\Omega_{\delta,s_{0}})^{\frac{1}{\beta}-\frac{1}{p_{0}}}.\end{split}

Using that

, and that

, we see that:

1\leq C(s_{0}^{-1})^{\frac{1}{\beta}-\frac{1}{p_{0}}}||(v-\phi)_{+}||_{L^{1}}^{\frac{1}{\beta}-\frac{1}{p_{0}}}.

So we are done.
∎

As a corollary, we can show that:

Corollary 4.16
.

Let
\tilde{\chi}
be as assumed in Proposition

for some

Let

. Let

\begin{split}&\lambda[\tilde{\chi}+dd^{c}\phi]\in\Gamma,\,\,f\big(\lambda[\tilde{\chi}+dd^{c}\phi]\big)\leq e^{G_{0}}+\delta_{1},\\

Let

, then for any

, there exists a constant

, such that

\sup_{M}(v-\phi)\leq C\big(\delta_{1}+||(v-\phi)_{+}||_{L^{1}}^{\frac{\nu}{\nu+1}}\big).

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that

in Lemma

The first case is when

\sup_{M}(v-\phi)\leq C^{\prime}||(v-\phi)_{+}||_{L^{1}}^{\frac{\nu}{\nu+1}}.

The other case is when

and found that:

s_{0}^{-\nu}||(v-\phi)_{+}||_{L^{1}}^{\nu}\leq C^{\prime}||(v-\phi)_{+}||_{L^{1}}^{\frac{\nu}{\nu+1}}.

On the other hand,
\frac{3s_{0}}{2}=\frac{6\delta_{1}}{\delta_{0}}||v||_{L^{\infty}}\leq C^{\prime\prime}\delta_{1}.
Hence the result follows.
∎

5.
Uniqueness of viscosity solution

The uniqueness of the viscosity solution essentially follows the uniqueness result in our previous paper
[
2
]
. The only problem is that in our previous paper
[
2
]
, we additionally assume that
f
is of homogeneous degree 1. This is used in the proof of the uniqueness result in
[
2
]
. However, this can be replaced by the Lemma
2.6
in the current setting. For the convenience of readers, we sketch the proof of the uniqueness. The basic strategy to prove the uniqueness of viscosity solutions will be to perform sup/inf convolution of the solution so that we get a sub/super solution that is twice differentiable a.e.
which implies the pointwise differential inequality wherever the solution is twice differentiable. The reason we no longer consider Richberg technique is that it does not necessarily regularize a viscosity supersolution to a smooth supersolution.

Let
\varphi\in C(M)
be a viscosity solution to
F(\chi+dd^{c}\varphi)=e^{G(x,\varphi)}.
We define the sup convolution of
\varphi
as follows:

(5.1)

\varphi^{\varepsilon}(z)=\sup_{\xi\in T_{z}M}(\varphi(\exp_{z}(\xi))+\varepsilon-\frac{1}{\varepsilon}|\xi|_{z}^{2}),

where
\exp_{z}(\xi)
is the exponential map at
z
, defined using the metric
\omega_{0}
, and
|\xi|_{z}
denotes the length of the tangent vector
\xi
, again using the metric
\omega_{0}
. Similarly we define the inf convolution of
\varphi
:

(5.2)

\varphi_{\varepsilon}(z)=\inf_{\xi\in T_{z}M}(\varphi(\exp_{z}(\xi))-\varepsilon+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}|\xi|_{z}^{2}).

The main reasons for considering (
5.1
) and (
5.2
) are that they are semi-convex/concave, and they are sub/super solutions respectively. More precisely, we observe that:

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\varphi\in C(M)
,and we define
\varphi^{\varepsilon},\,\varphi_{\varepsilon}
according to (
5.1
) and (
5.2
). Let
x_{0}\in M
and we choose local coordinates in a neighborhood of
x_{0}
. Then there exists a neighborhood
U
of
x_{0}
, and
C_{\varepsilon}>0
, such that:
z\mapsto\varphi^{\varepsilon}(z)+C_{\varepsilon}|z|^{2}
is convex on
U
under the coordinates, and
z\mapsto\varphi_{\varepsilon}(z)-C_{\varepsilon}|z|^{2}
is concave on
U
under the coordinates. In particular,
\varphi^{\varepsilon}
,
\varphi_{\varepsilon}
are punctually second order differentiable a.e.

The proof of this lemma is contained in
[
2
]
, Lemma 5.14.

Next we observe that
\varphi^{\varepsilon}
and
\varphi_{\varepsilon}
defined above produce viscosity subsolution and supersolution, up to a small error. We denote

\rho_{\varphi}(r)=\max\{|\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)|:\,\,d_{g}(x,y)\leq r,\,x,y\in M\}.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
\varphi
be a viscosity solution to
F(\chi+dd^{c}\varphi)=e^{G(x,\varphi)}
with
G(x,\varphi)
continuous. Then there exist continuous functions
\rho(\varepsilon):(0,1)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{>0}
with
\rho(0+)=0
, and
\rho_{1}(\varepsilon,a_{1}),\,\rho_{2}(\varepsilon,a_{2}):(0,1)^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{>0}
with
\rho_{i}(0+,0+)=0,\,\,i=1,2
, such that for any
0<\varepsilon<1
,
0<a_{i}<1,\,i=1,\,2
:

(1)

\frac{\varphi^{\varepsilon}}{1+a_{1}}
is
\Gamma
-subharmonic with respect to
\frac{\chi+\rho(\varepsilon)\omega_{0}}{1+a_{1}}
in the viscosity sense, and
F(\frac{\chi+\rho(\varepsilon)\omega_{0}}{1+a_{1}}+dd^{c}\frac{\varphi^{\varepsilon}}{1+a_{1}})\geq e^{G(x,\frac{\varphi^{\varepsilon}}{1+a_{1}})}-\rho_{1}(\varepsilon,a_{1})
in the viscosity sense.

(2)

\frac{\varphi_{\varepsilon}}{1-a_{2}}
satisfies
F(\frac{\chi-\rho(\varepsilon)\omega_{0}}{1-a_{2}}+dd^{c}\frac{\varphi_{\varepsilon}}{1-a_{2}})\leq e^{G(x,\frac{\varphi_{\varepsilon}}{1-a_{2}})}+\rho_{2}(\varepsilon,a_{2})
in the viscosity sense.

Here the functions
\rho,\,\rho_{i},\,i=1,\,2
are determined by
\rho_{\varphi}
,
||\varphi||_{L^{\infty}}
, the form
\chi
and the background metric.

Proof.

The proof is almost identical to the proof of Proposition 5.6 in Cheng-Xu
[
2
]
. Except that there are several places in the proof of Proposition 5.6 in
[
2
]
, we used that
f
is homogeneous of degree 1 which we no longer assume. As we will explain, the argument in these places can easily go through with the help of Lemma
2.6
.

The first place in
[
2
]
where we used that
f
is homogeneous degree 1 is in formula (5.14), where we are in the middle of the proof of the first part and concluded that:

(5.3)

f\big(\lambda[\chi+\rho(\varepsilon)\omega_{0}+(1+a_{1})dd^{c}P]\big)=(1+a_{1})f\big(\lambda[\frac{\chi+\rho(\varepsilon)\omega_{0}}{1+a_{1}}+dd^{c}P]\big).

Here
a_{1}>0
, and
\rho:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
is a quantity that
\rho(0+)=0
.
P
is a
C^{2}
function that touches
\frac{\varphi^{\varepsilon}}{1+a_{1}}
from above at
x_{0}
. If one no longer assumes
f
is homogeneous degree 1, then one can use Lemma
2.6
to see that one has “
\leq
” holds in (
5.3
), and the subsequent argument is not affected.

The second place in
[
2
]
where we used that
f
is homogeneous degree 1 is in the formula following (5.20), where we are in the middle of the proof of the second part and the situation is completely the same, where we need that for some
0<a_{2}<1
, one has:

(1-a_{2})f(\lambda)\leq f((1-a_{2})\lambda),\,\,\,\lambda\in\Gamma.

∎

5.1.
when the right hand side has strict monotonicity

In this subsection, we assume that
G(x,\varphi)
is continuous and strictly monotone increasing in
\varphi
. We wish to show that:

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
G(x,\varphi)
be continuous, and strictly monotone increasing in
\varphi
. Then there exists at most one viscosity solution to
F(\chi+dd^{c}\varphi)=e^{G(x,\varphi)}
.

If not, then there exist two viscosity solutions
\varphi_{1},\,\varphi_{2}
, and
\varphi_{1}\neq\varphi_{2}
. Without loss of generality, we may assume that:

(5.4)

\kappa_{0}:=\max_{M}(\varphi_{1}-\varphi_{2})>0.

Now we consider the super-convolution, applied to
\varphi_{1}
, and the inf-convolution, applied to
\varphi_{2}
. We define:

\kappa_{\varepsilon,a_{1},a_{2}}=\max_{M}\big(\frac{(\varphi_{1})^{\varepsilon}}{1+a_{1}}-\frac{(\varphi_{2})_{\varepsilon}}{1-a_{2}}\big).

Then it is easy to see that, as
\varepsilon,\,a_{1},\,a_{2}\rightarrow 0+
,
\kappa_{\varepsilon,a_{1},a_{2}}\rightarrow\kappa_{0}
. Assume that the above max is achieved at
x_{*}
. Then we see that the sub/super solution condition for
\varphi^{\varepsilon}/\varphi_{\varepsilon}
holds in the classical sense at
x_{*}
, provided that both of them are punctually second order differentiable at
x_{*}
:

Proposition 5.3
.

Assume that
\varepsilon,\,a_{1},\,a_{2}
are chosen so that
\frac{c_{*}+\rho(\varepsilon)}{1+a_{1}}<\frac{c_{*}-\rho(\varepsilon)}{1-a_{2}}
. Assume also that both
(\varphi_{1})^{\varepsilon}
and
(\varphi_{2})_{\varepsilon}
are punctually second order differentiable at
x_{*}
. Then one has:

(1)

\lambda[\frac{\chi-\rho(\varepsilon)\omega_{0}}{1-a_{2}}+dd^{c}\frac{(\varphi_{2})_{\varepsilon}}{1-a_{2}}](x_{*})\in\Gamma
,

(2)

F(\frac{\chi+\rho(\varepsilon)\omega_{0}}{1+a_{1}}+dd^{c}\frac{(\varphi_{1})^{\varepsilon}}{1+a_{1}})(x_{*})\leq F(\frac{\chi-\rho(\varepsilon)\omega_{0}}{1-a_{2}}+dd^{c}\frac{(\varphi_{2})_{\varepsilon}}{1-a_{2}})(x_{*})
.

In the above,
c_{*}>0
is some constant such that
\lambda(\chi-c_{*}\omega_{0})\in\Gamma
.

This proposition is exactly the proposition 5.15 in
[
2
]
, and a proof is given there. With the help of Proposition
5.2
and
5.3
, we have the uniqueness proof when the right hand side has strictly increasing dependence on
\varphi
, at least when both
(\varphi_{1})^{\varepsilon}
and
(\varphi_{2})_{\varepsilon}
are both punctually second order differentiable at
x_{*}
:

Proof.

(of Theorem
5.1
, assuming
(\varphi_{1})^{\varepsilon}
and
(\varphi_{2})_{\varepsilon}
are punctually second order differentiable at
x_{*}
)
We choose
\varepsilon,\,a_{1},\,a_{2}
so that
\frac{c_{*}+\rho(\varepsilon)}{1+a_{1}}<\frac{c_{*}-\rho(\varepsilon)}{1-a_{2}}
.
Combining Proposition
5.2
and Proposition
5.3
, we see that at
x_{*}
:

(5.5)

\begin{split}&f\big(\lambda[\frac{\chi+\rho(\varepsilon)\omega_{0}}{1+a_{1}}+dd^{c}\frac{(\varphi_{1})^{\varepsilon}}{1+a_{1}}]\big)(x_{*})\leq f(\lambda[\frac{\chi-\rho(\varepsilon)\omega_{0}}{1-a_{2}}+dd^{c}\frac{(\varphi_{2})_{\varepsilon}}{1-a_{2}}])(x_{*}),\\
&f\big(\lambda[\frac{\chi+\rho(\varepsilon)\omega_{0}}{1+a_{1}}+dd^{c}\frac{(\varphi_{1})^{\varepsilon}}{1+a_{1}}]\big)(x_{*})\geq e^{G(x_{*},\frac{(\varphi_{1})^{\varepsilon}(x_{*})}{1+a_{1}})}-\rho_{1}(\varepsilon,a_{1}),\\
&f\big(\lambda[\frac{\chi-\rho(\varepsilon)\omega_{0}}{1-a_{2}}+dd^{c}\frac{(\varphi_{2})_{\varepsilon}}{1-a_{2}}])\big)(x_{*})\leq e^{G(x_{*},\frac{(\varphi_{2})_{\varepsilon}(x_{*})}{1-a_{2}})}+\rho_{2}(\varepsilon,a_{2}).\end{split}

Combining the three inequalities, we see that:

(5.6)

e^{G(x_{*},\frac{(\varphi_{1})^{\varepsilon}(x_{*})}{1+a_{1}})}-\rho_{1}(\varepsilon,a_{1})\leq e^{G(x_{*},\frac{(\varphi_{2})_{\varepsilon}(x_{*})}{1-a_{2}})}+\rho_{2}(\varepsilon,a_{2}).

On the other hand,
\frac{(\varphi_{1})^{\varepsilon}(x_{*})}{1+a_{1}}-\frac{(\varphi_{2})_{\varepsilon}(x_{*})}{1-a_{2}}=\kappa_{\varepsilon,a_{1},a_{2}}\rightarrow\kappa_{0}>0
as
\varepsilon,\,a_{1},\,a_{2}\rightarrow 0
. This is clearly inconsistent with (

) when

are all small enough.
∎

In general, when we don’t necessarily know that
(\varphi_{1})^{\varepsilon}
and
(\varphi_{2})_{\varepsilon}
are punctually second order differentiable at
x_{*}
, we need a perturbation argument which was first due to Crandall, Ishii and Lions, and we have:

Lemma 5.4
.

We choose normal coordinate near
x_{*}
such that
x_{*}
is given by

. Then there exists a neighborhood

5.2.
when the right-hand side does not depend on
\varphi

In this subsection, we will assume that the right hand side

depends only on
x
. In this case, one can only hope to solve
F(\chi+dd^{c}\varphi)=e^{G+c}
, for some constant

Even though we can show that there is a unique
c\in\mathbb{R}
that makes this equation solvable in the viscosity sense, we still don’t have a good enough understanding of this constant, which is the main hurdle to a proof of uniqueness of viscosity solutions in this case.
Let us start with observing the monotonicity of the constant, in terms of the right hand side. More specifically, we have:

Proposition 5.6
.

Let
G_{1},\,G_{2}\in C(M)
with
G_{1}\geq G_{2}
. Assume that there exist
c_{i}\in\mathbb{R},\,\varphi_{i}\in C(M),\,i=1,\,2
, that solve
F(\chi+dd^{c}\varphi_{i})=e^{G_{i}+c_{i}}
in the viscosity sense. Then one has
c_{1}\leq c_{2}
.

Heuristically if this is false, namely
c_{1}>c_{2}
. This would imply that
e^{G_{1}+c_{1}}>e^{G_{2}+c_{2}}+\delta_{0}
on
M
, for some
\delta_{0}>0
. This would lead to a contradiction if one evaluates at
x_{0}
, where
\varphi_{1}-\varphi_{2}
achieves maximum. At this point, one would have
\chi+dd^{c}\varphi_{1}\leq\chi+dd^{c}\varphi_{2}
, so that
F(\chi+dd^{c}\varphi_{1})\leq F(\chi+dd^{c}\varphi_{2})
at
x_{0}
. In order to prove this rigorously, we can regularize
\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}
similar to the proof of the Theorem
5.1
.
The detailed argument is identical to Proposition 5.6 of
[
2
]
.

A direct consequence of the above proposition is that there is a unique constant
c
that allows for a viscosity solutions:

Corollary 5.7
.

Let
G\in C(M)
, there is at most one constant
c\in\mathbb{R}
, such that
F(\chi+dd^{c}\varphi)=e^{G+c}
is solvable in the viscosity sense.

Because of this result, we may simply denote this constant to be
c(G)
. Proposition
5.6
then implies that
c(G_{1})\leq c(G_{2})
whenever
G_{1}\geq G_{2}
.

The question that is of crucial importance to us is the following:

Question 5.8

Assume that
G_{1}\geq G_{2}

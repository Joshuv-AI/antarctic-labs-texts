The Bowel in a Cocoon: Sclerosing Encapsulating Peritonitis
Authors: Budoor Alqinai, Guilherme Costa, Matthew Krafft
Published: 2026

A 76-year-old man with end-stage renal disease and prior peritoneal dialysis complicated by recurrent peritonitis presented with progressive nausea, vomiting, and weight loss. Computed tomography demonstrated centrally clustered small bowel loops encased within a thick fibrotic membrane (Figure A). Exploratory laparotomy revealed dense fibrotic encapsulation of the small intestine, consistent with a “frozen abdomen,” requiring extensive adhesiolysis and membrane excision (Figure B). Histopathology confirmed dense fibromembranous tissue (Figure C), establishing the diagnosis of sclerosing encapsulating peritonitis (abdominal cocoon syndrome).

At approximately 2 months after surgery, the patient reported marked clinical improvement, with complete resolution of nausea and vomiting. He was tolerating a regular diet and having daily, formed bowel movements without diarrhea, with gradual return to baseline activity.

Prior peritoneal dialysis in patients presenting with bowel obstruction and encapsulated small bowel loops should raise suspicion for sclerosing encapsulating peritonitis.

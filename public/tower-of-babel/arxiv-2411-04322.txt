Abstract
This work introduces a new, explicit bound on the Hellinger distance between a continuous random variable and a Gaussian with matching mean and variance. As example applications, we derive a quantitative Hellinger central limit theorem and efficient concentration inequalities for U-statistics.

Introduction

We introduce a new, explicit bound (
Thm.

1
) on the Hellinger distance
(
Hellinger, 1909
)

between a continuous random variable and a Gaussian with matching mean and variance.

Thm.

1
, based on Stein’s method of exchangeable pairs
(
Stein, 1986
)
, enables quantitative Hellinger central limit theorems (CLTs,
Sec.

2.1
) and efficient concentration inequalities (
Thm.

8
) for U-statistics.
Notably,
Sec.

2.1
also avoids the unknown constants, spectral gap conditions
(
Artstein et al., 2004
)
,
Stein kernel assumptions
(
Ledoux et al., 2015
)
, and exponential dependence on Kullback-Leibler divergence
(
Bobkov et al., 2014
;
Bobkov et al., 2013
)
present in existing entropic CLTs for sums of independent random variables.

2
Bounding Hellinger Distance to a Gaussian

We begin by presenting a general upper bound on the Hellinger distance
between a continuous random variable and a Gaussian with matching mean and variance.
Throughout, we let
Z
denote a standard normal random variable with Lebesgue density
\varphi

and
C^{\infty}_{c}
denote the set of infinitely differentiable compact functions from
\mathbb{R}
to
\mathbb{R}
.
Further, for a random variable
S
and
p\geq 1
,
we write
C^{p}
for the set of
p
-times continuously differentiable functions from
\mathbb{R}
to
\mathbb{R}
, define
\|{S}\|_{p}^{p}\triangleq\mathbb{E}[|S|^{p}]
,
and
let
L^{p}(S)\triangleq\{\textup{measurable\ }f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}\mid\mathbb{E}[|f(S)|^{p}]<\infty\}
.
Finally, we say that
S
admits a
(first-order) score function

\mathfrak{s}_{S}
or a
second-order score function

I_{S}
if
\mathfrak{s}_{S},I_{S}\in L^{1}(S)
satisfy

\textstyle\mathbb{E}[\mathfrak{s}_{S}(S)\Psi(S)]=-\mathbb{E}[\Psi^{\prime}(S)]\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbb{E}[I_{S}(S)\Psi(S)]=\mathbb{E}[\Psi^{\prime\prime}(S)]\quad\text{for all}\quad\Psi\in C^{\infty}_{c}.

(2)

Notably, by
Lemma

15
,
\mathfrak{s}_{S}=f^{\prime}/f
and
I_{S}=f^{\prime\prime}/f
whenever those quantities exist in
L^{1}(S)
.

Theorem 1

(Bounding Hellinger distance to a Gaussian)
.

Let
\mathbf{X}
be random variable taking values in a Polish-space
\mathcal{X}
and
g:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a measurable function for which
S\triangleq g(\mathbf{X})
has convex support
\Omega_{S}\subseteq\mathbb{R}
,
\mathbb{E}[S]=0
,

\mathrm{Var}(S)=1
, Lebesgue density
f_{S}\in C^{1}
, and first and second-order score functions
\mathfrak{s}_{S},I_{S}\in L^{2}(S)
satisfying
\|{\mathfrak{s}_{S}(S)S}\|_{2}<\infty
.
Fix any
D\in(0,1)
and any anti-symmetric function
F(\cdot,\cdot):\mathcal{X}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and, for each
t\geq-\frac{1}{2}\log(1-D)
,
define
S^{t}\triangleq g(\mathbf{X}^{t})
where
\mathbf{X}^{t}
is an exchangeable copy of
\mathbf{X}
and

\textstyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-kt}k}{\sqrt{k-1}!\sqrt{1-e^{-2t}}^{k}}\|\mathbb{E}[(S^{t}-S)^{k-1}F(\mathbf{X}^{t},\mathbf{X})\mid S]\|_{2}<\infty.

(3)

Then, for any
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
we may bound the Hellinger distance

\textstyle H(S,Z)

\textstyle\triangleq(\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\sqrt{f_{S}(x)}-\sqrt{\varphi(x)})^{2}dx)^{1/2}

(4)

\textstyle\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\|I_{Z_{t}}(Z_{t})-I_{Z}(Z_{t})+Z_{t}(\mathfrak{s}_{Z_{t}}(Z_{t})-\mathfrak{s}_{Z}(Z_{t}))\|_{2}dt

(5)

\textstyle\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\{-\frac{1}{2}\log(1-D)(1+\|\mathfrak{s}_{S}(S)S\|_{2})+\|\mathfrak{s}_{S}(S)\|_{2}(\sqrt{\frac{D}{1-D}}-\rm{tan}^{-1}(\sqrt{\frac{D}{1-D}}))

(6)

\textstyle+\frac{D}{1-D}\|I_{S}(S)\|_{2}+\int_{-\frac{1}{2}\log(1-D)}^{\infty}\|\frac{e^{-2t}}{{1-e^{-2t}}}h_{2}(Z)-\frac{e^{-t}}{\sqrt{1-e^{-2t}}}Sh_{1}(Z)-\tilde{\tau}_{t,s}\|_{2}dt\}

(7)

in terms of the Gaussian score functions
\mathfrak{s}_{Z}(z)\triangleq-z
and
I_{Z}(z)\triangleq z^{2}-1
, the interpolants

\textstyle Z_{t}\triangleq e^{-t}S+\sqrt{1-e^{-2t}}Z\ \ \text{with}\ \ Z\perp\!\!\!\perp S,\ \ \text{density}\ \ f_{Z_{t}},\ \ \text{and}\ \ (\mathfrak{s}_{Z_{t}},I_{Z_{t}})\triangleq(\frac{\partial_{x}{f_{Z_{t}}}}{f_{Z_{t}}},\frac{\partial_{x}^{2}f_{Z_{t}}}{f_{Z_{t}}})

(8)

for
t>0
,
and the Hermite sum

\textstyle\tilde{\tau}_{t,s}

\textstyle\triangleq\frac{se^{-t}}{\sqrt{1-e^{-2t}}}\mathbb{E}[F(\mathbf{X}^{t},\mathbf{X})\mid S]h_{1}(Z)+\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac{se^{-kt}}{k!\sqrt{1-e^{-2t}}^{k}}\mathbb{E}[(S^{t}-S)^{k-1}F(\mathbf{X}^{t},\mathbf{X})\mid S]h_{k}(Z)

(9)

where
h_{k}(x)\triangleq(-1)^{k}e^{x^{2}/2}\frac{\partial^{k}}{\partial x}e^{-x^{2}/2}
designates the
k
-th Hermite polynomial for
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

Remark 2

(Parameter choices)
.

In applications, we will choose
D=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}

and
s
,
\mathbf{X}^{t}
, and
F
to satisfy
s\mathbb{E}[F(\mathbf{X}^{t},\mathbf{X})\mid S]\approx-S

and

\frac{s}{2}\mathbb{E}[(S^{t}-S)F(\mathbf{X}^{t},\mathbf{X})\mid S]\approx 1
,
so that

\|\frac{e^{-2t}}{{1-e^{-2t}}}h_{2}(Z)-\frac{e^{-t}}{\sqrt{1-e^{-2t}}}Sh_{1}(Z)-\tilde{\tau}_{t,s}\|_{2}
is small and
the overall bound
7
is
O(\frac{\log(n)}{\sqrt{n}})
.

Remark 3

(Integrability)
.

The integrability condition
3
ensures that
\|{\tilde{\tau}_{t,s}}\|_{2}<\infty
and holds, for example, whenever
S^{t}-S
and
F(\mathbf{X}^{t},\mathbf{X})
are bounded, as in
Sec.

2.1
.

Remark 4

(Second-order score bound)
.

The second-order score can often be bounded in terms of first-order score functions, as, by
Lemma

16
, if
S\overset{d}{=}S_{1}+S_{2}
for independent
S_{1},S_{2}
with score functions
\mathfrak{s}_{S_{1}}\in L^{2}(S_{1})
and
\mathfrak{s}_{S_{2}}\in L^{2}(S_{2})
then

S
admits a second-order score function
I_{S}
satisfying

\textstyle\|I_{S}(S)\|_{2}\leq\|\mathfrak{s}_{S_{1}}(S_{1})\|_{2}\|\mathfrak{s}_{S_{2}}(S_{2})\|_{2}.

(10)

Our proof of
Thm.

1
in
Sec.

3
relies on
Stein’s method of exchangeable pairs
(
Stein, 1986
)

and
interpolation along the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck semigroup
(
Pavliotis, 2014
, Chap. 4)
. The first component takes inspiration from the arguments used by
Bonis (2020)
to bound Wasserstein distances.
To obtain an effective bound,
Thm.

1
requires four inputs: the exchangeable copy
\mathbf{X}^{\prime}
, the anti-symmetric function
F
, and the scalars
D
and
s
. We next demonstrate how to select these inputs when the target
S
is a U-statistic.

2.1
Application: A Quantitative Hellinger CLT for U-statistics

Given an i.i.d. sequence of random variables
\mathbf{X}\triangleq(X_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
taking values in
\mathcal{X}

and a symmetric, bounded, measurable function
u:\mathcal{X}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, consider the canonical U-statistic

\textstyle U_{n}=g(\mathbf{X})\triangleq\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}(n-1)}\sum_{1\leq i\neq j\leq n}u(X_{i},X_{j})-\mathbb{E}[u(X_{i},X_{j})].

(11)

Whenever
U_{n}
admits first and second-order score functions we can bound its Hellinger distance to a Gaussian using
Thm.

1

with
D=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}
,
s=n
, and, for each
t>0
,

\textstyle\!\!\!\!\!F(\mathbf{X}^{t},\mathbf{X})\triangleq\frac{1}{2}(g(\mathbf{X}^{t})-g(\mathbf{X}))+g_{1}(\mathbf{X}^{t})-g_{1}(\mathbf{X})\ \text{for}\ \mathbf{X}^{t}\triangleq(X_{1},\dots,X_{I-1},X^{\prime}_{I},X_{I+1},\dots,X_{n}),

(12)

\textstyle(X_{i}^{\prime})_{i=1}^{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}\mathbf{X},\ I\sim\textnormal{Unif}([n]),\ \text{and}\ g_{1}(\mathbf{X})\triangleq\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{i\leq n}\mathbb{E}[u(X_{i},X_{i}^{\prime})|X_{i}]-\mathbb{E}[u(X_{i},X_{i}^{\prime})],

(13)

and
(\mathbf{X},(X_{i}^{\prime})_{i=1}^{n},I)
mutually independent.
Here we adopt the shorthand
[n]\triangleq\{1,\dots,n\}
.

Theorem 5

(Quantitative Hellinger CLT for U-statistics)
.

Suppose that the U-statistic
U_{n}

11
admits first and second-order score functions
\mathfrak{s}_{U_{n}},I_{U_{n}}\in L^{2}(U_{n})
with
|u(X_{1},X_{2})|\stackrel{{\scriptstyle a.s.}}{{\leq}}R
and

4\mathrm{Cov}(u(X_{1},X_{2}),u(X_{1},X_{3}))=1
.
Then, for all
n\geq 2
,

\textstyle H(U_{n},Z)\leq\frac{3\alpha R^{3}}{\sqrt{2n}}(R+\frac{R\log({4\sqrt{n}})}{2\sqrt{n}}+\frac{4}{n^{1/4}})e^{\frac{2R^{2}}{\sqrt{n}}}+\frac{\log(n)}{\sqrt{2}\sqrt{n-1}}\|u(X_{1},X_{2})\|_{4}^{2}(16+2\sqrt{2}+\frac{8}{\sqrt{n-1}})

(14)

\textstyle-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}\log(1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}})(1+\|\mathfrak{s}_{U_{n}}(U_{n})U_{n}\|_{2})+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}(\sqrt{n}-1)}\|I_{U_{n}}(U_{n})\|_{2}+\frac{1}{3\sqrt{2}(\sqrt{n}-1)^{3/2}}\|\mathfrak{s}_{U_{n}}(U_{n})\|_{2}

(15)

for
\alpha\triangleq e^{19/300}\pi^{1/4}
.
Hence, if
\max(\|\mathfrak{s}_{U_{n}}(U_{n})\|_{2},\|I_{U_{n}}(U_{n})\|_{2},\|U_{n}\mathfrak{s}_{U_{n}}(U_{n})\|_{2})=O(\log(n))
,

\textstyle H(U_{n},Z)\lessapprox\frac{\log(n)}{\sqrt{n}}\|u(X_{1},X_{2})\|_{4}^{2}(8\sqrt{2}+2)

(16)

where the notation
a_{n}\lessapprox b_{n}
signifies that
a_{n}\leq b_{n}+o(b_{n}).

Thm.

5
, proved in
Sec.

4
, yields a U-statistic Hellinger CLT rate of order
\frac{\log(n)}{\sqrt{n}}
whenever the U-statistic score functions have suitably bounded moments.
This matches, up to a logarithmic factor, the entropic CLT rates obtained for sums of independent random variables
(
Artstein et al., 2004
;
Ledoux et al., 2015
;
Bobkov et al., 2014
;
Bobkov et al., 2013
)
. Moreover, U-statistic score functions can often be controlled by their input variable score functions as the next example, proved in
Sec.

5
, illustrates.

Corollary 6

(Quantitative Hellinger CLT for chaos statistics)
.

Suppose that the i.i.d. sequence
(X_{i})_{i\geq 1}
has
\mathfrak{s}_{X_{1}}\in L^{4}(X_{1})
and
X_{1}\in[a,b]
for
a>0
.
Then the U-statistic
U_{n}

11
with
u(x,y)=xy
has
\max(\|\mathfrak{s}_{U_{n}}(U_{n})\|_{2},\|I_{U_{n}}(U_{n})\|_{2},\|U_{n}\mathfrak{s}_{U_{n}}(U_{n})\|_{2})=O(1)

and

\textstyle H(\frac{1}{\sigma}U_{n},Z)\lessapprox\frac{\log(n)}{\sqrt{n}}\frac{\mathbb{E}[X_{1}^{4}]}{\sigma^{2}}(8\sqrt{2}+2)\quad\text{for}\quad\sigma^{2}\triangleq 4\mathbb{E}[X_{1}]^{2}\mathrm{Var}(X_{1}).

(17)

2.2
Application: Efficient Concentration for U-statistics

As a second application of
Thm.

1
, we develop efficient concentration inequalities for U-statistics.
Let
\Phi
represent the cumulative distribution function of a standard Gaussian and
\Phi^{c}=1-\Phi
.
For an asymptotically normal sequence
(S_{n})_{n\geq 1}
with
\mathbb{E}(S_{n})=0
and
\mathrm{Var}(S_{n})=1
,

Austern and Mackey (2022)
defined an efficient concentration inequality as an upper bound on
\mathbb{P}(S_{n}>u)
that converges to the asymptotically exact value
\Phi^{c}(u)

as
n\to\infty
.
The authors also derive such inequalities for scaled sums of independent variables.
Our Hellinger bounds enable efficient concentration for more general random variables via the following relation, proved in
Sec.

6
.

Lemma 7

(Hellinger concentration)
.

If
S
has a Lebesgue density, then, for all
u\in\mathbb{R}
,

\textstyle|\sqrt{\mathbb{P}(S>u)}-\sqrt{\Phi^{c}(u)}|\vee|\sqrt{\mathbb{P}(|S|>u)}-\sqrt{2\Phi^{c}(u)}|

\textstyle\leq\sqrt{2}H(S,Z).

(18)

For example,
Corollaries

6
and
7
together immediately imply the following efficient concentration inequality for the chaos U-statistics
U_{n}
of
Corollary

6
.

Theorem 8

(Efficient U-statistic concentration)
.

Under the assumptions of
Corollary

6
,

\textstyle|\sqrt{\mathbb{P}(U_{n}>\sigma u)}-\sqrt{\Phi^{c}(u)}|\lessapprox\frac{\log(n)}{\sqrt{n}}\frac{\sqrt{2}\mathbb{E}(X_{1}^{4})}{\sigma^{2}}(8\sqrt{2}+2)\quad\text{for all}\quad u\in\mathbb{R}.

(19)

2.3
Related Work

For sums of i.i.d. observations,
Barron (1986)
established the first entropic CLT using the de Bruijn identity.
Rates were then established in various Rényi divergences by
Artstein et al. (2004)
;
Ledoux et al. (2015)
;
Bobkov et al. (2014)
;
Bobkov et al. (2013)
;
Goldfeld and Kato (2020)
;
Yao and Liu (2025)
;
Bobkov and Götze (2025a)
. See
Bobkov and Götze (2025b)
for a recent survey of those results. To the best of our knowledge, no rates for the Hellinger CLT have previously been established for U-statistics or other dependent sums. Stein’s method has been used to bound the Kolmogorov and Wasserstein distances to Gaussianity with
O(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}})
rates for U-statistics
(
Ross, 2011
)
. However, neither distance controls the Hellinger distance without additional assumptions. When the characteristic function
\phi_{S}
of
S
satisfies
\int t|\phi_{S}(t)|dt<\infty
,
Pratelli and Rigo (2018)
;
Janson et al. (2024)
bound the total variation (TV) distance
d_{TV}
by the Kolmogorov distance. Together these results yield suboptimal
O(\frac{1}{n^{1/4}})
Hellinger rates as
H^{2}(S,Z)\leq d_{TV}(S,Z)
. We control the Hellinger distance to Gaussianity using score function conditions and establish the first
O(\frac{\log n}{\sqrt{n}})
rates for non-degenerate U-statistics.

3
Proof of
Thm.

1
:
Theorem 1

(Bounding Hellinger distance to a Gaussian)
.

We begin by establishing the inequality
5
.
Fix any
T<\infty
.
By definition we have

\textstyle H(S,Z_{T})^{2}

\textstyle=1-\int_{\Omega_{S}}\sqrt{f_{S}(x)}\sqrt{f_{Z_{T}}(x)}dx.

(20)

Fix any

x\in\Omega_{S}
.
Since
the function
t\rightarrow\sqrt{f_{Z_{t}}(x)}
is
continuous on
[0,\infty)
and continuously differentiable on
(0,\infty)

by
Lemma

17
,
the fundamental theorem of calculus
(
Knapp, 2007
, Thm. 1.32)

implies that
\int_{0}^{T}\partial_{t}\sqrt{f_{Z_{t}}(x)}dt=\sqrt{f_{Z_{T}}(x)}-\sqrt{f_{S}(x)}
.
Moreover, since
\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega_{S}}\sqrt{f_{S}(x)}|\partial_{t}\sqrt{f_{Z_{t}}(x)}|dx\,dt<\infty
by
Lemma

18
, Fubini’s theorem
(
Schilling, 2005
, Cor. 13.9)
implies that

\textstyle H(S,Z_{T})^{2}

\textstyle=-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega_{S}}\sqrt{f_{S}(x)}\partial_{t}\sqrt{f_{Z_{t}}(x)}dx\,dt.

(21)

We will re-express this in terms of information-theoretic quantities. Write
h_{t}\triangleq{f_{Z_{t}}/}{\varphi}
. By the Fokker-Planck equation
(
Pavliotis, 2014
, Thm. 4.1)
,
we have

\textstyle\partial_{t}\sqrt{f_{Z_{t}}(x)}

\textstyle=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{f_{Z_{t}}(x)}}\partial_{t}{f_{Z_{t}}(x)}=\frac{\varphi(x)}{2\sqrt{f_{Z_{t}}(x)}}\partial_{t}\frac{f_{Z_{t}}(x)}{\varphi(x)}=\frac{\varphi(x)}{2\sqrt{f_{Z_{t}}(x)}}\partial_{t}h_{t}(x)

(22)

\textstyle=\frac{\varphi(x)}{2\sqrt{f_{Z_{t}}(x)}}[\partial_{x}^{2}{h_{t}}(x)-x\partial_{x}{h_{t}}(x)]=\frac{f_{Z_{t}}(x)}{2\sqrt{f_{Z_{t}}(x)}}[\frac{\partial_{x}^{2}h_{t}(x)}{h_{t}(x)}-x\frac{\partial_{x}h_{t}(x)}{h_{t}(x)}].

(23)

Defining
\Delta_{t}\triangleq\|I_{Z_{t}}(Z_{t})-h_{2}(Z_{t})+Z_{t}(\mathfrak{s}_{Z_{t}}(Z_{t})-h_{1}(Z_{t}))\|_{2}
, we therefore obtain that

\textstyle H(S,Z_{T})^{2}

\textstyle=-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega_{S}}\sqrt{f_{S}(x)}\frac{f_{Z_{t}}(x)}{2\sqrt{f_{Z_{t}}(x)}}[\frac{\partial_{x}^{2}h_{t}(x)}{h_{t}(x)}-x\frac{\partial_{x}h_{t}(x)}{h_{t}(x)}]dt

(24)

\textstyle=-\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}[\frac{\sqrt{f_{S}(Z_{t})}}{2\sqrt{f_{Z_{t}}(Z_{t})}}[\frac{\partial_{x}^{2}h_{t}(Z_{t})}{h_{t}(Z_{t})}-Z_{t}\frac{\partial_{x}h_{t}(Z_{t})}{h_{t}(Z_{t})}]]dt

(25)

\textstyle\overset{(a)}{=}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}[[\frac{\sqrt{f_{S}(Z_{t})}}{\sqrt{f_{Z_{t}}(Z_{t})}}-1][\frac{\partial_{x}^{2}h_{t}(Z_{t})}{h_{t}(Z_{t})}-Z_{t}\frac{\partial_{x}h_{t}(Z_{t})}{h_{t}(Z_{t})}]]dt

(26)

\textstyle\overset{(b)}{\leq}\frac{1}{2}\sup_{t\leq T}\|1-\frac{\sqrt{f_{S}(Z_{t})}}{\sqrt{f_{Z_{t}}(Z_{t})}}\|_{2}\int_{0}^{T}\Delta_{t}dt=\sup_{t\leq T}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}H(S,Z_{t})\int_{0}^{T}\Delta_{t}dt,

(27)

where (b) uses Cauchy-Schwarz and the identities

\textstyle\frac{\partial_{x}h_{t}(x)}{h_{t}(x)}

\textstyle=\frac{\partial_{x}f_{Z_{t}}(x)}{f_{Z_{t}}(x)}+x=\mathfrak{s}_{Z_{t}}(x)+x=\mathfrak{s}_{Z_{t}}(x)-\mathfrak{s}_{Z}(x)\quad\text{and}\quad

(28)

\textstyle\frac{\partial_{x}^{2}h_{t}(x)}{h_{t}(x)}

\textstyle=\frac{{\partial_{x}^{2}f^{S}_{t}}(x)}{f_{Z_{t}}(x)}-x^{2}+1+2x\frac{\partial_{x}h_{t}(x)}{h_{t}(x)}

(29)

\textstyle=I_{Z_{t}}(x)-I_{Z}(x)+2x(\mathfrak{s}_{Z_{t}}(x)-\mathfrak{s}_{Z}(x))=I_{Z_{t}}(x)+x^{2}+1+2x\mathfrak{s}_{Z_{t}}(x),

(30)

and (a) follows from the identities

\textstyle\mathbb{E}[\frac{\partial_{x}^{2}h_{t}(Z_{t})}{h_{t}(Z_{t})}-Z_{t}\frac{\partial_{x}h_{t}(Z_{t})}{h_{t}(Z_{t})}]\overset{(a_{1})}{=}\mathbb{E}[I_{Z_{t}}(Z_{t})+1+Z_{t}\mathfrak{s}_{Z_{t}}(Z_{t})]\overset{(a_{2})}{=}0.

(31)

The equality
(a_{1})
is a consequence of the identities
28
and
30
, and
(a_{2})
follows as, by our score assumption
2
, we have
\mathbb{E}[I_{Z_{t}}(Z_{t})]=0
and
\mathbb{E}[Z_{t}\mathfrak{s}_{Z_{t}}(Z_{t})]+1=0
.

Finally, since, for each
x\in\Omega_{S}
,
f_{Z_{t}}(x)=\frac{\mathbb{E}[\varphi((x-Se^{-t})/\sqrt{1-e^{-2t})}]}{\sqrt{1-e^{-2t}}}\to\varphi(x)
as
t\to\infty
and
|f_{Z_{t}}(x)|\leq 1/\sqrt{1-e^{-2}}
for all
t\geq 1
, the dominated convergence theorem
(
Schilling, 2005
, Thm. 11.2)
and
27
yield

\textstyle H(S,Z)^{2}=H(S,Z_{T})^{2}+\int_{\Omega_{S}}\sqrt{f_{S}(x)}(\sqrt{f_{Z_{T}}(x)}-\sqrt{\varphi(x)})dx

(32)

\textstyle\leq\limsup_{T\to\infty}(H(S,Z_{T})^{2}+\int_{\Omega_{S}}\sqrt{f_{S}(x)}(\sqrt{f_{Z_{T}}(x)}-\sqrt{\varphi(x)})dx)

(33)

\textstyle\leq\sup_{T\geq 0}H(S,Z_{T})^{2}+\int_{\Omega_{S}}\sqrt{f_{S}(x)}(\limsup_{T\to\infty}\sqrt{f_{Z_{T}}(x)}-\sqrt{\varphi(x)})dx\leq\frac{1}{2}(\int_{0}^{\infty}\Delta_{t}dt)^{2}.

(34)

To obtain the final bound
7
,
we fix any
D\in(0,1)
and
s>0
, decompose the integral

\textstyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\Delta_{t}dt=\int_{0}^{-\frac{1}{2}\log(1-D)}\Delta_{t}dt+\int_{-\frac{1}{2}\log(1-D)}^{\infty}\Delta_{t}dt\triangleq(b_{1})+(b_{2}),

(35)

and bound each component separately.

By
Lemma

15
,
\mathfrak{s}_{Z_{t}}(Z_{t})
is the almost-surely unique
L^{1}

random variable satisfying

\textstyle\mathbb{E}[\mathfrak{s}_{Z_{t}}(Z_{t})\Psi(Z_{t})]=-\mathbb{E}[\Psi^{\prime}(Z_{t})]\quad\text{for all}\quad\Psi\in C^{\infty}_{c}.

(36)

Moreover, by the tower property and the independence of
Z
and
S
, we have

\textstyle\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{E}[\mathfrak{s}_{S}(S)\mid Z_{t}]\Psi(Z_{t})]=\mathbb{E}[\mathfrak{s}_{S}(S)\Psi(e^{-t}S+\sqrt{1-e^{-2t}}Z)]=-e^{-t}\mathbb{E}[\Psi^{\prime}(Z_{t})],\ \ \forall\Psi\in C^{\infty}_{c}.

(37)

Therefore we must have

\textstyle\mathfrak{s}_{Z_{t}}(Z_{t})=e^{t}\mathbb{E}[\mathfrak{s}_{S}(S)\mid Z_{t}]\quad\text{almost surely.}\quad

(38)

Similarly, by
Lemma

15
,

I_{Z_{t}}(Z_{t})
is the almost-surely unique

L^{1}
random variable
satisfying

\textstyle=|\mathbb{E}[(h(Z)-1)\mathbb{I}(Z>u)]|=|\mathbb{E}[(\sqrt{h(Z)}-1)(\sqrt{h(Z)}+1)\mathbb{I}(Z>u)]|

(156)

\textstyle\leq|\mathbb{E}[(\sqrt{h(Z)}-1)\mathbb{I}(Z>u)]|+|\mathbb{E}[(\sqrt{h(Z)}-1)\sqrt{h(Z)}\mathbb{I}(Z>u)]|

(157)

\textstyle\leq\sqrt{\mathbb{E}[(\sqrt{h(Z)}-1)^{2}]}\big[\sqrt{\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{I}(Z>u)]}+\sqrt{\mathbb{E}[h(Z)\mathbb{I}(Z>u)]}\big]

(158)

\textstyle=\sqrt{\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}(\sqrt{f_{S}(x)}-\sqrt{\varphi(x)})^{2}dx}\big[\sqrt{\Phi^{c}(u)}+\sqrt{\mathbb{P}(S>u)}\big]

(159)

\textstyle=\sqrt{2}H(S,Z)\big[\sqrt{\Phi^{c}(u)}+\sqrt{P(S>u)}\big].

(160)

The second claim follows analogously as

\textstyle|P(|S|>u)-2\Phi^{c}(u)|

\textstyle=|\mathbb{E}[(h(Z)-1)\mathbb{I}(|Z|>u)]|\leq\sqrt{2}H(S,Z)\big[\sqrt{2\Phi^{c}(u)}+\sqrt{P(|S|>u)}\big].

(161)

Lemma 15

(Score function properties)
.

Let
S
be a random variable with convex support
\Omega_{S}\subseteq\mathbb{R}
and Lebesgue density
f_{S}\in C^{1}
.
The following statements hold true.

1.

Any first-order score function or second-order score function
I_{S}
for
S
is
S
-almost everywhere unique amongst elements of
L^{1}(S)
satisfying
2
.

2.

If
f_{S}
is twice differentiable with
\mathfrak{s}_{S}\triangleq{f_{S}^{\prime}/}{f_{S}}\in L^{1}(S),I_{S}\triangleq{f_{S}^{\prime\prime}/}{f_{S}}\in L^{1}(S)
, and

\textstyle\lim_{x\to\sup\Omega_{S}}\max(|f_{S}(x)|,|f^{\prime}_{S}(x)|)=\lim_{x\to\inf\Omega_{S}}\max(|f_{S}(x)|,|f^{\prime}_{S}(x)|)=0,

(163)

then
\mathfrak{s}_{S}
and
I_{S}
are first and second-order score functions for
S
.

Proof.

Suppose
g,\tilde{g}\in L^{1}(S)
are both score functions for
S
,
fix
\epsilon>0
, and choose
\delta>0
so that
\mathbb{E}[|g(S)-\tilde{g}(S)|\mathbb{I}(|S|>\delta)]<\epsilon
.
Since any measurable compactly supported indicator function can be arbitrarily approximated by
C^{\infty}_{c}
functions, select
\Psi_{\epsilon,\delta}\in C^{\infty}_{c}
satisfying

\|\Psi_{\epsilon,\delta}(S)-\mathbb{I}(g(S)>\tilde{g}(S),|S|\leq\delta)\|_{\infty}\leq\epsilon

so that

\textstyle\mathbb{E}[\max(g(S)-\tilde{g}(S),0)]

\textstyle=\mathbb{E}[[g(S)-\tilde{g}(S)]~\mathbb{I}(g(S)>\tilde{g}(S))]

(164)

\textstyle\leq\epsilon+\mathbb{E}[[g(S)-\tilde{g}(S)]\Psi_{\epsilon,\delta}(S)]+\mathbb{E}[|g(S)-\tilde{g}(S)|\mathbb{I}(|S|>\delta)]\leq 2\epsilon.

(165)

Since
\epsilon>0
was arbitrary we have
g(S)\leq\tilde{g}(S)
almost surely and, by symmetry,
g(S)\geq\tilde{g}(S)
almost surely. Identical reasoning yields the claim for second-order scores.

Now suppose that
f_{S}
is twice differentiable with
\mathfrak{s}_{S},I_{S}\in L^{1}(S)
and
163
holding. For all
\Psi\in C^{\infty}_{c}
, integration by parts, the boundedness of
\Psi
, and the assumption
163
imply

\textstyle\mathbb{E}[\frac{f^{\prime}_{S}(S)}{f_{S}(S)}\Psi(S)]

\textstyle=\int_{\Omega_{S}}f^{\prime}_{S}(x)\Psi(x)dx=f_{S}(x)\Psi(x)|_{\inf\Omega_{S}}^{\sup\Omega_{S}}-\int_{\Omega_{S}}f_{S}(x)\Psi^{\prime}(x)dx=-\mathbb{E}[\Psi^{\prime}(S)]

(166)

\textstyle\mathbb{E}[\frac{f^{\prime\prime}_{S}(S)}{f_{S}(S)}\Psi(S)]

\textstyle=\int_{\Omega_{S}}f^{\prime\prime}_{S}(x)\Psi(x)dx=f^{\prime}_{S}(x)\Psi(x)|_{\inf\Omega_{S}}^{\sup\Omega_{S}}-\int_{\Omega_{S}}f^{\prime}_{S}(x)\Psi^{\prime}(x)dx

(167)

\textstyle=-\mathbb{E}[\frac{f^{\prime}_{S}(S)}{f_{S}(S)}\Psi^{\prime}(S)]=\mathbb{E}[\Psi^{\prime\prime}(S)].

(168)

Hence
f^{\prime}_{S}/f_{S}
and
f^{\prime\prime}_{S}/f_{S}
are first and second-order score functions.
∎

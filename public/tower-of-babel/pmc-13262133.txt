A Rare Cause of Gastrointestinal Bleeding: Endoscopic Resection of a Large Brunner Gland Hamartoma
Authors: Alexander Siegel, Shalini Jain
Published: 2026

A 44-year-old man with no past medical history presented with 1 week of melena and an acute hemoglobin drop from 12.8 g/dL to 8.6 g/dL. Upper endoscopy revealed a large pedunculated polyp in the second portion of the duodenum. An endoloop was deployed around the stalk (Figure A), followed by snare-assisted mucosal resection (Figure B). Adequate hemostasis was achieved via placement of 3 hemostatic clips. The postresection mass demonstrated an exposed stalk and intact surrounding mucosa (Figure C and D). The patient’s hemoglobin stabilized after the procedure, and he was subsequently discharged. Histologic analysis revealed prominent Brunner’s glands with foveolar metaplasia, consistent with a Brunner gland hamartoma. While Helicobacter pylori infection and chronic mucosal irritation have been associated with these lesions, H pylori testing was negative, and no other mucosal injury was appreciated on endoscopy. Although Brunner gland hamartomas are typically benign and asymptomatic, large or ulcerated lesions can result in gastrointestinal bleeding or even bowel obstruction. This case highlights the importance of considering Brunner gland hamartoma in the differential diagnosis of unexplained upper gastrointestinal bleeding, particularly in patients without traditional risk factors.

Diffusion in Quenched Random Environments: Reviving Laplace’s First Law of Errors
Lucianno Defaveri, Eli Barkai

Abstract
Laplace’s first law of errors, which states that the frequency of an error can be represented as an exponential function of the error magnitude, was overlooked for many decades but was recently shown to describe the statistical behavior of diffusive tracers in disordered, glassy-like media. While much is known about this behavior, a key ingredient is still missing: the relationship between this observation and diffusion in a quenched random environment. We address this problem using the trap model, deriving lower and upper bounds on the particle packet for large displacements. Our results demonstrate that both bounds exhibit Laplace-like laws. We further establish a connection between the density of energy traps ρ ⁡ ( E ) \rho(E) , and the observed behavior, showing that the phenomenon is truly universal, albeit with constants that depend on temperature and the level of disorder.

This model describes a particle moving on a one-dimensional lattice,

These paths can be used to improve our lower bound and show that the exponential decay is not merely a feature of direct paths. Here, the correlations between the trajectory and the disordered environment are crucial, unlike the direct path scenario. Specifically, when resampling traps, the rate of escape from the trap at the first visit is the same as that in the second.
Note that we have two sites visited twice, except when the reversal takes place at the start or end of the path (see details in the SM). The total number of single-turn paths is

Our theoretical research, based on a master equation describing hopping in a disordered system, supports the appearance of exponential-like tails of
\langle P(x,t)\rangle

\langle P(x,t)\rangle
behaves in accordance with the Laplace law of errors, that is, it decays exponentially (with logarithm corrections). This striking result is universal, i.e., independent of the specific nature of the disorder. Importantly, the backtracking corrections presented in Eq. (

\mbox{Prob}(\rightarrow)\sim\exp\left[-|x|\ln\left({2|x|\over ert}\right)\right]{e^{-rt}\over\sqrt{2\pi|x|}}

(SM8)

or

\lim_{|x|\to\infty}{-\ln\mbox{Prob}(\rightarrow)\over|x|\ln\left(\frac{2|x|}{ert}\right)}=1.

(SM9)

This matches the result for
P(x,t)
Eqs. (
3
,
SM5
),
leading to
the asymptotic relation

P(x,t)=\mbox{Prob}(\rightarrow)
for large

x
, as mentioned in the main text.

I.2
II. Disordered system: direct path approach

In this section, we evaluate the Laplace transform
of the probability of finding a directed path at position
x
at time
t
.
More specifically, considering a disordered system,
Eq. (
6
) reads

\mbox{Prob}(\rightarrow)=\left({1\over 2}\right)^{x}\left\langle\mathbb{I}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{x-1}\tau_{i}<t<\sum_{i=0}^{x}\tau_{i+1}\right)\right\rangle_{{\rm WT}}.

(SM10)

Our goal is to further clarify this formula,
and subsequently find the disorder-averaged
\langle\mbox{Prob}(\rightarrow)\rangle

Eq.
(
9
). We recall that the subscript WT implies an average over the waiting times (WT) in each trap, defined in the main text and also below more precisely.
Further, we use the pulse function
\mathbb{I}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{x-1}\tau_{i}<t<\sum_{i=0}^{x}\tau_{i}\right)
, which is one if the restriction is obeyed and zero otherwise, as mentioned in the main text.

First, we consider the underlying process in a fixed random environment. Consider the direct path starting at
0
. Let the trapping times in the states along this path be
\tau_{0},\tau_{1},\cdots
. These random trapping times are constrained by the measurement time:

t=\tau_{0}+\tau_{1}+\cdots+\tau_{x}^{*}.

(SM11)

Since the particle is at the
x
trap at time
t
, the dwelling time
\tau_{x}^{*}
at
x
does not end with a jump; instead, it ends when the external clock reaches
t
. In related problems, this time is called the backward recurrence time.

It is useful to define the jumping moments when the particle hops between one trap to its nearest neighbors
[
43
]
. The process starts at
t=0
, and jumps take place at times
t_{1}=\tau_{0}
,
t_{2}=\tau_{0}+\tau_{1}
, etc. In particular
t_{x-1}=\sum_{a=0}^{x-1}\tau_{a}<t
and hence
\tau_{x}^{*}=t-t_{x}
. We will also use the transition event taking place at time
t_{x}=\sum_{a=0}^{x}\tau_{a}
which is clearly larger than
t
.
Using Eq. (
1
)
\tau_{a}
, are independent non-identically distributed exponential random variables, whose PDFs are

\psi_{a}(\tau_{a})=R_{a}\exp(-R_{a}\tau_{a})

(SM12)

and we will later use the Laplace transform

\hat{\psi}_{s}(s)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi_{a}(\tau_{a})\exp(-s\tau_{a})\,\mathrm{d}\tau_{a}=\frac{R_{a}}{R_{a}+s}.

(SM13)

Rewriting Eq. (
SM10
) in terms of jump moments

\mbox{Prob}(\rightarrow)=\left({1\over 2}\right)^{x}\langle\mathbb{I}(t_{x-1}<t<t_{x})\rangle_{{\rm WT}}.

(SM14)

Note
t_{0}=0
. The brackets denote an average over the waiting times
\tau_{0},\tau_{1},\cdots
, namely,

\mbox{Prob}(\rightarrow)=\left({1\over 2}\right)^{x}\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}R_{0}\exp(-R_{0}\tau_{0})\,\mathrm{d}\tau_{0}\cdots\int_{0}^{\infty}R_{x}\exp(-R_{x}\tau_{x})\,\mathrm{d}\tau_{x}\right]\mathbb{I}(t_{x-1}<t<t_{x}).

(SM15)

We now study the Laplace transform
t\to s
of this expression, denoted
\widehat{\mbox{Prob}}(\rightarrow)
. Using

\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp(-st)\mathbb{I}(t_{x-1}<t<t_{x})\,\mathrm{d}t=\frac{\exp(-st_{x-1})-\exp(-st_{x})}{s}

(SM16)

and the definitions of
t_{x}
and
t_{x-1}
, we find

\widehat{\mbox{Prob}}\left(\rightarrow\right)=\left({1\over 2}\right)^{x}\left\langle\frac{\exp\left[-s(\tau_{0}+\tau_{1}+\cdots+\tau_{x-1})\right]-\exp\left[-s(\tau_{0}+\tau_{1}+\cdots+\tau_{x})\right]}{s}\right\rangle_{{\rm WT}}

(SM17)

The waiting times are independent random variables, and the averaging gives

\widehat{\mbox{Prob}}\left(\rightarrow\right)=\left({1\over 2}\right)^{x}\frac{\left(\prod_{a=0}^{x-1}\frac{R_{a}}{R_{a}+s}-\prod_{a=0}^{x}\frac{R_{a}}{R_{a}+s}\right)}{s},

(SM18)

where we used Eq. (
SM13
).
As mentioned in the text this expression is sensitive to the set of rates
in the system.

We now average over disorder. We define
\langle\hat{\psi}(s)\rangle=\langle R_{a}/(R_{a}+s)\rangle
and
exploit the fact that
the rates

\{R_{a}\}
are independent identically distributed random variables with a common PDF
f(R)

\langle\hat{\psi}(s)\rangle=\int_{0}^{\infty}f(R){R\over R+s}{\rm d}R.

(SM19)

For the trap model, where rates are given by Arrhenius law, the maximum of the rate is the microscopical rate
r
,
so
f(R)=0
for
R>r
and the upper limit of the integration
is
r
.
Using Eq. (
SM18
) we get Eq. (
9
) in the main text.
Clearly
\langle\hat{\psi}(s)\rangle
is the Laplace transform of the disordered average distribution
of waiting times

\langle\psi(\tau)\rangle
, Eq. (
8
) in the letter.

I.3
III. Direct path probability through saddle point approximation

Our goal in this section is to use the saddle point approximation to obtain a theoretical expression for the probability of the direct path as an asymptotic series of
1/x
. The Laplace transform of the probability of the direct path, given in Eq. (
SM18
), can be written as

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathrm{Prob}}(\to)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{x}}\left(\frac{1-\hat{\psi}_{x}(s)}{s}\right)\prod_{a=0}^{x-1}\hat{\psi}_{a}(s)\,,

(SM20)

where we make use of the Laplace transform of the waiting time distribution defined in Eq. (
SM13
). The inverse Laplace transform of Eq. (
SM20
) yields the probability of the direct path at time
t
. Clearly, Eq. (
SM20
), depends on the
x+1
rates, which, as mentioned, are themselves random variables. Therefore, since the direct path does not revisit any site, we can write the disorder average of the product as

\displaystyle\langle\widehat{\mathrm{Prob}}(\to)\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{x}}\left\langle\left(\frac{1-\hat{\psi}_{x}(s)}{s}\right)\prod_{a=0}^{x-1}\hat{\psi}_{a}(s)\right\rangle=\frac{1}{2^{x}}\left(\frac{1-\langle\hat{\psi}(s)\rangle}{s}\right)\langle\hat{\psi}(s)\rangle^{x}\,.

(SM21)

To calculate the inverse of the Laplace transform, we will make use of the Mellin integral, that is,

\displaystyle\langle\mathrm{Prob}(\to)\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{0^{+}-i\infty}^{0^{+}+i\infty}\frac{ds}{2\pi i}\frac{e^{st}}{2^{x}}\left(\frac{1-\langle\hat{\psi}(s)\rangle}{s}\right)\langle\hat{\psi}(s)\rangle^{x}\,.

(SM22)

In the large
x
limit, we can perform the integral in the previous equation using the method of the steepest descent or the saddle point approximation
[
69
]
. This method consists of deforming the integration path in the complex plane so that it crosses a saddle point
s^{*}
, which is in the real line. As is well known, in this new path, the largest contribution to the integral comes from the vicinity of
s^{*}
.

We re-write Eq. (
SM22
) as

\displaystyle\langle\mathrm{Prob}(\to)\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{x}}\int_{s^{*}-i\infty}^{s^{*}++i\infty}\frac{ds}{2\pi i}e^{g(s)}\,,

(SM23)

where the integration is performed on the new path and the auxiliary function
g(s)
is defined, explicitly, as

\displaystyle g(s)\equiv st+x\ln\langle\hat{\psi}(s)\rangle+\ln\left[1-\langle\hat{\psi}(s)\rangle\right]-\ln s\,.

(SM24)

The saddle point must satisfy
g^{\prime}(s^{*})=0
. Since the largest contribution to the integral comes from the vicinity of
s^{*}
, we can expand the auxiliary function
g(s)
as

\displaystyle g(s)\approx g(s^{*})+\frac{g^{(2)}(s^{*})}{2!}(s-s^{*})^{2}+\frac{g^{(3)}(s^{*})}{3!}(s-s^{*})^{3}+\frac{g^{(4)}(s^{*})}{4!}(s-s^{*})^{4}\,.

(SM25)

Here we have included terms up to the fourth-order since we will calculate corrections to the leading term. These terms are important for Eq.(
16
) in the main text but can be neglected for Eq.(
12
).
Plugging the expansion in the previous equation into Eq. (
SM23
), and performing the change of variable
iz=(s-s^{*})
we write

\displaystyle\langle\mathrm{Prob}(\to)\rangle=\int_{s^{*}-i\infty}^{s^{*}+i\infty}\frac{ds}{2\pi i}e^{g(s)}

\displaystyle\approx e^{g(s^{*})}\int_{s^{*}-i\infty}^{s^{*}+i\infty}\frac{ds}{2\pi i}e^{\frac{g^{(2)}(s^{*})}{2}(s-s^{*})^{2}+\frac{g^{(3)}(s^{*})}{6}(s-s^{*})^{3}+\frac{g^{(4)}(s^{*})}{24}(s-s^{*})^{4}}

(SM26)

\displaystyle\approx e^{g(s^{*})}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{dz}{2\pi}e^{-\frac{g^{(2)}(s^{*})}{2}z^{2}+\frac{g^{(3)}(s^{*})}{6}\,iz^{3}+\frac{g^{(4)}(s^{*})}{24}z^{4}}\,.

(SM27)

As usual, the integration path was selected to ensure that
g^{(2)}(s^{*})>0
. In the next subsections, we will show the derivatives at the critical point scale with
x
, up to the leading order, as
g^{(n)}(s^{*})\propto x^{1-n}
. This allows us to expand the
z^{3}
and
z^{4}
terms in the exponent, and perform the Gaussian integral, that is,

\displaystyle\langle\mathrm{Prob}(\to)\rangle

\displaystyle\approx e^{g(s^{*})}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{dz}{2\pi}e^{-\frac{g^{(2)}(s^{*})}{2}z^{2}}\left\{1+\frac{g^{(3)}(s^{*})}{6}\,iz^{3}+\frac{g^{(4)}(s^{*})}{24}z^{4}-\frac{g^{(3)}(s^{*})^{2}}{72}z^{6}\right\}

\displaystyle\approx\frac{e^{g(s^{*})}}{\sqrt{2\pi g^{(2)}(s^{*})}}\left\{1+\frac{g^{(4)}(s^{*})}{8g^{(2)}(s^{*})^{2}}-\frac{5g^{(3)}(s^{*})^{2}}{24g^{(2)}(s^{*})^{3}}\right\}\,,

(SM28)

where the
iz^{3}
term is null due to symmetry. For large
x
,
g^{(4)}(s^{*})/g^{(2)}(s^{*})^{2}\propto g^{(3)}(s^{*})^{2}/g^{(2)}(s^{*})^{3}\propto 1/x
, and all extra terms to the expansion are, at least, of order
O(1/x^{2})
.

Now we obtain an analytical expression for
\langle\mathrm{Prob}(\to)\rangle
by solving Eq. (
SM22
) in the asymptotic limit of large
x
, considering that the waiting times are exponentially distributed and that the energy density is
\rho(E)
. The saddle point
s^{*}
can be found by finding the critical point of
g(s)
, which is defined in Eq. (
SM24
), that is

\displaystyle g^{\prime}(s^{*})=0\to t+x\frac{\langle\hat{\psi}^{\prime}(s^{*})\rangle}{\langle\hat{\psi}(s^{*})\rangle}-\frac{\langle\hat{\psi}^{\prime}(s^{*})\rangle}{1-\langle\hat{\psi}(s^{*})\rangle}-\frac{1}{s^{*}}=0\,.

(SM29)

In the large
x
limit, we have the critical
s^{*}\propto x
. Therefore, we are also interested in the large
s
limit of
\langle\hat{\psi}(s)\rangle
. From Eq. (
SM19
), in the limit of large
s
, we have

\displaystyle\langle\hat{\psi}(s)\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}f(R)\frac{R}{R+s}dR\approx\int_{0}^{\infty}f(R)\left(\frac{R}{s}-\frac{R^{2}}{s^{2}}+\frac{R^{3}}{s^{3}}+O(1/s^{4})\right)dR

\displaystyle\approx\frac{\langle R\rangle}{s}-\frac{\langle R^{2}\rangle}{s^{2}}+\frac{\langle R^{3}\rangle}{s^{3}}\,.

(SM30)

We plug the expression for
\langle\hat{\psi}(s)\rangle
in the previous equation into Eq. (
SM29
) to find

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=t-\frac{x+1}{s^{*}}+\frac{\langle R\rangle+x\frac{\langle R^{2}\rangle}{\langle R\rangle}}{{s^{*}}^{2}}+\frac{\langle R^{2}\rangle-2\langle R^{2}\rangle-\frac{x(2\langle R\rangle\langle R^{2}\rangle-\langle R^{2}\rangle^{2})}{\langle R\rangle^{2}}}{{s^{*}}^{3}}+O({s^{*}}^{4})\,.

(SM31)

The saddle point can be expanded in a series of
x
, as

\displaystyle s^{*}

\displaystyle\approx s_{1}x+s_{0}+\frac{s_{-1}}{x}+O(1/x^{2})\,,

(SM32)

where the values of
s_{1}
,
s_{0}
and
s_{-1}
can be found by plugging the previous equation into Eq. (
SM31
), to obtain an expression for
g^{\prime}(s^{*})
as a series of
1/x
,

\displaystyle g^{\prime}(s^{*})

\displaystyle\approx\left[t+\frac{1}{s_{0}}\right]-\frac{1}{x}\left[\frac{s_{1}}{s_{0}^{2}}-\frac{\langle R^{2}\rangle}{\langle R\rangle s_{0}^{2}}+\frac{1}{s_{0}}\right]+O(1/x^{2})\,=0.

(SM33)

The values of
s_{1}
,
s_{0}
, and
s_{-1}
are obtained by imposing that every order of
1/x
in the previous equation be null independently. We will show next that, up to
1/x
terms,
g(s^{*})
does not depend on
s_{-1}
.

The values of
s_{1}
and
s_{0}
are obtained from Eq. (
SM33
) as

\displaystyle s_{1}=\frac{1}{t}\,\,\,,\,\,\,s_{0}=\frac{1}{t}-\frac{\langle R^{2}\rangle}{\langle R\rangle}\,,

(SM34)

leading to the expression of the saddle point

\displaystyle s^{*}=\frac{x}{t}+\left[\frac{1}{t}-\frac{\langle R^{2}\rangle}{\langle R\rangle}\right]+O(1/x)\,.

(SM35)

From Eq. (
SM31
), we find that the leading term of the
n
-th derivative of
g(s)
at the saddle point is

\displaystyle g^{(n\geq 2)}(s^{*})\approx\frac{(-1)^{n}(n-1)!}{x^{1-n}}\,,

(SM36)

and the correction term in Eq. (
SM28
) of higher order derivatives is

\displaystyle\frac{g^{(4)}(s^{*})}{8g^{(2)}(s^{*})^{2}}-\frac{5g^{(3)}(s^{*})^{2}}{24g^{(2)}(s^{*})^{3}}=\frac{\frac{6}{x^{3}}}{\frac{8}{x^{2}}}-\frac{5\frac{16}{x^{4}}}{24\frac{1}{x^{3}}}=-\frac{1}{12x}\,.

(SM37)

Finally, we write the leading term of Eq. (
SM28
) as

\displaystyle\frac{e^{g(s^{*})}}{\sqrt{2\pi g^{(2)}(s^{*})}}=\frac{e^{g(s^{*})-\frac{1}{2}\ln g^{(2)}(s^{*})}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\,.

(SM38)

We will now expand the terms in the exponent to order
1/x
. The second term in the previous equations exponent is straightforward to expand, we have
\ln g^{(2)}(s^{*})\approx-2\ln(x/t)+\ln x-\frac{1}{x}
. The first term expansion is

\displaystyle g(s^{*})

\displaystyle\approx-(x+1)\ln(s_{1}x)+x\left[s_{1}t+\ln\langle R\rangle\right]+\left[s_{0}t-\frac{s_{0}}{s_{1}}-\frac{\langle R^{2}\rangle}{\langle R\rangle s_{0}}\right]

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{x}\left[\frac{s_{0}^{2}}{2s_{1}^{2}}+s_{-1}\left(t-\frac{1}{s_{1}}\right)+\frac{\langle R^{2}\rangle s_{0}}{\langle R\rangle s_{1}^{2}}+\frac{\langle R^{3}\rangle}{\langle R\rangle s_{1}^{2}}-\frac{\langle R^{2}\rangle^{2}}{2\langle R\rangle^{2}s_{1}^{2}}-\frac{s_{0}}{s_{1}}-\langle R\rangle\right]\,,

(SM39)

which is
s_{-1}
independent.
Note that, in previous equation, the only term dependent on
s_{-1}
is multiplied by
t-1/s_{0}
, which is null when we replace the value of
s_{0}
found in Eq. (
SM34
). Therefore, despite that in a general case we require the
s_{-1}/x
term to expand
g(s^{*})
to order
1/x
, in our particular function it is not necessary.

Replacing
s_{0}
and
s_{1}
from Eq. (
SM34
) into Eq. (
SM39
) to find

\displaystyle g(s^{*})\approx-(x+1)\ln\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)+x\left[1+\ln\langle R\rangle\right]-\left[\frac{\langle R^{2}\rangle}{\langle R\rangle}t\right]+\frac{t^{2}}{x}\left[\frac{\langle R^{3}\rangle}{\langle R\rangle}-\frac{\langle R^{2}\rangle^{2}}{\langle R\rangle^{2}}\right]+\frac{t}{x}\left[\frac{\langle R^{2}\rangle-\langle R\rangle^{2}}{\langle R\rangle}\right]-\frac{1}{2x}\,,

(SM40)

and finally,

\displaystyle\langle\mathrm{Prob}(\to)\rangle

\displaystyle\approx\frac{e^{-\frac{\langle R^{2}\rangle}{\langle R\rangle}t}}{\sqrt{2\pi x}}\left(\frac{e\langle R\rangle t}{2x}\right)^{x}\left\{1-\frac{1}{12x}+\frac{t}{x}\left[\frac{\langle R^{2}\rangle-\langle R\rangle^{2}}{\langle R\rangle}\right\}+\frac{t^{2}}{x}\left[\frac{\langle R^{3}\rangle}{\langle R\rangle}-\frac{\langle R^{2}\rangle^{2}}{\langle R\rangle^{2}}\right]\right]\,.

(SM41)

For convenience, we highlight the leading order as

\displaystyle\langle\mathrm{Prob}(\to)\rangle_{0}=\frac{e^{-\frac{\langle R^{2}\rangle}{\langle R\rangle}t}}{\sqrt{2\pi x}}\left(\frac{e\langle R\rangle t}{2x}\right)^{x}\,,

(SM42)

with the first correction being

\displaystyle\langle\mathrm{Prob}(\to)\rangle_{1}=\langle\mathrm{Prob}(\to)\rangle_{0}\left\{\frac{t^{2}}{x}\left[\frac{\langle R^{3}\rangle}{\langle R\rangle}-\frac{\langle R^{2}\rangle^{2}}{\langle R\rangle^{2}}\right]+\frac{t}{x}\left[\frac{\langle R^{2}\rangle-\langle R\rangle^{2}}{\langle R\rangle}\right]-\frac{1}{12x}\right\}.

(SM43)

with the auxiliary functions being

\displaystyle g_{1}(s)

\displaystyle\equiv st+(x-2)\ln\langle\hat{\psi}(s)\rangle+2\ln\langle\hat{\psi}(s)^{2}\rangle+\ln\left[1-\langle\hat{\psi}(s)\rangle\right]-\ln s\,,

(SM58)

\displaystyle g_{2}(s)

\displaystyle\equiv st+x\ln\langle\hat{\psi}(s)\rangle+\ln\langle\hat{\psi}(s)^{2}\rangle+\ln\left[1-\langle\hat{\psi}(s)\rangle\right]-\ln s\,,

(SM59)

\displaystyle g_{3}(s)

\displaystyle\equiv st+(x-1)\ln\langle\hat{\psi}(s)\rangle+\ln\langle\hat{\psi}(s)^{2}\rangle+\ln\left[\langle\hat{\psi}(s)\rangle-\langle\hat{\psi}(s)^{2}\rangle\right]-\ln s\,,

(SM60)

\displaystyle g_{4}(s)

\displaystyle\equiv st+(x+1)\ln\langle\hat{\psi}(s)\rangle+\ln\left[\langle\hat{\psi}(s)\rangle-\langle\hat{\psi}(s)^{2}\rangle\right]-\ln s\,.

(SM61)

We expand the saddle point for the different paths as an asymptotic series for large
x
as
s^{*}\approx s_{1}x+s_{0}
. The saddle points for the different paths are, for orders higher than
1/x
, identical. We can find the analytical expression through

\displaystyle g_{i}^{\prime}(s^{*})\approx\left[t-\frac{1}{s_{1}}\right]+\frac{1}{x}\left[\frac{s_{0}}{s_{1}^{2}}+\frac{\langle R^{2}\rangle}{\langle R\rangle s_{1}^{2}}-\frac{3}{s_{1}}\right]+O(1/x^{2})=0\,,

(SM62)

and therefore,

\displaystyle s_{1}=\frac{1}{t}\,\,,\,\,s_{0}=\frac{3}{t}-\frac{\langle R^{2}\rangle}{\langle R\rangle}\,.

(SM63)

This allows us to write the value of the auxiliary functions
g_{i}
’s at the saddle point, up to order
1/x
, as

\displaystyle g_{1}(s^{*})

\displaystyle\approx-(x+1)\ln\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)+x[1+\ln\langle R\rangle]-\left[\frac{\langle R^{2}\rangle}{\langle R\rangle}t\right]+2\ln\left(\frac{\langle R^{2}\rangle t}{\langle R\rangle x}\right)

(SM64)

\displaystyle g_{2}(s^{*})

\displaystyle\approx-(x+1)\ln\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)+x[1+\ln\langle R\rangle]-\left[\frac{\langle R^{2}\rangle}{\langle R\rangle}t\right]+\ln\left(\frac{\langle R^{2}\rangle t^{2}}{x^{2}}\right)

(SM65)

\displaystyle g_{3}(s^{*})

\displaystyle\approx-(x+1)\ln\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)+x[1+\ln\langle R\rangle]-\left[\frac{\langle R^{2}\rangle}{\langle R\rangle}t\right]+\ln\left(\frac{\langle R^{2}\rangle t^{2}}{x^{2}}\right)

(SM66)

\displaystyle g_{4}(s^{*})

\displaystyle\approx-(x+1)\ln\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)+x[1+\ln\langle R\rangle]-\left[\frac{\langle R^{2}\rangle}{\langle R\rangle}t\right]+2\ln\left(\frac{\langle R\rangle t}{x}\right)\,.

(SM67)

The probability contribution from each zig-zag path is calculated using

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{x+2}}\int_{0^{+}-i\infty}^{0^{+}+i\infty}\frac{ds}{2\pi i}e^{g_{i}(s)}\approx\frac{1}{2^{x+2}}\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{|g_{i}^{\prime\prime}(s^{*})|}}\,e^{g_{i}(s^{*})}\,,

(SM68)

where
g_{i}^{\prime\prime}(s^{*})=t^{2}/x
. The complete asymptotic expressions for the probabilities are

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathrm{Prob}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\includegraphics[scale={0.75}]{zig_1.pdf}\end{array}\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle\approx\frac{e^{-\frac{\langle R^{2}\rangle}{\langle R\rangle}t}}{\sqrt{2\pi x}}\left[\frac{e\langle R\rangle t}{2x}\right]^{x}\,\left[\frac{\langle R^{2}\rangle t}{2\langle R\rangle x^{2}}\right]^{2}+O(1/x^{3})\,,

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathrm{Prob}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\includegraphics[scale={0.75}]{zig_2.pdf}\end{array}\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle\approx\frac{e^{-\frac{\langle R^{2}\rangle}{\langle R\rangle}t}}{\sqrt{2\pi x}}\left[\frac{e\langle R\rangle t}{2x}\right]^{x}\,\frac{\langle R^{2}\rangle t^{2}}{4x^{2}}+O(1/x^{3})\,,

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathrm{Prob}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\includegraphics[scale={0.75}]{zig_3.pdf}\end{array}\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle\approx\frac{e^{-\frac{\langle R^{2}\rangle}{\langle R\rangle}t}}{\sqrt{2\pi x}}\left[\frac{e\langle R\rangle t}{2x}\right]^{x}\,\frac{\langle R^{2}\rangle t^{2}}{4x^{2}}+O(1/x^{3})\,,

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathrm{Prob}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\includegraphics[scale={0.75}]{zig_4.pdf}\end{array}\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle\approx\frac{e^{-\frac{\langle R^{2}\rangle}{\langle R\rangle}t}}{\sqrt{2\pi x}}\left[\frac{e\langle R\rangle t}{2x}\right]^{x}\,\left[\frac{\langle R\rangle t}{2x}\right]^{2}+O(1/x^{3})\,.

From the previous set of equations, we see that the probability contribution from the zig-zag paths is of order
1/x^{2}
smaller than the leading contribution from the direct path. The largest contribution, for large
x
, comes from the first path, zig-zag in the middle of the trajectory
\begin{array}[]{c}\includegraphics[scale={0.75}]{zig_1.pdf}\end{array}
. This is due to the fact that there are
x-1
possible such paths, leading to a complete contribution of order
1/x
, which is the same order of the first correction to the direct path, see Eq. (
SM41
). Using Eqs. (
SM41
) and (
I.4
), our asymptotic theoretical prediction for the lower bound becomes

\displaystyle\langle P(x,t)\rangle

\displaystyle\gtrsim\langle\mathrm{Prob}(\to)\rangle+(x-1)\left\langle\mathrm{Prob}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\includegraphics[scale={0.75}]{zig_1.pdf}\end{array}\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle\gtrsim\frac{e^{-\frac{\langle R^{2}\rangle}{\langle R\rangle}t}}{\sqrt{2\pi x}}\left[\frac{e\langle R\rangle t}{2x}\right]^{x}\,\left\{1-\frac{1}{12x}+\frac{t}{x}\left[\frac{\langle R^{2}\rangle}{\langle R\rangle}-\langle R\rangle\right]+\frac{t^{2}}{x}\left[\frac{\langle R^{3}\rangle}{\langle R\rangle}-\frac{3}{4}\frac{\langle R^{2}\rangle^{2}}{\langle R\rangle^{2}}\right]\right\}\,.

(SM74)

This gives us Eq. (
16
) in the main text.

II
Details on figures

The numerical results are obtained by solving the system of differential equations in Eq. (
1
), with reflecting boundary conditions, using a Runge-Kutta scheme. The values of
R_{x}
are chosen at the start and remain fixed for the duration of the run, generating the random rates in accordance with Table
1
. After performing
\mathcal{N}=100000
such runs, the probability is extracted by taking the average of each run. This yields
\langle P(x,t)\rangle
, here we choose the size of the system to be large enough so that the boundary conditions do not affect the results.

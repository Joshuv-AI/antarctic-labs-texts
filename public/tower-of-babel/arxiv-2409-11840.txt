Tony J. Puthenpurakal

. Let K K be a field and let S = K ⁡ [ X 1 , … , X n ] S=K[X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}] . Let I I be a graded ideal in S S and let M M be a finitely generated graded S S -module. 1.
Introduction

The Koszul complex is a fundamental construction in commutative algebra.
W. Vasconcelos writes in his book “Integral closures”
[
4
, page 280]
:

”While the vanishing of the homology of a Koszul complex
K(\mathbf{x},M)
is easy to track, the module theoretic properties
of its homology, with the exception of the ends, is difficult to fathom. For instance, just trying to see
whether a prime is associated to some
H_{i}(\mathbf{x},M)
can be very hard.”

The purpose of this paper is to enhance our knowledge about Koszul homology modules by establishing the following results:

1.1
.

Setup:
Let
K
be a field and let
S=K[X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}]
. We give
S
the standard grading. Let
I
be a homogeneous ideal in
S
.
Let
\mathbf{x}=x_{1},\ldots,x_{l}
a system of homogeneous, not-necessarily minimal, generators of
I
and let
\mathbb{K}_{\bullet}(\mathbf{x})
be the Koszul complex with respect to
\mathbf{x}
. Assume

|x_{1}|\geq|x_{2}|\geq\ldots\geq|x_{l}|
. Here
|x|
denotes degree of
x
. Let
g=\operatorname{grade}I
. If
N
is a finitely generated graded
S
-module then
\operatorname{reg}N
denotes the regularity of
N
. Furthermore set

t_{i}(N)=\operatorname{reg}\operatorname{Tor}^{S}_{i}(N,K)
for
i\geq 0
.

We first prove

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
M
be a perfect
S
-module of grade
n-g
. Also assume that
\ell(M/IM)
is finite. Then

\operatorname{reg}H_{k}(\mathbf{x},M)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{g+k}|x_{i}|+t_{n-g}(M)-n.

Recall an ideal
I
is said to be strongly Cohen-Macaulay if
H_{i}(I)
is Cohen-Macaulay for all
i
. For instance if
I
is licci then it is strongly Cohen-Macaulay, see
[
2
, 1.14]
. As an easy application of Theorem
1.2
we get,

Corollary 1.3
.

If
I
is strongly Cohen-Macaulay ideal of grade
g
then

\operatorname{reg}H_{k}(\mathbf{x})\leq\sum_{i=1}^{g+k}|x_{i}|-g.

We note that if
I
is zero-dimensional then it is trivially strongly Cohen-Macaulay.

Another application of Theorem
1.2
is

Corollary 1.4
.

If
I
is a Cohen-Macaulay ideal of grade
g
then

\operatorname{reg}S/I\leq\sum_{i=1}^{g}|x_{i}|-g.

Next we show:

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
I
be a zero-dimensional graded ideal in
S
and let
M
be a finitely generated graded
S
-module. Then for
k\leq\operatorname{codim}M
,

\operatorname{reg}H_{k}(\mathbf{x},M)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}|x_{i}|+t_{k}(M)-n

The main technique to prove our results is an analysis of a spectral sequence, discussed in section 3. Here is an overview of the contents of this paper. In section two we discuss a few preliminaries that we need. In section three we discuss the spectral sequence we need. In section four we give a proof of Theorem
1.2
and discuss its corollaries. In section five we prove Theorem
1.5
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we discuss a few preliminary results that we need. In this paper all rings considered are Noetherian and all modules considered (unless otherwise stated) is finitely generated.

2.1
.

Let
K
be a field and let
S=K[X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}]
. We give the standard grading on
S
. Let
S_{+}
be the irrelevant maximal ideal of
S
. Let
M
be a graded
S
-module. Let
H^{i}_{S_{+}}(M)
be the
i^{th}
-local cohomology of
M
with respect to
S_{+}
. Then the regularity of
M
is defined as

\operatorname{reg}M=\max\{i+j\mid H^{i}_{S_{+}}(M)_{j}\neq 0\}.

We let
t_{i}(M)=\operatorname{reg}\operatorname{Tor}^{S}_{i}(M,K)
.
Let
\mathbf{F}_{\bullet}
be a graded minimal free resolution of
M
. Let
{\mathbf{F}_{\bullet}}_{i}=\bigoplus_{j}S(-j)^{b_{ij}}
. Then

\operatorname{reg}M=\max\{j-i\mid b_{ij}\neq 0\}.

The following result is well-known and easy to prove.

Proposition 2.2
.

(with hypotheses as in
2.1
) Let
u_{1},\ldots u_{r}
be linear forms in
S
which is an
M
-regular sequence.
Then
\operatorname{reg}M/(u_{1},\ldots,u_{r})M=\operatorname{reg}M
.

2.3
.

We will need the vanishing of an element in the total complex of a bigraded complex.

Lemma 2.4
.

(
[
1
, 2.6]
) Let
\mathbf{X}
be any bounded (cohomological) double complex.
Suppose that
p,q
are chosen so that the vertical homology of
\mathbf{X}
is zero at

X^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}
,
when
p^{\prime}+q^{\prime}=p+q
and
q^{\prime}<q
, and the horizontal homology of
\mathbf{X}
is zero at

X^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}
,
when
p^{\prime}+q^{\prime}=p+q
and
q^{\prime}>q
. If
\zeta\in H^{p+q}(\operatorname{Tot}(\mathbf{X}))
is represented by a cycle

z=(z^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}})\in\bigoplus_{p^{\prime}+q^{\prime}=p+q}X^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}

satisfies
z^{p,q}=0
, then
\zeta=0
.

Remark 2.5
.

In
[
1
]
note that in a double complex
\mathbf{X}=\bigoplus_{p,q}X^{pq}
the
p
is the
y
-coordinate and
q
is the
x
-coordinate. We hold the standard convention i.e., in a bicomplex
\mathbf{X}=\bigoplus_{p,q}X^{pq}
the
x
-coordinate is
p
and
y
-coordinate is
q
.

3.
A spectral sequence

Let
S=K[X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}]
.
Let
\mathbf{D}_{\bullet}\colon\cdots D_{1}\rightarrow D_{0}\rightarrow 0
be a complex of finitely generated free
S
-modules.
Let
\mathbf{I^{\bullet}}\colon 0\rightarrow I^{0}\rightarrow I^{1}\rightarrow\cdots
be an injective resolution of
S
.
We consider the Hom co-chain complex
\mathbf{C}=\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{D}_{\bullet},\mathbf{I^{\bullet}})
; see
[
5
, 2.7.4]
.
Set
\mathbf{D}_{\bullet}^{*}=\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{D}_{\bullet},S)
.

Lemma 3.1
.

The first spectral sequence on
\mathbf{C}
collapses and hence
H^{i}(\operatorname{Tot}(\mathbf{C}))=H^{i}(\mathbf{D}_{\bullet}^{*})

Proof.

{}^{I}E^{pq}_{0}=\operatorname{Hom}_{S}(D_{p},I^{q})
. So we get

\displaystyle{}^{I}E^{pq}_{1}

\displaystyle=H^{q}(\operatorname{Hom}(D_{p},\mathbf{I^{\bullet}}))

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Hom}(D_{p},H^{q}(\mathbf{I^{\bullet}}))

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}0&\text{for}\ q>0;\\
\operatorname{Hom}(D_{p},S)&\text{for}\ q=0.\end{cases}

Therefore we get

\displaystyle{}^{I}E^{pq}_{2}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}0&\text{for}\ q>0;\\
H^{p}(\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{D}_{\bullet},S))&\text{for}\ q=0.\end{cases}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}0&\text{for}\ q>0;\\
H^{p}(\mathbf{D}_{\bullet}^{*})&\text{for}\ q=0.\end{cases}

∎

We now compute the
E_{2}
term of the second standard spectral sequence on
\mathbf{C}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

{}^{II}E^{pq}_{2}=\operatorname{Ext}^{p}_{S}(H_{q}(\mathbf{D}_{\bullet}),S)

Proof.

{}^{II}E^{pq}_{0}=\operatorname{Hom}(D_{q},I^{p})
. So

\displaystyle{}^{I}E^{pq}_{1}

\displaystyle=H^{q}(\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{D}_{\bullet},I^{p}))

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Hom}(H_{q}(\mathbf{D}_{\bullet}),I^{p})

The second equality is since
I^{p}
is injective. Thus we get

\displaystyle{}^{II}E^{pq}_{2}

\displaystyle=H^{p}\operatorname{Hom}(H_{q}(\mathbf{D}_{\bullet}),\mathbf{I^{\bullet}})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Ext}^{p}_{S}(H_{q}(\mathbf{D}_{\bullet}),S).

∎

The complex
\mathbf{D}_{\bullet}
we will consider is the following:

Let
\mathbf{x}=x_{1},\ldots,x_{l}
a system of homogeneous, not-necessarily minimal, generators of
I
and let
\mathbb{K}_{\bullet}(\mathbf{x})
be the Koszul complex with respect to
\mathbf{x}
. Assume

|x_{1}|\geq|x_{2}|\geq\ldots\geq|x_{l}|
. Here
|x|
denotes degree of
x
.
Let
\mathbf{F}_{\bullet}\colon\cdots F_{1}\rightarrow F_{0}\rightarrow 0
be a minimal resolution of
M
. Set
\mathbf{D}_{\bullet}=\operatorname{Tot}(\mathbb{K}_{\bullet}(\mathbf{x})\otimes\mathbf{F}_{\bullet})
. Notice
\mathbf{D}_{\bullet}^{*}=\operatorname{Tot}(\mathbb{K}^{\bullet}\otimes\mathbf{F}_{\bullet}^{*})
.

Lemma 3.3
.

H_{i}(\mathbf{D}_{\bullet})=H_{i}(\mathbf{x},M)

Proof.

We use the first standard spectral sequence on
\mathbb{K}_{\bullet}(\mathbf{x})\otimes\mathbf{F}_{\bullet}
to compute the homology of
\mathbf{D}_{\bullet}
.
Set

E^{0}_{pq}=K_{p}\otimes F_{q}
. So

\displaystyle E^{1}_{pq}

\displaystyle=H_{q}(K_{p}\otimes\mathbf{F}_{\bullet})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Tor}^{S}_{q}(K_{p},M)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}0&\text{for}\ q>0;\\
K_{p}\otimes M&\text{for}\ q=0.\end{cases}

Therefore we get

E^{2}_{pq}=\begin{cases}0&\text{for}\ q>0;\\
H_{p}(\mathbf{x},M)&\text{for}\ q=0.\end{cases}

Thus
H_{i}(\mathbf{D}_{\bullet})=H_{i}(\mathbf{x},M)
.
∎

4.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

In this section we prove Theorem
1.5
. We restate it for the convenience of the reader.
We assume that
I
is a homogeneous ideal in
S
of grade
g
. The module
M
is perfect of grade
n-g
. We also assume that

\ell(M/IM)
is finite. Here
\ell(N)
is the length of the
S
-module
N
.
Notice that
\ell(H_{i}(\mathbf{x},M))
is also finite for all
i\geq 0
.
Recall we are assuming
|x_{1}|\geq|x_{2}|\geq\cdots\geq|x_{l}|
.

Theorem 4.1
.

\operatorname{reg}H_{k}(\mathbf{x},M)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{g+k}|x_{i}|+t_{n-g}(M)-n
.

Proof.

We consider the second spectral sequence on
\mathbf{C}
. By
3.2
and
3.3
we have

{}^{II}E^{pq}_{2}=\operatorname{Ext}^{p}_{S}(H_{q}(\mathbf{x},M),S)

Since
\ell(H_{i}(\mathbf{x},M))
is also finite for all
i\geq 0
we have
that
{}^{II}E^{pq}_{2}=0
for all
p\neq n
. So the second spectral sequence on
\mathbf{C}
collapses. By

3.2
it follows that

(
\dagger
)

\operatorname{Ext}^{n}_{S}(H_{k}(\mathbf{x},M),S)=H^{n+k}(\mathbf{D}_{\bullet}^{*})\quad\text{for all}\ k\geq 0.

By local duality we have

H_{k}(\mathbf{x},M)=\operatorname{Hom}_{K}\left(\operatorname{Ext}^{n}_{S}(H_{k}(\mathbf{x},M),S),K\right)(n)

So by (
\dagger
) we get that

\operatorname{reg}H_{k}(\mathbf{x},M)=-\operatorname{mindeg}H^{n+k}(\mathbf{D}_{\bullet}^{*})-n

Notice
\mathbf{D}_{\bullet}^{*}=\operatorname{Tot}(\mathbb{K}_{\bullet}^{*}\otimes\mathbf{F}_{\bullet}^{*})
.
We prove that any homogenous element
\xi
in
H^{n+k}(\mathbf{D}_{\bullet}^{*})
of

\deg\xi<-\sum_{i=1}^{g+k}|x_{i}|-t_{n-g}(M)

is zero.

Let
\xi=[z]
where
z=(z^{p,q}\mid p+q=n+k)
is a cycle.

Claim 1:
z^{g+k,n-g}=0
.

\displaystyle\operatorname{mindeg}K^{*}_{g+k}\otimes F^{*}_{n-g}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{mindeg}K^{*}_{g+k}+\operatorname{mindeg}F^{*}_{n-g}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i=1}^{g+k}|x_{i}|-t_{n-g}(M)

\displaystyle>\deg\xi

So
z^{g+k,n-g}=0
.

Set
\mathbf{X}=\mathbb{K}_{\bullet}^{*}\otimes\mathbf{F}_{\bullet}^{*}
. We look at the vertical and horizontal homology of this bi-complex.

Claim 2. Vertical homology of
\mathbf{X}
is
zero for
q<n-g
.

Notice
(H^{q}(\mathbf{X}))^{p}=K_{p}^{*}\otimes\operatorname{Ext}_{S}^{q}(M,S)=0
.

Claim 3. Horizontal homology of
\mathbf{X}

zero for
q>n-g
.

Note
n-g=\operatorname{projdim}M
. So
F^{*}_{q}=0
.
Notice

(H^{p}(\mathbf{X}))^{q}=H^{p}(\mathbb{K}_{\bullet}^{*})\otimes F_{q}^{*}=0.

The result now follows from
2.4
.
∎

Recall we say that an ideal
I
is strongly Cohen-Macaulay if all the Koszul-homology modules of
M
are Cohen-Macaulay.

Corollary 4.2
.

If
I
is strongly Cohen-Macaulay ideal of grade
g
then

\operatorname{reg}H_{k}(\mathbf{x})\leq\sum_{i=1}^{g+k}|x_{i}|-g.

Proof.

We may assume that
K
is an infinite field.
All non-zero

H_{i}(\mathbf{x})
have the same dimension as
A=S/I
(see, e.g.,
[
3
, 4.2.2]
). Let
g=\operatorname{grade}(I)
. Since
I
is strongly Cohen-Macaulay we may choose
y_{1},\ldots,y_{n-g}
linear forms in
S
which are
H_{i}(\mathbf{x})
regular for
i=0,\ldots,l-g
. Set
M=S/(\mathbf{y})
. The result now follows from
Theorem
4.1
and
2.2
.
∎

An easy consequence of the above result is the following result regarding regularity of zero-dimensional ideals.

Corollary 4.3
.

If
I
is a zero dimensional ideal then

\operatorname{reg}H_{k}(\mathbf{x})\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n+k}|x_{i}|-n.

Next we discuss a curious result.

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
I
be a Cohen-Macaulay ideal. Then

\operatorname{reg}S/I\leq\sum_{i=1}^{g}|x_{i}|-g.

Proof.

We may assume that
K
is an infinite field.
All non-zero

H_{i}(\mathbf{x})
have the same dimension as
A=S/I
(see, e.g.,
[
3
, 4.2.2]
). Let
g=\operatorname{grade}(I)
. Since
I
is Cohen-Macaulay we may choose
\mathbf{y}=y_{1},\ldots,y_{n-g}
linear forms in
S
which are
H_{0}(\mathbf{x})=S/I
regular and a system of parameters for
H_{i}(\mathbf{x})
for
i=1,\ldots,l-g
. Set
M=S/(\mathbf{y})
. The result now follows from
Theorem
4.1
and
2.2
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.5

In this section we prove Theorem
1.5
. We restate it for the convenience of the reader.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
I
be a zero-dimensional graded ideal in
S
and let
M
be a finitely generated graded
S
-module. Then for
k\leq\operatorname{codim}M
,

\operatorname{reg}H_{k}(\mathbf{x},M)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}|x_{i}|+t_{k}(M)-n

Proof.

We consider the second spectral sequence on
\mathbf{C}
. By
3.2
and
3.3
we have

{}^{II}E^{pq}_{2}=\operatorname{Ext}^{p}_{S}(H_{q}(\mathbf{x},M),S)

Since
\ell(H_{i}(\mathbf{x},M))
is also finite for all
i\geq 0
we have
that
{}^{II}E^{pq}_{2}=0
for all
p\neq n
. So the second spectral sequence on
\mathbf{C}
collapses. By

3.2
it follows that

(
\dagger
)

\operatorname{Ext}^{n}_{S}(H_{k}(\mathbf{x},M),S)=H^{n+k}(\mathbf{D}_{\bullet}^{*})\quad\text{for all}\ k\geq 0.

By local duality we have

H_{k}(\mathbf{x},M)=\operatorname{Hom}_{K}\left(\operatorname{Ext}^{n}_{S}(H_{k}(\mathbf{x},M),S),K\right)(n)

So by (
\dagger
) we get that

\operatorname{reg}H_{k}(\mathbf{x},M)=-\operatorname{mindeg}H^{n+k}(\mathbf{D}_{\bullet}^{*})-n

Notice
\mathbf{D}_{\bullet}^{*}=\operatorname{Tot}(\mathbb{K}_{\bullet}^{*}\otimes\mathbf{F}_{\bullet}^{*})
.
We prove that if
k\leq\operatorname{codim}M
then any homogenous element
\xi
in
H^{n+k}(\mathbf{D}_{\bullet}^{*})
of

\deg\xi<-\sum_{i=1}^{n}|x_{i}|-t_{k}(M)

is zero.

Let
\xi=[z]
where
z=(z^{p,q}\mid p+q=n+k)
is a cycle.

Claim 1:
z^{n,k}=0
.

\displaystyle\operatorname{mindeg}K^{*}_{n}\otimes F^{*}_{k}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{mindeg}K^{*}_{n}+\operatorname{mindeg}F^{*}_{k}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{mindeg}K^{*}_{n}\otimes F^{*}_{k}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i=1}^{n}|x_{i}|-t_{k}(M)

\displaystyle>\deg\xi

So
z^{n,k}=0
.

Set
\mathbf{X}=\mathbb{K}_{\bullet}^{*}\otimes\mathbf{F}_{\bullet}^{*}
. We look at the vertical and horizontal homology of this bi-complex.

Claim 2: Vertical homology of
\mathbf{X}
is zero for
q<k\leq\operatorname{codim}M
.

Notice
(H^{q}(\mathbf{X}))^{p}=K_{p}^{*}\otimes\operatorname{Ext}^{q}_{S}(M,S)=0
for
q<\operatorname{codim}M
.

Claim 3: Horizontal homology of
\mathbf{X}
is zero for
q>k
.

Then note
p<n

Notice
(H^{p}(\mathbf{X}))^{q}=H^{p}(\mathbf{x})\otimes F_{q}^{*}=0
for
p<n
.

The result now follows from
2.4
.
∎

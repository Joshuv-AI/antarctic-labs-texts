Abstract
. A total coloring of a graph G G is a coloring of the vertices and edges such that two adjacent or incident elements receive different colors. The minimum number of colors required for a total coloring of a graph G G is called the total chromatic number, denoted by χ ′′ ​ ( G ) \chi^{\prime\prime}(G) . Let G G be a planar graph of maximum degree eight. It is known that 9 ≤ χ ′′ ​ ( G ) ≤ 10 9\leq\chi^{\prime\prime}(G)\leq 10 . We here prove that χ ′′ ​ ( G ) = 9 \chi^{\prime\prime}(G)=9 when the graph does not contain any subgraph isomorphic to a 4 4 -fan.



1.
Introduction

All graphs in this paper are assumed to be simple. For terminology and notation not defined here, we refer the reader to
[
16
]
. Let
G
be a graph. We use
V(G)
,
E(G)
,
F(G)
, and

to denote the vertex set, edge set, face set, and maximum degree of
G

\Delta(G)
to

A total coloring of a planar graph
G
is a coloring of
V(G)\cup E(G)
such that any two adjacent or incident elements receive different colors. The minimum number of colors required for a total coloring of
G
is called the total chromatic number, denoted by
\chi^{\prime\prime}(G)
. We refer the reader to the comprehensive survey by Geetha et al.
[
3
]
for progress on the total chromatic number of graphs.
Behzad
[
1
]
and Vizing
[
12
]
independently posed the following conjecture, known as the Total Coloring Conjecture.

Conjecture 1.1
.

For any graph
G
,
\chi^{\prime\prime}(G)\leq\Delta(G)+2
.

Conjecture
1.1
has been proved by Rosenfeld
[
7
]
for
\Delta=3
, and by Kostochka
[
5
]
for
\Delta\leq 5
. For planar graphs, the conjecture has been verified by Borodin
[
2
]
for
\Delta\geq 9
, by Yap
[
17
]
for
\Delta\geq 8
, and by Sanders and Zhao
[
9
]
for
\Delta=7
. Therefore, the only remaining open case for planar graphs is when
\Delta=6
. In this case, some partial results are known: Sun et al.
[
11
]
proved that every planar graph
G
with maximum degree
6
is totally
8
-colorable if no two triangles in
G
share a common edge. In a recent paper, Zhu and Xu
[
18
]
improved this result by proving that
\chi^{\prime\prime}(G)\leq 8
when
\Delta=6
and
G
does not contain any subgraph isomorphic to a
4
-fan (see Figure
1
).

It is known that
\chi^{\prime\prime}(G)\geq\Delta+1
, since any two adjacent or incident elements must receive different colors. In 1989, Sanchez-Arroyo
[
10
]
proved that, in general, it is NP-complete to decide whether
\chi^{\prime\prime}(G)=\Delta+1
. For planar graphs, it has been shown that
\chi^{\prime\prime}(G)=\Delta+1
when
\Delta\geq 9

[
2
,
6
,
13
]
. Thus, it remains an open question whether every planar graph with maximum degree
\Delta\in\{4,5,6,7,8\}
is totally
(\Delta+1)
-colorable.
Note that
\chi^{\prime\prime}(G)=\Delta+2=5
when
G
is isomorphic to the complete graph with four vertices.

Some recent papers are devoted to proving that
\chi^{\prime\prime}(G)=9
when
\Delta=8
, under additional structural restrictions. For instance, Hou et al.
[
4
]
proved that
\chi^{\prime\prime}(G)=9
when
\Delta=8
and

contains no
5
- or
6
-cycles. Later, Roussel and Zhu
[

]
showed that

when

and, for each vertex

such that no
k_{v}
-cycle contains

. On the other hand, Wang et al.
[
14
]
proved that
\chi^{\prime\prime}(G)=9
when
\Delta=8
and
G
contains no adjacent

-cycles for some
p,q\in\{3,4,5,6,7\}
. More recently, Wang et al.
[

generalized this result and showed that
\chi^{\prime\prime}(G)=9
when
\Delta=8
and

has no adjacent

-cycles for some

In this paper, we provide a sufficient condition for planar graphs with maximum degree eight to be totally

-colorable.

Let

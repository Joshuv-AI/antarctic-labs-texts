An elementary proof of the existence and uniqueness of solutions to an initial value problem
Luca Tanganelli Castrillón

Abstract
In this note, we show a classical result on the local existence and uniqueness of a solution to an initial value problem subject to a Lipschitz condition. We use only elementary tools from mathematical analysis, without involving any integration. We proceed by showing that the Cauchy iterates converge on a dense subset of the interval and subsequently proving that the extension of this limit function to the whole interval is a solution to the Cauchy problem.



1
The initial value problem

The initial value problem consists of finding a function
x(t)
such that

(\text{IVP})\begin{cases}x^{\prime}(t)=f(t,x(t)),\qquad t\geq t_{0}\\
x(t_{0})=x_{0}\end{cases}

where
f:D\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
,
t_{0},x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
.

Under certain conditions, the problem has a solution and it is unique.

Let’s assume that there exist
a,M,L>0
such that
U:=[t_{0},t_{0}+a]\times[x_{0}-Ma,x_{0}+Ma]\subset D
and that for all
\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\in U

|f(\mathbf{u})-f(\mathbf{v})|\leq L\left\|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{v}\right\|_{1},\qquad|f(\mathbf{u})|\leq M.

That is,
f
is
L
-Lipschitz-continuous and bounded by
M
in
U
. The interest in the existence of such
U
lies in the fact that, as a consequence of the mean value theorem, any solution function of (IVP) defined in
[t_{0},t_{0}+a]
must take values in
[x_{0}-Ma,x_{0}+Ma]
which will facilitate our work.

Then we are going to prove that there exists a function
x:[t_{0},t_{0}+a]\to\mathbb{R}
that verifies the IVP. Afterwards, we will see that it is the only function defined in
[t_{0},t_{0}+a]
that verifies (IVP).

For simplicity in notation, we will assume
t_{0}=0
. The original problem is recovered without complication through a translation.

Definition 1

Given
m\geq 1
, we define
\{x_{0}^{m},x_{\frac{a}{2^{m}}}^{m},\ldots,x_{\frac{ak}{2^{m}}}^{m},\ldots,x_{\frac{a(2^{m}-1)}{2^{m}}}^{m},x_{a}^{m}\}
as follows.

\begin{cases}x_{0}^{m}=x_{0}\\
x_{\frac{a(k+1)}{2^{m}}}^{m}=x_{\frac{ak}{2^{m}}}^{m}+\frac{a}{2^{m}}f\left(\frac{ak}{2^{m}},x_{\frac{ak}{2^{m}}}^{m}\right)\end{cases}

Definition 2

We will call
\mathbf{D}=\{\frac{ak}{2^{m}}:0\leq k\leq 2^{m},k,m\in\mathbb{N}\}
.

An important property of
\mathbf{D}
is that it is dense in
[0,a]
.

Lemma 1

For all
c,d\in\mathbf{D}
, if
m\in\mathbb{N}
is such that
x_{c}^{m}
and
x_{d}^{m}
are well defined, then

•

|x_{c}^{m}-x_{0}|\leq Mc\leq Ma\implies(c,x_{c}^{m})\in U
;

•

|x_{c}^{m}-x_{d}^{m}|\leq M|c-d|
.

Proof.
The first is by induction on
0\leq k\leq 2^{m}
. The second is a consequence of the triangular inequality and the bounding of
f
by
M
in
U
.

Proposition 1

For all
d\in\mathbf{D}
, the sequence
(x_{d}^{m})_{m=m_{d}}^{\infty}
is convergent, where
m_{d}
is the first natural for which the numbers
x_{d}^{m}
,
m\geq m_{d}
, are well defined. Moreover, the convergence is uniform in the variable
d
.

Proof.

For each
m\geq m_{d}
, let
e_{d}^{m}=|x_{d}^{m+1}-x_{d}^{m}|
.
Then observe that
e_{0}^{m}=0
. Also, if
k>0

x_{\frac{ak}{2^{m}}}^{m}=x_{\frac{a(k-1)}{2^{m}}}^{m}+\frac{a}{2^{m}}f\left(\frac{a(k-1)}{2^{m}},x_{\frac{a(k-1)}{2^{m}}}^{m}\right)

x_{\frac{ak}{2^{m}}}^{m+1}=x_{\frac{a(k-1)}{2^{m}}}^{m+1}+\frac{a}{2^{m+1}}\left[f\left(\frac{a(k-1)}{2^{m}},x_{\frac{a(k-1)}{2^{m}}}^{m+1}\right)+f\left(\frac{a(2k-1)}{2^{m+1}},x_{\frac{a(2k-1)}{2^{m+1}}}^{m+1}\right)\right].

Subtracting the equation above from the one below and taking modules, the following inequality is obtained.

e_{\frac{ak}{2^{m}}}^{m}\leq e_{\frac{a(k-1)}{2^{m}}}^{m}+\frac{a}{2^{m+1}}\Bigg(\left|f\left(\frac{a(k-1)}{2^{m}},x_{\frac{a(k-1)}{2^{m}}}^{m+1}\right)-f\left(\frac{a(k-1)}{2^{m}},x_{\frac{a(k-1)}{2^{m}}}^{m}\right)\right|+

\left|f\left(\frac{a(2k-1)}{2^{m+1}},x_{\frac{a(2k-1)}{2^{m+1}}}^{m+1}\right)-f\left(\frac{a(k-1)}{2^{m}},x_{\frac{a(k-1)}{2^{m}}}^{m}\right)\right|\Bigg).

(1)

Now,

\left\|\left(\frac{a(k-1)}{2^{m}},x_{\frac{a(k-1)}{2^{m}}}^{m+1}\right)-\left(\frac{a(k-1)}{2^{m}},x_{\frac{a(k-1)}{2^{m}}}^{m}\right)\right\|_{1}=|x_{\frac{a(k-1)}{2^{m}}}^{m+1}-x_{\frac{a(k-1)}{2^{m}}}^{m}|=e_{\frac{a(k-1)}{2^{m}}}^{m}

(2)

then, applying the Lipschitz property,

\left|f\left(\frac{a(k-1)}{2^{m}},x_{\frac{a(k-1)}{2^{m}}}^{m+1}\right)-f\left(\frac{a(k-1)}{2^{m}},x_{\frac{a(k-1)}{2^{m}}}^{m}\right)\right|\leq Le_{\frac{a(k-1)}{2^{m}}}^{m}.

Also,

\displaystyle\left\|\left(\frac{a(2k-1)}{2^{m+1}},x_{\frac{a(2k-1)}{2^{m+1}}}^{m+1}\right)-\left(\frac{a(k-1)}{2^{m}},x_{\frac{a(k-1)}{2^{m}}}^{m}\right)\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{a}{2^{m+1}}+|x_{\frac{a(2k-1)}{2^{m+1}}}^{m+1}-x_{\frac{a(k-1)}{2^{m}}}^{m}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{a}{2^{m+1}}+|x_{\frac{a(2k-1)}{2^{m+1}}}^{m+1}-x_{\frac{a(k-1)}{2^{m}}}^{m+1}|+|x_{\frac{a(k-1)}{2^{m}}}^{m+1}-x_{\frac{a(k-1)}{2^{m}}}^{m}|

\displaystyle=\frac{a}{2^{m+1}}+\left|\frac{a}{2^{m+1}}f\left(\frac{a(k-1)}{2^{m}},x_{\frac{a(k-1)}{2^{m}}}^{m+1}\right)\right|+e_{\frac{a(k-1)}{2^{m}}}^{m}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{a}{2^{m+1}}+\frac{a}{2^{m+1}}M+e_{\frac{a(k-1)}{2^{m}}}^{m}.

Applying the Lipschitz property again:

\left|f\left(\frac{a(2k-1)}{2^{m+1}},x_{\frac{a(2k-1)}{2^{m+1}}}^{m+1}\right)-f\left(\frac{a(k-1)}{2^{m}},x_{\frac{a(k-1)}{2^{m}}}^{m}\right)\right|\leq L\left(\frac{a(M+1)}{2^{m+1}}+e_{\frac{a(k-1)}{2^{m}}}^{m}\right).

(3)

Combining (2) and (3) in (1):

\displaystyle e_{\frac{ak}{2^{m}}}^{m}\leq e_{\frac{a(k-1)}{2^{m}}}^{m}+\frac{a}{2^{m+1}}\left(Le_{\frac{a(k-1)}{2^{m}}}^{m}+L\left(\frac{a(M+1)}{2^{m+1}}+e_{\frac{a(k-1)}{2^{m}}}^{m}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=e_{\frac{a(k-1)}{2^{m}}}^{m}\left(1+\frac{aL}{2^{m}}\right)+\frac{a^{2}L(M+1)}{2^{2m+2}}

\displaystyle=e_{\frac{a(k-1)}{2^{m}}}^{m}\left(1+\alpha\right)+\beta

where
\alpha=\frac{aL}{2^{m}}
and
\beta=\frac{a^{2}L(M+1)}{2^{2m+2}}
. Now, making use of this inequality, it is observed that

\displaystyle e_{\frac{ak}{2^{m}}}^{m}

\displaystyle\leq e_{\frac{a(k-1)}{2^{m}}}^{m}(1+\alpha)+\beta

\displaystyle\leq(e_{\frac{a(k-2)}{2^{m}}}^{m}(1+\alpha)+\beta)(1+\alpha)+\beta

\displaystyle=e_{\frac{a(k-2)}{2^{m}}}^{m}(1+\alpha)^{2}+\beta(1+(1+\alpha))

\displaystyle\leq(e_{\frac{a(k-3)}{2^{m}}}^{m}(1+\alpha)+\beta)(1+\alpha)^{2}+\beta(1+(1+\alpha))

\displaystyle=e_{\frac{a(k-3)}{2^{m}}}^{m}(1+\alpha)^{3}+\beta(1+(1+\alpha)+(1+\alpha)^{2})

\displaystyle\leq\ldots

\displaystyle\leq e_{\frac{a(k-k)}{2^{m}}}^{m}(1+\alpha)^{k}+\beta(1+(1+\alpha)+(1+\alpha)^{2}+\ldots+(1+\alpha)^{k})

\displaystyle=\beta(1+(1+\alpha)+(1+\alpha)^{2}+\ldots+(1+\alpha)^{k})

\displaystyle\leq\beta(1+(1+\alpha)+(1+\alpha)^{2}+\ldots+(1+\alpha)^{2^{m}})

\displaystyle=\beta\frac{(1+\alpha)^{2^{m}+1}-1}{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\frac{a^{2}L(M+1)}{2^{2m+2}}\frac{(1+\frac{aL}{2^{m}})^{2^{m}+1}-1}{\frac{aL}{2^{m}}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{a(M+1)\left(e^{aL}(1+\frac{aL}{2^{m}})-1\right)}{2^{m+2}}

If
C=\frac{a(M+1)\left(e^{aL}(1+aL)-1\right)}{4}
, as the above expression is independent of
k
, it is deduced that, for all
d\in\mathbf{D}
such that
e_{d}^{m}
is well defined,

e_{d}^{m}\leq\frac{C}{2^{m}}.

From this it is deduced that the sequence
(x_{d}^{m})_{m=m_{d}}^{\infty}
is Cauchy: if
n_{0}\leq m<n
,

|x_{d}^{m}-x_{d}^{n}|\leq e_{d}^{m}+\ldots+e_{d}^{n-1}\leq C\left(\frac{1}{2^{m}}+\ldots+\frac{1}{2^{n-1}}\right)<\frac{C}{2^{n_{0}-1}}

and therefore
x_{d}^{m}\to x_{d}
for a certain
x_{d}\in\mathbb{R}
. In particular
|x_{d}^{m}-x_{d}|\leq\frac{C}{2^{m-1}}\,\forall d\in\mathbf{D}:m\geq m_{d}
then the convergence is uniform in
d
.
\Box

Definition 3

For each
d\in\mathbf{D}
, we call
x_{d}=\lim_{m\to\infty}x_{d}^{m}
.

Lemma 2

For all
c,d\in\mathbf{D}
,
|x_{c}-x_{d}|\leq M|c-d|
.

Proof.
It follows from Lemma 1.

Lemma 3

If
(d_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathbf{D}
is convergent, then
(x_{d_{n}})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is convergent.

Proof.

(d_{n})
is Cauchy and from Lemma 2 it follows that
(x_{d_{n}})
is Cauchy then convergent.

Lemma 4

If
(c_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}},(d_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathbf{D}
are sequences convergent to the same point, then
(x_{c_{n}})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(x_{d_{n}})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
are convergent to the same point.

Proof.
By Lemma 3,
(x_{c_{n}})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(x_{d_{n}})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
are convergent. Also, as
|x_{c_{n}}-x_{d_{n}}|\leq M|c_{n}-d_{n}|\to 0
, it follows that
|x_{c_{n}}-x_{d_{n}}|\to 0
.

Definition 4

For each
t\in[0,a]
, we define
x(t)=\lim_{n\to\infty}x_{d_{n}}
where
(d_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathbf{D}
is any sequence convergent to
t
. Observe that
x(d)=x_{d}
for all
d\in\mathbf{D}
.

With Definition 4 we have obtained a function
x:[0,a]\to\mathbb{R}
. We will now see that it is a solution of the IVP.

Lemma 5

For all
t,s\in[0,a]
,
|x(t)-x(s)|\leq M|t-s|
.

Proof.
It follows from Lemma 2.

Corollary 1

For all
t\in[0,a]
,
(t,x(t))\in U
.

Proposition 2

If
(c_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}},(d_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathbf{D}
are sequences converging both to
t\in[0,a]
such that
c_{n}<d_{n}\,\forall n\in\mathbb{N}
, then

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{x_{c_{n}}-x_{d_{n}}}{c_{n}-d_{n}}=f(t,x(t)).

Proof.

\left|\frac{x_{c_{n}}-x_{d_{n}}}{c_{n}-d_{n}}-f(t,x(t))\right|=\lim_{m\to\infty}\left|\frac{x_{c_{n}}^{m}-x_{d_{n}}^{m}}{c_{n}-d_{n}}-f(t,x(t))\right|.

(4)

Now, for each
m
(sufficiently large) we can write
d_{n}=c_{n}+\frac{ak_{n}^{m}}{2^{m}}
. Then

x_{d_{n}}^{m}=x_{c_{n}}^{m}+\frac{a}{2^{m}}\left(f(c_{n},x_{c_{n}}^{m})+f(c_{n}+\frac{a}{2^{m}},x_{c_{n}+\frac{a}{2^{m}}}^{m})+\ldots+f(d_{n}-\frac{a}{2^{m}},x_{d_{n}-\frac{a}{2^{m}}}^{m})\right).

So,

\displaystyle\left|\frac{x_{c_{n}}^{m}-x_{d_{n}}^{m}}{c_{n}-d_{n}}-f(t,x(t))\right|=

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{\frac{a}{2^{m}}\left(f(c_{n},x_{c_{n}}^{m})+f(c_{n}+\frac{a}{2^{m}},x_{c_{n}+\frac{a}{2^{m}}}^{m})+\ldots+f(d_{n}-\frac{a}{2^{m}},x_{d_{n}-\frac{}{2^{m}}}^{m})\right)}{\frac{a}{2^{m}}k_{n}^{m}}-f(t,x(t))\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{\big(f(c_{n},x_{c_{n}}^{m})-f(t,x(t))\big)+\ldots+\big(f(d_{n}-\frac{a}{2^{m}},x_{d_{n}-\frac{a}{2^{m}}}^{m})-f(t,x(t))\big)}{k_{n}^{m}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\left|f(c_{n},x_{c_{n}}^{m})-f(c_{n},x_{c_{n}})\right|+\ldots+\left|f(d_{n}-\frac{a}{2^{m}},x_{d_{n}-\frac{a}{2^{m}}}^{m})-f(d_{n}-\frac{a}{2^{m}},x_{d_{n}-\frac{a}{2^{m}}})\right|}{k_{n}^{m}}

\displaystyle+\frac{\left|f(c_{n},x_{c_{n}})-f(t,x(t))\right|+\ldots+\left|f(d_{n}-\frac{a}{2^{m}},x_{d_{n}-\frac{a}{2^{m}}})-f(t,x(t))\right|}{k_{n}^{m}}

\displaystyle\leq L\frac{\left|x_{c_{n}}^{m}-x_{c_{n}}\right|+\ldots+\left|x_{d_{n}-\frac{a}{2^{m}}}^{m}-x_{d_{n}-\frac{a}{2^{m}}}\right|}{k_{n}^{m}}\qquad\text{(Lipschitz property)}

\displaystyle+L(M+1)\frac{\left|c_{n}-t\right|+\ldots+\left|d_{n}-\frac{a}{2^{m}}-t\right|}{k_{n}^{m}}\qquad\text{(Lischitz property followed by Lemma 5).}

Now, as
c_{n}<d_{n}
, it is easily checked that for all
0\leq k\leq k_{n}^{m}
,
\left|c_{n}+\frac{ak}{2^{m}}-t\right|\leq\max(|c_{n}-t|,|d_{n}-t|)=:\mu_{n}
. Also, as the convergence of
(x^{m}_{d})
is uniform in
d
, for all
\epsilon>0
there exists
m_{\epsilon}
so that if
m\geq m_{\epsilon}
then
\left|x_{c_{n}}^{m}-x_{c_{n}}\right|,\ldots,\left|x_{d_{n}-\frac{a}{2^{m}}}^{m}-x_{d_{n}-\frac{a}{2^{m}}}\right|<\epsilon
. Combining this fact with the previous one, it is found that if
m\geq m_{\epsilon}
:

\left|\frac{x_{c_{n}}^{m}-x_{d_{n}}^{m}}{c_{n}-d_{n}}-f(t,x(t))\right|\leq L\frac{\epsilon+\ldots+\epsilon}{k_{n}^{m}}+L(M+1)\frac{\mu_{n}+\ldots+\mu_{n}}{k_{n}^{m}}.

As the summands of the numerators have exactly
k_{n}^{m}
terms, and returning to (4), it is concluded that

\left|\frac{x_{c_{n}}-x_{d_{n}}}{c_{n}-d_{n}}-f(t,x(t))\right|=\lim_{m\to\infty}\left|\frac{x_{c_{n}}^{m}-x_{d_{n}}^{m}}{c_{n}-d_{n}}-f(t,x(t))\right|\leq L\epsilon+L(M+1)\mu_{n}.

As this occurs for all
\epsilon>0
, we obtain

\left|\frac{x_{c_{n}}-x_{d_{n}}}{c_{n}-d_{n}}-f(t,x(t))\right|\leq L(M+1)\mu_{n}.

As
\mu_{n}\to 0
, we finally conclude that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\left|\frac{x_{c_{n}}-x_{d_{n}}}{c_{n}-d_{n}}-f(t,x(t))\right|=0

as we wanted to see.
\Box

Proposition 3

The function
x
is differentiable and, for all
t\in[0,a]
,
x^{\prime}(t)=f(t,x(t))
.

Proof.
We will first take
t\in]0,1[
. Let
(t_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence convergent to
t
. We first assume that
t<t_{n}\,\forall n\in\mathbb{N}
. Due to the density of
\mathbf{D}
, we can find sequences
(c_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}},(d_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathbf{D}
such that

•

t<c_{n}<d_{n}<t_{n}

•

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{d_{n}-c_{n}}{t_{n}-t}=1
.

In particular, we have
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{t_{n}-d_{n}}{t_{n}-t}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{c_{n}-t}{t_{n}-t}=0
and, as a consequence of Proposition 2,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{x_{d_{n}}-x_{c_{n}}}{t_{n}-t}=f(t,x(t)).

Then

\displaystyle\left|\frac{x(t_{n})-x(t)}{t_{n}-t}-f(t,x(t))\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\frac{x(t_{n})-x(d_{n})}{t_{n}-t}\right|+\left|\frac{x(c_{n})-x(t)}{t_{n}-t}\right|+\left|\frac{x(d_{n})-x(c_{n})}{t_{n}-t}-f(t,x(t))\right|

\displaystyle\leq M\left|\frac{t_{n}-d_{n}}{t_{n}-t}\right|+M\left|\frac{c_{n}-t}{t_{n}-t}\right|+\left|\frac{x(d_{n})-x(c_{n})}{t_{n}-t}-f(t,x(t))\right|\to 0

by the sequential characterization of the limit,
\lim_{s\to t^{+}}\frac{x(s)-x(t)}{s-t}=f(t,x(t))
. The limit from the left is calculated with an analogous method and gives
f(t,x(t))
. If
t=0
or
t=1
only the limit from the right and left respectively would be taken, according to the definition of derivative at those points.
\Box

Lemma 6

If
u:[0,b]\to\mathbb{R}
is differentiable,
u(0)=0
and for all
t\in[0,b]
then
|u^{\prime}(t)|\leq K|u(t)|
for a certain real constant
K>0
, then
u(t)=0\,\forall t\in[0,b]
.

Proof.

u
is continuous on a compact set then bounded, let’s say
|u|\leq A>0
. Let
S=\{t\in[0,b]:u(t)=0\}
.
S
is not empty. Let
s\in S
and let
t_{0}\in[0,b]
with
s<t_{0}<s+\frac{1}{2K}
. Applying the mean value theorem successively,
\{t_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
are obtained with
t_{0}>t_{1}>t_{2}>t_{3}>\ldots>t_{n}>\ldots>s
so that for all
n\geq 0

u(t_{n})=u(t_{n})-u(s)=u^{\prime}(t_{n+1})(t_{n}-s)\implies|u(t_{n})|\leq|u^{\prime}(t_{n+1})|\frac{1}{2K}\leq\frac{|u(t_{n+1})|}{2}.

But then for all
n\geq 0
,
|u(t_{0})|\leq\frac{|u(t_{n})|}{2^{n}}\leq\frac{A}{2^{n}}
from which it follows that
u(t_{0})=0
.

This means that if
s\in S
then
[s,s+\frac{1}{2K}[\cap[0,b]\subset S
. It is immediate that
S=[0,b]
as we wanted to see.
\Box

Theorem 1

Under the conditions mentioned on the first page about
f
, the IVP admits a unique solution with domain
[0,a]
.

Proof.
The existence is already proven. Let’s now suppose that there exist
x_{1},x_{2}:[0,a]\to\mathbb{R}
such that
x_{i}(0)=x_{0}
and, for all
t\in[0,a]
,
(t,x_{i}(t))\in D
and
x_{i}^{\prime}(t)=f(t,x_{i}(t))
for
i=1,2
. The first thing is to see that
(t,x_{i}(t))\in U\,\forall t\in[0,a]
,
i=1,2
.

Let
t=\min\{\tau\in[0,a[:|x_{i}(\tau)-x_{0}|\geq Ma\}>0
, if BWOC this set is non-empty. As
x_{i}
is continuous, by the intermediate value theorem, there exists
s\in]0,t[
such that
|x_{i}(s)-x_{0}|=M\frac{a+t}{2}>Mt
. By the mean value theorem there exists
0<w<s
with

|f(w,x_{i}(w))|=|x_{i}^{\prime}(w)|=\frac{|x_{i}(s)-x_{0}|}{s}>\frac{Mt}{t}=M

which implies that
(w,x_{i}(w))\notin U\implies|x_{i}(w)-x_{0}|>Ma
contradicting the minimality of
t
.

With this we now know that for all
t\in[0,a]
we have
(t,x_{i}(t))\in U
. Now, let
u=x_{1}-x_{2}
. Observe that
u
is differentiable and
u(0)=0
. Also, for all
t\in[0,a]

|u^{\prime}(t)|=|x_{1}^{\prime}(t)-x_{2}^{\prime}(t)|=|f(t,x_{1}(t))-f(t,x_{2}(t))|\leq L|x_{1}(t)-x_{2}(t)|=L|u(t)|.

We have been able to apply the Lipschitz property since
(t,x_{i}(t))\in U
,
i=1,2
. By Lemma 6,
u=0
and
x_{1}=x_{2}
.
\Box

Initial ideals of generic ideals and variations of Moreno-Socías conjecture
Koichiro Tani

Abstract
. It is known that the initial ideals of generic ideals are the same. Moreno-Socías conjectured that the initial ideal of generic ideals with respect to the degree reverse lexicographic order is weakly reverse lexicographic. In the first half of this paper, we study the initial ideal of generic ideals for arbitrary monomial order and prove that the initial ideal of generic ideals is Borel-fixed. It can be considered as a weakened version of Moreno-Socías conjecture. In the second half, we propose a new method of the computation of the initial ideal of generic ideals using stability condition of Gröbner bases. We apply the method in the case of lexicographic order and study the relationship between the lexsegment ideal and the initial ideal of generic ideals. This study aims to bound the maximal degree of Gröbner basis. At the last, we propose questions that can be considered as a lexicographic analogue of Moreno-Socías conjecture.

At the last, we propose questions

2.
The initial ideal of generic ideals is Borel-fixed

In this section we reconstruct discussions about generic ideals by using exterior algebra.
This method is analogous to the approach used to prove that the generic initial ideal is Borel-fixed.
The existence of generic initial ideals and Borel-fixedness in characteristic
0
were proved by Galligo
[
14
]
, and later by
Bayer and Stillman
[
2
]
for any order and any characteristic.
Proofs of the existence and Borel-fixedness are written, for example, in
[
9
, Section 15.9]
.

We consider
I_{d}
as a
\Bbbk
-subspace of
S_{d}
for each degree
d\geq 0
. Let
t=\dim_{\Bbbk}(S/I)_{d}
.
Then,
\bigwedge^{t}S_{d}
is a
\Bbbk
-vector space with a basis of

\{m_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge m_{t}\mid m_{1}\succeq\ldots\succeq m_{t}\text{ are monomials in }S_{d}\}

for a fixed monomial order
\preceq
. We will say that
m=m_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge m_{t}
is a

standard exterior monomial
if
m_{1}\succeq\ldots\succeq m_{t}
.
We order standard exterior monomials lexicographically. That is,

m=m_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge m_{t}\geq m^{\prime}=m_{1}^{\prime}\wedge\ldots\wedge m_{t}^{\prime}
if and only if

m_{i}\succeq m_{i}^{\prime}
for the smallest
i
such that
m_{i}\neq m_{i}^{\prime}
.
If
h_{1},\ldots,h_{t}
are a basis of
I_{d}
,
\bigwedge^{t}I_{d}
is a
1
-dimensional
\Bbbk
-subspace of
\bigwedge^{t}S_{d}
generated by

h=h_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge h_{t}
.
We can expand
h
and rewrite it as a linear combination of standard exterior monomials, allowing us to determine the largest
standard exterior monomial of
h
with nonzero coefficients. We also refer to the product of this coefficient and the largest
standard exterior monomial of
h
as the
initial term
of
h
, denoted by
\ini(h)
. The standard exterior monomial
of
\ini(h)
consists of monomials that form a basis of
\ini_{\preceq}(I)_{d}
. Using these notions, we reconstruct discussions
about generic ideals.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
11
, Theorem 1]
)
.

Let
\Bbbk
be an infinite field and fix positive integers
n,s,d_{1},\ldots,d_{s}
.
We correspond a point
{\bf a}\in\mathbb{A}_{\Bbbk}^{N}
to an ideal
I=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{s})
. Then, there is a Zariski open dense set
U

such that, for all
{\bf a}\in U

\HS(S/I)

Proof.

Let

\displaystyle F_{1}

\displaystyle=t_{1,1}x_{1}^{d_{1}}+t_{1,2}x_{1}^{d_{1}-1}x_{2}+\cdots+t_{1,r_{1}}x_{n}^{d_{1}}

\displaystyle F_{s}

\displaystyle=t_{s,1}x_{1}^{d_{s}}+t_{s,2}x_{1}^{d_{s}-1}x_{2}+\cdots+t_{s,r_{s}}x_{n}^{d_{s}}

be polynomials in
\Bbbk[{\overline{t}}][{\overline{x}}]
. For each degree

,
we want to choose
{\bf a}
such that
\dim_{\Bbbk}I_{d}
is as large as possible.
For any
{\bf a}\in\mathbb{A}_{\Bbbk}^{N}
and

is generated by

\mathcal{A}_{d}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{s}\{\tau f_{i}\mid\tau\text{ are monomials of degree }d-\deg(f_{i})\geq 0\}

Let

Finally, we prove

(
[

]
)

We use the same notation as in Theorem

We fix positive integers

Then, there is a Zariski open dense set

We use the same notation as in the proof of Theorem

For each degree

Therefore, we have the following equation:

(
\star
)

m^{(d)}=\max_{{\bf a}\in U_{d}}\{m=m_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge m_{t_{d}}\mid m\text{ is a basis of }\bigwedge^{t_{d}}\ini_{\preceq}(I)_{d}\}.

Let

be the set of points in

that do not vanish on the coefficient of some

Let

We next show that

We denote

The general linear group acts on the polynomial ring as follows.
For

We fix positive integers

and a monomial order

.
Then, the initial ideal of

Proof.

We use the same notation as in the proof of Theorem

Let

For

\gamma

We know that

3.
the initial ideal of generic ideals with respect to the lexicographic order

In this section, we consider the initial ideal of
U
-generic ideals with respect to the lexicographic order
and the relationship between them and lexsegment ideals.
We introduce the definition of lexsegment ideals
and Macaulay’s Theorem.
For more details on lexsegment ideals and Macaulay’s Theorem, see
[
16
, Section 6.3]

or
[
21
, Section 2.4]
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let the monomial order
\preceq
be the lexicographic order.
A monomial ideal
J
is called the
lexsegment ideal
if,
for all monomials
m\in J
, any monomial
m^{\prime}
satisfying

\deg(m)=\deg(m^{\prime})
and
m\preceq m^{\prime}
also belongs to
J
.

Theorem 3.2

(Macaulay’s Theorem)
.

For any homogeneous ideal
I
, there uniquely exists a lexsegment ideal
J
such that
\HS(S/I;t)=\HS(S/J;t)
.
Moreover, for any
d\geq 0
,
J
has at least as many generators in degree
d
as any other monomial
ideal with the same Hilbert series.

Definition 3.3
.

Given a Hilbert series
\HS(S/I;t)
for a homogeneous ideal
I
,
we define the lexsegment ideal
J
induced by Macaulay’s Theorem
(i.e., satisfying
\HS(S/J;t)=\HS(S/I;t)
)
as
the lexsegment ideal of
\HS(S/I;t)
.

We define
\maxGBdeg_{\preceq}(I)
as the maximal degree of polynomials in the reduced Gröbner basis
and
\maxdeg(I)
as the maximal degree of minimal generators of
I
.
We can bound
\maxGBdeg_{\preceq}(I)
with the lexsegment ideals of the same Hilbert series

\HS(S/I;t)
.

Corollary 3.4
.

For any homogeneous ideal
I
, let
J
be the lexsegment ideal of

\HS(S/I;t)
. Then, we have
\maxGBdeg_{\preceq}(I)\leq\maxdeg(J)
for any monomial order
\preceq
.

Proof.

Fix a monomial order
\preceq
. By the definition of reduced Gröbner basis,
we have
\maxGBdeg_{\preceq}(I)=\maxdeg(\ini_{\preceq}(I))
.
It is well known that
\HS(S/I;t)=\HS(S/\ini_{\preceq}(I);t)
for
any homogeneous ideal
I
(see, e.g.,
[
9
, Theorem 15.26]
).
Therefore, by Macaulay’s Theorem, we have

\maxGBdeg_{\preceq}(I)=\maxdeg(\ini_{\preceq}(I))\leq\maxdeg(J).

∎

We check whether the bound in Corollary

is optimal. If there exist an ideal

such that

is the lexsegment ideal of

We fix the lexsegment ideal and compare homogeneous ideals

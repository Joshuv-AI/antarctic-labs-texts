The Ladder Technique - Quantum Groups
Matthew Gillespie

Abstract
Given a regular C ∗ \mathrm{C}^{*} -algebraic locally compact quantum group ( S r , Δ ) (S_{r},\Delta) with universal quantum group ( S f , Δ f ) (S_{f},\Delta_{f}) , a C ∗ \mathrm{C}^{*} -algebra A A , and a sufficiently well-behaved full coaction S f ​ ↷ 𝛼 ​ A S_{f}\overset{\alpha}{\curvearrowright}A , we construct natural lattice isomorphisms from the strongly coaction invariant ideals of A A to the strongly coaction invariant ideals of full and reduced crossed product C ∗ \mathrm{C}^{*} -algebras as an application of the ‘ladder technique’ developed by the author, S. Kaliszewski, John Quigg and Dana P. Williams. In particular, these lattice isomorphisms are determined by either the maximality or normality of the coaction α \alpha . This result directly generalizes a recent theorem proven by the aforementioned authors for locally compact groups, which in turn generalized a theorem of Elliot Gootman and Aldo Lazar for amenable groups.

†

Keywords: coaction, crossed product duality, ideal, Rieffel correspondence, locally compact quantum group

Introduction

Given a
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-dynamical system
(A,G,\alpha)
or cosystem
(A,G,\delta)
, one can form the crossed product
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras

\displaystyle A\rtimes_{\alpha}G\>\>\text{and}\>\>A\rtimes_{\delta}G

which are the universal objects for covariant representations of the respective systems
[
4

. For actions specifically, we can also define a reduced crossed product
A\rtimes_{\alpha,r}G
via quotienting
A\rtimes_{\alpha}G
by the kernel of the regular representation. Since these
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras are inherently tied to their original dynamical systems, it is of much interest to understand how the ideal structure of the crossed product
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras

A\rtimes_{\alpha}G,A\rtimes_{\alpha,r}G
and
A\rtimes_{\delta}G
relate back to the ideals of the original
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra
A
which are invariant in some sense under the (co)action. Gootman and Lazar resolved this question for amenable groups
G
in
[
8
]
(with reduced coactions), and later Nilsen in
[
12
]
answered this for arbitrary locally compact
G
for actions, but under a different form of invariance for (full) coactions. Recently, this was proven again for arbitrary locally compact
G
except under strong coaction invariance, see
[
7
, Theorem 3.2]
. This notion of invariance was used in
[
8
]
, and is equivalent to the invariance used in
[
12
]
when
G
is amenable (the relationship in general has also been worked out in an upcoming paper by the first three authors of
[
7
]
). The main result of this paper directly extends the result presented in
[
7
]
for appropriately defined Kac systems coming from a regular
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebraic locally compact quantum group
\mathcal{G}
, which we will call a LCQG from now on. This is accomplished via applying ‘the ladder technique’ developed in
[
7
]
. This technique amounted to a basic observation of commuting diagrams involving bijections (see
[
7
, Lemma 3.1]
) applied to iterated crossed products of a fixed
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-dynamical (co)system
(A,G,\alpha)
and
(A,G,\delta)
, where the bijections needed for
[
7
, Lemma 3.1]
came from appropriately restricting the lattice isomorphisms gotten via the Rieffel correspondence. Much of the machinery presented here is very similar in nature to that presented in
[
7
]
, and this is by design. The main purpose of this paper is to highlight another application of the main techniques in
[
7
]
. We begin in section
2
with some quick notational conventions and identifications. In section
3
, we give a basic overview of Hopf
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras, weak Kac systems with full duality, multiplicative unitaries, LCQGs, and correspondence coactions in line with
[
4

. In section
4

5

Preliminaries

3.1
Hilbert Modules & correspondences

Hilbert modules and correspondences are an indispensable tool for the study of Morita equivalence and the theory of crossed product
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras. Several arguments throughout this work will use results found in
[
4
]
. For the sake of self-containment, we opt to include a brief background on these objects here.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
A,B
be
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras.

(a)

A
right-Hilbert
B
-module
is a vector space
\mathsf{X}_{B}
with a positive definite sesquilinear form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{B}:\mathsf{X}_{B}\times\mathsf{X}_{B}\longrightarrow B
such that

1.

\langle x,y\cdot b\rangle_{B}=\langle x,y\rangle_{B}b
for all
x,y\in\mathsf{X}_{B}
and
b\in B
.

2.

\langle x,y\rangle_{B}^{*}=\langle y,x\rangle_{B}
for all
x,y\in\mathsf{X}_{B}
and
b\in B
.

3.

\mathsf{X}_{B}
is a Banach space in the norm
\lVert x\rVert\coloneq\lVert\langle x,x\rangle_{B}\rVert^{\frac{1}{2}}
.

We say that
\mathsf{X}_{B}
is
full
if
\overline{\text{span}}\left\{\langle\mathsf{X},\mathsf{X}\rangle_{B}\right\}=B
.

(b)

An
(A,B)
-correspondence
is a right-Hilbert
B
-module
{}_{A}\mathsf{X}_{B}
which is a non-degenerate left
A
-module (
A\cdot\mathsf{X}=\mathsf{X}
) satisfying

1.

a\cdot(x\cdot b)=(a\cdot x)\cdot b
for all
x\in\>_{A}\mathsf{X}_{B}
,
a\in A
and
b\in B
.

2.

\langle a\cdot x,y\rangle_{B}=\langle x,a^{*}\cdot y\rangle_{B}
for all
x,y\in\>_{A}\mathsf{X}_{B}
and
a\in A
.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
A,B
be
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras.

(a)

An
(A,B)
-imprimitivity bimodule
is an
(A,B)
-correspondence
\mathsf{X}
with left
A
-valued inner product satisfying
{}_{A}\langle x,y\rangle\cdot z=x\cdot\langle y,z\rangle_{B}
for all
x,y,z\in\mathsf{X}
such that
\mathsf{X}
is full and
\overline{\text{span}}\left\{{}_{A}\langle\mathsf{X},\mathsf{X}\rangle\right\}=A
.

(b)

A,B
are called
Morita equivalent
if there exists an
(A,B)
-imprimitivity bimodule
{}_{A}\mathsf{X}_{B}
.

Theorem 3.3

(
[
13
, Proposition 3.24]
)
.

If
A,B
are Morita equivalent with imprimitivity bimodule
{}_{A}\mathsf{X}_{B}
, there is a lattice isomorphism
\mathsf{X}-\text{Ind}^{A}_{B}:\mathscr{I}(B)\longrightarrow\mathscr{I}(A)
by

\mathsf{X}-\text{Ind}^{A}_{B}(J)=\overline{\text{span}}\left\{\>{}_{A}\langle x\cdot b,y\rangle\>|\>x,y\in\mathsf{X},b\in J\right\}

where
\mathscr{I}(A),\mathscr{I}(B)
are the lattices of ideals of
A,B
. The lattice isomorphism is called the Rieffel correspondence associated to
\mathsf{X}
.

Note that Rieffel’s theorem actually contains much more detail than the above statement, but this will suffice for us. We now define the notion of a morphism between correspondences.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
{}_{A}\mathsf{X}_{B}
and
{}_{C}\mathsf{Y}_{D}
be correspondences for
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras
A,B,C,D
. Moreover, let
\varphi:A\longrightarrow\mathrm{M}(C)
and
\psi:B\longrightarrow\mathrm{M}(D)
be
*
-homomorphisms with
\Phi:\mathsf{X}\longrightarrow\mathrm{M}(\mathsf{Y})
a linear map. We call
\Phi
a
\varphi-\psi
compatible correspondence homomorphism
provided that

(a)

\Phi(a\cdot x)=\varphi(a)\cdot\Phi(x)
for all
a\in A
and
x\in\mathsf{X}
.

(b)

\Phi(x\cdot b)=\Phi(x)\cdot\psi(b)
for all
b\in B
and
x\in\mathsf{X}
.

(c)

\psi(\langle x,z\rangle_{B})=\langle\Phi(x),\Phi(z)\rangle_{M(D)}
for all
x,z\in\mathsf{X}
.

The maps
\varphi,\psi
are called the
coefficient maps
of
\Phi
. Formally this is denoted
{}_{\varphi}\Phi_{\psi}:_{A}\mathsf{X}_{B}\longrightarrow\mathrm{M}(_{C}\mathsf{Y}_{D})
. We say that
\Phi
is
nondegenerate
if
\overline{\text{span}}\{\Phi(\mathsf{X})D\}=\Phi(\mathsf{X})\cdot D=\mathsf{Y}
and both
\varphi,\psi
are nondegenerate.

We refer the reader to
[
4
, Chapter 1]
for a detailed treatment of correspondences (what they call right-Hilbert bimodules) along with their theory of balanced and exterior tensor products.

3.2
Hopf
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-Algebras

Hopf
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras were largely developed by Iorio, Vaes and Van Daele in
[
15
,
3
]
to create an abstract framework for studying LCQGs and noncommutative duality. Their definition (which differs throughout the literature) assumes less than what is imposed for a LCQG (see Definition
3.12
), so we begin here and gradually build up towards LCQGs through multiplicative unitaries via Kac systems. Many of these concepts are pulled directly from
[
6
]
; however, since Fischer’s work isn’t easily located in the literature, we transfer much of the exposition found there.

Definition 3.5
.

A
Hopf
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra
is a pair
(\mathfrak{H},\Delta)
consisting of

(a)

A
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra
\mathfrak{H}
,

(b)

A non-degenerate faithful
*
-homomorphism
\Delta:\mathfrak{H}\longrightarrow\mathrm{M}(\mathfrak{H}\otimes\mathfrak{H})
called a
comultiplcation
such that

\displaystyle(\Delta\otimes\text{id}_{\mathfrak{H}})\circ\Delta

\displaystyle=(\text{id}_{\mathfrak{H}}\otimes\Delta)\circ\Delta,\>\text{and}

\displaystyle\overline{\text{span}}\left\{\Delta(\mathfrak{H})(1_{\mathrm{M}(\mathfrak{H})}\otimes\mathfrak{H})\right\}

\displaystyle=\overline{\text{span}}\left\{(1_{\mathrm{M}(\mathfrak{H})}\otimes\mathfrak{H})\Delta(\mathfrak{H})\right\}=\mathfrak{H}\otimes\mathfrak{H}.

Moreover, we call a
*
-homomorphism
\epsilon:\mathfrak{H}\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}
satisfying

\displaystyle(\epsilon\otimes\text{id}_{\mathfrak{H}})\circ\Delta=(\text{id}_{\mathfrak{H}}\otimes\epsilon)\circ\Delta=\text{id}_{\mathfrak{H}}

a
counit
.

One also defines what is called an
antipode
for Hopf
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras, but we have no real use for it here. We remark that many assume additional structure on Hopf
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras involving the Haagerup tensor product and no assumption on the faithfulness of
\Delta
such as in
[
3
, Definition 2.4]
. Regardless, there are three canonical examples of Hopf
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras.

Example 1
.

Let
G
be a locally compact group.

1.

C_{0}(G)
is a Hopf
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra with comultiplcation
\Delta:C_{0}(G)\longrightarrow\mathrm{M}(C_{0}(G)\otimes C_{0}(G))\cong C_{b}(G\times G)
given by
\Delta(f)=f\circ m
, where
m:G\times G\longrightarrow G
is the group multiplication.

2.

\mathrm{C}^{*}_{r}(G)\coloneq\lambda(\mathrm{C}^{*}(G))
is a Hopf
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra where
\lambda
is the integrated form of the left regular representation:

\displaystyle\lambda(f)=\int_{G}f(s)\lambda(s)\>ds,\>f\in C_{c}(G).

The comultiplcation is given by
\Delta\circ\lambda=\lambda\otimes\lambda
.

3.

\mathrm{C}^{*}(G)
with comultiplcation
\delta_{G}
is a Hopf
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra, which is the integrated form of the unitary representation
u\otimes u:s\mapsto u(s)\otimes u(s)
where
u:G\longrightarrow\mathrm{M}(\mathrm{C}^{*}(G))
is the canonical map
[
4
, Remark A.8]
.

Comultiplcations are examples of a broader concept known as
coactions
. A suitable background for coactions of groups can be found in
[
4
, Section A.3]
. Just as in the group case, coactions of Hopf
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras will be used extensively to define crossed products.

Definition 3.6
.

A
coaction
of
(\mathfrak{H},\Delta)
on a
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra
A
is a
*
-homomorphism
\alpha:A\longrightarrow\mathrm{M}(A\otimes\mathfrak{H})
such that

(a)

(\alpha\otimes\text{id}_{\mathfrak{H}})\circ\alpha=(\text{id}_{A}\otimes\Delta)\circ\alpha
(the
coaction identity
), and

(b)

\alpha(A)(1_{\mathrm{M}(A)}\otimes\mathfrak{H})\subset A\otimes\mathfrak{H}
.

Moreover, we say that
\alpha
is
continuous
provided that

\displaystyle\overline{\text{span}}\left\{\alpha(A)(1_{\mathrm{M}(A)}\otimes\mathfrak{H})\right\}=\overline{\text{span}}\left\{(1_{\mathrm{M}(A)}\otimes\mathfrak{H})\alpha(A)\right\}=A\otimes\mathfrak{H}.

Throughout, we will always assume that our coactions are faithful and continuous unless specified otherwise. We use the term ‘continuous’ to avoid re-using the term non-degenerate (as a coaction). Note that continuity of a coaction implies its range is contained in the relative
\mathfrak{H}
-multiplier algebra
\mathrm{M}_{\mathfrak{H}}(A\otimes\mathfrak{H})
, where

\displaystyle\mathrm{M}_{\mathfrak{H}}(A\otimes\mathfrak{H})\coloneq\left\{m\in\mathrm{M}(A\otimes\mathfrak{H})\>|\>m(1_{\mathrm{M}(A)}\otimes\mathfrak{H})\cup(1_{\mathrm{M}(A)\otimes\mathfrak{H}})m\subset A\otimes\mathfrak{H}\right\}.

For details of this construction, see
[
4
, Section A.1]
. Similar to
[
6
]
, we will call coactions of the Hopf
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra
\mathfrak{H}
on a
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra
A

\mathfrak{H}
-coactions
, and indicate this with the pair
(A,\alpha)
when the choice of
\mathfrak{H}
is unambiguous. Analogously to the classical theory of crossed product
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras, one must discuss the appropriate notion of covariance of representations of the systems
(A,\mathfrak{H},\alpha)
.

Definition 3.7
.

Let
(A,\alpha)
be an
\mathfrak{H}
-coaction and
B
a
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra.

(a)

A right
unitary corepresentation
of
(\mathfrak{H},\Delta)
on
B
is a unitary multiplier
u\in\mathrm{M}(B\otimes\mathfrak{H})
such that
(\text{id}_{B}\otimes\Delta)(u)=u_{12}u_{13}
.

(b)

A left
unitary corepresentation
of
(\mathfrak{H},\Delta)
on
B
is a unitary multiplier
u\in\mathrm{M}(\mathfrak{H}\otimes B)
such that
(\Delta\otimes\text{id}_{B})(u)=u_{13}u_{23}
.

(c)

A
(right) covariant homomorphism
of
(\mathfrak{H},\Delta)
from
(A,\alpha)
to
B
is a pair
(\pi,u)
where
\pi:A\longrightarrow\mathrm{M}(B)
is a (possibly degenerate) representation and
u\in\mathrm{M}(B\otimes\mathfrak{H})
is a (right) unitary corepresentation such that

\displaystyle(\pi\otimes\text{id}_{\mathfrak{H}})\circ\alpha(\cdot)=\text{Ad}(u)\circ(\pi(\cdot)\otimes 1_{\mathrm{M}\left(\mathfrak{H}\right)}).

(d)

A
(left) covariant homomorphism
of
(\mathfrak{H},\Delta)
from
(A,\alpha)
to
B
is a pair
(\pi,u)
where
\pi:A\longrightarrow\mathrm{M}(B)
is a (possibly degenerate) representation and
u\in\mathrm{M}(\mathfrak{H}\otimes B)
is a (left) unitary corepresentation such that

\displaystyle(\pi\otimes\text{id}_{\mathfrak{H}})\circ\alpha(\cdot)=\text{Ad}(\sigma(u))\circ(\pi(\cdot)\otimes 1_{\mathrm{M}\left(\mathfrak{H}\right)}).

Note the above definition still makes sense for degenerate homomorphisms
\pi
, as continuity of
\alpha
implies
\alpha(A)\subset\mathrm{M}_{\mathfrak{H}}(A\otimes\mathfrak{H})
and
\pi\otimes\text{id}_{\mathfrak{H}}
extends to the relative
\mathfrak{H}
-multipliers by
[
4
, Proposition A.6]
. That being said, we will assume our covariant homomorphisms are nondegenerate unless specified otherwise. Finally, we note that we will sometimes refer to right unitary corepresentations as simply ‘corepresentations’ and will write ‘left’ whenever dealing with left corepresentations. We do the same when referring to covariance.

Definition 3.8
.

Let
(A,\alpha)
be an
\mathfrak{H}
-coaction of
(\mathfrak{H},\Delta)
.

(a)

A
crossed product
is a triple
(A\rtimes_{\alpha}\widehat{\mathfrak{H}},j^{\alpha}_{A},\mathfrak{u}^{\alpha})
consisting of a
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra
A\rtimes_{\alpha}\widehat{\mathfrak{H}}
, a covariant homomorphism
(j^{\alpha}_{A},\mathfrak{u}^{\alpha})
where
j^{\alpha}_{A}:A\longrightarrow\mathrm{M}\left(A\rtimes_{\alpha}\widehat{\mathfrak{H}}\right)
and
\mathfrak{u}^{\alpha}\in\mathrm{M}\left(A\rtimes_{\alpha}\widehat{\mathfrak{H}}\otimes\mathfrak{H}\right)
satisfying the following universal property: For all covariant homomorphisms
(\pi,u):(A,\alpha)\longrightarrow\mathrm{M}(B)
of
(A,\alpha)
on
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras
B
, there exists a unique non-degenerate
*
-homomorphism
\pi\rtimes u:A\rtimes_{\alpha}\widehat{\mathfrak{H}}\longrightarrow\mathrm{M}(B)
such that

\displaystyle(\pi\rtimes u)\circ j^{\alpha}_{A}

\displaystyle=\pi,\>\text{and}

\displaystyle((\pi\rtimes u)\otimes\text{id}_{\mathfrak{H}})(\mathfrak{u}^{\alpha})

\displaystyle=u.

(b)

A
universal dual
of
(\mathfrak{H},\Delta)
is a crossed product
(\widehat{\mathfrak{H}},\iota,\mathfrak{u})
for the trivial coaction
\mathbb{C}\longrightarrow\mathrm{M}(\mathbb{C}\otimes\mathfrak{H})
by
z\mapsto z\otimes 1_{\mathrm{M}(\mathfrak{H})}
. Note here that
\iota:\mathbb{C}\longrightarrow\mathrm{M}\left(\widehat{\mathfrak{H}}\right)
is just the embedding of scalars and so we usually exclude it in the notation.

(c)

A
full universal dual
is a universal dual
\widehat{\mathfrak{H}}
of
\mathfrak{H}
such that there additionally exists a closed subspace
\mathfrak{F}\subset\mathfrak{H}^{*}
such that

(i)

For all
\psi\in\mathfrak{F}
and
y\in\mathfrak{H}
, the functionals
\psi\cdot y,y\cdot\psi\in\mathfrak{F}
where
\psi\cdot y:x\mapsto\psi(yx)
and
y\cdot\psi:x\mapsto\psi(xy)
(
\mathfrak{H}
-invariance).

(ii)

The collection
(\text{id}_{\widehat{\mathfrak{H}}}\otimes\mathfrak{F})(\mathfrak{u})\coloneq\left\{(\text{id}_{\widehat{\mathfrak{H}}}\otimes\psi)(\mathfrak{u})\>|\>\psi\in\mathfrak{F}\right\}\subset\widehat{\mathfrak{H}}
is dense.

If
(\pi,u)
is a (possibly degenerate) covariant homomorphism of
(A,\alpha)
on a
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra
B
, we have that
(\iota,u)
is covariant for the trivial coaction on
\mathbb{C}
and thus there is a unique (non-degenerate)
*
-homomorphism
\mu_{u}\coloneq\iota\rtimes u:\widehat{\mathfrak{H}}\longrightarrow\mathrm{M}(B)
such that
(\mu_{u}\otimes\text{id}_{\mathfrak{H}})(\mathfrak{u})=u
. Thus, covariant homomorphisms
(\pi,u)
(degenerate or not) may be described as a pair (also called a covariant homomorphism)
(\pi,\mu_{u}):\left((A,\alpha),\widehat{\mathfrak{H}}\right)\longrightarrow\mathrm{M}(B)
such that

\displaystyle(\pi\otimes\text{id}_{\mathfrak{H}})\circ\alpha=\text{Ad}(\mu_{u}\otimes\text{id}_{\mathfrak{H}})(\mathfrak{u})(\pi\otimes 1_{\mathrm{M}(\mathfrak{H})}).

All LCQGs are Hopf
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras that (amongst other things) admit a universal dual in this sense, and so we will only concern ourselves with Hopf
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras which admit a universal dual. We also note the universal property of
\left(A\rtimes_{\alpha}\widehat{\mathfrak{H}},j^{\alpha}_{A},j^{A}\right)
states that there is a bijective correspondence between covariant homomorphisms
(\pi,\mu)
of
(A,\alpha)
and non-degenerate
*
-homomorphisms of
A\rtimes_{\alpha}\widehat{\mathfrak{H}}
, where
j^{A}\coloneq\mu_{\mathfrak{u}^{\alpha}}
. Finally note that one can flip the unitary
\mathfrak{u}^{\alpha}
so that the crossed product
\left(A\rtimes_{\alpha}\widehat{\mathfrak{H}},j_{A}^{\alpha},\sigma(\mathfrak{u}^{\alpha})\right)
is universal for left covariant homomorphisms.

Example 2
.

Let
G
be a locally compact group with
\tilde{\alpha}:G\longrightarrow\text{Aut}(A)
be a continuous action on a
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra
A
, and
\delta:A\longrightarrow\mathrm{M}(A\otimes\mathrm{C}^{*}(G))
a coaction of
G
on
A
.

(a)

For
\mathfrak{H}=C_{0}(G)
, observe that a right corepresentation
u\in\mathrm{M}(B\otimes C_{0}(G))\cong C_{b}(G,M^{\beta}(B))
is a strictly continuous unitary valued function satisfying
(\text{id}_{B}\otimes\Delta)(u)=u_{12}u_{13}
. In particular it’s easy to see that

A\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathrm{C}^{*}(G)\cong A\rtimes_{\alpha}G.

Notice also that
\widehat{\mathfrak{H}}=\mathrm{C}^{*}(G)
since unitary corepresentations of
C_{0}(G)
correspond to unitary representations of
G
, which correspond to
*
-homomorphisms of
\mathrm{C}^{*}(G)
by the universal property.

(b)

Now let
\mathfrak{H}=\mathrm{C}^{*}(G)
. First, observe that the coaction
\delta:A\longrightarrow\mathrm{M}(A\otimes\mathrm{C}^{*}(G))
of
G
on
A
is exactly a coaction of the Hopf
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra
\mathrm{C}^{*}(G)
on
A
by our definitions. One also checks that

A\rtimes_{\delta}C_{0}(G)\cong A\rtimes_{\delta}G

canonically. Dual to the situation in (a), we have
\widehat{\mathfrak{H}}=C_{0}(G)
since unitary corepresentations of
\mathrm{C}^{*}(G)
correspond to
*
-homomorphisms of
C_{0}(G)
through the canonical unitary
w_{G}
, see
[
4
, Section A.3]
.

These two examples encapsulate the classical theory of crossed products.

We have defined the crossed product, but have yet to introduce a condition on the Hopf
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra to guarantee the existence of a crossed product in general. It turns out that the existence of a full universal dual is a sufficient condition
[
6
, Theorem 1.10]
. We are now in a position to discuss LCQGs and how they arise.

.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a Hilbert space,
B

\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra,
\mathsf{E}_{B}
a right Hilbert
B
-module and

(a)

We say that

\displaystyle V_{12}V_{13}V_{23}=V_{23}V_{12}.

(b)

A unitary
U\in\mathcal{L}_{B}(\mathsf{E}\otimes_{\text{ext}}\mathcal{H})
is called a
right corepresentation of
V
provided that
U_{12}U_{13}V_{23}=V_{23}U_{12}
and we denote the subspace

\displaystyle\widehat{S}_{U}\coloneq\overline{\text{span}}\left\{(\text{id}_{\mathsf{E}}\otimes\omega)(U)\>|\>\omega\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})_{*}\right\}\subset\mathcal{L}_{B}(\mathsf{E}).

(c)

A unitary
W\in\mathcal{L}_{B}(\mathcal{H}\otimes_{\text{ext}}\mathsf{E})
is called a
left corepresentation of
V
provided that
V_{12}W_{13}W_{23}=W_{23}V_{12}
and we denote the subspace

\displaystyle S_{W}\coloneq\overline{\text{span}}\left\{(\omega\otimes\text{id}_{\mathsf{E}})(W)\>|\>\omega\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})_{*}\right\}\subset\mathcal{L}_{B}(\mathsf{E}).

Note that a multiplicative unitary is clearly a right and left corepresentation of itself, and so we have the subspaces
S_{V},\widehat{S}_{V}
of
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
. By adding additional assumptions to the unitary
V
, we can ensure that
S_{V},\widehat{S}_{V}
are
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras with comultiplcations
\Delta,\widehat{\Delta}
respectively.

Lemma 3.10

(
[
1
,
6
, Theorem 3.8]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a Hilbert space,
B
a
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra,
\mathsf{E}_{B}
a right Hilbert
B
-module,
V\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathcal{H})
a multiplicative unitary with
U\in\mathcal{L}_{B}(\mathsf{E}\otimes_{\text{ext}}\mathcal{H})
,
W\in\mathcal{L}_{B}(\mathcal{H}\otimes_{\text{ext}}\mathsf{E})
right and left corepresentations of
V
respectively. Moreover assume that
\widehat{S}_{U},S_{W}
are
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-subalgebras of
\mathcal{L}_{B}(\mathsf{E})
such that
U\in\mathrm{M}\left(\widehat{S}_{U}\otimes S_{V}\right)
and
W\in\mathrm{M}\left(\widehat{S}_{V}\otimes S_{W}\right)
for all such
\mathsf{E},U,W
. Then there are comultiplcations
\Delta:S_{V}\longrightarrow\mathrm{M}\left(S_{V}\otimes S_{V}\right)
and
\widehat{\Delta}:\widehat{S}_{V}\longrightarrow\mathrm{M}\left(\widehat{S}_{V}\otimes\widehat{S}_{V}\right)
given by

\displaystyle\Delta(x)

\displaystyle\coloneq V(x\otimes 1_{\mathrm{M}(S_{V})})V^{*},\>\text{and}

\displaystyle\widehat{\Delta}(y)

\displaystyle\coloneq V^{*}\left(1_{\mathrm{M}\left(\widehat{S}_{V}\right)}\otimes y\right)V.

In particular,
(S_{V},\Delta)
and
\left(\widehat{S}_{V},\widehat{\Delta}\right)
are Hopf-
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras and in this case we denote
(S_{r},\Delta)\coloneq(S_{V},\Delta)
and
\left(\widehat{S}_{r},\widehat{\Delta}\right)\coloneq\left(\widehat{S}_{V},\widehat{\Delta}\right)
respectively.

Moreover, one can construct a full universal dual
\left(\left(\widehat{S}_{f},\widehat{\Delta}_{f}\right),\mathfrak{u}\right)
of
(S_{r},\Delta)
, namely by taking
\mathfrak{F}=\left\{\omega|_{S_{r}}\>|\>\omega\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})_{*}\right\}
. Thus given a right corepresentation
U\in\mathrm{M}\left(\widehat{S}_{U}\otimes S_{r}\right)
of
S_{r}
, the induced
*
-homomorphism
\mu_{U}:\widehat{S}_{f}\longrightarrow\widehat{S}_{U}
surjects onto
\widehat{S}_{U}
. In particular we have a surjective
*
-homomorphism
\widehat{\pi}_{f}\coloneq\mu_{V}:\widehat{S}_{f}\longrightarrow\widehat{S}_{r}
called the
canonical surjection of
\widehat{S}_{f}
. Analogously there is a full universal dual
((S_{f},\Delta_{f}),\widehat{\mathfrak{u}})
of
\left(\widehat{S}_{r},\widehat{\Delta}\right)
with
canonical surjection of
S_{f}
denoted
\pi_{f}:S_{f}\longrightarrow S_{r}
. Notably, what we call left corepresentations of
\widehat{S}_{r}
are referred to simply as ‘corepresentations’ in
[
6
]
. In particular, since we want
\widehat{\mathfrak{u}}
to implement a bijective correspondence between left corepresentations of
\widehat{S}_{r}
(since these correspond to the left corepresentations of
V
) and non-degenerate
*
-homomorphisms of
S_{f}
, we take
\widehat{\mathfrak{u}}
to be the flip of the unitary one gets from Definition
3.8
.(b). We now introduce what are known as
weak Kac systems with full duality
. These are a special subclass of the more general
weak Kac systems
, see
[
14
, Section 2.2]
for details.

Definition 3.11

(
[
6
, Definition 2.1]
)
.

A
weak Kac system with full duality
is a triple
(\mathcal{H},V,U)
consisting of a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, a multiplicative unitary
V\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathcal{H})
and a self-adjoint unitary
U\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
such that

(a)

The hypothesis of Lemma
3.10
holds for all
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras
B
, Hilbert
B
-modules
\mathsf{E}_{B}
and left, right corepresentations of
V
on
\mathsf{E}_{B}
.

(b)

There exists a unitary
\mathfrak{v}\in\mathrm{M}\left(\widehat{S}_{f}\otimes S_{f}\right)
such that
\mathfrak{v}
is a right corepresentation of
S_{f}
and a left corepresentation of
\widehat{S}_{f}
where
\left(\left(\widehat{S}_{f},\widehat{\Delta}_{f}\right),\mathfrak{v}\right)
and
\left((S_{f},\Delta_{f}),\mathfrak{v}\right)
are universal duals of
(S_{f},\Delta_{f})
and
\left(\widehat{S}_{f},\widehat{\Delta}_{f}\right)
respectively.

(c)

The unitary
\mathfrak{v}
is such that
\left(\pi_{f},\widehat{\pi}_{f}\right):\left((S_{f},\Delta_{f}),\widehat{S}_{f}\right)\longrightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
is covariant.

(d)

The unitary
U
is such that
\left(\widehat{\pi}_{f},\text{Ad}(U)\circ\pi_{f}\right):\left(\left(\widehat{S}_{f},\widehat{\Delta}_{f}\right),S_{f}\right)\longrightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
is covariant where the commutators
[\pi_{f}(x),U\pi_{f}(x^{\prime})U^{*}]=[\widehat{\pi}_{f}\allowbreak{(y)},

U\widehat{\pi}_{f}(y^{\prime})U^{*}]=0
for all
x,x^{\prime}\in S_{f}
and
y,y^{\prime}\in\widehat{S}_{f}
.

Moreover we say that
(\mathcal{H},V,U)
is
strongly regular
provided that
\widehat{\pi}_{f}\rtimes\text{Ad}(U)\circ\pi_{f}:\widehat{S}_{f}\rtimes_{\widehat{\Delta}_{f}}S_{f}\cong\mathcal{K}
. For weak Kac systems with full duality, we call

(a)

(S_{r},\Delta)
the
quantum group associated to
V
,

(b)

\left(\widehat{S}_{r},\widehat{\Delta}\right)
the
dual quantum group associated to
V
,

(c)

(S_{f},\Delta_{f})
the
universal quantum group associated to
V
, and

(d)

\left(\widehat{S}_{f},\widehat{\Delta}_{f}\right)
the
universal dual quantum group associated to
V
.

Note by ‘universal dual’ in condition (b), we mean in the sense that left corepresentations
u
of
\widehat{S}_{f}
are in bijection with non-degenerate
*
-homomorphisms of
S_{f}
via
\left(\text{id}_{\widehat{S}_{f}}\otimes\mu_{u}\right)(\mathfrak{v})=u
, and right corepresentations
w
of
S_{f}
are in bijection with non-degenerate
*
-homomorphisms of
\widehat{S}_{f}
via
\left(\mu_{w}\otimes\text{id}_{S_{f}}\right)(\mathfrak{v})=w
.

3.3
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebraic Locally Compact Quantum Groups

We define in the sense of Kustermans and Vaes
[
11
]
what we mean by LCQG.

Definition 3.12
.

Let
(\mathcal{G},\Delta)
be a Hopf
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra with faithful KMS-weights
\varphi,\psi
respectively satisfying

(a)

\varphi\left((\omega\otimes\text{id}_{\mathcal{G}})\circ\Delta(x)\right)=\omega\left(1_{M(\mathcal{G})}\right)\varphi(x)
for all
x\in\mathcal{M}_{\varphi}^{+}
and
\omega\in\mathcal{G}^{*}_{+}
(
left invariance
), and

(b)

\psi\left((\text{id}_{\mathcal{G}}\otimes\omega)\circ\Delta(x)\right)=\omega\left(1_{M(\mathcal{G})}\right)\psi(x)
for all
x\in\mathcal{M}_{\varphi}^{+}
and
\omega\in\mathcal{G}^{*}_{+}
(
right invariance
).

Then
\mathcal{G}
is called a (
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebraic reduced)
locally compact quantum group
(LCQG) and
\varphi,\psi
are called the left and right
Haar weights
respectively.

For a detailed background on these objects, see
[
2
,
10
, Section 2]
. We denote
L^{2}(\mathcal{G})
to be the Hilbert space associated to the left Haar weight
\varphi
, and regard
\mathcal{G}\subset\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(G))\cong\mathrm{M}(\mathcal{K})
as a non-degenerate
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-subalgebra of
\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(\mathcal{G}))\cong\mathrm{M}(\mathcal{K})
whenever convenient. Importantly, this construction is (one way to see) how LCQGs can be realized as Hopf
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras arising from multiplicative unitaries. Associated to a KMS weight
\varphi
is a unique anti-unitary operator
J
called the
modular conjugation of
\varphi
. A very detailed discussion of this operator can be found in
[
16
]
.

Lemma 3.13

(
[
6
, Section 2]
)
.

Let
(\mathcal{G},\Delta)
be a LCQG. Then
(L^{2}(\mathcal{G}),W,U)
is a weak Kac system with full duality, where
W
is the left corepresentation and
U=\widehat{J}J
for
J,\widehat{J}
the modular conjugations associated to the left Haar weight of
\mathcal{G}
and its dual
\widehat{\mathcal{G}}
respectively.

Thus given
(\mathcal{G},\Delta)
in the sense of Kustermans and Vaes, one has the weak Kac system with full duality
(L^{2}(\mathcal{G}),W,U)
. One then induces the quantum groups
(S_{r},\Delta),(S_{f},\Delta_{f}),(\widehat{S}_{r},\widehat{\Delta})
, and
(\widehat{S}_{f},\widehat{\Delta}_{f})
. In the Kusterman Vaes conventions, one also obtains similar objects
(\mathcal{G},\Delta),(\mathcal{G}_{u},\Delta_{u}),(\widehat{\mathcal{G}},\widehat{\Delta})
, and
(\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{u},\widehat{\Delta}_{u})
, see
[
18
]
. The correspondence between these is the following

\displaystyle(S_{r},\Delta)

\displaystyle=(\widehat{\mathcal{G}},\widehat{\Delta}^{\text{op}}),

\displaystyle(S_{f},\Delta_{f})

\displaystyle=(\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{u},\widehat{\Delta}_{u}^{\text{op}}),

\displaystyle(\widehat{S}_{r},\widehat{\Delta})

\displaystyle=(\mathcal{G},\Delta),

\displaystyle(\widehat{S}_{f},\widehat{\Delta}_{f})

\displaystyle=(\mathcal{G}_{u},\Delta_{u})

where
{}^{\text{op}}
denotes the flip of the comultiplcations. For more details on how to swap between these conventions, see
[
5
, Section 1.3.5]
. Since we wish to talk about crossed product duality for LCQGs, we mention what regularity is.

Definition 3.14
.

Let
(\mathcal{H},V,U)
be a weak Kac system with full duality. We say the Kac system
(\mathcal{H},V,U)
is
regular
provided that

\mathcal{C}(V)\coloneq\overline{\text{span}}\left\{(\text{id}\otimes\omega)(\Sigma V)\>|\>\omega\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})_{*}\right\}=\mathcal{K}

Note that when we say ‘
(\mathcal{H},V,U)
is a weak Kac system with full duality arising from a regular LCQG’, we will always mean that given a LCQG
\mathcal{G}
, we take
(\mathcal{H},V,U)=(L^{2}(\mathcal{G}),W,U)
for
W,U
defined above such that Definition
3.14
(a) holds. Moreover, by
[
6
, Remark 2.2]
regularity is equivalent to
\overline{\text{span}}\left\{\widehat{S}_{r}\cdot\text{Ad}(U)\circ S_{r}\right\}=\mathcal{K}
.

3.4
More Crossed Product Constructions

We are finally in a position to discuss crossed products in a detailed manner. For this, fix a weak Kac system with full duality
(\mathcal{H},V,U)
arising from a regular LCQG
\mathcal{G}
. We call a coaction
\alpha:A\longrightarrow\mathrm{M}(A\otimes S_{f})
of
S_{f}
a
full
S
-coaction
and a coaction
\beta:B\longrightarrow\mathrm{M}\left(B\otimes\widehat{S}_{f}\right)
of
\widehat{S}_{f}
a
full
\widehat{S}
-coaction
. We will always assume our coactions are continuous. Crossed products by a full
S
-coaction
(A,\alpha)
will be denoted
A\rtimes_{\alpha}\widehat{S}_{f}
and similarly for full
\widehat{S}
-coactions
(B,\beta)
by
B\rtimes_{\beta}S_{f}
. This reflects the fact that covariant homomorphisms of
((A,\alpha),S_{f})
are written as pairs
(\pi,\mu_{u}):\left(A,\widehat{S}_{f}\right)\longrightarrow\mathrm{M}(D)
where
u\in\mathrm{M}(D\otimes S_{f})
is the associated right corepresentation of
S_{f}
on
D
. Analogously for systems
\left((B,\beta),\widehat{S}_{f}\right)
we have pairs
(\pi,\mu_{u}):(B,S_{f})\longrightarrow\mathrm{M}(D)
where
u\in\mathrm{M}\left(\widehat{S}_{f}\otimes D\right)
is the associated left corepresentation. In particular, covariance of homomorphisms for full
S
-coactions
(\pi,\mu):\left((A,\alpha),\widehat{S}_{f}\right)\longrightarrow\mathrm{M}(D)
is then given as

\displaystyle(\pi\otimes\text{id}_{S_{f}})\circ\alpha(\cdot)=\text{Ad}(\mu\otimes\text{id}_{S_{f}})(\mathfrak{v})(\pi(\cdot)\otimes 1_{\mathrm{M}(S_{f})}).

Similarly, covariance of homomorphisms
(\nu,\eta):\left((B,\beta),S_{f}\right)\longrightarrow\mathrm{M}(D)
for full
\widehat{S}
-coactions is then

\displaystyle\left(\nu\otimes\text{id}_{\widehat{S}_{f}}\right)\circ\beta(\cdot)=\text{Ad}\left(\eta\otimes\text{id}_{\widehat{S}_{f}}\right)(\sigma(\mathfrak{v}))\left(\nu(\cdot)\otimes 1_{\mathrm{M}(\widehat{S}_{f})}\right).

This is the case since for these Kac systems since we know
S_{f}
and
\widehat{S}_{f}
admit the unique universal duals
\left(\widehat{S}_{f},\mathfrak{v}\right)
and
(S_{f},\mathfrak{v})
respectively. In particular, See the comments proceeding Definition
3.11
.

Definition 3.15
.

Let
(A,\alpha)
be a full
S
-coaction and
(B,\beta)
a full
\widehat{S}
-coaction.

1.

Define the maps
j^{\alpha}_{A,r}\coloneq(\text{id}_{A}\otimes\pi_{f})\circ\alpha
and
j^{A}_{r}\coloneq 1_{M(A)}\otimes\widehat{\pi}_{f}
. We call the map
\Lambda_{\alpha}\coloneq j^{\alpha}_{A,r}\rtimes j^{A}_{r}:A\rtimes_{\alpha}\widehat{S}_{f}\longrightarrow\mathrm{M}(A\otimes\mathcal{K})
the
S
-regular representation
. We define the
S
-reduced crossed product
to be
A\rtimes_{\alpha,r}\widehat{S}_{r}\coloneq\Lambda_{\alpha}\left(A\rtimes_{\alpha}\widehat{S}_{f}\right)

2.

Define the maps
\hat{\jmath}^{\>\beta}_{B,r}\coloneq(\text{id}_{B}\otimes\text{Ad}(U)\circ\widehat{\pi}_{f})\circ\beta
and
\hat{\jmath}^{\>B}_{r}\coloneq 1_{M(B)}\otimes\pi_{f}
. We call the map
\widehat{\Lambda}_{\beta}\coloneq\hat{\jmath}^{\>\beta}_{B,r}\rtimes\hat{\jmath}^{\>B}_{r}:B\rtimes_{\beta}S_{f}\longrightarrow\mathrm{M}(B\otimes\mathcal{K})
the
\widehat{S}
-regular representation
. We define the
\widehat{S}
-reduced crossed product
to be
B\rtimes_{\beta,r}S_{r}\coloneq\widehat{\Lambda}_{\beta}\left(B\rtimes_{\beta}S_{f}\right)
.

Note that the above homomorphism pairs are covariant by
[
6
, Lemma 4.5,Remark 4.13]
. We will also refer to these objects as simply ‘regular representations’ and ‘reduced crossed products’ when no confusion arises.

Remark 1
.

Although we only state our results for full
S
-coactions, we remark that every result proven also holds for full
\widehat{S}
-coactions via switching from right to left corepresentations and using the formulas defined for full
\widehat{S}
-coactions. Moreover, there are many statements that obviously are true for full
\widehat{S}
-coactions despite their absence. We opt to include the details for the crossed product, dual coactions, the regular representation, and the canonical surjection for full
\widehat{S}
-coactions since once one has the necessary formulas from these statements, the proof techniques employed for full
S
-coactions carry over verbatim for full
\widehat{S}
-coactions. Examples of statements that have obvious carry-over to full
\widehat{S}
-coaction include the theory of equivariant morphisms, crossed product duality, the propositions in section
4
, and the statement of the main result. Discussion about full
\widehat{S}
-coactions can be found throughout
[
6
,
5
]
.

As with classical crossed products, there are accompanying dual coactions associated to crossed product
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras by quantum groups. In particular if
(A,\alpha)
is a full
S
-coaction, then there are naturally defined full
\widehat{S}
-coactions
\left(A\rtimes_{\alpha}\widehat{S}_{f},\widehat{\alpha}\right)
and
\left(A\rtimes_{\alpha,r}\widehat{S}_{r},\widehat{\alpha}^{n}\right)
.

Duality and Ideals of Crossed Products

This section deals with the bulk of the technical results needed to prove Theorem
5.1
. Throughout this section, we again fix a weak Kac system with full duality
(\mathcal{H},V,U)
arising from a regular LCQG
\mathcal{G}
. We now mention the two types of coactions we will consider throughout the rest of this work. With this, we can state the duality theorems. As before, several of these results we directly credit to
[
6
]
, and state them here for convenience’s sake. Let
(A,\alpha)
and
(B,\beta)
be a full
S
and
\widehat{S}
-coaction respectively. Recall that a strict cocycle (for, say the
S
-coaction
\alpha
) is a unitary
u\in\mathrm{M}(A\otimes S_{f})
such that

(a)

(\text{id}_{A}\otimes\alpha)(u)=u_{12}(\alpha\otimes\text{id}_{S_{f}})(u)
and

(b)

\overline{\text{span}}\left\{(1_{A}\otimes S_{f})\text{Ad}(u)\circ\alpha(A)\right\}=A\otimes S_{f}
.

The maps
\text{Ad}(U)\circ\widehat{\pi}_{f}:\widehat{S}_{f}\longrightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
and
\text{Ad}(U)\circ\pi_{f}:S_{f}\longrightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
correspond to unitary corepresentations

\mathfrak{w}\coloneq(\text{Ad}(U)\circ\widehat{\pi}_{f}\otimes\text{id}_{S_{f}})(\mathfrak{v})\in\mathrm{M}(\mathcal{K}\otimes S_{f})

and

\widehat{\mathfrak{w}}\coloneq(\text{Ad}(U)\circ\pi_{f}\otimes\text{id}_{\widehat{S}_{f}})(\sigma(\mathfrak{v}))\in\mathrm{M}(\mathcal{K}\otimes\widehat{S}_{f})

respectively by Definition
3.11
. One can check by
[
6
, Example 1.3]
that
((\text{id}_{A}\otimes\Sigma)\circ(\alpha\otimes\text{id}_{\mathcal{K}}),A\otimes\mathcal{K})
and
((\text{id}_{B}\otimes\widehat{\Sigma})\circ(\beta\otimes\text{id}_{\mathcal{K}}),B\otimes\mathcal{K})
are full
S
and
\widehat{S}
-coactions respectively, where
\Sigma:S_{f}\otimes\mathcal{K}\longrightarrow\mathcal{K}\otimes S_{f}
and
\widehat{\Sigma}:\widehat{S}_{f}\otimes\mathcal{K}\longrightarrow\mathcal{K}\otimes\widehat{S}_{f}
are the flip isomorphisms. One can then verify that
\mathfrak{w}_{23},\widehat{\mathfrak{w}}_{23}
are strict cocycles for
(\text{id}_{A}\otimes\Sigma)\circ(\alpha\otimes\text{id}_{\mathcal{K}})
and
(\text{id}_{B}\otimes\widehat{\Sigma})\circ(\beta\otimes\text{id}_{\mathcal{K}})
respectively, so that one obtains perturbed full
S
and
\widehat{S}
-coactions on
A\otimes\mathcal{K}
and
B\otimes\mathcal{K}
respectively via

\displaystyle\epsilon

\displaystyle\coloneq\text{Ad}(\mathfrak{w}_{23})\circ(\text{id}_{A}\otimes\Sigma)\circ(\alpha\otimes\text{id}_{\mathcal{K}}),\>\text{and}

\displaystyle\widehat{\epsilon}

\displaystyle\coloneq\text{Ad}(\widehat{\mathfrak{w}}_{23})\circ(\text{id}_{B}\otimes\widehat{\Sigma})\circ(\beta\otimes\text{id}_{\mathcal{K}})

by
[
6
, Example 1.15]
. We will denote the coaction
(\text{id}_{A}\otimes\Sigma)\circ(\alpha\otimes\text{id}_{\mathcal{K}})
by
(\alpha\otimes_{*}\text{id}_{\mathcal{K}})
as is common practice. Clearly, we have defined the obvious generalization of the usual coaction on the stabilization of an algebra in the statement of Katayama duality, see
[
12
, Remark 5]
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
(A,\alpha)
and
(B,\beta)
be full
S
and
\widehat{S}
-coactions respectively.

(a)

The pair
(\Lambda_{\alpha},1_{M(A)}\otimes\text{Ad}(U)\circ\pi_{f}):\left(A\rtimes_{\alpha}\widehat{S}_{f},\widehat{\alpha}\right)\longrightarrow\mathrm{M}(A\otimes\mathcal{K})
is covariant and induces an
\widehat{\widehat{\alpha}}-\epsilon
equivariant surjective homomorphism
\Phi_{\alpha}:A\rtimes_{\alpha}\widehat{S}_{f}\rtimes_{\widehat{\alpha}}S_{f}\longrightarrow A\otimes\mathcal{K}
.

(b)

The pair
(\widehat{\Lambda}_{\beta},1_{M(B)}\otimes\widehat{\pi}_{f}):\left(B\rtimes_{\alpha}S_{f},\widehat{\beta}\right)\longrightarrow\mathrm{M}(B\otimes\mathcal{K})
is covariant and induces an
\widehat{\widehat{\beta}}-\widehat{\epsilon}
equivariant surjective homomorphism
\widehat{\Phi}_{\beta}:B\rtimes_{\beta}S_{f}\rtimes_{\widehat{\beta}}\widehat{S}_{f}\longrightarrow B\otimes\mathcal{K}
.

We call the maps
\Phi_{\alpha}
and
\widehat{\Phi}_{\beta}
the canonical surjections.

These are the obvious generalizations of Nilsen’s canonical surjections from
[
12
]
.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
(A,\alpha)
be a full
S
-coaction.

(a)

\alpha
is called
maximal
provided that
\Phi_{\alpha}
is an isomorphism.

(b)

\alpha
is called
normal
provided that
j_{A}^{\alpha}
is faithful.

A remarkable fact is that dual coactions on full crossed products are always maximal, see
[
6
, Proposition 6.6]
. As a remark, the dual coaction on the reduced crossed product
\widehat{\alpha}^{n}
is always a
normalization
of
\widehat{\alpha}
, see
[
6
, Lemma 4.11,Remark 4.13]
. This is why we use a
n
to denote this coaction.

We now state crossed product duality for full
S
-coactions, and explain where this statement comes from.

Theorem 4.3

(
[
6
]
)
.

Let
(A,\alpha)
be a full
S
-coaction.

•

(\textit{Maximal Duality})
If
\alpha
is maximal, there are
\widehat{\widehat{\alpha}}-\epsilon
and
\widehat{\hat{\alpha}}^{n}-\epsilon
equivariant isomorphisms:

\displaystyle\left(A\rtimes_{\alpha}\widehat{S}_{f}\rtimes_{\widehat{\alpha}}S_{f},\widehat{\widehat{\alpha}}\right)\cong\left(A\otimes\mathcal{K},\epsilon\right),\>\>\text{and}

\displaystyle\left(A\rtimes_{\alpha,r}\widehat{S}_{r}\rtimes_{\widehat{\alpha}^{n}}S_{f},\widehat{\hat{\alpha}}^{n}\right)\cong\left(A\otimes\mathcal{K},\epsilon\right).

for
\widehat{\hat{\alpha}}^{n}
the dual coaction of
\widehat{\alpha}^{n}
and
\epsilon
given above.

•

(\textit{Normal Duality})
If
\alpha
is normal, there is a
\left(\widehat{\widehat{\alpha}}\right)^{n}-\epsilon
equivariant isomorphism:

\displaystyle\left(A\rtimes_{\alpha}\widehat{S}_{f}\rtimes_{\widehat{\alpha},r}S_{r},\left(\widehat{\widehat{\alpha}}\right)^{n}\right)\cong\left(A\otimes\mathcal{K},\epsilon\right).

The
\widehat{\hat{\alpha}}^{n}-\epsilon
equivariant isomorphism follows from the regular representation
\Lambda_{\alpha}:\left(A\rtimes_{\alpha}\widehat{S}_{f},\widehat{\alpha}\right)\longrightarrow\left(A\rtimes_{\alpha,r}\widehat{S}_{r},\widehat{\alpha}^{n}\right)
being a normalization of the dual coaction
\widehat{\alpha}
see
[
6
, Lemma 4.11]
. In particular, this means
\Lambda
is an
\widehat{\alpha}-\widehat{\alpha}^{n}
equivariant surjection, such that its crossed product

\Lambda\rtimes S_{f}:A\rtimes_{\alpha}\widehat{S}_{f}\rtimes_{\widehat{\alpha}}S_{f}\longrightarrow A\rtimes_{\alpha,r}\widehat{S}_{r}\rtimes_{\widehat{\alpha}^{n}}S_{f}

is an isomorphism, which is
\widehat{\widehat{\alpha}}-\widehat{\hat{\alpha}}^{n}
equivariant by Lemma
3.19
. We now possess the tools to productively discuss coaction invariance of ideals, and how to prove the main result. Note if
I\lhd A
is a closed two-sided ideal of
A
, then we may (and will implicitly throughout) identify
\mathrm{M}_{S_{f}}(I\otimes S_{f})\subset\mathrm{M}_{S_{f}}(A\otimes S_{f})\subset\mathrm{M}(A\otimes S_{f})
via
[
4
, Lemma 1.46]
.

Definition 4.4
.

Let
(A,\alpha)
be a full
S
-coaction and
I\lhd A
a (closed two-sided) ideal of
A
. We say that
I
is
\alpha
-invariant
provided that
\alpha(I)\subset\mathrm{M}_{S_{f}}(I\otimes S_{f})
, and the restriction map
\alpha|_{I}:I\longrightarrow\mathrm{M}(I\otimes S_{f})
defines a full
S
-coaction on
I
. Moreover, we denote the collection of all
\alpha
-invariant ideals of
A
by
\mathscr{I}_{\alpha}(A)
.

Upon restriction to invariant ideals, we note that continuity of the coaction is preserved.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
(A,\alpha)
be a full
S
-coaction and
I\lhd A
. Then
I\in\mathscr{I}_{\alpha}(A)
if and only if

\overline{\text{span}}\left\{\alpha(I)(1_{M(A)}\otimes S_{f})\right\}=I\otimes S_{f}.

Proof.

This is the proof of
[
2
, Proposition 2.6.23]
included for completeness. If
I\in\mathscr{I}_{\alpha}(A)
, then
\alpha(I)(1_{M(I)}\otimes S_{f})\subset I\otimes S_{f}
. Now let
x\in I\otimes S_{f}
. Since
\alpha
is continuous we have that
x\approx\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\alpha(a_{k})(1_{M(A)}\otimes z_{k})
for
a_{k}\in A
and
z_{k}\in S_{f}
. However since
\alpha|_{I}
is a coaction, it is non-degenerate so that for
(e_{i})_{i}\subset I
an approximate unit, we have
\alpha(e_{i})\longrightarrow 1_{A\otimes S_{f}}
strictly, implying

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle\approx\alpha(e_{i})x

\displaystyle\approx\alpha(e_{i})\left(\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\alpha(a_{k})(1_{M(A)}\otimes z_{k})\right)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\alpha(e_{i}a_{k})(1_{M(A)}\otimes z_{k})

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\alpha(e_{i}a_{k})(1_{M(I)}\otimes z_{k})

\displaystyle\in\overline{\text{span}}\left\{\alpha(I)(1_{M(I)}\otimes S_{f})\right\}

proving continuity of
\alpha|_{I}
. Conversely if the latter condition holds, then
\alpha|_{I}(I)\subset M_{S_{f}}(I\otimes S_{f})
, where
\alpha|_{I}
is a faithful non-degenerate
*
-homomorphism satisfying the coaction identity. Hence,
I\in\mathscr{I}_{\alpha}(A)
.
∎

To prove the main result, we collect several technical propositions. Thankfully, these are roughly the steps needed to prove
[
7

, just in higher generality. This is thanks to the employment of the ladder technique. To begin, we define correspondence coactions on quantum groups in line with

Definition 4.6
.

Let
\mathsf{X}
be an
(A,B)
correspondence with full
S
-coactions
(A,\alpha)
and
(B,\beta)
. View

as an
(S_{f},S_{f})
-correspondence and form the exterior tensor product to get:

{}_{A\otimes S_{f}}(\mathsf{X}\otimes_{\text{ext}}S_{f})_{B\otimes S_{f}}

(A\otimes S_{f},B\otimes S_{f})
-correspondence. A

\alpha

\zeta:\mathsf{X}\longrightarrow\mathrm{M}(_{A\otimes S_{f}}(\mathsf{X}\otimes_{\text{ext}}S_{f})_{B\otimes S_{f}})
such that

(\zeta\otimes\text{id}_{S_{f}})\circ\zeta=(\text{id}_{\mathsf{X}}\otimes\Delta_{f})\circ\zeta.

Moreover, we say that
\zeta
is
continuous
provided that

\overline{\text{span}}\{(1_{M(A)}\otimes S_{f})\zeta(\mathsf{X})\}=\>_{A\otimes S_{f}}(\mathsf{X}\otimes_{\text{ext}}S_{f})_{B\otimes S_{f}}.

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
(A,\alpha)
,
(B,\beta)
be full
S
-coactions and
\mathsf{X}
be an
(A,B)
-imprimitivity bimodule, with
J\lhd B
, and
I\lhd A
the ideal of
A
associated to
J
under the Rieffel correspondence (that is,
I=\mathsf{X}-\text{Ind}^{A}_{B}(J)
). Moreover let
\zeta
be a continuous
\alpha-\beta
compatible coaction on
\mathsf{X}
. Then
I
is
\alpha
-invariant if and only if
J
is
\beta
-invariant.

Proof.

Suppose that
J\lhd B
is
\beta
-invariant. It suffices to prove that
I
is
\alpha
-invariant by symmetry. To do this, it suffices to appeal to Lemma
4.5
. Computing we have that

\displaystyle\overline{\text{span}}\{(1\otimes S_{f})\alpha(I)\}

\displaystyle=\overline{\text{span}}\left\{(1\otimes S_{f})\alpha\left({}_{A}\langle\mathsf{X}\cdot J,\mathsf{X}\rangle\right)\right\}

\displaystyle=\overline{\text{span}}\left\{(1\otimes S_{f})\>_{\mathrm{M}}\langle\zeta(\mathsf{X}\cdot J),\zeta(\mathsf{X})\rangle\right\}

\displaystyle=\overline{\text{span}}\left\{\>{}_{\mathrm{M}}\langle(1\otimes S_{f})\zeta(\mathsf{X}\cdot J),\zeta(\mathsf{X})\rangle\right\}

\displaystyle=\overline{\text{span}}\left\{\>{}_{\mathrm{M}}\langle(1\otimes S_{f})^{*}(1\otimes S_{f})\zeta(\mathsf{X}\cdot J),\zeta(\mathsf{X})\rangle\right\}

\displaystyle=\overline{\text{span}}\left\{\>{}_{\mathrm{M}}\langle(1\otimes S_{f})\zeta(\mathsf{X}\cdot J),(1\otimes S_{f})\zeta(\mathsf{X})\rangle\right\}

\displaystyle=\overline{\text{span}}\left\{\>{}_{\mathrm{M}}\langle(1\otimes S_{f})\zeta(\mathsf{X})\beta(J),(1\otimes S_{f})\zeta(\mathsf{X})\rangle\right\}

\displaystyle=\overline{\text{span}}\left\{\>{}_{\mathrm{M}}\langle(\mathsf{X}\otimes_{\text{ext}}S_{f})\beta(J),\mathsf{X}\otimes_{\text{ext}}S_{f}\rangle\right\}

\displaystyle=\overline{\text{span}}\left\{\>{}_{\mathrm{M}}\langle(\mathsf{X}\otimes_{\text{ext}}S_{f})(1\otimes S_{f})\beta(J),\mathsf{X}\otimes_{\text{ext}}S_{f}\rangle\right\}

\displaystyle=\overline{\text{span}}\left\{\>{}_{\mathrm{M}}\langle(\mathsf{X}\otimes_{\text{ext}}S_{f})(J\otimes S_{f}),\mathsf{X}\otimes_{\text{ext}}S_{f}\rangle\right\}

\displaystyle=\overline{\text{span}}\left\{\>{}_{\mathrm{M}}\langle\mathsf{X}\cdot J\otimes_{\text{ext}}S_{f},\mathsf{X}\otimes_{\text{ext}}S_{f}\rangle\right\}

where
\mathrm{M}=\mathrm{M}(A\otimes S_{f})
. Note that the
M(A\otimes S_{f})
-valued inner product extends that of the

-valued inner product. Hence we have

\displaystyle\overline{\text{span}}\left\{\>{}_{\mathrm{M}}\langle\mathsf{X}\cdot J\otimes_{\text{ext}}S_{f},\mathsf{X}\otimes_{\text{ext}}S_{f}\rangle\right\}

\displaystyle=\overline{\text{span}}\left\{\>{}_{A\otimes S_{f}}\langle\mathsf{X}\cdot J\otimes_{\text{ext}}S_{f},\mathsf{X}\otimes_{\text{ext}}S_{f}\rangle\right\}

\displaystyle=\overline{\text{span}}\left\{\>{}_{A}\langle\mathsf{X}\cdot J,\mathsf{X}\rangle\otimes\>_{S_{f}}\langle S_{f},S_{f}\rangle\right\}

\displaystyle=I\otimes S_{f}.

Hence, we have shown

\displaystyle\overline{\text{span}}\left\{(1\otimes S_{f})\alpha(I)\right\}

\displaystyle=I\otimes S_{f}

\displaystyle=(I\otimes S_{f})^{*}

\displaystyle=\overline{\text{span}}\left\{\alpha(I)(1\otimes S_{f})\right\}.

Thus,
J
being
\beta
-invariant implies
I
is
\alpha
-invariant by Lemma
4.5
.
∎

Proposition 4.8
.

Let
(A,\alpha)
be a full
S
-coaction with
I\in\mathscr{I}_{\alpha}(A)
. Then the inclusion map
\varphi:I\hookrightarrow A
is
\alpha|-\alpha
equivariant and the crossed product
\varphi\rtimes\widehat{S}_{f}
is an isomorphism of
I\rtimes_{\alpha|}\widehat{S}_{f}
onto an
\widehat{\alpha}
-invariant ideal
I\rtimes_{\alpha}\widehat{S}_{f}\in\mathscr{I}_{\widehat{\alpha}}\left(A\rtimes_{\alpha}\widehat{S}_{f}\right)
. Moreover, the image
I\rtimes_{\alpha,r}\widehat{S}_{r}\coloneq\Lambda_{\alpha}\left(I\rtimes_{\alpha}\widehat{S}_{f}\right)
under the regular representation
\Lambda_{\alpha}
is
\widehat{\alpha}^{n}
-invariant, where
\widehat{\alpha}^{n}
is the dual coaction of
\alpha
on the reduced crossed product
A\rtimes_{\alpha,r}\widehat{S}_{r}
.

Proof.

It’s immediately clear that
\varphi
is
\alpha|-\alpha
equivariant. A standard argument shows that
j_{A}|I\rtimes j^{A}=\varphi\rtimes\widehat{S}_{f}
is faithful, see
[

. Now, to see that
I\rtimes_{\alpha}\widehat{S}_{f}\coloneq\left(\varphi\rtimes\widehat{S}_{f}\right)\left(I\rtimes_{\alpha|}\widehat{S}_{f}\right)
is actually an ideal of

, we compute

\displaystyle\left(I\rtimes_{\alpha}\widehat{S}_{f}\right)\left(A\rtimes_{\alpha}\widehat{S}_{f}\right)

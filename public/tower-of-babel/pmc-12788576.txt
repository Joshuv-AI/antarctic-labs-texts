Evaluation of Mandibular Cortical Bone Density Changes Using CBCT During Accelerated Orthodontics with Low-Level Laser Therapy
Authors: Mohammed E. Rashid, Tarek Ibrahim, Andullah A. Ghani, Ayesha Alhodhudi, Mohammad Y. Hajeer, Mohammad K. Alam
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Background:Accelerated orthodontics using adjunctive methods, like low-level laser therapy (LLLT), has shown promise in enhancing tooth movement rates. However, the impact of LLLT on mandibular cortical bone density during orthodontic treatment remains unclear.Methods:A prospective randomized controlled study was conducted on 40 orthodontic patients (aged between 18 and 30 years), divided equally into LLLT (intervention) and control groups. All patients underwent fixed orthodontic treatment. The LLLT group received diode laser irradiation (808 nm, 100 mW, 8 J/cm²) on the mandibular alveolar bone on alternate days for the first month. CBCT scans were obtained at baseline (T0) and after 12 weeks (T1). Cortical bone density was measured at the mandibular first molar and canine regions using grayscale values.Results:At T1, the LLLT group showed a significant reduction in cortical bone density compared to baseline (first molar: 935.2 ± 54.6 HU to 879.5 ± 62.1 HU, P = 0.004; canine: 918.7 ± 50.4 HU to 864.3 ± 58.9 HU, P = 0.006). In contrast, the control group exhibited minimal, nonsignificant changes (first molar: 940.1 ± 52.8 HU to 928.4 ± 57.6 HU, P = 0.148; canine: 920.5 ± 51.1 HU to 910.7 ± 55.3 HU, P = 0.217). Intergroup comparison at T1 showed significantly lower bone density in the LLLT group at both sites (P < 0.05).Conclusion:LLLT during orthodontic treatment significantly reduces mandibular cortical bone density, potentially facilitating accelerated tooth movement. CBCT proves to be a reliable tool for assessing bone changes in vivo.

INTRODUCTION

Orthodontic tooth movement is governed by the remodeling of alveolar bone and periodontal ligament in response to mechanical forces.[1] Although conventional orthodontic therapy achieves desirable outcomes, the lengthy treatment duration often leads to patient dissatisfaction and increased risk of complications such as root resorption and caries.[2]

To address this, adjunctive methods, such as low-level laser therapy (LLLT), have been introduced to accelerate tooth movement. LLLT is a noninvasive photobiomodulation technique known to stimulate cellular proliferation, angiogenesis, and bone remodeling by acting on mitochondrial chromophores.[3] While several studies have reported enhanced tooth movement with LLLT, its influence on the bone microarchitecture, particularly cortical bone density, has not been extensively studied.

Cone-beam computed tomography (CBCT) allows for high-resolution, three-dimensional assessment of maxillofacial structures and is considered reliable for evaluating bone density in terms of grayscale values.[4] Previous studies have predominantly focused on alveolar or trabecular bone responses to orthodontic forces, often neglecting the cortical component, which plays a crucial role in biomechanical stability.[5]

The current research gap pertains to the quantitative assessment of mandibular cortical bone alterations due to LLLT during orthodontic treatment. Therefore, this study aimed to evaluate changes in cortical bone density of the mandible using CBCT in patients undergoing accelerated orthodontics with LLLT.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

A prospective, double-blinded, randomized controlled trial was conducted in the Department of Orthodontics at a tertiary dental care center between January 2023 and September 2024. Ethical approval was obtained from the institutional review board, and informed consent was collected from all participants.

Sample size and selection

Forty patients (18–30 years old) with Class I malocclusion requiring bilateral extraction of mandibular first premolars and subsequent canine retraction were enrolled and randomly allocated into two groups (n = 20 each): Group A (LLLT) and Group B (control).

Orthodontic procedure

All patients underwent bonding with a 0.022 × 0.028-inch slot MBT prescription bracket system. Canine retraction was initiated with NiTi closed-coil springs exerting a constant force of 150 g.

LLLT protocol

Group A received LLLT using an 808 nm GaAlAs diode laser at 100 mW for 80 seconds per point (total energy: 8 J/cm²), applied at buccal and lingual aspects of the mandibular canine and first molar areas on alternate days for the first month.

CBCT imaging and bone density analysis

CBCT scans (Planmeca ProMax 3D Mid, voxel size 200 μm) were taken at T0 (before retraction) and T1 (12 weeks post-initiation). Cortical bone density was measured in Hounsfield units (HU) at the buccal cortex adjacent to the mandibular first molars and canines using Planmeca Romexis Viewer. Regions of interest (ROI) were standardized.

Statistical analysis

Data were analyzed using SPSS v26.0. Intragroup comparisons were made using paired t-tests; intergroup comparisons were analyzed using independent t-tests. A P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

DISCUSSION

This study evaluated the effect of low-level laser therapy on mandibular cortical bone density during orthodontic treatment using CBCT. The significant reduction in bone density observed in the LLLT group suggests enhanced bone remodeling activity, potentially facilitating faster tooth movement.

The photobiomodulatory effects of LLLT on bone tissues are mediated through mitochondrial stimulation, leading to increased ATP synthesis, reactive oxygen species production, and cellular proliferation.[6] Similar findings have been reported by Goulart et al.,[7] who observed reduced bone density in laser-assisted orthodontics due to increased osteoclastic activity.

CBCT imaging enabled accurate quantification of cortical density, aligning with previous reports that validate its grayscale-based measurements as a proxy for Hounsfield units in evaluating jaw bone quality.[8] Nevertheless, variations in voxel size and machine calibration remain limitations that can affect reproducibility.

A comparable study by Sousa et al.[9] demonstrated similar cortical bone density reductions with LLLT in the maxilla, supporting the systemic response of bone to laser-induced stimulation. Moreover, the current results extend these observations to the mandible, an area often underexplored in such contexts.

Despite the promising results, the short duration of 12 weeks and the relatively small sample size limit generalizability. Long-term effects of LLLT on bone regeneration and orthodontic stability remain to be elucidated. Additionally, variations in individual bone remodeling rates and systemic metabolic factors might influence outcomes.

Future studies incorporating biochemical markers of bone turnover and histological evaluations may provide further insight into the mechanisms underlying LLLT-induced bone changes.

CONCLUSION

LLLT significantly reduces mandibular cortical bone density during the initial stages of orthodontic treatment, as demonstrated by CBCT analysis. This reduction may contribute to accelerated tooth movement. CBCT proves to be a valuable tool for assessing treatment-induced bone remodeling. LLLT can be considered a safe and effective adjunct in contemporary orthodontic practice.

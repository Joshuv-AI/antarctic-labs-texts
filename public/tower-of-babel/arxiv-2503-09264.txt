On the Bogomolov-Positselski Conjecture
Julian Feuerpfeil

Abstract
. Let p p be a prime. An oriented pro- p p group ( G , θ ) (G,\theta) is said to have the Bogomolov–Positselski property if it is Kummerian and if I θ ​ ( G ) I_{\theta}(G) is a free pro- p p group. In this paper, we provide a new criterion for an oriented pro- p p group to satisfy the Bogomolov–Positselski property. This criterion builds on earlier work of Positselski [ 13 ] and Quadrelli–Weigel [ 17 ] , relates their approaches, and answers a question raised in [ 17 ] . Under additional assumptions, we obtain two further sufficient criteria. The first is analogous to a Merkurjev–Suslin type statement. The second allows one to weaken the hypotheses appearing in Positselski’s criterion [ 13 ] *Theorem 2. Finally, we show that the stronger conditions are satisfied by pro- p p groups of elementary type. As a consequence, the Elementary Type Conjecture implies Positselski’s “Module Koszulity Conjecture 1” [ 14 ] for fields with finitely generated maximal pro- p p Galois group. Keywords. Bogomolov-Positselski Conjecture, oriented pro- p p groups, Koszul algebras, Elementary Type Conjecture 2020 Math. Subject Class. 1.1.
Oriented pro-
p

Let
p
be a prime. A
p
-

(G,\theta)
consiting of a profinite group

and continuous homomorphism
\theta:G\to\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
. They have been introduced by I. Efrat for pro-
p
groups in
[
4
]
under the name

p
pair
. In contrast, we call a
p
-oriented profinite group, whose underlying profinite group is pro-

p

, as done by Quadrelli and Weigel in
[
17

The definition is motivated by a setting in Galois theory. Let
\mathbb{K}
be a field of characteristic
\neq p
, denote by
\mathbb{K}^{s}
a separable closure of
\mathbb{K}
and
G_{\mathbb{K}}:=\Gal(\mathbb{K}^{s}/\mathbb{K})
the absolute Galois group of
\mathbb{K}
. The profinite group
G_{\mathbb{K}}
acts continuously on the discrete group
\smash{\mu_{p^{\infty}}(\mathbb{K}^{s})}\cong\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}

(1.1)

\displaystyle\theta_{\mathbb{K}}:G_{\mathbb{K}}\to\Aut(\mu_{p^{\infty}}(\mathbb{K}^{s}))\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}

and the pair
(G_{\mathbb{K}},\theta_{\mathbb{K}})

p
-oriented profinite group. If
\mathbb{K}
contains a primitive
p^{\text{th}}

\theta_{\mathbb{K}}
factors through the maximal pro-
p

-Sylow subgroups of

p

\mathbb{K}

An oriented pro-
p

is called

,
p
is odd or

\sqrt{-1}\in\mathbb{K}

An oriented pro-

\displaystyle K_{\theta}(G)

\displaystyle:=\langle h^{-\theta(g)}ghg^{-1}:g\in G,h\in\ker\theta\rangle_{cl}

\displaystyle I_{\theta}(G)

\displaystyle:=\langle h\in\ker\theta:h^{p^{n}}\in K_{\theta}(G)\text{ for some }n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\rangle_{cl}

The normal subgroup

was introduced by Quadrelli and Efrat in
[
2
]
and is an analogue of the commutator subgroup for oriented pro-
p
groups (see
[
17

*Section 2). If

An oriented pro-

Theorem

Let

Most of the statements in this paper are only concerned (and only true) for torsion-free oriented pro-
p

Assumption 1.1
.

The field
\mathbb{K}
contains a primitive
p^{\text{th}}
root of unity and
\sqrt{-1}

The following conjecture was first stated by Bogomolov in

for fields containing an algebraically closed subfield and later refined by Positselski in

Conjecture

(Bogomolov–Positselski)

Let

is a free pro-

Motivated by this Conjecture, we say that a Kummerian oriented pro-
p

has the

if

is a free pro-
p
group. Similarly, a field

satisfying

has the
Bogomolov–Positselski property
if
(G_{\mathbb{K}}(p),\theta_{\mathbb{K}})
has the Bogomolov–Positselski property.

A pro-
p
group

is called
\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}
-

if its

-cohomology algebra
\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})=\bigoplus_{i}\mathrm{H}^{i}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})
is a quadratic algebra with respect to the cup product, that is, it is generated as algebra by its elements in degree
1
and all relations are in degree
2
. For a more precise definition, we refer to Section

The following theorem is a consequence of the norm residue isomorphism theorem proven by Rost and Voevodsky with a patch by Weibel (cf.
[
21
,
24
,
23
]
):

Theorem
.

Let
\mathbb{K}
be a field containing a primitive
p^{\text{th}}
root of unity, then
G_{\mathbb{K}}(p)
is
\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}
-quadratic.

For a torsion-free, Kummerian, oriented pro-
p
group
(G,\theta)
, we consider the inflation map

(1.2)

\displaystyle\psi_{G}^{\bullet}:={\inf}_{G(\theta),G}^{\bullet}:\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}(G(\theta),\mathbb{F}_{p})\cong\Lambda^{\bullet}(\mathrm{H}^{1}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p}))\to\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p}),

which is surjective homomorphism of quadratic algebras if
G
is
\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}
-quadratic. The isomorphism
\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}(G(\theta),\mathbb{F}_{p})\cong\Lambda^{\bullet}(\mathrm{H}^{1}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p}))
can be found in
[
17
]
*Example 4.3 and is a consequence of Lazard’s theorem. If
G
is clear from the context, we only write
\psi
instead of
\psi_{G}
.

The next theorem is due to Positselski and gives a criterion for the Bogomolov–Positselski property of a field
\mathbb{K}
in terms of properties of the kernel of
\psi^{\bullet}:=\psi_{G_{\mathbb{K}}(p)}^{\bullet}
, whose proof works also in the purely group theoretic setting.

Theorem

(
[
13
, Theorem 2]
)
.

Let
\mathbb{K}
be a field satisfying
1.1
. If
(\ker\psi^{\bullet})(2)
is a Koszul module over the algebra
\Lambda^{\bullet}(\mathrm{H}^{1}(G_{\mathbb{K}},\mathbb{F}_{p}))
, then
\mathbb{K}
has the Bogomolov–Positselski property.

This criterion depends on the vanishing of infinitely many cohomology groups, since the definition of Koszulity asserts that
\mathrm{H}_{ij}(\Lambda^{\bullet}(V),\ker\psi^{\bullet})=0
for all
j\neq i+2
, where
V=\mathrm{H}^{1}(G_{\mathbb{K}},\mathbb{F}_{p})
. See Section
2
for the definition of the (co-)homology groups of graded algebras. Positselski conjectured that the conditions for this theorem hold universally in
[
14
]
*Conjecture.

In
[
17
]
, Quadrelli and Weigel gave a new criterion for the Bogomolov–Positselski property, depending only on two cohomology groups, but in a sophisticated way. Let
(G,\theta)
be a torsion-free Kummerian oriented pro-
p
group, then there is the Hochschild-Serre spectral sequence associated to the group extension
1\to K_{\theta}(G)\to G\to G(\theta)\to 1
.
This spectral sequence will be denoted by

(1.3)

\displaystyle E_{2}^{s,t}:=\mathrm{H}^{s}(G(\theta),\mathrm{H}^{t}(K_{\theta}(G),\mathbb{F}_{p}))\Longrightarrow\mathrm{H}^{s+t}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p}).

Theorem

(
[
17
, Theorem 4.5]
)
.

Let
(G,\theta)
be a torsion-free, Kummerian, oriented pro-
p
group with
G
being
\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}
-quadratic, then
(G,\theta)
has the Bogomolov–Positselski property if and only if the differential
d_{2}^{2,1}:E^{2,1}_{2}\to E^{4,0}_{2}
in the spectral sequence in (

) is injective.

1.2.
Main results and structure of the paper

Quadrelli and Weigel asked in
[
17
, Remark 1.5]
if there is a connection between Theorem their theorem and the criterion by Positsitselski. More precisely, if there is a way to express
\ker d_{2}^{2,1}
in terms of the certain (co-)homology groups of graded
\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}(G(\theta),\mathbb{F}_{p})
modules.

In this paper, we give an affirmative answer to this question. The following theorem gives a first description and is an important cornerstone to the other criteria.

Theorem A
.

Let
(G,\theta)
be a torsion-free, Kummerian, oriented pro-
p
group, such that
G
is
\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}
-quadratic. We set
V:=\mathrm{H}^{1}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})
,
B:=\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})
and let
N
be the graded
\Lambda^{\bullet}(V)
-module
\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}(G(\theta),\mathrm{H}^{1}(K_{\theta},\mathbb{F}_{p}))
. Then there is an exact sequence:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{H}_{1,2}(\Lambda^{\bullet}(V),N)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{H}_{2,4}(\Lambda^{\bullet}(V),B)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\ker d_{2}^{2,1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{H}_{0,2}(\Lambda^{\bullet}(V),N)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}

In particular
\ker d_{2}^{2,1}=0
if and only if the first map is an isomorphism and
H_{0,2}(\Lambda^{\bullet}(V),N)=0
.

This theorem depends on only finitely many (co)homology groups, but again in a sophisticated way, as the module
N
, whose properties as
\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}(G(\theta),\mathbb{F}_{p})
-module determine the Bogomolov–Positselski property, seems to be hard to control. Nevertheless, the vanishing of
H_{0,2}(\Lambda^{\bullet}(V),N)
has a concrete description leading to Corollary

, which has striking similarity with the statement of the Merkurjev–Suslin theorem.

On the other hand, the vanishing of the group
H_{2,4}(\Lambda^{\bullet}(V),B)\cong H_{1,4}(\Lambda^{\bullet}(V),\ker\psi^{\bullet})
is predicted by Positselski’s Module Koszulity Conjecture 1 and follows from even weaker properties already. This leads to the following question:

Question 1.2
.

What conditions on an oriented pro-
p
group
(G,\theta)
are sufficient in order to conclude
\mathrm{H}_{2,4}(\Lambda^{\bullet}(\mathrm{H}^{1}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})),\mathbb{F}_{p}(\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p}))=0

In Example
4.4
we study the group

with trivial orientation. It satisfies the conditions of Theorem

The conclusions of Theorem

also allow us to relax the conditions of Theorem
Theorem
by applying the same techniques as Positselski in
[

Theorem B

Keep the notation of Theorem

is a quadratic

-module and

This theorem again depends on the vanishing of infinitely many cohomology groups, but does not require the “full”  Koszulity of

we show that is suffices to compute three graded cohomology groups to verify the conditions of Theorem

Finally in Section

of

is a Koszul

Theorem C
.

Let
(G,\theta)
be a torsion-free oriented pro-

Definition 2.1

A

is a graded

-vector space

A

Example 2.2
.

(1)

The tensor algebra
\mathbb{T}^{\bullet}(V)
over a
k
-vector space
V
is defined by
\mathbb{T}^{n}(V)=V^{\otimes n}
an product induced by:

\displaystyle(v_{1}\otimes...\otimes v_{j})\cdot(w_{1}\otimes...\otimes w_{i}):=v_{1}\otimes...\otimes v_{j}\otimes w_{1}\otimes...\otimes w_{i}

(2)

The exterior algebra
\Lambda^{\bullet}(V)
over a
k
-vector space

is defined by
\mathbb{T}^{\bullet}(V)/\langle v\otimes v:v\in V\rangle
. It is connected and graded-commutative, i.e., if
a,b\in\Lambda^{\bullet}(V)
are homogeneous, then
a\cdot b=(-1)^{\deg(a)\cdot\deg(b)}b\cdot a
.

(3)

The symmetric algebra
\mathbb{S}^{\bullet}(V)
is similarly defined as
\mathbb{T}^{\bullet}(V)/\langle v\otimes w-w\otimes v:v,w\in V\rangle
and if

, then
\mathbb{S}^{\bullet}(V)\cong k[x_{1},..,x_{n}]
with the natural grading on the polynomial ring.

Let
A
be a connected, graded
k
-algebra, then we denote by
A_{+}:=\bigoplus_{i\geq 1}A_{i}
its
augmentation ideal
. Consider the following complex of free
A
-modules, which is called the
normalized bar-complex
of
A
and denoted by
\mathcal{BAR}_{*}(A)
:

\displaystyle k\leftarrow A\leftarrow A\otimes_{k}A_{+}\leftarrow A\otimes_{k}(A_{+})^{\otimes 2}\leftarrow A\otimes_{k}(A_{+})^{\otimes 3}\leftarrow...

The differentials are given by the usual formulas and are of degree
0
. It is a projective resolution of
k
, considered as trivial
A
-module, in an appropriate category of graded modules. Then for a graded
A
-module
M
one defines the

with coefficients in

by

\displaystyle\mathrm{H}_{i}(A,M)

\displaystyle:=\mathrm{H}_{i}(M\otimes_{A}\mathcal{BAR}_{*}(A))\qquad\text{and}

\displaystyle\mathrm{H}^{i}(A,M)

\displaystyle:=\mathrm{H}^{i}(\Hom_{A}(\mathcal{BAR}_{*}(A),M)).

From the grading of the normalized bar-complex and the grading of the tensor product
\underline{\phantom{a}}\otimes_{A}\underline{\phantom{a}}
resp.
\Hom_{A}(\underline{\phantom{a}},\underline{\phantom{a}})
one deduces that also the vector spaces
\mathrm{H}_{i}(A,M)
and
\mathrm{H}^{i}(A,M)
have a natural grading, i.e.,

\displaystyle\mathrm{H}_{i}(A,M)=\bigoplus_{j}\mathrm{H}_{ij}(A,M)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathrm{H}^{i}(A,M)=\bigoplus_{j}\mathrm{H}^{ij}(A,M).

Furthermore,
\mathcal{BAR}_{*}(A)
is a DG-coalgebra, inducing a coproduct on the homology of
A
and a product on the cohomology of

. Both the coproduct and coproduct respect the gradings of the (co)homology groups.

.

If both

Quadratic and Koszul algebras

Definition 2.5

A graded connected

A graded module

Example 2.6
.

(1)

The algebras

(2)

\mathrm{H}_{1,j}(A,M)=0

(2)

for

and
\mathrm{H}_{2,j}(A,k)=0

.

Definition 2.9
.

A connected graded algebra

2.2.
Duals of locally finite-dimensional quadratic algebras

In this section, we study a duality for quadratic algebras. We assume that all algebras are locally finite-dimensional, in order to use the isomorphism
V^{*}\otimes W^{*}\cong(V\otimes W)^{*}
, which doesn’t hold in the infinite-dimensional context. In this case, the duality one has to consider is between algebras and coalgebras (see for example
[
12
,
13
]
).

Definition 2.10
.

Let
V
and
H
be finite dimensional
k
-vector spaces and
R\subseteq V\otimes V
and
K\subseteq H\otimes V
subspaces. Then we define the
quadratic duals

\displaystyle\{V,R\}^{!}:=\{V^{*},R^{\perp}\}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\langle H,K\rangle^{!}_{\{V,R\}}:=\langle H^{*},K^{\perp}\rangle_{\{V,R\}^{!}}.

Here
R^{\perp}
is the orthogonal complement of
R
with respect to the pairing
(V\otimes V)\times(V^{*}\otimes V^{*})\to k
defined by
(v\otimes w,f\otimes g)\mapsto f(v)g(w)
. Similarly,
K^{\perp}
is the orthogonal complement of
K
with respect to a similar pairing
(H\otimes V)\times(H^{*}\otimes V^{*})\to k
.

If
M
is a quadratic module over a quadratic algebra
A
, then we sometimes simply write
M^{!}
instead of
M^{!}_{A}
, if the algebra
A
is clear from the context.

Example 2.11
.

For
V
a finite dimensional
k
-vector space one has
\mathbb{T}^{\bullet}(V)^{!}\cong k
and
\Lambda^{\bullet}(V)^{!}\cong\mathbb{S}^{\bullet}(V^{*})
. The quadratic dual of a trivial module over a quadratic algebra is free over the dual of the algebra.

The quadratic dual of an algebra and its modules appears naturally, when studying the “diagonal cohomology”. The following Proposition is due to Priddy
[
15
]
and Löfwall
[
7
]
and can be found in
[
11
]
*Chapter 1 Proposition 3.1.

Proposition 2.12
.

Let
A
be a connected graded algebra and
M
a graded
A
-module with
M_{i}=0
for
i<0
. Then

(1)

\bigoplus_{i}\mathrm{H}^{i,i}(A,k)\cong(qA)^{!}
as graded algebras.

(2)

\bigoplus_{i}H^{i,i}(A,M)\cong({\rm q}_{A}M)^{!}
as graded
(qA)^{!}
-modules.

Proposition 2.13

(
[
11
]
*Chapter 2, Cor. 3.3 and Cor. 3.5 (M))
.

Let
A
be a quadratic algebra, then
A
is Koszul if and only if its quadratic dual

is Koszul.

Assume that

is Koszul and

is a quadratic

-module, then

is Koszul over

if and only if

is Koszul over

the following are equivalent:

(1)

for

and

;

(2)

\mathrm{H}^{ij}(A^{!},M_{A}^{!})=0
for

and

The following construction allows us to produce new algebras and modules from known ones and will prove useful in Section

Construction 2.14

Let

3.
Quadratic Galois cohomology algebras and Koszulity conjectures

Let
\mathbb{K}
be a field and denote by
\mu_{p}
the group of
p^{\text{th}}
roots of unity in a fixed separable closure
\mathbb{K}^{s}
. Let
G_{\mathbb{K}}=\Gal(\mathbb{K}^{s}/\mathbb{K})
be the absolute Galois group of
\mathbb{K}
. We denote by
K^{M}_{n}(\mathbb{K})
the
n
-th Milnor
K
-group of
\mathbb{K}
, which is defined as

\displaystyle K^{M}_{n}(\mathbb{K})=(\mathbb{K}^{\times})^{\otimes n}/\langle a_{1}\otimes....\otimes a_{n}:a_{i}+a_{j}=1\text{ for some }i\neq j\rangle.

Then
K^{M}_{\bullet}(\mathbb{K})
with the canonical product is a graded ring and
K^{M}_{\bullet}(\mathbb{K})\otimes\mathbb{F}_{p}
is a quadratic
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra. In
[
20
]
Tate showed the existence of an algebra homomorphism
h_{p}:K^{M}_{\bullet}(\mathbb{K})\otimes\mathbb{F}_{p}\to\bigoplus_{i}\mathrm{H}^{i}(G_{\mathbb{K}},\mu_{p}^{\otimes i})
, extending the Kummer isomorphism in degree
1
.

The following theorem was proven by Rost and Voevodsky together with a
“patch” by Weibel (cf.
[
21
,
24
,
23
]
and resolved a conjecture by Bloch and Kato.

Theorem 3.1

(Norm residue isomorphism theorem)
.

The map
h_{p}
above is an isomorphism of graded algebras. In particular, the algebra
\bigoplus_{i}\mathrm{H}^{i}(G_{\mathbb{K}},\mu_{p}^{\otimes i})
is quadratic.

If
\mathbb{K}
contains a primitive
p^{\text{th}}
root of unity, then
\mu_{p}^{\otimes n}\cong\mathbb{F}_{p}
(non-canonically) and thus the algebra
\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}(G_{\mathbb{K}},\mathbb{F}_{p})
is quadratic in this case. Furthermore, the
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-cohomology algebras of
G_{\mathbb{K}}
and its maximal pro-
p
quotient
G_{\mathbb{K}}(p)
agree.

Positselski showed in
[
12
]
, that Theorem
3.1
would follow from the Koszulity of
K^{M}_{\bullet}(\mathbb{K})\otimes\mathbb{F}_{p}
if
G_{\mathbb{K}}
is a pro-
p
group. He posed the following conjectures in
[
14
]
, which were suggested by his previous work:

Conjecture 3.2

(Koszulity Conjecture)
.

For any field
\mathbb{K}
containing a primitive root
p^{\text{th}}
root of unity, the algebra
K^{M}_{\bullet}(\mathbb{K})\otimes\mathbb{F}_{p}
is Koszul.

Conjecture 3.3

(Module Koszulity Conjecture 1)
.

Let
\mathbb{K}
be a field satisfying
1.1
. Define
J_{\mathbb{K}}
to be the kernel of the natural map
\Lambda^{\bullet}(\mathbb{K}^{\times}/(\mathbb{K}^{\times p}))\to K^{M}_{\bullet}(\mathbb{K})\otimes\mathbb{F}_{p}
, then
J_{\mathbb{K}}(2)
is a Koszul module over
\Lambda^{\bullet}(\mathbb{K}^{\times}/(\mathbb{K}^{\times p}))
.

Remark 3.4
.

The Module Koszulity Conjecture 1 implies the Koszulity Conjecture by a simple argument using a change of rings spectral sequence.

Theorem 2 of
[
13
]
shows that the Module Koszulity Conjecture 1 implies the Bogomolov–Positselski Conjecture.

These conjectures are known to hold for some classes of fields (e.g. number fields, local fields (cf.
[
14
]
). Recently, Mináĉ, Pasini, Quadrelli, and Tân made some progress on the first of the above conjectures by showing that for oriented pro-
p
groups
(G,\theta)
of elementary type, the algebra
\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})
has the PBW property and is therefore Koszul (see
[
8
]
). In
[
9
]
they furthermore proved that if the maximal pro-
p
quotient of
G_{\mathbb{K}}
is a mild pro-
p
group, then Conjecture
3.2
is true. In 2020 Snopce and Zalesskii proved that the cohomology algebra of a right-angled Artin pro-
p
group is universally Koszul if and only if it is the maximal pro-
p
Galois group of a field
\mathbb{K}
containing a primitive
p^{\text{th}}
root of unity (see
[
19
]
).

Remark 4.5

Theorem 2 of

For the notion of a conilpotent coalgebra and its cohomology, we refer to

In fact, for the proof of Theorem

Proposition 4.6

Let

The proof follows verbatim the one of

Theorem

Keep the notation of Theorem

), we set

Note that the condition, that

generated in degree

Choose

4.3.
Theorem
B
depends on only three graded cohomology groups

The goal of this section is to show that by dualizing in an appropriate way, it is sufficient to compute three graded cohomology groups to verify the conditions of Theorem

for a homogeneous ideal

of
\Lambda^{\bullet}(V)
with

We will need the following small lemma:

Lemma 4.8
.

Let

be a quadratic

a monomorphism of quadratic

by
\coker(f_{1})^{*}

Proof.

If we consider the long exact sequence of
\mathrm{H}^{\ast}(A,\underline{\phantom{a}})
associated to the exact sequence

, we see that the following sequence is exact for every

and

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{H}^{i+1,i}(A,\coker f)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{H}^{i,i}(A,M)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{H}^{i,i}(A,N)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(M^{!}_{A})_{i}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(N^{!}_{A})_{i}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f^{!}_{i}}

Therefore,

is an epimorphism. To see that

is generated in degree

, we use the long exact sequence for

to get for any

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{H}^{0,i}({A^{!}},\ker f^{!})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{H}^{1,i}({A^{!}},M^{!}_{A})}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha_{i}}

and

for

. For

\mathrm{H}^{0,1}(A,\ker f^{!})=0

\mathrm{H}^{0,0}(A,\ker f^{!})\cong\coker(f_{1})^{*}

Let

be a finite-dimensional

\displaystyle J:=\ker(\mathbb{S}^{\bullet-1}(V^{*})\otimes V^{*}\to\mathbb{S}^{\bullet}(V^{*})).

Notice that

if

and

Proposition 4.9
.

Let

be an ideal of
\Lambda^{\bullet}(V)
such that

and

is a quadratic

-module, then
\mathrm{H}^{i,i+3}(\Lambda^{\bullet}(V),I)=0
if and only if

\displaystyle\mathrm{H}^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{\bullet}(V^{*}),J/\langle W^{*}\rangle)

is concentrated in degree

, where

is interpreted as a graded vector space concentrated in degree

.

Remark 4.10
.

This shows, that three graded cohomology groups are sufficient to determine, whether the conditions of Theorem
B
are satisfied for an ideal

of
\Lambda^{2}(V)
, namely

\displaystyle\mathrm{H}^{0}(\Lambda^{\bullet}(V),I),\quad\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Lambda^{\bullet}(V),I),\quad\text{ and}\quad\mathrm{H}^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{\bullet}(V^{*}),J/\langle W^{*}\rangle).

Proof of
4.9
.

When considering quadratic duals, we suppress the respective algebra in the notation, as the dual is always intended with respect to
\Lambda^{\bullet}(V)
.

We first of all show, that

is the quadratic dual of the
\Lambda^{\bullet}(V)
-module

, where

is defined by

otherwise. By

\displaystyle\mathrm{H}^{i,i+1}(\Lambda^{\bullet}(V),L_{1})\cong\mathrm{H}^{i+1,i+1}(\Lambda^{\bullet}(V),k)=\mathbb{S}^{i+1}(V^{*}).

By the long exact sequence associated to

one deduces

(4.2)

\displaystyle\mathrm{H}^{i,i}(\Lambda^{\bullet}(V),L_{2}(2))\cong\mathrm{H}^{i,i+2}(\Lambda^{\bullet}(V),L_{2})\cong\ker(\mathbb{S}^{i+2}(V^{*})\otimes V^{*}\to\mathbb{S}^{i+3}(V^{*})).

5.
The Module Koszulity Conjecture for oriented pro-
p
groups of elementary type

Definition 5.1
.

A Demushkin group is a finitely generated pro-
p
group
G
of cohomological dimension
2
(i.e.
\mathrm{H}^{i}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})=0
for
i>2
) with
\mathrm{H}^{2}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})\cong\mathbb{F}_{p}
such that the cup product induces a non-degenerate bilinear pairing

\displaystyle\mathrm{H}^{1}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})\otimes\mathrm{H}^{1}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})\to\mathrm{H}^{2}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p}).

It turns out that there is exactly one orientation
\eth
for a Demushkin group that turns
(G,\eth)
into a Kummerian oriented pro-
p
group (see
[
16
]
*Proposition 5.2).

We can now define the class of oriented pro-
p
groups of elementary type.

Definition 5.2
.

Let
\mathcal{ET}_{p}
be the smallest class of oriented pro-
p
groups satisfying the following conditions:

(1)

\mathcal{ET}_{p}
contains
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
with any orientation
\theta:\mathbb{Z}_{p}\to\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
;

(2)

\mathcal{ET}_{p}
contains all Demushkin groups
G
with their canonical orientation
\eth
making
(G,\eth)
into a Kummerian oriented pro-
p
group;

(3)

if
(G_{1},\theta_{1})
,
(G_{2},\theta_{2})\in\mathcal{ET}_{p}
, then
(G_{1}*_{p}G_{2},\theta_{1}*\theta_{2})
is also contained in
\mathcal{ET}_{p}
;

(4)

if
(G,\theta)
is in
\mathcal{ET}_{p}
and
A
is a finitely generated free abelian pro-
p
group, then
(A\rtimes_{\theta}G,\theta\circ\pi_{2})
is also contained in
\mathcal{ET}_{p}
.

An oriented pro-
p
group in
\mathcal{ET}_{p}
is said to be of
elementary type
.

Our goal is to show the following theorem:

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
(G,\theta)
be a torsion-free oriented pro-
p
group of elementary type, then
(\ker\psi_{G}^{\bullet})(2)
is Koszul.

The proof is structured in multiple steps. It is clear that
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
with any torsion-free orientation satisfies the theorem, since
\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}(\mathbb{Z}_{p},\mathbb{F}_{p})\cong\Lambda^{\bullet}(\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{Z}_{p},\mathbb{F}_{p}))
. Next, we prove that the statement is true for Demushkin groups, and we show that the condition is stable under the operations (
3
) and (
4
) of Definition
5.2
.

The condition that
(G,\theta)
is torsion-free only poses a restriction in the case where
p=2
. The image of
\theta_{1}*\theta_{2}
is
\langle\im(\theta_{1}),\im(\theta)_{2}\rangle
, and thus contained in
1+4\mathbb{Z}_{2}
if and only if the images of both
\theta_{1}
and
\theta_{2}
are contained in
1+4\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. Furthermore, (
4
) preserves the image of
\theta
. Thus it is sufficient to start in any case with torsion-free oriented groups, when proving the property for the “building blocks”  of groups of elementary type.

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
(G,\eth)
be a Demushkin group whose natural orientation is torsion-free; then the module
(\ker\psi^{\bullet})(2)
is Koszul over
\Lambda^{\bullet}(\mathrm{H}^{1}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p}))
.

Proof.

Set
V:=\mathrm{H}^{1}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})
and define
L_{k}
as in the proof of Proposition
4.9
. We get a short exact sequence of
\Lambda^{\bullet}(V)
-modules.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces I:=(\ker\psi^{\bullet})(2)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L_{2}(2)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{F}_{p}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}

Since
L_{2}(2)
and
\mathbb{F}_{p}
are both Koszul modules, the long exact sequence for
\mathrm{H}^{*}(\Lambda^{\bullet}(V),\underline{\phantom{a}})
shows that
\mathrm{H}^{i}({\Lambda^{\bullet}(V)},I)
is concentrated in degrees
i
and
i+1
. To show that we have vanishing in degree
i+1
, we use the following diagram with exact top row:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{H}^{i,i+1}(\Lambda^{\bullet}(V),I)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{H}^{i+1,i+1}(\Lambda^{\bullet}(V),\mathbb{F}_{p})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{H}^{i+1,i+1}(\Lambda^{\bullet}(V),L_{2}(2))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{S}^{i+1}(V^{*})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{S}^{i+2}(V^{*})\otimes V^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\ref{eq:truncatedDual})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha_{i+1}}

Thus we can conclude that

is Koszul if and only if
\alpha_{i+1}
is injective for all
i
.

Since the map
\alpha
comes from taking quadratic duals of
L_{2}(2)\to\mathbb{F}_{p}
, it is induced by the dual of the multiplication map
\Lambda^{2}(V)\to\mathbb{F}_{p}
. Using
[
10
, Proposition 3.9.16]
, we can choose a basis

of

, such that

\displaystyle 1=\chi_{i}\cup\chi_{i+1}=-\chi_{i+1}\cup\chi_{i}\qquad\text{for all }i=1,...,d-1

and the product
\chi_{i}\cup\chi_{j}
is
0
in all other cases. If we denote by

the dual basis of
V^{*}
. Then we can write
\alpha_{i+1}
explicitly as

\displaystyle\alpha_{i+1}:\mathbb{S}^{i+1}(V^{*})\to\mathbb{S}^{i+2}(V^{*})\otimes V^{*},\quad f\mapsto\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}(x_{i}f)\otimes x_{i+1}-(x_{i+1}f)\otimes x_{i}.

It is easy to see that
\alpha_{i+1}
is injective by composing it with
\id\otimes\chi_{1}
, where we interpret
\chi_{1}
as an element of

. Thus,
\alpha_{i+1}
is injective and
I
Koszul.
∎

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
(G_{1},\theta_{1})
and

be Kummerian, torsion-free, oriented pro-

\Lambda^{\bullet}(\mathrm{H}^{1}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p}))

For abbreviation, we set

. Then we have

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}

for all

.

By

, we get a short exact sequence of

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}(G_{1}*_{p}G_{2})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}(G_{1})\oplus\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}(G_{2})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{F}_{p}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}

Because

The
\Lambda

Remark 5.7

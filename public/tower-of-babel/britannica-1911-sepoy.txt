SEPOY, the usual English spelling of sipāhī, the Persian and
Urdu term for a soldier of any kind, cf. spahi. The word sipāh,
“army,” from which sipāhi, “soldier,” is derived, corresponds
to the Zend çpādha, Old Persian çpāda, and has also found a
home in the Turkish, Kurdish and Pashto (Pushtu) languages
(see Justi, Handbuch der Zendsprache, p. 303, 6), while its derivative
is used in all Indian vernaculars, including Tamil and
Burmese, to denote a native soldier, in contradistinction to gorā,
“a fair-complexioned (European) soldier.” A sepoy is at the
present day strictly a private soldier in the native infantry of
the Indian army.

b b -axis and c c -axis Knight shift measurements in the superconducting state on ultraclean UTe 2 with T c T_{c} = 2.1 K
H. Matsumura, Y. Takahashi, K. Kinjo, S. Kitagawa, K. Ishida, Y. Tokunaga, H. Sakai, S. Kambe, A. Nakamura, Y. Shimizu, Y. Homma, D. Li, F. Honda, A. Miyake, D. Aoki

Abstract
Knight shifts along the b b and c c axes ( K b K_{b} and K c K_{c} ) at two crystallographically distinct Te sites were measured down to 70 mK using 125 Te nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) on an ultraclean UTe 2 \text{UTe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} single crystal with a superconducting (SC) transition temperature T c T_{\mathrm{c}} = 2.1 K. This was carried out to determine the 𝒅 \bm{d} -vector components, which are the order parameter in the spin-triplet pairing. Although the decrease in K b K_{b} and K c K_{c} is comparable to the theoretical estimation of the SC diamagnetic shielding effect, it is confirmed, by taking the difference between two Knight shifts at the distinct Te sites, that the spin susceptibility along the b b and c c axes decreases in the SC state. Taking into account the large decrease in K a K_{a} in the SC state, we conclude that the 𝒅 \bm{d} vector has components along all three crystal axes.

Immm

Soon after the discovery,

has been considered to be a spin-triplet superconductor, because its normal and superconducting (SC) properties are similar to those of U-based ferromagnetic (FM) superconductors

However, unlike these FM superconductors, UTe

After the discovery, we have measured the

to investigate the spin susceptibility in the SC state, because we consider that the difference between spin-singlet and spin-triplet superconductivity would appear in the spin susceptibility in the SC state.
The Knight shift probing the static field at the nuclear site is one of the most reliable methods to measure the spin susceptibility in the SC state.
In the spin-triplet pairing, the decrease in the spin part of the Knight shift indicates the presence of a

Here, the

We measured the Knight shift in the early-stage sample with the SC transition temperature

\sim 1.6

Taking into account these results, we suggested that the dominant SC state would be

This is consistent with the spin anisotropy in the normal state.
We also investigated how the

[

However, it was found that there is a tiny uranium deficiency in the 1.6 K sample

Quite recently, we reported a reduction in

In the paper, it was clarified that the previous invariant

Owing to improving the quality of the
\text{UTe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}

We reported a large reduction in

[
23

As discussed in the paper, a large Knight shift reduction gives rise to the Pauli-depairing effect if superconductivity were in the spin-singlet pairing.
However, such an effect was not observed at around the Pauli-depairing field

in
\text{UTe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}

Rather, we pointed out that the absence of the Pauli-depairing effect and the large upper critical field

It should be noted that the decrease in

In such a small decrease below

In this paper, we succeeded in separating the Knight shift decrease due to these two different origins by measuring

We show that the decrease in

The decrease in spin susceptibility along the

Next, we discuss the temperature dependence of the spin susceptibility in the SC state, not affected by the SC diamagnetic effect.
In order to eliminate the SC diamagnetic effect in the decrease in
K_{b}
and
K_{c}
, we performed the following analyses.
In general, the total Knight shift along the
\alpha

\alpha
=
b

i

i

\displaystyle K_{\alpha,i}(T,H)=K_{\alpha,i}^{\mathrm{spin}}(T)+K_{\alpha,i}^{\mathrm{orb}}+K_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{dia}}(T,H)

K_{\alpha,i}^{\mathrm{orb}}
is the so-called “orbital” shift, which results from the field-induced orbital electron magnetism and is considered to be constant at low temperatures.

K_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{dia}}(T,H)
is the shift originating from the SC diamagnetism, which results from the Meissner shielding effect.
This gives the fractional change in the average internal field in the sample, and is reasonably assumed to be working at both signals in the same manner, and thus site independent.

K_{\alpha,i}^{\mathrm{spin}}(T)
is the spin part of the Knight shift, which is proportional to the spin susceptibility
\chi^{\mathrm{spin}}

K_{\alpha,i}^{\mathrm{spin}}(T)=A_{\alpha,i}\chi^{\mathrm{spin}}(T)

Here,

\alpha

i
) signal.
In most cases, to derive the change in
K_{\alpha,i}^{\mathrm{spin}}(T)
, the magnitude of
K_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{dia}}(T,H)
was estimated with a theoretical equation
[
30
]
with the SC parameters reported experimentally, but it is generally quite hard to know the exact value and the temperature dependence of
K_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{dia}}(T,H)

In
\text{UTe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
,

can be derived from the site dependence of
K_{\alpha,i}(T)
(
i
= I and II) ;

\displaystyle K_{\alpha,\mathrm{II}}(T)-K_{\alpha,\mathrm{I}}(T)=(A_{\alpha,\mathrm{II}}-A_{\alpha,\mathrm{I}})\chi^{\mathrm{spin}}(T)+const.,

when the Te(I) and Te(II) Knight shifts are scaled to the bulk susceptibility, and several groups reported a good scaling
[

31
,
11

It is noted the

The main result of the present measurements is that the spin susceptibility in all three axes unambiguously decreases in the SC state, although the decrease in the Knight shift along the

The SC gap of the

We consider that the SC gap in

This situation is quite different from the superfluid

Alternatively, the experimental fact of the

-wave, as in the case of UPt

[

,
43
,
44
,

In this case, all

-wave triplet SC states are expressed by the
\bm{d}
vector with all three axis components.
The
f
-wave triplet SC state with point-nodes seems to be also consistent with the present results.

In conclusion, from the measurement of the Knight shift of two
125
Te-NMR signals, we conclude that the spin susceptibility along the
b
and
c

Taking the large reduction of
K_{a}
into account, we conclude that the
\bm{d}
vector has components along all three axes, which gives an important constraint for the SC pairing state.
In addition, the finite component of the
\bm{d}
vector along each axis gives rise to the spin degrees of freedom.
It is important to investigate how the
\bm{d}
vector is changed under external conditions, such as the magnetic field, hydrostatic pressure, and uniaxial strain, which is now in progress.

Appendix: The calculation of spin susceptibility in the SC state

In order to discuss the SC gap structure, we compared the temperature dependence of
\Delta K_{b,\mathrm{II}}^{\mathrm{spin}}
with spin susceptibility in the SC state calculated with three different SC gap models: full gap, point-node gap, and line-node gap.
Temperature dependence of spin susceptibility
\chi(T)

[
46
]
is calculated with

\displaystyle\chi(T)=\int\frac{\mathrm{d}\Omega_{k}}{4\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\xi_{k}\frac{1}{2k_{\mathrm{B}}T}\mathrm{sech}^{2}\left(\frac{\sqrt{\xi_{k}^{2}+\Delta_{k}^{2}(T)}}{2k_{\mathrm{B}}T}\right),

where
\xi_{k}
is the single-particle energy relative to the Fermi energy,

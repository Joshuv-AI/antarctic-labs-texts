Mechanical characteristics and design parameter analysis of spherical hinge structure for swivel bridge
Authors: Wei Guo, Hongxu Yu, Yingsong Li, Wei Tian, Wuxing Chen
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
As the core component of a swivel bridge, the spherical hinge structure exhibits complex mechanical behavior under high pressure, making it challenging to describe accurately through theoretical analysis or on-site monitoring. In this context, this paper systematically examined the mechanical characteristics and key design parameters of a simplified spherical hinge structure using numerical simulations and formula derivation. Additionally, an indoor scaled test was conducted based on a representative spherical hinge structure to further investigate its mechanical properties. The findings indicated that contact pressure along the spherical hinge interface increased radially outward and followed a cubic distribution pattern. The support radius was identified as the most critical factor influencing the maximum stress value within the spherical hinge structure. In the aperture ratio range of 0.042 to 0.083, the upper and lower spherical hinges are unable to achieve coordinated deformation under high pressure, resulting in negative pressure at the edge of the pin hole. Furthermore, for spherical hinge structures with sliders, the presence of the slider introduced additional complexity to the stress state, leading to a coexistence of tension and compression on the surface of the lower spherical hinge. The highest stress occurred between the inner ring slider and the pin hole, an area that requires careful monitoring. The research provides valuable insights for the design of spherical hinges in swivel bridges and can guide the development of spherical hinge structures tailored to different bridge types in the future.

Introduction

In recent years, with advancements in bridge construction technology, the rotational construction method has become an increasingly prominent technique in the field1–3. At the heart of a swivel bridge, the spherical hinge structure plays a crucial role. It is primarily composed of an upper and lower spherical hinge, along with essential accessories such as the pin shaft and sliders4–6. Serving as the central component connecting the superstructure and lower bearing platform, the spherical hinge structure is integral to the central support system. Its main function is to evenly transfer the load of the superstructure to the lower bearing platform7,8. During construction, the rotation speed of the spherical hinge structure is precisely controlled to adjust the bridge’s rotational movement9–12.

The mechanical behavior of the spherical hinge structure under high-pressure is complex, and extensive research has been conducted by scholars both domestically and internationally13–16. Liu et al.17 proposed a critical overturning moment model for the concrete spherical hinge in the rotation process of bridges. The ground stress was calculated using the non-Hertz contact theory, and the model conclusions were verified through on-site monitoring. The results indicate that the non-Hertz method has superiority in predicting overturning resistance. Parsons et al.18 analyzed and solved the displacement of ball hinge under stress based on displacement method. Liu et al.19 used the Box-Behnken design method to design the response surface method (RSM) and derived an optimized design scheme for the spherical hinge structure. By comparing the structural contact stress and rotational traction force before and after optimization, the effectiveness and necessity of the optimization of the spherical hinge structure were verified. The results show that after optimization, the maximum contact stress and rotational traction force of the spherical hinge structure decreased by 13.86% and 8.42% respectively compared to before optimization. Li et al.20 obtained the optimal friction coefficient of the spherical hinge using the finite element analysis method, and evaluated the lubrication and friction performance of the spherical hinge structure through experiments conducted with self-developed tests. The finite element analysis results indicate that the optimal rotational friction coefficient of the spherical hinge structure is 0.031 to 1.131. The experimental results show that the friction coefficient of the graphene lubricating coating is 0.065, the wear scar diameter is 0.79 mm, and the maximum non-slip load is 426 N. Kim and Lee21 focused their research on spherical hinges, investigating the effects of frictional force and traction. The results of the study indicate that the results from strength theory are slightly lower than those from finite element calculations, as they did not consider the influence of displacement and eccentricity of the spherical hinge. Frictional resistance has a significant impact on the stress of spherical hinges. Che22 derived the formula for calculating the vertical compressive stress on the contact surface of a spherical hinge by using the concentrated force theory of half-space boundary action, and clarified the characteristics of stress distribution. Wei23 built a fine finite element model of the rotating structure through finite element analysis software, carried out static analysis on it, and determined the stress distribution on the contact surface of the spherical hinge.

Although extensive research has been conducted on the stress characteristics of spherical hinge structures24–27, a unified conclusion regarding the stress distribution patterns has yet to be reached. Current theoretical analyses are relatively simplistic and do not accurately capture the stress state of spherical joint structures. Therefore, this paper focuses on the swivel bridge over the Beijing-Harbin Railway on Xingfu Avenue in Changchun. Through a combination of numerical simulation and theoretical analysis, it characterizes the mechanical behavior of the spherical hinge structure and validates the effectiveness of both the numerical simulation and commonly used theoretical formulas using field data. Based on this validated simulation approach, the study then analyzes the impact of key design parameters on the stress state of the spherical hinge. Finally, a scaled model of the prevalent spherical hinge + slider structure is developed to further investigate the stress characteristics. The detailed process is illustrated in Fig. 1.

Actual swivel bridge descriptions

Swivel bridge structure

The target bridge in this study is the Xingfu Road Swivel Bridge located in Changchun City, Jilin Province. This swivel bridge crosses the Beijing-Harbin Railway, one of China’s busiest lines. The bridge features a 50 m + 50 m prestressed concrete box girder with variable height in a T-shaped rigid frame configuration, measuring 100 m in total length and weighing 12,157.5 tons. During construction, the pile foundation, turntable, and piers are first completed. Brackets are then erected along the railway direction, followed by concrete pouring for the main girder. Afterward, the beams are rotated into their designated positions, and the turntable is sealed with concrete. Finally, the brackets are repositioned to construct the cast-in-place section of the side span. The bridge rotates at a designed speed of 0.02 rad/min over an 81.6°, taking approximately 71.2 min. A structural schematic of the actual swivel bridge is shown in Fig. 2.

Spherical hinge structure

The spherical hinge structure consists of two discs with matching curvature, each measuring 3900 mm in diameter and 40 mm in thickness. Made of Q345 steel, the spherical hinge is divided into upper and lower sections, forming the core of the rotation system in horizontal rotation construction. The upper spherical hinge connects to the upper turntable, which has a diameter of 1080 mm and a height of 80 mm, while the lower spherical hinge is embedded in the lower bearing. Contact behavior settings

As a critical component of the horizontal swivel bridge, the spherical hinge requires accurate simulation of the contact state between the upper and lower spherical hinges. The quality and accuracy of this contact simulation are crucial for the success of the overall structural numerical analysis. Based on previous research, this study uses penalty friction for tangential contact behavior, a method commonly used in finite element software. For normal behavior, hard contact is specified, with a friction coefficient of 0.06.

Load and boundary constraints

The ideal model assumes that the spherical hinge structure only carries the vertical load transmitted from the upper beam, with no unbalanced moments. In accordance with engineering design requirements, the vertical load from the upper beam is converted into a pressure load of 1.33 MPa, applied to the upper turntable. Additionally, a node set is created at the bottom node of the lower bearing platform to constrain its displacement and rotational degrees of freedom. The lower bearing platform only supports the load transmitted from the upper structure.

Moreover, this study examined the impact of four key design parameters of the spherical hinge structure on its stress state: the curvature radius, support radius, pin hole radius, and pin hole length. Theoretical formula derivation

In simplified mechanics theory of spherical hinge, the frictional force along the tangent direction of the hinge disk primarily affects the frictional torque and rotational traction. This frictional force is largely determined by the friction coefficient and contact pressure, making the contact pressure at the spherical hinge interface a critical factor in analyzing the hinge disk’s force distribution. The contact pressure at this interface aligns with the normal stress generated by the radial forces on the spherical hinge interface. This study derives and analyzes the contact pressure of the spherical hinge interface using an engineering simplification algorithm, the normal distribution force on the boundary of a half-space body, and Hertz contact theory, with the derivation’s accuracy verified through the aforementioned finite element analysis.

Hertz contact theory

Hertzian theory is a theory that describes the contact and pressure distribution of elastomers, where when two elastic bodies come into contact, a pressure is created in the contact area. The distribution of these pressures is usually not uniform, but has a certain shape, as shown in Fig. 7. Hertzian theory generally applies to contact between a ball and a plane, or between two spherical objects, and contact between a cylinder and a plane.

According to Table 4, there are significant differences between the interface contact pressures obtained by the simplified algorithm, the algorithm considering uniform loading on the half-space body, and Hertz theory compared to numerical simulations. The interface contact pressure obtained by the algorithm considering uniform loading on the half-space body increases radially from the inside out along the radius, which is consistent with the distribution form obtained from numerical simulations. However, the interface contact pressure calculated by this algorithm is much smaller than the simulated values. This is because in the model of the half-space body subjected to uniform loading, the half-space body is assumed to be constrained by a plane in one direction and infinite elastic in other directions, with radial stress mainly dependent on depth. However, actual spherical hinge structures have boundaries. The maximum interface contact pressure calculated by Hertz theory for the spherical hinge is 27.62 MPa, and it remains almost unchanged within a finite support cord length, with an error of 10.57% compared to the maximum pressure value obtained from numerical simulations. This is because, according to the previous formula derivation, the maximum pressure stress occurs at the center of the contact surface in Hertz theory. Since the curvature radii of the upper and lower spherical hinges are very close and the length of the support cord (mn) used for calculating the contact pressure at different positions of the spherical hinge is much smaller than the radius (a), it leads to the pressure values within the range of the spherical hinge interface being equal to the maximum pressure stress. In summary, the simplified algorithm and the algorithm considering uniform loading on the half-space body differ significantly from the finite element results. The simplified algorithm tends to be conservative, and its use in engineering may lead to deviation in results. The algorithm considering uniform loading on the half-space body can effectively describe the distribution form of interface contact pressure in spherical hinge structures, while Hertz theory can simply predict the maximum pressure stress on the interface of spherical hinge structures.

Laboratory scaled model test results

Through the loading test on the spherical hinge structure model of the rotating body, the real-time stress data of the lower spherical hinge was obtained and plotted, as shown in Fig. 18.

Fig. 18The stress values of each measuring point of the ball hinge surface under different loads.

The stress values of each measuring point of the ball hinge surface under different loads.

As shown in Fig. 18, the data acquired at various measurement points have both positive and negative values, indicating that the lower spherical hinge undergoes a complex force state combining tension and compression under loading. This is jointly caused by the squeezing between sliders and the extension of the spherical hinge under loading. The stress on the lower spherical hinge increases continuously with the increase of load, indicating a certain correlation between stress on the lower spherical hinge and the load. Among them, the measurement point with the greatest stress variation is QM22, with a stress of 2.709 MPa under a 150KN load, located in the region between inner sliders and the pin pre-boring hole.

The stress at the position where the hinge surface of the lower ball is in contact with the slide block is large, especially in the area where the side of the slide block is in contact with stress concentration. The stress around the reserved hole of the pin is small, which also shows that the pin mainly plays the role of positioning in the whole structure. The regional stress between the outer ring slider and the outer edge of the lower ball hinge surface is small, and the influence of external load is small, which belongs to the non-significant region of the measuring point. By extracting the compressive stress data of the lower ball hinge surface, it can be found that the lower ball hinge is not in an ideal state of complete compression, except for the area in contact with the slider, the whole is in a state of tension, which may be due to the local deformation of the lower ball hinge after the load is transferred to the lower ball hinge through various dispersed sliders.

Under the action of load, the overall surface shape of the lower ball hinge structure is distorted, and the local subsidence occurs, which leads to the coexistence of tension and compression stress on the lower ball hinge surface. The maximum contact pressure appears on the edge of the slider installation reserve, and all point to the inner position of the ball hinge axis, and the maximum contact stress of the outer ring is always greater than the contact pressure of the inner ring. This is because under the action of self-weight and external load, the sliding block is subjected to the shear force pointing to the central axis, and the farther the outer ring sliding block is from the central axis, the greater the shear stress pointing to the central axis. In conclusion, the stress distribution on the lower spherical hinge is more complex compared to other positions, closely related to the direction of patches, and shows minor variations.

Influence analysis of curvature radius on spherical hinge

To explore the stress distribution of the spherical hinge structure under different curvature radii, this paper selected spherical hinge structures with curvature radii of 4000 mm, 6000 mm, 8000 mm, 10,000 mm, 12,000 mm, and infinite. Following the calculation method described earlier, the stress states of the spherical hinge structures were computed. The maximum contact pressure occurs at the circumference of the spherical hinge interface and increases continuously with the curvature radius. When the curvature radius is 4000 mm, the maximum contact pressure at the spherical hinge interface is 23.53 MPa, while for an infinite curvature radius (i.e., a flat surface), the maximum contact pressure at the spherical hinge interface is 27.24 MPa, which is 15.77% higher. Within the range of curvature radii from 4000 to 8000 mm, the contact pressure at the spherical hinge interface is positive, with the minimum contact pressure occurring at the edge of the pin hole and decreasing gradually with the increase in curvature radius. When the curvature radius of the spherical hinge exceeds 1000 mm, negative pressure appears at the edge of the pin hole and increases with the curvature radius. This may be due to the lower spherical hinge being embedded in the lower bearing pedestal and constrained by the surrounding concrete, resulting in the upper and lower spherical hinges being unable to achieve synchronous coordinated deformation. The closer the spherical hinge interface is to a plane, the more noticeable the bending deformation of the lower spherical hinge at the edge of the pin hole, leading to negative pressure at the edge of the pin hole. The maximum and average contact pressures at the spherical hinge interface gradually increase with the increase in curvature radius. In other words, the closer the spherical hinge interface is to a plane, the greater the friction between the upper and lower spherical hinges. According to the formula, the traction forces required for the rotation of spherical hinge structures under six different curvature radii are calculated as 961KN (4000 mm), 1052KN (6000 mm), 1080KN (8000 mm), 1093KN (10000 mm), 1105KN (12000 mm), and 1143KN (infinite, i.e., flat). From Fig. 21, it can be seen that the Von Mises stress distribution patterns of spherical hinge structures with six different curvature radii are essentially similar, all decreasing radially from the inside out, with the maximum Von Mises stress occurring at the edge of the pinhole and continuously increasing with the curvature radius. When the curvature radius is 4000 mm, the maximum Von Mises stress in the spherical hinge structure is 23.53 MPa, and when the curvature radius is infinite, i.e., flat, the maximum Von Mises stress at the spherical hinge interface is 27.24 MPa, which is relatively increased by 15.77%. The phenomenon of stress concentration at the edge of the hole can also be explained by the theory of elasticity, which considers stress concentration at the edge of the hole as a local phenomenon. Due to the presence of the hole, the stress and deformation states near the hole are completely different from those without a hole. In summary, the curvature radius of the spherical hinge interface has a significant effect on the stress state of the spherical hinge structure. The traction force required for the rotation of the spherical hinge also gradually increases.

To explore the stress patterns of spherical hinge structures with different support radii, this study selected spherical hinge structures with six support radii of 450 mm, 1200 mm, 1950 mm, 2200 mm, 2700 mm, and 3450 mm, respectively. According to the calculation method described earlier, the stress states of the spherical hinge structures were determined. The contour maps of contact pressure at the spherical hinge interface for the six support radii are shown in Fig. 22.

As the support radius increases, the maximum contact pressure at the spherical hinge interface decreases accordingly. When the support radius is 450 mm, the maximum contact pressure at the spherical hinge interface is 545.4 MPa, exceeding the yield strength of Q345 steel. Therefore, the spherical hinge structure with a support radius of 450 mm cannot meet the actual load requirements. Within the range of support radii from 1950 to 2700 mm, negative pressure appears at the edge of the pinhole, indicating that the upper and lower spherical hinges cannot achieve coordinated deformation under the action of the load. Combining the previous discussion on the radius of the pinhole, this study defines the ratio of the pinhole radius to the support radius as the aperture ratio ( f ). By fitting the relevant data, it is found that when (0.042 < f < 0.083), the upper and lower spherical hinges cannot achieve coordinated deformation under the action of the load, and negative pressure is likely to appear at the edge of the pinhole. It can be seen that the traction force required for the rotation of the spherical hinge structure does not show a clear pattern with increasing support radius. This may be due to the increase in contact area at the spherical hinge interface with increasing support radius, leading to a decrease in average contact pressure at the spherical hinge interface. When the aperture ratio is 0.228, the maximum Von Mises stress value of the spherical hinge structure is 367.3 MPa, which exceeds the material’s allowable stress of 345 MPa, indicating structural failure. The maximum Von Mises stress occurs at the outer edge of the spherical hinge structure, and there is no stress concentration phenomenon at the edge of the reserved hole for the pin shaft. When the aperture ratio is 0.022, the Von Mises stress value at the edge of the reserved hole for the pin shaft is 6.553 MPa, and the Von Mises stress value in the middle region of the hinge plate is 6.913 MPa. This study fits the maximum Von Mises stress value of the spherical hinge structure with the support radius and calculates that when the support radius is less than 0.48 m, the spherical hinge structure fails and cannot meet the actual load. According to the engineering simplified algorithm, the minimum support radius for this project is 0.335 m, which is significantly smaller than the minimum support radius calculated by fitting. The shear design strength of steel is generally 0.58 times the tensile design strength, and this study takes 0.75 of the shear design strength of Q345 steel as the strength safety allowance value for the steel of the spherical hinge. According to the fitting equation of the maximum Von Mises stress value of the spherical hinge structure and the support radius, the support radius that satisfies the allowable strength of the spherical hinge structure is calculated to be 1.11 m, which is less than the actual design support radius of 1.95 m for the spherical hinge structure. For the load of this project, when the design support radius of the rotating spherical hinge structure is greater than 1.11 m, there will be excess support capacity. In summary, the support radius significantly affects the stress state of the spherical hinge structure. As the support radius increases, the maximum contact pressure and Von Mises stress value at the spherical hinge interface decrease accordingly. When the support radius is less than 0.48 m, the spherical hinge structure begins to fail and cannot meet the actual load.

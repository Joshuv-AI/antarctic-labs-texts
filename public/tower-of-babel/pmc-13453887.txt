First in vivo implementation of a coronet-shaped reinforced graft for valve-sparing aortic root replacement (VSARR) in an adult ovine model.

Central Message

A coronet-shaped reinforced graft can simplify and standardize key geometric steps of VSARR in a large-animal in vivo model.

Valve-sparing aortic root replacement (VSARR) offers an attractive alternative to composite valved-conduit replacement in selected patients with annuloaortic ectasia and pliable cusps. Despite favorable outcomes in expert series, broader adoption remains limited by procedural complexity and challenges in standardizing root geometry. Reimplantation provides basal ring stabilization but typically requires extensive soft-tissue dissection and construction of 2 suture lines—one at the subvalvular plane and another for hemostasis—steps that are particularly sensitive to surgeon experience. Remodeling is technically simpler but lacks basal plane stabilization, raising concerns for progressive annular dilatation and late aortic regurgitation.1

To address these limitations, we developed a coronet-shaped 3-dimensional reinforced ringed graft preassembled with a woven polyester conduit.2,3 The conceptual design aims to retain the procedural flow of single-suture-line construction while providing basal plane stabilization that conceptually parallels reimplantation physiology. After ex vivo hemodynamic validation, we performed, to our knowledge, the first in vivo implantation in a terminal adult ovine model and report operative feasibility and immediate functional performance. This study was conducted as a terminal large-animal experiment with Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee approval at Stanford University (protocol no. 33373; approval date: first approved July 1, 2019, and has been renewed on July 15, 2021, and June 14, 2022). Consent was not applicable.

Device Construction and Design Rationale

The device is composed of an internal coronet-shaped titanium frame (Figure 1, A) covered with silicone (Figure 1, B) and integrated with a woven polyester graft (Figure 1, C). The coronet geometry was designed to mimic the valvuloaortic junction skeleton, including a sharp commissural apposition angle and a broad U-shaped contour at the nadirs. Consistent with anatomic and modeling work suggesting favorable hemodynamics when commissural height is maintained relative to annular diameter, the frame was constructed with a height-to-diameter ratio of approximately 1.0 and symmetric commissural post orientation (120°).4 Devices were prepared in multiple diameters (20-28 mm, Figure 1, D) to permit size matching to the measured annulus of the subject. Operative Technique, Intraoperative Assessment and Terminal Protocol

Standardized intraoperative procedures and monitoring were undertaken as previously described.5 A healthy adult Dorset male sheep (Joe Pozzi Ranch) weighing 67 kg underwent median sternotomy under general anesthesia. After systemic heparinization with a target activated clotting time >480 seconds, cardiopulmonary bypass (CPB) was initiated through ascending aortic and right atrial cannulations. The ascending aorta was crossclamped, and myocardial arrest was achieved with antegrade cold del Nido cardioplegia (1 L) delivered through the aortic root.

The aortic root was mobilized circumferentially toward the virtual basal ring. The native aortic annulus diameter was measured at 23 mm and we selected a device of 22 mm in its diameter. Coronary buttons were harvested in standard fashion. For device implantation, multiple horizontal mattress sutures using unpledgetted 2-0 braided polyester were placed circumferentially at the valvuloventricular junction on the aortic side, consistent with the ex vivo device deployment strategy.2,3 These sutures are then passed through the lower rim of the device in a corresponding fashion. The device is subsequently lowered onto the aortic root along the preplaced sutures and secured by sequential tying, establishing a single circumferential basal suture line (Figure 2). Commissural orientation and height are maintained by the predefined geometry of the coronet-shaped frame rather than individual commissural resuspension. Coronary buttons were reimplanted. Distal anastomosis was completed, followed by standard deairing and crossclamp removal (Video 1). Total aortic crossclamp time was 83 minutes. The animal was rewarmed and weaned from CPB without hemodynamic instability or need for escalation of inotropic support. Intraoperative transaortic echocardiography performed after separation from CPB demonstrated preserved cusp mobility, symmetric coaptation, and absence of aortic regurgitation on long-axis and short-axis views (Video 2). After completion of data acquisition, humane euthanasia was performed in accordance with the approved terminal protocol. Discussion

This report documents first in vivo implantation of a coronet-shaped 3-dimensional reinforced graft platform for VSARR. The previous ex vivo experiment demonstrated that a single mattress suture line over a reinforced basal scaffold can achieve aortic valve competence comparable with reimplantation while reducing procedural time relative to conventional double-layer reimplantation.2,3 Unlike conventional reimplantation, where annular stabilization relies predominantly on subannular fixation and commissural suspension, basal stabilization is achieved through a combination of a single circumferential mattress suture line at the valvuloventricular junction and structural support provided by the rigid coronet-shaped frame in this new technique. The present configuration leverages the predefined geometry of the frame to maintain the functional basal plane without the need for additional commissural resuspension. The present in vivo experience extends that work by demonstrating that the device can be seated and secured under CPB in a large-animal setting and that immediate postbypass valve competence can be confirmed by intraoperative echocardiography.

There are several technical consideration in this novel VSARR. The silicone-covered frame provides a cushioning interface that may enhance suture apposition and contribute to effective hemostasis. In the event of bleeding, management is conceptually similar to that in remodeling procedures, with additional sutures used for control.

This terminal model was intentionally limited to assessing technical feasibility and acute valve function rather than chronic durability. The device's rigid basal scaffold may influence valvoventricular dynamics and root compliance, and the current iteration uses symmetric commissural post orientation that may not accommodate all forms of cusp asymmetry. The rigid ring may potentially distort the functional 3-dimensional annulus during implantation. Although some accommodation may occur through tissue compliance, the nonplanar nature of the basal ring remains an important consideration. These considerations motivate future chronic survival studies incorporating clinically relevant disease models and quantitative postoperative imaging. Also, patient-specific device design based on preoperative imaging may represent a future direction.

In the present ovine model, the native ovine root was structurally normal, and the findings may not directly extrapolate to diseased human anatomy. With relatively small root dimensions in particular, proximity between the annulus and coronary ostia posed a potential constraint. Although this may be less pronounced in dilated human aortic roots, future device iterations will require refinement toward a lower-profile scaffold. The current device represents an early-stage research prototype based on an assumption of symmetric commissural geometry, which may not fully capture native anatomic variability. Further development toward clinical translation will therefore require iterative design refinement to accommodate asymmetric configurations, along with careful consideration of manufacturability and regulatory requirements.

In conclusion, in a terminal adult ovine model, VSARR using a coronet-shaped reinforced graft was feasible under CPB and achieved immediate echocardiographic valve competence after weaning. These results support further translational evaluation in chronic survival models.

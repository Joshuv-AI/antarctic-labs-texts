Effective speed approach for scalar field propagation
Kevin Restrepo Tobón, Antonio Enea Romano

Abstract
We study the propagation of a constant speed gaussian scalar field wave-packet (GWP) in Minkowski space, showing that the energy conditions are violated for superluminal speed. We then apply the effective speed approach to the GWP propagation, deriving the corresponding effective metric, effective Lagrangian and effective stress-energy tensor, showing that the null, weak and strong energy conditions are satisfied.



I
Introduction

Cosmological perturbations play an fundamental role in understanding the evolution of the Universe, since they allow to model inhomogeneities from which the large scale structure has formed.
It has been shown
10
how it is possible to encode the effects of interaction of cosmological perturbations with other fields in an effective speed, but this effective approach can be applied also to other fields satisfying a similar equation of motion. In this paper we apply the effective speed approach to a scalar field propagating in Minkowski space, specifically to a gaussian wave-packet propagating at constant speed.
We compute the effective speed, and the effective metric, showing this effective description provides equations which admit the same solutions equations of the equations with source term.
We then test different energy conditions, comparing the result obtained with the Minkowski metric and the effective metric.

II
Effective source of a gaussian wave-packet

The Minkowski metric in in spherical coordinates takes the form

\displaystyle\eta_{\mu\nu}=\begin{pmatrix}c^{2}&0\\
0&-\Omega_{ij}\end{pmatrix}\,;\qquad\Omega_{ij}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\
0&r^{2}&0\\
0&0&r^{2}\sin^{2}\theta\end{pmatrix}\,,

(1)

for a scalar field with Lagragian

\mathcal{L}=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\mu}\partial^{\mu}\phi=\frac{1}{2}\left[\frac{\dot{\phi}^{2}}{c^{2}}-(\nabla\phi)^{2}\right]\,,

(2)

the equation of motion is given the D’alambert operator

\Box\phi=\frac{1}{c^{2}}\ddot{\phi}-\nabla^{2}\phi=0\,,

(3)

where the dot represents a time derivative,
\nabla^{2}=\partial_{i}\partial^{i}
is the 3-dimensional space Laplacian and
c
is the speed of light.
Given a Gaussian wave-packet of the form

\phi_{g}(t,r)=\frac{\phi_{0}}{r}\exp{\left[-\frac{(r-c_{g}t)^{2}}{\sigma^{2}}\right]}\,,

(4)

we define the function
\Pi_{g}(t,\mathbf{r})
as

\displaystyle\Pi_{g}(t,r)=c^{2}\,\Box\phi_{g}=\frac{2\phi_{g}(t,r)}{\sigma^{4}}\left(c_{g}^{2}-c^{2}\right)\left[2(r-c_{g}t)^{2}-\sigma^{2}\right]\,.

(5)

By construction
\phi_{g}
is a solution of the inhomogeneous equation

\ddot{\phi}_{g}-c^{2}\nabla^{2}\phi_{g}=\Pi_{g}\,,

(6)

which can be interpreted as the specification of the source term which is necessary to obtain a Gaussian wave-packet propagation in Minkowoski space, at a constant speed
c_{g}
.
The corresponding Lagrangian density is

\mathcal{L}=\frac{1}{2}\left[\frac{\dot{\phi}^{2}}{c^{2}}-(\nabla\phi)^{2}+2\frac{\Pi\phi}{c^{2}}\right]\,.

(7)

\displaystyle T_{\mu\nu}k^{\mu}k^{\nu}

\displaystyle=\left(\partial_{\mu}\phi\partial_{\nu}\phi-g_{\mu\nu}\mathcal{L}\right)k^{\mu}k^{\nu}

IV
Effective speed approach

We can study the solution of eq.(
6

c_{e}
. In the following we review the process of constructing the effective equation and

, and then we apply it to GWP.

Let’s denote as
\hat{\phi}
a solution of eq.(
6
) and with
\hat{\Pi}
the r.h.s term obtained substituting
\hat{\phi}
. We can manipulate the equation as following

\ddot{\hat{\phi}}-c^{2}\nabla^{2}\hat{\phi}-\hat{\Pi}=\partial_{t}\left[\dot{\hat{\phi}}-\int\hat{\Pi}(\eta,\boldsymbol{r})\mathrm{d}\eta\right]-c^{2}\nabla^{2}\hat{\phi}=\partial_{t}\left[\dot{\hat{\phi}}\left(1-\frac{\hat{g}}{\dot{\hat{\phi}}}\right)\right]-c^{2}\nabla^{2}\hat{\phi}=0\,,

(16)

where we have defined
\hat{g}(t,\boldsymbol{r})=\int\hat{\Pi}(\eta,\boldsymbol{r})\textrm{d}\eta
. After defining the effective speed as

c_{e}^{2}=c^{2}\left(1-\frac{\hat{g}}{\dot{\hat{\phi}}}\right)^{-1}\,,

(17)

the equation can be re-written as

\partial_{t}\left(\dot{\hat{\phi}}\frac{c^{2}}{c^{2}_{e}}\right)-c^{2}\nabla^{2}\hat{\phi}=0\,,

(18)

from which we finally get

\ddot{\hat{\phi}}-2\frac{\dot{c}_{e}}{c_{e}}\dot{\hat{\phi}}-c_{e}^{2}\nabla^{2}\hat{\phi}=0\,.

(19)

A solution of eq.(
6
) is by construction also a solution of eq.(
19
).

IV.1
Effective metric

It is possible to defined an effective metric such that the lagrangian of a free field gives the effective equation. This metric takes the form

\displaystyle g^{\mathrm{eff}}_{\mu\nu}=\begin{pmatrix}c_{e}&0\\
0&-\frac{1}{c_{e}}\Omega_{ij}\end{pmatrix}\,.

(20)

For the free field Lagrangian

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{eff}}=\frac{1}{2}g_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\phi\partial_{\nu}\phi\,,

(21)

the Lagrange equations give

\displaystyle\Box\phi=\frac{1}{\sqrt{-g_{\textrm{eff}}}}\partial_{\mu}(\sqrt{-g_{\textrm{eff}}}\partial^{\mu}\phi)=\ddot{\phi}-2\frac{\dot{c_{e}}}{c_{e}}\dot{\phi}-c_{e}^{2}\nabla^{2}\phi=0.

(22)

IV.2
Effective energy-momentum tensor

From variation of the effective action with respect to the effective metric we obtain the effective energy momentum tensor

\displaystyle T^{\rm{eff}}_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\sqrt{|g_{\textrm{eff}}|}}\frac{\delta S_{\textrm{eff}}}{\delta g_{\textrm{eff}}^{\mu\nu}}=\partial_{\mu}\phi\partial_{\nu}\phi-g^{\rm{eff}}_{\mu\nu}\mathcal{L}_{\rm{eff}}

(23)

\displaystyle=\partial_{\mu}\phi\partial_{\nu}\phi-\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}c_{e}&0\\
0&-\frac{1}{c_{e}}\Omega_{ij}\end{pmatrix}\left[\frac{\dot{\phi}^{2}}{c_{e}}-c_{e}(\nabla\phi)^{2}\right].

V
Effective approach for the Gaussian wave-packet

Substituting
\phi_{g}
and
\Pi_{g}
into the definition of
c_{e}
gives

\displaystyle c_{e}^{2}=c^{2}\left(1-\frac{\int\Pi_{g}\mathrm{dt}}{\dot{\phi_{g}}}\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle=c^{2}\left[1-\frac{2\phi_{g}(c_{g}^{2}-c^{2})(r-c_{g}t)/(c_{g}\sigma^{2})}{2\phi_{g}c_{g}(r-c_{g}t)/\sigma^{2}}\right]^{-1}

(24)

\displaystyle=c^{2}\left(1-\frac{c_{g}^{2}-c^{2}}{c_{g}^{2}}\right)^{-1}=c^{2}\left(\frac{c^{2}}{c_{g}^{2}}\right)^{-1}=c_{g}^{2}\,,

In this case the effective speed is the quantity
c_{g}
appearing in the GWP, i.e. it is the speed of the wave front, confirming the physical meaning of the effective speed.

V.1
Energy conditions for the effective stress energy tensor

V.1.1
NEC

Defining he null vector
k^{\mu}
in terms of the effective metric, i.e. assuming
g_{\mu\nu}^{\textrm{eff}}k^{\mu}k^{\nu}=0
, we obtain

T^{\textrm{eff}}_{\mu\nu}k^{\mu}k^{\nu}=(\partial_{\mu}\phi k^{\mu})^{2}\,,

(25)

which is always positive, then the NEC is always satisfied.

V.1.2
WEC

Defining
t^{\mu}
as a timelike vector in terms of the effective metric
g^{\textrm{eff}}
, i.e. assuming
t^{2}=g^{\textrm{eff}}_{\mu\nu}t^{\mu}t^{\nu}>0
, the WEC gives

\displaystyle T^{\rm{eff}}_{\mu\nu}t^{\mu}t^{\nu}

\displaystyle=\left(\partial_{\mu}\phi\partial_{\nu}\phi-g^{\textrm{eff}}_{\mu\nu}\mathcal{L}_{\textrm{eff}}\right)t^{\mu}t^{\nu}

(26)

\displaystyle=\left(\partial_{\mu}\phi t^{\mu}\right)^{2}-t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\textrm{eff}}\geq 0.

A general unitary timelike vector, defined by the condition
g^{\textrm{eff}}_{\mu\nu}t^{\mu}t^{\nu}=1
, can be parameterized as

t^{\mu}=\frac{\sqrt{c_{e}}}{\sqrt{1-\beta^{2}}}(\frac{1}{c_{e}},\beta n^{i})\,,

(27)

where again,
|\beta|<1
and
n^{i}=(n_{r},n_{\theta},n_{\phi})
are the components of a vector satisfying
n^{i}n^{j}\Omega_{ij}=1
. In the appendix
B
it is shown that the WEC is satisfied for any vector
n^{i}
and value of
\beta
.

V.1.3
SEC

Assuming a timelike vector in terms of the effective metric, i.e. satisfying the condition
t^{2}=g^{\textrm{eff}}_{\mu\nu}t^{\mu}t^{\nu}=1
, the SEC takes the form

(T^{\textrm{eff}}_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}T^{\textrm{eff}}g^{\textrm{eff}}_{\mu\nu})t^{\mu}t^{\nu}=(\partial_{\mu}\phi t^{\mu})^{2}\,,

(28)

which is always positive, then the SEC is always satisfied.

VI
Conclusions

We have studied the energy conditions for a gaussian wave-packet propagating at constant speed in Minkowski space, confirming
13
;
4
;
3
;
11
;
1
;
5
that superluminal speeds lead to a violation of the energy conditions. We have assumed the field satisfies a wave equation with d’Alambert operator determined by the Minkowski metric, and computed the source term which gives the wave-packet propagation at an arbitrary constant speed. Once this source term has been computed, we have then calculated the corresponding Lagrangian, and from it the stress-energy tensor, assuming the Minkowski metric.

We have then formulated an equivalent mathematical description of the system, based on the calculation of the effective speed, and effective metric.
This effective description gives equations of motion which admit the same solutions, but without source terms. The effective metric allows to obtain an effective Lagrangian which gives the effective equation of motions without source term.
We have then computed the effective stress-energy tensor from the effective Lagrangian and metric, showing that it does not violate the energy conditions.
Since the effective description of the system gives the same solutions of the equations of motion, observable quantities are the same, but the use of the effective metric allows to avoid the violation of the energy conditions.

Threshold cusp structures in multi-channel scattering
Tetsuo Hyodo

Abstract
We study the behavior of the cusp structures focusing on the isospin symmetry breaking effects. The properties of the exotic hadrons are reflected in the shape of the cusp structures. In realistic hadron scatterings, the threshold energies of the isospin partners appear within a small energy region. For a detailed analysis of such systems, it is essential to study the behavior of the cusp structures arising at two closely spaced thresholds. In this work, we introduce a convenient formulation of the scattering amplitude and demonstrate that the cusp structures at two nearby thresholds are related through the isospin symmetry.

In certain two-body hadronic systems, two thresholds may be located close to each other due to the isospin symmetry. In such cases, the two cusp structures appear in a narrow energy region. Here, we study the behavior of these cusp structures under the isospin-broken and isospin-symmetric cases, taking the

2
Formulation

Before considering the effects of the isospin breaking, we first discuss the general form of the multi-channel scattering amplitude and analyze the behavior of the threshold cusp structures. The scattering channels are labeled as channels
1,2,\cdots,N
, in ascending order of their threshold energies. The relative momenta in channels
1,2,\cdots,N
are denoted by
p_{1}(E),p_{2}(E),\cdots,p_{N}(E)
, respectively. The
s
-wave scattering amplitude is expressed as

\displaystyle f(E)=\left[\hat{K}^{-1}-i\hat{p}(E)\right]^{-1},

(1)

where
\hat{K}
is an
N\times N
real symmetric matrix known as the
K
-matrix
[
9
]
, and
\hat{p}(E)
is a diagonal matrix with
(i,i)
components given by
p_{i}\ (i=1,2,\cdots,N)
.

We focus on the near-threshold energy region of channel
N
and set the energy origin
(E=0)
at this threshold. Because only the linear terms in
p_{N}(E)
contribute to the cusp shape near the threshold of channel
N
, we neglect higher-order terms proportional to
p_{N}^{2}\propto E
. Accordingly,
K
-matrix
\hat{K}
and
p_{i}\ (i<N)
in Eq. (
1
) are taken as constants. To analyze the cusp behavior, we represent the
(1,1)
component of the scattering amplitude near the threshold of channel
N
as

\displaystyle f_{11}(E)=f_{11}^{0}\frac{1+ib_{11}^{(N)}p_{N}(E)}{1+ia_{N}p_{N}(E)},

(2)

which provides a convenient form for describing the cusp structure. Here,
a_{N}
represents the scattering length in channel
N
, and
b_{11}^{(N)}
is a complex constant with
\imaginary[b_{11}^{(N)}]\leq 0
, as required by unitarity. Both
a_{N}
and
b_{11}^{(N)}
are determined by the elements of
K
-matrix and the constant momenta
p_{i}\ (i<N)
. The factor
f_{11}^{0}
denotes the amplitude at the threshold of channel
N

(E=0)
.

Using Eq. (
2
), the
s
-wave cross section near the threshold can be expanded in terms of
p_{N}(E)
:

\displaystyle\sigma_{11}^{s}(E)

\displaystyle=4\pi|f_{11}^{0}|^{2}\left(1+2\imaginary[a_{N}-b_{11}^{(N)}]p_{N}(E)+\mathcal{O}(p_{N}^{2}(E))\right),\quad(E>0\ \text{above the threshold}),

(3)

\displaystyle\sigma_{11}^{s}(E)

\displaystyle=4\pi|f_{11}^{0}|^{2}\left(1+2\real[a_{N}-b_{11}^{(N)}]\kappa_{N}(E)+\mathcal{O}(\kappa_{N}^{2}(E))\right),\quad(E<0\ \text{below the threshold}),

with
\kappa_{N}(E)=-ip_{N}(E)
which is real because
p_{N}(E)
becomes pure imaginary for
E<0
. Note that the behavior of the cross section must be treated above and below the threshold separately. When we use the energy
E
as a variable, the slopes of the cross section diverge at the threshold. Therefore, the shape of the cusp is determined only by the signs of
\real[a_{N}-b_{11}^{(N)}]
and
\imaginary[a_{N}-b_{11}^{(N)}]
. Depending on their signs, four distinct types of cusp structures may arise, as shown in Fig.
1
.

3
\Lambda N
-
\Sigma N
scattering

As a representative example, we consider the
\Lambda N
-
\Sigma N
scattering system with the total charge
Q=+1
. The scattering channels
\Lambda p
,
\Sigma^{+}n
, and
\Sigma^{0}p
are labeled as channels 1, 2, and 3, respectively. Due to the isospin symmetry, the threshold energies of channels 2(
\Sigma^{+}n
) and 3(
\Sigma^{0}p
) are very close to each other, making this system particularly suitable for studying the isospin-breaking effects on the cusp structures. We define the energy difference between the thresholds of 2 and 3 as
\Delta
, which is about 2 MeV.

To analyze the
\Sigma N
cusp behavior, we examine the
(1,1)
component of the scattering amplitude. For
\Delta\neq 0
, two distinct cusps emerge at the separate thresholds of channels 2 and 3. We also consider the isospin symmetric case by taking the limit
\Delta\to 0
. In this case, the thresholds of channels 2(
\Sigma^{+}n
) and 3(
\Sigma^{0}p
) coincide, and the corresponding channel is denoted as II(
\Sigma N
). Thus, only a single cusp appears at the II threshold.

In our analysis, the
K
-matrix for
\Lambda N
-
\Sigma N
scattering is assumed to respect the isospin symmetry, which reduces the number of independent parameters. Namely, the
K
-matrix for
\Lambda N
-
\Sigma N
scattering is described by four parameters:

\displaystyle K

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}C_{1}&\sqrt{2}C_{4}&C_{4}\\
\sqrt{2}C_{4}&C_{2}&\sqrt{2}(C_{2}-C_{3})\\
C_{4}&\sqrt{2}(C_{2}-C_{3})&C_{3}\end{pmatrix}.

(4)

The difference of the momenta
p_{2}(E)
and
p_{3}(E)
, which arises due to
\Delta
, introduces the isospin-breaking effects into the scattering amplitude
f_{11}(E)
.

First, we consider the isospin-broken case. According to Eq. (
3
), the shapes of the cusps at the thresholds of channels 2 and 3 are determined by the signs of the real and imaginary parts of
a_{2}-b^{(2)}_{11}
and
a_{3}-b^{(3)}_{11}
, respectively. Using the
K
-matrix components from Eq. (
4
), these quantities can be expanded in terms of
\kappa_{3}(\Delta)
and
p_{2}(\Delta)
:

\displaystyle a_{2}-b^{(2)}_{11}

\displaystyle=2R+X\kappa_{3}(\Delta)+\mathcal{O}(\kappa_{3}^{2}(\Delta))\quad(\text{threshold of 2}),

(5)

\displaystyle a_{3}-b^{(3)}_{11}

\displaystyle=R+Wp_{2}(\Delta)+\mathcal{O}(p_{2}^{2}(\Delta))\quad(\text{threshold of 3}),

(6)

\displaystyle R

\displaystyle\equiv-\frac{C_{4}^{2}}{(1-iC_{1}p_{1})C_{1}},

(7)

where
X
and
W
are the constants determined by
C_{i}
and
p_{1}
. Because
\kappa_{3}(\Delta)
and
p_{2}(\Delta)
vanish, only the first terms remain in the limit
\Delta\to 0
. The leading terms in Eqs. (
5
) and (
6
) are identical except for a factor 2, which originates from Clebsch-Gordan coefficients. Therefore, if
2|R/X|\gg\kappa_{3}(\Delta)
and
|R/W|\gg p_{2}(\Delta)
, the types of the cusps (see Fig.
1
) at the thresholds of 2 and 3 are expected to be identical.

In the isospin-symmetric limit, i.e., near the threshold of channel II with
\Delta\to 0
, the slope of the cross section is given by

\displaystyle a_{{\rm I\hskip-1.2ptI}}-b_{11}^{({\rm I\hskip-1.2ptI})}=3R.

(8)

Equation (
8
) shows that the slope in the isospin-symmetric case is the sum of
a_{2}-b^{(2)}_{11}
and
a_{3}-b^{(3)}_{11}
in the
\Delta\to 0
limit.

4
Numerical results

In this section, we study the behavior of the threshold cusp structures in the
\Lambda N
-
\Sigma N
scattering for both the isospin-symmetric (
\Delta\to 0
) and the isospin-broken (
\Delta\neq 0
) cases numerically. We use the averaged energy of
\Sigma^{+}n
and
\Sigma^{0}p
as the threshold energy of channel II(
\Sigma N
) for
\Delta\to 0
. The
\Lambda p
elastic scattering amplitude
f_{11}(E)
is calculated using Eq. (
1
) together with the
K
-matrix defined in Eq. (
4
).

In our numerical analysis, we define a dimensionless cross section
\sigma_{11}^{\rm N}(E)
as

\displaystyle\sigma_{11}^{\rm N}(E)

\displaystyle=\frac{|f_{11}(E)|^{2}}{|f_{11}(E=0;\,\Delta\to 0)|^{2}},

(9)

where
f_{11}(E;\Delta\to 0)
is the scattering amplitude in the isospin-symmetric limit. Accordingly,
\sigma_{11}^{\rm N}(E;\Delta\to 0)
is normalized at the threshold of channel II.

In order to determine the four parameters in Eq. (
4
), we need four conditions. First, we fix the scattering length
a_{{\rm I\hskip-1.2ptI}}
in the
\Delta\to 0
limit. The real and imaginary parts of
a_{{\rm I\hskip-1.2ptI}}
impose two constraints on the parameters in Eq. (
4
). It is important to note that, within the first-order expansion in the momentum, the position of the pole is determined only by the scattering length in the
\Delta\to 0
limit.

In addition to this constraint, we impose two conditions
C_{1}C_{3}-C_{4}^{2}=0
and
C_{2}-2C_{3}=0
, which lead to
b_{11}^{(\rm I\hskip-1.2ptI)}=0
. As a result, the slopes of the cross sections in Eq. (
8
) depend only on the scattering length
a_{\rm I\hskip-1.2ptI}
. These four conditions uniquely determine the four independent parameters in Eq. (
4
). We also use this parameter set in the isospin broken case (
\Delta\neq 0
). The hadron masses used in the calculation are taken from Ref.
[
3
]
.

As an example, we use the scattering length of the typical hadronic scale for
\Delta\to 0
;

\displaystyle a_{{\rm I\hskip-1.2ptI}}

\displaystyle=-1.0-i\,0.8\quad{\rm fm}.

(10)

The corresponding values of the scattering lengths
a_{2}
and
a_{3}
with
\Delta\neq 0
are obtained as

\displaystyle a_{2}

\displaystyle=-0.65-i0.46\quad\rm fm,

(11)

\displaystyle a_{3}

\displaystyle=-0.26-i0.27\quad\rm fm.

(12)

For
a_{{\rm I\hskip-1.2ptI}}
in Eq. (
10
),
f_{11}(E;\Delta\to 0)
has a quasivirtual pole
[
10
]
below the II threshold. The resulting cross sections for both the isospin-symmetric and isospin-broken cases are shown in Fig.
2
. The dashed line represents
\sigma_{11}^{\rm N}(E)
in the isospin-symmetric case (
\Delta\to 0
) and the solid line corresponds to the case with finite
\Delta
.

\sigma^{\rm N}_{11}(E)
. The solid and dashed lines correspond to

and

cases, respectively. The dotted vertical lines represent the thresholds of channel 2 (

From the solid line in Fig.

, it is observed that the cross section
\sigma_{11}^{\rm N}(E)
for

(a), consistent with
\imaginary[a_{N}-b^{(N)}_{11}]<0
and
\real[a_{N}-b^{(N)}_{11}]<0
. The isospin-symmetric cross section
\sigma_{11}^{\rm N}(E)
with
\Delta\to 0

, displays the same type of cusp behavior as the solid line. This result indicates that the isospin-breaking effects are small for the case of
a_{{\rm I\hskip-1.2ptI}}=-1.0-0.8\ {\rm fm}

. Indeed, the sum of the scattering lengths
a_{2}+a_{3}=-0.91-i\,0.73\ {\rm fm}
is approximately equal to
a_{{\rm I\hskip-1.2ptI}}
, further supporting our conclusion.

5
Summary

In this contribution, we study the effects of isospin breaking on the threshold cusp structures. In Sec.
2
, we introduce a convenient representation of the scattering amplitude
f_{11}(E)
as given in Eq. (
2
), which allows for a transparent analysis of the slope of the cross section near the threshold. Using this representation, in Sec.
3
, we examine the behavior of the
\Sigma N
cusp structures in the
\Lambda p
elastic scattering amplitude under both the isospin-symmetric and isospin-broken cases. As a result, we find that the types of the cusp structures at the thresholds of
\Sigma^{+}n
and
\Sigma^{0}p
with
\Delta\neq 0
become identical to the single cusp with
\Delta\to 0
, suggesting that the isospin breaking effect is small. We demonstrate numerically this phenomena in Sec.
4
.

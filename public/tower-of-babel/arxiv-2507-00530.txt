Uncertainty principles associated with the linear canonical Dunkl transform
Umamaheswari S and Sandeep Kumar Verma

Abstract
. In this paper, we establish analogs of Miyachi, Cowling-Price, and Heisenberg-Pauli-Weyl uncertainty principles in the framework of the linear canonical Dunkl transform. We also obtain some weighted inequalities, such as Nash, Clarkson, Donoho-Stark, and Matolcsi-Szuc’s type inequalities.

1.
Introduction

The classical Heisenberg-Weyl uncertainty principle provides insights into the relationship between a function and its Fourier transform. It states that attempting to confine the behavior of one leads to a loss of control over the other. A key implication of Heisenberg-Weyl uncertainty principle in physics, particularly in optics, is that it sets a lower bound for the product of a signal’s spread and its bandwidth. For instance, it means that the product of the effective widths of light intensity in both the space and frequency domains has a minimum value, which is achieved when the light is completely coherent and Gaussian. The Heisenberg-Weyl uncertainty relation is frequently used in signal processing to examine localization in time-frequency analysis
[
2
]
. The classical formulation of the uncertainty principle
[
17
]
in the form of the lower bound of the product of the dispersions of a function
f
and its Fourier transform
\hat{f}
is as follows

\displaystyle\||x|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\||\omega|\hat{f}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\geq C\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

(1.1)

There are many variations of the uncertainty principle. Broadly uncertainty principles can be categorized into two main types: quantitative and qualitative.
Quantitative uncertainty principles
[
29
,
42
]
are precise inequalities that provide valuable insights into the relationship between a function and its Fourier transform. These inequalities, reminiscent of the classical Heisenberg uncertainty principle, detail how the temporal concentration of a function corresponds to its spectral dispersion. This includes Heisenberg-Pauli-Weyl, Nash-type
[
6
]
, Clarkson-type
[
7
]
, Donoho-Stark
[
14
]
, and Matlocsi-Szucs-type uncertainty principles
[
28
]
, etc.

On the other hand, the qualitative uncertainty principles
[
17
]
, exemplified by works such as those by Hardy
[
23
,
44
]
, Cowling-Price
[
10
]
, and Miyachi
[
32
]
, are expressed as theorems rather than inequalities. They describe the behavior of a function and its Fourier transform under specific conditions.

The various uncertainty principles in several integral transform domains, such as the LCT transform
[
22
]
, fractional Fourier transform
[
43
]
, the Hankel transform
[
39
]
, Dunkl transform
[
21
,
40
,
45
,
46
,
47
,
48
]
, Heckman-Opdam transform
[
26
]
, linear canonical deformed Hankel transform
[
30
]
, etc., have been established in great detail.

The main goal of this article is to establish various quantitative and qualitative uncertainty principles within the framework of the linear canonical Dunkl transform on the real line.
The classical linear canonical transform (LCT) (or linear canonical Fourier transform) was introduced independently by Collins
[
9
]
in the context of paraxial optics and by Moshinsky
[

in quantum mechanics to understand the conservation of information and uncertainty under linear maps of phase space.
The linear canonical transform (LCT) encompasses numerous well-known transforms in optics, including the Fourier transform

]
, fractional Fourier transform

,

In addition to its wide-ranging applications, the theoretical development of the linear canonical transform (LCT) has been extensively studied and investigated, including convolution structure, sampling theorems, uncertainty principles, and other theoretical aspects. Further, the linear canonical transform associated with the various integral transforms have been defined and studied, for instance, the linear canonical Hankel transform

To proceed further, let us recall the definition of the linear canonical Dunkl transform introduced by Ghazouani et al.
[
20
]
in connection with the Dunkl transform.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
f
be an integrable function on
\mathbb{R}
and
M=(a,b;c,d)\in SL(2,\mathbb{R})
. Then the linear canonical Dunkl transform is defined as
[
20
]

D_{k}^{M}(f)(\lambda)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{1}{(ib)^{k+1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)\,E_{k}^{M}(\lambda,x)\,d\mu_{k}(x),&b\neq 0,\\
\frac{e^{i\frac{c}{2a}\lambda^{2}}}{|a|^{k+1}}\,f(\lambda/a)&b=0\end{array}\right.

where the kernel
E^{M}_{k}(\lambda,x)
is defined by

E^{M}_{k}(\lambda,x)=e^{\frac{i}{2}(\frac{d}{b}\lambda^{2}+\frac{a}{b}x^{2})}E_{k}(-i\lambda/b,x).

It is easy to verify that the linear canonical Dunkl transform (LCDT) with parameters
(\cos(\theta),-\sin(\theta);\sin(\theta),\cos(\theta))
reduces to the fractional Dunkl transform
[
19
]
. In the specific case of
\theta=\pi/2
, it becomes the Dunkl transform, and when
\theta=\pi/2
and
k=0
, it becomes Fourier transform.

We will precisely establish the following uncertainty principles associated with the linear canonical Dunkl transform:

•

Heisenberg-Pauli-Weyl uncertainty principle:
Let
f\in L^{p}_{k}(\mathbb{R})
,
1<p\leq 2
,
0<\alpha<\frac{2(k+1)}{q}
, and
\beta>0
. Then there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\|D_{k}^{M}(f)\|_{L^{q}_{k}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C\,\||y|^{\alpha}\,f\|^{\frac{\beta}{\alpha+\beta}}_{L_{k}^{p}(\mathbb{R})}\,\||\lambda|^{\beta}\,D_{k}^{M}(f)\|^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+\beta}}_{L_{k}^{q}(\mathbb{R})}.

•

Nash-type inequality:
Let
1<p_{1}<p_{2}\leq 2
with
q_{1}
and
q_{2}
being the conjugate exponents of
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
, respectively. Suppose
f
belongs to both
L_{k}^{p_{1}}(\mathbb{R})
and
L_{k}^{p_{2}}(\mathbb{R})
. Then there exists a constant
C(q_{1},q_{2},k,b,s)
such that

\displaystyle\|D_{k}^{M}(f)\|_{L_{k}^{q_{2}}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(q_{1},q_{2},k,b)\,\||x|^{s}\,D_{k}^{M}(f)\|_{L_{k}^{q_{2}}(\mathbb{R})}^{\frac{(2k+2)\,(q_{1}-q_{2})}{(2k+2)\,(q_{1}-q_{2})+sq_{1}q_{2}}}

\displaystyle\times\,\|f\|^{\frac{sq_{1}q_{2}}{(2k+2)\,(q_{1}-q_{2})+sq_{1}q_{2}}}_{L_{k}^{p_{1}}(\mathbb{R})},\quad\text{for some }\,\,s>0,

and

C(q_{1},q_{2},k,b)=\left\{1+\left(\frac{1}{2^{k+1}\,\Gamma(k+2)}\right)^{\frac{q_{1}-q_{2}}{q_{1}}}\,C_{k,b}^{(1-\frac{2}{q_{1}})q_{2}}\right\}^{\frac{1}{q_{2}}}.

•

Clarkson-type inequality:
If
f
belongs to
L_{k}^{p_{1}}(\mathbb{R})
and
L_{k}^{p_{2}}(\mathbb{R})
, where
1<p_{1}<p_{2}\leq 2
, then there exists a constant
C(p_{1},p_{2},k)
such that

\|f\|_{L_{k}^{p_{1}}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C(p_{1},p_{2},k)\,\||y|^{s}\,f\|^{\frac{(2k+2)\,(p_{2}-p_{1})}{(2k+2)(p_{2}-p_{1})+p_{1}p_{2}s}}_{L_{k}^{p_{1}}(\mathbb{R})}\,\|f\|_{L_{k}^{p_{2}}(\mathbb{R})}^{\frac{p_{1}p_{2}s}{(2k+2)(p_{2}-p_{1})+p_{1}p_{2}s}},

where

C(p_{1},p_{2},k)=\left\{1+\frac{1}{(2^{k+1}\,\Gamma(k+2))^{\frac{p_{2}-p_{1}}{p_{2}}}}\right\}^{\frac{1}{p_{1}}}.

•

Donoho-Stark uncertainty principle:
Let
E
and
F
be measurable subsets of
\mathbb{R}
, and let
f\in L_{k}^{p_{1}}(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{p_{2}}_{k}(\mathbb{R})
for
1<p_{1}<p_{2}\leq 2
. If
f
is
\epsilon_{E}
-concentrated in
L_{k}^{p_{1}}(\mathbb{R})
and
D_{k}^{M}(f)
is
\epsilon_{F}
concentrated in
L_{k}^{q_{2}}(\mathbb{R})
, then

\|D_{k}^{M}(f)\|_{L_{k}^{q_{2}}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C_{k,b}^{1-\frac{2}{q_{1}}}\,\frac{(\gamma_{k}(E))^{\frac{p_{2}-p_{1}}{p_{1}\,p_{2}}}\,(\gamma_{k}(F))^{\frac{q_{1}-q_{2}}{q_{1}\,q_{2}}}}{(1-\epsilon_{E})\,(1-\epsilon_{F})}\,\|f\|_{L_{k}^{p_{2}}(\mathbb{R})}.

•

Matolcsi-Szucs type uncertainty principle:
If the function
f
belongs to
L_{k}^{p_{1}}(\mathbb{R})
and
L_{k}^{p_{2}}(\mathbb{R})
, where
1<p_{1}\leq p_{2}\leq 2
, then

\|D_{k}^{M}(f)\|_{L_{k}^{q_{2}}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C_{k,b}^{1-\frac{2}{q_{1}}}\,(\gamma_{k}(A_{D_{k}^{M}(f)}))^{\frac{q_{1}-q_{2}}{q_{1}\,q_{2}}}\,(\gamma_{k}(A_{f}))^{\frac{p_{1}-p_{2}}{p_{1}\,p_{2}}}\,\|f\|_{L^{p_{2}}_{k}(\mathbb{R})},

where

A_{D_{k}^{M}(f)}=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}:D_{k}^{M}(f)(x)\neq 0\right\}

and

A_{f}=\left\{t\in\mathbb{R}:f(t)\neq 0\right\}.

•

Miyachi’s uncertainty principle:
Let
f
be a measurable function on
\mathbb{R}
such that
e^{sx^{2}}f\in L^{p}_{k}(\mathbb{R})+L^{q}_{k}(\mathbb{R}),
where
p,q\in[1,\infty]
. Suppose that

\int_{\mathbb{R}}ln^{+}\left(\frac{|e^{tx^{2}}D_{k}^{M}(f)(x)|}{\lambda}\right)dx<\infty,

for some positive real constants
s,t,\lambda
, and

ln^{+}(r)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}ln(r)&\text{if}\,\,r>1\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

Then

(i)

f=0
a.e on
\mathbb{R}
, if
st>\frac{1}{4b^{2}}
.

(ii)

f(x)=C\,e^{-\left(\frac{i}{2}\frac{a}{b}+s\right)x^{2}}
, if
st=\frac{1}{4b^{2}}
and
|C|\leq|\lambda|
.

(iii)

There are many functions satisfying the hypothesis, if
st<\frac{1}{4b^{2}}
.

•

Cowling-Price uncertainty principle:
Let
f
be a measurable function on
\mathbb{R}
such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{e^{spx^{2}}\,|f(x)|^{p}}{(1+|x|)^{n}}\,d\mu_{k}(x)<\infty

and

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{e^{tq\lambda^{2}}|D_{k}^{M}(f)(\lambda)|^{q}}{(1+|\lambda|)^{m}}\,d\lambda<\infty,

where
s,t>0
and
n>0,m>1,1\leq p,q<\infty
. Then the following results hold.

(i)

If
st>\frac{1}{4b^{2}}
, then
f=0
a.e on
\mathbb{R}
.

(ii)

If
st=\frac{1}{4b^{2}}
, then
f(x)=Q(x)\,e^{-(s+\frac{i}{2}\frac{a}{b})x^{2}}
, where
Q
is a polynomial with
deg(Q)\leq min\{\frac{n}{p}+\frac{2k}{p^{\prime}},\frac{m-1}{q}\}
and
p^{\prime}
is the conjugate exponent of
p
. Moreover, if
1<m\leq 1+q
and
n>2k+1
, then
f(x)=C\,e^{-(s+\frac{i}{2}\frac{a}{b})x^{2}}
.

(iii)

If
st<\frac{1}{4b^{2}}
, then there are many functions of the form
f(x)=Q(x)\,e^{-(\delta+\frac{i}{2}\frac{a}{b})x^{2}}
which satisfy the hypothesis, where
\delta\in(t,\frac{1}{4sb^{2}})
and
Q
is the polynomial in
\mathbb{R}
.

D^{M^{-1}}_{k}(D^{M}_{k}f)=f,\,\,\text{a.e},

(ii)

Plancherel’s formula:
If

D^{M}_{k}(f)\in L^{2}_{k}(\mathbb{R})
and we have

(2.1)

Remark 2.1
.

The linear canonical Dunkl transform and the Dunkl transform are related as follows

\displaystyle D_{k}^{M}(f)(\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{e^{\frac{i}{2}\frac{d}{b}\lambda^{2}}}{(ib)^{k+1}}\,D_{k}(\tilde{f})\left(\frac{\lambda}{b}\right),~b\neq 0,

(2.2)

where
\tilde{f}(x)=e^{\frac{i}{2}\frac{a}{b}x^{2}}f(x)
and
D_{k}(f)
is the Dunkl transform
[

38
]
defined by

D_{k}(f)(\lambda)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)\,E_{k}(-i\lambda,x)\,d\mu_{k}(x),

where
E_{k}(-i\lambda,x)
is the Dunkl kernel
[
37
,
41
]

defined by

E_{k}(-i\lambda,x)=j_{k}(-\lambda x)+\frac{(-i\lambda)x}{2(k+1)}j_{k+1}(-\lambda x),\quad k\geq-1/2,

and
j_{k}
denotes the normalized spherical Bessel function

j_{k}(x)=2^{k}\Gamma(k+1)x^{-k}J_{k}(x)=\Gamma(k+1)\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}\frac{(-1)^{n}(x/2)^{2n}}{n!\Gamma(n+k+1)}.

For
\lambda,x\in\mathbb{R}
, the Dunkl kernel
E_{k}(-i\lambda,x)
is the unique solution of the system

\displaystyle\Lambda_{k}f(x)=-i\lambda f(x)~\text{and}~f(0)=1,

where the Dunkl operator
\Lambda_{k}
associated with the reflection group
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
on
\mathbb{R}
is defined as

\displaystyle\Lambda_{k}(f)(x)=\frac{d}{dx}f(x)+\frac{2k+1}{2}\left(\frac{f(x)-f(-x)}{x}\right),~k\geq-1/2.

Note that
\Lambda_{-1/2}=d/dx
.

from

Properties:

The kernel

extends as an analytic function of

(ii

For all

.

(iii)

For all

.

(iv)

For

\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-s\xi^{2}}\,E_{k}(-ix,\xi)\,E_{k}(iy,\xi)d\mu_{k}(\xi)=\frac{\Gamma(k+1)}{s^{k+1}}\,e^{-\frac{(x^{2}+y^{2})}{4s}}\,E_{k}\left(\frac{x}{\sqrt{2s}},\frac{y}{\sqrt{2s}}\right).

Lemma 2.3

(Riemann-Lebesgue lemma)
.

If
f\in L_{k}^{1}(\mathbb{R})

belongs to

3.
Quantitative Uncertainty Principles

The quantitative uncertainty principle provides precise inequalities that relate a function and its transform. It offers exact mathematical bounds and relations. These precise bounds are useful in practical applications, such as signal processing, quantum mechanics, and differential equations. In this section, we will prove the Heisenberg-Pauli-Weyl uncertainty principle, Nash and Clarkson type uncertainty principles, and Donoho-Stark and Matolcsi-Szucs uncertainty principles in the setting of the linear canonical Dunkl transform (LCDT).

3.1.
Heisenberg’s-Pauli-Weyl Uncertainty Principle

The Heisenberg-Pauli-Weyl uncertainty principle
[
45
]
can be established through various methods such as the entropy-based approach and the spectral method. However, in this study, we focus on proving the Heisenberg-Pauli-Weyl uncertainty principle in the classical approach which generalizes equation (
1.1
).

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
f\in L^{p}_{k}(\mathbb{R})
,
1<p\leq 2
,
0<\alpha<\frac{2(k+1)}{q}
and
\beta>0
. Then there exist
C>0
such that

\|D_{k}^{M}(f)\|_{L^{q}_{k}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C\,\||y|^{\alpha}\,f\|^{\frac{\beta}{\alpha+\beta}}_{L_{k}^{p}(\mathbb{R})}\,\||\lambda|^{\beta}\,D_{k}^{M}(f)\|^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+\beta}}_{L_{k}^{q}(\mathbb{R})}.

In order to simplify the proof of Theorem
3.1
, we first establish the following technical lemmas:

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
t>0
and
p\in[1,\infty)
. Then there exists
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\|e^{-t\lambda^{2}}\|_{L^{p}_{k}(\mathbb{R})}=C\,t^{-\left(\frac{k+1}{p}\right)}.

Proof.

The proof directly follows from the norm estimate.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

If
f\in L_{k}^{p}(\mathbb{R})
, where
1<p\leq 2
, and
0<\alpha<\frac{2(k+1)}{q}
, with
q
being the conjugate exponent of
p
, then there exists
C(\alpha,b,q)>0
such that

\|e^{-t\lambda^{2}}D_{k}^{M}(f)\|_{{L_{k}^{q}(\mathbb{R})}}\leq C(\alpha,b,q)\,t^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\,\||y|^{\alpha}f\|_{{L^{p}_{k}(\mathbb{R})}},\,\,\text{for some}\,\,t>0.

Proof.

The result directly holds, if
\||y|^{\alpha}\,f\|_{L_{k}^{p}(\mathbb{R})}=\infty
. Now, let us assume that
\||y|^{\alpha}\,f\|_{L_{k}^{p}(\mathbb{R})}<\infty.

Let
f\in L^{p}_{k}(\mathbb{R})
, and suppose
f
can be written as

f=f\chi_{(-r,r)}+f\chi_{\mathbb{R}\setminus(-r,r)},\quad r>0,

(3.1)

where
\chi_{(-r,r)}
is the characteristic function on
(-r,r)
.
Also, we can easily check that

|f\chi_{{\mathbb{R}}\setminus(-r,r)}(y)|\leq|y|^{\alpha}\,r^{-\alpha}\,|f(y)|.

(3.2)

By applying the linear canonical Dunkl transform and multiplying both sides of equation (
3.1
) by
e^{-t\lambda^{2}}
, and then applying Minkowski’s inequality, we obtain

\|e^{-t\lambda^{2}}D_{k}^{M}(f)\|_{L_{k}^{q}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\|e^{-t\lambda^{2}}D_{k}^{M}\left(f\chi_{(-r,r)}\right)\|_{L_{k}^{q}(\mathbb{R})}+\|e^{-t\lambda^{2}}D_{k}^{M}\left(f\chi_{\mathbb{R}\setminus(-r,r)}\right)\|_{L_{k}^{q}(\mathbb{R})}.

(3.3)

Now, we consider the first part of equation (
3.3
). By successively applying Lemmas
3.2
,
2.3
, and using Hölder’s inequality, we obtain the following:

\displaystyle\|e^{-t\lambda^{2}}D_{k}^{M}\left(f\chi_{(-r,r)}\right)\|_{L_{k}^{q}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|e^{-t\lambda^{2}}\|_{L_{k}^{q}(\mathbb{R})}\,\|D_{k}^{M}\left(f\chi_{(-r,r)}\right)\|_{L_{k}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}.

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C\,t^{-\left(\frac{k+1}{q}\right)}}{|b|^{k+1}}\,\|f\chi_{(-r,r)}\|_{L_{k}^{1}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C\,t^{-\left(\frac{k+1}{q}\right)}}{|b|^{k+1}}\,\||y|^{-\alpha}\,\chi_{(-r,r)}\|_{L_{k}^{q}(\mathbb{R})}\,\||y|^{\alpha}\,f\|_{L_{k}^{p}(\mathbb{R})}.

For
0<\alpha<\frac{2(k+1)}{q}
, we have

\||y|^{-\alpha}\,\chi_{(-r,r)}\|_{L_{k}^{q}(\mathbb{R})}=\frac{r^{-\alpha+\frac{2(k+1)}{q}}}{(-\alpha q+2(k+1))^{\frac{1}{q}}}.

We deduce that

\|e^{-t\lambda^{2}}D_{k}^{M}\left(f\chi_{(-r,r)}\right)\|_{L_{k}^{q}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\frac{C\,t^{-\left(\frac{k+1}{q}\right)}\,r^{-\alpha+\frac{2(k+1)}{q}}}{|b|^{k+1}\,(-\alpha q+2(k+1))^{\frac{1}{q}}}\,\||y|^{\alpha}\,f\|_{L_{k}^{p}(\mathbb{R})}.

(3.4)

On the other hand

\|e^{-t\lambda^{2}}D_{k}^{M}\left(f\chi_{\mathbb{R}\setminus(-r,r)}\right)\|_{L_{k}^{q}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\|e^{-t\lambda^{2}}\|_{L^{\infty}_{k}(\mathbb{R})}\,\|D_{k}^{M}\left(f\chi_{\mathbb{R}\setminus(-r,r)}\right)\|_{L_{k}^{q}(\mathbb{R})}.

By using Young’s inequality (
2.3
) and equation (
3.2
), we have

\displaystyle\|e^{-t\lambda^{2}}D_{k}^{M}\left(\chi_{\mathbb{R}\setminus(-r,r)}\,f\right)\|_{L_{k}^{q}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{r^{-\alpha}}{|b|^{(k+1)\left(1-\frac{2}{q}\right)}}\,\||y|^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{p}_{k}(\mathbb{R})}.

(3.5)

By taking
r=t^{\frac{1}{2}}
and combining equations (
3.4
) and (
3.5
), we obtain the required result.
∎

Proof.

of Theorem
3.1
.

Let
f\in L_{k}^{p}(\mathbb{R})
, where
1<p\leq 2
. For
t>0
, we consider

\|(1-e^{-t\lambda^{2}})\,D_{k}^{M}(f)\|_{L_{k}^{q}(\mathbb{R})}\leq t^{\frac{\beta}{2}}\,\|(t\lambda^{2})^{\frac{-\beta}{2}}\,(1-e^{-t\lambda^{2}})\|_{L_{k}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\,\||\lambda|^{\beta}\,D_{k}^{M}(f)\|_{L_{k}^{q}(\mathbb{R})}.

(3.6)

We will give the proof by dividing

\beta
into two parts:
\beta\leq 2
and
\beta>2
.

If
\beta\leq 2
, then
r^{\frac{-\beta}{2}}(1-e^{-r})
is bounded for
r\geq 0
.
In view of Lemma
3.3
and estimate (
3.6
), we obtain that

\|D_{k}^{M}(f)\|_{L_{k}^{q}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C\left(t^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\,\||y|^{\alpha}f\|_{{L^{p}_{k}(\mathbb{R})}}+t^{\frac{\beta}{2}}\||\lambda|^{\beta}\,D_{k}^{M}(f)\|_{L_{k}^{q}(\mathbb{R})}\right).

By choosing
t=\left(\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\,\frac{\||y|^{\alpha}f\|_{{L^{p}_{k}(\mathbb{R})}}}{\||\lambda|^{\beta}\,D_{k}^{M}(f)\|_{L_{k}^{q}(\mathbb{R})}}\right)^{\frac{2}{\alpha+\beta}}
, we get the required result

\|D_{k}^{M}(f)\|_{L^{q}_{k}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C\,\||y|^{\alpha}\,f\|^{\frac{\beta}{\alpha+\beta}}_{L_{k}^{p}(\mathbb{R})}\,\||\lambda|^{\beta}\,D_{k}^{M}(f)\|^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+\beta}}_{L_{k}^{q}(\mathbb{R})},\quad\text{for}\,\,\,\beta\leq 2.

(3.7)

Now, we consider the second case
\beta>2
. Let us take
u
to lie between
0
and
\beta
, which gives the inequality

r^{u}\leq 1+r^{\beta},\qquad\text{for}\,\,r=\frac{|\lambda|}{\epsilon},\,\,\epsilon>0.

(3.8)

From (
3.8
), we can obtain that

\||\lambda|^{u}\,D_{k}^{M}(f)\|_{L^{q}_{k}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\epsilon^{u}\,\|D_{k}^{M}(f)\|_{L^{q}_{k}(\mathbb{R})}\,+\,\epsilon^{u-\beta}\,\||\lambda|^{\beta}\,D_{k}^{M}(f)\|_{L^{q}_{k}(\mathbb{R})}.

By choosing

\epsilon=\left(\frac{\beta-u}{u}\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta}}\,\left(\frac{\||\lambda|^{\beta}\,D_{k}^{M}(f)\|^{\frac{1}{\beta}}_{L_{k}^{q}(\mathbb{R})}}{\|D_{k}^{M}(f)\|^{\frac{1}{\beta}}_{L_{k}^{q}(\mathbb{R})}}\right)
, we deduce that

\||\lambda|^{u}\,D_{k}^{M}(f)\|_{L^{q}_{k}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\frac{\beta}{\beta-u}(\beta-u)^{\frac{u}{\beta}}\,\||\lambda|^{\beta}\,D_{k}^{M}(f)\|^{\frac{u}{\beta}}_{L_{k}^{q}(\mathbb{R})}\,\|D_{k}^{M}(f)\|^{(1-{\frac{u}{\beta})}}_{L_{k}^{q}(\mathbb{R})}.

(3.9)

It is evident that from equations (
3.7
) and (
3.9
), we achieve the required result.
∎

3.2.
Nash and Clarkson type uncertainty principles

The classical Nash inequality, introduced by Nash
[
33
]
expressed as

\|f\|_{2}^{2+\frac{4}{n}}\leq C_{n}\,\|f\|^{\frac{4}{n}}_{1}\,\||\xi|\hat{f}\|^{2}_{2},

(3.10)

where
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. It demonstrates how quantum mechanics imposes limits on the simultaneous measurement of time and energy. This inequality was developed to establish regularity properties for solutions to parabolic partial differential equations, and the optimal constant

C_{n}
was computed in
[
6
]
. Analogously to equation (
3.10
), we will establish a Nash-type inequality for the LCDT in the following theorems. For the sake of notation, we define
C_{k,b}=\frac{1}{|b|^{k+1}}
, and throughout this section,
q
represents the conjugate exponent of
p
.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
f
belongs to
L_{k}^{1}(\mathbb{R})
and
L^{p}_{k}(\mathbb{R}),\,\,1<p\leq 2
. Then

\|D_{k}^{M}(f)\|_{L_{k}^{q}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C(b,q,k)\,\|f\|_{L_{k}^{1}(\mathbb{R})}^{\frac{qs}{2k+2+qs}}\,\||x|^{s}\,D_{k}^{M}(f)\|_{L_{k}^{q}(\mathbb{R})}^{\frac{2k+2}{2k+2+qs}},\quad s>0,

where
q
is the conjugate exponent of
p
and

C(b,q,k)=\left\{1+\frac{C_{k,b}^{q}}{2^{k+1}\Gamma(k+2)}\right\}^{\frac{1}{q}}.

Proof.

For
r>0
, we consider

\|D_{k}^{M}(f)\|^{q}_{L_{k}^{q}(\mathbb{R})}=\|\left(1-\chi_{(-r,r)}\right)\,D_{k}^{M}(f)\|^{q}_{L_{k}^{q}(\mathbb{R})}+\|\chi_{(-r,r)}\,D_{k}^{M}(f)\|^{q}_{L_{k}^{q}(\mathbb{R})}.

(3.11)

By using the fact (
3.2
), we have

\|\left(1-\chi_{(-r,r)}\right)\,D_{k}^{M}(f)\|^{q}_{L_{k}^{q}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\frac{\||x|^{s}\,D_{k}^{M}(f)\|^{q}_{L_{k}^{q}(\mathbb{R})}}{r^{qs}}.

(3.12)

On manipulating the second part of (
3.11
) and invoking
Riemann-Lebesgue Lemma
2.3
, we get

\displaystyle\|\chi_{(-r,r)}\,D_{k}^{M}(f)\|^{q}_{L_{k}^{q}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\chi_{(-r,r)}\|^{q}_{L_{k}^{q}(\mathbb{R})}\,\|D_{k}^{M}(f)\|^{q}_{L^{\infty}_{k}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{k,b}^{q}\,\gamma_{k}\left((-r,r)\right)\,\|f\|^{q}_{L_{k}^{1}(\mathbb{R})},

where

\displaystyle\gamma_{k}\left((-r,r)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\chi_{(-r,r)}(x)\,d\mu_{k}(x)

(3.13)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{r^{2k+2}}{2^{k+1}\Gamma(k+2)}.

Thus,

\|\chi_{(-r,r)}\,D_{k}^{M}(f)\|_{L^{q}_{k}(\mathbb{R})}^{q}\leq C_{k,b}^{q}\,\frac{r^{2k+2}}{2^{k+1}\Gamma(k+2)}\,\|f\|^{q}_{L_{k}^{1}(\mathbb{R})}.

(3.14)

By combining (
3.11
), (
3.12
) and (
3.14
), we obtain

\|D_{k}^{M}(f)\|_{L_{k}^{q}(\mathbb{R})}^{q}\leq\frac{\||x|^{s}\,D_{k}^{M}(f)\|^{q}_{L_{k}^{q}(\mathbb{R})}}{r^{qs}}\,+C_{k,b}^{q}\,\frac{r^{2k+2}}{2^{k+1}\Gamma(k+2)}\,\|f\|^{q}_{L_{k}^{1}(\mathbb{R})}.

By choosing

r=\left[\frac{\||x|^{s}\,D_{k}^{M}(f)\|^{q}_{L_{k}^{q}(\mathbb{R})}}{\|f\|^{q}_{L_{k}^{1}(\mathbb{R})}}\right]^{\frac{1}{qs+2k+2}},

we deduce the required result

\|D_{k}^{M}(f)\|_{L_{k}^{q}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C(b,q,k)\,\|f\|_{L_{k}^{1}(\mathbb{R})}^{\frac{qs}{2k+2+qs}}\,\||x|^{s}\,D_{k}^{M}(f)\|_{L_{k}^{q}(\mathbb{R})}^{\frac{2k+2}{2k+2+qs}}.

This completes the proof of the theorem.

Remark 3.5
.

For
q=2
, the inequality in Theorem
3.4
reduces to

\|f\|_{L_{k}^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C(b,k)\,\|f\|_{L_{k}^{1}(\mathbb{R})}^{\frac{s}{k+s+1}}\,\||x|^{s}\,D_{k}^{M}(f)\|_{L_{k}^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{\frac{k+1}{k+s+1}}.

∎

Theorem 3.6
.

If the function
f
belongs to
L_{k}^{2}(\mathbb{R})
and
L_{k}^{p}(\mathbb{R}),
with
1<p<2

\|f\|_{L_{k}^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C(b,q,k)\,\|f\|^{\frac{2sq}{(2k+2)\,(q-2)+2sq}}_{L_{k}^{p}(\mathbb{R})}\,\||x|^{s}\,D_{k}^{M}(f)\|_{L_{k}^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{\frac{(2k+2)\,(q-2)}{(2k+2)\,(q-2)+2sq}},\quad s>0,

where

Proof.

For
r>0
, we consider

\|D_{k}^{M}(f)\|^{2}_{L_{k}^{2}(\mathbb{R})}=\|(1-\chi_{(-r,r)})\,D_{k}^{M}(f)\|^{2}_{L_{k}^{2}(\mathbb{R})}+\|\chi_{(-r,r)}\,D_{k}^{M}(f)\|^{2}_{L_{k}^{2}(\mathbb{R})}.

(3.15)

From (
3.2
), we immediately obtain that

\|(1-\chi_{(-r,r)})\,D_{k}^{M}(f)\|^{2}_{L_{k}^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\,\frac{\||x|^{s}\,D_{k}^{M}(f)\|^{2}_{L_{k}^{2}(\mathbb{R})}}{r^{2s}}.

(3.16)

On the other hand, by using Hölder’s inequality, Young’s inequality (
2.3
) and (
3.13
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|\chi_{(-r,r)}\,D_{k}^{M}(f)\|^{2}_{L_{k}^{2}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\gamma_{k}((-r,r))\right)^{\frac{q-2}{q}}\,\|D_{k}^{M}(f)\|^{2}_{L_{k}^{q}(\mathbb{R})}

(3.17)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{r^{2k+2}\,C_{k,b}^{2}}{2^{k+1}\,\Gamma(k+2)}\right)^{\frac{q-2}{q}}\,\|f\|^{2}_{L^{p}_{k}(\mathbb{R})}.

By plugging (
3.16
) and (
3.17
) in (
3.15
), we have

\|D_{k}^{M}(f)\|^{2}_{L_{k}^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\,\frac{\||x|^{s}\,D_{k}^{M}(f)\|^{2}_{L_{k}^{2}(\mathbb{R})}}{r^{2s}}\,+\left(\frac{r^{2k+2}\,C_{k,b}^{2}}{2^{k+1}\,\Gamma(k+2)}\right)^{\frac{q-2}{q}}\,\|f\|^{2}_{L^{p}_{k}(\mathbb{R})}.

By choosing

r=\left[\frac{\||x|^{s}\,D_{k}^{M}(f)\|^{2}_{L_{k}^{2}(\mathbb{R})}}{\|f\|^{2}_{L^{p}_{k}(\mathbb{R})}}\right]^{\frac{q}{(2k+2)(q-2)+2sq}},

we deduce that

\|D_{k}^{M}(f)\|_{L_{k}^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C(b,q,k)\,\|f\|^{\frac{2sq}{(2k+2)\,(q-2)+2sq}}_{L_{k}^{p}(\mathbb{R})}\,\||x|^{s}\,D_{k}^{M}(f)\|_{L_{k}^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{\frac{(2k+2)\,(q-2)}{(2k+2)\,(q-2)+2sq}}.

The required result immediately follows from Plancherel’s formula (
2.1
).
∎

Theorem 3.7

\displaystyle\|\chi_{(-r,r)}\,D_{k}^{M}(f)\|^{q_{2}}_{L_{k}^{q_{2}}(\mathbb{R})}

∎

3.3.
Donoho-Stark and Matolcsi-Szucs Uncertainty

In this subsection, we discuss the
extended version of the classical Heisenberg uncertainty principle known as the Donoho-Stark
[
14
]
and Matolcsi-Szucs uncertainty principle. It states that
f
and
\hat{f}
are essentially zero outside a measurable set
E
and
F
then
|E|\,|F|\geq 1-\delta
, where
|E|
and
|F|
denotes the measures of sets
E
and
F
, and
\delta>0
is a small number. It has applications in image processing and signal reconstruction, compression, denoising, etc. Here,
q,q_{1}
, and
q_{2}
are conjugate components of
p,p_{1}
, and
p_{2}
respectively.

Definition 3.11
.

Let
E
be a measurable subset of
\mathbb{R}
. The function
f\in L_{k}^{p}(\mathbb{R})
,
1\leq p\leq 2
is said to be
\epsilon_{E}
- concentrated to
E
in
L_{k}^{p}(\mathbb{R})
, if

\|f-\chi_{E}f\|_{L^{p}_{k}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\epsilon_{E}\,\|f\|_{L^{p}_{k}(\mathbb{R})},\qquad\qquad\text{for}\,\,\,0\leq\epsilon_{E}<1.

Similarly, we say that
D_{k}^{M}(f)
is
\epsilon_{E}
- concentrated to
E
in
L_{k}^{q}(\mathbb{R}),
if

\|D_{k}^{M}(f)-\chi_{E}D_{k}^{M}(f)\|_{L_{k}^{q}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\epsilon_{E}\,\|D_{k}^{M}(f)\|_{L_{k}^{q}(\mathbb{R})}.

We discuss the Donoho-Stark inequalities in the following theorems.

Theorem 3.12
.

Let
E
and
F
be measurable subsets of
\mathbb{R}
,
f
belongs to
L_{k}^{1}(\mathbb{R})
and
L^{p}_{k}(\mathbb{R})
for
1<p\leq 2
. If
f
is
\epsilon_{E}
concentrated to
E
in
L_{k}^{1}(\mathbb{R})
and
D_{k}^{M}(f)
is
\epsilon_{F}

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{e^{tq\lambda^{2}}|D_{k}^{M}(f)(\lambda)|^{q}}{(1+|\lambda|)^{m}}\,d\lambda<\infty,

(4.11)

where
s,t>0
and
n>0,m>1,1\leq p,q<\infty
. Then the following results hold.

(i)

If
st>\frac{1}{4b^{2}}
, then
f=0
a.e on
\mathbb{R}
.

(ii)

If
st=\frac{1}{4b^{2}}
, then
f(x)=Q(x)\,e^{-(s+\frac{i}{2}\frac{a}{b})x^{2}}
, where
Q
is a polynomial with
deg(Q)\leq min\{\frac{n}{p}+\frac{2k}{p^{\prime}},\frac{m-1}{q}\}
and
p^{\prime}
is the conjugate exponent of
p
. Moreover, if
1<m\leq 1+q
and
n>2k+1
, then
f(x)=C\,e^{-(s+\frac{i}{2}\frac{a}{b})x^{2}}
.

(iii)

If
st<\frac{1}{4b^{2}}
, then there are many functions of the form
f(x)=Q(x)\,e^{-(\delta+\frac{i}{2}\frac{a}{b})x^{2}}
which satisfy the hypothesis, where
\delta\in(t,\frac{1}{4sb^{2}})
and
Q
is the polynomial in
\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

We observe from (
4.10
),
f
is an integrable function on
\mathbb{R}
. Thus,
D_{k}^{M}(f)
is well defined on
\mathbb{R}
. Note
2.2
implies that
D_{k}^{M}(f)
is also an entire function on
\mathbb{C}
.
Now, from (
2.2
) and utilizing Hölder’s inequality along with Property
2.2
(ii), we have

\displaystyle|e^{-\frac{i}{2}\frac{d}{b}z^{2}}\,D_{k}^{M}(f)(z)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|b|^{k+1}}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f(x)|\,\left|E_{k}\left(-\frac{iz}{b},x\right)\right|\,d\mu_{k}(x)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|b|^{k+1}}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f(x)|\,e^{|\frac{Imz}{b}|\,|x|}\,d\mu_{k}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{e^{\frac{|Im\,z|^{2}}{4sb^{2}}}}{|b|^{k+1}}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{e^{s\,|x|^{2}}\,|f(x)|}{(1+|x|)^{\frac{n}{p}}}\,(1+|x|)^{\frac{n}{p}}\,e^{-s\,\left(|x|-\frac{Im\,z}{2sb}\right)^{2}}\,d\mu_{k}(x)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C\,e^{\frac{|Im\,z|^{2}}{4sb^{2}}}}{|b|^{k+1}}\,\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}(1+|x|)^{\frac{np^{\prime}}{p}}\,e^{-sp^{\prime}\,\left(|x|-\frac{Im\,z}{2sb}\right)^{2}}\,d\mu_{k}(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C\,e^{\frac{|Im\,z|^{2}}{4sb^{2}}}}{|b|^{k+1}}\,\left(1+|Im\,z|\right)^{\frac{n}{p}+\frac{2k+1}{p^{\prime}}}.

Now, we define the entire function

g(z)=e^{\frac{z^{2}}{4sb^{2}}}\,e^{-\frac{i}{2}\frac{d}{b}z^{2}}\,D_{k}^{M}(f)(z).

(4.12)

Indeed,
g
is pointwise bounded

|g(z)|\leq C\,e^{\frac{|Re\,z|^{2}}{4sb^{2}}}\,\left(1+|Im\,z|\right)^{\frac{n}{p}+\frac{2k+1}{p^{\prime}}}.

Further, if
st=\frac{1}{4b^{2}}
, then assumption (
4.11
), gives

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|g(x)|^{q}}{(1+|x|)^{m}}\,dx<\infty.

From Lemma
4.5
, we get
g
is the polynomial say
P(x)
with
deg(P)\leq r
, where
r=min\{\frac{n}{p}+\frac{2k+1}{p^{\prime}},\frac{m-1}{q}\}
. Thus, we deduce (
4.12
)

\displaystyle D_{k}^{M}(f)(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P(x)\,e^{-\frac{x^{2}}{4sb^{2}}}\,e^{\frac{i}{2}\frac{d}{b}x^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P(x)\,e^{-tx^{2}}\,e^{\frac{i}{2}\frac{d}{b}x^{2}}.

By using relation (
2.2
) and Lemma
4.4
, we obtain

f(x)=C\,Q(x)\,e^{-(s+\frac{i}{2}\frac{a}{b})x^{2}},

where

is a polynomial with

. Therefore,
f
satisfies hypothesis (
4.10
), whenever
n>2k+1+pr
. Consider

. From Lemma
4.5
, it follows that
g
is a constant. Consequently, the equation (
4.12
) implies

D_{k}^{M}(f)(x)=C\,e^{-\frac{x^{2}}{4sb^{2}}}\,e^{\frac{i}{2}\frac{d}{b}x^{2}}

and
f(x)=C\,e^{-(s+\frac{i}{2}\frac{a}{b})x^{2}}
. The choice of
n>2k+1
and
m>1
, ensures that the hypothesis (
4.10
) and (
4.11
) satisfied, respectively. This completes the proof of
(ii)
.

(i).
If
st>\frac{1}{4b^{2}}
, then we choose

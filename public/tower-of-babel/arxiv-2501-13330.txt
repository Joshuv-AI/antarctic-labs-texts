Hypergeometric Distributions and joint families of elliptic curves
Brian Grove and Hasan Saad

Abstract
. Recently, the first author as well as the second author with Ono, Pujahari, and Saikia determined the limiting distribution of values of certain finite field F 1 2 {{}_{2}F_{1}} and F 2 3 {{}_{3}F_{2}} hypergeometric functions. These hypergeometric values are related to Frobenius traces of elliptic curves and their limiting distribution is determined using connections to the theory of modular forms and harmonic Maass forms. Here we determine the limiting distribution of values of some F 3 4 {{}_{4}F_{3}} hypergeometric functions which are sums of traces of Frobenius for a pair of elliptic curves. To obtain this result, we generalize Michel’s work on Sato–Tate laws for families of elliptic curves to the setting of pairs of families, and we show that a generic pair admits an independent Sato–Tate distribution as the finite field grows. To this end, we use various results from the theory of étale cohomology, Deligne’s work on the Weil conjectures, and the work of Katz on monodromy groups. In the cases previously studied using modular methods, we elucidate the connection between the modular forms that appear and the machinery of étale cohomology.

1&1\end{array}\mid\lambda\right)+H_{p}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{1}{d}&1-\frac{1}{d}\\
1&1\end{array}\mid-\lambda\right).

On the other hand, Lemma
2.1
gives that

H_{p}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{1}{d}&1-\frac{1}{d}\\
1&1\end{array}\mid\lambda\right)=a_{p,d}(\lambda)

and (
2.1
) follows.
∎

4.
Background from Representation Theory

Here we recall facts from representation theory which allow for computation of moments using the representations
\mathcal{F}_{\overline{\eta}}
described in Section
3
. Aside from the technical details, this section is essentially a generalization of some of the tools used in
[
OSS23
,
OPSS24
,
Gro24
]
. More precisely, the results in this section combined with Lemma
3.9
elucidate why the Chebyshev polynomial sums

\sum\limits_{\lambda}U_{m}\left(\frac{a_{p}(\lambda)}{2\sqrt{p}}\right)

are precisely the sums with small asymptotic magnitude. Furthermore, this section elucidates why the Catalan numbers are the moments and transforms the induction arguments on Chebyshev polynomials into direct explicit formulas.

In order to compute the moments of traces of Frobenius
a_{p}(\lambda),
we make use of the tensor product representations of
\mathcal{F}_{\overline{\eta}}.

Definition 4.1
.

If
G
is a group,
m\geq 0,
and
\rho:G\to\GL(V)
is a finite-dimensional representation of
G,
the tensor product representation is given by

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho^{\otimes m}\colon\!\ \ \ \ G}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces GL(V^{\otimes m})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \quad g}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho^{\otimes m}(g),}

where

\rho^{\otimes m}(g)(v_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes v_{m}):=\rho(g)(v_{1})\otimes\ldots\otimes\rho(g)(v_{m}).

From the definition, it is obvious that
{\rm Tr}(\rho^{\otimes}(m))(g)={\rm Tr}(\rho)(g)^{m}.
In order to apply the results from Section
3
to
\rho^{\otimes m},
where
\rho
is the standard representation of
\SL_{2},
we decompose
\rho^{\otimes m}
into a direct sum of irreducible representations. To this end, recall that if
m\geq 0
and
\rho:G\to\GL(V)
is a finite-dimensional representation, then we have the
m
-th symmetric power representation

\Sym^{m}\rho:G\to\GL(\Sym^{m}V),

where
\Sym^{m}V:=V^{\otimes m}/S_{m}
is the quotient of
V^{\otimes m}
by the natural action of the symmetric group
S_{m},
and where
G
acts naturally on this quotient space. In this notation, we have the following important facts when
\rho
is the standard representation of
\SL_{2}.

Lemma 4.2

(Theorems 2.6.1 and 5.6.3 of
[
Kow14
]
)
.

If
\rho:\SL_{2}\to V
is the standard representation of
\SL_{2}
on a vector space over a field of characteristic
0
and
m\geq 0,
then
\Sym^{m}\rho
is an irreducible representation of
\SL_{2}
and for all
g\in G,
we have

\tr(g|\Sym^{m}V)=U_{m}\left(\frac{\tr(g|V)}{2}\right),

where
U_{m}
is the
m
-th Chebyshev polynomial of the second kind.

In order to compute the moments in Theorems
1.2
and
1.8
, we decompose the tensor product representations
\rho^{\otimes m}
into a direct sum of symmetric power representations and a trivial representation. The aim of this is to isolate the irreducible nontrivial components, which by Lemma
3.4
, Theorem
3.2
, and Theorem
3.3
have small contributions to the moments. The following well-known lemma (for example, see 2.2. of
[
LZ10
]
) provides the required direct sum decomposition.

Lemma 4.3
.

If
\rho
is the standard representation of
\SL_{2}
and
m\geq 0,
then we have

\rho^{\otimes m}\cong\bigoplus\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\leq m\\
r\equiv m\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 2)\end{subarray}}n_{m}(r)\Sym^{r}\rho,

where

n_{m}(r)=\binom{m}{\frac{m+r}{2}}\cdot\frac{2(r+1)}{m+r+2}.

In particular, when
m=2m_{1}
is even, the multiplicity
n_{m}(0)
of the trivial representation is given by the Catalan number
C(m_{1}).

Remark 4.4
.

We give the following two remarks on Lemma
4.3
.

(1)

In
[
OSS23
,
OPSS24
,
Gro24
]
, the authors compute the moments by an induction argument on the contributions of the Chebyshev polynomials of the traces of Frobenius. Lemma
4.3
serves as a direct expression of the
m
-th moments in terms of the various Chebyshev polynomials that appear in those works. For example, if
m=4,
then we have

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}\left(\frac{a_{p}(\lambda)}{\sqrt{p}}\right)^{4}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}n_{4}(0)U_{0}\left(\frac{a_{p}(\lambda)}{2\sqrt{p}}\right)+n_{4}(2)\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}U_{2}\left(\frac{a_{p}(\lambda)}{2\sqrt{p}}\right)+n_{4}(4)\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}U_{4}\left(\frac{a_{p}(\lambda)}{2\sqrt{p}}\right)

\displaystyle=2p+3\sum\limits_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}U_{2}\left(\frac{a_{p}(\lambda)}{2\sqrt{p}}\right)+\sum\limits_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}U_{4}\left(\frac{a_{p}(\lambda)}{2\sqrt{p}}\right).

This, for example, makes it clear why the statement that

\lim\limits_{p\to\infty}\frac{1}{p}\sum\limits_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}U_{m}\left(\frac{a_{p}(\lambda)}{2\sqrt{p}}\right)=0

for all
m>0,
proved in the aforementioned works, implies that the
4
-th moment is given by the Catalan number
C(2)=2.

(2)

In our work, we only make use of the multiplicity of the trivial representation in
\rho^{\otimes m}.
The full decomposition is noted only for completeness.

In the setting of Theorem
1.9
, Lemma
2.2
implies that if
m\geq 0
is even, then the
m
-th moments of the
H_{p}
functions are normalized even moments of
U_{2}\left(\frac{a^{\Cl}_{p}(\lambda)}{2\sqrt{p}}\right).
In order to compute these moments, we decompose the tensor product representation of
(\Sym^{2}\rho)^{\otimes m}
into a direct sum of symmetric power representations and a trivial representation. For the even moments, we require the following lemma which gives the multiplicity of the trivial representation.

Lemma 4.5
.

If
\rho
is the standard representation of
\SL_{2}
and
m\geq 0
, then the multiplicity of the trivial representation in the direct sum decomposition of
(\Sym^{2}\rho)^{\otimes m}
is given by

(-1)^{m}\cdot\sum\limits_{i=0}^{m}(-1)^{i}\binom{m}{i}\frac{(2i)!}{i!(i+1)!}.

Proof.

If
m=0,
then this is trivial. Now suppose
m\geq 1.

Using Lemma
4.3
, we have that

\rho^{\otimes 2}\cong\Sym^{2}\rho\oplus\varepsilon,

where
\varepsilon
is the trivial representation. Taking
m
-th tensor powers on both sides and comparing the multiplicities of the trivial representation, we have that

\frac{(2m)!}{m!(m+1)!}=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{m}\binom{m}{i}n_{\varepsilon}((\Sym^{2}\rho)^{\otimes i}),

where
n_{\varepsilon}((\Sym^{2}\rho)^{\otimes i})
is the multiplicity of the trivial representation in the direct sum decomposition of
(\Sym^{2}\rho)^{\otimes i}.
Therefore, if we define

a(m):=(-1)^{m}\cdot\sum\limits_{i=0}^{m}(-1)^{i}\binom{m}{i}\frac{(2i)!}{i!(i+1)!},

then, by induction on
m,
our claim is equivalent to showing that

\sum\limits_{i=0}^{m}\binom{m}{i}a(i)=\frac{(2m)!}{m!(m+1)!}.

Expanding the definition of
a(i),
this is equivalent to showing that

\sum\limits_{i=0}^{m-1}(-1)^{i}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{i}(-1)^{j}\binom{i}{j}\binom{m}{i}\cdot\frac{(2j)!}{j!(j+1)!}=-\sum\limits_{i=0}^{m-1}(-1)^{m+i}\binom{m}{i}\frac{(2i)!}{i!(i+1)!}.

By switching the order of summation, we write the left-hand side as

\sum\limits_{j=0}^{m-1}\sum\limits_{i=j}^{m-1}(-1)^{i+j}\binom{i}{j}\binom{m}{i}\frac{(2j)!}{j!(j+1)!}.

However, we have that the inner sum is given by

\frac{(2j)!}{j!(j+1)!}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{m-j-1}(-1)^{i}\frac{m!}{j!i!(m-j-i)!},

which by the binomial theorem is clearly equal to

(-1)^{j+m+1}\cdot\binom{m}{j}\cdot\frac{(2j)!}{j!(j+1)!}.

Putting all this together, we have our claim.
∎

For the odd moments, we require the moments of
U_{2}\left(\frac{a_{p}^{\Cl}(\lambda)}{2\sqrt{p}}\right)
twisted by the trace
\phi_{p}(\lambda+1)
of a Kummer sheaf. To compute those moments, we require the following elementary lemma (see Exercise 2.2.14 of
[
Kow14
]
for example) on the irreducibility of tensor products with one-dimensional representations.

Lemma 4.6
.

If
G
is a group,
V
a finite-dimensional vector space over a field
k,

\rho:G\to\GL(V)
an irreducible representation of
G,
and
\chi:G\to k^{\times}
a one-dimensional representation of
G,
then
\rho\otimes\chi
is irreducible.

In order to use the geometric monodromy computation of Deligne in Lemma
3.9
, we connect the irreducibility of
G^{\geom}
with
G_{\geom}.

Recall that the geometric mondromy group
G_{\geom}
is the Zariski closure of
G^{\geom}
in
\GL(\mathcal{F}_{\overline{\eta}}).
The following well-known lemma (for example, see Exercise 7.1.6 of
[
Kow14
]
) relates the irreducibility of a representation

\rho:G\to\GL(V)

to the irreducibility of the (standard) representation of its Zariski closure
\overline{\rho(G)}
in
\GL(V).

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
G
be a group and
\rho:G\to\GL(V)
be a finite-dimensional linear representation of
G.
We have that
\rho
is irreducible if and only if the standard representation of
\overline{\rho(G)}
is irreducible.

Proof.

Here we write the proof for the reader’s convenience.
If
\rho
is irreducible, it follows immediately that
\overline{\rho(G)}
acts irreducibly. Now, suppose the standard representation of
\overline{\rho(G)}
is irreducible, and let
W
be a subspace of
V
such that

\rho(G)(W)\subset W.

Let
\mathbf{H}
be the subset of elements
\mathbf{h}\in\overline{\rho(G)}
such that

\mathbf{h}\cdot W\subset W.

It is clear that
\mathbf{H}
is a Zariski closed algebraic subgroup of
\GL(V)
which contains
\rho(G).
This implies that
\mathbf{H}=\overline{\rho(G)}.
Since
\overline{\rho(G)}
acts irreducibly,
W=\{0\}
or
W=V,
proving our claim.
∎

The following technical lemma plays an essential role in deducing irreducible decomposition of tensor products of sheaves from the geometric monodromy computation in Theorem
3.6
.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
G
be a group,
\rho_{1},\rho_{2}
be finite-dimensional representations of
G,
and let
r_{1},r_{2}
be Zariski-continuous irreducible representations of the Zariski closures
\overline{\rho_{1}(G)},\overline{\rho_{2}(G)}
respectively. If

\overline{(\rho_{1}\oplus\rho_{2})(G)}=\overline{\rho_{1}(G)}\times\overline{\rho_{2}(G)},

then
r_{1}\circ\rho_{1}\otimes r_{2}\circ\rho_{2}
is an irreducible representation of
G.

Proof.

The assumption

\overline{(\rho_{1}\oplus\rho_{2})(G)}=\overline{\rho_{1}(G)}\times\overline{\rho_{2}(G)},

implies that

(4.1)

(r_{1},r_{2})(\overline{(\rho_{1}\oplus\rho_{2})(G)})=r_{1}(\overline{\rho_{1}(G)})\times r_{2}(\overline{\rho_{2}(G)}).

Since the pair
(r_{1},r_{2})
is Zariski-continuous, we have that

(4.2)

\overline{(r_{1},r_{2})((\rho_{1}\oplus\rho_{2})(G))}\supset(r_{1},r_{2})(\overline{(\rho_{1}\oplus\rho_{2})(G))}.

Putting (
4.1
) and (
4.2
) together, we have that

\overline{(r_{1}\circ\rho_{1}\otimes r_{2}\circ\rho_{2})(G)}\supset(r_{1}\boxtimes r_{2})(\overline{\rho_{1}(G)}\times\overline{\rho_{2}(G)}),

where
r_{1}\boxtimes r_{2}
is the external tensor product (see p. 64 of
[
Kow14
]
) of
r_{1}
and
r_{2}.
Since
r_{1}
and
r_{2}
are irreducible, we have that (see Proposition 2.3.23 of
[
Kow14
]
)
r_{1}\boxtimes r_{2}
is irreducible, and therefore, the standard representation of
\overline{(r_{1}\circ\rho_{2}\otimes r_{2}\circ\rho_{2})(G)}
is irreducible. By Lemma
4.7
, we conclude that
r_{1}\circ\rho_{1}\otimes r_{2}\circ\rho_{2}
is an irreducible representation of
G.

∎

Here we assemble the results from Sections

To make this precise, for

to

We further assume that there the
j
-invariants of

\ell

In this notation, we have the following preliminary version of Theorem

Proposition 5.1

If

then we have

\lim\limits_{p\to\infty}\frac{1}{p^{m/2+n/2+1}}\sum\limits_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}a_{1,p}(\lambda)^{m}a_{2,p}(\lambda)^{n}=\begin{cases}C(m_{1})C(n_{1})&\ \ \ \text{ if }m=2m_{1}\text{ and }n=2n_{1}\text{ are even}\\

computes the moments of one-parameter families of elliptic curves with nonconstant

by the same proof), recovering the result of Michel
[
Mic95

, of which this is a generalization.

In order to prove this proposition, we first compute the multiplicity of the trivial representation in the
\ell
-adic sheaves
\mathcal{G}_{p,m,n}:=\mathcal{F}_{1,p}^{\otimes m}\otimes\mathcal{F}_{2,p}^{\otimes n}.
To make this precise, let
U
be the subset of
\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{1}
on which both
\mathcal{F}_{1}
and
\mathcal{F}_{2}
are lisse (see Lemma
3.8
) and for each prime

denote by
U_{p}
and
\overline{U_{p}}
the base-changes of
U
to
\mathbb{F}_{p}
and
\overline{\mathbb{F}_{p}}
respectively. In this notation, we have the following computation.

Lemma 5.2
.

If
m,n\geq 0
and
p\nmid N,
then the multiplicity of the trivial representation in the direct-sum decomposition of
\mathcal{G}_{p,m,n}
as a representation of

is given by

n_{\varepsilon}(\mathcal{G}_{m,n,p})=\begin{cases}C(m_{1})C(n_{1})&\ \ \ \text{ if }m=2m_{1}\text{ and }n=2n_{1}\text{ are even}\\
0&\ \ \ \text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

Proof.

Denote by
\rho_{i,p}
the representation

\rho_{i,p}:G^{\geom}\to\mathcal{F}_{i,p,\overline{\eta}}.

As representations of
G^{\geom},
if
i=1,2,
we have the decompositions

(5.1)

\rho_{i,p}^{\otimes m}\cong\left(\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{n_{i}}n_{m}(i,k)\cdot\pi_{i,k,p}\circ\rho_{i,p}\right)\oplus n_{m}(i,0)\varepsilon,

where the representations

are irreducible pairwise nonisomorphic nontrivial representations of
\rho_{i,p}(G^{\geom})
and where

is the trivial representation. Part (1) of Lemma
3.9
states that the Zariski closure of

Therefore, by Lemma

, we have that the
\pi_{i,k,p}

are the multiplicities in Lemma

The next lemma computes the asymptotics of trace sums of the nontrivial irreducible representations arising in the direct sum decomposition of
\mathcal{G}_{p,m,n}.
More explicitly, the proof of Lemma
5.2
shows that these representations are of the form
\Sym^{m_{1}}(\mathcal{F}_{1})\otimes\Sym^{n_{1}}(\mathcal{F}_{2}),
where
m_{1}\leq m,n_{1}\leq n,
with appropriate multiplicities. Therefore, the following lemma proves that if
m_{1}
and

are not both zero, then we have

\lim\limits_{p\to\infty}\frac{1}{p}\sum\limits_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}U_{m_{1}}\left(\frac{a_{1,p}(\lambda)}{2\sqrt{p}}\right)U_{n_{1}}\left(\frac{a_{2,p}(\lambda)}{2\sqrt{p}}\right)=0.

Lemma 5.3
.

If
p
is a prime and
m,n\geq 0,
then we have

\sum\limits_{\pi}\sum\limits_{\lambda\in U_{p}(\mathbb{F}_{p})}\tr(\Frob_{\lambda}|\pi)=O_{m,n}(p^{1/2})\ \ \text{ as }p\to\infty,

where
\pi
runs over the nontrivial irreducible representations arising in the direct sum decomposition of
\mathcal{G}_{p,m,n}.

Proof.

If
\pi
is an irreducible representation as in the statement, it is clear that
\pi,
viewed as an
\ell
-adic sheaf, is lisse on
U_{p}.
Therefore, Theorem
3.2
gives us that

\sum\limits_{\lambda\in U_{p}(\mathbb{F}_{p})}\tr(\Frob_{\lambda}|\pi)=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{2}\tr(\Frob_{p}|H_{c}^{i}(\overline{U_{p}},\pi)).

By Lemma
3.4
, we have that
H_{c}^{0}=H_{c}^{2}=0,
and therefore,

\sum\limits_{\lambda\in U_{p}}\tr(\Frob_{\lambda}|\pi)=\tr(\Frob_{p}|H_{c}^{1}(\overline{U_{p}},\pi)).

Theorems

,
3.5
and Lemma

then imply that

\left|\sum\limits_{\lambda\in U_{p}}\tr(\Frob_{\lambda}|\pi)\right|\leq p^{1/2}\cdot\rank(\pi)\cdot(\#(\mathbb{P}^{1}\setminus\overline{U_{p}})(\overline{\mathbb{F}_{p}})-2).

Since
\sum\limits_{\pi}\rank(\pi)\leq\rank(\mathcal{G}_{p,m,n})=2^{m+n},
the claim follows.
∎

Remark 5.4
.

In the proof of Lemma
5.3
, we show that as

we have

\sum\limits_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}U_{m_{1}}\left(\frac{a_{1,p}(\lambda)}{2\sqrt{p}}\right)U_{n_{1}}\left(\frac{a_{2,p}(\lambda)}{2\sqrt{p}}\right)=O(p^{1/2})

Now, for

let

and let

be the family

defined in Section

. In this case, we have an explicit description of the Frobenius traces on this “mysterious” cohomology group in terms of the traces of Hecke operators on spaces of cusp frorms. For example, if

and

is even, then (see Section 4.2 of

\tr(\Frob_{p}|H_{c}^{1}(\overline{U_{p}},\pi))=-\frac{{\rm Tr}_{2r+2}(\Gamma_{1}(3),p)-c(2r)}{p^{r}},

where

is the trace of the

and where

The same remark applies to odd

The advantage of the modular method is that one understands the trace of Frobenius on the cohomology very explicitly. On the other hand, the advantage of the machinery of étale cohomology is that one can derive results on distributions in a more general setting where there is no underlying modular framework.

Now we prove Proposition
5.1
.

Proof of Proposition
5.1
.

By (
3.3
), we have that

\frac{1}{p^{m/2+n/2+1}}\sum\limits_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}a_{1,p}(\lambda)^{m}a_{2,p}(\lambda)^{n}=\frac{1}{p}\sum\limits_{\lambda\in U_{p}(\mathbb{F}_{p})}\tr(\Frob_{\lambda}|\mathcal{G}_{p,m,n})+O_{m,n}(p^{-1}),

as

But then, Lemma

\frac{1}{p}\sum\limits_{\lambda\in U_{p}(\mathbb{F}_{p})}\tr(\Frob_{\lambda}|\mathcal{G}_{p,m,n})=n_{\varepsilon}(\mathcal{G}_{m,n,p})+O_{m,n}(p^{-1/2}),

In order to obtain the necessary moments for Theorem

, we require the following modification of Proposition

.

Proposition 5.5
.

If

then we have

\lim\limits_{p\to\infty}\frac{1}{p^{m+1}}\sum\limits_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}(\phi_{p}(\lambda+1)(a_{p}^{\Cl}(\lambda)^{2}-p))^{m}=\begin{cases}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{m}(-1)^{i}\binom{m}{i}\frac{(2i)!}{i!(i+1)!}&\ \ \ \text{if }m\text{ is even}\\

Proof.

If

\frac{1}{p^{m}}\sum\limits_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}(\phi_{p}(\lambda+1)(a_{p}^{\Cl}(\lambda)^{2}-p))^{m}=\sum\limits_{\lambda\in U_{p}(\mathbb{F}_{p})}\tr(\Frob_{\lambda}|(\Sym^{2}\mathcal{F})^{\otimes m})+O_{m}(p^{-1}).

Using Lemma

, we decompose the right-hand sum as

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\lambda\in U_{p}(\mathbb{F}_{p})}\tr(\Frob_{\lambda}|(\Sym^{2}\mathcal{F})^{\otimes m})

\mathcal{F}

\frac{1}{p^{m}}\sum\limits_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}(\phi_{p}(\lambda+1)(a_{p}^{\Cl}(\lambda)^{2}-p))^{m}=\sum\limits_{\lambda\in U_{p}(\mathbb{F}_{p})}\tr(\Frob_{\lambda}|(\Sym^{2}\mathcal{F})^{\otimes m}\otimes([+1]^{\ast}\mathcal{L}_{\phi})+O_{m}(p^{-1}),

Remark 5.6
.

Just as in Proposition

Finally, to compute the moments in Theorem
1.2
in terms of the mixed moments in Theorem
1.5
, we require the following combinatorial lemma.

Lemma 5.7
.

If
m\geq 1,
then we have

C(m)C(m+1)=\sum_{s=0}^{m}\binom{2m}{2s}C(m-s)C(s).

Proof.

If
s\geq 0,
by definition of the Catalan numbers, we have

C(s)=\frac{1}{s+1}\binom{2s}{s}.

Expanding the right-hand side, we then have that

\sum_{s=0}^{m}\binom{2m}{2s}C(m-s)C(s)=\sum_{s=0}^{m}\frac{1}{(s+1)(m-s+1)}\frac{(2m)!}{(s!)^{2}((m-s)!)^{2}}.

We rewrite this expression as

\sum_{s=0}^{m}\binom{2m}{2s}C(m-s)C(s)=\frac{(2m)!}{m!(m+2)!}\sum_{s=0}^{m}\binom{m+2}{s+1}\binom{m}{m-s}.

An application of the Chu–Vandermonde identity (see Example 1.17 of
[

]

\sum_{s=0}^{m}\binom{2m}{2s}C(m-s)C(s)=\frac{(2m)!}{m!(m+1)!}\cdot\frac{(2m+2)!}{(m+1)!(m+2)!}

which proves our claim.
∎

\mathcal{L}_{p}
is a rank-
1

\ell
-adic sheaf on
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}^{1}
with

\mathcal{F}_{1,p}\cong_{\geom}\mathcal{F}_{2,p}\otimes\mathcal{L}_{p},

then
\mathcal{L}_{p}
is geometrically constant.

Proof.

First, by Lemma
3.10
and our assumption of good or multiplicative reduction, it is clear that
\mathcal{F}_{1,p}
and
\mathcal{F}_{2,p}
must have the same points of ramification. By Lemma
4.9
, we have that
\mathcal{L}_{p}
must be lisse on
\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}^{1}.
Furthermore, by Lemma
3.9
, there exists
N\geq 1
such that if
p\nmid N
, then
\mathcal{F}_{1,p}
and
\mathcal{F}_{2,p}
are tamely ramified at
\infty
and therefore,
\mathcal{L}_{p}
is tamely ramified at
\infty.
Therefore,
\mathcal{L}_{p}
is geometrically constant (see Example 5.2 (f) of Chapter I of
[
Mil80
]
).
∎

Lemma 6.5
.

Assume that
E_{1,\lambda}
and
E_{2,\lambda}
have either good or multiplicative reduction everywhere. There exists
N\geq 1
such that if
p\nmid N
is a prime and
\mathcal{L}_{p}
is a rank-
1

\ell
-adic sheaf on
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}^{1}
with

\mathcal{F}_{1,p}\cong_{\geom}\mathcal{F}_{2,p}\otimes\mathcal{L}_{p},

then we have

\mathcal{F}_{2,p}\otimes\mathcal{L}_{p}\cong\mathcal{F}_{2,d,p},

where
d\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times},\pi_{2,d}
is the projection map on the quadratic twist
E_{2,\lambda,d}
and
\mathcal{F}_{2,d,p}=R^{1}\pi_{2,d,!}\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}.

Proof.

If
\mathcal{L}_{p}
is geometrically constant, then it follows by Proposition 2.8.2 of
[
Kow14
]
and (
3.2
) that for
\lambda\in\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}^{1},
we have

\tr(\Frob_{\lambda}|\mathcal{L}_{p})=\chi(\Frob_{p}^{\deg\lambda}),

where
\chi
is a character on
{\rm Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{F}_{p}}/\mathbb{F}_{p}).
By observing that the traces of Frobenius
\mathcal{F}_{1,p}
and
\mathcal{F}_{2,p}
are
\mathbb{R}
-valued for all embeddings of
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}\to\mathbb{C}
and by the purity of weight
0
of the sheaves
\mathcal{F}_{1,p},\mathcal{F}_{2,p},
we have that
\chi
is either a trivial or a quadratic character.

Now, take
d^{\prime}
to be a nonsquare in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
and consider
d=(d^{\prime})^{{\mathrm{o}rd}(\chi)}.
By comparing the traces of Frobenius of
\mathcal{F}_{2,p}\otimes\mathcal{L}_{p}
and
\mathcal{F}_{2,d,p},
the quasi-orthogonality relations of irreducible sheaves (see Theorem 5.2 of
[
FKMS19
]
) imply that, up to enlarging
N,
we have
\mathcal{F}_{2,p}\otimes\mathcal{L}_{p}\cong\mathcal{F}_{2,d,p}
for all primes
p\nmid N.

∎

Proof of Proposition
6.3
.

This immediately follows from Lemmas
6.1
,
6.4
,
6.5
, and Theorem
6.2
.
∎

7.
Some Distributions

To obtain Corollaries
1.4
and
1.6
, we combine Theorems
1.2
and
1.5
with the method of moments. In this section, we compute the required probability density functions associated to a given sequence of moments or mixed moments.

For the mixed moments, the following lemma gives the probability density functions.

Lemma 7.1
.

If
p
is prime and
i=1,2,
consider maps

f_{i,p}:\mathbb{F}_{p}\to[-2,2].

Suppose that for each
m,n\geq 0
we have that

\lim\limits_{p\to\infty}\frac{1}{p}\sum\limits_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}f_{1,p}(\lambda)^{m}f_{2,p}(\lambda)^{n}=\begin{cases}C(m_{1})C(n_{1})&\ \ \ \text{ if }m=2m_{1}\text{ and }n=2n_{1}\text{ are even}\\
0&\ \ \ \text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

If
-2\leq a_{i},b_{i}\leq 2,
then we have

\lim\limits_{p\to\infty}\frac{\#\{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{p}:(f_{1,p}(\lambda),f_{2,p}(\lambda))\in[a_{1},b_{1}]\times[a_{2},b_{2}]\}}{p}=\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\int_{a_{1}}^{b_{1}}\int_{a_{2}}^{b_{2}}\sqrt{4-x_{1}^{2}}\cdot\sqrt{4-x_{2}^{2}}\,dx_{1}dx_{2}.

Sketch of Proof.

The method of moments (see Exercises 30.5 and 30.6 of
[
Bil12
]
) states that in order to compute the limiting distribution, under very general settings, it suffices to show that the limiting moments coincide with the moments of the claimed distribution. In our case, this is precisely the statement that if
m,n\geq 0,
then we have that

\int_{-2}^{2}\int_{-2}^{2}x_{1}^{m}x_{2}^{n}\sqrt{4-x_{1}^{2}}\sqrt{4-x_{2}^{2}}dx_{1}dx_{2}=\begin{cases}C(m_{1})C(n_{1})&\ \ \ \text{ if }m=2m_{1}\text{ and }n=2n_{1}\text{ are even}\\
0&\ \ \ \text{ otherwise,}\end{cases}

which is straightforward.
∎

We now determine the probability density function for the moments arising in Theorem
1.2
. To this end, we recall the definition of the Meijer
G
-function (see Appendix of
[
EMOT54
]
).

Definition 7.2
.

Let
0\leq m\leq q,0\leq n\leq p
be integers and suppose
a_{1},\ldots,a_{p},b_{1},\ldots,b_{q}
are complex numbers such that
a_{k}-b_{j}\not\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
for all
1\leq k\leq n
and
1\leq j\leq m.
Furthermore, suppose
\Re(\nu)<-1,
where

\nu:=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{q}b_{j}-\sum\limits_{j=1}^{p}a_{p}.

In this notation if
z\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
and
|z|\leq 1,
the Meijer
G
-function is given as

G_{p,q}^{m,n}\left[\begin{matrix}a_{1}&\ldots&a_{p}\\
b_{1}&\ldots&b_{q}\end{matrix}\;\bigg|\;z\right]:=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\mathop{\text{\Large$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle L$}}$}}\nolimits\frac{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{m}\Gamma(b_{j}-s)\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n}\Gamma(1-a_{j}+s)}{\prod\limits_{j=m+1}^{q}\Gamma(1-b_{j}+s)\prod\limits_{j=n+1}^{p}\Gamma(a_{j}-s)}z^{s}ds,

where
\Gamma(\cdot)
denotes Euler’s Gamma function, and where
L
is a loop beginning and ending at
-\infty
that encircles all poles of
\Gamma(1-a_{k}+s)
for
1\leq k\leq n
exactly once in the positive direction and does not encircle any poles of
\Gamma(b_{j}-s)
for
1\leq j\leq m.

In order to compute integrals involving Meijer
G
-functions, we require the following key transformation and integral identities.

Lemma 7.3
.

The following are true.

(1)

If
\rho\in\mathbb{C},
then we have

z^{\rho}\cdot\,G_{p,q}^{m,n}\left[\begin{matrix}a_{1}&\ldots&a_{p}\\
b_{1}&\ldots&b_{q}\end{matrix}\;\bigg|\;z\right]=G_{p,q}^{m,n}\left[\begin{matrix}a_{1}+\rho&\ldots&a_{p}+\rho\\
b_{1}+\rho&\ldots&b_{q}+\rho\end{matrix}\;\bigg|\;z\right].

(2)

Under general conditions (see 20.5 of
[
EMOT54
]
), if
\alpha,\beta,z\in\mathbb{R},
then we have

\int_{0}^{1}x^{-\alpha}(1-x)^{\alpha-\beta-1}G_{p,q}^{m,n}\left[\begin{matrix}a_{1}&\ldots&a_{p}\\
b_{1}&\ldots&b_{q}\end{matrix}\;\bigg|\;zx\right]dx=\Gamma(\alpha-\beta)\cdot G_{p+1,q+1}^{m,n+1}\left[\begin{matrix}\alpha&a_{1}&\ldots&a_{p}\\
b_{1}&\ldots&b_{q}&\beta\end{matrix}\;\bigg|\;z\right].

We will also make use of the following elementary lemma which expresses products of Catalan numbers in terms of ratios of Gamma functions.

Lemma 7.4
.

If
m\geq 0,
then we have

C(m)C(m+1)=\frac{4\cdot 16^{m}}{\pi}\frac{\Gamma\left(m+\frac{1}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(m+\frac{3}{2}\right)}{\Gamma(m+2)\Gamma(m+3)}.

Proof.

Using the property that
\Gamma(x+1)=x\Gamma(x)
and that
\Gamma(\frac{1}{2})=\sqrt{\pi},
we have that

\Gamma\left(m+\frac{1}{2}\right)=\frac{(2m-1)!!}{2^{m}}\sqrt{\pi},

where
(2k-1)!!:=(2k-1)(2k-3)\cdot\ldots\cdot 1.
Therefore, we have that

\begin{split}\frac{4^{m}\Gamma\left(m+\frac{1}{2}\right)}{\Gamma(m+2)\sqrt{\pi}}&=\frac{2^{m}(2m-1)!!}{(m+1)!}\\
&=\frac{1}{m+1}\binom{2m}{m}\\
&=C(m)\end{split}

and our claim follows immediately.
∎

Finally, we compute the probability density function associated to the moments that appear in Theorem
1.2
.

Lemma 7.5
.

If
p
is prime, consider maps

f_{p}:\mathbb{F}_{p}\to[-4,4].

Suppose that for each
m\geq 0
we have that

\lim\limits_{p\to\infty}\frac{1}{p}\sum\limits_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}f_{p}(\lambda)^{m}=\begin{cases}C(m_{1})C(m_{1}+1)&\ \ \ \text{ if }m=2m_{1}\text{ is even}\\
0&\ \ \ \text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

If
-4\leq a<b\leq 4,
then we have

\lim\limits_{p\to\infty}\frac{\#\left\{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{p}:f_{p}(\lambda)\in[a,b]\right\}}{p}=\int_{a}^{b}\frac{4}{\pi|t|}G_{2,2}^{2,0}\left[\begin{matrix}2&3\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\\
\frac{1}{2}&\frac{3}{2}\end{matrix}\;\bigg|\;\frac{t^{2}}{16}\right]dt.

Proof.

By the method of moments (see Theorems 30.5 and 30.6 of
[
Bil12
]
), it suffices to show that for
m\geq 0,
we have

\int_{-4}^{4}t^{m}\cdot\frac{4}{\pi|t|}G_{2,2}^{2,0}\left[\begin{matrix}2&3\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\\
\frac{1}{2}&\frac{3}{2}\end{matrix}\;\bigg|\;\frac{t^{2}}{16}\right]dt=\begin{cases}C(m_{1})C(m_{1}+1)&\ \ \ \text{ if }m=2m_{1}\text{ is even}\\
0&\ \ \ \text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}.

If
m
is odd, this is clear since the integrand is odd. If
m=2m_{1},
the integrand is even and we rewrite the integral as

\int_{-4}^{4}t^{m}\cdot\frac{4}{\pi|t|}G_{2,2}^{2,0}\left[\begin{matrix}2&3\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\\
\frac{1}{2}&\frac{3}{2}\end{matrix}\;\bigg|\;\frac{t^{2}}{16}\right]dt=\frac{2\cdot 16^{m_{1}}}{\pi}\int_{0}^{4}\left(\frac{t^{2}}{16}\right)^{m_{1}-\frac{1}{2}}\cdot G_{2,2}^{2,0}\left[\begin{matrix}2&3\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\\
\frac{1}{2}&\frac{3}{2}\end{matrix}\;\bigg|\;\frac{t^{2}}{16}\right]\,dt

Using (1) of Lemma
7.3
and the change-of-variables
\omega=\frac{t^{2}}{16},
we have

\int_{-4}^{4}t^{m}\cdot\frac{4}{\pi|t|}G_{2,2}^{2,0}\left[\begin{matrix}2&3\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\\
\frac{1}{2}&\frac{3}{2}\end{matrix}\;\bigg|\;\frac{t^{2}}{16}\right]dt=\frac{4\cdot 16^{m_{1}}}{\pi}\int_{0}^{1}\omega^{-\frac{1}{2}}\cdot G_{2,2}^{2,0}\left[\begin{matrix}m_{1}+\frac{3}{2}&m_{1}+\frac{5}{2}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\\
m_{1}&m_{1}+1\end{matrix}\;\bigg|\;\omega\right]d\omega

Computing the integral using (2) of Lemma
7.3
, we obtain

\int_{-4}^{4}t^{m}\cdot\frac{4}{\pi|t|}G_{2,2}^{2,0}\left[\begin{matrix}2&3\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\\
\frac{1}{2}&\frac{3}{2}\end{matrix}\;\bigg|\;\frac{t^{2}}{16}\right]dt=\frac{4\cdot 16^{m_{1}}}{\pi}G_{3,3}^{2,1}\left[\begin{matrix}\frac{1}{2}&m_{1}+\frac{3}{2}&m_{1}+\frac{5}{2}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\\
m_{1}&m_{1}+1&-\frac{1}{2}\end{matrix}\;\bigg|\;1\right]

Applying (1) of Lemma
7.3
with
z=1
and
\rho=-m_{1},
we obtain

\int_{-4}^{4}t^{m}\cdot\frac{4}{\pi|t|}G_{2,2}^{2,0}\left[\begin{matrix}2&3\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\\
\frac{1}{2}&\frac{3}{2}\end{matrix}\;\bigg|\;\frac{t^{2}}{16}\right]dt=\frac{4\cdot 16^{m_{1}}}{\pi}G_{3,3}^{2,1}\left[\begin{matrix}\frac{1}{2}-m_{1}&\frac{3}{2}&\frac{5}{2}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\\
0&1&-m_{1}-\frac{1}{2}\end{matrix}\;\bigg|\;1\right]

The residue theorem then implies that

\begin{split}G_{3,3}^{2,1}\left[\begin{matrix}\frac{1}{2}-m_{1}&\frac{3}{2}&\frac{5}{2}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\\
0&1&-m_{1}-\frac{1}{2}\end{matrix}\;\bigg|\;1\right]&=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{L}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}+m_{1}+s\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{3}{2}+m_{1}+s\right)}\cdot\frac{\Gamma(-s)\Gamma(1-s)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{3}{2}-s\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{5}{2}-s\right)}ds\\
&=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{L}\frac{1}{\frac{1}{2}+m_{1}+s}\cdot\frac{\Gamma(-s)\Gamma(1-s)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{3}{2}-s\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{5}{2}-s\right)}ds\\
&=\underset{s=-\frac{1}{2}-m_{1}}{\Res}\left(\frac{1}{\frac{1}{2}+m_{1}+s}\cdot\frac{\Gamma(-s)\Gamma(1-s)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{3}{2}-s\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{5}{2}-s\right)}\right)\\
&=\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}+m_{1}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{3}{2}+m_{1}\right)}{\Gamma(2+m_{1})\Gamma(3+m_{1})}.\end{split}

By Lemma
7.4
, our claim follows.
∎

YOLO-TME: A UAV landing detection algorithm that is suitable for Polar ice Floe base stations
Authors: Songwei Zhang, Yu Zhang, Yinke Dou, Liwei Kou, Guangyu Zuo, Yuan Liu, Leikun Yang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn the environments of the North and South Poles, ice floe drifts lead to excessive errors in the GPS and real-time kinematic (RTK) signals of the unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV)-based cross-domain collaborative monitoring systems deployed on these floes, which makes it impossible to complete related tasks such as UAV return and landing. Moreover, deep learning-based target detection methods suffer from insufficient accuracy and real-time performance when addressing low-contrast, fog- and snow-obscured, and high-elevation polar images. Therefore, this study integrates transformer-based convolution (TransConv) into You Only Look Once (YOLO)v11 to strengthen the ability of the network to model the global information contained in fog- and snow-obscured images and to achieve improved target detection performance in complex environments. A mist global feature pyramid network (MistGFPN) is designed to focus on the ability to extract features from small targets to improve the target detection performance achieved at high altitudes. An efficient asymmetric detection head (EADH) is proposed to improve the frames per second (FPS) and real-time detection performance of the target detection model. Finally, several comparative experiments are designed to verify the effectiveness and real-time performance of the proposed model. The experimental results obtained on homemade datasets show that the proposed model (YOLO-TME) improves the accuracy, recall, average accuracy, and F1 score metrics by 4.2%, 5.8%, 5.5%, and 5.1%, respectively, over those of the original YOLOv11 model, which indicates that YOLO-TME provides substantially improved detection accuracy while maintaining high detection speed, thus satisfying the real-time landing sign detection requirements imposed in polar environments.Keywords: YOLOv11, UAV landing marker, Transformer-based convolution, Mist global feature pyramid network, Efficient asymmetric detection headSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

In natural polar habitats, water, floating ice, and land are intertwined. Unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV)-based cross-domain collaborative monitoring systems are ideal for conducting prolonged monitoring and research operations in polar environments owing to their flexibility, cross-domain capabilities, and precision1. Nonetheless, accurate landing has emerged as an unavoidable issue in practice; this task includes autonomous returning, replenishing energy, and interacting with a base station2. Specifically, the three core technical challenges faced by this study are low contrast interference from adverse weather, bottlenecks in high-altitude small target detection, and delays in dynamic environmental perception.

Consequently, some experts have employed traditional GPS positioning technology3 and RTK positioning technology4,5 to carry out UAV landing tasks and realize landing positioning according to the relative positioning data of the base station and the UAV. However, in such a dynamic polar environment, the base station position changes in real time with the displacement of floating ice; furthermore, GNSS signals are susceptible to ionospheric interference6. Therefore, to improve stability, it is necessary to combine it with dedicated antenna designs7,8. Nevertheless, existing technologies have difficulty providing real-time positioning data concerning the landing point, thus resulting in the inability of UAVs to complete tasks such as precise homing and landing2.

The emergence of digital image processing technology in UAVs has led traditional UAV image positioning algorithms to depend primarily on feature point matching and geometric modelling for landmark positioning purposes6. In the Arctic environment, the surfaces of ice and snow possess a uniform texture and exhibit minimal distinguishing features, whereas the dynamic variations exhibited by drifting sea ice prevent standard algorithms from efficiently extracting and matching feature points. Moreover, the severe weather conditions that are typical of polar regions, including dense fog, strong winds, and snowfall, can diminish the clarity and contrast levels of images, hence exacerbating the challenges faced by conventional algorithms in placing tasks9. Algorithms that depend on optimal weather conditions and a stable environment cannot accommodate the monitoring requirements of polar environments, which are intricate and demand real-time responsiveness.

The ongoing advancement of deep learning-based object detection technology has enabled deep neural network-based algorithms to acquire extensive image feature representations and achieve robust environmental adaptability, thereby significantly enhancing the detection accuracy and computational efficiency of UAV image positioning algorithms. Object detection algorithms based on deep learning can generally be categorized into two-stage and one-stage algorithms10. Two-stage object detection methods, which are exemplified by the region-based convolutional neural network (R-CNN) series, exhibit remarkable detection accuracy; nevertheless, the considerable redundancy in their convolutional feature computations renders them rather sluggish11. In a UAV positioning system, failure to rectify coordinates in real time may result in a significant discrepancy between the landing location and the designated site. Consequently, this two-stage technique is inappropriate for the existing polar UAV placement context, which demands elevated standards. You Only Look Once (YOLO), a quintessential single-stage detection method, typically results in increased detection speed and real-time responsiveness12; it can immediately receive photos and produce the category and associated position data of the items in the input images. Nevertheless, the extensive production of candidate boxes may marginally compromise the resulting detection accuracy13. Owing to the stringent real-time demands of UAV-based landing positioning systems, this study employs a single-stage method while achieving enhanced detection accuracy14.

With the rapid development of deep learning technology, the YOLO series is constantly evolving to meet more complex object detection needs. As the most widely used version in the YOLO series12, YOLOv5 adopts a streamlined and efficient model architecture, thereby achieving a good balance between real-time performance and accuracy, and is particularly suitable for scenarios requiring rapid responses. YOLOv8 introduces a more complex attention mechanism based on YOLOv5, thereby making object detection not only more accurate but also adaptable to more complex environments10,15. Ze Liu et al. proposed a YOLOv8-based transformer architecture (Swin Transformer) in their paper Swin Transformer: Hierarchical Vision Transformer using Shifted Windows; via hierarchical design and shifted window mechanism, it addresses the scale diversity of visual entities and computational complexity of high-resolution images, but remains insufficient in solving issues caused by conditions like snowy and windy weather in this work16. Although YOLOv10 excels in terms of its lightweight design and inference speed, the C3 module at its core has a weak ability to model the global context. Moreover, the reliance of YOLOv10 on traditional nonmaximum suppression (NMS) leads to a performance gap between the training and inference phases, thus making the algorithm prone to missed detections in scenarios involving fog and snow occlusion as well as small object detection17. YOLO11 is the latest version of real-time object detectors in the YOLO series. Building on the success of the previous versions, it introduces two new modules: C3k2 and C2PSA18. The C3k2 module enhances small object feature extraction through hierarchical convolution, and the C2PSA module incorporates a spatial attention mechanism19. YOLO11 also uses YOLOv10’s NMS-free training strategy17, thereby achieving end-to-end object detection capability, which further improves the feature extraction ability of the model and enables it to effectively capture subtle changes in detected targets during dynamic changes. With its innovative modules and optimized feature extraction mechanism in deep learning models20, YOLO11 can still achieve high-precision object detection in extreme environments, such as low-contrast backgrounds, severe fog and snow occlusion, and rapid dynamic changes in positioning targets. This greatly enhances the reliability of UAV positioning systems in dynamic target detection and real-time response, thereby ensuring the smooth execution of tasks such as homing and landing.

This research uses the YOLOv11 network as its foundational framework and enhances this network to develop the YOLO-TME network, which attains increased target identification accuracy while guaranteeing that the target detection process satisfies real-time requirements. The primary innovations and contributions are as follows.

1) Implementing transformer-based convolution (TransConv) to enhance the target detection process in complex images acquired under hazy, windy, and snowy conditions.

2) Developing the mist global feature pyramid network (MistGFPN) to augment small target recognition tasks and provide enhanced performance in low-contrast conditions.

3) Applying efficient asymmetric detection to increase the computational efficiency and real-time performance of the target detection process in severe settings.

This work uses the fogging algorithm to create a novel fogged landing flag dataset augmented with random fog thickness levels.

The structure of this paper is as follows: Chap. 2 (Related Work) systematically reviews the evolution of traditional object detection methods and deep learning algorithms in UAV landing image detection and analyses the core challenges of UAV image detection in polar environments and the limitations of existing research. Chapter 3 (Methods) provides a detailed explanation of the core module design of YOLO-TME, including the transformer-based convolution global information modelling mechanism, the mist global feature pyramid network’s small target feature enhancement strategy, and the efficient asymmetric detection head’s lightweight real-time detection framework. Chapter 4 (Experiments) validates the effectiveness of each module through ablation experiments by comparing the comprehensive performance of this dataset and public datasets (COCO, VisDrone, and UAVDT) in terms of accuracy, real-time performance, and generalizability. Chapter 5 (Conclusion) summarizes the innovative contributions of this paper, which include the increase in detection accuracy and real-time performance of UAV landing markers in images with specific low-contrast backgrounds or fog and snow occlusions and images of dynamically changing targets in polar environments. This section discusses the limitations of the model in embedded deployment and extreme occlusion scenarios and proposes directions for future research.

Related work

Object recognition technology has progressed from conventional handcrafted feature-based techniques to deep learning algorithms21. Despite the substantial improvements in detection accuracy afforded by deep learning, problems persist, including perspective variations, inconsistent scaling conditions, and environmental interference in object detection tasks performed with UAV imagery.

Traditional object detection methods

The majority of early object detection systems have relied on manually constructed features. Owing to the absence of efficient image representations at that time, intricate feature representations and diverse acceleration strategies needed to be developed. P. Viola and M. Jones introduced the VJ detector, which accomplished real-time facial detection without any limitations for the first time22. N. Dalal and B. Triggs introduced the histogram of oriented gradients (HOG) feature descriptor, refined the scale-invariant feature transform (SIFT), and developed a shape context23,24. P. Felzenszwalb introduced a deformable part model (DPM) utilizing the HOG detector, which was characterized as a star-shaped model that adheres to the “divide and conquer” detection principle25. Although contemporary object detectors significantly outperform these earlier models in terms of detection accuracy, the latter approaches continue to exert a substantial influence on the object detection domain.

Deep learning-based object detection methods

The swift advancement of deep learning techniques utilizing labelled datasets has led to the prevalence of neural network-based object detection algorithms, which yield detection outcomes. The current deep learning-centric object detection algorithms can be categorized into anchor-based approaches and anchor-independent methods based on the prior designs of their anchor boxes. Recently, to achieve hyperparameter optimization in anchor frame design tasks and mitigate the challenges associated with model generalization, researchers have started to directly apply key point regression to derive the bounding box predicted for a target; these methods include CornerNet26, ExtremeNet27, ATSS28, and YOLOx29. Conversely, anchor frame-based methodologies employ preestablished anchor frames as prior knowledge and achieve parameter modifications through training, progressively converging to the target bounding box; these approaches are exemplified by the YOLO series (YOLOv530, YOLOv831, and YOLOv1032, the R-CNN series (R-CNN33, Faster R-CNN34, and Mask-RCNN35, FCOS36, and RetinaNet36. In recent years, anchor-based algorithms have dominated the object detection domain. Given its speed and performance, the most recent YOLOv11 network serves as the benchmark model for the investigation conducted herein.

UAV image-based object detection methods

The swift advancement of UAV platforms has demonstrated the significant application potential of conducting UAV image-based target recognition in diverse domains, including environmental monitoring, industrial inspection, and disaster assistance. Target detection is more difficult to perform in UAV RGB photos than in general scenarios because of factors such as perspective, scale, and viewing condition variations, along with significant proportional disparities between objects in aerial images. Rui Jing employed the SiLU activation function in place of the leaky rectified linear unit (LeakyReLU) function and developed the DeformAtt-ELAN architecture. The proposed Sunflower-YOLO network was utilized for sunflower head detection in UAV-acquired remote sensing photos; nevertheless, it is ineffective at detecting targets in scenes with low-contrast backgrounds37. Yangang Li created an innovative neck module called the balanced spatial and semantic information fusion-based pyramid network (BSSI-FPN) neck module, which significantly enhanced the detection accuracy achieved for small objects in UAV photos. Nonetheless, the precision of this approach remains relatively inadequate in terms of identifying small objects concealed by fog and snow38. Jinfan Wei presented a GFS-YOLO11 model that integrates a feature refinement module (FRM) and an SPPFELAN module for recognizing various target crops in intricate field situations; nonetheless, it has limitations with respect to addressing quick dynamic changes exhibited by targets39. In addition, domain adaptation techniques based on semiautomated dataset construction40 and fusion methods using semi–self-constructed datasets and the improved YOLOv441 still have limitations in extreme fog and snow scenarios. The residual channel-attention (RCA) network42 has demonstrated strong feature extraction capabilities in remote sensing image classification, thus providing references for polar low-contrast image processing. The applications of transformer architectures in real-time detection tasks have also verified the advantages of global modelling for dynamic targets43.

Transformer-based convolution

In the polar sea ice environment, object detection is often disturbed by multiple factors, such as fog and snow occlusion, low-contrast backgrounds, and fast target displacement. Traditional convolutional neural networks (CNNs) rely on local receptive fields and fixed weights, making it difficult to model long-range dependencies in images. When dealing with low signal-to-noise ratios and dynamic background scenes, CNNs tend to overlook small targets that are severely occluded or at a far distance. Additionally, these methods suffer from high computational redundancy and weak global modelling capabilities, limiting their real-time detection performance in complex environments. To improve the detection accuracy and robustness, this paper introduces a transformer-based convolution (TransConv) module into the YOLO11n framework. This module integrates the global modelling ability of transformers with the efficiency of lightweight separable convolutions. On the one hand, the multihead self-attention mechanism helps capture the global contextual information between targets and backgrounds in the image, enhancing the ability to identify blurry edges and occluded targets in extreme scenes with dense fog and snowstorms. On the other hand, by replacing traditional convolution operations with separable convolutions, the module effectively reduces computational complexity and memory usage while maintaining perceptual capability, improving the model’s deployment friendliness and response speed. Mist global feature pyramid network

When a UAV-based cross-domain collaborative monitoring system executes activities such as returning and surveillance, it must ascend to a greater altitude to broaden its field of view for detection and encompass additional detection targets. Consequently, a substantial number of minor targets must be detected. Nonetheless, the diminutive sizes and remoteness of the targets, coupled with the intricate and dynamic background, frequently render the conventional YOLOv11n architecture ineffective in terms of accurately capturing the characteristics of these small targets in conditions with low contrast levels, large amounts of noise, and detail losses, leading to recurrent misdetections or omissions. Xianzhe Xu proposed DAMO-YOLO in his study entitled “DAMO-YOLO: A Report on Real-Time Object Detection Design,” which demonstrates outstanding accuracy and speed in small object detection. However, its performance falls short when it is applied to highly dynamic and complex environments such as UAV-based object detection. As shown in Fig. 3(a), the original neck module in the latest YOLO version v11 adopts a path aggregation network (PAN) + spatial pyramid pooling (SPP) structure. This structure enhances feature representation and contextual receptive fields through bottom−up and top−down information flow interactions, as well as multiscale pooling. Nonetheless, it still faces the following bottlenecks:

YOLOv11n neck and YOLO-TME neck.

(1) High overhead of multiple up/downsampling operations: The frequent scale transformations in the PAN increase the computational latency.

(2) Rigid channel allocation: Uniform channel assignment across features of different scales leads to inefficient use of computational resources.

(3) Redundant feature fusion: Cross-layer fusion is performed merely through addition or concatenation, resulting in insufficient information interaction.

To address the above limitations, this study proposes a mist-optimized global feature pyramid network (Mist Global Feature Pyramid Network, MistGFPN) through topological structure optimization on both the Efficient RepGFPN NECK network of DAMO-YOLO and the YOLO11n Neck network. This network incorporates innovative optimizations tailored for foggy images, thereby enhancing the network’s adaptability to low-contrast and high-noise images. As shown in Fig. 3(b), the arrows represent the “dynamic channel flow”, thereby indicating that the network adaptively adjusts the number of channels at each scale according to feature importance; the interaction paths between layers are the “queen-fusion feature interaction paths”, which indicate that features of different scales can interact directly across layers. Although RepGFPN adopts a fixed channel ratio, MistGFPN adaptively allocates more channels to small target features in the fog and snow scenarios by learning a channel allocation vector, thus resulting in a 30% improvement in channel utilization. Furthermore, MistGFPN adds a “fog and snow noise filtering branch”, which generates a noise mask through a 1 × 1 convolution combined with Sigmoid and filters the icy and snowy reflective areas before feature fusion.

MistGFPN adopts a dynamic channel allocation strategy across multiscale feature maps. For each feature map at scale , MistGFPN learns a channel allocation vector , on which the number of output channels at that scale is adaptively adjusted.

By jointly optimizing and convolutional weights during training, the network automatically allocates computations to the most important scales, preserving high-resolution details while maintaining low-resolution semantics.

MistGFPN introduces and optimizes the queen-fusion mechanism. The “queen-style global interaction” is analogous to the movement rules of the queen in chess—in chess, the queen can move in multiple directions along horizontal, vertical, and diagonal lines. Similarly, each scale feature map in MistGFPN (like the queen) can also establish cross-level interactions with all other scale feature maps, rather than only interacting with adjacent scales (e.g., only small scale → medium scale, medium scale → large scale). Efficient asymmetric detection head

To accommodate the swift motion of the detection target and the environmental fluctuations encountered by the UAV-based cross-domain collaborative monitoring system, a real-time target detection method is essential. In the initial algorithms of the YOLO series, the conventional linked head structure for managing classification and regression tasks results in considerable resource consumption and restricts the possible inference speed because of the use of shared feature branches. Despite the introduction of a decoupled head structure in YOLOv8 and the subsequent versions, which provides enhanced detection accuracy by segregating the classification and regression tasks, the resultant computational and parameter overhead levels adversely impact the inference speed of the model.

Figure 4 shows that in the coupled head structure, the input feature map undergoes sequential processing through a 1 × 1 convolution followed by a 3 × 3 convolution. The classification (Cls.) and regression (Reg.) tasks utilize the same branch, necessitating substantial numbers of parameters and computational resources and hence diminishing the real-time performance of the model. In the decoupled head structure, the input feature map undergoes a 1 × 1 convolution to reduce the number of feature channels by 256. The structure subsequently presents two concurrent branches for the classification and regression tasks. Each branch incorporates two 3 × 3 convolutions to streamline the model and enhance its convergence velocity. Furthermore, an intersection-over-union (IoU) branch is incorporated into the regression branch to signify whether each feature point corresponds to a detected item. Independently processing the classification and regression tasks enhances the detection performance of the model; nevertheless, this leads to increased computational demands and a higher parameter count, thereby diminishing the real-time performance of the model.

Decoupled head and coupled head.

Accordingly, this study proposes an EADH, which reduces the number of required model parameters and improves the real-time performance of the model while ensuring high detection accuracy through the introduction of multistage channel compression and depthwise separable convolution (DWConv) techniques. The EADH reduces the number of channels in the input feature map to 256 via a 1 × 1 convolution. The feature map is subsequently allocated to three parallel branches, each of which is dedicated to independently processing classification, regression, and IoU tasks. To augment the receptive field of the IoU branch and improve the task parameters, several convolutional layers are employed to further reduce the channel dimensionality, where multiple 3 × 3 DWConvs are utilized in place of the conventional decoupled head structure with 3 × 3 convolutions44.

The primary distinction between the depthwise separable convolution in the EADH and the standard convolution lies in the decomposition of the convolution operation into two phases, namely, depthwise convolution and pointwise convolution, which are employed to independently address classification and bounding-box regression tasks. This mitigates the loss of positive samples and task conflicts, thereby preventing the excessive expansion of both while decreasing the number of model parameters and enhancing the attained detection speed. Module collaboration mechanism

The three core modules of YOLO-TME collaborate through the logic of ‘global feature guidance–multiscale fusion enhancement–lightweight detection adaptation’. TransConv captures the global context of fog and snow scenes (such as the spatial relationship between landing markers and snowy backgrounds) via multihead self-attention, thereby encoding this global information into a “context weight vector”, which is fed into MistGFPN’s dynamic channel allocation module. When allocating channels to features at different scales, MistGFPN prioritizes allocating more channel resources to features relevant to landing markers (e.g., edges, shapes) according to this weight vector, thereby preventing small target features from being drowned out by background noise. The multiscale fused feature output by MistGFPN (which contains high-resolution small-object details and low-resolution semantic information) must adapt to the EADH’s lightweight design. Through structural reparameterization, MistGFPN merges multibranch features into a single-channel feature map, thereby reducing the input dimension of the EADH. Simultaneously, the depth-separable convolutions of the EADH optimize the kernel sizes for the “detail-semantic fusion features” of the MistGFPN, thereby balancing the detection accuracy and speed. Furthermore, TransConv outputs global features containing an “invalid feature mask” for fog/snow-obscured regions. The EADH uses this mask in both the classification and regression branches to skip computations in invalid areas, thereby reducing redundant operations and further enhancing real-time performance.

Experiments

This work employs a meticulously curated multiscenario dataset consisting of 8,500 photos of landing signs to assess the efficacy of the YOLO-TME model in polar regions encompassing several landing sign contexts. In this study, precision (P), recall (R), the F1 score (the harmonic mean of precision and recall), mAP@0.5 (mean average precision, with IoU = 0.5), mAP@0.5:0.95 (mean average precision, with IoU ranging from 0.5 to 0.95), and GFLOPS (giga floating point operations per second) are used as evaluation metrics45. Precision (which is also referred to as the “accuracy rate” in this context) is a measure of the model’s ability to correctly identify actual targets and is known as the precision rate. Recall, which is known as the recall rate, measures the proportion of positive samples that a classification model successfully identifies among all actual positive samples. The F1 score is a comprehensive metric for evaluating model performance and is primarily used to measure the balance between precision and recall in classification models. mAP (mean average precision) is the average detection accuracy of an object detection model across multiple categories. Specifically, mAP@0.5 refers to the average precision calculated when an intersection over union (IoU) threshold of 0.5 is used in the mAP computation. mAP@0.5:0.95 refers to the average precision calculated when IoU (intersection over union) values ranging from 0.5 to 0.95 (in increments of 0.05) are used in the mAP computation. GFLOPS is used to evaluate the computational complexity, operation speed, and real-time performance of a neural network model. FPS (frames per second) represents the number of image frames that can be processed per second and is typically used to evaluate the real-time performance of a model. The test conditions for FPS measurement are as follows: the input size is 640 × 640, the batch size is 1, the precision format is FP16, the GPU model is NVIDIA RTX 4090, and the framework is PyTorch 2.1.0. According to the analysis of the results in Table 2, TransConv and MistGFPN replace the backbone module and feature fusion network, respectively, in YOLO11n while the EADH is introduced. TransConv models the contextual relationships of fog and snow-occluded targets through global attention, MistGFPN strengthens shallow details via dynamic channel allocation, and the EADH reduces the computational load through asymmetric compression. The combined design of these three components achieves a Pareto optimal balance between accuracy and speed, thereby enhancing the performance of YOLO11n in UAV target detection under polar environments. When only the TransConv module is included (Model B), the precision increases by 2.3%, the recall increases by 2.4%, the F1 score increases by 2.4%, the mAP@0.5 increases by 3.2%, and the mAP@0.5:0.95 increases by 4.2%. However, the model’s GFLOPS increases by 0.2, the FPS decreases by 18, and the number of parameters increases from 2.6 M to 2.9 M. These results indicate that TransConv improves global information modelling capabilities, thereby enabling the model to better handle target detection in complex backgrounds with fog and low contrast, albeit with a slight increase in computational complexity and a decrease in real-time performance. When YOLO11n is modified using only the MistGFPN (Model C), the precision increases by 3.4%, the recall by 1.9%, the F1 score by 2.5%, the mAP@0.5 by 3.6%, and the mAP@0.5:0.95 by 1.0%. However, the GFLOPS increases by 1.9, the FPS decreases by 27.1, and the number of parameters increases to 3.7 M. These results suggest that MistGFPN can effectively enhance local image features and improve small target detection capabilities in distant complex images, but it significantly increases model complexity and compromises real-time performance. When YOLO11n replaces only its detection head with the EADH (Model D), compared with the original model (Model A) using a standard decoupled head with depthwise separable convolution (DWConv), the number of GFLOPS decreases significantly by 1.1, the FPS increases by 27.7, the precision increases by 1.3%, the mAP@0.5 improves notably by 3.6%, the mAP@0.5:0.95 increases by 5.0%, and the number of parameters increases slightly to 3.1 M. Nevertheless, the recall decreases by 1.5%, and the F1 score decreases by 0.2%; these results indicate that the EADH module has advantages in improving detection speed through its lightweight design and DWConv, but there is a certain trade-off in maintaining detection accuracy. When both TransConv and MistGFPN are introduced simultaneously (Model E), the precision and mAP further improve, with the mAP@0.5 reaching 0.924, the number of parameters increasing to 3.8 M, the number of GFLOPS increasing to 9.3, and the number of FPS increasing to 106.3. This finding demonstrates significant improvements in target detection and small target detection in low-contrast complex backgrounds (such as haze, wind, and snow), but Model E also results in substantial computational and parameter overhead. Finally, Model H (YOLO-TME), which combines YOLO11n with TransConv, MistGFPN, and an EADH, achieves significant increases of 4.2% in precision, 5.8% in recall, 5.1% in the F1 score, and 5.5% in mAP@0.5; additionally, the mAP@0.5:0.95 increases to 0.769. Moreover, the GFLOPS is only 7.1, the FPS reaches 108.7, and the number of parameters is controlled within 3.4 M, thus indicating superior performance in terms of both accuracy and efficiency compared with those of the other combined structures. These results indicate that YOLO-TME achieves substantial improvements in multiple key metrics while keeping the increases in computational load, parameters, and complexity within acceptable ranges. This finding verifies that through the collaborative optimization of each module, YOLO-TME can achieve efficient and accurate UAV landing target detection in complex polar environments with low contrast, fog, and snow occlusion, thereby balancing detection accuracy and real-time performance.

Conclusion

This study addresses the inadequate accuracy and timeliness of drones when detecting dynamic landing signs in real time in polar environments characterized by low-contrast backgrounds, fog, snow cover, and continuously changing targets. This work presents a YOLO-TME network that enhances the real-time target detection performance achieved under extreme conditions and provides increased detection accuracy on a custom-built dataset. Compared with the original approach (YOLOv11n), YOLO-TME yields a detection accuracy enhancement of 4.2% and an overall accuracy improvement of 5.5%. In addition, during general scenario validation (such as on the COCO, VisDrone, and UAVDT datasets), YOLO-TME also demonstrates certain advantages, with an FPS of 108.7, highlighting its robustness and real-time performance in complex scenarios. Furthermore, the model incorporates extensive lightweight design features.

This study focuses on algorithm research for detecting landing markers with real-time dynamic changes during UAV operations in polar environments. The 108.7 FPS of YOLO-TME is tested on a GPU (NVIDIA RTX 4090). If the model used in this study is deployed on embedded hardware with limited resources, the FPS of YOLO-TME is approximately 35 on the Jetson AGX Xavier (16 GB video memory), thus meeting the real-time requirement (≥ 30 FPS). In contrast, the FPS decreases to 18 on the Jetson Nano (4 GB video memory), thus falling below the real-time threshold. This finding indicates that the model still requires optimization for embedded devices with low computing power. Therefore, in the future, techniques such as pruning, quantization, and knowledge distillation can be integrated to optimize the model’s lightweight design to further improve inference speed and hardware adaptability. With these improvements, the YOLO-TME model achieves efficient and accurate target detection in a wider range of application scenarios, thereby providing a more reliable technical solution for autonomous UAV navigation and mission execution.

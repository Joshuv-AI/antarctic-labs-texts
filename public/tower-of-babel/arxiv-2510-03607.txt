Multiplication Operator Semigroups on Banach lattice valued continuous function spaces
Tobi David Olabiyi

Abstract
We introduce and characterize, on the Banach lattice valued continuous function space, multiplication operators generating strongly continuous multiplication operator semigroups. Our characterization is the generalization of known results for the scalar-valued continuous functions C 0 ​ ( Ω ) C_{0}(\Omega) vanishing at infinity, on a locally compact (Hausdorff) space Ω \Omega , to Banach lattice C 0 ​ ( Ω , E ) C_{0}(\Omega,E) of continuous Banach lattice E E -valued functions vanishing at infinity.



Key words:
multiplication operators, multiplication semigroups, centre of Banach lattices, Banach lattice of continuous functions,
C_{0}
-semigroups, uniformly continuous operator semigroups.

Mathematics Subject Classification (2020):
47D06; 46E05, 46B42, 47B60, 46H25.

Introduction

The study of multiplication operators and their operator semigroups have attracted a lot of attention, see for example
[
9
,
14
,
8
,
10
,
11
]
, majorly because multiplication operators arise naturally. Indeed, (i) multiplication operators can be considered, in a classical sense, as the infinite-dimensional generalization of diagonal matrices; and (ii)
there are several instances where certain kind of operators, e.g., normal operators on a separable infinite-dimensional Hilbert space or differential operators on a scalar-valued function space via Fourier transforms, can be represented as multiplication operators.

These multiplication operators are also very much connected with certain kinds of multipliers, e.g., some operator multipliers, see
[
7
]
, on Banach space valued function spaces and the Hadamard multipliers, see
[
6
]
, on scalar-valued analytic function spaces, e.t.c. In particular, in
[
7
,
2
]
, the authors combine the study of extrapolation spaces with operator-valued multiplication operators. More so, these multiplication operators have a relation with the multiplier algebras, see
[
13
]
, in the setting of Banach algebras.

In
[
3
, Chapter I, section 3, p.19-29]
and
[
3
, Chapter II, section 2, p.50]
, certain multiplication operators and their operator semigroups on concrete spaces
C_{0}(\Omega)
and
L^{p}(\Omega,\mu),\ 1\leq p<\infty
, where either
\Omega
is a locally compact (Hausdorff) space or
(\Omega,\mu)
is a (
positive
)
\sigma
-finite measure space respectively, are considered. We are in particular interested in the generalization of the results for the scalar-valued continuous functions
C_{0}(\Omega)
vanishing at infinity to Banach lattice
C_{0}(\Omega,E)
of continuous Banach lattice
E
-valued functions vanishing at infinity. Generally, for a Banach lattice
E
, the lattice algebra

\boldsymbol{\mathcal{Z}}(E):=\left\{T\in\mathscr{L}(E):\exists\ \lambda>0\ \text{such that}\ \textbf{$|Tz|$}\leq\lambda\textbf{$|z|$}\ \forall\ z\in E\right\}

of central operators, the so-called centre of
E
equipped with the operator norm (equivalently,
||T||_{\mathscr{L}(E)}=\inf\left\{\lambda>0:\textbf{$|Tz|$}\leq\lambda\textbf{$|z|$}\ \forall\ z\in E\right\}
), is a commutative 1-Banach lattice algebra. We refer to
[
15
]
for concepts of Banach lattice algebras, including examples. Moreover,
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{Z}}(E)
is an AM-space with unit. Elements of this lattice algebra are called multiplication operators, for obvious reasons. Indeed, for concretes spaces
C_{0}(\Omega)
and
L^{p}(\Omega,\mu),\ 1\leq p\leq\infty
, their centres can be identified uniquely with multiplications by functions in
C_{b}(\Omega)
and
L^{\infty}(\Omega,\mu)
respectively. And probably what is important to note here is that for a Banach lattice
E
, its centre
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{Z}}(E)
, with the uniform operator topology, is isometrically isomorphic to
C(Q)
as commutative Banach lattice algebras for some compact (Hausdorff) space
Q
(see also
[
1
, Section C-I.9, p.246]
).
We let
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{Z}}(E)_{\textbf{s}}
denotes
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{Z}}(E)
equipped with the strong operator topology.

The major results of this paper are summarized in the following theorem.

Theorem 1
.

Let
m_{t}\in C_{b}(\Omega,\boldsymbol{\mathcal{Z}}(E)_{\textbf{s}})
for each
t\geq 0
such that the induced multiplication semigroup
\mathcal{T}(t)_{t\geq 0}
given by

\mathcal{T}(t):C_{0}(\Omega,E)\longrightarrow C_{0}(\Omega,E);\ s(\cdot)\mapsto\mathcal{T}(t)s(\cdot):=m_{t}(\cdot)s(\cdot)

is a
C_{0}
-semigroup on
C_{0}(\Omega,E)
. And denote its (infinitesimal) generator on
C_{0}(\Omega,E)
by
(\mathcal{M},D(\mathcal{M}))
. Furthermore, suppose that, for each
x\in\Omega
, the induced pointwise multiplication semigroup
T_{x}(t)_{t\geq 0}
given by

T_{x}(t):E\longrightarrow E;\ z\mapsto T_{x}(t)z:=m_{t}(x)z

is uniformly continuous on
E
.
Then we have the following.

(i)

There exists a (generally unbounded) continuous function
\phi:\Omega\longrightarrow\boldsymbol{\mathcal{Z}}(E)_{\textbf{s}}
, where
(T_{x}(t))_{t\geq 0}=(e^{t\phi(x)})_{t\geq 0}
for each
x\in\Omega
with

\sup_{x\in\Omega}||e^{t_{0}\phi(x)}||_{\mathscr{L}(E)}<\infty\ \text{ for some }\ t_{0}\in(0,1]

such that
m_{t}(\cdot)=e^{t\phi(\cdot)}
for all
t\geq 0
. That is
\mathcal{T}(t)_{t\geq 0}=(\mathcal{T}_{\phi}(t))_{t\geq 0}
, where
\mathcal{T}_{\phi}(t)s(\cdot):=e^{t\phi(\cdot)}s(\cdot)
for all
s\in C_{0}(\Omega,E)
and
t\geq 0
.

(ii)

(\mathcal{M},D(\mathcal{M}))=(\mathcal{M}_{\phi},D(\mathcal{M}_{\phi}))
, where
(\mathcal{M}_{\phi},D(\mathcal{M}_{\phi}))
denotes the (generally unbounded) multiplication operator on
C_{0}(\Omega,E)
induced by the continuous function
\phi
and defined on its ”maximal domain”
D(\mathcal{M}_{\phi})
.

(iii)

The multiplication
C_{0}
-semigroup
(\mathcal{T}_{\phi}(t))_{t\geq 0}
is uniformly continuous if and only if
\phi\in C_{b}(\Omega,\boldsymbol{\mathcal{Z}}(E)_{\textbf{s}})
.

The following is the organization of this paper. We start in section

induced by a continuous function

generating a strongly continuous multiplication semigroup

on
C_{0}(\Omega,E)
; and that the semigroup is uniformly continuous if and only if
\phi
and hence
\mathcal{M}_{\phi}
is bounded (see Propositions
5

1
). We concluded this paper in section

2
Multiplication Operators on
C_{0}(\Omega,E)
.

Given a locally compact (Hausdorff) space
\Omega
and a Banach lattice
E
, we know that the centre
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{Z}}(C_{0}(\Omega,E))
of the Banach lattice
C_{0}(\Omega,E)
is isometrically isomorphic to the space
C_{b}(\Omega,\boldsymbol{\mathcal{Z}}(E)_{\textbf{s}})
as commutative 1-Banach lattice algebras (see
[
4
, Theorem 6.2, p.135]
). In his thesis, see
[
12
, Chapter 3, Proposition 3.6.6.1, p.113-116]
, the author proved that
C_{b}(\Omega,\boldsymbol{\mathcal{Z}}(E)_{\textbf{s}})\cong\Gamma_{b}(\Omega,E_{\textbf{z}})
where
\Gamma_{b}(\Omega,E_{\textbf{z}})
denotes the Banach lattice algebra of bounded continuous sections associated with the trivial bundle
E_{\textbf{z}}:=\Omega\times\boldsymbol{\mathcal{Z}}(E)_{\textbf{s}}
, by which we, in particualr, realize
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{Z}}(C_{0}(\Omega,E))\cong C_{b}(\Omega,\boldsymbol{\mathcal{Z}}(E)_{\textbf{s}})\cong\Gamma_{b}(\Omega,E_{\textbf{z}})
as isomorphism of AM m-lattice modules over the centre
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{Z}}(C_{0}(\Omega))\cong C_{b}(\Omega)
.

Just a quick note: AM m-lattice modules over Banach lattice algebra
C_{0}(\Omega)
are also what we referred to as locally convex
C_{0}(\Omega)
-m-lattice modules or equivalently upper semicontinuous
C_{0}(\Omega)
-functions m-lattice modules (see
[
12
, Chapter 2, Corollary 2.4.1.10, p.32]
). The author proved that, see
[
12
, Chapter 3, Proposition 3.5.0.9, p.85-88]
, these are precisely those Banach lattice modules over
C_{0}(\Omega)
which can uniquely be represented as Banach lattices of continuous sections of some topological bundles of Banach lattices over
\Omega
. See also
[
5
, Corollary 7.28, p.78-79]
.

Throughout we denote by
\mathcal{I}\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{Z}}(C_{0}(\Omega,E))
the identity operator on
C_{0}(\Omega,E)
,
I_{E}\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{Z}}(E)_{\textbf{s}}
the identity operator on
E
and
e\in C_{b}(\Omega,\boldsymbol{\mathcal{Z}}(E)_{\textbf{s}})
the unit element of
C_{b}(\Omega,\boldsymbol{\mathcal{Z}}(E)_{\textbf{s}})
.

With a continuous function
\phi:\Omega\longrightarrow\boldsymbol{\mathcal{Z}}(E)_{\textbf{s}}
we associate a linear operator
\mathcal{M}_{\phi}
on
C_{0}(\Omega,E)
defined on its ”maximal domain”
D(\mathcal{M}_{\phi})
in
C_{0}(\Omega,E)
. This definition is motivated by
[
3
, Definition 3.1, p.20]
and
[
8
, Definition 1.1, p.575]
.

Definition 1
.

(Multiplication and pointwise multiplication operators)
The multiplication operator
\mathcal{M}_{\phi}
induced on
C_{0}(\Omega,E)
by a continuous function
\phi:\Omega\longrightarrow\boldsymbol{\mathcal{Z}}(E)_{\textbf{s}}
is defined by

\mathcal{M}_{\phi}s=\phi\circ s,\text{ i.e., }\mathcal{M}_{\phi}s(x):=\phi(x)s(x)\text{ for all }x\in\Omega\text{ and domain}

D(\mathcal{M}_{\phi}):=\left\{s\in C_{0}(\Omega,E):\ \phi(\cdot)s(\cdot)\in C_{0}(\Omega,E)\right\}.

In this context, we call the operators
\phi(x)
with
x\in\Omega
the pointwise multiplication operators on
E
.

We show in the following proposition certain relationships existing between the multiplication operator (
\mathcal{M}_{\phi},D(\mathcal{M}_{\phi})
) and the continuous function
\phi
. This can be seen as a generalization of
[
3
, Chapter 1, Proposition 3.2, p.20-21]
. See also
[
7
, Proposition 2.3 & Theorem 2.6, p.70]
.

Proposition 2
.

Let
\mathcal{M}_{\phi}:D(\mathcal{M}_{\phi})\subseteq C_{0}(\Omega,E)\longrightarrow C_{0}(\Omega,E);s\mapsto\phi\circ s
be the multiplication operator induced by a continuous function
\phi:\Omega\longrightarrow\boldsymbol{\mathcal{Z}}(E)_{\textbf{s}}
. Then, we have the following.

(i)

The operator
(\mathcal{M}_{\phi},D(\mathcal{M}_{\phi}))
is closed and densely defined.

(ii)

The operator
\mathcal{M}_{\phi}
is bounded (with
D(\mathcal{M}_{\phi})=C_{0}(\Omega,E)
) if and only if
\phi
is bounded, i.e.,
\phi\in C_{b}(\Omega,\boldsymbol{\mathcal{Z}}(E)_{\textbf{s}})
. In this case,
\mathcal{M}_{\phi}\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{Z}}(C_{0}(\Omega,E))
and one has

||\mathcal{M}_{\phi}||=||\phi||=\sup_{x\in\Omega}||\phi(x)||_{\mathscr{L}(E)}.

(iii)

The operator
\mathcal{M}_{\phi}
has a bounded inverse if and only if
\phi
has a bounded inverse
\phi^{-1}\in C_{b}(\Omega,\boldsymbol{\mathcal{Z}}(E)_{\textbf{s}})
, i.e.,
\phi(x)
is invertible in
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{Z}}(E)_{\textbf{s}}
for all
x\in\Omega
, and the function
x\overset{\phi^{-1}}{\longmapsto}\phi(x)^{-1}
is in
C_{b}(\Omega,\boldsymbol{\mathcal{Z}}(E)_{\textbf{s}})
.
In this case, one has

\mathcal{M}_{\phi}^{-1}=\mathcal{M}_{\phi^{-1}}.

(iv)

For the spectrum of
\mathcal{M}_{\phi}
, we have that

\sigma(\mathcal{M}_{\phi})=\Delta_{\phi}:=\left\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:\lambda\in\bigcup_{x\in\Omega}\sigma(\phi(x))\ \text{ or }\ \sup_{x\in\Omega}||R(\lambda,\phi(x))||_{\mathscr{L}(E)}=\infty\right\}.

That is, the resolvent set of
\mathcal{M}_{\phi}
is given by

\rho(\mathcal{M}_{\phi})=\Delta_{u}:=\left\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:\lambda\in\bigcap_{x\in\Omega}\rho(\phi(x))\ \text{ and }\ \sup_{x\in\Omega}||R(\lambda,\phi(x))||_{\mathscr{L}(E)}<\infty\right\}.

Proof.

(i)

It is clear that, the domain
D(\mathcal{M}_{\phi})
always contain the space

C_{c}(\Omega,E):=\left\{s\in C(\Omega,E):\text{supp }s\text{ is compact}\right\}

of all continuous functions
s:\Omega\longrightarrow E
having compact support

\text{supp }s:=\overline{\left\{x\in\Omega:s(x)\neq 0\in E\right\}}.

So, to prove that the operator
(\mathcal{M}_{\phi},D(\mathcal{M}_{\phi}))
is densely defined, it suffices to show
C_{c}(\Omega,E)
is norm dense in
C_{0}(\Omega,E).
Indeed, if we take approximate identity
(e_{j})_{j}
of
C_{0}(\Omega)
, and without loss of generality, we may assume that
e_{j}
has compact support for each
j
, so that for every
s\in C_{0}(\Omega,E)
, we have that
e_{j}\cdot s\in C_{c}(\Omega,E)
for each
i
, and
\lim_{j}||s-e_{j}\cdot s||=0
.

Next, we show that
(\mathcal{M}_{\phi},D(\mathcal{M}_{\phi}))
is a closed operator. Let
(s_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq D(\mathcal{M}_{\phi})
be a sequence converging to
s\in C_{0}(\Omega,E)
such that
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\phi\circ s_{n}=:s_{0}\in C_{0}(\Omega,E)
exists. Then, it must be that, for each
x\in\Omega
,
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}||s_{n}(x)-s(x)||_{E}=0
and
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}||\phi(x)s_{n}(x)-s_{0}(x)||_{E}=0
, from which it follows that
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}||\phi(x)s_{n}(x)-\phi(x)s(x)||_{E}=0
. Thus, for each
x\in\Omega
,
s_{0}(x)=\phi(x)s(x)
, so that
s\in D(\mathcal{M}_{\phi})
and
s_{0}=\phi\circ s
.

(ii)

If
\phi\in C_{b}(\Omega,\boldsymbol{\mathcal{Z}}(E)_{\textbf{s}})
, we have that

||\mathcal{M}_{\phi}s||=\sup_{x\in\Omega}||\phi(x)s(x)||_{E}\leq||\phi||||s||

for every
s\in C_{0}(\Omega,E)
; hence
\mathcal{M}_{\phi}
is bounded with
||\mathcal{M}_{\phi}||\leq||\phi||
. And on the other hand, assume
\mathcal{M}_{\phi}
is bounded. Now, for each
x\in\Omega
, let
f_{x}
be a continuous function in
C_{0}(\Omega)
with compact support satisfying
||f_{x}||=1=f_{x}(x).
And for (fixed)
z_{0}\in E
with
||z_{o}||=1
, consider, for each
x\in\Omega
, the continuous mapping
f_{x}\otimes z_{0}:\Omega\longrightarrow E;y\mapsto(f_{x}\otimes z_{0})(y):=f_{x}(y)z_{0}
. Then, we have that
f_{x}\otimes z_{0}\in C_{c}(\Omega,E)
with
||f_{x}\otimes z_{0}||=1
; and moreover, for each
x\in\Omega
,

||\phi(x)z_{0}||=||\mathcal{M}_{\phi}(f_{x}\otimes z_{0})(x)||\leq||\mathcal{M}_{\phi}(f_{x}\otimes z_{0})||\leq||\mathcal{M}_{\phi}||

implies that
||\phi(x)z||\leq||\mathcal{M}_{\phi}||
for every
z\in E
with
||z||=1
. And as such, we have that

||\phi(x)||\leq||\mathcal{M}_{\phi}||
for every
x\in\Omega
; hence,
\phi\in C_{b}(\Omega,\boldsymbol{\mathcal{Z}}(E)_{\textbf{s}})
with

||\phi||=\sup_{x\in\Omega}||\phi(x)||_{\mathscr{L}(E)}\leq||\mathcal{M}_{\phi}||.

The fact that
\mathcal{M}_{\phi}
is a multiplication operator on
C_{0}(\Omega,E)
follows, since the pointwise
(\phi(x)_{x\in\Omega})
are multiplication operators on
E
which are continuously bounded on
\Omega
. Indeed, for each
s\in C_{0}(\Omega,E)
,

\displaystyle|\mathcal{M}_{\phi}s|(x)=

\displaystyle|\phi(x)s(x)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle||\phi(x)||_{\mathscr{L}(E)}|s(x)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle||\phi||\cdot|s|(x)

for all
x\in\Omega
, implies that
|\mathcal{M}_{\phi}s|\leq||\phi||\cdot|s|
. Thus
\mathcal{M}_{\phi}\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{Z}}(C_{0}(\Omega,E))
.

(iii)

Suppose
\phi
has bounded inverse
\phi^{-1}\in C_{b}(\Omega,\boldsymbol{\mathcal{Z}}(E)_{\textbf{s}})
. This implies that
\phi^{-1}\circ\phi=\phi\circ\phi^{-1}=e\in C_{b}(\Omega,\boldsymbol{\mathcal{Z}}(E)_{\textbf{s}})
. Then, the induced (bounded) multiplication operator

\mathcal{M}_{\phi^{-1}}:C_{0}(\Omega,E)\longrightarrow C_{0}(\Omega,E);s\mapsto\phi^{-1}\circ s

is the inverse of
\mathcal{M}_{\phi}
, since
\mathcal{M}_{\phi^{-1}}\mathcal{M}_{\phi}s=s
for all
s\in D(\mathcal{M}_{\phi})
and
\mathcal{M}_{\phi}\mathcal{M}_{\phi^{-1}}=\mathcal{M}_{e}=\mathcal{I}\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{Z}}(C_{0}(\Omega,E))
.

Now, suppose
\mathcal{M}_{\phi}
has bounded inverse, say
\mathcal{T}
, i.e.,
\mathcal{T}\mathcal{M}_{\phi}s=s
for all
s\in D(\mathcal{M}_{\phi})
and
\mathcal{M}_{\phi}\mathcal{T}=\mathcal{M}_{e}=\mathcal{I}
. Then, we obtain

||s||\leq||\mathcal{T}||\cdot||\mathcal{M}_{\phi}s||\quad\text{ for all }s\in D(\mathcal{M}_{\phi}),

whence, the estimate

\delta:=\frac{1}{||\mathcal{T}||}\leq\sup_{x\in\Omega}||\phi(x)s(x)||_{E}\quad\text{ for all }s\in D(\mathcal{M}_{\phi}),\ ||s||=1.

And suppose on the contrary, without loss of generality, we assume either
\inf_{x\in\Omega}\inf_{||z||=1}||\phi(x)z||<\frac{\delta}{2}
or
\inf_{x\in\Omega}\frac{1}{||\phi(x)^{-1}||}<\frac{\delta}{2}
; then we can find an open set
\mathcal{O}\subseteq\Omega
such that either
\inf_{||z||=1}||\phi(x)z||<\frac{\delta}{2}
or
\frac{2}{\delta}<\sup_{||z||=1}||\phi(x)^{-1}z||
for all
x\in\mathcal{O}
respectively. Let
f_{0}
be a continuous function in
C_{0}(\Omega)
such that
||f_{0}||=1
and
f_{0}(x)=0
for all
x\in\Omega\backslash\mathcal{O}
, and consider, for any
z\in E
with
||z||=1
, the continuous mappings
f_{0}\otimes z:\Omega\longrightarrow E;x\mapsto(f_{0}\otimes z)(x):=f_{0}(x)z
; so that
f_{0}\otimes z\in D(\mathcal{M}_{\phi})
and
||f_{0}\otimes z||=1
. Then, the assumptions imply either
\sup_{x\in\Omega}||\phi(x)(f_{0}\otimes z)(x)||\leq\frac{\delta}{2}
or
\frac{2}{\delta}>\sup_{x\in\Omega}||\phi(x)^{-1}(f_{0}\otimes z)(x)||=\sup_{x\in\Omega}||\mathcal{T}(f_{0}\otimes z)(x)||
for some
z\in E
respectively, which is contradicting the above estimate.

Hence, we can conclude that each
\phi(x)
is invertible for all
x\in\Omega
and
\sup_{x\in\Omega}||\phi(x)^{-1}||=||\mathcal{T}||
, which would implies
\phi
has bounded inverse
\phi^{-1}\in C_{b}(\Omega,\boldsymbol{\mathcal{Z}}(E)_{\textbf{s}})
; so that
\mathcal{M}_{\phi^{-1}}
is the bounded multiplication operator on
C_{0}(\Omega,E)
which coincides with the bounded inverse
\mathcal{T}
.

Equivalently,
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{Z}}(C_{0}(\Omega,E))
is an inverse-closed subalgebra of
\mathscr{L}(C_{0}(\Omega,E))
and
\mathcal{T}(\phi\circ s)=s=\phi\circ\mathcal{T}s
for all
s\in D(\mathcal{M}_{\phi})
; it must be that the bounded inverse
\mathcal{T}
is a bounded multiplication operator on
C_{0}(\Omega,E)
, i.e., there exists
\psi\in C_{b}(\Omega,\boldsymbol{\mathcal{Z}}(E)_{\textbf{s}})
such that
\mathcal{M}_{\psi}=\mathcal{T}
. It, thus, follows that
\phi\circ\psi=e=\psi\circ\phi
, so that
\phi
has bounded inverse
\phi^{-1}:=\psi
.

(iv)

By definition, one has
\lambda\in\sigma(\mathcal{M}_{\phi})
if and only if
\lambda-\mathcal{M}_{\phi}=\mathcal{M}_{\lambda-\phi}
does not have bounded inverse. Thus, by (iii) above, it suffices to show that
\lambda-\phi
has bounded inverse if and only if
\lambda\in\Delta_{u}
. The forward implication is clear, that is if
\lambda-\phi
has inverse
x\overset{{(\lambda-\phi)}^{-1}}{\longmapsto}{(\lambda-\phi(x))}^{-1}
in
C_{b}(\Omega,\boldsymbol{\mathcal{Z}}(E)_{\textbf{s}})
, then
\lambda\in\Delta_{u}
. For the reverse, suppose
\lambda\in\Delta_{u}
and consider the mapping
R:\Omega\longrightarrow\boldsymbol{\mathcal{Z}}(E)_{\textbf{s}};x\mapsto R(x):=R(\lambda,\phi(x))=(\lambda-\phi(x))^{-1}
. Then, we see that
R
is the pointwise inverse of
\lambda-\phi
and is bounded. To prove that
R
is continuous, let
x_{\gamma}
be a net in
\Omega
converging to
x
in
\Omega
. For all
\varepsilon>0
and each
z\in E
, using the continuity of
\lambda-\phi
, we eventually have
||z-(\lambda-\phi(x_{\gamma}))R(x)z||<\varepsilon
. As such, for each
z\in E
, we obtain

\displaystyle||R(x_{\gamma})z-R(x)z||=

\displaystyle||R(x_{\gamma})[z-(\lambda-\phi(x_{\gamma}))R(x)z]||

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle||R(x_{\gamma})||_{\mathscr{L}(E)}\varepsilon

eventually, which can be made small as much as desired, since
R
is bounded, i.e.,
||R||:=\sup_{x\in\Omega}||R(x)||_{\mathscr{L}(E)}<\infty
. Thus,
R\in C_{b}(\Omega,\boldsymbol{\mathcal{Z}}(E)_{\textbf{s}})
which is the desired bounded inverse of
\lambda-\phi
.

∎

3
Multiplication semigroup on
C_{0}(\Omega,E)
generated by
(\mathcal{M}_{\phi},D(\mathcal{M}_{\phi}))

Given a continuous function
\phi:\Omega\longrightarrow\boldsymbol{\mathcal{Z}}(E)_{\textbf{s}}
, we associate, for each
t\geq 0
, the exponential function

e^{t\phi}:\Omega\longrightarrow\boldsymbol{\mathcal{Z}}(E)_{\textbf{s}};\ x\mapsto e^{t\phi(x)}

which can immediately be seen to be continuous.
Therefore, we obtain certain multiplication semigroup
(\mathcal{T}_{\phi}(t))_{t\geq 0}
on
C_{0}(\Omega,E)
defined, for each
t\geq 0
, by

s(\cdot)\mapsto\mathcal{T}_{\phi}(t)s(\cdot):=e^{t\phi(\cdot)}s(\cdot)

which each
\mathcal{T}_{\phi}(t)
is bounded if and only if
e^{t\phi}\in C_{b}(\Omega,\boldsymbol{\mathcal{Z}}(E)_{\textbf{s}})
, using Proposition
2
(ii). Indeed, we see that
\mathcal{T}_{\phi}(0)=\mathcal{I}
, and
\mathcal{T}_{\phi}(t_{1}+t_{2})=\mathcal{T}_{\phi}(t_{1})\mathcal{T}_{\phi}(t_{2})
for all
t_{1},t_{2}\geq 0
. And consequently,
\mathcal{T}_{\phi}(t)\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{Z}}(C_{0}(\Omega,E))
and

||\mathcal{T}_{\phi}(t)||=||e^{t\phi}||=\sup_{x\in\Omega}||e^{t\phi(x)}||_{\mathscr{L}(E)}\ \text{ for all }t\geq 0.

Remark 3
.

We show that the condition
\sup_{x\in\Omega}||e^{t_{0}\phi(x)}||_{\mathscr{L}(E)}<\infty
for some
t_{0}\in(0,1]
is only what is required for each of the multiplication operator
\mathcal{T}_{\phi}(t)
to be bounded on
C_{0}(\Omega,E)
, and not necessarily that
\sup_{x\in\Omega}||\phi(x)||_{\mathscr{L}(E)}<\infty
. To this end, let
q:\Omega\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}
be an unbounded continuous function which is bounded from above, i.e.,
\sup_{x\in\Omega}|q(x)|=\infty
, but
\sup_{x\in\Omega}Req(x)<\infty
. Then the mapping
qI_{E}:\Omega\longrightarrow\boldsymbol{\mathcal{Z}}(E)_{\textbf{s}};x\mapsto q(x)I_{E}
is continuous but unbounded since
\sup_{x\in\Omega}||q(x)I_{E}||_{\mathscr{L}(E)}=\sup_{x\in\Omega}|q(x)|=\infty
. However, the fact that the exponential functions

e^{tqI_{E}}:\Omega\longrightarrow\boldsymbol{\mathcal{Z}}(E)_{\textbf{s}};\ x\mapsto e^{tq(x)I_{E}}

are continuous and bounded, since for each
t\in(0,t_{0}]
,

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\Omega}||e^{tq(x)I_{E}}||_{\mathscr{L}(E)}=

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\Omega}|e^{tq(x)}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\Omega}e^{tReq(x)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{t\sup_{x\in\Omega}Req(x)}<\infty,

implies that the multiplication operators
\mathcal{T}_{qI_{E}}(t)
are bounded on
C_{0}(\Omega,E)
, and
||\mathcal{T}_{qI_{E}}(t)||=e^{t\sup_{x\in\Omega}Req(x)}
for each
t\in(0,t_{0}]
. Hence, in general, given that
||\mathcal{T}_{\phi}(t_{0})||=\sup_{x\in\Omega}||e^{t_{0}\phi(x)}||_{\mathscr{L}(E)}<\infty
, then every other
\mathcal{T}_{\phi}(t)
would be bounded since every
t>t_{0}
can be written uniquely as
t=nt_{0}+r
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
with
r\in(0,t_{0}]
such that
\mathcal{T}_{\phi}(t)=\mathcal{T}_{\phi}(t_{0})^{n}\mathcal{T}_{\phi}(r)
.

We now introduce multiplication semigroup induced by (generally) unbounded multiplication operator
(\mathcal{M}_{\phi},D(\mathcal{M}_{\phi}))
. This definition is motivated by
[
3
, Definition 3.3, p.22]
.

Definition 4
.

Let
\phi:\Omega\longrightarrow\boldsymbol{\mathcal{Z}}(E)_{\textbf{s}}
be a continuous function such that

\sup_{x\in\Omega}||e^{t_{0}\phi(x)}||_{\mathscr{L}(E)}<\infty\ \text{ for some }\ t_{0}\in(0,1].

Then, we call the semigroup
(\mathcal{T}_{\phi}(t))_{t\geq 0}
comprised of (bounded) multiplication operators

\mathcal{T}_{\phi}(t):C_{0}(\Omega,E)\longrightarrow C_{0}(\Omega,E);\ s(\cdot)\mapsto\mathcal{T}_{\phi}(t)s(\cdot)=e^{t\phi(\cdot)}s(\cdot)

for all
t\geq 0
, the multiplication semigroup generated by the multiplication operator
(\mathcal{M}_{\phi},D(\mathcal{M}_{\phi}))
on
C_{0}(\Omega,E)
.

The following Proposition justifies the previous definition. These can be considered as generalizations of
[
3
, Proposition 3.4, p.23]
and
[
3
, Proposition 3.5, p.23-24]
. See also
[
3
, Lemma 2.8, p.50]
.

Proposition 5
.

Let
\phi:\Omega\longrightarrow\boldsymbol{\mathcal{Z}}(E)_{\textbf{s}}
be a continuous function such that
\sup_{x\in\Omega}||e^{t_{0}\phi(x)}||_{\mathscr{L}(E)}<\infty
for some
t_{0}\in(0,1]
. Furthermore, let
(\mathcal{T}_{\phi}(t))_{t\geq 0}
be the multiplication semigroup generated by
(\mathcal{M}_{\phi},D(\mathcal{M}_{\phi}))
on
C_{0}(\Omega,E)
as in Definition
4
. Then we have the following.

(i)

(\mathcal{T}_{\phi}(t))_{t\geq 0}
is a
C_{0}
-semigroup on
C_{0}(\Omega,E)
, i.e., the mapping

\mathbb{R}_{+}\longrightarrow C_{0}(\Omega,E);\ t\mapsto\mathcal{T}_{\phi}(t)s(\cdot)

is continuous for each
s\in C_{0}(\Omega,E)
; and its (infinitesimal) generator is the multiplication operator
(\mathcal{M}_{\phi},D(\mathcal{M}_{\phi}))
on
C_{0}(\Omega,E)
.

(ii)

The multiplication
C_{0}
-semigroup
(\mathcal{T}_{\phi}(t))_{t\geq 0}
is uniformly continuous if and only if
\phi\in C_{b}(\Omega,\boldsymbol{\mathcal{Z}}(E)_{\textbf{s}})
.

Proof.

(i)

Let
s\in C_{0}(\Omega,E)
with
||s||\leq 1
. And for
\varepsilon>0
, we can choose a compact subset
K\subseteq\Omega
, such that
||s(x)||\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{||e^{\phi}||+1}
for all
x\in\Omega\backslash K
. Moreover, we note that, the fact that the pointwise multiplication operators
(\phi(x)_{x\in\Omega})
induced uniformly continuous
C_{0}
-semigroup
(e^{t\phi(x)})_{t\geq 0}
on
E
generated by
\phi(x)\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{Z}}(E)_{\textbf{s}}
for each
x\in\Omega
, i.e., the mapping

\mathbb{R}_{+}\longrightarrow\boldsymbol{\mathcal{Z}}(E)_{\textbf{s}};\ t\mapsto e^{t\phi(x)}

is uniformly continuous for each
x\in\Omega
, implies that we can find
t_{1}\in(0,1]
such that
||e^{t\phi(x)}-I_{E}||_{\mathscr{L}(E)}\leq\varepsilon
for all
x\in K
and
0\leq t\leq t_{1}
. And therefore, we obtain that

\displaystyle||\mathcal{T}_{\phi}(t)s-s||

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\Omega}||e^{t\phi(x)}s(x)-s(x)||

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\Omega}||(e^{t\phi(x)}-I_{E})s(x)||

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in K}||(e^{t\phi(x)}-I_{E})||_{\mathscr{L}(E)}||s(x)||+\sup_{x\in\Omega\backslash K}||(e^{t\phi(x)}-I_{E})||_{\mathscr{L}(E)}||s(x)||

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\varepsilon\sup_{x\in K}||s(x)||+\sup_{x\in\Omega\backslash K}||(e^{t\phi(x)}-I_{E})||_{\mathscr{L}(E)}\frac{\varepsilon}{||e^{\phi}||+1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\varepsilon+\varepsilon

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\varepsilon

for all
0\leq t\leq t_{1}
, showing that
(\mathcal{T}_{\phi}(t))_{t\geq 0}
is strongly continuous, and hence a
C_{0}
-semigroup on
C_{0}(\Omega,E)
.

Next, we show that
(\mathcal{M}_{\phi},D(\mathcal{M}_{\phi}))
is indeed the (infinitesimal) generator of the multiplication
C_{0}
-semigroup
(\mathcal{T}_{\phi}(t))_{t\geq 0}
on
C_{0}(\Omega,E)
. To this ends, suppose that
(\mathcal{M},D(\mathcal{M}))
is the generator of the
C_{0}
-semigroup
(\mathcal{T}_{\phi}(t))_{t\geq 0}
on
C_{0}(\Omega,E)
.

Let
s\in D(\mathcal{M}_{\phi})
;
x\overset{e^{t\phi}s}{\longmapsto}e^{t\phi(x)}s(x)
is in
C_{0}(\Omega,E)
, and
\lim_{t\downarrow 0}\frac{e^{t\phi(x)}s(x)-s(x)}{t}=\phi(x)s(x)=(\mathcal{M}_{\phi}s)(x)
implies that
s\in D(\mathcal{M})
; so that
D(\mathcal{M}_{\phi})\subseteq D(\mathcal{M})
and
\mathcal{M}s=\phi\circ s=\mathcal{M}_{\phi}s
for all
s\in D(\mathcal{M}_{\phi})
.

And on the other hand, for
s\in C_{0}(\Omega,E)
the fact that

\lim_{t\downarrow 0}\frac{e^{t\phi}s-s}{t}\text{ exists}\iff x\mapsto\lim_{t\downarrow 0}\frac{e^{t\phi(x)}s(x)-s(x)}{t}=\phi(x)s(x)\text{ exists }

implies that
D(\mathcal{M})\subseteq D(\mathcal{M}_{\phi})
and
\mathcal{M}_{\phi}s=\mathcal{M}s
for all
s\in D(\mathcal{M})
. Hence,
(\mathcal{M}_{\phi},D(\mathcal{M}_{\phi}))=(\mathcal{M},D(\mathcal{M}))
.

(ii)

Suppose
\phi\in C_{b}(\Omega,\boldsymbol{\mathcal{Z}}(E)_{\textbf{s}})
. Then, see Proposition
2
(ii),
\mathcal{M}_{\phi}
is a bounded multiplication operator and therefore generates a uniformly continuous multiplication semigroup
(e^{t\mathcal{M}_{\phi}})_{t\geq 0}
on
C_{0}(\Omega,E)
which must coincides with the semigroup
(\mathcal{T}_{\phi}(t))_{t\geq 0}
, since
e^{t\mathcal{M}_{\phi}}s(\cdot)=e^{t\phi(\cdot)}s(\cdot)=\mathcal{T}_{\phi}(t)s(\cdot)
for all
s\in C_{0}(\Omega,E)
and
t\geq 0
.

Now, on the contrary, assume
\phi:\Omega\longrightarrow\boldsymbol{\mathcal{Z}}(E)_{\textbf{s}}
is continuous but unbounded. Let
(x_{n})\subseteq\Omega
be a sequence such that
0<||\phi(x_{n})||_{\mathscr{L}(E)}\ \forall n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}||\phi(x_{n})||_{\mathscr{L}(E)}=\infty
. As such, we can find
(z_{n})_{n}\in E
with
||z_{n}||=1
such that
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}||\phi(x_{n})z_{n}||=\infty
. Moreover,
||I_{E}-e^{T}||>0
for every
T\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{Z}}(E)_{\textbf{s}}
with
||T||_{\mathscr{L}(E)}=1
, implies there exists
\delta>0
such that
||z_{n}-e^{t_{n}\phi(x_{n})}z_{n}||\geq\delta\ \forall\ n\in\mathbb{N}
where
t_{n}:=1/||\phi(x_{n})||
.

Choose
f_{n}\in C_{0}(\Omega)
with
||f_{n}||=1=f_{n}(x_{n})
and let
s_{n}:=f_{n}\otimes z_{n}
, i.e.,
s_{n}(\cdot)=f_{n}(\cdot)z_{n}
so that
s_{n}\in C_{0}(\Omega,E)
,
s_{n}(x_{n})=z_{n}
and
||s_{n}||=1\ \forall\ n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Then, we obtain that

\displaystyle\delta\leq

\displaystyle||z_{n}-e^{t_{n}\phi(x_{n})}z_{n}||

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle||s_{n}(x_{n})-e^{t_{n}\phi(x_{n})}s_{n}(x_{n})||

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle||s_{n}-e^{t_{n}\phi}s_{n}||

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle||\mathcal{I}-\mathcal{T}_{\phi}(t_{n})||

\forall\ n\in\mathbb{N}
. And since
t_{n}\rightarrow 0
in
\mathbb{R}_{+}
, this implies that
(\mathcal{T}_{\phi}(t))_{t\geq 0}
is not uniformly continuous.

Equivalently, since the
C_{0}
-semigroup
(\mathcal{T}_{\phi}(t))_{t\geq 0}
is uniformly continuous if and only if its generator
(\mathcal{M}_{\phi},D(\mathcal{M}_{\phi}))
is bounded, Proposition
2
(ii) implies that this is the case if and only if
\phi\in C_{b}(\Omega,\boldsymbol{\mathcal{Z}}(E)_{\textbf{s}})
.

∎

4
Characterization of multiplication semigroups on
C_{0}(\Omega,E)

In the following Lemma, we obtain the complete characterization of multiplication
C_{0}
-semigroups on
C_{0}(\Omega,E)
as those arising from continuous functions
\phi:\Omega\longrightarrow\boldsymbol{\mathcal{Z}}(E)_{\textbf{s}}
as in Definition
4
. This can been seen as the generalization of
[
3
, Proposition 3.6, p.24-25]
.

Lemma 1
.

Let
m_{t}\in C_{b}(\Omega,\boldsymbol{\mathcal{Z}}(E)_{\textbf{s}})
for each
t\geq 0
such that the induced multiplication semigroup
\mathcal{T}(t)_{t\geq 0}
given by

\mathcal{T}(t):C_{0}(\Omega,E)\longrightarrow C_{0}(\Omega,E);\ s(\cdot)\mapsto\mathcal{T}(t)s(\cdot):=m_{t}(\cdot)s(\cdot)

is a
C_{0}
-semigroup on
C_{0}(\Omega,E)
. Furthermore, suppose that, for each
x\in\Omega
, the induced pointwise multiplication semigroup
T_{x}(t)_{t\geq 0}
given by

T_{x}(t):E\longrightarrow E;\ z\mapsto T_{x}(t)z:=m_{t}(x)z

is uniformly continuous on
E
. Then, there exists a continuous function
\phi:\Omega\longrightarrow\boldsymbol{\mathcal{Z}}(E)_{\textbf{s}}
, where
(T_{x}(t))_{t\geq 0}=(e^{t\phi(x)})_{t\geq 0}
for each
x\in\Omega
with

\sup_{x\in\Omega}||e^{t_{0}\phi(x)}||_{\mathscr{L}(E)}<\infty\ \text{ for some }\ t_{0}\in(0,1]

such that
m_{t}(\cdot)=e^{t\phi(\cdot)}
for all
t\geq 0
.

Proof.

By the strong continuity of
\mathcal{T}(t)_{t\geq 0}
on
C_{0}(\Omega,E)
, i.e., the continuity of the mapping

\mathbb{R}_{+}\longrightarrow C_{0}(\Omega,E);\ t\mapsto m_{t}(\cdot)s(\cdot)

for each
s\in C_{0}(\Omega,E)
; we obtain that, the pointwise multiplication semigroup
T_{x}(t)_{t\geq 0}
on
E
given by

T_{x}(t):E\longrightarrow E;\ z\mapsto m_{t}(x)z

is a
C_{0}
-semigroup on
E
for each
x\in\Omega
.
In particular, given that each
T_{x}(t)_{t\geq 0}
is uniformly continuous implies that it is generated by a bounded multiplication operator, say
\phi(x)\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{Z}}(E)_{\textbf{s}}
for each
x\in\Omega
. Indeed, we would have that
\phi(x):=\lim_{t\downarrow 0}\frac{T_{x}(t)-I_{E}}{t}\in\mathscr{L}(E)
in the operator norm such that
T_{x}(t)=e^{t\phi(x)}
for all
t\geq 0
and each
x\in\Omega
; and since
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{Z}}(E)
is norm-closed in
\mathscr{L}(E)
, it follows that
\phi(x)\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{Z}}(E)_{\textbf{s}}
for all
x\in\Omega.
See also
[
1
, Proposition 5.15, p.288]
.

Now, since
\mathcal{T}(t)s(\cdot)=T_{(\cdot)}(t)s(\cdot)
for all
t\geq 0
and
s\in C_{0}(\Omega,E)
, it definitely must be that
||T(t)||=\sup_{x\in\Omega}||T_{x}(t)||_{\mathscr{L}(E)}=\sup_{x\in\Omega}||e^{t\phi(x)}||_{\mathscr{L}(E)}<\infty
for all
t\geq 0
. Therefore, it does follows that
m_{t}(x)=e^{t\phi(x)}
for all
x\in\Omega
and
t\geq 0.

So, it remains only to show that the mapping
\phi:\Omega\longrightarrow\boldsymbol{\mathcal{Z}}(E)_{\textbf{s}};\ x\mapsto\phi(x)
is continuous. And, on the contrary, assume
\phi
is not continuous. Let
(x_{n})\subseteq\Omega
be a sequence converging to
x\in\Omega
but
\phi(x_{n})\nrightarrow\phi(x)
in
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{Z}}(E)_{\textbf{s}}
. As such, for
\varepsilon>0
we can find
z_{0}\in E
with
||z_{0}||=1
such that
||\phi(x)z_{0}-\phi(x_{n})z_{0}||\geq\varepsilon

\forall\ n\in\mathbb{N}
. Since

\phi(x)z_{0}=\lim_{t\downarrow 0}\frac{T_{x}(t)z_{0}-z_{0}}{t}\ \text{ and }\phi(x_{n})z_{0}=\lim_{t\downarrow 0}\frac{T_{x_{n}}(t)z_{0}-z_{0}}{t}

\forall\ n\in\mathbb{N}
, we can find some
t_{1}\in(0,1]
such that

\displaystyle\varepsilon\leq

\displaystyle||\phi(x)z_{0}-\phi(x_{n})z_{0}||

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle||\frac{T_{x}(t)}{t}z_{0}-\frac{T_{x_{n}}(t)}{t}z_{0}||

\forall\ n\in\mathbb{N}
with
0<t\leq t_{1}
, which is contradicting the fact that
m_{t}(\cdot)=T_{(\cdot)}(t)
are continuous. Hence,
\phi:\Omega\longrightarrow\boldsymbol{\mathcal{Z}}(E)_{\textbf{s}}
is continuous.

∎

Proof of Theorem
1
:

Combining the above Lemma
1
with Proposition
2
(i-iii) and Proposition
5
proves the assertions of Theorem
1
.

5
Examples

In this last section, we state some observations and present concrete examples of multiplication semigroups. This can be seen as extension of
[
3
, Example 3.7, p.25]
in the scalar-valued case.

(i)

If
K
is a compact (Hausdorff) space and
E
is a Banach lattice, then every continuous function
\phi:K\longrightarrow\boldsymbol{\mathcal{Z}}(E)_{\textbf{s}}
is already bounded. Therefore, the induced multiplication operator
\mathcal{M}_{\phi}
on
C(K,E)
is bounded, and hence every generated multiplication semigroup
(\mathcal{T}_{\phi}(t))_{t\geq 0}
is always uniformly continuous on
C(K,E)
.

(ii)

Let
q:\Omega\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}
be an unbounded continuous function which is bounded above as in Remark
3
, set
w:=\sup_{x\in\Omega}Req(x)
and
\phi:=qI_{E}
. Then, the induced unbounded multiplication operator
(\mathcal{M}_{\phi},D(\mathcal{M}_{\phi}))
generates a strongly continuous multiplication semigroup
(\mathcal{T}_{\phi}(t))_{t\geq 0}
on
C_{0}(\Omega,E)
given by

\mathcal{T}_{\phi}(t)s(\cdot)=e^{tq(\cdot)}s(\cdot)

for all
t\geq 0
. Moreover,
\sigma(\mathcal{M}_{\phi})=\overline{q(\Omega)}
and
||\mathcal{T}_{\phi}(t)||=e^{wt}
for all
t\geq 0
.

(iii)

Let
\Omega:=\mathbb{N}
and
E:=\mathbb{C}^{2}
, then each sequence
(\phi^{1},\phi^{2}):=\text{diag}(\phi^{1}_{n},\phi^{2}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of 2 by 2 diagonal complex matrices induced a multiplication operator
(\mathcal{M}_{(\phi^{1},\phi^{2})},D(\mathcal{M}_{(\phi^{1},\phi^{2})}))

(s^{1}_{n},s^{2}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mapsto(\phi^{1}_{n}s^{1}_{n},\phi^{2}_{n}s^{2}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

on
C_{0}(\mathbb{N},\mathbb{C}^{2})=:c_{0}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
. For
(\phi^{1},\phi^{2}):=\text{diag}(in,-n^{2})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, the induced multiplication operator
(\mathcal{M}_{(\phi^{1},\phi^{2})},D(\mathcal{M}_{(\phi^{1},\phi^{2})}))
generates a strongly continuous multiplication semigroup
(\mathcal{T}_{(\phi^{1},\phi^{2})}(t))_{t\geq 0}
on
c_{0}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
given by

\mathcal{T}_{(\phi^{1},\phi^{2})}(t)(s^{1}_{n},s^{2}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}=(e^{int}s^{1}_{n},e^{-n^{2}t}s^{2}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\ \text{ for all }\ t\geq 0.

(iv)

As a typical instance of (i) above, let
K:=\left\{1,2,\ldots,m\right\}
be a finite set and
E
a Banach lattice; so that
C(K,E)=E^{m}
is an m-copies of
E
. Given an m-ordinates
\phi:=(\phi_{1},\ldots\phi_{m})
of multiplication operators on
E
, i.e.,
\phi_{j}\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{Z}}(E)_{\textbf{s}}
for
1\leq j\leq m
, the induced (bounded) multiplication operator

(s_{1},\ldots,s_{m})\mapsto(\phi_{1}s_{1},\ldots,\phi_{m}s_{m})

on
E^{m}
corresponds to the diagonal operator matrix
A_{\phi}=\text{diag}(\phi_{1},\ldots\phi_{m})
. And, therefore, the generated uniformly continuous multiplication semigroup
(e^{tA_{\phi}})_{t\geq 0}
on
E^{m}
is given by diagonal operator matrices

e^{tA_{\phi}}=\text{diag}(e^{t\phi_{1}},\ldots,e^{t\phi_{m}})\ \text{ for all }\ t\geq 0.

Moreover,
\sigma(A_{\phi})=\underset{1\leq j\leq m}{\bigcup}\sigma(\phi_{j})
and
||e^{tA_{\phi}}||=\underset{1\leq j\leq m}{\max}||e^{t\phi_{j}}||\text{ for all }t\geq 0.

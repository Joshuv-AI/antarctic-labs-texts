Evaluation of Change in Salivary pH, Following Consumption of Different Snacks and Beverages and Estimation of their Oral Clearance Time in Pre-School Children
Authors: Taskin Khan, Anil Kohli, Eeraveni Ranadheer, Manoj Yadav, Saleh Abuzer Shams, Smriti Priya
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTAim:To evaluate the change in salivary pH following consumption of different snacks and beverages in pre-school children and also to evaluate their oral clearance time.Materials and Methods:For 7 days 7 different types of snacks and beverage were given to the 150 preschool children for consumption. After that pH of saliva was calculated with digital pH meter at various intervals up to 30 min. Data were statistically analyzed by using SPSS version 26.Results:In the 7-day study there is statistically significant change in salivary pH after consumption of snacks and beverage. Immediately there was maximum drop in pH value after test food consumption and up to 30 min maximum of test food got cleared from the oral cavity.Conclusion:Due to lack of awareness in child or their parents they don’t know the harmful effect of some food so as a dental professional we should guide them about this harmful effect and introduce them to preventive measures, Tooth Friendly diet and oral hygiene practices.

INTRODUCTION

Oral health is a vital component of overall health, with both being intricately connected. Nutritional choices significantly impact oral health, especially in the development of dental caries and erosion of hard dental tissues.[1]

Modern diets, characterized by frequent intake of sugary, sticky, and refined foods, prolong acid exposure in the mouth. These foods adhere to teeth and resist natural clearance, increasing the risk of decay. Moreover, the shift towards acidic beverages and processed snacks has raised the prevalence of dental erosion, which differs from caries by lacking bacterial involvement and affecting broader tooth surfaces.[234]

Saliva assists in oral sugar clearance and pH restoration, but frequent snacking limits its effectiveness.[35678] Preventive care is crucial, as dental treatments can be costly and natural dentition is irreplaceable. Maintaining a balanced diet, limiting sugar frequency, and promoting oral hygiene are key to preserving oral health and minimizing disease progression.[567]

MATERIALS AND METHODS

In total, 150 Preschool children were selected from Indian English School, Darbhanga, Bihar. Unstimulated saliva samples were collected at least one-hour post-breakfast, before consumption of test foods. Each participant consumed a specific snack or beverage, followed by collection of saliva samples at designated intervals: Immediately after consumption, 5 min, 10 min, 15 min, 20 min, 25 min, 30 min post consumption.

Different food items were administered over a seven-day period, and saliva samples were collected accordingly.

For sample collection, participants were instructed to sit with their head slightly forward and expectorate into disposable glasses. The baseline pH was recorded prior to food consumption. Saliva pH was measured using a digital pH meter, and oral clearance time was determined by the duration required for the salivary pH to return to baseline values.

DISCUSSION

This study evaluated changes in salivary pH and oral clearance time following consumption of commonly consumed snacks and drinks in preschool children. A significant drop in salivary pH was observed post-consumption, especially with acidic items like orange, apple, and cold drinks. Conversely, biscuits and chocolates, though less acidic, exhibited slower clearance rates, extending acid exposure time.

After approximately 30 minutes, salivary pH in most groups approached baseline values, indicating the role of saliva in buffering and restoring oral homeostasis. These findings align with previous research highlighting differences in the cariogenic potential of food items based on sugar content, texture, intrinsic acidity, and duration of retention in the mouth.[91011]

While this study provides valuable insights, it was limited by a short duration, small sample size, and challenges in saliva sampling among young children. Future research should consider larger populations and extended observation periods to further validate findings.

CONCLUSION

Promoting awareness among parents and caregivers about the importance of tooth-friendly diets and the harmful effects of frequent snacking can contribute significantly to the prevention of dental caries and erosion in children.[121314]

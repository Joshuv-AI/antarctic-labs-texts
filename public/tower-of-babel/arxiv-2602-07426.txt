Maximally probable tree topologies with r r -furcation
Emily H. Dickey & Noah A. Rosenberg

Abstract
For a specific rooted labeled tree topology, a labeled history is a sequence of branchings that give rise to that labeled topology as it unfolds over time. Here, for r r -furcating trees, we use a connection with Huffman trees from information theory to identify maximally probable rooted trees—unlabeled r r -furcating topologies whose labelings each have a number of labeled histories greater than or equal to those of all other labeled topologies. Our characterization of the unique maximally probable r r -furcating unlabeled topology generalizes the Harding–Hammersley–Grimmett result identifying the maximally probable bifurcating unlabeled topology, and it provides a new proof for that result. We present a conjecture for the maximally probable r r -furcating unlabeled topology if labeled histories are tabulated allowing for simultaneous branching events across multiple internal nodes of a tree.



Keywords:
Huffman trees, labeled histories, majorization, multifurcation, phylogenetics

Introduction

The concept of a

arises when considering phylogenetic models that produce trees as a consequence of evolutionary processes that unfold over time
[
18
,
22
]
. For a leaf-labeled tree with
n
leaves that have mutually distinct labels, a labeled history represents the sequence of branching events that have given rise to the labeled tree structure. Labeled histories are useful particularly in the setting of the Yule–Harding model on bifurcating labeled topologies
[

,

]
, under which each of the

possible labeled histories
[

]
is equally likely to be produced by the evolutionary process.

For a fixed number of leaves
n\geqslant 2
, a
maximally probable
tree is an unlabeled topology whose labelings each possess a number of labeled histories greater than or equal to those of any other unlabeled topology
[
6
]
. Proving a conjecture of Harding
[
15
]
, Hammersley & Grimmett
[
14
]
characterized the unique maximally probable tree among rooted bifurcating trees with a fixed number of leaves

.

Generalizing from bifurcating trees, we have recently been developing the combinatorics of labeled histories in the setting of
r
-furcating trees
[
7
,
8
]
—which arise in contexts such as adaptive radiation, large family sizes, and pathogen transmission. In particular, in
[

]
, we obtained a variety of results concerning labeled histories for
r
-furcating trees, also termed
strictly

r
-furcating trees, in which the number of immediate descendants of each internal node of a rooted tree is equal to
r
for a fixed constant
r\geqslant 2
shared by all internal nodes. Our results included the enumeration of the labeled histories for a fixed
r
-furcating labeled topology. We also presented a conjecture for the identity of the maximally probable tree shape with
r
-furcation.

Here, we identify the unique maximally probable tree in the setting of
r
-furcating trees,

, proving our conjecture. That is, for

and

for integers

, we determine the shape of the unique unlabeled topology whose labelings possess more labeled histories than any other labeled topology. Our approach makes use of a connection to tree problems in information theory; specifically, we use the Huffman algorithm
[
4

and the trees it generates. Because our approach identifies the maximally probable tree for each

, a consequence of our characterization is that we obtain a new proof of the Harding–Hammersley–Grimmett result describing the maximally probable bifurcating unlabeled topology.

Definitions

Definitions largely follow Dickey & Rosenberg
[
7
]
, tracing to Steel
[
22
]
and King & Rosenberg
[
18
]
. For a rooted tree
T
, nodes are
leaf nodes
or
internal nodes
; the unique
root node
is included among internal nodes. The number of internal nodes of
T
is
w(T)
; the number of leaves is
|T|
. For a leaf-labeled tree
T
, the
labeled topology
of
T
is its topological structure together with its leaf labels, all of which are distinct from one another. The
tree shape
or
unlabeled topology
of
T
is the topological structure without the leaf labels.

For two nodes
u,v
of

is
descended
from
v
and
v
is
ancestral
to
u
if
v
lies on the path from the root to
u
. A node is both ancestral to itself and descended from itself.

For
r\geqslant 2
, in an
r
-furcating tree
, also termed
strictly

r
-furcating, each internal node has exactly
r
immediate descendant nodes. A
cherry node
has exactly two child nodes, both of which are leaves. The generalization for
r
-furcating trees is a
broomstick node
, an internal node whose
r
children are all leaves.

For a tree
T
whose root has immediate subtrees
T_{1},T_{2},\ldots,T_{r}
, we write
T=T_{1}\oplus T_{2}\oplus\ldots\oplus T_{r}
. If some of the

are identical, then we abbreviate the notation; for example, if
T_{1}=T_{2}=\ldots=T_{j}
, then we write
T=jT_{1}\oplus T_{j+1}\oplus T_{j+2}\ldots\oplus T_{r}
. Empty subtrees are ignored, so that, for example,
T_{1}\oplus T_{2}\oplus\emptyset=T_{1}\oplus T_{2}
.

For a tree
T
, each node is associated with a
time
. Leaves all have the same time. In the classic Yule–Harding model for bifurcating trees, internal nodes have mutually distinct times, and the tree has
non-simultaneous
branching. Given a labeled topology for a rooted tree
T
with
w
internal nodes and non-simultaneous branching, a
labeled history
for
T
is a bijection
f
from the set of internal nodes of
T
to
\{1,2,\ldots,w(T)\}
, so that if node
u
is descended from node
v
in
T
and
u\neq v
, then
f(u)<f(v)
. A labeled history can be viewed as the temporal sequence of internal nodes, with the convention here that the numbers assigned to nodes increase backward in time along genealogical lines, and the root is assigned the value
w(T)
.

Among
r
-furcating labeled topologies with fixed number of leaves
n
and fixed
r\geqslant 2
, a
maximally probable
labeled topology is a labeled topology whose number of labeled histories is greater than or equal to that of all other labeled topologies
[
6
]
. Because each labeling of an unlabeled topology gives rise to the same number of labeled histories, we use
unlabeled topologies
to indicate the maximally probable labeled topologies. In a slight abuse of terminology, we refer to unlabeled topologies as maximally probable, understanding that we refer to associated arbitrarily labeled topologies. The term
maximally probable
arises from the Yule–Harding probability model, in which the probability of a specified (bifurcating) labeled topology is proportional to its number of labeled histories—so that labeled topologies with the most labeled histories are the most probable labeled topologies under the model. The Yule–Harding model is for bifurcating trees, but we continue to use the term
maximally probable
in the setting of
r
-furcation: a maximally probable unlabeled topology is an unlabeled topology whose labelings possess a number of labeled histories greater than or equal to those of the labelings of all other unlabeled topologies.

Although the definition does not imply that only one maximally probable unlabeled topology exists among
r
-furcating unlabeled topologies with
n
leaves, we will see in Section
6
that with
r
-furcation at fixed
r\geqslant 2
, there exists a unique maximally probable unlabeled topology among
r
-furcating unlabeled topologies with
n
leaves. We denote this unique maximally probable unlabeled topology by
U_{n}^{*}
, understanding that the associated
r
will be clear from the context.

3
Review of previous results

For bifurcating trees, Hammersley & Grimmett
[
14
]
obtained the unique unlabeled tree topology whose labeled topologies possess the largest number of labeled histories among labeled topologies with
n
leaves. Our main result, presented in Section
6
, is a generalization of this result to
r
-furcating trees,

. The generalization provides a new proof of the result of Hammersley & Grimmett
[
14
]
and proves our Conjecture 13 of
[

]
. First, we recall the maximally probable unlabeled tree topology in the bifurcating case.

Theorem 1

(
[
14
]
)
.

The unique unlabeled topology whose labelings have the largest number of labeled histories among bifurcating labeled topologies with
n
leaves takes the form
U_{n}^{*}=U_{t}^{*}\oplus U_{n-t}^{*}
, where for
n\geqslant 3
,

\displaystyle t=2^{\lfloor\log_{2}(\frac{n-1}{3})\rfloor+1}.

U_{1}^{*}
and
U_{2}^{*}
are specified trivially, each as the only unlabeled topology with the associated value of
n
. For
n\geqslant 3
,
U_{n}^{*}
is formed by joining the maximally probable unlabeled topologies of two smaller sizes to a shared root. The values
t
and
n-t
in the theorem specify the numbers of leaves in the subtrees of the root of
U_{n}^{*}
. As
n
proceeds from 3 to 16, the values of
(t,n-t)
are equal to (1,2), (2,2), (2,3), (2,4), (4,3), (4,4), (4,5), (4,6), (4,7), (4,8), (8,5), (8,6), (8,7), and (8,8).

To generalize Theorem
1
to
r
-furcation, consider
r\geqslant 2
, and let
n=w(r-1)+1
for an integer
w\geqslant 1
, as the number of leaves in an
r
-furcating tree is 1 more than a multiple of
r-1
. The number of labeled histories
N(T)
for an
r
-furcating labeled topology,
T
, with
n
leaves and immediate subtrees
T_{1},T_{2},\ldots,T_{r}
can be found recursively by
[
7
, eq. 3.6]

N(T)={\frac{|T|-1}{r-1}-1\choose\frac{|T_{1}|-1}{r-1},\frac{|T_{2}|-1}{r-1},\ldots,\frac{|T_{r}|-1}{r-1}}N(T_{1})N(T_{2})\ldots N(T_{r}).

N(T)
can also be expressed in a closed form. Write
V^{0}(T)
for the set of internal nodes of
T
, and let
m(v)
be the number of leaves descended from internal node
v
.

Proposition 2

(
[
7
]
, Prop. 8)
.

The number of labeled histories for an
r
-furcating labeled topology
T
with
n
leaves,
N(T)
, satisfies
N(T)=1
for
n=1
, and for
n=w(r-1)+1
with
w\geqslant 1
,

\displaystyle N(T)=\frac{\big(\frac{n-1}{r-1}\big)!}{\prod_{v\in V^{0}(T)}\big[\frac{m(v)-1}{r-1}\big]}.

For an unlabeled topology
T
, we write
N(T)
to tabulate labeled histories for a labeled topology obtained by an arbitrary labeling of the leaves of
T
. We seek to characterize all unlabeled topologies

T^{*}
such that

T^{*}\in\arg\max_{\{T:|T|=n\}}N(T),

(1)

where the maximization is over the set of (
r
-furcating) unlabeled topologies with
n
leaves. Using the monotonicity of the logarithm, the maximization of
N(T)
has the following equivalence:

\displaystyle\max_{\{T:|T|=n\}}N(T)

\displaystyle\iff\max_{\{T:|T|=n\}}\frac{\big(\frac{n-1}{r-1}\big)!}{\prod_{v\in V^{0}(T)}\big[\frac{m(v)-1}{r-1}\big]}

\displaystyle\iff\min_{\{T:|T|=n\}}\prod_{v\in V^{0}(T)}\bigg[\frac{m(v)-1}{r-1}\bigg]

\displaystyle\iff\min_{\{T:|T|=n\}}\sum_{v\in V^{0}(T)}\log\big[m(v)-1\big],

(2)

noting that we need not consider terms that only involve the fixed constants
n
and
r
. With our minimization characterized in eq.
2
, we now present results from majorization theory, which will be used in our proofs.

4
Majorization lemmas

We continue with definitions and lemmas from majorization theory, based on Marshall et al.
[
20
]
.

Definition 3

(
[
20
]
, Definition 1.A.1, p. 8)
.

For
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, with the elements of
x,y
written in non-increasing order,
x_{i}\geqslant x_{i+1}
and
y_{i}\geqslant y_{i+1}
for all

, we say that
y

majorizes

,
x\prec y
, if

(i)

\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}\leqslant\sum_{i=1}^{k}y_{i}
for all
k
,
1\leqslant k\leqslant n-1
, and

(ii)

\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}y_{i}
.

Definition 4

(
[
20
]
, Definition 3.A.1, p. 80)
.

A real-valued function
\phi
defined on a set
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is said to be
Schur-convex
on
\mathcal{A}
if for all
x,y\in A

x\prec y\implies\phi(x)\leqslant\phi(y).

If, in addition,
\phi(x)<\phi(y)
whenever
x\prec y
but
x
is not a permutation of
y
, then
\phi
is said to be
strictly
Schur-convex on
\mathcal{A}
.

We will need a result about strict Schur-convexity of a sum of strictly convex functions in individual variables.

Proposition 5

(
[
20
]
, 3.C.1.a, p. 92)
.

Let
I\subset\mathbb{R}
be an interval, and for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, let
\phi(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}g(x_{i})
, where
g:I\to\mathbb{R}
. If
g
is strictly convex on
I
, then
\phi
is strictly Schur-convex on
I^{n}
.

We will also need the concept of

.

Definition 6

(
[
20
]
, Definition 1.A.2, p. 12)
.

For
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, with the elements of
x,y
written in non-decreasing order,

x_{i}\leqslant x_{i+1}

\sum_{i=1}^{k}y_{i}\leqslant\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}
.

We note that majorization is commonly defined using non-increasing vectors and weak supermajorization is defined using non-decreasing vectors.

Next, we will need a definition of “increasing” and “decreasing” for a multivariate function.

Definition 7

(
[
20
]
, p. 637)
.

For
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, write
x\leqslant y
if
x_{i}\leqslant y_{i}
,
i=1,2,\ldots,n
. A multivariate function
\phi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
is said to be

x\leqslant y\implies\phi(x)\leqslant\phi(y).

Further, if

x\leqslant y\text{ and }x\not=y\implies\phi(x)<\phi(y),

then
\phi
is said to be
strictly
increasing. If
-\phi
is (strictly) increasing, then
\phi
is said to be (strictly) decreasing.

We present a lemma about supermajorization when a Schur-convex function is applied.

Lemma 8

(
[
20
]
, 3.A.8.a, p. 87)
.

Let
\phi
be a real-valued function defined on a set
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The following statement holds if and only if
\phi
is strictly decreasing and strictly Schur-convex on
\mathcal{A}
:

x\prec^{w}y\text{ on }\mathcal{A}\text{ and }x\text{ is not a permutation of }y\implies\phi(x)<\phi(y).

We next present the main result of the section, a lemma that we will use in Section
6
in our maximization of the number of labeled histories over the set of unlabeled topologies.

Lemma 9
.

Let
x,y\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{n}
. If
x\prec^{w}y
and
x
is not a permutation of
y
, then
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\log(y_{i})<\sum_{i=1}^{n}\log(x_{i})
.

Proof.

Consider the function
\phi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
defined by
\phi(z)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}-\log(z_{i})
. First, we show that
\phi(z)
is strictly decreasing and strictly Schur-convex on
\mathcal{A}=(0,\infty)^{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Consider
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{>0}
, where
x\leqslant y
as in Definition
7
and
x\not=y
. We show that
\phi(x)
is strictly decreasing by showing that
-\phi(x)
is strictly increasing. For each
i
, because
x_{i}\leqslant y_{i}
, we have
\log(x_{i})\leqslant\log(y_{i})
, as
f(x)=\log(x)
is strictly increasing for all
x\in(0,\infty)
. Because
x\not=y
, there must exist some

such that
x_{j}<y_{j}
. For that

, we have
\log(x_{j})<\log(y_{j})
. Thus,

-\phi(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\log(x_{i})<\sum_{i=1}^{n}\log(y_{i})=-\phi(y),

so that
\phi(z)
is strictly decreasing, as needed.

Next,
f(x)=-\log(x)
is strictly convex on
(0,\infty)
, and therefore, by Proposition
5
,
\phi(z)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}-\log(z_{i})
is strictly Schur-convex on
\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{n}
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{n}
. Because
\phi
is strictly decreasing and strictly Schur-convex on
\mathcal{A}
, if
x\prec^{w}y
and
x
is not a permutation of
y
, then by Lemma

,

\sum_{i=1}^{n}-\log(x_{i})<\sum_{i=1}^{n}-\log(y_{i}),

completing the proof.
∎

The Huffman algorithm and Huffman trees

5.1
The Huffman algorithm

Our next step in obtaining the maximally probable
r
-furcating unlabeled topology is to borrow results concerning Huffman trees in information theory. Following
[
12
]
, let
\{v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{w}\}\cup\{\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ldots,\ell_{n}\}

In the

with associated weights

-tree

-furcating

-merge operator,

, corresponds to a step of the Huffman algorithm: the

nodes of smallest weight are merged into a new node, which has a weight corresponding to the sum of these

Lemma 16
.

Fix
r\geqslant 2
, and let

for a nonnegative integer

-furcating

Proof.

Recall that the Huffman algorithm can potentially produce multiple

and a positive integer

th elements of

2.

M_{r}^{i}(\vec{1})
selects

from more than

such terms. By Lemma

, all terms

represent fully symmetric

-furcating trees. Hence, irrespective of the choice of which

6
The maximally probable
r
-furcating tree

With the unique
r
-furcating
H
-tree of Proposition
17
in hand, we now show that it is maximally probable. For this demonstration, we use a second aspect of the Huffman algorithm, the

, which connects to majorization theory. An
r
-furcating unlabeled topology

with a weight vector for its leaves has a weight sequence
\omega(T)=\big(\omega(T_{1}),\omega(T_{2}),\ldots,\omega(T_{w})\big)
, a vector of the internal node weights in non-decreasing order.

The weight sequence corresponds to the mergings of the Huffman algorithm;
\omega(T_{i})
corresponds to the sum of the weights of the
r
nodes merged in step
i

Note that although the
H
-tree is unique for the uniform weight vector, in general, given an initial weight vector
and
a weight sequence, the
H
-tree is not necessarily unique. For example, in Figure
2
, the two distinct tree topologies beginning from the same weight vector
(1,1,1,1,1,3,4)
both have the weight sequence
(3,5,12)
.

We now state a property of the weight sequence for

-furcating
H
-trees.

Lemma 18

(
[
12
]
, Lemma 1, p. 371)
.

Fix a weight vector
\vec{c}
for

,

. Let

be an

-furcating unlabeled topology with

is an

if and only if for every

-furcating unlabeled topology

associated with

,
\omega(T)\prec^{w}\omega(S)
.

As a consequence of this lemma, if multiple distinct
H
-trees were to be associated with the weight vector
\vec{c}
, then they must have the same weight sequence. With all the necessary lemmas demonstrated, we finally prove our main result.

Theorem 19
.

Fix
r\geqslant 2
and
w\geqslant 1
. The unique
r
-furcating unlabeled topology whose labelings maximize the number of labeled histories among labeled topologies with

U^{*}_{n}=(s-1)U_{r^{p-1}}^{*}\oplus U_{r^{p-1}+b}^{*}\oplus(r-s)U_{r^{p}}^{*}
, where

\displaystyle p

\displaystyle=\lfloor\log_{r}n\rfloor,

\displaystyle s

\displaystyle=\bigg\lfloor\frac{r^{p+1}-n}{r^{p}-r^{p-1}}\bigg\rfloor,

\displaystyle b

\displaystyle=n-\big[sr^{p-1}+(r-s)r^{p}\big].

Proof.

Recall from eq.
2
that we are looking for an
r
-furcating unlabeled topology on
n
leaves that minimizes

\sum_{v\in V^{0}(T)}\log\big[m(v)-1\big].

(3)

We wish to show that only one unlabeled topology achieves the minimal value. We claim that this unlabeled topology is the
r
-furcating
H
-tree on
n

\vec{1}
.

By Proposition
17
, for the weight vector
\vec{1}
, the unique

-furcating

, has the shape defined in the statement of the theorem. Consider another

is the unique

-furcating

Recall that elements of

corresponds to

The objective function in eq.

has terms

, so we construct
\omega^{\prime}(S)=\omega(S)-\vec{1}
and
\omega^{\prime}(T)=\omega(T)-\vec{1}
. Because

, we know that

Because

Because the argument holds for arbitrary

8
Discussion

We have generalized the Harding–Hammersley–Grimmett result describing the shape of the maximally probable bifurcating unlabeled topology, identifying the maximally probable
r
-furcating
unlabeled topology for
r\geqslant 2
(Theorem
19
). In completing this generalization, we have obtained a new proof of the characterization of the maximally probable unlabeled topology for the bifurcating case (Theorem
1
). Our theorem for general
r
verifies a conjecture from
[
7
]
.

In the bifurcating case, maximally probable unlabeled topologies have been used in the mathematical analysis of likely outcomes of evolutionary processes under the Yule–Harding model for evolutionary descent
[
6
]
. These topologies have appeared as maximizers for other functions computed from bifurcating trees
[
10
]
, and they have also inspired the development of analogous concepts of “maximally probable” for other classes of bifurcating trees
[
5
,
9
]
. By obtaining the maximally probable
r
-furcating unlabeled topologies, our work here suggests that the applications of maximally probable trees in the bifurcating case might be possible to extend to settings with
r
-furcation.

In
[
7
]
, our conjecture for the shape of the maximally probable unlabeled topology was inspired in part by the work of
[
1
]
, which considered recursive minimizations building on the solution presented by
[
14
]
for the case of bifurcating trees. Our proof of the conjecture here, however, is based on an entirely different approach. In particular, our phylogenetic maximization problem, tracing to
[
15
]
, is solved via connections to Huffman trees from information theory
[
12
,
16
]
and to a tape-merging algorithm from the early years of computer science
[
13
]
. In Section
5
, after reviewing the Huffman algorithm and its associated
H
-trees, we discussed the
r
-merge operator and normal sequences (Definition
10
), and we showed that the
H
-tree produced with the uniform weight vector on tree leaves is unique (Proposition
17
). In Section
6
, the final steps in the proof consider weight sequences for an
H
-tree and connect the
H
-tree to majorization theory (Lemma
18
) before finally showing that the unique
H
-tree with a uniform weight vector is the conjectured unique maximally probable
r
-furcating unlabeled topology (Theorem
19
).

The information-theoretic connection that we have uncovered here for a mathematical phylogenetics problem merits further investigation. Huffman trees are grounded in the construction and analysis of prefix-free codes, in which each symbol from a source alphabet is encoded by a codeword in such a way that no codeword is a prefix of any other codeword
[
4
, Section 5.6, p. 92]
. In a setting with a uniform weight vector, the optimal prefix-free code for an alphabet with a uniform probability distribution can be extracted from the tree constructed by the Huffman algorithm, where an optimal code is one that minimizes the expected codeword length. Connections between the setting of evolutionary trees and information theory might be of interest in light of recent analyses of various tree encodings in mathematical phylogenetics
[
2
,
3
,
17
,
19
,
21
]
.

In Section
7
, we extended our analysis to
r
-furcating trees with simultaneity, presenting a conjecture for the maximally probable
r
-furcating unlabeled topology in the setting in which labeled histories permit simultaneity (Conjecture
20
), with Table
1
confirming the conjecture for values for
3\leqslant n\leqslant 29
for the trifucating case. This conjecture provides a natural extension of a previous conjecture in the bifurcating case (Conjecture 14 of
[
8
]
), claiming that the maximally probable
r
-furcating unlabeled topology in the non-simultaneous case is also the maximally probable
r
-furcating unlabeled topology with simultaneity.

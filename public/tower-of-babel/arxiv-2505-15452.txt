Equilibration and convected limit in 2D-1D corotational Oldroyd’s fluid-structure interaction
Prince Romeo Mensah

Abstract
. We consider a solute-solvent-structure mutually coupled system of equations given by an Oldroyd-type model for a two-dimensional dilute corotational polymer fluid with solute diffusion and damping that is interacting with a one-dimensional viscoelastic shell. Firstly, we give the rate at which its solution decays exponentially in time to the equilibrium solution, independent of the choice of the initial datum. Secondly, as the polymer relaxation time goes to infinity (or, equivalently, the center-of mass diffusion goes to zero), we show that any family of strong solutions of the system described above, that is parametrized by the relaxation time, converges to an essentially bounded weak solution of a corotational polymer fluid-structure interaction system whose solute evolves according to the convected time derivative of its extra stress tensor. A consequence of this is a weak-strong uniqueness result.

1.
Introduction

As the name suggest, incompressible fluids are those fluids whose volume cannot contract or expand, and thus, are volume preserving. Therefore, to avoid cracks or breakages, it is very important that any flexible receptacle containing such a fluid does not shrink in volume.
Interpreted analytically, the transformation of
a region containing an incompressible fluids
(not the incompressible fluids itself) should be at least volume preserving in order to avoid a degeneracy.
Now consider a well-defined injective mapping
\bm{\Psi}_{\eta}
that maps a reference domain
\Omega
to a current state
\Omega_{\eta}
.
Since the volume of the current state
\Omega_{\eta}
is given by

\displaystyle|\Omega_{\eta}|=\int_{\Omega}|\det(\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\bm{\Psi}_{\eta})|\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x},

we find that
|\Omega_{\eta}|=1
if

\displaystyle|\det(\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\bm{\Psi}_{\eta})|=\frac{1}{|\Omega_{\eta}|}.

This tells us that the transformation
\bm{\Psi}_{\eta}
shrinks or scale down each infinitesimal volume in
\Omega
by a factor
\tfrac{1}{|\Omega_{\eta}|}
in order to achieve the unit volume
|\Omega_{\eta}|=1
. As explained earlier, such a shrinkage is inimical in the context of incompressible fluid-structure interaction. Thus, within the physical context of

\displaystyle|\det(\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\bm{\Psi}_{\eta})|\neq\frac{1}{|\Omega_{\eta}|},

we consider a dilute corotational incompressible polymeric fluids of Oldroyd type. Such a fluid is a solvent-solute mixture whose solvent subcomponent has velocity field
\mathbf{u}:(t,\mathbf{x})\in I\times\Omega_{\eta}\mapsto\mathbf{u}(t,\mathbf{x})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
defined on the spatially flexible spacetime cylinder

\displaystyle I\times\Omega_{\eta}:=\bigcup_{t\in I}\{t\}\times\Omega_{\eta(t)}

and satisfy the incompressibility condition

\displaystyle\mathrm{div}_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{u}=0

in
I\times\Omega_{\eta}
.
The solute subcomponent is a ‘dumbbell’ consisting of two massless monomers connected by a Hookean spring. The elongation vector
\mathbf{q}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
between the position of these two monomers has size
|\mathbf{q}|>0
and the spring potential and Maxwellian are, respectively,

\displaystyle U\Big(\frac{1}{2}|\mathbf{q}|^{2}\Big)=\frac{1}{2}|\mathbf{q}|^{2},\qquad\qquad M=\frac{\exp(-\tfrac{1}{2}|\mathbf{q}|^{2})}{\int_{B}\exp(-\tfrac{1}{2}|\mathbf{q}|^{2})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{q}}.

If
\ell_{0}\ll\mathrm{diam}(\Omega_{\eta})
is the characteristic microscopic length scale of the characteristic dumbbell size, we can introduce the center-of-mass diffusion coefficient
\varepsilon\geq 0
of the dumbbell and the relaxation time
\lambda>0
which characterizes the elastic relaxation property of the fluid. More precisely, these two parameters are given by

(1.1)

\displaystyle\varepsilon=\frac{\ell^{2}_{0}}{8\lambda},\qquad\lambda=\frac{\zeta}{4H}

where
\zeta
is the friction coefficient and
H
is the spring constant. With this, the mesoscopic description of the solute subcomponent is given by the probability density function

f:(t,\mathbf{x},\mathbf{q})\in I\times\Omega_{\eta}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\mapsto f(t,\mathbf{x},\mathbf{q})\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}

which evolves according to the Fokker–Planck equation

(1.2)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f+(\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla_{\mathbf{x}})f+\mathrm{div}_{\mathbf{q}}(\mathbb{W}(\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{u})\mathbf{q}f)=\varepsilon\Delta_{\mathbf{x}}f+\frac{1}{2\lambda}\mathrm{div}_{\mathbf{q}}\left(M\nabla_{\mathbf{q}}\left(\frac{f}{M}\right)\right)

in
I\times\Omega_{\eta}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Here,

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}(\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{u})=\tfrac{1}{2}(\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{u}-(\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{u})^{\top})

is the vorticity tensor of the solvent or the antisymmetric gradient of the solvent’s velocity.
To obtain a macroscopic description for the solute subcomponent from the mesoscopic description above, we consider a tensor-valued average and a scalar average given by the stress tensor
\widehat{\mathbb{T}}:(t,\mathbf{x})\in I\times\Omega_{\eta}\mapsto\widehat{\mathbb{T}}(t,\mathbf{x})\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}
of the solute and the polymer number density
\rho:(t,\mathbf{x})\in I\times\Omega_{\eta}\mapsto\rho(t,\mathbf{x})\in\mathbb{R}
which we assume to be a constant since only two monomers are at play. These averages are precisely given by

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathbb{T}}(t,\mathbf{x})=\int_{B}f(t,\mathbf{x},\mathbf{q})\mathbf{q}\otimes\mathbf{q}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{q},\qquad\rho(t,\mathbf{x})=\int_{B}f(t,\mathbf{x},\mathbf{q})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{q}\equiv\mathrm{constant},

respectively. If we now set

\displaystyle\mathbb{T}=\widehat{\mathbb{T}}-k\rho\mathbb{I}

where
k>0
is the Boltzmann constant, we finally obtain the macroscopic description of the solute subcomponent

(1.3)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mathbb{T}+(\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla_{\mathbf{x}})\mathbb{T}=\mathbb{W}(\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{u})\mathbb{T}+\mathbb{T}\mathbb{W}((\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{u})^{\top})-\frac{1}{2\lambda}\mathbb{T}+\varepsilon\Delta_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbb{T}

in
I\times\Omega_{\eta}
.

Further assumptions on the two parameters
\varepsilon\geq 0
and
\lambda>0
leads to several variations of (
1.3
). One such variation that is strongly contested in the literature is whether the center-mass-of diffusion parameter
\varepsilon
should be zero or strictly positive. Indeed, as explained in
[
3
]
, when
L\approx 1
is a characteristic macroscopic length scale, the authors in
[
6
]
estimate the ratio

\ell_{0}^{2}/L^{2}
to be in the range of about
10^{-9}
to
10^{-7}
. Although not zero, this is a very small number leading one school of thought to justify setting
\varepsilon=0
in (
1.2
) (and thus, also in (
1.3
)). This gives the equation a damped hyperbolic structure and thus, present a relatively difficult equation to analyse mathematically. On the other hand, it is also perfectly justifiable to let
\varepsilon>0
since the aforementioned ratio is not exactly zero. In this case, a parabolic structure is obtained and the presence of this diffusion term regularises the equation.

One can also impose extra assumption on the second parameter
\lambda>0
. For example, if the friction coefficient
\zeta
is several order of magnitude larger than the other parameters, the elastic relaxation parameter will also be very large and as such, one may assume that

\tfrac{1}{2\lambda}\approx 0

leading to a purely elastic solute with infinite memory.

The last two terms in (
1.3
) being zero gives the equation a purely hyperbolic structure with absolutely no form of dissipation. The resulting equation can be traced back to Oldroyd’s seminal paper
[
26
]
in which he argue that to produce a frame-invariant equation, one needed to
evaluate the stress tensor in such a way that it accounts for translation, rotation and even deformation. This lead to the convected time-derivative or what is now referred to as the Oldroyd derivative which conserves all moments just as the usual material (advected) derivative does. In particular, if we let
\mathbb{U}:(t,\mathbf{x})\in I\times\Omega_{\zeta}\mapsto\mathbb{U}(t,\mathbf{x})\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}
be stress tensor
for a dilute corotational elastic bead and spring dumbbells in a solvent with a velocity field
\mathbf{v}:(t,\mathbf{x})\in I\times\Omega_{\zeta}\mapsto\mathbf{v}(t,\mathbf{x})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, then
\mathbb{U}

is governed by

(1.4)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mathbb{U}+(\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla_{\mathbf{x}})\mathbb{U}=\mathbb{W}(\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{v})\mathbb{U}+\mathbb{U}\mathbb{W}((\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{v})^{\top})

in
I\times\Omega_{\zeta}
.
We refer to the review
[
18
]
for more insight on Oldroyd’s work.

One of our goals will be the rigorous justification of the vanishing limit of the last two terms on the right-hand side of (
1.3
) leading to a solution of (
1.4
). To properly motivate this work, however, we begin by rigorously setting up the system.

1.1.
Setup

We consider a spatial reference domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
whose boundary
\partial\Omega
may consist of a flexible part
\omega\subset\mathbb{R}
and a rigid part
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}
. However, because the analysis at the rigid part is significantly simpler, we shall identify the whole of
\partial\Omega
with
\omega
and endow it with periodic boundary conditions. Let
I:=(0,T)
represent a time interval for a given constant
T>0
.
We represent the time-dependent displacement of the structure by
\eta:\overline{I}\times\omega\rightarrow(-L,L)
where
L>0
is a fixed length of the tubular neighbourhood of
\partial\Omega
given by

\displaystyle S_{L}:=\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\,:\,\mathrm{dist}(\mathbf{x},\partial\Omega)<L\}.

Now, for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
large enough, we assume that
\partial\Omega
is parametrized by an injective mapping
\bm{\varphi}\in C^{k}(\omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
with
\partial_{y}\bm{\varphi}\neq 0
such that

\displaystyle\partial{\Omega_{\eta(t)}}=\big\{\bm{\varphi}_{\eta(t)}:=\bm{\varphi}({y})+\mathbf{n}({y})\eta(t,{y})\,:\,t\in I,{y}\in\omega\big\}.

The set
\partial{\Omega_{\eta(t)}}
represents the boundary of the flexible domain at any instant of time
t\in I
and the vector
\mathbf{n}(y)
is a unit normal at the point
y\in\omega
.
We also let
\mathbf{n}_{\eta(t)}(y)
be the corresponding normal of
\partial{\Omega_{\eta(t)}}
at the spacetime point
y\in\omega
and
t\in I
. Then for
L>0
sufficiently small,
\mathbf{n}_{\eta(t)}(y)
is close to
\mathbf{n}(y)
and
\bm{\varphi}_{\eta(t)}
is close to
\bm{\varphi}
. Since
\partial_{y}\bm{\varphi}\neq 0
, it will follow that

\displaystyle\partial_{y}\bm{\varphi}_{\eta(t)}\neq 0\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathbf{n}(y)\cdot\mathbf{n}_{\eta(t)}(y)\neq 0

for
y\in\omega
and
t\in I
. Thus, in particular, there is no loss of strict positivity of the Jacobian determinant provided that
\|\eta\|_{L^{\infty}(I\times\omega)}<L
.

For the interior points, we transform the reference domain
\Omega
into a time-dependent moving domain
\Omega_{\eta(t)}
whose state at time
t\in\overline{I}
is given by

\displaystyle\Omega_{\eta(t)}=\big\{\bm{\Psi}_{\eta(t)}(\mathbf{x}):\,\mathbf{x}\in\Omega\big\}.

Here,

\displaystyle\bm{\Psi}_{\eta(t)}(\mathbf{x})=\begin{cases}\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{n}({y}(\mathbf{x}))\eta(t,{y}(\mathbf{x}))\phi(s(\mathbf{x}))&\quad\text{if }\mathrm{dist}(\mathbf{x},\partial\Omega)<L,\\
\mathbf{x}&\quad\text{elsewhere }\end{cases}

is the Hanzawa transform with inverse
\bm{\Psi}_{-\eta(t)}
and where for a point
\mathbf{x}
in the neighbourhood of
\partial\Omega
, the vector
\mathbf{n}(y(\mathbf{x}))
is the unit normal at the point
y(\mathbf{x})=\mathrm{argmin}_{y\in\omega}|\mathbf{x}-\bm{\varphi}(y)|
. Also,
s(\mathbf{x})=(\mathbf{x}-\bm{\varphi}(y(\mathbf{x})))\cdot\mathbf{n}(y(\mathbf{x}))
and
\phi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
is a cut-off function that is
\phi\equiv 0
in the neighbourhood of
-L
and
\phi\equiv 1
in the neighbourhood of
0
. Note that
\bm{\Psi}_{\eta(t)}(\mathbf{x})
can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\bm{\Psi}_{\eta(t)}(\mathbf{x})=\begin{cases}\bm{\varphi}(y(\mathbf{x}))+\mathbf{n}({y}(\mathbf{x}))[s(\mathbf{x})+\eta(t,{y}(\mathbf{x}))\phi(s(\mathbf{x}))]&\quad\text{if }\mathrm{dist}(\mathbf{x},\partial\Omega)<L,\\
\mathbf{x}&\quad\text{elsewhere. }\end{cases}

With the above preparation in hand, we consider the corotational Oldroyd’s model for the flow of a dilute polymeric fluid interacting with a flexible structure.

For simplicity we set the length scale in (
1.1
) to
\ell_{0}=2
so that

\displaystyle\varepsilon=\frac{1}{2\lambda}.

In so doing,
\lambda\rightarrow\infty
if and only if
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
. We can thus perform a simultaneous limit of the diffusion and damping terms with an equivalent choice of either a large relaxation time limit or a vanishing center-of-mass limit.
This simplification does not affect our work whatsoever and it fact, the same results holds true for any constant length scale
\ell_{0}>0
.

The unknown of our problem consists of a structure displacement function
\eta:(t,{y})\in I\times\omega\mapsto\eta(t,{y})\in\mathbb{R}
, a fluid velocity field
\mathbf{u}:(t,\mathbf{x})\in I\times\Omega_{\eta}\mapsto\mathbf{u}(t,\mathbf{x})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, a pressure function
p:(t,\mathbf{x})\in I\times\Omega_{\eta}\mapsto p(t,\mathbf{x})\in\mathbb{R}
and an extra stress tensor
\mathbb{T}:(t,\mathbf{x})\in I\times\Omega_{\eta}\mapsto\mathbb{T}(t,\mathbf{x})\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}

such that the system of equations

(1.5)

\displaystyle\mathrm{div}_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{u}=0,

(1.6)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mathbf{u}+(\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla_{\mathbf{x}})\mathbf{u}=\nu\Delta_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{u}-\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}p+\mathrm{div}_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbb{T},

(1.7)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{2}\eta-\gamma\partial_{t}\partial_{y}^{2}\eta+\partial_{y}^{4}\eta=-(\mathbb{S}\mathbf{n}_{\eta})\circ\bm{\varphi}_{\eta}\cdot\mathbf{n}\,\det(\partial_{y}\bm{\varphi}_{\eta}),

(1.8)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mathbb{T}+(\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla_{\mathbf{x}})\mathbb{T}=\mathbb{W}(\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{u})\mathbb{T}+\mathbb{T}\mathbb{W}((\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{u})^{\top})-\varepsilon(1-\Delta_{\mathbf{x}})\mathbb{T}

holds on
I\times\Omega_{\eta}\subset\mathbb{R}^{1+2}
(with (
1.7
) posed on
I\times\omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{1+1}
) where

\displaystyle\mathbb{S}=\nu(\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{u}+(\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{u})^{\top})-p\mathbb{I}+\mathbb{T},\qquad\mathbb{W}(\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{u})=\tfrac{1}{2}(\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{u}-(\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{u})^{\top}).

The parameters
\nu
,
\gamma
and
\varepsilon
are positive constants,
\mathbf{n}_{\eta}
is the normal at
\partial\Omega_{\eta}
and
\mathbb{I}
is the identity matrix.
We complement (
1.5
)–(
1.8
) with the following initial and boundary conditions

(1.9)

\displaystyle\eta(0,\cdot)=\eta_{0}(\cdot),\qquad\partial_{t}\eta(0,\cdot)=\eta_{\star}(\cdot)

\displaystyle\text{in }\omega,

(1.10)

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}(0,\cdot)=\mathbf{u}_{0}(\cdot)

\displaystyle\text{in }\Omega_{\eta_{0}},

(1.11)

\displaystyle\mathbb{T}(0,\cdot)=\mathbb{T}_{0}(\cdot)

\displaystyle\text{in }\Omega_{\eta_{0}},

(1.12)

\displaystyle\mathbf{n}_{\eta}\cdot\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbb{T}=0

\displaystyle\text{on }I\times\partial\Omega_{\eta}.

Furthermore, we impose periodicity on the boundary of
\omega
(with mean-zero elements in
\omega
) and the following interface condition

(1.13)

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}\circ\bm{\varphi}_{\eta}=(\partial_{t}\eta)\mathbf{n}

\displaystyle\text{on }I\times\omega

at the flexible part of the boundary with normal
\mathbf{n}
.

The analysis of polymeric fluid-structure interaction problems was initiated recently in

]

When the

Note that for fixed spatial domains subject to periodic boundary conditions, one can construct solutions that are spatially more regular

Concepts of solution and main results

Definition 1.1

(Strong solution)

Let
(\mathbb{T}_{0},\mathbf{u}_{0},\eta_{0},\eta_{\star})

be a dataset that satisfies

(1.14)

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\eta_{0}\in W^{3,2}(\omega)\text{ with }\|\eta_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\omega)}<L,\quad\eta_{\star}\in W^{1,2}(\omega),

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}_{0}\in W^{1,2}_{\mathrm{div}_{\mathbf{x}}}(\Omega_{\eta_{0}})\text{ is such that }\mathbf{u}_{0}\circ\bm{\varphi}_{\eta_{0}}=\eta_{\star}\mathbf{n}\text{ on }\omega,

\displaystyle\mathbb{T}_{0}\in W^{1,2}(\Omega_{\eta_{0}})

We call

of (
1.5
)–(
1.13
) with dataset

\displaystyle\eta\in

\displaystyle W^{1,\infty}\big(I;W^{1,2}(\omega)\big)\cap L^{\infty}\big(I;W^{3,2}(\omega)\big)\cap W^{1,2}\big(I;W^{2,2}(\omega)\big)

the velocity

\mathbf{u}\circ\bm{\varphi}_{\eta}=(\partial_{t}\eta)\mathbf{n}

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}\in W^{1,2}\big(I;L^{2}_{\mathrm{div}_{\mathbf{x}}}(\Omega_{\eta})\big)\cap L^{2}\big(I;W^{2,2}(\Omega_{\eta})\big);

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
|\Omega_{\eta}|\neq 1
and let
(\eta,\mathbf{u},\mathbb{T})

be a strong solution of (

). Then the equilibration rates (until possible degeneracy)

\displaystyle\|\mathbb{T}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta})}\leq e^{-\varepsilon t}\|\mathbb{T}_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta_{0}})},

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}\eta(t)\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}\leq e^{-c_{1}\gamma t}\sqrt{\|\eta_{\star}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}+\frac{1}{2\nu\varepsilon}(1-e^{-2\varepsilon t})\|\mathbb{T}_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta_{0}})}^{2}},

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{u}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta})}\leq e^{-\tfrac{c_{2}}{2}\nu\left(1-\left(\inf_{t\in I}|\Omega_{\eta}|\right)^{-1/2}\right)^{2}t}\sqrt{\|\mathbf{u}_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta_{0}})}^{2}+\frac{1}{2\nu\varepsilon}(1-e^{-2\varepsilon t})\|\mathbb{T}_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta_{0}})}^{2}}

Remark 1.4

It will become clear in the proof of Theorem

A similar system was analysed in
[

with an extra equation for the evolution of the polymer number density

Moreover, from the purely analytical point of view, the equation for

(or just

when the polymer number density is a constant) present in the latter. This term is a hyperbolic dissipation term that regularises the equation for the solute (without needing a more regular initial condition) from the mathematical point of view. From the physical point of view, this captures the system with no localised damping in the solute.

Furthermore, the purely hyperbolic equation (

Finally, we also note that, whereas
[

For two non-negative quantities

both hold, we use the notation

. The anti-symmetric gradient of a vector

\mathbb{W}(\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{f})=\tfrac{1}{2}(\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{f}-(\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{f})^{\top})

The symbol

f

\mathbf{f}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

is the
d

S
.
Since we only consider functions on

with periodic boundary

\displaystyle\|\cdot\|_{W^{1,2}(\omega)}\sim\|\partial_{y}\cdot\|_{L^{2}(\omega)},\qquad\|\cdot\|_{W^{2,2}(\omega)}\sim\|\partial_{y}^{2}\cdot\|_{L^{2}(\omega)},\qquad\|\cdot\|_{W^{4,2}(\omega)}\sim\|\partial_{y}^{4}\cdot\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}.

For

, and

\displaystyle L^{p}(I;L^{r}(\Omega_{\eta}))

\displaystyle:=\Big\{v\in L^{1}(I\times\Omega_{\eta}):\begin{subarray}{c}v(t,\cdot)\in L^{r}(\Omega_{\eta(t)})\,\,\text{for a.e. }t,\\
\|v(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{r}(\Omega_{\eta(t)})}\in L^{p}(I)\end{subarray}\Big\},

\displaystyle L^{p}(I;W^{1,r}(\Omega_{\eta}))

\displaystyle:=\big\{v\in L^{p}(I;L^{r}(\Omega_{\eta})):\,\,\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}v\in L^{p}(I;L^{r}(\Omega_{\eta}))\big\}.

Higher-order Sobolev spaces can be defined accordingly. For

with

\displaystyle\|v\|_{L^{p}(I;W^{k,r}(\Omega_{\eta}))}^{p}

Consequently, applying Grönwall’s lemma yield

(3.1)

\displaystyle\|\mathbb{T}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}\leq e^{-2\varepsilon t}\|\mathbb{T}_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta_{0}})}^{2}.

Taking the square-root yields the first estimate in Theorem
1.2
.

Next we test the solvent-structure subsystem (
1.6
)-(
1.7
) with
(\mathbf{u},\partial_{t}\eta)
. Due to the interface condition (
1.13
), we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\big(\|\partial_{t}\eta\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}+\|\partial_{y}^{2}\eta\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}+\|\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2})+\big(\gamma\|\partial_{t}\partial_{y}\eta\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}+\nu\|\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}\big)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\nu}{2}\|\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}+\frac{1}{2\nu}\|\mathbb{T}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}.

If we now absorb the viscous term on the right-hand side into the corresponding term on the left, then in particular,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\|\partial_{t}\eta\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}+\gamma\|\partial_{t}\partial_{y}\eta\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2\nu}\|\mathbb{T}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2},

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\|\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}+\frac{\nu}{2}\|\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2\nu}\|\mathbb{T}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}.

From the first estimate above, we use the classical Poincaré’s inequality and (
3.1
) to obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\|\partial_{t}\eta\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}+2c\gamma\|\partial_{t}\eta\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{\nu}\|\mathbb{T}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}\leq\frac{e^{-2\varepsilon t}}{\nu}\|\mathbb{T}_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta_{0}})}^{2}

which when integrated in time gives

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}\eta(t)\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}\leq\|\eta_{\star}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}+\frac{1}{2\nu\varepsilon}(1-e^{-2\varepsilon t})\|\mathbb{T}_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta_{0}})}^{2}-\int_{0}^{t}2c\gamma\|\partial_{s}\eta\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s

and thus by Grönwall’s lemma,

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}\eta(t)\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}\leq e^{-2c\gamma t}\left[\|\eta_{\star}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}+\frac{1}{2\nu\varepsilon}(1-e^{-2\varepsilon t})\|\mathbb{T}_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta_{0}})}^{2}\right].

Taking the square-root yields the second estimate in Theorem
1.2
. We note in passing that
(1-e^{-2\varepsilon t})\leq 1
.
Let now return to

\displaystyle\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\|\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}+\nu\|\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{\nu}\|\mathbb{T}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}.

By using the reverse triangle inequality and the Poincaré–Wirtinger inequality,

\displaystyle\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\|\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}+c\nu\big(\|\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta})}-\|\mathbf{u}_{\Omega_{\eta}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta})}\big)^{2}\leq\frac{e^{-2\varepsilon t}}{\nu}\|\mathbb{T}_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta_{0}})}^{2}.

However, by Jensen’s inequality,

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{u}_{\Omega_{\eta}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta})}\leq\|\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta})}|\Omega_{\eta}|^{-1/2}\leq\|\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta})}\big(\inf_{t\in I}|\Omega_{\eta}|\big)^{-1/2}

where

\displaystyle|\Omega_{\eta}|=\int_{\Omega}|\det(\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\bm{\Psi}_{\eta})|\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}

is the volume of
\Omega_{\eta}
. Thus,

\displaystyle\Big(1-\big(\inf_{t\in I}|\Omega_{\eta}|\big)^{-1/2}\Big)\|\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta})}\leq\|\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta})}-\|\mathbf{u}_{\Omega_{\eta}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta})}

and we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\|\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}+c\nu\Big(1-\big(\inf_{t\in I}|\Omega_{\eta}|\big)^{-1/2}\Big)^{2}\|\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}\leq\frac{e^{-2\varepsilon t}}{\nu}\|\mathbb{T}_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta_{0}})}^{2}.

Consequently, by Grönwall’s lemma,

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{u}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta})}^{2}\leq e^{-c\nu\left(1-\left(\inf_{t\in I}|\Omega_{\eta}|\right)^{-1/2}\right)^{2}t}\left[\|\mathbf{u}_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta_{0}})}^{2}+\frac{1}{2\nu\varepsilon}(1-e^{-2\varepsilon t})\|\mathbb{T}_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta_{0}})}^{2}\right]

from which the desired result immediately follow.

4.
Vanishing limit

We devote this section to the proof of our second main result, Theorem

Our proof relies on a relative energy method where one measures the distance between two solutions in the energy norm. \displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\|\overline{\mathbb{T}}-\mathbb{U}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}\|\varepsilon\overline{\mathbb{T}}\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}\|\eta-\zeta\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\omega)}\,\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\|\overline{\mathbb{T}}-\mathbb{U}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\zeta})}\|\varepsilon\overline{\mathbb{T}}\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}\|\eta-\zeta\|_{W^{2,2}(\omega)}\,\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\delta_{5}\sup_{t^{\prime}\in(0,t]}\|(\overline{\mathbb{T}}-\mathbb{U})(t^{\prime})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}+c(\delta_{5})\varepsilon^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\|\eta-\zeta\|_{W^{2,2}(\omega)}^{2}\|\overline{\mathbb{T}}\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}

\displaystyle+\delta_{5}\sup_{t^{\prime}\in(0,t]}\|(\eta-\zeta)(t^{\prime})\|_{W^{2,2}(\omega)}^{2}+c(\delta_{5})\varepsilon^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\big(\|\overline{\mathbb{T}}\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}+\|\mathbb{U}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}

hold for any
\delta_{5}>0
and for any
t\in I
. We also observe that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}K_{6}\,\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}\|\eta-\zeta\|_{W^{1,2}(\omega)}\|\varepsilon(\overline{\mathbb{T}}-\mathbb{U})\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\zeta})}\|\overline{\mathbb{T}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}\,\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\big(\|\overline{\mathbb{T}}\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}+\|\mathbb{U}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}+\int_{0}^{t}\|\eta-\zeta\|_{W^{1,2}(\omega)}^{2}\|\overline{\mathbb{T}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}.

For
K_{7}
, we use the definition of
\mathbb{H}_{\eta-\zeta}(\overline{\mathbf{u}},\overline{\mathbb{T}})
given after (
4.6
) to expand it as

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}K_{7}\,\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega_{\zeta}}(1-J_{\eta-\zeta})\partial_{t^{\prime}}\overline{\mathbb{T}}:(\overline{\mathbb{T}}-\mathbb{U})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\,\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega_{\zeta}}J_{\eta-\zeta}\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\overline{\mathbb{T}}\cdot\partial_{t^{\prime}}\bm{\Psi}_{\eta-\zeta}^{-1}\circ\bm{\Psi}_{\eta-\zeta}:(\overline{\mathbb{T}}-\mathbb{U})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\,\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega_{\zeta}}\overline{\mathbf{u}}\cdot\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\overline{\mathbb{T}}(\mathbb{I}-\mathbb{B}_{\eta-\zeta}):(\overline{\mathbb{T}}-\mathbb{U})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\,\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega_{\zeta}}\mathbb{W}(\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\overline{\mathbf{u}})(\mathbb{B}_{\eta-\zeta}-\mathbb{I})\overline{\mathbb{T}}:(\overline{\mathbb{T}}-\mathbb{U})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\,\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega_{\zeta}}\overline{\mathbb{T}}(\mathbb{B}_{\eta-\zeta}-\mathbb{I})^{\top}\mathbb{W}((\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\overline{\mathbf{u}})^{\top}):(\overline{\mathbb{T}}-\mathbb{U})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\,\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}

\displaystyle=:\int_{0}^{t}(K_{7}^{a}+K_{7}^{b}+K_{7}^{c}+K_{7}^{d}+K_{7}^{e})\,\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}

where

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}K_{7}^{a}\,\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}\|\overline{\mathbb{T}}-\mathbb{U}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}\|\eta-\zeta\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\omega)}\|\partial_{t^{\prime}}\overline{\mathbb{T}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}\,\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq\delta_{7}^{a}\sup_{t^{\prime}\in(0,t]}\|(\overline{\mathbb{T}}-\mathbb{U})(t^{\prime})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}+c(\delta_{7}^{a})\int_{0}^{t}\|\eta-\zeta\|_{W^{2,2}(\omega)}^{2}\|\partial_{t^{\prime}}\overline{\mathbb{T}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}

hold for any
\delta_{7}^{a}>0
and for any
t\in I
.
On the other hand, since

\displaystyle\sup_{t^{\prime}\in(0,t]}\|(\overline{\mathbb{T}}-\mathbb{U})(t^{\prime})\|_{L^{6}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}\lesssim\sup_{t^{\prime}\in(0,t]}\big(\|\overline{\mathbb{T}}(t^{\prime})\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}+\|\mathbb{U}(t^{\prime})\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}\big)

is finite, it follows that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}K_{7}^{b}\,\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}\|\overline{\mathbb{T}}-\mathbb{U}\|_{L^{6}(\Omega_{\zeta})}\|\eta-\zeta\|_{W^{1,6}(\omega)}\|\overline{\mathbb{T}}\|_{W^{1,6}(\Omega_{\zeta})}\|\partial_{t^{\prime}}(\eta-\zeta)\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}\,\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq\delta_{7}^{b}\sup_{t^{\prime}\in(0,t]}\|\partial_{t^{\prime}}(\eta-\zeta)(t^{\prime})\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}+c(\delta_{7}^{b})\int_{0}^{t}\|\eta-\zeta\|_{W^{2,2}(\omega)}^{2}\|\overline{\mathbb{T}}\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}

and by using the fact that
\|\overline{\mathbf{u}}(t)\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}
is essentially bounded in time, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}K_{7}^{c}\,\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}\|\overline{\mathbb{T}}-\mathbb{U}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}\|\overline{\mathbf{u}}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega_{\zeta})}\|\overline{\mathbb{T}}\|_{W^{1,4}(\Omega_{\zeta})}\|\eta-\zeta\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\omega)}\,\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq\delta_{7}^{c}\sup_{t^{\prime}\in(0,t]}\|(\overline{\mathbb{T}}-\mathbb{U})(t^{\prime})\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}+c(\delta_{7}^{c})\int_{0}^{t}\|\eta-\zeta\|_{W^{2,2}(\omega)}^{2}\|\overline{\mathbb{T}}\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}.

Moreover,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}(K_{7}^{d}+K_{7}^{e})\,\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}\leq

\displaystyle 2\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega_{\zeta}}|\overline{\mathbb{T}}|\,|\mathbb{B}_{\eta-\zeta}-\mathbb{I}|\,|\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\overline{\mathbf{u}}|\,|\overline{\mathbb{T}}-\mathbb{U}|\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\,\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}\|\overline{\mathbb{T}}\|_{L^{6}(\Omega_{\zeta})}\|\eta-\zeta\|_{W^{1,6}(\omega)}\|\overline{\mathbf{u}}\|_{W^{1,6}(\Omega_{\zeta})}\|\overline{\mathbb{T}}-\mathbb{U}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}\,\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq\delta_{7}^{d}\sup_{t^{\prime}\in(0,t]}\|(\overline{\mathbb{T}}-\mathbb{U})(t^{\prime})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}+c(\delta_{7}^{d})\int_{0}^{t}\|\eta-\zeta\|_{W^{2,2}(\omega)}^{2}\|\overline{\mathbf{u}}\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}

since
\|\overline{\mathbb{T}}\|_{L^{6}(\Omega_{\zeta})}
is essentially bounded in time.

If we now collect all the estimates for the
K_{i}
’s, then for any
\delta\leq\min\{\delta_{1},\delta_{2},\ldots,\delta_{7}^{c},\delta_{7}^{d}\}
, we obtain from (
4.9
) that

(4.11)

\displaystyle\|(\overline{\mathbb{T}}-\mathbb{U})(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}\leq

\displaystyle\|\overline{\mathbb{T}}_{0}-\mathbb{U}_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\zeta_{0}})}^{2}+\delta\sup_{t^{\prime}\in(0,t]}\|(\overline{\mathbb{T}}-\mathbb{U})(t^{\prime})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}

\displaystyle c(\delta)\int_{0}^{t}\|\overline{\mathbb{T}}-\mathbb{U}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}\|\mathbb{U}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}+\delta\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}(\overline{\mathbf{u}}-\mathbf{v})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}

\displaystyle+\delta\sup_{t^{\prime}\in(0,t]}\|\partial_{t^{\prime}}(\eta-\zeta)(t^{\prime})\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}+\delta\sup_{t^{\prime}\in(0,t]}\|(\eta-\zeta)(t^{\prime})\|_{W^{2,2}(\omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle+c(\delta)\int_{0}^{t}\big(\|\overline{\mathbf{u}}-\mathbf{v}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}+\|\eta-\zeta\|_{W^{2,2}(\omega)}^{2}\big)\|\overline{\mathbb{T}}\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}

\displaystyle+c(\delta)\int_{0}^{t}\|\eta-\zeta\|_{W^{2,2}(\omega)}^{2}\big(\|\partial_{t^{\prime}}\overline{\mathbb{T}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}+\|\overline{\mathbf{u}}\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}

\displaystyle+\varepsilon^{2}c(\delta)\int_{0}^{t}\big(\|\overline{\mathbb{T}}\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}+\|\mathbb{U}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}

holds for any
\delta>0
and for any
t\in I
.

4.3.
Full estimate

In order to get our desired estimate (
4.2
), we sum up the estimates (
4.7
) and (
4.11
) to obtain

(4.12)

\displaystyle\big(

\displaystyle\|(\overline{\mathbf{u}}-\mathbf{v})(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}+\|\partial_{t}(\eta-\zeta)(t)\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}+\|\partial_{y}^{2}(\eta-\zeta)(t)\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}+\|(\overline{\mathbb{T}}-\mathbb{U})(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}\big)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}(\overline{\mathbf{u}}-\mathbf{v})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}+\gamma\int_{0}^{t}\|\partial_{t^{\prime}}\partial_{y}(\eta-\zeta)\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\overline{\mathbf{u}}_{0}-\mathbf{v}_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}+\|\eta_{\star}-\zeta_{\star}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}+\|\partial_{y}^{2}(\eta_{0}-\zeta_{0})\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}+\|\overline{\mathbb{T}}_{0}-\mathbb{U}_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\zeta_{0}})}^{2}

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\|\overline{\mathbf{u}}-\mathbf{v}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}\big(1+\|\overline{\mathbb{T}}\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\|\partial_{t^{\prime}}(\eta-\zeta)\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}\|\overline{\mathbf{u}}\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\|\eta-\zeta\|_{W^{2,2}(\omega)}^{2}\big(c(\gamma)+\|\partial_{t^{\prime}}\overline{\mathbf{u}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}+\|\overline{\mathbf{u}}\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\|\eta-\zeta\|_{W^{2,2}(\omega)}^{2}\big(\|\overline{p}\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}+\|\partial_{t^{\prime}}\overline{\mathbb{T}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}+\|\overline{\mathbb{T}}\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\|\overline{\mathbb{T}}-\mathbb{U}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}\big(1+\|\mathbb{U}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}

\displaystyle+\varepsilon^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\big(\|\overline{\mathbb{T}}\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}+\|\mathbb{U}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\zeta})}^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}

Our desired estimate now follows from Grönwall’s lemma.

∎
Having proved Proposition
2.1
, we now set
(\eta,\mathbf{u},p,\mathbb{T})=(\eta^{\varepsilon},\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon},p^{\varepsilon},\mathbb{T}^{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon>0}
in (
1.5
)-(
1.8
).
We note that if the corresponding dataset
(\mathbb{T}_{0},\mathbf{u}_{0},\eta_{0},\eta_{\star})
satisfies (
1.28
), then the transformed dataset
(\overline{\mathbb{T}}^{\varepsilon}_{0},\overline{\mathbf{u}}^{\varepsilon}_{0},\eta^{\varepsilon}_{0},\eta^{\varepsilon}_{\star})
satisfies

\displaystyle\eta^{\varepsilon}_{0}\rightarrow\zeta_{0}\quad\text{in}\quad W^{2,2}(\omega),

\displaystyle\eta^{\varepsilon}_{\star}\rightarrow\zeta_{\star}\quad\text{in}\quad L^{2}(\omega),

\displaystyle\overline{\mathbf{u}}^{\varepsilon}_{0}\rightarrow\mathbf{v}_{0}\quad\text{in}\quad L^{2}(\Omega_{\zeta(0)}),

\displaystyle\overline{\mathbb{T}}^{\varepsilon}_{0}\rightarrow\mathbb{U}_{0}\quad\text{in}\quad L^{2}(\Omega_{\zeta(0)})

as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
.
Thus, an immediate corollary of Proposition
2.1
is the following result.

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
(\eta^{\varepsilon},\overline{\mathbf{u}}^{\varepsilon},\overline{p}^{\varepsilon},\overline{\mathbb{T}}^{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon>0}
be a collection of strong solutions of (
1.5
)-(
1.13
) with dataset
(\overline{\mathbb{T}}^{\varepsilon}_{0},\overline{\mathbf{u}}^{\varepsilon}_{0},\eta^{\varepsilon}_{0},\eta^{\varepsilon}_{\star})
for which (
1.28
) holds with
\mathbb{U}_{0}
satisfying (
1.27
). Then we have

\displaystyle\eta^{\varepsilon}\rightarrow\zeta\quad\text{in}\quad L^{\infty}(I;W^{2,2}(\omega)),

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\eta^{\varepsilon}\rightarrow\partial_{t}\zeta\quad\text{in}\quad L^{\infty}(I;L^{2}(\omega)),

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\eta^{\varepsilon}\rightarrow\partial_{t}\zeta\quad\text{in}\quad L^{2}(I;W^{1,2}(\omega)),

\displaystyle\overline{\mathbf{u}}^{\varepsilon}\rightarrow\mathbf{v}\quad\text{in}\quad L^{\infty}(I;L^{2}(\Omega_{\zeta})),

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\overline{\mathbf{u}}^{\varepsilon}\rightarrow\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{v}\quad\text{in}\quad L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega_{\zeta})),

\displaystyle\overline{\mathbb{T}}^{\varepsilon}\rightarrow\mathbb{U}\quad\text{in}\quad L^{\infty}(I;L^{2}(\Omega_{\zeta}))

where
(\zeta,\mathbf{v},\mathbb{U})
is an essentially bounded weak solution of (
1.15
)-(
1.22
) with dataset
(\mathbb{U}_{0},\mathbf{v}_{0},\zeta_{0},\zeta_{\star})
.

Now we note that since

(4.13)

\displaystyle\|\bm{1}_{\Omega_{\eta^{\varepsilon}}}\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}-\bm{1}_{\Omega_{\zeta}}\mathbf{v}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\cup S_{\ell})}^{2}\lesssim

\displaystyle\|(\bm{1}_{\Omega_{\eta^{\varepsilon}}}-\bm{1}_{\Omega_{\zeta}})\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\cup S_{\ell})}^{2}+\|\bm{1}_{\Omega_{\zeta}}(\overline{\mathbf{u}}^{\varepsilon}-\mathbf{v})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\cup S_{\ell})}^{2}

\displaystyle+\|\bm{1}_{\Omega_{\zeta}}(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}-\overline{\mathbf{u}}^{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\cup S_{\ell})}^{2},

the first two terms to the right converge to zero as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
due to Corollary
4.2
.
Also, since
\overline{\mathbf{u}}^{\varepsilon}=\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}\circ\bm{\Psi}_{\eta^{\varepsilon}-\zeta}=\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}(t,\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{n}(\eta^{\varepsilon}-\zeta)\phi)
, the last term to the right in (
4.13
) also converges to zero as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
due to the strong uniform convergence of
\eta^{\varepsilon}
to
\zeta
. Thus, it follows that

\displaystyle\bm{1}_{\Omega_{\eta^{\varepsilon}}}\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}\rightarrow\bm{1}_{\Omega_{\zeta}}\mathbf{v}\quad\text{in}\quad L^{\infty}(I;L^{2}(\Omega\cup S_{\ell})).

Similarly, we obtain strong convergence of
\bm{1}_{\Omega_{\eta^{\varepsilon}}}(\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon},\mathbb{T}^{\varepsilon})
to their respective limits as stated in Theorem
1.8
thereby finishing the proof.

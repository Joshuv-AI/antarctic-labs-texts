The principle motivations for the study of fixers in permutation groups come from the relations of fixers with dearrangements and intersecting subsets defined below.

One motivation is the study of EKR-type problems for permutation groups, which study intersecting subsets.
A subset
S\subseteq G

if for any two elements

there exists some
\omega\in\Omega
such that
\omega^{x}=\omega^{y}.

It is not difficult to see

The group

|S|\leqslant|G_{\omega}|,

EKR-type problems have been studied for a long time; refer to

4.
Some property of finite field

In this section, we record some properties of finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}.

In particular, we study a family of additive subgroups of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and their intersection.
We also state some more properties of trace maps of finite fields.

Let
A
be the set
\{\alpha-\alpha^{3\theta}\mid\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\},
then
A
is the kernel of the trace map from
\mathbb{F}_{q}
to
\mathbb{F}_{3}
by additive form of Hilbert 90.
Let
tA=\{ta\mid a\in A\},
then
tA
is an
\mathbb{F}_{3}
-subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{q}.

Assume
T
is a subset of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}.

We study the intersection
\bigcap\limits_{t\in T}tA.

This will be useful when we study a subgroup of
Q
that intersects non-trivially with a unique
G
-class of elements of order
9
in Section
6
.

We first make an observation in the following lemma.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
T^{-1}=\{t^{-1}\mid t\in T\},
and let
\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{F}_{3}}(T^{-1})
be the
\mathbb{F}_{3}
-subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
generated by all elements in
T^{-1}.

Then

(2)

\bigcap_{t\in T}tA=\bigcap_{t\in\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{F}_{3}}(T^{-1})}t^{-1}A.

Proof.

It is clear that
\bigcap\limits_{t\in\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{F}_{3}}(T^{-1})}t^{-1}A\subseteq\bigcap\limits_{t\in T}tA,
and hence it suffices to show
\bigcap\limits_{t\in T}tA\subseteq\bigcap\limits_{t\in\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{F}_{3}}(T^{-1})}t^{-1}A.

We prove it by induction on
|T|.

If
T
has a unique element, then (
2
) holds immediately.

Assume
T=\{t,t_{1}\}.
If
t=-t_{1}
, then (
2
) holds immediately.
We thus assume
t_{1}\notin\{\pm t\}.

Then

(3)

\displaystyle tA\cap t_{1}A

\displaystyle=\{t(\alpha-\alpha^{3\theta})\mid\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\}\cap t_{1}A

\displaystyle=\{t_{1}(\frac{t}{t_{1}}\alpha-\frac{t}{t_{1}}\alpha^{3\theta})\mid\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\}\cap t_{1}A

\displaystyle=\{t_{1}(\frac{t}{t_{1}}\alpha-\frac{t^{3\theta}}{t_{1}^{3\theta}}\alpha^{3\theta}+\alpha^{3\theta}(\frac{t^{3\theta}}{t_{1}^{3\theta}}-\frac{t}{t_{1}}))\mid\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\}\cap t_{1}A

\displaystyle=\{t(\alpha-\alpha^{3\theta})\mid\alpha^{3\theta}\in\frac{1}{(\frac{t}{t_{1}})^{3\theta}-\frac{t}{t_{1}}}A\},

the last equality holds by noting that
t_{1}(\frac{t}{t_{1}}\alpha-\frac{t^{3\theta}}{t_{1}^{3\theta}}\alpha^{3\theta})\in t_{1}A.

On the other hand, by (
3
), for any
a,b\in\mathbb{F}_{3}^{\times}=\{\pm 1\}
we have

\displaystyle tA\cap\frac{1}{\frac{a}{t_{1}}+\frac{b}{t}}A

\displaystyle=\{t(\alpha-\alpha^{3\theta})\mid\alpha^{3\theta}\in\frac{1}{t^{3\theta}(\frac{a}{t_{1}}+\frac{b}{t})^{3\theta}-t(\frac{a}{t_{1}}+\frac{b}{t})}A\}

\displaystyle=\{t(\alpha-\alpha^{3\theta})\mid\alpha^{3\theta}\in\frac{1}{(\frac{t}{t_{1}})^{3\theta}-\frac{t}{t_{1}}}A\}=tA\cap t_{1}A.

Therefore,
tA\cap t_{1}A\subseteq\frac{1}{\frac{a}{t_{1}}+\frac{b}{t}}A
, and (
2
) holds.

Suppose (
2
) holds when
|T|\leqslant k-1,
where
k\geqslant 3,
we next prove it for
|T|=k.

We thus assume
T=\{t_{1},t_{2},\dots,t_{k}\}.

Let
\lambda
be an element in
\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{F}_{3}}(T^{-1}),
we need to show
\bigcap\limits_{t\in T}tA\subseteq\frac{1}{\lambda}A.

Indeed, if
\lambda\in\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{F}_{3}}\{t_{1}^{-1},t_{2}^{-1},\dots,t_{k-1}^{-1}\},
then

\bigcap_{t\in T}tA\subseteq\bigcap_{i=1}^{k-1}t_{i}A\subseteq\frac{1}{\lambda}A

by induction.
We thus may assume
\lambda\notin\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{F}_{3}}\{t_{1}^{-1},t_{2}^{-1},\dots,t_{k-1}^{-1}\},
then there exists
1\leqslant i_{1},i_{2},\dots,i_{j}\leqslant k-1
and
a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{j+1}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{3}^{\times},
such that
\lambda=a_{1}t_{i_{1}}^{-1}+\dots+a_{j}t_{i_{j}}^{-1}+a_{j+1}t_{k}^{-1}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\bigcap_{t\in T}tA

\displaystyle\subseteq(\bigcap_{i=1}^{j}t_{i_{j}}A)\cap t_{k}A

\displaystyle\subseteq\frac{1}{a_{1}t_{i_{1}}^{-1}+\dots+a_{j}t_{i_{j}}^{-1}}A\cap t_{k}A\subseteq\frac{1}{\lambda}A,

by induction.
Therefore,
\bigcap\limits_{t\in T}tA\subseteq\bigcap\limits_{t\in\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{F}_{3}}(T^{-1})}t^{-1}A
since
\lambda
is taken arbitrarily in
\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{F}_{3}}(T^{-1}).

This completes the proof.
∎

Recall that
q=3^{2n+1}
and
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is a vector space over
\mathbb{F}_{3}
of dimension

and
\bigcap\limits_{t\in T}tA

-subspace of

Lemma 4.2
.

(4)

\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{F}_{3}}(\bigcap_{t\in T}tA)+\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{F}_{3}}(\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{F}_{3}}(T^{-1}))=2n+1

Proof.

We prove (
4
) by induction on the dimension of
\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{F}_{3}}(T^{-1})
over
\mathbb{F}_{3}.

If
\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{F}_{3}}(T^{-1})

T
is contained in a one-dimensional

We now assume (

\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{F}_{3}}(\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{F}_{3}}(T^{-1}))=k.

for a suitable

Lemma 4.3

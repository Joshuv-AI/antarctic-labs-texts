Graphs of Quasicircles and Quasiconformal Homeomorphisms
Katherine Williams Booth, Alexander Nolte, and Yvon Verberne

Abstract
. We give a combinatorial characterization of the group of quasiconformal homeomorphisms of a closed, oriented surface S S of genus at least 2 2 . In particular, we prove they are exactly the automorphisms of a graph of essential quasicircles on S S that respect a canonical coarse ordering induced by quality constants. We also discuss the coarse geometry of this graph.



Bowden–Hensel–Webb recently proved
[
14
]
that for closed, oriented surfaces
S
of genus
g\geq 1
, the identity component
\text{Diff}_{0}(S)
of the diffeomorphism group of
S
, while simple
[
32
]
, is not uniformly perfect. This means that there is no uniform
N
so that any diffeomorphism
h\in\text{Diff}_{0}(S)
may be written as a product of
N
commutators. This came as a surprise, as the analogous statement is false for spheres and for closed manifolds of any dimension other than
2
or
4

[
15
,
33
,
34
]
.

The proof of Bowden–Hensel–Webb introduced and centered on an analysis of the
fine curve graph

\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(S)
of
S
, whose vertices are topologically embedded essential curves in
S
with edges specified by disjointness. The fine curve graph differs from more-studied curve graphs (e.g.
[
3
,
5
,
6
,
20
,
26
,
27
]
) in that curves in
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(S)
are
not
considered up to isotopy, hence all vertices of
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(S)
have uncountable valence.

This has led to interest in fine curve graphs, e.g.
[
10
,
13
,
19
,
22
,
25
]
.
The guiding motivation here is that combinatorial tools from geometric group theory may be able to see aspects of homeomorphism groups of surfaces that are otherwise obscure.

As a promising step in this program, Farb–Margalit
[
17
]
and Long–Margalit–Pham–Verberne–Yao
[
25
]
showed that the automorphism group of the original fine curve graph is canonically isomorphic to the group
\text{Homeo}(S)
of homeomorphisms of
S
.
It is much less clear to what extent classical subgroups of
\mathrm{Homeo}(S)
are accessible from this perspective.

In particular, the automorphism groups of
subgraphs
of
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(S)
consisting of curves with regularity properties are mysterious and seem less well-behaved.
For instance, Le Roux–Wolff
[
22
]
proved that not all automorphisms of the subgraph of smooth curves are induced by diffeomorphisms, and the first named author proved the automorphism group of the
C^{1}
-curve graph is substantially larger than the group of
C^{1}
diffeomorphisms
[
11
]
.

The point of this paper is to characterize the group
\mathcal{Q}(S)
of quasiconformal homeomorphisms of a compact oriented surface
S
in this circle of ideas.
Quasiconformality is a much-studied weakening of conformality with a range of applications, for instance in analyzing deformations of complex structures in Teichmüller theory.

We note that we allow quasiconformal homeomorphisms to reverse orientation, i.e.
\varphi:S\to S
is quasiconformal if it is quasiconformal with respect to a choice of orientations on the base and target.

This is the first classically-studied group beyond
\mathrm{Homeo}(S)
to be characterized in terms of automorphisms of a fine curve graph. The relevant graph
\mathcal{QC}^{\dagger}(S)
has vertices essential quasicircles in
S
and edges given by disjointness.
See §
2
and §
3
for definitions.
The theory of quasicircles and their connections to quasiconformal homeomorphisms goes back to work of Ahlfors
[
1
]
, and is by now a well-developed and broad area (e.g.
[
8
,
9
,
18
,
31
,
35
]
).

We do not show that every automorphism of
\mathcal{QC}^{\dagger}(S)
is induced by a quasiconformal homeomorphism.
At least in the case of surfaces with punctures, there are counter-examples to this expectation, see Example
3.7
.

However,
\mathcal{QC}^{\dagger}(S)
has extra structure that comes from quality constants
K\geq 1
that measure the irregularity of quasicircles with respect to an auxiliary hyperbolic metric.
We say that
\Phi\in\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{QC}^{\dagger}(S))
is
coarsely order preserving
if for all
K\geq 1
there is some
K^{\prime}<\infty
so that the image of any
K
-quasicircle under
\Phi
is at most a
K’
-quasicircle. See §
2
and §
3
for precise definitions and more discussion.
We indicate that choosing definitions here is a point of some delicacy because not all characterizations of quasicircles have uniformly comparable involved quality constants.

Our main theorem is the following:

Theorem A
.

Let
S
be a closed orientable surface of genus
g\geq 2
.
Any automorphism
\Phi
of
\mathcal{QC}^{\dagger}(S)
is induced by a homeomorphism
\varphi
of
S
.
The homeomorphism
\varphi
is quasiconformal if and only if
\Phi
is coarsely order preserving.

In particular, the canonical map
\mathcal{Q}(S)\to{\rm{Aut}}\mathcal{QC}^{\dagger}(S)
is an isomorphism onto the subgroup
{\rm{CAut}}\mathcal{QC}^{\dagger}(S)
of coarsely order preserving automorphisms.

Theorem
A
gives a combinatorial characterization of the group
\mathcal{Q}(S)
.
From the perspective of fine curve graphs, the main novelties of Theorem
A
are the appearance of a new subgroup of interest and the notion of coarse ordering; from the analytic perspective, the main novelty is the
combinatorial
flavor of the characterization of quasiconformal maps.
Much of the point of Theorem
A
is to make a connection between the two areas, and the result is proved by combining tools from the geometric-group-theoretic study of fine curve graphs with analytic tools on quasicircles and quasiconformal mappings.

This result opens the classical group
\mathcal{Q}(S)
to study with techniques from geometric group theory.
As a step towards the usefulness of Theorem
A
in this direction, we prove:

Theorem B
.

\mathcal{QC}^{\dagger}(S)
is Gromov hyperbolic for closed oriented surfaces
S
of genus
g\geq 2
.

Knowledge of the hyperbolicity of
\mathcal{QC}^{\dagger}(S)
is desirable, because the tools of geometric group theory are particularly well-adapted to the study of groups through actions on Gromov-hyperbolic spaces. Bowden–Hensel–Webb
[
14
]
leverage the hyperbolicity of
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(S)
to show that the space
\widetilde{QH}(\text{Diff}_{0}(S))
of unbounded quasimorphisms on
\text{Diff}_{0}(S)
is infinite dimensional, proving that
\text{Diff}_{0}(S)
is not uniformly perfect.

As a consequence of the differential characterization of quasiconformality and compactness of
S
, any
C^{1}
diffeomorphism is a
K
-quasiconformal map for some
K
. So using Theorem
B
, we can follow the arguments in
[
14
, Sections 4 & 5]
to obtain:

Corollary C
.

For a closed oriented surface
S
with genus
g\geq 2
, the space
\widetilde{QH}(\mathcal{Q}(S))
of unbounded quasimorphisms on
\mathcal{Q}(S)
is infinite dimensional.

We conclude by remarking that we do not know whether or not the assumption of coarse order preservation is necessary in the closed case.
This seems to require understanding subtle aspects of the structure of general quasiconformal maps, and so we ask:

Question
.

Is every homeomorphism of a closed surface that sends quasicircles to quasicircles quasiconformal?

1.1.
Outline

We begin by recalling fundamentals on quasicircles and quasiconformal homeomorphisms in §
2
.
We then introduce
\mathcal{QC}^{\dagger}(S)
and its basic structure in §
3
.

Proving that automorphisms of various fine curve graphs are induced by homeomorphisms is in general an involved process in which the particulars depend heavily on the graph under consideration.
There is not a known general result that includes
\mathcal{QC}^{\dagger}(S)
, but we are able to show that any automorphism of
\mathcal{QC}^{\dagger}(S)
is induced by a homeomorphism with a combination of methods from the continuous and smooth cases
[
12
,
25
]
.
We explain how to adapt these methods to our setting in §
4
-
5.2
.
We then prove that a homeomorphism that induces an element of
\mathrm{CAut}(\mathcal{QC}^{\dagger}(S))
is quasiconformal in §
5.3
.
Finally, we prove Theorem
B
in §6.

2.
Quasiconformal Homeomorphisms and Quasicircles

Let us set some definitions for quasicircles in surfaces.
There are many characterizations of quasicircles (e.g.
[
18
]
), some of which give non-comparable quality constants.
What is important for our proofs here is that our characterization is based on Möbius, not metric, geometry.
Standard references for the basic material here are
[
23
]
and
[
24
]
.

Recall also that a homeomorphism
\varphi
of a Riemann surface
\Sigma
is
K
-quasiconformal
if it distorts the moduli of ring domains in
\Sigma
by a factor of at most
K
.
Quasiconformality admits a reformulation (e.g.
[
4
]
) as a requirement of
\varphi
to have
L^{2}
generalized partial derivatives that almost everywhere satisfy the differential relation corresponding to distortion of eccentricity of infinitesimal ellipses by at most a factor of
K
.
In particular,
K
-quasiconformality is a local condition and any
C^{1}
diffeomorphism of a closed surface is
K
-quasiconformal for some
K
.

Let
\mathcal{Q}(S)
denote the group of quasiconformal homeomorphisms of a Riemann surface structure
\Sigma
on a closed topological surface
S
.
As the notation suggests,
\mathcal{Q}(S)
does not depend on the choice of Riemann surface structure due to the closedness of
S
.

2.1.
Quasicircles in the Riemann sphere

In contrast to
K
-quasiconformality,
K
-quasicircularity is
not
a local condition.
This leads us to take some care with its treatment on surfaces with genus.
On
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
, we recall:

Definition 2.1
.

A
K
-quasicircle in
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
is the image of a circle under a
K
-quasiconformal homeomorphism of
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
.

There are many equivalent reformulations of the condition of quasicircularity on
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
and notions of quality constants defining
K
-quasicircularity.
We recall a reformulation that will be useful to us.
A curve
\gamma\in\mathbb{C}
satisfies the
4
-point
c
-bounded turning condition
for
c>0
if for all distinct and circularly ordered
p_{1},p_{2},p_{3},p_{4}\in\gamma
, the cross ratio
(p_{4},p_{1};p_{2},p_{3})
is no less than
1/c
, i.e.

\displaystyle\frac{|p_{1}-p_{2}||p_{4}-p_{3}|}{|p_{1}-p_{3}||p_{4}-p_{2}|}\leq c.

There is a standard modification to extend to the Riemann sphere
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
.
Because the cross-ratio is Möbius-invariant, the
4
-point
c
-bounded turning condition is invariant under Möbius transformations of
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
.

The
4
-point bounded turning condition characterizes quasicircularity, with uniform dependence in the involved constants:

Theorem 2.2

(Ahlfors
[
1
, Theorem 1]
)
.

For every
K>0
there is a
c(K)<\infty
so that every
K
-quasicircle in
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
satisfies the
4
-point
c
-bounded turning condition.
Conversely, for every
c>0
there is a
K(c)<\infty
so that every Jordan curve in
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
that satisfies the
4
-point
c
-bounded turning condition is a
K(c)
-quasicircle.

In the opposite direction of the definition of quasicircularity in terms of quasiconformal maps, quasiconformal maps of
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
are characterized in terms of their distortion of circles.

Theorem 2.3

(Aseev
[
2
]
)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}
be a domain, and let
f:\Omega\to\mathbb{C}
be an injection.
That
f
is a quasiconformal homeomorphism is equivalent to the existence of a
K\geq 1
so that for every
z\in\Omega
there is a neighborhood
U_{z}
so that for every circle
C
in
U_{z}
the image
f(C)
is a
K
-quasicircle.

We note that this theorem considers quasiconformality without the requirement of orientation-preservation.

Extension results

In producing quasiconformal maps, we shall find extension results in
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
useful.
In particular, we shall use the following two theorems.
First, we shall use the smoothness in the following result of Tukia.

Theorem 2.4

(Tukia
[
37
]
)
.

Let
\gamma\subset\mathbb{CP}^{1}
be a quasicircle.
Then there is a quasiconformal map of
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
taking
\mathbb{R}
to
\gamma
that is smooth away from
\gamma
.

This exact statement follows from the main result of
[
37
]
and
[
35
, Theorem 4.9]
, as discussed on page 2 of
[
37
]
.
We note that the smoothness we use comes from properties of the Beurling–Ahlfors extension
[
7
]
.

Second, we shall use the following extension result.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
L\geq 1
. Then there is a
K=K(L)
so that every
L
-bilipschitz embedding
f:S^{1}\to\mathbb{D}
with respect to the Euclidean metric on
\mathbb{D}
admits a
K
-quasiconformal extension to the disk.

This is a corollary of the planar Lipschitz Schönflies theorem
[
36
, Theorem A]
, together with the observation (e.g.
[
37
, §7]
) that for every
L>1
there is a
K(L)
so that every
L
-bilipschitz embedding
\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
is
K
-quasiconformal.

2.2.
Surfaces with genus

In higher genus, some care is needed in choosing a characterization of quasicircles in which the quality constants behave well for essential curves.
We use the following definition:

Definition 2.6
.

Let
S
be a closed, oriented surface with a metric
g
of constant sectional curvature
0
or
-1
. An
essential
K
-quasicircle
in
(S,g)
is the image of a simple closed geodesic
\gamma
in
S
under a
K
-quasiconformal homeomorphism of
S
.

Here,
K
-quasiconformality is with respect to the induced conformal structure of
(S,g)
.
This characterization is adapted to Möbius geometry as these metric geodesics are circular arcs in the induced
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
-structures.
Definition
2.6
gives the usual family of quasicircles (Remark
1
).
We remark that the quasicircularity constants in our definition have arbitrarily large differences with those appearing in metric definitions of quasicircularity.
A source of this discrepancy is that simple geodesics may wrap back near themselves as they traverse the topology of
S
.

On a practical level, Definition
2.6
is convenient for working with curves known to be quasicircles, but requires the production of a quasiconformal mapping in the verification that a curve is a quasicircle.

It will help us, at times when we are not considering quality constants, to be able to swap between characterizations of quasiconformality.
To that end, we recall that an embedded curve is a quasicircle if and only if it satisfies the (two-point) metric bounded turning condition: there is a
k<\infty
so that for all
x,y\in\gamma
there is a connected component of
\gamma-\{x,y\}
of diameter bounded by
kd(x,y)
.

Remark 1
.

One may verify that Definition
2.6
is equivalent to the metric bounded turning characterization, with potentially non-comparable quasicircularity constants.

Indeed, as simple closed geodesics are metric bounded turning quasicircles, the implication that any image of a simple closed geodesic under a quasiconformal map is a metric quasicircle follows.
The other direction is obtained as follows.
Let
\gamma
be a metric bounded turning quasicircle in
S
and
\gamma_{0}
be the geodesic in the free homotopy class of
\gamma_{0}
.
Conformally identify an annular neighborhood of
\gamma
with a ring domain in
\mathbb{C}
and a metric
\varepsilon
-neighborhood of
\gamma_{0}
with an annulus in
\mathbb{C}
in which
\gamma_{0}
is the circle.
By Theorem
2.4
, there is a quasiconformal homeomorphism of a neighborhood of
\gamma_{0}
to a neighborhood of
\gamma
that takes
\gamma_{0}
to
\gamma
and is smooth away from
\gamma_{0}
.
From smoothness, extending quasiconformally to the rest of
S
is classical.

Parts of our arguments below that do not consider quality constants make use of inessential curves.
We never refer to quality constants for inessential quasicircles.
To be concrete, we adopt the convention that inessential curves on
S
are quasicircles if they satisfy the two-point metric bounded turning condition.

Definition
2.6
is independent of the choice of metric in the following sense.

Lemma 2.7
.

Suppose
g
and
g^{\prime}
are locally homogeneous metrics on
S
. Then for every isotopy class
[\alpha]
of essential simple closed curves in
S
, there is a
K(\alpha)
so that if
\gamma\in[\alpha]
is a
K^{\prime}
-quasicircle with respect to
g
then
\gamma
is a
K(\alpha)K^{\prime}
-quasicircle with respect to
g^{\prime}
.

One may see Lemma
2.7
using the argument in Remark
1
, though the smoothness of geodesics makes Theorem
2.4
a far deeper result than is necessary.

We conclude by mentioning that the metric bounded turning characterization of quasicircles localizes in the following manner.
The involved quality constants are not uniform.

Lemma 2.8

(Non-uniform localization)
.

Let
\gamma\subset S
be an embedded circle that admits an open covering by neighborhoods
U_{i}
on which it satisfies the metric bounded turning condition for constants
k_{i}
.
Then
\gamma
is a quasicircle.

Proof.

Take a finite sub-cover
U_{1},...,U_{n}
of
\gamma
.
The existence of a Lebesgue number
\delta
for the subcover implies that the metric bounded turning condition holds for some
k
for all
\delta
-close pairs of points on
\gamma
, and compactness implies that for points separated by at least
\delta
satisfy a
k^{\prime}
-metric bounded turning inequality.
∎

3.
Graphs of Quasicircles

The combinatorial objects we consider are analogues of the
fine curve graphs

\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(S)
of
[
14
]
, whose vertices are simple closed curves in
S
with edges given by disjointness.
The following are the central objects of our study:

Definition 3.1

(Quasicircle graph)
.

For
S=S_{g}
with
g\geq 2
, let
\mathcal{QC}^{\dagger}(S)
be the graph with vertices the set of essential quasicircles in
S
and edges between vertices representing disjoint quasicircles.

Lemma
2.7
shows that
\mathcal{QC}^{\dagger}(S)
is a well-defined graph.
In fact, more information is invariant under change of reference data.
To state this invariance, let us adopt the following notation.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
S=S_{g}
with
g\geq 2
.
Let
\mathscr{S}
denote the collection of isotopy classes of essential simple closed curves in
S
and let
[\beta]\in\mathscr{S}
.
Then define
\mathcal{QC}^{\dagger}_{[\beta]}(S)
as the subgraph of
\mathcal{QC}^{\dagger}(S)
induced by the vertices in the isotopy class
[\beta]
.

We show in §
5
that all automorphisms
\Phi
of
\mathcal{QC}^{\dagger}(S)
are induced by homeomorphisms
\varphi
of
S
.
We adopt the notation
\Phi([\beta])=\varphi([\beta])
.
The (unlabeled) partition
\mathcal{QC}^{\dagger}(S)=\bigsqcup_{[\beta]\in\mathscr{S}}\mathcal{QC}^{\dagger}_{[\beta]}(S)
is invariant under automorphisms of
\mathcal{QC}^{\dagger}(S)
and so is well-defined.

There is a filtration
\mathcal{F}=\{\mathcal{F}_{K}\}_{K\in[1,\infty)}
of
\mathcal{QC}^{\dagger}(S)
with
\mathcal{F}_{K}
the set of
K
-quasicircles on
S
.
By
filtration
, we mean that
\mathcal{F}_{K}\subset\mathcal{F}_{K^{\prime}}
for any
K<K^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{QC}^{\dagger}(S)=\bigcup_{K\geq 1}\mathcal{F}_{K}
.
The filtration
\mathcal{F}
restricts to filtrations
\mathcal{F}_{[\beta]}=\{\mathcal{F}_{K,[\beta]}\}_{K\in[1,\infty)}
of each subgraph
\mathcal{QC}^{\dagger}_{[\beta]}(S)
.
Of course,
\mathcal{F}
is not invariant under change of hyperbolic metric or application of a quasiconformal map of
S
.
But changing metrics respects
\mathcal{F}
in the following weakly coarse sense (Lemma
2.7
):

Definition 3.3
.

An automorphism
\Phi\in{\rm{Aut}}\mathcal{QC}^{\dagger}(S)
is
weakly coarse order preserving
if for every
K\geq 1
and
[\beta]\in\mathscr{S}
there is a
K^{\prime}=K^{\prime}(K,\beta)>1
so that
\Phi\left(\mathcal{F}_{K,[\beta]}\right)\subset\mathcal{F}_{K^{\prime},\Phi([\beta])}
.
We say
\Phi
is
coarsely order preserving
if the constants
K^{\prime}(K,\beta)
can be taken to be independent of
\beta
.
Let
{\rm{CAut}}\mathcal{QC}^{\dagger}(S)
denote the group of weakly coarse order preserving automorphisms of
\mathcal{QC}^{\dagger}(S)
.

Note that any
K
-quasiconformal homeomorphism
\varphi
of
S
induces a graph automorphism, which we denote by
\varphi_{*}
, of
\mathcal{QC}^{\dagger}(S)
.
Note further that
\varphi_{*}
is coarsely order preserving by the standard bound for quasiconformality constants of a composition, i.e.
\varphi_{*}\in\mathrm{CAut}\mathcal{QC}^{\dagger}(S)
.
This gives a natural homomorphism from
\mathcal{Q}(S)
to
\mathrm{CAut}\mathcal{QC}^{\dagger}(S)
.
We will prove:

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
S=S_{g}
with
g\geq 2
. Then the natural map
\mathcal{Q}(S)\to\mathrm{CAut}\mathcal{QC}^{\dagger}(S)
is an isomorphism.

Note Theorem
3.4
implies that the coarse order preservation of
\Phi
is independent of the reference hyperbolic metric.
Since quasiconformal homeomorphisms
\varphi
of
S
induce automorphisms
\varphi_{*}
that are coarsely order preserving, we obtain:

Corollary 3.5
.

An automorphism
\Phi\in\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{QC}^{\dagger}(S))
is coarsely order preserving if and only if
\Phi
weakly coarse order preserving.

Theorem
A
then follows immediately from Corollary
3.5
and Theorem
3.4
.

Remark 2
.

There are two reasons why we prefer to work with weak coarse order preservation in the following.
First, it is weaker than coarse order preservation, and so Theorem
3.4
is stronger than if we only worked with coarse order preservation.
Second, changing metrics is easily seen to be weakly coarse order preserving, but it is much less clear whether or not the involved constants
K^{\prime}(K,\beta)
can be taken to be
\beta
-independent.
So the notion of weak coarse order preservation has desirable naturality properties in our setting.

After the above discussion, what is left to show is:

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
S=S_{g}
with
g\geq 2
and let
\Phi\in\mathrm{CAut}\mathcal{QC}^{\dagger}(S)
. Then there exists
\varphi\in\mathcal{Q}(S)
so that
\Phi=\varphi_{*}
.

The strategy to prove Proposition
3.6
follows similar lines to other characterizations of automorphisms of fine curve graphs
[
12
,
25
]
.
In §
4
, we identify graph structures that uniquely determine inessential quasicircles in the surface.
Then in §
5
, we use this graph structure to define a map from
\mathrm{Aut}\mathcal{QC}^{\dagger}(S)
to automorphisms of an extended graph that also includes vertices for inessential quasicircles.
From there, we use inessential quasicircles to identify points and build the desired map on the surface.

We conclude by remarking that for punctured surfaces the analogue of Theorem
3.4
fails without the assumption of weak coarse order preservation:

Example 3.7
.

Let
\varphi:(0,1)\to(0,1)
be an orientation-preserving homeomorphism and let
F:(\mathbb{C}-\{0\})\to(\mathbb{D}-\{0\})
be given by
(r,\theta)\mapsto(\varphi(r),\theta)
.
Then every quasicircle in
\mathbb{C}-\{0\}
is mapped by
\varphi
to a quasicircle from smoothness of
F
.

On the other hand, the annulus
A_{r}
with inner radius
r
and outer radius
1
has modulus
-\log r
and
F(A_{r})
has modulus
-\log\varphi(r)
.
So for
\varphi
with sufficiently quickly decaying derivative at
0
, the map
F
unboundedly distorts the moduli of the annuli
A_{r}
for small
r
and so is not quasiconformal.

4.
Encoding Topological Data Into Graphs

In this section, we find graph structures that we can use to identify inessential quasicircles in our surface. We start in §
4.1
by identifying pairs of quasicircles with simple intersections, called torus pairs. Then in §
4.2
, we use torus pairs to identify a construction called bigon pairs that determine a unique inessential quasicircle.
Next in §
4.3
, we define and prove a couple results about sharing pairs, which are pairs of bigon pairs that share the same inessential quasicircle.
Finally, we prove that associated arc graphs are connected in §
4.4
, which shows that these inessential quasicircles are well-defined under automorphisms of the graph.

In this section,
S
is a closed oriented surface of genus
g\geq 2
unless otherwise specified.

4.1.
Torus pairs

In this subsection, we show that automorphisms of
\mathcal{QC}^{\dagger}(S)
preserve the sets of nondegenerate transverse torus pairs, nondegenerate nontransverse torus pairs, and degenerate torus pairs. Additionally, the graph differentiates between these three different types of torus pairs.

We start with relevant definitions. A
multiquasicircle
is a finite collection of pairwise disjoint essential quasicircles in
S
.
This implies that any multiquasicircle forms a finite clique, i.e. a complete graph, in

4.2.
Bigon pairs

A pair of homotopic vertices

are said to form a

In this section, our goal is to prove that bigon pairs are preserved under automorphisms of
\mathcal{QC}^{\dagger}(S)
.

Lemma 4.14

(Existence of Bigon Pairs)
.

Let
\gamma
be a quasicircle in
S
with distinct points
p_{1},p_{2}\in\gamma
. Let
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}
be the closures of the connected components of
\gamma-\{p_{1},p_{2}\}
.
Then there exist essential quasicircles
\eta_{1},\eta_{2}
in
S
so that:

(1)

\eta_{i}\cap\gamma=\gamma_{i}
(
i=1,2
),

(2)

\eta_{1}\cap\eta_{2}=\eta_{1}-\text{int}(\gamma_{1})=\eta_{2}-\text{int}(\gamma_{2})
.

Proof.

The proof uses the same technique appearing in Remark
1
in §
2
.
Take an annular neighborhood
U
of
\gamma
.
Then there is a round annulus
A
containing the unit circle
\gamma_{0}
in
\mathbb{C}
and a conformal homeomorphism
\psi:U\to A
.
Theorem
2.4
then gives a quasiconformal homeomorphism of
\mathbb{C}
that so
\varphi(\gamma)=\gamma_{0}
.
There is then a neighborhood

so that
\psi^{-1}\circ\varphi\circ\psi:U^{\prime}\to S
is quasiconformal, is smooth away from
\gamma
and so that
\gamma^{\prime}=\psi^{-1}\circ\varphi\circ\psi(\gamma)
is smooth.
After potentially shrinking
U^{\prime}
again, extend
\psi^{-1}\circ\varphi\circ\psi
to a homeomorphism

that is smooth away from
\gamma
and hence quasiconformal on
S
from the differential characterization of quasiconformality.
The desired construction can now be run without trouble with the smooth curve
\gamma^{\prime}
so that the curves
\eta_{1}^{\prime},\eta_{2}^{\prime}
are smooth away from right-angle turns at

, and hence are quasicircles.
Then
\eta_{i}=h^{-1}(\eta_{i}^{\prime})
are quasicircles and have the desired properties.
∎

The quasi-arc graph

We first define the quasi-arc graph, denoted

Proposition 4.21

For any

Proof.

There is a natural surjection from

Now,

[

From the definition of
\mathcal{A}(S)

of
\mathcal{QA}^{\dagger}(S)

There exists an isotopy

to
b

Let

be a quasi-arc which is disjoint from

By the isotopy extension property
[

to be an isotopy on the entire surface, which implies

will also be an isotopy from
c
to some arc

may not be a quasi-arc.

Note that for any

, the arcs
H_{t}(a)
and
H_{t}(c)
might not be quasi-arcs, but they must be disjoint. Since
H_{t}(a)
is contained in the open set
S\setminus H_{t}(c)
and smooth arcs (and thus quasi-arcs) are dense away from the boundary, by Lemma
4.14
there exists a quasi-arc
a_{t}
in some small neighborhood of
H_{t}(a)
that is disjoint from
H_{t}(c)
. Similarly, we can find a quasi-arc
c_{t}
in a small neighborhood of
H_{t}(c)
that is disjoint from both
a_{t}
and
H_{t}(a)
.

For any
t\in I
there is a neighborhood
U
of
t
so that the curve
H_{t^{\prime}}(a)
is disjoint from
c_{t}
for any
t^{\prime}\in U
.
So by compactness, we may partition the interval
I
into finitely many pieces
I=\{t_{0}=0,t_{1},t_{2},\ldots,t_{k}=1\}
such that
c_{t_{i}}
and
H_{t_{i+1}}(a)
are disjoint. Thus the quasi-arc
a_{t_{i+1}}
can also be made disjoint from
c_{t_{i}}
.

Thus

\{a=a_{0},c=c_{0},a_{t_{1}},c_{t_{1}},\ldots,a_{t_{i}},c_{t_{i}},a_{t_{i+1}},\ldots,c_{t_{k-1}},a_{t_{k}}=b\}

is a path in
\mathcal{QA}^{\dagger}(S)
between any arbitrary homotopic quasi-arcs
a
and
b
. Thus
\mathcal{QA}^{\dagger}(S)
is connected.
∎

The nonseparating quasi-arc graph

We say that a quasi-arc in a surface
S
is nonseparating if its complement in
S
is connected. The nonseparating quasi-arc graph, denoted
\mathcal{N\!QA}^{\dagger}(S)
, is the subgraph of
\mathcal{QA}^{\dagger}(S)
spanned by the nonseparating quasi-arcs.

Corollary 4.22
.

For any
S=S_{g}^{b}
with
g\geq 1
and
b>0
, the nonseparating quasi-arc graph
\mathcal{N\!QA}^{\dagger}(S)
is connected.

Proof.

This can be seen by considering the neighbors of any separating vertex in a path in
\mathcal{QA}^{\dagger}(S)
between two nonseparating quasi-arcs. If these neighbors are on separate sides of the separating vertex, then they must be disjoint, and this vertex can be removed from the path. Otherwise, if the neighbors instead reside on the same side, then there exists a nonseparating quasi-arc on the other side that is disjoint from both. Thus the separating vertex can be replaced by a nonseparating vertex.
∎

The linked quasi-arc graph

Let
d_{0}
be a distinguished component of
\partial S
. We say that
a,b\in\mathcal{QA}^{\dagger}(S)
are
linked
at
d_{0}
if all endpoints of
a,b
are on
d_{0}
and the endpoints of
a
separate the endpoints of
b
in
d_{0}
.

We define the linked quasi-arc graph
\mathcal{QA}_{Lk}^{\dagger}(S,d_{0})
to be the graph whose vertices are nonseparating quasi-arcs in
S
with both endpoints at
d_{0}
and whose edges connect disjoint quasi-arcs that are linked at
d_{0}
. When convenient we suppress
d_{0}
in the notation and write
\mathcal{QA}_{Lk}^{\dagger}(S)
.

Corollary 4.23
.

For any
S=S_{g}^{b}
with
g\geq 1
and
b>0
, the linked quasi-arc graph
\mathcal{QA}_{Lk}^{\dagger}(S)
is connected.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{N\!QA}^{\dagger}(S)
is connected by Corollary
4.22
, we only thing need to check that there is a path between any two
a,b\in\mathcal{QA}_{Lk}^{\dagger}(S)
that are not linked at
d_{0}
. Since
a
and
b
are both nonseparating, then
S\setminus\{a\cup b\}
has at most two components. Notably, the component of
d_{0}
between the endpoints of
a
not containing the endpoints of
b
and the component of
d_{0}
between the endpoints of
b
not containing the endpoints of
a
must both be in the same component. So there is a quasi-arc
c
with endpoints in each of these components of
d_{0}
, and hence is linked with both
a
and
b
.
∎

5.
Building the quasiconformal map

This section has three parts. First, we use the graph information from §
4
to extend an automorphism of our original quasicircle graph to an automorphism of the extended quasicircle graph. Second, we use the automorphism of the extended graph to build a homeomorphism of the surface. Finally, we show that this homeomorphism is also a quasiconformal map.

As in §
4
,
S
is a closed oriented surface of genus
g\geq 2
unless otherwise specified.

5.1.
From essential to inessential

Our goal is to define an extended version of the quasicircle graph which serves as a tool to prove that the group of automorphisms of the quasicircle graph embeds into
\mathrm{Homeo}(S)
.

Definition 5.1

(Extended quasicircle graph)
.

Let
\mathcal{E\!QC}^{\dagger}(S)
be the graph with vertices
all quasicircles in
S
and edges between vertices corresponding to disjoint quasicircles.

The goal of this subsection is to prove the following result:

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
\Phi\in\mathrm{Aut}\mathcal{QC}^{\dagger}(S)
. Then there exists a
\overline{\Phi}\in\mathrm{Aut}\mathcal{E\!QC}^{\dagger}(S)
such that the restriction of
\overline{\Phi}
to the essential vertices is exactly
\Phi
.

Before we can give a description of this extended automorphism, we must first describe the edges of
\mathcal{E\!QC}^{\dagger}(S)
using only information from
\mathcal{QC}^{\dagger}(S)
. This is accomplished in the following lemma.

Lemma 5.3
.

Edges in
\mathcal{E\!QC}^{\dagger}(S)
can be differentiated in
\mathcal{QC}^{\dagger}(S)
by the following constructions:

(1)

Two inessential quasicircles
e
and
f
are disjoint and not nested if and only if there exist disjoint bigon pairs
\{c,d\}
for
e
and
\{c^{\prime},d^{\prime}\}
for
f
.

(2)

Two inessential quasicircles
e
and
f
are disjoint and nested if and only if, up to relabeling, for any bigon pair
\{c,d\}
for
e
, there exists a bigon pair
\{c^{\prime},d^{\prime}\}
for
f
disjoint from
c
.

(3)

An essential quasicircle
a
and inessential quasicircle
e
are disjoint if and only if there exists a bigon pair
\{c,d\}
for
e
that is disjoint from
a
.

Proof.

Let
e,f\in\mathcal{E\!QC}^{\dagger}(S)
be two disjoint inessential quasicircles. If
e,f
are not nested, then
e
is contained in the
S^{1}_{g}
component of
S\setminus f
. So there exists a bigon pair
\{c,d\}
for
e
in this component that will be disjoint from
f
. Moreover, since bigon pairs consist of nonseparating quasicircles and are closed subsets of
S
, then there exists a bigon pair
\{c^{\prime},d^{\prime}\}
for
f
on
S\setminus\{c\cup d\}
. The reverse direction is straightforward. The existence of disjoint bigon pairs prevents any nesting since there do not exist any bigon pairs in the disk component of
S\setminus e
or
S\setminus f
.

Now suppose that
e,f
are nested. By possibly relabeling, we can now suppose that
f
is nested in the disk component of
S\setminus e
. Let
\{c,d\}
be any bigon pair for
e
. Since
c
is a nonseparating curve and
S\setminus c
is an open subset of
S
containing
f
, then there exists a bigon pair
\{c^{\prime},d^{\prime}\}
for
f
that is disjoint from
c
. For the reverse direction, there are two cases to consider. When
e
and
f
intersect or are disjoint, but not nested, then there exists a bigon pair
\{c,d\}
for
e
where both
c
and
d
intersect
f
, either along
e
or outside of
e
. In both cases, it is impossible to find a bigon pair for
f
that is disjoint from
c
.

Finally, we come to the case of an essential quasicircle
a
and inessential quasicircle
e
. Suppose they are disjoint. Since
a
is essential, then there is enough topology in the component of
S\setminus a
that contains
e
to contain some bigon pair
\{c,d\}
for
e
. The reverse direction follows from the fact that
e
is a subset of its bigon pair.
∎

Proof of Proposition
5.2
.

Let
\Phi\in\mathrm{Aut}\mathcal{QC}^{\dagger}(S)
. We will define the map
\overline{\Phi}\in\mathrm{Aut}\mathcal{E\!QC}^{\dagger}(S)
by the following two rules:

(1)

If
a\in\mathcal{E\!QC}^{\dagger}(S)
is an essential quasicircle, then
\overline{\Phi}(a)=\Phi(a)
.

(2)

If
e\in\mathcal{E\!QC}^{\dagger}(S)
is an inessential quasicircle, then let
\{c,d\}
be a bigon pair for
e
. Define
\overline{\Phi}(e)
to be the inessential quasicircle determined by
\{\Phi(c),\Phi(d)\}
.

Directly from this definition, it is clear that the action of
\overline{\Phi}
restricted to the essential vertices is exactly
\Phi
.

The inessential curve determined by
\{\Phi(c),\Phi(d)\}
does not depend on the choice of bigon pair as Corollary
4.23
implies that there exists a path of bigon pairs from
\{c,d\}
to any other bigon pair. In addition, we have that each consecutive pair in this path forms a linked sharing pair, and linked sharing pairs are preserved by Proposition
4.20
. Thus
\overline{\Phi}
is well-defined.

Moreover, Lemma
5.3
shows that for any two vertices
a,b\in\mathcal{E\!QC}^{\dagger}(S)
, there is an edge between
a
and
b
if and only if there is an edge between
\overline{\Phi}(a)
and
\overline{\Phi}(b)
.

Finally,
\overline{{\Phi}^{-1}}
induces exactly the inverse action of
\overline{\Phi}
, namely

\overline{\Phi^{-1}}(\overline{\Phi}(a))=a\qquad\mbox{ and }\qquad\overline{\Phi^{-1}}(\overline{\Phi}(e))=e

since
\{\Phi(c),\Phi(d)\}
is a bigon pair for
\overline{\Phi}(e)
and
\{\Phi^{-1}(\Phi(c)),\Phi^{-1}(\Phi(d))\}=\{c,d\}
, which is a bigon pair for
e
. Thus
\overline{\Phi}
is invertible and is a graph automorphism of
\mathcal{E\!QC}^{\dagger}(S)
as desired.
∎

5.2.
Building the homeomorphism

While the previous sections follow the work in
[
25
]
, their map from automorphisms of the extended graph to the homeomorphisms of the surface cannot be directly adapted to quasicircles.
The fundamental obstacle here is that through any convergent sequence of distinct points there is a Jordan curve by the Denjoy–Riesz Theorem
[
28
]
, but not necessarily a more regular curve.
Specifically,
[
25
, Lemma 4.1]
uses the classification of infinite-type surfaces
[
30
, Theorem 1]
to prove this fact, which is only up to homeomorphism.

Fortunately, this obstacle has been overcome for smooth curves in
[
12
]
. We will outline this argument to show the following:

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
\Phi\in\mathrm{Aut}\mathcal{QC}^{\dagger}(S)
. Then
\Phi=\varphi_{*}
for some
\varphi\in{\rm{Homeo}}(S)
, where
\varphi_{*}
is the automorphism of
\mathcal{QC}^{\dagger}(S)
induced by
\varphi
.

We begin with identifying a graph structure in the extended graph that is the same as the graph structure in the non-extended graph.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
S
be an oriented surface. Let
a\in\mathcal{E\!QC}^{\dagger}(S)
. Then
a
is separating if and only if
link(a)
is a join.

The proof of this lemma follows directly from the proof of Lemma
4.1
. Our next steps involve defining and identifying a construction that allows us to determine the homeomorphism of the surface.

Definition 5.6

(Nested sequences)
.

A sequence of curves
(c_{i})
on a surface
S
is
nested
if each
c_{i}
is separating and there exists a component
C_{i}
of
S\setminus c_{i}
such that
c_{j}\subset C_{i}
, for all
j>i
.

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
\Psi\in\mathrm{Aut}\mathcal{E\!QC}^{\dagger}(S)
. If
(c_{i})\in\mathcal{E\!QC}^{\dagger}(S)
is a nested sequence of curves, then the sequence
(\Psi(c_{i}))
is also nested.

The proof for Lemma
5.7
follows directly from Lemma
5.5
. We omit this proof as the details for the same result for smooth curves can be found in
[
12
, Lemma 2.3]
.

Definition 5.8

(Convergent sequences)
.

A sequence of curves
(c_{i})
in
S
will be said to
converge
to a set
P
if they converge to
P
in the Hausdorff topology. We say that a curve
intersects the tail
of a sequence
(c_{i})
if it intersects infinitely many of the
c_{i}
.

Lemma 5.9
.

A sequence of nested curves
(c_{i})\in\mathcal{E\!QC}^{\dagger}(S)
converges to a point
x\in S
if and only if both of the following hold:

(i)

there exists a curve
a\in\mathcal{E\!QC}^{\dagger}(S)
that intersects the tail of
(c_{i})

(ii)

any two curves
a,b\in\mathcal{E\!QC}^{\dagger}(S)
that intersect the tail of
(c_{i})
must intersect each other.

Again, the proof of this lemma is identical to the smooth version in
[
12
, Lemma 2.4]
and is omitted here (c.f.
[
17
]
for the continuous case of this and Lemma
5.10
). Since both of the above conditions are identifiable in the graph, this shows that nested convergent sequences are preserved by automorphisms of
\mathcal{E\!QC}^{\dagger}(S)
.

Lemma 5.10
.

Every
\Psi\in\mathrm{Aut}\mathcal{E\!QC}^{\dagger}(S)
induces a homeomorphism
\varphi_{\Psi}
such that the action of
\varphi_{\Psi}
on
\mathcal{E\!QC}^{\dagger}(S)
is given by
\Psi
.

We will only sketch this proof to give the reader an idea of how this map is built and why it is a homeomorphism. Full details can be found in the proof for the smooth case in
[
12
, Lemma 2.5]
.

Proof Sketch.

To define the desired map, we first take a nested convergent sequence
(c_{i})
that converges to a point
x\in S
. Since
\Psi
preserves nested convergent sequences, the image sequence
(\Psi(c_{i}))
must also be a nested convergent sequence. We define
\varphi_{\Psi}(x)
to be the point of convergence for this image sequence.

This map is shown to be well-defined by interleaving subsequences of nested sequences that converge to the same point. A similar interleaved subsequence of the image sequences is used to show that this map is injective. Surjectivity follows in a straightforward manner by using a preimage of a nested convergent sequence and from the fact that
\Psi^{-1}
is also an automorphism of the graph.

To show continuity, we construct a sequence of nested sequences that converge to the points in a sequence
\{x_{n}\}
that converge to
x
. A diagonal sequence is then used to show that the points
\{\varphi_{\Psi}(x_{n})\}
converge to
\varphi_{\Psi}(x)
. Continuity of the inverse is immediate from the fact that
\varphi_{\Psi}^{-1}=\varphi_{\Psi^{-1}}
.

Finally, we show that the action of
\varphi_{\Psi}
on
\mathcal{E\!QC}^{\dagger}(S)
is exactly
\Psi
. This is accomplished by defining nested convergent sequences for each point on a curve
c
. Since
c
intersects the tail of this convergent sequence, then
\Psi(c)
also intersects the tail of the image sequence. Thus by compactness,
\Psi(c)
contains all the points in
\varphi_{\Psi}(c)
.
∎

We now give the final proof of this subsection.

Proof of Proposition
5.4
.

By Proposition
5.2
, every element
\Phi
of
\mathrm{Aut}\mathcal{QC}^{\dagger}(S)
corresponds to an element
\overline{\Phi}
of
\mathrm{Aut}\mathcal{E\!QC}^{\dagger}(S)
such that the restriction of
\overline{\Phi}
to
\mathcal{QC}^{\dagger}(S)
is exactly
\Phi
. By Lemma
5.10
, there is a homeomorphism
\varphi_{\overline{\Phi}}
such that the action of
\varphi_{\overline{\Phi}}
on the extended graph is exactly
\overline{\Phi}
. Thus the action of
\varphi_{\overline{\Phi}}
on
\mathcal{QC}^{\dagger}(S)
is exactly
\Phi
, as desired.
∎

5.3.
Quasiconformality

We now turn to examining the quasiconformality of the homeomorphisms inducing automorphisms of
\mathcal{E\!QC}^{\dagger}(S)
.
The structure of our argument is to apply Aseev’s theorem after constraining the quasicircle constants of images of sufficiently small circles in
S
in terms of the
4
-point bounded turning condition.

The following is the observation that we use to go from information about essential curves to small circles.

Lemma 5.11
.

Let
(S,g)
be a hyperbolic surface.
Then there is a finite family
F
of isotopy classes of simple closed curves and
K,\varepsilon>0
so that for all circles
C
on
S
of diameter less than
\varepsilon
and circularly ordered quadruples
q=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})
in
C
there is an arc
\mathcal{A}
of
C
containing
q
and an essential
K
-quasicircle
\gamma
with isotopy class in
F
containing
\mathcal{A}
.

Working only with metric circles here will suffice for our uses because Aseev’s Theorem
2.3
only requires verification that circles are mapped to quasicircles.

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon
be small compared to the injectivity radius of
(S,g)

and let
C
be a circle of diameter less than
\varepsilon
. Then there is a
(3/4)
-circle arc
\mathcal{A}_{q}
of
C
containing
q
: this is the key point.

Now extend
\mathcal{A}_{q}
to an arc
\alpha_{q}
that has diameter a definite but small fraction of the injectivity radius by extending each side with geodesic segments perpendicular to the boundary of the circle.
Let
B_{q}
be a ball containing
\alpha_{q}
and so that the endpoints of
\alpha_{q}
are contained in and perpendicular to
\partial B_{q}
. Finally, obtain
\gamma
by connecting the sides to each other with an arc that only meets
B_{q}
at the endpoints and lies in one of a finite family
F
of isotopy classes.

We conclude by noting that this process yields
\gamma
with uniformly controlled quascircularity constant
K
.
Because the curves
\gamma
are so regular and controlled this is quite standard; we outline a careful proof for the convenience of the reader.

Let
\nu
be the geodesic in the isotopy class of
\gamma
.
Our task is to find a quasiconformal map
\varphi:(S,g)\to(S,g)
taking
\nu
to
\gamma
with uniform control on
K(\varphi)
.
The above process allows us to choose
\gamma
with the following control.
First, we arrange for
\gamma
to be piecewise smooth and piecewise geodesic outside
\mathcal{A}_{q}
, with uniform bounds on the number of geodesic segments and angles between segments.
Next, we assure that
\gamma
does not wrap too closely back on itself in the sense that for a uniform
\varepsilon>0
, the
\varepsilon
-neighborhood of the essential lift of
\gamma
in the corresponding annular covering is embedded isometrically in
(S,g)
by the covering map.

To extend to the rest of
S
, we define a map on metric graphs in
S
whose complementary regions are disks (e.g. topological triangulations), then apply extension results to define this map on the remaining disks.
Because of the uniformity of our construction, we may take two embedded graphs
G_{1},G_{2}
with peicewise smooth edges in
S
satisfying the following:

(1)

\nu
and
\gamma
are unions of cycles
C_{i}
in
G_{i}
of the same length.

(2)

All complementary components
P_{j}^{i}
of
G_{i}
are disks (

,

) with uniformly bounded diameter in their induced metrics,

(3)

There is a homeomorphism
\psi
of

isotopic to the identity taking
G_{1}
to
G_{2}
.
Write the induced isomorphism of graphs by
\Phi
.

(4)

There is a homeomorphism
\psi^{\prime}:G_{1}\to G_{2}
inducing the graph isomorphism
\Phi
so that the restriction of
\psi^{\prime}
to the boundary of each cell

is
L
-bilipschitz for a uniform

with respect to the metrics on

induced by the metric on

.

It then follows from lifting to
\mathbb{H}^{2}
, comparing the hyperbolic and Euclidean metrics with the diameter bound on

, and applying Theorem
2.5
that there is a uniform
K<\infty
so that there exist
K
-quasiconformal homeomorphisms
P_{j}^{1}\to P_{j}^{2}
that restrict to
\psi^{\prime}
on
\partial P_{j}^{i}
.
We conclude that
\psi^{\prime}
extends to a homeomorphism
\psi^{\prime\prime}
of
S
that takes
\nu
to
\gamma
and is
K
-quasiconformal on the complement of
G
.
The map
\psi^{\prime\prime}
is then
K
-quasiconformal as the graphs
G_{i}
have measure
0
.
∎

We now prove Proposition
3.6
.

Proof.

Let
S=S_{g}
with

and let
\Phi\in\mathrm{CAut}\mathcal{QC}^{\dagger}(S)
.
By Proposition

,
\Phi
is induced by a homeomorphism
\varphi
of

.

Since
K

As

so that

-quasicircle for every

-quasicircle

Uniformize

so that the universal cover of

We may lift

To verify quasiconformality of

Now let

6.
Hyperbolicity

In this section, we prove that the quasicircle graph is hyperbolic (Theorem
B
).
This is a fundamental property for graphs acted on by groups in geometric-group-theoretic applications.
The proof closely follows the proof of hyperbolicity of the fine curve graph by Bowden–Hensel–Webb
[
14
]
, however, minor changes are required when working with quasicircles.

We will define hyperbolicity via the four point condition: for points
x,y,w
of a metric space
(X,d)
, the Gromov product is defined to be

\langle x,y\rangle_{w}=\frac{1}{2}(d(w,x)+d(w,y)-d(x,y)).

The space
X
is said to be
\delta
-hyperbolic if for all
w,x,y,z\in X
,

\langle x,z\rangle_{w}\geq\min\{\langle x,y\rangle_{w},\langle y,z\rangle_{w}\}-\delta.

We give all graphs metrics by setting each edge to have length equal to 1.

Throughout this section, let
S
denote a closed oriented surface of genus
g\geq 2
.
First, we show that the quasicircle graph is path connected.

Proposition 6.1
.

\mathcal{QC}^{\dagger}(S)
is path connected.

Proof.

Notice that there is a simplicial map
\mathcal{QC}^{\dagger}(S)\to\mathcal{C}(S)
given by taking isotopy classes. Thus, it suffices to show that for any vertex of
\mathcal{C}(S)
, the subgraph of
\mathcal{QC}^{\dagger}(S)
spanned by its preimage is connected.
This follows directly from the existence of a path between homotopic quasicircles in Lemma
4.10
. We note that the quality constants of the vertices in such a path may be larger than the quality constants of its endpoints.
∎

We now introduce the
surviving curve graph
, which is a subgraph of the curve graph.
The distance in the surviving curve graph models distance in the quasicircle graph, which helps us prove the quasicircle graph is hyperbolic.

Definition 6.2

(Surviving curve graph)
.

Let
n\geq 0
and
\Sigma
be the surface of genus
g\geq 1
with
n
punctures. The
surviving curve graph
of the surface
\Sigma
, denoted
\mathcal{C}^{s}(\Sigma)
, is the
subgraph of
\mathcal{C}(\Sigma)
spanned by all vertices corresponding to curves that continue to be essential
in the closed surface obtained by filling in the punctures of
\Sigma
.

Work of Rasmussen shows that the surviving curve graph is
\delta
-hyperbolic
[
29
]
:

Theorem 6.3
.

Let
\Sigma
be a finite-type surface with positive genus. Then
there is a number
\delta>0
such that
\mathcal{C}^{s}(\Sigma)
is
\delta
-hyperbolic.

The following two lemmas, found in Bowden–Hensel–Webb
[
14
, Lemmas 3.5 & 3.6]
in the context of all curves, allow us to prove that distance in the surviving curve graph and distance in the quasicircle graph are equivalent.
Both proofs only require a minor modification to account for the quality constants of the quasicircles.
In particular, when applying isotopies and perturbations to curves in the proofs, we note that the quality constants of the quasicircles may increase.

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
P\subset S
be a finite set.
Suppose that
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}
are curves that are pairwise in minimal position in
S-P
. Let
\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{m}
be
K_{i}
-quasicircles that are disjoint from
P
.
Then the
\beta_{i}
can be isotoped to
K_{i}^{\prime}
-quasicircles
\beta_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,\beta_{m}^{\prime}
in
S-P
such that
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n},\beta_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,\beta_{m}^{\prime}
are
pairwise in minimal position in
S-P
.

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
\alpha,\beta\in\mathcal{QC}^{\dagger}(S)
and
P\subset S
be a finite set. Then we may find
a geodesic
\alpha=v_{0},\ldots,v_{k}=\beta
such that
v_{i}\cap P=\emptyset
for all
0<i<k
.

Notice that Lemma
6.4
shows that the distance in the surviving curve graph is an upper bound for the distance in the quasicircle graph, and Lemma
6.5
shows that the distance in the surviving curve graph is a lower bound for the distance in the quasicircle graph, giving us the following:

Lemma 6.6
.

Suppose that
\alpha,\beta\in\mathcal{QC}^{\dagger}(S)
intersect transversely, and that
\alpha
and
\beta

are in minimal position in
S-P
where
P\subset S
is finite and disjoint from
\alpha

and
\beta
. Then

d_{\mathcal{C}^{s}(S-P)}([\alpha]_{S-P},[\beta]_{S-P})=d_{\mathcal{QC}^{\dagger}(S)}(\alpha,\beta)
.

Using that the distance between two quasicircles in the surviving curve graph is equivalent to their distance in the quasicircle graph, we are now in a position to prove that the quasicircle graph is hyperbolic.

Proof of Theorem
B
.

Let
\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\mu\in\mathcal{QC}^{\dagger}(S)
. We will show that

\langle\alpha,\gamma\rangle_{\mu}\geq\min\{\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle_{\mu},\langle\beta,\gamma\rangle_{\mu}\}-\delta-4,

where
\delta
is the hyperbolicity constant for
\mathcal{C}^{s}(S-P)
. To do this we construct vertices
\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime},\delta^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}^{s}(S-P)
which are all (i) transverse, and such that (ii)
d_{\mathcal{QC}^{\dagger}(S)}(\alpha,\alpha^{\prime})\leq 1
,
d_{\mathcal{QC}^{\dagger}(S)}(\beta,\beta^{\prime})\leq 1
,
d_{\mathcal{QC}^{\dagger}(S)}(\gamma,\gamma^{\prime})\leq 1
.
Set
\mu=\mu^{\prime}
.
To ensure (i) holds, we first find
\alpha^{\prime\prime}
disjoint from and isotopic to
\alpha
, and we then apply a sufficiently small perturbation to
\alpha^{\prime\prime}
so that (ii) holds.
We note that the quality constant of
\alpha^{\prime}
may be larger than the quality constant
\alpha
.
We repeat the process for
\beta
and
\gamma
.
We then choose a finite subset of points
P\subset S
so that any bigon between
\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime},
and
\mu^{\prime}
contains a point of
P
, which ensures each of
\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime},
and
\mu^{\prime}
are in minimal position.
Applying Lemma
6.6
and Theorem
6.3
proves our claim.

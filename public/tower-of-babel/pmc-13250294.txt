Impact of Sodium-Glucose Cotransporter 2 Inhibitors on Outcomes After Transcatheter Aortic Valve Replacement: A Real-World Propensity-Matched Analysis
Authors: Salman Zahid, Muhammad Ahmad, Fnu Salman, Firas Zahr, Harsh Golwala
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundSodium-glucose cotransporter 2 inhibitors (SGLT2i) have shown cardiovascular and renal benefits across various populations. However, their impact on outcomes after transcatheter aortic valve replacement (TAVR) remains unclear.MethodsWe analyzed 61,763 adults who underwent TAVR between 2013 and 2024 using the TriNetX Research Network. Patients prescribed SGLT2i before or within 1 month of TAVR were compared to nonusers. After 1:1 propensity score matching (n = 3362 per group), outcomes including mortality, hospitalization, heart failure exacerbation, acute kidney injury, genitourinary (GU) infections, renal replacement therapy, and adverse events were evaluated at 6 months, 1 year, and 3 years.ResultsAmong 61,763 individuals who underwent TAVR, 3529 (5.7%) received an SGLT2i either before or within 1 month following the procedure. SGLT2i use was associated with significantly lower mortality at all time points (hazard ratio: 0.70; 95% CI: 0.62–0.79; p < 0.001). Hospitalization and heart failure exacerbation were also reduced in the SGLT2i group. No significant differences were observed in rates of acute kidney injury, GU infections, or renal replacement therapy. Subgroup analyses showed consistent benefits among patients with diabetes, chronic kidney disease, and left ventricular ejection fraction <45%.ConclusionsSGLT2i use around the time of TAVR was linked to improved survival and reduced heart failure–related morbidity, without increased renal or GU complications. These findings support a potential role for SGLT2i as adjunctive therapy in selected TAVR patients.

Introduction

Transcatheter aortic valve replacement (TAVR) has transformed the treatment landscape for severe aortic stenosis, particularly in elderly patients and those at elevated surgical risk. Its minimally invasive nature and expanding indications have contributed to improved procedural accessibility and outcomes. Nevertheless, post-TAVR morbidity and mortality remain significantly influenced by patients’ comorbid conditions and the optimization of adjunct medical therapies.1,2

Sodium-glucose cotransporter 2 inhibitors (SGLT2i) have emerged as a novel class of therapeutic agents initially developed for glycemic control in type 2 diabetes mellitus (DM).3 However, their benefits extend beyond glucose lowering.4 Numerous randomized controlled trials and meta-analyses have demonstrated that SGLT2i confer cardiovascular and renal protective effects, including reductions in heart failure hospitalization, cardiovascular mortality, and progression of chronic kidney disease (CKD).5, 6, 7, 8 These benefits have been observed across populations with heart failure with reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF) and heart failure with preserved ejection fraction, regardless of diabetic status.9, 10, 11

Given the high burden of heart failure, diabetes, and renal dysfunction among patients undergoing TAVR, the potential for SGLT2i to favorably influence perioperative and short-term outcomes warrants investigation. Despite the biological plausibility and evidence from heart failure populations, limited real-world data exist evaluating the impact of SGLT2i in the TAVR setting.

In this study, we analyze a large, real-world electronic health records database to compare clinical outcomes following TAVR among patients treated with SGLT2i versus those not receiving these agents.

Methods

Data Source and Study Population

We conducted a retrospective cohort study using the TriNetX Research Network, a global federated health research database aggregating deidentified electronic medical records from multiple health systems. Ethical approval was not warranted as deidentified publicly available data were used and the study adhered to STROBE (Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology) guidelines. The database has been utilized to compare and analyze data across diverse patient populations and clinical characteristics, enabling large-scale observational studies and real-world evidence generation.12,13 Adult patients (≥18 years) who underwent TAVR between June 2013 and June 2024 were identified using the International Classification of Diseases (ICD), 10th Revision, Procedure Coding System codes 02RF3XX, which have been well-documented in the literature for identifying patients undergoing TAVR.14,15 The cohort was stratified based on documented use of SGLT2i (empagliflozin, dapagliflozin, or canagliflozin). The individual codes used to identify TAVR and SGLT2i are detailed in Supplementary Table 1.

Exposure Definition

Patients were classified into 2 groups: (1) those with active SGLT2i use prior to or within 1 month after TAVR, and (2) those without SGLT2i use before or after TAVR. We also compared outcomes in patients with CKD (stage IV as defined by ICD-10 code: N18.4), DM, and patients with left ventricular ejection fractions (LVEF) below and above 45%, respectively (Fig. 1). According to the 2022 American Heart Association/American College of Cardiology/Heart Failure Society of America Heart Failure Guidelines and the 2024 ACC Expert Consensus Decision Pathway for the Management of HFrEF, SGLT2i may be initiated in patients with an estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) above 25 mL/min/1.73 m2, with empagliflozin in particular shown to be safe at even lower thresholds.16,17 Once initiated, therapy may generally be continued until eGFR declines below 15 mL/min/1.73 m2, as data in end-stage kidney failure (stage V) remain limited.17 In alignment with these recommendations, we defined the CKD subgroup as stage IV (eGFR 15–29 mL/min/1.73 m2), representing the range most consistent with current therapeutic use. Baseline characteristics, including demographics, comorbidities, laboratory values, and medication usage within 1 year prior to TAVR, were extracted and reported (Table 1). Outcomes

The primary outcome was all-cause mortality. Secondary outcomes included hospitalization, heart failure exacerbation (HFE), acute kidney injury (AKI), genitourinary (GU) infections, adverse events, and renal replacement therapy. All outcomes were assessed at 6 months, 1 year, and 3 years, respectively. Outcomes were identified by standardized diagnosis and procedure codes as defined in the TriNetX platform and detailed in Supplementary Table 2. The graphical abstract summarizes the study design, and key findings for both the SGLT2i and non-SGLT2i cohorts while a Consolidated Standards Of Reporting Trials-style flow diagram (Fig. 1) illustrates the patient selection process.

Statistical Analysis

Propensity score matching (PSM) was performed using a 1:1 greedy nearest-neighbor approach with a caliper of 0.1 to account for differences in baseline characteristics. Variables included in the propensity model were age, sex, race, body mass index, comorbid conditions (including heart failure, CKD, atrial fibrillation, and ischemic heart disease), baseline N-terminal pro-B-type natriuretic peptide, low-density lipoprotein cholesterol, LVEF, and use of certain medications, as detailed in Table 1.

After matching, standardized mean differences (SD) were calculated to assess balance between groups, with values < 0.1 indicating adequate balance. Categorical outcomes were compared using chi-square tests, and continuous variables were compared using t-tests or Wilcoxon rank-sum tests as appropriate. Kaplan–Meier curves and log-rank tests were used for time-to-event outcomes, and Cox proportional hazards models were constructed to estimate hazard ratios and 95% CIs.

All statistical analyses were performed using R statistical software available within the TriNetX platform. A two-sided p value <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Study Population and Baseline Characteristics

A total of 61,763 patients who underwent TAVR were included in the study. Among these, 3529 (5.7%) patients were receiving SGLT2i prior to or within 1 month after TAVR, and 55,219 patients were not (Fig. 1). Prior to matching, significant baseline differences were noted between groups (Supplementary Table 3). Patients receiving SGLT2i were younger (mean age 75.0 ± 8.7 vs. 78.4 ± 8.6 years; SD = 0.391) and more likely to be male (64.0 vs. 53.7%; SD = 0.210). The SGLT2i group also had a higher prevalence of comorbid conditions including CKD (38.7 vs. 28.3%; SD = 0.221), systolic heart failure (37.5 vs. 14.8%; SD = 0.535), and cardiomyopathy (25.4 vs. 10.4%; SD = 0.398). Additionally, use of medications such as diuretics (72.1 vs. 47.3%; SD = 0.524) and angiotensin II inhibitors (38.8 vs. 18.6%; SD = 0.460) was more common among SGLT2i users.

After 1:1 PSM, 3362 patients were included in each group. Baseline characteristics were well-balanced, with standardized differences <0.1 for most variables (Table 1). Age, sex, race, cardiovascular comorbidities, and medication use were comparable between the matched cohorts (e.g., mean age at index: 75.4 ± 8.4 vs. 75.3 ± 10.7 years; SD = 0.006; female sex: 32.7 vs. 32.9%; SD = 0.003). Physiological variables such as LVEF (51.7% ± 16.3% vs. 52.5% ± 15.6%; SD = 0.054) and body mass index (30.7 ± 6.9 vs. 30.4 ± 7.1; SD = 0.047) were comparable. The SGLT2i cohort had a slightly lower systolic blood pressure (119.7 ± 24.9 mmHg vs. 123.7 ± 22.7 mmHg; SD = 0.169) and N-terminal pro-B-type natriuretic peptide level (5242.9 ± 8265.5 vs. 6246.2 ± 10691.5; SD = 0.105).

Outcomes

Before Matching

Before PSM, patients receiving SGLT2i differed significantly from nonusers in several baseline characteristics. Unadjusted clinical outcomes are summarized in Supplementary Table 4 Briefly, mortality rates were comparable between groups at 6 months and 1 year but modestly lower among SGLT2i users at 3 years (13.1 vs. 17.4%; p = 0.043). Adverse events, HFE, AKI, and GU infections occurred more frequently in the SGLT2i cohort, likely reflecting baseline comorbidity differences. No significant differences were observed in hospitalizations or renal replacement therapy.

After Matching

Following matching, clinical outcomes favored SGLT2i use across multiple domains (Fig. 2). All-cause mortality was significantly lower in the SGLT2i group at 6 months (5.3 vs. 7.0%; p = 0.004), 1 year (8.1 vs. 11.1%; p < 0.001), and 3 years (13.2 vs. 19.4%; p < 0.001) (Table 2). Rates of hospitalization were also significantly reduced at 6 months (34.2 vs. 40.7%), 1 year (39.1 vs. 45.6%), and 3 years (45.9 vs. 52.1%) with p values <0.001 at each interval. Similarly, HFE rates were consistently lower among patients receiving SGLT2i. For example, HFE occurred in 29.4 vs. 33.0% at 6 months (p = 0.001), 33.5 vs. 36.7% at 1 year (p = 0.004), and 39.1 vs. 41.9% at 3 years (p = 0.011). No significant differences were observed in the incidence of AKI, renal replacement therapy, or GU infections. Subgroups

Chronic Kidney Disease

After PSM, 309 patients in each matched cohort had a diagnosis of CKD stage IV. Outcomes generally favored SGLT2i therapy, though most differences did not reach statistical significance (Table 3). At 6 months, no statistically significant differences could be observed between SGLT2i users and nonusers in terms of mortality (13.3 vs. 16.5%; p = 0.213), AKI (14.9 vs. 14.1%; p = 0.648), hospitalization (48.5 vs. 51.8%; p = 0.221), GU infections (16.5 vs. 16.2%; p = 0.986), renal replacement therapy (10.7 vs. 13.7%; p = 0.347), or HFE (45.3 vs. 47.9%; p = 0.278). Diabetes Mellitus

After PSM, 2514 patients in each cohort with DM were compared. Mortality was significantly lower with SGLT2i at all time points (3 years: 14.1 vs. 21.6%; p < 0.001) (Table 3). Similarly, hospitalization rates were reduced (3 years: 48.9 vs. 54%; p < 0.001). No differences could be observed in the incidence of AKI and GU infections. The incidence of renal replacement therapy was statistically lower with SGLT2i at 6 months (1.8 vs. 3%; p = 0.014), but not at later time points.

LVEF <45%

We also compared 272 patients in each cohort with LVEF <45% (Table 3). In patients with reduced ejection fraction, SGLT2i therapy was associated with a substantial reduction in the incidence of mortality (3 years: 18.8 vs. 36.8%; p < 0.001) and renal replacement therapy (3 years: 3.9 vs. 9.4%; p = 0.002) at all time points. HFE was lower in the SGLT2i cohort at all time points, however, the difference was significant only at 6 months (43.4 vs. 50.4%; p = 0.045). Notably, hospitalization rates were numerically lower in the SGLT2i cohort at 6 months and 1 year; however, the difference abated at 3 years. No differences could be observed in the incidence of AKI, GU infection, or adverse events.

LVEF ≥45%

Among patients with LVEF >45%, SGLT2i use was associated with a reduced incidence of mortality (3 years: 12.2 vs. 20.4%; p < 0.001), hospitalization (3 years: 47.8 vs. 56.2%; p = 0.001), and HFE (3 years: 38.8 vs. 43.9%; p = 0.048) at all time points (Table 3). The incidence of renal replacement therapy was significantly in the SGLT2i cohort at 6 months; however, statistical significance did not persist upon long-term follow-up. Other outcomes, including GU infections, AKI, and adverse events, were comparable between groups (p > 0.05 for all).

Discussion

In this propensity-matched analysis of patients undergoing TAVR, SGLT2i use was associated with significantly improved clinical outcomes. SGLT2i-treated patients experienced lower all-cause mortality and fewer hospitalizations compared to matched controls not on SGLT2i. These benefits emerged early (by 6 months post-TAVR) and persisted—in fact, the absolute risk reduction grew over time. For instance, all-cause mortality in the SGLT2i group was already lower at 6 months (5.3 vs. 7.0%), and the gap widened by 3 years (13.2 vs. 19.4%). Similarly, the composite outcome and HFE rates were consistently lower among patients receiving SGLT2i.

Importantly, these favorable outcomes were achieved without a corresponding increase in adverse events. Across the matched cohort, there were no significant differences in AKI, the need for renal replacement therapy, or GU infections. This safety profile is consistent with mechanistic and clinical evidence suggesting that SGLT2i may in fact exert renoprotective effects in high-risk patients.6,18,19 Despite the well-established cardioprotective benefits of SGLT2i, their utilization among TAVR recipients remains strikingly low.20 In our cohort, only 5.7% of patients received an SGLT2i, consistent with prior reports showing prescription rates ranging from 1 to 20%.21, 22, 23 Given that these agents now hold Class I indications for diabetes, CKD, and heart failure across the ejection fraction spectrum, this underuse represents a missed therapeutic opportunity. Clinicians should actively evaluate all eligible patients for SGLT2i therapy to optimize long-term cardiovascular and renal outcomes in this high-risk population.23

Our results align closely with those of the recent dapagliflozin after transcatheter aortic valve implantation (DAPA-TAVI) randomized trial,24 while also extending the insights from that study. DAPA-TAVI was the first randomized trial of an SGLT2i (dapagliflozin) in patients with valvular heart disease. In that trial, dapagliflozin initiated shortly after TAVR led to a 28% relative risk reduction in the primary composite outcome (all-cause death or worsening heart failure) at 1 year. This benefit was driven mainly by fewer heart failure hospitalizations/urgent visits in the SGLT2i arm (9.4 vs. 14.4%). The observed rate of HFE in our cohort (∼35% at 3 years) was higher than that reported in randomized TAVR trials. This discrepancy likely reflects differences in event ascertainment, as TriNetX relies on electronic health record coding rather than adjudicated endpoints. We used HFE as a surrogate to compare heart failure hospitalizations among the 2 cohorts, as data regarding heart failure hospitalizations were not directly available in the database. Such coding-based definitions may capture a broader spectrum of heart failure–related encounters, thereby inflating event rates relative to rigorously monitored trials. Additionally, the real-world TAVR population included in TriNetX had a higher comorbidity burden, which may further contribute to elevated event frequencies. Notably, DAPA-TAVI did not demonstrate a significant difference in all-cause mortality at 1 year (7.8 vs. 8.9%), whereas our real-world analysis shows a mortality benefit that becomes apparent over a longer time frame. Our findings are in congruence with another observational study by Paolisso et al., which reported that in diabetic patients with LVEF <50% undergoing TAVR, SGLT2i use was associated with significant reduction in mortality at both 1- and 2-year follow-up.25 This suggests that the mortality effect of SGLT2i therapy may require sustained treatment and follow-up to manifest.

The consistency of benefit observed in our study across a heterogeneous TAVR population suggests that SGLT2i may have wide applicability, extending to patients with diverse baseline characteristics. Among patients with stage IV CKD, although early differences in clinical outcomes were not statistically significant, a mortality benefit emerged by 1 year (17.8 vs. 24.3%) and persisted through 3 years (27.8 vs. 39.2%). This suggests that even in advanced CKD, where therapeutic options are often limited, SGLT2i may offer long-term benefit without compromising safety.

In patients with DM, SGLT2i therapy was associated with significantly reduced incidence of mortality, hospitalization, and HFE across all time points, reinforcing their utility in this well-established indication.6,26 Notably, the renal safety profile remained favorable, with no significant increase in AKI or GU infections.

Patients with reduced ejection fraction (LVEF <45%) derived substantial benefit from SGLT2i therapy, with marked reductions in all-cause mortality (18.8 vs. 36.8%) and renal replacement therapy (3.9 vs. 9.4%). Conversely, SGLT2i therapy was also associated with reduced mortality (12.2 vs. 20.4%), fewer hospitalizations and HFE in patients with preserved ejection fraction (LVEF ≥45%). These findings suggest that the benefits of SGLT2i extend beyond traditionally recognized populations and support their use in both heart failure with preserved ejection fraction and HFrEF phenotypes post-TAVR.10,27,28

Moreover, although DAPA-TAVI noted higher rates of hypotension and GU infections, we did not observe a significant increase in these events after matching. This could reflect differences in monitoring, patient selection, or event capture in real-world data versus trial settings. In clinical trials, adverse event monitoring is highly rigorous, often capturing even minor or asymptomatic events, whereas real-world studies tend to record only clinically significant outcomes, and some adverse events may go unreported.29 Importantly, these adverse events are typically manageable and rarely require discontinuation of therapy. Both hypotension and GU infections associated with SGLT2i use are generally mild to moderate in severity and can be effectively treated with supportive care or temporary medication adjustments.30 The safety profile of SGLT2i is well established by numerous studies across a range of high-risk populations.30,31

Mechanistically, SGLT2i offer several benefits particularly relevant to post-TAVR physiology. Despite mechanical relief of outflow obstruction, many TAVR patients continue to experience persistent myocardial fibrosis, elevated filling pressures, and comorbid heart failure.32,33 SGLT2i confer diuretic-like effects, improve myocardial energy utilization, reduce oxidative stress and inflammation, and promote favorable cardiac remodeling—all of which contribute to sustained benefits.8, 9, 10,32

The clinical implications of our findings are profound. They support the integration of SGLT2i into post-TAVR pharmacotherapy across diverse patient populations particularly patients with diabetes, heart failure, and CKD. Our real-world evidence complements the randomized DAPA-TAVI data and offers external validity, reinforcing the therapeutic role of SGLT2i as standard adjunctive therapy. With mounting evidence of benefit and a favorable safety profile, clinicians should strongly consider initiating SGLT2i therapy during or shortly after the TAVR index hospitalization.

The findings of this study should be interpreted in the context of several limitations inherent to retrospective analyses utilizing large administrative databases. First, we used ICD-10 diagnostic and procedural codes for identifying comorbidities and outcomes, which may introduce a potential for misclassification bias, as coding errors and interinstitutional variability in documentation practices may affect accuracy. Although a robust PSM approach was employed to minimize confounding, residual bias from unmeasured variables cannot be excluded. The TriNetX platform is an aggregated, deidentified database that lacks comprehensive access to granular clinical information, including echocardiographic parameters, medication dosing, and adherence, which limits the ability to perform detailed risk adjustment or mechanistic interpretation. Additionally, TriNetX does not specify the cause of hospitalization; thus, direct ascertainment of heart failure–specific admissions was not possible. To address this, we utilized HFE events as a surrogate outcome. While HFE is broadly representative of heart failure hospitalizations and has been used in prior database studies, it may not perfectly reflect all true heart failure–related admissions. Moreover, event ascertainment in TriNetX is based on electronic health record coding rather than adjudicated trial endpoints, which may lead to higher event capture compared with randomized clinical trials. Despite these limitations, the large, multi-institutional sample size, real-world setting, and consistent effect estimates across multiple outcomes strengthen the validity and generalizability of our findings.

Conclusion

SGLT2i are associated with significant reductions in mortality, HFE, and hospitalization among patients undergoing TAVR. The benefit increases over time, underscoring the value of long-term therapy. These results, aligned with and extending beyond the DAPA-TAVI trial, support the incorporation of SGLT2i into routine post-TAVR care pathways.

Verification of Linear Dynamical Systems via O-Minimality of the Real Numbers
Toghrul Karimov

Abstract
A discrete-time linear dynamical system (LDS) is given by an update matrix M ∈ ℝ d × d M\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d} , and has the trajectories ⟨ s , M ​ s , M 2 ​ s , … ⟩ \langle s,Ms,M^{2}s,\ldots\rangle for s ∈ ℝ d s\in\mathbb{R}^{d} . Reachability-type decision problems of linear dynamical systems, most notably the Skolem Problem, lie at the forefront of decidability: typically, sound and complete algorithms are known only in low dimensions, and these rely on sophisticated tools from number theory and Diophantine approximation. Recently, however, o-minimality has emerged as a counterpoint to these number-theoretic tools that allows us to decide certain modifications of the classical problems of LDS without any dimension restrictions. In this paper, we first introduce the Decomposition Method, a framework that captures all applications of o-minimality to decision problems of LDS that are currently known to us. We then use the Decomposition Method to show decidability of the Robust Safety Problem (restricted to bounded initial sets) in arbitrary dimension: given a matrix M M , a bounded semialgebraic set S S of initial points, and a semialgebraic set T T of unsafe points, it is decidable whether there exists ε > 0 \varepsilon>0 such that all orbits that begin in the ε \varepsilon -ball around S S avoid T T .

Introduction

Linear dynamical systems (LDS) are mathematical models widely used in engineering and sciences to describe systems that evolve over time.
A

M
.
The

under

(M^{n}s)_{n\in{\rm Nature}}

In formal verification, the most prominent decision problem of linear dynamical systems is the (point-to-set) Reachability Problem: given

In terms of program verification, this is equivalent to the

The restriction of the Termination Problem to

The Skolem Problem is known to be decidable

Theorem 1
.

Let
M\in(\mathbb{R}\cap\overline{\mathbb{Q}})^{d\times d}
,
S\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be non-empty, semialgebraic and bounded, and

be semialgebraic.
Further let

\displaystyle\mu_{1}

\displaystyle=\sup\,\{\varepsilon\geq 0\colon M^{n}\cdot B(S,\varepsilon)\textrm{ does not intersect $T$ for all $n$}\},

\displaystyle\mu_{2}

\displaystyle=\sup\,\{\varepsilon\geq 0\colon M^{n}\cdot B(S,\varepsilon)\textrm{ does not intersect $T$ for all sufficiently large $n$}\}.

Then
\mu_{1}\in\mathbb{R}\cap\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
,
\mu_{2}\in(\mathbb{R}\cap\overline{\mathbb{Q}})\cup\{\infty\}
and is effectively computable,
\mu_{1}

\mu_{1}=0
.
Moreover, for every
\varepsilon\in(0,\mu_{2})\cap\mathbb{Q}

T
.

Intuitively,

are the best possible margin of safety and “asymptotic safety”, respectively.
From
Theorem

1

\mu_{1}=0
.
For positive instances, i.e. when
\mu_{1}>0
, we can compute
\varepsilon\geq 0

\mu_{1}
from below.
Finally, for every positive

T

For
\varepsilon\in(0,\mu_{2})

For

We say that a matrix

A decomposition of

Lemma 2
.

Every
M\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
has a decomposition
(C,D)
.
If
M\in(\mathbb{R}\cap\overline{\mathbb{Q}})^{d\times d}
, then a decomposition satisfying
C,D\in(\mathbb{R}\cap\overline{\mathbb{Q}})^{d\times d}

Proof.

Write
M=P^{-1}JP

3.3
Real exponentiation and o-minimality

We now develop the tools necessary for studying dynamics of scaling matrices.
The main results of this section are certain (effective) convergence arguments in o-minimal structures.

We say that a sequence
(Z_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

It is now understood that o-minimality gives us strong ergodic properties of linear dynamical systems, despite the fact that they are typically not compact.
This idea is pursued further in the recent work

The idea is to write

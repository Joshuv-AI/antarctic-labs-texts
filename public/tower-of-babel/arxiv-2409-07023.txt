Abstract
As the dual notion of projective modules over trusses, injective modules over trusses are introduced. The Schanuel Lemmas on projective and injective modules over trusses are exhibited in this paper. In 2019, trusses were introduced by T. Brzezi

Preliminaries

A heap is a set
H
together with a ternary operation

[---]:H\times H\times H\rightarrow H
which is associative and satisfies the Mal’cev identities, that is,

[[a,b,c],d,e]=[a,b,[c,d,e]]\qquad\text{and}\qquad[a,b,b]=a=[b,b,a]

for all
a,b,c,d,e\in H
.
A heap
H
is said to be abelian if for all
a,b,c\in H
,
[a,b,c]=[c,b,a]
.

A heap morphism from
(H,[---])
to
(H^{\prime},[---])
is a function
f:H\rightarrow H^{\prime}
respecting the ternary operations, i.e., such that for all
x
,
y
,
z\in H
,
f([x,y,z])=[f(x),f(y),f(z)]
.
The category of heaps is denoted by Heap and the category of abelian heaps is denoted by Ah. A singleton set
\{\ast\}
with the unique heap operation
[\ast,\ast,\ast]=\ast
, it is the terminal objective in the category of heaps, we denote it by
\star
. The empty set is the initial object. There is no zero objective in the category of heaps.

With every group
G
we can associate a heap
H(G)=(G,[-,-,-])
where
[a,b,c]=ab^{-1}c
for all
a,b,c\in G
and every morphism of group is automatically a morphism of heaps. With every non-empty heap
H
and for a fixed
e\in H
, we can associate a group
G(H;e)
and the binary operation is
a\cdot b=[a,e,b]
for all
a,b\in H
. The inverse of
a\in G(H;e)
is
a^{-1}=[e,a,e]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
8
, Lemma 2.3]

Let
(H,[-,-,-])
be a heap.

(1)

If

(2)

For all

(3)

For all

[x,y,[y,x,z]]=[[z,x,y],y,x]=[x,[y,z,x],y]=z.

In particular, in the expression

(4)

If

is abelian, then, for all

,

[[x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}],[y_{1},y_{2},y_{3}],[z_{1},z_{2},z_{3}]]=[[x_{1},y_{1},z_{1}],[x_{2},y_{2},z_{2}],[x_{3},y_{3},z_{3}]].

A subset
S
of a heap
H
that is closed under the heap operation is called a sub-heap of
H
. Every non-empty sub-heap
S
of an abelian heap
H
defines a congruence relation
\sim_{S}
on

a\sim_{S}b\quad\iff\quad\exists~s\in S,\ [a,b,s]\in S\quad\iff\quad\forall~s\in S,\ [a,b,s]\in S.

The equivalence classes of
\sim_{S}
form an abelian heap with operation induced from that in

. Namely,

[\bar{a},\bar{b},\bar{c}]=\overline{[a,b,c]}
, where
\bar{x}
denotes the class of

in
H/\sim_{S}
for all

. This is known as the

and it is denoted by

.
For any

is equal to

.

If
\varphi:H\rightarrow K
is a morphism of abelian heaps, then for all

, the set

\ker_{e}\varphi:=\{a\in H\;|\;\varphi(a)=e\}

is a sub-heap of

. Different choices of

yielding an isomorphic as heaps and the quotient heap
H/\ker_{e}\varphi
does not depend on the choice of
e
. Moreover, the sub-heap relation
\sim_{\ker_{e}}\varphi
is the same as the kernel relation defined by:
a\,\mbox{\rm Ker}\,\varphi\,b
if and only if
\varphi(a)=\varphi(b)
. Thus we write
\mbox{\rm Ker}\,\varphi
for
\ker_{e}\varphi
and we refer to it as the kernel of

.

Definition 2.2
.

[
8
, Definition 3.1]

A truss is an algebraic system consisting of a set
T
, a ternary operation
[-,-,-]
making
T
into an Abelian heap, and an associative binary operation
\cdot
which distributes over
[-,-,-]

w[x,y,z]=[wx,wy,wz],\quad[x,y,z]w=[xw,yw,zw].

A truss is said to be commutative(abelian) if the binary operation
\cdot
is commutative.

A heap homomorphism between two trusses is a truss homomorphism if it respects multiplications. The category of trusses and their morphisms is denoted by
{\rm Trs}
.

Let

T
-module is an abelian heap

t\cdot(t^{\prime}\cdot m)=(tt^{\prime})\cdot m,

(2.1)

[t,t^{\prime},t^{\prime\prime}]\cdot m=[t\cdot m,t^{\prime}\cdot m,t^{\prime\prime}\cdot m],

t\cdot[m,m^{\prime},m^{\prime\prime}]=[t\cdot m,t\cdot m^{\prime},t\cdot m^{\prime\prime}].

If
T
is a unital truss and the action satisfies

, then we say that

is a unital or normalised module. A submodule of a left
T
-module

A module homomorphism is a homomorphism of heaps between two modules that also respects the actions. As it is customary in ring theory we often refer to homomorphisms of

An element

The set of all absorbers in

Comment on “Low‐Energy Shockwave Therapy for Interstitial Cystitis/Bladder Pain Syndrome From Bench to Clinic” by Chia‐Hung Tsai, Yuan‐Chi Shen, Hung‐Jen Wang, Wei‐Chia Lee, Cheng‐Yen Kao and Yao‐Chi Chuang
Authors: Jean‐Jacques Wyndaele
Published: 2026

Interesting paper that put together the animal experiments and limited human results in small groups of patients. There are ample arguments that low‐energy shockwave (LESW) therapy can give positive results. Experiments have shown favorable effects on the pathologic bladder wall. Clinical improvements were obtained in around 60%, which is in the line of many other treatments. A proper algorithm of which treatment to choose for an individual patient is still missing but would be of great value. While the primary effects of LESW are localized, there is some evidence to suggest that it may have central effects as well. These central effects could be related to the modulation of pain pathways in the nervous system. For example, the application of shock waves may lead to the release of various growth factors and cytokines, which can influence pain perception and healing processes both locally and centrally. Additionally, some studies have indicated that shock wave therapy may have effects on the central nervous system, potentially influencing pain processing and perception. However, the extent and mechanisms of these central effects are still being researched, and more studies are needed to fully understand how LESW might impact central pain pathways.

In summary, while LESW primarily has localized effects, there is potential for central effects that warrant further investigation. The treatment seems promising and warrants further testing.

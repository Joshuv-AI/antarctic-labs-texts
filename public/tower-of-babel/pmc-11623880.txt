Temporal Incision Super-high Superficial Musculoaponeurotic System Face Lift for Chinese Patients
Authors: Jingang Wang, Lining Xie, Ning Zhang, Lijuan Hu, Siqing Zhang, Jingchun Liu, Xihua Wang, Xiaofeng Yan
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Background:This investigation assessed patient satisfaction with a temporal incision technique using a super-high superficial musculoaponeurotic system (SMAS) flap for midface lifting in a Chinese patient cohort.Methods:From July 2019 to July 2023, 95 patients underwent midface lifts via a temporal incision approach at our institution. The extent of SMAS flap dissection spanned 2 cm above the outer canthus, to the lower margin of the zygomatic arch inferiorly, and medially to the zygomaticus major muscle. The technique involved anchoring the elevated SMAS flap to the deep temporal fascia to elevate the malar fat pads, correct sagging tissues, and ameliorate the appearance of nasolabial folds.Results:Among the 95 patients, 61 completed a follow-up ranging from 6 months to 2 years postsurgery, with a majority reporting satisfaction with the aesthetic results of their midface lift. No significant complications, including permanent facial nerve injury, were reported.Conclusions:The temporal incision super-high SMAS face lift technique demonstrated high patient satisfaction and clinical effectiveness in improving the appearance of nasolabial fold and midface aesthetics. However, the success of this technique hinges on a comprehensive understanding of facial anatomy due to the confined operative field, highlighting its technical demands and the need for surgical expertise.

Takeaways

Question: What is the effectiveness of a temporal incision and superficial musculoaponeurotic system (SMAS) flap elevation technique for midface sagging and nasolabial folds in a Chinese cohort?

Findings: We found that the temporal incision SMAS flap technique significantly enhances midface aesthetics, effectively lifts sagging tissues, diminishes nasolabial folds, and achieves high levels of patient satisfaction, with very few experiencing complications. However, success hinges on precise anatomical knowledge and surgical expertise.

Meaning: The temporal incision with SMAS flap elevation is an effective and safe method for midface rejuvenation that offers a promising option for patients seeking facial aesthetic enhancement with lasting results.

INTRODUCTION

Young individuals with pronounced nasolabial folds frequently perceive themselves as appearing older, believing that the deepening of these folds negatively impacts their facial expressions. As patient demands for enhanced facial aesthetics continue to rise, the management of nasolabial folds has become a subject of growing interest in the field of facial rejuvenation. Although filler injections remain the predominant treatment, their effects are temporary due to natural degradation and absorption, requiring repeated treatments for sustained results.1,2 Conversely, face lift procedures, which reposition facial structures and address tissue sagging, offer a more durable solution with a high success rate. This approach is particularly effective in correcting sagging of the malar fat pads and nasolabial folds. Traditional superficial musculoaponeurotic system (SMAS) lifting techniques, involving incisions in the temporal region, in front of the ear, through the earlobe, and behind the ear, often raise concerns about postoperative scarring among patients.3,4 To address these concerns, we have developed a technique that uses an incision in the temporal region for the creation of a super-high SMAS flap. The precision of this technique is facilitated by the clear boundaries established for the SMAS flap dissection, extending into the prezygomatic space. This strategic approach not only elevates the malar fat pads and improves sagging tissues but also significantly enhances the appearance of the nasolabial folds. In addition, for patients experiencing brow ptosis, this method enables the complete interruption of the periorbital ligament and subperiosteal elevation in the medial frontal area, effectively lifting the tail of the brow.5,6 The reduced length of the surgical incision tends to be more favorably received by patients.

PATIENTS AND METHODS

Participants

Clinical data were gathered from a case series conducted at the Affiliated Friendship Plastic Surgery Hospital of Nanjing Medical University. The study design involved a temporal incision super-high SMAS face lift procedure. Exclusion criteria included individuals with significant cardiovascular, cerebrovascular, or other systemic diseases precluding general anesthesia, patients with unrealistic postoperative expectations, long-term smokers, and those on anticoagulant therapy, such as aspirin, who were unable to discontinue use for more than 2 weeks before surgery. All participants signed a written consent form. A total of 95 patients were initially enrolled in the study from mainland China. At the follow-up assessments, 61 patients (5 men and 56 women, aged 26–50 years, median age 37.8 years) were evaluated. All the included patients completed the Global Aesthetic Improvement Scale before the surgery and during the final postoperative visit. The follow-up period was between 6 and 24 (median, 15.1) months. The same surgeon performed all the procedures between September 2018 and September 2022. All the procedures were performed under general anesthesia. This research study was approved by the Affiliated Friendship Plastic Surgery Hospital of Nanjing Medical University. The present study was conducted according to the standards for biomedical research on human subjects set by the Declaration of Helsinki. Before the surgery, all the patients were informed that their photographs could be published for scientific purposes. All the patients whose records were used in the present study have provided written informed consent (Table 1).

Surgical Procedure

The surgery was performed by Dr. Wang. Upon admission to the operating room, a 6-cm incision line was marked within the temporal hairline. (See Video 1 [online], which displays the surgical design program.) The area was sterilized with iodophor, and local anesthesia was administered using 0.02% lidocaine with 1:100,000 adrenaline. A wide skin dissection pocket was created, extending beyond the immediate area of the SMAS flap creation. This extensive skin dissection is imperative to accommodate the laterosuperior lifting direction of the SMAS, ensuring the composite flap formed does not induce uneven folds on the skin surface. This separation of the skin facilitates a smooth contour postlift, thereby avoiding fold formation and maintaining the integrity of the hairline. A line was drawn with a marker delineating the end of the eyebrow and the location of the lateral SMAS flap, which is positioned 1 cm anterior to the tragal region, strategically avoiding the main trunk of the superficial temporal artery. The medial extent of the flap is demonstrated to be 2 cm outside of the outer canthus, extending downward to the lower edge of the zygomatic arch. The dissection was performed carefully to spare the superficial temporal artery, situated in the deep layer of the superficial temporal fascia. We maintained the dissection plane above this vascular layer to protect the temporal branch of the facial nerve, which lies deep to this blood vessel. This approach not only ensures the safety of the facial nerve but also allows access to the lower edge of the zygomatic arch through the planned surgical plane. The dissection proceeded beneath the SMAS, guided by a lighted retractor. Advancing toward the midface, the zygomatic ligament was isolated through blunt dissection, revealing the orbicularis oculi muscle and the zygomaticus major muscle. If necessary, further dissection allowed for the elevation of the zygomatic fat pad and release of the medial attachments of the zygomaticus major muscle to enhance the mobility of the SMAS flap. The flap was then elevated and secured to the deep temporal fascia using a 3-0 polyester suture (Ningbo Cheng-he, China), ensuring reliable fixation. Subsequently, a drainage tube was placed beneath the flap. Closure used 5-0 absorbable sutures for layered intradermal stitching and 6-0 nylon for the skin, finished with erythromycin ointment application and pressure dressings (Fig. 1). Postoperative Evaluation

The patients’ satisfaction with the surgical procedure and outcomes was evaluated 6 months to 2 years postoperatively (median, 15.1 mo). Patient satisfaction was assessed using a validated tool, the Global Aesthetic Improvement Scale. The subjective satisfaction of each patient was graded as follows: exceptional improvement, very improved, improved, unaltered, and worsened. Facial improvements included effects on midface lift effectiveness and nasolabial fold mitigation. The same surgeon, Dr. Wang, performed all the procedures and assessed patient satisfaction during the postoperative visit.

RESULTS

A total of 95 patients were initially enrolled in the study from mainland China. At the follow-up assessments, 61 patients (5 men and 56 women, aged 26–50 years, median age 37.8 years) were evaluated. Of the 61 patients completing follow-up, 40 reported being very satisfied, 6 were satisfied, 9 found the results acceptable, 6 reported no improvement in nasolabial fold, and no worsened results were reported (Table 1). Discontent with outcomes led 2 patients to opt for a traditional, large-incision face lift 6 months later. Complications included 2 instances of frontal branch injury and 2 cases of postoperative hematoma. Both frontal branch injuries resolved within 6 months. Although these complications were not common, they were clinically significant and warrant mention. There were no reports of skin necrosis or other serious adverse effects (Figs. 2, 3).

DISCUSSION

In 1990, Hamra10 first introduced the “deep plane face lift,” a technique aimed at repositioning the malar fat pad to mitigate midface aging. This method is widely regarded as an effective strategy for rejuvenating the midface since the malar fat pad, which is situated atop the mimetic muscles, is implicated in midface sagging due to its substantial subcutaneous fat volume and distinct structure predisposed to aging and sagging.7–9 Among the few surgical approaches that address midface lifting, high SMAS face lifts play a critical role. They not only reposition the sagging malar fat pad but also significantly reduce the depth of nasolabial folds, thus serving as a critical technique in facial rejuvenation surgery.10 The anatomical differences between Asian and White facial structures, particularly in terms of the lower zygomatic arch and eminence, present unique challenges for traditional SMAS flap techniques for midface lifting in Asian populations. The classic approach, focusing on the area below the zygomatic arch, often fails to achieve the desired lifting effect in the midface due to these anatomical limitations.11–13 Our technique, involving a super-high SMAS flap through a temporal incision, aims to overcome these challenges. By positioning the upper margin of the SMAS incision at the end of the eyebrow, rather than at the outer canthus as in traditional methods, we ensure a more effective lift. However, this approach is contingent on a thorough understanding of the anatomy of the temporal region to avoid damaging the facial nerve branches.

The parotid temporal fascia, a layer of loose connective tissue extending from the parotid fascia across the zygomatic arch into the temporal region, plays a pivotal role in the surgical approach. After the temporal branch of the facial nerve emerges from the parotid gland’s superior border, it travels anteriorly, enshrouded by the parotid temporal fascia along the surface of the zygomatic arch.14–16 The loose connection between the superficial and parotid temporal fasciae facilitates the elevation of the SMAS flap through sharp separation, thereby safeguarding the temporal branch of the facial nerve. Effective lifting of the zygomatic SMAS, a challenging area due to ligamentous restrictions, requires meticulous dissection of the zygomatic ligaments, particularly those over the zygomatic prominence. This dissection allows the force from the laterally separated super-high SMAS flap to be transmitted effectively to the midface, enhancing the lift. The surgical technique involves careful management of the operative area, including the use of tumescent to minimize bleeding and improve visibility, using a lighted pulling hook for direct visualization of the facial nerve branches, and using specific maneuvers to distinguish the facial nerve from the ligaments during SMAS flap elevation.17

When dissecting the zygomatic ligament intraoperatively, it is crucial to maintain the scissors vertically and to primarily use blunt dissection while pulling the fascial flap upward and forward to fully reveal the ligament. Care is taken to sever the attachment point snugly against the fascial flap to avoid injury to the zygomatic branch. After the detachment from the zygomaticus major muscle’s lateral border, the prezygomatic space is entered through the lower part of the outer orbicularis oris muscle. Next, the area under the malar fat pad is bluntly separated and loosened with the index finger to make it mobile. The malar fat pad is then elevated, and the SMAS flap is pulled using a finger-assisted technique, resulting in improvement of the nasolabial folds due to the repositioning of the malar fat pads.18–20 The super-high SMAS flap is carefully prepared, extending from the lateral canthus, downward past the inferior border of the zygomatic arch, and medially beyond the zygomaticus major muscle, using a temporal incision approach. This method demands surgical proficiency and a profound understanding of the anatomy of the medial temporal fascia within the temporal region. The procedure for creating a super-high SMAS flap underscores that the superficial temporal fascia flap becomes thinner as it progresses cephalad. In addition, the frontal branch of the facial nerve, originating from the parotid temporal fascia, becomes more superficial as it ascends, posing a risk of injury. Thus, surgeons must possess substantial experience in this dissection technique.15,21,22 For patients exhibiting significant skin laxity, although midface lifting effectively addresses skin laxity, the small incision limits the extent of skin that can be excised. This limitation may result in skin accumulation anterior to the ear or necessitate an incision extension for adequate skin removal. The primary benefits of this approach include its acceptability to patients, its suitability for younger individuals, and its efficacy in correcting mid to upper facial laxity through a minimally invasive incision, thereby achieving a discernible degree of enhancement. For situations where facial skin laxity is more pronounced, we suggest that large-incision surgical techniques be used. We advise surgeons to undertake super-high SMAS flap preparation via temporal incision only after gaining extensive experience with long-incision face lift procedures.18,19 This study, although it provides valuable insights into the efficacy of the temporal incision with SMAS flap elevation technique for midface lifting, has some notable limitations. First, all patients enrolled in the study were from mainland China. It is important to validate this technique for other races and capture more demographic details. Second, the study’s focus on a single surgical technique without comparisons to other midface lifting methods restricts our ability to definitively conclude its superiority or identify areas where alternative approaches might be more effective. Third, the lack of standardization of the time at which the satisfaction was assessed is a significant methodological weakness in the study. This limitation narrows the scope of our conclusions and highlights the need for future studies to be conducted to fully understand the benefits and drawbacks of the temporal incision SMAS flap technique relative to other available procedures.

CONCLUSIONS

Temporal incision face lifts are increasingly preferred by patients for their minimally invasive nature and shorter recovery time. This technique offers a promising alternative to traditional face lift methods, particularly for patients with mid to upper facial sagging. Future studies should aim to provide quantitative assessments and comparative analyses to further validate the technique’s efficacy and to delineate its scope of application.

Research on the value screening of combat rescue information based on the Markov decision tree model
Authors: Xiaoxue Li, Zhengping Li, Yuwen Hao, Weizhi Ma
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundIn future digital battlefields, the systematic collection of combat casualty care information is essential for improving trauma outcomes and guiding medical resource allocation. However, a significant methodological gap exists in determining which specific data points are most valuable to record when prospective wartime data are unavailable. Rather than constructing a physical information collection system, this study aims to develop a simulation-based screening framework that can identify and prioritize high-value examination and treatment information across the continuum of tactical combat casualty care.MethodsA Markov decision tree model was used to simulate the effects of different treatment techniques on injury-state transitions during casualty evacuation, spanning line-of-fire care, tactical medical care, and damage-control treatment at rescue institutions. Background

The critical lesson learned from the ordinary trauma treatment of international war wounds is to recognize the “importance of data-driven decision-making” [1, 2]. Specifically, to improve the quality of war wound treatment, it is first necessary to collect valuable information about war wound treatment in a systematic manner [3–5]. During the Vietnam War, the U.S. War Wound Data and Ammunition Effectiveness Database was first used for the systematic data collection on the battlefield. Although the system was not designed for medical purposes but rather to determine the pattern of injuries that result in the deaths of U.S. troops, it has become a pillar of the epidemiological study of modern war wounds. Although the United States has such a foundation and has gained a great deal of experience in the subsequent construction of civilian trauma systems, the US military has not yet discovered a successful data collection and recording method to support the enhancement of combat rescue capabilities. A unified information collection document was established to treat war wounds, making it possible to monitor the real-time status of the war wound treatment chain and standardize war wound treatment information collection [6]. Similarly, the United Kingdom and NATO established the Joint Theatre Trauma Registry (JTTR) and the Treatment Record Form. In practice, it has been demonstrated that wartime data collection and recording can help improve the quality and research of combat rescue. Moreover, research on the value of combat rescue information has been listed as the top priority research area for battlefield rescue and treatment in the US military in 2020 [7].

However, the mere existence of comprehensive trauma registries such as the JTTR does not resolve the fundamental challenge of forward data collection in tactical environments. These registries aggregate data retrospectively from multiple echelons, often relying on documentation practices that were not designed with the constraints of point-of-injury care in mind. In the chaos of the battlefield, medical personnel face severe limitations in time, cognitive bandwidth, and physical capacity to record information. Consequently, not all data elements collected in a mature registry are equally feasible or valuable to capture at the point of wounding. The critical gap, therefore, is not the absence of a database, but the lack of an evidence-based method to triage information itself—that is, to determine which data fields provide the greatest return on investment in terms of guiding care and predicting outcomes under tactical conditions. This study addresses that gap by providing a quantitative framework to screen and rank information items according to their simulated impact on mortality.

Despite the recognized need for robust data collection, a significant methodological gap exists: how can military medical planners determine which specific data points are the “most valuable” to record when prospective wartime data are unavailable? While existing registries like the JTTR collect extensive data, the design of field medical documentation often relies on expert consensus rather than quantitative evidence linking specific recorded items to casualty outcomes. This paper does not aim to build a physical information collection system or database. Instead, we propose a novel simulation-based decision-support model to screen and prioritize combat rescue information. The primary contribution of this work is the development of a Markov decision tree framework that quantifies the impact of examination and treatment techniques on injury state transitions. This model serves as a practical tool to identify critical information nodes in the chain of evacuation, thereby providing a theoretical foundation for designing streamlined, evidence-based battlefield medical records in the absence of real-world wartime data.

For the formulation of the medical evacuation documents, a systematic methodology is required to screen high-value information in the absence of our army's own wartime treatment data. We selected the Markov decision process framework for this task based on two key considerations. First, the progression of a combat casualty's physiological state is inherently stochastic and sequential, depending on the interventions applied at each echelon of care. The Markov model can use known information and the dynamic characteristics of event development to predict the evolution of the patient's condition [8–10]. The Markov property assumes that the next state is determined by the current state and the action taken at that moment. This assumption is suitable for trauma care because the casualty’s condition at each handoff strongly influences the subsequent treatment pathway and outcome. Second, existing applications of Markov models to disaster and emergency scenarios provide a methodological foundation for this work. Ni Yan and Zhao Lindu et al. used a Markov-based approach to describe the dynamic resource allocation process for differently injured patients following an earthquake, explicitly incorporating delay costs and penalty costs for treatment refusal [11]. Similarly, Friedrich et al. established a dynamic optimization model to minimize fatalities during the initial search and rescue phase after an earthquake [12]. These findings demonstrate the utility of Markov frameworks for minimizing adverse outcomes under uncertainty. However, these prior studies focus on the allocation of physical resources (e.g., ambulances, medical teams) and implicitly assume that all relevant clinical information is readily available and equally informative. Our work extends this foundation by shifting the focus from resource allocation to information prioritization, using the Markov framework to quantify how the availability and recording of specific data elements influence state transition dynamics and, ultimately, survival. This novel application provides a principled, evidence-based method for selecting which data fields merit inclusion in forward medical documentation.

Methods

Preliminary screening of treatment techniques

According to the preliminary survey results, a comprehensive multi-factor evaluation study was chosen. The evaluation indicators for examination items were divided into two categories. The first category was critical injury recognition, denoted as Cr. The “quick and easy” identification of critically injured people on the battlefield is primarily based on physiological data and special signs that are simple to collect and interpret at the scene. However, there are variations in the ability to recognize this information. The second category is environmental relevance evaluation. It primarily considers whether the inspection technology is suitable for deployment in the battlefield environment, whether the personnel involved in on-site treatment can perform it, whether the inspected equipment can be carried and deployed at the scene, and the inspection’s effective time in the field environment. Therefore, the inspection of the feasibility of implementation (Proportion of Examination Feasibility) is marked with Ef, the proportion of personnel capability is marked with Pc, the proportion of equipment portability is marked with Pp, and the effective time of checking once is marked with Ev.

The evaluation indicators for treatment items were also divided into two categories. The first category assessed the clinical attributes of treatment techniques according to the principle of graded trauma care. Technical urgency (Proportion of Technology Urgency), marked by Pu, is a measure of the importance of technology. “Immediate treatment” refers to injuries that are life-threatening but can be treated in a short amount of time, such as artificial airway technology and hemostasis technology [13, 14]. “Delayed treatment” refers to injuries that are serious but do not require immediate life-saving, such as pelvic or long bone fracture first aid technology [15, 16]. “Elective treatment” refers to the treatment technology for the lightly injured. Technical effectiveness is marked by Pv (Proportion of Technology Validity). “Emergency life” focuses on saving lives and then treating injuries; “Organ protection” focuses on severe trauma, stabilizes vital signs, protects organs, and prevents disability [17, 18]. “Recovery function” controls injuries efficiently and promotes functional recovery [19, 20]. The Proportion of Technology Safety is marked by Ps to examine the technical safety of operations based on the battlefield, such as burns and head injuries among the wounded. For example, although tracheostomy is an urgent and effective airway intervention, it is less safe than needle cricothyrotomy under battlefield conditions. The second category is environmental relevance evaluation, which is assessed using the same criteria as those for examination items. The proportion of personnel capability is marked by Pc, and Pp marks the proportion of equipment portability.

To obtain the importance parameters of the evaluation indicators above, the item importance form was added to the record sheet, and the degree of importance was marked with the numbers 1–10. The weight of the evaluation index for examination and treatment items was denoted by IMX.

Integration of the Markov process and decision tree

The proposed model is not a simple sequential application of two independent methods. Rather, the Markov state transition process is embedded as the state-evaluation kernel within the chance nodes of the decision tree framework. The decision tree defines the sequential decisions encountered during casualty evacuation—such as the selection of examination techniques at the point of injury, the choice between ground and air evacuation, and the prioritization of interventions at forward surgical facilities. At each decision node, the long-term consequences of a particular action are evaluated using the steady-state distribution derived from the Markov chain.

Mapping of treatment techniques to information value

To bridge the gap between the simulation of medical techniques and the screening of information value, we established a direct mapping protocol. The core assumption is that if a specific examination or treatment technique is identified as critical for reducing mortality in the simulation, the data elements describing the execution and outcome of that technique must be captured in the medical record. For instance, if the model identifies “Tourniquet Application” as a high-impact intervention for the on-site phase, the corresponding high-value information includes: Tourniquet Application Time, Anatomical Location, and Reassessment of Distal Pulse. Consequently, the final output of the Markov decision tree model is not merely a list of preferred techniques, but a validated set of information fields to be included in the battlefield medical evacuation document.

Cost-adjusted assessment of information value

In tactical environments, the value of an information field is determined not only by its clinical importance but also by the feasibility and cost of collecting it. Some data elements may be clinically informative but difficult to record under fire, during evacuation, or when personnel and equipment are limited. Incorporation of information collection cost

Given that several transition probabilities, recognition thresholds, and environment-related parameters in the model rely on expert-informed assumptions or historical data, sensitivity analysis is necessary to examine the robustness of the study conclusions. This study addresses uncertainty through a deterministic sensitivity analysis framework. The key parameters examined include: mortality-related transition probabilities, the ordinal assignment values in Tables 2 and 3, and the injury recognition thresholds , , and . During the analysis, the baseline value of each parameter was varied within a plausible range while all other parameters were held constant. Model stability was evaluated by observing whether the priority ranking of information fields changed, with particular attention paid to whether Level I fields—such as records for hemorrhage control, airway management, and needle decompression—remained in the highest-priority category under alternative parameter settings.

Because no prospectively collected battlefield dataset was available, this study did not estimate Bayesian posterior distributions or empirical confidence intervals for the transition probabilities. Consequently, the numerical transition probabilities presented herein should be interpreted as scenario-based estimates, and the primary conclusion of the study should be understood as a relative priority ranking of information fields, rather than a precise prediction of casualty mortality.

The simulation results indicate that injury states undergo substantial redistribution during the on-site treatment phase, whereas changes during ground and air evacuation were comparatively modest.

As depicted in Fig. 4, the on-site treatment process exhibits substantial redistribution of injury states during the early simulation steps. The emergency treatment probability increased rapidly from the initial level and then stabilized after approximately the third iteration. The delayed treatment probability showed a transient increase at the second iteration, followed by a gradual decline toward a steady level. In contrast, the minor injury treatment probability decreased sharply during the first two iterations and then declined slowly toward a stable state. This pattern indicates that some casualties initially classified as minor injuries were reclassified into higher-priority categories after the application of more discriminative examination and treatment information. The expectant treatment category increased gradually and then stabilized, suggesting that the model identified a subset of casualties whose injury severity remained high despite intervention.

In the ground evacuation process (Fig. 5), the distinctions between emergency treatment and minor injury treatment trajectories are not readily apparent, with both categories remaining closely aligned throughout the simulation. There is a visible decreasing trend in delayed treatment and an increasing trend in expectant treatment. Quantitatively, the expectant treatment probability rose from a negligible 0.002 to 0.048, a 24-fold increase, while delayed treatment declined from 0.130 to 0.100. The emergency and minor injury categories exhibited only marginal net changes, confirming the limited impact of ground evacuation on major injury state reclassification.

During air evacuation (Fig. 6), the differences between minor injury treatment and delayed treatment are similarly not evident, with both trajectories following nearly parallel courses. A gradual decreasing trend is observed in emergency treatment, accompanied by a steady increase in expectant treatment. The expectant treatment probability increased from 0.000 to 0.090, whereas emergency treatment decreased from 0.178 to 0.140. Notably, the delayed treatment probability remained constant at 0.130 across all iterations, suggesting that the interventions modeled for air evacuation did not alter the status of this injury category.

Comparing the three processes quantitatively, the on-site treatment phase exhibited the largest absolute changes in state probabilities. The expectant treatment probability increased by 0.12 in the on-site phase, compared with increases of 0.046 in ground evacuation and 0.090 in air evacuation. The emergency treatment probability showed a net increase of 0.14 during on-site treatment, whereas changes during the evacuation phases were minimal (ground: +0.010; air: -0.038). These numerical contrasts reinforce the conclusion that the on-site phase exerts the most substantial influence on injury outcomes.

Based on the simulation outputs aiming to minimize the overall mortality ratio, distinct sets of high-value inspection and treatment techniques were identified for each phase of care. For the on-site treatment process, the model prioritized immediate life-saving interventions with high portability, specifically: tourniquet application for extremity hemorrhage, basic airway maneuvers (nasopharyngeal airway insertion), and tension pneumothorax needle decompression. During ground evacuation, the model highlighted the value of continuous monitoring technologies, including pulse oximetry and serial Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS) assessments, due to the longer transport times and potential for delayed deterioration. In contrast, the air evacuation process favored advanced diagnostic capabilities that could be performed en route, such as Focused Assessment with Sonography in Trauma (FAST) and capnography waveform monitoring, to detect subtle physiological changes at altitude. These specific technological findings directly inform the data fields required on corresponding tactical field care and evacuation documentation forms.

The identification of high-value inspection and treatment techniques for each echelon of care is derived directly from the injury state transition dynamics illustrated in Figs. 4,5 and 6. Within the integrated Markov decision tree framework, the value of a specific technique is quantified by its contribution to reducing the steady-state probability of the expectant treatment category, which serves as the primary driver of mortality in the simulation. Techniques that produce the most substantial downward deflection in the expectant treatment trajectory at a given echelon are consequently assigned the highest priority for information recording. For the on-site phase, the steepest decline in expectant treatment probability coincides with the simulated application of immediate hemorrhage control and airway management interventions, thereby establishing these as the highest-value information fields for that stage of care.

While the simulation does not generate absolute numerical weights for individual techniques owing to the absence of empirical calibration data, the relative ordering of information value is a robust output of the model architecture. To assess the sensitivity of these rankings to uncertainty in the baseline mortality parameters borrowed from historical U.S. military data, a theoretical sensitivity assessment was conducted by varying the scenario adaptability coefficient λ across the plausible range of [0.8, 1.2]. This analysis confirmed that the identity of the top-tier information items—specifically those related to on-site hemorrhage control, airway patency, and serial physiological monitoring—remains stable across the full parameter range. Formal statistical validation of these screening results against prospectively collected combat casualty data, including quantitative mortality comparisons, constitutes an essential direction for future research and will be pursued as relevant empirical datasets become available.

Conclusion

This study applied a Markov decision tree model to simulate the evolution of injury states across the tactical combat casualty care continuum, with the specific objective of screening high-value information items for inclusion in medical evacuation documentation. The simulation results, derived from the probability trajectories presented in Figs. 4, 5 and 6, indicate that the on-site treatment phase exerts the most substantial influence on casualty outcomes. Quantitatively, the expectant treatment probability increased by 0.12 during on-site care, compared with increases of only 0.046 and 0.090 during ground and air evacuation, respectively. This finding underscores the critical importance of documenting specific life-saving interventions at the point of injury.

Based on the model outputs and consideration of information collection costs, the recommended information fields are organized into three priority levels. This tiered structure is designed to support practical battlefield documentation by distinguishing a minimum essential dataset from extended monitoring fields.

Priority Level I: Minimum lifesaving dataset. These fields should be recorded whenever feasible, as they are directly associated with preventable mortality and entail relatively low collection cost. They comprise: mechanism and anatomical site of major injury; presence of massive hemorrhage; tourniquet application status, time, and anatomical location; type and patency of airway intervention; needle decompression status and time; and casualty triage category at the time of handoff.

Priority Level II: Evacuation monitoring dataset. These fields are intended for recording during ground or air evacuation when tactical conditions permit. They include: pulse oximetry values at departure and arrival; Glasgow Coma Scale scores at departure and arrival; respiratory status; signs of shock; administered analgesia or resuscitation measures; and changes in triage category during evacuation.

Priority Level III: Extended diagnostic dataset. These fields are recommended for settings with adequate equipment, personnel, and time, such as evacuation platforms or forward surgical facilities. They encompass: FAST examination findings; serial physiological trends; and other device-dependent diagnostic findings.

In contrast to conventional military medical records, which primarily emphasize the complete documentation of injury description, treatment procedures, and evacuation information, the proposed model offers a quantitative screening logic for prioritizing information fields when documentation capacity is constrained. The innovation of this study, therefore, lies not in expanding the record form, but in prioritizing information fields according to their simulated contribution to injury state transitions and mortality reduction. This approach may help shift battlefield documentation from a completeness-oriented record toward a priority-oriented decision-support tool.

This study contributes a decision-making methodology that couples expert-weighted technical priorities with objectively screened information fields. By concentrating documentation efforts on these high-value data points, military medical personnel can enhance the utility of battlefield medical records for both immediate clinical decision-making and subsequent performance improvement analysis. However, the applicability of the model to contemporary operational settings warrants careful interpretation: the empirical mortality parameters were derived from historical data spanning 2009 to 2011, whereas current and future military medical systems may entail different injury mechanisms, evacuation timelines, documentation technologies, and treatment capabilities. The model should therefore be regarded as a flexible screening framework rather than a fixed predictive tool. It offers an evidence-based foundation for optimizing the content of medical evacuation documents, provided that its parameters are recalibrated using contemporary military exercise data, trauma registry records, or prospectively collected battlefield medical data as these resources become available.

Several limitations warrant acknowledgment. First, the model parameters—particularly key transition probabilities—were derived from expert consensus, historical foreign military data, and expert-informed scoring rather than from prospectively collected battlefield data. Because the present dataset does not permit calculation of an exact inter-expert consistency coefficient, the reproducibility of the parameter assignment depends on the transparency of the scoring rules and the robustness of the results under alternative parameter settings. Second, the four-state Markov abstraction simplifies the complex pathophysiology of trauma. Future work should adopt a formal Delphi process, report the number and professional composition of the experts, calculate inter-expert agreement, and validate both the prioritized information list and the assigned parameters against prospectively collected empirical combat casualty datasets, high-fidelity military medical exercises, or clinical registries, thereby refining the state transition probabilities as such data become available.

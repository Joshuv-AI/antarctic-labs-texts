Monotonicity in half-spaces for singular quasilinear elliptic problems involving the gradient
Phuong Le

Abstract
. We study positive solutions to the problem − Δ p ​ u + ϑ ​ | ∇ u | q = 1 u γ + f ⁡ ( u ) -\Delta_{p}u+\vartheta|\nabla u|^{q}=\frac{1}{u^{\gamma}}+f(u) in ℝ + N \mathbb{R}^{N}_{+} with the zero Dirichlet boundary condition, where p > 1 p>1 , γ > 0 \gamma>0 , 0 < q ≤ p 0<q\leq p , ϑ ≥ 0 \vartheta\geq 0 and f : [ 0 , + ∞ ) → ℝ f:[0,+\infty)\to\mathbb{R} is a locally Lipschitz continuous function. We describe the behavior of solutions and their derivatives near the boundary. Then we exploit that information and the moving plane method to prove the monotonicity of solutions in the x N x_{N} -direction. This result holds for W loc 1 , p ​ ( ℝ + 2 ) ∩ L loc ∞ ​ ( ℝ + 2 ¯ ) W^{1,p}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})\cap L^{\infty}_{\rm loc}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}) solutions in dimension two and for W loc 1 , p ​ ( ℝ + N ) W^{1,p}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}) solutions which are bounded in strips in higher dimensions. Most of our results are new even in the case ϑ = 0 \vartheta=0 or p = 2 p=2 .

1.
Introduction

We are interested in the qualitative properties of solutions to the quasilinear elliptic problem

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{p}u+\vartheta|\nabla u|^{q}=\frac{1}{u^{\gamma}}+f(u)&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+},\\
u>0&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+},\\
u=0&\text{ on }\partial\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+},\end{cases}

(1)

where
N\geq 2
,
p>1
,
\gamma>0
,
0<q\leq p
,
\vartheta\geq 0
,
f:[0,+\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
is a locally Lipschitz continuous function and
\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}:=\{x=(x^{\prime},x_{N})\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\mid x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{N-1},x_{N}>0\}
is the upper half-space. We call
u\in W^{1,p}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})
a
weak solution
to problem (
1
) if

\essinf_{x\in K}u(x)>0\quad\text{ for every }K\subset\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+},

and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}}|\nabla u|^{p-2}\langle\nabla u,\nabla\varphi\rangle+\vartheta\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}}|\nabla u|^{q}\varphi=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}}\left(\frac{1}{u^{\gamma}}+f(u)\right)\varphi\quad\text{ for }\varphi\in C^{1}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}).

Moreover, the zero Dirichlet boundary condition also has to be understood in the weak meaning

(u-\varepsilon)^{+}\varphi\chi_{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}}\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})\quad\text{ for every }\varphi\in C^{1}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})\text{ and }\varepsilon>0.

One purpose of this paper is to study the monotonicity properties of solutions to problem (
1
) with respect to the
x_{N}
-direction. The main tool we use is the moving plane method, which was introduced by Alexandrov
[
1
]
and Serrin
[
38
]
. This method relies on several types of maximum principles and was used in the celebrated papers
[
3
,
21
]
, where the symmetry of solutions to semilinear elliptic equations in bounded domains was established. For semilinear elliptic equations
-\Delta u=f(u)
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}
, where the monotonicity of the solutions is expected, we refer to
[
2
,
3
,
7
,
11
,
12
,
28
]
.

The quasilinear elliptic equations involving the
p
-Laplacian are harder to deal with because of the nonlinear nature of the operator. Hence, comparison principles are not equivalent to maximum principles for the
p
-Laplacian. Furthermore, the singularity or degeneracy of the operator (corresponding to
1<p<2
and
p>2
, respectively) also causes the lack of
C^{2}
regularity of the weak solutions and other difficulties. First symmetry results for the regular
p
-Laplace problem

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{p}u=f(u)&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
u>0&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
u=0&\text{ on }\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

(2)

where
\Omega
is a bounded domain of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, were obtained in
[
8
]
for the case
1<p<2
. The case
p>2
requires the use of weighted Sobolev spaces and was solved in
[
9
]
for positive nonlinearities.
The monotonicity of (possibly unbounded) solutions to (
2
) with
\Omega=\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N}
was established for
1<p<2
in
[
17
]
and for
p>2
in
[
19
]
via the moving plane method. All these results assume that
f
is a positive locally Lipschitz continuous function. More recently,
[
17
]
was partially improved by the work
[
15
]
, where the restriction on positivity of
f
was relaxed.

Due to the presence of the term
\frac{1}{u^{\gamma}}
with
\gamma>0
, problem (
1
) is singular. As a result, it is notable that
C^{1}
regularity cannot be extended up to the boundary and the gradient generally blows up near the boundary (see
[
25
,
30
]
). Furthermore, the difficulty in understanding problem (
1
) lies in the fact that both sides of the problem may exhibit singularity near
\partial\Omega
.
Singular elliptic problems without a gradient term have been studied by various authors in the past. We refer in particular to the pioneering papers by Crandall, Rabinowitz and Tartar
[
6
]
and by Lazer and McKenna
[
25
]
, where they combine the method of subsolutions
and supersolutions with approximation technique to prove the existence, uniqueness and qualitative properties of solutions to

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{p}u=\frac{a(x)}{u^{\gamma}}&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
u>0&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
u=0&\text{ on }\partial\Omega\end{cases}

(3)

with
p=2
(see
[
23
]
for further insights). We also refer to the work by Boccardo and Orsina
[
4
]
on the same problem in a more general setting, exploiting truncation arguments and Schauder’s fixed point theorem. Later, the existence, uniqueness and regularity of solutions to problem (
3
) with
p>1
were derived in
[
13
,
5
]
. The Hopf type lemma for singular quasilinear equations was derived in
[
16
]
. The boundary behavior of such problems with gradient terms in bounded domains was recently studied by the author in
[
30
]
.

The monotonicity results for problem
1
was established only in the case that
\vartheta=0
and
\gamma>1
(see
[
27
, Theorem 3]
and also
[
28
]
). The other cases are not well understood. In this paper, we consider the general case
\vartheta>0
and
\gamma>0
. We stress that the difficulty in the case
\vartheta>0
is that the term
|\nabla u|^{q}
may be unbounded in strips

\Sigma_{\lambda}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\mid 0<x_{N}<\lambda\}

for any
\lambda>0
. Hence the estimates in
[
17
,
19
,
15
,
18
]
do not hold anymore since they strongly rely on the boundedness of the gradient in strips.
Furthermore, the behavior of solutions near the boundary in the case
0<\gamma\leq 1
, which was derived in
[
27
, Theorem 3]
, is completely different from that in the case
\gamma>0
. Hence, we need more delicate estimates to overcome these difficulties. To state our first result, we denote by
B_{r}(x_{0})
the open ball in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
of center
x_{0}
and radius
r
. We also write
B_{r}=B_{r}(0)
.
Then we denote
\mathbb{S}^{N-1}:=\partial B_{1}(0)
and
e_{N}:=(0,\dots,0,1)
.

We start with a result on the behavior of solutions near the boundary.

Theorem 1
.

Let
u\in W^{1,p}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})
be a solution to problem (
1
) with
u\in L^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\overline{\lambda}})
for some
\overline{\lambda}>0
. Then the following assertions hold:

(i)

When
\gamma>1
, we have
u\in C^{1,\alpha}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})\cap C^{\frac{p}{\gamma+p-1}}_{\rm loc}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}})
for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Moreover, for any
\eta_{0}\in\mathbb{S}^{N-1}
,

\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{N}\to 0^{+}\\
\eta\to\eta_{0}\end{subarray}}x_{N}^{\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma+p-1}}\frac{\partial u(x)}{\partial\eta}=\left(\frac{(\gamma+p-1)^{p}}{p^{p-1}(p-1)(\gamma-1)}\right)^{\frac{1}{\gamma+p-1}}\frac{p\langle\eta_{0},e_{N}\rangle}{\gamma+p-1}.

(4)

Consequently, for every
\beta\in(0,1)
, there exist
\lambda_{0},c_{1},c_{2}>0
such that

c_{1}x_{N}^{\frac{1-\gamma}{\gamma+p-1}}<\frac{\partial u(x)}{\partial\eta}<c_{2}x_{N}^{\frac{1-\gamma}{\gamma+p-1}}\quad\text{ in }\Sigma_{\lambda_{0}}

(5)

for all
\eta\in\mathbb{S}^{N-1}
with
\langle\eta,e_{N}\rangle\geq\beta
.

(ii)

When
\gamma=1
, we have
u\in C^{1,\alpha}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})\cap C^{s}_{\rm loc}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}})
for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
and all
s\in(0,1)
. Moreover, for every
\beta\in(0,1)
, there exist
0<\lambda_{0}<1
,
c_{1},c_{2}>0
such that

c_{1}\left(1-\ln x_{N}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq\frac{\partial u(x)}{\partial\eta}\leq c_{2}\left(1-\ln x_{N}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\quad\text{ in }\Sigma_{\lambda_{0}}

(6)

for all
\eta\in\mathbb{S}^{N-1}
with
\langle\eta,e_{N}\rangle\geq\beta
.

(iii)

When
0<\gamma<1
, we have
u\in C^{1,\alpha}_{\rm loc}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}})
for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Moreover, for every
\beta\in(0,1)
, there exist
\lambda_{0},c_{1},c_{2}>0
such that

c_{1}<\frac{\partial u(x)}{\partial\eta}<c_{2}\quad\text{ in }\Sigma_{\lambda_{0}}\cup\partial\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}

(7)

for all
\eta\in\mathbb{S}^{N-1}
with
\langle\eta,e_{N}\rangle\geq\beta
.

In particular, Theorem
1
implies that solutions are increasing in the
x_{N}
-direction in a narrow strip from the boundary. It provides us with a starting point to carry out the moving plane procedure to derive our next result. Consider the following assumptions:

(
F_{1}
)

\frac{2N+2}{N+2}<p\leq 2
and
\{t\in(0,+\infty)\mid\frac{1}{t^{\gamma}}+f(t)=0\}
is a discrete set,

(
F_{2}
)

p>1
and
\frac{1}{t^{\gamma}}+f(t)>0
for
t>0
.

We can prove the following monotonicity result.

Theorem 2
.

Assume that either
(

F
1

)
or
(

F
2

)
holds. We also assume that
1<q\leq p
if
1<p\leq 2
and
p-1\leq q\leq p
if
p>2
. Let
u\in W^{1,p}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})
be a solution to problem (
1
) with
u\in L^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\lambda})
for all
\lambda>0
. Then
u
is monotone increasing in
x_{N}
-direction, that is,

\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{N}}\geq 0\quad\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}.

Moreover, if
p>\frac{2N+2}{N+2}
and
\frac{1}{t^{\gamma}}+f(t)>0
for
t>0
, then

\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{N}}>0\quad\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}.

Next, we provide a nonexistence result for the pure singular problem

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{p}u=\frac{1}{u^{\gamma}}&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+},\\
u>0&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+},\\
u=0&\text{ on }\partial\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}.\end{cases}

(8)

Problem (
8
) has one-dimensional symmetry solutions when
\gamma>1
(see
[
16
, Theorem 1.2]
). Moreover, all
x_{N}
-sublinear solutions which are bounded in a strip are classified in
[
27
, Theorem 8]
when
\gamma>1
. A Liouville-type theorem was proved in the same reference for the case
0<\gamma<1
. However, the case
\gamma=1
is more delicate and has not been considered there yet. With the aid of new estimates in this paper, we can provide a Liouville result for the borderline case
\gamma=1
as follows.

Theorem 3
.

Problem (
8
) with
1<p<N
and
\gamma=1
has no solution
u\in W^{1,p}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})
with
u\in L^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\overline{\lambda}})
for some
\overline{\lambda}>0
.

We stress that all above results assume that
u
is bounded in some strip. Actually, some of our result can be formulated in a weaker form for solutions without this assumption. For
R>0
, we set

\mathcal{C}_{R}=\{x:=(x^{\prime},x_{N})\in\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}\mid|x^{\prime}|<R\}.

We have the following local version of Theorem
1
, which applies to solutions which may be unbounded in strips.

.

Let

\eta\in\mathbb{S}^{N-1}

(iii)

u\in C^{1,\alpha}_{\rm loc}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}})

in force, we can proceed as in

,

]
, exploiting the rotating and moving lines technique to prove the monotonicity of solutions in dimension two without the assumption on their boundedness in strips. The following result improves Theorem

.

Assume that one of the following holds:

(i)

\frac{3}{2}<p\leq 2
,

The rest of this paper is devoted to the proofs of the above theorems. In Section 2, we prove sharp bounds for solutions near the boundary via comparison arguments. Then we derive bounds on directional derivatives, namely Theorem
1

2.
Boundary behavior of solutions

In this section, we study behavior of solutions to (
1
) with are bounded in some strip.

2.1.
Sharp bounds for solutions near the boundary

The aim of this subsection is the following.

Theorem 6
.

Let
u\in W^{1,p}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})
be a solution to problem (
1
) with
u\in L^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\overline{\lambda}})
for some
\overline{\lambda}>0
. Then
u\in C^{1,\alpha}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})\cap C(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}})
for some
0<\alpha<1
. Moreover, the following assertions hold:

(i)

When
\gamma>1
, there exist
\lambda_{0},c_{1},c_{2}>0
such that

c_{1}x_{N}^{\frac{p}{\gamma+p-1}}\leq u(x)\leq c_{2}x_{N}^{\frac{p}{\gamma+p-1}}\quad\text{ in }\Sigma_{\lambda_{0}}.

(ii)

When
\gamma=1
, there exist
0<\lambda_{0}<1
,
c_{1},c_{2}>0
such that

c_{1}x_{N}\left(1-\ln x_{N}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq u(x)\leq c_{2}x_{N}\left(1-\ln x_{N}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\quad\text{ in }\Sigma_{\lambda_{0}}.

(iii)

When
0<\gamma<1
, there exist
\lambda_{0},c_{1},c_{2}>0
such that

c_{1}x_{N}\leq u(x)\leq c_{2}x_{N}\quad\text{ in }\Sigma_{\lambda_{0}}.

We list here two main tools we will exploit to prove Theorem
6
.
We recall the following comparison principle from
[
30
]
(see also
[
16
]
for the case
\vartheta=0
).

Lemma 7

(Lemma 9 in
[
30
]
)
.

Let
c>0
and
\Omega
be a domain of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Let
u_{1},u_{2}\in C^{1,\alpha}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
be such that

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{p}u_{1}+\vartheta|\nabla u_{1}|^{p}+\vartheta\leq\frac{c}{u_{1}^{\gamma}}&\text{ in } \Omega,\\
-\Delta_{p}u_{2}+\vartheta|\nabla u_{2}|^{p}+\vartheta\geq\frac{c}{u_{2}^{\gamma}}&\text{ in } \Omega,\\
u_{1},u_{2}>0&\text{ in } \Omega,\\
u_{1}\leq u_{2}&\text{ on }\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

If
\vartheta>0
, we further assume

u_{1}\leq\left(\frac{c}{\vartheta}\right)^{1/\gamma}\quad\text{ in }\Omega.

Then
u_{1}\leq u_{2}
in
\Omega
.

To obtain the sharp upper bound in the case
0<\gamma<1
, the following lemma is also needed.

Lemma 8

(Lemma 10 in
[
30
]
)
.

Assume
0<\gamma<1
. Then for every
M>0
and
r_{2}>r_{1}>0
, the problem

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{p}u=\frac{M}{u^{\gamma}}&\text{ in }B_{r_{2}}\setminus\overline{B_{r_{1}}},\\
u>0&\text{ in }B_{r_{2}}\setminus\overline{B_{r_{1}}},\\
u=0&\text{ on }\partial(B_{r_{2}}\setminus\overline{B_{r_{1}}})\end{cases}

admits a unique solution
U\in C^{1,\alpha}(B_{r_{2}}\setminus\overline{B_{r_{1}}})\cap C(\overline{B_{r_{2}}\setminus{B_{r_{1}}}})
. Moreover, there exists
C>0
such that

c{\rm dist}(x,B_{r_{2}}\setminus\overline{B_{r_{1}}})\leq U(x)\leq C{\rm dist}(x,B_{r_{2}}\setminus\overline{B_{r_{1}}})\quad\text{ for all }x\in B_{r_{2}}\setminus\overline{B_{r_{1}}}.

With the above lemmas, we are ready to prove Theorem

via comparison arguments.

Proof of Theorem
6
.

Since
\frac{1}{u^{\gamma}}+f(u)
is bounded in each compact set
K\subset\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}
, we have that
u\in C^{1,\alpha}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})
by standard elliptic regularity (see
[
14
,
39
]
).

Setting

x_{0}=-\overline{\lambda}e_{N},\quad\Omega=B_{3\overline{\lambda}}(x_{0})\setminus\overline{B_{\overline{\lambda}}(x_{0})}\quad\text{ and }\quad L=\sup_{\Sigma_{\overline{\lambda}}}u.

(9)

Let
\lambda_{1}>0
be the first eigenvalue and
\phi_{1}\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
a corresponding positive normalized eigenfunction of the
p
-Laplacian in
\Omega
with the Dirichlet boundary condition, namely,

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{p}\phi_{1}=\lambda_{1}\phi_{1}^{p-1}&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
\phi_{1}>0&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
\phi_{1}=0&\text{ on }\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

and
\|\phi_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}=1
. We consider three cases.

Case 1:
\gamma>1
.
Let

w_{s}=s\phi_{1}^{\frac{p}{\gamma+p-1}},

where
s>0
. A direct calculation yields

-\Delta_{p}w_{s}=\frac{a_{s}(x)}{w_{s}^{\gamma}}\quad\text{ in }\Omega,

where

a_{s}(x)=s^{\gamma+p-1}\left(\frac{p}{\gamma+p-1}\right)^{p-1}\left[\lambda_{1}\phi_{1}(x)^{p}+\frac{(\gamma-1)(p-1)}{\gamma+p-1}|\nabla\phi_{1}(x)|^{p}\right].

Since
u\leq L
in
\Sigma_{\overline{\lambda}}
, we can find
M>0
sufficiently large such that

\frac{1}{u^{\gamma}}+f(u)<\frac{M}{u^{\gamma}}\quad\text{ for }x\in\Sigma_{\overline{\lambda}}.

(10)

On the other hand, by the positivity of
\phi_{1}
and the Hopf boundary lemma for
\phi_{1}
, we know that

\inf_{x\in\Omega}\left[\lambda_{1}\phi_{1}(x)^{p}+\frac{(\gamma-1)(p-1)}{\gamma+p-1}|\nabla\phi_{1}(x)|^{p}\right]>0.

Hence, we can choose some large
s_{1}>0
such that

a_{s_{1}}(x)>M\text{ for }x\in\Omega\quad\text{ and }\quad w_{s_{1}}(x)>L\text{ for }x\in\partial B_{2\overline{\lambda}}(x_{0}).

Let any

y=(y^{\prime},y_{N})\in\Sigma_{\overline{\lambda}}

(11)

and set
\hat{y}=(y^{\prime},-\overline{\lambda})
, we have

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{p}u\leq\frac{M}{u^{\gamma}}&\text{ in } B_{2\overline{\lambda}}(\hat{y})\cap\Sigma_{\overline{\lambda}},\\

up to a subsequence for some

Moreover, (

.

\begin{cases}-\Delta w_{\infty}=\frac{1}{w_{\infty}^{\gamma}}&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+},\\
u>0&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+},\\
u=0&\text{ on }\partial\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}.\end{cases}

By

for some

Therefore,

On the other hand, by (

for some large

This completes the proof.
∎

3.
Monotonicity of solutions and a nonexistence result

Theorem

is only concerned about the bounds for solutions near
\partial\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}
. The following lemma will provide a global bound from below for solutions.

Lemma 9
.

Let
g:(0,+\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
be a locally Lipschitz continuous function such that

for all
0<t<t_{0}
, where
\gamma,c_{0},t_{0}>0
. Let

be a solution to the problem

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{p}u+\vartheta|\nabla u|^{q}=g(u)&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+},\\
u>0&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+},\\
u=0&\text{ on }\partial\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}.\end{cases}

Then there is a constant

such that

(28)

where
t_{1}:=\min\left\{t_{0},\left(\frac{c_{0}}{\vartheta}\right)^{1/\gamma}\right\}

Proof.

Let

be the first eigenvalue and

a corresponding positive eigenfunction of the

-Laplacian in

\phi_{1}>0&\text{ in }B_{1},\\
\phi_{1}=0&\text{ on }\partial B_{1}.\end{cases}

Setting

where
s>0
will be chosen later. Direct calculation yields that in the weak sense

-\Delta_{p}w+\vartheta|\nabla w|^{p}+\vartheta=\frac{a(x)}{w^{\gamma}}\quad\text{ in }B_{1},

where

\displaystyle a(x)

\displaystyle=s^{\gamma+p-1}\left(\frac{p}{\gamma+p-1}\right)^{p-1}\left[\lambda_{1}\phi_{1}^{p}+\left(\frac{\vartheta sp}{\gamma+p-1}\phi_{1}^{p}+\frac{(\gamma-1)(p-1)}{\gamma+p-1}\right)|\nabla\phi_{1}|^{p}\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\vartheta s^{\gamma}\phi_{1}^{\frac{\gamma p}{\gamma+p-1}}.

Now we fix
s>0
such that
a(x)\leq c_{0}
in
B_{1}
. Then let

be such that

For any

and

, where

Then
w_{x_{0},R}\leq t_{1}
in

(29)

is not empty. Moreover,

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{p}w_{x_{0},R}+\vartheta|\nabla w_{x_{0},R}|^{p}+\vartheta\leq\frac{c_{0}}{w_{x_{0},R}^{\gamma}}&\text{ in } \Omega,\\

can be applied to yield

in

, which is a contradiction.
Hence
u\geq w_{x_{0},R}
in
B_{R}(x_{0})
with
x_{0,N}\geq R+\varepsilon
. Since

is arbitrary, we deduce

Hence (

is chosen arbitrarily in
\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}
.
∎

Let

be a locally Lipschitz continuous function on
\mathbb{R}
. Fix
\lambda_{0}>0
and
L_{0}>0
. Let
u,v\in C^{1}(\Sigma_{\lambda_{0}})
such that

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\lambda_{0}})}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\lambda_{0}})}\leq L_{0},\quad\|v\|_{L^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\lambda_{0}})}+\|\nabla v\|_{L^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\lambda_{0}})}\leq L_{0}

and

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{p}u+\vartheta|\nabla u|^{q}\leq f(u)&\text{in }\Sigma_{\lambda_{0}},\\

where

and the open set
\mathcal{S}_{\tau,\varepsilon}\subset\Sigma_{\lambda_{0}}
is such that

\mathcal{S}_{\tau,\varepsilon}=\bigcup_{x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}\{x^{\prime}\}\times(A^{\tau}_{x^{\prime}}\cup B^{\varepsilon}_{x^{\prime}}),\quad A^{\tau}_{x^{\prime}}\cap B^{\varepsilon}_{x^{\prime}}=\emptyset

and for each

,

are measurable sets satisfying

Then there exist

\tau_{0}=\tau_{0}(N,p,q,\lambda_{0},L_{0})>0

and

\varepsilon_{0}=\varepsilon_{0}(N,p,q,\lambda_{0},L_{0})>0

such that, if

and

We are ready to prove Proposition

.

Proof of Proposition
15
.

We start moving the plane from
\{x_{N}=0\}
to
+\infty
. Let us consider

\Lambda:=\{\lambda>0\mid u\leq u_{t}\text{ in }\Sigma_{t}\text{ for all }0<t\leq\lambda\}.

By Theorem
1
, we have
\Lambda\neq\emptyset
. Hence, we can define

\overline{\lambda}=\sup\Lambda.

To conclude the proof, we have to show that

\overline{\lambda}=+\infty.

By contradiction, in what follows, we assume that
\overline{\lambda}<+\infty
. Then we have
u\leq u_{\overline{\lambda}}\text{ in }\Sigma_{\overline{\lambda}}
. Set
\lambda_{0}=\overline{\lambda}+1
and

L_{0}=\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Sigma_{2\lambda_{0}})}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Sigma_{2\lambda_{0}})}

and take
\tau_{0}=\tau_{0}(N,p,q,\lambda_{0},L_{0})>0
and
\varepsilon_{0}=\varepsilon_{0}(N,p,q,\lambda_{0},L_{0})>0
as in Theorem
17
.

Fix
0<\delta<\min\left\{\frac{\overline{\lambda}}{2},\frac{\tau_{0}}{4}\right\}
and
0<\rho<\varepsilon_{0}
. By Theorem
6
and Lemma
9
, we may assume

\sup_{\Sigma_{\delta}}u\leq\inf_{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}\setminus\Sigma_{\overline{\lambda}}}u.

(57)

We claim that there exists
\overline{\varepsilon}>0
such that for all
0<\varepsilon\leq\min\left\{\overline{\varepsilon},\frac{\tau_{0}}{2},1\right\}
it follows that

u\leq u_{\overline{\lambda}+\varepsilon}\quad\text{in }\Sigma_{\delta,\overline{\lambda}-\delta}\setminus\{|\nabla u|<\rho\}.

(58)

Indeed, if (
58
) does not hold, then we can find a sequence of positive numbers
(\varepsilon_{n})
converging to 0 and a sequence of points

x_{n}=(x^{\prime}_{n},y_{n})\in\Sigma_{\delta,\overline{\lambda}-\delta}

such that

u(x_{n})>u_{\overline{\lambda}+\varepsilon_{n}}(x_{n})\quad\text{and}\quad|\nabla u(x_{n})|\geq\rho

for all
n
. Passing to a subsequence if necessary, we may and do assume that
\lim_{n\to\infty}y_{n}=y_{0}\in[\delta,\overline{\lambda}-\delta]
. Let us define

u_{n}(x^{\prime},y)=u(x^{\prime}+x^{\prime}_{n},y)\quad\text{for }(x^{\prime},y)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N}.

Then
u_{n}
satisfies

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{p}u_{n}+\vartheta|\nabla u_{n}|^{q}=g(u_{n})&\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+},\\
u_{n}=0&\text{on }\partial\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}.\end{cases}

(59)

Notice that
(u_{n})
is bounded from both sides on
\Sigma_{M}\setminus\Sigma_{m}
for
M>m>0
.
By
C^{1,\alpha}
estimates, Ascoli-Arzelà’s theorem and a standard diagonal process, we deduce

u_{n}\to\tilde{u}\quad\text{in }C^{1,\alpha^{\prime}}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})

up to a subsequence for
0<\alpha^{\prime}<\alpha
. We can pass (
59
) to the limit to obtain

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{p}\tilde{u}+\vartheta|\nabla\tilde{u}|^{q}=g(\tilde{u})&\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+},\\
\tilde{u}=0&\text{on }\partial\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}.\end{cases}

Moreover, we have

(i)

\tilde{u}\geq 0
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}
,

(ii)

\tilde{u}\leq\tilde{u}_{\lambda}
in
\Sigma_{\lambda}
for all
0<\lambda\leq\overline{\lambda}
,

(iii)

\tilde{u}(x_{0})\geq\tilde{u}_{\overline{\lambda}}(x_{0})
,

(iv)

|\nabla\tilde{u}(x_{0})|\geq\rho
,

where
x_{0}=(0,y_{0})\in\Sigma_{\overline{\lambda}}
. Items (i), (iv) and Lemma
9
imply
\tilde{u}>0
in
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N}
. Hence
\tilde{u}
is a solution to (1). Items (ii) and (iii) imply that
\frac{\partial\tilde{u}}{\partial x_{N}}\geq 0
in
\Sigma_{\overline{\lambda}}
and
\tilde{u}(x_{0})=\tilde{u}_{\overline{\lambda}}(x_{0})
. However, the existence of such a solution
\tilde{u}
and such a point
x_{0}
contradicts Proposition
15
. Therefore, (
58
) must hold.

On the other hand, an application of Theorem [9] with
v=u_{\overline{\lambda}+\varepsilon}
yields

u\leq u_{\overline{\lambda}+\varepsilon}\quad\text{in }\Sigma_{\overline{\lambda}-\delta,\overline{\lambda}+\varepsilon}\cup\left(\Sigma_{\delta,\overline{\lambda}-\delta}\cap\{|\nabla u|<\rho\}\right).

Combining this with (
57
) and (
58
), we have that
u\leq u_{\overline{\lambda}+\varepsilon}
in
\Sigma_{\overline{\lambda}+\varepsilon}
for all
0<\varepsilon\leq\min\left\{\overline{\varepsilon},\frac{\tau_{0}}{2},1\right\}
. This is a contradiction with the definition of
\overline{\lambda}
. Therefore, it must hold that
\overline{\lambda}=+\infty
. This implies the monotonicity of
u
in the
x_{N}
-direction.
∎

UGP2 is a potential target for breast cancer, based on bioinformatics analysis and in vitro experiments
Authors: Xiaojun Zhang, Zilin Zheng, Yan Chang, Zehui Wang, JianJun Han, Jianchao He
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Breast cancer represents the most prevalent malignancy in women globally. While UDP-glucose pyrophosphorylase 2 (UGP2) has been implicated in tumor biology, its pathophysiological role in breast carcinogenesis remains undefined. This study systematically investigates the clinical relevance and functional mechanisms of UGP2 in breast cancer progression. Multi-omics analysis was conducted using GEPIA2, TIMER2, and TCGA databases to evaluate UGP2 expression patterns, clinicopathological correlations, and prognostic significance across breast cancer subtypes. Findings were validated through immunohistochemical analysis of 118 clinical specimens. Single-cell RNA sequencing data from GEO were employed to resolve UGP2 spatial distribution within tumor microenvironments. Functional enrichment analysis elucidated UGP2-associated pathways, while loss-of-function experiments using siRNA-mediated knockdown assessed UGP2’s impact on malignant phenotypes in breast cancer cell lines. Our research found that the expression level of UGP2 in the TNBC subtype was significantly higher than that in other subtypes. The elevated expression level of UGP2 suggests a poor prognosis for breast cancer patients. The high expression level of UGP2 is positively correlated with the level of CNV. UGP2 is mainly located in epithelial cells and CAFs subsets and can participate in biological processes such as collagen synthesis. Knockdown of UGP2 in vitro can inhibit the proliferation, migration and invasion abilities of breast cancer cells. This study demonstrates that elevated UGP2 expression is clinically associated with aggressive tumor behavior and unfavorable prognosis in breast cancer patients, particularly in the triple-negative subtype (TNBC). Mechanistic analyses reveal UGP2’s functional involvement in both tumor cell proliferation and stromal remodeling through cancer-associated fibroblast (CAF) interactions. The robust association between UGP2 overexpression and aggressive clinicopathological features positions it as both a promising prognostic biomarker and potential therapeutic target, particularly in TNBC management.

Introduction

Breast cancer (BC) accounts for nearly one-quarter of global female cancer cases and accounts for one-sixth of cancer-related mortality, ranking as the most prevalent malignancy among women worldwide in 20221. The complex molecular mechanisms and significant heterogeneity of BC present substantial challenges in oncological research2. In recent years, metabolic reprogramming has been recognized as a critical hallmark of cancer, offering pivotal insights into tumorigenesis and progression3. The Warburg effect, characterized by cancer cells’ preferential reliance on glycolysis over oxidative phosphorylation for energy production under normoxic conditions, underscores functional dysregulation of multiple metabolic enzymes during this process4. Uridine diphosphate glucose pyrophosphorylase 2 (UGP2), a key rate-limiting enzyme in glycogen metabolism, catalyzes the conversion of uridine triphosphate (UTP) and glucose-1-phosphate (Glc-1-P) to uridine diphosphate glucose (UDP-Glc) and pyrophosphate (PPi), playing central roles in glycogen synthesis, protein glycosylation, and cellular energy homeostasis5.

As a member of the nucleotidyltransferase family, UGP2 is located on chromosome 2q13-q14, encoding a 508-amino acid protein with an approximate molecular weight of 57 kDa6,7. Recent studies have shown that UGP2 exhibits aberrant expression in multiple solid tumors, including hepatocellular carcinoma, pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma, and colorectal cancer, where it promotes tumor proliferation, invasion, and metastasis through mechanisms involving glycolytic reprogramming and epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) induction8–10. Notably, BC’s marked dependence on glucose metabolism suggests that dysregulated activity of metabolic enzymes within the tumor microenvironment may drive malignant progression via metabolic network remodeling, cancer stem cell modulation, or immune microenvironment alteration. Nevertheless, the biological functions of UGP2 in BC pathogenesis remain insufficiently characterized. This study therefore conducts preliminary exploration of UGP2’s biological roles in BC development.

Materials and methods

TCGA database analysis

RNA sequencing data and corresponding clinical records from the TCGA-BRCA cohort were retrieved through The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) database (https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov/). These datasets were employed to investigate clinical correlations and perform Cox proportional hazards regression analyses. All computational workflows were implemented using the “limma“” and “ggplot2” packages within the R statistical environment.

Online database interrogation

The GEPIA2 (http://gepia2.cancer-pku.cn/) and UALCAN (http://ualcan.path.uab.edu/) platforms were interrogated to profile UGP2 mRNA expression patterns across molecular subtypes of BC. Somatic copy number alterations (sCNAs) of UGP2 were systematically evaluated via the sCNA module integrated in TIMER2 (http://timer.cistrome.org/). Prognostic implications of UGP2 transcript levels were further validated by generating Kaplan-Meier survival curves using the KMplot database (https://kmplot.com/analysis/).

Single-cell transcriptomic profiling

The GSE161529 dataset was accessed from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) repository (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/), with single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) data extracted from five BC specimens within this cohort. Raw data underwent rigorous quality filtering to retain cells expressing 300–5,000 genes and 1,000–25,000 unique molecular identifiers (UMIs). Cells demonstrating mitochondrial gene proportions > 5% or hemoglobin gene expression > 1% were systematically excluded. Cross-sample batch effect correction was achieved through the Harmony integration framework. Normalization and feature scaling were performed via the SCTransform pipeline, followed by identification of the top 3,000 variably expressed genes using the FindVariableFeatures algorithm. Dimensionality reduction employed uniform manifold approximation and projection (UMAP) based on the first 40 principal components, with cluster annotation guided by canonical marker expression. Intercellular communication networks were reconstructed using CellChat, while malignant potential was quantified through copy number variation (CNV) inference via the infercnv package.

Functional enrichment analysis

BC samples from the TCGA-BRCA cohort were stratified into high- and low-expression groups based on median UGP2 mRNA levels using transcriptomic data. Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) meeting thresholds of |log₂FC| ≥1 and adjusted p* < 0.05 were subjected to Gene Ontology (GO) enrichment analysis and Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) for Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG)11–13 pathways. For single-cell RNA sequencing data, analogous stratification was performed, followed by identification of DEGs under stricter criteria (|log₂FC| ≥1, adjusted p* < 0.001) prior to GO functional enrichment. All analytical workflows were executed using the “clusterProfiler”, “GSEABase”, and “enrichplot” packages in R.

Clinical cohort characteristics

A retrospective cohort of 118 consecutive BC cases undergoing surgical resection at the Breast Surgery Department of Affiliated Hospital of Hebei Engineering University was enrolled between January 2017 and June 2017. Inclusion criteria comprised: (1) histopathologically confirmed invasive ductal carcinoma; (2) complete clinicopathological documentation; (3) treatment-naïve status prior to sample acquisition; (4) standardized adjuvant therapy administration postoperatively. Exclusion criteria included: (1) age < 18 or > 80 years; (2) concurrent autoimmune disorders; (3) history of prior malignancies; (4) severe psychiatric comorbidities.

The study protocol received ethical approval from Hebei Engineering University Institutional Review Board (Approval No. 2024-K-024), with waiver of informed consent granted under provisions for retrospective analyses of anonymized clinical data in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki.

Immunohistochemical staining

Formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded BC specimens were sectioned at 4 μm thickness. Tissue sections underwent deparaffinization through xylene immersion followed by rehydration in graded ethanol series, with subsequent phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) rinses. Endogenous peroxidase activity was quenched via 15-minute incubation with 3% hydrogen peroxide at ambient temperature. Heat-induced epitope retrieval was performed in EDTA buffer (pH 8.0) at 95 °C for 15 min. To minimize nonspecific binding, sections were blocked with 1% fetal bovine serum (37 °C, 20 min) prior to overnight incubation (4 °C) with primary anti-UGP2 antibody (Proteintech, #10391-1-AP; 1:100 dilution). Secondary antibody (Zhongshan Golden Bridge Biotechnology, Beijing) was applied for 30 min at room temperature, followed by diaminobenzidine (DAB) chromogen development and hematoxylin counterstaining. Mounted slides were independently evaluated by two senior pathologists blinded to clinical parameters using standardized scoring criteria.

Cell culture

The MDA-MB-231, MDA-MB-468, SK-BR-3, HCC1937, and MCF-7 cell lines were procured from Procell Life Science. Cells were maintained in high-glucose Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium (DMEM) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) and 1% penicillin/streptomycin under standard culture conditions (37 °C, 5% CO₂). MDA-MB-468 and HCC1937 cells were cultured in RPMI-1640 medium under the same conditions.

Quantitative reverse transcription PCR (qRT-PCR)

Total RNA was isolated from BC cell lines using TRIzol reagent (Takara, Dalian). cDNA synthesis was performed with a PrimeScript™ RT reagent kit (Takara) following manufacturer guidelines. Primer sequences for UGP2 amplification were: Forward: 5′-GCTCAAGTGCCAAAAGCACA-3′.Reverse: 5′-GACATTCAGGCCTCCATCCA-3′.Relative mRNA expression was calculated via the 2 − ΔΔCt method using GAPDH as endogenous control.

Western blotting

Cellular proteins were extracted using ice-cold RIPA lysis buffer and quantified via BCA assay. Equal protein aliquots were resolved on 10% SDS-PAGE gels and transferred to 0.2 μm PVDF membranes. After blocking with 5% non-fat milk (1 h, RT), membranes were incubated with anti-UGP2 (Proteintech, 10391-1-AP; 1:1,000) at 4 °C overnight. Following TBST washes, HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies (1:5,000) were applied (1 h, RT), and signals were visualized using ECL substrate.

RNA interference

Cells at 40% confluence were serum-deprived for 24 h before transfection with siRNA-Lipofectamine 3000 complexes. Three UGP2-targeting siRNAs and scrambled controls were designed: siUGP2#1:Sense: 5′-GCAGGUACCCGAGGAUUAATT-3′.Antisense: 5′-UUAAUCCUCGGGUACCUGCTT-3′.siUGP2#2:Sense: 5′-GCCACAGUGGAUCUGUAUATT-3′.Antisense: 5′-UAUACAGAUCCACUGUGGCTT-3′.siUGP2#3:Sense: 5′-GGUGAUGUCAAACCUCUAUTT-3′.Antisense: 5′-AUAGAGGUUUGACAUCACCTT-3′.Scramble control: Sense: 5′-UUCUCCGAACGUGUCACGUTT-3′.Antisense: 5′-ACGUGACACGUUCGGAGAATT-3′.Knockdown efficiency was validated 48 h post-transfection.

Scratch wound assay

Confluent monolayers in 6-well plates were scratched using sterile 1,000 µL pipette tips. Wound closure was monitored at 0/48 h via phase-contrast microscopy. Migration rates were quantified using ImageJ by measuring residual wound area.

Transwell invasion assay

Serum-starved cells (2 × 10⁴/well) in serum-free medium were seeded into Matrigel-coated upper chambers (Corning). Complete medium containing 20% FBS served as chemoattractant in lower chambers. After 24 h, invaded cells were fixed (4% paraformaldehyde, 20 min), stained (0.1% crystal violet, 20 min), and counted across five random fields.

Colony formation assay

Single-cell suspensions (500 cells/well) were plated in 6-well plates. Colonies (> 50 cells) were fixed and stained after 10-day incubation. Clonogenic survival was calculated as (colony number/seeded cells) ×100%.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using non-paired t-tests for two-group comparisons and one-way ANOVA for multi-group analyses, with repeated measures applied to variables assessed across multiple time points. For clinical cohort data, ordinal variables were analyzed using the Wilcoxon signed-rank test, while categorical between-group differences were assessed via chi-square (χ²) tests. All analyses were conducted using SPSS 22.0 and GraphPad Prism 8.0, with measurement data presented as mean ± standard deviation. Statistical significance was defined as *p* < 0.05.

Result

The expression distribution of UGP2 in BC

GEPIA2 interrogation revealed significantly elevated UGP2 mRNA levels in triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) subtypes compared to other molecular classifications (p < 0.05; Fig. 1A). This TNBC-specific overexpression pattern was independently validated through UALCAN platform analysis (p < 0.05;Figure 1B). Stratification of TCGA-BRCA cases by median UGP2 expression identified significant associations between UGP2 levels and clinicopathological parameters including patient age, estrogen receptor (ER) status, progesterone receptor (PR) status, and PAM50 molecular subtyping (p < 0.05;Figure 1C). Chi-square testing further confirmed these correlations, demonstrating statistically robust linkages between UGP2 expression and ER status, PR status, and PAM50 categorization (p < 0.05;Figure 1D).

IHC evaluation of 118 BC specimens demonstrated predominant cytoplasmic localization of UGP2 protein (Fig. 2B), with a positive expression rate of 38.14% (45/118). Chi-square analysis demonstrated significant associations between UGP2 protein and ER status (P = 0.004), PR status (P = 0.017), Ki67 index (P = 0.047), and Luminal subtype (P = 0.028) (Table 1). Subsequent logistic regression modeling revealed UGP2 overexpression was inversely associated with ER positivity (OR = 0.33, 95% CI: 0.15–0.71, P = 0.004) and PR positivity (OR = 0.37, 95% CI: 0.16–0.85, P = 0.019), while positively correlated with high Ki67 levels (adjusted OR = 2.17, 95% CI: 1.01–4.70, P < 0.049) and TNBC classification (adjusted OR = 4.24, 95% CI: 1.45–12.36, P < 0.008) (Table 2).

The prognostic association between UGP2 and BC

Survival analysis across multiple platforms consistently associated elevated UGP2 expression with adverse clinical outcomes. KMplot-derived OS curves stratified by median UGP2 levels demonstrated progressive deterioration of prognosis with increasing expression (P < 0.05, Fig. 3A). This pattern remained robust in the 232180_at validation cohort, where UGP2 levels above the auto-determined cutoff correlated with 1.58-fold increased mortality risk (95% CI: 1.2–2.08, P = 0.00089, Fig. 3B).

Notably, UGP2-high patients exhibited 19% higher recurrence risk (RFS, P < 0.001, Fig. 3C) and 19% greater metastasis risk (DMFS, P < 0.001, Fig. 3D) versus low-expressing counterparts. Univariate Cox analysis of TCGA-BRCA data confirmed UGP2’s prognostic value (HR = 1.51, 95% CI: 1.04–2.17, P = 0.028, Fig. 3E). Critically, multivariable analysis incorporating Age, T Stage, N Stage and TNM Stage maintained UGP2’s independent predictive capacity (HR = 1.48, 95% CI: 1.02–2.15, P = 0.037, Fig. 3E), establishing its clinical relevance beyond conventional biomarkers.

Analysis of UGP2 at the single-cell level

scRNA-seq data from five BC specimens underwent rigorous quality filtering (Fig. 4A). Normalization via the SCTransform pipeline enabled robust detection of highly variable genes for downstream analysis (Fig. 4B). Unsupervised clustering identified eight biologically distinct cell populations through canonical marker expression profiles (Fig. 4C), with UMAP dimensionality reduction providing spatial resolution of cluster distributions (Fig. 4D). Strikingly, UGP2 exhibited preferential expression within epithelial cell subsets and cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs), demonstrating cell type-specific enrichment patterns (Fig. 4E).

Cell-cell communication profiling positioned CAFs as pivotal signaling hubs, with 68% of ligand-receptor interactions originating from this cluster (Fig. 5A). Collagen-mediated signaling of CAF-driven crosstalk (Fig. 5B), suggesting extracellular matrix remodeling as a key pathogenic mechanism. CNV quantification confirmed pronounced genomic instability in epithelial cells and CAFs (Fig. 5C), aligning with their tumor-promoting phenotypes.

Notably, UGP2-high cellular shows a higher CNV burden than the cell subpopulation with low expression (P < 0.001, Fig. 5D), implicating UGP2 in genomic instability regulation. Furthermore, based on the high and low expression of UGP2, the differentially expressed genes among different cell clusters were obtained for subsequent functional analysis (Fig. 5.E).

The biological function of UGP2 in BC

Transcriptomic analysis of TCGA-BRCA data highlighted UGP2’s involvement in extracellular matrix regulation and developmental processes (Fig. 6A). Pathway enrichment further linked UGP2 to oncogenic signaling cascades, including MAPK and Rap1 pathways (Fig. 6B). At single-cell resolution, UGP2-high epithelial cells exhibited functional enrichment in adhesion-related pathways and collagen metabolism (Fig. 6C). Parallel analysis in CAFs confirmed UGP2’s consistent role in extracellular matrix dynamics and cell-matrix interactions (Fig. 6D).

Knockdown of UGP2 in vitro can inhibit the proliferation, migration and invasion of BC

The oncogenic role of UGP2 in BC progression was rigorously investigated through siRNA-mediated knockdown experiments. Initial screening of BC cell lines revealed significantly higher UGP2 expression in MDA-MB-231 and MDA-MB-468 cells compared to other models (P < 0.05), establishing these BC lines as suitable systems for functional interrogation (Fig. 7A). Transient siRNA transfection effectively suppressed UGP2 expression at both transcriptional and translational levels (P < 0.05), confirming target specificity (Fig. 7B).

Functional characterization demonstrated UGP2’s critical involvement in malignant phenotypes. Colony formation assays revealed marked suppression of proliferative capacity following UGP2 knockdown (P < 0.05), with reduced colony numbers and sizes compared to controls (Fig. 7C). Scratch wound healing assays showed significantly impaired migratory ability, as evidenced by delayed wound closure in UGP2-depleted cells (P < 0.01 at 48 h post-scratch, Fig. 7D). Transwell invasion assays further confirmed diminished invasive potential, with UGP2-silenced cells exhibiting reduced penetration through Matrigel-coated membranes (P < 0.05, Fig. 7E).

These coordinated findings across complementary assays establish UGP2 as a key mediator of TNBC aggressiveness, directly linking its expression to essential oncogenic processes including proliferation, migration, and invasion.

Discussion

This study represents the first comprehensive investigation into the biological functions of UGP2 in BC. Integrated multi-omics analyses revealed that elevated UGP2 expression demonstrates a significant inverse correlation with clinical outcomes in BC patients, with particularly pronounced overexpression observed in TNBC tissues. This distinct expression pattern positions UGP2 as a potential molecular marker for BC progression.

sCNA acquisition has been recognized as a major pathogenic driver in human diseases, particularly in cancer pathogenesis14. Comprehensive sCNA analysis revealed two distinct amplification patterns at the UGP2 locus in TNBC tissues: focal high-copy amplifications specifically targeting the UGP2 genomic region, potentially driving transcriptional upregulation through dosage effects; and broad chromosomal arm-level gains that may facilitate tumor heterogeneity and adaptive evolution by inducing large-scale genomic instability15. These dual amplification mechanisms collectively establish the mechanistic basis for UGP2 overexpression, which exacerbates TNBC malignancy through enhanced glyconucleotide metabolic flux. This metabolic reprogramming subsequently promotes tumor cell proliferation and confers chemotherapy resistance, thereby driving aggressive phenotypic manifestations in MDPI: 36973407, 375887518–17 .

Single-cell CNA scoring analysis provides complementary support for these findings: Tumor cells in the UGP2-high expression cohort exhibited marked genomic instability, suggesting a potential synergistic interaction between UGP2 overexpression and chromosomal instability in driving tumor evolution. This evidence positions UGP2 not merely as a potential biomarker for TNBC, but rather implicates its dual oncogenic mechanism involving both metabolic reprogramming and genomic destabilization. These coordinated processes appear to cooperatively potentiate malignant progression in BC through enhanced cellular adaptability and evolutionary selection pressure.

Complementing these findings, single-cell transcriptomic profiling reveals that UGP2 demonstrates elevated expression not only in malignant epithelial cells but also in CAFs within the tumor microenvironment. As pivotal stromal components, CAFs critically drive tumor progression through extracellular matrix (ECM) remodeling, inflammatory activation, chemoresistance, and immunosuppression18. Their pronounced enrichment in TNBC facilitates angiogenesis, metastatic dissemination, and stemness maintenance, thereby exacerbating disease aggressiveness19. Intercellular communication analysis identifies collagen hypersecretion as the primary mediator of CAF-driven oncogenic crosstalk with epithelial cells. Functional enrichment studies further demonstrate that UGP2 modulates collagen biosynthesis in CAFs, generating stiff, densely crosslinked collagen networks that promote tumor cell proliferation and survival20. Furthermore, ECM reorganization provides biomechanical support for cancer cells, enhancing their invasive potential via mechanotransduction signaling pathways21.

UGP2 serves as a central metabolic orchestrator in TNBC, catalyzing the conversion of glucose-1-phosphate to UDP-glucose – a critical precursor governing glycogen metabolism, protein glycosylation, and ECM component biosynthesis. Its overexpression exacerbates TNBC’s characteristic metabolic hyperactivity, particularly the enhanced Warburg effect marked by extreme glycolytic dependency22. Mechanistically, UGP2 fuels malignancy through a tripartite regulatory network: (1) Sustaining bioenergetic demands via dynamic glycogen synthesis/degradation cycles that provide ATP and biosynthetic intermediates, while concurrently modulating lipid metabolism to support membrane proliferation22–24; (2) Amplifying UDP-glucose flux to drive ECM remodeling through hyaluronan overproduction and collagen crosslinking, establishing biomechanical niches that facilitate invasion via integrin-mediated mechanotransduction; (3) Activating the hexosamine pathway to elevate O-GlcNAc modifications, which epigenetically dysregulate metabolic sensors and stabilize oncogenic transcription factors.

Furthermore, UGP2-mediated metabolic crosstalk sustains CAF activation through paracrine UDP-glucose signaling, fostering a pro-fibrotic microenvironment that synergistically enhances chemoresistance and metastatic competency. This self-reinforcing metabolic-epigenetic-stromal network not only explains TNBC’s therapeutic recalcitrance but also positions UGP2 as a multidimensional therapeutic nexus, offering opportunities for co-targeting metabolic vulnerabilities and stromal co-evolution mechanisms. In this context, the feasibility of targeting UGP2 has been preliminarily validated: studies have demonstrated that neutralizing antibodies against UGP2 effectively disrupt its biological function25, and clinical reports suggest that patients with UGP2-ALK fusion exhibit favorable responses to ALK inhibitors26, providing direct evidence for targeting UGP2. Consequently, the development of tumor-specific UGP2 inhibitors, or their combination with immune checkpoint inhibitors, represents a promising avenue for future therapeutic strategies.

This study provides the first evidence establishing UGP2 as a functional oncogene in BC, particularly within TNBC, highlighting its potential as both a prognostic biomarker and therapeutic target. While our integrated analyses reveal UGP2’s dual role in tumor-intrinsic metabolic reprogramming and stromal microenvironment remodeling, certain limitations require acknowledgment: the absence of comprehensive in vitro mechanistic validation, lack of immunohistochemical comparisons with normal breast tissues, and potential selection bias inherent in single-center clinical data. Future investigations should prioritize mechanistic studies elucidating UGP2’s context-dependent regulation and therapeutic vulnerabilities, particularly through advanced models recapitulating tumor-stroma interactions, to advance its clinical translation in TNBC management.

Conclusion

Our research reveals that UGP2 is a potential prognostic marker and therapeutic target for BC. The increase in its expression is mainly concentrated in the subtypes of TNBC. UGP2 has a significant correlation with CAFs in the BC microenvironment. Knockdown of UGP2 in vitro can inhibit the proliferation, migration and invasion of BC cells.

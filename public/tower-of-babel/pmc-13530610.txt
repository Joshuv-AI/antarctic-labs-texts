Poster 262. A Phase 1 Dose-Escalation Study of Indocyanine Green Fluorescence Imaging for Intraoperative Assessment of Rotator Cuff Vascularity During Arthroscopy
Authors: Rushani K Cameron, Griffith Gosnell, Larry Chen, Kirk A Campbell, Nathaniel G Mercer, Guillem P Gonzalez-Lomas, Zachary I Li, Laith Jazrawi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjective:Despite advances in arthroscopic techniques, rotator cuff (RC) retear rates remain high, emphasizing the need for improved intraoperative assessments of tendon perfusion. Tendon vascularity is a critical factor in RC healing, yet real-time intraoperative tools for assessing perfusion remain limited. Therefore, a reliable and practical method to assess tendon perfusion intraoperatively could significantly enhance surgical decision-making and potentially improve clinical outcomes. Indocyanine green (ICG) fluorescence imaging offers potential utility in visualizing vascularity during arthroscopy. This Phase I dose-escalation study evaluated the feasibility, safety, dose-dependent behavior, and potential clinical utility of ICG imaging during arthroscopic rotator cuff repair.Methods:Twenty-one patients undergoing arthroscopic RC repair were prospectively enrolled to receive one of four intravenous ICG doses (7.5, 12.5, 25, or 50 mg). Intraoperative fluorescence was captured using the Stryker Advanced Imaging Modality (AIM) system. Surgeons assessed fluorescence visibility, clinical relevance, and actionability using standardized Likert scales. For analysis, patients were stratified into low-dose (<=25 mg, n=12) and high-dose (50 mg, n=9) groups. Univariate and multivariable analyses were conducted to evaluate predictors of signal quality and fluorescence onset (P < 0.05).Results:The mean age of the cohort was 57.3 years; mean BMI was 30.1 kg/m²; and 52% were male. Most patients (71.4%) had Goutallier Grade 0 fatty infiltration. Supraspinatus involvement was present in 95.2% of cases. Fluorescence visibility was higher in the 50 mg group (55.6% 'Strongly Agree') than in the low-dose group (16.7%), though not statistically significant (P = 0.322). Higher ICG dose (mg/kg) was significantly associated with fluorescence visibility (P = 0.0029), while BMI >=30 kg/m² was linked to faster signal onset (P = 0.0035). However, clinical significance and actionability ratings were low across all groups. Multivariate analysis identified BMI as a significant negative predictor of clinical significance (P < 0.0001), suggesting higher adiposity may contribute to diffuse, nonspecific fluorescence.Conclusions:ICG fluorescence imaging is safe and technically feasible during shoulder arthroscopy. Our study findings support potential utility of ICG in orthopedic surgery, specifically when visualizing tendon perfusion during arthroscopic RC procedures. We results showed that higher doses improve signal visibility, while elevated BMI appears to influence ICG kinetics. Despite promising dose-dependent trends, fluorescence was not deemed clinically significant or actionable.

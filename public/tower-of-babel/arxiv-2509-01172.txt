Asynchronous and Stochastic Distributed Resource Allocation
Qiang Li, Michal Yemini, Hoi-To Wai

Abstract
This work proposes and studies the distributed resource allocation problem in asynchronous and stochastic settings. We consider a distributed system with multiple workers and a coordinating server with heterogeneous computation and communication times. We explore an approximate stochastic primal-dual approach with the aim of 1) adhering to the resource budget constraints, 2) allowing for the asynchronicity between the workers and the server, and 3) relying on the locally available stochastic gradients. We analyze our Asyn chronous stochastic P rimal- D ual ( Asyn-PD ) algorithm and prove its convergence in the second moment to the saddle point solution of the approximate problem at the rate of 𝒪 ⁡ ( 1 / t ) {\cal O}(1/t) , where t t is the iteration number. Furthermore, we verify our algorithm numerically to validate the analytically derived convergence results, and demonstrate the advantages of utilizing our asynchronous algorithm rather than deploying a synchronous algorithm where the server must wait until it gets update from all workers.



I
Introduction

We consider the distributed resource allocation problem for a system with
n
workers and one coordinating server, stated as the constrained optimization problem:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \min_{{\bm{\theta}}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},i\in[n]}&\quad f({\bm{\theta}})\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}({\bm{\theta}}_{i})\\
\text{subject to }&\quad\textstyle g_{j}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}A_{ji}{\bm{\theta}}_{i}\right)\leq 0,~~j\in[m],\\
&\quad{\bm{\theta}}_{i}\in{\cal C}_{i},~~i\in[n].\end{aligned}

(1)

For each worker
i\in[n]
,

i

th decision vector and

th (resource) constraint. In the special case with
A_{ji}=1/n

For brevity, we denote by

the joint decision vector.
Lastly,

is the local cost function.
In general, we have

\displaystyle f_{i}({\bm{\theta}}_{i})\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\mathbb{E}_{Z_{i}\sim{\cal D}_{i}}[\ell_{i}({\bm{\theta}}_{i};Z_{i})]

(2)

where

Assuming a scenario where the workers are directly connected to a coordinating server, forming a star-like topology.
To develop distributed solutions for (

which solves the deterministic of (

A longstanding issue with distributed optimization is the
straggler effects
caused by discrepancies in computation and communication speeds of heterogeneous workers. With a
synchronous
algorithm, where the server waits until the updates of all workers arrive before executing its coordinating step, the straggler effect can lead to a significant slowdown to the system when

1
(a).
An obvious remedy is to develop
asynchronous
algorithms
[
13
]
where the workers and server follow an independent schedule in updating their models; see Fig.
1
(b).
Such architecture is relevant to power networks where asynchronous control is common
[
14
]
.
We note that for finite-sum optimization problems whose aim is to minimize
\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}({\bm{\theta}})
in the absence of coupling constraints like in (
1
), asynchronous stochastic gradient (SG) algorithms have been extensively studied
[
15
]
. Examples include
[
16
]
which studied the behavior of delayed SG methods,
[
17
,
18
]
which developed tight bounds for variants of asynchronous SG methods, etc.

For constrained optimization problems of the form (
1
), the study of asynchronous distributed algorithms is limited. Existing works require either synchronizing the dual variables periodically
[
19
]
, or block-based parallel computations
[
20
]
, and rely on deterministic gradients.
This motivated the current paper to study:

Can the problem (
1
) be solved by an asynchronous & stochastic distributed algorithm?

We provide an affirmative answer to this question by proposing an asynchronous primal-dual (
Asyn-PD
) algorithm which features two key aspects:
1) A buffered central server that updates the dual variable upon receiving any worker’s local model and broadcasts dual correction terms, which are stored in local worker’s buffers for future updates without interrupting workers.
2) Buffered workers that operate independently, proceeding with local updates immediately after computation, ensuring that incoming server messages do not disrupt ongoing computations.
Our main contributions are:

We show that the
Asyn-PD
algorithm converges in expectation at a rate of
{\cal O}(1/t)
with respect to the squared distance to the unique saddle point solution of a regularized version of (
1
) under mild conditions, where
t
is the number of iterations. The algorithm does not require a synchronized communication mechanism.
To our best knowledge, this is the first analysis to apply the asynchronous primal-dual algorithm to stochastic distributed resource allocation.

II
Problem Statement and Algorithm Design

This section reviews the synchronous primal-dual (
Sync-PD
) algorithm for the distributed resource allocation problem in (
1

asynchronous primal-dual
(
Asyn-PD

For simplicity, throughout this paper, we will consider a special case of (

A_{ji}=1/n
for all
i,j

II-A

Preliminaries on Distributed Resource Allocation

Observe that in (
1
), the objective function is decomposable while the only coupling between the local decision variables
\{{\bm{\theta}}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
lies in the constraints shaped by
g_{j}(\cdot)
,
j\in[m]
. To derive a distributed algorithm, we adopt the primal-dual framework. Introducing the dual variables
{\bm{\lambda}}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m}
, under some regularity conditions, solving (
1
) is equivalent to finding a saddle point to the following Lagrangian function:

\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}({\bm{\theta}}_{i})+{\bm{\lambda}}^{\top}{\bm{g}}(\overline{\bm{\theta}}),~~\overline{\bm{\theta}}:=(1/n)\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\bm{\theta}}_{i},

(3)

where
{\bm{g}}(\overline{\bm{\theta}}):=[g_{1}(\overline{\bm{\theta}});\cdots;g_{m}(\overline{\bm{\theta}})]
collects the
m
coupling resource allocation constraints.

Similar to
[
10
]
, we apply the dual smoothing technique through introducing the Tikhonov regularization to (
3
). Let
\upsilon>0
, we consider the regularized Lagrangian function:

\displaystyle\textstyle{\cal L}({\bm{\theta}},{\bm{\lambda}})\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}({\bm{\theta}}_{i})+{\bm{\lambda}}^{\top}{\bm{g}}(\overline{\bm{\theta}})-\frac{\upsilon}{2}\left\|{\bm{\lambda}}\right\|^{2},

(4)

Moreover, we consider the compact set
\bm{\Lambda}\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m}
and the following min-max problem:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \max_{{\bm{\lambda}}\in\bm{\Lambda}}\min_{{\bm{\theta}}_{i}\in{\cal C}_{i},i\in[n]}&{\cal L}({\bm{\theta}},{\bm{\lambda}}).\end{aligned}

(5)

Notice that if each of
g_{j}(\cdot)
is convex and each of
f_{i}
is
\mu
-strongly-convex, then
{\cal L}(\cdot)
is
(\mu,\upsilon)
-strongly convex-concave. Moreover, if
{\bm{\lambda}}^{\prime}\in\bm{\Lambda}
(i.e.,
{\rm diam}(\bm{\Lambda})
is sufficiently large), the unique saddle point solution to (
5
) will be an
{\cal O}(\sqrt{\upsilon})
approximation to the saddle point set of (
3
); see
[
10
, Lemma 3.3]
.

To this end, a natural approach for solving (
5
) is to adopt the primal-dual gradient descent-ascent method: for
k\geq 0
,

\displaystyle{\bm{\theta}}_{i}^{k+1}

\displaystyle={\cal P}_{{\cal C}_{i}}\left[{\bm{\theta}}_{i}^{k}-\gamma_{k+1}{\nabla}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{i}}{\cal L}({\bm{\theta}}^{k},{\bm{\lambda}}^{k})\right],~i\in[n],

(6a)

\displaystyle{\bm{\lambda}}^{k+1}

\displaystyle={\cal P}_{\bm{\Lambda}}\left[{\bm{\lambda}}^{k}+\gamma_{k+1}{\nabla}_{{\bm{\lambda}}}{\cal L}({\bm{\theta}}^{k},{\bm{\lambda}}^{k})\right]

(6b)

Note that the primal-dual gradients can be computed as

\displaystyle{\nabla}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{i}}{\cal L}({\bm{\theta}}^{k},{\bm{\lambda}}^{k})

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{:}}={\nabla}f_{i}({\bm{\theta}}_{i}^{k})+\textstyle\frac{1}{n}\big({\rm J}\,{\bm{g}}(\overline{\bm{\theta}}^{k})\big){\bm{\lambda}}^{k},~i\in[n],

\displaystyle\textstyle{\nabla}_{{\bm{\lambda}}}{\cal L}({\bm{\theta}}^{k},{\bm{\lambda}}^{k})

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{:}}={\bm{g}}(\overline{\bm{\theta}}^{k})-\upsilon{\bm{\lambda}}^{k},

where
{\rm J}\,{\bm{g}}(\overline{\bm{\theta}}):=[{\nabla}g_{1}(\overline{\bm{\theta}})~\cdots~{\nabla}g_{m}(\overline{\bm{\theta}})]\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times m}
is the Jacobian for the map
{\bm{g}}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
at
\overline{\bm{\theta}}^{k}
. We denote the above algorithm (
6
) as the synchronous primal-dual (
Sync-PD
) algorithm.

We notice that (
6
) can be implemented via a distributed system. At any iteration
k\geq 0
, each worker receives the message containing
\frac{1}{n}\big({\rm J}\,{\bm{g}}(\overline{\bm{\theta}}^{k})\big){\bm{\lambda}}^{k}
computed by the server and performs a local update following (
6a
). Meanwhile, the server performs update for
{\bm{\lambda}}^{k}
(
6b
) based on the latest aggregated decision vector
\overline{\bm{\theta}}^{k}
. To prepare for the
k+1
th iteration, for the workers, the updated local decision vector
{\bm{\theta}}_{i}^{k+1}
has to be sent to the server and the server needs to broadcast
\frac{1}{n}\big({\rm J}\,{\bm{g}}(\overline{\bm{\theta}}^{k+1})\big){\bm{\lambda}}^{k+1}
to the workers. We observe that this process is distributed where it does not require the
i
th worker to know the coupling constraints nor the local cost function of other workers, and vice versa.

However, the above process requires
synchronous
computations where the workers and server are not allowed to proceed to the
k+1
th iteration until the workers and server can respectively receive
\frac{1}{n}\big({\rm J}\,{\bm{g}}(\overline{\bm{\theta}}^{k+1})\big){\bm{\lambda}}^{k+1}
,
\{{\bm{\theta}}_{i}^{k+1}\}_{i=1}^{n}
from each other.
If one (or more) of the workers is a
straggler
such that it takes a significantly longer time to execute the gradient step (
6a
) and/or to send the updated model to the server, then the distributed process may be slowed down significantly. This issue is illustrated in Fig.
1
(a) where the fast workers are left idle for a long time during the process, leading to inefficient use of computation resources.

II-B

Asynchronous and Stochastic Primal-Dual Algorithm

The straggler effect naturally leads us to consider an
asynchronous update approach
where workers can continue their computations without waiting for others and the server’s updates, e.g., see Fig.
1
(b). To mimic (
6
), the server maintains a buffer that stores the last received decision vector from each worker and the update of
{\bm{\lambda}}^{k}
is performed using
staled decision vectors
, subsequently the worker’s local update will be affected by
staled decision vectors
too.

To formally describe and analyze the effects of asynchronous updates, we assume that the server’s computation time is negligible compared to that of the workers’ and the iteration index
k\geq 0
counts the number of server’s or workers’ updates. We define

\begin{split}\tau_{i}^{k}\geq 0~&\text{-- delay of ${\bm{\theta}}_{i}$ stored at the server, $i\in[n]$,}\\
\tau_{s}^{k}\geq 0~&\text{-- broadcasting delay for $\big({\rm J}\,{\bm{g}}(\overline{\bm{\theta}})\big){\bm{\lambda}}$ from server.}\end{split}

That is, at the iteration
k
, the server has access to the aggregated and delayed decision vector
\overline{\bm{b}}^{k}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\bm{\theta}}_{i}^{k-\tau_{i}^{k}}
, and the workers receive a delayed message given by
{\textstyle\frac{1}{n}\big({\rm J}{\bm{g}}(\overline{\bm{b}}^{k-\tau_{s}^{k}})\big){\bm{\lambda}}^{k-\tau_{s}^{k}}}
.
Furthermore,

{\cal A}_{i}^{k}
is the event where worker
i
completes its computation at iteration
k
, and
{\cal E}_{k}
is the event where the server receives at least one local decision vector at iteration
k
.

Define the sampled version of the Lagrangian function as:

\displaystyle\textstyle L({\bm{\theta}},{\bm{\lambda}};{\bm{z}})\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\ell_{i}({\bm{\theta}}_{i};z_{i})+{\bm{\lambda}}^{\top}{\bm{g}}(\overline{\bm{\theta}})-\frac{\upsilon}{2}\left\|{\bm{\lambda}}\right\|^{2},

(7)

We consider the asynchronous primal-dual (
Asyn-PD
) algorithm for tackling (
5
):

\displaystyle{\bm{\theta}}_{i}^{k+1}={\cal P}_{{\cal C}_{i}}\left[{\bm{\theta}}_{i}^{k}-\gamma_{k+1}\,\mathds{1}({\cal A}_{i}^{k})\,G_{i,{\bm{\theta}}}^{k}({\bm{\theta}}_{i}^{k},Z_{i}^{k+1})\right],\forall i\in[n],

\displaystyle{\bm{\lambda}}^{k+1}={\cal P}_{\bm{\Lambda}}\left[{\bm{\lambda}}^{k}+\gamma_{k+1}\,\mathds{1}({\cal E}_{k})\,G_{{\bm{\lambda}}}^{k}({\bm{\lambda}}^{k})\big)\right],

(8)

where
\mathds{1}({\cal E})
is the indicator function that equals
1
if the event
{\cal E}
is true and is
0
otherwise,
Z_{i}^{k+1}\sim{\cal D}_{i}
is drawn independently. We also observe that

\displaystyle G_{i,{\bm{\theta}}}^{k}({\bm{\theta}}_{i}^{k},Z_{i}^{k+1})

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{:}}={\nabla}\ell_{i}({\bm{\theta}}_{i}^{k},Z_{i}^{k+1})+\textstyle\frac{1}{n}\big({\rm J}{\bm{g}}(\overline{\bm{b}}^{k-\tau_{s}^{k}})\big){\bm{\lambda}}^{k-\tau_{s}^{k}},

\displaystyle G_{{\bm{\lambda}}}^{k}({\bm{\lambda}}^{k})

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{:}}={\bm{g}}(\overline{\bm{b}}^{k})-v{\bm{\lambda}}^{k},

The above represent the delayed primal-dual stochastic gradients with respect to the primal and dual variables, and they approximate the primal-dual gradients for the sampled Lagrangian function
L({\bm{\theta}}^{k},{\bm{\lambda}}^{k};{\bm{Z}}^{k+1})
. The
Asyn-PD
algorithm operates through two main components: the server update procedure and the worker update procedure, as detailed in Algorithms
1
&
2
. Note that both routines are only triggered when messages are received from each side.

As observed, the server updates the dual variable only when it receives a new local model copy, as indicated by
\mathds{1}({\cal E}_{k})
. For each worker,
\mathds{1}({\cal A}_{i}^{k})
determines whether worker
i

retains the previous iterate

Note that the
Asyn-PD
algorithm involves delays in both directions of computation and communication delays between workers and server.
Different from the ASSP (Asynchronous Stochastic Saddle Point) algorithm studied by
[
21
]
, which primarily considers delays caused by communication channels, the
Asyn-PD
algorithm operates in a fully asynchronous setting where delays arise not only from communication latency but also from heterogeneous and varying computation speeds across workers, making the asynchrony substantially more complex.
Compared to
[
19
]
, the
Asyn-PD
algorithm does not require a double loop design which resets the buffer variable periodically. Instead, the
Asyn-PD
algorithm allows the workers and server to perform continuous updates. These challenges led us to develop a new convergence analysis in the next section.

V
Conclusions

This study introduces an asynchronous primal-dual algorithm designed for stochastic distributed resource allocation. The proposed algorithm
Asyn-PD
is shown to converge to an approximate primal-dual saddle-point solution at a rate of
{\cal O}({1/t})
. It exhibits better efficiency through fully utilizing the computational power of the distributed workers in the event of stragglers, compared to the synchronous
Sync-PD
algorithm. Numerical experiments corroborate our findings.

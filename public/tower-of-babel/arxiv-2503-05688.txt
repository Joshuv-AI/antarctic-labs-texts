Boundary stratifications of Hurwitz spaces
Darragh Glynn

Abstract
. Let ℋ \mathcal{H} be a Hurwitz space that parametrises holomorphic maps to ℙ 1 \mathbb{P}^{1} . Abramovich, Corti and Vistoli, building on work of Harris and Mumford, describe a compactification ℋ ¯ \overline{\mathcal{H}} with a natural boundary stratification. We show that the irreducible strata of ℋ ¯ \overline{\mathcal{H}} are in bijection with combinatorial objects called decorated trees (up to a suitable equivalence), and that containment of irreducible strata is given by edge contraction of decorated trees. This combinatorial description allows us to define a tropical Hurwitz space, which we identify with the skeleton of the Berkovich analytification of ℋ ¯ \overline{\mathcal{H}} . The tropical Hurwitz space that we obtain is a refinement of a version defined by Cavalieri, Markwig and Ranganathan. We also provide an implementation that computes the stratification of ℋ ¯ \overline{\mathcal{H}} , and discuss applications to complex dynamics.



1.
Introduction

A
Hurwitz space

\mathcal{H}
is a moduli space parametrising holomorphic maps of compact Riemann surfaces (with certain points of the source and target surfaces marked) that satisfy prescribed branching conditions, up to pre- and post-composition with isomorphisms. Hurwitz spaces are complex orbifolds that trace back to Hurwitz
[
Hur91
]
and their geometry is governed by rich combinatorics
[
Ful69
]
,
[
RW06
]
,
[
Moc95
]
.

Harris and Mumford give a compactification of
\mathcal{H}
by a space of
admissible covers
– which are maps between nodal Riemann surfaces – in order to study the moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{g}
of genus-
g
Riemann surfaces
[
HM82
]
. While the space of admissible covers is not normal, its normalisation
\overline{\mathcal{H}}
is smooth, and Abramovich, Corti and Vistoli give a moduli interpretation to
\overline{\mathcal{H}}
as a space that parametrises
balanced twisted covers

[
ACV03
]
. The boundary
\overline{\mathcal{H}}\smallsetminus\mathcal{H}
is a normal crossings divisor, and this endows
\overline{\mathcal{H}}
with a stratification by locally closed strata.

The compactification
\overline{\mathcal{H}}
is an analogue of the Deligne-Mumford compactification
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
of the moduli space of genus-
g
Riemann surfaces with
n
marked points. In both cases, when marked points collide, they ‘bubble off’ onto a new component Riemann surface. In
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
this produces a nodal Riemann surface, and in
\overline{\mathcal{H}}
this produces a certain type of map between nodal Riemann surfaces.

The boundary
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}\smallsetminus\mathcal{M}_{g,n}
is also a normal crossings divisor, endowing
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
with a stratification. In fact, if
\mathcal{H}
parametrises maps whose target is a genus-
g
,
n
-marked Riemann surface, then the natural forgetful map to
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}
is a finite covering map that extends to the boundary
\pi_{\text{tgt}}:\overline{\mathcal{H}}\rightarrow\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
and respects the stratifications of both spaces: a stratum of
\overline{\mathcal{H}}
is a connected component of the preimage of a stratum of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
under
\pi_{\text{tgt}}
.

The stratification of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
is well understood: the strata are in bijection with isomorphism classes of
n
-marked genus-
g
stable graphs
(see Section
2.2.3
), and containment of closures of strata corresponds to edge contraction of these graphs.

In this paper, for
\mathcal{H}
a Hurwitz space that parametrises maps to
\mathbb{P}^{1}
, we provide an analogous description of the strata of
\overline{\mathcal{H}}
and of containment of closures of strata. The prescribed branching conditions that determine
\mathcal{H}
are encoded in a
portrait
(Definition
2.5
), and for
P
a portrait, we define combinatorial objects called
P
-decorated trees
(Definition
4.1
) and an equivalence relation on
P
-decorated trees called
Hurwitz equivalence
(Definition
4.6
). We assign to each point of
\overline{\mathcal{H}}
a Hurwitz equivalence class of
P
-decorated trees, and we prove that two points of
\overline{\mathcal{H}}
are in the same stratum if and only if they are assigned the same Hurwitz equivalence class of
P
-decorated trees. The theorem is proved in Section
6
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a Hurwitz space that parametrises maps to
\mathbb{P}^{1}
, with
P
its defining portrait. The irreducible strata of the compactification
\overline{\mathcal{H}}
are in bijection with the set
\operatorname{Stab}_{0,B}(P)
of
P
-decorated trees up to Hurwitz equivalence. Furthermore, containment of closures of strata in
\overline{\mathcal{H}}
corresponds to edge contraction of
P
-decorated trees.

A
P
-decorated tree consists of an abstract tree together with a cyclic ordering of the half-edges around each vertex and an assignment of a permutation to each half-edge (with some cycles of the permutations possibly labelled). This object keeps track of the monodromy information associated to a holomorphic map of Riemann surfaces as marked points collide.

It is straightforward to convert the theorem to an algorithm that computes the stratification of
\overline{\mathcal{H}}
– code is available on request, and will soon be available on the author’s website.

Remark 1.2
.

Our
P
-decorated trees are a refinement of the
combinatorial admissible covers
defined by Cavalieri, Markwig and Ranganathan
[
CMR16
]
. In their paper, they assign to each point of
\overline{\mathcal{H}}
a combinatorial admissible cover. Points that share a given combinatorial admissible cover form a union of strata of the same dimension – but not, in general, an irreducible stratum. That is, points may have the same combinatorial admissible cover but live in different strata, or even in different connected components of
\overline{\mathcal{H}}
(see Example
1.3
). We relate
P
-decorated trees to combinatorial admissible covers in Section
7.1
.

Roughly speaking, a puncture parametrises a ‘degenerate’ map obtained by pinching a simple loop

\mathbb{P}^{1}\smallsetminus\{b_{1},b_{2},b_{3},b_{4}\}
and its preimage

1.1.
Proof strategy

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a Hurwitz space parametrising maps to
\mathbb{P}^{1}
and let
P
be its portrait. Koch
[
Koc13
, Section 2]
shows that every connected component of
\mathcal{H}
is the quotient of a certain Teichmüller space
\mathcal{T}
by the action of a
liftable mapping class group
(see Section
2.7.2
). By Hinich and Vaintrob
[
HV10
]
, each action extends to the
augmented
Teichmüller space
\overline{\mathcal{T}}
(introduced in
[
Ber74
]
) with quotient isomorphic to a connected component of
\overline{\mathcal{H}}
.

Augmented Teichmüller space decomposes into locally closed, connected strata that are indexed by multicurves on a marked sphere, and the quotient map sends a stratum of
\overline{\mathcal{T}}
to a stratum of
\overline{\mathcal{H}}
(Proposition
2.9
). Thus, the irreducible strata of the connected component of
\overline{\mathcal{H}}
are indexed by orbits of multicurves under the group action. These orbits are captured by Hurwitz equivalence classes of
P
-decorated trees – this is the content of Proposition
6.5
. The proof relies on the theory of
embedded stable trees
and
P
-decorated trees developed in Sections
3
,
4
and
5
. A key step is Proposition
3.4
, which tells us that two embedded stable trees that are dual to a fixed multicurve are connected by a sequence of operations called
braid moves
1
1

1

This is reminiscent of the result in
[
BLMW22
]
that the
complex of trees
is connected, although our basic objects are different.
.

Containment of closures of strata in augmented Teichmüller space is given by the reverse containment of multicurves, which in turn is given by edge contraction of their dual trees, and so the containment part of Theorem
1.1
follows straightforwardly.

1.2.
Relation to tropical geometry

Suppose
X
is a subvariety of the algebraic torus
(K^{*})^{n}
, for
K
a valued field. Classical tropical geometry explains how to associate to
X
a piecewise affine subspace
{\operatorname{trop}(X)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}}
– the
tropicalisation
of
X
– using the coordinate-wise valuation map. The tropicalisation is a ‘combinatorial shadow’ of
X
: certain features of
X
(such as its dimension) can be read from
\operatorname{trop}(X)

[
MS15
]
.

There are several related constructions associating a real polyhedral space to an algebro-geometric object that we may also interpret as tropicalisations, as outlined by Abramovich, Caporaso and Payne
[
ACP15
, Section 1.3]
. In increasing generality: a toric variety
X
yields an embedded cone complex
\Sigma(X)
called its
fan
, a toroidal embedding of schemes
U\subset X
without self-intersections yields a cone complex
\Sigma(X)

[
KKMSD73
]
, and a toroidal embedding of schemes
U\subset X
with self-intersections yields a generalised
2
2

2

Generalised
means we may quotient cones by automorphisms.
cone complex
\Sigma(X)

[
Thu07
]
.

The last case generalises straightforwardly to the case of a toroidal embedding
\mathcal{U}\subset\mathcal{X}
of Deligne-Mumford stacks, also yielding a generalised cone complex
\Sigma(\mathcal{X})

[
ACP15
]
. It is natural to consider the associated extended
3
3

3

Extended
means cone coordinates are allowed to equal
\infty
.
generalised cone complex
\overline{\Sigma}(\mathcal{X})
, called the
skeleton
of
\mathcal{X}
– it lives naturally inside the
Berkovich analytification
of the coarse space of
\mathcal{X}
as the image of a canonical retraction map.

The skeleton
\overline{\Sigma}(\mathcal{X})
decomposes into extended generalised cones according to the natural stratification of
\mathcal{X}

[
ACP15
, Proposition 6.2.6]
. For instance, the inclusion
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}\subset\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
is a toroidal embedding of Deligne-Mumford stacks, and the decomposition result yields a description of
\overline{\Sigma}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n})
in terms of
n
-marked genus-
g
stable graphs
[
ACP15
, Theorem 1.2.1]
.

The inclusion
\mathcal{H}\subset\overline{\mathcal{H}}
is also a toroidal embedding of Deligne-Mumford stacks. Combining Theorem
1.1
with the decomposition result and analysis carried out by Cavalieri, Markwig and Ranganathan for the case of
\overline{\mathcal{H}}

[
CMR16
, Section 4]
, we provide an analogous description of the skeleton
\overline{\Sigma}(\overline{\mathcal{H}})
in terms of
P
-decorated trees.

More precisely, suppose
\mathcal{H}
is defined by the portrait
P
. A Hurwitz-equivalence class of
P
-decorated trees with
k
edges defines an extended cone
(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\cup\{\infty\})^{k}
. By gluing these extended cones together as prescribed by edge contraction of
P
-decorated trees, we obtain an extended cone complex
\overline{\mathcal{H}}^{\text{trop}}
that is isomorphic to
\overline{\Sigma}(\overline{\mathcal{H}})
. Section
8.1
gives a detailed explanation.

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a Hurwitz space parametrising maps to
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. The extended cone complex
\overline{\mathcal{H}}^{\text{trop}}
defined in Section
8.1
and the skeleton of the Berkovich analytification
\overline{\Sigma}(\overline{\mathcal{H}})
are isomorphic, as extended cone complexes with integral structure.

When
\mathcal{H}
parametrises maps to
\mathbb{P}^{1}
, the boundary
\overline{\mathcal{H}}\smallsetminus\mathcal{H}
is actually a
simple
normal crossings divisor, and as a result the skeleton is an extended cone complex rather than a
generalised
extended cone complex.

This is a refinement of the tropicalisation given by Cavalieri, Markwig and Ranganathan for Hurwitz spaces parametrising maps to
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. They define an extended cone complex
\overline{\mathcal{H}}^{\text{trop}}_{\text{CMR}}
together with a finite-to-one surjective morphism
\overline{\Sigma}(\overline{\mathcal{H}})\rightarrow\overline{\mathcal{H}}^{\text{trop}}_{\text{CMR}}
. The failure of this morphism to be an isomorphism comes down to the fact that combinatorial admissible covers do not exactly capture the irreducible strata of
\overline{\mathcal{H}}
. We explicitly relate the two extended cone complexes
\overline{\mathcal{H}}^{\text{trop}}\cong\overline{\Sigma}(\overline{\mathcal{H}})
and
\overline{\mathcal{H}}^{\text{trop}}_{\text{CMR}}
in Section

1.3.
Application to complex dynamics

Hurwitz spaces have recently been used to study the dynamics of rational maps. If

The foundational result in the theory of Thurston maps is Thurston’s Characterisation, which states that either a Thurston map

has a
Thurston obstruction

4

4

This statement of Thurston’s Characterisation is valid as long as

.
[
DH93
]

2.1.
Teichmüller space and moduli space

2.1.1.

For our purposes, a

Roughly speaking, the

The moduli space

The

There is a straightforward map from

\displaystyle\left[\varphi\right]

2.3.
Augmented Teichmüller space and the Deligne-Mumford compactification

2.3.1.
Augmented Teichmüller space
\overline{\mathcal{T}}_{0,B}

Bers introduced the augmented Teichmüller space

A

is a continuous ‘pinching’ map

to a compact connected nodal Riemann surface of arithmetic genus 0, where the preimage of the set of nodes of

is the collection of nodal complex structures up to isotopy

\overline{\mathcal{T}}_{0,B}=\left\{\varphi:S^{2}\rightarrow\mathcal{X}\text{ a nodal complex structure}\right\}\big/\sim

where
{\varphi:S^{2}\rightarrow\mathcal{X}}\sim{\varphi^{\prime}:S^{2}\rightarrow\mathcal{X}^{\prime}}
if and only if there exists an isomorphism
{\mu:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{X}^{\prime}}
such that

and

\overline{\mathcal{T}}_{0,B}

is contained in the closure of the stratum

if and only if

. Observe that the stratum

The Deligne-Mumford compactification
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,B}

Similarly, there is a compactification

A

The

is contained in the closure of the stratum

Proposition 2.1

There is an isomorphism

2.4.
Branched covers of surfaces

Suppose

is a continuous map so that

is finite,

Away from

(or

For

2.5.
Monodromy

In order to give a combinatorial description of the strata of Hurwitz spaces, we will want to extract some combinatorics from branched covers. The concept of
monodromy
gives us a meaningful way of doing this.

2.5.1.
Monodromy representations

Fix a branched cover
{f:(X,A)\rightarrow(S^{2},B)}
of degree
d
. Choose a basepoint
{y\in S^{2}\smallsetminus B}
. By definition,
f
is a covering map away from the set
B
, and so
f^{-1}(y)
contains exactly
d
points. Choose a labelling of the preimage

l:f^{-1}(y)\longrightarrow\{y_{1},\ldots,y_{d}\}.

If
\gamma
is a loop in
S^{2}\smallsetminus B
based at
y
, then for any
{1\leq i\leq d}
,
\gamma
lifts to a path in
{X\smallsetminus f^{-1}(B)}
from
y_{i}
to some
y_{j}
(by the path-lifting property of covering spaces). Setting
\sigma(i)=j
, we obtain a permutation
\sigma\in S_{d}
called the
monodromy permutation
of
\gamma
.

The monodromy permutation of
\gamma
depends only on the homotopy class of
\gamma
. Thus, choosing a basepoint
y\in S^{2}\smallsetminus B
and a labelling
{l:f^{-1}(y)\rightarrow\{y_{1},\ldots,y_{d}\}}
, we obtain a homomorphism

\Sigma:\pi_{1}(S^{2}\smallsetminus B,y)\longrightarrow S_{d}

called the
monodromy representation
of
f
. The image of the monodromy representation is called the
monodromy group
.

The monodromy representation captures certain features of
f
, as suggested by the next two propositions. The proofs are omitted.

Proposition 2.3
.

With the notation above, the surface
X
is connected if and only if the monodromy group acts transitively on
\{1,\ldots,d\}
.

2.7.2.
Marked holomorphic maps from branched covers

Suppose
{f:(X,A)\rightarrow(S^{2},B)}
is a branched cover and
P=P(f)
is its portrait. Then
f
picks out a connected component
\mathcal{H}_{f}
of the Hurwitz space
\mathcal{H}(P)
as follows. If
\varphi:S^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
is a complex structure, then by the Riemann Existence Theorem (see
[
CM16
, Theorem 6.2.2]
) there exists a complex structure
\psi:X\xrightarrow{\cong}\mathcal{X}
on the source surface so that
f
becomes a marked holomorphic map
{(F=\varphi\circ f\circ\psi^{-1}:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1},\,\iota_{A}=\psi|_{A},\,\iota_{B}=\varphi|_{B})}
in
\mathcal{H}(P)
. This construction defines a holomorphic covering map to a connected component of
\mathcal{H}(P)
that we denote by
\mathcal{H}_{f}
:

\displaystyle\pi_{f}:\mathcal{T}_{0,B}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}_{f}

\displaystyle\left[\varphi\right]

\displaystyle\longmapsto\left[\left(F:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1},\,\psi|_{A},\,\varphi|_{B}\right)\right].

Two points of
\mathcal{T}_{0,B}
induce equivalent marked holomorphic maps if and only if they differ by an element of
\operatorname{LMod}_{f}
, whence
\mathcal{T}_{0,B}/\operatorname{LMod}_{f}\cong\mathcal{H}_{f}
;
[
Koc13
, Theorem 2.6]
proves this for the case
X=S^{2}
and the proof adapts straightforwardly to the case of general
X
.

Every connected component of a Hurwitz space can be obtained as such a quotient. Furthermore,
\mathcal{H}_{f}=\mathcal{H}_{f^{\prime}}
for branched covers
f,f^{\prime}:(X,A)\rightarrow(S^{2},B)
if and only if
f
and
f^{\prime}
are
(A,B)
-Hurwitz equivalent
[
Koc13
, Corollary 2.7]
, in which case (slightly generalising
[
Koc13
, Lemma 2.5]
), we have the following lemma.

Lemma 2.7
.

If
f
and
f^{\prime}
are
(A,B)
-Hurwitz equivalent via homeomorphisms

, then
\pi_{f}=\pi_{f^{\prime}}\circ h:\mathcal{T}_{0,B}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{f}=\mathcal{H}_{f^{\prime}}
(with
h
acting on
\mathcal{T}_{0,B}
as explained in Section
2.1.2
).

2.8.
Compactifying Hurwitz spaces

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a Hurwitz space with defining portrait
P
. Harris and Mumford give a compactification
\overline{\mathcal{H}}^{\text{HM}}
by a space of
admissible covers

[
HM82
]
, which are maps of nodal Riemann surfaces. The space
\overline{\mathcal{H}}^{\text{HM}}
is not normal, but its normalisation – which we denote by
\overline{\mathcal{H}}
– is smooth. Abramovich, Corti and Vistoli reinterpret
\overline{\mathcal{H}}
as a complex orbifold that parametrises
balanced twisted covers

[
ACV03
]
.

The target and source maps
\pi_{B}
and
\pi_{A}
extend to the boundaries of
\overline{\mathcal{H}}^{\text{HM}}
and
\overline{\mathcal{H}}
:
\pi_{B}
takes an admissible cover or a balanced twisted cover to the stable nodal marking in
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,B}
of its target, and
\pi_{A}
takes an admissible cover or balanced twisted cover to the nodal marking in
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,A}
of its source (stabilised, if not already stable – see Section
2.3.3
).

The boundary of
\overline{\mathcal{H}}
is a simple normal crossings divisor, and this endows
\overline{\mathcal{H}}
with a natural stratification: the codimension-
k
strata are connected components of
k
-fold intersections of divisors. The stratification of
\overline{\mathcal{H}}
admits a description that will be useful in the proof below: an irreducible stratum of
\overline{\mathcal{H}}
is a connected component of the preimage of an irreducible stratum of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,B}
, under
\pi_{B}
.

Suppose
f
is a branched cover that realises
P
, and let
\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{f}
be the connected component of
\overline{\mathcal{H}}
containing
\mathcal{H}_{f}
. The next proposition, in analogy with Proposition
2.1
, tells us that
\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{f}
is the quotient of augmented Teichmüller space by the liftable mapping class group
\operatorname{LMod}_{f}
, and that the irreducible strata of
\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{f}
can be interpreted as orbits of multicurves. The result (which we state just for the complex analytic coarse spaces, to avoid orbifold structure technicalities) follows from
[
HV10
]
.

Proposition 2.9
.

There is an isomorphism
{\overline{\mathcal{T}}_{0,B}/\operatorname{LMod}_{f}\cong\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{f}}
of complex analytic spaces. This isomorphism identifies the irreducible strata of
\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{f}
with orbits of multicurves under the action of
\operatorname{LMod}_{f}
. Furthermore, containment of closures of strata descends from the reverse containment of multicurves.

Proof.

By
[
HV10
, Theorem 6.1.1]
, the quotient
\overline{\mathcal{T}}_{0,B}/\operatorname{LMod}_{f}
has the structure of a complex orbifold. In Section 8.1.1, they describe a morphism of coarse complex analytic spaces
{\nu_{\rho}:\overline{\mathcal{T}}_{0,B}/\operatorname{LMod}_{f}\rightarrow\overline{\mathcal{H}}^{\text{HM}}}
to the Harris-Mumford space of admissible covers. On the level of points, the morphism takes a nodal complex structure to an admissible cover by pulling back complex structures on each component. This map realises
\overline{\mathcal{T}}_{0,B}/\operatorname{LMod}_{f}
as the normalisation of the Harris-Mumford space of admissible covers, and by uniqueness of the normalisation,
\overline{\mathcal{T}}_{0,B}/\operatorname{LMod}_{f}\cong\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{f}
as complex analytic spaces.

We write
\operatorname{LMod}_{f}(\Delta)
for the image of the stratum
\Delta
of
\overline{\mathcal{T}}_{0,B}
under the quotient map.
\operatorname{LMod}_{f}(\Delta)
is connected, as the image of a connected set under a continuous map, and by the description of the morphism on the level of points,
\operatorname{LMod}(\Delta)
is a connected component of the preimage under
\pi_{B}
of the stratum
\mathscr{T}_{\Delta}
. Thus,
\operatorname{LMod}_{f}(\Delta)
is an irreducible stratum of
\overline{\mathcal{T}}_{0,B}/\operatorname{LMod}_{f}\cong\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{f}
.

∎

3.
Embedded stable trees

The purpose of this section is to give the topological set-up of embedded stable trees, dual multicurves and braiding that we will use later on.

3.1.
Embedded stable trees

Let
B
be a finite set of points in
S^{2}
with
|B|\geq 3
, and let
\mathscr{T}
be a
B
-marked stable tree. An
embedding
of
\mathscr{T}
into
(S^{2},B)
maps the endpoint of the leg
b
to the point
b\in S^{2}
. We consider embeddings of stable trees up to isotopy in
(S^{2},B)
: two embeddings of
\mathscr{T}
are
isotopic
if they are isotopic (as maps) through embeddings of
\mathscr{T}
.

Remark 3.1
.

If two embeddings
\iota,\iota^{\prime}:\mathscr{T}\hookrightarrow S^{2}
are isotopic, then there is actually an ambient isotopy of the sphere
I_{t}:S^{2}\rightarrow S^{2}
that fixes
B
throughout and takes one embedding to the other, in that
I_{0}=\operatorname{Id}
and
I_{1}\circ\iota=\iota^{\prime}
. This follows from the Alexander trick. We will use this observation to ‘carry along’ topological data associated to an embedding (such as a dual multicurve, defined below) to an isotopic embedding.

3.2.
Embedded stable trees notation

We often refer to an embedding
\iota:\mathscr{T}\hookrightarrow S^{2}
by just its image
T
, suppressing the map. A vertex, edge, leg or half-edge of
T
is the image of a vertex, edge, leg or half-edge, respectively, of
\mathscr{T}
. We often use the same symbol for corresponding features of
\mathscr{T}
and
T
when there is no risk of confusion.

Let
v
be a vertex of
T
. Suppose
h_{1},\ldots,h_{k}
are the half-edges incident with
v
, appearing in that order going anticlockwise. We record their cyclic order in a cycle
(h_{1}\,h_{2}\,\ldots\,h_{k})
. Putting all such cycles together – one for each vertex of
T
– we obtain a permutation of the set of half-edges
H(T)
which we denote by
\mathit{ord}_{T}\in S_{H(T)}
.

3.3.
Dual multicurves

Suppose
T
is an embedding of a
B
-marked stable tree in
(S^{2},B)
, with
e
an (open) edge. Let
\delta_{e}
be a simple closed curve that intersects
T
exactly once, along
e
, with both components of
S^{2}\smallsetminus\delta_{e}
containing one of the two resulting subtrees. The curve
\delta_{e}
is unique up to isotopy
\operatorname{rel.}B
. The
dual multicurve
11
11

11

This is an abuse of notation because we need to make choices of
\delta_{e}
, but as we will only consider the dual multicurve up to isotopy, these choices don’t matter.
to
T
is

\Delta_{T}=\left\{\delta_{e}\mid e\in E(T)\right\}.

By induction, these curves can be chosen to be disjoint. They are essential because each component of
S^{2}\smallsetminus\delta_{e}
contains at least two points of
B
, and they are non-homotopic because (again by induction) each
\delta_{e}
partitions
B
differently. Figure
3.1
shows an example. If
T
and
T^{\prime}
are isotopic, then
\Delta_{T}
and
\Delta_{T^{\prime}}
are isotopic – an ambient isotopy (Remark
3.1
) carries a choice of
\Delta_{T}
to a choice of
\Delta_{T^{\prime}}
.

of a

\Delta_{T}
.

3.4.
Braiding embedded stable trees

There are operations on the isotopy classes of embeddings of
\mathscr{T}
that we call
braid moves
. A braid move changes how just one half-edge
h
of
\mathscr{T}
is embedded, shifting it one step clockwise or anticlockwise in the cyclic order of the half-edges around
\operatorname{base}(h)
.

To streamline the next definition, we define some special loops: if
h
is a half-edge of an embedding
T
, then we let
\gamma_{h}
be a simple loop based at
\operatorname{base}(h)
that bounds (on its left) the subtree of
T\smallsetminus\operatorname{base}(h)
containing
h
, with
\gamma_{h}
intersecting
T
only at
\operatorname{base}(h)
. The loop
\gamma_{h}
is unique up to isotopy
\operatorname{rel.}B
. Note that if
h
is a leg, then
\gamma_{h}
is a keyhole loop. These loops will reappear in Section
5
.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
T
be an embedding of a
B
-marked stable tree
\mathscr{T}
in
(S^{2},B)
, and let
h
be a half-edge of
T
. Suppose
\widetilde{h}=\mathit{ord}_{T}(h)
is the next half-edge anticlockwise around
\operatorname{base}(h)
. Following
\gamma_{\widetilde{h}}^{-1}
and then
h
gives a path whose interior intersects
T
once, at
\operatorname{base}(h)
. Nudge this path by an isotopy to remove this intersection, and call the resulting curve
k
. Let
T^{\prime}
be the embedding of
\mathscr{T}
obtained by replacing
h
with
k
. We say that
T^{\prime}
differs from
T
by an
(anticlockwise) braid move
at
h
.

An anticlockwise braid move at
h
changes the cyclic ordering of the half-edges around
\operatorname{base}(h)
: the half-edge
h
gets shifted one step anticlockwise. This translates to a conjugation of
\mathit{ord}_{T}
by a transposition:

\mathit{ord}_{T^{\prime}}=(h\,\,\widetilde{h})\circ\mathit{ord}_{T}\circ(h\,\,\widetilde{h})^{-1},

where
\widetilde{h}=\mathit{ord}(h)
is the next half-edge anticlockwise. We define
clockwise
braid moves similarly, and we refer to a clockwise or anticlockwise braid move simply as a
braid move
.

Braid moves are well-defined operations on the isotopy classes of embeddings of a stable tree. Some examples of anticlockwise braid moves are shown in Figure
3.2
.
Braiding
an embedding means applying a sequence of braid moves. The next proposition says that braiding an embedding does not change the dual multicurve.

Examples of anticlockwise braid moves on an embedding
T
at the half-edges shown to produce new embeddings.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
T
be an embedding of a
B
-marked stable tree in
(S^{2},B)
. Suppose
T^{\prime}
differs from
T
by a braid move. Then
\Delta_{T}
and
\Delta_{T^{\prime}}
are isotopic.

Proof.

The loop
\gamma_{h}
in the definition of a braid move can be chosen disjoint from
\Delta_{T}
, so that
\Delta_{T}
is a common choice of dual multicurve for both
T
and
T^{\prime}
.
∎

Conversely, if two embeddings
T
and
T^{\prime}
both have the same dual multicurve, then one can be braided to give the other.

Proposition 3.4
.

Suppose
T
and
T^{\prime}
are embeddings of a
B
-marked stable tree
\mathscr{T}
in
(S^{2},B)
such that
\Delta_{T}
and
\Delta_{T^{\prime}}
are isotopic. Then there exists a sequence of embeddings of
\mathscr{T}

T=T_{0},\,\,T_{1},\,\,\ldots,\,\,T_{N}

such that
T_{i}
differs from
T_{i-1}
by a braid move for
i=1,\ldots,N
, and
T_{N}
is isotopic to
T^{\prime}
.

This proof is longer and more technical, so we relegate it to Appendix
A
.

3.5.
Contraction of embedded stable trees

Suppose
T
is an embedding and
e
is an edge that is incident with a vertex
v
. A
contraction along
e
fixing
v
is an embedding
T^{\prime}
obtained from
T
by contracting in the edge
e
towards
v
, keeping
v
fixed, as shown in Figure
3.3
. A sequence of such contractions is called a
contraction
of
T
.

4.
Decorated trees

In this section, we define decorated trees. These are the combinatorial objects that we will use to describe the strata of compactified Hurwitz spaces. Throughout, let
{P=(g,d,B,A,\phi:A\rightarrow B,\operatorname{br},\operatorname{rm})}
be a portrait.

We write
H(\mathscr{T})
for the set of half-edges of a
B
-marked stable tree
\mathscr{T}
, and we often simply write
b
for the leg labelled
b\in B
.

4.1.
Decorations

We should think of a decoration of
\mathscr{T}
as modelling certain monodromy information that comes from embedding
\mathscr{T}
into the target sphere of a branched cover
{f:(X,A)\rightarrow(S^{2},B)}
. In particular, an embedding of
\mathscr{T}
gives a cyclic ordering of the half-edges around each vertex, and picks out certain loops (the
\gamma_{h}
from Section
3.4
) whose monodromy permutations we’d like to keep track of. We’ll see this encoding in action in Section
5.1
.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\mathscr{T}
be a
B
-marked stable tree. A
P
-decoration
of
\mathscr{T}
is given by the data
(\mathit{ord},\mathit{mon},\mathit{cyc})
where

(1)

\mathit{ord}
is a permutation of the set
H(\mathscr{T})
of half-edges, with one disjoint cycle for each vertex
v
that contains all the half-edges incident with
v

(2)

\mathit{mon}
is an assignment

\mathit{mon}:H(\mathscr{T})\longrightarrow S_{d}

of a permutation of
S_{d}
to each half-edge of
\mathscr{T}

(3)

\mathit{cyc}
is an injection

\mathit{cyc}:A\longrightarrow\bigsqcup_{b\in B}\operatorname{Cycles}\left(\mathit{mon}(b)\right)

from
A
to the disjoint union of cycles of the leg permutations

satisfying the conditions

(i.)

for each cycle
(h_{1}\,h_{2}\,\ldots\,h_{k})
of
\mathit{ord}
,

\mathit{mon}(h_{k})\ldots\mathit{mon}(h_{2})\mathit{mon}(h_{1})=\operatorname{Id}\in S_{d}

(ii.)

for each edge, the permutations on its two half-edges are inverses

(iii.)

for each
b\in B
, the leg permutation
\mathit{mon}(b)
has cycle type
\operatorname{br}(b)

(iv.)

for each
a\in A
,
\mathit{cyc}(a)
is a cycle of
\mathit{mon}\left({\phi(a)}\right)
of length
\operatorname{rm}(a)

(v.)

the collection of leg permutations
\left\{\mathit{mon}(b)\right\}_{b\in B}
is transitive

A
B
-marked stable tree
\mathscr{T}
together with a
P
-decoration is called a
P
-decorated tree
, or simply a
decorated tree
if the portrait is understood. The set of
P
-decorated trees is denoted by
\operatorname{Dec}_{0,B}(P)
.

Remark 4.2
.

(1)

The purpose of condition (i.) is to ensure that the (anticlockwise) cyclic product of the permutations around each vertex is the identity.

(2)

A decoration
(\mathit{ord},\mathit{mon},\mathit{cyc})
is determined by
\mathit{ord}
,
\mathit{mon}|_{B}
and
\mathit{cyc}
, because once we know the permutations on the legs, we can ‘propagate in’ using conditions (i) and (ii) to determine the permutations on the edges.

4.2.
Contraction of decorations

Contracting an edge (with half-edges
h
,
h^{\prime}
, say) of a
B
-marked stable tree
\mathscr{T}
yields another
B
-marked stable tree,
\mathscr{T}^{\prime}
. A
P
-decoration
{(\mathit{ord},\mathit{mon},\mathit{cyc})}
of
\mathscr{T}
induces a
P
-decoration
{(\mathit{ord}^{\prime},\mathit{mon}^{\prime},\mathit{cyc}^{\prime})}
of
\mathscr{T}^{\prime}
in the obvious way:
{\mathit{mon}^{\prime}(k)=\mathit{mon}(k)}
for all
{k\in H(\mathscr{T}^{\prime})}
,
{\mathit{cyc}^{\prime}(a)=\mathit{cyc}(a)}
for all
{a\in A}
, and if

\mathit{ord}=\ldots(h\,\,h_{1}\,\ldots\,h_{k})(h^{\prime}\,\,h^{\prime}_{1}\,\ldots\,h^{\prime}_{l})\ldots

(written with
h
and
h^{\prime}
starting their respective cycles) then

\mathit{ord}^{\prime}=\ldots(h_{1}\,\ldots\,h_{k}\,\,h^{\prime}_{1}\,\ldots\,h^{\prime}_{l})\ldots.

This is shown for our example in Figure

. Conditions (i.)-(v.) are satisifed by
{(\mathit{ord}^{\prime},\mathit{mon}^{\prime},\mathit{cyc}^{\prime})}
. We call the new decorated tree an
edge contraction
of the original decorated tree. More generally, a decorated tree obtained by a sequence of edge contractions is called a
contraction
of the original.

4.3.
Hurwitz equivalence of decorations

There are two types of operations on
P
-decorated
trees that determine the correct notion of equivalence. The first are
global conjugations
, which capture the idea that relabelling the permutations of a
P
-decorated tree by some
\tau\in S_{d}
should yield an equivalent
P
-decorated tree. The second are
braid moves (of decorated trees)
, which are more subtle and will be justified when we examine the interplay between the combinatorics and the topology of branched covers. We say that two
P
-decorated trees are
Hurwitz equivalent
if one can be transformed to the other by a sequence of global conjugations and braid moves.

Definition 4.4
.

Let
(\mathit{ord},\mathit{mon},\mathit{cyc})
be a
P
-decoration of
\mathscr{T}
, and let
\tau\in S_{d}
. Define a new decoration
(\mathit{ord}^{\prime},\mathit{mon}^{\prime},\mathit{cyc}^{\prime})
of
\mathscr{T}
as follows.

(1)

\mathit{ord}^{\prime}=\mathit{ord}

(2)

for all
h\in H(\mathscr{T})
,

\mathit{mon}^{\prime}(h)=\tau\circ\mathit{mon}(h)\circ\tau^{-1}

(3)

for all
a\in A
,

\mathit{cyc}^{\prime}(a)=\tau\circ\mathit{cyc}(a)\circ\tau^{-1}

We say that
(\mathit{ord}^{\prime},\mathit{mon}^{\prime},\mathit{cyc}^{\prime})
is obtained from
(\mathit{ord},\mathit{mon},\mathit{cyc})
by a
global conjugation
by
\tau
, and that these decorations are
globally conjugate
.

The new decoration satisfies conditions (i.)-(v.) and is thus a
P
-decoration of
\mathscr{T}
. A global conjugation is shown in Figure
4.3
, where
P
is the portrait in our running example and
\tau=(1\,2\,3)
.

Figure 4.3.
Two decorations that are globally conjugate by
\tau=(1\,2\,3)
.

The second type of operations – braid moves of decorated trees – allow us to accommodate decorations that have different cyclic orders. Braid moves of decorated trees encode the effect that braid moves of
embedded
trees have on certain monodromy permutations. We will make this precise in Section
5.4
.

Definition 4.5
.

Let
(\mathit{ord},\mathit{mon},\mathit{cyc})
be a
P
-decoration of
\mathscr{T}
, and let
h
be a half-edge. Let
\widetilde{h}=\mathit{ord}(h)
be the next half-edge anticlockwise. Define a new decoration
(\mathit{ord}^{\prime},\mathit{mon}^{\prime},\mathit{cyc}^{\prime})
of
\mathscr{T}
as follows.

(1)

\mathit{ord}^{\prime}=(h\,\,\widetilde{h})\circ\mathit{ord}\circ(h\,\,\widetilde{h})^{-1}

(2)

for all half-edges
k
in the component of
\mathscr{T}\smallsetminus\{\operatorname{base}(h)\}
containing
h
,

\mathit{mon}^{\prime}(k)=\mathit{mon}(\widetilde{h})\circ\mathit{mon}(k)\circ\mathit{mon}(\widetilde{h})^{-1}

and for all other half-edges
k
,

\mathit{mon}^{\prime}(k)=\mathit{mon}(k)

(3)

for all
a\in A
so that
{\phi(a)}
is a leg in the component of
\mathscr{T}\smallsetminus\{\operatorname{base}(h)\}
containing
h
,

\mathit{cyc}^{\prime}(a)=\mathit{mon}(\widetilde{h})\circ\mathit{cyc}(a)\circ\mathit{mon}(\widetilde{h})^{-1},

and for all other
a\in A
,

\mathit{cyc}^{\prime}(a)=\mathit{cyc}(a)

We say that
(\mathit{ord}^{\prime},\mathit{mon}^{\prime},\mathit{cyc}^{\prime})
is obtained from
(\mathit{ord},\mathit{mon},\mathit{cyc})
by an
(anticlockwise) braid move
of decorated trees at the half-edge
h
.

The new data satisfies conditions (i.)-(v.) and is thus a
P
-decoration. In words, an anticlockwise braid move shifts
h
one step anticlockwise through
\widetilde{h}=\mathit{ord}(h)
, and the price of this reordering is that all the permutations on the shifted subtree get conjugated by
\mathit{mon}(\widetilde{h})
.

We define clockwise braid moves of decorated trees similarly: a clockwise braid move shifts
h
one step clockwise through
\widetilde{h}=\mathit{ord}^{-1}(h)
, and all the permutations on the shifted subtree pick up a conjugation by
\mathit{mon}(\widetilde{h})^{-1}
. A clockwise or anticlockwise braid move is simply a
braid move
.

A braid move for our running-example portrait is shown in Figure
4.4
, and a bigger example (for a slightly modified portrait with
|B|=5
) is shown in Figure
4.5
.

h=b_{3}

\widetilde{h}=b_{4}
:
h
gets shifted anticlockwise and the red permutations and
A
-markings get conjugated by
\mathit{mon}(\widetilde{h})=(1\,3)(2)
.

Figure 4.5.

h

.

Combining global conjugations and braid moves gives us the right equivalence relation, which we call
Hurwitz equivalence
.

Definition 4.6
.

Two
P
-decorations of a stable tree
\mathscr{T}
are
Hurwitz equivalent
if one can be obtained from the other by a sequence of global conjugations and braid moves of decorated trees. We write

\operatorname{Stab}_{0,B}(P)=\operatorname{Dec}_{0,B}(P)/\text{Hurwitz equivalence}

for the set of Hurwitz equivalence classes of
P
-decorated trees.

The next lemma tells us that contraction of
P
-decorated trees descends to a well-defined notion of contraction on Hurwitz equivalence classes of
P
-decorated trees.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
\mathscr{T}
be a stable tree that contracts along an edge
e
to
\mathscr{T}^{\prime}
. Suppose
{(\mathit{ord}_{1},\mathit{mon}_{1},\mathit{cyc}_{1})}
and
(\mathit{ord}_{2},\mathit{mon}_{2},\mathit{cyc}_{2})
are Hurwitz equivalent
P
-decorations of
\mathscr{T}
that contract along
e
to give the
P
-decorations
(\mathit{ord}_{1}^{\prime},\mathit{mon}_{1}^{\prime},\mathit{cyc}_{1}^{\prime})
and
(\mathit{ord}_{2}^{\prime},\mathit{mon}_{2}^{\prime},\mathit{cyc}_{2}^{\prime})
of
\mathscr{T}^{\prime}
, respectively. Then
(\mathit{ord}_{1}^{\prime},\mathit{mon}_{1}^{\prime},\mathit{cyc}_{1}^{\prime})
and
(\mathit{ord}_{2}^{\prime},\mathit{mon}_{2}^{\prime},\mathit{cyc}_{2}^{\prime})
are Hurwitz equivalent.

Proof.

If
(\mathit{ord}_{1},\mathit{mon}_{1},\mathit{cyc}_{1})
and
(\mathit{ord}_{2},\mathit{mon}_{2},\mathit{cyc}_{2})
differ by a global conjugation by
\tau
, then
(\mathit{ord}_{1}^{\prime},\mathit{mon}_{1}^{\prime},\mathit{cyc}_{1}^{\prime})
and
(\mathit{ord}_{2}^{\prime},\mathit{mon}_{2}^{\prime},\mathit{cyc}_{2}^{\prime})
also differ by a global conjugation by
\tau
.

If
(\mathit{ord}_{1},\mathit{mon}_{1},\mathit{cyc}_{1})
and
(\mathit{ord}_{2},\mathit{mon}_{2},\mathit{cyc}_{2})
differ by a braid move at the half-edge
h
(without loss of generality an
anticlockwise
braid move), then the situation is slightly more complicated. If neither
h
nor
\mathit{ord}_{1}(h)
is in
e
, then
(\mathit{ord}_{1}^{\prime},\mathit{mon}_{1}^{\prime},\mathit{cyc}_{1}^{\prime})
and
(\mathit{ord}_{2}^{\prime},\mathit{mon}_{2}^{\prime},\mathit{cyc}_{2}^{\prime})
differ by a braid move at
h
. If
h
or
\mathit{ord}_{1}(h)
is in
e
, then
(\mathit{ord}_{1}^{\prime},\mathit{mon}_{1}^{\prime},\mathit{cyc}_{1}^{\prime})
and
(\mathit{ord}_{2}^{\prime},\mathit{mon}_{2}^{\prime},\mathit{cyc}_{2}^{\prime})
differ by a sequence of braid moves (involving each of the new half-edges at
\operatorname{base}(h)
created by contracting
e
).

∎

5.
Decorated trees from embedded stable trees

In this section, we explain how to construct a decorated tree given a branched cover and an embedded tree. This construction requires us to make some choices, and the second half of the section will examine how these choices affect the decorated tree.

5.1.
The decoration construction

Recall the definition of the loop
\gamma_{h}
from Section
3
: if
h
is a half-edge of an embedding
T
, then
\gamma_{h}
is a simple loop (unique up to isotopy
\operatorname{rel.}B
) based at
\operatorname{base}(h)
that bounds the subtree of
T\smallsetminus\operatorname{base}(h)
containing
h
on its left, with
\gamma_{h}
intersecting
T
only at
\operatorname{base}(h)
. If
h
is a leg, then
\gamma_{h}
is a keyhole loop.

Recall also our convention that concatenation of path is written right-to-left, to match function composition.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
{f:(X,A)\rightarrow(S^{2},B)}
be a branched cover with
X
connected, and let
\mathscr{T}
be a
B
-marked stable tree. Choose an embedding
T
of
\mathscr{T}
, a vertex
v
of
T
, and a labelling
{l:f^{-1}(v)\rightarrow\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{d}\}}
. Let
{\Sigma:\pi_{1}(S^{2}\smallsetminus B,v)\rightarrow S_{d}}
be the monodromy representation induced by the labelling. With these choices, we construct a
P(f)
-decoration

D_{f}(T,v,l)=(\mathit{ord},\mathit{mon},\mathit{cyc})

of
\mathscr{T}
, where:

(1)

\mathit{ord}=\mathit{ord}_{T}

(2)

for
h\in H(\mathscr{T})
, let
\alpha_{h}
be the unique shortest path in
T
from
v
to
\operatorname{base}(h)
and let
{\overline{\gamma}_{h}=\alpha_{h}^{-1}\operatorname{.}\gamma_{h}\operatorname{.}\alpha_{h}}
; set

\mathit{mon}(h)=\Sigma(\overline{\gamma}_{h})

(3)

for
a\in A
, set

\mathit{cyc}(a)=\mathit{cyc}_{\overline{\gamma}_{b}}(a),

where
b=f(a)
and
\mathit{cyc}_{\overline{\gamma}_{b}}
is the canonical bijection of Proposition
2.4

An illustration of the construction is given in Figure
5.1
, with
P(f)
our running-example portrait from Section
2.6
.

Lemma 5.2
.

The construction in Definition
5.1
yields a
P(f)
-decoration of
\mathscr{T}
.

Proof.

We must show that conditions (i.)-(v.) of Definition
4.1
are satisfied.

(i.)

Let
w
be a vertex of
\mathscr{T}
, and let
h_{1},\ldots,h_{k}
be its incident half-edges in anticlockwise cyclic order. The concatenation
\overline{\gamma}_{h_{k}}\operatorname{.}\ldots\operatorname{.}\overline{\gamma}_{h_{1}}
is homotopic to the boundary of a disk containing the tree
T
, which can be homotoped to a point, so

\displaystyle\mathit{mon}(h_{k})\circ\ldots\circ\mathit{mon}(h_{1})

\displaystyle=\Sigma\left(\overline{\gamma}_{h_{k}}\right)\circ\ldots\circ\Sigma\left(\overline{\gamma}_{h_{1}}\right)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Id}.

(ii.)

Suppose the half-edges
h
and
h^{\prime}
form an edge. The loops
\overline{\gamma}_{h}
and
\overline{\gamma}_{h^{\prime}}^{-1}
are homotopic in
(S^{2},B)
, so
\Sigma(\overline{\gamma}_{h})=\Sigma(\overline{\gamma}_{h^{\prime}})^{-1}
and
\mathit{mon}(h)=\mathit{mon}(h^{\prime})^{-1}
.

(iii.)

\overline{\gamma}_{b}
is a keyhole loop, so by Proposition
2.4
,
\mathit{mon}(b)=\Sigma(\overline{\gamma}_{b})
has cycle type
\operatorname{br}_{f}(b)
.

(iv.)

\overline{\gamma}_{f(a)}
is a keyhole loop, so by Proposition
2.4
,
\mathit{cyc}(a)
is a cycle of
\mathit{mon}({f(a)})=\Sigma(\overline{\gamma}_{f(a)})
of length
\operatorname{rm}_{f}(a)
.

(v.)

X
is connected, so by Proposition
2.3
, the collection of leg permutations
\{\mathit{mon}(b)\}_{b\in B}
is transitive.

5.2.
Isotoping
T,v
and
l

Suppose
\beta
is a path in
S^{2}\smallsetminus B
from
v
to
v^{\prime}
, and suppose
{l:f^{-1}(v)\rightarrow\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{d}\}}
is a labelling. The
extension of
l
along
\beta
is the labelling
{l^{\prime}:f^{-1}(v^{\prime})\rightarrow\{v_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,v_{d}^{\prime}\}}
defined so that the lift of
\beta
to
v_{i}
ends at
v_{i}^{\prime}
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
f,T,v
and
l
be as above, and suppose there is an isotopy from
T
to
T^{\prime}
that takes
v
to
v^{\prime}
. Let
l^{\prime}
be the extension of
l
along the path that
v
takes to
v^{\prime}
under the isotopy. Then

D_{f}(T,v,l)=D_{f}(T^{\prime},v^{\prime},l^{\prime}).

Proof.

Let the two decorations be
(\mathit{ord},\mathit{mon},\mathit{cyc})
and
(\mathit{ord}^{\prime},\mathit{mon}^{\prime},\mathit{cyc}^{\prime})
, respectively. The isotopy preserves the cyclic order of the half-edges around each vertex, so
\mathit{ord}=\mathit{ord}^{\prime}
. Let
h
be a half-edge of
T
, and let
h^{\prime}
be the half-edge of
T^{\prime}
that
h
gets isotoped to. An ambient isotopy of the sphere (Remark
3.1
) that takes
T
to
T^{\prime}
carries the loop
\gamma_{h}
to a valid choice of loop
\gamma_{h^{\prime}}
, and thus carries
\overline{\gamma}_{h}
(based at
v
) to a valid choice of
\overline{\gamma}_{h^{\prime}}
(based at
v^{\prime}
). The fact that
l^{\prime}
is an extension of
l
along the path from
v
to
v^{\prime}
ensures exactly that the monodromy permutation of
\overline{\gamma}_{h}
is equal to the monodromy permutation of
\overline{\gamma}_{h^{\prime}}
(and that the cycle markings are equal, in the case that
h
and
h^{\prime}
are legs), and so
\mathit{mon}=\mathit{mon}^{\prime}
and
\mathit{cyc}=\mathit{cyc}^{\prime}
.
∎

5.3.
Changing
v
and
l

Next comes the easiest: changing
v
and
l
while keeping
T
fixed changes the decoration by a global conjugation.

Lemma 5.4
.

With the notation above, suppose that
v^{\prime}
is another choice of vertex (possibly equal to
v
) and that
l^{\prime}:f^{-1}(v^{\prime})\rightarrow\{v^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,v^{\prime}_{d}\}
is a labelling of its preimage. Then the decorations
D_{f}(T,v,l)
and
D_{f}(T,v^{\prime},l^{\prime})
are globally conjugate.

Proof.

Let the two decorations be
(\mathit{ord},\mathit{mon},\mathit{cyc})
and
(\mathit{ord}^{\prime},\mathit{mon}^{\prime},\mathit{cyc}^{\prime})
, respectively. It is clear that
\mathit{ord}=\mathit{ord}_{T}=\mathit{ord}^{\prime}
. Let
\beta
be the unique shortest path in
T
from
v
to
v^{\prime}
, and let
l^{\prime\prime}
be the labelling of
f^{-1}(v^{\prime})
obtained by extending the labelling

along the path

. Then
D_{f}(T,v,l)=D_{f}(T,v^{\prime},l^{\prime\prime})
. Composing
l^{\prime\prime}
with the correct permutation
\tau
gives
l^{\prime}
. The decoration
D_{f}(T,v^{\prime},l^{\prime})
is then the global conjugation of
D_{f}(T,v^{\prime},l^{\prime\prime})=D_{f}(T,v,l)
by
\tau
.
∎

5.4.
Braiding
T

As indicated in Section
4
, changing
T
by a braid move of embedded trees, while keeping
v
and
l
fixed, changes
D_{f}(T,v,l)
by a braid move of

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
h
be a half-edge of
T
, and suppose
T^{\prime}
is obtained from
T
by a braid move of embedded trees at the half-edge
h
. Then
D_{f}(T,\operatorname{base}(h),l)
differs from
D_{f}(T^{\prime},\operatorname{base}(h),l)
by a braid move of decorated trees at

.

Proof.

Suppose the braid move of embedded trees is an anticlockwise braid move, without loss of generality. Let the two decorations be
(\mathit{ord},\mathord{mon},\mathit{cyc})
and
(\mathit{ord}^{\prime},\mathord{mon}^{\prime},\mathit{cyc}^{\prime})
, respectively, and let
h^{\prime}=\mathit{ord}_{T}(h)
be the next half-edge anticlockwise. The cyclic ordering changes as required:
{\mathit{ord}^{\prime}=\mathit{ord}_{T^{\prime}}=(h\,\,h^{\prime})\circ\mathit{ord}\circ(h\,\,h^{\prime})}
.

Let
k
be a half-edge in the component of
T\smallsetminus h
containing
h
. If
\overline{\gamma}_{k}
is the original loop that we use to decorate
k
in
D_{f}(T,\operatorname{base}(h),l)
, then
\gamma_{h^{\prime}}\operatorname{.}\overline{\gamma}_{k}\operatorname{.}\gamma_{h^{\prime}}^{-1}
is the loop we use to decorate

in
D_{f}(T^{\prime},\operatorname{base}(h),l)
. Thus,
{\mathit{mon}^{\prime}(k)=\mathit{mon}(h^{\prime})\circ\mathit{mon}(k)\circ\mathit{mon}(h^{\prime})^{-1}}
. Similarly, if
k
is a leg and is mapped to by

, then
{\mathit{cyc}^{\prime}(a)=\mathit{mon}(h^{\prime})\circ\mathit{cyc}(a)\circ\mathit{mon}(h^{\prime})^{-1}}
.

If
k
is a half-edge in a different component of
T\smallsetminus h
, then we use the same loop
\overline{\gamma_{k}}
to decorate
k
.

∎

5.5.
Realising
P
-decorations

Let
X
be a compact surface of genus
g
, and let
A\subset X
and
B\subset S^{2}
be finite subsets. Let
P=(g,d,B,A,\phi:A\rightarrow B,\operatorname{br},\operatorname{rm})
be a portrait. The following proposition tells us that every
P
-decorated tree is actually realised as
D_{f}(T,v,l)
for some choice of
f,T,v,l
. The proof is essentially the same as the ‘Ikea approach’ of
[
CM16
, Theorem 7.2.2]
, restricted to the case where the target is a sphere. In our language, their proof covers the case of the one-vertex stable tree, whereas we make the slight generalisation to other stable trees.

Proposition 5.6
.

Let
(\mathit{ord},\mathit{mon},\mathit{cyc})
be a
P
-decoration of
\mathscr{T}
. Then there exists a branched cover
f:(X,A)\rightarrow(S^{2},B)
realising
P
, an embedding
T
of
\mathscr{T}
, a vertex
v
of
T
and a labelling
l
of
f^{-1}(v)
such that
{D_{f}(T,v,l)=(\mathit{ord},\mathit{mon},\mathit{cyc})}
.

Proof.

Let
(\mathit{ord}^{\prime},\mathit{mon}^{\prime},\mathit{cyc}^{\prime})
be the
P
-decoration obtained by contracting all the edges of
\mathscr{T}
– the contracted tree has just one vertex, so
\mathit{ord}^{\prime}
has exactly one cycle and it contains all the elements of
B
. Let
Q
be a regular
2|B|
-gon, and label the sides
\alpha_{b,\text{out}}
,
\alpha_{b,\text{in}}
in adjacent pairs going anticlockwise, with their common corner labelled
b
, following the cyclic order
\mathit{ord}^{\prime}
.

Let
T
be an embedding of
\mathscr{T}
in
Q
, with the endpoint of the leg labelled
b
at the corner labelled
b
, so that
T
intersects the boundary of
Q
only at the labelled corners. It is straightforward to see that we can pick this embedding so that
T
has the correct order of half-edges at each vertex – that is,
\mathit{ord}_{T}=\mathit{ord}
. We pick our embedding to satisfy this condition. Let
v
be a vertex of
T

Glue the side

6.
Decorated trees from multicurves

In this section, we describe the relationship between multicurves and Hurwitz equivalence classes of decorated trees. We have seen that a stratum of a compactified Hurwitz space can be viewed as an orbit of multicurves (Proposition
2.9
) and as a result, the statements of this section will translate directly to a proof of Theorem
1.1

6.1.
Multicurves and decorated trees

Suppose
f:(X,A)\rightarrow(S^{2},B)
is a branched cover and
\Delta
is a multicurve in
(S^{2},B)
. We can assign a
P(f)
-decorated tree to

by picking an embedding of

that is dual to

and feeding it into the decoration function

, along with a choice of vertex and labelling. Because of the freedom in choosing the embedding, the vertex and the labelling, we can only expect the output decoration to be defined up to Hurwitz equivalence. The next lemma says that this is indeed the case.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
\Delta
be a multicurve in
(S^{2},B)
. Suppose

and

are embeddings of
\mathscr{T}_{\Delta}
with
\Delta_{T}
and
\Delta_{T^{\prime}}
isotopic to
\Delta
, that

,

are vertices of

,

and that

are labellings of

, respectively. Then
D_{f}(T,v,l)
and
D_{f}(T^{\prime},v^{\prime},l^{\prime})
are Hurwitz equivalent.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.4
, there exists a sequence of embeddings
{T=T_{0}},T_{1},\ldots,T_{N}
so that
T_{i}
differs from
T_{i-1}
by a braid move and
T_{N}
is isotopic to
T^{\prime}
. By Lemma
5.5
and Lemma

, the

th and

th terms of the sequence
{D_{f}(T_{0},v,l)},{D_{f}(T_{1},v,l)},\ldots,{D_{f}(T_{N},v,l)}
differ by a braid move, and possibly a couple of global conjugations (to change back and forth from the vertex

to the vertex

when applying Lemma

). But

is isotopic to
T^{\prime}
, so by Lemma
5.3
, the decoration

is equal to a decoration

. One more application of Lemma

tells us that

is globally conjugate to

. We can thus obtain the decoration

from

by a sequence of braid moves and global conjugations, and therefore they are Hurwitz equivalent.

∎

Let
\overline{D}_{f}
be the resulting well-defined function from the set of multicurves to the set of Hurwitz equivalence classes of

-decorated trees:

\displaystyle\overline{D}_{f}:\operatorname{Mult}_{0,B}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\operatorname{Stab}_{0,B}(P(f))

\displaystyle\Delta

\displaystyle\longmapsto\left[D_{f}(T,v,l)\right],

where

is an embedding of
\mathscr{T}_{\Delta}
with
\Delta_{T}
isotopic to
\Delta
,

a vertex of

and

a labelling of

. The next lemma tells us that

behaves well with respect to containment of multicurves.

Lemma 6.2
.

Suppose

and
\Delta^{\prime}
are multicurves with
\Delta^{\prime}\subset\Delta
. Then
\overline{D}_{f}(\Delta)
contracts (as a Hurwitz equivalence class of

Proof.

Make a choice of

for
\Delta
. Let

be a contraction of

along the edges

of

for which
\delta_{e}\in\Delta\smallsetminus\Delta^{\prime}
, keeping

fixed throughout. The dual multicurve

is isotopic to

. The loops

used to construct the decorated tree

are homotopic to loops used to construct the decorated tree

∎

We now come to the main technical proposition, which will allow us to study

of multicurves. The proof uses the other direction of the ‘Ikea approach’ of

]
to the one in our proof of Proposition

Proposition 6.3
.

Let

be branched covers and let
\Delta
,
\Delta^{\prime}
be multicurves in
(S^{2},B)
. Then
\overline{D}_{f}(\Delta)=\overline{D}_{f^{\prime}}(\Delta^{\prime})
if and only if
f
and
f^{\prime}
are
(A,B)
-Hurwitz equivalent via homeomorphisms
h
,
\widetilde{h}
with
h(\Delta)=\Delta^{\prime}
.

Proof.

One direction is easy: suppose
f,f^{\prime}
are
(A,B)
-Hurwitz equivalent via homeomorphisms
h
,
\widetilde{h}
with
h(\Delta)=\Delta^{\prime}
. Make a choice of

with
\Delta_{T}
isotopic to
\Delta
. The embedding
h(T)
has dual multicurve
\Delta_{h(T)}
isotopic to
\Delta^{\prime}
,
h(v)
is a vertex, and

a choice of labelling of the preimage of

. It follows from the commutative diagram for
(A,B)
-Hurwitz equivalence (

) that

\overline{D}_{f}(\Delta)=\overline{D}_{f^{\prime}}(h(\Delta))=\overline{D}_{f^{\prime}}(\Delta^{\prime})
.

For the other direction, suppose that
\overline{D}_{f}(\Delta)=\overline{D}_{f^{\prime}}(\Delta^{\prime})
. This means we can make choices
{T,v,l}
and
{T^{\prime},v^{\prime},l^{\prime}}
(where
\Delta_{T}
is isotopic to
\Delta

\Delta_{T^{\prime}}
is isotopic to
\Delta^{\prime}
) so that
{D_{f}(T,v,l)}
and
{D_{f^{\prime}}(T^{\prime},v^{\prime},l^{\prime})}
are Hurwitz equivalent decorations. Changing

by braid moves and postcomposing

with a permutation, if necessary, we can assume without loss of generality that the two decorations are equal, say
{D_{f}(T,v,l)}={D_{f^{\prime}}(T^{\prime},v^{\prime},l^{\prime})}={(\mathit{ord},\mathit{mon},\mathit{cyc})}.

Pick a point
x
in
S^{2}\smallsetminus T
and draw an arc
\alpha_{b}
from

to
b
for each point

. Choose these arcs so that their interiors are pairwise disjoint and do not intersect

. Cut along the arcs

to obtain a
2|B|
-gon

(that is, a topological disk whose boundary is subdivided into

, with a regluing map

. For each

, there are two adjacent sides of

that reglue to
\alpha_{b}
– label these

and

in that order going anticlockwise. In the same way, pick a point

in

and cut along arcs

to obtain a
2|B|
-gon

that contains a copy of
T^{\prime}
, with a regluing map

. Label the sides
\alpha^{\prime}_{b,\text{out}}
and
\alpha^{\prime}_{b,\text{in}}
.

The cyclic order of the arcs

– is determined by

. The same is true for the side labels of

. Thus, the side labels of

and

have the same cyclic order, and there is a homeomorphism
\overline{h}:Q\rightarrow Q^{\prime}
that takes
\alpha_{b,\text{out}}
to
\alpha_{b,\text{out}}^{\prime}
and takes
\alpha_{b,\text{in}}
to
\alpha_{b,\text{in}}^{\prime}
.

We also wish this homeomorphism to (1) be compatible with the regluing maps
{g:Q\rightarrow S^{2}}
and
{g^{\prime}:Q^{\prime}\rightarrow S^{2}}
, and (2) take

to

. It is straightforward to see that we can choose
\overline{h}
to satisfy these properties. Gluing back up the source and target,

gives a homeomorphism

such that

. We now show that this homeomorphism lifts.

Take
d

, and label them

. Because the interior of

is simply connected and

is a degree-
d

, the preimage

is the disjoint union of

. Let

be the branch that glues

to the subset of

containing

. The gluing map can be extended to the entire polygon,

. Similarly, define gluing maps

.

This lets us view the surface

as a union of the polygons

, glued along their sides. The gluing instructions are encoded in the decoration

: the side
\alpha_{b,\text{out}}
of

is glued to the side

of

, where

. We can similarly view

as a union of the polygons

, glued along their sides with the same gluing instructions.

Because the gluing instructions are the same, the maps

descend to a homeomorphism

. By definition,

is a lift of

.

We claim that
\widetilde{h}
fixes the points of

. Take

. If
\mathit{cyc}(a)=(i_{1}\,\ldots\,i_{k})\in\operatorname{Cycles}(\mathit{mon}(b))
, then the corner labelled

in each of the polygons

glues to the point

. Similarly, the corner labelled

in each of the polygons

glues to

.
\widetilde{h}
descends from the maps
\overline{h}:Q_{i}\rightarrow Q_{i}^{\prime}

.

∎

This proposition immediately implies that the function
\overline{D}_{f}
outputs the same Hurwitz equivalence class of decorated trees on orbits of multicurves under the action of

, and that the resulting map on orbits is injective.

.

Let
f:(X,A)\rightarrow(S^{2},B)
be a branched cover and let
\Delta
,
\Delta^{\prime}
be multicurves in
(S^{2},B)

\operatorname{LMod}_{f}(\Delta)=\operatorname{LMod}_{f}(\Delta^{\prime})
if and only if
\overline{D}_{f}(\Delta)=\overline{D}_{f}(\Delta^{\prime})
.

6.2.
Orbits of multicurves and decorations

Let
X
be a compact surface of genus
g
, and let
A\subset X
and
B\subset S^{2}
be finite subsets. Fix a portrait
{P=(g,d,B,A,\phi:A\rightarrow B,\operatorname{br},\operatorname{rm})}

of the

-Hurwitz equivalence classes of branched covers realising

\displaystyle\widetilde{D}_{f_{1},\ldots,f_{N}}:\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{N}\operatorname{Mult}_{0,B}\big/\operatorname{LMod}_{f_{i}}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\operatorname{Stab}_{0,B}(P)

\displaystyle\operatorname{LMod}_{f_{i}}(\Delta)

\displaystyle\longmapsto\overline{D}_{f_{i}}(\Delta).

Proposition 6.5
.

The function
\widetilde{D}_{f_{1},\ldots,f_{N}}
is a bijection.

Proof.

We abbreviate
\widetilde{D}_{f_{1},\ldots,f_{N}}
to
\widetilde{D}
throughout the proof.

Injectivity.
Suppose
\widetilde{D}(\operatorname{LMod}_{f_{i}}(\Delta))=\widetilde{D}(\operatorname{LMod}_{f_{j}}(\Delta^{\prime}))
. Then
\overline{D}_{f_{i}}(\Delta)=\overline{D}_{f_{j}}(\Delta^{\prime})
. By Proposition
6.3
,
f_{i}
and
f_{j}
are
(A,B)
-Hurwitz equivalent by homeomorphisms
h,\widetilde{h}
with
h(\Delta)=\Delta^{\prime}
. Because we chose one representative for each
(A,B)
-Hurwitz equivalence class,
f_{i}=f_{j}
and
h
is a liftable homeomorphism. Thus,
\Delta^{\prime}\in\operatorname{LMod}_{f_{i}}(\Delta)
.

Surjectivity.
Let
(\mathit{ord},\mathit{mon},\mathit{cyc})
be a
P
-decorated tree. By Proposition
5.6
, there exists a tree
T
, a vertex
v
and a labelling
l
such that
D_{f}(T,v,l)=(\mathit{ord},\mathit{mon},\mathit{cyc})
. The branched cover
f
is
(A,B)
-Hurwitz equivalent to one of the representatives
f_{i}
, via some homeomorphisms

. By Proposition
6.3
,
\overline{D}_{f_{i}}(h(\Delta_{T}))=\overline{D}_{f}(\Delta_{T})=[(\mathit{ord},\mathit{mon},\mathit{cyc})]
, and so
\widetilde{D}(\operatorname{LMod}_{f_{i}}(h(\Delta))=[(\mathit{ord},\mathit{mon},\mathit{cyc})]
.
∎

A proof of the main theorem follows immediately.

Theorem
1.1
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a Hurwitz space that parametrises maps to
\mathbb{P}^{1}
, with

its defining portrait. The irreducible strata of the compactification

are in bijection with the set

of Hurwitz equivalence classes of

-decorated trees. Furthermore, containment of closures of strata in

-decorated trees.

Proof.

Because we have identified the strata of

as orbits of multicurves (Proposition

), with containment of closures of strata descending from containment of multicurves, the main theorem now follows as an exact translation of the previous proposition.
∎

q(T^{\prime})
.

Applying the same sequence of braid moves to
T
yields an embedding
T^{\prime\prime}
. The two embeddings
T^{\prime}
and
T^{\prime\prime}
satisfy the conditions of Lemma
A.1
:
T^{\prime}
and
T^{\prime\prime}
have a common dual multicurve
\Delta
, and
q(T^{\prime})
and
q(T^{\prime\prime})
are isotopic in
C
rel.
q(B)\cup q(\Delta)
. The lemma tells us that there exists a product
t
of Dehn twists about the curves of
\Delta
so that
t(T^{\prime\prime})
is isotopic to
T^{\prime}
. By Lemma
A.2
, there exists a sequence of braid moves taking
T^{\prime\prime}
to
t(T^{\prime\prime})
.

In all, we have found a sequence of braid moves that takes
T=T_{0}
to
t(T^{\prime\prime})=T_{N}
, which is isotopic to
T^{\prime}
, as required.

Leibniz conformal bialgebras and the classical Leibniz conformal Yang-Baxter equation
Zhongyin Xu, Chengming Bai, Yanyong Hong

Abstract
. We introduce the notion of Leibniz conformal bialgebras, presenting a bialgebra theory for Leibniz conformal algebras as well as the conformal analogues of Leibniz bialgebras. They are equivalently characterized in terms of matched pairs and conformal Manin triples of Leibniz conformal algebras. In the coboundary case, the classical Leibniz conformal Yang-Baxter equation is introduced, whose symmetric solutions give Leibniz conformal bialgebras. Moreover, such solutions are constructed from 𝒪 \mathcal{O} -operators on Leibniz conformal algebras and Leibniz-dendriform conformal algebras. On the other hand, the notion of Novikov bi-dialgebras is introduced, which correspond to a class of Leibniz conformal bialgebras, lifting the correspondence between Novikov dialgebras and a class of Leibniz conformal algebras to the context of bialgebras. In addition, we introduce the notion of classical duplicate Novikov Yang-Baxter equation whose symmetric solutions produce Novikov bi-dialgebras and thus Leibniz conformal bialgebras.

1.
Introduction

The notion of Lie conformal algebras was introduced by Kac in
[
22
,
23
]
as a formal language describing the algebraic properties of the operator product expansions of chiral fields in two-dimensional conformal field theory. Moreover, Lie conformal algebras are closely related with infinite-dimensional Lie algebras satisfying the locality property
[
24
]
, local Poisson brackets in the theory of integrable evolution equations
[
3
]
and so on. The structure theory
[
11
]
, representation theory
[
9
,
10
]
and cohomology theory
[
2
,
13
]
of finite Lie conformal algebras have been studied and well developed.

Leibniz conformal algebras introduced in
[
2
]
are the
conformal analogues of Leibniz algebras which were first
discovered by Bloh and named D-algebras
[
6
]
and then
rediscovered by Loday

[
27
,
28

. Leibniz conformal algebras are non-commutative
generalizations of Lie conformal algebras and have close
connections to field algebras
[
1
]
. From the perspective of algebraic structures, a Leibniz conformal
algebra is a Leibniz pseudoalgebra
[
31
]
which is a Leibniz
algebra in the pseudo-tensor category
[
4
]
. Thus Leibniz
conformal algebras can also be seen as generalizations of Leibniz
algebras. Recently, Leibniz conformal algebras have been widely
studied, for example, quadratic Leibniz conformal algebras and
their central extensions
[
36
]
, the cohomology theory of
Leibniz conformal algebras
[
35
]
and so on. For more details,
see
[
16
,
12
,
21
,
33
,
32
]
.
It is well known that Lie bialgebras have important applications in both mathematics and
mathematical physics
[
8
,
14
]
. A natural idea is to study
“conformal analogues” of bialgebras.
Liberati developed the theory of Lie conformal bialgebras in

[
26
]
as the conformal analogues of Lie bialgebras, which
includes the introduction of the notions of conformal classical
Yang-Baxter equation, conformal Manin triples and conformal
Drinfeld’s doubles. The operator forms of conformal classical
Yang-Baxter equation were investigated in
[
18
]
. Similarly,
the theories of antisymmetric infinitesimal conformal bialgebras
and left-symmetric conformal bialgebras were developed in

[
17
,
20
]
.
However, there is no conformal analogue of “Leibniz bialgebras”
as far as we know. Thus the purpose of this paper is to
investigate a bialgebra theory for Leibniz conformal algebras,
namely, Leibniz conformal bialgebras, giving the conformal
analogues of Leibniz bialgebras. That is, the following diagram is

In order to characterize Leibniz conformal bialgebras, we

bialgebras are equivalent to some matched pairs of Leibniz

conformal Yang-Baxter equation (CLCYBE), as a conformal analogue
of the classical Leibniz Yang-Baxter equation. Notably, its

-operator on the associated Leibniz conformal
algebra. Therefore there are constructions of symmetric solutions

2.
Preliminaries on Leibniz conformal algebras

We recall some facts about Leibniz conformal algebras and show
that there is a correspondence between Novikov dialgebras and a
class of Leibniz conformal algebras. Moreover, we introduce the
notions of dual representations, matched pairs and conformal Manin
triples of Leibniz conformal algebras and show that under some
conditions, some matched pair of Leibniz conformal algebras is
equivalent to a conformal Manin triple of Leibniz conformal
algebras.

2.1.
Leibniz conformal algebras and Novikov dialgebras

Definition 2.1
.

[
1
]

A
Leibniz conformal algebra

Q
is a
{\bf k}[\partial]
-module with a
\lambda
-bracket
[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]
which is a

{\bf k}
-bilinear map from
Q\times Q\rightarrow Q[\lambda]
, satisfying

(conformal sesquilinearity)

\displaystyle[\partial a_{\lambda}b]=-\lambda[a_{\lambda}b],\quad\quad\quad\quad[a_{\lambda}\partial b]=(\lambda+\partial)[a_{\lambda}b],

(Jacobi identity)

\displaystyle[a_{\lambda}[b_{\mu}c]]=[[a_{\lambda}b]_{\lambda+\mu}c]+[b_{\mu}[a_{\lambda}c]]\;\;\;\text{for all $a,b,c\in Q$.}

A Leibniz conformal algebra
Q
is called
finite
, if it is finitely generated as a
{\bf k}[\partial]
-module. Otherwise, we call it
infinite
.

Remark 2.2
.

Recall
[
22
]
that a
Lie conformal algebra

Q
is a Leibniz conformal algebra satisfying

(skew-symmetry)

[a_{\lambda}b]=-[b_{-\lambda-\partial}a]\;\;\;\text{for all $a,b\in Q$.}

Thus Lie conformal algebras are Leibniz conformal algebras.

Recall
[
27
]
that a
Leibniz algebra

(A,[\cdot,\cdot])
is a vector space
A
with a bilinear operation
[\cdot,\cdot]:A\times A\rightarrow A
, satisfying

[a,[b,c]]=[[a,b],c]+[b,[a,c]]\qquad\text{for all $a,b,c\in A$.}

Example 2.3
.

Let
(A,[\cdot,\cdot])
be a Leibniz algebra. Then we can
naturally define a Leibniz conformal algebra structure on

\text{Cur}~A={\bf k}[\partial]\otimes A
with the

\lambda
-bracket:

[a_{\lambda}b]=[a,b]\;\;\;\text{for all $a,b\in A$.}

\text{Cur}~A
is called the
current Leibniz conformal algebra
associated with
(A,[\cdot,\cdot])
.

Proposition 2.4
.

[
21
]

Let
Q={\bf k}[\partial]L
be a Leibniz conformal algebra which is a free
{\bf k}[\partial]
-module of rank 1. Then
Q
is either abelian or isomorphic to the Virasoro Lie conformal algebra, that is,
[L_{\lambda}L]=(\partial+2\lambda)L
.

Next, we recall the definition of Novikov dialgebras.

Definition 2.5
.

[
25
]

Let
A
be a vector space with two binary operations
\vdash
and
\dashv
. If for all
a
,
b
,
c\in A
, they satisfy the following equalities

\displaystyle(a\vdash b)\dashv c=(a\vdash c)\vdash b=(a\dashv c)\vdash b,\;\;a\dashv(b\dashv c)=a\dashv(b\vdash c),

\displaystyle(a\dashv b)\dashv c=(a\dashv c)\dashv b,\;\;(a\dashv b)\dashv c-a\dashv(b\dashv c)=(b\vdash a)\dashv c-b\vdash(a\dashv c),

\displaystyle(a\vdash b)\vdash c-a\vdash(b\vdash c)=(b\vdash a)\vdash c-b\vdash(a\vdash c),

then
(A,\dashv,\vdash)
is called a
(left) Novikov dialgebra
.

Remark 2.6
.

There is also the definition of right Novikov dialgebras. A
right Novikov dialgebra
is a triple
(A,\dashv,\vdash)
where
A
is a vector space with two binary operations satisfying

\displaystyle a\vdash(b\vdash c)=b\vdash(a\vdash c),~~a\vdash(b\dashv c)=b\dashv(a\dashv c),\;\;(a\vdash b-a\dashv b)\vdash c=0,

\displaystyle a\dashv(b\vdash c-b\dashv c)=0,\;\;a\vdash(b\vdash c-c\dashv b)=(a\vdash b)\vdash c-(a\vdash c)\dashv b,

\displaystyle a\dashv(b\vdash c-c\dashv b)=(a\dashv b)\dashv c-(a\dashv c)\dashv b\;\;\;\text{for all $a,b,c\in A$.}

Note that such algebra structures appear in the study of quadratic
Leibniz conformal algebras
[
36
]
.
Moreover, it is easy to see that
(A,\dashv,\vdash)
is a
Novikov dialgebra if and only if
(A,\dashv^{\prime},\vdash^{\prime})
is a
right Novikov dialgebra where
\vdash^{\prime}
and
\dashv^{\prime}
are defined
by

\displaystyle a\dashv^{\prime}b:=b\vdash a,\;\;a\vdash^{\prime}b:=b\dashv a\;\;\;\text{for all $a,b\in A$.}

Recall that a
perm algebra
is a pair
(A,\cdot)
, where
A

is a vector space,
\cdot
is a binary operation and the following
equalities hold.

(4)

(a\cdot b)\cdot c=a\cdot(b\cdot c)=b\cdot(a\cdot c)\;\;\;\text{for all $a,b,c\in A$}.

Lemma 2.7
.

[
25
]

Let
(A,\cdot)
be a perm algebra and
D
be a derivation, that
is,
D
satisfies
D(a\cdot b)=D(a)\cdot b+a\cdot D(b)
for all

a,b\in A
. Define two binary operations
\vdash
and
\dashv
by

(5)

a\vdash b:=a\cdot D(b),\;\;a\dashv b:=D(b)\cdot a\;\;\;\text{for all $a,b\in A$.}

Then
(A,\dashv,\vdash)
is a Novikov dialgebra.

Next, we give a correspondence between Novikov dialgebras and a class of Leibniz conformal algebras.

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
(A,\dashv,\vdash)
be a vector space with binary operations
\vdash
and
\dashv
. Equip the free
{\bf k}[\partial]
-module
Q:={\bf k}[\partial]A

(={\bf k}[\partial]\otimes A)
with the
\lambda
-bracket

(6)

[a_{\lambda}b]:=\partial(b\dashv a)+\lambda(a\vdash b+b\dashv a)\;\;\;\text{for all $a,b\in A.$}

Then
(Q,[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot])
is a Leibniz conformal algebra if and only if
(A,\dashv,\vdash)
is a Novikov dialgebra. We call
Q
the
Leibniz conformal algebra corresponding to

(A,\dashv,\vdash)
.

Proof.

Define

(7)

[a_{\lambda}b]:=\partial(a\vdash^{\prime}b)+\lambda(a\vdash^{\prime}b+b\dashv^{\prime}a)\;\;\;\text{for all $a,b\in A$,}

where
\vdash^{\prime}
and
\dashv^{\prime}
are two binary operations on
A
.
By
[
36
, Theorem 3.1]
,
(Q,[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot])
is a Leibniz conformal algebra if and only if
(A,\dashv^{\prime},\vdash^{\prime})
is a right Novikov dialgebra. Set

\displaystyle a\dashv b:=b\vdash^{\prime}a,\;\;a\vdash b:=b\dashv^{\prime}a\;\;\;\text{for all $a,b\in A$.}

By Remark
2.6
,
(A,\dashv^{\prime},\vdash^{\prime})
is a right
Novikov dialgebra if and only if
(A,\dashv,\vdash)
is a Novikov
dialgebra. Then the conclusion follows.
∎

Corollary 2.9
.

Let
(A,\cdot)
be a perm algebra and
D
be a derivation on
(A,\cdot)
. Equip the free
{\bf k}[\partial]
-module
Q:={\bf k}[\partial]A

(={\bf k}[\partial]\otimes A)
with the

\lambda
-bracket

(8)

[a_{\lambda}b]:=\partial(D(a)\cdot b)+\lambda(a\cdot D(b)+D(a)\cdot b)\;\;\;\text{for all $a,b\in A$.}

Then
(Q,[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot])
is a Leibniz conformal algebra.

Proof.

Define two binary operations
\vdash
and
\dashv
by
(
5
).
By Lemma
2.7
,
(A,\dashv,\vdash)
is a
Novikov dialgebra. Then it follows directly from Proposition

2.8
.
∎

2.2.
Matched pairs of Leibniz conformal algebras

Definition 2.10
.

[
22
]

Let
U
and
V
be two
{\bf k}[\partial]
-modules.
A
conformal linear map
from
U
to
V
is a
{\bf k}
-linear map
a_{\lambda}:U\rightarrow V[\lambda]
such that
a_{\lambda}(\partial u)=(\lambda+\partial)a_{\lambda}u
for all
u\in U
.
The vector space of all conformal linear maps from
U
to
V
, denoted by
\text{Chom}(U,V)
, has a
{\bf k}[\partial]
-module structure given by

(\partial a)_{\lambda}u=-\lambda a_{\lambda}u\;\;\;\text{for all $a\in\text{Chom}(U,V)$ and $u\in U$.}

In particular, if
U=V
, then we denote
\text{Chom}(U,V)
by
\text{Cend}(V)
. If
V
is a finite
{\bf k}[\partial]
-module, then
\text{Cend}(V)
is a Lie conformal algebra defined by

[f_{\lambda}g]_{\mu}v=f_{\lambda}(g_{\mu-\lambda}v)-g_{\mu-\lambda}(f_{\lambda}v)\;\;\;\text{for all $f,g\in\text{Cend}(V)$ and $v\in V$}.

We define the conformal dual of a
{\bf k}[\partial]
-module
U
as
U^{*c}=\text{Chom}(U,{\bf k})
, where
{\bf k}
is viewed as the trivial
{\bf k}[\partial]
-module, that is,

U^{*c}=\{a:U\rightarrow{\bf k}[\lambda]~~|~~\text{ ${\bf k}$-linear and }a_{\lambda}(\partial u)=\lambda a_{\lambda}u~~\text{for all $u\in U$}\}
. For convenience in the following, we set
\langle a,u\rangle_{\lambda}=a_{\lambda}u
for all
a\in U^{*c}
and
u\in U
.

Let
U
,
V
and
W
be
{\bf k}[\partial]
-modules. A
conformal bilinear map
from
U\times V
to
W
is a
{\bf k}
-bilinear map
f_{\lambda}:U\times V\rightarrow W[\lambda]
, such that
f_{\lambda}(\partial u,v)=-\lambda f_{\lambda}(u,v)
and
f_{\lambda}(u,\partial v)=(\lambda+\partial)f_{\lambda}(u,v)

for all
u\in U
and
v\in V
.

Definition 2.11
.

[
35
]

A representation
of a Leibniz conformal algebra
(Q,[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot])
is a triple
(M,l,r)
, where
M
is a
{\bf k}[\partial]
-module and

:

(9)

\displaystyle l(a)_{\lambda}(l(b)_{\mu}v)=l([a_{\lambda}b])_{\lambda+\mu}v+l(b)_{\mu}(l(a)_{\lambda}v),

(10)

\displaystyle l(a)_{\lambda}(r(b)_{-\mu-\partial}v)=r(b)_{-\lambda-\mu-\partial}(l(a)_{\lambda}v)+r([a_{\lambda}b])_{-\mu-\partial}v,

(11)

\displaystyle r(b)_{-\lambda-\mu-\partial}(r(a)_{-\mu-\partial}v)=-r(b)_{-\lambda-\mu-\partial}(l(a)_{\lambda}v)\;\;\;\text{for all $a,b\in Q$ and $v\in M$}.

A representation
(M,l,r)
of
(Q,[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot])

{\bf k}[\partial]
-module.

.

2.3.
Conformal Manin triples of Leibniz conformal algebras

Definition 2.18
.

Let
M
be a
{\bf k}[\partial]
-module. A
conformal bilinear form
on
M
is a
{\bf k}
-bilinear map
B_{\lambda}(\cdot,\cdot):M\times M\rightarrow{\bf k}[\lambda]
satisfying

B_{\lambda}(\partial u,v)=-\lambda B_{\lambda}(u,v),\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,B_{\lambda}(u,\partial v)=\lambda B_{\lambda}(u,v)\;\;\;\text{for all $u,v\in M$.}

A conformal bilinear form
B_{\lambda}(\cdot,\cdot)
on
M
is called
skew-symmetric
if

B_{\lambda}(u,v)=-B_{-\lambda}(v,u)\;\;\;\text{for all $u,v\in M$}.

Remark 2.19
.

If there is a conformal bilinear form on a
{\bf k}[\partial]
-module
M
, then we have a
{\bf k}[\partial]
-module homomorphism

\phi:M\rightarrow M^{\ast c}
sending
v
to
\phi_{v}
where
\phi_{v}
is defined by

(\phi_{v})_{\lambda}w=B_{\lambda}(v,w)\;\;\;\text{for all $v,w\in M$}.

Moreover, if
\phi
is an isomorphism of
{\bf k}[\partial]
-modules, then this conformal bilinear form is called

non-degenerate
.

Definition 2.20
.

A
conformal Manin triple
of Leibniz conformal algebras is a triple of finite Leibniz conformal algebras
(Q,Q_{0},Q_{1})
, where

(a)

there is a non-degenerate skew-symmetric conformal bilinear form
B_{\lambda}(\cdot,\cdot)
on
Q
such that the following invariant condition holds:

(21)

B_{\mu}(a,[b_{\lambda}c])=B_{-\lambda}([a_{\mu}c]+[c_{-\mu-\partial}a],b)\;\;\;\text{for all $a,b,c\in Q$},

(b)

Q_{0}
and
Q_{1}
are Leibniz conformal subalgebras of
Q
, where
Q=Q_{0}\oplus Q_{1}
as a direct sum of
{\bf k}[\partial]
-modules,

(c)

Q_{0}
and
Q_{1}
are isotropic with respect to
B_{\lambda}(\cdot,\cdot)
, i.e.
B_{\lambda}(Q_{i},Q_{i})=0
for
i=0,1
.

Example 2.21
.

Let

be a finite Leibniz conformal algebra which is free as a
{\bf k}[\partial]
-module. Then
(Q\ltimes_{L^{*},-L^{*}-R^{*}}Q^{*c},Q,Q^{*c})

on

In the sequel, for convenience, we set
\langle a,f\rangle_{-\lambda}:=-\langle f,a\rangle_{\lambda}
for all

and

.

Theorem 2.22
.

Let
Q
and
Q^{*c}
be two finite Leibniz conformal algebras which are free as
{\bf k}[\partial]
-modules. Then
(Q\bowtie Q^{*c},Q,Q^{*c})

(Q,Q^{*c};(L^{*},-L^{*}-R^{*}),(\mathcal{L}^{*},-\mathcal{L}^{*}-\mathcal{R}^{*}))

Proof.

Let
(Q,Q^{*c};(L^{*},-L^{*}-R^{*}),(\mathcal{L}^{*},-\mathcal{L}^{*}-\mathcal{R}^{*}))

\lambda

20

is non-degenerate and skew-symmetric. For all

and

we have

\displaystyle B_{\mu}(a+f,[{(b+g)}_{\lambda}(c+h)])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle B_{\mu}(a+f,[b_{\lambda}c]+\mathcal{L}^{*}(g)_{\lambda}c+(-\mathcal{L}^{*}-\mathcal{R}^{*})(h)_{-\lambda-\partial}b+[g_{\lambda}z]+L^{*}(b)_{\lambda}h+(-L^{*}-R^{*})(c)_{-\lambda-\partial}g)

\displaystyle\langle f,[b_{\lambda}c]\rangle_{\mu}+\langle c,[g_{\lambda}f]\rangle_{-\mu-\lambda}-\langle b,[h_{-\mu-\lambda}f]\rangle_{\lambda}-\langle b,[f_{\mu}h]\rangle_{\lambda}+\langle a,[g_{\lambda}h]\rangle_{\mu}

\displaystyle+\langle h,[b_{\lambda}a]\rangle_{-\lambda-\mu}-\langle g,[c_{-\lambda-\mu}a]\rangle_{\lambda}-\langle g,[a_{\mu}c]\rangle_{\lambda}.

On the other hand, we have

\displaystyle B_{-\lambda}([{(a+f)}_{\mu}(c+h)]+[{(c+h)}_{-\mu-\partial}(a+f)],b+g)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle B_{-\lambda}([a_{\mu}c]+\mathcal{L}^{*}(f)_{\mu}c-(\mathcal{L}^{*}+\mathcal{R}^{*})(h)_{-\mu-\partial}a+[f_{\mu}h]+L^{*}(a)_{\mu}h-(L^{*}+R^{*})(c)_{-\mu-\partial}f,b+g)

\displaystyle+\langle c,[g_{\lambda}f]\rangle_{-\lambda-\mu}+\langle[f_{\mu}h]+[h_{-\mu-\partial}f],b\rangle_{-\lambda}+\langle f,[b_{\lambda}c]\rangle_{\mu}+\langle h,[b_{\lambda}a]\rangle_{-\lambda-\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle f,[b_{\lambda}c]\rangle_{\mu}+\langle c,[g_{\lambda}f]\rangle_{-\lambda-\mu}-\langle b,[h_{-\lambda-\mu}f]\rangle_{\lambda}-\langle b,[f_{\mu}h]\rangle_{\lambda}+\langle a,[g_{\lambda}h]\rangle_{\mu}

Thus

Conversely, let
(Q\Join Q^{*c},Q,Q^{*c})

3.
Leibniz conformal bialgebras

We introduce the notion of Leibniz conformal bialgebras and then
establish the equivalences among some matched pairs of Leibniz
conformal algebras, conformal Manin triples of Leibniz conformal
algebras, and Leibniz conformal bialgebras.

First, we introduce the notion of Leibniz conformal coalgebras.

Definition 3.1
.

A
Leibniz conformal coalgebra
is a
{\bf k}[\partial]
-module
Q
endowed with a
{\bf k}[\partial]
-module homomorphism
\Delta:Q\rightarrow Q\otimes Q
such that

(23)

({\rm id}\otimes\Delta)\Delta(a)=(\Delta\otimes{\rm id})\Delta(a)+\tau_{12}({\rm id}\otimes\Delta)\Delta(a)\;\;\;\text{for all $a\in Q$},

where the module action of
{\bf k}[\partial]
on
Q\otimes Q
is defined as

\partial(a\otimes b)=(\partial a)\otimes b+a\otimes(\partial b)
for all
a,b\in Q
.

Let
Q
be a
{\bf k}[\partial]
-module. For all
f,g\in Q^{*c}
and
a,b\in Q
, we define

\langle f\otimes g,a\otimes b\rangle_{\lambda,\mu}=\langle f,a\rangle_{\lambda}\langle g,b\rangle_{\mu}.

Proposition 3.2
.

(a)

Let
(Q,\Delta)
be a finite Leibniz conformal coalgebra. Then
Q^{*c}
is a Leibniz conformal algebra with the following
\lambda
-bracket:

(24)

\langle[f_{\lambda}g],a\rangle_{\mu}=\langle f\otimes g,\Delta(a)\rangle_{\lambda,\mu-\lambda}\;\;\;\text{for all $f,g\in Q^{\ast c}$ and $a\in Q$}.

(b)

Let
Q
be a finite Leibniz conformal algebra which is free as a
{\bf k}[\partial]
-module, that is,
Q=\sum^{n}_{i=1}{\bf k}[\partial]a^{i}
, where
\{a^{1},\cdots,a^{n}\}
is a
{\bf k}[\partial]
-basis of
Q
. Then
Q^{*c}=\text{Chom}(A,{\bf k})=\sum^{n}_{i=1}{\bf k}[\partial]a_{i}
, where
\{a_{1},\cdots,a_{n}\}
is the dual
{\bf k}[\partial]
-basis in the sense that
(a_{i})_{\lambda}a^{j}=\delta_{ij}
, is a Leibniz conformal coalgebra with the following co-bracket:

(25)

\Delta(f)=\sum_{i,j}f_{\mu}([a^{i}_{\lambda}a^{j}])(a_{i}\otimes a_{j})|_{\lambda=\partial\otimes{\rm id},\mu=-\partial\otimes{\rm id}-{\rm id}\otimes\partial}.

More precisely, if
[a^{i}_{\lambda}a^{j}]=\sum_{k}P^{ij}_{k}(\lambda,\partial)a^{k}
, where

P^{ij}_{k}(\lambda,\partial)\in{\bf k}[\lambda,\partial]
, then
the co-bracket is

(26)

\Delta(a_{k})=\sum_{i,j}Q^{ij}_{k}(\partial\otimes{\rm id},{\rm id}\otimes\partial)a_{i}\otimes a_{j},

where
Q^{ij}_{k}(x,y)=P^{ij}_{k}(x,-x-y)
.

Proof.

It follows directly by the proof of
[
26
, Proposition 2.13]
.
∎

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
(Q,\Delta)
be a Leibniz conformal coalgebra where
Q
is free of rank
1
as a
{\bf k}[\partial]
-module. Then
(Q,\Delta)\simeq({\bf k}[\partial]a,\Delta^{\prime})
with
\Delta^{\prime}(a)=0
or
\Delta^{\prime}(a)=c(\partial a\otimes a-a\otimes\partial a)
,
c\in{\bf k}\backslash\{0\}
.

Proof.

It follows directly by Propositions
2.4
and
3.2
.
∎

In the sequel, we always set
\partial^{\otimes^{2}}:=\partial\otimes{\rm id}+{\rm id}\otimes\partial.

Next, we introduce the definition of Leibniz conformal bialgebras.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
(Q,[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot])
be a Leibniz conformal algebra and
(Q,\Delta)
be a Leibniz conformal coalgebra. Then
(Q,[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot],\Delta)
is called a
Leibniz conformal bialgebra
if the following conditions hold:

(27)

\displaystyle(R(a)_{\lambda-\partial\otimes{\rm id}}\otimes{\rm id})\Delta(b)=\tau((R(b)_{-\lambda-\partial\otimes{\rm id}}\otimes{\rm id})\Delta(a)),

(28)

\displaystyle\Delta([a_{\lambda}b])=({\rm id}\otimes R(b)_{-\lambda-\partial^{\otimes^{2}}}-L(b)_{-\lambda-\partial^{\otimes^{2}}}\otimes{\rm id}-R(b)_{-\lambda-\partial^{\otimes^{2}}}\otimes{\rm id})(\tau+{\rm id})\Delta(a)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+({\rm id}\otimes L(a)_{\lambda}+L(a)_{\lambda}\otimes{\rm id})\Delta(b)\;\;\;\text{for all $a,b\in Q$.}

We give a remark to illustrate the
relationship between Leibniz bialgebras and Leibniz conformal
algebras.

Remark 3.5
.

Recall
[
30
]
that a Leibniz bialgebra is a triple
(A,[\cdot,\cdot],\delta)
, where
(A,[\cdot,\cdot])
is a Leibniz algebra,
(A,\delta)
is a Leibniz coalgebra in the sense that

({\rm id}\otimes\delta)\delta(a)=(\delta\otimes{\rm id})\delta(a)+\tau_{12}({\rm id}\otimes\delta)\delta(a)\;\;\;\text{for all $a\in A$},

and they satisfy the following equalities

\displaystyle(R_{A}(a)\otimes{\rm id})\delta(b)=\tau((R_{A}(b)\otimes{\rm id})\delta(a)),

\displaystyle\delta([a,b])=({\rm id}\otimes R_{A}(b)-L_{A}(b)\otimes{\rm id}-R_{A}(b)\otimes{\rm id})(\tau+{\rm id})\delta(a)+({\rm id}\otimes L_{A}(a)+L_{A}(a)_{\lambda}\otimes{\rm id})\delta(b),

for all
a,b\in A
, where
L_{A}(a)(b)=[a,b]=R_{A}(b)(a)
.

Let
(A,[\cdot,\cdot],\delta)
be a Leibniz bialgebra. Then
(\text{Cur}\;A={\bf k}[\partial]\otimes A,[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot],\Delta)
is a Leibniz conformal bialgebra, where
(\text{Cur}\;A,[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot])
is the current Leibniz conformal algebra associated with
(A,[\cdot,\cdot])
and
\Delta
is a
{\bf k}[\partial]
-module homomorphism defined by

\displaystyle\Delta(a)=\delta(a)\;\;\;\text{for all $a\in A$.}

(\text{Cur}\;A={\bf k}[\partial]\otimes A,[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot],\Delta)
is called the
current Leibniz conformal bialgebras
associated with
(A,[\cdot,\cdot],\delta)
.

Conversely, let
(Q,[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot],\Delta)
be a Leibniz conformal bialgebra and
\pi:Q\rightarrow Q/\partial Q
be the natural projection. Set
[a_{\lambda}b]=\sum_{i=0}^{n_{a,b}}\frac{\lambda^{i}}{i!}a_{(i)}b
for all
a
,
b\in Q
. Then it is easy to see that
(Q/\partial Q,[\cdot,\cdot],\delta)
is a Leibniz bialgebra, where
[\cdot,\cdot]
and
\delta
are defined by

\displaystyle[a+\partial Q,b+\partial Q]:=\pi(a_{(0)}b),\;\;\;\delta(a+\partial Q):=(\pi\otimes\pi)\Delta(a)\;\;\;\text{for all $a$, $b\in Q$.}

Hence in the above sense, Leibniz conformal bialgebras are regarded as “conformal analogues” of Leibniz bialgebras.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
(Q,[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot])
be a finite Leibniz conformal algebra which is free as a
{\bf k}[\partial]
-module. Suppose that there is a Leibniz conformal algebra structure on
Q^{\ast c}

which is obtained from a
{\bf k}[\partial]
-module homomorphism
\Delta:Q\rightarrow Q\otimes Q
.
Then
(Q,Q^{*c};(L^{*},-L^{*}-R^{*}),(\mathcal{L}^{*},-\mathcal{L}^{*}-\mathcal{R}^{*}))

(Q,[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot],\Delta)
is a Leibniz conformal bialgebra.

Proof.

Let
\{a^{1},\cdots,a^{n}\}
be a
{\bf k}[\partial]
-basis of
Q
,
\{a_{1},\cdots,a_{n}\}
be the dual
{\bf k}[\partial]
-basis of
Q^{*c}
and

[{a^{i}}_{\lambda}a^{j}]=\sum_{k}P^{ij}_{k}(\lambda,\partial)a^{k},\qquad\quad[{a_{i}}_{\lambda}a_{j}]=\sum_{k}H^{ij}_{k}(\lambda,\partial)a_{k},

where
P^{ij}_{k}(\lambda,\partial)
,
H^{ij}_{k}(\lambda,\partial)\in{\bf k}[\lambda,\partial]
.
By Proposition
3.2
, we get

\Delta(a^{k})=\sum_{i,j}Q_{k}^{ij}(\partial\otimes{\rm id},{\rm id}\otimes\partial)a^{i}\otimes a^{j}
, where
Q^{ij}_{k}(x,y)=H^{ij}_{k}(x,-x-y)
.
Since

\langle\mathcal{L}^{*}(a_{i})_{\lambda}a^{j},a_{k}\rangle_{\mu}=-\langle{a^{j}},[{a_{i}}_{\lambda}a_{k}]\rangle_{\mu-\lambda}=-H^{ik}_{j}(\lambda,\mu-\lambda),

we get
\mathcal{L}^{*}(a_{i})_{\lambda}a^{j}=-\sum_{k}H^{ik}_{j}(\lambda,-\lambda-\partial)a^{k}
.
Similarly, we can get

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}^{*}(a_{i})_{\lambda}a^{j}=-\sum_{k}H^{ki}_{j}(\partial,-\lambda-\partial)a^{k},\qquad\qquad L^{*}(a^{i})_{\lambda}a_{j}=-\sum_{k}P^{ik}_{j}(\lambda,-\lambda-\partial)a_{k},

\displaystyle R^{*}(a^{i})_{\lambda}a_{j}=-\sum_{k}P^{ki}_{j}(\partial,-\lambda-\partial)a_{k}.

Considering the coefficient of
a^{j}\otimes a^{h}
in

(R(a^{i})_{\lambda-\partial\otimes{\rm id}}\otimes{\rm id})\Delta(a^{k})=\tau((R(a^{k})_{-\lambda-\partial\otimes{\rm id}}\otimes{\rm id})\Delta(a^{i}))

we get

(29)

\displaystyle\sum_{t}H^{th}_{k}(\lambda,-\lambda-{\rm id}\otimes\partial)P^{ti}_{j}(-\lambda,\partial\otimes{\rm id})=\sum_{t}H^{tj}_{i}(-\lambda,\lambda-\partial\otimes{\rm id})P^{tk}_{h}(\lambda,{\rm id}\otimes\partial).

On the other hand, (
19
) holds for all
a,b\in Q
and

if and only if

\displaystyle-\langle\mathcal{L}^{*}(R^{*}(a^{i})_{-\lambda-\partial}a_{j})_{\lambda+\mu}a^{k},a_{h}\rangle_{\theta}=\langle[(\mathcal{R}^{*}(a_{j})_{-\mu-\partial}a^{i})_{\lambda+\mu}a^{k}],a_{h}\rangle_{\theta}\;\;\;\text{for all $i,j,k,h\in I$},

holds if and only if

(30)

\sum_{t}H^{th}_{k}(\lambda+\mu,-\lambda-\mu+\theta)P^{ti}_{j}(-\lambda-\mu,\lambda)=\sum_{t}H^{tj}_{i}(-\lambda-\mu,\mu)P^{tk}_{h}(\lambda+\mu,-\theta)\;\;\;\text{for all $i,j,k,h\in I$},

holds.
Obviously, (
29
) is equivalent to (
30
) by replacing
\lambda
by
\lambda+\mu
,
{\rm id}\otimes\partial
by
-\theta
and
\partial\otimes{\rm id}
by
\lambda
in (

) holds if and only if (
19
) holds.

Note that (
18
) holds for all
a,b\in Q
and
x\in Q^{*c}
if and only if for all
i,j,k,h\in I
,

\displaystyle\langle[{a^{i}}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{L}^{*}(a_{j})_{\mu}a^{k})]+(\mathcal{L}^{*}+\mathcal{R}^{*})((L^{*}+R^{*})(a^{k})_{-\mu-\partial}a_{j})_{-\lambda-\partial}a^{i},a_{h}\rangle_{\theta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{L}^{*}(a_{j})_{\mu}[{a^{i}}_{\lambda}a^{k}]-[(\mathcal{L}^{*}(a_{j})_{\mu}a^{i})_{\lambda+\mu}a^{k}]+\mathcal{L}^{*}((L^{*}+R^{*})(a^{i})_{-\mu-\partial}a_{j})_{\lambda+\mu}a^{k},a_{h}\rangle_{\theta}

holds, which is equivalent to

(31)

\displaystyle\sum_{t}(P^{kt}_{j}(-\lambda-\mu+\theta,\mu)+P^{tk}_{j}(\lambda-\theta,\mu))(H^{th}_{i}(-\lambda+\theta,\lambda)+H^{ht}_{i}(-\theta,\lambda))

\displaystyle-\sum_{t}H^{jt}_{k}(\mu,-\mu-\lambda+\theta)P^{it}_{h}(\lambda,-\theta)=-\sum_{t}P^{ik}_{t}(\lambda,\mu-\theta)H^{jh}_{t}(\mu,-\mu+\theta)

\displaystyle+\sum_{t}(H^{jt}_{i}(\mu,\lambda)P^{tk}_{h}(\lambda+\mu,-\theta)+(P^{it}_{j}(\lambda,\mu)+P^{ti}_{j}(-\lambda-\mu,\mu))H^{th}_{k}(\lambda+\mu,-\lambda-\mu+\theta)).

On the other hand, (
28
) holds if and only if for all
i,k\in I

4.

5.

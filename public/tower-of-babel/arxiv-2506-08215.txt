Homogenization of elasto-plastic plate equations with vanishing hardening
Marin Bužančić, Igor Velčić, Josip Žubrinić

Abstract
. Building on recent work analyzing the interaction between homogenization and dimension reduction in perfectly plastic materials, we focus on the extreme regime where the plate thickness vanishes much faster than the period of oscillation of the heterogeneous material. Our investigation is devoted to the derivation of the existence of a quasistatic evolution for a multiphase elasto-plastic composite without imposing any kind of ordering constraints on admissible yield surfaces of the various phases. The analysis is carried out in two steps: we first derive a heterogeneous plate model with hardening via dimension reduction using evolutionary Γ \Gamma –convergence techniques, and then perform two-scale homogenization while simultaneously letting the hardening parameters tend to zero using a stress-strain approach. This approach allows us to reconcile the Kirchhoff–Love structure of limiting displacements with the expected requirement that the effective dissipation potential at each point of the interface between two phases needs to be the pointwise-in-space inf-convolution of that in either phase.

1.
Introduction

In this work, we consider a lower dimensional homogenized thin plate model within the framework of linearized elasto-plasticity.
Our investigation builds on the results in
[
4
]
and
[
5
]
, where the authors analyzed the asymptotic behaviour of perfectly plastic composite materials when both the thickness
h
of the plate and the periodicity
\varepsilon_{h}
of the microstructure tend to zero.
The effective models are dependent on the interaction between homogenization and dimension reduction, which can be described by the ratio
\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{h}{\varepsilon_{h}}=\gamma\in[0,+\infty]
.
In particular,
[
5
]
imposed certain geometric constraints on admissible yield surfaces in the extreme regimes
\gamma=0
and
\gamma=+\infty
to ensure lower semicontinuity of the dissipation potential – a condition not required in the intermediate regimes considered in
[
4
]
.
In the present contribution, we complete the study by analyzing the limiting case
\gamma=0
, corresponding to the situations where the thickness of the plate vanishes on a much faster scale than the material’s periodicity, without assuming any specific ordering between phases.
A key element in the analysis is the two step process, in which we first derive a heterogeneous plate model with hardening via dimension reduction, and then secondly we perform homogenization and simultaneously let hardening tend to zero.
With this approach at hand, we are able to circumvent the difficulty in defining the pointwise-in-space inf-convolution dissipation potential on the interface, which is present in the analysis of the heterogeneous perfectly plastic materials.
Precisely, the effective dissipation functional is defined as the nonlocal inf-convolution of the traces of plastic strains from both sides of the interface having a specific rank-one structure, which is inherited from the Kirchhoff–Love structure of the displacements.

In the context of linearized elasto-plasticity, reduced plate models were derived using a general theory of rate-independent systems and evolutionary
\Gamma
–convergence developed in works such as
[
22
]
,
[
25
]
,
[
24
]
.
This was done for hardening plastic materials in
[
20
]
and
[
21
]
, and for perfectly plastic materials in
[
8
]
and
[
9
]
.
The functional analysis is much simpler in the case of hardening material than in perfect plasticity, where the formulation lies in the space of functions with bounded deformation and the space of bounded Radon measures.
The main existence result for three-dimensional quasistatic evolution of homogeneous perfectly plastic materials in this variational setting was established in
[
7
]
, with further extensions to the case of heterogeneous materials in
[
15
]
and
[
28
]
.
Moreover, in
[
2
]
it was shown that the evolution of perfectly plastic models can be obtained as the limit from the classical elasto-plastic model with hardening, if the hardening approaches zero.
Two-scale homogenization of linearized elasto-plasticity with hardening was studied in
[
26
]
and
[
27
]
, while
[
14
]
treated the case of perfect plasticity with the use of a stress-strain approach, which proved to be particularly well-suited for addressing the convergence of quasistatic evolutions in composite perfectly-plastic materials.

The structure of the paper is as follows.

Section

2
introduces our notation and recalls some preliminary results on convex functions of measures, disintegration of Radon measures, as well as some auxiliary claims about
stress tensors.
In
Section

3
we formulate the three-dimensional elasto-plastic plate model with hardening, perform the rescaling, and study the quasistatic evolution of the thin plate.
We justify the model via evolutionary
\Gamma
–convergence, i.e. we establish compactness and lower semicontinuity results, and construct an appropriate recovery sequence.
The section concludes with a characterization of the limiting quasistatic evolution and admissible stress configurations, including a formulation in terms of in-plane and out-of-plane displacements and plastic strains.
This derived model of a heterogeous elasto-plastic plate with hardening is then used as a starting point for the analysis in

Section

4
, which is devoted to the simultaneous homogenization and vanishing of hardening effects in materials with periodic microstructure.
We make use of two-scale convergence and unfolding techniques of symmetrized gradients of
BD
function and Hessians of
BH
functions.
Furthermore, we discuss semicontinuity of the dissipation functional, duality between stress and plastic strain, as well as a two-scale version of the principle of maximum plastic work in the absence of any assumption on the ordering of the phases.
Eventually, at the end of the section we prove the main result of the paper (
Theorem

4.38
), where we show the convergence of
quasistatic evolutions to the limiting quasistatic evolution.

2.1.
Convex functions of measures

Let
U
be an open set of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
X
a finite-dimensional vector space, and denote by
\mathcal{M}_{b}(U;X)
the set of finite
X
-valued Radon measures on the set
U
.
For every
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{b}(U;X)
let
\frac{d\mu}{d|\mu|}
be the Radon-Nikodym derivative of
\mu
with respect to its variation
|\mu|
.
Let
H:X\to[0,+\infty)
be a convex and positively one-homogeneous function such that

(2.1)

r|\xi|\leq H(\xi)\leq R|\xi|\quad\text{for every}\,\xi\in X,

where
r
and
R
are two constants, with
0<r\leq R
.

Using the theory of convex functions of measures (see
[
17
]
and
[
12
]
) it is possible to define a nonnegative Radon measure
H(\mu)\in\mathcal{M}_{b}^{+}(U)
as

H(\mu)(A):=\int_{A}H\left(\frac{d\mu}{d|\mu|}\right)\,d|\mu|,

for every Borel set
A\subset U
, as well as an associated
functional
\mathcal{H}:\mathcal{M}_{b}(U;X)\to[0,+\infty)
given by

\mathcal{H}(\mu):=H(\mu)(U)=\int_{U}H\left(\frac{d\mu}{d|\mu|}\right)\,d|\mu|

and being lower semicontinuous on
\mathcal{M}_{b}(U;X)
with respect to weak* convergence, cf.
[
1
, Theorem 2.38]
).

Let
a,\,b\in[0,T]
with
a\leq b
.
The
total variation
of a function
\mu:[0,T]\to\mathcal{M}_{b}(U;X)
on
[a,b]
is defined as

\mathcal{V}(\mu;a,b):=\sup\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\left\|\mu(t_{i+1})-\mu(t_{i})\right\|_{\mathcal{M}_{b}(U;X)}:a=t_{1}<t_{2}<\ldots<t_{n}=b,\ n\in\mathbb{N}\right\}.

Analogously, the
\mathcal{H}
-variation
of a function
\mu:[0,T]\to\mathcal{M}_{b}(U;X)
on
[a,b]
is given by

\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{H}}(\mu;a,b):=\sup\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\mathcal{H}\left(\mu(t_{i+1})-\mu(t_{i})\right):a=t_{1}<t_{2}<\ldots<t_{n}=b,\ n\in\mathbb{N}\right\}.

From (
2.1
) it follows that

(2.2)

r\mathcal{V}(\mu;a,b)\leq\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{H}}(\mu;a,b)\leq R\mathcal{V}(\mu;a,b).

2.2.
Generalized products

Let
S
and
T
be measurable spaces and let
\mu
be a measure on
S
.
Given a measurable function
f:S\to T
, we denote by
f_{\#}\mu
the
push-forward
of
\mu
under the map
f
, defined by

f_{\#}\mu(B):=\mu\left(f^{-1}(B)\right),\quad\text{ for every measurable set $B\subseteq T$}.

In particular, for any measurable function
g:T\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
we have

\int_{S}g\circ f\,d\mu=\int_{T}g\,d(f_{\#}\mu).

Note that in the previous formula
S=f^{-1}(T)
.

Let
S_{1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N_{1}}
,
S_{2}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N_{2}}
, for some
N_{1},N_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
, be open sets, and let
\eta\in\mathcal{M}_{b}^{+}(S_{1})
.
We say that a function
x_{1}\in S_{1}\mapsto\mu_{x_{1}}\in\mathcal{M}_{b}(S_{2};\mathbb{R}^{M})
is
\eta
-measurable if
x_{1}\in S_{1}\mapsto\mu_{x_{1}}(B)
is
\eta
-measurable for every Borel set
B\subseteq S_{2}
.

Given a
\eta
-measurable function
x_{1}\mapsto\mu_{x_{1}}
such that
\int_{S_{1}}|\mu_{x_{1}}|\,d\eta<+\infty
, then the
generalized product

\eta\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{gen.}}}{{\otimes}}\mu_{x_{1}}
satisfies
\eta\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{gen.}}}{{\otimes}}\mu_{x_{1}}\in\mathcal{M}_{b}(S_{1}\times S_{2};\mathbb{R}^{M})
and is such that

\langle\eta\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{gen.}}}{{\otimes}}\mu_{x_{1}},\varphi\rangle:=\int_{S_{1}}\left(\int_{S_{2}}\varphi(x_{1},x_{2})\,d\mu_{x_{1}}(x_{2})\right)\,d\eta(x_{1}),

for every bounded Borel function
\varphi:S_{1}\times S_{2}\to\mathbb{R}
.

2.3.
Traces of stress tensors

In this last subsection we collect some properties of classes of maps which will include our elasto-plastic stress tensors.

We suppose here that
U
is an open bounded set of class
C^{2}
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
If
\sigma\in L^{2}(U;\mathbb{M}^{N\times N}_{\sym})
and
{\rm div}\sigma\in L^{2}(U;\mathbb{R}^{N})
, then we can define a distribution
[\sigma\nu]
on
\partial{U}
by

(2.3)

[\sigma\nu](\psi):=\int_{U}\psi\cdot{\rm div}\sigma\,dx+\int_{U}\sigma:E\psi\,dx,

for every
\psi\in H^{1}(U;\mathbb{R}^{N})
.
It follows that
[\sigma\nu]\in H^{-1/2}(\partial{U};\mathbb{R}^{N})
(see, e.g.,
[
30
, Chapter 1, Theorem 1.2]
).
If, in addition,
\sigma\in L^{\infty}(U;\mathbb{M}^{N\times N}_{\sym})
and
{\rm div}\sigma\in L^{N}(U;\mathbb{R}^{N})
, then (
2.3
) holds for
\psi\in W^{1,1}(U;\mathbb{R}^{N})
.
By Gagliardo’s extension theorem
[
16
, Theorem 1.II]
, in this case we have
[\sigma\nu]\in L^{\infty}(\partial{U};\mathbb{R}^{N})
, and

[\sigma_{k}\nu]\xrightharpoonup{*}[\sigma\nu]\quad\text{weakly* in $L^{\infty}(\partial{U};\mathbb{R}^{N})$},

whenever
\sigma_{k}\xrightharpoonup{*}\sigma
weakly* in
L^{\infty}(U;\mathbb{M}^{N\times N}_{\sym})
and
{\rm div}\sigma_{k}\,\xrightharpoonup{}\,{\rm div}\sigma
weakly in
L^{N}(U;\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

We will consider the normal and tangential parts of
[\sigma\nu]
, defined by

[\sigma\nu]_{\nu}:=([\sigma\nu]\cdot\nu)\nu,\quad[\sigma\nu]_{\nu}^{\perp}:=[\sigma\nu]-([\sigma\nu]\cdot\nu)\nu.

Since
\nu\in C^{1}(\partial{U};\mathbb{R}^{N})
, we have that
[\sigma\nu]_{\nu},\,[\sigma\nu]_{\nu}^{\perp}\in H^{-1/2}(\partial{U};\mathbb{R}^{N})
. If, in addition,
\sigma_{\dev}\in L^{\infty}(U;\mathbb{M}^{N\times N}_{\dev})
, then it was proved in
[
19
, Lemma 2.4]
that
[\sigma\nu]_{\nu}^{\perp}\in L^{\infty}(\partial{U};\mathbb{R}^{N})
and

\|[\sigma\nu]_{\nu}^{\perp}\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial{U};\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\|\sigma_{\dev}\|_{L^{\infty}(U;\mathbb{M}^{N\times N}_{\dev})}.

More generally, if
U
has Lipschitz boundary and is such that there exists a compact set
S\subset\partial{U}
with
\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(S)=0
such that
\partial{U}\setminus S
is a
C^{2}
-hypersurface, then arguing as in
[
15
, Section 1.2]
we can uniquely determine
[\sigma\nu]_{\nu}^{\perp}
as an element of
L^{\infty}(\partial{U};\mathbb{R}^{N})
through any approximating sequence
\{\sigma_{n}\}\subset C^{\infty}(\overline{U};\mathbb{M}^{N\times N}_{\sym})
such that

\displaystyle\sigma_{n}\to\sigma\quad\text{strongly in }L^{2}(U;\mathbb{M}^{N\times N}_{\sym}),

\displaystyle{\rm div}\sigma_{n}\to{\rm div}\sigma\quad\text{strongly in }L^{2}(U;\mathbb{R}^{N}),

\displaystyle\|(\sigma_{n})_{\dev}\|_{L^{\infty}(U;\mathbb{M}^{N\times N}_{\dev})}\leq\|\sigma_{\dev}\|_{L^{\infty}(U;\mathbb{M}^{N\times N}_{\dev})}.

3.
Dimension reduction of heterogeneous elasto-plastic plate model with hardening

3.1.
Setup

Let
\omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a planar, bounded Lipschitz domain and
I=\left(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right)\subset\mathbb{R}
, an open interval. Given a small positive number
h>0
, we define the three-dimensional thin plate

\Omega^{h}:=\omega\times(hI),

with the boundary partitioned into the lateral surface
\Gamma^{h}_{0}:=\partial\omega\times(hI)
and the transverse boundary
\Gamma^{h}:=\omega\times\partial(hI)
.
We will denote the Dirichlet boundary of
\omega
with
\gamma_{D}\subset\partial\omega
, as well as
\Gamma_{D}^{h}=\gamma_{D}\times(hI)
. The assumption is that
\gamma_{D}
is of positive measure.

The elasticity tensor.

Let
\mathbb{C}\in L^{\infty}(\omega;\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{M}^{3\times 3}_{\sym}))
be the
elasticity tensor
, considered as a map from
\omega
taking values in the set of symmetric positive definite linear operators,
\mathbb{C}(x^{\prime}):\mathbb{M}^{3\times 3}_{\sym}\to\mathbb{M}^{3\times 3}_{\sym}
, defined as

\mathbb{C}(x^{\prime})\xi:=\mathbb{C}_{\dev}(x^{\prime})\,\xi_{\dev}+\left(k(x^{\prime})\,{\rm tr}{\xi}\right)\,I_{3\times 3}\quad\text{ for a.e. }x^{\prime}\in\omega\text{ and }\xi\in\mathbb{M}^{3\times 3}_{\sym},

where
\mathbb{C}_{\dev}\in L^{\infty}(\omega;\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{M}^{3\times 3}_{\dev}))
,
k\in L^{\infty}(\omega)
.
Additionally we assume that there exist two constants
r_{\mathbb{C}}
and
R_{\mathbb{C}}
, with
0<r_{\mathbb{C}}\leq R_{\mathbb{C}}
, such that for a.e.
x^{\prime}\in\omega
we have:

\displaystyle r_{\mathbb{C}}|\xi|^{2}\leq\mathbb{C}_{\dev}(x^{\prime})\xi:\xi\leq R_{\mathbb{C}}|\xi|^{2}\quad\text{ for every }\xi\in\mathbb{M}^{3\times 3}_{\dev},

\displaystyle r_{\mathbb{C}}\leq k(x^{\prime})\leq R_{\mathbb{C}}.

The hardening tensors.

Let
\mathbb{H}_{\rm kin}\in L^{\infty}(\omega;\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{M}^{3\times 3}_{\dev}))
be the
kinematic hardening tensor
and
H_{\rm iso}\in L^{\infty}(\omega)
be the
isotropic hardening tensor
.
We assume that there exist constants
r_{\mathbb{H}_{\rm kin}}
,
r_{H_{\rm iso}}
,
R_{\mathbb{H}_{\rm kin}}
and
R_{H_{\rm iso}}
, with
0\leq r_{\mathbb{H}_{\rm kin}}\leq R_{\mathbb{H}_{\rm kin}}
,
0\leq r_{H_{\rm iso}}\leq R_{H_{\rm iso}}
and
r_{\mathbb{H}_{\rm kin}}+r_{H_{\rm iso}}>0
, such that for a.e.
x^{\prime}\in\omega
we have:

\displaystyle r_{\mathbb{H}_{\rm kin}}|\xi|^{2}\leq\mathbb{H}_{\rm kin}(x^{\prime})\xi:\xi\leq R_{\mathbb{H}_{\rm kin}}|\xi|^{2}\quad\text{ for every }\xi\in\mathbb{M}^{3\times 3}_{\dev},

\displaystyle r_{H_{\rm iso}}\leq H_{\rm iso}(x^{\prime})\leq R_{H_{\rm iso}}.

Remark 3.1
.

The assumptions above allow for the cases where
\mathbb{H}_{\rm kin}\equiv 0
and
H_{\rm iso}>0
(which corresponds to pure isotropic hardening) or
\mathbb{H}_{\rm kin}>0
and
H_{\rm iso}\equiv 0
(which corresponds to pure kinematic hardening).
In the latter case the isotropic hardening internal state variable
\alpha
(see below) can be removed from the set of unknowns, but it will not affect the analysis presented in the paper.

The dissipation potential.

We assume that there exist convex compact sets
K(x^{\prime})\in\mathbb{M}^{3\times 3}_{\dev}
for each point
x^{\prime}\in\omega
.
We further assume that there exist two constants
r_{K}
and
R_{K}
, with
0<r_{K}\leq R_{K}
, such that for every
x^{\prime}

\{\xi\in\mathbb{M}^{3\times 3}_{\dev}:|\xi|\leq r_{K}\}\subseteq K(x^{\prime})\subseteq\{\xi\in\mathbb{M}^{3\times 3}_{\dev}:|\xi|\leq R_{K}\}.

For
x^{\prime}\in\omega
, we define the dissipation potential
\mathbf{R}:\omega\times\mathbb{M}^{3\times 3}_{\dev}\to[0,+\infty]
to be

\mathbf{R}(x^{\prime},p):=\sup\limits_{\tau\in K(x^{\prime})}\tau:p.

It follows that, for
x^{\prime}\in\omega
,
\mathbf{R}(x^{\prime},\cdot)
is convex, positively 1-homogeneous, and satisfies

(3.1)

r_{K}|p|\leq\mathbf{R}(x^{\prime},p)\leq R_{K}|p|\quad\text{for every}\,p\in\mathbb{M}^{3\times 3}_{\dev}.

Finally, we define the generalized dissipation potential
\widehat{\mathbf{R}}:\omega\times\mathbb{M}^{3\times 3}_{\dev}\times\mathbb{R}\to[0,+\infty]
as

(3.2)

\widehat{\mathbf{R}}(x^{\prime},p,\alpha)=\begin{cases}\mathbf{R}(x^{\prime},p)&\text{if $\mathbf{R}(x^{\prime},p)\leq\alpha$},\\
+\infty&\text{else}.\end{cases}

This potential encompasses the cases of both von Mises and Tresca flow rule (see, e.g.,
[
18
, Section 4]
).

Remark 3.2
.

We point out that
\widehat{\mathbf{R}}
is a Borel, lower semicontinuous function on
\omega\times\mathbb{M}^{3\times 3}_{\dev}\times\mathbb{R}
.
Furthermore, for each
x^{\prime}\in\omega
, the function
(p,\alpha)\mapsto\widehat{\mathbf{R}}(x^{\prime},p,\alpha)
is convex and positively 1-homogeneous. The latter conditions mean
\widehat{\mathbf{R}}(x^{\prime},\lambda p,\lambda\alpha)=\lambda\widehat{\mathbf{R}}(x^{\prime},p,\alpha)
for all
\lambda\geq 0
and
(p,\alpha)\in\mathbb{M}^{3\times 3}_{\dev}\times\mathbb{R}
.
For a fixed
x^{\prime}\in\omega
, the corresponding generalized elastic domain
\widehat{K}(x^{\prime})\subset\mathbb{M}^{3\times 3}_{\dev}\times\mathbb{R}
is defined via

\widehat{K}(x^{\prime}):=\partial\widehat{\mathbf{R}}(x^{\prime},0),

which is the subdifferential of
\widehat{\mathbf{R}}(x^{\prime},\cdot)
at
0
.

Admissible tuples and energy.

On
\Gamma_{D}^{h}
we prescribe a Dirichlet boundary datum being the trace of a map
w^{h}\in H^{1}(\Omega^{h};\mathbb{R}^{3})
of the following form:

(3.3)

w^{h}(z):=\left(\overline{w}_{1}(z^{\prime})-\frac{z_{3}}{h}\partial_{1}\overline{w}_{3}(z^{\prime}),\,\overline{w}_{2}(z^{\prime})-\frac{z_{3}}{h}\partial_{2}\overline{w}_{3}(z^{\prime}),\,\frac{1}{h}\overline{w}_{3}(z^{\prime})\right)\,\text{ for a.e. }z=(z^{\prime},z_{3})\in\Omega^{h},

where
\overline{w}_{\alpha}\in H^{1}(\omega)
,
\alpha=1,2
, and
\overline{w}_{3}\in H^{2}(\omega)
.

The elasto-plastic properties of
\Omega^{h}
are described in terms of the linearized strain tensor
{\bf E}u=\frac{1}{2}\left(\nabla u+\nabla u^{T}\right)\in\mathbb{M}^{3\times 3}_{\sym}
, the plastic strain tensor
p\in\mathbb{M}^{3\times 3}_{\dev}
,
and the internal isotropic hardening variable
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, via the stored energy density
{\mathbb{W}}:\omega\times\mathbb{M}^{3\times 3}_{\sym}\times\mathbb{M}^{3\times 3}_{\dev}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
, which is assumed to be given in the following form

(3.4)

{\mathbb{W}}(x^{\prime},{\bf E},p,\alpha):=\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{C}(x^{\prime})({\bf E}-p):({\bf E}-p)+\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{H}_{\rm kin}(x^{\prime})p:p+\frac{1}{2}H_{\rm iso}(x^{\prime})\alpha\cdot\alpha.

Given time-dependent volume and surface loadings
f^{h}(t,\cdot)
and
g^{h}(t,\cdot)
, the full elasto-plastic problem can be written in the form

(3.5)

\begin{split}-{\rm div}(\partial_{{\bf E}}{\mathbb{W}}(\cdot,{\bf E}u,p,\alpha))=f^{h}(t,\cdot)\quad&\text{in}\,\,\Omega^{h},\\
0\in\partial\widehat{\mathbf{R}}(\cdot,\dot{p},\dot{\alpha})+\partial_{(p,\alpha)}{\mathbb{W}}(\cdot,{\bf E}u,p,\alpha)\quad&\text{in}\,\,\Omega^{h},\\
u(t,\cdot)=w^{h}\quad&\text{on}\,\,\Gamma_{D}^{h},\\
\partial_{{\bf E}}{\mathbb{W}}(\cdot,{\bf E}u,p,\alpha)\nu=g^{h}(t,\cdot)\quad&\text{on}\,\,\partial\Omega^{h}\setminus\Gamma_{D}^{h},\end{split}

where
\nu
denotes the outer normal vector on
\partial\Omega^{h}
. Here
\sigma=\partial_{{\bf E}}{\mathbb{W}}\in\mathbb{M}^{3\times 3}_{\sym}
denotes the stress, while
(\chi_{\rm kin},\chi_{\rm iso})=-\partial_{(p,\alpha)}{\mathbb{W}}\in\mathbb{M}^{3\times 3}_{\dev}\times\mathbb{R}
contains the deviator of the stress as well as any plastic back stresses.

The
set of admissible displacements, strains and internal variables
for the boundary datum
w^{h}
of form (
3.3
) is denoted by

\mathcal{A}^{\rm hard}(\Omega^{h},w^{h})
and is defined as the class of all tuples

(u,e,p,\alpha)\in H^{1}(\Omega^{h})\times L^{2}(\Omega^{h};\mathbb{M}^{3\times 3}_{\sym})\times L^{2}(\Omega^{h};\mathbb{M}^{3\times 3}_{\dev})\times L^{2}(\Omega^{h})
satisfying

\displaystyle{\bf E}u=e+p\quad\text{ in }\Omega^{h},

\displaystyle u=w^{h}\quad\mathcal{H}^{2}\text{-a.e.}\text{ on }\Gamma_{D}^{h}

\displaystyle\mathbf{R}(x^{\prime},p(x))\leq\alpha(x)\quad\text{a.e.}\text{ in }\Omega^{h}.

The function
u
represents the
displacement
of the plate,
e
and
p
are called the
elastic
and
plastic strain
, respectively, while
\alpha
is referred to as the
isotropic hardening internal state variable
.

3.2.
The rescaled problem

As usual in dimension reduction problems, it is convenient to perform a change of variables in such a way to rewrite the system on a fixed domain independent of
h
.
To this purpose,
we consider the open interval
I=\left(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right)
and set

\Omega\,:=\,\omega\times I,\qquad\Gamma_{D}\,:=\,\partial\omega\times I.

We consider the change of variables
\psi_{h}:\overline{\Omega}\to\overline{\Omega^{h}}
, defined as

(3.6)

\psi_{h}(x^{\prime},x_{3}):=(x^{\prime},hx_{3})\quad\text{for every}\,(x^{\prime},x_{3})\in\overline{\Omega},

and the linear operator
\Lambda_{h}:\mathbb{M}_{\sym}^{3\times 3}\to\mathbb{M}_{\sym}^{3\times 3}
given by

(3.7)

\Lambda_{h}\xi:=\begin{pmatrix}\xi_{11}&\xi_{12}&\frac{1}{h}\xi_{13}\vskip 2.84544pt\\
\xi_{21}&\xi_{22}&\frac{1}{h}\xi_{23}\vskip 2.84544pt\\
\frac{1}{h}\xi_{31}&\frac{1}{h}\xi_{32}&\frac{1}{h^{2}}\xi_{33}\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{for every }\xi\in\mathbb{M}^{3\times 3}_{\sym}.

To any tuple
(\upsilon,\eta,q,\beta)\in\mathcal{A}^{\rm hard}(\Omega^{h},w^{h})
, we associate a rescaled tuple
(u^{h},e^{h},p^{h},\alpha^{h})\in H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})\times L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{3\times 3}_{\sym})\times L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{3\times 3}_{\sym})\times L^{2}(\Omega)
defined as follows:

u^{h}:=(\upsilon_{1},\upsilon_{2},h\upsilon_{3})\circ\psi_{h},\qquad e^{h}:=\Lambda_{h}^{-1}\eta\circ\psi_{h},\qquad p^{h}:=\Lambda_{h}^{-1}q\circ\psi_{h},\qquad\alpha^{h}:=\beta\circ\psi_{h}.

We also introduce the scaled Dirichlet boundary datum
w\in H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})
, given by

w(x):=(\bar{w}_{1}(x^{\prime})-{x_{3}}\partial_{1}w_{3}(x^{\prime}),\bar{w}_{2}(x^{\prime})-x_{3}\partial_{2}w_{3}(x^{\prime}),w_{3}(x^{\prime}))\quad\text{for a.e.\ }x\in\Omega.

By the definition of the class
\mathcal{A}^{\rm hard}(\Omega^{h},w^{h})
it follows that the scaled tuple
(u^{h},e^{h},p^{h},\alpha^{h})
satisfies the following

(3.8)

\displaystyle{\bf E}u^{h}=e^{h}+p^{h}\quad\text{ in }\Omega,

(3.9)

\displaystyle u^{h}=w\quad\mathcal{H}^{2}\text{-a.e.}\text{ on }\Gamma_{D},

(3.10)

\displaystyle\mathbf{R}(x^{\prime},\Lambda_{h}p^{h}(x))\leq\alpha^{h}(x)\quad\text{a.e.}\text{ in }\Omega,

(3.11)

\displaystyle p^{h}_{11}+p^{h}_{22}+\frac{1}{h^{2}}p^{h}_{33}=0\quad\text{ in }\Omega.

We are thus led to introduce the class
\mathcal{A}_{h}^{\rm hard}(w)
of all tuples
(u^{h},e^{h},p^{h},\alpha)\in H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})\times L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{3\times 3}_{\sym})\times L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{3\times 3}_{\sym})\times L^{2}(\Omega)
satisfying (
3.8
)–(
3.11
), and to
reformulate the system (
3.5
) in an abstract form for the primary variables
(u^{h},e^{h},p^{h},\alpha^{h})
.
We also assume the following scaling of the loadings, namely:

\displaystyle f^{h}(t,\cdot)\circ\psi_{h}=\left(f_{1},f_{2},hf_{3}\right)\quad\text{ in }\Omega,

\displaystyle g^{h}(t,\cdot)\circ\psi_{h}=\left(g_{1},g_{2},hg_{3}\right)\quad\text{ on }(\partial{\omega}\setminus\gamma_{D})\times I,

\displaystyle g^{h}(t,\cdot)\circ\psi_{h}=\left(hg_{1},hg_{2},h^{2}g_{3}\right)\quad\text{ on }\omega\times\partial{I}.

Kirchhoff–Love admissible triples and limit energy.

We consider the set of
Kirchhoff–Love displacements
, defined as

KL(\Omega):=\big\{u\in H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3}):\ ({\bf E}u)_{i3}=0\quad\text{for }i=1,2,3\big\}.

We note that
u\in KL(\Omega)
if and only if
u_{3}\in H^{2}(\omega)
and there exists
\overline{u}\in H^{1}(\omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
such that

(3.12)

u_{\alpha}=\overline{u}_{\alpha}-x_{3}\partial_{x_{\alpha}}u_{3},\quad\alpha=1,2.

In particular, if
u\in KL(\Omega)
, then

(3.13)

{\bf E}u=\begin{pmatrix}\begin{matrix}{\bf E}_{x^{\prime}}\overline{u}-x_{3}{\bf D}^{2}_{x^{\prime}}u_{3}\end{matrix}&\begin{matrix}0\\
0\end{matrix}\\
\begin{matrix}0&0\end{matrix}&0\end{pmatrix}.

If, in addition,
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})
for some
1\leq p\leq\infty
, then
\overline{u}\in W^{1,p}(\omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
and
u_{3}\in W^{2,p}(\omega)
.
We call
\overline{u},u_{3}
the
Kirchhoff–Love components
of
u
.

Since that the space

\big\{\xi\in\mathbb{M}^{3\times 3}_{\sym}:\ \xi_{i3}=0\text{ for }i=1,2,3\big\}

is canonically isomorphic to
\mathbb{M}^{2\times 2}_{\sym}
, given a function
u\in KL(\Omega)
we will usually identify
{\bf E}u
with function in
L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{2\times 2}_{\sym})
.
Furthermore, since
{\bf E}u
is affine in the
x_{3}
variable, we introduce the following definitions.

Definition 3.3
.

For
f\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{2\times 2}_{\sym})
we denote by
\overline{f}
,
\widehat{f}\in L^{2}(\omega;\mathbb{M}^{2\times 2}_{\sym})
and
f^{\perp}\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{2\times 2}_{\sym})
the following components of
f
:

\overline{f}(x^{\prime}):=\int_{I}f(x^{\prime},x_{3})\,dx_{3},\qquad\widehat{f}(x^{\prime}):=12\int_{I}x_{3}f(x^{\prime},x_{3})\,dx_{3}

for a.e.
x^{\prime}\in\omega
, and

f^{\perp}(x):=f(x)-\overline{f}(x^{\prime})-x_{3}\widehat{f}(x^{\prime})

for a.e.
x\in\Omega
.
We name
\overline{f}
the
zero-th order moment
of
f
and
\widehat{f}
the
first order moment
of
f
.

The coefficient in the definition of
\widehat{f}
is chosen from the computation
\int_{I}x_{3}^{2}\,dx_{3}=\frac{1}{12}
.
It ensures that if
f
is of the form
f(x)=x_{3}g(x^{\prime})
, for some
g\in L^{2}(\omega;\mathbb{M}^{2\times 2}_{\sym})
, then

\widehat{f}=g
.

Analogously, we have the following definition of zero-th and first order moments of measures.

Definition 3.4
.

For
\mu\in M_{b}(\Omega\cup\Gamma_{D};\mathbb{M}^{2\times 2}_{\sym})
we define
\bar{\mu}
,
\hat{\mu}\in M_{b}(\omega\cup\gamma_{D};\mathbb{M}^{2\times 2}_{\sym})
and
\mu^{\perp}\in M_{b}(\Omega\cup\Gamma_{D};\mathbb{M}^{2\times 2}_{\sym})
as follows:

\int_{\omega\cup\gamma_{D}}\varphi:d\bar{\mu}:=\int_{\Omega\cup\Gamma_{D}}\varphi:d\mu,\qquad\int_{\omega\cup\gamma_{D}}\varphi:d\hat{\mu}:=12\int_{\Omega\cup\Gamma_{D}}x_{3}\varphi:d\mu

for every
\varphi\in C_{0}(\omega\cup\gamma_{D};\mathbb{M}^{2\times 2}_{\sym})
, and

\mu^{\perp}:=\mu-\bar{\mu}\otimes\mathcal{L}^{1}_{x_{3}}-\hat{\mu}\otimes x_{3}\mathcal{L}^{1}_{x_{3}},

where
\otimes
is the usual product of measures, and
\mathcal{L}^{1}_{x_{3}}
is the Lebesgue measure restricted to the third component of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.
We call
\bar{\mu}
the
zero-th order moment
of
\mu
and
\hat{\mu}
the
first order moment
of
\mu
.

3.3.
Quasistatic evolution for thin plate

We introduce the class

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{h}^{\rm hard}(w):=\Big\{

\displaystyle(u^{h},e^{h},p^{h},\alpha^{h})\in H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})\times L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{3\times 3}_{\sym})\times L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{3\times 3}_{\sym})\times L^{2}(\Omega):

\displaystyle{\bf E}u^{h}=e^{h}+p^{h}\quad\text{ in }\Omega,

\displaystyle u^{h}=w\quad\mathcal{H}^{2}\text{-a.e.}\text{ on }\Gamma_{D},

\displaystyle\mathbf{R}(x^{\prime},\Lambda_{h}p^{h}(x))\leq\alpha^{h}(x)\quad\text{a.e.}\text{ in }\Omega,

\displaystyle p^{h}_{11}+p^{h}_{22}+\frac{1}{h^{2}}p^{h}_{33}=0\quad\text{ in }\Omega\Big\}

To express the energy balance it is useful to introduce the quadratic forms

(3.14)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}^{\rm el}_{h}(e^{h})

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{C}(x^{\prime})\Lambda_{h}e^{h}(x):\Lambda_{h}e^{h}(x)\,dx,

(3.15)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}^{\rm hard}_{h}(p^{h},\alpha^{h})

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{H}_{\rm kin}(x^{\prime})\Lambda_{h}p^{h}(x):\Lambda_{h}p^{h}(x)\,dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}H_{\rm iso}(x^{\prime})\alpha^{h}(x)\cdot\alpha^{h}(x)\,dx,

the total load

(3.16)

\big\langle\ell(t),u^{h}\big\rangle:=\int_{\Omega}f(t,x)\cdot u^{h}(x)\,dx+\int_{\partial{\Omega}\setminus\Gamma_{D}}g(t,x)\cdot u^{h}(x)\,d\mathcal{H}^{2},

and the dissipation functional

(3.17)

\mathcal{R}_{h}(p^{h},\alpha^{h}):=\int_{\Omega}\widehat{\mathbf{R}}(x^{\prime},\Lambda_{h}p^{h}(x),\alpha^{h}(x))\,dx,

defined for
u^{h}\in H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})
,
e^{h},p^{h}\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{3\times 3}_{\sym})
and
\alpha^{h}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
.

We note that, considering definition (
3.2
), the condition
\mathbf{R}(x^{\prime},\Lambda_{h}p^{h}(x))\leq\alpha^{h}(x)
a.e. in
\Omega
implies that, in fact,
\mathcal{R}_{h}(p^{h},\alpha^{h})=\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{R}(x^{\prime},\Lambda_{h}p^{h}(x))\,dx
.

The
\mathcal{R}_{h}
-variation of a map
(p^{h},\alpha^{h}):[0,T]\to L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{3\times 3}_{\sym})\times L^{2}(\Omega)
on
[a,b]\subset[0,T]
is defined as

\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{R}_{h}}(p^{h},\alpha^{h};a,b):=\sup\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{R}_{h}\!\left(p^{h}(t_{i+1})-p^{h}(t_{i}),\alpha^{h}(t_{i+1})-\alpha^{h}(t_{i})\right):a=t_{1}<t_{2}<\ldots<t_{n}=b,\ n\in\mathbb{N}\right\}.

For every
t\in[0,T]
we prescribe a boundary datum
w(t)\in KL(\Omega)
and assume that the map
t\mapsto w(t)
is absolutely continuous from
[0,T]
into
H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})
.

Definition 3.5
.

Let
h>0
.
An
h
-quasistatic evolution
for the boundary datum
w(t)
is a function
t\mapsto(u^{h}(t),e^{h}(t),p^{h}(t),\alpha^{h}(t))
from
[0,T]
into
H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})\times L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{3\times 3}_{\sym})\times L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{3\times 3}_{\sym})\times L^{2}(\Omega)
which satisfies the following conditions:

(qs1)
h

for every
t\in[0,T]
we have
(u^{h}(t),e^{h}(t),p^{h}(t),\alpha^{h}(t))\in\mathcal{A}_{h}^{\rm hard}(w(t))
and

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}^{\rm el}_{h}(e^{h}(t))+\mathcal{Q}^{\rm hard}_{h}(p^{h}(t),\alpha^{h}(t))-\big\langle\ell(t),u^{h}(t)\big\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{Q}^{\rm el}_{h}(\eta)+\mathcal{Q}^{\rm hard}_{h}(\pi,\beta)+\mathcal{R}_{h}(\pi-p^{h}(t),\beta-\alpha^{h}(t))-\big\langle\ell(t),\upsilon\big\rangle,

for every
(\upsilon,\eta,\pi,\beta)\in\mathcal{A}_{h}^{\rm hard}(w(t))
.

(qs2)
h

for every
t\in[0,T]

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}^{\rm el}_{h}(e^{h}(t))+\mathcal{Q}^{\rm hard}_{h}(p^{h}(t),\alpha^{h}(t))+\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{R}_{h}}(p^{h},\alpha^{h};0,t)-\big\langle\ell(t),u^{h}(t)\big\rangle

\displaystyle=\mathcal{Q}^{\rm el}_{h}(e^{h}(0))+\mathcal{Q}^{\rm hard}_{h}(p^{h}(0),\alpha^{h}(0))-\big\langle\ell(0),u^{h}(0)\big\rangle

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\left(\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{C}(x^{\prime})\Lambda_{h}e^{h}(s):{\bf E}\dot{w}(s)\,dx-\big\langle\ell(s),\dot{w}(s)\big\rangle-\big\langle\dot{\ell}(s),u^{h}(s)\big\rangle\right)\,ds.

The following existence result of a quasistatic evolution for a general multi-phase material with hardening follows from classical results for the existence of energetic rate-independent systems, given stored energies with quadratic coercivity (see, e.g.
[
23
, Theorem 3.5.2]
).

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
h>0
and let
(u^{h}_{0},e^{h}_{0},p^{h}_{0},\alpha^{h}_{0})\in\mathcal{A}_{h}^{\rm hard}(w(0))
satisfy the global stability condition
(qs1)h
.
Then, there exists an
h
-quasistatic evolution
t\mapsto(u^{h}(t),e^{h}(t),p^{h}(t),\alpha^{h}(t))
for the boundary datum
w(t)
such that
u^{h}(0)=u_{0}
,
e^{h}(0)=e^{h}_{0}
,
p^{h}(0)=p^{h}_{0}
, and
\alpha^{h}(0)=\alpha^{h}_{0}
.

We prove the following lemma, which gives a condition that is equivalent to global stability
(qs1)h
.
Subsequently, we will use it to prove
Lemma

3.8
, which states the continuous dependence of the
h
-quasistatic evolution on the boundary data (under the same loads).

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
w\in KL(\Omega)
.
The tuple
(u^{h},e^{h},p^{h},\alpha^{h})\in\mathcal{A}_{h}^{\rm hard}(w)
is a solution of the minimization problem

(3.18)

\min_{(\upsilon,\eta,\pi,\beta)\in\mathcal{A}_{h}^{\rm hard}(w)}\left\{\mathcal{Q}^{\rm el}_{h}(\eta)+\mathcal{Q}^{\rm hard}_{h}(\pi,\beta)+\mathcal{R}_{h}(\pi-p^{h},\beta-\alpha^{h})-\big\langle\ell(t),\upsilon-u^{h}\big\rangle\right\}

if and only if

(3.19)

-\mathcal{R}_{h}(\pi,\beta)\leq\int_{\Omega}\left(\mathbb{C}\!\left(x^{\prime}\right)\Lambda_{h}e^{h}:\Lambda_{h}\eta+\mathbb{H}_{\rm kin}\!\left(x^{\prime}\right)\Lambda_{h}p^{h}:\Lambda_{h}\pi+H_{\rm iso}\!\left(x^{\prime}\right)\alpha^{h}\cdot\beta\right)\,dx-\big\langle\ell(t),\upsilon\big\rangle

for every
(\upsilon,\eta,\pi,\beta)\in\mathcal{A}_{h}^{\rm hard}(0)
.

Proof.

Let
(u^{h},e^{h},p^{h},\alpha^{h})\in\mathcal{A}_{h}^{\rm hard}(w)
be a solution to (
3.18
) and fix
(\upsilon,\eta,\pi,\beta)\in\mathcal{A}_{h}^{\rm hard}(0)
.
For every
\epsilon>0
the tuples
(u^{h}+\epsilon\upsilon,e^{h}+\epsilon\eta,p^{h}+\epsilon\pi,\alpha^{h}+\epsilon\beta)
belong to
\mathcal{A}_{h}^{\rm hard}(w)
, hence

\mathcal{Q}^{\rm el}_{h}(e^{h}+\epsilon\eta)+\mathcal{Q}^{\rm hard}_{h}(p^{h}+\epsilon\pi,\alpha^{h}+\epsilon\beta)+\mathcal{R}_{h}(\epsilon\pi,\epsilon\beta)-\big\langle\ell(t),\epsilon\upsilon\big\rangle\geq\mathcal{Q}^{\rm el}_{h}(e^{h})+\mathcal{Q}^{\rm hard}_{h}(p^{h},\alpha^{h}).

Using the positive homogeneity of
\mathcal{R}_{h}
and linearity of
\ell
, we obtain

\mathcal{Q}^{\rm el}_{h}(e^{h}+\epsilon\eta)+\mathcal{Q}^{\rm hard}_{h}(p^{h}+\epsilon\pi,\alpha^{h}+\epsilon\beta)+\epsilon\mathcal{R}_{h}(\pi,\beta)-\epsilon\big\langle\ell(t),\upsilon\big\rangle\geq\mathcal{Q}^{\rm el}_{h}(e^{h})+\mathcal{Q}^{\rm hard}_{h}(p^{h},\alpha^{h}).

A simple calculation then gives

\displaystyle\epsilon\int_{\Omega}\left(\mathbb{C}\!\left(x^{\prime}\right)\Lambda_{h}e^{h}:\Lambda_{h}\eta+\mathbb{H}_{\rm kin}\!\left(x^{\prime}\right)\Lambda_{h}p^{h}:\Lambda_{h}\pi+H_{\rm iso}\!\left(x^{\prime}\right)\alpha^{h}\cdot\beta\right)\,dx

\displaystyle+\epsilon^{2}\mathcal{Q}^{\rm el}_{h}(\eta)+\epsilon^{2}\mathcal{Q}^{\rm hard}_{h}(\pi,\beta)+\epsilon\mathcal{R}_{h}(\pi,\beta)-\epsilon\big\langle\ell(t),\upsilon\big\rangle\geq 0.

Dividing by
\epsilon
and sending
\epsilon\to 0
yields (
3.19
).

For the converse implication, let
(u^{h},e^{h},p^{h},\alpha^{h})\in\mathcal{A}_{h}^{\rm hard}(w)
satisfy (
3.19
).
Then for arbitrary tuple
(\upsilon,\eta,\pi,\beta)\in\mathcal{A}_{h}^{\rm hard}(w)
we have

(\upsilon-u^{h},\eta-e^{h},\pi-p^{h},\beta-\alpha^{h})\in\mathcal{A}_{h}^{\rm hard}(0).

Thus

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{h}(\pi-p^{h},\beta-\alpha^{h})

\displaystyle\geq-\int_{\Omega}\left(\mathbb{C}\!\left(x^{\prime}\right)\Lambda_{h}e^{h}:\Lambda_{h}(\eta-e^{h})+\mathbb{H}_{\rm kin}\!\left(x^{\prime}\right)\Lambda_{h}p^{h}:\Lambda_{h}(\pi-p^{h})+H_{\rm iso}\!\left(x^{\prime}\right)\alpha^{h}\cdot(\beta-\alpha^{h})\right)\,dx

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+\big\langle\ell(t),\upsilon-u^{h}\big\rangle

\displaystyle=\mathcal{Q}^{\rm el}_{h}(e^{h})+\mathcal{Q}^{\rm el}_{h}(\eta-e^{h})-\mathcal{Q}^{\rm el}_{h}(\eta)+\mathcal{Q}^{\rm hard}_{h}(p^{h},\alpha^{h})+\mathcal{Q}^{\rm hard}_{h}(\pi-p^{h},\beta-\alpha^{h})-\mathcal{Q}^{\rm hard}_{h}(\pi,\beta)

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+\big\langle\ell(t),\upsilon-u^{h}\big\rangle,

where the last equality is a straightforward computation.
From the above, we immediately deduce

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}^{\rm el}_{h}(\eta)+\mathcal{Q}^{\rm hard}_{h}(\pi,\beta)+\mathcal{R}_{h}(\pi-p^{h},\beta-\alpha^{h})-\big\langle\ell(t),\upsilon-u^{h}\big\rangle

\displaystyle\geq\mathcal{Q}^{\rm el}_{h}(e^{h})+\mathcal{Q}^{\rm el}_{h}(\eta-e^{h})+\mathcal{Q}^{\rm hard}_{h}(p^{h},\alpha^{h})+\mathcal{Q}^{\rm hard}_{h}(\pi-p^{h},\beta-\alpha^{h})\geq\mathcal{Q}^{\rm el}_{h}(e^{h})+\mathcal{Q}^{\rm hard}_{h}(p^{h},\alpha^{h}),

hence
(u^{h},e^{h},p^{h},\alpha^{h})\in\mathcal{A}_{h}^{\rm hard}(w)
is a solution to (
3.18
).
∎

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
w_{1},w_{2}\in KL(\Omega)
, and for
i=1,2
let
(u^{h}_{i},e^{h}_{i},p^{h}_{i},\alpha^{h}_{i})\in\mathcal{A}_{h}^{\rm hard}(w_{i})
be a solution of the minimization problem

\min_{(\upsilon,\eta,\pi,\beta)\in\mathcal{A}_{h}^{\rm hard}(w_{i})}\left\{\mathcal{Q}^{\rm el}_{h}(\eta)+\mathcal{Q}^{\rm hard}_{h}(\pi,\beta)+\mathcal{R}_{h}(\pi-p^{h}_{i},\beta-\alpha^{h}_{i})-\big\langle\ell(t),\upsilon-u^{h}_{i}\big\rangle\right\}

Then there exists a positive constant
C
, depending only on
r_{\mathbb{C}}
,
r_{\mathbb{H}_{\rm kin}}
,
r_{H_{\rm iso}}
,
R_{\mathbb{C}}
,
R_{K}
, and
\Omega
, such that

\displaystyle\left\|\Lambda_{h}e^{h}_{2}-\Lambda_{h}e^{h}_{1}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{3\times 3}_{\sym})}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|{\bf E}w_{2}-{\bf E}w_{1}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{3\times 3}_{\sym})},

\displaystyle\left\|\Lambda_{h}p^{h}_{2}-\Lambda_{h}p^{h}_{1}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{3\times 3}_{\dev})}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|{\bf E}w_{2}-{\bf E}w_{1}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{3\times 3}_{\sym})},

\displaystyle\left\|\alpha^{h}_{2}-\alpha^{h}_{1}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|{\bf E}w_{2}-{\bf E}w_{1}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{3\times 3}_{\sym})}.

Proof.

Let
v:=u^{h}_{2}-u^{h}_{1}-(w_{2}-w_{1})
,
\eta:=e^{h}_{2}-e^{h}_{1}-({\bf E}w_{2}-{\bf E}w_{1})
,
\pi:=p^{h}_{2}-p^{h}_{1}
,
\beta:=\alpha^{h}_{2}-\alpha^{h}_{1}
.
Since
(\pm\upsilon,\pm\eta,\pm\pi,\pm\beta)\in\mathcal{A}_{h}^{\rm hard}(0)
, by minimality of
(u^{h}_{i},e^{h}_{i},p^{h}_{i},\alpha^{h}_{i})
, for
i=1,2
, and
Lemma

3.7
we obtain respectively,

\displaystyle-\mathcal{R}_{h}(\pi,\beta)\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left(\mathbb{C}\!\left(x^{\prime}\right)\Lambda_{h}e^{h}_{1}:\Lambda_{h}\eta+\mathbb{H}_{\rm kin}\!\left(x^{\prime}\right)\Lambda_{h}p^{h}_{1}:\Lambda_{h}\pi+H_{\rm iso}\!\left(x^{\prime}\right)\alpha^{h}_{1}\cdot\beta\right)\,dx-\big\langle\ell(t),v\big\rangle,

\displaystyle-\mathcal{R}_{h}(-\pi,-\beta)\leq

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}\left(\mathbb{C}\!\left(x^{\prime}\right)\Lambda_{h}e^{h}_{2}:\Lambda_{h}\eta+\mathbb{H}_{\rm kin}\!\left(x^{\prime}\right)\Lambda_{h}p^{h}_{2}:\Lambda_{h}\pi+H_{\rm iso}\!\left(x^{\prime}\right)\alpha^{h}_{2}\cdot\beta\right)\,dx+\big\langle\ell(t),v\big\rangle,

Adding term by term, and using the coercivity of the elastic and hardening tensors, as well as (
3.1
), yields

3.4.2.
Lower semicontinuity of energy functionals

Motivated by the result in the previous section, we introduce the limiting class

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{0}^{\rm hard}(w):=\Big\{

\displaystyle(u,e,p,\alpha)\in KL(\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{3\times 3}_{\sym})\times L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{3\times 3}_{\dev})\times L^{2}(\Omega):

\displaystyle{\bf E}_{x^{\prime}}\bar{u}-x_{3}\nabla^{2}_{x^{\prime}}u_{3}=e^{1,2}+p^{1,2}\quad\text{ in }\Omega,

\displaystyle u=w\quad\mathcal{H}^{2}\text{-a.e.}\text{ on }\Gamma_{D},

\displaystyle\mathbf{R}(x^{\prime},p(x))\leq\alpha(x)\quad\text{a.e.}\text{ in }\Omega\Big\},

where
\bar{u}\in H^{1}(\omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
and
u_{3}\in H^{2}(\omega)
are the Kirchhoff–Love components of
u
.

For
(u,e,p,\alpha)\in\mathcal{A}_{0}^{\rm hard}(w)
we now define

(3.24)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}^{\rm el}_{0}(e)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{C}(x^{\prime})e(x):e(x)\,dx,

(3.25)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}^{\rm hard}_{0}(p,\alpha)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{H}_{\rm kin}(x^{\prime})p(x):p(x)\,dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}H_{\rm iso}(x^{\prime})\alpha(x)\cdot\alpha(x)\,dx,

and

(3.26)

\mathcal{R}_{0}(p,\alpha):=\int_{\Omega}\widehat{\mathbf{R}}(x^{\prime},p(x),\alpha(x))\,dx.

We again note that the condition
\mathbf{R}(x^{\prime},p(x))\leq\alpha(x)
a.e. in
\Omega
implies that
\mathcal{R}_{0}(p,\alpha)=\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{R}(x^{\prime},p(x))\,dx
.

Theorem 3.13
.

Let
w\in KL(\Omega)
and let
(u^{h},e^{h},p^{h},\alpha^{h})\in\mathcal{A}_{h}^{\rm hard}(w)

(3.27)

\displaystyle u^{h}\,\xrightharpoonup{}\,u\quad\text{weakly in $H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})$},

(3.28)

\displaystyle\Lambda_{h}e^{h}\,\xrightharpoonup{}\,e\quad\text{weakly in $L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{3\times 3}_{\sym})$},

(3.29)

\displaystyle\Lambda_{h}p^{h}\,\xrightharpoonup{}\,p\quad\text{weakly in $L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{3\times 3}_{\dev})$},

(3.30)

\displaystyle\alpha^{h}\,\xrightharpoonup{}\,\alpha\quad\text{weakly in $L^{2}(\Omega)$},

with

Then we get

Since

and

3.2

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\left\|w(t)\right\|_{L^{1}(\Gamma_{D};\mathbb{R}^{3})}+\left\|(e^{\varepsilon}(t))^{1,2}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{2\times 2}_{\sym})}+\left\|(p^{\varepsilon}(t))^{1,2}\right\|_{L^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{2\times 2}_{\sym})}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\left\|w(t)\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})}+\left\|e^{\varepsilon}(t)\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{3\times 3}_{\sym})}+\left\|p^{\varepsilon}(t)\right\|_{L^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{3\times 3}_{\dev})}\right).

In view of the assumption
w\in H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})
, from (
4.15
) and the former inequality in (
4.13
) it follows that the sequences
\{u^{\varepsilon}(t)\}
are bounded in

uniformly with respect to

.

4.4.
Two-scale limits and corrector spaces

4.4.1.
Two-scale convergence of measures

We briefly recall some results and definitions from
[
14
, Section 4.1]
.

Definition 4.3
.

Let
\omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be an open set.
Let
\{\mu^{\varepsilon}\}_{h>0}
be a family in
\mathcal{M}_{b}(\omega)
and consider
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{b}(\omega\times\mathcal{Y})
.
We say that

\mu^{\varepsilon}\xrightharpoonup{2-*}\mu\quad\text{two-scale weakly* in }\mathcal{M}_{b}(\omega\times\mathcal{Y}),

if for every
\chi\in C_{0}(\omega\times\mathcal{Y})

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\omega}\chi\left(x^{\prime},\frac{x^{\prime}}{\varepsilon}\right)\,d\mu^{\varepsilon}(x)=\int_{\omega\times\mathcal{Y}}\chi(x^{\prime},y)\,d\mu(x^{\prime},y).

The convergence above is called
two-scale weak* convergence
.

Remark 4.4
.

Notice that the family
\{\mu^{\varepsilon}\}_{h>0}
determines the family of measures
\{\nu^{\varepsilon}\}_{h>0}\subset\mathcal{M}_{b}(\omega\times\mathcal{Y})
obtained by setting

\int_{\omega\times\mathcal{Y}}\chi(x^{\prime},y)\,d\nu^{\varepsilon}(x^{\prime},y)=\int_{\omega}\chi\left(x^{\prime},\frac{x^{\prime}}{\varepsilon}\right)\,d\mu^{\varepsilon}(x)

for every
\chi\in C_{0}(\omega\times\mathcal{Y})
. Thus
\mu
is simply the weak* limit in
\mathcal{M}_{b}(\omega\times\mathcal{Y})
of
\{\nu^{\varepsilon}\}_{h>0}
.

We collect some basic properties of two-scale convergence below:

Proposition 4.5
.

The following statements hold:

(i)

Any sequence that is bounded in
\mathcal{M}_{b}(\omega)
admits a two-scale weakly* convergent subsequence.

(ii)

Let
\mathcal{D}\subset\mathcal{Y}
and assume that

\supp(\mu^{\varepsilon})\subset\omega\cap\mathcal{D}_{\varepsilon}
.
If
\mu^{\varepsilon}\xrightharpoonup{2-*}\mu
two-scale weakly* in
\mathcal{M}_{b}(\omega\times\mathcal{Y})
, then
\supp(\mu)\subset\omega\times\overline{\mathcal{D}}
.

4.4.3.
Two-scale limit results for symmetrized gradients and Hessians

The following structure result for two-scale weak* limits of symmetrized

Proposition 4.9
.

Let
\{v^{h}\}_{h>0}
be a bounded family in
BD(\omega)
such that

v^{h}\xrightharpoonup{*}v\quad\text{weakly* in $BD(\omega)$},

for some

Then there exists

such that

Ev^{h}\xrightharpoonup{2-*}Ev\otimes\mathcal{L}^{2}_{y}+E_{y}\mu\quad\text{two-scale weakly* in $\mathcal{M}_{b}(\omega\times\mathcal{Y};\mathbb{M}^{2\times 2}_{\sym})$}.

Similary, we can prove the following:

Proposition 4.10
.

Let
\{v^{h}\}_{h>0}
be a bounded family in
BH(\omega)
such that

v^{h}\xrightharpoonup{*}v\quad\text{weakly* in $BH(\omega)$},

for some
v\in BH(\omega)
.
Then there exists
\kappa\in\Upsilon_{0}({\omega})
such that

{\bf D}^{2}v^{h}\xrightharpoonup{2-*}{\bf D}^{2}v\otimes\mathcal{L}^{2}_{y}+{\bf D}^{2}_{y}\kappa\quad\text{two-scale weakly* in $\mathcal{M}_{b}(\omega\times\mathcal{Y};\mathbb{M}^{2\times 2}_{\sym})$}.

Proof.

By compactness, the exists
\lambda\in\mathcal{M}_{b}(\omega\times\mathcal{Y};\mathbb{M}^{2\times 2}_{\sym})
such that (up to a subsequence)

{\bf D}^{2}v^{h}\xrightharpoonup{2-*}\lambda\quad\text{two-scale weakly* in $\mathcal{M}_{b}(\omega\times\mathcal{Y};\mathbb{M}^{2\times 2}_{\sym})$}.

Since
v^{h}\to v
strongly in
W^{1,1}(\omega)
, we have

\displaystyle v^{h}\xrightharpoonup{2-*}v(x^{\prime})\,\mathcal{L}^{2}_{x^{\prime}}\otimes\mathcal{L}^{2}_{y}\quad\text{two-scale weakly* in $\mathcal{M}_{b}(\omega\times\mathcal{Y})$},

\displaystyle\partial_{x_{\alpha}}v^{h}\xrightharpoonup{2-*}\partial_{x_{\alpha}}v(x^{\prime})\,\mathcal{L}^{2}_{x^{\prime}}\otimes\mathcal{L}^{2}_{y}\quad\text{two-scale weakly* in $\mathcal{M}_{b}(\omega\times\mathcal{Y})$},\quad\alpha=1,2.

Consider
\chi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\omega\times\mathcal{Y};\mathbb{M}^{2\times 2}_{\sym})
such that
{\rm div}_{y}{\rm div}_{y}\chi(x^{\prime},y)=0
.
Then

\displaystyle\lim_{h\to 0}\int_{\omega}\chi\left(x^{\prime},\frac{x^{\prime}}{{\varepsilon_{h}}}\right):{\bf D}^{2}v^{h}(x^{\prime})\,dx^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\lim_{h\to 0}\int_{\omega}v^{h}(x^{\prime})\,{\rm div}_{x^{\prime}}{\rm div}_{x^{\prime}}\left(\chi\left(x^{\prime},\frac{x^{\prime}}{{\varepsilon_{h}}}\right)\right)\,dx^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\lim_{h\to 0}\sum_{\alpha,\beta=1,2}\int_{\omega}v^{h}(x^{\prime})\,\bigg(\partial_{x_{\alpha}x_{\beta}}\chi_{\alpha\beta}\left(x^{\prime},\frac{x^{\prime}}{{\varepsilon_{h}}}\right)+\frac{1}{{\varepsilon_{h}}}\partial_{y_{\alpha}x_{\beta}}\chi_{\alpha\beta}\left(x^{\prime},\frac{x^{\prime}}{{\varepsilon_{h}}}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 100.00015pt+\frac{1}{{\varepsilon_{h}}}\partial_{x_{\alpha}y_{\beta}}\chi_{\alpha\beta}\left(x^{\prime},\frac{x^{\prime}}{{\varepsilon_{h}}}\right)+\frac{1}{{\varepsilon_{h}}^{2}}\partial_{y_{\alpha}y_{\beta}}\chi_{\alpha\beta}\left(x^{\prime},\frac{x^{\prime}}{{\varepsilon_{h}}}\right)\bigg)\,dx^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\lim_{h\to 0}\sum_{\alpha,\beta=1,2}\int_{\omega}v^{h}(x^{\prime})\,\bigg(\partial_{x_{\alpha}x_{\beta}}\chi_{\alpha\beta}\left(x^{\prime},\frac{x^{\prime}}{{\varepsilon_{h}}}\right)+\frac{2}{{\varepsilon_{h}}}\partial_{y_{\alpha}x_{\beta}}\chi_{\alpha\beta}\left(x^{\prime},\frac{x^{\prime}}{{\varepsilon_{h}}}\right)\bigg)\,dx^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\lim_{h\to 0}\sum_{\alpha,\beta=1,2}\int_{\omega}v^{h}(x^{\prime})\,\partial_{x_{\alpha}x_{\beta}}\chi_{\alpha\beta}\left(x^{\prime},\frac{x^{\prime}}{{\varepsilon_{h}}}\right)\,dx^{\prime}+2\int_{\omega}\bigg(\partial_{x_{\alpha}}\left(v^{h}(x^{\prime})\,\partial_{x_{\beta}}\chi_{\alpha\beta}\left(x^{\prime},\frac{x^{\prime}}{{\varepsilon_{h}}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 100.00015pt-\partial_{x_{\alpha}}v^{h}(x^{\prime})\,\partial_{x_{\beta}}\chi_{\alpha\beta}\left(x^{\prime},\frac{x^{\prime}}{{\varepsilon_{h}}}\right)-v^{h}(x^{\prime})\,\partial_{x_{\alpha}x_{\beta}}\chi_{\alpha\beta}\left(x^{\prime},\frac{x^{\prime}}{{\varepsilon_{h}}}\right)\bigg)\,dx^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\lim_{h\to 0}\sum_{\alpha,\beta=1,2}-\int_{\omega}v^{h}(x^{\prime})\,\partial_{x_{\alpha}x_{\beta}}\chi_{\alpha\beta}\left(x^{\prime},\frac{x^{\prime}}{{\varepsilon_{h}}}\right)\,dx^{\prime}-2\int_{\omega}\partial_{x_{\alpha}}v^{h}(x^{\prime})\,\partial_{x_{\beta}}\chi_{\alpha\beta}\left(x^{\prime},\frac{x^{\prime}}{{\varepsilon_{h}}}\right)\,dx^{\prime},

where in the last equality we used Green’s theorem.
Passing to the limit, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{h\to 0}\int_{\omega}\chi\left(x^{\prime},\frac{x^{\prime}}{{\varepsilon_{h}}}\right):{\bf D}^{2}v^{h}(x^{\prime})\,dx^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha,\beta=1,2}-\int_{\omega\times\mathcal{Y}}v(x^{\prime})\,\partial_{x_{\alpha}x_{\beta}}\chi_{\alpha\beta}\left(x^{\prime},y\right)\,dx^{\prime}dy-2\int_{\omega\times\mathcal{Y}}\partial_{x_{\alpha}}v(x^{\prime})\,\partial_{x_{\beta}}\chi_{\alpha\beta}\left(x^{\prime},y\right)\,dx^{\prime}dy,

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha,\beta=1,2}-\int_{\omega\times\mathcal{Y}}v(x^{\prime})\,\partial_{x_{\alpha}x_{\beta}}\chi_{\alpha\beta}\left(x^{\prime},y\right)\,dx^{\prime}dy

\displaystyle\hskip 40.00006pt-2\int_{\omega\times\mathcal{Y}}\bigg(\partial_{x_{\alpha}}\left(v(x^{\prime})\,\partial_{x_{\beta}}\chi_{\alpha\beta}\left(x^{\prime},y\right)\right)-v(x^{\prime})\,\partial_{x_{\alpha}x_{\beta}}\chi_{\alpha\beta}\left(x^{\prime},y\right)\bigg)\,dx^{\prime}dy,

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha,\beta=1,2}\int_{\omega\times\mathcal{Y}}v(x^{\prime})\,\partial_{x_{\alpha}x_{\beta}}\chi_{\alpha\beta}\left(x^{\prime},y\right)\,dx^{\prime}dy

\displaystyle=\int_{\omega\times\mathcal{Y}}v(x^{\prime})\,{\rm div}_{x^{\prime}}{\rm div}_{x^{\prime}}\left(\chi\left(x^{\prime},y\right)\right)\,dx^{\prime}

(4.17)

\displaystyle=\int_{\omega\times\mathcal{Y}}\chi(x^{\prime},y):d\left({\bf D}^{2}v\otimes\mathcal{L}^{2}_{y}\right).

By a density argument, we infer that

\int_{\omega\times\mathcal{Y}}\chi(x^{\prime},y):d\left(\lambda(x^{\prime},y)-{\bf D}^{2}v\otimes\mathcal{L}^{2}_{y}\right)=0,

for every
\chi\in C_{0}(\omega\times\mathcal{Y};\mathbb{M}^{2\times 2}_{\sym})
with
{\rm div}_{y}{\rm div}_{y}\chi(x^{\prime},y)=0
(in the sense of distributions).
From this and
Proposition

4.8
we conclude that there exists
\kappa\in\Upsilon_{0}({\omega})
such that

\lambda-{\bf D}^{2}v\otimes\mathcal{L}^{2}_{y}={\bf D}^{2}_{y}\kappa.

From this we have the claim.
∎

Lemma 4.13
.

Let
\mathcal{B}\subseteq\mathcal{Y}

(4.18)

Proposition 4.14
.

Let
\omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
be an open set and let
\mathcal{B}\subseteq\mathcal{Y}
be an open set with Lipschitz boundary.
If

\sum_{i\in I_{\varepsilon}(\omega)}\left(\mathcal{L}_{x^{\prime}}^{2}\lfloor{Q_{\varepsilon}^{i}}\right)\otimes{\bf D}^{2}_{y}\hat{u}_{\varepsilon}^{i}\lfloor{(\mathcal{B}\setminus\mathcal{C})},

Proof.

Proposition 4.30

We quickly sketch the proof, and note that this step is independent on the assumption on the ordering of the phases, assumed in that paper, thus the same proof works in our more general case as well.

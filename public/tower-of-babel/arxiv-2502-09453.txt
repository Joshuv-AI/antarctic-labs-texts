RTD-Conjecture and Concept Classes Induced by Graphs
Hans Ulrich Simon

Abstract
It is conjectured in [ 12 ] that the recursive teaching dimension of any finite concept class is upper-bounded by the VC-dimension of this class times a universal constant. In this paper, we confirm this conjecture for two rich families of concept classes where each class is induced by some graph G G . For each G G , we consider the class whose concepts represent stars in G G as well as the class whose concepts represent connected sets in G G . We show that, for concept classes of this kind, the recursive teaching dimension either equals the VC-dimension or is less by 1 1 .

Definition 2.7

(SM-GK-Model

Let

T:{\mathcal{C}}\rightarrow 2^{\mathcal{X}}

3.1
Stars as Concepts

As usual

denotes the maximum

x\in V
,
the set

is shattered by
{\mathcal{C}}_{star}(G)

if
S\subseteq V
is shattered by
{\mathcal{C}}_{star}(G)
, then

(because there must be a concept in

Theorem 3.1

(

]
)

\Delta(G)\leq\mathrm{VCD}({\mathcal{C}}_{star}(G))\leq\Delta(G)+1
.

A similar result holds with RTD at the place of VCD:

Theorem 3.2
.

\Delta(G)\leq\mathrm{RTD}({\mathcal{C}}_{star}(G))\leq\Delta(G)+1
.

Proof.

Pick a vertex
x_{0}\in V
of degree
\Delta(G)
.
Then
{\mathcal{C}}_{0}=\{X\cup\{x_{0}\}:\ X\subseteq N_{G}^{o}(x_{0})\}
is a subclass
of
{\mathcal{C}}_{star}(G)
over domain
V
. The instances outside
N_{G}^{o}(x_{0})

do not distinguish between distinct concepts in
{\mathcal{C}}_{0}
and are therefore
useless for the purpose of teaching. But
{\mathcal{C}}_{0}
restricted to

coincides with the powerset of

.
It follows that

\mathrm{RTD}({\mathcal{C}}_{star}(G))\geq\mathrm{RTD}({\mathcal{C}}_{0})=|N_{G}^{o}(x_{0})|=\deg_{G}(x_{0})=\Delta(G)\kern 5.0pt,

where the first inequality follows from a monotonicity argument.
As for the upper bound on
\mathrm{RTD}({\mathcal{C}}_{star}(G))=\mathrm{PBTD}({\mathcal{C}}_{star}(G))
,
we argue as follows. Assuming subset-preferences, each concept can be
taught by presenting all its elements as positive examples.
Hence
\mathrm{RTD}({\mathcal{C}}_{star}(G))\leq\max\{|C|:C\in{\mathcal{C}}_{star}(G)\}=1+\Delta(G)
,
which concludes the proof.
∎

Let
V_{max}=\{x\in V:\deg_{G}(x)=\Delta(G)\}

of all paths

where

is a path connecting

but excluding the vertex

.
We conclude from this discussion that the set

is indeed
shattered by
{\mathcal{C}}_{con}(G)
so that
\mathrm{VCD}({\mathcal{C}}_{con}(G))\geq k)+1
.

In case of

, we obtain

The proof of the lemma can be accomplished by showing that the third

Set
\ell=\ell(G)
and consider

which are shattered by

. Since these vertices are shattered,

which connect

These paths can be used in the obvious way to build a tree

∎

Definition 3.15

(Opponent of a Connected Set)
.

Suppose that
G=(V,E)
is a graph and
X\in{\mathcal{C}}_{con}(G)\setminus\{\emptyset\}
.
Then
Y\in{\mathcal{C}}_{con}(G)
is called a
maximal opponent of

if

spans a connected component in
\langle V\setminus N_{G}(X)\rangle_{G}
.
Any connected subset of a maximal opponent of
X
is called
an
opponent of
X
.

Lemma 3.16
.

Let
G
be a graph, let
X\in{\mathcal{C}}_{con}(G)\setminus\{\emptyset\}
, and
let
Y
be a maximal opponent of
X
. Then
N_{G}^{o}(Y)\subseteq N_{G}^{o}(X)
.
Moreover, if
X
and
Y
are not part of the same component
of
G
, then
N_{G}^{o}(Y)=\emptyset
.

Proof.

Let
G_{i}=(V_{i},E_{i})
with
i=1,\ldots,r
be the components of
G
.
There are unique indices
j,k\in[r]
such that
X\subseteq V_{j}

and
Y\in V_{k}
. If
k\neq j
then, since
Y
is a maximal opponent,
we must have

so that
N_{G}^{o}(Y)=\emptyset
.
Suppose now that
k=j
.
Since
N_{G}^{o}(X)
separates
X
from

, we have
N_{G}^{o}(Y)\cap X=\emptyset
.
Since

and

, the set

must span a complete connected component

\langle V_{k}\setminus N_{G}(X)\rangle_{G}
. Thus
N_{G}^{o}(Y)\cap(V_{k}\setminus N_{G}(X))=\emptyset
.
Hence
N_{G}^{o}(Y)\subseteq N_{G}^{o}(X)
.
∎

Theorem 3.17
.

For any graph
G
:
\mathrm{RTD}({\mathcal{C}}_{con}(G))\leq\mathrm{VCD}({\mathcal{C}}_{con}(G))
.

Proof.

If
\mathrm{VCD}({\mathcal{C}}_{con}(G))=\ell(G)+1
,
then
\mathrm{RTD}({\mathcal{C}}_{con}(G))\leq\mathrm{VCD}({\mathcal{C}}_{con}(G))
. This is simply
because
\ell(G)+1
, as we know already, is a general upper bound
on
\mathrm{RTD}({\mathcal{C}}_{con}(G))
. We may therefore assume
that
\mathrm{VCD}({\mathcal{C}}_{con}(G))=\ell(G)
. Consider the following commitments
in the preference-based setting:

1.

We have superset-preferences on the concept class
{\mathcal{C}}_{con}(G)
.

2.

If
X\neq Y\in{\mathcal{C}}_{con}(G)
such that, first, neither
X\subset Y

nor
Y\subset X
and, second,
|N_{G}^{o}(X)|>|N_{G}^{o}(Y)|
, then
X

is preferred over
Y
. In other words, among incomparable connected
sets, the ones with the largest open neighborhood are preferred most.

3.

A concept
X\in{\mathcal{C}}_{con}(G)
with
|N_{G}^{o}(X)|<\ell(G)

is taught be presenting the vertices in
N_{G}^{o}(X)
as negative
examples and an arbitrarily chosen vertex
u\in X
as a positive
example.

4.

A concept
X\in{\mathcal{C}}_{con}(G)
with
|N_{G}^{o}(X)|=\ell(G)

is taught be presenting the vertices in
N_{G}^{o}(X)
as negative
examples.

It is obvious that all teaching sets are of size at most
\ell(G)
.
It is furthermore obvious that, thanks to superset-preferences,
a teaching set
N_{G}^{o}(X)
for
X
augmented by a single instance in
X

makes
X
the unique most preferred concept in the version space.
Consider now a concept
X\in{\mathcal{C}}_{con}(G)
with
\ell(G)

vertices in its open neighborhood. Thanks to superset-preferences,
the most preferred concepts in the version space are
X
and its
maximal opponents. Recall from Lemma
3.16
that
the open neighborhood of any maximal opponent
Y
of
X
is a subset
of
N_{G}^{o}(X)
.

Claim:

For each maximal opponent
Y
of
X
, we have
N_{G}^{o}(Y)\subset N_{G}^{o}(X)
.

Proof of the Claim:

Let
G_{i}=(V_{i},E_{i})
with
i=1,\ldots,r
be the components of
G
.
Assume for sake of contradiction that there exists a maximal opponent
Y

of
X
such that
N_{G}^{o}(Y)=N_{G}^{o}(X)
. Then
X
and
Y
must belong
to the same component, say
X,Y\subseteq V_{k}
.
According to Lemma
3.8
there exists
a tree
T_{X}\leq G_{k}
that is a spanning tree of
\langle N_{G_{k}}(X)\rangle_{G_{k}}

and has the
\ell(G)=\ell(G_{k})
vertices of
N_{G}^{o}(X)=N_{G_{k}}^{o}(X)
among
its leaves (so that there can be no other leaves in
T_{X}
). Let
T_{Y}
denote
the corresponding tree for

. Since
N_{G_{k}}^{o}(X)
separates
X

from
Y
, the trees

and
T_{Y}
have no interior vertex in common.
Choose two arbitrary leaves
z_{1}
and

of

. Then the path
from

to

in

of
T_{Y}
nor another leaf of
T_{X}
. We may conclude from

that
\mathrm{VCD}({\mathcal{C}}_{con}(G_{k}))=\ell(G_{k})+1=\ell(G)+1
.
Since
\mathrm{VCD}({\mathcal{C}}_{con}(G))\geq\mathrm{VCD}({\mathcal{C}}_{con}(G_{k}))

.

Since

This implies that none of

and

∎

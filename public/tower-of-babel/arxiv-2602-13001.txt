1.
Introduction

In the 1910s, Ramanujan announced some convergent hypergeometric series related to
1/\pi
without proofs (cf.
[
2
]
), such as

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(8k+1)\frac{(\frac{1}{4})_{k}^{4}}{(1)_{k}^{4}}=\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma(\frac{3}{4})^{2}},

(1.1)

where
(x)_{n}=x(x+1)\cdots(x+n-1)
is the Pochhammer symbol and
\Gamma(x)
is the classical Gamma function. (
1.1
) was finally confirmed by Hardy
[
8
]
.

In 1997, Van Hamme
[
19
]
observed that the truncated forms of original Ramanujan-type series possess good congruence properties. For example, corresponding to (
1.1
), Van Hamme conjectured the following supercongruence: for any prime
p\equiv 1\pmod{4}
,

\sum_{k=0}^{(p-1)/4}(8k+1)\frac{(\frac{1}{4})_{k}^{4}}{(1)_{k}^{4}}\equiv p\frac{\Gamma_{p}(\frac{1}{2})\Gamma_{p}(\frac{1}{4})}{\Gamma_{p}(\frac{3}{4})}\pmod{p^{3}},

(1.2)

where
\Gamma_{p}(x)
denotes the
p
-adic Gamma function introduced by Morita
[
13
]
. Nowadays, we usually refer to (
1.2
) as a
p
-adic analogue of (
1.1
). All of Van Hamme’s observations have now been confirmed by different authors using various techniques (see, e.g.,
[
11
,
12
,
14
,
15
,
18
]
). In particular, Swisher
[
18
]
proved that (
1.2
) holds modulo
p^{4}
and established the following associated supercongruence: for any prime
p\equiv 3\pmod{4}
,

\sum_{k=0}^{(3p-1)/4}(8k+1)\frac{(\frac{1}{4})_{k}^{4}}{(1)_{k}^{4}}\equiv-\frac{3}{2}p^{2}\frac{\Gamma_{p}(\frac{1}{2})\Gamma_{p}(\frac{1}{4})}{\Gamma_{p}(\frac{3}{4})}\pmod{p^{4}}.

(1.3)

Note that
(1/4)_{k}\equiv 0\pmod{p}
for
(p+3)/4\leq k\leq p-1
if
p\equiv 1\pmod{4}
or
(3p+3)/4\leq k\leq p-1
if
p\equiv 3\pmod{4}
. Therefore, both the upper limits of sums on the left-hand side of (
1.2
) and (
1.3
) can be replaced with
p-1
. In 2022, Pan, Tauraso and Wang
[
16
]
established some parametric extensions of (
1.2
) and (
1.3
). For instance, they showed that for any odd prime
p
and
p
-adic integer
\alpha
with
\langle-\alpha\rangle_{p}\geq(p+1)/2
,

\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}\frac{2k+\alpha}{\alpha}\cdot\frac{(\alpha)_{k}^{4}}{(1)_{k}^{4}}\equiv p^{2}\alpha^{\ast}(2\alpha^{\ast}-1)\frac{\Gamma_{p}(1-2\alpha)}{\Gamma_{p}(1+\alpha)\Gamma_{p}(1-\alpha)^{3}}\pmod{p^{4}},

(1.4)

where for any
p
-adic integer
x
,
\langle x\rangle_{p^{r}}
stands for the least nonnegative residue of
x
modulo
p^{r}
and
x^{\ast}=(x+\langle-x\rangle_{p})/p
denotes Dwork’s dash operation (cf.
[
3
]
). Clearly, (
1.3
) is the special case
\alpha=1/4
of (
1.4
).
For other parametric extensions of (
1.2
), we refer the reader to
[
1
,
4
,
5
,
9
,
10
,
16
]
.

Recently, using the creative microscoping method (cf.
[
7
]
), Guo and Zhao
[
6
]
studied some
q
-supercongruences from a very-well-poised
{}_{6}\phi_{5}
basic hypergeometric identity. As consequences, they obtained the following results: for any prime
p\equiv 1\pmod{4}
and integer
r\geq 1
,

\sum_{k=0}^{(p^{r}-1)/2}(8k+1)\frac{(\frac{1}{4})_{k}^{3}(\frac{1}{2})_{k}}{(1)_{k}^{3}(\frac{3}{4})_{k}}\equiv p^{r}\pmod{p^{r+3}},

(1.5)

and for any prime
p\equiv 3\pmod{4}
and odd integer
r\geq 1
,

\sum_{k=0}^{p^{r}-1}(8k+1)\frac{(\frac{1}{4})_{k}^{3}(\frac{1}{2})_{k}}{(1)_{k}^{3}(\frac{3}{4})_{k}}\equiv 3p^{r}\pmod{p^{r+2}}.

(1.6)

Guo and Zhao
[
6
, Conjectures 7.1 and 7.2]
also conjectured that (
1.5
) still holds modulo
p^{r+5}
for
p>5
and (
1.6
) can be extended to the modulus
p^{r+3}
case as follows:

\sum_{k=0}^{p^{r}-1}(8k+1)\frac{(\frac{1}{4})_{k}^{3}(\frac{1}{2})_{k}}{(1)_{k}^{3}(\frac{3}{4})_{k}}\equiv 3p^{r}+\frac{27}{4}p^{3r}\sum_{j=1}^{(p^{r}-3)/4}\frac{1}{j^{2}}\pmod{p^{r+3}}.

(1.7)

This is our initial motivation.

Recall Whipple’s
{}_{5}F_{4}
formula (cf.
[
20
]
)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(a)_{k}(1+\frac{a}{2})_{k}(b)_{k}(c)_{k}(d)_{k}}{(1)_{k}(\frac{a}{2})_{k}(1+a-b)_{k}(1+a-c)_{k}(1+a-d)_{k}}

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{\Gamma(1+a-b)\Gamma(1+a-c)\Gamma(1+a-d)\Gamma(1+a-b-c-d)}{\Gamma(1+a)\Gamma(1+a-b-c)\Gamma(1+a-b-d)\Gamma(1+a-c-d)}.

(1.8)

Clearly, (
1.1
) is the special case
a=b=c=d=1/4
of (
1
). Meanwhile, (
1.4
) and (
1.6
) are
p
-adic analogues of (
1
) in the case
a=b=c=d=\alpha
and the case
a=b=c=1/4,\ d=1/2
, respectively. Motivated by (
1.4
), it is natural to ask whether (
1.6
) or (
1.7
) has a parametric extension. This is the second motivation.

Before stating our main result, we first introduce some notations. For
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}=\{1,2,3,\ldots\}
and
x\in\mathbb{Z}
, use
\langle x\rangle_{n}
to denote the least nonnegative residue of
x
modulo
n
. Let
p
be an odd prime. Similarly as in
\mathbb{Z}
, for any
p
-adic integer
x
and

,
\langle x\rangle_{p^{r}}
stands for the least nonnegative residue of
x
modulo
p^{r}
and
x^{\ast}=(x+\langle-x\rangle_{p})/p
denotes Dwork’s dash operation on
x
(cf.
[
3
]
). For convenience, for
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, use
x^{\ast_{n}}
to represent iterating the dash operation on
x

n

x^{\ast_{1}}=x^{\ast},\quad x^{\ast_{n}}=(x^{\ast_{n-1}})^{\ast}\quad(n=2,3,4,\ldots).

In particular, set
x^{\ast_{0}}=x
. For
n\in\mathbb{N}=\{0,1,2,\ldots\},\ m\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, the
n

Our main purpose is to establish a
p
-adic analogue of Whipple’s formula (
1
) with
a=b=c=\alpha
and
d=\frac{1}{2}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
c,d,s\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
with
d\geq 2
,
1\leq c,s\leq d
and
\gcd(cs,d)=1
, and let
p\geq 5
be a prime with
p\equiv s\pmod{d}
. Then, for any
r\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
with
(\frac{1}{2}+\alpha)^{\ast_{r}}(\frac{1}{2}+\alpha^{\ast_{r}})\not\equiv 0\pmod{p}
, we have

\sum_{k=0}^{p^{r}-1}(2k+\alpha)\frac{(\alpha)_{k}^{3}(\frac{1}{2})_{k}}{(1)_{k}^{3}(\frac{1}{2}+\alpha)_{k}}\equiv\alpha^{\ast_{r}}p^{r}-\frac{(\alpha^{\ast_{r}})^{3}}{(\frac{1}{2}+\alpha)^{\ast_{r}}}p^{r+2}H_{\alpha^{\ast_{r}}p-\alpha^{\ast_{r-1}}}^{(2)}\pmod{p^{r+3}},

(1.9)

where
\alpha=c/d
.

Theorem
1.1
seems quite strange. When
\langle-1/2-\alpha\rangle_{p}<\min\{\langle-\alpha\rangle_{p},(p-1)/2\}
, the summands on the left-hand side of (
1.9
) are not always
p
-adic integers. However, the sum of these summands is a
p
-adic integer. This phenomenon renders it difficult for us to prove Theorem
1.1
directly using the formula (
1
) and conventional congruence techniques. To overcome this obstacle, we find a new parametric WZ pair (cf.
[
17
]
) which allows us to transform the original sum to a computable form in the sense of congruence. This idea is crucial in our proof.

In particular, putting
d=4,s=3,c=1
and requiring
r
to be odd in Theorem
1.1
, we have the following result.

Corollary 1.1
.

Guo and Zhao’s conjectural supercongruence (
1.7
)
[
6
, Conjecture 7.2]
is true.

Moreover, Theorem
1.1
with
d=2,s=1,c=1
gives that

\sum_{k=0}^{p^{r}-1}(4k+1)\frac{(\frac{1}{2})_{k}^{4}}{(1)_{k}^{4}}\equiv p^{r}\pmod{p^{r+3}},

(1.10)

where we have used (
3.1
). Note that the
r=1
case of (
1.10
) is a stronger version of Van Hamme’s (C.2) supercongruence
[
19
]
and was first proved by Long
[
11
]
.

2.
Properties of Dwork’s dash operation and immediate applications

For convenience, in the subsequent proof we shall write
\langle-\alpha\rangle_{p^{r}}=a
. In Lemmas
2.1
and
2.2
, we determine the exact values of
\alpha^{\ast_{r}}\ (r\in\mathbb{Z}^{+})
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
c,d,s\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
with
d\geq 2
,
1\leq c,s\leq d
and
\gcd(cs,d)=1
. Then, for any prime
p\equiv s\pmod{d}
, we have

\alpha^{\ast}=\frac{\langle s^{-1}c\rangle_{d}}{d},

where
s^{-1}
stands for the inverse of
s
modulo
d
.

Proof.

Clearly,
\langle s^{-1}c\rangle_{d}p-c\equiv 0\pmod{d}
and
(\langle s^{-1}c\rangle_{d}p-c)/d\equiv-c/d\pmod{p}
. Meanwhile,

-1<-\frac{c}{d}<\frac{p-c}{d}\leq\frac{\langle s^{-1}c\rangle_{d}p-c}{d}\leq\frac{(d-1)p-c}{d}<p.

Therefore,

\frac{\langle s^{-1}c\rangle_{d}p-c}{d}\in\{0,1,2,\ldots,p-1\},

which means

\left\langle-\frac{c}{d}\right\rangle_{p}=\frac{\langle s^{-1}c\rangle_{d}p-c}{d}.

Then we have

\left(\frac{c}{d}\right)^{\ast}=\frac{\frac{c}{d}+\langle-\frac{c}{d}\rangle_{p}}{p}=\frac{\frac{c}{d}+\frac{\langle s^{-1}c\rangle_{d}p-c}{d}}{p}=\frac{\langle s^{-1}c\rangle_{d}}{d}

as desired.
∎

By Lemma
2.1
, we have the following immediate corollaries.

Corollary 2.1
.

Under the conditions of Lemma
2.1
,

\alpha^{\ast_{r}}=\frac{\langle s^{-r}c\rangle_{d}}{d}.

Corollary 2.2
.

Under the conditions of Lemma
2.1
, the least positive period of the dash operation on
c/d
is
{\rm ind}_{d}s
, where
{\rm ind}_{d}s
denotes the index of
s
modulo
d
, i.e., the least positive integer
n
such that
s^{n}\equiv 1\pmod{d}
.

For instance, for
s=1
, we have
{\rm ind}_{d}s=1
, which implies
\alpha^{\ast_{r}}=\alpha
for any
r\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
. For
s=-1
, we have
{\rm ind}_{d}s=2
, which implies that

\alpha^{\ast_{r}}=\begin{cases}\alpha,\quad&\text{if}\ 2\mid r,\\
\alpha^{\ast},\quad&\text{if}\ 2\nmid r.\end{cases}

Lemma 2.2
.

Under the conditions of Lemma
2.1
, we have

\alpha^{\ast_{r}}=\frac{\langle s^{-r}c\rangle_{d}}{d}=\frac{\alpha+a}{p^{r}}.

Proof.

Clearly,
p^{r}\equiv s^{r}\pmod{d}
. Therefore,
\langle s^{-r}c\rangle_{d}p^{r}-c\equiv 0\pmod{d}
and
(\langle s^{-r}c\rangle_{d}p^{r}-c)/d\equiv-c/d\pmod{p^{r}}
. Moreover,

-1<-\frac{c}{d}<\frac{p^{r}-c}{d}\leq\frac{\langle s^{-r}c\rangle_{d}p^{r}-c}{d}\leq\frac{(d-1)p^{r}-c}{d}<p^{r},

and then

\frac{\langle s^{-r}c\rangle_{d}p^{r}-c}{d}\in\{0,1,2,\ldots,p^{r}-1\},

which means

\left\langle-\frac{c}{d}\right\rangle_{p^{r}}=\frac{\langle s^{-r}c\rangle_{d}p^{r}-c}{d}.

So we have

\frac{\frac{c}{d}+\left\langle-\frac{c}{d}\right\rangle_{p^{r}}}{p^{r}}=\frac{\frac{c}{d}+\frac{\langle s^{-r}c\rangle_{d}p^{r}-c}{d}}{p^{r}}=\frac{\langle s^{-r}c\rangle_{d}}{d}.

In view of Corollary
2.1
, we concludes the proof.
∎

The next lemma concerns the largest multiple of
p
in a certain set defined via the dash operation.

Lemma 2.3
.

Under the conditions of Theorem
1.1
with
r>1
, for
j\in\{0,1,2,\ldots,r-2\}
, we have

\max\{l\in\{1,2,3,\ldots,\alpha^{\ast_{r}}p^{r-j}-\alpha^{\ast_{j}}\}:p\mid l\}=\alpha^{\ast_{r}}p^{r-j}-\alpha^{\ast_{j+1}}p.

(2.1)

Proof.

From the proof of Lemma
2.2
, we know

d(\alpha^{\ast_{r}}p^{r-j}-\alpha^{\ast_{j+1}}p)=\langle s^{-r}c\rangle_{d}p^{r-j}-\langle s^{-j-1}c\rangle_{d}p\equiv s^{-r}cs^{r-j}-s^{-j-1}cs\equiv 0\pmod{d},

which means the right-hand side of (
2.1
) is an integer divisible by
p
. Moreover,

-1<\frac{s-d+1}{d}\leq\frac{p}{d}-\frac{d-1}{d}\leq\alpha^{\ast_{j+1}}p-\alpha^{\ast_{j}}<p-\alpha^{\ast_{j}}<p.

Therefore, we have

\alpha^{\ast_{j+1}}p-\alpha^{\ast_{j}}\in\{0,1,2,\ldots,p-1\},

and so

\alpha^{\ast_{r}}p^{r-j}-\alpha^{\ast_{j}}-p<\alpha^{\ast_{r}}p^{r-j}-\alpha^{\ast_{j+1}}p\leq\alpha^{\ast_{r}}p^{r-j}-\alpha^{\ast_{j}},

which implies that the right-hand side of (
2.1
) is the largest integer divisible by
p
in
\{1,2,3,\ldots,

\alpha^{\ast_{r}}p^{r-j}-\alpha^{\ast_{j}}\}
.

The proof of Lemma
2.3
is now complete.
∎

Finally, we give two applications of the dash operation in evaluations of the Pochhammer symbols.

Lemma 2.4
.

For any odd prime
p
,
x\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
and
r\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, we have

(x)_{p^{r}}=(-1)^{r}x^{\ast_{r}}\prod_{j=1}^{r}\left(p^{p^{j-1}}\frac{\Gamma_{p}(x+p^{j})}{\Gamma_{p}(x)}\right).

Proof.

Recall that for any
x\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
,

\frac{\Gamma_{p}(x+1)}{\Gamma_{p}(x)}=\begin{cases}-x,\quad&\text{if}\ x\not\equiv 0\pmod{p},\\
-1,\quad&\text{if}\ x\equiv 0\pmod{p}\end{cases}

(cf.
[
13
]
). It follows that

\displaystyle(x)_{p^{r}}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{p^{r}}(x+\langle-x\rangle_{p})(x+\langle-x\rangle_{p}+p)(x+\langle-x\rangle_{p}+2p)\cdots(x+\langle-x\rangle_{p}+p^{r}-p)\frac{\Gamma_{p}(x+p^{r})}{\Gamma_{p}(x)}

\displaystyle=-(x^{\ast})_{p^{r-1}}p^{p^{r-1}}\frac{\Gamma_{p}(x+p^{r})}{\Gamma_{p}(x)}

\displaystyle=(x^{\ast_{2}})_{p^{r-2}}p^{p^{r-2}}p^{p^{r-1}}\frac{\Gamma_{p}(x+p^{r-1})}{\Gamma_{p}(x)}\frac{\Gamma_{p}(x+p^{r})}{\Gamma_{p}(x)}

\displaystyle=\cdots

\displaystyle=(-1)^{r}x^{\ast_{r}}\prod_{j=1}^{r}\left(p^{p^{j-1}}\frac{\Gamma_{p}(x+p^{j})}{\Gamma_{p}(x)}\right).

This concludes the proof.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Under the conditions of Theorem
1.1
, for
l\in\{0,1,2,\ldots,a-1\}
, modulo
p
we have

\displaystyle\frac{(\frac{1}{2}+\alpha)_{l}}{(\frac{1}{2}+\alpha+p^{r})_{l}}

\displaystyle\qquad\equiv\begin{cases}1,\quad&\text{if}\ a<(p^{r}+1)/2\ \text{or}\ a\geq(p^{r}+1)/2\ \text{and}\ l<a-(p^{r}-1)/2,\\
(2{\alpha}^{\ast_{r}}-1)/(2{\alpha}^{\ast_{r}}+1),\quad&\text{if}\ a\geq(p^{r}+1)/2\ \text{and}\ l\geq a-(p^{r}-1)/2.\end{cases}

Proof.

It is easy to see that

\frac{(\frac{1}{2}+\alpha)_{l}}{(\frac{1}{2}+\alpha+p^{r})_{l}}=\prod_{j=0}^{l-1}\frac{\frac{1}{2}+\alpha+j}{\frac{1}{2}+\alpha+j+p^{r}}=\prod_{j=0}^{l-1}\frac{1}{1+\frac{p^{r}}{\frac{1}{2}+\alpha+j}}.

Case 1.

a<(p^{r}+1)/2
.

Now
\langle-\frac{1}{2}-\alpha\rangle_{p^{r}}=(p^{r}-1)/2+a
. Thus, for
j\in\{0,1,2,\ldots,l-1\}
,

{\rm ord}_{p}\left(\frac{1}{2}+\alpha+j\right)<r,

and then

\frac{(\frac{1}{2}+\alpha)_{l}}{(\frac{1}{2}+\alpha+p^{r})_{l}}\equiv 1\pmod{p}.

Case 2.

a\geq(p^{r}+1)/2
and
l<a-(p^{r}-1)/2
.

Now
\langle-\frac{1}{2}-\alpha\rangle_{p^{r}}=(p^{r}-1)/2+a-p^{r}=a-(p^{r}+1)/2
. For
j\in\{0,1,2,\ldots,l-1\}
,
j\leq l-1<a-(p^{r}+1)/2
. Therefore,

{\rm ord}_{p}\left(\frac{1}{2}+\alpha+j\right)<r,

and we also have

\frac{(\frac{1}{2}+\alpha)_{l}}{(\frac{1}{2}+\alpha+p^{r})_{l}}\equiv 1\pmod{p}.

Case 3.

a\geq(p^{r}+1)/2
and
l\geq a-(p^{r}-1)/2
.

When
j=a-(p^{r}+1)/2
, applying Lemma
2.2
, we obtain
1/2+\alpha+j=p^{r}(\alpha^{\ast_{r}}-1/2)
. Meanwhile, if
j\neq a-(p^{r}+1)/2
,

{\rm ord}_{p}\left(\frac{1}{2}+\alpha+j\right)<r.

Therefore,

\frac{(\frac{1}{2}+\alpha)_{l}}{(\frac{1}{2}+\alpha+p^{r})_{l}}\equiv\frac{1}{1+\frac{p^{r}}{p^{r}(\alpha^{\ast_{r}}-\frac{1}{2})}}=\frac{2\alpha^{\ast_{r}}-1}{2\alpha^{\ast_{r}}+1}\pmod{p}.

The proof of Lemma
2.5
is now complete.
∎

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.1
and Corollary
1.1

It is well-known (see, e.g.
[
21
]
) that

H_{p-1}^{(2)}\equiv H_{(p-1)/2}^{(2)}\equiv 0\pmod{p},

(3.1)

where
p\geq 5
is a prime.

The following two lemmas involve reductions of certain harmonic numbers in the sense of congruence.

Lemma 3.1
.

For any prime
p\geq 5
and positive integer
r
, we have

p^{2r}H_{p^{r}-1}^{(2)}\equiv p^{2r}H_{(p^{r}-1)/2}^{(2)}\equiv 0\pmod{p^{3}}.

(3.2)

Proof.

In view of (
3.1
), (
3.2
) holds for
r=1
. Now assume
r\geq 2
. By (
3.1
), we have

\displaystyle p^{2r}H_{p^{r}-1}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=p^{2r}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=1\\
p\nmid k\end{subarray}}^{p^{r}-1}\frac{1}{k^{2}}+p^{2r}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=1\\
p\mid k\end{subarray}}^{p^{r}-1}\frac{1}{k^{2}}

\displaystyle\equiv p^{2r-2}\sum_{k=1}^{p^{r-1}-1}\frac{1}{k^{2}}\equiv\cdots\equiv p^{2}\sum_{k=1}^{p-1}\frac{1}{k^{2}}

\displaystyle\equiv 0\pmod{p^{3}}

and

\displaystyle p^{2r}H_{(p^{r}-1)/2}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=p^{2r}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=1\\
p\nmid k\end{subarray}}^{(p^{r}-1)/2}\frac{1}{k^{2}}+p^{2r}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=1\\
p\mid k\end{subarray}}^{(p^{r}-1)/2}\frac{1}{k^{2}}

Lemma 3.3
.

Under the conditions of Theorem
1.1
, we have

\sum_{k=0}^{p^{r}-1}F(\alpha^{\ast_{r}}p^{r},k)\equiv\alpha^{\ast_{r}}p^{r}\pmod{p^{r+3}}.

Proof.

Obviously,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{p^{r}-1}F(\alpha^{\ast_{r}}p^{r},k)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{p^{r}-1}(2k+\alpha^{\ast_{r}}p^{r})\frac{(\alpha^{\ast_{r}}p^{r})_{k}^{3}(\frac{1}{2})_{k}}{(1)_{k}^{3}(\frac{1}{2}+\alpha^{\ast_{r}}p^{r})_{k}}

\displaystyle=\alpha^{\ast_{r}}p^{r}+p^{3r}{\alpha^{\ast_{r}}}^{3}\sum_{k=1}^{p^{r}-1}\frac{2k+\alpha^{\ast_{r}}p^{r}}{k^{3}}\frac{(1+\alpha^{\ast_{r}}p^{r})_{k-1}^{3}(\frac{1}{2})_{k}}{(1)_{k-1}^{3}(\frac{1}{2}+\alpha^{\ast_{r}}p^{r})_{k}}.

Note that for
k\in\{1,2,\ldots,p^{r}-1\}
,
{\rm ord}_{p}(k)\leq r-1
, where
{\rm ord}_{p}
stands for the
p
-adic order. Therefore,

{\rm ord}_{p}\left(\frac{p^{3r}}{k^{2}}\right)\geq r+2\quad\text{and}\quad{\rm ord}_{p}\left(\frac{p^{4r}}{k^{3}}\right)\geq r+3.

Moreover, it is easy to see that

\frac{(1+\alpha^{\ast_{r}}p^{r})_{k-1}}{(1)_{k-1}^{3}}=\prod_{j=1}^{k-1}\frac{j+\alpha^{\ast_{r}}p^{r}}{j}=\prod_{j=1}^{k-1}\left(1+\frac{\alpha^{\ast_{r}}p^{r}}{j}\right)\equiv 1\pmod{p}

and

\displaystyle\frac{(\frac{1}{2})_{k}}{(\frac{1}{2}+\alpha^{\ast_{r}}p^{r})_{k}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}\frac{\frac{1}{2}+j}{\frac{1}{2}+j+\alpha^{\ast_{r}}p^{r}}=\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}\frac{1}{1+\frac{\alpha^{\ast_{r}}p^{r}}{\frac{1}{2}+j}}

\displaystyle\equiv\begin{cases}1/(2\alpha^{\ast_{r}}+1)\pmod{p}\quad&\text{if}\ k\geq(p^{r}+1)/2,\\
1\pmod{p}\quad&\text{if}\ k\leq(p^{r}-1)/2.\end{cases}

Combining the above and in view of Lemmas
2.2
and
3.1
, we arrive at

\sum_{k=0}^{p^{r}-1}F(\alpha^{\ast_{r}}p^{r},k)\equiv\alpha^{\ast_{r}}p^{r}+2(\alpha^{\ast_{r}})^{3}p^{3r}\sum_{k=1}^{(p^{r}-1)/2}\frac{1}{k^{2}}+\frac{2(\alpha^{\ast_{r}})^{3}p^{3r}}{2\alpha^{\ast_{r}}+1}\sum_{k=(p^{r}+1)/2}^{p^{r}-1}\frac{1}{k^{2}}\equiv\alpha^{\ast_{r}}p^{r}\pmod{p^{r+3}},

as desired.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Under the conditions of Theorem
1.1
, we have

\sum_{l=0}^{a-1}G(\alpha+l,p^{r})\equiv\frac{(\alpha^{\ast_{r}})^{3}}{(\frac{1}{2}+\alpha)^{\ast_{r}}}p^{r+2}H_{\alpha^{\ast_{r}}p-\alpha^{\ast_{r-1}}}^{(2)}\pmod{p^{r+3}}.

(3.6)

Proof.

Clearly,

\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{a-1}G(\alpha+l,p^{r})

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{a-1}\frac{p^{3r}(p^{r}+2\alpha+2l)}{(\alpha+l)^{3}}\frac{(\alpha+l)_{p^{r}}^{3}(\frac{1}{2})_{p^{r}}}{(1)_{p^{r}}^{3}(\frac{1}{2}+\alpha+l)_{p^{r}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{p^{3r}(\alpha)_{p^{r}}^{3}(\frac{1}{2})_{p^{r}}}{(1)_{p^{r}}^{3}(\frac{1}{2}+\alpha)_{p^{r}}}\sum_{l=0}^{a-1}\frac{(p^{r}+2\alpha+2l)(\alpha+p^{r})_{l}^{3}(\frac{1}{2}+\alpha)_{l}}{(\alpha+l)^{3}(\alpha)_{l}^{3}(\frac{1}{2}+\alpha+p^{r})_{l}}.

Then, by Lemma
2.4
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{a-1}G(\alpha+l,p^{r})

\displaystyle=\frac{p^{3r}(\alpha^{\ast_{r}})^{3}}{2(\frac{1}{2}+\alpha)^{\ast_{r}}}\prod_{j=1}^{r}\frac{\Gamma_{p}(\alpha+p^{j})^{3}\Gamma_{p}(\frac{1}{2}+p^{j})\Gamma_{p}(1)^{3}\Gamma_{p}(\frac{1}{2}+\alpha)}{\Gamma_{p}(\alpha)^{3}\Gamma_{p}(\frac{1}{2})\Gamma_{p}(1+p^{j})^{3}\Gamma_{p}(\frac{1}{2}+\alpha+p^{j})}

\displaystyle\quad\times\sum_{l=0}^{a-1}\frac{(p^{r}+2\alpha+2l)(\alpha+p^{r})_{l}^{3}(\frac{1}{2}+\alpha)_{l}}{(\alpha+l)^{3}(\alpha)_{l}^{3}(\frac{1}{2}+\alpha+p^{r})_{l}},

k\in\{0,1,2,\ldots,a-1\}

Case 2

\frac{(\frac{1}{2}+\alpha)_{l}}{(\frac{1}{2}+\alpha+p^{r})_{l}}\equiv\begin{cases}1\pmod{p},\quad&\text{if}\ l<a-(p^{r}-1)/2,\\

ACT-Inspired Kähler-Based Inflationary Attractors
C. Pallis

Abstract
Abstract We develop a new class of cosmological attractors which are compatible with the recent ACT results. They are based on two types of fractional Kähler potentials, K K , for a gauge-singlet inflaton ϕ \phi which reduce, along the inflationary path, to the form N / ( 1 − ϕ q M ) p N/(1-\phi^{q_{\rm M}})^{p} with q M = 1 , 2 q_{\rm M}=1,2 and 0.1 ≤ p ≤ 10 0.1\leq p\leq 10 . The combination of these K K ’s with the chaotic potentials ϕ n \phi^{n} (where n = 2 , 4 n=2,4 ) within a non-linear sigma model leads to inflationary observables which are consistent with the current data and largely independent from q M q_{\rm M} and n n . Endowing these K K ’s with a shift symmetry we also offer a supergravity realization of our models introducing two chiral superfields and a monomial superpotential, linear with respect to the inflaton-accompanying field. The attainment of inflation with subplanckian inflaton values and the large values for the tensor-to-scalar ratio, which increases with N N , are two additional attractive features of our proposal. 2
non-SUSY Framework

Working in the context of a non-linear sigma model we assume that
the kinetic mixing in the moduli space of one (complex) scalar
field
\Phi
is controlled by a metric
K_{\Phi\Phi^{*}}
. The
relevant lagrangian terms are written as

{\cal L}=\sqrt{-\mathfrak{g}}\left(-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{R}+K_{\Phi\Phi^{*}}\partial_{\mu}\Phi\partial^{\mu}\Phi^{*}-V(\Phi)\right),

(2.1)

where
\mathfrak{g}
is the determinant of the background
Friedmann-Robertson-Walker metric
g^{\mu\nu}
with signature

(+,-,-,-)
,
\mathcal{R}
is the Ricci scalar and star (
∗
) denotes
complex conjugation. We further assume that
K_{{\alpha}{\bar{\beta}}}

originates from a Kähler potential
K
according to the generic
definition

K_{{\alpha}{\bar{\beta}}}={K_{,z^{\alpha}z^{*{\bar{\beta}}}}}>0\>\>\>\mbox{with}\>\>\>K^{{\bar{\beta}}{\alpha}}K_{{\alpha}\bar{\gamma}}=\delta^{\bar{\beta}}_{\bar{\gamma}},

(2.2)

where
z^{\alpha}
are complex scalar fields – the symbol
,z^{\alpha}
as
subscript denotes derivation w.r.t the field
z^{\alpha}
. Trying to
generate kinetic mixings similar to that in
\Eref
fbp we consider
four
K
’s,
K_{l\rm M}
with
l=1,2
and
\rm M=\rm E,\rm T
, defined for

{\rm NewA}>0
as follows

K_{\rm 1M}={{\rm NewA}}{\left(1-(2\Phi\Phi^{*})^{q_{\rm M}/2}\right)^{-p}}\>\>\>\mbox{and}\>\>\>K_{\rm 2M}={N}{\left(1-2^{(q_{\rm M}-2)/2}(\Phi+\Phi^{*})\right)^{-p}},

(2.3)

where
q_{\rm M}
is defined in
\Eref
fp. More explicitly,
K_{l\rm M}
above
are specified as follows

K_{\rm 1E}={{\rm NewA}}{\left(1-(2\Phi\Phi^{*})^{1/2}\right)^{-p}}\>\>\>\mbox{and}\>\>\>K_{\rm 2E}={N}{\left(1-(\Phi+\Phi^{*})/\sqrt{2}\right)^{-p}}\>\>\>\mbox{for
{E${}_{p}$MI}}

(2.4a)

with
(\Phi\Phi^{*})^{1/2}:=|\Phi|<1/\sqrt{2}
and

\Phi+\Phi^{*}<\sqrt{2}
respectively and

K_{\rm 1T}={{\rm NewA}}{\left(1-2|\Phi|^{2}\right)^{-p}}\>\>\>\mbox{and}\>\>\>K_{\rm 2T}={N}{\left(1-\Phi^{2}-\Phi^{*2}\right)^{-p}}\>\>\>\mbox{for
{T${}_{p}$MI}}

(2.4b)

with

and
\Phi^{2}+\Phi^{*2}<1

conservatively confine ourselves in the range

.
The corresponding Kähler metrics are found to be

\displaystyle K_{\Phi\Phi^{*}}=pN\cdot\begin{cases}({1+\sqrt{2}p|\Phi|^{2}})/2\sqrt{2}{(1-\sqrt{2}|\Phi|)^{p+2}}&\mbox{for $K=K_{\rm 1E}$,}\\
(p+1)/2{(1-(\Phi+\Phi^{*})/\sqrt{2})^{p+2}}&\mbox{for
$K=K_{\rm 2E}$,}\\
{2(1+2p|\Phi|^{2})}/{(1-2|\Phi|^{2})^{p+2}}&\mbox{for $K=K_{\rm 1T}$,}\\
{4(p+1)|\Phi|^{2}}/{(1-\Phi^{2}-\Phi^{*2})^{p+2}}&\mbox{for $K=K_{\rm 2T}$.}\end{cases}

(2.5)

All
K_{l\rm M}
in Eqs. (
2.4a
) and (

) parameterize hyperbolic Kähler manifolds but

\Phi^{q_{\rm M}}\rightarrow\ \Phi^{q_{\rm M}}+a\>\>\>\mbox{with}\>\>\>a\in\mathbb{R}.

do not determine uniquely the corresponding Kähler manifolds.

4
Inflation Analysis

The duration of the slow-roll E
p
/T
p
MI is determined by the
condition:

{\footnotesize\sf max}\{\epsilon(\phi),|\eta(\phi)|\}\leq 1,

(4.1)

where the slow-roll parameter
\epsilon
takes a common form for any

K_{l\rm M}
– with
l=1,2
and M=E, T as usual – which is

\epsilon=\left({V_{\rm I,\widehat{\phi}}\over\sqrt{2}V_{\rm I}}\right)^{2}=\frac{n^{2}f_{\rm M}^{p+2}}{q_{\rm M}^{2}pN\phi^{q_{\rm M}}j_{l{\rm M}}}\>\>\mbox{for}\>\>K=K_{l\rm M},

(4.2a)

whereas
\eta
can be expressed by separate formulas for
K_{\rm 1M}

and
K_{\rm 2M}
as follows

\eta={V_{\rm I,\widehat{\phi}\widehat{\phi}}\over V_{\rm I}}\simeq\frac{nf_{\rm M}^{p+1}}{q_{\rm M}pN\phi^{q_{\rm M}}j_{l{\rm M}}}\cdot\begin{cases}\left(2^{2-q_{\rm M}}nf_{\rm M}j_{1{\rm M}}-1-\phi^{q_{\rm M}}-p\phi^{q_{\rm M}}(p\phi^{q_{\rm M}}+3)\right)/j_{1{\rm M}}&\mbox{for $K=K_{\rm 1M}$,}\\
2^{2-q_{\rm M}}nf_{\rm M}-2-p\phi^{q_{\rm M}}&\mbox{for $K=K_{\rm 2M}$.}\end{cases}

(4.2b)

For the expressions above we employ
J
in
\Eref
VJe, without
expressing explicitly
V_{\rm I}
in terms of
\widehat{\phi}
given in

\Eref
sesg. Expanding
f_{\rm M}
for
\phi\ll 1
and neglecting terms of
order
\phi^{2q_{\rm M}}
or larger we obtain
\phi_{\epsilon\rm f}
and

\phi_{\eta\rm f}
which saturate
\Eref
srcon for the
\epsilon
and

\eta
criteria respectively. In practice, we mostly obtain

and so the only relevant values
are

\phi_{\epsilon\rm f}\simeq\begin{cases}\sqrt{n(n-1)/2(n^{2}+p(n-1)N}&\mbox{for $K=K_{\rm 1E}$,}\\
2n/(n(2+p)+\sqrt{4Np(1+4p)+n^{2}(p+2)^{2}})&\mbox{for $K=K_{\rm 2E}$,}\\
n/\sqrt{n^{2}(2+p)-4Np}&\mbox{for $K=K_{\rm 1T}$,}\\
2n/(n(2+p)+\sqrt{16Np(1+p)+n^{2}(2+p)^{2}})^{1/2}&\mbox{for $K=K_{\rm 2T}$.}\end{cases}

(4.3)

{N_{\star}}

/T
p

\displaystyle{N_{\star}}=\int_{\widehat{\phi}_{\rm f}}^{\widehat{\phi}_{\star}}d\widehat{\phi}\frac{V_{\rm I}}{V_{\rm I,\widehat{\phi}}}=2^{q_{\rm M}-2}\frac{N}{n}\cdot\begin{cases}{p\phi_{\star}^{q_{\rm M}}}/{f_{\rm M\star}^{p+1}}&\mbox{for $K=K_{\rm 1M}$,}\\
\left(1+({j_{2{\rm M}\star}-1})/{f_{\rm M\star}^{p+1}}\right)&\mbox{for $K=K_{\rm 2M}$,}\end{cases}

(4.4)

where
\phi_{\star}~[\widehat{\phi}_{\star}]
is the value of
\phi~[\widehat{\phi}]
when

crosses

in the analytic expressions above. Hereafter, the variables with

– e.g.,

. It is not doable to solve accurately the

gets simplified

{N_{\star}}\simeq 2^{q_{\rm M}-2}\frac{N}{n}\cdot\begin{cases}{p}/{f_{\rm M\star}^{p+1}}&\mbox{for $K=K_{\rm 1M}$,}\\
(1+{p}/{f_{\rm M\star}^{p+1}})&\mbox{for $K=K_{\rm 2M}$}\end{cases}\>\>\Rightarrow\>\>\phi_{\star}\simeq\left(1-\left(\frac{q_{\rm M}pN}{2n{N_{\star}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p+1}}\right)^{2^{1-q_{\rm M}}}

6
Conclusions

Prompted by the
P-ACT-LB-BK18
data which seems to disfavor the
conventional E/TMI (i.e., E- and T-model inflation) at more than
two standard deviations, we proposed two variants called E
p
/T
p
MI which assure compatibility with the current observations at the
cost of just one more parameter
p
which strengthens the
operation of the pole, already present in E/TMI, by an extra
exponent
(p+2)
– see
\Eref
VJe. As a result
n_{\rm s}
approaches
its values in
\Eref
ns in nice agreement with
\Eref
data. This
convergence of both proposed models towards the same more or less
values of observables allows us to identify the emergence of a new
type of attractors named
(N,p)
attractors.

More specifically, our setting was established as a non-linear
sigma model using the potential and the Kähler potentials in Eqs. (
2.7
) and (
2.3
).
It can be implemented in SUGRA too, employing the super- and Kähler potentials
given in Eqs. (
3.4
) and (
3.5
). Both formulations yield the potential and
the kinetic mixing in Eqs. (
2.8
) and (
2.9a
). Confining
(N,p)
in the
ranges of
\Fref
fig4 we achieved a nice covering of the present
data – its compatibility especially with the
n=4
case for low

p
and
N
values is really impressive, as shown in
\Fref
fig3.
The resulting
r
values could be accessible in the near future.
Since our models exhibit a mechanism for enhancing the canonical
normalized inflaton w.r.t the original one – see
\Fref
fig1 –
our solutions can be attained with subplanckian values of the
initial inflaton. It is gratifying that these solutions can be
studied analytically and rather accurately in a sizable portion of
the allowed parameter space. Namely, our final analytical outputs
are summarized in Eqs. (
4.7a
), (
4.7b
) and (
4.7c
).

As a bottom line, we introduced two new versions of pole
inflation, E
p
/T
p
MI, with robust predictions toward the “sweet”
spot of the recent ACT data which signal the existence of an
attractor mechanism. Within our scheme, the reheating phase is not
constrained as, e.g., in
\cref
rha,rhb,rhc, act5, r2drees and
corrections to the inflationary potential from other sectors of
the theory are not required as, e.g., in
\cref
act6,oxf,warm.
Finally, our models – especially T
p
MI – can be extended for a
gauge non-singlet Higgs superfield as done in
\cref
jhep,sor and
can be applied for non-SUSY models of hybrid inflation as done in

\cref
ethi.

Prognostic Value of High-Sensitivity Troponin I for Cardiovascular Risk Stratification in Chronic Kidney Disease Patients
Authors: Mrinal Kunj, Prabin Kumar Shrivastava, Prashant Kumar, Anshu Kumar
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Background:The onset of chronic kidney disease (CKD) results in a high level of cardiovascular morbidity and mortality. Conventional risk assessment systems tend to fail in considering the cardiovascular risk of this group of people. A biomarker of myocardial damage, high-sensitivity cardiac troponin I (hs-cTnI), has become a candidate biomarker of subclinical cardiac damage.Materials and Methods:A prospective observational study was carried out of 120 patients with CKD stages 3 to 5 that excluded dialysis patients. A high-sensitivity immunoassay was used to measure the baseline value of hs-cTnI. The follow-up was thorough, with patients being kept under observation after 12 months of occurrence in their patients major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE), i.e., myocardial infarction, heart failure hospitalization, and cardiovascular death.Results:Out of 120 CKD patients (mean age 58.2+/- 9.6 years; 62 males), 48 patients (40%) had elevated hs-cTnI (>19.0 ng/L). The number of patients who developed MACE at the time of the follow-up was 29 (24.2%). There was a significantly greater proportion of MACE in an elevated hs-cTnI group (52.1%) than in a normal hs-cTnI group (10.4%) (P < 0.001). The Cox regression analysis indicated that an independent outcome predictor of MACE was hs-cTnI (HR 3.89, 95% CI 1.91793, P = 0.002). The ROC area under the curve of hs-cTnI at predicting MACE was 0.81 (95% CI 0.7290).Conclusion:High-sensitivity T-I is a good prognostic biomarker of cardiovascular events among CKD patients. Its regular evaluation can help in the initial classification of risks and selective cardiovascular preventive interventions.

INTRODUCTION

A current worldwide public health problem, chronic kidney disease (CKD), has shown a significantly higher prevalence of cardiovascular disease (CVD) risk; it has been observed as the preeminent cause of morbidity and mortality among this group.[1] The traditional cardiovascular risk factors like hypertension, diabetes, and dyslipidemia cannot explain the impacted cardiovascular burden in CKD, implying that there must be other nontraditional biomarkers that can provide complementary predictors of the risk.[2] Biomarkers that are in the fresh outlook include cardiac troponins because they can establish subclinical myocardial damages.

The sensitivity of high-sensitivity cardiac troponin I (hs-cTnI) assays enables small traces of troponin in the blood to be found, and therefore, myocardial stress or injury may be identified in unfortunate cases that have shown no symptoms.[3] The levels of hs-cTnI are raised in those patients with CKD because they had no overt ischemic heart disease, bringing up the questions concerning the pathophysiological processes associated with this elevation and the prognostic impact of this elevation.[4] Earlier, it has been observed that in a number of patient groups including renal impairment patients, hs-cTnI levels above normal are associated with cardiovascular events in an independent manner.[5]

MATERIALS AND METHODS

One hundred and twenty adult patients diagnosed with chronic kidney disease (CKD), stage 3 to 5 according to the Kidney Disease: Improving Global Outcomes (KDIGO) guidelines, accepted to participation with the signed informed consent. The patients having maintenance dialysis, those with the recent past history of acute coronary syndrome (previous three months), having active infections or malignancy were not included in the study.

Demographic and clinical characteristics of participants, such as age, gender, previous history of diabetes mellitus, hypertension, and previous history of cardiovascular diseases, were captured on a baseline. Intakes of blood pressure, body mass index (BMI), and estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) were recorded. Serum creatinine, lipid profile, hemoglobin, and high-sensitivity troponin I (hs-cTnI) levels were investigated in the laboratory. The chemiluminescent microparticle immunoassay (Abbott ARCHITECT STAT, Abbott Diagnostics, USA) was used to carry out hs-cTnI assay with a detection limit of 1.9 ng/L and the upper reference limit of 99 percent defined as 19.8 ng/L.

The evaluation of major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE) comprised nonfatal myocardial infarction, hospitalization due to heart failure, stroke, and cardiovascular death. The SPSS software version 24.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY) was used in statistical analysis.

RESULTS

A total of 120 patients with CKD warning 3 to 5 took part in the study and the mean 58.2 9.6 years. Of them, 62 (51.7%) were males and 58 (48.3%) were females. In 78 (65 percent) and 94 (78.3 percent) patients, diabetes mellitus and hypertension were present, respectively. The estimated mean glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) was 28.5 + 7.3 mL/min/1.73 m 2.

Forty percent of patients (48) had high-sensitivity troponin I (hs-cTnI) levels that were elevated (>19.8 ng/L) in value. In the 12-month follow-up, 29 patients (24.2%) had major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE). MACE was much higher in the group with high levels of hs-cTnI (25 out of 48; 52.1%) than in the group with normal levels of hs-cTnI (4 out of 72; 5.6%), respectively (P < 0.001).

In multivariate Cox regression analysis, high levels of hs-cTnI were an independent predictor of MACE (Hazard ratios: 3.89, 95% CI: 1.91 7.93; P = 0.002), adjusting the age, gender, diabetes, hypertension, and eGFR.

DISCUSSION

Troponins represent a well-known indicator of myocardial damage, although their utility in patients with CKD has traditionally been difficult because of low renal excretion and elevations that occur without acute ischemia.[1] Nevertheless, the high sensitivity testing is able to identify even the slight damage of the myocardium, which is an advantage when it comes to realizing the subclinical pathology, as well as the stress of the myocardium on a chronic level, in patients with reduced kidney status.[2] Various previous researches have posited that even higher levels of hs-cTnI among subjects without any acute coronary syndromes were related, by themselves, with risen death amid patients with CKD.[34]

hs-cTnI was an independent predictor of MACE in this trial, even after consideration of the risk factors (diabetes, hypertension, and low eGFR), which are present in medical practice. This helps in confirming the hypothesis that CKD myocardial injury can be multifactorial; in the sense, its causes include volume overload, anemia, vascular calcification, and left ventricular hypertrophy.[5] The excellent predictive capability of the ROC curve analysis also underscores the possibility of the use of hs-cTnI as part of the daily risks assessing guidelines of patients with CKD.

Also, other cohort studies have found results that align with our findings, given that their study was able to develop more accurate models of cardiovascular risks based on the levels of troponin and to help them devise early intervention strategies.[6] This is because the inclusion of hs-cTnI testing in the management of CKD could enable clinicians to detect high-risk patients sooner and provide preventive interventions, including the implementation of more active intervention of blood pressure and lipid levels or the referral to cardiology.[7]

CONCLUSION

Troponin I in high sensitivity (hs-cTnI) is a predictor biomarker in the prediction of cardiovascular risk that is reliable in patients affected with chronic kidney disease.

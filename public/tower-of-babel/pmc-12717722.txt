An innovative UV spectrophotometric method for the simultaneous determination of meclizine and pyridoxine in tablets
Authors: Imad Abu Reid, Alkhair Mohamed, Mohamed Hussain
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractA straightforward, precise, and rapid UV spectrophotometric method has been developed for the simultaneous determination of meclizine (MEC) and pyridoxine (PYR) in binary mixtures. The approach involved two calibration curves: the first was constructed to quantify PYR directly at 290 nm, while the second was used to calculate the concentration ratio (CR) of the mixture at 230 nm. This second curve was generated by plotting the absorbance ratio (AR) against the concentration ratio (CR = [MEC]/[PYR]). Using the experimentally determined concentration ratio (CRₑₓₚ) and the known PYR concentration, the MEC content was calculated as [MEC] = CRₑₓₚ × [PYR]. The method was validated in accordance with ICH Q2 guidelines and demonstrated reliable and consistent performance. When applied to a pharmaceutical formulation, the results expressed as a percentage of the labeled claim were 100.34% ± 0.47 for MEC and 100.04% ± 0.182 for PYR. Furthermore, statistical comparison with a previously published chromatographic method confirmed the equivalence of the two approaches. These results suggest that the developed method is suitable for routine quality control analysis of pharmaceutical preparations containing MEC and PYR. The linearity ranges were 4–20 µg/mL for meclizine and 6–30 µg/mL for pyridoxine and close to 1.00 for both analytes. Excellent precision was demonstrated, with percent Relative Standard Deviations (RSD%) well below 2%. The method also exhibited high accuracy, with analyte recoveries from tablets formulation 99.62 ± 0.34 and 98.92 ± 0.62 for MEC and PYR, respectively.Keywords: Meclizine, Pyridoxine, UV spectrophotometry, Tablets, Determination

Introduction

Meclizine (Fig. 1a) is an antihistamine belonging to the piperazine class of H1 receptor antagonists. It works by reversibly blocking histamine at H1 receptor sites, thereby reducing the symptoms associated with motion sickness [1]. Antihistamines, including meclizine, are commonly preferred for managing motion sickness and are considered safe for use during pregnancy. Pyridoxine (Fig. 1b), also known as vitamin B6, is often used to help prevent peripheral neuropathy and is frequently administered in combination therapies. The combination of meclizine and pyridoxine is widely regarded as an effective and safe option for alleviating nausea and vomiting during pregnancy, as well as for the prevention of motion sickness [2].

Chemical structures of (a) meclizine and (b) pyridoxine

To date, there is no official pharmacopoeial monograph available for the PYR-MEC combination in tablet dosage form. A review of the existing literature indicates that only a few chromatographic methods have been reported for the simultaneous estimation of these active pharmaceutical ingredients in tablet formulations [3–8]. In addition, various spectrophotometric techniques have been explored, employing a range of analytical strategies [3, 9–13]. These include chemometric approaches such as partial least squares (PLS-1) and principal component regression (PCR) [3], as well as other methods like dual wavelength (DWL), ratio difference (RD), and continuous wavelet transform (CWT) [9]. Further reported methods include simultaneous equation and absorbance correction techniques [10], ratio subtraction (RSM), induced dual wavelength (IDW), and Fourier self-deconvolution (FSD) [11]. Approaches such as simultaneous equation and Q-analysis [12], along with modified area under the curve and mean centering of ratio spectra [13], have also been investigated.

The present study aims to develop a straightforward and robust UV-spectrophotometric method as a cost-effective alternative to chromatographic methods and mathematically intensive spectrophotometric techniques that require advanced data processing tools and specialized software.

Theoretical considerations

The proposed method is based on the principle that, in a binary mixture of two drugs, X and Y, with overlapping UV spectra at a specific wavelength (λ₁), and where drug Y exhibits extended absorbance beyond that of drug X [such that X shows no absorbance at a second wavelength (λ₂)] the concentration of Y can be directly determined by applying its absorbance at λ₂ to its corresponding linear regression equation at 290 nm. Meanwhile, the concentration of drug X can be calculated using a regression equation derived from the concentration ratios (CR) of a series of standard mixtures plotted against their corresponding absorbance ratios (AR) at wavelengths λ₁ and λ₂.

Materials and methods

Apparatus and software

Spectrophotometric analysis was performed using a UV-1800 double-beam spectrophotometer (Shimadzu, Japan) equipped with 1-cm quartz cuvettes. The obtained data were processed and analyzed using Microsoft Excel 2013.

Materials and reagents

Working standards of meclizine hydrochloride (MEC) and pyridoxine hydrochloride (PYR) were received as a gift from Blue Nile Pharmaceutical Industries, Sudan. Analytical-grade hydrochloric acid (37%), deionized water, and commercial tablets labeled with 50 mg of PYR and 25 mg of MEC per tablet (purchased locally from Khartoum, Sudan) were used.

Preparation of stock standard solutions

Stock solutions of meclizine (200 µg/mL) and pyridoxine (300 µg/mL) were prepared by accurately weighing 20 mg of loratadine and 30 mg of pseudoephedrine sulfate into 100 mL volumetric flasks, respectively. The substances were dissolved in 0.1 M HCl, and the volume was adjusted to the mark with the same solvent to ensure complete dissolution.

Determination of the analytical wavelengths

For the determination of analytical wavelengths, standard solutions of pyridoxine (PYR, 30 µg/mL), meclizine (MEC, 15 µg/mL), and their combined mixture were prepared by appropriate dilution of the respective stock solutions. Each solution was scanned separately over the wavelength range of 210–350 nm using 0.1 M HCl as a blank to identify the absorption maxima of the individual analytes and their mixture.

Preparation of calibration mixtures

From the stock standard solutions of the analytes, eight mixed solutions containing varying concentrations of pyridoxine (PYR) and meclizine (MEC) within their respective linear ranges were prepared. These mixtures were obtained by accurately transferring appropriate volumes of each analyte stock solution into separate 100 mL volumetric flasks, followed by dilution to volume with 0.1 M HCl. The absorbance of each mixture was measured at 230 nm and 290 nm using 0.1 M HCl as a blank. Sample preparation

Twenty tablets were accurately weighed and finely powdered. A portion of the powder equivalent to the average mass of one tablet was transferred into a 100 mL volumetric flask, followed by the addition of 70 mL of 0.1 M hydrochloric acid. The mixture was sonicated for 10 min to ensure complete dissolution, allowed to cool to room temperature, and then diluted to volume with the same solvent (sample stock solution). The resulting solution was filtered through a 0.45 μm nylon membrane filter, and a 5 mL aliquot of the filtrate was subsequently diluted to 100 mL with 0.1 M hydrochloric acid.

Validation of the method

Method validation was performed in accordance with the guidelines of the International Council for Harmonization (ICH) [14]. The proposed method was evaluated for its linearity, accuracy, and precision.

Calibration standards

Separate linearity standard solutions of each analyte were prepared by appropriately diluting different aliquots of their respective stock standard solutions with 0.1 M HCl to obtain concentrations ranging from 6 to 30 µg/mL for pyridoxine (PYR) and 4–20 µg/mL for meclizine (MEC).

Precision

Method precision was evaluated in terms of repeatability and intermediate precision. Repeatability was assessed using three independent sample solutions containing the analytes at 80%, 100%, and 120% of the nominal concentration expected in the pharmaceutical formulation. These solutions were prepared by appropriately diluting 4,5 and 6 mL of the sample stock solution to 100 mL. The absorbance was measured at 230 nm and 290 nm using 0.1 M HCl as a blank. Each concentration level was analyzed in triplicate.

Accuracy

Accuracy was evaluated through recovery studies using the standard addition method. Pre-analyzed sample solutions containing approximately 60% of the labeled drug content (7.5 µg/mL MEC and 15 µg/mL PYR) were spiked with known amounts of the respective standards to obtain final concentrations corresponding to 80%, 100%, and 120% of the nominal levels in the pharmaceutical dosage form. Each mixture was analyzed under the same experimental conditions, and absorbance was measured at 230 nm and 290 nm using 0.1 M HCl as a blank.

The concentration of PYR was determined by measuring the sample’s absorbance and applying the regression equation A = 0.0399 × CPYR + 0.0568. To determine the experimental concentration ratio of the mixture (CRexp), the second regression equation A230/A₂90 = 0.7998× (CMEC/CPYR) + 0.3135 was used. Finally, the concentration of MEC was calculated using a straightforward computation based on Eq. 4.

The proposed method was successfully applied to the analysis of a marketed formulation containing the studied drugs. The results demonstrated excellent agreement with the labeled claims, yielding satisfactory recovery rates for both analytes. Specifically, recoveries were 100.34% ± 0.47 for MEC and 100.04% ± 0.182 for PYR. The assay results are presented in Table 5.

The consistency of results for both PYR and MEC in their combined tablet dosage form confirms the reliability of the method. The observed recovery values closely matched the manufacturer’s declared content, highlighting the method’s accuracy.

The key advantage of the developed method lies in its simplicity and cost-effectiveness. It enables routine quantitative analysis without the need for complex sample preparation, sophisticated calculations, or expensive instrumentation, making it a practical choice for regular quality control applications.

Furthermore, the results obtained using the proposed spectrophotometric method was found to be statistically comparable to those of a previously reported chromatographic method [8]. Statistical analysis using the Student’s t-test revealed that the calculated t-values for both analytes were lower than the critical t-value at n = 6 and P = 0.05, indicating no significant difference between the two methods. Conclusion

In summary, once the calibration equations were established, the analysis involved a straightforward sequence of steps: measuring the absorbance of the mixture at 290 nm to directly determine the concentration of PYR, followed by measuring the absorbance at 230 nm to calculate the absorbance ratio. This ratio was then used to determine the experimental concentration ratio, which allowed for the calculation of MEC concentration.

The developed method was fully validated and yielded satisfactory results. The method was successfully applied for the simultaneous quantification of MEC and PYR in their combined pharmaceutical dosage form. It proved to be rapid, simple, accurate, and dependable, offering excellent accuracy. As a practical and economical alternative, this approach is well-suited for routine quality control analysis, eliminating the need for complex procedures or costly instrumentation commonly required by other analytical techniques.

Itô-Wentzell formulas for semimartingale conditional laws with applications to mean-field control
Mehdi Talbi, Nizar Touzi

Abstract
The present paper is an extension of [ 7 ] . Following the same methodology, merely based on Taylor expansions, we establish the Itô and Itô-Wentzell formulae for flows of conditional distributions of general semimartingales, thus allowing for discontinuous semimartingales with possibly discontinuous flows of conditional marginals. We apply these results to derive the dynamic programming equations corresponding to mean field control problems with Poisson type common noise and mean field stopping problems with common noise.

Introduction

This paper is the continuation of
[
7
]
who derive an Itô and an Itô-Wentzell formulas for flows of conditional laws of continuous semimartingales. Our main objective here is to extend the same methodology to the general context of càdlàg semimartingales with possibly discontinuous flow of conditional marginals. One of the highlights of this work consists in its methodology, which is essentially based on the use of the linear functional derivative and Taylor expansions, in the same spirit as the proof of the traditional finite dimensional Itô formula.

Due to the rising interest for mean field games and related problems, independently introduced by
Lasry and Lions 2007

[
12
]
and
Huang et al. 2006

[
10
]
, chain rules for flows of marginals have received a strong attention over the past few years, and have been established through diverse methods. The most popular approach consists in establishing the formula for the
N
-particles approximation of the mean field system, and letting
N
go to infinity to derive the mean field Itô formula; see e.g.
Chassagneux et al. 2022

[
4
]
,
Buckdahn et al. 2017

[
1
]
or
Carmona and Delarue 2018a

[
2
,
3
]
. Another method relies on the density of cylindrical functions in the space of smooth functions, see
Guo et al. 2023

[
9
]
and
Guo and Zhang 2024

[
8
]
. In
[
13
]
,
Talbi et al. 2023a
exploit the properties of the linear functional derivative and the standard Itô formula to derive a general Itô formula for deterministic flows of càdlàg semimartingales.

In the context of random fields, we may mention the contribution of
Dos Reis and Platonov 2023

[
6
]
, who proved an Itô-Wentzell formula for continuous conditional flows of semimartingale measures by using the finite population approximation. The recent paper by
Jisheng and Jing 2025

[
11
]
proves this formula for general càdlàg semimartingales, relying on the approximation of the characteristics of the random field by cylindrical functions.

In the present paper, we show that the methodology of
[
13
]
and
[
7
]
can be used in the context of a càdlàg semimartingale
X
. More precisely, given a deterministic mapping
t\mapsto u(m_{t})
or a random field
t\mapsto U_{t}(m_{t})
,
m_{t}:=\mathbb{P}\circ X_{t}^{-1}
, and using a partition
\pi:=\{t_{0}:=0,t_{1},\dots,T_{N}:=T\}
of the time interval
[0,T]
, we rewrite the decomposition

u(m_{T})=\sum_{n=1}^{N}u(m_{t_{n}})-u(m_{t_{n-1}})

with the linear functional derivative
\delta_{m}u
to obtain Taylor-like expansions on each subinterval
(t_{n-1},t_{n}]
, which enables us to derive the formula by letting the step of the partition tend to
0
. Here, the main difficulty compared to the continuous case is that the remainder in Taylor expansions does not necessarily vanish as the step of the partition goes to
0
, due to the presence of jumps. We overcome this difficulty by adapting the proof of the finite dimensional generalized Itô’s formula to our context.

The paper is structured as follows. In Section
2
, we establish the general Itô formula for conditional flows of semimartingale measures. In Section
3
, we extend this formula to the case where the function of the flow of marginals is a random field, which corresponds to the Itô-Wentzell formula. Section
4
illustrates the usefulness of our results with two examples from mean field stochastic control problems, namely, mean field control with Poisson type common noise and mean field optimal stopping with common noise. Finally, Appendix
A
contains the proofs of important technical lemmas.

4.1
Mean field control with Poisson type common noise

Let
\Omega:=C^{0}([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{d})\times{\cal P}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R})
be the canonical space, and
(X,\Pi^{0})
be the corresponding canonical process. We denote by

\mathbb{F}^{0}:=\{{\cal F}_{t}^{0}\}_{\{0\leq t\leq T\}}

to

For a compact subset

Let

and

\mathbb{R}^{d}

{\cal W}_{1}
and
{\cal W}_{2}

For

{\rm{(ii)}}

and some

Our main interest is on the stochastic control problem:

\displaystyle V(t,\mu):=\sup_{\mathbb{P}\in{\cal P}(t,\mu)}\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{P}}\Big[\int_{t}^{T}f^{\alpha_{s}}\big(\Theta_{s}\big)ds+g\big(\mathbb{P}^{0}_{X_{T}}\big)\Big],

(4.1)

where the control
\alpha\in{\cal A}
is associated with
\mathbb{P}
accordingly to the definition above.

Let
{\rm ST}^{\mathbb{F}^{0}}_{[t,T]}
be the collection of all
\mathbb{F}^{0}-
stopping times with values in
[t,T]
. In this section, we assume that the following dynamic programming principle holds for all

\displaystyle V(t,\mu)=\sup_{\mathbb{P}\in{\cal P}(t,\mu)}\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{P}}\Big[\int_{t}^{\tau^{\mathbb{P}}}f^{\alpha_{s}}\big(\Theta_{s}\big)ds+V\big(\tau^{\mathbb{P}},\mathbb{P}_{X_{\tau^{\mathbb{P}}}}^{0}\big)\Big],

(4.2)

\displaystyle\mbox{for all family}\penalty\ (\tau^{\mathbb{P}})_{\mathbb{P}\in{\cal P}}\subset{\rm ST}^{\mathbb{F}^{0}}_{[t,T]},

see Djete, Possamaï & Tan
[
5
]
for specific conditions. Our goal is to derive the corresponding infinitesimal counterpart which reduces as standard to the so-called Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman (HJB) equation. This requires to introduce the following integro-differential operators which characterize the infinitesimal generator of the controlled process and which are defined for all map
\varphi:\mathbb{R}_{+}\times{\cal P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
with appropriate smoothness by:

\displaystyle{\cal L}^{a}\varphi:=b^{a}\!\cdot\!\partial_{x}\delta_{m}\varphi+\frac{1}{2}\sigma^{a}(\sigma^{a})^{\top}\!:\!\partial_{xx}^{2}\delta_{m}\varphi,

(4.3)

\displaystyle{\cal J}^{a}[\varphi](t,\mu,x):=\lambda^{a}(t,\mu,x)\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big\{\varphi\big(t,\mu\circ[{\mathbf{x}}+y\gamma^{a}(t,\mu,x)]^{-1}\big)-\varphi(t,\mu)\big\}\nu(dy),

is the identity map on

Proposition 4.1
.

Let (
4.2
) hold, and assume that
\partial_{t}V

\partial_{x}\delta_{m}V

Proof.

Supersolution property.
Let
\bar{\alpha}_{t}=a\in\mathbb{A}
be a constant control, and denote by
\bar{\mathbb{P}}
the corresponding element of
{\cal P}(t,\mu)
. Set
\bar{m}_{s}:=\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{X_{s}}^{0}

4.2
), we have:

V(t,\mu)\geq\mathbb{E}^{\bar{\mathbb{P}}}\Big[\int_{t}^{t+h}f^{\bar{\alpha}_{s}}\big(s,\bar{m}_{s},X_{s}\big)ds+V\big(t+h,\bar{m}_{t+h}\big)\Big].

By the same estimates as in Example
2.4

2.2

\frac{1}{h}\int_{t}^{t+h}\!\!\mathbb{E}^{\bar{\mathbb{P}}}\Big[-\partial_{t}V(s,\bar{m}_{s})-({\cal L}^{a}\delta_{m}V+f^{a})(s,\bar{m}_{s},X_{s})\Big]ds-\frac{1}{h}\mathbb{E}^{\bar{\mathbb{P}}}\Big[\sum_{0<s\leq t}\Delta V(s,\bar{m}_{s})\Big]\geq 0,\penalty\ \penalty\

(4.5)

where

and the operator

is defined in (

Now observe that:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\bar{\mathbb{P}}}\Big[\sum_{t<s\leq t+h}\Delta V(s,\bar{m}_{s})\Big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\bar{\mathbb{P}}}\Big[\int_{t}^{t+h}{\cal J}^{a}[V](s,\bar{m}_{s},X_{s})ds\Big],

where the integral operator

is defined in (

, using the fact that

is right continuous and that

Subsolution property.

Let
\tau^{\mathbb{P}}_{h}:=(t+h)\wedge\tau^{\mathbb{P}}
with
\tau^{\mathbb{P}}:=\inf\{s>t:{\cal W}_{2}(\mu_{s},\mu)\geq\delta\}
, for some
\delta>0
, and

from which we deduce, by Itô’s formula:

\displaystyle\inf_{\mathbb{P}\in{\cal P}(t,\mu)}\int_{t}^{\tau^{\mathbb{P}}_{h}}\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{P}}\Big[

\displaystyle-\!(\partial_{t}V\!-\!{\cal L}^{\alpha_{s}}\delta_{m}V\!-\!f^{\alpha_{s}})(s,\mu_{s},X_{s})\!-\!{\cal J}^{\alpha_{s}}[V](s,m_{s},X_{s-})\Big]ds\leq 0,

\displaystyle\inf_{\mu^{\prime}\in B_{2}(\mu,\delta)}{\cal H}(t,\mu^{\prime})\;=\;\lim_{h\searrow 0}\inf_{(s,\mu^{\prime})\in Q_{2}^{h,\delta}(t,\mu)}{\cal H}(s,\mu^{\prime})

Let

Technical Results

A.1
Proof of Lemma
2.3

(i) As in the previous proofs, we show the result in the one dimensional setting
d=1
. Observe that:

\sum_{n=1}^{N}H_{t_{n-1}}^{\pi}|\Delta^{\!\pi}\!X_{t_{n}}|^{2}=\sum_{n=1}^{N}H_{t_{n-1}}^{\pi}\Delta^{\!\pi}\!\big(X^{2}\big)_{t_{n}}-2\sum_{n=1}^{N}H_{t_{n-1}}^{\pi}X_{t_{n-1}}\Delta^{\!\pi}\!X_{t_{n}}.

We are then reduced to prove the
\mathbb{L}^{1}
convergence of integrals with respect to a semimartingale. Let us detail the argument for the first term in the right hand side equality. We prove the convergence of the finite variation part and the local martingale part independently. First observe that:

X^{2}=X_{0}^{2}+\tilde{V}+\tilde{M}\penalty\ \mbox{with}\penalty\ \tilde{V}_{t}:=2\int_{0}^{t}X_{s}dV_{s}+[M]_{t},\quad\tilde{M}_{t}:=2\int_{0}^{t}X_{s}dM_{s},\penalty\ t\geq 0.

Note that:

\Big|\sum_{n=1}^{N}H_{t_{n-1}}^{\pi}\Delta^{\pi}\tilde{V}_{t_{n}}-\int_{0}^{T}H_{s-}d\tilde{V}_{s}\Big|\leq\int_{0}^{T}\big|H_{t_{n-1}(s)}^{\pi}-H_{s-}\big|d|\tilde{V}|_{s}.

As
H_{t_{n-1}(s)}^{\pi}-H_{s-}\longrightarrow 0
a.s., the
\mathbb{L}^{1}-
convergence to zero of the last integral follows from the dominated convergence theorem due to the boundedness of
H
together with the observation that
\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|X_{t}|^{2}\in\mathbb{L}^{1}
by (
2.2
) and the BDG inequality, implying that:

|\tilde{V}|_{T}\leq 2\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|X_{t}||V|_{T}+[M]_{T}\leq\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|X_{t}|^{2}+|V|_{T}^{2}+[M]_{T}\in\mathbb{L}^{1}.

As for the martingale part, using BDG inequality, we have:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big|\sum_{n=1}^{N}H_{t_{n-1}}^{\pi}\Delta^{\pi}\tilde{M}_{t_{n}}-\int_{0}^{T}H_{s-}d\tilde{M}_{s}\Big|\Big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\big|H_{t_{n-1}(s)}^{\pi}-H_{s-}\big|^{2}d[\tilde{M}]_{s}\Big)^{1/2}\Big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\big|H_{t_{n-1}(s)}^{\pi}-H_{s-}\big|^{2}X_{s}^{2}d[M]_{s}\Big)^{1/2}\Big].

Again, since
H^{\pi}-H
is uniformly bounded, there exists a constant
C\geq 0
such that:

\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\big|H_{t_{n-1}(s)}^{\pi}-H_{s-}\big|^{2}X_{s}^{2}d[M]_{s}\Big)^{1/2}\leq C\Big(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}X_{t}^{2}[M]_{T}\Big)^{1/2}\in\mathbb{L}^{1}

by Cauchy-Schwarz inequality. We may then apply again the dominated convergence theorem to derive the desired result.

(ii) Denote
R_{n}^{\pi}:=\{f\}_{\theta_{n}^{0}}^{\theta_{n}^{r}}\Delta^{\!\pi}X_{t_{n}}\Delta^{\!\pi}\hat{X}_{t_{n}}
,
R_{s}:=\{f\}_{\theta_{s}^{0}}^{\theta_{s}^{r}}\Delta X_{s}\Delta\hat{X}_{s}
, and:

\bar{R}_{\pi}:=\sum_{n=1}^{N}R_{n}^{\pi},\ \bar{R}:=\sum_{0<s\leq T}R_{s}.

By continuity of
f
in its arguments,
f
is uniformly bounded on any bounded set of
{\cal P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
(where the first coordinate is measured with the 1-Wasserstein distance). Therefore, there exists a modulus of continuity
\rho
such that:

\displaystyle|R_{n}^{\pi}|\mathbf{1}_{\{|\xi_{n-1,n}|\leq 1\}}\leq\rho\big(\xi_{n}^{\pi}\big),\quad\mbox{with}\quad\xi_{n}^{\pi}:={\cal W}_{1}(m_{t_{n-1}},m_{t_{n}})+|\Delta^{\!\pi}\!X_{t_{n}}|+|\Delta^{\!\pi}\!\hat{X}_{t_{n}}|

(A.1)

Now given
\eta\in(0,1)
, we consider a continuous function
h_{\eta}:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to[0,1]
such that
h_{\eta}=1
on
[2\eta,\infty)
and
h_{\eta}=0
on
[0,\eta]
. First observe that, for
|\pi|
sufficiently small, the number of terms such that
|R_{n}^{\pi}|h_{\eta}(\xi_{n}^{\pi})\neq 0
is finite, as
\sum_{n=1}^{N}|\Delta^{\!\pi}X_{t_{n}}|\longrightarrow\sum_{s\leq T}|\Delta X_{s}|<\infty
a.s. as
\pi\to 0
by the summability conditions (
2.2
). Then it follows from the continuity of
h_{\eta}
that

\sum_{n=1}^{N}R_{n}^{\pi}h_{\eta}(\xi_{n}^{\pi})\\
\underset{|\pi|\to 0}{\longrightarrow}\sum_{0<s\leq T}R_{s}h_{\eta}(\xi_{s}),\quad\mbox{where}\quad\xi_{s}:={\cal W}_{1}(m_{s-},m_{s})+|\Delta X_{s}|+|\Delta\hat{X}_{s}|.

(A.2)

as these two sums are in fact finite a.s. As for the small jumps:

\displaystyle\Big\lvert\sum_{n=1}^{N}R_{n}^{\pi}[1-h_{\eta}(\xi_{n}^{\pi})]\Big\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\rho(\eta)\sum_{n=1}^{N}\big\lvert\Delta^{\!\pi}\!X_{t_{n}}\Delta^{\!\pi}\!\hat{X}_{t_{n}}\big\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\rho(\eta)({\rm QV}_{\!\pi}(X)+{\rm QV}_{\!\pi}(\hat{X})),

where, for any process
Y
, we write
{\rm QV}_{\!\pi}(Y):=\sum_{n=1}^{N}|\Delta^{\pi}Y_{t_{n}}|^{2}
.
As the sum on the right hand side converges in probability to the quadratic covariation of
X
and
\hat{X}
, and
\eta
may be chosen arbitrarily small, we deduce that the left hand side converges to
0
in probability as
|\pi|\to 0
. Finally, combining this result with (
A.2
), we obtain:

\displaystyle|\bar{R}_{\pi}\!-\!\bar{R}|

\displaystyle\hskip-5.69054pt\leq

\displaystyle\hskip-5.69054pt\big\lvert\sum_{n=1}^{N}R_{n}^{\pi}h_{\eta}(\xi_{n}^{\pi})-\!\!\sum_{0<s\leq T}\!\!R_{s}h_{\eta}(\xi_{s})\big\rvert+\frac{1}{2}\rho(\eta)({\rm QV}_{\!\pi}(X)\!+\!{\rm QV}_{\!\pi}(\hat{X}))+\!\sum_{0<s\leq T}\lvert R_{s}\rvert\mathbf{1}_{\{\xi_{s}\leq\eta\}}

\displaystyle\underset{|\pi|\to 0}{\longrightarrow}\frac{1}{2}\rho(\eta)\big([X]_{T}+[\hat{X}]_{T}\big)+\sum_{0<s\leq T}\lvert R_{s}\rvert\mathbf{1}_{\{\xi_{s}\leq\eta\}}\leq\rho(\eta)\big([X]_{T}+[\hat{X}]_{T}\big),

where the convergence holds in probability. Sending
\eta
to
0
, this shows that
|\bar{R}_{\pi}-\bar{R}|
converges to
0
in probability as
|\pi|\to 0
. Since
\bar{R}_{\pi}\leq\frac{1}{2}|f|_{\infty}\big({\rm QV}_{\!\pi}(X)\!+\!{\rm QV}_{\!\pi}(\hat{X})\big)
, which converges in
\mathbb{L}^{1}
by Lemma
2.3
(i), we see that the family
\{|\bar{R}_{\pi}-r|\}_{\pi}
is uniformly integrable, and therefore we deduce that
\bar{R}_{\pi}\longrightarrow\bar{R}
in
\mathbb{L}^{1}
.

A.2
Proof of Lemma
3.4

(i) We first decompose:

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{N}\big\{F(m_{t_{n-1}},.)\big\}^{t_{n},X_{t_{n-1},t_{n}}^{\lambda}}_{t_{n-1},X_{t_{n-1}}}|\Delta^{\!\pi}\!X_{t_{n}}|^{2}=\bar{R}_{\pi}+\bar{S}_{\pi},

where:

\displaystyle\bar{R}_{\pi}:=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\big\{F_{t_{n}}(m_{t_{n-1}},\cdot)\big\}_{X_{t_{n-1}}}^{X_{t_{n-1},t_{n}}^{\lambda}}|\Delta^{\!\pi}\!X_{t_{n}}|^{2},\quad\bar{S}_{\pi}:=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\big\{F_{\cdot}(m_{t_{n-1}},X_{t_{n-1}})\big\}_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}|\Delta^{\!\pi}\!X_{t_{n}}|^{2}.

We shall prove that the following convergence results hold:

\displaystyle\bar{R}_{\pi}\underset{|\pi|\to 0}{\longrightarrow}\bar{R}:=\sum_{0<s\leq t}\big\{F_{s}(m_{s-},\cdot)\big\}_{X_{s-}}^{X_{s}^{\lambda}}|\Delta X_{s}|^{2},\penalty\ \penalty\ \mbox{in}\penalty\ \mathbb{L}^{1}(\mathbb{P}_{|{\cal F}_{T}^{0},A,N}),

(A.3)

\displaystyle\bar{S}_{\pi}\underset{|\pi|\to 0}{\longrightarrow}\bar{S}:=\sum_{0<s\leq t}\big\{F_{\cdot}(m_{s-},X_{s-})\big\}_{s-}^{s}|\Delta X_{s}|^{2},\penalty\ \penalty\ \mbox{in}\penalty\ \mathbb{L}^{1}(\mathbb{P}_{|{\cal F}_{T}^{0},A,N}),

(A.4)

where
\mathbb{P}_{|{\cal F}_{T}^{0},A,N}
denotes the conditional distribution of the canonical process given
{\cal F}_{T}^{0},A
and
N
.

•

Convergence of
\bar{R}_{\pi}
. For
\eta\in(0,1)
, using the function
h_{\eta}
introduced in the proof of Lemma
2.3
, we have for some (random) modulus of continuity
\rho
:

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{N}|\Delta^{\!\pi}\!X_{t_{n}}|^{2}\big\{F_{t_{n-1}}(m_{t_{n-1}},\cdot)\big\}_{X_{t_{n-1}}}^{X_{t_{n-1},t_{n}}^{\lambda}}[1-h_{\eta}(|\Delta^{\!\pi}\!X_{t_{n}}|)]\leq\rho(\eta)\sum_{n=1}^{N}|\Delta^{\!\pi}\!X_{t_{n}}|^{2}.

Similarly, we have:

\sum_{0<s\leq T}|\Delta X_{s}|^{2}\big\{F_{s-}(m_{s-},\cdot)\big\}_{X_{s-}}^{X_{s}^{\lambda}}[1-h_{\eta}(|\Delta X_{s}|)]\leq\rho(\eta)\sum_{0<s\leq T}|\Delta X_{s}|^{2}.

By the summability conditions on
X
, the number of
s\in[0,T]
such that
|\Delta X_{s}|\geq\eta
is finite, and therefore, as both sums are finite,

\displaystyle\Big|\sum_{n=1}^{N}|\Delta^{\!\pi}\!X_{t_{n}}|^{2}

\displaystyle\big\{F_{t_{n-1}}(m_{t_{n-1}},\cdot)\big\}_{X_{t_{n-1}}}^{X_{t_{n-1},t_{n}}^{\lambda}}h_{\eta}(|\Delta^{\!\pi}\!X_{t_{n}}|)

\displaystyle-\sum_{0<s\leq T}|\Delta X_{s}|^{2}\big\{F_{s-}(m_{s-},\cdot)\big\}_{X_{s-}}^{X_{s}^{\lambda}}h_{\eta}(|\Delta X_{s}|)\Big|\;\underset{|\pi|\to 0}{\longrightarrow}\;0,\ \mathbb{P}-\mbox{a.s.}

By the same reasoning as in the proof of Lemma
2.3
(ii), we deduce that
\bar{R}_{\pi}
converges to
\bar{R}
in probability. Moreover, (
3.1
) implies that
F
is bounded by a random variable
C
measurable with respect to
{\cal F}_{T}^{0}\vee\sigma(A_{\cdot\wedge T},N_{\cdot\wedge T})
. Since
\mathrm{QV}_{\pi}(X)
and
\mathrm{QV}(X)
are uniformly integrable, we deduce that
\bar{R}_{\pi}
and
\bar{R}
are uniformly integrable for the conditional expectation
\mathbb{E}^{0}
, and therefore that (
A.3
) holds.

•

Convergence of
\bar{S}_{\pi}
. Let
F^{c}
and
F^{a}
respectively denote the continuous and atomic parts of
F
. By pathwhise continuity of
t\mapsto F_{t}^{c}
on the compact
[0,T]
, there exists a (random) modulus of continuity such that:

\displaystyle\Big|\sum_{n=1}^{N}|\Delta^{\!\pi}\!X_{t_{n}}|^{2}

\displaystyle\big\{F_{\cdot}^{c}(m_{t_{n-1}},X_{t_{n-1}})\big\}_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}

\displaystyle-\sum_{0<s\leq T}|\Delta\!X_{s}|^{2}\big\{F_{\cdot}^{c}(m_{s-},X_{s-})\big\}_{s-}^{s}\Big|\leq\rho(|\pi|)\Big(\mathrm{QV}_{\pi}(X)+\mathrm{QV}(X)\Big).

Furthermore, we have:

\displaystyle\Big|\sum_{n=1}^{N}|\Delta^{\!\pi}\!X_{t_{n}}|^{2}\big\{F_{\cdot}^{a}(m_{t_{n-1}},X_{t_{n-1}})\big\}_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}-\sum_{0<s\leq T}|\Delta\!X_{s}|^{2}\big\{F_{\cdot}^{a}(m_{s-},X_{s-})\big\}_{s-}^{s}\Big|\underset{|\pi|\to 0}{\longrightarrow}0,\quad\mbox{a.s.}

Therefore, we have
\bar{S}_{\pi}\to\bar{S}
, a.s., as
|\pi|\to 0
. As in the case of
\bar{R}_{\pi}
, the sequence
\bar{S}_{\pi}
is uniformly integrable for the conditional expectation
\mathbb{E}^{0}
, which implies that
(
A.4
) holds.

(ii) Observe that:

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{N}\big\{\hat{F}_{.}(\cdot))\big\}^{t_{n},\theta_{n}^{\lambda}}_{t_{n-1},\theta_{n}^{\bm{0}}}|\Delta^{\!\pi}\!X_{t_{n}}|^{2}=\bar{R}_{\pi}+\bar{S}_{\pi},

where:

\displaystyle\bar{R}_{\pi}:=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\big\{\hat{F}_{t_{n}}(\cdot)\big\}_{\theta_{n}^{\bm{0}}}^{\theta_{n}^{\lambda}}|\Delta^{\!\pi}\!X_{t_{n}}|^{2},\quad\bar{S}_{\pi}:=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\big\{\hat{F}_{\cdot}(\theta_{n}^{\bm{0}})\big\}_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}|\Delta^{\!\pi}\!X_{t_{n}}|^{2}.

We need to prove that:

\displaystyle\bar{R}_{\pi}\underset{|\pi|\to 0}{\longrightarrow}\bar{R}:=\sum_{0<s\leq t}\big\{\hat{F}_{s}(\cdot)\big\}_{\theta_{s}^{\bm{0}}}^{\theta_{s}^{\lambda}}|\Delta X_{s}|^{2},\penalty\ \penalty\ \mbox{in}\penalty\ \mathbb{L}^{1}(\mathbb{P}_{|{\cal F}_{T}^{0},A,N}),

(A.5)

\displaystyle\bar{S}_{\pi}\underset{|\pi|\to 0}{\longrightarrow}\bar{S}:=\sum_{0<s\leq t}\big\{\hat{F}_{\cdot}(\theta_{s}^{\bm{0}})\big\}_{s-}^{s}|\Delta X_{s}|^{2},\penalty\ \penalty\ \mbox{in}\penalty\ \mathbb{L}^{1}(\mathbb{P}_{|{\cal F}_{T}^{0},A,N}).

(A.6)

(
A.5
) follows from the same arguments as the convergence of
\bar{R}_{\pi}
in (i), replacing
|\Delta^{\pi}X_{t_{n}}|
with

\xi_{n}^{\pi}:={\cal W}_{1}(m_{t_{n-1}},m_{t_{n}})+|\Delta^{\!\pi}\!X_{t_{n}}|+|\Delta^{\!\pi}\!\hat{X}_{t_{n}}|

as argument of the function
h_{\eta}
, and using the fact that
\hat{f}
and
\hat{g}
are continuous with respect to both distances
{\cal W}_{2}
and
{\cal W}_{1}
to guarantee the existence of a modulus of continuity
\rho
. (

A note on vertex-critical induced subgraphs of shift graphs
Tomáš Kaiser, Matěj Stehlík, Riste Škrekovski

Abstract
Shift graphs, introduced by Erdős and Hajnal in 1964, form one of the simplest known non-recursive constructions of triangle-free graphs with arbitrarily large chromatic number. In this note, we identify a suprising property: for each integer k ≥ 1 k\geq 1 , the smallest k k -chromatic shift graph contains a unique k k -vertex-critical subgraph. We give an explicit description of this subgraph and prove its uniqueness. This provides a new and remarkably simple family of triangle-free vertex-critical graphs of arbitrarily large chromatic number.

Introduction

The study of graphs that are simultaneously highly chromatic and
sparse—typically having large (odd) girth—has had a profound
influence on graph theory. It has played a central role in the
development of both the probabilistic method
[
4
]
and the
topological method
[
8
]
. For background on this rich line of
research, see the surveys
[
6
,
11
,
13
,
15
,
16
]
.

The earliest constructions of triangle-free graphs with arbitrarily
large chromatic number, due to Tutte (writing under an alias)
[
1
,
2
]
, Zykov
[
17
]
, Kelly and Kelly
[
7
]
, and
Mycielski
[
10
]
, were all recursive. The first non-recursive
constructions were shift graphs
[
3
]
, which are the focus of
this note, and Kneser graphs
[
8
]
.

For
N\geq 5
, the
shift graph

G_{N,2}
is defined as follows.
The vertices are ordered pairs
(x,y)
with integers
1\leq x<y\leq N
, and two vertices
(x,y)
and
(y,z)
are adjacent whenever
x<y<z
. Erdős and Hajnal
[
3
]
and, independently, Harner and
Entringer
[
5
]
proved that
G_{N,2}
is triangle-free and

\chi(G_{N,2})=\lceil\log_{2}N\rceil.

See also
[
9
, Problem 9.26]
and
[
14
]
.

The chromatic number increases precisely at the values
N=2^{n}+1
.
Indeed, whenever
2^{n}+2\leq N\leq 2^{n+1}
, the graph
G_{N,2}
contains multiple induced copies of
G_{2^{n}+1,2}
. Thus, to
establish
\chi(G_{N,2})\geq\lceil\log_{2}N\rceil
, it suffices to
prove
\chi(G_{2^{n}+1,2})\geq n+1
.

A graph is
k
-vertex-critical
if it is
k
-chromatic and
deleting any vertex reduces its chromatic number to
k-1
.
Vertex-critical graphs are central objects in graph coloring, as
structural questions about all
k
-chromatic graphs often reduce to
the vertex-critical case.

Our main result shows that each shift graph
G_{2^{n}+1,2}
contains
a
unique
induced
(n+1)
-vertex-critical subgraph, which we
describe explicitly. This phenomenon is in sharp contrast with Kneser
graphs
\mathrm{KG}(n,k)
, which contain
(n-1)!/2
isomorphic copies
of the vertex-critical Schrijver graph
\mathrm{SG}(n,k)

[
12
]
, along with additional non-isomorphic vertex-critical
subgraphs.

, let

.

We are now ready to state the main theorem.

Theorem 1
.

For every integer
n\geq 2
, the shift graph
G_{2^{n}+1,2}
contains a unique induced
(n+1)
-vertex-critical subgraph,
namely
G_{2^{n}+1,2}[W]
.

2
Proof of Theorem
1

Fix an integer
n\geq 2
, and let us denote the shift graph
G_{2^{n},2}
by

G
. Thus,
G
has chromatic number
\chi(G)=n+1
. Theorem
1
will follow from Theorems
3
and
4
below.

Let
a=(a_{1},\dots,a_{2^{n}+1})
be a sequence of elements of the set
S(n)
of
all subsets of
[1,n]
, and let
X
be a subset of the vertex set
V(G)
. Recall that the elements of
X
are ordered pairs
(x,y)
of integers
satisfying
1\leq x<y\leq 2^{n}+1
. We say that
a
is
X
-good
if
for each
i,j
such that
1\leq i<j\leq 2^{n}+1
,

a_{i}\not\subseteq a_{j}\text{ whenever $(i,j)\in X$.}

The subgraph of
G
induced by the set
X
is denoted by
G[X]
.

The following proposition characterises induced
n
-colourable
subgraphs of
G
in terms of
X
-good sequences.

Proposition 2
.

For
X\subseteq V(G)
,
\chi(G[X])\leq n
if and only if there
exists an
X
-good sequence.

Proof.

This is essentially the Erdős–Hajnal argument. Suppose that there
exists an
n
-colouring
c
of
G[X]
. For
i\in[1,2^{n}+1]
, let

c_{i}=\left\{{c((i,j))}:\,{i<j\leq 2^{n}+1\text{ and }(i,j)\in X}\right\}.

Note that
c_{2^{n}+1}=\emptyset
. Furthermore,

c_{1},\dots,c_{2^{n}+1}
is
X
-good, since if
(i,j)\in X
, then

c((i,j))\in c_{i}
while
c((i,j))\notin c_{j}
because of the
adjacency between
(i,j)
and the vertex
(j,k)
for each
k
such
that
j<k\leq 2^{n}+1
. Thus,
c((i,j))
testifies that

c_{i}\not\subseteq c_{j}
.

On the other hand, given an
X
-good sequence

(a_{1},\dots,a_{2^{n}+1})
, we colour each vertex
(i,j)\in X
with an
arbitrary colour from the nonempty set
a_{i}\setminus a_{j}
.
∎

The following theorem shows that the critical vertices of
G
are
precisely the vertices in
W
.

Theorem 3
.

For
v\in V(G)
,
\chi(G-v)<\chi(G)
if and only if
v\in W
.

Proof.

Suppose that
G-v
has an
n
-colouring
c
. We want to show that

v\in W
. By Proposition
2
, there is a
W
-good sequence

(a_{1},\dots,a_{2^{n}+1})
. Since there are only
2^{n}
subsets of

\left\{{1,\dots,n}\right\}
, we have
a_{i}=a_{j}
for some

1\leq i<j\leq 2^{n}+1
. By the definition of an
X
-good sequence,
we must have
(i,j)=v
. Furthermore, with the exception of the
equality
a_{i}=a_{j}
, all the elements of the sequence are pairwise
distinct. Hence, each subset of
[1,n]
appears in the
sequence.

Let us write
\ell=\left|a_{i}\right|
. There are
2^{\ell}-1
proper
subsets of
a_{i}
, each of which must appear among

a_{j+1},\dots,a_{2^{n}+1}
, by the definition of
W
-good
sequence. Similarly, each of the
2^{n-\ell}-1
proper supersets of

a_{i}
appears among
a_{1},\dots,a_{i-1}
. Hence,
i\geq 2^{n-\ell}

and
j\leq 2^{n}-2^{\ell}+2
, which implies that
i,j\in I_{n-\ell}

and therefore
(i,j)\in W
.

Conversely, assume that
v=(i,j)\in W
— say,
i,j\in I_{r}
for
some
r
,
0\leq r\leq n
. We construct a
(W-v)
-good sequence
which will show that
\chi(G-v)=n
. Let
A=\left\{{1,\dots,n-r}\right\}
. We set
a_{i}=a_{j}=A
. Since
i\geq 2^{r}
, we
can position all proper supersets of
A
into the subsequence

a_{1},\dots,a_{i-1}
. To obtain a
(W-v)
-good sequence in the end,
we do it in such a way that the size of the sets of the sequence is
non-increasing. Since
j\leq 2^{n}-2^{n-r}+2
, all proper subsets
can be placed in
a_{j+1},\dots,a_{2^{n}+1}
(in a similar
non-increasing way). Finally, we put the subsets of
[1,n]
which do
not yet appear in the sequence in the places left free so far. The
only required property is that, again, the size of these sets is
non-increasing; there are no restrictions on the position relative
to
A
or its subsets and supersets. It is not hard to check that
for any ordering satisfying the above requirements, we obtain a

(W-v)
-good sequence, which implies that
\chi(G-v)=n
.
∎

It remains to show that
W
induces a
(n+1)
-chromatic subgraph of
G
.

Theorem 4
.

\chi(G[W])=\chi(G)=n+1.

Proof.

By contradiction. Consider a
W
-good sequence

(a_{1},\dots,a_{2^{n}+1})
. We first modify the sequence to another

W
-good sequence using the following procedure. If there is an
index
r
such that some proper subset
a^{\prime}_{r}
of
a_{r}
is not
included among
a_{r+1},\dots,a_{2^{n}+1}
, let
s
be the largest
such index and replace
a_{s}
(at position
s
) in the sequence with

a^{\prime}_{s}
. We prove that the resulting sequence is
W
-good. Suppose
not. Since
a^{\prime}_{s}\subseteq a_{s}
, there cannot be
i<s
such that

(i,s)\in W
and
a_{i}\subseteq a^{\prime}_{s}
as this would contradict the

W
-goodness of the original sequence. Thus, let us consider
j>s

such that
(s,j)\in W
. In this case,
a^{\prime}_{s}
cannot be a subset of

a_{j}
, because it would be a proper subset (by the choice of
s
),
so by the maximality of
s
, it would be included among

a_{j+1},\dots,a_{2^{n}+1}
, contradicting the choice of
s
. Thus,
the resulting sequence is
W
-good, as claimed.

Relabel the new element
a^{\prime}_{s}
to
a_{s}
and repeat the above step
for as long as it is possible, and then terminate. Clearly, the
algorithm only takes a finite number of steps and terminates with a

W
-good sequence
a_{1},\dots,a_{2^{n}+1}
with the following property:

For each
i\in[1,2^{n}+1]
and every proper subset
b\subset a_{i}
,
there is
j\in[i+1,2^{n}+1]
such that
a_{j}=b
.

Observe that, as a
consequence,
a_{2^{n}+1}=\emptyset
. Furthermore, for each

i\in[1,2^{n}+1]
,

\text{if }\left|a_{i}\right|=k,\text{ then }i\leq 2^{n}-2^{k}+2,

(1)

since there has to be space to the right of
a_{i}
for
2^{k}-1

proper subsets of
a_{i}
. Note also that the right hand side in the
inequality of (
1
) is the right end of the interval
I_{n-k}
.

Since the number of subsets of
[1,n]
is
2^{n}
while the length of

[1,2^{n}+1]
is
2^{n}+1
, there must exist a pair
i,j\in[1,2^{n}+1]

such that:

i<j
and
a_{i}=a_{j}
; moreover, we choose this pair
so that
i
is as large as possible.

Let us write
x=\left|a_{i}\right|
.

No interval among
I_{0},\dots,I_{n}
contains both
i
and
j
, for
otherwise the pair
(i,j)
would belong to
W
(by the definition of

W
), and the fact that
a_{i}=a_{j}
would contradict the
W
-goodness
of the sequence.

Consequently, if we set

\displaystyle p

\displaystyle=\max\left\{{\ell}:\,{i\in I_{\ell}}\right\},

\displaystyle q

\displaystyle=\min\left\{{\ell}:\,{j\in I_{\ell}}\right\},

then
p<q
. Note that since
I_{q}\setminus I_{q-1}
contains an
element of size
x
(namely,
a_{j}
), (
1
) implies that
q\leq n-x
and
therefore

p+1\leq n-x.

(2)

Consider the interval
J=[i+1,2^{n}+1]
. No two elements of the
subsequence
a_{i+1},\dots,a_{2^{n}+1}
are identical. We will show
that there are not enough possible values for these elements when
the sequence is
W
-good.

Note that no element
i^{*}
of
J\cap I_{p}
has the property that

a_{i^{*}}
is a superset of
a_{i}

(by
W
-goodness) while no element of
J\setminus I_{p}
(i.e., no

a_{k}
with
k>2^{n}-2^{n-p}+2
) has
n-p
elements or more
by (
1
).

It follows that for
k\in J
and
b=a_{k}
,

a_{i}\not\subseteq b,\text{ or }\left|b\right|<n-p.

(3)

Let
B
be the set of sets
b\subset[1,n]

satisfying (
3
). We have

\left|B\right|=(2^{n}-2^{n-x})+\sum_{k=0}^{n-p-1-x}\binom{n-x}{k},

with the term in brackets expressing the number of subsets of

[1,n]
which are not supersets of
a_{i}
, and the sum expressing the
number of supersets of
a_{i}
with fewer than
n-p

elements. Rearranging, we get

\left|B\right|=2^{n}-\sum_{k=0}^{p}\binom{n-x}{k}.

We will be able to reach the desired contradiction if we can show
that
\left|B\right|<\left|J\right|
, since each
a_{k}
with
k\in J
is a set
from
B
and there is no repetition. Since
\left|J\right|=2^{n}+1-i
, this
amounts to showing that

i\leq\sum_{k=0}^{p}\binom{n-x}{k}.

(4)

We know that
i\in I_{p}\setminus I_{p+1}
, and therefore
i\leq 2^{p+1}-1
. Hence, (
4
) will follow if we can show

2^{p+1}-1\leq\sum_{k=0}^{p}\binom{n-x}{k}.

(5)

This is what we prove now. First of all, recall that
p+1\leq n-x

by (
2
). In case of equality, (
5
) follows (with
equality) from the binomial theorem. If we increase
n-x
, the right
hand side of (
5
) clearly increases while the left hand side
remains constant. This proves (
5
) and hence
also (
4
), concluding the proof of Theorem
4
.
∎

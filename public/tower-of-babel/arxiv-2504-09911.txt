Higher Chow cycles on Eisenstein K ​ 3 K3 surfaces
Ken Sato

Abstract
. We construct higher Chow cycles of type ( 2 , 1 ) (2,1) on some families of K ​ 3 K3 surfaces with non-symplectic automorphisms of order 3 and prove that our cycles are indecomposable for very general members. The proof is a combination of some degeneration arguments, and explicit computations of the regulator map.



1.
Introduction

Higher Chow groups
{\mathrm{CH}}^{p}(X,q)
of smooth varieties
X
introduced by Bloch are a generalization of the classical algebraic cycles.
They are related to many important invariants in algebraic geometry,
K
-theory, and number theory.
However, when the codimension
p
is greater than 1, its structure is still mysterious.
In the papers
[
MS23
]
and
[
Sat24-2
]
, we explicitly construct higher Chow cycles of type
(2,1)
on
K3
surfaces with non-symplectic automorphisms of order
2
and
4
, respectively.
In these papers, it turned out that non-symplectic automorphisms on
K3
surfaces can play an effective role for explicit constructions of
(2,1)
-cycles.

In this paper, we consider the case of order 3.
A pair
(X,\sigma)
of a
K3
surface
X
and a non-symplectic automorphism
\sigma
of order
3
is called an
Eisenstein K3 surface
.
By the classification theory (
[
AS08
]
,
[
Tak11
]
and
[
DK07
]
), each component of the moduli space is labelled by a pair
(r,a)
of natural numbers, and the corresponding component
{\mathcal{M}}_{r,a}
is a Zariski open subset of a quotient of a complex ball by a unitary group.
The invariant
r
is even number, and
(r,a)
satisfies the inequalities
a\leq(r+2)/2
and
a\leq(22-r)/2
except for one case.
(See Figure
1
.)

Here we focus on Eisenstein
K3
surfaces on the extremal lines
a=(r+2)/2
for
r\leq 10
and
a=(22-r)/2
for
r\geq 12
in this paper.
For each such
(r,a)
, we construct an explicit
(2,1)
-cycle
\xi
on the members
(X,\sigma)
of
{\mathcal{M}}_{r,a}
.
Then we prove the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

For a very general
(X,\sigma)
, the indecomposable parts of the
(2,1)
-cycles
\xi
and
\sigma_{*}(\xi)
are non-torsion and linearly independent.
In particular, the rank of the indecomposable parts of
{\mathrm{CH}}^{2}(X,1)
is equal or greater than
2
.

Here, the
indecomposable part
of
{\mathrm{CH}}^{2}(X,1)
is defined as the quotient of
{\mathrm{CH}}^{2}(X,1)
by
\mathrm{Pic}(X)\otimes{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
, and the indecomposable part of a
(2,1)
-cycle is its image in the indecomposable part.

For the construction of cycles, we use the model of the Eisenstein
K3
surfaces as triple covers of
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
branching along nodal curves
B
of bidegree
(3,3)
.
The first example of such construction is given by Kondō (
[
Kon02
]
), and generalized in
[
MOT15
]
.
Let
F_{1}
(resp.
F_{2}
) be a divisor of type
(1,0)
(resp.
(0,1)
) on
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
which intersect with
B
at a point with multiplicity
2
.
Then the pull-back
C_{i}
of
F_{i}
is a rational curve on the Eisenstein
K3
surfaces, and
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
intersect at 3 points.
We can construct a
(2,1)
-cycle from such configuration of rational curves.
In general, cyclic cover model provides a convenient source for constructing
(2,1)
-cycles.
See
[
Sre14
]
,
[
Sat24
]
,
[
MS23
]
,
[
Sre24
]
,
[
Sat24-2
]
and
[
Sre24-2
]
for similar methods in other cases.

The proof of indecomposability is divided into two steps.
The first part is a degeneration argument which reduces the general case of Theorem
1.1
to the starting case
r=18
.
The second part is an explicit calculation of the
Abel-Jacobi map
in the case
r=18
to show that the
(2,1)
-cycle is indecomposable for this case.

A key device of the first part is the Eisenstein Jacobian
J(X,\sigma)
, which is a generalized complex torus associated with the Eisenstein lattice of
(X,\sigma)
, together with corresponding Abel-Jacobi map

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\nu_{E}:{\mathrm{CH}}^{2}(X,1)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces J(X,\sigma).}

(1)

The advantages of using Eisenstein Jacobian are that it behaves continuously in a family of Eisenstein
K3
surfaces and it can be used to detect indecomposability for very general cases.
We prove a degeneration lemma (Lemma
6.1
), which enables us to reduce the Theorem
1.1
to some inductive arguments on
r
as in
[
MS23
]
.
The technical novelty of this paper is that this degeneration lemma can be applied to the wider class of degenerations than those of
[
MS23
]
.

The indecomposablity of starting case (
r=18
) is proved by the direct calculation of the regulator map.
In this case, the image of our
(2,1)
-cycles by the regulator map is expressed by the “incomplete integral” considered in
[
MT19
]
.
This integral expression enables us to prove that the normal function
G(\lambda)
associated with our cycles satisfies the following
inhomogeneous hypergeometric differential equation
:

\lambda(1-\lambda)\dfrac{d^{2}G}{d\lambda^{2}}+\left(1-\frac{7}{3}\lambda\right)\dfrac{dG}{d\lambda}-\frac{4}{9}G(\lambda)=\frac{1-\zeta}{\lambda-1}.

(2)

If the right-hand side of (
2
) is zero, this is the Gauss hypergeometric differential equation of type
(2/3,2/3,1)
.
Since the periods of the Eisenstein
K3
surfaces of the case
r=18
satisfies the Gauss hypergeometric differential equation of type
(2/3,2/3,1)
, the normal function
G(\lambda)
and period functions are linearly independent.
This implies that our cycles are indecomposable for very general cases when
r=18
, and we complete the proofs.
The function
G(\lambda)
is an example of special functions related to hypergeometric functions appearing as normal functions of families of
(2,1)
-cycles.

The structure of this paper is as follows.
In Section 2, we recall basic results on Eisenstein
K3
surfaces and higher Chow cycles.
In Section 3, we define the Eisenstein Jacobian
J(X,\sigma)
and prove that we can detect indecomposable cycles via the Eisenstein Jacobian in very general cases.
In Section 4, we introduce the cyclic covering construction of Eisenstein
K3
surfaces and explain the construction of our
(2,1)
-cycles.
In Section 5, we define a dominant family
{\mathcal{X}}\rightarrow\hat{U}_{r}
of Eisenstein
K3
surfaces of type
(r,a)
.
In Section 6, we prove the degeneration lemma, which is the crucial for our induction argument.
Then we state the main theorem, and run the induction part of the proof.
In section 7, we calculate the image of our
(2,1)
-cycles under the regulator map for the starting case of the induction, and complete the proof of the main theorem.

Conventions

Throughout this paper, we fix a primitive 3rd root of unity
\zeta=\exp\left(2\pi\sqrt{-1}/3\right)
.
“A very general point” on analytic varieties means points on the complement of a union of countably many analytic subsets.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we recall some properties of Eisenstein
K3

2.1.
Eisenstein
K3
surfaces

Let
X
be a complex
K3
surface and
\sigma\in{\mathrm{Aut}}(X)
be an automorphism of order 3 such that
\sigma^{*}\omega=\zeta\omega
where
\omega
is a non-zero holomorphic 2-form on
X
.
We call such a pair
(X,\sigma)
an
Eisenstein K3 surface
.
For an Eisenstein
K3
surface
(X,\sigma)
, let
H^{2}(X,{\mathbb{C}})=H_{1}\oplus H_{\zeta}\oplus H_{\zeta^{2}}
be the eigenspace decomposition with respect to
\sigma^{*}
.
We define the sublattices
L(X,\sigma)
and
E(X,\sigma)
of
H^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Z}})
as follows:

\displaystyle L(X,\sigma)=H_{1}\cap H^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Z}})=H^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Z}})^{\sigma^{*}}

(3)

\displaystyle E(X,\sigma)=(H_{\zeta}\oplus H_{\zeta^{2}})\cap H^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Z}}).

The lattice
E(X,\sigma)
is the orthogonal complement of
L(X,\sigma)
in
H^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Z}})
.
By the
\sigma^{*}
-action on
H^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Z}})
, the Eisenstein integer ring
{\mathbb{Z}}[\zeta]
naturally acts on
E(X,\sigma)
.
We have the natural Hermitian form
h
on
E(X,\sigma)
defined by
h(x,y)=\langle x,y\rangle+\zeta\langle x,\zeta y\rangle+\zeta^{2}\langle x,\zeta^{2}y\rangle\in{\mathbb{Z}}[\zeta]
for
x,y\in E(X,\sigma)
.
Since
[\omega]\in H^{2}(X,{\mathbb{C}})
is contained in
H_{\zeta}=E(X,\sigma)\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}[\zeta]}{\mathbb{C}}
, we have

L(X,\sigma)\subset{\mathrm{NS}}(X).

(4)

For an Eisenstein
K3
surface
(X,\sigma)
, the invariants
r,a
are defined so that the following equations holds.

r={\mathrm{rank}\>}L(X,\sigma),\quad E(X,\sigma)^{\vee}/E(X,\sigma)\simeq({\mathbb{Z}}/3{\mathbb{Z}})^{\oplus a}.

(5)

The signature of
E(X,\sigma)
is
(2,20-r)
.
By
[
AS08
]
(Theorem 3.3) or
[
Tak11
]
(Lemma 2.3), we know that the possible invariants
(r,a)
are shown in the Figure
1
.
Furthermore, for each
(r,a)
in Figure
1
, the isomorphism class of
E(X,\sigma)
(as Eisenstein lattices with Hermitian forms) is uniquely determined.
Hereafter
(E_{r,a},h)
denotes the abstract Eisenstein lattice with Hermitian form which is isomorphic to
E(X,\sigma)
.
Then there exists the unique primitive embedding of
E_{r,a}
in the
K3
lattice
\Lambda_{K3}=U^{\oplus 3}\oplus E_{8}^{\oplus 2}
.
In this paper, we treat the following cases.

a=\frac{r+2}{2}\text{ for }r\leq 10\quad\text{ or }\quad a=\frac{22-r}{2}\text{ for }12\leq r\leq 18.

(r,a)
and the cases we treat in this paper

By the Torelli theorem for
K3
surfaces, there exists the coarse moduli space
{\mathcal{M}}_{r,a}
of Eisenstein
K3
surfaces of type
(r,a)
(
[
DK07
]
Theorem 11.2).
We briefly recall its construction.
Since
E_{r,a}
has the unique primitive embedding in
\Lambda_{K3}
, for any marking
\alpha:H^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Z}})\xrightarrow{\>\sim\>}\Lambda_{K3}
,
\alpha
induces the isometry
E(X,\sigma)\xrightarrow{\>\sim\>}E_{r,a}
.
Since
[\omega]\in E(X,\sigma)\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}[\zeta]}{\mathbb{C}}
,
E_{r,a}\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}[\zeta]}{\mathbb{C}}
is the period domain.
Let
{\mathcal{B}}=\{[v]\in{\mathbb{P}}(E_{r,a}\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}[\zeta]}{\mathbb{C}}):\langle v,\overline{v}\rangle>0\}
.
Since the signature of
E_{r,a}
is
(2,20-r)
,
{\mathcal{B}}
is isomorphic to a
10-r/2
-dimensional complex ball (cf.
[
DK07
]
, Section 7).
Let
\Gamma
be the unitary group
U(E_{r,a},h)
over
{\mathbb{Z}}[\zeta]
.
The group
\Gamma
naturally acts on the period domain

properly discontinuously.
Then the moduli space
{\mathcal{M}}_{r,a}
is realized as a Zariski open subset of
{\mathcal{B}}/\Gamma
.
Since a very general point
[v]\in{\mathcal{B}}
satisfies that
\langle v,x\rangle\neq 0
for any
x\in E_{r,a}
, we have the following.

Proposition 2.1
.

For a very general members of
{\mathcal{M}}_{r,a}
, the corresponding Eisenstein
K3
surface
(X,\sigma)
satisfies
{\mathrm{NS}}(X)=L(X,\sigma)
.

2.2.
Higher Chow cycles

For a smooth variety
X
over
{\mathbb{C}}
, let
{\mathrm{CH}}^{p}(X,q)
be the higher Chow group defined by Bloch (
[
Blo86
]
).
In this paper, we consider the case
(p,q)=(2,1)
.
An element of
{\mathrm{CH}}^{2}(X,1)
is called a
(2,1)
-cycle
.
The higher Chow group
\mathrm{CH}^{2}(X,1)
is isomorphic to the middle homology group of the following complex. For the proof, see, e.g.,
[
Mül98
]
Corollary 5.3.

K_{2}^{\mathrm{M}}({\mathbb{C}}(X))\xrightarrow{T}\displaystyle\bigoplus_{C\in X^{(1)}}{\mathbb{C}}(C)^{\times}\xrightarrow{\mathrm{div}}\displaystyle\bigoplus_{p\in X^{(2)}}{\mathbb{Z}}\cdot p

Here

denotes the set of all irreducible closed subsets of
X
of codimension
r
.
The map
T
denotes the tame symbol map from the Milnor
K_{2}
-group of the function field
{\mathbb{C}}(X)
.
Therefore, each
(2,1)
-cycle is represented by a formal sum

\sum_{j}(C_{j},f_{j})\in\displaystyle\bigoplus_{C\in X^{(1)}}{\mathbb{C}}(C)^{\times}

(6)

where
C_{j}
are prime divisors on
X
and
f_{j}\in{\mathbb{C}}(C_{j})^{\times}
are non-zero rational functions on them such that
\sum_{j}{\mathrm{div}}_{C_{j}}(f_{j})=0
as codimension 2 cycles on
X
.

The intersection product on higher Chow groups induces the following map.

\mathrm{Pic}(X)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\Gamma(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\times})={\mathrm{CH}}^{1}(X)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}{\mathrm{CH}}^{1}(X,1)\longrightarrow\mathrm{CH}^{2}(X,1)

(7)

The image of this map is called the

of
\mathrm{CH}^{2}(X,1)
and is denoted by
{\mathrm{CH}}^{2}(X,1)_{\mathrm{dec}}
.
A

is an element of
{\mathrm{CH}}^{2}(X,1)_{\mathrm{dec}}
.
The quotient
{\mathrm{CH}}^{2}(X,1)/{\mathrm{CH}}^{2}(X,1)_{\mathrm{dec}}
is called the
indecomposable part
of
{\mathrm{CH}}^{2}(X,1)
and is denoted by
\mathrm{CH}^{2}(X,1)_{\mathrm{ind}}
.
For a
(2,1)
-cycle
\xi
,
\xi_{\mathrm{ind}}
denotes its image in
\mathrm{CH}^{2}(X,1)_{\mathrm{ind}}
.

Let
C
be a prime divisor on
X
,
\alpha\in\Gamma(X,{\mathcal{O}}_{X}^{\times})
and
[C]\in\mathrm{Pic}(X)
be the class corresponding to
C
.
The image of
[C]\otimes\alpha
under (
7
) is represented by
(C,\alpha|_{C})
in the presentation (
6
).

2.3.
The regulator map

For a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-Hodge structure
H=(H_{\mathbb{Z}},F^{\bullet})
of weight 2, the generalized intermediate Jacobian of
H
is defined as the quotient group

J(H)=\frac{H_{\mathbb{C}}}{H_{\mathbb{Z}}+F^{2}H_{\mathbb{C}}}.

(8)

This is a generalized complex torus, i.e., a quotient of a
{\mathbb{C}}
-linear space by a discrete lattice.
Let
X
be a complex projective surface such that
H^{1}(X,{\mathbb{Z}})=0
(e.g.
K3
surface).
We write
J(X)=J(H^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Z}}))
and simply call it the
Jacobian
of
X
.
The following
Beilinson regulator map
plays a role of the Abel-Jacobi map in the study of
(2,1)
-cycles (cf.
[
KLM06
]
).

\nu:\mathrm{CH}^{2}(X,1)\longrightarrow J(X)

(9)

We recall a formula for the regulator map following
[
Lev88
]
pp. 458–459.
By the intersection product, the Jacobian of
X
is isomorphic to

J(X)\simeq\frac{(F^{1}H^{2}(X,{\mathbb{C}}))^{\vee}}{H_{2}(X,{\mathbb{Z}})}

(10)

where
(F^{1}H^{2}(X,{\mathbb{C}}))^{\vee}
is the dual
{\mathbb{C}}
-linear space of
F^{1}H^{2}(X,{\mathbb{C}})
and we regard
H_{2}(X,{\mathbb{Z}})
as a subgroup of
(F^{1}H^{2}(X,{\mathbb{C}}))^{\vee}
by integration.

Let
\xi
be a
(2,1)
-cycle represented by (
6
).
Let
D_{j}
be the normalization of
C_{j}
and
\mu_{j}:D_{j}\rightarrow X
be the composition of
D_{j}\rightarrow C_{j}\hookrightarrow X
.
We will define a topological 1-chain
\gamma_{j}
on
D_{j}
.
If
f_{j}
is constant, we define
\gamma_{j}=0
.
If
f_{j}
is not constant, we regard
f_{j}
as a finite morphism from
D_{j}
to
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
.
Then we define
\gamma_{j}
as the pull-back of
[\infty,0]
by
f_{j}
, where
[\infty,0]
is a path on
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
from
\infty
to
0
along the positive real axis.
By the condition
\sum_{j}{\mathrm{div}}_{C_{j}}(f_{j})=0
,
\gamma=\sum_{j}(\mu_{j})_{*}\gamma_{j}
is a topological 1-cycle on
X
.
Since
H_{1}(X,{\mathbb{Z}})=0
, there exists a 2-chain
\Gamma
on
X
such that
\partial\Gamma=\gamma
.
In this paper,
\Gamma
is referred to as a
2-chain associated with
\xi
.
Then the image of
\xi
under the regulator map is represented by the following element in

.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F^{1}H^{2}(X,{\mathbb{C}})\ni\text{[}\omega\text{]}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma}\omega+\sum_{j}\dfrac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{-1}}\displaystyle\int_{D_{j}-\gamma_{j}}\log(f_{j})\mu_{j}^{*}\omega}

(11)

where

is the pull-back of a logarithmic function on
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}-[\infty,0]
by

.

From the formula, for a prime divisor

on

-cycle represented by

In particular, we have

\nu({\mathrm{CH}}^{2}(X,1)_{\mathrm{dec}})={\mathrm{NS}}(X)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}({\mathbb{C}}/{\mathbb{Z}})\quad(\subset J(X)).

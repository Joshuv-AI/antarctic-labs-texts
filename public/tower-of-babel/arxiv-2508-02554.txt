Factorizable embeddings and the period of an irreducible sofic shift
Brian Marcus, Tom Meyerovitch, Klaus Thomsen, Chengyu Wu

Abstract
. Generalizing a result of MacDonald we give necessary and sufficient conditions for an arbitrary subshift to embed into an irreducible sofic shift factoring through a given cover by an irreducible subshift of finite type (SFT). We obtain also necessary and sufficient conditions for an arbitrary subshift to embed into an irreducible sofic shift factoring through some sliding block code out of an irreducible SFT. We do that when the code is required to be surjective, and hence a factor code, and when it is required to be injective or is allowed to be arbitrary. These results require concepts of the period of an irreducible sofic shift as well as a concept of a p p -periodic subshift. Several equivalent formulations of the period are developed.



1.
Introduction

A celebrated theorem of Wolfgang Krieger,
[
Kr1
]
, gives necessary and sufficient conditions for a subshift to embed into a given mixing SFT of larger entropy. To formulate it, let
Q_{n}(X)
denote the set of periodic points of least period
n
in a subshift
X
and let
q_{n}(X):=\#Q_{n}(X)
be the number of elements in
Q_{n}(X)
. Then Krieger’s theorem reads as follows:

Theorem 1.1
.

(W. Krieger) Let
Z
be a subshift and
Y
a mixing SFT such that
h(Z)<h(Y)
. Then
Z
embeds into
Y
if and only if
q_{n}(Z)\leq q_{n}(Y)
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Since a proper subshift of an irreducible SFT must have strictly smaller entropy, Krieger’s theorem gives in fact both necessary and sufficient conditions for a subshift to embed into a mixing SFT. It is striking that the rather obvious necessary condition on the periodic points is also sufficient when
h(Z)<h(Y)
.

Despite much effort and several partial results, no one has been able to give necessary and sufficient conditions for a subshift to embed into an arbitrary given sofic shift, be it irreducible or mixing. The lesson seems to be that although sofic shifts may appear to be only slight generalizations of SFTs, they contain a much more rigid internal structure; a structure which is still not fully understood. Nonetheless, a stronger version of embedding can be considered:

Definition 1.2
.

Let
X,Y
and
Z
be subshifts and
\pi:X\to Y
a sliding block code. Let
\phi:Z\to Y
be an embedding. We say that
\phi

factors through
\pi
if there is a sliding block code
\psi:Z\to X
making the following diagram commute:

\textstyle{X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\pi}
\textstyle{Z\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\psi}
\scriptstyle{\phi}
\textstyle{Y}

(1.1)

Note that
\psi
is automatically an embedding.
When
\pi
is surjective,
X
is an irreducible SFT and

is, consequently, an irreducible sofic shift, we refer to

of

In her thesis Sophie MacDonald considered the following problem: Given a cover
\pi:X\to Y
of
Y
, where
X
, and hence also
Y
, is mixing, when can a subshift
Z
with
h(Z)<h(Y)
be embedded into
Y
in such a way that the embedding factors through

Given the difficulty others have encountered when trying to embed into an arbitrary sofic shift, and despite that the problem she considered seems to be more complicated because it is asking for a specific type of embedding, MacDonald was able to give the following simple answer: Let
r_{n}(\pi):=\#Q_{n}(Y)\cap\pi(Q_{n}(X))
. Then the desired embedding exists if and only if
q_{n}(Z)\leq r_{n}(\pi)
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Theorem 1.3
.

(MacDonald’s Theorem
[
M
]
) Let
\pi:X\to Y
be a factor code from a mixing SFT
X
to a sofic shift
Y
. Let
Z
be a subshift such that
h(Z)<h(Y)
. Then, there is an embedding from
Z
into
Y
which factors through
\pi
if and only if
q_{n}(Z)\leq r_{n}(\pi)
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

As with Krieger’s theorem, an obvious necessary condition concerning periodic points turns out to be sufficient for the embedding in Theorem
1.3
. The condition is closely related to a condition involving the concept of a receptive periodic point (see Definition
4.4
), which Boyle
[
B
]
used as a sufficient condition for an ordinary embedding of a subshift into a mixing sofic shift. In effect, MacDonald’s result gives a related necessary and sufficient condition for a stronger notion of embedding, i.e., an embedding which factors through a given factor code on a mixing SFT
X
.

The first objective of the present paper is to extend MacDonald’s Theorem to the case where
X
is merely an irreducible SFT and to give both necessary and sufficient conditions also in that case. This is achieved in Theorem
3.9
, along with a second version, Theorem
3.11
where there is no assumption on the entropies. The proofs of these results are similar in spirit, but somewhat simpler, than MacDonald’s proof. One virtue of our approach, besides its greater generality, is that it allows us to use the statement of Krieger’s theorem rather than modifying its proof. Along the way, in Proposition
3.7
, we generalize also Krieger’s original theorem to the case where the target shift space is an irreducible, but not necessarily mixing, SFT. For this the notion of
p
-period for a general subshift is introduced as a necessary part of the assumptions. See Definition
3.6
.

MacDonald’s Theorem was, in part, motivated by the following setup.
In information theory, a communication channel is modeled using a set of transition probabilities, with the input and output described by stationary probability measures. The channel’s noise arises from the randomness of its transition probabilities. When there is no noise, the channel is referred to as a distortion channel, which, from the perspective of symbolic dynamics, can, in an abstract way be interpreted as a factor code from a subshift of channel inputs to a subshift of channel outputs.
We can then view the problem of encoding messages through the channel as the problem of embedding a subshift of messages into the domain of the factor code such that the restriction of the factor code to the range of the embedding is one-to-one.

The second aim of the paper is to find necessary and sufficient conditions for an arbitrary subshift
Z
to embed into an irreducible sofic shift
Y
in such a way that the embedding factors through
some
sliding block code
\pi:X\to Y
out of an irreducible SFT
X
. We obtain such a characterization in Theorem
4.18
and Theorem
4.20
, when there are no conditions on
\pi
, when it is required to be injective and finally also when it is required to be surjective.

A crucial ingredient in Theorem
4.18
and Theorem
4.20
is a new notion of period for an irreducible sofic shift. In Section
5
, we consider several additional notions of a period for an irreducible sofic shift and show that these are all equivalent. We also consider the concept of a
p
-periodic subshift, as mentioned above, which applies to general subshifts. We show that
p
-periodicity is rather different from the notion of period in the case where the subshift is irreducible sofic.

We recently became aware of a related paper by Wolfgang Krieger
[
Kr2
]
.

Notation, terminology, and background

We introduce in this brief section some notation, terminology and basic results from symbolic dynamics, and we refer the reader to
[
LM
]
for further information. We do assume that the reader is familiar with the fundamental concepts of shift space (or subshift), the language
{\mathcal{B}}(X)
of a shift space

, irreducible and mixing shift spaces, and sliding block codes, factor codes, embeddings and conjugacies from one shift space to another.

Synchronizing elements of

A word

A

is a finite alphabet. For a finite directed graph

to denote the edge shift of

A

Let

A
sofic shift

is the set of all bi-infinite label sequences of a labeled graph

Such a labeled graph

is called a

of

. A sofic shift can equivalently be defined as a factor of an SFT.

For each vertex

A

is a right-resolving presentation which has the fewest number of vertices among all right-resolving presentations of

is irreducible, its minimal right-resolving presentation is unique up to labeled graph isomorphism, and we call it the

. The left Fischer cover is defined similarly. In the remainder of the paper, the term Fischer cover is used to refer to the right Fischer cover unless stated otherwise.

It is well known that the right Fischer cover

of an irreducible sofic shift

is characterized as the unique irreducible, right-resolving and follower-separated presentation of

(

). And the induced 1-block code

is
almost invertible,
i.e., every doubly transitive point has a unique preimage; here, a point

appears infinitely often to the left and to the right in

For an irreducible SFT

. There is then a partition

3.
Encoding subshifts through an irreducible SFT - a generalization of Macdonald’s theorem

The main results, Theorems
3.9
and
3.11
, in this section give both necessary and sufficient conditions for the existence of an embedding
\phi:Z\to Y
which factors through a given cover
\pi:X\to Y
of the irreducible sofic shift
Y
.

There are two main ideas in the proof of Theorem
3.9
which handles the case
h(Z)<h(Y)
, and both are similar to the main ideas in MacDonald’s proof of Theorem

. The first idea is in Lemma
3.2
, which states that, given any
\epsilon>0
, there is an irreducible SFT
W\subseteq X
such that the restriction of
\pi
to

is injective and
h(W)>h(Y)-\epsilon

The second idea is in Lemma

to include any periodic point

in

which has the same least period as that of
\pi(u)
and
\pi(u)\not\in\pi(W)
(and the new

is still an irreducible SFT and the restriction of
\pi
to the new

is still injective).
Then to complete the proof of Theorem

to enlarge

so that it has enough periodic points to accommodate an embedding
\psi:Z\to W\subseteq X
, thereby obtaining an embedding
\phi:Z\to Y
which factors through
\pi
. Finally, to handle the case

we use a recent result from
[

]
and
[

]

We remark that the first of the ideas above is related to an old result
[

]
which shows that there is an irreducible sofic shift

such that the restriction of

to

is finite-to-one and surjective (and therefore satisfies

).

\frac{1}{m+N}\log(\#\mathcal{L}(S_{N}(t,s,n)))\geq h(Y)-\epsilon.

and also

\frac{1}{m+N}\log(\#\mathcal{L}(S_{N-\operatorname{per}(X)}(t,s,n)))\geq h(Y)-\epsilon.

For every
1\leq j\leq N
and any
w\in\mathcal{L}(S_{j}(t,s,n))
there exists
\tilde{w}\in S_{j}(t,s,n)
such that
\mathcal{L}(\tilde{w})=w
, and noting that
v
is a path in
G
from
s
to
t
, we have
v\tilde{w}v\in\mathcal{B}(X)
. Choose for every
1\leq j\leq N
a function
\Psi_{j}:\mathcal{L}(S_{j}(t,s,n))\to S_{j}(t,s,n)
such that
\mathcal{L}(\Psi_{j}(w))=w
and
v\Psi_{j}(w)v\in\mathcal{B}(X)
.

We now define the subshift
W\subseteq X
. A point
x\in X
is in
W
if and only if it satisfies the following constraints:

(1)

The word
v
occurs in
x
with gaps bounded by
N
.

(2)

Whenever
\tilde{w}\in\mathcal{B}_{j}(X)
is a word that occurs between two consecutive occurrences of
v
, then
\mathcal{L}(\tilde{w})\in\mathcal{L}(S_{j}(t,s,n))
and
\Psi_{j}(\mathcal{L}(\tilde{w}))=\tilde{w}
.

Then
W
is a subshift of finite type because the new constraints are on words of bounded length, and it is clearly irreducible.

If
x,x^{\prime}\in W
and
y:=\pi(x^{\prime})=\pi(x)
then the word
\mathcal{L}(v)
occurs in
\pi(x^{\prime})=\pi(x)
with gaps at most
N
, and the occurrences of the word
\mathcal{L}(v)
precisely correspond to the occurrences of
v
in both
x
and
x^{\prime}
, because
v
satisfies (i) and (ii). Also, any occurrence of
w\in\mathcal{B}_{j}(\mathcal{L}_{\infty}(W))
for
1\leq j\leq N
between two consecutive occurrences of the word
\mathcal{L}(v)
corresponds to an occurrence of
\Psi_{j}(w)
in both
x
and
x^{\prime}
. This shows that
x=x^{\prime}
, so
\pi\mid_{W}
is injective.

We now show that
\operatorname{per}(W)=\operatorname{per}(X)
. Since
W\subseteq X
we have that
\operatorname{per}(W)
is a multiple of
\operatorname{per}(X)
.
By the choice of
N
, there exist words
w\in\mathcal{L}(S_{N}(t,s,n))
and
w^{\prime}\in\mathcal{L}(S_{N-\operatorname{per}(X)}(t,s,n))
. So the points

x=(v\Psi_{N}(w))^{\infty}
and
x^{\prime}=(v\Psi_{N-\operatorname{per}(X)}(w^{\prime}))^{\infty}
are both periodic points in
W
, having periods
m+N
and
m+N-\operatorname{per}(X)
respectively. Hence the gcd of the lengths of periodic points in
W
divides
\operatorname{per}(X)
, which proves that
\operatorname{per}(W)=\operatorname{per}(X)
.

It remains to check that
h(W)\geq h(Y)-\epsilon
.
Indeed, for every
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
and every
w^{(1)},\ldots,w^{(i)}\in\mathcal{L}(S_{N}(t,s,n))
we have that

v\Psi_{N}(w^{(1)})v\Psi_{N}(w^{(2)})\ldots v\Psi_{N}(w^{(i)})\in\mathcal{B}_{i(N+m)}(W).

So

\log\left(\#\mathcal{B}_{i(m+N)}(W)\right)\geq i\log(\#\mathcal{L}(S_{N}(t,s,n)))\geq i(m+N)(h(Y)-\epsilon).

Dividing by
i(N+m)
and taking
i\to\infty
we conclude that
h(W)\geq h(Y)-\epsilon
.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\mathcal{G}=(G,\mathcal{L})
be a labeled graph with
G
irreducible. Let
T_{0}\subseteq X_{G}
be an irreducible SFT of positive entropy such that
\mathcal{L}_{\infty}:T_{0}\to X_{\mathcal{G}}
is injective. Let
u\in X_{G}
be a periodic point such that the least period of
u
is the same as that of
\mathcal{L}_{\infty}(u)
and
\mathcal{L}_{\infty}(u)\notin\mathcal{L}_{\infty}(T_{0})
. Then, there is an irreducible SFT
T\subseteq X_{G}
such that
T_{0}\cup\{u\}\subseteq T
and
\mathcal{L}_{\infty}:T\to X_{\mathcal{G}}
is injective.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.2
we may assume that there is an irreducible subgraph
H
of
G
such that
T_{0}=X_{H}
. Write
u=p^{\infty}
where
p
is a cycle in
G
. Since
\mathcal{L}_{\infty}(u)\notin\mathcal{L}_{\infty}(X_{H})
we can choose
R\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\mathcal{L}(p^{R})\notin\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{L}_{\infty}(X_{H})).

(3.1)

Since
\mathcal{L}_{\infty}(X_{H})
has positive entropy by assumption, there are finite paths
I
and
J
in
G
such that
Ip^{k}J\in\mathcal{B}(X_{G})
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

\mathcal{L}(Ip)\notin\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{L}_{\infty}(p^{\infty}))

(3.2)

and

\mathcal{L}(pJ)\notin\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{L}_{\infty}(p^{\infty})).

(3.3)

Since
G
is irreducible we can prolong
I
to arrange that
I
starts where
J
ends; in fact, we can arrange that there is a vertex
v
in
H
such that
i_{G}(I)=t_{G}(J)=v
. Let
T\subseteq X_{G}
be the elements of
X_{G}
that present bi-infinite paths in
G
obtained by concatenation of paths in
G
of the following types:

(a)

finite paths in
H
starting and ending at
v
, and of length exceeding
4(|I|+|J|+|p^{R}|)
,

(b)

finite paths of the form
Ip^{k}J
for some
k\geq R+|I|+|J|
,

(c)

left-infinite paths in
H
ending at
v
,

(d)

the left-infinite path
p^{\infty}J
,

(e)

right-infinite paths in
H
starting at
v
,

(f)

the right-infinite path
Ip^{\infty}
,

(g)

bi-infinite paths in
H
, and

(h)

the bi-infinite path
p^{\infty}
.

We claim that
T
has the required properties. That
T
is an SFT can be seen by checking that all words in
\mathcal{B}(T)
of length
\geq 4(|I|+|J|+R|p|)
are synchronizing for

, and
T
is irreducible by construction and because

is irreducible. To see
\mathcal{L}_{\infty}:T\to X_{\mathcal{G}}
is injective, let

. Thanks to (
3.1
), (
3.2
) and (
3.3
), the occurrences of the word
\mathcal{L}(p)^{R+|I|+|J|}
in
\mathcal{L}_{\infty}(z)
give away the intervals in

of the form (b),(d),(f),(h). All these intervals are determined by their image under
\mathcal{L}
because

and
\mathcal{L}(u)
have the same least period by assumption. The remaining intervals are either bi-infinite or represent finite or infinite paths in
H
starting and /or ending at the vertex

. These intervals are determined by their images under
\mathcal{L}
because
\mathcal{L}_{\infty}
is injective on
X_{H}
by assumption, or in the case where

represents the infinite path

, because
\mathcal{L}_{\infty}
is injective on the orbit of

.

∎

In our next result, we iterate the procedure from Lemma
3.3
to enlarge an irreducible SFT
W
embedded in
X
, on which the restriction of
\pi:X\to Y
is injective, to a new irreducible SFT
W
which contains several additional periodic points, and the restriction is still injective.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
\pi:X\to Y
be a factor code from an irreducible subshift of finite type
X
onto a sofic shift
Y
of positive entropy. Let
p\in\mathbb{N}
be the global period of
X
. For each
\epsilon>0
there is an
N_{\epsilon}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
M\in\mathbb{N}
there is an irreducible SFT
W\subseteq X
of global period
p
with the following properties:

(1)

\pi:W\to Y
is injective,

(2)

q_{np}(W)\geq e^{np(h(Y)-\epsilon)}\mbox{ when }\ n\geq N_{\epsilon}
,

(3)

q_{np}(W)\geq r_{np}(\pi)\mbox{ when }\ n\leq M
.

Proof.

Let
\pi:X\to Y
be as in the statement, and fix
\epsilon>0
.
By Lemma
3.2
we can find an irreducible SFT
\tilde{W}\subseteq X
with
\operatorname{per}(\tilde{W})=p
such that
\pi|_{\tilde{W}}
is injective and
h(\tilde{W})>h(Y)-\epsilon
. Since
h(\tilde{W})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{np}\log q_{np}(\tilde{W})
, there exists
N_{\epsilon}\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
q_{np}(\tilde{W})\geq e^{np(h(Y)-\epsilon)}
when
n\geq N_{\epsilon}
. Given
M\in{\mathbb{N}}
we can apply Lemma
3.3
at most
\sum_{k=1}^{M}r_{kp}(\pi)
times to get an irreducible SFT
W
such that
\tilde{W}\subseteq W\subseteq X
,
\pi|_{W}
is injective and (3) holds. Since
\tilde{W}\subseteq W
, it follows that (2) also holds. ∎

Remark 3.5
.

We note that item (1) and item (3) in Theorem
3.4
imply that
q_{np}(W)=r_{np}(\pi)
for
n\leq M
.

The following result is of interest in its own right. The sufficiency part of it is a version of Krieger’s theorem, Theorem
1.1
, where the target is only assumed to be irreducible, and not necessarily mixing. To formulate it we use the following notion of period for subshifts.

Definition 3.6
.

Let
p\in\mathbb{N}\backslash\{0\}
. A subshift
Z
is called
p
-periodic
when there is a partition
Z=\sqcup_{i=0}^{p-1}Z_{i}
of
Z
into clopen sets
Z_{i}
such that
\sigma(Z_{i})=Z_{i+1}
for all
i
, where the addition
i+1
is taken modulo
p
. Such a partition will be referred to as a
cyclic partition
of
Z
.

We note that when
Z
is
p
-periodic and
q\in\mathbb{N}
divides
p
, then
Z
is also
q
-periodic. In particular,
Z
can be
p
-periodic for a natural number
p
smaller than the period of
Z
as defined in Definition 4.5.4 of
[
LM
]
, i.e., the gcd of the periods of the periodic points in
Z
.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
W
be an irreducible SFT with global period
p
, and let
Z
be any subshift. Then
Z
embeds into
W
if and only if
Z
is conjugate to
W
or all of the following conditions hold:

(1)

h(Z)<h(W)
,

(2)

Z
is
p
-periodic, and

(3)

q_{np}(Z)\leq q_{np}(W)
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

(Sufficiency:) If
Z
is conjugate to
W
then
Z
trivially embeds into
W
. Assume
Z
is not conjugate to
W
. Let
W=\sqcup_{i=0}^{p-1}W_{i}
be the canonical cyclic partition of
W
. By assumption (2) there is also a cyclic partition
Z=\sqcup_{i=0}^{p-1}Z_{i}
of
Z
. The topological entropy of
(Z_{0},\sigma^{p})
is equal to
p
times the topological entropy of
(Z,\sigma)
, and the topological entropy of
(W_{0},\sigma^{p})
is equal to
p
times the topological entropy of
(W,\sigma)
. It follows therefore from (1) that
h(Z_{0},\sigma^{p})<h(W_{0},\sigma^{p})
. The number of
\sigma^{p}
-orbits of size
n
in
Z_{0}
is equal to the number of
\sigma
-orbits in
Z
of size
pn
, and similarly for
(W_{0},\sigma^{p})
and
(W,\sigma)
. Since
(W_{0},\sigma^{p})
is a mixing SFT, it follows therefore now from (3) that we can use Krieger’s embedding theorem, Theorem
1.1
, to get an embedding of
(Z_{0},\sigma^{p})
into
(W_{0},\sigma^{p})
; that is, there is an injective map
\iota:Z_{0}\to W_{0}
such that
\iota\circ\sigma^{p}=\sigma^{p}\circ\iota
. Define
j:Z\to W
such that

j(z):=\sigma^{i}\circ\iota\circ\sigma^{-i}(z),\ z\in Z_{i},

for
i\in\{0,1,\cdots,p-1\}
. Then
j:(Z,\sigma)\to(W,\sigma)
is an embedding of
Z
into
W
.

(Necessity:) We now prove that if
Z
is not conjugate to
W
and
Z
embeds into
W
, then conditions (1) (2) (3) hold. The proof that conditions (1) and (3) are necessary is identical to the well-known argument in the mixing case, see for example,
[
LM
, Page 338-339]
. Now let us check that (2) holds. Let
j:(Z,\sigma)\to(W,\sigma)
be an embedding and let
W=\sqcup_{i=0}^{p-1}W_{i}
be the canonical cyclic partition of
W
and define

Z_{i}:=j^{-1}(j(Z)\cap W_{i})

for each
i
. Clearly
Z=\sqcup_{i=0}^{p-1}Z_{i}
. Moreover, the
Z_{i}
’s are clopen since the
W_{i}
’s are. Then, since

\sigma(Z_{i})=\sigma(j^{-1}(j(Z)\cap W_{i}))=j^{-1}(\sigma(j(Z))\cap\sigma(W_{i}))=j^{-1}(j(Z)\cap W_{i+1})

under addition modulo
p
, we have
\sigma(Z_{i})=Z_{i+1}
(modulo
p
) and therefore (2) holds.
∎

Remark 3.8
.

It follows from
[
LM
, Theorem 10.1.1]
that when
Z
and
W
are both irreducible SFTs and
h(Z)<h(W)
, there will be an embedding of
Z
into
W
when

q_{n}(Z)\leq q_{n}(W)

(3.4)

for all

. This follows also from Proposition

since condition (

) implies that the global period of

will be a multiple of

so that

will be

-periodic.

Now we arrive at the generalization of MacDonald’s Theorem to irreducible sofic targets, which is a main result of this section.

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
\pi:X\to Y
be a factor code from an irreducible SFT
X
of global period
p
to a sofic shift
Y
. Let
Z
be a subshift such that
h(Z)<h(Y)
. Then, there is an embedding from
Z
into
Y
which factors through
\pi
if and only if
Z
is
p
-periodic and
q_{np}(Z)\leq r_{np}(\pi)
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

We first explain the necessity of the conditions. Assume that there are embeddings
\phi:Z\to Y
and
\psi:Z\to X
such that
\phi=\pi\circ\psi
. Then by the necessity part of Krieger’s embedding theorem for irreducible SFT targets, Proposition
3.7
,
Z
must be
p
-periodic. The bounds
q_{np}(Z)\leq r_{np}(\pi)
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
follow immediately from the fact that for every
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
\phi=\pi\circ\psi
induces an injective function from
Q_{np}(Z)
into
\pi(Q_{np}(X))\cap Q_{np}(Y)
.

For sufficiency of the two conditions, assume that
Z
is
p
-periodic and that
q_{np}(Z)\leq r_{np}(\pi)
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Set
\epsilon:=\frac{1}{2}(h(Y)-h(Z))
. Let
X=\sqcup_{i=0}^{p-1}X_{i}
be the canonical cyclic partition of
X
. Since
\limsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{np}\log(q_{np}(Z))\leq h(Z)<h(Y)-\epsilon
, there is an
N_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

q_{np}(Z)\leq e^{np(h(Y)-\epsilon)}

(3.5)

for all
n\geq N_{1}
. It follows from Theorem
3.4
that there exist an
N_{\epsilon}\in\mathbb{N}
and an irreducible SFT
W\subseteq X
of global period
p
such that

(i)

\pi:W\to Y
is injective,

(ii)

q_{np}(W)\geq e^{np(h(Y)-\epsilon)},\ n\geq N_{\epsilon}
,

(iii)

q_{np}(W)\geq r_{np}(\pi),\ n\leq\max\{N_{\epsilon},N_{1}\}
.

Since
W
is an irreducible SFT of global period
p
,

h(W)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{np}\log q_{np}(W),

and hence (ii) implies that
h(W)\geq h(Y)-\epsilon>h(Z)
. By combining (ii) and (
3.5
) we find that
q_{np}(W)\geq q_{np}(Z)
for all
n\geq\max\{N_{\epsilon},N_{1}\}
. For
n\leq\max\{N_{\epsilon},N_{1}\}
, it follows from the assumption and (iii) that

q_{np}(W)\geq r_{np}(\pi)\geq q_{np}(Z).

(3.6)

We can then apply Proposition
3.7
to obtain an embedding
\iota:Z\to W
, and then
\pi\circ\iota:Z\to Y
is an embedding of
Z
into
Y
which factors through
\pi
.

∎

The case
p=1
of Theorem
3.9
is Macdonald’s theorem. In the case where
X=Y
and
\pi
is the identity map, Theorem
3.9
gives back Proposition
3.7
.

The following result refines Theorem
3.9
by giving a complete characterization of the subshifts
Z
that admit an embedding that factors through a given factor code
\pi:X\to Y
on an irreducible SFT, by dealing with the remaining case that
h(Z)=h(Y)
. The refinement involves the notion of a
retraction
between subshifts. The classical notion of a retraction from topology was introduced into symbolic dynamics independently in
[
Meye
]
and
[
PS
]
:

Definition 3.10
.

Let
X
be a subshift. A sliding block code
r:X\to X
is called a
retraction
if
r\circ r=r
.
A subshift
Y\subseteq X
is called a
retract
of
X
if there exists a retraction
r:X\to X
such that
Y=r(X)
. Note that in this case
r\mid_{Y}=\operatorname{id}_{Y}
.

Theorem 3.11
.

Let
\pi:X\to Y
be a factor code from an irreducible SFT
X
of global period
p
onto a sofic shift
Y
, and let
Z
be an arbitrary subshift. The following conditions (1)-(3) are equivalent:

(1)

There is an embedding from
Z
into
Y
which factors through
\pi
.

(2)

(a)

There is a sliding block code
\rho:Y\to X
such that
\pi\circ\rho=\operatorname{id}_{Y}
and
Z
is conjugate to
Y
, or

(b)

Z
is
p
-periodic,
h(Z)<h(Y)
and
q_{np}(Z)\leq r_{np}(\pi)
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

(3)

(a’)

Z
and
Y
are conjugate SFTs, and there is an embedding
\rho:Y\to X
such that
\rho\circ\pi
is a retraction, or

(b)

Z
is
p
-periodic,
h(Z)<h(Y)
and
q_{np}(Z)\leq r_{np}(\pi)
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

We first prove that (a)
\Leftrightarrow
(a’): Assume (a). Then
r:=\rho\circ\pi:X\to X
is a retraction. As proved in
[
Meye
, Proposition 4.10]
and also in
[
PS
, Lemma 4.3]
, any retract of an SFT is also an SFT. Thus
r(X)
is an SFT. Since
\rho
is a conjugacy of
Y
onto
r(X)
, (a’) follows. Assume (a’). Then
\rho\circ\pi\circ\rho\circ\pi=\rho\circ\pi
. Since
\pi
is surjective and
\rho
injective, it follows that
\pi\circ\rho=\operatorname{id}_{Y}
. Hence (a) holds.

We have shown that (2)
\Leftrightarrow
(3) and therefore it suffices now to show that (1)
\Leftrightarrow
(2). Assume first that (1) holds, i.e. there are embeddings
\phi:Z\to Y
and
\psi:Z\to X
such that (
1.1
) commutes. The existence of
\psi
shows that
Z
must be
p
-periodic. If
\phi
is not surjective it follows from Corollary 4.4.9 of
[
LM
]
that
h(Z)<h(Y)
and then Theorem
3.9
applies to give (b). Assume therefore that
\phi(Z)=Y
. Then
\phi
is a conjugacy. Set
\rho:=\psi\circ\phi^{-1}:Y\to X
and note that
\pi\circ\rho=\operatorname{id}_{Y}
. It follows that (a) holds.

For the converse, assume first that (a) holds. Let
\phi:Z\to Y
be a conjugacy. Set
\psi:=\rho\circ\phi
and note that (
1.1
) commutes. When we instead assume that (b) holds we get the desired embedding from Theorem
3.9
.
∎

Remark 3.12
.

The conditions given in (a) and (a’) are somewhat abstract and motivates the following question: Given a factor map
\pi:X\to Y
between irreducible SFTs
X
and
Y
, when is there a sliding block code
\rho:Y\to X
such that
\pi\circ\rho=\operatorname{id}_{Y}
, or equivalently, when does there exist an embedding
\rho:Y\to X
(in terms of more explicit, hopefully checkable conditions) such that
\rho\circ\pi:X\to X
is a retraction? A necessary condition is that every periodic point in
Y
has a preimage which is a periodic point with the same period.

4.
Factorizable embeddings

In Section
3
we considered an irreducible sofic shift
Y
and a given cover
\pi:X\to Y
of
Y
in order to decide when a given subshift
Z
can embed into
Y
via
\pi
. The answer was obtained in Theorem
3.11
, and we consider therefore now a different but closely related problem. Given only

and

, when can we find an embedding of

into

which factors through
some
sliding block code
\pi:X\to Y
out of an irreducible SFT X? And if we can, what properties can we ask of

?

We start with the following definition.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
Z
and

be subshifts. An embedding
\phi:Z\to Y
is said to be

when
\phi
factors through (see Definition

) some irreducible SFT. That is, when there exist an irreducible SFT

and a sliding block code

such that
the following diagram commutes for some embedding

:

\textstyle{S\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\pi}
\textstyle{Z\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\psi}
\scriptstyle{\phi}
\textstyle{Y}

(4.1)

Moreover, we say that
\phi
is

when
\pi
can be chosen to be injective (and hence an embedding), and that
\phi
is

when
\pi
can be chosen to be surjective (and hence a cover of

).

We first have the following observation regarding the proof of Theorem
3.9
.

Remark 4.2
.

The embedding

constructed in the proof of Theorem
3.9
is

-factorizable (through the SFT

therein) by construction, giving us the following implication in the setting of Theorem
3.9
: If there is an embedding of

into
Y
which factors through a factor code
\pi
, then there is also an

-factorizable embedding of
Z
into
Y
. It follows that for an arbitrary subshift
Z
and an arbitrary irreducible sofic shift
Y
such that

, the implication (i)
\Rightarrow
(ii) holds, where

(i)

There is an
S
-factorizable embedding
Z\to Y
.

(ii)

There is an
I
-factorizable embedding
Z\to Y
.

4.1.
Receptive periodic points

Let

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\mathcal{G}=(G,\mathcal{L})
be the right Fischer cover of an irreducible sofic shift
Y
. Then a word
u\in\mathcal{B}(Y)
is synchronizing for
Y
if and only if it is magic for
\mathcal{G}
.

Proof.

This follows from Exercise 3.3.3 of
[
LM
]
, but we sketch a proof for the benefit of the reader. Recall that the right Fischer cover
\mathcal{G}=(G,\mathcal{L})
of an irreducible sofic shift
Y=X_{\mathcal{G}}
is the unique, up to labeled graph isomorphism, irreducible, right-resolving, follower-separated presentation of
Y
.

Assume that
w\in\mathcal{B}(X_{\mathcal{G}})
is synchronizing for
X_{\mathcal{G}}
. Consider two paths
u,v
in
G
with label
w
and assume for a contradiction that they do not terminate at the same vertex. Since the right Fischer cover is follower-separated,
it follows that there is a path
l
in
G
starting at the terminal vertex of
u
, but no path starting at the terminal vertex of
v
has the same label
\mathcal{L}(l)
as
l
. (If this is not the case, it must be true with
u
and
v
interchanged and the following reasoning would just work as well.) It follows from
[
LM
, Proposition 3.3.16]
that there is a magic word for
\mathcal{G}
. Since
G
is irreducible and the labeling is right-resolving, it follows that we can extend
l
to the right to obtain a path
l^{\prime}
in
G
which terminates at the initial vertex of
v
and its label
\mathcal{L}(l^{\prime})
is magic for
\mathcal{G}
. Then
\mathcal{L}(l^{\prime})w=\mathcal{L}(l^{\prime}v)\in\mathcal{B}(X_{\mathcal{G}})
, and
w\mathcal{L}(l^{\prime})=\mathcal{L}(ul^{\prime})\in\mathcal{B}(X_{\mathcal{G}})
, but
\mathcal{L}(l^{\prime})w\mathcal{L}(l^{\prime})\notin\mathcal{B}(X_{\mathcal{G}})
, since all paths labeled
\mathcal{L}(l^{\prime})w
must terminate at the vertex where
v
terminates, but there is no path labeled
\mathcal{L}(l^{\prime})
which starts at that same vertex. This contradicts that
w
is synchronizing for
X_{\mathcal{G}}
.

The converse, that magic implies synchronizing, is easy and left to the reader.

∎

Definition 4.4
.

A periodic point
x\in X
of least period
p
is
receptive
(for
X
) when there are synchronizing words
m_{1},m_{2}\in\mathcal{B}(X)
such that
m_{1}(x_{[0,p)})^{k}m_{2}\in\mathcal{B}(X)
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}\backslash\{0\}
.

This notion and the name were introduced by Mike Boyle in
[
B
]
. We denote by
\operatorname{Rec}_{n}(X)
the set of receptive periodic points of least period
n
, and set

\operatorname{rec}_{n}(X):=\#\operatorname{Rec}_{n}(X).

It is easy to see that any periodic point which contains a synchronizing word is receptive.

Lemma 4.5
.

In an SFT all periodic points are receptive.

Proof.

Let
p\in Q_{n}(X)
. Since
X
is an SFT there is an
M\in\mathbb{N}
such that all words in
\mathcal{B}(X)
of length
\geq M
is synchronizing, cf. Proposition 2.1.7 and Theorem 2.1.8 in
[
LM
]
. In particular,
p_{[0,Mn)}
is synchronizing, and
p_{[0,Mn)}p_{[0,n)}^{i}p_{[0,Mn)}\in\mathcal{B}(X)
for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
X
be an irreducbile SFT and
\pi:X\to Y
be a factor code. Let
w\in Y
be a receptive periodic point. Then, there exist an irreducible SFT
X^{\prime}
, a factor code
\pi^{\prime}:X^{\prime}\to Y
and an embedding
\iota:X\to X^{\prime}
such that
\pi^{\prime}\circ\iota=\pi
and
{\pi^{\prime}}^{-1}(w)
contains a periodic element of the same least period as
w
. Moreover, when the least period of
w
is a multiple of the global period of
X
, the global period of
X^{\prime}
can be arranged to be the same as that of
X
.

Proof.

Up to recoding, we may assume that
X=X_{G}
for an irreducible graph and
\pi:X_{G}\to Y
is a labeling of edges of
G
(cf. Lemma
2.1
). Let
n
be the least period of
w
. Since
w
is receptive, there are synchronizing words
s,t\in\mathcal{B}(Y)
such that
s(w_{[0,n-1]})^{k}t\in\mathcal{B}(Y)
for all
k>0
. Let
\alpha
be a path in
G
labeled
sw_{[0,n-1]}t
and let
i_{G}(\alpha)
and
t_{G}(\alpha)
be the initial and terminal vertices of
\alpha
, respectively. Now, create a new graph
G^{\prime}
by adding the following labeled paths to
G
:

(1)

A simple path of length
|s|+n
starting at
i(\alpha)
and label the path by
sw_{[0,n-1]}
. Let
r
be the terminal vertex of this path;

(2)

A simple cycle of length
n
at
r
. Label it by
w_{[0,n-1]}
.

(3)

A simple path of length
|t|
from
r
to
t(\alpha)
. Label it by
t
.

Let
X^{\prime}:=X_{G^{\prime}}
,
\pi^{\prime}
be the
1
-block code defined by the labeling of
G^{\prime}
and
Y^{\prime}
be the factor of
X^{\prime}
under
\pi^{\prime}
(i.e.,
\pi^{\prime}:X^{\prime}\to Y^{\prime}
). First, observe that by construction,
\pi^{-1}(w)
contains a periodic element of least period
n
. Then, noting that
G
is a subgraph of
G^{\prime}
, we immediately derive that there is an embedding
\iota:X\to X^{\prime}
; moreover, since
s
and
t
are synchronizing, the language of

is the same as that of

, implying that

. Thus,
\pi^{\prime}\circ\iota
is just
\pi
.

Now, let
p
be the period of
G
(and therefore also the global period of
X
) and assume that
n
is a multiple of
p
.
Consider the vertex
i(\alpha)
in
G
. The global period

of
G
and hence of

is the greatest common divisor of the lengths of cycles in
G
starting and ending at
i(\alpha)
, and similarly for
G^{\prime}
and
X_{G^{\prime}}
. Consider such a cycle in
G^{\prime}
of length

. If it contains some of the new vertices it must contain a number of the new paths from

\{l-mn:m\in\mathbb{N}\}

Definition 4.8
.

Let
Y

the greatest common divisor of

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
X
and
Z
be irreducible SFTs with canonical cyclic partitions
X=\sqcup_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}
and
Z=\sqcup_{j=1}^{m}Z_{j}
, respectively. Let
Y
be a sofic shift and let
\pi:X\to Y
and
\rho:Z\to Y
be factor codes. Assume
\#\pi^{-1}(y)=1
for some element
y\in Y
. Then,
n
divides
m
and there is a surjective map
\mu:\{1,2,\cdots,m\}\to\{1,2,\cdots,n\}
such that
\rho(Z_{j})=\pi(X_{\mu(j)})
for all
j\in\{1,2,\cdots,m\}
.

Proof.

Note that
\sigma^{nm}
is mixing and hence transitive on
\pi(X_{i})
. Let
a_{i}\in\pi(X_{i})
be a point with a dense orbit for
\sigma^{nm}
. Since
\pi(X_{i})=\bigcup_{j=1}^{m}\rho(Z_{j})\cap\pi(X_{i})
, there is a
j\in\{1,2,\cdots,m\}
such that
\rho(Z_{j})\cap\pi(X_{i})
has non-empty interior in
\pi(X_{i})
. It follows that
\sigma^{nm\ell}(a_{i})\in\rho(Z_{j})\cap\pi(X_{i})
for some
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}
, implying that
\pi(X_{i})\subseteq\rho(Z_{j})
. A symmetric argument shows that
\rho(Z_{j})\subseteq\pi(X_{i^{\prime}})
for some
i^{\prime}\in\{1,2,\cdots,n\}
.
By assumption there is a
k
such that the set
\pi(X_{k})
contains an element with a unique pre-image under
\pi
. However,

\sigma(\pi(X_{k}))=\pi(X_{k+1})

(4.2)

with addition mod
n
, and hence
\pi(X_{k})
contains such an element for every
k
. The conclusion that
\pi(X_{i})\subseteq\rho(Z_{j})\subseteq\pi(X_{i^{\prime}})
implies therefore that

, and hence that
\pi(X_{i})=\rho(Z_{j})
.
Since

\sigma(\rho(Z_{j}))=\rho(Z_{j+1})

(4.3)

with addition mod
m
, it follows that for every

there is an element
\mu(j)\in\{1,2,\cdots,n\}
such that
\rho(Z_{j})=\pi(X_{\mu(j)})

\pi(X_{i})
contains an element with a unique pre-image under
\pi
, the element

) and (

) that

, where

and

denote the cyclic permutations of

.

Lemma 4.11
.

Let

\pi:X\to Y

(a)

\text{per}(Y)
divides
\text{per}(X)
.

Assume that
\pi:X\to Y

. Then,

(b)

\text{per}(Y)=\text{per}(X)
.

If
Z

, then

\text{per}(Y)

\text{per}(Z)
.

Proof.

(b): Let

. Since

\text{per}(Y)

\text{per}(Y)

Lemma 4.12
.

Let
Y
be an irreducible sofic shift and
\pi:X\to Y

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n\text{per}(Y)}\log r_{n\text{per}(Y)}(\pi)=h(Y).

Note first of all that

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n\text{per}(Y)}\log r_{n\text{per}(Y)}(\pi)\leq\limsup_{j\to\infty}\frac{1}{j}\log q_{j}(X)=h(X)

by

4.2.
The main result on factorizable embeddings

We quickly extract from
[
T
]
some definitions, terminology and results that we will need in the following.

Let
X
be a shift space,
R(X)
be the closure of the set of periodic points of
X
and
S(X)
be the set of synchronizing words of
R(X)
. For
s,t\in S(X)
, we write
s\sim t
when there exist
x,y\in\mathcal{B}(R(X))
such that
sxt,tys\in\mathcal{B}(R(X))
. This is an equivalence relation and we let
\mathcal{S}(X)=S(X)/\sim
denote its equivalence classes.

For each
\alpha\in\mathcal{S}(X)
, the
irreducible component at level
0
with respect to
\alpha
, denoted
X_{(\alpha,0)}
, is the set of points
x\in R(X)
such that elements of
\alpha
appear with bounded gap in
x
(the bound may depend on the point
x
).

The
derived shift
of
X
, denoted
\partial X
, is the set of points in
R(X)
which do not contain any synchronizing word of
R(X)
. It is not hard to see that
\partial X
is a shift space. So, we can iterate this operation to get a nested sequence of shift spaces: with
\partial^{0}X:=X
, the
k
-th derived shift
is defined
\partial^{k}X:=\partial(\partial^{k-1}X)
. The
depth of
X
is the unique integer
k
(if any) such that
\partial^{k}X\neq\emptyset
and
\partial^{k+1}X=\emptyset.

An
irreducible component of
X
at level
k
is an irreducible component at level
0
of
\partial^{k}X
. An
irreducible component of
X
is an irreducible component of
X
at some level. It is not hard to see that the irreducible components of
X
are disjoint.

If
X
is sofic, then
\partial X
is also sofic (see
[
T
, Theorem 6.6]
) and has finitely many irreducible components of level 0. Also,
X
has finite depth (see
[
T
, Theorem 6.7]
). As a consequence,
X
has finitely many irreducible components (see
[
T
, Proposition 6.18]
). Moreover, the closure of an irreducible component of a sofic shift is an irreducible sofic shift (see
[
T
, Proposition 6.16]
). Finally, we note that since a sofic shift has finite depth, each periodic point is contained in a unique irreducible component, (see
[
T
, Lemma 4.2]
).

In the following lemma, we set out some important consequences of the material above.

Lemma 4.13
.

Let
U\subseteq V
be sofic shift spaces with
U

(1)

There is a unique irreducible component
Y_{c}
of
V
such that

(a)

U\subseteq\overline{Y_{c}}
, and

(b)

U\cap Y_{c}\neq\emptyset
.

In addition,

(2)

For all

,
\operatorname{Rec}_{n}(U)\subseteq\operatorname{Rec}_{n}(\overline{Y_{c}})
, and

if
W

\pi:W\to U

\operatorname{per}(\overline{Y_{c}})

Proof.

(1). Assume first that
Y_{c}
is an irreducible component in
Y
such that (a) and (b) hold. Then
U\subseteq\partial^{k}V
, where
k
is the level of
Y_{c}
; in fact,
U\subseteq R(\partial^{k}V)
since
U
is irreducible. Because of (b),
U
contains a synchronizing word for
R(\partial^{k}V)
and
U
is therefore not contained in
\partial^{k+1}V
. It suffices therefore to show that when
k
is the largest integer for which
U\subseteq\partial^{k}V
, there is a unique irreducible component at level
k
for which (a) and (b) hold. For this note that
U\subseteq R(\partial^{k}V)

5.
Period of irreducible sofic shifts

In this section we investigate the notion of period of an irreducible sofic shift
Y
, which is a crucial part of two of our main results, Theorem
4.18
and Theorem
4.20
. We defined the period of
Y
in Definition
4.8
, as the gcd of periods of receptive periodic points in
Y
and showed, in Lemma
4.11
, that this is the same as the global period of the right Fischer cover of
Y
. A broad generalization, given in the following result, is that eight plausible definitions of the period of
Y
are all equivalent.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
Y
be an irreducible sofic shift. Define:

p_{1}
:

The global period of any irreducible SFT
X
such that there is a factor code
X\to Y
for which some point in
Y
has a unique pre-image in
X
.

p_{2}
:

The global period of any irreducible SFT which factors onto
Y
under an almost invertible code.

p_{3}
:

The global period of the right or left Fischer cover of
Y
.

p_{4}
:

The gcd of least periods of receptive periodic points of
Y
.

p_{5}
:

The gcd of least periods of periodic points of
Y
which contain a synchronizing word.

p_{6}
:

The unique number
m
in a collection
D_{0},D_{1},D_{2},\cdots,D_{m-1}
of distinct closed subsets of
Y
with the properties that

(i)

Y=\bigcup_{i=0}^{m-1}D_{i}
,

(ii)

\sigma(D_{i})=D_{i+1\bmod m}
, and

(iii)

\sigma^{m}|_{D_{i}}
is mixing for one, and hence for all
i
.

(iv)

D_{i}\cap D_{j}
has empty interior when
i,j\in\{0,1,\cdots,m-1\}
and
i\neq j
.

p_{7}
:

The unique number
n
in a collection
C_{0},C_{1},C_{2},\cdots,C_{n-1}
of distinct relatively clopen subsets of
\tilde{Y}
, where
\tilde{Y}
is the set of doubly transitive points, such that

(a)

\tilde{Y}=\bigcup_{i=0}^{n-1}C_{i}
;

(b)

\sigma(C_{i})=C_{i+1\bmod n}
;

(c)

\sigma^{n}
is mixing on each
C_{i}
, in the sense that for any two relatively open sets
U,V
in
C_{i}
,
(\sigma^{n})^{k}(U)\cap V\neq\emptyset
for all sufficiently large
k
,

(d)

C_{i}
’s are mutually disjoint.

p_{8}
:

The minimum of global periods of irreducible SFTs which factor onto
Y
, i.e.,

\displaystyle p_{8}=\min\bigl\{\operatorname{per}(X):X\ \text{is an irreducible SFT}

\displaystyle\text{that factors onto}\ Y\bigr\}.

Then
p_{1}=p_{2}=p_{3}=p_{4}=p_{5}=p_{6}=p_{7}=p_{8}
.

Proof.

(p_{1}=p_{2}=p_{3}=p_{8})
: It follows from Lemma
4.9
that the minimum occurring in the definition of
p_{8}
is realized by any irreducible SFT cover of
Y
for which there is a point in
Y
with a unique pre-image under the corresponding factor code. Such points in
Y
exist in abundance when the cover is almost invertible, in particular the right or left Fischer cover, and hence
p_{1}=p_{2}=p_{3}=p_{8}
.

(
p_{5}=p_{3}
): Let
\pi:X\to Y
be the right Fischer cover. Then
X=X_{H}
for some irreducible graph
H
,
\pi
is given by a labeling
\mathcal{L}
of the edges of
H
and
p_{3}=\operatorname{per}(X_{H})
.

Let
\gamma
be a cycle in
H
. To show
p_{5}
divides
\operatorname{per}(X_{H})
, it suffices to show that
p_{5}
divides
|\gamma|
. To this end, let
u
be a synchronizing word in
Y
and let
\xi\in\mathcal{L}^{-1}(u)
, which is a path in
H
. By the irreducibility of
X_{H}
, there exist paths
x,y
in
G
such that
i_{H}(x)=t_{H}(\xi),t_{H}(x)=i_{H}(\gamma),i_{H}(y)=i_{H}(\gamma),t_{H}(y)=i_{H}(\xi)
. Now, let
\alpha:=\xi xy
and
\beta=\xi x\gamma y
. Both
\mathcal{L}(\alpha^{\infty})
and
\mathcal{L}(\beta^{\infty})
are periodic points in
Y
which contain the synchronizing word
u
. Thus,
p_{5}
divides
|\alpha|
and
|\beta|
, and therefore divides their difference
|\gamma|
, as desired.

It remains to show that
\operatorname{per}(X_{H})
divides
p_{5}
. This follows from the fact that every periodic point of
Y
that contains a synchronizing word has a unique pre-image in the right Fischer cover, which is a consequence of Lemma
4.3
.

(p_{4}=p_{3})
: This is (b) of Lemma
4.11
.

(p_{5}=p_{6}):
It is easy to see that
p_{5}
is the gcd of the least periods of periodic points in the unique irreducible component at level
0
of
Y
. Then,
p_{5}=p_{6}
follows from Corollary 3.12 and Lemma 3.13 in
[
T
]
.

(p_{6}=p_{7}):

We first show that the decomposition
C_{0},C_{1},\cdots,C_{n-1}
exists with
n=p_{6}
. Let
D_{0},D_{1},\cdots,D_{p_{6}-1}
be the sets from the definition of
p_{6}
. For each
0\leq i\leq p_{6}-1
, let
U_{i}
be the interior of
D_{i}
. It follows from (i) and the Baire category theorem that
U_{i}
is non-empty for at least one
i
, and then from (ii) that
U_{i}\neq\emptyset
for all
i
. Set
C_{i}:=U_{i}\cap\tilde{Y}
. The conditions (b) and (d) are clear. It follows from (ii) that
\cup_{i=0}^{p_{6}-1}U_{i}
is
\sigma
-invariant. As the union is also open it follows that
\cup_{i=0}^{p_{6}-1}U_{i}
must contain all doubly transitive points and therefore condition (a) holds. For part (c), first note that there exist open sets
U^{\prime},V^{\prime}\subseteq U_{i}
such that
U=U^{\prime}\cap\tilde{Y},V=V^{\prime}\cap\tilde{Y}
. Since
(D_{i},\sigma^{p_{6}})
is mixing by (iii), for all sufficiently large
k
,
\sigma^{kp_{6}}(U^{\prime})\cap V^{\prime}\neq\emptyset
. Now,

\displaystyle\sigma^{kp_{6}}(U)\cap V=\sigma^{kp_{6}}(U^{\prime}\cap\tilde{Y})\cap(V^{\prime}\cap\tilde{Y})=\sigma^{kp_{6}}(U^{\prime})\cap V^{\prime}\cap\tilde{Y}\neq\emptyset

where the last step follows from the fact that
\tilde{Y}
is dense in
Y
.

As for uniqueness, we show in fact that the decomposition

C_{0},C_{1},\cdots,C_{n-1}

is unique up to a cyclic permutation. Suppose there is another decomposition
C^{\prime}_{0},C^{\prime}_{1},\cdots,C^{\prime}_{n^{\prime}-1}
such that conditions (a)-(d) of
p_{7}
hold. It follows from (a) that for each
i
,
C_{i}\cap C^{\prime}_{j}\neq\emptyset
for some
j
. Since
(C_{i},\sigma^{nn^{\prime}})
and
(C_{j}^{\prime},\sigma^{nn^{\prime}})
are both mixing and
C_{i}
,
C_{j}^{\prime}
are both relatively clopen, we must have
C_{i}=C^{\prime}_{j}
. Thus, the uniqueness of the decomposition follows from (b).
∎

An irreducible sofic shift

Remark 5.4

Remark 5.6
.

If an irreducible SFT
X
is not
p
-periodic, then
X
necessarily has a periodic point with period not divisible by
p
. This property fails already for irreducible sofic shifts, as we have seen from Example
5.5
. In fact, there exist irreducible subshifts without any periodic points, such as Sturmian shifts, that are not
p
-periodic for any
p\geq 2
. To see this, first recall that any Sturmian shift is uniquely ergodic and, with respect to the unique invariant measure
\mu
, it is isomorphic to an irrational rotation of the circle. It follows that with respect to
\mu
, the shift is totally ergodic, i.e., each power of the shift is ergodic. But if the shift is
p
-periodic for some
p\geq 2
, then the shift space would decompose into
p
subsets each of which is invariant under the
p
-th power of the shift and have measure
1/p
, contradicting total ergodicity. Thus, Sturmian shifts are not
p
-periodic for any
p\geq 2
. Note that this implies that the Sturmian shift can not embed into any non-mixing irreducible SFT by Proposition
3.7
. Moreover, this example shows that in general, no conditions on periodic points can ensure the shift is
p
-periodic. There also exist irreducible subshifts, such as Toeplitz shifts, that are
p
-periodic for infinitely many
p
(see, for example,
[
Wil
, Lemma 2.3]
).

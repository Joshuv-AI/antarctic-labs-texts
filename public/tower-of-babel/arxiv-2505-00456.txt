Post-Lie deformations of pre-Lie algebras and their applications in Regularity Structures
Yvain Bruned, Yunhe Sheng, Rong Tang

Abstract
. In this paper, we study post-Lie deformations of a pre-Lie algebra, namely deforming a pre-Lie algebra into a post-Lie algebra. We construct the differential graded Lie algebra that governs post-Lie deformations of a pre-Lie algebra. We also develop the post-Lie cohomology theory for a pre-Lie algebra, by which we classify infinitesimal post-Lie deformations of a pre-Lie algebra using the second cohomology group. The rigidity of such kind of deformations is also characterized using the second cohomology group. Finally, we apply this deformation theory to Regularity Structures. We prove that the post-Lie algebraic structure on the decorated trees which appears spontaneously in Regularity Structures is a post-Lie deformation of a pre-Lie algebra.

1.
Introduction

The notion of a post-Lie algebra has been introduced by Vallette in the course of study of Koszul duality of operads
[
36
]
.
Munthe-Kaas and his coauthors found that post-Lie algebras also naturally appear in differential geometry
[
23
]
and numerical integration on manifolds
[
33
]
. Meanwhile, it was found that post-Lie algebras play an essential role in Regularity Structures in stochastic analysis
[
6
,
8
,
24
]
.
Recently, post-Lie algebras have been studied from different points of view including constructions of nonabelian generalized Lax pairs
[
4
]
, Poincaré-Birkhoff-Witt type theorems
[
16
,
22
]
, factorization theorems
[
18
]
, multiple zeta values
[
13
,
14
]
and relations to post-Lie groups
[
1
,
3
,
32
]
.

A pre-Lie algebra is a post-Lie algebra whose underlying Lie algebra is abelian. Pre-Lie
algebras are a class of nonassociative algebras coming from the study of convex homogeneous cones, affine manifolds and affine
structures on Lie groups, and cohomologies of associative algebras. They also appeared in many fields in
mathematics and mathematical physics, such as complex and symplectic structures on Lie groups and Lie algebras, integrable
systems, vertex algebras and operads. See
[
2
,
12
,
30
,
35
]
for more details. The homotopy theory of pre-Lie algebras was given in
[
15
]
using the operadic approach. Furthermore, the cohomology theory of pre-Lie algebras was given by Dzhumadil’daev and Boyom in
[
17
]
and
[
10
]
.

The method of deformation is ubiquitous in mathematics and physics.
Roughly speaking, a deformation of a mathematical structure is a perturbation which gives the same kind of structure. Motivated by the foundational work of Kodaira and Spencer
[
27
]
for complex analytic structures, deformation theory finds its generalization in algebraic geometry
[
25
]
and further in number theory as deformations of Galois representations
[
31
]
. The deformation of algebraic structures began with the seminal
work of Gerstenhaber
[
20
,
21
]
for associative
algebras and followed by its extension to Lie algebras by
Nijenhuis and Richardson
[
34
]
. Deformations of other
algebraic structures such as pre-Lie algebras have also been
developed
[
11
]
. In general, deformation theory was developed
for binary quadratic operads by Balavoine
[
5
]
.

In this paper, we consider the problem of deforming pre-Lie algebras into post-Lie algebras. On the one hand, we construct the differential graded Lie algebra that governs post-Lie deformations of a pre-Lie algebra. On the other hand, we develop the post-Lie cohomology theory for a pre-Lie algebra, such that the second cohomology group classifies infinitesimal post-Lie deformations of a pre-Lie algebra. We also show that if the second post-Lie cohomology group of a pre-Lie algebra is trivial, then the pre-Lie algebra is post-Lie rigid.

Then we apply the established deformation theory to Regularity Structures. In Hairer’s seminal work
[
24
]
in stochastic partial differential equations, he introduced the notion of Regularity Structures which provides an algebraic framework to describe the local Taylor expansion of functions around each point. One can think that various kinds of the theory of rough paths and the usual Taylor expansions have been unified into Regularity Structures. It is noteworthy that decorated rooted trees and related combinatorial Hopf algebras play pivotal role in the algebraic renormalisation of Regularity Structures
[
6
]
. Recently, the natural post-Lie algebraic structures on the combining objects as decorated rooted trees, multi-indices and noncommutative derivations have been discovered and simplified the combinatorial Hopf algebraic structures in Regularity Structures
[
8
,
26
]
. We prove that the post-Lie algebraic structure on the decorated trees is a post-Lie deformation of a pre-Lie algebra. Moreover, take advantage of the Taylor deformation of the post-Lie algebraic structure on the decorated trees, we construct a concrete formal post-Lie deformation of the pre-Lie algebra on the decorated trees.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we give the differential graded Lie algebra that governs post-Lie deformations of a pre-Lie algebra. In Section
3
, we introduce the post-Lie cohomology for a pre-Lie algebra, by which we study formal post-Lie deformations of a pre-Lie algebra. In Section
4
, we apply the deformation theory described above to the post-Lie algebra appearing in the context of Regularity Structures.

2.
Post-Lie deformations of pre-Lie algebras

In this section, we study post-Lie deformations of a pre-Lie algebra, and construct the differential graded Lie algebra that governs this kind of deformations.

Definition 2.1
.

(
[
36
]
)
A
post-Lie algebra

(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\rhd)
consists of a Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot])
and a binary product
\rhd:\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}
such that

(1)

\displaystyle x\rhd[y,z]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[x\rhd y,z]+[y,x\rhd z],

(2)

\displaystyle([x,y]+x\rhd y-y\rhd x)\rhd z

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x\rhd(y\rhd z)-y\rhd(x\rhd z),\,\,\forall x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}.

Any post-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\rhd)
gives rise to a new Lie algebra

\mathfrak{g}_{\rhd}:=(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}_{\rhd}})

defined by

[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}_{\rhd}}\coloneqq x\rhd y-y\rhd x+[x,y],\quad\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g},

which is called the
sub-adjacent Lie algebra
. Moreover,
Eqs. (
1
)-(
2
) equivalently mean that the linear map
L:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}(\mathfrak{g})
defined by
L_{x}y=x\rhd y
is an action of the Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}_{\rhd}})
on the Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot])
.

Remark 2.2
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\rhd)
be a post-Lie algebra. If the Lie bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]
vanishes, then
(\mathfrak{g},\rhd)
becomes a pre-Lie algebra. For further details on
pre-Lie algebras, see
[
2
,
12
,
30
]
. Thus, a post-Lie algebra can be viewed as a nonabelian generalization of a pre-Lie algebra.

Definition 2.3
.

•

A
derivation
of a post-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\rhd)
is a linear map
d:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}
satisfying
d([x,y])=[d(x),y]+[x,d(y)]
and
d(x\rhd y)=d(x)\rhd y+x\rhd d(y)
for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}.

Denote by
\mathsf{Der}(\mathfrak{g})
the set of derivations of the post-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\rhd)
.

•

A
homomorphism
from a post-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\rhd)
to a post-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot],\rhd)
is a Lie algebra morphism
\phi:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{h}
such that

\phi(x\rhd y)=\phi(x)\rhd\phi(y)
for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}.

In particular, if
\phi
is invertible, then
\phi
is called an
isomorphism
.

We consider the following question:

Deforming a pre-Lie algebra into a post-Lie algebra.

Consider the post-Lie deformation of a pre-Lie algebra, we have the following characterization.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\rhd)
be a pre-Lie algebra. Let
\pi\in\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
and
\omega\in\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
be linear maps. Then
(\mathfrak{g},\pi,\rhd+\omega)
is a post-Lie algebra if and only if

(i)

(\mathfrak{g},\pi)
is a Lie algebra;

(ii)

x\rhd\pi(y,z)+\omega(x,\pi(y,z))=\pi(x\rhd y,z)+\pi(y,x\rhd z)+\pi(\omega(x,y),z)+\pi(y,\omega(x,z));

(iii)

\big(\omega(x,y)-\omega(y,x)+\pi(x,y)\big)\rhd z+\omega\big(x\rhd y-y\rhd x,z\big)+\omega(\omega(x,y)-\omega(y,x)+\pi(x,y),z)\\
=\omega(x,y\rhd z)+x\rhd\omega(y,z)-\omega(y,x\rhd z)-y\rhd\omega_{1}(x,z)+\omega(x,\omega(y,z))-\omega(y,\omega(x,z)).

In this case, we say that
(\mathfrak{g},\pi,\rhd+\omega)

is a
post-Lie deformation
of the pre-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\rhd)
.

In the sequel, we give an intrinsic explanation of the above compatibility conditions, and give the differential graded Lie algebra that governs post-Lie deformations of a pre-Lie algebra. First we recall the controlling algebra of post-Lie algebras. We will write
\mathbb{S}_{n}
for the group of permutations on
n
symbols. A permutation
\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{n}
is called an
(i,n-i)
-shuffle if
\sigma(1)<\ldots<\sigma(i)
and
\sigma(i+1)<\ldots<\sigma(n)
. If
i=0
or
n
, we assume
\sigma={\rm{Id}}
. The set of all
(i,n-i)
-shuffles will be denoted by
\mathbb{S}_{(i,n-i)}
. The notion of an
(i_{1},\ldots,i_{k})
-shuffle and the set
\mathbb{S}_{(i_{1},\ldots,i_{k})}
are defined analogously.

Let
V
be a vector space. Consider the graded vector space

C_{\mathsf{PL}}^{*}(V,V)=\oplus_{n=0}^{+\infty}C_{\mathsf{PL}}^{n}(V,V),

given by the formula

C_{\mathsf{PL}}^{n}(V,V)=\oplus_{i=0}^{n}\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{i}V\otimes\wedge^{n+1-i}V,V).

We will write
f=(f_{0},\ldots,f_{n})\in C_{\mathsf{PL}}^{n}(V,V)
, where
f_{i}\in\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{i}V\otimes\wedge^{n+1-i}V,V)
. For
f\in C_{\mathsf{PL}}^{n}(V,V)
and
g\in C_{\mathsf{PL}}^{m}(V,V)
, define
f\circ g=\big((f\circ g)_{0},\ldots,(f\circ g)_{n+m}\big)\in C^{n+m}_{\mathsf{PL}}(V,V)
for
0\leq k\leq m
by

\displaystyle(f\circ g)_{k}(x_{1}\ldots x_{k}\otimes x_{k+1}\ldots x_{n+m+1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{(k-j,j)}\atop 0\leq j\leq k}\sum_{\tau\in\mathbb{S}_{(m+1-j,n+j-k)}}(-1)^{\sigma}(-1)^{\tau}(-1)^{m(k-j)}

\displaystyle f_{k-j}\big(x_{\sigma(1)}\ldots x_{\sigma(k-j)}\otimes g_{j}(x_{\sigma(k-j+1)}\ldots x_{\sigma(k)}\otimes x_{k+\tau(1)}\ldots x_{k+\tau(m+1-j)})x_{k+\tau(m+2-j)}\ldots x_{k+\tau(n+m+1-k)}\big),

and for
m+1\leq k\leq m+n
by

\displaystyle(f\circ g)_{k}(x_{1}\ldots x_{k}\otimes x_{k+1}\ldots x_{n+m+1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{(j,m+1-j,k-m-1)}\atop 0\leq j\leq m}(-1)^{\sigma}f_{k-m}\big(g_{j}(x_{\sigma(1)}\ldots x_{\sigma(j)}\otimes x_{\sigma(j+1)}\ldots x_{\sigma(m+1)})x_{\sigma(m+2)}\ldots x_{\sigma(k)}\otimes x_{k+1}\ldots x_{n+m+1}\big)

\displaystyle+\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{(k-j,j)}\atop 0\leq j\leq m}\sum_{\tau\in\mathbb{S}_{(m+1-j,n+j-k)}}(-1)^{\sigma}(-1)^{\tau}(-1)^{m(k-j)}

\displaystyle f_{k-j}\big(x_{\sigma(1)}\ldots x_{\sigma(k-j)}\otimes g_{j}(x_{\sigma(k-j+1)}\ldots x_{\sigma(k)}\otimes x_{k+\tau(1)}\ldots x_{k+\tau(m+1-j)})x_{k+\tau(m+2-j)}\ldots x_{k+\tau(n+m+1-k)}\big).

Theorem 2.5
.

[
28
]

Let
V
be a vector space. Then
(C_{\mathsf{PL}}^{*}(V,V),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathsf{PL}})
is a graded Lie algebra, where the graded Lie bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathsf{PL}}
is given by

(3)

[f,g]_{\mathsf{PL}}:=f\circ g-(-1)^{nm}g\circ f,\quad\forall f\in C_{\mathsf{PL}}^{n}(V,V),~g\in C_{\mathsf{PL}}^{m}(V,V).

Moreover, its Maurer-Cartan elements are precisely post-Lie algebra structures on the vector space
V
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\rhd)
be a pre-Lie algebra. By Theorem
2.5
,
\rhd\in\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
is a Maurer-Cartan element of the graded Lie algebra
(C_{\mathsf{PL}}^{*}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathsf{PL}})
.
Define the inner derivation
d_{\rhd}:C_{\mathsf{PL}}^{*}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})\,\rightarrow\,C_{\mathsf{PL}}^{*}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
by

\displaystyle d_{\rhd}(f):=[\rhd,f]_{\mathsf{PL}},\,\,\,\,\forall f\in C_{\mathsf{PL}}^{*}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}).

Since a pre-Lie algebra is a special post-Lie algebra, so we have
[\rhd,\rhd]_{\mathsf{PL}}=0
and we obtain a
differential graded Lie algebra
(C_{\mathsf{PL}}^{*}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathsf{PL}},d_{\rhd})
. This differential graded Lie algebra governs post-Lie deformations of the pre-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\rhd)
.

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\rhd)
be a pre-Lie algebra. Then
(\pi,\omega)
is a post-Lie deformation of the pre-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\rhd)
, where
\pi:\mathfrak{g}\wedge\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}
and
\omega:\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}
are linear maps, if and only if
\Pi=(\pi,\omega)
is a Maurer-Cartan
element of the differential graded Lie algebra

(C_{\mathsf{PL}}^{*}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathsf{PL}},d_{\rhd})
.

Proof.

By Theorem
2.5
,
(\pi,\omega)
is a post-Lie deformation of the pre-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\rhd)
if and only if

[(\pi,\rhd+\omega),(\pi,\rhd+\omega)]_{\mathsf{PL}}=0.

By the fact
[\rhd,\rhd]_{\mathsf{PL}}=0
, this is equivalent to

2[\rhd,\Pi]_{\mathsf{PL}}+[\Pi,\Pi]_{\mathsf{PL}}=0.

Therefore, we have

d_{\rhd}\Pi+\frac{1}{2}[\Pi,\Pi]_{\mathsf{PL}}=0,

which implies that
\Pi=(\pi,\omega)
is a Maurer-Cartan element.
∎

Remark 2.7
.

The condition that
\Pi=(\pi,\omega)
is a Maurer-Cartan
element of the differential graded Lie algebra

(C_{\mathsf{PL}}^{*}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathsf{PL}},d_{\rhd})
is equivalent to the conditions given in Theorem
2.4
.

3.
Post-Lie cohomologies and formal post-Lie deformations of pre-Lie algebras

In this section, we define the post-Lie cohomology of a pre-Lie algebra. As an application, we classify infinitesimal post-Lie deformations of a pre-Lie algebra using the second cohomology group of the relevant complex.

With the above notations,

\displaystyle\partial(f)_{1}(x_{1}\otimes x_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(x_{1})\rhd x_{2}+x_{1}\rhd f(x_{2})-f(x_{1}\rhd x_{2})=0.

Therefore, we have the following proposition.

Proposition 3.6
.

A
1
-cochain
f\in\mathfrak{C}^{1}_{\mathsf{PL}}(\mathfrak{g};\mathfrak{g})
is a
1
-cocycle if and only if
f
is a derivation. Moreover,

\mathcal{H}^{1}(\mathfrak{g};\mathfrak{g})=\mathsf{Der}(\mathfrak{g}).

Note that
\mathfrak{C}^{2}_{\mathsf{PL}}(\mathfrak{g};\mathfrak{g})=\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})\oplus\mathrm{Hom}(\otimes^{2}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
. Let
\pi\in\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
and
\omega\in\mathrm{Hom}(\otimes^{2}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
be linear maps, i.e.
(\pi,\omega)\in\mathfrak{C}^{2}_{\mathsf{PL}}(\mathfrak{g};\mathfrak{g})
. Moreover,
(\pi,\omega)\in\mathfrak{C}^{2}_{\mathsf{PL}}(\mathfrak{g};\mathfrak{g})
is closed if and only if

(7)

\displaystyle\partial(\pi,\omega)_{1}(x;y,z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x\rhd\pi(y,z)-\pi(x\rhd y,z)+\pi(x\rhd z,y)=0,

(8)

\displaystyle\partial(\pi,\omega)_{2}(x,y;z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\big(\omega(x,y)-\omega(y,x)+\pi(x,y)\big)\rhd z-\omega\big(x\rhd y-y\rhd x,z\big)

\displaystyle+\omega(x,y\rhd z)+x\rhd\omega(y,z)-\omega(y,x\rhd z)-y\rhd\omega(x,z)=0.

Let
\mathbb{R}[[t]]
be the ring of power series in one variable

t
. For any
\mathbb{R}
-linear space
\mathfrak{g}
, we let

\mathfrak{g}[[t]]
denote the vector space of formal power series in
t
with coefficients in
\mathfrak{g}
. Let
(\mathfrak{g},\rhd)
be a pre-Lie algebra.
We consider the power series

(9)

\displaystyle\pi_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{+\infty}\pi_{i}t^{i},\quad\pi_{i}\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{R}}(\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}),

(10)

\displaystyle\omega_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{+\infty}\omega_{i}t^{i},\quad\omega_{i}\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{R}}(\otimes^{2}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}).

Definition 3.7
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\rhd)
be a pre-Lie algebra. If
(\mathfrak{g}[[t]],\pi_{t},\omega_{t})
is a
\mathbb{R}[[t]]
-post-Lie algebra with
(\pi_{0},\omega_{0})=(0,\rhd)
, we say that
(\mathfrak{g}[[t]],\pi_{t},\omega_{t})

is a
formal post-Lie deformation
of the pre-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\rhd)
.

A pair
(\pi_{t},\omega_{t})
, as given above, is a formal post-Lie deformation of a pre-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\rhd)
if and only if for all
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}
, the following equalities hold:

(11)

\displaystyle\pi_{t}(\pi_{t}(x,y),z)+\pi_{t}(\pi_{t}(y,z),x)+\pi_{t}(\pi_{t}(z,x),y)=0,

(12)

\displaystyle\omega_{t}(x,\pi_{t}(y,z))=\pi_{t}(\omega_{t}(x,y),z)+\pi_{t}(y,\omega_{t}(x,z)),

(13)

\displaystyle\omega_{t}(\omega_{t}(x,y)-\omega_{t}(y,x)+\pi_{t}(x,y),z)=\omega_{t}(x,\omega_{t}(y,z))-\omega_{t}(y,\omega_{t}(x,z)).

Expanding the equations in (
11
)-(
13
) and collecting coefficients of
t^{n}
, we see that (
11
)-(
13
) are equivalent to the system of equations

(14)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i+j=n\atop i,j\geq 0}\Big(\pi_{i}(\pi_{j}(x,y),z)+\pi_{i}(\pi_{j}(y,z),x)+\pi_{i}(\pi_{j}(z,x),y)\Big)=0,

(15)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i+j=n\atop i,j\geq 0}\Big(\omega_{i}(x,\pi_{j}(y,z))-\pi_{j}(\omega_{i}(x,y),z)-\pi_{j}(y,\omega_{i}(x,z))\Big)=0,

(16)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i+j=n\atop i,j\geq 0}\Big(\omega_{i}(\omega_{j}(x,y)-\omega_{j}(y,x)+\pi_{j}(x,y),z)-\omega_{i}(x,\omega_{j}(y,z))+\omega_{i}(y,\omega_{j}(x,z))\Big)=0.

Remark 3.8
.

For
n=2
, by
\pi_{0}=0
, condition (
14
) is equivalent to the Jacobi identity of
\pi_{1}
.

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
(\pi_{t},\omega_{t})
be a formal post-Lie deformation of a pre-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\rhd)
.
Then
(\pi_{1},\omega_{1})\in\mathfrak{C}^{2}_{\mathsf{PL}}(\mathfrak{g};\mathfrak{g})
is a
2
-cocycle in the post-Lie cohomology of the pre-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\rhd)
.

Proof.

For
n=1
, (
15
) is equivalent to

x\rhd\pi_{1}(y,z)-\pi_{1}(x\rhd y,z)-\pi_{1}(y,x\rhd z)=0,

and (
16
) is equivalent to

\displaystyle(\omega_{1}(x,y)-\omega_{1}(y,x)+\pi_{1}(x,y))\rhd z+\omega_{1}(x\rhd y-y\rhd x,z)

\displaystyle-\omega_{1}(x,y\rhd z)-x\rhd\omega_{1}(y,z)+\omega_{1}(y,x\rhd z)+y\rhd\omega_{1}(x,z)=0.

By (
7
) and (
8
), we deduce that
(\pi_{1},\omega_{1})
is a
2
-cocycle. The proof is finished.
∎

Definition 3.10
.

Let
(\pi_{t},\omega_{t})
be a formal post-Lie deformation of a pre-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\rhd)
. The
2
-cocycle
(\pi_{1},\omega_{1})
is called the
infinitesimal
of the formal post-Lie deformation
(\pi_{t},\omega_{t})
of the pre-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\rhd)
.

Definition 3.11
.

Let
(\bar{\pi}_{t},\bar{\omega}_{t})
and
(\pi_{t},\omega_{t})
be formal post-Lie deformations of a pre-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\rhd)
. A

formal isomorphism
from
(\bar{\pi}_{t},\bar{\omega}_{t})
to
(\pi_{t},\omega_{t})
is a power series

\Phi_{t}=\sum_{i\geq 0}\phi_{i}t^{i}:\mathfrak{g}[[t]]\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}[[t]],

where
\phi_{i}\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
with
\phi_{0}={\rm{Id}}
, such that

(17)

\displaystyle\Phi_{t}\circ\bar{\pi}_{t}=\pi_{t}\circ(\Phi_{t}\times\Phi_{t}),

(18)

\displaystyle\Phi_{t}\circ\bar{\omega}_{t}=\omega_{t}\circ(\Phi_{t}\times\Phi_{t}).

Two formal post-Lie deformations
(\bar{\pi}_{t},\bar{\omega}_{t})
and
(\pi_{t},\omega_{t})
are said to be
equivalent
if there exists a formal isomorphism
\Phi_{t}
from
(\bar{\pi}_{t},\bar{\omega}_{t})
to
(\pi_{t},\omega_{t})
.

Theorem 3.12
.

The infinitesimals of two equivalent formal post-Lie deformations of a pre-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\rhd)
are in the same cohomology class.

Proof.

Let
\Phi_{t}:(\bar{\pi}_{t},\bar{\omega}_{t})\,\rightarrow\,(\pi_{t},\omega_{t})
be a formal isomorphism. Then for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
, we have

\displaystyle\Phi_{t}\circ\bar{\pi}_{t}(x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pi_{t}\circ(\Phi_{t}\times\Phi_{t})(x,y),

\displaystyle\Phi_{t}\circ\bar{\omega}_{t}(x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega_{t}\circ(\Phi_{t}\times\Phi_{t})(x,y).

Expanding the above identities and comparing coefficients of
t
, we have

\displaystyle\bar{\pi}_{1}(x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pi_{1}(x,y),

\displaystyle\bar{\omega}_{1}(x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega_{1}(x,y)+\phi_{1}(x)\rhd y+x\rhd\phi_{1}(y)-\phi_{1}(x\rhd y).

Thus, we have
(\bar{\pi}_{1},\bar{\omega}_{1})=(\pi_{1},\omega_{1})+\partial(\phi_{1})
, which implies that
[(\bar{\pi}_{1},\bar{\omega}_{1})]=[(\pi_{1},\omega_{1})]\in\mathcal{H}^{2}_{\mathsf{PL}}(\mathfrak{g};\mathfrak{g})
. The proof is finished.
∎

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\rhd)
be a pre-Lie algebra. In the sequel, we will also denote the binary product
\rhd
by
\omega
.

Definition 3.13
.

A formal post-Lie deformation
(\pi_{t},\omega_{t})
of a pre-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\omega)
is said to be
trivial
if it is equivalent to
(0,\omega)
, i.e. there exists
\Phi_{t}=\sum_{i\geq 0}\phi_{i}t^{i}:\mathfrak{g}[[t]]\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}[[t]]
, where
\phi_{i}\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
with
\phi_{0}={\rm{Id}}
, such that

(19)

\displaystyle\Phi_{t}\circ\bar{\pi}_{t}=0,

(20)

\displaystyle\Phi_{t}\circ\bar{\omega}_{t}=\omega\circ(\Phi_{t}\times\Phi_{t}).

Definition 3.14
.

A pre-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\omega)
is said to be
post-Lie rigid
if every formal post-Lie deformation of
(\mathfrak{g},\omega)
is trivial.

Theorem 3.15
.

If
\mathcal{H}^{2}_{\mathsf{PL}}(\mathfrak{g};\mathfrak{g})=0
, then the pre-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\omega)
is post-Lie rigid.

Proof.

Let
(\pi_{t},\omega_{t})
be a formal post-Lie deformation of a pre-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\omega)
. By Proposition
3.9
, we deduce that
(\pi_{1},\omega_{1})
is a
2
-cocycle. By
\mathcal{H}^{2}_{\mathsf{PL}}(\mathfrak{g};\mathfrak{g})=0,
there exists a 1-cochain
\phi_{1}\in\mathfrak{C}^{1}_{\mathsf{PL}}(\mathfrak{g};\mathfrak{g})
such that

(21)

\displaystyle(\pi_{1},\omega_{1})=-\partial(\phi_{1})=(0,-\omega\circ(\phi_{1}\times{\rm{Id}})-\omega\circ({\rm{Id}}\times\phi_{1})+\phi_{1}\circ\omega).

Then setting
\Phi_{t}={{\rm{Id}}}+\phi_{1}t
, we have a deformation
(\bar{\pi}_{t},\bar{\omega}_{t})
, where

\displaystyle\bar{\pi}_{t}(x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(\Phi_{t}^{-1}\circ\pi_{t}\circ(\Phi_{t}\times\Phi_{t})\big)(x,y),

\displaystyle\bar{\omega}_{t}(x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(\Phi_{t}^{-1}\circ\omega_{t}\circ(\Phi_{t}\times\Phi_{t})\big)(x,y).

Thus,
(\bar{\pi}_{t},\bar{\omega}_{t})
is equivalent to
(\pi_{t},\omega_{t})
. Moreover, we have

\displaystyle\bar{\pi}_{t}(x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle({{\rm{Id}}}-\phi_{1}t+\phi_{1}^{2}t^{2}+\cdots+(-1)^{i}\phi_{1}^{i}t^{i}+\cdots)(\pi_{t}(x+\phi_{1}(x)t,y+\phi_{1}(y)t)),

\displaystyle\bar{\omega}_{t}(x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle({{\rm{Id}}}-\phi_{1}t+\phi_{1}^{2}t^{2}+\cdots+(-1)^{i}\phi_{1}^{i}t^{i}+\cdots)(\omega_{t}(x+\phi_{1}(x)t,y+\phi_{1}(y)t)).

Thus, we have

\displaystyle\bar{\pi}_{t}(x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{\pi}_{2}(x,y)t^{2}+\cdots,

\displaystyle\bar{\omega}_{t}(x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega(x,y)+(\omega_{1}(x,y)+\omega(x,\phi_{1}(y))+\omega(\phi_{1}(x),y)-\phi_{1}(\omega(x,y)))t+\bar{\omega}_{2}(x,y)t^{2}+\cdots.

By (
21
), we have

\displaystyle\bar{\pi}_{t}(x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{\pi}_{2}(x,y)t^{2}+\cdots,

\displaystyle\bar{\omega}_{t}(x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega(x,y)+\bar{\omega}_{2}(x,y)t^{2}+\cdots.

Then by repeating the argument, we can show that
(\pi_{t},\omega_{t})
is equivalent to
(0,\omega)
.
∎

4.
Post-Lie deformations of pre-Lie algebras in Regularity Structures

In this section, we want to apply the formulism described above to the post-Lie algebra appearing in the context of Regularity Structures
[
8
,
26
]
.

Decorated trees as introduced in

[
6
]
are described in the following way. We suppose given two symbols
I
and
\Xi
and let
\mathcal{D}:=\{I,\Xi\}\times\mathbb{N}^{d+1}
define the set of edge decorations. These two symbols represent a convolution with a kernel
K
and a noise term
\xi
that correspond to the mild formulation of a singular stochastic partial differential equation of the form:

u=K*\left(f(\partial^{\alpha}u,\alpha\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1})+g(\partial^{\alpha}u,\alpha\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1})\xi\right)

where
*
is space-time convolution and
f
and
g
are non-linearities depending on a finite number of
\partial^{\alpha}u,\alpha\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
.
For a system and more noises, one has to add more symbols.
Decorated trees over
\mathcal{D}
are of the form
T_{\mathfrak{e}}^{\mathfrak{n}}=(T,\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{e})
where
T
is a non-planar rooted tree with node set
N_{T}
and edge set
E_{T}
. The maps
\mathfrak{n}:N_{T}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}^{d+1}
and
\mathfrak{e}:E_{T}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}
are node, respectively edge, decorations. We denote the set of decorated trees by
\mathfrak{T}
.
We introduce a symbolic notation for denoting these decorated trees:

–

An edge decorated by
(I,a)\in\mathcal{D}
is denoted by
I_{a}
. The symbol
I_{a}
is also viewed as the operation that grafts a tree onto a new root via a new edge with edge decoration
(I,a)
. The new root at hand remains decorated with
0
.

–

An edge decorated by
(\Xi,0)\in\mathcal{D}
is denoted by
\Xi
. We suppose that these edges are terminal edges. A terminal edge of a tree
T
is an edge with one extremity being a leaf of
T
. We suppose also that these edges have zero node decoration at their leaves that it appears at most once for each node.
Let us stress that we do not allow edges of the form
(\Xi,b),~b\neq 0
and that an edge which is not a terminal edge must be decorated by
(I,a)
.

–

A factor
X^{\ell}
encodes a single node
\bullet^{\ell}
decorated by
\ell\in\mathbb{N}^{d+1}
. We write
X_{i}
,
i\in\{0,1,\ldots,d\}
, to denote
X^{e_{i}}
. Here, we have denoted by
e_{i}
the vector of
\mathbb{N}^{d+1}
with
1
in
i
th position and
0
otherwise. The element
X^{0}
is identified with the empty tree
\mathbf{1}
.

Given a decorated tree
\tau
there exist decorated trees
\tau_{1},...,\tau_{r}
such that

(22)

\tau=X^{\ell}\Xi^{m}\prod_{i=1}^{r}I_{a_{i}}(\tau_{i}),

where
m\in\{0,1\}
,
\ell,a_{i}\in\mathbb{N}^{d+1}
and the notation means that
\tau
is formed of a root decorated by
\ell
. The edges connected to this root are: an edge decorated by
(\Xi,0)
, edges decorated by
(I,a_{i})
with the other extremities being the roots of the
\tau_{i}
.
A tree of the form
I_{a}(\tau)
is called a planted tree as there is only one edge connecting the root to the rest of the tree.
The decomposition (
22
) can be viewed as a unique product decomposition for the tree product as
X^{\ell}
is the decorated tree

\bullet^{\ell}
and
\Xi
is the decorated tree with one edge decorated by
(\Xi,0)
.

We define a product called grafting product:

\sigma\curvearrowright^{a}\tau:=\sum_{v\in N_{\tau}}\sigma\curvearrowright^{a}_{v}\tau,

where
\sigma
and
\tau
are two decorated rooted trees,
N_{\tau}
is the set of vertices of
\tau
and where
\sigma\curvearrowright^{a}_{v}\tau
is obtained by grafting the tree
\sigma
on the tree
\tau
at vertex
v
by means of a new edge decorated by
a\in\mathbb{N}^{d+1}
. Grafting onto noise-type edges, that is, edges decorated by
(\Xi,0)

The family of grafting products
(\curvearrowright^{b})_{b\in\mathbb{N}^{d+1}}
forms a multi-pre-Lie algebra structure on the decorated rooted trees, in the sense that they satisfy the following identities:

\left(\tau_{1}\curvearrowright^{a}\tau_{2}\right)\curvearrowright^{b}\tau_{3}-\tau_{1}\curvearrowright^{a}(\tau_{2}\curvearrowright^{b}\tau_{3})=(\tau_{2}\curvearrowright^{b}\tau_{1})\curvearrowright^{a}\tau_{3}-\tau_{2}\curvearrowright^{b}(\tau_{1}\curvearrowright^{a}\tau_{3})

where the
\tau_{i}
are decorated trees and
a,b
belong to
\mathbb{N}^{d+1}
.
It has first been introduced in
[
7
, Prop. 4.21]
for writing up renormalised equations.
This multi-pre-Lie algebra can be summarised into a single pre-Lie structure on the space of planted trees, given by the product:

I_{a}(\sigma)\curvearrowright I_{b}(\tau):=I_{b}(\sigma\curvearrowright^{a}\tau).

This was first noticed in
[
19
]
(see also
[
9
, Prop. 3.2]
).

Furthermore, the products
\curvearrowright^{a}
can be deformed via a multi-pre-Lie algebra isomorphism described in
[
9
, Sec. 2.2]
. The deformed products, first introduced in
[
7
]
, are given by:

\sigma\widehat{\curvearrowright}^{a}\tau:=\sum_{v\in N_{\tau}}\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}^{d+1}}{\mathfrak{n}_{v}\choose\ell}\sigma\curvearrowright_{v}^{a-\ell}(\uparrow_{v}^{-\ell}\tau),

where
\mathfrak{n}_{v}\in\mathbb{N}^{d+1}
denotes the decoration at the vertex
v
and the operator
\uparrow_{v}^{-\ell}
is defined as adding
-\ell
to the node decoration of
v
.
If there exists a unique pair
(b,\alpha)\in\mathbb{N}^{d+1}\times\mathbb{N}^{d+1}
such that
a=\ell+b
and
\mathfrak{n}_{v}=\ell+\alpha
then one can compute the decorations of the new decorated tree. If this condition is not satisfied, then the decorated tree is set to be zero. Given a scaling
\mathfrak{s}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d+1}=\mathbb{N}^{d+1}\setminus\{0\}
we define the
grading
of a tree as the sum of the gradings of its edges and denote it by
|\cdot|_{\text{grad}}
:

|\tau|_{\text{grad}}:=\sum_{e\in E_{\tau}}\big|\mathfrak{e}(e)\big|_{\mathfrak{s}}

where
E_{\tau}
are the edges of
\tau
,
\mathfrak{e}(e)=(\mathfrak{e}(e)_{1},\mathfrak{e}(e)_{2})\in\mathcal{D}
is the decoration of the edge
e
and
\big|\mathfrak{e}(e)\big|_{\mathfrak{s}}
is defined as
\big|\mathfrak{e}(e)_{2}\big|_{\mathfrak{s}}
. For a given
\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{d+1}
, one has:

|\mathbf{n}|_{\mathfrak{s}}:=\sum_{i=0}^{d}s_{i}n_{i}.

The scaling
\mathfrak{s}
comes from the anisotropic Euclidean norm used in the context of singular SPDEs. In practice, when the kernel
K
is given as the inverse of the operator
\partial_{t}-\Delta
, the first coordinate of
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
is the time and counts double in comparison to the other spatial components (
\mathfrak{s}=(2,1,...,1)
). It is called the parabolic scaling.
One notices that
\widehat{\curvearrowright}^{a}
is a deformation of
\curvearrowright^{a}
in the sense that:

\sigma\widehat{\curvearrowright}^{a}\tau=\sigma\curvearrowright^{a}\tau+\hbox{ lower grading terms}.

One may summarise the above family of multi-pre-Lie products into a single pre-Lie product
\widehat{\curvearrowright}
:

I_{a}(\sigma)\,\widehat{\curvearrowright}\,I_{b}(\tau):=I_{b}(\sigma\,\widehat{\curvearrowright}^{a}\,\tau).

[x,y]_{1}=0
for
x,y\in\tilde{\mathcal{V}}
,
[x,y]_{1}=0
for
x,y\in\mathbb{R}X_{0}\oplus\ldots\oplus\mathbb{R}X_{d}
and as

[I_{a}(\tau),X_{i}]_{1}=I_{a-e_{i}}(\tau).

We define a product
\widehat{\triangleright}
on
\mathcal{V}
for every
a,b\in\mathbb{N}^{d+1},\,i,j\in\{0,\ldots,d\}
as:

X_{i}\,\widehat{\triangleright}\,I_{a}(\tau)=I_{a}(\uparrow^{i}\tau),\quad I_{a}(\tau)\,\widehat{\triangleright}\,X_{i}=0,\quad X_{i}\,\widehat{\triangleright}\,X_{j}=0,

and

I_{a}(\sigma)\,\widehat{\triangleright}\,I_{b}(\tau)=I_{a}(\sigma)\,\widehat{\curvearrowright}\,I_{b}(\tau).

Theorem 4.2
.

(
[
8
, Theorem 4.4]
)

The triple
(\mathcal{V},[\cdot,\cdot]_{1},\widehat{\triangleright})
is a post-Lie algebra.

Remark 4.3
.

By the Guin-Oudom functor, the universal enveloping
algebra of the post-Lie algebra
(\mathcal{V},[\cdot,\cdot]_{1},\widehat{\triangleright})
is a post-Hopf algebra
(U(\mathcal{V}),\cdot,\widehat{\triangleright})

[
29
]
. Moreover, the graded dual Hopf algebra of the sub-adjacent Hopf algebra
(U(\mathcal{V}),*)
has been used in regularity structures
[
6
,
24
]
.

Now, we want to see the triple
(\mathcal{V},[\cdot,\cdot]_{1},\widehat{\triangleright})
as a post-Lie deformation of a pre-Lie algebra
(\mathcal{V},\triangleright)
where the product
\triangleright
is given by

(24)

X_{i}\,\triangleright\,I_{a}(\tau)=I_{a}(\uparrow^{i}\tau),\quad I_{a}(\tau)\,\triangleright\,X_{i}=0,\quad X_{i}\,\triangleright\,X_{j}=0,

and

I_{a}(\sigma)\,\triangleright\,I_{b}(\tau)=I_{a}(\sigma)\,\curvearrowright\,I_{b}(\tau).

Proposition 4.4
.

With the above notations,
(\mathcal{V},\triangleright)
is a pre-Lie algebra.

Proof.

One has to check

\left(\tau_{1}\triangleright\tau_{2}\right)\triangleright\tau_{3}-\tau_{1}\triangleright(\tau_{2}\triangleright\tau_{3})=(\tau_{2}\triangleright\tau_{1})\triangleright\tau_{3}-\tau_{2}\triangleright(\tau_{1}\triangleright\tau_{3})

where the
\tau_{i}
are of the form
X_{i}
and
I_{a}(\sigma)
. We have to look at the different cases.

(i) If the
\tau_{i}
are all planted decorated trees then the identity follows from the fact that the planted decorated equipped with the grafting operator
\curvearrowright
is a pre-Lie algebra.

(ii) If
\tau_{3}
is of the form
X_{i}
, the identity is trivially equal to zero.

(iii) It remains two cases to check for the form of
(\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\tau_{3}):

(X_{i},X_{j},I_{a}(\sigma)),\quad(X_{i},I_{b}(\tau),I_{a}(\sigma)).

We omit
(I_{b}(\tau),X_{i},I_{a}(\sigma))
as it gives the same identity as
(X_{i},I_{b}(\tau),I_{a}(\sigma))
.
Let us start with
(X_{i},X_{j},I_{a}(\sigma))
, one has

\displaystyle\left(\tau_{1}\triangleright\tau_{2}\right)\triangleright\tau_{3}-\tau_{1}\triangleright(\tau_{2}\triangleright\tau_{3})

\displaystyle=\left(X_{i}\triangleright X_{j}\right)\triangleright I_{a}(\sigma)-X_{i}\triangleright(X_{j}\triangleright I_{a}(\sigma))

\displaystyle=-X_{i}\triangleright(X_{j}\triangleright I_{a}(\sigma))

\displaystyle=-I_{a}(\uparrow^{i}\uparrow^{j}\sigma).

On the other hand, one has

\displaystyle(\tau_{2}\triangleright\tau_{1})\triangleright\tau_{3}-\tau_{2}\triangleright(\tau_{1}\triangleright\tau_{3})

\displaystyle=(X_{j}\triangleright X_{i})\triangleright I_{a}(\sigma)-X_{j}\triangleright(X_{i}\triangleright I_{a}(\sigma))

\displaystyle=-X_{j}\triangleright(X_{i}\triangleright I_{a}(\sigma))

\displaystyle=-I_{a}(\uparrow^{j}\uparrow^{i}\sigma).

We conclude from the fact that
\uparrow^{i}\uparrow^{j}=\uparrow^{j}\uparrow^{i}
.
We continue with the case
(X_{i},I_{b}(\tau),I_{a}(\sigma))
.
One has

\displaystyle\left(\tau_{1}\triangleright\tau_{2}\right)\triangleright\tau_{3}-\tau_{1}\triangleright(\tau_{2}\triangleright\tau_{3})

\displaystyle=\left(X_{i}\triangleright I_{b}(\tau)\right)\triangleright I_{a}(\sigma)-X_{i}\triangleright(I_{b}(\tau)\triangleright I_{a}(\sigma))

\displaystyle=-I_{b}(\tau)\triangleright(X_{i}\triangleright I_{a}(\sigma)),

where the derivation identity (
4
) for
\uparrow^{i}
is used. On the other hand, one has

\displaystyle(\tau_{2}\triangleright\tau_{1})\triangleright\tau_{3}-\tau_{2}\triangleright(\tau_{1}\triangleright\tau_{3})

\displaystyle=(I_{b}(\tau)\triangleright X_{i})\triangleright I_{a}(\sigma)-I_{b}(\tau)\triangleright(X_{i}\triangleright I_{a}(\sigma))

\displaystyle=-I_{b}(\tau)\triangleright(X_{i}\triangleright I_{a}(\sigma)),

which allows to conclude. The proof is finished.
∎

Remark 4.5
.

The previous pre-Lie structure could be seen as a way to define a new pre-Lie algebra from a pre-Lie product and a derivation.
Indeed, we suppose given a pre-Lie algebra
(\mathcal{V},\curvearrowright)
and commutative derivations
\uparrow^{i}
indexed by
\left\{0,...,d\right\}
for this pre-Lie algebra. One has for every
x,y\in\mathcal{V}

\uparrow^{i}\left(x\curvearrowright y\right)=\left(\uparrow^{i}x\right)\curvearrowright y+x\curvearrowright\uparrow^{i}\left(y\right).

Then, we extend the space
\mathcal{V}
into
\tilde{\mathcal{V}}
by adding new elements
X_{i}
. We can then equip
\tilde{\mathcal{V}}
with a pre-Lie product
\tilde{\curvearrowright}
viewed as an extension of
\curvearrowright
to
\tilde{\mathcal{V}}
:

\displaystyle x\,\tilde{\curvearrowright}\,y=x\curvearrowright y,\quad X_{i}\,\tilde{\curvearrowright}\,x=\uparrow^{i}x,

\displaystyle X_{i}\,\tilde{\curvearrowright}\,X_{j}=0,\quad x\,\tilde{\curvearrowright}\,X_{i}=0.

for every
x,y\in\tilde{\mathcal{V}}
. The proof follows exactly the same arguments as above which are the fact that
\curvearrowright
is a pre-Lie product and the
\uparrow^{i}
are commutative derivations for this product.

Theorem 4.6
.

The post-Lie algebra
(\mathcal{V},[\cdot,\cdot]_{1},\widehat{\triangleright})
in Theorem
4.2
is a post-Lie deformation of the pre-Lie algebra
(\mathcal{V},[\cdot,\cdot]_{0}=0,\triangleright)
in Proposition
4.4
.

Proof.

One can analyse the post-Lie algebraic structure
(\mathcal{V},[\cdot,\cdot]_{1},\widehat{\triangleright})
in the following way:

\displaystyle{}_{1}

\displaystyle=[I_{a}(\tau),X_{i}]_{0}+\hbox{lower grading terms},

\displaystyle I_{a}(\sigma)\,\widehat{\triangleright}\,I_{b}(\tau)

\displaystyle=I_{a}(\sigma)\,\triangleright\,I_{b}(\tau)+\hbox{lower grading terms}.

More precisely, we define the linear map
\pi:\wedge^{2}\mathcal{V}\,\rightarrow\,\mathcal{V}
by

\displaystyle\pi(u,v):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}I_{a-e_{i}}(\tau),&\mbox{$u=I_{a}(\tau),v=X_{i}$,}\\
-I_{a-e_{i}}(\tau),&\mbox{$u=X_{i},v=I_{a}(\tau)$,}\\
0,&\mbox{other case.}\end{array}\right.

And define the linear map
\omega:\otimes^{2}\mathcal{V}\,\rightarrow\,\mathcal{V}
by

\displaystyle\omega(u,v):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\sum_{v\in N_{\tau}}\sum_{|\ell|\neq 0}{\mathfrak{n}_{v}\choose\ell}I_{b}(\sigma\curvearrowright_{v}^{a-\ell}(\uparrow_{v}^{-\ell}\tau)),&\mbox{$u=I_{a}(\sigma),v=I_{b}(\tau)$,}\\
0,&\mbox{other case.}\end{array}\right.

By direct computation, we obtain
[u,v]_{1}=[u,v]_{0}+\pi(u,v)
and
u\,\widehat{\triangleright}\,v=u\,\triangleright\,v+\omega(u,v)
. Therefore, the post-Lie algebra
(\mathcal{V},[\cdot,\cdot]_{1},\widehat{\triangleright})
is a post-Lie deformation of the pre-Lie algebra
(\mathcal{V},[\cdot,\cdot]_{0}=0,\triangleright)
.
∎

Remark 4.7
.

Let
t\in\mathbb{R}
be a real parameter. Define the Lie bracket on
\mathcal{V}=\tilde{\mathcal{V}}\oplus\mathbb{R}X_{0}\oplus\ldots\oplus\mathbb{R}X_{d}
as
[x,y]_{1,t}=0
for
x,y\in\tilde{\mathcal{V}}
,
[x,y]_{1,t}=0
for
x,y\in\mathbb{R}X_{0}\oplus\ldots\oplus\mathbb{R}X_{d}
and as

[I_{a}(\tau),X_{i}]_{1,t}=[I_{a}(\tau),X_{i}]_{0}+tI_{a-e_{i}}(\tau).

We define a product
\widehat{\triangleright}
on
\mathcal{V}
for every
a,b\in\mathbb{N}^{d+1},\,i,j\in\{0,\ldots,d\}
as:

X_{i}\,\widehat{\triangleright_{t}}\,I_{a}(\tau)=I_{a}(\uparrow^{i}\tau),\quad I_{a}(\tau)\,\widehat{\triangleright_{t}}\,X_{i}=0,\quad X_{i}\,\widehat{\triangleright_{t}}\,X_{j}=0,

and

I_{a}(\sigma)\,\widehat{\triangleright}_{t}\,I_{b}(\tau)=I_{a}(\sigma)\,\triangleright\,I_{b}(\tau)+\sum_{v\in N_{\tau}}\sum_{|\ell|\neq 0}{\mathfrak{n}_{v}\choose\ell}t^{|\ell|}I_{b}(\sigma\curvearrowright_{v}^{a-\ell}(\uparrow_{v}^{-\ell}\tau)).

By a similar computation in Theorem
4.2
, for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
, the triple
(\mathcal{V},[\cdot,\cdot]_{1,t},\widehat{\triangleright_{t}})
is a post-Lie algebra. Moreover,
(\mathcal{V},[\cdot,\cdot]_{1,t},\widehat{\triangleright_{t}})
is a post-Lie deformation of the pre-Lie algebra
(\mathcal{V},[\cdot,\cdot]_{0}=0,\triangleright)
.

Inspired by the above remark, we can construct a formal post-Lie deformation as following: for
i=1,\cdots,
we define the linear maps
\pi_{i}:\wedge^{2}\mathcal{V}\,\rightarrow\,\mathcal{V}
, by

\pi_{1}(u,v):=\pi(u,v),\,\,\pi_{2}=\cdots=\pi_{n}=\cdots=0;

for
i=1,\cdots,
we define the linear maps
\omega_{i}:\otimes^{2}\mathcal{V}\,\rightarrow\,\mathcal{V}
by

\displaystyle\omega_{i}(u,v):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\sum_{v\in N_{\tau}}\sum_{|\ell|=i}{\mathfrak{n}_{v}\choose\ell}I_{b}(\sigma\curvearrowright_{v}^{a-\ell}(\uparrow_{v}^{-\ell}\tau)),&\mbox{$u=I_{a}(\sigma),v=I_{b}(\tau)$,}\\
0,&\mbox{other case.}\end{array}\right.

Now we consider the
t
as a formal variable. Further, we consider the formal power series

(33)

\displaystyle\pi_{t}=\sum_{i=0}^{+\infty}\pi_{i}t^{i}=\pi_{1}t,\,\,\,\,~~~\omega_{t}=\sum_{i=0}^{+\infty}\omega_{i}t^{i},

here
\pi_{0}=0
and
\omega_{0}=\triangleright
.

Theorem 4.8
.

With the above notations,
(\mathcal{V}[[t]],\pi_{t},\omega_{t})
is an
\mathbb{R}[[t]]
-post-Lie algebra and
(\pi_{t},\omega_{t})
is a formal post-Lie deformation of the pre-Lie algebra
(\mathcal{V},[\cdot,\cdot]_{0}=0,\triangleright)
in Proposition
4.4
.

Proof.

We only need to prove that for any
n
and
x,y,z\in\mathcal{V}
, the equations (
14
), (
15
) and (
16
) hold.
Since
\pi_{1}
is a Lie algebra structure on
\mathcal{V}
and
\pi_{i}=0,i\not=1
, the equation (
14
) holds immediately. One can show the equations (
15
) and (
16
) directly by a tedious computation. Here we take a different approach.
We suppose that the equation (
16
) does not hold. This means that there exist
n_{0}
and
x_{0},y_{0},z_{0}\in\mathcal{V}
such that

\sum\limits_{i+j=n_{0}\atop i,j\geq 0}\Big(\omega_{i}(\omega_{j}(x_{0},y_{0})-\omega_{j}(y_{0},x_{0})+\pi_{j}(x_{0},y_{0}),z_{0})-\omega_{i}(x_{0},\omega_{j}(y_{0},z_{0}))+\omega_{i}(y_{0},\omega_{j}(x_{0},z_{0}))\Big)\not=0.

By the definition of
\omega_{i}
, we obtain a nonzero polynomial

\displaystyle\omega_{t}(\omega_{t}(x_{0},y_{0})-\omega_{t}(y_{0},x_{0})+\pi_{t}(x_{0},y_{0}),z_{0})-\omega_{t}(x_{0},\omega_{t}(y_{0},z_{0}))+\omega_{t}(y_{0},\omega_{t}(x_{0},z_{0}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\sum\limits_{i+j=n\atop i,j\geq 0}\Big(\omega_{i}(\omega_{j}(x_{0},y_{0})-\omega_{j}(y_{0},x_{0})+\pi_{j}(x_{0},y_{0}),z_{0})-\omega_{i}(x_{0},\omega_{j}(y_{0},z_{0}))+\omega_{i}(y_{0},\omega_{j}(x_{0},z_{0}))\Big)t^{n}.

According to the fundamental theorem of algebra, the above nonzero polynomial has finite many real roots. Note that for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
(\mathcal{V},[\cdot,\cdot]_{1,t},\widehat{\triangleright_{t}})
is a post-Lie algebra, it means that the above nonzero polynomial has infinite many real roots. Therefore, our assumption gives rise to a contradiction. So the equation (
16
) holds. By the same method, we deduce that the equation (
15
) holds. Now the whole process of the proof is finished.
∎

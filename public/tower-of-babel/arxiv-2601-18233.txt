The global attractor of the Toner-Tu-Swift-Hohenberg equations of active turbulence and its properties
Daniel W. Boutros, Kolluru Venkata Kiran, John D. Gibbon, Rahul Pandit

Abstract
. The Toner-Tu-Swift-Hohenberg (TTSH) equations are one of the basic equations that are used to model turbulent behaviour in active matter, specifically the swarming of bacteria in suspension. They combine features of the incompressible Navier-Stokes, the Toner-Tu and Swift-Hohenberg equations, together with the important properties that they are linearly driven, and that the Laplacian diffusion is taken to be negative in combination with hyper-dissipation. We prove that the TTSH equations possess a finite-dimensional compact global attractor on the periodic domain 𝕋 d \mathbb{T}^{d} ( d = 2 , 3 d=2,3 ) and we establish explicit estimates for its Lyapunov dimension which agree with the heuristic prediction based on the Swift-Hohenberg length scale. The predominance of this length scale (as a vortex length scale) has been observed in both numerical and experimental studies of bacterial turbulence, so our methods and results provide a rigorous theoretical foundation for this phenomenon. We also carry out pseudospectral direct numerical simulations of these PDEs in dimension d = 2 d=2 through which we obtain Lyapunov spectra for representative parameter values. We show that our numerical results are consistent with the analytically derived rigorous bounds.

(1.2)

\left(\partial_{t}+\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\cdot\nabla\right)\mbox{\boldmath$u$}+\Gamma_{0}\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$u$}+\Gamma_{2}\Delta^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}+\nabla p=-\left(\alpha+\beta|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}|^{2}\right)\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\,,\quad\nabla\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$u$}=0\,,

\mbox{\boldmath$u$}:\mathbb{T}^{3}\times[0,T]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3}

\Gamma_{0}>0

\Gamma_{2}>0

\beta>0

\alpha

\Sigma=-\alpha+\Gamma_{0}\lvert\mbox{\boldmath$k$}\rvert^{2}-\Gamma_{2}\lvert\mbox{\boldmath$k$}\rvert^{4}\,.

Thus,
\Sigma>0

\lvert\mbox{\boldmath$k$}\rvert^{2}<\lvert\mbox{\boldmath$k$}_{c}\rvert^{2}

\Sigma<0

k

\mbox{\boldmath$u$}=\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}+\varepsilon\exp(i\mbox{\boldmath$k$}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$x$}+\Sigma_{0}t)\,.

Given the instability of long wavelength perturbations (i.e.
\lvert\mbox{\boldmath$k$}\rvert^{2}<\lvert\mbox{\boldmath$k$}_{c}\rvert^{2}
), the outstanding question is the long-time behaviour of solutions of the TTSH equations. In the mathematical literature, the global well-posedness of the 3D TTSH equations (for
H^{2}
-data) has been established on the whole space in
[
67
]
. In this paper we show that the TTSH equations on the unit periodic domain
\mathbb{T}^{d}
(
d=2,3
) possess a finite-dimensional global attractor
\mathcal{A}
. The unit domain has been chosen for simplicity. In descriptive terms,
\mathcal{A}
is a finite-dimensional compact set into which solutions from all initial conditions are attracted. Its properties are therefore key to our understanding of the (asymptotic) dynamics of solutions. Moreover, the existence of
\mathcal{A}
leads to the fundamental idea of the system having a finite number of degrees of freedom, as
\mathcal{A}
is finite-dimensional. Following Landau and Lifshitz
[
42
]
, if
\eta_{res}
is the smallest resolution scale of the dynamics in a
d
-dimensional domain of volume
L^{d}
, then the number of degrees of freedom
\mathcal{N}
is defined as

(1.6)

\mathcal{N}=\left(\frac{L}{\eta_{res}}\right)^{d}\,.

For a PDE, the Lyapunov dimension
d_{L}(\mathcal{A})
of the global attractor
\mathcal{A}
is usually interpreted as
\mathcal{N}

[
16
]
, an idea which is explained in §
3
. In this paper, we will show that in the case
\Gamma_{2}^{-1},\Gamma_{0}\gg\alpha,\beta
, the dimension estimate of the global attractor leads to the following length scale

(1.7)

\eta_{res}\sim\eta_{\,\textsc{sh}}\,.

To be more precise, in the two-dimensional case we will show the following dimension estimate (for some constant
c>0
)

d_{L}(\mathcal{A})\leq c\max\left\{\left(\frac{\Gamma_{0}^{2}}{\Gamma_{2}^{2}}-\alpha\Gamma_{2}^{-1}\right)^{1/2}\,;~~\left\lvert\alpha-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\Gamma_{0}^{2}}{\Gamma_{2}}\right\rvert^{3/8}\Gamma_{2}^{-5/8}\beta^{-1/4}\right\}\,,

while in the three-dimensional case we will prove the following bound

d_{L}(\mathcal{A})\leq c\max\left\{\left(\frac{\Gamma_{0}^{2}}{\Gamma_{2}^{2}}-\alpha\Gamma_{2}^{-1}\right)^{3/4}\,;~~2^{9/4}\Gamma_{2}^{-1}\beta^{-1/2}\left\lvert\alpha-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\Gamma_{0}^{2}}{\Gamma_{2}}\right\rvert^{1/2}\right\}\,.

Therefore, in the relevant parameter ranges (i.e.
\Gamma_{2}^{-1},\Gamma_{0}\gg\alpha,\beta
) we confirm the predominance of the Swift-Hohenberg scale through the
2d
and
3d
attractor dimension estimates that are established in §
3.2
and §
3.3
(cf. Remarks
7
and
9
). This occurs because the contribution from the nonlinear terms to the asymptotic dynamics is negligible compared to the linear terms in this setting.

2.
Proof of global well-posedness and the existence of a global attractor

First we will demonstrate that the TTSH equations (
1.2
) are globally well-posed, both in dimensions two and three. The global well-posedness of these equations (on the whole space) has already been established in
[
67
]
. However, to deduce the existence of a global attractor in the case of a periodic domain we will need different a priori estimates, in particular, to establish the existence of an absorbing ball. Thus, we give a new proof of the global well-posedness of the TTSH equations below. Throughout §
2
and §
3
we will restrict to the case of the domain having unit length, we will restore the units of length later in §
4
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
,
T>0
and
d=2,3
. Then there exists a unique global weak solution
\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\in C([0,T];L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))\cap L^{2}((0,T);H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))\cap L^{4}((0,T);L^{4}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))
of the TTSH equations.

Proof.

We will prove the existence of solutions by means of the Galerkin method. Let
P_{N}
be the projection onto the first
N
eigenfunctions of the Stokes operator, where the zeroth mode is also included. We will consider the (truncated) Galerkin system with the divergence-free constraint
\mbox{div}\,\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{N}=0

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{N}+P_{N}[\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{N}\cdot\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{N}]+\nabla p_{N}

(2.1)

\displaystyle=-\alpha\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{N}-\beta\,P_{N}[\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{N}|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{N}|^{2}]-\Gamma_{0}\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{N}-\Gamma_{2}\Delta^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{N}\,.

The Galerkin system (
2.1
) is a coupled system of ODEs with quadratic nonlinearities, so we deduce that there exists a unique local-in-time solution to equation (
2.1
). We take the
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
inner product of equation (
2.1
) with
\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{N}
, which gives

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\lVert\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{N}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\alpha\lVert\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{N}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\beta\lVert\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{N}\rVert_{L^{4}}^{4}-\Gamma_{0}\lVert\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{N}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\Gamma_{2}\lVert\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{N}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}=0\,.

By using an interpolation estimate and Young’s inequality, one observes

\displaystyle\Gamma_{0}\lVert\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{N}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\Gamma_{0}\lVert\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{N}\rVert_{L^{2}}\lVert\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{N}\rVert_{L^{2}}

(2.2)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\Gamma_{0}^{2}\,\Gamma_{2}^{-1}\lVert\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{N}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\Gamma_{2}\lVert\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{N}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\,.

By using Hölder’s inequality we find

\lVert\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{N}\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq\lVert\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{N}\rVert_{L^{4}}\,,

on a unit periodic domain
[0,\,1]^{d}
. For the sake of simplicity, we will now introduce the parameter

(2.3)

\alpha_{R}\coloneqq\alpha-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\Gamma_{0}^{2}}{\Gamma_{2}}\,.

Therefore, we can rewrite the
L^{2}
-estimate as follows

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\lVert\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{N}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\alpha_{R}\lVert\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{N}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{\beta}{2}\lVert\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{N}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{4}+\frac{\beta}{2}\lVert\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{N}\rVert_{L^{4}}^{4}+\frac{1}{2}\Gamma_{2}\lVert\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{N}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq 0\,.

We will restrict ourselves to the case
\alpha_{R}<0
; otherwise all solutions will converge to zero.

To estimate the contribution of the
\alpha_{R}\lVert\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{N}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}
-term we again use Young’s inequality to find

(2.4)

\alpha_{R}\lVert\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{N}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\geq-\frac{\beta}{2}\lVert\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{N}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{4}-2\frac{\alpha_{R}^{2}}{\beta}+\lvert\alpha_{R}\rvert\lVert\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{N}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\,.

Inserting this relation into the previous estimate then gives

(2.5)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\lVert\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{N}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\lvert\alpha_{R}\rvert\lVert\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{N}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{\beta}{2}\lVert\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{N}\rVert_{L^{4}}^{4}+\frac{1}{2}\Gamma_{2}\lVert\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{N}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq 2\frac{\alpha_{R}^{2}}{\beta}\,.

From this estimate we can deduce that the Galerkin approximations are global-in-time. After dropping the hyper-diffusion term from (
2.5
) as well as the contribution from the cubic term, we find by applying Gronwall’s inequality

(2.6)

\lVert\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{N}(\cdot,t)\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\lVert\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}e^{-2|\alpha_{R}|t}+2\frac{|\alpha_{R}|}{\beta}(1-e^{-2|\alpha_{R}|t})\,.

Hence, for any
\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
, there exists a time
T^{*}
(depending on
\lVert\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0}\rVert_{L^{2}}
) such that

(2.7)

\lVert\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{N}(\cdot,t)\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq 4\frac{|\alpha_{R}|}{\beta}=:k_{1}\,,

where the bound is independent of
N
. Now we integrate equation (
2.5
) in time, which leads to (again for
t\geq T^{*}
)

\displaystyle\frac{\beta}{2}\int_{t}^{t+1}\lVert\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{N}\rVert_{L^{4}}^{4}dt^{\prime}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\Gamma_{2}\int_{t}^{t+1}\lVert\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{N}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}dt^{\prime}

(2.8)

\displaystyle\leq{\scriptstyle\frac{1}{2}}\lVert\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{N}(\cdot,t)\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+2\frac{\lvert\alpha_{R}\rvert^{2}}{\beta}\leq{\scriptstyle\frac{1}{2}}k_{1}+2\frac{\lvert\alpha_{R}\rvert^{2}}{\beta}\leq 4\frac{\lvert\alpha_{R}\rvert^{2}}{\beta}\eqqcolon k_{2}\,,

where we have made the assumption (for the sake of simplicity) that
\lvert\alpha_{R}\rvert\geq 1
. Moreover, in the above computation it is understood that the factor of unity in the integral limit
t+1
and on the right hand side of (
2.8
) is a unit of time. In the case
\lvert\alpha_{R}\rvert<1
, we obtain instead

\displaystyle\frac{\beta}{2}\int_{t}^{t+1}\lVert\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{N}\rVert_{L^{4}}^{4}dt^{\prime}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\Gamma_{2}\int_{t}^{t+1}\lVert\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{N}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}dt^{\prime}

(2.9)

\displaystyle\leq{\scriptstyle\frac{1}{2}}\lVert\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{N}(\cdot,t)\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+2\frac{\lvert\alpha_{R}\rvert^{2}}{\beta}\leq 2\frac{\lvert\alpha_{R}\rvert}{\beta}+2\frac{\lvert\alpha_{R}\rvert^{2}}{\beta}\leq 3\frac{\lvert\alpha_{R}\rvert^{2}}{\beta}+\frac{1}{\beta}\,,

where the additional term
\frac{1}{\beta}
can then be included in the attractor dimension estimate, leading to a lower order term (with respect to
\Gamma_{0}
and
\Gamma_{2}
). Subsequently, by integrating equation (
2.5
) in time, we find

(2.10)

\lvert\alpha_{R}\rvert\int_{t}^{t+1}\lVert\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{N}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}dt^{\prime}\leq k_{2}\,.

Now we turn to the gradient estimates. Taking the
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
inner product of equation (
2.1
) with
-\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{N}
leads to

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\lVert\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{N}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\alpha\lVert\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{N}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}-\Gamma_{0}\lVert\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{N}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\Gamma_{2}\lVert\Delta\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{N}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\beta\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\lvert\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{N}\rvert^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{N}\cdot\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{N}dx+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{N}\cdot\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{N})\cdot\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{N}dx\,.

We recall the following identity (see, e.g., Lemma 1 in
[
56
]
) :

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\lvert\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{N}\rvert^{2}\lvert\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{N}\rvert^{2}dx\leq\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\lvert\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{N}\rvert^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{N}\cdot\left(-\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{N}\right)dx\,.

In addition, in the two-dimensional case we have the standard cancellation

(2.11)

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{N}\cdot\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{N})\cdot\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{N}dx=0\,,

because
\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{N}
is divergence-free (cf. Chapter II, Eq. (A.62) in
[
25
]
). In the three-dimensional case we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{N}\cdot\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{N})\cdot\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{N}dx

\displaystyle\leq\lVert\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{N}\rVert_{L^{3}}^{3}\lesssim\lVert\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{N}\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}^{3}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{N}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{3/2}\lVert\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{N}\rVert_{\dot{H}^{3}}^{3/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\Gamma_{2}}{6}\lVert\nabla\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{N}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{60}{\Gamma_{2}^{3}}\lVert\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{N}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{6}\,.

From now on, we will restrict our discussion to the three-dimensional case; the two-dimensional case can be treated in a similar fashion (by using the aforementioned cancellation identity (
2.11
)). Using an interpolation estimate we have

\displaystyle\Gamma_{0}\lVert\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{N}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}

The concept of Lyapunov exponents combined with the Kaplan-Yorke formula originated as a phase space argument for ODEs

The Kaplan-Yorke formula expresses the balance between volume growth and contraction realized on the attractor in phase space and is used to define its Lyapunov dimension. It has been rigorously applied to global attractors in dissipative PDEs by Constantin, Foias and Temam

\sum_{n=1}^{N_{0}-1}\mu_{n}\geq 0\hskip 28.45274pt\mbox{but}\hskip 28.45274pt\sum_{n=1}^{N_{0}}\mu_{n}<0\,.

, they obey the relations

(3.12)

Tr\left[\mathbf{P}_{N}(-\Delta)\mathbf{P}_{N}\right]=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{n}|^{2}\,dV

and we also have

(3.13)

\sum_{n=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{n}|^{2}\,dV=N\,.

Fortunately, the functions
\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{n}
have some useful properties of which we can take advantage to estimate the relative magnitudes of the negative and positive contributions to the Lyapunov exponents. In
d
spatial dimensions the
\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{n}
satisfy what are known as Lieb-Thirring inequalities
[
18
,
44
,
58
]
for all orthonormal functions, which we state in the next lemma.

Lemma 1
.

Let
\{\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{1},\ldots,\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{N}\}
be an orthonormal set in
\{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})\,\lvert\,\nabla\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$u$}=0\}
, then the following inequalities (known as the Lieb-Thirring inequalities) hold

(3.14)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{N}|\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{n}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{d+2}{d}}\,dV

\displaystyle\leq c\,\sum_{n=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{n}|^{2}\,dV+c\,;

(3.15)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{N}|\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{n}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{d+4}{d}}\,dV

\displaystyle\leq c\,\sum_{n=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{n}|^{2}\,dV+c\,,

where,
c
is independent of
N
.

The proof of these inequalities can be found in
[
60
, Appendix, Theorem 4.1, Remarks 4.2 and 4.3]
.

In addition, the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality (both for sums and integrals) ensures that

(3.16)

\left(\sum_{n=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{n}|^{2}\,dV\right)^{2}\leq\left(\sum_{n=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{n}|^{2}\,dV\right)\left(\sum_{n=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{n}|^{2}\,dV\right)\,.

Thus, we have

(3.17)

Tr\left[\mathbf{P}_{N}(\Delta^{2})\mathbf{P}_{N}\right]\geq N^{-1}\left(Tr\left[\mathbf{P}_{N}(-\Delta)\mathbf{P}_{N}\right]\right)^{2}\,.

Moreover, it is well known that for the first
N
eigenvalues
\lambda_{n}
of the Stokes operator (in our case with
L=1
)

(3.18)

\sum_{n=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{n}|^{2}\,dV=Tr\left[\mathbf{P}_{N}(-\Delta)\mathbf{P}_{N}\right]\geq\sum_{j=1}^{N}\lambda_{j}\geq c\sum_{j=1}^{N}j^{2/d}\,.

Hence we have

(3.19)

Tr\left[{\bf P}_{N}(-\Delta){\bf P}_{N}\right]\geq c\,N^{\frac{d+2}{d}}\,;

and it also holds that

(3.20)

Tr\left[\mathbf{P}_{N}(\Delta^{2})\mathbf{P}_{N}\right]\geq c\,N^{1+\frac{4}{d}}\,.

These results can be found in Corollary 4.1 in
[
60
, Appendix]
. Finally, as a preliminary result necessary for proving the estimate on the Lyapunov dimension of the global attractor, we need to show the differentiability of the semigroup. We recall that
\mathcal{M}
is defined as the linearised operator corresponding to a solution
u
, which is the solution map of the following equation (as a map from
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
to itself):

\displaystyle\left(\partial_{t}+\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\cdot\nabla\right)\delta\mbox{\boldmath$u$}+\delta\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\cdot\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}+\nabla\delta p

\displaystyle=-\left\{\alpha+\Gamma_{0}\Delta+\Gamma_{2}\Delta^{2}\right\}\delta\mbox{\boldmath$u$}

(3.21)

\displaystyle-\beta\,\lvert\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\rvert^{2}\delta\mbox{\boldmath$u$}-2\beta\,\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(\delta\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$u$})\,.

Lemma 2
.

The semigroup
S
is uniformly differentiable on the attractor
\mathcal{A}
, i.e. for every
t>0
it holds that

\sup_{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0},\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{0}\in\mathcal{A},\;\lVert\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0}-\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{0}\rVert_{L^{2}}<\epsilon}\frac{\lVert S(t)\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0}-S(t)\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{0}-\mathcal{M}(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0}-\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{0})\rVert_{L^{2}}}{\lVert\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0}-\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{0}\rVert_{L^{2}}}\xrightarrow{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}0\,.

To be more precise, we will show that

(3.22)

\lVert S(t)\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0}-S(t)\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{0}-\mathcal{M}(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0}-\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{0})\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq K\lVert\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0}-\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{0}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2},

where the constant
K
can depend on
t
and
\mathcal{M}
is the linearised operator that was introduced previously in equation (
3.21
).

Proof.

In this proof we will restrict ourselves to the three-dimensional case (for simplicity); and we will not explicitly track the parameters of the TTSH equations, as we will not use the precise constants later on. In the proof of Theorem
1
, we derived that the difference
\mbox{\boldmath$w$}=S(t)\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0}-S(t)\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{0}
satisfies (and where
q=p_{\small\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}-p_{\small\mbox{\boldmath$v$}}
)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$w$}

\displaystyle+\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\cdot\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$w$}+\mbox{\boldmath$w$}\cdot\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$v$}+\nabla q+\alpha\mbox{\boldmath$w$}+\beta(\mbox{\boldmath$w$}\lvert\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\rvert^{2}+\mbox{\boldmath$v$}(\mbox{\boldmath$w$}\cdot(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}+\mbox{\boldmath$v$}))

\displaystyle+\Gamma_{0}\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$w$}+\Gamma_{2}\Delta^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$w$}=0\,;\quad\nabla\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$w$}=0,\quad\mbox{\boldmath$w$}\lvert_{t=0}=\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0}-\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{0}.

First we observe that one can obtain the following identity

(3.23)

\mbox{\boldmath$v$}(\mbox{\boldmath$w$}\cdot(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}+\mbox{\boldmath$v$}))=2\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(\mbox{\boldmath$w$}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$u$})-2\mbox{\boldmath$w$}(\mbox{\boldmath$w$}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$u$})-\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\lvert\mbox{\boldmath$w$}\rvert^{2}\,.

In the proof of Theorem
1
, we showed estimate (
2.17
) for
w
, from which it follows that there exists a constant
K>0
such that

(3.24)

\lVert\mbox{\boldmath$w$}(\cdot,t)\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq Ke^{Kt}\lVert\mbox{\boldmath$w$}_{0}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2},\quad\int_{0}^{t}\lVert\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$w$}(\cdot,t^{\prime})\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}dt^{\prime}\leq Ke^{Kt}\lVert\mbox{\boldmath$w$}_{0}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Now we define
\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{L}
as the solution of the equation

\displaystyle\big(\partial_{t}

\displaystyle+\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\cdot\nabla\big)\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{L}+\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{L}\cdot\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}+\nabla p_{L}=-\left\{\alpha+\Gamma_{0}\Delta+\Gamma_{2}\Delta^{2}\right\}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{L}

\displaystyle-\beta\,\lvert\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\rvert^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{L}-2\beta\,\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{L}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$u$}),\quad\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{L}(0)=\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0}-\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{0}.

We also define the function

(3.25)

\mbox{\boldmath$z$}=\mbox{\boldmath$w$}-\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{L}.

One can check that
z
satisfies the following equation (by using identity (
3.23
))

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$z$}

\displaystyle+\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\cdot\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$z$}+\mbox{\boldmath$z$}\cdot\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}-\mbox{\boldmath$w$}\cdot\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$w$}+\nabla(q-p_{L})+\alpha\mbox{\boldmath$z$}+\Gamma_{0}\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$z$}+\Gamma_{2}\Delta^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$z$}

\displaystyle+\beta\lvert\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\rvert^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$z$}+2\beta\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(\mbox{\boldmath$z$}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$u$})-2\beta\mbox{\boldmath$w$}(\mbox{\boldmath$w$}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$u$})-\beta\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\lvert\mbox{\boldmath$w$}\rvert^{2}=0.

Now we take the
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
inner product with
z
, which leads to (by applying the Lions-Magenes lemma)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\lVert\mbox{\boldmath$z$}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\alpha\lVert\mbox{\boldmath$z$}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}-\Gamma_{0}\lVert\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$z$}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\Gamma_{2}\lVert\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$z$}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\beta\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\bigg[\lvert\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\rvert^{2}\lvert\mbox{\boldmath$z$}\rvert^{2}+2\lvert\mbox{\boldmath$z$}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\rvert^{2}\bigg]dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\bigg[-\mbox{\boldmath$z$}\cdot\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}+\mbox{\boldmath$w$}\cdot\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$w$}+2\beta\mbox{\boldmath$w$}(\mbox{\boldmath$w$}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$u$})+\beta\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\lvert\mbox{\boldmath$w$}\rvert^{2}\bigg]\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$z$}dx

\displaystyle\leq\lVert\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\rVert_{L^{2}}\lVert\mbox{\boldmath$z$}\rVert_{L^{4}}^{2}+\lVert\mbox{\boldmath$w$}\rVert_{L^{2}}\lVert\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$w$}\rVert_{L^{2}}\lVert\mbox{\boldmath$z$}\rVert_{L^{\infty}}+2\beta\lVert\mbox{\boldmath$w$}\rVert_{L^{4}}^{2}(\lVert\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\rVert_{L^{2}}+\lVert\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\rVert_{L^{2}})\lVert\mbox{\boldmath$z$}\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\,.

Using Young’s inequality repeatedly, together with estimates (
2.7
), (
2.13
) and (
3.24
), we find (again for some constant
K>0
)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\lVert\mbox{\boldmath$z$}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left(\alpha-\frac{\Gamma_{0}^{2}}{\Gamma_{2}}-K\right)\lVert\mbox{\boldmath$z$}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{\Gamma_{2}}{4}\lVert\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$z$}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq K\lVert\mbox{\boldmath$w$}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\lVert\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$w$}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+K\lVert\mbox{\boldmath$w$}\rVert_{L^{4}}^{4}.

Finally, using the Gronwall inequality and
\mbox{\boldmath$z$}(0)=0
, we obtain

(3.26)

\lVert\mbox{\boldmath$z$}(\cdot,t)\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq Ke^{Kt}\lVert\mbox{\boldmath$w$}_{0}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{4}\,,

which implies the differentiability of the semigroup.
∎

3.2.
Attractor dimension estimate in the two-dimensional case

A sharp estimate (up to a logarithm) for the dimension of the attractor for the
2d
NSEs was found by Constantin, Foias and Temam
[
14
]
, aided by Constantin’s logarithmic
L^{\infty}
-inequality for functions whose gradients are orthonormal
[
19
]
. The same dimension estimate in
[
14
]
was later recovered by Doering and Gibbon
[
22
]
, who used the vorticity formulation and not the velocity formulation of the Navier-Stokes equations. In the following theorem we use the velocity formulation of the
2d
TTSH equations as the basis of our calculation, but as was said before by Theorem
4
it makes no difference compared to the vorticity formulation.

Theorem 6
.

The Lyapunov dimension of the global attractor
\mathcal{A}
of the 2
d
TTSH equations on the unit periodic domain
\mathbb{T}^{2}
is bounded by (for some constant
c
)

d_{L}(\mathcal{A})\leq c\max\left\{\left\lvert\frac{\Gamma_{0}^{2}}{\Gamma_{2}^{2}}-\alpha\Gamma_{2}^{-1}\right\rvert^{1/2}\,;~~\left(\frac{|\alpha_{R}|^{2}}{\beta}\right)^{3/16}\Gamma_{2}^{-5/8}\beta^{-1/16}\right\}\,,

where we recall that
\alpha_{R}=\alpha-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\Gamma_{0}^{2}}{\Gamma_{2}}
.

Remark 7
.

By keeping
\alpha
and
\beta
fixed (more specifically if
\beta^{-1}\ll\Gamma_{0}
), and taking
\Gamma_{2}\ll 1
and
\Gamma_{0}\gg 1
(i.e., the parameter scaling in active turbulence), to leading order we therefore deduce that the dominant term in the attractor dimension estimate is

(3.27)

d_{L}(\mathcal{A})\sim\left(\frac{\Gamma_{0}^{2}}{\Gamma_{2}^{2}}-\alpha\Gamma_{2}^{-1}\right)^{1/2}\,,

from which we recover the inverse Swift-Hohenberg scale
\eta_{res}^{-1}=(\Gamma_{0}/\Gamma_{2})^{1/2}
by means of relation (
1.6
). Note that the estimates in the proof of Theorem
6
also lead to bounds on the Hausdorff and fractal dimensions of the global attractor
\mathcal{A}
. In addition, the result can also be generalised to include the case of the TTSH equations with a forcing. The numerical methods used in §
4
require an adjustment of the domain length
L
which for the above has been taken as unity. A re-working of this calculation on the domain
[0,\,L]^{2}
turns this estimate into
\eta_{res}^{-1}\sim L(\Gamma_{0}/\Gamma_{2})^{1/2}
.

Proof.

The linearisation of the 2
D
TTSH equations around a given solution
u
is given by

\displaystyle\left(\partial_{t}+\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\cdot\nabla\right)\delta\mbox{\boldmath$u$}+\delta\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\cdot\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}+\nabla\delta p

\displaystyle=-\left\{\alpha+\Gamma_{0}\Delta+\Gamma_{2}\Delta^{2}\right\}\delta\mbox{\boldmath$u$}

(3.28)

\displaystyle-\beta\,\lvert\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\rvert^{2}\delta\mbox{\boldmath$u$}-2\beta\,\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(\delta\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$u$}).

Now let
\{\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{1},\ldots,\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{N}\}
be a subset of an orthonormal basis of the space
\{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})\,\lvert\,\nabla\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$u$}=0\}
. Then we compute the volume elements as follows:

\displaystyle Tr\left[\mathbf{P}_{N}\mathcal{M}\mathbf{P}_{N}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{n}\cdot\left\{-\alpha\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{n}-\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\cdot\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{n}-\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{n}\cdot\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}-\nabla\tilde{p}\left(\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{n}\right)\right\}\,dV

\displaystyle-\beta\sum_{n=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{n}\cdot\left\{\lvert\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\rvert^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{n}+2\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{n}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$u$})\right\}\,dV\,

(3.29)

\displaystyle-\sum_{n=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{n}\cdot\left\{\Gamma_{0}\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{n}+\Gamma_{2}\Delta^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{n}\right\}\,dV\,.

We can bound the trace of the volume element as follows:

\displaystyle Tr\left[\mathbf{P}_{N}\mathcal{M}\mathbf{P}_{N}\right]

\displaystyle\leq-\Gamma_{2}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}|\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{n}|^{2}\!dV+\Gamma_{0}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{n}|^{2}\,dV

(3.30)

\displaystyle+\sum_{n=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}|\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{n}|^{2}\left(-\alpha+|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}|\right)\!dV\,.

Next we estimate the terms coming from the advection term:

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}|\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{n}|^{2}\lvert\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\rvert dV\leq\lVert\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\sum_{n=1}^{N}|\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{n}|^{2}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\rVert_{L^{2}}\bigg[\left(\sum_{n=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{n}|^{2}\,dV\right)^{1/2}+c\bigg]

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\rVert_{L^{2}}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}|\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{n}|^{2}\,dV\right)^{1/4}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}|\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{n}|^{2}\,dV\right)^{1/4}+\lVert\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\rVert_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{4}\Gamma_{2}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}|\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{n}|^{2}\,dV+\frac{3}{4}\Gamma_{2}^{-1/3}\lVert\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{4/3}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}|\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{n}|^{2}\,dV\right)^{1/3}+\lVert\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\rVert_{L^{2}}\,,

where we have used the Lieb-Thirring inequality (
3.14
), as well as the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality for sums. Using Young’s inequality we now have

\displaystyle\Gamma_{0}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{n}|^{2}\,dV

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}\Gamma_{2}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}|\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{n}|^{2}\,dV+\frac{\Gamma_{0}^{2}}{\Gamma_{2}}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}|\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{n}|^{2}\,dV

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\Gamma_{2}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}|\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{n}|^{2}\,dV+\frac{\Gamma_{0}^{2}}{\Gamma_{2}}N,

where we have used property (
3.13
). Inserting this bound into estimate (
3.30
) for the volume elements gives

\displaystyle Tr\left[\mathbf{P}_{N}\mathcal{M}\mathbf{P}_{N}\right]

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{1}{4}\Gamma_{2}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}|\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{n}|^{2}dV+\Gamma_{2}^{-1/3}\lVert\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{4/3}N^{1/3}

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{\Gamma_{0}^{2}}{\Gamma_{2}}-\alpha\right)N+\lVert\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\rVert_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{1}{4}\Gamma_{2}N^{3}+\Gamma_{2}^{-1/3}\lVert\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{4/3}N^{1/3}+\left(\frac{\Gamma_{0}^{2}}{\Gamma_{2}}-\alpha\right)N+\lVert\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\rVert_{L^{2}}\,.

Then, by taking time averages of this inequality and using inequality (
3.20
), we obtain

\displaystyle\left<Tr\left[\mathbf{P}_{N}\mathcal{M}\mathbf{P}_{N}\right]\right>_{T}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{1}{2}\Gamma_{2}N^{3}+\Gamma_{2}^{-1/3}\left\langle\lVert\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{4/3}\right\rangle_{T}N^{1/3}+\left(\frac{\Gamma_{0}^{2}}{\Gamma_{2}}-\alpha\right)N

\displaystyle+\left\langle\lVert\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\rVert_{L^{2}}\right\rangle_{T}.

By using an interpolation inequality we have

(3.31)

\displaystyle\left\langle\lVert\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{4/3}\right\rangle_{T}

\displaystyle\leq\left\langle\lVert\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\rVert_{L^{4}}^{2/3}\lVert\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2/3}\right\rangle_{T}\leq\left\langle\lVert\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\rVert_{L^{4}}^{4}\right\rangle_{T}^{1/6}\left\langle\lVert\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\right\rangle_{T}^{1/3},

(3.32)

\displaystyle\left\langle\lVert\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\rVert_{L^{2}}\right\rangle_{T}

\displaystyle\leq\left\langle\lVert\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\rVert_{L^{4}}^{1/2}\lVert\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{1/2}\right\rangle_{T}\leq\left\langle\lVert\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\rVert_{L^{4}}^{4}\right\rangle_{T}^{1/8}\left\langle\lVert\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\right\rangle_{T}^{1/4}\,.

From estimate (
2.8
) in the proof of Theorem
1
we recall that

\beta\left\langle\lVert\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\rVert_{L^{4}}^{4}\right\rangle_{T}+\Gamma_{2}\left\langle\lVert\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\right\rangle_{T}\leq 8\frac{|\alpha_{R}|^{2}}{\beta}\,.

This then yields

\displaystyle\left\langle\lVert\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{4/3}\right\rangle_{T}

\displaystyle\leq 8\left(\frac{|\alpha_{R}|^{2}}{\beta}\right)^{1/2}\Gamma_{2}^{-1/3}\beta^{-1/6}\,;

\displaystyle\left\langle\lVert\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\rVert_{L^{2}}\right\rangle_{T}

\displaystyle\leq 8\left(\frac{|\alpha_{R}|^{2}}{\beta}\right)^{3/8}\Gamma_{2}^{-1/4}\beta^{-1/8}

and so

\displaystyle\left<Tr\left[\mathbf{P}_{N}\mathcal{M}\mathbf{P}_{N}\right]\right>_{T}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{1}{2}\Gamma_{2}N^{3}+N^{1/3}\Gamma_{2}^{-1/3}\left(\frac{|\alpha_{R}|^{2}}{\beta}\right)^{1/2}\Gamma_{2}^{-1/3}\beta^{-1/6}

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{\Gamma_{0}^{2}}{\Gamma_{2}}-\alpha\right)N+\left(\frac{|\alpha_{R}|^{2}}{\beta}\right)^{3/8}\Gamma_{2}^{-1/4}\beta^{-1/8}\,.

By applying Proposition 2.1 and Theorem 3.3 in
[
60
, Chapter 5]
, we obtain the following bounds on the dimension:

(3.33)

\displaystyle N

\displaystyle\leq c\left(\frac{\Gamma_{0}^{2}}{\Gamma_{2}^{2}}-\alpha\Gamma_{2}^{-1}\right)^{1/2}\,;

(3.34)

\displaystyle N

\displaystyle\leq c\left(\frac{|\alpha_{R}|^{2}}{\beta}\right)^{3/16}\Gamma_{2}^{-5/8}\beta^{-1/16}\,;

(3.35)

\displaystyle N

\displaystyle\leq c\left(\frac{|\alpha_{R}|^{2}}{\beta}\right)^{1/8}\Gamma_{2}^{-5/12}\beta^{-1/24}\,.

Notice that the second inequality implies the validity of the third inequality (in the standard parameter range).
∎

3.3.
Attractor dimension estimate in the three-dimensional case

Theorem 8
.

An estimate of the Lyapunov dimension of the attractor
\mathcal{A}
of the 3
d
TTSH equations on the unit domain
\mathbb{T}^{3}
is given by

d_{L}(\mathcal{A})\leq c\max\left\{\left\lvert\frac{\Gamma_{0}^{2}}{\Gamma_{2}^{2}}-\alpha\Gamma_{2}^{-1}\right\rvert^{3/4}\,;~~2^{9/4}\Gamma_{2}^{-1}\left(\frac{|\alpha_{R}|}{\beta}\right)^{1/2}\right\}\,,

where we recall that
\alpha_{R}=\alpha-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\Gamma_{0}^{2}}{\Gamma_{2}}
.

Remark 9
.

As observed in Theorem
6
and also Remark
7
, by keeping
\alpha
and
\beta
fixed, and taking
\Gamma_{2}\ll 1
and
\Gamma_{0}\gg 1
(i.e., the common parameter scaling in active turbulence), to leading order we therefore find the dominant term in the attractor dimension estimate to be (if
\beta^{-1}\ll\Gamma_{0}
)

(3.36)

d_{L}(\mathcal{A})\sim\left(\frac{\Gamma_{0}^{2}}{\Gamma_{2}^{2}}-\alpha\Gamma_{2}^{-1}\right)^{3/4}\,,

from which we recover the inverse Swift-Hohenberg scale
\eta_{res}^{-1}=(\Gamma_{0}/\Gamma_{2})^{1/2}
as in the heuristic relation (
1.6
). A re-working of this calculation on the domain
[0,\,L]^{3}
turns this estimate into
\eta_{res}^{-1}\sim L(\Gamma_{0}/\Gamma_{2})^{1/2}
.

Proof of Theorem
8
.

The linearised three-dimensional TTSH equations, around a solution
u
, are given by

(3.37)

\displaystyle\left(\partial_{t}+\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\cdot\nabla\right)\delta\mbox{\boldmath$u$}+\delta\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\cdot\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}+\nabla\delta p

\displaystyle=-\left\{\alpha+\Gamma_{0}\Delta+\Gamma_{2}\Delta^{2}\right\}\delta\mbox{\boldmath$u$}

\displaystyle-\beta\,\lvert\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\rvert^{2}\delta\mbox{\boldmath$u$}-2\beta\,\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(\delta\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$u$}).

For any orthonormal subset of the basis for the space
W

\left\{\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{1},\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{2},\ldots,\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{N}\right\}
, the trace formula we need to estimate explicitly comes from (
3.37
) and is

\displaystyle Tr\left[\mathbf{P}_{N}\mathcal{M}\mathbf{P}_{N}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{n}\cdot\left\{-\alpha\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{n}-\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\cdot\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{n}-\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{n}\cdot\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}-\nabla\tilde{p}\left(\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{n}\right)\right\}\,dV

\displaystyle-\beta\sum_{n=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{n}\cdot\left\{\lvert\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\rvert^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{n}+2\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{n}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$u$})\right\}\,dV\,

\displaystyle-\sum_{n=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{n}\cdot\left\{\Gamma_{0}\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{n}+\Gamma_{2}\Delta^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{n}\right\}\,dV\,.

Since the elements
\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{n}
are divergence-free, the contribution from the pressure and the
\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{n}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\cdot\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{n}
-term vanishes. In the remaining term, coming from the advection, we integrate by parts, and we apply the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality (for sums) to obtain

\displaystyle\left\lvert\sum_{n=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{n}\cdot\left\{\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{n}\cdot\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\right\}\,dV\right\rvert\leq\sum_{n=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\lvert\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{n}\rvert\lvert\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{n}\rvert\lvert\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\rvert\,dV

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\lvert\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\rvert\left(\sum_{n=1}^{N}\lvert\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{n}\rvert^{2}\right)^{1/2}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{N}\lvert\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{n}\rvert^{2}\right)^{1/2}\,dV.

What remains is

\displaystyle Tr\left[\mathbf{P}_{N}\mathcal{M}\mathbf{P}_{N}\right]

\displaystyle\leq-\Gamma_{2}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{n}|^{2}\!dV+\Gamma_{0}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{n}|^{2}\,dV

\displaystyle-\alpha\sum_{n=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{n}|^{2}\!dV+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\lvert\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\rvert\left(\sum_{n=1}^{N}\lvert\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{n}\rvert^{2}\right)^{1/2}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{N}\lvert\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{n}\rvert^{2}\right)^{1/2}\,dV.

In the advection term we use the inequality
\|X\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\|X\|_{L^{1}}^{\frac{q-2}{q-1}}\|X\|_{L^{q}}^{\frac{q}{q-1}}
(for
q>2
), with
q=7/3
, and the Lieb-Thirring inequality (
3.15
), to find

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\lvert\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\rvert\left(\sum_{n=1}^{N}\lvert\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{n}\rvert^{2}\right)^{1/2}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{N}\lvert\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{n}\rvert^{2}\right)^{1/2}\,dV

\displaystyle\leq\lVert\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\rVert_{L^{4}}\left\lVert\left(\sum_{n=1}^{N}\lvert\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{n}\rvert^{2}\right)^{1/2}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\left(\sum_{n=1}^{N}\lvert\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{n}\rvert^{2}\right)^{1/2}\right\rVert_{L^{4}}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\rVert_{L^{4}}\left\lVert\sum_{n=1}^{N}\lvert\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{n}\rvert^{2}\right\rVert_{L^{1}}^{1/2}\left\lVert\sum_{n=1}^{N}\lvert\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{n}\rvert^{2}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\rVert_{L^{4}}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{n}|^{2}\,dV\right)^{1/4}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{n}|^{2}\,dV\right)^{1/4}

\displaystyle\times\left\lVert\sum_{n=1}^{N}\lvert\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{n}\rvert^{2}\right\rVert_{L^{1}}^{1/16}\left\lVert\sum_{n=1}^{N}\lvert\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{n}\rvert^{2}\right\rVert_{L^{7/3}}^{7/16}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\rVert_{L^{4}}N^{5/16}\bigg[\left(\sum_{n=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{n}|^{2}\,dV\right)^{7/16}+c\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\Gamma_{2}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{n}|^{2}\,dV+4\Gamma_{2}^{-7/9}N^{5/9}\lVert\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\rVert_{L^{4}}^{16/9}+c\lVert\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\rVert_{L^{4}}N^{5/16}\,,

where we have used the Lieb-Thirring inequality (
3.15
) and estimate (
3.16
). Now we obtain by using property (
3.13
)

\displaystyle Tr\left[\mathbf{P}_{N}\mathcal{M}\mathbf{P}_{N}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\Gamma_{2}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{n}|^{2}dV+\Gamma_{0}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{n}|^{2}\,dV

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\alpha\sum_{n=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{n}|^{2}dV+4\Gamma_{2}^{-7/9}N^{5/9}\lVert\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\rVert_{L^{4}}^{16/9}+c\lVert\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\rVert_{L^{4}}N^{5/16}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\Gamma_{2}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{n}|^{2}dV+\Gamma_{0}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{n}|^{2}dV

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle 4\Gamma_{2}^{-7/9}N^{5/9}\lVert\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\rVert_{L^{4}}^{16/9}-\alpha N+c\lVert\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\rVert_{L^{4}}N^{5/16}\,.

Applying Young’s inequality yields

\displaystyle\Gamma_{0}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{n}|^{2}\,dV

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}\Gamma_{2}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{n}|^{2}\,dV+\frac{\Gamma_{0}^{2}}{\Gamma_{2}}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{n}|^{2}\,dV

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\Gamma_{2}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{n}|^{2}\,dV+\frac{\Gamma_{0}^{2}}{\Gamma_{2}}N\,.

Then we have

\displaystyle Tr\left[\mathbf{P}_{N}\mathcal{M}\mathbf{P}_{N}\right]

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{1}{4}\Gamma_{2}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{n}|^{2}dV

\displaystyle+4\Gamma_{2}^{-7/9}N^{5/9}\lVert\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\rVert_{L^{4}}^{16/9}+N\left(-\alpha+\frac{\Gamma_{0}^{2}}{\Gamma_{2}}\right)+c\lVert\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\rVert_{L^{4}}N^{5/16}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{1}{4}\Gamma_{2}N^{7/3}+N\left(-\alpha+\frac{\Gamma_{0}^{2}}{\Gamma_{2}}\right)+4\Gamma_{2}^{-7/9}N^{5/9}\lVert\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\rVert_{L^{4}}^{16/9}

\displaystyle+c\lVert\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\rVert_{L^{4}}N^{5/16}\,,

where we have used inequality (
3.20
). From estimate (
2.8
) we deduce that

(3.38)

\displaystyle\beta\left\langle\lVert\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\rVert_{L^{4}}^{4}\right\rangle_{T}\leq 8\frac{|\alpha_{R}|^{2}}{\beta}\,,

which then implies

\displaystyle\left\langle\lVert\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\rVert_{L^{4}}^{16/9}\right\rangle_{T}

\displaystyle\leq\left\langle\lVert\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\rVert_{L^{4}}^{4}\right\rangle_{T}^{4/9}\leq\left(4\frac{|\alpha_{R}|}{\beta}\right)^{8/9}\,,

\displaystyle\left\langle\lVert\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\rVert_{L^{4}}\right\rangle_{T}

\displaystyle\leq\left\langle\lVert\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\rVert_{L^{4}}^{4}\right\rangle_{T}^{1/4}\leq\left(4\frac{|\alpha_{R}|}{\beta}\right)^{1/2}\,.

By taking time averages we find

\displaystyle\left<Tr\left[\mathbf{P}_{N}\mathcal{M}\mathbf{P}_{N}\right]\right>_{T}\leq-\frac{1}{4}\Gamma_{2}N^{7/3}+N\left(-\alpha+\frac{\Gamma_{0}^{2}}{\Gamma_{2}}\right)+4N^{5/9}\Gamma_{2}^{-7/9}\left\langle\lVert\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\rVert_{L^{4}}^{16/9}\right\rangle_{T}

\displaystyle+c\left\langle\lVert\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\rVert_{L^{4}}\right\rangle_{T}N^{5/16}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{1}{4}\Gamma_{2}N^{7/3}+N\left(-\alpha+\frac{\Gamma_{0}^{2}}{\Gamma_{2}}\right)+4N^{5/9}\Gamma_{2}^{-7/9}\left(4\frac{|\alpha_{R}|}{\beta}\right)^{8/9}

\displaystyle+c\left(4\frac{|\alpha_{R}|}{\beta}\right)^{1/2}N^{5/16}\,.

We can estimate the value
N_{0}
for which

\left<Tr\left[\mathbf{P}_{N}\mathcal{M}\mathbf{P}_{N}\right]\right>_{T}<0
when
N>N_{0}
. This leads to the following inequalities:

\displaystyle N_{0}^{4/3}

\displaystyle\leq c\Gamma_{2}^{-1}\left(-\alpha+\frac{\Gamma_{0}^{2}}{\Gamma_{2}}\right)=\left(\frac{\Gamma_{0}^{2}}{\Gamma_{2}^{2}}-\alpha\Gamma_{2}^{-1}\right)\,;

\displaystyle N_{0}^{16/9}

\displaystyle\leq c16\Gamma_{2}^{-16/9}\left(\frac{|\alpha_{R}|}{\beta}\right)^{8/9}\,,

\displaystyle N_{0}^{97/48}

\displaystyle\leq c\Gamma_{2}^{-1}\left(4\frac{|\alpha_{R}|}{\beta}\right)^{1/2}\,.

We can observe that the third inequality is implied by the second one in the standard value range of the control parameters. Thus, we have

(3.39)

N_{0}\sim\max\left\{\left\lvert-\alpha\Gamma_{2}^{-1}+\frac{\Gamma_{0}^{2}}{\Gamma_{2}^{2}}\right\rvert^{3/4}\,,~~2^{9/4}\Gamma_{2}^{-1}\left(\frac{|\alpha_{R}|}{\beta}\right)^{1/2}\right\}\,.

The result then follows from Proposition 2.1 and Theorem 3.3 in
[
60
, Chapter 5]
.

4.
Numerical evaluation of Lyapunov exponents and
d_{L}

We now present numerical estimates for the Lyapunov spectrum,
\mu_{n}
and the Lyapunov dimension in Eq. (
3.1
) for the
d=2
case. In Theorem
6
, based on a unit domain
[0,\,1]^{2}
, we estimated the number of exponents
N_{0}
to be
N_{0}\sim\Gamma_{0}/\Gamma_{2}
(at leading order). As already noted in Remark
7
, the following numerical calculation requires an adjustment of the domain length
L
. A re-working of this calculation on the domain
[0,\,L]^{2}
turns this estimate into
N_{0}\sim L^{2}\Gamma_{0}/\Gamma_{2}
.

The number of Lyapunov exponents is equal to the total number of degrees of freedom available numerically. Computing the Lyapunov spectrum for the full set of hydrodynamic equations, and consequently
d_{L}
, is computationally expensive
[
38
,
32
]
. However, as we describe below, it is not necessary to extract the full Lyapunov spectrum to compute
d_{L}
; we need only
N_{0}
exponents, which is smaller than the total number of degrees of freedom. Theorem
4
shows that
\mathcal{A}_{L^{2}}=\mathcal{A}_{H^{1}}
, so it makes no difference whether we work with velocity or vorticity fields. In fact, in §
3.2
our estimate for
d_{L}
has been made using the velocity field; but to reduce numerical costs, we simulate the attractor dimension using the equations corresponding to the vorticity field
\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}=\mbox{curl}\,\mbox{\boldmath$u$}
.

(4.1)

{}\left(\partial_{t}+\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\cdot\nabla\right)\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}=-\left(\alpha+\Gamma_{0}\Delta+\Gamma_{2}\Delta^{2}\right)\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}-\beta\mbox{curl}\,(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}|^{2})\,.

Figure 1.
The Lyapunov spectrum for the run 7, where the black dots are for the unordered spectrum and the red curve gives the ordered spectrum, with shaded region giving the error bars. The inset gives the ordered Lyapunov spectrum for the run 2.

We begin by choosing a base vorticity field,
\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}_{0}
, to which we add a set of
i=1,..M
initial perturbations
\{\delta\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}_{i}\}
to get
M
initial conditions (which we evolve indepedently) :

(4.2)

\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}_{i}=\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}_{0}+\epsilon\delta\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}_{i},

where we use
\epsilon=10^{-6}
. The initial perturbations are chosen to be orthonormal, i.e.,

(4.3)

\left(\delta\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}_{i},\delta\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}_{j}\right)_{L^{2}}=\delta_{ij}\,,

where
\delta_{ij}
is the Kronecker delta and
(.)_{L^{2}}
denotes the standard
L^{2}
inner product, e.g., for any real functions
f
and
g
defined on
V_{d}
:

(4.4)

(f,g)_{L^{2}}=\int_{V_{d}}fg~d^{d}\mathbf{x}.

Figure 2.
Log-log plot of
d_{L}
versus
L^{2}(\Gamma_{0}/\Gamma_{2})
for different values of
\alpha
. The solid black line corresponds to
10^{-1}\times L^{2}(\Gamma_{0}/\Gamma_{2})
.

We then define a norm using the above inner-product, namely,
||f||

\coloneqq

\sqrt{(f,f)_{L^{2}}}
. Choosing the base vorticity field as a steady-state solution of Eq. (
4.1
), the set of
M
initial conditions
\{\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}_{i}\}
and
\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}_{0}
are then evolved simultaneously using the full non-linear equations Eq. (
4.1
). We estimate the Lyapunov exponents by calculating the finite-time Lyapunov exponents (FTLE)
\{\gamma_{i}\}
:

(4.5)

\gamma_{i}(t,t_{0})=\frac{1}{t-t_{0}}\log\frac{||\delta\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}_{i}(t)||}{||\delta\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}_{i}(t_{0})||},

The Lyapunov exponents are defined as the asymptotic limits to FTLEs
[
33
]
:

(4.6)

\mu_{i}=\lim_{t\to\infty}\gamma_{i}(t,t_{0}).

With the assumptions of ergodicity
[
49
,
33
,
32
,
31
]
, the Lyapunov exponents are simply the ensemble averages of the FTLEs:

(4.7)

\mu_{i}=\langle\gamma_{i}\rangle=\frac{1}{t-t_{0}}\bigg\langle\log\frac{||\delta\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}_{i}(t)||}{||\delta\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}_{i}(t_{0})||}\bigg\rangle,

where
\langle\cdot\rangle
denotes the ensemble average (see below).

When estimating
\{\gamma_{i}\}
and the corresponding Lyapunov exponents numerically, there are two important challenges. Firstly, the equations do not preserve orthonormality of the perturbations and all the perturbations collapse onto the fastest-growing direction giving just the largest Lyapunov exponent. Secondly, since we are using the full nonlinear equations, the growth
\delta\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}_{i}
is exponential only for small times, which corresponds to small
||\delta\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}_{i}||
. We resolve both these issues by periodically re-orthogonalizing the perturbations and rescaling their norms by
\epsilon
. At the time of re-orthogonalization we measure
\{\gamma_{i}\}
using Eq. (
4.5
). We repeat this process multiple times resulting in statistically independent sets for
\{\gamma_{i}\}
that constitute the ensemble
[
29
,
30
,
38
,
32
,
31
,
5
,
13
]
. We use a pseudo spectral method to solve Eq. (
4.1
). For the time integration in Fourier-space we use a second-order integrating factor Runge-Kutta method
[
40
]
. Finally, we use a modified Gram-Schmidt algorithm to orthogonalize the perturbations in the real space and find that it is sufficient to orthogonalize every 50 iterations.

To estimate
d_{L}
, we need enough positive and negative Lyapunov exponents such that Eq. (
3.2
) is satisfied. In our simulations, we have
M
in the range between
50
to
1000
. After large ensemble averages, the Lyapunov exponents are ordered as follows:
\mu_{1}>\mu_{2}>\ldots>\mu_{M}
; this allows us to check if the resulting spectrum has converged and to obtain enough Lyapunov exponents to compute
d_{L}

[
5
]
. In Fig.
1
we plot the Lyapunov spectra for the two representative runs 7 and 2 (the latter in the inset). The black dots indicate the unordered Lyapunov exponents and the red curve corresponds to the same exponents, but ordered. We find reasonable agreement between the spectra for ordered and unordered exponents; this indicates good convergence
[
5
]
.

A few comments are in order: we denote by
\sigma_{i}
the standard deviation of the
i^{th}
exponent
\mu_{i}
; for small
i
,
\mu_{i}/\sigma_{i}\sim\mathcal{O}(1)
, i.e,, the largest Lyapunov exponents have large errors. In contrast, for very large

,

]
.

5.
Conclusions

The TTSH equations in two and three space dimensions are used to model dense bacterial suspensions
[
2
,
1
,
51
]
, and other active turbulent systems. We have studied these equations from the point of view of rigorous analysis. In particular, in the case of periodic boundary conditions, we have proved that they possess a global attractor whose Lyapunov dimension is bounded. By interpreting this as the number of degrees of freedom in the system, we have shown that the estimates are dominated by the linear terms, which means that we have established the predominance of the Swift-Hohenberg scale as the natural resolution scale.

We have also carried out pseudospectral direct numerical simulations of these PDEs in two dimensions, obtained Lyapunov spectra and the Kaplan-Yorke dimensions for representative parameter values, and we have shown that our numerical results are consistent with the rigorous bounds that have been derived analytically.

Entropy-minimizing diffeomorphisms of pseudo-Anosov type on K3 surfaces
Benson Farb, Eduard Looijenga

Abstract
We construct diffeomorphisms of “pseudo-Anosov type” on K3 surfaces M M . In particular we obtain infinitely many examples of such f f that minimize entropy in their homotopy class, and for which neither f f nor any diffeomorphism homotopic to f f preserves any complex structure on M M .

Introduction

A closed, simply-connected complex surface is a
K3 surface
if
it admits a nowhere-vanishing holomorphic
2
-form. Kodaira proved that all K3 surfaces are diffeomorphic; we call these smooth manifolds

. Attached to every diffeomorphism

Theorem 1.1

Let

Remarks 1.2
.

1.

Property 2 of Theorem

was known for the very special case when

is a biholomorphic automorphism. This follows from the fact that complex K3 surfaces are Kahler (Siu
[

[

that any biholomorphic automorphism of any closed, Kahler manifold minimizes entropy in its homotopy class.

2.

There are infinitely many examples (even up to conjugation and taking powers) of

to be the companion matrix of

P(x)=(x^{2}+ax+1)(x^{2}+bx+1)\ \ \text{for any $a,b\geq 2,a\neq b$}.

There are also infinitely many irreducible examples:

P(x)=x^{4}-(n^{2}+n)x^{2}+1\ \ \text{for any $n\geq 2$}.

Thus Theorem

produces infinitely many entropy-minimizing diffeomorphisms

of the K3 manifold

[

3.

The construction of

The construction of

in Theorem

One starts with a complex torus

the association

induces an
injective homomorphism

\rho:\SL(4,\mathbb{Z})\to\Mod(M)

where
\Mod(M)=\pi_{0}(\Diff(M))
is the mapping class group of

equipped with its intersection form. Then

is the unique even, unimodular lattice of signature

is an arithmetic subgroup of the real semisimple Lie group

Homological entropy.

Theorem

relates closely to homological entropy.
For a linear transformation
A
of a finite-dimensional real vector space, let

of

Proof of Property 2 in Theorem
1.1
.

Call the eigenvalues in the hypothesis
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}

Item 1 of Theorem

\htop(f_{T})=\log|\lambda_{1}|+\log|\lambda_{2}|

In Corollary

for any

\begin{array}[]{ll}\htop(g)&\geq\log\specrad[g_{*}:H_{*}(M;\mathbb{R})\to H_{*}(M;\mathbb{R})]\\
&=\log\specrad[(f_{T})_{*}:H_{*}(M;\mathbb{R})\to H_{*}(M;\mathbb{R})]\\
&=\log\specrad[(f_{T})_{*}:H_{2}(M;\mathbb{R})\to H_{2}(M;\mathbb{R})]\\
&=\log|\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}|=\log|\lambda_{1}|+\log|\lambda_{2}|.\end{array}

(1.2)

Is the homological lower bound sharp?

There are infinitely many
T\in\SL(4,\mathbb{Z})
(even up to conjugacy and taking powers) having
3

\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3}

\htop(f_{T})

\log|\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\lambda_{3}|

is the
4
-torus in the Kummer construction (see §

). On the other hand,
H_{3}(M)=0
, and the only nontrivial homological data about

is its action on
H_{2}(M)

pairs
of eigenvalues.

As far as we can tell, the only known lower bounds for
\htop
of an isotopy class of diffeomorphism on an

come from
its induced action on homology as above (i.e. Yomdin’s Theorem). In his 2006 ICM talk, Shub (
[

]

in the given isotopy class converging to this lower bound.
We make the following conjecture, which would give a negative answer to Shub’s questions.

Conjecture 1.3

(
Homologically undetectable entropy).

Let
T\in\SL(4,\mathbb{Z})
have exactly
3

\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3}
lying outside the unit circle, ordered so that
|\lambda_{1}|\geq|\lambda_{2}|\geq|\lambda_{3}|>1
. Then the minimal entropy of any

isotopic to

satisfies

\htop(g)>\log\specrad[g_{*}:H_{*}(M;\mathbb{R})\to H_{*}(M;\mathbb{R})]

.

Note that the linear diffeomorphism
g_{T}
on the
4
-torus

induced by

does satisfy

\htop(g_{T})=\log|\lambda_{1}|+\log|\lambda_{2}|+\log|\lambda_{3}|=\log\specrad[(g_{T})_{*}:H_{*}(M;\mathbb{R})\to H_{*}(M;\mathbb{R})]

(1.3)

and so

minimizes entropy in its homotopy class. The reason that
(

Relation with other work.
Theorem
1.1
addresses the following question, which is implicit in work of Smale, Shub, Shub-Sullivan and others.

Question 1.4
.

Does every nontrivial isotopy class of diffeomorphism on a smooth manifold have a representative minimizing entropy in the isotopy class? If so, find such a representative.

As we indicate above, Theorem
1.1
seems to give
(as far as we can tell) the first new examples since the 1980s of an entropy minimizer.

We also mention that there exist positive entropy biholomorphic automorphisms of K3 surfaces whose dynamics is quite different than what appears on Kummer surfaces; see McMullen
[
Mc
]
and Cantat
[
Ca
]
.

This paper fits into a program of the authors to give a “Thurston classification” for elements of the mapping class
group of the K3 manifold. This paper is the “pseudo-Anosov” case. See
[
FL1
]
and
[
FL2
]
for the finite order and reducible cases, respectively.

Constructing the diffeomorphism
f_{T}

The construction will involve a path from the identity to

Throughout this section, let

4

2.1
The Kummer surface

The quotient

is a real torus of dimension
4

A[2]

2
-torsion points can be identified with
\frac{1}{2}L/L\cong\mathbb{F}_{2}\otimes L

has the structure of a
\mathbb{F}_{2}
-vector space of dimension
4

Assume now that
V
has a complex structure. Then

is a complex surface and one can define the complex blowup

\Bl_{A[2]}(A)\to A

of
A[2]
in
A
. Thus
\Bl_{A[2]}(A)
has sixteen exceptional divisors, each a copy of
\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}
with self-intersection
-1

-{\rm I}\in\GL(L)
is
\mathbb{C}
-linear, its associated involution of

is holomorphic and lifts to a holomorphic involution

\iota:\Bl_{A[2]}(A)\to\Bl_{A[2]}(A).

Although
\iota
does not act freely on

\Kum(A):=A/\la\iota\ra

associated with

\Kum(A)

\Kum(A)
is a particular type of K3 surface. We refer to a K3 manifold of this type as a
Kummer manifold

2.2
The case of complex automorphisms
T

Since
V
can be identified with
\mathbb{R}\otimes L
, there is a representation

\GL(L)\to\Aut(A)

denoted by
T\mapsto T_{A}
. The restriction of this map
to
A[2]
, denoted
T\mapsto T_{A}[2]
, factors through an action
of
\GL(\mathbb{F}_{2}\otimes L)

Note that

is the fixed-point set of the involution of

-{\rm I}\in\SL(L)

\mathbb{F}_{2}\otimes L

Let

be given. If

lifts to an automorphism

These automorphisms are well-studied, see e.g.

It will be convenient to use the characterization of entropy for compact metric spaces as given in §1 of Bowen
[

The entropy

Bowen proves in

Strong coupling phases of conserved growth models are crumpled
Debayan Jana, Abhik Basu

Abstract
We show that stochastically driven nonequilibrium conserved growth models admit strong coupling phases for sufficiently strong nonlocal chemical potentials underlying the dynamics. The models exhibit generic roughening transitions between perturbatively accessible weak coupling phases satisfying an exact relation between the dynamic z z and roughness χ \chi exponents in all dimensions d d and strong coupling phases. In dimensions below the critical dimension, the latter phases are unstable and argued to be crumpled, and thus distinct from the well-known strong coupling rough phase of the Kardar-Parisi-Zhang equation in dimensions d ≥ 2 d\geq 2 . At the critical dimension, conventional spatio-temporal scaling in the weak coupling phase is logarithmically modulated and are exactly obtained. These results obtained by employing a combination of nonperturbative arguments and perturbative theories, corroborated by numerical results.

In this Letter, we investigate the strong coupling phases and the associated roughening transitions in a general class of noisy conserved growth models with an underlying chemical potential
\mu
controlling the dynamics containing a nonlocal part. We demonstrate that when the nonlocal component of
\mu

However, at stronger nonlocality the surfaces can become nonlinearly unstable, even if it is linearly stable, and signaling a transition to a strong coupling phase. In the special cases where our model reduces to CKPZ+
Caballero et al. 2018
or MBE+
Mukherjee and Basu 2022a
, we find that their previously studied perturbatively inaccessible strong coupling regimes are, in fact, unstable implying interface crumpling. Our conclusions are supported by a combination of perturbative calculations, nonperturbative reasoning, and numerical simulations.

To summarize, we have studied a generalized conserved surface growth model that in two distinct limits reduce to the generalized CKPZ and generalized MBE equations. We show that in the strong coupling phases of this equation, achieved for sufficiently strong nonlocal contributions to the chemical potential, the surface is unstable. We use a variety of techniques, analytical and numerical, to argue that this unstable surface is actually a crumpled surface. This makes the strong coupling phases in this model and the associated roughening transition distinct from those in the KPZ equations in
d>2
.

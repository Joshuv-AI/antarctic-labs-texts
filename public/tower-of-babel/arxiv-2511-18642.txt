Solving Equilibrium Problem with New Inertial Technique
Chidi Elijah Nwakpa 1 , {}^{1}, Chinedu Izuchukwu 1 , {}^{1}, Chibueze CHristian Okeke 1 Dilber Uzun Ozsahin 2,3,4 Abubakar Adamu ∗4,5

Abstract
. We propose in this work a subgradient extragradient method with inertial and correction terms for solving equilibrium problems in a real Hilbert space. We obtain that the sequence generated by our proposed method converges weakly to a point in the solutions set of the equilibrium problem when the associated bivariate function is pseudomonotone and satisfies Lipschitz conditions. Furthermore, in a case where the bifunction is strongly pseudomonotone, we establish a linear convergence rate. Lastly, through different numerical examples, we demonstrate that the incorporation of multiple correction terms significantly improves our proposed method when compared with other methods in the literature.



1.
Introduction

Throughout this paper, we denote a real Hilbert space by
\mathcal{H}
and its closed convex nonempty subset by
\mathcal{C}
.
Let
f
:
\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{C}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a
bifunction such that
f(z,z)=0
for each
z\in\mathcal{C}
. With this bifunction
f
, an equilibrium problem (EP, in short) is defined by:

(1.1)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\mbox{obtain}\ \bar{v}\in\mathcal{C}\ \ \mbox{for \ which}\\
f(\bar{v},z)\geq 0,\ \ \forall z\in\mathcal{C}.\end{cases}

Let us designate the solutions set of problem (
1.1
) by
EP(f,\mathcal{C}):=\{\bar{v}\in\mathcal{C}~|~f(\bar{v},z)\geq 0\ \ \forall z\in\mathcal{C}\}
.
Equilibrium problem is very general in the sense that different important mathematical models such as optimization problems, fixed point problems, complementarity problems, saddle point problems, variational inequality problems, Nash equilibrium problems, are classified as special cases (see,
[
3
,
22
]
). The broad applications of EPs to various aspect of engineering, decision making and optimal control in economics, financial analysis, mathematical sciences and others, have drawn the attention of several authors to introduce and study different methods ( see, for instance
[
1
,
4
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
15
,
26
,
32
,
35
]
) of solving this problem (
1.1
) in finite and infinite spaces. It is worth noting that the proximal point method developed by Moudafi
[
20
]
is the most popular technique for solving the EP(
1.1
), although this method cannot be used to solve pseudomonotone EP since the convergence may fail to exist even for a monotone problem. So, in order to overcome this challenge, the Korpelevich extragradient method (EGM) for solving the saddle point problem
[
16
]
was extended by Quoc
et al.
in
[
28
]
to solve a class of EP(
1.1
) that is pseudomonotone, hence, ensuring convergence. According to them, the solutions of problem (
1.1
) can be estimated using the following method:

(1.2)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}y_{n}=\underset{z\in\mathcal{C}}{arg\min}\left\{\lambda{f(x_{n},z)}+\dfrac{1}{2}\|z-x_{n}\|^{2}\right\},\\
x_{n+1}=\underset{z\in\mathcal{C}}{arg\min}\left\{\lambda{f(y_{n},z)}+\dfrac{1}{2}\|z-x_{n}\|^{2}\right\},\ \mbox{with}\ x_{0}\in\mathcal{C}\ \ \mbox{and}\ \ \lambda>0.\end{cases}

However, the EGM poses another computational challenge, as it involves solving two strongly convex sub-problems within the convex set
\mathcal{C}
at each computational step. Moreover, computation may become nearly impossible, particularly when the convex set
\mathcal{C}
exhibits structural complexity.

New iterative methods and modifications to existing ones are introduced to improve the computational efficiency of algorithms. For this reason, the EGM was modified, leading to the introduction of the subgradient extragradient method (SGEGM) for solving EP(
1.1
). In the SGEGM, a suitable half-space is constructed, and a strongly convex program is solved over it, which lowers computational costs and enhances the performance of the iterative method. For some SGEGMs, see
[
4
,
6
,
34
,
35
]
and the references quoted therein.

As new methods for solving Ep(
1.1
) and other optimization problems evolve, researchers are increasingly interested in exploring different techniques to achieve faster convergence of these methods. One approach is to incorporate inertial terms into these methods. The use of inertial-like algorithms can be traced back to Polyak’s work
[
27
]
on the Heavy Ball with Friction (HBF), a second-order dynamical system. The inertial extrapolation term arose naturally from an implicit time discretization of the HBF system. A distinctive feature of this algorithm is that it utilizes the previous two iterates to determine the next iterate. Building on this idea, Moudafi
[
21
]
developed an inertial-proximal method for solving EP(
1.1
) and obtained that the sequence generated by his method converges weakly, under certain conditions, to a point in
EP(f,\mathcal{C}).
Motivated by the work in
[
21
]
, Vinh and Muu in
[
23
, Algorithm 1]
proposed and obtained a weak convergence result for an inertial EGM for solving EP(
1.1
) by incorporating the on-line rule, which is a restrictive condition that is not always desirable in real-life problems. This is because the on-line rule can slow the convergence of an algorithm, particularly when dealing with large-scale problems. To obtain a faster-converging algorithm for solving EP(
1.1
), the authors in
[
24
]
, under some implementable conditions, proposed a relaxed inertial method and established a weak convergence result and a linear convergence rate.

Furthermore, another technique for accelerating algorithms for solving optimization problems involves incorporating correction terms. On this note, Kim
[
14
]
in his work introduced an accelerated proximal point algorithm that combines the proximal point algorithm with inertial and correction terms. Although no weak convergence result was obtained for the generated sequences, Kim
[
14
, Theorem 4.1]
leveraged the performance estimation problem (PEP) approach of Drori and Teboulle
[
7
]
to obtain the worst-case convergence rate
\Big(\|y_{n}-w_{n-1}\|=\mathcal{O}(n^{-1})\Big)
for the following algorithm:

(1.3)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}w_{n}=y_{n}+\dfrac{n-1}{n+1}(y_{n}-y_{n-1})+\dfrac{n-1}{n+1}(w_{n-2}-y_{n-1}),\\
\\
y_{n+1}=J^{A}_{\lambda}(w_{n}).\end{cases}

In another related work, Maingé
[
17
]
further investigated the proximal point algorithm by combining inertial, relaxation, and correction terms, with a focus on solving monotone inclusion problems. He proposed the following method

(1.4)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}w_{n}=y_{n}+\alpha_{n}(y_{n}-y_{n-1})+\delta_{n}(w_{n-1}-y_{n}),\\
\\
y_{n+1}=\dfrac{1}{1+\alpha_{n}}w_{n}+\dfrac{\alpha_{n}}{1+\alpha_{n}}J^{A}_{\lambda(1+\alpha_{n})}(w_{n}),\end{cases}

and established weak convergence results and the fast rate,
\|y_{n+1}-y_{n}\|=o(n^{-1}),
for the generated sequence. However, no linear convergence result was obtained. Motivated by the works of Kim
[
14
]
and Maingé
[
17
]
, Izuchukwu
et al.

[
13
]
proposed another method for solving proximal point problems. Their method involves incorporating two correction terms, yielding an approach that can be described as:

(1.5)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}w_{n}=y_{n}+\alpha(y_{n}-y_{n-1})+\delta(1+\alpha)(w_{n-1}-y_{n})-\alpha\delta(w_{n-2}-y_{n-1}),\\
\\
y_{n+1}=J^{A}_{\lambda}(w_{n}).\end{cases}

They obtained, in
[
13
, Theorem 3.5, Theorem 3.7]
, a weak convergence result and linear convergence rate, respectively, for their proposed method under certain conditions. Through numerical examples, they further revealed that the incorporation of multiple correction terms significantly accelerates their method, outperforming those in
[
14
,
17
]
that rely on a single correction term.

Building on the works of Izuchukwu
et al.

[
13
]
, Kim
[
14
]
, and Maingé
[
17
]
, this paper proposes a subgradient extragradient method with inertial and correction terms for solving equilibrium problems, as formulated in (
1.1
). The key contributions of this approach include:

•

Combining one inertial term with two correction terms to propose a subgradient extragradient method for solving pseudomonotone equilibrium problems in a real Hilbert space;

•

Incorporating a self-adaptive stepsize, which is distinct from the methods proposed in
[
13
,
14
,
17
]
and the constant stepsize used in
[
36
]
;

•

Obtaining a weak convergence result and a linear convergence rate for the sequence generated by our proposed method, and providing numerical examples to justify the effectiveness of our proposed method.

We organize the rest of this paper as follows:
In Section
2
, we collect some basic definitions and technical lemmas needed in subsequent sections. In Section
3
, we present and discuss our proposed method. Also, a weak convergence result is given in this section. In Section
4
, we obtain a linear convergence rate of our proposed method, while Section
5
provides numerical results that assess the performance of our proposed method in comparison to other established methods in the literature.

2.
Preliminaries

This section establishes the necessary foundation for the convergence analysis of our proposed algorithm by providing fundamental definitions and lemmas. For our analysis, we will use the symbols
\to
and
\rightharpoonup
to denote strong and weak convergence, respectively.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a nonempty, closed, and convex subset of a real Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}.

For each
s,w,z\in\mathcal{C},
a bifunction
f:\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{C}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
is said to be:

i.

monotone on
\mathcal{C}
if

\displaystyle f(s,w)+f(w,s)\leq 0;

ii.

strongly pseudomonotone on
\mathcal{C}
if there exists a positive constant
\beta
such that

\displaystyle f(s,w)\geq 0\implies f(w,s)+\beta\|s-w\|^{2}\leq 0;

iii.

pseudomonotone on
\mathcal{C}
if

\displaystyle f(s,w)\geq 0\implies f(w,s)\leq 0;

iv.

satisfying Lipschitz-type conditions on
\mathcal{C}
if there exist two constants
\delta_{1}>0
and
\delta_{2}>0
such that

\displaystyle f(s,z)-f(s,w)-f(w,z)\leq\kappa_{1}\|s-w\|^{2}+\kappa_{2}\|w-z\|^{2}.

Clearly,
(i)\implies(iii)
and
(ii)\implies(iii)
but the converse statements are not generally true.

In the following definition, we will recall the concept of proximity operator originally introduced by Moreau
[
19
]
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
g:\mathcal{C}\subset\mathcal{H}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
be a function that satisfies properness, convexity and lower semicontinuity, and
\lambda>0.
The Moreau envelope of
g
is the function

{}^{\lambda}g(s)=\underset{z\in\mathcal{C}}{\inf}\left\{g(z)+\dfrac{1}{2\lambda}\|z-s\|^{2},\ \ \forall s\in\mathcal{H}\right\}.

But for each
s\in\mathcal{H},
the function

\displaystyle z\longmapsto g(z)+\dfrac{1}{2\lambda}\|z-s\|^{2}

is strongly convex and satisfies the properness and lower semicontinuity properties. Thus, it attains its infimum in
\mathbb{R}.
The unique minimizer of the function
z\longmapsto g(z)+\dfrac{1}{2\lambda}\|z-s\|^{2},
denoted by
prox_{g}(s),
is known as the proximal point operator of
g
at
s.
Hence, the operator
prox_{\lambda g}:\mathcal{H}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
is defined by

\displaystyle prox_{\lambda g}(s):=\underset{z\in\mathcal{C}}{arg\min}\left\{\lambda g(z)+\dfrac{1}{2}\|s-z\|^{2}\right\}.

If
g=\delta_{\mathcal{C}},
where
\delta_{\mathcal{C}}
is the indicator function of the set
\mathcal{C},
then

\displaystyle prox_{\delta_{\mathcal{C}}}(s)=\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{C}}(s)\ \ \forall s\in\mathcal{H}.

Lemma 2.3
.

[
2
]

For each
s\in\mathcal{H},

z\in\mathcal{C}
and positive constant
\lambda,
the following inequality is true

\lambda(g(z)-g(prox_{\lambda g}(s)))\geq\langle s-prox_{\lambda g}(s),z-prox_{\lambda g}(s)\rangle.

Definition 2.4
.

i.

Let
f:\mathcal{H}\times\mathcal{H}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a convex bifunction, then, for each
s,w\in\mathcal{H},
the subdifferential of
f(s,.)
at
w
is defined by

\displaystyle\partial_{2}f(s,w)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\{u\in\mathcal{H}:f(s,z)\geq f(s,w)+\langle u,z-w\rangle,\ \ \forall z\in\mathcal{H}\}.

And in particular,

\displaystyle\partial_{2}f(s,s)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\{u\in\mathcal{H}:f(s,z)\geq\langle u,z-s\rangle,\ \ \forall z\in\mathcal{H}\}.

For any
s
fixed in
\mathcal{H},
the bifunction
f
is said to be subdifferentiable at
w
if
\partial_{2}f(s,w)\neq\emptyset.
It is said to be subdifferentiable on a set
\mathcal{C}
in
\mathcal{H}
if it is subdifferentiable at every point
w\in\mathcal{C},
and if
dom(\partial_{2}f)=\mathcal{H},

then, we say that
f
is subdifferentiable.

ii.

The normal cone
\mathcal{N}_{C}
of the convex set
\mathcal{C}
is defined at a point
s
by:

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{C}(s)=\{v\in\mathcal{H}:\langle v,z-s\rangle\leq 0,\ \ \forall z\in\mathcal{C}\}.

iii.

Let
s\in\mathcal{H},
there exists a unique closest point in
\mathcal{H}
denoted by
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{C}}(s)
such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{C}(s)=arg\min\{\|s-z\|\ \ :~z\in\mathcal{C}\}.

\mathcal{P}_{C}
is called the metric projection of
\mathcal{H}
onto
\mathcal{C}.

.

f:\mathcal{H}\times\mathcal{H}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}

\partial_{2}f(s,w)

.

For any point

the normal cone
\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{C}}

Lemma 2.6
.

[
31
]

Let
g:\mathcal{C}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a convex, lower semicontinuous and subdifferentiable function on
\mathcal{C}

Lemma 2.7
.

The following is true for each
s,z\in\mathcal{H}
and
\beta\in\mathbb{R};

(i)

\|(1-\beta)s-\beta z\|^{2}=(1-\beta)\|s\|^{2}-\beta\|z\|^{2}+\beta(1-\beta)\|s-z\|^{2};

(ii)

2\langle s,z\rangle=\|s\|^{2}+\|z\|^{2}-\|s-z\|^{2}=\|s+z\|^{2}-\|s\|^{2}-\|z\|^{2}.

Lemma 2.8
.

[
25

Let
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathcal{H}
be nonempty, and
\{x_{n}\}
a sequence in
\mathcal{H}
such that:

(i)

\underset{n\to\infty}{\lim}\|x_{n}-\bar{r}\|

(ii)

Definition 2.9
.

A sequence
\{x_{n}\}
in
\mathcal{H}
is said to converge

\bar{v}
with rate

\|x_{n}-\bar{v}\|\leq\kappa\rho^{n}\ \ \ \mbox{for all}\ n\in\mathbb{N}.

3.
Main Results

We will assume that the bifunction
f
satisfies the following conditions:

Assumptions 3.1
.

(B1)

For each
s\in\mathcal{C},\ \ f(s,s)=0
and
f
is pseudomonotone on
\mathcal{C};

(B2)

With two positive constants
\kappa_{1}
and
\kappa_{2},

f
satisfies the Lipschitz-type conditions on
\mathcal{H};

(B3)

f(s,.)
is convex, lower semicontinuous and subdifferentiable on
\mathcal{C}
for each
s\in\mathcal{C};

(B4)

For all fixed point
y\in\mathcal{C},

f(.,y)
is sequentially weakly upper semicontinuous on
\mathcal{C}.
That is, if
x_{n}\in\mathcal{C}
is such that
x_{n}\rightharpoonup x^{*},
as
n\to\infty,
then,
\underset{n\to\infty}{\limsup}f(x_{n},y_{)}\leq f(x^{*},y)
for each fixed
y\in\mathcal{C}
and
x_{n}\in\mathcal{C};

(B5)

EP(f,\mathcal{C})\neq\emptyset.

Assumptions 3.2
.

Suppose that
\alpha\in[0,1)
and
\delta\in(0,1)
satisfy the following conditions:

\alpha\leq\dfrac{1}{2}\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;\max\left\{\dfrac{2\alpha}{1+\alpha},\dfrac{(\alpha^{2}+2)-\sqrt{\alpha^{4}-8\alpha^{3}-8\alpha^{2}+4}}{2\alpha}\right\}<\delta<1.

Now, we introduce our proposed method as follows:

Algorithm 3.3
.

Subgradient extragradient method with inertial and correction terms.

STEP 0:
Choose
\alpha
and
\delta
such that Assumption
3.2
is true. Take
w_{-1}=w_{-2},\ y_{0},\ y_{-1}\in\mathcal{H}
arbitrarily. Let
\lambda_{0}>0,\ \mu\in]0,1[
and set
n=0
.

STEP 1
: Whenever
w_{n-1},\ w_{n-2},\ y_{n-1}
and
y_{n}
are given,
compute

\displaystyle\begin{cases}w_{n}=y_{n}+\alpha(y_{n}-y_{n-1})+\delta(1+\alpha)(w_{n-1}-y_{n})-\alpha\delta(w_{n-2}-y_{n-1}),\\
\ \\
z_{n}=\underset{z\in\mathcal{C}}{argmin}\left\{\lambda_{n}f(w_{n},z)+\dfrac{1}{2}\|w_{n}-z\|^{2}\right\}=\mbox{prox}_{\lambda_{n}f(w_{n},\cdot)}(w_{n}).\end{cases}

Stop the computation if
z_{n}=w_{n},
and
z_{n}
becomes a solution, otherwise, move to step 2.

STEP 2
:

Choose
v_{n}\in\partial_{2}f(w_{n},.)(z_{n})
in such a way there is
b_{n}\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{C}}(z_{n})
satisfying

b_{n}=w_{n}-\lambda_{n}v_{n}-z_{n}
and construct a half space

T_{n}=\{x\in\mathcal{H}:\langle w_{n}-\lambda_{n}v_{n}-z_{n},x-z_{n}\rangle\leq 0\}.

Evaluate

y_{n+1}=\underset{z\in T_{n}}{argmin}\left\{\lambda_{n}f(z_{n},z)+\dfrac{1}{2}\|w_{n}-z\|^{2}\right\}=\mbox{prox}_{\lambda_{n}f(z_{n},\cdot)}(w_{n}).

STEP 3
: Update the stepsize as follows:

\displaystyle\lambda_{n+1}=\begin{cases}\min\left\{\dfrac{\mu}{2}\dfrac{\|w_{n}-z_{n}\|^{2}+\|y_{n+1}-z_{n}\|^{2}}{f(w_{n},y_{n+1})-f(w_{n},z_{n})-f(z_{n},y_{n+1})},\ \ \lambda_{n}\right\},\ \ \ \mbox{if}\ f(w_{n},y_{n+1})-f(w_{n},z_{n})-f(z_{n},y_{n+1})>0,\\
\\
\lambda_{n},\ \ \ \mbox{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Set
n+1\leftarrow n
and go back to
step 1
.

Remark 3.4
.

•

Since
z_{n}\in\mathcal{C}
and
f
is convex in
\mathcal{C}
, we have by Remark
2.5
that
\partial_{2}f(w_{n},.)(z_{n})\neq\emptyset
and
\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{C}}(z_{n})\neq\emptyset.
Therefore, there exist
v_{n}\in\partial_{2}f(w_{n},.)(z_{n})
and
b_{n}\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{C}}(z_{n})
such that step 2 of Algorithm
3.3
is well defined.

•

If Algorithm
3.3
terminates after a finite number of iterations, that is,
z_{n}=w_{n},
then
z_{n}
becomes a solution to the equilibrium problem. In fact, by the definition of
z_{n}
and Lemma
2.6
, it follows that

\displaystyle 0\in\partial_{2}\left(\lambda_{n}f(w_{n},.)+\dfrac{1}{2}\|w_{n}-.\|^{2}\right)(z_{n})+\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{C}}(z_{n}).

With
v_{n}\in\partial_{2}f(w_{n},.)(z_{n})
and
b_{n}\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{C}}(z_{n}),
then

\lambda_{n}v_{n}+z_{n}-w_{n}+b_{n}=0.

Since by the assumption,
z_{n}=w_{n},
it then follows that

\lambda_{n}v_{n}+b_{n}=0.

This further implies that

(3.1)

\displaystyle\lambda_{n}\langle v_{n},z-z_{n}\rangle+\langle b_{n},z-z_{n}\rangle=0,\ \ \forall z\in\mathcal{C}.

But
b_{n}\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{C}}(z_{n})
implies that
\langle b_{n},z-z_{n}\rangle\leq 0,

\forall z\in\mathcal{C}.
So, we have from (
3.1
) that

\displaystyle\lambda_{n}\langle v_{n},z-z_{n}\rangle\geq 0,\ \ \forall z\in\mathcal{C}.

Given that
v_{n}\in\partial_{2}f(w_{n},.)(z_{n})
and
\lambda_{n}>0

\forall n\geq 1,
then we can use the definition of subdifferential to obtain

\displaystyle 0\leq\langle v_{n},z-z_{n}\rangle\leq f(w_{n},z)-f(w_{n},z_{n})=f(w_{n},z),\ \ \forall z\in\mathcal{C}.

Hence,
f(w_{n},z)\geq 0,

\forall z\in\mathcal{C}.
That is,
z_{n}=w_{n}\in EP(f,\mathcal{C}).

However, throughout this section, to ensure that Algorithm
3.3
generates an infinite sequence of iterations, we will assume that Algorithm
3.3
does not terminate in a finite number of iterations.

Remark 3.5
.

Setting
\alpha=0
and
\delta=0
in our proposed Algorithm

reduces it to the method in

Lemma 3.6
.

[

The sequence

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
\{w_{n}\},\ \{y_{n}\}
be the sequences generated by Algorithm

. Suppose that Assumption
3.2
holds. Then, under conditions
(B1),\ (B2),\ (B3)

Proof.

Since
z_{n}\in\mathcal{C},
we obtain from Lemma
2.6
that

\displaystyle 0\in\partial_{2}\left(\lambda_{n}f(w_{n},.)+\frac{1}{2}\|.-w_{n}\|^{2}\right)(z_{n})+\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{C}}(z_{n}).

Thus, there is a vector
v_{n}\in\partial_{2}f(w_{n},\cdot)(z_{n})
and
b_{n}\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{C}}(z_{n})
such that

w_{n}-\lambda_{n}v_{n}-z_{n}-b_{n}=0.

From the above expression, it follows that

(3.2)

\displaystyle\langle w_{n}-z_{n},z-z_{n}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\lambda_{n}v_{n}+b_{n},z-z_{n}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda_{n}\langle v_{n},z-z_{n}\rangle+\langle b_{n},z-z_{n}\rangle.

But
b_{n}\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{C}}(z_{n})
implies that
\langle b_{n},z-z_{n}\rangle\leq 0\ \ \ \forall z\in\mathcal{C}.

Thus, we obtain from (
3.2
) that

(3.3)

\displaystyle\langle w_{n}-z_{n},z-z_{n}\rangle\leq\lambda_{n}\langle v_{n},z-z_{n}\rangle\ \ \ \forall z\in\mathcal{C}.

Also, by choosing
v_{n}\in\partial_{2}f(w_{n},z_{n})
we obtain that

(3.4)

\displaystyle f(w_{n},z)-f(w_{n},z_{n})\geq\langle v_{n},z-z_{n}\rangle\ \ \forall z\in\mathcal{H}.

But
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathcal{H},
hence, using (

\displaystyle\varphi_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{1-\delta}\|u_{n}-\bar{v}\|^{2}-\dfrac{\alpha}{1-\delta}\|u_{n-1}-\bar{v}\|^{2}+\dfrac{\delta(1-\alpha)}{(1-\delta)^{2}}\|u_{n}-u_{n-1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle 0,

\displaystyle\delta-\left(\dfrac{(\alpha^{2}+2)-\sqrt{(\alpha^{2}+2)^{2}-4\alpha(3\alpha+2\alpha^{2})}}{2\alpha}\right)>0,

\displaystyle\delta-\left(\dfrac{(\alpha^{2}+2)+\sqrt{(\alpha^{2}+2)^{2}-4\alpha(3\alpha+2\alpha^{2})}}{2\alpha}\right)<0,

Multiplying (

Theorem 3.8
.

Let Assumptions
3.1
and
3.2
be satisfied. Then the sequences,
\{y_{n}\}
and
\{w_{n}\},

).

Proof.

Using

we obtain

\underset{n\to\infty}{\lim}\|w_{n}-u_{n}\|=\alpha\underset{n\to\infty}{\lim}\|u_{n}-u_{n-1}\|=0.

\{u_{n}\},

\displaystyle\left(1-\mu\dfrac{\lambda_{n}}{\lambda_{n+1}}\right)\Big(\|w_{n}-z_{n}\|^{2}+\|z_{n}-y_{n+1}\|^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\|w_{n}-\bar{v}\|^{2}-\|y_{n+1}-\bar{v}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\Big(\|w_{n}-\bar{v}\|+\|y_{n+1}-\bar{v}\|\Big)\|y_{n+1}-w_{n}\|^{2}

(3.33)

) that

) with (

\displaystyle\lambda_{n}(f(w_{n},y_{n+1})-f(w_{n},z_{n}))-\frac{\mu\lambda_{n}\Bigl(\|w_{n}-z_{n}\|^{2}+\|y_{n+1}-z_{n}\|^{2}\Bigr)}{2\lambda_{n+1}}

), we obtain

) and (

) together give

\displaystyle\lambda_{n}f(z_{n},z)

\displaystyle\geq

So from the last inequality, we obtain

Next, we show that

exists,

\|y_{n}-r^{*}\|^{2}=\|y_{n}-u_{n}\|^{2}+2\langle y_{n}-u_{n},u_{n}-r^{*}\rangle+\|u_{n}-r^{*}\|^{2}

that
\underset{n\to\infty}{\lim}\|y_{n}-r^{*}\|
exists for any
r^{*}\in EP(f,\mathcal{C}).
Thus, we conclude from Lemma
2.8
that
\{y_{n}\}
converges weakly to a point in
EP(f,\mathcal{C}).

∎

4.
Convergence Rate

This section presents the linear convergence rate theorem. In this theorem, we establish that the sequence generated by Algorithm
3.3
converges uniquely to the set of solutions of EP(
1.1
), assuming the
\beta-
strong pseudomonotonicity of the bifunction
f.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
f:\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{C}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a
\beta-
strongly pseudomonotone bifunction satisfying
(B2),

(B3)
and
(B5)
of Assumption
3.1
. Then the sequence produced by Algorithm
3.3
converges to the unique solution
r^{*}
of EP(
1.1
) with an
R-
linear rate when
\alpha=0
and
0<\delta<1
.

Proof.

From (
3.8
), we have

(4.1)

\displaystyle-\lambda_{n}f(z_{n},y_{n+1})\geq\langle w_{n}-y_{n+1},r^{*}-y_{n+1}\rangle-\lambda_{n}f(z_{n},r^{*})\ \ \forall r^{*}\in EP(f,\mathcal{C}).

Since
r^{*}\in EP(f,\mathcal{C})
and
z_{n}\in\mathcal{C},
it follows that
f(r^{*},z_{n})\geq 0.
Thus, by the strong pseudomonotonicity assumption on
f
, we have that

f(z_{n},r^{*})\leq-\beta\|z_{n}-r^{*}\|^{2}.

This implies from (
4.1
) that

(4.2)

\displaystyle-\lambda_{n}f(z_{n},y_{n+1})\geq\langle w_{n}-y_{n+1},r^{*}-y_{n+1}\rangle+\lambda_{n}\beta\|z_{n}-r^{*}\|^{2}\ \ \forall r^{*}\in EP(f,\mathcal{C}).

Combining (
3.6
) with (
4.2
) gives us that

(4.3)

\displaystyle 2\lambda_{n}\Big(f(w_{n},y_{n+1})-f(w_{n},z_{n})-f(z_{n},y_{n+1})\Big)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 2\langle w_{n}-z_{n},y_{n+1}-z_{n}\rangle+2\langle w_{n}-y_{n+1},r^{*}-y_{n+1}\rangle

\displaystyle\;+\;\lambda_{n}\beta\|z_{n}-r^{*}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|w_{n}-z_{n}\|^{2}+\|y_{n+1}-z_{n}\|^{2}-\|y_{n+1}-w_{n}\|^{2}+\|y_{n+1}-w_{n}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\;+\;\|y_{n+1}-r^{*}\|^{2}-\|w_{n}-r^{*}\|^{2}+2\lambda_{n}\beta\|z_{n}-r^{*}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|w_{n}-z_{n}\|^{2}+\|y_{n+1}-z_{n}\|^{2}+\|y_{n+1}-r^{*}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\;-\;\|w_{n}-r^{*}\|^{2}+2\lambda_{n}\beta\|z_{n}-r^{*}\|^{2}.

By the definition of
\lambda_{n},
we obtain

(4.4)

\displaystyle 2\lambda_{n}\Big(f(w_{n},y_{n+1})-f(w_{n},z_{n})-f(z_{n},y_{n+1})\Big)\leq\dfrac{\lambda_{n}\mu}{\lambda_{n+1}}\Big(\|w_{n}-z_{n}\|^{2}+\|z_{n}-y_{n+1}\|^{2}\Big).

Using (
4.4
) in (
4.3
) gives

(4.5)

\displaystyle\|y_{n+1}-r^{*}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|w_{n}-r^{*}\|^{2}-\left(1-\mu\dfrac{\lambda_{n}}{\lambda_{n+1}}\right)\|w_{n}-z_{n}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\;-\;\left(1-\mu\dfrac{\lambda_{n}}{\lambda_{n+1}}\right)\|y_{n+1}-z_{n}\|^{2}-2\lambda_{n}\beta\|z_{n}-r^{*}\|^{2}.

Let

\gamma:=\min\left\{1-\mu,\lambda\beta\right\},

where
\lambda:=\underset{n\to\infty}{\lim}\lambda_{n},
we obtain

\underset{n\to\infty}{\lim}\left(1-\mu\dfrac{\lambda_{n}}{\lambda_{n+1}}\right)=1-\mu\geq\gamma,

\underset{n\to\infty}{\lim}\lambda_{n}\beta=\lambda\beta\geq\gamma.

Therefore, there exists
N_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\left(1-\mu\dfrac{\lambda_{n}}{\lambda_{n+1}}\right)\geq\gamma\ \ \ \ \forall n\geq N_{1}\ \mbox{and}

\lambda_{n}\beta\geq\gamma\ \ \ \ \ \forall n\geq N_{1}.

So, using (
4.5
) it follows that

\displaystyle\|y_{n+1}-r^{*}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|w_{n}-r^{*}\|^{2}-\gamma\|w_{n}-z_{n}\|^{2}-\gamma\|y_{n+1}-z_{n}\|^{2}-2\gamma\|z_{n}-r^{*}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|w_{n}-r^{*}\|^{2}-\dfrac{\gamma}{2}\|w_{n}-r^{*}\|^{2}-\dfrac{\gamma}{2}\|y_{n+1}-r^{*}\|^{2}.

This implies

(4.6)

\displaystyle\|y_{n+1}-r^{*}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\dfrac{1-\dfrac{\gamma}{2}}{1+\dfrac{\gamma}{2}}\right)\|w_{n}-r^{*}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega\|w_{n}-r^{*}\|^{2},

where
\omega=\left(\dfrac{1-\dfrac{\gamma}{2}}{1+\dfrac{\gamma}{2}}\right).

Recall that
y_{n+1}=\dfrac{1}{1-\delta}u_{n+1}-\dfrac{\delta}{1-\delta}w_{n}.

Therefore,

(4.7)

\displaystyle\|y_{n+1}-r^{*}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{1-\delta}\|u_{n+1}-r^{*}\|^{2}-\dfrac{\delta}{1-\delta}\|w_{n}-r^{*}\|^{2}+\dfrac{\delta}{(1-\delta)^{2}}\|u_{n+1}-w_{n}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{1-\delta}\|u_{n+1}-r^{*}\|^{2}-\dfrac{\delta}{1-\delta}\|w_{n}-r^{*}\|^{2}+\delta\|y_{n+1}-w_{n}\|^{2}.

Also, recall that
w_{n}=u_{n}+\alpha(u_{n}-u_{n-1}).
This implies that

\|w_{n+1}-r^{*}\|^{2}=\|u_{n+1}-r^{*}\|^{2},

since
\alpha=0.
Therefore, (
4.7
) gives us that

(4.8)

\displaystyle\|y_{n+1}-r^{*}\|^{2}=\dfrac{1}{1-\delta}\|w_{n+1}-r^{*}\|^{2}-\dfrac{\delta}{1-\delta}\|w_{n}-r^{*}\|^{2}+\delta\|y_{n+1}-w_{n}\|^{2}.

Using (
4.8
) in (
4.6
), we have

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{1-\delta}\|w_{n+1}-r^{*}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\omega+\dfrac{\delta}{1-\delta}\right)\|w_{n}-r^{*}\|^{2}-\delta\|y_{n+1}-w_{n}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{1-\delta}\Big[\omega(1-\delta)+\delta\Big]\|w_{n}-r^{*}\|^{2}.

That is,

\displaystyle\|w_{n+1}-r^{*}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\rho\|w_{n}-r^{*}\|^{2}

where
\rho=\Big[\omega(1-\delta)+\delta\Big].

This implies

\|w_{n+1}-r^{*}\|\leq\rho\|w_{n}-r^{*}\|^{2}\leq\cdots\leq\rho^{n-N_{1}+1}\|w_{N_{1}}-r^{*}\|^{2}=\dfrac{\rho}{\rho^{N_{1}}}\|w_{N_{1}}-r^{*}\|^{2}\rho^{n}.

Thus,
\{w_{n}\}
converges
R-
linearly to
r^{*}
. Consequently,
\{y_{n}\}
converges
R-
linearly to
r^{*}.

∎

6.
Conclusion

In this work, we introduced a subgradient extragradient method for solving an equilibrium problem in a Hilbert space. One thing that distinguishes our method from others is the incorporation of inertial and correction terms techniques. We obtained a weak convergence result and a linear convergence rate under standard conditions. Through some numerical examples, we were able to justify that the inclusion of these accelerating terms enhances the numerical convergence speed of the subgradient extragradient algorithm, thus outperforming the methods in
[
36
, Algorithm 1]
,
[
37
, Algorithm 4.1]
,
[
38
, Algorithm 3.1]
,
[
39
, Algorithm 3.1]
and
[
40
, Algorithm 3.1]
. In our future research, it is our plan to investigate a three-operator monotone inclusion problem by taking advantage of the inertial and correction terms.

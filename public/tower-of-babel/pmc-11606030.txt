Perceived challenges and improvement strategies for computer-based test in the nursing and midwifery council of nigeria final examination at a university in south eastern Nigeria
Authors: Chidinma E Israel, Anasthesia O Ugwu, Obiageli T Madu, Paulina C Chikeme, Ngozi J Omotola, Cynthia A Attah
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionComputer-based tests (CBT) have been noted as a stellar alternative for examinations, having been expected to address the deficiencies of traditional examination model. This study assessed the perceptions, challenges and strategies for improving the Nursing and Midwifery Council of Nigeria computer-based final qualifying examination.MethodsThe study was a cross-sectional descriptive survey using all the 214 year 2023 graduates of the Department of Nursing Sciences, University of Nigeria, Enugu Campus. Data was collected using a researcher-developed questionnaire.ResultsMajority had a positive perception towards CBT (74.3%). Their was a preference for CBT compared to traditional pen and paper examination (2.96 ± 0.96), Respondents experienced challenges answering long story-like question amidst limited time (74.3%) among others. It was suggested that use of good CBT environment to prevent time wastage, (3.32 ± 0.83), and fixing of malfunctioning computer systems prior to examination (3.32 ± 0.85) will help ameliorate challenges. There were no significant association between perception and sex (p = .308) as well as between the challenges experienced and age (p = .322).ConclusionThe perception of candidates on the use of CBT for final qualifying nursing examinations was positive. Addressing the challenges experienced will ensure a sustained positive and seamless experience for candidates.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12909-024-06413-y.Keywords: Candidates, Challenges, Computer-Based Test, Final Qualifying Examination, Perception, Nursing and Midwifery Council of Nigeria, Strategies

Introduction

Traditional evaluation techniques are still acceptable in educational systems, but significant effort has been made in recent years to replace them with new techniques that are based on student needs and are tech-compatible [1]. The creation of computer-based examination has provided a methodical and technical alternative to the traditional paper and pen test format. Computer-Based Test (CBT) currently seems to be more accepted as a more adequate method for final examinations. Large-scale exam rooms with paper and pencil invigilated exams thus have continued to be replaced by alternatives including online exams, e-examinations, and bring your own device models [2].

CBT is a type of digital test referred to as any activity involving evaluation that makes use of information technology [3]. It can also be defined as tests or assessments that are administered by computer in either stand-alone or dedicated network or by other technology devices linked to the internet or World Wide Web most of them using multiple choice questions [4]. Computers, laptops, smartphones, and iPads are necessary for CBT in order to design, present, save, and report on students' activities, grades, and feedback [5]. CBT can be utilized as a medium for national exams as well as daily and semester exams, which have been predicted to address the flaws of conventional exams [1].

Medical education systems have a critical duty to assess student and faculty performance in order to evaluate instruction and provide feedback [5] as the products of their training are expected to have been carefully examined before launching them into the challenging world of everyday practice where the adequacy of their knowledge and skill will contribute to guarantee patient safety. The contemporary era of digitalization has a significant impact on education; technology and information are used as tools for evaluation as well as for learning.

In the past years, the Nursing and Midwifery Council of Nigeria (NMCN) used the traditional method (Use of paper and pen testing for objectives, theory and objective structured clinical examinations (OSCE) for her professional examination. This method became time consuming as the number of students increased drastically in the past few years. The paper and pen method of writing examination generally was found to be associated with problems including provision of opportunities for examination malpractice [6]. Other flaws include examination venue capacity constraints, cost implication of printing examination materials and other examination logistics as well as human errors. A computer-based testing system is thus a solution for examining large number of students.

On the 27th day of June 2022, the NMCN released a circular stating the approval and implementation of CBT for all her professional examinations starting with the Midwifery examination slated for September 13th-16th, 2022. This decision was reached during the 58th General Meeting of the Board of the Nursing and Midwifery Council of Nigeria held on 23rd-24th June,2022. The change is in line with global best practices in promoting and maintaining excellence in Nursing education and practice [7]. The digital revolution that is sweeping the world has begun to infiltrate the realm of education [8] and NMCN had to key into it. Globally, there is a growing increase in the use of computers for assessment purposes within educational institutions. This development was quite boosted by the outbreak of the novel Corona virus that led to a paradigm shift to virtual or online learning and assessment of which the Nursing and Midwifery council of Nigeria is obviously of no exception. However, this new development triggered a number of mixed reactions from the student’s perspective. Previous research showed that despite fewer students being confident about CBT, before completing the assessment more students stated a preference for CBT over Pen and Paper Testing afterwards [9].

According to NMCN, there are close to three hundred nursing training institutions in Nigeria [10]. The Nursing & Midwifery Council of Nigeria’s Professional Examinations are conducted simultaneously in all the venues for a particular examination across the country. Four examinations are conducted in a year as follows: March – Professional Qualifying Examination for Midwives (2nd Tuesday), May—Professional Qualifying Examination for General Nurses (1st Tuesday), September—Professional Qualifying Examination for Midwives (2nd Tuesday) and November—Professional Qualifying Examination for General and Post Basic Nurses (1st Tuesday) [10].

In spite of the general improvement in the use of CBT reported in recent years, certain challenges may be encountered. In a society where technology have become pervasive, individuals are expected to acquire new set of digital skills in order to efficiently navigate the new terrain. For example, in the use of CBT for nurses final qualifying examinations, candidates are expected to have maximum computer skills for optimum maximization of time. This implies that individuals with little or no ICT skills may find CBT examination difficult. Other challenges which candidates may encounter include combining creative digital skills needed for critical thinking, problem-solving digital skills and measuring digital skills during CBT exams.

CBTs have been reported to provide several benefits which included easy and fast upload of results [11], elimination of unnecessary noise during exams [12] reduction in cheating during examinations [13] and many others. Despite these advantages and how much CBT have infiltrated into the academic setting, there is a dearth of research in this area especially in nursing final qualifying examinations in Nigeria. The researchers have observed that notwithstanding the presumed gains of the use of CBT, some nursing undergraduates have reported poor user interface, insufficient laptops and power failure in the course of writing CBT. Sufficient research have not explored how candidates have fared in this very important examination since the introduction of CBT.

Okocha (2019) recommended that a computer-based pre-test should be given to the students occasionally to help sharpen the ICT skills needed for navigation of computers during examination [14]. For nursing training, the pre-professional examination for nurses and midwife usually presents an opportunity for a computer-based pre-test before the main examination. It has also been noted that in institutions with well-equipped computer laboratories, students reported positive perception of CBT and effective use of CBT due to adequate access to computers both for personal learning and for writing examinations [13]. To curb the challenge of test anxiety during CBT experienced by the students due to faulty development of computers, it was suggested that institutions should invest in frequent training of the ICT staff to improve knowledge and skills thereby promoting prompt fixing of faulty computers needed for CBT [15]. Also, institutions should inculcate the use of computer in day-to-day lectures to get the students more acquainted to ICT skill [3].

Naturally, candidates are likely to hold diverse perceptions on the use of CBT for NMCN examinations. The change from conventional exams to CBT may pose challenges for the student including ability to combine creative thinking skills with ICT skills for adequate performance in CBT examinations. Assessing issues related to quality factors that may influence performance and student perceptions regarding computer-based tests are therefore germane for providing a road-map necessary for improving CBT system performance as well as satisfaction among test takers [14]. Data on perception and challenges associated with CBT will help the students, administrators, training schools, NMCN and other researchers gain access to information needed for a better understanding of the difficulties encountered during CBT examinations and the strategies to curb the challenges. This will help to develop and implement more effective computer-based testing strategies that can enhance student performance and outcomes. However, there is no documented study in the study area that have attempted to assess candidates perceptions and strategies for the improvement of the NMCN final qualifying CBT examination since its introduction. This study was therefore designed to assess the perception of use and challenges towards computer-based test for final qualifying examination for nurses among student nurses in University of Nigeria Enugu, Nigeria.

Materials and methods

The study was a cross-sectional descriptive survey. The population consisted of all the 214 final year class of Nursing students in the Department of Nursing Sciences, University of Nigeria, Enugu Campus in 2023. All final year student who have not sat for any of the CBT examination were excluded from the study.This population was selected because they were among the first set that wrote the CBT Final Qualifying Examinations of the NMCN. The instrument of Data collection was a researcher-developed questionnaire with a reliability coefficient of 0.921. Ethical approval with certificate number NHREC/05/01/2008B-FWA00002456-1RB00002323 was obtained from the Health Research Ethics Committee of University of Nigeria Teaching Hospital, Ituku Ozalla, Enugu State. A participant information leaflet was attached to each questionnaire with contact details of the researchers made available to participants in the event of any queries or clarifications. Individual informed written consent was obtained from all the participants after assuring them of their rights to confidentiality, anonymity and privacy. Participation was strictly voluntary and participants were informed of freedom to decline or withdraw without fear of being affected adversely. No risk was foreseen from the study and collected data was handled in a confidential and protective manner with access only to the researchers and the statistician. Data was analyzed using both descriptive and inferential statistics. The descriptive statistics – frequency, percentage, mean and standard deviation were used to summarize the items of the questionnaire. For scaled items assessed with mean and standard deviation, mean (M) = 2.5 was used as the criterion for decision; item with mean above 2.5 was considered to be accepted by the respondents; item with mean 2.5 and below was considered rejected. An overall perception score was obtained for each respondent by scoring and summing each correct response of the perception items. The perception score was grouped as good (if score above 50%) and poor (if score 50% or below). Chi-Square test and independent samples t-test and paired samples t-test was used to test the hypothesis at 5% level of significance. These analyses were done with the aid of the Statistical Package for Social Sciences (SPSS) version 25 and Microsoft Excel.

Discussion

Perceptions of the CBT in the NMCM for final qualifying examination

The overall perception of respondents was high (2.88 ± 0.58), with majority having a positive perception towards the use of CBT (74.3%). This means that in every one hundred (100) students who have written basic nursing examination in University of Nigeria, Enugu Campus (UNEC) over seventy-four of them are at home with the CBT nature of the exam. It shows further that majority of Nursing Students in UNEC are computer literate and also shows that most UNEC Nursing Science students are ICT compliant and therefore perceive the use of Computer Based Test for the final qualifying exams ideal. This finding is not surprising because students who had opportunities of writing semester examinations in the university through the CBT model affording them the privilege of exposure and familiarization. This finding resonates with that of Alek (2020) who found that almost all the students were satisfied with the process of the CBT [12]. In another studyreported most of the students perceived CBT exams more efficient than pen-pencil test [14]. These findings support the impetus for introducing CBT into educational program which is improve the conduct of the process.

Challenges experienced in the use of CBT in the NMCM for final qualifying examination

Long story-like questions during CBT amidst limited time (74.3%) were among the challenges identified by the respondents. Such question may create tension further complicating the process. malfunctioning of computer system during CBT was another challenge. A malfunctioning of the computer systems will invariably affect the students’ performance in the CBT examinations. A study among secondary school students in Kaduna also agreed that time allocated for CBT examination is inadequate [16]. Organizers of CBT examination should ensure that every computer systems to be used for the exam are properly tested and confirmed working before using it to administer the examination. Poor ventilation and unkempt nature of the environment of the CBT centre keeps students worried and these make the CBT examinations seem difficult and worrisome according to our findings. These finding are in line with the findings of Okocha (2019) [14]. In contrast, Danladi and Audu Dodo (2019) revealed the challenges associated with use of CBT for JAMB included compromising of the integrity of the computerized examination, the problem of ICT infrastructure, poor internet network, inadequate computers and cybersecurity [11]. The differences as noted in these studies might be because the nature and scope of both exams differ greatly.

Strategies to curb challenges experienced in the CBT of the NMCM for final qualifying examination

The major ways noted by the respondents to curb challenges experienced in the use of CBT for final nursing qualifying exam in UNEC were provision of good CBT environment and improving ventilation in CBT centers, fixing any malfunctioning computer systems and removing dysfunctional computer systems prior to the final qualifying examination (3.32 ± 0.85). The above strategies if applied would improve in general the quality of the use of CBT for final nursing qualifying examination (3.32 ± 0.83). To ensure seamless CBT examinations, examiners should ensure that CBT centres should be well ventilated, very neat and computer systems are correctly spaced in line with the results from similar studies [11].

Association between the perception of the use of CBT for the final qualifying examination and sex

There was no significant association between perception of CBT and gender (p = 0.308). This is in line with Okocha (2019) [14]. Also it aligns with the findings at the university of Lagos where there was no significant gender difference in the views [17]. This may be because currently there is no discipline or skill including proficiency in computer use that is entirely specific to the sex of the individual.

Association between the challenges experienced during CBT at their final nursing qualifying examination and age

It was revealed that there was no significant association between the challenges experienced among the younger and older nursing students using CBT at their final qualifying examinations as nurses (p = 0.322). Nevertheless, those aged 20 years and below were associated more with high challenges in the use of CBT (53.8%) than other age groups. This research reveals that Nursing students within 21 and 25 years of age are more compliant to the use of CBT for final qualifying examination than students of other ages. Lack of significant association between challenges and age maybe because respondents belong to almost the same age group therefore their exposure and experiences with computers and its application may not vary greatly. This is in line with the findings in a study that correlated computer based assessment and students exam anxiety among students in public and private tertiary institutions in Anambra state, Nigeria though not during NMCN final qualifying CBT examination [13].

Implications of the study

Findings from this study have generated feedback for continuous and sustained improvement to the CBT format. Universities and other nursing institutions should integrate the use of CBT in administering other school exams other than the final qualifying exams as this would help the students get acquainted to the use of CBT in general. There is also a need to channel more funds into the provision of functional computers for more favourable CBT experiences in the future. Needs assessment and advocacy by relevant stakeholders is a step in the right direction.

Conclusion

The perception of nursing undergraduates on the use of CBT for final qualifying examinations was positive for majority. The perceived challenges experienced by nursing undergraduates were majorly problems of long story-like questions during CBT amidst limited time, malfunctioning of computer system of students during the examination, and poor ventilation of the CBT centres. Implementation of strategies such as provision of a good CBT environment and fixing of malfunctioning computer systems prior to examination (3.32 ± 0.85) were some of the strategies suggested for the improvement of the CBT for final qualifying examination for nursing students.

Strengths of the study

Candidate perception surveys as conducted in this study provides a good measure for evaluating the conduct of examinations. Furthermore studying the entire population provided data that can be generalized to the research setting.

Weaknesses of the study

As a descriptive study, the study did not determine causality. Thus the study focused on perception and strategies for curbing challenges encountered during NMCN CBT examination. The study was carried out in one university in South East Nigeria and, therefore, cannot be used for generalization to candidates from other universities in the region.

Direction for future research

Further researches should be focused on other university populations within and outside of south eastern Nigeria. Data from such studies are valuable for comparative analysis and more generalization. Subsequent researches should evaluate if there are differences in perception and challenges encountered during CBT examinations among basic and post basic candidates.

Remdesivir safety and clinical outcomes in pediatric COVID-19: a retrospective study
Authors: Leila Barati, Narges lashkarbolouk, Somayeh Ghorbani, Lobat Shahkar
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThis study evaluated clinical features of pediatric and adolescent patients with COVID-19 who received Remdesivir and assessed the adverse effects of Remdesivir in this population.MethodsIn a retrospective cross-sectional study, the documents of all patients under 18 years old were admitted due to severely respiratory distress and suspicious to COVID-19 in Taleghani hospital and according to Iranian COVID-19 treatment protocol received Remdesivir between February 2021 and September 2022, were studied.ResultsThe study included children aged 25 days to 17 years with a median age of 10 years and 50%being female. Fifteen patients were admitted to the ICU. 50% of ICU admissions were failure to thrive (FTT). BMI was significantly higher in patients over 10 years of age compared to others. Abnormal CT finding were observed in 90% of obese patients. Forty-one cases tested positive for PCR. The odds of PCR positivity were 2.87 times higher in patients over 10 years of age. Remdesivir was generally well tolerated, with no adverse events leading to drug discontinuation and no statistically significant difference in laboratory parameters before and after treatment. There were no significant hepatic or renal toxicity. Mild, transient elevations in liver transaminases (AST and ALT) were observed but did not require discontinuation of therapy. Transient bradycardia was not observed in our study.ConclusionRemdesivir appears to be a safe therapeutic option for pediatric COVID-19 patients, with minimal adverse effects and good tolerability. Nutritional status, particularly obesity and FTT, may influence disease severity. Further prospective studies are needed to better define Remdesivir’s role and its impact on viral clearance and clinical outcomes in children.Clinical trial numberNot applicable.

Introduction

Since the outbreak of COVID-19, initially identified as severe pneumonia in Wuhan, China, pediatric involvement has gradually been recorded [1, 2]. During the pandemic, the number of affected children was lower than that of adults; however, severe illness and mortality have also been documented in this group [3–5]. Studies have reported that 8–33% of critically ill children required ICU admission [4, 5] with a study from Madrid indicating that 10% of pediatric patients with confirmed COVID-19 needed intensive care [6].

Remdesivir, a new generation ribonucleoside analog with potent antiviral activity against coronaviruses, was prescribed in 4.5% of hospitalized children [4, 5], despite children not being initially included in clinical, its benefits were seen in these patients [7–10]. Remdesivir has been approved for emergency use in adults and has shown significant efficacy, although its effects in children remain unknown [1–14], it is recommended for children with severe COVID-19 disease [15] and is the only antiviral drug that has shown some efficacy in clinical trials [7]. Taleghani Hospital in Gorgan, which is a referral center for critically ill children, treated children with COVID-19 with Remdesivir according to the Iranian protocol. Given that information on critically ill patients with COVID-19 and the prescription of Remdesivir in the pediatric population is limited, this study examined the demographic information, laboratory signs, and imaging of children with COVID-19 treated with Remdesivir and side effects of this new drug in children.

Materials and methods

Study design and setting

In a retrospective cross-sectional study the documents of all patients under 18 years old were admitted due to severely respiratory distress and suspicious to COVID-19 in Taleghani hospital and according to Iranian COVID-19 treatment protocol received Remdesivir between 20.2.2021 and 20.09.2022 were studied. Therapeutic and educational pediatric Taleghani hospital in Gorgan is the only third level pediatrics hospital under the Golestan University of medical Sciences supervision in Golestan province and covers urban and rural referral patients and all socioeconomic and demographic characteristics.

Population and sample

Inclusion criteria were records of all patients referred to the Taleghani Hospital because of respiratory distress and based on clinical features, suspicious to COVID-19 and received Remdesivir according to Iranian COVID-19 treatment protocol; 200 mg on the first day and 100 mg daily for children weighing 40 kg and above, and 5 mg per kg on the first day and 2.5 mg per kg for children weighing less than 40 kg. Each dose was diluted in 0.9% normal saline and infused slowly over approximately 60 min. Exclusion criteria were cases with incomplete data by more than 10% missing data in demographic information and other causes of respiratory distress like cardiac diseases, metabolic disorders and immunocompromised patients. Diagnosis of respiratory distress and suspicious of COVID-19 was based on pediatrics pulmonologist diagnosis.

Data collection

Information was collected by studying the documents of patients. For ensuring the consistency and accuracy of data, it was collected by pediatrician who was the author of the survey. All of the data were coded and entered in to SPSS version 24 software.

Variables and definitions

Suspected COVID-19 cases were defined as patients presenting with clinical features of respiratory distress with negative PCR results. Confirmed COVID-19 cases were defined as patients presenting with clinical features of respiratory distress with positive PCR results. Demographic information: Age (under 10 years, 10–17 Years old), Gender (Girl, Boy), BMI ( less than 3th (FTT)), 3-84th (normal range), 85-94th (overweight), upper than 95th (Obese)), Admission ward (ICU, General), Laboratory data: Reverse Transcription polymerase chain reaction (RT- PCR) ( positive, negative), C-reactive protein (CRP) (positive, negative), Erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR) (mm/hr), Anemia ( Hemoglobin (Hb) (g/dl) under 11), Leukopenia (white blood cell (WBC) (µl) under 4500), Thrombocytopenia (Platelet (µl) under 150000).

Statistical analysis

The data was statistically analyzed with SPSS software version 24 (SPSS Inc, Chicago, IL.). Mean, frequency and percentage were used to describe the data. Chi-square was used to compare the analysis of categorical groups. Kolmogorov-Smirnov test was used for assess the normality of variables, and Independent t-test was used for quantitative data and to compare the Mean levels in two groups. The significance level in this study was considered to be less than 0.05.

Ethical considerations

This study was the result of a research project approved by the Research Council and the Regional Ethics Committee of Golestan University of Medical Sciences (GOUMS) with the code of ethics IR.GOUMS.REC.1403.060.

Results

General demographics

In this study, 76 patients under the age of 18 years old with Covid19 who were treated with Remdesivir were included in the study. Seven cases (9.2%) were under 1 year, 16 cases (21.1%) were 1–5 years, 9 cases (11.8%) were 6–9 years, 34 cases (44.7%) were 10–15 years, and 10 cases (13.2%) were 16–17 years old. The youngest age was 25 days and the oldest was 17 years. The mean age was 9.3 ± 5.38 years. The median age of the total was 10 [4–13]. The median age of girls was 11 [6–13] and boys was 9.5 [3–15]. Thirty-eight cases (50%) were girls and 38 cases (50%) were boys.

BMI details

Fourteen (21.9%) patients had a BMI above 95th, which was considered obese. Three (4.7%) had a BMI between 85-94th, which was considered overweight, and all were over 10 years of age. Sixteen (25%) were below the 3th, and were considered FTT. Laboratory finding

RT PCR

In this study, 28 (40.6%) cases were reported as PCR negative and 41 (59.4%) as PCR positive. 55.2% of cases were PCR negative under 10 years of age compared to 30% at the age of 10 years and above. There was a statistically significant difference between age group and PCR positive (p = 0.036) and patients over 10 years old were 2.87 times higher than those under 10 years of age had PCR positive with Odds ratio: 2.87(1.060–7.779). 66% of patients admitted to the ICU and 60% of patients referred with hypoxia had PCR negative. There were three expired patients (60%) with PCR negative. Table 1 showed that there was not any statically significance between PCR and BMI (p = 0.230).

CRP

Twenty-seven cases (38%) were reported to be negative for CRP. There was no statistically significant difference between CRP and BMI (p = 0.158) (Table 1).

One case (1.3%) had blood sugar below 60 and two cases had blood sugar above 200, and the others were in the normal range. There was not statistically significant difference in blood glucose before and after treatment (p = 0.225) (Table 2).

Twelve cases (15.8%) had a WBC count below 4500 and 30 cases (40.5%) had a WBC count above 10,000 and 43.2% were in the range of 4500–10,000. Vitamin D was checked in 11 patients, of which four cases were below 10 (36.4%) and 10 cases (90.9%) were below 30.

Liver transaminase

In this study, no statically significant difference was observed in AST (Aspartate aminotransferase), ALT (Alanine Aminotransferase), ALP (Alkaline phosphatase) before and after treatment with Remdesivir, and all patients had normal baseline tests (Table 2).

Table 2 demonstrated that there was not statically difference between Blood Urea Nitrogen (BUN) and Creatinine (Cr) before and after treatment with Remdesivir.

Fifteen patients (19.7%) were admitted to the ICU. Table 1 showed that 50% of patients admitted to ICU were FTT.

Imaging finding

Discussion

In this retrospective study involving 76 pediatric patients with suspected or confirmed COVID-19, Remdesivir was generally well tolerated, with no adverse events leading to drug discontinuation. No significant laboratory abnormalities were detected after treatment, and both genders across all pediatric age groups, including neonates, were represented.

Obesity and FTT were notable risk factors in this study, with 26.6% of patients being overweight or obese (all aged over 10 years) and 25% experiencing FTT which accounting for 50% of ICU admissions. In the Ye P study, obese and underweight patients with Covid-19 were 6.4 and 5.96 times more likely to have acute lung injury than those with normal BMI [16]. Alongside with this study, some studies mentioned that adolescent range age and obese patients experienced more severe acute illness [5, 17, 18]. This observation aligns with previous reports suggesting that malnutrition, whether under- or overweight, may exacerbate COVID-19 severity in children, likely due to chronic inflammation and immune dysfunction [5, 17, 19].

Regarding diagnostic testing, approximately 40.6% of our patients had a negative RT-PCR result at admission. Most of patients admitted to the ICU and 60% of patients referred with hypoxia had PCR negative. There were three expired patients (60%) with PCR negative. Notably, children over 10 years of age had a 2.87 times higher chance of testing positive. It is important to highlight that negative PCR results do not rule out COVID-19 infection, potentially due to improper sampling techniques or the limitations of nasopharyngeal swabbing. This is consistent with findings from studies that showed positive detection rates ranging between 1.8% and 70% depending on sampling sites [20–26]. In cases of negative nasopharyngeal swabs, stool PCR testing could be considered, given its similar sensitivity and the prolonged viral shedding observed in feces [27] and or PCR acquisition techniques in young children should be reviewed.

Inflammatory markers (CRP and ESR) in a few patients were slightly elevated, and most patients had normal WBC counts, consistent with reports from previous studies (range15-63%) [16, 19–23, 28–32]. Leukopenia was observed in 15.8% of cases, comparable to rates reported by other studies (19–23%) [22, 29, 31] and leukocytosis was noted in 40.5% of patients, higher than previously reported figures (13%-23%) [22, 31]. However, there was no significant correlation between elevated CRP or ESR levels and clinical severity markers such as ICU admission, hypoxia, or abnormal chest CT findings. Similarly, leukocyte parameters (leukopenia and leukocytosis) did not reliably predict disease severity, contrasting with adult patterns [30].

Chest CT was performed in half of the patients, with abnormalities found in 85% of cases. Obesity was significantly associated with abnormal CT findings, reinforcing the link between BMI and disease severity. As reported in previous studies, CT abnormalities may be absent in 24%-46% of pediatric COVID-19 cases [4, 29–32], Given concerns about radiation exposure, CT imaging should be judiciously reserved for select pediatric cases based on clinical and radiographic criteria [33–36].

Regarding the safety of Remdesivir, our study found no significant hepatic or renal toxicity. Mild, transient elevations in liver transaminases (AST and ALT) were observed, consistent with previous reports (18%-32%) [18, 28, 37–40]; but did not require discontinuation of therapy. No significant changes in serum creatinine were reported, aligning with prior studies where renal toxicity was rare [18, 28, 39–41]. Additionally, transient bradycardia was noted in some studies (6%-20%) [18, 38, 42], though not observed in our study.

Overall, Remdesivir appeared to be safe in the pediatric population, with adverse events being mild to moderate and self-limiting [18, 37–42]. Based on these findings, baseline liver function tests and renal function assessments are recommended before initiation of Remdesivir, with subsequent monitoring, particularly in patients with pre-existing comorbidities or severe disease. However, due to the retrospective nature of the study, the absence of a control group, and the lack of follow-up data to evaluate viral clearance, definitive conclusions about Remdesivir’s efficacy in pediatric COVID-19 patients could not be drawn. Future prospective studies focusing on the pharmacokinetics, pharmacodynamics, and virologic outcomes of Remdesivir in children are needed.

Conclusion

In conclusion, Remdesivir appears to be a safe therapeutic option for pediatric COVID-19 patients, with minimal adverse effects and good tolerability. Nutritional status, particularly obesity and FTT, may influence disease severity especially in adolescent range ages, likely due to chronic inflammation and immune dysfunction. Accurate diagnostic sampling and cautious use of imaging modalities are essential components of pediatric COVID-19 management. Further prospective studies are needed to better define Remdesivir’s role and its impact on viral clearance and clinical outcomes in children.

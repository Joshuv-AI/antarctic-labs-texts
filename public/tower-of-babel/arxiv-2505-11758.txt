Generalizable Vision-Language Few‑Shot Adaptation with Predictive Prompts and Negative Learning
Sriram Mandalika

Abstract
Few-shot adaptation remains a core challenge for vision-language models (VLMs), especially under limited supervision and noisy support samples. We propose PromptFuseNL , a unified framework that enhances few-shot generalization by combining predictive prompt tuning with dual-branch positive and negative learning. The method refines class prototypes through task-conditioned residuals, multi-stage cross-modal coordination, and semantic hard negative mining. To address label noise, we introduce an unsupervised instance reweighting strategy that downweights unreliable support examples without requiring additional labels or structural changes. PromptFuseNL fuses visual and textual cues through lightweight modules for efficient and discriminative prediction. Evaluated across 15 benchmarks, it consistently surpasses existing prompt- and adapter-based methods in all shot settings while remaining highly efficient, achieving up to 300 × \times faster training and 1000 × \times lower FLOPs compared to full prompt tuning, achieving a new state-of-the-art for robust and scalable few-shot vision-language adaptation.

Introduction

In many real-world settings, such as medical diagnosis, remote sensing, or wildlife monitoring, deep models are expected to learn from just a handful of labelled examples. While deep learning has shown remarkable success in visual recognition
[
Redmon et al.(2015)Redmon, Divvala, Girshick, and Farhadi
,
Russakovsky et al.(2014)Russakovsky, Deng, Su, Krause, Satheesh, Ma, Huang, Karpathy, Khosla, Bernstein, Berg, and Fei-Fei
]
, these gains largely rely on the availability of large-scale labelled datasets
[
Deng et al.(2009)Deng, Dong, Socher, Li, Li, and Fei-Fei
,
Lin et al.(2014)Lin, Maire, Belongie, Hays, Perona, Ramanan, Dollár, and Zitnick
]
, which are difficult or impractical to obtain in specialized domains. This makes few-shot learning a critical and unsolved challenge for deploying visual models in-the-wild.

Vision-language models (VLMs) such as CLIP
[
Radford et al.(2021)Radford, Kim, Hallacy, Ramesh, Goh, Agarwal, Sastry, Askell, Mishkin, Clark, Krueger, and Sutskever
]
and ALIGN
[
Jia et al.(2021)Jia, Yang, Xia, Chen, Parekh, Pham, Le, Sung, Li, and Duerig
]
, pretrained on web-scale image-text pairs, offer strong zero-shot capabilities
[
Li et al.(2021)Li, Liang, Zhao, Cui, Ouyang, Shao, Yu, and Yan
,
Radford et al.(2021)Radford, Kim, Hallacy, Ramesh, Goh, Agarwal, Sastry, Askell, Mishkin, Clark, Krueger, and Sutskever
,
Yu et al.(2022a)Yu, Wang, Vasudevan, Yeung, Seyedhosseini, and Wu
,
Zhai et al.(2021)Zhai, Wang, Mustafa, Steiner, Keysers, Kolesnikov, and Beyer
]
. These models provide a powerful foundation for few-shot transfer. However, the current few-shot adaptation strategies fall into two broad categories: adapter-based fine-tuning
[
Gao et al.(2021)Gao, Geng, Zhang, Ma, Fang, Zhang, Li, and Qiao
,
Li et al.(2023)Li, Lian, Lu, Bai, Chen, and Wang
,
Yu et al.(2022b)Yu, Lu, Jin, Chen, and Wang
,
Zhang et al.(2022)Zhang, Wei, Fang, Gao, Li, Dai, Qiao, and Li
]
and prompt-based tuning
[
Zhang et al.(2024c)Zhang, Zhang, Yu, Tang, and He
,
Zhou et al.(2022)Zhou, Yang, Loy, and Liu
,
Zhou et al.(2021)Zhou, Yang, Loy, and Liu
,
Tian et al.(2023)Tian, Zou, Yang, and Zhang
,
Shu et al.(2022)Shu, Nie, Huang, Yu, Goldstein, Anandkumar, and Xiao
]
. Both approaches struggle under low supervision, especially with fine-grained classes, due to their over-reliance on fixed prompts or shallow adaptation modules that cannot adequately encode task-specific variation.

To address this, we propose
PromptFuseNL
, a unified framework for few-shot adaptation that learns both
“what is”
and
“what is not”
a class. Inspired by negative learning principles
[
Sun et al.(2022)Sun, Hu, and Saenko
,
Yu et al.(2017)Yu, Liu, Gong, and Tao
,
Ishida et al.(2017)Ishida, Niu, Hu, and Sugiyama
]
, our method enhances class separation by introducing discriminative negative features in both visual and textual modalities. PromptFuseNL combines task-conditioned residual routing, predictive prompt generation, hard negative mining, and multi-stage cross-modal attention to yield robust, semantically aligned prototypes. These mechanisms improve adaptation without requiring any updates to the pretrained VLM backbone.

Importantly, few-shot models often assume that support examples are clean and representative, a strong assumption that rarely holds in practice. Noisy labels or outlier support samples can easily dominate prototypes and derail adaptation under low supervision. To tackle this, PromptFuseNL incorporates a lightweight, unsupervised instance reweighting module that assigns confidence scores to support samples based on their similarity to the class prototype and episodic memory interpolations. This improves stability under label corruption (e.g., up to 50% noise) and boosts performance without any architectural changes. Notably, this reweighting is plug-and-play and can improve robustness in other adapter-based methods like Tip-Adapter-F
[
Zhang et al.(2022)Zhang, Wei, Fang, Gao, Li, Dai, Qiao, and Li
]
.

We conduct comprehensive experiments across 15 datasets, spanning general, fine-grained, and domain-shifted tasks, to evaluate both few-shot learning and distributional robustness. Our results show that PromptFuseNL consistently outperforms prior work, achieving state-of-the-art accuracy with strong resilience to label noise and domain shift—while remaining computationally efficient.
Our key contributions are as follows:

•

We propose PromptFuseNL, a unified vision–language few-shot framework that learns both positive and negative class signals via prompt-guided residual adaptation and dual-branch alignment.

•

We introduce a lightweight instance reweighting module that improves robustness under label noise and can be integrated into existing adapter frameworks without architectural changes.

•

We demonstrate that PromptFuseNL achieves state-of-the-art performance across 15 diverse benchmarks, with superior generalization under noise and distribution shifts.

Related Works

Efficient Adaptation for VLMs.
Prompt-based methods adapt frozen text encoders by learning task-specific prompts. CoOp
[
Zhou et al.(2021)Zhou, Yang, Loy, and Liu
]
learns fixed prompts via gradient descent, while CoCoOp
[
Zhou et al.(2022)Zhou, Yang, Loy, and Liu
]
generates instance-aware prompts conditioned on image features. ProGrad
[
Zhu et al.(2023)Zhu, Niu, Han, and etc.
]
mitigates overfitting through gradient projection, and TPT
[
Shu et al.(2022)Shu, Nie, Huang, Yu, Goldstein, Anandkumar, and Xiao
]
enhances robustness via inference-time prompt refinement. In parallel, adapter-style methods retain frozen backbones and inject lightweight modules into the vision stream. CLIP-Adapter
[
Gao et al.(2021)Gao, Geng, Zhang, Ma, Fang, Zhang, Li, and Qiao
]
uses residual adapters for supervised tuning, while Tip-Adapter-F
[
Zhang et al.(2022)Zhang, Wei, Fang, Gao, Li, Dai, Qiao, and Li
]
constructs a non-parametric cache for training-free alignment. Graph-based extensions like Dual-Knowledge Graph tuning
[
Anonymous(2023)
]
exploit semantic relations between classes. TaskRes
[
Yu et al.(2022b)Yu, Lu, Jin, Chen, and Wang
]
introduces task-specific residual routing, and Sus-X
[
Udandarao and etc.(2023)
]
achieves plug-and-play adaptation with zero training cost. Our work builds on these efficient adaptation strategies by combining predictive prompts, cross-modal routing, and reweighting to improve robustness under limited supervision and noisy labels.

Few-Shot Learning.
Few-shot learning (FSL) aims to recognize unseen classes from limited labeled examples. Meta-learning approaches such as Matching Networks
[
Vinyals et al.(2016)Vinyals, Blundell, Lillicrap, and Wierstra
]
, Prototypical Networks
[
Snell et al.(2017)Snell, Swersky, and Zemel
]
, and MAML
[
Finn et al.(2017)Finn, Abbeel, and Levine
]
simulate
N
-way
K
-shot tasks to learn transferable inductive biases. Meta-Baseline
[
Chen et al.(2021)Chen, Liu, Xu, Darrell, and Wang
]
and Jiang et al.
[
Jiang et al.(2024)Jiang, Liu, He, and He
]
enhance prototype quality via base-class knowledge. DeepEMD
[
Zhang et al.(2020)Zhang, Cai, Lin, and Shen
]
uses optimal transport for metric learning, while transductive models like UNEM
[
Smith et al.(2022)Smith, Jones, and Lee
]
refine class prototypes with unlabeled queries. Other innovations include attentive regularization
[
Li et al.(2024)Li, Feng, and Yan
]
, graph-based propagation
[
Liu et al.(2021)Liu, Hu, Wang, and Chan
]
, and domain-adaptive methods
[
Gupta et al.(2024)Gupta, Kumar, and Singh
,
Kumar et al.(2025)Kumar, Verma, and Roy
,
Patel et al.(2024)Patel, Das, and Sen
]
. In vision-language FSL, CoOp
[
Zhou et al.(2021)Zhou, Yang, Loy, and Liu
]
and CoCoOp
[
Zhou et al.(2022)Zhou, Yang, Loy, and Liu
]
learn task-specific prompts, Tip-Adapter-F
[
Zhang et al.(2022)Zhang, Wei, Fang, Gao, Li, Dai, Qiao, and Li
]
enables training-free adaptation via feature caching, and SimNL
[
Zhang et al.(2024b)Zhang, Stepputtis, Sycara, and Xie
]
extends this with dual-branch modeling of positive and negative class prototypes.

Negative Learning.
Negative learning aims to improve class separation by explicitly modeling “what a class is not.” Early works use complementary labels to train on classes an instance does not belong to
[
Ishida et al.(2017)Ishida, Niu, Hu, and Sugiyama
]
, and Yu et al.
[
Yu et al.(2017)Yu, Liu, Gong, and Tao
]
show that negative prototypes reduce spurious correlations in prototype-based classifiers. DualCoOp
[
Sun et al.(2022)Sun, Hu, and Saenko
]
extends this idea to prompt tuning by learning both positive and negative prompts to suppress confusing classes. SimNL
[
Zhang et al.(2024a)Zhang, Stepputtis, Sycara, and Xie
]
builds on this by mining task-specific negatives from CLIP similarity and aligning dual visual-textual branches. Recent advances explore more expressive negative generation: DeltaAug
[
Anonymous(2024b)
]
synthesizes hard negatives via embedding arithmetic, DualAdapter
[
Anonymous(2024c)
]
injects positive-negative adapter paths, and SCHaNe
[
Anonymous(2024e)
]
applies supervised contrastive learning with hard negatives. NAS
[
Wang et al.(2024)Wang, Xie, and Stepputtis
]
introduces token-level visual negatives during pretraining, NtUA
[
Anonymous(2024a)
]
uses hard negative mining for noise robustness in few-shot ImageNet, and MDPI
[
Anonymous(2024d)
]
integrates hard negatives into cross-modal contrastive training. Together, these works highlight the growing role of negative cues in improving generalization and robustness.

3
Methodology

3.1
Problem Formulation

We consider the standard
N
‑way
K
‑shot classification task in a vision–language setting. Each episode comprises a support set

\mathcal{S}=\{(x_{i},y_{i})\}_{i=1}^{N\times K}

and a query set

\mathcal{Q}=\{(x_{j},y_{j})\}_{j=1}^{N\times Q},

sampled from
N
novel classes. Images
x
are mapped to
d
‑dimensional features
v=f_{v}(x)
by CLIP’s frozen visual encoder, while class names
c
yield text embeddings
t=f_{t}(c)
via its frozen text encoder. Our goal is to learn lightweight adaptation modules, without updating
f_{v}
or
f_{t}
that refine these base embeddings into discriminative class prototypes and hard negatives. Queries are then classified by cosine similarity to the adapted prototypes. We optimize the module parameters to minimize a unified loss over
\mathcal{Q}
, thereby teaching the model to generalize from the small support set to unseen queries.

3.2
Core Learning Objectives

We incorporate positive and negative learning objectives to enable discriminative adaptation in the few-shot setting. These components operate at the class level, encouraging the model to align queries with relevant class prototypes while actively repelling them from semantically similar but incorrect classes. This dual objective improves class separation and mitigates overfitting to spurious correlations in low-data regimes.

3.2.1
Positive Prototype Alignment

Given a query embedding
q\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we construct a fused prototype
z_{c}^{+}
for each class
c
by combining base and adapted features from both modalities:

z_{c}^{+}=\lambda\,(t_{c}^{+}+v_{c}^{+})+(1-\lambda)\,(t_{c}+v_{c}),

where
t_{c}
and
v_{c}
are the frozen CLIP text and visual embeddings for class
c
, while
t_{c}^{+}
and
v_{c}^{+}
are their adapted counterparts incorporating prompt and residual enhancements. The hyperparameter
\lambda\in[0,1]
controls the balance between base and adapted features. To align the query with its correct class, we minimize the temperature-scaled cross-entropy loss over cosine similarities:

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{pos}}=-\log\frac{\exp(\cos(q,z_{y}^{+})/\tau)}{\sum_{j=1}^{N}\exp(\cos(q,z_{j}^{+})/\tau)},

where
\tau
is the softmax temperature and
y
is the ground-truth class label for
q
. This formulation encourages high similarity with the target prototype while maintaining separation from other classes.

3.2.2
Negative Learning and Hard Negative Mining

To reinforce decision boundaries, we introduce a negative learning branch that explicitly penalizes similarity to closely related, non-support classes. First, we identify a set of hard negatives
\mathcal{N}
by ranking all base prototypes
z_{j}
(for
j\notin\mathcal{S}
) by their cosine similarity to the mean support feature
\bar{s}
:

\mathcal{N}=\mathrm{TopK}_{k}\left\{j\mid j\notin\mathcal{S},\ \cos(\bar{s},z_{j})\right\}.

For each hard negative
n\in\mathcal{N}
, we form an adapted prototype
z_{n}^{-}
and enforce a hinge margin:

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{neg}}=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{N}|}\sum_{n\in\mathcal{N}}\max\left(0,\,\tau-\cos(q,z_{n}^{-})\right),

which encourages the similarity between the query and hard negatives to remain below a specified margin
\tau
. This constraint pushes the query embedding away from confusing classes, improving fine-grained discrimination under limited supervision.

3.2.3
Contrast with Instance-Level Contrastive Learning

Unlike instance-level approaches such as SimCLR
[
Chen et al.(2020)Chen, Kornblith, Norouzi, and Hinton
]
and MoCo
[
He et al.(2020)He, Fan, Wu, Xie, and Girshick
]
, which rely on large negative sets, our method performs class-level contrastive alignment using semantically hard negatives mined from support–query interactions. This enables structured prototype matching guided by task-specific, cross-modal cues. Moreover, our loss integrates prompt-conditioned residuals for more effective few-shot adaptation.

3.3
Modality-Specific Branch Designs

3.3.1
Textual Branch

Prompt Prediction & Style Conditioning.

To adapt frozen CLIP text embeddings to downstream tasks, we introduce a learnable prompt prediction module that enhances class semantics through soft style conditioning. Let
t_{c}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
denote the frozen CLIP text embedding for class
c
. We define a set of
S
learnable style vectors
\{s_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\}_{i=1}^{S}
and a two-layer MLP
f_{\phi}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{S}
that predicts the relevance of each style vector given
t_{c}
.

We compute unnormalized attention logits over the style bank as:

\tilde{\alpha}_{c}=f_{\phi}(t_{c}),

and normalize them using softmax:

\alpha_{c,i}=\frac{\exp(\tilde{\alpha}_{c,i})}{\sum_{j=1}^{S}\exp(\tilde{\alpha}_{c,j})},\quad i=1,\dots,S.

The resulting prompt token
p_{c}
is computed as a convex combination of style vectors:

p_{c}=\sum_{i=1}^{S}\alpha_{c,i}\cdot s_{i}.

Finally, the prompt-enhanced text prototype is formed by a residual addition:

t_{c}^{\prime}=t_{c}+p_{c}.

This soft prompt prediction strategy allows PromptFuseNL to dynamically condition each class embedding on relevant semantic styles, improving the flexibility and generalization of the textual branch during few-shot adaptation without updating the backbone.

Cross‑modal Coordination.

To further enhance the textual prototypes with grounded visual context, we apply a cross-modal attention mechanism over the support set’s visual embeddings. Let
V\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{S}|\times d}
represent the matrix of CLIP-encoded support features. We compute a contextually refined text prototype
\tilde{t}_{c}
using a cross-attention module where the prompt-enhanced embedding
t_{c}^{\prime}
serves as the query:

\tilde{t}_{c}=\mathrm{CrossAttn}(q=t_{c}^{\prime},\,k=V,\,v=V).

This operation allows the textual representation to selectively attend to informative visual cues from the support set, resulting in a more discriminative prototype that aligns both semantically and visually. Multiple layers of cross-attention can be stacked to progressively refine
\tilde{t}_{c}
, facilitating deeper grounding in visual evidence.

3.3.2
Visual Branch

The visual branch parallels the textual pathway by forming class prototypes from support examples, refined via residuals and projection. For each class
c
, define
\mathcal{S}_{c}=\{i\mid(x_{i},y_{i})\in\mathcal{S},\ y_{i}=c\}
as the support indices, with
x_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
denoting CLIP features. Each example is weighted by relevance
w_{i}
(see Section
3.4
), and the prototype is:

v_{c}=\frac{1}{\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}_{c}}w_{i}}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}_{c}}w_{i}\cdot x_{i}.

We enhance it using a learnable residual token
r_{c}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
:

v_{c}^{\prime}=v_{c}+r_{c},\quad\hat{v}_{c}=\mathrm{LayerNorm}(v_{c}^{\prime}),\quad\tilde{v}_{c}=W_{v}\cdot\hat{v}_{c},

where
W_{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
is the projection matrix. The resulting
\tilde{v}_{c}
is later fused with its textual counterpart to compute similarity with queries across modalities.

3.4
Robustness Mechanism

3.4.1
Instance Reweighting for Noise-Robust Prototype Refinement

To mitigate the impact of noisy or unrepresentative support samples, we apply an unsupervised reweighting strategy that adjusts each instance’s contribution based on contextual alignment.

For a support feature
x_{i}
of class
y_{i}
, we define its weight
w_{i}\in[0,1]
as:

w_{i}=\frac{1}{2}\left[\cos(x_{i},\bar{s})+\cos(x_{i},z_{y_{i}}^{+})\right],

where
\bar{s}
is the mean support embedding and
z_{y_{i}}^{+}
is the adapted class prototype. This promotes higher influence for representative samples and naturally downweights outliers, improving prototype stability under label noise and distribution shifts.

3.5
Training & Optimization

Unified Loss Formulation.

We train PromptFuseNL using a composite loss:

\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{pos}}+\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{neg}}+\gamma\|\theta_{\mathrm{attn}}\|_{2}^{2},

where
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{pos}}
and
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{neg}}
are defined in Section
3.2
, and
\theta_{\mathrm{attn}}
includes parameters from the prompt attention module. The final term regularizes prompt weights to prevent overfitting. Optimization is performed using AdamW with modality-specific learning rates.

Inference Strategy.

At inference, we compute cosine similarity between the query feature
q
and each class’s adapted visual and textual prototypes,
\tilde{v}_{c}
and
\tilde{t}_{c}
. The final score is:

s_{c}=\lambda\cdot\cos(q,\tilde{v}_{c})+(1-\lambda)\cdot\cos(q,\tilde{t}_{c}),\quad\hat{y}=\arg\max_{c}s_{c},

where
\lambda\in[0,1]
balances the two modalities. Temperature scaling is applied to logits to improve calibration.

6
Conclusion and Future Work

We introduced PromptFuseNL, a unified vision-language few-shot adaptation framework that integrates task-conditioned residuals, dual-branch contrastive learning, predictive prompt tuning, and cross-modal coordination to achieve robust and efficient generalization from limited supervision. Through multi-branch fusion and unsupervised instance reweighting, our method consistently outperforms prior baselines across 15 diverse benchmarks, demonstrating strong performance under both distribution shift and label noise, while preserving a lightweight design. These results establish PromptFuseNL as a scalable and practical solution for real-world few-shot scenarios. In future work, we plan to extend this framework to continual learning, open-vocabulary tasks, and adaptive multi-modal settings, enabling broader applicability in dynamic and low-resource environments.

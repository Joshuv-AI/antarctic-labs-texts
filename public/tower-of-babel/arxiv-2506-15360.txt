Stochastic Diagonal Estimation Based on Matrix Quadratic Form Oracles
Haishan Ye, Xiangyu Chang

Abstract
We study the problem of estimating the diagonal of an implicitly given matrix 𝑨 \bm{A} . For such a matrix we have access to an oracle that allows us to evaluate the matrix quadratic form 𝒖 ⊤ ​ 𝑨 ​ 𝒖 \bm{u}^{\top}\bm{A}\bm{u} . Based on this query oracle, we propose a stochastic diagonal estimation method with random variable 𝒖 \bm{u} drawn from the standard Gaussian distribution. We provide the element-wise and norm-wise sample complexities of the proposed method. Our numerical experiments on different types and dimensions matrices demonstrate the effectiveness of our method and validate the tightness of theoretical results.

Introduction

Estimating the diagonal entries of a matrix is important in many areas of science and engineering.
Originally, the diagonal estimation is used in electronic structure calculations
(
Bekas et al., 2007
;
Goedecker & Teter, 1995
;
Goedecker, 1999
)
.
Because the diagonal preconditioners can accelerate the convergence of iterative optimization algorithms
(
Wathen, 2015
)
, the diagonal estimator is applied in accelerating training machine learning models
(
Yao et al., 2021
;
Liu et al., 2023
)
.
The diagonal estimation is also used in network science
(
Constantine & Diaz, 2017
;
Kucherenko & Iooss, 2015
)
.

Because of its importance, the diagonal estimation has attracted much research attention in the past years
(
Hallman et al., 2023
;
Baston & Nakatsukasa, 2022
;
Bekas et al., 2007
;
Epperly et al., 2024
)
.
These diagonal estimation algorithms construct the following matrix

\widehat{\bm{A}}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\bm{A}\bm{w}_{i}\bm{w}_{i}^{\top},

(1.1)

where
\bm{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
and
\bm{w}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
are independent random vectors,
and use the diagonal entries of
\widehat{\bm{A}}
as the diagonal estimation of
\bm{A}
.
Thus, this kind of diagonal estimation is mainly based on the matrix-vector product.

In this paper, we will study stochastic diagonal estimation by only accessing the quadratic form of a matrix
\bm{u}^{\top}\bm{A}\bm{u}
and we will construct the following estimation:

\bm{g}=\frac{1}{2N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left[\bm{u}^{(j)}\right]^{\top}\bm{A}\bm{u}^{(j)}\cdot\Big([\bm{u}^{(j)}]^{2}-\bm{1}_{d}\Big),

(1.2)

where
\bm{u}^{(j)}
’s are independent standard Gaussian vectors,
\mathbf{1}_{d}
is a
d
dimensional all one vector, and
[\bm{u}]^{2}
denotes the element-wise square.
Compared with the diagonal estimation based on Eq. (
1.1
) which needs the matrix-vector product, Eq. (
1.2
) can only access the quadratic form of a matrix
\bm{u}^{\top}\bm{A}\bm{u}
.

Our study is motivated by the zeroth-order optimization for high-dimensional problems.
Recently, post-training large language models with first-order algorithms suffers from the giant memory costs.
Thus the memory-efficient zeroth-order algorithms attract much research attention because they only need forward passes and achieve the memory efficiency
(
Malladi et al., 2023
;
Zhao et al., 2025
;
Zhang et al., 2024
;
Dery et al., 2024
;
Chen et al., 2025
)
.
Similar to the first-order algorithms, one can also use the Hessian diagonal as the preconditioner to accelerate the convergence of zeroth-order algorithms
(
Zhao et al., 2025
)
.
Because zeroth-order algorithms can only access function values, the diagonal estimation in Eq. (
1.1
) which requires Hessian-vector product can
not
be used to estimate the Hessian diagonals.
Instead, our estimation in Eq. (
1.2
) only needs the quadratic form
\bm{u}^{\top}\bm{A}\bm{u}
with
\bm{A}
being the Hessian
\nabla^{2}f(\bm{x})
which can be approximately computed by three function value queries as follows:

\bm{u}^{\top}\bm{A}\bm{u}\approx\frac{f(\bm{x}+\alpha\bm{u})+f(\bm{x}-\alpha\bm{u})-2f(\bm{x})}{\alpha^{2}},

(1.3)

where
0<\alpha
is a constant of a small value. Please refer to Proposition
1
to get a detailed description.

1.1
Literature Review

To our knowledge, Monte Carlo diagonal estimators based on matrix-vector product were first
studied by
Bekas et al., 2007
.
This kind of diagonal estimation is further explored in
(
Hallman et al., 2023
;
Baston & Nakatsukasa, 2022
;
Epperly et al., 2024
)
.
In contrast, our work studies the diagonal estimation which only accesses the quadratic form of a matrix instead of accessing the matrix-vector product.

Another important research topic closely related to our study is the Hessian approximation by zeroth-order oracles and
several works have been proposed
(
Ye, 2023
;
Lyu & Tsang, 2021
;
Lattimore & György, 2023
)
.
More Hessian matrix estimation methods by function values can be found in Chapter 6 of
Prashanth et al., 2025
.
Our diagonal estimation is a special case of the Hessian approximation when matrix
\bm{A}
is the Hessian.
However, the sample complexities of Hessian approximation are rarely analyzed
(
Lyu & Tsang, 2021
;
Lattimore & György, 2023
;
Prashanth et al., 2025
)
.
Though
Ye, 2023
provides a sample complexity of Hessian approximation, the result of
Ye, 2023
can
not
derive the sample complexities in this work and our work achieves much tighter sample complexities.

1.2
Contributions

The novel features of our contributions are the following:

1.

We propose a stochastic diagonal estimation method that only queries matrix quadratic form oracles.
Our method does
not
require the matrix to be symmetric nor positive (semi)-definite.

2.

We provide the sample complexity analysis of our method to achieve the target precision.
Our element-wise bound (Theorem
1
) shows that the sample complexity mainly depends on the square of the trace and the square of the “Frobenius” norm of
\bm{A}+\bm{A}^{\top}
.
Thus, to achieve the same relative error, diagonal entries with small absolute values require large sample sizes.
Our numerical experiments (Section
4
) confirms this in different types and different dimension matrices.

3.

We provide the sample complexity of norm-wise bound (Theorem
2
) and show that it linearly depends the dimension
d
.
The trace of the matrix and off-diagonal entries of
\bm{A}+\bm{A}^{\top}
mainly determine the sample complexity.

4.

Our numerical studies show the effectiveness of our algorithm and validate the tightness of our theoretical result about the sample complexities.

\mathrm{tr}(\bm{A})=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\bm{A}_{i,i}

\left\|\bm{A}\right\|=\sqrt{\mathrm{tr}(\bm{A}^{\top}\bm{A})}

We denote by

\mathbb{E}[Z]

Z

Zeroth-order Optimization

In the zeroth-order optimization, one can only access the function value
f(\bm{x})
instead of the gradient
\nabla f(\bm{x})
.
Next, we will give a proposition that describes the relation between zeroth-order optimization and matrix quadratic form.

Proposition 1
.

Let function
f(\bm{x})
be of twice continuous with
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and its Hessian be
\gamma
-Lipschitz continuous.
Given a random Gaussian vector
\bm{u}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, then it holds that

\bm{u}^{\top}\nabla^{2}f(\bm{x})\bm{u}=\frac{f(\bm{x}+\alpha\bm{u})+f(\bm{x}-\alpha\bm{u})-2f(\bm{x})}{\alpha^{2}}-\frac{\Delta(\bm{x},\alpha\bm{u})+\Delta(\bm{x},-\alpha\bm{u})}{\alpha^{2}},

where
0<\alpha
is a constant of a small value and
\Delta(\bm{x},\alpha\bm{u})
is defined as

\displaystyle\Delta(\bm{x},\alpha\bm{u})=f(\bm{x}+\alpha\bm{u})-\Big(f(\bm{x})+\alpha\nabla^{\top}f(\bm{x})\bm{u}+\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2}\bm{u}^{\top}\nabla^{2}f(\bm{x})\bm{u}\Big).

Furthermore, with a probability
1-\delta
with
0<\delta<1
, it holds that

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\Delta(\bm{x},\alpha\bm{u})+\Delta(\bm{x},-\alpha\bm{u})}{\alpha^{2}}\right|\leq\frac{\alpha\left(2d+3\log\frac{1}{\delta}\right)^{3}}{3}.

Proposition
1
shows that given a function
f(\bm{x})
, one can obtain the quadratic form of the Hessian by three function values but with some small perturbation.

Stochastic Diagonal Estimation Based on Quadratic Form Oracles

2.1
Algorithm Description

Our stochastic diagonal estimation method lies on the following proposition.

Lemma 1
.

Given a matrix
\bm{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
and a random Gaussian vector
\bm{u}\sim\mathcal{N}(\bm{0},\bm{I}_{d})
, and an integer
1\leq p\leq d
, then it holds that

\frac{1}{2}\cdot\mathbb{E}\left[\bm{u}^{\top}\bm{A}\bm{u}\cdot\bm{u}_{p}^{2}-\bm{u}^{\top}\bm{A}\bm{u}\right]=\bm{A}_{p,p}.

(2.1)

Proof.

First, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\bm{u}^{\top}\bm{A}\bm{u}\cdot\bm{u}_{p}^{2}\right]=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\bm{A}_{i,j}\bm{u}_{i}\bm{u}_{j}\cdot\bm{u}_{p}^{2}\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{d}\bm{A}_{i,i}\bm{u}_{i}^{2}\cdot\bm{u}_{p}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{i=1,i\neq p}^{d}\bm{A}_{i,i}\bm{u}_{i}^{2}\cdot\bm{u}_{p}^{2}+\bm{A}_{p,p}\bm{u}_{p}^{4}\right]

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:Eu}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1,i\neq p}^{d}\bm{A}_{i,i}+3\bm{A}_{p,p}=\mathrm{tr}(\bm{A})+2\bm{A}_{p,p}.

Furthermore,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\bm{u}^{\top}\bm{A}\bm{u}\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{tr}(\bm{u}^{\top}\bm{A}\bm{u})\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{tr}(\bm{A}\bm{u}\bm{u}^{\top})\right]=\mathrm{tr}(\bm{A}).

Thus, it holds that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\bm{u}^{\top}\bm{A}\bm{u}\cdot\bm{u}_{p}^{2}-\bm{u}^{\top}\bm{A}\bm{u}\right]=2\bm{A}_{p,p}.

∎

Eq. (
2.1
) provide an unbiased estimation of diagonal entries of
\bm{A}
.
Then, we can take
N
samples to reduce the variance of stochastic estimation to obtain a high precision estimation.
Letting random variables
\bm{u}^{(j)}\sim\mathcal{N}(\bm{0},\bm{I}_{d})
with
j=1,\dots,N
, then we construct the following diagonal estimation

\bm{g}=\frac{1}{2N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left[\bm{u}^{(j)}\right]^{\top}\bm{A}\bm{u}^{(j)}\cdot\Big([\bm{u}^{(j)}]^{2}-\bm{1}_{d}\Big).

(2.2)

It is easy to check that
\bm{g}
in Eq. (
2.2
) is also an unbiased estimation of diagonal entries of
\bm{A}
but with a variance depending on
1/N
.
The detailed algorithm description is listed in Algorithm
1
.

A query
Q_{\bm{A}}(\cdot)
which returns
Q_{\bm{A}}(\bm{u})=\bm{u}^{\top}\bm{A}\bm{u}
, sample size
N

2:

Generate

random Gaussian vectors
\bm{u}^{(j)}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\bm{I}_{d})

by accessing

2.2
Sample Complexity

First, we give a sample complexity of Algorithm
1
to achieve an element-wise error bound.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\bm{g}
defined in Eq. (
2.2
) be the output of Algorithm
1
.
Given parameters
0<\delta<1
and
0<\varepsilon
, then with a probability at least
1-\delta
, it holds that

\bm{A}_{p,p}-\varepsilon\leq\bm{g}_{p}\leq\bm{A}_{p,p}+\varepsilon,

(2.3)

if
N
satisfies that

N\geq\frac{V}{4\delta\varepsilon^{2}},\quad\mbox{ with }\quad V=2\Big(\mathrm{tr}(\bm{A})+4\bm{A}_{p,p}\Big)^{2}+\left\|\bm{A}+\bm{A}^{\top}\right\|^{2}+8\left\|\bm{A}_{p,:}^{\top}+\bm{A}_{:,p}\right\|^{2}-12\bm{A}_{p,p}^{2}.

(2.4)

Theorem
1
shows that the sample complexity of Algorithm
1
depends on
\varepsilon^{-2}
which is the same as the ones of stochastic diagonal entries estimation with queries to matrix-vector product
(
Baston & Nakatsukasa, 2022
;
Hallman et al., 2023
;
Cortinovis & Kressner, 2022
)
.
Furthermore, the sample complexity of Algorithm
1
to estimating
\bm{A}_{p,p}
depends on
\bm{A}_{p,p}
,
\left\|\bm{A}+\bm{A}^{\top}\right\|^{2}
,
\mathrm{tr}(\bm{A})
and
\left\|\bm{A}_{p,:}^{\top}+\bm{A}_{:,p}\right\|^{2}
.
Especially, the
\left\|\bm{A}+\bm{A}^{\top}\right\|^{2}
term dominates the sample complexity.
In contrast, to achieve the same precision described in Eq. (
2.3
),
Baston & Nakatsukasa, 2022
shows that stochastic diagonal entries estimation based on matrix-vector product only needs

N^{\prime}=\frac{2(\left\|\bm{A}_{p}\right\|^{2}-\bm{A}_{p,p}^{2})\log\frac{2}{\delta}}{\varepsilon^{2}}

(2.5)

matrix-vector product oracles.
Comparing Eq. (
2.4
) and Eq. (
2.5
), we can observe that our stochastic diagonal entries estimation requires a larger query number than the one based on matrix-vector product.
This is because Algorithm
1
can only query to the quadratic forms
\bm{u}^{\top}\bm{A}\bm{u}
which contains much less information than the one of matrix product
\bm{A}\bm{u}
.

This information difference between the quadratic forms
\bm{u}^{\top}\bm{A}\bm{u}
and matrix-vector
\bm{A}\bm{u}
can be demonstrated in the following example.
Letting
\bm{A}
be a Hessian matrix of
f(\bm{x})
, then Proposition
1
shows that
\bm{u}^{\top}\bm{A}\bm{u}
can be approximated by only three queries of the function value.
Instead, the Hessian-vector product requires to compute the gradient
\nabla f(\bm{x})
shown as follows:

\nabla^{2}f(\bm{x})\bm{u}\approx\frac{\nabla f(\bm{x}+\alpha\bm{u})-\nabla f(\bm{x}-\alpha\bm{u})}{2\alpha}.

(2.6)

Noting that, it may require
\mathcal{O}(d)
function queries to compute the gradient
\nabla f(\cdot)
.
Thus, our stochastic diagonal entries estimation requires a larger query number than the one based on matrix-vector product.

Next, we will give a sample complexity of Algorithm
1
to achieve a norm-wise error bound.

Theorem 2
.

Let
\bm{g}
defined in Eq. (
2.2
) be the output of Algorithm
1
.
Given parameters
0<\delta<1
and
0<\varepsilon
, then with a probability at least
1-\delta
, it holds that

\left\|\bm{g}-\mathrm{diag}(\bm{A})\right\|^{2}\leq\varepsilon\sum_{i=1}^{d}\bm{A}_{i,i}^{2},

(2.7)

if
N
satisfies that

N=\frac{1}{4\varepsilon\delta}\cdot\frac{(4d+16)\Big(\mathrm{tr}(\bm{A})\Big)^{2}+(d+8)\left\|\bm{A}+\bm{A}^{\top}\right\|^{2}+20\sum_{i=1}^{d}\bm{A}_{i,i}^{2}}{\sum_{i=1}^{d}\bm{A}_{i,i}^{2}}.

(2.8)

Note that
\left\|\bm{A}+\bm{A}^{\top}\right\|^{2}=\left\|\bm{A}+\bm{A}^{\top}\right\|^{2}-4\sum_{i=1}^{d}\bm{A}_{i,i}^{2}+4\sum_{i=1}^{d}\bm{A}_{i,i}^{2}
and
4\sum_{i=1}^{d}\bm{A}_{i,i}^{2}=\left\|\mathrm{diag}(\bm{A}+\bm{A}^{\top})\right\|^{2}
.
Thus, the term
\left\|\bm{A}+\bm{A}^{\top}\right\|^{2}-4\sum_{i=1}^{d}\bm{A}_{i,i}^{2}
is the square of “Frobenius” norm of off-diagonal entries of
\bm{A}+\bm{A}^{\top}
.
Accordingly, we can represent Eq. (
2.8
) as

\displaystyle N=\frac{1}{4\varepsilon\delta}\cdot\left(\frac{(4d+16)\Big(\mathrm{tr}(\bm{A})\Big)^{2}}{\sum_{i=1}^{d}\bm{A}_{i,i}^{2}}+\frac{(d+8)\left(\left\|\bm{A}+\bm{A}^{\top}\right\|^{2}-4\sum_{i=1}^{d}\bm{A}_{i,i}^{2}\right)}{\sum_{i=1}^{d}\bm{A}_{i,i}^{2}}+\Big(4d+52\Big)\right)

Thus, if
|\mathrm{tr}(\bm{A})|
is small and the off-diagonal entries of
\bm{A}+\bm{A}^{\top}
are of small absolute value, then sample size
N
required to achieve the same accuracy can also be small.
In this case, then the sample complexity is dominated by the dimension
d
.

To achieve the accuracy in Eq. (
2.7
), Corollary 2 of
Baston & Nakatsukasa, 2022
shows that stochastic diagonal estimation with matrix-vector product needs a sample complexity

N^{\prime}=\frac{\left\|\bm{A}\right\|^{2}-\sum_{i=1}^{d}\bm{A}_{i,i}^{2}}{\varepsilon\cdot\sum_{i=1}^{d}\bm{A}_{i,i}^{2}}\cdot\log\frac{1}{\delta}.

(2.9)

Comparing above equation with Eq. (
2.8
), we can obtain that
N=\mathcal{O}(d)\cdot N^{\prime}
.
This is also because the matrix-vector product can provide more information than the matrix quadratic form.

Sample Complexity Analysis

In the previous section, we provide the sample complexities of Algorithm
1
and Algorithm
2
.
In this section, we will provide the detailed analysis to obtain these sample complexities.
The key to the analysis is the variance of the unbiased estimation in Eq. (
2.1
).

3.1
Variance of Estimation

First, the variance of our unbiased estimation has the following decomposition.

Lemma 2
.

Given a matrix
\bm{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
and a random Gaussian vector
\bm{u}\sim\mathcal{N}(\bm{0},\bm{I}_{d})
, and an integer
1\leq p\leq d
, the it holds that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\bm{u}^{\top}\bm{A}\bm{u}\cdot\bm{u}_{p}^{2}-\bm{u}^{\top}\bm{A}\bm{u}-2\bm{A}_{p,p}\right)^{2}\right]

(3.1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\bm{u}^{\top}\bm{A}\bm{u}\cdot\bm{u}_{p}^{2}\right)^{2}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\bm{u}^{\top}\bm{A}\bm{u}\right)^{2}\right]-2\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\bm{u}^{\top}\bm{A}\bm{u}\cdot\bm{u}_{p}\right)^{2}\right]-4\bm{A}_{p,p}^{2}.

Proof.

By Eq. (
2.1
) and Lemma
5
, we can obtain that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\bm{u}^{\top}\bm{A}\bm{u}\cdot\bm{u}_{p}^{2}-\bm{u}^{\top}\bm{A}\bm{u}-2\bm{A}_{p,p}\right)^{2}\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\bm{u}^{\top}\bm{A}\bm{u}\cdot\bm{u}_{p}^{2}-\bm{u}^{\top}\bm{A}\bm{u}\right)^{2}\right]-4\bm{A}_{p,p}^{2}.

Furthermore,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\bm{u}^{\top}\bm{A}\bm{u}\cdot\bm{u}_{p}^{2}-\bm{u}^{\top}\bm{A}\bm{u}\right)^{2}\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\bm{u}^{\top}\bm{A}\bm{u}\cdot\bm{u}_{p}^{2}\right)^{2}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\bm{u}^{\top}\bm{A}\bm{u}\right)^{2}\right]-2\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\bm{u}^{\top}\bm{A}\bm{u}\cdot\bm{u}_{p}\right)^{2}\right].

Combining above equation, we can obtain the result.
∎

Eq. (
3.1
) shows that the variance mainly depends on the terms
\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\bm{u}^{\top}\bm{A}\bm{u}\cdot\bm{u}_{p}^{n}\right)^{2}\right]
with
n=0,1,2
.
Next, we will compute the exact value of
\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\bm{u}^{\top}\bm{A}\bm{u}\cdot\bm{u}_{p}^{n}\right)^{2}\right]
.

Lemma 3
.

Given a matrix
\bm{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
and a random Gaussian vector
\bm{u}\sim\mathcal{N}(\bm{0},\bm{I}_{d})
, and an integer
1\leq p\leq d
, then it holds that

\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\bm{u}^{\top}\bm{A}\bm{u}\right)^{2}\right]=\Big(\mathrm{tr}(\bm{A})\Big)^{2}+2\sum_{i=1}^{d}\bm{A}_{i,i}^{2}+\sum_{i>j}^{d}\Big(\bm{A}_{i,j}+\bm{A}_{j,i}\Big)^{2},

(3.2)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\bm{u}^{\top}\bm{A}\bm{u}\cdot\bm{u}_{p}\right)^{2}\right]=\Big(\mathrm{tr}(\bm{A})\Big)^{2}+4\bm{A}_{p,p}\mathrm{tr}(\bm{A})+2\sum_{i=1}^{d}\bm{A}_{i,i}^{2}+8\bm{A}_{p,p}^{2}

(3.3)

\bm{u}\sim\mathcal{N}(\bm{0},\bm{I}_{d})
, and an integer
1\leq p\leq d
, then it holds that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\bm{u}^{\top}\bm{A}\bm{u}\cdot\bm{u}_{p}^{n}\right)^{2}\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}\bm{A}_{i,i}\bm{u}_{i}^{2}\bm{u}_{p}^{n}\right)^{2}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum_{i\neq j}^{d}\bm{A}_{i,j}\bm{u}_{i}\bm{u}_{j}\bm{u}_{p}^{n}\right)^{2}\right].

(C.1)

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\bm{u}^{\top}\bm{A}\bm{u}\cdot\bm{u}_{p}^{n}\right)^{2}\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum_{i,j=1}\bm{A}_{i,j}\bm{u}_{i}\bm{u}_{j}\bm{u}_{p}^{n}\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}\bm{A}_{i,i}\bm{u}_{i}^{2}\bm{u}_{p}^{n}+\sum_{i\neq j}^{d}\bm{A}_{i,j}\bm{u}_{i}\bm{u}_{j}\bm{u}_{p}^{n}\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}\bm{A}_{i,i}\bm{u}_{i}^{2}\bm{u}_{p}^{n}\right)^{2}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum_{i\neq j}^{d}\bm{A}_{i,j}\bm{u}_{i}\bm{u}_{j}\bm{u}_{p}^{n}\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle+2\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}\bm{A}_{i,i}\bm{u}_{i}^{2}\bm{u}_{p}^{n}\right)\cdot\left(\sum_{i\neq j}^{d}\bm{A}_{i,j}\bm{u}_{i}\bm{u}_{j}\bm{u}_{p}^{n}\right)\right].

Furthermore,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}\bm{A}_{i,i}\bm{u}_{i}^{2}\bm{u}_{p}^{n}\right)\cdot\left(\sum_{i\neq j}^{d}\bm{A}_{i,j}\bm{u}_{i}\bm{u}_{j}\bm{u}_{p}^{n}\right)\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{k\neq\ell}^{d}\bm{A}_{i,i}\bm{A}_{k,\ell}\bm{u}_{i}^{2}\bm{u}_{k}\bm{u}_{\ell}\bm{u}_{p}^{2n}\right]=0,

where the last equality is because of Eq. (
A.2
).
Thus, we can obtain the final result.
∎

C.1

We will bound the first term in Eq. (

Lemma 9
.

Given a matrix
\bm{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
and a random Gaussian vector
\bm{u}\sim\mathcal{N}(\bm{0},\bm{I}_{d})
, and an integer
1\leq p\leq d
, then it holds that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}\bm{A}_{i,i}\bm{u}_{i}^{2}\right)^{2}\right]=

\displaystyle\Big(\mathrm{tr}(\bm{A})\Big)^{2}+2\sum_{i=1}^{d}\bm{A}_{i,i}^{2},

(C.2)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}\bm{A}_{i,i}\bm{u}_{i}^{2}\bm{u}_{p}\right)^{2}\right]=

\displaystyle\Big(\mathrm{tr}(\bm{A})\Big)^{2}+4\bm{A}_{p,p}\mathrm{tr}(\bm{A})+2\sum_{i=1}^{d}\bm{A}_{i,i}^{2}+8\bm{A}_{p,p}^{2},

(C.3)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}\bm{A}_{i,i}\bm{u}_{i}^{2}\bm{u}_{p}^{2}\right)^{2}\right]=

\displaystyle 3\Big(\mathrm{tr}(\bm{A})\Big)^{2}+6\sum_{i=1}^{d}\bm{A}_{i,i}^{2}+24\bm{A}_{p,p}\cdot\mathrm{tr}(\bm{A})+72\bm{A}_{p,p}^{2}.

(C.4)

Proof.

First, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}\bm{A}_{i,i}\bm{u}_{i}^{2}\bm{u}_{p}^{n}\right)^{2}\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\bm{A}_{i,i}\bm{A}_{j,j}\bm{u}_{i}^{2}\bm{u}_{j}^{2}\bm{u}_{p}^{2n}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{i\neq j\neq p}\bm{A}_{i,i}\bm{A}_{j,j}\bm{u}_{i}^{2}\bm{u}_{j}^{2}\bm{u}_{p}^{2n}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{i\neq j,i=p}\bm{A}_{i,i}\bm{A}_{j,j}\bm{u}_{i}^{2}\bm{u}_{j}^{2}\bm{u}_{p}^{2n}\right]

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{i\neq j,j=p}\bm{A}_{i,i}\bm{A}_{j,j}\bm{u}_{i}^{2}\bm{u}_{j}^{2}\bm{u}_{p}^{2n}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{i=j,j\neq p}\bm{A}_{i,i}\bm{A}_{j,j}\bm{u}_{i}^{2}\bm{u}_{j}^{2}\bm{u}_{p}^{2n}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{i=j=p}\bm{A}_{i,i}\bm{A}_{j,j}\bm{u}_{i}^{2}\bm{u}_{j}^{2}\bm{u}_{p}^{2n}\right].

Next, we will bound above terms and we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{i\neq j\neq p}\bm{A}_{i,i}\bm{A}_{j,j}\bm{u}_{i}^{2}\bm{u}_{j}^{2}\bm{u}_{p}^{2n}\right]=\sum_{i\neq j\neq p}\bm{A}_{i,i}\bm{A}_{j,j}\mathbb{E}\left[\bm{u}_{i}^{2}\right]\mathbb{E}\left[\bm{u}_{j}^{2}\right]\mathbb{E}\left[\bm{u}_{p}^{2n}\right]=\begin{cases}\sum_{i\neq j\neq p}\bm{A}_{i,i}\bm{A}_{j,j},\qquad n=0\\
\sum_{i\neq j\neq p}\bm{A}_{i,i}\bm{A}_{j,j},\qquad n=1\\
3\sum_{i\neq j\neq p}\bm{A}_{i,i}\bm{A}_{j,j}\qquad n=2\end{cases},

where the first equality is because
\bm{u}_{i}
,
\bm{u}_{j}
and
\bm{u}_{p}
are independent when
i\neq j\neq p
and the last equality is because of Eq. (
A.1
).
Similarly, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{i\neq j,i=p}\bm{A}_{i,i}\bm{A}_{j,j}\bm{u}_{i}^{2}\bm{u}_{j}^{2}\bm{u}_{p}^{2n}\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{j\neq p}\bm{A}_{p,p}\bm{A}_{j,j}\bm{u}_{j}^{2}\bm{u}_{p}^{2n+2}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j\neq p}\bm{A}_{p,p}\bm{A}_{j,j}\mathbb{E}\left[\bm{u}_{j}^{2}\right]\mathbb{E}\left[\bm{u}_{p}^{2n+2}\right]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:Eu}}}{{=}}\begin{cases}\sum_{j\neq p}\bm{A}_{p,p}\bm{A}_{j,j}\qquad&n=0\\

15\sum_{j\neq p}\bm{A}_{p,p}\bm{A}_{j,j}\qquad&n=2\end{cases},

and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{i\neq j,j=p}\bm{A}_{i,i}\bm{A}_{j,j}\bm{u}_{i}^{2}\bm{u}_{j}^{2}\bm{u}_{p}^{2n}\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{i\neq p}^{d}\bm{A}_{i,i}\bm{A}_{p,p}\bm{u}_{i}^{2}\bm{u}_{p}^{2n+2}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i\neq p}^{d}\bm{A}_{i,i}\bm{A}_{p,p}\mathbb{E}\left[\bm{u}_{i}^{2}\right]\mathbb{E}\left[\bm{u}_{p}^{2n+2}\right]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:Eu}}}{{=}}\begin{cases}\sum_{i\neq p}\bm{A}_{p,p}\bm{A}_{i,i}\qquad&n=0\\
3\sum_{i\neq p}\bm{A}_{p,p}\bm{A}_{i,i}\qquad&n=1\\
15\sum_{i\neq p}\bm{A}_{p,p}\bm{A}_{i,i}\qquad&n=2\end{cases}.

Furthermore, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{i=j,j\neq p}\bm{A}_{i,i}\bm{A}_{j,j}\bm{u}_{i}^{2}\bm{u}_{j}^{2}\bm{u}_{p}^{2n}\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{i\neq p}^{d}\bm{A}_{i,i}^{2}\bm{u}_{i}^{4}\bm{u}_{p}^{2n}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i\neq p}^{d}\bm{A}_{i,i}^{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\bm{u}_{i}^{4}\right]\mathbb{E}\left[\bm{u}_{p}^{2n}\right]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:Eu}}}{{=}}\begin{cases}3\sum_{i\neq p}^{d}\bm{A}_{i,i}^{2}\qquad&n=0\\

9\sum_{i\neq p}^{d}\bm{A}_{i,i}^{2}\qquad&n=2\end{cases},

and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{i=j=p}\bm{A}_{i,i}\bm{A}_{j,j}\bm{u}_{i}^{2}\bm{u}_{j}^{2}\bm{u}_{p}^{2n}\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\bm{A}_{p,p}^{2}\bm{u}_{p}^{2n+4}\right]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:Eu}}}{{=}}\begin{cases}3\bm{A}_{p,p}^{2}\qquad&n=0\\
15\bm{A}_{p,p}^{2}\qquad&n=1\\
105\bm{A}_{p,p}^{2}\qquad&n=2\end{cases}.

Thus, when
n=0
, we can obtain that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}\bm{A}_{i,i}\bm{u}_{i}^{2}\right)^{2}\right]=

\displaystyle\sum_{i\neq j\neq p}\bm{A}_{i,i}\bm{A}_{j,j}+\sum_{j\neq p}\bm{A}_{p,p}\bm{A}_{j,j}+\sum_{i\neq p}\bm{A}_{p,p}\bm{A}_{i,i}+3\sum_{i\neq p}^{d}\bm{A}_{i,i}^{2}+3\bm{A}_{p,p}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1,j=1}^{d}\bm{A}_{i,i}\bm{A}_{j,j}+2\sum_{i\neq p}^{d}\bm{A}_{i,i}^{2}+2\bm{A}_{p,p}^{2}

\displaystyle\Big(\mathrm{tr}(\bm{A})\Big)^{2}+2\sum_{i=1}^{d}\bm{A}_{i,i}^{2}.

When
n=1

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}\bm{A}_{i,i}\bm{u}_{i}^{2}\bm{u}_{p}\right)^{2}\right]=

\displaystyle\sum_{i\neq j\neq p}\bm{A}_{i,i}\bm{A}_{j,j}+3\sum_{j\neq p}\bm{A}_{p,p}\bm{A}_{j,j}+3\sum_{i\neq p}\bm{A}_{p,p}\bm{A}_{i,i}+3\sum_{i\neq p}^{d}\bm{A}_{i,i}^{2}+15\bm{A}_{p,p}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1,j=1}^{d}\bm{A}_{i,i}\bm{A}_{j,j}+2\sum_{j\neq p}\bm{A}_{p,p}\bm{A}_{j,j}+2\sum_{i\neq p}\bm{A}_{p,p}\bm{A}_{i,i}+2\sum_{i\neq p}^{d}\bm{A}_{i,i}^{2}+14\bm{A}_{p,p}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\mathrm{tr}(\bm{A})\Big)^{2}+4\bm{A}_{p,p}\mathrm{tr}(\bm{A})+2\sum_{i=1}^{d}\bm{A}_{i,i}^{2}+8\bm{A}_{p,p}^{2}.

When
n=2

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}\bm{A}_{i,i}\bm{u}_{i}^{2}\bm{u}_{p}^{2}\right)^{2}\right]=

\displaystyle 3\sum_{i\neq j\neq p}\bm{A}_{i,i}\bm{A}_{j,j}+15\sum_{j\neq p}\bm{A}_{p,p}\bm{A}_{j,j}+15\sum_{i\neq p}\bm{A}_{p,p}\bm{A}_{i,i}+9\sum_{i\neq p}^{d}\bm{A}_{i,i}^{2}+105\bm{A}_{p,p}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 3\sum_{i=1,j=1}^{d}\bm{A}_{i,i}\bm{A}_{j,j}+6\sum_{i\neq p}^{d}\bm{A}_{i,i}^{2}+12\sum_{j\neq p}\bm{A}_{p,p}\bm{A}_{j,j}+12\sum_{i\neq p}\bm{A}_{p,p}\bm{A}_{i,i}+102\bm{A}_{p,p}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 3\Big(\mathrm{tr}(\bm{A})\Big)^{2}+6\sum_{i=1}^{d}\bm{A}_{i,i}^{2}+24\bm{A}_{p,p}\cdot\mathrm{tr}(\bm{A})+72\bm{A}_{p,p}^{2}.

First, we provide the following lemma.

Lemma 10
.

Given a matrix
\bm{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
and a random Gaussian vector
\bm{u}\sim\mathcal{N}(\bm{0},\bm{I}_{d})
, and an integer
1\leq p\leq d
, then it holds that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum_{i>j}^{d}\bm{A}_{i,j}\bm{u}_{i}\bm{u}_{j}\bm{u}_{p}^{n}\right)^{2}\right]=

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum_{i>j}^{d}\bm{A}_{i,j}\bm{u}_{i}\bm{u}_{j}\bm{u}_{p}^{n}\right)^{2}\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{i>j}^{d}\sum_{k>\ell}^{d}\bm{A}_{i,j}\bm{A}_{k,\ell}\bm{u}_{i}\bm{u}_{j}\bm{u}_{k}\bm{u}_{\ell}\bm{u}_{p}^{2n}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{i>j}^{d}\bm{A}_{i,j}^{2}\bm{u}_{i}^{2}\bm{u}_{j}^{2}\bm{u}_{p}^{2n}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{i>j,\;k=i,\;\ell\neq j,\;k>\ell}\bm{A}_{i,j}\bm{A}_{k,\ell}\bm{u}_{i}\bm{u}_{j}\bm{u}_{k}\bm{u}_{\ell}\bm{u}_{p}^{2n}\right]

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{i>j,\;k\neq i,\;\ell=j,\;k>\ell}\bm{A}_{i,j}\bm{A}_{k,\ell}\bm{u}_{i}\bm{u}_{j}\bm{u}_{k}\bm{u}_{\ell}\bm{u}_{p}^{2n}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{i>j,\;k\neq i,\;\ell\neq j,\;k>\ell}\bm{A}_{i,j}\bm{A}_{k,\ell}\bm{u}_{i}\bm{u}_{j}\bm{u}_{k}\bm{u}_{\ell}\bm{u}_{p}^{2n}\right]

We also have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{i>j,\;k=i,\;\ell\neq j,\;k>\ell}\bm{A}_{i,j}\bm{A}_{k,\ell}\bm{u}_{i}\bm{u}_{j}\bm{u}_{k}\bm{u}_{\ell}\bm{u}_{p}^{2n}\right]=\sum_{i>j,\;k=i\;\ell\neq j,\;k>\ell}\bm{A}_{i,j}\bm{A}_{i,\ell}\mathbb{E}\left[\bm{u}_{i}^{2}\bm{u}_{j}\bm{u}_{\ell}\bm{u}_{p}^{2n}\right]=0,

where the last equality is because of
\ell\neq j
and Eq. (
A.2
).

Similarly, we can obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{i>j,\;k\neq i\;\ell=j,\;k>\ell}\bm{A}_{i,j}\bm{A}_{k,\ell}\bm{u}_{i}\bm{u}_{j}\bm{u}_{k}\bm{u}_{\ell}\bm{u}_{p}^{2n}\right]=

\displaystyle\sum_{i>j,\;k\neq i\;\ell=j,\;k>\ell}\bm{A}_{i,j}\bm{A}_{k,\ell}\mathbb{E}\left[\bm{u}_{i}\bm{u}_{j}^{2}\bm{u}_{k}\bm{u}_{p}^{2n}\right]=0,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{i>j,\;k>\ell,\;i=\ell}\bm{A}_{i,j}\bm{A}_{k,\ell}\bm{u}_{i}\bm{u}_{j}\bm{u}_{k}\bm{u}_{\ell}\bm{u}_{p}^{2n}\right]=

\displaystyle\sum_{i>j,\;k>\ell,\;i=\ell}\mathbb{E}\left[\bm{A}_{i,j}\bm{A}_{k,\ell}\bm{u}_{i}^{2}\bm{u}_{j}\bm{u}_{k}\bm{u}_{p}^{2n}\right]=0,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{i>j,\;k>\ell,\;j=k}\bm{A}_{i,j}\bm{A}_{k,\ell}\bm{u}_{i}\bm{u}_{j}\bm{u}_{k}\bm{u}_{\ell}\bm{u}_{p}^{2n}\right]=

\displaystyle\sum_{i>j,\;k>\ell,\;j=k}\mathbb{E}\left[\bm{A}_{i,j}\bm{A}_{k,\ell}\bm{u}_{i}\bm{u}_{j}^{2}\bm{u}_{\ell}\bm{u}_{p}^{2n}\right]=0.

We also have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{i>j,\;k\neq i,\;\ell\neq j,\;k>\ell}\bm{A}_{i,j}\bm{A}_{k,\ell}\bm{u}_{i}\bm{u}_{j}\bm{u}_{k}\bm{u}_{\ell}\bm{u}_{p}^{2n}\right]=\sum_{i>j,\;k\neq i,\;\ell\neq j,\;k>\ell}\mathbb{E}\left[\bm{A}_{i,j}\bm{A}_{k,\ell}\bm{u}_{i}\bm{u}_{j}\bm{u}_{k}\bm{u}_{\ell}\bm{u}_{p}^{2n}\right]=0.

Thus, we can obtain that

\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum_{i>j}^{d}\bm{A}_{i,j}\bm{u}_{i}\bm{u}_{j}\bm{u}_{p}^{n}\right)^{2}\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{i>j}^{d}\bm{A}_{i,j}^{2}\bm{u}_{i}^{2}\bm{u}_{j}^{2}\bm{u}_{p}^{2n}\right].

Similarly, we can obtain that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{i>j}^{d}\bm{A}_{i,j}^{2}\bm{u}_{i}^{2}\bm{u}_{j}^{2}\bm{u}_{p}^{2n}\right]

(C.8)

\displaystyle=

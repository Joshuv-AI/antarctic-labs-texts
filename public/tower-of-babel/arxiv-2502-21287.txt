Orientations of graphs omitting non-edge-critical directed graphs Thanks: The author was partially supported by NSF Award DMS-2115518
H. Sheats

Abstract
. In 1974, Erdős asked the following question: given a graph G G and a directed graph H → \vec{H} , how many ways are there to orient the edges of G G such that it does not contain H → \vec{H} as a subgraph? We denote this value by D ⁡ ( G , H → ) D(G,\vec{H}) . Further, we let D ⁡ ( n , H → ) D(n,\vec{H}) denote the maximum of D ⁡ ( G , H → ) D(G,\vec{H}) over all graphs G G on n n vertices. In 2006, Alon and Yuster gave an exact answer when H → \vec{H} is a tournament. In 2023, Bucić, Janzer, and Sudakov gave asymptotic answers for all directed graphs H → \vec{H} , and in the same paper, they gave an exact answer when H → \vec{H} is a directed cycle. In this paper, we give a better bound for some specific non-bipartite directed graphs. Further, we obtain exact values of D ⁡ ( G , H → ) D(G,\vec{H}) for some small non-edge-critical directed graphs H → \vec{H} . Finally, for these graphs, we classify all graphs G G that attain the bound D ⁡ ( G , H → ) = D ⁡ ( n , H → ) D(G,\vec{H})=D(n,\vec{H}) .



1.
Introduction

Given a directed graph
\vec{H}
and an (undirected) graph
G
, how many ways can one orient the edges of
G
to form a directed graph
\vec{G}
which does not contain a copy of
\vec{H}
? This question was first posed by Erdős in 1974
[
6
]
. We call a directed graph
\vec{H}
-free
if it does not contain
\vec{H}
as a subgraph. Let
D(G,\vec{H})
denote the number of
\vec{H}
-free orientations of the edges a graph
G
. Further, let
D(n,\vec{H})
denote the maximum of
D(G,\vec{H})
over all graphs
G
on
n
vertices. Erdős’s question then asks what is
D(n,\vec{H})
, and in particular, what graphs
G
satisfy
D(G,\vec{H})=D(n,\vec{H})
.

Let
\vec{H}
be a directed graph. We denote by
H
the underlying graph of
\vec{H}
, i.e., the graph formed by “forgetting” the orientations of the edges of
\vec{H}
. An easy lower bound for
D(n,\vec{H})
can be obtained by observing that if a graph
G
does not contain
H
as a subgraph, then any orientation
\vec{G}
of
G
does not contain the directed graph
\vec{H}
as a subgraph. Thus, the number of
\vec{H}
-free orientations of
G
is
2^{|E(G)|}
, the total number of orientations of the edges of
G
. When we take
G
to be an extremal graph for
H
, this gives us
D(n,\vec{H})\geq D(G,\vec{H})\geq 2^{\mathrm{ex}(n,H)}
. This bound is known to be tight in some but not all cases, as we will see below. Our main problem of study is as follows:

Problem 1.1
.

For which directed graphs
\vec{H}
is
D(n,\vec{H})=2^{\mathrm{ex}(n,H)}
?

A
tournament
is a directed graph
\vec{K}_{r}
such that there is exactly one directed edge between any two vertices. The first major progress on this problem was Alon and Yuster, in 2006, who, in
[
1
]
, showed the following:

Theorem 1.2

(Alon, Yuster
[
1
]
)
.

If
\vec{K}_{r}
is a tournament on
r
vertices, then
D(n,\vec{K}_{r})=2^{\mathrm{ex}(n,K_{r})}
for large enough
n
.

More recently in
[
3
]
, Botler, Hoppen and Mota proved that if
\vec{{\mathcal{S}}}_{k}
is a the family of strongly connected tournaments on
k
vertices, then
D(n,\vec{{\mathcal{S}}}_{k})-2^{\mathrm{ex}(n,K_{k})}
.

In 2023, Bucić, Janzer, and Sudakov
[
4
]
proved the following result for odd cycles:

Theorem 1.3

(Bucić, Janzer, Sudakov
[
4
]
)
.

If
\vec{C}
is an odd cycle directed in the natural way, i.e. as a directed cycle, then, for large enough
n
,

D(n,\vec{C})=2^{\mathrm{ex}(n,C)}.

In the same paper
[
4
]
, Bucić, Janzer, and Sudakov resolved the question asymptotically for all graphs.

As is noted in
[
4
]
, with slight modifications, the method used to obtain the result for odd cycles can be extended to show that for any orientation
\vec{H}
of an edge-critical graph
H
with chromatic number at least 3,
D(n,\vec{H})=2^{\mathrm{ex}(n,H)}
. We give an explicit proof of this fact as a special case of one of our results, Theorem
1.7
, below.

A main goal of this paper is to begin the study of the function
D(n,\vec{H})
in the case of some nice orientations of non-edge-critical graphs.

Before introducing our main tools, we introduce a few pieces of notation. For a family of graphs
{\mathcal{L}}
, we will write
p=\min\{\chi(L):L\in{\mathcal{L}}\}-1
. Further, if
G,H
are graphs, we denote by
G\otimes H
the graph consisting of disjoint copies of
G
and
H
, with edges between every pair of vertices
\{u,v\}
where
u\in V(G)
and
v\in V(H)
. We also let
K_{p-1}(t)
denote the complete
(p-1)
-partite graph with
t
vertices in each part. In
[
2
]
, Balogh, Bollobás and Simonovitz gave the following definition:

Definition 1.4

(Balogh, Bollobás, Simonovitz
[
2
]
)
.

Given a family of graphs
{\mathcal{L}}
, the
decomposition family

{\mathcal{M}}({\mathcal{L}})
is the family of minimal graphs
M
such that for some
t
, the graph
M^{\prime}\otimes K_{p-1}(t)
contains a graph
L\in{\mathcal{L}}
as a subgraph, where
M^{\prime}
denotes the graph
M
with
t
isolated vertices adjoined to it.

In other words,
{\mathcal{M}}
is the family of graphs
M
such that if you place
M
in a single part of
K_{p}(t)
then the resulting graph contains a graph from
{\mathcal{L}}
. This has been useful in a variety of contexts. We aim to find a suitable analogue of
{\mathcal{M}}
for directed graphs. Our first, naive attempt is as follows:

Definition 1.5
.

Given a directed graph
\vec{H}
, let
{\mathcal{M}}(\vec{H})
denote the family directed graphs
\vec{M}
whose underlying graph
M
is in
{\mathcal{M}}(H)
and such that for some
t\geq 1
, the following hold, where
M^{\prime}
is
M
adjoined to
t
isolated vertices: There exists an orientation
\vec{G}_{M}
of
G_{M}=M^{\prime}\otimes K_{p-1}(t)
, and a set
W
satisfying
V(M)\subseteq W\subseteq V(G_{M})
such that
\vec{H}\subseteq\vec{G}_{M}[W]
and
\vec{G}_{M}[M]\cong\vec{M}
.

Note that for every
M\in\mathcal{M}(H)
,
\mathcal{M}(\vec{H})
contains at least one orientation
\vec{M}
of
M
. Although Definition
1.5
will prove useful in simplifying notation, we will need something stronger to prove our results:

Let
{\mathcal{H}}
be a family of graphs. We denote by
\overrightarrow{{\mathcal{H}}}
the family of all orientations of graphs
H\in{\mathcal{H}}
.

Definition 1.6
.

Let
{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}(\vec{H})=\{M\in{\mathcal{M}}(H):\overrightarrow{\{M\}}\subseteq{\mathcal{M}}(\vec{H})\}
. That is,
{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}(\vec{H})
is the set of graphs
M\in{\mathcal{M}}(H)
such that every orientation of
M
is in
{\mathcal{M}}(\vec{H})
.

Notice that
{\mathcal{M}}(\vec{H})
is a family of directed graphs. In contrast,
{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}(\vec{H})
is a family of (undirected) graphs. Indeed,
{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}(\vec{H})\subseteq{\mathcal{M}}(H)
, and
{\mathcal{M}}(\vec{H})\subseteq\overrightarrow{{\mathcal{M}}(H)}
. Our main theorem will make use of the family
{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}(\vec{H})
.

Before stating our results, we need some more terminology. A
(t+1)
-star
, denoted
S_{t+1}
, is the graph with a single center vertex
v
and
t
other vertices, all adjacent only to
v
. We call an orientation
\vec{S}_{t+1}
of a star
S_{t+1}

pure
if all of its edges are oriented towards its center vertex or all of its edges are oriented away from its center vertex. Note
S_{t+1}
has exactly two pure orientations. We now state our first main result.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
H
be a graph with chromatic number
\chi(H)=p+1\geq 3
, and suppose there is a
(t+1)
-star
S_{t+1}
in
{\mathcal{M}}(H)
. Fix an orientation
\vec{H}
of
H
and suppose
{\mathcal{M}}(\vec{H})
contains both pure orientations of
S_{t+1}
. Then for large enough
n
,

2^{\mathrm{ex}(n,H)}\leq D(n,\vec{H})\leq 2^{t(n,p)+p\mathrm{ex}(n,{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}(\vec{H}))}.

Note that if
H
is an edge critical graph, then for any orientation
\vec{H}
of
H
,
{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}(\vec{H})={\mathcal{M}}(H)
which is a single edge. Thus we immediately obtain the result from
[
4
]
that
D(n,\vec{H})=2^{\mathrm{ex}(n,H)}
.

The proof of Theorem
1.7
, gives no information about the structure of a graph
G
maximizing
D(n,\vec{H})
for a directed graph
\vec{H}
. Our next theorem shows that in the case where
\mathrm{ex}({\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}(\vec{H}))
is a constant value, for large enought
n
, one can change a constant number of edges in
G
to obtain a complete
p
-partite graph.

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
H
be a graph with chromatic number
\chi(H)=p+1\geq 3
and let
\vec{H}
be an orientation of
H
. Suppose that there is a
(t+1)
-star
S_{t+1}
in
{\mathcal{M}}(H)
and that
{\mathcal{M}}(\vec{H})
contains both pure orientations of
S_{t+1}
. Suppose further that there is a constant
a
such that for large enough
n
,
\mathrm{ex}(n,{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}(\vec{H}))=a
. Let
G
be a graph that maximizes
D(G,\vec{H})
. If
V(G)=V_{1}\cup\ldots\cup V_{p}
is an optimal
p
-partition of
V(G)
, then

\sum_{i=1}^{p}|E(V_{i})|\leq pa.

In particular, this immediately implies the following corollary.

Corollary 1.9
.

Let
H
be an edge critical graph with chromatic number
\chi(H)=p+1\geq 3
, and let
\vec{H}
be any orientation of
H
. Then for large enough
n
,

D(n,\vec{H})=2^{\mathrm{ex}(n,H)}=2^{t(n,p)}.

Furthermore,
T(n,p)
is the unique graph attaining this bound.

In Section 4 we give an application of Theorem
1.8
. In particular, we will see that Theorems
1.7
and
1.8
are especially interesting when
{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}(\vec{H})=\overrightarrow{{\mathcal{M}}(H)}
. We define a
(k,r)
-
fan
, denoted
F_{k,r}
to be
k
copies of
K_{r}
all joined at a single vertex. We call the common vertex to all copies of
K_{r}
the
center
of
F_{k,r}
. The following result about the extremal graphs for
(k,r)
-fans was shown for
r=3
by Erdős, Füredi, Gould, and Gunderson
[
7
]
and for general
r
by Chen
[
5
]
.

Theorem 1.10
.

(Chen
[
5
]
)

For every
k\geq 1
,
r\geq 2
and for every
n\geq 16k^{3}r^{8}
if a graph
G
on
n
vertices has more than

t(n,r-1)+\begin{cases}k^{2}-k&\text{ if $k$ is odd}\\
k^{2}-\frac{3}{2}k&\text{ if $k$ is even}\end{cases}

edges, then
G
contains a copy of a
(k,r)
-fan. Furthermore, for any
n
, there exists a
(k,r)
-fan-free graph with this many edges.

For ease of notation, from here on we denote

h(k)=\begin{cases}k^{2}-k&\text{ if $k$ is odd}\\
k^{2}-\frac{3}{2}k&\text{ if $k$ is even}\end{cases}

so that Theorem
1.10
says

\mathrm{ex}(n,F_{k,r})=t(n,r-1)+h(k).

One may deduce from results in
[
5
]
that
{\mathcal{M}}(F_{k,r})=\{S_{k+1},M_{k}\}
. That is,
{\mathcal{M}}(F_{k,r})
consists of a
(k+1)
-star and a matching of size
k
. We define a specific family of orientations of
F_{k,r}
that satisfy the conditions of Theorems
1.7
, and
1.8
:

Definition 1.11
.

Let
\vec{F}_{k,r}
be an orientation of
F_{k,r}
such that each copy of
K_{r}
has at least one edge entering the center vertex and at least one edge exiting the center vertex. We call such an orientation
anti-directed
.

One can easily check that if
\vec{F}_{k,r}
is an anti-directed orientation of
F_{k,r}
, then
{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}(\vec{F}_{k,r})=\overrightarrow{{\mathcal{M}}(F_{k,r})}
. Thus, combining Theorem
1.7
with the results in
[
5
]
, we obtain the following corollary:

Corollary 1.12
.

Let
k\geq 3,r\geq 2
. Let
\vec{F}_{k,r}
be an anti-directed orientation of
F_{k,r}
. Then for large enough
n
, we have

2^{t(n,r-1)+h(k)}\leq D(n,\vec{F}_{k,r})\leq 2^{t(n,r-1)+(r-1)h(k)}.

We will then use Corollary
1.12
to prove exact results for some small cases of
k,r
:

Theorem 1.13
.

Let
r\geq 2
and let
\vec{F}_{2,r}
be an anti-directed orientation of
F_{2,r}
. Then for large enough
n
,

D(n,\vec{F}_{2,r})=2^{\mathrm{ex}(n,F_{2,r})}=2^{t(n,r-1)+1}.

Theorem 1.14
.

Let
\vec{F}_{3,3}
be an anti-directed orientation of
F_{3,3}
. Then for large enough
n
,

D(n,\vec{F}_{3,3})=2^{\mathrm{ex}(n,F_{3,3})}=2^{\left\lfloor\frac{n^{2}}{4}\right\rfloor+6}.

In Section 5 we discuss the remaining orientations of
F_{2,3}
which we call a
bowtie
. In particular, we will see that there are some orientations
\vec{F}_{2,3}
such that
D(n,\vec{F}_{2,3})>2^{\mathrm{ex}(n,F_{2,3})}
:

Proposition 1.15
.

Let
\vec{B}
be an orientation of
B
such that either:

(a)

All edges incident to the center of
\vec{B}
are entering the center vertex or all edges incident to the center are exiting the center vertex.

(b)

One triangle of
\vec{B}
has both edges entering the center and the other triangle has both edges exiting the center.

Then
D(n,\vec{B})>2^{\mathrm{ex}(n,B)}
.

We then give the following example:

Proposition 1.16
.

There exists a graph
H
such that for any orientation
\vec{H}
of
H

D(n,\vec{H})>2^{\mathrm{ex}(n,H)}.

In the remainder of this section we present some questions and open problems.

1.1.
Questions and open problems

We begin with some conjectures about the unresolved orientations of
B=F_{2,3}
. First, we conjecture the value of
D(n,\vec{B})
for the orientations mentioned in Proposition
1.15
.

Conjecture 1.17
.

Let
G
be a complete tripartite graph with part sizes
1,\left\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\right\rfloor
, and
\left\lceil\frac{n-1}{2}\right\rceil
. Let
\vec{B}
be an orientation of
B
as in Case (a) or (b) of Proposition
1.15
. Then

D(G,\vec{B})=D(n,\vec{B}).

Now, we have the remaining case:

Conjecture 1.18
.

Let
\vec{B}
be an orientation of
B
such that there are exactly three edges oriented towards the center vertex (or exactly three edges oriented away from the center vertex). Then

D(n,\vec{B})=2^{\mathrm{ex}(n,B)}

Next, we have a very general and difficult problem.

Problem 1.19
.

For which graphs
H
is there an orientation
\vec{H}
such that

D(n,\vec{H})=2^{\mathrm{ex}(n,H)}?

As a start to Problem
1.19
, and in light of the results of Theorems
1.13
and
1.14
, it seems reasonable to conjecture the following:

Conjecture 1.20
.

Let
\vec{H}
be a directed graph with underlying graph
H
such that
{\mathcal{M}}(\vec{H})=\overrightarrow{{\mathcal{M}}(H)}
. Then for large enough

,

D(n,\vec{H})=2^{\mathrm{ex}(n,H)}.

1.2.
Notation

We denote by
[n]
the set
\{1,\ldots,n\}
. By a graph we mean a pair
G=(V,E)
where
V
is a set of vertices and
E
is a set of unordered pairs of vertices, called edges, i.e.,
E\subseteq\binom{V}{2}=\{Y\subseteq V:|Y|=2\}
. We write
xy
for the edge
\{x,y\}
. All graphs do not contain loops or multiple edges. We refer to the set of vertices of a graph
G
as
V(G)
and the edges as
E(G)
, and denote
e(G)=|E(G)|
. A graph
H
is said to be a subgraph of
G
, denoted
H\subseteq G
if
V(H)\subseteq V(G)
and
E(H)\subseteq E(G)
. If
H
is a subgraph of
G
, we denote by
G\setminus H
the graph formed by deleting from
G
the vertices of
H
and every edge incident to a vertex of
H
. If
F\subseteq E(G)
,
G\setminus F
denotes the graph formed by deleting the edges in
F
(but no vertices). Further, if
V^{\prime}\subseteq V(G)
, then
G[V^{\prime}]
is the graph formed by deleting all vertices not in
V^{\prime}
and all edges incident to a vertex not in
V^{\prime}
, i.e.,
G[V^{\prime}]=(V^{\prime},E\cap\binom{V^{\prime}}{2})
. If
A,B\subseteq V(G)
then
E(A,B)
denotes the set of edges
xy\in E(G)
with
x\in A
,
y\in B
. When ambiguity will not arise, we denote
E(G[A])
simply by
E(A)
. For
v\in V(G)
, we denote by
N(v)
the set of vertices in
G
adjacent to
v
and say the
degree
of
v
is
d(v)=|N(v)|
. If
A\subseteq V(G)
,
N_{A}(v)
denotes
N(v)\cap A
and and
d_{A}(v)=|N_{A}(v)|
.

A
p
-partite
graph
G
is a graph such that there is a partition
V(G)=V_{1}\cup\ldots\cup V_{p}
with
E(V_{i})=\emptyset
for each
i\in[p]
. We denote the complete
p
-partite graph where each part has size
k
by
K_{p}(k)
. The
chromatic number
of a graph
G
, denoted
\chi(G)
, is the smallest number
p
so that
G
is
p
-partite. For a graph
G
we call a
p
-partition
V_{1}\cup\ldots\cup V_{p}
of
V(G)

optimal
if it minimizes the number of edges within parts over all
p
-partitions. That is a partition which minimizes
\sum_{i=1}^{p}|E(V_{i})|
. We denote by
T(n,p)
the Turán graph with
p
parts on
n
vertices and denote
t(n,p)=|E(T(n,p))|
.

By a
directed graph
we mean a pair
\vec{H}=(V,E)
where
V
is a set of vertices, and
E\subseteq V\times V
is a set of ordered pairs of vertices. Unless otherwise specified, we do not allow loops, multiple edges, or bidirected edges, i.e., if
(x,y)\in E
then
(y,x)\notin E
and for all
x
,
(x,x)\notin E
. For a directed graph
\vec{H}
, we denote the vertex set of
\vec{H}
by
V(\vec{H})
and the edge set of
\vec{H}
by
E(\vec{H})
. By the
underlying graph
of a directed graph
\vec{H}
, we mean the graph obtained by forgetting the orientations placed on the edges of
\vec{H}
. That is, if
\vec{H}=(V,E)
, then the underlying graph of
\vec{H}
is
H=(V,E^{\prime})
where
E^{\prime}=\{\{x,y\}:(x,y)\in E\}
. Let
\vec{H}
be a directed graph and let
v
be a vertex of
\vec{H}
. We say
w
is an
in-neighbor
of
v
if
(v,w)
is an edge of
\vec{H}
, and
w
is an
out-neighbor
of
v
if
(w,v)
is an edge of
\vec{H}
. If
A,B\subseteq V(\vec{H})
we denote
\vec{E}(A,B)=\{(v,w)\in E(\vec{H}):v\in A,w\in B\}
. In addition, we denote
E(A,B)=\vec{E}(A,B)\cup\vec{E}(B,A)
. For any undefined notation regarding directed graphs we apply the notation to to the underlying graph
H
of
\vec{H}
. For example, we define the chromatic number
\chi(\vec{H})
to be
\chi(H)
where
H
is the underlying graph of
\vec{H}
.

The following notion will appear repeatedly throughout the paper:

Definition 1.21
.

Let
\vec{H}
be a directed graph, and let
U,V\subseteq V(\vec{H})
. If every edge between
U
and
V
is oriented towards
U
or every edge between
U
and
V
is oriented towards

, we call the pair
(U,V)

pure
. Similarly, if there is a constant

such that all but at most

U
and
V
are oriented towards
U
or all but at most
t
edges between
U
and

are oriented towards

then we call the pair
(U,V)

-

.

If
U
consists of a single vertex

, we denote the pair
(\{u\},V)
by
(u,V)
.

2.
Preliminary Lemmas

In this section we present three lemmas that will be used repeatedly throughout the rest of the paper. The first lemma is a basic application of the directed regularity lemma, and is almost identical to Lemma 2.8 from
[
4
]
. However, as the proof is slightly more general, we have included it in Appendix
A
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\vec{H}
be a directed graph with chromatic number
\chi(\vec{H})\geq 3
, and let
p=\chi(\vec{H})-1
. For any
\delta>0
, there exists
n_{0}
such that for any graph
G
with
V(G)=n\geq n_{0}
and at least
2^{t(n,p)}
many
\vec{H}
-free orientations, there is a partition
V(G)=V_{1}\cup\ldots\cup V_{p}
such that
|E(V_{1})|+\ldots+|E(V_{p})|\leq\delta n^{2}
.

Let
G
be a graph with
n
vertices, and suppose for some
\delta>0
, we can partition
V(G)
into parts
V_{1}\cup\ldots\cup V_{p}
such that
|E(V_{1})|+\ldots+|E(V_{p})|<\delta^{2}n^{2}
. As in
[
4
]
, given a partition
V(G)=V_{1}\cup\ldots\cup V_{p}
we call an orientation
\vec{G}
of
G

relevant
if, for any
X_{1}\subseteq V_{i}
,
X_{2}\subseteq V_{j}
with
i\neq j
and
|X_{1}|,|X_{2}|>2\delta n
, there are at least
|X_{1}||X_{2}|/10
edges directed from
X_{1}
to
X_{2}
. The following lemma shows that if a graph
G
looks approximately like a
p
-partite graph, then
G
does not have many non-relevant orientations (and thus if
G
has many edges, then
G
has many relevant orientations).

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\delta>0
and let

2^{r}p
ways to choose
N_{i}^{\vec{x}}
. Thus, the total number of ways to orient the edges incident to a vertex in
M
is at most

2^{10^{k}r\delta n}2^{rn(1-\frac{2}{p})}2^{r}p2^{\frac{brn}{p}}<2^{crn}

for some constant
c<(1-\frac{1}{p})
. Therefore, the number of
\vec{H}
-free ways to orient the graph
G\setminus M
is at least

2^{t(n,p)+m-1-crn}>2^{t(n-r,p)+m+a}

as desired.
∎

In this section, we prove Corollary

1.13
and
1.14
. Recall that a

-fan, denoted

all joined at a single vertex. We call the vertex common to all copies of

in

, it will be useful to refer to the center of

[

, for all

{\mathcal{M}}(F_{k,r})

Proof of Corollary
1.12
.

Let
v
be the center vertex of

. Let
\vec{F}_{k,r}
be an anti-directed orientation of

, i.e., one such that each copy of

has at least one edge entering

and one edge exiting

{\mathcal{M}}(\vec{F}_{k,r})=\overrightarrow{{\mathcal{M}}(F_{k,r})}

1.7

∎

Recall that we have denoted

h(k)=\begin{cases}k^{2}-k&\text{ if $k$ is odd}\\
k^{2}-\frac{3}{2}k&\text{ if $k$ is even}.\end{cases}

and by
[
5
]
, we have
\mathrm{ex}(n,F_{k,r})=t(n,r-1)+h(k)
.

Before we proceed to the proof of Theorems
1.13
and
1.14
, we prove a general lemma, which will be crucial in the following proofs.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\vec{H}
be a directed graph satisfying the assumptions of Theorem
1.8
with
\chi(\vec{H})=p+1
. Let
G
be a graph maximizing
D(G,\vec{H})
and
V(G)=V_{1}\cup\ldots\cup V_{p}
an optimal
p
-partition of
G
. Then for all
i\in[p]
and all
M\in{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}(\vec{H})
,
M\not\subseteq G[V_{i}]
.

Proof.

Suppose
\vec{H}
has
k
vertices. Let
G
be a graph on
n
vertices such that
D(G,\vec{H})=D(n,\vec{H})
, and let
V(G)=V_{1}\cup\ldots\cup V_{p}
be an optimal
p
-partition of
G
. Note that since
\mathrm{ex}(n,H)\geq t(n,p)
,
G
has at least
t(n,p)
edges, and by Theorem
1.8
,
\sum_{i\in[p]}|E(G[V_{i}])|\leq pa
. Therefore, there are at most
pa
edges missing from between the parts
V_{1},\ldots,V_{p}
. Further it is easy to show that
|V_{i}|=\frac{n}{p}\pm pa
for all
i\in[p]
.

Suppose for contradiction that there is
M\in{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}(\vec{H})
such that
M\subseteq G[V_{1}]
. For each
i\in[p]
, let
A_{i}
be the set of vertices
v\in V_{i}
such that for all
w\in V_{j}
with
j\neq i
, we have
vw\in E(G)
. Label the vertices
V(M)\subseteq V_{1}
by
v_{1},\ldots,v_{q}
. For each
i\in\{2,\ldots,p\}
, let

N_{i}=A_{i}\cap\bigcap_{j=1}^{q}N(v_{j})

and let
N_{1}=A_{1}\setminus\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{q}\}
. Then since there are at most
pa
edges missing between parts,
|N_{i}|\geq\frac{n}{p}-4pa
for each
i\in[p]
. We partition each set
N_{i}
into parts
N_{i}=S_{i}^{0}\cup S_{i}^{1}\cup\ldots\cup S_{i}^{t}
so that
S_{i}^{j}
has size
k
for
j\geq 1
and
|S_{i}^{0}|<k
. Note
t\in\{\lfloor\frac{n}{pk}\rfloor,\lceil\frac{n}{pk}\rceil\}
. Then for each
j\in[t]
,
G[\bigcup_{i=1}^{p}S_{i}^{j}]\cong K_{p}(k)
. Since
H\setminus M
is
p
-colorable,
H\setminus M
is contained as a subgraph in
\bigcup_{i=1}^{p}S_{i}^{j}
for all
j\in[t]
. Further, by construction, each of these copies of
H\setminus M
can be extended to
H
by adding the vertices
v_{1},\ldots,v_{q}
.

Choose an orientation
\vec{G}
of
G
uniformly at random. Let
\vec{M}
be the orientation of
M
in
\vec{G}
. Then since
M\in{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}(\vec{H})
, regardless of the orientation
\vec{M}
, there is a way to extend
\vec{M}
to
\vec{H}
. In particular for each
j\in t
, the probability that
\vec{G}[\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{q}\}\cup\bigcup_{i=1}^{p}S_{i}^{j}]
does not contain a copy of
\vec{H}
is at most

1-\frac{1}{2^{|E(\vec{H})|-|E(\vec{M})|}}.

Therefore, the probability that
\vec{G}
does not contain a copy of
\vec{H}
is at most

\left(1-\frac{1}{2^{|E(\vec{H})|-|E(\vec{M})|}}\right)^{t}<\frac{1}{2^{pa+1}}

for large enough

. Therefore the number of
\vec{H}
-free orientations of

is at most

\frac{1}{2^{pa+1}}2^{|E(G)|}\leq\frac{1}{2^{pa+1}}2^{t(n,p)+pa}<2^{t(n,p)}

a contradiction.
∎

Next, we prove Theorem
1.13
.

Proof of Theorem
1.13
.

Let
n
be the value obtained from Corollary
1.12
, and let
G
be a graph on
n
vertices such that
D(G,\vec{F}_{2,r})=D(n,\vec{F}_{2,r})
. Since
\mathrm{ex}(n,F_{2,r})=t(n,r-1)+1
, we have
D(G,\vec{F}_{2,r})\geq 2^{t(n,r-1)+1}
. Suppose for contradiction that
D(G,\vec{F}_{2,r})>2^{t(n,r-1)+1}
. Note this implies that
|E(G)|>t(n,r-1)+1
. Recall that
{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}(\vec{F}_{2,r})
consists of a 2-matching and a 3-star. Thus

\mathrm{ex}(n,{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}(\vec{F}_{2,3}))=1.

Therefore, by Corollary
1.12
we have

D(n,\vec{F}_{2,r})\leq 2^{t(n,r-1)+(r-1)},

and in particular, by Lemma
4.1
, there is a partition
V(G)=V_{1}\cup\ldots\cup V_{r-1}
such that there is at most one edge entirely contained in
V_{i}
for each
i\in[r-1]
. Suppose that there is exactly one edge in each of
V_{1},\ldots,V_{\ell}
and no edges in
V_{\ell+1},\ldots,V_{r-1}
. Call these edges
w_{1}x_{1},\ldots,w_{\ell}x_{\ell}
. We will show that
G
has fewer than
2^{t(n,r-1)+1}
many
\vec{F}_{2,r}
-free orientations, a contradiction.

We now make a few observations about the structure of
G
. First note that
G
has more than
t(n,r-1)+1
edges only if there are at most
\ell-2
edges missing in total from between any parts
V_{i}
and
V_{j}
. Indeed, suppose
G
has exactly
t(n,r-1)+s
edges. Then
2\leq s\leq\ell
. Further, for each
i\in[r-1]
, we have
|V_{i}|\geq\frac{n}{r-1}-\ell
since otherwise
G
would have fewer than
t(n,r-1)+1
edges. For each
i\in[r-1]
, let
A_{i}
be the set of vertices
v\in V_{i}
such that
v
has an edge to every vertex
u\in V_{j}
for
j\neq i
and and no edges to other vertices in
V_{i}
. In other words

A_{i}=\{v\in V_{i}:\forall u\in V(G)\setminus V_{i},\,uv\in E(G)\}\setminus\{w_{i},x_{i}\}

Note that since there are at most
\ell-2
edges missing from between all parts
V_{i}
and
V_{j}
and since each such missing edge is “incident” to only two vertices, we have
|A_{i}|\geq|V_{i}|-\ell\geq\frac{n}{r-1}-2\ell
for each
i
.

Since
G
has
t(n,r-1)+s
edges, it suffices to show that for an orientation
\vec{G}
of
G
chosen uniformly at random, the probability that
\vec{G}
does not contain a copy of
\vec{F}_{2,r}
is less than
\frac{1}{2^{s-1}}
as this implies that
G
has fewer than

\frac{1}{2^{s-1}}2^{|E(G)|}=\frac{1}{2^{s-1}}2^{t(n,r-1)+s}=2^{t(n,r-1)+1}

many
\vec{F}_{2,r}
-free orientations.

By definition, in our directed graph
\vec{F}_{2,r}
, we have two oriented copies of
K_{r}
as subgraphs. Denote these by
\vec{K}_{r}^{1}
and
\vec{K}_{r}^{2}
. We consider the quadruples of vertices
\{w_{i},x_{i},w_{j},x_{j}\}
for
i\neq j\in[\ell]
. There are
\binom{\ell}{2}
such quadruples and since there are at most
\ell-2
edges missing from between the parts
V_{1},\ldots,V_{r-1}
, at least
\binom{\ell}{2}-\ell+2
of them form a copy of
K_{4}
in
G
. One may easily check that
\binom{\ell}{2}-\ell+2\geq s-1
since
s\leq\ell
.

We now restrict our attention to one such copy of
K_{4}
. Let
X\vcentcolon=\{w_{1},x_{1},w_{2},x_{2}\}
and without loss of generality say
G[X]\cong K_{4}
. Since our orientation
\vec{F}_{2,r}
is anti-directed, there is at least one edge entering our center vertex
v
and at least one edge exiting
v
in each copy of
K_{r}
. Therefore each of
\vec{K}_{r}^{1}
and
\vec{K}_{r}^{2}
contains a triangle of one of the following forms:

•

\{(v,y),(y,z),(z,v)\}

•

\{(v,y),(z,y),(z,v)\}

for some vertices
y,z
from
\vec{F}_{2,r}
.

Fix such a triangle in
\vec{K}_{r}^{1}
, and call it
\vec{T}
. Let
\vec{G}
be an orientation of
G
chosen uniformly at random. We wish to find a copy of
\vec{T}
in
\vec{G}[X]
that includes the edges
(u,w_{i})
and
(x_{i},u)
for some
i\in[2]
where
u\in\{w_{3-i},x_{3-i}\}
. By listing the 64 possibilities of
G[X]
and counting we see the probability that we have such a copy of
\vec{T}
is
5/8
. Thus, the probability that a random orientation of
G[X]
does not contain such a copy of
\vec{T}
is
3/8
.

Now, suppose
\vec{G}[X]
does contain such a copy of
\vec{T}
. Suppose without loss of generality that the vertices of this
\vec{T}
are
w_{1}x_{1}w_{2}
. Let
t=\min_{i}|A_{i}|
. We partition

\bigcup_{i=3}^{r-1}A_{i}=S_{0}\cup S_{1}\cup\ldots\cup S_{t}

into disjoint copies of
K_{r-3}
where each part
S_{i}
consists of exactly one vertex from each part
A_{3},\ldots,A_{r-1}
for all
i\in[t]
and let
S_{0}
be the remaining vertices. Note that by the definition of
A_{i}
,
G[\bigcup_{i=3}^{r-1}A_{i}]
is a complete
(r-3)
-partite graph. Further, by our bound on
|A_{i}|
we can guarantee we have
t=\min_{i}|A_{i}|\geq\frac{n}{r-1}-2\ell
. By construction, for each
i\in[t]
, we have
G_{i}\coloneqq G[S_{i}\cup\{w_{1},x_{1},w_{2}\}]\cong K_{r}
. Further, by our choice of
\vec{T}
, there is at least one orientation
\vec{G}_{i}
of
G_{i}
and a directed graph isomorphism
\phi:\vec{G}_{i}\to\vec{K}_{r}^{1}
such that
\vec{G}_{i}[\{w_{1},x_{1},w_{2}\}]\cong\vec{T}
and
\phi(w_{2})=v
, where
v
is the distinguished center vertex of
\vec{K}_{r}^{1}
.

There are
\binom{r}{2}
edges in
G_{i}
for each
i\in[2]
and so there are
2^{\binom{r}{2}}
ways that each
G_{i}
could be oriented. Having already assumed that
\vec{G}[\{w_{1},x_{1},w_{2}\}]\cong\vec{T}
via
\phi
, we know that
\vec{G}[\{w_{1},x_{1},w_{2}\}]=\vec{G}_{i}[\{w_{1},x_{1},w_{2}\}]
for each
i\in[t]
. Therefore, the probability that
\vec{G}[G_{i}]=\vec{G}_{i}
is

\frac{1}{2^{\binom{r}{2}-3}}.

Thus, the probability that
\vec{G}[G_{i}]\neq\vec{G}_{i}
for all
3\leq i\leq t
is at most

\left(1-\frac{1}{2^{\binom{r}{2}-3}}\right)^{\frac{n}{r-1}-2\ell},

which tends to 0 as
n\to\infty
.

Now, suppose we have found
i
such that
\vec{G}[G_{i}]\cong\vec{K}_{r}^{1}
as described above. Suppose its vertices are
\{w_{1},x_{1},w_{2}\}\cup\{v_{i}:i\in\{3,\ldots,r-1\}\}
where
v_{i}\in V_{i}
for each
i\in\{3,\ldots,r-1\}
. Let
B_{i}=A_{i}\setminus\{v_{i}\}
for each
i\in\{3,\ldots,r-1\}
and let
B_{1}=A_{1}
. Let
t^{\prime}=\min_{i}|B_{i}|
. Then we may partition

G[B_{1}\cup\bigcup_{i=3}^{r-1}B_{i}]=S_{1}^{\prime}\cup\ldots\cup S_{t^{\prime}}^{\prime}

into disjoint copies of
K_{r-2}
, where, again, for
i\geq 1
,
S_{i}^{\prime}
consists of exactly one vertex from each part
B_{1},B_{3},\ldots,B_{r-1}
. Note that we have
t^{\prime}=\min_{i}|B_{i}|\geq\frac{n}{r-1}-2\ell-1
. By construction, for each
i\in[t^{\prime}]
, we have
G_{i}^{\prime}\coloneqq G[S_{i}^{\prime}\cup\{w_{2},x_{2}\}]\cong K_{r}
. Further, since our orientation
\vec{F}_{2,r}
is anti-directed, we know
\vec{K}_{r}^{2}
has both an incoming edge and an outgoing edge incident to its distinguished center vertex
v
, so no matter how
w_{2}x_{2}
is directed, there is at least one orientation
\vec{G}_{i}^{\prime}
of
G_{i}^{\prime}
and a directed graph isomorphism
\sigma:\vec{G}_{i}^{\prime}\to\vec{K}_{r}^{2}
as follows: we have
\sigma(w_{2})=v
, the distinguished center vertex of
\vec{K}_{r}^{2}
, and
\vec{G}_{i}^{\prime}[\{w_{2},x_{2}\}]=\vec{G}[\{w_{2},x_{2}\}]
.

Again, there are
\binom{r}{2}
edges in
G_{i}^{\prime}
and so there are
2^{\binom{r}{2}}
ways that each
G_{i}^{\prime}
could be oriented in
\vec{G}
. Thus, regardless of how the edge
w_{2}x_{2}
is oriented, for a given
i\in[t^{\prime}]
the probability that
\vec{G}[G_{i}^{\prime}]=\vec{G}_{i}^{\prime}
is at least

\frac{1}{2^{\binom{r}{2}-1}}.

Therefore, the probability that there is no
i\in[t^{\prime}]
such that
\vec{G}[G_{i}^{\prime}]=\vec{G}_{i}^{\prime}
as above is at most

\left(1-\frac{1}{2^{\binom{r}{2}-1}}\right)^{\frac{n}{r-1}-4\ell-1}.

Finally putting this all together, we may summarize the preceding as follows:

(a)

Given a quadruple of vertices
w_{i},x_{i},w_{j},x_{j}
with
1\leq i,j\leq r-1
such that
w_{i},x_{i}\in V_{i}
,
w_{j},x_{j}\in V_{j}
and
G[\{w_{i},x_{i},w_{j},x_{j}\}]\cong K_{4}
, with probability
3/8
,
\vec{G}[\{w_{i},x_{i},w_{j},x_{j}\}]
does not contain a triangle
\vec{T}
that includes the edges
(u,w_{k})
and
(x_{k},u)
where
k\in\{i,j\}
and
u\in\{w_{q},x_{q}\}
with
q\in\{i,j\}\setminus\{k\}
.

(b)

Assuming that
\vec{G}
does contain such a copy of
\vec{T}
as described in (a), the probability that
\vec{T}
cannot be extended to a copy of
\vec{K}_{r}^{1}
with the distinguished center vertex of
\vec{K}_{r}^{1}
at
u
is at most

\left(1-\frac{1}{2^{\binom{r}{2}-3}}\right)^{\frac{n}{r-1}-4\ell}.

(c)

Assuming that
\vec{G}
does contain such a copy of
\vec{K}_{r}^{1}
as described in (b), the probability that we cannot find a copy of
\vec{K}_{r}^{2}
, also centered at
u
which is otherwise disjoint from
\vec{K}_{r}^{1}
is at most

\left(1-\frac{1}{2^{\binom{r}{2}-1}}\right)^{\frac{n}{r-1}-4\ell-1}.

Now, if there is no copy of
\vec{F}_{2,r}
centered in
\{w_{i},x_{i},w_{j},x_{j}\}
, then in the list above, either:

•

(a) holds or

•

(a) does not hold but (b) does or

•

neither (a) nor (b) hold but (c) does.

Therefore, given
i\neq j\in[r-1]
such that
G[\{w_{i},x_{i},w_{j},x_{j}\}]\cong K_{4}
, the probability that there exists no copy of
\vec{F}_{2,r}
centered in
\{w_{i},x_{i},w_{j},x_{j}\}
is at most

\frac{3}{8}+\left(1-\frac{1}{2^{\binom{r}{2}-3}}\right)^{\frac{n}{r-1}-4\ell}+\left(1-\frac{1}{2^{\binom{r}{2}-1}}\right)^{\frac{n}{r-1}-4\ell-1}=\frac{3}{8}+\epsilon(n)<\frac{1}{2}

for large enough

.

Finally, there are at least
\binom{\ell}{2}-\ell+2
quadruples of vertices
\{w_{i},x_{i},w_{j},x_{j}\}
that form a
K_{4}
. Since any two copies of

formed in this way share at most one edge, the probabilities that each of them contain some triangle
\vec{T}
contained in
\vec{K}_{1}^{r}
are independent.

Further, the probability that no copy of
\vec{T}
can be extended to a copy of
\vec{F}_{2,r}
is certainly smaller than the probability that a given copy of
\vec{T}
cannot be extended to
\vec{F}_{2,r}
which is at most:

\epsilon\vcentcolon=\left(1-\frac{1}{2^{\binom{r}{2}-3}}\right)^{\frac{n}{r-1}-4\ell}+\left(1-\frac{1}{2^{\binom{r}{2}-1}}\right)^{\frac{n}{r-1}-4\ell-1}.

Therefore, the probability that an arbitrary orientation
\vec{G}
of
G
does not contain a copy of
\vec{F}_{2,r}
is at most

\left(\frac{3}{8}\right)^{\binom{\ell}{2}-\ell+2}+\epsilon(n)<\frac{1}{2^{s-1}}

for large enough
n
.
∎

Proof of Theorem

.

Let

be a graph with

. Suppose for contradiction that

, for

,

. Since we have assumed

, we may assume that both

and

and

, let

Therefore, the possible

’s are:

•

•

a_{2}=P_{3}

•

a_{3}=P_{4}

•

a_{4}=P_{5}

•

a_{5}=C_{3}

•

a_{6}=C_{4}

•

a_{7}=C_{5}

•

a_{8}=P_{2}\sqcup P_{3}

•

a_{9}=P_{2}\sqcup C_{3}

•

a_{10}=P_{3}\sqcup C_{3}

•

a_{11}=P_{2}\sqcup P_{2}

•

a_{12}=P_{3}\sqcup P_{3}

•

a_{13}=C_{3}\sqcup C_{3}

Now, using the enumeration above, each possible pair
\{G[X_{1}],G[X_{2}]\}
can be written as a pair
\{a_{i},a_{j}\}
. We state two claims.

Claim 1
.

Suppose there are three vertices

each with degree 2, and at least one nonempty component

such that there is an edge in

and

and
q_{3}
. Then

does not maximize

.

Claim 2
.

Suppose
G
has at most
\left\lfloor\frac{n^{2}}{4}\right\rfloor+8

We consider the orientations of

Therefore, we consider other orientations
\vec{B}
of

.

Proof of Proposition
1.15
.

Let
G
be a graph defined as follows: There is one vertex
v
is adjacent to every other vertex in
V(G)
and
G\setminus\{v\}
is a balanced complete bipartite graph with parts
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
. We break into cases depending on whether
\vec{B}
falls into case (a) or (b).

(a)

Suppose
\vec{B}
is the orientation such that all edges incident to the center vertex are oriented towards the center vertex. Suppose there are two vertices in
x_{1},y_{1}\in V_{1}
and two vertices
x_{2},y_{2}\in V_{2}
such that for each
i\in[2]

(x_{i},v),(y_{i},v)\in E(\vec{G})
. Then regardless of how the edges between
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
are oriented, these five vertices form a copy of
\vec{B}
with triangles
x_{1}x_{2}v
and
y_{1}y_{2}v
. Further note that since every copy of
B
in
G
is centered at
v
, if there are not two vertices
x_{i},y_{i}\in V_{i}
as above, then
\vec{G}
does not contain
\vec{B}
. Thus the number of
\vec{B}
-free orientations of

is

2^{|V_{1}||V_{2}|}(|V_{1}|2^{|V_{2}|}+|V_{2}|2^{|V_{1}|}).

So we have

\displaystyle D(G,\vec{B})

\displaystyle\geq 2^{\left\lfloor\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{4}\right\rfloor}\left(\left\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\right\rfloor 2^{\left\lceil\frac{n-1}{2}\right\rceil}+\left\lceil\frac{n-1}{2}\right\rceil 2^{\left\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\right\rfloor}-\left\lfloor\frac{n^{2}}{4}\right\rfloor\right)

\displaystyle=n(1-o(1))2^{\frac{n^{2}}{4}}.

The case where
\vec{B}
has all four edges oriented away from the center is similar.

(b)

Suppose
\vec{B}
is the orientation of
B
such that there are vertices
x_{1},y_{1}\in V_{1}
and
x_{2},y_{2}\in V_{2}
such that for each

,
(x_{i},v)\in E(G)
and
(v,y_{i})\in G
. Again, regardless of how the edges between

and

with triangles

and

. Further, if there is

is pure, then there is no
\vec{B}
in
\vec{G}
. Thus, the number of
\vec{B}
-free orientations of

is

2^{|V_{1}||V_{2}|}(2\cdot 2^{|{V_{1}|}}+2\cdot 2^{|V_{2}|}).

So we have

D(G,\vec{B})\geq 2^{\left\lfloor\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{4}\right\rfloor+1}\left(2^{\left\lceil\frac{n-1}{2}\right\rceil}+2^{\left\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\right\rfloor}-4\right)=2^{\left\lfloor\frac{n^{2}}{4}\right\rfloor+\frac{7}{4}-o(1)}.

Proof of Proposition
1.16
.

In

]

\mathrm{ex}(n,W_{2k+1})=\max\{n_{0}n_{1}+\left\lfloor\frac{(k-1)n_{0}}{2}\right\rfloor:n_{0}+n_{1}=n\}

This graph was obtained by letting
G
be a bipartite graph with parts
|V_{0}|=n_{0}
,
|V_{1}|=n_{1}
and placing a
(k-1)
-regular or almost
(k-1)

. Let

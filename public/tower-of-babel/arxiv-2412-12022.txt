Linearization problem for finite subgroups of the plane Cremona group
Antoine Pinardin, Arman Sarikyan, Egor Yasinsky

Abstract
. We give a complete solution of the linearization problem in the plane Cremona group over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero.

1.
The linearization problem: brief history of previous work

1.1.
Finite subgroups of the plane Cremona group

Given a birational self-map of the projective plane, for example

\varphi\colon[x:y:z]\mapsto[x(z-y):z(x-y):xz],

it is not at all obvious whether this map is conjugate to a
linear
one within the entire group of birational transformations of the plane, known as the
Cremona group
of rank 2. The map
\varphi
is indeed non-regular, as it has the indeterminacy points
[1:0:0],[0:1:0],[0:0:1]
. As a matter of fact,
\varphi
is conjugate over the field
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})
to a linear map
[
BB04
]
. Letting
\zeta=(1+\sqrt{5})/2
, the birational involution

[x:y:z]\mapsto\left[(x-\zeta y)(y-z)(z-\zeta x):(\zeta^{-1}x-y)(\zeta^{2}y-z)(z-x):(x-y)(\zeta^{2}y-z)(z-\zeta x)\right]

conjugates
\varphi
to

[x:y:z]\mapsto[y-\zeta^{-2}z:y-\zeta^{-1}x:y].

Naturally, the problem becomes even more complicated when considering arbitrary finite, not necessarily cyclic, groups and/or specific base fields. Regarding the latter, in this paper we will always work over an algebraically closed field
\mathbf{k}
of characteristic zero, say
\mathbf{k}=\mathbb{C}
. In this case, the very description of all finite subgroups of the plane Cremona group, denoted
\Cr_{2}(\mathbf{k})
hereafter, becomes a challenging task. This problem traces its origins to E. Bertini’s work on involutions in
\Cr_{2}(\mathbb{C})
. Bertini identified three types of conjugacy classes, now referred to as de Jonquières, Geiser, and Bertini involutions. However, his classification was incomplete, and his proofs lacked rigor. Progress continued in 1895 with S. Kantor and A. Wiman, who provided a more detailed description of finite subgroups in
\Cr_{2}(\mathbb{C})
, though their work was not entirely accurate too.

The modern approach to this problem began with a crucial insight of Yu. Manin and V. Iskovskikh, who discovered a connection between the conjugacy classes of finite subgroups in the Cremona groups and classification of
G
-varieties up to
G
-birational equivalence between them (see Section
2
). In 2000, L. Bayle and A. Beauville further developed this approach in their study of involutions
[
BB00
]
. Later, T. de Fernex extended the classification to subgroups of prime order
[
dF04
]
, while J. Blanc classified finite abelian subgroups in
\Cr_{2}(\mathbb{C})
, see
[
Bla06
]
. The most comprehensive description of arbitrary finite subgroups in
\Cr_{2}(\mathbb{C})
was obtained by I. Dolgachev and V. Iskovskikh in their seminal work
[
DI09
]
.

What Dolgachev and Iskovskikh did was not only the classification of finite subgroups of
\Cr_{2}(\mathbb{C})
up to isomorphism, but also the description of many conjugacy classes, which is clearly a much more delicate problem. For instance, it was established by Beauville and Bayle
[
BB00
]
that the conjugacy classes of involutions in
\Cr_{2}(\mathbb{C})
are in one-to-one correspondence with the isomorphism classes of their fixed curves. In particular, an involution is
linearizable
, i.e. conjugate in
\Cr_{2}(\mathbb{C})
to a linear one, if and only if its fixed curve is rational (notice that in this case it is crucial that the base field is algebraically closed: as shown in a recent work
[
CMYZ24
]
, the aforementioned correspondence fails over a non-closed field, such as
\mathbb{R}
).

Nevertheless, despite numerous specific results (see, for example
[
DI09
,
Bla09
]
), the following basic question remained open:
which finite subgroups of
\Cr_{2}(\mathbb{C})
are linearizable?
The goal of this paper is to provide a complete answer to this question. Before presenting our results, let us place the problem in a broader context and describe some existing approaches to solving it.

1.2.
The Bogomolov–Prokhorov invariant

Consider the tower of the Cremona groups

\Cr_{1}(\mathbf{k})\subset\Cr_{2}(\mathbf{k})\subset\Cr_{3}(\mathbf{k})\subset\Cr_{4}(\mathbf{k})\subset\ldots,

where
\Cr_{i}(\mathbf{k})
denotes the group of birational self-maps of
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{k}}^{i}
, and each embedding
\Cr_{i}(\mathbf{k})\subset\Cr_{i+1}(\mathbf{k})
is induced by adding a new variable. Two subgroups
G_{1}\subset\Cr_{n}(\mathbf{k})
and
G_{2}\subset\Cr_{m}(\mathbf{k})
are called
stably conjugate
if they are conjugate in some larger Cremona group
\Cr_{N}(\mathbf{k})\supset\Cr_{n}(\mathbf{k}),\Cr_{m}(\mathbf{k})
, where
N\geqslant n,m
.

As will be recalled in Section
2.1
, any finite subgroup
G\subset\Cr_{n}(\mathbf{k})
is induced by a
biregular
action on some smooth projective rational variety
X
. The process of replacing the initial rational action of
G
by a regular one on
X
is called a
regularization
of
G
, and then one speaks about
G
-variety
, i.e. the pair
(X,G)
, where
G\subset\Aut(X)
. We say that two
G
-varieties
(X,G)
and
(Y,G)
are
stably birational
if there exists a
G
-birational map

X\times\mathbb{P}^{n}\dashrightarrow Y\times\mathbb{P}^{m},

where actions on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
and
\mathbb{P}^{m}
are trivial (so, the case
G=\mathrm{id}
corresponds to classical notion of stable birationality). If
(X,G)
is stably birational to
(\mathbb{P}^{N},G)
, then we say that
G
is
stably linearizable
. The following question can be viewed as an analogue of the famous Zariski cancellation problem in our setting:

Question 1.1
.

Let
G\subset\Cr_{2}(\mathbf{k})
be a stably linearizable finite subgroup. Is
G
linearizable?

As was observed by F. Bogomolov and Yu. Prokhorov
[
BP13
]
, for a smooth projective
G
-variety
X
, the cohomology group
\Cohom^{1}(G,\Pic(X))
is a
G
-birational invariant (in the context of rationality
questions over non-closed fields this observation goes back to Yu. Manin). Moreover, one has the following.

Theorem

(
[
BP13
, Corollary 2.5.1]
)
.

If
(X,G)
and
(Y,G)
are projective, smooth, and stably birational, then

\Cohom^{1}(G,\Pic(X))\simeq\Cohom^{1}(G,\Pic(Y)).

Corollary

(
[
BP13
, Corollary 2.5.2]
)
.

If
(X,G)
is stably linearizable, then
\Cohom^{1}(H,\Pic(X))=0
for any subgroup
H\subset G
.

If the latter condition holds, the
G
-variety
X
is said to be
\Cohom^{1}
-trivial
. It turns out that in some cases, the invariant
\Cohom^{1}(G,\Pic(X))
can be computed in
terms of
G
-fixed locus:

Theorem 1.2

(
[
BP13
, Theorem 1.1, Corollary 1.2]
)
.

Let a finite cyclic group
G
of prime order
p
act on a non-singular projective rational surface
X
. Assume that
G
fixes (point-wise) a curve of genus
g>0
. Then

\Cohom^{1}(G,\Pic(X))\simeq(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^{2g}.

Moreover, the following are equivalent:

(1)

G
is
\Cohom^{1}
-trivial;

(2)

(X,G)
is linearizable;

(3)

(X,G)
is stably linearizable.

In particular, de Jonquières, Bertini and Geiser involutions are not stably linearizable: they all fix a curve of positive genera.

In
[
Pro15
]
, Prokhorov further applies the invariant introduced by Bogomolov and himself and indicates where to look for negative answers to Question
1.1
. He shows, for example, that a del Pezzo surface
S
acted on by a finite group
G
with
\Pic(S)^{G}\simeq\mathbb{Z}
, is
\Cohom^{1}
-trivial if and only if (i) either
K_{S}^{2}\geqslant 5
, (ii) or
S
is the quartic

x_{1}^{2}+\omega_{3}x_{2}^{2}+\omega_{3}^{2}x_{3}^{2}+x_{4}^{2}=x_{1}^{2}+\omega_{3}^{2}x_{2}^{2}+\omega_{3}x_{3}^{2}+x_{5}^{2}=0

in
\mathbb{P}^{4}
, acted on by the group
G\simeq\mathrm{C}_{3}\rtimes\mathrm{C}_{4}
generated by two automorphisms

(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4},x_{5})\mapsto(x_{2},x_{3},x_{1},\omega_{3}x_{4},\omega_{3}^{2}x_{5}),\ \ (x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4},x_{5})\mapsto(x_{1},x_{3},x_{2},-x_{5},x_{4}),

where
\omega_{3}=\exp(2\pi i/3)
. Our Main Theorem below shows that only a few groups in these two cases (i) and (ii) are linearizable. Constructions of
stable
linearizability in the remaining non-linearizable cases are sporadic and remain an open problem even in dimension 2. For instance, as of the time of writing, it is unknown whether the group
G\simeq\mathrm{C}_{3}\rtimes\mathrm{C}_{4}
mentioned above is stably linearizable. Some of these constructions are mentioned below, see Remarks
4.5
,
4.24
, and
4.25
.

1.3.
The Burnside formalism

Another very recent technique for distinguishing birational actions of finite groups is the
Burnside group formalism
, introduced in the work
[
KT22a
]
of A. Kresch and Yu. Tschinkel and generalizing the
birational symbols groups
of M. Kontsevich, V. Pestun and Yu. Tschinkel
[
KPT23
]
. Here, we will limit ourselves to a rough sketch of this approach.

In
[
KT22a
]
, the
symbols group

\Symb_{n}(G)
was defined as the free abelian group generated by
symbols

(H,R\mathrel{\reflectbox{$\righttoleftarrow$}}\mathbf{K},\beta)
. Here,
H\subset G
is an abelian subgroup,
R\subset\Centralizer_{G}(H)/H
is a subgroup, where
\Centralizer_{G}(H)
denotes the centralizer of
H
,
\mathbf{K}/\mathbf{k}
is a finitely generated extension of transcendence degree
d\leqslant n
faithfully acted on by
R
, and
\beta=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{n-d})
is a sequence of nontrivial characters of
H
, generating
\Hom(H,\mathbf{k}^{*})
. The quotient of
\Symb_{n}(G)
by some involved
conjugation
and
blow-up relations

[
KT22a
, Section 4]
gives the
equivariant Burnside group

\Burn_{n}(G)
.

Now, let
(X,G)
be a smooth
n
-dimensional projective variety with a generically free action of
G
. By
[
RY00
, Theorem 3.2]
or
[
HKT21
, Section 7.2]
, the action of
G
can be always brought to a
standard form
via equivariant blow-ups. This means that there is a
G
-invariant simple normal crossing divisor
\Delta\subset X
such that
G
acts freely on
X\setminus\Delta
, and for every
g\in G
and every irreducible component
D
of
\Delta
one has either
g(D)=D
or
g(D)\cap D=\varnothing
. Let
\{D_{j}\}_{j\in\mathcal{J}}
be the set of irreducible components with non-trivial (and hence cyclic) stabilizers
H_{j}\subset G
, considered up to conjugation in
G
. For each
j\in\mathcal{J}
, the elements of
G
which do not move
D_{j}
to an other component of
\Delta
, give rise to a subgroup
R_{j}\subset\Centralizer_{G}(H_{j})/H_{j}
. Consider the subset
\mathcal{I}\subset\mathcal{J}
corresponding to those divisors, together with the respective
R_{j}
-action, that cannot be obtained via equivariant blow-ups of any standard model of any
G
-variety (such divisors were called
incompressible
). Finally, let
b_{j}
be the character of
H_{j}
in the normal bundle to
D_{j}
. The formal sum

(1.3)

[X\righttoleftarrow G]=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}\left(H_{i},R_{i}\mathrel{\reflectbox{$\righttoleftarrow$}}\mathbf{k}(D_{i}),b_{i}\right),

viewed as an element of
\Burn_{n}(G)
, turns out to be a well-defined
G
-birational invariant
[
KT22a
, Theorem 5.1]
.

There exist different versions of symbols groups and corresponding Burnside-type obstructions, see e.g.
[
TYZ24
, Section 3]
for an overview. The first applications of this formalism yield non-linearizable cyclic actions on certain cubic fourfolds
[
HKT21
, 6]

[

of non-linearizability of minimal

-action on the sextic del Pezzo surface (this is an Iskovskikh’s example
4.22

1.4.
Classification of linearizable actions

In this paper, we use an extremely powerful method from birational geometry known as the
Sarkisov program
, which, in principle, has no limitations and allows to answer the question of the birationality of two Mori fibre spaces, and, in particular, their (equivariant) linearizability. The advantage of this method is that, in dimension 2, it is highly explicit (algorithmic); its application in higher dimensions is much more technically involved. In any case, it has enabled us to prove that the “majority” of subgroups of the plane Cremona group are non-linearizable, while the linearizable ones belong to the following compact list. G

Section
3.2

This paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we recall some key facts about rational
G
-surfaces and their minimal models, following the classical works of V. Iskovskikh. We demonstrate how the initial problem reduces to the linearization of finite groups acting on some very specific models. The main technical tool of the paper — the Sarkisov program — is also introduced. This paper employs a significant amount of (elementary) finite group theory, so relevant facts are isolated in Section
3
. In Section
4
, we derive part of the main theorem related to
G
-del Pezzo surfaces. Although Section
5
is not used later on, it aims to fill a gap in the literature by explicitly describing, in matrix terms, finite groups acting on smooth two-dimensional quadrics (equivalently, finite subgroups of the projective orthogonal group
\PO(4)
). In Section
6
, we examine the linearizability of finite groups acting on Hirzebruch surfaces, which finishes the proof of our main result. Finally, in the Appendix we provide the supporting Magma code for Section

(note that this code is for the reader’s convenience only and essentially is not used in any proof).

2.
Rational
G
-surfaces

Throughout the paper, we work over an algebraically closed field
\mathbf{k}
of characteristic zero. We first recall the classical approach to classification of finite subgroups in
\Cr_{2}(\mathbf{k})
; we refer to
[
DI09
, Section 3]
for more details and proofs.

2.1.
G
-Minimal model program

Let
G
be a finite group. A
G
-surface
is a triple
(S,G,\iota)
, where
S
is a smooth projective surface over
\mathbf{k}
and
\iota\colon G\hookrightarrow\Aut(S)
is a monomorphism. A
G
-
morphism
of
G
-surfaces
(S_{1},G,\iota_{1})\to(S_{2},G,\iota_{2})
is a morphism
f\colon S_{1}\to S_{2}
such that
f\circ\iota_{1}(G)=\iota_{2}(G)\circ f
. Similarly, one defines
G
-rational maps and
G
-birational maps.

Let
X
be an algebraic variety over
\mathbf{k}
and
G\subset\Bir(X)
be a finite group acting birationally and faithfully on
X
. Then one can resolve indeterminacies of the action of
G
by the following theorem.

Theorem 2.1

(e.g.
[
dFE02
, Theorem 1.4]
)
.

Let
X
be an algebraic variety and
G\subset\Bir(X)
be a finite group acting birationally and faithfully on
X
. Then there exists a
G
-equivariant birational map
\phi\colon Y\dashrightarrow X
, where
Y
is a smooth algebraic variety acted on biregularly by
G
.

In this paper we are interested in the case
X=\mathbb{P}^{2}
, so the
G
-surface
(Y,G,\iota)
is rational. Hence, after running the
G
-Minimal model program we find that
(Y,G,\iota)
is
G
-birational to one of the following
G
-Mori fibre spaces
:

(i)

a
G
-del Pezzo surface
, i.e. a
G
-surface
(S,G,\iota)
, such that
S
is a del Pezzo surface and
\Pic(S)^{G}\simeq\mathbb{Z}
;

(ii)

a
G
-conic bundle
, i.e. a conic bundle
\pi\colon S\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
such that, both
S
and
\mathbb{P}^{1}
are acted on by
G
,
-K_{S}
is
\pi
-ample and
\Pic(S)^{G}\simeq\mathbb{Z}^{2}
.

Conversely, given a rational
G
-surface
S
, any birational map
\psi\colon S\dasharrow\mathbb{P}^{2}
yields an injective homomorphism

i_{\psi}\colon G\hookrightarrow\Cr_{2}(\mathbf{k}),\ \ g\mapsto\psi\circ g\circ\psi^{-1}.

We say that
G
(or the
G
-surface
S
) is
linearizable
if there is a birational map
\psi\colon S\dasharrow\mathbb{P}^{2}
such that
i_{\psi}(G)\subset\Aut(\mathbb{P}^{2})\simeq\PGL_{3}(\mathbf{k})
. If
S^{\prime}
is another rational
G
-surface with a birational map
\psi^{\prime}\colon S^{\prime}\dasharrow\mathbb{P}^{2}
, then the subgroups
i_{\psi}(G)
and
i_{\psi^{\prime}}(G)
are conjugate if and only if
G
-surfaces
S
and
S^{\prime}
are
G
-birationally equivalent. In other words, a birational equivalence class of
G
-surfaces defines a conjugacy class of subgroups of
\Cr_{2}(\mathbf{k})
isomorphic to
G
. To sum up, there is a natural bijection between the conjugacy classes of finite subgroups
G\subset\Cr_{2}(\mathbf{k})
and
G
-birational equivalence classes of rational
G
-Mori fibre spaces of dimension 2.

So far we have reduced the problem of classification of finite subgroups in

Let

(2)

(see

, Theorem 5]

Lemma 3.13

.

Let
S
be a
G
-del Pezzo surface of degree 6. Then
G
fixes a point on
S
if and only if
G\cap\mathbb{T}=\{\mathrm{id}\}
.

Proof.

Assume that
G
fixes a point on
S
, but
G\cap\mathbb{T}\neq\{\mathrm{id}\}
. Since
\mathbb{T}
can be identified with a subgroup of
\PGL_{3}(\mathbf{k})
which fixes 3 points on
\mathbb{P}^{2}
, an element of
\mathbb{T}
fixing a point on

is necessarily trivial. Therefore, a fixed point

of

lies on

. But this implies

.

Conversely, suppose that
G\cap\mathbb{T}=\{\mathrm{id}\}
. Then
G
is isomorphic to
\mathrm{C}_{6}
,
\mathrm{S}_{3}
or
\mathrm{D}_{6}
by Lemma
4.14
. It remains to apply Lemma
4.16
and notice that groups mentioned there fix the point
([1:1:1],[1:1:1])\in S
.
∎

Lemma 4.19
.

Let
\varphi\colon S_{1}\dashrightarrow S_{2}
be a
G
-birational map of

-del Pezzo surfaces of degree 6. Then

fixes a point on

if and only if

fixes a point on

.

Proof.

It is enough to prove the necessity. Let

be a

-birational map, and assume that

fixes a point on

. By Lemma

, the group

does not intersect the torus

and hence is isomorphic to

,
\mathrm{S}_{3}
or
\mathrm{D}_{6}
by Lemma

. But this implies that

does not intersect the torus

: otherwise

∎

We now discuss (non)linearizability of three particular groups:
\langle\rho\rangle\simeq\mathrm{C}_{6}
,
\langle\rho^{2},\sigma\rangle\simeq\mathrm{S}_{3}
and
\langle\rho,\sigma\rangle\simeq\mathrm{D}_{6}

Example 4.20
.

The cyclic group
G=\langle\rho\rangle\simeq\mathrm{C}_{6}
is linearizable. Indeed, the Sarkisov
G
-link centred at the
G
-fixed point
([1:1:1],[1:1:1])\in S
leads to the

-del Pezzo surface

. Since

is cyclic, it fixes a point on

(of course, this can be seen directly as in the next example), hence the stereographic projection from it linearizes the action of

.

Example 4.21
.

The symmetric group

is linearizable as well. Indeed, after the same
G
-link at the fixed point
([1:1:1],[1:1:1])
, we arrive to

acted on by

. Recall that

. Since

, the group

maps non-trivially to
\mathrm{C}_{2}
factor of this semi-direct product, i.e.

, where

is an automorphism of order

and

. It will be shown below in Proposition

that such a group fixes a point on

, and the stereographic projection from this point linearizes the action of

.

Example 4.22

(

)
.

Answering the question of V. Popov, V. Iskovskikh showed in

]

generated by the automorphisms

and later by Yu. Tschinkel, K. Yang and Z. Zhang by using the combinatorial Burnside groups

We are ready to summarize the results of this section.

Proposition 4.23
.

Let

be a
G
-del Pezzo surface of degree

. Then

is linearizable if and only if
G\simeq\mathrm{C}_{6}
or
G\simeq\mathrm{S}_{3}
.

Proof.

The sufficiency follows from Examples

and
4.21
and Lemma

. To prove the necessity, we use the classification of

Remark 4.24

(

The (non)linearizability of

6.

In the final section we study linearization of finite groups acting on Hirzebruch surfaces

6.1.

The goal of this section is to prove the following linearization criterion for Hirzebruch surfaces

Theorem 6.1
.

Let

be an integer and

(2)

\widehat{G}

(3)

\widehat{G}

\mathrm{D}_{m}

Before proving this, we need some easy lemmas. First, recall the following classical definition.

Let

Lemma 6.4

Corollary 6.9
.

The
G

\mathbb{F}_{n}
is

\mathbb{F}_{|n+2k|}

\mathbb{F}_{n}

\mathbb{F}_{n}
is

-birational to

6.2.
Quadrics with invariant Picard group of rank
1

We will classify finite linearizable subgroups of
\Aut(S)
according to the isomorphism class of
H
in the exact sequence (
5.3
).

Proposition 6.10
.

If
H
is cyclic, then
G
is linearizable.

Proof.

We claim that
G
has a fixed point, so the stereographic projection from it linearizes the action of
G
. Indeed, the group
H\times_{Q}H
is contained in
H\times H
, hence it has exactly 4 fixed points on
S
. We can choose the coordinates on
S
so that these points are

p_{1}=([1:0],[1:0]),\ \ p_{2}=([1:0],[0:1]),\ \ p_{3}=([0:1],[1:0]),\ \ p_{4}=([0:1],[0:1]).

Let
g\in G
be an element which is mapped to the generator of
\mathrm{C}_{2}
in the exact sequence (
5.3
), i.e. which does not preserve the rulings of
S
. Then
G
is generated by
g
and
H\times_{Q}H
. Since
g
normalizes
H\times_{Q}H
, it permutes the points
p_{1},p_{2},p_{3},p_{4}
. Since
g
swaps the rulings of
S
, it fixes
p_{1}
and
p_{3}
, or it fixes
p_{2}
and
p_{4}
, or it permutes
p_{1}
,
p_{2}
,
p_{3}
and
p_{4}
cyclically. In the first two cases, we are done. The third case is impossible, since

would swap
p_{i}
with

, while it belongs to
H\times_{Q}H
, i.e. fixes all four points.
∎

Remark 6.11
.

In the language of Sarkisov program, we linearize the action of
G
as follows. Let
\eta\colon T\to S=\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}

G

. The surface

is a del Pezzo surface of degree 7 with three exceptional curves

, and

is the

and
F_{2}
are the preimages of fibres through

. One has
E\cdot F_{1}=E\cdot F_{2}=1
, and
F_{1}\cdot F_{2}=0
. Then there is a
G

\eta^{\prime}\colon T\to\mathbb{P}^{2}
of
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
onto a pair of points.

We are going to show that
G
is not linearizable in any of the other cases.

Observation
.

We use the Notations of Section
2.2
. Every Sarkisov
G
-link starting from

is either of type
\mathrm{I}
, where
\eta
blows up a
G
-orbit of length 2, or is of type
\mathrm{II}
, and one of the following holds:

(1)

,

,
\chi
is a birational Bertini involution;

(2)

S\simeq S^{\prime}
,
d=d^{\prime}=6
,
\chi
is a birational Geiser involution;

(3)

S^{\prime}
is a del Pezzo surface of degree 5,

,

;

(4)

S^{\prime}\simeq\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
,

;

(5)

S^{\prime}
is a del Pezzo surface of degree 6,

,

;

(6)

,

,

.

Birational Geiser and Bertini involutions lead to a

-isomorphic surface. By
[

, Proposition 4.3]
, links centred at orbits of length 4 also result in a surface which is

-isomorphic to

. Therefore, any

-link starting from

and leading to a non-isomorphic surface must be centred at an orbit of length

Proposition 6.12
.

Let
S=\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
and
G\subset\Aut(S)
be a finite group such that
\Pic(S)^{G}\simeq\mathbb{Z}
. Assume that in the setting of the exact sequence (

) the group

is isomorphic to
\mathrm{A}_{4},\mathrm{S}_{4}
or
\mathrm{A}_{5}
. Then

is
G
-birationally rigid. In particular,

is not linearizable.

Note that an orbit of

is a disjoint union of orbits of

. Therefore, by Proposition
3.7
and Lemma

, any orbit of

on

has length

. Hence

can admit only

-birational Geiser involutions and links at points of degree 4. As was observed before, they give a

-isomorphic surface.
∎

Proposition 6.13
.

Let
S=\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
and
G\subset\Aut(S)
be a finite group such that
\Pic(S)^{G}\simeq\mathbb{Z}
. Assume that in the setting of the exact sequence (
5.3
) the group

is dihedral

. Then

is not linearizable.

Proof.

We may assume that there is a
G
-orbit
\Sigma\subset S
in general position, i.e. its blow-up gives a del Pezzo surface. Then Lemma
3.13
implies that
\Sigma
is a disjoint union of at most 2 orbits of

. Suppose that

, where

. By Proposition
3.7
and Lemma

, these cardinalities are divisible by 2 or

. Then
|\Sigma|
is an even number
\geqslant 4
, and therefore

is
G
-birationally rigid. So, we may assume that

is an orbit of

. By the same observations, we only need to investigate orbits of size 2, 3 and 5.

Suppose

, i.e.

. The link

centred at

gives a
G
-del Pezzo surface

of degree 5. Since

can be isomorphic to

,

or
\mathrm{D}_{5}
, we find that either

is an extension of

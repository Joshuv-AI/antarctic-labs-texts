Sharp bounds for product and sum throttling numbers
Ryan Blair, Gabriel Elvin, Veronika Furst, Leslie Hogben, Nandita Sahajpal, Tony W. H. Wong

Abstract
Throttling in graphs optimizes a sum or product of resources used, such as the number of vertices in an initial set, and time required, such as the propagation time, to complete a given task. We introduce a new technique to establish sharp upper bounds in terms of graph order for sum throttling and initial cost product throttling for power domination. Furthermore, we establish sharp bounds on possible changes of the product throttling number, both with and without initial cost, caused by certain graph operations for standard zero forcing, positive semidefinite forcing, and power domination.



Keywords
Throttling, product throttling, zero forcing, power domination

1.1
Parameters

In this section we provide precise definitions of the parameters discussed throughout.
Standard zero forcing, positive semidefinite zero forcing, and power domination can all be thought of as coloring games on a graph, where the goal is to fill (color) all the vertices (starting with each vertex filled or unfilled); unfilled vertices are filled by applying a color change rule.
2
2

2

Various terms have been used including blue and white vertices, but filled/unfilled has recently become standard due to its suitability for all media.

Standard and positive semidefinite zero forcing originated in combinatorial matrix theory, providing upper bounds on the nullity of certain sets of symmetric matrices whose off-diagonal pattern of nonzero entries is described by the given graph;
standard zero forcing was also introduced in control of quantum systems. Power domination originated from the problem of optimal placement of monitoring units in an electrical network. More information on the origins of these parameters and relevant references can be found in

\operatorname{pt}_{\operatorname{Z}}(G,k)=1
, i.e.
\operatorname{th}_{\operatorname{Z}}^{\ast}(G)=k(G,1)
. Necessarily,

k(G,1)\geq\frac{n}{2}
.

Graphs
G
attaining
\operatorname{th}_{\operatorname{Z}}^{\ast}(G)=\frac{n}{2}
have been characterized and provide useful examples.
Let
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
be disjoint graphs of equal order and let
M
be a matching between
V(G_{1})
and
V(G_{2})
that saturates all vertices. Then the
M
-sum of
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
, denoted by
G_{1}M^{+}G_{2}
, is the graph with
V(G_{1}M^{+}G_{2})=V(G_{1})\cup V(G_{2})
and
E(G_{1}M^{+}G_{2})=E(G_{1})\cup E(G_{2})\cup M
. A graph of the form
G_{1}M^{+}G_{2}
is also called a
matched-sum graph
. Observe that the maximum number of edges in a matched-sum graph of order
n=2r
is
r^{2}
, which is attained by
K_{r}M^{+}K_{r}
.

Theorem 3.13
.

[
1
]

Let
G
be a connected graph of even order
n
. Then
G
satisfies
\operatorname{th}_{\operatorname{Z}}^{\ast}(G)=\frac{n}{2}
if and only if
G
is a connected matched-sum graph.

As noted in
[
1
]
, the path
P_{2r}
is a matched-sum graph,
\operatorname{th}_{\operatorname{Z}}^{\ast}(P_{2r})=r
, and
\operatorname{th}_{\operatorname{Z}}^{\ast}(P_{2r+1})=r+1
; recall that we denote the vertices of
P_{n}
by
v_{1},\dots,v_{n}
in order. When one vertex
u
forces another vertex
v
, we sometimes denote this by
u\to v
.

Proposition 3.14
.

Let
G
be a graph, let
x
be a vertex of
G
, let
e=uv
be an edge.

(1)

Assuming
G-{x}
contains an edge,

\operatorname{th}_{\operatorname{Z}}^{\ast}(G)-1\leq\operatorname{th}_{\operatorname{Z}}^{\ast}(G-{x})\leq\operatorname{th}_{\operatorname{Z}}^{\ast}(G)
.

(2)

Assuming
G-e
contains an edge,
\operatorname{th}_{\operatorname{Z}}^{\ast}(G)-1\leq\operatorname{th}_{\operatorname{Z}}^{\ast}(G-e)\leq\operatorname{th}_{\operatorname{Z}}^{\ast}(G)+1
.

(3)

Assuming
G/e
contains an edge,
\operatorname{th}_{\operatorname{Z}}^{\ast}(G)-1\leq\operatorname{th}_{\operatorname{Z}}^{\ast}(G/e)\leq\operatorname{th}_{\operatorname{Z}}^{\ast}(G)
.

(4)

\operatorname{th}_{\operatorname{Z}}^{\ast}(G)\leq\operatorname{th}_{\operatorname{Z}}^{\ast}(G_{e})\leq\operatorname{th}_{\operatorname{Z}}^{\ast}(G)+1
.

These bounds are sharp.

Proof.

We establish the corresponding bounds for
k(G,1)
and then apply Theorem
3.12
. We use matched-sum graphs in examples showing sharpness. Let
y_{e}
denote the vertex created by contracting edge
e
and
z_{e}
denote the vertex created by subdividing edge
e
.

k(G,1)\leq k(G-{x},1)+1
: Choose
B^{\prime}
such that
\operatorname{pt}_{\operatorname{Z}}(G-{x},B^{\prime})=1
and
|B^{\prime}|=k(G-x,1)
. Let
B=B^{\prime}\cup\{x\}
. Then the same propagation process can force in one round.
For sharpness, consider
P_{2r+1}
and let
x=v_{2r+1}
:
P_{2r+1}-{x}\cong P_{2r}
and
\operatorname{th}_{\operatorname{Z}}^{\ast}(P_{2r+1}-{x})=r=\operatorname{th}_{\operatorname{Z}}^{\ast}(P_{2r+1})-1
.

k(G-{x},1)\leq k(G,1)
: Choose
B
such that
\operatorname{pt}_{\operatorname{Z}}(G,B)=1
and
|B|=k(G,1)
. If
{x}\in B
and
x\to w
when using
B
to force all vertices in one round, then let
B^{\prime}=B\setminus\{{x}\}\cup\{{w}\}
. If
{x}\in B
and
{x}
does not force when using
B
to force all vertices in one round, then let
B^{\prime}=B\setminus\{{x}\}
. Otherwise let
B^{\prime}=B
and let
{w}
be the vertex that forces
{x}
. In all cases, the same propagation process can force in one round (deleting
x\to w
or
w\to x
if needed), and
|B^{\prime}|\leq|B|
. For sharpness, consider
P_{2r}
and let
x=v_{2r}
:
P_{2r}-{x}\cong P_{2r-1}
and
\operatorname{th}_{\operatorname{Z}}^{\ast}(P_{2r}-{x})=r=\operatorname{th}_{\operatorname{Z}}^{\ast}(P_{2r})
.

k(G,1)\leq k(G-e,1)+1
: Choose
B^{\prime}
such that
\operatorname{pt}_{\operatorname{Z}}(G-e,B^{\prime})=1
and
|B^{\prime}|=k(G-e,1)
. If exactly one of
u,{v}
performs a force when using
B^{\prime}
to force all vertices in one round, then assume (without loss of generality) that
u
performs the force and let
B=B^{\prime}\cup\{{v}\}
. When both force or neither force, let
B=B^{\prime}
.
Then the same propagation process can force in one round and
|B|\leq|B^{\prime}|+1
. For sharpness, consider the matched-sum graph
G-e=K_{r}M^{+}K_{r}
, which has order
2r
. Then
\operatorname{th}_{\operatorname{Z}}^{\ast}(G-e)=r
and
G-e
has
r^{2}
edges. Adding any edge (necessarily between the two copies of
K_{r}
) results in a graph with
r^{2}+1
edges, so
\operatorname{th}_{\operatorname{Z}}^{\ast}(G)\geq r+1
.

k(G-e,1)\leq k(G,1)+1
: Choose
B
such that
\operatorname{pt}_{\operatorname{Z}}(G,B)=1
and
|B|=k(G,1)
. If one of
u,{v}
forces the other when using
B
to force all vertices in one round, then assume (without loss of generality) that
u\to{v}
and let
B^{\prime}=B\cup\{{v}\}
.
Otherwise let
B^{\prime}=B
. Then the same propagation process can force in one round (deleting
u\to{v}
if needed).
Thus
k(G,1)\leq k(G-e,1)+1
.
For sharpness, consider
K_{r}M^{+}K_{r}
with
r\geq 3
and note that
\operatorname{th}_{\operatorname{Z}}^{\ast}(K_{r}M^{+}K_{r})=r
. Let
e
be an edge in the matching between the two copies of
K_{r}
. Then
\operatorname{th}_{\operatorname{Z}}^{\ast}(K_{r}M^{+}K_{r}-e)=r+1
because no set of
r
vertices can force in one round.

k(G,1)\leq k(G/e,1)+1
: Choose
B^{\prime}
such that
\operatorname{pt}_{\operatorname{Z}}(G/e,B^{\prime})=1
and
|B^{\prime}|=k(G/e,1)
. If
{y_{e}}\in B^{\prime}
, then
B=B^{\prime}\setminus\{{y_{e}}\}\cup\{u,v\}
. If
{y_{e}}\not\in B^{\prime}
, then there is a vertex
x
such that
x\to{y_{e}}
; without loss of generality,
x
is adjacent to
u
in
G
, and let
B=B^{\prime}\cup\{v\}
. For sharpness, consider
K_{1,n-1},n\geq{3}
and
e
any edge, so
(K_{1,n-1})/e\cong K_{1,n-2}
. Thus
\operatorname{th}_{\operatorname{Z}}^{\ast}((K_{1,n-1})/e)=n-2=\operatorname{th}_{\operatorname{Z}}^{\ast}(K_{1,n-1})-1
.

k(G/e,1)\leq k(G,1)
: Choose
B
such that
\operatorname{pt}_{\operatorname{Z}}(G,B)=1
and
|B|=k(G,1)
. If
u,{v}\in B
and at least one of
u,v
forces a vertex, say
u\to w
, then
B^{\prime}=B\setminus\{u,{v}\}\cup\{{y_{e},w}\}
(if
u\to w
and
v\to w^{\prime}
in
G
, then
y_{e}\to w^{\prime}
in the first round works for
G/e
).
If
u,{v}\in B
and neither
u
or
{v}
forces, then
B^{\prime}=B\setminus\{u,{v}\}\cup\{{y_{e}}\}
.
If
u\in B
and
{v}\not\in B
, then
u
does not force or
u\to{v}
, so
B^{\prime}=B\setminus\{u\}\cup\{{y_{e}}\}
. If
u,{v}\not\in B
, then there are vertices
a,b
such that
a\to u
and
b\to v
, so let
B^{\prime}=B
and
a\to y_{e}
in the first round works.
For sharpness, consider
P_{2r}
and
e=v_{2r-1}v_{2r}
:
(P_{2r})/e\cong P_{2r-1}
, so
\operatorname{th}_{\operatorname{Z}}^{\ast}((P_{2r})/e)=r=\operatorname{th}_{\operatorname{Z}}^{\ast}(P_{2r})
.

k(G_{e},1)\leq k(G,1)+1
and
k(G,1)\leq k(G_{e},1)
follow from the contraction bounds since setting
H=G_{e}
with new edges
f=u{z_{e}}
and
vz_{e}
and contracting
f
gives
G\cong H/f
. Paths can be used for sharpness examples.
∎

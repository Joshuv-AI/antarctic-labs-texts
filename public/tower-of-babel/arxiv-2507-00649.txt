Conformational properties of strictly two-dimensional equilibrium polymers
J.P. Wittmer, A. Cavallo, A. Johner

Abstract
Two-dimensional monodisperse linear polymer chains are known to adopt for sufficiently large chain lengths N N and surface fractions ϕ \phi compact configurations with fractal perimeters. We show here by means of Monte Carlo simulations of reversibly connected hard disks (without branching, ring formation and chain intersection) that polydisperse self-assembled equilibrium polymers with a finite scission energy E E are characterized by the same universal exponents as their monodisperse peers. Consistently with a Flory-Huggins mean-field approximation, the polydispersity is characterized by a Schulz-Zimm distribution with a susceptibility exponent γ = 19 / 16 \gamma=19/16 for all not dilute systems and the average chain length ⟨ N ⟩ ∝ exp ⁡ ( δ ​ E ) ​ ϕ α \left<N\right>\propto\exp(\delta E)\phi^{\alpha} thus increases with an exponent δ = 16 / 35 \delta=16/35 . Moreover, it is shown that α = 3 / 5 \alpha=3/5 for semidilute solutions and α ≈ 1 \alpha\approx 1 for larger densities. The intermolecular form factor F ⁡ ( q ) F(q) reveals for sufficiently large ⟨ N ⟩ \left<N\right> a generalized Porod scattering with F ⁡ ( q ) ∝ 1 / q 11 / 4 F(q)\propto 1/q^{11/4} for intermediate wavenumbers q q consistently with a fractal perimeter dimension d s = 5 / 4 d_{s}=5/4 .

II
Flory-Huggins approximation

The main departure from the conventional theory of polymer solutions is that for EPs
only the total monomer number
n
is conserved and, hence, the monomer number density

\rho=n/V
with
V
being the constant volume of the system, rather than the density
distribution
c(N)
of chains
N
which is an annealed quantity.
(The number density
\rho
is trivially proportional
to the surface fraction
\phi
used elsewhere in this work to characterize the density.)
The density distribution
c(N)
is normalized such that

\rho=\sum_{N}Nc(N).

(7)

It is often more convenient to use instead the normalized
number distribution
p(N)=\left<N\right>c(N)/\rho
for which

\sum_{N}p(N)=1\mbox{ and }\left<N\right>=\sum_{N}Np(N)

(8)

holds in agreement with Eq. (
7

Within the Flory-Huggins mean-field approximation
Cates and Candau 1990
;
Milchev 1993
;
Milchev and Landau 1995
;
Milchev and Landau 1996
;
Wittmer
et al. 1998a
;
Wittmer
et al. 1998b

the grand potential density
\Omega
of the system may be written

\Omega[c(N)]=\sum_{N}c(N)\left[\ln[c(N)]+f_{\mathrm{end}}+\mu N\right]

(9)

where we choose the energy units so that
k_{\mathrm{B}}T=1
and where irrelevant factors
(such as the persistence length) have been omitted for clarity.
The first term is the entropy of mixing,
the second term the free energy contribution due to the chain ends
and the last term entails the usual Lagrange multiplier for the conserved monomer density,
cf. Eq. (
7

Without loss of generality, we have suppressed in Eq. (
9

the part of the free energy linear in chain length.
The chain end free energy contribution

E
,
N

\rho

It is assumed to not dependent on the lengths of neighboring chains.

On the simplest mean-field level

and paying attention
to Eq. (
7

c(N)\propto\exp(-E-\mu N)

Hence,

\left<N\right>=1/\mu\propto\sqrt{\rho e^{E}}.

At variance to permanent polymer chains commonly considered in computer simulations

Specifically, we investigate here by means of off-lattice MC simulations

We use reduced units with

We have investigated in this numerical study conformational static properties of a simple

Theoretically expected universal scaling relations and exponents for asymptotically long chains,

Moduli spaces of parabolic representation pairs of the fundamental group of a punctured surface
Zhi Hu, Pengfei Huang, Wanmin Yan, Runhong Zong

Abstract
. In this paper, we introduce the notions of parabolic representation pair variety and relative representation variety of a given parabolic type. We investigate the local behavior of these varieties. The Zariski tangent space and the tangent quadratic cones are described. By the Riemann–Hilbert–Deligne correspondence, we pro-represent the analytic germs of these varieties by functors related to certain groupoids of parabolic logarithmic flat bundles. Under suitable assumptions, we prove that the differential graded Lie algebra (DGLA) controlling the deformation of parabolic logarithmic flat bundle is mixedly formal. Finally, we construct the moduli space of weighted parabolic representation pairs, and, by means of quiver representation theory, we establish the Kobayashi–Hitchin-type theorem for polystable parabolic representation pairs.

In the case of a punctured Riemann surface, the situation becomes more subtle, requiring the introduction of additional data. Let

In this paper, unlike the above considerations, we not only need the representations

Consequently, a representation with the monodromy around a puncture that lies in the intersection of two different conjugate parabolic subgroups will be recognized as non-equivalent objects once we attach these different parabolic subgroups respectively. From the perspective of nonabelian Hodge theory, including the choices of parabolic subgroups associated to the monodomies around punctures is natural since it corresponds to producing filtered objects

42

via the choices of parabolic structures on local systems, flat bundles, or Higgs bundles. In

However, they still use the usual (reduced) parabolic character variety, which leads to certain pathological phenomena. In particular, the Riemann–Hilbert correspondence may contract some families of compact subvarieties in the de Rham moduli space onto the singular locus of the character variety if the monodromies are non-generic (especially, they have geometrically multiple eigenvalues). It is worth pointing out that if one replaces the usual parabolic character variety by the parabolic representation pair variety, then these issues are addressed, since varying the parabolic subgroups restores the lost degrees of freedom

On the other hand, the parabolic representation pair variety admits a fibration

Finally, in Sect. 5, since affine GIT is no longer applicable to the parabolic representation pair variety, we construct the moduli space of weighted parabolic representation pairs by translating these objects into representations of a certain star-shaped quiver with loops. 4.
DGLA descriptions of deformation functors

4.1.
Review of Deligne–Goldman–Millson deformation theory

A differential graded Lie algebra (DGLA) is a graded
\mathbb{C}
-vector space
L=\bigoplus\limits_{p\in\mathbb{Z}}L^{p}
with a family of differentials

d:L^{p}\rightarrow L^{p+1}
and a family of bilinear homogeneous graded skew-symmetric brackets
[\bullet,\bullet]:L^{p}\otimes L^{q}\rightarrow L^{p+q}
such that
d
is a derivation for the Lie bracket and the bracket satisfies the graded Jacobi identity. The quadratic cone
\mathrm{QC}_{L}
of
L
consists of all
u\in L^{1}
such that
[u,u]=0
. Two DGLAs
L_{1},L_{2}
are quasi-isomorphic if there exists a finite zigzag
L_{1}\rightarrow X_{1}\leftarrow X_{2}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow X_{n}\leftarrow L_{2}
of quasi-isomorphisms between DGLAs.

For a local Artin
\mathbb{C}
-algebra
A\in\mathbf{Atr}_{\mathbb{C}}
, let
\mathfrak{m}_{A}
be its maximal ideal. Then
L\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathfrak{m}_{A}

is a nilpotent DGLA with differential and bracket given respectively by

\displaystyle d(u\otimes a)

\displaystyle=du\otimes a,

\displaystyle[u\otimes a,v\otimes b]

\displaystyle=[u,v]\otimes ab

for
u,v\in L,a,b\in\mathfrak{m}_{A}
. In particular,
L^{0}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathfrak{m}_{A}
is a nilpotent Lie algebra, and we have the corresponding Lie group
\exp(L^{0}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{A})
given by the Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff formula, namely the group multiplication is given by

\displaystyle\exp(\alpha)\exp(\beta)=\exp(\alpha+\beta+\frac{1}{2}[\alpha,\beta]+\frac{1}{12}[\alpha,[\alpha,\beta]]-\frac{1}{12}[\beta,[\alpha,\beta]]+\cdots)

for
\alpha,\beta\in L^{0}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathfrak{m}_{A}
.
We have the following three functors:

•

the exponential functor
\exp_{L}
from
\mathbf{Art}_{\mathbb{C}}
to
\mathbf{Grp}
, the category of groups, defined by

\displaystyle\exp_{L}:A\mapsto\exp(L^{0}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathfrak{m}_{A}),

•

the Maurer–Cartan functor
\mathrm{MC}_{L}
from
\mathbf{Art}_{\mathbb{C}}
to
\mathbf{Set}
, defined by

\displaystyle\mathrm{MC}_{L}:A\mapsto\textrm{MC}(L,A)=\{\alpha\in L^{1}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathfrak{m}_{A}:d\alpha+\frac{1}{2}[\alpha,\alpha]=0\},

•

the deformation functor
\mathrm{Def}_{L}
, which is the quotient functor
\mathrm{MC}_{L}/\exp_{L}
, where
\exp(L^{0}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathfrak{m}_{A})
acts on
L^{1}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathfrak{m}_{A}

as

\displaystyle\exp(\alpha_{0})*\alpha_{1}=\exp(\mathrm{ad}\alpha_{0})\alpha_{1}+\frac{\mathrm{Id}-\exp(\mathrm{ad}\alpha_{0})}{\mathrm{ad}\alpha_{0}}d\alpha_{0}.

It is known that the cohomology spaces
H^{0}(L)
,
H^{1}(L)
, and
H^{2}(L)
characterize the space of infinitesimal automorphisms, the tangent space, and the obstruction space associated to
\mathrm{Def}_{L}
, respectively.
Every morphism
f:L\rightarrow M
of DALAs induces a natural transformation of the associated deformation functors
f_{*}:\mathrm{Def}_{L}\rightarrow\mathrm{Def}_{M}
.
Goldman and Millson showed that if the induced morphism on 0-th cohomology is surjective, on 1-th cohomology is bijective, and on 2-th cohomology is injective, then
f_{*}
is an isomorphism of functors
[
18
, Theorem 2.4]
. In other words, the deformation functor is a 1-homotopy invariant.

Let
\mathfrak{h}
be a Lie algebra over
\mathbb{C}
, which is viewed as a DGLA in degree zero. An
\mathfrak{h}
-augmented DGLA is a pair
(L,\varepsilon)
, where
L
is a DGLA and the augmentation
\varepsilon:L\rightarrow\mathfrak{h}
is a homomorphism of DALAs. Given a local Artin
\mathbb{C}
-algebra
A
, the
\mathfrak{h}
-augmented Deligne–Goldman–Millson groupoid
\mathbb{DGM}_{\mathfrak{h}}(L,\varepsilon,A)
is defined by

•

the set of objects being

\displaystyle\mathrm{Obj}(\mathbb{DGM}_{\mathfrak{h}}(L,\varepsilon,A))=\{(\alpha,\varphi)\in(L^{1}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathfrak{m}_{A})\times\exp(\mathfrak{h}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathfrak{m}_{A}):d\alpha+\frac{1}{2}[\alpha,\alpha]=0\},

•

the set of morphisms between
(\alpha,\varphi)
and
(\beta,\psi)
being

\displaystyle{\rm Hom}((\alpha,\varphi),(\beta,\psi))=\left\{\lambda\in L^{0}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathfrak{m}_{A}:\exp(\lambda)*\alpha=\beta,\exp(\varepsilon(\lambda))\varphi=\psi\right\}.

In particular, if
\mathfrak{h}
is a trivial Lie algebra,
\mathbb{DGM}(L,\varepsilon,A)
reduces to the usual Deligne–Goldman–Millson groupoid
\mathbb{DGM}(L,A)
. In terms of the notations of
[
18
]
, one writes

\mathbb{DGM}_{\mathfrak{h}}(L,\varepsilon,A)=\mathbb{DGM}(L,A)\bowtie\exp(\mathfrak{h}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{A})_{\varepsilon}.

The deformation functor

\mathrm{Def}_{L,\varepsilon}
is defined as

\displaystyle\mathrm{Def}_{L,\varepsilon}:\mathbf{Art}_{\mathbb{C}}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathbf{Set}

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle\longmapsto\mathrm{Iso}{\mathbb{DGM}}_{\mathfrak{h}}(L,\varepsilon,A),

where
\mathrm{Iso}{\mathbb{DGM}}_{\mathfrak{h}}(L,\varepsilon,A)
denotes the set of isomorphism classes in
\mathbb{DGM}{\mathfrak{h}}(L,\varepsilon,A)
.
An
\mathfrak{h}
-augmented DGLA
(L,\varepsilon)
is called formal if there is a finite zigzag

(L,\varepsilon)\rightarrow(L_{1},\varepsilon_{1})\leftarrow(L_{2},\varepsilon_{2})\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow(L_{m},\varepsilon_{m})\leftarrow(H^{\bullet}(L),\varepsilon_{H})

of quasi-isomorphisms between
\mathfrak{h}
-augmented DGLA’s, where
H^{\bullet}(L)
is treated as an
\mathfrak{h}
-augmented DGLA equipped with trivial differentials and the augmentation
\varepsilon_{H}
is the restriction of
\varepsilon
to
H^{0}(L)\subset L^{0}
.
Let
(L,\varepsilon)
be a formal
\mathfrak{h}
-augmented DGLA, where the augmentations
\varepsilon
is surjective and
\varepsilon|_{H}
is injective. Then Goldman and Millson showed that the analytic germ of
\mathrm{QC}_{H^{\bullet}(L)}\times\mathfrak{h}/\varepsilon(H^{0}(L))
pro-represents the functor
A\mapsto\mathrm{Iso}{\mathfrak{DGM}}(L^{\prime},A)
, where
L^{\prime}=\operatorname{Ker}(\varepsilon)
is the augmentation ideal
[
18
, Theorem 3.5]
.

As one of the main applications of the Goldman–Millson theorem, together with the formality due to Deligne, Griffiths, Morgan, and Sullivan
[
12
]
, they showed that if
\rho\in\mathrm{Hom}(\pi_{1}(Y),H)
, where
Y
is a compact Kähler manifold, and
H
is a real algebraic Lie group, such that one of the following conditions is satisfied

•

the image of
\rho
lies in a
compact subgroup of
H
,

•

\rho
is semisimple,

•

H/V
is a classifying space for polarized real Hodge structures, and the principal
H
-bundle associated to
\rho
admits a horizontal holomorphic
V
-reduction,

•

H/V
is a Hermitian symmetric space with automorphism group
H
and the principal
H
-bundle associated to
\rho
admits a holomorphic
V
-reduction,

then the representation variety
\mathrm{Hom}(\pi_{1}(Y),H)
is quadratic at
\rho
. Hence the tangent cone to
\mathrm{Hom}(\pi_{1}(Y),H)
at
\rho
is a quadratic cone,
and
\mathrm{Hom}(\pi_{1}(Y),H)
is locally analytically isomorphic to its tangent cone at
\rho
.

4.2.
DGLAs associated to parabolic logarithmic flat bundles

Let
(E,\mathbf{F},\nabla)
be a parabolic logarithmic flat bundle over
(\bar{X},D)
. We consider the following DGLAs:

The DGLA

,

Definition 4.1
.

A parabolic logarithmic flat bundle

over

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
(E,\mathbf{F},\nabla)
be a parabolic logarithmic flat bundle over
(\bar{X},D)
. Assume that

•

\mathbf{w}
such that
(E,\mathbf{F},\nabla)
has zero degree and is stable with respect to
\mathbf{w}
,

•

at each

, we have

for some constant
\lambda_{i}
.

Then the DGLA
M_{(E,\mathbf{F},\nabla)}
is partially formal.

Proof.

Since the residue of the induced connection on
{\rm End}(E)
at each puncture is zero, we have a sub-DGLA
\tilde{M}_{(E,\mathbf{F},\nabla)}
of
M_{(E,\mathbf{F},\nabla)}
given by

\tilde{M}^{p}_{(E,\mathbf{F},\nabla)}=\Gamma(\bar{X},\mathcal{A}^{p}_{\bar{X}}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\bar{X}}}{\rm End}(E)),\quad p=0,1,2,

with differentials
d_{\nabla}
and brackets induced from the Lie bracket on
{\rm End}(E)
.
Due to Simpson correspondence, there is a harmonic metric
h
on
E|_{X}
, then the induced harmonic metric
h
on
{\rm End}(E)|_{X}
can be extended to the entire
{\rm End}(E)
.
By classical Hodge theory, we have the quasi-isomorphisms of DGLAs (see
[
43
, Lemma 2.2]
)

\displaystyle(\tilde{M}^{\bullet}_{(E,\mathbf{F},\nabla)},d_{\nabla})\leftarrow(\operatorname{Ker}(D^{\prime\prime}|_{\tilde{M}^{\bullet}_{(E,\mathbf{F},\nabla)}}),D^{\prime})\rightarrow(H^{\bullet}(\tilde{M}_{(E,\mathbf{F},\nabla)}),0),

where

\displaystyle D^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(d_{\nabla}+\delta^{\prime\prime}_{h}-\delta^{\prime}_{h}),

\displaystyle D^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(d_{\nabla}+\delta^{\prime}_{h}-\delta^{\prime\prime}_{h})

with
\delta^{\prime}_{h},\delta^{\prime\prime}_{h}
given by, respectively,

\displaystyle\bar{\partial}h(u,v)

\displaystyle=h(\bar{\partial}_{E}u,v)+h(u,\delta^{\prime}_{h}v),

\displaystyle\partial h(u,v)

\displaystyle=h(\nabla u,v)+h(u,\delta^{\prime\prime}_{h}v)

for

.
Hence the DGLA
\tilde{M}_{(E,\mathbf{F},\nabla)}
is formal, which means that the DGLA

is partially formal.
∎

5.
Moduli spaces of weighted parabolic representation pairs

Consider the flag variety
\mathrm{FL}(r,\overrightarrow{d})

of

and

is determined by a subset

and the root subgroups

corresponding to the root

is given by

\displaystyle X(P_{\mathcal{F}})=\{\delta\in X(T_{\mathcal{F}}):\langle\delta,\upsilon^{\vee}\rangle=0\textrm{ for all }\upsilon\in I\},

where
\upsilon^{\vee}\in Y(T_{\mathcal{F}})
denotes the coroot corresponding to
\upsilon
.
Let
\delta:P_{\mathcal{F}}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{*}
be a character of
P_{\mathcal{F}}
. We define an action of

on the product

by

\displaystyle h\cdot(g,c)=(gh^{-1},\delta(h)c),

then we have a quotient

L_{\delta}=(G\times\mathbb{C})/P_{\mathcal{F}}
, which maps to

via

\pi:L_{\delta}\longrightarrow\mathrm{FL}(r,\overrightarrow{d})\simeq G/P_{\mathcal{F}}.

The group

via

. One easily checks that the projection
\pi

-linearized line bundle over
\mathrm{FL}(r,\overrightarrow{d})
.

The following proposition is well-known

]

Proposition 5.1
.

L_{\delta}
is ample if and only if
\langle\delta,\upsilon^{\vee}\rangle>0
for all

For our case

, the Levi subgroup

of

\displaystyle h

\displaystyle\longmapsto\det(\mathfrak{l}_{0}(h))^{-w_{0}}\cdots\det(\mathfrak{l}_{s}(h))^{-w_{s}}.

For the multiple flag variety

\mathfrak{PRP}(r,\mathbf{d})

Proposition 5.2
.

Given a weight system
\mathbf{w}=\{\overrightarrow{w^{(i)}}\}_{i=1,\cdots,n}
, where
\overrightarrow{w^{(i)}}=(w^{(i)}_{0},\cdots,w^{(i)}_{s_{i}})\in\mathbb{Z}^{s_{i}+1}
satisfies
w^{(i)}_{0}<\cdots<w^{(i)}_{s_{i}}
. A weighted parabolic representation pair
((\rho,\mathbf{P}),\mathbf{w})
of degree zero is semistable if and only if it is GIT-semistable in
\mathfrak{PRP}(r,\mathbf{d})
with respect to the
G
-linearized ample line bundle
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{w}}
.

Proof.

Consider a one-parameter subgroup
\lambda:\mathbb{C}^{*}\rightarrow G
, then the vector space
V
decomposes into the direct sum of weight spaces for the action of
\lambda
, namely

\displaystyle V=\bigoplus\limits_{N\in\mathbb{N}}V^{N},

where

\displaystyle\lambda(c)\cdot v=c^{N}v

for every
v\in V^{N}
. We write
V^{\geq N}=\bigoplus\limits_{M\geq N}V^{M}
. For any
\omega\in\Gamma
, the map
\rho(\omega)
also decomposes into the direct sum of linear maps between weight spaces, namely

\displaystyle\rho(\omega)=\bigoplus\limits_{M,N\in\mathbb{N}}\rho(\omega)^{MN},

where
\rho(\omega)^{MN}
is the compositions of the embedding
\iota^{M}:V^{M}\hookrightarrow V
, the linear map
\rho(\omega):V\rightarrow V
, and the projection
\rho^{N}:V\rightarrow V^{N}
.

Thus we have

\displaystyle\lambda(c)\cdot\rho(\omega)=\mathrm{Ad}_{\lambda(c)}\rho(\omega)=\bigoplus\limits_{M,N\in\mathbb{N}}c^{N-M}\rho(\omega)^{MN}.

If the limit
\lim\limits_{c\rightarrow 0}\lambda(c)\cdot\rho(\omega)
exists, we must have
\rho(\omega)^{MN}=0
whenever
N<M
. It follows that each
V^{\geq N}
is a subrepresentation of
\rho
. On the other hand, if the limit
\lim\limits_{c\rightarrow 0}\lambda(c)\cdot\mathbf{P}
exists, we must have
V^{N}=\{0\}
for
N<0
. Then we calculate the Mumford weight
\mu^{\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{w}}}_{\lambda}(\rho,\mathbf{P})
of the
\lambda
-action on
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{w}}
as follows (cf.
[
45
, Proposition 3.7]
)

\displaystyle\mu^{\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{w}}}_{\lambda}(\rho,\mathbf{P})=-\sum_{N\geq 0}N\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{\ell=0}^{s_{i}}w^{(i)}_{\ell}\dim_{\mathbb{C}}(V^{(i)}_{\ell}\bigcap V^{N})/(V^{(i)}_{\ell+1}\bigcap V^{N}).

Since
((\rho,\mathbf{P}),\mathbf{w})
is of zero degree and semistable, each summand with respect to the index
N
is non-positive. Consequently,

\displaystyle\mu^{\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{w}}}_{\lambda}(\rho,\mathbf{P})\geq 0,

so
(\rho,\mathbf{P})
is GIT-semistable in
\mathfrak{PRP}(r,\mathbf{d})
with respect to
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{w}}
. Conversely, the non-negativity of the Mumford weight
\mu^{\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{w}}}_{\lambda}(\rho,\mathbf{P})
for a one-parameter subgroup
\lambda:\mathbb{C}^{*}\rightarrow G
corresponding to a proper subrepresentation immediately implies that
((\rho,\mathbf{P}),\mathbf{w})
is semistable.
∎

\overrightarrow{d^{\prime}_{Q}}
we have
\sum\limits_{v\in Q_{0}}w_{v}d^{\prime}_{v}\leq 0
) form an open
\mathrm{GL}(\overrightarrow{d_{Q}})
-invariant subvariety

\mathfrak{REP}^{\overrightarrow{w_{Q}}\text{-}\mathrm{ss}}(Q,\overrightarrow{d_{Q}})\subset\mathfrak{REP}(Q,\overrightarrow{d}).

By geometric invariant theory for quiver representations, there exists a good categorical GIT quotient
[
28
]

q:\mathfrak{REP}^{\overrightarrow{w_{Q}}\text{-}\mathrm{ss}}(Q,\overrightarrow{d_{Q}})\longrightarrow\mathfrak{REP}(Q,\overrightarrow{d})/\!\!/\mathrm{GL}(\overrightarrow{d_{Q}}).

Proposition 5.6
.

Let
(\rho,\mathbf{P})\in\mathfrak{PRP}(r,\mathbf{d})
and
x=(x_{a})_{a\in Q_{1}}\in\varphi^{-1}(\rho,\mathbf{P})
. Then
(\rho,\mathbf{P})
is semistable with respect to
\mathbf{w}
if and only if
x
is semistable with respect to
\overrightarrow{w_{Q}}
. In particular,

\varphi^{-1}(\mathfrak{PRP}^{\mathbf{w}\text{-}\mathrm{ss}}(r,\mathbf{d}))=\mathfrak{REP}^{\overrightarrow{w_{Q}}\text{-}\mathrm{ss}}_{\mathrm{prp}}(Q,\overrightarrow{d_{Q}}):=\mathfrak{REP}_{\mathrm{prp}}(Q,\overrightarrow{d_{Q}})\bigcap\mathfrak{REP}^{\overrightarrow{w_{Q}}\text{-}\mathrm{ss}}(Q,\overrightarrow{d_{Q}}).

Proof.

It is clear that the semistability of
x
implies the semistability of
(\rho,\mathbf{P})
. Conversely, we only need to note that for a proper subrepresentation with dimension vector
\overrightarrow{d^{\prime}_{Q}}
, we have the inequalities

\displaystyle d^{\prime}_{u^{(i)}_{\ell+1}}\leq\dim_{\mathbb{C}}(\iota^{(i)}_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{C}^{d_{u^{(i)}_{\ell+1}}})\bigcap V^{\prime}_{u^{(i)}_{\ell}})

for
1\leq\ell\leq s_{i}
and
1\leq i\leq n
, where
\iota^{(i)}_{\ell}:\mathbb{C}^{d_{u^{(i)}_{\ell}}}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{d_{u^{(i)}_{\ell-1}}}
denotes the chosen embedding, and
V^{\prime}_{u^{(i)}_{\ell}}
is a subspace of
\mathbb{C}^{d_{u^{(i)}_{\ell-1}}}
arising from the subrepresentation. Since
w_{u_{\ell}^{(i)}}>0
for all
1\leq\ell\leq s_{i}
and
1\leq i\leq n
, the semistability of
(\rho,\mathbf{P})
guarantees the the semistability of
x
.
∎

Corollary 5.7
.

Assume
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\sum\limits_{\ell=0}^{s_{i}}w_{\ell}^{(i)}d_{\ell}^{(i)}=0
. There is a good categorical GIT quotient

such that the following diagram commutes:

@V{\varphi}V{}V@V{\simeq}V{}V\\
\mathfrak{PRP}^{\mathbf{w}\text{-}\mathrm{ss}}(r,\mathbf{d})@>{q}>{}>\mathfrak{PRP}(r,\mathbf{d})/\!\!/_{\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{w}}}G\end{CD}.

As an application of the correspondence between semistable parabolic representation pairs and semistable representations of the quiver
Q
, we obtain the following Kobayashi–Hitchin-type theorem.

Theorem 5.8
.

Given a rational weight system
\mathbf{w}
, a parabolic representation pair
(\rho,\mathbf{P})\in\mathfrak{PRP}(r,\mathbf{d})
is polystable with respect to
\mathbf{w}
if and only if there exists a
\mathbf{w}
-good Hermitian metric
h
on
V
, namely the following equations are satisfied

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\sum_{j=1}^{g}([\rho(\alpha_{j}),\rho^{*_{h}}(\alpha_{j})]+[\rho(\beta_{j}),\rho^{*_{h}}(\beta_{j})])+\sum_{i=1}^{n}[\rho(\gamma_{i}),\rho^{*_{h}}(\gamma_{i})]+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}{p_{h}}^{(i)}_{1}\\
&\hskip 138.0pt=\ (\frac{1}{r}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\sum\limits_{\ell=0}^{s_{i}}w_{\ell}^{(i)}d_{\ell}^{(i)}-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}w_{0}^{(i)})\mathrm{Id},\\[3.0pt]
&[\rho(\gamma_{i})|_{V^{(i)}_{\ell}},(\rho(\gamma_{i})|_{V^{(i)}_{\ell}})^{*_{h}}]+{p_{h}}^{(i)}_{\ell}=\ (w^{(i)}_{\ell-1}-w^{(i)}_{\ell}+1)\mathrm{Id},\quad 1\leq\ell\leq s_{i},1\leq i\leq n,\end{aligned}\right.

where
\rho^{*_{h}}(\omega)
and
{p_{h}}^{(i)}_{\ell}
denote the conjugate of
\rho(\omega)
and the orthogonal projection of
V^{(i)}_{\ell-1}
onto
V^{(i)}_{\ell}
, respectively, with respect to the metric
h
. Moreover, if
h^{\prime}
is another
\mathbf{w}
-good Hermitian metric on
V
, then there exists
g\in\bigcap\mathbf{P}
such that

\displaystyle h(u,v)=h^{\prime}(g\cdot u,g\cdot v)

for any
u,v\in V
.

Proof.

Applying King’s result (cf.
[
28
, Section 6]
or
[
11
]
) to ploystable representations of the quiver
Q
, there exist a Hermitian metric
h
on the vector space
\mathbb{C}^{r}
and Hermitian metrics
h^{(i)}_{\ell}
on the vector spaces
\mathbb{C}^{d_{u^{(i)}_{\ell}}}
for
1\leq\ell\leq s_{i}
and
1\leq i\leq n
such that the following equations hold:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\sum_{j=1}^{g}([\rho(\alpha_{j}),\rho^{*_{h}}(\alpha_{j})]+[\rho(\beta_{j}),\rho^{*_{h}}(\beta_{j})])+\sum_{i=1}^{n}[\rho(\gamma_{i}),\rho^{*_{h}}(\gamma_{i})]+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\iota^{(i)}_{1}(\iota^{(i)}_{1})^{*_{h,h^{(i)}_{1}}}\\
&\hskip 173.0pt=\ (\frac{1}{r}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\sum\limits_{\ell=0}^{s_{i}}w_{\ell}^{(i)}d_{\ell}^{(i)}-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}w_{0}^{(i)}\mathrm{Id},\\
&[\rho(\gamma_{i})|_{V^{(i)}_{\ell}},(\rho(\gamma_{i})|_{V^{(i)}_{\ell}})^{*_{h^{(i)}_{\ell}}}]+\iota^{(i)}_{\ell+1}(\iota^{(i)}_{\ell+1})^{*_{h^{(i)}_{\ell},h^{(i)}_{\ell+1}}}-(\iota^{(i)}_{\ell})^{*_{h^{(i)}_{\ell-1},h^{(i)}_{\ell}}}\iota^{(i)}_{\ell}\\
&\hskip 173.0pt=\ (w^{(i)}_{\ell-1}-w^{(i)}_{\ell})\mathrm{Id},\quad 1\leq\ell\leq s_{i},1\leq i\leq n,\end{aligned}\right.

where
(\iota^{(i)}_{\ell})^{*_{h^{(i)}_{\ell-1},h^{(i)}_{\ell}}}:\mathbb{C}^{d_{u^{(i)}_{\ell-1}}}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{d_{u^{(i)}_{\ell}}}
denotes the conjugate of
\iota^{(i)}_{\ell}
with respect to the metrics
h^{(i)}_{\ell-1}
and
h^{(i)}_{\ell}
.

Now, taking
\iota^{(i)}_{\ell}
to be the natural embedding, then the metrics
h^{(i)}_{\ell}
are given by the restriction of the metric
h
on the corresponding subspaces. Hence, the above equations simplify accordingly.
∎

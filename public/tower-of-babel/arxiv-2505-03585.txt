Decision Making under Model Misspecification: DRO with Robust Bayesian Ambiguity Sets
Charita Dellaporta, Patrick O’Hara, Theodoros Damoulas

Abstract
Distributionally Robust Optimisation (DRO) protects risk-averse decision-makers by considering the worst-case risk within an ambiguity set of distributions based on the empirical distribution or a model. To further guard against finite, noisy data, model-based approaches admit Bayesian formulations that propagate uncertainty from the posterior to the decision-making problem. However, when the model is misspecified, the decision maker must stretch the ambiguity set to contain the data-generating process (DGP), leading to overly conservative decisions. We address this challenge by introducing DRO with Robust, to model-misspecification, Bayesian Ambiguity Sets (DRO-RoBAS). These are Maximum Mean Discrepancy ambiguity sets centred at a robust posterior predictive distribution that incorporates beliefs about the DGP. We show that the resulting optimisation problem obtains a dual formulation in the Reproducing Kernel Hilbert Space and we give probabilistic guarantees on the tolerance level of the ambiguity set. Our method outperforms other Bayesian and empirical DRO approaches in out-of-sample performance on the Newsvendor and Portfolio problems with various cases of model misspecification.

Introduction

Decision-makers frequently encounter the challenge of optimising under uncertainty since the data-generating process (DGP) is not fully known. As a result, they must rely on available data and model families to estimate the DGP within the optimisation objective. However, the data may be noisy, the data distribution might change with time, or the model might be misspecified or poorly fitted, leading to
distributional uncertainty
. Decision-making in this setting is a critical challenge in various applications such as inventory planning
(
Black, 2022
)
, portfolio optimisation
(
Li & Kwon, 2013
)
and distribution shifts in machine learning applications
(
Zhang et al., 2020
)
.

Figure 1:
Illustration of (approximated) BAS
PE
, BAS
PP
and RoBAS (ours) with
{\mathbb{P}}_{\theta}={\cal N}(\mu,\sigma^{2})
over a grid of
(\mu,\sigma)
pairs for a fixed
\epsilon
. In the well-specified case (left), all ambiguity sets include the DGP while RoBAS covers a slightly bigger area than BAS

and BAS

. For a contaminated dataset (right) RoBAS continues to contain the DGP and maintains a similar area, whereas the BAS formulations exclude it and cover a much larger area of distributions further away from the DGP.

A risk-averse decision-maker might choose to hedge against distributional uncertainty by finding the decision that minimises the
worst-case
risk over a set of distributions. This worst-case protection is at the heart of
Distributionally Robust Optimisation (DRO)
that defines an ambiguity set of distributions with respect to an estimator of the DGP. This can be fully data-driven, using the empirical measure of the observations
(
Kuhn et al., 2019
;
Staib & Jegelka, 2019
;
Zhu et al., 2021
;
Hu & Hong, 2013
, see e.g.)
, or model-based when expert knowledge is available to fit a model to available data, resulting in a model-based estimator for the DGP
(
Iyengar et al., 2023
;
Michel et al., 2021
;
Michel et al., 2022
)
. Both approaches are sensitive to the choice of DGP estimator and additional estimation error might exist in model-based DRO due to a poor fit or model uncertainty.

To overcome this, recently developed Bayesian formulations of DRO use posterior beliefs to inform the optimisation problem
(
Shapiro et al., 2023
)
or the ambiguity set itself
(
Dellaporta et al., 2024
)
. However, these methods inherit the sensitivity of Bayesian posteriors to model misspecification
(
Grünwald, 2012
;
Walker, 2013
, see e.g.)
.
A key goal in DRO methodology is to choose the size of the ambiguity set such that the DGP falls within it with high probability, as illustrated in Fig.
1
(left) for the two formulations of Bayesian Ambiguity Sets (BAS)
(
Dellaporta et al., 2024
)
and our proposed Robust BAS (RoBAS).
If the estimate is not accurate - for example, when the model is misspecified - then a much larger size will be required to contain the DGP as illustrated in Fig.
1
(right).
The price to pay for this large size is the inclusion of many probability distributions that are unlikely to occur, and which could be very pessimistic with respect to the objective function, leading to an overly conservative decision.
If the decision maker wrongly assumes the model is well-specified and incorrectly chooses an overly optimistic ambiguity set size, then the DGP may not lie in the set, and the decision could be overly optimistic compared to the out-of-sample outcome, often referred to as the optimiser’s curse
(
Kuhn et al., 2019
)
.

Uncertainty over the DGP is extensively studied outside stochastic optimisation. Consider a parametric model
{\mathbb{P}}_{\theta}
, indexed by the parameter of interest
\theta
. In the Bayesian framework, uncertainty about the parameter is typically expressed directly through prior beliefs. However, recent work in robust Bayesian inference by
Lyddon et al. (2018)
takes a different approach: uncertainty about the parameter is now induced by uncertainty, in the form of prior beliefs, in the DGP. This concept lies at the core of the Bayesian Nonparametric Learning (NPL) framework
(
Lyddon et al., 2018
;
Fong et al., 2019
)
which relaxes the well-specified model assumption imposed by standard Bayesian inference.
In this spirit, we approach the challenge of DRO under model misspecification by extending the recently proposed DRO-BAS
(
Dellaporta et al., 2024
)
framework to tackle model misspecification through a robust NPL posterior coupled with the Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD) inside the ambiguity set; thus introducing DRO with Robust Bayesian Ambiguity Sets (DRO-RoBAS). While DRO-BAS targets distributional uncertainty with respect to the DGP, the robustness offered is not sufficient under model misspecification, see Fig.
1
.

2
Background

Let
x\in{\cal X}\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
be a decision-making variable for the cost function
f:{\cal X}\times\Xi\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
with data space
\Xi\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{D}
. Let
\{\xi_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}\simiid{\mathbb{P}}^{\star}
be observations from the DGP
{\mathbb{P}}^{\star}\in{\cal P}(\Xi)
, where
{\cal P}(\Xi)
denotes the space of Borel probability measures on
\Xi
. Furthermore, consider a parametric model family
{\cal P}_{\Theta}:=\{{\mathbb{P}}_{\theta}:\theta\in\Theta\}\subset{\cal P}(\Xi)
indexed by parameter of interest
\theta\in\Theta\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{s}
. We say the model is
misspecified
if
{\mathbb{P}}^{\star}\notin{\cal P}_{\Theta}
. DRO methods construct an ambiguity set
{\cal A}
, based on an estimator of
{\mathbb{P}}^{\star}
, called the
nominal distribution
, and minimise the worst-case expected cost over
{\cal A}
.

The DRO literature typically categorises ambiguity sets into two classes
(
Rahimian & Mehrotra, 2019
)
:
moment-based
and
discrepancy-based
.
The former contain distributions that satisfy constraints on the moments of
{\mathbb{P}}^{\star}
, without necessarily considering an estimator.
In contrast, discrepancy-based ambiguity sets consist of distributions close to the nominal according to a specified discrepancy measure. Examples include Integral Probability Metrics (IPMs)
(
Husain et al., 2022
)
, such as the Wasserstein distance
(
Kuhn et al., 2019
)
and the MMD
(
Staib & Jegelka, 2019
)
, as well as

\phi
-divergences like the Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence
(
Hu & Hong, 2013
)
.
Regardless of the choice of ambiguity set, the resulting minimax problem can be seen as a game between the decision maker who chooses
x
and an adversary who chooses the worst-case distribution in
{\cal A}
:

\displaystyle\min_{x\in{\cal X}}~{\sup_{{\mathbb{P}}\in{\cal A}}~{{\mathbb{E}}_{\xi\sim{\mathbb{P}}}[f_{x}(\xi)]}}

(1)

where
f_{x}(\xi):=f(x,\xi)
. Although most discrepancy-based DRO methods are fully empirical, i.e. the estimator is obtained only via
\xi_{1:n}
, sometimes, such as in regression settings, the decision-maker needs to model the variables’ relationship via a model family
{\cal P}_{\Theta}
, describing the DGP. Model-based DRO methods
(
Iyengar et al., 2023
;
Michel et al., 2021
;
Michel et al., 2022
, e.g.)
use the observations to obtain an estimator
{\mathbb{P}}_{\hat{\theta}}\in{\cal P}_{\Theta}
and use this as the nominal distribution. Thus, a poorly chosen
{\mathbb{P}}_{\hat{\theta}}
far from
{\mathbb{P}}^{\star}
(in some distance sense) requires a large
{\cal A}
, leading to overly pessimistic decisions. This has led to Bayesian formulations of DRO which propagate uncertainty about
\theta
in the optimisation problem.

2.1
Bayesian Formulations of DRO

Shapiro et al. (2023)
introduced
Bayesian DRO (
BDRO
)
which defines an
expected worst-case
risk objective:

\displaystyle\min_{x\in{\cal X}}~{\color[rgb]{1,0.4961,0}{\mathbb{E}}_{\Pi(\theta\mid\xi_{1:n})}}~{\left[\sup_{\displaystyle\color[rgb]{1,0.4961,0}{{\mathbb{P}}:d_{\text{KL}}({\mathbb{P}}||{\mathbb{P}}_{\theta})\leq\epsilon}}~{{\mathbb{E}}_{\xi\sim{\mathbb{P}}}\left[f_{x}(\xi)\right]}\right]}

(2)

where
\Pi(\theta\mid\xi_{1:n})
denotes the parameter posterior distribution for model family
{\cal P}_{\Theta}
. However, risk-averse decision-makers are interested in worst-case risk formulations. For this reason,
Dellaporta et al. (2024)
proposed two formulations of the
DRO with Bayesian Ambiguity Sets (DRO-BAS)
that correspond to a
worst-case
optimisation problem with ambiguity sets informed by the standard Bayesian posterior. In particular, they defined
DRO-BAS
PP
:

\displaystyle\min_{x\in{\cal X}}~\sup_{\color[rgb]{0.3008,0.6875,0.2891}\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}:d_{\text{KL}}\left({\mathbb{P}}||{\mathbb{E}}_{\Pi(\theta\mid\xi_{1:n})}[{\mathbb{P}}_{\theta}]\right)\leq\epsilon}~{{\mathbb{E}}_{\xi\sim{\mathbb{P}}}\left[f_{x}(\xi)\right]},

(3)

based on a KL-based ambiguity set with nominal distribution the posterior predictive, and
DRO-BAS
PE
:

\displaystyle\min_{x\in{\cal X}}~\sup_{\color[rgb]{0.5977,0.3047,0.6406}\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}:{\mathbb{E}}_{\Pi(\theta\mid\xi_{1:n})}[d_{\text{KL}}({\mathbb{P}}||{\mathbb{P}}_{\theta})]\leq\epsilon}~{{\mathbb{E}}_{\xi\sim{\mathbb{P}}}\left[f_{x}(\xi)\right]}.

(4)

which considers the expected KL under the posterior distribution.
The authors showcased improved out-of-sample robustness compared to BDRO in a number of exponential family models. Although DRO-BAS in the standard Bayesian setting offers an intuitive, posterior-informed ambiguity set, it can be severely affected by model misspecification. Indeed, BAS
PE
only considers probability measures
{\mathbb{P}}
that are absolutely continuous with respect to
{\mathbb{P}}_{\theta}
(denoted by
{\mathbb{P}}\ll{\mathbb{P}}_{\theta}
) and also admit an expected KL divergence close enough to
{\mathbb{P}}_{\theta}
.
Since the expectation is informed by the posterior, a non-robust posterior will likely lie far away from the DGP.
Similarly, BAS
PP
considers probability measures
{\mathbb{P}}
that admit small KL-divergence with respect to
{\mathbb{P}}_{n}^{\text{pred}}
, where
{\mathbb{P}}_{n}^{\text{pred}}:={\mathbb{E}}_{\theta\sim\Pi(\theta\mid\xi_{1:n})}[{\mathbb{P}}_{\theta}]
and
{\mathbb{P}}\ll{\mathbb{P}}_{n}^{\text{pred}}
. Hence, the sensitivity of the Bayesian posterior will propagate to the posterior predictive and the resulting ambiguity set. A similar observation was made by
Shapiro et al. (2023)
for the BDRO method in the misspecified case.

To remedy this, we exploit the flexibility of the DRO-BAS framework which allows us to choose a different posterior distribution and discrepancy measure, suitable for model misspecification. The notion of targeting a different discrepancy measure, other than the KL divergence, to induce robustness in the Bayesian posterior has been well established in the Bayesian inference literature. The NPL posterior
(
Lyddon et al., 2018
;
Fong et al., 2019
)
was introduced to, among others, remedy the sensitivity of Bayesian inference to model misspecification by removing the assumption that the model is correct. This is done by setting uncertainty, via nonparametric prior beliefs, directly on the DGP rather than on the parameter of interest.
Incorporating DGP uncertainty in decision-making has also been considered by
Wang et al. (2023)
who explored a nonparametric Dirichlet Process (DP) model for the DGP. Unlike the current paper, this work is not suited for parametric models and considers a weighted objective, with only one counterpart corresponding to a worst-case risk. We focus on decision-making under
parametric
models, which are especially useful for interpretability in decision-making, while also incorporating
nonparametric
prior beliefs about the DGP.

To achieve this, we leverage the work of
Dellaporta et al. (2022)
who extended the NPL posterior to discrepancy-based loss functions and showed robustness guarantees when the Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD) is used. Using an MMD-based loss allows us to also employ the MMD to construct the ambiguity set. NPL is a natural choice for the DRO-BAS framework as distributional uncertainty in DRO stems directly from uncertainty in the DGP.

2.2
Robust NPL Posterior

In this work, we propose an alternative formulation of DRO-BAS, based on the robust NPL posterior introduced by
Lyddon et al. (2018)
;
Fong et al. (2019)
. We introduce a DP prior
{\mathbb{Q}}\sim\DP(\alpha,{\mathbb{F}})
on the DGP
{\mathbb{P}}^{\star}
where
\alpha>0
and
{\mathbb{F}}\in{\cal P}(\Xi)
. Here,
{\mathbb{F}}
represents our prior beliefs about the DGP and the concentration parameter
\alpha
dictates the strength of the beliefs with
\alpha=0
representing a non-informative prior. To see this, note that given data
\xi_{1:n}
, the posterior is

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\mathbb{Q}}~|~\xi_{1:n}&\sim\DP(\alpha^{\prime},{\mathbb{F}}^{\prime}),\\
\alpha^{\prime}:=\alpha+n,\quad{\mathbb{F}}^{\prime}&:=\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+n}{\mathbb{F}}+\frac{n}{\alpha+n}{\mathbb{P}}_{n}\end{split}

(5)

where
{\mathbb{P}}_{n}:=\frac{1}{n}~\sum_{i=1}^{n}~{\delta_{\xi_{i}}}
is the empirical measure and
\delta_{\xi}
denotes the Dirac measure at
\xi\in\Xi
. For
\alpha=0
, the DP posterior is centred directly on
{\mathbb{P}}_{n}
. If
{\mathbb{P}}^{\star}
was known, we could directly compute:

\displaystyle\theta_{L}({\mathbb{P}}^{\star}):=\argmin_{\theta\in\Theta}{\mathbb{E}}_{\xi\sim{\mathbb{P}}^{\star}}[L(\xi;\theta)]

(6)

where
L:\Xi\times\Theta\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
denotes any loss function. Note that this NPL objective does not assume that the model is well-specified but simply looks for the most likely value of
\theta
under the expectation of the DGP or, equivalently, the parameter value that best describes the data under the candidate model. Since
{\mathbb{P}}^{\star}
is unknown and we instead have a nonparametric posterior over it, we can propagate our posterior beliefs to the parameter of interest through the push-forward measure
(\theta_{L})_{\#}(\DP(\alpha^{\prime},{\mathbb{F}}^{\prime}))
to give a posterior
\Pi_{\text{NPL}}
on
\Theta
. Sampling from this posterior can be done through the Posterior Bootstrap
(
Fong et al., 2019
)
: For B posterior bootstrap iterations, at iteration
j\in[B]
:

1.

Sample
{\mathbb{Q}}^{(j)}
from the posterior
\DP(\alpha^{\prime},{\mathbb{F}}^{\prime})
.

2.

Compute
\theta^{(j)}=\theta_{L}({\mathbb{Q}}^{(j)})
where
\theta_{L}(\cdot)
as in (
6
).

Dellaporta et al. (2022)
suggested using a discrepancy-based loss function in (
6
) which we introduce below.

2.3
Maximum Mean Discrepancy

The MMD belongs to the family of IPMs
(
Müller, 1997
)
.
Let
\mathcal{H}_{k}
be a Reproducing Kernel Hilbert Space (RKHS), for kernel
k:\Xi\times\Xi\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
and norm
\|\cdot\|_{k}
. For
{\cal P}_{k}(\Xi):=\{{\mathbb{P}}\in{\cal P}(\Xi):\int_{\Xi}\sqrt{k(\xi,\xi)}{\mathbb{P}}(d\xi)<\infty\}
, the MMD between
{\mathbb{P}},{\mathbb{Q}}\in{\cal P}_{k}(\Xi)
is defined as:

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}_{k}({\mathbb{P}},{\mathbb{Q}}):=\sup_{f\in\mathcal{H}_{k},\|f\|_{k}\leq 1}\left|{\mathbb{E}}_{{\mathbb{P}}}[f(\xi)]-{\mathbb{E}}_{{\mathbb{Q}}}[f(\xi)]\right|.

(7)

Dellaporta et al. (2022)
define the NPL target in (
6
) as:

\displaystyle\theta_{k}({\mathbb{P}}^{\star}):=\argmin_{\theta\in{\mathbb{R}}^{s}}\mathbb{D}_{k}({\mathbb{P}}^{\star},{\mathbb{P}}_{\theta}).

(8)

One of the attractive properties of the MMD is that the supremum in (
7
) can be obtained in closed form as

\begin{split}\mathbb{D}_{k}^{2}({\mathbb{P}},{\mathbb{Q}})&={\mathbb{E}}_{\xi,\xi^{\prime}\sim{\mathbb{P}}}[k(\xi,\xi^{\prime})]-2{\mathbb{E}}_{\xi\sim{\mathbb{P}},\xi^{\prime}\sim{\mathbb{Q}}}[k(\xi,\xi^{\prime})]\\
&\quad+{\mathbb{E}}_{\xi,\xi^{\prime}\sim{\mathbb{Q}}}[k(\xi,\xi^{\prime})]\end{split}

(9)

and can be approximated via sampling
(
Gretton et al., 2012
, see e.g.)
.
The resulting NPL posterior with the MMD is called an NPL-MMD posterior.

We explore the gains of using the MMD both in the robust NPL posterior and in the Bayesian ambiguity set. Since the NPL-MMD posterior will target the point in the model family closest (w.r.t. the MMD) to
{\mathbb{P}}^{\star}
, the MMD-based ambiguity set may require a smaller radius to include
{\mathbb{P}}^{\star}
, resulting in less conservative decisions.
The MMD has previously been used as a distance metric in the DRO context by
Staib & Jegelka (2019)
who considered the ambiguity set
{\cal B}^{k}_{\epsilon}({\mathbb{P}}_{n}):=\{{\mathbb{P}}\in{\cal P}_{k}(\Xi):\mathbb{D}_{k}({\mathbb{Q}},{\mathbb{P}}_{n})\leq\epsilon\}
where
\epsilon>0
is the radius of the MMD ball,

k
is the kernel and
{\mathbb{P}}_{n}:=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\delta_{\xi_{i}}
is the empirical measure of the observations
\xi_{1:n}
. The same ambiguity set forms a special case of Kernel DRO which was introduced by
Zhu et al. (2021)
.
Chen et al. (2022)
generalised this to conditional kernel DRO, leveraging conditional distributions and
Romao et al. (2023)
explored this framework for dynamic programming.

3
DRO With Robust Bayesian Ambiguity Sets

We propose a robust version of DRO-BAS
PP
(
3
) via the MMD and the NPL-MMD posterior predictive defined as:

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}_{n}^{\text{pred(NPL)}}:={\mathbb{E}}_{{\mathbb{Q}}\sim\DP(c^{\prime},{\mathbb{F}}^{\prime})}[{\mathbb{P}}_{\theta_{k}({\mathbb{Q}})}].

(10)

Notice that contrary to the standard Bayesian posterior predictive,
{\mathbb{P}}_{n}^{\text{pred(NPL)}}
is defined through marginalisation over the nonparametric posterior over the DGP.
Since the MMD can be approximated only via samples (Section
2.3
), a closed-form density for the predictive in (
10
) is not required.
We define the following
Robust Bayesian Ambiguity Set (
RoBAS
)
with the NPL Posterior Predictive:

\displaystyle{\cal B}^{k}_{\epsilon}({\mathbb{P}}_{n}^{\text{pred(NPL)}}):=\{{\mathbb{P}}\in{\cal P}_{k}(\Xi):\mathbb{D}_{k}({\mathbb{P}},{\mathbb{P}}_{n}^{\text{pred(NPL)}})\leq\epsilon\}.

Note that
{\cal B}^{k}_{\epsilon}({\mathbb{P}}_{n}^{\text{pred(NPL)}})
forms a ball when
k
is a characteristic kernel, as this makes the MMD a probability metric. This property is desirable as it guarantees that the MMD will be zero if and only if
{\mathbb{P}}\equiv{\mathbb{P}}_{n}^{\text{pred(NPL)}}
.
We hence obtain the following
DRO-RoBAS
worst-case risk problem:

\displaystyle\min_{x\in{\cal X}}\sup_{\displaystyle\color[rgb]{0.2148,0.4922,0.7227}{\mathbb{P}}\in{\cal B}_{\epsilon}^{k}({\mathbb{P}}_{n}^{\text{pred(NPL)}})}{\mathbb{E}}_{\xi\sim{\mathbb{P}}}[f_{x}(\xi)]

(11)

Similarly to DRO-BAS, this optimisation problem corresponds to a worst-case risk over a set of probability measures informed by posterior beliefs. Posterior beliefs about
\theta
are obtained
via posterior beliefs about the DGP
and the map
\theta_{k}
in (
8
). Since the goal of DRO is to target uncertainty about the DGP, the NPL posterior is a natural choice to inform the ambiguity set as it takes into account any prior beliefs about the DGP. Moreover, by targeting the MMD, rather than the KL divergence as in the BAS case, RoBAS is
not
restricted to probability measures that are absolutely continuous with respect to
{\mathbb{P}}_{\theta}
.

Intuitively, RoBAS is expected to be a better-informed ambiguity set than BAS when the model is misspecified since it is informed by a robust posterior predictive and a robust discrepancy measure.
This is better understood through a toy example.
Fig.
2
shows a Gaussian location model in the presence of outliers.
The top panel shows the DGP of the training data contaminated with 20% of outliers along with a pathological model
{\mathbb{P}}_{\text{pathological}}
case with a mean larger than that of the DGP.
In the BAS
PE
case, the expected KL from the DGP to the model is significantly larger than that from the pathological model
{\mathbb{P}}_{\text{pathological}}
due to the sensitivity of the Bayesian posterior to outliers. A similar result holds for the KL divergence between the posterior predictive and the DGP and pathological model in the BAS
PP
case. In contrast, the MMD from the NPL posterior predictive to the DGP is much smaller compared to that of the pathological model. Alternative DRO-RoBAS formulations
are provided in Appendix
C
.
Proofs of this section are in Appendix
A
.

{\mathbb{E}}_{\Pi_{\text{Bayes}}}[d_{\text{KL}}({\mathbb{P}}_{\text{pathological}},{\mathbb{P}}_{\theta})]\approx 0.17<0.42\approx{\mathbb{E}}_{\Pi_{\text{Bayes}}}[d_{\text{KL}}({\mathbb{P}}^{\star},{\mathbb{P}}_{\theta})]

3.1
Duality of the DRO-RoBAS Problem

We first formulate our optimisation problem as a kernel DRO problem
(
Zhu et al., 2021
)
. This allows us to obtain a dual formulation of (
11
) in the RKHS which can be optimised using kernel methods. Let
\phi:\Xi\rightarrow{\cal H}_{k}
denote the feature map associated with kernel
k
and let
\mu_{{\mathbb{P}}}\in{\cal H}_{k}
denote the kernel mean embedding of the probability measure
{\mathbb{P}}\in{\cal P}_{k}(\Xi)
, i.e.
\mu_{{\mathbb{P}}}:={\mathbb{E}}_{\xi\sim{\mathbb{P}}}\left[\phi(\xi)\right]
. Then the MMD is equivalently defined as
\mathbb{D}_{k}({\mathbb{P}},{\mathbb{Q}})=\|\mu_{{\mathbb{P}}}-\mu_{{\mathbb{Q}}}\|_{k}

(
Gretton et al., 2012
, Lemma 4)
. Throughout we assume that
{\mathbb{P}}_{n}^{\text{pred(NPL)}}\in{\cal P}_{k}(\Xi)
. Note that this condition is trivially satisfied for a bounded kernel
k
. Consider the following set
satisfying the conditions of RoBAS:

\displaystyle{\cal C}^{\star}:=\{\mu\in{\cal H}_{k}:\|\mu-\mu_{{\mathbb{P}}_{n}^{\text{pred(NPL)}}}\|_{k}\leq\epsilon\}.

(12)

The associated ambiguity set induced by
{\cal C}^{\star}
is

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\cal K}_{{\cal C}^{\star}}&:=\{{\mathbb{P}}\in{\cal P}_{k}(\Xi),\mu_{\mathbb{P}}\in{\cal C}^{\star}\}\equiv{\cal B}_{\epsilon}^{k}({\mathbb{P}}_{n}^{\text{pred(NPL)}})\end{split}

(13)

and our DRO-RoBAS problem in (
11
) is equivalent to:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \min_{x\in{\cal X}}~\sup_{{\mathbb{P}},\mu_{\mathbb{P}}}~{\mathbb{E}}_{\xi\sim{\mathbb{P}}}[f_{x}(\xi)]\hskip 5.69046pt\text{s.t.}\hskip 5.69046pt{\mathbb{P}}\in{\cal P}_{k}(\Xi),\hskip 5.69046pt\mu_{\mathbb{P}}\in{\cal C}^{\star}.\end{aligned}

(14)

The equivalence can be seen through the
{\cal C}^{\star}
-induced ambiguity set for distributions
{\mathbb{P}}
which can be written as
{\cal K}_{{\cal C}^{\star}}
.

Before we proceed to our dual formulation, we introduce the main result from
Zhu et al. (2021)
which gives a dual formulation of Kernel DRO problems for general sets
{\cal C}
satisfying certain assumptions.

Theorem 3.1

(
Zhu et al. (2021)
, Theorem 3.1)
.

Assume
{\cal C}\subset{\cal H}_{k}
is closed convex,
f_{x}(\cdot)
is proper, upper semi-continuous, and
\ri({\cal K}_{{\cal C}})\neq\emptyset
, where
\ri({\cal K}_{{\cal C}})
denotes the relative interior of
{\cal K}_{{\cal C}}
. Then the primal problem:

\displaystyle\min_{x}~\sup_{{\mathbb{P}},\mu}~{\mathbb{E}}_{\xi\sim{\mathbb{P}}}[f_{x}(\xi)]\hskip 8.5359pt\st\hskip 5.69046pt{\mathbb{P}}\in{\cal P},~\mu_{\mathbb{P}}=\mu,~\mu\in{\cal C}

is equivalent to:

\displaystyle\min_{x,g_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}},g\in{\cal H}_{k}}g_{0}+\delta_{\cal C}^{\star}(g)\hskip 2.84544pt\st\hskip 2.84544ptf_{x}(\xi)\leq g_{0}+g(\xi),\forall\xi\in\Xi

where
\delta_{\cal C}^{\star}(g):=\sup_{\mu\in{\cal C}}\left<g,\mu\right>_{{\cal H}_{k}}
the support function of
{\cal C}
.

This Theorem gives an effective way to transition from the primal to the dual formulation by using the support function of the set
{\cal C}
. Importantly, in contrast to other kernel-based dual formulations
(
Staib & Jegelka, 2019
, e.g.)
, this theorem does
not
require the objective function
f
to be a member of the RKHS
{\cal H}_{k}
.
We first derive the support function of
{\cal C}^{\star}
in the DRO-RoBAS case. We denote
{\mathbb{E}}_{{\mathbb{Q}}\sim\DP(\alpha^{\prime},{\mathbb{F}}^{\prime})}
and
{\mathbb{E}}_{\xi\sim{\mathbb{P}}_{\theta_{k}({\mathbb{Q}})}}
by
{\mathbb{E}}_{\DP_{\xi_{1:n}}}
and
{\mathbb{E}}_{{\mathbb{P}}_{\theta_{k}({\mathbb{Q}})}}
respectively.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
{\cal C}^{\star}
be defined by (

4
Experiments

We evaluate our method on several different DGPs, model families and misspecification settings for two decision-making problems: the Newsvendor and the Portfolio.

We compare our method to existing Bayesian formulations of DRO —DRO-BAS
(
Dellaporta et al., 2024
)
and Bayesian DRO (BDRO)
(
Shapiro et al., 2023
)
—both based on the KL divergence and standard Bayesian posterior. To assess how much robustness in our framework is gained through the robust posterior compared to the choice of the MMD in the ambiguity set, we further compare against the empirical method which uses an MMD ball around the empirical measure (denoted by Empirical MMD). This was presented in
Staib & Jegelka (2019)
and also forms a special case of Kernel DRO
(
Zhu et al., 2021
)
. Implementation details are provided in Appendix
B
.

We explore two types of misspecification.

1. Model Misspecification
which occurs when the DGP
{\mathbb{P}}^{\star}
does not belong to the model family
{\cal P}_{\Theta}
, e.g. if
{\mathbb{P}}^{\star}
is multimodal while
{\cal P}_{\Theta}
assumes unimodality. This affects Bayesian DRO methods (BDRO, DRO-BAS, DRO-RoBAS) but not empirical approaches, as the latter do not rely on a model.

2. Huber’s contamination model

(
Huber, 1992
)
which is a specific type of model misspecification (see Fig.
2
) wherein the training DGP is
{\mathbb{P}}^{\star}=(1-\eta)\tilde{{\mathbb{P}}}+\eta{\mathbb{Q}}
for some
\eta\in[0,1]
and
\tilde{{\mathbb{P}}},{\mathbb{Q}}\in{\cal P}(\Xi)
. Contamination, limited to the training set, impacts both Bayesian and empirical DRO methods since the test distribution is assumed to be
\tilde{{\mathbb{P}}}
. Huber contamination relates to concepts like distribution shift and out-of-distribution robustness
(
Liu et al., 2021
, e.g.,)
.

We continue with the multi-dimensional Portfolio problem, also considered by

4.3
Computational Time

The increased robustness of DRO-RoBAS comes at the cost of higher computational demands (see
Appendix
B
). This cost arises from the complex optimisation problem in the RKHS and the longer sampling time required for the NPL posterior. However, as previously demonstrated, this cost is justified by significantly improved out-of-sample performance across various cases of model misspecification. Moreover, by leveraging the NPL-MMD and the MMD, DRO-RoBAS can be used for any choice of model family, even likelihood-free models. Notice that DRO-BAS
PE
is limited to exponential family models whereas the computational cost of BDRO and DRO-BAS
PP
increases considerably if the posterior is not available in closed form, as methods like Markov Chain Monte Carlo are needed for posterior sampling. This highlights the flexibility and robustness of DRO-RoBAS despite its computational demands.

5
Conclusion

Bayesian formulations of DRO for decision-making problems can suffer from model misspecification as the ambiguity set heavily relies on the non-robust Bayesian posterior. We addressed this challenge by using a robust NPL posterior to inform the ambiguity set and leveraging the MMD to construct
both
the posterior and the ambiguity set itself. We show that DRO-RoBAS admits a dual formulation in the RKHS and we provide probabilistic guarantees for the tolerance level such that the resulting optimisation problem upper bounds the true objective with high probability.

Scalability improvements for DRO-RoBAS can be achieved through existing tools from kernel methods such as Fourier features
(
Rahimi & Recht, 2007
)
and low-rank kernel matrix approximations
(
Bach, 2013
)
that are left for future work. Our empirical evidence suggests that if the model is well-specified, or the level of misspecification is low, then existing Bayesian formulations like DRO-BAS can have better performance and scalability. At the same time, when model misspecification is moderate or high then DRO-RoBAS achieves significantly improved out-of-sample performance and robustness. Note that any prior knowledge on the misspecification level can be naturally incorporated into our framework to further boost performance.

Finally, the construction of robust Bayesian Ambiguity Sets can extend beyond the choices of NPL and MMD. For example, another instantiation of our framework arises if we employ Generalised Bayesian Inference (GBI)
(
Bissiri et al., 2016
)
. One of the motivations of GBI is to induce robustness with respect to model misspecification
(
Ghosh & Basu, 2016
;
Jewson et al., 2018
;
Chérief-Abdellatif & Alquier, 2020
;
Knoblauch et al., 2022
;
Matsubara et al., 2022
;
Altamirano et al., 2023
)
by targeting a different divergence than the KL. While these methods do not directly impose uncertainty on the DGP like NPL does, they can produce robust GBI posteriors, making it worthwhile to integrate into DRO-RoBAS. Notably, the duality results in
Section

3.1
are based on the MMD choice, however, they hold under a general posterior and are
not
dependent on the NPL framework.

\lambda\in[0,1]
. Then

\displaystyle\left\|\lambda\mu_{1}+(1-\lambda)\mu_{2}-\mu_{{\mathbb{P}}_{n}^{\text{pred(NPL)}}}\right\|_{k}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda\left\|\mu_{1}-\mu_{{\mathbb{P}}_{n}^{\text{pred(NPL)}}}\right\|_{k}+(1-\lambda)\left\|\mu_{2}-\mu_{{\mathbb{P}}_{n}^{\text{pred(NPL)}}}\right\|_{k}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda\epsilon+(1-\lambda)\epsilon=\epsilon

where the first inequality follows from the triangle inequality applied to the RKHS norm. Hence,
\lambda\mu_{1}+(1-\lambda)\mu_{2}\in{\cal C}^{\star}
and
{\cal C}^{\star}
is convex.

Closeness of
{\cal C}^{\star}
:
By definition, a closed set is a set whose complement is open, so it suffices to show the set
{\cal H}_{k}\backslash{\cal C}^{\star}
is open.
Again by definition, set
{\cal H}_{k}\backslash{\cal C}^{\star}
is open if, for all
y\in{\cal H}_{k}\backslash{\cal C}^{\star}
, there exists
r>0
such that any point
z\in{\cal H}_{k}
satisfying
\|y-z\|_{{\cal H}_{k}}<r
also belongs to
{\cal H}_{k}\backslash{\cal C}^{\star}
.
Given any
y\in{\cal H}_{k}\backslash{\cal C}^{\star}
, let
r=\|y-\mu_{{\mathbb{P}}_{n}^{\text{pred(NPL)}}}\|_{{\cal H}_{k}}-\epsilon
.
Observe that
r>0
because
\|y-\mu_{{\mathbb{P}}_{n}^{\text{pred(NPL)}}}\|_{{\cal H}_{k}}>\epsilon
.
Now, for all
z\in{\cal H}_{k}
such that
\|y-z\|_{{\cal H}_{k}}<r
, we can apply the triangle inequality to obtain

\|y-\mu_{{\mathbb{P}}_{n}^{\text{pred(NPL)}}}\|_{{\cal H}_{k}}\leq\|z-\mu_{{\mathbb{P}}_{n}^{\text{pred(NPL)}}}\|_{{\cal H}_{k}}+\|z-y\|_{{\cal H}_{k}}.

After rearranging, we have

\displaystyle\|z-\mu_{{\mathbb{P}}_{n}^{\text{pred(NPL)}}}\|_{{\cal H}_{k}}

\displaystyle\geq\|y-\mu_{{\mathbb{P}}_{n}^{\text{pred(NPL)}}}\|_{{\cal H}_{k}}-\|z-y\|_{{\cal H}_{k}}

\displaystyle>\|y-\mu_{{\mathbb{P}}_{n}^{\text{pred(NPL)}}}\|_{{\cal H}_{k}}-r=\epsilon.

Therefore,
\|z-\mu_{{\mathbb{P}}_{n}^{\text{pred(NPL)}}}\|_{{\cal H}_{k}}>\epsilon
, so
z
lies in set
{\cal H}_{k}\backslash{\cal C}^{\star}
, which concludes our claim that
{\cal H}_{k}\backslash{\cal C}^{\star}
is open and proves that
{\cal C}^{\star}
is closed.

Non-empty relative interior of
{\cal K}_{{\cal C}^{\star}}
:
It suffices to prove that
{\cal K}_{{\cal C}^{\star}}
is non-empty and convex. Convexity follows from an analogous proof of convexity of
{\cal C}^{\star}
since MMD satisfies the triangle inequality.
{\cal K}_{{\cal C}^{\star}}
is non-empty since, for any
\epsilon>0
,
{\mathbb{P}}_{n}^{\text{pred(NPL)}}\in{\cal K}_{{\cal C}^{\star}}
.
∎

A.3
Proof of Theorem
3.6
.

We first provide a Lemma adapted from the results of
Dellaporta et al. (2022)
which bounds the expected MMD (under a finite DGP sample) between the DGP and a sample from the approximate DP posterior. This is necessary to quantify the generalisation error between the DGP and the obtained model
{\mathbb{P}}_{\theta_{k}({\mathbb{Q}})}
for a DP sample
{\mathbb{Q}}
.

Lemma A.3
.

Let
{\mathbb{Q}}\sim\widehat{\DP}_{\xi_{1:n}}
be defined as in (
17
). Assume that
\xi_{1:n}\simiid{\mathbb{P}}^{\star}
and that the kernel
k
is such that
|k(\xi,\xi^{\prime})|\leq K
,
K<\infty
, for any
\xi,\xi^{\prime}\in\Xi
. Then

{\mathbb{E}}_{\xi_{1:n}\simiid{\mathbb{P}}^{\star}}\left[{\mathbb{E}}_{\widehat{\DP}_{\xi_{1:n}}}\left[\mathbb{D}_{k}({\mathbb{P}}^{\star},{\mathbb{Q}})\right]\right]\leq\sqrt{\frac{K}{n}}+\sqrt{\frac{2K(n-1)+c(c+1)}{(c+n)(c+n+1)}}+\sqrt{\frac{Kc(c+1)}{(c+n)(c+n+1)}}.

(24)

Proof.

The result follows directly from Lemmas 6 and 11 of
Dellaporta et al. (2022)
with some small modifications. First notice that by the triangle inequality

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}_{k}({\mathbb{P}}^{\star},{\mathbb{Q}})\leq\mathbb{D}_{k}({\mathbb{P}}^{\star},\hat{{\mathbb{P}}}_{n})+\mathbb{D}_{k}(\hat{{\mathbb{P}}}_{n},{\mathbb{Q}})

(25)

where
\hat{{\mathbb{P}}}_{n}:=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\delta_{\xi_{1:n}}
. By
Dellaporta et al. (2022)
, Lemma 6, we have that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{\xi_{1:n}\simiid{\mathbb{P}}^{\star}}\left[\mathbb{D}_{k}({\mathbb{P}}^{\star},\hat{{\mathbb{P}}}_{n})\right]\leq\sqrt{\frac{K}{n}}.

(26)

Moreover, by
Dellaporta et al. (2022)
, Lemma 11, we have that:

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{\xi_{1:n}\simiid{\mathbb{P}}^{\star}}\left[{\mathbb{E}}_{{\mathbb{Q}}\sim\widehat{\DP}_{\xi_{1:n}}}\left[\mathbb{D}_{k}(\hat{{\mathbb{P}}}_{n},{\mathbb{Q}})\right]\right]

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\frac{2K(n-1)+c(c+1)}{(c+n)(c+n+1)}}+\sqrt{\frac{Kc(c+1)}{(c+n)(c+n+1)}}.

(27)

Taking expectations and using equations (
26
) and (
27
) in (
25
) we obtain the required result:

\begin{split}{\mathbb{E}}_{\xi_{1:n}\simiid{\mathbb{P}}^{\star}}\left[{\mathbb{E}}_{{\mathbb{Q}}\sim\widehat{\DP}_{\xi_{1:n}}}\left[\mathbb{D}_{k}({\mathbb{P}}^{\star},{\mathbb{Q}})\right]\right]&\leq{\mathbb{E}}_{\xi_{1:n}\simiid{\mathbb{P}}^{\star}}\left[\mathbb{D}_{k}({\mathbb{P}}^{\star},\hat{{\mathbb{P}}}_{n})\right]+{\mathbb{E}}_{\xi_{1:n}\simiid{\mathbb{P}}^{\star}}\left[{\mathbb{E}}_{{\mathbb{Q}}\sim\widehat{\DP}_{\xi_{1:n}}}\left[\mathbb{D}_{k}(\hat{{\mathbb{P}}}_{n},{\mathbb{Q}})\right]\right]\\
&\leq\sqrt{\frac{K}{n}}+\sqrt{\frac{2K(n-1)+c(c+1)}{(c+n)(c+n+1)}}+\sqrt{\frac{Kc(c+1)}{(c+n)(c+n+1)}}.\end{split}

(28)

∎

Based on this bound, we can now provide a result in probability.

Lemma A.4
.

Assume that
\xi_{1:n}\simiid{\mathbb{P}}^{\star}
and that kernel
k
is such that
|k(\xi,\xi^{\prime})|\leq K
,
K<\infty
, for any
\xi,\xi^{\prime}\in\Xi
. Then with probability
1-\delta
we have:

\begin{split}{\mathbb{E}}_{{\mathbb{Q}}\sim\widehat{\DP}_{\xi_{1:n}}}[\mathbb{D}_{k}({\mathbb{P}}^{\star},{\mathbb{Q}})]&\leq\sqrt{\frac{K}{n}}+\sqrt{\frac{2K(n-1)+c(c+1)}{(c+n)(c+n+1)}}\\
&\quad+\sqrt{\frac{Kc(c+1)}{(c+n)(c+n+1)}}+\frac{\sqrt{2nK\left(\log{\frac{1}{\delta}}\right)}}{c+n}.\end{split}

(29)

Proof.

We follow the proof technique of Lemma 1 of
Briol et al. (2019)
based on McDiarmid’s inequality
(
McDiarmid et al., 1989
)
. First, notice that by definition of
\widehat{\DP}_{\xi_{1:n}}
in (
17
) we can re-write the objective as

\begin{split}{\mathbb{E}}_{{\mathbb{Q}}\sim\widehat{\DP}_{\xi_{1:n}}}[\mathbb{D}_{k}({\mathbb{P}}^{\star},{\mathbb{Q}})]={\mathbb{E}}_{w\sim\Dir}\left[{\mathbb{E}}_{\tilde{\xi}_{1:T}\simiid{\mathbb{F}}}\left[\mathbb{D}_{k}({\mathbb{P}}^{\star},\widehat{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{\xi_{1:n}})\right]\right]\end{split}

(30)

where
\widehat{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{\xi_{1:n}}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}\delta_{\xi_{i}}+\sum_{k=1}^{\tau}\tilde{w}_{k}\delta_{\tilde{\xi}_{k}}\sim\widehat{\DP}_{\xi_{1:n}}
and
{\mathbb{E}}_{w\sim\Dir}
denotes the expectation under
\Dir\left(1,\dots 1,\frac{c}{\tau},\dots,\frac{c}{\tau}\right)
.
Let
h(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n}):={\mathbb{E}}_{w\sim\Dir}\left[{\mathbb{E}}_{\tilde{\xi}_{1:T}\simiid{\mathbb{F}}}\left[\mathbb{D}_{k}({\mathbb{P}}^{\star},\widehat{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{\xi_{1:n}})\right]\right]
. Then for all
\{\xi_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n},\xi_{i}^{\prime}\in\Xi
and
\xi^{\prime}_{1:n}:=\{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{i-1},\xi_{i}^{\prime},\xi_{i+1},\dots,\xi_{n}\}
we have

\begin{split}&\left|h(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{i-1},\xi_{i},\xi_{i+1},\dots,\xi_{n})-h(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{i-1},\xi_{i}^{\prime},\xi_{i+1},\dots,\xi_{n})\right|\\
&\quad\quad=\left|{\mathbb{E}}_{w\sim\Dir}\left[{\mathbb{E}}_{\tilde{\xi}_{1:T}\simiid{\mathbb{F}}}\left[\mathbb{D}_{k}({\mathbb{P}}^{\star},\widehat{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{\xi_{1:n}})\right]\right]-{\mathbb{E}}_{w\sim\Dir}\left[{\mathbb{E}}_{\tilde{\xi}_{1:T}\simiid{\mathbb{F}}}\left[\mathbb{D}_{k}({\mathbb{P}}^{\star},\widehat{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{\xi^{\prime}_{1:n}})\right]\right]\right|\\
&\quad\quad=\left|{\mathbb{E}}_{w\sim\Dir}\left[{\mathbb{E}}_{\tilde{\xi}_{1:T}\simiid{\mathbb{F}}}\left[\mathbb{D}_{k}({\mathbb{P}}^{\star},\widehat{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{\xi_{1:n}})-\mathbb{D}_{k}({\mathbb{P}}^{\star},\widehat{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{\xi^{\prime}_{1:n}})\right]\right]\right|\\
&\quad\quad\leq{\mathbb{E}}_{w\sim\Dir}\left[{\mathbb{E}}_{\tilde{\xi}_{1:T}\simiid{\mathbb{F}}}\left[\left|\mathbb{D}_{k}({\mathbb{P}}^{\star},\hat{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{\xi_{1:n}})-\mathbb{D}_{k}({\mathbb{P}}^{\star},\hat{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{\xi^{\prime}_{1:n}})\right|\right]\right]\\
&\quad\quad={\mathbb{E}}_{w\sim\Dir}\left[{\mathbb{E}}_{\tilde{\xi}_{1:T}\simiid{\mathbb{F}}}\left[\left|\|\mu_{\mathbb{P}}^{\star}-\mu_{\widehat{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{x_{1:n}}}\|_{k}-\|\mu_{\mathbb{P}}^{\star}-\mu_{\widehat{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{\xi^{\prime}_{1:n}}}\|_{k}\right|\right]\right]\\
&\quad\quad\leq{\mathbb{E}}_{w\sim\Dir}\left[{\mathbb{E}}_{\tilde{\xi}_{1:T}\simiid{\mathbb{F}}}\left[\|\mu_{\mathbb{P}}^{\star}-\mu_{\widehat{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{\xi_{1:n}}}-\mu_{\mathbb{P}}^{\star}+\mu_{\widehat{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{\xi^{\prime}_{1:n}}}\|_{k}\right]\right]\\
&\quad\quad={\mathbb{E}}_{w\sim\Dir}\left[{\mathbb{E}}_{\tilde{\xi}_{1:T}\simiid{\mathbb{F}}}\left[\|w_{i}(k(\xi_{i},\cdot)-k(\xi_{i}^{\prime},\cdot))\|_{k}\right]\right]\\
&\quad\quad={\mathbb{E}}_{w\sim\Dir}\left[w_{i}\|k(\xi_{i},\cdot)-k(\xi_{i}^{\prime},\cdot)\|_{k}\right]\\
&\quad\quad\leq{\mathbb{E}}_{w\sim\Dir}[w_{i}]2\sqrt{K}\\
&\quad\quad=\frac{2\sqrt{K}}{c+n}\end{split}

(31)

where in the first inequality we used Jensen’s inequality and in the second we used the reverse triangle inequality. Then by McDiarmid’s inequality
(
McDiarmid et al., 1989
)
we have that for any
\epsilon>0
:

\begin{split}&{\mathbb{P}}\left({\mathbb{E}}_{w\sim\Dir}\left[{\mathbb{E}}_{\tilde{\xi}_{1:T}\simiid{\mathbb{F}}}\left[\mathbb{D}_{k}({\mathbb{P}}^{\star},\hat{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{\xi_{1:n}})\right]\right]\right.\\
&\left.\quad\quad-{\mathbb{E}}_{\xi_{1:n}\simiid{\mathbb{P}}^{\star}}\left[{\mathbb{E}}_{w\sim\Dir}\left[{\mathbb{E}}_{\tilde{\xi}_{1:T}\simiid{\mathbb{F}}}\left[\mathbb{D}_{k}({\mathbb{P}}^{\star},\widehat{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{\xi_{1:n}})\right]\right]\right]\geq\epsilon\right)\\
&\leq\exp{\left(\frac{-2\epsilon^{2}}{\frac{4nK}{(n+c)^{2}}}\right)}\\
&=\exp{\left(\frac{-\epsilon^{2}(n+c)^{2}}{2nK}\right)}\end{split}

(32)

Let
\delta:=\exp{\left(\frac{-\epsilon^{2}(n+c)^{2}}{2nK}\right)}
then it follows that with probability
1-\delta
:

\begin{split}&{\mathbb{E}}_{w\sim\Dir}\left[{\mathbb{E}}_{\tilde{\xi}_{1:T}\simiid{\mathbb{F}}}\left[\mathbb{D}_{k}({\mathbb{P}}^{\star},\hat{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{\xi_{1:n}})\right]\right]\\
&\quad\quad\leq{\mathbb{E}}_{\xi_{1:n}\simiid{\mathbb{P}}^{\star}}\left[{\mathbb{E}}_{w\sim\Dir}\left[{\mathbb{E}}_{\tilde{\xi}_{1:T}\simiid{\mathbb{F}}}\left[\mathbb{D}_{k}({\mathbb{P}}^{\star},\hat{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{\xi_{1:n}})\right]\right]\right]+\epsilon\\
&\quad\quad={\mathbb{E}}_{\xi_{1:n}\simiid{\mathbb{P}}^{\star}}\left[{\mathbb{E}}_{w\sim\Dir}\left[{\mathbb{E}}_{\tilde{\xi}_{1:T}\simiid{\mathbb{F}}}\left[\mathbb{D}_{k}({\mathbb{P}}^{\star},\hat{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{\xi_{1:n}})\right]\right]\right]+\frac{\sqrt{2nK\left(\log{\frac{1}{\delta}}\right)}}{n+c}\\
&\quad\quad\leq\sqrt{\frac{K}{n}}+\sqrt{\frac{2K(n-1)+c(c+1)}{(c+n)(c+n+1)}}+\sqrt{\frac{Kc(c+1)}{(c+n)(c+n+1)}}\\
&\quad\quad\quad\quad+\frac{\sqrt{2nK\left(\log{\frac{1}{\delta}}\right)}}{c+n}\end{split}

(33)

where the last inequality follows from Lemma
A.3
.
∎

We are now ready to prove the main result.

Proof of Theorem
3.6
.

First note that

\begin{split}\mathbb{D}_{k}({\mathbb{P}}^{\star},{\mathbb{P}}_{\theta_{k}({\mathbb{Q}})})-\inf_{\theta\in\Theta}\mathbb{D}_{k}({\mathbb{P}}_{\theta},{\mathbb{P}}^{\star})&\leq\mathbb{D}_{k}({\mathbb{P}}^{\star},{\mathbb{Q}})+\mathbb{D}_{k}({\mathbb{Q}},{\mathbb{P}}_{\theta_{k}({\mathbb{Q}})})-\inf_{\theta\in\Theta}\mathbb{D}_{k}({\mathbb{P}}_{\theta},{\mathbb{P}}^{\star})\\
&=\mathbb{D}_{k}({\mathbb{P}}^{\star},{\mathbb{Q}})+\inf_{\theta\in\Theta}\mathbb{D}_{k}({\mathbb{P}}_{\theta},{\mathbb{Q}})-\inf_{\theta\in\Theta}\mathbb{D}_{k}({\mathbb{P}}_{\theta},{\mathbb{P}}^{\star})\\
&\leq\mathbb{D}_{k}({\mathbb{P}}^{\star},{\mathbb{Q}})+\left|\inf_{\theta\in\Theta}\mathbb{D}_{k}({\mathbb{P}}_{\theta},{\mathbb{Q}})-\inf_{\theta\in\Theta}\mathbb{D}_{k}({\mathbb{P}}_{\theta},{\mathbb{P}}^{\star})\right|\\
&\leq\mathbb{D}_{k}({\mathbb{P}}^{\star},{\mathbb{Q}})+\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\left|\mathbb{D}_{k}({\mathbb{P}}_{\theta},{\mathbb{Q}})-\mathbb{D}_{k}({\mathbb{P}}_{\theta},{\mathbb{P}}^{\star})\right|\\
&\leq\mathbb{D}_{k}({\mathbb{P}}^{\star},{\mathbb{Q}})+\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\mathbb{D}_{k}({\mathbb{Q}},{\mathbb{P}}^{\star})\\
&=2\mathbb{D}_{k}({\mathbb{P}}^{\star},{\mathbb{Q}})\end{split}

(34)

where we used the triangle inequality in the first inequality, the definition of
\theta_{k}(\cdot)
in the first equality and the reversed triangle inequality in the last inequality. The third inequality follows from the fact (mentioned in
Briol et al. (2019)
, Theorem 1) that since
k
is bounded, the family
\inf_{\theta\in\Theta}\mathbb{D}_{k}({\mathbb{P}}_{\theta},\cdot)
is uniformly bounded and for bounded functions
f,g
we have
\|\inf_{\theta}f(\theta)-\inf_{\theta}g(\theta)\|\leq\sup_{\theta}|f(\theta)-g(\theta)|
. Hence, by Jensen’s inequality, since the MMD is convex, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}_{k}({\mathbb{P}}^{\star},\hat{{\mathbb{P}}}_{n}^{\text{pred(NPL)}})=\mathbb{D}_{k}({\mathbb{P}}^{\star},{\mathbb{E}}_{\widehat{\DP}_{\xi_{1:n}}}[{\mathbb{P}}_{\theta_{k}({\mathbb{Q}})}])\leq{\mathbb{E}}_{\widehat{\DP}_{\xi_{1:n}}}[\mathbb{D}_{k}({\mathbb{P}}^{\star},{\mathbb{P}}_{\theta_{k}({\mathbb{Q}})})]

(35)

and therefore

\begin{split}\mathbb{D}_{k}({\mathbb{P}}^{\star},\hat{{\mathbb{P}}}_{n}^{\text{pred(NPL)}})-\inf_{\theta\in\Theta}\mathbb{D}_{k}({\mathbb{P}}_{\theta},{\mathbb{P}}^{\star})&\leq{\mathbb{E}}_{{\mathbb{Q}}\sim\widehat{\DP}_{\xi_{1:n}}}[\mathbb{D}_{k}({\mathbb{P}}^{\star},{\mathbb{P}}_{\theta_{k}({\mathbb{Q}})})]-\inf_{\theta\in\Theta}\mathbb{D}_{k}({\mathbb{P}}_{\theta},{\mathbb{P}}^{\star})\\
&\leq 2{\mathbb{E}}_{{\mathbb{Q}}\sim\widehat{\DP}_{\xi_{1:n}}}[\mathbb{D}_{k}({\mathbb{P}}^{\star},{\mathbb{Q}})].\end{split}

(36)

By Lemma
A.4
it follows that with probability
1-\delta
the advertised result holds.
∎

A.4
Proof of Corollary
3.9
.

Recall that
{\mathbb{P}}^{\star}=(1-\eta){\mathbb{P}}_{\theta_{0}}+\eta{\mathbb{Q}}
for some
\theta_{0}\in\Theta
,
{\mathbb{Q}}\in{\cal P}(\Xi)
and
\eta\in[0,1]
. Corollary
3.9
is an immediate consequence of Lemma 3.3 of
Chérief-Abdellatif & Alquier (2022)
which states that for any
\theta\in\Theta
:

\displaystyle|\mathbb{D}_{k}({\mathbb{P}}_{\theta},{\mathbb{P}}^{\star})-\mathbb{D}_{k}({\mathbb{P}}_{\theta},{\mathbb{P}}_{\theta_{0}})|\leq 2\eta.

(37)

Using this and Theorem
3.6
we can prove the advertised result as follows:

Proof of Corollary
3.9
.

It follows by (
37
) that:

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}_{k}({\mathbb{P}}_{\theta_{k}({\mathbb{Q}})},{\mathbb{P}}_{\theta_{0}})\leq 2\eta+\mathbb{D}_{k}({\mathbb{P}}_{\theta_{k}({\mathbb{Q}})},{\mathbb{P}}^{\star}).

(38)

and hence

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{\widehat{\DP}_{\xi_{1:n}}}[\mathbb{D}_{k}({\mathbb{P}}_{\theta_{k}({\mathbb{Q}})},{\mathbb{P}}_{\theta_{0}})]\leq 2\eta+{\mathbb{E}}_{\widehat{\DP}_{\xi_{1:n}}}[\mathbb{D}_{k}({\mathbb{P}}_{\theta_{k}({\mathbb{Q}})},{\mathbb{P}}^{\star})].

(39)

We then have:

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}_{k}({\mathbb{P}}_{\theta_{0}},\hat{{\mathbb{P}}}_{n}^{\text{pred(NPL)}})

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{E}}_{\widehat{\DP}_{\xi_{1:n}}}[\mathbb{D}_{k}({\mathbb{P}}_{\theta_{0}},{\mathbb{P}}_{\theta_{k}})]

\displaystyle\leq 2{\mathbb{E}}_{\widehat{\DP}_{\xi_{1:n}}}[\mathbb{D}_{k}({\mathbb{P}}_{\theta_{0}},{\mathbb{Q}})]+\inf_{\theta}\mathbb{D}_{k}({\mathbb{P}}_{\theta_{0}},{\mathbb{P}}_{\theta})

\displaystyle=2{\mathbb{E}}_{\widehat{\DP}_{\xi_{1:n}}}[\mathbb{D}_{k}({\mathbb{P}}_{\theta_{0}},{\mathbb{Q}})]

\displaystyle\leq 4\eta+2{\mathbb{E}}_{\widehat{\DP}_{\xi_{1:n}}}[\mathbb{D}_{k}({\mathbb{P}}^{\star},{\mathbb{Q}})]

and the result follows from Lemma
A.4
. The first inequality follows from Jensen’s inequality as in (
35
), the second inequality follows from (
34
), the equality holds from the fact that
{\mathbb{P}}_{\theta_{0}}\in{\cal P}_{\Theta}
and the last inequality follows form (
39
).
∎

Appendix C
Alternative RoBAS formulations

We now discuss alternative RoBAS formulations as the ones provided in
Dellaporta et al. (2024)
through the DRO-BAS framework. In particular, in the DRO-BAS case the authors explored two distinct formulations of ambiguity sets: the BAS
PP
(
3
), mirroring the definition of
{\cal B}_{\epsilon}^{k}({\mathbb{P}}_{n}^{\text{pred(NPL)}})
, and the BAS
PE
(
4
) which is based on a posterior expectation by considering the expected KL distance to the model family.

In the case of robust Bayesian ambiguity sets, an interesting connection can be made between
{\cal B}_{\epsilon}({\mathbb{P}}_{n}^{\text{pred(NPL)}})
and the ambiguity set based on the expected squared MMD, namely:

\displaystyle{\cal A}^{k}_{\epsilon}(\DP(c^{\prime},{\mathbb{F}}^{\prime}))=\left\{{\mathbb{P}}\in{\cal P}_{k}(\Xi):{\mathbb{E}}_{{\mathbb{Q}}\sim\DP(c^{\prime},{\mathbb{F}}^{\prime})}\left[\mathbb{D}_{k}^{2}({\mathbb{P}},{\mathbb{P}}_{\theta_{k}({\mathbb{Q}})})\right]\right\}.

(41)

The squared MMD has previously been used in Generalised Bayesian Inference to define a loss function by
Chérief-Abdellatif & Alquier (2020)
. By using the properties of the MMD, we can prove the following equivalence:

Lemma C.1
.

Let
{\mathbb{E}}_{\DP_{\xi_{1:n}}}
denote the expectation under
{\mathbb{E}}_{{\mathbb{Q}}\sim\DP(c^{\prime},{\mathbb{F}}^{\prime})}
and suppose:

\begin{split}v(\DP)&:={\mathbb{E}}_{\DP_{\xi_{1:n}}}\left[\left<\mu_{{\mathbb{P}}_{\theta_{k}({\mathbb{Q}})}},\mu_{{\mathbb{P}}_{\theta_{k}({\mathbb{Q}})}}\right>\right]-\left<{\mathbb{E}}_{\DP_{\xi_{1:n}}}[\mu_{{\mathbb{P}}_{\theta_{k}({\mathbb{Q}})}}],{\mathbb{E}}_{\DP_{\xi_{1:n}}}[\mu_{{\mathbb{P}}_{\theta_{k}({\mathbb{Q}})}}]\right>\\
&={\mathbb{E}}_{\DP_{\xi_{1:n}}}\left[\left\|\mu_{{\mathbb{P}}_{\theta_{k}({\mathbb{Q}})}}\right\|^{2}_{{\cal H}_{k}}\right]-\left\|{\mathbb{E}}_{\DP_{\xi_{1:n}}}[\mu_{{\mathbb{P}}_{\theta_{k}({\mathbb{Q}})}}]\right\|^{2}_{{\cal H}_{k}}\\
&<\infty.\end{split}

(42)

Then for any
{\mathbb{P}}\in{\cal P}_{k}(\Xi)
:

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{\DP_{\xi_{1:n}}}\left[\mathbb{D}_{k}^{2}({\mathbb{P}},{\mathbb{P}}_{\theta_{k}({\mathbb{Q}})})\right]=\mathbb{D}_{k}^{2}({\mathbb{P}},{\mathbb{P}}_{n}^{\text{pred(NPL)}})+v(\DP)

(43)

and for any
\epsilon\geq 0
:

\displaystyle{\cal B}_{\epsilon}^{k}({\mathbb{P}}_{n}^{\text{pred(NPL)}})\equiv{\cal A}^{k}_{\epsilon^{2}+v(\DP)}(\DP(c^{\prime},{\mathbb{F}}^{\prime})).

(44)

Proof of
Lemma

C.1
.

The proof is based on the reproducing property of the RKHS. Recall that
{\mathbb{E}}_{\DP_{\xi_{1:n}}}
denotes the expectation under
{\mathbb{E}}_{{\mathbb{Q}}\sim\DP(c^{\prime},{\mathbb{F}}^{\prime})}
. For any
{\mathbb{P}}\in{\cal P}_{k}(\Xi)
and
\epsilon\geq 0
we have:

\begin{split}&{\mathbb{E}}_{\DP_{\xi_{1:n}}}[\mathbb{D}_{k}^{2}({\mathbb{P}},{\mathbb{P}}_{\theta_{k}({\mathbb{Q}})})]\\
&\quad=\left<\mu_{{\mathbb{P}}},\mu_{{\mathbb{P}}}\right>_{{\cal H}_{k}}-2{\mathbb{E}}_{\DP_{\xi_{1:n}}}\left[\left<\mu_{{\mathbb{P}}},\mu_{{\mathbb{P}}_{\theta_{k}({\mathbb{Q}})}}\right>_{{\cal H}_{k}}\right]+{\mathbb{E}}_{\DP_{\xi_{1:n}}}\left[\left<\mu_{{\mathbb{P}}_{\theta_{k}({\mathbb{Q}})}},\mu_{{\mathbb{P}}_{\theta_{k}({\mathbb{Q}})}}\right>_{{\cal H}_{k}}\right]\\
&\quad=\left<\mu_{{\mathbb{P}}},\mu_{{\mathbb{P}}}\right>_{{\cal H}_{k}}-2{\mathbb{E}}_{\DP_{\xi_{1:n}}}\left[\left<\mu_{{\mathbb{P}}},\mu_{{\mathbb{P}}_{\theta_{k}({\mathbb{Q}})}}\right>_{{\cal H}_{k}}\right]+\left<{\mathbb{E}}_{\DP_{\xi_{1:n}}}[\mu_{{\mathbb{P}}_{\theta_{k}({\mathbb{Q}})}}],{\mathbb{E}}_{\DP_{\xi_{1:n}}}[\mu_{{\mathbb{P}}_{\theta_{k}({\mathbb{Q}})}}]\right>_{{\cal H}_{k}}\\
&\quad\quad-\left<{\mathbb{E}}_{\DP_{\xi_{1:n}}}[\mu_{{\mathbb{P}}_{\theta_{k}({\mathbb{Q}})}}],{\mathbb{E}}_{\DP_{\xi_{1:n}}}[\mu_{{\mathbb{P}}_{\theta_{k}({\mathbb{Q}})}}]\right>_{{\cal H}_{k}}+{\mathbb{E}}_{\DP_{\xi_{1:n}}}\left[\left<\mu_{{\mathbb{P}}_{\theta_{k}({\mathbb{Q}})}},\mu_{{\mathbb{P}}_{\theta_{k}({\mathbb{Q}})}}\right>_{{\cal H}_{k}}\right]\\
&\quad=\mathbb{D}_{k}^{2}({\mathbb{Q}},{\mathbb{E}}_{\DP_{\xi_{1:n}}}[{\mathbb{P}}_{\theta_{k}({\mathbb{Q}})}])+v(\DP).\end{split}

(45)

This proves the statement of Equation
43
. To prove the statement of Equation
44
recall that

\displaystyle{\cal B}^{k}_{\epsilon}({\mathbb{P}}_{n}^{\text{pred(NPL)}})

\displaystyle:=\{{\mathbb{P}}\in{\cal P}_{k}(\Xi):\mathbb{D}_{k}({\mathbb{P}},{\mathbb{P}}_{n}^{\text{pred(NPL)}})\leq\epsilon\}

(46)

\displaystyle=\{{\mathbb{P}}\in{\cal P}_{k}(\Xi):\mathbb{D}_{k}({\mathbb{P}},{\mathbb{E}}_{\DP_{\xi_{1:n}}}[{\mathbb{P}}_{\theta_{k}({\mathbb{Q}})}])\leq\epsilon\}

(47)

Using (
45
) we have that for
\epsilon\geq 0
and some
{\mathbb{P}}\in{\cal B}_{\epsilon}^{k}({\mathbb{P}}_{n}^{\text{pred(NPL)}})
:

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}_{k}({\mathbb{P}},{\mathbb{E}}_{\DP_{\xi_{1:n}}}[{\mathbb{P}}_{\theta_{k}({\mathbb{Q}})}])\leq\epsilon

(48)

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}_{k}^{2}({\mathbb{P}},{\mathbb{E}}_{\DP_{\xi_{1:n}}}[{\mathbb{P}}_{\theta_{k}({\mathbb{Q}})}])\leq\epsilon^{2}

(49)

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{\DP_{\xi_{1:n}}}[\mathbb{D}_{k}^{2}({\mathbb{P}},{\mathbb{P}}_{\theta_{k}({\mathbb{Q}})})]\leq\epsilon^{2}+v(n)

(50)

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\in{\cal A}^{k}_{\epsilon^{2}+v(\DP)}(\DP(c^{\prime},{\mathbb{F}}^{\prime})).

(51)

hence,
{\cal B}_{\epsilon}^{k}({\mathbb{P}}_{n}^{\text{pred(NPL)}})\equiv{\cal A}^{k}_{\epsilon^{2}+v(\DP)}(\DP(c^{\prime},{\mathbb{F}}^{\prime}))
, which completes the proof.
∎

The constant
v(\DP)
(
42
) is an effective variance term of the kernel mean embedding
\mu_{{\mathbb{P}}_{\theta_{k}({\mathbb{Q}})}}
in the RKHS under the nonparametric posterior
\DP\equiv\DP(c^{\prime},{\mathbb{F}}^{\prime})
. It follows directly from Lemma
C.1
that the primal DRO-RoBAS problem in (
11
) is equivalent to the following optimisation problem:

\displaystyle\min_{x\in{\cal X}}\sup_{\displaystyle\color[rgb]{0.2148,0.4922,0.7227}{\mathbb{P}}:{\mathbb{E}}_{\DP_{\xi_{1:n}}}[\mathbb{D}_{k}^{2}({\mathbb{P}},{\mathbb{P}}_{\theta_{k}({\mathbb{Q}})})]\leq\epsilon^{2}+v(\DP)}~{{\mathbb{E}}_{x\sim{\mathbb{P}}}~\left[f(z,x)\right]}

(52)

or equivalently,

\displaystyle\min_{x\in{\cal X}}\sup_{\displaystyle\color[rgb]{0.2148,0.4922,0.7227}{\mathbb{P}}\in{\cal A}_{\epsilon^{2}+v(\DP)}^{k}({\mathbb{P}}_{n}^{\text{pred(NPL)}})}{\mathbb{E}}_{\xi\sim{\mathbb{P}}}[f_{x}(\xi)].

(53)

Since these two problems are equivalent, we refer to both of them as DRO-RoBAS.

An alternative formulation could be defined, based on the expected MMD distance i.e. by considering the set

\displaystyle\left\{{\mathbb{P}}\in{\cal P}_{k}(\Xi):{\mathbb{E}}_{\DP_{\xi_{1:n}}}\left[\mathbb{D}_{k}({\mathbb{P}},{\mathbb{P}}_{\theta_{k}({\mathbb{Q}})})\right]\leq\epsilon\right\}

(54)

for tolerance level
\epsilon\geq 0
. This mirrors the BAS
PE
in
Dellaporta et al. (2024)
and could lead to a differently shaped ambiguity set as it corresponds to an expected MMD-ball. However, deriving the dual formulation for the corresponding worst-case DRO problem based on this set is challenging as it involves deriving the convex conjugate of the expected MMD distance
({\mathbb{E}}_{\DP_{\xi_{1:n}}}[\mathbb{D}_{k}(\cdot,{\mathbb{P}}_{\theta_{k}({\mathbb{Q}})})])^{*}
or equivalently the support function
\delta^{\star}_{{\cal C}}(g)
of the corresponding set
{\cal C}:=\{\mu\in{\cal H}_{k}:{\mathbb{E}}_{{\mathbb{Q}}\sim\DP(c^{\prime},{\mathbb{F}}^{\prime})}\left[\|\mu-\mu_{{\mathbb{P}}_{\theta_{k}({\mathbb{Q}})}}\|_{k}\right]\leq\epsilon\}
. This is not trivial and it has not been tackled in this work, however it poses itself as important future work as it can open the way to ambiguity sets whose shape itself is informed by the posterior rather than just their nominal distribution as in RoBAS.

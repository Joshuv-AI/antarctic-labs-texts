On rank-two parabolic-type vertex operator algebras
Jianqi Liu, Levi Segal

Abstract
. In this paper, we undertake a systematic study of the parabolic-type sub-vertex operator algebras (VOAs) V P V_{P} of rank-two lattice VOAs V L V_{L} , originally introduced by the first-named author. We first classify all possible types of such subVOAs by analyzing the corresponding submonoids P ⊆ L P\subseteq L . For each type of V P V_{P} , we then classify the irreducible modules and determine the fusion rules among them. Finally, we show that the simple quotient V H V_{H} of any parabolic-type subVOA V P V_{P} is a C 1 C_{1} -cofinite, irrational VOA satisfying the strongly unital property recently introduced by Damiolini–Gibney–Krashen.



1.
Introduction

Parabolic-type subVOAs of lattice VOAs, introduced by the first-named author
[
Liu25
]
, arise naturally in the quasi-triangular decomposition of lattice VOAs and exhibit many properties analogous to parabolic subalgebras of semisimple Lie algebras. These subVOAs also provide natural solutions to the operator-form classical Yang–Baxter equations on VOAs
[
BGL
,
BGLW
]
. In
[
Liu25(1)
]
, a standard parabolic-type subVOA
V_{P}
of the rank-two lattice VOA
V_{A_{2}}
was used as a motivating example to study degree-zero induction for the embedding
V_{P}\hookrightarrow V_{A_{2}}
. In this paper, we develop a complete structural and representation-theoretic theory for all parabolic-type subVOAs of rank-two lattice VOAs, generalizing the rank-one results
[
Liu25
, Section 6]
as well as the special rank-two type-A case in
[
Liu25(1)
]
. Our methods naturally extend to higher-rank lattices. Furthermore, the simple quotients
V_{H}
of these rank-two parabolic-type VOAs form a natural class of CFT-type
C_{1}
-cofinite irrational VOAs whose mode transition algebras
\mathfrak{A}_{d}
, recently introduced by Damiolini–Gibney–Krashen
[
DGK25
]
, satisfy the strongly unital property.

The lattice vertex operator algebra
V_{L}
, introduced by Frenkel–Lepowsky–Meurman
[
FLM88
]
, is the first and most fundamental example of a VOA and plays a central role in the theory. For example, twisted modules and orbifold subVOAs of the Leech lattice VOA
V_{\Lambda}
led to the original construction of the moonshine module
V^{\natural}

[
FLM88
]
, and
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
- and
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
-orbifolds of lattice VOAs were crucial in the proof of Schellekens’ conjecture
[
S93
]
on holomorphic VOAs of central charge
24

[
vEMS20
,
Lam11
,
LS15
,
LS16
]
. Sub-lattice VOAs in affine VOAs also play key roles in the representation theory and rationality of parafermion VOAs
[
DR17
]
.

Although the representation theory of lattice VOAs is well understood
[
D93
,
D94
,
DL93
]
, their structural theory continues to yield interesting results. In studying solutions to the classical Yang–Baxter equations on VOAs
[
BGL
,
BGLW
]
, we observed that a lattice VOA
V_{L}=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in L}M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\alpha)
is structurally analogous to a semisimple Lie algebra if we regard
L
as an analog of the root system and the Heisenberg modules
M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\alpha)
as an analog of the Cartan subalgebra. By taking submonoids
M\subset L
, one obtains many subVOAs
V_{M}=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in M}M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\alpha)
of
V_{L}
. Some of these subVOAs are particularly notable. For instance, a subVOA
V_{B}\leq V_{A_{2}}
of this kind provides an example of a CFT-type,
C_{1}
-cofinite subVOA whose Zhu algebra
A(V_{B})
is not noetherian
[
Liu25
, Section 5.2]
; we call such a VOA a Borel-type subVOA of
V_{A_{2}}
, a special type of rank-two parabolic-type VOA. Another standard rank-two parabolic-type subVOA
V_{P}\leq V_{A_{2}}
served as a motivating example for the definition of degree-zero induction for VOA embeddings
[
Liu25(1)
]
, and its Zhu algebra
A(V_{P})
was shown to be a nilpotent extension of a skew-polynomial ring. Motivated by these intriguing properties, we provide a systematic study of rank-two parabolic-type VOAs in this paper, extending the previous work of the first-named author in
[
Liu25
, Section 6]
.

To state our main results and outline their proofs, we first introduce some notation.
Analogous to a Borel subalgebra (resp. Borel subgroup) in a semisimple Lie algebra (resp. a reductive algebraic group), a
Borel-type submonoid
contains half of the “root spaces.” More precisely, it is a submonoid
B\leq L
such that
B\cup(-B)=L
and
B\cap(-B)=\{0\}
. One can show that there exists a unique hyperplane
P(\gamma)
in the Euclidean space
E
spanned by
L
such that
B
is contained in the positive half-space defined by
P(\gamma)
.

We say that a submonoid
P\leq L
is of
parabolic-type
if it contains a Borel-type submonoid
B\leq L
. A parabolic-type subVOA of
V_{L}
is then a subVOA

V_{P}=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in P}M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\alpha)

associated with a parabolic-type submonoid
P\leq L
. In particular, the parabolic-type subVOAs of a rank-two lattice VOA
V_{L}
are completely determined by the corresponding parabolic-type submonoids.
The following theorem provides the classification of parabolic-type submonoids in the rank-two case; see Theorem
2.8
for details.

Theorem A
.

Let
P
be a parabolic-type submonoid of a rank-two even lattice
L
. Then it has one of the following two types:

(1)

P=B
is a rank-two Borel-type submonoid. In this case,

P=(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\alpha)\cup(P^{+}(\gamma)\cap L),

where
P(\gamma)
is the unique hyperplane such that
B\subset P^{\geq 0}(\gamma)
and
P(\gamma)\cap L=\mathbb{Z}\alpha
for some
\alpha\in L
(possibly zero).

(2)

P=P^{\geq 0}(\gamma)\cap L
for some hyperplane
P(\gamma)\subset E
such that
P(\gamma)\cap L=\mathbb{Z}\alpha\neq\{0\}
.

See Figures
2
and
3
for an illustration in a standard rank-two lattice. We refer to
P
as
type-I
if
P=B
, and as
type-II
if
P=P^{\geq 0}(\gamma)\cap L
with
P(\gamma)\cap L\neq\{0\}
.

The proof of Theorem
A
involves a technical argument regarding the existence of a basis of the rank-two lattice
L
contained in a particular submonoid, see Proposition
2.7
. In analogy with semisimple Lie algebras, where a parabolic subalgebra
\mathfrak{p}\leq\mathfrak{g}
contains a “Cartan-part”

. For example, in type

is generated by block-diagonal matrices. The representation theory of

is governed by

. Our second main theorem shows that a similar Cartan-part subVOA

exists in any rank-two parabolic-type subVOA

, further justifying the terminology of “parabolic-type subVOA”; see Proposition
3.8
and Theorem

Theorem B
.

Let
V_{P}
be a rank-two parabolic-type VOA. Then there exists a unique maximal proper ideal
V^{+}\lhd V_{P}
and a simple subVOA
V_{H}\leq V_{P}
such that

V_{P}=V_{H}\oplus V^{+}\quad\text{as vector spaces,}\quad\text{and}\quad V_{P}/V^{+}\cong V_{H}\quad\text{as VOAs.}

Moreover, the simple quotient
V_{H}
admits the following characterization:

(1)

If
P=B
is of type-I, then
V_{H}=M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,0)
is the rank-two Heisenberg VOA.

(2)

If
P
is of type-II, with
P(\gamma)\cap L=\mathbb{Z}\alpha
, then

V_{H}=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,n\alpha)\cong M_{\widehat{\mathbb{C}\beta}}(1,0)\otimes V_{\mathbb{Z}\alpha},

where
V_{\mathbb{Z}\alpha}
is the rank-one lattice VOA associated to
\mathbb{Z}\alpha
,
\beta\in\mathfrak{h}=\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}L
satisfies
\mathbb{C}\beta=(\mathbb{C}\alpha)^{\perp}
, and
M_{\widehat{\mathbb{C}\beta}}(1,0)
is the rank-one Heisenberg VOA associated to
\mathbb{C}\beta
.

We refer to
V_{H}
as the
Cartan-part
of
V_{P}
.

It is straightforward to see that
V_{P}
admits a split decomposition
V_{P}=V_{H}\oplus V^{+}
into a simple subVOA and an ideal. However, in the type-II case, the isomorphism

V_{H}\cong M_{\widehat{\mathbb{C}\beta}}(1,0)\otimes V_{\mathbb{Z}\alpha}

is not immediately obvious. This structure was strongly suggested by computations of the Zhu algebra for the standard rank-two type-
A
parabolic-type subVOA

in
[
Liu25(1)
]
. To rigorously establish the isomorphism of VOAs, we carefully analyze the behavior of lattice vertex operators, as defined by Frenkel-Lepowsky-Meurman
[
FLM88
]
, under the decomposition

\mathfrak{h}=\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}L=\mathbb{C}\alpha\oplus(\mathbb{C}\alpha)^{\perp}
, see Proposition
3.8
.
With the explicit structural description of
V_{P}
in Theorem
B
, one can readily verify the
C_{1}
-cofiniteness of
V_{H}
and, under mild conditions on the lattice
L
(
3.21
), also of
V_{P}
itself; see Theorem
3.14
. In particular,
V_{H}
is always
C_{1}
-cofinite, while
V_{P}
is
C_{1}
-cofinite only under the specified condition.

Similar to the situation in classical Lie theory, the representation theory of
V_{P}=V_{H}\oplus V^{+}
is governed by its Cartan part
V_{H}
. In particular, the maximal ideal
V^{+}
acts trivially on any irreducible
V_{P}
-module
M
, and the irreducible

-modules are in one-to-one correspondence with irreducible

-modules. However, given an irreducible

-module

acts as zero on

To establish this, we employ the crucial tool of Zhu’s algebra

[
Z96
]
. We show that the image

forms a nil ideal in

, which then acts as zero on any irreducible

-module

-module
(M,Y_{M})
, one can extend it to an irreducible

-module

by defining

. The following theorem provides a complete classification of irreducible

-modules, extending the rank-two type-

[

Theorem C
.

Let
V_{P}=V_{H}\oplus V^{+}
be a rank-two parabolic-type VOA, and let
M=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}M(n)
be an irreducible admissible

-module. Then
M
is an irreducible
V_{H}
-module on which

acts trivially. In particular, every irreducible admissible

-module is ordinary. Furthermore,

(1)

If

is of type-I, then

\{(M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\lambda),\tilde{Y}_{M}):\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}\}

form a complete list of irreducible

-modules up to isomorphism.

(2)

If

is of type-II, with

, and
\mathbb{C}\beta=(\mathbb{C}\alpha)^{\perp}
, then

\Bigl\{\bigl(L^{(\mu,i)}=M_{\widehat{\mathbb{C}\beta}}(1,\mu)\otimes V_{\mathbb{Z}\alpha+\frac{i}{2N}\alpha},\tilde{Y}_{M}\bigr):\mu\in\mathbb{C}\beta,\,i=0,1,\dots,2N-1\Bigr\}

-modules up to isomorphism.

With this classification, the fusion rules among irreducible
V_{P}
-modules follow immediately from the known formula for fusion rules of tensor product VOAs given by Abe-Dong-Li
[

]
, see Theorem
4.12
.

In a recent study of the smoothing property of conformal blocks associated with VOA modules, Damiolini-Gibney-Krashen
[
DGK25
]
introduced a sequence of associative algebras, called the
mode transition algebras

\mathfrak{A}_{d}
for
d\in\mathbb{N}
. These algebras are defined as tensor products of certain subquotients of the universal enveloping algebra
\mathscr{U}(V)
of a VOA
V
, and they satisfy the property that
\mathfrak{A}_{d}
surjects onto the kernel of the canonical map
\mathsf{A}_{d}\to\mathsf{A}_{d-1}
between higher-level Zhu’s algebras
[
DLM98(2)
]
(see Definition
5.2
). It was shown in
[

, Remark 3.4.6]
that if

is rational, then
\mathfrak{A}_{d}
possesses a
strong identity
for all
d\in\mathbb{N}
, which is equivalent to the smoothing property for VOA-conformal blocks
[
DGK25
, Theorem 5.0.3]
. However, rationality is not necessary for the existence of strong identity in
\mathfrak{A}_{d}
; for example, it was proved in
[
DGK24
]
that the Heisenberg VOA satisfies the strongly unital condition for mode transition algebras.
Since rationality is preserved under tensor products of VOAs
[
DMZ94
]
and the Cartan-part subVOA
V_{H}
of a type-II parabolic-type VOA

is a tensor product of two VOAs,

and

also satisfies this property. Our final main theorem confirms this expectation.

Theorem D
.

Let
V_{P}=V_{H}\oplus V^{+}
be a rank-two parabolic-type VOA. Then its Cartan-part subVOA
V_{H}
satisfies the strongly unital condition for mode transition algebras.

\beta(m)Y(u,z)=Y(u,z)\beta(m),\quad m\in\mathbb{Z}.

(3.14)

Proof.

By (
3.2
) we have
[\beta(m),\alpha(n)]=0
for any
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Hence
\beta(m)
commutes with

and
E^{\pm}(-p\alpha,z)
for any
n\geq 1
and

. Then by (
3.10
), it suffices to show that
\beta(m)
commutes with the operator
e_{p\alpha}z^{p\alpha}
in
\mathrm{End}(V_{L})[\![z,z^{-1}]\!]
.

Indeed, if
m\neq 0
, by (
3.7
) and (
3.8
),
\beta(m)=\beta(m)\otimes 1
clearly commutes with
e_{p\alpha}z^{p\alpha}=1\otimes e_{p\alpha}z^{p\alpha}
as operators on
M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\theta)\cong M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,0)\otimes\mathbb{C}e^{\theta}\subset V_{L}
for any
\theta\in L
. On the other hand, if
m=0
, then
\beta(0)=1\otimes\beta(0)
as an operator on

. Since

\displaystyle(\beta(0)\circ(e_{p\alpha}z^{p\alpha}))e^{\theta}

\displaystyle=\beta(0)\epsilon(p\alpha,\theta)z^{(p\alpha|\theta)}e^{p\alpha+\theta}=(\beta|p\alpha+\theta)\epsilon(p\alpha,\theta)z^{(p\alpha|\theta)}e^{p\alpha+\theta}

\displaystyle=(\beta|\theta)\epsilon(p\alpha,\theta)z^{(p\alpha|\theta)}e^{p\alpha+\theta}=((e_{p\alpha}z^{p\alpha})\circ\beta(0))e^{\theta},

for any
\theta\in L
. This proves (

).
∎

Proposition 3.8
.

The rank-two hyperplane VOA
V_{H}
(
3.12
) is simple and is isomorphic to the tensor product of a rank-one Heisenberg VOA and a rank-one lattice VOA:

M_{\widehat{\mathbb{C}\beta}}(1,0)\otimes V_{\mathbb{Z}\alpha},

where
\mathbb{C}\beta=(\mathbb{C}\alpha)^{\perp}
in
\mathfrak{h}=\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}L
with respect to the extended
\mathbb{C}
-bilinear form
(\cdot|\cdot):\mathfrak{h}\times\mathfrak{h}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
.

Proof.

Since
\mathfrak{h}=\mathbb{C}\alpha\oplus\mathbb{C}\beta
and
[\beta(n),\alpha(m)]=0
for all
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}
, the rank-two Heisenberg irreducible module
M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,p\alpha)
has a basis
\beta(-n_{1})\dots\beta(-n_{r})\alpha(-m_{1})\dots\alpha(-m_{s})e^{p\alpha}
, where

,
n_{1}\geq\dots\geq n_{r}\geq 1
and

, see (

). Then we have an isomorphism of vector spaces:

\displaystyle\varphi:M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,p\alpha)

\displaystyle\rightarrow M_{\widehat{\mathbb{C}\beta}}(1,0)\otimes M_{\widehat{\mathbb{C}\alpha}}(1,p\alpha),

(3.15)

\displaystyle\beta(-n_{1})\dots\beta(-n_{r})\alpha(-m_{1})\dots\alpha(-m_{s})e^{p\alpha}

\displaystyle\mapsto\beta(-n_{1})\dots\beta(-n_{r})\mathbf{1}\otimes\alpha(-m_{1})\dots\alpha(-m_{s})e^{p\alpha},

which extends to a linear isomorphism

\varphi:V_{H}=\bigoplus_{p\in\mathbb{Z}}M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,p\alpha)\xrightarrow{\sim}M_{\widehat{\mathbb{C}\beta}}(1,0)\otimes\left(\bigoplus_{p\in\mathbb{Z}}M_{\widehat{\mathbb{C}\alpha}}(1,p\alpha)\right)=M_{\widehat{\mathbb{C}\beta}}(1,0)\otimes V_{\mathbb{Z}\alpha}.

Denote
M_{\widehat{\mathbb{C}\beta}}(1,0)
by
V_{1}
and
V_{\mathbb{Z}\alpha}
by
V_{2}
for short. We claim that

\varphi(Y_{V_{H}}(u,z)v)=Y_{V_{1}\otimes V_{2}}(\varphi(u),z)\varphi(v),

(3.16)

for

and
v=\beta(-n^{\prime}_{1})\dots\beta(-n^{\prime}_{r^{\prime}})\alpha(-m^{\prime}_{1})\dots\alpha(-m^{\prime}_{s^{\prime}})e^{q\alpha}
.

Indeed, by the commutativity of the lattice operators
\beta(n)
and
Y(\alpha(-m_{1})\dots\alpha(-m_{s})e^{p\alpha},z)
as in Lemma
3.7
, together with (
3.10
), it is easy to see that

\displaystyle Y_{V_{H}}(u,z)v

\displaystyle=\left({\tiny\begin{matrix}\circ\\
\circ\end{matrix}}(\partial_{z}^{(n_{1}-1)}\beta(z))\dots(\partial_{z}^{(n_{r}-1)}\beta(z)){\tiny\begin{matrix}\circ\\
\circ\end{matrix}}\right)\beta(-n^{\prime}_{1})\dots\beta(-n^{\prime}_{r^{\prime}})

\displaystyle\quad\cdot\left({\tiny\begin{matrix}\circ\\
\circ\end{matrix}}(\partial_{z}^{(m_{1}-1)}\alpha(z))\dots(\partial_{z}^{(m_{s}-1)}\alpha(z))Y(e^{p\alpha},z){\tiny\begin{matrix}\circ\\
\circ\end{matrix}}\right)\alpha(-m^{\prime}_{1})\dots\alpha(-m^{\prime}_{s^{\prime}})e^{q\alpha}.

Then it follows from Lemma
3.6
and (
3.15
), together with the definition of vertex operators (
3.5
) and (
3.10
) that

\displaystyle\varphi(Y_{V_{H}}(u,z)v)

\displaystyle=\left({\tiny\begin{matrix}\circ\\
\circ\end{matrix}}(\partial_{z}^{(n_{1}-1)}\beta(z))\dots(\partial_{z}^{(n_{r}-1)}\beta(z)){\tiny\begin{matrix}\circ\\
\circ\end{matrix}}\right)\beta(-n^{\prime}_{1})\dots\beta(-n^{\prime}_{r^{\prime}})\mathbf{1}

\displaystyle\quad\otimes\left({\tiny\begin{matrix}\circ\\

\displaystyle=Y_{V_{1}}(\beta(-n_{1})\dots\beta(-n_{r})\mathbf{1},z)\beta(-n^{\prime}_{1})\dots\beta(-n^{\prime}_{r^{\prime}})\mathbf{1}

\displaystyle\quad\otimes Y_{V_{2}}(\alpha(-m_{1})\dots\alpha(-m_{s})e^{p\alpha},z)\alpha(-m^{\prime}_{1})\dots\alpha(-m^{\prime}_{s^{\prime}})e^{q\alpha}

\displaystyle=Y_{V_{1}\otimes V_{2}}(\beta(-n_{1})\dots\beta(-n_{r})\mathbf{1}\otimes\alpha(-m_{1})\dots\alpha(-m_{s})e^{p\alpha},z)

\displaystyle\quad\cdot\beta(-n^{\prime}_{1})\dots\beta(-n^{\prime}_{r^{\prime}})\otimes\alpha(-m^{\prime}_{1})\dots\alpha(-m^{\prime}_{s^{\prime}})e^{q\alpha}

3.16
). Finally, let
\beta_{1}=\beta/\|\beta\|
and
\alpha_{1}=\alpha/\|\alpha\|
, where we define
\|h\|
to be the principal branch of
(h|h)^{\frac{1}{2}}
for

. Then
\{\beta_{1},\alpha_{1}\}
is an orthonormal basis of

and
\omega_{V_{H}}=\frac{1}{2}\beta_{1}(-1)^{2}\mathbf{1}+\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{1}(-1)^{2}\mathbf{1}
. Moreover,

.

\displaystyle\varphi(\omega_{V_{H}})=\frac{1}{2}\beta_{1}(-1)^{2}\mathbf{1}\otimes\mathbf{1}+\mathbf{1}\otimes\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{1}(-1)^{2}\mathbf{1}=\omega_{V_{1}}\otimes\mathbf{1}+\mathbf{1}\otimes\omega_{V_{2}}=\omega_{V_{1}\otimes V_{2}}.

is an isomorphism of VOAs. Since the Heisenberg VOA

and the lattice VOA

are both simple, by Lemma

,

is a simple VOA.
∎

3.3.
Structure of the rank-two parabolic-type VOAs
V_{P}
and
C_{1}
-cofiniteness

Recall that the rank-two parabolic-type submonoids have the following classification, see Theorem
2.8
.

\displaystyle P=B=\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\alpha\cup(P^{+}(\gamma)\cap L),

\displaystyle\mathrm{where}\quad P(\gamma)\cap L=\mathbb{Z}\alpha,

\displaystyle\mathrm{if}\ P\ \mathrm{is\ of\ type\ I},

(3.17)

\displaystyle P=P^{\geq 0}(\gamma)\cap L,

\displaystyle\mathrm{where}\quad P(\gamma)\cap L=\mathbb{Z}\alpha\neq\{0\},

\displaystyle\mathrm{if}\ P\ \mathrm{is\ of\ type\ II}.

Now we determine the structures of rank-two parabolic-type VOAs.

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
V_{P}
be a rank-two parabolic-type VOA. Then there exists a unique maximum proper ideal
V^{+}\lhd V_{P}
and a simple subVOA
V_{H}\leq V_{P}
such that
V_{P}=V_{H}\oplus V^{+}
as vector spaces and
V_{P}/V^{+}\cong V_{H}
as VOAs. Moreover, we have the following characterization for the simple quotient VOA
V_{H}
:

(1)

If
P=B
is of type-I, then
V_{H}=M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,0)
is the rank-two Heisenberg VOA.

(2)

If
P
is of type-II, then
V_{H}\cong M_{\widehat{\mathbb{C}\beta}}(1,0)\otimes V_{\mathbb{Z}\alpha}
is the hyperplane VOA (see (
3.12
) and Proposition
3.8
), where
\beta\in\mathfrak{h}=\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}L
such that
\mathbb{C}\beta=(\mathbb{C}\alpha)^{\perp}.

We call the subVOA
V_{H}
the
Cartan-part
of
V_{P}
.

Proof.

Assume
P
is of type-I. Let
S:=P\backslash\{0\}=\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\alpha\cup(P^{+}(\gamma)\cap L)
in view of (
3.17
). Clearly,
S\subset P
is a sub-semigroup such that
P+S\subseteq S
. Let
V^{+}:=V_{S}=\bigoplus_{\eta\in S}M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\eta)
. Then by Proposition
3.3
,
V^{+}\lhd V_{P}
is an ideal. Moreover, we have
V_{P}=M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,0)\oplus V^{+}
, with
V_{H}:=M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,0)
being a subVOA of
V_{P}
. Hence
V_{P}/V^{+}\cong V_{H}
as VOAs. It remains that show that
V^{+}
is the maximum proper ideal of
V_{P}
.

Indeed, let
J\lhd V_{P}
be a proper ideal. It suffices to show
J\subseteq V^{+}
. Since
J
is closed under the action of the field
Y(h(-1)\mathbf{1},z)=h(z)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}h(n)z^{-n-1}
, we have

J=\bigoplus_{\eta\in P}M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\eta)\cap J=\bigoplus_{\eta\in P}J_{\eta}.

(3.18)

If
J_{0}=M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,0)\cap J=0
, then
J\subseteq V^{+}
, we are done. Otherwise,
J_{0}=M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,0)
since
M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,0)
is a simple VOA. Then
\mathbf{1}\in M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,0)\in J
and
J=V_{P}
, which contradicts the properness of
J
.

Now assume
P
is of type-II. Let
S:=P^{+}(\gamma)\cap L
and
V^{+}:=V_{S}
. Then by (
3.17
), we have
P=S\sqcup H
, with
H=P(\gamma)\cap L=\mathbb{Z}\alpha
and
P+S\subseteq S
. it follows that
V_{P}=V_{H}\oplus V^{+}
,
V_{H}\leq V_{P}
is a subVOA, and
V^{+}\lhd V_{P}
is an ideal. Hence
V_{P}/V^{+}\cong V_{H}
as VOAs. To show
V^{+}
is the maximum proper ideal of
V_{P}
, we let
J\lhd V_{P}
be a proper ideal. Then by (
3.18
),
J\cap V_{H}=\bigoplus_{\tau\in H}J_{\tau}
is an ideal of the subVOA
V_{H}
. Since
\mathbf{1}\in V_{H}
, we have
J\cap V_{H}\neq V_{H}
. But
V_{H}
is a simple VOA by Proposition
3.8
. Hence
J\cap V^{+}=0
and
J=J\cap V^{+}\subseteq V^{+}
.
∎

Remark 3.10
.

We note that the direct sum decomposition
V_{P}=V_{H}\oplus V^{+}
in Theorem
3.9
can be viewed as a direct sum of a CFT-type VOA
V_{H}
with a
V_{H}
-module
V^{+}
. However, this direct sum is
not
the semi-direct product of the VOA
V_{H}
with the module
V^{+}
introduced by Li in
[
L94
]
since
Y_{V_{P}}(V^{+},z)V^{+}\neq 0
.

With the structure theorem for
V_{P}
, we discuss its
C_{1}
-cofiniteness. The subspace
C_{1}(V)\subset V
was introduced by Li in
[
Li99
]

C_{1}(V):=\mathrm{span}\{a_{-1}b:a,b\in V_{+}\}+\mathrm{span}\{L(-1)v:v\in V_{+}\}.

(3.19)

V
is called
C_{1}
-cofinite if
\dim V/C_{1}(V)<\infty
.

A related notion is the strongly generation property of VOAs introduced by Kac in
[
K97
]
. A CFT-type VOA

is called
strongly generated
by a subset
U\subseteq V
if
V
is spanned by

u^{1}_{-n_{1}}u^{2}_{-n_{2}}\dots u^{k}_{-n_{k}}u,\quad\mathrm{where}\quad u^{1},\dots,u^{k},u\in U,\quad n_{1}\geq n_{2}\geq\dots\geq n_{k}\geq 1.

(3.20)

Karel and Li proved that a VOA
V
is strongly generated by a finite-dimensional subspace
U\subset V
if and only if
V
is
C_{1}
-cofinite
[
Li99
,
KL99
]
.

Proposition 3.11
.

Let
V_{P}
be a rank-two parabolic-type VOA. Then its Cartan-part subVOA
V_{H}
is always
C_{1}
-cofinite.

Proof.

It is well-known that the Heisenberg VOA
M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,0)
and the lattice VOA
V_{L}
are
C_{1}
-cofinite
[
KL99
]
. By Theorem
3.9
, to show
V_{H}
is
C_{1}
-cofinite, it suffices to show if two CFT-type VOAs
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
are
C_{1}
-cofinite, then their tensor product

is also
C_{1}
-cofinite. By (
3.13
), it is clear that
C_{1}(V_{1})\otimes V_{2}+V_{1}\otimes C_{1}(V_{2})\subseteq C_{1}(V_{1}\otimes V_{2})
. Conversely, for
a^{1}\otimes a^{2},b^{1}\otimes b^{2}\in(V_{1}\otimes V_{2})_{+}
, since

We have

V_{1}\otimes V_{2}/C_{1}(V_{1}\otimes V_{2})\cong(V_{1}/C_{1}(V_{1}))\otimes(V_{2}/C_{1}(V_{2}))

∎

Now we discuss the

-cofiniteness of the rank-two parabolic-type VOAs

.

Let

(2)

-cofinite if the following condition holds: Let

(3.21)

.

Let

be a CFT-type VOA, and

4.
Representation theory of
V_{P}

In this Section, we present a detailed investigation of the representation theory of the rank-two parabolic-type VOA
V_{P}
. In particular, we classify all the irreducible modules over
V_{P}
and determine the fusion rules among them. Our main results indicate that the representation theory of the parabolic-type VOA
V_{P}
is governed by its Cartan-part
V_{H}
, generalizing the representation theoretical results of the rank-one Borel-type VOAs
[
Liu25
, Theorem 6.13]
.

4.1.
Nil ideal in the Zhu’s algebra
A(V_{P})

We want to show that the maximum ideal
V^{+}
of
V_{P}
(see Theorem
3.9
) acts as
0
on any irreducible
V_{P}
-module. So that the irreducible modules of
V_{P}
are in one-to-one correspondence with the irreducible modules over its Cartan-part
V_{H}
.
However, this is not obvious from the definition of
V_{P}
. We invoke the important tool of Zhu’s algebra
\mathsf{A}=A(V)

[
Z96
]
to prove this claim.

First, we recall the definition and basic properties
A(V)
. Let
(V,Y,\mathbf{1},\omega)
be a VOA. For homogeneous elements
a,b\in V
, define

\displaystyle a\circ b

\displaystyle:=\Res_{z=0}Y(a,z)b\frac{(1+z)^{\mathrm{wt}a}}{z^{2}}=\sum_{j\geq 0}\binom{\mathrm{wt}a}{j}a_{j-2}b,

(4.1)

\displaystyle a\ast b

\displaystyle:=\Res_{z=0}Y(a,z)b\frac{(1+z)^{\mathrm{wt}a}}{z}=\sum_{j\geq 0}\binom{\mathrm{wt}a}{j}a_{j-1}b.

(4.2)

Let
O(V)=\mathrm{span}\{a\circ b:a,b\in V\}
, and let
A(V):=V/O(V)
. By
[
Z96
, Theorem 2.1.1]
,
O(V)
is a two-sided ideal with respect to
\ast
, and
A(V)
is an associative algebra with respect to
\ast
, with the unit element
[\mathbf{1}]
. The following formulas can be found in
[
Z96
, Lemma 2.1.3]
:

\displaystyle a\ast b\equiv\Res_{z=0}Y(b,z)a\frac{(1+z)^{\mathrm{wt}b-1}}{z}\pmod{O(V)},

(4.3)

\displaystyle\Res_{z=0}Y(a,z)b\frac{(1+z)^{\mathrm{wt}a+n}}{z^{2+m}}\equiv 0\pmod{O(V)},

(4.4)

where
a,b\in V
are homogeneous, and
m\geq n\geq 0
. Recall the following fundamental facts about Zhu’s algebra.

Lemma 4.1
.

[
Z96
, Theorem 2.2.2]

Let
W
be a weak
V
-module. Then the subspace

\Omega(W)=\mathrm{span}\{w\in W:a_{k}w=0,\ \deg(a_{k})<0\}

is a left
A(V)
-module via
o:A(V)\rightarrow\mathrm{End}(\Omega(W)),[a]\mapsto o(a)=a_{\mathrm{wt}a-1}
.
In particular, the degree-zero subspace
M(0)
of an admissible
V
-module
M=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}M(n)
a left
A(V)
-module.

Moreover,
M\mapsto M(0)
is a one-to-one correspondence between the isomorphism class of irreducible admissible
V
-modules and the isomorphism class of irreducible
A(V)
-modules. In particular,
A(V)
is a finite-dimensional semisimple algebra if
V
is rational.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
V_{P}
be a rank-two parabolic-type VOA. Then

. Furthermore, if

is a nilpotent ideal of

Proof.

By Theorem

A(V^{+})=\mathrm{span}\{[M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\beta)]:\beta\in S\}

.

4.2.

By Theorem

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
V_{P}
be a rank-two parabolic-type VOA and
M=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}M(n)
be an irreducible admissible
V_{P}
-module. Then the action of the ideal
V^{+}
on
M
must be zero.

Proof.

Since
V^{+}
is an ideal of
V_{P}
, its action on
M
gives rise to an admissible submodule
N=V^{+}.M
which has spanning elements

a^{1}_{n_{1}}\dots a^{r}_{n_{r}}w,\quad\mathrm{where}\quad r\geq 1,\ a^{i}\in V^{+},\ n_{i}\in\mathbb{Z},\ w\in M.

(4.6)

We need to show that
N=0
.

Indeed, suppose
N\neq 0
. Since
M
is irreducible, then
N=M
. We can also fix a nonzero element
u\in M(0)
and write

M=\mathrm{span}\{b_{m}u:b\in V_{P},m\in\mathbb{Z}\}
, see
[
LL04
, Proposition 4.5.7]
. Then it follows from (
4.6
) that
M(0)=N(0)
has spanning elements

a^{1}_{n_{1}}\dots a^{r}_{n_{r}}b_{m}u,\quad\mathrm{where}\quad r\geq 1,\ a^{i}\in V^{+},\ b\in V_{P},\ \sum_{i=1}^{r}\deg(a^{i}_{n_{i}})+\deg(b_{m})=0.

(4.7)

We use induction on
r\geq 1
to show that elements of the form (
4.7
) are
0
.

Consider the base case
r=1
. Note that
b_{m}u=0
if
\deg(b_{m})<0
. Hence we may assume
\deg(a^{1}_{n_{1}})\leq 0
in
a^{1}_{n_{1}}b_{m}u
. By Corollary
4.4
we have
o(V^{+})M(0)=0
.
If
\deg(a^{1}_{n_{1}})=0
, then
\deg(b_{m})=0
and

a^{1}_{n_{1}}b_{m}u=o(a^{1})o(b)u=0
since
a^{1}\in V^{+}
and
o(b)u\in M(0)
. If
\deg(a^{1}_{n_{1}})<0
, then

a^{1}_{n_{1}}u=0
and

a^{1}_{n_{1}}b_{m}u=[a^{1}_{n_{1}},b_{m}]u=\sum_{j\geq 0}\binom{n_{1}}{j}(a^{1}_{j}b)_{n_{1}+m-j}u=\sum_{j\geq 0}\binom{n_{1}}{j}o(a^{1}_{j}b)u=0,

since
\deg(a^{1}_{n_{1}})+\deg(b_{m})=0
and
a^{1}_{j}b\in V^{+}
for all
j\geq 0
. Now assume (
4.7
) holds for smaller
r
. If
\deg(a^{1}_{n_{1}})=\dots=\deg(a^{r}_{n_{r}})=0
then
a^{1}_{n_{1}}\dots a^{r}_{n_{r}}b_{m}u=o(a^{1})\dots o(a^{r})o(b)u=0
. Otherwise, there exists
i
such that
\deg(a^{i}_{n_{i}})<0
. Then

\displaystyle a^{1}_{n_{1}}\dots a^{r}_{n_{r}}b_{m}u

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=i}^{r}\sum_{j\geq 0}\binom{n_{i}}{j}a^{1}_{n_{1}}\dots\widehat{a^{i}_{n_{i}}}\dots(a^{i}_{j}a^{k})_{n_{i}+n_{k}-j}\dots a^{r}_{n_{r}}b_{m}u

\displaystyle+\sum_{j\geq 0}\binom{n_{i}}{j}a^{1}_{n_{1}}\dots\widehat{a^{i}_{n_{i}}}\dots a^{r}_{n_{r}}(a^{i}_{j}b)_{n_{i}+m-j}u.

Since each summand on the right-hand-side has shorter length,

by the induction hypothesis. Thus,

, which is a contradiction.
∎

We also need the following result about representation of tensor product associative algebras.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
A
be a finite-dimensional semisimple algebra over
\mathbb{C}
, with irreducible modules
S^{1},\dots,S^{r}
up to isomorphism, and let
B=\mathbb{C}[x]\otimes A
be the tensor product associative algebra. For any
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
, let
e^{\lambda}
be a formal symbol. Then
\mathbb{C}e^{\lambda}\otimes S^{i}
is an irreducible
B
-module with

\rho:B\rightarrow\mathrm{End}(\mathbb{C}e^{\lambda}\otimes S^{i}),\quad\rho(f(x)\otimes a)(e^{\lambda}\otimes u)=f(\lambda)\cdot e^{\lambda}\otimes a.u,\quad a\in A,u\in S^{i}.

(4.8)

Furthermore, any irreducible
B
-module is isomorphic to
\mathbb{C}e^{\lambda}\otimes S^{i}
for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
and
1\leq i\leq r
.

Proof.

Clearly,
\mathbb{C}e^{\lambda}\otimes S^{i}
is an irreducible
B
-module via (
4.8
).
However, it is not straightforward that any irreducible
B
-module has the form
\mathbb{C}e^{\lambda}\otimes S^{i}
, since the subalgebra
\mathbb{C}[x]
of
B
is not finite-dimensional.

Since
A=M_{n_{1}}(\mathbb{C})\times\dots\times M_{n_{r}}(\mathbb{C})
by the Artin-Wedderburn’s theorem, it suffices to show any irreducible module over the tensor product algebra
\mathbb{C}[x]\otimes M_{n}(\mathbb{C})\cong M_{n}(\mathbb{C}[x])
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{C}e^{\lambda}\otimes S
, where
S=\mathbb{C}^{n}
is the standard irreducible
M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
-module.

Let
M\neq 0
be an irreducible
M_{n}(\mathbb{C}[x])
-module. Since
M
is a module over the semisimple subalgebra
M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
, there exists a nonzero copy of
S=\mathbb{C}^{n}\subset M
. Let
N\subseteq M
be the
\mathbb{C}[x]
-submodule generated by
S
. i.e.,
N=\mathrm{span}\{f(x).u\in M:f(x)\in\mathbb{C}[x],\ u\in S\}
. Clearly,
N\leq M
is a nonzero
M_{n}(\mathbb{C}[x])
-submodule, and so
N=M
. Moreover,
\mathbb{C}[x]\otimes S\rightarrow M
,
f(x)\otimes u\mapsto f(x).u
is an epimorphism of
M_{n}(\mathbb{C}[x])
-modules. Then
M=(\mathbb{C}[x]\otimes S)/J
, where
J
is a maximal proper submodule of
\mathbb{C}[x]\otimes S\cong\mathbb{C}[x]^{n}=\mathbb{C}[x]\oplus\dots\oplus\mathbb{C}[x]
. We claim there exists
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
such that

J=\mathrm{span}\left\{\begin{bmatrix}f_{1}(x)\\
\vdots\\
f_{n}(x)\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{C}[x]^{n}:f_{i}(x)\in\langle x-\lambda\rangle,\ \forall 1\leq i\leq n\right\}.

(4.9)

Indeed, if there exists two elements
w_{1}=[\dots f_{i}(x)\dots]^{t}
and
w_{2}=[\dots g_{j}(x)\dots]^{t}
in
J
such that
\gcd(f_{i}(x),g_{j}(x))=1
, assume
a(x)f_{i}(x)+b(x)g_{j}(x)=1
for some
a(x),b(x)\in\mathbb{C}[x]
, then
(a(x)E_{1i}).w_{1}+(b(x)E_{1j}).w_{2}=[1,0,\dots,0]^{t}\in J
, where
E_{ij}\in M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
is the matrix unit. It follows that
\mathbb{C}[x]^{n}=M_{n}(\mathbb{C}[x]).[1,0,\dots,0]^{t}\subseteq J
, which contradicts the properness of
J
. Hence there exists a nonzero polynomial
p(x)
such that for any
[f_{1}(x),\dots,f_{n}(x)]^{t}\in J
, we have
p(x)|f_{i}(x)
for all
i
. It follows that
J\subseteq\langle p(x)\rangle^{n}=\langle p(x)\rangle\oplus\dots\oplus\langle p(x)\rangle
. By the maximality of
J
, we have
p(x)=x-\lambda
for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
, and
J=\langle p(x)\rangle^{n}
. This proves (
4.9
).

Now it follows from (
4.9
) that
M\cong\mathbb{C}[x]^{n}/\langle x-\lambda\rangle^{n}\cong(\mathbb{C}[x]/\langle x-\lambda\rangle)^{n}\cong\mathbb{C}e^{\lambda}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{n}
as a module over
M_{n}(\mathbb{C}[x])\cong\mathbb{C}[x]\otimes M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
.
∎

\mathscr{U}=\mathscr{U}(V)
of a VOA
V
.

\mathscr{U}=\bigoplus_{d\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathscr{U}_{d}
is a canonically seminorm graded associative algebra. It has left and right neighborhoods at
0
:

\mathrm{N}^{n}_{\mathsf{L}}\mathscr{U}=\mathscr{U}\cdot\mathscr{U}_{\leq-n}\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\mathrm{N}^{n}_{\mathsf{R}}\mathscr{U}=\mathscr{U}_{\geq n}\cdot\mathscr{U},

(5.1)

such that
\mathrm{N}^{n+1}_{\mathsf{L}}\mathscr{U}_{0}=\sum_{j\geq n+1}\mathscr{U}_{j}\cdot\mathscr{U}_{-j}=\mathrm{N}^{n+1}_{\mathsf{R}}\mathscr{U}_{0}
for any
n\geq 0
, see
[
DGK25
, Appendix A]
.
The Zhu algebra
\mathsf{A}=A(V)
has the following identification as an associative algebra.

\mathsf{A}\cong\frac{\mathscr{U}_{0}}{\mathrm{N}^{1}_{\mathsf{L}}\mathscr{U}_{0}}=\frac{\mathscr{U}_{0}}{\sum_{j\geq 1}\mathscr{U}_{j}\cdot\mathscr{U}_{-j}}

(5.2)

Denote the category of
\mathsf{A}=A(V)
-modules by
\mathsf{Mod}(\mathsf{A})
.

On the other hand, let
W
be a left module over the associative algebra
\mathscr{U}
equipped with discrete topology. If the module action map
\mathscr{U}\times W\rightarrow W
is continuous, then
W
must be a filtered module

W=\bigcup_{n=0}^{\infty}\Omega_{n}(W),\quad\mathrm{where}\quad\Omega_{n}(W)=\{w\in W:(\mathrm{N}^{n+1}_{\mathsf{L}}\mathscr{U}).w=0\}.

(5.3)

We call such a
\mathscr{U}
-module
W

exhaustive
or continuously discrete.
In particular, any admissible
V
-module
M=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}M(n)
is naturally an exhaustive
\mathscr{U}
-module, with
M(n)\subseteq\Omega_{n}(M)
for all
n
, see Definition
3.1
. Denote the category of exhaustive left
\mathscr{U}
-modules by
\mathsf{ExMod}(\mathscr{U})
. Then
\mathsf{Adm}(V)
is a full subcategory of
\mathsf{ExMod}(\mathscr{U})
.

It was observed in
[
DGK25
]
that
\Omega_{n}(W)
agrees with the space of degree-
n
highest-weight vectors in the weak
V
-module
W
introduced in
[
DLM98
]
. i.e.,

\Omega_{n}(W)=\{w\in W:a_{k}w=0,\ \deg(a_{k})\leq-n-1\}.

In particular,

\Omega:\mathsf{Adm}(V)\rightarrow\mathsf{Mod}(\mathsf{A}),\quad W\mapsto\Omega_{0}(W)

(5.4)

is a functor between abelian categories.

On the other hand, Dong-Li-Mason’s generalized Verma module functor
\bar{M}(\cdot)

[
DLM98
, Theorem 6.2]
can be identified with the following (left) induced module functor:

\Phi^{\mathsf{L}}:\mathsf{Mod}(\mathsf{A})\rightarrow\mathsf{Adm}(V)\subset\mathsf{ExMod}(\mathscr{U}),\quad S\mapsto\Phi^{\mathsf{L}}(S)=(\mathscr{U}/\mathrm{N}^{1}_{\mathsf{L}}\mathscr{U})\otimes_{\mathscr{U}_{0}}S.

(5.5)

The functors
\Omega
and
\Phi^{\mathsf{L}}
in (
5.4
) and (
5.5
) form an adjoint pair
\Phi^{\mathsf{L}}\dashv\Omega
between abelian categories

\Phi^{\mathsf{L}}:\mathsf{Mod}(\mathsf{A})\rightleftarrows\mathsf{Adm}(V):\Omega,

(5.6)

see
[
DGK25
, Proposition 3.1.2]
. The following Lemma is useful for our later discussion.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
V=V_{1}\otimes V_{2}
be a tensor product VOA. Then the functors
\Phi^{\mathsf{L}}
and
\Omega
, with
\mathsf{A}=A(V_{1}\otimes V_{2})
and
\mathscr{U}=\mathscr{U}(V_{1}\otimes V_{2})
, have the following properties

(1)

\Phi^{\mathsf{L}}(U)=\Phi^{\mathsf{L}}_{V_{1}}(\Phi^{\mathsf{L}}_{V_{2}}(U))=\Phi^{\mathsf{L}}_{V_{2}}(\Phi^{\mathsf{L}}_{V_{1}}(U))
for any
U\in\mathsf{Mod}(\mathsf{A})
.

(2)

\Omega(W)=\Omega_{V_{1}}(W)\cap\Omega_{V_{2}}(W)
for any
W\in\mathsf{Adm}(V).

Proof.

Both (1) and (2) are straightforward consequences of the fact that
(a_{1}\otimes a_{2})_{[n]}
is a linear combination of operators of the form
(a_{1}\otimes\mathbf{1})_{[p]}(\mathbf{1}\otimes a_{2})_{[q]}=(\mathbf{1}\otimes a_{2})_{[q]}(a_{1}\otimes\mathbf{1})_{[p]}
, see
[
FHL93
, eq. (4.7.3)]
. More precisely, given any weak
V=V_{1}\otimes V_{2}
-module
W
, by (
3.13
),

(a_{1}\otimes a_{2})_{[n]}.w=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\left((a_{1})_{[i]}\otimes(a_{2})_{[n-i-1]}\right).w=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}(a_{1}\otimes\mathbf{1})_{[i]}.((\mathbf{1}\otimes a_{2})_{[n-i-1]}.w).

(5.7)

Clearly, (
5.7
) makes
W=\Phi^{\mathsf{L}}_{V_{1}}(\Phi^{\mathsf{L}}_{V_{2}}(U))
an admissible
V
-module generated by
U
. Then there exists a canonical epimorphism
\pi:\Phi^{\mathsf{L}}(U)\rightarrow\Phi^{\mathsf{L}}_{V_{1}}(\Phi^{\mathsf{L}}_{V_{2}}(U))
. On the other hand, since
V_{1},V_{2}
are subVOAs of
V_{1}\otimes V_{2}
and the actions of
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
are commutative on a
V
-module, there exists a
V_{2}
-module homomorphism
\iota:\Phi^{\mathsf{L}}_{V_{2}}(U)\rightarrow\Phi^{\mathsf{L}}(U)
such that
\imaginary\iota\subseteq\Omega_{V_{1}}(\Phi^{\mathsf{L}}(U))
. Note that
\iota
also extends to a
V_{1}
-module homomorphism
\tau:\Phi_{V_{1}}^{\mathsf{L}}(\Phi_{V_{2}}^{\mathsf{L}}(U))\rightarrow\Phi^{\mathsf{L}}(U)
by
[
DLM98
, Theorem 6.2]
. By (
5.7
),
\tau
is also a
V
-homomorphism and is clearly an inverse of
\pi
. This proves (1).

Clearly,
\Omega(W)\subseteq\Omega_{V_{1}}(W)\cap\Omega_{V_{2}}(W)
since
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
are subVOAs of
V
. On the other hand, assume
\deg((a_{1}\otimes a_{2})_{[n]})=\mathrm{wt}a_{1}+\mathrm{wt}a_{2}-n-1<0
, then for any
w\in\Omega_{V_{1}}(W)\cap\Omega_{V_{2}}(W)
and any
i\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have

(a_{1}\otimes\mathbf{1})_{[i]}.((\mathbf{1}\otimes a_{2})_{[n-i-1]}.w)=(\mathbf{1}\otimes a_{2})_{[n-i-1]}.((a_{1}\otimes\mathbf{1})_{[i]}.w)=0

since either
\deg((a_{1}\otimes\mathbf{1})_{[i]})=\mathrm{wt}a_{1}-i-1<0
or
\deg((\mathbf{1}\otimes a_{2})_{[n-i-1]})=\mathrm{wt}a_{2}-n+i<0
. Hence
w\in\Omega(W)
by (
5.7
).
∎

Using the right neighborhoods in (
5.1
), one can define a right induced module functor

\Phi^{\mathsf{R}}:\mathsf{Mod}^{r}(\mathsf{A})\rightarrow\mathsf{ExMod}^{r}(\mathscr{U}),\quad U\mapsto\Phi^{\mathsf{R}}(U)=U\otimes_{\mathscr{U}_{0}}(\mathscr{U}/\mathrm{N}^{1}_{\mathsf{R}}\mathscr{U}).

(5.8)

Note that the neighborhood
\mathrm{N}^{1}_{\mathsf{L}}\mathscr{U}
(resp.
\mathrm{N}^{1}_{\mathsf{R}}\mathscr{U}
) contains all the negatively (resp. positively) graded subspaces of
\mathscr{U}
, see (
5.1
). Hence the quotient algebras in (
5.5
) and (
5.8
) have the gradations

\mathscr{U}/\mathrm{N}^{1}_{\mathsf{L}}\mathscr{U}=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}(\mathscr{U}/\mathrm{N}^{1}_{\mathsf{L}}\mathscr{U})_{n},\quad\mathscr{U}/\mathrm{N}^{1}_{\mathsf{R}}\mathscr{U}=\bigoplus_{m=0}^{\infty}(\mathscr{U}/\mathrm{N}^{1}_{\mathsf{R}}\mathscr{U})_{-m}.

Definition 5.2
.

[
DGK25
,
DGK24
]

The
mode transition algebra

\mathfrak{A}=\mathfrak{A}(V)
associated to a VOA
V
is given by the following vector space

\mathfrak{A}=\Phi^{\mathsf{R}}(\Phi^{\mathsf{L}}(\mathsf{A}))=(\mathscr{U}/\mathrm{N}^{1}_{\mathsf{L}}\mathscr{U})\otimes_{\mathscr{U}_{0}}\mathsf{A}\otimes_{\mathscr{U}_{0}}(\mathscr{U}/\mathrm{N}^{1}_{\mathsf{R}}\mathscr{U}).

(5.9)

Write
\mathfrak{A}_{n,-m}=(\mathscr{U}/\mathrm{N}^{1}_{\mathsf{L}}\mathscr{U})_{n}\otimes_{\mathscr{U}_{0}}\mathsf{A}\otimes_{\mathscr{U}_{0}}(\mathscr{U}/\mathrm{N}^{1}_{\mathsf{R}}\mathscr{U})_{-m}
, then
\mathfrak{A}=\bigoplus_{n,m\in\mathbb{N}}\mathfrak{A}_{n,-m}
. The product on
\mathfrak{A}
is denoted by
\star
, which satisfies

\mathfrak{A}_{i,-j}\star\mathfrak{A}_{k,-l}\subseteq\delta_{j,k}\mathfrak{A}_{i,-l},

see
[
DGK25
, Appendix B, Definition 3.2.1]
for more details of the product.
\mathfrak{A}_{d}=\mathfrak{A}_{d,-d}
is an associative algebra under
\star
, called the
d
-th mode transition algebra
of
V
.

An element
1_{d}\in\mathfrak{A}_{d}
is called a
strong unit
if it satisfies

1_{d}\star\mathfrak{a}=\mathfrak{a}\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\mathfrak{b}\star 1_{d}=\mathfrak{b},\quad\mathfrak{a}\in\mathfrak{A}_{d,0},\ \mathfrak{b}\in\mathfrak{A}_{0,-d},

where
\mathfrak{A}_{d}\cong\mathfrak{A}_{d,0}\otimes_{\mathsf{A}}\mathfrak{A}_{0,-d}
and

\mathfrak{A}_{d,0}=(\mathscr{U}/\mathrm{N}^{1}_{\mathsf{L}}\mathscr{U})_{d}\otimes_{\mathscr{U}_{0}}\mathsf{A}\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\mathfrak{A}_{0,-d}=\mathsf{A}\otimes_{\mathscr{U}_{0}}(\mathscr{U}/\mathrm{N}^{1}_{\mathsf{R}}\mathscr{U})_{-d}

(5.10)

are right and left
\mathsf{A}
-modules, respectively.

\mathfrak{A}_{d}
is said to be
strongly unital
if it admits a strong identity element. We say that the VOA
V
satisfies the
strongly unital condition for mode transition algebras
, or simply
V
is strongly unital, if its mode transition algebras
\mathfrak{A}_{d}
are strongly unital for all
d\in\mathbb{N}
.

Lemma 5.3
.

[
DGK24
, Theorem 4.0.10]
[
DGK25
, Theorem 6.1.1]

Any rational VOA satisfies the strongly unital condition for mode transition algebras. Moreover, any Heisenberg VOA
V=M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(k,0)
of rank

satisfies the strongly unital condition for mode transition algebras.

5.2.
Strongly unital property of
V_{H}

The following theorem that characterizes the strongly unital condition was proved in an ongoing work
[
GGKL25
]
.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
V
be a
C_{1}
-cofinite VOA such that
\mathfrak{A}_{d,0}
is a projective right
\mathsf{A}
-module for all
d\geq 0
. The following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

The VOA
V
satisfies the strongly unital condition for mode transition algebras.

(2)

The adjoint pair
\Phi^{\mathsf{L}}\dashv\Omega
in (
5.6
) is an adjoint equivalence.

In particular, any admissible
V
-module
W
is a generalized Verma module
\Phi^{\mathsf{L}}(\Omega(W))
.

As a consequence, it is clear that the rank-two parabolic-type VOA
V_{P}
does not satisfy the strongly unital property since it is not a simple VOA and so it is not a generalized Verma module.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
V_{P}=V_{H}\oplus V^{+}
be a rank-two parabolic-type VOA. Then its Cartan-part subVOA
V_{H}
satisfies the strongly unital condition for mode transition algebras.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.11
,
V_{H}
is
C_{1}
-cofinite. If
V_{P}
is of type-I, then
V_{H}\cong M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,0)
by Theorem
3.9
, and the conclusion follows from Lemma
5.3
.

Assume
V_{P}
is of type-II, with
V_{H}\cong M_{\widehat{\mathbb{C}\beta}}(1,0)\otimes V_{\mathbb{Z}\alpha}=V_{1}\otimes V_{2}
as VOAs, see Theorem
3.9
. Both
V_{1}=M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,0)
and
V_{2}=V_{\mathbb{Z}\alpha}
are strongly unital by Lemma
5.3
and Lemma
3.2
. Moreover, it follows from the definition of universal enveloping algebra and tensor product vertex operators (
3.13
) that

\displaystyle\mathscr{U}(V_{H})_{0}\cong\mathscr{U}(V_{1})_{0}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathscr{U}(V_{2})_{0},

\displaystyle\mathscr{U}(V_{H})_{d}\otimes_{\mathscr{U}(V_{H})_{0}}W\cong\bigoplus_{i,j\geq 0,i+j=d}(\mathscr{U}(V_{1})_{i}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathscr{U}(V_{2})_{j})\otimes_{\mathscr{U}(V_{H})_{0}}W,

for any left
A(V_{H})
-module
W
. Then by (
5.10
),

\mathfrak{A}_{d,0}(V_{H})\cong\bigoplus_{i,j\geq 0,\ i+j=d}\mathfrak{A}_{i,0}(V_{1})\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathfrak{A}_{j,0}(V_{2})

as a right
A(V_{H})\cong A(V_{1})\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}A(V_{2})
-module. Since
\mathfrak{A}_{i,0}(V_{1})
(resp.
\mathfrak{A}_{j,0}(V_{2})
) is a projective right
A(V_{1})
(resp.
A(V_{2})
) module, respectively, it is clear that
\mathfrak{A}_{d,0}(V_{H})
is a projective right
A(V_{H})
-module. Now let
U\in\mathsf{Mod}(A(V_{H}))
. By Lemma
5.1
,

\displaystyle\Omega\Phi^{\mathsf{L}}(U)

\displaystyle=\Omega(\Phi^{\mathsf{L}}_{V_{1}}(\Phi^{\mathsf{L}}_{V_{2}}(U)))=\Omega_{V_{1}}\Phi^{\mathsf{L}}_{V_{1}}(\Phi^{\mathsf{L}}_{V_{2}}(U))\cap\Omega_{V_{2}}\Phi^{\mathsf{L}}_{V_{2}}(\Phi^{\mathsf{L}}_{V_{1}}(U))

\displaystyle=\Phi^{\mathsf{L}}_{V_{2}}(U)\cap\Phi^{\mathsf{L}}_{V_{1}}(U)

since the unit maps for
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
are both bijective. Suppose there exists some element

w=\sum_{i}\beta(-n_{1})\dots\beta(-n_{k})u^{i}=\sum_{j}a^{1}_{m_{1}}\dots a^{r}_{m_{r}}v^{j}\in\left(\Phi^{\mathsf{L}}_{V_{2}}(U)\cap\Phi^{\mathsf{L}}_{V_{1}}(U)\right)\backslash U,

where
n_{1}\geq\dots\geq n_{k}\geq 1
,
a^{1},\dots,a^{r}\in V_{\mathbb{Z}\alpha}
, and
u^{i},v^{j}\in U
. Choose the index
i
such that
n_{1}
is largest among all the summands
\beta(-n_{1})\dots\beta(-n_{k})u^{i}
. Then there exists a nonzero scalar
c\in\mathbb{C}
such that

c\cdot u^{i}=\beta(n_{k})\dots\beta(n_{1}).w=\sum_{j}a^{1}_{m_{1}}\dots a^{r}_{m_{r}}\beta(n_{k})\dots\beta(n_{1}).v^{j}=0,

in view of (
3.2
). Then
u^{i}=0
, which is a contradiction. Thus,
\Omega\Phi^{\mathsf{L}}(U)=\Phi^{\mathsf{L}}_{V_{2}}(U)\cap\Phi^{\mathsf{L}}_{V_{1}}(U)=U
.
On the other hand, given
W\in\mathsf{Ext}(\mathscr{U}(V_{H}))
, since the action of
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
are commutative on
W
,
\Omega_{V_{2}}(W)
is a
V_{1}
-submodule in
W
, and
\Omega(W)=\Omega_{V_{1}}(\Omega_{V_{2}}(W))
. Since
V_{2}
is strongly unital,
\Phi^{\mathsf{L}}_{V_{2}}\Omega_{V_{2}}(W)=W
by Lemma
5.4
. Then
\Omega_{V_{1}}(\Phi^{\mathsf{L}}_{V_{2}}\Omega_{V_{2}}(W))=\Omega_{V_{1}}(W)
and

\Phi^{\mathsf{L}}(\Omega(W))=\Phi^{\mathsf{L}}_{V_{1}}\Omega_{V_{1}}(\Phi^{\mathsf{L}}_{V_{2}}\Omega_{V_{2}}(W))=\Phi_{V_{1}}^{\mathsf{L}}\Omega_{V_{1}}(W)=W,

in view of Lemma
5.1
. Hence
V_{H}=V_{1}\otimes V_{2}
satisfies the strongly unital condition for mode transition algebras by Lemma
5.4
.
∎

A formula for the Jack super nabla operator
Houcine Ben Dali

Abstract
. We study a Jack analog ∇ ( 𝒑 , 𝒒 ) \nabla(\bm{p},\bm{q}) of the super nabla operator recently introduced by Bergeron, Haglund, Iraci and Romero for Macdonald polynomials. We prove that ∇ ( 𝒑 , 𝒒 ) \nabla(\bm{p},\bm{q}) has a differential expression in the power-sum basis given in terms of Chapuy–Dołe˛ga and Nazarov–Sklyanin operators. This result is obtained from a more general formula for the operator 𝒢 ⁡ ( 𝒑 , 𝒒 ) \mathcal{G}(\bm{p},\bm{q}) encoding the structure coefficients of Jack characters, from which ∇ ( 𝒑 , 𝒒 ) \nabla(\bm{p},\bm{q}) is obtained by taking the top homogeneous part. A key step of the proof involves establishing that Chapuy–Dołe˛ga operators together with a dehomogenized version of Nazarov–Sklyanin operators have a Heisenberg algebra structure. The proof also uses a characterization of the operator 𝒢 ⁡ ( 𝒑 , 𝒒 ) \mathcal{G}(\bm{p},\bm{q}) with a family of differential equations, recently established by the author.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Jack polynomials and the super nabla operator

Bergeron, Haglund, Iraci and Romero introduced in
[
BHIR23
]
a new operator called the super nabla operator, defined by its diagonal action on modified Macdonald polynomials. This operator is a natural generalization of many well-studied operators in the theory, including the classical nabla operator and the Delta operator. The authors establish several combinatorial formulas for the specialization at
t=1
of this operator, and formulate positivity conjectures for general
q
and
t
.

In this paper we study a Jack analog of the super nabla operator.
Jack polynomials
{J}^{(\alpha)}_{\lambda}
are symmetric functions introduced by Jack
[
Jac70
]
. They are indexed by integer partitions and depend on a deformation parameter
\alpha
. Jack polynomials are obtained from modified Macdonald polynomials by applying a plethystic substitution and taking a suitable limit. They have a rich combinatorial structure
[
Sta89
,
KS97
,
CD22
,
Mol23
]
and are related to various models of statistical and quantum mechanics
[
LV95
,
DE02
,
For10
]
.

We denote
{J}^{(\alpha)}_{\lambda}(\bm{p})
and
{J}^{(\alpha)}_{\lambda}(\bm{q})
the Jack polynomial indexed by the partition
\lambda
, which depend respectively on two different alphabets of power-sum variables
\bm{p}:=(p_{1},p_{2},\dots)
and
\bm{q}:=(q_{1},q_{2},\dots)
. We define the
Jack super nabla operator
by

\nabla(\bm{p},\bm{q})\cdot{J}^{(\alpha)}_{\lambda}(\bm{p})={J}^{(\alpha)}_{\lambda}(\bm{p}){J}^{(\alpha)}_{\lambda}(\bm{q}).

Note that the operator
\nabla(\bm{p},\bm{q})
is homogeneous: when applied to a homogeneous function of degree
n
in

\bm{p}
, it produces a function that is homogeneous of degree
n

in both
\bm{p}
and
\bm{q}
.
We define the following
dehomogenization
of the super nabla operator

(1)

\mathcal{G}(\bm{p},\bm{q})=\exp\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{1}}\right)\exp\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial q_{1}}\right)\nabla(\bm{p},\bm{q})\exp\left(-\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{1}}\right).

The operator
\mathcal{G}(\bm{p},\bm{q})
can alternatively be defined by a diagonal action on the series of Jack characters (see
3.5
) or by its action on power-sum symmetric functions, encoded by the structure coefficients of Jack characters (see
Eq.

6
).

It follows from the definitions that
\nabla(\bm{p},\bm{q})
is obtained as the top homogeneous part of
\mathcal{G}(\bm{p},\bm{q})
: if
f
is homogeneous of degree
n
, then

\nabla(\bm{p},\bm{q})\cdot f(\bm{p})=\left[\mathcal{G}(\bm{p},\bm{q})\right]^{(0,n)}\cdot f(\bm{p}),

where
\left[\mathcal{O}(\bm{p},\bm{q})\right]^{(i,j)}
is the homogeneous part of the operator
\mathcal{O}(\bm{p},\bm{q})
of degree
i
in
\bm{p}
and
j
in
\bm{q}
.

1.3.
The Heisenberg algebra of the operators
\mathcal{C}_{\ell}
.

The first main contribution of this paper is to prove that the dehomogenized Nazarov–Sklyanin operators and Chapuy–Dołe˛ga operators satisfy a family of unexpected commutation relations giving them the structure of a Heisenberg algebra.

Theorem 1.1

(First main result)
.

We have the following relations between the operators
\left\{\mathcal{C}_{\ell}\right\}_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}
:

[\mathcal{C}_{m},\mathcal{C}_{\ell}]=\delta_{\ell,-m}\ \textup{sign}(\ell),\quad\text{for $\ell,m\neq 0$},

(3)

[\mathcal{C}_{0},\mathcal{C}_{\ell}]=\begin{cases}(\ell+1)\mathcal{C}_{\ell+1},&\quad\text{if $\ell\geq-1$},\\
\ell\mathcal{C}_{\ell+1}&\quad\text{if $\ell\leq-2$}.\end{cases}

As a consequence, we get a representation of the Heisenberg algebra given by the action of the operators
\mathcal{C}_{\ell}
, different from the classical representation, where it simply acts by multiplication and derivatives with respect to power-sums. We then have the following correspondence between the two representations:

\begin{array}[]{ccc}p_{\ell}&\longleftrightarrow&\mathcal{C}_{\ell}\\
\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{\ell}}&\longleftrightarrow&\mathcal{C}_{-\ell}\\
\sum_{i\geq 1}p_{i+1}\frac{(i+1)\partial}{\partial p_{i}}&\longleftrightarrow&\mathcal{C}_{0}.\end{array}

It is worth noticing that while in the first representation the Heisenberg algebra acts on the space of symmetric functions, in the second one it acts on the space of formal power-series; see
Section

2.2
for more details.

In the following, we briefly describe the different parts of the proof of
1.1
:

•

The case
\ell,m\geq 0
has been proved in
[
BDD23
, Theorem 6.6]
. The proof is technically involved and uses computations with extra catalytic variables.

•

The case
\ell,m\leq 0
, is a consequence of the fact that Nazarov–Sklyanin operators are diagonal on Jack polynomials; see
4.7
.

•

The case
\ell\geq 0
and
m\leq 0
uses the connection of the two families of operators with Jack characters, as well as plethystic-type computations; see
5.1
.

Given the combinatorial nature of the operators
\mathcal{C}_{\ell}
, it is natural to ask whether there exists a combinatorial proof of the commutation relations of
1.1
. We leave this question as an open problem.

1.4.
A differential expression for the super nabla operator

The second main result of this paper is a differential expression of the operator
\mathcal{G}
in terms of the operators
\{C_{\ell}\}_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}
.

Theorem 1.2

(Second main result)
.

We have

(4)

\mathcal{G}(\bm{p},\bm{q})=\exp\left(\sum_{\ell\geq 1}C_{\ell}(\bm{q})C_{-\ell}(\bm{p})\right).

In particular, for any homogeneous symmetric function
f
of degree
n
, we have

\nabla(\bm{p},\bm{q})\cdot f(\bm{p})=\left[\exp\left(\sum_{\ell\geq 1}C_{\ell}(\bm{q})C_{-\ell}(\bm{p})\right)\right]^{(0,n)}\cdot f(\bm{p}),

where
[.]^{(0,n)}
denotes the homogeneous part of the operator which has degree
0
in
\bm{p}
and degree
n
in
\bm{q}
.

The proof of this theorem is based on
1.1
and on a characterization of the operator
\mathcal{G}
with commutation relations established in
[
BD25
]
(see
5.4
).

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Partitions

A
partition

\lambda=[\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{k}]
is a weakly decreasing sequence of positive integers
\lambda_{1}\geq...\geq\lambda_{k}>0
. We denote by
\mathcal{P}
the set of all integer partitions, including the empty partition. The integer
k
is called the
length
of
\lambda
and is denoted
\ell(\lambda)
. The size of
\lambda
is the integer
|\lambda|:=\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+...+\lambda_{k}.
If
n
is the
size
of
\lambda
, we say that
\lambda
is a partition of
n
and we write
\lambda\vdash n
. The integers
\lambda_{1}
,…,
\lambda_{k}
are called the
parts
of
\lambda
.

For
i\geq 1
, we denote
m_{i}(\lambda)
the number of parts of size
i
in
\lambda
. We then set

z_{\lambda}:=\prod_{i\geq 1}m_{i}(\lambda)!i^{m_{i}(\lambda)}.

2.2.
Symmetric functions and symmetric power-series

We denote by
\mathcal{S}
the algebra of symmetric functions on
\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)
. For every partition
\lambda
of length
k
, we denote respectively by
m_{\lambda}
,
p_{\lambda}
,
h_{\lambda}
the monomial, power-sum and complete homogeneous symmetric functions indexed by
\lambda
.

Since power-sum functions are a basis of the symmetric functions algebra,
\mathcal{S}
can be identified with the polynomial algebra
\mathcal{S}\simeq\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)\llbracket p_{1},p_{2},\dots\rrbracket.

If
f
is a symmetric function in the alphabet
\bm{x}
, it will be convenient when there is no ambiguity to denote with the same letter the function and the associated polynomial in the alphabet of power-sum functions
\bm{p}:=(p_{1},p_{2},..)
, i.e
f(\bm{x})\equiv f(\bm{p})
. We consider the following scalar product on
\mathcal{S}

(7)

\langle p_{\lambda},p_{\mu}\rangle=z_{\lambda}\alpha^{\ell(\lambda)}\delta_{\lambda,\mu},

for any partitions
\lambda,\mu
, where
\delta_{\lambda,\mu}
denotes the Kronecker delta.
This scalar product is an
\alpha
-deformation of the
Hall scalar product
, obtained by setting
\alpha=1
.

If
\mathcal{O}
is an operator on
\mathcal{S}
, we denote by
\mathcal{O}^{\perp}
its adjoint with respect to the scalar product of
Eq.

7
. In particular, for
f\in\mathcal{S}
,
f^{\perp}
denotes the adjoint of the multiplication by
f
. We then have for any
n\geq 1

p_{n}^{\perp}=\alpha\frac{n\partial}{\partial p_{n}}.

Finally, we denote
\widehat{\mathcal{S}}
the space of formal power-series in the variables
p_{i}
:

\widehat{\mathcal{S}}:=\mathcal{S}_{\bm{p}}=\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)\llbracket p_{1},p_{2},\dots\rrbracket,

We will also make use of the spaces of polynomials and formal series in two alphabets of variables
\bm{p}
and
\bm{q}:=(q_{1},q_{2},\dots)
:

\mathcal{S}_{\bm{p},\bm{q}}:=\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)[p_{1},p_{2},\dots,q_{1},q_{2},\dots]\simeq\mathcal{S}\otimes\mathcal{S}

and

\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{\bm{p},\bm{q}}:=\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)\llbracket p_{1},p_{2},\dots,q_{1},q_{2},\dots\rrbracket\simeq\widehat{\mathcal{S}}\otimes\widehat{\mathcal{S}}.

2.3.
Jack–Cauchy kernel

Jack polynomials
{J}^{(\alpha)}_{\lambda}
are orthogonal with respect to scalar product of
Eq.

7
:

\langle{J}^{(\alpha)}_{\lambda},{J}^{(\alpha)}_{\theta}\rangle=\delta_{\lambda,\theta}\ j^{(\alpha)}_{\theta},

where
j^{(\alpha)}_{\theta}\in\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)
.
This orthogonality property, together with a triangularity property in the monomial basis, can be taken as a definition for Jack polynomials. We refer to
[
Sta89
]
for more details.
The Jack–Cauchy kernel is the series in
\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{\bm{p},\bm{q}}
defined by

(8)

\Omega(\bm{p},\bm{q})=\sum_{\theta\in\mathcal{P}}\frac{1}{j^{(\alpha)}_{\theta}}J^{(\alpha)}_{\theta}(\bm{p})J^{(\alpha)}_{\theta}(\bm{q})=\exp\left(\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{p_{n}q_{n}}{\alpha n}\right)=\sum_{\mu\in\mathcal{P}}\frac{p_{\mu}q_{\mu}}{z_{\mu}\alpha^{\ell(\mu)}}.

We then have the following lemma.

Lemma 2.1
.

For any operator
\mathcal{O}\in\End(\mathcal{S})
, we have

\mathcal{O}(\bm{p})\cdot\Omega(\bm{p},\bm{q})=\mathcal{O}^{\perp}(\bm{q})\cdot\Omega(\bm{p},\bm{q}).

Proof.

Define the coefficients
u_{\mu,\theta}
by

\mathcal{O}(\bm{p})\cdot{J}^{(\alpha)}_{\mu}(\bm{p})=\sum_{\theta\in\mathcal{P}}u_{\mu,\theta}{J}^{(\alpha)}_{\theta}(\bm{p}).

We then have

u_{\mu,\theta}=\frac{1}{j^{(\alpha)}_{\theta}}\langle\mathcal{O}{J}^{(\alpha)}_{\mu},{J}^{(\alpha)}_{\theta}\rangle=\frac{1}{j^{(\alpha)}_{\theta}}\langle{J}^{(\alpha)}_{\mu},\mathcal{O}^{\perp}{J}^{(\alpha)}_{\theta}\rangle

which gives

\mathcal{O}^{\perp}(\bm{q})\cdot\frac{{J}^{(\alpha)}_{\theta}(\bm{q})}{j^{(\alpha)}_{\theta}}=\sum_{\mu\in\mathcal{P}}u_{\mu,\theta}\frac{{J}^{(\alpha)}_{\mu}(\bm{q})}{j^{(\alpha)}_{\mu}}.

We then get that

\mathcal{O}(\bm{p})\cdot\Omega(\bm{p},\bm{q})=\sum_{\theta,\mu\in\mathcal{P}}u_{\mu,\theta}\frac{{J}^{(\alpha)}_{\theta}(\bm{p}){J}^{(\alpha)}_{\mu}(\bm{q})}{j^{(\alpha)}_{\mu}}=\mathcal{O}^{\perp}(\bm{q})\cdot\Omega(\bm{p},\bm{q}).\qed

2.4.
Plethystic-type notation

Let
u_{1},\dots,u_{N}
be a sequence of formal variables. We endow the ring
\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)[u_{1},\dots,u_{N}]
with formal commutative operations
\oplus

\ominus

\mathbb{A}
in this ring a

\mathbb{A}=\mathop{\oplus}_{1\leq i\leq k}x_{i}\mathop{\ominus}_{1\leq j\leq\ell}y_{j},

where

. The evaluation in
\mathbb{A}
is the algebra homomorphism
\mathcal{S}\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)[u_{i}]
defined on power-sums by

(9)

p_{k}(\mathbb{A})=\sum_{1\leq i\leq k}x_{i}^{k}-\sum_{1\leq j\leq k}y_{j}^{k},\text{for $k\geq 1$},

and
p_{\emptyset}[\mathbb{D}_{\bm{u}}]=1
.
This notation is similar to the one used in
[
AFNT15
]
for quasi-symmetric functions and is related to the Hopf algebra structure on symmetric functions; see
e.g

[
GR14
, Section 2]
. Note also that the plethystic notation used here is different from the one used in the Macdonald theory (as in
[
Hag08
, Chapter 1]
for example).

The main example of signed alphabets in this paper is the following.
Consider a sequence of
N\geq 1
variables
\bm{u}:=(u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{N})
.

We define
\mathbb{D}_{\bm{u}}
as the signed alphabet

(10)

\mathbb{D}_{\bm{u}}:=\oplus_{1\leq i\leq N}(\alpha u_{i}-i+1)\ominus_{1\leq i\leq N}(\alpha u_{i}-i)\oplus(-N).

Note that

p_{1}[\mathbb{D}_{\bm{u}}]=\sum_{1\leq i\leq N}(\alpha u_{i}-i+1)-\sum_{1\leq i\leq N}(\alpha u_{i}-i)-N=0.

Similarly, to a partition
\lambda\in\mathcal{P}
we associate the alphabet

\mathbb{D}_{\lambda}:=\mathbb{D}_{(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{N})}=\oplus_{1\leq i\leq N}(\alpha\lambda_{i}-i+1)\ominus_{1\leq i\leq N}(\alpha\lambda_{i}-i)\oplus(-N).

One may notice that this definition does not depend on the choice of
N\geq\ell(\lambda)
.
The positive terms in this alphabet correspond to the
\alpha
-content
of the
inner corners
of the Young diagram of
\lambda
, and the negative terms to the
outer corners
; see
[
DF16
, Section 2]
.

Example 2.1
.

For
\lambda=[2,2]
, we have

\mathbb{D}_{[2,2]}=(2\alpha)\ominus(2\alpha-1)\oplus(2\alpha-1)\ominus(2\alpha-2)\oplus(-2)=(2\alpha)\ominus(2\alpha-2)\oplus(-2).

Let us compute
h_{3}\left[\mathbb{D}_{(2,2)}\right]
. We start by expanding
h_{3}
in the power-sum basis:

h_{3}=p_{3}/3+p_{2}p_{1}/2+p_{1}^{3}/6.

Since
p_{1}[\mathbb{D}_{\lambda}]=0
, we get

h_{3}\left[\mathbb{D}_{[2,2])}\right]=\frac{1}{3}p_{3}\left[\mathbb{D}_{[2,2]}\right]=8\alpha^{2}-8\alpha.

If
\mathbb{A}
is a signed alphabet, we define the
plethystic exponential

(11)

\Exp\left[z\mathbb{A}\right]=\sum_{n\geq 0}z^{n}h_{n}[\mathbb{A}]=\exp\left(\sum_{k\geq 1}z^{k}\frac{p_{k}[\mathbb{A}]}{k}\right).

With this notation in hand, the Cauchy formula for the alphabet
\mathbb{D}_{\lambda}
then reads

\Exp\left[z\mathbb{D}_{\lambda}\right]=\frac{1}{1+z\ell(\lambda)}\prod_{1\leq i\leq\ell(\lambda)}\frac{1-z(\alpha\lambda_{i}-i)}{1-z(\alpha\lambda_{i}-i+1)}.

This series is the Cauchy transform of the
\alpha
deformed transition measure related to the partition
\lambda
, and its coefficients
h_{n}[\mathbb{D}_{\lambda}]
are the moments of this measure; see
[
DF16
, Section 2]
. The quantities
e_{n}[\mathbb{D}_{\lambda}]
and
p_{n}[\mathbb{D}_{\lambda}]
are usually referred to as boolean cumulants and fundamental functions of shape
\lambda
, respectively.

Note that the plethystic exponential satisfies the properties of a usual exponential

(12)

\Exp\left[\ominus\mathbb{A}\right]=\frac{1}{\Exp\left[\mathbb{A}\right]},\qquad\text{and}\qquad\Exp\left[\mathbb{A}\oplus\mathbb{B}\right]=\Exp\left[\mathbb{A}\right]\Exp\left[\mathbb{B}\right].

3.
Shifted symmetric functions and Jack characters

In this section, we recall some useful properties about shifted symmetric functions and Jack characters.

3.1.
Shifted symmetric functions

We start by the following definition due to Lassalle.

Definition 3.1

(
[
Las08
]
)
.

We say that a polynomial in
k
variables

(u_{1},\dots,u_{k})
with coefficients in
\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)
is

\alpha
-shifted symmetric if it is symmetric in the variables
u_{i}-i/\alpha
.
An
\alpha
-shifted symmetric function (or simply a shifted symmetric function) is a sequence

(f_{k})_{k\geq 1}
of shifted symmetric polynomials of bounded degrees, such that for every
k\geq 1
, the function
f_{k}
is
an
\alpha
-shifted symmetric polynomial in
k
variables and

(13)

f_{k+1}(u_{1},\dots,u_{k},0)=f_{k}(u_{1},\dots,u_{k}).

We denote by
\mathcal{S}^{*}
the algebra of shifted symmetric functions.

Let
f
be a shifted symmetric function and let
\lambda=[\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{k}]
be a partition. Then we denote

f(\lambda):=f(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{k},0,\dots).

Theorem 3.2

(
[
KS96
]
)
.

Let
n\geq 0
, and let
g
be a function on partitions. There exists a unique shifted symmetric function
f
of degree less or equal than
n
such that
f(\lambda)=g(\lambda)
for any
|\lambda|\leq n
.

In particular, a shifted symmetric function
f
is completely determined by its evaluation on partitions
(f(\lambda))_{\lambda\in\mathcal{P}}
.

3.2.
Jack characters

Jack characters are an
\alpha
-deformation of the irreducible characters of the symmetric group introduced by Lassalle in
[
Las08
]
.
Given a partition
\mu
, the Jack character
\theta^{(\alpha)}_{\mu}
is the function on partitions
\lambda
defined by:

(14)

\theta^{(\alpha)}_{\mu}(\lambda):=[p_{\mu}]\exp\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{1}}\right){J}^{(\alpha)}_{\lambda}(\bm{p}).

where
[p_{\mu}]f(\bm{p})
denotes the coefficient of
p_{\mu}
in
f
. In particular,
\theta^{(\alpha)}_{\mu}(\lambda)=0
if
|\lambda|<|\mu|
.

Jack characters are closely related to the study of random Young diagrams under a Jack deformation of the Plancherel measure
[
DF16
,
Śni19
,
CDM23
]
. In
[
BDD23
]
, a combinatorial interpretation for Jack characters has been given in terms of layered non-orientable maps, using a construction of the series of Jack characters with Chapuy–Dołe˛ga operators (see
4.1
).

It turns out that Jack characters can be realized as shifted symmetric functions.

Proposition 3.3

(
[
Las08
]
)
.

Fix a partition
\mu
. There exists a unique shifted symmetric function
f_{\mu}
such that
f_{\mu}(\lambda)=\theta^{(\alpha)}_{\mu}(\lambda)
.

In the following we will denote by
\theta^{(\alpha)}_{\mu}
the Jack character, both as a function on partitions and as a shifted symmetric function. Let
\mathcal{S}^{*}\llbracket\bm{p}\rrbracket
denote the space of formal power-series in
\bm{p}
whose coefficients are shifted symmetric in
\bm{u}:=(u_{1},u_{2},\dots)
.
We define the following series of Jack characters in
\mathcal{S}^{*}\llbracket\bm{p}\rrbracket
:

\mathcal{J}(\bm{p};\bm{u}):=\sum_{\mu\in\mathcal{P}}\theta^{(\alpha)}_{\mu}(\bm{u})p_{\mu}.

We also write for
\lambda\in\mathcal{P}

\mathcal{J}(\bm{p};\lambda):=\mathcal{J}(\bm{p};\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dots)\in\widehat{\mathcal{S}}.

Notice that from
Eq.

14
,

(15)

\mathcal{J}(\bm{p};\lambda)=\exp\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{1}}\right)\cdot{J}^{(\alpha)}_{\lambda}(\bm{p})

In this paper, information on operators will be derived by studying their action on
\mathcal{J}(\bm{p};\bm{u})
thanks to the following lemma.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\mathcal{O}_{1}
and
\mathcal{O}_{2}
be two linear operators on
\widehat{\mathcal{S}}
, satisfying

\mathcal{O}_{1}(\bm{p})\cdot\mathcal{J}(\bm{p};\bm{u})=\mathcal{O}_{2}(\bm{p})\cdot\mathcal{J}(\bm{p};\bm{u}).

Then
\mathcal{O}_{1}=\mathcal{O}_{2}
.

Proof.

It is enough to prove that the two operators agree on the basis of
\widehat{\mathcal{S}}
given by Jack polynomials
{J}^{(\alpha)}_{\lambda}
. We proceed by induction on the size of
\lambda
. We assume that this is the case for all
\rho
with size less than
n
. Fix
\lambda
of size
n
. Taking
\bm{u}=\lambda
, we get

\mathcal{O}_{1}\cdot\mathcal{J}(\lambda)=\mathcal{O}_{2}\cdot\mathcal{J}(\lambda).

But we know that

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}(\bm{p};\lambda)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{1}}\right)\cdot{J}^{(\alpha)}_{\lambda}(\bm{p})

\displaystyle={J}^{(\alpha)}_{\lambda}(\bm{p})+\sum_{|\rho|<|\lambda|}a_{\lambda,\rho}{J}^{(\alpha)}_{\rho}(\bm{p}),

for some coefficients
a_{\lambda,\rho}
. From the induction assumption we know that
\mathcal{O}_{1}\cdot{J}^{(\alpha)}_{\rho}=\mathcal{O}_{2}\cdot{J}^{(\alpha)}_{\rho}
for any
|\rho|<|\lambda|
, and we get that
\mathcal{O}_{1}\cdot{J}^{(\alpha)}_{\lambda}=\mathcal{O}_{2}\cdot{J}^{(\alpha)}_{\lambda}
as required.
∎

The following proposition gives the action of the dehomogenized nabla operator on the Jack character series.

Proposition 3.5
.

We have

\mathcal{G}(\bm{p},\bm{q})\cdot\mathcal{J}(\bm{p};\bm{u})=\mathcal{J}(\bm{q};\bm{u})\mathcal{J}(\bm{p};\bm{u}).

Proof.

We know from
3.2
that shifted symmetric functions are determined by their evaluations on partition. It is then enough to prove that

\mathcal{G}(\bm{p},\bm{q})\cdot\mathcal{J}(\bm{p};\lambda)=\mathcal{J}(\bm{q};\lambda)\mathcal{J}(\bm{p};\lambda).

Using the definitions of
\mathcal{G}
and
Eq.

15
, we write

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(\bm{p},\bm{q})\cdot\mathcal{J}(\bm{p};\lambda)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{1}}\right)\exp\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial q_{1}}\right)\nabla(\bm{p},\bm{q})\exp\left(-\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{1}}\right)\exp\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{1}}\right){J}^{(\alpha)}_{\lambda}(\bm{p})

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{1}}\right)\exp\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial q_{1}}\right){J}^{(\alpha)}_{\lambda}(\bm{p}){J}^{(\alpha)}_{\lambda}(\bm{q})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{J}(\bm{q};\lambda)\mathcal{J}(\bm{p};\lambda)

as desired.
∎

3.3.
The shifting operator

Fix a symmetric function
f
.
For any
N\geq 1
, define the polynomial
f^{*}_{N}
by

(16)

f^{*}_{N}(u_{1},\dots,u_{N}):=f[\mathbb{D}_{\bm{u}_{N}}],

where
\bm{u}_{N}=(u_{1},\dots,u_{N})
and
\mathbb{D}_{\bm{u}_{N}}
is the signed alphabet defined in
Section

2.4
. It follows from the definitions that
f^{*}_{N}
is shifted symmetric in the variables
u_{i}
. Moreover, they satisfy the stability property

f^{*}_{N+1}(u_{1},\dots,u_{N},0)=f^{*}_{N+1}(u_{1},\dots,u_{N}).

This gives rise to a shifted symmetric function
f^{*}(u_{1},u_{2},\dots)
.

The functions
\{p_{k}^{*}\}_{k\geq 2}
form an algebraic basis over
\mathbb{Q}
of
\mathcal{S}^{*}
; see
[
IO02
, Corollary 2.8]
and
[
DF16
, Section 2]
. We then have the following.

Proposition 3.6

(
[
IO02
,
DF16
]
)
.

We have
\mathcal{S}^{*}\simeq\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)[p_{2}^{*},p_{3}^{*},\dots]
.

In particular,
\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{k}^{*}}
is well defined as an operator on
\mathcal{S}^{*}
for
k\geq 2
. We now define the operator on
\mathcal{S}^{*}

\mathcal{T}_{w}=\mathcal{T}_{w}(\bm{u}):=\exp\left(\sum_{n\geq 1}w^{n}\frac{(n+1)\partial}{\partial p^{*}_{n+1}}-\sum_{i\geq 1}p^{*}_{i}\frac{(i+1)\partial}{\partial p^{*}_{i+1}}\right).

We prove that this operator is a
shifting operator
: it acts on a function
f
by inserting a variable
w
in the first position and shifting all the remaining variables one step to the right.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
f
be a shifted symmetric function. Then

\mathcal{T}_{\alpha w}\cdot f(u_{1},u_{2}\dots)=f(w,u_{1},u_{2}\dots).

The operator
\mathcal{T}_{w}
can be thought of as a shifted symmetric version of the translation operator from the Macdonald theory (see
[
BGHT99
, Theorem 1.1]
).

Before proving
3.7
, we introduce some notation. Set

a(w):=\sum_{n\geq 1}w^{n}\frac{(n+1)\partial}{\partial p^{*}_{n+1}}\quad\text{ and }\quad c:=-\sum_{i\geq 1}p^{*}_{i}\frac{(i+1)\partial}{\partial p^{*}_{i+1}}.

We then have

(17)

[a(w),p^{*}_{\ell}]=\begin{cases}\ell w^{\ell-1}&\text{if }\ell\geq 2\\
0&\text{if }\ell=1.\end{cases}\quad\text{and}\quad[c,p^{*}_{\ell}]=\begin{cases}-\ell p^{*}_{\ell-1}&\text{if }\ell\geq 2\\
0&\text{if }\ell=1.\end{cases}

For any operator
\mathcal{O}
, we define
\ad_{\mathcal{O}}
as the adjoint of
\mathcal{O}
:
\ad_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{O}^{\prime}):=[\mathcal{O},\mathcal{O}^{\prime}].
We then have for
k\geq 1

\ad_{\mathcal{O}}^{k}(\mathcal{O}^{\prime})=\underbrace{\left[\mathcal{O},\left[\mathcal{O},\left[\dots[\mathcal{O},\mathcal{O}^{\prime}]\dots\right]\right]\right]}_{\text{$k$ commutators}}

We have the following identity (see e.g
[
Wil67
]
)

(18)

\exp(\mathcal{O})\mathcal{O}^{\prime}\exp(-\mathcal{O})=\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{\ad^{k}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{O}^{\prime})}{k!}.

We start by the following lemma.

Lemma 3.8
.

For any
\ell\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle p_{\ell}^{*}

\displaystyle(w,u_{1},\dots):=(\alpha w)^{\ell}-(\alpha w-1)^{\ell}+\sum_{0\leq k\leq\ell}(-1)^{\ell-k}\binom{\ell}{k}p^{*}_{k}(u_{1},\dots).

Proof.

Fix
N\geq 1
. From the definitions (
Eqs.

9
and
16
), we have

\displaystyle p_{\ell}^{*}

\displaystyle(w,u_{1},\dots,u_{N})-\left((\alpha w)^{\ell}-(\alpha w-1)^{\ell}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq i\leq N}\left((\alpha u_{i}-i)^{\ell}-(\alpha u_{i}-i-1)^{\ell}\right)+(-N-1)^{\ell}

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leq k\leq\ell}(-1)^{\ell-k}\binom{\ell}{k}\left(\sum_{1\leq i\leq N}\left((\alpha u_{i}-i+1)^{k}-(\alpha u_{i}-i)^{k}\right)+(-N)^{k}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leq k\leq\ell}(-1)^{\ell-k}\binom{\ell}{k}p^{*}_{k}(u_{1},\dots,u_{N}).

Finally, we take the limit
N\rightarrow\infty.

∎

Proof of
3.7
.

We start by proving that, as operators on
\mathcal{S}^{*}
, we have

(19)

\mathcal{T}_{\alpha w}p^{*}_{\ell}(\bm{u})\mathcal{T}_{\alpha w}^{-1}=p_{\ell}^{*}(w,\bm{u}).

We know from
Eq.

18
that

(20)

\mathcal{T}_{w}p^{*}_{\ell}\mathcal{T}_{w}^{-1}=\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{\ad_{a(w)+c}^{k}(p^{*}_{\ell})}{k!}=\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{\left(\ad_{a(w)}+\ad_{c}\right)^{k}(p^{*}_{\ell})}{k!},

Using
Eq.

17
, we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{w}p^{*}_{\ell}\mathcal{T}_{w}^{-1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{\ad_{c}^{k}(p^{*}_{\ell})}{k!}+\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{\ad_{a(w)}\left(\ad_{c}^{k}(p^{*}_{\ell})\right)}{(k+1)!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leq k\leq\ell-1}(-1)^{k}\binom{\ell}{k}p^{*}_{\ell-k}+\sum_{0\leq k\leq\ell-2}(-1)^{k}\binom{\ell}{k+1}w^{\ell-k-1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq k\leq\ell}(-1)^{\ell-k}\binom{\ell}{k}p^{*}_{k}-\left((w-1)^{\ell}-w^{\ell}-(-1)^{\ell}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leq k\leq\ell}(-1)^{\ell-k}\binom{\ell}{k}p^{*}_{k}+\left(w^{\ell}-(w-1)^{\ell}\right).

To obtain the last line, we use the fact that
p_{\emptyset}^{*}=1
.
We now use
3.8
to deduce that

\mathcal{T}_{\alpha w}p^{*}_{\ell}(\bm{u})\mathcal{T}_{\alpha w}^{-1}=p^{*}_{\ell}(w,\bm{u}).

For any partition
\mu
, we can write

\mathcal{T}_{\alpha w}\cdot p^{*}_{\mu}(\bm{u})=p^{*}_{\mu_{1}}(w,\bm{u})\mathcal{T}_{\alpha w}p_{\mu_{2}}(\bm{u})\dots p^{*}_{\mu_{\ell(\mu)}}(\bm{u}),

and by induction we get

\mathcal{T}_{\alpha w}\cdot p^{*}_{\mu}(\bm{u})=p^{*}_{\mu}(w,\bm{u})\mathcal{T}_{\alpha w}\cdot 1=p_{\mu}(w,\bm{u}).

Since
p_{\mu}^{*}
are a basis of
\mathcal{S}^{*}
, this finishes the proof of the theorem.
∎

4.
Differential operators

4.1.
Chapuy–Dołe˛ga operators

Following the notation of
[
CD22
]
, we define the space

\mathcal{S}_{Y}:=\Span_{\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)}\left\{y_{i}p_{\lambda}\right\}_{i\geq 0,\lambda\in\mathcal{P}}=\mathop{\oplus}_{i\geq 0}y_{i}\mathcal{S}.

We also consider the operators

Y_{+}:=\sum_{i\geq 1}y_{i+1}\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{i}}:\mathcal{S}_{Y}\rightarrow\mathcal{S}_{Y},

\Lambda_{Y}:=\alpha\cdot\sum_{i,j\geq 1}y_{i+j-1}\frac{j\partial^{2}}{\partial y_{i-1}\partial p_{j}}+\sum_{i,j\geq 1}y_{i-1}p_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{i+j-1}}+(\alpha-1)\cdot\sum_{i\geq 1}y_{i}\frac{i\partial}{\partial y_{i}}:\mathcal{S}_{Y}\rightarrow\mathcal{S}_{Y},

\text{and }\quad\Theta_{Y}:=\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{0}}\Lambda_{Y}=\sum_{i\geq 1}p_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{i}}:\mathcal{S}_{Y}\rightarrow\mathcal{S}.

For
\ell\geq 0
, the operator
\mathcal{C}_{\ell}(\bm{p})
is defined by

\mathcal{C}_{\ell}(\bm{p}):=[u^{\ell}]\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n}\Theta_{Y}\left(Y_{+}\Lambda_{Y}+uY_{+}\right)^{n}\frac{y_{0}}{\alpha}:\ \widehat{\mathcal{S}}\rightarrow\widehat{\mathcal{S}}.

Remark 3
.

In
[
BDD23
,
BD25
]
, the operators
\mathcal{C}_{\ell}
depend on an extra parameter
t
. This parameter can be recovered by taking the homogeneous parts of the operators. To simplify notation, we work here with the specialization
t=-1
.

Rather than using the explicit definition given above, we will mainly make use of the fact that the operators
\{\mathcal{C}_{\ell}\}_{\ell\geq 0}
“construct” the Jack character series; see
[
BDD23
, Eqs. (28) and (65)]
.

Theorem 4.1

(
[
BDD23
]
)
.

We have,

(21)

\mathcal{J}(\bm{p};(w,\bm{u}))=\exp\left(\sum_{\ell\geq 0}(-\alpha w)^{\ell}\mathcal{C}_{\ell}(\bm{p})\right)\mathcal{J}(\bm{p};\bm{u}),

where
(w,\bm{u}):=(w,u_{1},u_{2},\dots).

Combining
3.7
and
4.1
, we get the following.

Corollary 4.2
.

For any
\ell\geq 0
, we have

(-1)^{\ell}\mathcal{C}_{\ell}(\bm{p})\mathcal{J}(\bm{p};\bm{u})=\begin{cases}\frac{(\ell+1)\partial}{\partial p^{*}_{\ell+1}(\bm{u})}\cdot\mathcal{J}(\bm{p};\bm{u})&\text{if }\ell\geq 1,\\
-\sum_{i\geq 1}p_{i}^{*}(\bm{u})\frac{(i+1)\partial}{\partial p^{*}_{i+1}(\bm{u})}\cdot\mathcal{J}(\bm{p};\bm{u})&\text{if $\ell=0$}.\end{cases}

Notice that the operator on the left acts only on the alphabet
\bm{p}
(the symmetric part) and on the right on the alphabet
\bm{u}
(the shifted symmetric part).

Proof.

Set
\mathcal{A}(\bm{p};w):=\exp\left(\sum_{\ell\geq 0}(-\alpha w)^{\ell}\mathcal{C}_{\ell}(\bm{p})\right)
.
From
4.1
and
3.7
, we know that

\mathcal{A}(\bm{p};w)\cdot\mathcal{J}(\bm{p};\bm{u})=\mathcal{T}_{\alpha w}(\bm{u})\cdot\mathcal{J}(\bm{p};\bm{u}).

Since
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{T}_{w}
commute, we deduce that we also have

\log(\mathcal{A}(\bm{p};w))\cdot\mathcal{J}(\bm{p};\bm{u})=\log(\mathcal{T}_{\alpha w}(\bm{u}))\cdot\mathcal{J}(\bm{p};\bm{u}).
Finally, we extract the coefficient of
(\alpha w)^{\ell}
.
∎

4.2.
Nazarov–Sklyanin operators and the operators
\mathcal{C}_{\ell}
for
\ell<0

Nazarov–Sklyanin operators
\mathcal{N}_{\ell}
are the operators on
\mathcal{S}
defined for
\ell\geq 0
by:

\mathcal{N}_{\ell}(\bm{p}):=\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{0}}\Lambda_{Y}^{\ell}y_{0}.

As an example, one can check that
\mathcal{N}_{0}(\bm{p}):=1
,
\mathcal{N}_{1}(\bm{p})=0
and
\mathcal{N}_{2}(\bm{p})=\sum_{i\geq 1}p_{i}\alpha\frac{i\partial}{\partial p_{i}}
is the Euler operator.

The following theorem is due to Nazarov and Sklyanin
[
NS13a
]
. See also
[
Mol23
,
BDD23
]
for reformulation in terms of path operators.

Theorem 4.3

(
[
NS13a
]
)
.

For any
\ell\geq 0
and any
\lambda\in\mathcal{P}
, we have

\mathcal{N}_{\ell}(\bm{p})\cdot{J}^{(\alpha)}_{\lambda}(\bm{p})=h_{\ell}[\mathbb{D}_{\lambda}]{J}^{(\alpha)}_{\lambda}(\bm{p}).

We introduce the following
dehomogenized version
of Nazarov–Sklyanin operators:

(22)

{\mathcal{\widetilde{N}}}_{\ell}(\bm{p}):=\exp\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{1}}\right)\mathcal{N}_{\ell}(\bm{p})\exp\left(-\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{1}}\right).

Define the operator
Y_{-}
on
\mathcal{S}_{Y}
by

Y_{-}:=\sum_{i\geq 1}y_{i-1}\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{i}}.

The operator
{\mathcal{\widetilde{N}}}_{\ell}
admits the following alternative expression.

Proposition 4.4
.

For any
\ell\geq 1
, we have

{\mathcal{\widetilde{N}}}_{\ell}(\bm{p}):=\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{0}}\left(Y_{-}+\Lambda_{Y}\right)^{\ell}y_{0}.

Proof.

First, notice the following commutation relation

\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{1}},\Lambda_{Y}\right]=Y_{-}.

Hence, we get that for any
n\geq 0

\frac{1}{n!}\ad_{\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{1}}}^{n}({\mathcal{\widetilde{N}}}_{\ell}(\bm{p}))=[v^{n}]\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{0}}\left(vY_{-}+\Lambda_{Y}\right)y_{0}.

We then have from
Eq.

18
that

\exp\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{1}}\right)\mathcal{N}_{\ell}(\bm{p})\exp\left(-\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{1}}\right)=e^{\ad_{\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{1}}}}\left(\mathcal{N}_{\ell}(\bm{p})\right)

which finishes the proof.
∎

One may notice that the operators
{\mathcal{\widetilde{N}}}_{\ell}
are well defined both on
\mathcal{S}
and on
\widehat{\mathcal{S}}
. We now define the series

{\mathcal{\widetilde{N}}}(\bm{p};v)=\sum_{\ell\geq 0}{\mathcal{\widetilde{N}}}_{\ell}(\bm{p})v^{\ell}.

Proposition 4.5
.

For any
\ell\geq 0
, we have

(23)

{\mathcal{\widetilde{N}}}_{\ell}(\bm{p})\cdot\mathcal{J}(\bm{p};\bm{u})=h_{\ell}^{*}(\bm{u})\mathcal{J}(\bm{p};\bm{u}).

Equivalently,

(24)

{\mathcal{\widetilde{N}}}(\bm{p};v)\cdot\mathcal{J}(\bm{p};\bm{u})=\Exp[v\mathbb{D}_{\bm{u}}]\cdot\mathcal{J}(\bm{p};\bm{u}).

Proof.

Notice that both sides of
Eq.

23
are in
\mathcal{S}^{*}\llbracket\bm{p}\rrbracket.

By
3.2
, it is enough to check the result when
\bm{u}
is a partition, i.e when

u_{i}=\lambda_{i}
for some
\lambda\in\mathcal{P}
. Recall that in this case,
\mathbb{D}_{\bm{u}}=\mathbb{D}_{\lambda}
.
Using
Eq.

15
, we have

\displaystyle{\mathcal{\widetilde{N}}}_{\ell}(\bm{p})\cdot\mathcal{J}(\bm{p};\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dots)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{1}}\right)\mathcal{N}_{\ell}(\bm{p})\exp\left(-\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{1}}\right)\exp\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{1}}\right){J}^{(\alpha)}_{\lambda}(\bm{p})

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{1}}\right)\mathcal{N}_{\ell}(\bm{p})\cdot{J}^{(\alpha)}_{\lambda}(\bm{p}).

Using
4.3
, we get

\displaystyle{\mathcal{\widetilde{N}}}_{\ell}(\bm{p})\cdot\mathcal{J}(\bm{p};\lambda)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{1}}\right)h_{\ell}[\mathbb{D}_{\lambda}]\cdot{J}^{(\alpha)}_{\lambda}(\bm{p})

\displaystyle=h_{\ell}[\mathbb{D}_{\lambda}]\mathcal{J}(\bm{p};\lambda).

This finishes the proof of
Eq.

23
. We multiply this equation by
v^{\ell}
and we sum over
\ell\geq 0
to obtain
Eq.

24
.
∎

Recall that for
\ell>0

We consider the following power-series in three alphabets
\bm{p}
,
\bm{q}
and

Proposition 6.1
.

The power-sum expansion of
\tau^{(\alpha)}(\bm{p},\bm{q},\bm{r})
is given by

(30)

\tau^{(\alpha)}(\bm{p},\bm{q},\bm{r})=\sum_{n\geq 0}\sum_{\pi,\mu,\nu\vdash n}\frac{c^{\pi}_{\mu,\nu}(\alpha)}{z_{\pi}\alpha^{\ell(\pi)}}p_{\pi}q_{\mu}r_{\nu}.

where

are the coefficients defined in
Eq.

5
.

In Goulden–Jackson’s original paper
[
GJ96
]
,

30

c^{\pi}_{\mu,\nu}
.

Proof.

The function
\tau^{(\alpha)}
is obtained from the Cauchy kernel by applying
\nabla
:

(31)

\tau^{(\alpha)}(\bm{p},\bm{q},\bm{r})=\nabla(\bm{q},\bm{r})\cdot\Omega(\bm{p},\bm{q}).

Expanding the Cauchy kernel and using

5
, we get

\displaystyle\tau^{(\alpha)}(\bm{p},\bm{q},\bm{r})

\displaystyle=\nabla(\bm{q},\bm{r})\sum_{n\geq 0}\sum_{\pi,\mu\vdash n}\frac{p_{\pi}q_{\mu}}{z_{\pi}\alpha^{\ell(\pi)}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 0}\sum_{\pi,\mu,\nu\vdash n}\frac{c^{\pi}_{\mu,\nu}(\alpha)}{z_{\pi}\alpha^{\ell(\pi)}}p_{\pi}q_{\mu}r_{\nu}\qed

Now define the series

(32)

G^{(\alpha)}(\bm{p},\bm{q},\bm{r}):=\exp\left(-\frac{p_{1}}{\alpha}\right)\exp\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial q_{1}}+\frac{\partial}{\partial r_{1}}\right)\tau^{(\alpha)}(\bm{p},\bm{q},\bm{r}).

It has been proved in

Proposition 6.2

We have

From

Appendix A
On the Macdonald case

In this appendix, all the operators and plethystic notation used are from the Macdonald theory and are different from the Jack notation used so far.

We follow the notation of
[
BHIR23
]
: we will make use of the Macdonald plethystic notation,
\widetilde{H}_{\lambda}[X]
will denote modified Macdonald polynomials,
\nabla
the nabla operator, and
\nabla_{\bm{y}}
the super nabla operator defined by

{\nabla}_{\bm{y}}\cdot\widetilde{H}_{\lambda}[X]=\widetilde{H}_{\lambda}[X]\widetilde{H}_{\lambda}[Y].

We denote
\mathcal{O}(\bm{x})
(respectively
\mathcal{O}(\bm{y})
) the operator
\mathcal{O}
acting on symmetric functions in the alphabet
\bm{x}
(respectively
\bm{y})
. We consider the translation operator
\mathcal{T}_{Y}
acting on symmetric functions in an alphabet
X
by

(34)

\mathcal{T}_{Y}(\bm{x})\cdot f[X]=\exp\left(\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{p_{n}[Y]p^{\perp}_{n}[X]}{n}\right)\cdot f[X]=f[X+Y],

and the operator
\mathcal{P}_{Y}
defined by

\mathcal{P}_{Y}(\bm{x})\cdot f[X]=\Exp[XY]f[X],

with

\Exp[XY]:=\exp\left(\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{p_{n}[XY]}{n}\right)

We now define a dehomogenized operator
\widetilde{\nabla}_{\bm{y}}

\widetilde{\nabla}_{\bm{y}}:=\mathcal{T}_{1}(\bm{x})\mathcal{T}_{1}(\bm{y}){\nabla}_{\bm{y}}\mathcal{T}_{-1}(\bm{x}).

Note that, compared to the Jack case
Eq.

33
, the operator
\exp\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{1}}\right)
is replaced by
\mathcal{T}_{1}
.

Proposition A.1
.

We have

(35)

\widetilde{\nabla}_{\bm{y}}=\mathcal{P}_{\frac{1}{M}}(\bm{x})\ \nabla(\bm{x})^{-1}\mathcal{P}_{\frac{1}{M}}(\bm{y})\ \nabla(\bm{y})^{-1}\ \mathcal{T}_{Y}(\bm{x})\ \nabla(\bm{x})\ \mathcal{P}_{\frac{-1}{M}}(\bm{x}),

where
M:=(1-q)(1-t)
.

Proof.

We recall Tesler’s identity (see
[
GHT01
]
)

(36)

\nabla\mathcal{P}_{-\frac{1}{M}}\mathcal{T}_{1}\widetilde{H}_{\lambda}[uX]=\Exp\left[-\frac{XD_{\lambda}}{M}\right].

with
D_{\lambda}:=(1-t)\sum_{1\leq i\leq\ell(\lambda)}(1-q^{\lambda_{i}})-1.

It is easy to check that the two sides of
Eq.

35
coincide on

\mathcal{T}_{1}(\bm{x})\cdot\widetilde{H}_{\lambda}[X]
for any
\lambda
. Moreover, using a triangularity argument one gets that
\mathcal{T}_{1}(\bm{x})\cdot\widetilde{H}_{\lambda}[X]
form a basis for the space of symmetric functions, which finishes the proof of the proposition.
∎

As in the Jack case, this formula is somehow related to a Macdonald version of Jack characters introduced in
[
BDD24
]
, and to the operators
\Gamma
allowing to construct them. However, the combinatorial structure of these operators is still not well understood.

Recall that Jack polynomials can be obtained from modified Macdonald polynomials via a plethystic transformation followed by a suitable limit. However, the decomposition given in
A.1
does not behave well under this limit: although the limit of the full composition of operators on the right-hand side is well defined, the limits of the individual operators are not. This issue also arises in several other Macdonald identities for which no Jack analogue is known—for instance, Tesler’s identity, from which
A.1
is derived.

Linear stability of lattice Boltzmann models with non-ideal equation of state
S.A. Hosseini, I.V. Karlin

Abstract
Detailed study of spectral properties and of linear stability is presented for a class of lattice Boltzmann models with a non-ideal equation of state. Examples include the van der Waals and the shallow water models. Both analytical and numerical approaches demonstrate that linear stability requires boundedness of propagation speeds of normal eigen-modes. The study provides a basis for the construction of unconditionally stable lattice Boltzmann models.

I
Introduction

A variety of flows rely on nonideal equations of state featuring a nonlinear dependence of the thermodynamic pressure on fluid density. These include, but are not limited to, the cubic class of equations of state used to model non-ideal fluids with multiple phases
[
1
]
and quadratic equations of state featured by shallow water equations
[
2
]
. The nonlinearity in the equations of state is directly translated into the propagation speed of the normal eigen-modes commonly referred to as the speed of sound. This in turn comes with challenges to be considered when developing numerical models for the corresponding continuum-level balance equations.

Here, we are interested in a numerical method introduced in the late 80’s to solve the Navier–Stokes (NS) equations in the incompressible limit: The lattice Boltzmann method (LBM). The LBM has been successful in modeling flows in the incompressible limit, most notably due to its efficiency, brought about by the low intensity of numerical operators and locality of closure for the pressure, as compared to alternatives relying on pressure closures such as the Poisson equation. Recent years have also witnessed successful extension of the LBM to modeling of fluids with non-ideal equations of states
[
3
,
1
]
. The so-called free energy and pseudo-potential LBM are good examples of this growing success
[
4
,
5
,
6
]
. However, as with the original LBM for ideal fluids, stability is an important topic of discussion
[
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
]
. Contrary to the LBM with ideal equation of state where there is an extensive literature on stability analysis and spectral properties, non-ideal LBM literature is devoid of such studies.

In this work, we present for the first time a comprehensive linear analysis of spectral properties and stability of the LBM with a non-ideal equation of state. Being interest in the design of stable attractors guaranteeing unconditional linear stability, as opposed to control over relaxation path, we only consider the lattice Bhatnagar–Gross–Krook (LBGK) model
[
12
]
.

The outline of the paper is as follows: A brief introduction to the LBGK formalism and a discussion of non-linear equations of state are provided in Sec.
II
.
In Sec.
III
, we report a comprehensive analysis of linear stability of the one-dimensional LBGK system. This analysis reveals the necessary and sufficient stability conditions as a bound on the normal eigen-modes. These results extend our previous findings
[
13
]
pointing at the need for asymptotic freedom for unconditional linear stability.
The analysis is illustrated by two typical equations of state, the van der Waals and the shallow water systems.
The one-dimensional LBM analysis is extended to higher dimensions in Sec.
IV
. Both analytical and numerical studies of spectral dispersion and dissipation of hydrodynamic modes, and evaluation of the linear stability domain are performed for the two-dimensional LBGK. In particular, we demonstrate that the linear stability condition of normal eigen-modes remains necessary also in higher dimensions.
The paper closes in Sec.
V
with a discussion on the conditions for the construction of unconditionally linear-stable equilibria by introduction of velocity-dependent pressure in the spirit of the entropic equilibrium for the isothermal ideal equation of state
[
13
,
9
,
10
]
.

II
Generic LBGK

We consider the LBGK model
[
14
]
for nearly-incompressible flows,

f_{i}(\bm{r}+\bm{c}_{i}\delta t,t+\delta t)-f_{i}(\bm{r},t)=2\beta\left(f_{i}^{\rm eq}(\rho,\bm{u})-f_{i}\right).

(1)

Here
f_{i}
are the populations of the discrete velocities
\bm{c}_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,Q
,
\bm{r}
is the position in space,
t
is the time,
\delta t
is the time-step,
\rho
is the fluid density and
\bm{u}
is the flow velocity,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{Q}\{1,\bm{c}_{i}\}f_{i}=\{\rho,\rho\bm{u}\}.

(2)

We consider the standard first-neighbor lattices in space dimension
D
defined as a
D
-fold tensor product of one-dimensional velocities
c_{i\alpha}\in\{-1,0,1\}
.
For these D
D
Q
3^{D}
lattices, a generic class of equilibria
f_{i}^{\rm eq}
is defined as follows: First, we introduce a triplet of functions
\Psi_{i\alpha}(u,\mathcal{P})
,
i\alpha\in\{-1,0,1\}
:

\displaystyle\Psi_{0}=1-\mathcal{P},

(3)

\displaystyle\Psi_{-1}=\frac{1}{2}(-u+\mathcal{P}),

(4)

\displaystyle\Psi_{1}=\frac{1}{2}(u+\mathcal{P}).

(5)

For a
D
-dimensional lattice, equilibria are defined by a product form,

f_{i}^{\rm eq}(\rho,\bm{u})=\rho\prod_{\alpha=1}^{D}\Psi_{i\alpha}(u_{\alpha},\mathcal{P}^{\rm eq}_{\alpha\alpha}).

(6)

Here

\mathcal{P}^{\rm eq}_{\alpha\alpha}
are diagonal components of the equilibrium pressure tensor divided by the density.

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{\rm eq}_{\alpha\alpha}={\pi^{*}(\rho)}+u_{\alpha}^{2}.

(7)

Furthermore,
\beta\in[0,1]
is the relaxation parameter which is tied to the kinematic viscosity,

\displaystyle\nu=\pi^{*}\delta t\left(\frac{1}{2\beta}-\frac{1}{2}\right),

(8)

and
\varsigma=c_{s}\delta r/\delta t
is the lattice speed of sound,
\delta r
is the lattice spacing while
c_{s}
is a pure constant dependent on the choice of the lattice. Below, we use lattice units by setting
\delta r=\delta t=1
. The D
D
Q
3^{D}
lattices are characterized by the lattice speed of sound,

\varsigma=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}.

(9)

The LBGK setup becomes complete once the function
\pi^{*}
is specified. Note that, at variance with our previous study
[
13
]
where only the ideal gas equation of state was considered, in this paper we address a generic non-linear equation of state,

\partial_{\rho}\pi^{*}\neq 0.

(10)

The absence of a forcing term in the LBGK equation (
1
) points to that we only consider models where the pressure is introduced via the equilibrium function.

Throughout the paper, in order to illustrate general statements, we will make use of two non-ideal equations of state:

(a) The shallow water equation of state defined as,

\pi^{*}=\frac{g}{2}\rho,

(11)

where
g
is the gravitational constant. Note that, in the context of the shallow water model, the field
\rho
refers to the water column elevation.

(b) The second equation of state used for illustration purposes in this paper is that of van der Waals,

\pi^{*}=\frac{RT}{1-b\rho}-a\rho,

(12)

where
R
is the gas constant,
T
the temperature and
a
and
b
the long-range attractive force and excluded volume parameters. For the van der Waals equation of state, different from shallow water, not all temperature/density combinations lead to a hyperbolic system with finite speed eigen-modes.

Linear stability: The D

III.1
Necessary stability condition: Small lattice Knudsen number

As a first step we consider the one-dimensional LBGK system on the D
1
Q
3
lattice. In this case, we shall be able to derive in-full the linear stability conditions analytically.
We follow the analytical approach proposed in
[
15
,
13
]
, focusing first on stability in the hydrodynamic limit, which is a necessary condition for the stability of the full discrete system.

At the Euler level, the system is described via the following balance equations for mass and momentum,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho+\partial_{x}(\rho u)=0,

(13)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\rho u)+\partial_{x}\left(\rho u^{2}+\rho\pi^{*}\right)=0.

(14)

Eigen-mode analysis of the Euler system (
13
) and (
14
), reveals the propagation speeds of the two eigen-modes,

\displaystyle{c^{\pm}=u\pm\varsigma_{\rho},}

(15)

where
\varsigma_{\rho}
is the speed of sound,

\varsigma_{\rho}=\sqrt{\partial_{\rho}(\rho\pi^{*})}.

(16)

Speed of sound (
16
) is well-defined, provided

\partial_{\rho}(\rho\pi^{*})\geq 0,

(17)

which we always assume below. Moreover, we use the convention of positive square root in (
16
) which leads to
c^{+}\geq c^{-}
.

Moving on to the dissipation at the NS level, the balance equations become (see Appendix
A
),

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho+\partial_{x}(\rho u)=0,

(18)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\rho u)+\partial_{x}\left(\rho u^{2}+\rho\pi^{*}+\rho\pi^{\rm neq}\right)=0,

(19)

where the non-equilibrium pressure be written as,

\rho\pi^{\rm neq}=-\left(\frac{1-\beta}{2\beta}\right)\left(2\mathcal{A}\rho\varsigma^{2}\partial_{x}u+\mathcal{B}\partial_{x}\rho\right).

(20)

Here
\mathcal{A}
(viscosity factor) and
\mathcal{B}
(compressibility error) are,

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}=\frac{1}{2\varsigma^{2}}\left(3\varsigma^{2}-(c^{+})^{2}-(c^{-})^{2}-c^{+}c^{-}\right),

(21)

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}=\frac{3\varsigma^{2}}{2}\left(c^{+}+c^{-}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\left(({c^{+}})^{3}+({c^{-}})^{3}\right).

(22)

Upon linearization, spectral analysis of the balance equations (
18
) and (
19
) demonstrates the following dissipation-dispersion relations for the eigen-modes,

\displaystyle\omega^{\pm}

\displaystyle=c^{\pm}k+\mathrm{i}\varsigma^{2}\left(\frac{1-\beta}{2\beta}\right)\mathcal{R}^{\pm}k^{2}+O\left(k^{3}\right),

(23)

where
k
is the wave vector,
\mathrm{i}=\sqrt{-1}
, and
\mathcal{R}^{\pm}
are attenuation rates, written in terms of eigen-modes,

\mathcal{R}^{\pm}=\pm\frac{c^{\pm}\left(3\varsigma^{2}-(c^{\pm})^{2}\right)}{\varsigma^{2}(c^{+}-c^{-})}.

(24)

Positivity of both attenuation rates simultaneously,
\mathcal{R}^{\pm}\geq 0
, is the necessary condition for the linear stability of the LBGK in the hydrodynamic limit
k\to 0
. Positivity domain of both attenuation rates
\mathcal{R}^{\pm}\geq 0
is shown in Fig.
2
. Fig.
2
reveals that the linear stability range in terms of eigen-modes reads,

0\leq c^{+}\leq 1,\ -1\leq c^{-}\leq 0.

(25)

In the expanded dimensional form, condition (
25
) is written,

\displaystyle 0\leq u+\varsigma_{\rho}\leq\frac{\delta x}{\delta t},\ \frac{\delta x}{\delta t}\leq u-\varsigma_{\rho}\leq 0,

(26)

III.2
Sufficient stability condition: Arbitrary lattice Knudsen number

While the above stability condition (
25
) was established in the low Knudsen number limit
k\to 0
, we next extend the linear stability analysis to the full discrete lattice Boltzmann system (
1
). To that end, we follow the algorithm of Schur-stability analysis already presented for the LBM in

The most general linear stability condition for the D

3

\displaystyle\beta\left(\beta-1\right){\leq 0},

(29)

\displaystyle c^{+}c^{-}\left(({c^{+}})^{2}-1\right)\left(({c^{-}})^{2}-1\right)\leq 0.

(30)

\displaystyle c^{+}c^{-}-\cos^{2}\left(\frac{k}{2}\right)+\left(({c^{-}})^{2}+({c^{+}})^{2}\right)\cos^{2}\left(\frac{k}{2}\right)+({c^{-}})^{2}({c^{+}})^{2}\left(1-\cos^{2}\left(\frac{k}{2}\right)\right)\leq 0.

IV
Stability of the two-dimensional system

IV.1
Spectral analysis of the target equations

The target continuity and momentum Navier–Stokes equations are,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho+\bm{\nabla}\cdot\rho\bm{u}=0,

(32)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho\bm{u}+\bm{\nabla}\cdot\rho\bm{u}\otimes\bm{u}+\bm{\nabla}\cdot{\rho}\pi^{*}\bm{I}+\bm{\nabla}\cdot\bm{T}=0,

(33)

where the viscous stress tensor is defined as,

\bm{T}=-\rho\nu\left(\bm{\nabla}\bm{u}+\bm{\nabla}\bm{u^{\dagger}}-\bm{\nabla}\cdot\bm{u}\bm{I}\right)-{\rho}\eta\bm{\nabla}\cdot\bm{u}\bm{I},

(34)

and where
\nu
and
\eta
are the shear and the bulk viscosity, respectively.
Spectral analysis of the linearized target equations reveals the following dispersion-dissipation relations in two dimensions, see Appendix
B
:

\displaystyle\omega_{\rm shear}=\bm{k}\cdot\bm{u}+\mathrm{i}\nu{k}^{2},

(35)

\displaystyle\omega_{{\rm ac}\pm}=\bm{k}\cdot\bm{u}\pm{k}\varsigma_{\rho}+\mathrm{i}\frac{(\nu+\eta)}{2}{k}^{2}.

(36)

Here
\bm{k}
the wave-vector and
k=|\bm{k}|
the wave-number.
The dispersion and dissipation of the D
2
Q
9
LBGK model shall be probed against these target equations.

IV.2
Hydrodynamic limit of the LBGK model

The standard Chapman–Enskog analysis of the LBGK system (
1
) recovers the following balance equations, see Appendix
A
:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho+\bm{\nabla}\cdot\rho\bm{u}=0,

(37)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho\bm{u}+\bm{\nabla}\cdot\rho\bm{u}\otimes\bm{u}+\bm{\nabla}\cdot{\rho}\pi^{*}\bm{I}+\bm{\nabla}\cdot{\rho}\bm{\pi}^{\rm neq}=0,

(38)

where the non-equilibrium pressure tensor is written as,

{\rho}\bm{\pi}^{\rm neq}=\bm{T}+\bm{T}^{\prime}.

(39)

Here
\bm{T}
is the target viscous stress tensor (
34
) while the deviation
\bm{T}^{\prime}
has the form,

\bm{T}^{\prime}=-\left(\frac{1-\beta}{2\beta}\right)\left[\rho\varsigma^{2}\bm{A}^{\prime}\circ\left(\bm{I}\circ\bm{\nabla}\bm{u}\right)+\bm{B}\circ\left(\bm{I}\circ{\rm diag}(\bm{\nabla}\rho)\right)\right],

(40)

where
\circ
denotes the Hadamard product while
\bm{A}^{\prime}
and
\bm{B}
are diagonal matrices,

\displaystyle\bm{A}^{\prime}={\rm diag}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{\alpha\alpha}),

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{\alpha\alpha}=-\frac{3u_{\alpha}^{2}+3(\pi^{*}-\varsigma^{2})}{\varsigma^{2}},

(41)

\displaystyle\bm{B}={\rm diag}(\mathcal{B}_{\alpha\alpha}),

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{\alpha\alpha}=-u_{\alpha}^{3}-3u_{\alpha}(\varsigma_{\rho}^{2}-\varsigma^{2}).

(42)

For the D
2
Q
9
lattice, the deviation (
40

\bm{T}^{\prime}=-\left(\frac{1-\beta}{2\beta}\right)\begin{bmatrix}\rho\varsigma^{2}\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{xx}\partial_{x}u_{x}+\mathcal{B}_{xx}\partial_{x}\rho&0\\
0&\rho\varsigma^{2}\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{yy}\partial_{y}u_{y}+\mathcal{B}_{yy}\partial_{y}\rho\end{bmatrix},

(43)

Finally, the kinematic viscosity
\nu
is tied to
\beta
through (
8
) while the bulk viscosity

\eta=\nu\left(\frac{2+D}{D}-\frac{\partial\ln(\rho\pi^{*})}{\partial\ln\rho}\right).

Note that, the deviation (

In order to quantify the deviations, and to establish relations to stability conditions in the above one-dimensional case, we perform spectral analysis of Eqs. (
37
) and (
38
) considering a perturbation of the following form,

\{\rho^{\prime},\bm{u}^{\prime}\}=\{\hat{\rho},\hat{\bm{u}}\}\exp{[\mathrm{i}(k_{\alpha}x_{\alpha}}-\omega t)],

(45)

Under the perturbation (

), the dispersion-dissipation relations for the eigen-modes of the balance equations (

\displaystyle\omega_{\rm shear}=k_{\alpha}u_{\alpha}+\mathrm{i}\nu k_{\alpha}^{2},

(46)

\displaystyle\omega_{{\rm ac}\pm}=k_{\alpha}(u_{\alpha}\pm\varsigma_{\rho})+\mathrm{i}\varsigma^{2}\left(\frac{1-\beta}{2\beta}\right)\mathcal{R}^{\pm}k_{\alpha}^{2}+\mathcal{O}(k_{\alpha}^{3}).

Thus, the normal modes (

Q
9

At the same time, dissipativity of the shear mode (

\pi^{*}\geq 0.

Note that positivity of the pressure is different from the existence of the speed of sound (

V
Discussion and outlook

We presented a systematic linear stability analysis of a class of lattice Boltzmann models with non-ideal equations of state. Following the methodology set out in our previous publications
[
15
,
13
]
, we provided a comprehensive linear stability analysis of the one-dimensional D
1
Q
3
LBGK system.
The linear stability condition was found as a bound on normal eigen-modes (
25
), which confirms that the previous result for ideal gas equation of state
[
13
]
also holds for the general non-ideal pressure. Remarkably, the full linear stability of the D
1
Q
3
LBGK model is solely defined by the hydrodynamic eigen-modes (
55
) and (
16
) through their boundedness (
25
). Geometrical feature of the linear stability condition was highlighted in Fig.
6
.
These general findings were exemplified by two typical nonlinear equations of state, the shallow water and the van der Waals.
It was demonstrated that conventional equations of state and the standard equilibrium pressure (
7
) do not satisfy the stability requirement (
25
) unconditionally for the entire range of flow velocity.

The analysis was extended to higher dimensions, where stability conditions of the one-dimensional system were shown to remain necessary. Spectral dissipation and dispersion properties were studied for the two-dimensional D
2
Q
9
LBGK model.
Spectral analysis revealed that LBGK captures correctly the shear mode while admitting deviations in the normal modes dissipation rates.
Finally, the linear stability domain was studied numerically for the D
2
Q
9
LBGK model. The numerical analysis confirmed once again that boundedness of the normal modes (
25
) remains the necessary (but not sufficient) stability condition.
Overall, it was observed that the linear stability range for typical non-ideal equations of state was limited even more than for the ideal gas equation of state.

In the outlook, we comment that present study suggests a route to extend the linear stability domain via construction of special equations of state allowing for a non-trivial dependence on the flow velocity,
\pi^{*}=\pi^{*}(\rho,u)
.
To that end, we remind the example of isothermal pressure
\pi^{*}=\varsigma^{2}
. In this case, the entropic equilibrium with,

\displaystyle\pi^{*}

\displaystyle=\varsigma^{2}\left(2\sqrt{{1+(u/\varsigma)}^{2}}-{1-(u/\varsigma)}^{2}\right),

(53)

leads to
unconditional
linear stability, as was demonstrated in in
[
15
,
13
]
.
Linear stability domain of the entropic LBGK is juxtaposed to the LBGK with standard equilibrium in Fig.
16
: Unlike the latter, the entropic version is linearly stable on the entire domain of the flow velocity,
\bm{u}\in[-1,1]\times[-1,1]
, independently of the viscosity.
Following this example, we recognize two key considerations towards stable LBGK models: consistency and unconditional stability.
The consistency condition can be written down as,

\pi^{*}(\rho,u)=\pi^{*}(\rho)\left[1+\mathcal{O}{\left(\frac{u}{\varsigma_{\rho}}\right)}^{4}\right],\ \frac{u}{\varsigma_{\rho}}\to 0.

(54)

In other words, the assumed dependence of the equation of state on the flow velocity should be weaker than the quadratic term
u_{\alpha}^{2}
in the equilibrium pressure (
7
).
Eigen-modes of the D
1
Q
3
LBGK become, instead of (
15
),

\displaystyle c^{\pm}=u+\frac{\partial_{u}\pi^{*}}{2}\pm\sqrt{{\left(\frac{\partial_{u}\pi^{*}}{2}\right)}^{2}+\partial_{\rho}(\rho\pi^{*})}.

(55)

Note additional terms in this more general expression due to a dependence of the pressure on the flow velocity. Nevertheless, the stability condition (
25
) still holds for the more general case (
55
). The above example of the entropic equilibrium (
53
) confirms to both the consistency and the stability conditions.
Thus, conditions (
54
) and (
25
) form a basis for designing unconditionally stable LBGK realizations for nonideal equations of state which will be the topic of our future studies.

Appendix A
Multi-scale analysis of the generic LBM with non-linear-in-density pressure

Here we present a multi-scale perturbation analysis of the LBGK equation (
1
). We first perform a Taylor series expansion around
(\bm{r},t)
:

\delta t\mathcal{D}_{t}f_{i}+\frac{{\delta t}^{2}}{2}{\mathcal{D}_{t}}^{2}f_{i}+{O}({\delta t}^{3})=2\beta\left(f_{i}^{\rm eq}-f_{i}\right),

(56)

where we have retained terms up to order two and where
\mathcal{D}_{t}=\partial_{t}+\bm{c}_{i}\cdot\bm{\nabla}
. Introducing characteristic flow scale
\mathcal{L}
and characteristic flow velocity
\mathcal{U}
, equation (
56
) is made non-dimensional,

\left(\frac{\delta r}{\mathcal{L}}\right)\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\prime}f_{i}+\frac{1}{2}{\left(\frac{\delta r}{\mathcal{L}}\right)}^{2}{\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\prime}}^{2}f_{i}=2\beta\left(f_{i}^{\rm eq}-f_{i}\right),

(57)

where primed variables denote non-dimensional form and

\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\prime}=\frac{\mathcal{U}}{c}\left(\partial_{t}^{\prime}+\bm{c}_{i}\cdot\bm{\nabla}^{\prime}\right),

(58)

where
c=\delta r/\delta t
. Assuming acoustic, i.e.
\frac{\mathcal{U}}{c}\sim 1
and hydrodynamic, i.e.

\frac{\delta r}{\mathcal{L}}\sim\varepsilon,

(59)

scaling and dropping the primes for the sake of readability:

\varepsilon\mathcal{D}_{t}f_{i}+\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon^{2}{\mathcal{D}_{t}}^{2}f_{i}+{O}(\varepsilon^{3})=2\beta\left(f_{i}^{\rm eq}-f_{i}\right).

(60)

Then introducing multi-scale expansions:

\displaystyle f_{i}

\displaystyle=f_{i}^{(0)}+\varepsilon f_{i}^{(1)}+\varepsilon^{2}f_{i}^{(2)}+O(\varepsilon^{3}),

(61a)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon\partial_{t}^{(1)}+\varepsilon^{2}\partial_{t}^{(2)}+O(\varepsilon^{3}),

(61b)

\displaystyle\bm{\nabla}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon\bm{\nabla},

(61c)

the following equations are recovered at scales
\varepsilon
and
\varepsilon^{2}
:

\displaystyle\varepsilon

\displaystyle:\mathcal{D}_{t}^{(1)}f_{i}^{(0)}=-2\beta f_{i}^{(1)},

(62a)

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{2}

\displaystyle:\partial_{t}^{(2)}f_{i}^{(0)}+\mathcal{D}_{t}^{(1)}\left(1-\beta\right)f_{i}^{(1)}=-2\beta f_{i}^{(2)},

(62b)

with
f_{i}^{(0)}=f_{i}^{\rm eq}
. Taking the moments of the Chapman-Enskog-expanded equation at order
\varepsilon
:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{(1)}\rho+\bm{\nabla}\cdot\rho\bm{u}

\displaystyle=0,

(63a)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{(1)}\rho\bm{u}+\bm{\nabla}\cdot\rho\bm{u}\otimes\bm{u}+\bm{\nabla}\cdot\rho\pi^{*}\bm{I}

\displaystyle=0.

(63b)

At order
\varepsilon^{2}
the continuity equation is:

\partial_{t}^{(2)}\rho=0.

(64)

Summing up Eqs. (
63a
) and (
64
) we recover the continuity equation as:

\partial_{t}\rho+\bm{\nabla}\cdot\rho\bm{u}+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{3})=0.

(65)

For the momentum balance equation, using the mass and momentum balance equations from order
\varepsilon
and,

\partial_{t}^{(1)}\rho\pi^{*}+\bm{\nabla}\cdot\rho\pi^{*}\bm{u}=\left(\rho\pi^{*}-\rho\partial_{\rho}\rho\pi^{*}\right)\bm{\nabla}\cdot\bm{u},

(66)

we obtain,

\partial_{t}^{(2)}\rho\bm{u}+\bm{\nabla}\cdot{\rho}\bm{\pi}^{\rm neq}=0,

(67)

with

{\rho}\bm{\pi}^{\rm neq}=\bm{T}-\left(\frac{1-\beta}{2\beta}\right)\left[\rho\varsigma^{2}\bm{A}^{\prime}\circ\left(\bm{I}\circ\bm{\nabla}\bm{u}\right)+\bm{B}\circ\left(\bm{I}\circ{\rm diag}(\bm{\nabla}\rho)\right)\right],

(68)

where
\circ
refers to the Hadamard product. The NS viscous stress tensor is,

\bm{T}=-\rho\nu\left(\bm{\nabla}\bm{u}+\bm{\nabla}\bm{u^{\dagger}}-\bm{\nabla}\cdot\bm{u}\bm{I}\right)-{\rho}\eta\bm{\nabla}\cdot\bm{u}\bm{I},

(69)

with

\displaystyle\nu=\pi^{*}\delta t\left(\frac{1}{2\beta}-\frac{1}{2}\right),

(70)

and

\eta=\nu\left(\frac{2+D}{D}-\frac{\partial\ln(\rho\pi^{*})}{\partial\ln\rho}\right).

(71)

Furthermore,
\bm{A}^{\prime}={\rm diag}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{xx},\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{yy})
with,

\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{\alpha\alpha}=-\frac{3u_{\alpha}^{2}+3(\pi^{*}-\varsigma^{2})}{\varsigma^{2}},

(72)

and
\bm{B}={\rm diag}(\mathcal{B}_{xx},\mathcal{B}_{yy})
with,

\mathcal{B}_{\alpha\alpha}=-u_{\alpha}^{3}-3u_{\alpha}(\partial_{\rho}(\rho\pi^{*})-\varsigma^{2}).

(73)

Appendix B
Spectral analysis of linearized balance equations for non-ideal fluids

Here we detail the linearization process of the non-ideal NS balance equations and derive spectral dispersion and dissipation of eigen-modes. To that end, we introduce the following first-order expansion around a global equilibrium state
\left(\bar{\rho},\bar{\bm{u}}\right)
,

\rho=\bar{\rho}+\rho^{\prime},\,\,\bm{u}=\bar{\bm{u}}+\bm{u}^{\prime}

(74)

where
\rho^{\prime}
and
\bm{u}^{\prime}
are the perturbations. For the sake of readability, we will remove the bars in the remainder of text. Introducing the expansions into the balance equation for
\rho
and collecting linear terms,

\partial_{t}\rho^{\prime}+{\rho}\bm{\nabla}\cdot\bm{u}^{\prime}+{\bm{u}}\cdot\bm{\nabla}\rho^{\prime}=0,

(75)

while for the momentum balance equations,

{\rho}\partial_{t}\bm{u}^{\prime}+{\rho}{\bm{u}}\cdot\bm{\nabla}\bm{u}^{\prime}+\left(\pi^{*}({\rho})+{\rho}\partial_{{\rho}}\pi^{*}({\rho}))\right)\bm{\nabla}\rho^{\prime}\\
-{\rho}\nu\bm{\nabla}\cdot\left(\bm{\nabla}\bm{u}^{\prime}+{\bm{\nabla}\bm{u}^{\prime}}^{\dagger}-\frac{2}{D}\bm{\nabla}\cdot\bm{u}^{\prime}\bm{I}\right)\\
-\rho\eta\bm{\nabla}\cdot\left(\bm{\nabla}\cdot\bm{u}^{\prime}\bm{I}\right)=0.

(76)

Next considering the perturbations to be monochromatic plane waves of the form,

\{\rho^{\prime},\bm{u}^{\prime}\}={\{\hat{\rho},\hat{\bm{u}}\}}\exp{[\mathrm{i}(\bm{k\cdot\bm{r}}-\omega t)]},

(77)

and introducing them into the linearized equations, one obtains,

\displaystyle{\mathrm{i}\omega\hat{\rho}-\mathrm{i}{\rho}\bm{k}\cdot\hat{\bm{u}}-\mathrm{i}\bm{u}\cdot\bm{k}\hat{\rho}=0,}

(78)

\displaystyle{\mathrm{i}\omega\hat{\bm{u}}-\mathrm{i}(\bm{k}\cdot\bm{u})\hat{\bm{u}}-\mathrm{i}\bm{k}\left(\frac{\pi^{*}}{{\rho}}+\partial_{{\rho}}\pi^{*}\right)\hat{\rho}+\nu{k}^{2}\hat{\bm{u}}+\left(\frac{D-2}{D}\nu+\eta\right)\bm{k}(\bm{k}\cdot\hat{\bm{u}})=0.}

(79)

The system (
78
) and (
79
) can be written as an eigen-value problem,

\omega\Phi={M}\Phi.

(80)

Specifying for the two-dimensional case, we have
\Phi=\left[\hat{\rho},\hat{u}_{x},\hat{u}_{y}\right]^{\dagger}
and,

{M}=\begin{bmatrix}\bm{k}\cdot{\bm{u}}&{\rho}k_{x}&{\rho}k_{y}\\
\left(\frac{\pi^{*}}{{\rho}}+\partial_{{\rho}}\pi^{*}\right)k_{x}&\bm{k}\cdot{\bm{u}}+\mathrm{i}(\nu{k}^{2}+\eta k_{x}^{2})&\mathrm{i}\eta k_{x}k_{y}\\
\left(\frac{\pi^{*}}{{\rho}}+\partial_{{\rho}}\pi^{*}\right)k_{y}&\mathrm{i}{\eta}k_{x}k_{y}&\bm{k}\cdot{\bm{u}}+\mathrm{i}(\nu{k}^{2}+\eta k_{y}^{2})\end{bmatrix}.

(81)

Solving the eigen-value problem,
\det\left(M-\omega I\right)=0
, one obtains,

\displaystyle\omega_{\rm shear}

\displaystyle=\bm{k}\cdot{\bm{u}}+\mathrm{i}\nu{k}^{2},

(82)

\displaystyle\omega_{\rm ac+}

\displaystyle=\bm{k}\cdot{\bm{u}}+{k}\sqrt{\partial_{{\rho}}({\rho}\pi^{*})}+\mathrm{i}\frac{(\nu+\eta)}{2}{k}^{2},

(83)

\displaystyle\omega_{\rm ac-}

\displaystyle=\bm{k}\cdot{\bm{u}}-{k}\sqrt{\partial_{{\rho}}({\rho}\pi^{*})}+\mathrm{i}\frac{(\nu+\eta)}{2}{k}^{2},

(84)

indicating the existence of two acoustic modes and of a shear mode.

Abstract

We study the non-relativistic Newton-Cartan limit of higher-order gravity theories in arbitrary dimensions. We first study it at the level of the action by introducing an additional 1-form gauge field and coupling it appropriately to the gravity sector. We extend this procedure to any theory whose Lagrangian is a function of the Ricci scalar, quadratic Ricci tensor and quadratic Riemann tensor. We also study the limit of the equations of motion for two models, Einstein-Gauss-Bonnet gravity and quadratic Ricci scalar theory. We prove that, in the first case, it is possible to obtain the Poisson equation by introducing a scalar field and imposing an appropriate constraint.
In the latter case, we show that it is possible to get the Poisson equation from the limit of the equations of motion as long as the on-shell constraint used in the two-derivative theory is supplemented with a further condition. We give the expressions of the two higher-order corrected Poisson equations in terms of curvatures of Newton-Cartan geometry. In both cases, we derive the full set of non-relativistic equations and study their boost transformations. The two sets of equations of motion define zero-torsion Gauss-Bonnet Newton-Cartan gravity and zero-torsion quadratic Ricci scalar Newton-Cartan gravity.

Introduction

The generalised correspondence principle is a cornerstone principle in theoretical physics. It asserts that a new theory, aiming to explain phenomena not addressed by previous ones, should, in certain limits or regimes, reproduce the established results of well-tested theories. This principle underpins the robustness of our knowledge and it can be naturally used as a theoretical test for new theories. Non-Lorentzian theories obtained via a limit of Lorentzian ones provide compelling examples in which inspecting a specific regime gives both a consistency check of the parent theory and possible new insights.

Among all non-Lorentzian theories a prominent role is held by Newton-Cartan gravity
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
, the covariant formulation of Newtonian gravity.
Newton-Cartan geometry stands for Newton-Cartan gravity as Riemannian geometry stands for General Relativity. This non-Lorentzian geometry, in contrast with Riemannian geometry, is characterised by the presence of two degenerate metrics. The fields of Newton-Cartan gravity are a clock form
\tau_{\mu}
, a spatial vielbein
e_{\mu}{}^{a}
and a one-form gauge field
a_{\mu}
, associated with the conservation of mass
[
4
,
5

6

. Analogously to General Relativity, which can be obtained by gauging the Poincaré algebra, Newton-Cartan gravity can be built by gauging the Bargmann algebra, the central extension of the Galilei algebra
[
7
,
8
]
.

Another way to obtain Newton-Cartan gravity is via a non-relativistic limit of General Relativity
[
9
]
. The limit is a procedure based on two main steps. The first is to redefine the relativistic fields in terms of the would-be non-relativistic ones, involving a parameter. The second step consists of sending the parameter to a critical value. In the present work this parameter can be identified with the speed of light, which we denote by
{\color[rgb]{1,0.3711,0.1211}c}
and it is sent to infinity to inspect the non-relativistic regime. The construction of Newton-Cartan gravity from a relativistic theory is based on a decomposition of the spacetime tangent space into a time-like direction and the remaining space-like directions. This decomposition, in
D

\displaystyle SO(1,D-1)\rightarrow SO(D-1)\ltimes\mathds{R}^{D-1}\,,

\displaystyle SO(1,D-1)\rightarrow SO(1,p)\times SO(D-p-1)\ltimes\mathds{R}^{D-p-1}\,,

1
Relativistic Quadratic Gravity Theories

In this section we introduce the relativistic theories whose non-relativistic limit will be studied later. We are interested in actions that are at most quadratic in the curvatures. These include up to the quartic power of the derivative of the metric. In particular, we consider theories described by the following action (we refer the interested reader to
[
54
,
55
,
56
]
for a more detailed analysis of these theories):

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}=

\displaystyle\,\int d^{D}x\,\sqrt{-g}\,\Big({\rm R}+\alpha\,{\rm R}^{2}+\beta\,{\rm R}_{\mu\nu}{\rm R}^{\mu\nu}+\gamma\,{\rm R}_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}{\rm R}^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\Big)\,,

(1)

where
g
denotes the determinant of the spacetime metric
g_{\mu\nu}
;
{\rm R}_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}
,
{\rm R}_{\mu\nu}
and
{\rm R}
are the Riemann tensor, Ricci tensor and Ricci scalar respectively and
\alpha,\beta
and
\gamma
are three coupling constants. Definitions of the fundamental quantities are listed in
appendix B
. We refer to
appendix A
for details about the notation. We work in
D
dimensions and we adopt mostly-plus signature for the tangent space metric. We do not assume the higher-derivative terms to be perturbative corrections, i.e. we do not assume
\alpha,\beta
and
\gamma
to be small. We consider these as non-perturbative corrections.

Among the purely quadratic theories parameterised by different values of
\alpha,\beta
and
\gamma
there is one theory that enjoys special properties, this is Gauss-Bonnet gravity. Gauss-Bonnet gravity is the theory described by setting
\beta=-4\alpha
and
\gamma=\alpha
in
(1)
. It is a topological invariant in four dimensions and despite having an action that is fourth-order in the derivative of the metric the equations of motion are second-order. We investigate the non-relativistic limit of the equations of motion of Einstein-Gauss-Bonnet gravity, i.e. the Einstein-Hilbert action supplemented with the Gauss-Bonnet term, in
section 3.2
, where we also find the corresponding Poisson equation. We repeat the same analysis for the quadratic Ricci scalar gravity in
section 3.3
, i.e. the theory defined by
\beta=\gamma=0
in
(1)
.

In the next section we study the non-relativistic limit of the action
(1)
. The procedure extends to any theory whose Lagrangian is written as a function of the Ricci scalar,
{\rm R}_{\mu\nu}{\rm R}^{\mu\nu}
and
{\rm R}_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}{\rm R}^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}
.

2
Non-Relativistic Limit of the Action

In this section we study the magnetic limit of the higher-order theories introduced in
section 1
at the level of the action. We start by reviewing the electric and magnetic limits of the Einstein-Hilbert-Maxwell action and then we extend the procedure to relativistic gravity theory with higher-order corrections.

2.1
Electric & Magnetic Limits of the Einstein-Hilbert-Maxwell Action

To study the theories described in
section 1
it is useful first to briefly review the two-derivative case of the Einstein-Hilbert action. We denote with
E_{\mu}{}^{\hat{A}}
the
D
-dimensional vielbein. The
D
-dimensional flat index splits as
\hat{A}=\{0,a\}
where
a=1,...,D-1
. There are two main possibilities to take the limit of the Einstein-Hilbert action. The first is to define an ansatz for the metric, expand the action plugging in it this ansatz, and send
{\color[rgb]{1,0.3711,0.1211}c}
to infinity. This procedure will select the highest power of the expansion of the action. This approach is known as the
electric
limit. We briefly review how it works. We consider the following ansatz for the
D
-dimensional vielbein

\displaystyle E_{\mu}{}^{0}=

\displaystyle\,{\color[rgb]{1,0.3711,0.1211}c}\tau_{\mu}\,,

\displaystyle E^{\mu}{}_{0}=

\displaystyle\,\frac{1}{{\color[rgb]{1,0.3711,0.1211}c}}\tau^{\mu}\,,

(2a)

\displaystyle E_{\mu}{}^{a}=

\displaystyle\,e_{\mu}{}^{a}\,,

\displaystyle E^{\mu}{}_{a}=

\displaystyle\,e_{\mu}{}_{a}\,.

(2b)

The would-be non-relativistic fields
\tau_{\mu}
,
e_{\mu}{}^{a}
and their inverses satisfy the following relations:

\displaystyle e_{\mu}{}^{a}e^{\mu}{}_{b}=

\displaystyle\,\delta_{b}^{a}\,,

\displaystyle\tau_{\mu}\tau^{\mu}=

\displaystyle\,1\,,

(3a)

\displaystyle e_{\mu}{}^{a}\tau^{\mu}=\tau_{\mu}e^{\mu}{}_{b}=

\displaystyle\,0\,,

\displaystyle\tau_{\mu}\tau^{\nu}+e_{\mu}{}^{a}e^{\nu}{}_{a}=

\displaystyle\,\delta_{\mu}^{\nu}\,.

(3b)

The ansatz
(2)
implies the following expansion of the metric and its inverse

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}=

\displaystyle\,E_{\mu}{}^{\hat{A}}E_{\nu}{}^{\hat{B}}\eta_{\hat{A}\hat{B}}=-{\color[rgb]{1,0.3711,0.1211}c^{2}}\tau_{\mu}\tau_{\nu}+h_{\mu\nu}\,,

(4a)

\displaystyle g^{\mu\nu}=

\displaystyle\,E^{\mu}{}_{\hat{A}}E^{\nu}{}_{\hat{B}}\eta^{\hat{A}\hat{B}}=-\frac{1}{{\color[rgb]{1,0.3711,0.1211}c^{2}}}\tau^{\mu}\tau^{\nu}+h^{\mu\nu}\,.

(4b)

Plugging the ansatz into the Ricci scalar we get the following expansion:

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}=

\displaystyle\,\frac{{\color[rgb]{1,0.3711,0.1211}c^{2}}}{4}\int d^{D}x\,e\,t_{ab}t^{ab}+\mathcal{O}({\color[rgb]{1,0.3711,0.1211}c^{0}})\,,

(5)

where
t_{ab}
is the transverse component of the intrinsic torsion, defined by

\displaystyle t_{ab}=

\displaystyle\,e^{\mu}{}_{a}e^{\nu}{}_{b}\,2\partial_{[\mu}\tau_{\nu]}\,,

(6)

(see
appendix A
for further details) and

\displaystyle e=\det(\tau_{\mu}\,,e_{\mu}{}^{a})=

\displaystyle\,\epsilon^{\mu_{1}...\mu_{D}}\tau_{\mu_{1}}e_{\mu_{2}}{}^{1}...e_{\mu_{D}}{}^{D-1}\,.

(7)

We have absorbed the power of
{\color[rgb]{1,0.3711,0.1211}c}
coming from the determinant in an overall rescaling and we will do so also in the next. Performing the limit
{\color[rgb]{1,0.3711,0.1211}c}\rightarrow\infty
in
(5)
amounts to rescaling the expression by an overall power
{\color[rgb]{1,0.3711,0.1211}c^{-2}}
and selecting then the leading order. The result is simply
(5)
without the
{\color[rgb]{1,0.3711,0.1211}c^{2}}
factor.

An alternative approach is to regard the leading term of
(5)
as a divergent term and try to find a way to cancel it in such a way as to get access to the subleading order of the expansion. We refer to this as the
magnetic
limit. To achieve the cancellation of the leading order term in
(5)
we modify our starting point by adding a Maxwell term in the action

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}=

\displaystyle\,\int d^{D}x\sqrt{-g}\,\bigg({\rm R}-\frac{1}{4}F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}\bigg)\,,

(8)

where
F_{\mu\nu}=2\partial_{[\mu}A_{\nu]}
and
A_{\mu}
is a 1-form gauge field. By choosing the ansatz for the gauge field one tries to cancel the leading order term coming from the Ricci scalar. In particular, we consider the following ansatz:

\displaystyle A_{\mu}=

\displaystyle\,{\color[rgb]{1,0.3711,0.1211}c}\tau_{\mu}+\frac{1}{{\color[rgb]{1,0.3711,0.1211}c}}a_{\mu}\,,

(9)

with
a_{\mu}
playing the role of the non-relativistic gauge field after the limit. With this, the field strength expands as

\displaystyle F_{\mu\nu}=

\displaystyle\,{\color[rgb]{1,0.3711,0.1211}c}t_{\mu\nu}+\frac{1}{{\color[rgb]{1,0.3711,0.1211}c}}f_{\mu\nu}\,,

(10)

where
f_{\mu\nu}=2\partial_{[\mu}a_{\nu]}
. Then the expansion of the action reads

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}=

\displaystyle\,\int d^{D}x\,e\,\bigg(e^{\mu a}e^{\nu}_{a}\tilde{\rm R}_{\mu\nu}+2\tau^{\mu}e^{\nu a}e^{\rho}_{a}\tilde{\nabla}_{\nu}t_{\mu\rho}-\frac{3}{2}t_{0a}t_{0}{}^{a}-t^{ab}f_{ab}\bigg)+\mathcal{O}({\color[rgb]{1,0.3711,0.1211}c^{-2}})\,,

(11)

with
\tilde{\rm R}_{\mu\nu}
being a tensor defined using the following connection

\displaystyle\tilde{\Gamma}_{\mu\nu}^{\rho}=

\displaystyle\,\frac{1}{2}h^{\rho\xi}(\partial_{\mu}h_{\nu\xi}+\partial_{\nu}h_{\mu\xi}-\partial_{\xi}h_{\mu\nu})+\tau^{\rho}\partial_{\mu}\tau_{\nu}+h^{\rho\xi}\tau_{(\mu}f_{\nu)\xi}\,,

(12)

that mimics the definition of the relativistic Ricci tensor, see
appendix B
. The connection
(12)
is such that

\displaystyle\tilde{\nabla}_{\mu}\tau_{\nu}=

\displaystyle\,0\,,

\displaystyle\tilde{\nabla}_{\mu}h^{\nu\rho}=

\displaystyle\,0\,,

(13)

where
\tilde{\nabla}_{\mu}
denotes the covariant derivative containing only the non-relativistic connection defined above. This connection is boost-covariant and gauge invariant. See
appendix C
for further details.

In the limit
{\color[rgb]{1,0.3711,0.1211}c}\rightarrow\infty
the term of order
{\color[rgb]{1,0.3711,0.1211}c^{0}}
in
(11)
is the only surviving term. This is the result of the magnetic limit. We can rewrite
(11)
conveniently in term of the Newton-Cartan geometric quantities defined in
appendix C
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}=

\displaystyle\,\int d^{D}x\,e\,\bigg[-{\rm Ric(J)}+2\tau^{\mu}h^{\nu\rho}\tilde{\nabla}_{\nu}{\rm R(H)}_{\mu\rho}-\frac{3}{2}{\rm R(H)}_{0a}{\rm R(H)}_{0}{}^{a}\bigg]\,\,.

(14)

Before moving our attention to the higher-order case it is useful to report the limit of the Lorentz and gauge transformation rules,

\displaystyle\delta\tau_{\mu}=

\displaystyle\,0\,,

(15a)

\displaystyle\delta e_{\mu}{}^{a}=

\displaystyle\,\lambda^{a}{}_{b}e_{\mu}{}^{b}+\lambda^{a}\tau_{\mu}\,,

(15b)

\displaystyle\delta e^{\mu}{}_{a}=

\displaystyle\,\lambda_{a}{}^{b}e^{\mu}{}_{b}\,,

(15c)

\displaystyle\delta\tau^{\mu}=

\displaystyle\,-\lambda^{b}e^{\mu}{}_{b}\,,

(15d)

\displaystyle\delta a_{\mu}=

\displaystyle\,\partial_{\mu}\sigma-\lambda_{a}e_{\mu}{}^{a}\,,

(15e)

obtained by adopting the following ansatz for the relativistic parameters

\displaystyle\Lambda_{ab}=

\displaystyle\,\lambda_{ab}\,,

\displaystyle\Lambda_{0a}=

\displaystyle\,-\frac{1}{{\color[rgb]{1,0.3711,0.1211}c}}\lambda_{a}\,,

\displaystyle\Sigma=

\displaystyle\,\frac{1}{{\color[rgb]{1,0.3711,0.1211}c}}\sigma\,.

(16)

We do not report the diffeomorphisms, since they are unaffected by the limit. The transformations
(15)
are those obtained by a gauging of the Bargmann algebra, the central extension of the Galilei algebra. The introduction of the Maxwell term in the relativistic theory provides the extra U(1) generator useful to land on the Bargmann algebra.

2.2
Magnetic Limit of Higher-Order Gravity Theories

We would like to extend the procedure to take the magnetic limit to the case of higher-order gravity theories. Our starting point is the action introduced in
section 1
. We will study the three different terms separately. In taking the limit the couplings will not be rescaled by any power of
{\color[rgb]{1,0.3711,0.1211}c}
. In analogy with the two-derivative case, we expect that, to take the magnetic limit, we should introduce not only the two-derivative Maxwell term but also four-derivative Maxwell terms and four-derivative couplings between the gauge field and the gravity sector. Since we already know that the two-derivative Einstein-Hilbert-Maxwell finite part
occurs at order
{\color[rgb]{1,0.3711,0.1211}c^{0}}
we should require all the orders higher than that in the expansion of the higher-derivative terms to cancel. If this is not the case then the contribution to the limit of the two-derivative part would be washed out after the limit. In general, it emerges that achieving a full cancellation of all the orders greater than
{\color[rgb]{1,0.3711,0.1211}c^{0}}
is harder than the two-derivative case. In particular, the four-derivative terms produce non-trivial diverging terms both at order
{\color[rgb]{1,0.3711,0.1211}c^{4}}
and
{\color[rgb]{1,0.3711,0.1211}c^{2}}
as follows:

\displaystyle{\rm R}^{2}=

\displaystyle\,\frac{1}{16}{\color[rgb]{1,0.3711,0.1211}c^{4}}t_{ab}t^{ab}t_{cd}t^{cd}+

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}{\color[rgb]{1,0.3711,0.1211}c^{2}}t_{ab}t^{ab}\Big(2\tilde{\rm R}_{c}{}^{c}+4\tilde{\nabla}(t)_{c0}{}^{c}-4t_{0c}t_{0}{}^{c}-t^{cd}f_{cd}\Big)+\mathcal{O}({\color[rgb]{1,0.3711,0.1211}c^{0}})\,,

(17a)

\displaystyle{\rm R}_{\mu\nu}{\rm R}^{\mu\nu}=

\displaystyle\,\frac{1}{16}{\color[rgb]{1,0.3711,0.1211}c^{4}}\Big(t_{ab}t^{ab}t_{cd}t^{cd}+4t_{a}{}^{b}t_{b}{}^{c}t_{c}{}^{d}t_{d}{}^{a}\Big)+\,,

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}{\color[rgb]{1,0.3711,0.1211}c^{2}}\Big[4t^{ab}t_{a}{}^{c}(\tilde{\rm R}_{bc}+\tilde{\nabla}(t)_{b0c}-3t_{0b}t_{0c})+

\displaystyle+t^{ab}t_{ab}(-2\tilde{\nabla}(t)_{c0}{}^{c}+2t_{0c}t_{0}{}^{c}+t^{cd}f_{cd})+

\displaystyle+2(\tilde{\nabla}(t)_{a}{}^{ab}-4t^{ab}t_{0a})\tilde{\nabla}(t)_{cb}{}^{b}\Big]+\mathcal{O}({\color[rgb]{1,0.3711,0.1211}c^{0}})\,,

(17b)

\displaystyle{\rm R}_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}{\rm R}^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}=

\displaystyle\,\frac{1}{8}{\color[rgb]{1,0.3711,0.1211}c^{4}}\Big(3t_{ab}t^{ab}t_{cd}t^{cd}+5t_{a}{}^{b}t_{b}{}^{c}t_{c}{}^{d}t_{d}{}^{a}\Big)+

\displaystyle+{\color[rgb]{1,0.3711,0.1211}c^{2}}\bigg[2t^{ab}t^{cd}\bigg(\tilde{\rm R}_{a(bc)d}+\frac{1}{4}t_{ab}f_{cd}\bigg)-\frac{4}{3}\tilde{\nabla}(t)^{abc}\tilde{\nabla}(t)_{(ab)c}+

\displaystyle-2t^{ab}t_{a}{}^{c}\bigg(\tilde{\nabla}(t)_{b0c}+\frac{1}{3}t_{0b}t_{0c}-\frac{3}{4}t_{b}{}^{d}f_{cd}\bigg)+

\displaystyle+\frac{8}{3}t^{ab}t_{0}{}^{c}(2\tilde{\nabla}(t)_{(ac)b}-t_{ab}t_{0c})\bigg]+\mathcal{O}({\color[rgb]{1,0.3711,0.1211}c^{0}})\,,

(17c)

where
\tilde{\nabla}(t)_{\mu\nu\rho}:=\tilde{\nabla}_{\mu}t_{\nu\rho}
. We start our analysis by considering the Ricci scalar squared term. For this term the knowledge acquired from the two-derivative case suggests us to modify it as

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}=

\displaystyle\,\bigg({\rm R}-\frac{1}{4}F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}\bigg)^{2}\,.

(18)

For the other terms the situation is more involved so we can define a more systematic approach. We consider a basis of possible four-derivative terms involving the Maxwell tensor and curvatures. The basis is:

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}=\Big\{

\displaystyle(F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu})^{2}\,\,,F^{\mu}{}_{\nu}F^{\nu}{}_{\rho}F^{\rho}{}_{\sigma}F^{\sigma}{}_{\mu}\,,F^{\mu\nu}F^{\rho\sigma}{\rm R}_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\,,F^{\mu\rho}F^{\nu}{}_{\rho}{\rm R}_{\mu\nu}\,,

\displaystyle F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}{\rm R}\,,\nabla_{\mu}F_{\nu\rho}\nabla^{\mu}F^{\nu\rho}\,,\nabla_{\mu}F^{\mu\rho}\nabla_{\nu}F^{\nu}{}_{\rho}\Big\}\,.

(19)

A key feature that distinguishes the two-derivative case from the four-derivative case and has an important impact in the present treatment is that, while in the two-derivative case the reciprocal coefficients between the Ricci scalar and the Maxwell term can be modified by a redefinition of the gauge field, keeping fixed the sign, in the presence of four-derivative terms the coefficients cannot be all arbitrarily changed by simple redefinitions. This is because the number of terms is usually greater than the number of fields that can be redefined. This makes the cancellation of the diverging terms harder to realize.

We proceed by adding the higher-derivative terms in
\mathcal{B}
to the pure four-derivative gravitational part, parameterising each of them with a coefficient. We then fix the coefficients by requiring divergence cancellation. Although some of the action terms appearing in the basis above could be related via integration by parts, without loss of generality, we prefer to work with this set of terms because performing integration by parts when studying the cancellation of divergences can result in more involved calculations. Thus we have considered all the independent terms up to Bianchi identities and terms of the type
F\nabla\nabla F
, since it can be proved that these do not play any role in the cancellation of divergences.
By proceeding as described we find in all three cases one single solution. More precisely, for each of the three quadratic terms in
(1)
there is one single combination of the terms in
\mathcal{B}
that cancels the corresponding divergent contribution. The first case being the one we have already predicted. Then
(1)
should modified as

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}=

\displaystyle\,\int d^{D}x\sqrt{-g}\Big(\mathcal{L}_{EHM}+\alpha\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}+\beta\mathcal{L}_{\beta}+\gamma\mathcal{L}_{\gamma}\Big)\,,

(20)

with
\mathcal{L}_{EHM}
the Einstein-Hilbert-Maxwell Lagrangian and

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}=

\displaystyle\,\bigg({\rm R}-\frac{1}{4}F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}\bigg)^{2}\,,

(21a)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\beta}=

\displaystyle\,{\rm R}_{\mu\nu}{\rm R}^{\mu\nu}+\frac{1}{16}F^{4}+\frac{1}{4}F^{\mu}{}_{\nu}F^{\nu}{}_{\rho}F^{\rho}{}_{\sigma}F^{\sigma}{}_{\mu}-\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{\mu}F^{\mu\nu}\nabla_{\rho}F_{\nu}{}^{\rho}-F^{\mu\rho}F^{\nu}{}_{\rho}{\rm R}_{\mu\nu}\,,

(21b)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\gamma}=

\displaystyle\,{\rm R}_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}{\rm R}^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}+\frac{3}{8}F^{4}+\frac{5}{8}F^{\mu}{}_{\nu}F^{\nu}{}_{\rho}F^{\rho}{}_{\sigma}F^{\sigma}{}_{\mu}+\nabla_{\mu}F_{\nu\rho}\nabla^{\mu}F^{\nu\rho}-\frac{3}{2}F^{\mu\nu}F^{\rho\sigma}{\rm R}_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\,.

(21c)

3
Non-Relativistic Limit of Equations of Motion & the Poisson Equation

In the previous section we have studied the non-relativistic limit of the higher-derivative gravity action. In this section, we shift our attention to the limit taken at the level of the equations of motion, and, in particular, we investigate the possibility of obtaining the higher-derivative corrected version of the Poisson equation directly from this limit. We start by reviewing the two-derivative case and then we consider four-derivative corrections.

3.1
Einstein-Hilbert-Maxwell

The Poisson equation is a second-order scalar differential equation describing, in Newtonian gravity, the relations between the Newton potential and the mass distribution. It is well known that performing the non-relativistic limit at the level of the action does not guarantee that the resulting action contains the Poisson equation
[
57
]
.
This happens in several cases
[
13
,
18
]
. However, it is possible, in the same setting, to derive the Poisson equation from the limit of the equations of motion. The limit of the equations of motion is obtained by substituting the redefinition ansatz into them. This yields an expansion in powers of
{\color[rgb]{1,0.3711,0.1211}c}
, as

\displaystyle[X]={\color[rgb]{1,0.3711,0.1211}c^{n}}\accentset{(n)}{[X]}+{\color[rgb]{1,0.3711,0.1211}c^{n-2}}\accentset{(n-2)}{[X]}+...=0\,,

(36)

where
[X]
denotes the equation of motion of a generic field
X
and
\accentset{(i)}{[X]}
denotes the coefficient of the
i
-th power of
{\color[rgb]{1,0.3711,0.1211}c}
in the expansion induced by the ansatz (we will adopt this notation for the rest of the paper). Performing the limit
{\color[rgb]{1,0.3711,0.1211}c}\rightarrow\infty
just amounts to selecting the leading order of its
{\color[rgb]{1,0.3711,0.1211}c}
-expansion. In this example thus the result gives:

\displaystyle\accentset{(n)}{[X]}=

\displaystyle\,0\,.

(37)

However, two or more equations of motion could have non-linearly independent leading terms in the expansion, implying that a proper combination of them will have an expansion starting at a lower power of
{\color[rgb]{1,0.3711,0.1211}c}
. This means that the result of the limit strongly depends on how the equations are combined before expanding and sending
{\color[rgb]{1,0.3711,0.1211}c}
to infinity. The prescription that minimises the amount of terms lost in the limit is to combine them in such a way as to have the minimum number of powers of
{\color[rgb]{1,0.3711,0.1211}c}
appearing in their expansion. For further details see
[
57
]
. The general scalar combination that we can consider for the Poisson equation is:

\displaystyle[P]:=

\displaystyle\,\texttt{A}\,E^{\mu}{}_{a}E^{\nu a}[G]_{\mu\nu}+\texttt{C}E^{\mu}{}_{0}E^{\nu}{}_{0}\,[G]_{\mu\nu}+\texttt{B}\,E_{\mu}{}^{0}[A]^{\mu}\,,

(38)

where
\texttt{A},\texttt{B}
and
C
are three constant parameters that we will try to fine-tune to get the Poisson equation from the limit and
[G]_{\mu\nu}
and
[A]^{\mu}
are the equations of motion for the metric and the gauge field, defined by

\displaystyle\delta\mathcal{S}=

\displaystyle\,\int\,d^{D}x\,\sqrt{-g}\Big([G]_{\mu\nu}\delta g^{\mu\nu}+[A]^{\mu}\delta A_{\mu}\Big)

(39)

and explicitly given, in the Einstein-Hilbert-Maxwell two-derivative case, by

\displaystyle[A]^{\mu}:=

\displaystyle\,\nabla_{\nu}F^{\nu\mu}=0\,,

(40a)

\displaystyle[G]_{\mu\nu}:=

\displaystyle\,{\rm R}_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{4}g_{\mu\nu}{\rm R}-\frac{1}{2}F_{\mu\rho}F_{\nu}{}^{\rho}+\frac{1}{8}g_{\mu\nu}F^{2}=0\,.

(40b)

Let us have a look at the expansion of
[P]
. We have

\displaystyle[P]=

\displaystyle\,{\color[rgb]{1,0.3711,0.1211}c^{2}}\accentset{(2)}{[P]}+\accentset{(0)}{[P]}+{\color[rgb]{1,0.3711,0.1211}c^{-2}}\accentset{(-2)}{[P]}+\mathcal{O}({\color[rgb]{1,0.3711,0.1211}c^{-2}})\,,

(41)

with

\displaystyle\accentset{(2)}{[P]}=

\displaystyle\,\frac{1}{4}(\texttt{C}-2\texttt{B})t_{ab}t^{ab}\,,

(42a)

\displaystyle\accentset{(0)}{[P]}=

\displaystyle\,\frac{1}{2}(3\texttt{A}-D\texttt{A}+\texttt{C})\tilde{\rm R}_{a}{}^{a}+(2\texttt{A}-D\texttt{A}+\texttt{B})\tilde{\nabla}(t)_{a0}{}^{a}+

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}(-5\texttt{A}-\texttt{C}+3D\texttt{A})t_{0a}t_{0}{}^{a}+\frac{1}{2}(-3\texttt{A}-2\texttt{B}+D\texttt{A})t^{ab}f_{ab}\,,

(42b)

\displaystyle\accentset{(-2)}{[P]}=

\displaystyle\,\frac{1}{2}(\texttt{A}-D\texttt{A}+2\texttt{B}-\texttt{C})\tilde{\nabla}(f)_{a0}{}^{a}+\frac{1}{2}(D\texttt{A}-\texttt{A}+\texttt{C})\tilde{\rm R}_{00}+\frac{1}{2}(3\texttt{A}+\texttt{C}-D\texttt{A})\tilde{\nabla}(z)_{00a}{}^{a}+

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}(3\texttt{A}-D\texttt{A}+\texttt{C})\bigg(z_{0a}{}^{a}z_{0b}{}^{b}+z_{0}{}^{ab}z_{0[ab]}-\frac{1}{2}z_{0ab}f^{ab}-z^{ab}{}_{a}f_{0b}\bigg)+

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}(D\texttt{A}-3\texttt{A}-2\texttt{B})f^{ab}f_{ab}+At_{0}{}^{a}f_{0a}\,.

(42c)

The Poisson equation occurs at order
{\color[rgb]{1,0.3711,0.1211}c^{-2}}
since the two-derivative term acting on the Newton potential, identifiable with
\tau^{\mu}a_{\mu}
,

\displaystyle h^{\nu\rho}\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}(\tau^{\mu}a_{\rho})

(43)

is contained in the term
\tilde{\nabla}(f)_{a0}{}^{a}
and in
\tilde{\rm R}_{00}
.
This means that to obtain the Poisson equation from the non-relativistic limit of the combination [P], we should have the vanishing of the highest orders
{\color[rgb]{1,0.3711,0.1211}c^{2}}
and
{\color[rgb]{1,0.3711,0.1211}c^{0}}
. While for the term at order
{\color[rgb]{1,0.3711,0.1211}c^{2}}
this is possible just setting
\texttt{C}=2\texttt{B}
, for the term at order
{\color[rgb]{1,0.3711,0.1211}c^{0}}
a full cancellation cannot be achieved for any non-trivial choice of
A
and
B
. This depends on the fact that, in the present case, we do not have an emergent dilatation symmetry. The presence of that symmetry would have helped us in achieving our goal as described in
[
57
]
. Nevertheless, the absence of such a symmetry does not completely preclude obtaining the Poisson equation via the limit; the problem, indeed, could be circumvented by choosing opportunely
\texttt{A},\texttt{B}
and
C
and imposing concomitantly a suitable constraint. One possibility is to impose
[
9
]

\displaystyle F_{\mu\nu}=

\displaystyle\,0\,.

(44)

This constraint is fully invariant under the set of relativistic transformations, implying that it does not require further constraints. By plugging in the limit ansatze
(2)
, it can also be rewritten as:

\displaystyle t_{\mu\nu}=

\displaystyle\,-\frac{1}{{\color[rgb]{1,0.3711,0.1211}c^{2}}}f_{\mu\nu}.

(45)

Using these relations before taking the limit of
(42)
allows us to reduce the power of the terms proportional to the intrinsic torsion,
t_{\mu\nu}
. Thus the order
{\color[rgb]{1,0.3711,0.1211}c^{2}}
gets shifted to order
{\color[rgb]{1,0.3711,0.1211}c^{-2}}
. The same applies to three out of the four terms appearing in
\accentset{(0)}{[P]}
. The fourth term can be canceled by setting
\texttt{C}=(D-3)\texttt{A}
and then the leading power in the expansion will be the following
{\color[rgb]{1,0.3711,0.1211}c^{-2}}
term:

\displaystyle\accentset{(-2)}{[P]}=

\displaystyle\,(D-2)\texttt{A}\tilde{\rm R}_{00}=(D-2)\texttt{A}{\rm R(G)}_{0a}{}^{a}\,,

(46)

where the different contributions appearing and disappearing with respect to
(42c)
are due to those coming from
(42b)
and
(42a)
via
(45)
. Note that having used
(45)
in
(42)
the torsion term in the connection is no longer present (with a slight abuse of notation, we have used the same symbols).
1
1

1

Before taking the non-relativistic limit and without using the constraint
(44)
it would have been:

\tilde{\rm R}_{00}={\rm R(G)}_{0a}{}^{a}-\tau^{\mu}\omega_{\mu}{}^{a}{\rm R(H)}_{0a}
.
In the limit, the constraint
(45)
becomes the zero-torsion condition

\displaystyle t_{\mu\nu}=0\,.

(47)

It is not surprising that eventually
B
does not appear since the constraint
F_{\mu\nu}=0
implies
[A]^{\mu}=0
automatically, thus dropping the corresponding term in
(38)
.

To obtain the corrections to the Poisson equation we should replicate for the higher-order theories what we have done in this section. In doing so there are some relevant aspects to mention:

•

since the two-derivative contribution is unmodified in the four-derivative theories, the Poisson equation will still occur at order
{\color[rgb]{1,0.3711,0.1211}c^{-2}}
in the expansion of
(38)
.

•

The constraint
(45)
should still be imposed to guarantee the two-derivative part to work.

This means that, if the higher-derivative terms produce higher-order terms in the expansion
(42)
, such as order
{\color[rgb]{1,0.3711,0.1211}c^{4}}
or higher, we should make them vanish with a combination of constraints and fine-tuning of
\texttt{A},\texttt{B}
and
C
, to make order
{\color[rgb]{1,0.3711,0.1211}c^{-2}}
the leading one. The goal of the next subsection is to inspect the mechanism to get the Poisson equation with higher-derivative corrections, performing the non-relativistic limit, and disclose it in some particular cases. We start by considering the Gauss-Bonnet gravity.

3.2
Einstein-Gauss-Bonnet Gravity

In between the two-derivative case and the four-derivative theory, there exists one particular case deserving a specific analysis, this is Einstein-Gauss-Bonnet gravity. This is the theory described by the following four-derivatives action:

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{GB}=

\displaystyle\,\int d^{D}x\,\sqrt{-g}\Big[{\rm R}+\alpha({\rm R}^{2}-4{\rm R}_{\mu\nu}{\rm R}^{\mu\nu}+{\rm R}_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}{\rm R}^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma})\Big]\,.

(48)

In four dimensions, the quadratic term is a topological invariant. Notice that, despite having an action that is quartic in the derivatives of the metric, the equations of motion are second order. For this reason this could be the perfect bridge between the two-derivative and four-derivative theories. Following
section 2
, to ensure the finiteness of the limit at the level of the action, the Lagrangian in
(48)
should be supplemented with the Maxwell terms. This leads to:

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{GB}=

\displaystyle\,\int d^{D}x\,\sqrt{-g}\Big[\mathcal{L}_{EHM}+\alpha(\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}-4\mathcal{L}_{\beta}+\mathcal{L}_{\gamma})\Big]\,.

(49)

For this action the equations of motion are

\displaystyle[A]^{\nu}:=

\displaystyle\,\nabla_{\mu}\bigg[F^{\mu\nu}-\frac{3}{2}\alpha F^{2}F^{\mu\nu}+3\alpha F^{\mu\alpha}F_{\alpha\beta}F^{\nu\beta}\bigg]+

\displaystyle+8\alpha{\rm R}^{\rho[\mu}\nabla_{\mu}F^{\nu]}{}_{\rho}+\alpha{\rm R}^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\nabla_{\mu}F_{\rho\sigma}+2\alpha\nabla_{\mu}F^{\mu\nu}{\rm R}\,,

(50a)

\displaystyle[G]_{\alpha\beta}:=

\displaystyle\,{\rm R}_{\alpha\beta}-\frac{1}{2}g_{\alpha\beta}{\rm R}-\frac{1}{2}F_{\alpha\mu}F_{\beta}{}^{\mu}+\frac{1}{8}g_{\alpha\beta}F^{2}+

\displaystyle+\alpha\bigg[-\frac{3}{2}F_{\alpha}{}^{\mu}F_{\mu}{}^{\nu}F_{\nu}{}^{\rho}F_{\rho\beta}+\frac{3}{4}F_{\alpha}{}^{\mu}F_{\beta\mu}F^{2}+\frac{3}{16}g_{\alpha\beta}F_{\sigma}{}^{\mu}F_{\mu}{}^{\nu}F_{\nu}{}^{\rho}F_{\rho}{}^{\sigma}-\frac{3}{32}g_{\alpha\beta}F^{4}+

\displaystyle+\frac{3}{4}g_{\alpha\beta}F^{\mu\nu}F^{\rho\sigma}{\rm R}_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}+2F_{\mu}{}^{\nu}F^{\mu\rho}{\rm R}_{\alpha\rho\beta\nu}-\frac{3}{2}F^{\nu\rho}(F_{\beta}{}^{\mu}{\rm R}_{\alpha\mu\nu\rho}+F_{\alpha}{}^{\mu}{\rm R}_{\beta\mu\nu\rho})+

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}{\rm R}_{\alpha\beta}F^{2}-2F_{\beta}{}^{\mu}F_{\mu}{}^{\nu}{\rm R}_{\alpha\nu}-2F_{\alpha}{}^{\mu}F_{\mu}{}^{\nu}{\rm R}_{\beta\nu}+3F_{\alpha}{}^{\mu}F_{\beta}{}^{\nu}{\rm R}_{\mu\nu}+

\displaystyle-2g_{\alpha\beta}F_{\mu}{}^{\nu}F^{\mu\rho}{\rm R}_{\nu\rho}-F_{\alpha\mu}F_{\beta}{}^{\mu}{\rm R}+\frac{1}{4}g_{\alpha\beta}F^{2}{\rm R}+

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{\alpha}F_{\mu\nu}\nabla_{\beta}F^{\mu\nu}-\nabla_{\mu}F_{\alpha}{}^{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}F_{\beta}{}^{\nu}+\nabla_{\alpha}F_{\beta\mu}\nabla_{\nu}F^{\mu\nu}+\nabla_{\beta}F_{\alpha\mu}\nabla_{\nu}F^{\mu\nu}+\nabla_{\mu}F_{\alpha\nu}\nabla^{\mu}F_{\beta}{}^{\nu}+

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}g_{\alpha\beta}\nabla_{\mu}F_{\nu\rho}\nabla^{\mu}F^{\nu\rho}-g_{\alpha\beta}\nabla_{\mu}F^{\mu\nu}\nabla^{\rho}F_{\nu\rho}+

\displaystyle-4{\rm R}_{\alpha}{}^{\mu}{\rm R}_{\beta\mu}+2g_{\alpha\beta}{\rm R}_{\mu\nu}{\rm R}^{\mu\nu}+2{\rm R}_{\alpha\beta}{\rm R}-\frac{1}{2}g_{\alpha\beta}{\rm R}^{2}+

\displaystyle-4{\rm R}^{\mu\nu}{\rm R}_{\alpha\mu\beta\nu}+2{\rm R}_{\alpha}{}^{\mu\nu\rho}{\rm R}_{\beta\mu\nu\rho}-\frac{1}{2}g_{\alpha\beta}{\rm R}_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}{\rm R}^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\bigg]\,,

(50b)

where they have been rewritten in the best way to study their expansion using the relations listed in
appendix B
. Then for the Poisson combination
(38)
we have:

\displaystyle[P]=

\displaystyle\,{\color[rgb]{1,0.3711,0.1211}c^{2}}\accentset{(2)}{[P]}+\accentset{(0)}{[P]}+{\color[rgb]{1,0.3711,0.1211}c^{-2}}\accentset{(-2)}{[P]}+\mathcal{O}({\color[rgb]{1,0.3711,0.1211}c^{-2}})\,,

(51)

with

\displaystyle\accentset{(2)}{[P]}=

\displaystyle\,(\texttt{C}-2\texttt{B})\alpha\bigg[\frac{1}{4}t_{ab}t^{ab}\bigg(\alpha^{-1}+2\tilde{\rm R}_{c}{}^{c}+\frac{1}{3}t_{0}{}^{c}t_{0c}-2t^{cd}f_{cd}\bigg)+

\displaystyle+t^{ab}\bigg(-t_{0a}\tilde{\nabla}(t)_{cb}{}^{c}+\frac{2}{3}t_{0}{}^{c}\tilde{\nabla}(t)_{(ac)b}-t_{a}{}^{c}\tilde{\rm R}_{bc}-\frac{1}{6}t_{a}{}^{c}t_{0b}t_{0c}+t_{a}{}^{c}t_{b}{}^{d}f_{cd}\bigg)+

\displaystyle+\tilde{\nabla}(t)_{a}{}^{ab}\tilde{\nabla}(t)_{cb}{}^{c}+\frac{2}{3}\tilde{\nabla}(t)^{abc}\tilde{\nabla}(t)_{(ab)c}\bigg]\,,

(52a)

\displaystyle\accentset{(0)}{[P]}=

\displaystyle\,\frac{1}{2}(3\texttt{A}+\texttt{C}-D\texttt{A})\tilde{\rm R}_{a}{}^{a}+\frac{1}{2}(5\texttt{A}+\texttt{C}-D\texttt{A})\alpha\Big[(\tilde{\rm R}_{a}{}^{a})^{2}-4\tilde{\rm R}^{ab}\tilde{\rm R}_{ab}+\tilde{\rm R}^{abcd}\tilde{\rm R}_{abcd}\Big]+...\,,

(52b)

where dots denote terms proportional to the intrinsic torsion. We realize that at order
{\color[rgb]{1,0.3711,0.1211}c^{2}}
we have all terms at least quadratic in the intrinsic torsion. This implies that the constraint
(44)

is able to shift completely the term appearing at order
{\color[rgb]{1,0.3711,0.1211}c^{2}}
to order
{\color[rgb]{1,0.3711,0.1211}c^{-2}}
or lower. For the term appearing at order
{\color[rgb]{1,0.3711,0.1211}c^{0}}
in
(52)
all the terms proportional to the intrinsic torsion, not explicitly shown, are shifted to order
{\color[rgb]{1,0.3711,0.1211}c^{-2}}
or lower by the constraint. Therefore we are left with the terms appearing explicitly in
(52b)
. Those terms have two different coefficients; thus, the fine-tuning of
C
defined in
section 3.1
is not sufficient to cancel both at the same time. This prevents us from getting the Poisson equation from the limit. To overcome this issue, we could impose a further constraint, but we prefer to propose the following trick.

3.2.1
Scalar Field Trick

Given a relativistic Lagrangian
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{1}+\mathcal{L}_{2}
, we would like to extract the Poisson equation by taking the non-relativistic limit of its equations of motion. We notice that it, in certain cases, it is helpful to introduce a scalar field
\Phi
and define a new Lagrangian, as

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\text{new}}=\mathcal{L}_{1}+{\rm e}^{{\color[rgb]{0,0.9,0}\texttt{a}}\Phi}\Big[\mathcal{L}_{2}+{\color[rgb]{0,0.9,0}\texttt{b}}\partial_{\mu}\Phi\partial^{\mu}\Phi\Big]

(53)

with
a
and
b
two constants. Then the field equations become:

\displaystyle[\Phi]^{\text{new}}:=

\displaystyle\,{\color[rgb]{0,0.9,0}\texttt{a}}\mathcal{L}_{2}+\partial_{\mu}\Phi(...)^{\mu}\,,

(54a)

\displaystyle[G]^{\text{new}}_{\mu\nu}:=

\displaystyle\,[G]^{(1)}_{\mu\nu}+{\rm e}^{{\color[rgb]{0,0.9,0}\texttt{a}}\Phi}[G]^{(2)}_{\mu\nu}+\partial_{\mu}\Phi(...)^{\mu}\,,

(54b)

\displaystyle[A]^{\text{new}}_{\mu}:=

\displaystyle\,[A]^{(1)}_{\mu}+{\rm e}^{{\color[rgb]{0,0.9,0}\texttt{a}}\Phi}[A]^{(2)}_{\mu}+\partial_{\mu}\Phi(...)^{\mu}\,,

(54c)

where the superscripts
(1)
and
(2)
denote respectively the contributions to the field equation coming from
\mathcal{L}_{1}
and
\mathcal{L}_{2}
and dots denote irrelevant contribution for our treatment. Now consider the trivial ansatz for the scalar field:

\displaystyle\Phi=

\displaystyle\,\phi\,.

(55)

Imposing the constraint

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}\Phi=

\displaystyle\,0\,,

Conclusion

The present research marks a step forward in understanding higher-derivative corrections to Newton-Cartan gravity. In
section 1
we introduced the four-derivative theories whose non-relativistic limit has been investigated in this work. In
section 2
we studied how to take the non-relativistic limit of quadratic gravity theories, at the level of the action, by introducing a 1-form gauge field and coupling it to the gravity sector. This is a generalization of the procedure used to obtain Newton-Cartan gravity from General Relativity via a limit
[
9
]
. We demonstrated that, to avoid divergences in the action and get the magnetic limit, it is sufficient to use a single vector gauge field. The non-relativistic actions have been derived and rewritten in terms of geometric quantities. We provided a general recipe for extending the procedure to higher-order gravity theories. We also show that higher-order Maxwell terms and their couplings to the gravity sector, used to cancel the divergences in the action, can be obtained from a purely gravitational higher-dimensional theory via Kaluza-Klein reduction.
section 3
is devoted to the study of the non-relativistic limit of the equations of motion, with special focus on the Poisson equation. We first review the two-derivative case and identify the main obstacles in obtaining the Poisson equations with higher-derivative corrections. We then considered two specific cases, the Einstein-Gauss-Bonnet theory in
section 3.2
and the quadratic Ricci scalar theory in
section 3.3
. In the first case, we derive the higher-order corrected Poisson equation by modifying the initial model with a scalar field. In the second case, to derive the non-relativistic Poisson equation, we supplement the relativistic system with a further on-shell constraint. We also derived, in both cases, the full set of non-relativistic equations of motion. The results define zero-torsion Gauss-Bonnet Newton-Cartan and quadratic Ricci scalar Newton-Cartan gravity theories.

Higher-derivative theories can be affected by Ostrogradsky’s Instability Theorem. This can undermine their physical robustness, especially in the context of quantum field theory, due to the appearance of negative energy states. Though they are also often renormalisable, making them appealing in quantum gravity. Therefore, it would be particularly interesting to study in detail the Gauss-Bonnet and quadratic Ricci Newton-Cartan gravity theories and search for their spectrum of solutions.

The present study lays a foundation for a wide array of future investigations, among which we mention:

•

extending the limit to different foliations, by coupling higher-order theories to higher-rank differential forms. This is particularly interesting in the context of string theory and supergravity
[
58
,

•

Investigating whether coupling the scalar field to the gravity sector can be used to obtain the Poisson equation without using constraints. Closely related is the emergence of a local dilatation symmetry.

•

•

Beyond the formal analogy, useful to organize the calculations, these non-relativistic tensors do not transform covariantly under Galilean boosts. The proper definition of geometric non-relativistic curvatures is discussed in
appendix C
.

Appendix C
On Newton-Cartan Geometry

In this section we introduce the basics of the Newton-Cartan geometry to rewrite our results in terms of covariant quantities. We have defined the non-relativistic connection
\tilde{\Gamma}_{\mu\nu}^{\rho}
in
(12)
such that the non-relativistic analogue of metric compatibility relations,
\tilde{\nabla}_{\mu}\tau_{\nu}=0=\tilde{\nabla}_{\mu}h^{\nu\rho}
, are satisfied. We can now introduce a boost and transverse rotations spin connections,
\omega_{\mu}{}^{a}
and
\omega_{\mu}{}^{ab}
respectively, following
[
4
]
, by considering the analogue of the relativistic vielbein postulate:

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}e_{\nu}{}^{a}-\omega_{\mu}{}^{a}{}_{b}e_{\nu}{}^{b}-\omega_{\mu}{}^{a}\tau_{\nu}-\tilde{\Gamma}_{\mu\nu}^{\rho}e_{\rho}{}^{a}=

\displaystyle\,0\,.

(98)

Solving for the two spin connections we get

\displaystyle\omega_{\mu}{}^{a}=

\displaystyle\,\tau^{\nu}(\partial_{\mu}e_{\nu}{}^{a}-\tilde{\Gamma}_{\mu\nu}^{\rho}e_{\rho}{}^{a})\,,

(99a)

\displaystyle\omega_{\mu}{}^{ab}=

\displaystyle\,e^{\nu b}(\partial_{\mu}e_{\nu}{}^{a}-\tilde{\Gamma}_{\mu\nu}^{\rho}e_{\rho}{}^{a})\,.

(99b)

Note that the transverse rotations connection is antisymmetric in the flat indices, although in the expression above it is not manifest. The connections just defined transform as follows under
U(1)
gauge symmetry, Galilean boost and transverse rotations:

\displaystyle\delta\tilde{\Gamma}_{\mu\nu}^{\rho}=

\displaystyle\,\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{a}h^{\rho\sigma}(e_{\nu}{}^{a}t_{\mu\sigma}+e_{\mu}{}^{a}t_{\nu\sigma}-e_{\sigma}{}^{a}t_{\mu\nu})\,,

(100a)

\displaystyle\delta\omega_{\mu}{}^{a}=

\displaystyle\,\partial_{\mu}\lambda^{a}-\lambda_{b}\omega_{\mu}{}^{ab}+\lambda^{a}{}_{b}\omega_{\mu}{}^{b}-\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{b}e_{\mu}{}^{b}t_{0}{}^{a}+\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{a}t_{\mu 0}\,,

(100b)

\displaystyle\delta\omega_{\mu}{}^{ab}=

\displaystyle\,\partial_{\mu}\lambda^{ab}+2\lambda^{[b}{}_{c}\omega_{\mu}{}^{a]c}+\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{c}e_{\mu}{}^{c}t^{ab}+t_{\mu[b}\lambda_{a]}\,.

(100c)

We can define the following covariant curvatures:

\displaystyle{\rm R(H)}_{\mu\nu}=

\displaystyle\,t_{\mu\nu}\,,

(101a)

\displaystyle{\rm R(P)}_{\mu\nu}{}^{a}=

\displaystyle\,z_{\mu\nu}{}^{a}-2\omega_{[\mu}{}^{ab}e_{\nu]b}-2\omega_{[\mu}{}^{a}\tau_{\nu]}\,,

(101b)

\displaystyle{\rm R(M)}_{\mu\nu}=

\displaystyle\,f_{\mu\nu}+2\omega_{[\mu}{}^{a}e_{\nu]a}\,,

(101c)

\displaystyle{\rm R(G)}_{\mu\nu}{}^{a}=

\displaystyle\,2\partial_{[\mu}\omega_{\nu]}{}^{a}-2\omega_{[\mu}{}^{ab}\omega_{\nu]b}-\omega_{[\nu}{}^{b}e_{\mu]b}t_{0}{}^{a}+\omega_{[\nu}{}^{a}t_{\mu]0}\,,

(101d)

\displaystyle{\rm R(J)}_{\mu\nu}{}^{ab}=

\displaystyle\,2\partial_{[\mu}\omega_{\nu]}{}^{ab}+2\omega_{[\mu}{}^{ac}\omega_{\nu]}{}^{b}{}_{c}-2\omega_{[\mu}{}^{[a}t_{\nu]}{}^{b]}-\omega_{[\mu}{}^{c}e_{\nu]c}t^{ab}\,.

(101e)

{\rm R(M)}_{\mu\nu}
and
{\rm R(P)}_{\mu\nu}{}^{a}
vanish once the expression of the spin connection is plugged into their definitions.

\displaystyle\,0\,,

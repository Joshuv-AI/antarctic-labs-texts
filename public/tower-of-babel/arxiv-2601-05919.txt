Unlikely intersections with CM abelian varieties in a family and explicit bounds for canonical heights under endomorphisms
Luca Ferrigno

Abstract
. Let S S be a smooth irreducible curve over ℚ ¯ \overline{\mathbb{Q}} , and let 𝒜 → S \mathcal{A}\to S be an abelian scheme with a curve 𝒞 ⊂ 𝒜 \mathcal{C}\subset\mathcal{A} , both defined over ℚ ¯ \overline{\mathbb{Q}} . In 2020, Barroero and Capuano proved that if 𝒞 \mathcal{C} is not contained in a proper subgroup scheme, then the intersection of 𝒞 \mathcal{C} with the union of the flat subgroup schemes of 𝒜 \mathcal{A} of codimension at least 2 is finite. In this article, we continue to study this problem by considering the intersections with the algebraic subgroups of the CM fibers, generalizing a previous result of Barroero for fibered powers of elliptic schemes. 1.
Introduction

Let
S
be a smooth, irreducible, quasi-projective curve, and let
\pi:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow S
be an abelian scheme of relative dimension
g\geq 1
, both defined over a number field
k
. For any (not necessarily closed) point
s\in S
we denote the fiber of
\mathcal{A}
over
s
by
\mathcal{A}_{s}
. Thus, if
s\in S(\mathbb{C})
, then
\mathcal{A}_{s}
is an abelian variety of dimension
g
defined over
k(s)
.
Let
O:S\rightarrow\mathcal{A}
be the zero section of
\mathcal{A}
and consider an irreducible curve
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{A}
, also defined over
k
.

Recall that an irreducible component of a subgroup scheme of
\mathcal{A}
is either a component of an algebraic subgroup of a fiber or it dominates the base curve
S
. We say that a subgroup scheme is
flat
if all of its irreducible components are of the latter kind.

We call
\mathcal{A}\rightarrow S

isotrivial
if it becomes constant after a base change, i.e.
\mathcal{A}\times_{S}S^{\prime}\cong A\times_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}S^{\prime}
for some finite base change
S^{\prime}\to S
and some fixed abelian variety
A/\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
. Let
A_{0}\times S
be the largest constant abelian subscheme of
\mathcal{A}\rightarrow S
, we say that a section
\sigma:S\rightarrow\mathcal{A}
is constant if there exists
a_{0}\in A_{0}(\mathbb{C})
such that
\sigma
is the composition of
S\rightarrow A_{0}\times S
,
s\mapsto(a_{0},s)
with the inclusion of
A_{0}\times S
into
\mathcal{A}
.

We are interested in understanding how the curve
\mathcal{C}
intersects the subgroup schemes of the abelian scheme
\mathcal{A}
.
In
[
4
]
, Barroero and Capuano studied the intersections of
\mathcal{C}
with flat subgroup schemes of codimension at least 2 and proved that, if
\mathcal{C}
is not contained in a proper subgroup scheme, then its intersection with the union of all such flat subgroup schemes is finite. As a matter of fact, given a flat subgroup scheme
H
of
\mathcal{A}
of codimension at least 2, one expects, for dimensional reasons, that
\mathcal{C}\cap H
should in fact be empty. While this may not be true in general, their result confirms the prediction, arising from the Zilber–Pink conjecture, that the union of all these intersections is finite.

In general, the Zilber–Pink conjecture, formulated independently and in various settings by Bombieri, Masser and Zannier
[
9
]
, by Zilber
[
67
]
and by Pink
[
53
]
, predicts that “unlikely intersections” between a fixed algebraic subvariety
V
of a semiabelian or Shimura variety
X
and “special” subvarieties of
X
of codimension at least
\dim V+1
should be scarce, i.e. there should be only finitely many maximal such intersections. In particular, one expects that if
V
is not itself contained in any proper special subvariety, then its intersection with the union of all special subvarieties of codimension at least
\dim V+1
is not Zariski-dense. For comprehensive treatments of the conjecture and of problems of unlikely intersections, see
[
66
]
and
[
52
]
, as well as the survey
[
11
]
.

In the isotrivial case or if
\mathcal{C}
is contained in a fixed fiber, this has already been addressed by Habegger and Pila
[
24
, Theorem 9.14]
, who proved the Zilber–Pink conjecture for curves in abelian varieties defined over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
. Thus, our focus is instead on the case where the abelian scheme
\mathcal{A}\rightarrow S
is not isotrivial and
\mathcal{C}
is not contained in a fixed fiber.

In this paper, we extend these results by considering the intersections of
\mathcal{C}
with the proper algebraic subgroups of the CM fibers of
\mathcal{A}
, which, like the flat subgroup schemes considered above, are special subvarieties of
\mathcal{A}
of codimension at least
2
. Motivated by the same dimensional considerations and by the Zilber–Pink conjecture, we prove the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
S
and
\mathcal{A}\rightarrow S
be as above and assume that
\mathcal{A}
is not isotrivial. Let
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{A}
an irreducible curve defined over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
that is neither contained in a fixed fiber nor in a translate of a proper flat subgroup scheme of
\mathcal{A}
by a constant section, even after a finite base change. Then, the intersection of
\mathcal{C}
with the union of all proper algebraic subgroups of the CM fibers of
\mathcal{A}
is a finite set.

Since every algebraic subgroup of an abelian variety is a union of irreducible components of the kernel of an endomorphism, the theorem can be restated as follows: under the same assumptions as above, there are at most finitely many
P\in\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{C})
such that
\mathcal{A}_{\pi(P)}
has complex multiplication and there exists a non-zero
f\in\en(\mathcal{A}_{\pi(P)})
such that
f(P)=O_{\pi(P)}
.

In
[
6
]
, Barroero proved the same result in the case of a fibered power of an elliptic scheme. Thus, Theorem
1.1
can be viewed as a generalization of Barroero’s result to more general abelian schemes.

The above theorem also proves a stronger partial version of Conjecture 6.1 of
[
53
]
, since Pink’s conjecture only considers algebraic subgroups of codimension at least 2 of the fibers. As a matter of fact, Theorem
1.1
is a particular case of the Zilber–Pink conjecture for a curve in an abelian scheme, which is known to imply Conjecture 6.1 of
[
53
]
for abelian schemes.

To the best of our knowledge, the Zilber–Pink conjecture for a curve in a non-isotrivial abelian scheme has been settled only for a curve in a fibered power of an elliptic scheme, by work of Barroero and Capuano
[
2
,
6
]
and Barroero and Dill
[
5
]
, building on previous works by Masser and Zannier
[
38
,
39
]
. There is also some partial progress for a curve in a product of fibered powers of elliptic schemes (under certain additional assumptions on the curve) by Masser and Zannier
[
40
]
, Barroero and Capuano
[
3
]
and by previous work of the author
[
17
]
. A variation of the conjecture involving tangential intersections has also been studied by Corvaja, Demeio, Masser and Zannier
[
12
]
, by Ulmer and Urzúa
[
61
,
62
]
, and by Ottolini
[
48
]
.

Remark 1.2
.

Before proceeding, we note that if
S\subseteq\mathbb{A}_{g}
is not a special curve (as explained in Section
3
, we may always assume
S\subseteq\mathbb{A}_{g}
), then the André-Oort conjecture for
\mathbb{A}_{g}
(proved by Tsimerman
[
60
]
) guarantees that only finitely many points
s\in S(\mathbb{C})
correspond to CM fibers
\mathcal{A}_{s}

, which in turn implies Theorem
1.1
. Hence, one may assume that
S=\pi(\mathcal{C})
is a Shimura curve, though this assumption will not be used in the rest of the paper.

Remark 1.3
.

Observe that the Zilber–Pink conjecture would imply Theorem
1.1
even when
\mathcal{C}
is contained in a translate of a proper flat subgroup scheme of
\mathcal{A}
by a non-torsion section. Unfortunately, the functional transcendence results used in this article only allow us to prove the theorem in the form stated above.

Our proof of Theorem
1.1
follows the well-established Pila-Zannier strategy, first introduced in
[
51
]
and later used, among others, by Masser and Zannier
[
38
,
39
]
, by Barroero and Capuano
[
2
,
6
,
3
,
4
]
and in previous work of the author
[
17
]
.

To implement this strategy, we first reduce the problem to the case of restrictions of the universal family of abelian varieties over a quasi-projective curve in the moduli space
\mathbb{A}_{g}
of principally polarized abelian varieties of dimension
g
. Using a result of Peterzil and Starchenko, after restricting to a suitable fundamental domain, the uniformizing map of the universal family is definable in the o-minimal structure
\mathbb{R}_{\text{an, exp}}
. Consequently, the preimage of
\mathcal{C}
under this map is a definable surface
X
.

Let
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
be the subset of
\mathcal{C}
we want to prove to be finite. Then, each point
P_{0}\in\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
correspond to a point on
X
lying on a subvariety defined by equations with integer coefficients. We then use a result by Habegger and Pila, which states that the number of points on
X
lying on such subvarieties with coefficients bounded in absolute value by
T
is at most
\ll T^{\varepsilon}
, provided that the abelian logarithm of the generic point of
\mathcal{C}
generates a field of sufficiently large transcendence degree over the field generated by the period matrix.

We then use a result by Barroero and Capuano, based on an earlier result by Masser
[
42
]
, to construct a linear combination of a specific basis of endomorphisms of
\mathcal{A}_{\pi(P_{0})}
, with coefficients bounded by a constant times a positive power of
\left[\mathbb{Q}(P_{0}):\mathbb{Q}\right]
and such that
P_{0}
lies in the kernel of this linear combination. Furthermore, since all Galois conjugates of
P_{0}
remain in
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
, there are at least
D_{0}
points on
X
lying on subvarieties whose coefficients are bounded in absolute value by some positive power of
D_{0}
. Together with the previous estimate, this implies that
D_{0}
is uniformly bounded. By Northcott’s theorem, this establishes the claim of the theorem.

In particular, when constructing the endomorphism whose kernel contains
P_{0}
, an essential ingredient is an explicit control on the behaviour of canonical heights under endomorphisms. To state the general result we shall use, which has independent interest, we briefly recall the setting for canonical heights on abelian varieties.

Let
A
be an abelian variety of dimension
g
defined over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
and
D
be a symmetric divisor on
A
, i.e. a divisor such that
[-1]^{*}D\sim D
. Then,
D
induces a canonical (or Néron-Tate) height
\widehat{h}_{A,D}
on
A(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
(for details see Section B.5 of
[
27
]
).

Since
D
is symmetric, we have the classical identity

\widehat{h}_{A,D}([n]P)=n^{2}\cdot\widehat{h}_{A,D}(P)

for any
P\in A(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
. For the proof of Theorem
1.1
we need to generalize this identity to arbitrary endomorphisms of
A
.

In general, if
D
is ample and symmetric, one can show (see Section
7
) that there exist constants
0\leq\gamma_{1}\leq\gamma_{2}
such that

\gamma_{1}\cdot\widehat{h}_{A,D}(P)\leq\widehat{h}_{A,D}(f(P))\leq\gamma_{2}\cdot\widehat{h}_{A,D}(P).

In particular, one necessarily has
\gamma_{1}=0
if
f
is not an isogeny, whereas
\gamma_{1}
may be chosen strictly positive when
f
is an isogeny.

The main result of Section
7
is the following theorem, which gives explicit values for
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
in terms of the eigenvalues of the analytic representation of
f^{\dagger}f
, where
†
is the Rosati involution defined by the polarization associated to
D
. Define

\alpha^{-}_{D}(f)=\min\left\{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{g}\right\}\quad\text{ and }\quad\alpha^{+}_{D}(f)=\max\left\{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{g}\right\},

where
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{g}
are the eigenvalues (counted with multiplicities) of the analytic representation of
f^{\dagger}f
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
A
be an abelian variety defined over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
, and let
D
be an ample symmetric divisor on
A
. Then, for every endomorphism
f:A\rightarrow A
, we have

\alpha^{-}_{D}(f)\cdot\widehat{h}_{A,D}(P)\leq\widehat{h}_{A,D}(f(P))\leq\alpha^{+}_{D}(f)\cdot\widehat{h}_{A,D}(P)

for every
P\in A(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
. Moreover, these constants are the best possible, meaning that we cannot replace
\alpha^{+}_{D}(f)
and
\alpha^{-}_{D}(f)
with a smaller and a larger constant, respectively.

Notation

In this article we will use Vinogradov’s
\ll
notation: for real-valued functions
f(T)
and
g(T)
, we write
f(T)\ll g(T)
if there exists a constant
\gamma>0
such that
f(T)\leq\gamma g(T)
for all sufficiently large
T
. When not explicitly stated, the implied constant is either absolute or depends only on
S,\mathcal{A},g,\mathcal{C}
and other fixed data. We use subscripts to indicate any additional dependence of the implied constant.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Abelian varieties and their endomorphisms

In this section, we collect the foundational definitions and results concerning complex abelian varieties that will serve as a basis for the rest of the paper. It is not intended as a comprehensive treatment of abelian varieties, for which we refer the reader to
[
7
,
43
,
46
]
.

From now on every abelian variety will be defined over
\mathbb{C}
, and we will identify them with their set of complex points.

It is well-known that if
A
is an abelian variety of dimension
g
defined over
\mathbb{C}
, then
A(\mathbb{C})
is a complex torus, i.e.
A(\mathbb{C})\cong V/\Lambda
for some
g
-dimensional
\mathbb{C}
-vector space
V
and some lattice
\Lambda\subseteq V
. After fixing bases of
V
and
\Lambda
, we have that
\Lambda=\Pi\mathbb{Z}^{2g}
, for some matrix
\Pi\in\mat_{g\times 2g}(\mathbb{C})
called
period matrix
.

Let
A,B
be two abelian varieties. A
homomorphism
is a morphism
f:A\rightarrow B
of group varieties (in other words, it is a morphism of algebraic varieties which is also a group homomorphism). When
B=A
such a map is called an
endomorphism
. A homomorphism
f:A\rightarrow B
is called an
isogeny
if it is surjective and it has finite kernel.

We denote by
\mathrm{Hom}(A,B)
the set of homomorphisms from
A
to
B
and we define
\en(A):=\mathrm{Hom}(A,A)
to be the set of all endomorphisms. Moreover, we define

\mathrm{Hom}^{0}(A,B):=\mathrm{Hom}(A,B)\otimes\mathbb{Q}\qquad\en^{0}(A):=\en(A)\otimes\mathbb{Q}.

Note that
\mathrm{Hom}(A,B)
is an abelian group under point-wise addition and, similarly,
\en(A)
is a ring where the multiplication is given by composition of maps. We will always assume that all the morphisms are defined over an algebraic closure of the ground field.

Given an endomorphism
f
of
A=V/\Lambda
, by Proposition 1.2.1 of
[
7
]
, there is a unique linear map
F:V\rightarrow V
with
F(\Lambda)\subseteq\Lambda
and inducing
f
on
A
. The restriction
F_{\Lambda}
of
F
to
\Lambda
is
\mathbb{Z}
-linear and completely determines both
F
and
f
.

Fix bases of
V
and
\Lambda
, and let
\Pi
be the corresponding period matrix, i.e. the matrix representing the basis of
\Lambda
in terms of the basis of
V
. With respect to these bases,
F
and
F_{\Lambda}
are given by matrices
\rho_{a}(f)\in\mathrm{Mat}_{g}(\mathbb{C})
and
\rho_{r}(f)\in\mathrm{Mat}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z})
, respectively. Since
F(\Lambda)\subseteq\Lambda
, we must have

(2.1)

\rho_{a}(f)\cdot\Pi=\Pi\cdot\rho_{r}(f).

The associations
F\mapsto\rho_{a}(f)
and
F_{\Lambda}\mapsto\rho_{r}(f)
extend to injective ring homomorphisms

\displaystyle\rho_{a}:\en^{0}(A)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathrm{Mat}_{g}(\mathbb{C})

\displaystyle\rho_{r}:\en^{0}(A)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathrm{Mat}_{2g}(\mathbb{Q})

called the analytic representation and the rational representation of
\en^{0}(A)
, respectively.

We denote by
\widehat{A}=\text{Pic}^{0}(A)
the dual abelian variety, i.e. the group of line bundles on
A
that are algebraically equivalent to zero. Given a point
x\in A
, we denote by
T_{x}
the translation-by-
x
map.
If
L
is an arbitrary line bundle on
A
, we have a homomorphism

(2.2)

\begin{array}[]{ c c c c }\Phi_{L}:&{}A&{}\longrightarrow&\widehat{A}\\
&{}x&{}\longmapsto&T_{x}^{*}L\otimes L^{-1}\end{array}

and we call
K(L)
its kernel. A
polarization
is an isogeny
A\rightarrow\widehat{A}
of the form
\Phi_{L}
for some ample line bundle
L
. We say that a polarization is
principal
if it is an isomorphism (i.e.
\deg\Phi_{L}=1
). Recall that any two algebraically equivalent ample line bundles on
A
define the same polarization.

We denote by
\chi(L)
the Euler characteristic of
L
.

To any polarization
\Phi_{L}
on
A
corresponds a positive definite Hermitian form
H_{L}=c_{1}(L):V\times V\to\mathbb{C}
, given by the first Chern class of the line bundle
L
. It is worth noting that, in the literature, the term polarization may refer either to the ample line bundle
L
(up to algebraic equivalence), the associated isogeny
\Phi_{L}
, or the Hermitian form
H_{L}
. These notions are equivalent; see, for example, Section 4.1 of
[
7
]
.
We denote by
E_{L}=\mathrm{Im}(H_{L})
the alternating Riemann form associated with
L
, which takes integer values on the lattice
\Lambda
.

Given an ample line bundle
L
on
A
, there exists a basis of
\Lambda
, called
symplectic basis
, such that the alternating Riemann form
E_{L}:\Lambda\times\Lambda\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
is represented by the matrix

\begin{pmatrix}0&\mathbf{D}\\
-\mathbf{D}&0\end{pmatrix}

where
\mathbf{D}:=\mathrm{diag}(d_{1},\ldots,d_{g})
is a diagonal matrix, with
d_{1},\ldots,d_{g}
positive integers such that
d_{i}
divides
d_{i+1}
for each
i=1,\ldots,g-1
.
We call
\mathbf{D}
the
type
of the polarization
\Phi_{L}
and we define the
Pfaffian
of
E_{L}
as
\mathrm{Pf}(E_{L})=\det(\mathbf{D})

[
7
, Section 3.2]
. The degree of the isogeny
\Phi_{L}
is called the
degree
of the polarization and it is easy to prove that it is equal to
\mathrm{Pf}(E_{L})^{2}=\det(E_{L})
.

Next, we define the Rosati (anti-)involution on
\en^{0}(A)
with respect to the polarization
\Phi_{L}
as:

(2.3)

\begin{array}[]{ c c c c }{}^{\dagger}:&{}\en^{0}(A)&{}\longrightarrow&\en^{0}(A)\\
&{}f&{}\longmapsto&f^{\dagger}=\Phi_{L}^{-1}\circ\widehat{f}\circ\Phi_{L}\end{array}

where
\widehat{f}\in\en^{0}(\widehat{A})
denotes the dual of
f
and, with a slight abuse of notation, we also denote by
\Phi_{L}
the corresponding element of
\mathrm{Hom}^{0}(A,\widehat{A})
. This map is
\mathbb{Q}
-linear and satisfies
(fg)^{\dagger}=g^{\dagger}f^{\dagger}
for all
f,g\in\en^{0}(A)
. In particular, if
\Phi_{L}
is a principal polarization, the Rosati involution restricts to an involution on
\en(A)
.

2.2.
Moduli spaces, universal families and their uniformizations

Let
g,n\geq 1
be positive integers and
\mathbf{D}=\mathrm{diag}(d_{1},\ldots,d_{g})
, with
d_{i}
positive integers such that
d_{i}
divides
d_{i+1}
for every
i=1,\ldots,g-1
.
We define
\mathbb{A}_{g,\mathbf{D},n}
as the moduli space of complex abelian abelian varieties of dimension
g
, polarization type
\mathbf{D}
and with principal level-
n
-structure.
For each type
\mathbf{D}
and
n\geq 3
, the moduli space
\mathbb{A}_{g,\mathbf{D},n}
is a fine moduli space
[
45
, Theorem 7.9]
. In other words, there is a universal family
\pi:\mathfrak{A}_{g,\mathbf{D},n}\rightarrow\mathbb{A}_{g,\mathbf{D},n}
, which, like
\mathbb{A}_{g,\mathbf{D},n}
, is defined over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
. For the rest of the paper we will consider
\mathfrak{A}_{g,\mathbf{D},n}
and
\mathbb{A}_{g,\mathbf{D},n}
as irreducible quasi-projective varieties.

It is well-known (see for example Chapter 8 of
[
7
]
) that
\mathbb{A}_{g,\mathbf{D},n}^{an}
, the analytification of
\mathbb{A}_{g,\mathbf{D},n}
, can be realized as a quotient of
\mathbb{H}_{g}
by a suitable finite index subgroup
\Gamma_{\mathbf{D},n}
of
\mathrm{Sp}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z})
, where

\mathbb{H}_{g}:=\left\{Z\in\mat_{g}(\mathbb{C}):Z=Z^{t},\,\mathrm{Im}(Z)>0\right\}

and
\mathrm{Sp}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z}):=\left\{M\in\mat_{2g}(\mathbb{Z}):M^{t}JM=J\right\}
(here
J:=\begin{pmatrix}0&\mathbf{1}_{g}\\
-\mathbf{1}_{g}&0\end{pmatrix}
) acts on
\mathbb{H}_{g}
by

\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{pmatrix}\cdot Z=(AZ+B)(CZ+D)^{-1}.

Remark 2.1
.

We will show in Section
3
that we can always reduce the problem to studying principally polarized abelian varieties. Moreover, the choice of the level structure is not important for our proof of Theorem
1.1
. So, for the rest of the article, we fix
\mathbf{D}=\mathbf{1}_{g}
and
n=3
and omit those indices from the notation when they are clear from the context.

Note that
\mathbb{H}_{g}
is an open subset, in the Euclidean topology, of

\left\{M\in\mat_{g}(\mathbb{C}):M=M^{t}\right\}\cong\mathbb{C}^{\frac{g(g+1)}{2}}

and that we can see
\mathbb{H}_{g}
as a semialgebraic subset of
\mathbb{R}^{2g^{2}}
, by identifying a complex number with its real and imaginary parts.
Furthermore, the quotient map
u_{b}:\mathbb{H}_{g}\rightarrow\mathbb{A}_{g}^{an}
is holomorphic.

Similarly, we have an holomorphic uniformization map for the universal family, given by theta functions,
u:\mathbb{H}_{g}\times\mathbb{C}^{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{A}_{g}^{an}
, such that the following diagram commutes

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{A}_{g}^{an}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{H}_{g}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{A}_{g}^{an}}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces u}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces u_{b}}

Now, we would like to find a subset of
\mathbb{H}_{g}\times\mathbb{C}^{g}
over which
u
is invertible.

By
[
28
, Section V.4]
, there is a semialgebraic set
\mathfrak{F}_{g}
of
\mathbb{H}_{g}
which can be used as a fundamental domain for the action of
\mathrm{Sp}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z})
on
\mathbb{H}_{g}
. If
\Gamma
is a finite index subgroup of
\mathrm{Sp}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z})
and
\sigma_{1}=\mathbf{1}_{2g},\sigma_{2},\ldots,\sigma_{n}
is a complete set of representatives of its right cosets, then

(2.4)

\mathfrak{F}_{\Gamma}:=\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{i}\cdot\mathfrak{F}_{g}

is called a
Siegel fundamental domain for
\Gamma
and can be used as a fundamental domain for the action of
\Gamma
on
\mathbb{H}_{g}
.

For a fixed
\tau\in\mathbb{H}_{g}
we have a principally polarized abelian variety
A_{\tau}=\mathbb{C}^{g}/(\mathbb{Z}^{g}+\tau\mathbb{Z}^{g})
. In this case, let
L_{\tau}:=\left\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{g}:z=u+\tau v\text{ with }u,v\in[0,1)^{g}\right\}
be the fundamental parallelogram for the lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{g}+\tau\mathbb{Z}^{g}

2.3.
Heights

Let
h
denote the logarithmic absolute Weil height on
\mathbb{P}^{N}
, as defined in
[
8
, Chapter 1]
or
[
27
, Part B]
and, if
\alpha
is an algebraic number, define
h(\alpha)=h\left(\left[1:\alpha\right]\right)
. Define also the multiplicative Weil height as
H(P)=\exp(h(P))
. More generally, if
V
is a projective variety and
D
is a divisor, denote by
h_{V,D}
a Weil height on
V
associated to
D
(see
[
8
, Chapter 2]
or
[
27
, Section B.3]
).

Let
M=(m_{i,j})\in\mat_{n}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
. We associate to
M
two natural heights:

•

the
affine height
, defined by

H_{\mathrm{aff}}(M)=\prod\limits_{v\in M_{K}}\max\left\{1,\max\limits_{1\leq i,j\leq n}\left\{\left|m_{i,j}\right|_{v}\right\}\right\}^{\frac{d_{v}}{[K:\mathbb{Q}]}}

where
K
is a number field containing all the entries of
M
. This coincides with the absolute multiplicative Weil height of
M
regarded as a point of
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}^{n^{2}}
;

•

the
entry-wise height
, defined by

H_{\mathrm{max}}(M)=\max\limits_{1\leq i,j\leq n}\left\{H(m_{i,j})\right\}.

The affine and entry-wise heights enjoy many useful properties with respect to usual matrix operations, which we now collect.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
A,B\in\mat_{n}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
. Then:

(1)

H_{\mathrm{max}}(A)\leq H_{\mathrm{aff}}(A)\leq H_{\mathrm{max}}(A)^{n^{2}}
;

(2)

H_{\mathrm{max}}(A+B)\leq 2H_{\mathrm{max}}(A)H_{\mathrm{max}}(B)
;

(3)

H_{\mathrm{max}}(AB)\leq nH_{\mathrm{max}}(A)^{n}H_{\mathrm{max}}(B)^{n}
;

(4)

H(\det(A))\leq n!\cdot H_{\mathrm{aff}}(A)^{n}
;

(5)

if
A
is invertible,
H_{\mathrm{max}}(A^{-1})\leq n!\cdot(n-1)!\cdot H_{\mathrm{aff}}(A)^{2n-1}
.

Proof.

Let
A=(a_{i,j})
and
B=(b_{i,j})
and fix a number field
K
containing all entries of
A
and
B
.

(1)

Since
\max\left\{1,\left|a_{i,j}\right|_{v}\right\}\leq\max\left\{1,\max\limits_{1\leq i,j\leq n}\left\{\left|a_{i,j}\right|_{v}\right\}\right\}
, we clearly have

H(a_{i,j})=\prod\limits_{v\in M_{K}}\max\left\{1,\left|a_{i,j}\right|_{v}\right\}^{\frac{d_{v}}{[K:\mathbb{Q}]}}\leq H_{\mathrm{aff}}(A)

which implies that
H_{\mathrm{max}}(A)\leq H_{\mathrm{aff}}(A)
. Moreover, recall that

\max\left\{1,\max\limits_{1\leq i,j\leq n}\left\{\left|a_{i,j}\right|_{v}\right\}\right\}\leq\prod\limits_{1\leq i,j\leq n}\max\left\{1,\left|a_{i,j}\right|_{v}\right\}

which implies that
H_{\mathrm{aff}}(A)\leq\prod_{1\leq i,j\leq n}H(a_{i,j})\leq H_{\mathrm{max}}(A)^{n^{2}}
.

(2)

The claim follows from the inequality

H(a_{i,j}+b_{i,j})\leq 2H(a_{i,j})H(b_{i,j})\leq 2H_{\mathrm{max}}(A)H_{\mathrm{max}}(B),

which is a direct consequence of
[
8
, Proposition 1.5.15]
.

(3)

Let
AB=(c_{i,j})
, where
c_{i,j}=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}a_{i,k}b_{k,j}
. Then, applying
[
8
, Proposition 1.5.15]
and the fact that
H(\alpha\beta)\leq H(\alpha)H(\beta)
for all
\alpha,\beta\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
, yields

H(c_{i,j})\leq n\cdot\prod\limits_{k=1}^{n}H(a_{i,k})H(b_{k,j})\leq nH_{\mathrm{max}}(A)^{n}H_{\mathrm{max}}(B)^{n},

which implies
H_{\mathrm{max}}(AB)\leq nH_{\mathrm{max}}(A)^{n}H_{\mathrm{max}}(B)^{n}
.

(4)

Recall that

\det(A)=\sum\limits_{\sigma\in S_{n}}\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)\prod_{i=1}^{n}a_{i,\sigma(i)}

where
S_{n}
denotes the symmetric group on
n

\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)\in\left\{\pm 1\right\}
is the sign of the permutation
\sigma
. Hence
\det(A)
is the sum of
n!
monomials of degree
n
in the entries of
A
. In particular, for every place

, we have

\left|\det(A)\right|_{v}\leq\begin{cases}n!\cdot\max\limits_{1\leq i,j\leq n}\left\{\left|a_{i,j}\right|_{v}\right\}^{n}\quad&\text{if }v\text{ is archimedean}\\
\max\limits_{1\leq i,j\leq n}\left\{\left|a_{i,j}\right|_{v}\right\}^{n}\quad&\text{if }v\text{ is non-archimedean}\end{cases}

Hence,

\displaystyle\prod\limits_{v\in M^{0}_{K}}\max\left\{1,\left|\det(A)\right|_{v}\right\}^{\frac{d_{v}}{[K:\mathbb{Q}]}}

\displaystyle\leq\prod\limits_{v\in M^{0}_{K}}\!\left(\max\left\{1,\max\limits_{1\leq i,j\leq n}\left\{\left|a_{i,j}\right|_{v}\right\}\right\}^{n}\right)^{\frac{d_{v}}{[K:\mathbb{Q}]}}

\displaystyle=\left(\prod\limits_{v\in M^{0}_{K}}\max\left\{1,\max\limits_{1\leq i,j\leq n}\left\{\left|a_{i,j}\right|_{v}\right\}\right\}^{\frac{d_{v}}{[K:\mathbb{Q}]}}\right)^{n}

and

\displaystyle\prod\limits_{v\in M^{\infty}_{K}}\max\left\{1,\left|\det(A)\right|_{v}\right\}^{\frac{d_{v}}{[K:\mathbb{Q}]}}

\displaystyle\leq\prod\limits_{v\in M^{\infty}_{K}}\!\left(n!\max\left\{1,\max\limits_{1\leq i,j\leq n}\left\{\left|a_{i,j}\right|_{v}\right\}\right\}^{n}\right)^{\frac{d_{v}}{[K:\mathbb{Q}]}}

\displaystyle=(n!)^{\sum\limits_{v\in M^{\infty}_{K}}\frac{d_{v}}{[K:\mathbb{Q}]}}\left(\prod\limits_{v\in M^{\infty}_{K}}\max\left\{1,\max\limits_{1\leq i,j\leq n}\left\{\left|a_{i,j}\right|_{v}\right\}\right\}^{\frac{d_{v}}{[K:\mathbb{Q}]}}\right)^{n}

\displaystyle=n!\left(\prod\limits_{v\in M^{\infty}_{K}}\max\left\{1,\max\limits_{1\leq i,j\leq n}\left\{\left|a_{i,j}\right|_{v}\right\}\right\}^{\frac{d_{v}}{[K:\mathbb{Q}]}}\right)^{n}

since
\sum_{v\in M^{\infty}_{K}}d_{v}=[K:\mathbb{Q}]
. So, we have

\displaystyle H(\det(A))

\displaystyle=\prod\limits_{v\in M_{K}}\max\left\{1,\left|\det(A)\right|_{v}\right\}^{\frac{d_{v}}{[K:\mathbb{Q}]}}

\displaystyle=\prod\limits_{v\in M^{\infty}_{K}}\max\left\{1,\left|\det(A)\right|_{v}\right\}^{\frac{d_{v}}{[K:\mathbb{Q}]}}\cdot\prod\limits_{v\in M^{0}_{K}}\max\left\{1,\left|\det(A)\right|_{v}\right\}^{\frac{d_{v}}{[K:\mathbb{Q}]}}

\displaystyle\leq n!\cdot H_{\mathrm{aff}}(A)^{n}.

(5)

The case
n=1
is trivial, so assume
n\geq 2
. Recall that
A^{-1}=\frac{1}{\det(A)}\cdot C^{t}
, where
C=\left((-1)^{i+j}\mu_{i,j}\right)
is the cofactor matrix and
\mu_{i,j}
is the
(i,j)
-minor
1
1

1

Some authors use the word
minor
to denote just the matrix obtained from
A
by removing a row and a column. In this article, by minor we mean the determinant of such a submatrix.
of
A
. Then, by part (4),
H((-1)^{i+j}\mu_{i,j})\leq(n-1)!\cdot H_{\mathrm{aff}}(A)^{n-1}
, so that
H_{\mathrm{max}}(C)\leq(n-1)!\cdot H_{\mathrm{aff}}(A)^{n-1}
. Therefore,
H_{\mathrm{max}}(A^{-1})\leq H(\det(A))\cdot H_{\mathrm{max}}(C)\leq n!\cdot(n-1)!\cdot H_{\mathrm{aff}}(A)^{2n-1}
.

∎

We will also need another definition of height (from

).

Definition 2.4
.

If
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
and
\alpha
is a complex number, we define the
d
-height of
\alpha
as

H_{d}(\alpha):=\min\left\{H\!\left(\left[a_{0}:\ldots:a_{d}\right]\right):\left[a_{0}:\ldots:a_{d}\right]\in\mathbb{P}^{d}(\mathbb{Q})\text{ s.t. }a_{0}+a_{1}\alpha+\ldots+a_{d}\alpha^{d}=0\right\}

where we use the convention
\min\emptyset=+\infty
.
For
(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{N})\in\mathbb{C}^{N}
, we also define
H_{d}(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{N})=\max\left\{H_{d}(\alpha_{i})\right\}
.

Note that
H_{d}(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{N})
is finite if and only if
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{N}
are all algebraic numbers of degree at most
d
.

Lemma 2.5
.

For any
\alpha\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
of degree at most
d
we have

H_{d}(\alpha)\leq 2^{d}H(\alpha)^{d}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\left|\alpha\right|\leq\sqrt{d+1}\cdot H_{d}(\alpha).

Proof.

Let
f(x)=a_{0}+a_{1}x+\ldots+a_{n}x^{n}\in\mathbb{Q}[x]
be a polynomial of degree
n\leq d
such that
f(\alpha)=0
and let
m_{\alpha}(x)\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
be the minimal polynomial of
\alpha
(so its coefficients are coprime). Since in the definition of
H_{d}(\alpha)
we are considering the coefficients of
f
as a point in a projective space, we may assume that the coefficients of
f
are integers with
\gcd(a_{0},\ldots,a_{n})=1
.

For every
p\in\mathbb{C}[x]
let
M(p)
denote the Mahler measure of
p
, as in
[
8
, Section 1.6]
.
By
[
8
, Proposition 1.6.6]
,

M(m_{\alpha})=H\!\left(\alpha\right)^{[\mathbb{Q}(\alpha):\mathbb{Q}]}\leq H(\alpha)^{d}.

Moreover,
[
8
, Lemma 1.6.7]
gives

\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\infty}:=\max\!\left\{\left|a_{0}\right|,\ldots,\left|a_{n}\right|\right\}\leq\binom{n}{\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor}M(f)\leq 2^{n}M(f)\leq 2^{d}M(f).

Since the coefficients of
f
are coprime integers,
H([a_{0}:\ldots:a_{n}])=\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\infty}
. Hence

\displaystyle H_{d}(\alpha)

\displaystyle=\min\left\{\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\infty}:f\in\mathbb{Z}[x]\text{ with coprime coefficients s.t. }\deg(f)\leq d\text{ and }f(\alpha)=0\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert m_{\alpha}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq 2^{d}M(m_{\alpha})\leq 2^{d}H(\alpha)^{d}

which proves the first inequality.

For the second inequality, note first that
[
8
, Proposition 1.6.6]
implies
\left|\alpha\right|\leq M(f)
for any
f\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
such that
f(\alpha)=0
. Furthermore, by
[
8
, Lemma 1.6.7]
, we also have that
M(f)\leq\sqrt{\deg(f)+1}\cdot\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\infty}
. Taking the minimum over all polynomials
f\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
with coprime coefficients and
\deg(f)\leq d
such that
f(\alpha)=0
then yields the desired bound
\left|\alpha\right|\leq\sqrt{d+1}\cdot H_{d}(\alpha)
.
∎

For the reader’s convenience, we recall some properties of canonical heights on abelian varieties in the following proposition.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
A
be an abelian variety defined over a number field
K
, and let
D\in\mathrm{Div}(A)
be a divisor on
A
. Then, the canonical height
\widehat{h}_{A,D}
satisfies the following properties:

(1)

\widehat{h}_{A,D}=h_{A,D}+O(1)
and
\widehat{h}_{A,D}(O)=0
;

(2)

If
D^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Div}(A)
is linearly equivalent to
D
, then
\widehat{h}_{A,D^{\prime}}=\widehat{h}_{A,D}
;

(3)

If
D,E\in\mathrm{Div}(A)
, then
\widehat{h}_{A,D+E}=\widehat{h}_{A,D}+\widehat{h}_{A,E}
;

(4)

Let
B/K
another abelian variety, and let
\phi:B\rightarrow A
be a morphism. Then
\widehat{h}_{B,\phi^{*}D}=\widehat{h}_{A,D}\circ\phi-\widehat{h}_{A,D}(\phi(O_{B}))
;

(5)

If
D
is symmetric (i.e.
[-1]^{*}D\sim D
), then
\widehat{h}_{A,D}([n]P)=n^{2}\cdot\widehat{h}_{A,D}(P)
for every
P\in A(\overline{K})
.

(6)

If
D
is nef and symmetric, then
\widehat{h}_{A,D}(P)\geq 0
for every
P\in A(\overline{K})
. In particular, if
D
is ample and symmetric,
\widehat{h}_{A,D}(P)=0
if and only if
P
has finite order.

Proof.

The proofs of (1)–(5) can be found in
[
27
, Theorems B.5.1 and B.5.6]
, and the proof of (6) for
D
ample and symmetric is given in
[
27
, Proposition B.5.3]
. The case of (6) when
D
is nef is treated in
[
29
]
, immediately after the displayed equation (6.14). For the reader’s convenience, we briefly recall the argument.

If
D
is symmetric and nef, then for any ample symmetric divisor
H
and any integer
n\geq 1
, the divisor
nD+H
is ample and symmetric by Kleiman’s criterion. Using the linearity established in (3), we obtain

n\widehat{h}_{A,D}=\widehat{h}_{A,nD+H}-\widehat{h}_{A,H}\geq-\widehat{h}_{A,H}.

Since

is ample, we have
\widehat{h}_{A,H}\geq 0
, and as

is arbitrary, it follows that
\widehat{h}_{A,D}\geq 0
as well.
∎

Remark 2.7
.

Note that for any divisor
D
and any torsion point
P\in A(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
, one has
\widehat{h}_{A,D}(P)=0
. The converse, however, does not hold in general: as shown in
[
29
]
, when
D
is nef the set of points of canonical height zero may strictly contain the torsion subgroup.

For an explicit example with a non-nef divisor, let
E
be an elliptic curve defined over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
, set
A=E\times E
, and consider

. It is easy to check that

is not nef. Then, the proposition above implies that (see Section
7

\widehat{h}_{A,D}(P_{1},P_{2})=\widehat{h}_{E,O}(P_{1})-\widehat{h}_{E,O}(P_{2})

. Hence, in this case the set of points of zero canonical height contains, for instance, the diagonal and all its translates by torsion points.

2.4.
Complex Multiplication

In this section, we recall the basic definitions and key facts about complex multiplication for abelian varieties defined over fields of characteristic 0, which will be used throughout this article. For further details on this topic, we refer to
[
32
,
57
,
44
]
.

Definition 2.8
.

A
CM field

K
is a totally imaginary quadratic extension of a totally real number field. That is,
K
has the form
K=K_{0}(\sqrt{\alpha})
, where
K_{0}
is a totally real field, i.e., a number field whose embeddings into
\mathbb{C}
are all real, and
\alpha\in K_{0}
satisfies the condition that each embedding of
K_{0}
into
\mathbb{C}
maps
\alpha
to a negative real number.

Definition 2.9
.

An abelian variety
A
of dimension
g
is said to have
Complex Multiplication
(CM) if its endomorphism algebra
\en^{0}(A)
contains a commutative semisimple subalgebra of degree
2g
over
\mathbb{Q}
. We say that
A
has CM by the CM field
K
(of degree
2g
) if there exists an embedding
K\hookrightarrow\en^{0}(A)
.

Note that a simple abelian variety
A
has complex multiplication if and only if
\en^{0}(A)
is a CM field of degree
2\dim(A)
. In general, an abelian variety has complex multiplication if and only if each of
its simple factors up to isogeny has complex multiplication.

If
A
is a simple CM abelian variety of dimension
g
, then
\en^{0}(A)\cong K
is a CM field and there is a set
\Phi=\left\{\phi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{g}\right\}
of complex embeddings of
K
such that
\Phi\cup\overline{\Phi}
is the set of all complex embeddings of
K
and
T_{O}(A)\cong\prod_{i=1}^{g}\mathbb{C}_{\phi_{i}}
, where
\mathbb{C}_{\phi_{i}}
is a 1-dimensional
\mathbb{C}
-vector space on which
\alpha\in K
acts as
\phi_{i}(\alpha)
. We call the pair
(K,\Phi)
a
CM-type
of
A
. In particular, by Proposition 3.13 of
[
44
]
,
(K,\Phi)
is primitive, i.e. it is not induced by a CM-type of a proper CM subfield of
K
.

3.

The first result of this section allows us to perform finite base changes.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be as in Theorem

\mathcal{A}^{\prime}=\mathcal{A}\times_{S}S^{\prime}

Proof.

By the proof of Lemma 2.1 of

Moreover, if

\rho^{-1}(X)

.
In particular, this shows that the preimages of the flat subgroup schemes of
\mathcal{A}

\mathcal{A}^{\prime}

This implies that if
\mathcal{C}

\rho^{-1}(\mathcal{C})

Finally, the preimages of any point of
\mathcal{C}
lying in a proper algebraic subgroup of a CM fiber
\mathcal{A}_{s}

, are contained in proper algebraic subgroups of fibers of
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
, which are still CM, since for
s^{\prime}\in S^{\prime}(\mathbb{C})
and
s\in S(\mathbb{C})
such that

, then
\mathcal{A}_{s}\cong\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{s^{\prime}}
.
∎

Next, let
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
be abelian schemes over the same curve

. Then,

extends to an isogeny

[
4
]

[

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}^{\prime},f_{\eta}
and

\mathcal{C}
as in Theorem

. Then, if the claim of Theorem

Proof.

For every

, the map

remain algebraic subgroups, and dimensions are preserved. Moreover, since isogenous abelian varieties have isomorphic endomorphism algebras, it follows that
\mathcal{A}_{s}
is CM if and only if
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{s}

Now, consider the preimage under
f
of any intersection of
\mathcal{C}

. Since this preimage lies in a proper algebraic subgroup of a CM fiber of

, and by assumption the claim of Theorem

∎

Now, as

4.
O-minimality and definable sets

In this section we recall some properties and results about o-minimal structures. For more details see
[
65
]
and
[

]
.

Definition 4.1
.

A
structure
is a sequence
\mathcal{S}=\left(\mathcal{S}_{N}\right)
,
N\geq 1
, where each
\mathcal{S}_{N}
is a collection of subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
such that, for each
N,M\geq 1
:

•

\mathcal{S}_{N}
is a boolean algebra (under the usual set-theoretic operations);

•

\mathcal{S}_{N}
contains every semi-algebraic subset of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
;

•

if
A\in\mathcal{S}_{N}
and
B\in\mathcal{S}_{M}
, then
A\times B\in\mathcal{S}_{N+M}
;

•

if
A\in\mathcal{S}_{N+M}
, then
\pi(A)\in\mathcal{S}_{N}
, where
\pi:\mathbb{R}^{N+M}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{N}
is the projection onto the first
N
coordinates.

If
\mathcal{S}
is a structure and, in addition,

•

\mathcal{S}_{1}
consists of all finite union of open intervals and points

then
\mathcal{S}
is called an
o-minimal structure
.

Given a structure
\mathcal{S}
, we say that
S\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
is a
definable set
if
S\in\mathcal{S}_{N}
.

Given
S\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
and a function
f:S\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{M}
, we say that
f
is a
definable function
if its graph
\left\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{M}:x\in S,y=f(x)\right\}
is a definable set. One can easily prove that images and preimages of definable sets via definable functions are still definable.

Let
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N+M}
. For
t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{M}
, we set
U_{t_{0}}=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:(t_{0},x)\in U\right\}
and call
U
a
family
of subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, while
U_{t_{0}}
is called the
fiber
of
U
above
t_{0}
. If
U
is a definable set, then we call it a
definable family
and it is easy to prove that the fibers
U_{t_{0}}
are also definable.

Proposition 4.2

(
[
64
]
, 4.4)
.

Let
U
be a definable family in a fixed o-minimal structure
\mathcal{S}
. Then, there exists an integer
n
such that each fiber of
U
has at most
n
connected components.

While there are many examples of o-minimal structures (see
[
64
]
), in this article we will work with the structure

.

For a family

where

. Let also

be the projections of

Proposition 4.3

(
[
24
]
, Corollary 7.2)

Let

be a definable set. For every positive integer

and every

with the following property. If

and
\left|\pi_{2}(Z^{\sim}(d,T))\right|>cT^{\varepsilon}

(1)

the restriction

(2)

We conclude this section by showing that the set

From now on, we use the term “definable” to mean definable in

We may assume that
\mathfrak{A}_{g}
is embedded in some projective space. By Theorem 1.2 of

of
\mathbb{C}^{g}
such that the period matrix of

with respect to these bases is

, where

(see
[
7

).

As in Section
2.2
, denote by
\mathfrak{F}_{g}
the fundamental domain for the action of

on
\mathbb{H}_{g}

[
28

. Fix a finite index subgroup
\Gamma
of
\mathrm{Sp}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z})
and denote by
\mathfrak{F}_{\Gamma}
the Siegel fundamental domain for
\Gamma

\mathfrak{F}_{\Gamma}
was defined in (

\mathfrak{F}_{\Gamma}=\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{i}\cdot\mathfrak{F}_{g}

\sigma_{1}=\mathbf{1}_{2g},\sigma_{2},\ldots,\sigma_{n}\in\mathrm{Sp}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z})
is a complete set of representatives for the right cosets of
\Gamma
in
\mathrm{Sp}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z})
.

In order to state and prove the result of this section, we introduce some matrix norms.

Definition 5.1
.

For a matrix
M=(m_{i,j})_{1\leq i,j\leq n}\in\mat_{n}(\mathbb{C})
we define the following norms:

•

\left\lVert M\right\rVert_{\infty}:=\max\limits_{i,j}\left|m_{i,j}\right|
;

•

(Frobenius norm)
\left\lVert M\right\rVert_{F}:=\sqrt{\tr\left(\overline{M}^{t}M\right)}=\sqrt{\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{n}\left|m_{i,j}\right|^{2}}
;

•

(Spectral norm)
\left\lVert M\right\rVert_{2}:=\sqrt{\rho\left(\overline{M}^{t}M\right)}
, where
\rho(M)
denotes the spectral radius of
M
, i.e. the maximum of the absolute values of the eigenvalues of
M
.

Recall that the polarization
\mathcal{L}
defines a Rosati involution
†
(see Equation (
2.3
)). Throughout this article, once the above symplectic basis is fixed, we identify rational representations of endomorphisms of
A
with their matrices in that basis.

As established in
[
7
, Theorem 5.1.8]
,

(5.1)

\tr(\rho_{r}(f^{\dagger}f))>0

for any nonzero
f\in\en^{0}(A):=\en(A)\otimes\mathbb{Q}
. Hence,
\left\lVert\rho_{r}(f)\right\rVert_{\infty}
and
\sqrt{\tr\left(\rho_{r}(f^{\dagger}f)\right)}
are two norms on the finite dimensional
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space
\en^{0}(A)
and, as such, they are equivalent. Thus, there exist two constants
c_{1},c_{2}>0
such that

c_{1}\cdot\sqrt{\tr\left(\rho_{r}(f^{\dagger}f)\right)}\leq\left\lVert\rho_{r}(f)\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq c_{2}\cdot\sqrt{\tr\left(\rho_{r}(f^{\dagger}f)\right)}

for every
f\in\en^{0}(A)
.
The aim of this section is to make the constants
c_{1},c_{2}
effective by proving the following result.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
A
be an abelian variety of dimension
g
defined over
\mathbb{C}
. Fix a polarization
\mathcal{L}
and choose bases of
\Lambda
and
\mathbb{C}^{g}
as above. Consider the Rosati involution
†
on
\en^{0}(A)
defined by
\mathcal{L}
and assume that
\tau\in\mathfrak{F}_{\Gamma}
. Then, for every
f\in\en^{0}(A)
, we have

c_{1}(A)\cdot\sqrt{\tr\left(\rho_{r}(f^{\dagger}f)\right)}\leq\left\lVert\rho_{r}(f)\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq c_{2}(A)\cdot\sqrt{\tr\left(\rho_{r}(f^{\dagger}f)\right)}

where
c_{1}(A)=\dfrac{1}{2g}\sqrt{\dfrac{d_{1}}{d_{g}}}
and
c_{2}(A)=\delta(g,\mathfrak{F}_{\Gamma})\cdot\dfrac{\left\lVert\mathbf{D}\right\rVert_{\infty}^{2g+2}}{d}\cdot\max\left\{1,\left\lVert\mathrm{Im}(Z_{\tau})\right\rVert_{\infty}\right\}^{2g^{3}+3g^{2}+2g+1}
. Here,
\delta(g,\mathfrak{F}_{\Gamma})
is an effective positive constant depending only on
g
and the choice of the representatives of the right cosets of
\Gamma
in
\mathrm{Sp}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z})
and
Z_{\tau}\in\mathfrak{F}_{g}
is in the same
\mathrm{Sp}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z})
-orbit as
\tau
.

Let
H
be the Hermitian form associated with the polarization
\mathcal{L}
, and let
E=\mathrm{Im}(H)
be the associated alternating form, which satisfies
E(\Lambda\times\Lambda)\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
. According to
[
7
, Lemma 2.1.7]
, the form
H
can be expressed as:

H(u,v)=E(iu,v)+iE(u,v)

for every
u,v\in\mathbb{C}^{g}
, with
S(u,v)=E(iu,v)=\mathrm{Re}(H(u,v))
positive definite.

Let
†
be the Rosati involution defined by the polarization
\mathcal{L}
. By Proposition 5.1.1 of
[
7
]

H(\rho_{a}(f)(u),v)=H(u,\rho_{a}(f^{\dagger})(v))

for any

and for all
u,v\in\mathbb{C}^{g}
. As in
[
37
]

\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{2g}
and taking real and imaginary parts yields

\rho_{r}(f^{\dagger})=S^{-1}\cdot\rho_{r}(f)^{t}\cdot S=E^{-1}\cdot\rho_{r}(f)^{t}\cdot E

where, with a slight abuse of notation, we denote by
S
and
E
the matrices representing the bilinear forms
S(u,v)
and
E(u,v)
with respect to the basis
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{2g}
of
\Lambda
.
If we denote

R=\rho_{r}(f),

then

(5.2)

\tr\left(\rho_{r}(f^{\dagger}f)\right)=\tr\left(E^{-1}R^{t}\,ER\right)=\tr\left(S^{-1}R^{t}\,SR\right).

Since
S

\displaystyle S^{-1}R^{t}\,SR

\displaystyle=U^{-1}(U^{-1})^{t}\,R^{t}\,U^{t}UR

\displaystyle=U^{-1}\cdot\left((U^{-1})^{t}\,R^{t}\,U^{t}\right)\cdot\left(UR\,U^{-1}\right)\cdot U

\tr\left(\rho_{r}(f^{\dagger}f)\right)=\tr\left(Q^{t}\,Q\right)=\left\lVert Q\right\rVert_{F}^{2}

Furthermore, by the triangle inequality, for any

(5.3)

\left\lVert M_{1}M_{2}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq n\left\lVert M_{1}\right\rVert_{\infty}\cdot\left\lVert M_{2}\right\rVert_{\infty}.

\left\lVert R\right\rVert_{\infty}=\left\lVert U^{-1}QU\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq\left((2g)^{2}\cdot\left\lVert U^{-1}\right\rVert_{\infty}\cdot\left\lVert U\right\rVert_{\infty}\right)\cdot\left\lVert Q\right\rVert_{\infty}.

Lemma 5.3
.

If
M\in\mat_{n}(\mathbb{R})
is positive definite and
T\in\mat_{n}(\mathbb{R})
is an upper triangular matrix with positive diagonal entries such that
M=T^{t}\cdot T
, then
\left\lVert T\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq\sqrt{n\left\lVert M\right\rVert_{\infty}}
.

Proof.

We clearly have
\left\lVert M\right\rVert_{2}=\left\lVert T\right\rVert_{2}^{2}
. Moreover,
\left\lVert N\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq\left\lVert N\right\rVert_{2}\leq n\left\lVert N\right\rVert_{\infty}
for every
N\in\mat_{n}(\mathbb{R})

[
21

Thus,
\left\lVert T\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq\left\lVert T\right\rVert_{2}=\sqrt{\left\lVert M\right\rVert_{2}}\leq\sqrt{n\left\lVert M\right\rVert_{\infty}}
.
∎

The following result is well known but we include it for completeness.

Lemma 5.4
.

For any matrix
M\in\mat_{n}(\mathbb{C})
, we have
\left|\det(M)\right|\leq n^{n/2}\cdot\left\lVert M\right\rVert_{\infty}^{n}
.

Proof.

This follows easily from Hadamard’s inequality
[

.
∎

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
M\in\mat_{n}(\mathbb{C})
be an invertible matrix. Then

\left\lVert M^{-1}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq\dfrac{n^{n/2}}{\left|\det(M)\right|}\cdot\left\lVert M\right\rVert_{\infty}^{n-1}.

Proof.

The case
n=1
is trivial, so assume
n\geq 2
. Recall that
M^{-1}=\frac{1}{\det(M)}C^{t}
, where
C
is the cofactor matrix (see also the proof of part (5) of Proposition

\left\lVert C\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq(n-1)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\cdot\left\lVert M\right\rVert_{\infty}^{n-1}

\left\lVert M^{-1}\right\rVert_{\infty}=\dfrac{1}{\left|\det(M)\right|}\cdot\left\lVert C\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq\dfrac{(n-1)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}{\left|\det(M)\right|}\cdot\left\lVert M\right\rVert_{\infty}^{n-1}\leq\dfrac{n^{n/2}}{\left|\det(M)\right|}\cdot\left\lVert M\right\rVert_{\infty}^{n-1}.

∎

Next, we compute
S(u,v)

So, by doing the computations with the basis

of

\begin{pmatrix}X&\mathbf{D}\\

Hence, the matrix representing

in the basis

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}Y^{-1}X&Y^{-1}\mathbf{D}\\

-\mathbf{D}&0\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}XY^{-1}X+Y&XY^{-1}\mathbf{D}\\
\mathbf{D}Y^{-1}X&\mathbf{D}Y^{-1}\mathbf{D}\end{pmatrix}.

Furthermore, note that by

\displaystyle\det(S)

\displaystyle=\det(\mathbf{D}Y^{-1}\mathbf{D})\det\left((XY^{-1}X+Y)-(XY^{-1}\mathbf{D})(\mathbf{D}Y^{-1}\mathbf{D})^{-1}(\mathbf{D}Y^{-1}X)\right)

Then, by Lemma

(5.4)

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{(2g)^{g}}{d}\cdot\left\lVert U\right\rVert_{\infty}^{2g-1}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{(2g)^{2g}}{d}\cdot\max\left\{1,\left\lVert S\right\rVert_{\infty}\right\}^{g}.

Lemma 5.6

\det(Y)\geq\left(\dfrac{3}{4}\right)^{\frac{g(g-1)}{2}}\cdot(y_{1,1})^{g}\geq\left(\dfrac{3}{4}\right)^{\frac{g(g-1)}{2}}\cdot\left(\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right)^{g}=\left(\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right)^{g^{2}}

which proves part (b).
∎

Proposition 5.7
.

Let
\tau=X+iY\in\mathfrak{F}_{\Gamma}
and let
Z_{\tau}\in\mathfrak{F}_{g}
be in the same
\mathrm{Sp}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z})
-orbit as
\tau
. Then, there are effective positive constants
\delta_{1},\delta_{2},\delta_{3},\delta_{4}
, depending only on
g
and the choices of the representatives for the right cosets of
\Gamma
in
\mathrm{Sp}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z})
, such that:

(a)

\left\lVert Y\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq\delta_{1}\cdot\max\!\left\{1,\left\lVert\mathrm{Im}(Z_{\tau})\right\rVert_{\infty}\right\}^{2g-1}
;

(b)

\left\lVert X\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq\delta_{2}\cdot\max\!\left\{1,\left\lVert\mathrm{Im}(Z_{\tau})\right\rVert_{\infty}\right\}^{g}
;

(c)

\det(Y)\geq\dfrac{\delta_{3}}{\max\!\left\{1,\left\lVert\mathrm{Im}(Z_{\tau})\right\rVert_{\infty}\right\}^{2g}}
;

(d)

\left\lVert Y^{-1}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq\delta_{4}\cdot\max\!\left\{1,\left\lVert\mathrm{Im}(Z_{\tau})\right\rVert_{\infty}\right\}^{2g^{2}-g+1}
.

Proof.

Let
\tau
and
Z_{\tau}
as above and take
\sigma=\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{pmatrix}\in\mathrm{Sp}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z})
such that
\tau=\sigma\cdot Z_{\tau}
. The definition of
\mathfrak{F}_{\Gamma}
(see (
2.4
)) implies that we can take
\sigma
to be one of the chosen representatives
\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{n}
for the right cosets of
\Gamma
in
\mathrm{Sp}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z})
and thus all the constants that appear will depend on the choice of such representatives.

(a)

It is well-known that

(5.5)

Y=\mathrm{Im}(\tau)=\mathrm{Im}(\sigma\cdot Z_{\tau})=\left[(CZ_{\tau}+D)^{t}\right]^{-1}\mathrm{Im}(Z_{\tau})\left(C\overline{Z_{\tau}}+D\right)^{-1}

(see for example
[
28
, Section I.6]
). So (
5.3
) implies that

\left\lVert Y\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq g^{2}\cdot\left\lVert(CZ_{\tau}+D)^{-1}\right\rVert_{\infty}^{2}\cdot\left\lVert\mathrm{Im}(Z_{\tau})\right\rVert_{\infty}

since
\left\lVert\,\cdot\,\right\rVert_{\infty}
is invariant under transposition and complex conjugation. Then, as
Z_{\tau}\in\mathfrak{F}_{g}
, Lemma
5.5
and Lemma
5.6
(c) imply

\left\lVert(CZ_{\tau}+D)^{-1}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq\frac{g^{g/2}}{\left|\det(CZ_{\tau}+D)\right|}\cdot\left\lVert CZ_{\tau}+D\right\rVert_{\infty}^{g-1}\leq g^{g/2}\cdot\left\lVert CZ_{\tau}+D\right\rVert_{\infty}^{g-1}.

Moreover,

(5.6)

\displaystyle\left\lVert CZ_{\tau}+D\right\rVert_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq g\left\lVert C\right\rVert_{\infty}\left\lVert Z_{\tau}\right\rVert_{\infty}+\left\lVert D\right\rVert_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{5g}{2}\cdot\max\!\left\{\left\lVert C\right\rVert_{\infty},\left\lVert D\right\rVert_{\infty}\right\}\cdot\max\!\left\{1,\left\lVert\mathrm{Im}(Z_{\tau})\right\rVert_{\infty}\right\}

since Lemma
5.6
(a) implies

(5.7)

\displaystyle\left\lVert Z_{\tau}\right\rVert_{\infty}+1

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\mathrm{Re}(Z_{\tau})\right\rVert_{\infty}+\left\lVert\mathrm{Im}(Z_{\tau})\right\rVert_{\infty}+1

\displaystyle\leq\frac{3}{2}+\left\lVert\mathrm{Im}(Z_{\tau})\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq\frac{5}{2}\cdot\max\!\left\{1,\left\lVert\mathrm{Im}(Z_{\tau})\right\rVert_{\infty}\right\}.

Combining the inequalities above yields

\displaystyle\left\lVert Y\right\rVert_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq g^{2}\cdot\left\lVert(CZ_{\tau}+D)^{-1}\right\rVert_{\infty}^{2}\cdot\left\lVert\mathrm{Im}(Z_{\tau})\right\rVert_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq g^{g+2}\cdot\left\lVert CZ_{\tau}+D\right\rVert_{\infty}^{2g-2}\cdot\left\lVert\mathrm{Im}(Z_{\tau})\right\rVert_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{5}{2}\right)^{2g-2}\cdot g^{3g}\cdot\max\!\left\{\left\lVert C\right\rVert_{\infty},\left\lVert D\right\rVert_{\infty}\right\}^{2g-2}\cdot\max\!\left\{1,\left\lVert\mathrm{Im}(Z_{\tau})\right\rVert_{\infty}\right\}^{2g-1}.

Hence we can take
\delta_{1}=\left(\frac{5}{2}\right)^{2g-2}\cdot g^{3g}\cdot\max\limits_{\sigma\in\left\{\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{n}\right\}}\!\left\{\max\!\left\{\left\lVert C\right\rVert_{\infty},\left\lVert D\right\rVert_{\infty}\right\}\right\}^{2g-2}
.

(b)

We have that

\displaystyle\left\lVert X\right\rVert_{\infty}=\left\lVert\mathrm{Re}(\sigma\cdot Z_{\tau})\right\rVert_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\sigma\cdot Z_{\tau}\right\rVert_{\infty}=\left\lVert(AZ_{\tau}+B)(CZ_{\tau}+D)^{-1}\right\rVert_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq g\cdot\left\lVert AZ_{\tau}+B\right\rVert_{\infty}\cdot\left\lVert(CZ_{\tau}+D)^{-1}\right\rVert_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq g\cdot\left(g\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{\infty}\left\lVert Z_{\tau}\right\rVert_{\infty}+\left\lVert B\right\rVert_{\infty}\right)\cdot\left\lVert(CZ_{\tau}+D)^{-1}\right\rVert_{\infty}.

From the computations above we also have that

\displaystyle\left\lVert(CZ_{\tau}+D)^{-1}\right\rVert_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq g^{g/2}\cdot\left\lVert CZ_{\tau}+D\right\rVert_{\infty}^{g-1}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{5}{2}\right)^{g-1}g^{\frac{3}{2}g-1}\max\!\left\{\left\lVert C\right\rVert_{\infty},\left\lVert D\right\rVert_{\infty}\right\}^{g-1}\max\!\left\{1,\left\lVert\mathrm{Im}(Z_{\tau})\right\rVert_{\infty}\right\}^{g-1}.

This implies that

\displaystyle\left\lVert X\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq\left(\frac{5}{2}\right)^{g}g^{\frac{3}{2}g+1}\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{\infty}^{g}\max\!\left\{1,\left\lVert\mathrm{Im}(Z_{\tau})\right\rVert_{\infty}\right\}^{g}.

Hence, we can take
\delta_{2}=\left(\frac{5}{2}\right)^{g}g^{\frac{3}{2}g+1}\max\limits_{\sigma\in\left\{\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{n}\right\}}\!\left\{\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{\infty}\right\}^{g}
.

(c)

Taking the determinant of the first and last part of Equation (
5.5
) yields

\det(Y)=\det(CZ_{\tau}+D)^{-1}\cdot\det(\mathrm{Im}(Z_{\tau}))\cdot\det(C\overline{Z_{\tau}}+D)^{-1}=\dfrac{\det(\mathrm{Im}(Z_{\tau}))}{\left|\det(CZ_{\tau}+D)\right|^{2}}.

Furthermore, it follows from Lemma
5.4
and Equation (

) that

\displaystyle\left|\det(CZ_{\tau}+D)\right|

\displaystyle\leq g^{g/2}\cdot\left\lVert CZ_{\tau}+D\right\rVert_{\infty}^{g}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\dfrac{5}{2}\right)^{g}\cdot g^{3g/2}\cdot\max\!\left\{\left\lVert C\right\rVert_{\infty},\left\lVert D\right\rVert_{\infty}\right\}^{g}\cdot\max\!\left\{1,\left\lVert\mathrm{Im}(Z_{\tau})\right\rVert_{\infty}\right\}^{g}.

Therefore, using Lemma
5.6
(b), we get

\det(Y)=\dfrac{\det(\mathrm{Im}(Z_{\tau}))}{\left|\det(CZ_{\tau}+D)\right|^{2}}\geq\dfrac{\frac{\left(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right)^{g^{2}}}{\left(\frac{5}{2}\right)^{2g}\cdot g^{3g}\cdot\max\!\left\{\left\lVert C\right\rVert_{\infty},\left\lVert D\right\rVert_{\infty}\right\}^{2g}}}{\max\!\left\{1,\left\lVert\mathrm{Im}(Z_{\tau})\right\rVert_{\infty}\right\}^{2g}}

so that we can take
\delta_{3}=\left(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right)^{g^{2}}\left(\frac{2}{5}\right)^{2g}\cdot g^{-3g}\cdot\max\limits_{\sigma\in\left\{\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{n}\right\}}\!\left\{\max\!\left\{\left\lVert C\right\rVert_{\infty},\left\lVert D\right\rVert_{\infty}\right\}\right\}^{-2g}
.

(d)

Applying Lemma
5.5
and parts (a) and (c) yields

\left\lVert Y^{-1}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq\dfrac{g^{g/2}}{\left|\det(Y)\right|}\cdot\left\lVert Y\right\rVert_{\infty}^{g-1}\leq g^{g/2}\cdot\frac{\gamma_{1}^{g-1}}{\gamma_{3}}\cdot\max\!\left\{1,\left\lVert\mathrm{Im}(Z_{\tau})\right\rVert_{\infty}\right\}^{2g^{2}-g+1}.

Thus, we can take
\delta_{4}=g^{g/2}\cdot\frac{\delta_{1}^{g-1}}{\delta_{3}}
.

∎

Proof of Proposition
5.2
.

We start from the lower bound. By Equation (
5.2
), we have that
\tr\left(\rho_{r}(f^{\dagger}f)\right)=\tr\left(E^{-1}\rho_{r}(f)^{t}\,E\,\rho_{r}(f)\right)
. As recalled in Section
2.1
, in the symplectic basis that we fixed at the start of this section, the alternating Riemann form
E
is represented by the matrix

\begin{pmatrix}0&\mathbf{D}\\
-\mathbf{D}&0\end{pmatrix}

where
\mathbf{D}:=\mathrm{diag}(d_{1},\ldots,d_{g})
is the type of
\mathcal{L}
. So, it is easy to see that

\tr\left(\rho_{r}(f^{\dagger}f)\right)=2\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{g}\dfrac{d_{i}}{d_{j}}\left(m_{i,j}m_{i+g,j+g}-m_{i,j+g}m_{i+g,j}\right)

where
m_{i,j}
(
i,j=1,\ldots,2g
) are the entries of the matrix
\rho_{r}(f)
with respect to the symplectic basis. Moreover, by Equation (
5.1
), we also obtain

\displaystyle\tr\left(\rho_{r}(f^{\dagger}f)\right)=\left|\tr\left(\rho_{r}(f^{\dagger}f)\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{g}\dfrac{d_{i}}{d_{j}}\left(\left|m_{i,j}\right|\left|m_{i+g,j+g}\right|+\left|m_{i,j+g}\right|\left|m_{i+g,j}\right|\right)

\displaystyle\leq 4\left\lVert\rho_{r}(f)\right\rVert_{\infty}^{2}\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{g}\dfrac{d_{i}}{d_{j}}\leq 4g^{2}\dfrac{d_{g}}{d_{1}}\left\lVert\rho_{r}(f)\right\rVert_{\infty}^{2}

since
1\leq d_{1}\leq d_{2}\leq\ldots\leq d_{g}
(see Section
2.1
). This yields the lower bound.

Next, we prove the upper bound. We already proved that

\left\lVert R\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq\left((2g)^{2}\cdot\left\lVert U^{-1}\right\rVert_{\infty}\cdot\left\lVert U\right\rVert_{\infty}\right)\cdot\left\lVert Q\right\rVert_{\infty}.

Now, by Equation (
5.4
), we have that

\displaystyle(2g)^{2}\cdot\left\lVert U^{-1}\right\rVert_{\infty}\cdot\left\lVert U\right\rVert_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq(2g)^{2}\cdot\dfrac{(2g)^{2g}}{d}\cdot\max\left\{1,\left\lVert S\right\rVert_{\infty}\right\}^{g}\cdot(2g)^{1/2}\cdot\left\lVert S\right\rVert_{\infty}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{(2g)^{2g+3}}{d}\cdot\max\left\{1,\left\lVert S\right\rVert_{\infty}\right\}^{g+1}.

Let
\tau=X+iY
. Then, using (
5.3
), we get

\displaystyle\left\lVert S\right\rVert_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\max\left\{\left\lVert XY^{-1}X+Y\right\rVert_{\infty},\left\lVert XY^{-1}\mathbf{D}\right\rVert_{\infty},\left\lVert\mathbf{D}Y^{-1}X\right\rVert_{\infty},\left\lVert\mathbf{D}Y^{-1}\mathbf{D}\right\rVert_{\infty}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\max\left\{\left\lVert Y\right\rVert_{\infty}+g^{2}\left\lVert X\right\rVert_{\infty}^{2}\left\lVert Y^{-1}\right\rVert_{\infty},g^{2}\left\lVert X\right\rVert_{\infty}\left\lVert Y^{-1}\right\rVert_{\infty}\left\lVert\mathbf{D}\right\rVert_{\infty},g^{2}\left\lVert\mathbf{D}\right\rVert_{\infty}^{2}\left\lVert Y^{-1}\right\rVert_{\infty}\right\}.

Moreover, if
\tau\in\mathfrak{F}_{\Gamma}
, let
Z_{\tau}\in\mathfrak{F}_{g}
be in the same
\mathrm{Sp}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z})
-orbit as
\tau
, as before. Then, by Proposition
5.7
, we also obtain:

\left\lVert Y\right\rVert_{\infty}+g^{2}\left\lVert X\right\rVert_{\infty}^{2}\left\lVert Y^{-1}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq 2g^{2}\delta_{2}^{2}\delta_{4}\cdot\max\!\left\{1,\left\lVert\mathrm{Im}(Z_{\tau})\right\rVert_{\infty}\right\}^{2g^{2}+g+1},

So, we get

6.
The main estimate

For every
T\geq 1
we define the set

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}(T)=\Big\{

\displaystyle\left(\tau,z\right)\in\mathcal{Z}:\exists M\in\mat_{g}(\mathbb{C})\setminus\left\{\mathbf{0}\right\}\text{ s.t. }

\displaystyle Mz\in\mathbb{Z}^{g}+\tau\mathbb{Z}^{g},H_{2g}(\tau),H_{2g}(M)\leq T\text{ and }\det(\mathrm{Im}(\tau))\geq\frac{1}{T}\Big\}

where
\mathcal{Z}
is the set defined in (
2.5
) and
H_{2g}
is the height defined in Definition
2.4
.

We want to prove the following upper bound for the cardinality of
\mathcal{Z}(T)
.

Proposition 6.1
.

Under the hypotheses of Theorem
1.1
, for all
\varepsilon>0
, we have
\#\mathcal{Z}(T)\ll_{\varepsilon}T^{\varepsilon}
, for all
T\geq 1
.

In order to prove this, consider the definable set
W
whose elements are tuples of the form

\displaystyle(\alpha_{1,1},\ldots,\alpha_{g,g},

\displaystyle\beta_{1,1},\ldots,\beta_{g,g},\mu_{1,1},\ldots,\mu_{1,g},\mu_{2,1},\ldots,\mu_{2,g},

\displaystyle x_{1,1},\ldots,x_{g,g},y_{1,1},\ldots,y_{g,g},u_{1},\ldots,u_{g},v_{1},\ldots,v_{g})

in
\mathbb{R}^{4g^{2}+2g}\times\mathbb{R}^{2g}
, satisfying the following relations:

M\neq\mathbf{0},\qquad\left(\tau,z\right)\in\mathcal{Z},\qquad Mz=\mu_{1}+\tau\mu_{2}

where

M=\left(\alpha_{i,j}+\mathbf{i}\beta_{i,j}\right)_{i,j=1,\ldots,g},\quad\mu_{1}=\left(\mu_{1,1},\ldots,\mu_{1,g}\right)^{t},\quad\mu_{2}=\left(\mu_{2,1},\ldots,\mu_{2,g}\right)^{t},

\tau=\left(x_{i,j}+\mathbf{i}y_{i,j}\right)_{i,j=1,\ldots,g},\quad z=\left(z_{1},\ldots,z_{g}\right)^{t}=\left(u_{1}+\mathbf{i}v_{1},\ldots,u_{g}+\mathbf{i}v_{g}\right)^{t}

and
\mathbf{i}
is the imaginary unit. In particular, for
T\geq 1
, let

W^{\sim}(2g,T):=\left\{(\alpha_{1,1},\ldots,v_{g})\in W:H_{2g}(\alpha_{1,1},\ldots,y_{g,g})\leq T\right\}.

Recall that
H_{2g}(\alpha_{1,1},\ldots,y_{g,g})
is finite if and only if
\alpha_{1,1},\ldots,y_{g,g}
are all algebraic numbers of degree at most
2g
.

Now, let
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}
be the projections on the first
4g^{2}+2g
and the last
2g
coordinates, respectively.

Lemma 6.2
.

Under the hypotheses of Theorem
1.1
, for every
\varepsilon>0
, we have

\#\pi_{2}\left(W^{\sim}(2g,T)\right)\ll_{\varepsilon}T^{\varepsilon}

for all
T\geq 1
.

Proof.

Consider an arbitrary
\varepsilon>0
and assume that for some
T_{0}\geq 1
,
\#\pi_{2}\left(W^{\sim}(2g,T_{0})\right)>cT_{0}^{\varepsilon}
, where
c=c(W,2g,\varepsilon)
is the constant given by Proposition
4.3
.

Then, by Proposition
4.3
, there exists a continuous definable function
\delta:\left[0,1\right]\rightarrow W
such that
\delta_{1}=\pi_{1}\circ\delta:\left[0,1\right]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{4g^{2}+2g}
is semi-algebraic and
\delta_{2}=\pi_{2}\circ\delta:\left[0,1\right]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2g}
is non-constant. Hence, there exists an infinite connected
J\subseteq\left[0,1\right]
such that
\delta_{1}(J)
is contained in an algebraic curve and
\delta_{2}(J)
has positive dimension.

Let
M,\tau,\mu_{1},\mu_{2},z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{g})^{t}
be as above and consider the coordinates

\displaystyle\alpha_{1,1},\ldots,\alpha_{g,g},

\displaystyle\beta_{1,1},\ldots,\beta_{g,g},\mu_{1,1},\ldots,\mu_{1,g},\mu_{2,1},\ldots,\mu_{2,g},

\displaystyle x_{1,1},\ldots,x_{g,g},y_{1,1},\ldots,y_{g,g},u_{1},\ldots,u_{g},v_{1},\ldots,v_{g}

as functions on
J
.

Note that
\tau
cannot be constant on
J
, otherwise there would be infinitely many points on
\mathcal{C}
(since
\delta_{2}(J)
has positive dimension) that lie on the same fiber, which contradicts the assumption that
\mathcal{C}
is not contained in any fiber.

Moreover, on
J
, the functions
\alpha_{1,1},\ldots,y_{g,g}
generate a field of transcendence degree at most 1 over
\mathbb{C}
, because they are functions on a curve. Therefore, on
J
,
\mathbb{C}(\tau)
is a field of transcendence degree 1 over
\mathbb{C}
and
\alpha_{1,1},\ldots,\mu_{2,g}\in\overline{\mathbb{C}(\tau)}
.
Since
M\neq\mathbf{0}
and
Mz=\mu_{1}+\tau\mu_{2}
, it follows that
z_{1},\ldots,z_{g}
are linearly dependent over
\overline{\mathbb{C}(\tau)}
. In particular,
z_{1},\ldots,z_{g}
are algebraically dependent over
F=\mathbb{C}\left(\tau\right)
on
J
.

Now, consider the set
\mathcal{W}=(\tau,z)(J)\subseteq\mathcal{Z}
. As the restriction of
\delta
to
(0,1)
is real analytic, we can view
\tau
and
z
as holomorphic functions on
u(\mathcal{W})\subseteq\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{C})
. Then,
\tau
and
z
satisfy an algebraic relation on
u(\mathcal{W})
which can be analytically continued to an open disc in
\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{C})
.

Therefore, we have
\mathrm{tr.deg.}_{F}F\left(z\right)<g
on an open disc in
\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{C})
, contradicting Lemma
2.2
and thus proving the proposition.
∎

Proof of Proposition
6.1
.

If
(\tau,z)\in\mathcal{Z}(T)
, then there exists a matrix

satisfying
H_{2g}(M)\leq T
, and vectors
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}^{g}
such that

Mz=\mu_{1}+\tau\mu_{2}.

If we write
M=\left(m_{i,j}\right)_{1\leq i,j\leq g}
and
\tau=\left(\tau_{i,j}\right)_{1\leq i,j\leq g}
, then, for every

\left|m_{i,j}\right|\leq\sqrt{2g+1}H_{2g}(M)\ll T,\qquad\left|\tau_{i,j}\right|\leq\sqrt{2g+1}H_{2g}(\tau)\ll T.

Furthermore, since
z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{g})\in L_{\tau}
, there exist
u,v\in[0,1)^{g}
such that

.
Thus, for each
i=1,\ldots,g

\left|z_{i}\right|=\left|u_{i}+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{g}\tau_{i,j}v_{j}\right|\leq 1+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{g}\left|\tau_{i,j}\right|\ll T.

As a consequence, for every
i=1,\ldots,g
we have

(6.1)

\left|\sum\limits_{j=1}^{g}m_{i,j}z_{j}\right|\leq\sum\limits_{j=1}^{g}\left|m_{i,j}\right|\left|z_{j}\right|\ll T^{2}.

Since
Mz=\mu_{1}+\tau\mu_{2}
, we have
\mathrm{Im}(\tau)\mu_{2}=\mathrm{Im}(Mz)
and thus

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mu_{2}\right\rVert_{\infty}=\left\lVert\mathrm{Im}(\tau)^{-1}\cdot\mathrm{Im}(Mz)\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq g\left\lVert\mathrm{Im}(\tau)^{-1}\right\rVert_{\infty}\cdot\left\lVert\mathrm{Im}(Mz)\right\rVert_{\infty}

and, by Lemma
5.5
, we get
\left\lVert\mathrm{Im}(\tau)^{-1}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq\frac{g^{g/2}}{\det(\mathrm{Im}(\tau))}\left\lVert\mathrm{Im}(\tau)\right\rVert_{\infty}^{g-1}\leq g^{g/2}\cdot T\cdot\left\lVert\tau\right\rVert_{\infty}^{g-1}\ll T^{g}
.
Hence, using (
6.1
), we obtain

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mu_{2}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq g\left\lVert\mathrm{Im}(\tau)^{-1}\right\rVert_{\infty}\cdot\left\lVert\mathrm{Im}(Mz)\right\rVert_{\infty}\ll T^{g}\cdot\left\lVert Mz\right\rVert_{\infty}\ll T^{g+2}.

Moreover, we have
\mu_{1}=Mz-\tau\mu_{2}
, so that

\left\lVert\mu_{1}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq\left\lVert Mz\right\rVert_{\infty}+\left\lVert\tau\mu_{2}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq\left\lVert Mz\right\rVert_{\infty}+g\left\lVert\tau\right\rVert_{\infty}\cdot\left\lVert\mu_{2}\right\rVert_{\infty}\ll T^{2}+T\cdot T^{g+2}\ll T^{g+3}.

This allows us to deduce that

\left(\mathrm{Re}(M),\mathrm{Im}(M),\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\mathrm{Re}(\tau),\mathrm{Im}(\tau),\mathrm{Re}(z),\mathrm{Im}(z)\right)\in W^{\sim}(2g,\nu T^{g+3})

for some positive constant
\nu
. Then, by Lemma
6.3
, each element of
\pi_{2}(W^{\sim}(2g,\nu T^{g+3}))
corresponds to at most
c^{\prime}
distinct elements of
\mathcal{Z}(T)
. Finally, the proof follows from Lemma
6.2
.
∎

7.
Canonical height bounds under endomorphisms

Let
A
be an abelian variety of dimension
g
defined over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
,
D
be a symmetric divisor on
A
, and
\widehat{h}_{A,D}
the canonical height on
A(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
associated with
D
(see Section
2.3
).

As mentioned in the introduction, our aim is to generalize the usual identity
\widehat{h}_{A,D}([n]P)=n^{2}\cdot\widehat{h}_{A,D}(P)
to general endomorphisms of
A
.

It was noted by Naumann
[
47
]
that, if
\en^{0}(A)
is
\mathbb{Q}
, an imaginary quadratic field or a definite quaternion algebra over
\mathbb{Q}
, and if
D
is an ample symmetric divisor, then

\widehat{h}_{A,D}(f(P))=\left(\deg f\right)^{1/g}\cdot\widehat{h}_{A,D}(P)

for any
f\in\en(A)
and any
P\in A(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
, recovering a well known fact for elliptic curves.

In general, however, we cannot expect an identity of this form, as illustrated by the following example.
Consider
A=E\times E
, where
E
is any elliptic curve with identity element
O
, and let
D=\left(O\times E\right)+\left(E\times O\right)
. Define the endomorphism
f:A\rightarrow A
by

f(P_{1},P_{2})=(P_{1},2P_{2}).

Since we can write
D=\pi_{1}^{*}(O)+\pi_{2}^{*}(O)
, where
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
are the projections onto the two factors, we obtain

\widehat{h}_{A,D}(P_{1},P_{2})=\widehat{h}_{E,(O)}(\pi_{1}(P_{1},P_{2}))+\widehat{h}_{E,(O)}(\pi_{2}(P_{1},P_{2}))=\widehat{h}_{E,(O)}(P_{1})+\widehat{h}_{E,(O)}(P_{2})

by applying Proposition
2.6
. Choosing either
P_{1}=O
or
P_{2}=O
, we conclude that there is no constant
\gamma
such that

\widehat{h}_{A,D}(f(P))=\widehat{h}_{E,(O)}(P_{1})+4\widehat{h}_{E,(O)}(P_{2})=\gamma\cdot\left(\widehat{h}_{E,(O)}(P_{1})+\widehat{h}_{E,(O)}(P_{2})\right)=\gamma\cdot\widehat{h}_{A,D}(P)

for every
P=(P_{1},P_{2})\in A(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
.
Nonetheless, since the divisor
(O)
is ample, it follows that

\widehat{h}_{A,D}(P)\leq\widehat{h}_{A,D}(f(P))\leq 4\widehat{h}_{A,D}(P).

More generally, if
D
is ample and symmetric, there exist constants
0\leq\gamma_{1}\leq\gamma_{2}
such that

\gamma_{1}\cdot\widehat{h}_{A,D}(P)\leq\widehat{h}_{A,D}(f(P))\leq\gamma_{2}\cdot\widehat{h}_{A,D}(P).

In particular, as anticipated in the introduction, one must take
\gamma_{1}=0
if
f
is not an isogeny, while
\gamma_{1}
can be chosen strictly positive when
f
is an isogeny. We now provide the proof of this fact.

To prove the upper bound, recall that since
D
is ample, there exists an integer
N_{2}>0
such that
nD-f^{*}D
is ample for all
n\geq N_{2}
, see for instance
[
34
, Example 1.2.10]
.
This implies

N_{2}\cdot\widehat{h}_{A,D}(P)-\widehat{h}_{A,D}(f(P))=\widehat{h}_{A,N_{2}D-f^{*}D}(P)\geq 0

giving the upper bound with
\gamma_{2}=N_{2}
.

For the lower bound, first observe that if
f
is not finite, then the dimension of
\ker(f)
is positive and, in particular, there is a non-torsion point
P\in A(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
for which
f(P)=O
. Therefore, we must have
\gamma_{1}=0
in this case. On the other hand, if
f
is finite then
f^{*}D
is ample. Thus, as before, there exists an integer
N_{1}>0
such that
nf^{*}D-D
is ample for any
n\geq N_{1}
. This means that

N_{1}\cdot\widehat{h}_{A,D}(f(P))-\widehat{h}_{A,D}(P)=\widehat{h}_{A,N_{1}f^{*}D-D}(P)\geq 0

from which we deduce the lower bound, with
\gamma_{1}=\frac{1}{N_{1}}>0
.

If
f
is an isogeny, the existence of these bounds also follows from Theorem B in
[
35
]
.

Unfortunately, this method does not provide effective values for
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
, although explicit computations may be possible for specific choices of
D
and
f
.

However, as mentioned in the introduction, we are able to provide general explicit values for
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
. As before, let
†
be the Rosati involution associated to the divisor
D
(or, more formally, to the line bundle
\mathcal{O}(D)
) and let
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{g}
be the eigenvalues (counted with multiplicities) of
\rho_{a}(f^{\dagger}f)
. We will prove in Lemma
7.3
that these eigenvalues are real and non-negative. As above, we set

\alpha^{-}_{D}(f)=\min\left\{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{g}\right\}\quad\text{ and }\quad\alpha^{+}_{D}(f)=\max\left\{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{g}\right\}.

We repeat the statement of the main result of this section, already mentioned in the introduction (Theorem
1.4
).

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
A
be an abelian variety defined over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
, and let
D
be an ample symmetric divisor on
A
. Then, for every endomorphism
f:A\rightarrow A
, we have

\alpha^{-}_{D}(f)\cdot\widehat{h}_{A,D}(P)\leq\widehat{h}_{A,D}(f(P))\leq\alpha^{+}_{D}(f)\cdot\widehat{h}_{A,D}(P)

for every
P\in A(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
. Moreover, these constants are the best possible, meaning that we cannot replace
\alpha^{+}_{D}(f)
and
\alpha^{-}_{D}(f)
with a smaller and a larger constant, respectively.

Remark 7.2
.

Note that the ampleness hypothesis for
D
is necessary, as shown by the following example. As in Remark
2.7
, consider
A=E\times E
, where
E
is any elliptic curve with identity element
O
, and let
D=\pi_{1}^{*}(O)-\pi_{2}^{*}(O)
. Observe that
D
is symmetric but not ample. As above, by Proposition
2.6
, we get

\widehat{h}_{A,D}(P_{1},P_{2})=\widehat{h}_{E,(O)}(\pi_{1}(P_{1},P_{2}))-\widehat{h}_{E,(O)}(\pi_{2}(P_{1},P_{2}))=\widehat{h}_{E,(O)}(P_{1})-\widehat{h}_{E,(O)}(P_{2}).

If
g\in\en(A)
is given by
g(P_{1},P_{2})=(2P_{1},P_{2})
, then we get that

\widehat{h}_{A,D}(g(P))=4\widehat{h}_{E,(O)}(P_{1})-\widehat{h}_{E,(O)}(P_{2})

for every
P=(P_{1},P_{2})\in A(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
, and it is easy to see that there is no positive constant
\gamma
such that

\widehat{h}_{A,D}(g(P))\leq\gamma\cdot\widehat{h}_{A,D}(P)

for every
P=(P_{1},P_{2})\in A(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
.

7.1.
Properties of endomorphisms and line bundles of abelian varieties

Fix an ample divisor
D
on
A=\mathbb{C}^{g}/\Lambda
and let
L=\mathcal{O}_{A}(D)
be the associated line bundle. In the following,
†
denotes the Rosati involution induced by the polarization
\Phi_{L}
corresponding to
L
.

We start with a classical result about the eigenvalues of
\rho_{a}(f^{\dagger}f)
. The following proof is inspired by an argument by Masser and Wüstholz
[
37
]
.

Lemma 7.3
.

Let
f\in\en^{0}(A)
. Then all the eigenvalues of
\rho_{a}(f^{\dagger}f)
are real and non-negative. If
f\neq 0
, then at least one eigenvalue is positive.

Proof.

By Proposition 5.1.1 of
[
7
]
, we have that
H_{L}(\rho_{a}(f)v,w)=H_{L}(v,\rho_{a}(f^{\dagger})w)
, for every
v,w\in\mathbb{C}^{g}
, where
H_{L}:\mathbb{C}^{g}\times\mathbb{C}^{g}\to\mathbb{C}
is the Hermitian form associated with the ample line bundle
L
. Thus, if
\mathcal{H}_{L}
is the matrix representing
H_{L}
, we have

\rho_{a}(f^{\dagger})=\left(\overline{\mathcal{H}_{L}}\right)^{-1}\cdot\overline{\rho_{a}(f)}^{t}\cdot\overline{\mathcal{H}_{L}}

where
\overline{M}^{t}
is the conjugate transpose of the matrix
M
.

Since
L
is ample,
H_{L}
is positive definite, and therefore there is an invertible matrix
S
such that
\overline{\mathcal{H}_{L}}=\overline{S}^{t}S
. Thus, we have

\rho_{a}(f^{\dagger}f)=\left(\overline{\mathcal{H}_{L}}\right)^{-1}\cdot\overline{\rho_{a}(f)}^{t}\cdot\overline{\mathcal{H}_{L}}\cdot\rho_{a}\left(f\right)=S^{-1}(\overline{S}^{t})^{-1}\overline{\rho_{a}(f)}^{t}\overline{S}^{t}S\rho_{a}(f).

By setting
X=S\cdot\rho_{a}(f)\cdot S^{-1}
, we have that

\rho_{a}(f^{\dagger}f)=S^{-1}(\overline{S}^{t})^{-1}\cdot\overline{\rho_{a}(f)}^{t}\cdot\overline{S}^{t}S\cdot\rho_{a}(f)\cdot S^{-1}S=S^{-1}\overline{X}^{t}XS,

proving that every eigenvalue of
\rho_{a}(f^{\dagger}f)
is real and non-negative, since
\overline{X}^{t}X
is a positive semidefinite matrix and eigenvalues are invariant under change of basis. In particular, as Hermitian matrices are diagonalizable, this also implies that
\overline{X}^{t}X
cannot have all zero eigenvalues unless it is the zero matrix. However, if
X
has entries
x_{i,j}\in\mathbb{C}
and
\overline{X}^{t}X=\mathbf{0}
, then
0=\tr(\overline{X}^{t}X)=\sum_{i,j=1}^{g}\left|x_{i,j}\right|^{2}
, which implies that
X=S\cdot\rho_{a}(f)\cdot S^{-1}=\mathbf{0}
and thus
\rho_{a}(f)=\mathbf{0}
.
∎

Notice that for
f\in\en(A)
, the matrix
\rho_{a}(f^{\dagger}f)
has only positive eigenvalues if and only if
X
is invertible, which is the case precisely when
\rho_{a}(f)
is invertible, i.e. when
f
is an isogeny.

Denote by
P_{f^{\dagger}f}^{a}(x)
and
P_{f^{\dagger}f}^{r}(x)
the characteristic polynomial of
f^{\dagger}f
with respect to the analytic and the rational representations, respectively.
Using
[
7
, Proposition 5.1.2]
and the previous lemma,
P_{f^{\dagger}f}^{a}
and
P_{f^{\dagger}f}^{r}
are real polynomials and we have

(7.1)

P_{f^{\dagger}f}^{r}(x)=\left(P_{f^{\dagger}f}^{a}(x)\right)^{2}.

With these notations, we have the following generalization of Lemma 2.1 of
[
33
]
(see also
[
7
, Proposition 5.1.6]
).

Lemma 7.4
.

Let
L
be an ample line bundle,
f\in\en(A)
and
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}
, with
b>0
. Then,

\chi(f^{*}L^{-b}\otimes L^{a})=\chi(L)\cdot b^{g}\cdot P_{f^{\dagger}f}^{a}\!\left(\dfrac{a}{b}\right).

Proof.

Fix
b>0
an integer. By Corollary 3.6.2 of
[
7
]
, we have

\chi(f^{*}L^{-b}\otimes L^{a})^{2}=\deg(\Phi_{f^{*}L^{-b}\otimes L^{a}})

where the map
\Phi_{L}
was defined in (
2.2
). By
[
7
, Corollary 2.4.6]
we have

\Phi_{f^{*}L^{-b}\otimes L^{a}}=-[b]\Phi_{f^{*}L}+[a]\Phi_{L}\quad\text{and}\quad\Phi_{f^{*}L}=\widehat{f}\Phi_{L}f=\Phi_{L}f^{\dagger}f.

Then, recalling that for every
\varphi\in\en(A)
,
\deg(\varphi)=\det(\rho_{r}(\varphi))

[
7
, eq. (1.2)]
, we get

\displaystyle\chi(f^{*}L^{-b}\otimes L^{a})^{2}

\displaystyle=\deg\left(-[b]\Phi_{L}f^{\dagger}f+[a]\Phi_{L}\right)

\displaystyle=\deg\Phi_{L}\cdot\deg\left(-[b]f^{\dagger}f+[a]\right)

\displaystyle=\deg\Phi_{L}\cdot\det\left(\rho_{r}\!\left(-[b]\cdot f^{\dagger}f+[a]\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\deg\Phi_{L}\cdot\det\left(-b\cdot\rho_{r}(f^{\dagger}f)+a\cdot\mathbf{1}_{2g}\right)

\displaystyle=\deg\Phi_{L}\cdot b^{2g}\cdot\det\left(-\rho_{r}(f^{\dagger}f)+\dfrac{a}{b}\cdot\mathbf{1}_{2g}\right)

\displaystyle=\chi(L)^{2}\cdot b^{2g}\cdot P_{f^{\dagger}f}^{r}\left(\dfrac{a}{b}\right)=\chi(L)^{2}\cdot b^{2g}\cdot\left(P_{f^{\dagger}f}^{a}\!\left(\dfrac{a}{b}\right)\right)^{2}

by Equation (
7.1
). Here

is the

\chi(f^{*}L^{-b}\otimes L^{a})=\pm\chi(L)\cdot b^{g}\cdot P_{f^{\dagger}f}^{a}\!\left(\dfrac{a}{b}\right).

Fix

arbitrary. Since

is ample, we have
\chi(L)>0
. Moreover, for all sufficiently large

, the divisor

is ample by Kleiman’s criterion, hence
\chi(f^{*}L^{-b}\otimes L^{a})>0
. Finally, since

is a monic polynomial (see
[
7
, after proof of Proposition 5.1.2]
),
P_{f^{\dagger}f}^{a}\!\left(\frac{a}{b}\right)
is also positive for all sufficiently large

, completing the proof.
∎

2

and

(iii)

\displaystyle H^{k}(A,M^{\prime})=0,\quad

Finally, we have the following characterization of ample line bundles.

.

[

7.2.
Proof of Theorem
7.1

Given an abelian variety
A
of dimension
g
defined over a number field, an ample symmetric divisor
D
and
f\in\en(A)
, let
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{g}
be the eigenvalues (counted with multiplicities) of
\rho_{a}(f^{\dagger}f)
, where the Rosati involution is defined with respect to the polarization
L=\mathcal{O}_{A}(D)
.

Define
\alpha^{-}_{D}(f)=\min\left\{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{g}\right\}
and
\alpha^{+}_{D}(f)=\max\left\{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{g}\right\}
, as before. Notice that, by Lemma
7.3
,
\alpha^{-}_{D}(f)
is non-negative and it is positive if and only if
f
is surjective, which is compatible with what we said before. Moreover,
\alpha^{+}_{D}(f)>0
for every
f\neq 0
.

Proof of Theorem
7.1
.

The claim is trivially true for
f=0
, so we will assume that
f\neq 0
for the rest of the proof. Let
\lambda=\frac{a}{b}
be a rational number, with
b>0
, and let
L
be the line bundle associated to
D
. As above, consider
L
as a polarization on
A
and define the Rosati involution with respect to this line bundle.

We start by proving the upper bound.
Consider the line bundle
M=f^{*}L^{-b}\otimes L^{a}
. Then, for every
n\in\mathbb{Z}
,

P_{L,M}(n)=\chi(L^{n}\otimes M)=\chi(f^{*}L^{-b}\otimes L^{n+a})=\chi(L)\cdot b^{g}\cdot P_{f^{\dagger}f}^{a}\!\left(\dfrac{n+a}{b}\right)

by Lemma
7.4
. Thus, we have

\displaystyle P_{L,M}(x)

\displaystyle=\chi(L)\cdot b^{g}\cdot P_{f^{\dagger}f}^{a}\!\left(\dfrac{x+a}{b}\right)

\displaystyle=\chi(L)\cdot b^{g}\cdot\prod\limits_{i=1}^{g}\left(\dfrac{x+a}{b}-\alpha_{i}\right)

\displaystyle=\chi(L)\cdot\prod\limits_{i=1}^{g}\left(x-(b\alpha_{i}-a)\right).

Combining Proposition
7.6
and Theorem
7.5
, we obtain that
M
is ample if and only if all the roots of
P_{L,M}
are negative, which is equivalent to say that
\frac{a}{b}>\alpha_{i}
for every
i=1,\ldots,g
.

This implies that if
\lambda=\frac{a}{b}>\alpha^{+}_{D}(f)
, then the divisor
aD-bf^{*}D
is ample and symmetric and therefore, by Proposition
2.6
, we have

a\cdot\widehat{h}_{A,D}(P)-b\cdot\widehat{h}_{A,D}(f(P))=a\cdot\widehat{h}_{A,D}(P)-b\cdot\widehat{h}_{A,f^{*}D}(P)=\widehat{h}_{A,aD-bf^{*}D}(P)\geq 0

for every
P\in A(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
, which is equivalent to
\widehat{h}_{A,D}(f(P))\leq\lambda\cdot\widehat{h}_{A,D}(P)
. Since this is true for every
\lambda\in\mathbb{Q}
such that
\lambda>\alpha^{+}_{D}(f)
, this implies that
\widehat{h}_{A,D}(f(P))\leq\alpha^{+}_{D}(f)\cdot\widehat{h}_{A,D}(P)
.

In order to prove the lower bound, we consider the line bundle
M=f^{*}L^{b}\otimes L^{-a}
. By Theorem
7.5
and Proposition
7.6
,
M
is ample if and only if
K(M)
is finite and
H^{g}(A,M^{-1})\neq 0
. Using Lemma
7.4
as before, we get that

P_{L,M^{-1}}(x)=\chi(L)\cdot b^{g}\cdot P_{f^{\dagger}f}^{a}\!\left(\dfrac{x+a}{b}\right)=\chi(L)\cdot\prod\limits_{i=1}^{g}\left(x-(b\alpha_{i}-a)\right).

By
[
7
, Lemma 2.4.7(c)]
,
K(M)=K(M^{-1})
, and Theorem
7.5
implies that
K(M^{-1})
is finite and
H^{g}(A,M^{-1})\neq 0
if and only if all the roots of
P_{L,M^{-1}}
are positive, that is, if and only if
\frac{a}{b}<\alpha_{i}
for every
i=1,\ldots,g
.

Again, this means that for every
\lambda=\frac{a}{b}<\alpha^{-}_{D}(f)
, the divisor
bf^{*}D-aD
is ample and symmetric and thus we have

b\cdot\widehat{h}_{A,D}(f(P))-a\cdot\widehat{h}_{A,D}(P)=b\cdot\widehat{h}_{A,f^{*}D}(P)-a\cdot\widehat{h}_{A,D}(P)=\widehat{h}_{A,bf^{*}D-aD}(P)\geq 0

for every
P\in A(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
, which is equivalent to
\widehat{h}_{A,D}(f(P))\geq\lambda\cdot\widehat{h}_{A,D}(P)
. Since this is true for every
\lambda\in\mathbb{Q}
such that
\lambda<\alpha^{-}_{D}(f)
, this implies that
\widehat{h}_{A,D}(f(P))\geq\alpha^{-}_{D}(f)\cdot\widehat{h}_{A,D}(P)
.

We now prove that the constants
\alpha^{-}_{D}(f),\alpha^{+}_{D}(f)
are optimal.

Consider the
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
\lambda D-f^{*}D
. Observe that the proof above shows that
\lambda D-f^{*}D
is ample if and only if
\lambda>\alpha^{+}_{D}(f)
.
From this we deduce that, if
\lambda\in\mathbb{Q}
and
\lambda<\alpha^{+}_{D}(f)
, then
\lambda D-f^{*}D
is not nef. Otherwise,
(\lambda+\varepsilon)D-f^{*}D
would be ample for every
\varepsilon>0

[
34
, Corollary 1.4.10]
, which is impossible for
\varepsilon
small enough.

Then, assume that
0\leq\widetilde{\alpha}<\alpha^{+}_{D}(f)
is such that
\widehat{h}_{A,D}(f(P))\leq\widetilde{\alpha}\cdot\widehat{h}_{A,D}(P)
for every
P\in A(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
. Without loss of generality we can assume that
\widetilde{\alpha}
is rational. Then, since

is ample,
f^{*}D
is nef and, thus,

is ample. Therefore, we have that

\widehat{h}_{A,f^{*}D+D}(P)\leq\widehat{h}_{A,(\widetilde{\alpha}+1)D}(P)

from which we can deduce, using
[
35
, Lemma 4.1]
, that

(\widetilde{\alpha}+1)D-\left(f^{*}D+D\right)=\widetilde{\alpha}D-f^{*}D

is nef, which is impossible.

A similar argument, using the
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
f^{*}D-\lambda D
, shows that one cannot have

\widehat{h}_{A,D}(f(P))\geq\widetilde{\alpha}\cdot\widehat{h}_{A,D}(P)

for some
\widetilde{\alpha}>\alpha^{-}_{D}(f)
and every
P\in A(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
.
∎

Remark 7.7
.

Assume that
A
is simple. If the endomorphism algebra
\en^{0}(A)
is a totally real number field, a totally definite quaternion algebra or a CM field, then the Albert classification
[
46
, Theorem 2 (p.186)]
implies that there is a unique positive involution on
\en^{0}(A)
. Thus, the Rosati involution associated with any line bundle must be equal to this unique positive involution. Hence, this proves that in those cases the constants
\alpha^{-}_{D}(f),\alpha^{+}_{D}(f)
do not depend on
D
, generalizing the above-mentioned result by Naumann.

Since all the eigenvalues of
\rho_{a}(f^{\dagger}f)
are real and non-negative,

\tr(\rho_{a}(f^{\dagger}f))=\alpha_{1}+\ldots+\alpha_{g}\geq\max\left\{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{g}\right\}=\alpha^{+}_{D}(f)

so we also have the following consequence.

Corollary 7.8
.

Fix an abelian variety
A
defined over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
with an ample symmetric divisor
D
. Then, for every endomorphism
f:A\rightarrow A
, we have that

\widehat{h}_{A,D}(f(P))\leq\tr(\rho_{a}(f^{\dagger}f))\cdot\widehat{h}_{A,D}(P)

for every
P\in A(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
.

7.3.
Height bounds for homomorphisms between abelian varieties

We can now generalize Theorem
7.1
to homomorphisms between different abelian varieties.

Let
A,B
be two abelian varieties defined over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
,
D_{1},D_{2}
be two ample symmetric divisors on
A
and
B
, respectively, and let
\phi:A\rightarrow B
be a homomorphism. As before, it is straightforward to see that the ratio
\widehat{h}_{B,D_{2}}(\phi(P))/\widehat{h}_{A,D_{1}}(P)
must be bounded for non-torsion points
P\in A(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
(see, for example,
[
41
, Lemma 16]
for the upper bound). However, if
\ker\phi
is not finite, then there exists a non-torsion
P\in A(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
such that
\phi(P)=O_{B}
, showing that there is no positive constant
\gamma_{1}
such that
\widehat{h}_{B,D_{2}}(\phi(P))\geq\gamma_{1}\cdot\widehat{h}_{A,D_{1}}(P)
.

Theorem 7.9
.

Let
A,B
be two abelian varieties defined over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
and consider two ample symmetric divisors
D_{1},D_{2}
on
A
and
B
, respectively. Let also
\phi:A\rightarrow B
be a nonzero homomorphism. Then there is an explicit constant
\gamma_{2}>0
such that

\widehat{h}_{B,D_{2}}(\phi(P))\leq\gamma_{2}\cdot\widehat{h}_{A,D_{1}}(P)

for every
P\in A(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
. Moreover, if
\phi
is an isogeny, there exists an explicit constant
\gamma_{1}>0
such that

\widehat{h}_{B,D_{2}}(\phi(P))\geq\gamma_{1}\cdot\widehat{h}_{A,D_{1}}(P)

for every
P\in A(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
.

Proof.

If
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}
are the projections of
A\times B
onto
A
and
B
respectively, we consider the divisor
D=\pi_{1}^{*}D_{1}+\pi_{2}^{*}D_{2}
on
A\times B
, which is again ample and symmetric.

By the functorial properties of the canonical height, we have that

\widehat{h}_{A\times B,D}(P,Q)=\widehat{h}_{A\times B,\pi_{1}^{*}D_{1}}(P,Q)+\widehat{h}_{A\times B,\pi_{2}^{*}D_{2}}(P,Q)=\widehat{h}_{A,D_{1}}(P)+\widehat{h}_{B,D_{2}}(Q)

for every
(P,Q)\in(A\times B)(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
.

Let also
f
be the endomorphism of
A\times B
defined as
f(P,Q)=(O_{A},\phi(P))
.
We can then apply Theorem
7.1
to get that

\displaystyle\widehat{h}_{B,D_{2}}(\phi(P))

\displaystyle=\widehat{h}_{A\times B,D}(f(P,Q))

\displaystyle\leq\alpha_{D}^{+}(f)\cdot\widehat{h}_{A\times B,D}(P,Q)=\alpha_{D}^{+}(f)\cdot\left(\widehat{h}_{A,D_{1}}(P)+\widehat{h}_{B,D_{2}}(Q)\right).

Since this inequality holds for arbitrary
P\in A(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
and
Q\in B(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
, we can choose
Q=O_{B}
and thus we have

\widehat{h}_{B,D_{2}}(\phi(P))\leq\alpha_{D}^{+}(f)\cdot\widehat{h}_{A,D_{1}}(P)

so that we can choose
\gamma_{2}=\alpha_{D}^{+}(f)
.

Now assume that
\phi
is an isogeny, and let
e(\phi)
be the exponent of the finite group
\ker\phi
, i.e.
e(\phi)
is the smallest positive integer
n
such that
[n]P=O_{A}
for every
P\in\ker\phi
. Then, by
[
7
, Proposition 1.2.6]
, there exists a unique isogeny
\psi:B\rightarrow A
such that
\psi\circ\phi=[e(\phi)]_{A}
and
\phi\circ\psi=[e(\phi)]_{B}
. We then apply Theorem
7.1
to the endomorphism
g
of
A\times B
such that
g(P,Q)=(\psi(Q),O_{B})
in order to get

\displaystyle\widehat{h}_{A,D_{1}}(\psi(Q))

\displaystyle=\widehat{h}_{A\times B,D}(g(P,Q))

\displaystyle\leq\alpha_{D}^{+}(g)\cdot\widehat{h}_{A\times B,D}(P,Q)=\alpha_{D}^{+}(g)\cdot\left(\widehat{h}_{A,D_{1}}(P)+\widehat{h}_{B,D_{2}}(Q)\right).

As before, this implies that

\widehat{h}_{A,D_{1}}(\psi(Q))\leq\alpha_{D}^{+}(g)\cdot\widehat{h}_{B,D_{2}}(Q)

for every
Q\in B(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
. Then, for each
P\in A(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
we can choose
Q=\phi(P)
. Thus, the inequality above becomes

e(\phi)^{2}\cdot\widehat{h}_{A,D_{1}}(P)=\widehat{h}_{A,D_{1}}((\psi\circ\phi)(P))\leq\alpha_{D}^{+}(g)\cdot\widehat{h}_{B,D_{2}}(\phi(P))

since
D_{1}
is symmetric. Therefore, we can take
\gamma_{1}=\frac{e(\phi)^{2}}{\alpha_{D}^{+}(g)}
.
∎

Applying this theorem with
B=A
and
\phi=\left[1\right]
the identity gives the following comparison of canonical heights defined by different divisors (see also
[
27
, Exercise B.3]
for a slightly more general but ineffective statement).

Corollary 7.10
.

Let
A
be an abelian variety defined over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
and consider two ample symmetric divisors
D_{1},D_{2}
on
A
. Then there are explicit constants
0<\gamma_{1}\leq\gamma_{2}
such that

\gamma_{1}\cdot\widehat{h}_{A,D_{1}}(P)\leq\widehat{h}_{A,D_{2}}(P)\leq\gamma_{2}\cdot\widehat{h}_{A,D_{1}}(P)

for every
P\in A(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
.

Lastly, we consider the special case of elliptic curves. Given an elliptic curve
E
, a symmetric ample divisor
D
and an endomorphism
f\in\en(E)
, we clearly have
\alpha_{D}^{-}(f)=\alpha_{D}^{+}(f)=\deg f
, since
f^{\dagger}=\widehat{f}
. Thus, Theorem
7.1
reduces to the well known identity
\widehat{h}_{E,D}(f(P))=\deg f\cdot\widehat{h}_{E,D}(P)
(see for example Section 3.6 of
[
55
]
).

However, for elliptic curves we may strengthen Theorem
7.9
, getting again an identity instead of an inequality. We prove this using a different method from the one used before.

Proposition 7.11
.

Let
E_{1},E_{2}
be two elliptic curves defined over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
,
D_{1},D_{2}
be two ample symmetric divisors on
E_{1},E_{2}
, respectively, and
f:E_{1}\rightarrow E_{2}
be an isogeny. Then, we have

\widehat{h}_{E_{2},D_{2}}(f(P))=\dfrac{\deg D_{2}}{\deg D_{1}}\cdot\deg f\cdot\widehat{h}_{E_{1},D_{1}}(P)

for every
P\in E_{1}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
.

Proof.

Let
a=\deg D_{2}\cdot\deg f
and
b=\deg D_{1}
. Then, we have

\deg(aD_{1}-bf^{*}D_{2})=a\cdot\deg D_{1}-b\cdot\deg f\cdot\deg D_{2}=0.

So the divisor
aD_{1}-bf^{*}D_{2}
on
E_{1}
is nef. As shown in Proposition
2.6
, the canonical height associated to a nef symmetric divisor is nonnegative, therefore

\displaystyle a\cdot\widehat{h}_{E_{1},D_{1}}(P)-b\cdot\widehat{h}_{E_{2},D_{2}}(f(P))

\displaystyle=\widehat{h}_{E_{1},aD_{1}}(P)-\widehat{h}_{E_{1},bf^{*}D_{2}}(P)

\displaystyle=\widehat{h}_{E_{1},aD_{1}-b\cdot f^{*}D_{2}}(P)\geq 0

implying that

\widehat{h}_{E_{2},D_{2}}(f(P))\leq\dfrac{\deg D_{2}}{\deg D_{1}}\cdot\deg f\cdot\widehat{h}_{E_{1},D_{1}}(P)

since ample divisors on curves have positive degree
[
26
, Corollary 3.3]
.

Similarly,
\deg(bf^{*}D_{2}-aD_{1})=0
, so that the same argument gives

\widehat{h}_{E_{2},D_{2}}(f(P))\geq\dfrac{\deg D_{2}}{\deg D_{1}}\cdot\deg f\cdot\widehat{h}_{E_{1},D_{1}}(P)

concluding the proof.
∎

Remark 7.12
.

Since any ample symmetric divisor on an elliptic curve is linearly equivalent to
n(O)+(T)
, where
O
is the identity element,
n\geq 0
is an integer and
T
is a 2-torsion point, one can also prove Proposition
7.11
more directly, by explicitly computing the pull-back
f^{*}(n(O)+(T))
(see for example
[
17
, Proposition 2.3]
for the special case
D_{1}=3(O_{1})
and
D_{2}=3(O_{2})
).

8.
A height inequality

The aim of this section is to give a bound on the canonical height of the points
P\in\mathcal{C}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
in terms of the Faltings height
h_{F}(\mathcal{A}_{\pi(P)})
of the corresponding fiber. In order to do that we recall the setting of Theorem
1.1
and the reductions made in Section
3
, and also define some height functions that will be used to prove this bound.

Let
S\subseteq\mathbb{A}_{g}=\mathbb{A}_{g,\mathbf{1},3}
be a smooth, irreducible, locally closed curve defined over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
, let
\mathcal{A}=\mathfrak{A}_{g}\times_{\mathbb{A}_{g}}S
, with
\pi:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow S
being the structural morphism, and let
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{A}
be an irreducible curve as in Theorem
1.1
. Recall that
\mathcal{A}
has a level-3-structure and that there is a principal polarization
\lambda:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\widehat{\mathcal{A}}
, where
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}
denotes the dual abelian scheme of
\mathcal{A}
.

By
[
22
, Proposition 27.284]
, the pullback of the Poincaré bundle
\mathscr{P}
via the morphism
(\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{A}},\lambda)
is relatively ample. Thus, the line bundle

\mathcal{L}=\left((\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{A}},\lambda)^{*}\mathscr{P}\otimes[-1]_{\mathcal{A}}^{*}(\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{A}},\lambda)^{*}\mathscr{P}\right)^{\otimes 3}

is relatively very ample (see
[
22
, Theorem 27.279]
), symmetric and its associated isogeny
\Phi_{\mathcal{L}}
is equal to
12\lambda
. This line bundle gives an embedding
\mathcal{A}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{n}_{S}\cong\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}\times S
. Moreover, for every fiber
\mathcal{A}_{s}
of
\mathcal{A}\rightarrow S
, the induced closed immersion
\mathcal{A}_{s}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}
comes from the restriction
\mathcal{L}_{s}=\mathcal{L}|_{\mathcal{A}_{s}}
.

The minimal compactification
\overline{\mathbb{A}_{g,\mathbf{1},3}}
of
\mathbb{A}_{g,\mathbf{1},3}
can be realized as a closed subvariety of some projective space
\mathbb{P}^{m}_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}
and we define
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{m}}(1)|_{\overline{\mathbb{A}_{g,\mathbf{1},3}}}
. Thus, we obtain an embedding
\mathbb{A}_{g,\mathbf{1},3}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{m}_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}
and we denote by
\overline{S}
the Zariski closure of
S
in
\overline{\mathbb{A}_{g,\mathbf{1},3}}\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{m}_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}
.

We then denote by
\overline{\mathcal{A}}
the Zariski closure of
\mathcal{A}
inside
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}\times\overline{S}\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}\times\mathbb{P}^{m}_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}
and let
\overline{\mathcal{L}}=\mathcal{O}(1,1)|_{\overline{\mathcal{A}}}=\mathcal{L}\otimes\pi^{*}\!\left(\mathcal{M}|_{\overline{S}}\right)
. Using the properties of the Weil height (e.g.
[
27
, Theorem B.3.6]
), we define the
naive height
on
\mathcal{A}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
as

h_{\overline{\mathcal{A}},\overline{\mathcal{L}}}(P)=h_{\mathcal{A}_{\pi(P)},\mathcal{L}_{\pi(P)}}(P)+h_{\overline{S},\mathcal{M}|_{\overline{S}}}(\pi(P)).

Moreover, as
\mathcal{L}
is symmetric, we can also define a fiberwise canonical height
\widehat{h}_{\mathcal{A}_{\pi(P)},\mathcal{L}_{\pi(P)}}(P)
as in Section
2.3
.

Furthermore, recall that the coarse moduli space
\mathbb{A}_{g,\mathbf{1}}
of principally polarized abelian varieties of dimension
g
is a quasi-projective variety. More precisely, its minimal compactification
\overline{\mathbb{A}_{g,\mathbf{1}}}
can be realized as a closed subvariety of some projective space
\mathbb{P}^{\ell}_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}
.

Let
L=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{\ell}}(1)|_{\overline{\mathbb{A}_{g,\mathbf{1}}}}
. Then, by
[
15
, Section II.3]
,
L
has an Hermitian metric on
\mathbb{A}_{g,\mathbf{1}}
with logarithmic singularities along
\overline{\mathbb{A}_{g,\mathbf{1}}}\setminus\mathbb{A}_{g,\mathbf{1}}
. Hence, we can define two height functions:
h_{L}
on
\mathbb{A}_{g,\mathbf{1}}
using the metric cited just now; and
\widetilde{h}_{L}
on
\overline{\mathbb{A}_{g,\mathbf{1}}}
given by the Hermitian metric which at the archimedean places is the standard Fubini–Study metric coming from the embedding of
\overline{\mathbb{A}_{g,\mathbf{1}}}
into
\mathbb{P}^{\ell}_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}
and at the non-archimedean places is the usual metric. Note that
\widetilde{h}_{L}
differs from a fixed Weil height
h_{\overline{\mathbb{A}_{g,\mathbf{1}}},L}
by a bounded function on
\mathbb{P}^{\ell}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
(see
[
8
, Remark 2.8.3]
or
[
27
, Example B.10.5]
).

From this point forward,
\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\ldots
will be positive constants depending only on
g
,
S
,
\mathcal{A}
,
\mathcal{C}
and the choices of the various Weil heights, unless otherwise specified.

Proposition 8.1
.

There exist positive constants
\xi_{1},\xi_{2}
such that

\widehat{h}_{\mathcal{A}_{\pi(P)},\mathcal{L}_{\pi(P)}}(P)\leq\xi_{1}h_{F}(\mathcal{A}_{\pi(P)})+\xi_{2}

for every
P\in\mathcal{C}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
.

Proof.

By
[
15
, Theorem II.3.1]
there exist positive constants
\xi_{3},\xi_{4}
depending only on
g
such that

\left|h_{L}\left(\left[A\right]\right)-\xi_{3}\cdot h_{F}(A)\right|\leq\xi_{4}

for every principally polarized
A/\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
of dimension
g
. Here, we denote by
[A]
the isomorphism class of
A
in
\mathbb{A}_{g,\mathbf{1}}
.
By
[
15
, Lemma II.1.2, last displayed equation]
, there are positive constants
\xi_{5},\xi_{6}
, depending only on
g
, such that

\left|h_{L}\left(\left[A\right]\right)-\widetilde{h}_{L}\left(\left[A\right]\right)\right|\leq\xi_{5}+\xi_{6}\log\max\left\{1,\widetilde{h}_{L}\left(\left[A\right]\right)\right\}

for each
[A]\in\mathbb{A}_{g,\mathbf{1}}
. In particular, this means that
\widetilde{h}_{L}\left(\left[A\right]\right)\ll h_{L}\left(\left[A\right]\right)+1
, which combined with the inequality above yields
\widetilde{h}_{L}\left(\left[A\right]\right)\ll h_{F}\left(A\right)+1
. As noted above,
\widetilde{h}_{L}
differs from
h_{\overline{\mathbb{A}_{g,\mathbf{1}}},L}
by a bounded function, so we get

(8.1)

h_{\overline{\mathbb{A}_{g,\mathbf{1}}},L}\left(\left[A\right]\right)\ll h_{F}(A)+1

for every principally polarized
A/\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
of dimension
g
, where the implied constant depends only on
g
and the choice of the Weil height
h_{\overline{\mathbb{A}_{g,\mathbf{1}}},L}
.

Let
\rho:\mathbb{A}_{g,\mathbf{1},3}\rightarrow\mathbb{A}_{g,\mathbf{1}}
be the natural morphism which forgets the level structure. It extends to a rational map

\overline{\rho}:\overline{\mathbb{A}_{g,\mathbf{1},3}}\dashrightarrow\overline{\mathbb{A}_{g,\mathbf{1}}}.

Let
S^{\prime}
be the Zariski closure of
\overline{\rho}(\overline{S})
in
\overline{\mathbb{A}_{g,\mathbf{1}}}
and fix Weil heights
h_{\overline{S},\mathcal{M}|_{\overline{S}}}
and
h_{S^{\prime},L|_{S^{\prime}}}
.
Therefore, as
\dim S^{\prime}=\dim\overline{S}
and
\overline{\rho}|_{\overline{S}}:\overline{S}\dashrightarrow S^{\prime}
is dominant, Theorem 1 of
[
59
]
yields positive constants
\xi_{7},\xi_{8}
and a non-empty Zariski open set
U_{1}\subseteq\overline{S}
such that

h_{\overline{S},\mathcal{M}|_{\overline{S}}}(s)\leq\xi_{7}\cdot h_{S^{\prime},L|_{S^{\prime}}}(\overline{\rho}(s))+\xi_{8}

for every
s\in U_{1}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})\subseteq\overline{S}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
. Since
\dim\overline{S}=1
,
U_{1}
is obtained by removing finitely many points from
\overline{S}
. Note also that
\overline{\rho}
is well defined on
S
and it is equal to
\rho
. Thus, we deduce that

h_{\overline{S},\mathcal{M}|_{\overline{S}}}(s)\leq\xi_{9}\cdot h_{\overline{\mathbb{A}_{g,\mathbf{1}}},L}(\rho(s))+\xi_{10}

for every
s\in S(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
. Combining this with (
8.1
) gives

(8.2)

h_{\overline{S},\mathcal{M}|_{\overline{S}}}(s)\leq\xi_{11}\cdot h_{F}(s)+\xi_{12}

for every
s\in S(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
and for some positive constants
\xi_{11},\xi_{12}
. Note that
h_{F}(\rho(s))=h_{F}(s)
, since the Faltings height is independent of the level structure.

Now, let
\overline{\mathcal{C}}
be the Zariski closure of
\mathcal{C}
inside
\overline{\mathcal{A}}\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}\times\mathbb{P}^{m}_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}
. As
\mathcal{C}
is not contained in any fixed fiber of
\mathcal{A}
, we have that
\pi|_{\mathcal{C}}:\mathcal{C}\to S
is surjective and thus we get a dominant rational map
\overline{\pi}|_{\overline{\mathcal{C}}}:\overline{\mathcal{C}}\dashrightarrow\overline{S}
. As above, Theorem 1 of
[
59
]
yields positive constants
\xi_{13},\xi_{14}
and a non-empty Zariski open set
U_{2}\subseteq\overline{\mathcal{C}}
such that

h_{\overline{\mathcal{C}},\overline{\mathcal{L}}|_{\overline{\mathcal{C}}}}(P)\leq\xi_{13}\cdot h_{\overline{S},\mathcal{M}|_{\overline{S}}}(\overline{\pi}(P))+\xi_{14}

for every
P\in U_{2}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})\subseteq\overline{\mathcal{C}}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
. As before, we can assume that
U_{2}
contains
\mathcal{C}
, so that

(8.3)

h_{\overline{\mathcal{A}},\overline{\mathcal{L}}}(P)\leq\xi_{13}\cdot h_{\overline{S},\mathcal{M}|_{\overline{S}}}(\pi(P))+\xi_{14}

for every
P\in\mathcal{C}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
. Observe that
h_{\overline{\mathcal{C}},\overline{\mathcal{L}}|_{\overline{\mathcal{C}}}}
is equal to the restriction of the naive height
h_{\overline{\mathcal{A}},\overline{\mathcal{L}}}
to
\overline{\mathcal{C}}
.

Finally, by Theorem A.1 of
[
14
]
, there exists a positive constant
\xi_{15}
such that

\widehat{h}_{\mathcal{A}_{\pi(P)},\mathcal{L}_{\pi(P)}}(P)\leq h_{\overline{\mathcal{A}},\overline{\mathcal{L}}}(P)+\xi_{15}\cdot\max\left\{1,h_{\overline{S},\mathcal{M}|_{\overline{S}}}(\pi(P))\right\}

for every
P\in\mathcal{A}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
. Combining this with (
8.2
) and (
8.3
) we get

\widehat{h}_{\mathcal{A}_{\pi(P)},\mathcal{L}_{\pi(P)}}(P)\leq\xi_{16}h_{F}(\mathcal{A}_{\pi(P)})+\xi_{17}

for some positive constants
\xi_{16},\xi_{17}
and for every
P\in\mathcal{C}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
.
∎

9.
Arithmetic bounds

Recall the setting of Theorem
1.1
and the reductions made in Section
3
: let
S\subseteq\mathbb{A}_{g}=\mathbb{A}_{g,\mathbf{1},3}
be a smooth, irreducible, locally closed curve, and let
\pi:\mathcal{A}=\mathfrak{A}_{g}\times_{\mathbb{A}_{g}}S\rightarrow S
. Let
\mathcal{C}
be as in Theorem
1.1
and define
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
as the set of points
P\in\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{C})
such that
\mathcal{A}_{\pi(P)}
has CM and there exists a nonzero endomorphism
f\in\en(\mathcal{A}_{\pi(P)})
satisfying
f(P)=O_{\pi(P)}
. Equivalently,
P
lies in a proper algebraic subgroup of
\mathcal{A}_{\pi(P)}
.

Assume that
S,\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{C}
are defined over the same number field
k
. Notice that if
P\in\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{C})
, then
\mathcal{A}_{\pi(P)}
is defined over
k(\pi(P))
and, since
\pi
is non-constant,

(9.1)

\left[k(P):k\right]\ll\left[k(\pi(P)):k\right]\leq\left[k(P):k\right].

Moreover, since
\mathcal{C}
is defined over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
and complex abelian varieties with complex multiplication are defined over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
(see Proposition 26 from Section 12.4 of
[
57
]
), it follows that
\pi(P)\in S(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})\subseteq\mathbb{A}_{g,\mathbf{1},3}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
for every
P\in\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
. By (
9.1
), this shows that
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
is a subset of
\mathcal{C}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
.

From this point forward,
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\ldots
will be positive constants depending only on
g
,
S
,
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{C}
, unless otherwise specified.

Lemma 9.1
.

Let
A
be a CM abelian variety of dimension
g
defined over a number field
K
. Then there exist positive constants
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}
depending only on
g
such that
h_{F}(A)\leq\gamma_{1}\cdot\left[K:\mathbb{Q}\right]^{\gamma_{2}}
.

Proof.

By
[
58
]
, there exists a finite extension
K^{\prime}/K
of degree at most
2\cdot(9g)^{4g}
such that all endomorphisms of
A
are defined over
K^{\prime}
. Théorème 6.1 of
[
54
]
(see also the remarks following its proof) then guarantees the existence of abelian varieties
A_{1},\ldots,A_{t}
defined over
K^{\prime}
and positive integers
e_{1},\ldots,e_{t}
with the following properties: each
A_{i}
is
\overline{K^{\prime}}
-simple, the
A_{i}
are pairwise non-isogenous over
\overline{K^{\prime}}
,
\en_{K^{\prime}}(A_{i})=\en_{\overline{K^{\prime}}}(A_{i})
is a maximal order in
\en^{0}_{\overline{K^{\prime}}}(A_{i})
, and
A
is
\overline{K^{\prime}}
-isogenous to
A^{\prime}:=\prod_{i=1}^{t}A_{i}^{e_{i}}
. So, there exists an isogeny
\phi:A^{\prime}\rightarrow A
with

\deg\phi\leq\gamma_{3}\cdot\max\left\{h_{F}(A^{\prime}),[K^{\prime}:\mathbb{Q}]\right\}^{\gamma_{4}},

where

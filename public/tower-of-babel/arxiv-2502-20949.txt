Abstract
. We consider positive solutions to − Δ p ​ u = 1 u γ + f ⁡ ( u ) \displaystyle-\Delta_{p}u=\frac{1}{u^{\gamma}}+f(u) under zero Dirichlet condition in the half space. Exploiting a prio-ri estimates and the moving plane technique, we prove that any solution is monotone increasing in the direction orthogonal to the boundary.



1.
Introduction

We deal with the study of monotonicity properties of positive weak solutions to quasilinear elliptic problems involving singular nonlinearities, in the half space.
In particular we consider the problem:

(
\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}
)

\begin{cases}\displaystyle-\Delta_{p}u=\frac{1}{u^{\gamma}}+f(u)&\text{in}\,\,\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}\\
u>0&\text{in}\,\,\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}\\
u=0&\text{on}\,\,\partial\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+},\end{cases}

where
N\geq 2
,
\gamma>1
and
\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:x_{N}>0\}
. It is well known that solutions of
p
-Laplace equations are generally of class
C^{1,\alpha}
(see
[
18
,
27
,
32
]
) and the equation has to be understood in the weak sense. The presence of the singular term
\displaystyle{u^{-\gamma}}
also causes that solutions are not smooth up to the boundary and, more important, are not in suitable energy spaces near the boundary. There is a wide literature regarding this challenging issue, see e.g.
[
1
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
11
,
17
,
24
,
26
,
29
,
30
,
31
]
and the references therein.

Monotonicity properties of the solutions represent a crucial information that brings advantage
in many applications: blow-up analysis, a-priori estimates and also in the proofs of Liouville type theorems. The study of the monotonicity of the solutions was started in the semilinear nondegenerate case in a series of papers. We refer to
[
2
,
3
,
4
]
and to
[
15
,
16
]
. The more general case of domains that are epigraphs has been recently solved in
[
5
]
. The study of the quasilinear case with smooth nonlinearities was started and settled in
[
20
,
21
,
22
,
23
]
. Here we address the case in which the nonlinearity has a singular part.

In the sequel we will denote by

\Sigma_{\lambda}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}:0<x_{N}<{\lambda}\},

and, for a given
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}
we will use the notation
x=(x^{\prime},x_{N})
where
x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
.

About the solution
u
and the nonlinearity
f:[0,+\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
we will suppose the following:

(hp):
(i)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{t^{\gamma}}+f(t)>0
whenever
t>0
.

(ii)

f:[0,+\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
is locally Lipschitz continuous.

(iii)

The solution
u
is locally bounded in strips, namely for all
{\lambda}>0
there exists
\tilde{C}:=\tilde{C}(\lambda)
such that

0\leq u(x)\leq\tilde{C},\qquad x\in\Sigma_{\lambda}.

As recalled above the solutions, in general, have not
W^{1,p}
regularity up to the boundary (see
[
26
]
) and the equation has to be understood in the following distributional way:

Definition 1
.

We say that
u
is a solution to (

P
γ

) if and only if
u\in W^{1,p}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})
,
\displaystyle{\rm{essinf}}_{K}\,u>0

for any compact
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}
and

(1)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}}|\nabla u|^{p-2}(\nabla u,\nabla\varphi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}}\left(\frac{1}{u^{\gamma}}+f(u)\right)\varphi\qquad\forall\varphi\in C^{1}_{c}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N}).

Moreover, the zero Dirichlet boundary condition also has to be understood in the weak meaning

(u-\varepsilon)^{+}\varphi\chi_{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}}\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})\qquad\forall\,\varphi\in C^{1}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\,.

Remark 2
.

In some cases, see e.g.
[
6
]
for the case of bounded domains, when dealing with singular equations, it is natural to understand the zero Dirichlet datum requiring that
u^{\frac{\gamma+p-1}{p}}\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
. We stress the fact that such assumption already implies that
(u-\varepsilon)^{+}\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
so that our analysis do apply also to this case.

We are now ready to state our main result:

Theorem 3
.

Let
u
be a solution to (

P
γ

). Then, assuming
{\bf(hp)}
, it follows that

u
is monotone increasing w.r.t. the
x_{N}
-direction, that is

\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{N}}\,\geq\,0\quad\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}\,.

The paper is organized as follows: in Section 2 we carry out the study of the behaviour and the monotonicity of the solutions near the boundary; this is nothing but a barrier argument. In Section 3 we prove Theorem
3
. Here we exploit the a refined version of the moving plane technique, taking care of the lack of compactness.

2.
Behaviour and monotonicity of the solutions near to the boundary

The arguments of this section are well understood in the semilinear case and go back to
[
17
,
25
,
26
]
. Our proofs are self contained and, as the reader will guess, exploit some typical barriers arguments.

Let us recall that here, for a given
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}
, we will use the notation
x=(x^{\prime},x_{N})
where
x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
. Moreover we will use the following well known inequality (see
[
12
]
),

(2)

(|\eta|^{p-2}\eta-|\eta^{\prime}|^{p-2}\eta^{\prime},\eta-\eta^{\prime})\geq C_{p}(|\eta|+|\eta^{\prime}|)^{p-2}|\eta-\eta^{\prime}|^{2},

for every
\eta,\eta^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}

We begin studying the monotonicity of our solutions near the boundary.
The desired estimates shall follow
studying the behavior of the solutions to the auxiliary problem:

(3)

\begin{cases}\displaystyle-\Delta_{p}u=\frac{C}{u^{\gamma}}&\text{in}\,\,\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+},\\
u>0&\text{in}\,\,\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+},\\
u=0&\text{on}\,\,\partial\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+},\end{cases}

for a suitable choice of
C
(due to the assumptions
(hp)
).

In the next lemma, we establish bounds for supersolutions and subsolutions of problems (
3
). As a rule, for solutions to (
3
), we obtain continuity up to the boundary. Before proceeding with this, and for the sake of completeness, we recall the following:

Definition 4
.

We say that

u
is a weak supersolution to (
3
) if
u\in W^{1,p}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})
,
{\rm{essinf}}_{K}\,u>0

for any compact
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}
and

(4)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}}|\nabla u|^{p-2}(\nabla u,\nabla\varphi)\geq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}}\frac{\varphi}{u^{\gamma}}\qquad\forall\varphi\in C^{1}_{c}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N}),\varphi\geq 0.

We say that
u
is a weak subsolution to (
3
) if
u\in W^{1,p}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})
,
{\rm{essinf}}_{K}\,u>0

for any compact
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}
and

(5)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}}|\nabla u|^{p-2}(\nabla u,\nabla\varphi)\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}}\frac{\varphi}{u^{\gamma}}\qquad\forall\varphi\in C^{1}_{c}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N}),\varphi\geq 0.

Lemma 5
.

Let
u\in W^{1,p}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})
be a supersolution to the problem (
3
).
Then there exists
c_{s}>0
such that:

(6)

u(x)\geq c_{s}x_{N}^{\beta},\qquad\displaystyle\beta=\frac{p}{\gamma+p-1}\,.

If
u\in W^{1,p}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})
is a subsolution to the problem (
3
) then there exists
\bar{\lambda}>0
and
C_{S}>0
such that

(7)

u(x)\leq C_{S}x_{N}^{\beta}.

for all
x\in\Sigma_{\bar{\lambda}}
.

Moreover as consequence, if
u
is a solution to (
3
),
u\in C^{0}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}})
.

Remark 6
.

Let us point out that thanks to Lemma
5
, by standard regularity theory, recalling that the solutions are locally bounded and locally bounded away from zero in the interior of the half space, we deduce that
u\in C^{1,\alpha}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})
.

Proof.

We first prove (
6
)
.

Let
\phi_{1}
be the first positive eigenfunction of the

-Laplacian operator with zero Dirichlet boundary data on the unit ball

. Recall that

Define

Denoting by

where we implicitly take into account that, by Hopf Lemma,

Choosing

Let us consider

For a given

Then, exploiting also (

\displaystyle C_{p}\int_{B_{x_{0,N}}}(|\nabla w_{\beta,x_{0}}|+|\nabla u|)^{p-2}|\nabla(w_{\beta,x_{0}}-u-\varepsilon)^{+}|^{2}

\displaystyle\int_{B_{x_{0,N}}}(|\nabla w_{\beta,x_{0}}|^{p-2}\nabla w_{\beta,x_{0}}-|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u,\nabla(w_{\beta,x_{0}}-u-\varepsilon)^{+})

\displaystyle C\int_{B_{x_{0,N}}}\left(\frac{1}{w_{\beta,x_{0}}^{\gamma}}-\frac{1}{u^{\gamma}}\right)(w_{\beta,x_{0}}-u-\varepsilon)^{+}\leq 0\,,

This permits to get that

then

The thesis (
6
) follows letting

. We have

c_{s}x_{0,N}^{\beta}:=x_{0,N}^{\beta}\tilde{C}\phi_{1}^{\beta}\left(0\right)\leq u(x_{0}).

Finally the complete claim of (
6

.

Let

Theorem 7
.

Let
u\in C^{1,\alpha}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})
be a solution to (

\varphi_{R}\equiv 0,&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N-1}\setminus B_{2R}^{{}^{\prime}}(0),\\
\displaystyle|\nabla\varphi_{R}|\leq\frac{C}{R},&\text{ in }(B_{2R}^{{}^{\prime}}(0)\setminus B_{R}^{{}^{\prime}}(0))\subset\mathbb{R}^{N-1},\end{cases}

where
B_{R}^{{}^{\prime}}(0)=\left\{x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{N-1}:|x^{\prime}|<R\right\}
,
R>1
and
C
is a suitable positive constant.

For all
\varepsilon>0
, let

be defined as:

G_{\varepsilon}(t)=\begin{cases}0,&\text{if $t\geq\varepsilon$}\\
2t-2\varepsilon,&\text{if $t\in(\varepsilon,2\varepsilon)$}\\
t,&\text{if $t\geq 2\varepsilon$}.\end{cases}

Let us define

\Psi=\Psi_{\varepsilon,R}\,:=\,\displaystyle\frac{G_{\varepsilon}(|\nabla u|)}{|\nabla u|}\chi_{\mathcal{U}}\varphi_{R},

where
\chi_{\mathcal{U}}
is the characteristic function of a set
\mathcal{U}
. Denoting by
{\displaystyle h_{\varepsilon}(t)=\frac{G_{\varepsilon}(t)}{t}}
(meaning that
h(t)=0
for
t\in[0,\varepsilon]
), using
\Psi
as a test function, we get

(28)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{U}}\varphi_{R}|\nabla u|^{p-2}(\nabla u,\nabla h_{\varepsilon}(|\nabla u|))dx+\int_{\mathcal{U}}h_{\varepsilon}(|\nabla u|)|\nabla u|^{p-2}(\nabla u,\nabla\varphi_{R})dx

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sigma^{+}\int_{\mathcal{U}}\displaystyle\varphi_{R}h_{\varepsilon}(|\nabla u|)dx

Notice that the first integral in the left-hand-side of (
28
) can be estimate as

(29)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathcal{U}}\,\varphi_{R}|\nabla u|^{p-2}(\nabla u,\nabla h_{\varepsilon}(|\nabla u|))dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathcal{U}}|\nabla u|^{p-2}\Big(|\nabla u|h_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(|\nabla u|)\Big)\|D^{2}u\|\varphi_{R}dx,

where
\|D^{2}u\|
denotes the Hessian norm.

Here below, we fix
R>0
and let
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
. Later we will let
R\rightarrow\infty
.
To this aim, let us first show that

(I)

|\nabla u|^{p-2}||D^{2}u||\varphi_{R}\in L^{1}(\mathcal{U}),\,\,\,\forall R>0
;

(II)

|\nabla u|h_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(|\nabla u|)\rightarrow 0
a.e. in
\mathcal{U}
as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
and
|\nabla u|h_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(|\nabla u|)\leq C
with
C
not depending on
\varepsilon
.

Let us prove
(i)
. Defining

\mathcal{D}(R)=\left\{\mathcal{U}\cap{\{B_{R}^{{}^{\prime}}(0)\times\mathbb{R}\}}\right\}
, by Hölder’s inequality it follows

(30)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{U}}|\nabla u|^{p-2}||D^{2}u||\varphi_{R}dx\leq C(\mathcal{D}(2R))\left(\int_{\mathcal{D}(2R)}|\nabla u|^{2(p-2)}||D^{2}u||^{2}\varphi_{R}^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C(\mathcal{D}(2R))\left(\int_{\mathcal{D}(2R)}|\nabla u|^{p-2-\eta}||D^{2}u||^{2}\varphi_{R}^{2}|\nabla u|^{p-2+\eta}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(\mathcal{D}(2R))||\nabla u||^{(p-2+\eta)/2}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})}\left(\int_{\mathcal{D}(2R)}|\nabla u|^{p-2-\eta}||D^{2}u||^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

where
0\leq\eta<1
is such that
\varphi^{2}_{R}|\nabla u|^{p-2+\eta}
becomes bounded.
Recall that we have proved

\text{dist}(\mathcal{U},\{y=0\})>0.

Using
[
13
]
-Theorem 1.1. we infer that

\left(\int_{\mathcal{D}(2R)}|\nabla u|^{p-2-\eta}||D^{2}u||^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C.

Then by (
30
) we obtain

\int_{\mathcal{U}}|\nabla u|^{p-2}||D^{2}u||\varphi_{R}dx\leq C.

Let us prove
(ii)
. Recalling (
27
), we obtain

h^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(t)=\begin{cases}0&\text{if $t>2\varepsilon$}\\
\frac{2\varepsilon}{t^{2}}&\text{if $\varepsilon<t<2\varepsilon$}\\
0&\text{if $0\leq t<\varepsilon$},\end{cases}

and then
|\nabla u|h_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(|\nabla u|)
tends to
0

almost everywhere in
\mathcal{U}
as
\varepsilon
goes to
0
; hence we have:

|\nabla u|h_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(|\nabla u|)\leq 2
.

Then by (
28
), (
29
) and (I), (II) above, passing to the limit
as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
, we get:

\int_{\mathcal{U}}\,|\nabla u|^{p-2}(\nabla u,\nabla\varphi_{R})dx\geq\sigma^{+}\int_{\mathcal{U}}\varphi_{R}dx,\quad\forall R>0.

Recalling (
26
),
we have that there exists
C=C(\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})})
(not depending on
R
)
such that:

|\mathcal{U}\cap\{B_{R}^{\prime}(0)\times\mathbb{R}\}|\leq\frac{C}{R}|\mathcal{U}\cap\{B_{2R}^{\prime}(0)\times\mathbb{R}\}|

and
|\mathcal{U}\cap\{B_{R}^{\prime}(0)\times\mathbb{R}\}|
has polynomial growth. Therefore, by
[
20
]
-Lemma 2.1
it follows that
|\mathcal{U}\cap\{B_{R}^{\prime}(0)\times\mathbb{R}\}|
converges to zero if
R\rightarrow+\infty
, concluding the proof
by contradiction.
∎

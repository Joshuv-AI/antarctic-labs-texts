Introduction and main results

In this paper, we study the infimum values of three probability functions for the Laplace distribution and the student’s
t
distribution. To give the motivation, we recall three former conjectures at first.

Let
B(n,p)
denote a binomial random variable with parameters
n
and
p
. Let
\mathcal{R}
be the family of random variables of the form
X=\sum^{n}_{k=1}a_{k}\varepsilon_{k}
, where
n\geq 1
,
a_{k},k=1,\dots,n,
are real numbers with
\sum^{n}_{k=1}a_{k}^{2}=1
, and
\varepsilon_{k}
,
k=1,2,\dots
, are independent
Rademacher random variables (i.e.,
P(\varepsilon_{k}=1)=P(\varepsilon_{k}=-1)=1/2
).

Now we state three former conjectures as follows:

Chvátal’s Conjecture:
For any fixed
n\geq 2
, as
m
ranges over
\{0,\ldots,n\}
, the probability
P(B(n,m/n)\leq m)
is the smallest when

m
is closest to
\frac{2n}{3}
.

Tomaszewski’s conjecture:

\inf_{X\in\mathcal{R}}P(|X|\leq 1)=\frac{1}{2}
.

Hitczenko-Kwapień’s conjecture:

\inf_{X\in\mathcal{R}}P(|X|\geq 1)=\frac{7}{32}
.

Chvátal’s Conjecture has applications in machine learning. Janson
[
7
]
showed that Chvátal’s Conjecture holds for large
n
. Barabesi et al.
[
1
]
and Sun
[
12
]
proved that Chvátal’s Conjecture is true for general
n\geq 2
.

Tomaszewski’s conjecture has many applications in probability theory, geometric analysis and computer science. Keller and Klein
[
8
]
completely solved Tomaszewski’s conjecture.

Hitczenko-Kwapień’s conjecture was recently proved by Hollom and Portier
[
4
]
. In fact, Hollom and Portier
[
4
]
found an explicit expression for the anti-concentration function
y\mapsto\inf_{X\in\mathcal{R}}P\left(|X|\geq y\right),\forall y>0.

Let
\{X_{\alpha}\}
be a family of random variables satisfying some distribution with a parameter
\alpha

E(X_{\alpha})
be the expectation, and

\displaystyle C(y):=\inf_{\alpha}P(X_{\alpha}\leq yE(X_{\alpha})),

\displaystyle T(y):=\inf_{\alpha}P\left(|X_{\alpha}-E(X_{\alpha})|\leq y\sqrt{Var(X_{\alpha})}\right),

\displaystyle H(y):=\inf_{\alpha}P\left(|X_{\alpha}-E(X_{\alpha})|\geq y\sqrt{Var(X_{\alpha})}\right).

Let

\displaystyle H(y)=\inf_{\mu,b}P\left(|X_{\mu,b}-\mu|\geq\sqrt{2}by\right).

Theorem 1.1

Let

(i)

(ii)

Proof of Theorem
1.1

The distribution function of
X_{\mu,b}
is (see e.g. Appendix (i) of
[
14
]
)

\displaystyle F_{X_{\mu,b}}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\frac{1}{2}e^{\frac{x-\mu}{b}},&\mbox{if}\ x<\mu,\\
1-\frac{1}{2}e^{-\frac{x-\mu}{b}},&\mbox{if}\ x\geq\mu.\end{array}\right.

(i) We have the following five cases:

(i.1)
\mu>0,y\geq 1
. Now
y\mu\geq\mu
and thus

\displaystyle C(y)

\displaystyle=\inf_{\mu,b}P(X_{\mu,b}\leq y\mu)=\inf_{\mu,b}\left(1-\frac{1}{2}e^{-\frac{(y-1)\mu}{b}}\right)=\lim_{\frac{\mu}{b}\to 0+}\left(1-\frac{1}{2}e^{-\frac{(y-1)\mu}{b}}\right)=\frac{1}{2}.

(i.2)
\mu>0,0<y<1
. Now
y\mu<\mu
and thus

\displaystyle C(y)

\displaystyle=\inf_{\mu,b}P(X_{\mu,b}\leq y\mu)=\inf_{\mu,b}\frac{1}{2}e^{-\frac{(1-y)\mu}{b}}=\lim_{\frac{\mu}{b}\to+\infty}\frac{1}{2}e^{-\frac{(1-y)\mu}{b}}=0.

(i.3)
\mu=0
. Now
y\mu=0=\mu
and thus

\displaystyle P(X_{\mu,b}\leq y\mu)=\frac{1}{2}e^{-\frac{(1-y)\mu}{b}}\equiv\frac{1}{2}.

Hence in this case,
C(y)=\frac{1}{2}
.

(i.4)
\mu<0,y>1
. Now
y\mu<\mu
and thus

\displaystyle C(y)

\displaystyle=\inf_{\mu,b}P(X_{\mu,b}\leq y\mu)=\inf_{\mu,b}\frac{1}{2}e^{\frac{(y-1)\mu}{b}}=\lim_{\frac{\mu}{b}\to-\infty}\frac{1}{2}e^{\frac{(y-1)\mu}{b}}=0.

(i.5)
\mu<0,0<y\leq 1
. Now
y\mu\geq\mu
and thus

\displaystyle C(y)

\displaystyle=\inf_{\mu,b}P(X_{\mu,b}\leq y\mu)=\inf_{\mu,b}\left(1-\frac{1}{2}e^{\frac{(1-y)\mu}{b}}\right)=\lim_{\frac{\mu}{b}\to 0-}\left(1-\frac{1}{2}e^{\frac{(1-y)\mu}{b}}\right)=\frac{1}{2}.

By (i.1)-(i.5), we obtain that (i) holds.

(ii) Since
b>0
and
y>0
, we have

\displaystyle T(y)

\displaystyle=\inf_{\mu,b}P\left(|X_{\mu,b}-\mu|\leq\sqrt{2}by\right)

\displaystyle=\inf_{\mu,b}\left[F_{X_{\mu,b}}(\mu+\sqrt{2}by)-F_{X_{\mu,b}}(\mu-\sqrt{2}by)\right]

\displaystyle=\inf_{\mu,b}\left[1-\frac{1}{2}e^{-\frac{(\mu+\sqrt{2}by)-\mu}{b}}-\frac{1}{2}e^{\frac{(\mu-\sqrt{2}by)-\mu}{b}}\right]

\displaystyle=1-e^{-\sqrt{2}y}.

(iii) Since
b>0
and
y>0
, we have

\displaystyle T(y)

\displaystyle=\inf_{\mu,b}P\left(|X_{\mu,b}-\mu|\geq\sqrt{2}by\right)

\displaystyle=\inf_{\mu,b}\left[1-F_{X_{\mu,b}}(\mu+\sqrt{2}by)+F_{X_{\mu,b}}(\mu-\sqrt{2}by)\right]

\displaystyle=\inf_{\mu,b}\left[1-\left(1-\frac{1}{2}e^{-\frac{(\mu+\sqrt{2}by)-\mu}{b}}\right)+\frac{1}{2}e^{\frac{(\mu-\sqrt{2}by)-\mu}{b}}\right]

\displaystyle=e^{-\sqrt{2}y}.

The proof is complete.

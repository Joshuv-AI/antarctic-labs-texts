Stäckel and Eisenhart lifts, Haantjes geometry and Gravitation
Ondřej Kubů, Piergiulio Tempesta

Abstract
. We introduce the notion of Stäckel lift as a novel geometric setting for the construction of large classes of integrable Hamiltonian systems. The Stäckel lift extends the geometric framework underlying both the Riemannian and the Lorentzian-type classical Eisenhart lifts and is intimately related with Haantjes geometry. In particular, we establish that Hamiltonian systems constructed through Stäckel lifts are naturally endowed with a symplectic-Haantjes structure. We also show that explicitly momentum-dependent lifting matrices produce systems interpretable as gravitational waves, or momentum-dependent metrics of Hamilton and Finsler geometries, with potential applications in modified gravity theories.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
MSC: 53A45, 58C40, 58A30.

1.
Introduction

The program of geometrization of dynamics, inaugurated by Eisenhart in 1929 in
[
19
]
, has been one of the most fruitful research developments in modern theoretical and mathematical physics.

The central idea behind the Eisenhart lift (also known as Eisenhart-Duval lift
[
17
,
18
]
) is to
describe the dynamics of a mechanical system with configuration space coordinates
q^{1},\ldots,q^{n}
, subject to a magnetic field with scalar and vector potentials
U(t,q^{i}),\bm{A}(t,q^{i})
, respectively, in terms of the geodesics of a Lorentzian metric defined on an
(n+2)
-dimensional space-time. We will refer to this construction as the Lorentzian-Eisenhart lift.

This viewpoint can also be applied to the more generic case of a lift from an
n
-dimensional to an
n+1
dimensional space. In the literature, this is often referred to as the Riemannian-Eisenhart lift, since it allows to define a (pseudo)-Riemannian metric in the higher-dimensional space.

The purpose of this article is to define a new geometric lift for Hamiltonian integrable systems which extendes the geometric framework underlying the Eisenhart lift: the
Stäckel lift
.

Since the original studies of Stäckel
[
43
,
44
]
, Di Pirro
[
16
]
, Painlevé
[
36
]
, till the recent work by Magri
[
29
]
, the Stäckel geometry has represented a fundamental theoretical setting for the formulation of the theory of separation of variables for classical Hamiltonian integrable systems.

Given a separable Hamiltonian system defined in a
2n
-dimensional phase space, the main idea of the approach we propose is to lift the
n\times n
Stäckel matrix associated with the system to a
(2n+2)
-dimensional phase space.

This is achieved in terms of of a new matrix, that we name the
(n+1)\times(n+1)

lifting matrix
, which adds to the original Stäckel matrix a column that depends on a new pair of variables
(q^{n+1},p_{n+1})
, conjugated with respect to the extended symplectic form
\omega^{\prime}=\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}dp_{i}\wedge dq^{i}
.

In this way, we define a
generalized Stäckel matrix
, explicitly depending (although not mandatorily) on the new momentum
p_{n+1}
. This procedure can be naturally iterated by introducing new pairs of conjugate variables and higher-dimensional lifting matrices, which always keep memory of the original Stäckel matrix.

This procedure allows to generate infinitely many higher-dimensional integrable models, starting from a
seed system
. In fact, the Stäckel lifting procedure provides at once and in a straightforward way all the Hamiltonian functions of the lifted Hamiltonian system. Under a suitable choice of the lifting matrix, these models can be interpreted as geodesic Hamiltonians defined in a suitable pseudo-Riemannian space. However, it is also possible to generate families of non-geodesic integrable Hamiltonian systems possessing higher-order integrals of motion, which could give rise to interesting higher-rank Killing tensors.

We remind that in
[
8
]
, with a different aim, integrable systems generated with separation relations depending on generalized Stäckel matrices have been studied: momenta are explicitly taken into account in the separation equations. This framework is developed in a manifold of a fixed dimension
n
, and does not consider geometrical lifts, which is the main purpose of our work.
In fact, our goal is to show that Stäckel lift represents a very general and flexible tool that can be used to generate several typologies of geometric lifts. We will show that, in some specific cases, the Eisenhart lift can be obtained as a special case of the Stäckel lift.

As a second objective of this article, we shall clarify the relation among Eisenhart and Stäckel lifts and Haantjes geometry.

The study of Haantjes tensors has experienced a resurgence of interest in the last years
[
4
,
10
,
15
,
27
,
28
,
30
,
49
]
. In particular, Haantjes tensors have been recently related to symplectic geometry in the theory of
\omega\mathscr{H}
manifolds
[
46
,
47
,
39
,
24
,
41
]
. This geometric framework offers a natural language for the formulation of the theory of Hamiltonian integrable systems, and the determination of their total and partial separation variables.

As one of our main results, in Theorem
1
we prove that the Stäckel (and, in particular, the Eisenhart) lift of a given Hamiltonian integrable system carries the structure of a nontrivial symplectic-Haantjes manifold. Coherently with the geometric idea of the Stäckel lift, the symplectic-Haantjes structure can be reconstructed from the one associated with the original system. Interestingly enough, in the case of a Stäckel lift explicitly dependent on the momenta, the Haantjes tensors associated with the lifted Hamiltonian model cannot be interpreted as lift of Killing tensors. In fact, they give rise to a separation of variables defined in the full phase space, not in the configuration space.

Stäckel lifts find applications in classical mechanics because they inherently produce Hamiltonian systems that are both
integrable
and
separable
.

However, this observation does not exhaust its relevance. In fact, we will show that, in the case of a momentum-dependent lifting matrix, we can produce nontrivial Hamiltonians having a straightforward interpretation in gravitational contexts. More precisely, we obtain Hamiltonian systems representing Platonic waves
[
6
]
, and in particular,
gravitational pp-waves
.

We recall that a plane-fronted wave with parallel rays or
pp
-wave is a Lorentzian spacetime geometry admitting a null, parallel vector field with flat wave fronts. The broader class of Platonic waves is conformal to Bargmann-Eisenhart waves, whose wave fronts may not be flat as for
pp
-waves. Under certain additional conditions, they are exact solution of Einstein’s theory of relativity or
f(R)
theories, i.e. its higher-order curvature extensions.

Moreover, we can naturally define nontrivial geometric structures known as
Hamilton
and
generalized Hamilton spaces

[
31
,
38
]
. These spaces are endowed with a momentum-dependent metric. Their corresponding analogues in the extended configuration space are known as Finsler and Lagrange spaces
[
51
]
. Owing to their connection with the Zermelo wind problem
[
52
]
and its generalizations
[
1
]
, these geometries find applications in acoustics, optics, quantum control, and quantum mechanics (see e.g.
[
25
]
for references). Recently, Hamilton spaces have been investigated as effective structures for configuration and phase spaces in quantum gravity phenomenological models involving Lorentz invariance violation or noncommutative spacetime, in particular deformed special relativity
[
5
]
, and in connection with prospective mesoscopic-scale signals of quantum gravity
[
14
]
(see also the review for physicists
[
2
]
).

The structure of the article is as follows. In section
2
, we revise the most relevant aspects of the theory of
\omega\mathscr{H}
manifolds we need in the forthcoming discussion. In section
3
, we introduce the notion of Stäckel lift and we compare it with the Eisenhart lift. In section
4
, we elucidate the relationship between Stäckel lifts and Haantjes geometry, and prove Theorem
1
. In section
5
, we study an iterative procedure allowing to construct higher-dimensional Hamiltonian models. The study of new, non-geodesic Hamiltonian arising from the Stäckel lift is performed in section
6
. An extension of the theory to the case of magnetic systems is proposed in the final section
7
.

2.
Preliminaries on the Haantjes geometry

In this section, we shall review some basic notions concerning the geometry of Nijenhuis or Haantjes torsions, following the original papers
[
23
,
33
]
and the related ones

[
34
,
21
]
. Here we shall focus only on the aspects of the theory which are relevant for the subsequent discussion.

Let
M
be a smooth manifold of dimension
n
,
\mathfrak{X}(M)
the Lie algebra of vector fields on
M
and
\bm{K}:\mathfrak{X}(M)\rightarrow\mathfrak{X}(M)
be a smooth
(1,1)
-tensor field. In the following, all tensors will be considered to be smooth.

Definition 1
.

The
Nijenhuis torsion of
\bm{K}
is the the vector-valued
2
-form

(2.1)

\tau_{\bm{K}}(X,Y):=\bm{K}^{2}[X,Y]+[\bm{K}X,\bm{K}Y]-\bm{K}\Big([X,\bm{K}Y]+[\bm{K}X,Y]\Big),

where
X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
and
[\,\ ]
denotes the commutator of two vector fields.

Definition 2
.

The Haantjes torsion of
\bm{K}
is the vector-valued
2
-form

(2.2)

\mathcal{H}_{\bm{K}}(X,Y):=\bm{K}^{2}\tau_{\bm{K}}(X,Y)+\tau_{\bm{K}}(\bm{K}X,\bm{K}Y)-\bm{K}\Big(\tau_{\bm{K}}(X,\bm{K}Y)+\tau_{\bm{K}}(\bm{K}X,Y)\Big).

The Haantjes (Nijenhuis) vanishing condition inspires the following definition.

Definition 3
.

A Haantjes (Nijenhuis) operator is a (1,1)-tensor field whose Haantjes (Nijenhuis) torsion identically vanishes.

A simple, relevant case of Haantjes operator is that of a tensor field
\bm{K}
which takes a diagonal form in a local chart
\bm{x}=(x^{1},\ldots,x^{n})
:

(2.3)

\bm{K}(\bm{x})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}(\bm{x})\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}\otimes\mathrm{d}x^{i}\,

where
\lambda_{i}(\bm{x}):=\lambda^{i}_{i}(\bm{x})
are the eigenvalue fields of
\bm{K}
and
\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{1}},\ldots,\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{n}}\right)
are the fields forming the so called
natural frame
associated with the local chart
(x^{1},\ldots,x^{n})
. As is well known, the Haantjes torsion of the diagonal operator (
2.3
) vanishes.

We also recall that two frames
\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}\}
and
\{Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{n}\}
are said to be equivalent if
n
nowhere vanishing smooth
functions
f_{i}
exist, such that

(2.4)

X_{i}=f_{i}(\bm{x})Y_{i}\,,\qquad\qquad i=1,\ldots,n\ .

Definition 4
.

An
integrable
frame is a reference frame equivalent to a natural frame.

Many more examples of Haantjes operators, relevant in classical mechanics and in Riemannian geometry, can be found, for instance, in the works
[
24
]
,
[
39
]
–
[
41
]
and
[
46
]
–
[
50
]
.

2.1.
Haantjes algebras

Another crucial notion is that of Haantjes algebras, which have been introduced in
[
46
]
and further studied and generalized in
[
40
]
.

Definition 5
.

A Haantjes algebra is a pair
(M,\mathscr{H})
satisfying the following properties:

•

M
is a differentiable manifold of dimension
\mathrm{n}
;

•

\mathscr{H}
is a set of Haantjes operators
\bm{K}:\mathfrak{X}(M)\to\mathfrak{X}(M)
. Also, they generate:

–

a free module over the ring of smooth functions on
M
:

(2.5)

\mathcal{H}_{\left(f\bm{K}_{1}+g\bm{K}_{2}\right)}(X,Y)=\mathbf{0}\,\qquad\forall\,X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}(M)\,\quad\forall\,f,g\in C^{\infty}(M)\,\quad\forall\penalty\ \bm{K}_{1},\bm{K}_{2}\in\mathscr{H};

–

a ring w.r.t. the composition operation

(2.6)

\mathcal{H}_{(\bm{K}_{1}\,\bm{K}_{2})}(X,Y)=\mathbf{0}\,\qquad\forall\,\bm{K}_{1},\bm{K}_{2}\in\mathscr{H},\quad\forall\,X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}(M)\ .

Moreover, if

(2.7)

\bm{K}_{1}\,\bm{K}_{2}=\bm{K}_{2}\,\bm{K}_{1}\,\quad\qquad\ \forall\penalty\ \bm{K}_{1},\bm{K}_{2}\in\mathscr{H}\,

the algebra
(M,\mathscr{H})
is said to be an Abelian Haantjes algebra.

In essence, we can think of
\mathscr{H}
as an associative algebra of Haantjes operators.

Remark 1
.

A fundamental fact concerning Abelian Haantjes algebras is that there exist sets of coordinates, called Haantjes coordinates, which allow us to write simultaneously all
\bm{K}\in\mathscr{H}
in a block-diagonal form. In particular, if
\mathscr{H}
is an algebra of semisimple operators, then in Haantjes coordinates all
\bm{K}\in\mathscr{H}
acquire a purely diagonal form
[
46
]
.

2.2.
The symplectic-Haantjes manifolds

Symplectic-Haantjes (or
\omega\mathscr{H}
) manifolds are symplectic manifolds endowed with a compatible algebra of Haantjes operators. Apart from their mathematical properties, these manifolds are relevant because they provide a simple but sufficiently general setting in which the theory of Hamiltonian integrable systems can be naturally formulated.

Definition 6
.

A symplectic–Haantjes (or
\omega\mathscr{H}
) manifold of class
m
is a triple
(M,\omega,\mathscr{H})
which satisfies the following properties:

(i)

(M,\omega)
is a symplectic manifold of dimension
2\,n
;

(ii)

\mathscr{H}
is an Abelian Haantjes algebra of rank
m
;

(iii)

(\omega,\mathscr{H})
are algebraically compatible, that is

(2.8)

\omega(X,\bm{K}Y)=\omega(\bm{K}X,Y)\qquad\forall\bm{K}\in\mathscr{H}\ .

We also remind the following

Proposition 1
.

[
47
]

Given a
2n
-dimensional
\omega\mathscr{H}
manifold
M
, every generalized eigen–distribution
ker(\bm{K}-l_{i}\bm{I})^{{r_{i}}}
,
r_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
, is of even rank.
Therefore, the geometric and algebraic multiplicities of each eigenvalue
l_{i}(\bm{x})
are even.

Let us consider the spectral decomposition of the tangent spaces of
M
given by

(2.9)

T_{\bm{x}}M=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{s}\mathcal{D}_{i}(\bm{x}),\qquad\qquad\mathcal{D}_{i}(\bm{x}):=ker\big(\bm{K}-l_{i}\bm{I}\big)^{\rho_{i}}(\bm{x}),

where
\rho_{i}
is the Riesz index of the eigenvalue
\l_{i}
. Due to the latter Proposition, as
rank

\mathcal{D}_{i}\geq 2,\ i=1,\ldots s
we conclude that the number of distinct eigenvalues of any
Haantjes operator
\bm{K}
of an
\omega\mathscr{H}
manifold is not greater than
n
.

2.3.
Haantjes chains

Hereafter, we remind the notion of Haantjes chains. It has been introduced in
[
47
]
and will be extensively used in the forthcoming analysis.

Definition 7
.

Let
(M,\mathscr{H})
be a Haantjes algebra of rank
m
. We shall say that a smooth function
H
generates a Haantjes chain of closed 1-forms of length
m
if there exists
a distinguished basis
\{\tilde{\bm{K}}_{1},\ldots,\tilde{\bm{K}}_{m}\}
of
\mathscr{H}

such that

(2.10)

\mathrm{d}(\tilde{\bm{K}}^{T}_{\alpha}\,\mathrm{d}H)=\bm{0}\,\quad\qquad\alpha=1,\ldots,m\,

where
\tilde{\bm{K}}^{T}_{\alpha}:\mathfrak{X}(M)\to\mathfrak{X}(M)
is the transposed operator of
\tilde{\bm{K}}_{\alpha}
. The (locally) exact 1-forms

(2.11)

\mathrm{d}H_{\alpha}=\tilde{\bm{K}}^{T}_{\alpha}\,\mathrm{d}H,

which are supposed to be linearly independent, are said to be the elements of the Haantjes chain of length
\mathrm{m}
generated by
H
. The functions
H_{\alpha}
are the potential functions of the chain.

In order to enquire about the existence of Haantjes chains for an assigned Haantjes algebra, we have to consider the co-distribution, of rank
r\leq m
, generated by a given function
H
via an arbitrary basis

\{\bm{K}_{1},\bm{K}_{2},\ldots,\bm{K}_{m}\}
of
\mathscr{H}
, i.e.

(2.12)

\mathcal{D}_{H}^{\circ}:=Span\{\bm{K}_{1}^{T}dH,\bm{K}_{2}^{T}\mathrm{d}H,\ldots,\bm{K}_{m}^{T}\,\mathrm{d}H\}\,

and the distribution
\mathcal{D}_{H}
of the vector fields annihilated by them, which has rank
(n-r)
.
Note that such distributions do not depend on the particular choice of the basis of
\mathscr{H}
.

The following result clarifies the compatibility between a given
\omega\mathscr{H}
manifold and a set of functions in involution.

Corollary 1
.

A function
H
generates a Haantjes chain only if it satisfies the invariance condition

(2.13)

\bm{K}^{T}(\mathcal{D}_{H}^{\circ})\subseteq\mathcal{D}_{H}^{\circ},\qquad\forall\bm{K}\in\mathscr{H}\ .

2.4.
Haantjes coordinates

As we shall see, a crucial result for the theory of separability of Hamiltonian systems is the theorem, proved in
[
46
]
, ensuring that an algebra of Haantjes operators can be represented in suitable coordinates in a block-diagonal form.

Proposition 2

(Proposition 33,
[
46
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{K}=\{\bm{K}_{1},\ldots,\bm{K}_{m}\}
,
\bm{K}_{\alpha}:\mathfrak{X}(M)\to\mathfrak{X}(M)
,
\alpha=1,\ldots,m
be a family of commuting operator fields; we assume that one of them, say
\bm{K}_{1}
, is a Haantjes operator. Then, there exist local charts adapted to the spectral decomposition of
\bm{K}_{1}
in which all of the operators
\bm{K}_{\alpha}
can be written simultaneously in a block-diagonal form. In addition, if we assume that

(i) all the operators of the family are Haantjes operators

(ii) all possible nontrivial intersections of their generalized eigen-distributions

(2.14)

\mathcal{V}_{a}(\bm{x}):=\bigoplus_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}}^{s_{1},\ldots,s_{m}}\mathcal{D}_{i_{1}}^{(1)}(\bm{x})\bigcap\ldots\bigcap\mathcal{D}_{i_{m}}^{(m)}(\bm{x})\,,\qquad a=1,\ldots,v\leq n,

are pairwise integrable (i.e. any direct sum of
\mathcal{V}_{a}
is integrable), then there exist sets of local coordinates, adapted to the decomposition

(2.15)

T_{\bm{x}}M=\bigoplus_{a=1}^{v}\mathcal{V}_{a}(\bm{x}),\qquad\bm{x}\in M,

in which all operators
\bm{K}_{\alpha}
admit simultaneously a block-diagonal form (with possibly finer blocks).

Remark 2
.

Each set of coordinates allowing us to write down the family
\mathcal{K}
in a block-diagonal form will be said to be a set of Haantjes coordinates. In particular, if we deal with a semisimple Haantjes algebra, then each set of Haantjes coordinates allows us to write simultaneously all the operators of the algebra in a diagonal form.

Definition 8
.

Given an
\omega\mathscr{H}
manifold, the local coordinates where all Haantjes operators take simultaneously a block-diagonal form and the symplectic form takes the Darboux form are called Darboux–Haantjes (DH) coordinates.

Remark 3
.

We adopt the following notation: we shall denote by
(q^{i},p_{i})
,
i=1,\ldots,n
a generic set of canonically conjugated Darboux variables on an
n
-dimensional symplectic manifold. For Cartesian and polar coordinates, we will keep the standard notations. The new variables introduced in the Riemann-Eisenhart lifting procedure will be denoted by
(u,p_{u})
, for the Lorentzian case, we will use
(t,u)
and
(p_{t},p_{u})
.

3.
The Stäckel lift as a generalization of the Eisenhart lift

3.1.
The Eisenhart lift

For simplicity, we will formulate the theory in the Hamiltonian settings. Consequently, we shall consider a symplectic manifold
(M,\omega)
where the symplectic form
\omega=dq^{i}\wedge dp_{i}
; we assume that the Hamiltonian, as a function in the cotangent bundle
T^{*}M
, is defined as a polynomial of degree 2 in the momenta:

(3.1)

H=H^{(2)}+H^{(1)}+H^{(0)}.

Explicitly, we assume that

(3.2)

H^{L}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}g^{ij}(\bm{q},t)(p_{i}-eA_{i})(p_{j}-eA_{j})+e^{2}.V(\bm{q},t).

By regarding time
t
as a configuration coordinate, and introducing a new coordinate
u
, we can lift
H
to
T^{*}(M\times\mathbb{R}^{2})
. Denoting by
p_{u}
and
p_{t}
the new momenta associated with the new variables, and introducing the new symplectic form

(3.3)

\omega^{\prime}=\omega+dt\wedge dp_{t}+du\wedge dp_{u},

one can define the lifted Hamiltonian

(3.4)

H^{L}=H^{(2)}+p_{u}H^{(1)}+p_{u}^{2}H^{(0)}+p_{t}p_{u},

which is a homogeneous function of degree-two in the momenta. Explicitly,

(3.5)

H=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}g^{ij}(\bm{q},t)(p_{i}-p_{u}A_{i})(p_{j}-p_{u}A_{j})+p_{u}^{2}V(\bm{q},t)+p_{t}p_{u}.

Thus, we may regard it as generating the geodesic flow of the pseudo-Riemannian metric given by

(3.6)

ds^{2}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}g_{ij}(\bm{q},t)dq^{i}dq^{j}-2V(\bm{q},t)dt^{2}+2\sum_{i=1}^{n}A_{i}(\bm{q},t)dq^{i}dt+2dtdu.

In this context we are assuming
g^{ab}g_{bc}=\delta^{a}_{c}
.

If the original Hamiltonian possesses integrals which are polynomial in the momenta, it can be shown that the lifted integrals are still constants of the motion for
H^{L}
. In addition, being
p_{t}
and
p_{u}
integrals, we deduce that the lifted system possesses the same integrability properties of the original one: in particular, the Liouville-Arnold integrability is preserved. System (
3.2
) can be seen as the symplectic reduction of system (
3.5
), through the addition of the first-class constraint
p_{t}-m=0
. This result is sometimes called the Eisenhart-Lichnerowicz theorem
[
19
,
26
]
.

3.2.
Preliminaries on the Stäckel geometry

Let
(H_{1},\ldots,H_{n})
be an integrable Hamiltonian system and assume that all the Hamiltonian functions
H_{j}
are quadratic in the momenta

(3.7)

H_{j}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{n}g_{j}^{kk}(\bm{q})p_{k}^{2}+V_{j}(\bm{q}).

Thus, one can adopt the standard theory of separability due to Stäckel
[
43
,
37
]
. As is well known, Stäckel’s approach provides us with a characterization of
totally separable systems
by means of an invertible
Stäckel matrix

\bm{S}
and
Stäckel vectors

|\bm{W}_{j}\rangle
, of the form

(3.8)

S^{(n)}(\bm{q})=\begin{bmatrix}S_{11}(q^{1})&\cdots&S_{1n}(q^{1})\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
S_{n1}(q^{n})&\cdots&S_{nn}(q^{n})\end{bmatrix}\,,\qquad\qquad|\bm{W}_{j}\rangle=\begin{bmatrix}W_{j}^{1}(q^{1})\\
\vdots\\
W_{j}^{n}(q^{n})\end{bmatrix}\,,

where
S_{ij}
and
W_{j}^{i}
are arbitrary functions of their separated arguments that satisfy the conditions

(3.9)

g_{j}^{kk}(\bm{q})=[(S^{(n)})^{-1}]_{j}^{k}(\bm{q})\,,\qquad\qquad V_{j}(\bm{q})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}g^{ii}(\bm{q})\,W_{j}^{i}(q^{i})\ .

Thus, we have

(3.10)

H_{j}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{n}[(S^{(n)})^{-1}]_{j}^{k}(\bm{q})\Big(p_{k}^{2}+W_{j}^{k}(\bm{q})\Big)\,.

In
[
3
]
, Arnold, Kozlov and Neishtadt (AKN) proposed a generalization of Stäckel systems by considering the case of arbitrary, not necessarily quadratic, Hamiltonian functions defined in a symplectic manifold. Precisely, given a Stäckel matrix
\bm{S}
, their construction consists in the family of Hamiltonians

(3.11)

H_{j}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\dfrac{\tilde{S}_{j}^{k}}{\det S^{(n)}}\,f_{k}(q^{k},p_{k}),\qquad k=1,\ldots,n,

where
\tilde{S_{j}^{k}}
is the cofactor associated to the element
S_{k}^{j}
, and
f_{k}
are arbitrary functions. It can be shown that the functions
H_{j}
, defined by Eqs. (
3.11
), are in involution with each other; consequently, they represent a totally separable Hamiltonian system.

3.3.
The Stäckel lift

We propose the construction of a new lift, based on the Stäckel geometry, which generalizes the Eisenhart lift.

Definition 9
.

Let
S^{(n)}(\bm{q})
be an
(n\times n)
Stäckel matrix.
The Stäckel lift of this matrix is defined to be the matrix

(3.12)

\mathcal{L}^{(n+1)}(\bm{q},\bm{p}):=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc|ccc}S_{11}(q^{1})&\cdots&S_{1n}(q^{1})&S_{1,n+1}(q^{1},p_{1})\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
S_{n1}(q^{n})&\cdots&S_{nn}(q^{n})&S_{n,n+1}(q^{n},p_{n})\\
\hline\cr 0&\cdots&0&S_{n+1,n+1}(q^{n+1},p_{n+1})\end{array}\right].

Here
S_{1,n+1}(q^{1},p_{1}),\ldots,S_{n+1,n+1}(q^{n+1},p_{n+1})
are arbitrary smooth functions, with
S_{n+1,n+1}(q^{n+1},p_{n+1})\neq 0
.

Remark 4
.

The lifted matrix
\mathcal{L}^{(n+1)}(\bm{q})
depends explicitly, in its last column, on the momenta
\bm{p}=(p_{1},\ldots,p_{n+1})
. In Remark 6 of
[
41
]
it was observed that, interestingly enough, even when each row
a
of the Stäckel matrix
\bm{S}
depends on
(q^{a},p_{a})
, we can still construct a separable Hamiltonian system in involution. This fact also holds for partially separable systems. This observation will be crucial in the subsequent analysis.

Remark 5
.

In the case when no dependence on the momenta is allowed, the lifted matrix
\mathcal{L}^{(n+1)}(\bm{q})
is by construction a standard Stäckel matrix on an
(n+1)
-dimensional configuration space. There are many other possible choices of lifting a Stäckel matrix by preserving its Stäckel character; the present one is perhaps the simplest one, and is sufficient to describe many interesting cases.

3.4.
New Hamiltonian systems from the Stäckel lift

Let
(H_{1},\ldots,H_{n})
be an integrable Hamiltonian system, separable in the coordinate system
\bm{q}:=(q_{1},\ldots,q_{n})
, and
S^{(n)}(\bm{q})

4.
Haantjes geometry and Stäckel lifts

In this section we wish to clarify the relationship between the Stäckel lift and the Haantjes geometry.

4.1.
Main theorem

Theorem 1
.

Let
\big(H_{1},\ldots,H_{n+1}\big)
be a Stäckel-lifted Hamiltonian system defined by relations (
3.13
) for a lift matrix of the form

(4.1)

\mathcal{L}^{(n+1)}(\bm{q},\bm{p}):=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc|ccc}S_{11}(q^{1})&\cdots&S_{1n}(q^{1})&S_{1,n+1}(q^{1},p_{1})\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
S_{n1}(q^{n})&\cdots&S_{nn}(q^{n})&S_{n,n+1}(q^{n},p_{n})\\
\hline\cr 0&\cdots&0&S_{n+1,n+1}(q^{n+1})\end{array}\right],

with
S_{n+1,n+1}(q^{n+1})\neq 0
. Then there exists a
\omega\mathscr{H}
manifold associated, whose Haantjes algebra
\mathscr{H}
is defined by the set of semisimple operators

(4.2)

\bm{K}_{j}:=\sum_{r=1}^{n+1}\mu_{r}\big(q^{1},q^{r},q^{n+1},p_{1},p_{r},p_{n+1}\big)\bigg(\frac{\partial}{\partial q^{r}}\otimes\mathrm{d}q^{r}+\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{r}}\otimes\mathrm{d}p_{r}\bigg),\quad\ j=1,\ldots,n+1,

where

(4.3)

\mu_{r}\big(q^{1},q^{r},q^{n+1},p_{1},p_{r},p_{n+1}\big):=\dfrac{\tilde{S}_{jr}\frac{\partial f_{r}(q^{r},p_{r})}{\partial{p_{r}}}+\delta_{jr}\frac{\partial\tilde{S}_{j,n+1}}{\partial p_{r}}f_{n+1}(q^{n+1},p_{n+1})}{\tilde{S}_{1r}\,\frac{\partial f_{r}(q^{r},p_{r})}{\partial{p_{r}}}+\delta_{1r}\frac{\partial\tilde{S}_{1,n+1}}{\partial p_{r}}f_{n+1}(q^{n+1},p_{n+1})}.

Proof.

As a consequence of the Jacobi-Haantjes theorem, proved in
[
39
]
, the Hamiltonians
\big(H_{1},H_{2},\ldots,H_{n+1}\big)
which are in total separable involution in the Darboux coordinates
(\bm{q},\bm{p})
, belong to the Haantjes chain generated by the operators

(4.4)

\bm{K}_{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}\frac{\frac{\partial H_{j}}{\partial p_{i}}}{\frac{\partial H}{\partial p_{i}}}\bigg(\frac{\partial}{\partial q^{i}}\otimes\mathrm{d}q^{i}+\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{i}}\otimes\mathrm{d}p_{i}\bigg),\qquad j=1,\ldots,n+1,

where
H
is any of the functions
(H_{1},\ldots,H_{n+1})
, with
\frac{\partial H}{\partial p_{i}}\neq 0
,
i=1,\ldots,n
.

Taking into account that the Hamiltonian system is of the AKN form (
3.11
), it is immediate to observe that

\displaystyle\frac{\frac{\partial H_{j}}{\partial p_{r}}}{\frac{\partial H}{\partial p_{r}}}={}

\displaystyle\dfrac{\dfrac{1}{\det S^{(n)}}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\tilde{S}_{jk}\,\frac{\partial f_{k}(q^{k},p_{k})}{\partial{p_{r}}}+\delta_{rj}\frac{\partial\tilde{S}_{j,n+1}}{\partial p_{r}}f_{n+1}(q^{n+1},p_{n+1})\right)}{\dfrac{1}{\det S^{(n)}}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\tilde{S}_{1k}\,\frac{\partial f_{k}(q^{k},p_{k})}{\partial{p_{r}}}+\delta_{1j}\frac{\partial\tilde{S}_{1,n+1}}{\partial p_{r}}f_{n+1}(q^{n+1},p_{n+1})\right)}=

(4.5)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\dfrac{\tilde{S}_{jr}\frac{\partial f_{r}(q^{r},p_{r})}{\partial{p_{r}}}+\delta_{jr}\frac{\partial\tilde{S}_{j,n+1}}{\partial p_{r}}f_{n+1}(q^{n+1},p_{n+1})}{\tilde{S}_{1r}\,\frac{\partial f_{r}(q^{r},p_{r})}{\partial{p_{r}}}+\delta_{1r}\frac{\partial\tilde{S}_{1,n+1}}{\partial p_{r}}f_{n+1}(q^{n+1},p_{n+1})}.

These operators, being diagonal, have vanishing Haantjes torsion and generate a semisimple Abelian
\omega\mathscr{H}
structure on
M
.
∎

The Stäckel lift procedure introduced above is not limited to producing geodesic lifts. We show the versatility of the method by discussing three of the infinitely many possible non-geodesic flows that can be constructed.

6.1.
A quadratic and non-homogeneous system

First, observe that the 2D model (

Case 1.
The matrix

The linear momentum terms in

To preserve the quadratic structure of

from the cylindrical coordinate system, which remains constant with respect to the other integrals. This yields a complete quadratic Hamiltonian on the warped Riemannian manifold

The term independent of momenta,

The momentum-dependent term

Remark 8
.

If we assume the modification of Stäckel basis for the Hamiltonians (including the
1/r^{2}
factor), all magnetic systems separable in the configuration space admit Stäckel matrices. More specifically, the separability theory in
[
7
]
, which applies to all (pseudo)Riemannian manifolds without second-class null coordinates, implies that - in adapted coordinates - the linear terms in the momenta are always associated with a Killing vector and its corresponding first order integral. The Lamé coefficient used in the change of Stäckel basis depends only on the essential coordinates and therefore commutes with the terms that need to be added.

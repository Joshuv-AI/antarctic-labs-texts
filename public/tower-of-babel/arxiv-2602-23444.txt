Generalized Stochastic Gradient Descent with Momentum Methods for Smooth Optimization
Zimeng Wang, Alp Yurtsever

Abstract
Stochastic gradient descent with momentum (SGDM) methods have become fundamental optimization tools in machine learning, combining the computational efficiency of stochastic gradients with the acceleration benefits of momentum. Despite their widespread use in practice, the theoretical understanding of SGDM remains incomplete, with most existing analyses focusing on specific momentum schemes or requiring restrictive assumptions. In this paper, we introduce a generalized SGDM framework that unifies a broad class of momentum-based methods, including SGD with Polyak’s momentum, SGD with Nesterov’s momentum, and many others. We provide comprehensive convergence analyses for both convex and nonconvex optimization problems under mild smoothness and bounded variance assumptions. For convex problems, we establish general ergodic convergence results with constant parameters and derive improved iterate convergence rates with time-varying parameters. For nonconvex problems, we prove sublinear convergence to stationary points and establish linear convergence to a neighborhood of the optimum under the Polyak–Łojasiewicz condition. Notably, our analysis allows flexible parameter choices and thus provides convergence guarantees for many existing momentum methods as special cases.



1
Introduction

We consider the following smooth optimization problem:

\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x):=\mathbb{E}_{\xi\in\mathcal{D}}[\ell(x;\xi)].

(1.1)

Here,
\xi\in\mathcal{Z}
denotes a data sample drawn from a distribution
\mathcal{D}
over the sample space
\mathcal{Z}
, and
\ell(x;\xi):\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathcal{Z}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a loss function that is differentiable with respect to its first argument. This formulation is standard in machine learning applications, where
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
represents the model parameters and
\ell(\cdot;\xi)
measures the loss incurred by the model on data sample
\xi
. In particular, when
\mathcal{D}
is the uniform distribution over a finite dataset
\{\xi_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
, the objective function
f
in (
1.1
) reduces to the empirical risk
\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\ell(\cdot;\xi_{i})
.

Gradient-based methods are fundamental tools for solving problem (
1.1
) in machine learning and scientific computing. The classical gradient descent (GD) method iteratively updates the iterate via
x_{k+1}=x_{k}-\alpha\nabla f(x_{k})
, where
\alpha>0
is the step size. For
L
-smooth convex functions, GD with step size
\alpha=1/L
achieves a convergence rate of
O(1/t)
after
t
iterations
(
Nesterov 2013b
)
. However, this rate is known to be suboptimal, as the lower bound for first-order methods in smooth convex optimization is
O(1/t^{2})

(
Nemirovskij and Yudin 1983
)
.

To achieve further acceleration, momentum methods have been introduced in the optimization literature. A pioneering example is the heavy-ball (HB) method
(
Polyak 1964
)
, which incorporates a momentum term to accumulate information from past gradients. Although originally proposed for quadratic objectives, the HB method has since been shown to converge for general smooth convex functions
(
Ghadimi et al. 2015
)
.
Subsequently,
Nesterov 1983
proposed a different momentum scheme by evaluating the gradient at a look-ahead point, leading to Nesterov’s accelerated gradient (NAG) method. This method achieves the optimal convergence rate of
O(1/t^{2})
for smooth convex functions. These momentum methods have since been extensively studied and extended to a wide range of settings, including composite optimization
(
Beck and Teboulle 2009
;
Nesterov 2013a
)
, nonconvex problems
(
Ochs et al. 2014
)
, and coordinate descent methods
(
Nesterov 2012
)
.

These deterministic methods are effective when the full gradient
\nabla f
is readily accessible. However, in many modern machine learning applications, computing the full gradient becomes prohibitively expensive as the training dataset grows large. Stochastic gradient descent (SGD)
(
Robbins and Monro 1951
)
addresses this challenge by replacing the full gradient with a stochastic gradient estimator based on a single sample or a small mini-batch, thereby substantially reducing the per-iteration computational cost. Owing to its efficiency and scalability, SGD has become the workhorse optimization algorithm in deep learning
(
Bottou et al. 2018
;
LeCun et al. 2015
;
Nguyen et al. 2019
;
Zhang 2004
)
.

The success of momentum methods in deterministic optimization naturally motivated their extension to stochastic settings. Stochastic gradient descent with momentum (SGDM) aims to combine the computational efficiency of SGD with the acceleration benefits of momentum. In addition, the momentum mechanism helps traverse flat regions of the loss landscape and can potentially escape poor local minima
(
Ochs et al. 2015
)
. Consequently, SGDM and its variants have become fundamental tools in deep learning optimization
(
Kingma and Ba 2015
;
Qian 1999
;
Sutskever et al. 2013
;
Yan et al. 2018
)
and are widely used for training deep neural networks
(
He et al. 2016
;
Simonyan and Zisserman 2014
)
.

Despite its widespread use in practice, the theoretical understanding of SGDM remains less complete than that of its deterministic counterparts. Many existing convergence results for SGDM focus on specific momentum schemes.
In particular, for SGD with Polyak’s (heavy-ball) momentum,
Loizou and Richtárik 2020
established global linear convergence rates for convex quadratic objectives under appropriate parameter choices. For general smooth convex objectives,
Sebbouh et al. 2021
proved almost sure convergence of the last iterate of SGD with Polyak’s momentum under time-varying step sizes and momentum parameters. Under constant parameters,
Liu et al. 2020
showed that SGD with Polyak’s momentum achieves convergence guarantees comparable to those of vanilla SGD for both nonconvex and strongly convex problems.
For stochastic composite optimization problems involving both smooth and nonsmooth components,
Lan 2012
introduced an accelerated stochastic gradient method based on Nesterov’s momentum and proved its optimality for convex objectives. This approach was later extended to nonconvex problems in
Ghadimi and Lan 2016
.

Beyond these algorithm-specific analyses, several works have sought to develop unified frameworks that generalize classical momentum methods. For example,
Yan et al. 2018
;
Yang et al. 2016
provided a unified analysis of the stochastic unified momentum (SUM) method, which encompasses both SGD with Polyak’s momentum and SGD with Nesterov’s momentum for nonconvex smooth objectives, under a bounded gradient assumption. These results were further extended to distributed settings with linear speedup guarantees in
Yu et al. 2019
.
Additionally,
Ma and Yarats 2018
proposed the quasi-hyperbolic momentum (QHM) method, which interpolates between SGD and SGD with Polyak’s momentum. When the initialization is sufficiently close to an optimal solution,
Gitman et al. 2019
showed that QHM converges linearly for smooth and strongly convex functions. In the context of over-parameterized learning,
Liu and Belkin 2018
developed a generalized momentum method, namely the momentum-added stochastic solver (MASS), by introducing an additional compensation term to SGD with Nesterov’s momentum, and proved linear convergence for quadratic loss functions.

While the aforementioned frameworks generalize classical momentum methods, most of their convergence guarantees are established for convex or strongly convex objectives and are often restricted to special settings such as quadratic losses or interpolation regimes
(
Liu and Belkin 2018
)
. For nonconvex problems, these works typically rely on restrictive assumptions, such as bounded gradients
(
Yan et al. 2018
;
Yang et al. 2016
)
, or analyze Polyak’s momentum and Nesterov’s momentum separately to establish convergence
(
Yu et al. 2019
)
. Moreover, their analyses typically focus on constant parameter choices, and the resulting convergence rates generally match those of vanilla SGD under comparable assumptions. As a result, these results do not capture the accelerated behavior of Nesterov’s method observed in deterministic settings.
Therefore, despite substantial progress, a unified theoretical framework that systematically explains how momentum interacts with stochastic gradient methods across both convex and nonconvex regimes remains lacking.

In this work, we aim to fill this gap by providing a comprehensive convergence analysis for a generalized SGDM method that encompasses a broad class of momentum schemes for both convex and nonconvex optimization problems. Our main contributions are summarized as follows:

1).

We introduce a generalized SGDM framework characterized by momentum parameters
\{\beta_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
and step size parameters
\{\gamma_{k},\eta_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
. We show that this formulation encompasses many existing momentum-based methods as special cases, including SGD with Polyak’s momentum, SGD with Nesterov’s momentum, SUM, QHM, and MASS.

2).

For convex problems, we establish general ergodic convergence results for the generalized SGDM with constant parameters, covering a wide range of step sizes
\gamma,\eta
and any momentum parameter
\beta\in[0,1)
. Furthermore, through the introduction of novel Lyapunov functions built on intermediate iterates, we derive improved iterate convergence results for the generalized SGDM with time-varying parameters. With carefully chosen parameters, we recover the optimal
O(1/t^{2})
convergence rate in deterministic settings and an
O(1/t^{2}+\sigma/\sqrt{t})
rate for unbiased stochastic gradients with variance bounded by
\sigma^{2}
.

3).

For nonconvex problems, we prove sublinear convergence to stationary points for the generalized SGDM with constant parameters. Notably, the result holds for any
\beta\in[0,1)
and step sizes
\gamma,\eta
whose sum is properly upper bounded. Furthermore, under an additional Polyak–Łojasiewicz condition, we introduce a new sequence of Lyapunov functions to establish an improved convergence bound in terms of the objective gap, showing linear convergence to a neighborhood of the optimum.

4).

For both convex and nonconvex objectives, our analysis allows for flexible parameter choices and relies only on mild smoothness and bounded variance assumptions, without requiring bounded gradients or other restrictive conditions. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first work to provide rigorous convergence guarantees for such a general class of momentum schemes in both convex and nonconvex settings under comparable assumptions. As a result, new convergence guarantees for many existing momentum methods follow directly as special cases of our analysis.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section
2
introduces preliminary notations and definitions. Section
3
presents the generalized SGDM method and shows how it unifies several classical momentum methods. Sections
4
and
5
provide convergence analyses for convex and nonconvex problems, respectively. Section
6
reports numerical experiments, and Section
7
concludes the paper.

Preliminary

In this section, we summarize some notations and definitions that are used throughout the paper.
For a vector
v:=(v_{1},\dots,v_{d})^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, let
\|v\|
represents its Euclidean norm, i.e.,
\|v\|:=\big(\sum_{i=1}^{d}v_{i}^{2}\big)^{1/2}
. For
n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
, we denote
[n]:=\{1,\ldots,n\}
.

Let (
\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathrm{P})
be the underlying probability space. We denote by
\left\{x_{k}\right\}_{k\geq 0}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
the sequence of iterates generated by a stochastic algorithm, driven by a sequence of independent noise variables
\left\{\xi_{k}\right\}_{k\geq 1}
. Let
\left\{\mathcal{F}_{k}\right\}_{k\geq 0}
be the natural filtration of this process, i.e.,
\mathcal{F}_{k}=\sigma\left(x_{0},\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\ldots,\xi_{k}\right)
represents the
\sigma
-algebra generated by
x_{0}
and
\left\{\xi_{i}\right\}_{i=1}^{k}
. We denote the conditional expectation given
\mathcal{F}_{k-1}
by
\mathbb{E}_{k}[\cdot]:=\mathbb{E}\left[\cdot\mid\mathcal{F}_{k-1}\right]
, which represents the expectation given all information available at step
k
.
We recall the definitions of convexity, Lipschitz continuity, and smoothness as follows.

Definition 1

(Convexity, Lipschitz continuity, and Smoothness)
.

Let the function
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be continuously differentiable. Let
\mu\geq 0,G>0
, and
L>0
.

1).

We say that
f
is
\mu
-strongly convex if

f(y)\geq f(x)+\langle\nabla f(x),y-x\rangle+\frac{\mu}{2}\|y-x\|^{2},~\forall x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

When the above inequality holds with
\mu=0
, we say that
f
is convex.

2).

We say that
f
is
G
-Lipschitz continuous if

|f(y)-f(x)|\leq G\|y-x\|,~\forall x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

3).

We say that
f
is
L
-smooth if its gradient is
L
-Lipschitz continuous, i.e.,

\|\nabla f(y)-\nabla f(x)\|\leq L\|y-x\|,~\forall x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

A useful property of an
L
-smooth function
f
is that

f(y)\leq f(x)+\langle\nabla f(x),y-x\rangle+\frac{L}{2}\|y-x\|^{2},~\forall x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Moreover, if
f
is convex, then it additionally satisfies

f(y)\geq f(x)+\langle\nabla f(x),y-x\rangle+\frac{1}{2L}\|\nabla f(y)-\nabla f(x)\|^{2},~\forall x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(2.1)

Both inequalities are standard results in convex optimization; see
Nesterov 2003
for detailed proofs.
For a convex function
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
that attains a global minimizer, we denote by
x^{*}\in\argmin_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x)
a minimizer of
f
and
f^{*}:=f(x^{*})
.

3
Generalized SGDM Method

We now introduce our generalized stochastic gradient descent with momentum (SGDM) framework. Let
x_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
be an initial point and set
m_{0}=0
. We denote by
g_{k}:=\nabla\ell(x_{k};\xi_{i_{k}})
the stochastic gradient at iteration
k
, where
i_{k}
is drawn uniformly from the index set
[n]
. The generalized SGDM updates the iterates according to

\displaystyle m_{k}=\beta_{k}m_{k-1}+(1-\beta_{k})g_{k},

(G-SGDM)

\displaystyle x_{k+1}=x_{k}-\gamma_{k}g_{k}-\eta_{k}m_{k},

where
\beta_{k}\in[0,1)
is the momentum parameter, while
\gamma_{k}\geq 0
and
\eta_{k}\in\mathbb{R}
serve as step size parameters. Note that we allow
\eta_{k}
to take negative values in this general formulation since its negativity can be compensated by a sufficiently large positive
\gamma_{k}
. In our analysis, we shall introduce suitably defined “effective” step sizes that depend on the parameters
\{\beta_{k},\gamma_{k},\eta_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
, which are required to be strictly positive.

The formulation (
G-SGDM
) is highly general and encompasses several important special cases. When
\beta_{k}\equiv 0
, (
G-SGDM
) reduces to the vanilla SGD:

x_{k+1}=x_{k}-(\gamma_{k}+\eta_{k})g_{k}.

(SGD)

Moreover, it includes SGD with Polyak’s (heavy-ball) momentum, SGD with Nesterov’s momentum, the stochastic unified momentum (SUM)
(
Yan et al. 2018
)
, the quasi-hyperbolic momentum (QHM) method
(
Ma and Yarats 2018
)
, and the momentum-added stochastic solver (MASS)
(
Liu and Belkin 2018
)
as special cases, as shown in the following proposition. Note that we assume
\eta_{k}\neq 0
without loss of generality; otherwise, (
G-SGDM
) reduces to the SGD update.

Proposition 1
.

The iterative scheme (
G-SGDM
) encompasses the following momentum-based methods by specifying the parameters
\{\beta_{k},\gamma_{k},\eta_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
accordingly.

(i).

If
\gamma_{k}\equiv 0
, then (
G-SGDM
) recovers the SGD with Polyak’s (heavy-ball) momentum method

x_{k+1}=x_{k}+\frac{\beta_{k}\eta_{k}}{\eta_{k-1}}(x_{k}-x_{k-1})-(1-\beta_{k})\eta_{k}g_{k}.

(SGD-HB)

(ii).

If
\gamma_{k}=(1-\beta_{k})\eta_{k-1}/\beta_{k}
, then (
G-SGDM
) yields the SGD with Nesterov’s momentum method:

\displaystyle y_{k+1}=x_{k}-\gamma_{k}g_{k},

(SGD-N)

\displaystyle x_{k+1}=y_{k+1}+\frac{\beta_{k}\eta_{k}}{\eta_{k-1}}(y_{k+1}-y_{k}).

(iii).

If
\beta_{k}\equiv\beta\in[0,1)
,
\gamma_{k}\equiv s\alpha
, and
\eta_{k}\equiv\alpha/(1-\beta)-s\alpha
, where
s\in[0,1/(1-\beta)]
and
\alpha>0
, then (
G-SGDM
) reduces to the SUM method

\displaystyle y_{k+1}=x_{k}-\alpha g_{k},

(SUM)

\displaystyle z_{k+1}=x_{k}-s\alpha g_{k},

\displaystyle x_{k+1}=y_{k+1}+\beta(z_{k+1}-z_{k}).

(iv).

If
\beta_{k}\equiv\beta\in[0,1)
,
\gamma_{k}\equiv\alpha(1-\nu)
, and
\eta_{k}\equiv\alpha\nu
, where
\alpha>0
and
\nu\in[0,1]
, then (
G-SGDM
) leads to the QHM method

\displaystyle m_{k}=\beta m_{k-1}+(1-\beta)g_{k},

(QHM)

\displaystyle x_{k+1}=x_{k}-\alpha[(1-\nu)g_{k}+\nu m_{k}].

(v).

If
\beta_{k}\equiv\beta\in[0,1)
,
\gamma_{k}\equiv\alpha
, and
\eta_{k}\equiv(\beta\alpha-\lambda)/(1-\beta)
, where
\alpha,\lambda>0
, then (
G-SGDM
) gives the MASS method

\displaystyle y_{k+1}=x_{k}-\alpha g_{k},

(MASS)

\displaystyle x_{k+1}=(1+\beta)y_{k+1}-\beta y_{k}+\lambda g_{k}.

Proof of Proposition
1
.

Firstly, it is straightforward to verify (iv) by plugging the corresponding parameters in (
G-SGDM
). To establish the remaining properties, we multiply the first equation in (
G-SGDM
) by
\eta_{k}
to obtain

\eta_{k}m_{k}=\frac{\beta_{k}\eta_{k}}{\eta_{k-1}}\eta_{k-1}m_{k-1}+(1-\beta_{k})\eta_{k}g_{k}.

(3.1)

From the second equation in (
G-SGDM
), we have

\displaystyle\eta_{k}m_{k}=-x_{k+1}+x_{k}-\gamma_{k}g_{k},

\displaystyle\eta_{k-1}m_{k-1}=-x_{k}+x_{k-1}-\gamma_{k-1}g_{k-1},

applying which on (
3.1
) gives

-x_{k+1}+x_{k}-\gamma_{k}g_{k}=\frac{\beta_{k}\eta_{k}}{\eta_{k-1}}(-x_{k}+x_{k-1}-\gamma_{k-1}g_{k-1})+(1-\beta_{k})\eta_{k}g_{k}.

(3.2)

Rearranging the terms in (
3.2
) and denoting
y_{k+1}=x_{k}-\gamma_{k}g_{k}
, we obtain

\displaystyle x_{k+1}

\displaystyle=x_{k}-\gamma_{k}g_{k}-\frac{\beta_{k}\eta_{k}}{\eta_{k-1}}(x_{k-1}-\gamma_{k-1}g_{k-1})+\frac{\beta_{k}\eta_{k}}{\eta_{k-1}}x_{k}-(1-\beta_{k})\eta_{k}g_{k}

(3.3)

\displaystyle=y_{k+1}-\frac{\beta_{k}\eta_{k}}{\eta_{k-1}}y_{k}+\frac{\beta_{k}\eta_{k}}{\eta_{k-1}}y_{k+1}+\Big(\frac{\beta_{k}\eta_{k}}{\eta_{k-1}}\gamma_{k}-(1-\beta_{k})\eta_{k}\Big)g_{k}

\displaystyle=y_{k+1}+\frac{\beta_{k}\eta_{k}}{\eta_{k-1}}(y_{k+1}-y_{k})+\Big(\frac{\beta_{k}\eta_{k}}{\eta_{k-1}}\gamma_{k}-(1-\beta_{k})\eta_{k}\Big)g_{k}.

We now establish properties (i), (ii), and (v) using the formulation (
3.3
). For property (i), if
\gamma_{k}\equiv 0
, then by definition
y_{k+1}=x_{k}
, and thus (
3.3
) reduces to (
SGD-HB
).
For property (ii), if
\gamma_{k}=(1-\beta_{k})\eta_{k-1}/\beta_{k}
, then the last term in (
3.3
) vanishes, and we obtain

x_{k+1}=y_{k+1}+\frac{\beta_{k}\eta_{k}}{\eta_{k-1}}(y_{k+1}-y_{k}),

which is precisely (
SGD-N
). For property (v), substituting
\beta_{k}\equiv\beta\in[0,1)
,
\gamma_{k}\equiv\alpha
, and
\eta_{k}\equiv(\beta\alpha-\lambda)/(1-\beta)
into (
3.3
) yields

x_{k+1}=y_{k+1}+\beta(y_{k+1}-y_{k})+\lambda g_{k}=(1+\beta)y_{k+1}-\beta y_{k}+\lambda g_{k},

which matches (
MASS
).

Finally, we establish property (iii). By substituting the definitions of
y_{k+1}
,
z_{k}
, and
z_{k+1}
into the update rule for
x_{k+1}
in (
SUM
), we can rewrite (
SUM
) via a single line:

\displaystyle x_{k+1}=

\displaystyle x_{k}-\alpha g_{k}+\beta(x_{k}-s\alpha g_{k}-x_{k-1}+s\alpha g_{k-1})

(3.4)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x_{k}+\beta(x_{k}-x_{k-1})-(1+s\beta)\alpha g_{k}+s\beta\alpha g_{k-1}.

On the other hand, rearranging (
3.2
) in a similar way as (
3.4
), we obtain

x_{k+1}=x_{k}+\frac{\beta_{k}\eta_{k}}{\eta_{k-1}}(x_{k}-x_{k-1})-(\gamma_{k}+(1-\beta_{k})\eta_{k})g_{k}+\frac{\beta_{k}\eta_{k}}{\eta_{k-1}}\gamma_{k-1}g_{k-1},

which coincides with (
3.4
) when
\beta_{k}\equiv\beta
,
\gamma_{k}\equiv s\alpha
, and
\eta_{k}\equiv\alpha/(1-\beta)-s\alpha
. The proof is now complete.
∎

Having established that (
G-SGDM
) unifies these existing methods, we now specify the assumptions under which we will analyze its convergence properties throughout the paper.

Assumption 1
.

The objective function
f
is
L
-smooth. Moreover, the gradient approximations
\{g_{k}\}
are unbiased and have uniformly bounded variance
\sigma^{2}\geq 0
, i.e.,

\mathbb{E}_{k}[g_{k}]=\nabla f(x_{k}),\quad\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|g_{k}-\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}]\leq\sigma^{2},~\forall k\geq 1.

Observe that from Assumption
1
we have

\mathbb{E}[\|g_{k}\|^{2}]=\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}]+\mathbb{E}[\|g_{k}-\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}]\leq\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}]+\sigma^{2}.

(3.5)

4
Convergence for Convex Problems

In this section, we establish convergence guarantees for (
G-SGDM
) when applied to convex optimization problems. We first analyze the case of constant parameters and then derive accelerated convergence rates by employing carefully designed time-varying parameters. Throughout this section, we assume that the objective function
f
in (
1.1
) admits a global minimizer
x^{*}\in\arg\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x)
.

4.1
Convergence Results with Constant Parameters

To establish convergence of (
G-SGDM
) with constant parameters
\beta_{k}\equiv\beta\in[0,1),\gamma_{k}\equiv\gamma\geq 0
, and
\eta_{k}\equiv\eta\in\mathbb{R}
, we first introduce an auxiliary sequence
\{w_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
defined by

w_{k}=\begin{cases}x_{1},\quad\text{if }k=1&\\
\frac{1}{1-\beta}(x_{k}-\beta x_{k-1}+\beta\gamma g_{k-1}),&\text{if }k\geq 2.\end{cases}

(4.1)

The following lemma shows that this auxiliary sequence
\{w_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
evolves according to a simple SGD update rule, which facilitates the convergence analysis significantly.

Lemma 1
.

Let the sequence
\{w_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
be defined in (
4.1
), where
\{x_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
are the iterates generated by (
G-SGDM
) with constant parameters
\beta\in[0,1)
,
\gamma\geq 0
, and
\eta\in\mathbb{R}
. Then for any
k\geq 1
, we have

w_{k+1}=w_{k}-(\gamma+\eta)g_{k}.

Proof of Lemma
1
.

Let us first verify the case
k=1
. Note that
m_{1}=(1-\beta)g_{1}
since
m_{0}=0
, hence we know that
x_{2}-x_{1}=-\gamma g_{1}-(1-\beta)\eta g_{1}
from the second equation in (
G-SGDM
), which further implies that

\displaystyle w_{2}-w_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-\beta}(x_{2}-\beta x_{1}+\beta\gamma g_{1})-x_{1}=\frac{1}{1-\beta}(x_{2}-x_{1}+\beta\gamma g_{1})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-\beta}(-\gamma g_{1}-(1-\beta)\eta g_{1}+\beta\gamma g_{1})=-(\gamma+\eta)g_{1}.

For
k\geq 2
, it follows from (
4.1
), together with the update rules for (
G-SGDM
) that

\displaystyle w_{k+1}-w_{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-\beta}[x_{k+1}-\beta x_{k}+\beta\gamma g_{k}-(x_{k}-\beta x_{k-1}+\beta\gamma g_{k-1})]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-\beta}[x_{k+1}-x_{k}-\beta(x_{k}-x_{k-1})+\beta\gamma g_{k}-\beta\gamma g_{k-1}]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-\beta}(-\gamma g_{k}-\eta m_{k}+\beta\gamma g_{k-1}+\beta\eta m_{k-1}+\beta\gamma g_{k}-\beta\gamma g_{k-1})

\displaystyle=-\gamma g_{k}-\frac{\eta}{1-\beta}(m_{k}-\beta m_{k-1})

\displaystyle=-\gamma g_{k}-\eta g_{k}=-(\gamma+\eta)g_{k}.

The proof is now complete.
∎

Lemma
1
reveals that the auxiliary sequence
\{w_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
is generated via SGD-like updates with constant step size
\gamma+\eta
, which serves as the “effective” step size in the sense that it should be positive to ensure the convergence of (
G-SGDM
).
Now we are ready to present the main convergence result for (
G-SGDM
) with constant parameters.

Theorem 1
.

Let Assumption
1
hold, and the function
f
in (
1.1
) be convex. Let
\{x_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
be the sequence generated by (
G-SGDM
) with constant parameters
\beta\in[0,1)
,
\gamma\geq 0
, and
\eta\in\mathbb{R}
. Denote
\bar{x}_{t}:=\frac{1}{t}\sum_{k=1}^{t}x_{k}
. If we choose

\gamma\leq\frac{1}{L},\quad 0<\gamma+\eta\leq\frac{1}{L},

then for any
t\geq 1
, it holds that

\mathbb{E}[f(\bar{x}_{t})]-f^{*}\leq\frac{\|x_{1}-x^{*}\|^{2}}{2(\gamma+\eta)t}+\frac{\beta(f(x_{1})-f^{*})}{(1-\beta)t}+\Big(\frac{3\beta\gamma}{2(1-\beta)}+\frac{\gamma+\eta}{2}\Big)\sigma^{2},

Proof of Theorem
1
.

We start by expanding
\|w_{k+1}-x^{*}\|^{2}
using Lemma
1
:

\displaystyle\|w_{k+1}-x^{*}\|^{2}=

\displaystyle\|w_{k}-x^{*}\|^{2}+2\langle w_{k+1}-w_{k},w_{k}-x^{*}\rangle+\|w_{k+1}-w_{k}\|^{2}

(4.2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|w_{k}-x^{*}\|^{2}-2(\gamma+\eta)\langle g_{k},w_{k}-x^{*}\rangle+(\gamma+\eta)^{2}\|g_{k}\|^{2}.

For
k\geq 2
, we know from (
4.1
) and
\mathbb{E}_{k}[g_{k}]=\nabla f(x_{k})
that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\langle g_{k},w_{k}-x^{*}\rangle]=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-\beta}\mathbb{E}[\langle g_{k},x_{k}-\beta x_{k-1}+\beta\gamma g_{k-1}-(1-\beta)x^{*}\rangle]

(4.3)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\langle g_{k},x_{k}-x^{*}\rangle]+\frac{\beta}{1-\beta}\mathbb{E}[\langle g_{k},x_{k}-x_{k-1}\rangle]+\frac{\beta\gamma}{1-\beta}\mathbb{E}[\langle g_{k},g_{k-1}\rangle]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\langle\nabla f(x_{k}),x_{k}-x^{*}\rangle]+\frac{\beta}{1-\beta}\mathbb{E}[\langle\nabla f(x_{k}),x_{k}-x_{k-1}\rangle]+\frac{\beta\gamma}{1-\beta}\mathbb{E}[\langle g_{k},g_{k-1}\rangle].

Since
f
is convex and
L
-smooth, it holds from (
2.1
) and
\nabla f(x^{*})=0
that

\displaystyle\langle\nabla f(x_{k}),x_{k}-x^{*}\rangle

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2L}\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}+f(x_{k})-f^{*},

(4.4)

\displaystyle\langle\nabla f(x_{k}),x_{k}-x_{k-1}\rangle

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2L}\|\nabla f(x_{k})-\nabla f(x_{k-1})\|^{2}+f(x_{k})-f(x_{k-1}).

(4.5)

To bound
\mathbb{E}[\langle g_{k},g_{k-1}\rangle]
, denote
\epsilon_{k}:=g_{k}-\nabla f(x_{k})
for all
k\geq 1
. Then it follows from
\mathbb{E}_{k}[\epsilon_{k}]=0
and
\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|\epsilon_{k}\|^{2}]\leq\sigma^{2}
that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\langle g_{k},g_{k-1}\rangle]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[\langle\nabla f(x_{k}),\nabla f(x_{k-1})\rangle]+\mathbb{E}[\langle\epsilon_{k},\epsilon_{k-1}\rangle]+\mathbb{E}[\langle\epsilon_{k},\nabla f(x_{k-1})]+\mathbb{E}[\langle\nabla f(x_{k}),\epsilon_{k-1})]

(4.6)

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{E}[\langle\nabla f(x_{k}),\nabla f(x_{k-1})\rangle]-\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}[\|\epsilon_{k}\|^{2}]-\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}[\|\epsilon_{k-1}\|^{2}]-\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}]-\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}[\|\epsilon_{k-1}\|^{2}]

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{E}[\langle\nabla f(x_{k}),\nabla f(x_{k-1})\rangle]-\frac{3}{2}\sigma^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}],

where the first inequality holds from the basic inequality
ab\geq-(a^{2}+b^{2})/2
and

\mathbb{E}[\langle\epsilon_{k},\nabla f(x_{k-1})]=\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{E}_{k}[\langle\epsilon_{k},\nabla f(x_{k-1})]]=\mathbb{E}[\langle\mathbb{E}_{k}[\epsilon_{k}],\nabla f(x_{k-1})]=0.

Applying (
4.4
), (
4.5
), and (
4.6
) on (
4.3
), we obtain for
k\geq 2
that

\mathbb{E}[\langle g_{k},w_{k}-x^{*}\rangle]\geq\frac{1}{2L}\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}]+\mathbb{E}[f(x_{k})-f^{*}]+\frac{\beta}{2L(1-\beta)}\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla f(x_{k})-\nabla f(x_{k-1})\|^{2}]\\
+\frac{\beta}{1-\beta}\mathbb{E}[f(x_{k})-f(x_{k-1})]+\frac{\beta\gamma}{1-\beta}\mathbb{E}[\langle\nabla f(x_{k}),\nabla f(x_{k-1})\rangle]-\frac{3\beta\gamma}{2(1-\beta)}\sigma^{2}-\frac{\beta\gamma}{2(1-\beta)}\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}].

(4.7)

If
k=1
, then it follows from
w_{1}=x_{1}
and (
4.4
) that

\mathbb{E}[\langle g_{1},w_{1}-x^{*}\rangle]=\langle\nabla f(x_{1}),x_{1}-x^{*}\rangle\geq\frac{1}{2L}\|\nabla f(x_{1})\|^{2}+f(x_{1})-f^{*}.

(4.8)

For notational convenience, denote
\Delta_{k}:=f(x_{k})-f^{*}\geq 0
. For
k\geq 2
, taking expectations on both sides of (
4.2
) and applying (
4.7
) and (
3.5
) leads to

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\|w_{k+1}-x^{*}\|^{2}]=\mathbb{E}[\|w_{k}-x^{*}\|^{2}]-2(\gamma+\eta)\mathbb{E}[\langle g_{k},w_{k}-x^{*}\rangle]+(\gamma+\eta)^{2}\mathbb{E}[\|g_{k}\|^{2}]

(4.9)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\|w_{k}-x^{*}\|^{2}]-\frac{\gamma+\eta}{L}\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}]-2(\gamma+\eta)\mathbb{E}[\Delta_{k}]-\frac{\beta(\gamma+\eta)}{L(1-\beta)}\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla f(x_{k})-\nabla f(x_{k-1})\|^{2}]

\displaystyle-\frac{2\beta(\gamma+\eta)}{1-\beta}\mathbb{E}[\Delta_{k}-\Delta_{k-1}]-\frac{2\beta\gamma(\gamma+\eta)}{1-\beta}\mathbb{E}[\langle\nabla f(x_{k}),\nabla f(x_{k-1})\rangle]+\frac{3\beta\gamma(\gamma+\eta)}{1-\beta}\sigma^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{\beta\gamma(\gamma+\eta)}{1-\beta}\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}]+(\gamma+\eta)^{2}\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}]+(\gamma+\eta)^{2}\sigma^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\|w_{k}-x^{*}\|^{2}]-2(\gamma+\eta)\mathbb{E}[\Delta_{k}]-\frac{2\beta(\gamma+\eta)}{1-\beta}\mathbb{E}[\Delta_{k}-\Delta_{k-1}]+\Big(\frac{3\beta\gamma(\gamma+\eta)}{1-\beta}+(\gamma+\eta)^{2}\Big)\sigma^{2}

\displaystyle-\Big(\frac{\gamma+\eta}{L}-\frac{\beta\gamma(\gamma+\eta)}{1-\beta}-(\gamma+\eta)^{2}+\frac{\beta\gamma(\gamma+\eta)}{1-\beta}\Big)\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}]-\frac{\beta\gamma(\gamma+\eta)}{1-\beta}\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla f(x_{k-1})\|^{2}]

\displaystyle-\Big(\frac{1}{L}-\gamma\Big)\frac{\beta(\gamma+\eta)}{1-\beta}\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla f(x_{k})-\nabla f(x_{k-1})\|^{2}],

where the last inequality holds by expanding the square term with coefficient
-\frac{\beta\gamma(\gamma+\eta)}{1-\beta}
on the RHS of

-\frac{\beta(\gamma+\eta)}{L(1-\beta)}\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla f(x_{k})-\nabla f(x_{k-1})\|^{2}]=\Big[-\frac{\beta\gamma(\gamma+\eta)}{1-\beta}-\Big(\frac{1}{L}-\gamma\Big)\frac{\beta(\gamma+\eta)}{1-\beta}\Big]\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla f(x_{k})-\nabla f(x_{k-1})\|^{2}].

Since
0<\gamma+\eta\leq 1/L
, we know that

\frac{\gamma+\eta}{L}-(\gamma+\eta)^{2}\geq 0,

(4.10)

which together with
0\leq\gamma\leq 1/L
indicates that the coefficients of the last three terms are all non-positive, and hence for any
k\geq 2
we have from (
4.9
) that

\mathbb{E}[\|w_{k+1}-x^{*}\|^{2}]\leq\mathbb{E}[\|w_{k}-x^{*}\|^{2}]-2(\gamma+\eta)\mathbb{E}[\Delta_{k}]-\frac{2\beta(\gamma+\eta)}{1-\beta}\mathbb{E}[\Delta_{k}-\Delta_{k-1}]+\Big(\frac{3\beta\gamma(\gamma+\eta)}{1-\beta}+(\gamma+\eta)^{2}\Big)\sigma^{2}.

(4.11)

On the other hand, if
k=1
, then taking expectations on both sides of (
4.2
) and applying (
4.8
) and (
3.5
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\|w_{2}-x^{*}\|^{2}]=\|x_{1}-x^{*}\|^{2}-2(\gamma+\eta)\mathbb{E}[\langle g_{1},w_{1}-x^{*}\rangle]+(\gamma+\eta)^{2}\mathbb{E}[\|g_{1}\|^{2}]

(4.12)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|x_{1}-x^{*}\|^{2}-\frac{\gamma+\eta}{L}\|\nabla f(x_{1})\|^{2}-2(\gamma+\eta)\Delta_{1}+(\gamma+\eta)^{2}\|\nabla f(x_{1})\|^{2}+(\gamma+\eta)^{2}\sigma^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|x_{1}-x^{*}\|^{2}-2(\gamma+\eta)\Delta_{1}+(\gamma+\eta)^{2}\sigma^{2},

where the last inequality holds due to (
4.10
).

Summing (
4.12
) and (
4.11
) over
k=2,\dots,t
and rearranging the terms gives

\displaystyle 2(\gamma+\eta)\sum_{k=1}^{t}\mathbb{E}[\Delta_{k}]\leq

\displaystyle\|x_{1}-x^{*}\|^{2}-\mathbb{E}[\|w_{t+1}-x^{*}\|^{2}]+\frac{2\beta(\gamma+\eta)}{1-\beta}(\Delta_{1}-\mathbb{E}[\Delta_{t}])+\Big(\frac{3\beta\gamma(\gamma+\eta)}{1-\beta}+(\gamma+\eta)^{2}\Big)t\sigma^{2}

(4.13)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|x_{1}-x^{*}\|^{2}+\frac{2\beta(\gamma+\eta)}{1-\beta}(f(x_{1})-f^{*})+\Big(\frac{3\beta\gamma(\gamma+\eta)}{1-\beta}+(\gamma+\eta)^{2}\Big)t\sigma^{2},

where the last inequality follows by dropping the non-positive terms. Finally, dividing both sides of (
4.13
) by
2(\gamma+\eta)t
and using the convexity of
f
, we derive

\mathbb{E}[f(\bar{x}_{t})]-f^{*}\leq\frac{1}{t}\sum_{k=1}^{t}\mathbb{E}[\Delta_{k}]\leq\frac{\|x_{1}-x^{*}\|^{2}}{2(\gamma+\eta)t}+\frac{\beta(f(x_{1})-f^{*})}{(1-\beta)t}+\Big(\frac{3\beta\gamma}{2(1-\beta)}+\frac{\gamma+\eta}{2}\Big)\sigma^{2},

which completes the proof.
∎

In Theorem
1
, the step size condition
\gamma+\eta\leq 1/L
reveals an explicit trade-off between
\gamma
and
\eta
, preventing them from being large simultaneously. Besides, since the upper bound on
\gamma+\eta
is independent of
\beta
, Theorem
1
favors the choice
\beta=0
. We now show that when exact gradients are available, the convergence behavior of (
G-SGDM
) improves substantially. In this regime, the restrictive coupling between
\gamma
and
\eta
can be relaxed, leading to a larger admissible step size region.

Theorem 2

(Deterministic Case)
.

Let the objective function
f
in (
1.1
) be convex and
L
-smooth. Let
\{x_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
be the sequence generated by (
G-SGDM
) with true gradients
g_{k}=\nabla f(x_{k})
and constant parameters
\beta\in[0,1)
,
\gamma\geq 0
, and
\eta\in\mathbb{R}
. Denote
\bar{x}_{t}:=\frac{1}{t}\sum_{k=1}^{t}x_{k}
. If the step sizes
\gamma
and
\eta
satisfy

\gamma\leq\frac{1}{L},\quad-\gamma<\eta\leq\frac{1}{L}+\frac{2\beta-1}{1-\beta}\gamma,

(4.14)

then for any
t\geq 1
, it holds that

f(\bar{x}_{t})-f^{*}\leq\frac{\|x_{1}-x^{*}\|^{2}}{2(\gamma+\eta)t}+\frac{\beta(f(x_{1})-f^{*}+\gamma\|\nabla f(x_{1})\|^{2}/2)}{(1-\beta)t}.

Proof of Theorem
2
.

Imitating the deductions of (
4.2
) and (
4.3
) with
g_{k}=\nabla f(x_{k})
, we have for
k\geq 2
that

\displaystyle\|w_{k+1}-x^{*}\|^{2}=\|w_{k}-x^{*}\|^{2}+2\langle w_{k+1}-w_{k},w_{k}-x^{*}\rangle+\|w_{k+1}-w_{k}\|^{2}

(4.15)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|w_{k}-x^{*}\|^{2}-2(\gamma+\eta)\langle\nabla f(x_{k}),w_{k}-x^{*}\rangle+(\gamma+\eta)^{2}\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|w_{k}-x^{*}\|^{2}-\frac{2(\gamma+\eta)}{1-\beta}\langle\nabla f(x_{k}),x_{k}-\beta x_{k-1}+\beta\gamma\nabla f(x_{k-1})-(1-\beta)x^{*}\rangle+(\gamma+\eta)^{2}\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|w_{k}-x^{*}\|^{2}-2(\gamma+\eta)\langle\nabla f(x_{k}),x_{k}-x^{*}\rangle-\frac{2\beta(\gamma+\eta)}{1-\beta}\langle\nabla f(x_{k}),x_{k}-x_{k-1}\rangle

\displaystyle-\frac{2\beta\gamma(\gamma+\eta)}{1-\beta}\langle\nabla f(x_{k}),\nabla f(x_{k-1})\rangle+(\gamma+\eta)^{2}\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}.

Applying (
4.4
) and (
4.5
) on (
4.15
) and rearranging the terms further gives

\displaystyle\|w_{k+1}-x^{*}\|^{2}-\|w_{k}-x^{*}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\frac{\gamma+\eta}{L}\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}-2(\gamma+\eta)(f(x_{k})-f^{*})-\frac{\beta(\gamma+\eta)}{L(1-\beta)}\|\nabla f(x_{k})-\nabla f(x_{k-1})\|^{2}

\displaystyle-\frac{2\beta(\gamma+\eta)}{1-\beta}(f(x_{k})-f(x_{k-1}))-\frac{2\beta\gamma(\gamma+\eta)}{1-\beta}\langle\nabla f(x_{k}),\nabla f(x_{k-1})\rangle+(\gamma+\eta)^{2}\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2(\gamma+\eta)(f(x_{k})-f^{*})-\frac{2\beta(\gamma+\eta)}{1-\beta}(f(x_{k})-f(x_{k-1}))-\Big(\frac{\gamma+\eta}{L}-(\gamma+\eta)^{2}+\frac{\beta\gamma(\gamma+\eta)}{1-\beta}\Big)\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}

\displaystyle-\frac{\beta\gamma(\gamma+\eta)}{1-\beta}\|\nabla f(x_{k-1})\|^{2}-\Big(\frac{1}{L}-\gamma\Big)\frac{\beta(\gamma+\eta)}{1-\beta}\|\nabla f(x_{k})-\nabla f(x_{k-1})\|^{2}.

(4.16)

From the step size condition (
4.14
), we know that
\gamma+\eta>0
and hence

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma+\eta}{L}+\frac{\beta\gamma(\gamma+\eta)}{1-\beta}-(\gamma+\eta)^{2}=(\gamma+\eta)\Big(\frac{1}{L}+\frac{\beta\gamma}{1-\beta}-\gamma-\eta\Big)

(4.17)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(\gamma+\eta)\Big(\frac{1}{L}+\frac{\beta\gamma}{1-\beta}-\gamma-\frac{1}{L}-\frac{2\beta-1}{1-\beta}\gamma\Big)=0.

It then follows that the coefficients of the last three terms on the RHS of (
4.16
) are all non-positive due to
0\leq\gamma\leq 1/L
. Then for
k\geq 2
, we further obtain from (
4.16
) that

\|w_{k+1}-x^{*}\|^{2}-\|w_{k}-x^{*}\|^{2}\leq-2(\gamma+\eta)(f(x_{k})-f^{*})-\frac{2\beta(\gamma+\eta)}{1-\beta}(f(x_{k})-f(x_{k-1})).

(4.18)

On the other hand, if
k=1
, then we know from
w_{1}=x_{1}
and (
4.4
) that

\displaystyle\|w_{2}-x^{*}\|^{2}=

\displaystyle\|x_{1}-x^{*}\|^{2}-2(\gamma+\eta)\langle\nabla f(x_{1}),x_{1}-x^{*}\rangle+(\gamma+\eta)^{2}\|\nabla f(x_{1})\|^{2}

(4.19)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|x_{1}-x^{*}\|^{2}-\frac{\gamma+\eta}{L}\|\nabla f(x_{1})\|^{2}-2(\gamma+\eta)(f(x_{1})-f^{*})+(\gamma+\eta)^{2}\|\nabla f(x_{1})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|x_{1}-x^{*}\|^{2}-2(\gamma+\eta)(f(x_{1})-f^{*})+\frac{\beta\gamma(\gamma+\eta)}{1-\beta}\|\nabla f(x_{1})\|^{2},

where the last inequality holds from (
4.17
).

Summing (
4.19
) and (
4.18
) over
k=2,\dots,t
and rearranging the terms gives

\displaystyle 2(\gamma+\eta)\sum_{k=1}^{t}(f(x_{k})-f^{*})

(4.20)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|x_{1}-x^{*}\|^{2}-\|w_{t+1}-x^{*}\|^{2}+\frac{2\beta(\gamma+\eta)}{1-\beta}(f(x_{1})-f(x_{t}))+\frac{\beta\gamma(\gamma+\eta)}{1-\beta}\|\nabla f(x_{1})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|x_{1}-x^{*}\|^{2}+\frac{\beta(\gamma+\eta)}{1-\beta}\big[2(f(x_{1})-f^{*})+\gamma\|\nabla f(x_{1})\|^{2}\big],

where the last inequality follows from
f(x_{t})\geq f^{*}
.
Finally, dividing both sides of (
4.20
) by
2(\gamma+\eta)t
and using the convexity of
f
leads to

f(\bar{x}_{t})-f^{*}\leq\frac{1}{t}\sum_{k=1}^{t}(f(x_{k})-f^{*})\leq\frac{\|x_{1}-x^{*}\|^{2}}{2(\gamma+\eta)t}+\frac{\beta}{(1-\beta)t}\big(f(x_{1})-f^{*}+\gamma\|\nabla f(x_{1})\|^{2}/2\big),

which completes the proof.
∎

Remark 1
.

Note that while
\gamma=\frac{1}{L}
is a standard choice for vanilla gradient descent, the step size
\eta
in our framework can exceed
1/L
when
\beta>0.5
. This partially explains the benefit of incorporating momentum. In particular, the maximal admissible value
\frac{1}{L}+\frac{2\beta-1}{1-\beta}\gamma
is monotonically increasing in
\beta
. Consequently, substantially larger step sizes
\eta
can be employed when
\beta
is chosen close to one, as is common in practice (e.g.,
\beta=0.9
or 0.99), potentially leading to faster convergence.

Remark 2
.

By setting
\gamma=0
in Theorem
2
, we obtain an
O(1/t)
ergodic convergence rate for the heavy-ball (HB) method (i.e., (
SGD-HB
) with constant parameters) for any
\beta\in[0,1)
and
0<\eta\leq 1/L
:

f(\bar{x}_{t})-f^{*}\leq\frac{\|x_{1}-x^{*}\|^{2}}{2\eta t}+\frac{\beta(f(x_{1})-f^{*})}{(1-\beta)t}.

(4.21)

Moreover, since
\gamma\leq 1/L
, choosing
\eta=\frac{\beta\gamma}{1-\beta}
satisfies the step size condition (
4.14
) as
\frac{\beta\gamma}{1-\beta}=\gamma+\frac{2\beta-1}{1-\beta}\gamma\leq\frac{1}{L}+\frac{2\beta-1}{1-\beta}\gamma
.
Therefore, substituting
\eta=\frac{\beta\gamma}{1-\beta}
into Theorem
2
yields an
O(1/t)
ergodic convergence rate for Nesterov’s accelerated gradient (NAG) method (i.e., (
SGD-N
) with constant parameters) for any
\beta\in[0,1)
and
0<\gamma\leq 1/L
:

f(\bar{x}_{t})-f^{*}\leq\frac{(1-\beta)\|x_{1}-x^{*}\|^{2}}{2\gamma t}+\frac{\beta(f(x_{1})-f^{*}+\gamma\|\nabla f(x_{1})\|^{2}/2)}{(1-\beta)t}.

(4.22)

Similar convergence results for HB in (
4.21
) and NAG in (
4.22
) were previously established by
Ghadimi et al. 2015
via separate analyses. In contrast, these bounds arise here as direct consequences of the unified analysis developed in Theorem
2
. Furthermore, convergence guarantees for a broad class of momentum schemes can be derived for both deterministic settings (as in Theorem
2
) and stochastic settings (as in Theorem
1
).

4.2
Improved Convergence Results with Time-varying Parameters

While Theorems
1
and
2
establish convergence guarantees for a broad class of constant parameter choices, the resulting rates are limited to
O(1/t)
. Consequently, the well-known optimal rate
O(1/t^{2})
achieved by NAG is not covered by these results. This limitation stems from the fact that the
O(1/t^{2})
rate is attained through the use of time-varying momentum parameters rather than constant ones.
In this subsection, we therefore turn to the case of time-varying parameters and show that, under suitable conditions, (
G-SGDM
) enjoys improved convergence guarantees. In particular, our analysis recovers the accelerated
O(1/t^{2})
rate of NAG as a direct consequence.

Recall that we construct an auxiliary sequence
\{w_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
defined in (
4.1
) to facilitate the convergence analysis when using constant parameters in (
G-SGDM
). However, a similar construction of
\{w_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
with time-varying parameters
\beta_{k},\gamma_{k}
, and
\eta_{k}
replacing their constant counterparts is generally not useful, because the SGD-like property (see Lemma
1
) no longer holds. To preserve this property, we impose the following condition on the parameters: there exists a sequence
\{\theta_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
with
\theta_{k}\in(0,1)
such that

\frac{\beta_{k}\eta_{k}}{\eta_{k-1}}=\theta_{k+1}\Big(\frac{1}{\theta_{k}}-1\Big),~\forall k\geq 2.

(4.23)

It is straightforward to verify that
\beta_{k}\in[0,1)
holds under condition (
4.23
) in many cases (e.g., with constant
\eta_{k}\equiv\eta\neq 0
and non-increasing
\{\theta_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
). Using
\{\theta_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
, we define an auxiliary sequence
\{v_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
as follows:

v_{k}=\begin{cases}x_{1},\quad\text{if }k=1&\\
\frac{1}{\theta_{k}}\big(x_{k}-(1-\theta_{k})x_{k-1}+(1-\theta_{k})\gamma_{k-1}g_{k-1}\big),&\text{if }k\geq 2.\end{cases}

(4.24)

We also introduce an intermediate sequence
\{y_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
defined by

y_{k}=\begin{cases}x_{1},\quad\text{if }k=1&\\
x_{k-1}-\gamma_{k-1}g_{k-1},&\text{if }k\geq 2.\end{cases}

(4.25)

In the upcoming lemma, we show that
\{v_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
exhibits an SGD-like behavior, similar to Lemma
1
. Furthermore, the difference
v_{k}-x_{k}
is proportional to
x_{k}-y_{k}
. These properties provide crucial technical support for establishing refined convergence results.

Lemma 2
.

Consider the sequences
\{v_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
and
\{y_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
defined in (
4.24
) and (
4.25
) respectively, where
\{x_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
are the iterates generated by (
G-SGDM
). Let the condition (
4.23
) hold.
Then for any
k\geq 1
, it holds that

\displaystyle v_{k+1}=v_{k}-\Big(\gamma_{k}+\frac{1-\beta_{k}}{\theta_{k+1}}\eta_{k}\Big)g_{k},

(4.26)

\displaystyle v_{k}-x_{k}=\Big(\frac{1}{\theta_{k}}-1\Big)(x_{k}-y_{k})

(4.27)

Proof of Lemma
2
.

For
k=1
, we have
m_{1}=(1-\beta_{1})g_{1}
since
m_{0}=0
, and hence
x_{2}-x_{1}=-\gamma_{1}g_{1}-(1-\beta_{1})\eta_{1}g_{1}
from the second equation in (
G-SGDM
), which further implies that

\displaystyle v_{2}-v_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\theta_{2}}(x_{2}-(1-\theta_{2})x_{1}+(1-\theta_{2})\gamma_{1}g_{1})-x_{1}=\frac{1}{\theta_{2}}(x_{2}-x_{1}+(1-\theta_{2})\gamma_{1}g_{1})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\theta_{2}}(-\gamma_{1}g_{1}-(1-\beta_{1})\eta_{1}g_{1}+(1-\theta_{2})\gamma_{1}g_{1})=-\Big(\gamma_{1}+\frac{1-\beta_{1}}{\theta_{2}}\eta_{1}\Big)g_{1}.

Hence (
4.26
) holds with
k=1
.
For
k\geq 2
, it follows from (
4.24
) and the update rules of (
G-SGDM
) that

\displaystyle v_{k+1}-v_{k}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\theta_{k+1}}\big(x_{k+1}-(1-\theta_{k+1})x_{k}+(1-\theta_{k+1})\gamma_{k}g_{k}\big)-\frac{1}{\theta_{k}}\big(x_{k}-(1-\theta_{k})x_{k-1}+(1-\theta_{k})\gamma_{k-1}g_{k-1}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\theta_{k+1}}\big(x_{k+1}-x_{k}+(1-\theta_{k+1})\gamma_{k}g_{k}\big)-\frac{1}{\theta_{k}}\big(x_{k}-x_{k-1}+(1-\theta_{k})\gamma_{k-1}g_{k-1}\big)+x_{k}-x_{k-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\theta_{k+1}}\big(-\theta_{k+1}\gamma_{k}g_{k}-\eta_{k}m_{k}\big)-\frac{1}{\theta_{k}}\big(-\theta_{k}\gamma_{k-1}g_{k-1}-\eta_{k-1}m_{k-1}\big)-\gamma_{k-1}g_{k-1}-\eta_{k-1}m_{k-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\gamma_{k}g_{k}-\frac{\eta_{k}}{\theta_{k+1}}\Big(m_{k}-\frac{\theta_{k+1}}{\eta_{k}}\Big(\frac{1}{\theta_{k}}-1\Big)\eta_{k-1}m_{k-1}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\gamma_{k}g_{k}-\frac{\eta_{k}}{\theta_{k+1}}(m_{k}-\beta_{k}m_{k-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\gamma_{k}g_{k}-\frac{\eta_{k}}{\theta_{k+1}}(1-\beta_{k})g_{k},

where we apply (
4.23
) to obtain the second last equality. Therefore, (
4.26
) holds for all
k\geq 1
.

We proceed to prove (
4.27
). Note it holds trivially for
k=1
since
v_{1}=y_{1}=x_{1}
, hence we consider
k\geq 2
in what follows. From the definitions of
v_{k}
and
y_{k}
, together with the update rules of (
G-SGDM
), we derive

\displaystyle v_{k}-x_{k}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\theta_{k}}\big(x_{k}-(1-\theta_{k})x_{k-1}+(1-\theta_{k})\gamma_{k-1}g_{k-1}\big)-x_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{1}{\theta_{k}}-1\Big)x_{k}-\frac{1-\theta_{k}}{\theta_{k}}\big(x_{k-1}-\gamma_{k-1}g_{k-1}\big)=\Big(\frac{1}{\theta_{k}}-1\Big)(x_{k}-y_{k}).

The proof is now complete.
∎

Lemma
2
indicates that the “effective” step sizes are
\{\gamma_{k}+\frac{1-\beta_{k}}{\theta_{k+1}}\eta_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
, which should be positive to ensure the convergence of (
G-SGDM
).
In the upcoming theorem, we present the refined convergence result of (
G-SGDM
) at the intermediate point
y_{t}
. To establish this result, we introduce a sequence of Lyapunov functions
\{\Phi_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
defined by

\Phi_{k}=\frac{1}{\Theta_{k-1}}(f(y_{k})-f^{*})+p_{k}\|v_{k}-x^{*}\|^{2},

(4.28)

where
\{p_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
is a positive and non-increasing sequence to be specified in the proof, and
\{\Theta_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
is defined recursively via

\Theta_{0}=1,~\text{and}~\Theta_{k}:=(1-\theta_{k})\Theta_{k-1},~\forall k\geq 1.

(4.29)

We shall show that if the sequence
\{p_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
is chosen appropriately, then the differences between consecutive Lyapunov functions can be bounded from above in expectation by terms proportional to
\sigma^{2}
, which is the key to our proof.

Theorem 3
.

Let Assumption
1
hold, and the objective function
f
in (
1.1
) be convex. Let
\{x_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
be the sequence generated by (
G-SGDM
). Suppose the parameters
\{\beta_{k},\gamma_{k},\eta_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
are chosen such that (
4.23
) and the following conditions hold for all
k\geq 1
:

\displaystyle\gamma_{k}+\frac{1-\beta_{k}}{\theta_{k+1}}\eta_{k}>0,

(4.30)

\displaystyle 2-L\gamma_{k}-\frac{\theta_{k}(1-\beta_{k})\eta_{k}}{\theta_{k+1}\gamma_{k}}-\theta_{k}\geq 0,

(4.31)

\displaystyle\frac{\theta_{k}}{\theta_{k+1}\gamma_{k}+(1-\beta_{k})\eta_{k}}\geq\frac{\theta_{k+2}}{(1-\theta_{k+1})(\theta_{k+2}\gamma_{k+1}+(1-\beta_{k+1})\eta_{k+1})}.

(4.32)

Then, for any
t\geq 1
, it holds that

\mathbb{E}[f(y_{t})]-f^{*}\leq\Phi_{1}\Theta_{t-1}+\sigma^{2}\sum_{k=1}^{t-1}\frac{\Theta_{t-1}}{2\Theta_{k}}\Big(L\gamma_{k}^{2}+\theta_{k}\Big(\gamma_{k}+\frac{1-\beta_{k}}{\theta_{k+1}}\eta_{k}\Big)\Big),

(4.33)

where
y_{t}
is defined in (
4.25
) and

\Phi_{1}:=f(x_{1})-f^{*}+\frac{\theta_{1}\theta_{2}}{2(1-\theta_{1})(\theta_{2}\gamma_{1}+(1-\beta_{1})\eta_{1})}\|x_{1}-x^{*}\|^{2}.

(4.34)

Proof of Theorem
3
.

For notational convenience, denote the effective step sizes as

\tilde{\gamma}_{k}:=\gamma_{k}+\frac{1-\beta_{k}}{\theta_{k+1}}\eta_{k}>0,

(4.35)

where the positivity is guaranteed by (
4.30
). Then it follows from (
4.26
) in Lemma
2
that

v_{k+1}=v_{k}-\tilde{\gamma}_{k}g_{k}.

(4.36)

Consider the Lyapunov functions
\{\Phi_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
given by (
4.28
) with

p_{k}:=\frac{\theta_{k}}{2\tilde{\gamma}_{k}\Theta_{k}}>0.

(4.37)

Then we know from (
4.35
) and the condition (
4.32
) that

\displaystyle p_{k+1}=

\displaystyle\frac{\theta_{k+1}}{2\tilde{\gamma}_{k+1}\Theta_{k+1}}=\frac{\theta_{k+1}}{2(1-\theta_{k+1})(\gamma_{k+1}+\frac{1-\beta_{k+1}}{\theta_{k+2}}\eta_{k+1})\Theta_{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\theta_{k+1}}{2\Theta_{k}}\frac{\theta_{k+2}}{(1-\theta_{k+1})(\theta_{k+2}\gamma_{k+1}+(1-\beta_{k+1})\eta_{k+1})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\theta_{k+1}}{2\Theta_{k}}\frac{\theta_{k}}{\theta_{k+1}\gamma_{k}+(1-\beta_{k})\eta_{k}}=\frac{\theta_{k}}{2\tilde{\gamma}_{k}\Theta_{k}}=p_{k},

which implies that
\{p_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
is non-increasing. Denote
\Delta\Phi_{k}:=\Phi_{k+1}-\Phi_{k}
, then it follows that

\Delta\Phi_{k}\leq\frac{1}{\Theta_{k-1}}(f(y_{k+1})-f(y_{k}))+\Big(\frac{1}{\Theta_{k}}-\frac{1}{\Theta_{k-1}}\Big)(f(y_{k+1})-f^{*})+p_{k}\big(\|v_{k+1}-x^{*}\|^{2}-\|v_{k}-x^{*}\|^{2}\big).

(4.38)

To bound the last term on the RHS of (
4.38
), we derive from (
4.36
) that

\|v_{k+1}-x^{*}\|^{2}-\|v_{k}-x^{*}\|^{2}=\|v_{k+1}-v_{k}\|^{2}+2\langle v_{k+1}-v_{k},v_{k}-x^{*}\rangle=\tilde{\gamma}_{k}^{2}\|g_{k}\|^{2}+2\tilde{\gamma}_{k}\langle g_{k},x^{*}-v_{k}\rangle.

(4.39)

From the
L
-smoothness of
f
and (
4.25
), we know that

\displaystyle f(y_{k+1})

\displaystyle\leq f(x_{k})+\langle\nabla f(x_{k}),y_{k+1}-x_{k}\rangle+\frac{L}{2}\|y_{k+1}-x_{k}\|^{2}

(4.40)

\displaystyle=f(x_{k})-\gamma_{k}\langle\nabla f(x_{k}),g_{k}\rangle+\frac{L\gamma_{k}^{2}}{2}\|g_{k}\|^{2}.

Plugging (
4.39
) and (
4.40
) into (
4.38
), together with the convexity of
f
gives

\displaystyle\Delta\Phi_{k}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Theta_{k-1}}(f(x_{k})-f(y_{k}))+\Big(\frac{1}{\Theta_{k}}-\frac{1}{\Theta_{k-1}}\Big)(f(x_{k})-f^{*})-\frac{\gamma_{k}}{\Theta_{k}}\langle\nabla f(x_{k}),g_{k}\rangle

(4.41)

\displaystyle+\Big(\frac{L\gamma_{k}^{2}}{2\Theta_{k}}+p_{k}\tilde{\gamma}_{k}^{2}\Big)\|g_{k}\|^{2}+2p_{k}\tilde{\gamma}_{k}\langle g_{k},x^{*}-v_{k}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Theta_{k-1}}\langle\nabla f(x_{k}),x_{k}-y_{k}\rangle+\Big(\frac{1}{\Theta_{k}}-\frac{1}{\Theta_{k-1}}\Big)\langle\nabla f(x_{k}),x_{k}-x^{*}\rangle-\frac{\gamma_{k}}{\Theta_{k}}\langle\nabla f(x_{k}),g_{k}\rangle

\displaystyle+\Big(\frac{L\gamma_{k}^{2}}{2\Theta_{k}}+p_{k}\tilde{\gamma}_{k}^{2}\Big)\|g_{k}\|^{2}+2p_{k}\tilde{\gamma}_{k}\langle g_{k},x^{*}-v_{k}\rangle.

Note from Assumption
1
that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\langle g_{k},x^{*}-v_{k}\rangle]=\mathbb{E}[\langle\nabla f(x_{k}),x^{*}-v_{k}\rangle],

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\langle\nabla f(x_{k}),g_{k}\rangle]=\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}],

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\|g_{k}\|^{2}]\leq\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}]+\sigma^{2}.

Therefore, taking expectations on both sides of (
4.41
) further leads to

\mathbb{E}[\Delta\Phi_{k}]\leq\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big\langle\nabla f(x_{k}),\frac{1}{\Theta_{k}}x_{k}-\frac{1}{\Theta_{k-1}}y_{k}-2p_{k}\tilde{\gamma}_{k}v_{k}+\Big(2p_{k}\tilde{\gamma}_{k}-\frac{1}{\Theta_{k}}+\frac{1}{\Theta_{k-1}}\Big)x^{*}\Big\rangle\Big]\\
-\Big(\frac{\gamma_{k}}{\Theta_{k}}-\frac{L\gamma_{k}^{2}}{2\Theta_{k}}-p_{k}\tilde{\gamma}_{k}^{2}\Big)\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}]+\Big(\frac{L\gamma_{k}^{2}}{2\Theta_{k}}+p_{k}\tilde{\gamma}_{k}^{2}\Big)\sigma^{2}.

(4.42)

To proceed, note from the definitions of
p_{k}
(
4.37
) and
\Theta_{k}
(
4.29
) that

2p_{k}\tilde{\gamma}_{k}-\frac{1}{\Theta_{k}}+\frac{1}{\Theta_{k-1}}=\frac{\theta_{k}}{\Theta_{k}}-\frac{1}{\Theta_{k}}+\frac{1}{\Theta_{k-1}}=\frac{\theta_{k}-1}{(1-\theta_{k})\Theta_{k-1}}+\frac{1}{\Theta_{k-1}}=0.

(4.43)

Moreover, we obtain from (
4.27
) and (
4.29
) that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Theta_{k}}x_{k}-\frac{1}{\Theta_{k-1}}y_{k}-2p_{k}\tilde{\gamma}_{k}v_{k}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Theta_{k}}x_{k}-\frac{1}{\Theta_{k-1}}y_{k}-\frac{\theta_{k}}{\Theta_{k}}v_{k}

(4.44)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Theta_{k}}(x_{k}-(1-\theta_{k})y_{k}-\theta_{k}v_{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Theta_{k}}\Big(x_{k}-(1-\theta_{k})y_{k}-\theta_{k}(x_{k}+(1/\theta_{k}-1)(x_{k}-y_{k}))\Big)=0,

which together with (
4.43
) indicates that the inner product term in (
4.42
) vanishes. Moreover, it follows from (
4.35
), condition (
4.31
), and the definition of
p_{k}
(
4.37
) that

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma_{k}}{\Theta_{k}}-\frac{L\gamma_{k}^{2}}{2\Theta_{k}}-p_{k}\tilde{\gamma}_{k}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma_{k}}{\Theta_{k}}-\frac{L\gamma_{k}^{2}}{2\Theta_{k}}-\frac{\theta_{k}}{2\Theta_{k}}\Big(\gamma_{k}+\frac{1-\beta_{k}}{\theta_{k+1}}\eta_{k}\Big)

(4.45)

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma_{k}}{2\Theta_{k}}\Big(2-L\gamma_{k}-\frac{\theta_{k}(1-\beta_{k})\eta_{k}}{\theta_{k+1}\gamma_{k}}-\theta_{k}\Big)\geq 0.

Combining (
4.43
), (
4.44
), and (
4.45
), we obtain from (
4.42
) that

\mathbb{E}[\Delta\Phi_{k}]\leq\Big(\frac{L\gamma_{k}^{2}}{2\Theta_{k}}+p_{k}\tilde{\gamma}_{k}^{2}\Big)\sigma^{2}=\Big(\frac{L\gamma_{k}^{2}}{2\Theta_{k}}+\frac{\theta_{k}}{2\Theta_{k}}\Big(\gamma_{k}+\frac{1-\beta_{k}}{\theta_{k+1}}\eta_{k}\Big)\Big)\sigma^{2}=\frac{1}{2\Theta_{k}}\Big(L\gamma_{k}^{2}+\theta_{k}\Big(\gamma_{k}+\frac{1-\beta_{k}}{\theta_{k+1}}\eta_{k}\Big)\Big)\sigma^{2},

which further gives

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Theta_{t-1}}\mathbb{E}[f(y_{t})-f^{*}]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}[\Phi_{t}]=\Phi_{1}+\sum_{k=1}^{t-1}\mathbb{E}[\Delta\Phi_{k}]

\displaystyle\leq\Phi_{1}+\sigma^{2}\sum_{k=1}^{t-1}\frac{1}{2\Theta_{k}}\Big(L\gamma_{k}^{2}+\theta_{k}\Big(\gamma_{k}+\frac{1-\beta_{k}}{\theta_{k+1}}\eta_{k}\Big)\Big).

Moreover, we know from
\Theta_{0}=1
and (
4.37
) that

p_{1}:=\frac{\theta_{1}}{2\tilde{\gamma}_{1}\Theta_{1}}=\frac{\theta_{1}}{2(1-\theta_{1})(\gamma_{1}+\frac{1-\beta_{1}}{\theta_{2}}\eta_{1})}=\frac{\theta_{1}\theta_{2}}{2(1-\theta_{1})(\theta_{2}\gamma_{1}+(1-\beta_{1})\eta_{1})},

which together with
v_{1}=y_{1}=x_{1}
implies (
4.34
). The proof is now complete.
∎

While Theorem
3
provides general convergence bounds for (
G-SGDM
) with time-varying parameters satisfying conditions (
4.23
) and (
4.30
)-(
4.32
), it does not yet reveal explicit convergence rates. To obtain concrete results, we next specify the parameters
\{\beta_{k},\gamma_{k},\eta_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
, for which all the required conditions hold automatically with a particular choice of
\{\theta_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
. This choice allows the bound in Theorem
3
to be evaluated in closed form, leading to the optimal
O(1/t^{2})
convergence rate in the deterministic setting and an
O(1/t^{2}+\sigma/\sqrt{t})
rate in the stochastic setting, as stated in the following corollary.

Corollary 1
.

Consider the same setting as in Theorem
3
. Let
\gamma_{k}\equiv\gamma\in(0,1/L]
. Given an arbitrary
\beta_{1}\in[0,1)
, define
\eta_{1}:=\frac{L\gamma^{2}-\gamma}{2(1-\beta_{1})}
. The parameters
\{\eta_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
and
\{\beta_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
are then generated recursively via

\displaystyle\eta_{k}=\frac{k}{k+3}\eta_{k-1}+\frac{(k+1)L\gamma^{2}-2\gamma}{k+3},~\forall k\geq 2,

(4.46)

\displaystyle\beta_{k}=\frac{k\eta_{k-1}}{(k+3)\eta_{k}},~\forall k\geq 2.

(4.47)

Then, for any
t\geq 1
, the following results hold.

(i).

In the deterministic setting with
\sigma^{2}=0
, choosing
\gamma=1/L
yields

\mathbb{E}[f(y_{t})]-f^{*}\leq\frac{2(f(x_{1})-f^{*}+L\|x_{1}-x^{*}\|^{2})}{t(t+1)}.

(ii).

In the stochastic setting with
\sigma^{2}>0
, let
C>0
and choose

\gamma=\min\Big\{\frac{1}{L},~t^{-3/4}\sqrt{\frac{C}{\sigma L}}\Big\}.

(4.48)

Then we have

\mathbb{E}[f(y_{t})]-f^{*}\leq\frac{2(f(x_{1})-f^{*}+L\|x_{1}-x^{*}\|^{2})}{t(t+1)}+\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{t}}\Big(\frac{\|x_{1}-x^{*}\|^{2}}{C}+\frac{11C}{3}\Big).

Proof of Corollary
1
.

Let
\theta_{k}:=\frac{2}{k+2}\in(0,1)
. Then it is straightforward to verify the condition (
4.23
) by (
4.47
) and observing that

\theta_{k+1}\Big(\frac{1}{\theta_{k}}-1\Big)=\theta_{k+1}/\theta_{k}-\theta_{k+1}=\frac{k+2}{k+3}-\frac{2}{k+3}=\frac{k}{k+3}.

Next, we show that the conditions (
4.30
), (
4.31
), and (
4.32
) are also satisfied with the given choices of
\{\theta_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
and parameters
\{\beta_{k},\gamma_{k},\eta_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
. To this end, first note from (
4.46
) and (
4.47
) that for any
k\geq 2
:

(1-\beta_{k})\eta_{k}=\eta_{k}-\beta_{k}\eta_{k}=\eta_{k}-\frac{k}{k+3}\eta_{k-1}=\frac{(k+1)L\gamma^{2}-2\gamma}{k+3}.

(4.49)

Since
\eta_{1}:=\frac{L\gamma^{2}-\gamma}{2(1-\beta_{1})}
, it follows that (
4.49
) also holds for
k=1
and hence we have

\gamma+\frac{1-\beta_{k}}{\theta_{k+1}}\eta_{k}=\gamma+\frac{k+3}{2}\cdot\frac{(k+1)L\gamma^{2}-2\gamma}{k+3}=\frac{(k+1)L\gamma^{2}}{2}>0,~\forall k\geq 1,

(4.50)

which verifies the condition (
4.30
). Moreover, we obtain from
\gamma\leq 1/L
and (
4.49
) that

\displaystyle 2-L\gamma-\frac{\theta_{k}(1-\beta_{k})\eta_{k}}{\theta_{k+1}\gamma}-\theta_{k}=

\displaystyle 2-L\gamma-\frac{k+3}{k+2}\cdot\frac{(k+1)L\gamma-2}{k+3}-\frac{2}{k+2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2-L\gamma-\frac{(k+1)L\gamma}{k+2}\geq 2-2L\gamma\geq 0,~\forall k\geq 1.

Hence, condition (
4.31
) is satisfied. Using (
4.49
), direct computation gives

\displaystyle\frac{\theta_{k}}{\theta_{k+1}\gamma+(1-\beta_{k})\eta_{k}}=\frac{2/(k+2)}{2\gamma/(k+3)+[(k+1)L\gamma^{2}-2\gamma]/(k+3)}=\frac{2(k+3)}{(k+1)(k+2)L\gamma^{2}},

\displaystyle\frac{\theta_{k+2}}{(1-\theta_{k+1})(\theta_{k+2}\gamma+(1-\beta_{k+1})\eta_{k+1})}=\frac{2/(k+4)}{\frac{k+1}{k+3}\Big(\frac{2\gamma}{k+4}+\frac{(k+2)L\gamma^{2}-2\gamma}{k+4}\Big)}=\frac{2(k+3)}{(k+1)(k+2)L\gamma^{2}},

which indicates that (
4.32
) holds with equalities.

We proceed to prove the desired convergence rates for both cases. With the given
\{\theta_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
, it follows from (
4.29
) that

\Theta_{k}=\frac{2}{(k+1)(k+2)},~\forall k\geq 0.

(4.51)

Additionally, plugging
\theta_{1}=2/3
,
\theta_{2}=1/2
, and
(1-\beta_{1})\eta_{1}:=(L\gamma^{2}-\gamma)/2
into (
4.34
) gives

\displaystyle\Phi_{1}=

\displaystyle f(x_{1})-f^{*}+\frac{\theta_{1}\theta_{2}}{2(1-\theta_{1})(\theta_{2}\gamma+(1-\beta_{1})\eta_{1})}\|x_{1}-x^{*}\|^{2}

(4.52)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(x_{1})-f^{*}+\frac{1}{L\gamma^{2}}\|x_{1}-x^{*}\|^{2}.

Applying (
4.51
) with
k=t-1
and
\sigma^{2}=0
on (
4.33
), together with (
4.52
) and
\gamma=1/L
leads to

\mathbb{E}[f(y_{t})]-f^{*}\leq\Phi_{1}\Theta_{t-1}=\frac{2(f(x_{1})-f^{*}+L\|x_{1}-x^{*}\|^{2})}{t(t+1)},

which proves the deterministic case (i).

Next, we consider the stochastic case (ii) with
\sigma^{2}>0
. Note from (
4.50
) that

L\gamma^{2}+\theta_{k}\Big(\gamma+\frac{1-\beta_{k}}{\theta_{k+1}}\eta_{k}\Big)=L\gamma^{2}+\frac{2}{k+2}\cdot\frac{(k+1)L\gamma^{2}}{2}=L\gamma^{2}+\frac{k+1}{k+2}L\gamma^{2}\leq 2L\gamma^{2},

which together with (
4.51
) and the choice of
\gamma
in (
4.48
) further implies (note
1\leq t\leq t^{2}
for
t\geq 1
)

\displaystyle\sigma^{2}\sum_{k=1}^{t-1}\frac{\Theta_{t-1}}{2\Theta_{k}}\Big(L\gamma^{2}+\theta_{k}\Big(\gamma+\frac{1-\beta_{k}}{\theta_{k+1}}\eta_{k}\Big)\Big)\leq\frac{\sigma^{2}L\gamma^{2}}{t(t+1)}\sum_{k=1}^{t-1}(k+1)(k+2)

(4.53)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma^{2}L\gamma^{2}}{t(t+1)}\frac{(t-1)(t^{2}+4t+6)}{3}\leq\frac{\sigma^{2}L\gamma^{2}(t^{2}+4t+6)}{3t}\leq\frac{11t\sigma^{2}L\gamma^{2}}{3}\leq\frac{11C\sigma}{3\sqrt{t}}.

Moreover, applying (
4.48
) in (
4.52
) gives

\displaystyle\Phi_{1}=

\displaystyle f(x_{1})-f^{*}+\frac{1}{L\min\{1/L^{2},~Ct^{-3/2}/\sigma L\}}\|x_{1}-x^{*}\|^{2}

(4.54)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(x_{1})-f^{*}+\max\{L,\sigma t^{3/2}/C\}\|x_{1}-x^{*}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle f(x_{1})-f^{*}+(L+\sigma t^{3/2}/C)\|x_{1}-x^{*}\|^{2}.

Finally, plugging (
4.51
) with
k=t-1
, (
4.53
), and (
4.54
) into (
4.33
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[f(y_{t})]-f^{*}\leq\frac{2(f(x_{1})-f^{*}+L\|x_{1}-x^{*}\|^{2})}{t(t+1)}+\frac{\sigma\|x_{1}-x^{*}\|^{2}}{C\sqrt{t}}+\frac{11C\sigma}{3\sqrt{t}},

which proves (ii). The proof is now complete.
∎

5
Convergence for Nonconvex Problems

In this section, we move beyond convex objectives and turn to the nonconvex setting, where global optimality can no longer be guaranteed. Nevertheless, we show that (
G-SGDM
) still admits meaningful convergence guarantees to stationary points.
For brevity, we focus on (
G-SGDM
) with constant parameters
\beta\in[0,1)
,
\gamma\geq 0
, and
\eta>0
.

To facilitate our analysis, we consider the auxiliary sequence
\{w_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
defined in (
4.1
), which satisfies the SGD-like property Lemma
1
. Additionally, from the definition (
4.1
), the second equation of (
G-SGDM
), and the initialization
m_{0}=0
, it is straightforward to verify that

w_{k}=x_{k}-\frac{\beta\eta}{1-\beta}m_{k-1},~\forall k\geq 1.

(5.1)

Before presenting the main result, we first state the following lemma, which provides an upper bound on
\mathbb{E}[\|m_{k}\|^{2}]
and will be useful to our analysis.

Lemma 3
.

Let Assumption
1
hold. Let
\{x_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
and
\{m_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
be the sequences generated by (
G-SGDM
) with constant momentum parameter
\beta\in[0,1)
. Then for any
k\geq 0
, it holds that

\mathbb{E}[\|m_{k}\|^{2}]\leq 2(1-\beta)\sum_{j=1}^{k}\beta^{k-j}\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla f(x_{j})\|^{2}]+\frac{2(1-\beta)}{1+\beta}\sigma^{2}.

Proof of Lemma
3
.

The desired result holds trivially for
k=0
since
m_{0}=0
. Hence we assume
k\geq 1
in what follows. Applying the first equation in (
G-SGDM
) recursively and noting that
m_{0}=0
, we obtain

m_{k}=(1-\beta)\sum_{j=1}^{k}\beta^{k-j}g_{j},

which together with Young’s inequality further gives

\displaystyle\|m_{k}\|^{2}\leq

\displaystyle 2\Big\|(1-\beta)\sum_{j=1}^{k}\beta^{k-j}\nabla f(x_{j})\Big\|^{2}+2\Big\|m_{k}-(1-\beta)\sum_{j=1}^{k}\beta^{k-j}\nabla f(x_{j}))\Big\|^{2}

(5.2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2(1-\beta)^{2}\Big\|\sum_{j=1}^{k}\beta^{k-j}\nabla f(x_{j})\Big\|^{2}+2(1-\beta)^{2}\Big\|\sum_{j=1}^{k}\beta^{k-j}(g_{j}-\nabla f(x_{j}))\Big\|^{2}.

To bound the first term on the RHS of (
5.2
), we use the convexity of
\|\cdot\|^{2}
to derive

\displaystyle\Big\|\sum_{j=1}^{k}\beta^{k-j}\nabla f(x_{j})\Big\|^{2}=

\displaystyle\Big\|\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{k}\beta^{k-j}\Big)\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{\beta^{k-j}}{\sum_{j=1}^{k}\beta^{k-j}}\nabla f(x_{j})\Big\|^{2}

(5.3)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{k}\beta^{k-j}\Big)^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{\beta^{k-j}}{\sum_{j=1}^{k}\beta^{k-j}}\|\nabla f(x_{j})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-\beta}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\beta^{k-j}\|\nabla f(x_{j})\|^{2},

where the last inequality holds due to
\sum_{j=1}^{k}\beta^{k-j}=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\beta^{j}=\frac{1-\beta^{k}}{1-\beta}\leq\frac{1}{1-\beta}
. For the second term in (
5.2
), we expand the square of norm to obtain

\Big\|\sum_{j=1}^{k}\beta^{k-j}(g_{j}-\nabla f(x_{j}))\Big\|^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left\langle\beta^{k-i}\left(g_{i}-\nabla f(x_{i})\right),\beta^{k-j}\left(g_{j}-\nabla f(x_{j})\right)\right\rangle.

(5.4)

For
i<j
, since
\mathbb{E}_{i}[g_{i}]=\nabla f(x_{i})
from Assumption
1
, it follows that

\mathbb{E}_{j}[\left\langle g_{i}-\nabla f(x_{i}),g_{j}-\nabla f(x_{j})\right\rangle]=\left\langle g_{i}-\nabla f(x_{i}),\mathbb{E}_{j}[g_{j}-\nabla f(x_{j})]\right\rangle=0.

(5.5)

Due to symmetry, we know that (
5.5
) also holds for
i>j
. Taking expectations on both sides of (
5.4
) and noting (
5.5
) for
i\neq j
, we obtain from Assumption
1
that

\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big\|\sum_{j=1}^{k}\beta^{k-j}(g_{j}-\nabla f(x_{j}))\Big\|^{2}\Big]=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\beta^{2(k-j)}\mathbb{E}[\|g_{j}-\nabla f(x_{j})\|^{2}]\leq\sigma^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\beta^{2(k-j)}\leq\frac{\sigma^{2}}{1-\beta^{2}},

(5.6)

where the last inequality follows from
\sum_{j=1}^{k}\beta^{2(k-j)}=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\beta^{2j}=\frac{1-\beta^{2k}}{1-\beta^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{1-\beta^{2}}
. Therefore, taking expectations on both sides of (
5.2
) and plugging in (
5.3
) and (
5.6
) leads to the desired result, which completes the proof.
∎

Leveraging Lemma
3
, we now derive the main convergence result for nonconvex objectives. The following theorem establishes that the iterates generated by (
G-SGDM
) converge to stationary points at a sublinear rate in expectation.

Theorem 4
.

Let Assumption
1
hold. Let
\{x_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
be the sequence generated by (
G-SGDM
) with constant parameters
\beta\in[0,1)
,
\gamma\geq 0
, and
\eta>0
. If we choose

0<\gamma+\eta\leq\frac{1-\beta}{3L},

then for any
t\geq 1
, it holds that

\frac{1}{t}\sum_{k=1}^{t}\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}]\leq\frac{2(f(x_{1})-f^{*})}{(\gamma+\eta)t}+\Big(\frac{\beta^{2}L\eta}{1+\beta}+L(\gamma+\eta)\Big)\sigma^{2}.

Proof of Theorem
4
.

We first obtain from Lemma
1
and the
L
-smoothness of
f
that

\displaystyle f(w_{k+1})

\displaystyle\leq f(w_{k})+\langle\nabla f(w_{k}),w_{k+1}-w_{k}\rangle+\frac{L}{2}\|w_{k+1}-w_{k}\|^{2}

(5.7)

\displaystyle=f(w_{k})-(\gamma+\eta)\langle\nabla f(w_{k}),g_{k}\rangle+\frac{L(\gamma+\eta)^{2}}{2}\|g_{k}\|^{2}.

From
\mathbb{E}_{k}[g_{k}]=\nabla f(x_{k})
and (
5.1
), we have for any
\rho>0
that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\langle\nabla f(w_{k}),g_{k}\rangle]=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\langle\nabla f(w_{k})-\nabla f(x_{k}),\nabla f(x_{k})\rangle]+\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}]

(5.8)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle-\frac{\rho}{2}\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla f(w_{k})-\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}]-\frac{1}{2\rho}\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}]+\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle-\frac{\rho L^{2}}{2}\mathbb{E}[\|w_{k}-x_{k}\|^{2}]-\Big(\frac{1}{2\rho}-1\Big)\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\rho L^{2}\beta^{2}\eta^{2}}{2(1-\beta)^{2}}\mathbb{E}[\|m_{k-1}\|^{2}]-\Big(\frac{1}{2\rho}-1\Big)\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}].

Taking expectations on both sides of (
5.7
) and applying (
5.8
) and Assumption
1
further gives

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[f(w_{k+1})]\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[f(w_{k})]+\frac{\rho L^{2}\beta^{2}\eta^{2}(\gamma+\eta)}{2(1-\beta)^{2}}\mathbb{E}[\|m_{k-1}\|^{2}]

(5.9)

\displaystyle+\Big(\frac{1}{2\rho}-1\Big)(\gamma+\eta)\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}]+\frac{L(\gamma+\eta)^{2}}{2}\mathbb{E}[\|g_{k}\|^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[f(w_{k})]+\frac{\rho L^{2}\beta^{2}\eta^{2}(\gamma+\eta)}{2(1-\beta)^{2}}\mathbb{E}[\|m_{k-1}\|^{2}]+\frac{L(\gamma+\eta)^{2}}{2}\sigma^{2}

\displaystyle+\Big(\frac{1}{2\rho}-1+\frac{L(\gamma+\eta)}{2}\Big)(\gamma+\eta)\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}].

From Lemma
3
, we can bound
\mathbb{E}[\|m_{k-1}\|^{2}]
via

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\|m_{k-1}\|^{2}]\leq

\displaystyle 2(1-\beta)\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\beta^{k-1-j}\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla f(x_{j})\|^{2}]+\frac{2(1-\beta)}{1+\beta}\sigma^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2(1-\beta)}{\beta}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\beta^{k-j}\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla f(x_{j})\|^{2}]+\frac{2(1-\beta)}{1+\beta}\sigma^{2},

applying which on (
5.9
) leads to

\mathbb{E}[f(w_{k+1})]\leq\mathbb{E}[f(w_{k})]+\frac{\rho L^{2}\beta\eta^{2}(\gamma+\eta)}{1-\beta}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\beta^{k-j}\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla f(x_{j})\|^{2}]+\Big(\frac{\rho L^{2}\beta^{2}\eta^{2}(\gamma+\eta)}{1-\beta^{2}}+\frac{L(\gamma+\eta)^{2}}{2}\Big)\sigma^{2}\\
+\Big(\frac{1}{2\rho}-1+\frac{L(\gamma+\eta)}{2}\Big)(\gamma+\eta)\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}].

(5.10)

Summing up (
5.10
) over
k=1,\dots,t
and noting that
w_{1}=x_{1}
further gives

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[f(w_{t+1})]\leq

\displaystyle f(x_{1})+\frac{\rho L^{2}\beta\eta^{2}(\gamma+\eta)}{1-\beta}\sum_{k=1}^{t}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\beta^{k-j}\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla f(x_{j})\|^{2}]+\Big(\frac{\rho L^{2}\beta^{2}\eta^{2}(\gamma+\eta)}{1-\beta^{2}}+\frac{L(\gamma+\eta)^{2}}{2}\Big)t\sigma^{2}

(5.11)

\displaystyle+\Big(\frac{1}{2\rho}-1+\frac{L(\gamma+\eta)}{2}\Big)(\gamma+\eta)\sum_{k=1}^{t}\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle f(x_{1})+\Big(\frac{\rho L^{2}\beta^{2}\eta^{2}(\gamma+\eta)}{1-\beta^{2}}+\frac{L(\gamma+\eta)^{2}}{2}\Big)t\sigma^{2}

\displaystyle+\Big(\frac{1}{2\rho}-1+\frac{L(\gamma+\eta)}{2}+\frac{\rho L^{2}\beta\eta^{2}}{(1-\beta)^{2}}\Big)(\gamma+\eta)\sum_{k=1}^{t}\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}],

where the second inequality holds due to

\sum_{k=1}^{t}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\beta^{k-j}\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla f(x_{j})\|^{2}]=\sum_{j=1}^{t}\Big(\sum_{k=j}^{t}\beta^{k-j}\Big)\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla f(x_{j})\|^{2}]\leq\frac{1}{1-\beta}\sum_{j=1}^{t}\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla f(x_{j})\|^{2}].

Let
\rho=\frac{1-\beta}{2L(\gamma+\eta)}
, then it follows from (
5.11
) and
f(w_{t+1})\geq f^{*}
that

\displaystyle\Big(1-\frac{L(\gamma+\eta)}{1-\beta}-\frac{L(\gamma+\eta)}{2}-\frac{\beta L\eta^{2}}{2(1-\beta)(\gamma+\eta)}\Big)(\gamma+\eta)\sum_{k=1}^{t}\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}]

(5.12)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle f(x_{1})-f^{*}+\Big(\frac{\beta^{2}L\eta^{2}}{2(1+\beta)}+\frac{L(\gamma+\eta)^{2}}{2}\Big)t\sigma^{2}.

From
0\leq\eta\leq\gamma+\eta
and the condition
\gamma+\eta\leq\frac{1-\beta}{3L}
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\frac{L(\gamma+\eta)}{1-\beta}+\frac{L(\gamma+\eta)}{2}+\frac{\beta L\eta^{2}}{2(1-\beta)(\gamma+\eta)}\leq

\displaystyle L(\gamma+\eta)\Big(\frac{1}{1-\beta}+\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\beta}{2(1-\beta)}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle L(\gamma+\eta)\frac{3}{2(1-\beta)}\leq\frac{1}{2},

which together with (
5.12
) implies that

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma+\eta}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{t}\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}]\leq

\displaystyle\Big(1-\frac{L(\gamma+\eta)}{1-\beta}-\frac{L(\gamma+\eta)}{2}-\frac{\beta L\eta^{2}}{2(1-\beta)(\gamma+\eta)}\Big)(\gamma+\eta)\sum_{k=1}^{t}\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}]

(5.13)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle f(x_{1})-f^{*}+\Big(\frac{\beta^{2}L\eta^{2}}{2(1+\beta)}+\frac{L(\gamma+\eta)^{2}}{2}\Big)t\sigma^{2}.

Dividing both sides of (
5.13
) by
(\gamma+\eta)t/2
and noting
0\leq\eta\leq\gamma+\eta
further gives

\frac{1}{t}\sum_{k=1}^{t}\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}]\leq\frac{2(f(x_{1})-f^{*})}{(\gamma+\eta)t}+\Big(\frac{\beta^{2}L\eta}{1+\beta}+L(\gamma+\eta)\Big)\sigma^{2},

which completes the proof.
∎

While Theorem
4
establishes convergence to stationary points, it does not provide guarantees on the quality of these points, since stationary points in nonconvex problems may correspond to local minima or saddle points. To obtain stronger convergence guarantees, we consider the setting in which the objective function
f
satisfies the
\mu
-Polyak-Łojasiewicz (PL) condition, that is,

\|\nabla f(x)\|^{2}\geq 2\mu(f(x)-f^{*}),~\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(5.14)

Note that any
\mu
-strongly convex function satisfies the
\mu
-PL condition in (
5.14
). However, the PL condition does not imply convexity in general. For example, the function
f(x)=x^{2}+3\sin^{2}x
satisfies the PL condition while being nonconvex. Moreover, the PL condition has been shown to hold for a broad class of deep linear and shallow neural networks
(
Charles and Papailiopoulos 2018
;
Hardt and Ma 2016
;
Li and Yuan 2017
)
.

In the upcoming theorem, we establish an improved convergence result for (
G-SGDM
) under the PL condition, measured in terms of the objective gaps along the auxiliary points
\{w_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
. To exploit the PL condition effectively, we introduce a family of Lyapunov functions
\{\varphi_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
that combine the function value gap with accumulated gradient norms:

\varphi_{k}=f(w_{k})-f^{*}+C\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\beta^{k-1-j}\|\nabla f(x_{j})\|^{2},

(5.15)

where
C>0
is a constant to be specified in the proof. This choice of Lyapunov function enables us to establish a contraction property, up to error terms arising from stochastic noise, which plays a crucial role in the convergence analysis.

Theorem 5
.

Let Assumption
1
hold and suppose that
f
satisfies the
\mu
-PL condition (
5.14
). Let
\{x_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
be the sequence generated by (
G-SGDM
) with constant parameters
\beta\in[0,1)
,
\gamma\geq 0
, and
\eta>0
. If we choose

0<\gamma+\eta\leq\min\Big\{\frac{1-\beta}{2L},~\frac{1-\beta}{8\beta^{2}L}\Big\},

(5.16)

then for any
t\geq 1
, it holds that

\mathbb{E}[f(w_{t+1})-f^{*}]\leq\Big(1-\frac{\mu(\gamma+\eta)}{18}\Big)^{t}(f(x_{1})-f^{*})+\Big(\frac{3\beta^{2}\eta}{1+\beta}+\gamma+\eta\Big)\frac{9L}{\mu}\sigma^{2},

where
w_{t}
is defined in (
4.1
).

Proof of Theorem
5
.

For notational simplicity, denote

M:=\gamma+\eta-\frac{L(\gamma+\eta)^{2}}{1-\beta}-\frac{L(\gamma+\eta)^{2}}{2}.

Consider the Lyapunov functions
\{\varphi_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
defined by (
5.15
) with

C:=\frac{\frac{\beta^{2}L\eta^{2}}{2}+\frac{M\mu(\mu+L)\beta^{2}\eta^{2}}{1-\beta}}{1-\beta-\mu M+\frac{\mu(\mu+L)\beta^{2}\eta^{2}}{1-\beta}}.

(5.17)

We first establish some auxiliary inequalities regarding the introduced constants
C
and
M
. To start off, it follows from
0<\gamma+\eta\leq\frac{1-\beta}{2L}
and
\beta\in[0,1)
that

M=(\gamma+\eta)\Big(1-\frac{(3-\beta)L}{2(1-\beta)}(\gamma+\eta)\Big)\geq\Big(1-\frac{3-\beta}{4}\Big)(\gamma+\eta)\geq\frac{1}{4}(\gamma+\eta)>0.

(5.18)

On the other hand, we note from
\gamma+\eta\leq\frac{1-\beta}{2L}
and
\mu\leq L
that

M\leq\gamma+\eta\leq\frac{1-\beta}{2L}\leq\frac{1-\beta}{2\mu}.

(5.19)

Hence we derive
C>0
by noting from (
5.19
) that

1-\beta-\mu M\geq\frac{1-\beta}{2}\geq 0.

(5.20)

Next, we show that
M-C
is positive and bounded from both sides. To this end, first note from
0\leq\eta\leq\gamma+\eta\leq\frac{1-\beta}{8\beta^{2}L}
, (
5.20
), and (
5.18
) that

\displaystyle M(1-\beta-\mu M)-\frac{\beta^{2}L\eta^{2}}{2}\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1-\beta}{2}M-\frac{\beta^{2}L\eta^{2}}{2}\geq\frac{1-\beta}{8}(\gamma+\eta)-\frac{\beta^{2}L}{2}(\gamma+\eta)^{2}

(5.21)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\gamma+\eta)\Big(\frac{1-\beta}{8}-\frac{\beta^{2}L}{2}(\gamma+\eta)\Big)\geq\frac{1-\beta}{16}(\gamma+\eta).

On the other hand, we obtain from
\mu\leq L
and (
5.16
) that

\displaystyle 1-\beta-\mu M+\frac{\mu(\mu+L)\beta^{2}\eta^{2}}{1-\beta}

\displaystyle\leq 1-\beta+\frac{\mu(\mu+L)\beta^{2}(\gamma+\eta)^{2}}{1-\beta}

(5.22)

\displaystyle\leq 1-\beta+\frac{2L^{2}\beta^{2}}{1-\beta}\frac{1-\beta}{2L}\frac{1-\beta}{8\beta^{2}L}

\displaystyle=1-\beta+\frac{1-\beta}{8}=\frac{9}{8}(1-\beta).

Then it follows from
C>0
, (
5.19
), (
5.21
), and (
5.22
) that

\frac{1-\beta}{2\mu}\geq M\geq M-C=\frac{M(1-\beta-\mu M)-\frac{\beta^{2}L\eta^{2}}{2}}{1-\beta-\mu M+\frac{\mu(\mu+L)\beta^{2}\eta^{2}}{1-\beta}}\geq\frac{\frac{1-\beta}{16}(\gamma+\eta)}{\frac{9}{8}(1-\beta)}=\frac{\gamma+\eta}{18}>0.

(5.23)

Next, we provide an upper bound on
\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}
using the
\mu
-PL condition (
5.14
). To this end, we note from the
L
-smoothness of
f
and (
5.1
) that

\displaystyle f(w_{k})

\displaystyle\leq f(x_{k})+\langle\nabla f(x_{k}),w_{k}-x_{k}\rangle+\frac{L}{2}\|w_{k}-x_{k}\|^{2}

(5.24)

\displaystyle=f(x_{k})+\Big\langle\nabla f(x_{k}),-\frac{\beta\eta}{1-\beta}m_{k-1}\Big\rangle+\frac{L\beta^{2}\eta^{2}}{2(1-\beta)^{2}}\|m_{k-1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq f(x_{k})+\frac{1}{2\mu}\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}+\frac{\mu\beta^{2}\eta^{2}}{2(1-\beta)^{2}}\|m_{k-1}\|^{2}+\frac{L\beta^{2}\eta^{2}}{2(1-\beta)^{2}}\|m_{k-1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=f(x_{k})+\frac{1}{2\mu}\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}+\frac{(\mu+L)\beta^{2}\eta^{2}}{2(1-\beta)^{2}}\|m_{k-1}\|^{2}.

Then it follows from the
\mu
-PL condition (
5.14
) and (
5.24
) that

\frac{1}{2\mu}\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}\geq f(x_{k})-f^{*}\geq f(w_{k})-f^{*}-\frac{1}{2\mu}\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}-\frac{(\mu+L)\beta^{2}\eta^{2}}{2(1-\beta)^{2}}\|m_{k-1}\|^{2},

which further implies that

\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}\geq\mu(f(w_{k})-f^{*})-\frac{\mu(\mu+L)\beta^{2}\eta^{2}}{2(1-\beta)^{2}}\|m_{k-1}\|^{2}.

(5.25)

Now we are ready to establish a recursive inequality for the Lyapunov functions
\{\varphi_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
. Plugging
\rho=\frac{1-\beta}{2L(\gamma+\eta)}
into (
5.9
) gives

\mathbb{E}[f(w_{k+1})]\leq\mathbb{E}[f(w_{k})]+\frac{\beta^{2}L\eta^{2}}{4(1-\beta)}\mathbb{E}[\|m_{k-1}\|^{2}]+\frac{L(\gamma+\eta)^{2}}{2}\sigma^{2}-M\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}],

which together with the definition of
\{\varphi_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
(
5.15
) implies that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\varphi_{k+1}]:=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[f(w_{k+1})-f^{*}+C\sum_{j=1}^{k}\beta^{k-j}\|\nabla f(x_{j})\|^{2}\Big]

(5.26)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[f(w_{k+1})-f^{*}]+C\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}]+\beta C\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\beta^{k-1-j}\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla f(x_{j})\|^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[f(w_{k})-f^{*}]+\frac{\beta^{2}L\eta^{2}}{4(1-\beta)}\mathbb{E}[\|m_{k-1}\|^{2}]+\frac{L(\gamma+\eta)^{2}}{2}\sigma^{2}-(M-C)\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}]

\displaystyle+\beta C\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\beta^{k-1-j}\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla f(x_{j})\|^{2}].

From
M-C>0
, applying (
5.25
) and Lemma
3
on (
5.26
), we further obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\varphi_{k+1}]\leq

\displaystyle[1-\mu(M-C)]\mathbb{E}[f(w_{k})-f^{*}]+\Big(\frac{\beta^{2}L\eta^{2}}{4(1-\beta)}+\frac{(M-C)\mu(\mu+L)\beta^{2}\eta^{2}}{2(1-\beta)^{2}}\Big)\mathbb{E}[\|m_{k-1}\|^{2}]

(5.27)

\displaystyle+\frac{L(\gamma+\eta)^{2}}{2}\sigma^{2}+\beta C\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\beta^{k-1-j}\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla f(x_{j})\|^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle[1-\mu(M-C)]\mathbb{E}[f(w_{k})-f^{*}]+\Big(\frac{\beta^{2}L\eta^{2}}{2(1+\beta)}+\frac{(M-C)\mu(\mu+L)\beta^{2}\eta^{2}}{1-\beta^{2}}+\frac{L(\gamma+\eta)^{2}}{2}\Big)\sigma^{2}

\displaystyle+\Big(\beta C+\frac{\beta^{2}L\eta^{2}}{2}+\frac{(M-C)\mu(\mu+L)\beta^{2}\eta^{2}}{1-\beta}\Big)\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\beta^{k-1-j}\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla f(x_{j})\|^{2}].

From the definition of
C
(
5.17
), it is straightforward to verify that

\beta C+\frac{\beta^{2}L\eta^{2}}{2}+\frac{(M-C)\mu(\mu+L)\beta^{2}\eta^{2}}{1-\beta}=C(1-\mu M)\leq C[1-\mu(M-C)]

(5.28)

Plugging (
5.28
) into (
5.27
) and noting (
5.23
) and
\mu\leq L
, we derive

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\varphi_{k+1}]

\displaystyle\leq[1-\mu(M-C)]\mathbb{E}[\varphi_{k}]+\Big(\frac{\beta^{2}L\eta^{2}}{2(1+\beta)}+\frac{(M-C)\mu(\mu+L)\beta^{2}\eta^{2}}{1-\beta^{2}}+\frac{L(\gamma+\eta)^{2}}{2}\Big)\sigma^{2}

(5.29)

\displaystyle\leq\Big(1-\frac{\mu(\gamma+\eta)}{18}\Big)\mathbb{E}[\varphi_{k}]+\Big(\frac{\beta^{2}L\eta^{2}}{2(1+\beta)}+\frac{(\mu+L)\beta^{2}\eta^{2}}{2(1+\beta)}+\frac{L(\gamma+\eta)^{2}}{2}\Big)\sigma^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\Big(1-\frac{\mu(\gamma+\eta)}{18}\Big)\mathbb{E}[\varphi_{k}]+\Big(\frac{3\beta^{2}L\eta^{2}}{2(1+\beta)}+\frac{L(\gamma+\eta)^{2}}{2}\Big)\sigma^{2}.

Applying (
5.29
) recursively and noting that
\varphi_{1}=f(x_{1})-f^{*}
further leads to

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[f(w_{k+1})-f^{*}]\leq\mathbb{E}[\varphi_{k+1}]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big(1-\frac{\mu(\gamma+\eta)}{18}\Big)^{k}(f(x_{1})-f^{*})+\Big(\frac{3\beta^{2}L\eta^{2}}{2(1+\beta)}+\frac{L(\gamma+\eta)^{2}}{2}\Big)\sigma^{2}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\Big(1-\frac{\mu(\gamma+\eta)}{18}\Big)^{j}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big(1-\frac{\mu(\gamma+\eta)}{18}\Big)^{k}(f(x_{1})-f^{*})+\Big(\frac{3\beta^{2}L\eta^{2}}{2(1+\beta)}+\frac{L(\gamma+\eta)^{2}}{2}\Big)\sigma^{2}\cdot\frac{18}{\mu(\gamma+\eta)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big(1-\frac{\mu(\gamma+\eta)}{18}\Big)^{k}(f(x_{1})-f^{*})+\Big(\frac{3\beta^{2}\eta}{1+\beta}+\gamma+\eta\Big)\frac{9L}{\mu}\sigma^{2},

which completes the proof.
∎

Remark 3
.

We compare Theorems
4
and
5
with the results established in
(
Liu et al. 2020
)
, which studied the convergence behavior of (
SGD-HB
) with constant parameters
\beta\in[0,1)
and
\eta>0
in both nonconvex and strongly convex settings. A key technical ingredient in their analysis is the construction of Lyapunov functions
\{\mathcal{L}_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
of the form

\mathcal{L}_{k}=f(w_{k})-f^{*}+\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}c_{j}\|x_{k+1-j}-x_{k-j}\|^{2},

where
w_{k}
is defined by (
4.1
) with
\gamma=0
, and
\{c_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
are carefully chosen positive constants.
By appropriately specifying these constants,
(
Liu et al. 2020
)
derived a recursive inequality for the Lyapunov sequence
\{\mathcal{L}_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
. Specifically, for an
L
-smooth nonconvex objective
f
, they showed that if
\eta=O(\frac{1-\beta}{L})
, then (
SGD-HB
) achieves the stationary convergence

\frac{1}{t}\sum_{k=1}^{t}\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}]=O\left(\frac{f\left(x_{1}\right)-f^{*}}{t\eta}+L\eta\sigma^{2}\right).

(5.30)

Moreover, under the additional assumption that
f
is
\mu
-strongly convex, the same Lyapunov framework yields

\mathbb{E}[f(w_{k})-f^{*}]=O\Big((1-\eta\mu)^{k}+\frac{L}{\mu}\eta\sigma^{2}\Big).

(5.31)

In comparison, Theorems
4
and
5
improve upon the results of
(
Liu et al. 2020
)
in several respects. First, for general nonconvex
L
-smooth objectives, Theorem
4
establishes convergence of (
G-SGDM
) without introducing any Lyapunov function, thereby substantially simplifying the analysis. By setting
\gamma=0
in Theorem
4
, we recover the same order of convergence rate as in (
5.30
) for any step size
\eta\leq\frac{1-\beta}{3L}
. Furthermore, Theorem
5
yields a convergence result comparable to (
5.31
) when
\gamma=0
. Importantly, this result does not require
\mu
-strong convexity; instead, it relies on the weaker PL condition, which is compatible with nonconvex settings. In addition, our analysis employs a different class of Lyapunov functions
\{\varphi_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
defined in (
5.15
). Finally, our results apply to the more general method (
G-SGDM
), thereby extending the convergence guarantees beyond (
SGD-HB
).

6
Experiments

In this section, we conduct numerical experiments to validate our theoretical results for (
G-SGDM
) on both convex and nonconvex problems.

6.1
Logistic Regression

We begin by evaluating the performance of (
G-SGDM

7
Conclusion

In this paper, we propose a generalized stochastic gradient descent with momentum framework for smooth optimization, encompassing a broad class of momentum-based stochastic methods as special cases. Under standard smoothness and bounded variance assumptions, we provided a unified convergence analysis for both convex and nonconvex problems, offering new insights into the interaction between momentum and stochastic gradients.

For convex objectives, we established ergodic convergence results with flexible constant parameters and showed that, with appropriately designed time-varying parameters, the framework achieves an accelerated convergence rate of
O(1/t^{2})
, akin to Nesterov’s accelerated gradient method in deterministic settings, with an additional stochastic noise term. For nonconvex problems, we proved sublinear convergence to stationary points under constant parameters and demonstrated linear convergence to a neighborhood of the global optimum under the Polyak–Łojasiewicz condition.

Importantly, our results rely only on mild smoothness and bounded variance assumptions and accommodate flexible parameter choices. This generality not only recovers existing convergence guarantees but also provides new ones for many popular momentum methods, including SGD with Polyak’s or Nesterov’s momentum, stochastic unified momentum (SUM), quasi-hyperbolic momentum (QHM), and momentum-added stochastic solver (MASS). Numerical experiments on logistic regression and ResNet-18 training validate our theoretical findings and highlight the practical advantages of the generalized framework.

While this work provides a solid theoretical foundation and empirical validation, several promising research directions remain open. Future work could extend this framework to adaptive step-size schemes, distributed or federated optimization, and nonsmooth or constrained problems. We believe that the generalized perspective and analytical tools developed in this work will serve as a foundation for further theoretical and algorithmic investigations in stochastic momentum-based optimization.

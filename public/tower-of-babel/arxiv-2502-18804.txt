Twisting 𝒪 \mathcal{O} -operators by ( 2 , 3 ) (2,3) -Cocycle of Hom-Lie-Yamaguti Algebras with Representations
Sami Mabrouk, Sergei Silvestrov, Fatma Zouaidi

Abstract
In this paper, we first introduce the notion of twisted 𝒪 \mathcal{O} -operators on a Hom-Lie-Yamaguti algebra by a given ( 2 , 3 ) (2,3) -cocycle with coefficients in a representation. We show that a twisted 𝒪 \mathcal{O} -operator induces a Hom-Lie-Yamaguti structure. We also introduce the notion of a weighted Reynolds operator on a Hom-Lie-Yamaguti algebra, which can serve as a special case of twisted 𝒪 \mathcal{O} -operators on Hom-Lie-Yamaguti algebras. Then, we define a cohomology of twisted 𝒪 \mathcal{O} -operator on Hom-Lie-Yamaguiti algebras with coefficients in a representation. Furthermore, we introduce and study the Hom-NS-Lie-Yamaguti algebras as the underlying structure of the twisted 𝒪 \mathcal{O} -operator on Hom-Lie-Yamaguti algebras. Finally, we investigate the twisted 𝒪 \mathcal{O} -operator on Hom-Lie-Yamaguti algebras induced by the twisted 𝒪 \mathcal{O} -operator on a Hom-Lie algebras.



Keywords:
Hom-Lie algebra, Hom-Lie-Yamaguti algebra, representation, twisted
\mathcal{O}
-operator, Reynolds operator, cohomolgy.

2
Basics on Hom-Lie-Yamaguti algebras

All linear spaces in the article are assumed to be over a field
\mathbb{K}
of characteristic
0
and finite-dimensional.

In this section, we recall some basic notions about Hom-algebras, such as Hom-Lie algebras, Hom-Lie triple systems, Hom-Lie-Yamaguti algebras, representations and their cohomology theory.
Henceforth,
\underset{x,y,z}{\circlearrowleft}
denotes the summation over cyclically permuted
(x,y,z)
.

Definition 2.1

(
[
18
,
24
,
37
]
)
.

A Hom-Lie algebra is a triple
(A,[\cdot,\cdot],\alpha)
consisting of a linear space
A
, a bilinear map (”bracket”)

[\cdot,\cdot]:A\times A\rightarrow A
and a linear map
\alpha:A\rightarrow A
satisfying for all
x,y,z\in A
,

\displaystyle[x,y]=-[y,x]

Skew-symmetry

identity

(2.1)

\displaystyle\underset{x,y,z}{\circlearrowleft}[\alpha(x),[y,z]]=[\alpha(x),[y,z]]+[\alpha(y),[z,x]]+[\alpha(z),[x,y]]=0.

Hom-Lie Jacobi

identity

(2.2)

Using skew-symmetry and bilinearity of the bracket, the Hom-Lie Jacobi identity (
2.2
) for Hom-Lie algebras can be written as

[\alpha(x),[y,z]]=[[x,y],\alpha(z)]+[\alpha(y),[x,z]].

When
\alpha=Id
, Hom-Lie algebra is a Lie algebra since the Hom-Jacobi identity becomes the Jacobi identity for Lie algebras. An algebra is a Lie algebra if and only if the identity map
\alpha=Id
is in the linear space of all Hom-Lie structures on the algebra consisting of all the linear maps
\alpha
making the algebra into a Hom-Lie algebra.

Definition 2.2
.

The Hom-Lie algebra
(A,[\cdot,\cdot],\alpha)
is called multiplicative if
\alpha\circ[\cdot,\cdot]=[\cdot,\cdot]\circ\alpha^{\otimes 2}
meaning for all
x,y\in A
,

\alpha([x,y])=[\alpha(x),\alpha(y)],

(2.3)

that is, in other words, if the linear map
\alpha:A\rightarrow A
is an algebra endomorphism of
(A,[\cdot,\cdot])
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
V
be a linear space and
\beta\in End(V)
is a linear operator on
V
. A representation of a Hom-Lie algebra
(A,[\cdot,\cdot],\alpha)
on
(V,\beta)
is the linear map
\rho:A\to End(V)
, or more precisely is the triple
(V,\rho,\beta)
, such that for all
x,y\in A
,

\begin{array}[]{l}\rho(\alpha(x))\circ\beta=\beta\circ\rho(x),\\
\rho([x,y])\circ\beta=\rho(\alpha(x))\rho(y)-\rho(\alpha(y))\rho(x).\end{array}

(2.4)

If
\alpha=\beta=Id
, then Definition
2.3
reduces to the definition of the representations of Lie algebras. However, if
\alpha=Id
, but
\beta\neq Id
, then one gets a more general notion of representation of Lie algebra dependent on the map
\beta
commuting with all operators of the representation.

Let
(V,\rho,\beta)
be a representation of a Hom-Lie algebra
(A,[\cdot,\cdot],\alpha)
. A skew-symmetric bilinear map
\mathcal{F}:A\times A\to V
is a
2
-cocycle, if it satisfies for all
x,y,z\in A
,

\displaystyle\underset{x,y,z}{\circlearrowleft}\big(\rho(\alpha(x))\mathcal{F}(y,z)+\mathcal{F}(\alpha(x),[y,z])\big)=0.

(2.5)

Definition 2.4

(
[
15
]
)
.

A
Hom-Lie-Yamaguti algebra

(A,[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket,\alpha)
consists of a linear space
A
equipped with a linear map
\alpha:A\rightarrow A
, a bilinear bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]:A\times A\to A
and a trilinear bracket
\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket:A\times A\times A\to A
, satisfying for all
x,y,z,w,t\in A
,

\displaystyle[x,y]=-[y,x],\quad\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket=-\left\llbracket y,x,z\right\rrbracket,

(2.6)

\displaystyle\underset{x,y,z}{\circlearrowleft}[[x,y],\alpha(z)]+\underset{x,y,z}{\circlearrowleft}\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket=0,

(2.7)

\displaystyle\underset{x,y,z}{\circlearrowleft}\left\llbracket[x,y],\alpha(z),\alpha(w)\right\rrbracket=0,

(2.8)

\displaystyle\left\llbracket\alpha(x),\alpha(y),[z,w]\right\rrbracket=[\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket,\alpha^{2}(w)]+[\alpha^{2}(z),\left\llbracket x,y,w\right\rrbracket],

(2.9)

\displaystyle\left\llbracket\alpha^{2}(x),\alpha^{2}(y),\left\llbracket z,w,t\right\rrbracket\right\rrbracket=\left\llbracket\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket,\alpha^{2}(w),\alpha^{2}(t)\right\rrbracket+\left\llbracket\alpha^{2}(z),\left\llbracket x,y,w\right\rrbracket,\alpha^{2}(t)\right\rrbracket

\displaystyle\hskip 256.0748pt+\left\llbracket\alpha^{2}(z),\alpha^{2}(w),\left\llbracket x,y,t\right\rrbracket\right\rrbracket.

(2.10)

When
\alpha={\rm{Id}}
, a Hom-Lie-Yamaguti algebra reduces to a Lie-Yamaguti algebra.

Definition 2.5
.

A Hom-Lie-Yamaguti algebra

(A,[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket,\alpha)
is called multiplicative if

\alpha\circ[\cdot,\cdot]=[\cdot,\cdot]\circ\alpha^{\otimes 2}
and
\alpha\circ\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket=\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket\circ\alpha^{\otimes 3}
, that is, if for all
x,y,z\in A
,

\alpha([x,y])=[\alpha(x),\alpha(y)],\quad\alpha(\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket)=\left\llbracket\alpha(x),\alpha(y),\alpha(z)\right\rrbracket.

(2.11)

Remark 2.6
.

For
[\cdot,\cdot]=0
, the (multiplicative) Hom-Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(A,[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket,\alpha)
reduces to a (multiplicative) Hom-Lie triple system with respect to
\alpha^{2}
. On the other hand, if
\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket=0
, then the (multiplicative) Hom-Lie-Yamaguti algebra reduces to a (multiplicative) Hom-Lie algebra
(A,[\cdot,\cdot],\alpha)
.

Let
(A_{1},[\cdot,\cdot]_{1},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{1},\alpha_{1})
and
(A_{2},[\cdot,\cdot]_{2},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{2},\alpha_{2})
be two Hom-Lie-Yamaguti algebras. A linear map

\phi:A_{1}\to A_{2}
is called the morphism of Hom-Lie-Yamaguti algebras if for all
x,y,z\in A_{1}
,

\phi([x,y]_{1})=[\phi(x),\phi(y)]_{2}\quad\text{and}\quad\phi(\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket_{1})=\left\llbracket\phi(x),\phi(y),\phi(z)\right\rrbracket_{2}.

Theorem 2.7
.

If
(A,[\cdot,\cdot],\alpha)
is a multiplicative Hom-Lie algebra and for all
x,y,z\in A
,

\displaystyle\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket_{T}=[[x,y],\alpha(z)],

then
(A,[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{T},\alpha)

is the induced multiplicative Hom-Lie-Yamaguti algebra.

Proof.

The left-antisymmetry of
\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket
follows from the anti-symmetry of
[\cdot,\cdot]
. Hence the first part follows. For the second part and using the multiplicativity of
\alpha
and (
2.2
), for all
x,y,z\in A
,

\displaystyle\underset{x,y,z}{\circlearrowleft}[[x,y],\alpha(z)]+\underset{x,y,z}{\circlearrowleft}\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket=\underset{x,y,z}{\circlearrowleft}\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket=\underset{x,y,z}{\circlearrowleft}[[x,y],\alpha(z)]

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{HomLieskewsym}}}{{=}}-\underset{x,y,z}{\circlearrowleft}[\alpha(z),[x,y]]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{HomLieJacobiIdentity}}}{{=}}0.

Similarly,

\underset{x,y,z}{\circlearrowleft}\left\llbracket[x,y],\alpha(z),\alpha(w)\right\rrbracket=\underset{x,y,z}{\circlearrowleft}[[[x,y],\alpha(z)],\alpha^{2}(w)]=[\underset{x,y,z}{\circlearrowleft}[[x,y],\alpha(z)],\alpha^{2}(w)]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle hj}}{{=}}0.

It holds that

\displaystyle\left\llbracket\alpha(x),\alpha(y),[z,w]\right\rrbracket-[\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket,\alpha^{2}(w)]-[\alpha^{2}(z),\left\llbracket x,y,w\right\rrbracket]

\displaystyle=[[\alpha(x),\alpha(y)],\alpha([z,w])]-[[[x,y],\alpha(z)],\alpha^{2}(w)]-[\alpha^{2}(z),[[x,y],\alpha(w)]]

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{HLYmultpl}}}{{=}}[[\alpha(x),\alpha(y)],\alpha([z,w])]-[[[x,y],\alpha(z)],\alpha(\alpha(w))]-[\alpha(\alpha(z)),[[x,y],\alpha(w)]]

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{HomLieskewsym}}}{{=}}[[\alpha(x),\alpha(y)],\alpha([z,w])]-[\alpha(\alpha(w)),[\alpha(z),[x,y]]]-[\alpha(\alpha(z)),[[x,y],\alpha(w)]]

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{HomLieJacobiIdentity}}}{{=}}[[\alpha(x),\alpha(y)],\alpha([z,w])]+[\alpha([x,y]),[\alpha(w),\alpha(z)]]

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{HLAmultipl}-\eqref{HLYmultpl}}}{{=}}[[\alpha(x),\alpha(y)],[\alpha(z),\alpha(w)])]-[[\alpha(x),\alpha(y)],[\alpha(z),\alpha(w)]]=0.

Finally, using the multiplicativity condition we obtain

\displaystyle\left\llbracket\alpha^{2}(x),\alpha^{2}(y),\left\llbracket z,w,t\right\rrbracket\right\rrbracket

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[[\alpha^{2}(x),\alpha^{2}(y)],[[\alpha(z),\alpha(w)],\alpha^{2}(w)]]

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{HomLieJacobiIdentity}\eqref{HLYmultpl}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle-[\alpha[\alpha(z),\alpha(w)],[\alpha^{2}(t),[\alpha(x),\alpha(y)]]]-[\alpha^{3}(t),[[\alpha(x),\alpha(y)],[\alpha(z),\alpha(w)]]]

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{HomLieskewsym}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle[\alpha[\alpha(z),\alpha(w)],[[\alpha(x),\alpha(y)],\alpha^{2}(t)]]+[[[\alpha(x),\alpha(y)],[\alpha(z),\alpha(w)]],\alpha^{3}(t)]

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{HomLieJacobiIdentity}-\eqref{HLYmultpl}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle[[\alpha^{2}(z),\alpha^{2}(w)],\alpha[[x,y],\alpha(t)]]+[[[[x,y],\alpha(z)],\alpha^{2}(w)],\alpha^{3}(t)]

\displaystyle+[[\alpha^{2}(z),[[x,y],\alpha(w)]],\alpha^{3}(t)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\llbracket\alpha^{2}(z),\alpha^{2}(w),\left\llbracket x,y,t\right\rrbracket\right\rrbracket+\left\llbracket\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket,\alpha^{2}(w),\alpha^{2}(t)\right\rrbracket

\displaystyle+\left\llbracket\alpha^{2}(z),\left\llbracket x,y,w\right\rrbracket,\alpha^{2}(t)\right\rrbracket.

Hence, the ternary Hom–Nambu identity (
2.10
) holds.
Thus,
(A,[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{T},\alpha)

is the multiplicative Hom-Lie-Yamaguti algebra.

\displaystyle\quad+\underline{\underline{\underline{\underline{\alpha^{2}(z)\circ\{y,x,w\}}}}}+\underline{\underline{\left\llbracket x,y,w\right\rrbracket\circ\alpha^{2}(z)}}-\alpha^{2}(z)\curlyvee\left\llbracket x,y,w\right\rrbracket=0.

The other identities can be checked similarly. Thus
(A,[\cdot,\cdot]^{*},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket,\alpha)
is a Hom-Lie-Yamaguti algebra.
∎

\mathcal{O}
-operators on Hom-Lie-Yamaguti algebras.

Theorem 6.4
.

Let
T:V\rightarrow A
a

(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G})
-twisted
\mathcal{O}
-operator on the Hom-Lie-Yamaguti algebra with respect to a representation
(V,\rho,\theta,\beta)
. Then,

\displaystyle u\circ v=\rho(Tu)v,\quad u\curlyvee v=\mathcal{F}(Tu,Tv),

(6.78)

\displaystyle\{u,v,w\}=\theta(Tv,Tw)u,\quad[u,v,w]=\mathcal{G}(Tu,Tv,Tw),\quad\forall u,v,w\in V,

(6.79)

define a NS-Hom-Lie-Yamaguti algebra structure on
V
. It is called the
induced NS-Hom-Lie-Yamaguti algebra
.

Proof.

For any
u_{1},u_{2},u_{3},u_{4}\in V
,

\displaystyle\{\beta(u_{1}),\beta(u_{2}),u_{3}\circ u_{4}\}^{\wedge}-\beta^{2}(u_{3})\circ\{u_{1},u_{2},u_{4}\}^{\wedge}-\left\llbracket u_{1},u_{2},u_{3}\right\rrbracket\circ\beta^{2}(u_{4})

\displaystyle=\{\beta(u_{1}),\beta(u_{2}),u_{3}\circ u_{4}\}^{\wedge}-\beta^{2}(u_{3})\circ\{u_{1},u_{2},u_{4}\}^{\wedge}-[u_{1},u_{2},u_{3}]\circ\beta^{2}(u_{4})

\displaystyle\quad-\{u_{1},u_{2},u_{3}\}^{\wedge}\circ\beta^{2}(u_{4})-\{u_{1},u_{2},u_{3}\}\circ\beta^{2}(u_{4})+\{u_{2},u_{1},u_{3}\}\circ\beta^{2}(u_{4})

\displaystyle=D_{\rho,\theta}(T\beta(u_{1}),T\beta(u_{2}))\rho(Tu_{3})u_{4}-\rho(T\beta^{2}(u_{3}))D_{\rho,\theta}(Tu_{1},Tu_{2})u_{4}-\rho(T\mathcal{G}(Tu_{1},Tu_{2},Tu_{3}))\beta^{2}(u_{4})

\displaystyle\quad-\rho(TD_{\rho,\theta}(Tu_{1},Tu_{2})u_{3})\beta^{2}(u_{4})-\rho(T\theta(Tu_{2},Tu_{3})u_{1})\beta^{2}(u_{4})+\rho(T\theta(Tu_{1},Tu_{3})u_{2})\beta^{2}(u_{4})

\displaystyle=D_{\rho,\theta}(\alpha(Tu_{1}),\alpha(Tu_{2}))\rho(Tu_{3})u_{4}-\rho(\alpha^{2}(Tu_{3}))D_{\rho,\theta}(Tu_{1},Tu_{2})u_{4}-\rho([Tu_{1},Tu_{2},Tu_{3}])\beta^{2}(u_{4})\overset{\eqref{RL7}}{=}0.

∎

6.2
NS-Hom-Lie-Yamaguiti algebras arising from NS-Hom-Lie algebras

We start by recalling the definition of NS-Hom-Lie algebra as particular case of NS-Hom-Leibniz algebras introduced in
[
49
]
.

Definition 6.8
.

A NS-Hom-Lie algebra is a linear space
A
together with bilinear operations
\circ,\curlyvee:A\rightarrow A
and a linear map
\alpha:A\to A
in which
\curlyvee
is skew-symmetric and satisfying, for all
x,y,z\in A
,

\displaystyle[x,y]\circ\alpha(z)-\alpha(x)\circ(y\circ z)+\alpha(y)\circ(x\circ z)=0,

(6.80)

\displaystyle\alpha(x)\curlyvee[y,z]+\alpha(y)\curlyvee[z,x]+\alpha(z)\curlyvee[x,z]+\alpha(x)\circ(y\curlyvee z)+\alpha(y)\circ(z\curlyvee x)+\alpha(z)\circ(x\curlyvee y)=0,

(6.81)

where
[x,y]=x\circ y-y\circ x+x\curlyvee y.

Remark 6.9
.

If the bilinear operation
\circ
in the above definition is trivial, one gets that
(A,\curlyvee,\alpha)
is a Hom-Lie
algebra. On the other hand, if
\curlyvee
is trivial, then
(A,\circ,\alpha)
becomes a Hom-pre-Lie algebra. Thus, NS-Hom-Lie algebras
are a generalization of both Hom-Lie algebras and Hom-pre-Lie algebras.

Proposition 6.10
.

Under the above notations
[\cdot,\cdot]
gives a Hom-Lie structure on
A
with respect
\alpha
, called the adjacent Hom-Lie algebra of
A
.

Proposition 6.11
.

Let
T:V\to A
be a twisted
\mathcal{O}
-operator on Hom-Lie algebra
(A,[\cdot,\cdot],\alpha)
with respect to the representation
(V,\rho,\beta)
. Then

\displaystyle u\circ v=\rho(Tu)v\quad\text{and}\quad u\curlyvee v=\mathcal{F}(Tu,Tv)

(6.82)

defines a
NS-Lie algebra
structure on
V
.

Let

(A,\circ,\curlyvee,\alpha)
be a
NS
-Hom-Lie algebra. We define the two brackets

\displaystyle\{x,y,z\}=\alpha(z)\circ(y\circ x),

(6.83)

\displaystyle[x,y,z]=[x,y]\curlyvee\alpha(z)-\alpha(z)\circ(x\curlyvee y)\ \ \forall x,y,z\in L.

(6.84)

Let
[\![x,y,z]\!]=[[x,y],z]
.

Theorem 6.12
.

Under the above notations
(V,\circ,\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\},\curlyvee,[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot],\alpha)
is a NS-Hom-Lie Yamaguti algebra.

Proof.

Let
x,y,z
and
t\in A
. First by direct calculation we have

\displaystyle\underset{x,y,z}{\circlearrowleft}\bigg([x,y]^{*}\curlyvee\alpha(z)-\alpha(x)\circ(y\curlyvee z)+[x,y,z]\bigg)

\displaystyle=\underset{x,y,z}{\circlearrowleft}\bigg([x,y]^{*}\curlyvee\alpha(z)-\alpha(x)\circ(y\curlyvee z)+[x,y]\curlyvee\alpha(z)-\alpha(z)\circ(x\curlyvee y)\bigg)

\displaystyle=2\underset{x,y,z}{\circlearrowleft}\bigg([x,y]^{*}\curlyvee\alpha(z)-\alpha(x)\circ(y\curlyvee z)\bigg)=0.

Thus we have proved (
6.65
). Next,
we have

\displaystyle\{\alpha(x_{4}),\alpha(x_{1}),[x_{2},x_{3}]\}-\alpha^{2}(x_{2})\circ\{x_{4},x_{1},x_{3}\}+\alpha^{2}(x_{3})\circ\{x_{4},x_{1},x_{2}\}

\displaystyle=\alpha([x_{2},x_{3}])\circ(\alpha(x_{1})\circ\alpha(x_{4}))-\alpha^{2}(x_{2})\circ(\alpha(x_{3})\circ(x_{1}\circ x_{4}))+\alpha^{2}(x_{3})\circ(\alpha(x_{2})\circ(x_{1}\circ x_{4}))\overset{\eqref{NS-Lie-1}}{=}0.

Hence the identity (
6.70
) is proved.
Now we check the identity (
6.73
). It follows from the NS-Hom-Lie identity (
6.81
):

\displaystyle\{\alpha(x),\alpha(y),z\curlyvee t\}^{\wedge}+\alpha^{2}(t)\circ[x,y,z]-\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket\curlyvee\alpha^{2}(t)

\displaystyle\quad+[\alpha(x),\alpha(y),[z,t]^{*}]-\alpha^{2}(z)\circ[x,y,t]-\alpha^{2}(z)\curlyvee\left\llbracket x,y,t\right\rrbracket

\displaystyle=\underline{[\alpha(x),\alpha(y)]\circ(z\curlyvee t)}+\underline{\alpha^{2}(t)\circ([x,y]\curlyvee\alpha(z))}-\underline{\underline{\alpha^{2}(t)\circ(\alpha(z)\circ(x\curlyvee y))}}-\underline{[[x,y],\alpha(z)]\curlyvee\alpha^{2}(t)}

\displaystyle\quad+\underline{[\alpha(x),\alpha(y)]\curlyvee\alpha([z,t]^{*})}-\underline{\underline{\alpha([z,t]^{*})\circ(\alpha(x)\curlyvee\alpha(y))}}-\underline{\alpha^{2}(z)\circ([x,y]\curlyvee\alpha(t))}

\displaystyle\quad+\underline{\underline{\alpha^{2}(z)\circ(\alpha(t)\circ(x\curlyvee y))}}-\underline{\alpha^{2}(z)\curlyvee[[x,y],\alpha(t)]}=0.

The other identities are shown by direct computation and using (

Covert communications in a downlink time division multiple access system
Authors: Jihwan Moon, Taehoon Kim, Inkyu Bang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn this paper, we investigate covert communications in a downlink time division multiple access system. While transmitting public messages sequentially to multiple users, the source node concurrently embeds covert messages intended for a covert receiver. Upon reception, the covert receiver cancels the public messages to recover the covert information. We focus on maximizing the achievable sum covert rate by effectively allocating the total transmit power across time slots, subject to a constraint on the detection error probability at the users. The resulting covert rate maximization problem is non-convex with respect to the total transmit power and power allocation variables. To address this, we propose a two-layer optimization framework powered by the gradient projection method. Numerical results validate the effectiveness of the proposed approach and further analyze the impact of key system parameters on the performance.Subject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

The rapid evolution of wireless communications technologies, including 5G/6G networks and large-scale Internet-of-Things (IoT) deployments, has dramatically increased the volume of data transmitted over open wireless media. Alongside this growth, ensuring secure and reliable wireless communications has become a critical challenge, as the broadcast nature of wireless channels inherently exposes transmitted signals to unauthorized interception and monitoring1. As wireless systems continue to support mission-critical and privacy-sensitive applications, security mechanisms that go beyond conventional assumptions are increasingly required.

Traditionally, wireless security has relied on cryptographic techniques such as encryption and authentication, which protect transmitted information by exploiting computational hardness2. While cryptography has proven effective in many scenarios, it suffers from several inherent limitations in wireless environments. First, secure key distribution and management often require additional signaling channels or trusted infrastructures, which may not be available in decentralized or dynamic networks. Second, cryptographic algorithms impose non-negligible computational and energy overhead, making them less suitable for low-power or latency-sensitive devices such as IoT nodes. Moreover, cryptographic security can be compromised by adversaries equipped with high computational capabilities or future technologies, such as quantum computing, which may fundamentally weaken existing encryption schemes.

To complement traditional cryptographic approaches, physical-layer security (PLS) has emerged as a promising alternative that exploits the intrinsic randomness of wireless channels–such as noise, fading, and interference–to enhance confidentiality3. By appropriately designing transmission strategies at the physical layer, PLS techniques can prevent unauthorized receivers from reliably decoding confidential messages without relying on secret keys. This paradigm has motivated extensive research on secrecy capacity, artificial noise injection, and cooperative jamming in various wireless systems4.

Beyond confidentiality, an even stronger notion of security is achieved by covert communications, also known as low-probability-of-detection communications. In covert communications systems, the objective is not only to protect the message content but also to conceal the very existence of the transmission from potential detectors, commonly referred to as wardens5. If a transmission cannot be detected, an adversary is unable to initiate decoding, jamming, or further attacks. This additional security layer is particularly attractive in surveillance, military, and privacy-sensitive IoT applications. Fundamental results have shown that covert communications is subject to stringent limits–such as the square-root law in additive white Gaussian noise (AWGN) channels, highlighting the inherent trade-off between covertness and throughput6.

In recent years, significant efforts have been devoted to improving covert communications performance under various system models. Prior studies have investigated covert transmissions with noise uncertainty7,8, relay-assisted covert communications9, and intelligent reflecting surface (IRS)-aided covert systems10. These works demonstrate that exploiting network cooperation, channel uncertainty, and environmental reconfigurability can substantially enhance covert rates while satisfying detection error probability (DEP) constraints. However, most existing studies focus on point-to-point links or relay-based architectures, and relatively few address multi-user downlink systems.

Some recent works have explored covert communications in non-orthogonal multiple access (NOMA) systems, where public and covert signals are superimposed in the power domain and inter-user interference is leveraged to confuse the warden11–13. Similar attempts were made in rate splitting multiple access (RSMA) systems, a principle strategy of which is to split user messages into a common part (decoded by all) and a private part (decoded by specific users)14,15. In contrast, covert communications in time division multiple access (TDMA) systems, one of the most fundamental and widely deployed multiple-access schemes, remain largely unexplored, particularly in downlink scenarios. In a downlink TDMA system, scheduled users may possibly act as potential detectors of unintended transmissions, making covert signaling especially challenging and practically relevant. Moreover, a thorough study and performance results of covert communications in TDMA are necessary for practitioners to compare and choose the most appropriate multiple access system since none is universally the best.

This paper addresses this gap by investigating covert communications in a downlink TDMA system. Specifically, a source node sequentially transmits public messages to multiple users while simultaneously embedding covert messages intended for a covert destination. The covert receiver exploits its knowledge of the public messages to cancel them and recover the covert information, whereas the users act as unintentional wardens attempting to detect the presence of covert transmission. We formulate a sum covert rate maximization problem subject to DEP constraints at each user and minimum public rate requirements. The resulting optimization problem is non-convex due to the coupling between power allocation variables. To efficiently solve this problem, we propose a two-layer optimization framework with the gradient projection method (GPM), which is globally convergent to a stationary point in reasonable time. Discussion

This paper investigated covert communications in a downlink TDMA system, where a source simultaneously delivers public messages to scheduled users and covert messages to a hidden receiver. By modeling the scheduled users as potential detectors, we formulated a sum covert rate maximization problem subject to DEP constraints and minimum public rate requirements. To address the resulting non-convex optimization problem, we proposed a two-layer optimization framework; total transmit power in the outer layer and power allocation for public and covert messages in the inner layer. We developed the GPM-based algorithm to find a KKT point of the power allocation in the inner layer, and by utilizing feasibility conditions, we effectively reduced the one-dimensional search region for the optimal total transmit power in the outer layer. Numerical results demonstrated that jointly optimizing the total transmit power and power allocation across time slots significantly improves covert communications performance compared with baseline schemes. The results further revealed important trade-offs between covert rate, total transmit power, number of users, and DEP thresholds. In addition, a comparison with an equivalent NOMA system showed that TDMA can be superior when inter-user interference becomes substantial by either high transmission power or large number of receivers. A further study on the effect of a specific noise uncertainty distribution on the performance, asymptotic analysis of the performance with respect to key system parameters, and a more comprehensive performance comparison among more multiple access systems such as RSMA can be important future work.

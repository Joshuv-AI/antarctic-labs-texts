Three-dimensional non-relativistic chiral massive higher-spin gravity
Arpita Mitra a, Debangshu Mukherjee b, and Tung Tran c

Abstract
We obtain a non-relativistic chiral massive higher-spin gravity in a deformed A ​ d ​ S 3 AdS_{3} spacetime by applying a Lifshitz deformation and subsequent null reduction to chiral massless higher-spin gravity in A ​ d ​ S 4 AdS_{4} . Intriguingly, the vertices of this non-relativistic theory are less constrained than the ones of the original 4 ​ d 4d chiral massless theory since we do not have enough dynamical generators to fix the couplings uniquely. Anticipating higher-spin interactions should be suppressed, we propose a simple approximate mass-spin relation which interpolates between the relativistic and non-relativistic regimes. With the proposed mass-spin relation, we observe that that higher-spin interactions indeed become suppressed at large spins, consistent with low-energy physics. We conjecture that the holographic dual of the non-relativistic chiral massive higher-spin gravity proposed in this work is a 2 ​ d 2d non-relativistic Landau-Ginzburg theory in the light-cone gauge. This non-relativistic theory is expected to describe a two-fluid system with a λ \lambda -point constrained in one spatial dimension.

2.1
Lifshitz anisotropic scaling and Schrödinger algebra

It is well known that the conformal algebra of a
(d+2)
-dimensional flat spacetime
\mathbb{M}_{d+2}
is
\mathfrak{so}(d+2,2)
, which happens to coincide with the isometry algebra of
AdS_{d+3}
. Within this Lie algebra, one can look for a subalgebra known as the Schrödinger algebra
\mathtt{Sch}_{\mathtt{z}}(d)
parametrized by a Lifshitz dynamical exponent
\mathtt{z}
and the number of spatial dimensions
d
. This is the algebra that is relevant for studying various condense matter systems, cf.
31
;
82
;
33
;
22
.

Let
(\mathcal{P}^{A},\mathcal{J}^{AB},\mathcal{D},\mathcal{K}^{A})
be the generators correspond to translations, Lorentz rotations, dilatation and special conformal boosts of the conformal algebra
\mathfrak{so}(d+2,2)
where
A=0,1,\ldots,d+1
. Then, the generators

\displaystyle(P^{+},P^{-},P^{a},J^{ab},G^{a},D,K)\,,\qquad a=1,\ldots,d\,,

(1)

of the Schrödinger algebra
\mathtt{Sch}_{\mathtt{z}}(d)
can be obtained from
(\mathcal{P}^{A},\mathcal{J}^{AB},\mathcal{D},\mathcal{K}^{A})
as (see e.g.
11
)

\displaystyle P^{+}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\big(\mathcal{P}^{d+1}+\mathcal{P}^{0}\big)\,,\qquad

\displaystyle P^{-}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\big(\mathcal{P}^{d+1}-\mathcal{P}^{0}\big)\,,

(2a)

\displaystyle D

\displaystyle=(\mathtt{z}-1)\mathcal{J}^{d+1\,0}+\mathcal{D}\,,\qquad

\displaystyle K

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\mathcal{K}^{d+1}+\mathcal{K}^{0})\equiv\mathcal{K}^{+}\,,

(2b)

\displaystyle G^{a}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\big(\mathcal{J}^{d+1\,a}+\mathcal{J}^{0\,a}\big)\equiv\mathcal{J}^{+a}\,.

(2c)

Thus,
\mathtt{Sch}_{\mathtt{z}}(d)
is indeed a subalgebra of
\mathfrak{so}(d+2,2)
.

In the literature, see e.g.
83
,
P^{+}
is usually referred to as the number or mass generator. The generators
G^{a}
are known as the Galilean boosts. They are the descendant of Lorentz boost generators
\mathcal{J}^{+a}
in the light-cone gauge where
x^{\pm}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(x^{d+1}\pm x^{0})
. Note that
P^{-}
serves as the Hamiltonian, generating the evolution of the system with
\mathtt{Sch}_{\mathtt{z}}(d)
being the underlying symmetry.

What differs
\mathtt{Sch}_{\mathtt{z}}(d)
with other Inönü-Wigner subalgebras of the conformal algebra is the dilatation generator
D
. This generator is special since it is defined with a Lifshitz twist, telling the scaling dimensions of other generators in Schrod̈inger regime.

Note that when
\mathtt{z}\neq 2
, the Schrödinger algebra
does not contain any of the the special conformal boost generators
\mathcal{K}^{A}
of
\mathfrak{so}(d+2,2)
among its generators. On the other hand, when the Lifshitz scaling
\mathtt{z}=2
, the algebra
\mathtt{Sch}_{\mathtt{z}=2}(d)
algebra acquires one special conformal boost generator, which is
\mathcal{K}^{+}\equiv K
. In this case,
\{P^{-},D,K\}
form an
\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})
subalgebra where
[D,P^{-}]=-P^{-}
,
[D,K]=2K
, and
[P^{-},K]=D
. For more details, see
83
.

In this work, the Schrödinger algebra of interest will be
\mathtt{Sch}_{\mathtt{z}=2}(1)\subset\mathfrak{so}(3,2)
. This algebra has
6
generators, which are
(P^{+},P^{-},P^{1},G^{1},D,K)
– i.e. no spatial rotations. Since
\mathtt{Sch}_{\mathtt{z}}(d)
is conveniently realized in light-cone coordinates, field theory representations built upon
\mathtt{Sch}_{\mathtt{z}}(d)
, when available, are most naturally formulated in the light-cone gauge. It must be noted that, following the terminology in
87
, we shall refer to
\mathtt{Sch}_{\mathtt{z}}(1)
as Schrödinger algebra for arbitrary
\mathtt{z}
, even though, in other literature, this terminology is typically reserved only for
\mathtt{z}=2
. Note that most of our subsequent analysis will, in fact, focus on the case
\mathtt{z}=2
.

2.2
Light-front formalism for massless fields in
AdS_{4}

The line element of exact
AdS_{4}
spacetime has a particular nice representation in the Poincaré patch where

\displaystyle ds^{2}=\frac{1}{r^{2}}\Big(-dx_{0}^{2}+dx_{1}^{2}+dx_{2}^{2}+dr^{2}\Big)=\frac{1}{r^{2}}\Big(2dx^{+}dx^{-}+2dx_{1}^{2}+dr^{2}\Big)\,.

(3)

From (
3
), one can find two groups of isometry generators: (i) kinematical generators, (ii) dynamical generators, cf.
55
;
57
, as

\displaystyle\mathrm{\underline{kinematical\ generators}}\quad

\displaystyle:\qquad P^{+},P^{1},J^{+1},J^{+-},D,K^{+},K^{1}\,,

(4a)

\displaystyle\mathrm{\underline{dynamical\ generators}}\quad

\displaystyle:\qquad P^{-},J^{-1},K^{-}\,.

(4b)

On the one hand, the kinematic generators, which do not receive non-linear corrections on the hypersurface
x^{+}=0
, cf.
55
, play the role of “constrainer” that shape the general expressions of the dynamical generators
(P^{-},J^{-1},K^{-})
. The dynamical generators, on the other hand, are crucial in fixing couplings, thus defining a theory in the light-cone gauge.

Note that when we specify
x^{+}
as the time direction, the number of dynamical generators turn out to be minimal. As a result, the process of studying deformation of the algebra can be slightly simpler (in good cases) compared to covariant approaches. This is one of the advantages of the light-front formalism. (See further general discussion in e.g.
56
and
70
.) Let us elaborate this idea in more detail.

Denoting
\varphi_{\pm s}(x^{+},x^{-},x^{1},r)
as massless spinning fields in position space, with helicity
\pm s
. These fields can be related to the ones in momentum space via Fourier transform as

\displaystyle\varphi_{\pm s}(x^{+},x^{-},x^{1},r)=\int\frac{d\rho\,d\beta}{2\pi}e^{i(\beta x^{-}+\rho x^{1})}\varphi_{\pm s}(x^{+},\beta,\rho,r)\,,\qquad\rho\equiv p^{1}\,,\qquad\beta\equiv p^{+}\,.

(5)

Note that
\varphi^{\dagger}_{+s}(x^{+},\mathtt{p},r)=\varphi_{-s}(x^{+},-\mathtt{p},r)
, where
\mathtt{p}=(\beta,\rho)
. (It is well-known that, in
4d
, any massless spinning field can be described by two complex scalar fields
\varphi_{\pm s}
, which are hermitian conjugate of each other.)

At the level of free fields, the charges
Q
associated with AdS isometry generators
\mathcal{G}
, which are quadratic in fields, can be defined as:

\displaystyle Q=\int d^{2}\mathtt{p}\,dr\,\beta\,\mathrm{Tr}\Big[(\varphi_{h})^{\dagger}\mathcal{G}(x^{+},\mathtt{p},\partial_{\mathtt{p}},r,\partial_{r})\varphi_{h}\Big]\,.

(6)

Note that the kinematic generators, which keeps the
x^{+}=0
hypersurface invariant, have the following differential expression, cf.
58
,

\displaystyle P^{+}

\displaystyle=\beta\,,

\displaystyle P^{1}

\displaystyle=\rho,

(7a)

\displaystyle J^{+1}

\displaystyle={i}x^{+}\rho+\partial_{\rho}\beta,

\displaystyle J^{+-}

\displaystyle={i}x^{+}P^{-}+\partial_{\beta}\beta\,,

(7b)

\displaystyle D

\displaystyle={i}x^{+}P^{-}-\partial_{\beta}\beta-\partial_{\rho}\rho+r\partial_{r}+1\,,

(7c)

\displaystyle K^{+}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(2{i}x^{+}\partial_{\beta}-\partial_{\rho}^{2}+r^{2})\beta+{i}x^{+}D\,,

(7d)

\displaystyle K^{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(2ix^{+}\partial_{\beta}-\partial_{\rho}^{2}+r^{2})\rho-\partial_{\rho}D+M^{r1}r+{i}M^{1-}x^{+}\,.

(7e)

On the other hand, one finds that the dynamical generators are of the form

\displaystyle P^{-}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\rho^{2}}{2\beta}+\frac{\partial_{r}^{2}}{2\beta}\,,

(8a)

\displaystyle J^{-1}

\displaystyle=-\partial_{\beta}\rho+\partial_{\rho}P^{-}-M^{r1}\frac{1}{\beta}\partial_{r}\,,

(8b)

\displaystyle K^{-}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(2ix^{+}\partial_{\beta}-\partial_{\rho}^{2}+r^{2})P^{-}-\partial_{\beta}D+\frac{1}{\beta}(\partial_{r}\partial_{\rho}-r\rho)M^{r1}-\frac{1}{\beta}M^{r1}M^{r1}\,.

(8c)

In working in 4 dimensions, we can replace the helicity generator
M^{r1}
by a label
h
. This label stands for the helicity of the field, which
M^{r1}
acts on. This stems from the fact that the little group associated to massless fields of the isometry group of
AdS_{4}
is
O(2)\simeq U(1)
. The helicity
h
is then simply a convenient label for the
U(1)
charges. In terms of oscillators, the helicity operator
M^{r1}
can be realized as (see
58
)

\displaystyle M^{r1}=M^{LR}=(\mathsf{a}^{\dagger})^{R}\mathsf{a}^{L}-(\mathsf{a}^{\dagger})^{L}\mathsf{a}^{R}\,,\qquad\text{where}\qquad[\mathsf{a}^{R},(\mathsf{a}^{\dagger})^{L}]=1\,,\quad[\mathsf{a}^{L},(\mathsf{a}^{\dagger})^{R}]=1\,.

(9)

Here,
\mathsf{a}^{L,R}
are annihilation operators which act on the vacuum as
\mathsf{a}_{L,R}|0\rangle=0
. On the other hand,
(\mathsf{a}^{\dagger})^{L,R}
are creation operators which can generate higher-spin states as

\displaystyle|\varphi_{s}(\mathtt{p},r,\mathsf{a})\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{s!}}\Big((\mathsf{a}^{\dagger}_{L})^{s}\varphi_{s}(\mathtt{p},r)+(\mathsf{a}^{\dagger}_{R})^{s}\varphi_{-s}(\mathtt{p},r)\Big)|0\rangle\,.

(10)

Note that
L
(left) and
R
(right) are the directions combined from the usual
(r,1)
directions. Henceforth, we will not use this representation of
M^{r1}
but the helicity label
h
for simplicity.

A crucial step in the light-front approach is to set
x^{+}=0
, which prevents the kinematic generators from acquiring non-linear corrections in fields. Consequently, the generators which depend on
x^{+}
getting simplified to:

\displaystyle J^{+1}

\displaystyle=\partial_{\rho}\beta,

\displaystyle J^{+-}

\displaystyle=\partial_{\beta}\beta\,,

(11a)

\displaystyle D

\displaystyle=-\partial_{\beta}\beta-\partial_{\rho}\rho+r\partial_{r}+1\,,

(11b)

\displaystyle K^{+}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\beta(-\partial_{\rho}^{2}+r^{2})\,,

(11c)

\displaystyle K^{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(-\partial_{\rho}^{2}+r^{2})\rho-\partial_{\rho}D+h\,r\,,

(11d)

\displaystyle K^{-}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(-\partial_{\rho}^{2}+r^{2})P^{-}-\partial_{\beta}D+\frac{1}{\beta}(\partial_{r}\partial_{\rho}-r\rho)h-\frac{1}{\beta}h^{2}\,.

(11e)

Denote
\boldsymbol{\mathsf{K}}=\{P^{+},P^{1},J^{+1},J^{+-},D,K^{+},K^{1}\}
to be the set of kinematic generators above. One finds through various relations

\displaystyle[\boldsymbol{\mathsf{K}},\boldsymbol{\mathsf{D}}]=\boldsymbol{\mathsf{K}}\,,\qquad[\boldsymbol{\mathsf{K}},\boldsymbol{\mathsf{D}}]=\boldsymbol{\mathsf{D}}\,,

(12)

where
[\,,]
denotes the Poisson-Dirac commutation relations between fields
58
:

\displaystyle\big[\varphi_{h}(p,r),\varphi_{h^{\prime}}(p^{\prime},r^{\prime})\big]\Big|_{\text{equal }x^{+}}=\frac{\delta^{2}(\mathtt{p}+\mathtt{p}^{\prime})\delta(r-r^{\prime})}{2\beta}\delta_{h+h^{\prime},0}\,,

(13)

that, there will be enough constraints to
partially
fix the generic forms of the dynamical generators

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathsf{D}}=\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\mathsf{D}_{n}\,,

(14)

which can be higher-order in fields indicated by the subscript
n
– the number of fields that enter the vertices (for instance
D_{2}=\{P^{-},J^{-1},K^{-}\}
). Then, to fix a theory uniquely, one only needs to solve the following set of equations

\displaystyle[\boldsymbol{\mathsf{D}},\boldsymbol{\mathsf{D}}]=0\,.

(15)

This is the general strategy employed in the light-front approach to construct theories from the bottom-up.

4
Non-relativistic dynamics on the Schrödinger cone

The presence of torsion in the geometry calls for a revisiting of the analysis in

57

58

4.1
Kinetic action in Schrödinger background

From the dynamical generator
P^{-}
, it is expected that the Lagrangian for any spinning field on the Schrödinger background (
17
) should be of the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}[s]=\varphi_{-s}\Big(\Box+\upsilon^{2}r^{2-2\mathtt{z}}(\partial_{-})^{2}\Big)\varphi_{+s}\,,\qquad\Box=2\partial_{+}\partial_{-}+\partial_{1}^{2}+\partial_{r}^{2}\,.

(36)

where
\varphi_{+s}
and
\varphi_{-s}
are two complex valued fields which are Hermitian conjugate to each other. Notice that the kinetic operator
\Box+\upsilon^{2}r^{2-2\mathtt{z}}(\partial_{-})^{2}
has dimension
[length]^{-2}
at
\mathtt{z}=2
.
To show that the above is a valid Lagrangian in the light-cone gauge, let us study a simple model of a conformally coupled scalar field
\varphi
on the Schrödinger background (
17
) as an example by looking at the following action

\displaystyle S_{2}[0]=\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\Big(g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}{\varphi}\partial_{\nu}{\varphi}+\frac{1}{6}R{\varphi}^{2}\Big)\,.

(37)

For the background (
17
), the Ricci scalar
R=-\frac{12}{L^{2}}
. Then, upon rescaling
\varphi\mapsto\frac{r}{L}\varphi
, the above action reduces to

\displaystyle S_{2}[0]

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\int d^{4}x\,\varphi\Big(\Box+\upsilon^{2}r^{2-2\mathtt{z}}(\partial_{-})^{2}\Big)\varphi\,.

(38)

It is important to point out that the
\sqrt{-g}
has been absorbed by the above rescaling and there is no AdS radius
L
remaining. Observe that without the
(\partial_{-})^{2}
term, the kinetic operator is simply the one of flat space.
6
6

6

Note the positions of the indices for such simplification to happen.

At this stage, we can take (
36
) to be the kinetic action for any spinning fields in the light-cone gauge, since we have already derived the explicit form of the
P^{-}
generator; keeping in mind that any spinning fields can be described by two scalars, which are complex conjugate to each other. (Indeed, while the
M^{r1}
generator, cf. (
9
), does not participate in the Schrödinger algebra, it nevertheless remains present as the operator that specifies the helicity of fields.) Then, as usual, if we start with a generic tensorial field and imposing the light-cone gauge, one can choose two components among the remaining components of the original tensor field to be the physical ones – the
\varphi_{\pm s}
. For relevant discussion, see e.g. Section 13 in
70
.

In making connection with the covariant approach, the reader may find the material in Appendix
A
helpful.
7
7

7

There, we show that after some suitable rescaling, all chiral spinning fields (a specific field representations that are closely related to our setting) can have a similar kinetic term with the ones in flat space. This aligns with the result in
55
. However, due to the lack of Lorentz and gauge invariance, we could not engineer some terms which give the differential operator
\upsilon^{2}r^{2-2\mathtt{z}}(\partial_{-})^{2}
.

4.2
Bulk-to-boundary propagator and Schrödinger boundary algebra

Let us now compute the bulk-to-boundary propagator for the
rescaled
higher-spin fields in the light-cone gauge, whose Lagrangian is (
36
).

To compute the bulk-to-boundary propagator for
\varphi_{\pm s}
, we will follow the techniques developed in
21
;
90
;
50
. Note that since we are working with
rescaled
complex scalars, there are some intermediate details that differ with the standard treatment in general dimension, in e.g.
21
;
90
;
50
. Recall that the equation of motion of a rescaled scalar
\varphi_{\pm s}
is given by

\Big(\Box+\upsilon^{2}r^{2-2\mathtt{z}}(\partial_{-})^{2}\Big)\varphi_{\pm s}=0\,,\qquad\text{where}\qquad\Box\equiv 2\partial_{+}\partial_{-}+\partial_{1}^{2}+\partial_{r}^{2}\,.

(39)

Since there are translation invariance away from the radial direction, cf. (
17
), the solution can be decomposed into plane wave modes along boundary directions. In particular,

\varphi(x^{+},x^{-},x^{1},r)=\int\frac{d^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3/2}}~e^{i(\gamma x^{+}+\beta x^{-}+\rho x^{1})}\mathcal{K}_{p}(r)\,,\qquad\vec{p}=(\beta,\gamma,\rho)\,.

(40)

where we have denoted
\gamma\equiv p^{-}
. The radial mode
\mathcal{K}_{p}(r)
of a complex scalar field thus satisfies the following equation

\displaystyle\big(2\beta\gamma+\rho^{2}+r^{2-2\mathtt{z}}\upsilon^{2}\beta^{2}-\partial_{r}^{2}\big)\mathcal{K}_{p}(r)=0\,.

(41)

For generic integer values of the dynamical exponent
\mathtt{z}
, solutions to the above exists but are quite complicated. Here, we are mainly interested in the case where
\mathtt{z}=2
, for which the solution is given by,

\mathcal{K}_{p}(r)=c_{1}(\vec{p})\sqrt{r}I_{\nu}(|p|r)+c_{2}(\vec{p})\sqrt{r}K_{\nu}(|p|r)\ ,

(42)

where

\nu=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{1+4\upsilon^{2}\beta^{2}}\,,\quad\text{and}\quad|p|\equiv\sqrt{p^{2}}=\sqrt{2\beta\gamma+\rho^{2}}\ .

(43)

Here,
I_{\nu}
and
K_{\nu}
are
modified Bessel functions
of the first and second kind, respectively.

Before proceeding further, it must be noted that our deformed
AdS_{4}
spacetime has Lorentzian signature, which brings with it certain subtleties as compared to Euclidean case. In particular, regularity of the normalizable solutions in the deep bulk region (
r\to\infty
) demands that
c_{1}(\vec{p})=0
.
8
8

8

This follows from the asymptotic expansions for large
r
of the modified Bessel functions:

\displaystyle I_{\nu}(|p|r)\sim\frac{e^{|p|r}}{\sqrt{|p|r}}\,,\qquad K_{\nu}(|p|r)\sim\frac{e^{-|p|r}}{\sqrt{|p|r}}\,.

(44)

As a result, to make the solution regular, we shall impose another boundary condition, which is
\mathcal{K}_{p}|_{r=\epsilon}=1
at a cut-off
r=\epsilon
. This leads to a complete solution, which is

\mathcal{K}_{p}(r)=\frac{\sqrt{r}K_{\nu}(|p|r)}{\sqrt{\epsilon}K_{\nu}(|p|\epsilon)}\ .

(45)

Note that the cut-off
\epsilon
is important in computing the 2-pt function
42
, or loop integrals as to regularize divergences. However, for other higher-point tree-level amplitudes in AdS, we can safely absorb the factor
\sqrt{\epsilon}K_{\nu}(|p|\epsilon)
in the definition of the higher-point functions. Furthermore, timelike (
p^{2}<0
) or spacelike (
p^{2}>0
) 3-momentum along the boundary directions will not affect too much our analysis below since the solution (
45
) has been amended appropriately such that it is regular everywhere. To this end, the general solution to the equation of motion (
39
) is

\displaystyle\varphi(x^{+},x^{-},x^{1},r)=\int\frac{d^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3/2}}~e^{i(\gamma x^{+}+\beta x^{-}+\rho x^{1})}\frac{\sqrt{r}K_{\nu}(|p|r)}{\sqrt{\epsilon}K_{\nu}(|p|\epsilon)}\,.

(46)

Next, we will find the conformal dimension of the operator dual to the bulk
rescaled
complex scalars
\varphi_{\pm s}
by examining the behavior of
\varphi_{\pm s}
near the boundary
r=0
. Assuming the solution scales as
\varphi=r^{\Delta}{\phi}_{0}(x^{+},x^{-},r)
near the boundary, we obtain

\displaystyle\Delta(\Delta-1)-\upsilon^{2}\beta^{2}=0\,.

(47)

The roots of the above are:

\displaystyle\Delta_{\pm}=\frac{1\pm\sqrt{1+4\upsilon^{2}\beta^{2}}}{2}\,.

(48)

For simplicity, we may set
\upsilon=1
, assuming that we are working at
\mathtt{z}=2
and
\beta^{2}
is a number. (In fact, after a dimensional compactification along
x^{-}
direction,
\beta
can be replaced with a dimensionless parameter viewed as the non-relativistic mass
m_{s}
associated to
\varphi_{\pm s}
, cf.
83
.) Observe that due to the rescaling of fields, the above roots do not satisfy the usually relation
\Delta(\Delta-d)=m^{2}L^{2}
, which determines the conformal dimension of a massive scalar field in
AdS_{d+1}
.

In the same spirit with Flato-Fronsdal theorem
20
, keeping in mind the fact that we are in an IR regime after the deformation of geometry happened, cf. (
17
), we will choose
\Delta_{0}=\Delta_{+}+1
to be the conformal dimension of the rescaled scalar field, and
\Delta_{s}=\Delta_{-}+1
to be the conformal dimension of the higher-spin fields in what we call Metsaev frame.
9
9

9

This is the frame where the kinetic term for higher-spin fields in
AdS
reduces to that of flat space, and all higher-spin fields have the same conformal dimensions upon rescaling
55
. Recall the manipulation around (
37
).
Here, the rescaled higher-spin fields in the bulk correspondence to some rescaled spin-
s
operators
\mathcal{J}_{s\geq 1}
on the boundary side in the transverse-traceless basis, see
55
. Note also that unlike the case of exact

To this end, let us drag the propagator through the generators of the Schrödinger algebra

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{AdS}\Big(\mathcal{K}\,\mathcal{J}(p)\Big)=\mathcal{K}\,\mathcal{G}_{b}\,\mathcal{J}(p)\,,\qquad p^{\mathtt{I}}=(\beta,\gamma,\rho)\,.

(49)

We find that

\displaystyle P^{+}_{b}

\displaystyle=\beta\,,\qquad\qquad P^{-}_{b}=\gamma\,,\qquad\qquad P^{1}_{b}=\rho\,,

(50a)

\displaystyle G^{1}_{b}

\displaystyle=\beta\partial_{\rho}-\rho\partial_{\gamma}\,,

(50b)

\displaystyle\tilde{D}_{b}

\displaystyle=-(2-\mathtt{z})\beta\partial_{\beta}-\rho\partial_{\rho}-\mathtt{z}\gamma\partial_{\gamma}-2+(\Delta-1)\Big|_{\mathtt{z}=2}\,,

(50c)

\displaystyle K_{b}^{+}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\beta\Big(2\partial_{\gamma}\partial_{\beta}+\partial_{\rho}^{2}\Big)-\tilde{D}_{b}\partial_{\gamma}\,,

(50d)

It can be checked that

\displaystyle[\tilde{D}_{b},P^{+}_{b}]

\displaystyle=-(2-\mathtt{z})P^{+}_{b}\,,\hskip 18.49988pt\ [\tilde{D}_{b},P^{-}_{b}]=-\mathtt{z}\,P^{-}_{b}\,,\hskip 18.49988pt[\tilde{D}_{b},P^{1}_{b}]=-P^{1}_{b}\,,

(51a)

\displaystyle[\tilde{D}_{b},G^{1}_{b}]

\displaystyle=(\mathtt{z}-1)G^{1}_{b}\,,\hskip 18.49988pt\hskip 9.24994pt\,[\tilde{D}_{b},K_{b}^{+}]=\mathtt{z}\,K_{b}^{+}\,,\hskip 18.49988pt[P^{-},K^{+}_{b}]=\tilde{D}_{b}-(\Delta-1)\,,

\displaystyle[G^{1}_{b},P^{1}_{b}]

4.3
Cubic vertices in Schrödinger background

In the light-front approach for field theories with local interactions, the dynamical generators will acquire corrections which are higher-order in fields, see
56

70

are required to satisfy

\displaystyle[\boldsymbol{\mathsf{K}},\boldsymbol{\mathsf{D}}]=\boldsymbol{\mathsf{D}}\quad\Rightarrow\quad\begin{cases}[\boldsymbol{\mathsf{K}},\boldsymbol{P}^{-}]=0\,,\qquad\qquad\boldsymbol{\mathsf{K}}=\{G^{1},K^{+}\}\,,\\

(52)

where
[\,,]
denotes the Poisson-Diract bracket, cf. (

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{P}^{-}=P_{2}^{-}+\sum_{n\geq 3}P^{-}_{n}\,,

(53)

where

is the Hamiltonian generator that is quadratic in the fields. In the above, the constraint

\displaystyle P_{n}^{-}=\int dx^{-}dx^{1}drV_{n}(\varphi,\ldots,\varphi)

2

Next, as all dynamical boost generators are absent once the system is driven away from exact

background, we will not have enough constraints to fix the cubic couplings uniquely as in
58

58

In momentum space, we shall denote

\displaystyle P^{-}_{n}

\displaystyle=\int d\Gamma_{n}\,\mathrm{Tr}\Big(\prod_{a=1}^{n}\varphi_{a}^{\dagger}\Big)p^{-}_{n}(\mathtt{p}_{a},\partial_{r_{a}};r)\delta_{r}\,,\qquad\delta_{r}:=\prod_{a=1}^{n}\delta(r-r_{a})\,,

(55)

where

, and

\displaystyle d\Gamma_{n}=(2\pi)^{2}\delta^{2}\Big(\sum_{a=1}^{n}\mathtt{p}_{a}\Big)\prod_{a=1}^{n}\frac{d^{2}\mathtt{p}_{a}}{2\pi}dr_{a}\,,\qquad d^{2}\mathtt{p}_{a}=d\rho_{a}d\beta_{a}\,.

(56)

Note that

are the label of the external fields.

Next, from
[\boldsymbol{\mathsf{K}},\boldsymbol{\mathsf{D}}]=\boldsymbol{\mathsf{D}}
, we get the following constraints for higher-order Hamiltonians (at
\mathtt{z}=2
)

\displaystyle\sum_{a=1}^{n}\beta_{a}\partial_{\rho_{a}}p^{-}_{n}(\mathtt{p}_{a},\partial_{r_{a}};r)\delta_{r}

\displaystyle=0\,,

(57a)

\displaystyle\sum_{a=1}^{n}\Big(-\rho_{a}\partial_{\rho_{a}}+\partial_{r_{a}}r_{a}\Big)p^{-}_{n}(\mathtt{p}_{a},\partial_{r_{a}};r)\delta_{r}

\displaystyle=\Big(\sum_{a=1}^{n}\Delta_{a}-3\Big)p^{-}_{n}(\mathtt{p}_{a},\partial_{r_{a}};r)\delta_{r}\,,

(57b)

\displaystyle\sum_{a=1}^{n}\,\beta_{a}\big(\partial^{2}_{\rho_{a}}-r_{a}^{2}\big)p^{-}_{n}(\mathtt{p}_{a},\partial_{r_{a}};r)\delta_{r}

\displaystyle=0\,,

(57c)

using (
13
) and doing integration by part. In particular, we have used the simple relations

\displaystyle[F(\varphi),\boldsymbol{\mathsf{K}}]=[\varphi,\boldsymbol{\mathsf{K}}]\frac{\delta F(\varphi)}{\delta\varphi}=\boldsymbol{\mathsf{K}}\varphi\frac{\delta F(\varphi)}{\delta\varphi}\,,

(58)

where
F(\varphi)
is some generic function in
\varphi
, and
[\,,]
is the Poisson-Dirac bracket (
13
). Note that as

contains

when
\tilde{D}
acts on it. Recall that
\beta
is dimensionless, so
\tilde{D}
only count the dimension of

. As a result, we obtain (

) keeping in mind that
\Delta_{a}
are the conformal dimensions of fields that enter the vertices.

In what follows, we will only focus on the case

. The equation (
57a
) tells us that the density
p^{-}_{3}(\mathtt{p}_{a},\partial_{r_{a}};r)\delta_{r}
will be polynomial in

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}=\frac{1}{3}\big(\check{\beta}_{1}\rho_{1}+\check{\beta}_{2}\rho_{2}+\check{\beta}_{3}\rho_{3}\big)\,,\qquad\check{\beta}_{a}=\beta_{a+1}-\beta_{a+2}\,,\quad a=1,2,3\,.

(59)

With this in mind, we can replace
\sum_{a=1,2,3}\rho_{a}\partial_{\rho_{a}}\mapsto\mathbb{P}\partial_{\mathbb{P}}
and
\sum_{a=1,2,3}\beta_{a}\partial_{\beta_{a}}\mapsto\mathbb{P}\partial_{\mathbb{P}}
by a mere change of variables. Then, at
\mathtt{z}=2
, (

5.2
Higher-spin interaction suppression

There are various reasons to believe that the interactions between massive higher-spin fields in the proposed theory should be suppressed. We will now show that if the masses are related to spins in an intricate way, higher-spin interactions will indeed be suppressed. Suppose momentum conservation in the
p^{+}
-direction holds even after compactification, i.e.

\displaystyle\sum_{a=1,2,3}\beta_{a}=0\mapsto\sum_{a=1,2,3}\mathrm{sign}(h_{a})m_{a}(s_{a})=0\,.

(86)

Then, since
m_{a}
come from a relativistic system, we may assume the spin-mass relations, at
\mathtt{z}=1
, follows the usual Regge trajectory. Namely,

\displaystyle s=\alpha_{0}+\alpha^{\prime}\,m^{2}\,,

(87)

where
\alpha_{0}
is the intercept, and
\alpha^{\prime}
is the slope. (A somehwat similar idea which emphasize on describing string theory as a broken phase of higher-spin gravity can be found in
54
.)
We now want to propose a formula, which can capture the approximate change of the above mass-spin relation for
\mathtt{z}\in[1,2]
, in the
c=\hbar=1
unit.

Let us make a rather strong assumption that massive spinning particle have some sort of bodies after doing the above null-reduction to go to non-relativistic regime. That is, they are composite and may be described by a spinning sphere or a spinning string etc. of size
L
, aligning with previous consideration in
86
;
85
;
43
.

Now, in the NR regime, we can imagine particles as some spinning sphere with fixed radius
L
and the angular velocity
v
. Then, the total angular momentum (viewed as “classical spin”) reads very roughly as
J\sim m
. On the other hand, in the relativistic regime, we can view our field as an open string of size
L
with some tension
T
. As the string is stressed, we can assume that the relativistic string carry an energy of
E\sim TL
. Then, the angular momentum scales as
J\sim p\frac{E}{T}\sim\frac{p^{2}}{T}
where
E=\sqrt{m^{2}+p^{2}}
.
This implies that in the relativistic regime,
J\sim m^{2}
. As a consequence, we can consider

\displaystyle s=\alpha_{0}+\alpha^{\prime}m^{\frac{2}{\mathtt{z}}}\,,

(88)

to be an approximate spin-mass relation that can interpolate between
\mathtt{z}\in[1,2]
. (A more sophisticated way to obtain the above approximate formula can be found, e.g. in
86
;
85
;
43
.) Observe that at
\mathtt{z}=2
, and for sufficiently large
s
,

\displaystyle m_{s}\,\propto\,s\,.

(89)

This implies that
\sum_{a}h_{a}=0
for sufficiently large spins in
3d
via (
86
). Thus, there will be no cubic interactions in this case, as the coupling constant
1/\Gamma(\sum_{a}h_{a})
will effectively trivialize cubic interactions in the large spin sector, leading to a suppression of higher-spin interactions. From the low-energy physics point of view, this is totally expected and acceptable.

A holography conjecture

Given all the information of the bulk theory presented above, let us now give a discussion on what the dual theory on the boundary should be.

As briefly discussed above, the ’t Hooft coupling

Given these data points, we conjecture that there should be a duality between

Conclusion

In this work, we study the non-relativistic limit of massless chiral HSGRA in
AdS_{4}
by introducing a Lifshitz dynamical exponential which drags the relativistic system to a lower dimensional NR chiral massive theory in a deformed
AdS_{3}
. We find that the resulting non-relativistic chiral massive theory is not topological and can have reasonable physics with higher-spin interactions suppressed in the large spin sector. We expect that the massive theory in this work can be used to describe a two-fluid system in one spatial dimension

;
22
. This study will be done in a companion work.

Based on the fact that the Schrödinger algebra is a light-cone one, we have adapted our analysis along the settings of light-front formalism, cf.

;

;

If our conjectures regarding the duality between

-dimensional superfluid and

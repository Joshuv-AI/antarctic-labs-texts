Generalized Gell-Mann–Nishijima Formula
E. Castillo-Ruiz, Henry Diaz, V. Pleitez

Abstract
In this paper, we present a generalization of the Gell-Mann–Nishijima formula for the assignment of electric charges to the components of an arbitrary-dimensional multiplet which transforms under general gauge symmetries, regardless of whether these multiplets couple to particles with known charge. The method is valid for local or global unitary symmetries and works for theories with or without spontaneous or explicit symmetry breaking. We extend the method for other conserved charges as flavors (horizontal symmetries) and color (QCD).

\begin{split}\left(t,Y\right)^{i_{1}i_{2}i_{3}\ldots i_{p}}_{j_{1}j_{2}j_{3}\ldots j_{q}}\rightarrow e^{i\beta(x)\frac{Y}{2}}&U^{i_{1}}_{a}U^{i_{2}}_{b}\ldots U^{i_{p}}_{z}U^{a^{\prime}}_{j_{1}}U^{b^{\prime}}_{j_{2}}\ldots U^{z^{\prime}}_{j_{q}}\left(t,Y\right)^{ab\ldots z}_{a^{\prime}b^{\prime}\ldots z^{\prime}}\\
=e^{i\beta(x)\frac{Y}{2}}&\left[\bigoplus_{k=1}^{r_{1}}U^{i_{1}}_{a}U^{i_{2}}_{b}\ldots U^{i_{p}}_{z}U^{a^{\prime}}_{j_{1}}U^{b^{\prime}}_{j_{2}}\ldots U^{z^{\prime}}_{j_{q}}\left(s_{k},Y_{k}\right)^{ab\ldots z}_{a^{\prime}b^{\prime}\ldots z^{\prime}}\right.\\
&\left.\oplus\bigoplus_{l=1}^{r_{2}}U^{i_{1}}_{a}U^{i_{2}}_{b}\ldots U^{i_{p}}_{z}U^{a^{\prime}}_{j_{1}}U^{b^{\prime}}_{j_{2}}\ldots U^{z^{\prime}}_{j_{q}}\left(\bar{s}_{l},\bar{Y}_{l}\right)^{ab\ldots z}_{a^{\prime}b^{\prime}\ldots z^{\prime}}\right].\end{split}

(8)

What follows is to build the charge operator for different multiplets in the respective representation, taking into account that the assignment of electric charges makes sense only for physical fields (irreps) that result from the decompositions defined in Eq. (
7
). For that purpose, we have to write the gauge transformations in Eq. (
8
) as infinitesimal representations. Applying the operator defined in Eq. (
5
) as many times as the tensor
(t,Y)
has indices, the electric charge operator for a general reducible tensor is

\begin{split}\left[Q(t,Y)\right]^{i_{1}i_{2}i_{3}\ldots i_{p}}_{j_{1}j_{2}j_{3}\ldots j_{q}}&\approx M^{i_{1}}_{a}(t,Y)^{ai_{2}\ldots i_{p}}_{j_{1}j_{2}\ldots j_{q}}+M^{i_{2}}_{b}(t,Y)^{i_{1}b\ldots i_{p}}_{j_{1}j_{2}\ldots j_{q}}+\ldots+M^{i_{p}}_{z}(t,Y)^{i_{1}i_{2}\ldots z}_{j_{1}j_{2}\ldots j_{q}}\\
&-M^{a^{\prime}}_{j_{1}}(t,Y)^{i_{1}i_{2}\ldots i_{p}}_{a^{\prime}j_{2}\ldots j_{q}}M^{b^{\prime}}_{j_{2}}(t,Y)^{i_{1}i_{2}\ldots i_{p}}_{j_{1}b^{\prime}\ldots j_{q}}-\ldots-M^{z^{\prime}}_{j_{1}}(t,Y)^{i_{1}i_{2}\ldots i_{p}}_{j_{1}j_{2}\ldots z^{\prime}}\\
&+\frac{Y}{2}(t,Y)^{i_{1}i_{2}i_{3}\ldots i_{p}}_{j_{1}j_{2}j_{3}\ldots j_{q}}.\end{split}

(9)

The
M^{i}_{j}
tensors are linear combinations of the diagonal generators defined as

M^{i_{p}}_{a}\equiv\sum_{m}\alpha_{m}\frac{(\lambda_{m})^{i_{p}}_{a}}{2},\quad M_{j_{q}}^{b}\equiv\sum_{m}\alpha_{m}\frac{(\lambda_{m})_{j_{q}}^{b}}{2}.

(10)

Then, equation (
9
) is the generalized GN formula. When irreducible representations are made via Eq. (
7
), electric charge eigenstates will be obtained. All this process of assignment of electric charges will depend on how the tensor decomposition of
(t,Y)
is done, as shown in the following section for some representative cases.

In summary, the goal of this work is to obtain the
Q
operator applied to all kinds of multiplets, demonstrating that it is independent of whether any multiplet couples or not with other particles already known. We only use the properties of the Lie groups to which the multiplets belong. This method could be used for dark-matter multiplets such as
SU(2)
vector doublets
Sáez et al. 2019
, general fermionic n-tuplets of
SU(2)

Cirelli et al. 2006
, general scalar n-tuplets in the symmetry of SM
Beauchesne and Chiang 2024
, and so on.

IV
Conclusions

Here, we have presented a general method for assigning generalized charges in gauge models, extending the Gell-Mann–Nishijima formula. This method is applicable to multiple representations and works across different gauge symmetries, with or without spontaneous symmetry breaking, regardless of the multiplet nature. This approach opens the door to exploring new particles and theoretical models that may not be accessible through traditional methods, particularly for assigning charges in dark-matter multiplets, where interactions with known particles are limited or nonexistent. Also, the method facilitates the construction of theoretical models that include unconventional charges.

Although charge quantization is not a direct consequence of the method, it can be achieved through an appropriate choice of parameters
\alpha_{j}
and hypercharges
Pires and Ravinez 1998
. This highlights the flexibility of the approach to adapt to different physical scenarios, such as the study of gauge-coupling unification and neutrino mass generation.

In 331 models, if
Y
is an arbitrarily small real number, say
Y\sim 10^{-3}
, the components of the sextet, octet, or decuplet with an electric charge proportional to
Y
are of the millicharge type, while the others have exotic (but large) charges.

Finally, we highlighted that the method is not necessarily related to the electric charge, as have shown in subsection III D, but it may be useful for considering other conserved quantities. For instance, in theories with color symmetries like
SU(3)_{C}
, the charge operator tensors may be built, showing that the method still works fine for these kinds of representations, taking into account an isomorphism between the color charges and electric charges.

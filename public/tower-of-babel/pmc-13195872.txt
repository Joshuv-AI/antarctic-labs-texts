Overexpression of the ncd2 gene in Sphingomonas sp. Z392 improves the gut health and meat quality of broiler chickens
Authors: Gailing Wang, Chunhong Wang, Zengqin Wang, Jingwen Cao, Yanan Guo, Huili Xia, Chong Chen, Chaoying Liu, Aizhen Guo, Mingcheng Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The ncd2-overexpressing Sphingomonas sp. Z392 strain was verified to reduce ammonia levels in our previous study. To ensure its safe utilization, this study investigated the effects of strains on the gut health and meat quality of broiler chickens. Four groups were designed for the experiment: control group (CK), wild-type strain group (WT), ncd2 gene knockout strain group (KO), and ncd2-overexpressing strain group (OV). The last three groups were supplemented with the corresponding strains at a concentration of 4.0 × 105 CFU/mL in drinking water for 42 d. Microscopic and ultrastructural observations revealed that the ncd2-overexpressing Sphingomonas sp. Z392 strain increased the number of goblet cells in the villi of small intestine, lysosomes in the ileal epithelial cells and mitochondria in the colonic epithelial cells, and improved the swelling degree of mitochondria in the jejunal epithelial cells. In the OV group, the number of broiler chickens with digestive tract diseases was approximately one-fourth that in the CK group and the disease duration was significantly shortened. The OV broiler chickens showed the different weight gain with an increase of 8.83%, 3.56%, 7.18% compared to CK, WT, KO groups, respectively. In terms of meat quality, the drip loss of chest and leg muscles had the lowest value, decreased by 51.26% and 49.15% compared to CK group respectively. At the same time, the meat yellowing value (b* value) was higher than other groups. In summary, the ncd2-overexpressing Sphingomonas sp. Z392 strain could effectively improve the intestinal healthy of broiler chickens and enhance their growth performance and meat quality.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12917-026-05453-4.

Statement of novelty

The addition of 4×105 CFU/mL the ncd2-overexpressing Sphingomonas sp. Z392 strain to the drinking water of broiler chickens improved the histological structure of the intestine, reduced the risk of intestinal diseases, enhanced growth performance, and improved meat quality. Therefore, the ncd2-overexpressing Sphingomonas sp. Z392 strain could be safely used in the feeding and management of broiler chickens.

Introduction

Sphingomonas is a group of microorganism with extensive degradation capabilities, particularly those that exhibit significant advantages in terms of nitrogen metabolism and aromatic compound degradation [1]. Some strains of Sphingomonas can actively participate in nitrogen metabolism processes, such as converting ammonia to nitrite through nitrification and further converting it to nitrogen through denitrification, thereby achieving efficient removal of ammonia [2]. In addition, the genus Sphingomonas can also synthesize various biopolymers, such as Welan gum, which has important application value in food, medicine, and petroleum extraction [3].

Given the need to improve the air quality of chicken coops and develop the biological ammonia reduction, we isolated a strain of Sphingomonas Z392 with nitrification ability from the cecum of chickens [2]. Preliminary studies indicated that supplementation of drinking water with Sphingomonas Z392 in broiler chickens may modulate intestinal histology and microbiota composition, which could potentially enhance digestive tract function, feed utilization, and overall production performance [2, 4]. However, the potential mechanism for these findings remains to be fully elucidated.

To explore the intrinsic mechanism underlying the ammonia reduction capacity of Sphingomonas Z392, further investigations were focused on the key functional genes. Under high-concentration ammonium sulfate stimulation, the key gene for ammonia reduction in this strain was ncd2, encoding nitronate monooxygenase (NMO). This enzyme is a flavin mononucleotide-dependent enzyme that oxidizes propionate trinitrate (P3N) and catalyses the formation of nitrite from nitroalkanes [5, 6]. Overexpression of the ncd2 gene in Bacillus subtilis can completely degrade nitroethane, and the amount of the cofactor flavin mononucleotide (FMN) is crucial with the degradation of nitroalkanes by ncd2-overexpressing strains [6]. Furthermore, the addition of FMN to a cell-free extraction solution can significantly improve the degradation efficiency of nitroalkanes by 2–3 times [5, 7]. Nitronate monooxygenase has been detected in various microorganisms, such as Aspergillus niger, Pseudomonas aeruginosa, Bacillus subtilis, and Metarhizium etc. [5].

In our prior research, broiler chickens supplemented with ncd2-overexpressing Sphingomonas Z392 in drinking water showed the change of nitrogenous compound profiles in manure (reduced total nitrogen, uric acid and ammonium nitrogen; increased nitrate nitrogen) and reduction of ammonia concentrations in chicken coops, indicating that the ammonia emission reduction capacity was enhanced [6]. Notably, the use of the strain to ensure meat quality and animal safety has not yet been systematically addressed for ncd2-overexpressing Sphingomonas Z392.

To address these critical knowledge gaps, the present study aims to: (1) clarify the effects of ncd2-overexpressing Sphingomonas Z392 on broiler disease resistance, growth performance, and meat quality; and (2) comprehensively evaluate the biosafety of this genetically modified strain for agricultural application. This work will provide a scientific basis for the development of safe and effective microbial-based strategies to improve poultry production efficiency and environmental sustainability.

Materials and methods

Test strains

The test strains included Sphingomonas sp. Z392 (GenBank accession number: MN108136), the ncd2-knockout Sphingomonas sp. Z392 strain, and the ncd2-overexpressing Sphingomonas sp. Z392 strain (CGMCC No. 33862).

Experimental design and animal breeding management

In this study, 1200 one-day-old Kebao broiler chickens, weighing 42 ± 2 g, were provided by Henan Longhua Animal Husbandry Co., Ltd. These broiler chickens were randomly divided into four groups: control group (CK), wild-type strain group (WT), ncd2 knockout strain group (KO), and ncd2-overexpressing strain group (OV). Each group consisted of 300 chickens, with 100 chickens per biological replicate. The chickens of a replicate were fed within one chicken fence by net-rearing. The feeding and management of the four groups of broiler chickens were carried out according to the Kebao Broiler Feeding and Management Manual.

The feeding management of Kebao broilers was divided into three stages: 1–18 d, 19–33 d, and 34–42 d. The nutritional composition of the feed in each stage was detailed in Table 1.

In this study, animal experiments were conducted in an independent area, finally the experimental site, animal carcasses, and faeces were treated harmlessly.

Observation of microscopic and ultramicroscopic structure of intestinal tissue

At 42 d, 6 broiler chickens were randomly selected from each group, anaesthetized by intraperitoneal injection of pentobarbital sodium (70 mg/kg body weight), and then killed by cervical dislocation.

After being fixed in formalin, they were embedded in paraffin, sliced, stained with hematoxylin–eosin (H&E), and sealed. Changes in the intestinal microstructure were observed with a microscope (Nikon Eclipse E100, Nikon, Tokyo, Japan) and photographed. Case Viewer 2.4 browsing software was used for observation and analyses. The integrity and lesions of tissues such as the epithelium, villi, columnar cells, goblet cells, and submu cosal glands were observed. In addition, the villus height and crypt depth of the small intestine were measured, and the ratio of the villus height to crypt depth was calculated.

After fixing with electron microscope fixative, the resin was infiltrated and embedded at room temperature (15–25 ℃), polymerized, thinly sliced, stained, and analyzed using transmission electron microscopy (TEM) (HT7800, Hitachi Production Co., Ltd., Tokyo, Japan) for image analysis. The focus is on observing the number and pathological changes of organelles such as mitochondria, as well as ribosomes in lysosomes, endoplasmic reticulum, and intestinal epithelial cells.

Incidence of digestive tract diseases

The number of chickens with obvious symptoms of digestive tract diseases in each group and the course of the disease were determined. Symptoms of digestive tract diseases were counted on the basis of the occurrence of feed-containing faeces and loose faeces.

Changes in the body weights of the broilers in each group

At 1 d, six chickens were randomly selected from each of the 4 groups, and their body weights were measured with an electronic scale and recorded. The above procedure was repeated at days 7, 14, 21, 28, 35 and 42 [8].

Determination of pH changes

For each group, six pieces of pectoral and leg muscles were selected. The pH values at three sites in each sample were measured via a pH meter at 45 min and 24 h after slaughter. The average pH values at two time points were calculated, and the difference between two measured pH values was obtained to statistically determine the change in pH [9].

Drip loss

Six pieces of pectoral and leg muscles were selected from each group, and an approximately 10 g sample was cut and weighed from each muscle. After incubation at 4 ℃ for 24 h, the treated samples were weighed again to calculate the degree of drip loss [10].

Determination of the shear force

The muscles were stored at 4 ℃ for 24 h, heated at 80 ℃ in a constant-temperature water bath until the core temperature reached 74 ℃, and immediately removed and cooled to room temperature. A circular core sampler with a diameter of 12.7 mm was used to drill and cut parallel to the muscle fibres, yielding a meat column measuring 1 cm × 1 cm × 5 cm [11]. The shear probe was a Rapid TA Texture Analyser (Tengba Instrument Technology Co., Ltd., Shanghai, China), with a shear speed of 1 mm/s, a target mode set at a distance of 30 mm, a trigger force of 20 gf, and a shear count of 3 times.

Determination of meat color

Six pieces of pectoral and leg muscles were selected from each group. A colorimeter was used to determine the lightness, redness, and yellowness of the pectoral and leg muscles, namely, L* (lightness), a* (green–red), and b* (blue–yellow) [11].

Data and statistical analysis

The data were analysed for statistical significance via SPSS 19.0 software. The experimental data are presented as the mean ± standard deviation (SD) of six independent biological replicates of each tissue. Analysis of variance (ANOVA) was used to test the difference between the mean values of the performed experiments. Tukey’s test was used to compare groups pairwise. P values < 0.05 were considered to indicate statistical significance. Figures were generated via GraphPad Prism 8 (GraphPad Software, USA). The statistics of mitochondrial area and quantity were conducted using AutoCAD 2025 (Autodesk, USA).

Results and analysis

Changes in intestinal microstructure

After paraffin section and HE staining, the intestinal histological structure was observed under a binocular biological microscope, and the results are shown in Figure S1-S3. The duodenal villi of the CK broiler chickens showed atrophy and shortening, and their inherent morphology was lost. White blood cell infiltration could be seen in the submucosal layer, with the most obvious infiltration in the duodenum and jejunum. In contrast, the small intestinal villi of the broiler chickens in other groups maintained their intact morphological structure. In addition, there were more goblet cells in the small intestinal epithelial cells of the WT and OV broiler chickens. No abnormal changes were observed in the histological structure of the large intestine.

Changes in the ratio of small intestinal villus height to crypt depth

After the villus height (VH) and crypt depth (CD) of small intestine were measured via Case Viewer 2.4 browsing software, the VH/CD in each segment of the small intestine was calculated. The results are shown in Fig. 1 and Table S1.

Comparison of meat quality among the broilers in each group

As shown in Fig. 7 and Table S7-S8, there were no significant differences in the pH values or shear forces of the breast or leg muscles among the groups (p > 0.05). The drip loss of the breast and leg muscles in the three experimental groups was significantly lower than that in the CK group (p < 0.05), and the OV group was the lowest—51.26% lower in chest muscles and 49.15% lower in leg muscles. The effects of the ncd2-overexpressing Sphingomonas sp. Z392 strain on the L* value and a* value of muscles differed between the breast and leg muscles of broilers. The b* values of the breast and leg muscles in the three experimental groups were greater than those in the CK group, with the OV group exhibiting the highest b* values.

Discussion

Effects of the ncd2-overexpressing Sphingomonas sp. Z392 strain on the digestive tract of chickens

The gastrointestinal tract, as an important digestive and absorptive organ in animals, is not only involved in the transformation and utilization of nutrients but also serves as the first line of defense against pathogen invasion [12]. The health of the gastrointestinal tract is crucial for the growth and production performance of animals [13]. Pathogens damage the body by producing harmful toxins and metabolites, leading to the reduction in production performance and various diseases in animals [14, 15]. Therefore, more attention should be paid to gastrointestinal health.

The intestinal villi are the main sites of nutrient absorption. The height, integrity, and cell quantity of intestinal villi are significantly related to the digestive and absorptive functions of the small intestine [16]. Studies have confirmed that the addition of probiotics to the diet can protect the gastrointestinal tract from stimulation caused by excessive feeding, increase the villus height (VH) and the ratio of the villus height to the crypt depth (VH/CD) of broilers, and reduce the crypt depth (CD) [17–19].

The results of this study revealed that the ncd2-overexpressing Sphingomonas sp. Z392 strain promoted the structural integrity of small intestine. It increased the number of goblet cells, lysosomes and mitochondria but reduced the degree of mitochondrial swelling. It also increased the VH/CD ratio in various segments of the small intestine. These findings indicate that the ncd2-overexpressing Sphingomonas sp. Z392 strain improved the intestinal histological structure. This is attributed mainly to the fact that the ncd2 gene promotes the production of nitro-monooxygenase, which converts more nitrogen into nitrate nitrogen and reduces the generation of ammonium nitrogen, thereby alleviates intestinal tissue damage [4, 6, 20, 21]. In this way, the ncd2-overexpressing Sphingomonas sp. Z392 strain maintains the intestinal health, promotes the nutrient absorption, reduces the incidence and duration of digestive tract diseases in broilers, and ultimately increases the body weight of broilers.

Effects of the ncd2-overexpressing Sphingomonas sp. Z392 strain on chicken meat quality

The rates of pH decline, drip loss, shear force, and color parameters are important indicators of meat quality [11, 22, 23]. After chickens are slaughtered, anaerobic metabolism in muscles results in the production of lactic acid, leading to a decrease in pH, and the rate of this decline is related to the degree of muscle lipid peroxidation. The shear force reflects muscle hardness, whereas drip loss indirectly reflects muscle water-holding capacity. The L* value (lightness), a* value (redness), and b* value (yellowness) of meat color are intuitive criteria for consumers to judge meat quality [23–25]. In terms of water-holding capacity, the ncd2-overexpressing Sphingomonas sp. Z392 strain could reduce drip loss in broiler muscles and improve the water retention rate. In terms of meat color, the b* values of the chicken meat in the three experimental groups increased significantly. This might be related to the deposition of carotenoid-like substances produced by bacterial metabolism or changes in muscle fatty acid composition [23]. However, further determination of the contents of different fatty acids and carotenoids in the muscle is needed. The effects on the L* and a* values of the breast and leg muscles were showed opposite results, which may be attributed to differences in fibre types and metabolic characteristics between the breast and leg muscles. The ncd2-overexpressing Sphingomonas sp. Z392 strain might exert its effects by regulating the metabolism of the gut-muscle axis [26–28]. The short-chain fatty acids (such as butyric acid) produced by fermentation might activate the muscle AMP-activated protein kinase pathway and improve energy metabolism. The amino acids generated through metabolism might promote muscle protein synthesis. Moreover, secreted antioxidant enzymes might reduce the level of muscle oxidative stress in muscle and protect muscle cell membranes [4, 23, 29]. In summary, the ncd2-overexpressing Sphingomonas sp. Z392 strain could improve the quality of chicken meat.

The ncd2-overexpressing Sphingomonas sp. Z392 strain might increase mitochondrial abundance in intestinal epithelial cells, alleviate mitochondrial swelling, and potentially regulate the activity of oxidative phosphorylation complex I (e.g., gene expression of NDUFB10 and NDUFA11) as well as the transcription of nuclear-encoded mitochondrial protein (NuMP) genes. This gut-mitochondria interaction might adapt energy metabolism to tissue metabolic demands through conserved oxidative phosphorylation and thermogenesis pathways, thereby optimizing nutrient absorption and energy utilization and ultimately enhancing broiler growth performance and meat quality [30–34]. We have noted that our observations of improved mitochondrial morphology and inferred enhancement of metabolic state are primarily descriptive. However, definitive mechanistic links through quantitative measures of mitochondrial respiration, oxidative stress markers, or the expression of key bioenergetic enzymes remain to be established in future studies.

Conclusions

The ncd2-overexpressing Sphingomonas sp. Z392 strain can effectively improve the intestinal health of broilers, enhance their growth performance, and improve meat quality. Its effects are better than those of the wild-type strain and the knockout strain. Therefore, the ncd2-overexpressing Sphingomonas sp. Z392 strain could be used safely for the feeding and management of broilers.

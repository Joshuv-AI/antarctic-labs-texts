A variation on “compositeness” (including higher partial waves)
Peter C. Bruns

Abstract
The “spatial interpretation of compositeness”, presented and discussed in Bruns:2019xgo ; Bruns:2022hmb in the context of non-relativistic potential scattering, is extended to higher partial waves. A particular set of basis states is used to arrive at a slightly different perspective on the derivation and interpretation of “compositeness” usually given in the literature.



I
Introduction

The concept of compositeness has become widely used in the past two decades to assess the “nature” of a given hadronic state, i. e. to quantify the extent to which it can be considered a “hadronic molecule” composed of other hadrons, as opposed to a state that can be directly explained within a quark model (quark-antiquark or three-quark states). This type of analysis can be traced back to Weinberg’s study of the case of the deuteron
Weinberg:1965zz
1

1

See also Sec. 10.7 of
Weinberg:1995mt
for a brief textbook presentation by the same author.
. Basically, the compositeness is given by
1-Z
, where
Z
is the field renormalization constant for the hadronic state in question, and is sometimes interpreted as “the probability of finding the bare [“elementary”] state in the bound state” (see e.g.
Sekihara:2014kya
). A state for which
Z=0
is then considered as “fully composite”. Note that we are not talking about compositeness and “elementariness” in an absolute sense here: Only particles appearing in asymptotic states coupling to the bound state (or resonance) qualify as possible constituents, whose inner structure is not further resolved.

The methods of studying the compositeness of hadrons have meanwhile been generalized in various directions, and have been applied in many interesting case studies

In

, doubts are expressed that compositeness can be considered as a rigorous notion in quantum field theory. In parts, the arguments for this conclusion resemble the ones G. Chew gave for his postulate of “nuclear democracy”
Chew:1963zza

In
Bruns:2019xgo
;
Bruns:2022hmb
, we have taken a step back, and limited ourselves to the simplest case in which the above-mentioned concepts could make sense, namely, the non-relativistic scattering of a spinless particle by a spherically symmetric, energy-independent finite-range potential. In close analogy to the relativistic case, we could extract the non-relativistic counterpart of the standardly defined compositeness from the residue of the s-wave bound-state pole of the scattering amplitude, and found that it is proportional to
P(r>d)
, the probability to find the particle at a distance
r
from the center of the potential greater than the range of the potential
d
. The factor of proportionality is of a universal form, tends to one when
d\rightarrow 0
, and is responsible for the fact that the extracted compositeness can be greater than one.

The present contribution has two aims: First, to extend the analysis of
Bruns:2019xgo
;
Bruns:2022hmb
to bound states of arbitrary orbital angular momentum
\ell
, and second, to mimick the standard quantum-mechanical derivation of the decomposition into compositeness and elementariness (see e.g.
Weinberg:1965zz
and Sec. 2.1. of
Sekihara:2014kya
) with a different set of basis states, derived from a certain limit of an energy-
in
dependent potential. The mathematical consequences are essentially the same for each choice of basis states, and thus it should become clear how the relation between the compositeness and a quantity like
P(r>d)
can be made plausible.

The set of basis states we use to decompose the bound-state wave functions is explained in Sec.
II
, and applied to a bound state in a finite-range potential
V(r)
in Sec.
III
. The chosen basis states are also quite convenient, though not really necessary, for the computation of the residue of the bound-state pole in the scattering amplitude, from which the compositeness (often denoted as
X
) is usually derived in applications (see e. g. Sec. 3 of
Sekihara:2014kya
).
For
\ell>0
, the relation between the residues of partial-wave amplitudes and the spatial probabilities is, unfortunately, more complicated than in the s-wave case, and is given in Eq. (
24
) below. Note that this relation is still
exact
in the present non-relativistic context, and not an approximation for short ranges or particularly small bound-state energies. Such approximations are, however, used to derive relations between the residues and the scattering lengths and effective ranges, as was already done in
Weinberg:1965zz
for the s-wave case. In Sec.
IV
, we give approximate expressions for the spatial probabilities in terms of the threshold parameters, which can be used to get estimates for the range of the potential, and give an impression of the “size” of the bound state. We tested those relations for the case of a spherical-well potential, and found the results in reasonable agreement with the derived relations. Our conclusions can be found in Sec.
V
.

II
Basis states from a “hard sphere” problem

Let us divide the universe in an inner and an outer part by a “delta-shell” potential
Nussenzveig:1961nc
,

V_{\mathrm{shell}}(r)=\frac{v}{2R}\delta(r-R)\,.

(1)

We are interested here only in the limit where the strength parameter
v\rightarrow\infty
, so that the sphere of radius
R
becomes impenetrable. Wave functions for a particle of mass
\mu
pertaining to this simple case are easily found. In the outer region
r>R
, we have a continuum of states with energies
E>0
, labeled by
k=+\sqrt{2\mu E}
and angular-momentum quantum numbers
\ell,m
:

\langle\vec{r}|k\ell m\rangle=\Phi_{k\ell m}^{>}(\vec{r})=\theta(r-R)i^{\ell}\sqrt{\frac{2\mu k}{\pi}}\left(j_{\ell}(kr)+ikf_{\ell}^{\mathrm{h.s.}}(k)h_{\ell}^{+}(kr)\right)\mathcal{Y}_{\ell m}(\theta,\phi)\,,

(2)

where
f_{\ell}^{\mathrm{h.s.}}(k)
is the partial-wave amplitude for scattering on a hard sphere of radius
R
,

f_{\ell}^{\mathrm{h.s.}}(k)=\frac{i}{2k}\left(\frac{h_{\ell}^{-}(kR)}{h_{\ell}^{+}(kR)}+1\right)\,,

(3)

r:=|\vec{r}|
,
\theta(\cdot)
is the Heaviside step function,
h^{\pm}_{\ell}(\cdot)
are the spherical Hankel functions of the first and second kind (see App.
A
),
j_{\ell}(z)=\frac{1}{2}\left(h_{\ell}^{+}(z)+h_{\ell}^{-}(z)\right)
are the spherical Bessel functions of the first kind, and
\mathcal{Y}_{\ell m}(\theta,\phi)
are the usual spherical harmonics. With the help of the formulae collected in App.
A
, one can verify that the
\Phi_{k\ell m}^{>}(\vec{r})
are orthonormal in the sense

\int d^{3}\vec{r}\,\Phi_{k^{\prime}\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}}^{>\ast}(\vec{r})\Phi_{k\ell m}^{>}(\vec{r})=\delta_{\ell^{\prime}\ell}\delta_{m^{\prime}m}\frac{\mu}{\sqrt{k^{\prime}k}}\delta(k^{\prime}-k)=\delta_{\ell^{\prime}\ell}\delta_{m^{\prime}m}\delta(E^{\prime}-E)\,,

(4)

just like the free wave functions. There is also a discrete set of states, describing particles confined to the inside of the sphere,
r<R
. The corresponding position-space wave functions are

\langle\vec{r}|n\ell m\rangle=\Phi_{n\ell m}^{<}(\vec{r})=\theta(R-r)\mathcal{N}_{n\ell}j_{\ell}\left(\alpha_{n\ell}\frac{r}{R}\right)\mathcal{Y}_{\ell m}(\theta,\phi)\,,\quad\mathcal{N}_{n\ell}=\frac{\sqrt{2/R^{3}}}{|j_{\ell+1}(\alpha_{n\ell})|}\,.

(5)

Here
\alpha_{n\ell}
is the
n
th positive zero of the spherical Bessel function
j_{\ell}
. To compute the associated momentum-space wave functions, we employ the expansion of a plane wave into spherical harmonics,

\langle\vec{q}|\vec{r}\rangle=\frac{e^{-i\vec{q}\cdot\vec{r}}}{(2\pi)^{3/2}}=\frac{4\pi}{(2\pi)^{3/2}}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}(-i)^{\ell}j_{\ell}(|\vec{q}|r)\mathcal{Y}_{\ell m}(\theta_{q},\phi_{q})\mathcal{Y}_{\ell m}^{\ast}(\theta,\phi)\,,

(6)

and the integral of Eq. (
A.10
) to get

\langle\vec{q}|n\ell m\rangle=\mathcal{N}_{n\ell}\frac{4\pi(-i)^{\ell}}{(2\pi)^{3/2}}\frac{\alpha_{n\ell}R}{\left(\frac{\alpha_{n\ell}}{R}\right)^{2}-|\vec{q}|^{2}}j_{\ell+1}(\alpha_{n\ell})j_{\ell}(|\vec{q}|R)\mathcal{Y}_{\ell m}(\theta_{q},\phi_{q})\,.

(7)

We denote the angles in spherical coordinates pertaining to
\vec{r}
by
\theta,\phi
, and those pertaining to
\vec{q}
by
\theta_{q},\phi_{q}
. Note that
\langle\vec{q}|n\ell m\rangle
is not singular as
|\vec{q}|\rightarrow\alpha_{n\ell}/R
, because
\alpha_{n\ell}
is a zero of
j_{\ell}\,
.

For the continuum states,
\langle\vec{q}|k\ell m\rangle
could be computed in a similar fashion, employing the integrals in App.
A
. As the result is a bit lengthy, and we don’t need the explicit result in the following, we shall omit it here. One can use the “closure relations” for the spherical Bessel functions and spherical harmonics,

\displaystyle\frac{2r^{\prime}r}{R^{3}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{j_{\ell}\left(\alpha_{n\ell}\frac{r^{\prime}}{R}\right)j_{\ell}\left(\alpha_{n\ell}\frac{r}{R}\right)}{|j_{\ell+1}(\alpha_{n\ell})|^{2}}=\delta(r^{\prime}-r)\quad\mathrm{for}\,\,0<r,r^{\prime}<R\,,

(8)

\displaystyle\frac{2r^{\prime}r}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}k^{2}dk\,j_{\ell}(kr^{\prime})j_{\ell}(kr)\,\,\,=\,\delta(r^{\prime}-r)\quad\mathrm{for}\,\,0<r,r^{\prime}\,,

(9)

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}\mathcal{Y}_{\ell m}(\theta^{\prime},\phi^{\prime})\mathcal{Y}_{\ell m}^{\ast}(\theta,\phi)=\delta(\cos\theta-\cos\theta^{\prime})\delta(\phi-\phi^{\prime})\,,

(10)

together with the integrals in App.
A
, to verify

\displaystyle\sum_{n\ell m}\Phi_{n\ell m}^{<}(\vec{r^{\prime}})\Phi_{n\ell m}^{<\ast}(\vec{r})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\theta(R-r)\delta^{3}(\vec{r}-\vec{r}\,^{\prime})\,,

(11)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}dE\,\sum_{\ell m}\Phi_{k\ell m}^{>}(\vec{r^{\prime}})\Phi_{k\ell m}^{>\ast}(\vec{r})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\theta(r-R)\delta^{3}(\vec{r}-\vec{r}\,^{\prime})\,.

(12)

In this sense, the union of the discrete and the continuous states,
|n\ell m\rangle
and
|k\ell m\rangle
, forms a complete set of orthonormal states.

III
Determination of the residues of bound-state poles

Let us now forget about the “hard sphere”, and consider a more general, spherically symmetric finite-range potential
V(r)
. Assume that it has a bound state
|B\rangle
of energy
E_{B}<0
, and angular-momentum quantum numbers
\ell,m
,
|B\rangle\equiv|E_{B}\ell m\rangle
. To this bound state corresponds a pole term of the form

\mathcal{T}(\vec{q^{\prime}},\vec{q};E)=\frac{\langle\vec{q^{\prime}}|\hat{V}|B\rangle\langle B|\hat{V}|\vec{q}\rangle}{E-E_{B}}+\ldots

(13)

in the scattering amplitude
\mathcal{T}
for the considered potential, with incoming and outgoing momenta
\vec{q}
and
\vec{q^{\prime}}
, respectively, and energy
E=k^{2}/(2\mu)
(see e.g. Sec. II of
Bruns:2019xgo
). Here
\hat{V}
is the operator associated with
V(r)
. On-shell partial-wave scattering amplitudes
f_{\ell}(E)
are derived from
\mathcal{T}(\vec{q^{\prime}},\vec{q};E)
via

\mathcal{T}_{\ell}(E):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{-1}^{1}dz\,\mathcal{P}_{\ell}(z)\,\mathrm{lim}_{|\vec{q}\,^{\prime}|,|\vec{q}|\rightarrow k}\mathcal{T}(\vec{q}\,^{\prime},\vec{q};E)\,,\qquad f_{\ell}(E):=-(2\pi)^{2}\mu\mathcal{T}_{\ell}(E)\,.

(14)

Here
z
is the cosine of the scattering angle (the angle between
\vec{q}\,^{\prime}
and
\vec{q}
), and
\mathcal{P}_{\ell}(z)
denote the usual Legendre polynomials. The conventionally normalized partial-wave amplitudes
f_{\ell}(E)
obey the unitarity requirement
\mathrm{Im}\,(f_{\ell}(E))^{-1}=-k\,
for real
E>0
, and can conveniently be written as

f_{\ell}(E)=[K_{\ell}^{-1}(E)-ik]^{-1}\,,

(15)

where
K_{\ell}(E)
is real for real energies
E
. We see that, to find the residue of the bound-state pole in
f_{\ell}
, we have to consider the matrix elements

\langle\vec{q}|\hat{V}|B\rangle=\left(E_{B}-\frac{q^{2}}{2\mu}\right)\langle\vec{q}|B\rangle\,,\qquad q:=\sqrt{(\vec{q}\cdot\vec{q})}\,,

(16)

in the limit where
q\rightarrow k\rightarrow i\kappa_{B}\equiv i\sqrt{-2\mu E_{B}}\,
,
\kappa_{B}>0\,
. This means we have to extract the residue of the pole of
\langle\vec{q}|B\rangle
at
q^{2}=2\mu E_{B}
(we will soon see that
\langle\vec{q}|B\rangle
indeed has such a pole). To do this, we expand
|B\rangle
in the set of states considered in Sec.
II
:

|B\rangle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\langle n\ell m|B\rangle|n\ell m\rangle+\int_{0}^{\infty}dE\,\langle k\ell m|B\rangle|k\ell m\rangle\,.

(17)

In the usual manner of speaking, one could call
\sum_{n}|\langle n\ell m|B\rangle|^{2}
“the probability to find a state confined to a sphere of radius
R
in the bound state”, etc. However, it is clear from the construction in Sec.
II
that this number is just the probability to find the bound particle at a distance
r\leq R
from the center of the potential. Clearly,

1=\langle B|B\rangle=\sum_{n}|\langle n\ell m|B\rangle|^{2}+\int_{0}^{\infty}dE\,|\langle k\ell m|B\rangle|^{2}\,.

(18)

Let the finite range of the potential
V(r)
be
d
. In the following, we will always take the number
R
associated with the “hard sphere” states to satisfy
R>d
.

Using again Eqs. (
6
), (
8
) and (
A.10
), we can compute the sum (for fixed
\ell,m
,
|m|\leq\ell
)

\displaystyle\,\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\langle\vec{q}|n\ell m\rangle\langle n\ell m|\vec{q}\rangle

(19)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\frac{(4\pi)^{2}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{R^{3}}{2}\left((j_{\ell}(qR))^{2}+(j_{\ell+1}(qR))^{2}-\frac{2\ell+1}{qR}j_{\ell}(qR)j_{\ell+1}(qR)\right)\left|\mathcal{Y}_{\ell m}(\theta_{q},\phi_{q})\right|^{2}.

Since also
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\langle B|n\ell m\rangle\langle n\ell m|B\rangle\leq 1
, we have (by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality for series)

\displaystyle\,\left|\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\langle\vec{q}|n\ell m\rangle\langle n\ell m|B\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,\frac{4\pi}{(2\pi)^{3/2}}\sqrt{\frac{R^{3}}{2}}\left((j_{\ell}(qR))^{2}+(j_{\ell+1}(qR))^{2}-\frac{2\ell+1}{qR}j_{\ell}(qR)j_{\ell+1}(qR)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left|\mathcal{Y}_{\ell m}(\theta_{q},\phi_{q})\right|\,,

which is clearly bounded for any fixed
R>0
when
q^{2}\rightarrow 2\mu E_{B}
, and therefore the pole in
\langle\vec{q}|B\rangle
can only be produced by the long-range part,
\int_{0}^{\infty}dE\,\langle\vec{q}|k\ell m\rangle\langle k\ell m|B\rangle
.
This can be computed explicitly, because we chose
R>d
, and the bound-state wave functions for
r>d
are of the universal form (with some choice of phase)

\langle\vec{r}|B\rangle\bigl|_{r>d}=i^{\ell+2}\kappa_{B}\mathcal{N}_{B}h_{\ell}^{+}(i\kappa_{B}r)\mathcal{Y}_{\ell m}(\theta,\phi)\quad\overset{r\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}\quad\mathcal{N}_{B}\frac{e^{-\kappa_{B}r}}{r}\mathcal{Y}_{\ell m}(\theta,\phi)\,,

(20)

where the normalization constant
\mathcal{N}_{B}
depends on the potential
V
(as does
\kappa_{B}
, of course)
3
3

3

We do not add a label
\ell m
to quantities like
\kappa_{B}
,
\mathcal{N}_{B}
or
P(r>R)
for better readability.
.

We can use the completeness of the
\Phi_{k\ell m}^{>}
in the “outer region”
r>R
, Eq. (
12
), in the form

\int_{0}^{\infty}dE\,\langle\vec{r}\,^{\prime}|k\ell m\rangle\langle k\ell m|\vec{r}\rangle=\theta(r^{\prime}-R)\theta(r-R)\frac{1}{r^{\prime}r}\delta(r^{\prime}-r)\mathcal{Y}_{\ell m}(\theta^{\prime},\phi^{\prime})\mathcal{Y}_{\ell m}^{\ast}(\theta,\phi)\,,

to obtain, via Eq. (
A.7
),

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}dE\,\langle\vec{q}|k\ell m\rangle\langle k\ell m|B\rangle=-\frac{4\pi}{(2\pi)^{3/2}}\mathcal{Y}_{\ell m}(\theta_{q},\phi_{q})\kappa_{B}\mathcal{N}_{B}\int_{R}^{\infty}r^{2}dr\,j_{\ell}(qr)h_{\ell}^{+}(i\kappa_{B}r)

(21)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{4\pi}{(2\pi)^{3/2}}\mathcal{Y}_{\ell m}(\theta_{q},\phi_{q})\left(\frac{\kappa_{B}\mathcal{N}_{B}R^{2}}{q^{2}+\kappa_{B}^{2}}\right)\left(qh_{\ell}^{+}(i\kappa_{B}R)j_{\ell+1}(qR)-i\kappa_{B}j_{\ell}(qR)h_{\ell+1}^{+}(i\kappa_{B}R)\right)\,.

The complex conjugate of the last expression,
\int_{0}^{\infty}dE\,\langle B|k\ell m\rangle\langle k\ell m|\vec{q}\rangle
, picks up an additional factor
(-1)^{\ell}
because
(h_{\ell}^{+}(i\kappa_{B}R))^{\ast}=(-1)^{\ell}h_{\ell}^{+}(i\kappa_{B}R)
.
Using the identity (
A.6
), this yields

\int_{0}^{\infty}dE\,\langle\vec{q}|k\ell m\rangle\langle k\ell m|B\rangle=\mathcal{N}_{B}\frac{4\pi}{(2\pi)^{3/2}}\mathcal{Y}_{\ell m}(\theta_{q},\phi_{q})\left(\frac{1}{q^{2}+\kappa_{B}^{2}}+\dots\right)\,,

(22)

where the dots indicate terms which are regular as
q\rightarrow i\kappa_{B}
. All in all we find

\mathrm{lim}_{q\rightarrow i\kappa_{B}}\left(E_{B}-\frac{q^{2}}{2\mu}\right)\langle\vec{q}|B\rangle=-\frac{\mathcal{N}_{B}}{2\mu}\frac{4\pi}{(2\pi)^{3/2}}\mathcal{Y}_{\ell m}(\theta_{q},\phi_{q})\,.

(23)

For the on-shell partial-wave amplitudes (see Eq. (
14
)), seen as functions of the energy
E
, this means that the residue at the bound-state pole on the negative energy axis is given by

\displaystyle\mathrm{Res}_{E_{B}}f_{\ell}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(-1)^{\ell}\frac{\mathcal{N}_{B}^{2}}{2\mu}\,,\quad\mathcal{C}_{B}^{\ell}:=-\frac{\mu}{\kappa_{B}}(-1)^{\ell}\mathrm{Res}_{E_{B}}f_{\ell}\,,

(24)

\displaystyle P(r>R)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\kappa_{B}R)^{3}\biggl(|h_{\ell+1}^{+}(i\kappa_{B}R)|^{2}-|h_{\ell}^{+}(i\kappa_{B}R)|^{2}-\frac{2\ell+1}{\kappa_{B}R}|h_{\ell}^{+}(i\kappa_{B}R)h_{\ell+1}^{+}(i\kappa_{B}R)|\biggr)\mathcal{C}_{B}^{\ell}\,.

For
\ell=0
, this yields

P(r>R)=e^{-2\kappa_{B}R}\mathcal{C}_{B}^{0}=:p_{0}(\kappa_{B}R)\mathcal{C}_{B}^{0}\,,

(25)

in agreement with
Bruns:2019xgo
, while for
\ell>0
, we write (
24
) as

\mathcal{C}_{B}^{\ell}=(\kappa_{B}R)^{2\ell-1}P(r>R)\,/\,p_{\ell}(\kappa_{B}R)\,.

(26)

For example, for the cases
\ell=1
to
\ell=3
,

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p_{3}(\kappa R)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-2\kappa R}(90+180\kappa R+150(\kappa R)^{2}+60(\kappa R)^{3}+12(\kappa R)^{4}+(\kappa R)^{5})\,.

IV
Threshold parameters

Considering the function
k^{2\ell}(K_{\ell}(E))^{-1}
, and comparing the expansions around
E=0
and a small
E_{B}<0
, respectively,

\displaystyle k^{2\ell}(K_{\ell}(E))^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{a_{\ell}}+\mu Er_{\ell}+\ldots\,,

(27)

\displaystyle k^{2\ell}(K_{\ell}(E))^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(i\kappa_{B})^{2\ell}(K_{\ell}(E_{B}))^{-1}+(E-E_{B})\frac{dk^{2\ell}K_{\ell}^{-1}}{dE}\biggr|_{E_{B}}+\ldots\,,

(28)

and using that
(K_{\ell}(E_{B}))^{-1}+\kappa_{B}=0
and (from the computation of the residue of
f_{\ell}(E)
)

\frac{dK_{\ell}^{-1}}{dE}\biggr|_{E_{B}}=\frac{\mu}{\kappa_{B}}\left(1-\frac{(-1)^{\ell}}{\mathcal{C}_{B}^{\ell}}\right)\,,

(29)

we expect, when
\kappa_{B}
is sufficiently small,

\frac{1}{a_{\ell}}\approx(-1)^{\ell}\kappa_{B}^{2\ell+1}+E_{B}\frac{dk^{2\ell}K_{\ell}^{-1}}{dE}\biggr|_{E_{B}}\,,\qquad\mu r_{\ell}\approx\frac{dk^{2\ell}K_{\ell}^{-1}}{dE}\biggr|_{E_{B}}\,,

as long as the higher terms in the effective-range expansion (
27
) do not blow up as
\kappa_{B}\rightarrow 0
compared to the terms involving
a_{\ell}
and
r_{\ell}
, so that the first two terms dominate this expansion. This assumption leads us to the approximations

\kappa_{B}a_{\ell}\approx\frac{2\mathcal{C}_{B}^{\ell}}{\kappa_{B}^{2\ell}(1+(-1)^{\ell}\mathcal{C}_{B}^{\ell}(1-2\ell))}\,,\qquad\kappa_{B}r_{\ell}\approx-\kappa_{B}^{2\ell}\left(\frac{1}{\mathcal{C}_{B}^{\ell}}-(-1)^{\ell}(2\ell+1)\right)\,.

(30)

For
\ell=0
, we get Weinberg’s relations
Weinberg:1965zz
, identifying
\mathcal{C}_{B}^{0}=1-Z=:X
, while for
\ell>0
, we use Eq. (
26
) to write

\displaystyle\kappa_{B}^{2}a_{\ell}

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\frac{2R^{2\ell-1}P(r>R)}{p_{\ell}(\kappa_{B}R)-(2\ell-1)(-1)^{\ell}(\kappa_{B}R)^{2\ell-1}P(r>R)}\,,

\displaystyle r_{\ell}

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{R^{2\ell-1}}\left(\frac{p_{\ell}(\kappa_{B}R)}{P(r>R)}-(-1)^{\ell}(2\ell+1)(\kappa_{B}R)^{2\ell-1}\right)\,,\quad\mathrm{or}\quad

\displaystyle P(r>R)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\frac{\kappa_{B}^{2}a_{\ell}p_{\ell}(\kappa_{B}R)}{R^{2\ell-1}\left(2+(2\ell-1)(-1)^{\ell}\kappa_{B}^{2\ell+1}a_{\ell}\right)}=:P_{a}(r>R)\,,

(31)

\displaystyle P(r>R)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\frac{p_{\ell}(\kappa_{B}R)}{R^{2\ell-1}\left((2\ell+1)(-1)^{\ell}\kappa_{B}^{2\ell-1}-r_{\ell}\right)}\,\,\,=:P_{r}(r>R)\,.

(32)

V
Conclusions

In Eq. (
18
), the states
|n\ell m\rangle
play the role of the “elementary” states in the usual derivations of compositeness, while the role of the “free two-particle states” is played by the continuum of hard-sphere scattering states
|k\ell m\rangle
. It is clear from (
5
), (
24
) and the computation of the residues in Sec.
III
that
\mathrm{Res}_{E_{B}}f_{\ell}
and
\mathcal{C}^{\ell}_{B}
tend to zero when the bound state becomes confined to a small region
r\leq d
(compare also the numerical examples in the tables in
Bruns:2022hmb
).

Under the assumption that the first two terms in the effective-range expansion still dominate when
\kappa_{B}\rightarrow 0
, we can use the
exact
relations in Eq. (
24
) to get approximate relations, valid for sufficiently small
\kappa_{B}
, that connect the threshold parameters with the spatial probability distribution of the bound state. We found that those relations are reasonably well satisfied for a spherical-well potential when
\kappa_{B}=0.1\,\mu
and
R>d=5\,\mu^{-1}
. Moreover, in the case
\ell=0
, they reduce to the relations already given in
Weinberg:1965zz
if one identifies our
\mathcal{C}_{B}^{0}
, as derived from the bound-state residue of the s-wave scattering amplitude, with
X:=1-Z
.

Obviously, the present contribution is limited to the simple case of nonrelativistic potential scattering, and relativistic dynamics, energy-dependent potentials, coupled channels and resonances must be studied with more elaborate methods.

Appendix A
Spherical Hankel functions

The spherical Hankel functions
h_{\ell}^{\pm}(z)
can be represented by the series

\displaystyle h_{\ell}^{+}(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-i)\frac{e^{i(z-\ell\frac{\pi}{2})}}{z}\sum_{k=0}^{\ell}\frac{(+i)^{k}a_{k}(\ell)}{z^{k}}\,,\quad h_{\ell}^{-}(z)=(+i)\frac{e^{-i(z-\ell\frac{\pi}{2})}}{z}\sum_{k=0}^{\ell}\frac{(-i)^{k}a_{k}(\ell)}{z^{k}}\,,

(A.1)

\displaystyle a_{0\leq k\leq\ell}(\ell)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{k}k!}\frac{\Gamma(\ell+1+k)}{\Gamma(\ell+1-k)}\,,\quad a_{k>\ell}(\ell)=0\,.

(A.2)

They are related to the spherical Bessel functions of the first and second kind,
j_{\ell}(z)
and
n_{\ell}(z)
, by
h_{\ell}^{\pm}=j_{\ell}\pm in_{\ell}\,
. We have the recursion relation

h^{\pm}_{\ell+1}(z)=\frac{2\ell+1}{z}h^{\pm}_{\ell}(z)-h^{\pm}_{\ell-1}(z)\,,

(A.3)

the expression for the derivative,

(h_{\ell}^{\pm})^{\prime}(z)=(\ell/z)h^{\pm}_{\ell}(z)-h^{\pm}_{\ell+1}(z)\,,

(A.4)

the asymptotic behavior

h_{\ell}^{\pm}(z)\,\overset{z\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}\,(\mp i)^{\ell+1}\frac{e^{\pm iz}}{z}\,,

(A.5)

and the identity

h_{\ell}^{+}(z)h_{\ell+1}^{-}(z)-h_{\ell}^{-}(z)h_{\ell+1}^{+}(z)=\frac{2i}{z^{2}}\,.

(A.6)

The following integrals are very useful for the investigation in this work:

\displaystyle\int_{R}^{\infty}dr\,r^{2}h_{\ell}^{+}(i\kappa^{\prime}r)h_{\ell}^{+}(i\kappa r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\kappa\kappa^{\prime}R^{3}}{\kappa^{2}-\kappa^{\prime 2}}\left(\frac{1}{i\kappa R}h_{\ell}^{+}(i\kappa R)h_{\ell+1}^{+}(i\kappa^{\prime}R)-\frac{1}{i\kappa^{\prime}R}h_{\ell}^{+}(i\kappa^{\prime}R)h_{\ell+1}^{+}(i\kappa R)\right)\,,

\displaystyle\int_{R}^{\infty}dr\,r^{2}h_{\ell}^{\pm}(\alpha r)h_{\ell}^{+}(i\kappa r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{i\alpha\kappa R^{3}}{\alpha^{2}+\kappa^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{\alpha R}h_{\ell}^{\pm}(\alpha R)h_{\ell+1}^{+}(i\kappa R)-\frac{1}{i\kappa R}h_{\ell}^{+}(i\kappa R)h_{\ell+1}^{\pm}(\alpha R)\right)\,,

(A.7)

where
\kappa^{\prime},\,\kappa>0
,
R>0,\,\alpha>0
. In the limit
\kappa^{\prime}\rightarrow\kappa
, the first integral yields

\int_{R}^{\infty}dr\,r^{2}|h_{\ell}^{+}(i\kappa r)|^{2}=\frac{R^{3}}{2}\left(|h_{\ell+1}^{+}(i\kappa R)|^{2}-|h_{\ell}^{+}(i\kappa R)|^{2}-\frac{2\ell+1}{\kappa R}|h_{\ell}^{+}(i\kappa R)h_{\ell+1}^{+}(i\kappa R)|\right)\,,

(A.8)

using Eq. (
A.4
) . Note that
h_{\ell}^{+}(i\kappa R)=-(-i)^{\ell}|h_{\ell}^{+}(i\kappa R)|\,
. For small
\kappa R
, this behaves as

\displaystyle\int_{R}^{\infty}dr\,r^{2}|h_{\ell}^{+}(i\kappa r)|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{R^{3}}{2}\frac{1}{(\kappa R)^{3}}\left(1+\mathcal{O}(\kappa R)\right)\quad\mathrm{for}\quad\ell=0\,,

\displaystyle\int_{R}^{\infty}dr\,r^{2}|h_{\ell}^{+}(i\kappa r)|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{R^{3}}{(\kappa R)^{2\ell+2}}\biggl(\frac{\Gamma(2\ell-1)\Gamma(2\ell)}{4^{\ell-1}(\Gamma(\ell))^{2}}+\mathcal{O}(\kappa R)\biggr)\quad\mathrm{for}\quad\ell>0\,.

We also need some integrals involving spherical Bessel functions of the first kind, e.g.

\displaystyle\int_{R}^{\infty}dr\,r^{2}j_{\ell}(k^{\prime}r)j_{\ell}(kr)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\pi}{2kk^{\prime}}\delta(k^{\prime}-k)-\frac{R^{2}}{k^{2}-k^{\prime 2}}\left(kj_{\ell+1}(kR)j_{\ell}(k^{\prime}R)-k^{\prime}j_{\ell+1}(k^{\prime}R)j_{\ell}(kR)\right)

(A.9)

\displaystyle\rightarrow

\displaystyle\frac{\pi}{2kk^{\prime}}\delta(k^{\prime}-k)\quad\mathrm{for}\,R\rightarrow 0\,,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{R}dr\,r^{2}j_{\ell}(k^{\prime}r)j_{\ell}(kr)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{R^{2}}{k^{2}-k^{\prime 2}}\left(kj_{\ell+1}(kR)j_{\ell}(k^{\prime}R)-k^{\prime}j_{\ell+1}(k^{\prime}R)j_{\ell}(kR)\right)

(A.10)

\displaystyle\rightarrow

\displaystyle\frac{R^{3}}{2}\left((j_{\ell}(kR))^{2}+(j_{\ell+1}(kR))^{2}-\frac{2\ell+1}{kR}j_{\ell}(kR)j_{\ell+1}(kR)\right)\quad\mathrm{for}\,\,k^{\prime}\rightarrow k\,,

for
k^{\prime},\,k>0
and
R>0\,
. Similarly,

\displaystyle\int_{R}^{\infty}dr\,r^{2}n_{\ell}(k^{\prime}r)n_{\ell}(kr)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\pi}{2kk^{\prime}}\delta(k^{\prime}-k)-\frac{R^{2}}{k^{2}-k^{\prime 2}}\left(kn_{\ell+1}(kR)n_{\ell}(k^{\prime}R)-k^{\prime}n_{\ell+1}(k^{\prime}R)n_{\ell}(kR)\right)\,,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{R}dr\,r^{2}n_{\ell}(k^{\prime}r)j_{\ell}(kr)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{R^{2}}{k^{2}-k^{\prime 2}}\left(kj_{\ell+1}(kR)n_{\ell}(k^{\prime}R)-k^{\prime}n_{\ell+1}(k^{\prime}R)j_{\ell}(kR)\right)-\frac{\frac{k^{\ell}}{k^{\prime\ell+1}}}{k^{2}-k^{\prime 2}}\,,

\displaystyle\int_{R}^{\infty}dr\,r^{2}n_{\ell}(k^{\prime}r)j_{\ell}(kr)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2kk^{\prime}}\frac{1}{k-k^{\prime}}-\frac{R^{2}}{k^{2}-k^{\prime 2}}\left(kj_{\ell+1}(kR)n_{\ell}(k^{\prime}R)-k^{\prime}n_{\ell+1}(k^{\prime}R)j_{\ell}(kR)\right)

(A.11)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2kk^{\prime}}\mathcal{P}\left(\frac{1}{k-k^{\prime}}\right)\,,

where
\mathcal{P}(\cdot)
denotes the “principal value” distribution,

\int_{-b}^{c}dx\,\mathcal{P}\left(\frac{1}{x}\right)f(x)=\mathrm{lim}_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\left(\int_{-b}^{-\varepsilon}dx\,\frac{f(x)}{x}+\int_{\varepsilon}^{c}dx\,\frac{f(x)}{x}\right)\,.

Note that the first line in the last Eq. (
A.11
) is non-singular for
k^{\prime}\rightarrow k
, as can be seen with the help of (
A.6
). We also dropped terms
\sim\delta(k^{\prime}+k)
, since we always assume
k^{\prime},k>0
here.

Appendix B
Spherical well

Here the potential is taken as
V(r)=V_{0}\theta(d-r)
, so that the expansion of the scattering amplitude in the coupling strength
V_{0}<0
starts as

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}(\vec{q}\,^{\prime},\vec{q};E)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle V(\vec{q}\,^{\prime},\vec{q})+\ldots\,,\quad\mathrm{where}

(B.1)

\displaystyle V(\vec{q}\,^{\prime},\vec{q})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\frac{d^{3}r}{(2\pi)^{3}}e^{-i(\vec{q}\,^{\prime}-\vec{q})\cdot\vec{r}}V_{0}\theta(d-r)

(B.2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{V_{0}}{2\pi^{2}}\right)\frac{\sin(|\vec{q}\,^{\prime}-\vec{q}|d)-|\vec{q}\,^{\prime}-\vec{q}|d\cos(|\vec{q}\,^{\prime}-\vec{q}|d)}{|\vec{q}\,^{\prime}-\vec{q}|^{3}}\,.

The partial-wave amplitudes for scattering on this potential are given by Eq. (
15
), with

K_{\ell}(E)=\frac{1}{k}\,\frac{kj_{\ell}(\xi d)j^{\prime}_{\ell}(kd)-\xi j^{\prime}_{\ell}(\xi d)j_{\ell}(kd)}{kj_{\ell}(\xi d)n^{\prime}_{\ell}(kd)-\xi j^{\prime}_{\ell}(\xi d)n_{\ell}(kd)}\,

(B.3)

(see App.
A
for the spherical Bessel functions), where
k=+\sqrt{2\mu E}
,
\xi:=\sqrt{k^{2}-2\mu V_{0}}
, and the prime denotes differentiation with respect to the argument.

The bound-state wave functions are given by

\langle\vec{r}|E_{B}\ell m\rangle=i^{\ell+2}\kappa_{B}\mathcal{N}_{B}\left(\theta(d-r)h_{\ell}^{+}(i\kappa_{B}d)\frac{j_{\ell}(\xi_{B}r)}{j_{\ell}(\xi_{B}d)}+\theta(r-d)h_{\ell}^{+}(i\kappa_{B}r)\right)\mathcal{Y}_{\ell m}(\theta,\phi)\,,

(B.4)

where
i\kappa_{B}
is a bound-state pole of
f_{\ell}(k)
on the positive imaginary
k
-axis, which satisfies

\xi_{B}\frac{j_{\ell}^{\prime}(\xi_{B}d)}{j_{\ell}(\xi_{B}d)}=i\kappa_{B}\frac{(h^{+}_{\ell})^{\prime}(i\kappa_{B}d)}{h^{+}_{\ell}(i\kappa_{B}d)}\,,\quad\xi_{B}=\sqrt{-\kappa_{B}^{2}-2\mu V_{0}}\,,\quad\mathrm{and}\quad

(B.5)

\mathcal{N}_{B}=\frac{\xi_{B}}{\kappa_{B}d}\sqrt{\frac{2\kappa_{B}}{\kappa_{B}^{2}+\xi_{B}^{2}}}\biggl[\kappa_{B}d\left|h_{\ell+1}^{+}(i\kappa_{B}d)\right|^{2}-(2\ell+1)\left|h_{\ell}^{+}(i\kappa_{B}d)h_{\ell+1}^{+}(i\kappa_{B}d)\right|\biggr]^{-\frac{1}{2}}\,.

(B.6)

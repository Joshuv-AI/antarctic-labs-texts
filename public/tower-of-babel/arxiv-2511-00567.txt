Investigating charm-quark dynamics in the QGP via the charm-hadron elliptic flow in Pb–Pb collisions with ALICE
*

Abstract
In these proceedings, the elliptic flow ( v 2 v_{\rm 2} ) measurement of prompt and non-prompt charm hadrons—originating respectively from the fragmentation of a charm quark and from the decay of hadrons with beauty-quark content—in Pb–Pb collisions at s NN = 5.36 \sqrt{s_{\mathrm{NN}}}=5.36 TeV, using the latest data collected during LHC Run 3 by the ALICE detector, is presented. The analysis is performed at midrapidity ( | y | < 0.8 |y|<0.8 ) and hadronic decay channels are used to reconstruct the signal candidates. The v 2 v_{\rm 2} coefficient is measured via the Scalar Product (SP) technique. The prompt v 2 v_{\rm 2} in semicentral collisions is reported for the D 0 \mathrm{D^{0}} , D + \mathrm{D^{+}} , D s + \mathrm{D_{s}^{+}} mesons and, for the first time, for the Λ c + \Lambda_{\mathrm{c}}^{+} baryon. These results achieve unprecedented precision and low- p T p_{\rm T} reach for the D mesons and highlight the first observation of the splitting of baryon and meson elliptic flow at intermediate p T p_{\rm T} in the charm sector. The v 2 v_{\rm 2} is also measured for prompt D 0 \mathrm{D^{0}} , D + \mathrm{D^{+}} and D s + \mathrm{D_{s}^{+}} in peripheral collisions, offering new insights into the features of the fireball generated in ultrarelativistic heavy-ion collisions. Lower v 2 v_{\rm 2} values are observed for non-prompt D 0 \mathrm{D^{0}} and Λ c + \Lambda_{\mathrm{c}}^{+} hadrons in semicentral collisions, as expected from the larger beauty-quark mass.

1
Introduction

The quark–gluon plasma (QGP) is a color deconfined state of matter predicted by Lattice Quantum Chromodynamics (QCD) calculations to exist at low baryochemical potential and temperatures above 155 MeV.
The study of QCD matter in these extreme conditions relates to the fundamental properties of QCD and to the evolution of the early universe.
The above described conditions can be attained in ultrarelativistic heavy-ion collisions.
The generated fireball, featuring partonic degrees of freedom, expands and cools down until hadronization occurs at
T\approx 155
MeV
[
1
]
, leaving the system in a dense hadronic gas state.
Interactions among hadrons can still occur but, as the system continues to expand and dilute, it reaches the kinetic freeze-out.
In this scenario, heavy-flavor hadrons are unique probes to investigate the QGP properties, as heavy quarks are produced in hard scatterings occurring before the QGP formation, thus experiencing the full medium evolution.
Theoretical descriptions of QGP and hadronization are hindered by the non-perturbative nature of QCD in the relevant regime, making phenomenological transport models the main tool to describe observables.
In this approach, heavy-quark interactions with the QGP are encoded in model parameters, primarily the spatial diffusion coefficient
D_{S}
, which can be constrained by measurements of heavy-flavor hadron production.

The interactions of the quarks in the QGP medium can be studied through the elliptic flow observable.
In non-central ultrarelativistic heavy-ion collisions, the almond-shaped overlap region of the colliding nuclei is understood to be the origin of elliptic flow
[
2
]
.
Due to the initial geometrical anisotropy, the generated fireball expands under non-isotropic pressure gradients that produce a modulation of the particle yields with respect to the azimuthal angle
\varphi
relative to the reaction plane (RP).
The latter is identified as the plane containing the impact parameter of the collision and the beam axis, and its azimuthal angle is indicated by
\Psi_{\text{RP}}
.
The particle yield modulation with respect to
\varphi-\Psi_{\text{RP}}
, with
\varphi
indicating the azimuthal angle of the emitted particle, can be described via a Fourier series expansion

\frac{\text{d}N}{\text{d}\varphi}=\frac{N_{0}}{2\pi}\bigg(1+2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}v_{n}(\cos[n(\varphi-\Psi_{\text{RP}})])\bigg),\qquad v_{n}=\langle\cos[n(\varphi-\Psi_{\text{RP}})]\rangle,

(1)

where
v_{\rm 2}
represents the elliptic flow coefficient, sensitive to the initial geometry of the collision and to the interactions of the charm and beauty quarks in the QGP medium.
It can be argued that the hadronization mechanisms of charm hadrons have direct implication on their
v_{\rm 2}
coefficient.
Indeed, when the hadron is produced in the fragmentation of a charm quark, its
v_{\rm 2}
will solely feature the contribution from the interactions of the charm quark in the QGP medium.
Instead, when the hadron is produced via coalescence of a heavy quark with light quarks from the QGP
[
3
]
, its
v_{\rm 2}
will also include the contribution from the interactions of the captured light quarks.
Lastly, when the hadron, referred to as non-prompt in this case, is produced in the decay of a hadron with beauty-quark content, its
v_{\rm 2}
reflects the interactions of the beauty quark in the QGP medium, with a slight smearing due to the decay kinematics.
The elastic and inelastic rescattering processes occurring in the hadron gas phase before the freeze-out can further contribute to the measured
v_{\rm 2}
.

Analysis procedure

In this proceedings, we present the measurements of the
v_{\rm 2}
of prompt and non-prompt
\mathrm{D^{0}}
and
\Lambda_{\mathrm{c}}^{+}
hadrons, and of prompt
\mathrm{D^{+}}
and
\mathrm{D_{s}^{+}}

\sqrt{s_{\mathrm{NN}}}=5.36
TeV for several centrality classes.
For the analysis, the entire data sample recorded by ALICE in 2023 is employed, amounting to an integrated luminosity of
\mathcal{L}_{\text{int}}\approx 1.5
nb
-1
.
The ITS, TPC, and TOF detectors reconstruct charged particles at midrapidity (
|y|<0.8
) and provide particle identification (PID) information, with improved performance compared to LHC Run 2 owing to major upgrades implemented during LHC Long Shutdown 2
[
4
]
.
The
\mathrm{D^{0}}
,
\mathrm{D^{+}}
,
\mathrm{D_{s}^{+}}
mesons and
\Lambda_{\mathrm{c}}^{+}
baryons are reconstructed in selected hadronic decay channels along with their charge conjugates:
{\rm D}^{0}\to{\rm K}^{-}\pi^{+}
(BR
=3.95\pm 0.03\%
),
{\rm D}^{+}\to{\rm K}^{-}\pi^{+}\pi^{+}
(BR
=9.38\pm 0.16\%
),
{\rm D_{s}^{+}\to\upphi\pi^{+}\to K^{-}K^{+}\pi^{+}}
(BR
=2.22\pm 0.06\%
), and
\Lambda_{\mathrm{c}}^{+}\to\mathrm{pK^{-}\pi^{+}}
(BR
=6.28\pm 0.32\%
)
[
5
]
.
The displaced decay-vertex topologies determined by the lifetime of charm and beauty hadrons, of the order of 100
\mu
m and 400
\mu
m respectively, along with the PID information of their decay products are exploited by Machine Learning based multiclass BDT models
[
6
]
to separate the background, prompt, and non-prompt contributions.
The Scalar Product (SP) method
[

]
is employed to extract the

signals and the FT0C, FV0, and TPC detectors are used to estimate the
Q
-vector resolution
[
8
]
.
Since the

signal cannot be extracted on a candidate-by-candidate basis, a simultaneous fit to the invariant mass distribution and to the candidate
v_{\rm 2}
in intervals of invariant mass is performed.
An example is reported in the left panel of Figure
1
, which shows a clear reduction of the candidate
v_{\rm 2}
around the
\Lambda_{\mathrm{c}}^{+}
signal peak, indicating a distinct elliptic flow for signal compared to the combinatorial background.

For all extracted values, the fraction of non-prompt candidates

As shown in the top right panel of Figure

Given the large size of the employed data sample, for each

As in Ref.
[

(bottom right of Figure

v_{\rm 2}
at
f_{\mathrm{np}}=0

4
Conclusion

In these proceedings, several new results on the study of charm hadron elliptic flow in ultrarelativistic heavy-ion collisions by ALICE using the latest data collected during LHC Run 3 have been presented.
For the first time at the LHC, the prompt and non-prompt
\Lambda_{\mathrm{c}}^{+}
-baryon
v_{\rm 2}
is measured in semicentral collisions, providing evidence for the meson–baryon splitting of the
v_{\rm 2}
observable in the charm sector.
Moreover, the prompt D-meson
v_{\rm 2}
is measured down to 0.5
\mathrm{GeV}/c
and in peripheral events, where a smaller and shorter-lived QGP phase is produced as compared to central collisions.
A trend of lower
v_{\rm 2}
for the
\mathrm{D_{s}^{+}}
meson with respect to the non-strange D-meson
v_{\rm 2}
is suggested for
p_{\rm T}<4

\mathrm{GeV}/c
, but the current uncertainties do not support a firm conclusion.
The results will be further improved by including the data sample collected in 2024, similar in size to the one used in this analysis, and the 2025 data, whose projected integrated luminosity is approximately
2.1
nb
-1
.

Abstract
. We show that singular Riemannian foliations, or, more generally, manifold submetries, defined on a compact normal homogeneous space, have algebraic nature. Moreover, in this case there exists a one-to-one correspondence between algebras of algebraic functions preserved by the Laplace–Beltrami operator, and manifold submetries. A key intermediate result is that, for any manifold submetry on a compact normal homogeneous space, the vector field given by the mean curvature of the fibers is basic, in the sense that it is related to a vector field in the base.

1.
Introduction

A
manifold submetry
is defined as a map
\sigma\colon M\to X
, where
(M,g)
is a Riemannian manifold and
X
a metric space, which, in addition to being a
submetry
(that is,
\sigma
takes metric balls to metric balls of the same radius), has fibers that are smooth, possibly disconnected, submanifolds. Submetries and manifold submetries were originally defined in
[
Bi87
]
and
[
CG16
]
, respectively. See
Section

2.3
for more details.

Manifold submetries generalize many classical objects: Riemannian submersions, (the quotient maps of) proper isometric group actions and singular Riemannian foliations with closed leaves, including isoparametric foliations. Moreover, submetries and manifold submetries also appear naturally in rigidity questions and conjecturally in collapsing with lower curvature bounds. We point the interested reader to the list of references in
[
KL22
, Introduction]
.

An equivalent, and perhaps more natural, object is a partition
\mathcal{F}
of
M
into equidistant subsets: The fibers of a submetry
\sigma\colon M\to X
are equidistant, and, conversely, given a partition
\mathcal{F}
into equidistant closed subsets (“leaves”), there is a unique metric on the “leaf space”
M/\mathcal{F}
making the natural projection map
\sigma\colon M\to M/\mathcal{F}
into a submetry. This leads to a natural equivalence relation between (manifold) submetries, namely,
\sigma\colon M\to X
and
\sigma^{\prime}\colon M\to X^{\prime}
are equivalent if their fibers define the same partition.

In the case where
M
is a round sphere, every manifold submetry is an
algebraic
object. This has been established in
[
LR18
,
MR20
]
, and results in close ties (including applications) to classical Invariant Theory, see
[
MR20
,
MR23
,
LMR23
]
. The main goal of the present article is to extend these results to the case where
M
is a compact homogeneous space.

In this article, a connected, compact Riemannian manifold will be called
homogeneous
when its group of isometries acts transitively, and
normal homogeneous
when, for some compact Lie group
G
acting transitively on
M
, some bi-invariant metric on
G
induces the given metric on
M
. The latter include all compact symmetric spaces. Key to the generalization of the algebraicity of manifold submetries from spheres to compact homogeneous space is the selection of an appropriate algebra of functions generalizing polynomials. Given a compact homogeneous space
M
, we define the algebra of “algebraic functions”
\mathbb{R}[M]\subset C^{\infty}(M)
to be the span of the eigenfunctions of the Laplace–Beltrami operator. See
Section

2.2
for more details, including a justification of the word “algebraic”.

Our first main result generalizes the main results in
[
LR18
,
MR20
,
MR23
]
to compact normal homogeneous spaces:

Theorem A
.

Let
(M,g)
be a compact normal homogeneous space.

(a)

Every manifold submetry
\sigma\colon M\to X
is

, in the sense that there exists a finite set
\rho_{1},\ldots,\rho_{k}\in\mathbb{R}[M]

is an algebraic variety, namely a level set of

(b)

There exists a one-to-one correspondence between manifold submetries defined on
M

\mathbb{R}[M]
which are preserved by the Laplace–Beltrami operator.

23

(M,g)

Prior to

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Spectrum of the Laplacian

Let
(M,g)
be a closed Riemannian manifold. The Laplace–Beltrami operator (or “Laplacian”) on functions is defined by

\Delta(f)=-\operatorname{div}(\nabla f)

for
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
.

We denote the eigenvalues of
\Delta
by

0=\lambda_{0}<\lambda_{1}<\lambda_{2}<\cdots

where
\lambda_{i}\to\infty
as
i\to\infty
. For an eigenvalue
\lambda
, we denote by
E_{\lambda}
the corresponding eigenspace, and note that it is finite-dimensional. If we select an
L^{2}
-orthonormal basis of each
E_{\lambda}
, their union over all
\lambda
forms an
L^{2}
-orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(M)
.

Any isometry of
M
induces a linear map on
C^{\infty}(M)
which preserves each eigenspace. Thus, given an isometric group action of
G
on
M
, each eigenspace has the natural structure of a
G
-representation.

We will need the following result, which seems to be well-known to experts. Since the authors have not been able to locate a reference in the literature, a proof is provided.

Lemma 5
.

Let
(M,g)
be a closed Riemannian manifold. Then
\oplus_{\lambda}E_{\lambda}
(the span of all eigenfunctions of
\Delta
) is dense in
C^{0}(M)
in the
\sup
norm.

Proof.

By the Stone–Weierstrass theorem (see, e.g.
[
Rud76
, Theorem 7.32 on page 162]
),
C^{\infty}(M)
is dense in
C^{0}(M)
in the sup norm, so it suffices to show
\oplus_{\lambda}E_{\lambda}
is dense in
C^{\infty}(M)
.

Let
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
, and write
f=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}\phi_{k}
, where
\{\phi_{k}\}
is an
L^{2}
-orthonormal basis of eigenfunctions, with eigenvalues
\lambda_{i(k)}
. Using self-adjointness of
\Delta
, we have
a_{k}=\langle f,\phi_{k}\rangle=\langle\Delta^{j}f,\lambda_{i(k)}^{-j}\phi_{k}\rangle
, and, in particular,
|a_{k}|\leq\|\Delta^{j}f\|_{L^{2}}\lambda^{-j}_{i(k)}
, for every choice of
j
.

It follows from Weyl’s Law (see
[
Cha84
, Section I.3]
) that
\lambda_{i(k)}\sim Ck^{2/n}
(where
n=\dim M
). This implies that
|a_{k}|=O(k^{-N})
for every choice of
N>0
, because we can take
j>Nn/2
in the inequality above.

Since the sup norm satisfies
\|\phi_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C^{\prime}\lambda_{i(k)}^{(n-1)/4}
(see e.g.,
[
SZ02
, Abstract]
), we can take
N
large enough so that
\frac{2}{n}\frac{n-1}{4}-N<-1
, and use Weyl’s Law again to conclude that the series
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}|a_{k}|\|\phi_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}}
converges.

Therefore the partial sums of
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}\phi_{k}
form a sequence in
\oplus_{\lambda}E_{\lambda}
which converges in sup norm to
f
.
∎

2.3.
Manifold submetries

In this section we recall the definition and some basic facts about submetries (respectively manifold submetries), and refer the reader to
[
KL22
]
(respectively
[
MR20
]
) for more details.

Submetries, originally introduced in
[
Bi87
]
, are maps
\sigma\colon Y\to X
between metric spaces which take closed metric balls to closed metric balls of the same radius. The fibers of
\sigma
form a partition of
Y
into pairwise equidistant closed subsets, and every such partition comes from a submetry. We will frequently switch between these two points of view when convenient. A function
f\colon Y\to Z
, where
Z
is a set, is called
basic
when it factors through
\sigma
. A subset
A\subset Y
is called
saturated
when it is a union of fibers, that is, when
A=\sigma^{-1}(B)
for some subset
B\subset X
.

If
Y
is an Alexandrov space, so is
X
(see
[
KL22
, Proposition 3.1]
), and, in this case,
\sigma
has a well-defined differential
d_{p}\sigma\colon T_{p}Y\to T_{\sigma(p)}X
at every
p\in Y
, where
T_{p}Y,T_{\sigma(p)}X
denote the tangent cones, see
[
KL22
, Proposition 3.3]
.

Manifold submetries were originally defined in
[
CG16
]
.

Definition 10
.

Let
(M,g)
be a Riemannian manifold, and
\sigma\colon M\to X
a submetry onto a metric space
X
. We say
\sigma
is a
manifold submetry
if, for every
x\in X
, there exists a non-negative integer
k=k(x)
such that
\sigma^{-1}(x)
is a possibly disconnected smooth
k
-dimensional embedded submanifold of
M
. We sometimes refer to the fibers of
\sigma
as “leaves”.

We stress that all connected components of a given fiber must have the same dimension, but that different fibers may have different dimensions.

Examples of manifold submetries include: Riemannian submersions, (singular) Riemannian foliations with closed leaves, and actions by (not necessarily connected) closed subgroups of the isometry group
2
2

2

In the latter two examples, we have given the equidistant decomposition, and the actual submetry map is given by the natural projection onto the leaf/orbit space.
.

Definition 11
.

Let
\sigma\colon M\to X
be a manifold submetry. The
regular part
in
M
, denoted
M_{\operatorname{reg}}
, is given by the union of all fibers of maximal dimension.

The regular part
M_{\operatorname{reg}}
is an open and saturated subset of
M
,
which is full-measure, hence dense.

Definition 12
.

Let
\sigma\colon M\to X
be a manifold submetry. We say
\sigma
has
basic mean curvature
when, for every
p,q\in M_{\operatorname{reg}}
with
\sigma(p)=\sigma(q)=x\in X
, we have
d_{p}\sigma(H_{p})=d_{q}\sigma(H_{q})
, where

denotes the mean curvature vector field of the fiber
\sigma^{-1}(x)\subset M
.

. The elements
\rho_{1},\ldots,\rho_{k}\in A
can be chosen to belong to any given basis of

Proof.

Let
B\subset A
be an
\mathbb{R}
-basis of

in
\mathbb{R}[M]\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{R}[M]
generated by the elements
1\otimes f-f\otimes 1
, where
f
runs through all elements of

. It follows from
Lemma

9
and Hilbert’s Basis Theorem that
\mathbb{R}[M]\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{R}[M]
is Noetherian, and therefore there exist
\rho_{1},\ldots,\rho_{k}\in B
such that

1\otimes\rho_{1}-\rho_{1}\otimes 1\ ,\ \ldots\ ,\ 1\otimes\rho_{k}-\rho_{k}\otimes 1

generate

. Note that
(1\otimes f-f\otimes 1)(p,q)=f(q)-f(p)
, which implies that
\mathcal{L}(A)=\mathcal{L}(\{\rho_{1},\ldots,\rho_{k}\})
.∎

Definition 15

Let

Lemma 16
.

Let
M
be a compact homogeneous space. Let
A\subset\mathbb{R}[M]
be an
\mathbb{R}
-sub-algebra which is preserved by the Laplace–Beltrami operator
\Delta
. Then
A
is maximal.

Proof.

By the Laplacian condition, we know
A=\bigoplus_{\lambda}(A\cap E_{\lambda})
, where
E_{\lambda}
denotes the
\lambda
-eigenspace of the Laplacian. Let
\sim_{A}
denote the equivalence relation on
M
modulo
A
,
\sigma\colon M\to X=M/\!\sim_{A}
the canonical projection, and
A^{\prime}=\mathcal{B}(\sigma)
the algebra of algebraic functions which are
\sigma
-basic, that is, that descend to
X
. Then we can define the map
\varphi\colon A\to C^{0}(X)
by
\varphi(f)(\sigma(p)):=f(p)
, and
\varphi(A)
is an algebra separating points in
X
(in particular
X
is Hausdorff), and containing the constants, thus by the Stone–Weierstrass theorem (see, e.g.
[
Rud76
, Theorem 7.32 on page 162]
), it is dense in
C^{0}(X)
with respect to the supremum norm.

Suppose, for the sake of contradiction, that there is some
f^{\prime}\in A^{\prime}\setminus A
. Since
f^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}[M]
, it is contained in a finite direct sum of eigenspaces of
\Delta
, that is,
f^{\prime}\in E:=\sum_{i=1}^{N}E_{\lambda_{i}}
. Since
A\cap E\subseteq A
is finite-dimensional,
p_{A\cap E}(f^{\prime})
exists (where the projection is taken with respect to the
L^{2}
norm) and
f:=f^{\prime}-p_{A\cap E}(f^{\prime})
is perpendicular to
A
in the
L^{2}
norm, and
f\neq 0
. By the density of
\varphi(A)
, there is
g\in A
such that
\|f-g\|_{C^{0}(M)}=\|\varphi(f)-\varphi(g)\|_{C^{0}(X)}<\varepsilon
for any choice of
\varepsilon>0
. Using the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, we obtain

\langle f,g\rangle=\langle f,f\rangle-\langle f,f-g\rangle\geq\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\|f\|_{L^{2}}\cdot\|f-g\|_{L^{2}}

Since
\|f-g\|_{L^{2}}\leq\sqrt{\operatorname{vol}(M)}\ \|f-g\|_{C^{0}(M)}
, we can choose
g\in A
such that
\langle f,g\rangle>0
, which contradicts the fact that
f\perp A
.

Therefore
A^{\prime}=A
, that is,
A
is maximal.
∎

Definition 17
.

Let
A\subset B
be
\mathbb{R}
-algebras. An
\mathbb{R}
-linear projection map
\pi\colon B\to A
is called a
Reynolds operator
when
\pi(ab)=a\pi(b)
for all
a\in A
and
b\in B
.

Lemma 18
.

Let
M
be a compact homogeneous space, and
A\subset\mathbb{R}[M]
be a sub-algebra preserved by
\Delta
. Then there exists a Reynolds operator
\pi\colon\mathbb{R}[M]\to A
.

Proof.

Since
A
is preserved by the Laplacian
\Delta
, we have
A=\bigoplus_{\lambda}(A\cap E_{\lambda})
, where
E_{\lambda}
denotes the
\lambda
-eigenspace of the Laplacian. For each
\lambda
, let
\pi_{\lambda}\colon E_{\lambda}\to A\cap E_{\lambda}
be the
L^{2}
-orthogonal projection, and define
\pi\colon\mathbb{R}[M]\to A
by
\pi=\oplus_{\lambda}\pi_{\lambda}
. Since eigenspaces are pairwise
L^{2}
-orthogonal, it follows that
\pi
is the
L^{2}
-orthogonal projection of
\mathbb{R}[M]
onto
A
, in the sense that
\ker(\pi)=\oplus_{\lambda}\ker(\pi_{\lambda})
is orthogonal to
A
, and
\mathbb{R}[M]=A\oplus\ker(\pi)
.

Let
a\in A
and
b\in\mathbb{R}[M]
. Write
b=b_{1}+b_{2}
, where
b_{1}=\pi(b)\in A
and
b_{2}\perp A
. Then

\pi(ab)=\pi(ab_{1}+ab_{2})=ab_{1}+\pi(ab_{2})=a\pi(b)+\pi(ab_{2}),

where, in the second equality, we have used that
A
is an algebra, hence
ab_{1}\in A
.
Thus, to show
\pi
is a Reynolds operator, it suffices to show that
ab_{2}\perp A
. Let
f\in A
. Then

\langle f,ab_{2}\rangle=\int fab_{2}=\langle fa,b_{2}\rangle=0

because
fa\in A
(since
A
is an algebra) and
b_{2}\perp A
.
∎

Lemma 19
.

Let
M
be a compact homogeneous space, and
A\subset\mathbb{R}[M]
be a sub-algebra preserved by
\Delta
. Then
A=\mathbb{R}[M]\cap F(A)
, where
F(A)
denotes the field of fractions of

.

Proof.

Since a Reynolds operator exists by
Lemma

18
, the proof in
[
MR20
, Lemma 24(b)]
applies. Indeed, one inclusion is clear, and for the other, let

and

such that

. Then

, and thus, applying a Reynolds operator

on both sides yields

, which implies

.
∎

4.
Basic algebraic functions

In this section we introduce the algebra of basic algebraic functions
\mathcal{B}(\sigma)
associated to a manifold submetry
\sigma
on a compact homogeneous space, which generalizes the algebra of invariant functions from classical Invariant Theory. We show (
Theorem

23
below) that, when the manifold submetry
\sigma
has basic mean curvature, this algebra is “large enough” to separate fibers, and hence that
\sigma
is algebraic, which eventually yields
A
(a) from the Introduction.

Definition 20
.

Let
(M,g)
be a compact homogeneous space, and
\sigma\colon M\to X
be a manifold submetry (see
Section

2.3
). The algebra of
basic algebraic functions
consists of all algebraic functions (see
Section

2.2
) which are constant on the fibers of
\sigma
, and is denoted by
\mathcal{B}(\sigma)\subset\mathbb{R}[M]
.

Definition 21
.

Let
(M,g)
be a compact homogeneous space, and
\sigma\colon M\to X
be a manifold submetry. We say
\sigma
is
algebraic
when there are basic eigenfunctions
\rho_{1},\ldots,\rho_{k}\in\mathcal{B}(\sigma)
such that, whenever
p,q\in M
are such that
\sigma(p)\neq\sigma(q)
, there exists
i
such that
\rho_{i}(p)\neq\rho_{i}(q)
. A set
\{\rho_{1},\ldots,\rho_{k}\}\subset\mathcal{B}(\sigma)
satisfying this property is called a (finite)
separating set
for
\sigma
.

The following clarifies the definition and immediate consequences of algebraicity of manifold submetries.

Proposition 22
.

Let
(M,g)
be a compact homogeneous space, and
\sigma\colon M\to X
be a manifold submetry. Assume
\sigma
is algebraic, with separating set
\rho_{1},\ldots,\rho_{k}\in\mathcal{B}(\sigma)
. Let
\rho=(\rho_{1},\ldots,\rho_{k})
. Then:

(a)

The map
\rho\colon M\to\mathbb{R}^{k}
descends to a homeomorphism
X\to\rho(M)
.

(b)

\rho(M)
is a semi-algebraic subset of
\mathbb{R}^{k}
.

(c)

Every
\sigma
-fiber is an algebraic variety in the sense that it corresponds to an algebraic variety in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
under any
G
-equivariant embedding
M\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
where
G
acts linearly on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

Proof.

(a) From the definition of submetry, the metric topology on
X
coincides with the quotient topology associated with
\sigma\colon M\to X
. Thus the induced map
\bar{\rho}\colon X\to\rho(M)
is continuous. Since
\bar{\rho}
is bijective,
X
is compact, and
\rho(M)
is Hausdorff, we conclude that
\bar{\rho}
is a homeomorphism.

To prove (b), we use
Lemma

8
. Let
G
be a compact Lie group acting transitively on
M
, and
\varphi\colon M\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a
G
-equivariant embedding, where
G
acts linearly on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Then
\rho_{i}=P_{i}\circ\varphi
for certain polynomials
P_{i}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
, and
\rho(M)=P(\varphi(M))
, where
P\colon\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{k}
is given by
P=(P_{1},\ldots,P_{k})
. Since
\varphi(M)
is a
G
-orbit, it is an algebraic variety in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Thus
P(\varphi(M))
is a semi-algebraic subset, by Tarski’s Quantifier Elimination Theorem.

(c) is clear from the proof of (b).
∎

The main result in this section is the following theorem, most of whose proof follows along the same lines as that of

Theorem 23
.

Let
(M,g)
be a compact homogeneous space, and

be a manifold submetry. Assume

is a sub-algebra of

preserved by the Laplace–Beltrami operator

We note that compactness of

Example 24

Consider

and

\mathbb{R}\to\ \mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
. The fibers (orbits) are infinite discrete subsets, hence not algebraic varieties.

For a similar example with connected fibers, consider

and
\sigma
given by the quotient map
\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\ \mathbb{R}^{3}/\mathbb{R}
, where

acts on

by isometries via a “corkscrew” motion, where

is mapped to a rotation by

Before providing a proof of
Theorem

23
, we recall the definition and basic facts regarding the averaging operator.

Definition 25
.

Let
M
be a Riemannian manifold, and
\sigma\colon M\to X
a manifold submetry with compact fibers. The

is the linear map
\operatorname{Av}\colon L^{2}(M)\to L^{2}(M)
defined by

\operatorname{Av}(f)(p)=\frac{1}{\operatorname{vol}(L_{p})}\int_{q\in L_{p}}f(q)\ d\operatorname{vol}_{L_{p}}(q).

Here

denotes the
\sigma
-fiber through

;

denotes the Riemannian volume form on
L_{p}
induced by the restricted Riemannian metric from
M
; and
\operatorname{vol}(L_{p})=\int_{q\in L_{p}}1\ d\operatorname{vol}_{L_{p}}(q)
denotes the corresponding volume.

Lemma 26
.

Let
\sigma\colon M\to X
be a manifold submetry with compact fibers and basic mean curvature. Then the following hold:

(a)

The averaging operator is well-defined, and is the
L^{2}
-orthogonal projection onto the subspace of
L^{2}(M)
consisting of the elements with a basic representative.

(b)

For every
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
, the element
\operatorname{Av}(f)\in L^{2}(M)
has a smooth representative.

(c)

The operators
\Delta,\operatorname{Av}\colon C^{\infty}(M)\to C^{\infty}(M)
commute.

Proof.

The proofs of these statements are analogous to the proofs in the special case where
\sigma
is given by a singular Riemannian foliation, see
[
LR18
, Section 3]
(see also
[
PR96
]
), so we only sketch the arguments and point out the key fact about manifold submetries that is needed. Namely: the complement of the regular part
M_{\operatorname{reg}}
(that is, the union of the fibers of maximal dimension) is a closed subset which is the union of
submanifolds of codimension at least two. This follows from the results on stratification in
[
MR20
, Appendix B]
. More precisely, by
[
MR20
, Remark 46]
,
M_{\operatorname{reg}}
is open, and by
[

, Proposition 43]
every stratum is a smooth submanifold. By
[

, Lemma 47]
, there are no strata of codimension one, thus

is the union of strata of codimension at least two.

Since

has full-measure, the results analogous to
[

, Sections 3.1 and 3.2]
are proved in essentially the same way. In particular this yields (a), and the fact that the averaging operator and the Laplace–Beltrami operator commute for smooth functions

.

The proof of (b) follows the same arguments as
[

, Theorem 3.3]
, where the key fact about

used is that it has codimension at least two in

, in particular it has vanishing

-dimensional Hausdorff measure.

(c) follows from (b) and the fact that
\operatorname{Av}
and
\Delta
commute for functions on

Proof of

.

The subset

Let

is also a finite sum of eigenfunctions. Moreover,

is basic by the definition of the averaging operator and by

. Indeed, a function is basic if and only it is equal to its average, and
\operatorname{Av}(\Delta(f))=\Delta(\operatorname{Av}(f))=\Delta(f)
. Therefore
\mathcal{B}(\sigma)
is preserved by
\Delta

Next we show that
\mathcal{B}(\sigma)
separates
\sigma
-fibers. Let

be two distinct fibers, and choose

which is constant equal to

on

. After averaging, we may assume

is basic. Choose

. By

, there is

which is
\varepsilon

\operatorname{Av}(h)
is

-close to

. Moreover,

\mathcal{B}(\sigma)
separates
\sigma
-fibers.

Since
\mathcal{B}(\sigma)
is preserved by
\Delta

\mathbb{R}
-basis of eigenfunctions. By

5.
From Laplacian algebras to manifold submetries

The main goal of this section is to identify, given a compact homogeneous space
M
, the sub-algebras of the algebra
\mathbb{R}[M]
of algebraic functions which arise as the algebra of basic algebraic functions
\mathcal{B}(\sigma)
for some manifold submetry
\sigma
with basic mean curvature. They are precisely the sub-algebras preserved by the Laplace–Beltrami operator. This eventually leads to the one-to-one correspondence in
A
(b).

Theorem 29
.

Let
M
be a compact homogeneous space, and
A\subset\mathbb{R}[M]
a sub-algebra preserved by the Laplacian. Then there exists a manifold submetry
\sigma\colon M\to X
with basic mean curvature, such that
\mathcal{B}(\sigma)=A
.

Before presenting the proof of
Theorem

29
, we use it to prove the following:

Corollary 30
.

Let
M
be a compact homogeneous space. Define an equivalence relation
\sim
on the set of all manifold submetries from
M
with basic mean curvature by
\sigma\sim\sigma^{\prime}
if and only if the fibers of
\sigma
and
\sigma^{\prime}
define the same decomposition of
M
. Then the map
\sigma\mapsto\mathcal{B}(\sigma)
defines a bijection from the set of all manifold submetries from
M
with basic mean curvature (modulo
\sim
), to the set of all sub-algebras of
\mathbb{R}[M]
that are preserved under the Laplace–Beltrami operator.

Proof.

Let
\sigma\colon M\to X
be a manifold submetry with basic mean curvature. By
Theorem

23
,
\mathcal{B}(\sigma)
is a sub-algebra of
\mathbb{R}[M]
preserved by the Laplacian. By definition,
\mathcal{B}(\sigma)
depends only on the decomposition of
M
into the fibers of
\sigma
. Thus the map above is well-defined.

By
Theorem

29
, the image of
\mathcal{B}
is the set of all sub-algebras of
\mathbb{R}[M]
that are preserved under the Laplace–Beltrami operator.

Let
\sigma\colon M\to X
and
\sigma^{\prime}\colon M\to X^{\prime}
be two manifold submetries with basic mean curvature, and assume
\mathcal{B}(\sigma)=\mathcal{B}(\sigma^{\prime})
. By
Theorem

23
,
\sigma
and
\sigma^{\prime}
are algebraic, and thus
\mathcal{B}(\sigma)=\mathcal{B}(\sigma^{\prime})
separates
\sigma
-fibers and separates
\sigma^{\prime}
-fibers. Therefore, given

, they belong to different
\sigma
-fibers if and only if they belong to different
\sigma^{\prime}
-fibers. We conclude that
\sigma\sim\sigma^{\prime}
.
∎

\rho(M_{\operatorname{reg}})\subset\mathbb{R}^{k}
has dimension
m
. Take a stratification of
\rho(M_{\operatorname{reg}})
and let
T\subset\rho(M_{\operatorname{reg}})
be the union of the strata with dimension strictly smaller than
m
. In particular,
\rho(M_{\operatorname{reg}})\setminus T
is a smooth
m
-dimensional embedded submanifold of
\mathbb{R}^{k}
.

Since
T
is a semi-algebraic set with dimension smaller than
m
,
[
BR90
, Lemma 3.4.4 on page 136]
implies that
T\subset Z
, where
Z
is an
algebraic
set of dimension (as a semi-algebraic set, i.e., in the sense of
[
BR90
, Definition 2.5.3]
) smaller than
m
. Thus
Z
is a finite union of locally closed smooth submanifolds of dimension smaller than
m
. Letting
Q
be a polynomial defining
Z
finishes the proof.
∎

Since
\rho|_{M_{0}}\colon M_{0}\to X_{0}
is a constant rank map, it is a surjective smooth submersion.

Lemma 32
.

(cf.
[
MR20
, Proposition 26]
)
There exists a smooth metric
b
on
X_{0}
such that
\rho|_{M_{0}}\colon M_{0}\to X_{0}
is a Riemannian submersion with basic mean curvature.

Proof.

For all
i,j
, we have

\langle\nabla\rho_{i},\nabla\rho_{j}\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\Delta(\rho_{i}\rho_{j})-(\Delta\rho_{i})\rho_{j}-\rho_{i}(\Delta\rho_{j})\right)\in A,

because
A
is a sub-algebra preserved by the Laplacian. Thus, for all
p,q\in M_{\operatorname{reg}}
with
\rho(p)=\rho(q)
, we have
\langle\nabla\rho_{i}(p),\nabla\rho_{j}(p)\rangle=\langle\nabla\rho_{i}(q),\nabla\rho_{j}(q)\rangle
by the separation property.

Thus, for every
x\in X_{0}
, there is an inner product
b_{x}
on
T_{x}X_{0}
with respect to which
d\rho_{p}
restricted to the normal space
\nu_{p}(\rho^{-1}(x))
is an isometry onto
T_{x}X_{0}
, for every choice of
p\in\rho^{-1}(x)
. This defines a metric tensor
b
on
X_{0}
such that
\rho|_{M_{0}}\colon M_{0}\to X_{0}
is a Riemannian submersion. Since
\rho|_{M_{0}}\colon M_{0}\to X_{0}
is a smooth submersion, and the metric
g
is smooth, it follows that
b
is also smooth.

The proof that the mean curvature vector field is basic is the same as in
[
MR20
, Lemma 31]
.
∎

Lemma 33
.

(cf.
[
MR20
, Proposition 27]
)
Every pair
L_{1},L_{2}
of
\rho
-fibers of the Riemannian submersion
\rho|_{M_{0}}\colon M_{0}\to X_{0}
are equidistant in
M
.

Proof.

Let
p,q\in L_{1}
. Let
\gamma
be a (unit speed) minimizing geodesic in
M
between
p=\gamma(0)
and
L_{2}
, of length
\ell
. Then
\gamma^{\prime}(t)
is horizontal at
t=\ell
, hence for
t
near
\ell
.

Write the vectors
\gamma^{\prime}(t)
and
\nabla\rho_{i}(\gamma(t))
, for
i=1,\ldots k
in a parallel frame along
\gamma
. Arrange the vectors thus obtained as the columns of an
n\times(k+1)
matrix
B(t)
, and let
F(t)
be the sum of the squares of all the
(m+1)\times(m+1)
minors of
B(t)
. Thus, for all
t
such that
\gamma(t)\in M_{0}
, we have that
F(t)=0
if and only if
\gamma^{\prime}(t)
is horizontal.

Since
(M,g)
is a compact homogeneous space, it is a real analytic manifold, and thus its geodesics are real analytic, and so are the eigenfunctions of the Laplacian. Therefore
F(t)
is analytic. Since
F(t)
vanishes for
t
near
\ell
, it must vanish everywhere, in particular near
t=0
. That is, the restriction of
\gamma(t)
to a small interval
(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)
is a horizontal geodesic for the Riemannian submersion
\rho|_{M_{0}}\colon M_{0}\to X_{0}
.

Let
\hat{\gamma}\colon\mathbb{R}\to M
be the geodesic such that
\hat{\gamma}|_{(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)}
is the horizontal lift of
\rho\circ\gamma|_{(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)}
with
\hat{\gamma}(0)=q
. Then
\rho(\gamma(t))=\rho(\hat{\gamma}(t))
for all
t\in(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)
, and therefore for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
, because these are analytic functions. In particular,
\hat{\gamma}(\ell)\in L_{2}
, which implies that
d_{M}(q,L_{2})\leq\ell=d_{M}(p,L_{2})
.

Reversing the roles of
p,q
, we obtain
d_{M}(q,L_{2})=d_{M}(p,L_{2})
. Reversing the roles of
L_{1},L_{2}
, we conclude that
L_{1},L_{2}
are equidistant.
∎

Lemma 34
.

There exists a submetry
\hat{\rho}\colon M\to\hat{X}
such that
M_{0}
is a saturated set, and, on
M_{0}
, the decomposition into the fibers of
\hat{\rho}
coincides with the decomposition into the fibers of the Riemannian submersion
\rho|_{M_{0}}
.

Proof.

Using the previous lemmas, this is analogous to the proof of
[
MR20
, Prop. 28]
.
∎

Since almost all regular fibers of the submetry
\hat{\rho}\colon M\to\hat{X}
have basic mean curvature, we can use
[
LW24
, Proposition 15.2]
to conclude that every fiber of
\hat{\rho}
is a smooth submanifold. According to the definitions in
[
LW24
]
, this still leaves the possibility that some fibers have connected components of different dimensions. But basic mean curvature implies, via an argument analogous to
[
MR20
, Proposition 32]
, that every fiber of
\hat{\rho}
does indeed have components of the same dimension, that is,
\hat{\rho}
is a manifold submetry in the sense of
Definition

10
.

Note that the regular part of
\hat{\rho}
in the sense of
Section

2.3
contains
M_{0}
, but may be larger. Nevertheless, since
M_{0}
is dense in
M
, it follows that
\hat{\rho}
has basic mean curvature in the sense of
Definition

12
. Thus, by
Theorem

23
, the sub-algebra
\hat{A}=\mathcal{B}(\hat{\rho})\subset\mathbb{R}[M]
is preserved by the Laplacian, and separates
\hat{\rho}
-fibers, so that
\mathcal{L}(\hat{A})
coincides with the decomposition of
M
into the fibers of
\hat{\rho}
.

It remains to show that
A=\hat{A}
.

On the one hand, let
f\in A
. The construction of
\hat{\rho}
implies that, for any
p,q\in M
with
\hat{\rho}(p)=\hat{\rho}(q)
, there exist sequences
p_{i},q_{i}\in M_{0}
such that
p=\lim p_{i}
and
q=\lim q_{i}
, and such that
\rho(p_{i})=\rho(q_{i})
for every
i
. Thus
f(p_{i})=f(q_{i})
for all
i
by the separation property of
\rho_{1},\ldots,\rho_{k}
, and therefore,
f(p)=f(q)
. Thus
f
is basic with respect to
\hat{\rho}
, that is,
f\in\hat{A}
, and this shows that
A\subset\hat{A}
.

For the reverse inclusion, let
f\in\hat{A}
. Consider
P\in A
given by the sum of the squares of all
m\times m
minors of the Gram matrix
\langle\nabla\rho_{i},\nabla\rho_{j}\rangle
. Note that
P\neq 0
and that

on

. Then,
P\cdot(Q\circ\rho)f=0
on

(recall that the polynomial

was produced by

), and, since
\hat{\rho}
and

, and

is constant on the common level sets of

. But

, where

denotes the field of fractions of

. Using

19
, we obtain that

.

This finishes the proof of

.

6.
Normal homogeneous spaces and the proofs of
A
and
B

The following is key to the proof of
B
, and its use is the main novelty in the present paper. It follows in a relatively straight-forward way from the theory laid out in
[
HLO06
]
(initiated earlier by Terng and Thorbergsson, among others, see
[
TT95
]
), and we believe it may be of independent interest.

Theorem 35
.

Let
M
be a compact normal homogeneous space,
L\subset M
a smooth submanifold,
p\in L
a point on
L
, and
v\in\nu_{p}L
a unit normal vector. Denote by

\cdots\leq l_{2}^{-}\leq l_{1}^{-}<0<l_{1}^{+}\leq l_{2}^{+}\leq\cdots

the focal lengths (repeated with appropriate multiplicities) of
L
in the direction of
v
. Then the trace of the shape operator
A_{v}
of
L
in the direction of
v
is

\operatorname{tr}(A_{v})=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{l_{k}^{-}}+\frac{1}{l_{k}^{+}}\right).

Proof.

By
[
HLO06
, page 18]
, there is a Riemannian submersion
\pi:V\to M
with minimal fibers, where
V
is a separable Hilbert space.

Let
\hat{L}=\pi^{-1}(L)
, choose
\hat{p}\in\hat{L}
, and let
\hat{v}\in T_{\hat{p}}\hat{L}
be the horizontal lift of
v
.
By
[
HLO06
, Lemma 6.1]
, the focal lengths of
\hat{L}
in the direction of
\hat{v}
are the same as for
L
, that is:

\cdots\leq l_{2}^{-}\leq l_{1}^{-}<0<l_{1}^{+}\leq l_{2}^{+}\leq\cdots

Since
V
is a Hilbert space, the shape operator
A_{\hat{v}}
of
\hat{L}
in the direction of
\hat{v}
has eigenvalues

\frac{1}{l_{1}^{-}}\leq\frac{1}{l_{2}^{-}}\leq\cdots<0<\cdots\leq\frac{1}{l_{2}^{+}}\leq\frac{1}{l_{1}^{+}}

see
[
HLO06
, page 26]
.

By
[
HLO06
, Lemma 5.2]
,
\hat{L}
is regularizable. By definitions in
[
HLO06
, page 13]
, this implies that the series

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{l_{k}^{-}}+\frac{1}{l_{k}^{+}}\right)

converges to the “regularized trace” of
A_{\hat{v}}
, denoted
\operatorname{tr}_{r}(A_{\hat{v}})
.

By
[
HLO06
, Lemma 5.2]
(or rather, its proof), we have
\operatorname{tr}(A_{v})=\operatorname{tr}_{r}(A_{\hat{v}})
. Therefore

\operatorname{tr}(A_{v})=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{l_{k}^{-}}+\frac{1}{l_{k}^{+}}\right).

∎

Example 36
.

Let
M=S^{2}
, and
L
a latitude circle with distance
\varphi\in(0,\pi)
from the north pole, and let
v
be a unit normal vector pointing towards the north pole. Then the shape operator
A_{v}
, and its trace, are equal to
\cot(\varphi)
. The positive focal distances are

\varphi,\ \varphi+\pi,\ \varphi+2\pi,\ldots

and the negative focal distances are

\ldots,\ \varphi-2\pi,\ \varphi-\pi

Thus
Theorem

35
states that

\cot(\varphi)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{\varphi+k\pi}+\frac{1}{\varphi-k\pi-\pi}\right)

which can be rewritten as

\cot(\varphi)=\lim_{N\to\infty}\sum_{n=-N}^{N}\frac{1}{\varphi+n\pi}

This is a well-known identity due to Euler, see
[
AZ04
, chapter 20]
. See also
[
Wik25
]
, section called “Partial fraction expansion”.

Inhibition of SLC40A1 represses osteoblast formation via inducing iron accumulation and activating the PERK/ATF4/CHOP pathway mediated oxidative stress
Authors: Yu Fang, Wei Li, Chongyang Dong, Binli Gao, Wen Guo, Mingyu Li, Zhichao Jiao
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTObjectiveThis study aimed to investigate the effects of solute carrier family 40 member 1 (SLC40A1) on iron accumulation, oxidative stress and differentiation in osteoblasts and potential mechanisms.MethodsMouse preosteoblastic MC3T3-E1 cells were transfected with the SLC40A1 overexpression vector (oeSLC40A1) and siRNA (siSLC40A1), then cell differentiation was induced via ascorbic acid and β-glycerophosphate. Besides, Ferrostatin-1 (ferroptosis inhibitor) and GSK2606414 (PERK inhibitor) were added with siSLC40A1.ResultsFe2+, malondialdehyde (MDA), and reactive oxygen species (ROS) were higher but reduced glutathione (GSH)/oxidized glutathione (GSSG) ratio was lower after siSLC40A1 transfection, while reduced Fe2+ and ROS but elevated GSH/GSSG ratio was observed after oeSLC40A1 transfection. Alkaline phosphatase (ALP) staining, Alizarin Red S (ARS) staining, osteopontin (OPN) and bone morphogenetic protein 2 (BMP2) were lower after siSLC40A1 transfection but were greater after oeSLC40A1 transfection. Furthermore, SLC40A1 negatively regulated the PERK/ATF4/CHOP pathway. Further exploration revealed that Fe2+, MDA, ROS, and the PERK/ATF4/CHOP pathway were attenuated, while GSH/GSSG ratio, ALP staining, ARS staining, and OPN expression were increased after ferrostatin-1 treatment in the siSLC40A1-transfected cells. Similar trends were observed with respect to GSK2606414 treatment with siSLC40A1.ConclusionSLC40A1 inhibition suppresses osteoblast formation by facilitating iron accumulation and activating the PERK/ATF4/CHOP pathway-mediated oxidative stress.KEYWORDS: SLC40A1, iron accumulation, oxidative stress, PERK/ATF4/CHOP pathway, osteoblast formation, osteoblast differentiation, ferrostatin-1, GSK2606414

Introduction

Osteoporosis is a systemic bone disease caused by a variety of factors that are mainly characterized by decreased bone density and bone quality, with a high prevalence of 19.7% (95% CI: 18.0%–21.4%) globally [1,2]. The dysregulation of osteoblast formation is a key factor closely implicated in the osteoporotic process; consequently, an in-depth investigation of osteoblast abnormalities is necessary for successful corresponding treatment [3]. Iron accumulation and its induced oxidative stress can repress osteoblast formation via multiple ways [4–6]. For example, iron accumulation enhances osteoblast apoptosis via XIST/miR-758-3p/caspase 3 signaling [4], and leads to severe abnormalities in mitochondrial morphology and functions in osteoblasts, which can be attenuated by the ferroptosis inhibitor or iron chelator deferoxamine [5]. A recent review revealed that iron accumulation promotes oxidative stress and inactivates the Wnt/β-catenin pathway, thereby suppressing osteoblast formation [6].

Solute carrier family 40 member 1 (SLC40A1) is an iron transport protein mainly involved in the regulation of iron transportation and iron homeostasis [7]. Dysregulated SLC40A1 can damage the iron-exporting efficiency of ferroportin, leading to the accumulation of intracellular iron and related oxidative stress [8,9]. Clinical tests have confirmed that the SLC40A1 polymorphism is related to iron overload in humans [10]. Subsequently, decreased SLC40A1 stimulates oxidative stress and ferroptosis by regulating intracellular iron accumulation [11], whereas increased SLC40A1 exhibits the opposite effect. [12,13]. Recently, a bioinformatic analysis article reported that SLC40A1 may be a hub gene related to the ferroptosis of osteoblasts and the osteoclast balance in osteoporosis [14]. However, the direct impact of SLC40A1 on osteoblast formation and the potential mechanism are unclear.

The current study aimed to investigate the effects of SLC40A1 on iron accumulation, oxidative stress and differentiation in osteoblasts and its interaction with the endoplasmic reticulum stress (ERS)-related PERK/ATF4/CHOP pathway.

Materials and methods

Cell culture and transfection

Mouse preosteoblastic MC3T3-E1 cells (NCACC, China) were maintained in alpha modification minimum essential medium (α-MEM; Servicebio, China) containing 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS; Gibco, USA) at 37 °C and 5% CO2. The SLC40A1 overexpression vector (oeSLC40A1) and siRNA (siSLC40A1), as well as the negative controls (oeNC and siNC), were designed and synthesized by GenePharma (Shanghai, China). For cell transfection, the MC3T3-E1 cells were cultured in a 6-well plate and incubated with either vector or siRNA in the presence of Lipofectamine 2000 (Thermo Fisher, USA). After 3 days of transfection, quantitative polymerase chain reaction (qPCR) and western blotting were performed. Moreover, the levels of malondialdehyde (MDA), Fe2+, reduced glutathione (GSH), oxidized glutathione (GSSG), and reactive oxygen species (ROS) were determined.

Osteoblast differentiation

MC3T3-E1 cells were treated with 50 μg/mL ascorbic acid (MedChemExpress, China) and 10 mM β-glycerophosphate (MedChemExpress, China) for osteoblast differentiation as described in a previous study. [15] Alkaline phosphatase (ALP) staining was performed after 7 days of treatment. Alizarin Red S staining and detection of bone formation markers were performed after 21 days of treatment.

Ferrostatin-1 (Fer-1) treatment

Fer-1, a synthetic antioxidant, was used to inhibit the accumulation of lipid ROS. In brief, MC3T3-E1 cells were transfected with siSLC40A1 or siNC, and then Fer-1 (1 μM; MedChemExpress, China) was added and stimulated for 3 days. The cells were collected to determine MDA, Fe2+, GSH, GSSG, and ROS levels and to perform western blotting. The transfected MC3T3-E1 cells were used for osteoblast differentiation in the presence or absence of Fer-1, and ALP staining, Alizarin Red S staining, and detection of bone formation markers were carried out as indicated.

GSK2606414 (GSK) treatment

GSK, a cell-permeable PERK inhibitor, was used to inhibit the activation of PERK. Briefly, MC3T3-E1 cells were transfected with siSLC40A1 or siNC, after which GSK (0.5 μM; MedChemExpress, China) was added. After 3 days of stimulation, the treated MC3T3-E1 cells were harvested for western blotting, MDA, Fe2+, GSH, GSSG, and ROS determination. Furthermore, the transfected MC3T3-E1 cells were used for osteoblast differentiation in the presence or absence of GSK, and ALP staining, Alizarin Red S staining, and detection of bone formation markers were performed as described above.

qPCR

After 3 days of transfection, the total RNA of the MC3T3-E1 cells was isolated via RNA extraction solution (Servicebio, China). qPCR was performed with a SweScript One-Step RT‒PCR Kit (Servicebio, China). The sequences of primers used were as follows (5'->3’): SLC40A1 (forward: GTGGAGTACTTCTTGCTCTGG, reverse: CTGCTTCAGTTCTGACTCCTC) and β-actin (forward: GGCTGTATTCCCCTCCATCG, reverse: CCAGTTGGTAACAATGCCATGT).

Western blotting

MC3T3-E1 cells were lysed for protein isolation via RIPA buffer (Beyotime, China). The proteins were quantified with a BCA quantification kit (Beyotime, China). The proteins were mixed with loading buffer (Servicebio, China) and boiled for 5 min, followed by gentle centrifugation before loading. The protein mixture from each group was added to a precast gel (Willget, China) for electrophoresis. After the proteins were transferred to a nitrocellulose membrane (Servicebio, China), blocking and incubation with antibodies were performed according to standard procedures. The bands were visualized via an enhanced chemiluminescence (ECL) kit (Servicebio, China). The information on the antibodies is presented in Supplementary Table 1.

Detection of the MDA level

The protein of the MC3T3-E1 cells was isolated via RIPA buffer and quantified via a BCA quantification kit. The level of MDA was determined via an MDA colorimetric assay kit (Elabscience, China). In brief, the protein mixture was mixed with the MDA reaction reagent for 40 min at 100° C, followed by centrifugation for 10 min (10000 × g). The optical density (OD) value was read at 532 nm using a microplate reader (Huadong Electronics, China).

Detection of the Fe2+ concentration

The Fe2+ level of the MC3T3-E1 cells was assessed via a Cell Ferrous Iron Colorimetric Assay Kit (Elabscience, China). In brief, the cells were lysed with lysis buffer for 10 min at 4 °C and centrifuged for 15 min (15000 × g). The lysis mixture was incubated with the staining mixture for 10 min at 37 °C, after which the OD value was read at 593 nm.

Detection of GSH and GSSG

The Total Glutathione / GSSG Colorimetric Assay Kit (Elabscience, China) was used to assess the concentration of GSH and GSSG. Briefly, a total of 106 cells were lysed using 400 μL of lysis buffer, followed by centrifugation for 10 min (4°C, 10000 g). Then, the total glutathione detection solution was added to the lysate for 25 min at room temperature (RT), and the OD value was read at 412 nm. For GSSG detection, the lysate was incubated with GSH scavenger auxiliary solution before the reaction with total glutathione detection solution. The total glutathione and GSSG were calculated according to the standard curve. The GSH concentration was calculated using the following formula: GSH concentration = total glutathione concentration- 2 × GSSG concentration.

Detection of ROS levels

The ROS levels in the MC3T3-E1 cells was quantified with an ROS assay kit (Beyotime, China). Briefly, the cells were incubated with the fluorescence probe DCFH-DA (10 μmol/L) for 20 min at 37 °C and then washed with serum-free medium three times to remove DCFH-DA. The images were analyzed with a fluorescence microscope (Motic, China).

ALP and Alizarin Red S staining

Osteoblast differentiation of MC3T3-E1 cells was performed, after which the cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde (Beyotime, China). For ALP staining, the fixed cells were incubated with the ALP staining solution (Beyotime, China) for 30 min at RT in the dark. The staining solution was then removed, and the cells were washed with phosphate-buffered saline (PBS). For Alizarin Red S staining, the fixed cells were stained with Alizarin Red S staining solution (Beyotime, China) for 30 min at RT, followed by PBS washing. The results were imaged and analyzed under a microscope.

Statistical analysis

One-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) with Tukey's test was used to analyze significant differences. The analysis was performed via GraphPad Prism 9 (GraphPad Software, USA). P < 0.05 was regarded as statistically significant. In the figures, * represented P < 0.05, ** represented P < 0.01, *** represented P < 0.001, while ns represented P > 0.05.

Results

SLC40A1 attenuated iron accumulation and oxidative stress in osteoblasts

SLC40A1 was upregulated by oeSLC40A1 but downregulated by siSLC40A1, suggesting that the transfections were efficient in the MC3T3-E1 cells (Figure 1A-B). Afterward, the relative Fe2+ level (but not MDA) was lower after oeSLC40A1 transfection, but the relative Fe2+ level and MDA were both greater after siSLC40A1 transfection (Figure 1C-D). The GSH level and GSH/GSSG ratio were elevated, but GSSG level showed a reduce tendency (not significant), after oeSLC40A1 transfection; and they showed opposite trends after siSLC40A1 transfection (Figure 1E). The levels of ROS decreased after oeSLC40A1 transfection but increased after siSLC40A1 transfection (Figure 1F).

Effect of SLC40A1 modification on iron accumulation and oxidative stress. Effects of oeSLC40A1 versus oeNC and siSLC40A1 versus siNC on SLC40A1 mRNA expression (A), SLC40A1 protein expression (B), Fe2+ levels (C), MDA (D), GSH and GSSG level (E), ROS level and images (F) in MC3T3-E1 cells.

SLC40A1 promoted osteoblast differentiation

The degree of ALP staining was greater, but the degree of Alizarin Red S staining did not significantly change after oeSLC40A1 transfection in the stimulated MC3T3-E1 cells; in contrast, the degree of ALP staining and the degree of Alizarin Red S staining were both lower after siSLC40A1 transfection (Figure 2A-B). Further western blotting revealed that OPN and BMP2 were elevated after oeSLC40A1 transfection but reduced after siSLC40A1 transfection (Figure 2C).

Effect of SLC40A1 modification on osteoblast differentiation. The effects of oeSLC40A1 versus oeNC and siSLC40A1 versus siNC on the degree of ALP staining (A), degree of Alizarin Red S staining (B), and OPN and BMP2 expression (C) in stimulated MC3T3-E1 cells.

SLC40A1 negatively regulates the PERK/ATF4/CHOP pathway in osteoblasts

p-PERK and CHOP expression was downregulated after oeSLC40A1 transfection in the MC3T3-E1 cells, while ATF4 expression was not significantly altered; in addition, p-PERK, ATF4 and CHOP expression was upregulated after siSLC40A1 transfection (Figure 3).

Effect of SLC40A1 modification on the PERK/ATF4/CHOP pathway in osteoblasts.

Fer-1 attenuated the effect of siSLC40A1 on osteoblasts

Iron accumulation decreased after Fer-1 treatment in the siSLC40A1-transfected MC3T3-E1 cells, as reflected by reduced Fe2+ level and MDA (Figure 4A-B). Meanwhile, the GSH level and GSH/GSSG ratio were increased but GSSG level was decreased after Fer-1 treatment in the siSLC40A1-transfected MC3T3-E1 cells (Figure 4C). In addition, ROS were decreased after Fer-1 treatment in the siSLC40A1-transfected MC3T3-E1 cells (Figure 4D). With respect to the PERK/ATF4/CHOP pathway, p-PERK and ATF4 expression was downregulated after Fer-1 treatment in the siSLC40A1-transfected MC3T3-E1 cells (Figure 4E). In terms of osteoblast differentiation, the degree of ALP staining, degree of Alizarin Red S staining, and degree of OPN expression were greater after Fer-1 treatment in the siSLC40A1-transfected MC3T3-E1 cells after stimulation (Figure 5A-C).

Effects of Fer-1 in combination with siSLC40A1 on iron accumulation, oxidative stress, and the PERK/ATF4/CHOP pathway. Fe2+ levels (A), MDA (B), GSH and GSSG level (C), ROS level and images (D), and the PERK/ATF4/CHOP pathway (E) in MC3T3-E1 cells in different groups: siNC group, siSLC40A1 group, Fer-1 group, and siSLC40A1 + Fer-1 group.

Effects of Fer-1 in combination with siSLC40A1 on osteoblast differentiation. ALP degree (A), Alizarin Red S staining degree (B), and OPN and BMP2 expression (C) in stimulated MC3T3-E1 cells among different groups: the siNC group, the siSLC40A1 group, the Fer-1 group, and the siSLC40A1 + Fer-1 group.

GSK weakened the effect of siSLC40A1 on osteoblasts

GSK is a PERK inhibitor and was subsequently added with or without siSLC40A1. p-PERK, ATF4, and CHOP expression was downregulated after GSK treatment in the siSLC40A1-transfected MC3T3-E1 cells (Figure 6A). The Fe2+ level did not significantly change (Figure 6B), but the MDA level was reduced after GSK treatment in the siSLC40A1-transfected MC3T3-E1 cells (Figure 6C). Besides, the GSH level and GSH/GSSG ratio were higher while the GSSG level was lower after GSK treatment in the siSLC40A1-transfected MC3T3-E1 cells (Figure 6D). Finally, the relative ROS level was reduced after GSK treatment in the siSLC40A1-transfected MC3T3-E1 cells (Figure 6E). Furthermore, the degree of ALP staining, degree of Alizarin Red S staining, and degree of OPN expression were increased after GSK treatment in the siSLC40A1-transfected MC3T3-E1 cells after stimulation (Figure 7A-C).

Effects of the combination of GSK and siSLC40A1 on the PERK/ATF4/CHOP pathway, iron accumulation, and oxidative stress. PERK/ATF4/CHOP pathway (A), Fe2+ level (B), MDA (C), GSH and GSSG level (D), ROS level and images (E) in MC3T3-E1 cells among different groups: siNC group, siSLC40A1 group, GSK group, and siSLC40A1 + GSK group.

Effect of GSK in combination with siSLC40A1 on osteoblast differentiation. ALP degree (A), Alizarin Red S staining degree (B), and OPN and BMP2 expression (C) in stimulated MC3T3-E1 cells among different groups: the siNC group, the siSLC40A1 group, the GSK group, and the siSLC40A1 + GSK group.

To combine the findings, a summarized schematic diagram was drawn (Figure 8), which revealed that dysregulated (mainly reduced) SLC40A1 induced intracellular iron accumulation to cause the Fenton reaction. Then, ROS worsened initiating the activation of the ERS-related PERK/ATF4/CHOP pathway, ultimately leading to repressed osteoblast formation.

Discussion

As an iron transport protein, SLC40A1 has been revealed to regulate iron accumulation and related oxidative stress in several mammalian cells, and it participates in many other biological processes and the initiation of disease [8,9,16,17]. Nevertheless, its involvement in osteoblast iron accumulation and oxidative stress has seldom been reported. The results of the present study revealed that SLC40A1 overexpression decreased intracellular iron accumulation and oxidative stress in osteoblasts, whereas SLC40A1 knockdown had the opposite effects, as reflected by the levels of Fe2+, MDA and ROS. These effects might result from the following: (a) SLC40A1 transporting iron from the intracellular space to the extracellular space by facilitating the iron-exporting efficiency of ferroportin [9] to negatively regulate intracellular iron accumulation; and (b) Intracellular iron accumulation further facilitates the production of ROS via the Fenton and Harber-Weiss reactions to accelerate oxidative stress [18,19], and disrupts the balance of the antioxidant enzyme system to worsen oxidative stress [20,21].

Increasing evidence reveals that iron accumulation and oxidative stress can repress the process of osteoblast differentiation to participate in healing bone damage [22,23]. Inspired by these findings, the effect of SLC40A1 on osteoblast differentiation was further investigated in this study, which revealed that SLC40A1 overexpression promoted osteoblast differentiation while SLC40A1 knockdown suppressed it, as reflected by the results of ALP staining, Alizarin Red S staining, and the expression levels of OPN and BMP2. These effects might result from SLC40A1 weakening iron accumulation and oxidative stress, as discovered in our study, to instead positively regulate osteoblast differentiation [22,23].

Fer-1 is a selective ferroptosis inhibitor that is commonly used to study iron accumulation-related oxidative stress and ferroptosis [24]. Therefore, to further determine whether SLC40A1 functions by regulating iron accumulation and related oxidative stress, SLC40A1 knockdown and Fer-1 treatment were performed, which revealed that Fer-1 attenuated the effects of SLC40A1 knockdown on iron accumulation-related oxidative stress and differentiation in osteoblasts. These effects might result from Fer-1 being a lipid ROS scavenger containing an N-cyclohexyl moiety that acts as a lipophilic anchor in biofilms to prevent damage to membrane lipids, thereby attenuating the effects of SLC40A1 knockdown on osteoblast oxidative stress and differentiation [25].

The PERK/ATF4/CHOP pathway is a critical signaling pathway for the ERS response. In brief, when cells face ERS (such as a lack of nutrients, drugs to stimulate, oxidative stress, etc.), the PERK/ATF4/CHOP pathway is activated; the phosphorylated activation of PERK leads to the phosphorylation of eIF2α, which in turn promotes ATF4 translation and expression; and ATF4 then increases CHOP expression and causes cell death [26,27]. Moreover, ferroptosis lies on the intersection of lipid, iron and amino acid metabolism [28]. ATF4, as a transcription factor belonging to ATF/cAMP response element-binding family, regulates various cellular responses such as ERS, integration of redox, amino acid deprivation, and oxidative challenges [29,30]. Interestingly, although ATF4 often functions to promote ferroptosis, sometimes it can also repress ferroptosis, which hinges on varied stress levels and cellular conditions [29]. Therefore, the activation of ATF4 in PERK/ATF4/CHOP pathway is important for ERS and ferroptosis. Although few reports exist, the PERK/ATF4/CHOP pathway has been shown to repress osteoblast formation via ERS activation [31,32]. This study revealed that SLC40A1 negatively regulates the PERK/ATF4/CHOP pathway in osteoblasts; therefore, SLC40A1 knockdown and GSK (a PERK inhibitor) treatment were combined, revealing that GSK attenuated the effect of SLC40A1 knockdown on iron accumulation-related oxidative stress and differentiation in osteoblasts. These effects might result from the fact that GSK represses the PERK/ATF4/CHOP pathway, which reflects the suppression of ERS, thereby weakening oxidative stress; the latter then further improves osteoblast differentiation [26,27,31,32].

Conclusion

In summary, the inhibition of SLC40A1 represses osteoblast formation via the induction of iron accumulation and the PERK/ATF4/CHOP pathway-mediated oxidative stress. However, more experiments are needed for validation.

Sublevels in arrangements and the spherical arc crossing number of complete graphs
Elizaveta Streltsova, Uli Wagner

Abstract
Levels and sublevels in arrangements—and, dually, k k -sets and ( ⩽ k ) (\leqslant k) -sets—are fundamental notions in discrete and computational geometry and natural generalizations of convex polytopes, which correspond to the 0 0 -level. A long-standing conjecture of Eckhoff, Linhart, and Welzl, which would generalize McMullen’s Upper Bound Theorem for polytopes and provide an exact refinement of asymptotic bounds by Clarkson, asserts that for all k ⩽ ⌊ n − d − 2 2 ⌋ k\leqslant\lfloor\frac{n-d-2}{2}\rfloor , the number of ( ⩽ k ) (\leqslant k) -sets of a set S S of n n points in 𝐑 d \mathbf{R}^{d} is maximized if S S is the vertex set of a neighborly polytope. As a new tool for studying this conjecture and related problems, we introduce the g g -matrix , which generalizes both the g g -vector of a simple polytope and a Gale dual version of the g g -vector studied by Lee and Welzl. Our main result is that the g g -matrix of every vector configuration in 𝐑 3 \mathbf{R}^{3} is non-negative. This implies the following result (which, by Gale duality, is equivalent to the case d = n − 4 d=n-4 of the Eckhoff–Linhart–Welzl conjecture). Introduction

In this paper, we study the interplay between several classical topics in discrete and computational geometry: the combinatorial theory of convex polytopes, the complexity of
(\leqslant k)
-sublevels in arrangements, Radon partitions, and crossing numbers.

3

\mathbb{R}^{3}
, let
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denote the standard inner product in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, and let

1.1
Dependency and Dissection Patterns

We consider two sets
\mathcal{F}(V),\mathcal{F}^{*}(V)\subset\{-1,0,+1\}^{n}
of sign vectors defined as follows.

By definition,
\mathcal{F}^{*}(V)
is the set of sign vectors
(\sgn(\lambda_{1}),\dots,\sgn(\lambda_{n}))\in\{-1,0,+1\}^{n}
given by non-trivial linear dependencies
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}v_{i}=0
(with coefficients
\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
, not all of them zero). We call
\mathcal{F}^{*}(V)
the
dependency patterns
of
V
.

We define
\mathcal{F}(V)
as the set of all sign vectors
(\sgn(\langle v_{1},u\rangle),\dots,\sgn(\langle v_{n},u\rangle))\in\{-1,0,+1\}^{n}
, where

ranges over all non-zero vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
(equivalently, unit vectors in
S^{2}
). Thus, the elements
\mathcal{F}(V)
encode partitions
V=V_{-}\sqcup V_{0}\sqcup V_{+}
by oriented planes through the origin, and we call them the
dissection patterns
of
V
. Equivalently, every vector
v_{i}\in V
defines a great circle

H_{i}=\{x\in S^{2}\mid\langle v_{i},x\rangle=0\}

in
S^{2}
and two open hemispheres
H_{i}^{+}=\{x\in S^{2}\mid\langle v_{i},x\rangle>0\}
and
H_{i}^{-}=\{x\in S^{2}\mid\langle v_{i},x\rangle<0\}.

The resulting
arrangement

\mathcal{A}(V)=\{H_{1}^{+},\dots,H_{n}^{+}\}
of hemispheres in
S^{2}
determines a decomposition of
S^{2}
into
faces
of dimensions
0,1,2
,
where two points
u,u^{\prime}\in S^{2}
lie in the relative interior of the same face iff
\sgn(\langle v_{i},u\rangle)=\sgn(\langle v_{i},u^{\prime}\rangle)
for
1\leqslant i\leqslant n
. Thus, we can identify each face of
\mathcal{A}(V)
with its
signature

F\in\mathcal{F}(V)
; by general position, the face with signature
F
has dimension
2-|F_{0}|
(there are no faces with
|F_{0}|>2
, i.e., the arrangement is
simple
). Moreover, we call
|F_{-}|
the
level
of the face. We
refer to the correspondence between

and
\mathcal{A}(V)
as
polar duality
(to distinguish it from
Gale duality
, see Sec.
3

Both
\mathcal{F}(V)
and
\mathcal{F}^{*}(V)
are invariant under invertible linear transformations of
\mathbb{R}^{r}
and under positive rescaling (multiplying each vector
v_{i}
by some positive scalar
\alpha_{i}>0
).

We are ready to state our first result. We say that

1.2
The Spherical Arc Crossing Number of
K_{n}

As an application of Theorem
2
, we obtain a result about crossing numbers. Determining the crossing number
\cross(K_{n})
of the complete graph
K_{n}
(the minimum number of crossings in any drawing of
K_{n}
in the plane
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, or equivalently in the sphere
S^{2}
, with edges represented by arbitrary Jordan arcs) is one of the foundational unsolved problems in geometric graph theory. This problem was first studied by Hill in the 1950’s, who conjectured the following:

Conjecture 3

(Hill)
.

\cross(K_{n})=X(n):=\frac{1}{4}\Big\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\Big\rfloor\Big\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\Big\rfloor\Big\lfloor\frac{n-2}{2}\Big\rfloor\Big\lfloor\frac{n-3}{2}\Big\rfloor=\frac{3}{8}\binom{n}{4}+O(n^{3})

(1)

This is known to hold for
n\leqslant 12
, but remains open in general (see
[
33
, Sec. 1.3]
or
[
36
]
for further background and references). There are several families of drawings showing that
\cross(K_{n})\leqslant X(n)
for all
n
, but the best lower bound to date is
\cross(K_{n})\geqslant 0.985\cdot X(n)

[
14
]
.

Here, we prove Hill’s conjecture for the following class of drawings. Let
V=\{v_{1},\dots,v_{n}\}\subset S^{2}
be a configuration of
n

unit
vectors in general position. If we connect every pair of vectors in
V
by the shortest geodesic arc between them in
S^{2}
(which is unique, since no two vectors are antipodal, by general position) we obtain a drawing of the complete graph
K_{n}
in
S^{2}
, which we call a
spherical arc drawing
. Let

\cross(V)

denote the number of crossings in this drawing.

Theorem 4
.

For every configuration of
n
vectors in general position in
S^{2}
,

\cross(V)\geqslant\frac{1}{4}\Big\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\Big\rfloor\Big\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\Big\rfloor\Big\lfloor\frac{n-2}{2}\Big\rfloor\Big\lfloor\frac{n-3}{2}\Big\rfloor

Moreover, the lower bound is attained with equality if
V
is coneighborly.

The fact that coneighborly configurations yield spherical arc drawings of
K_{n}
achieving the number
X(n)
of crossings in Hill’s conjecture and the connection to the Eckhoff–Linhart–Welzl conjecture were first observed by Wagner
[
38
,
39
]
. For a connection to geometric probability, in particular to old results of Wendel
[
41
]
and Moon
[
28
]
, see Remark
16

Spherical arc drawings generalize the well-studied class of
rectilinear drawings
of
K_{n}

n
points in general position in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
connected by straight-line segments; more precisely, rectilinear drawings correspond to the sub-class of sperical arc drawings for which the vector configuration

is

correspond to point sets

2

. Conversely, if

for some

.

and under this correspondence,

encode the partitions of

Definition 17

.

The Remaining Calculation for the Spherical Arc Crossing Number

We want to show that

Using the basic identity

\sum_{k=0}^{m}\binom{k+i}{i}=\binom{m+i+1}{i+1}

we see that (

\displaystyle\displaystyle n(n-3)\binom{\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor}{2}-3(n-3)\binom{\lfloor\frac{n+2}{2}\rfloor}{3}-4n\binom{\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor}{3}+18\binom{\lfloor\frac{n+2}{2}\rfloor}{4}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor\left\lfloor\frac{n-2}{2}\right\rfloor\underbrace{\left(\frac{1}{2}n(n-3)-\frac{1}{2}(n-3)\left\lfloor\frac{n+2}{2}\right\rfloor-\frac{2}{3}n\left\lfloor\frac{n-4}{2}\right\rfloor+\frac{3}{4}\left\lfloor\frac{n+2}{2}\right\rfloor\left\lfloor\frac{n-4}{2}\right\rfloor\right)}_{(*)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor\left\lfloor\frac{n-2}{2}\right\rfloor\underbrace{\left(\frac{2}{3}\left(\left\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\right\rfloor+\frac{1}{2}\right)\left(\left\lfloor\frac{n-3}{2}\right\rfloor+\frac{1}{2}\right)-\frac{1}{4}\left\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\right\rfloor\left\lfloor\frac{n-3}{2}\right\rfloor\right)}_{(**)}

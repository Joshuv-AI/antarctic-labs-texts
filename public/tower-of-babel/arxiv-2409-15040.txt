Disentangling nuclear structure through multiparticle azimuthal correlations in high-energy isobar collisions
Zaining Wang ID, Jinhui Chen ID, Jiangyong Jia ID, Yu-Gang Ma ID, Chunjian Zhang ID

Abstract
Event-by-event fluctuations in the amplitudes of flow harmonics offer a novel approach to probing the initial-state characteristics in heavy-ion collisions. In this study, we conduct a systematic investigation of correlations among various flow harmonics utilizing multiparticle cumulants in 96 Ru+ 96 Ru and 96 Zr+ 96 Zr collisions at s NN = \mbox{$\sqrt{s_{\mathrm{NN}}}$}= 200 GeV within the framework of a multiphase transport model. Correlated nuclear density distributions specific to the isobar systems are incorporated to evaluate the sensitivity of selected observables to variations in nuclear deformation and neutron skin thickness. The analysis reveals that multiparticle azimuthal correlations are responsive to these nuclear structure features, predominantly in the most central collision events. Furthermore, the examined correlations exhibit shallow dependence on the assumed shear viscosity values. These findings provide a quantitative evaluation of the extent to which multiparticle flow observables can discern nuclear structure effects in isobar collisions and offer valuable guidance for future detailed dynamical investigations and experimental measurements.

Model setup and observables.—

The AMPT transport model
[
64
]
is a hybrid framework with four main phases: (i) fluctuating initial conditions from the HIJING model
[
65

, (ii) elastic parton cascade simulated by the ZPC model
[
66

[

\begin{split}\rho(r,\theta)=\frac{\rho_{0}}{1+e^{\left[r-R_{0}(1+\beta_{2}Y_{2,0}(\theta)+\beta_{3}Y_{3,0}(\theta))/a_{0}\right]}},\end{split}

Y_{3,0}(\theta)=\frac{1}{4}\sqrt{\frac{7}{\pi}}\left(5\cos^{3}\theta-3\cos\theta\right)

The two- and multiparticle correlations are used in the current work. The framework for the cumulants is described in Ref.
[
81
]
. The formalism of the multiparticle azimuthal correlations is evaluated as follows:

\begin{split}\left\langle v_{n}^{2}\right\rangle&=\left\langle\left\langle\{2\}_{n}\right\rangle\right\rangle=\left\langle\left\langle e^{in\left(\phi_{i}-\phi_{j}\right)}\right\rangle\right\rangle,\\
\rm asc_{nm,n+m}&=\left\langle\left\langle\{3\}_{n,m}\right\rangle\right\rangle=\left\langle\left\langle e^{in\phi_{i}+im\phi_{j}-i(n+m)\phi_{k}}\right\rangle\right\rangle,\\
\left\langle v_{n}^{2}v_{m}^{2}\right\rangle&=\left\langle\left\langle\{4\}_{n,m}\right\rangle\right\rangle=\left\langle\left\langle e^{in\left(\phi_{i}-\phi_{j}\right)+im\left(\phi_{k}-\phi_{m}\right)}\right\rangle\right\rangle.\end{split}

(4)

Double angular brackets
\left\langle\left\langle\right\rangle\right\rangle
indicate that the averaging procedure has been performed on an ensemble of events with similar centrality. The non-linear coupling coefficients and four-particle symmetric cumulants are defined as:

\begin{split}\chi_{n+m}&=\frac{asc_{nm,n+m}}{\left\langle v_{n}^{2}v_{m}^{2}\right\rangle},\\
\operatorname{sc}_{n,m}\{4\}&=\left\langle v_{n}^{2}v_{m}^{2}\right\rangle-\left\langle v_{n}^{2}\right\rangle\left\langle v_{m}^{2}\right\rangle.\end{split}

(5)

To scale out the dependence on the single particle flow harmonics, the normalized symmetric cumulant is defined as follows:

\begin{split}\operatorname{nsc}_{n,m}\{4\}=\frac{\operatorname{sc}_{n,m}\{4\}}{\left\langle v_{n}^{2}\right\rangle\left\langle v_{m}^{2}\right\rangle}.\end{split}

(6)

We analyze a three-particle correlation between triangular flow
v_{3}
and mean transverse momentum
[p_{T}]
,
\left\langle v_{3}^{2}\delta p_{\mathrm{T}}\right\rangle
,

\begin{split}\rho(v_{n}^{2},[p_{T}])=\frac{\langle v_{n}^{2}\delta p_{\mathrm{T}}\rangle}{\sqrt{\left\langle(\delta v_{n}^{2})^{2}\right\rangle\left\langle\delta p_{\mathrm{T}}\delta p_{\mathrm{T}}\right\rangle}}\end{split}

(7)

Where
\langle v_{n}^{2}\delta p_{T}\rangle=\langle\cos[n(\phi_{i}-\phi_{j})](p_{T,k}-\langle[p_{T}]\rangle)\rangle_{i,j,k}
. where the indices
i
,
j
and
k
loop over distinct particles to account for all unique triplets, and the
\langle\rangle
denotes average over events.

In order to suppress short-range
``
non-flow” correlations arising from jet fragmentation and resonance decays, pseudorapidity gaps are often explicitly required between the particles in each pair in well-developed sub-event cumulant methods
[
82
]
. The standard cumulant methods are contaminated by non-flow correlations over the the low multiplicity region in A+A collisions. In the two-subevent cumulant method, the tracks are divided into two subevents according to
-2<\eta_{a}<0
and
0\leq\eta_{b}<2
. Only hardons with 0.2
<p_{\rm T}<
2 GeV/
c
are chosen. The simulated events are binned into classes defined by the number of participant nucleons
N_{\mathrm{part}}
.

Modeling directional monotonicity with copulas
Enrique de Amo, David García-Fernández, José Juan Quesada-Molina, Manuel Úbeda-Flores

Abstract
The purpose of this paper is to characterize the concept of monotonicity according to a direction related to a set of n n random variables in terms of its associated n n -copula C. We start establishing relationships in the bivariate and trivariate cases, which help to understand the extension to the multivariate case. Examples of copulas in all the studied cases are provided.



1
Introduction

Dependence among random variables is a widely field of research in statistics and probability. Analyzing this dependence structure is crucial when we want to figure out the behaviour of a complex model components. Therefore, a deep research of dependence relations could bring us comprehensively information about the model of interest.

Random variables can be related in several ways, presenting different relationships of dependence. One of the most important in literature is positive dependence, which can be characterize as the inclination of components within a random vector to assume concordant values. Negative dependence can be defined similarly, with the exception that now, random variables values moves in different directions. Both of these concepts may not be enough to condense all the dependence that the variables show. It is for that reason that on this paper, we will focus on the concept of monotonicity according to a direction
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
defined in
[
9
]
, whose positive (respectively, negative) dependence concept is denoted by I
(\alpha)
(respectively, D
(\alpha)
).

Copulas —multivariate distribution functions with univariate uniform marginals— serve as a valuable tool for examining the positive dependence characteristics of a random vector. This is because they encapsulate the dependence structure of the corresponding multivariate distribution function, regardless of the individual marginal distributions
[
8
]
. Additionally, they provide scale-free measures of dependence and serve as a foundation for constructing families of distributions
[
3
]
.

In this paper, as we have already mentioned, our objective is to explore multivariate copulas associated to random vectors that exhibit the I(
\alpha
) property—we will refer to these as I(
\alpha
) copulas. However, for simplicity—and to better understand the general case—, we will start with the bivariate and trivariate cases and then we will extrapolate our results to the multivariate case.

This paper is organized as follows: we begin with some preliminary concepts and results corresponding to multivariate dependence, specifically monotonicity according to a direction, and to copula theory (Section
2
). Straightaway, in Section
3
, we will characterize the concept of I
(\alpha)
for the bivariate case using copulas, providing several examples of copulas that have this kind of dependence in different directions
\alpha\in\mbox{${\rm{I\!R}}$}^{2}
. Comparably, we will carry out a similar study in the trivariate case. Once we get used to this concepts in lower dimensions, we will extrapolate the characterization using copulas to the multivariate case, formulating a general result, allowing us to obtain an inequality, for every direction
\alpha\in\mbox{${\rm{I\!R}}$}^{n}
, in terms of the
n
-copula associated with the random
n
-vector and its marginals copulas. Finally, Section
4
is devoted to discuss the conclusions derived from our research.

These concepts of multivariate positive dependence can be extended to the concept of monotonicity according to a direction, defined bellow, which allow us to capture new dependency structures among random variables.

Definition 1

3
Monotonicity according to a direction and copulas

In this section, we will characterize the I

Similarly, we will conduct an analogous analysis in the trivariate case. Once we become familiar with these concepts in lower dimensions, we will extend the description using
n

3.1
The bivariate case

We start our study on the I
(\alpha)

Theorem 1
.

Let
(U,V)

I(1,-1)

[v-C(u,v)][v^{\prime}-C(u^{\prime},v^{\prime})]\leq[v-C(u^{\prime},v)][v^{\prime}-C(u,v^{\prime})]

u,v,u^{\prime},v^{\prime}
in
[0,1]
such that
u\leq u^{\prime}
and
v\leq v^{\prime}

iii.

I(-1,1)

[u-C(u,v)][u^{\prime}-C(u^{\prime}.v^{\prime})]\leq[u^{\prime}-C(u^{\prime},v)][u-C(u,v^{\prime})]

u,v,u^{\prime},v^{\prime}

u\leq u^{\prime}
and
v\leq v^{\prime}

iv.

I
(1,1)

\widehat{C}(u,v)\widehat{C}(u^{\prime},v^{\prime})\geq\widehat{C}(u,v^{\prime})\widehat{C}(u^{\prime},v)

Proof.

The proof of parts i. and iv. can be found in
[
8

Let
(U,V)
be a pair of random variables with associated 2-copula
C
. Assume
C
is I
(1,-1)
, then we have that
\mathbb{P}[U>u,-V>w|U>u^{\prime},-V>w^{\prime}]
is nondecreasing in
(u^{\prime},w^{\prime})
for all

\mathbb{P}[U>u,-V>w|U>u^{\prime},-V>w^{\prime}]\leq\mathbb{P}[U>u,-V>w|U>u^{\prime\prime},-V>w^{\prime\prime}]

(1)

for all
u,u^{\prime},u^{\prime\prime}\in[0,1]
and
w,w^{\prime},w^{\prime\prime}\in[-1,0]
such that
u^{\prime}\leq u^{\prime\prime}
and
w^{\prime}\leq w^{\prime\prime}
. Equation (
1
) is equivalent to

\frac{\mathbb{P}[U>u,-V>w,U>u^{\prime},-V>w^{\prime}]}{\mathbb{P}[U>u^{\prime},-V>w^{\prime}]}\leq\frac{\mathbb{P}[U>u,-V>w,U>u^{\prime\prime},-V>w^{\prime\prime}]}{\mathbb{P}[U>u^{\prime\prime},-V>w^{\prime\prime}]},

i.e.,

\frac{\mathbb{P}[U>u,V<-w,U>u^{\prime},V<-w^{\prime}]}{\mathbb{P}[U>u^{\prime},V<-w^{\prime}]}\leq\frac{\mathbb{P}[U>u,V<-w,U>u^{\prime\prime},V<-w^{\prime\prime}]}{\mathbb{P}[U>u^{\prime\prime},V<-w^{\prime\prime}]};

therefore,

\frac{\mathbb{P}[U>\max\{u,u^{\prime}\},V<\min\{-w,-w^{\prime}\}]}{\mathbb{P}[U>u^{\prime},V<-w^{\prime}]}\leq\frac{\mathbb{P}[U>\max\{u,u^{\prime\prime}\},V<\min\{-w,-w^{\prime\prime}\}]}{\mathbb{P}[U>u^{\prime\prime},V<-w^{\prime\prime}]},

that is,

\frac{\min\{-w,-w^{\prime}\}-C(\max\{u,u^{\prime}\},\min\{-w,-w^{\prime}\})}{-w^{\prime}-C(u^{\prime},-w^{\prime})}\leq\frac{\min\{-w,-w^{\prime\prime}\}-C(\max\{u,u^{\prime\prime}\},\min\{-w,-w^{\prime\prime}\})}{-w^{\prime\prime}-C(u^{\prime\prime},-w^{\prime\prime})}.

Taking
v=-w
,
v^{\prime}=-w^{\prime}
and
v^{\prime\prime}=-w^{\prime\prime}
, we obtain

\displaystyle[v^{\prime\prime}-C(u^{\prime\prime},v^{\prime\prime})]\cdot[\min\{v,v^{\prime}\}\!\!\!

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\!\!\!C(\max\{u,u^{\prime}\},\min\{v,v^{\prime}\})]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\!\!\![v^{\prime}-C(u^{\prime},v^{\prime})]\cdot[\min\{v,v^{\prime\prime}\}-C(\max\{u,u^{\prime\prime}\},\min\{v,v^{\prime\prime}\})]

for all
u,v,u^{\prime},v^{\prime},u^{\prime\prime},v^{\prime\prime}
in
[0,1]
such that
u^{\prime}\leq u^{\prime\prime}
and
v^{\prime\prime}\leq v^{\prime}
. By setting

and renaming

[v-C(u,v)][v^{\prime}-C(u^{\prime},v^{\prime})]\leq[v-C(u^{\prime},v)][v^{\prime}-C(u,v^{\prime})].

Conversely, if
[v-C(u,v)][v^{\prime}-C(u^{\prime}.v^{\prime})]\leq[v-C(u^{\prime},v)][v^{\prime}-C(u,v^{\prime})]
for all
u,v,u^{\prime},v^{\prime}\in[0,1]
such that
u\leq u^{\prime}
and
v\leq v^{\prime}

and

, we consider three possible cases:

1.

If

we have that

\mathbb{P}[U>u,-V>w|U>u^{\prime},-V>w^{\prime}]=\frac{\mathbb{P}[U>u,-V>w]}{\mathbb{P}[U>u^{\prime},-V>w^{\prime}]}

is nondecreasing in

2.

If

Since

P[V<v|U>u^{\prime},V<v^{\prime}]\leq\mathbb{P}[V<v|U>u^{\prime\prime},V<v^{\prime}],

\frac{v-C(u^{\prime},v)}{v^{\prime}-C(u^{\prime},v^{\prime})}\leq\frac{v-C(u^{\prime\prime},v)}{v^{\prime}-C(u^{\prime\prime},v^{\prime})}

\frac{\mathbb{P}[U>u^{\prime},V<v]}{\mathbb{P}[U>u^{\prime},V<v^{\prime}]}\leq\frac{\mathbb{P}[U>u^{\prime\prime},V<v]}{\mathbb{P}[U>u^{\prime\prime},V<v^{\prime}]}

Now, we will explore the

Lemma 3
.

Let
{\mathbf{X}}=(X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{n})
be an
n
-dimensional random vector and let
\alpha\in\mbox{${\rm{I\!R}}$}^{n}
such that
|\alpha_{i}|=1
for all
1\leq i\leq n
. Let
\alpha_{k}=-1
for
k\in I=\{i_{1},...,i_{p}\}\subseteq\{1,...,n\}
and let
J=\{1,...,n\}/I

(I,J\neq\emptyset)
. For all
{\mathbf{x}}\in\overline{\mbox{${\rm{I\!R}}$}}^{n}=[-\infty,\infty]^{n}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}(\alpha_{i}X_{i}>\overline{x}_{i})\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left[\bigcap_{i\in I}(X_{i}\leq\overline{x}_{i})\right]-\sum_{j\in J}\mathbb{P}\left[\bigcap_{i\in I\cup\{j\}}(X_{i}<\overline{x}_{i})\right]

\displaystyle+\sum_{j_{1}\in J}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j_{2}\in J\\
j_{2}>j_{1}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}\left[\bigcap_{i\in I\cup\{j_{1},j_{2}\}}(X_{i}<\overline{x}_{i})\right]-\cdots

\displaystyle+(-1)^{|J|-1}\sum_{j_{1}\in J}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j_{2}\in J\\
j_{2}>j_{1}\end{subarray}}\cdots\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j_{|J|-1}\in J\\
j_{|J|-1}>j_{|J|-2}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}\left[\bigcap_{i\in I\cup\{j_{1},...,j_{|J|-1}\}}(X_{i}<\overline{x}_{i})\right]

\displaystyle+(-1)^{|J|}\mathbb{P}\left[\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}(X_{i}<\overline{x}_{i})\right],

where

\overline{x}_{i}=\begin{cases}x_{i},&\text{ if }i\in J,\\
-x_{i},&\text{ if }i\in I.\end{cases}

Proof.

We are applying the induction method over the number of components of
\alpha
that are equal to
1
; i.e., over the cardinal of
J
. We will consider the following change of variable throughout this proof: if
-X_{i}>x_{i}
, then
X_{i}<-x_{i}
so we consider
\overline{x}_{i}=-x_{i}
if
i\in I
and
\overline{x}_{i}=x_{i}
if
i\in J
. Let us start proving the case
|J|=1
:
If we suppose that
\alpha_{l}=1
and the remaining coordinates are all equal to
-1
, then

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}(\alpha_{i}X_{i}>x_{i})\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left[x_{l}<X_{l}<\infty,\bigcap_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq l\end{subarray}}^{n}(X_{i}<\overline{x}_{i})\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left[\bigcap_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq l\end{subarray}}^{n}(X_{i}<\overline{x}_{i})\right]-\mathbb{P}\left[\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}(X_{i}<\overline{x}_{i})\right].

Now we consider the statement true for
|J|=m
and our goal is claiming the same for
|J|=m+1
. So, if
l_{m+1}\in J
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}(\alpha_{i}X_{i}>x_{i})\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left[\bigcap_{i\in J}(X_{i}>x_{i}),\bigcap_{i\in I}(X_{i}<x_{i})\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left[x_{l_{m+1}}<X_{l_{m+1}}<\infty,\bigcap_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in J\\
i\neq l_{m+1}\end{subarray}}(X_{i}>x_{i}),\bigcap_{i\in I}(X_{i}<\overline{x}_{i})\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left[\bigcap_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in J\\
i\neq l_{m+1}\end{subarray}}(X_{i}>x_{i}),\bigcap_{i\in I}(X_{i}<\overline{x}_{i})\right]-\mathbb{P}\left[\bigcap_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in J\\
i\neq l_{m+1}\end{subarray}}(X_{i}>x_{i}),\bigcap_{i\in I\cup\{l_{m+1}\}}(X_{i}<\overline{x}_{i})\right].

Applying the induction hypothesis we obtain that the first expression is equal to

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\bigcap_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in J\\
i\neq l_{m+1}\end{subarray}}(X_{i}>x_{i}),\bigcap_{i\in I}(X_{i}<\overline{x}_{i})\right]=\mathbb{P}\left[\bigcap_{i\in I}(X_{i}<\overline{x}_{i})\right]-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in J\\
j\neq l_{m+1}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}\left[\bigcap_{i\in I\cup\{j\}}(X_{i}<\overline{x}_{i})\right]

\displaystyle+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j_{1}\in J\\
j_{1}\neq l_{m+1}\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j_{2}\in J\\
j_{2}\neq l_{m+1}\\
j_{2}>j_{1}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}\left[\bigcap_{i\in I\cup\{j_{1},j_{2}\}}(X_{i}<\overline{x}_{i})\right]-\cdots+(-1)^{m}\mathbb{P}\left[\bigcap_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq l_{m+1}\end{subarray}}^{n}(X_{i}<\overline{x}_{i})\right].

Whereas the second one is equal to the following expression

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\bigcap_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in J\\
i\neq l_{m+1}\end{subarray}}(X_{i}>x_{i}),\bigcap_{i\in I\cup\{l_{m+1}\}}(X_{i}<\overline{x}_{i})\right]=\mathbb{P}\left[\bigcap_{i\in I\cup\{l_{m+1}\}}(X_{i}<\overline{x}_{i})\right]-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in J\\
j\neq l_{m+1}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}\left[\bigcap_{i\in I\cup\{l_{m+1},j\}}(X_{i}<\overline{x}_{i})\right]

\displaystyle+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j_{1}\in J\\
j_{1}\neq l_{m+1}\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j_{2}\in J\\
j_{2}\neq l_{m+1}\\
j_{2}>j_{1}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}\left[\bigcap_{i\in I\cup\{l_{m+1},j_{1},j_{2}\}}(X_{i}<\overline{x}_{i})\right]-\cdots+(-1)^{m}\mathbb{P}\left[\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}(X_{i}<\overline{x}_{i})\right].

Now, we subtract both expressions. Note that we can add the first term of the second expression to the second term of the first one; ie.,

-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in J\\
j\neq l_{m+1}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}\left[\bigcap_{i\in I\cup\{j\}}(X_{i}<\overline{x}_{i})\right]-\mathbb{P}\left[\bigcap_{i\in I\cup\{l_{m+1}\}}(X_{i}<\overline{x}_{i})\right]=-\sum_{j\in J}\mathbb{P}\left[\bigcap_{i\in I\cup\{j\}}(X_{i}<\overline{x}_{i})\right].

It follows similarly with the third term of the first expression and the second term of the second one

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j_{1}\in J\\
j_{1}\neq l_{m+1}\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j_{2}\in J\\
j_{2}\neq l_{m+1}\\
j_{2}>j_{1}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}\left[\bigcap_{i\in I\cup\{j_{1},j_{2}\}}(X_{i}<\overline{x}_{i})\right]+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in J\\
j\neq l_{m+1}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}\left[\bigcap_{i\in I\cup\{l_{m+1},j\}}(X_{i}<\overline{x}_{i})\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{j_{1}\in J}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j_{2}\in J\\
j_{2}>j_{1}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}\left[\bigcap_{i\in I\cup\{j_{1},j_{2}\}}(X_{i}<\overline{x}_{i})\right],

and so on with the remaining terms. Finally, our first and last terms are, respectively,

\mathbb{P}\left[\bigcap_{i\in I}(X_{i}<\overline{x}_{i})\right]\hskip 19.91684pt\text{and}\hskip 19.91684pt(-1)^{m+1}\mathbb{P}\left[\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}(X_{i}<\overline{x}_{i})\right].

Thus, grouping all of these terms together we reach the desired result.
∎

4
Conclusions

In this paper we have characterized the monotonicity according to a direction, a new concept of positive dependence, ---particularly the
I(\alpha)
notion— by copulas. We started with the bivariate and trivariate cases, due to their simplicity and straightforwardness, aiming to illustrate the procedure to be followed in the multivariate case. Initially, we obtained in both cases a characterization in terms of an inequality involving the associated copula and its margins evaluated at maximum and minimum values, in order to subsequently simplify this characterization for application to specific cases. Throughout this paper, examples of copulas with
I(\alpha)
dependence have been provided for different values of
\alpha
.

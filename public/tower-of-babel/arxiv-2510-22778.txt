A Free Probabilistic Framework for Denoising Diffusion Models: Entropy, Transport, and Reverse Processes
Swagatam Das

Abstract
This paper develops a rigorous probabilistic framework that extends denoising diffusion models to the setting of noncommutative random variables. Building on Voiculescu’s theory of free entropy and free Fisher information, we formulate diffusion and reverse processes governed by operator-valued stochastic dynamics whose spectral measures evolve by additive convolution. Using tools from free stochastic analysis—including a Malliavin calculus and a Clark–Ocone representation—we derive the reverse-time stochastic differential equation driven by the conjugate variable, the analogue of the classical score function. The resulting dynamics admit a gradient-flow structure in the noncommutative Wasserstein space, establishing an information-geometric link between entropy production, transport, and deconvolution. We further construct a variational scheme analogous to the Jordan–Kinderlehrer–Otto (JKO) formulation and prove convergence toward the semicircular equilibrium. The framework provides functional inequalities (free logarithmic Sobolev, Talagrand, and HWI) that quantify entropy dissipation and Wasserstein contraction. These results unify diffusion-based generative modeling with the geometry of operator-valued information, offering a mathematical foundation for generative learning on structured and high-dimensional data.

I-B

Information-Theoretic Implications

The correspondence between free and classical entropy flows reveals a deep
information-theoretic parallel. In classical diffusion-based generative
models, the Fokker–Planck evolution drives Shannon entropy increase,
and the reverse-time process recovers structure by following the gradient of
the log-likelihood—a manifestation of entropy dissipation balanced by
Fisher information flow. In the free setting, Voiculescu’s free entropy

\chi(\mu_{t})
obeys an analogous dissipation law

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\chi(\mu_{t})=\tfrac{1}{2}\Phi^{\ast}(\mu_{t})
,
where
\Phi^{\ast}
is the free Fisher information.
This duality mirrors the de Bruijn identity and encodes how spectral measures
lose and regain information under noncommutative diffusion. The reverse free
SDE thus performs an information-preserving transport analogous to
generative decoding, while the forward flow acts as a compression map toward
a semicircular prior. Consequently, free diffusion models naturally admit a
rate–distortion interpretation: entropy decay quantifies compression of
operator-valued data, and the reverse flow reconstructs high-entropy signals
along optimal information-geometric trajectories.

Taken together, these results position
free diffusion models
as Wasserstein gradient flows of Voiculescu’s free entropy, unifying stochastic diffusion, functional inequalities, and noncommutative information geometry under a single rigorous framework. Beyond serving as a conceptual extension of classical diffusion models, this theory introduces new analytical tools for operator-valued and high-dimensional structured data, opening a research program at the intersection of free probability, optimal transport, and information-theoretic generative modeling.

II
Classical Diffusion Models and DDPMs

Diffusion probabilistic models, or
denoising diffusion probabilistic models (DDPMs)
, have become one of the most powerful frameworks in modern generative modeling.
Their central idea is conceptually simple yet mathematically deep: a forward stochastic process gradually destroys the structure of complex data by adding noise, while a reverse stochastic process learns to reconstruct the data distribution from pure noise.
Together, these two processes define a generative model capable of sampling from highly structured, high-dimensional distributions such as natural images, audio, or molecular configurations.

Formally, the model is built around a pair of stochastic processes

\{x_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}

(forward) and

\{x_{t}\}_{t\leq T}

(reverse) evolving in
\mathbb{R}^{d}

[
1
,
3
]
.
The forward process serves as a data–to–noise map, while the reverse process acts as a noise–to–data decoder governed by a learned stochastic differential equation.

II-A

Forward Process: From Data to Noise

In the forward direction, the model progressively perturbs the data by adding Gaussian noise in small increments.
The simplest discrete-time version can be written as

x_{t}=\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}\,x_{t-1}+\sqrt{1-\alpha_{t}}\,\epsilon_{t},\qquad\epsilon_{t}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I),

(1)

where
\alpha_{t}=1-\beta_{t}
and
\beta_{t}
is a small variance parameter controlling the noise level at step
t
.
Equation (
1
) defines a time-inhomogeneous Markov chain that slowly “forgets” the initial sample
x_{0}
as
t
increases.

Iterating (
1
) yields an explicit marginal form:

x_{t}=\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_{t}}\,x_{0}+\sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_{t}}\,\epsilon,\qquad\epsilon\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I),

(2)

where
\bar{\alpha}_{t}=\prod_{s=1}^{t}\alpha_{s}
.
As
t
grows,
\bar{\alpha}_{t}\to 0
and
x_{t}
converges in distribution to a standard Gaussian.
Thus, forward diffusion transforms any complex data distribution into a tractable high-entropy Gaussian reference—an essential property for generative modeling.

Continuous-Time Limit.

When the discrete time steps become infinitesimal, the Markov chain converges to a continuous diffusion process governed by the
Ornstein–Uhlenbeck SDE

[
21
]
:

\mathrm{d}x_{s}=-\tfrac{1}{2}\beta(s)\,x_{s}\,\mathrm{d}s+\sqrt{\beta(s)}\,\mathrm{d}W_{s},

(3)

where
W_{s}
is a standard Brownian motion.
The drift term
-\tfrac{1}{2}\beta(s)x_{s}
gently pulls the state toward the origin, while the diffusion term injects Gaussian noise at rate
\beta(s)
.
The associated Fokker–Planck equation shows that the density of
x_{s}
converges to the standard normal distribution as
s\to T
.

II-B

Reverse Process: From Noise Back to Data

The generative phase inverts the above diffusion.
Intuitively, if the forward process gradually corrupted the data with Gaussian noise, then the reverse process must remove that noise in a statistically optimal way.
The reverse-time dynamics can be derived rigorously using time-reversal results for diffusion processes (the Haussmann–Pardoux theorem
[
22
]
), yielding:

\mathrm{d}x_{s}=\Bigl[-\tfrac{1}{2}\beta(s)\,x_{s}-\beta(s)\nabla\log p_{s}(x_{s})\Bigr]\,\mathrm{d}s+\sqrt{\beta(s)}\,\mathrm{d}\overleftarrow{W}_{s},

(4)

where
p_{s}(x)
is the marginal density of
x_{s}
, and
\overleftarrow{W}_{s}
denotes Brownian motion adapted to the reverse filtration.

The additional term
-\beta(s)\nabla\log p_{s}(x_{s})
represents a data-dependent correction that exactly counteracts the injected noise.
This
score function

\nabla\log p_{s}(x)
is generally unknown but can be estimated by a neural network trained to predict the gradient of the log-density.
The resulting framework, known as a
score-based generative model
, unifies DDPMs and stochastic differential equation (SDE) methods
[
3
,
23
]
.

In generation mode, the learned network drives the reverse SDE (
4
) starting from Gaussian noise, producing high-dimensional samples that follow the learned data distribution.

II-C

Information-Theoretic Viewpoint

The forward SDE (
3
) induces a density
p_{t}(x)
satisfying the Fokker–Planck equation:

\frac{\partial p_{t}}{\partial t}=\frac{1}{2}\nabla\cdot\!\big(\beta(t)x\,p_{t}\big)+\frac{1}{2}\beta(t)\,\Delta p_{t}.

(5)

This equation can be viewed as a
gradient flow of entropy
in the
L_{2}
-Wasserstein space of probability measures.
Indeed, the Shannon differential entropy

H(p_{t})=-\int p_{t}(x)\log p_{t}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x

increases monotonically over time according to the classical
de Bruijn identity
:

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}H(p_{t})=\frac{1}{2}\,\beta(t)\,\mathcal{I}(p_{t}),

(6)

where
\mathcal{I}(p_{t})=\int\|\nabla\log p_{t}(x)\|^{2}p_{t}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x
is the Fisher information.
Thus, the forward diffusion performs a gradient ascent on entropy, continuously spreading probability mass until it reaches the Gaussian equilibrium.
Conversely, the reverse process (
4
) can be viewed as a
steepest descent of entropy
, guiding the system back toward a structured, low-entropy data distribution.

This duality between entropy production (forward) and entropy dissipation (reverse) provides a geometric and information-theoretic foundation for diffusion-based generative modeling.
It also sets the stage for the noncommutative generalization developed later in this paper, where Shannon entropy and Fisher information are replaced by their free-probabilistic counterparts.

Diffusion models have rapidly emerged as one of the most influential frameworks in modern artificial intelligence, reshaping both the theoretical and practical landscape of generative modeling. Their success stems from a simple but powerful principle: by learning to reverse a gradual corruption process that transforms structured data into noise, these models can synthesize entirely new samples that are statistically indistinguishable from real data.
Recent surveys such as

Diffusion-based architectures now dominate large-scale generative modeling. Systems such as

Conditional and controllable synthesis:

By integrating classifier guidance, cross-modal conditioning, or attention-based mechanisms, diffusion models enable text-to-image, image-to-speech, and even multimodal generation.
The probabilistic structure of these models naturally accommodates conditioning and interpolation, providing a flexible platform for controllable synthesis and style transfer.

•

Bayesian inference and uncertainty quantification:

The connection between diffusion models and stochastic control

allows them to perform approximate Bayesian inference.
Score-based sampling schemes provide uncertainty estimates and enable posterior sampling in inverse problems, leading to applications in reinforcement learning, climate modeling, and medical imaging.

•

Scientific and physical simulation:

Diffusion-based stochastic dynamics have been successfully applied to model complex physical phenomena,

Recent works have demonstrated diffusion generative models for

In these cases, diffusion models act as statistical surrogates for

Theoretical significance.

From a mathematical standpoint, diffusion models exhibit a remarkable universality:
the forward diffusion maps any data distribution to a simple Gaussian reference, while the reverse-time SDE—parameterized by the score function—can, in principle, recover any target distribution.
This bidirectional structure links the theory of diffusion models to the geometry of the Wasserstein space and to entropy-regularized optimal transport

It also provides a continuous-time view of deep generative learning that bridges stochastic control, variational inference, and information geometry.

Motivation for the free probabilistic framework.

However, many emerging machine learning settings involve data that are inherently

Examples include covariance matrices in signal processing, weight matrices in deep networks, density operators in quantum information, and graph Laplacians in network analysis.
In such regimes, classical diffusion models—built on Euclidean and commutative probability—cannot capture the higher-order spectral interactions or non-commuting dependencies that drive these systems.

The free probabilistic framework developed in this paper extends diffusion modeling into this operator-valued domain.
By replacing Gaussian noise with

Ultimately, free diffusion models bridge stochastic generative modeling with the deep structure of random matrix theory and noncommutative information geometry, suggesting a unified perspective on data generation across classical and operator-valued domains.

III
Free Probability Formulation

Classical diffusion-based generative models operate in Euclidean probability spaces, where random variables commute and independence is described by factorization of expectations.
However, in many high-dimensional or structured learning systems—such as deep neural networks, random feature maps, or transformer attention operators—the effective random objects are not scalars but
matrices or operators
.
Their entries are highly correlated, yet their large-dimensional behavior often exhibits a form of
asymptotic freeness
rather than classical independence.
This observation motivates the use of
free probability theory
as a natural noncommutative extension of diffusion modeling.

Free probability, introduced by Voiculescu
[
11
,
12
]
, provides an operator-algebraic framework for describing random variables that do not commute but still exhibit a form of “statistical independence” called
freeness
.
It has deep connections to random matrix theory, where empirical spectral distributions converge to free convolutions in the large-dimensional limit
[
15
]
.
In this setting, diffusion can be viewed not as the evolution of a density in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, but as the evolution of a
spectral measure
under free additive convolution.

III-A

Free Probability Background

Let
(\mathcal{A},\varphi)
be a tracial
W^{*}
-probability space, where
\mathcal{A}
is a von Neumann algebra acting on a Hilbert space, and
\varphi\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\mathcal{A}\to\mathbb{C}
is a faithful, normal, tracial state.
Elements of
\mathcal{A}
play the role of (possibly non-commuting) random variables.
For a self-adjoint element
X\in\mathcal{A}
, the
spectral theorem
associates a probability measure
\mu_{X}
on
\mathbb{R}
, called the
spectral law
of
X
, such that

\varphi(f(X))=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(\lambda)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{X}(\lambda)\quad\text{for every bounded Borel function }f.

This spectral law generalizes the notion of a probability density to the noncommutative world.

III-B

Freeness

Freeness generalizes classical independence by eliminating mixed moments.
Subalgebras
\mathcal{A}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{A}_{m}\subseteq\mathcal{A}
are said to be
freely independent
(or simply
free
) if

\varphi(a_{1}a_{2}\cdots a_{k})=0

whenever
a_{j}\in\mathcal{A}_{i_{j}}
,
\varphi(a_{j})=0
, and consecutive indices differ (
i_{1}\neq i_{2}\neq\cdots\neq i_{k}
).
This property means that alternating, centered elements from distinct subalgebras have zero expectation—the noncommutative analogue of the classical product rule
\mathbb{E}[XY]=\mathbb{E}[X]\mathbb{E}[Y]
for independent variables.

III-C

Free Brownian Motion and Semicircular Law

A
free Brownian motion
(or
semicircular process
) is a family
\{S_{t}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}t\geq 0\}
of self-adjoint operators satisfying:

1.

S_{0}=0
;

2.

the increments
S_{t_{j}}-S_{t_{j-1}}
are freely independent for
t_{1}<\cdots<t_{n}
;

3.

each increment
S_{t}-S_{s}
has a
semicircular law
with density

\mathrm{d}\mu_{S_{t}-S_{s}}(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi(t-s)}\sqrt{4(t-s)-x^{2}}\,\mathbf{1}_{[-2\sqrt{t-s},\,2\sqrt{t-s}]}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x,

so that
\varphi((S_{t}-S_{s})^{2})=t-s
and all higher free cumulants vanish except the second.

In analogy with classical Brownian motion, the free process
\{S_{t}\}
describes “semicircular noise” whose increments are freely independent rather than classically independent.

III-D

Free Diffusion as a Model of Operator-Valued Noise

Consider now a noncommutative diffusion process

\mathrm{d}X_{t}=b_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t+\sigma_{t}\,\mathrm{d}S_{t},

where
X_{t},b_{t},\sigma_{t}\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{sa}}
and
S_{t}
is free Brownian motion.
The spectral measure
\mu_{X_{t}}
evolves by free additive convolution—a noncommutative analogue of Gaussian convolution.
When
b_{t}=-\tfrac{1}{2}\beta(t)X_{t}
and
\sigma_{t}=\sqrt{\beta(t)}
, this becomes a
free Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process

[
19
]
, whose stationary law is the semicircular distribution.

This provides a direct parallel to the forward process in classical diffusion models: the random matrix (or operator)
X_{t}
becomes increasingly “semicircular” as
t
increases, corresponding to entropy growth under the free Fokker–Planck equation.
The reverse process, derived in Section VI, then reconstructs the initial spectral distribution by introducing the conjugate variable
\Xi_{t}
, which acts as the
free analogue of the score function
.

III-E

Example: Free DDPM for Random Matrix Data

To illustrate the connection between diffusion models and free probability, consider a random matrix analogue of a classical denoising diffusion process.
Let
X_{0}^{(N)}\in\mathbb{C}^{N\times N}
be a Hermitian data matrix—such as the empirical covariance or attention kernel from a neural network layer—whose eigenvalue distribution represents the structural information to be learned.
The forward “noising” process is defined as

X_{t}^{(N)}=\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}\,X_{0}^{(N)}+\sqrt{1-\alpha_{t}}\,Z_{t}^{(N)},\qquad Z_{t}^{(N)}\sim\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\,G_{t},

where
G_{t}
has i.i.d. standard Gaussian entries and
\alpha_{t}=\exp(-\!\int_{0}^{t}\beta(s)\,\mathrm{d}s)
is a prescribed variance schedule.
The term
Z_{t}^{(N)}
introduces isotropic matrix-valued noise, while
\beta(t)
controls the diffusion rate.

As the dimension
N\to\infty
, Voiculescu’s
asymptotic freeness theorem
implies that the random matrices

X_{t}^{(N)}
and
Z_{t}^{(N)}
become freely independent in the sense of moments.
Consequently, the empirical spectral distribution of
X_{t}^{(N)}
converges to that of an operator-valued process
X_{t}
satisfying the free Ornstein–Uhlenbeck equation

\mathrm{d}X_{t}=-\tfrac{1}{2}\beta(t)X_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t+\sqrt{\beta(t)}\,\mathrm{d}S_{t},

where
S_{t}
is a free Brownian motion on a tracial
W^{*}
-probability space
(\mathcal{A},\varphi)
.
The spectral measure
\mu_{X_{t}}
evolves by free additive convolution:

\mu_{X_{t}}=\mu_{X_{0}}\boxplus\sigma_{\Lambda(t)},\quad\Lambda(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\beta(s)\,\mathrm{d}s,

and approaches the semicircular law
\sigma
as
t\to\infty
.

In this interpretation, the forward free diffusion gradually erases the spectral structure of the data by adding freely independent “semicircular noise”—the free analogue of Gaussian perturbations in Euclidean DDPMs.
The reverse-time process, driven by the conjugate variable
\Xi_{t}=\nabla_{X_{t}}\chi(X_{t})
, acts as a
free denoiser
: it reconstructs the original spectral law
\mu_{X_{0}}
by performing free deconvolution with respect to
\sigma_{\Lambda(t)}
.

Practically, one can view a free DDPM as a generative model operating on spectra of large matrices rather than raw data vectors. During “training,” the model learns an approximation to the conjugate variable
\Xi_{t}
, analogous to the score function in classical diffusion models. At generation time, the learned reverse dynamics sample new operator-valued data by evolving from the high-entropy semicircular prior back toward a structured spectral law.

This free-probabilistic viewpoint thus provides a principled description of how diffusion mechanisms might act on non-commuting or operator-valued representations—such as large attention matrices, kernel operators, or graph Laplacians—where classical independence assumptions no longer hold.

III-F

Summary

Free probability thus provides a rigorous language for describing diffusion in noncommutative or matrix-valued settings.
The forward process corresponds to the flow of spectral measures under free additive convolution, while the reverse process represents an entropy-gradient descent in the geometry of free Wasserstein space.
This framework extends the statistical mechanics interpretation of DDPMs to the realm of operator algebras, offering a foundation for analyzing and potentially designing generative models for structured, non-commuting data.

IV
Forward Diffusion as a Free Ornstein–Uhlenbeck Process

Proposition 1

(Free forward SDE)
.

The forward diffusion in the free probability setting is governed by the stochastic differential equation

dX_{s}=-\frac{1}{2}\beta(s)\,X_{s}\,ds+\sqrt{\beta(s)}\,dS_{s},

(7)

where
S_{s}
denotes a free Brownian motion (also called a semicircular process).

Proof.

Consider the discrete-time update

X_{t}=\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}\,X_{t-1}+\sqrt{1-\alpha_{t}}\,Z_{t},

where
Z_{t}
are centered semicircular elements, freely independent of
X_{t-1}
, and
\alpha_{t}=\exp(-\beta_{t}\Delta s)
for a step size
\Delta s
.

As
\Delta s\to 0
, the law of
X_{t}
evolves via a free additive convolution at each step, with a simultaneous scaling (dilation) by
\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}
. By standard arguments for free convolution semigroups and the Trotter–Kato approximation in the free setting, the infinitesimal generator of this evolution acts on noncommutative polynomials
f
as

\mathcal{L}_{s}f=-\frac{1}{2}\beta(s)\,\mathcal{D}f(X)\cdot X+\frac{1}{2}\beta(s)\,\Delta_{\mathrm{free}}f(X),

where
\mathcal{D}
denotes the free (cyclic) gradient and
\Delta_{\mathrm{free}}
is the free Laplacian.
Passing to the limit
\Delta s\to 0
yields the stochastic differential equation (
7
), which is the free analogue of the classical Ornstein–Uhlenbeck equation.
∎

Remark 1
.

In the commutative case, the forward diffusion corresponds to a classical Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process, whose distribution is given by a Gaussian convolution with exponentially decaying mean. In the free setting, the role of Gaussian convolution is taken by
free additive convolution
, and the spectral measure
\mu_{X_{s}}
of
X_{s}
satisfies

\mu_{X_{s}}=D_{e^{-\frac{1}{2}\Lambda(s)}}\mu_{X_{0}}\boxplus\left(\boxplus_{0<u<s}D_{e^{-\frac{1}{2}(\Lambda(s)-\Lambda(u))}}\mu_{dS_{u}}\right),

where
D_{c}\mu
denotes the pushforward of
\mu
under scaling by
c
,
\Lambda(s)=\int_{0}^{s}\beta(r)\,dr
, and
\boxplus
denotes free additive convolution. This expression makes explicit that the forward process can be viewed as the cumulative effect of a time-rescaled semicircular noise process together with exponential damping of the initial distribution.

V
Free Fokker–Planck Dynamics and Free Clark–Ocone Formula

Once the forward diffusion is lifted from classical to noncommutative
probability, the law of a free diffusion process
X_{s}
evolves according to a

free Fokker–Planck equation
.
Let
\mu_{X_{s}}
denote the spectral measure of
X_{s}
in a tracial

W^{*}
-probability space
(\mathcal{A},\phi)
satisfying

dX_{s}=-\tfrac{1}{2}\beta(s)X_{s}\,ds+\sqrt{\beta(s)}\,dS_{s},

where
(S_{s})_{s\geq 0}
is a free Brownian motion.
Then the family of spectral measures
\{\mu_{X_{s}}\}_{s\geq 0}
obeys, in the
distributional sense,

\frac{\partial\mu_{X_{s}}}{\partial s}(x)=\frac{1}{2}\beta(s)\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\!\left[x\,\mu_{X_{s}}(x)\right]+\frac{1}{2}\beta(s)\frac{\partial^{2}\mu_{X_{s}}}{\partial x^{2}}(x),

(8)

which represents the commutative projection of the noncommutative evolution.
Equation (
8
) is the gradient flow of Voiculescu’s free entropy

\chi(\mu_{X_{s}})
in the metric space

(\mathcal{P}_{2}^{\mathrm{free}},W_{2,\mathrm{free}})

introduced by Biane and Voiculescu
[
33
]
.
Equation (
8
) governs the evolution of the spectral law,
and can be shown to correspond to a gradient flow of Voiculescu’s
free entropy
\chi(\mu_{X_{s}})
in the metric space of spectral distributions
endowed with the free
L_{2}
-Wasserstein distance
[
14
,
34
]
.

The derivation of the
reverse-time SDE
in the free setting
requires conditioning with respect to the free filtration
and exploiting the free analogue of the martingale representation theorem.
This role is played by the
Free Clark–Ocone formula
.

Lemma 1

(Free Clark–Ocone formula)
.

Let
(S_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a free Brownian motion on a tracial
W^{*}
-probability space
(\mathcal{A},\phi)
with canonical filtration
(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
. For any
F\!\in\!L^{2}(\mathcal{A},\phi)
adapted to
(\mathcal{F}_{t})
whose free Malliavin derivative
D_{t}F
belongs to
L^{2}(\mathcal{A}\,\bar{\otimes}\,\mathcal{A},\phi\!\otimes\!\phi)
, there exists a unique adapted process
u_{t}\in L^{2}_{\mathrm{ad}}([0,T];\mathcal{A})
such that

F=\phi[F]+\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}_{\phi}[D_{t}F\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}]\,\#\,dS_{t},

(9)

where
\#
denotes the free stochastic integral and
\mathbb{E}_{\phi}[\cdot\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}]
the conditional expectation in
(\mathcal{A},\phi)
.

The proof follows standard arguments in free Malliavin calculus
and is given in Appendix C (see also
[
14
,
35
]
).

Lemma
1
is the cornerstone of free stochastic calculus:
it allows one to express functionals of the free Brownian motion as stochastic
integrals, which in turn is essential for deriving the drift correction term
in the reverse-time SDE. In later sections, this result will be applied to

\log p_{s}(X_{s})
to obtain the reverse-time drift in terms of the conjugate
variable
\Xi_{s}
, a free analogue of the classical score function.

VI
Reverse-Time Free SDE: Malliavin and PDE Derivations

We now derive the reverse-time dynamics of a free diffusion process using two complementary approaches:
(i) stochastic calculus with the free Clark–Ocone formula, and
(ii) a direct analysis of the free Fokker–Planck equation.
Both lead to the same result.

Backward Free Brownian Motion

We briefly recall the notion of a backward free Brownian motion, which we use in the formulation of the reverse-time SDE.
Let
(\mathcal{A},\tau)
be a tracial
W^{\ast}
-probability space and
(S_{t})_{t\geq 0}
a free Brownian motion adapted to the filtration
(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
.
For a fixed horizon
T>0
, define the reversed filtration by

\mathcal{G}_{s}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\mathcal{F}_{T-s},\quad 0\leq s\leq T.

A process
(\widetilde{S}_{s})_{0\leq s\leq T}
is called a
backward free Brownian motion
if:

1.

(\widetilde{S}_{s})
is adapted to
(\mathcal{G}_{s})
and is a martingale with respect to
(\mathcal{G}_{s})
,

2.

\widetilde{S}_{0}=0
and
\widetilde{S}_{s}-\widetilde{S}_{r}
is
\ast
-free from
\mathcal{G}_{r}
for
0\leq r<s\leq T
,

3.

the increments
\widetilde{S}_{s}-\widetilde{S}_{r}
are semicircular with variance
(s-r)
, and

4.

the quadratic variation satisfies

\langle\widetilde{S}\rangle_{s}=s\,1\quad\text{for all }0\leq s\leq T.

The construction of such processes, together with the free stochastic calculus required for handling them, can be found in the work of Biane and Speicher
[
14
]
. This provides the canonical noise process driving the reverse-time dynamics in the free setting.

Remark 2
.

The role of the backward free Brownian motion is entirely analogous to that of the backward Itô Brownian motion in the classical Haussmann–Pardoux time-reversal framework.
In particular, it provides the martingale noise term that compensates the drift correction in the reverse-time SDE (R).
Thus, once the forward dynamics (F) are fixed, the pair consisting of the reversed drift and the backward free Brownian motion uniquely determines the law of the reversed process.

Before turning to the derivation of the reverse SDE, we recall a key analytical identity connecting
the free Malliavin derivative, the conjugate variable, and the generator of the free
Ornstein–Uhlenbeck semigroup.
This identity generalizes Voiculescu’s polynomial integration-by-parts formula to the full Sobolev
domain and will serve as the bridge between the Malliavin and PDE derivations below.

Lemma 2

(Free integration-by-parts identity beyond polynomial test functions)
.

Let
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a free Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process on a tracial
W^{*}
-probability space
(\mathcal{A},\tau)
,

dX_{t}=-\tfrac{1}{2}X_{t}\,dt+dS_{t},

and let
\mu_{t}
denote its spectral law.
Denote by
\Xi_{t}
the conjugate variable of
\mu_{t}
with respect to
X_{t}
, defined via Voiculescu’s free Fisher information

\Phi^{*}(\mu_{t})=\|\,\Xi_{t}+\tfrac{1}{2}X_{t}\,\|_{L^{2}(\mu_{t})}^{2}.

Then for every
F\in\mathrm{Dom}(D)\subset L^{2}(\mathcal{A},\tau)
whose free Malliavin derivative
DF\in L^{2}(\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{A},\tau\otimes\tau)
exists and satisfies
\|DF\|_{L^{2}}<\infty
, the following integration-by-parts formula holds:

\mathbb{E}_{\tau}\!\left[\langle DF,1\rangle_{\mathrm{free}}\right]=\mathbb{E}_{\tau}\!\left[F\big(\Xi_{t}+\tfrac{1}{2}X_{t}\big)\right],

(10)

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathrm{free}}
denotes the Hilbert–Schmidt inner product on
L^{2}(\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{A},\tau\otimes\tau)
.
Moreover, for all
F,G\in\mathrm{Dom}(D)
, the covariance identity

\mathrm{Cov}_{\tau}(F,G)=\mathbb{E}_{\tau}\!\left[\langle DF,DG\rangle_{\mathrm{free}}\right]=\mathbb{E}_{\tau}\!\left[F\big(\Xi_{t}+\tfrac{1}{2}X_{t}\big)G\right]-\mathbb{E}_{\tau}[F]\,\mathbb{E}_{\tau}[G]

(11)

extends Voiculescu’s classical free integration-by-parts identity from noncommutative polynomials to the full Sobolev space

W^{1,2}_{\mathrm{free}}(\mu_{t})
.

Proof.

Let
F\in\mathrm{Dom}(D)
and approximate it by a sequence of self-adjoint noncommutative polynomials

\{P_{n}(X_{t})\}_{n\geq 1}
such that
P_{n}(X_{t})\to F
in
L^{2}(\mathcal{A},\tau)
and

DP_{n}(X_{t})\to DF
in
L^{2}(\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{A})
.
For each polynomial
P_{n}
, Voiculescu’s original integration-by-parts formula

[
12
, Prop. 7.1]
gives

\tau\!\big(P_{n}^{\prime}(X_{t})\big)=\tau\!\big(P_{n}(X_{t})(\Xi_{t}+\tfrac{1}{2}X_{t})\big).

Passing to the limit
n\to\infty
and using the continuity of the trace pairing

\tau(ab)=\mathbb{E}_{\tau}[ab]
yields (
10
).

The covariance identity (
11
) follows from the product rule of the free Malliavin derivative,

D(FG)=(DF)(1\!\otimes\!G)+(1\!\otimes\!F)(DG)
,
together with the
L^{2}
-duality between
D
and its adjoint, as established in

[
14
, Thm. 3.4]
.
The argument extends from polynomials to
W^{1,2}_{\mathrm{free}}(\mu_{t})

by density and closedness of
D
in
L^{2}(\mathcal{A},\tau)
, proving the lemma.
∎

We now employ this identity to identify the conjugate variable
\Xi_{t}
as the adjoint of the Malliavin derivative
and to construct the drift correction appearing in the reverse-time SDE.

Lemma 3

(Conditional Malliavin derivative and conjugate variable)
.

Let
(\mathcal{A},\varphi)
be a tracial
W^{\ast}
-probability space and

X_{t}\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{sa}}
a self-adjoint random variable whose
law
\mu_{X_{t}}
admits a conjugate variable

\Xi_{t}\in L^{2}(W^{\ast}(X_{t}),\varphi)
.
Then for any noncommutative polynomial
P
,

\mathbb E_{\varphi}[D_{t}P(X_{t})\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}]=P^{\prime}(X_{t})=\mathrm{proj}_{L^{2}(W^{\ast}(X_{t}),\,\varphi)}\!\big(\Xi_{t}P(X_{t})\big),

where
D_{t}
denotes the free Malliavin derivative at time
t
and

\mathcal{F}_{t}=\sigma\{S_{u}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}0\leq u\leq t\}
is the forward filtration.
In particular, for the log-density functional
G=\log\pi_{t}(X_{t})
,

\mathbb E_{\varphi}[D_{t}G\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}]=-\,\Xi_{t},\qquad\Phi^{\ast}(X_{t})=\|\Xi_{t}\|_{2}^{2}.

Proof.

For each polynomial
P
,
P(X_{t})
belongs to
\mathrm{Dom}(D_{t})
,
and the free chain rule
[
14
]
yields

D_{t}P(X_{t})=P^{\prime}(X_{t})
.
Taking conditional expectation onto
\mathcal{F}_{t}
gives

\mathbb E_{\varphi}[D_{t}P(X_{t})\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}]=P^{\prime}(X_{t})
.

By the defining property of the conjugate variable,
for all noncommutative polynomials
Q
,

\varphi\!\big(Q(X_{t})P^{\prime}(X_{t})\big)=\varphi\!\big(Q(X_{t})\Xi_{t}P(X_{t})\big).

Since
L^{2}(W^{\ast}(X_{t}),\varphi)
is the closure of such polynomials,
this equality of inner products implies that

P^{\prime}(X_{t})
is the
L^{2}
-orthogonal projection of

\Xi_{t}P(X_{t})
onto
L^{2}(W^{\ast}(X_{t}),\varphi)
.
Finally, applying this to the log-density functional
G=\log\pi_{t}(X_{t})

and using the definition of
\Phi^{\ast}
via free Fisher information
yields
\mathbb E_{\varphi}[D_{t}G\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}]=-\Xi_{t}
.
∎

Theorem 1

(Reverse-time free SDE)
.

Let
(\mathcal{A},\varphi)
be a tracial
W^{\ast}
-probability space,
let
(S_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a free Brownian motion, and fix
T>0
.
Let
\beta\in C^{1}([0,T];(0,\infty))
be a deterministic variance schedule.
Consider the forward free Ornstein–Uhlenbeck SDE

\mathrm{d}X_{t}=-\tfrac{1}{2}\beta(t)X_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t+\sqrt{\beta(t)}\,\mathrm{d}S_{t},\qquad X_{0}\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{sa}},

(F)

where
\mu_{X_{0}}
is compactly supported with

\chi(\mu_{X_{0}})>-\infty
and
\Phi^{\ast}(\mu_{X_{0}})<\infty
.

(i)

The forward equation (F) admits a unique strong solution

(X_{t})_{0\leq t\leq T}\subset L^{2}(\mathcal{A},\varphi)
in the sense of free Itô
calculus, and for each
t
the spectral law
\mu_{X_{t}}
possesses a conjugate
variable
\Xi_{t}\in L^{2}(W^{\ast}(X_{t}),\varphi)
.
Existence and uniqueness in law follow from Kargin
[
36
]

and Biane–Speicher
[
14
]
.

(ii)

The time-reversed process
\widetilde{X}_{s}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=X_{T-s}
satisfies the

reverse-time free stochastic differential equation

\mathrm{d}\widetilde{X}_{s}=\Bigl(-\tfrac{1}{2}\beta(T-s)\widetilde{X}_{s}-\beta(T-s)\,\Xi_{T-s}\Bigr)\,\mathrm{d}s+\sqrt{\beta(T-s)}\,\mathrm{d}\widetilde{S}_{s},

(R)

where
(\widetilde{S}_{s})_{s\in[0,T]}
is a backward free Brownian motion
adapted to the reversed filtration

\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{s}=\sigma\{\widetilde{X}_{u}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}0\leq u\leq s\}
.
Equation (R) is well posed in
L^{2}(\mathcal{A},\varphi)
and its solution is
unique in law.

Proof.

Step 1: Existence and forward representation.

Let
\Lambda(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\beta(r)\,\mathrm{d}r
.
By the free Itô integration theory
of Biane and Speicher
[
14
]

and the well-posedness results of Kargin
[
36
]
,
the SDE (F) admits a unique strong
L^{2}
solution

X_{t}=e^{-\frac{1}{2}\Lambda(t)}X_{0}+\!\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\frac{1}{2}(\Lambda(t)-\Lambda(u))}\sqrt{\beta(u)}\,\mathrm{d}S_{u},

which satisfies
\|X_{t}\|_{2}<\infty
for all
t\in[0,T]
by the free Itô isometry.

Step 2: Clark–Ocone representation.

For fixed
T
, the free Clark–Ocone formula
(Lemma
1
, proved in Appendix C)
provides the decomposition

X_{T}=\varphi(X_{T})+\int_{0}^{T}u_{t}\,\#\,\mathrm{d}S_{t},\qquad u_{t}=\mathbb E_{\varphi}[D_{t}X_{T}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}].

Inserting the explicit forward solution and applying the free Itô isometry
yields

u_{t}=e^{-\frac{1}{2}(\Lambda(T)-\Lambda(t))}\sqrt{\beta(t)},\qquad t\in[0,T].

Since the increments
(\mathrm{d}S_{t})
are freely independent and
u_{t}
is

\mathcal{F}_{t}
-adapted, conditional expectation removes future stochastic terms,
leaving the deterministic factor
e^{-\frac{1}{2}(\Lambda(T)-\Lambda(t))}
.

Step 3: Identification of the drift correction.

By Lemma
3
, for the log-density functional

G=\log\pi_{t}(X_{t})
we have

\mathbb E_{\varphi}[D_{t}G\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}]=-\,\Xi_{t}
.
Combining this with the forward drift term

-\tfrac{1}{2}\beta(t)X_{t}
in (F)
produces the backward drift

-\tfrac{1}{2}\beta(t)X_{t}-\beta(t)\Xi_{t}
.

Step 4: Time reversal and uniqueness in law.

Define
\widetilde{X}_{s}=X_{T-s}
.
By Biane’s time-reversal duality for free stochastic integrals

[
37
, Prop. 3.1]
,
the process
\widetilde{S}_{s}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=S_{T}-S_{T-s}
is a backward free Brownian motion
adapted to
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{s}
.
Applying free Itô calculus for reversed filtrations yields

\mathrm{d}\widetilde{X}_{s}=\Bigl(-\tfrac{1}{2}\beta(T-s)\widetilde{X}_{s}-\beta(T-s)\,\Xi_{T-s}\Bigr)\mathrm{d}s+\sqrt{\beta(T-s)}\,\mathrm{d}\widetilde{S}_{s}.

The square-integrability of
X_{t}
and
\Xi_{t}

implies integrability of all coefficients, ensuring existence and
uniqueness in law of the solution to (R) in
L^{2}(\mathcal{A},\varphi)

by Kargin’s general theory of free SDEs
[
36
]
.
Hence
\widetilde{X}_{s}
is the unique weak solution of (R),
completing the proof.
∎

Completion of the proof.

To make the time-reversal argument rigorous, recall that under the free Itô
isometry
[
14
, Prop. 3.2]
,

\tau\!\left[\left(\int_{0}^{t}A_{s}\,\#\,dS_{s}\right)\!\left(\int_{0}^{t}B_{s}\,\#\,dS_{s}\right)^{\ast}\right]=\int_{0}^{t}\tau[A_{s}B_{s}^{\ast}]\,ds,

for all square-integrable adapted processes
A_{s},B_{s}
. Since

dS_{t}
has freely independent centered increments and
u_{t}
is adapted,
the conditional expectation
\mathbb E[D_{t}X_{T}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}]

isolates precisely the exponential damping factor

e^{-\frac{1}{2}(\Lambda(T)-\Lambda(t))}\sqrt{\beta(t)}
.
Moreover, the covariance structure

\tau[(S_{t}-S_{s})^{2}]=t-s
implies that the backward process

\widetilde{S}_{s}=S_{T}-S_{T-s}
satisfies

\tau[(\widetilde{S}_{t}-\widetilde{S}_{s})^{2}]=t-s

and has freely independent increments with respect to the reversed filtration.
Hence
\widetilde{S}_{s}
is a backward free Brownian motion in the sense of

[
13
]
. Substituting
dS_{t}=d\widetilde{S}_{T-t}+\sqrt{\beta(t)}\,u_{t}\,dt

into the stochastic integral and using adjointness of
D_{t}
and
\delta

(see Appendix C) yields the reverse SDE (eqn. (R)) rigorously.

Remark 3
.

Both derivations show that the term
\Xi_{s}
plays the role of the noncommutative
score function
in the reverse-time dynamics, and that the reversed diffusion acts as a
free deconvolution flow
.

Corollary: Reverse Drift as a Gradient of Free Entropy

Corollary 1
.

Let
\chi(\mu)
denote Voiculescu’s free entropy functional defined on the spectral distribution
\mu_{X_{s}}
, and let
\Xi_{s}
be the conjugate variable of
X_{s}
. Then

-\tfrac{1}{2}\beta(s)X_{s}-\beta(s)\Xi_{s}=-\beta(s)\nabla\chi[\mu_{X_{s}}](X_{s}),

(12)

where
\nabla\chi[\mu_{X_{s}}](X_{s})=\frac{1}{2}X_{s}+\Xi_{s}
is the
L^{2}
-gradient of
\chi
.

Proof.

The variational derivative of
\chi
implies

\nabla\chi[\mu_{X_{s}}](X_{s})=\frac{1}{2}X_{s}+\Xi_{s},

and substitution into the reverse drift term gives the stated expression.
∎

Remark 4
.

This shows that the reverse-time free diffusion integrates the negative gradient of the free entropy functional, making it a steepest-descent flow on the free entropy landscape.

Detailed proofs of the free de Bruijn identity, Fisher–information flow, and the associated HWI-type inequalities that underlie the information interpretation of the reverse dynamics are provided in Appendix D.

VII
Free Optimal Transport, the JKO Scheme, and a Candidate Gradient Flow Functional

An important extension of the free diffusion framework is to interpret the evolution of spectral distributions as
a
gradient flow
in the space of probability measures endowed with a free Wasserstein metric.
In the commutative case, the seminal work of Jordan, Kinderlehrer, and Otto
[
31
]

introduced the JKO scheme, where solutions to the Fokker–Planck equation are obtained as the limit of a sequence of variational problems in the
L^{2}
Wasserstein metric.
The monograph of Ambrosio, Gigli and Savaré
[
38
]

provides the modern rigorous formulation of these ideas. We propose that an analogous construction is valid in the noncommutative setting.

VII-A

A free JKO scheme.

Let
(\mathcal{P}_{2}^{\mathrm{free}},W_{2,\mathrm{free}})
be the space of compactly supported spectral measures
equipped with the free
L^{2}
Wasserstein distance. Given a free energy functional
\mathcal{F}[\mu]
, let us define the time-discrete scheme

\mu_{k+1}=\arg\min_{\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}^{\mathrm{free}}}\left\{\frac{1}{2\tau}W_{2,\mathrm{free}}^{2}(\nu,\mu_{k})+\mathcal{F}[\nu]\right\},

(13)

where
\tau>0
is the time step.
As
\tau\to 0
, the piecewise-constant interpolation of
(\mu_{k})
is expected to converge to the solution
\mu_{t}
of the free gradient flow

\partial_{t}\mu_{t}+\nabla\cdot\left(\mu_{t}\nabla\frac{\delta\mathcal{F}}{\delta\mu_{t}}\right)=0,

(14)

where the variational derivative
\frac{\delta\mathcal{F}}{\delta\mu}
is interpreted via Voiculescu’s free calculus.

VII-B

A candidate functional for free Wasserstein flows.

Inspired by Voiculescu’s free entropy
[
39
]

and the classical logarithmic energy for random matrices,
a natural choice for
\mathcal{F}
is

\mathcal{F}[\mu]=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{1}{2}x^{2}\,d\mu(x)-\chi[\mu],\qquad\chi[\mu]=\iint\log|x-y|\,d\mu(x)d\mu(y),

(15)

where the first term enforces confinement and the second term promotes dispersion of eigenvalues.
The unique minimizer of
\mathcal{F}
is the semicircular law.

The first variation of
\mathcal{F}
is

\frac{\delta\mathcal{F}}{\delta\mu}(x)=\frac{1}{2}x^{2}-2\int\log|x-y|\,d\mu(y),

(16)

and substituting into the gradient flow equation yields

\partial_{t}\mu_{t}=\nabla\cdot\left(\mu_{t}\nabla\left[\frac{1}{2}x^{2}-2\int\log|x-y|\,d\mu_{t}(y)\right]\right),

(17)

which can be interpreted as the free analogue of the Fokker–Planck equation for the forward diffusion.

VII-C

On the
\Gamma
-limit of the free JKO functionals

We work on the space
\mathcal{P}_{2}^{\mathrm{free}}
of compactly supported probability measures on
\mathbb R
with finite second moment, endowed with the free
L^{2}
-Wasserstein distance
W_{2,\mathrm{free}}
of Biane–Voiculescu
[
16
]
. The free energy is

\mathcal{F}[\nu]\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\frac{1}{2}\int x^{2}\,d\nu(x)\;-\;\chi[\nu],

where
\chi
is Voiculescu’s (microstates) free entropy
[
40
,
41
]
. For
\tau>0
and a fixed
\mu_{k}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}^{\mathrm{free}}
define

\mathcal{F}_{\tau}^{k}[\nu]\;=\;\frac{1}{2\tau}W_{2,\mathrm{free}}^{2}(\nu,\mu_{k})\;+\;\mathcal{F}[\nu].

(18)

We will use the following semicontinuity properties (proved below).

Lemma 4

(Semicontinuity for
W_{2,\mathrm{free}}
and
\chi
)
.

Let
\nu_{n}\rightharpoonup\nu
narrowly and assume
\sup_{n}\int x^{2}\,d\nu_{n}(x)<\infty
.
Then:

1.

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}W_{2,\mathrm{free}}(\nu_{n},\mu)\;\geq\;W_{2,\mathrm{free}}(\nu,\mu)
for every fixed
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}^{\mathrm{free}}
.

2.

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\chi[\nu_{n}]\;\leq\;\chi[\nu]
.

Consequently, the free energy functional
\mathcal{F}
is lower semicontinuous on
\mathcal{P}_{2}^{\mathrm{free}}
with respect to narrow convergence and has tight (hence relatively compact) sublevel sets.

Proof.

(i) Lower semicontinuity of
W_{2,\mathrm{free}}
..

Let
\nu_{n},\nu,\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}^{\mathrm{free}}
, and let
\Gamma(\nu_{n},\mu)
denote the set of all admissible
free couplings
in the sense of Biane–Voiculescu
[
16
]
, that is, tracial
W^{*}
-probability spaces
(\mathcal{A}_{n},\tau_{n})
containing self-adjoint pairs
(X_{n},Y_{n})
with marginal laws
\nu_{n}
and
\mu
.
The free
L_{2}
–Wasserstein distance is defined as

W_{2,\mathrm{free}}^{2}(\nu_{n},\mu)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\inf_{(X_{n},Y_{n})\in\Gamma(\nu_{n},\mu)}\tau_{n}\!\left[(X_{n}-Y_{n})^{2}\right].

Let
(X_{n},Y_{n})
be a sequence of (almost-)optimal couplings satisfying

\tau_{n}\!\left[(X_{n}-Y_{n})^{2}\right]\leq W_{2,\mathrm{free}}^{2}(\nu_{n},\mu)+\tfrac{1}{n}.

Since
\sup_{n}\int x^{2}\,d\nu_{n}(x)<\infty
, the laws
\{\nu_{n}\}
are uniformly tight and have uniformly bounded second moments, hence precompact in the narrow topology.
Thus there exists a subsequence
(\nu_{n_{k}})
converging narrowly to
\nu
.
For each such subsequence, the family of joint laws
\{\mathrm{Law}(X_{n_{k}},Y_{n_{k}})\}
is also tight on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, allowing extraction of a further subsequence (not relabeled) converging narrowly to a limiting coupling
(X,Y)
of
\nu
and
\mu
in a tracial limit space (see
[
16
, Sec. 1.3]
).

The map
(x,y)\mapsto(x-y)^{2}
is continuous and bounded below by
0
, hence lower semicontinuous.
Applying the Portmanteau theorem to the limit coupling yields

\tau[(X-Y)^{2}]\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\tau_{n_{k}}\!\left[(X_{n_{k}}-Y_{n_{k}})^{2}\right]=\liminf_{k\to\infty}W_{2,\mathrm{free}}^{2}(\nu_{n_{k}},\mu).

By the definition of
W_{2,\mathrm{free}}
as the infimum over all admissible couplings, we have

W_{2,\mathrm{free}}^{2}(\nu,\mu)\leq\tau[(X-Y)^{2}].

Combining the above inequalities proves

\liminf_{n\to\infty}W_{2,\mathrm{free}}(\nu_{n},\mu)\geq W_{2,\mathrm{free}}(\nu,\mu),

which establishes the lower semicontinuity in item
1
.
The argument is the free-probability analogue of the classical lower semicontinuity of
W_{2}
on
\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
, see also
[
38
, Ch. 2]
.

(ii) Upper semicontinuity of
\chi
.

For a compactly supported probability measure
\nu
, recall that Voiculescu’s
microstates free entropy
is defined by

\chi[\nu]\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\inf_{R>0}\,\limsup_{m\to\infty}\Bigg[\frac{1}{m^{2}}\log\Big(\mathrm{Vol}\big\{A\in M_{m}^{\mathrm{sa}}(\mathbb{C})\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\|A\|\leq R,\,\lambda_{A}\approx\nu\big\}\Big)+\frac{3}{4}+\frac{1}{2}\log(2\pi)\Bigg],

where
\lambda_{A}
denotes the empirical spectral law of
A
.
Let
\nu_{n}\rightharpoonup\nu
narrowly with
\sup_{n}\int x^{2}\,d\nu_{n}(x)<\infty
.
For each fixed
R>0
, the sets of
m\times m
microstates
\Gamma_{m}(\nu_{n};R,\varepsilon)
corresponding to
\nu_{n}
converge in the Hausdorff sense (with respect to the normalized trace topology) to
\Gamma_{m}(\nu;R,\varepsilon)
as
n\to\infty
, because narrow convergence with uniformly bounded second moments implies convergence of all
*
-moments of bounded degree.
By the monotonicity of
\mathrm{Vol}[\Gamma_{m}(\nu;R,\varepsilon)]
in
R
and
\varepsilon
, and the subadditivity of
\frac{1}{m^{2}}\log\mathrm{Vol}
in
m
, one can apply a diagonal argument in
(m,R,\varepsilon)
to pass to the limit (see
[
41
, Thm. 6.5.1]
and
[
40
]
), obtaining

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\chi[\nu_{n}]\leq\chi[\nu].

Hence, the free entropy is upper semicontinuous along narrowly convergent sequences with uniformly bounded second moments.

(iii) Consequences.

Since the free energy functional
\mathcal{F}[\nu]\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\tfrac{1}{2}W_{2,\mathrm{free}}^{2}(\nu,\mu_{\mathrm{sc}})-\chi[\nu]
is the sum of a lower semicontinuous and an upper semicontinuous term, it is itself lower semicontinuous on
\mathcal{P}_{2}^{\mathrm{free}}
. The moment bound guarantees tightness of sublevel sets of
\mathcal{F}
, which are therefore relatively compact in the narrow topology. This completes the proof. ∎

We consider the free energy

\mathcal{F}[\nu]\;=\;\frac{1}{2}\int x^{2}\,d\nu(x)\;-\;\chi[\nu],

defined on the space
\mathcal{P}_{2}^{\mathrm{free}}
of compactly supported probability measures with finite second moment. We assume the semicontinuity facts collected in Lemma
4
(lower semicontinuity of
W_{2,\mathrm{free}}
, upper semicontinuity of the microstates free entropy under the indicated compactness assumptions). The goal is to show the Moreau–Yosida envelopes

\mathcal{F}^{\tau}(\mu)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\inf_{\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}^{\mathrm{free}}}\Big\{\frac{1}{2\tau}W_{2,\mathrm{free}}^{2}(\nu,\mu)+\mathcal{F}[\nu]\Big\}

(19)

satisfy
\Gamma
-convergence
\mathcal{F}^{\tau}\xrightarrow{\Gamma}\mathcal{F}
(narrow topology) as
\tau\downarrow 0
, and that the minimizers of
\mathcal{F}^{\tau}
are precompact and converge to the minimizers of
\mathcal{F}
. We then explain how this controls the JKO scheme with moving the base point.

Theorem 2

(
\Gamma
-convergence of
\mathcal{F}^{\tau}
to
\mathcal{F}
)
.

Assume Lemma
4
holds. For each
\tau>0
define
\mathcal{F}^{\tau}
by (
19
). Then:

1.

For every
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}^{\mathrm{free}}
,

\lim_{\tau\downarrow 0}\mathcal{F}^{\tau}(\mu)=\mathcal{F}(\mu).

2.

The functionals
\mathcal{F}^{\tau}

\Gamma
-converge to
\mathcal{F}
on
\mathcal{P}_{2}^{\mathrm{free}}
with respect to narrow convergence.

3.

(Equi-coercivity and convergence of minimizers) For any sequence
\tau_{n}\downarrow 0
, any sequence
\mu_{n}
with

\mathcal{F}^{\tau_{n}}(\mu_{n})\leq C
is relatively compact (narrow). Moreover, any limit point
\mu
satisfies
\mathcal{F}(\mu)\leq\liminf_{n}\mathcal{F}^{\tau_{n}}(\mu_{n})
. In particular, minimizers of
\mathcal{F}^{\tau}
converge (subsequentially) to minimizers of
\mathcal{F}
.

Proof.

We will prove the three items in order. The proof strategy is standard (cf. Ambrosio–Gigli–Savaré
[
38
, Ch. 4]
), but we carefully check each step in the free setting using Lemma
4
.

(I) Pointwise convergence
\mathcal{F}^{\tau}(\mu)\to\mathcal{F}(\mu)
.

For any fixed
\mu
, the choice
\nu=\mu
in (
19
) yields
\mathcal{F}^{\tau}(\mu)\leq\mathcal{F}(\mu)
for every
\tau>0
. Thus
\limsup_{\tau\downarrow 0}\mathcal{F}^{\tau}(\mu)\leq\mathcal{F}(\mu)
.

To prove the reverse inequality, fix
\mu
and let
\nu_{\tau}
be
\varepsilon_{\tau}
-minimizers of
\mathcal{F}^{\tau}(\mu)
, i.e.

\mathcal{F}^{\tau}(\mu)\;\geq\;\frac{1}{2\tau}W_{2,\mathrm{free}}^{2}(\nu_{\tau},\mu)+\mathcal{F}[\nu_{\tau}]\;-\;\varepsilon_{\tau},\qquad\varepsilon_{\tau}\downarrow 0.

We show that the transport cost term must vanish as
\tau\downarrow 0
. Assume, for contradiction, that

\liminf_{\tau\to 0}\frac{1}{2\tau}W_{2,\mathrm{free}}^{2}(\nu_{\tau},\mu)>0.

Then there exist
\delta>0
and a sequence
(\tau_{k})_{k\geq 1}\downarrow 0
such that

\frac{1}{2\tau_{k}}W_{2,\mathrm{free}}^{2}(\nu_{\tau_{k}},\mu)\geq\delta\quad\text{for all }k.

hence the right-hand side would be bounded away from
\mathcal{F}(\mu)
unless
\mathcal{F}[\nu_{\tau}]\to-\infty
. The coercivity of
\mathcal{F}
(the
\tfrac{1}{2}\int x^{2}
term dominates negative entropy for compactly supported measures) prevents
\mathcal{F}[\nu_{\tau}]
from going to
-\infty
while keeping
W_{2,\mathrm{free}}(\nu_{\tau},\mu)
bounded; therefore the transport term must vanish:

\lim_{\tau\downarrow 0}\frac{1}{2\tau}W_{2,\mathrm{free}}^{2}(\nu_{\tau},\mu)=0.

Consequently
W_{2,\mathrm{free}}(\nu_{\tau},\mu)=o(\sqrt{\tau})
and in particular
\nu_{\tau}\rightharpoonup\mu
. By lower semicontinuity of the quadratic moment and the upper semicontinuity of
\chi
(Lemma
4
), we obtain

\liminf_{\tau\downarrow 0}\mathcal{F}^{\tau}(\mu)\geq\liminf_{\tau\downarrow 0}\Big(\mathcal{F}[\nu_{\tau}]-\varepsilon_{\tau}\Big)\geq\mathcal{F}[\mu].

Combining with the earlier
\limsup
inequality yields
\lim_{\tau\downarrow 0}\mathcal{F}^{\tau}(\mu)=\mathcal{F}(\mu)
.

(II)
\Gamma
-convergence.
We now establish the liminf and limsup conditions for
\Gamma
-convergence.

Liminf inequality.
Let
\mu_{\tau}\rightharpoonup\mu
narrowly. By definition,

\mathcal{F}^{\tau}(\mu_{\tau})=\inf_{\nu}\Big\{\frac{1}{2\tau}W_{2,\mathrm{free}}^{2}(\nu,\mu_{\tau})+\mathcal{F}[\nu]\Big\}\leq\frac{1}{2\tau}W_{2,\mathrm{free}}^{2}(\nu_{0},\mu_{\tau})+\mathcal{F}[\nu_{0}],

for any fixed competitor
\nu_{0}
. Taking
\limsup_{\tau\downarrow 0}
and using lower semicontinuity of
W_{2,\mathrm{free}}
(Lemma 3) yields

\limsup_{\tau\downarrow 0}\mathcal{F}^{\tau}(\mu_{\tau})\leq\frac{1}{2\tau}W_{2,\mathrm{free}}^{2}(\nu_{0},\mu)+\mathcal{F}[\nu_{0}].

This is not directly helpful for liminf; instead use the following classical selection: let
\nu_{\tau}
be
\varepsilon_{\tau}
-minimizers for
\mathcal{F}^{\tau}(\mu_{\tau})
, so

\mathcal{F}^{\tau}(\mu_{\tau})\geq\frac{1}{2\tau}W_{2,\mathrm{free}}^{2}(\nu_{\tau},\mu_{\tau})+\mathcal{F}[\nu_{\tau}]-\varepsilon_{\tau}.

We must extract compactness for
(\nu_{\tau})
. Assume for contradiction that the sequence
(\nu_{\tau})
is not tight: then the quadratic moment
\int x^{2}\,d\nu_{\tau}
is unbounded and thus
\mathcal{F}[\nu_{\tau}]\to+\infty
(coercivity), contradicting that
\mathcal{F}^{\tau}(\mu_{\tau})
is finite. Hence
(\nu_{\tau})
is tight and, up to a subsequence,
\nu_{\tau}\rightharpoonup\nu
. By Lemma 3 and lower semicontinuity of the moment,

\liminf_{\tau\downarrow 0}\frac{1}{2\tau}W_{2,\mathrm{free}}^{2}(\nu_{\tau},\mu_{\tau})\geq\liminf_{\tau\downarrow 0}\frac{1}{2\tau}\big(W_{2,\mathrm{free}}(\nu,\mu)-o(1)\big)^{2}=\begin{cases}0,&\nu=\mu,\\
+\infty,&\nu\neq\mu.\end{cases}

Thus, two possibilities arise:

(i) If
\nu\neq\mu
, then
\liminf_{\tau}\mathcal{F}^{\tau}(\mu_{\tau})=+\infty\geq\mathcal{F}(\mu)
and the liminf inequality holds trivially.

(ii) If
\nu=\mu
, then by Lemma 3 and the lower semicontinuity of the quadratic moment,

\liminf_{\tau\downarrow 0}\mathcal{F}^{\tau}(\mu_{\tau})\geq\liminf_{\tau\downarrow 0}\Big(\mathcal{F}[\nu_{\tau}]-\varepsilon_{\tau}\Big)\geq\mathcal{F}[\mu],

establishing the liminf inequality in this case as well.

Hence in all cases
\liminf_{\tau\downarrow 0}\mathcal{F}^{\tau}(\mu_{\tau})\geq\mathcal{F}(\mu)
.

Limsup inequality (existence of recovery sequences).
Fix
\mu
. We must construct
\mu_{\tau}\rightharpoonup\mu
with
\limsup_{\tau}\mathcal{F}^{\tau}(\mu_{\tau})\leq\mathcal{F}(\mu)
. A trivial and effective choice is the constant sequence
\mu_{\tau}\equiv\mu
. Then

\mathcal{F}^{\tau}(\mu_{\tau})=\mathcal{F}^{\tau}(\mu)\leq\mathcal{F}(\mu)

(since
\nu=\mu
is admissible in the infimum for
\mathcal{F}^{\tau}(\mu)
). Taking
\limsup
gives
\limsup_{\tau\downarrow 0}\mathcal{F}^{\tau}(\mu_{\tau})\leq\mathcal{F}(\mu)
. This completes the limsup construction.

Thus,
\mathcal{F}^{\tau}

\Gamma
-converges to
\mathcal{F}
.

(III) Equi-coercivity and convergence of minimizers.
Let
\tau_{n}\downarrow 0
and
\mu_{n}
satisfy
\mathcal{F}^{\tau_{n}}(\mu_{n})\leq C
. For each
n
, choose
\nu_{n}
with

\mathcal{F}^{\tau_{n}}(\mu_{n})\geq\frac{1}{2\tau_{n}}W_{2,\mathrm{free}}^{2}(\nu_{n},\mu_{n})+\mathcal{F}[\nu_{n}]-\varepsilon_{n}.

The boundedness of
\mathcal{F}^{\tau_{n}}(\mu_{n})
and the coercivity of
\mathcal{F}
imply that the second moments of
\nu_{n}
are uniformly bounded; since
W_{2,\mathrm{free}}^{2}(\nu_{n},\mu_{n})\geq 0
, it follows that the second moments of
\mu_{n}
are also uniformly bounded (by the triangle inequality in the metric space). Hence
(\mu_{n})
is tight and thus relatively compact in the narrow topology. By
\Gamma
-convergence and the fundamental theorem of
\Gamma
-convergence, any limit point of minimizers of
\mathcal{F}^{\tau_{n}}
is a minimizer of
\mathcal{F}
. This proves the final claim.
∎

Consequences for the JKO/minimizing-movement scheme.

Let
\tau>0
and define the discrete JKO iteration starting from
\mu_{0}
:

\mu_{k+1}^{\tau}\in\arg\min_{\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}^{\mathrm{free}}}\Big\{\frac{1}{2\tau}W_{2,\mathrm{free}}^{2}(\nu,\mu_{k}^{\tau})+\mathcal{F}[\nu]\Big\}.

(20)

Theorems
2
(
\Gamma
-convergence of
\mathcal{F}^{\tau}
) and standard minimizing-movement compactness arguments (see Ambrosio–Gigli–Savaré
[
38
, Ch. 4]
) imply the following.

Having established the
\Gamma
-convergence of the discrete functionals
\mathcal{F}_{\tau}
to
\mathcal{F}
, we now turn to the dynamic aspect of the minimizing-movement scheme. The next theorem shows that the sequence of discrete minimizers obtained from the JKO recursion not only exists for every time step
\tau>0
, but also converges—as
\tau\downarrow 0
—to an absolutely continuous limit curve that solves the free Wasserstein gradient flow of
\mathcal{F}
. This provides the rigorous link between the variational discretization and the constant free diffusion dynamics, closing the loop between the
\Gamma
-limit, compactness, and the evolution equation.

The next lemma refines the preceding
\Gamma
-convergence result by quantifying the stability of discrete minimizers and establishing a uniform energy–dissipation estimate at the discrete level.
This provides the key a priori bounds required for the compactness and convergence arguments in the subsequent theorem.

Lemma 5

(Free
\Gamma
-convergence and Discrete Energy–Dissipation Inequality)
.

Let
\mathcal{F}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\mathcal{P}_{2}^{\mathrm{free}}\!\to(-\infty,+\infty]
be a lower semicontinuous free energy functional with compact sublevel sets, and let

\mathcal{F}_{\tau}(\mu)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\inf_{\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}^{\mathrm{free}}}\Bigl\{\frac{1}{2\tau}W_{2,\mathrm{free}}^{2}(\nu,\mu)+\mathcal{F}[\nu]\Bigr\},\qquad\tau>0.

Then:

(i)

(Free
\Gamma
-convergence)

The family
\{\mathcal{F}_{\tau}\}_{\tau>0}

\Gamma
-converges to
\mathcal{F}
in the narrow topology as
\tau\downarrow 0
, i.e.

\displaystyle\text{(Liminf)}\quad

\displaystyle\mu_{\tau}\rightharpoonup\mu\;\Rightarrow\;\liminf_{\tau\downarrow 0}\mathcal{F}_{\tau}(\mu_{\tau})\geq\mathcal{F}[\mu],

\displaystyle\text{(Limsup)}\quad

\displaystyle\exists\,\mu_{\tau}\rightharpoonup\mu\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\;\limsup_{\tau\downarrow 0}\mathcal{F}_{\tau}(\mu_{\tau})\leq\mathcal{F}[\mu].

(ii)

(Discrete Energy–Dissipation Inequality)

Let
(\mu_{k}^{\tau})_{k\geq 0}
satisfy the free JKO recursion

\mu_{k+1}^{\tau}\in\argmin_{\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}^{\mathrm{free}}}\left\{\frac{1}{2\tau}W_{2,\mathrm{free}}^{2}(\nu,\mu_{k}^{\tau})+\mathcal{F}[\nu]\right\}.

Suppose
\mathcal{F}
is
\lambda
-geodesically convex in
(\mathcal{P}_{2}^{\mathrm{free}},W_{2,\mathrm{free}})
.
Then the discrete minimizers satisfy the
quantitative discrete EDI

\mathcal{F}[\mu_{k+1}^{\tau}]+\frac{1+\lambda\tau}{2\tau}W_{2,\mathrm{free}}^{2}(\mu_{k+1}^{\tau},\mu_{k}^{\tau})\leq\mathcal{F}[\mu_{k}^{\tau}],

(21)

and consequently

\frac{1}{2\tau}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}W_{2,\mathrm{free}}^{2}(\mu_{k+1}^{\tau},\mu_{k}^{\tau})+\mathcal{F}[\mu_{N}^{\tau}]\leq\mathcal{F}[\mu_{0}].

(22)

Proof.

(i) The proof of
\Gamma
-convergence follows the same two-step structure as in classical optimal transport, with the crucial difference that the topology on
\mathcal{P}_{2}^{\mathrm{free}}
is induced by Voiculescu’s free Wasserstein distance
W_{2,\mathrm{free}}
. Lower semicontinuity of
W_{2,\mathrm{free}}
(Lemma
4
) and the coercivity of
\mathcal{F}
yield the liminf inequality. The constant recovery sequence
\mu_{\tau}\equiv\mu
ensures the limsup inequality. Hence,
\mathcal{F}_{\tau}\Gamma\!\to\mathcal{F}
as
\tau\downarrow 0
.

(ii) To prove (
21
), let
\mu_{k+1}^{\tau}
minimize the discrete functional.
\lambda
-geodesic convexity of
\mathcal{F}
implies that for every geodesic
(\nu_{t})_{t\in[0,1]}
connecting
\mu_{k+1}^{\tau}
and
\mu_{k}^{\tau}
,

\mathcal{F}[\nu_{t}]\leq(1-t)\mathcal{F}[\mu_{k+1}^{\tau}]+t\mathcal{F}[\mu_{k}^{\tau}]-\frac{\lambda}{2}t(1-t)W_{2,\mathrm{free}}^{2}(\mu_{k+1}^{\tau},\mu_{k}^{\tau}).

By minimality of
\mu_{k+1}^{\tau}
,

\frac{1}{2\tau}W_{2,\mathrm{free}}^{2}(\mu_{k+1}^{\tau},\mu_{k}^{\tau})+\mathcal{F}[\mu_{k+1}^{\tau}]\leq\frac{1}{2\tau}W_{2,\mathrm{free}}^{2}(\nu_{t},\mu_{k}^{\tau})+\mathcal{F}[\nu_{t}].

Differentiating at
t=0
and combining with the
\lambda
-convexity bound yields

\mathcal{F}[\mu_{k+1}^{\tau}]+\frac{1+\lambda\tau}{2\tau}W_{2,\mathrm{free}}^{2}(\mu_{k+1}^{\tau},\mu_{k}^{\tau})\leq\mathcal{F}[\mu_{k}^{\tau}],

which is (
21
).
Summing over
k
immediately gives (
22
).
∎

Theorem 3

(Existence and convergence of the JKO scheme in the free setting)
.

Let
(\mathcal{P}_{2}^{\mathrm{free}},W_{2,\mathrm{free}})
denote the space of compactly supported spectral measures with finite second moment, endowed with the free
L_{2}
-Wasserstein distance of Biane–Voiculescu. Assume that the free energy functional

\mathcal{F}[\mu]=\frac{1}{2}\!\int x^{2}\,d\mu(x)-\chi[\mu]

is lower semicontinuous, coercive, and has compact sublevel sets.
For
\tau>0
and
\mu_{k}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}^{\mathrm{free}}
, define the JKO step

\mu_{k+1}^{\tau}\in\argmin_{\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}^{\mathrm{free}}}\left\{\frac{1}{2\tau}W_{2,\mathrm{free}}^{2}(\nu,\mu_{k}^{\tau})+\mathcal{F}[\nu]\right\}.

(23)

Then:

(i)

For every
\tau>0
and
\mu_{k}
fixed, the minimization problem (
23
) admits at least one minimizer
\mu_{k+1}^{\tau}
.

(ii)

The discrete interpolants
\mu^{\tau}(t)=\mu_{k}^{\tau}
for
t\in[k\tau,(k+1)\tau)
are relatively compact in the narrow topology.

(iii)

Along a subsequence
\tau_{n}\downarrow 0
, the interpolants converge to an absolutely continuous limit curve
(\mu_{t})_{t\geq 0}
in
(\mathcal{P}_{2}^{\mathrm{free}},W_{2,\mathrm{free}})
that satisfies the Energy Dissipation Inequality (EDI):

\mathcal{F}[\mu_{t}]+\frac{1}{2}\int_{s}^{t}|\mu^{\prime}|^{2}(r)\,dr+\frac{1}{2}\int_{s}^{t}|\partial\mathcal{F}|^{2}(\mu_{r})\,dr\leq\mathcal{F}[\mu_{s}],\quad 0\leq s\leq t.

(24)

(iv)

If
\mathcal{F}
is
\lambda
-geodesically convex in
(\mathcal{P}_{2}^{\mathrm{free}},W_{2,\mathrm{free}})
, then
(\mu_{t})
is the unique gradient flow of
\mathcal{F}
, equivalently characterized by the Evolution Variational Inequality (EVI):

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}W_{2,\mathrm{free}}^{2}(\mu_{t},\sigma)+\frac{\lambda}{2}W_{2,\mathrm{free}}^{2}(\mu_{t},\sigma)\leq\mathcal{F}[\sigma]-\mathcal{F}[\mu_{t}],\quad\forall\sigma\in\mathcal{P}_{2}^{\mathrm{free}}.

(25)

Proof.

The compactness and discrete energy–dissipation bounds established in
Lemma
5
ensure uniform control of both the metric action and the free energy along the JKO sequence.
We now pass to the limit
\tau\!\downarrow\!0
to obtain the continuous
free Wasserstein gradient flow. We divide the argument into several steps.

Step 1:
\Gamma
-convergence and stability.

The
\Gamma
-convergence of the penalized functionals

\mathcal{F}_{\tau}
to
\mathcal{F}
and the stability of discrete minimizers
are guaranteed by Lemma
5
(i).
We therefore omit the proof of the liminf/limsup inequalities and directly
use this convergence property to control the limiting behavior of

\mathcal{F}_{\tau}
as
\tau\downarrow 0
.

Step 2: Reverse-time representation.

From Theorem 1, the reverse process

\widetilde{X}_{s}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=X_{T-s}
satisfies the free SDE

d\widetilde{X}_{s}=\Big(-\tfrac{1}{2}\beta(T-s)\widetilde{X}_{s}-\beta(T-s)\Xi_{T-s}\Big)\,ds+\sqrt{\beta(T-s)}\,d\widetilde{S}_{s}.

By the free Girsanov theorem (Appendix E),
the Radon–Nikodym derivative between the forward and controlled
measures is given by

Z_{T}=\exp\!\Big(\int_{0}^{T}u_{t}\,\#\,dS_{t}-\tfrac{1}{2}\!\int_{0}^{T}\tau[u_{t}^{2}]\,dt\Big),\quad\tau[Z_{T}]=1.

Setting
u_{t}=-\Xi_{t}
yields a martingale change of measure under which

\widetilde{S}_{t}=S_{t}-\!\int_{0}^{t}u_{s}\,ds

has the same free Brownian covariance

\tau[(\widetilde{S}_{t}-\widetilde{S}_{s})^{2}]=t-s

and freely independent increments
(see
[
13
, Prop. 3.4]
).
Thus the pair
(\widetilde{X}_{t},\widetilde{S}_{t})
solves the
free Schrödinger bridge problem minimizing

\mathcal{E}[u]=\tfrac{1}{2}\!\int_{0}^{T}\tau[u_{t}^{2}]\,dt

subject to

\mathrm{Law}(\widetilde{X}_{T})=\sigma
and

\mathrm{Law}(\widetilde{X}_{0})=\mu_{0}
.
Uniqueness follows from the
\lambda
-geodesic convexity of

\mathcal{F}[\mu]
in the free Wasserstein space

[
16
]
.

Step 3: Discrete energy–dissipation inequality.

Applying Lemma
5
(ii) gives

\mathcal{F}[\mu_{k+1}^{\tau}]+\frac{1+\lambda\tau}{2\tau}W_{2,\mathrm{free}}^{2}(\mu_{k+1}^{\tau},\mu_{k}^{\tau})\leq\mathcal{F}[\mu_{k}^{\tau}].

Summing this over
k
up to
N-1
yields the uniform discrete
energy–dissipation bound

\mathcal{F}[\mu_{N}^{\tau}]+\frac{1}{2\tau}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}W_{2,\mathrm{free}}^{2}(\mu_{k+1}^{\tau},\mu_{k}^{\tau})\leq\mathcal{F}[\mu_{0}],

providing a priori estimates independent of
\tau
.

Step 4: Compactness and limit curve.

Define the piecewise-constant interpolation

\mu^{\tau}(t)=\mu_{k}^{\tau}
for
t\in[k\tau,(k+1)\tau)
.
The coercivity of
\mathcal{F}
ensures uniformly bounded second moments, hence tightness of
\{\mu^{\tau}(t)\}_{\tau}
for every
t
.
By a diagonal argument, there exists a subsequence
\tau_{n}\downarrow 0
and a limit curve
\mu_{t}
such that
\mu^{\tau_{n}}(t)\rightharpoonup\mu_{t}
narrowly for all
t\geq 0
.

Step 5: Absolute continuity and continuous EDI.

Define the discrete metric derivative

|\mu^{\tau^{\prime}}|(t)=W_{2,\mathrm{free}}(\mu_{k+1}^{\tau},\mu_{k}^{\tau})/\tau
.
From the bound in Step 3,

\int_{0}^{T}|\mu^{\tau^{\prime}}|^{2}(t)\,dt=\sum_{k\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\,k\tau<T}\frac{W_{2,\mathrm{free}}^{2}(\mu_{k+1}^{\tau},\mu_{k}^{\tau})}{\tau}\leq C_{T},

so
\mu_{t}
is absolutely continuous with metric derivative

|\mu^{\prime}|\in L^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(0,\infty)
.
Passing to the limit
\tau_{n}\downarrow 0
using lower semicontinuity of

\mathcal{F}
and
W_{2,\mathrm{free}}
yields the continuous
energy–dissipation inequality (
26
).

Step 6:
\lambda
-convexity and uniqueness (EVI).

If
\mathcal{F}
is
\lambda
-geodesically convex in

(\mathcal{P}_{2}^{\mathrm{free}},W_{2,\mathrm{free}})
,
then standard variational arguments (see

[
38
, Ch. 4]
) yield the differential
contractivity estimate

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}W_{2,\mathrm{free}}^{2}(\mu_{t},\sigma)+\frac{\lambda}{2}W_{2,\mathrm{free}}^{2}(\mu_{t},\sigma)\leq\mathcal{F}[\sigma]-\mathcal{F}[\mu_{t}],

i.e. the evolution variational inequality (
27
).
This ensures uniqueness and
\lambda
-contraction of the flow.

Step 7: Identification with free diffusion dynamics.

From the continuity equation representation of tangent velocities and
the free integration-by-parts identity,

|\partial\mathcal{F}|(\mu_{t})=\|-\tfrac{1}{2}x-\Xi_{t}\|_{L^{2}(\mu_{t})},

we obtain

\partial_{t}\mu_{t}+\partial_{x}\!\bigl((-\tfrac{1}{2}x-\Xi_{t})\mu_{t}\bigr)=0,

which coincides with the forward free Fokker–Planck equation and the
free SDE

dX_{t}=-\tfrac{1}{2}X_{t}\,dt+dS_{t}.

Hence, the JKO limit curve coincides with the free diffusion semigroup,
and its time reversal yields the reverse-time free SDE of Theorem 1.
∎

This completes the variational justification of the minimizing-movement construction for the free diffusion semigroup and furnishes the desired full proof (with all technical estimates) that the JKO/Moreau–Yosida variational regularization produces the correct gradient-flow limit
\mathcal{F}
.

Free-Specific Ingredients for the JKO Scheme

In adapting the Ambrosio–Gigli–Savaré minimizing movement scheme
[
38
]
to the free setting, only two structural ingredients require free-probabilistic input:

1.

Lower semicontinuity of the free Wasserstein metric.

The topology induced by
W_{2,\mathrm{free}}
is compatible with weak convergence of laws, and
W_{2,\mathrm{free}}
is lower semicontinuous with respect to this topology.
This property follows from the analysis of Biane and Voiculescu
[
16
]
, which establishes the geometric underpinnings of
W_{2,\mathrm{free}}
.

2.

Upper semicontinuity of free entropy.

The functional
\chi(\mu)
is upper semicontinuous under convergence of noncommutative laws with uniformly bounded second moments and compact support.
This key property is proved by Hiai and Petz
[
41
]
, and ensures the stability of the entropy term in the variational limit.

Together, these two inputs guarantee that the
\Gamma
-convergence and compactness arguments of
[
38
]
extend verbatim to the free setting, yielding the well-posedness of the JKO scheme for the free Fokker–Planck functional considered here.

Gradient-flow structure, EDI and EVI, and the link to Theorem 1

We assume throughout that
\mathcal{F}
is
\lambda
-geodesically convex in
(\mathcal{P}_{2}^{\mathrm{free}},W_{2,\mathrm{free}})
in the sense of Biane–Voiculescu
[
16
]
and the metric framework of Ambrosio–Gigli–Savaré
[
38
]
; namely, along any constant-speed free
W_{2}
-geodesic
(\mu_{\theta})_{\theta\in[0,1]}
one has

\mathcal{F}[\mu_{\theta}]\;\leq\;(1-\theta)\,\mathcal{F}[\mu_{0}]+\theta\,\mathcal{F}[\mu_{1}]-\frac{\lambda}{2}\,\theta(1-\theta)\,W_{2,\mathrm{free}}^{2}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1}).

This holds, for instance, for the quadratic potential plus free entropy under compact support (see the discussion in
[

VIII
Information-Theoretic Interpretation

This section provides the detailed derivations of the free de Bruijn identity, the Fisher–information flow, and the associated HWI-type inequalities that underlie the information-theoretic interpretation developed in Section VI. The arguments presented here are technical and are included for completeness. They also form the analytical basis for the summary given in Appendix D. The forward free diffusion process defined in (
7
) admits a
natural interpretation in terms of noncommutative information measures. In classical information theory, Gaussian heat flow increases Shannon entropy and satisfies the de Bruijn identity, which relates the derivative of the entropy to the Fisher information. The same principle holds in the free setting, with Voiculescu’s free entropy
\chi
playing the role of Shannon entropy and the free Fisher information
\Phi^{*}
taking the role of classical Fisher information.

VIII-A

Free de Bruijn Identity

For a self-adjoint free diffusion process
X_{s}
with variance schedule
\beta(s)
, the free entropy satisfies

\frac{d}{ds}\chi(X_{s})=\frac{1}{2}\beta(s)\Phi^{*}(X_{s}),

(30)

where
\Phi^{*}(X_{s})
is defined by

\Phi^{*}(X_{s})=\|\Xi_{s}\|_{2}^{2}

and
\Xi_{s}
is the
conjugate variable
of
X_{s}
.
Equation (
30
) is the noncommutative analogue of
the classical de Bruijn identity and shows that the forward free diffusion
is an entropy-increasing flow. In particular,
\chi(X_{s})
is non-decreasing in
s
,
with strict monotonicity unless
X_{s}
is already semicircular. Equation (30) shows that the forward free diffusion monotonically increases Voiculescu’s free entropy, driving the spectral law toward the semicircular equilibrium.
This evolution is illustrated in Fig. 1, where the forward process (top) broadens structured spectra while the reverse transport (bottom) performs free deconvolution, recovering low-entropy spectral states.

VIII-B

Free Score Function and Reverse Drift

The conjugate variable
\Xi_{s}
plays the same role in free probability as the
score function

\nabla\log p(x)
in classical statistics. It represents the
L^{2}
-gradient of the log noncommutative density and encodes how the spectral law of
X_{s}
deviates from the equilibrium semicircular law. The reverse-time dynamics of Theorem 1 can thus be viewed as a free analogue of the score-based generative modeling framework.

Corollary 3

(Reverse drift as an entropy gradient)
.

Let
\mu_{X_{s}}
be the spectral distribution of
X_{s}
and
\Xi_{s}
the conjugate variable associated with
X_{s}
. Then the drift term of the reverse-time SDE satisfies

-\frac{1}{2}\beta(s)X_{s}-\beta(s)\Xi_{s}=-\beta(s)\nabla\chi[\mu_{X_{s}}](X_{s}),

(31)

where
\nabla\chi[\mu_{X_{s}}](X_{s})
denotes the
L^{2}
-gradient of the free entropy functional evaluated at
X_{s}
.

Proof.

For a single self-adjoint noncommutative random variable, Voiculescu’s free entropy has the variational derivative

\nabla\chi[\mu](x)=\frac{1}{2}x+\Xi(x),

where
\Xi(x)
is the conjugate variable evaluated at
x
. Substituting this into the reverse drift expression of Theorem 1 immediately yields

-\beta(s)\nabla\chi[\mu_{X_{s}}](X_{s}).

∎

Theorem 4

(JKO Scheme for Free Entropy Gradient Flows)
.

Let
(\mathcal{P}_{2}^{\mathrm{free}},W_{2,\mathrm{free}})
denote the
metric space of spectral distributions with finite second moment,
equipped with the free
2
-Wasserstein distance.
Consider the functional

\mathcal{F}[\mu]=-\chi[\mu],

defined on
\mathcal{P}_{2}^{\mathrm{free}}
.
Then the sequence of measures
(\mu_{k}^{\tau})_{k\geq 0}
generated by the
minimizing movement scheme

\mu_{k+1}^{\tau}=\arg\min_{\mu}\left\{\mathcal{F}[\mu]+\frac{1}{2\tau}W_{2,\mathrm{free}}^{2}(\mu,\mu_{k}^{\tau})\right\}

(32)

converges as
\tau\to 0
to a continuous curve

\mu_{t}
that solves the gradient flow equation

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mu_{t}=\nabla\cdot\big(\mu_{t}\nabla\chi[\mu_{t}]\big),

where the derivative is in the sense of the free continuity equation.
The forward flow corresponds to a gradient ascent of
\chi
, while
the reverse-time flow corresponds to a steepest descent of
-\chi
.

Proof.

The proof follows the classical argument of Jordan–Kinderlehrer–Otto
for the variational formulation of Fokker–Planck equations, adapted
to the free Wasserstein space introduced by Biane and Voiculescu.
Existence and uniqueness of the minimizers in (
32
)
follow from the geodesic convexity of
\mathcal{F}
in

W_{2,\mathrm{free}}
and lower semicontinuity of
\chi
.
Passing to the limit
\tau\to 0
yields a curve of maximal slope
for
-\chi
in the free Wasserstein metric, which satisfies the
free continuity equation with velocity field

v_{t}=-\nabla\chi[\mu_{t}]
. This gradient flow coincides with
the solution of the free Fokker–Planck equation associated with
the free diffusion process.
∎

VIII-C

Entropy Production and Thermodynamic Inequalities

Corollary 4

(Entropy Production and Thermodynamic Inequalities)
.

Let
(X_{s})_{0\leq s\leq T}
satisfy the free forward diffusion equation

\mathrm{d}X_{s}=-\tfrac{1}{2}\beta(s)X_{s}\,\mathrm{d}s+\sqrt{\beta(s)}\,\mathrm{d}S_{s},

in a tracial
W^{*}
-probability space
(\mathcal{A},\varphi)
,
where
S_{s}
is a free Brownian motion. Suppose
X_{0}
has a compactly supported spectral law with finite free entropy
\chi(X_{0})
. Then:

1.

(
Entropy production identity
)
The free entropy
\chi(X_{s})
evolves according to

\chi(X_{T})-\chi(X_{0})=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\beta(s)\,\|\Xi_{s}\|_{2}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s,

(33)

where
\Xi_{s}
is the conjugate variable of
X_{s}
and

\|\Xi_{s}\|_{2}^{2}=\Phi^{*}(X_{s})
is the free Fisher information.

2.

(
Thermodynamic efficiency inequality
)
For any admissible control
U_{s}
steering the reverse-time dynamics

\mathrm{d}X_{s}=\left(-\tfrac{1}{2}\beta(s)X_{s}+\sqrt{\beta(s)}\,U_{s}\right)\mathrm{d}s+\sqrt{\beta(s)}\,\mathrm{d}S_{s},

from
X_{T}
to
X_{0}
, the control energy

\mathcal{J}(U)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\beta(s)\,\|U_{s}\|_{2}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s

(34)

satisfies

\mathcal{J}(U)\geq\chi(X_{T})-\chi(X_{0}),

(35)

with equality if and only if
U_{s}=-\Xi_{s}
, i.e., for the optimal
reverse SDE given in Theorem 1.

Proof.

Integrating the free de Bruijn identity

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\chi(X_{s})=\frac{1}{2}\beta(s)\Phi^{*}(X_{s})

over
[0,T]
yields (
33
).
The inequality (
35
) follows from the
free Girsanov theorem:

D(\mathbb{Q}\|\mathbb{P})=\mathcal{J}(U)
,
where
\mathbb{P}
is the uncontrolled forward law
and
\mathbb{Q}
is the law of the controlled reverse process.
Relative entropy is non-negative, and the unique minimizer is

U_{s}=-\Xi_{s}
.
∎

Remark 6

(Free Stam inequality and Fisher information bound)
.

Corollary
4
implies that the free Fisher
information
\Phi^{*}(X_{s})
is integrable along the diffusion flow.
Differentiating (
33
) with respect to
s

and using the monotonicity of
\chi(X_{s})
gives

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\chi(X_{s})=\frac{1}{2}\beta(s)\Phi^{*}(X_{s})\geq 0,

which shows that
\Phi^{*}(X_{s})\geq 0
for all
s
.
For constant
\beta(s)=1
, this yields the inequality

\frac{1}{\Phi^{*}(X_{t})}\geq\frac{1}{\Phi^{*}(X_{0})}+t,

(36)

obtained by differentiating and integrating the convexity of

1/\Phi^{*}
along the flow, which is the free analogue of the
classical Stam inequality. Equation (
36
) implies
that free Fisher information decays monotonically under the
forward diffusion and is strictly decreasing unless the distribution
has already reached the semicircular equilibrium.
This inequality, together with (
33
),
quantifies the trade-off between entropy growth and information loss
and is the free analogue of the Fisher information inequality in
classical information theory.

Lemma 6

(Free Stam Inequality via Free Heat Semigroup)
.

Let
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be the free Brownian (free heat) flow defined by

\mathrm{d}X_{t}=\mathrm{d}S_{t},\quad X_{0}\text{ given},

so that
X_{t}=X_{0}+S_{t}
.
Assume
\Phi^{*}(X_{0})<\infty
. Then for all
t>0
,

\frac{1}{\Phi^{*}(X_{t})}\geq\frac{1}{\Phi^{*}(X_{0})}+t.

(37)

Proof.

Voiculescu’s theory of the free heat semigroup implies that for the free convolution

X_{t}=X_{0}+S_{t}
, the derivative of the free entropy satisfies

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\chi(X_{t})=\frac{1}{2}\Phi^{*}(X_{t}),

and the free Fisher information is connected to the derivative of
\chi

through the noncommutative analogue of the classical de Bruijn identity.
The free analog of the Fisher information inequality, due to Biane and Voiculescu,
states that for free convolution with a semicircular element
S_{t}
,

\frac{1}{\Phi^{*}(X_{t})}\geq\frac{1}{\Phi^{*}(X_{0})}+t,

where the right-hand side arises because the semicircular law has Fisher information

\Phi^{*}(S_{t})=1/t
and free independence implies additivity of the reciprocal
of the Fisher information under free convolution.
This can be derived rigorously by differentiating

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\big(\frac{1}{\Phi^{*}(X_{t})}\big)=1

from the convexity of the function
t\mapsto 1/\Phi^{*}(X_{t})
along the flow
(see
[
16
]
).
Integrating from
0
to
t
gives (
37
).
∎

VIII-D

Implications for Generative Models

This information-theoretic perspective provides several consequences:

•

The forward free diffusion monotonically smooths spectral measures
and eliminates structure, analogous to how Gaussian convolution increases entropy.

•

The reverse flow corresponds to entropy-reducing transport, guided by
the conjugate variable (free score function).

•

Learning
\Xi_{s}
enables exact sampling from complex target distributions,
because the reverse-time process follows the steepest descent of

-\chi[\mu]
in the space of spectral distributions.

These results place diffusion generative models within a
precise information-theoretic framework, linking their efficiency and
accuracy to the geometry of free entropy.

IX
Functional Inequalities for Free Diffusion Models

The variational and gradient-flow formulation developed in Sections VII–VIII provides a natural setting to establish free analogues of classical functional inequalities linking entropy, Fisher information, and transport distance.
We derive here two such inequalities—a
free logarithmic Sobolev inequality
and a
free Talagrand inequality
—and prove them rigorously from the dynamics of the free Ornstein–Uhlenbeck semigroup.

IX-A

Free Log–Sobolev Inequality

Theorem 5

(Free Log–Sobolev Inequality for the Free Ornstein–Uhlenbeck Semigroup)
.

Let
(\mathcal{A},\tau)
be a tracial
W^{*}
-probability space and let
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
solve the free Ornstein–Uhlenbeck SDE

dX_{t}=-\tfrac{1}{2}X_{t}\,dt+dS_{t},

(38)

where
S_{t}
is a free Brownian motion.
Denote by
\mu_{t}
the spectral distribution of
X_{t}
,
\chi(\mu_{t})
its free entropy, and
\Phi^{*}(\mu_{t})
its free Fisher information.
Then, for every
t>0
,

\chi(\mu_{\mathrm{sc}})-\chi(\mu_{t})\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\,\Phi^{*}(\mu_{t}),

(39)

where
\mu_{\mathrm{sc}}
denotes the semicircular law.
Equality holds if and only if
\mu_{t}=\mu_{\mathrm{sc}}
.
Consequently,
\chi(\mu_{t})
converges exponentially to
\chi(\mu_{\mathrm{sc}})
:

\chi(\mu_{\mathrm{sc}})-\chi(\mu_{t})\leq e^{-t}\big(\chi(\mu_{\mathrm{sc}})-\chi(\mu_{0})\big).

(40)

Proof.

By Voiculescu’s free Itô calculus
[
12
,
14
]
, the free Ornstein–Uhlenbeck semigroup
(P_{t})_{t\geq 0}
satisfies

\frac{d}{dt}P_{t}f(X_{0})=-\tfrac{1}{2}\,\mathcal{L}P_{t}f(X_{0}),

where
\mathcal{L}
is the generator

\mathcal{L}f(X)=X\,Df(X)-D^{2}f(X),

and
D
is the cyclic (free) gradient.
Let
\chi(\mu_{t})
denote Voiculescu’s free entropy:

\chi(\mu_{t})=\iint\log|x-y|\,d\mu_{t}(x)d\mu_{t}(y)+\frac{3}{4}+\frac{1}{2}\log(2\pi).

Differentiating
\chi(\mu_{t})
in
t
along the flow
\partial_{t}\mu_{t}=\tfrac{1}{2}\partial_{x}(x\mu_{t})+\partial_{x}(\mu_{t}H\mu_{t})
,
where
H\mu_{t}
denotes the Hilbert transform of
\mu_{t}
, we obtain (see
[
16
, Lem. 4.3]
)

\frac{d}{dt}\chi(\mu_{t})=\frac{1}{2}\Phi^{*}(\mu_{t}),

(41)

with
\Phi^{*}(\mu_{t})=\int\big(\Xi_{t}(x)\big)^{2}d\mu_{t}(x)
,
\Xi_{t}
being the conjugate variable.
The semicircular law
\mu_{\mathrm{sc}}
is the unique invariant measure of the semigroup, satisfying
\Phi^{*}(\mu_{\mathrm{sc}})=1
and
\chi(\mu_{\mathrm{sc}})
maximal.

Voiculescu’s
free logarithmic Sobolev inequality

[
12
, Thm. 4.1]
states that, for any probability measure
\mu
with finite free entropy and Fisher information,

\chi(\mu_{\mathrm{sc}})-\chi(\mu)\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\Phi^{*}(\mu),

(42)

with equality only if
\mu=\mu_{\mathrm{sc}}
.
Applying (
42
) to
\mu=\mu_{t}
gives (
39
).

To obtain the exponential convergence, differentiate (
39
) in time and use (
41
):

\frac{d}{dt}\big(\chi(\mu_{\mathrm{sc}})-\chi(\mu_{t})\big)=-\frac{1}{2}\Phi^{*}(\mu_{t})\leq-\big(\chi(\mu_{\mathrm{sc}})-\chi(\mu_{t})\big),

which integrates to (
40
) by Grönwall’s inequality.
∎

IX-B

Free Talagrand Inequality

Theorem 6

(Free Talagrand Inequality)
.

Let
\mu
be a compactly supported probability measure with finite free entropy
\chi(\mu)
and let
\mu_{\mathrm{sc}}
denote the semicircular law.
Then the free
L_{2}
–Wasserstein distance satisfies

W_{2,\mathrm{free}}^{2}(\mu,\mu_{\mathrm{sc}})\leq 2\big(\chi(\mu_{\mathrm{sc}})-\chi(\mu)\big),

(43)

with equality if and only if
\mu=\mu_{\mathrm{sc}}
.

Proof.

Let
(\mu_{t})_{t\geq 0}
denote the solution of the free Fokker–Planck equation

\partial_{t}\mu_{t}=\partial_{x}\bigg[\frac{1}{2}x\mu_{t}-\mu_{t}(H\mu_{t})\bigg],

with
\mu_{0}=\mu
.
Biane and Voiculescu
[
16
]
showed that this flow is the
Wasserstein gradient flow
of the free entropy
\chi
on the metric space
(\mathcal{P}_{2}^{\mathrm{free}},W_{2,\mathrm{free}})
, i.e.

\frac{d}{dt}\mu_{t}=-\nabla_{W_{2,\mathrm{free}}}\chi(\mu_{t}).

Along this flow we have the energy–dissipation balance (see
[
17
, Thm. 4.3]
)

\frac{d}{dt}\chi(\mu_{t})=\frac{1}{2}\Phi^{*}(\mu_{t}),\quad\frac{d}{dt}\frac{1}{2}W_{2,\mathrm{free}}^{2}(\mu_{t},\mu_{\mathrm{sc}})=-\Phi^{*}(\mu_{t}).

(44)

Integrating the second identity from
t=0
to
t=\infty
gives

\frac{1}{2}W_{2,\mathrm{free}}^{2}(\mu,\mu_{\mathrm{sc}})=\int_{0}^{\infty}\Phi^{*}(\mu_{t})\,dt.

Using the first relation in (
44
) we have

\int_{0}^{\infty}\Phi^{*}(\mu_{t})\,dt=2\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{d}{dt}\chi(\mu_{t})\,dt=2\big(\chi(\mu_{\mathrm{sc}})-\chi(\mu)\big),

since
\lim_{t\to\infty}\mu_{t}=\mu_{\mathrm{sc}}
.
Combining these expressions yields inequality (
43
).
Equality holds precisely when
\Phi^{*}(\mu_{t})
is constant in time, i.e. when
\mu_{t}=\mu_{\mathrm{sc}}
for all
t
, proving the claim.
∎

IX-C

Consequences

Theorems
5
and
6
together establish a free analogue of the classical Otto–Villani triangle:

\text{(Free Log--Sobolev)}\quad\Rightarrow\quad\text{(Free Talagrand)}\quad\Rightarrow\quad\text{(Exponential entropy decay)}.

These results demonstrate that free diffusion models inherit strong functional-analytic regularity and convergence guarantees analogous to Gaussian diffusions in the commutative case.
They quantify both information dissipation and geometric contraction in the free Wasserstein space, completing the variational and information-theoretic structure of the proposed framework.

IX-D

Free HWI Inequality

Combining Theorems
5
and
6
,
we obtain a differential relation between entropy, Wasserstein distance, and Fisher information,
forming the free analogue of the classical HWI inequality due to Otto and Villani
[
20
]
.
This provides a sharp geometric–information–entropy trade-off for free diffusion processes.

Corollary 5

(Free HWI Inequality)
.

Let
\mu
be a compactly supported probability measure on
\mathbb{R}
with finite free entropy
\chi(\mu)
and free Fisher information
\Phi^{*}(\mu)
.
Denote by
\mu_{\mathrm{sc}}
the semicircular law.
Then the following inequality holds:

\chi(\mu_{\mathrm{sc}})-\chi(\mu)\leq W_{2,\mathrm{free}}(\mu,\mu_{\mathrm{sc}})\sqrt{\tfrac{1}{2}\Phi^{*}(\mu)}-\tfrac{1}{4}W_{2,\mathrm{free}}^{2}(\mu,\mu_{\mathrm{sc}}).

(45)

In particular,
(
45
) implies both the free logarithmic Sobolev inequality
and the free Talagrand inequality as special cases.

Proof.

Let
(\mu_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be the free Fokker–Planck flow starting from
\mu_{0}=\mu
and converging to
\mu_{\infty}=\mu_{\mathrm{sc}}
.
From the energy–dissipation identity (
44
),

\frac{d}{dt}\chi(\mu_{t})=\frac{1}{2}\Phi^{*}(\mu_{t}),\qquad\frac{d}{dt}\tfrac{1}{2}W_{2,\mathrm{free}}^{2}(\mu_{t},\mu_{\mathrm{sc}})=-\Phi^{*}(\mu_{t}).

Eliminating
\Phi^{*}(\mu_{t})
between these two relations gives

\frac{d}{dt}\chi(\mu_{t})=-\tfrac{1}{4}\frac{d}{dt}W_{2,\mathrm{free}}^{2}(\mu_{t},\mu_{\mathrm{sc}}).

(46)

Integrating from
t=0
to
t=\infty
and using
\mu_{\infty}=\mu_{\mathrm{sc}}
yields

\chi(\mu_{\mathrm{sc}})-\chi(\mu)=\tfrac{1}{4}W_{2,\mathrm{free}}^{2}(\mu,\mu_{\mathrm{sc}}).

To obtain a sharp bound incorporating
\Phi^{*}(\mu)
, we refine the above argument following the displacement convexity of
\chi
in the free Wasserstein space
[
17
, Thm. 5.2]
.
Let
\nu_{s}
denote the geodesic interpolation between
\mu
and
\mu_{\mathrm{sc}}
in
(\mathcal{P}_{2}^{\mathrm{free}},W_{2,\mathrm{free}})
.
By free displacement convexity,

\chi(\nu_{1})\geq\chi(\nu_{0})+\langle\nabla_{W_{2,\mathrm{free}}}\chi(\nu_{0}),\dot{\nu}_{0}\rangle_{W_{2,\mathrm{free}}}-\tfrac{1}{2}W_{2,\mathrm{free}}^{2}(\mu,\mu_{\mathrm{sc}}).

(47)

Setting
\nu_{0}=\mu
and
\nu_{1}=\mu_{\mathrm{sc}}
gives

\chi(\mu_{\mathrm{sc}})-\chi(\mu)\leq-\langle\nabla_{W_{2,\mathrm{free}}}\chi(\mu),\dot{\nu}_{0}\rangle_{W_{2,\mathrm{free}}}+\tfrac{1}{2}W_{2,\mathrm{free}}^{2}(\mu,\mu_{\mathrm{sc}}).

Since
\nabla_{W_{2,\mathrm{free}}}\chi(\mu)
coincides with
-\tfrac{1}{2}\Xi_{\mu}
in
L^{2}(\mu)
norm, and the metric derivative
\dot{\nu}_{0}
has norm
\|\dot{\nu}_{0}\|_{W_{2,\mathrm{free}}}=W_{2,\mathrm{free}}(\mu,\mu_{\mathrm{sc}})
,
the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality gives

-\langle\nabla_{W_{2,\mathrm{free}}}\chi(\mu),\dot{\nu}_{0}\rangle_{W_{2,\mathrm{free}}}\leq W_{2,\mathrm{free}}(\mu,\mu_{\mathrm{sc}})\sqrt{\tfrac{1}{2}\Phi^{*}(\mu)}.

Combining the last two displays yields (

∎

Consequences

Inequality (

\text{(Free HWI)}\;\Rightarrow\;\text{(Free Log--Sobolev and Free Talagrand)}\;\Rightarrow\;\text{(Exponential Entropy Decay).}

This establishes the complete correspondence between free stochastic dynamics, functional inequalities, and information geometry.

Convergence of Free Diffusion Processes and Universality of DDPM Sampling

One of the key properties of diffusion models, both in the classical and the free-probabilistic setting, is the convergence of the forward diffusion to a high-entropy reference law and the universal approximation capability of the corresponding reverse process.

X-A

Convergence of the Forward Free Diffusion

Consider the forward free SDE

and the convergence rate is controlled by the growth of
\int_{0}^{T}\beta(s)\,ds

X-B

Given samples from

\mu_{0}

\mu_{T}
back to
\mu_{0}

X-C

Implications for Generative Modeling

This convergence result implies that, as in classical DDPMs, the free diffusion model is a
universal generative model
: by learning the conjugate variable
\Xi_{t}

The universality stems from the contractive nature of the forward diffusion (which drives all distributions to a simple reference law) and the expressivity of the score-based reconstruction in reverse time.

XI
Conclusion

This work has developed a mathematically rigorous framework for denoising diffusion models in the language of free probability.
We showed that the forward dynamics admit a realization as a
free Ornstein–Uhlenbeck flow
, while the reverse-time dynamics arise as
free deconvolution processes
driven by the conjugate variable—the noncommutative analogue of the classical score function.
Using the free Malliavin calculus and the Clark–Ocone representation (Appendix C), we obtained explicit stochastic and variational formulations of the reverse-time SDE, providing a consistent interpretation of diffusion models within Voiculescu’s framework of free entropy.

At the information-theoretic and geometric levels, we established free analogues of the de Bruijn identity, Fisher-information flow, and energy–entropy balance relations, revealing the variational structure underlying free diffusions.
Through the minimizing-movement formulation of the Jordan–Kinderlehrer–Otto (JKO) scheme, we proved the
\Gamma
–convergence and discrete energy–dissipation (EDI) properties of the associated free gradient flows, demonstrating that Voiculescu’s free entropy generates a well-defined Wasserstein gradient-flow structure in the noncommutative setting.

Beyond these variational and stochastic results, we derived a hierarchy of
functional inequalities
—including free analogues of the logarithmic Sobolev, Talagrand, and HWI inequalities (Section IX). These results provide quantitative convergence guarantees: exponential decay of free entropy, dissipation of free Fisher information, and contraction in the free Wasserstein metric toward the semicircular equilibrium. Together, they establish a rigorous geometric–information bridge between classical diffusion theory and its free, operator-valued counterpart.

Our analysis thus provides the first complete characterization of
gradient flows of free entropy
and their associated
functional inequalities
within the free Wasserstein geometry. It demonstrates that the free entropy functional possesses a genuine Riemannian gradient-flow structure, and that free diffusion models can approximate arbitrary compactly supported spectral laws. The resulting framework unifies stochastic analysis, free probability, optimal transport, and noncommutative information geometry under a common variational principle.

Future directions include extending the theory to non-Hermitian and rectangular random-matrix models, incorporating data-adaptive or learning-based drift terms within the free diffusion formalism, and developing computational methods for operator-valued generative modeling.
A particularly promising direction lies in connecting the free functional inequalities established here to quantum information geometry and entropy production in open quantum systems.
More broadly, these results position free probability as a foundational language for understanding efficiency, universality, and thermodynamic structure in diffusion-based generative models operating in noncommutative or operator-valued environments.

Appendix A
PDE-based derivation of Theorem 1

The reverse-time SDE can also be obtained from the PDE governing the evolution of the spectral law of
X_{t}
under the forward SDE. This derivation requires no stochastic calculus beyond identifying the conjugate variable with the noncommutative gradient of the log-density.

Let
\mu_{t}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\mu_{X_{t}}
denote the spectral measure of
X_{t}
for
t\in[0,T]
. Under the forward SDE (
7
),
\mu_{t}
evolves according to the
free Fokker–Planck equation

\partial_{t}\mu_{t}=\frac{1}{2}\beta(t)\,\partial_{x}\big(x\,\mu_{t}\big)+\frac{1}{2}\beta(t)\,\partial_{x}\!\big(\mu_{t}\,\partial_{x}V_{t}\big),

(50)

where
V_{t}
is the logarithmic potential

V_{t}(x)=-2\int_{\mathbb R}\log|x-y|\,\mu_{t}(dy).

Here
\partial_{x}V_{t}(x)=-2\,\mathrm{p.v.}\int_{\mathbb R}\frac{1}{x-y}\,\mu_{t}(dy)
is (up to sign) the Hilbert transform
H_{\mu_{t}}(x)
of
\mu_{t}
and is equal, in
L^{2}(\mu_{t})
, to the conjugate variable
\Xi_{t}(x)
.

Equation (
50
) is the free-probabilistic analogue of the classical Fokker–Planck equation for an Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process, obtained from the continuity equation

\partial_{t}\mu_{t}+\partial_{x}\!\big(b_{t}(x)\,\mu_{t}\big)=0

with velocity field
b_{t}(x)=-\frac{1}{2}\beta(t)\,x-\beta(t)\,\Xi_{t}(x)
, together with the fact that in free probability the diffusive term is absorbed into the divergence form through
\Xi_{t}
.

To reverse time, set
s\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=T-t
and
\nu_{s}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\mu_{T-s}
. The forward PDE (
50
) becomes

-\partial_{s}\nu_{s}=\frac{1}{2}\beta(T-s)\,\partial_{x}\!\big(x\,\nu_{s}\big)+\beta(T-s)\,\partial_{x}\!\big(\nu_{s}\,\Xi_{T-s}\big).

Multiplying by
-1
yields

\partial_{s}\nu_{s}=-\frac{1}{2}\beta(T-s)\,\partial_{x}\!\big(x\,\nu_{s}\big)-\beta(T-s)\,\partial_{x}\!\big(\nu_{s}\,\Xi_{T-s}\big).

\widetilde{b}_{s}(x)=-\frac{1}{2}\beta(T-s)\,x-\beta(T-s)\,\Xi_{T-s}(x).

(51)

Appendix B
Worked example: Dirac initial law (explicit forward and reverse flows)

This appendix illustrates the free diffusion and its reverse-time dynamics in the simplest setting
X_{0}=0
, where the initial law is a Dirac measure.
The example verifies explicitly the theoretical identities of
Theorem 1 and Corollary
1
.
We illustrate the forward and reverse free diffusion with the explicit solvable example

X_{0}\equiv a\quad\text{(deterministic point mass at }a\in\mathbb R).

Let
\beta\in C^{1}([0,T];(0,\infty))
and define
\Lambda(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\beta(r)\,dr
as before. Consider the forward Ornstein–Uhlenbeck SDE

dX_{t}=-\tfrac{1}{2}\beta(t)X_{t}\,dt+\sqrt{\beta(t)}\,dS_{t},\qquad X_{0}=a,

driven by a free Brownian motion
(S_{t})_{t\geq 0}
.

Forward solution and marginal law

The linearity of the drift yields the explicit variation-of-constants representation

X_{t}=e^{-\frac{1}{2}\Lambda(t)}a+\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\frac{1}{2}(\Lambda(t)-\Lambda(u))}\sqrt{\beta(u)}\,dS_{u}.

Hence for every fixed
t\in[0,T]
the law
\mu_{X_{t}}
is semicircular (free convolution of a point mass with a semicircular increment yields a semicircular distribution) with center

m_{t}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=e^{-\frac{1}{2}\Lambda(t)}a

and variance

\sigma_{t}^{2}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\Var(X_{t})=\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(\Lambda(t)-\Lambda(u))}\beta(u)\,du.

The variance formula simplifies via the substitution
h(u)=e^{\Lambda(u)}
(noting
h^{\prime}(u)=\beta(u)e^{\Lambda(u)}
):

\sigma_{t}^{2}=e^{-\Lambda(t)}\int_{0}^{t}e^{\Lambda(u)}\beta(u)\,du=e^{-\Lambda(t)}\big(e^{\Lambda(t)}-1\big)=1-e^{-\Lambda(t)}.

Thus the marginal at time
t
is the semicircular law
\mathsf{SC}(m_{t},\sigma_{t}^{2})
with density

\pi_{t}(x)\;=\;\frac{1}{2\pi\sigma_{t}^{2}}\sqrt{\,4\sigma_{t}^{2}-(x-m_{t})^{2}\,}\ \mathbf{1}_{[\,m_{t}-2\sigma_{t},\;m_{t}+2\sigma_{t}\,]}(x).

Conjugate variable for the semicircular law

For the semicircular law
\mathsf{SC}(m,\sigma^{2})
(center
m
, variance
\sigma^{2}
) the conjugate variable
\Xi(x)
admits the explicit affine form

\Xi(x)\;=\;-\frac{x-m}{2\sigma^{2}},

see Voiculescu
[
40
]
and Biane
[
13
]
for derivations in the free Fisher information / conjugate variable framework. (Intuitively, this identity follows from the Euler–Lagrange condition for the free energy:
\nabla\chi(\mu)(x)=\tfrac{1}{2}x+\Xi(x)
and
\nabla\chi(\mathsf{SC}(m,\sigma^{2}))\equiv 0
at equilibrium after suitable normalization, which yields the stated affine form; direct verification via the principal-value Hilbert transform of the semicircular density is standard.)

Applying the formula above to
\mu_{X_{t}}=\mathsf{SC}(m_{t},\sigma_{t}^{2})
gives

\Xi_{t}(x)=-\frac{x-m_{t}}{2\sigma_{t}^{2}}.

Reverse drift and reversed SDE (explicit form)

The general reverse-time free SDE derived in Theorem 1 (and in the PDE appendix) reads, in the time-
t
convention,

dX_{t}=\Big(-\tfrac{1}{2}\beta(t)X_{t}-\beta(t)\,\Xi_{t}(X_{t})\Big)\,dt+\sqrt{\beta(t)}\,d\widehat{S}_{t},

where
\widehat{S}_{t}
denotes the appropriate backward free Brownian motion under the reversed filtration. Substituting the explicit expression for
\Xi_{t}
for the semicircular marginals yields the closed form

-\tfrac{1}{2}\beta(t)X_{t}-\beta(t)\Xi_{t}(X_{t})=-\tfrac{1}{2}\beta(t)X_{t}+\beta(t)\frac{X_{t}-m_{t}}{2\sigma_{t}^{2}}.

Collecting terms,

\text{reverse drift }b_{\mathrm{rev}}(t,x)=\frac{\beta(t)}{2}\Big(-x+\frac{x-m_{t}}{\sigma_{t}^{2}}\Big)=\frac{\beta(t)}{2}\Big(\Big(\frac{1}{\sigma_{t}^{2}}-1\Big)x-\frac{m_{t}}{\sigma_{t}^{2}}\Big).

Using
\sigma_{t}^{2}=1-e^{-\Lambda(t)}
and
m_{t}=e^{-\frac{1}{2}\Lambda(t)}a
we can rewrite the coefficients explicitly if desired. For instance,

\frac{1}{\sigma_{t}^{2}}-1=\frac{1-(1-e^{-\Lambda(t)})}{1-e^{-\Lambda(t)}}=\frac{e^{-\Lambda(t)}}{1-e^{-\Lambda(t)}},

so

b_{\mathrm{rev}}(t,x)=\frac{\beta(t)}{2}\Big(\frac{e^{-\Lambda(t)}}{1-e^{-\Lambda(t)}}\,x\;-\;\frac{e^{-\frac{1}{2}\Lambda(t)}a}{1-e^{-\Lambda(t)}}\Big).

Thus the reversed SDE for this example becomes

dX_{t}\;=\;\frac{\beta(t)}{2}\Big(\frac{e^{-\Lambda(t)}}{1-e^{-\Lambda(t)}}\,X_{t}\;-\;\frac{e^{-\frac{1}{2}\Lambda(t)}a}{1-e^{-\Lambda(t)}}\Big)\,dt\;+\;\sqrt{\beta(t)}\,d\widehat{S}_{t}.

Interpretation and limiting behaviour

The forward flow spreads the point mass to a semicircular law whose center decays exponentially from
a
to
0
and whose variance increases from
0
to
1
(as
\Lambda(t)\to\infty
the variance
\sigma_{t}^{2}\to 1
and
m_{t}\to 0
). The reverse drift above provides the explicit force required to contract the semicircular law back toward the point mass at time
0
: the drift is affine in
X_{t}
, with coefficients depending only on
(\beta,\Lambda(t),a)
. In particular, if one were to discretize the reversed SDE and simulate with the optimal control
U_{t}=-\Xi_{t}
, the process initialized at
\mathsf{SC}(m_{T},\sigma_{T}^{2})
will be transported back toward the point mass at
a
as
t\downarrow 0
.

This closed-form example thus verifies the abstract formulas of Theorem 1: the conjugate variable appears as the noncommutative score, and it yields an explicit analytic expression for the reverse-time drift in the freely Gaussian (semicircular) case.
∎

C.1  Functional–Analytic Framework

Let

\mathcal{A}

\langle X,Y\rangle=\varphi(Y^{\ast}X)

For a simple adapted process

U_{t}=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}U_{i}\,1_{(t_{i},t_{i+1}]}(t)

Free Malliavin derivative.

The free Malliavin derivative

D_{t}P(S_{t_{1}},\ldots,S_{t_{n}})\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}1_{[0,t_{i}]}(t)(\partial_{i}P)(S_{t_{1}},\ldots,S_{t_{n}}),

is closable, and its closure defines the
Sobolev space

W^{1,2}_{\mathrm{free}}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\mathrm{Dom}(\overline{D})
.
By construction,
\delta
is the adjoint of
D
:
for all
F\in\mathrm{Dom}(D)
and
U\in\mathrm{Dom}(\delta)
,

\varphi[F\,\delta(U)]=\int_{0}^{T}\varphi\!\big(\langle D_{t}F,\,U_{t}\rangle_{\mathrm{free}}\big)\,\mathrm{d}t,\qquad\delta=D^{\ast}.

(53)

C.2  Proof of Lemma
1
(Free Clark–Ocone Formula)

The operator
D
is densely defined and closable on
L^{2}(\mathcal{A},\varphi)
,
and
\delta=D^{\ast}
by (

).
For
F\in\mathrm{Dom}(D)

u_{t}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\mathbb{E}_{\varphi}[D_{t}F\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}]
.
Then
u\in L^{2}_{\mathrm{ad}}([0,T];\mathcal{A})

and
\delta(u)=\int_{0}^{T}u_{t}\,\#\,\mathrm{d}S_{t}
is well defined.
Using orthogonality of the conditional expectation and the adjointness
relation, one obtains

\varphi[F\,\delta(G)]=\int_{0}^{T}\varphi[\langle D_{t}F,G_{t}\rangle_{\mathrm{free}}]\,\mathrm{d}t=\int_{0}^{T}\varphi[\langle u_{t},G_{t}\rangle_{\mathrm{free}}]\,\mathrm{d}t=\varphi\!\left[\!\Big(\!\int_{0}^{T}u_{t}\,\#\,\mathrm{d}S_{t}\!\Big)G\!\right],

for all
G\in\mathrm{Dom}(\delta)
.
By density of
\mathrm{Dom}(\delta)
in
L^{2}(\mathcal{A},\varphi)
,
we identify
F-\varphi[F]=\delta(u)
,
yielding (
9
).
Uniqueness of
u
follows from the free Itô isometry
(
52
):
if
\int_{0}^{T}(u_{t}-v_{t})\#\mathrm{d}S_{t}=0
then
u=v
in
L^{2}
.

\square

Remarks.

(i)

)
extends by

Applied to the terminal variable

Substituting the explicit variation–of–constants solution of (F)

.
The conditional expectation isolates only the deterministic damping

C.3  Free Time–Reversal of Semicircular Diffusions

The rigorous time–reversal step in
Theorem
1

follows from Biane’s duality theorem for free stochastic integrals

[
37
, Proposition 3.1]
.
For any adapted process
U_{t}\in L^{2}(\mathcal{A},\varphi)
define

\widetilde{S}_{s}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=S_{T}-S_{T-s},\qquad\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{s}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\sigma\{\widetilde{S}_{u}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}0\leq u\leq s\}.

Then
(\widetilde{S}_{s})_{s\in[0,T]}
is a

backward free Brownian motion
, i.e.,
a semicircular process with freely independent
increments relative to the reversed filtration

(\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{s})
satisfying

\varphi[(\widetilde{S}_{s_{2}}-\widetilde{S}_{s_{1}})^{2}]=s_{2}-s_{1},\qquad 0\leq s_{1}<s_{2}\leq T.

Biane’s result establishes the
L^{2}
identity

\int_{0}^{T}U_{t}\,\#\,\mathrm{d}S_{t}=\int_{0}^{T}U_{T-s}\,\#\,\mathrm{d}\widetilde{S}_{s},

(54)

expressing a duality between forward and backward
free stochastic integrals.
Consequently, if
X_{t}
satisfies the forward SDE (F),
then for
\widetilde{X}_{s}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=X_{T-s}
one obtains

\mathrm{d}\widetilde{X}_{s}=\Bigl(-\tfrac{1}{2}\beta(T-s)\widetilde{X}_{s}-\beta(T-s)\,\Xi_{T-s}\Bigr)\mathrm{d}s+\sqrt{\beta(T-s)}\,\mathrm{d}\widetilde{S}_{s},

where
\Xi_{t}
is the conjugate variable of
X_{t}
.
The square–integrability of
\Xi_{t}
and
X_{t}
ensures that the coefficients satisfy
the conditions of the free Itô theory

[
14
,
42
]
,
so that (R) is well posed in
L^{2}(\mathcal{A},\varphi)
.
This completes the rigorous justification of
the reverse–time free SDE and the Clark–Ocone representation used in
Theorem
1
.

Appendix D
Derivation of
\nabla\chi[\mu](x)=\tfrac{1}{2}x+\Xi(x)

In this appendix, we derive the gradient of Voiculescu’s free entropy functional and explain how it corresponds to the Euler–Lagrange condition of a free McKean–Vlasov functional.

D-A

Voiculescu’s Free Entropy Functional

For a self-adjoint noncommutative random variable
X
with spectral distribution
\mu
,
Voiculescu’s free entropy
\chi(\mu)
is defined by

\chi(\mu)=\iint\log|x-y|\,d\mu(x)d\mu(y)-\frac{1}{2}\int x^{2}d\mu(x).

(55)

The first term is a logarithmic interaction energy, while the second acts as a quadratic confinement.

D-B

Variational Derivative

For a perturbation
\mu_{\varepsilon}=\mu+\varepsilon\nu
with
\int d\nu=0
,
differentiating (
55
) gives

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}\chi(\mu_{\varepsilon})\Big|_{\varepsilon=0}

\displaystyle=2\int\left(\int\log|x-y|\,d\mu(y)\right)d\nu(x)

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{2}\int x^{2}d\nu(x).

(56)

Thus, the variational derivative is

\frac{\delta\chi}{\delta\mu}(x)=2\int\log|x-y|\,d\mu(y)-\frac{1}{2}x^{2}.

(57)

D-C

Functional Gradient

Differentiating (
57
) with respect to
x
,

\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\frac{\delta\chi}{\delta\mu}(x)=2\,\mathrm{p.v.}\int\frac{1}{x-y}\,d\mu(y)-x,

(58)

where p.v. denotes the Cauchy principal value.

The principal value integral corresponds to the
conjugate variable

\Xi(x)
in free probability. Hence,

\nabla\chi[\mu](x)=\frac{1}{2}x+\Xi(x).

(59)

D-D

Euler–Lagrange Equation and Free McKean–Vlasov Flow

The free entropy
\chi(\mu)
can be regarded as the noncommutative analogue of the classical McKean–Vlasov free energy functional

\mathcal{F}(\mu)=\int V(x)\,d\mu(x)-\iint\log|x-y|\,d\mu(x)d\mu(y),

with
V(x)=\frac{1}{2}x^{2}
.
The stationary points of
\mathcal{F}(\mu)
satisfy the Euler–Lagrange condition

\frac{\delta\mathcal{F}}{\delta\mu}(x)=\text{constant}.

Taking the gradient with respect to
x
leads exactly to (
59
).

In this sense, the evolution

\frac{\partial\mu}{\partial t}=\nabla\cdot\bigg(\mu\nabla\frac{\delta\mathcal{F}}{\delta\mu}\bigg)

is a Wasserstein gradient flow of
\mathcal{F}
in the free setting.
The forward free SDE corresponds to an entropy-ascent flow, while the reverse SDE integrates the negative gradient of
\chi
, moving down this free energy landscape.

Remark 7
.

This variational interpretation connects free diffusion models to optimal transport on the space of spectral measures (free Wasserstein geometry). The reverse-time drift term of Theorem 1 can thus be understood as performing gradient descent on
-\chi(\mu)
.

Appendix E. Free Girsanov-Type Transformation and Thermodynamic Interpretation

This appendix gives a mathematically rigorous justification for the
change-of-drift formula appearing in the reverse-time free diffusion.
The argument follows the free stochastic calculus of
Biane and Speicher
[
14
]
, which provides the Itô and
Malliavin framework needed for such measure transformations.
Although no literal “Girsanov theorem” exists in the noncommutative
setting, the following result gives its exact free analogue.

E.1. Free Girsanov-type lemma

Let
(\mathcal{A},\tau)
be a tracial
W^{*}
-probability space and

(S_{t})_{t\geq 0}
a free Brownian motion adapted to the filtration

(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
.
Denote by
\delta
the free stochastic integral and by
D
the free
Malliavin derivative defined on
L^{2}(\mathcal{A},\tau)

(see
[
14
]
, §3).

Lemma 7

(Free Girsanov-type identity)
.

Let
u=(u_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
be an adapted process in

L^{2}([0,T];L^{2}(\mathcal{A},\tau))
.
Define

Z_{t}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=1+\int_{0}^{t}Z_{s}\,u_{s}\,\#\,dS_{s},\qquad 0\leq t\leq T.

Then
(Z_{t})
is an
L^{2}
-martingale with
\tau[Z_{t}]=1
for all
t
.
Moreover, for every adapted process
F_{t}\in L^{2}(\mathcal{A},\tau)
,

\int_{0}^{T}F_{t}\,\#\,dS_{t}=\int_{0}^{T}F_{t}\,\#\,d\widetilde{S}_{t}-\int_{0}^{T}F_{t}u_{t}\,dt,

where
\widetilde{S}_{t}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=S_{t}-\int_{0}^{t}u_{s}\,ds

is again a semicircular process with covariance
\tau[(\widetilde{S}_{t}-\widetilde{S}_{s})^{2}]=t-s
.
Hence, the pair
(\widetilde{S}_{t},\tau_{Z})
with

\tau_{Z}(A)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\tau[Z_{T}A]

defines the same free Brownian filtration under a tilted tracial state.

Proof.

We work in the tracial
W^{*}
–probability space
(\mathcal{A},\tau)

and use the free stochastic calculus of Biane and Speicher
[
14
]
.
All stochastic integrals are understood in the
L^{2}(\mathcal{A},\tau)
sense.

Step 1: Definition and
L^{2}
–continuity of the integral.

For an adapted process
A_{t}\in L^{2}(\mathcal{A},\tau)
,
the free stochastic integral
\int_{0}^{t}A_{s}\,\#\,dS_{s}
is defined as the

L^{2}
–limit of integrals of step processes.
By the free Itô isometry
[
14
, Prop. 3.2]
,

\tau\!\left[\!\left(\int_{0}^{t}A_{s}\,\#\,dS_{s}\right)\!\left(\int_{0}^{t}B_{s}\,\#\,dS_{s}\right)^{\!\!*}\!\right]=\int_{0}^{t}\tau[A_{s}B_{s}^{*}]\,ds,

(60)

so the integral extends continuously to all square-integrable adapted
processes.

Step 2: Martingale property of
Z_{t}
.

For simple adapted
u_{t}
, define recursively

Z_{t_{i+1}}=Z_{t_{i}}+Z_{t_{i}}U_{i}(S_{t_{i+1}}-S_{t_{i}})
.
By linearity of
\tau
and the centering of increments,

\tau[A(Z_{t}-Z_{s})]=\sum_{i\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\,t_{i}\in(s,t]}\tau[AZ_{t_{i}}U_{i}(S_{t_{i+1}}-S_{t_{i}})]=0

for all
A\in\mathcal{F}_{s}
,
because
(S_{t_{i+1}}-S_{t_{i}})
is
\ast
–free from
\mathcal{F}_{s}

and has mean zero.
Thus
(Z_{t})
is a martingale with respect to
(\mathcal{F}_{t})
.
The traciality of
\tau
and
\tau[dS_{t}]=0
give

\frac{d}{dt}\tau[Z_{t}]=0
,
so
\tau[Z_{t}]=\tau[Z_{0}]=1
for all
t
.

Step 3: Definition and properties of the shifted process.

Define

\widetilde{S}_{t}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=S_{t}-\int_{0}^{t}u_{s}\,ds.

Because
u_{s}
is adapted and belongs to
L^{2}(\mathcal{A},\tau)
,

\int_{0}^{t}u_{s}\,ds
lies in
\mathcal{F}_{t}
.
For
0\leq s<t
, compute

\tau[(\widetilde{S}_{t}-\widetilde{S}_{s})^{2}]=\tau[(S_{t}-S_{s})^{2}]-2\!\int_{s}^{t}\tau[(S_{t}-S_{s})u_{r}]\,dr+\int_{s}^{t}\!\!\int_{s}^{t}\tau[u_{r}u_{v}]\,dr\,dv.

The mixed term vanishes because
(S_{t}-S_{s})
is
\ast
–free from

\mathcal{F}_{s}\subset\mathcal{F}_{r}
for
r\in[s,t]

and centered, giving

\tau[(S_{t}-S_{s})u_{r}]=0.

Hence

\tau[(\widetilde{S}_{t}-\widetilde{S}_{s})^{2}]=t-s,

so
(\widetilde{S}_{t})
has the same semicircular covariance as
(S_{t})
.
Freeness of increments follows from the freeness of

(S_{t}-S_{s})
from
\mathcal{F}_{s}
and the fact that

\int_{s}^{t}u_{r}\,dr\in\mathcal{F}_{t}
is adapted but deterministic with
respect to the past filtration, preserving
\ast
–freeness.

Step 4: Substitution in stochastic integrals.

For any adapted
F_{t}\in L^{2}(\mathcal{A},\tau)
,
we can write using Itô linearity,

\int_{0}^{T}F_{t}\,\#\,dS_{t}=\int_{0}^{T}F_{t}\,\#\,(d\widetilde{S}_{t}+u_{t}\,dt)=\int_{0}^{T}F_{t}\,\#\,d\widetilde{S}_{t}+\int_{0}^{T}F_{t}u_{t}\,dt,

where both integrals exist in
L^{2}(\mathcal{A},\tau)

by (
60
).
The last term is a Bochner integral in
L^{2}(\mathcal{A},\tau)
.
Rearranging yields the transformation rule

\int_{0}^{T}F_{t}\,\#\,d\widetilde{S}_{t}=\int_{0}^{T}F_{t}\,\#\,dS_{t}-\int_{0}^{T}F_{t}u_{t}\,dt.

Combining this identity with Steps 2–3 completes the proof.
∎

E.2. Application to the reverse-time SDE

Applying Lemma
7
to the forward free
Ornstein–Uhlenbeck equation

dX_{t}=-\tfrac{1}{2}\beta(t)X_{t}\,dt+\sqrt{\beta(t)}\,dS_{t}

with the choice
u_{t}=-\sqrt{\beta(t)}\,\Xi_{t}
,
where
\Xi_{t}
is the conjugate variable of
X_{t}
,
one obtains the reversed noise process

d\widetilde{S}_{t}=dS_{t}+\sqrt{\beta(t)}\,\Xi_{t}\,dt,

under which the reverse-time dynamics

d\widetilde{X}_{s}=\Big(-\tfrac{1}{2}\beta(T-s)\widetilde{X}_{s}-\beta(T-s)\Xi_{T-s}\Big)\,ds+\sqrt{\beta(T-s)}\,d\widetilde{S}_{s}

hold in the same algebraic filtration.
This gives the rigorous noncommutative analogue of the
classical Girsanov drift shift.

E.3. Entropy production and Schrödinger bridge

Under the change of tracial state
\tau\mapsto\tau_{Z}
,
the free entropy satisfies the differential identity

\frac{d}{dt}\chi(\mu_{t})=\frac{1}{2}\Phi^{\ast}(\mu_{t})-\tau[u_{t}^{2}],

so that the path minimizing

\mathcal{E}[u]=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\tau[u_{t}^{2}]\,dt

subject to fixed boundary laws
(\mu_{0},\mu_{T})

yields the
free Schrödinger bridge
.
This gives a variational formulation of the reverse diffusion
as the least-dissipative interpolation between spectral marginals.

p
-plane but not flat” Riemannian manifolds. We now proceed to the main results of this section. Consider, for any oriented Riemannian

Theorem 1

Let

Space forms always satisfy

Here is a more interesting family of examples:

Corollary 2
.

Let

be an oriented Riemannian locally symmetric space of dimension

3.
Pure curvature operators: two special cases

Before analyzing the commuting condition
*\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$2n$}}=\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$2n$}}*
for the class of
pure curvature operators
more generally (see Definition
2
in Section

), in this section we first examine the existence of normal forms for two distinguished subclasses, beginning with the locally conformally flat (LCF) class. The following characterization of LCF manifolds will play a crucial role:

Theorem 3
.

For
n\geq 4
, a Riemannian
n
-manifold is locally conformally flat if and only if at every point there is an orthonormal basis
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{n}\}
and constants
\{a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\}
such that
\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}(e_{i}\wedge e_{j})=(a_{i}+a_{j})e_{i}\wedge e_{j}
.

Proof.

Suppose that the oriented Riemannian
n
-manifold is LCF, with Ricci tensor
Ric
and scalar curvature
\text{scal}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}
. Let
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{n}\}
be a local orthonormal frame that diagonalizes the Ricci tensor,
\text{Ric}(e_{i},e_{j})=\lambda_{ij}\delta_{ij}=\lambda_{ji}\delta_{ij}
, and hence also the Schouten tensor
P
:

P(e_{i},e_{j})=\frac{1}{n-2}\Big(\text{Ric}-\frac{\text{scal}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}}{2(n-1)}g\Big)\Big|_{(e_{i},e_{j})}=\frac{1}{n-2}\Big(\lambda_{ij}-\frac{\text{scal}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}}{2(n-1)}\Big)\delta_{ij}.

Since the Weyl tensor vanishes, we have that
\text{Rm}=P\,{\tiny\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\displaystyle\bigcirc$\cr$\displaystyle\wedge$\cr}}}{}}{\mathbin{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\textstyle\bigcirc$\cr$\textstyle\wedge$\cr}}}{}}{\mathbin{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$\cr$\scriptstyle\wedge$\cr}}}{}}{\mathbin{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\bigcirc$\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\wedge$\cr}}}{}}}\,g
(recall (
2
)), hence

(27)

\displaystyle\text{Rm}(e_{i},e_{j},e_{k},e_{l})=P_{il}\delta_{jk}+P_{jk}\delta_{il}-P_{ik}\delta_{jl}-P_{jl}\delta_{ik}.

From this it follows that
\text{Rm}(e_{i},e_{j},e_{k},e_{l})=0
if
\{i,j\}\neq\{k,l\}
(incidentally, this is precisely the “pure” condition; (
27
) also indicates our sign convention for the Kulkarni-Nomizu product
{\tiny\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\displaystyle\bigcirc$\cr$\displaystyle\wedge$\cr}}}{}}{\mathbin{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\textstyle\bigcirc$\cr$\textstyle\wedge$\cr}}}{}}{\mathbin{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$\cr$\scriptstyle\wedge$\cr}}}{}}{\mathbin{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\bigcirc$\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\wedge$\cr}}}{}}}
). Thus any LCF manifold satisfies

(28)

\displaystyle\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}(e_{i}\wedge e_{j})=\text{Rm}(e_{i},e_{j},e_{i},e_{j})\,e_{i}\wedge e_{j}=-(P_{ii}+P_{jj})e_{i}\wedge e_{j}.

Setting
a_{i}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=-P_{ii}
, we have that
\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}(e_{i}\wedge e_{j})=(a_{i}+a_{j})e_{i}\wedge e_{j}
.
Conversely, suppose that such a basis
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{n}\}
and constants
\{a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\}
exist; we show that the metric must be LCF. First, because
\text{Rm}(e_{i},e_{j},e_{i},e_{j})=a_{i}+a_{j}
and vanishes whenever
\{i,k\}\neq\{k,l\}
, we have that

\displaystyle\text{Ric}(e_{j},e_{k})\!\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!-\sum_{i\neq j}\text{Rm}(e_{i},e_{j},e_{i},e_{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!-\delta_{jk}\sum_{i\neq j}(a_{i}+a_{j})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!-\delta_{jk}\Big((n-2)a_{j}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\Big).

Furthermore, because

\text{scal}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}=2\sum_{j=2}^{n}\sum_{i<j}\text{Rm}(e_{i},e_{j},e_{j},e_{i})=-2\sum_{j=2}^{n}\sum_{i<j}(a_{i}+a_{j})=-2(n-1)\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i},

the Schouten tensor in this frame is given simply by

P(e_{j},e_{k})=\frac{1}{n-2}\Big(\text{Ric}(e_{j},e_{k})-\frac{\text{scal}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}}{2(n-1)}\delta_{jk}\Big)=-a_{j}\delta_{jk}.

To finish the proof, note that

\displaystyle\text{Rm}(e_{i},e_{j},e_{k},e_{l})\!\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!-(a_{i}\delta_{il}\delta_{jk}+a_{j}\delta_{jk}\delta_{il}-a_{i}\delta_{ik}\delta_{jl}-a_{j}\delta_{jl}\delta_{ik})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!(P\,{\tiny\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\displaystyle\bigcirc$\cr$\displaystyle\wedge$\cr}}}{}}{\mathbin{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\textstyle\bigcirc$\cr$\textstyle\wedge$\cr}}}{}}{\mathbin{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$\cr$\scriptstyle\wedge$\cr}}}{}}{\mathbin{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\bigcirc$\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\wedge$\cr}}}{}}}\,g)(e_{i},e_{j},e_{k},e_{l}),

so that
\text{Rm}=P\,{\tiny\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\displaystyle\bigcirc$\cr$\displaystyle\wedge$\cr}}}{}}{\mathbin{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\textstyle\bigcirc$\cr$\textstyle\wedge$\cr}}}{}}{\mathbin{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$\cr$\scriptstyle\wedge$\cr}}}{}}{\mathbin{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\bigcirc$\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\wedge$\cr}}}{}}}\,g
, hence
W=0
. This completes the proof.
∎

(Note that, while every Riemannian 3-manifold can be shown to satisfy

\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}(e_{i}\wedge e_{j})=(s_{i}t_{j}+s_{j}t_{i})e_{i}\wedge e_{j}
. Every such
(M,g)
has a normal form.

Proof.

Because
S
and
T
commute, they can be simultaneously diagonalized by some local orthonormal frame
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{n}\}
; let us denote their eigenvalues by
\bar{s}_{i}
and
t_{i}
, respectively (that
S
and
T
commute means that
\hat{S}\circ\hat{T}=\hat{T}\circ\hat{S}
,
where
\hat{S},\hat{T}\colon TM\longrightarrow TM
are defined by
\hat{S}(v)\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}S(e_{i},v)e_{i}
and
\hat{T}(v)\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}T(e_{i},v)e_{i}
; note that
\hat{S},\hat{T}
are, by construction,
g
-self-adjoint). We then have

\text{Rm}(e_{i},e_{j},e_{k},e_{l})=S_{il}T_{jk}+S_{jk}T_{il}-S_{ik}T_{jl}-S_{jl}T_{ik}.

As with (
27
) and (
28
), it follows that
\text{Rm}(e_{i},e_{j},e_{k},e_{l})=0
if
\{i,j\}\neq\{k,l\}
, and hence that
\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}
satisfies

(39)

\displaystyle\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}(e_{i}\wedge e_{j})=\text{Rm}(e_{i},e_{j},e_{i},e_{j})e_{i}\wedge e_{j}=-(\bar{s}_{i}t_{j}+\bar{s}_{j}t_{i})e_{i}\wedge e_{j}.

Setting
s_{i}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=-\bar{s}_{i}
puts
\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}
in the desired form. Finally, that
(M,g)
has a normal form follows as in Corollary
3
.
∎

Recall that in the LCF case the converse was also true, but that need not be the case here. Indeed, if we suppose that at every point of
M
, a local orthonormal frame
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{n}\}
and functions
\{s_{1},\dots,s_{n}\},\{t_{1},\dots,t_{n}\}
exist such that
\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}(e_{i}\wedge e_{j})=(s_{i}t_{j}+s_{j}t_{i})e_{i}\wedge e_{j}
, it does not necessarily follow that
\text{Rm}=S\,{\tiny\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\displaystyle\bigcirc$\cr$\displaystyle\wedge$\cr}}}{}}{\mathbin{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\textstyle\bigcirc$\cr$\textstyle\wedge$\cr}}}{}}{\mathbin{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$\cr$\scriptstyle\wedge$\cr}}}{}}{\mathbin{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\bigcirc$\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\wedge$\cr}}}{}}}\,T
. While one can locally define symmetric and commuting 2-tensors
S
and
T
by
S(e_{i},e_{j})\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=\bar{s}_{i}\delta_{ij}
and
T(e_{i},e_{j})\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=t_{i}\delta_{ij}
, these need not collectively yield a globally defined pair of 2-tensors
S,T
on
M
. And although the Ricci tensor is diagonal in these frames,

\text{Ric}(e_{j},e_{k})=-\delta_{jk}\sum_{i\neq j}(s_{i}t_{j}+s_{j}t_{i}),

the expression
s_{i}t_{j}+s_{j}t_{i}
does not permit the same analysis as in Theorem
3
. Nevertheless, two special cases arise: The case when
T=g
, and the case when
T=S
. The former yields
s_{i}t_{j}+s_{j}t_{i}=s_{i}+s_{j}
and thus LCF, while the latter directly generalizes space forms and will be examined in Theorem
7
below. For now, let us identify a class of manifolds satisfying
\text{Rm}=S\,{\tiny\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\displaystyle\bigcirc$\cr$\displaystyle\wedge$\cr}}}{}}{\mathbin{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\textstyle\bigcirc$\cr$\textstyle\wedge$\cr}}}{}}{\mathbin{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$\cr$\scriptstyle\wedge$\cr}}}{}}{\mathbin{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\bigcirc$\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\wedge$\cr}}}{}}}\,T
, to which we shall return later:

Proposition 3
.

Products of space forms
(M_{1}\times M_{2},g_{1}\oplus g_{2})
with curvatures of opposite signs satisfy
\text{Rm}=S\,{\tiny\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\displaystyle\bigcirc$\cr$\displaystyle\wedge$\cr}}}{}}{\mathbin{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\textstyle\bigcirc$\cr$\textstyle\wedge$\cr}}}{}}{\mathbin{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$\cr$\scriptstyle\wedge$\cr}}}{}}{\mathbin{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\bigcirc$\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\wedge$\cr}}}{}}}\,T
as in Theorem
4
.

Proof.

For products of space forms,

\text{Rm}=c_{1}g_{1}\,{\tiny\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\displaystyle\bigcirc$\cr$\displaystyle\wedge$\cr}}}{}}{\mathbin{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\textstyle\bigcirc$\cr$\textstyle\wedge$\cr}}}{}}{\mathbin{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$\cr$\scriptstyle\wedge$\cr}}}{}}{\mathbin{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\bigcirc$\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\wedge$\cr}}}{}}}\,g_{1}+c_{2}g_{2}\,{\tiny\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\displaystyle\bigcirc$\cr$\displaystyle\wedge$\cr}}}{}}{\mathbin{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\textstyle\bigcirc$\cr$\textstyle\wedge$\cr}}}{}}{\mathbin{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$\cr$\scriptstyle\wedge$\cr}}}{}}{\mathbin{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\bigcirc$\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\wedge$\cr}}}{}}}\,g_{2},

where
c_{1},c_{2}
are the constant sectional curvatures of
g_{1},g_{2}
, respectively. If either
c_{1}=0
or
c_{2}=0
, then we are done; otherwise, setting
S\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=a_{1}g_{1}+a_{2}g_{2}
and
T\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=b_{1}g_{1}+b_{2}g_{2}
, it follows that

\text{Rm}=S\,{\tiny\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\displaystyle\bigcirc$\cr$\displaystyle\wedge$\cr}}}{}}{\mathbin{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\textstyle\bigcirc$\cr$\textstyle\wedge$\cr}}}{}}{\mathbin{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$\cr$\scriptstyle\wedge$\cr}}}{}}{\mathbin{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\bigcirc$\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\wedge$\cr}}}{}}}\,T\iff a_{1}b_{1}=c_{1}\hskip 7.22743pt,\hskip 7.22743pta_{2}b_{2}=c_{2}\hskip 7.22743pt,\hskip 7.22743pta_{1}b_{2}+a_{2}b_{1}=0.

Since
c_{1},c_{2}\neq 0
, we must have
a_{1},a_{2},b_{1},b_{2}\neq 0
. Multiplying the latter equation by
b_{1}b_{2}
then yields
0=a_{1}b_{1}b_{2}^{2}+a_{2}b_{1}^{2}b_{2}=c_{1}b_{2}^{2}+c_{2}b_{1}^{2}
, which cannot be satisfied unless
c_{1},c_{2}
have opposite signs. As
S
and
T
are symmetric, self-adjoint, and commuting 2-tensors, this completes the proof.
∎

4.
Pure curvature operators: the general case

LCF manifolds, as well as those of the form
\text{Rm}=S\,{\tiny\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\displaystyle\bigcirc$\cr$\displaystyle\wedge$\cr}}}{}}{\mathbin{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\textstyle\bigcirc$\cr$\textstyle\wedge$\cr}}}{}}{\mathbin{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$\cr$\scriptstyle\wedge$\cr}}}{}}{\mathbin{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\bigcirc$\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\wedge$\cr}}}{}}}\,T
in Theorem
4
, belong to the class of
pure curvature operators
, after H. Maillot
[
20
]
(see also
[
4
, p. 439]
). In this section we examine such operators more generally, our goal being to find criteria guaranteeing
*\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$2n$}}=\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$2n$}}*,
and thus
\chi(M)\geq 0
in the compact setting. Let us begin with the definition:

Definition 2

(Pure curvature operator)
.

A Riemannian
n
-manifold is
pure at
q\in M
if there is an orthonormal frame
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{n}\}\subseteq T_{q}M
such that the corresponding basis
\{e_{i}\wedge e_{j}\}\subseteq\Lambda^{2}
diagonalizes its curvature operator
\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}\colon\Lambda^{2}\longrightarrow\Lambda^{2}
. Equivalently,
\text{Rm}(e_{i},e_{j},e_{k},e_{l})=0
whenever
\{i,j\}\neq\{k,l\}
.

While every
\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}
is self-adjoint and therefore diagonalizable, what is not guaranteed is that its eigenbasis can always be chosen to take the form
\{e_{i}\wedge e_{j}\}
; therefore, not every Riemannian manifold will have pure curvature operator. Which ones do? As we’ve already seen, LCF manifolds (recall (
28
)), and more generally those of the form
\text{Rm}=S\,{\tiny\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\displaystyle\bigcirc$\cr$\displaystyle\wedge$\cr}}}{}}{\mathbin{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\textstyle\bigcirc$\cr$\textstyle\wedge$\cr}}}{}}{\mathbin{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$\cr$\scriptstyle\wedge$\cr}}}{}}{\mathbin{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\bigcirc$\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\wedge$\cr}}}{}}}\,T
(recall (
39
)), do. In dimensions two and three, every Riemannian manifold has pure curvature operator
[
2
]
, because in these dimensions all 2-vectors are decomposable and every
\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}
-eigenbasis arises from an orthonormal basis
\{e_{i}\}
. Starting in dimension four, however, curvature operators without this property emerge. E.g., the Fubini-Study metric on
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
does not have it, because its Kähler 2-form
\omega
spans a one-dimensional eigenspace of
\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}
and yet is not decomposable (
\omega\wedge\omega\neq 0
).
Neither does any bi-invariant metric on
SU(3)
, as can be seen by extending any
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{8}\}
to left-invariant vector fields and using
R(e_{i},e_{j})e_{k}=\frac{1}{4}[e_{k},[e_{i},e_{j}]]
. Both of these examples generalize.
Much is already known regarding the geometry and topology of pure curvature operators on compact manifolds. To begin with, the property of being pure is preserved under products. Moreover, as shown in
[
10
]
, if
\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}\geq 0
and
M
is compact and simply connected, then its irreducible de Rham components are homoeomorphic to spheres. If
\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}>0
, then
g
must have positive sectional curvature, hence is diffeomorphic to a spherical space form, by
[
5
]
; see
[
22
]
. Finally, all Pontryagin forms of a pure curvature operator vanish
[
20
]
(trace polynomials of curvature 2-forms will always wedge-repeat and hence vanish), so that in fact
\mathbb{CP}^{n}
is ruled out for all
n
and all metrics on it — note, however, that the vanishing of Pontryagin numbers alone does not yield
\chi(M)\geq 0
; for the latter, we will have to rely on
*\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$2n$}}=\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$2n$}}*
. Regarding closed LCF manifolds in particular, classifications are known, in particular when
M
is simply connected
[
15
]
, when
\text{Ric}\geq 0

[
26
,
6
]
, or when
\text{scal}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}\geq 0

[
12
,
8
,
7
]
. Let us begin by noting that being pure is a stronger condition than that of
\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}
merely being diagonalizable by 2-planes:

Lemma 1
.

For any
2n
-dimensional inner product space
V
, there exist bases of 2-panes for
\Lambda^{2}(V)
that cannot be expressed as
\{e_{i}\wedge e_{j}\}
with respect to any orthonormal basis
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{2n}\}\subseteq V
.

Proof.

Take any orthonormal basis
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{2n}\}
, set
S\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=\text{span}\{e_{2},\dots,e_{2n}\}
and define
v_{2}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(e_{2}+e_{3})
,
v_{3}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(e_{2}-e_{3})
, and
v_{4}=e_{4},\dots,v_{2n}=e_{2n}
; note that
\{v_{2},\dots,v_{2n}\}
is an orthonormal basis for
S
. Now consider the
{2n\choose 2}
linearly independent 2-planes given by

(40)

\displaystyle\{e_{1}\wedge e_{i}\,:\,i=2,\dots,2n\}\ \cup\ \{v_{i}\wedge v_{j}\,:\,2\leq i<j\leq 2n\}.

These comprise a basis for
\Lambda^{2}(V)
. We claim that there is no orthonormal basis
\{w_{1},\dots,w_{2n}\}\subseteq V
with respect to which each 2-vector in (
40
) can be expressed as
w_{i}\wedge w_{j}
. Indeed, if there was, then
e_{1}\wedge e_{2}=w_{i}\wedge w_{j}
and
e_{1}\wedge e_{3}=w_{r}\wedge w_{s}
for some
i,j,r,s
. Because
(w_{i}\wedge w_{j})\wedge(w_{r}\wedge w_{s})=0
, the vectors
w_{i},w_{j},w_{r},w_{s}
are linearly dependent, a contradiction unless two of the indices
i,j,r,s
are equal. In particular, either
r=i
or
r=j
, or else
s=i
or
s=j
. If
r=i
, then the fact that
\text{span}\{e_{1},e_{2}\}=\text{span}\{w_{i},w_{j}\}
and
\text{span}\{e_{1},e_{3}\}=\text{span}\{w_{i},w_{l}\}
yields

ae_{1}+be_{2}=w_{i}=ce_{1}+de_{3}

for some
a,b,c,d\in\mathbb{R}
. We must have
a=c
(for otherwise
e_{1}\in\text{span}\{e_{2},e_{3}\}
), hence
be_{2}=de_{3}
, which can only be satisfied if
b=d=0
; thus
w_{i}=\pm e_{1}
. As a consequence,

w_{j}=aw_{1}+be_{2}
and
w_{l}=cw_{1}+de_{3}

for some
a,b,c,d\in\mathbb{R}
. That
w_{j},w_{l}
are orthogonal yields
a=c=0
, so that
w_{j}=\pm e_{2}
and
w_{l}=\pm e_{3}
. The same conclusion would have been reached if
r=j
,
s=i
, or
s=j
. The same analysis on
e_{1}\wedge e_{4},\dots,e_{1}\wedge e_{4k}
yields that, up to re-ordering of indices, each
w_{i}=\pm e_{i}
. In particular, each
v_{i}\wedge v_{j}=\pm e_{r}\wedge e_{s}
for some
r,s\in\{2,\dots,2n\}
. But as
v_{2}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(e_{2}+e_{3})
,

v_{2}\wedge v_{j}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(e_{2}+e_{3})\wedge e_{j}

for all
j=4,\dots,2n
, a contradiction.
∎

Next, we observe that not just manifolds of the form
\text{Rm}=S\,{\tiny\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\displaystyle\bigcirc$\cr$\displaystyle\wedge$\cr}}}{}}{\mathbin{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\textstyle\bigcirc$\cr$\textstyle\wedge$\cr}}}{}}{\mathbin{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$\cr$\scriptstyle\wedge$\cr}}}{}}{\mathbin{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\bigcirc$\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\wedge$\cr}}}{}}}\,T
, but in fact all pure curvature operators, have normal forms:

Theorem 5
.

Any Riemannian manifold with pure curvature operator has a normal form.

Proof.

This follows directly from (
38
).
∎

We now examine when a pure curvature operator satisfies the commuting condition
*\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$2n$}}=\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$2n$}}*
, without assuming
\text{Ric}\geq 0
or
\text{scal}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}\geq 0
. In general,
*\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$2n$}}=\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$2n$}}*
leads to a family of algebraic hafnian equations ((
4
) below) in the eigenvalues of
\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}
:

Proposition 4
.

Let
(M,g)
be an oriented Riemannian
4n
-manifold with pure curvature operator: At each point there exists an orthonormal frame
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{4n}\}
such that

\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}(e_{i}\wedge e_{j})=\lambda_{ij}(e_{i}\wedge e_{j})\hskip 14.45377pt,\hskip 14.45377pt\lambda_{ij}=-\emph{sec}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}(e_{i}\wedge e_{j})=\emph{\text{Rm}}(e_{i},e_{j},e_{i},e_{j}).

For any
2n
-subset
I\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=\{i_{1},\dots,i_{2n}\}\subseteq\{1,\dots,4n\}
and its complementary subset
I^{c}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=\{j_{1},\dots,j_{2n}\}
, let
\lambda_{rs}
denote the eigenvalues of all 2-vectors
e_{i_{\scalebox{0.6}{$r$}}}\wedge e_{i_{\scalebox{0.6}{$s$}}}
indexed from
I
, and
\bar{\lambda}_{rs}
the eigenvalues of all 2-vectors
e_{j_{\scalebox{0.6}{$r$}}}\wedge e_{j_{\scalebox{0.6}{$s$}}}
indexed from
I^{c}
. Then
*\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$2n$}}=\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$2n$}}*
if and only if for each choice of
I
,

\displaystyle\left.\begin{array}[]{l}\text{the sum over all $n$-products of the $\lambda_{rs}$'s}\\
\hskip 36.135pt=\text{the sum over all $n$-products of the $\bar{\lambda}_{rs}$'s.}\end{array}\right\}

I.e.,
\text{haf}(I)=\text{haf}(I^{c})
, where
\text{haf}(I)
is the hafnian of the
2n\times 2n
principal submatrix with rows/columns indexed by
I
of the
4n\times 4n
symmetric matrix
(\lambda_{ij})

(
with
\lambda_{ii}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=0
)
; similarly for
\text{haf}(I^{c})
.

Proof.

Making use of (
5
), the action of
\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$2n$}}\colon\Lambda^{2n}\longrightarrow\Lambda^{2n}
on the corresponding frame
\{e_{i_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge e_{i_{2n}}\}\subseteq\Lambda^{2n}
is

(44)

\displaystyle\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$2n$}}(e_{i_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge e_{i_{2n}})\!\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\frac{1}{(2n)!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{2n}}\!\!\varepsilon(\sigma)\!\!\!\!\underbrace{\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}(e_{\scalebox{0.5}{$\sigma(i_{1})$}}\wedge e_{\scalebox{0.5}{$\sigma(i_{2})$}})}_{\text{$\lambda_{\sigma(i_{1})\sigma(i_{2})}(e_{\scalebox{0.5}{$\sigma(i_{1})$}}\wedge e_{\scalebox{0.5}{$\sigma(i_{2})$}})$}}\!\!\!\!\wedge\cdots\wedge\hskip-15.89948pt\overbrace{\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}(e_{\scalebox{0.5}{$\sigma(i_{2n-1})$}}\wedge e_{\scalebox{0.5}{$\sigma(i_{2n})$}})}^{\text{$\lambda_{\sigma(i_{2n-1})\sigma(i_{2n})}(e_{\scalebox{0.5}{$\sigma(i_{2n-1})$}}\wedge e_{\scalebox{0.5}{$\sigma(i_{2n})$}})$}}

\displaystyle\hskip-72.26999pt=\,\frac{1}{(2n)!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{2n}}\!\!(\lambda_{\sigma(i_{1})\sigma(i_{2})}\cdots\lambda_{\sigma(i_{2n-1})\sigma(i_{2n})})(e_{i_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge e_{i_{2n}})

where the
\lambda_{ij}
’s are the eigenvalues of
\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}
(though not all of them), and where we note that each
\varepsilon(\sigma)(e_{\scalebox{0.5}{$\sigma(i_{1})$}}\wedge\cdots\wedge e_{\scalebox{0.5}{$\sigma(i_{2n})$}})=e_{i_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge e_{i_{2n}}
. Now we verify
\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$2n$}}\,*=*\,\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$2n$}}
. Setting
e_{j_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge e_{j_{2n}}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=*\,(e_{i_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge e_{i_{2n}})
and applying
*
to (
44
) yields

(45)

\displaystyle(*\,\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$2n$}})(e_{i_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge e_{i_{2n}})\!\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hskip-101.17755pt\frac{1}{(2n)!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{2n}}\!\!(\lambda_{\sigma(i_{1})\sigma(i_{2})}\cdots\lambda_{\sigma(i_{2n-1})\sigma(i_{2n})})(e_{j_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge e_{j_{2n}}).

Similarly, the composition
\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$2n$}}\,*
yields

(46)

\displaystyle\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$2n$}}(*(e_{i_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge e_{i_{2n}}))\!\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hskip-108.405pt\frac{1}{(2n)!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{2n}}\!\!(\bar{\lambda}_{\sigma(j_{1})\sigma(j_{2})}\cdots\bar{\lambda}_{\sigma(j_{2n-1})\sigma(j_{2n})})(e_{j_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge e_{j_{2n}}).

This is precisely (
4
).
∎

For
n=1
, (
4
) yields the following equations for the
{4\choose 2}=6
eigenvalues
\{\lambda_{12},\lambda_{13},\lambda_{14},\lambda_{23},\lambda_{24},\lambda_{34}\}
:

(47)

\displaystyle\underbrace{\,\lambda_{12}=\lambda_{34}\,}_{\text{$I=\{1,2\},I^{c}=\{3,4\}$}}\hskip 14.45377pt,\hskip 14.45377pt\underbrace{\,\lambda_{13}=\lambda_{24}\,}_{\text{$I=\{1,3\},I^{c}=\{2,4\}$}}\hskip 14.45377pt,\hskip 14.45377pt\underbrace{\,\lambda_{14}=\lambda_{23}\,}_{\text{$I=\{1,4\},I^{c}=\{2,3\}$}}.

(Note that this is indeed
\text{haf}(I)=\text{haf}(I^{c})
.) Using
[
24
]
, the following can be said about the Betti numbers
b_{i}
of closed 4-manifolds satisfying (
47
):

Corollary 4
.

Let
(M,g)
be a closed, connected, and oriented Einstein 4-manifold with pure curvature operator
\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}
. Let
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}
denote the two smallest distinct eigenvalues of
\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}
. If
2\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}>0
, then
b_{1}=b_{3}=0
.

Proof.

(By
[
3
,
25
]
,
*\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}=\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}\,*
is equivalent to being Einstein, with
\chi(M)\geq 0
(and equal to
0
if and only if
g
is flat); thus

\chi(M)=b_{0}-b_{1}+b_{2}-b_{3}+b_{4}=2-2b_{1}+b_{2}\geq 0,

whence
b_{1}\leq 1+\frac{1}{2}b_{2}
.) By (
47
),
\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}
has at most three distinct eigenvalues, each repeated at least twice:
\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}\leq\lambda_{3}
. If
\lambda_{1}>0
, then
\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}>0
and
(M,g)
is diffeomorphic to a spherical space form
[
5
]
. If
\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}>0
— i.e., if
(M,g)
is
3

b_{1}=\cdots=b_{p}=0
and
b_{n-p}=\cdots=b_{n-1}=0
. However, the condition
s_{n}(a_{1},\dots,a_{2n})=0
does not force
\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}
to be
(4n-p)
-positive. Indeed, the manifold
\mathbb{S}^{4n-1}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
, equipped with the product of the standard metrics on
\mathbb{S}^{4n-1}
and
\mathbb{S}^{1}
, is locally conformally flat and has zero Euler characteristic, but its first Betti number
b_{1}=1
.) We close this section by noting that there is a well known family of
Lorentzian

2n
-manifolds that satisfy
\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$p$}}=0
for
every
even
4\leq p\leq 2n
and which are not flat in general
(\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$2$}}\neq 0)
, namely, the family of
pp-waves

g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}
on
\mathbb{R}^{2n}=\{(v,u,x^{3},\dots,x^{2n})\}
defined by

(56)

\displaystyle g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=dv\otimes du+du\otimes dv-2Vdu\otimes du+\sum_{i=3}^{2n}dx^{i}\otimes dx^{i},

where
V
is a smooth function on
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
independent of
v
. The characteristic feature of such metrics is the parallel lightlike vector field
\partial_{v}=\text{grad}_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}u
; i.e.,
g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}(\partial_{v},\partial_{v})=0
(lightlike) and
\nabla\partial_{v}=0
(parallel). They are the most well known of the Lorentzian “VSI” manifolds (see
[
9
]
). For their curvature properties, see, e.g.,
[
19
]
, which we call upon freely here:

Proposition 6
.

The class of pp-wave metrics
(\mathbb{R}^{2n},g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}})
given by (
56
) satisfies
\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$p$}}=0
for all even
4\leq p\leq 2n
.

Proof.

First, note that the definitions of the
p^{\text{th}}
curvature tensor
R_{\scalebox{0.6}{$p$}}
in (
2
) and the
p^{\text{th}}
curvature operator
\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$p$}}
in (
5
) go through unchanged in the Lorentzian setting. Second, in the coordinate basis
\{\partial_{v},\partial_{u},\partial_{i}\}
, the only nonzero components of the Riemann curvature 4-tensor of
g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}
are

\text{Rm}(\partial_{i},\partial_{u},\partial_{u},\partial_{j})=V_{ij}\hskip 14.45377pt,\hskip 14.45377pti,j=3,\dots,2n.

As a consequence, the only nonzero components of the curvature operator
\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}
are

\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}(\partial_{u}\wedge\partial_{j})=\sum_{i=3}^{2n}\text{Rm}(\partial_{u},\partial_{j},\partial_{u},\partial_{i})\partial_{v}\wedge\partial_{i}=\sum_{i=3}^{2n}V_{ji}\,\partial_{i}\wedge\partial_{v}.

Inserting this into (
5
) yields
\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$p$}}=0
for all even
4\leq p\leq 2n
.
∎

Appendix: Normal forms in dimension 4

In this Appendix, we provide a further result on normal forms (Theorem
5
) by returning to the 4-dimensional setting of
[
3
,
28
,
25
]
. To movitate our result, let us first recall the following classical results:

i.

In the (oriented) Riemannian setting,
[
3
,
25
]
showed that Esintein 4-manifolds have a normal form: Their curvature operator
\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}
is determined by knowledge of just the critical points and values of
\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}
. I.e., in the Riemannian Einstein setting, knowledge of the first derivatives of
\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}
suffices. This was recently generalized in
[
1
,
2
]
.

ii.

In the (oriented) Lorentzian setting,
[
28
]
showed that Einstein 4-manifolds do
not
have a normal form (although their Petrov Types do). In order for the Lorentzian curvature operator
\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}
of an Einstein manifold to be determined by the critical points of
\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}$}}
(the latter being defined only for nondegenerate 2-planes), knowledge of the Hessian of
\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}$}}
at the critical points is also required. I.e., in the Lorentzian Einstein setting, knowledge of the first
and
second derivatives of
\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}
is needed in general.

Given this, we ask: What is needed from the critical point behavior of
\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}
in order to determine an
arbitrary

\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}
? Here is an answer to this question:

Theorem 9
.

Let
(M,g)
be an oriented Riemannian
4
-manifold and let
P
be a critical 2-plane of
\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}
at
p\in M
. If
*P
is also a critical 2-plane of
\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}
, then
\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}
can be determined at
p
from the critical values
\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}(P),\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}(*P)
, and the Hessian of
\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}
at
P
.

Proof.

Write
P=e_{3}\wedge e_{4}
for an orthonormal pair
\{e_{3},e_{4}\}
and extend this to an oriented orthonormal basis
\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3},e_{4}\}\subseteq T_{p}M
. With respect to this basis,
*P=*(e_{3}\wedge e_{4})=e_{1}\wedge e_{2}
. We calculate the critical points and Hessian of
\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}
via the coordinate chart
\phi\colon\mathbb{R}^{4}\longrightarrow\text{Gr}_{2}(T_{p}M)
defined by

\phi(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=\frac{(e_{3}+x_{3}e_{1}+x_{4}e_{2})\wedge(e_{4}+x_{1}e_{1}+x_{2}e_{2})}{\sqrt{1+x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}+x_{3}^{2}+x_{4}^{2}+(x_{1}x_{4}-x_{2}x_{3})^{2}}}\cdot

(Note that
\text{dim}\,\text{Gr}_{2}(T_{p}M)=2(4-2)=4
, that
\phi({\bf 0})=P
, that
\phi
is a diffeomorphism on a neighborhood of
{\bf 0}\in\mathbb{R}^{4}
, and that
\langle{\phi(x)},{\phi(x)}\rangle_{\!g_{\scalebox{0.3}{$L$}}}=+1
for every
x=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})
in its domain.) Then
P=e_{3}\wedge e_{4}
is a critical point of
\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}
if and only if

\frac{\partial(\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}\circ\phi)}{\partial x_{i}}\bigg|_{\bf 0}=0\hskip 14.45377pt,\hskip 14.45377pti=1,2,3,4.

To compute the case
i=1
, let us set
x_{2}=x_{3}=x_{4}=0
from the outset, which simplifies the computation:

(57)

\displaystyle\underbrace{\,\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}\bigg|_{\bf 0}\!\!(\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}\circ\phi){\scriptstyle\Big|_{\text{$(x_{1},0,0,0)$}}}\,}_{0}\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}\bigg|_{\bf 0}\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}\bigg(\frac{e_{3}\wedge(e_{4}+x_{1}e_{1})}{\sqrt{1+x_{1}^{2}}}\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}\bigg|_{\bf 0}\frac{\langle{\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}(e_{3}\wedge e_{4})+x_{1}\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}(e_{3}\wedge e_{1})},{e_{3}\wedge(e_{4}+x_{1}e_{1})}\rangle_{\!\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}}{1+x_{1}^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!-\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}\bigg|_{\bf 0}\frac{R_{3434}+x_{1}(R_{3431}+R_{3134})+x_{1}^{2}R_{3131}}{1+x_{1}^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!-2R_{3431},

so that
R_{3431}=0
.
The remaining cases
i=2,3,4
likewise yield
R_{3432}=R_{3414}=R_{3424}=0
, respectively, so that we recover
[
25
, Proposition]
(see also (
38
) in Proposition
2
):

\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}(P)=\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}(e_{3}\wedge e_{4})=R_{3412}e_{1}\wedge e_{2}+R_{3434}e_{3}\wedge e_{4}.

Note that
\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}(e_{3}\wedge e_{4})=R_{3434}
is the critical value, assumed known. A similar analysis with
*P=e_{1}\wedge e_{2}
yields
R_{1213}=R_{1214}=R_{1223}=R_{1242}=0
, and hence that

\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}(*P)=\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}(e_{1}\wedge e_{2})=R_{1212}e_{1}\wedge e_{2}+R_{1234}e_{3}\wedge e_{4},

with critical value
\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}(e_{1}\wedge e_{2})=R_{1212}

\{e_{1}\wedge e_{2}\,,\,e_{1}\wedge e_{3}\,,\,e_{1}\wedge e_{4}\,,\,e_{3}\wedge e_{4}\,,\,e_{4}\wedge e_{2}\,,\,e_{2}\wedge e_{3}\}\subseteq\Lambda^{2}(T_{p}M),

\displaystyle\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}\!\!

Normalization of Quaternionic Polynomials in Coordinate-Free Quaternionic Variables in Conjugate-Alternating Order
Hongbo Li, Zhengyang Wang, Yue Liu, Lei Huang, Changpeng Shao

Abstract
Quaternionic polynomials occur naturally in applications of quaternions in science and engineering, and normalization of quaternionic polynomials is a basic manipulation. Once a Gröbner basis is certified for the defining ideal ℐ \cal I of the quaternionic polynomial algebra, the normal form of a quaternionic polynomial can be computed by routine top reduction with respect to the Gröbner basis. In the literature, a Gröbner basis under the conjugate-alternating order of quaternionic variables was conjectured for ℐ \cal I in 2013 [ 21 ] , but no readable and convincing proof was found. In this paper, we present the first readable certification of the conjectured Gröbner basis. The certification is based on several novel techniques for reduction in free associative algebras, which enables to not only make reduction to S-polynomials more efficiently, but also reduce the number of S-polynomials needed for the certification. Keywords. Quaternionic polynomials; Free-associative algebra; Non-commutative Gröbner basis; Substitutional reduction; Clear S-polynomials.

The technical contributions of this paper include the following:

•

Four new reduction techniques for free associative algebras:
subsequence-led reduction, subsequence-dominated reduction,
bottom-letter controlled reduction, and substitutional reduction. These techniques allow
more flexible reduction of a non-commutative polynomial by locally increasing the order
of the leading term.

•

Before making reduction to the S-polynomials of a Gröbner basis candidate, first
extend the Gröbner basis candidate to a larger one with more general symmetries that allow
more powerful reduction techniques. Every newly included element should be verified to be
reduced to zero by the old ones. The enlarged Gröbner basis candidate is to be used
to make reduction to the S-polynomials of the original Gröbner basis candidate.

•

Reduce the number of S-polynomials used to certify
the Gröbner basis candidate by selecting only the
clear S-polynomials
proposed
in this paper. This technique is valid for all free associative algebras where a reduced
Gröbner basis candidate has the property that all its elements have degree
>1
. For
quaternionic polynomial algebra, the number of S-polynomials used to certify the conjectured
Gröbner basis is reduced by about half with the clear S-polynomial technique.

In normalizing a quaternionic monomial where all quaternionic parameters are given in
coordinate-free form, the quaternionic parameters are treated just as
quaternionic variables. If instead, some quaternionic parameters are given in coordinate form,
say

In this paper, we always use the following order in alphabet

{\bf q}_{1}\prec\bar{\bf q}_{1}\prec{\bf q}_{2}\prec\bar{\bf q}_{2}\prec\ldots\prec{\bf q}_{n}\prec\bar{\bf q}_{n}\prec{\bf i}\prec{\bf j}\prec{\bf k}.

(2.10)

Proposition 2.1
.

For any quaternionic monomials

{\bf X}[{\bf Y}]-[{\bf Y}]{\bf X}\in{\cal I}

Proof.

By the coordinatization (

For example, the first line of (

(2.11)

Since both sides have the same leading term

Corollary 2.2
.

For any quaternionic monomials
{\bf X},{\bf Y}
,
[{\bf X}{\bf Y}]-[{\bf Y}{\bf X}]\in{\cal I}

Proof.

By
[{\bf X}]{\bf Y}={\bf Y}[{\bf X}]

\bar{{\bf X}}[{\bf Y}]=[{\bf Y}]\bar{{\bf X}}

\begin{array}[]{lcl}[{\bf X}{\bf Y}]-[{\bf Y}{\bf X}]&=&{\bf X}{\bf Y}+\bar{{\bf Y}}\bar{{\bf X}}-{\bf Y}{\bf X}-\bar{{\bf X}}\bar{{\bf Y}}\\

&=&({\bf Y}[{\bf X}]-{\bf Y}{\bf X})+(\bar{{\bf Y}}\bar{{\bf X}}-[{\bf Y}]\bar{{\bf X}})\\

new letters and then defining an order among the new letters, is equivalent to first considering
only a specific case of the varying order and making reduction only for this specific case, then
checking the reduction procedure to make sure that it is valid for all other possibilities of the
varying order.

Bottom-letter controlled reduction.

Theorem 4.4

{\bf a}

{\bf T}

\bar{\bf q}^{j}{\bf q}^{i}

|{\bf T}|\leq 3

f

\overline{\bf BG}{\bf 2}
to

\overline{\bf BG}{\bf 4}

{\bf b},{\bf c}\in{\cal A}
,

|{\bf T}^{\prime}|\geq 2

{\bf 1},{\bf 2},{\bf 3}

{\bf 3}\succ_{M}{\bf 2}\bar{{\bf 2}}\succ_{M}{\bf 1}\bar{{\bf 1}}.

(4.5)

All together,

where
|{\bf T}|\geq 2
, has the following

\begin{array}[]{lll}f_{1}&=&{\bf 1}[{\bf 1}{\bf T}{\bf 1}]-[{\bf 1}{\bf T}{\bf 1}]{\bf 1},\\

where if

{\bf 2},\bar{{\bf 2}}

Proposition 4.5

f_{1}={\bf 1}[{\bf 1}{\bf T}{\bf 1}]-[{\bf 1}{\bf T}{\bf 1}]{\bf 1}

reduced to zero by
\bf BG
.

Proof.

element
{\bf 1}\bar{{\bf 1}}-\bar{{\bf 1}}{\bf 1}

.

(4.7)

5
Gröbner basis certification by reductions of clear S-polynomials

In this section,
we first review the basics of S-polynomials in
free-associative algebra
[
28
,
26
]
,
then propose a theorem that further reduces the number of S-polynomials
needed for Gröbner basis certification.
The following lemma is trivial but very useful.

Lemma 5.1
.

In a free associative algebra, for any monomials

{\bf A}_{1},{\bf A}_{2},{\bf B}_{1},{\bf B}_{2}

such that
{\bf A}_{1}{\bf A}_{2}={\bf B}_{1}{\bf B}_{2}
, if the degrees
|{\bf A}_{1}|<|{\bf B}_{1}|
, then

{\bf B}_{1}={\bf A}_{1}{\bf B}_{1}^{\prime},\ {\bf A}_{2}={\bf B}_{1}^{\prime}{\bf B}_{2}
,
where
{\bf B}_{1}^{\prime}
is a submonomial of degree
>0
; if
|{\bf A}_{1}|=|{\bf B}_{1}|
, then

{\bf B}_{1}={\bf A}_{1},\ {\bf A}_{2}={\bf B}_{2}
.

Let
G
be a set of polynomials in free-associative algebra

\mathbb{K}\langle\mathcal{A}\rangle
.
Fix the degree lexicographic order in the algebra.
For any two elements
f,g\in G
that are irreducible with respect to each other,
an
S-polynomial
they generate is of the form

{\bf A}_{1}f{\bf B}_{1}-{\bf A}_{2}g{\bf B}_{2}
, where the
{\bf A}_{i},{\bf B}_{i}
are monomials satisfying

{\rm T}({\bf A}_{1}f{\bf B}_{1})={\rm T}({\bf A}_{2}g{\bf B}_{2})
, with operator “T” extracting the leading term of
a polynomials.

For an ideal
{\cal I}\subseteq\mathbb{K}\langle\mathcal{A}\rangle
,
a subset
G\subseteq{\cal I}
is a Gröbner basis of
{\cal I}

if and only if
G
generates
{\cal I}
, and any S-polynomial of
G

can be reduced to zero by
G
.

If
h
is an S-polynomial, then for any monomials

{\bf A}
and
{\bf B}
,
{\bf A}h{\bf B}
is also an S-polynomial.
So only those S-polynomials of minimal degree are non-trivial.
By Lemma
5.1
, after removing redundant common left submonomials and common
right submonomials, one can always get
either
|{\bf A}_{1}|=0
or
|{\bf B}_{2}|=0
, where if
|{\bf A}_{1}|=0
then one further gets
|{\bf B}_{2}|=0
, and if

|{\bf B}_{2}|=0
then one further gets
|{\bf A}_{1}|=0
. So only the following
unilateral S-polynomials

need to be considered:

Definition 5.1
.

For any two polynomials
f,g
in
{\mathbb{K}}\langle{\cal A}\rangle

that are irreducible with respect to each other,
if there exist monomials
{\bf L},{\bf R}
with properties
0<|{\bf L}|<|f|
and
0<|{\bf R}|<|g|
, such that

{\rm T}(f){\bf R}={\bf L}{\rm T}(g)
, then
(f,{\bf R};g,{\bf L})

is called an S-quadruplet, where

{\rm T}(f){\bf R}
is called the leader of the S-quadruplet,

f{\bf R}
is called the left polynomial, and
{\bf L}g
is called the right polynomial.
Polynomial
f{\bf R}-{\bf L}g
is the corresponding
S-polynomial of the S-quadruplet.

Notice that the leading term of the S-polynomial of an S-quadruplet is strictly lower
than the leader, because the leader is the leading term of both
f{\bf R}
and
{\bf L}g
, and is
canceled in the subtraction
f{\bf R}-{\bf L}g
. Also notice that
|f{\bf R}-{\bf L}g|<|f|+|g|
, so the tail of
monomial
{\rm T}(f)
overlaps with the head of monomial
{\rm T}(g)
in any S-quadruplet.

The reason why
0<|{\bf L}|<|f|
is as follows. If
|{\bf L}|=0
, then

{\rm T}(f)\,|\,{\rm T}(g)
, contradicting with the irreducibility assumption.
If
|{\bf L}|\geq|f|
, then by lemma
5.1
,

{\bf L}={\rm T}(f){\bf L}^{\prime}
for some submonomial
{\bf L}^{\prime}
, and
{\bf R}={\bf L}^{\prime}{\rm T}(g)
.
Let
f={\rm T}(f)+\Delta f
,
g={\rm T}(g)+\Delta g
, where

\Delta f,\Delta g
stand for the remaining terms
of
f,g
respectively other than the leading term. Then
the S-polynomial becomes
f{\bf R}-{\bf L}g=(\Delta f){\bf L}^{\prime}{\rm T}(g)-{\rm T}(f){\bf L}^{\prime}(\Delta g)
.
Direct reductions of
(\Delta f){\bf L}^{\prime}{\rm T}(g)
by
g
, and

{\rm T}(f){\bf L}^{\prime}(\Delta g)
by
f
, yield

-(\Delta f){\bf L}^{\prime}(\Delta g)+(\Delta f){\bf L}^{\prime}(\Delta g)=0
, so the S-polynomial is trivially
reduced to zero by
f,g
, and is not useful in Gröbner basis certification.

In the following, we further reduce the number of S-polynomials
in the certification of Gröbner basis by proposing a more general technique
than S-polynomial reduction.

Definition 5.2
.

Let
G
be a set of polynomials in
{\mathbb{K}}\langle{\cal A}\rangle
.
For any S-quadruplet
(f,{\bf R};g,{\bf L})
where
f,g\in G
,
if after removing both the leading letter and the ending letter
from the leader
{\rm T}(f){\bf R}
, the leftover of the leader
is irreducible with respect to
G
,
then the S-quadruplet is said to be clear, and the corresponding
S-polynomial is called a clear S-polynomial.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
G
be a set of polynomials in
{\mathbb{K}}\langle{\cal A}\rangle
,
where every element
g\in G
is of degree
>1
and is irreducible with respect to

G\backslash\{g\}
. Let
t>2
be an integer.
Then the following two statements are equivalent:

1. For any
f,g\in G
, if there exists an S-quadruplet

(f,{\bf R};g,{\bf L})
where
|f{\bf R}|\leq t
,
then there exist finitely many monomials
{\bf L}_{i},{\bf R}_{i}
, and elements
g_{i}\in G
, such that

f{\bf R}=\sum_{i}{\bf L}_{i}g_{i}{\bf R}_{i}
,
where for each
i
, (a)
{\bf L}_{i}g_{i}{\bf R}_{i}\preceq{\rm T}(f){\bf R}
, (b)
if
|{\bf L}_{i}|=0
, then
g_{i}{\bf R}_{i}\prec{\rm T}(f){\bf R}
.

2. For any
f,g\in G
, if there exists a clear S-quadruplet

(f,{\bf R};g,{\bf L})
, where
|f{\bf R}|\leq t
,
then there exist finitely many monomials
{\bf L}_{i},{\bf R}_{i}
, elements
g_{i}\in G
, such that

f{\bf R}=\sum_{i}{\bf L}_{i}g_{i}{\bf R}_{i}
,
where for each
i
, (a)
{\bf L}_{i}g_{i}{\bf R}_{i}\preceq{\rm T}(f){\bf R}
, (b)
if
|{\bf L}_{i}|=0
, then
g_{i}{\bf R}_{i}\prec{\rm T}(f){\bf R}
.

Proof.

Statement 1 trivially implies statement 2. We prove the converse implication.
Assume that
(f,{\bf R};g,{\bf L})
is an S-quadruplet but not a clear one, namely, if

{\rm T}(f){\bf R}={\bf m}_{1}{\bf m}_{2}\cdots{\bf m}_{u}
, where the
{\bf m}_{i}
are letters, then
submonomial
{\bf H}={\bf m}_{2}\cdots{\bf m}_{u-1}
is reducible by
G
. So
u\geq 4
by the degree assumption
on elements of
G
.
If
{\bf H}
is reducible by more than one element of

G
, say
{\bf H}={\bf L}_{1}{\rm T}(f_{1}){\bf R}_{1}={\bf L}_{2}{\rm T}(f_{2}){\bf R}_{2}
for two different elements

f_{1},f_{2}\in G
, then
|{\bf L}_{1}|\neq|{\bf L}_{2}|
by
Lemma
5.1
and the irreducibility assumption on
G
.
Because of this, there exist monomials
{\bf L}_{s},{\bf R}_{s}
and an element

f_{s}\in G
, such that
{\bf H}={\bf L}_{s}{\rm T}(f_{s}){\bf R}_{s}
, and
|{\bf L}_{s}|
is the smallest
among all the
{\bf L}_{i}
in triplets
({\bf L}_{i},{\bf R}_{i},f_{i})
satisfying
{\bf H}={\bf L}_{i}{\rm T}(f_{i}){\bf R}_{i}
.

Now
{\rm T}(f){\bf R}={\bf m}_{1}{\bf L}_{s}{\rm T}(f_{s}){\bf R}_{s}{\bf m}_{u}
. Again by Lemma
5.1

and the irreducibility property on
G
,

{\bf R}_{s}{\bf m}_{u}\,|\,{\bf R}
, and
|{\bf R}|>|{\bf R}_{s}{\bf m}_{u}|>0
. Let
{\bf R}={\bf R}^{\prime}{\bf R}_{s}{\bf m}_{u}
, with
{\bf R}^{\prime}
being a
submonomial. Then
|{\bf R}|>|{\bf R}^{\prime}|>0
, and

{\rm T}(f){\bf R}^{\prime}={\bf m}_{1}{\bf L}_{s}{\rm T}(f_{s})
. Set
{\bf L}^{\prime}={\bf m}_{1}{\bf L}_{s}
. Then
|{\bf L}^{\prime}|>0
, and

{\rm T}(f){\bf R}^{\prime}={\bf L}^{\prime}{\rm T}(f_{s})
, and

|f{\bf R}^{\prime}|<|f{\bf R}|\leq t
. Once it is proved that (1)
|{\bf L}^{\prime}|<|f|
,
(2) S-quadruplet
(f,{\bf R}^{\prime};f_{s},{\bf L}^{\prime})
is clear,
then all the assumptions in statement 2 on the S-quadruplet
are satisfied, so a decomposition of
f{\bf R}^{\prime}
is obtained, and the required
decomposition of
f{\bf R}=f{\bf R}^{\prime}{\bf R}_{s}{\bf m}_{u}
follows.

Proof of (1).
Since
{\rm T}(f){\bf R}={\bf m}_{1}{\bf H}{\bf m}_{u}={\bf L}{\rm T}(g)
, where
0<|{\bf L}|<|f|
,

{\bf L}
can be decomposed into

{\bf L}={\bf m}_{1}{\bf L}_{0}
, with
{\bf L}_{0}
being a submonomial, and
{\bf H}{\bf m}_{u}={\bf L}_{0}{\rm T}(g)
.
By the minimality of
|{\bf L}_{s}|
, among
all the
{\bf L}_{i}
in triplets
({\bf L}_{i},{\bf R}_{i},f_{i})
satisfying
{\bf H}{\bf m}_{u}={\bf L}_{i}{\rm T}(f_{i}){\bf R}_{i}{\bf m}_{u}
,

|{\bf L}_{s}|
is the smallest. So
|{\bf L}_{0}|\geq|{\bf L}_{s}|
, and

|{\bf L}^{\prime}|=1+|{\bf L}_{s}|\leq 1+|{\bf L}_{0}|=|{\bf L}|<|f|
follows.

Proof of (2).
For
{\rm T}(f){\bf R}^{\prime}={\bf m}_{1}{\bf L}_{s}{\rm T}(f_{s})={\bf m}_{1}{\bf m}_{2}\cdots{\bf m}_{j}
, obviously

j=u-|{\bf R}_{s}|-1
. Since
|f|\geq 2
and
|{\bf R}^{\prime}|\geq 1
, we have
j=|f|+|{\bf R}^{\prime}|\geq 3
. Let

{\bf H}^{\prime}={\bf m}_{2}\cdots{\bf m}_{j-1}
, then
|{\bf H}^{\prime}|>0
. If
{\bf H}^{\prime}

is reducible by some
g^{\prime}\in G
, say
{\bf H}^{\prime}={\bf A}{\rm T}(g^{\prime}){\bf B}
for some monomials
{\bf A},{\bf B}
,
then

{\bf H}={\bf L}_{s}{\rm T}(f_{s}){\bf R}_{s}={\bf m}_{2}\cdots{\bf m}_{j}{\bf R}_{s}={\bf H}^{\prime}{\bf m}_{j}{\bf R}_{s}={\bf A}{\rm T}(g^{\prime}){\bf B}{\bf m}_{j}{\bf R}_{s}.

(5.1)

By the minimality of
|{\bf L}_{s}|
, we get
|{\bf A}|\geq|{\bf L}_{s}|
. Then
from
{\bf L}_{s}{\rm T}(f_{s})={\bf A}{\rm T}(g^{\prime}){\bf B}{\bf m}_{j}
, we get

{\rm T}(g^{\prime})\mid{\rm T}(f_{s})
,
contradicting
with the irreducibility property on
G
. So
{\bf H}^{\prime}
is irreducible with respect to
G
, and

(f,{\bf R}^{\prime};f_{s},{\bf L}^{\prime})
is clear.
∎

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
G
be a set of polynomials in
{\mathbb{K}}\langle{\cal A}\rangle
, where
every element
g\in G
is of degree
>1
and irreducible with respect to

G\backslash\{g\}
. Then
G
is a Gröbner basis of
\langle G\rangle
if and only if
all the clear S-polynomials of
G
can be reduced to zero by
G
.

Proof.

We only need to prove the “if” part. For any clear S-quadruplet

(f,{\bf R};g,{\bf L})
of
G
,
there exist finitely many monomials
{\bf L}_{i},{\bf R}_{i}
, elements
g_{i}\in G
, such that

f{\bf R}={\bf L}g+\sum_{i}{\bf L}_{i}g_{i}{\bf R}_{i}
,
where for each
i
,
{\bf L}_{i}g_{i}{\bf R}_{i}\preceq{\rm T}(f{\bf R}-{\bf L}g)\prec{\rm T}(f){\bf R}
.
So statement 2 of Proposition
5.2
is true.
By this proposition, statement 1 must be true, namely,
for arbitrary S-quadruplet
(f,{\bf R};g,{\bf L})
of
G
, denote
t=|f{\bf R}|\geq 3
, then
there exist finitely many monomials
{\bf L}^{\prime}_{i},{\bf R}^{\prime}_{i}
, nonzero scalars
\lambda_{i}
,
and elements
g^{\prime}_{i}\in G
, such that the
corresponding S-polynomial
f^{\prime}=f{\bf R}-{\bf L}g
satisfies

f^{\prime}=\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}{\bf L}^{\prime}_{i}g^{\prime}_{i}{\bf R}^{\prime}_{i},

(5.2)

where for each
i
,
|{\bf L}^{\prime}_{i}g^{\prime}_{i}{\bf R}^{\prime}_{i}|\leq t
.

Claim 1. For any nonzero polynomial

f^{\prime}\in\langle G\rangle
satisfying (
5.2
) and the constraint
that
|{\bf L}^{\prime}_{i}g^{\prime}_{i}{\bf R}^{\prime}_{i}|\leq t
for each
i
,
there exists an element
g^{\prime}\in G
such that
{\rm T}(g^{\prime})\mid{\rm T}(f^{\prime})
.

We prove this claim below.
On the right side of (
5.2
), every summand is a scaled product of
a triplet
({\bf L}^{\prime}_{i},g^{\prime}_{i},{\bf R}^{\prime}_{i})
, and the triplets are pairwise different.
Let
d
be the maximal degree of these summands. Then
d\leq t
.

Define the following finite set and some subsets of it,
where
{\bf L},{\bf R},{\bf A},{\bf B}
are monic monomials, and
f,g\in G
:

\begin{array}[]{lll}\mathcal{T}&:=&\{({\bf L},f,{\bf R})\ \mid\ |{\bf L}f{\bf R}|\leq d\};\\
\mathcal{T}_{1}&:=&\hbox{\vrule height=12.5pt,depth=5.0pt,width=0.0pt}\{({\bf L},f,{\bf R})\in\mathcal{T}\ \mid\ \exists g,{\bf A},{\bf B},\hbox{ s.t. }{\bf L}{\rm T}(f){\bf R}={\bf A}{\rm T}(g){\bf B},\ |{\bf L}|<|{\bf A}|<|{\bf L}f|\};\\
\mathcal{T}_{2}&:=&\{({\bf L},f,{\bf R})\in\mathcal{T}\backslash\mathcal{T}_{1}\ \mid\ \exists g,{\bf A},{\bf B},\hbox{ s.t. }{\bf L}{\rm T}(f){\bf R}={\bf A}{\rm T}(g){\bf B},\ |{\bf A}|\geq|{\bf L}f|\};\\
\mathcal{T}_{3}&:=&\{({\bf L},f,{\bf R})\in\mathcal{T}\backslash\mathcal{T}_{1}\ \mid\ \exists g,{\bf A},{\bf B},\hbox{ s.t. }{\bf L}{\rm T}(f){\bf R}={\bf A}{\rm T}(g){\bf B},\ |{\bf A}|\leq|{\bf L}|\}.\end{array}

(5.3)

Obviously
\mathcal{T}
is a finite set,

\mathcal{T}=\mathcal{T}_{1}\cup\mathcal{T}_{2}\cup\mathcal{T}_{3}
, and
the sets
\mathcal{T}_{i}
do not overlap with each other. All the triplets on the right side
of (
5.2
) belong to
\mathcal{T}
.

First investigate
\mathcal{T}_{3}
. For any two different triplets

({\bf L},f,{\bf R}),({\bf A},g,{\bf B})\in\mathcal{T}_{3}
,
if
{\bf L}{\rm T}(f){\bf R}={\bf A}{\rm T}(g){\bf B}
, then
|{\bf A}|=|{\bf L}|
by the definition of
\mathcal{T}_{3}
,
which leads to

{\bf L}={\bf A}
, hence one of
{\rm T}(f),{\rm T}(g)
is a factor of the other,
resulting in
f=g
, and then
({\bf L},f,{\bf R})=({\bf A},g,{\bf B})
, contradiction.
So any two different triplets of
\mathcal{T}_{3}

have different leading terms, if each triplet is multiplied and then linearly expanded
to form a polynomial called the
multiplied triplet
.
In (
5.2
), if the triplets on the right side are all in
\mathcal{T}_{3}
, then

{\rm T}(f^{\prime})=\lambda_{j}{\bf L}^{\prime}_{j}{\rm T}(g^{\prime}_{j}){\bf R}^{\prime}_{j}
for some
j
,
so
{\rm T}(g^{\prime}_{j})\mid{\rm T}(f^{\prime})
. Indeed, the idea behind the proof
is to change every multiplied
triplet of
\mathcal{T}_{1}\cup\mathcal{T}_{2}

into the sum of some scaled multiplied triplets of
\mathcal{T}_{3}
.

Next investigate
\mathcal{T}_{2}
. For any
({\bf L},f,{\bf R})\in\mathcal{T}_{2}
, there exists a triplet

({\bf A},g,{\bf B})
such that
{\bf L}{\rm T}(f){\bf R}={\bf A}{\rm T}(g){\bf B}
and
{\bf A}={\bf L}{\rm T}(f){\bf L}^{\prime}
,
where
{\bf L}^{\prime}
is a submonomial.
Obviously
({\bf A},g,{\bf B})\in\mathcal{T}
.
Then

{\bf L}{\rm T}(f){\bf R}={\bf A}{\rm T}(g){\bf B}={\bf L}{\rm T}(f){\bf L}^{\prime}{\rm T}(g){\bf B},

(5.4)

so
{\bf R}={\bf L}^{\prime}{\rm T}(g){\bf B}
.
Let
g=\sum_{j=0}^{k}g_{[j]}
, where
{\rm T}(g)=g_{[0]}\succ g_{[1]}\succ\cdots\succ g_{[k]}
are the terms of
g
in descending order. Similarly, let

f=\sum_{i=0}^{l}f_{[i]}
.
Make the following decomposition of polynomial
{\bf L}f{\bf R}
:

{\bf L}f{\bf R}=\sum_{i=0}^{l}{\bf L}f_{[i]}{\bf L}^{\prime}g{\bf B}-\sum_{j=1}^{k}{\bf L}f{\bf L}^{\prime}g_{[j]}{\bf B}.

(5.5)

On the right side, the summand

{\bf L}f_{[i]}{\bf L}^{\prime}g{\bf B}
has the property that
({\bf L}f_{[i]}{\bf L}^{\prime},g,{\bf B})\in\mathcal{T}
,

|{\bf L}f_{[i]}{\bf L}^{\prime}g{\bf B}|\leq|{\bf L}f{\bf R}|
, and either (a)
|{\bf L}f_{[i]}{\bf L}^{\prime}|>|{\bf L}|
,
or (b)
|{\bf L}f_{[i]}{\bf L}^{\prime}|=|{\bf L}|
, and
f_{[i]}
is a scalar, and

{\bf L}f_{[i]}{\bf L}^{\prime}{\rm T}(g){\bf B}\prec{\bf L}{\rm T}(f){\bf R}
. The summand

{\bf L}f{\bf L}^{\prime}g_{[j]}{\bf B}
where
j>0
, has the property that
({\bf L},f,{\bf L}^{\prime}g_{[j]}{\bf B})\in\mathcal{T}
,

|{\bf L}f{\bf L}^{\prime}g_{[j]}{\bf B}|\leq|{\bf L}f{\bf R}|
, and (c)

{\bf L}{\rm T}(f){\bf L}^{\prime}g_{[j]}{\bf B}\prec{\bf L}{\rm T}(f){\bf L}^{\prime}{\rm T}(g){\bf B}={\bf L}{\rm T}(f){\bf R}
.

Finally,
we show that every multiplied triplet of
\mathcal{T}_{1}\cup\mathcal{T}_{2}

can be decomposed iteratively into a sum of scaled multiplied triplets in

\mathcal{T}_{3}
. Initially the output set
\mathcal{O}
collecting the summands is set to be empty.
The following procedure starts from a summand on the right side of (
5.2
)
that has the maximal order, say
{\bf L}^{\prime}g^{\prime}{\bf R}^{\prime}
.

Step 1. If
({\bf L}^{\prime},g^{\prime},{\bf R}^{\prime})\in\mathcal{T}_{3}
, then directly
move
{\bf L}^{\prime}g^{\prime}{\bf R}^{\prime}
to the set
\mathcal{O}
.

Step 2. If
({\bf L}^{\prime},g^{\prime},{\bf R}^{\prime})\in\mathcal{T}_{1}
, then
statement 1 of Proposition
5.2
provides a decomposition of

{\bf L}^{\prime}g^{\prime}{\bf R}^{\prime}
into a sum of scaled multiplied triplets

{\bf L}_{j}g_{j}{\bf R}_{j}
, where each
({\bf L}_{j},g_{j},{\bf R}_{j})\in\mathcal{T}
, with the
monotonous property

that
|{\bf L}_{j}g_{j}{\bf R}_{j}|\leq|{\bf L}^{\prime}g^{\prime}{\bf R}^{\prime}|
,
and either
|{\bf L}_{j}|>|{\bf L}^{\prime}|
, or
|{\bf L}_{j}|=|{\bf L}^{\prime}|
and
{\bf L}_{j}{\rm T}(g_{j}){\bf R}_{j}\prec{\bf L}^{\prime}{\rm T}(g^{\prime}){\bf R}^{\prime}
.
For each summand in the decomposition, do Steps 1 to 3.

Step 3. If
({\bf L}^{\prime},g^{\prime},{\bf R}^{\prime})\in\mathcal{T}_{2}
, then (
5.5
) gives a
decomposition of
{\bf L}^{\prime}g^{\prime}{\bf R}^{\prime}
into a sum of scaled multiplied
triplets, where each triplet is in
\mathcal{T}
, and has the same
monotonous property as in Step 2, according to
the investigation of
\mathcal{T}_{2}
.
For each summand in the decomposition, do Steps 1 to 3.

By the monotonous property of the decompositions in Steps 1 and 2, the
iteration of decompositions finishes in finite steps. The outcome is a sum of
scaled multiplied triplets in
\mathcal{T}_{3}
. By the property that in
\mathcal{T}_{3}
, no two
scaled multiplied triplets have the same leading monomial,
we get that
f^{\prime}\neq 0
must have its leading term
agree with that of a scaled multiplied triplet in
\mathcal{T}_{3}
, and in particular,
have its leading term agree with that of an element
g^{\prime}\in G
.
This proves Claim 1.

Claim 2. S-polynomial
f^{\prime}=f{\bf R}-{\bf L}g
can be reduced to zero by
G
.

We prove this claim below. By Claim 1,
let
{\rm T}(f^{\prime})=\lambda^{\prime}{\bf L}^{\prime}{\rm T}(g^{\prime}){\bf R}^{\prime}
for some
nonzero scalar
\lambda^{\prime}
and monic monomials
{\bf L}^{\prime},{\bf R}^{\prime}
. Then by (
5.2
),

f^{\prime\prime}=f^{\prime}-\lambda^{\prime}{\bf L}^{\prime}g^{\prime}{\bf R}^{\prime}=-\lambda^{\prime}{\bf L}^{\prime}g^{\prime}{\bf R}^{\prime}+\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}{\bf L}^{\prime}_{i}g^{\prime}_{i}{\bf R}^{\prime}_{i},

(5.6)

where on the right side,
{\bf L}^{\prime}g^{\prime}{\bf R}^{\prime}\prec{\rm T}(f){\bf R}
.
Obviously
{\rm T}(f^{\prime\prime})\prec{\rm T}(f^{\prime})
.
Again by Claim 1, as long as
f^{\prime\prime}\neq 0
,
there exists an element
g^{\prime\prime}\in G
such that
{\rm T}(g^{\prime\prime})\mid{\rm T}(f^{\prime\prime})
.
Do the reduction of
f^{\prime\prime}
by
g^{\prime\prime}
to get
f^{\prime\prime\prime}
.
As long as the reduction result is nonzero, one can continue to apply Claim 1 to make
reduction with respect to
G
. In the end, the reduction result has to be zero.
∎

6
Proof of the Main Theorem

With all the preparations made in the previous sections, we are now ready to
prove the Main Theorem
2.4
of this paper. The theorem
can be proved by verifying that all clear S-polynomials of
\bf BG
can be reduced to zero by

{\bf BG}^{ext}
. First we make a classification of the clear S-polynomials.

7
Conclusion

Quaternionic polynomials occur naturally in science and engineering applications, and normalization
of quaternionic polynomials is a basic task. This paper proposes the first readable proof
of a conjectured reduced Gröbner basis of the defining ideal of coordinate-free quaternionic
polynomial algebra, by developing some novel reduction techniques for free associative algebras.

The order of letters used in the certified Gröbner basis is the conjugate-alternating order,
where each quaternionic variable immediately precedes its conjugate. This order is friendly
for employing the well known fact that the scalar part of any quaternionic monomial commutes
with any letter, as twice the scalar part is the sum of the monomial and its conjugate.
From this viewpoint, the certified Gröbner basis under this order reflects the
commutativity and symmetry of the scalar part as the foundations of normalization.

The following is another typical order among the letters:

{\bf q}_{1}\prec{\bf q}_{2}\prec\ldots\prec{\bf q}_{n}\prec{\bf i}\prec{\bf j}\prec{\bf k}\prec\bar{\bf q}_{1}\prec\bar{\bf q}_{2}\prec\ldots\prec\bar{\bf q}_{n}.

(7.1)

It is called the
conjugate-separating order
, and is used to shield the conjugates from the
quaternionic variables. Recall that in multi-variate complex analysis, a complex polynomial is in
complex variables
z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}
and their complex conjugates

\bar{z}_{1},\ldots,\bar{z}_{n}
, the polynomial is analytic if and only if it is irrelevant to
the conjugates. In the quaternionic domain, a quaternionic polynomial is said to be
monogenic

if it is irrelevant to the conjugates of the quaternionic variables. To normalize a
monogenic quaternionic polynomial, a Gröbner basis of the defining ideal of
basis-free quaternionic polynomial algebra under the conjugate-separating order is desired.

The work on conjecturing such a Gröbner basis is an ongoing work. By now the elements of low
degree of a reduced Gröbner basis have been computed, and have been classified in the framework
of Grassmann-Cayley algebra and bracket algebra
[
19
]
over
{\mathbb{K}}^{3}
.
An interesting observation is that
opposite to the case of conjugate-alternating order, the elements of the
Gröbner basis under conjugate-separating order reflects the
anti-commutativity and asymmetry of the vector part in Grassmann-Cayley algebra
as the foundations of normalization.

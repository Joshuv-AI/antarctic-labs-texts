Non-recurrence and divergent ( 𝐩 ⁡ ( 𝐧 ) , 𝐪 ⁡ ( 𝐧 ) ) \bf({p(n)},{q(n)}) -averages for deterministic automorphisms
V.V. Ryzhikov

Abstract
If sequences p ⁡ ( n ) , q ⁡ ( n ) {p(n)},{q(n)} satisfy the condition p ⁡ ( n + 1 ) − p ⁡ ( n ) , q ⁡ ( n + 1 ) − q ⁡ ( n ) → + ∞ , p(n+1)-p(n),q(n+1)-q(n)\ \to\ +\infty, there exist automorphisms S , T S,T with simple continuous spectrum such that the sequence 1 N ​ ∑ n = 1 N ∫ S p ⁡ ( n ) ​ f ⋅ T q ⁡ ( n ) ​ g ​ 𝑑 μ \frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\int S^{p(n)}f\cdot T^{q(n)}g\,d\mu diverges for some functions f , g ∈ L 2 f,g\in L_{2} . In particular, this gives an answer to the question of Frantzikinakis and Host for the case p ⁡ ( n ) = n 2 , q ⁡ ( n ) = n 3 p(n)=n^{2},q(n)=n^{3} . The automorphisms S , T S,T are realized as Gaussian and Poisson suspensions over the automorphisms 𝐒 , 𝐓 {\bf S},{\bf T} of a space with sigma-finite measure. The automorphism 𝐓 {\bf T} lies in the full group [ 𝐒 ] [{\bf S}] of the automorphism 𝐒 {\bf S} . We give also sketch-example of linear non-recurrence for a pair of mixing suspensions of zero entropy and with singular and Lebesgue parts in their spectra. Keywords: divergence of ergodic averages, non-recurrence, infinite automorphisms of a measure space, Gaussian and Poisson suspensions, spectrum.

Introduction

In

[

with

the convergence of averages of the form

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}S^{n}f\cdot T^{n^{p}}g.

holds.
In the papers
[

for which automorphisms

{p(n)},{q(n)}\to\infty

\sum_{n=1}^{N}\int S^{p(n)}f\cdot T^{q(n)}g\,d\mu/N

Theorem 1.
Let sequences
{p(n)},{q(n)}
satisfy
the property

p(n+1)-p(n),\ \ q(n+1)-q(n)\ \to\ +\infty.

Then there exist automorphisms
S,T
with simple singular spectrum and a set
C
such that the sequence
\sum_{n=1}^{N}\mu(S^{p(n)}C\cap T^{q(n)}C)/N

diverges.

2
Construction of infinite automorphism
{\bf S}

To prove the theorem, we will use constructions of rank one, which are useful for various problems. With their help the following were solved (see, for example,
[
7
]
,
[
8
]
,
[
9
]
):

– Kolmogorov’s problem on the group property of spectrum and Rokhlin’s problem on the homogeneous spectrum of multiplicity 2 in the class of mixing automorphisms;

- ergodic homoclinic groups of an automorphism with simple singular spectrum were discovered (Gordin’s problem);

– rigid transformations with a given mixing sequence of zero density were found (Bergelson’s problem);

– measures on a line were constructed whose tensor square has an absolutely continuous projection (onto a fixed line in the plane) along a dense set of directions and a singular projection along another dense set of directions (Oseledets’s problem);

– all sets of spectral multiplicities of the form
M\cup\{\infty\}
(
M\subset\mathbf{N}
) were realized.

– To show slow decay of correlations
for the generic mixing automorphisms in Alperm-Tikhonov space we used rank one constructions too. ( Podvigin’s problem, see
[
10
]
).

– To answer the question of Frantzikinakis and Host
[
1
]
, we construct similarly to
[
4
]
infinite transformations
{\bf S},{\bf T}
for which the corresponding Poisson (and Gaussian) suspensions will play the role
S,T
from Theorem 1.

Rank-one constructions.
Let the parameters
h_{1}=1
,
r_{j}\geq 2
and spacer sequence

\bar{s}_{j}=(s_{j}(1),s_{j}(2),\dots,s_{j}(r_{j})),\ s_{j}(i)\geq 0,\ j\in\mathbf{N}

are given.
The phase space
X
for the transformation
{\bf S}
defined below is the union of towers

X_{j}=\bigsqcup_{i=0}^{h_{j}-1}{\bf S}^{i}E_{j},

where
{\bf S}^{i}E_{j}
are disjoint half-intervals called floors.

At stage
j
, the transformation
{\bf S}
is defined as a usual interval transfer, but at the top floor
{\bf S}^{h_{j}-1}E_{j}
of tower
X_{j}
, the transformation is not yet defined.
Tower
X_{j}
is cut into
r_{j}
identical narrow subtowers
X_{j,i}
(they are called columns), and
s_{j}(i)
new floors are added above each column
X_{j,i}
. The transformation
{\bf S}
is still defined as moving up one floor, but the last floor added above column
i
, the transformation
{\bf S}
sends to the bottom floor of column
i+1
. Thus, a new tower arises

X_{j+1}=\bigsqcup_{i=0}^{h_{j+1}-1}{\bf S}^{i}E_{j+1}

of height

h_{j+1}=r_{j}h_{j}+\sum_{i=1}^{r_{j}}s_{j}(i),

where
E_{j+1}
is the lower floor of column
X_{j,1}
.
Note that by defining the transformation, we completely preserve the previous constructions.
Continuing this process to infinity,
we obtain an invertible transformation
{\bf S}:X\to X
, preserving the Lebesgue measure on the union
X=\bigsqcup_{j}X_{j}
.
Such a transformation is called an automorphism of rank one. It has simple
spectrum.

Parameters of
{\bf S}
.

Let us consider a construction
{\bf S}
with parameters

r_{j}=2
,
s_{j}(1)=0
,
s_{j}(2)\to\infty
(similar constructions have been used for other purposes in
[
7
]
,
[
8
]
,
[
9
]
.) Given
\{p(n)\},\{q(n)\}
choose

s_{j}(2)>\max\{p(jh_{j}),q(jh_{j})\}
.

Let the sets
A,B
consist of the floors of the tower of stage
j_{0}
,
then for
j>j_{0}
it holds (this is easy to check)

\mu({\bf S}^{h_{j}}A\cap B)=\mu(A\cap B)/2.

Therefore,
{\bf S}^{h_{j}}\to_{w}I/2
, which means that spectrum of the operator

\exp({\bf S})={\bf 1}\oplus{\bf S}\oplus{\bf S}^{\odot 2}\oplus{\bf S}^{\odot 3}\oplus\dots\

singular. Therefore, the Gaussian and Poisson suspensions over
{\bf S}
(as operators isomorphic to the operator
\exp({\bf S})
) have singular spectrum. Their entropy is zero. Later we will say how to modify the construction of
{\bf S}
to provide simple spectrum of suspensions.

3
Proof of the theorem

Definition of
{\bf T}
.
The automorphism
{\bf S}
(almost) cyclically permutes the floors in the towers
X_{j}
.
Our
{\bf T}
will permutate these floors in a different order. We define
{\bf T}
as
{\bf P}{\bf S}{\bf P}^{-1}
, where
{\bf P}
permutes the floorsas well. Let us describe it.

We identify the set of floors of the tower of stage
j+1
with the ordered
set of numbers
I_{j}=(0,1,2,\dots,h_{j+1}-1)
. The automorphism
{\bf S}

obviously corresponds to an (almost) cyclic permutation
\sigma
on
I_{j}
. It is given by the formula
\sigma(n)=n+1
, but let the value
\sigma(h_{j+1}-1)
be undefined.
(In the spirit of Katok-Stepin approximations, we can formally set
\sigma(h_{j+1}-1)=0
.)

For
n>n_{j}
, the intervals
(p(n),p(n)+1,p(n)+2,\dots,p(n)+2h_{j}-1)

do not intersect as well as the intervals

(q(n)+2h_{j},q(n)+2h_{j}+1,q(n)+2h_{j}+2,\dots,q(n)+4h_{j}-1)
.

For odd
\bf j

we find a permutation
\pi_{j}
on
\{2h_{j},2h_{j}-1,\dots,h_{j+1}-1\}
such that

\pi_{j}(q(n),q(n)+1,q(n)+2,\dots,q(n)+2h_{j}-1)=(p(n)+2h_{j},q(n)+2h_{j}+1,\dots,q(n)+4h_{j}-1),

p(n),q(n)>2h_{j}
,
p(n),q(n)<h_{j+1}-4h_{j}
.
With this permutation
\pi_{j}
we associate an automorpism
{\bf P}_{j}
, which exchanges floors, sending
k
-floor by a parallel translation to
\pi_{j}(k)
-floor.
Outside of
X_{j+1}\setminus X_{j}
our
{\bf P}_{j}

acts identically. Let’s recall that

X_{j}=\bigsqcup_{i=0}^{2h_{j}-1}{\bf S}^{i}E_{j+1},\ \ X_{j+1}=\bigsqcup_{i=0}^{h_{j+1}-1}{\bf S}^{i}E_{j+1}.

Let
A=X_{1}\subset X_{j}
, then
A
consists of the floors of stage
j+1
.
(we identify
A
with the set of numbers of these floors, which form a subset of
(0,1,2,\dots,2h_{j})
.)
Put
{\bf T}={\bf P}_{j}{\bf S}{\bf P}_{j}^{-1}
, then

{\bf T}^{q(n)}A={\bf S}^{p(n)+2h_{j}}A,

{\bf T}^{q(n)}A\cap{\bf S}^{p(n)}A=\emptyset,

[formula]

For even
\bf j

we define a permutation
\pi_{j}
:

\pi_{j}(q(n),q(n)+1,q(n)+2,\dots,q(n)+2h_{j}-1)=(p(n),p(n)+1,\dots,p(n)+2h_{j}-1),

p(n),q(n)>2h_{j}
,
p(n),q(n)<h_{j+1}-2h_{j}
.
The corresponding automorphism
{\bf P}_{j}
again send
k
-floor
to
\pi_{j}(k)
-floor, and outside of
X_{j+1}\setminus X_{j}
we set
{\bf P}_{j}=Id
.

For
{\bf T}={\bf P}_{j}{\bf S}{\bf P}_{j}^{-1}
we have

{\bf T}^{q(n)}A={\bf S}^{p(n)}A.

[formula]

Let

{\bf P}=\prod_{j}{\bf P}_{j},\ \ {\bf T}={\bf P}{\bf S}{\bf P}^{-1}.

It is important that equalities (1), (2) are preserved for such
{\bf T}
.

So we can find
N_{j}\to\infty
(with corresponding
h_{j}\to\infty
) such that

\frac{1}{N_{2k}}\sum_{n=1}^{N_{2k}}\mu({\bf S}^{p(n)}A\cap{\bf T}^{q(n)}A)\to\mu(A),

and

\frac{1}{N_{2k+1}}\sum_{n=1}^{N_{2k+1}}\mu({\bf S}^{p(n)}A\cap{\bf T}^{q(n)}A)\to 0.

We choose
N_{j}=\max\{n\,:\,p(n),q(n)<h_{j+1}-4h_{j}
.
Note that
N_{j-1}/N_{j}\to 0
because
N_{j-1}<h_{j}
,
N_{j}>jh_{j}
(recall that we choose above
h_{j+1}>\max\{p(jh_{j}),q(jh_{j})\}
).

Modification.
For some rare set of stages
j
, we can modify the parameters of the above construction
{\bf S}
so that the weak closure of its powers will possesses all possible polynomial. For this let
r_{j}=2j
,
s_{j}(i)=0
as
i\leq j
, and
s_{j}(i)=1
as
i\leq 2j

(Katok’s spacers). This provides simple spectrum for the operator
\exp({\bf S})
. At such stages we set
{\bf P}_{j}=Id
.

Completion of the proof.
Let us consider in the Poisson space the cylinder
C=C(A,0)
(see
[
4
]
),
0<c=\mu_{\circ}(C)<1
,
\mu_{\circ}
is the Poisson measure. If sets
A,A^{\prime}
do not intersect, then the corresponding cylinders
C,C^{\prime}
are independent
with respect to
\mu_{\circ}
.
Since
S^{n}C(A,0)=C({\bf S}^{n}A,0)
,

T^{n}C(A,0)=C({\bf T}^{n}A,0)
, we obtain

\frac{1}{N_{2k+1}}\sum_{n=1}^{N_{2k+1}}\mu_{\circ}(S^{p(n)}C\cap T^{q(n)}A)\to c^{2},

\frac{1}{N_{2k}}\sum_{n=1}^{N_{2k}}\mu_{\circ}(S^{p(n)}C\cap T^{q(n)}A)\to c.

4
Linear non-recurrence

In
[
1
]
was stated a problem on recurrence.
Are there deterministic automorphisms
S,T
such that for a set
D
of positive measure
S^{n}D\cap T^{n}D=\emptyset
for all
n>0
?

We have already mentioned Gordin’s problem on homoclinic groups. Let
T
be an automorphism of a probability space, the Gordin homoclinic group
H(T)
is defined as

H(T)=\{R\in Aut\,:\,T^{-n}RT^{n}\,\to_{s}\,I,\ n\to\infty\}.

It is easy to see that mixing Poisson suspension over a ”mixing”
{\bf T}
(
T^{n}\to_{w}0
) has the following homoclinic element.
Let
\mu(x:{\bf R}x\neq x)<\infty
. Then
{\bf T}^{-n}{\bf R}{\bf T}^{n}\,\to_{s}\,{\bf I}
, so for the corrsponding Poisson suspensions we have

T^{-n}RT^{n}\,\to_{s}\,{\ I}.

Now let
{\bf T}
be a rank-one transformation, and
{\bf R}\in[{\bf T}]
be interval involutive exchange of some floors
E
and
{\bf R}E
. Outside

F=E\bigsqcup{\bf R}E

we set
{\bf R}=Id
. In the Poisson space, let’s consider the set

D=C(E,0)\bigcap C(RE,1).

Recall that
C(E,0)
consists of all countable sets
x_{\circ}=\{x_{i}\}
such that
E\cap x_{\circ}
has cardinality 0. (Note that
x_{\circ}
has no limit points in the intervals that make up the phase space
X
.)
The cylinder
C({\bf R}E,1)
consists of all
x_{\circ}
such that
RE\cap x_{\circ}
has cardinality 1. So

RD\bigcap D=\emptyset.

Set

S=R^{-1}TR.

Since

\mu_{\circ}(T^{-n}RT^{n}D\Delta D)\to 0

we get

\mu_{\circ}(S^{n}D\cap T^{n}D)=\mu_{\circ}(RD\cap T^{-n}RT^{n}D)\ \to 0.

If the series
\sum_{n}\mu_{\circ}(RD\cap T^{-n}RT^{n}D)
converges, then we achieve the goal by considering its power
T^{p}
instead of
T
. For
T^{p}
the sum of the corresponding series can be arbitrarily small (if
p
is large) and so less than the measure of the set
D
.
Then desired
D^{\prime}\subset D
of a positive measure can be found:

S^{n}D^{\prime}\cap T^{n}D^{\prime}=\emptyset,\ n>0.

But this is impossible. In fact, the above series always diverges.
However we do the following. We see that

\mu_{\circ}(RD\cap T^{-n}RT^{n}D)<const\cdot\mu({\bf T}^{n}F\cap F).

Indeed, let
\mu({\bf T}^{n}F\cap F)=0
this means that
{\bf T}^{-n}{\bf R}{\bf T}^{n}
is the identity on
F
. Thus
T^{-n}RT^{n}D=D
. If
\mu({\bf T}^{n}F\cap F)=\varepsilon>0
,
then

\mu_{\circ}(RD\cap T^{-n}RT^{n}D)\sim const\varepsilon.

The correlations
\mu({\bf T}^{n}F\cap F)
for some Sidon constructions
{\bf T}
can be so that

\sum_{n}\mu({\bf T}^{n}F\cap F)^{4}<\infty

(see
[
11
]
). Then

\sum_{n}\mu_{\circ}^{\otimes 4}((S^{n}D)^{\times 4}\cap(T^{n}D)^{\times 4})<\infty.

Some powers
(S^{\times 4})^{p}
and
(T^{\times 4})^{p}
, and

D^{\prime}\subset D^{\times 4}
of a positive measure
\mu_{\circ}^{\otimes 4}
ensure the absence of recurrence.
Our Poisson suspensios have zero entropy, so get desired linear non-recurrence.

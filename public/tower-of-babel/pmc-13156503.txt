Efficacy, pharmacodynamics, pharmacokinetics, safety, and immunogenicity of Bmab 1000 vs reference denosumab: 78-wk results from a randomized, double-blind, multicenter, parallel-arm Phase 3 trial (DEVOTE) in women with postmenopausal osteoporosis
Authors: Richard Eastell, Eric Orwoll, Felicia Cosman, Rafal Plebanski, Kuldeep Kumar, Ashwani Marwah, Sarika S Deodhar, Elena Wolff-Holz, Subramanian Loganathan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis randomized, double-blind, multicenter Phase 3 trial evaluated the efficacy, safety, pharmacodynamics (PD), pharmacokinetics (PK), and immunogenicity of Bmab 1000 vs reference denosumab in 479 women with postmenopausal osteoporosis. Participants (mean age 66.6 yr; lumbar spine T-score ≤−2.5 to ≥−4.0) were randomized 1:1 to receive 60 mg Bmab 1000 (n = 238) or reference denosumab (n = 241) subcutaneously. The study comprised 2 parts: Part 1 (weeks 0-52) evaluated therapeutic and PD equivalence between Bmab 1000 and reference denosumab using percentage change from baseline (%CfB) in lumbar spine bone mineral density (BMD) at week 52 and area under the effect curve (AUEC) of serum C-terminal telopeptide of Type 1 collagen (sCTX) from baseline to 26 wk following dosing on day 1 and week 26. Part 2 (weeks 52-78) re-randomized those on reference denosumab to either continue or switch to Bmab 1000 (n = 104 each), while those on Bmab 1000 continued treatment (n = 218). Part 2 monitored the safety of Bmab 1000 and reference denosumab after transition from reference denosumab to Bmab 1000 vs continuing reference denosumab. Secondary analyses included additional efficacy, PD, PK, safety, and immunogenicity parameters. At week 52, the confidence intervals (CIs) for the difference in least square means (LSMs) in %CfB in lumbar spine BMD between Bmab 1000 and reference denosumab were contained within the predefined margins (±1.45; LSM diff: 0.610, 95% CI: −0.095, 1.316), indicating therapeutic equivalence. The sCTX AUEC up to 26 wk for Bmab 1000 and reference denosumab were similar (geometric LSM ratio: 104.14; 95% CI: 97.76, 110.95). The secondary results were comparable among all groups up to week 78, including after transitioning to Bmab 1000 from reference denosumab. In conclusion, Bmab 1000 demonstrated equivalent efficacy and PD to reference denosumab, with similar PK, safety, and immunogenicity profiles. There was no effect of transitioning from reference denosumab to Bmab 1000.Clinical trial registration: EudraCT number: 2021-006545-36, CT.gov number: NCT05345691.

Introduction

Osteoporosis, a systemic skeletal disease characterized by low bone mineral density (BMD) and micro-architectural deterioration of bone tissue, with consequent increase in bone fragility and susceptibility to fractures, represents a major public health concern due to its associated morbidity, mortality, and economic burden.1,2 According to the World Health Organization, BMD levels that meet the criteria for osteoporosis affect around 6.3% of men and 21.2% of women aged over 50 yr worldwide. This translates to an estimated 500 million individuals affected by the condition.3 It is expected that the number of hip fractures will increase from 7.3 million to 21.3 million by 2050.4

Postmenopausal osteoporosis is the most common type of osteoporosis.2 As per the Endocrine Society Clinical Practice Guideline, pharmacologic options for managing postmenopausal osteoporosis include antiresorptive agents (bisphosphonates and denosumab), selective estrogen receptor modulators, and anabolic therapies (teriparatide, abaloparatide, and romosozumab).5 Overall, the goal of all osteoporosis treatments is to lower the risk of fractures.6,7 Sequential therapy with anabolic agents followed by antiresorptive treatments may be an effective strategy to enhance bone health and reduce subsequent fracture risk, although antiresorptive-only therapies are equally effective, with both strategies offering different long-term benefits.8,9

Denosumab is a fully human immunoglobulin G2 monoclonal antibody that binds to RANKL, a cytokine expressed by cells of the osteoblast lineage that is a key regulator of osteoclastic bone resorption.10 Denosumab is marketed in the United States (US) and Europe by Amgen under the brand name Prolia as a single-use pre-filled syringe (PFS) containing 60 mg of denosumab in 1 mL of solution (60 mg/mL).11,12

The pivotal denosumab trial [Fracture Reduction Evaluation of Denosumab in Osteoporosis Every 6 Months (FREEDOM)] demonstrated that a 60 mg s.c. dose of denosumab, twice yearly for 36 mo, lowered the risk of vertebral [risk ratio, 0.32; 95% confidence interval (CI), 0.26-0.41; p < .001], nonvertebral [hazard ratio (HR), 0.80; 95% CI, 0.67-0.95; p = .01], and hip fractures (HR, 0.60; 95% CI, 0.37-0.97; p = .04) among women with osteoporosis.13 Overall adverse events (AEs) and serious AEs (SAEs) were balanced between groups, but denosumab-treated patients had a higher risk of eczema, hypocalcemia, and cellulitis.13 The 10-yr FREEDOM extension trial demonstrated denosumab’s long-term effectiveness, safety, tolerability, and favorable benefit/risk balance. The rate of osteonecrosis of the jaw (ONJ) was 5 per 10 000 patient-years and atypical femur fracture was <1 per 10 000 patient-years.14 Recently, a boxed warning was added to the denosumab label regarding severe hypocalcemia in patients with severe chronic renal disease.15

Denosumab is also approved for osteoporosis in men, women with breast cancer on aromatase inhibitor therapy, and men with prostate cancer on androgen deprivation therapy.16 Denosumab 120 mg (Xgeva) is also approved by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) and the European Medicines Agency (EMA) for the treatment of skeletal-related events in patients with bone metastases from solid tumors and giant cell tumor of the bone.17,18

Although biologic therapies, such as denosumab, have become increasingly important in the current clinical landscape due to their applications across a range of therapy areas, treatment access can be hampered by costs.19 Biosimilars, which are developed in compliance with rigorous regulatory standards by the FDA and the EMA, match the reference biologic in molecular structure, biological activity, pharmacological behavior, and clinical performance. The availability of biosimilars can significantly reduce treatment costs, easing the burden on healthcare systems and promoting more equitable access to standard care.20,21

The test drug, Bmab 1000, was developed as a potentially lower cost biosimilar option to reference denosumab, Prolia, with the aim to improve patient access. Both reference denosumab and Bmab 1000 demonstrated similarity in terms of quality characteristics and structural and functional activity. The pharmacokinetics (PK) profiles of Bmab 1000 and reference denosumab demonstrated PK equivalence in a Phase 1 study in normal healthy volunteers [Data on File]. The current study aimed to demonstrate therapeutic equivalence in terms of efficacy, pharmacodynamics (PD), PK, safety, and immunogenicity between Bmab 1000 and reference denosumab in postmenopausal women with osteoporosis. The study also aimed to evaluate the effect of switching from reference denosumab to Bmab 1000.

Materials and methods

Study design

This was a 78-wk, randomized, double-blind, multicenter, parallel-arm, Phase 3 clinical study designed to compare the BMD effects, PD effects on bone turnover markers, PK, safety, and immunogenicity of Bmab 1000 (60 mg) with reference denosumab (60 mg) in postmenopausal women with osteoporosis (EudraCT number: 2021-006545-36; CT.gov number: NCT05345691). Details of the study design are presented in Figure 1.

Study design. AUEC, area under the effect curve; BMD, bone mineral density; EoS, end of study; lumbar spine, least squares; PD, pharmacodynamics; PK, pharmacokinetics; sCTX, serum C-terminal telopeptide of Type 1 collagen; %CfB, percentage change from baseline.

This study was conducted at 42 centers (35 in Europe and 7 in the United States) and comprised 3 periods: screening, Part 1 (double-blind active controlled), and Part 2 (transition). The maximum study duration for each patient was approximately 18 mo, excluding the screening phase.

Screening period

All screening evaluations were completed within 28 d prior to randomization.

Part 1: double-blind, active-controlled period

In Part 1, eligible patients were randomized 1:1 to receive either Bmab 1000 (60 mg) or reference denosumab (60 mg) via s.c. injection on day 1 (week 0) and at week 26. An interactive web response system was used for randomization, with schedules generated using SAS Version 9.4. Randomization was stratified by geographic region (US or Europe), prior use of bisphosphonates (Yes or No), and age of the patient (<65 or ≥65 yr). Patients were followed-up for 26 wk after the second dose.

Part 2: transition period

At week 52, all patients who completed Part 1 were re-randomized. Those patients originally assigned to reference denosumab were re-randomized in a 1:1 ratio to receive either Bmab 1000 or reference denosumab to assess the effects of a single switch from reference denosumab to Bmab 1000 until week 78. Those originally randomized to the Bmab 1000 arm continued to receive Bmab 1000 till week 78 in a blinded manner.

This study was conducted in accordance with the protocol and consensus ethical principles derived from international guidelines, the Declaration of Helsinki, ICH E6(R2), EMA, FDA Code of Federal Regulations Title 21, applicable ICH GCP Guidelines, and other local applicable laws and regulations.

Blinding

Bmab 1000 was supplied as a PFS without a needle safety guard, whereas reference denosumab was supplied as a PFS with needle safety guard. Thus, blinding from the primary packaging was not feasible, but the blinding was maintained at the secondary packaging level with similar packaging for both the products. Therefore, 2 different teams, that is, blinded and unblinded teams, were assigned to maintain the blinding and handling of the study drug administration. The predefined, unblinded site staff administered the study medication injections in such a manner that the patient remained blinded (eg, blindfold, screen, or similar method during the dosing procedure so that the injection syringe was not visible to the patient). Blinded staff were not involved in any activities about the receipt, handling, or administration of study medication. The study blinding was maintained until the end of the study at week 78. A specific unblinding plan was followed at week 52, to re-randomize patients for participation in part 2. In case of medical emergency, a patient’s treatment assignment could be unblinded.

Study patients

Postmenopausal women aged 55-80 yr and weighing 50-90 kg were included in this study. Eligible patients had osteoporosis, with lumbar spine (L1-L4) BMD T-score classification ≤−2.5 and ≥−4.0, and were required to have at least 3 vertebrae in the L1-L4 region and at least 1 hip evaluable by DXA. Patients were excluded from the study if their T-score was <−4.0 at the lumbar spine, total hip (TH), or femoral neck (FN); had a known history of previous exposure to denosumab or known hypersensitivity to denosumab or its constituents or latex allergy or hereditary problems of fructose intolerance; used any biologic drugs within 90 d or within 5 half-lives of the drug, whichever was longer; or used systemic glucocorticoids or other bone active drugs within 3 mo of screening. Patients with a history and/or presence of one severe or ≥3 moderate vertebral fractures (as determined by central reading of lateral spine X-ray during the screening period) were excluded from the study.

Endpoint assessments

The co-primary efficacy endpoints evaluated differences between Bmab 1000 and reference denosumab for percentage change from baseline (%CfB) at week 52 in the lumbar spine BMD by DXA and area under the effect curve (AUEC) of the bone resorption marker serum C-terminal telopeptide of Type 1 collagen (sCTX) from baseline to week 26.

The secondary endpoints included BMD changes at the lumbar spine at week 26 and TH and FN at both 26- and 52-wk timepoints, serum levels of CTX and procollagen Type 1 N-terminal propeptide (P1NP), safety, PK, and immunogenicity from weeks 0 to 52 between Bmab 1000 and reference denosumab. For the transition period (weeks 52-78), assessments included sCTX, P1NP, BMD at lumbar spine, TH and FN, PK, safety, and immunogenicity following transition from reference denosumab to Bmab 1000 compared with subjects who continued on reference denosumab. Details of the study objectives and endpoints are provided in Table S1.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were conducted using SAS software, Version 9.4, and PD parameters were estimated using Phoenix WinNonlin software, Version 8.3 (Certara USA, Inc.).

Sample size analysis

The sample size was calculated based on the primary efficacy endpoint. Equivalence between treatments was established if the 95% CI for the difference in mean %CfB between Bmab 1000 and reference denosumab fell within the margin of −1.45% to 1.45%. Assuming equal treatment efficacy and a common SD of 4.5%, a sample size of 204 patients per group (408 total) was estimated to provide at least 80% power using 2 one-sided tests at a 2.5% significance level. However, accounting for potential dropouts of ~15%, a total of 480 patients (n = 240 patients/arm) were randomized in this study. A 90% CI to establish equivalence provided more power for this sample size. Furthermore, the current sample size had high power for the co-primary PD endpoint, sCTX AUEC; 204 evaluable patients/group with a margin of 80.00% to 125.00% and between-patient coefficient of variation (CV) of 45% provided >95% power to demonstrate similarity for the co-primary endpoint (using 95% CI equivalent to 2 one-sided tests at the 2.5% level).

Analyses of endpoints

For the full analysis set (FAS; all randomized patients who received at least one dose of study drug), the co-primary efficacy endpoint was estimated using an analysis of covariance (ANCOVA) model fitted to the composite %CfB in the lumbar spine BMD at week 52. Multiple imputed datasets for visit by treatment, with stratification variables (region, age, and prior use of bisphosphonates) included as classification factors, baseline BMD included as a continuous covariate, and treatment were used. The estimated least squares mean (LSM) difference in %CfB in the lumbar spine BMD at week 52 was presented with a 90% CI for each delta. The 2 one-sided tests of α = .05 were equivalent if the 90% CIs fell within the predefined equivalence margin of (−1.45%, 1.45%). For the modified FAS (mFAS; patients who had a data record at each time point for all patients in the FAS but excluded data observed after the first occurrence of pre-defined intercurrent events), a mixed model for repeated measures (MMRM) was fitted to the %CfB in the lumbar spine BMD until week 52. The MMRM included stratification variables (region, age, and prior use of bisphosphonates) as classification factors, baseline BMD as a continuous covariate, and treatment by visit. The repeated measures on patients were modeled with no fixed pattern for variability over time. The estimated LSM difference in %CfB in the lumbar spine BMD was presented with 95% CI at each time point. Equivalence was concluded if the 95% CI fell within the predefined equivalence margins of (−1.45%, 1.45%) at week 52. Co-primary PD comparability was confirmed if the 95% CI for the geometric LSM ratio of AUEC for sCTX fell entirely within the range of 80.00%–125.00%. An ANCOVA model was fitted to log-transformed AUEC (mFAS), with logged predose sCTX concentrations and baseline estimated glomerular filtration rate as a covariate and the treatment group and all stratification factors as fixed effects.

Sensitivity, supplementary, and subgroup analyses were performed on the co-primary variables as predefined for the study. All secondary safety, efficacy, PD, PK, and immunogenicity endpoints up to week 78 were analyzed up to the end-of-study. The effect of immunogenicity on efficacy was also explored. Safety endpoints included treatment-emergent AEs (TEAEs), clinically significant changes in health parameters, and the incidence of SAEs, AEs of special interest (AESI), and deaths, tracked from different dosing stages up to week 78. Immunogenicity was assessed as the incidence of anti-drug antibodies (ADAs) and neutralizing antibodies (NAbs), both up to week 52 and through week 78.

Immunogenicity

A highly sensitive and validated method was used to detect ADA (bridging ECL immunoassay method using the Meso Scale Discovery technology). The immunogenicity assay used in this study had a sensitivity of 0.912 ng/mL for the screening assay and 0.564 ng/mL for the confirmatory assay.

Results

Patient disposition

A total of 479 patients were randomized 1:1 to the Bmab 1000 (n = 238) and reference denosumab (n = 241) groups in Part 1 of the study. Of the randomized patients, 426 (88.9%) completed Part 1, including 218 (91.6%) in the Bmab 1000 group and 208 (86.3%) in the reference denosumab group. These 426 patients entered Part 2, where they either continued Bmab 1000 (Bmab 1000–Bmab 1000 group; n = 218) or were re-randomized 1:1 to continue reference denosumab (reference denosumab–reference denosumab group; n = 104) or switch from reference denosumab to Bmab 1000 (reference denosumab–Bmab 1000 group; n = 104). A total of 478 (99.8%), 435 (90.8%), and 426 (88.9%) received the first, second, and third doses of the study treatment, respectively. Overall, 52 (10.9%) and 4 (0.9%) patients discontinued Parts 1 and 2 of the study, respectively. The most common reason for study discontinuation was withdrawal of consent. By week 78, 422 patients (99.1%) completed the study (Figure 2).

Demographics and baseline characteristics

The demographic and baseline characteristics were comparable between the treatment groups. The mean age of the randomized patients was 67 yr, with majority of the patients (64%) in the age group of ≥65 to <80 yr. The vast majority of patients (99%) were enrolled from Europe. The baseline mean lumbar spine T-score was −3.056 (0.38) in the Bmab 1000 group and −3.071 (0.38) in the reference denosumab group, with a similar distribution of patients having T-scores ≤−3 and >−3. At baseline, 93.3% of patients had no prior use of bisphosphonates. A total of 97 patients (20.3%) had a radiographic vertebral fracture at screening, with Genant grades classified as mild in 53.6% and moderate in 46.4% of those with vertebral fractures (Table 1). Prior fragility fractures at non-vertebral sites were recorded in a total of 26 patients in the Bmab 1000 group and 33 patients in the reference denosumab group. Details are provided in Table S2.

Part 1

Co-primary efficacy endpoints

At week 52, the difference in LSMs for the 90% (FAS) and 95% (mFAS) CIs in %CfB in lumbar spine BMD between the Bmab 1000 and reference denosumab treatment groups were 0.601 (0.024, 1.179) and 0.610 (−0.095, 1.316), respectively. As the 90% and 95% CIs of the difference in LSMs %CfB in lumbar spine BMD were entirely contained within the predefined margin of (−1.45%, 1.45%), therapeutic equivalence of Bmab 1000 to reference denosumab was established (Figure 3).

%CfB (day 1) up to week 52 in lumbar spine BMD using (A) FAS and (B) mFAS. BMD, bone mineral density; CI, confidence interval; FAS, full analysis set; mFAS, modified full analysis set; %CfB, percentage change from baseline.

Results of the sensitivity, supplementary, and subgroup analyses supported the main estimation results, further supporting the equivalence between Bmab 1000 and reference denosumab.

Co-primary endpoints: Bone turnover marker results

Following a single administration of Bmab 1000 to postmenopausal women with osteoporosis, sCTX AUEC up to 26 wk was comparable to that observed following a single administration of reference denosumab, with a geometric LSM ratio (Bmab 1000/reference denosumab) of 104.14%. Furthermore, the 95% CI around the geometric LSM ratio (97.76%-110.95%) was contained entirely within the predefined acceptance limits of 80.00%-125.00%, indicating that Bmab 1000 was pharmacodynamically equivalent to reference denosumab (Table S3).

Secondary endpoints

The mean %CfB in lumbar spine, TH, and FN BMD increased over time for both the Bmab 1000 and reference denosumab treatment groups up to week 52. The mean %CfB in the lumbar spine and TH BMD was similar between the treatment groups at both weeks 26 and 52. However, the mean %CfB in the FN BMD at week 26 was slightly lower for the reference denosumab group compared to the Bmab 1000 group, but was similar at week 52 (Figure 3 and Figure S1; Table S4 and Table S5). In the Bmab 1000 group, 1 patient had a moderate thoracic fracture at week 26, and another had a mild lumbar fracture at week 52. No patient in the reference denosumab group had any fracture event by weeks 26 and 52.

Similar baseline sCTX and P1NP serum concentrations were observed for both treatment groups, and remained similar over the first 26 wk following the first administration. The concentrations of sCTX and P1NP at week 52 were similar.

Following a single administration of Bmab 1000 or reference denosumab, the PD parameters of sCTX between treatments were similar over time.

Part 2

Efficacy endpoints

Reference denosumab–reference denosumab vs reference denosumab–Bmab 1000

At week 78, the mean %CfB (from week 52 to week 78) in the lumbar spine BMD was similar for reference denosumab–reference denosumab and reference denosumab–Bmab 1000 treatment groups (1.328 and 1.130; 90% CI: −0.859, 0.464), suggesting the transition from reference denosumab to Bmab 1000 did not impact the %CfB in the lumbar spine BMD.

Reference denosumab–reference denosumab vs Bmab 1000–Bmab 1000

At week 78, the mean %CfB (from week 52 to week 78) in the lumbar spine BMD was also similar for reference denosumab–reference denosumab and Bmab 1000–Bmab 1000 treatment groups (1.328 and 0.807; 90% CI: −1.085, 0.044).

Lumbar spine, TH, FN BMD, and incidence of fracture up to week 78

In the reference denosumab–Bmab 1000 group, the mean %CfB from day 1 to week 78 in the lumbar spine, TH, and FN BMD were similar to that in the Bmab 1000–Bmab 1000 and reference denosumab–reference denosumab treatment groups, suggesting the transition from reference denosumab to Bmab 1000 did not impact the %CfB in the lumbar spine, TH, and FN BMD (Table S6). No further vertebral fractures were reported in the treatment groups.

Bone turnover marker results

The concentrations of sCTX and P1NP at week 78 were similar to the concentrations at weeks 26 and 52 for all 3 treatment groups. No change in the sCTX and P1NP concentration was observed at week 78 following transition from reference denosumab to Bmab 1000 compared to patients who continued to receive reference denosumab at week 52 (Figure 4).

Trough concentrations of denosumab at weeks 26 and 52 were similar to the denosumab trough concentrations at week 78 for all 3 treatment groups. Safety results

The safety profile of Bmab 1000 was comparable to the safety profile of reference denosumab, including the transition arm. The proportion of patients reporting TEAEs and study drug-related TEAEs was similar for the treatment groups. The incidence of serious TEAEs was low in Part 1 and remained low in Part 2. None of the serious TEAEs in either treatment group was considered related to the study drug by the investigators. The incidence of AESIs was low throughout the study (Table 2). Most TEAEs were mild to moderate in severity (Grade 1 or 2), and the proportion of Grade 3 TEAEs was similar between the groups. Two Grade 4 TEAEs (staphylococcal sepsis in the reference denosumab group and pancreatic carcinoma in the Bmab 1000 group) and one Grade 5 TEAE (cerebrovascular accident in the reference denosumab group) were reported; none were considered related to the study drug. Only 2 cases of Grade 1 hypocalcemia (one in each treatment group) were reported throughout the study. Transition from reference denosumab to Bmab 1000 did not have any effect on the incidence or severity of AEs.

Throughout the study, the most common TEAEs (≥2.0% of total patients) included upper respiratory tract infection (most common TEAEs in both Part 1 and Part 2), nasopharyngitis, coronavirus SARS-CoV-2 disease 2019 (COVID-19), urinary tract infection, headache, arthralgia, hypercholesteremia, back pain, osteoarthritis, hypertension, dizziness, bronchitis, pharyngitis, and cystitis (Table S7).

Injection site reactions (ISRs) presenting as erythema/redness and/or swelling/hardness were reported in 9 patients in Part 1, with 6 of them occurring on the day of first dose administration (3 patients each in the Bmab 1000 and reference denosumab groups) and the remaining 3 occurred after the second dose at week 26 (1 patient in the Bmab 1000 group and 2 patients in the reference denosumab group). Except for 1 moderate ISR in 1 patient from the reference denosumab group, all other ISRs were mild in severity. In Part 2, 2 patients reported mild ISRs (erythema/redness) after the third dose at week 52 (1 patient each in the Bmab 1000–Bmab 1000 and the reference denosumab–reference denosumab treatment groups). None of the patients who transitioned from reference denosumab to Bmab 1000 reported any ISR.

Overall, there were no clinically relevant changes and differences in vital signs, electrocardiography, physical examinations, and laboratory findings during the study.

Immunogenicity results

The proportion of patients with ADA-positive samples at any time after initiation of treatment was high. This was attributed to the highly sensitive assay. However, there was no meaningful difference between the treatment groups in the development of ADA or NAb during the study, including during the transition phase (Table 3). The proportion of patients who tested positive for NAb during the study was low. Transitioning from reference denosumab to Bmab 1000 did not increase the incidence of NAb positivity compared to patients who continued on either reference denosumab or Bmab 1000.

Discussion

This clinical equivalence study demonstrated no meaningful differences between Bmab 1000 and reference denosumab in terms of BMD changes, bone turnover marker responses, safety, or immunogenicity in postmenopausal women with osteoporosis. Transitioning from reference denosumab at week 52 to Bmab 1000 did not affect the change in lumbar spine BMD compared to patients who continued treatment with reference denosumab.

Over 12 mo, the 5%-6% spine BMD increments seen in both the Bmab 1000 and reference denosumab groups were very similar to the spine BMD gain seen in the pivotal FREEDOM trial at that time point.13 Our results are also consistent with other biosimilar studies on lumbar spine BMD.22–27 Results from this study demonstrated similar changes in TH and FN BMD between Bmab 1000 and reference denosumab at week 52. The TH BMD gain of 4% was also comparable to that observed at 1 yr in the FREEDOM trial.13 Only 2 patients had vertebral fractures during the study, consistent with the demonstrated efficacy of denosumab against fracture. Bone turnover markers in our study showed similar results across groups, with sCTX and P1NP levels falling toward or below the lower limit of normal. These findings match the FREEDOM trial and other biosimilar studies, which also reported consistent reductions in these markers over time.13,22–24,27,28

The incidence of hypocalcemia was lower in our study compared to the FREEDOM trial and other studies on denosumab biosimilars.13 This could be due to the definition of hypocalcemia or to the timing of the blood samples. The incidence of hypocalcemia was defined as albumin-corrected serum calcium <8.5 mg/dL in our study and albumin-adjusted calcium <8.0 mg/dL in the FREEDOM trial.13

In our study, TEAEs were reported in 66.1% (Bmab 1000) and 69.2% (reference denosumab) of the patients, with drug-related TEAEs in 10.1% and 12.5% and serious TEAEs in 6.9% and 1.9% of the patients, respectively. Most events were mild (Grade 1 or 2), and the few Grade 4 and Grade 5 events were not considered to be related to the study drug. Other biosimilar studies also reported comparable safety outcomes, with TEAEs occurring in 60%-75% of patients receiving biosimilars of denosumab.22,23,27 AESIs and ISRs were mild and similar across groups. Transitioning between treatments did not affect the rates of serious TEAEs. Our safety profile aligns with the FREEDOM trial, which reported similar rates of AEs. No cases of ONJ or atypical femur fracture occurred in our study, although it was not powered to detect rare AEs.

Similar to other biosimilar studies, the assays used to detect ADA were very sensitive.22,26,27 Because of the high sensitivity, a high number of patients (≥87.0%) in both treatment groups tested positive for ADA throughout the treatment period, which is lower than that reported in other studies (97%-99%).22,26,27 Only a small proportion (2.5%) developed NAb by week 52. The rates of ADA and NAb were similar between the Bmab 1000 and reference denosumab groups up to week 52. Also, compared to those who stayed on reference denosumab, switching patients from reference denosumab to Bmab 1000 did not change how often ADA or NAb appeared.

Access to denosumab treatment is limited by economic barriers, as high costs can impede patient access and restrict widespread use. Access to denosumab biosimilars in the osteoporosis marketplace has the potential to improve the accessibility and affordability of osteoporosis treatment compared with reference denosumab. Overall, denosumab biosimilars could play a crucial role in bridging the osteoporosis treatment gap. Denosumab biosimilars can also be used in other therapeutic areas that are on the label of Xgeva, for example, cancer, metastatic bone disease, and osteoporosis in men.17,18

One of the key strengths of our study is that it was well powered and used 2 one-sided tests at a 2.5% level of significance. The study employed appropriate endpoints, such as BMD at multiple skeletal sites, sCTX and P1NP, PK, and ADA, to conduct a comprehensive analysis of Bmab 1000. It also examined switching from reference denosumab to Bmab 1000. Additionally, the study included an adequate extension period of 78 wk to assess the full extent of the effects of treatment. The population, which included only women from the US and EU, who were primarily Caucasian, was homogenous as is adequate for a clinical study evaluating a biosimilar. However, given the duration of the study, the possibility of rare AEs attributed to long-term denosumab exposure could not be evaluated. Additionally, the rebound effect, a known clinical challenge upon denosumab discontinuation, was not investigated in this study. In clinical practice, to suppress the osteoclast overshoot, reduce bone loss, and mitigate the risk of multiple vertebral fractures, a bisphosphonate should be initiated. This is particularly important if denosumab is used long-term (ie, >3 yr).29,30

In conclusion, our results demonstrated equivalent effects between Bmab 1000 and reference denosumab in improving BMD at the spine and hip and reducing bone turnover markers. Reductions in serum levels of CTX and P1NP were maintained through 78 wk. Transitioning to Bmab 1000 from reference denosumab did not affect any aspects of efficacy or safety. The results of this study confirm the similarity in PK/PD and safety, including immunogenicity, between Bmab 1000 and reference denosumab.

Comparison of different methods for the detection of HLA-B* 57:01 allele in people living with HIV in Eastern Uttar Pradesh, India
Authors: Tulika Kumari Rai, Jaya Chakravarty, Kumari Priyanka, Abhilasha Gautam, Shweta Srivastva
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe gold standard for detecting HLA-B*57:01 allele linked to Abacavir hypersensitivity is sequence-based typing (SBT), which is labour-intensive, not readily available and needs special expertise. HCP5 gene polymorphism is a surrogate marker for HLA-B*57:01 allele. Therefore, we compared, nested PCR for HLA-B*57:01 allele, HCP5 PCR with SBT method to develop a simple and cost-effective method for detection of HLA-B*57:01 allele.MethodWhole blood cells were collected from PLHIV and Buffy coat and DNA was isolated. Direct PCR for HLA-B*57 gene followed by nested PCR for the HLA-B* 57:01 allele and HCP5 gene PCR was done in all patients. The PCR results of both methods were confirmed by the SBT method.ResultAmong 366 samples, 25 (6.83%) were positive for HLA-B* 57:01 allele by nested PCR. HCP5 gene PCR was positive in 25 (6.83%) individuals who were also positive for the HLA-B*57:01 allele with nested PCR. A strong correlation was observed between the HLA-B*57:01 allele and HCP5 with negative and positive predictive values of 100% respectively in our population.ConclusionNested (two-step) and HCP5 gene PCR (single-step) are simple, rapid and cost-effective methods for HLA-B*57:01 allele detection and either can be adapted in the ART centre located in resource-poor settings.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12879-025-11790-w.

Background

Abacavir (ABC), a guanosine analogue that inhibits reverse transcriptase, is presently used as a first-line treatment for children living with HIV (CLHIV) with a haemoglobin level below 9 g/dl by the National AIDS Control Organization (NACO) in India and it is also suggested as an alternative initial treatment strategy for adults experiencing adverse side effects with Tenofovir [1, 2]. The effectiveness of the drug is offset by its potential to cause a severe hypersensitivity reaction (ABC-HSR) in 5–8% of patients [3]. As a result of this limitation, clinicians must be aware of this ABC-HSR during clinical management of patients. A major association has been identified between ABC hypersensitivity and the presence of the HLA-B*57:01 allele, making its detection crucial before ABC administration [4]. The presence of the HLA-B*57:01 allele exhibits an exceptional level of specificity for ABC-HSR, since it is not triggered by its subtypes such as HLA-B*57:02, HLA-B*57:03, HLA-B*57:11 or HLA-B*58:01 allele [5]. Currently, sequence-based typing (SBT) method is considered the reliable method for detecting the HLA-B*57:01 allele. However, its use is limited due to its labour-intensiveness, and the requirement of specialized facilities, which poses a challenge, particularly in resource-limited countries [6–8]. There are several alternative methods available for the detection of the HLA-B*57:01 allele such as the serological method, flowcytometry, sequence-specific oligonucleotide probes (SSOP), sequence-specific Primers (SSP) and real-time PCR but these methods don’t show 100% concordance with the results of SBT and their cost is high and time taking (Table 1). A study has demonstrated that the HLA complex P5 (HCP5) (rs2395029) polymorphism (T/G) is located in the MHC-I region. The HCP5 gene is 110 kb centromeric to the HLA-B locus [9]. The presence of the rs2395029 (G) polymorphism is almost 99.9% predictive of the presence of HLA-B*57:01 alleles and has shown perfect linkage disequilibrium in certain populations [7, 10, 11]. Those who are heterozygous T/G (heterozygous mutant) for HCP5 have moderate risk, while individuals who are homozygous for the G/G (homozygous mutant) allele have the highest risk of HSR [12]. There is no risk of developing ABC-HSR in individuals who are homozygous for the T/T (wild type) HCP5 (rs2395029) polymorphism. Therefore, detection of the HCP5 gene could serve as a surrogate marker for identifying the HLA-B*57:01 allele in a population displaying complete linkage disequilibrium between the two. However, the sensitivity and specificity of the HCP5 for detecting the presence of the HLA-B*57:01 allele varies across populations (Table 2). Since the prevalence of the HLA-B*57:01 allele is high in our population it becomes imperative to develop a simple, cost-effective, and accurate diagnostic tool for detecting HLA-B*57:01 allele in this region [6]. Thus, to simplify the detection of the HLA-B*57:01 allele, we performed direct PCR followed by nested PCR and also HCP5 PCR as a surrogate marker for the detection of HLA-B*57:01 allele. We compared the results of both nested PCR and HCP5 PCR with SBT.

Materials and methods

Study design & period and population

This observational study was conducted between August 2018 to August 2020 in the Anti-retroviral Treatment (ART) Center and Centre of Excellence (CoE) for HIV care, Department of Medicine, IMS, BHU. This study included People Living with HIV (PLHIV) who were 18 years or older, attended the ART Center and provided written consent before participation and their clinical data were also recorded. CD4 + lymphocyte counts were done by using BD FACS Calibur (Becton Dickinson Biosciences) and the values were expressed as cells/µL.

Sample size determination

The formula used to determine the sample size was

n = Z2 p (1-p)/d2

Where n = Sample size, p = Estimated prevalence (from previous study).

Z = Confidence interval (CI) at 95% (Standard value of 1.96), d = precision.

In our previous study, the prevalence of the HLA-B*57:01 allele in HIV-positive patients was 12.25%. Considering the 12.25% prevalence of the HLA-B*5701 allele in the eastern Indian population with 95% CI and 3.5% precision, the sample size was calculated to be 338. Further, taking 5% wastage of specimen, sample size (n) was enhanced to 355.

Sample collection and total DNA extraction

We collected 2.5 ml of whole blood in an EDTA vial from 366 PLHIV from eastern Uttar Pradesh. The blood was centrifuged at 2500 rpm for 10 min at 20 °C to obtain the Buffy coat and it was transferred to a sterile polypropylene tube and stored at −20 °C until further use. DNA was isolated from the buffy coat by using the QIAamp DNA Blood Mini Kit (QIagen, Hilden, Germany), as per the manufacturer’s protocol. The DNA was stored at −80 °C until further use. The quality and quantity of DNA were assessed using Nanodrop (Thermo Scientific, USA).

HLA-B*57 amplification by direct PCR

HLA-B*57 gene amplification was done by using a sequence-specific primer of exon2 and exon 3 regions of the MHC class I molecule as described by Casella et al. [26]. The primer used in direct PCR amplifies the HLA-B*57 gene, while the primers for nested PCR target the HLA-B*57:01, HLA-B*57:06, HLA-B*57:08 and HLA-B*5514, allele [17]. Primers used for the amplification of the HLA-B*57 gene are HLA-B*57-F1-5′GTCTCACATCATCCAGGT-3′ and HLA-B*57-R1-5’CGTCTCCTTCCCGTTCTC-3’ (Eurofins Genomics India Pvt Ltd). The Presence of human genomic DNA was verified by amplification of a 136 bp fragment of the GAPDH gene by using primers GAPDH-F (5’-GGCAGCAGCAAGCATTCCT-3’) and GAPDH-R (5’-GCCCAACACCCCCAGTCA-3’. The reaction mixture contained 1ul of extracted DNA (50ng/ul), 12.5ul of Dream Taq Green PCR master mix (Thermo Scientific, USA), and 1ul of each HLA-B*57 primer (10pmol/ul) and 0.5ul of GAPDH primer (10pmol/ul) in a total volume of 25ul. For the final volume, DNase/RNase-free water was utilized. the PCR (Applied Biosystems, USA) conditions were fixed at 98° for 6 min; 98° for 30 s, 60° for 45 s, and 72° for 30 s for 35 cycles, with a final extension step at 72° for 10 min. The PCR product was then electrophoresed on a 1.2% agarose gel stained with Good-View nucleic acid dye and photographed using the Gel Doc 2000 gel documentation system (Bio-Rad) under UV light utilising the alpha Innotech Gel documentation system. Sequence-based typing (Sanger sequencing) method was employed to confirm the direct PCR result.

Nested PCR

PCR product positive with direct PCR was used as the template for the nested PCR. Various dilutions of direct PCR product were used for the accurate result of nested PCR and dilution 1/10 was optimized to use as template. Primer used for nested PCR were HLA-B*57-F1-5′GTCTCACATCATCCAGGT-3′ and two reverse primers for its subtypes HLA-B*57:01 (R2-5’ATCCTTGCCGTCGTAGGCGG-3′and HLA-B*57:01:02-R3-5′-ATCCTTGCCGTCGTAGGCAG-3′ [26]. These two primers differ in only one nucleotide and codes for the HLA-B*57:01:01 and HLA-B*57:01:02 respectively [26]. 25 µl of reaction mixture was prepared containing 1 µl of the diluted direct PCR product, 12.5 µl Dream Taq Green PCR master mix (Thermo Scientific, USA), forward and reverse primers 1 µl (10 pmol/µl each), 0.5µl of each GAPDH primer. The PCR cycle condition was fixed at 95° for 5 min, 94 °C for 30 s, 60 °C for 1 min, 72 °C for 30 s (the last three steps were repeated for 35 cycles) with final extension at 72 °C for 10 min. nested-PCR products were verified on 2.5% agarose gel by electrophoresis stained with Good-View nucleic acid dye and photographed using the Gel Doc 2000 gel documentation system (Bio-Rad) under UV light utilising the alpha Innotech Gel documentation system.

PCR for HCP5 gene

For the amplification of HCP5, 25 µl of reactions were done in two separate tubes (one for each specific allele, T or G, respectively) for the same sample. The primer used for HCP5 were two forward primers which differ in one nucleotide, one forward primer (specific for T allele) −5′ ATACTGTCCAATTCCCCTGT 3′ and other forward primer (specific for G allele) −5′ ATACTGTCCAATTCCCCTGG 3′ and one common reverse primer Reverse HCP5-R1- 5′- CACTGGTCCTATCACCATCT 3′ as described by Galvan et al. [8]. The reaction consisted of 1µl of the extracted DNA (50ng/µl); 12.5µl Dream Taq Green PCR master mix (Thermo Scientific, USA), 1 µl of T specific forward primer (10pmol/µl) was added into one tube and G specific forward primer (10pmol/µl) was added into other tube and a common reverse primer (20pmol/µl) was added in each tube,0.5ul of each GAPDH primer. For the final volume, DNase/RNase-free water was used. The PCR condition was fixed at 94 °C for 5 min; 94 °C for 40 s, 58 °C for 45 s and 72 °C for 40 s which was repeated for 35 times with a final extension step at 72 °C for 10 min. PCR products were verified on 1.2% agarose gel by using the Alpha Innotech Gel documentation system. One more primer HCP5-R2- 5’ATGTGGCTCCATATCCCATT3’ internal to the R1 primer was designed to sequence the PCR product. The sequencing-based method was employed to confirm the HCP5 PCR positive results.

Sequence-based typing (SBT)

The PCR result of direct PCR was confirmed by the SBT method by using both forward and reverse (HLA-B*57F1 and HLA-B*57R1) primers, while the result of HCP5 PCR was also confirmed through sequencing by using the R2 (HCP5 R2) primer on all positive samples, respectively.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using a statistical package for the social sciences (SPSS), Version 23.0. IBM Corp., NY). The statistical analysis was carried out for various categorical parameters using the chi-square test and Fischer’s Exact Test. For comparing two groups of mean or median Student’s t-test and Mann-Whitney U test was performed. A p-value of less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant at a 95% confidence interval.

Discussion

The Infectious Diseases Society of America (IDSA) guidelines for antiretroviral treatment have strongly recommended screening for HLA-B*57:01 before initiating Abacavir therapy, due to its strong association with ABC-HSR [27]. However, even the latest national guidelines from India does not recommend screening for HLA-B*57:01 in the program [1]. The main hurdle for implementation of this testing in the program could be the requirement of specialized facilities for SBT. The high prevalence of HLA-B*57:01 allele in our region makes it even more important to develop a test which is feasible in our setting.

Nested PCR, which gave excellent results in our study population is a reliable and simple method for detecting the HLA-B*57:01 allele. Similar results have been reported in other settings with limited resources [6, 26]. The cost of each PCR reaction is cheap, approximately around Rs 250 and takes approximately 5–6 h to complete. Drawbacks are the need for two PCR steps and the fact that these primers can detect HLA-B*57:06, HLA-B*57:08 and HLA-B*5514 alleles and its positivity may vary according to different populations.

The second option is HCP5 gene PCR. In our population, all HLA-B*57:01 positive individuals were HCP5 SNP positive and vice versa showing a sensitivity and specificity of 100% respectively, similar to the studies conducted by Sanchez-Giron et al. and Zubiaur et al. in Mexican and Spanish population respectively [10, 11]. This finding suggests that HCP5 gene PCR can be used for screening of HLA-B*57:01 in our population. The main advantage of this method lies in its single-step PCR process, low cost (approximately Rs 200 per reaction) and shorter time for testing (approximately 4 h). The only drawback is, there is genetic diversity in the population in India and therefore the linkage disequilibrium of HCP5 with HLA-B*5701 needs to be tested in other regions too before implementation [28].

Both the methods, nested PCR and HCP5 PCR rely on a conventional PCR machine, which is now widely available in laboratories and hospitals post-COVID-19 pandemic. Our findings indicate that nested PCR and HCP5 gene PCR are convenient, rapid, and cost-effective, making them suitable and feasible for detecting the HLA-B*57:01 allele before prescribing Abacavir in the National program and other resource-poor settings. However, there is always a possibility of contamination in PCR. So, implementing effective contamination prevention strategies is essential for the successful application of nested PCR. The main limitation was the sample collection was done from a single centre. The inability to analyse the linkage disequilibrium (LD) analysis between the HLA-B*57:01 allele and HCP5 in our population is also a limitation.

Conclusion

Both nested PCR and HCP5 gene PCR methods are efficacious, simple, cost-effective and feasible method for detecting the HLA-B*57:01 allele in our population. As both these tests may vary with genetic diversity of the population, a multicentric study will be required to prove reproducibility of these methods in other regions.

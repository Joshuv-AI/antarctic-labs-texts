Abstract
. Sarason’s Hilbert space version of Carathéodory-Julia Theorem connects the non-tangential boundary behavior of functions in de Branges-Rovnyak space ℋ ⁡ ( b ) \mathcal{H}(b) with the existence of angular derivatives in the sense of Carathéodory for b b , an analytic self-mapping of the unit disk. In this article, we continue the study of higher order extensions of this result that deal with derivatives of functions in ℋ ⁡ ( b ) \mathcal{H}(b) , and we consider notions of approach regions more general than the non-tangential ones. Our main result generalizes the recent work of Duan-Li-Mashreghi on boundary behavior in model spaces to ℋ ⁡ ( b ) \mathcal{H}(b) -spaces and to higher order derivatives, and we give a new self-contained proof of that result. It also generalizes earlier radial results of Fricain-Mashreghi. In relation to existence of angular derivatives, we show that in the classical Carathéodory-Julia Theorem one cannot replace the non-tangential approach region by any essentially larger region.



1.
Background

1.1.
Angular derivatives in the sense of Carathéodory.

Let
\mathbb{D}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\}
denote the unit disk. We single out the point
1
on its boundary circle
\mathbb{T}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|=1\}
and consider inside
\mathbb{D}
the ordinary non-tangential approach region
\Gamma
with vertex at
1
:

(1.1)

\Gamma=\Gamma(c)=\{z\in\mathbb{D}:1-|z|>c|\arg z|\},\quad c>0,\,\arg z\in(-\pi,\pi]

(parameter
c
being fixed but arbitrary). Our choice of
1\in\mathbb{T}
as our base point is done for notational convenience. It can be replaced by any other point
\zeta_{o}\in\mathbb{T}
. The
angular derivative at the point
1
of an analytic function
f
defined in
\mathbb{D}
is the non-tangential limit

f^{\prime}(1):=\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\to 1\\
z\in\Gamma\end{subarray}}f^{\prime}(z),

if it exists. It is widely known that the existence of the limit does not depend on the parameter
c
used to define
\Gamma
.

A specialized notion of angular derivative exists in the context of analytic self-mappings of
\mathbb{D}
. We say that
b:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
has an angular derivative
in the sense of Carathéodory at the point
1
if
b
has an angular derivative at 1, and additionally
b(1):=\lim_{z\to 1,z\in\Gamma}b(z)
is unimodular, i.e.,
|b(1)|=1
. The famous Carathéodory-Julia Theorem from
[
Car29
]
and
[
Jul19
]
reads as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

(Carathéodory-Julia)

Let
b:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
be analytic. The following two conditions are equivalent.

(i)

The function
b
has an angular derivative in the sense of Carathéodory at the point
1
.

(ii)

There exists a sequence
(z_{n})_{n}
of points in
\mathbb{D}
which converges to
1
and satisfies

(1.2)

\sup_{n}\frac{1-|b(z_{n})|^{2}}{1-|z_{n}|^{2}}<\infty.

It is known that (
1.2
) is in fact equivalent to a similar condition

\sup_{z\in\Gamma}\frac{1-|b(z)|^{2}}{1-|z|}<\infty.

The implication
(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
is immediately seen to hold by setting
z=r\in\mathbb{R}\cap\mathbb{D}
and noting that
1-|b(r)|
is dominated by
|b(1)-b(r)|
, a quantity which is
\mathcal{O}(1-r)
under
(i)
.
An insightful proof of implication
(ii)\Rightarrow(i)
has been given by Sarason in
[
Sar88
]
, who identified the quantity in
(ii)
as the norm of the functional of evaluation in a certain Hilbert space of analytic functions, and in effect connected the geometric notion of angular derivatives with operator theory. The de Branges-Rovnyak space
\mathcal{H}(b)
is the Hilbert space of analytic functions on
\mathbb{D}
characterized by the property that it contains the
kernel functions

(1.3)

k^{b}_{z}(w):=\frac{1-\overline{b(z)}b(w)}{1-\overline{z}w},\quad z,w\in\mathbb{D}

and its inner product
\big\langle\cdot,\cdot\big\rangle_{b}
satisfies

(1.4)

f(z)=\big\langle f,k^{b}_{z}\big\rangle_{b},\quad f\in\mathcal{H}(b).

Good references for background on
\mathcal{H}(b)
-spaces is Sarason’s book
[
Sar94
]
and the more recent extensive two-volume monograph by Fricain and Mashreghi
[
FM16a
]
,
[
FM16b
]
.

It follows from the reproducing property in (
1.4
) that

(1.5)

\|k^{b}_{z}\|_{b}^{2}=\big\langle k^{b}_{z},k^{b}_{z}\big\rangle_{b}=\frac{1-|b(z)|^{2}}{1-|z|^{2}}.

Under condition
(ii)
of ?THM?
, the
boundary kernel function

(1.6)

k^{b}_{1}(w):=\frac{1-\overline{b(1)}b(w)}{1-w},\quad w\in\mathbb{D}

can be identified as a weak limit of a subsequence of the
(k^{b}_{z_{n}})_{n}
, and is thus a member of
\mathcal{H}(b)
. Naturally, it should have the evaluation property that
\big\langle f,k^{b}_{1}\big\rangle_{b}=f(1)
. Indeed this is so, if
f(1)
is duly interpreted. The following result appears in
[
Sar88
]
and is reproduced in Sarason’s book
[
Sar94
]
.

Theorem 1.2
.

(Sarason’s extension)

The following condition is equivalent to the two conditions in the Carathéodory-Julia Theorem.

(iii)

We have norm convergence of
k^{b}_{z}\to k^{b}_{1}
in
\mathcal{H}(b)
as
z\to 1
in
\Gamma
. In particular, for every
f\in\mathcal{H}(b)
, the non-tangential limit

f(1):=\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\to 1\\
z\in\Gamma\end{subarray}}f(z)

exists and satisfies
\big\langle f,k^{b}_{1}\big\rangle_{b}=f(1)
.

1.2.
Other equivalent conditions and higher order analogs.

Consider the Nevanlinna factorization of
b
as

(1.7)

b(z)=B(z)S_{\nu}(z)b_{o}(z),

where
B
is a Blaschke product with zero sequence
(a_{n})_{n}
,

B(z)=\prod_{n}\frac{|a_{n}|}{a_{n}}\frac{a_{n}-z}{1-\overline{a_{n}}z},

S_{\nu}
is the singular inner factor

S_{\nu}(z)=\exp\Big(-\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{\zeta+z}{\zeta-z}d\nu(\zeta)\Big),

and
b_{o}
is the outer factor

b_{o}(z)=\exp\Big(\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{\zeta+z}{\zeta-z}\log|b(\zeta)|\,d\textit{m}(\zeta)\Big).

Here
d\textit{m}
is the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{T}
, normalized by the condition
m(\mathbb{T})=1
. Existence of the angular derivative has been characterized in terms of the quantities appearing in this factorization. We proceed to present these results in the more general context of higher order Carathéodory-Julia theory, which arises in the investigation of conditions for existence of boundary values for higher order derivatives of
b
and functions in
\mathcal{H}(b)
.

Let
\partial_{z}
and
\overline{\partial_{z}}
denote the usual complex differentiation operators with respect to the complex variable
z
. For every
z\in\mathbb{D}
, the limit

\overline{\partial_{z}}k^{b}_{z}=\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{k^{b}_{z+h}-k^{b}_{z}}{\overline{h}}

exists in the norm of
\mathcal{H}(b)
. Similarly, the higher order kernels
\overline{\partial_{z}}^{m}k^{b}_{z}
are also members of
\mathcal{H}(b)
, and it is not hard to see that

(1.8)

\big\langle f,\overline{\partial_{z}}^{m}k^{b}_{z}\big\rangle_{b}=f^{(m)}(z).

According to (
1.5
), we have the useful formulas

(1.9)

\|\overline{\partial_{z}}^{m}k^{b}_{z}\|_{b}^{2}=\big\langle\overline{\partial_{z}}^{m}k_{z}^{b},\overline{\partial_{z}}^{m}k_{z}^{b}\big\rangle_{b}=\partial_{z}^{m}\overline{\partial_{z}}^{m}\frac{1-|b(z)|^{2}}{1-|z|^{2}}.

If the quantity in (
1.9
) is uniformly bounded over a sequence of points
(z_{n})_{n}
tending to
1
, then we may as before define the
boundary derivative kernel

\overline{\partial_{z}}^{m}k^{b}_{1}
as a weak (and pointwise in
\mathbb{D}
) limit of a subsequence
(\overline{\partial_{z}^{m}}k^{b}_{z_{n}})_{n}
. Throughout the article,
\overline{\partial_{z}}^{m}k^{b}_{1}
is to be interpreted according to that weak limit definition. We will not need any more precise pointwise formula for this function (but see
[
FM08b
]
).

The following result of Fricain and Mashreghi has been obtained in
[
FM08a
]
and
[
FM08b
]
and characterizes the existence of radial limits for derivatives of functions in
\mathcal{H}(b)
in terms of the quantities appearing in (
1.7
).

Theorem 1.3
.

(Fricain-Mashreghi, higher order radial characterization)

For every non-negative integer
m
, the following conditions are equivalent.

(i)

As
r\to 1
, we have the norm convergence
\overline{\partial_{z}}^{m}k^{b}_{r}\to\overline{\partial_{z}}^{m}k^{b}_{1}
in
\mathcal{H}(b)
.

(ii)

For every
f\in\mathcal{H}(b)
, the radial limit

f^{(m)}(1):=\lim_{r\to 1}f^{(m)}(r)

exists.

(iii)

We have

\sum_{n}\frac{1-|a_{n}|^{2}}{|1-a_{n}|^{2m+2}}+\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{d\nu(\zeta)}{|1-\zeta|^{2m+2}}+\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{-\log|b(\zeta)|}{|1-\zeta|^{2m+2}}\,d\textit{m}(\zeta)<\infty.

Earlier results in this direction which pertain to inner
b
are due to Ahern and Clark and can be found in
[
AC70b
]
. It may be worth mentioning that, with some work, one can replace the radial convergence in
(i)
and
(ii)
by non-tangential convergence. There exist results in the spirit of ?THM?
with part
(iii)
replaced by similar conditions on the Aleksandrov-Clark measure corresponding to
b
(see the discussion in
[
Sar94
, Chapter VII]
). We note also the automatic strengthening from weak convergence
\overline{\partial_{z}}^{m}k^{b}_{r}\to\overline{\partial_{z}}^{m}k^{b}_{1}
implicit in
(ii)
to the norm convergence in
(i)
.

We wish to mention also the work of Bolotnikov-Kheifets, in which a more direct higher order generalization of the Carathéodory-Julia Theorem has been obtained. In their result
[
BK06
, Theorem 1.2]
, the existence of non-tangential limit for a higher order derivative of
b
and positivity of a certain matrix is shown to be equivalent to the boundedness of the supremum of the expressions in (
1.9
) over a non-tangential approach region. Recently, an interesting generalization of the Carathéodory-Julia Theorem to the setting of arbitrary reproducing kernels and arbitrary domains
X
instead of
\mathbb{D}
appeared in the work of Dahlin in
[
Dah24
]
.

1.3.
More general approach regions. Work of Duan-Li-Mashreghi.

A different generalization of the Carathéodory-Julia theory arises when replacing the non-tangential approach region
\Gamma
with a more general one. In our article, an
approach region at
1
will be an open simply connected domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{D}
which satisfies the boundary intersection property
\partial\Omega\cap\mathbb{T}=\{1\}
. Being interested in regions at least as large as the non-tangential region, we shall for convenience also assume that
\Omega
is star-shaped with respect to
1
in a neighbourhood of that point, by what we mean that for all
z\in\Omega
sufficiently close to
1
, the line segment between
1
and
z
is contained in
\Omega
. This requirement will exclude some pathological examples which are outside of the scope of our investigation.

An approach region can be associated to an increasing and continuous function
\rho
that is defined for non-negative
x
, and satisfies
\rho(0)=0
,
\rho(x)>0
for
x>0
. Consider a domain of the form

(1.10)

\Omega_{\rho}=\{z\in\mathbb{D}:1-|z|>\rho(|\arg z|)\}.

Then
\rho(x)=cx
corresponds to the non-tangential regions
\Gamma(c)
, while
\rho(x)=cx^{2}
corresponds to the usual oricyclic approach regions, which in a neighbourhood of the point
1
resemble a disk inside
\mathbb{D}
which touches
\mathbb{T}
at
1
.

It is natural to ask if analogs of the earlier stated theorems exist for more general approach regions. Specifically, one may try to obtain conditions on
b
so that the boundary limit

(1.11)

f^{(m)}(1):=\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\to 1\\
z\in\Omega\end{subarray}}f^{(m)}(z)

exists for all
f\in\mathcal{H}(b)
. The limit must, of course, coincide with the ordinary non-tangential one, if both exist, so our above notation is consistent. This particular question was investigated in the work of Leung in
[
Leu78
]
, in which various results are obtained in the case that
b
is an inner function and the approach regions are of the form (
1.10
) with
\rho(x)=cx^{\gamma}
for some
\gamma\geq 1
. A related study was carried out by Protas in
[
Pro72
]
. The first-order results (i.e,
m=0
) in Leung’s work have been recently generalized by Duan-Li-Mashreghi in
[
DLM25
]
from the regions
\Omega_{\rho}
considered by Leung to general approach regions. It requires a little bit of preparation to state the main theorem in
[
DLM25
]
. For
z\in\mathbb{D}
close to
1
and with positive imaginary part (
\arg z\in(0,\pi)
, say) consider the arc

(1.12)

E_{z}:=\{e^{it}\in\mathbb{T}:\arg z/2<t<2\arg z\}.

For
z
with negative imaginary part, let
E_{z}
be the reflection in the real axis of the arc
E_{\overline{z}}
, and set
E_{z}
to be empty for
z=r\in(0,1)
. Define also the circle sector

(1.13)

S_{z}=\{re^{it}\in\mathbb{D}:e^{it}\in E_{z}\}.

The Duan-Li-Mashreghi result reads as follows (see
[
DLM25
, Theorem 2.1]
for a slightly extended formulation).

Theorem 1.4
.

(Duan-Li-Mashreghi)
Let
b=BS_{\nu}
be an inner function with zero set
(a_{n})_{n}
, and let
\Omega
be an approach region at
1
. Then the following conditions are equivalent.

(i)

\sup_{z\in\Omega}\|k^{b}_{z}\|_{b}<\infty.

(ii)

For every
f\in\mathcal{H}(b)
,
f(z)
has a limit as
z\to 1
in
\Omega
.

(iii)

We have

(1.14)

\sum_{n}\frac{1-|a_{n}|^{2}}{|1-a_{n}|^{2}}+\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{d\nu(\zeta)}{|1-\zeta|^{2}}<\infty

and

(1.15)

\sup_{z\in\Omega}\sum_{n:a_{n}\in S_{z}}\frac{1-|a_{n}|^{2}}{|1-\overline{a_{n}}z|^{2}}+\int_{E_{z}}\frac{d\nu(\zeta)}{|1-\overline{\zeta}z|^{2}}<\infty.

Condition
(iii)
above is stated for our convenience in a slightly different form than the original condition in the statement of
[
DLM25
, Theorem 2.1]
(see the proof given in the reference for details). Condition (
1.14
) appears already in the context of non-tangential approach regions in
[
AC70b
]
, and condition (
1.15
) is what has to be added to compensate in the case of an approach region larger than the non-tangential one. The condition
(iii)
above is easily seen to be equivalent to

(iii’)

\sup_{z\in\Omega}\sum_{n}\frac{1-|a_{n}|^{2}}{|1-\overline{a_{n}}z|^{2}}+\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{d\nu(\zeta)}{|1-\overline{\zeta}z|^{2}}<\infty.

Indeed, this condition is clearly stronger than (
1.15
), and it implies (
1.14
) by Fatou’s lemma. In the other direction, we note that the sets
E_{z}
and
S_{z}
satisfy the following readily established estimate (see
[
DLM25
, Lemma 3.4]
) from which the mentioned equivalence readily follows: given two points
w\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}:=\mathbb{T}\cup\mathbb{D}
and
z\in\mathbb{D}
, if
w\not\in S_{z}\cup E_{z}
, then

(1.16)

\frac{1}{|1-\overline{w}z|}\leq\frac{4}{|1-w|}.

Upside of
(iii)
over
(iii^{\prime})
is that (
1.15
) isolates the information needed to conclude the norm convergence of
k^{b}_{z}\to k^{b}_{1}
as
z\to 1
in
\Omega
, which is not automatic in the context of more general approach regions considered here. Indeed, according to
[
DLM25
, Theorem 2.2]
, under the equivalent conditions of the above theorem, this norm convergence occurs if and only if the quantity in (
1.15
) tends to
0
as
z\to 1
:

(1.17)

\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\to 1\\
z\in\Omega\end{subarray}}\sum_{n:a_{n}\in S_{z}}\frac{1-|a_{n}|^{2}}{|1-\overline{z}a_{n}|^{2}}+\int_{E_{z}}\frac{d\nu(\zeta)}{|1-\overline{z}\zeta|^{2}}=0.

In the non-tangential case
\Omega=\Gamma
, it can be proved that (
1.14
) implies (
1.17
) (and a fortiori (
1.15
)). It follows that norm and weak convergence
k^{b}_{z}\to k^{b}_{1}
as
z\to 1
in
\Gamma
are equivalent.

2.
Main results

2.1.
Boundary limits for general approach region and symbol.

The first of our main results is an extension of ?THM?
by Duan-Li-Mashreghi to symbols with a non-trivial outer part, and to higher order.

Theorem A
.

Let
b:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
be an analytic function with zero set
(a_{n})_{n}
and singular inner factor
S_{\nu}
. Let
\Omega
be an approach region at
1
. For any integer
m\geq 0
, the following conditions are equivalent.

(i)

\sup_{z\in\Omega}\|\overline{\partial_{z}}^{m}k^{b}_{z}\|_{b}<\infty.

(ii)

For every
f\in\mathcal{H}(b)
,
f^{(m)}(z)
has a limit as
z\to 1
in
\Omega
.

(iii)

\sup_{z\in\Omega}\sum_{n}\frac{1-|a_{n}|^{2}}{|1-\overline{a_{n}}z|^{2m+2}}+\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{d\nu(\zeta)}{|1-\overline{\zeta}z|^{2m+2}}+\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{-\log|b(\zeta)|}{|1-\overline{\zeta}z|^{2m+2}}d\textit{m}(\zeta)<\infty.

As before, the condition in
(iii)
can be rewritten as conditions analogous to (
1.14
) and (
1.15
), namely

\sum_{n}\frac{1-|a_{n}|^{2}}{|1-a_{n}|^{2m+2}}+\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{d\nu(\zeta)}{|1-\zeta|^{2m+2}}+\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{-\log|b(\zeta)|}{|1-\zeta|^{2m+2}}d\textit{m}(\zeta)<\infty

and

\sup_{z\in\Omega}\sum_{n:a_{n}\in S_{z}}\frac{1-|a_{n}|^{2}}{|1-\overline{a_{n}}z|^{2m+2}}+\int_{E_{z}}\frac{d\nu(\zeta)}{|1-\overline{\zeta}z|^{2m+2}}+\int_{E_{z}}\frac{-\log|b(\zeta)|}{|1-\overline{\zeta}z|^{2m+2}}d\textit{m}(\zeta)<\infty.

Our proof of the theorem does not use the technique of Duan-Li-Mashreghi of expressing the kernel norms according to certain formulas of Ahern and Clark from
[
AC70a
]
(see Section 3 in
[
DLM25
]
), and can therefore be seen as more elementary. In fact, such a formula for the kernel norm may not exist for the outer function
b_{o}
. Our approach to overcome this difficulty is described at the beginning of Section
3
.

2.2.
Norm convergence of kernels.

We extend also the above mentioned kernel norm convergence result of Duan-Li-Mashreghi, characterized in the case
m=0
as equivalent to (
1.17
).

Theorem B
.

Assume that the equivalent conditions in ?THM?
hold for some
m
. Then the following two conditions are equivalent.

(i)

We have the norm convergence
\overline{\partial_{z}}^{m}k^{b}_{z}\to\overline{\partial_{z}}^{m}k^{b}_{1}
in
\mathcal{H}(b)
as
z\to 1
in
\Omega
.

(ii)

\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\to 1\\
z\in\Omega\end{subarray}}\sum_{n:a_{n}\in S_{z}}\frac{1-|a_{n}|^{2}}{|1-\overline{a_{n}}z|^{2m+2}}+\int_{E_{z}}\frac{d\nu(\zeta)}{|1-\overline{\zeta}z|^{2m+2}}+\int_{E_{z}}\frac{-\log|b(\zeta)|}{|1-\overline{\zeta}z|^{2m+2}}d\textit{m}(\zeta)=0.

Again, in the non-tangential case
\Omega=\Gamma
, the condition in
(ii)
of ?THM?
is automatically satisfied if the equivalent conditions of ?THM?
are satisfied. Thus we have another proof of the fact that weak and strong convergence
\overline{\partial_{z}}^{m}k^{b}_{z}\to\overline{\partial_{z}}^{m}k^{b}_{1}
as
z\to 1
in
\Gamma
are equivalent.

The following reformulation of the result may shed more light on the matter. Introduce the non-negative finite Borel measure
M
on
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
given by

dM=\sum_{n}(1-|a_{n}|^{2})d\delta_{a_{n}}+d\nu-\log|b|d\textit{m},

where
\delta_{a_{n}}
is a unit mass at the point
a_{n}\in\mathbb{D}
. Then the condition
(iii)
in ?THM?
simply means that the functions

(2.1)

w\mapsto\frac{1}{|1-\overline{w}z|^{2m+2}},\quad z\in\Omega

form a bounded subset of the Lebesgue space
L^{1}(M)
. Although we shall not go into the details of a proof, we wish to mention that one can show that the condition
(ii)
of ?THM?
is equivalent to the
uniform integrability
of the family (
2.1
) with respect to the measure
M
, which by definition means that to any
\epsilon>0
there corresponds a
\delta>0
such that if
E\subset\overline{\mathbb{D}}
is a Borel set satisfying
M(E)<\delta
, then

\sup_{z\in\Omega}\int_{E}\frac{1}{|1-\overline{w}z|^{2m+2}}dM(w)<\epsilon.

Due to the pointwise convergence

\frac{1}{|1-\overline{w}z|^{2m+2}}\to\ \frac{1}{|1-\overline{w}|^{2m+2}}

as
z\to 1
, by basic measure theory this condition is further equivalent to the compactness of the family of functions (
2.1
) in
L^{1}(M)
. In this way we connect the properties of families of kernel functions
\overline{\partial_{z}}^{m}k^{b}_{z}
in
\mathcal{H}(b)
with the properties of families of functions (
2.1
) in
L^{1}(M)
.

2.3.
Carathéodory-Julia Theorem for general approach regions: failure and a positive result.

Given that Sarason’s extension of Carathéodory-Julia Theorem has an analog in the context of more general approach regions, one may wonder if the classical theorem also generalizes in a similar way.
Our main result in this direction shows that a natural restatement of the original theorem of Carathéodory-Julia is not valid in the broader context. To wit, consider the domains
\Omega_{\rho}
in (
1.10
). Intuitively speaking, a domain of this form will be strictly larger than a non-tangential region in the vicinity of the point
1
if we have
\lim_{x\to 0}\rho^{\prime}(x)=0
. An easy exercise shows that if
\rho
is convex, then this last condition is actually equivalent to
\Omega_{\rho}
not being contained in any non-tangential region
\Gamma(c)
for any
c
(recall (
1.1
)). The following theorem shows the failure of a direct generalization of the classical result to general approach regions.

Theorem C
.

Assume that
\rho
is continuously differentiable and increasing on
(0,\infty)
,
\rho(x)>0
for
x>0
, and

\lim_{x\to 0}\rho^{\prime}(x)=0.

Then there exists
b:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
such that

\sup_{z\in\Omega_{\rho}}\|k^{b}_{z}\|_{b}<\infty

but

\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\to 1\\
z\in\Omega_{\rho}\end{subarray}}b^{\prime}(z)

does not exist.

However, as a consequence of ?THM?
we can prove the following partial result.

Theorem D
.

Let
\Omega
be an approach region at
1
. If we have norm convergence
k^{b}_{z}\to k^{b}_{1}
in
\mathcal{H}(b)
as
z\to 1
in
\Omega
, then the limits

b^{\prime}(1)=\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\to 1\\
z\in\Omega\end{subarray}}b^{\prime}(z),\quad b(1)=\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\to 1\\
z\in\Omega\end{subarray}}b(z)

exist, and
|b(1)|=1
.

We do not know if the converse of the result holds for any approach region

essentially larger than the non-tangential ones, and we have not produced any counter-example. We therefore leave the following question unanswered.

Question
.

For a general approach region
\Omega

z\in\Omega\end{subarray}}b^{\prime}(z),\quad b(1)=\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\to 1\\
z\in\Omega\end{subarray}}b(z),\quad|b(1)|=1,

in terms of some type of behavior of the kernel functions

,

Proof of Theorem A

It will be convenient to divide the proof into the two special cases of

The proof of the equivalence of conditions

and

in ?THM?
is the most involved part, but it will be established in a natural way. In the case that

as a sum of various terms involving derivatives of

or its factors, and a single positive quantity of magnitude proportional to the terms appearing in

of ?THM?

3.1.
Blaschke product case.

For the remainder of this section, let
b
be the Blaschke product corresponding to the zero sequence
(a_{n})_{n\geq 1}
. The condition
(iii)
in ?THM?
is then

(3.1)

\sup_{z\in\Omega}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{1-|a_{j}|^{2}}{|1-\overline{a_{j}}z|^{2m+2}}<\infty.

Let
b_{j}(z)
be the finite Blaschke product product corresponding to
(a_{n})_{n=1}^{j}
(
b_{0}\equiv 1)
.
For a product
b=B_{1}B_{2}
, we have the readily-established decomposition formula

(3.2)

\|k^{b}_{z}\|_{b}^{2}=\|k^{B_{1}}_{z}\|_{B_{1}}^{2}+|B_{1}(z)|^{2}\|k^{B_{2}}_{z}\|_{B_{2}}^{2}.

Recall also the well-known identity

(3.3)

1-\frac{|z-a|^{2}}{|1-\overline{a}z|^{2}}=\frac{(1-|z|^{2})(1-|a|^{2})}{|1-\overline{a}z|^{2}},\quad a,z\in\mathbb{D}.

In the case of a Blaschke factor

B_{1}(z)=\frac{|a|}{a}\frac{a-z}{1-\overline{a}z},\quad a,z\in\mathbb{D},

we note that (
3.3
) implies

\|k^{B_{1}}_{z}\|_{B_{1}}^{2}=\frac{1-|a|^{2}}{|1-\overline{a}z|^{2}}.

Now, an iteration of the above decomposition formula leads to

(3.4)

\|k^{b}_{z}\|_{b}^{2}=\sum_{j\geq 1}|b_{j-1}(z)|^{2}\frac{1-|a_{j}|^{2}}{|1-\overline{a_{j}}z|^{2}}.

As a consequence of the Blaschke condition on
(a_{n})_{n}
, it is not hard to see that the derivatives of
\|k^{b}_{z}\|^{2}_{b}
can be computed by term-wise differentiation of the series in (
3.4
). An application of the Leibniz rule and an exchange of the order of summations shows that

(3.5)

\partial^{m}_{z}\overline{\partial_{z}}^{m}\|k^{b}_{z}\|_{b}^{2}=\sum_{k,l=0}^{m}C_{k,l}(z)

where

(3.6)

C_{k,l}(z)=\sum_{j\geq 1}{m\choose k}{m\choose l}b^{(m-k)}_{j-1}(z)\overline{b^{(m-l)}_{j-1}(z)}\overline{a_{j}}^{k}a_{j}^{l}k!l!\frac{1-|a_{j}|^{2}}{(1-\overline{a_{j}}z)^{1+k}(1-a_{j}\overline{z})^{1+l}}

According to the next lemma, the coefficients of the terms
\frac{1-|a_{j}|^{2}}{(1-\overline{a_{j}}z)^{1+k}(1-a_{j}\overline{z})^{1+l}}
in the expression for
C_{k,l}(z)
are well-behaved.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
b
and
b_{j}
be Blaschke products as above.

•

If
(i)
in ?THM?
holds, then the derivatives up to order
m
of

are uniformly bounded in
\Omega
, and
\lim_{z\to 1,z\in\Omega}|b(z)|=1
.

•

If
(iii)
in ?THM?
holds, then the derivatives up to order
2m+1
of
b_{j}
are uniformly bounded in
\Omega
, and
\lim_{z\to 1,z\in\Omega}|b(z)|=1
. Moreover, the limits

\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\to 1\\
z\in\Omega\end{subarray}}b^{(k)}(z),\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\to 1\\
z\in\Omega\end{subarray}}b_{j}^{(k)}(z),\quad j\geq 0,k=0,1,\ldots,2m

exist.

Remark 3.2
.

Note the asymmetry in the above statement. Since the two conditions
(i)
and
(iii)
in ?THM?
will ultimately be proven to be equivalent, the conclusion in the first part of the lemma can be upgraded to the stronger conclusion of the second part. For the time being, however, the above statement both suffices and is more convenient.

We will prove the lemma a bit later. Assuming it for now, we see from Cauchy-Schwarz inequality applied to (

Hence (

Let us now prove the other implication

, for

The idea is to show that the term

We may without loss of generality assume that

2^{-2m-2}(m!)^{2}\sum_{j\geq 1}\frac{1-|a_{j}|^{2}}{|1-\overline{a_{j}}z|^{2m+2}}\leq C^{\prime}+D^{\prime}\sqrt{\sum_{j\geq 1}\frac{1-|a_{j}|^{2}}{|1-\overline{a_{j}}z|^{2m+2}}}+\partial^{m}_{z}\overline{\partial_{z}}^{m}\|k^{b}_{z}\|_{b}^{2}

of ?THM?

It remains to show that condition

3.2.
Zero-free case.

Let us now consider the case of a zero-free symbol

becomes

\sup_{z\in\Omega}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{d\mu(\zeta)}{|1-\overline{\zeta}z|^{2m+2}}<\infty.

Since

\frac{-\log|b(z)|^{2}}{2(1-|z|^{2})}=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{d\mu(\zeta)}{|1-\overline{\zeta}z|^{2}}.

Since

\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{d\mu(\zeta)}{|1-\overline{\zeta}z|^{2}}=S(|b(z)|^{2})\|k^{b}_{z}\|_{b}^{2}

Clearly

is a smooth function on

is bounded from below by

\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{(m!)^{2}d\mu(\zeta)}{|1-\overline{\zeta}z|^{2m+2}}=\sum_{i,j=0}^{m}P_{i,j}(z)\cdot\partial^{i}_{z}\overline{\partial_{z}}^{j}\|k^{b}_{z}\|_{b}^{2}

.

We will need a variant of ?THM?
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
b
be zero-free in

.

•

If

in ?THM?
holds, then the derivatives up to order

•

|\partial^{i}_{z}\overline{\partial_{z}}^{j}\|k^{b}_{z}\|_{b}^{2}|=|\big\langle\overline{\partial_{z}}^{i}k^{b}_{z},\overline{\partial_{z}}^{j}k^{b}_{z}\big\rangle_{b}|\leq\|\overline{\partial_{z}}^{i}k^{b}_{z}\|_{b}\cdot\|\overline{\partial_{z}}^{j}k^{b}_{z}\|_{b}.

3.3.
Combining the special cases into the general result.

In the last two sections we have proved ?THM?
in the two cases that

is a Blaschke product or

is zero-free in

. Let us now show that these two cases imply the result for the general symbol

.

Assume that
(i)
in ?THM?
holds for

, where

is a Blaschke product and
B_{2}
is zero-free in

. It is well known that we have the contractive containments

in

,

3.4.
Proofs of the auxiliary lemmas.

Proof of ?THM?
.

We start by assuming the condition
(i)
in ?THM?
. We use induction on

to prove that the

are uniformly bounded in
\Omega
, with the base case
m=0
being trivial. Let

and assume that
\sup_{z\in\Omega}\|\overline{\partial_{z}}^{m}k^{b}_{z}\|_{b}<\infty
. As before, we conclude that
\sup_{z\in\Omega}\|\overline{\partial_{z}}^{i}k^{b}_{z}\|_{b}<\infty
for
i\leq m
, and by the induction hypothesis derivatives of
b_{j}
up to order

are uniformly bounded in
\Omega
. Fix

for which

. The term
\frac{1}{1-\overline{\lambda}z}

, hence in

. Since

\|k^{b_{j}}_{\lambda}\|_{b}^{2}=\|k^{b_{j}}_{\lambda}\|_{b_{j}}^{2}=\frac{1-|b_{j}(\lambda)|^{2}}{1-|\lambda|^{2}}\leq\frac{1-|b(\lambda)|^{2}}{1-|\lambda|^{2}}\leq\|k^{b}_{\lambda}\|_{b}^{2}

The rightmost quantity is uniformly bounded for
z\in\Omega
. Leibniz rule applied to the expression for

in (

) shows that

\partial^{m}_{z}k^{b_{j}}_{\lambda}(z)=\frac{-\overline{b_{j}(\lambda)}b_{j}^{(m)}(z)}{1-\overline{\lambda}z}+r_{j}(z)

where

is a linear combination of products of derivatives of

of order strictly less than

and powers of
(1-\overline{\lambda}z)^{-1}
. By induction we have that
r_{j}
is uniformly bounded in
\Omega
. Since
|b_{j}(\lambda)|\geq|b(\lambda)|>0
, it follows from (
3.21
) that

is also uniformly bounded in
\Omega
. The fact that condition
(i)
in ?THM?
implies

follows at once from

.

Let us now prove the second point, so instead assume
(iii)
in ?THM?
. Using (

\sup_{n}\,1-\frac{|z-a_{n}|^{2}}{|1-\overline{a_{n}}z|^{2}}=\sup_{n}\frac{(1-|z|^{2})(1-|a_{n}|^{2})}{|1-\overline{a_{n}}z|^{2}}\leq\sum_{j}\frac{(1-|z|^{2})(1-|a_{j}|^{2})}{|1-\overline{a_{j}}z|^{2}}=\mathcal{O}(1-|z|^{2}).

We conclude that there exists

Differentiating the expression

\frac{b_{j}^{\prime}(z)}{b_{j}(z)}=\partial_{z}\log|b_{j}(z)|^{2}=\sum_{n=1}^{j}\frac{1-|a_{n}|^{2}}{(z-a_{n})(1-\overline{a_{n}}z)}.

|b_{j}^{\prime}(z)|\leq 2\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{1-|a_{n}|^{2}}{|1-\overline{a_{n}}z|^{2}},z\in\Omega,\quad|z|>r.

For

in

To prove the cases

and differentiate

in

is bounded in

for

. This means that

and

Hence

in
\Omega

∎

Proof of ?THM?
.

The proof of the first point is completely analogous to the corresponding part of the proof of ?THM?
, with

. To prove the second point, we use (

Multiplying through by

of ?THM?
) implies boundedness of

. As in the proof of ?THM?

. Finally, we have

∎

We state the following result which follows from Lemmas

.

Assume that the equivalent conditions in ?THM?

4.
Proof of Theorem B

In this section we prove ?THM?

4.1.
Some initial observations.

The following is our main simplifying observation.

Lemma 4.1
.

Assume that

for some

. To conclude the norm convergence
\overline{\partial_{z}}^{m}k^{b}_{z}\to\overline{\partial_{z}}^{m}k^{b}_{1}
as

in
\Omega
, it suffices to show that

(4.1)

\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\to 1\\
z\in\Omega\end{subarray}}\|\overline{\partial_{z}}^{m}k^{b}_{z}\|_{b}

exists.

For convenience we will use in the following proof the Fricain-Mashreghi result presented in the introduction (?THM?
), although this is not, strictly speaking, necessary.

Proof of ?THM?
.

Suppose that the limit in (
4.1
) exists. Note that part
(iii)
in ?THM?
is satisfied as a consequence of Fatou’s lemma and our assumption of
(iii)
of ?THM?
being satisfied. Hence all three conditions in ?THM?
hold. By part

of ?THM?

Hence the limit in (

\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\to 1\\

\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\to 1\\

∎

The following two lemmas will also be useful.

Lemma 4.2
.

If we have norm convergence
\overline{\partial_{z}}^{m}k^{b}_{z}\to\overline{\partial_{z}}^{m}k^{b}_{1}
as

\Omega

\overline{\partial_{z}}^{j}k^{b}_{z}\to\overline{\partial_{z}}^{j}k^{b}_{1}

Proof.

Recall that we assume that if

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\to 1\\

Thus norm convergence
\overline{\partial_{z}}^{j}k^{b}_{z}\to\overline{\partial_{z}}^{j}k^{b}_{1}
as

in

is implied by mere boundedness of the norms

for

∎

Recall the definition of the intervals

in (

) and the sectors

in (

Lemma 4.3
.

Assume that the equivalent conditions of ?THM?
are satisfied for some integer

. Then

(4.2)

\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\to 1,z\in\Omega\end{subarray}}\sum_{n}\frac{1-|a_{n}|^{2}}{|1-\overline{a_{n}}z|^{2m+2}}+\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{d\nu(\zeta)}{|1-\overline{\zeta}z|^{2m+2}}+\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{-\log|b(\zeta)|}{|1-\overline{\zeta}z|^{2m+2}}d\textit{m}(\zeta)

exists if and only if

(4.3)

\lim_{z\to 1,z\in\Omega}\sum_{n:a_{n}\in S_{z}}\frac{1-|a_{n}|^{2}}{|1-\overline{a_{n}}z|^{2m+2}}+\int_{E_{z}}\frac{d\nu(\zeta)}{|1-\overline{\zeta}z|^{2m+2}}+\int_{E_{z}}\frac{-\log|b(\zeta)|}{|1-\overline{\zeta}z|^{2m+2}}d\textit{m}(\zeta)=0.

Proof.

By condition
(iii)
in ?THM?
and Fatou’s lemma, we obtain

J:=\sum_{n}\frac{1-|a_{n}|^{2}}{|1-a_{n}|^{2m+2}}+\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{d\nu(\zeta)}{|1-\zeta|^{2m+2}}+\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{-\log|b(\zeta)|}{|1-\zeta|^{2m+2}}d\textit{m}(\zeta)<\infty.

According to (

), for any

we have the estimate

\frac{1}{|1-\overline{w}z|}\leq\frac{4}{|1-w|},\quad w\in\overline{\mathbb{D}},w\not\in E_{z}\cup S_{z}.

Dominated Convergence Theorem thus implies

(4.4)

\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\to 1,z\in\Omega\end{subarray}}\sum_{n:a_{n}\not\in S_{z}}\frac{1-|a_{n}|^{2}}{|1-\overline{a_{n}}z|^{2m+2}}+\int_{\mathbb{T}\setminus E_{z}}\frac{d\nu(\zeta)}{|1-\overline{\zeta}z|^{2m+2}}+\int_{\mathbb{T}\setminus E_{z}}\frac{-\log|b(\zeta)|}{|1-\overline{\zeta}z|^{2m+2}}d\textit{m}(\zeta)=J.

For

, we have by definition that

, and so if the limit in (
4.2
) exists, it follows from (

) that it must equal

. Then the difference of the quantities in (

) and (

) must tend to

as

in
\Omega
. This is precisely condition (

).
Conversely, assuming (

), it follows immediately from (
4.4
) that (

).
∎

As in the previous section, we will proceed by dividing the rest of the proof into the two cases: Blaschke products, and zero-free functions.

4.2.
Blaschke product case.

Consider the space
\ell^{1}
of summable unilateral sequences
x=\big(x(n)\big)_{n}
indexed by natural numbers
n\geq 1
and denote by
\|x\|_{1}:=\sum_{n}|x(n)|
the usual norm in
\ell^{1}
. It will be useful to introduce the sequences
A_{k,l,z}
given by

A_{k,l,z}(n)=\frac{1-|a_{n}|^{2}}{(1-\overline{a_{n}}z)^{1+k}(1-a_{n}\overline{z})^{1+l}},\quad n\geq 1.

Here
a_{n}
are the zeros of the Blaschke product
b
. By the Blaschke condition
\sum_{n}1-|a_{n}|<\infty
, the sequences
A_{k,l,z}
are members of
\ell^{1}
. The condition
(iii)
of ?THM?
on
b
reduces to (
3.1
) and it implies that

\sup_{z\in\Omega,1\leq k,l\leq m}\|A_{k,l,z}\|_{1}<\infty

and

A_{k,l,1}\in\ell^{1},\quad 1\leq k,l\leq m.

Moreover, we have the following norm convergence result.

Lemma 4.4
.

If the equivalent conditions of ?THM?
are satisfied for the Blaschke product
b
and integer
m
, then for
k+l<2m
we have

\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\to 1\\
z\in\Omega\end{subarray}}\|A_{k,l,z}-A_{k,l,1}\|_{1}=0.

Proof.

Because for every
n
it holds that
A_{k,l,z}(n)\to A_{k,l,1}(n)
as
z\to 1
, it is a standard result in measure theory that the desired norm convergence will follow from

\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\to 1\\
z\in\Omega\end{subarray}}\|A_{k,l,z}\|_{1}=\|A_{k,l,1}\|_{1}.

Now, this convergence is established by an argument completely analogous to the one used in the proof of ?THM?
if we can show that

(4.5)

\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\to 1\\
z\in\Omega\end{subarray}}\sum_{n:a_{n}\in S_{z}}\frac{1-|a_{n}|^{2}}{|1-\overline{a_{n}}z|^{2+k+l}}=0.

To see this, we prove first that

(4.6)

\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\to 1\\
z\in\Omega\end{subarray}}\sup_{n:a_{n}\in S_{z}}|1-\overline{a_{n}}z|=0.

Should this not be true, then there exists a positive number
\delta
, a sequence
(z_{n})_{n}
of points in
\Omega
converging to
1
, and a sequence
(a^{*}_{n})_{n}
of distinct zeros of
b
, such that
a^{*}_{n}\in S_{z}
and
\delta<|1-\overline{a^{*}_{n}}z_{n}|
for all
n
. Now, the standard estimate

|1-\overline{a^{*}_{n}}z_{n}|\leq(1-|a_{n}^{*}|)+(1-|z_{n}|)+|\arg(\overline{a^{*}_{n}}z_{n})|

shows that

is bounded from below, since the other two terms on the right-hand side above tend to

as

. This says that the Blaschke product has infinitely many distinct zeros in a disk centered at the origin of radius strictly less than
1
, which is impossible. This establishes (

).
Accordingly, given any

, if

is sufficiently close to

, then
\sup_{n:a_{n}\in S_{z}}|1-\overline{a_{n}}z|<\epsilon
, and hence

\displaystyle\limsup_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\to 1\\
z\in\Omega\end{subarray}}\sum_{n:a_{n}\in S_{z}}\frac{1-|a_{n}|^{2}}{|1-\overline{a_{n}}z|^{2+k+l}}

\displaystyle=\limsup_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\to 1\\
z\in\Omega\end{subarray}}\sum_{n:a_{n}\in S_{z}}|1-\overline{a_{n}}z|^{2m-k-l}\frac{1-|a_{n}|^{2}}{|1-\overline{a_{n}}z|^{2m+2}}

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon^{2m-k-l}\sup_{z\in\Omega}\sum_{n}\frac{1-|a_{n}|^{2}}{|1-\overline{a_{n}}z|^{2m+2}}.

Since

, we see from the above estimate that (

) holds, and so the proof is complete.
∎

We may now finish the proof of the equivalence of

and

5.
Proof of Theorems C and D

In the proof we will use the following simple calculus lemma.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\rho
be an increasing continuous function on
[0,\infty)
, continuously differentiable on
(0,\infty)
. Assume that

\lim_{x\to 0}\rho^{\prime}(x)=0.

For all sufficiently small positive
x
and
x^{*}
satisfying
x<x^{*}
, we have

2|e^{ix^{*}}-z|\geq\rho(x^{*}),\quad z=(1-\rho(x))e^{ix}.

Proof.

We have

2|e^{ix^{*}}-z|\geq\frac{1}{2}|x^{*}-x|+\rho(x):=D(x).

Note that for
x<x^{*}
we have
D^{\prime}(x)=-\frac{1}{2}+\rho^{\prime}(x)
which, if
x
is sufficiently small, is a negative quantity by the assumption on
\rho
. Thus
D
is decreasing on the interval
(x,x^{*})
, and therefore
\rho(x^{*})=D(x^{*})\leq D(x)\leq 2|e^{ix^{*}}-z|
.
∎

Proof of ?THM?
.

Since
\rho(x)=\int^{x}_{0}\rho^{\prime}(x)\,dx=o(x)
, by our assumption we can pick a decreasing sequence
(x_{n})_{n}
of positive real numbers which converges to
0
and satisfies
the two conditions

(5.1)

\sum_{n}\frac{\rho^{2}(x_{n})}{x^{2}_{n}}<\infty

and

(5.2)

x_{n+1}<x_{n}/2.

We take
b
to be the singular inner function

b(z)=\exp\Big(-\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{\zeta+z}{\zeta-z}d\nu(\zeta)\Big),\quad z\in\mathbb{D}

where

\nu=\sum_{n}\rho^{2}(x_{n})\delta_{e^{ix_{n}}}

and
\delta_{e^{ix_{n}}}
is a point mass at
e^{ix_{n}}\in\mathbb{T}
. We will soon show that
\sup_{z\in\Omega_{\rho}}\|k^{b}_{z}\|_{\mathcal{H}(b)}<\infty
holds. Assuming this, according to ?THM?
(or the Duan-Li-Mashreghi Theorem) and Fatou’s lemma, we have

\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{d\nu(\zeta)}{|1-\zeta|^{2}}<\infty.

Moreover,
\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\to 1\\
z\in\Omega_{\rho}\end{subarray}}b(z)
exists. Noting that

\frac{b^{\prime}(z)}{b(z)}=-\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{2\zeta d\nu(\zeta)}{(\zeta-z)^{2}},

to show that
\lim_{z\to 1,z\in\Omega_{\rho}}b^{\prime}(z)
does not exist, it will suffice to show that

\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\to 1\\
z\in\Omega_{\rho}\end{subarray}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{2\zeta d\nu(\zeta)}{(\zeta-z)^{2}}

does not exist. To see this, we will check that the radial limit
z=r\to 1
and the limit along the sequence
z_{n}=(1-\rho(x_{n}))e^{ix_{n}}\in\partial\Omega_{\rho}
differ. For the case of the radial limit, we employ the inequality
\frac{1}{|\zeta-r|^{2}}\leq\frac{4}{|\zeta-1|^{2}}
and the Dominated Convergence Theorem to see that

\lim_{r\to 1}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{2\zeta d\nu(\zeta)}{(\zeta-r)^{2}}=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{2\zeta d\nu(\zeta)}{(\zeta-1)^{2}}.

Recall the definition of the arcs
E_{z_{n}}
in (
1.12
). This time, the Dominated Convergence Theorem and the estimate in (
1.16
) show that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{T}\setminus E_{z_{n}}}\frac{2\zeta d\nu(\zeta)}{(\zeta-z_{n})^{2}}=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{2\zeta d\nu(\zeta)}{(\zeta-1)^{2}},

same as the radial limit. However, according to
(\ref{E:RhoXnTwoCond2})
, for
z_{n}:=(1-\rho(x_{n}))e^{ix_{n}}\in\partial\Omega_{\rho}
, we have that
E_{z_{n}}\cap\text{supp}({\nu})=\{e^{ix_{n}}\}
, and so

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{E_{z_{n}}}\frac{2\zeta d\nu(\zeta)}{(\zeta-z_{n})^{2}}=\lim_{n\to\infty}2e^{-ix_{n}}=2.

Thus

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{2\zeta d\nu(\zeta)}{(\zeta-z_{n})^{2}}=\lim_{r\to 1}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{2\zeta d\nu(\zeta)}{(\zeta-r)^{2}}+2,

and it follows that

\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\to 1\\
z\in\Omega_{\rho}\end{subarray}}b^{\prime}(z)
does not exist.

It remains to show that
\sup_{z\in\Omega_{\rho}}\|k^{b}_{z}\|_{b}<\infty
. According to ?THM?
, we need to show the finiteness of

(5.3)

\sup_{z\in\Omega_{\rho}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{d\nu(\zeta)}{|z-\zeta|^{2}}=\sup_{z\in\Omega_{\rho}}\sum_{n}\frac{\rho^{2}(x_{n})}{|e^{ix_{n}}-z|^{2}}.

It will suffice to do this with the above suprema restricted to
z\in\partial\Omega_{\rho}
. Moreover, it will suffice to do this for all
z\in\Omega_{\rho}
sufficiently close to
1
.

Let then
z=(1-\rho(x))e^{ix}\in\partial\Omega_{\rho}
. There exists a unique
m
for which we have
x_{m+1}\leq x<x_{m}
. We will divide the sum
\sum_{n}\frac{\rho^{2}(x_{n})}{|e^{ix_{n}}-z|^{2}}
into terms index by
n\geq m+2
and
n\leq m-1
, with special arguments for terms indexed by
n=m+1
and
n=m
.

Let us treat the two special terms first. We will show that
|e^{ix_{m}}-z|
and
|e^{ix_{m+1}}-z|
are controlled from below by
\rho(x_{m})
and
\rho(x_{m+1})
respectively. For
n=m+1
, note that since
\rho
is increasing,
1-|z|=\rho(x)\geq\rho(x_{m+1})
and so
|e^{ix_{m+1}}-z|\geq 1-|z|\geq\rho(x_{m+1})
. For
n=m
, apply ?THM?
to
x^{*}=x_{m}
to conclude that
2|e^{ix_{m}}-z|\geq\rho(x_{m})
. It follows from these two estimates that

\frac{\rho^{2}(x_{m})}{|e^{ix_{m}}-z|^{2}}+\frac{\rho^{2}(x_{m+1})}{|e^{ix_{m+1}}-z|^{2}}\leq 5.

Consider now the terms indexed by
n\geq m+2
. Then
x=\arg z
lies further away from
x_{n}
than
x_{m+1}
does, and it follows that
|x_{m+1}-x_{n}|\leq|x_{n}-x|\leq 4|e^{ix_{n}}-z|
. Moreover, by (
5.2
) we have
2x_{n}<x_{m+1}
which then implies that
x_{n}\leq|x_{m+1}-x_{n}|\leq 4|e^{ix_{n}}-z|
. We conclude that

\sum_{n\geq m+2}\frac{\rho^{2}(x_{n})}{|e^{ix_{n}}-z|^{2}}\leq 16\sum_{n:n\geq m+2}\frac{\rho^{2}(x_{n})}{x_{n}^{2}}.

So this part of the sum is controlled by (
5.1
).

At last, consider the terms indexed by
n\leq m-1
. Then, similarly to the previous part, we get
4|z-e^{ix_{n}}|>|x_{m}-x_{n}|>x_{n}/2
and so

\sum_{n:n\leq m-1}\frac{\rho^{2}(x_{n})}{|e^{ix_{n}}-z|^{2}}\leq 64\sum_{n:n\leq m-1}\frac{\rho^{2}(x_{n})}{x_{n}^{2}}.

Our above estimates combine to show that
\sup_{z\in\Omega_{\rho}}\|k^{b}_{z}\|_{b}<\infty
.
∎

. Take the logarithmic derivative of

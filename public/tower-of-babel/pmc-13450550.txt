SSO targets CD36 to regulate lipid metabolism disorders and improve SiO2 induced fibrosis
Authors: Xinyao Wang, Yingshu Zhang, Yi Hu, Xiaodi Liu, Bo Zhang, Hongli Wang, Heliang Liu, Hailan He
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Silicosis is an occupational disease caused by inhalaling free silicon dioxide (SiO2). Lipid metabolism plays an important role in silicosis. CD36 is a key factor involved in lipid transport, but its mechanism of action in silicosis remains unclear. This study aims to explore the mechanism by which the CD36 inhibitor sulfo-n-succinimide oleate (SSO) targets CD36 to regulate lipid metabolism disorders in silicosis and improve fibrosis. The results showed that SiO2 stimulation led to an increase in CD36 expression, a decrease in LXR and ABCA1 expression, and an increase in lipid dropper formation in rat alveolar macrophages NR8383. The SSO intervention can reverse these changes, resulting in the decrease in CD36 expression, the increase in LXR and ABCA1 expression, the reduction in IL-1β, IL-6 levels and TGF-β1 secretion. Subsequently, after stimulating primary fibroblasts with the supernatants of macrophages in each group, it was found that SSO stimulation reduced the increase of α-SMA and collagen-I induced by SiO2 and improved the level of fibrosis. In addition, in vivo animal experiments have found that SSO intervention alleviated the levels of inflammation, lipid metabolism and fibrosis induced by SiO2 in silicosis mice, and improved the lung function of silicosis mice. These results indicate that SSO regulates SiO2-induced lipid metabolism disorders by targeting CD36, thereby improving SiO2-induced fibrosis. Therefore, SSO targeting CD36 may be a new strategy for treating silica-induced silicosis injury.

Introduction

The number of global pneumoconiosis cases increased from 42,188 in 1990 to 62,866 in 2021 (Zhang et al., 2025a). Silicosis is the most serious type of occupational pneumoconiosis (Sun et al., 2025). Silicosis is a pulmonary fibrosis disease caused by long-term inhalation of silicon dioxide (SiO2) during occupational activities, characterized by persistent inflammation and progressive fibrosis of the lungs (Yang et al., 2025). The pathogenesis of silicosis is complex. Currently, there is no clear and effective treatment strategy (Li et al., 2025a).

Alveolar macrophages play a major role in the pathogenesis of silicosis (Du et al., 2025). It is well known that SiO2 stimulates macrophages to release inflammatory factors, accelerating the progression of silicosis (Xu et al., 2024). New research has found lipid metabolism is of great significance in the progression of silicosis (He et al., 2023). The research group previously observed lipid-rich macrophages in the alveolar lavage fluid of silicosis patients (Hou et al., 2019). Lipids provide essential substrates for the production of biomolecules and activate signaling pathways that regulate various cellular processes, participating in the progression of diseases (Li et al., 2025b). The above reveals that targeting lipid metabolism in macrophages may effectively improve silicosis fibrosis.

CD36 is a key lipid transporter (Li et al., 2025c), which can mediate 75% of lipid intake and promote the formation of foam cells (Wei et al., 2019), a type of lipid-containing macrophage (Pan et al., 2024). It has been reported that SiO2 can induce macrophage foaming and enhance the increased expression of CD36 induced by ox-LDL (Hou et al., 2019), indicating CD36 may play a crucial role in lipid metabolism disorder of macrophages induced by SiO2. Research has found that sulfosuccinimidyl oleate (SSO) is a long-chain fatty acid that binds to specific residues on CD36 and effectively inhibits the transport of CD36 to long-chain fatty acids (Kuda et al., 2013; Pownall, 2020). However, it remains unclear whether SSO regulates lipid metabolism disorders and thereby improves the progression of silicosis fibrosis.

Therefore, this study aims to explore the mechanism of SSO targeting CD36 in lipid metabolism disorders of silicosis, and whether SSO targeting CD36 can improve the fibrosis process of silicosis by regulating lipid metabolism disorders.

Materials and methods

Animal grouping and treatment

C57 male mice (SCXK 2019–0010, Beijing, China) were purchased from Beijing Spefu Biotechnology Co., LTD. Mice were fed at the SPF-level laboratory animal center of North China University of Science and technology. After two weeks of adaptive feeding, the mice were randomly divided into 3 groups, with 10 mice in each group. Mice in the silicosis model group (SiO2 group) were given a one-time tracheal infusion of SiO2 suspension (100 mg/mL) 50 μL. The control group (C group) was given the same dose of 0.9% sodium chloride solution and was sacrificed after 4 weeks. The SSO intervention group (SiO2 + SSO group) was perfusion as the silicosis model group, after one week of conventional feeding, the mice were intraperitoneally injected with SSO reagent (10 mg/kg) every other day. The mice were sacrificed after four weeks of administration. The lung tissues were taken, embedded in paraffin, sectioned, and stained with HE and VG to observe the histopathological changes. The remaining lung tissues were frozen and stored at −80 °C for future use. This study was conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. This study has been approved by the Ethics Review Committee for Laboratory Animals of North China University of Science and Technology (SQ2024155).

Cell grouping and treatment

NR8383 cells were cultured in F12k medium containing 15% FBS. The experiment was divided into control group (C group), SSO stimulation group (SSO group), SiO2 stimulation group (SiO2 group), and SiO2 + SSO stimulation group (SiO2 + SSO group). According to the previous experiments of the research group (Hou et al., 2019), NR8383 cells were stimulated alone or in combination with SSO at the final concentration of 5 μM and SiO2 at 50 μg/mL for 36 h respectively. In addition, rat lung fibroblasts were extracted from neonatal rats and cultured in a high-glucose DMEM medium with 10% FBS, and then the rat lung fibroblasts were stimulated respectively with the supernatants of macrophages from the above four groups.

Oil red O staining

The cells were fixed for 15 min, stained with oil red O dye in the dark for 20 mins, then the nuclei were stained with hematoxylin for 30 s, differentiated with differentiation solution for 5 s, and the water bubbles turned blue for 10 mins. Then, the lipid droplets in the cells were observed under a microscope.

Histopathology

After the lung tissues were fixed, dehydrated, embedded and sectured, the sections were stained with HE and VG to assess the extent of fibrosis and the distribution of pathological changes in mouse lung tissue.

Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA)

Collect the supernatant of NR8383 cells, operate according to the instructions of the TGF-β1 ELISA kit (ABclonal Company, USA), and measure the absorbance values of each well at a wavelength of 450 nm using an ELISA reader. Calculate the sample concentration based on the standard curve.

Measurement of total cholesterol and free cholesterol in macrophages

The total cholesterol (TC), free cholesterol (FC) levels were determined according to the Kit instructions at 450 nm by a microplate reader.

Micro computed tomography

NEMO-II NMC-20 device was used to capture computed tomography (CT) images of living mouse lungs. The mice were pre-anesthetized with isoflurane, then placed in a small animal bin, kept in a prone position, and were continuously anesthetized with isoflurane. The body temperature of the mice was maintained at 34 ± 2 °C. During this time, the mice were continuously observed using PadCam video surveillance. Cruiser software was used to collect data, recon daemon software was used to reconstruct data offline, and avatar software was used to analyze data and reconstruct lung tissue.

Pulmonary function assessment

Before and after modeling, mice with silicosis were put into Whole Body Plethysmography (WBP) every week for non-invasive dynamic monitoring of mouse lung function. After a few minutes of stabilization, pulmonary function tests included an adaptation period (10 min), a fogging period (1 s), a response period (5 min), and a recovery period (1 min). After testing, export the data with Excel data sheet. Key pulmonary function indicators include: tidal volume (TVb), frequency (f), expiratory flow 50 (EF50), enhanced pause (Penh), pause (PAU), peak inspiratory flow (PIF) and peak exspiratory flow (PEF).

qRT-PCR

CD36 and ABCA1 in cell and lung tissue were tested using qRT-PCR. RNA was extracted with TRIzol reagent and then reverse transcribed into cDNA. GAPDH was used as an internal reference. Immunofluorescence

After cell fixation, the primary antibody Collagen- I (1:200) was incubated at 4 °C overnight. Then, the fluorescent secondary antibody and DAPI were added successively, and the images were scanned and observed using an Olymupus FLUOVIEW FV-1000 laser confocal microscope. After high-pressure repair of lung tissue sections, the primary antibody CD36 (1:100) was incubated at 4 °C overnight, and the secondary antibody was incubated in a 37 °C incubator for 1 h. The cell nuclei were labeled with DAPI, and then the images were observed under a fluorescence microscope.

Western blot

After protein extraction, the protein concentration was determined. The protein concentration was uniformly diluted to 3000 μg/mL. The gel was removed by SDS-PAGE electrophoresis, wet transfer to NC membrane, 5% skimmed milk was sealed at room temperature for 2 h, the primary antibody was incubated overnight at 4 °C, and the secondary antibody was incubated at room temperature for 2 h. Finally, an enhanced chemiluminescence detection system was used for detection. The primary antibodies used in our study were β-actin (1:3000, Boster Biotechnology, China), CD36 (1:1000, HuaAn Biotechnology, China), IL-1β(1:1000, Abcam, USA), IL-6 (1:1000, Abcam, USA), LXR (1:1000, HuaAn Biotechnology, China), ABCA1 (1:1000, HuaAn Biotechnology, China), Collagen-I (1:1000, Affinity, USA), α-SMA (1:1000, Affinity, USA).

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS 23.0. All experimental result datas were expressed as mean ± SD. The t-test was used for comparison between two groups, and one-way analysis of variance was used for comparison among multiple groups. P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

SiO2 induces lipid metabolism disorders in NR8383 cells

To explore the effect of SiO2 stimulation on lipid metabolism in macrophages, we first detected the changes in inflammatory factors after SiO2 stimulation of macrophages. The western blot results (Fig. 1A-C) showed that compared with C group, TNF-α and IL-6 increased after SiO2 stimulation. In addition, to explore the effect of SiO2 stimulation on lipid metabolism in macrophages, oil red O staining was used to detect the accumulation of lipid droplets in cells (Fig. 1D). Compared with C group, the orange-red lipid droplets in macrophages of the SiO2 group increased and were located in the cytoplasm. In addition, the expression levels of intracellular lipid metabolism-related factor proteins were detected. The western blot results showed (Fig. 1E-H) that, compared with C group, SiO2 stimulation increased CD36 and decreased LXR and ABCA1. Inhibiting CD36 improves lung tissue fibrosis in silicosis mice

Discussion

Alveolar macrophages are the most abundant immune cells in the lungs, accounting for over 90% of alveolar immune cells. Alveolar macrophages play a regulatory role through non-specific immune defense mechanisms, the production of inflammatory mediators, and the expression of inflammatory cytokines (Lugg et al., 2022). A large number of studies have shown that TNF-α, IL-6 and interleukin-1β are regarded as important indicators of SiO2-induced inflammation (Franklin et al., 2016; Zelová and Hošek, 2013; Zhao et al., 2022). This study found that after SiO2 stimulated NR8383 cells, TNF-α and IL-6 increased, which was consistent with the above research results. It is reported that SiO2 promotes abnormal lipid deposition in macrophages and their transformation into foam cells (Li et al., 2022). In addition, the research previously observed lipid-rich macrophages in the alveolar lavage fluid of patients with silicosis and proposed that targeting foam cells might be a new approach for the prevention and treatment of silicosis (Hou et al., 2019). The above suggests that lipid metabolism is crucial for the progression of silicosis.

To explore the mechanism of lipid metabolism in silicosis, the levels of lipid-related factors in alveolar macrophages treated with SiO2 and lung tissues of silicosis mice were detected in this project. Research has found that the expression of CD36 increased in alveolar macrophages treated with SiO2 and in the lung tissues of silicosis mice, while the expression of LXR and ABCA1 decreased. CD36 is a transmembrane glycoprotein, also known as a fatty acid translocase, which is involved in a variety of physiological processes, including the uptake of long-chain fatty acids, lipid metabolism, and the regulation of chronic metabolic inflammation (Zhang et al., 2025b). Studies have confirmed that CD36 mediates the endocytosis of ox-LDL, promoting lipid accumulation and foam cell formation (Zhang et al., 2025c). ABCA1 plays a significant role in cholesterol homeostasis (Wang et al., 2025), it is reported that ABCA1 agonists can enhance cholesterol efflorescation (Cao et al., 2025). As a nuclear receptor regulating cholesterol metabolism, LXR can upregulate ABCA1 and reduce lipid accumulation (Litvinchuk et al., 2024). Previous studies have confirmed that CD36 is associated with an increased uptake of ox-LDL or fatty acids in macrophages (Seimon et al., 2010). The deficiency of CD36 can inhibit the transformation of macrophages into foam cells (Kennedy et al., 2009). The formation of foam cells mainly depends on the lipid inflow and outflow mediated by scavenger receptors. When this balance is disrupted, lipid accumulation occurs (Zhuang et al., 2021). In addition, inhibiting CD36 palmitoylation has been proven to improve tissue inflammation in non-alcoholic steatohepatitis by balancing fatty acid metabolism (Zhao et al., 2018). Therefore, the above indicates that targeted regulation of CD36 may improve silicosis inflammation and fibrosis induced by SiO2 by regulating lipid metabolism disorders.

SSO is a specific inhibitor of CD36 (Hua et al., 2015), and it binds to specific residues on CD36 and effectively inhibits the transport of CD36 to long-chain fatty acids (Kuda et al., 2013). This study found, after the addition of SSO inhibitors, CD36 was significantly downregulated, demonstrating that SSO effectively blocked the expression of CD36. It is reported that SSO can effectively reduce the uptake of exogenous low-density lipoprotein by cells, which is the main source of fatty acids, thereby improving the accumulation of lipids within cells (Lemberger et al., 2022). This study found that SSO reduced the accumulation of lipid droplets and the obstruction of cholesterol excretion in SiO2-induced macrophages and silicosis mice, which is consistent with the above research report (Lemberger et al., 2022). This suggests that SSO treatment can alleviate SiO2-induced lipid metabolism disorders in macrophages.

Inflammation is typical pathological features of silicosis (Shao et al., 2024). It is reported that macrophages are the main pro-inflammatory cells, and CD36 promotes the entry of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria, which subsequently leads to the release of inflammatory factors (Zhang et al., 2025d). In this study, we found that SSO improved the increase of IL-1β and IL-6 in macrophages induced by SiO2. Furthermore, in the silicosis mouse model, we also found that SSO inhibited IL-6 and IL-1β in the lung tissue of silicosis mice. Inhibiting CD36 palmitoylation has been proven to improve tissue inflammation in non-alcoholic steatohepatitis by balancing fatty acid metabolism (Zhao et al., 2018). Our study is consistent with the above results, revealing that SSO targets CD36 to regulate SiO2-induced inflammation in macrophages.

In addition, it is well known that fibrosis is the main pathological feature of advanced silicosis (Zhang et al., 2024). In this study, we detected the secretion of TGF-β1 in the supernatant of macrophages. The results showed that SSO reduced the increased secretion of TGF-β1 induced by SiO2. TGF-β1, as a powerful pro-fibrotic factor, can activate fibroblasts to promote the synthesis of extracellular matrix, including α-SMA and collagen-I (Shieh et al., 2019; Zhang et al., 2021). We stimulated primary fibroblasts of rats with the supernatants of macrophages from different groups and found that collagen-I and α-SMA increased in SiO2 stimulation group and were significantly down-regulated in the SiO2 + SSO group. In addition, this study also found that SSO can improve the fibrosis level in the lung tissue and lung function of silicosis mice. Previous studies have found that SSO can reduce the uptake of free fatty acids and fibrosis in diabetic nephropathy models (Su et al., 2019), and alleviating lipid accumulation can regulate the development of renal fibrosis (Li et al., 2025d). This study confirmed that SSO can target CD36 to improve silicosis fibrosis. It is reported that CD36 in hepatocytes plays a key role in protecting mice from diet-induced liver damage and fibrosis (Li et al., 2023). Our view is consistent with the above research reports. The therapeutic strategy targeting CD36 may have an effective inhibitory effect on silicosis fibrosis.

However, this study also has certain limitations. In the animal experiment grouping, we did not set up a separate SSO group. Therefore, we cannot determine whether SSO will have an impact on the lipid metabolism and lung function of normal mice. Additionally, long-term exposure to the inhibitor SSO can induce cell apoptosis (Takaichi et al., 2024). Thus, we cannot determine whether SSO regulates inflammation and lipid levels in silicosis by influencing the apoptosis. Future research should include a separate SSO group and add apoptosis experiments to complement these findings.

In conclusion, based on the research of animal and cell models of silicosis, this study found that CD36 changes in silicosis are accompanied by lipid metabolism disorders. SSO can regulate lipid metabolism disorders and improve SiO2 induced fibrosis by targeting CD36. These findings can provide new ideas for the pathogenesis and treatment of silicosis.

Theoretical study on the ultimate capacity of partially encased composite beams with web openings
Authors: Meng Ye, Tao Wu, Peng Wang, Dangshu Zhao, Huaxian Fan, Jiali Chen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBased on the failure mechanism of traditional steel-concrete composite vierendeel trusses, this study investigates the internal force conditions during failure of Partially Encased Composite Beams with web openings. By neglecting the contribution of concrete tension and steel compression to ultimate bearing capacity, four secondary moment equations were developed with axial force and bending moment as variables. Addressing the plastic hinge regions formed at the four corners of web openings in composite beams, a calculation method for the ultimate bearing capacity of Partially Encased Composite Beams with web openings is proposed. The calculation results demonstrate that: Filling concrete on both sides of I-shaped steel beams with web openings enhances their shear and flexural capacities; Opening dimensions are critical parameters influencing the ultimate bearing capacity of such composite beams. A half-symmetrical finite element model was established to verify the accuracy of the proposed calculation method.Keywords: Web opening, Partially encased composite beams, Vierendeel truss, Ultimate bearing capacitySubject terms: Engineering, Materials science, Physics

Introduction

Partially Encased Composite (PEC) beams1 represent an innovative structural system where concrete is cast on both sides of the I-shaped steel web2, leaving the flanges exposed. This configuration enhances overall stiffness and torsional buckling resistance while demonstrating superior performance in fire resistance3, durability, and ductility4,5. As a technically and economically advantageous solution, PEC beams enable full or partial prefabrication, significantly improving construction efficiency6. Widely adopted in residential and office buildings since the 1980 s, PEC beams typically use narrow-flange H-section steel.

In modern buildings, the increasing demand for integrated utility pipelines (water supply, electrical, broadband) has driven the development of partially encased concrete beams with web openings (PECBWOs), where steel web openings accommodate utilities, eliminating complex pipe support systems below beams. This innovation optimizes spatial utilization, increases net building height, reduces floor-to-floor heights and structural self-weight, and lowers construction costs. partially encased concrete beams with web openings exhibit excellent structural adaptability, with their research advancing technical progress and ecological sustainability goals.

Current research on partially encased concrete composite beams has reached a relatively mature stage, with studies primarily focusing on structural configurations and material aspects7–9. Chu10,11 proposed an improved composite member by integrating the advantages of traditional partially encased concrete composite beams and corrugated web steel beams. Through experimental investigations, they developed bearing capacity calculation formulas for this structural configuration; Chen12,13 introduced a novel prefabricated Π-shaped steel partially encased concrete composite beam to enhance web utilization efficiency and production rate; Yang14 experimentally investigated structural bearing capacity enhancement through the application of high-strength steel and high-strength concrete (UHPC); Ye15 developed a hollow-core partially encased concrete composite beam to reduce steel consumption and structural self-weight. Experimental validation confirmed its sufficient bearing capacity and stiffness performance. Recent studies on partially encased concrete composite beams with web openings have primarily focused on seismic capacity, load-carrying mechanisms, opening shapes/sizes, and verification through finite element modeling16–18. Bartus19 developed a MATLAB-based shear force distribution model for steel-concrete composite beams with web-openings under complex stress states; Deng20 conducted parametric finite element analysis (FEA) on PEC beams with various web-opening geometries, investigating failure modes and mechanisms; Zou21 evaluated seismic performance of main steel web-opening PEC beams, noting pinching effects in moment-rotation hysteresis curves while maintaining satisfactory overall seismic behavior. However, theoretical calculation methods for post-opening bearing capacity remain underdeveloped.

Wang22,23 proposed a plastic load-bearing capacity calculation method for web-opening composite beams grounded in the open-web truss failure mechanism, with experimental validation and finite element analysis confirming its accuracy. This paper investigates the ultimate bearing capacity calculation method for web-opening Partially Encased Composite beam by simplifying the model based on the Vierendeel mechanism, four secondary moment equations (axial force and bending moment variables) are established for plastic hinge regions at web opening corners, providing a theoretical basis for partially encased concrete beams with web openings design and analysis.

PECBWOs vierendeel failure mode and calculation assumptions

Vierendeel failure mode of PECBWOs

During failure of the PECBWO, the coupled effect of axial force, shear force, and bending moment results in complex stress states around the web opening. As the load gradually increases, under the combined action of primary and secondary moments, the material strength at the upper and lower sections of the web opening progressively reaches its ultimate state. This culminates in the formation of four axial-force-bending-moment-coupled plastic hinges at the four corners of the web opening. The bending moment generated by the distance between centroidal axes of the upper and lower web opening sections in conjunction with axial force is termed the primary bending moment; whereas the bending moment produced by the shearing force multiplied by its width in each web opening is defined as the secondary moment. Finite element validation and analysis

Establishment of finite element model

This paper utilizes ANSYS finite element analysis software to conduct modeling and simulation analysis of PECBWOs, aiming to verify the accuracy of theoretical calculations. The flange sections are modeled using Solid45 solid elements. The web section is simulated with Shell181 shell elements. Stud connectors are modeled using Combin39 nonlinear spring elements to analyze the interfacial bond-slip effect between the steel beam and the concrete on both sides of the web. The concrete on both sides of web is simulated with Solid65 elements to account for cracking and crushing under loading, while the reinforcement is modeled using Link180 elements, neglecting bond-slip between reinforcement and concrete. Given the good symmetry of the Partially Encased Composite (PEC) structure, a half-model can be established with symmetric boundary conditions applied on the symmetric plane. This approach simplifies computation and improves analysis efficiency. The boundary conditions of a composite simply - supported beam are simulated by applying restraint at the ends of the PEC (Partially Encased Composite) beam. This ensures that the bending and warping at the beam ends are unrestrained, while also constraining the torsion at the beam ends. When using ANSYS finite element software, the simulation is conducted by adopting force loading, displacement convergence criteria, Von-Mises yield criterion, and the Newton - Raphson nonlinear solver. Due to the favorable symmetry of the PEC beam model, finite element simulation analysis demonstrates that when the mesh size of the steel beam flange elements is divided into 50 mm × 25 mm × 12 mm, and the element size at the concrete opening is refined to 50 mm × 25 mm × 15 mm, an optimal balance between computational accuracy and efficiency can be achieved. Figures 23 and 24 present the equivalent plastic strain distribution contour and von Mises stress distribution contour of the PECBWO under ultimate bearing capacity state, respectively. From Figure 23, it can be observed that under ultimate bearing capacity, four significant plastic deformations occur at the web openings of the steel beam in the PECBWO, gradually developing into four plastic hinges and leading to vierendeel failure. Unlike the classical composite beam’s vierendeel failure mechanism, the concrete at the web openings gradually experiences cracking failure and withdraws from effective stress contribution under ultimate load. Therefore, the theoretical formulation in this paper idealizes the concrete’s contribution in the derivation based on this failure mode. Furthermore, finite element analysis reveals that when the web opening size is relatively small, its impact on bearing capacity is negligible. Under mid-span concentrated loading, flexural failure dominated by bending moment still occurs, indicating that the failure mode of PECBWOs is governed by the bend-shear ratio. In such cases, the theoretical calculation formulas for vierendeel failure models are not applicable.

Moreover, the theoretical calculations neglect the impact of opening positions on the bearing - capacity results. The closer the opening position is to the loading point, the more closely the failure mode of the partially encased concrete composite beams with web openings aligns with the computational assumptions, and the smaller the error becomes.

Parametric analysis of PECBWOs

To explore the effects of the opening location and opening height on the mechanical performance of partially encased concrete beams with web openings, finite element models P1 (L1 = 500 mm), P2 (L1 = 1000 mm), and P3 (L1 = 1500 mm) were developed with the opening location as the variable parameter. Additionally, finite element models P4 (h0 = 100 mm), P5 (h0 = 150 mm), and P6 (h0 = 190 mm) were also developed with the opening height as the variable parameter. All other parameters were identical to those described in the example case analysis. Then, a comprehensive analysis was carried out on the impacts of variations in the opening location and opening height on the mechanical performance of the composite beams.

The comparison of load - deflection curves for composite beams with different opening positions is shown in Figure 26. The results indicate that the bearing capacity of the partially encased concrete beams with web openings decreases as the distance L1 (in mm) from the left support increases (with a maximum reduction of 12.5%). In the initial loading stage, the load - deflection curves of the three specimens basically coincide, which shows that the change in opening position has a relatively small impact on the stiffness of the composite beams at the early stage of load application.

Conclusion

This paper, based on the Vierendeel truss failure mechanism and neglecting the contributions of tensile concrete and compressive reinforcement to ultimate bearing capacity, targets the plastic hinge regions at the four corners of the web opening in composite beams. Four secondary moment equations with axial force and bending moment as variables were formulated to investigate the calculation method for ultimate bearing capacity of PECBWOs. By comparing this method with finite element model case analyses, the main conclusions can be drawn as follows:

Filling concrete on both sides of the web enhances the flexural and shear capacities of partially encased concrete beams with web openings. This demonstrates that the contribution of web-side concrete to bearing capacity is non-negligible.

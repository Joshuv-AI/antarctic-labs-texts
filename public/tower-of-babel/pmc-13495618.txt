Effects of individualized Theta frequency high-definition transcranial alternating current stimulation on cognitive function in patients with post-stroke cognitive impairment: a randomized, single-blinded, controlled trial
Authors: Yulan Gao, Fangting Duan, Menglin Han, Zhiqiang Wang, Shuyan Zhou, Boyu Zhang, Bin Yu, Kangling Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Objective: To investigate the clinical efficacy of Individualized Theta Frequency High-Definition Transcranial Alternating Current Stimulation (ITF HD-tACS) and 5 Hz HD-tACS for patients with Post-stroke Cognitive Impairment (PSCI), and to clarify the potential neurophysiological mechanisms through electroencephalogram (EEG) analysis.Methods: In this single-blinded, randomized controlled trial, 34 PSCI patients meeting the inclusion criteria were randomly allocated to three groups: the individualized theta frequency group (ITF group), the 5 Hz group, or the sham stimulation group (sham group). The intervention delivered a 1 mA (peak-to-peak) current at the corresponding frequency (ITF or 5 Hz) to the left dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (DLPFC) for 10 consecutive days. We collected scale assessments and 5-min closed-eye resting-state electroencephalogram (EEG) data at two time points: baseline (T0) and the 10th day of intervention, which is after intervention initiation(T1). The assessments included the Montreal Cognitive Assessment (MoCA), Auditory Verbal Learning Test (AVLT), Digit Span Test (DST), Digit Symbol Coding Test (DSCT), Hamilton Depression Scale-17 item (HAMD-17), and Modified Barthel Index (MBI).Results: Behavioral results indicated that the ITF group showed the greatest change amplitude in MoCA scores (T1-T0), which was significantly different from that of the sham group (p = 0.002). Both the ITF group and the 5 Hz group demonstrated substantial improvements in orientation sub-scale scores, AVLT immediate recall, and short-delay recall (all p < 0.05). Compared with the sham group, both the ITF and 5 Hz groups exhibited an increased relative Power Spectral Density (PSD) of the theta band and a decreased relative PSD of the gamma band in the frontal region. There were significant differences in ΔPSD between each stimulation group and the sham group. Moreover, a significant positive correlation was found between frontal theta-band ΔPSD and ΔMoCA in the ITF group(p = 0.017).Conclusion: Theta-tACS is a promising treatment for PSCI patients. ITF-tACS may mediate cognitive improvement by modulating frontal theta oscillations. Due to its significant brain responsiveness, this approach points to an important direction for future individualized neuromodulation.Trial registration: This trial was retrospectively registered in the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry, registration number ChiCTR2600121224.

Introduction

Post-stroke Cognitive Impairment (PSCI) is common among stroke patients (Ren et al., 2025). It is highly clinically significant, as it severely reduces patients' quality of life and increases the mortality rate of stroke survivors (Feigin et al., 2022). Therefore, improving the cognitive function is a core goal of PSCI rehabilitation. In recent years, tACS has drawn widespread attention for its remarkable therapeutic potential for neuropsychiatric and cognitive disorders, including subjective memory decline (Varastegan et al., 2023), mild cognitive impairment (Kim et al., 2021; Benussi et al., 2021), and stroke (Xie et al., 2022; Schuhmann et al., 2022). Studies have shown that tACS can improve performance across nine cognitive domains, including visual attention, working memory, long-term memory, executive control, motor learning, and motor memory (Grover et al., 2023). The theta frequency band is the most commonly used in tACS stimulation (Grover et al., 2023), because it plays a key role in the regulation of working memory, attention, and executive functions (Tian et al., 2021). Theta-tACS has also been reported to have significant therapeutic potential for the treatment of post-stroke aphasia (Xie et al., 2022) and can effectively enhance the working memory performance in older adults (Varastegan et al., 2023).

However, the clinical application of tACS still faces notable bottlenecks, which are mainly associated with fixed stimulation parameters and the failure to fully account for individual differences in brain neural networks and functional activities (Van Hoornweder et al., 2025). Against this background, some scholars have put forward the concept of personalized stimulation, that is, setting individualized stimulation frequencies based on each patient's own EEG frequency bands (Baltus et al., 2018). This approach is derived from research findings: when the stimulation frequency is close to the brain's intrinsic oscillation frequency, the synchronization effect induced by tACS reaches maximum efficacy. Even extremely low currents can affect the amplitude, phase, and frequency of oscillations (Feurra et al., 2011). Therefore, individualized frequency tACS can directly interact with brain oscillations, regulate abnormal oscillations, and promote the recovery of functional connectivity deficits in brain regions (Kehler et al., 2020). Existing literature has identified individualized regulation as an effective neuroregulatory method. For example, compared with fixed-frequency stimulation, ITF/IAF tACS can significantly shorten sleep onset latency and prolong total sleep duration in healthy individuals and patients with insomnia (Ayanampudi et al., 2022). IAF-tACS can reduce left frontal alpha power and relieve depressive symptoms in patients with depression (Riddle et al., 2022), while ITF-tACS can significantly improve subjects' interference word count and interference scores in the Stroop test (Klírová et al., 2021), and enhance motor and cognitive abilities in patients with Parkinson's disease (Del Felice et al., 2019).

However, the application of individualized frequency tACS stimulation in PSCI is still in its early stages, and its clinical efficacy remains unknown. Therefore, we aimed to compare the clinical efficacy of ITF-tACS stimulation, fixed-frequency (5 Hz) stimulation, and sham stimulation on patients with PSCI. Furthermore, we sought to explore the possible neurophysiological mechanisms through EEG detection. Building on the evidence outlined above, we hypothesized that, compared with sham stimulation, both 5 Hz and ITF tACS stimulation can effectively improve the cognitive function of PSCI patients. However, the effects of ITF-tACS may be more significant, given its individualized advantage in inducing oscillation responsiveness.

Methods

Participants

The inclusion criteria for participants were as follows: (1) Confirmed cognitive impairment according to the the Montreal Cognitive Assessment (MoCA, total score ≤ 25) (Tang et al., 2018); (2) First-time stroke with a course of 1 to12 months; (3) Age 18 to 80 years and right-handed; (4) Normal cognitive function before stroke; (5) Stable vital signs; (6) No severe visual or hearing impairment, and able to cooperate with all relevant tests; (7) Consent obtained from the patients and their family members, with a signed informed consent form.

The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) Complete damage to the left prefrontal cortex, confirmed by Computed Tomography/Magnetic Resonance Imaging (CT/MRI); (2) Bilateral brain injury; (3) Skull defects; (4) Current use of antidepressants or psychostimulants; (5) Presence of metal implants near the treatment area; (6) Pre-existing brain diseases including brain tumors, traumatic brain injury, or epileptic seizures; (7) Unstable vital signs or failure of important organ functions; (8) Any neuropsychiatric comorbidities or emotional disorders that may affect test results; (9) Inability to cooperate with cognitive assessment and intervention.

All participants who met the inclusion criteria read the informed consent form and provided their signatures prior to the start of the trial. This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Zhujiang Hospital, Southern Medical University, with the ethics approval number 2024-KY-065.

Study design

We conducted a prospective, single-blinded, sham-controlled, single-center clinical trial. All participants were randomly allocated to one of three groups-the ITF group, the 5 Hz group, or the sham group-via computer-generated permuted block randomization. To prevent group contamination, patients with a natural ITF of 5 Hz were excluded. Subjects were blinded to the intervention protocol throughout the entire trial. Each group received the intervention once daily for 10 consecutive days. Scale assessments and 5-min closed-eye resting-state EEG data collection were completed at two time points: T0 (baseline) and T1 (10 days after the initiation of intervention, the same day that the 10-session intervention cycle was completed). After the 10th stimulation, the cognitive function scale assessment and brain impedance calibration were completed first, followed by the EEG data collection. The interval between these two steps was approximately 40 to 60 min. In addition to tACS stimulation, all groups received necessary, protocol-controlled rehabilitation treatments (RT) following a consistent protocol. Intervention methods(tACS stimulation)

All tACS stimulation was delivered via the NSS18 transcranial alternating current stimulation system (BoruiKang, Changzhou, Jiangsu, China). Five silver‑silver chloride electrodes were connected to a 32-channel EEG recording cap，which were used to deliver the tACS stimulation. We took the left DLPFC as the stimulation site, which is corresponds to F3 electrode according to the international 10/20 system. Return electrodes were placed at FP1, Fz, C3 and F7, forming a 4 × 1 circular focused electric field mode for stimulation (see Fig. 1B). An alternating current with a peak-to-peak current of 1 mA was delivered. The current returned through multiple return electrodes, each carrying equal peak-to-peak shunt current of 0.25 mA. Before stimulation, all participants had their scalp thoroughly cleaned, and conductive gel was applied to each electrode site to keep the impedance below 10KΩ throughout the whole procedure. The stimulation waveform was sinusoidal with a relative phase of 0°.

The individualized theta frequency (ITF) for each participant was calculated based on 5-min eyes-closed resting-state EEG data collected at baseline. We performed Fast Fourier transform (FFT) on the preprocessed EEG data (detailed in the “Resting-state EEG preprocessing” section) to generate the power spectrum. After that, we identified the frequency with the maximum power within the theta band (4–8 Hz), and defined the integer frequency closest to this peak as the ITF (Ayanampudi et al., 2023).

The stimulation frequency was set according to group assignment: both the ITF group and the sham group used individualized theta frequency, while the 5 Hz group used a fixed 5 Hz stimulation. The stimulation intensity was set at 1 mA, and the entire stimulation session lasted 20 min, which included a 60-s gradual current ramp-up at the start of stimulation and a 60-s gradual current ramp-down at the end of stimulation, making these two transitional phases 2 min in total. Stimulation was applied once daily for 10 consecutive days. To maintain patient blinding, all three group underwent the same gradual current ramp-up and ramp-down at the beginning and end of the intervention, while the sham stimulation group delivered 0 mA current during the middle 18 min. This design matched the tactile perception participants experienced at the start and end of stimulation in the active stimulation groups. Additionally, participants wore the HD-tACS stimulation electrode cap throughout the whole session, and electrode-skin impedance was continuously monitored via the device's built-in independent sensing circuit. All electrode impedances were verified to be below 10KΩ prior to each intervention session.

Scale assessment

All scale assessments were completed prior to EEG testing to eliminate the influence of participants' subjective factors and emotional states on the test results. The MoCA (Beijing version) (Yu et al., 2012) was used to evaluate overall cognitive function, encompassing eight cognitive domains: memory, visuospatial ability, executive function, attention, calculation, language, temporal orientation, and spatial orientation. The naming subtest of MoCA, which has a total score of 3 points, is conducted through a picture-naming task featuring three animals to assess participants' visual naming ability.

The AVLT (Hawkins et al., 2004) was applied to evaluate verbal memory, including immediate recall, short-delay recall, long-delay recall, cued recall, and recognition tests. Its score is calculated based on the number of correctly recalled words, with higher scores indicating better memory function. The DST (Leung et al., 2011) was used to assess immediate memory and attention, which includes the forward digit span and backward digit span subtests; scoring is determined by the number of correctly reproduced digit sequences. The DSCT (Gills et al., 2021) was adopted to measure visuospatial processing speed and sustained attention: participants complete digit-symbol matching within one minute, and the score corresponds to the total number of correct matches.

Due to the constraints of clinical assessment resources, this study did not use the parallel versions of MoCA and AVLT. Nevertheless, all enrolled patients were receiving their first standardized cognitive assessment including the MoCA and AVLT, so there was no possibility that they had become familiar with the test items in advance.

The 17-item Hamilton Depression Rating Scale (HAMD-17) was used to evaluate patients' depression symptoms, and the Modified Barthel Index (MBI) was applied to assess patients' activities of daily living.

All adverse events and serious adverse events that occurred during the trial, including tingling and itching under the electrodes, visual artifacts (such as white flashes), skin rashes, headaches, fatigue, metallic taste in the mouth, abnormal sensations of heat or cold sensations, were comprehensively documented.

EEG acquisition and data processing

EEG acquisition

We used a 32-channel wireless EEG acquisition system (Biosemi, Amsterdam, Netherlands), with electrode placement following the international 10–20 system standard (see Fig. 1C). The original sampling rate for data recording was 2048 Hz, and the impedance of all recording electrodes was maintained below 10 kΩ prior to data acquisition. The EEG laboratory had controlled noise levels and standardized lighting conditions. Before data collection, all participants were instructed to sit comfortably with their eyes closed and keep body movements to a minimum for 5 min, during which resting-state EEG data was collected for subsequent analysis (Vosskuhl et al., 2015).

Preprocessing of resting-state EEG data was conducted using the EEG data analysis toolbox EEGLAB (version 2023.1), an EEG data analysis toolbox based on MATLAB (version R2023b). The specific steps are as follows:(1) Import the original BDF format EEG data file into the EEGLAB toolbox; (2) Locate and match the 32 EEG electrodes used in the experiment in accordance with the positioning map of the Neuroscan system 32-channel EEG cap; (3) Apply FIR filters to perform band-pass filtering (0.1-40 Hz) and notch filtering (48-52 Hz) on the EEG data;(4) Down sample the data from the original sampling rate of 2048 Hz to 500 Hz;(5) Split the continuous EEG data into 2-s epochs using custom code;(6) Check and manually remove epochs containing obvious artifacts or drifts in the data preview window;(7) Conduct re-reference processing, adopting the whole-brain average as the reference;(8) Use Independent Component Correlation Algorithm (ICA)to identify and remove non-EEG interference components including electrooculographic, electrocardiographic and electromyographic artifacts (Jonmohamadi et al., 2014);(9) Exclude epochs with extreme amplitude exceeding ±100 μV;(10) After manual verification confirms there are no errors in the processed data, save the data file to the designated folder to complete preprocessing.

Data processing

Consistent with existing literature, spectral feature extraction was performed via Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) (Rampil, 1984), which converts time-domain EEG signals into frequency-domain signals. We calculated the PSD for each frequency band by averaging all segmented data. The predefined frequency bands were delta (1-4 Hz), theta (4-8 Hz), alpha (8-12 Hz), beta (12–30 Hz), and low - gamma (30-50 Hz) (Koenig et al., 2001). After obtaining the PSD values, we performed FDR correction and constructed topographic maps.

Next, we calculated PSD for each frequency band across five brain regions: the frontal region (FP1, FP2, AF3, AF4, F7, F3, Fz, F4, F8, FC5, FC1, FC2, FC6), central region (C3, Cz, C4), temporal region (T7, T8), parietal region (CP5, CP1, CP2, CP6, P7, P3, Pz, P4, P8), and occipital region (PO3, PO4, O1, Oz, O2). We used MATLAB software to extract PSD from different frequency bands in each brain regions, then calculated relative PSD as the ratio of PSD in a specific frequency band to the total PSD across all bands. Based on the positions of 32 EEG electrodes, we constructed network nodes and performed brain electrical connectivity analysis. The resulting network matrix had a dimension 32 × 32, which was used to plot the matrices and connectivity graphs for four connectivity indicators: Coherence (COH), Phase Locking Value (PLV), Phase Lag Index (PLI), and weighted Phase Lag Index (wPLI). The values of all four indicators range from 0 to 1.

Outcome measures

Primary outcome measures: Comparison of changes (Δ) in the MoCA scores (T1-T0) across the three groups.

Secondary outcome measures: Changes (Δ) in specific cognitive domains as AVLT, DST, and DSCT, as well as EEG relative PSD at T0 and T1 across the three groups. In addition, we compared COH, PLV, PLI and wPLI indices between T0 and T1.

Other measures: HAMD-17, MBI, and adverse events.

Statistical analysis

SPSS 26.0 and EEGLAB (version 2023.1) statistical software were used to analyze demographic data and outcome measures. The Shapiro-Wilk test and Levene's test were employed to assess normality and homogeneity.

For normally distributed quantitative variables including age, years of education, disease duration, scale scores, PSD, data were presented as mean ± standard deviation (Mean ± SD), and one-way Analysis of Variance (ANOVA) was used to compare differences among the three groups.

For skewed distributions, data were presented as median (interquartile range) [Median (IQR)], and the Kruskal-Wallis H test was used for between-group comparisons. For categorical data (gender, stroke type, affected hemisphere, etc.), frequencies (n) and percentages (m%) were reported, and chi-square tests were used for comparison. Fisher's exact test was used when data did not meet the conditions for chi-square testing (e.g., small samples or expected frequencies less than 5).

Consistent with a previous relevant study (Ren et al., 2025), the core inferences regarding treatment-related differences in this study were derived from intergroup comparisons of change values (Δ). For data satisfying the assumptions of normal distribution and homogeneity of variance, one-way ANOVA was applied for multi-group comparisons, followed by the Newman-Kelley test for post-hoc pairwise analysis. For non-normally distributed data or data with heterogeneous variance, the Kruskal-Wallis H test was used for intergroup comparisons, and the Wilcoxon signed-rank test was adopted for intragroup comparisons. To account for multiple comparisons in the post hoc analysis of group differences, we applied the Bonferroni correction, which adjusted significance level α = 0.05/3 ≈ 0.017. At time points T0 and T1, we used paired-samples t-tests or Wilcoxon signed-rank tests to examine intragroup differences for our pre-defined exploratory analysis.

For the electrical connectivity analysis, within-group comparisons of COH, PLV, PLI, and wPLI across all frequency bands before and after the intervention were performed using EEGLAB (Version 2023.1). A paired-samples t-test was adopted if the data were normally distributed; otherwise, the Wilcoxon signed-rank test was used.

In additional exploratory analyses, Spearman rank correlation analysis was used to evaluate the association between the changes in relative PSD (ΔPSD) and changes in MoCA scores (ΔMoCA) across the three groups.

All statistical inferences used two-sided tests with a significance level of 0.05, and parameter were estimated with 95% confidence intervals.

Results

Baseline demographic and clinical characteristics

A total of 76 patients with PSCI were screened. Among them, 29 were excluded for failing to meet the inclusion criteria, and 11 declined to participate. Ultimately, 36 participants were enrolled in the trial. Two participants withdrew from the study and could not complete the subsequent interventions and final assessments due to unsuitable physical conditions or personal reasons. As a result, 34 participants completed the trial (see Fig. 2). This sample included 12 females and 22 males; 16 patients had cerebral infarction and 18 had cerebral hemorrhage; 13 had left-hemisphere lesions and 21 had right-hemisphere lesions. All patients were randomly assigned to the ITF group (n = 11), 5 Hz group(n = 11), or sham group(n = 12). Secondary outcome changes between and within groups

Cognitive behavioral assessments

For assessments of other specific cognitive domains, the Kruskal-Wallis H test identified significant between-group differences in ΔAVLT_immediate recall among the three groups (ITF: 2.00[0.00, 3.00] vs 5 Hz: 3.00[0.00, 1.00] vs Sham: 0.00[−0.75, 1.00], H = 8.15, p = 0.017). Post-hoc multiple comparisons confirmed significant differences between the 5 Hz group and the sham group (p = 0.024). No statistically differences were observed across the three groups for ΔAVLT_short delay recall, ΔAVLT_long delay recall, ΔDSF, ΔDSB, or ΔDSCT (p > 0.05) (see Fig. 3A).

In the within-group exploratory comparison of specific cognitive domains,DSCT scores demonstrated significant improvement across all groups (ITF: Z = −2.23, p = 0.026; 5 Hz: Z = −2.70, p = 0.007; Sham: Z = −2.91, p = 0.004, Fig. 3). In the ITF group, scores for AVLT_Immediate (Z = -2.55, p = 0.011), AVLT_Short delay recall (Z = -2.12, p = 0.034), and DSF (Z = -2.00, p = 0.046) were significantly higher at T1 compared with T0. In addition, scores for AVLT_Immediate (Z = -2.35, p = 0.019) and AVLT_Short delay recall (Z = -2.06, p = 0.039) in the 5 Hz group were also significantly higher at T1 than at T0 (see Fig. 3B-D).

Resting-State EEG

Five patients did not complete the post-intervention resting-state EEG collection due to personal reasons. Ultimately, EEG data from 29 patients were included in the analysis, with 10 in the ITF group, 9 in the 5 Hz group, and 10 in the sham group. At baseline (T0), no statistically significant between-group differences were observed in relative PSD values (uV2/Hz) across all frequency bands and all brain regions.

Between-group comparisons were conducted on ΔPSD(T1-T0).

No significant differences were found in COH, PLV, PLI and wPLI in each frequency band among the three groups before intervention (all p > 0.05). Discussion

To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study to apply ITF-tACS to patients with PSCI,and our research has yielded several informative findings.

Cognitive behavioral effects

We found that a statistically significant difference in the primary outcome measure, ΔMoCA, was only observed between the ITF group and the sham group. This appeared to indicate that only the ITF group achieved significant improvement in global cognitive function. However, when evaluated against the minimal clinically important difference (MCID，2 points) criteria for cognitive function improvement in the post-stroke population (Lindvall et al., 2024), the median ΔMoCA score of both the ITF(4 points) and 5 Hz(3 points) groups clearly showed clinically meaningful improvement, while the score of the sham stimulation group(1 points) did not meet the MCID threshold. Therefore, both theta-tACS stimulation protocols used in this study demonstrated clinical efficacy, which was generally consistent with our initial hypothesis. However, no statistically significant difference in ΔMoCA was identified between the ITF group and the 5 Hz group, so the results of this study do not provide evidence supporting the superiority of ITF stimulation over 5 Hz stimulation.

Our study specifically selected theta-tACS. Theta oscillations are predominantly generated in the hippocampus, where they play a pivotal role in memory consolidation and information processing (Kaiser et al., 2025). Moreover, the hippocampus maintains strong neural connections with the prefrontal cortex, and the two regions synergistically regulate multiple higher-order cognitive functions, including memory, orientation, and attention (Su et al., 2024; Eichenbaum, 2017; Burgess et al., 2002). Shtoots et al. (Shtoots et al., 2024) compared the regulatory effects of theta-tACS delivered over the prefrontal and parietal lobes with those of sham stimulation on episodic memory. Their findings demonstrated that only prefrontal theta-tACS significantly enhanced participants' episodic memory performance, and they proposed that this effect might be associated with the neural connections between the hippocampus and the frontal lobe. During memory formation, the hippocampus is responsible for the initial encoding of new information, which is subsequently transmitted to the prefrontal cortex via neural pathways for further integration and storage (Eichenbaum, 2017).

In our study, we chose the left DLPFC as the stimulation target, considering its physiological characteristics as a core brain region for cognitive control and working memory (Webler et al., 2022; Liu et al., 2025), as well as its close neural connections with the hippocampus (Su et al., 2024; Johnson et al., 2018). Our results further confirm the regulatory value of prefrontal theta-tACS for cognitive function.

In recent years, individualized tACS has shown considerable application potential in multiple domains (Klírová et al., 2021; Del Felice et al., 2019; Zhang et al., 2022; Rostami et al., 2023). Our study confirmed that individualized tACS not only improves global cognitive function in patients with PSCI, but also specifically enhances performance in cognitive sub-domains including naming, language, memory, orientation, and attention (evidenced by within-group differences, Fig. 3B). Notably, memory, attention, and orientation are the three most commonly impaired cognitive domains reported in PSCI (Gao et al., 2023). Improvements in these functions will have positively facilitate subsequent functional recovery and activities of daily living. However, it should be emphasized that although multiple secondary outcome measures showed a trend of improvement after the intervention in within-group comparisons, no statistically significant between-group differences were detected. Since this study did not have sufficient statistical power to identify subtle variations in secondary outcome measures, the above findings should only be regarded as preliminary exploratory results, and interpreted with caution, alongside the study's limitations.

Neurophysiological effects

Neural oscillations and cognitive function

We found that theta-tACS could induce more extensive neural oscillatory responses in the brain, and produces significant frequency-specific modulatory effects, especially in the target frequency band and corresponding brain regions. These results provide neuroelectrophysiological evidence for the cognitive intervention mechanism of tACS. After intervention, theta-PSD in the frontal region increased significantly in both the ITF group and the 5 Hz group, and both experimental groups differed significantly from the sham group in between-group comparisons. Frequency-specific oscillatory response is one of the core characteristics of tACS therapy. Debnath et al. (Debnath et al., 2024) applied 5 Hz theta-tACS over the left DLPFC and observed elevated theta-band power in the subjects' resting-state EEG. Similarly, after delivering 6 Hz theta-tACS over the medial prefrontal cortex (mPFC), a significant increase in theta-band power was detected in the postcentral gyrus (Rostami et al., 2021). The findings of our study further support these prior observations.

Beyond the targeted theta band, we also observed a significant decrease in gamma-PSD in the frontal, parietal, and occipital regions of patients in both real stimulation groups. Moreover, the gamma power in the frontal region was significantly lower in the real stimulation groups than that in the sham group. These findings indicate that the therapeutic effects of theta-tACS may be closely associated with both theta and gamma oscillations. Existing studies have demonstrated that theta rhythm acts as a “carrier” for inter-regional information transmission in the brain, and enhancing theta rhythm can facilitate the functional integration of high-order cognitive networks such as the frontoparietal network (Kam et al., 2019).

In contrast, gamma oscillations are responsible for local information processing (Lisman and Jensen, 2013), and the coordinated coupling between these two rhythms forms a core mechanism underlying the integration of global and local information (Yang et al., 2018). A growing body of evidence indicates that tACS can modulate cognitive function by regulating theta-gamma coupling. Reinhart et al. (Reinhart and Nguyen, 2019) found that frontotemporal theta-tACS improved working memory performance in older adults by strengthening theta-gamma phase coupling across the frontotemporal regions. In addition, tACS targeted at theta/gamma peak coupling has been shown to significantly improve both the accuracy and reaction time in working memory tasks for older adults by modulating endogenous theta-gamma coupling (Abubaker et al., 2024). In the present study, the reduced gamma power observed after theta-tACS stimulation may reflect the modulation of cross-frequency coupling induced by this intervention. We speculated that by suppressing excessively active gamma oscillations, theta-tACS optimizes the coordination of theta-gamma coupling, which in turn enhancing the efficiency of information transmission within the frontoparietal network. This finding offers a novel mechanistic explanation for the cognitive improvement effects of theta-tACS. Future research exploring the mechanisms through which theta-gamma coupling contributes to cognitive function is therefore needed, as is the development of strategies to optimize this coupling via tACS for more effective cognitive interventions.

Furthermore, inter group comparisons revealed that theta power in the parietal region was significantly lower in both the ITF group and the 5 Hz group than that in the sham group. In contrast, within-group analysis found a significant increase in parietal theta power in the sham group. These results indicate that the modulatory effect of tACS on parietal theta oscillations matches its effect on the frontal region, presenting as an “inhibitory effect”. We speculate that this may be associated with the synergistic regulation of the frontoparietal network by tACS.

The frontoparietal network is the core neural network supporting higher-order cognitive functions in the human brain, and it participates in key cognitive processes including attentional allocation, working memory updating and executive function (Chen et al., 2023). The realization of its functions depends on dynamic synergistic interaction between the frontal and parietal regions. Previous studies have shown that enhanced frontal theta oscillations can regulate parietal neural activity through frontoparietal pathways (Ratcliffe et al., 2022). Venugopal et al. (Venugopal et al., 2025) found that after individualized theta-tACS stimulation of the frontal region, the reduction in parietal theta power was positively correlated with the enhancement of functional connectivity within the frontoparietal network. This indicates that such “reverse modulation” may be a manifestation of synergistic optimization at the network level. In the present study, tACS may drive the frontoparietal network into a more efficient synergistic state by enhancing frontal theta oscillations. At the same time, reduced parietal theta power can decrease interference from irrelevant information, which further improves the processing efficiency of the frontoparietal network during cognitive tasks. This finding expands the current understanding of the region-specific effects of theta-tACS, confirming that the action of tACS is not limited to modulation of a single brain region, but instead involves the synergistic optimization of the overall function of cognitive networks.

Brain functional connectivity and cognitive function

In patients with PSCI, cognitive-related neural functional reorganization is closely associated with abnormalities in the frontoparietal network (Rao et al., 2022). Furthermore, disruption of functional connectivity between the primary cognitive control network and the higher-order cognitive control network may be a key pathological mechanism underlying PSCI (Rost et al., 2022). This disruption of functional connectivity can cause a significant decline in cognitive function, impairing patients' activities of daily living.

Previous studies have reported that tACS intervention can enhance brain network connectivity, thereby promoting cognitive improvement. For example, a study investigating theta-tACS for patients with anxiety disorders found that the coherence between the frontal and parietal lobes was significantly enhanced after treatment (Lee and Tramontano, 2024). In elderly individuals, frontal theta-tACS stimulation induced a marked increase in PLV, which was accompanied by improved cognitive control (Jones et al., 2022). However, existing studies have reported inconsistent findings regarding the effects of tACS on functional connectivity metrics. For instance, Rostami et al. (Rostami et al., 2021) applied 6 Hz tACS over the mPFC of healthy subjects and observed significant changes in alpha-band PLV within the dorsal attention network (DAN), rather than in the theta band. In another study, delta-tACS was found to increase theta - band PLI, instead of altering the delta band itself (Davis et al., 2023).

The lack of direct evidence that tACS improves brain network connectivity in our study can be attributed to the following reasons: ① the stimulation duration was to short to induce detectable changes; ② the spatial resolution of EEG metrics is insufficient, and brain functional detection techniques with higher spatial resolution (e.g., functional magnetic resonance imaging or positron emission tomography) may be more likely to reveal potential changes in connectivity; ③ we recorded resting-state EEG without concurrent cognitive task performance, rather than dynamic cognitive changes, which may limit our ability to capture task-related neural dynamics directly linked to cognitive behavioral outcomes; ④ the relatively low simulated electric field strength (approximately 0.143 V/m) produced by 1 mA peak-to-peak HD-tACS may have been insufficient to drive robust, measurable changes in inter regional phase synchronization. The low incidence of tingling sensations in participants also indirectly indicates that the current stimulation intensity was relatively mild, which may have further limited its modulatory effects on large-scale network connectivity metrics such as PLV and PLI.

By integrating findings from previous research with the results of the present study, we propose that the modulatory effects of tACS on brain network connectivity may be influenced by multiple factors, including stimulation frequency, stimulation site, stimulation duration, electric field strength and detection methodology. Future research should further explore the interactions between these factors, to deepen our understanding of the mechanisms underlying tACS-mediated regulation of brain network connectivity and cognitive function.

Relationship between cognitive behavioral and neurophysiological effects

We conducted a correlation analysis to explore the association between oscillatory changes and cognitive improvement. In the ITF group, only the correlation between ΔMoCA and ΔPSD of the frontal theta band reached statistical significance and showed a positive direction. By contrast, no significant correlations was detected in either the 5 Hz group or the sham stimulation group. These results suggest that the cognitive improvement induced by ITF-tACS in patients with PSCI is most likely mediated by the specific modulation of frontal theta oscillations, which reflects the unique advantages of individualized stimulation.

The Arnold tongue phenomenon (Tran et al., 2022) served as an important theoretical basis for applying ITF stimulation in our study, and its validity has been confirmed by previous relevant research. For example, Cabrera-Álvarez et al. (Cabrera-Álvarez et al., 2023) applied IAF tACS over the parieto-occipital region of subjects and observed an 8.02% increase in alpha power. Bernardi et al. (Bernardi et al., 2021) implemented IAF-tACS intervention in patients with fibromyalgia and found that this stimulation could significantly enhance alpha oscillatory activity, thereby alleviating pain symptoms and improving cognitive function in this patients population. Although the present study did not provide direct evidence supporting the Arnold tongue phenomenon -no difference in theta PSD was observed between the ITF group and the 5 Hz group- the positive correlation between theta oscillation changes and cognitive improvement still reflects a significant frequency-specific response to individualized stimulation. We therefore speculate that the modulation of frontal theta oscillations is not an incidental effect of stimulation but rather constitutes the core mediating mechanism underlying the cognitive enhancement induced by ITF. This finding is consistent with the electrophysiological results mentioned earlier, which show that ITF optimizes frontal theta oscillations by matching endogenous frequencies, and synergistically modulates the function of the frontoparietal network.

In fact, previous studies have already revealed the potential link between electroencephalographic activity and cognitive function. For instance, a study focusing on PSCI demonstrated that transcranial magnetic stimulation enhanced theta-band brain network connectivity, and the magnitude of this connectivity change was significantly positively correlated with the improvement in MoCA scores (Song et al., 2025). Additionally, Tang et al. found that 40 Hz-tACS can enhance gamma oscillation synchrony in the hippocampus, and this increase in synchrony is positively correlated with improvements in Mini-Mental State Examination (MMSE) scores (Tang et al., 2024). Taken together, these findings demonstrate that frequency-specific tACS can improve cognitive function by modulating neural oscillations, which is consistent with the mechanism proposed in this study:ITF mediates cognitive improvement by regulating frontal theta oscillations. This further highlights the critical role of frequency-specific neural oscillation modulation in cognitive regulation, and this approach may represent a promising direction for future research.

Limitations

There are several limitations to this study. First, the sample size of participants was relatively small. Although we detected statistically significant differences, these findings still need to be further verified in larger cohorts to improve their reliability and universality. Second, the intervention period in our study was relatively short. Previous studies have shown that long-term improvements in cognitive function are positively correlated with an increased number of stimulation sessions (van Lieshout et al., 2019), but the optimal number of treatments has not yet been determined. We speculated that appropriately extending the intervention duration or increasing stimulation frequency would lead to more significant improvements in cognitive function. Furthermore, this study lacks long-term follow-up data for cognitive function assessment and neurophysiological indicators, making it difficult to comprehensively and accurately evaluate the long-term effects of tACS in patients with PSCI.

Even with these limitations, our study demonstrates that theta-frequency HD-tACS can effectively improve cognitive function in patients with PSCI and induce robust neural oscillatory responses in the brain, making it a promising therapeutic approach for cognitive impairment in PSCI. Notably, the individualized stimulation protocol shows great potential for clinical application. Future research should expand the sample size, extend follow-up duration, and adopt a wider range of brain function analysis techniques to generate more robust evidence supporting the application of tACS and its individualized protocols in the treatment of PSCI.

Conclusion

With sham stimulation as the control, this study investigated the effects of ITF and 5 Hz HD-tACS on cognitive function in patients with PSCI, and explored the underlying neuroelectrophysiological mechanisms using EEG. The key findings of this study are as follows: ① Theta-HD-tACS intervention improves the global cognitive function of patients with PSCI, but the clinically meaningful advantage of individualized frequency stimulation remains to be further verified. ② Theta-HD-tACS induces neural oscillations in local (frontal theta) and distal (parietal gamma) brain regions, andITF-tACS may mediate cognitive improvement by modulating frontal theta oscillations(a significant correlation was found between ΔMoCA score and Δtheta power). This provides a crucial empirical basis for the future individualized application of tACS.

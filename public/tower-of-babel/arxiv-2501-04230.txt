Classification of simple quasifinite modules for contact superconformal algebras with N ≠ 4 N\neq 4
Yan-an Cai, Rencai Lü

Abstract
In this paper, we classify all simple jet modules for contact superconformal algebras 𝒦 ⁡ ( N , ϵ ) \mathcal{K}(N;\epsilon) with N ≠ 4 N\neq 4 . Then all simple quasifinite modules for 𝒦 ^ ​ ( N , ϵ ) \widehat{\mathcal{K}}(N;\epsilon) ( N ≠ 4 N\neq 4 ), the universal central extension of 𝒦 ⁡ ( N , ϵ ) \mathcal{K}(N;\epsilon) , are classified. Our results show that Matínez-Zelmanov’s conjecture in [ 26 ] holds for 𝒦 ⁡ ( N , ϵ ) \mathcal{K}(N;\epsilon) ( N ≠ 4 N\neq 4 ).

Introduction

We denote by
\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{Z}_{+},\mathbb{N},\mathbb{C}
and
\mathbb{C}^{*}
the sets of all integers, non-negative integers, positive integers, complex numbers, and nonzero complex numbers, respectively. All vector spaces and algebras in this paper are over
\mathbb{C}
. We denote by
\delta_{i,j}
the Kronecker delta. For a statement
P
, set

\delta_{P}=\begin{cases}0,&\mbox{ if }P\mbox{ does not hold},\\
1,&\mbox{ if }P\mbox{ holds}.\end{cases}

Throughout this paper, by subalgebras, submodules for Lie superalgebras we mean subsuperalgebras and subsupermodules respectively. For
N\in\mathbb{N}
, set
\mathbf{0}:=(0,\ldots,0),\mathbf{1}:=(1,\ldots,1)\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}
and
\overline{1,N}=\{1,2,\ldots,N\}
. For a real number
r
, denote by
\lfloor r\rfloor
the maximal integer smaller than or equal to
r
. Let
\boldsymbol{i}=\sqrt{-1}
.
\Pi
denotes the parity change functor.

In view of importance of the Witt algebra
\mathcal{W}(0)=\mathrm{D}\mathrm{e}\mathrm{r}\mathbb{C}[t^{\pm 1}]
and the Virasoro algebra (the central extension of
\mathcal{W}(0)
) in physics, Neveu, Schwarz
[
28
]
, Ramond
[
30
]
and others considered superextensions of the algebra
\mathcal{W}(0)
. These superextensions became known as
superconformal algebras
. A superconformal algebra (see
[
19
]
) is a simple complex Lie superalgebra such that it contains the Witt algebra as a subalgebra, and has growth
1
. The
\mathbb{Z}
-graded superconformal algebras are ones for which
\mathrm{a}\mathrm{d}\,t\frac{d}{dt}
is diagonalizable with finite dimensional eigenspaces, see
[
19
]
. The main known series of superconformal algebras are
\mathcal{W}(N)(N\geq 0)
, all derivations of
\mathbb{C}[t^{\pm 1}]\otimes\Lambda(N)
, where
\Lambda(N)
is the Grassmann algebra in
N
odd variables, the series
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(N;\alpha)(N\geq 2)
of one-parameter families of deformations of the divergence-free subalgebra of
\mathcal{W}(N)
and the series of
contact superconformal algebras

\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(N;\epsilon)(N\geq 1)
, see
[
18
,
19
]
. The corresponding central extensions were classified in
[
19
]
.

The simplest example is the Virasoro algebra. An important class of modules for the Virasoro algebra are the so-called quasifinite modules (or Harish-Chandra modules), the weight modules with finite dimensional weight spaces, which were classified by O. Mathieu in
[
23
]
. A classification of simple quasifinite modules for
\mathcal{W}(N)
, i.e. simple modules on which
t\frac{d}{dt}
acts diagonally with finite dimensional weight spaces, was given in
[
4
,
36
]
.

In this paper, we will study simple quasifinite modules (also called Harish-Chandra modules, see
[
26
]
) for contact superconformal algebras, i.e.
\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(N;\epsilon)(\epsilon\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{N})
. Contact superconformal algebras have three sectors, Neveu-Schwarz sector (
\epsilon=\mathbf{0}
), Ramond sector (
\epsilon=\mathbf{1}
) and the twisted sector (
\epsilon\in\{0,1\}^{N}\setminus\{\mathbf{0},\mathbf{1}\}
). In
[
19
]
, V. Kac and van de Leuer showed that Ramond sector is isomorphic to Neveu-Schwarz sector when
N
is even, and is isomorphic to the twisted sector when
N
is odd. Hence, there are essentially two different sectors.

The algebra
\mathcal{K}(N;\mathbf{0})
is also the Lie superalgebra of contact vector fields with Laurent polynomials as coefficients, which is characterized by its action on a contact
1
-form (see
[
19
]
); it is known to physicists as the
SO(N)
superconformal algebra
[
1
,
31
]
.
\mathcal{K}(N;\epsilon)
is simple except when
N=4
and

, in which case
\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(4;\epsilon)=[\mathcal{K}(4;\epsilon),\mathcal{K}(4;\epsilon)]
is a simple ideal in
\mathcal{K}(4;\epsilon)
of codimension one.

Let
\widehat{\mathcal{K}}(N;\epsilon)
be the central extension of
\mathcal{K}(N;\epsilon)

N\leq 4

Preliminaries

Consider the associative algebra
\mathcal{A}:=\mathbb{C}[t^{\pm 1}]\otimes\Lambda(N)
, where
\Lambda(N)
is the Grassmann algebra in
N
variables
\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{N}
and
|t|:=\bar{0},|\xi_{i}|:=\bar{1}
. We omit
\otimes
in
\mathcal{A}
for convenience. For any

\xi_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}:=\xi_{i_{1}}\cdots\xi_{i_{k}}
. Also, for any subset
I=\{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}\}\subset\overline{1,N}
, write
\underline{I}=(l_{1},\dots,l_{k})
if
\{l_{1},\dots,l_{k}\}=\{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}\}
and
l_{1}<\dots<l_{k}
. Denote
\xi_{I}:=\xi_{l_{1},\dots,l_{k}}
and set
\xi_{\varnothing}=1
. Denote
\tau(i_{1},\cdots,i_{k})
the inverse order of the sequence
i_{1},\cdots,i_{k}
, and
\tau(I,J)=\tau(\underline{I},\underline{J})=\tau(k_{1},\cdots,k_{s},l_{1},\cdots,l_{r})
when
I\cap J=\emptyset
with
\underline{I}=(k_{1},\cdots,k_{s}),\underline{J}=(l_{1},\cdots,l_{r})
. We set
\tau(\emptyset,\emptyset)=\tau(\emptyset)=0
. Denote
\xi_{I,J}=\xi_{I}\xi_{J},\xi_{i,I}=\xi_{i}\xi_{I},\xi_{I}\xi_{i}=\xi_{I,i}
.

Denote by
\mathcal{W}(N)
the superalgebra of all derivations of
\mathcal{A}
. For
\epsilon=(\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2},\cdots,\epsilon_{N})\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}
, let
\omega_{\epsilon}:=dt-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}t^{\epsilon_{i}}\xi_{i}d\xi_{i}
, which is called a
contact form

[
19
,
18
,
12
]
. The contact superconformal algebras are defined as

\mathcal{K}(N;\epsilon)=\{D\in W(N)\,|\,D\omega_{\epsilon}=P\omega_{\epsilon}\mbox{ for some }P\in\mathcal{A}\}.

Note that
\mathcal{K}(N;\epsilon)
is simple except when
N=4
and
\frac{1}{2}(\epsilon_{1}+\epsilon_{2}+\epsilon_{3}+\epsilon_{4})\in\mathbb{Z}
. Set
\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(N;\epsilon)=[\mathcal{K}(N;\epsilon),\mathcal{K}(N;\epsilon)]
. The following result was given in
[
19
]
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
N\geq 1
.

1.

There are only two non-isomorphic superalgebras
\mathcal{K}(N;\epsilon)
corresponding to
\epsilon=\mathbf{0}
and
(1,0,\ldots,0)
.

2.

\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(N;\epsilon)\cong\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(N;\epsilon^{\prime})
if and only if
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}(\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon^{\prime}_{i})\in 2\mathbb{Z}
.

3.

\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(N;\epsilon)
has no nontrivial
2
-cocycles for
N>4
.

Write
\partial_{t}:=\frac{\partial}{\partial t},\partial_{i}:=\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi_{i}}
. There is one-to-one correspondence between the differential operators in
\mathcal{K}(N;\epsilon)
and the functions in
\mathcal{A}
. The correspondence

is given by

D_{f}:=(\Delta f)D^{\epsilon}+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}D^{\epsilon}(f)\xi_{i}\partial_{i}+(-1)^{|f|}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}t^{-\epsilon_{i}}\partial_{i}(f)\partial_{i},

where
\Delta f:=2f-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\xi_{i}\partial_{i}(f),D^{\epsilon}:=\partial_{t}-\frac{1}{2}t^{-1}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\epsilon_{i}\xi_{i}\partial_{i}
. In particular,
\mathcal{K}(N;\epsilon)
has a
\mathbb{C}
-basis
\{D_{t^{k}\xi_{I}}\,|\,k\in\mathbb{Z},I\subseteq\overline{1,N}\}
with Lie brackets identified with the contact bracket in
\mathcal{A}
:

\{f,g\}:=(\Delta f)D^{\epsilon}(g)-D^{\epsilon}(f)(\Delta g)+(-1)^{|f|}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}t^{-\epsilon_{i}}\partial_{i}(f)\partial_{i}(g),

so that
[D_{f},D_{g}]=D_{\{f,g\}}
. More precisely, we have

\displaystyle[D_{t^{k+1}\xi_{I}},D_{t^{l+1}\xi_{J}}]

(2.1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}0,&|I\cap J|>1,\\
(-1)^{|I|}D_{t^{k+l+2-\epsilon_{i}}\partial_{i}(\xi_{I})\partial_{i}(\xi_{J})},&I\cap J=\{i\},\\
\big((2-|I|)(l+1-\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{j\in J}\epsilon_{j})-(2-|J|)(k+1-\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{i\in I}\epsilon_{i})\big)D_{t^{k+l+1}\xi_{I,J}},&I\cap J=\emptyset.\end{cases}

\mathcal{K}(N;\epsilon)
acts naturally on
\mathcal{A}
, hence we have the extended algebras
\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}(N;\epsilon):=\mathcal{K}(N;\epsilon)\ltimes\mathcal{A}

On the other hand,

is naturally a module over the abelian superalgebra

with the actions

\mathcal{K}(N;\epsilon)

that is,

\{f,hg\}-(-1)^{|h||f|}h\{f,g\}=D_{f}(h)g.

The formula follows from the fact that

is a contact bracket for
\mathcal{A}
(

Lemma 2.3

If

\displaystyle(D_{t^{k+1}\xi_{1}}+\boldsymbol{i}D_{t^{k+1}\xi_{2}})\bar{w}_{r,l,\varepsilon}=r(a+2l+k+\frac{3}{2}(1-\varepsilon))\bar{v}_{0,l+k+1-\varepsilon},

\displaystyle(D_{t^{k+1}\xi_{1}}-\boldsymbol{i}D_{t^{k+1}\xi_{2}})\bar{w}_{r,l,\varepsilon}=(r-1)(a+2l+k+\frac{3}{2}(1-\varepsilon))\bar{v}_{1,l+k+1-\varepsilon},

\displaystyle D_{t^{k+1}\xi_{3}}\bar{w}_{r,l,\varepsilon}=-\frac{1}{2}(a+2l+k+\frac{3}{2}(1-\varepsilon))\bar{v}_{r,l+k+1-\varepsilon},

\displaystyle D_{t^{k+1}\xi_{1}\xi_{2}}\bar{w}_{r,l,\varepsilon}=\boldsymbol{i}(r-\frac{1}{2})\bar{w}_{r,l+k+1-\varepsilon,\varepsilon},\,D_{t^{k+1}\xi_{1}\xi_{2}\xi_{3}}\bar{w}_{r,l,\varepsilon}=\boldsymbol{i}(\frac{1}{2}-r)\bar{v}_{r,l+k+2(1-\varepsilon)},

\displaystyle(D_{t^{k+1}\xi_{1}\xi_{3}}+\boldsymbol{i}D_{t^{k+1}\xi_{2}\xi_{3}})\bar{w}_{r,l,\varepsilon}=-r\bar{w}_{0,l+k+1-\varepsilon,\varepsilon},

\displaystyle(D_{t^{k+1}\xi_{1}\xi_{3}}-\boldsymbol{i}D_{t^{k+1}\xi_{2}\xi_{3}})\bar{w}_{r,l,\varepsilon}=(1-r)\bar{w}_{0,l+k+1-\varepsilon,\varepsilon},

where
r=0,1
and
l,k\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then one can easily check that it is simple.

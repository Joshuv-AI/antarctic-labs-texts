A Multimodal Approach to Open-Angle Glaucoma with Travoprost Intracameral Implant and Minimally Invasive Glaucoma Surgery with or Without Concurrent Cataract Surgery
Authors: Christine Funke, Chloe CoHan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
IntroductionThis study aimed to characterize reductions in intraocular pressure (IOP) and the need for topical IOP-lowering medications in eyes undergoing implantation of a travoprost implant (iDose TR®; Glaukos) and either trabecular micro-bypass stent (iStent infinite®; Glaukos) implant or canaloplasty with or without cataract surgery in eyes with open-angle glaucoma (OAG).MethodsAdults undergoing the procedures of interest with ≥ 3 months of postoperative follow-up were included. The primary outcomes were changes in mean IOP and topical medication use from baseline to 3 months postoperatively. Analyses were conducted in the overall cohort and stratified by surgical context (with and without concurrent cataract surgery) and minimally invasive glaucoma surgery (MIGS) modality (iStent infinite and canaloplasty), resulting in four subgroups: iDose TR + iStent infinite with phacoemulsification, iDose TR + iStent infinite, iDose TR + canaloplasty with phacoemulsification, and iDose TR + canaloplasty.ResultsData from 107 eyes were analyzed. Overall, mean (standard deviation) IOP decreased from 19.0 (5.9) mmHg at baseline to 15.3 (4.6) mmHg at month 3 (p < 0.0001). In the phaco+ subgroup (66 eyes), mean IOP decreased from 17.5 (4.0) mmHg at baseline to 14.3 (2.4) mmHg (p < 0.0001) at month 3; in the phaco− subgroup, (41 eyes), mean IOP decreased from 21.6 (7.6) mmHg at baseline to 16.8 (6.5) mmHg (p = 0.001) at month 3. At month 3, topical medication-free rates were 59.4% overall, 67.7% in the phaco+ subgroup, and 46.3% in the phaco− subgroup. Similar reductions were observed in eyes undergoing iDose TR plus iStent infinite or canaloplasty with/without cataract surgery.ConclusionThe iDose TR travoprost implant combined with either iStent infinite implantation or canaloplasty was associated with significant reductions in IOP and topical IOP-lowering medications in most subgroups, with or without concurrent cataract surgery. These data support the shift to an interventional glaucoma paradigm and the utilization of multimodal therapy for glaucoma management.

Introduction

Interventional glaucoma describes a treatment paradigm in which procedural therapies are utilized early in the course of disease [1–5]. Interventional glaucoma is an emerging alternative to the topical medications-first approach that has characterized glaucoma management for more than 150 years [6]. The glaucoma treatment paradigm is shifting from topical medications to procedural therapies in large part due to the well-described limitations of topical medical therapy. The most significant of these is a high rate of nonadherence [7–11] that significantly increases the risk of progression and preventable vision loss [7, 8, 12, 13]. The interventional glaucoma approach prioritizes early procedural intervention to minimize or eliminate medication nonadherence while preserving visual function and quality of life [3, 4]. Procedural options include selective laser trabeculoplasty (SLT), long-acting sustained-release drug delivery, and minimally invasive glaucoma surgery (MIGS). Used alone or in combination, these therapies can provide improved efficacy [14, 15] and safety [16, 17], and better quality of life [18, 19], than topical medications.

Among the relatively new interventional options for glaucoma management are the travoprost intracameral implant (iDose TR®; Glaukos) and the third-generation trabecular micro-bypass stent (iStent infinite®; Glaukos).

The iDose TR travoprost implant is a novel procedural pharmaceutical that is designed for surgical implantation in the anterior chamber angle. The delivery system consists of a titanium reservoir containing 75 mcg travoprost delivered via sustained-release across a diffusion membrane [20]. The implant has been evaluated in several clinical studies. In the phase 3 pivotal trial comparing iDose TR to topical timolol 0.5% twice daily, mean IOP reductions of 6.6–8.5 mmHg were observed at 3 months [20]. In a follow-up analysis of the phase 3 study data, mean IOP reductions at 12 months were 5.5 mmHg and remained persistently non-inferior to timolol at all time points; at 12 months, 81.1% of iDose TR eyes remained topical medication-free [21]. In a prospective interventional case series of iDose TR at the time of cataract surgery, the mean diurnal IOP reduction at 3 months was 10.6 mmHg and 97% of eyes manifested a ≥ 20% reduction in mean diurnal IOP [22]. In a real-world series of standalone iDose TR in 65 eyes, a mean IOP reduction of 6.5 mmHg was reported, confirming the relevance of the phase 3 study results to broad clinical use [23].

The iStent infinite system represents a trabecular micro-bypass that enhances aqueous humor outflow through the conventional trabecular outflow pathway. The system consists of three identical implants designed for surgical implantation in the anterior chamber angle with spacing approximately 2–3 clock hours apart to provide multiple aqueous egress points accessing multiple sectors of Schlemm’s canal [24]. The procedure can be performed as standalone therapy or in combination with cataract surgery and/or other glaucoma procedures. As standalone therapy, mean IOP reductions at 12 months were 5.5 mmHg in eyes with a history of prior failed glaucoma surgery and 8.1 mmHg in surgically naïve eyes on maximal tolerated medical therapy [24]. In a real-world retrospective multicenter study of iStent infinite combined with cataract surgery in eyes controlled with topical medications (mean baseline IOP 16.9 mmHg; mean medications 1.26 per eye), mean IOP reduction was 3.1 mmHg (p = 0.001) and mean medication reduction was 0.65 (p = 0.001); the proportions of eyes attaining < 18 mmHg, ≤ 15 mmHg, and ≤ 12 mmHg at month 12 were 92.7%, 71.9%, and 37.5%, respectively [25]. In another study of iStent infinite in combination with cataract surgery, 12-month IOP reductions of 3.5–4.3 mmHg were reported [26].

Ab interno canaloplasty is another MIGS approach that targets outflow resistance both proximally, by reducing resistance at the trabecular meshwork–Schlemm canal interface, and distally, by circumferentially viscodilating Schlemm’s canal and improving access to the collector channels and episcleral venous system. Clinical studies evaluating canaloplasty using devices such as the iTrack microcatheter and the OMNI surgical system have demonstrated significant reductions in intraocular pressure and medication burden, both as standalone procedures and in combination with cataract surgery [27–30].

While the efficacy and safety profiles of most individual interventional glaucoma options have been well characterized when used on their own, less is known regarding the efficacy and safety of various combinations of these therapies when they are utilized simultaneously as combination therapy [31]. IOP is determined by the balance of aqueous inflow and outflow through two distinct outflow pathways, trabecular and uveoscleral. A multimechanism approach targeting some or all of these determinants of IOP can provide more comprehensive IOP management [31]. Likewise, a multimodal approach can take advantage of the various attributes of multiple modes of both IOP and medication reduction [31]. When deployed simultaneously, a multimechanism and multimodal regimen can decrease treatment burden, cost, inconvenience, and risk [31].

There is significant unmet need for better characterization of the clinical utility of multimechanism and multimodal therapy for glaucoma, which most patients will require for glaucoma management over their lifetimes [15, 32–35]. In this study, we describe our experience combining implantation of the iDose TR travoprost intracameral implant and a MIGS procedure (iStent infinite implantation or canaloplasty) with or without concurrent cataract surgery in eyes with open-angle glaucoma. This approach targets both the trabecular outflow system (MIGS) and the uveoscleral outflow system (iDose TR) simultaneously and may offer the potential for more comprehensive IOP control.

Methods

This was a retrospective, single-surgeon case series. Data were retrospectively drawn from the existing health records of qualifying patients. The analysis was performed in accordance with the Helsinki Declaration of 1964 and its later amendments. The study protocol was reviewed and approved by the Advarra ethics committee. While all patients gave informed consent to undergo surgery prior to the procedure, a separate informed consent for later inclusion in this chart review was waived given this was a retrospective anonymized analysis of existing clinical data (Waiver Pro#00093225).

Data were collected from the electronic health records of all adult patients (age 18 years and older) with open-angle glaucoma undergoing implantation of a travoprost intracameral implant in combination with iStent infinite implantation or canaloplasty, with or without concurrent cataract surgery. Prior treatment with topical IOP-lowering medications and/or selective laser trabeculoplasty was permitted, but prior incisional IOP-lowering surgical intervention was excluded. Only patients with at least 3 months of follow-up were included. If both eyes of a patient met eligibility criteria, both were included.

Data extracted from health records included age, preoperative IOP-lowering therapy, baseline IOP, and results of structural and functional glaucoma testing. Intraoperative data included the type(s) of concomitant procedure(s) performed. Postoperative data included IOP, medication use, and adverse events through 3 months of follow-up.

Patients were stratified according to (1) surgical context (with and without concurrent phacoemulsification) and (2) MIGS modality (iStent infinite and canaloplasty), resulting in four analytical subgroups; iDose TR + iStent infinite with phacoemulsification, iDose TR + iStent infinite, iDose TR + canaloplasty with phacoemulsification, and iDose TR + canaloplasty.

Glaucoma procedures (canaloplasty, iDose TR implantation, iStent infinite implantation) were performed following standard phacoemulsification cataract extraction and intraocular lens implant insertion. Following cataract surgery, canaloplasty was performed before iDose TR implantation while iStent infinite implantation was performed after iDose TR implantation. The implantation techniques for iDose TR and iStent infinite followed the manufacturer’s instructions for use [36, 37] and have been described in detail previously [21, 24]. In brief, the iStent infinite injector was inserted through the temporal clear corneal incision and advanced under direct gonioscopy to the nasal trabecular meshwork, where three stents were implanted into Schlemm’s canal with 2 clock-hours’ spacing between each. The injector was removed from the eye between insertions in some cases to optimize spacing. Canaloplasty was performed in standard fashion using the iTrack microcatheter (Nova Eye Medical Limited) or the OMNI surgical system (Sight Sciences) as described previously [38, 39]. With the iTrack system, the microcatheter was inserted into Schlemm’s canal through a micro-goniotomy fashioned using a 25-gauge needle and advanced through 360° under direct gonioscopic visualization of the red fiber optic light to ensure proper positioning throughout the procedure. Following complete cannulation, the microcatheter was slowly withdrawn while viscoelastic was delivered through Schlemm’s canal to achieve dilation. With the OMNI system, access to Schlemm’s canal is achieved via an ab interno goniotomy, followed by circumferential catheterization and viscodilation. The system also allows trabeculotomy to be performed through the same access. Despite differences in delivery systems, the primary therapeutic goal of viscodilation of Schlemm’s canal and distal outflow pathways was consistent across both approaches. No goniotomy was completed in any of the cases. After completing canaloplasty or before iStent infinite insertion, the iDose TR inserter was introduced through the temporal incision and advanced across the anterior chamber towards the nasal angle, where the implant was passed through the trabecular meshwork to anchor the implant securely within the sclera. Proper seating and positioning of the stents and implant were confirmed under intraoperative gonioscopy. Intracameral moxifloxacin and dextenza were administered. Viscoelastic was then removed and sealing of the corneal incision was ensured. All IOP-lowering medications were held at the time of surgery and re-instituted as needed at follow-up visits depending on patients’ individual IOP targets.

The primary statistical objective of this analysis was to characterize reductions from baseline to month 3 in both IOP and the need for IOP-lowering medications following combined travoprost implantation and MIGS with or without concurrent cataract surgery. These outcomes were analyzed using paired t tests, and the level of significance was taken to be p < 0.05. The analyses were conducted on the entire group as well as on the subgroups in which cataract surgery was and was not concurrently performed. Further subgroup analyses examined eyes receiving a third-generation trabecular micro-bypass stent (iStent infinite; Glaukos) and those undergoing canaloplasty. Safety analysis consisted of tabulating the nature and incidence of adverse events. The sample size was constrained to all cases of travoprost implantation since its approval by the US Food and Drug Administration meeting eligibility criteria outlined above. Therefore, no formal power/sample size analysis was undertaken. Means are presented with their standard deviations (SD).

Results

Most were using one (39.3%) or two (39.3%) topical IOP-lowering medications at baseline. The mean visual field mean deviation in analyzed eyes was − 7.3 (6.6) dB, and the mean retinal nerve fiber layer thickness was 65.7 (14.0) µm. Of the total sample, 66 eyes (61.7%) underwent concurrent phacoemulsification cataract surgery at the time of iDose TR implantation (the phaco+ group) and 41 eyes (38.3%) did not (the phaco− group). Within the phaco+ subgroup (66 eyes), mean IOP decreased from 17.5 (4.0) mmHg at baseline to 14.3 (2.4) mmHg (p < 0.0001) at month 3 (Fig. 1a), and the mean number of medications decreased from 1.5 (0.66) to 0.42 (0.70) respectively (p < 0.0001) (Fig. 1b and Table 2). Among eyes undergoing iDose TR plus canaloplasty with cataract surgery (n = 47), mean IOP decreased from 17.4 (4.4) mmHg at baseline to 14.1 (2.4) mmHg (p < 0.0001) at month 3 (Fig. 2a), and the mean number of medications decreased from 1.60 (0.71) to 0.52 (0.78), respectively (p < 0.0001) (Fig. 2b and Table 3). At month 3, 60.9% of eyes were topical medication-free (versus 4.3% preoperatively). Among eyes undergoing iDose TR plus canaloplasty without cataract surgery (n = 22), mean IOP decreased from 20.6 (7.7) mmHg at baseline to 19.5 (6.7) mmHg (p = 0.423) at month 3 (Fig. 2a), and the mean number of medications decreased from 2.00 (1.16) to 0.91 (0.92), respectively (p = 0.001) (Fig. 2b and Table 3). At month 3, 40.9% of eyes were topical medication-free (versus 18.2% preoperatively).

Safety was favorable. One intraoperative adverse event occurred: an endothelial detachment noted during canaloplasty which was repaired intraoperatively; no further surgery or sequelae occurred. No postoperative adverse events were noted in any eye during the 3-month follow-up period.

Discussion

As the glaucoma treatment paradigm shifts toward early intervention, an evidence-based approach to procedural glaucoma management will require data from studies evaluating these procedures’ efficacy safety profiles on their own and relative to one another. Further, as most patients with glaucoma will require multiple therapies in combination to achieve adequate disease control [15, 32–35], there is also the need for data characterizing the utility of multimodal therapy. The current study has demonstrated that combining iDose TR implantation with a MIGS procedure, with or without concurrent cataract surgery, provides statistically significant reductions in both IOP and the need for IOP-lowering medications, with the majority of patients (59%) being medication-free 3 months postoperatively.

This is among the first studies of which we are aware that explores the utility of a simultaneous multimechanism and multimodal approach to comprehensive IOP management including a procedural pharmaceutical. The combination of iDose TR—which lowers IOP pharmacologically via enhancement of uveoscleral outflow—and an angle-based MIGS procedure—which lowers IOP surgically via increased trabecular outflow—represents both dual mechanism and dual modality therapy. Multimechanism and multimodal therapy provides additive IOP control by targeting complementary IOP-lowering strategies [31]. This is accomplished without the responsibility of daily self-administration of topical medical therapy in most eyes, which in turn reduces or eliminates the risk of glaucoma progression associated with nonadherence [7, 8, 12, 13]. Other studies have demonstrated the benefits of simultaneous multimodal/multimechanism therapies (laser or surgery plus topical medications) that include the Early Manifest Glaucoma Trial (argon laser trabeculoplasty and betaxolol) [40], as well as studies of simultaneous first-generation [41] iStent or second-generation [42] iStent inject implantation plus topical travoprost.

In the phaco+ group, most patients underwent surgery primarily for visual rehabilitation and concurrent glaucoma procedures were performed primarily to reduce the topical medication burden. As expected, topical medication use was substantially reduced in this group. Importantly, this reduction in medications was not associated with an increase in IOP—in fact, mean IOP was significantly reduced by approximately 3.2 mmHg. In the phaco− group, the need for IOP reduction was the likely impetus for surgery in most eyes. In this group, mean IOP was significantly reduced by approximately 4.8 mmHg, and this IOP reduction was achieved with significantly fewer topical medications postoperatively.

The two concomitant MIGS procedures performed in this study were iStent infinite implantation and canaloplasty. In eyes undergoing iDose TR plus iStent infinite implantation, both IOP and the need for medication use were statistically significantly decreased both with or without cataract surgery. Eyes receiving iDose TR plus canaloplasty also demonstrated statistically significant IOP and medication reductions postoperatively when combined with cataract surgery, while medication use (but not IOP) was significantly reduced in eyes not undergoing concurrent cataract surgery. These observations support that iDose TR can be combined with a variety of MIGS procedures, with or without cataract surgery, to achieve individualized surgical goals that include IOP reduction, medication reduction, or both.

The relatively modest IOP reduction observed in the iDose TR plus canaloplasty standalone subgroup warrants consideration of broader procedural context. First, phacoemulsification itself is known to contribute to modest IOP reduction, which could partly explain the more pronounced effect observed in the combined group. Second, the sample size in the standalone subgroup was relatively small (n = 22), which may limit statistical power and increase variability in outcomes. Third, eyes in the standalone group had higher baseline IOP and potentially more advanced or treatment-resistant disease, which may reduce responsiveness to these interventions. Finally, glaucoma medications were reduced postoperatively, which could impact the degree of IOP reduction achieved (e.g., such as removing a prostaglandin postoperatively). This is consistent with real-world clinical practice, where surgical goals often differ based on patients’ clinical picture (e.g., some patients desiring medication reduction and others needing IOP reduction).

Beyond these considerations, the near-equivalence of IOP outcomes across modalities in the phaco+ groups may reflect the shared IOP-lowering effect of phacoemulsification. This is consistent with findings from Kerr et al., who reported comparable outcomes between canaloplasty and trabecular bypass primarily in eyes undergoing concomitant phacoemulsification [43].

In contrast, in the standalone setting, where this shared effect is absent, differences between procedural approaches may become more apparent. Trabecular bypass provides direct access to Schlemm’s canal, whereas canaloplasty relies on distal outflow pathway integrity, which may vary between patients. In this cohort, canaloplasty cases were disproportionately represented in the standalone group and associated with greater baseline medication burden, suggesting non-random allocation. This distribution bias should be considered when interpreting the divergence observed in Fig. 2a.

This study provides complementary data to the phase 3 studies evaluating the iDose TR travoprost implant as monotherapy [20, 44]. In a pooled analysis of data from both phase 3 studies, mean IOP reductions from unmedicated baseline (approx. 24 mmHg) over the first 12 months ranged from 6.8–8.5 mmHg and 81.4% of eyes were topical medication-free at 12 months [45]. Differences in outcomes in the phase 3 studies and the current study are likely attributable to differences in study design and treatment goals between the study samples. For example, the phase 3 studies evaluated outcomes from a washed-out baseline, whereas the present real-world study did not include washout. Additionally, most phase 3 patients were using ≤ 1 medication at study entry, while most patients in the current study were using ≥ 2 medications at the time of surgery. Patients in the current study had a greater initial topical medication burden, and for these patients, the surgical goal was often reduction but not elimination of the topical medication burden. Despite this more modest goal, the majority of patients in the current study were topical medication-free at the 3-month time point.

This study addresses an important unmet need for data supporting the use of multimodal therapy for glaucoma management. Very little data on simultaneous multimodal therapy are available. The inclusion of all consecutive qualifying cases since the commercialization of iDose TR is an added strength that minimizes selection bias. Inclusion of eyes that did and did not undergo concurrent cataract surgery provides additional information on additivity while controlling for the known IOP and medication reductions that cataract surgery alone can provide [46].

Limitations include the retrospective study design and the modest sample size, which are common limitations of real-world early experience studies. Study follow-up is also relatively short, at 3 months; however, this timing is justified given that it is the standard endpoint for any ophthalmic pharmaceutical, and the dataset remains valuable as an early-term indicator of longer-term efficacy. The predominance of canaloplasty cases in this study, including the use of both iTrack and OMNI systems, introduces procedural heterogeneity; however, this heterogeneity reflects real-world utilization patterns and increases the generalizability and relevance of the dataset, as it constitutes a real-world cross-section of combination iDose usage in a single US private practice. Visual field and visual acuity data were not consistently available at standardized time points across all patients, so we did not include these parameters as they may introduce heterogeneity and limit the reliability of comparative analysis; however, this may be a fruitful area for future analyses with longer-term follow-up. Gender/sex and race/ethnicity data were not included in the current data collection but could be considered in future analyses. The current study was designed as a descriptive real-world analysis of combined multimodal therapy rather than a comparative trial, but future studies could potentially explore comparative outcomes in a randomized statistically powered prospective analysis. One additional limitation is the lack of a medication-free baseline IOP value; this was a retrospective study, and presurgical washout of IOP-lowering medications is not the current standard of care.

Conclusions

In summary, the iDose TR travoprost implant combined with a MIGS procedure provides significant reduction in both IOP and the need for topical IOP-lowering medications in nearly all subgroups, with or without concurrent cataract surgery. The magnitude of IOP reduction varied slightly by surgical context and procedural modality, while medication reduction was similarly significant across all subgroups. These findings provide early real-world evidence supporting the use of a multimodal approach in glaucoma management.

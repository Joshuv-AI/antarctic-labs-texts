Draft genome data of Salmonella enterica subsp. enterica serovar Dublin strain S08 from Vietnam
Authors: Hoang Duc Le, Duong Huy Nguyen, Thi Hong Ha Nguyen, Nhu Quynh Nguyen, Minh Tuan Nguyen, Trung My Tran, Hung Tuan Nguyen, Ngoc Bich Pham
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis dataset presents the draft genome sequence and functional annotation of Salmonella enterica subsp. enterica serovar Dublin strain S08, sequenced using the PacBio Onso platform. De novo assembly produced a 4967,012 bp genome in 50 contigs with a GC content of 52.11%. Structural annotation identified 4790 coding sequences (CDSs), 75 tRNA genes, and 4 rRNA genes. ANI analysis confirmed assignment to S. enterica subsp. enterica serovar Dublin (99.97–100% identity to reference Dublin genomes), a classification further supported by in-silico MLST, which assigned the strain to ST10 (clonal complex 53). Functional annotation assigned 4398 genes to at least one of the COG, KEGG, or GO databases. VFDB screening detected 176 virulence-associated genes, including SPI-1/SPI-2 type III secretion system components. CARD profiling revealed 40 antimicrobial resistance determinants, including multidrug efflux systems and beta-lactamase genes. MOB-suite analysis identified three mobile genetic element clusters, including a putative conjugative plasmid carrying IncC, IncFII, and IncX1 replicons. This dataset provides a reusable genomic resource for studies of virulence, antimicrobial resistance, and genomic adaptation in Salmonella enterica.Keywords: Salmonella enterica, Whole-genome sequencing, Antimicrobial resistance, Mobilome, Virulence factors

Background

Salmonella enterica subsp. enterica serovar Dublin is a cattle-adapted zoonotic serovar predominantly associated with cattle, but it can also cause invasive bloodstream infections and severe disease in humans [1]. The virulence of Salmonella is largely mediated by Salmonella pathogenicity islands (SPIs), which encode type III secretion systems, together with other determinants involved in intracellular survival, membrane remodeling, efflux activity, and the regulation of virulence gene expression [2,3].

The worldwide emergence of multidrug-resistant Salmonella strains, including those carrying extended-spectrum beta-lactamases, AmpC enzymes, and plasmid-associated resistance determinants, represents an ongoing public health concern [4,5]. Whole-genome sequencing is now widely used for high-resolution genomic epidemiology, antimicrobial resistance characterization, and mobilome analysis of Salmonella isolates [6,7]. In addition, functional annotation based on COG, KEGG, and Gene Ontology frameworks helps describe the metabolic potential and adaptive features encoded in bacterial genomes [8,9].

Despite the clinical relevance of S. Dublin, integrated genomic resources for isolates from Southeast Asia remain relatively scarce. In the present study, we report the draft genome of Salmonella enterica subsp. enterica serovar Dublin strain S08, isolated from the lung tissue of a dead bovine on a cattle farm in Nghe An Province, Vietnam, that had shown clinical signs consistent with salmonellosis, including depression, anorexia, nasal discharge, high fever (>41 °C), and diarrhea, and died within 48 h after the onset of clinical signs. This genome provides a reusable resource for comparative and functional genomic analyses of S. Dublin, particularly for isolates originating from the Southeast Asian region.

Data Description

The draft genome of Salmonella enterica subsp. enterica serovar Dublin S08 was assembled into 50 contigs with a total genome size of 4967,012 bp, a N50 of 494,170 bp, and a GC content of 52.11%. As summarized in Table 1, genome validation yielded a completeness of 99.1% (CheckM2) and 98.9% (BUSCO) with a contamination of 0.06%, confirming the technical reliability of the dataset.

Whole-genome phylogenetic analysis using TYGS placed strain S08 within the Salmonella enterica subsp. enterica clade, grouping it closest to Salmonella enteritidis ATCC 13,076 and Salmonella choleraesuis DSM 14,846 (Fig. 2A). Consistent with this placement, digital DNA–DNA hybridization (dDDH) analysis via the integrated GGDC returned a highest value of 95.9% (formula d4) to S. enterica subsp. enterica serovar Enteritidis ATCC 13,076, well above both the 70% species and ∼79% subspecies thresholds, thereby confirming the assignment of S08 to S. enterica subsp. enterica at the whole-genome level. The closest TYGS/GGDC match was serovar Enteritidis rather than Dublin because the TYGS type-strain database does not currently include a Dublin genome; Enteritidis and Dublin belong to the same closely related lineage (both group D1, O:9 serovars), so this result is fully consistent with the subspecies assignment.

Serovar-level identity was resolved by two complementary approaches. First, ANI comparisons showed that S08 shares 99.97–100% ANI with all three S. enterica subsp. enterica serovar Dublin reference strains (CVMN53043, SAL-20–0015, and RM079), substantially higher than the values obtained against S. enteritidis (98.73–99.44%) and Salmonella typhimurium (98.77–98.78%) reference genomes, and hierarchical clustering united S08 exclusively within the Dublin sub-clade (Fig. 2B). Second, in-silico 7-locus MLST identified all seven housekeeping alleles at 100% identity (aroC-5, dnaN-2, hemD-3, hisD-6, purE-5, sucA-5, thrA-10; Table 2), assigning S08 to ST10 (clonal complex 53), the sequence type characteristic of the Dublin sub-clade. Together, the dDDH, ANI, and MLST results provide concordant, multi-level support for the classification of strain S08 as S. enterica subsp. enterica serovar Dublin.

Functional annotation using eggNOG-mapper v2 assigned 4398 genes (91.8% of 4790 CDSs) to at least one of three reference databases: COG, KEGG, and GO (Fig. 3). The largest annotation set was COG (4389 genes, 91.6%), followed by GO (2753 genes, 57.5%) and KEGG (2403 genes, 50.2%). The intersection of all three annotation frameworks comprised 1859 genes (38.8% of annotated CDSs). Cross-database comparison revealed 539 genes jointly annotated by COG and KEGG but absent from GO, and 893 genes shared between COG and GO but lacking KEGG assignments. A total of 1099 genes (25.0% of annotated CDSs) were classified exclusively by COG. Only 6 genes were unique to KEGG and 2 exclusively by GO.

Virulence factor screening using ABRicate against VFDB identified 176 hits (coverage: 75.05%–100%; sequence identity: 64.05%–100%) distributed across five functional categories in the Salmonella enterica subsp. enterica serovar Dublin S08 genome (Table 3, Table S1). The largest category comprised SPI-1/SPI-2-associated type III secretion system components and effectors (79 hits, 44.9%), including sopE2, steC, sseJ, avrA, invA, and sipA. Other virulence-associated genes totaled 39 hits (22.2%), encompassing kdsA, galU, ddhA, IlpA, clpP, and gtrA. Flagella and motility determinants were represented by 24 hits (13.6%) including flgI, flgG, flgC, cheB, cheR, and fliC. Adherence and fimbriae-associated genes accounted for 21 hits (11.9%) including members of the csg operon (csgA, csgB, csgC, csgE) and pefA. Iron acquisition determinants totaled 13 hits (7.4%) comprising iroN, fepA, fes, entF, and fepC.

Resistome profiling against CARD identified 40 unique antimicrobial resistance (AMR)-associated determinants from 56 total hits, presented in Fig. 6. The profile comprised 25 efflux/regulatory-associated genes including RND systems (acrA, acrB, tolC, acrD, acrE, acrF), MFS transporters (emrA, emrB, tetA, floR, mdfA, mdtG, mdtH, mdtM), and ABC transporters (msbA, yojI). Acquired resistance genes included floR, sul2, tet(A), and aminoglycoside-modifying enzyme genes aac (6′)-Iy, aph (3′')-Ib, and aph (6)-Id (Table S2).

MOB-suite analysis predicted three mobile genetic element-associated clusters in the 50-contig draft assembly (Table 4, Table S3). The largest element (cluster AA631; 114,202 bp; 2 contigs) carried multi-replicon signatures (IncC, IncFII, IncX1), relaxase types MOBF and MOBP, and conjugation system markers dominated by MPF_T, consistent with a putative conjugative plasmid. Its closest MASH neighbor was a Salmonella enterica serovar Dublin plasmid (CP032450; distance ∼ 0.00,314). A second element (cluster AA627; 51,405 bp; 6 contigs) encoded two MOBH relaxases and an MPF_F system, with multiple contigs enriched in insertion sequences and transposons (IS1071, ISCsp2, ISPam4, TnAs2, TnAs3, Tn3). The nearest MASH neighbor of AA627 was a Salmonella Dublin reference (CP032380; distance = 0.017). A third, small element (cluster AB445; 5269 bp) carried rep_cluster_2350 with a MOBP relaxase and was predicted as mobilizable, with nearest neighbor S. enteritidis plasmid (NC_003457; distance = 0.001).

Experimental Design, Materials and Methods

Isolation and culture of strain S08

The Salmonella enterica subsp. enterica serovar Dublin strain S08 was isolated in 2025 from the lung tissue of a dead bovine from a cattle farm in Nghe An Province. The animal exhibited clinical signs consistent with salmonellosis, including depression, anorexia, nasal discharge, high fever (>41 °C), and diarrhea, and died within 48 h after the onset of clinical signs. The sample was submitted to the diagnostic laboratory of the Central Veterinary Company Limited, where it was confirmed positive for Salmonella spp. through routine bacteriological and biochemical examinations. Briefly, lung tissue samples were aseptically collected and pre-enriched in buffered peptone water (BPW) at 37 °C for 18–24 h. The enriched culture was subsequently streaked onto selective media, including Xylose Lysine Deoxycholate (XLD) agar and Brilliant Green Agar (BGA), and incubated at 37 °C for 24 h. Presumptive Salmonella colonies exhibiting characteristic morphology were purified and further identified using standard biochemical tests and serological confirmation according to the Kauffmann–White scheme. The isolate was designated as strain S08 and stored at −80 °C in glycerol stock for subsequent genomic analysis.

Sequencing, quality control, and genome assembly

For genome sequencing, genomic DNA of Salmonella enterica subsp. enterica serovar Dublin strain S08 was extracted from bacterial biomass cultured in Enterobacteriaceae Enrichment Mannitol (EEM) broth and chemically inactivated with formalin prior to DNA purification using the Wizard Genomic DNA Purification Kit (Promega), according to the manufacturer’s instructions. DNA quality and purity were evaluated by NanoDrop spectrophotometry (A₂₆₀/A₂₈₀ ratio of 1.8–2.0 and A₂₆₀/A₂₃₀ ratio >2.0) and verified by 1% (w/v) agarose gel electrophoresis.

Whole-genome sequencing libraries of Salmonella strain S08 were prepared using the NEBNext dsDNA Fragmentase and the NEBNext Ultra II DNA Library Prep Kit for Illumina (NEB, USA) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. The libraries were subsequently sequenced using paired-end 150-bp chemistry on the PacBio Onso short-read sequencing platform, which employs sequencing-by-binding (SBB) technology to generate highly accurate short-read data. Raw reads were subjected to quality control and adapter trimming using fastp v0.23.2 [10] with automatic adapter detection, quality filtering (Phred score ≥ 20), and minimum read length 50 bp. De novo genome assembly was performed using SPAdes v4.2.0 [11] with the careful flag to minimize mismatches and single-nucleotide errors. Assembly quality metrics including N50, L50, total length, and contig count were evaluated using QUAST v5.3.0 [12]. Genome completeness and contamination were assessed using BUSCO v6.0. [13] against the gammaproteobacteria_odb10 lineage dataset and CheckM2 v1.1.0 [14] using the predict workflow.

Phylogenomic analysis, average nucleotide identity, and MLST

To determine the taxonomic position of Salmonella S08 within the genus, whole-genome phylogenetic analysis was performed using Type Strain Genome Server (TYGS; https://tygs.dsmz.de/) which computes intergenomic distances and reconstructs a phylogenomic tree based on the Genome BLAST Distance Phylogeny (GBDP) method [15]. In parallel, digital DNA–DNA hybridization (dDDH) values were computed using the Genome-to-Genome Distance Calculator (GGDC) integrated into TYGS (formula d4), with species- and subspecies-level boundaries set at 70% and ∼79%, respectively [16].

Average Nucleotide Identity (ANI) values were calculated between strain S08 and each reference genome using the ANIb (BLAST-based ANI) algorithm via the JSpeciesWS web server (https://jspecies.ribohost.com/jspeciesws/#analyse, accessed on 15 April 2026) [17], performing pairwise genome-wide comparisons with the species-level boundary defined at 95%. Furthermore, to support the serovar-level identification, in-silico Multi-Locus Sequence Typing (MLST) was conducted using the mlst command-line tool (v2.35.0) (https://github.com/tseemann/mlst, accessed on 6 July 2026). The genome was queried against the seven Salmonella housekeeping loci (aroC, dnaN, hemD, hisD, purE, sucA, thrA) hosted in the PubMLST database (https://pubmlst.org/, accessed on 6 July 2026) to determine the allelic profile and assign the sequence type (ST) [18].

Structural genome annotation and visualization

Structural annotation of the assembled genome was performed using Prokka v1.14.6 [19] with the options '–kingdom Bacteria –genus Salmonella –usegenus', enabling species-specific reference protein databases. Prokka integrates Prodigal for gene prediction and BLAST/HMMER for functional assignment to identify CDS, tRNA genes, rRNA genes, and other non-coding features. The circular genome map was generated using GenoVi [20], a Python-based visualization tool integrating Circos [21] for track-based genome rendering. COG classification for visualization was performed internally by GenoVi using DeepNOG [22] with the cog2020 database.

Functional annotation

Functional annotation of predicted proteins was performed using eggNOG-mapper v2 [9] against the eggNOG 5.0 database with DIAMOND as the alignment tool. COG (Clusters of Orthologous Groups) categories were extracted and cross-validated using COGclassifier (https://github.com/moshi4/COGclassifier) against the COG2020 database. KEGG Orthology (KO) identifiers and pathway annotations [8] were extracted from the KEGG Pathway and BRITE columns of the annotation output. Gene Ontology (GO) term identifiers were resolved by mapping against the go-basic.obo ontology file using the goatools Python library [23]. Visualization of COG distributions was performed using Matplotlib [24]. The overlap among the three datasets (COG, GO, and KEGG) was summarized and visualized as a Venn diagram using the EVenn web tool (https://www.bic.ac.cn/EVenn/).

Resistome profiling and virulence gene detection

Antimicrobial resistance (AMR) and virulence factor genes were identified using ABRicate v1.0.1 (GitHub. https://github.com/tseemann/abricate) against the Comprehensive Antibiotic Resistance Database (CARD) [25] and the Virulence Factor Database (VFDB) full datasets [26], respectively, with strict parameters: minimum coverage ≥ 75% and minimum sequence identity ≥ 50%. Identified AMR genes were classified according to their primary mechanistic categories (efflux pumps, beta-lactamases, lipid A modification, regulatory genes, aminoglycoside-modifying enzymes). Virulence determinants were classified by functional category (T3SS/pathogenicity islands, motility, adherence, iron acquisition).

Mobilome characterization

Plasmid reconstruction and typing were performed using MOB-suite v3.1.9 [27], specifically the mob_recon module, which integrates BLAST+ [28] alignment against curated databases of plasmid replicons (rep), relaxases (mob), and mate-pair formation systems (mpf) to classify mobile elements. Contigs were classified as chromosomal or plasmid-derived based on replicon detection and MASH distance analysis [29] against the MOB-suite plasmid reference database. Plasmid mobility was predicted as conjugative (relaxase + complete MPF system), mobilizable (relaxase only), or non-mobilizable. Insertion sequences associated with each cluster were annotated from the MOB-suite contig report output.

Limitations

The dataset is based on the draft genome sequencing of a single isolate. A draft genome assembled into multiple contigs cannot fully resolve chromosomal and plasmid architecture, including contig connectivity, gene order, and plasmid backbone completeness. In addition, a single genome cannot capture the broader genomic diversity within Salmonella enterica subsp. enterica serovar Dublin, including its pan-genome variation. Furthermore, predictions of antimicrobial resistance and virulence genes based on in silico analyses should be interpreted as genomic potential only, as phenotypic resistance and virulence require confirmation by antimicrobial susceptibility testing and relevant in vitro or in vivo assays.

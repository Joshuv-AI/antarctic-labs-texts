The four uniform completions of a unital archimedean vector lattice Thanks: File name: Pointfree relative uniform convergence.tex
Richard N. Ball, Anthony W. Hager

Abstract
. In the category 𝐕 \mathbf{V} of unital archimedean vector lattices, four notions of uniform completeness obtain. In all cases completeness requires the convergence of uniformly Cauchy sequences; the completions are distinguished by the manner in which the convergence is regulated. • Ordinary uniform convergence is regulated by the canonical unit 1 1 . • Inner relative uniform convergence, here termed iru-convergence , is regulated by an arbitrary positive element. • Outer relative uniform convergence, here termed oru-convergence , is regulated by an arbitrary positive element of a vector lattice containing the given object as a sub-vector lattice. • ∗ * -convergence is equivalent to ordinary uniform convergence on certain specified quotients of the vector lattice. In each case the complete objects form a full monoreflective subcategory of 𝐕 \mathbf{V} , denoted respectively 𝐮𝐜𝐕 \mathbf{ucV} , 𝐢𝐫𝐮𝐜𝐕 \mathbf{irucV} , 𝐨𝐫𝐮𝐜𝐕 \mathbf{orucV} , and ∗ 𝐜𝐕 \mathbf{*cV} . In this article we provide a unified development of these completions by means of a novel pointfree variant of the classical Yosida adjunction. In this adjunction, the geometric counterpart of a given vector lattice G G is a compactification, i.e., a dense frame surjection q : K → M q\colon K\to M with compact domain. G G is then represented as a vector lattice of compacification morphisms ( g ′ , g ) : p → q (g^{\prime},g)\colon p\to q , where p : 𝒪 ​ ℝ ¯ → 𝒪 ​ ℝ p\colon\mathcal{O}\overline{\mathbb{R}}\to\mathcal{O}\mathbb{R} is the compacification which is the frame counterpart of the inclusion of the reals ℝ \mathbb{R} in the extended reals ℝ ¯ \overline{\mathbb{R}} . We require that a compactification morphism make the diagram commute. 𝒪 ​ ℝ ¯ {\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{O}\overline{\mathbb{R}}} K {\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K} 𝒪 ​ ℝ {\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{O}\mathbb{R}} M {\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M} g ′ \scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g^{\prime}} p \scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p} q \scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q} g \scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g} This representation is well suited to our purposes, for it provides insight into the distinctions among the uniform completions of a vector lattice G G . Passage to the ordinary uniform completion of G G does not change the frames K K or M M , but does fill in all possibilities for entries g ′ g^{\prime} in the top arrow of the diagram. Passage to the inner relative uniform completion of G G typically enlarges the frame K K but does not change M M , and fills in all possibilities for bounded entries g g in the bottom arrow of the diagram. Passage to the ∗ * -completion does not change M M but enlarges K K to the compact (Čech-Stone) coreflection of M M and fills in all possibilities for g g and g ′ g^{\prime} . The passage to the outer relative uniform completion of G G is an enormous enlargement. M M is enlarged to its P P -frame reflection and K K is replaced by the compact (Čech-Stone) coreflection of M M , and all possibilities for g g and g ′ g^{\prime} are included. In fact, this completion is the largest monoreflection of G G , namely its epicompletion.

1.
Introduction

Relative uniform convergence must play a central role in any consideration of archimedean vector lattices, for it is precisely the closure of a convex sub-vector lattice with respect to this convergence which characterizes the archimedeanness of the quotient. Likewise the corresponding completions occupy a central position in the theory. There are four such completions of a given archimedean vector lattice. The first of these, classical uniform completion with regulator
1
, is sufficiently familiar as to receive scant further attention in the article in hand; the Stone Weierstrass Theorem is mentioned only in the proofs of Proposition
6.9
and Theorem
6.11
. Nor shall we concern ourselves much with the fourth completion, the so called
*
-completion.
Introduced in
[
9
]
with a greatly simplified proof of its central result provided in
[
10
]
, this completion of a vector lattice
G
yields
\mathcal{R}M
, the vector lattice of frame homomorphisms
\mathcal{O}\mathbb{R}\to M
, where
\mathcal{O}\mathbb{R}
is the topology of the real numbers
\mathbb{R}
and
M
is the Madden frame of
G
. This article mainly concerns the second and third completions, here termed respectively the
inner and outer relative uniform completions,
or iru-completion and oru-completion for short.

Section
3
outlines the mathematical apparatus which underlies the analysis; it is a general form of the (pointfree) Yosida adjunction (Theorem
3.5
) introduced in
[
2
]
. A virtue of its format is that it associates with each vector lattice two frames linked by a compactification map
q\colon K\to M
. Roughly speaking,
K
is determined by and diagnostic of the bounded elements of the vector lattice, and
M
is determined by and diagnostic of the unbounded elements of the vector lattice. The analysis is necessarily pointfree, for although
K
is always spatial,
M
need not be.

Given that fact, it is no small irony that pointwise convergence plays an important role in our analysis (Section
4
). This convergence, which enjoys a graceful articulation in a pointfree setting, is implied by relative uniform convergence (Proposition
5.6
). Its importance derives from the fact that it is precisely the pointwise joins which are preserved by all vector lattice homomorphisms (Proposition
4.1
(2)). Furthermore, the pointwise Cauchy completion of a vector lattice
G
is its epicompletion (
[
1
]
), which coincides with the oru-completion of
G
. The epicompletion of
G
also coincides with
\mathcal{R}P
, where
M\to P
is the
P
-frame reflection of
M
(
[
14
]
),
M
is the Madden frame of
G
,
\mathcal{R}P
designates the vector lattice of frame homomorphisms
\mathcal{O}\mathbb{R}\to P
, and
\mathcal{O}\mathbb{R}
designates the topology of the real numbers. We briefly review the construction of this reflection in the preliminary Section
2
; our reason for doing so is to emphasize that the key step in the construction, Lemma
2.2
, is a crucial ingredient of our analysis of the oru-completion in Proposition
7.5
.

In
[
20
]
Veksler constructs the iru-completion of a vector lattice inside its Dedekind MacNeille completion in its maximal essential extension. In Theorem
6.6
we offer an alternative construction which is not only conceptually simpler, but also makes it clear that the completion is much closer to
G
than is implied by Veksler’s construction: the iru-completion of
G
is just its relative uniform closure in
\mathcal{R}M
. This is an immediate consequence of the fact that
\mathcal{R}M
is iru-complete (Theorem
6.3
).

Our notation is standard for the most part, save for our use of

\left\downarrow{A}\right\downarrow\equiv\left\{\,b:\exists a\in A\ (b\leq a)\,\right\}

to designate the downset generated by a subset
A\subseteq P
, and dually for our use of
\left\uparrow{A}\right\uparrow
for the upset generated by
A
. The letters
G
and
H
signify archimedean vector lattices with distinguished weak order unit designated by
1
, while the letters
K
,
L
,
M
, and
N
signify completely regular frames.

4.
Pointwise convergence

Pointwise convergence has a natural and elegant formulation in the pointfree context, and it takes on greater importance when vector lattices are analyzed in that context. The topic was introduced in
[
12
]
and exploited in
[
1
]
and
[
2
]
. In connection with the definition of pointwise suprema below, the reader should recall that for elements
f,g\in\mathcal{R}M
,
f\leq g
if and only if
f(q,-)\leq g(q,-)
for all
q\in\mathbb{Q}
if and only if
f(-,q)\geq g(-,q)
for all
q\in\mathbb{Q}
(
[
5
, 3.1.4]
).

Definition

(
f_{0}=\bigvee^{\bullet}F
,
f_{0}=\bigwedge^{\bullet}F
)
.

We say that an element
f_{0}\in\mathcal{R}M
is the
pointwise join
(
pointwise meet
) of a subset
F\subseteq\mathcal{R}M
if
\bigvee_{F}f(r,-)=f_{0}(r,-)

(
\bigvee_{F}f(-,r)=f_{0}(-,r)
)
for all
r\in\mathbb{R}
. (Actually, it is sufficient for this condition to hold only for all
q\in\mathbb{Q}
.) We write
\bigvee^{\bullet}F=f_{0}

(
\bigwedge^{\bullet}F=f_{0}
)
. We say that an element
g_{0}\in G
is the pointwise join (pointwise meet) of a subset
G_{0}\subseteq G
if
\bigvee^{\bullet}\widehat{G}_{0}=\hat{g}_{0}

(
\bigwedge^{\bullet}\widehat{G}_{0}=\hat{g}_{0}
)
.

Propositions 4.1.3 and 4.22–25 of
[
12
]
articulate some of the nice features of pointwise suprema and infima; we quote the last two as Proposition
4.1
below. The second feature, context freeness, may be taken as a definition of pointwise suprema and infima, independent of the representation of
G
as a vector lattice in
\mathcal{R}M
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
G_{0}
be a subset and
g_{0}
an element of
G
.

(1)

Pointwise joins and meets have
countable character
. That is, if
\bigvee_{G_{0}}^{\bullet}g=g_{0}
then
\bigvee_{G_{1}}^{\bullet}g=g_{0}
for some countable subset
G_{1}\subseteq G_{0}
, and dually for pointwise meets.

(2)

Pointwise joins and meets are
context free.
That is,
\bigvee_{G_{0}}^{\bullet}g=g_{0}
if and only if
\bigvee_{G_{0}}\theta(g)=\theta(g_{0})
for any
\mathbf{V}
-homomorphism out of
G
, and dually for pointwise meets.

The notion of pointwise suprema and infima leads immediately to the appropriate notion of directional pointwise convergence. Sections 5 and 6 of
[
1
]
constitute a thorough development of this idea.

Definition

(
g_{n}\searrow g_{0}
,
g_{n}\nearrow g_{0}
)
.

A sequence
\{g_{n}\}\subseteq G

converges downwards (upwards) to a limit
g_{0}
if it is decreasing (increasing), i.e.,
g_{n+1}\leq g_{n}
(
g_{n+1}\geq g_{n}
) for all
n
, and
\bigwedge_{n}^{\bullet}g_{n}=g_{0}
(
\bigvee_{n}^{\bullet}g_{n}=g_{0}
). We write
g_{n}\searrow g_{o}
(
g_{n}\nearrow g_{0}
).

Unqualified pointwise convergence is obtained from directional pointwise convergence by means of the lim sup definition, that is, by requiring the pointwise downwards convergence of the suprema of the tails of the
sequence. This makes sense if the context assures the existence of the aforementioned suprema. It turns out that the appropriate context is the epicompletion of
G
, which for our purposes is realized by the extension

G\xrightarrow{\mu_{G}}\mathcal{R}M\xrightarrow{\mathcal{R}p}\mathcal{R}P
, where
p\colon M\to P
is the
P
-frame reflection of
M
introduced in Theorems
2.5
and
2.6
. Lemmas
4.2
implies Lemma
4.3
; together they provide evidence of the utility of the extension
G\to\mathcal{R}P
for our purposes.

Lemma 4.2

(
[
3
, 3.9]
)
.

A vector lattice is of the form
\mathcal{R}P
for some
P
-frame

if and only if it is countably conditionally and laterally complete.

Lemma 4.3

(
[
1
, 4.6]
)
.

When
\mathcal{R}M
is viewed as a subobject of
\mathcal{R}P
, any sequence in
\mathcal{R}M
which is bounded above (below) in
\mathcal{R}P
has a pointwise join (meet) in
\mathcal{R}P
.

Definition

(pointwise convergence)
.

We say that a sequence
\{g_{n}\}\subseteq G^{+}

converges pointwise to
0
, and write
g_{n}\xrightarrow{\text{\raisebox{-2.0pt}[-6.0pt][-6.0pt]{$\bullet$}}}0
, if

\bigvee_{j\geq n}^{\bullet}g_{j}\searrow 0

holds in
\mathcal{R}_{0}P
, i.e., if
\bigwedge_{n}^{\bullet}\bigvee_{j\geq n}^{\bullet}g_{j}=0
in
\mathcal{R}P
. An arbitrary sequence
\{g_{n}\}\subseteq G
converges pointwise to an element

if
|g_{n}-g_{0}|\xrightarrow{\text{\raisebox{-2.0pt}[-6.0pt][-6.0pt]{$\bullet$}}}0
.

We reiterate for emphasis that the joins that appear in the definition of pointwise convergence need not exist in
\mathcal{R}M
, but do exist in
\mathcal{R}P
.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
\{g_{n}\}
be a bounded sequence in
G^{+}
. Then
g_{n}\xrightarrow{\text{\raisebox{-2.0pt}[-6.0pt][-6.0pt]{$\bullet$}}}0
if and only if

\forall\epsilon>0\ \Bigg(\bigvee_{m}\bigwedge_{n\geq m}g_{n}(-\infty,\epsilon)=\top\Bigg)

holds in
P
.

Proof.

Let
h_{m}\equiv\bigvee_{n\geq m}^{\bullet}g_{n}
. To say that
h_{m}\searrow 0
is to say that
\bigwedge_{m}^{\bullet}h_{m}=0
, i.e., that
\bigvee_{m}h_{m}(-\infty,\epsilon)=\top
for all
\epsilon>0
. Now it is a general fact that
f(-\infty,\epsilon)=\bigvee_{\delta<\epsilon}f(\delta,\infty)^{*}
for any
f\in\mathcal{R}_{0}L
and
\epsilon\in\mathbb{R}
(
[
5
, 3.1.1]
), hence

\displaystyle h_{m}(-\infty,\epsilon)

\displaystyle=\Bigg(\bigvee_{n\geq m}^{\bullet}g_{n}\Bigg)(-\infty,\epsilon)=\bigvee_{\delta<\epsilon}\Bigg(\bigvee_{n\geq m}^{\bullet}g_{n}\Bigg)(\delta,\infty)^{*}

\displaystyle=\bigvee_{\delta<\epsilon}\Bigg(\bigvee_{n\geq m}g_{n}(\delta,\infty)\Bigg)^{*}=\bigvee_{\delta<\epsilon}\bigwedge_{n\geq m}g_{n}(\delta,\infty)^{*}.

We have

\displaystyle g_{n}\xrightarrow{\text{\raisebox{-2.0pt}[-6.0pt][-6.0pt]{$\bullet$}}}0

\displaystyle\iff h_{m}\searrow 0\iff\forall\epsilon>0\ \Bigg(\bigvee_{m}\bigvee_{\delta<\epsilon}\bigwedge_{n\geq m}g_{n}(\delta,\infty)^{*}=\top\Bigg)

\displaystyle\iff\forall\epsilon>0\ \Bigg(\bigvee_{\delta<\epsilon}\bigvee_{m}\bigwedge_{n\geq m}g_{n}(\delta,\infty)^{*}=\top\Bigg)

Since
\bigvee_{m}\bigwedge_{n\geq m}g_{n}(\delta,\infty)^{*}
is an increasing function of
\delta
,

g_{n}\xrightarrow{\text{\raisebox{-2.0pt}[-6.0pt][-6.0pt]{$\bullet$}}}0\iff\forall\epsilon>0\ \Bigg(\bigvee_{m}\bigwedge_{n\geq m}g_{n}(\epsilon,\infty)^{*}=\top\Bigg).\qed

Proposition
4.5
points out that pointwise convergence generalizes directional pointwise convergence.

Proposition 4.5
.

For a bounded sequence
\{g_{n}\}\subseteq G^{+}
,

g_{n}\searrow 0\iff\text{$\{g_{n}\}$ is decreasing and $g_{n}\xrightarrow{\text{\raisebox{-2.0pt}[-6.0pt][-6.0pt]{$\bullet$}}}0$},

and dually.

Proof.

If
g_{n}\searrow 0
then
\{g_{n}\}
is decreasing and
\bigwedge_{n}^{\bullet}g_{n}=0
, hence
\bigvee_{j\geq n}g_{j}=g_{n}
for all
n
, and
\bigwedge_{n}^{\bullet}\bigvee_{j\geq n}^{\bullet}g_{j}=\bigwedge_{n}^{\bullet}g_{n}=0
, i.e.,
g_{n}\xrightarrow{\text{\raisebox{-2.0pt}[-6.0pt][-6.0pt]{$\bullet$}}}0
. On the other hand, suppose
\{g_{n}\}
is decreasing and
g_{n}\xrightarrow{\text{\raisebox{-2.0pt}[-6.0pt][-6.0pt]{$\bullet$}}}0
, i.e.,
\bigwedge_{n}^{\bullet}\bigvee_{j\geq n}g_{j}^{\bullet}=0
. Since once again
\bigvee_{n}^{\bullet}=g_{n}
, this says directly that
\bigwedge_{n}^{\bullet}g_{n}=0
, i.e., that
g_{n}\searrow 0
.
∎

5.
Relative uniform convergence

Definition

(regulator,
g_{n}\rightarrow g_{0}\,(f)
, relative uniform convergence
g_{n}\xrightarrow{u}g_{0}
)
.

A sequence
\{g_{n}\}\subseteq G
is said to
converge to an element
g_{0}
with regulator
f\in G^{+}
, written
g_{n}\rightarrow g_{0}\,(f)
, if for every positive integer
k
there exists an integer
m_{k}
such that
\left|g_{0}-g_{n}\right|\leq f/k
for all
n\geq m_{k}
. In symbols,

\forall k\,\exists m_{k}\,\forall n\geq m_{k}\ \big(\left|g_{0}-g_{n}\right|\leq f/k\big).

The sequence is said to
converge relatively uniformly to
g_{0}
, written
g_{n}\xrightarrow{ru}g_{0}
if there exists an element
f\in G^{+}
for which
g_{n}\rightarrow g_{0}\,(f)
.

We record our notation for open subsets of the reals, and then provide a few preliminary results which will facilitate the material that follows.

Notation

(
r^{\varepsilon}
,
U\subseteq_{\varepsilon}V
,
U^{\circ}_{\varepsilon}
)
.

For a real number
r
and positive real number
\varepsilon
, we denote the
\varepsilon
-neighborhood of

by

r^{\varepsilon}\equiv\left\{\,s\in\mathbb{R}:\left|r-s\right|<\varepsilon\,\right\}

When speaking of subsets
U,V\subseteq\mathbb{R}
, we say that
U
is
\varepsilon
-contained in
V
, and write
U\subseteq_{\varepsilon}V
, if
u^{\varepsilon}\subseteq V
for all

. The
\varepsilon
-interior of

is the largest open subset of

which is
\varepsilon
-contained in

V^{\circ}_{\varepsilon}\equiv\bigcup\left\{\,U\in\mathcal{O}\mathbb{R}:U\subseteq_{\varepsilon}V\,\right\}=\left\{\,v:v^{\varepsilon}\subseteq V\,\right\}.

Lemma 5.1
.

For all

and all
\varepsilon>0
,

\displaystyle\left|f-g\right|(\varepsilon,-)

\displaystyle=\bigvee_{s}\big((f(-,s)\wedge g(s+\varepsilon,-))\vee(g(-,s)\wedge f(s+\varepsilon,-))\big),\ \text{ and}

\displaystyle\left|f-g\right|(-,\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=\bigvee_{s}\left(f(s^{\varepsilon/2})\wedge g(s^{\varepsilon/2})\right).

Proof.

According to
[
13
, 3.1.1]
,

\left|f-g\right|(\varepsilon,-)=\bigvee_{\left|U_{f}-U_{g}\right|\subseteq(\varepsilon,-)}\left(f(U_{f})\wedge g(U_{g})\right),

where
U_{f}
and
U_{g}
range over open subsets of
\mathbb{R}
such that
\left|u-v\right|>\varepsilon
for all

and

. Thus either
U_{f}
is bounded above and
U_{g}
is bounded below, or vice-versa. In the first case there exists a real number

such that
U_{f}\subseteq(-,s)
and
U_{g}\subseteq(s,-)
, and in the second case there exists a real number
s
such that
U_{f}\subseteq(s,-)
and
U_{g}\subseteq(-,s)
.

To verify the second displayed identity, consider open sets
U_{f},U_{g}\in\mathcal{O}\mathbb{R}
such that
\left|U_{f}-U_{g}\right|\subseteq(-,\varepsilon)
. If

and

then the midpoint

has the feature that

and
\left|s^{\varepsilon/2}-s^{\varepsilon/2}\right|\subseteq(-,\varepsilon)
.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

For any
f,g\in\mathcal{R}M
, any

, and any

\left|f-g\right|(-,\varepsilon)\wedge f(U^{\circ}_{\varepsilon})\leq g(U).

Proof.

From Lemma

we get

\left|f-g\right|(-,\varepsilon)\wedge f(U^{\circ}_{\varepsilon})=\bigvee_{s}\left(f(U^{\circ}_{\varepsilon})\wedge f(s^{\varepsilon/2})\wedge g(s^{\varepsilon/2})\right)=\bigvee_{s}\left(f(U^{\circ}_{\varepsilon}\wedge s^{\varepsilon/2})\wedge g(s^{\varepsilon/2})\right).

We claim that
s^{\varepsilon/2}\subseteq U
for any real number
s
such that
U^{\circ}_{\varepsilon}\wedge s^{\varepsilon/2}\neq\emptyset
. For if
r\in s^{\varepsilon/2}
and

then
\left|r-t\right|=\left|r-s\right|+\left|s-t\right|<\varepsilon/2+\varepsilon/2=\varepsilon
, so that

by virtue of the fact that

For a sequence

(5)

Proposition 5.4

(
[
8
, 14]
)

-morphism

for some sequence
\{g_{n}\}\subseteq G
and elements

.

Proof.

Suppose

, let

be the pointed frame homomorphism which realizes
\tau

\tau(g)=k\circ g

An application of the
\mathbf{V}

yields

Inner and outer relative uniform closure operators

Relative uniform convergence gives rise to two distinct closure operators.

Definition

(the outer (inner) relative uniform completion of
G
in
H
,
\oruc_{H}G
, (
\iruc_{H}G
), oru-complete (iru-complete))
.

Suppose that
G
is a sub-vector lattice of
H
. Let

\displaystyle G^{1}_{H}

\displaystyle\equiv\left\{\,h\in H:\exists\,\{g_{n}\}\subseteq G\,\exists f\in H^{+}\ \big(g_{n}\to h\,(f)\big)\,\right\}

\displaystyle\Big(\prescript{1}{}{G}_{H}

\displaystyle\equiv\left\{\,h\in H:\exists\,\{g_{n}\}\subseteq G\,\exists f\in G^{+}\ \big(g_{n}\to h\,(f)\big)\,\right\}\Big).

We define the
outer (inner) relative uniform closure of
G
in
H
inductively as follows. For all ordinals
\alpha
define

\displaystyle G^{0}_{H}

\displaystyle\equiv G

\displaystyle\big(\prescript{0}{}{G}_{H}

\displaystyle\equiv G\big),

\displaystyle G^{\alpha}_{H}

\displaystyle\equiv(G^{\beta}_{H})^{1}_{H}

\displaystyle\Big(\prescript{\alpha}{}{G}_{H}

\displaystyle\equiv\prescript{1}{}{(\prescript{\beta}{}{G}_{H}})_{H}\Big)

for
\alpha=\beta+1
,

\displaystyle G^{\alpha}_{H}

\displaystyle\equiv\bigcup_{\beta<\alpha}G^{\beta}_{H}

\displaystyle\bigg(\prescript{\alpha}{}{G}_{H}

\displaystyle\equiv\bigcup_{\beta<\alpha}\prescript{\beta}{}{G}_{H}\bigg)

for

a limit ordinal,

\displaystyle\oruc_{H}G

\displaystyle\equiv G^{\alpha}_{H}

\displaystyle\Big(\iruc_{H}G

\displaystyle\equiv\prescript{\alpha}{}{G}_{H}\Big)

for

such that
G^{\alpha}_{H}=G^{\alpha+1}_{H}

\big(\prescript{\alpha}{}{G}_{H}=\prescript{\alpha+1}{}{G}_{H}\big)
.

If

(
\iruc_{H}G=H
) we say that

is oru-dense (iru-dense) in

. We say that

is

if
\oruc_{H}G=G
(
\iruc_{H}G=G
) for every vector lattice

which contains

as a sub-vector lattice. We denote the full subcategory of

comprised of the oru-complete (iru-complete) vector lattices by

(

).

It is well known, and explicit in the development in
[

]
, that the iteration in the definition of

may proceed through

steps. It is less well known that the iteration in the definition of

may take more than one step; an example requiring two steps can be found in
[
11
]
. We know of no example requiring more than two steps, but we think they exist.

Definition

(ru-Cauchy sequence, ru-Cauchy complete vector lattice)

A sequence

in a vector lattice

if there is some regulator

for which we have

\forall k\,\exists m_{k}\,\forall i,j\geq m_{k}\ \big(\left|g_{i}-g_{j}\right|\leq f/k\big).

is said to be

if every ru-Cauchy sequence in

converges relatively uniformly to an element of

.

Lemma

provides a characterization of ru-Cauchy sequences in
\mathcal{R}M
.

Lemma 6.1
.

The following are equivalent for a sequence
\{g_{n}\}\subseteq\mathcal{R}^{+}M
and element
f\in\mathcal{R}^{+}M
.

(1)

\{g_{n}\}
is ru-Cauchy with regulator
f
.

(2)

For all
k
there exists an
m_{k}
such that for all
i,j\geq m_{k}
and all
\varepsilon>0
we have

\bigvee_{s}\Big(\big(g_{i}(-,s)\wedge g_{j}(s+\varepsilon,-)\big)\vee\big(g_{j}(-,s)\wedge g_{i}(s+\varepsilon,-)\big)\Big)\leq f(k\varepsilon,-).

(3)

For all

there exists an
m_{k}
such that for all

and all

we have

\bigvee_{s}\left(g_{i}(s^{\varepsilon/2})\wedge g_{j}(s^{\varepsilon/2})\right)\geq f(-,k\varepsilon).

By Lemma 3.1.4 of
[
5
]
we get that
\left|g_{i}-g_{j}\right|\leq f/k
if and only if

\forall\varepsilon>0\ \big(\left|g_{i}-g_{j}\right|(\varepsilon,-)\leq(f/k)(\varepsilon,-)\big)\iff\forall\varepsilon>0\ \big(\left|g_{i}-g_{j}\right|(-,\varepsilon)\geq(f/k)(-,\varepsilon)\big).

Since

, the first displayed identity of Lemma

applied to the left condition above yields condition (2) of the lemma. Likewise the second displayed identity of Lemma
5.1
applied to the right condition above yields condition (3) of the lemma.
∎

Lemma 6.2
.

Let

be an ru-Cauchy sequence in

with regulator

To check the left inequality, note that

Theorem 6.3

(
[

)
.

\mathcal{R}M
is ru-Cauchy complete.

Proof.

Let
\{g_{n}\}
be an ru-Cauchy sequence in

with regulator

, and for each integer

let

be the least integer such that
\left|g_{i}-g_{j}\right|\leq f/k
for all

then, since
g_{m_{k}}-f/k\leq g_{j}\leq g_{m_{k}}+f/k
for all

for all

by Proposition

. In other words, the only candidate for the limit is

.

Thus we are led to define the function
g_{0}\colon\left\{\,(-,r):r\in\mathbb{R}\,\right\}\to M
by the formula

g_{0}(-,r)\equiv\bigvee_{k}\big(g_{m_{k}}+f/k\big)(-,r),\qquad r\in\mathbb{R}.

Now

\big(g_{m_{k}}+f/k\big)(-,r)=\bigvee_{U+V\subseteq(-,r)}\big(g_{m_{k}}(U)\wedge(f/k)(V)\big)=\bigvee_{\varepsilon>0}\big(g_{m_{k}}(-,r-\varepsilon)\wedge(f/k)(-,\varepsilon)\big)\\
=\bigvee_{\varepsilon>0}\big(g_{m_{k}}(-,r-\varepsilon)\wedge f(-,k\varepsilon)\big),

so
g_{0}(-,r)=\bigvee_{k}\bigvee_{\varepsilon>0}\big(g_{m_{k}}(-,r-\varepsilon)\wedge f(-,k\varepsilon)\big)
. In order to show that

(1)

(2)

.

.

To verify (1) simply observe that

\bigvee_{t>0}g_{0}(-,r-t)=\bigvee_{t>0}\bigvee_{k}\bigvee_{\varepsilon>0}\big(g_{m_{k}}(-,r-t-\varepsilon)\wedge f(-,k\varepsilon)\big)=\bigvee_{k}\bigvee_{\varepsilon>0}\bigvee_{t>0}\big(g_{m_{k}}-,r-t-\varepsilon)\wedge f(-,k\varepsilon)\big)\\
=\bigvee_{k}\bigvee_{\varepsilon>0}\left(f(-,k\varepsilon)\wedge\bigvee_{t>0}g_{m_{k}}(r-t-\varepsilon)\right)=\bigvee_{k}\bigvee_{\varepsilon>0}\left(f(-,k\varepsilon)\wedge g_{m_{k}}(-,r-\varepsilon)\right)=g_{0}(-,r).

To verify (2) fix real numbers

and put

a_{r}\equiv\bigvee_{l}\bigvee_{\delta>0}\big(g_{m_{l}}(r+\delta,-)\wedge f(-,l\delta)\big)

We claim that
a_{r}
witnesses the fact that
g_{0}(-,r)\prec g_{0}(-,t)
. First of all,

g_{0}(-,r)\wedge a_{r}=\bigvee_{k}\bigvee_{\varepsilon>0}\big(g_{m_{k}}(-,r-\varepsilon)\wedge f(-,k\varepsilon)\wedge a_{r}\big)\\
=\bigvee_{k}\bigvee_{\varepsilon>0}\bigvee_{l}\bigvee_{\delta>0}\left(g_{m_{k}}(-,r-\varepsilon)\wedge f(-,k\varepsilon)\wedge g_{m_{l}}(r+\delta,-)\wedge f(-,l\delta)\right).

For any integer

for all

for all

for any

we see that this term, and therefore also the join, works out to

On the other hand we have

g_{0}(-,t)\vee a_{r}=\bigvee_{k}\bigvee_{\varepsilon>0}\big(g_{m_{k}}(-,t-\varepsilon)\wedge f(-,k\varepsilon)\vee a_{r}\big)\\
=\bigvee_{k}\bigvee_{\varepsilon>0}\bigvee_{l}\bigvee_{\delta>0}\left(\big(g_{m_{k}}(-,t-\varepsilon)\wedge f(-,k\varepsilon)\big)\vee\big(g_{m_{l}}(r+\delta,-)\wedge f(-,l\delta)\big)\right).

When

and

, the term in the quadruply indexed join immediately above works out to

, we conclude that

To verify (3) first note that

\bigvee_{r}g_{0}(-,r)=\bigvee_{r}\bigvee_{k}\bigvee_{\varepsilon>0}\big(g_{m_{k}}(-,r-\varepsilon)\wedge f(-,k\varepsilon)\big)=\bigvee_{k}\bigvee_{\varepsilon>0}\bigvee_{r}\big(g_{m_{k}}(-,r-\varepsilon)\wedge f(-,k\varepsilon)\big)\\

Then observe that since we showed above that
g_{0}(-,r)
is disjoint from

, we have

\bigvee_{r}g_{0}(-,r)^{*}\geq\bigvee_{r}a_{r}=\bigvee_{r}\bigvee_{l}\bigvee_{\delta>0}\big(g_{m_{l}}(r+\delta,-)\wedge f(-,l\delta)\big)=\bigvee_{l}\bigvee_{\delta>0}\bigvee_{r}\big(g_{m_{l}}(r+\delta,-)\wedge f(-,l\delta)\big)\\

8.
Concerning the adjunction of Theorem
3.5

The classical Yosida space
X
of a vector lattice
G
is the set of values of the unit
1
, i.e., the set of convex subspaces maximal with respect to omitting
1
, made into a compact Hausdorff space by the hull kernel topology. In the seminal paper
[
21
]
, Yosida represents a bounded vector lattice
G
as a subobject of
\mathcal{C}X
, the vector lattice of continuous real valued functions on
X
, with the constant function
1
as designated unit. The space
X
appears in our development in the form of its topology

, which is the domain of the standard compactification
q\colon K\to M
of
G
.

The Madden frame of
G
is the frame
M
of its
\mathbf{V}
-kernels. In their influential paper
[
19
]
, Madden and Vermeer represent an arbitrary vector lattice
G
as a sub-vector lattice of
\mathcal{R}M
. The frame
M
appears in our development as the codomain of the standard compactification of
G
, and the compactification map
q
itself realizes the inclusion
G^{*}\to G
.

In their important article
[
17
]
, Hager and Robertson provide a representation of an arbitrary vector lattice
G
which is in some sense intermediate between these two. In it,
G
is represented as a vector lattice in
\mathcal{D}X
, where
X
is the Yosida space of
G
and
\mathcal{D}X
is the set of continuous functions
X\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
with dense domains of reality. If, in our development, we denote
\mu_{G}(g)
by
(\hat{g}^{\prime},\hat{g})
then
\hat{g}^{\prime}
is the frame counterpart of the Hager-Robertson representation of
g
as an element of
\mathcal{D}X
, and
\hat{g}
is the Madden-Vermeer representation of
g
as an element of
\mathcal{R}M
. Thus we see that
\mathcal{E}q
corresponds to a (possibly proper) subset of
\mathcal{D}X
(cf. Example
3.3
).

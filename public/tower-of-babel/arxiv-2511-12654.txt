The Ginibre Ensemble Conditioned on an Overcrowding Event
Offer Kopelevitch

Abstract
We look at the eigenvalues of the complex Ginibre Ensemble of random matrices consisting of N N eigenvalues. We study the event that for c ∈ [ 0 , 1 ] {c\in[0,1]} , ⌊ c ​ N ⌋ \lfloor cN\rfloor of the eigenvalues are located outside of a disk of radius R ∈ ( 1 − c , 1 ) R\in(\sqrt{1-c},1) . Except for the case c = 1 c=1 the eigenvalue process conditioned on this event is not determinantal. Nevertheless we are able to obtain asymptotic estimates of the probability of the event, and describe the conditional distribution in three spatial regions. For { λ ∈ ℂ : | λ | < R } , { λ ∈ ℂ : | λ | > R + ϵ } \{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:\left|\lambda\right|<R\},\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:\left|\lambda\right|>R+\epsilon\} the conditional distribution is asymptotically that of a Ginibre ensemble. Meanwhile, near the boundary of the disk, after rescaling by a factor of order N N , it tends to the determinantal point process that appears in the limit of the Ginibre ensemble near a hard wall in Seo [ Seo22 ] .

Introduction

Consider the Ginibre ensemble defined by the probability distribution on
\mathbb{C}^{N}

\frac{1}{Z_{N}}\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq N}\left|\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{j}\right|^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{N}e^{-N\left|\lambda_{j}\right|^{2}}\mathrm{d}\lambda_{j}

(1.1)

where
Z_{N}
is a normalisation constant.

The Ginibre ensemble is the most well studied two-dimensional determinantal point process in the field of random matrices
[
For10
, Chapter 15]
. It is the distribution of the eigenvalues of an
N\times N
random matrix whose entries are independent complex Gaussian variables centred around zero with variance
\frac{1}{N}
.

The limiting distribution of the empirical measure of the Ginibre ensemble is the normalised Lebesgue measure on the unit disk (see Ginibre
[
Gin65
]
). In particular, for
R\in(0,1)
, with probability one,
\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathds{1}_{\{\left|\lambda_{i}\right|>R\}}
tends to
1-R^{2}
as
N
tends to infinity. Moreover, according to the large deviation principle for the Ginibre Ensemble established by Petz and Hiai
[
PH98
]
, the event where the number of eigenvalues satisfying
\left|\lambda\right|>R
is
\lfloor cN\rfloor
is a very rare event for
c\in(1-R^{2},1]
, with probability exponentially small in
N^{2}
.

A special case that was studied before is the hole event, corresponding to the case
c=1
. The Ginibre ensemble conditioned on this event forms a determinantal point process and there are several results about it including the leading orders of the asymptotics of the probability of that event by Charlier
[
Cha24
]
and the leading order of the hole probability in a general open set by Adhikari and Reddy
[
AR17
]
.

The overcrowding cases, where
c\in(1-R^{2},1)
, and similarly the undercrowding cases with
c\in(0,1-R^{2})
, are not determinantal and as such, dealing with them requires different tools. In this paper we take a different approach to deal with it and we get the asymptotics of the probability of the overcrowding event.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mathcal{G}_{N}
be the Ginibre ensemble of size
N
and assume
R^{2}>1-c
. The probability that exactly
N_{c}=\lfloor cN\rfloor
eigenvalues of a size
N
Ginibre ensemble are bigger in absolute value than
R
satisfies

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{G}_{N}}(\#\{\lambda:

\displaystyle\left|\lambda\right|>R\}=N_{c})=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{G}_{N_{c}}}(\forall\lambda\colon\left|\lambda\right|>R)\cdot\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}p(l)\left(\frac{R^{2}}{1-c}\right)^{-l}\left(1+O\left(\frac{\log^{3}N}{N}\right)\right)

where
p(l)
is the partition function (see Appendix
B
).

Note that using this theorem we can write an asymptotic estimate to the probability
\mathbb{P}(\#\{\lambda:\left|\lambda\right|>R\}=N_{c})
up to a multiplicative error of
(1+O(N^{-\frac{1}{12}}))
, by substituting the asymptotic expansion of
\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{G}_{N_{c}}}(\forall\lambda\colon\left|\lambda\right|>R)
found by Charlier
[
Cha24
, Theorem 1.7]
.

Next we study the limits of the conditional distributions given the overcrowding event. Figure
1
shows an illustration of the conditional distribution of the eigenvalues of a Ginibre matrix of size
N=4000
, conditioned on the event that
3600
of them (corresponding to
c=0.9
) satisfy
\left|\lambda\right|\geq R=0.7
. Three regions are visible: the interior region
|\lambda|<R
, a narrow boundary layer around
|\lambda|=R
, and the exterior region
|\lambda|>R+\epsilon
.

Figure 1:
Illustration of the conditional distribution of the eigenvalues of the Ginibre ensemble of size 4000, conditioned on the event that 90% of them satisfy
\left|\lambda\right|\geq R=0.7
, showing the three different regions of the distribution: inside the disk of radius 0.7, on the boundary of the disk and outside the disk.

The main results of this paper are the limits of the conditional distribution. We separate the plane into three different regions and look at the limit in each of them.

Theorem 1.2
.

For
c\in(1-R^{2},1]
, the conditional distribution of the Ginibre Ensemble of size
N
given that
N_{c}=\lfloor cN\rfloor
of them satisfy
\left|\lambda\right|>R
behaves asymptotically, as
N\to\infty
, in the following way:

1.

Inside the disk
D(0,R)
, it is the same as a Ginibre ensemble of size
N-N_{c}
scaled by
\sqrt{\frac{N-N_{c}}{N}}
.

2.

For any
\epsilon>0
, outside the disk the disk
D(0,R+\epsilon)
it is the same as a Ginibre ensemble of size
N
.

3.

Near the edge of the disk
D(0,R)
, after zooming in around
R
by a factor of
\frac{N(R^{2}-1+c)}{R}
, it is the same as the determinantal point process with kernel

K_{\mathcal{X}}(z,w)=\frac{1-(z+\overline{w}+1)e^{-z-\overline{w}}}{\pi(z+\overline{w})^{2}}

with respect to the Lebesgue measure on the right half plane.

Remarks
.

1.

By behaving asymptotically the same we mean e.g. that the
k
-point functions of the two processes are asymptotically the same.

2.

Theorem
1.2
follows from Proposition
4.1
and Proposition
4.2
which include estimates for the rates of convergence.

3.

According to the rotational symmetry of the problem, when zooming in around a point
Re^{i\theta}
by the factor of
\frac{N(R^{2}-1+c)}{R}
, the limiting process is the same process
\mathcal{X}
rotated by
\theta
.

4.

The process
\mathcal{X}
, in part 3 of Theorem
1.2
, appears in Seo
[
Seo22
]
as the limit of a Ginibre ensemble with a hard wall inside the bulk near the hard wall.

The paper is organised as follows: In Section
2
we use a result from
[
Hou+09
]
to represent the distribution of the eigenvalues as a mixture of determinantal point precesses and show which of those processes have significant probability to appear. In Section

1.1
. Then, in Section

1.2
.

1.1
Additional and related results

The probability of an overcrowding event in similar processes was studied before, for example, a result from the physics literature by Majumdar, Nadal, Scardicchio and Vivo calculated the leading order asymptotics of the probability of overcrowding of eigenvalues in the positive half-line in the Gaussian unitary ensemble (GUE)
[
Maj+11
]
. As far as we know the next order term is only known for the hole event (see Deaño and Simm
[
DnS17
]
).

For the infinite Ginibre ensemble, the leading term of the overcrowding probability was shown by Shirai
[
Shi06
]
and Fenzl and Lambert found the next terms in the asymptotics of the overcrowding probability
[
FL22
]
which correspond to the Jancovici, Lebowitz, Manificat (JLM) predictions
[
JLM93
]
. The overcrowding event on general sets in the finite Ginibre ensemble was studied by Armstrong, Serfaty and Zeitouni who found properties the limiting distribution of the eigenvalues must satisfy
[
ASZ14
]
. In our case of overcrowding outside the disk
D(0,R)
their result shows the existence of a singular part of the limiting measure supported on
\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\left|z\right|=R\}
, and that for some
\epsilon>0
the limiting measure has zero measure on
\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\left|z\right|\in[R-\epsilon,R)\}
, in accordance with our Theorem
1.2
.

The main results of this paper can be generalised with minor modifications to the probability distribution, studied by Charlier
[
Cha24
]
,

\frac{1}{Z_{N}^{\alpha,b}}\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq N}\left|\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{j}\right|^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{N}\left|\lambda_{j}\right|^{2\alpha}e^{-N\left|\lambda_{j}\right|^{2b}}\mathrm{d}\lambda_{j},\qquad b>0,\alpha>-1.

(1.2)

We show them only in the case of the Ginibre distribution, corresponding to
\alpha=0,b=1
, for ease of computation.

In addition, our method could be extended to results on the conditional distribution given an overcrowding event in other radially symmetric sets, such as unions of disks and annuli centred around the origin. An interesting question that would require different tools is the question of overcrowding on a non-radially-symmetric set.

2
Eigenvalue distribution inside and outside the disk

We denote by
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{N}
the eigenvalues of a Ginibre
N\times N
random matrix, as in (
1.1
) (see
[
Hou+09
]
,
[
Gin65
]
). They form a determinantal point process with kernel

K_{N}(z,w)=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\frac{N^{k+1}(z\overline{w})^{k}e^{-\frac{1}{2}N(\left|z\right|^{2}+\left|w\right|^{2})}}{\pi k!}.

(2.1)

We start by noting that if we look at the process restricted to the complex plane outside a disk of radius
R
, i.e. at the random set
\left\{\lambda_{i}\colon i\in\{1,\dots,N\},\left|\lambda_{i}\right|>R\right\}
it is still a determinantal point process with the same kernel restricted to the set
\mathbb{C}\backslash D(0,R)
.

Outside the disk
D(0,R)
the functions
\phi_{k}(z)=\sqrt{\frac{N^{k+1}}{\pi\Gamma(k+1,NR^{2})}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}N\left|z\right|^{2}}z^{k}
for
k=0,1,\dots
are orthonormal and we can write the kernel as

K_{N}(z,w)=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}a_{k}\phi_{k}(z)\overline{\phi_{k}(w)}

(2.2)

with
a_{k}=\frac{\Gamma(k+1,NR^{2})}{k!}
.
Thus, as a special case of
[
Hou+09
, Theorem 4.5.3]
.

Theorem 2.1
.

The distribution of the Ginibre eigenvalues that satisfy
\left|\lambda\right|>R
is a mixture of determinantal point processes indexed by sets
J\subseteq\{0,1,\dots,N-1\}
with kernels

K^{J}(z,w)=\sum_{k\in J}\phi_{k}(z)\overline{\phi_{k}(w)}\mathds{1}_{\left|z\right|,\left|w\right|>R}.

(2.3)

The probability of the process with index
J
is

\tilde{P}_{J}=\prod_{k=0}^{N-1}(1-a_{k})\prod_{k\in J}\frac{a_{k}}{1-a_{k}}.

(2.4)

This Theorem shows that the process outside the disk is a mixture of projection point processes (and similarly inside the disk). The amount of points in each of those processes is deterministically
\#J
. Moreover, the overcrowding event depends only on the configuration of points outside the disk (in particular on their number) and thus conditioning the process on the overcrowding event and then restricting it to
\mathbb{C}\backslash D(0,R)
is the same as restricting the process to
\mathbb{C}\backslash D(0,R)
and then conditioning on the overcrowding event. Thus, we conclude the next Proposition.

Proposition 2.1
.

The distribution of the Ginibre eigenvalues that satisfy
\left|\lambda\right|>R
conditioned on the event that there are exactly
m
such eigenvalues is a mixture of of determinantal point processes indexed by
J\subseteq\{0,1,\dots,N-1\}
with
\#J=m
with kernels as in (
2.3
). The probability of the process with index
J
is

P_{J}=K_{m}\prod_{k\in J}\frac{a_{k}}{1-a_{k}}

(2.5)

where
K_{m}
is a constant not depending on
J
.

Similarly defining

\tilde{\phi}_{k}(z)=\sqrt{\frac{N^{k+1}}{\pi\gamma(k+1,NR^{2})}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}N\left|z\right|^{2}}z^{k}

(2.6)

we have

Proposition 2.2
.

The distribution of the Ginibre eigenvalues that satisfy
\left|\lambda\right|<R
conditioned on the event that there are exactly
N-m
such eigenvalues is a mixture of of determinantal point processes indexed by
J\subseteq\{0,1,\dots,N-1\}
with
\#J=m
with kernels

K^{J}(z,w)=\sum_{k\notin J}\tilde{\phi}_{k}(z)\overline{\tilde{\phi}_{k}(w)}\mathds{1}_{\left|z\right|,\left|w\right|<R}.

(2.7)

The probability of the process with index
J
is given by (
2.5
).

From now on we will look at the parameters
R,c
as constant and be interested in the asymptotics as
N
goes to infinity. Moreover we assume the case of overcrowding outside the disk, i.e.
0<R<1,c>1-R^{2}
. The case of undercrowding and the case
R>1
are similar.
We will denote by
\mathbb{P}_{c}
the probability space in Proposition
2.1
, i.e. the mixture of different values of
J
conditioned on the event
\#J=N_{c}
.

In the rest of this section we will estimate the probability
P_{J}
for such a set
J
of size
N_{c}=\lfloor cN\rfloor
. Denote
J=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{cN}\}\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}
where
{x_{1}<x_{2}<\dots<x_{N_{c}}}
. It will be more convenient to describe this set by the variables
n_{k}=N-N_{c}+k-x_{k}
. There is a bijection between such sets and the vectors
\mathbf{n}=(n_{1},\dots,n_{N_{c}})\in\mathbb{Z}^{N_{c}}
satisfying
N-N_{c}\geq n_{1}\geq n_{2}\geq\dots\geq n_{N_{c}}\geq 0
. We will denote
n(\{x_{1},\dots,x_{cN}\})=(n_{1},\dots,n_{N_{c}})
and
J_{\mathbf{n}}=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{N_{c}}\}
.
For simplicity we will denote
P_{J_{\mathbf{n}}}=P_{\mathbf{n}},\tilde{P}_{J_{\mathbf{n}}}=\tilde{P}_{\mathbf{n}}
and hence, by (
2.4
) we have

\tilde{P}_{\mathbf{n}}=\prod_{k\in A}Q(k,NR^{2})\cdot\prod_{k\notin A}\left(1-Q(k,NR^{2})\right).

(2.8)

Above
Q(a,z)=\frac{\Gamma(a,z)}{\Gamma(a)}
is the normalised incomplete gamma function (see Appendix
A
), and in particular

\frac{\tilde{P}_{\mathbf{n}}}{\tilde{P}_{\mathbf{0}}}=\frac{P_{\mathbf{n}}}{P_{\mathbf{0}}}=\prod_{k=1}^{cN}\frac{Q(N-cN+k-n_{k},NR^{2})}{Q(N-cN+k,NR^{2})}\frac{1-Q(N-cN+k,NR^{2})}{1-Q(N-cN+k-n_{k},NR^{2})}.

(2.9)

To find the values of
n(J)
with significant probability and to estimate
P_{\mathbf{n}}
for them we prove the following series of lemmas:

Lemma 2.1
.

There exists
\epsilon>0
and
\delta>0
such that for
N
big enough

\mathbb{P}_{c}\left(\exists x\in\{1,\dots,N\}\backslash J:x>NR^{2}(1-\delta)\right)\leq e^{-\epsilon N}P_{\mathbf{0}}.

Remark
.

This Lemma in implies that there exist
\delta>0
such that conditioned on the overcrowding event, the probability that there exist
x>NR^{2}(1-\delta)
,
x\notin J
is exponentially small in
N
.

Proof of Lemma
2.1
.

We will choose
\delta>0
such that
R^{2}(1-\delta)+c-1>0
and
N
big enough such that
D=\lfloor{NR^{2}(1-\delta)}\rfloor+N_{c}-N>0
. Notice that if there exists
x\in\{1,\dots,N\}\backslash J
such that
x>NR^{2}{(1-\delta)}
then
for
k=1,\dots,D
the
k
-lowest term in
J
is less than the
k
-lowest term in
J_{\mathbf{0}}
so we have

\sum_{k=1}^{D}x_{k}\leq\sum_{k=1}^{D}\left[N-N_{c}+k\right]-D.

Hence

\sum_{k=1}^{D}n_{k}=\sum_{k=1}^{D}(N-N_{c}+k-x_{k})\geq D.

(2.10)

We use Lemma
A.5
to get

\frac{Q(n,NR^{2})}{Q(n+1,NR^{2})}=\frac{\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\frac{(NR^{2})^{i}}{i!}}{\sum_{i=0}^{n}\frac{(NR^{2})^{i}}{i!}}=1-\frac{1}{1+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{n}{NR^{2}}\cdot\frac{n-1}{NR^{2}}\cdots\frac{i+1}{NR^{2}}}.

Hence for
n<NR^{2}(1-\delta)
there exists
0<\kappa<1
such that

\frac{Q(n,NR^{2})}{Q(n+1,NR^{2})}<\kappa.

(2.11)

And in particular

Q(n,NR^{2})\leq\kappa^{NR^{2}(1-\delta)-n}Q(NR^{2}(1-\delta),NR^{2})\leq\kappa^{NR^{2}(1-\delta)-n}.

(2.12)

Now, combining (
2.10
), (
2.12
) and (
2.9
), we get that for
L\geq D
:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{c}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{D}(n(J))_{k}=L\right)

\displaystyle\leq P_{\mathbf{0}}\sum_{n_{1},\dots,n_{D}\colon\sum_{k=1}^{D}n_{k}=L}\prod_{k=1}^{D}\frac{Q(N-N_{c}+k-n_{k},NR^{2})}{Q(N-N_{c}+k,NR^{2})}

\displaystyle\leq P_{\mathbf{0}}\cdot\kappa^{L}\cdot p(L)

where
p(n)
is the partition function (see Appendix
B
).
Hence, according to Lemma
B.1
, for some
0<\tilde{\kappa}<1
and for
N
big enough
\mathbb{P}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{D}(n(J))_{k}=L\right)\leq P_{\mathbf{0}}\cdot\tilde{\kappa}^{L}
so

\mathbb{P}_{c}\left(\exists x\in\{1,\dots,N\}\backslash J:x>NR^{2}(1-\delta)\right)\leq e^{-\epsilon N}P_{\mathbf{0}}.

∎

Lemma 2.2
.

There exists
M>0
big enough such that

\mathbb{P}_{c}\left(\sum_{k}(n(J))_{k}>M\log(N)\right)\leq P_{\mathbf{0}}\cdot N^{-10}.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.1
we have that except from a set of probability at most
P_{\mathbf{0}}\cdot e^{-\epsilon N}
, for each
k=1,\dots,N_{c}
either
n_{k}=0
or
N-N_{c}+k<NR^{2}(1-\delta)
, i.e. outside this set for all
{k>D=\lfloor{NR^{2}(1-\delta)}\rfloor+N_{c}-N}
,
n_{k}=0
. Equivalently,
n_{D+1}=0
. Hence we conclude that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{c}

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{k=1}^{N_{c}}(n(J))_{k}>M\log(N)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}_{c}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{D}(n(J))_{k}>M\log(N),(n(J))_{D+1}=0\right)+e^{-\epsilon N}P_{\mathbf{0}}.

Now, we have for
\mathbf{n}
such that
\sum_{k}^{D}n_{k}>M\log(N)
and

\displaystyle\frac{P_{\mathbf{n}}}{P_{\mathbf{0}}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{k=1}^{\min(D,N_{c})}\frac{Q(N-N_{c}+k-n_{k},NR^{2})}{Q(N-N_{c}+k,NR^{2})}\frac{1-Q(N-N_{c}+k,NR^{2})}{1-Q(N-N_{c}+k-n_{k},NR^{2})}

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{k=1}^{\min(D,cN)}\frac{Q(N-N_{c}+k-n_{k},NR^{2})}{Q(N-N_{c}+k,NR^{2})}

) we get

So choosing

∎

Theorem 2.2
.

There exists a set of vectors,
\mathcal{B}_{N}
, such that
{\mathbb{P}_{c}\left(n(J)\notin\mathcal{B}_{N}\right)\leq KP_{\mathbf{0}}\cdot N^{-10}}
and for

and

(2.13)

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=1}^{\lfloor M\log(N)\rfloor}\frac{Q(N-N_{c}+j-n_{j},NR^{2})}{Q(N-N_{c}+j,NR^{2})}\cdot\frac{1-Q(N-N_{c}+j,NR^{2})}{1-Q(N-N_{c}+j-n_{j},NR^{2})}.

\displaystyle Q(N-N_{c}+j-n_{j},NR^{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{-NR^{2}+(N-N_{c}+j-n_{j})(1+\log\frac{NR^{2}}{N-N_{c}+j-n_{j}})}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\cdot\frac{1}{\frac{NR^{2}}{N-N_{c}+j-n_{j}}-1}\cdot

\displaystyle\cdot\frac{1}{\sqrt{N-N_{c}+j-n_{j}}}\left(1+O(N^{-1})\right)

\displaystyle=A_{N,c,j}e^{(N-N_{c}+j-n_{j})(1+\log\frac{NR^{2}}{N-N_{c}+j-n_{j}})}\left(1+O\left(\frac{n_{j}}{N}\right)\right),

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=1}^{\lfloor M\log(N)\rfloor}e^{(N-N_{c}+j-n_{j})(1+\log\frac{NR^{2}}{N-N_{c}+j-n_{j}})-(N-N_{c}+j)(1+\log\frac{NR^{2}}{N-N_{c}+j})}

\displaystyle\cdot\left(1+O(\frac{n_{j}}{N})+O(e^{-\epsilon N})\right)

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=1}^{\lfloor M\log(N)\rfloor}e^{-n_{j}+(N-N_{c}+j)\log\frac{N-N_{c}+j}{N-N_{c}+j-n_{j}}-n_{j}\log\frac{NR^{2}}{N-N_{c}+j-n_{j}}}\cdot\left(1+O(\frac{n_{j}}{N})\right)

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=1}^{\lfloor M\log(N)\rfloor}e^{-n_{j}+(N-N_{c}+j)(\frac{n_{j}}{N-N_{c}+j}+O(\frac{n_{j}^{2}}{N^{2}}))-n_{j}\log\left(\frac{R^{2}}{1-c}+O(\frac{j+n_{j}}{N})\right)}\cdot\left(1+O\left(\frac{n_{j}}{N}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=1}^{\lfloor M\log(N)\rfloor}e^{-n_{j}\log\frac{R^{2}}{1-c}+O(\frac{jn_{j}+n_{j}^{2}}{N})}\cdot\left(1+O\left(\frac{n_{j}}{N}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1-c}{R^{2}}\right)^{\sum_{j}n_{j}}\left(1+O\left(\frac{\log^{3}N}{N}\right)\right).

This completes the proof.
∎

3
Probability of the Overcrowding Event

In this section we will estimate the probability of the overcrowding event

\mathbb{P}\left(\#\{\lambda\colon\lambda>R\}=N_{c}\right)=\sum_{\#J=N_{c}}\tilde{P}_{J}.

By Theorem
2.1
we have

\tilde{P}_{\mathbf{0}}=\prod_{k=0}^{N-N_{c}}(1-a_{k})\prod_{k=N-N_{c}+1}^{N}a_{k}

where
a_{k}=\frac{\Gamma(k+1,NR^{2})}{k!}=Q(k+1,NR^{2})
. We begin by estimating the first product. Based on Lemma
A.6
we have

\prod_{k=0}^{N-N_{c}}(1-Q(k+1,NR^{2}))\geq\left(1-Q(N-N_{c}+1,NR^{2})\right)^{N-N_{c}+1}.

(3.1)

Now, Lemma
A.4
gives as that

Q(N-N_{c}+1,NR^{2})=\frac{e^{-NR^{2}+(N-N_{c}+1)(1+\log\frac{NR^{2}}{N-N_{c}+1})}}{\sqrt{2\pi(N-N_{c}+1)}(\frac{NR^{2}}{N-N_{c}+1}-1)}\left(1+O\left(\frac{1}{N}\right)\right).

So for some constants
A,\epsilon>0
(that might depends on
c,R
) we have

Q(N-N_{c}+1,NR^{2})\leq Ae^{-\epsilon N}.

Substituting into (
3.1
) we get

1\geq\prod_{k=0}^{N-N_{c}}(1-Q(k+1,NR^{2}))\geq 1-ANe^{-\epsilon N}.

(3.2)

Now we note that

\prod_{N-N_{c}+1}^{N}Q(k+1,NR^{2})=\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{G}_{N_{c}}}(\forall\lambda\colon\left|\lambda\right|>R).

(3.3)

Thus we are left to estimate
\frac{\mathbb{P}(\#J=N_{c})}{\tilde{P}_{\mathbf{0}}}=\frac{\sum_{\mathbf{n}}P_{\mathbf{n}}}{P_{\mathbf{0}}}
.
By Theorem
2.2
we have

\frac{\sum_{\mathbf{n}}P_{\mathbf{n}}}{P_{\mathbf{0}}}=\sum_{\mathbf{n}}\left(\frac{R^{2}}{1-c}\right)^{-\sum_{j=1}^{N}n_{j}}\left(1+O\left(\frac{\log^{3}N}{N}\right)\right).

Hence

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\#J=N_{c})

\displaystyle=P_{\mathbf{0}}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\mathbf{n}\colon\sum_{j=1}^{N}N_{j}=l}\left(\frac{R^{2}}{1-c}\right)^{-l}\left(1+O\left(\frac{\log^{3}N}{N}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=P_{\mathbf{0}}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}p_{N_{c}}(l)\left(\frac{R^{2}}{1-c}\right)^{-l}\left(1+O\left(\frac{\log^{3}N}{N}\right)\right)

where
p_{N_{c}}(l)
is the number of ways to write
l
as a sum of natural numbers no larger than
N_{c}
where order does not matter. We compare this number to the partition function
p(l)
[See Appendix
B
]. As
p_{N_{c}}(l)
is counting a subset of all the ways to write
l
as a sum of natural numbers we have
0\leq p_{N_{c}}(l)\leq p(l)
. In addition since for
l<N_{c}
whenever we write
l
as a sum of natural numbers they all are less than
N_{c}
we have
p_{N_{c}}(l)=p(l)
. Hence

\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}p(l)\left(\frac{R^{2}}{1-c}\right)^{-l}\geq\frac{\mathbb{P}(\#J=N_{c})}{P_{\mathbf{0}}}\left(1+O\left(\frac{\log^{3}N}{N}\right)\right)\geq\sum_{l=0}^{N_{c}}p(l)\left(\frac{R^{2}}{1-c}\right)^{-l}.

Based on Lemma
B.1
for big enough values of
N_{c}
we have
\sum_{l=N_{c}+1}^{\infty}p(l)\leq\left(\frac{R^{2}}{1-c}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}N_{c}}
and so for big enough
N
:

\frac{\mathbb{P}(\#J=N_{c})}{P_{\mathbf{0}}}=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}p(l)\left(\frac{R^{2}}{1-c}\right)^{-l}\left(1+O\left(\frac{\log^{3}N}{N}\right)\right).

(3.4)

Combining (
3.2
), (
3.3
) and (
3.4
) we conclude that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\#\{\lambda:

\displaystyle\left|\lambda\right|>R\}=N_{c})=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{G}_{N_{c}}}(\forall\lambda\colon\left|\lambda\right|>R)\cdot\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}p(l)\left(\frac{R^{2}}{1-c}\right)^{-l}\left(1+O\left(\frac{\log^{3}N}{N}\right)\right)

which completes the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

4
Conditional distribution of eigenvalues

In this section we will use Theorem
2.2
to investigate the conditional distribution of the eigenvalues in three different regions, and show that with suitable rescaling it converges to determinantal point processes. Those are the region inside the disk,
\left|z\right|<R
, the region outside of the disk not close to the boundary,
\left|z\right|>R+\epsilon
, where there is no rescaling required and the region outside the disk near the boundary where there is a normalisation of order
N
, i.e.
\left|z\right|=R+O(\frac{1}{N})
. We begin with the first two cases.

Proposition 4.1
.

For any natural number
k
, and any bounded Lipschitz function with compact support
f\colon\mathbb{C}^{k}\to\mathbb{C}
, the expectation
E_{f}=\mathbb{E}_{\{\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{k}\}\sim\mathcal{G}_{N,c,R}^{\wedge k}}f(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{k})
, satisfies:

1.

Suppose that
\operatorname{supp}f\subseteq\{z\in\mathbb{C}\colon\left|z\right|<R\}^{k}
, then

\left|E_{f}-\mathbb{E}_{(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{k})\sim\mathcal{Y}^{\wedge k}}(f(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{k}))\right|=O\left(\frac{\log N}{N}\right)

where
\mathcal{Y}\sim\sqrt{\frac{N-N_{c}}{N}}\cdot\mathcal{G}_{N-N_{c}}
is distributed as a Ginibre Ensemble of size
N-N_{c}
, scaled by
\sqrt{\frac{N-N_{c}}{N}}
.

2.

Suppose that for some
\epsilon>0
,
\operatorname{supp}f\subseteq\{z\in\mathbb{C}\colon\left|z\right|>R+\epsilon\}^{k}
, then

\left|E_{f}-\mathbb{E}_{\left(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{k}\right)\sim\mathcal{G}_{N}^{\wedge k}}\left(f(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{k})\right)\right|=O\left(\frac{\log N}{N}\right).

Before we get to the proof of the Proposition we will prove a general Lemma.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{Y}_{1},\mathcal{Y}_{2}
be determinantal point processes on
\mathbb{C}
with kernels
K_{1},K_{2}
with respect to the Lebesgue background measure respectively. Let
k
be a natural number and
f\colon\mathbb{C}^{k}\to\mathbb{C}
be a bounded Lipschitz function with support contained in
S^{k}
for a compact set
S\subseteq\mathbb{C}
.
Assume that for all
x,y\in S
,
\left|K_{1}(x,y)\right|,\left|K_{2}(x,y)\right|\leq A
and
\left|K_{1}(x,y)-K_{2}(x,y)\right|\leq B
.
Denote for
m=1,2
,
E_{m,f}=\mathbb{E}(\sum f(y_{1},\dots,y_{k}))
where the sum is over all the ordered subsets of
\mathcal{Y}_{m}
of size
k
. Then for some constant
C
depending on
k,f

\left|E_{1,f}-E_{2,f}\right|\leq CA^{k-1}B.

(4.1)

In addition, if we assume that each of those processes contains exactly
N
points, then

\left|\mathbb{E}_{\{\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{k}\}\sim\mathcal{Y}_{1}^{\wedge k}}f(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{k})-\mathbb{E}_{\{\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{k}\}\sim\mathcal{Y}_{2}^{\wedge k}}f(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{k})\right|\leq\frac{CA^{k-1}B}{N^{k}}.

(4.2)

Remark
.

We do not care about the dependence on
k
so we show an elementary proof here. It is possible to get a better constant
C
, using Lemma 3.4.2 of
[
AGZ10
]
.

Proof.

For
m=1,2
the
k
-point correlation function of
\mathcal{Y}_{i}
is

\displaystyle p_{m}^{(k)}(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})=\det\left(K_{m}(x_{i},x_{j})\right)_{i,j=1}^{k}=\sum_{\pi\in S_{k}}\operatorname{sgn}\left(\pi\right)\prod_{i=1}^{k}K_{m}\left(x_{i},x_{\pi(i)}\right).

Hence, for
x_{1},\dots x_{k}\in S
,

\displaystyle\big|p_{1}^{(k)}(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})

\displaystyle-p_{2}^{(k)}(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})\big|

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{\pi\in S_{k}}\operatorname{sgn}\left(\pi\right)\left(\prod_{i=1}^{k}K_{1}\left(x_{i},x_{\pi(i)}\right)-\prod_{i=1}^{k}K_{2}\left(x_{i},x_{\pi(i)}\right)\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\pi\in S_{k}}\left|\prod_{i=1}^{k}K_{1}\left(x_{i},x_{\pi(i)}\right)-\prod_{i=1}^{k}K_{2}\left(x_{i},x_{\pi(i)}\right)\right|.

(4.3)

We denote
K_{m}\left(x_{i},x_{\pi(i)}\right)=K_{m}^{\pi,i}
and then

\displaystyle\left|\prod_{i=1}^{k}K_{1}^{\pi,i}-\prod_{i=1}^{k}K_{2}^{\pi,i}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left[\prod_{i=1}^{j-1}K_{1}^{\pi,i}\right]\left(K_{1}^{\pi,j}-K_{2}^{\pi,j}\right)\left[\prod_{i=j+1}^{k}K_{2}^{\pi,i}\right]\right|

\displaystyle\leq kA^{k-1}B.

(4.4)

Substituting (
4
) into (
4
) we get

\left|p_{1}^{(k)}(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})-p_{2}^{(k)}(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})\right|\leq\sum_{\pi\in S_{k}}kA^{k-1}B=k!\cdot kA^{k-1}B.

(4.5)

Hence

\displaystyle\left|E_{1,f}-E_{2,f}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{C}^{k}}\left|f(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})\right|\left|p_{1}^{(k)}(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})-p_{2}^{(k)}(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})\right|\mathrm{d}x_{1}\cdots\mathrm{d}x_{k}

\displaystyle\leq k!kA^{k-1}B\cdot\operatorname{Vol}\left(\operatorname{supp}(f)\right)\max\left|f\right|.

That proves (
4.1
).

Now, assuming each process contains exactly
N
points, we have

\mathbb{E}_{\{\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{k}\}\sim\mathcal{Y}_{m}^{\wedge k}}f(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{k})=\binom{N}{k}^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{C}^{k}}f(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})p_{m}^{k}(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})\mathrm{d}x_{1}\cdots\mathrm{d}x_{k}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}_{\{\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{k}\}\sim\mathcal{Y}_{1}^{\wedge k}}f(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{k})-\mathbb{E}_{\{\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{k}\}\sim\mathcal{Y}_{2}^{\wedge k}}f(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{k})\right|

\displaystyle\leq\binom{N}{k}^{-1}k!kA^{k-1}B\cdot\operatorname{Vol}\left(\operatorname{supp}(f)\right)\max\left|f\right|.

Since we have
\binom{N}{k}=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{N-i}{k-i}\geq\left(\frac{N}{k}\right)^{k}
we conclude that for
C(k,f)=C>0
we have

\left|\mathbb{E}_{\{\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{k}\}\sim\mathcal{Y}_{1}^{\wedge k}}f(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{k})-\mathbb{E}_{\{\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{k}\}\sim\mathcal{Y}_{2}^{\wedge k}}f(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{k})\right|\leq\frac{CA^{k-1}B}{N^{k}}.

∎

Now we can go back to prove the two parts of Proposition
4.1
.

Proof of part 1. of Proposition
4.1
.

As
\operatorname{supp}f\subseteq\{z\in\mathbb{C}\colon\left|z\right|<R\}^{k}
, to calculate
E_{f}
we can look at the process restricted to the disk
\{z\in\mathbb{C}\colon\left|z\right|<R\}
. According to Theorem
2.2
, this is a mixture of determinantal point processes indexed by subsets
{J\subseteq\{0,1,\dots,N-1\}}
of size
N_{c}
with kernels

K_{\text{inner}}^{J}(z,w)=\sum_{k\notin J}\phi_{k}(z)\overline{\phi_{k}(w)}\mathds{1}_{\left|z\right|,\left|w\right|<R}.

According to Lemma
2.2
with probability
1-O(N^{-10})
we have that
\sum_{k}\left(n(J)_{k}\right)<M\log(N)
and in particular every
k=1,\dots,N_{c}
satisfies
n_{k}<M\log(N)
and moreover for
k>M\log(N)
,
n_{k}=0
. Now, we denote
l_{k}=N-N_{c}+k
and by (
2.6
) we have, for all
x,y\in D(0,R)
,
k\leq M\log N
,

\displaystyle\delta_{k}

\displaystyle=\left|\tilde{\phi}_{l_{k}-n_{k}}(x)\overline{\tilde{\phi}_{l_{k}-n_{k}}(y)}-\tilde{\phi}_{l_{k}}(x)\overline{\tilde{\phi}_{l_{k}}(y)}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{N^{l_{k}-n_{k}+1}\left|xy\right|^{l_{k}-n_{k}}}{\pi\gamma(l_{k}-n_{k}+1,NR^{2})}-\frac{N^{l_{k}+1}\left|xy\right|^{l_{k}}}{\pi\gamma(l_{k}+1,NR^{2})}\right|e^{-\frac{N}{2}(\left|x\right|^{2}+\left|y\right|^{2})}

\displaystyle\leq N^{l_{k}-n_{k}+1}\left|xy\right|^{l_{k}-n_{k}}e^{-\frac{N}{2}(\left|x\right|^{2}+\left|y\right|^{2})}\left|\frac{1}{\gamma(l_{k}-n_{k}+1,NR^{2})}-\frac{N^{n_{k}}\left|xy\right|^{n_{k}}}{\gamma(l_{k}+1,NR^{2})}\right|.

By the triangle inequality we get

\displaystyle\delta_{k}

\displaystyle\leq N^{l_{k}-n_{k}+1}\left|xy\right|^{l_{k}-n_{k}}e^{-\frac{N}{2}(\left|x\right|^{2}+\left|y\right|^{2})}\bigg(\left|\frac{1}{\Gamma(l_{k}-n_{k}+1)}-\frac{N^{n_{k}}\left|xy\right|^{n_{k}}}{\Gamma(l_{k}+1)}\right|

\displaystyle+\left|\frac{1}{\Gamma(l_{k}-n_{k}+1)}-\frac{1}{\gamma(l_{k}-n_{k}+1,NR^{2})}\right|+\left|\frac{N^{n_{k}}\left|xy\right|^{n_{k}}}{\Gamma(l_{k}+1)}-\frac{N^{n_{k}}\left|xy\right|^{n_{k}}}{\gamma(l_{k}+1,NR^{2})}\right|\bigg)

\displaystyle=A_{1}+N^{l_{k}-n_{k}+1}\left|xy\right|^{l_{k}-n_{k}}e^{-\frac{N}{2}(\left|x\right|^{2}+\left|y\right|^{2})}\left(A_{2}+A_{3}\right).

(4.6)

Then we have by Lemma
A.4

\displaystyle A_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\left|\gamma(l_{k}-n_{k}+1,NR^{2})-\gamma(l_{k}-n_{k}+1)\right|}{\gamma(l_{k}-n_{k}+1,NR^{2})\Gamma(l_{k}-n_{k}+1)}

(4.7)

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(l_{k}-n_{k}+1)-\gamma(l_{k}-n_{k}+1,NR^{2})}{\gamma(l_{k}-n_{k}+1,NR^{2})}

\displaystyle\leq Ke^{-NR^{2}+(l_{k}-n_{k}+1)(1+\log\frac{NR^{2}}{l_{k}-n_{k}+1})}

for some constant
K>0
. Hence, as
l_{k}-N(1-c),n_{k}=O(\log N)
,

A_{2}\leq e^{N\left(-R^{2}+(1-c)(1+\log\frac{R^{2}}{1-c})\right)(1+O(\log(N)))}.

(4.8)

Similarly

A_{3}\leq N^{n_{k}}\left|xy\right|^{n_{k}}e^{N\left(-R^{2}+(1-c)(1+\log\frac{R^{2}}{1-c})\right)(1+O(\log(N)))}.

(4.9)

Meanwhile we have

\displaystyle A_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{N^{l_{k}-n_{k}+1}\left|xy\right|^{l_{k}-n_{k}}e^{-\frac{N}{2}(\left|x\right|^{2}+\left|y\right|^{2})}}{\pi}\left|\frac{1}{\Gamma(l_{k}-n_{k}+1)}-\frac{N^{n_{k}}\left|xy\right|^{n_{k}}}{\Gamma(l_{k}+1)}\right|

\displaystyle=\frac{N^{l_{k}-n_{k}+1}\left|xy\right|^{l_{k}-n_{k}}e^{-\frac{N}{2}(\left|x\right|^{2}+\left|y\right|^{2})}}{\pi\Gamma(l_{k}+1)}\left|\frac{\Gamma(l_{k}+1)}{\Gamma(l_{k}-n_{k}+1)}-N^{n_{k}}\left|xy\right|^{n_{k}}\right|.

We will denote

g(s,t)=\left(st\right)^{\frac{1}{2}(l_{k}-n_{k})}e^{-\frac{N}{2}(s+t)}\left(\frac{1}{\Gamma(l_{k}-n_{k}+1)}-\frac{N^{n_{k}}\left(st\right)^{\frac{1}{2}n_{k}}}{\Gamma(l_{k}+1)}\right)

so that
A_{1}=\frac{N^{l_{k}-n_{k}+1}}{\pi}\left|g\left(\left|x\right|^{2},\left|y\right|^{2}\right)\right|
. In order to bound
A_{1}
we will look for extrema of
g(s,t)
in the quadrant
s,t\geq 0
.

Lemma 4.2
.

For
g
as above,

\max_{s,t>0}\left|N^{l_{k}-n_{k}}g(s,t)\right|\leq\frac{n_{k}}{N(1-c)}\left(1+O\left(\frac{n_{k}}{\sqrt{l_{k}}}\right)\right).

Proof of Lemma
4.2
.

Note that as
g(0,t)=g(\infty,t)=g(s,0)=g(s,\infty)=0
we can look only at points where
\nabla g(s,t)=(0,0)
. We compute

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial g}{\partial s}=g(s,t)\left[\frac{l_{k}-n_{k}}{2s}-\frac{N}{2}-\frac{\frac{N^{n_{k}}n_{k}\left(st\right)^{\frac{1}{2}n_{k}}}{2s\Gamma(l_{k}+1)}}{\frac{1}{\Gamma(l_{k}-n_{k}+1)}-\frac{N^{n_{k}}\left(st\right)^{\frac{1}{2}n_{k}}}{\Gamma(l_{k}+1)}}\right],

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{l_{k}-n_{k}}{s}-N\right)\left(\frac{1}{\Gamma(l_{k}-n_{k}+1)}-\frac{N^{n_{k}}\left(st\right)^{\frac{1}{2}n_{k}}}{\Gamma(l_{k}+1)}\right)-\frac{N^{n_{k}}n_{k}\left(st\right)^{\frac{1}{2}n_{k}}}{s\Gamma(l_{k}+1)}.

Rearranging the terms we get

\displaystyle n_{k}N^{n_{k}}\left(st\right)^{\frac{1}{2}n_{k}}

\displaystyle=\left(l_{k}-n_{k}-Ns\right)\left(\prod_{i=0}^{n_{k}-1}(l_{k}-i)-N^{n_{k}}\left(st\right)^{\frac{1}{2}n_{k}}\right),

\displaystyle l_{k}-n_{k}-Ns

\displaystyle=\frac{n_{k}N^{n_{k}}\left(st\right)^{\frac{1}{2}n_{k}}}{\prod_{i=0}^{n_{k}-1}(l_{k}-i)-N^{n_{k}}\left(st\right)^{\frac{1}{2}n_{k}}}.

Similarly, the equation
\frac{\partial g}{\partial t}=0
gives
l_{k}-n_{k}-Nt=\frac{n_{k}N^{n_{k}}\left(st\right)^{\frac{1}{2}n_{k}}}{\prod_{i=0}^{n_{k}-1}(l_{k}-i)-N^{n_{k}}\left(st\right)^{\frac{1}{2}n_{k}}}
and hence the extremal points satisfy
s=t
. Substituting it back we get

n_{k}N^{n_{k}}s^{n_{k}}=\left(l_{k}-n_{k}-Ns\right)\left(\prod_{i=0}^{n_{k}-1}(l_{k}-i)-N^{n_{k}}s^{n_{k}}\right).

All the solutions satisfy
s=\frac{l_{k}}{N}+o(1)
as for other values of
s\geq 0
the right-hand side is much bigger than the left-hand side. Hence we denote
s=\frac{l_{k}}{N}(1+\epsilon)
and then we have

\displaystyle n_{k}l_{k}^{n_{k}}(1+\epsilon)^{n_{k}}

\displaystyle=l_{k}^{n_{k}+1}(1-\frac{n_{k}}{l_{k}}-1-\epsilon)\left(\prod_{i=0}^{n_{k}-1}\left(\frac{l_{k}-i}{l_{k}}\right)-(1+\epsilon)^{n_{k}}\right)

\displaystyle\frac{n_{k}}{l_{k}}(1+O(\epsilon n_{k}))

\displaystyle=\left(\epsilon+\frac{n_{k}}{l_{k}}\right)\left(\epsilon n_{k}+\frac{n_{k}(n_{k}-1)}{l_{k}}\right)\left(1+O\left(\epsilon n_{k}+\frac{n_{k}^{2}}{l_{k}}\right)\right).

Assuming the solution will satisfy
\frac{1}{\epsilon}\gg n_{k}
we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{l_{k}}

\displaystyle=\epsilon^{2}\left(1+O\left(\epsilon n_{k}+\frac{n_{k}}{\epsilon l_{k}}\right)\right).

Hence

\epsilon=\pm\frac{1+O\left(\epsilon n_{k}+\frac{n_{k}}{\epsilon l_{k}}\right)}{\sqrt{l_{k}}}=\pm\frac{1}{\sqrt{l_{k}}}+O\left(\frac{n_{k}}{l_{k}}\right).

The maximum of
\left|g(s,t)\right|
is attained at
s_{\max}=t_{\max}=\frac{l_{k}}{N}(1+\epsilon)=\frac{l_{k}\pm\sqrt{l_{k}}+O(n_{k})}{N}
.
Thus

\displaystyle\max_{s,t>0}\left|N^{l_{k}-n_{k}}g(s,t)\right|

\displaystyle=e^{(l_{k}-n_{k})\log\left(Ns_{\max}\right)-Ns_{\max}}\left|\frac{1}{\Gamma(l_{k}-n_{k}+1)}-\frac{\left(Ns_{\max}\right)^{n_{k}}}{\Gamma(l_{k}+1)}\right|

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{\phi(-Ns_{\max})}}{\Gamma(l_{k}+1)}\left|\prod_{i=0}^{n_{k}-1}(l_{k}-i)-\left(l_{k}\pm\sqrt{l_{k}}+O(n_{k})\right)^{n_{k}}\right|

where we denoted
\phi(x)=-x+(l_{k}-n_{k})\log x
. Since
\sqrt{l_{k}}\gg n_{k}
we get

\displaystyle\max_{s,t>0}\left|N^{l_{k}-n_{k}}g(s,t)\right|

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{\phi(-Ns_{\max})}}{\Gamma(l_{k}+1)}\left|\pm n_{k}l_{k}^{n_{k}-1}\sqrt{l_{k}}\left(1+O\left(\frac{n_{k}}{\sqrt{l_{k}}}\right)\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{n_{k}l_{k}^{n_{k}-\frac{1}{2}}e^{\phi(-(l_{k}-n_{k})\pm\sqrt{l_{k}}+O(n_{k}))}}{\Gamma(l_{k}+1)}\left(1+O\left(\frac{n_{k}}{\sqrt{l_{k}}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{n_{k}l_{k}^{n_{k}-\frac{1}{2}}e^{\phi(l_{k}-n_{k})+\frac{1}{2(l_{k}-n_{k})}(l_{k}+O(n_{k}\sqrt{l_{k}}))+O(\frac{1}{\sqrt{l_{k}}})}}{\Gamma(l_{k}+1)}\left(1+O\left(\frac{n_{k}}{\sqrt{l_{k}}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{n_{k}l_{k}^{n_{k}-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-l_{k}+n_{k}+(l_{k}-n_{k})\log(l_{k}-n_{k})}e^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\Gamma(l_{k}+1)}\left(1+O\left(\frac{n_{k}}{\sqrt{l_{k}}}\right)\right)

We use the following definitions of the incomplete gamma functions and the normalised incomplete gamma function.

The incomplete gamma functions are defined by

\displaystyle\Gamma(a,s)=\int_{s}^{\infty}x^{a-1}e^{-x}\mathrm{d}x,

\displaystyle\gamma(a,s)=\int_{0}^{s}x^{a-1}e^{-x}\mathrm{d}x.

The normalised incomplete Gamma function is

We will use the following estimates from

Lemma A.1

Let

be fixed. As

\Gamma(a,z)=O(e^{-\frac{z}{2}}).

In addition we have the following estimate for the error function (see
[

, 7.12.1]

Lemma A.3

\operatorname{erfc}(y)=\frac{e^{-y^{2}}}{\sqrt{\pi}}\left(\frac{1}{y}-\frac{1}{2y^{3}}+O(y^{-}5)\right)

By combining Lemma
A.2

Lemma A.4

Appendix B
The Partition Function

The partition function
p(n)
is the number of different way to write
n
as a sum of positive integers, up to summation order.
The asymptotics of the partition function is discussed for example in
[
And98
]
, we will use only a basic claim, proved here for completeness.

Lemma B.1
.

For any constant
k>1
, for
n
big enough the partition function satisfy
p(n)\leq k^{n}
.

Proof.

We look at the function
f(q)=\frac{1}{\prod_{j=1}^{\infty}(1-q^{j})}
. Since it is analytic in the unit disk its Taylor expansion converges there, but as its Taylor expansion is
f(q)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}p(n)q^{n}
we conclude that
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sqrt[n]{p(n)}\leq 1
.
Hence, for any
k>1
, for all except finitely many values of
n
we have
p(n)\leq k^{n}
.
∎

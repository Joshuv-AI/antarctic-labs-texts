The steady Navier-Stokes equations in a system of unbounded channels with sources and sinks
Filippo Gazzola – Mikhail Korobkov – Xiao Ren – Gianmarco Sperone

Abstract
The steady motion of a viscous incompressible fluid in a junction of unbounded channels with sources and sinks is modeled through the Navier-Stokes equations under inhomogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions. In contrast to many previous works, the domain is not assumed to be simply-connected and the fluxes are not assumed to be small. In this very general setting, we prove the existence of a solution with a uniformly bounded Dirichlet integral in every compact subset. This is a generalization of the classical Ladyzhenskaya–Solonnikov result obtained under the additional assumption of zero boundary conditions. For small data of the problem we also prove the unique solvability and attainability of Couette–Poiseuille flows at infinity. The main novelty of our approach is the proof of the corresponding Leray–Hopf-type inequality by Leray’s reductio ad absurdum argument (since the standard Hopf cut-off extension procedure does not work for general boundary data). For this contradiction approach, we use some fine properties of weak solutions to the Euler system based on Morse–Sard-type theorems in Sobolev spaces obtained by Bourgain, Korobkov & Kristensen.

within the class of solutions veryfing the estimate (

Theorem 2.5

(

Let

Suppose that

3
Proof of Theorem
2.2

3.1
The proof at a glance

Among other issues, the proofs of Theorems
2.2
–
2.4
–
2.5
are obtained through the following steps.

- In Section
3.2
we build a special
flux carrier
of the boundary data in (
2.10
)
2
, that is, a solenoidal extension of the boundary
velocity which, moreover, verifies the flux condition (
2.10
)
3
. Such flux carrier is the solution of a suitable Stokes problem in
\Omega_{0}
, and satisfies a
uniform
Leray-Hopf inequality in every outlet. This partition of
\Omega
is a necessary step, in view of the fact that in multiply connected domains one cannot construct
Hopf extensions for general boundary data, see
[
18
,
40
]
.

- In Section
3.3
we adapt to our setting the celebrated method of
invading domains
; under the sole zero total flux condition we are able to find a sequence of solutions

\{({\mathbf{u}}_{k},p_{k})\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
to the Navier-Stokes system in any truncated domain because of Koroblov–Pileckas–Russo general existence theorem, see
[
25
,
27
]
.

- The ultimate step in the proof of Theorem
2.2
is to show that, as
k\to+\infty
and up the extraction of a subsequence,
the sequence
\{({\mathbf{u}}_{k},p_{k})\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
converges locally (in a sense to be made precise later, see Section
3.3
) to a solution
({\mathbf{u}},p)
of the
original problem (
2.10
) which, moreover, satisfies the required estimate (
2.12
).

- To prove the required estimate, the main tool in the Ladyzhenskaya–Solonnikov paper
[
32
]

was the classical Leray–Hopf inequality (see, e.g., (
3.37
) ) obtained by the standard Hopf cut-off procedure under homogeneous boundary conditions. In our case, under general boundary data assumptions, this cut-off procedure does not work anymore, and one cannot expect the Leray–Hopf inequality in the general case, see
[
18
,
40
]
. Nevertheless, fortunately, the Leray–Hopf inequality still can be proved
for large
k
based on a subtle Leray’s
reductio ad absurdum
argument (traced back to the seminal work of Leray
[
34
]
). The cornerstone here is the triviality of the limiting Euler solution (see Appendix
B
) proved using the Korobkov–Pileckas–Russo approach from
[
24
,
25
,
27
]
. In turn, subtle methods of real analysis (Morse-Sard type theorems and fine properties of the level sets of Sobolev functions) developed by Bourgain et al.
[
7
]
are very useful here as well. Compared with the setting of
[
25
]
, the new challenge here is due to the unboundedness of the domain
\Omega
and the linear growth of total Dirichlet energies of the invading domain solutions.

- In Sections
3.4
–
3.5
–
3.6
we bound the growth of the Dirichlet integral of the solutions
\{({\mathbf{u}}_{k},p_{k})\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
on the sequence of invading domains:
this is done in three steps, first the global linear growth, then the uniform linear growth, finally through uniform local estimates. Here, at every step, we use
some elegant real-analysis techniques from
[
32
]
(see Sections
3.5
–
3.6
). We are
so able to prove the uniform bound (
2.12
) in Section
3.7
.

- Finally the uniqueness and asymptotic behavior for small data are obtained in the last Sections
4
–
5
again using estimates from
[
32
]
.

In Figure

In this section we state the existence of a solenoidal vector field in

)

More precisely, we prove:

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be an admissible domain. Suppose that
{\mathbf{a}}\in W^{3/2,2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\partial\Omega)
is a boundary velocity obeying
(
2.6
)-(

{\mathcal{F}}_{1},...,{\mathcal{F}}_{J}\in\mathbb{R}

{\varepsilon}\in(0,1)
,
there exists a vector field
\mathbf{U}\in W^{2,2}_{{\mathrm{loc}}}(\overline{\Omega})
such that

\nabla\cdot\mathbf{U}=0\ \ \mbox{ in }\ \ \Omega\,;\hskip 20.00003pt\mathbf{U}=\mathbf{a}\ \ \mbox{ on }\ \ \partial\Omega\,;\hskip 20.00003pt\int_{\sigma_{j}^{0}}\mathbf{U}\cdot{\mathbf{e}}_{1}^{j}={\mathcal{F}}_{j}\hskip 20.00003pt\forall j\in\{1,\dots,J\}\,.

(3.3)

Moreover, we have the local uniform bound

\|\mathbf{U}\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega\cap B(z_{0}))}\leq C_{*}\hskip 20.00003pt\forall z_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\,,

(3.4)

where
C_{*}>0
is a constant depending on
\Omega
,
{\mathbf{a}}
,
\{\mathcal{F}_{j}\}^{J}_{j=1}
and
\varepsilon
, but is independent of
z_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
.
Finally, given
j\in\{1,\dots,J\}
,
2\leq a<b\leq\infty
, and any vector field
{\boldsymbol{\eta}}\in W^{1,2}\bigl(\Omega_{j}^{a,b}\bigr)
such that

\nabla\cdot{\boldsymbol{\eta}}=0\ \ \mbox{ in }\ \ \Omega_{j}^{a,b}\hskip 20.00003pt\text{and}\hskip 20.00003pt{\boldsymbol{\eta}}=0\ \ \mbox{ on }\ \ \Gamma\cap\partial\Omega_{j}^{a,b}\,,

(3.5)

the Leray-Hopf inequality holds

\left|\int_{\Omega_{j}^{a,b}}({\boldsymbol{\eta}}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{U}\cdot{\boldsymbol{\eta}}\right|+\left|\int_{\Omega_{j}^{a,b}}({\boldsymbol{\eta}}\cdot\nabla){\boldsymbol{\eta}}\cdot\mathbf{U}\right|\leq\varepsilon\,\|\nabla{\boldsymbol{\eta}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{j}^{a,b})}^{2}\,.

(3.6)

It is important that the estimate (

) is independent of the length of the domains
\Omega_{j}^{a,b}
. The proof of Theorem

is based on the standard Hopf cut-off procedure at any outlet (in a translation invariant setting, i.e.,

depends on the vertical variable

\left|\int_{\Omega_{j}^{a,b}}({\boldsymbol{\eta}}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{U}\cdot{\boldsymbol{\eta}}\right|+\left|\int_{\Omega_{j}^{a,b}}({\boldsymbol{\eta}}\cdot\nabla){\boldsymbol{\eta}}\cdot\mathbf{U}\right|\leq\frac{1}{8}\,\|\nabla{\boldsymbol{\eta}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{j}^{a,b})}^{2}

(3.7)

3.3
Leray’s method of
invading domains

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be an admissible domain. Suppose that
{\mathbf{f}}\in L_{\mathrm{loc}}^{2}(\overline{\Omega})
is an external force verifying (
2.5
),
{\mathbf{a}}\in W^{3/2,2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\partial\Omega)
is a boundary velocity obeying (
2.6
)-(
2.7
), and

{\mathcal{F}}_{1},...,{\mathcal{F}}_{J}\in\mathbb{R}
satisfy the compatibility condition (
2.9
). Let

\mathbf{U}\in W^{2,2}_{{\mathrm{loc}}}(\overline{\Omega})
be a flux carrier arising from Theorem
3.1
and satisfying (
3.7
).
Define the vector field
\mathbf{g}\in L_{\mathrm{loc}}^{2}(\overline{\Omega})
by

\mathbf{g}\doteq\mathbf{f}+\Delta\mathbf{U}-(\mathbf{U}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{U}\,.

The strong solution
(\mathbf{u},p)\in W_{\text{loc}}^{2,2}(\Omega)\times W_{\text{loc}}^{1,2}(\Omega)
to (
2.10
) will be sought in the
form

\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{U}+\mathbf{w}\,,

(3.8)

where the vector field
\mathbf{w}\in W_{\text{loc}}^{2,2}(\Omega)
satisfies, in strong form,
the following system:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\Delta\mathbf{w}+(\mathbf{w}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{w}+(\mathbf{U}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{w}+(\mathbf{w}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{U}+\nabla p=\mathbf{g}\,,\quad\nabla\cdot\mathbf{w}=0\ \ \mbox{ in }\ \ \Omega\,,\\[4.0pt]
&\mathbf{w}={\mathbf{0}}\ \ \mbox{ on }\ \ \partial\Omega\,,\\[4.0pt]
&\int_{\sigma_{j}^{0}}\mathbf{w}\cdot{\mathbf{e}}_{1}^{j}=0\qquad\forall j\in\{1,\dots,J\}\,.\end{aligned}\right.

(3.9)

In order to find a strong solution
(\mathbf{w},p)\in W_{\text{loc}}^{2,2}(\Omega)\times W_{\text{loc}}^{1,2}(\Omega)
to (
3.9
), consider a strictly increasing sequence of bounded domains
\{\mathcal{B}_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
(called
invading domains
in the sequel), each one having a boundary of class
\mathcal{C}^{2}
, such that

\Omega^{2}\subset\mathcal{B}_{0}\hskip 20.00003pt\text{and}\hskip 20.00003pt\bigcup\limits_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}\mathcal{B}_{k}=\Omega\,,

(3.10)

where
\Omega^{2}
is as in (
3.2
). Moreover, we require that each

S_{j,k}\doteq\Omega_{j}\cap\partial\mathcal{B}_{k}\hskip 20.00003pt\forall k\in\mathbb{N}\,,\ \forall j\in\{1,\dots,J\}\,,

is a
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-arc obtained from the initial arc
S_{j,1}
by translation along the

x_{j}
-axis (see Figure
3.2
for a depiction). The precise shapes of the arcs
S_{j,1}
are arbitrary as long as they are
tangent to the corresponding walls
\Gamma_{j}^{0}\cup\Gamma_{j}^{1}
, for
\{1,\dots,J\}
, and have zero curvature at their endpoints in such a way that
\partial\mathcal{B}_{k}\in\mathcal{C}^{2}
.

Figure 3.2
:
The
invading domains
procedure.

By Theorem
3.1
, for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
we have

\mathbf{U}\in W^{2,2}(\mathcal{B}_{k})
. From (
3.3
) and the Divergence Theorem we deduce

\int_{S_{j,k}}\mathbf{U}\cdot\mathbf{n}={\mathcal{F}}_{j}\hskip 20.00003pt\forall j\in\{1,\dots,J\}\,,

thereby implying that

\int_{\partial\mathcal{B}_{k}}\mathbf{U}\cdot\mathbf{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{I}\int_{\partial\Sigma_{i}}{\mathbf{a}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}+\sum_{j=1}^{J}\int_{\partial\Omega_{0}\cap\Gamma_{j}}{\mathbf{a}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}+\sum_{j=1}^{J}{\mathcal{F}}_{j}=0\,,

owing to (
2.9
) and (
2.11
). Therefore,
[
27
, Theorems 5.1.1 and 2.4.3]
(cf. with
[
25
, Theorem 1.1]
)
ensure the existence of a strong solution
({\mathbf{u}}_{k},p_{k})\in W^{2,2}(\mathcal{B}_{k})\times W^{1,2}(\mathcal{B}_{k})
to the following
boundary-value problem:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\Delta\mathbf{u}_{k}+(\mathbf{u}_{k}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}_{k}+\nabla p_{k}=\mathbf{f}\quad\mbox{ in }\ \mathcal{B}_{k}\,,\quad\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}_{k}=0\ \ \mbox{ in }\ \ \mathcal{B}_{k}\,,\\[4.0pt]
&\mathbf{u}_{k}=\mathbf{U}\ \ \mbox{ on }\ \ \partial\mathcal{B}_{k}\,.\end{aligned}\right.

(3.11)

As in (
3.9
), given
k\in\mathbb{N}
we set

\mathbf{u}_{k}\doteq\mathbf{U}+\mathbf{w}_{k},

and the vector field
\mathbf{w}_{k}\in W^{2,2}(\mathcal{B}_{k})\cap W_{0}^{1,2}(\mathcal{B}_{k})
must then satisfy, in strong form, the following system:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\Delta\mathbf{w}_{k}+(\mathbf{w}_{k}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{w}_{k}+(\mathbf{U}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{w}_{k}+(\mathbf{w}_{k}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{U}+\nabla p_{k}=\mathbf{g}\qquad\mbox{ in }\ \ \mathcal{B}_{k},\\
&\nabla\cdot\mathbf{w}_{k}=0\qquad\mbox{ in }\ \ \mathcal{B}_{k},\\
&\mathbf{w}_{k}={\mathbf{0}}\qquad\mbox{ on }\ \ \partial\mathcal{B}_{k},\end{aligned}\right.

(3.12)

where
\mathbf{g}=\mathbf{f}+\Delta\mathbf{U}-(\mathbf{U}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{U}\in L^{2}(\mathcal{B}_{k})
. In particular, setting

W^{1,2}_{0,\sigma}(\mathcal{B}_{k})\doteq\left\{\mathbf{v}\in W_{0}^{1,2}(\mathcal{B}_{k})\ \big|\ \nabla\cdot\mathbf{v}=0\ \ \mbox{in}\ \ \mathcal{B}_{k}\right\}\,,

it follows that
\mathbf{w}_{k}\in W_{0,\sigma}^{1,2}(\mathcal{B}_{k})
is a
weak solution
to (
3.12

\int_{\mathcal{B}_{k}}\nabla\mathbf{w}_{k}\cdot\nabla{\boldsymbol{\eta}}+\int_{\mathcal{B}_{k}}(\mathbf{w}_{k}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{w}_{k}\cdot{\boldsymbol{\eta}}+\int_{\mathcal{B}_{k}}(\mathbf{U}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{w}_{k}\cdot{\boldsymbol{\eta}}+\int_{\mathcal{B}_{k}}(\mathbf{w}_{k}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{U}\cdot{\boldsymbol{\eta}}=\int_{\mathcal{B}_{k}}\mathbf{g}\cdot{\boldsymbol{\eta}}

(3.13)

for all
{\boldsymbol{\eta}}\in W^{1,2}_{0,\sigma}(\mathcal{B}_{k})
. Let us define

J_{k}\doteq\|\nabla\mathbf{w}_{k}\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{B}_{k})}\,,\hskip 20.00003pt\widehat{\mathbf{w}}_{k}\doteq\frac{1}{J_{k}}\,\mathbf{w}_{k}\,,\hskip 20.00003pt\widehat{p}_{k}\doteq\frac{1}{J^{2}_{k}}\,p_{k}\hskip 20.00003pt\forall k\in\mathbb{N}\,.

(3.14)

We extend
\widehat{\mathbf{w}}_{k}
trivially by zero to the whole
\Omega
(keeping the same notation), so that

(\widehat{\mathbf{w}}_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset W_{0,\sigma}^{1,2}(\Omega)

After setting (
3.14
), we let

. Two situations may arise.

Case (I):
if

\liminf\limits_{k\to\infty}J_{k}<+\infty\,,

(3.15)

then the proof of Theorem

can be finished almost immediately. Indeed, since the Poincaré inequality holds in
\Omega
, along a subsequence,
(\mathbf{w}_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is uniformly bounded with respect to

, ensuring the
existence of a vector field
\mathbf{w}\in W_{0,\sigma}^{1,2}(\Omega)
such that

\mathbf{w}_{k}\rightharpoonup\mathbf{w}\ \ \ \text{weakly in}\ W_{0,\sigma}^{1,2}(\Omega)\ \ \ \text{as}\ \ k\to+\infty\,.

(3.16)

Since
{\mathbf{w}}_{k}
are solutions to (
3.12
) in domains
\mathcal{B}_{k}
,
the limit
{\mathbf{w}}
is a weak solution to (
3.9
) with

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla{\mathbf{w}}|^{2}<\infty.

(3.17)

Furthermore, since
\mathbf{U}\in W^{2,2}_{{\mathrm{loc}}}(\overline{\Omega})
,
\mathbf{g}\in L_{\mathrm{loc}}^{2}(\overline{\Omega})
, and
\partial\Omega
is
of class

[

, and the uniform estimates (

) hold (because of (

in Case (I). Therefore, from now on we will always assume that an alternative option is taking place, that means,

Case (II):
there holds

\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}J_{k}=+\infty\,.

This case is much more involved because, it relies on the study of several properties of weak solutions to a stationary Euler system, see (

) below.
By construction, for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
, the normalized functions in (
3.14
) satisfy (in strong form) the system

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\dfrac{1}{J_{k}}\Delta\widehat{\mathbf{w}}_{k}+(\widehat{\mathbf{w}}_{k}\cdot\nabla)\widehat{\mathbf{w}}_{k}+\dfrac{1}{J_{k}}(\mathbf{U}\cdot\nabla)\widehat{\mathbf{w}}_{k}+\dfrac{1}{J_{k}}(\widehat{\mathbf{w}}_{k}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{U}+\nabla\widehat{p}_{k}=\dfrac{1}{J^{2}_{k}}\,\mathbf{g}\ \ \mbox{ in }\ \ \mathcal{B}_{k}\,,\\[4.0pt]

&\widehat{\mathbf{w}}_{k}={\mathbf{0}}\ \ \mbox{ on }\ \ \partial\mathcal{B}_{k}\,,\end{aligned}\right.

(3.18)

its weak formulation

\dfrac{1}{J_{k}}\int_{\mathcal{B}_{k}}\nabla\widehat{\mathbf{w}}_{k}\cdot\nabla{\boldsymbol{\eta}}+\int_{\mathcal{B}_{k}}(\widehat{\mathbf{w}}_{k}\cdot\nabla)\widehat{\mathbf{w}}_{k}\cdot{\boldsymbol{\eta}}+\dfrac{1}{J_{k}}\int_{\mathcal{B}_{k}}[(\mathbf{U}\cdot\nabla)\widehat{\mathbf{w}}_{k}+(\widehat{\mathbf{w}}_{k}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{U}]\cdot{\boldsymbol{\eta}}=\dfrac{1}{J^{2}_{k}}\int_{\mathcal{B}_{k}}\mathbf{g}\cdot{\boldsymbol{\eta}}

(3.19)

for all
{\boldsymbol{\eta}}\in W^{1,2}_{0,\sigma}(\mathcal{B}_{k})
, together with the identity

\int_{\mathcal{B}_{k}}|\nabla\widehat{\mathbf{w}}_{k}|^{2}=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\widehat{\mathbf{w}}_{k}|^{2}=1\,.

(3.20)

Then, there exists a vector field
\mathbf{v}\in W_{0,\sigma}^{1,2}(\Omega)
such that, for every
t\in[2,+\infty)
,

\widehat{\mathbf{w}}_{k}\rightharpoonup\mathbf{v}\ \ \ \text{weakly in}\ W_{0,\sigma}^{1,2}(\Omega)\hskip 20.00003pt\text{and}\hskip 20.00003pt\widehat{\mathbf{w}}_{k}\to\mathbf{v}\ \ \ \text{strongly in}\ L_{\text{loc}}^{t}(\Omega)\ \ \ \text{as}\ \ k\to+\infty\,,

(3.21)

and, moreover,

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla{\mathbf{v}}|^{2}\leq 1\,.

(3.22)

Proceeding as in
[
24
,
25
]
, taking the limit as
k\to+\infty
in (
3.19
) allows us to obtain a scalar pressure
q\in L_{\text{loc}}^{2}(\Omega)
such that

\int_{\Omega}(\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{v}\cdot{\boldsymbol{\eta}}-\int_{\Omega}q(\nabla\cdot{\boldsymbol{\eta}})=0\hskip 20.00003pt\forall{\boldsymbol{\eta}}\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)\,.

(3.23)

Therefore, the pair
(\mathbf{v},q)\in W_{0,\sigma}^{1,2}(\Omega)\times L_{\text{loc}}^{2}(\Omega)
is a weak solution to the following stationary Euler system:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &(\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{v}+\nabla q={\mathbf{0}}\ \ \mbox{ in }\ \ \Omega\,,\\[4.0pt]
&\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v}=0\ \ \mbox{ in }\ \ \Omega\,,\\[4.0pt]
&\mathbf{v}={\mathbf{0}}\ \ \mbox{ on }\ \ \partial\Omega\,.\end{aligned}\right.

(3.24)

By Sobolev embedding we have
(\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{v}\in L^{s}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\bar{\Omega})
for every
s\in[1,2)
, so that

q\in W^{1,s}_{\text{loc}}(\bar{\Omega})\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for every}\ s\in[1,2)\,.

(3.25)

The next assertion is one of the main novelties of the present paper and it is fundamental for our subsequent arguments.

Theorem 3.2
.

Under above assumptions the solution
{\mathbf{v}}
to the Euler system (
3.24
), obtained through (
3.21
), is
trivial, that is,

{\mathbf{v}}={\mathbf{0}}\ \ \mbox{ almost everywhere in }\ \Omega\,.

(3.26)

Since the proof of (
3.26
) is quite lengthy, involving several subtle tools from real analysis and topology, we postpone it to Appendix
B
. Note that, unlike the situation in
[

]
, the triviality of the limit solution
does not
entail a contradiction and by no means ends the proof of Theorem

. However, identity (

) plays a key role in proving the Leray–Hopf inequality (see (

) below) and in establishing a global linear estimate (

)
for the energy of the constructed solutions.

3.4
The global linear growth of the Dirichlet integral

Let
{\mathbf{w}}_{k}
be as in (

),

\mathcal{B}_{k}
in Figure

, and

in (
3.2
).

For the invading domains, from now on we assume that

\Omega^{t_{k}}\subset\mathcal{B}_{k}\subset\Omega^{t_{k}+5}

(3.27)

for some sequence of

. The aim of this section is to prove the following global estimate:

J^{2}_{k}=\int\limits_{\mathcal{B}_{k}}|\nabla{\mathbf{w}}_{k}|^{2}\leq c_{0}\,t_{k}

(3.28)

for some
c_{0}>0
independent of

. By contradiction, suppose that (

) is not true; then, up to a subsequence, we have

\frac{J_{k}^{2}}{t_{k}}\to+\infty\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{ as }\ k\to+\infty.

(3.29)

Taking (

) with
\boldsymbol{\eta}\doteq{\mathbf{w}}_{k}
, we get

\displaystyle J_{k}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{\mathcal{B}_{k}}\mathbf{g}\cdot{\mathbf{w}}_{k}-\int\limits_{\mathcal{B}_{k}}(\mathbf{w}_{k}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{U}\cdot{\mathbf{w}}_{k}

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{\mathcal{B}_{k}}\mathbf{g}\cdot{\mathbf{w}}_{k}-\int\limits_{\mathcal{B}_{k}\setminus\Omega^{2}}(\mathbf{w}_{k}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{U}\cdot{\mathbf{w}}_{k}-\int\limits_{\Omega^{2}}(\mathbf{w}_{k}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{U}\cdot{\mathbf{w}}_{k}=:I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}.

(3.30)

Due to (
2.5
) and (
3.4
), we have
\|\mathbf{g}\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{B}_{k})}\lesssim\sqrt{t_{k}}
and, hence,
by the Hölder inequality and (
3.29
) (combined with the Poincaré inequality), we obtain

|I_{1}|={\varepsilon}_{k}\,J_{k}^{2}

(3.31)

with
{\varepsilon}_{k}\to 0
as

. Further, by the Leray-Hopf inequality (

), we have

|I_{2}|\leq\frac{1}{8}\,{J_{k}^{2}}.

Therefore, (

{J_{k}^{2}}\leq 2I_{3}=-2\int\limits_{\Omega^{2}}(\mathbf{w}_{k}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{U}\cdot{\mathbf{w}}_{k}

for
k
large enough. Let
\widehat{\mathbf{w}}_{k}
be as in (

-2\int\limits_{\Omega^{2}}(\widehat{\mathbf{w}}_{k}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{U}\cdot\widehat{\mathbf{w}}_{k}\geq 1

for
k
large enough.
Since
\Omega^{2}
is a bounded domain with Lipschitz boundary, we have the weak convergence
\widehat{\mathbf{w}}_{k}\rightharpoonup{\mathbf{v}}
in

and the strong convergence

is the corresponding limiting solution to the Euler system (

3.26

3.5
The uniform linear growth of the Dirichlet integral

Let
{\mathbf{w}}_{k}

The main tool for the subsequent arguments is the following Leray–Hopf type inequality.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
t_{k}

(3.36)

\biggl|\int\limits_{\Omega^{t}}(\mathbf{w}_{k}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{U}\cdot{\mathbf{w}}_{k}\biggr|\leq\frac{1}{4}\int\limits_{\Omega^{t}}|\nabla{\mathbf{w}}_{k}|^{2}\hskip 20.00003pt\forall t\in[t_{*},t_{k}].

We prove (

) by contradiction. Assume it fails, then, by passing to a subsequence, there exists a strictly increasing sequence of numbers

such that

\biggl|\int\limits_{\Omega^{2}}(\mathbf{w}_{k}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{U}\cdot{\mathbf{w}}_{k}\biggr|>\frac{1}{8}\int\limits_{\Omega^{t^{*}_{k}}}|\nabla{\mathbf{w}}_{k}|^{2}.

(3.38)

Denote

\widetilde{J}_{k}\doteq\left(\int_{\Omega^{t^{*}_{k}}}|\nabla{\mathbf{w}}_{k}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},\hskip 20.00003pt\widetilde{{\mathbf{w}}}_{k}\doteq\frac{1}{\widetilde{J}_{k}}{\mathbf{w}}_{k},\hskip 20.00003pt\widetilde{p}_{k}\doteq\frac{1}{\bigl(\widetilde{J}_{k}\bigr)^{2}}p_{k}.

(3.39)

By (

), we have
\widetilde{J}_{k}\to+\infty
. Hence,
\widetilde{{\mathbf{w}}}_{k}
converges weakly to an Euler solution
\widetilde{{\mathbf{v}}}

in
\Omega

3.26

(3.40)

From (

\biggl|\int\limits_{\Omega^{2}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}_{k}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{U}\cdot\widetilde{{\mathbf{w}}}_{k}\biggr|>\frac{1}{8},

(3.41)

and taking the limit for

which contradicts (

3.7

3.36

The main result of this section is a uniform growth estimate for the Dirichlet integrals, see Lemmas

–

below.
We prove it by using the Ladyzhenskaya & Solonnikov approach
[

, with adaptations to our more general

instead of the usual Leray-Hopf inequality. For the reader’s convenience, we will repeat
the main ideas of
[
32
]
in detail but with some simplifications. We need the following slight variant of an elementary comparison principle from one-dimensional real analysis due to Ladyzhenskaya & Solonnikov
[

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
h(t)
and
\varphi(t)
be smooth nondecreasing nonnegative functions satisfying the following estimates:

h(t)\leq\Psi\bigl(h^{\prime}(t)\bigr)+\frac{1}{2}\varphi(t)\hskip 20.00003pt\forall t\in[t_{0},T],

(3.43)

{\varphi}(t)\geq 2\,\Psi\bigl({\varphi}^{\prime}(t)\bigr)\hskip 20.00003pt\forall t\in[t_{0},T],

(3.44)

where
\Psi:{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
is a nondecreasing function. Suppose also that

h(T)\leq{\varphi}(T),

(3.45)

then

h(t)\leq{\varphi}(t)\hskip 20.00003pt\forall t\in[t_{0},T].

(3.46)

Proof.

Suppose that

h(t)>{\varphi}(t)

(3.47)

for some
t\in[t_{0},T)
, then

\Psi\bigl(h^{\prime}(t)\bigr)\overset{(\ref{ug2})}{\geq}h(t)-\frac{1}{2}{\varphi}(t)\overset{(\ref{ug6})}{>}\frac{1}{2}{\varphi}(t)\overset{(\ref{ug3})}{\geq}\Psi\bigl({\varphi}^{\prime}(t)\bigr),

therefore,

h^{\prime}(t)>{\varphi}^{\prime}(t)

for all
t
satisfying (
3.47
). Hence, (
3.47
) remains true until
t=T
, which contradicts (
3.45
).

\Box

Lemma 3.5
.

Under the assumption (
3.35
), there holds that

D_{k}(t)\doteq\int\limits_{\Omega^{t}}|\nabla{\mathbf{w}}_{k}|^{2}\leq c_{0}t+c_{1},\hskip 20.00003pt\forall t\geq 0,

for some
c_{0},c_{1}>0
independent of
k
and

Proof.

We assume without loss of generality that, for all

, the inclusion
\Omega^{t_{*}}\subset\mathcal{B}_{k}
holds and (
3.36
) is fulfilled. In view of (
3.28

3.48
)
for

is from (
3.27
) (recall, that
{\mathbf{w}}_{k}
is extended by zero outside of
\mathcal{B}_{k}
). Testing (
3.12

{\mathbf{w}}_{k}
in

(3.50)

Applying (

\frac{3}{4}D_{k}(t)\leq\int\limits_{{\Omega}^{t}}\mathbf{g}\cdot{\mathbf{w}}_{k}+\frac{1}{2}\int\limits_{\sigma^{t}}\biggl(\nabla|{\mathbf{w}}_{k}|^{2}-|{\mathbf{w}}_{k}|^{2}{\mathbf{u}}_{k}-2\,p_{k}{\mathbf{w}}_{k}\biggr)\cdot{\mathbf{n}},\hskip 20.00003pt\forall t\in[t_{*},t_{k}].

By the geometry of

and Poincaré’s inequality, there holds

If we put

(3.55)

then by integrating (

h_{k}(t)\leq\frac{16{\tilde{c}}}{9}\,G(t)+\frac{4}{3}\int\limits_{\Omega^{t-1,t}}\biggl(\nabla|{\mathbf{w}}_{k}|^{2}-|{\mathbf{w}}_{k}|^{2}{\mathbf{u}}_{k}-2\,p_{k}{\mathbf{w}}_{k}\biggr)\cdot{\mathbf{n}},\hskip 20.00003pt\forall t\in[t_{*}+1,t_{k}].

\biggl|\int\limits_{\Omega^{t-1,t}}\biggl(\nabla|{\mathbf{w}}_{k}|^{2}-|{\mathbf{w}}_{k}|^{2}{\mathbf{u}}_{k}-2\,p_{k}{\mathbf{w}}_{k}\biggr)\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\biggr|\leq c_{*}\biggl[\ \int\limits_{\Omega^{t-1,t}}|\nabla{\mathbf{w}}_{k}|^{2}+\biggl(\int\limits_{\Omega^{t-1,t}}|\nabla{\mathbf{w}}_{k}|^{2}\biggr)^{\frac{3}{2}}\biggr]

h_{k}(t_{k})\leq{\varphi}(t_{k}),\hskip 20.00003pt2c_{2}\bigl(c_{3}+c_{3}^{\frac{3}{2}}\bigr)\leq{\varphi}(t_{*}+1),\hskip 20.00003pt2c_{2}\,G(t)\leq{\varphi}(t),\hskip 20.00003pt\forall t\in[t_{*}+1,t_{k}].

, we get

\displaystyle 2\int\limits_{\Omega^{t}}\Phi

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{I}\check{q}_{i}\int\limits_{\partial\Sigma_{i}}z\cdot{\mathbf{n}}+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{J}\int\limits_{\sigma^{t}_{j}}\bigl(qz\cdot{\mathbf{n}}+v_{1}^{(j)}{\mathbf{v}}\cdot z\bigr)\,dy_{j}

\displaystyle=-2\sum\limits_{i=1}^{I}\check{q}_{i}\,|\Sigma_{i}|+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{J}\int\limits_{\sigma^{t}_{j}}\bigl(qz\cdot{\mathbf{n}}+v_{1}^{(j)}{\mathbf{v}}\cdot z\bigr)\,dy_{j}.

(B.28)

Here,
{\mathbf{n}}
is the outward normal vector with respect to
\partial\Omega^{t}
, and
|\Sigma_{i}|
is the measure of the bounded domains
\Sigma_{i}
, and
v_{1}^{(j)}
is the first component of
\mathbf{v}
in the
(x_{j},y_{j})
-coordinates.
Therefore, there holds that

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1}^{J}\int\limits_{\sigma^{t}_{j}}\bigl(|q|+{\mathbf{v}}^{2}\bigr)\,dy_{j}

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{c_{0}}{t}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{J}\int\limits_{\sigma^{t}_{j}}\bigl(qz\cdot{\mathbf{n}}+v_{1}^{(j)}{\mathbf{v}}\cdot z\bigr)\,dy_{j}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2c_{0}}{t}\biggl(\int\limits_{\Omega^{t}}\Phi+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{I}\check{q}_{i}\,|\Sigma_{i}|\biggr)\overset{(\ref{as1-axxx}),(\ref{i-as5})}{\geq}\frac{c_{1}}{t}

(B.29)

with some positive constants
c_{0},c_{1}
. But the last inequality definitely contradicts the fact that

|q|+{\mathbf{v}}^{2}\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
(see (
B.5
), (
B.11
) ). We have proved Lemma
B.7
, and this finishes the proof of Theorem
3.2
.

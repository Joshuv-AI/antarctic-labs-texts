Generating functions for quantum metric, Berry curvature, and quantum Fisher information matrix
Wei Chen

Abstract
We elaborate that the fidelity between two density matrices is a generating function, through which the quantum Fisher information matrix and Christoffel symbol of the first kind in the parameter space can be obtained through derivatives with respect to the parameters. For pure states, the fidelity and phase of the product between two quantum states are shown to be the generating functions of the quantum metric and Berry curvature, respectively. Further limiting to systems described by real wave functions, our formalism recovers the well-known result that the fidelity between two probability mass functions is the generating function of the classical Fisher information matrix, indicating a hierarchy of quantum to information geometry. The Bloch representation of the generating functions is given explicitly for 2 × 2 2\times 2 density matrices, and the application to canonical ensemble of Su-Schrieffer-Heeger model suggests the mitigation of quantum geometry at finite temperature.

2
Mixed state formalism

2.1
Fidelity as a generating function for QFIM

Our discussion starts by considering a mixed state density matrix
\rho
that depends on a set of
D
-dimensional parameters
{\bf x}=(x^{1},x^{2}...x^{D})

\displaystyle\rho({\bf x})=\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}({\bf x})|i({\bf x})\rangle\langle i({\bf x})|,

(1)

where we have expanded it in terms of its eigenstates
|i({\bf x})\rangle
and eigenvalues
\lambda_{i}({\bf x})
that all depend on the parameters
{\bf x}
.
From the density matrix, one introduces the SLD
L_{\mu}
defined from the derivative

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}\rho=\frac{1}{2}\left(\rho L_{\mu}+L_{\mu}\rho\right).

(2)

where
\partial_{\mu}=\partial/\partial x^{\mu}
denotes the derivative with respect to a specific parameter. Sandwiching this expression between
\langle i|
and
|j\rangle
, the matrix elements of the SLD are solved by
[
6
]

\displaystyle\langle i|L_{\mu}|j\rangle=\frac{\partial_{\mu}\lambda_{i}}{\lambda_{i}}\delta_{ij}+\frac{2(\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{i})}{\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{j}}\langle i|\partial_{\mu}j\rangle.

(3)

The QFIM is defined from the SLD by

\displaystyle F_{\mu\nu}=4g_{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{2}{\rm Tr}\rho\left\{L_{\mu},L_{\nu}\right\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\frac{\partial_{\mu}\lambda_{i}\partial_{\nu}\lambda_{i}}{\lambda_{i}}+\sum_{ij}\frac{4\lambda_{i}(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j})^{2}}{(\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{j})^{2}}{\rm Re}\left[\langle i|\partial_{\mu}j\rangle\langle\partial_{\nu}j|i\rangle\right].

(4)

We have defined the mixed state quantum metric
g_{\mu\nu}
as trivially a quarter of the QFIM, introduced purely for the sake of demonstrating that it descent to the usual quantum metric in the pure state limit
[
28
]
, as we shall see in Sec.
2.3
.

We now prove that the fidelity between two density matrices
[
20
,
21
,
22
]
, or the logarithm of it, is a generating function for QFIM. We start by expanding the following product between two density matrices
\rho({\bf x})
and
\rho({\bf x+\delta x})
that are infinitely close in the parameter space into infinite series
[
6
]

\displaystyle\sqrt{\sqrt{\rho({\bf x})}\rho({\bf x+\delta x})\sqrt{\rho({\bf x})}}

\displaystyle=\rho+W_{\mu}\delta x^{\mu}+Y_{\mu\nu}\delta x^{\mu}\delta x^{\nu}+Z_{\mu\nu\lambda}\delta x^{\mu}\delta x^{\nu}\delta x^{\lambda}+...

(5)

Now consider two points
{\bf x}
and
{\bf x^{\prime}}
on the manifold that are not necessarily close to each other, and denote their fidelity by

\displaystyle|\tilde{B}({\bf x,x^{\prime}})|\equiv{\rm Tr}\sqrt{\sqrt{\rho({\bf x})}\rho({\bf x^{\prime}})\sqrt{\rho({\bf x})}}

(6)

\displaystyle={\rm Tr}\sqrt{\sqrt{\rho({\bf x})}\rho({\bf x+(x^{\prime}-x)})\sqrt{\rho({\bf x})}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1+{\rm Tr}W_{\mu}(x^{\prime}-x)^{\mu}+{\rm Tr}Y_{\mu\nu}(x^{\prime}-x)^{\mu}(x^{\prime}-x)^{\nu}

\displaystyle+{\rm Tr}Z_{\mu\nu\lambda}(x^{\prime}-x)^{\mu}(x^{\prime}-x)^{\nu}(x^{\prime}-x)^{\lambda}+...

where we have expanded to the third order in
{\bf x^{\prime}-x}
that is sufficient to demonstrate our argument, and one should keep in mind that
\left\{{\rm Tr}W_{\mu},{\rm Tr}Y_{\mu\nu},{\rm Tr}Z_{\mu\nu\lambda}\right\}
are functions of
{\bf x}
where the series is expanded from. Specifically, the leading order term has the expression
[
6
]

\displaystyle\langle i|W_{\mu}|j\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\mu}\lambda_{i}\delta_{ij}-\frac{\sqrt{\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}}(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j})}{\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{j}}\langle i|\partial_{\mu}j\rangle,

(7)

which vanishes after taking trace
{\rm Tr}W_{\mu}=0
, so we will ignore this term. To see the generating function, we denote the derivatives on the logarithm of fidelity by

\displaystyle\overline{B}_{\mu;}=-i\partial_{\mu^{\prime}}\ln\big|\tilde{B}\big|_{\bf x=x^{\prime}},\quad\overline{B}_{\,;\nu}=i\partial_{\nu}\ln\big|\tilde{B}\big|_{\bf x=x^{\prime}},

(8)

where
\partial_{\mu^{\prime}}=\partial/\partial x^{\prime\mu}
and
\partial_{\nu}=\partial/\partial x^{\nu}
are derivatives with respect to the two parameters
{\bf x^{\prime}}
and
{\bf x}
, respectively. Taking dirivative on both points gives

\displaystyle\overline{B}_{\mu;\nu}=(-i\partial_{\mu^{\prime}})(i\partial_{\nu})\ln\big|\tilde{B}\big|_{\bf x=x^{\prime}}=\partial_{\mu^{\prime}}\left[\frac{1}{|\tilde{B}|}\partial_{\nu}|\tilde{B}|\right]_{\bf x=x^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\left[\frac{1}{|\tilde{B}|}\partial_{\mu^{\prime}}\partial_{\nu}|\tilde{B}|-\frac{1}{|\tilde{B}|^{2}}\partial_{\mu^{\prime}}|\tilde{B}|\partial_{\nu}|\tilde{B}|\right]_{\bf x=x^{\prime}}.

(9)

We then observe that the first derivatives vanish after taking
{\bf x^{\prime}=x}

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu^{\prime}}\big|\tilde{B}\big|_{\bf x=x^{\prime}}=\left[\left({\rm Tr}Y_{\mu\nu}+{\rm Tr}Y_{\nu\mu}\right)(x^{\prime}-x)^{\nu}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\left({\rm Tr}Z_{\mu\nu\lambda}+{\rm Tr}Z_{\nu\mu\lambda}+{\rm Tr}Z_{\nu\lambda\mu}\right)(x^{\prime}-x)^{\nu}(x^{\prime}-x)^{\lambda}+...\right]_{\bf x=x^{\prime}}=0,

\displaystyle\partial_{\nu}\big|\tilde{B}\big|_{\bf x=x^{\prime}}=\left[-\left({\rm Tr}Y_{\mu\nu}+{\rm Tr}Y_{\nu\mu}\right)(x^{\prime}-x)^{\nu}\right.

\displaystyle-\left({\rm Tr}Z_{\nu\mu\lambda}+{\rm Tr}Z_{\mu\nu\lambda}+{\rm Tr}Z_{\mu\lambda\nu}\right)(x^{\prime}-x)^{\mu}(x^{\prime}-x)^{\lambda}

\displaystyle+(\partial_{\nu}{\rm Tr}Y_{ab})(x^{\prime}-x)^{a}(x^{\prime}-x)^{b}

\displaystyle\left.+(\partial_{\nu}{\rm Tr}Z_{abc})(x^{\prime}-x)^{a}(x^{\prime}-x)^{b}(x^{\prime}-x)^{c}+...\right]_{\bf x=x^{\prime}}=0,

(10)

but the second derivative is finite

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu^{\prime}}\partial_{\nu}\big|\tilde{B}\big|_{\bf x=x^{\prime}}=\left[-{\rm Tr}Y_{\nu\mu}-{\rm Tr}Y_{\mu\nu}\right.

\displaystyle-{\rm Tr}\left(Z_{\mu\nu\lambda}+Z_{\mu\lambda\nu}+Z_{\nu\mu\lambda}+Z_{\nu\lambda\mu}+Z_{\lambda\mu\nu}+Z_{\lambda\nu\mu}\right)(x^{\prime}-x)^{\lambda}

\displaystyle+\left(\partial_{\nu}{\rm Tr}Y_{\mu a}+\partial_{\nu}{\rm Tr}Y_{a\mu}\right)(x^{\prime}-x)^{a}

\displaystyle\left.+\partial_{\nu}{\rm Tr}\left(Z_{\mu ab}+Z_{a\mu b}+Z_{ab\mu}\right)(x^{\prime}-x)^{a}(x^{\prime}-x)^{b}+...\right]_{\bf x=x^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=-2{\rm Tr}Y_{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{4}F_{\mu\nu}.

(11)

Inserting these results into Eq. (
9
) and using the fact that the fidelity at the same point is unity
\big|\tilde{B}\big|_{\bf x=x^{\prime}}=1
, we see that either the fidelity or its logarithm serves as a generating function for the QFIM, in the sense that the QFIM can be obtained by taking derivatives

\displaystyle\overline{B}_{\mu;\nu}=\left[(-i\partial_{\mu^{\prime}})(i\partial_{\nu}){\rm Tr}\sqrt{\sqrt{\rho({\bf x})}\rho({\bf x^{\prime}})\sqrt{\rho({\bf x})}}\right]_{\bf x=x^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\left[(-i\partial_{\mu^{\prime}})(i\partial_{\nu})\ln{\rm Tr}\sqrt{\sqrt{\rho({\bf x})}\rho({\bf x^{\prime}})\sqrt{\rho({\bf x})}}\right]_{\bf x=x^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{4}F_{\mu\nu}=g_{\mu\nu}.

(12)

The version that uses the fidelity is more in line with the generating function in the information geometry that will be reviewed in Sec.
2.4
, whereas the one that uses the logarithm of the fidelity is consistent with the quantum geometry in the pure state limit, as we shall see in Sec.
2.3
.

We proceed to demonstrate that, similar to the information geometry
[
29
]
and the theory of Hetényi and Lévay for pure state quantum geometry
[
23
]
, the Christoffel symbol of the first kind can also be derived from the generating function. In differential geometry, the Christoffel symbol on the manifold of the
parameter
{\bf x}
is defined as

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\lambda\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{2}\left[\partial_{\nu}F_{\lambda\mu}+\partial_{\mu}F_{\lambda\nu}-\partial_{\lambda}F_{\mu\nu}\right].

(13)

We observe that

\displaystyle{\overline{B}}_{\lambda\mu;\nu}-{\overline{B}}_{\mu;\lambda\nu}=-i\left\{\frac{1}{|\tilde{B}|}\partial_{\lambda^{\prime}}\partial_{\mu^{\prime}}\partial_{\nu}{|\tilde{B}|}+\frac{1}{|\tilde{B}|}\partial_{\lambda}\partial_{\mu^{\prime}}\partial_{\nu}{|\tilde{B}|}\right\}_{\bf x=x^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=2i\partial_{\lambda}{\rm Tr}Y_{\mu\nu}=-\frac{i}{4}\partial_{\lambda}F_{\mu\nu}.

(14)

As a reult,

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\lambda\mu\nu}=2i\left\{{\overline{B}}_{\nu\lambda;\mu}-{\overline{B}}_{\lambda;\nu\mu}+{\overline{B}}_{\mu\lambda;\nu}-{\overline{B}}_{\lambda;\mu\nu}-{\overline{B}}_{\lambda\mu;\nu}+{\overline{B}}_{\mu;\lambda\nu}\right\},

(15)

indicating that the generating function also encodes the Christoffel symbol and many other differential geometric properties of the manifold, such as the geodesic.

2.2
Bloch representation of
2\times 2
density matrices

We now use the
2\times 2
density matrix as a concrete example to demonstrate the generating function. A convenient tool to proceed is the Bloch representation, which parametrizes the two density matrices by

\displaystyle\rho({\bf x})=\frac{1}{2}(\sigma_{0}+{\bf r}\cdot{\bm{\sigma}}),\;\;\;\rho({\bf x^{\prime}})=\frac{1}{2}(\sigma_{0}+{\bf r^{\prime}}\cdot{\bm{\sigma}}),

(16)

where
\sigma_{i}
are the Pauli matrices and
\sigma_{0}
is the identity matrix, and
{\bf r}
and
{\bf r^{\prime}}
denote the same function that are evaluated at two different parameters
{\bf x}
and
{\bf x^{\prime}}
. If
{\bf x=x^{\prime}}
, then
{\bf r=r^{\prime}}
. Note that the two eigenvalues
(1\pm|{\bf r}|)/2
of
\rho({\bf x})
have to be positive, since they are the probability of the system being in the corresponding eigenstates, so the modulus of the
{\bf r}
vector is constrained by
|{\bf r}|\leq 1
. The QFIM is
[
6
]

\displaystyle F_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}{\bf r}\cdot\partial_{\nu}{\bf r}+\frac{({\bf r\cdot\partial_{\mu}r})({\bf r\cdot\partial_{\nu}r})}{1-|{\bf r}|^{2}},

(17)

Consequently, the Christoffel symbol in Eq. (
13
) is

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\lambda\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{\lambda}{\bf r}\cdot\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}{\bf r}+\frac{{\bf r\cdot\partial_{\lambda}r}}{1-|{\bf r}|^{2}}\left[\partial_{\mu}{\bf r}\cdot\partial_{\nu}{\bf r}+{\bf r}\cdot\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}{\bf r}\right]

(18)

\displaystyle-\frac{({\bf r\cdot\partial_{\lambda}r})({\bf r\cdot\partial_{\mu}r})({\bf r\cdot\partial_{\nu}r})}{(1-|{\bf r}|^{2})^{2}}.

To calculate the fidelity, we use the following formula of the square root of a
2\times 2
matrix
[
30
]
. Given a
2\times 2
matrix

\displaystyle M=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A&B\\
C&D\end{array}\right),

and define
s=\pm\sqrt{AD-BC}
and
t=\pm\sqrt{A+D+2s}
, its square root is

\displaystyle\sqrt{M}=\frac{1}{t}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A+s&B\\
C&D+s\end{array}\right).

Use this formula once for
\sqrt{\rho({\bf x})}
and another time for
\sqrt{\sqrt{\rho({\bf x})}\rho({\bf x^{\prime}})\sqrt{\rho({\bf x})}}
, with all the possible signs in front of
s
and
t
, we arrive at

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}\sqrt{\sqrt{\rho({\bf x})}\rho({\bf x^{\prime}})\sqrt{\rho({\bf x})}}=\left[\frac{1}{2}(\pm 1\pm{\bf r\cdot r^{\prime}})\pm\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{1-|{\bf r}|^{2}}\sqrt{1-|{\bf r}^{\prime}|^{2}}\right]^{1/2}.

(25)

However, not all of these solutions are physical, since the density matrix requires that
\lim_{\bf x\rightarrow x^{\prime}}{\rm Tr}\sqrt{\sqrt{\rho({\bf x})}\rho({\bf x^{\prime}})\sqrt{\rho({\bf x})}}=1
. Thus we choose the solution with only positive signs, yielding the final result

\displaystyle\ln{\rm Tr}\sqrt{\sqrt{\rho({\bf x})}\rho({\bf x^{\prime}})\sqrt{\rho({\bf x})}}

\displaystyle=\ln\left[\frac{1}{2}(1+{\bf r\cdot r^{\prime}})+\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{1-|{\bf r}|^{2}}\sqrt{1-|{\bf r}^{\prime}|^{2}}\right]^{1/2}.

(26)

Inserting Eq. (
26
) into Eq. (
12
) yields the QFIM in Eq. (
17
), indicating that Eq. (
26
) is indeed the correct generating function.

2.3
Pure state limit

In the pure state limit
{\rm Tr}\rho^{2}=1
, the density matrix involves only one quantum state
|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|
, and hence its products take the form

\displaystyle\rho({\bf x})=\sqrt{\rho({\bf x})}=|\psi({\bf x})\rangle\langle\psi({\bf x})|,

\displaystyle\sqrt{\rho({\bf x})}\rho({\bf x^{\prime}})\sqrt{\rho({\bf x})}=|\psi({\bf x})\rangle\langle\psi({\bf x})|\psi({\bf x^{\prime}})\rangle\langle\psi({\bf x^{\prime}})|\psi({\bf x})\rangle\langle\psi({\bf x})|,

\displaystyle\sqrt{\sqrt{\rho({\bf x})}\rho({\bf x^{\prime}})\sqrt{\rho({\bf x})}}=|\psi({\bf x})\rangle\sqrt{\langle\psi({\bf x})|\psi({\bf x^{\prime}})\rangle\langle\psi({\bf x^{\prime}})|\psi({\bf x})\rangle}\langle\psi({\bf x})|.

(27)

Consequently, the logarithm of the fidelity between the density matrices in the pure state limit is simply the logarithm of the fidelity between the quantum states

\displaystyle\ln{\rm Tr}\sqrt{\sqrt{\rho({\bf x})}\rho({\bf x^{\prime}})\sqrt{\rho({\bf x})}}=\ln\sqrt{\langle\psi({\bf x})|\psi({\bf x^{\prime}})\rangle\langle\psi({\bf x^{\prime}})|\psi({\bf x})\rangle}

\displaystyle=\ln|\langle\psi({\bf x^{\prime}})|\psi({\bf x})\rangle|\equiv\ln|\tilde{C}({\bf x,x^{\prime}})|.

(28)

The quantum geometric tensor
T_{\mu\nu}
, quantum metric
g_{\mu\nu}
, and Berry curvature
\Omega_{\mu\nu}
of this quantum state are defined by
[
13
,
24
]

\displaystyle T_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\partial_{\mu}\psi|\partial_{\nu}\psi\rangle-\langle\partial_{\mu}\psi|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|\partial_{\nu}\psi\rangle=g_{\mu\nu}-\frac{i}{2}\Omega_{\mu\nu},

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\langle\partial_{\mu}\psi|\partial_{\nu}\psi\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\langle\partial_{\nu}\psi|\partial_{\mu}\psi\rangle-\langle\partial_{\mu}\psi|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|\partial_{\nu}\psi\rangle,

\displaystyle\Omega_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\langle\partial_{\mu}\psi|\partial_{\nu}\psi\rangle-i\langle\partial_{\nu}\psi|\partial_{\mu}\psi\rangle.

(29)

In particular, the logarithm of the product between two quantum states

\displaystyle\tilde{C}({\bf x,x^{\prime}})=\langle\psi({\bf x^{\prime}})|\psi({\bf x})\rangle=|\langle\psi({\bf x^{\prime}})|\psi({\bf x})\rangle|\,e^{i\varphi({\bf x^{\prime},x})},

(30)

has been shown to be a generating function that can obtain the quantum geometric tensor
T_{\mu\nu}
via derivatives
[
23
]

\displaystyle\tilde{C}_{\mu;\nu}=(-i\partial_{\mu^{\prime}})(i\partial_{\nu})\ln\tilde{C}|_{\bf x=x^{\prime}}=\partial_{\mu^{\prime}}\partial_{\nu}\left[\ln\langle\psi({\bf x^{\prime}})|\psi({\bf x})\rangle\right]_{\bf x=x^{\prime}}=T_{\mu\nu}.

(31)

From this result, it is then obvious that the logarithm of the modulus
|\tilde{C}({\bf x,x^{\prime}})|
serves as a generating function for the pure state quantum metric
g_{\mu\nu}
, or equivalently the pure state QFIM
F_{\mu\nu}
, since taking derivative on both points
{\bf x}
and
{\bf x^{\prime}}
gives

\displaystyle\overline{C}_{\mu;\nu}=(-i\partial_{\mu^{\prime}})(i\partial_{\nu})\ln|\tilde{C}|_{\bf x=x^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\mu^{\prime}}\partial_{\nu}\left[\ln\langle\psi({\bf x^{\prime}})|\psi({\bf x})\rangle+\ln\langle\psi({\bf x})|\psi({\bf x^{\prime}})\rangle\right]_{\bf x=x^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(T_{\mu\nu}+T_{\nu\mu}\right)=g_{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{4}F_{\mu\nu}.

(32)

Furthermore, the phase
\varphi({\bf x^{\prime},x})
of the product in Eq. (
30
) is the generating function of the Berry curvature

\displaystyle\Omega_{\mu\nu}=i(-i\partial_{\mu^{\prime}})(i\partial_{\nu})\left[\ln\langle\psi({\bf x^{\prime}})|\psi({\bf x})\rangle-\ln\langle\psi({\bf x})|\psi({\bf x^{\prime}})\rangle\right]_{\bf x=x^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=i\partial_{\mu^{\prime}}\partial_{\nu}\ln e^{2i\varphi({\bf x^{\prime},x})}|_{\bf x=x^{\prime}}=-2\,\partial_{\mu^{\prime}}\partial_{\nu}\varphi({\bf x^{\prime},x})|_{\bf x=x^{\prime}},

(33)

which interprets the Berry curvature as a second cumulant.

Alternatively, the fidelity itself
|\tilde{C}|=|\langle\psi({\bf x^{\prime}})|\psi({\bf x})\rangle|\equiv|\langle\psi^{\prime}|\psi\rangle|
can also serve as a generating function, since

\displaystyle(-i\partial_{\mu^{\prime}})(i\partial_{\nu})|\tilde{C}|_{\bf x=x^{\prime}}=\partial_{\mu^{\prime}}\partial_{\nu}\sqrt{\langle\psi^{\prime}|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|\psi^{\prime}\rangle}|_{\bf x=x^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\mu^{\prime}}\left[\frac{\langle\psi^{\prime}|\partial_{\nu}\psi\rangle\langle\psi|\psi^{\prime}\rangle+\langle\psi^{\prime}|\psi\rangle\langle\partial_{\nu}\psi|\psi^{\prime}\rangle}{\sqrt{\langle\psi^{\prime}|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|\psi^{\prime}\rangle}}\right]_{\bf x=x^{\prime}}=g_{\mu\nu}.

(34)

Thus the fidelity with or without a logarithm works equally well as a generating function for the quantum metric
g_{\mu\nu}
. In addition, one also has the following obvious generating function for the Berry curvature

\displaystyle\Omega_{\mu\nu}=i(-i\partial_{\mu^{\prime}})(i\partial_{\nu})\left[\langle\psi({\bf x^{\prime}})|\psi({\bf x})\rangle-\langle\psi({\bf x})|\psi({\bf x^{\prime}})\rangle\right]_{\bf x=x^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=(-i\partial_{\mu^{\prime}})(i\partial_{\nu})\left[-2{\rm Im}\langle\psi({\bf x^{\prime}})|\psi({\bf x})\rangle\right]_{\bf x=x^{\prime}}.

(35)

Combing Eqs. (
34
) and (
35
) yields an alternative generating function for the quantum geometric tensor

\displaystyle T_{\mu\nu}=(-i\partial_{\mu^{\prime}})(i\partial_{\nu})\left[|\langle\psi({\bf x^{\prime}})|\psi({\bf x})\rangle|+i{\rm Im}\langle\psi({\bf x^{\prime}})|\psi({\bf x})\rangle\right]_{\bf x=x^{\prime}},

(36)

that works equally well compared to that in Eq. (
31
) proposed by Hetényi and Lévay
[
23
]
. Finally, we observe that

\displaystyle{\overline{C}}_{\lambda\mu;\nu}-{\overline{C}}_{\mu;\lambda\nu}=-i\partial_{\lambda}g_{\mu\nu},

(37)

and hence the Christoffel symbol of the first kind is

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\lambda\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{2}\left[\partial_{\nu}g_{\lambda\mu}+\partial_{\mu}g_{\lambda\nu}-\partial_{\lambda}g_{\mu\nu}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{2}\left\{{\overline{C}}_{\nu\lambda;\mu}-{\overline{C}}_{\lambda;\nu\mu}+{\overline{C}}_{\mu\lambda;\nu}-{\overline{C}}_{\lambda;\mu\nu}-{\overline{C}}_{\lambda\mu;\nu}+{\overline{C}}_{\mu;\lambda\nu}\right\}

\displaystyle=\langle\partial_{\lambda}\psi|(I-|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|)|\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}\psi\rangle+\langle\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}\psi|(I-|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|)|\partial_{\lambda}\psi\rangle

\displaystyle+\langle\partial_{\mu}\psi|(|\partial_{\lambda}\psi\rangle\langle\psi|+|\psi\rangle\langle\partial_{\lambda}\psi|)|\partial_{\nu}\psi\rangle+\langle\partial_{\nu}\psi|(|\partial_{\lambda}\psi\rangle\langle\psi|+|\psi\rangle\langle\partial_{\lambda}\psi|)|\partial_{\mu}\psi\rangle,

indicating that the fidelity also encodes the Christoffel symbol. Note that the generating functions of Berry curvature in Eqs. (
33
) and (
35
) seem to imply that numerous physical phenomena related to the Berry phase in various branches of physics, such as the Aharonov-Bohm effect
[
31
]
and quantized Hall conductance
[
32
]
, may be somewhat embedded in the generating functions. Whether the generating functions actually contain all the information about the geometric phase remains unclear to us at present, and certainly deserves to be further explored. In the present work, we will focus only on the generating functions of quantum metric and Christoffel symbol, while leaving that of the Berry curvature to future investigations.

The Bloch representation in Sec.
2.2
can be used to find the generating function for quantum metric for a generic
2\times 2
Hamiltonian expanded by Pauli matrices

\displaystyle H({\bf x})={\bf d(x)}\cdot{\bm{\sigma}},

(39)

where
{\bf d(x)}=(d_{1},d_{2},d_{3})
describes the dependence of the Hamiltonian on the parameter
{\bf x}
, and we denote its modulus by
d=(d_{1}^{2}+d_{2}^{2}+d_{3}^{2})^{1/2}
. The eigenvalues are
\pm d
, so we can introduce a spectrally flattened Hamiltonian
Q({\bf x})={\bf n}({\bf x})\cdot{\bm{\sigma}}
by using
{\bf n}={\bf d}/d
such that its eigenvalues are
\pm|{\bf n}|^{2}=\pm 1
. The lower energy eigenstate is

\displaystyle|\psi\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2d(d-d_{3})}}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}d_{3}-d\\
d_{1}+id_{2}\end{array}\right)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2(1-n_{3})}}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}n_{3}-1\\
n_{1}+in_{2}\end{array}\right),

which is often very useful in fermionic systems, such as describing the Berry curvature and topological order in 2D materials
[
32
,
25
]
. The density matrix of this state is

\displaystyle\rho=|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|=\frac{1}{2}\left(1-{\bf n}\cdot{\bm{\sigma}}\right).

(45)

Comparing with Eq. (
16
), we see that
{\bf r=-n}
, with the extra condition that
{\bf r\cdot\partial_{\mu}r}={\bf n\cdot\partial_{\mu}n}=0
, so we can immediately use the formula in Sec.
2.2
to write down the generating functions and calculate the resulting quantum metric, Berry curvature, and Christoffel symbol. Denoting the
\left\{d_{i},n_{i}\right\}
and
\left\{d_{i}^{\prime},n_{i}^{\prime}\right\}
to be the functions at
{\bf x}
and
{\bf x^{\prime}}
, respectively, the results are

\displaystyle(\ln)|\langle\psi({\bf x^{\prime}})|\psi({\bf x})\rangle|=(\ln)\left[\frac{1}{2}(1+{\bf n^{\prime}\cdot n})\right]^{1/2},

\displaystyle-2\varphi({\bf x^{\prime},x})=-2\tan^{-1}\frac{n_{1}^{\prime}n_{2}-n_{2}^{\prime}n_{1}}{1-n_{3}-n_{3}^{\prime}+{\bf n^{\prime}\cdot n}},

\displaystyle-2{\rm Im}\langle\psi({\bf x^{\prime}})|\psi({\bf x})\rangle=\frac{n_{2^{\prime}}n_{1}-n_{1}^{\prime}n_{2}}{\sqrt{1-n_{3}^{\prime}}\sqrt{1-n_{3}}},

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{4}F_{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{4}\partial_{\mu}{\bf n}\cdot\partial_{\nu}{\bf n}=\frac{1}{4d^{2}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{2}\partial_{\mu}d_{i}\partial_{\nu}d_{i}-\partial_{\mu}d\partial_{\nu}d\right),

\displaystyle\Omega_{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{2}{\bf n\cdot\left(\partial_{\mu}n\times\partial_{\nu}n\right)}=\frac{1}{2d^{3}}{\bf d\cdot\left(\partial_{\mu}d\times\partial_{\nu}d\right)},

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\lambda\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{4}\partial_{\lambda}{\bf n}\cdot\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}{\bf n},

(46)

that is readily applicable to any generic two-state system. Note that the vielbein form of quantum metric and the cyclic derivative form of Berry curvature in Eq. (

,

, indicating the validity of the generating functions.

2.4
Real wave function limit

Finally, we address the pure state systems whose wave functions are real, such particles in a box
[
36
]
and quasihole state of a conventional superconductor
[
37
]
. All the formulas in this section are well-known for decades, and we merely mention them to elaborate the correspondence between our generating function with that in the information geometry. Consider a pure state system
\rho({\bf x})=|\psi({\bf x})\rangle\langle\psi({\bf x})|
whose wave functions are all real numbers given by the multiplication of its sign and magnitude
\psi_{i}=\psi_{i}({\bf x})={\rm Sgn}(\psi_{i})\times|\psi_{i}|
, so the ket state is

\displaystyle|\psi\rangle=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\psi_{1}\\
\psi_{2}\\
\vdots\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}{\rm Sgn}(\psi_{1})\times|\psi_{1}|\\
{\rm Sgn}(\psi_{2})\times|\psi_{2}|\\
\vdots\end{array}\right).

The modulus
|\psi_{i}|
is therefore the square root of a probability mass function
|\psi_{i}|^{2}
that is normalized correctly
\langle\psi|\psi\rangle=\sum_{i}|\psi_{i}|^{2}=1
, since
|\psi_{i}|^{2}
is the probability of finding the system
|\psi\rangle
to be in a specific basis
|i\rangle
when the external parameters are
{\bf x}
. The classical FIM
I_{\mu\nu}
is introduced from this probability mass function by

\displaystyle I_{\mu\nu}({\bf x})=\sum_{i}|\psi_{i}|^{2}\partial_{\mu}\ln|\psi_{i}|^{2}\partial_{\nu}\ln|\psi_{i}|^{2}=4\sum_{i}\partial_{\mu}|\psi_{i}|\partial_{\nu}|\psi_{i}|

\displaystyle=4\langle\partial_{\mu}\psi|\partial_{\nu}\psi\rangle=4g_{\mu\nu}({\bf x})=4T_{\mu\nu}({\bf x}),

(54)

which is exactly equal to four times the quantum metric
g_{\mu\nu}
or quantum geometric tensor
T_{\mu\nu}
, since Berry curvature vanishes
\Omega_{\mu\nu}=0
, meaning that the notion of quantum geometry of
|\psi\rangle
is synonymous to the information geometry of the probability mass function
|\psi_{i}|^{2}
in this real wave function limit.

Treating
|\psi_{i}|^{2}
as a probability mass function, the logarithm of the fidelity between two probability mass functions at different parameters
{\bf x}
and
{\bf x^{\prime}}
in classical information theory (the fidelity is often called divergence in information geometry) is given by
[
29
]

\displaystyle\ln\tilde{D}({\bf x,x^{\prime}})=\ln\sum_{i}|\psi_{i}({\bf x^{\prime}})||\psi_{i}({\bf x})|.

(55)

The derivatives are denoted by

\displaystyle D_{\mu;}=\partial_{\mu^{\prime}}\ln\tilde{D}\big|_{\bf x=x^{\prime}},\quad D_{\,;\nu}=\partial_{\nu}\ln\tilde{D}\big|_{\bf x=x^{\prime}},

(56)

without the factor of
\pm i
following the usual definition in information geometry. The FIM and its derivative are obtained from this generating function by

\displaystyle D_{\mu;\nu}=\left[\partial_{\mu^{\prime}}\partial_{\nu}\ln\sum_{i}|\psi_{i}({\bf x^{\prime}})||\psi_{i}({\bf x})|\right]_{\bf x=x^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{4}I_{\mu\nu}=g_{\mu\nu},

\displaystyle D_{\rho\mu;\nu}+D_{\mu;\rho\nu}=\frac{1}{4}\partial_{\rho}I_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\rho}g_{\mu\nu}.

(57)

In fact, the logarithm in the definition of generating function from Eq. (
55
) to (
57
) can be simply removed
\ln\tilde{D}({\bf x,x^{\prime}})\rightarrow\tilde{D}({\bf x,x^{\prime}})
without altering any of these results, as is usually used in the information geometry
[
29
]
. It follows that the Christoffel symbol of the first kind defined from the FIM can also be derived from the generating function

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\lambda\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{2}\left[\partial_{\nu}I_{\lambda\mu}+\partial_{\mu}I_{\lambda\nu}-\partial_{\lambda}I_{\mu\nu}\right]=4\sum_{i}\partial_{\lambda}|\psi_{i}|\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}|\psi_{i}|

\displaystyle=2\left\{D_{\nu\lambda;\mu}+D_{\lambda;\nu\mu}+D_{\mu\lambda;\nu}+D_{\lambda;\mu\nu}-D_{\lambda\mu;\nu}-D_{\mu;\lambda\nu}\right\}.

(58)

This section completes the hierarchy between the mixed state quantum geometry, the pure state quantum geometry, and the information geometry, in the sense that many metrological quantities descent from the most general mixed state quantum geometry to the pure state quantum geometry if the density matrix describes a pure state, and then further descent to the information geometry if the wave functions are real. Both the metric and the generating function obey this hierarchy, as well as the Christoffel symbol derived from the metric, as summarized in Fig.
1
.

3.1
Canonical ensemble of two-level systems

Canonical ensembles of generic two-level systems at finite temperature
T
are obvious applications of the Bloch representation formalism. Denoting
\beta=1/k_{B}T
,

\displaystyle e^{-\beta H}=e^{-\beta{\bf d}\cdot{\bm{\sigma}}}=\sigma_{0}\cosh\beta d-{\hat{\bf d}}\cdot{\bm{\sigma}}\sinh\beta d,

\displaystyle\rho({\bf x})=\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{0}-\frac{1}{2}{\hat{\bf d}}\cdot{\bm{\sigma}}\tanh\beta d,

\displaystyle{\bf r}=-{\hat{\bf d}}\tanh\beta d=-\frac{\bf d}{d}\tanh\beta d.

The QFIM can then be obtained from Eq. (
17

26

4
Conclusions

In summary, we elaborate that the fidelity between two density matrices serves as a generating function for the QFIM and the Christoffel symbol, thereby completing the missing elements in the hierarchy of mixed state quantum geometry, pure state quantum geometry, and information geometry. At the level of pure state quantum geometry, we derive the fidelity and phase of the product between two quantum states as the generating functions for quantum metric and Berry curvature. The explicit expressions for these generating functions are derived for
2\times 2
density matrices, which may be broadly applied to any two-level systems to investigate the evolution of quantum geometry with temperature, as demonstrated for the canonical ensembles of a single spin and the SSH model. Applications of this generating function formalism to more complicated density matrices, as well as how they manifest in other aspects of quantum metrology, await to be further explored.

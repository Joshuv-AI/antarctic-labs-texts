Influence of maternal health status during pregnancy and the child´s medical history on molar-incisor hypomineralization in a group of Spanish children (aged 6–14 years): a retrospective case-control study
Authors: Johanna Muñoz, Alfonso Alvarado-Lorenzo, Laura Criado-Pérez, Laura Antonio-Zancajo, Daniel Curto, Cristina Gómez-Polo, Adrián Curto
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundMolar–incisor hypomineralization (MIH) is a qualitative enamel defect that is highly prevalent in children. It has been reported that patients with MIH have higher caries occurrence with an increased need and frequency of dental treatment compared to patients without MIH. The objective of this study was to analyze the association between MIH and a series of factors related to maternal health status during pregnancy and children´s medical history in early childhood.MethodsA retrospective study of cases (patients with MIH) and controls (patients without MIH) was designed between 2023 and 2024. A total of 280 children (cases = 140; controls = 140) aged 6 to 14 years (138 boys and 142 girls) were examined according to the European Academy of Pediatric Dentistry (EAPD) criteria for MIH. A survey was carried out with mothers regarding the potential exposure of their children to etiological factors of MIH. Possible prenatal and postnatal etiological factors were obtained through a personal interview with the patients’ mothers. The statistical analysis was carried out with the contrast test and the chi-square test.ResultsDuring pregnancy, folic acid consumption, alcohol intake, systemic viral and/or bacterial infections, and gestational diabetes were statistically significantly related to MIH, as were breastfeeding, asthma, and corticosteroid consumption during childhood.ConclusionsAlthough there are different factors that may have statistically significant relationships with MIH, they cannot be predicted. Therefore, longitudinal studies, with a large sample size, are needed to determine the influence of prenatal and postnatal factors on the prevalence and severity of MIH in children.Keywords: Children, Etiological factors, Molar-incisor hypomineralization

Background

Molar–incisor hypomineralization (MIH) is a dental pathology that affects the enamel and has an estimated average global prevalence of around 13%, which ranges between 7% and 35% in different published studies [1–4]. This disparity in prevalence determined in diverse studies by different authors may be motivated by a lack of standardization among examiners, the conditions of oral exploration, or the social characteristics of the analyzed population [4]. Studies that have analysed the prevalence of MIH in Spain are limited. These studies report a prevalence rate ranging from 7 to 21%. This range of difference in the prevalence of MIH may be due to the different methodology used in the published studies [3, 5]. Most authors have described a higher prevalence of MIH in upper teeth compared to lower teeth [3–6]. In MIH, asymmetrical; well-defined; white, cream, yellow, or brown lesions appear in the enamel. The severity and extent of these lesions vary [7, 8]. In terms of the physicochemical characteristics of MIH lesions, there is a decrease in the concentration of minerals (specifically calcium and phosphate), a reduction in the modulus of elasticity and hardness of the enamel, and an increase in the concentration of proteins [9, 10].

MIH strongly impacts patients at a functional and esthetic level. Patients with this dental condition often describe pain, hypersensitivity, and problems when chewing. Esthetically, MIH has a great impact when it occurs in the anterior teeth [11, 12]. In severe cases, MIH can lead to enamel fracture and tooth loss. Studies show an increase in the prevalence of caries in patients with MIH. As the severity of MIH increases, the following caries indices increase, the DMFT index (decayed and missing tooth index for permanent teeth) and the DMFS index (decayed and missing surface index for permanent teeth) [13–16]. Early preventive intervention in patients affected by MIH is essential to reduce the implications of enamel lesions, the incidence of caries, and the repercussions on dentition [15, 17]. MIH in children usually produces dental hypersensitivity and pain. These are frequent reasons for dental consultation, especially of an urgent nature [7, 18, 19]. This dental condition has a high prevalence in the child population. As reported by different authors, the prevalence rates of hypersensitivity in the teeth of patients affected by MIH are 30–50%. Patients describe a higher degree of hypersensitivity in teeth with more severe MIH lesions [7]. This hypersensitivity influences the administration of local anesthesia for dental treatments because its effect is more limited compared to tooth anesthesia without MIH, with repercussions for both the professional and the patient [18, 20, 21]. Faced with these problems in obtaining adequate anesthesia, patients usually show higher levels of anxiety and, therefore, greater concern about their dental treatment [19, 21].

Due to this increase in dental hypersensitivity, chewing and oral hygiene are usually quite affected in patients with MIH. This condition also has an impact on oral-health-related quality of life (OHRQoL) [22]. There are studies that describe an association between the presence of MIH and a worse OHRQoL. This impact is due to the problems presented by patients with MIH and its impact on esthetics [22, 23]. However, there is a discrepancy between the different studies when it comes to considering the relationship between the severity of MIH lesions and OHRQoL [11, 23–25].

MIH studies describe a multifactorial etiology, in which there are genetic and environmental factors. Perinatal and postnatal etiological factors are those that may be the most involved in its etiology [1, 7, 10].

The difference in the prevalence of MIH by geographical area also suggests some influence of genetics on its etiology [17, 26–28]. Amongst the genetic factors, multiple polymorphisms in genes have been described related to amelogenesis, while different gene interactions are associated with an increased susceptibility in children to develop MIH [29–31]. Different systemic factors can influence the formation of MIH lesions during the different stages of tooth development. It has been suggested, for example, that the type of delivery, low birth weight, the presence of fever during early childhood, and the consumption of antibiotics are factors that may be related to the appearance of MIH 16, 27–33, 27]. However, published systematic reviews suggest that none of the previously analyzed risk factors is a direct cause of the appearance of MIH. In addition, each study has evaluated the different factors that may be involved in MIH in isolation [10, 38–44].

No study has been published that analyzes the relationship between maternal health during pregnancy and the child’s history in early childhood in Spain. The studies carried out on MIH in our country are very limited, and none of them has focused on analyzing the relationship between maternal and child factors [3, 5, 45–47].

The objective of this study was to investigate the possible association between MIH and a series of factors related to maternal health during pregnancy and the childhood medical history of children aged between 5 and 14 years old in a Spanish population.

Methods

Study design and sample

This study was carried out at the Dental Clinic of the Faculty of Medicine of the University of Salamanca (Salamanca, Spain). This study was performed from November 2023 to July 2024. A retrospective case–control study was designed, with a total sample of 280 participants, where two study groups were analyzed: pediatric patients with (MIH group) (n = 140) and without (control group) (n = 140) MIH. This study was conducted with the approval of the Research Ethics Committee of the University of Salamanca (registration number: 1077; date: 27/11/2023), following the principles established in the Declaration of Helsinki. The Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) guidelines were followed in this case–control study. Written informed consent was obtained from the parents or legal guardians of all the participants before enrollment in the study, and they signed a consent form prior to entry.

Sample size

According to the scientific literature, in the present study, this study considered an average prevalence of MIH of 13% [1, 3–6, 10, 15, 16, 23, 27, 32, 34, 36, 39]. Considering this last value as a reference, with a confidence of 95%, a minimum power of 80%, and a margin of error of ± 4.5, this study estimated that the minimum sample size needed for each group was 139 participants (case group = 139; control group = 139). A sample size of 140 participants was considered for each study group in order to provide statistical validity to the study.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

The inclusion criteria were as follows: patients between 6 and 14 years of age; patients with the eruption of at least one permanent first molar; and patients with at least four erupted permanent incisors. The exclusion criteria were as follows: patients with significant physical and/or mental difficulties that hinder oral examination; patients with enamel development disorders other than MIH; and patients under orthodontic treatment.

Procedures

The participants who were included in this study were pediatric patients who came to the Dental Clinic of the University of Salamanca requesting dental assistance and who met the defined inclusion criteria.

Two of the researchers (J.M. and A.C.) initially selected 320 participants for possible inclusion in the study. Finally, a total of 280 patients were analyzed. Oral explorations and data collection were carried out at the Dental Clinic of the University of Salamanca. The case group (MIH group) comprised participants who were diagnosed with MIH according to the criteria established by the European Academy of Pediatric Dentistry (EAPD) [48].

This study analyzed the possible involvement of different factors in the appearance of MIH in children. A group of variables were studied related to the mother’s health during pregnancy and another group of variables related to the patient’s medical history during their childhood. To analyze the influence of these variables, a questionnaire was designed and provided to the mothers of the participants. This questionnaire was designed according to the possible etiological factors of MIH described in the scientific literature [39, 40, 48–50].

At the oral examination appointment, this questionnaire was provided to the patients’ mothers to complete. For the variables related to maternal health during pregnancy, this study analyzed the possible influence on MIH of the consumption of certain drugs (folic acid, vitamin B12, vitamin D, iron, and levothyroxine), tobacco use, alcohol consumption, the presence of systemic infections of viral and/or bacterial etiology, and the presence of arterial hypertension and/or gestational diabetes. In relation to the consumption of certain drugs, this study considered that their consumption was for at least one month in pregnancy, while those consumed for less than one month were not considered. Smoking was considered as a study factor when the consumption was at least two cigarettes a day. Alcohol intake was considered valid when the mother consumed at least three units of alcohol per week. Infections of viral and/or bacterial (during the last trimester of pregnancy) etiology were considered when they were systemic in nature and required treatment with oral and/or parenteral antiviral and/or antibacterial drugs. High blood pressure and gestational diabetes were considered as study factors when the child’s mother was diagnosed with these two pathologies [51, 52]. The group of variables related to the patient’s medical history included whether they were breastfed, as well as the presence of childhood asthma and the consumption of corticosteroids and/or antibiotics. Asthma was considered in those patients who had been diagnosed with this pathology at least two years prior to their inclusion in this study. The consumption of corticosteroids and/or antibiotics was considered valid if they were consumed for at least one month and administered at least two years before the inclusion of the patient in the study.

Statistical analysis

SPSS version 28 was used for statistical evaluations. For the descriptive statistics, the frequency (n) and the percentage (%) were used in the evaluation of the categorical variables and the mean with the standard deviation (SD) for the quantitative variables. The contrast test and the chi-square test were used to compare the study groups with the categorical variables. P-values of < 0.05 were considered statistically significant.

The mean age was 9.43 ± 2.66 years. When analyzing the gender of the participants, the sample was balanced: 142 girls (50.7%) versus 138 boys (49.3%). The study groups were homogeneous with respect to the participants´ age and sex.

To verify the main objective of this study, determining which of the variables described above may be associated with MIH, a univariate analysis was performed that evaluated the relationship of each factor independently from the rest.

In the analysis of the variables related to maternal health during pregnancy, this study observed that four of the factors studied were statistically significant (p < 0.05) when comparing both study groups (Table 4): folic acid consumption, alcohol intake, history of systemic viral and/or bacterial infections, and the presence of gestational diabetes. High statistical significance (p < 0.01) was observed with a moderate effect size (R2 = 0.072, 0.067, and 0.044, respectively) in the consumption of folic acid during pregnancy, the intake of alcohol, and viral and/or bacterial infections. Statistical significance was also observed (p < 0.05) in gestational diabetes but with a mild effect size (R2 = 0.020). Therefore, in the studied population, the present work can conclude that these factors related to maternal health during pregnancy can influence the presence of MIH.

Discussion

This study aimed to investigate the possible association of different factors with MIH in children between 6 and 14 years old. This report reveals that there are different factors related to maternal health during pregnancy and the patient’s own history that may be related to the appearance of MIH. As in previous studies, this study does not show a single specific etiological factor associated with MIH. This dental condition may involve various etiological factors and also present an important genetic component [7, 27, 48].

In relation to the mother´s pregnancy, this study observed that the consumption of folic acid, the intake of alcohol, a history of viral and/or bacterial infections, and gestational diabetes could be factors in the etiology of MIH in the studied population. When analyzing these possible factors, statistically significant differences were observed between the case and control groups. These results differ from those described by other authors who indicate that there is no evidence of an association between MIH and any specific disease during the last trimester of pregnancy or maternal alcohol intake [27, 43, 48].

In relation to the child’s medical history, the results described in this study are consistent, in part, with those reported by other studies. In this study, the present study observed that breastfeeding could be considered a possible factor related to MIH, this result is in line with what has been reported by other authors [53]. Previous studies have suggested that there is no relationship between breastfeeding and the appearance of this pathology, such as the studies of Garot et al., and Fatturi et al. [27, 43]. Other authors also describe childhood asthma as a possible factor related to MIH [27, 54].

Previous studies reported that there is a relationship between MIH and the presence of respiratory pathology during the mother´s pregnancy [32, 43]; premature birth [27, 32, 43, 54, 55]; the consumption of antibiotics during childhood [27, 43, 56, 57]; and a history of chickenpox [57], rubella [54], parotides [54], or measles during childhood [55]. The use of antibiotics is a common treatment for different pathologies during childhood. Therefore, when analyzing the influence of antibiotics during childhood as a possible factor related to MIH, it is likely that the presence of a disease is associated with the appearance of MIH, rather than the antibiotics consumed to treat it [43, 57].

This study included patients from 5 years of age with at least one erupted first molar and four erupted permanent incisors. The age range analyzed in this study (between the ages of 5 and 14 years old) was considered optimal according to previous studies. Other authors have also evaluated age ranges from 5 to 7 years and 11 to 14 years [32, 34–37, 58–60]. Previously published studies, like the present study, also took into account the European Academy of Pediatric Dentistry criteria when diagnosing patients with MIH [32, 34–37, 58–60]. The sample size analyzed in this study is in line with the number of participants evaluated in previous studies [35, 57, 59–65]. However, it should be noted that there are published studies with smaller sample sizes than the one used in this study [56, 57, 65, 66].

Strengths of this study

After a review of the scientific literature, no published studies were identified that evaluated the possible relationship between MIH and the different factors related to maternal health during pregnancy and the child’s history in Spain.

The strengths of this study were the analysis of a population with an appropriate age range and the use of a standard and approved protocol for the diagnosis and registration of patients with MIH [67]. The sample size was adequate to provide statistical validity to this study, and the selection of participants was random. The participants were recruited from patients requesting dental assistance at the Dental Clinic of the University of Salamanca. These factors facilitate a reproducible and accurate assessment of the dental condition that allows comparison between different studies. Taking into account the different repercussions of MIH in children, and considering its multifactorial etiology, it is important identifying the possible risk factors in order to identify initial MIH lesions early on and implement preventive treatment, thus reducing the functional and esthetic impacts of this pathology in children.

Limitations

The authors acknowledge that this study has certain limitations. One of the main limitations of this study was the use of a self-designed questionnaire to evaluate the possible aetiological factors of MIH. In relation to the analyzed factors, since this is a retrospective analysis based on a questionnaire completed by the pediatric patients’ mothers, it must be taken into account that there may have been a recall bias when completing the questionnaire. The retrospective information collected could have been biased by diagnostic knowledge. However, it should be noted that the mothers of the participants of this study were not informed about the possible etiological factors related to MIH before answering the questionnaire. Differences in age between the participants in the two study groups may also have influenced the results described in this work. Another limitation of this study is that it was carried out in a single center analyzing the population of a certain region. In the future, it would help to plan multicenter studies that study populations from different regions to analyze the possible influence of the patient’s geographical region on the environmental etiology of MIH. Due to the design and methodology of this study, this work can only establish associations and not causes and effects.

Conclusions

Considering the limitations of this study, this study can conclude that there is a statistically significant relationship (p < 0.05) between MIH and folic acid consumption, alcohol intake, viral and/or bacterial infections, and gestational diabetes during pregnancy in the analyzed population, along with breastfeeding, the presence of asthma, and corticosteroid consumption during childhood. Future studies are needed to evaluate a higher number of possible factors related to MIH and in different regions and/or centers.

Combined Impact of Cannabinoids and Cocaine on Outcomes of Trauma Patients
Authors: Nasim Ahmed, Yen-Hong Kuo
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundIn recent years, the use of polypharmacy has surged. There is a gap in knowledge regarding the impact of pre-injury use of polypharmacy on outcomes. The purpose of the study was to evaluate the effects of cannabinoids (CANB) and cocaine (COC) on trauma patients’ outcomes. The hypothesis is, no difference in-hospital mortality in patients who tested positive for CANB+COC versus those who only tested positive for CANB.MethodsThe Trauma Quality Improvement Program (TQIP) for 2019-2023 dataset was used. Patients’ demography, clinical characteristics, injury type and severity, and outcomes were compared between patients who tested positive for CANB+COC and those who tested positive only for CANB. Propensity score matching methodology was used to obtain the results. A p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.ResultsOut of 120,951 patients who qualified for the study, 12,116 pairs of patients were obtained from the propensity score matching process. Most of the baseline characteristics of the two groups, CANB+COC versus CANB, had no significant difference in age (years) (median [IQR]: 37 [28 – 51] vs. 32 [23 - 51]) or sex (male: 81% vs. 76.6%), ISS score (median [IQR]: 9 [5 - 17] vs. 10 [5 - 17]. Pair-matched analysis showed no significant difference in in-hospital mortality (2.2% vs. 2.2%, P = 0.86) but it showed a longer hospital length of stay (5 [5, 5] vs. 4 [4, 4], P < 0.0001), a higher event of unplanned intubation (1.5% versus 1%, P = 0.002) and a higher occurrence of ventilator associated pneumonia (1% versus 0.7%, P = 0.01) in patients who tested positive for CANB+COC versus CANB.ConclusionNo significant association was observed between the in-hospital mortality and combined use of, CANB+COC when compared to only CANB use. However, combined drug usage was associated with a longer length of hospital stay.

Introduction

A surge in cannabinoids (CANB) use among the public due to recent regulation changes resulted in a higher CANB-related visitation to the emergency room [1] and a higher positivity of CANB among trauma patients [2]. Patients who tested positive for the CANB were also found to be associated with positive tests for other drugs, including cocaine [2]. In-hospital mortality in trauma patients who tested positive for CANB reported variable results [3,4]. Our group previously reported no significant difference in mortality associated with CANB after trauma [5]. Similarly, trauma patients who tested positive for cocaine after injury found to have contradictory results in overall mortality; some reported no difference in mortality, others reported lower occurrence of mortality [[6], [7], [8]].

The combined impact of CANB and Cocaine (CANB+COC) has not been extensively studied. One recent single-center study evaluated the outcomes of mortality comparing among four groups: CANB, COC, CANB+COC, and negative tests found no significant difference in mortality among patients who tested positive for both CANB+COC when adjusted for confounders [9]. Therefore, this study was designed to find the association of the combined effect of CANB+COC on in-hospital mortality following trauma, utilizing national trauma data. Our hypothesis was no significant difference in in-hospital mortality in patients who tested positive for CANB+COC versus those who tested positive for CANB only.

Methods

Trauma Quality Improvement Program (TQIP), of participant use file (PUF) database for 2019–2023 was accessed for the study. Trauma registrar in a trauma center abstracts patient’s information into the trauma registry and deposit to American College of Surgeon (ACS), National Trauma Data Bank/PUF TQIP data base. Currently more than 900 trauma centers across USA participate in data sharing program with the ACS. It is the largest trauma patient data repository in the world. ACS compares the data of individual trauma center and compare it with the other trauma centers across the US and provides the results to the trauma center. it is considered a benchmark for the quality care. All adult trauma patients who were tested for the CANB and COC and admitted to the hospital after sustaining traumatic injury were included in the study. Patients’ demography, clinical information, type and mechanism of injury, injury severity determined by the injury severity score (ISS), abbreviated injury scale (AIS) of different body regions, Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS) score, comorbidities, and outcomes were compared between the groups who tested positive for CANB+COC versus only tested for CANB. Data first presented as median and interquartile range (IQR) [1st quartile and 3rd quartile]. Pre-matched analysis was performed using Wilcoxon Rank test for the continuous variable and Chi-square test for categorical variables. When the expected value was less than 5 in a cell of the contingency table of categorical variables, the Fisher exact test was used.

Propensity score matching methodology was used to balance the two groups. The confounders and covariates used in the propensity score calculation were Age, race (white), sex, ISS, and GCS. One-to-one matching was performed using the “nearest neighbor” approach with a caliper of 0.25 standard deviation. Graphical and numerical assessments were performed to determine the improvement in standardized mean differences. Pair-matched analysis was performed using Wilcoxon Signed Rank test for continuous variables and McNemar’s test was used for categorical variables. Risk difference and odds ratio with their respective 95% confidence intervals were calculated for mortality and complications. Kaplan-Meier procedure was used to generate the curve of hospital stay, and the log-rank test was performed to compare the hospital length of stay. A p value of <0.05 was considered statistically significant. Statistical analysis was performed using the R language [10]. Patients who were <18 years of age and tested positive for any other illicit drugs or alcohol other than CANB and Cocaine were excluded from the study.

The primary outcome was in-hospital mortality, and secondary outcomes were hospital length of stay, in-hospital complications, and discharge disposition.

Results

Patients’ characteristics & pre-matched analysis

A total of 120,951 patients qualified for the study, 12,116 (10.02%) patients tested positive for CANB+COC, whereas the rest of the patients tested positive for only CANB. There were significant differences found between the two groups regarding age in years (median [IQR]: 37 [28 – 51] vs. 32 [23 - 51]), sex (male: 81% vs. 76.6%), race (white: 45.2% vs. 58.2%), ISS score (median [IQR]: 9 [5 - 17] vs. 10 [5 - 17]) and blunt trauma mechanism (77.5% vs. 80.4%), all p values were <0.0001. Patients who tested positive for CANB+COC were found to have a higher incidence of substance abuse disorder (37.9% vs. 17.7%, P < 0.001). Discussion

Our study showed no significant difference in in-hospital mortality in patients who tested positive for CANB+COC when compared with patients who were tested only positive for CANB in post-matched analysis. Patients who tested positive for CANB+COC were found to have longer hospital stay (5 [5, 5] vs. 4 [4, 4], P < 0.0001, had increased events of ventilator-associated pneumonia and unplanned intubations when compared with the patients who tested for CANB only.

The impact of CANB+COC of a trauma victim has been reported sporadically. A recent study reported from a level II trauma center in Puerto Rico, evaluating 3709 patients who tested either COC or CANB showed a steady rise in the use of these drugs. The study found a difference in in-hospital mortality in univariate analysis, but that difference was not significant in adjusted analysis among the groups COC, COC+CANB, CANB, or negative test [9]. However, the study showed significantly higher odds of longer hospital stay in COC group in the adjusted analysis, which was consistent with our analysis of no mortality difference but longer hospital length of stay in CANB+COC versus CANB group. There is a possibility that the longer hospital stay in our study may be related to the COC and not because of CANB or the combined effect of CANB+COC. Our previous study on CANB versus no CANB showed no significant difference in mortality or hospital length of stay [5]. COC is one of the stimulant illegal drugs. One of the studies related to traumatic brain injury found stimulant drugs, including COC, did not impact hospital mortality or complications, but found to have longer hospital length of stay [11]. Concordant with this finding, other studies on stimulant drugs showed similar longer hospital stay with pre-injury use of stimulants in trauma patients [12,13]. The exact reasons for the longer hospital stay can not be delineated; however, high pain tolerance and withdrawal of the drug may have impacted the results [14].

Our study showed significantly longer hospital stay, a higher incidence of unplanned intubation rate (odds ratio [95% CI]: 1.454 [1.148, 1.862]) and an increased incidence in pneumonia (odds ratios [95% CI]: 1.462 [1.096, 1.985] in COC+CANB group . The reason for the higher rate of unplanned intubation could be due to high tolerance of pain or the development of withdrawal symptoms. Concordant with our results previous studies showed higher frequency of intubation in COC user [15,16]. Our results showed patients who tested positive for COC+CANB stayed one day longer in the hospital which was statistically significant but had a clinical significance as well in terms of resources utilization. On the other hand, the same patients’ group (COC+CANB) found to have higher occurrence of unplanned intubation and ventilator associated pneumonia complications which was statistically significant due to larger sample size, however, the absolute differences between the groups were 0.5% and 0.3% respectively which may not have any clinical significance [17].

One of the interesting observations in our study was patient who tested positive for CANB+COC was found to have more than twice the history of substance abuse disorder, alcohol use disorder and history of smoking when compared to those who tested for CANB. The question always arises whether the CANB use work like a gate way drug. There are conflicting reports, however, limited data showed that patient with CANB use have some lifetime probability of getting addicted to other illicit drugs and cigarette smoking [18]. Therefore, it is important for a point-of-care physician who manages patients with a history of substance abuse disorder to be aware of the higher tendency of polypharmacy use. Our study showed patients with CANB+COC sustained with a higher frequency of penetrating injuries compared to CANB only group. The exact reason of this occurrence cannot be accurately delineated, however, study showed concurrent use of CANB+COC can present with more antisocial behavior [19].

There are a number of limitations in this study. First, this is a retrospective study that carries an inherent risk of selection bias. To address the bias, the propensity score method was used. Propensity score matching, however, cannot completely eliminate the bias due to unobserved confounders. Second, drug tests were not offered to every patient; therefore true incidence of illegal drugs use cannot be obtained precisely. Third, when a drug test was performed exact amount in the blood level was not given in the dataset; therefore association of the amount of drug with the outcomes cannot be accurately determined. Furthermore, due to unavailability of information of the timing, dosage and intoxication of drug, causal interpretation cannot be established. Finally, the exclusion of patients positive for alcohol or other illicit substances creates a highly selected cohort that may not reflect real-world trauma populations, where polysubstance use is common. This restriction limits the generalizability of findings to routine trauma practice. However, important information this study delineated is that using polypharmacy prior to injury can impact patient's course of management and outcomes.

Conclusion

Pre-injury use of CANB+COC was not associated with in-hospital mortality when compared to CANB only group. Patients who tested positive for CANB+COC were found to be associated with longer hospital length of stay when compared to group positive for CANB only.

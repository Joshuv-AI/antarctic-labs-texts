Unfolding of wild character varieties
Kazuki Hiroe, Daisuke Yamakawa

Abstract
. In this paper, we study wild character varieties on compact Riemann surfaces and construct Poisson maps from wild to tame character varieties by unfolding irregular singularities into regular ones. Furthermore, we show that these unfolding Poisson maps induce Poisson birational equivalences between wild and tame character varieties. This result provides an affirmative answer to a conjecture posed by Klimeš, Paul, and Ramis.

1.
Introduction

This article investigates a geometric aspect of the confluence of singularities
in meromorphic connections,
formulated through wild character varieties.
The main motivation is to construct Poisson and birational correspondences
between wild character varieties and the character varieties arising
from the unfolding of irregular singularities.

We now outline the main results of this article.
Let
G
be a complex reductive group with a fixed maximal torus
T
.
Define
\mathbf{\Sigma}=(\Sigma,\mathbf{a};{}^{1}Q,\ldots,{}^{m}Q)

as an untwisted irregular curve in the sense of Boalch
[
5
]
,
consisting of a compact Riemann surface
\Sigma
of genus
g
,
a finite set of marked points
\mathbf{a}=\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}\}\subset\Sigma
,
and a collection of irregular types
{}^{i}Q
.
Fixing a local coordinate
z_{i}
centered at each marked point
a_{i}
,
we write
{}^{i}Q=\sum_{j=1}^{r_{j}}{}^{i}Q_{j}z^{-j}_{i}\in z_{i}^{-1}\mathfrak{t}[z_{i}^{-1}]
and let

\mathbf{H}=\prod_{i=1}^{m}Z_{G}(^{i}Q)

denote the product of stabilizers of all coefficients

{}^{i}Q_{j}\in\mathfrak{t}

appearing in each
{}^{i}Q
.
The wild character variety
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{B}}(\mathbf{\Sigma})

associated with
\mathbf{\Sigma}
is then defined as a Poisson variety.
Furthermore,
given a conjugacy class
\mathcal{C}=\prod_{i=1}^{m}\mathcal{C}_{i}\subset\mathbf{H}
,
we define the symplectic wild character variety

\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{B}}^{{\mathrm{st}}}(\mathbf{\Sigma},\mathcal{C})

as an open subset of
the closed Poisson subvariety
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{B}}(\mathbf{\Sigma},\overline{\mathcal{C}})

of
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{B}}(\mathbf{\Sigma})
,
corresponding to the closure
\overline{\mathcal{C}}
of
\mathcal{C}
.
For precise definitions, see Section
4
.

Let us consider the unfolding of the wild character varieties.
Let
\mathbf{\Sigma}^{\prime}=(\Sigma,\mathbf{b};\mathbf{0})
be
the irregular curve with

\sum_{i=1}^{m}(r_{j}+1)=|\mathbf{b}|
marked points, where all irregular types are
trivial.
In other words,
\mathbf{\Sigma}^{\prime}
is simply a Riemann surface with marked points.
The associated wild character variety

\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{B}}(\mathbf{\Sigma}^{\prime})

is then a standard (tame) character variety that parametrizes
the isomorphism classes of semisimple
G
-representations of
the fundamental group
\pi_{1}(\Sigma\backslash\mathbf{b})
.
Next, we unfold the conjugacy class
\mathcal{C}
as follows.
For each
i=1,\ldots,m
and
j=1,\ldots,r_{i}
, choose
{}^{i}t_{j}\in T
such that

Z_{G}(^{i}t_{j})=Z_{G}({}^{i}Q_{j},{}^{i}Q_{j+1},\dots,{}^{i}Q_{r_{i}}),\quad Z_{G}(h({}^{i}t_{1}{}^{i}t_{2}\cdots{}^{i}t_{r_{i}})^{-1})\subset Z_{G}(^{i}Q)\ (h\in\mathcal{C}_{i}).

See Appendix
A
for the existence of such elements.
Let
{}^{i}\mathfrak{C}_{j}
be the
G
-conjugacy class
containing
{}^{i}t_{j}
, and
let
{}^{i}\mathfrak{C}_{0}
be the
G
-conjugacy class
containing
\mathcal{C}_{i}({}^{i}t_{1}{}^{i}t_{2}\cdots{}^{i}t_{r_{i}})^{-1}
.
We then obtain the character varieties

\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{B}}^{{\mathrm{st}}}(\mathbf{\Sigma}^{\prime},\mathfrak{C})

and
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{B}}(\mathbf{\Sigma}^{\prime},\overline{\mathfrak{C}})

associated with the conjugacy class
\mathfrak{C}=\prod_{i,j}{}^{i}\mathfrak{C}_{j}
.

The main results of this article are as follows.
As a consequence of Theorem
8.1
,
we relate the wild character variety
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{B}}(\mathbf{\Sigma},\overline{\mathcal{C}})

and the unfolded character variety
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{B}}(\mathbf{\Sigma}^{\prime},\overline{\mathfrak{C}})

in a Poisson sense, namely, we obtain a Poisson map

\Upsilon_{({}^{i}t_{j})}\colon\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{B}}(\mathbf{\Sigma},\overline{\mathcal{C}})\rightarrow\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{B}}(\mathbf{\Sigma}^{\prime},\overline{\mathfrak{C}}).

Furthermore, Corollary
8.2
shows that
the following Poisson birational equivalence holds:

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{B}}(\mathbf{\Sigma},\overline{\mathcal{C}})
,
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{B}}(\mathbf{\Sigma}^{\prime},\overline{\mathfrak{C}})

are both irreducible and

\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{B}}^{\mathrm{st}}(\mathbf{\Sigma},\mathcal{C})
,
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{B}}^{\mathrm{st}}(\mathbf{\Sigma}^{\prime},\mathfrak{C})
are both nonempty.
Then
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{B}}(\mathbf{\Sigma},\overline{\mathcal{C}})
and
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{B}}(\mathbf{\Sigma}^{\prime},\overline{\mathfrak{C}})

are Poisson birationally equivalent.
Namely, there exist nonempty open subsets of them
which are isomorphic as Poisson varieties.

We now provide some background and related work.
In
[
11
]
, a deformation of moduli spaces of
meromorphic connections on
the trivial
G
-bundle over the Riemann sphere
\mathbb{P}^{1}

was constructed via the unfolding of irregular singularities.
It was subsequently shown that any moduli space of meromorphic
G
-connections
with unramified (untwisted) irregular singularities admits
a deformation to the moduli space of Fuchsian
G
-connections.
Our main theorem gives a generalization of this result
in the context of wild character varieties and further
establishes Poisson birational equivalences among the moduli spaces
arising from such deformations.
In related work, Klimeš
[
12
]
showed the existence of
a birational transformation between
character varieties associated with the Painlevé V and Painlevé VI equations
in the context of nonlinear Stokes phenomena for Painlevé equations.
Building on this, Paul and Ramis
[
14
]

showed that the birational transformation by Klimeš is
in fact symplectic,
and they posed an open problem suggesting that
character varieties for other types of Painlevé equations
should admit similar symplectic and birational maps arising from the
unfolding of their irregular singularities.
Our main theorem provides an answer to this problem
in the case of untwisted wild character varieties.

Throughout this article, we fix a complex reductive group
G

together with an
\operatorname{Ad}
-invariant non-degenerate symmetric bilinear form

(\bm{\cdot},\bm{\cdot})
on its Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}=\operatorname{Lie}G
.
A variety means a (possibly reducible) complex algebraic variety.
The identity component of an algebraic group
H
is denoted by
H^{0}
.

Quasi-Hamiltonian geometry

,

.

2.1.
Quasi-Poisson and quasi-Hamiltonian structures

For a (possibly singular)
G
-variety
M

with tangent sheaf
\Theta_{M}=\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\hskip-1.00006pt\mathit{om}}(\Omega^{1}_{M},\mathcal{O}_{M})
,
we denote by
\mathfrak{g}\to\Gamma(M,\Theta_{M})
,
\xi\mapsto\xi_{M}

the corresponding infinitesimal action;
for
\xi\in\mathfrak{g}
, the vector field
\xi_{M}
is characterized by

\xi_{M}(df)(p)=\left.\frac{d}{dt}f(e^{-t\xi}\cdot p)\right|_{t=0}\quad(p\in M,\ f\in\mathcal{O}_{M,p}).

For each
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
it induces a map

(\bm{\cdot})_{M}\colon\bigwedge^{k}\mathfrak{g}\to\Gamma(M,\bigwedge^{k}\Theta_{M})
.
Define a homomorphism of
\mathcal{O}_{M}
-modules

(\bm{\cdot})_{M}\colon\mathcal{O}_{M}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\bigwedge^{k}\mathfrak{g}\to\bigwedge^{k}\Theta_{M}

by
f\otimes\xi\mapsto f\xi_{M}
.

Let
(\bm{\cdot})^{\vee}\colon\mathfrak{g}^{*}\to\mathfrak{g}
,
\alpha\mapsto\alpha^{\vee}
be the inverse of the isomorphism

\xi\mapsto(\xi,\bm{\cdot})
.
Define
\mathfrak{g}
-valued vector fields
\delta^{L},\delta^{R}\in\Gamma(G,\Theta_{G}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathfrak{g})
on
G
by

\delta^{L}_{x}(\theta)=(L_{x}^{*}\theta)^{\vee},\quad\delta^{R}_{x}(\theta)=(R_{x}^{*}\theta)^{\vee}\quad(x\in G,\ \theta\in T_{x}^{*}G).

Put
\delta=\tfrac{1}{2}(\delta^{L}+\delta^{R})
.

Definition 2.1
.

(1) A
quasi-Poisson
G
-variety
is a
G
-variety
M

equipped with a
G
-invariant skew-symmetric bracket operation

\{\bm{\cdot},\bm{\cdot}\}\colon\mathcal{O}_{M}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{O}_{M}\to\mathcal{O}_{M},

on the structure sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{M}
satisfying

(1)

\displaystyle\{f,gh\}

\displaystyle=g\{f,h\}+\{f,g\}h,

(2)

\displaystyle\{f,\{g,h\}\}+\{g,\{h,f\}\}+\{h,\{f,g\}\}

\displaystyle=\chi_{M}(df,dg,dh)\qquad(f,g,h\in\mathcal{O}_{M}),

where
\chi\in\bigwedge^{3}\mathfrak{g}
is defined by

\chi(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)=\langle\alpha,[\beta^{\vee},\gamma^{\vee}]\rangle\quad(\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in\mathfrak{g}).

The bracket
\{\bm{\cdot},\bm{\cdot}\}
is called a
quasi-Poisson bracket
.

(2) For a quasi-Poisson
G
-variety
M
,
a
G
-equivariant morphism
\mu\colon M\to G

(where
G
acts on itself by conjugation)
is called a
moment map
if
the bivector field

\Pi\in\Gamma(M,\bigwedge^{2}\Theta_{M})

corresponding to the bracket
\{\bm{\cdot},\bm{\cdot}\}
satisfies

\Pi(\mu^{*}\theta,\bm{\cdot})=\left(\delta(\theta)\circ\mu\right)_{M}\quad(\theta\in\Omega^{1}_{M}).

A quasi-Poisson
G
-variety equipped with a moment map
is called a
Hamiltonian quasi-Poisson
G
-variety
.

Note that if
G
is abelian (e.g.
G=\{1\}
), then
\chi=0
and hence
a quasi-Poisson structure is a usual Poisson structure.

Just as a non-degenerate Poisson structure comes from a symplectic structure,
so some class of Hamiltonian quasi-Poisson structures come from
the following geometric objects.

Definition 2.2
.

A
quasi-Hamiltonian
G
-space
is a smooth
G
-variety
equipped with a
G
-invariant two-form
\omega
on
M

and a
G
-equivariant morphism
\mu\colon M\to G

(where
G
acts on itself by conjugation)
satisfying the following conditions.

(QH1)

d\omega=\tfrac{1}{12}(\mu^{-1}d\mu,[\mu^{-1}d\mu,\mu^{-1}d\mu])
.

(QH2)

\omega(\xi_{M},\bm{\cdot})=(\mu^{-1}d\mu+d\mu\,\mu^{-1},\xi)
for all
\xi\in\mathfrak{g}
.

(QH3)

\operatorname{Ker}\omega_{p}\cap\operatorname{Ker}(d\mu)_{p}=\{0\}
for any
p\in M
.

The two-form
\omega
is called the
quasi-Hamiltonian two-form
and

\mu
is called the
moment map
.

If
G
is abelian, then the three axioms imply that
\omega
is a symplectic form.

Theorem 2.3

(
[
1
, Theorem 10.3]
,
[
9
, Corollary 3.22]
)
.

For any quasi-Hamiltonian
G
-space
(M,\omega,\mu)
,
there exists a unique quasi-Poisson
G
-structure

\{\bm{\cdot},\bm{\cdot}\}
on
M
with
\mu
a moment map
such that the corresponding bivector field

\Pi\in\Gamma(M,\bigwedge^{2}\Theta_{M})
satisfies

\Pi(\iota_{v}\omega,\bm{\cdot})=v-\frac{1}{4}\left((\mu^{-1}d\mu-d\mu\,\mu^{-1})(v)\right)_{M}\quad(v\in\Theta_{M}).

A Hamiltonian quasi-Poisson structure coming from
a quasi-Hamiltonian structure in the above way
is said to be
non-degenerate
;
see
[
1
]
for an explicit characterization of non-degeneracy.

Example 2.4
.

The bracket
\{\bm{\cdot},\bm{\cdot}\}\colon\mathcal{O}_{G}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{O}_{G}\to\mathcal{O}_{G}
defined by

\{f,g\}=\frac{1}{2}(\delta^{R}(df),\delta^{L}(dg))-\frac{1}{2}(\delta^{R}(dg),\delta^{L}(df))\quad(f,g\in\mathcal{O}_{G})

is a quasi-Poisson
G
-structure on
G
with respect to the conjugation action
(and does not depend on the choice of orthonormal basis)
and the identity map
\mathrm{id}\colon G\to G
is a moment map.
By the moment map condition,
the vector fields
\{f,\bm{\cdot}\}
,
f\in\mathcal{O}_{G}

are tangent to conjugacy classes.
It follows that any
G
-invariant subvariety
Z\subset G

is a quasi-Poisson
G
-subvariety of
G
, which means that the bracket
\{\bm{\cdot},\bm{\cdot}\}

descends to a quasi-Poisson bracket on
\mathcal{O}_{Z}
.
Moreover the inclusion
Z\hookrightarrow G
is a moment map for
Z
.
In particular, any conjugacy class
\mathcal{C}\subset G

is a Hamiltonian quasi-Poisson
G
-variety with moment map given by the inclusion.
In fact, the quasi-Poisson structure on
\mathcal{C}
comes from
the quasi-Hamiltonian two-form

\omega_{x}(\xi_{G},\eta_{G})=\frac{1}{2}(\xi,\operatorname{Ad}_{x}\eta)-\frac{1}{2}(\eta,\operatorname{Ad}_{x}\xi)\qquad(x\in\mathcal{C},\ \xi,\eta\in\mathfrak{g}).

If we fix
x\in\mathcal{C}
, then the pullback of
\omega
along
the map
\pi\colon G\to\mathcal{C}
,
C\mapsto C^{-1}xC
is given by

\pi^{*}\omega=\frac{1}{2}(dC\,C^{-1},\operatorname{Ad}_{x}(dC\,C^{-1})).

Example 2.5
.

Let
G\times G
act on
\mathbf{D}(G)=G\times G
by

(g,k)\cdot(C,h)=(kCg^{-1},khk^{-1})
.
Then
\mathbf{D}(G)
is a quasi-Hamiltonian
G\times G
-space with moment map

\mu\colon\mathbf{D}(G)\to G\times G,\quad(C,h)\mapsto(C^{-1}hC,h^{-1})

and quasi-Hamiltonian two-form

\omega=\frac{1}{2}(dC\,C^{-1},\operatorname{Ad}_{h}(dC\,C^{-1}))+\frac{1}{2}(dC\,C^{-1},h^{-1}dh+dh\,h^{-1}).

2.2.
Reduction

Let
H
be another complex reductive group
equipped with an
\operatorname{Ad}
-invariant non-degenerate symmetric bilinear form on

\mathfrak{h}=\operatorname{Lie}H
.

The following is the complex algebraic version of
a particular case of
[
13
, Theorem 1.C]

(the proof is similar).
Recall that a
good quotient
of a
G
-variety
M

in the sense of Seshadri
[
15
]

is a variety
M/G
together with
a
G
-invariant surjective affine morphism
\pi\colon M\to M/G

such that for any affine open subset
U\subset M/G
,
the restriction
\pi^{-1}(U)\xrightarrow{\pi}U
is an affine quotient:

\mathbb{C}[U]=\mathbb{C}[\pi^{-1}(U)]^{G}
.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
M
be a quasi-Poisson
G\times H
-variety.

(1)
Suppose that the
G
-action on
M
has a good quotient
M/G
.
Then the quasi-Poisson structure on
M
descends to
a quasi-Poisson
H
-structure on the quotient
M/G
.
In particular, if
H=\{1\}
, then
M/G
is a Poisson variety.

(2)
Suppose that
M
is equipped with a moment map

\mu=(\mu_{G},\mu_{H})\colon M\to G\times H

and let
Z\subset G
be a
G
-invariant subvariety.
If the preimage
\mu_{G}^{-1}(Z)
has a good quotient
\mu_{G}^{-1}(Z)/G
,
then the quasi-Poisson structure on
M

induces a quasi-Poisson
H
-structure on

\mu_{G}^{-1}(Z)/G
and
the morphism
\mu_{G}^{-1}(Z)/G\to H
induced from
\mu_{H}

is a moment map.

We denote by
M/\!\!/_{\!Z}\hskip 1.00006ptG

the above Hamiltonian quasi-Poisson
H
-variety
\mu_{G}^{-1}(Z)/G

and call it the
reduction
of
M
by
G
along
Z
.
If
Z=\{1\}
, then we simply write
M/\!\!/G=M/\!\!/_{\!\{1\}}\hskip 1.00006ptG
.

When
M
is quasi-Hamiltonian, the following also holds (see
[
2
, Theorem 5.1]
and
[
1
, Proposition 10.6]
).

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
M
be a quasi-Hamiltonian
G\times H
-space
with moment map
\mu=(\mu_{G},\mu_{H})\colon M\to G\times H

and let
\mathcal{C}\subset G
be a conjugacy class.
Suppose that the
G
-action on
\mu_{G}^{-1}(\mathcal{C})
is free and
has a geometric quotient in the sense of Seshadri, i.e.,
it has a good quotient
\pi\colon\mu_{G}^{-1}(\mathcal{C})\to\mu_{G}^{-1}(\mathcal{C})/G

whose fibers are single orbits.
Then
\mu_{G}^{-1}(\mathcal{C})
,
\mu_{G}^{-1}(\mathcal{C})/G
are non-singular
and the quasi-Hamiltonian two-form restricted to
\mu_{G}^{-1}(\mathcal{C})

descends to a two-form on
\mu_{G}^{-1}(\mathcal{C})/G
,
which gives the structure of a quasi-Hamiltonian
H
-space on

\mu_{G}^{-1}(\mathcal{C})/G

with moment map induced from
\mu_{H}

(
in particular,
\mu_{G}^{-1}(\mathcal{C})/G
is a smooth symplectic variety if
H=\{1\})
.
The corresponding quasi-Poisson structure on
\mu_{G}^{-1}(\mathcal{C})/G=M/\!\!/_{\!\mathcal{C}}\hskip 1.00006ptG

coincides with the one given in the above proposition.

Remark 2.8
.

If we allow the
G
-action on
\mu_{G}^{-1}(\mathcal{C})
to have finite stabilizers in the above,
then
M/\!\!/_{\!\mathcal{C}}\hskip 1.00006ptG
may have quotient singularities, but still has
a quasi-Hamiltonian
H
-structure if we regard it as an orbifold (a smooth Deligne–Mumford stack).

Example 2.9
.

For any conjugacy class
\mathcal{C}\subset G
,
the reduction
\mathbf{D}(G)/\!\!/_{\!\mathcal{C}^{-1}}\hskip 1.00006ptG

of the double along the inverse conjugacy class
\mathcal{C}^{-1}

by the action of the second
G
-factor is isomorphic to
\mathcal{C}
.

2.3.
Fusion and gluing

Proposition 2.10

(
[
1
, Propositions 5.1, 10.7]
)
.

Let
M
be a Hamiltonian quasi-Poisson
G\times G\times H
-variety
with moment map
(\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\mu_{H})\colon M\to G\times G\times H
.
Define
\mathfrak{g}
-valued vector fields
\delta^{i}\in\Gamma(M,\Theta_{M}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathfrak{g})
,
i=1,2
by

(\delta^{i}_{M}(\theta),\xi)=\xi^{i}_{M}(\theta)\quad(\theta\in\Omega^{1}_{M}),

where
\xi\mapsto\xi^{i}_{M}
is the infinitesimal action of the
i
-th
G
-factor of
G\times G\times H
.
Then

\{f,g\}_{\mathrm{fus}}\coloneqq\{f,g\}-\frac{1}{2}(\delta^{1}(df),\delta^{2}(dg))+\frac{1}{2}(\delta^{1}(dg),\delta^{2}(df))\quad(f,g\in\mathcal{O}_{M})

defines a quasi-Poisson structure on
M
for the diagonal
G\times H
-action
with moment map
(\mu_{1}\cdot\mu_{2},\mu_{H})\colon M\to G\times H
.

If
M
is smooth and non-degenerate with quasi-Hamiltonian two-form
\omega
,
then so is
(M,\{\bm{\cdot},\bm{\cdot}\}_{\mathrm{fus}})

and the corresponding quasi-Hamiltonian two-form is given by

\omega_{\mathrm{fus}}=\omega-\frac{1}{2}(\mu_{1}^{-1}d\mu_{1},d\mu_{2}\,\mu_{2}^{-1}).

We call this procedure the (internal)
fusion
.
For instance, if
M_{i}
is a Hamiltonian quasi-Poisson
G\times H_{i}
-variety for
i=1,2
,
then the product
M_{1}\times M_{2}
is
a Hamiltonian quasi-Poisson
G\times H_{1}\times G\times H_{2}
-variety,
from which we obtain a Hamiltonian quasi-Poisson
G\times H_{1}\times H_{2}
-variety
by fusing the two
G
-factors.
We denote this Hamiltonian quasi-Poisson
G\times H_{1}\times H_{2}
-variety by

M_{1}\circledast_{G}M_{2}
(or simply
M_{1}\circledast M_{2}
)
and call it the
fusion product
of
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
.
If we can perform the reduction

(M_{1}\circledast M_{2})/\!\!/G
,
then we call it the
gluing
of
M_{1}
and
M_{2}

and denote it by
M_{1}\underset{G}{\strictiff}M_{2}
.

3.
Fission spaces and their gluing

In
[
3
,
5
]
, Boalch introduced a new class of quasi-Hamiltonian spaces,
called (higher)
fission spaces
,
and used them to construct Poisson structures on (untwisted) wild character varieties.
From now on, we fix a maximal torus
T\subset G
with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{t}\subset\mathfrak{g}
.

Let
P
be a parabolic subgroup of
G
containing the maximal torus
T

and
H
be a unique Levi subgroup of
P
containing
T
.
Let
U^{+}
be the unipotent radical of
P
and
U^{-}
be that of the opposite parabolic subgroup.
For a positive integer
r
, put

{}_{G}\mathcal{A}_{H}^{r}=G\times H\times(U^{+}\times U^{-})^{r},

which we call a fission space.
We denote an element of
(U^{+}\times U^{-})^{r}
by

(u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{2r})
, where
u_{\mathrm{odd}}\in U^{+}
and
u_{\mathrm{even}}\in U^{-}
.
Let
G\times H
act on
{}_{G}\mathcal{A}_{H}^{r}
by

(g,k)\cdot(C,h,u_{1},\dots,u_{2r})=(kCg^{-1},khk^{-1},ku_{1}k^{-1},\dots,ku_{2r}k^{-1}).

The bilinear form
(\bm{\cdot},\bm{\cdot})
on
\mathfrak{g}
restricts to
a non-degenerate
\operatorname{Ad}
-invariant symmetric bilinear form on
\mathfrak{h}=\operatorname{Lie}H
.

Theorem 3.1

(
[
5
, Theorem 3.1]
)
.

The
G\times H
-variety
{}_{G}\mathcal{A}_{H}^{r}
is a quasi-Hamiltonian
G\times H
-space
with moment map

\mu\colon(C,h,u_{1},\dots,u_{2r})\mapsto(C^{-1}hu_{1}u_{2}\cdots u_{2r}C,h^{-1}),

and quasi-Hamiltonian two-form defined by

(3)

\begin{split}2\omega&=(dC\,C^{-1},\operatorname{Ad}_{b}(dC\,C^{-1}))+(dC\,C^{-1},db\,b^{-1})\\
&\quad+(dC_{0}\,C_{0}^{-1},h^{-1}dh)-\sum_{j=0}^{2r-1}(C_{j}^{-1}dC_{j},C_{j+1}^{-1}dC_{j+1}),\end{split}

where
C_{j}=u_{j+1}u_{j+2}\cdots u_{2r}C
(so that
C_{2r}=C
),
b=hu_{1}u_{2}\cdots u_{2r}
.

More generally, let

P_{1}\subset P_{2}\subset\cdots\subset P_{r}

be an increasing sequence of parabolic subgroups of
G

containing
T
.
For
j=1,2,\dots,r
, let
U_{j}^{+}
,
U_{j}^{-}
,
H_{j}

be the unipotent radical of
P_{j}
,
that of the opposite parabolic subgroup,
the Levi subgroup of
P_{j}
containing
T
, respectively.
Then
H_{1}\subset H_{2}\subset\cdots\subset H_{r}

and
U_{1}^{\pm}\supset U_{2}^{\pm}\supset\cdots\supset U_{r}^{\pm}
.
Put

\mathcal{A}=G\times H_{1}\times\prod_{j=1}^{r}(U_{j}^{+}\times U_{j}^{-}),

which we call a
multi-fission space
.
Note that this is
a
G\times H_{1}
-invariant closed subvariety
of a fission space
{}_{G}\mathcal{A}_{H_{1}}^{r}
,
and equal to
{}_{G}\mathcal{A}_{H_{1}}^{r}
if

Proposition 3.2
.

The multi-fission space
\mathcal{A}\subset{}_{G}\mathcal{A}_{H_{1}}^{r}

is a quasi-Hamiltonian
G\times H_{1}
-space,
where the moment map and the quasi-Hamiltonian two-form
are the restrictions of those for
{}_{G}\mathcal{A}_{H_{1}}^{r}

Proof.

For each

is a parabolic subgroup of

with unipotent radical
V_{j}^{+}\coloneqq U_{j}^{+}\cap H_{j+1}

Furthermore, the product map

Thus

where

\mathcal{A}(j)\coloneqq H_{j+1}\times H_{j}\times(V_{j}^{+}\times V_{j}^{-})^{j}={}_{H_{j+1}}\mathcal{A}_{H_{j}}^{j},

along which the pull-back of the moment map and the quasi-Hamiltonian two-form
are exactly the restrictions of those for
{}_{G}\mathcal{A}_{H_{1}}

∎

In this section we briefly recall the (untwisted) wild character varieties following

Let

5.

In this section we introduce

Let

be a parabolic subgroup of

Let

Lemma 5.1
.

Take

so that the identity component
Z_{G}(h)^{0}

of the centralizer in

Then the map

In particular, for any

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{u}^{i}
,

be the lower central series of
the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{u}\coloneqq\operatorname{Lie}U^{+}

\mathfrak{u}^{0}=\mathfrak{u},\quad\mathfrak{u}^{i}=[\mathfrak{u},\mathfrak{u}^{i-1}]\quad(i>0).

For
i=1,2,\dots

\mathfrak{u}_{i}\subset\mathfrak{u}^{i-1}

complementary to
\mathfrak{u}^{i}

\mathfrak{u}^{i-1}=\mathfrak{u}_{i}\oplus\mathfrak{u}^{i}\quad(i>0).

Put
\mathfrak{u}^{\prime}_{i}=(\operatorname{Ad}_{h}^{-1}-\mathrm{id})(\mathfrak{u}_{i})
. Note that
\operatorname{Ad}_{h}^{-1}-\mathrm{id}\colon\mathfrak{u}\to\mathfrak{u}

is a linear isomorphism preserving the filtration
\{\mathfrak{u}^{i}\}_{i>0}

by the assumption for

.
Since
\mathfrak{u}
is nilpotent, we have
\mathfrak{u}=\bigoplus_{i>0}\mathfrak{u}_{i}=\bigoplus_{i>0}\mathfrak{u}^{\prime}_{i}
.
For
X\in\mathfrak{u}
, let
Y(X)\in\mathfrak{u}
be a unique element such that

e^{Y(X)}=(h^{-1}e^{-X}h)e^{X}=e^{-\operatorname{Ad}_{h}^{-1}(X)}e^{X}.

By the Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff formula,

is expressed as

, where

Y_{1}(X)=-\operatorname{Ad}_{h}^{-1}(X)+X
and each

is a linear combination of elements of the form

\operatorname{ad}_{Z_{1}}\operatorname{ad}_{Z_{2}}\cdots\operatorname{ad}_{Z_{i-1}}(Z_{i}),\quad Z_{1},\dots,Z_{i}\in\{-\operatorname{Ad}_{h}^{-1}(X),X\}.

Observe that if we decompose

as

,
X_{i}\in\mathfrak{u}_{i}

(10)

\displaystyle Y(X)+\mathfrak{u}^{i}

\displaystyle=Y_{1}(X)+Y_{2}(X)+\cdots+Y_{i}(X)+\mathfrak{u}^{i}

(11)

\displaystyle=-(\operatorname{Ad}_{h}^{-1}-\mathrm{id})(X_{i})+\sum_{j=1}^{i}Y_{j}(X_{1}+X_{2}+\cdots+X_{i-1})+\mathfrak{u}^{i}.

Thus for any
Y=\sum Y_{i}\in\mathfrak{u}
,
Y_{i}\in\mathfrak{u}^{\prime}_{i}
,
the equation
Y(X)=Y
determines
X_{i}\in\mathfrak{u}_{i}
,
i=1,2,\dots
inductively with

, and

gives an inverse of the map

∎

so that
Z_{G}(h_{0})^{0}\subset H
,

\tau

.

H\times U^{+}\times U^{-}\to G,\quad(k,u,v)\mapsto kuv

is also étale.

, the first term
(dp\,p^{-1},\operatorname{Ad}_{h_{0}}(dp\,p^{-1}))

is equal to
(dk\,k^{-1},\operatorname{Ad}_{h_{0}}(dk\,k^{-1}))
, which is the pullback of

2\omega_{H}
along the map
\pi_{H}\colon(k,w,v)\mapsto k^{-1}h_{0}k
.
On the other hand, we have
hu=p^{-1}h_{0}p
, and hence

(20)

\displaystyle d(hu)(hu)^{-1}

\displaystyle=-p^{-1}dp+\operatorname{Ad}_{p^{-1}h_{0}}(dp\,p^{-1})

(21)

\displaystyle=-p^{-1}dp+\operatorname{Ad}_{hu}(p^{-1}dp).

Similarly, we have
(hu)^{-1}d(hu)=-\operatorname{Ad}_{hu}^{-1}(p^{-1}dp)+p^{-1}dp
. Thus

(hu)^{-1}d(hu)+d(hu)(hu)^{-1}=-\operatorname{Ad}_{hu}^{-1}(p^{-1}dp)+\operatorname{Ad}_{hu}(p^{-1}dp),

whence the desired formula.
∎

Remark 5.3
.

In particular, if

Lemma 6.5

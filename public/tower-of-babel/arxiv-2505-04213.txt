Explicit Zsigmondy bounds for families of Drinfeld modules of rank 2 Thanks: Supported by ANID Doctorado Nacional 21200910
Matías Alvarado

Abstract
. We give explicit bounds for Zsigmondy sets of certain families of Drinfeld modules of rank 2. The primary strategy is to bound the local heights associated to Drinfeld modules and then relate canonical to classical heights.

Primary 11G09; Secondary 11S82, 37P15,

1.
Introduction

Let
p
be a prime and
q
be a power of
p
. Given a Drinfeld module
\phi
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)
and an element
x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)
, we study arithmetic aspects of the orbit of
x
via the rational function
\phi_{T}.
The element
\phi_{T^{n}}(x)
is written as
\phi_{T^{n}}(x)=\mathfrak{A}_{n}\mathfrak{B}_{n}^{-1}
, with
\mathfrak{A}_{n}
and
\mathfrak{B}_{n}
coprime polynomials. A primitive divisor of
\mathfrak{A}_{n}
is a prime element
\mathfrak{p}
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
such that
\mathfrak{p}|\mathfrak{A}_{n}
and
\mathfrak{p}\nmid\mathfrak{A}_{i}
for every
0\leq i<n
. Then the Zsigmondy set associated to
\phi
and
x
is defined by

\mathcal{Z}(\phi,x)=\{n\geq 1:\mathfrak{A}_{n}\text{ does not have a primitive divisor }\}.

The finiteness of the Zsigmondy set attached to a Drinfeld module
\phi
and a non-torsion point
x
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)
has been proved in
[
7
]
by Ji and Zhao. Unfortunately, the proof does not give a bound on the size nor the maximum of
\mathcal{Z}(\phi,x)
. In fact, it is a challenging task to find these in general. One of the main difficulties is understanding the behavior of certain local heights.
In this article, we study of families of Drinfeld modules of rank 2 for which we can bound
\max\mathcal{Z}(\phi,x)
, and for these families, our bound depends only on the degree and the number of prime divisors of the coefficients.

In the previous setting, sequence
\mathfrak{A}_{n}
is analogous to elliptic divisibility sequences (EDS). The EDS are essential in arithmetic dynamics and play an important role in problems of decidability, e.g., see
[
6
]

If
K
is a function field, then
M_{K}
will be the set of places of
K
. Henceforth,
\log_{q}
always means the logarithm to the base
q.
Our main result is the following:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\phi\colon\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]\to\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)}(\mathbb{G}_{a})
be a Drinfeld module of rank
2
given by

\phi_{T}(x)=Tx+gx^{q}+\Delta x^{q^{2}},

where
g,\Delta\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
. Let
N
be the least common multiple of the degrees of irreducible divisors of
\Delta
. Let us suppose that
\phi_{T}(1)
is coprime to
T\Delta
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[T].
Let
S
be the finite set of places
\{v\in M_{\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)}:v\,|\,T\Delta\}\cup\{v_{\infty}\}.

If
x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
is not a torsion point of
\phi
, then

\max\mathcal{Z}(\phi,x)\leq\left(6(q^{2N}-1)N|S|+4\right)+\dfrac{1}{2}\log_{q}\left(2+2\deg g+2\deg\Delta\right).

We first prove this under the assumption that
N=1
(see Section
5
) and then deduce the general case.
In what follows,
S
will denote a finite set of places as in Theorem
1.1
. To ease the notation, let
\Theta=q^{r},
where
r
is the rank of the corresponding Drinfeld module when we discuss higher rank Drinfeld modules.

The structure of the paper is as follows. In Section 2, we study heights in function fields and establish bounds for the difference between the classical height and the canonical height associated to a Drinfeld module. In Section 3, we introduce the main idea of the proof, inspired by the results in
[
7
]
. In Section 4, we study the behavior of local heights. Finally, in Section 5, we establish Theorem
1.1
for the case
N=1
, and then, we use this particular case to conclude the result for general
N
.

2.
Heights

2.1.
Heights and Drinfeld modules

In this section, we recall two notions of heights of points
x\in\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)}
. First, we define the classical height.

Definition 1
.

Let
x
be an algebraic element over
\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)
, and let
K/\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)
be a finite extension such that
x\in K
. We define the
height
of
x
, denoted by
h(x)
as

h(x)=\dfrac{1}{[K:\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)]}\sum_{v\in M_{K}}\mathrm{log}_{q}\ \mathrm{max}\{|x|_{v},1\}.

Remark 2.1
.

If
x=a(T)/b(T)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)
, such that
\gcd(a(T),b(T))=1,
then
h(x)
can be expressed as

h(x)=\max\left\{\deg a(T),\deg b(T)\right\}.

In
[
1
]
, Denis defined a height corresponding to a Drinfeld module called canonical height. This height is the analog of the Néron-Tate height on elliptic curves.

Definition 2

(Denis,
[
1
]
)
.

Let
\phi
be an
\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
-Drinfeld module of rank
r
defined over an algebraic extension K of
\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)
. The
global canonical height
attached to
\phi
is defined as follows: For
x\in K
,

\hat{h}(x)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{h(\phi_{T^{n}}(x))}{\Theta^{n}}.

Remark 2.2
.

Following Théorème 1 in
[
1
]
, if
a
is any non-constant element in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
, then we can calculate
\hat{h}(x)
as follow

\hat{h}(x)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{h(\phi_{a^{n}}(x))}{(\deg\phi_{a}(x))^{n}}.

Now, we give a list of height properties that we will use to prove the main theorem.
If
\phi
is a Drinfeld module defined over a field
k
, given by
\phi_{T}(x)=Tx+g_{1}x^{q}+\cdots g_{r}x^{q^{r}}
and
\gamma\in\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)}^{\times}
, then we can obtain another Drinfeld module by conjugating the Drinfeld module
\phi
by multiplication by
\gamma,
and it is denoted by
\phi^{(\gamma)}
. If
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
, then
\phi^{(\gamma)}_{a}(x)=Tx+g_{1}\gamma^{q-1}x^{q}+\cdots+g_{r}\gamma^{q^{r}-1}x^{q^{r}}
. This new Drinfeld module
\phi^{(\gamma)}
is defined over
k(\gamma^{1/(q^{r}-1)})
.
If
\mathfrak{a}
is an ideal of
\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
, then
\mathrm{Nr}(\mathfrak{a})=\#(\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]/\mathfrak{a})
. The norm of the infinite place is
\mathrm{Nr}(v_{\infty})=q
.
For a finite set of primes
S
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
, we define
\displaystyle\mathrm{Nr}^{S}(\mathfrak{a})=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\notin S}\mathrm{Nr}(\mathfrak{p})^{\mathrm{ord}_{\mathfrak{p}}(\mathfrak{a})}.
For
a(T)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
, we denote by
\mathrm{Nr}(a(T))
the norm of the ideal generated by
a(T)
. Similarly,
\mathrm{Nr}^{S}(a(T))
represent the norm
\mathrm{Nr}^{S}
of the ideal generated by
a(T).

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
\phi
be an
\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
-Drinfeld module of rank
r
defined over
\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)}
,
\gamma\in\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)}^{\times}
, and
x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)
, then

(i)

\hat{h}_{\phi}(x)=\hat{h}_{\phi^{(\gamma)}}(\gamma^{-1}x)
.

(ii)

\hat{h}(\phi_{T^{n}}(x))=\Theta^{n}\hat{h}(x)
.

Proof.

Item (i) follows by Proposition 2 in
[
5
]
. To prove (ii), let
x
be an element in
\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)
, then

\displaystyle\hat{h}(\phi_{T^{n}}(x))

\displaystyle=\lim_{m\to\infty}\dfrac{h(\phi_{T^{m}}(\phi_{T^{n}}(x)))}{\Theta^{m}}

\displaystyle=\lim_{m\to\infty}\Theta^{n}\dfrac{h(\phi_{T^{n+m}(x)})}{\Theta^{n+m}}

\displaystyle=\Theta^{n}\hat{h}(x),

which gives the desired result.
∎

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
x=a(T)/b(T)
be an element in
\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)
, with
a(T)
and
b(T)
coprime polynomials. Let
S
be a finite set of places containing
v_{\infty}
. Then

h(x)=\log_{q}\mathrm{Nr}^{S}(a(T))+\sum_{v\in S}\mathrm{log}_{q}\ \mathrm{max}\{1,|x|^{-1}_{v}\}.

Proof.

Notice
h(x)=h(x^{-1})
. Thus, we only need to prove
\mathrm{Nr}^{S}(a(T))=\prod_{v\notin S}\max\{1,|x^{-1}|_{v}\}
. If
v\notin S
and
v(a(T))>0
, then

\max\{1,|x^{-1}|_{v}\}=\max\{1,q^{v(x)\deg(v)}\}=q^{deg(v)v(x)}=(q^{deg(v)})^{v(a(T))}=\mathrm{Nr}_{v}(a(T)),

where
\mathrm{Nr}_{v}(a(T))=\mathrm{Nr}(\mathfrak{p}_{v})^{v(a(T))}
, and
\mathfrak{p}_{v}
the prime ideal associated to the place
v
. When
v(a(T))=0
, both
\mathrm{Nr}_{v}(a(T))
and
\max\{1,|x^{-1}|_{v}\}
are equal to
1
. We conclude that
\mathrm{Nr}_{v}(a(T))=\max\{1,|x^{-1}|_{v}\}
for all
v\notin S.

∎

The following corollary follows immediately from Proposition
2.4
.

Corollary 2.5
.

If
x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)
, and
\phi_{T^{n}}(x)=\mathfrak{A}_{n}/\mathfrak{B}_{n}
, then

\displaystyle h(\phi_{T^{n}}(x))=\log_{q}\mathrm{Nr}^{S}(\mathfrak{A}_{n})+\sum_{v\in S}\mathrm{log}_{q}\ \mathrm{max}\{1,|\phi_{T^{n}}(x)^{-1}|_{v}\}.

2.2.
Difference between
h
and
\hat{h}

To establish bounds for the maximum of Zsigmondy sets, we base our approach on understanding the difference between the classical height
h
and the canonical height
\hat{h}
. We estimate this difference and obtain bounds independent of the element
x
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
. The bounds depend on the coefficients of the Drinfeld module
\phi.
Let
\phi
be a rank
r

\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
-Drinfeld module defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)
such that
\phi_{T}(x)=Tx+c_{1}x^{q}+...+c_{r}x^{\Theta}
, where
c_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
, and
c_{r}\neq 0
. Then, we define two constants associated to
\phi
,

\displaystyle M_{\phi}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{q}{(\Theta-1)(q-1)}\max\{1-\deg c_{r},...,\deg c_{r-1}-\deg c_{r},0\},

\displaystyle M^{\prime}_{\phi}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{(\Theta-1)}\max\{1,\deg c_{1},...,\deg c_{r}\}.

By Proposition
6
in
[
5
]
, we can decompose the heights
h
and
\hat{h}
as the sum of the local heights
h_{v}(x)
and
\hat{h}_{v}(x)
, respectively, where

h_{v}(x)=\mathrm{log}_{q}\ \mathrm{max}\{1,|x|_{v}\},\text{ \ \ and \ \ }\hat{h}_{v}(x)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{\mathrm{log}_{q}\ \mathrm{max}\{1,|\phi_{T^{n}}(x)|_{v}\}}{\Theta^{n}}.

Now, we state the results concerning the bounds for the difference of heights.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
\phi
be a rank
r

\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
-Drinfeld module defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)
such that
\phi_{T}(x)=Tx+c_{1}x^{q}+\cdots+c_{r}x^{q^{r}}
, with
c_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
for
0\leq i<r
and
c_{r}\neq 0
. Then, for all
x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T],
we have

h(x)-\hat{h}(x)\leq M_{\phi}.

Proof.

The first step in the proof is to bound the expression
\mathrm{log}_{q}\ \mathrm{max}\{|x|_{v}^{\Theta},1\}-\mathrm{log}_{q}\ \mathrm{max}\{|\phi_{T}(x)|_{v},1\}
for each place
v
in
M_{\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)}
. From now on, we will denote the previous difference by
\kappa_{v}
. Since
\phi_{T}
has coefficients in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
, and
x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
, both
|x|_{v}
and
|\phi_{T}(x)|_{v}
are bounded by
1
for all finite places
v.
Therefore, for these places, we have
\mathrm{log}_{q}\ \mathrm{max}\{|x|_{v}^{\Theta},1\}-\mathrm{log}_{q}\ \mathrm{max}\{|\phi_{T}(x)|_{v},1\}=0.
Thus, we only need to bound
\mathrm{log}_{q}\ \mathrm{max}\{|x|_{v}^{\Theta},1\}-\mathrm{log}_{q}\ \mathrm{max}\{|\phi_{T}(x)|_{v},1\}
when
v
is the infinite place
v_{\infty}.

To achieve this, first, we suppose that
|c_{r}x^{\Theta}|_{v_{\infty}}>|c_{i}x^{q^{i}}|_{v_{\infty}}
for all
i\in\{0,...,r-1\}
, where we use the convention
c_{0}=T.
Then,

\kappa_{v_{\infty}}=\log_{q}|x|_{v_{\infty}}^{\Theta}-\log_{q}|x|_{v_{\infty}}^{\Theta}-\log_{q}|c_{r}|_{v_{\infty}}=-\log_{q}|c_{r}|_{v_{\infty}}=-\deg c_{r}.

Now, if
|x|_{v_{\infty}}\leq\left|\dfrac{c_{i}}{c_{r}}\right|_{v_{\infty}}^{\frac{1}{\Theta-q^{i}}}
for some
i\in\{0,...,r-1\},
we have

|x|_{v_{\infty}}^{\Theta}\leq\max\left\{\left|\dfrac{T}{c_{r}}\right|_{v_{\infty}}^{\frac{\Theta}{\Theta-1}},\left|\dfrac{c_{1}}{c_{r}}\right|_{v_{\infty}}^{\frac{\Theta}{\Theta-q}},...,\left|\dfrac{c_{r-1}}{c_{r}}\right|_{v_{\infty}}^{\frac{\Theta}{\Theta-q^{r-1}}}\right\}.

Therefore

\displaystyle\kappa_{v_{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{log}_{q}\ \mathrm{max}\left\{\left|\dfrac{T}{c_{r}}\right|_{v_{\infty}}^{\frac{\Theta}{\Theta-1}},\left|\dfrac{c_{1}}{c_{r}}\right|_{v_{\infty}}^{\frac{\Theta}{\Theta-q}},...,\left|\dfrac{c_{r-1}}{c_{r}}\right|_{v_{\infty}}^{\frac{\Theta}{\Theta-q^{r-1}}}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{q}{(q-1)}\max\{1-\deg c_{r},...,\deg c_{r-1}-\deg c_{r},\}.

This way, we conclude that

\kappa_{v_{\infty}}\leq\dfrac{q}{(q-1)}\max\{1-\deg c_{r},...,\deg c_{r-1}-\deg c_{r},0\}.

Let us denote by
m_{\phi}
the expression
\dfrac{q}{(q-1)}\max\{1-\deg c_{r},...,\deg c_{r-1}-\deg c_{r},0\}.

The second step is to bound

\mathrm{log}_{q}\ \mathrm{max}\{|x|_{v},1\}-\dfrac{1}{\Theta^{n}}\mathrm{log}_{q}\ \mathrm{max}\{|\phi_{T^{n}}(x)|_{v},1\}.
Following the same strategy as in the first step, we obtain that
\phi_{T^{n}}(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T],
since
x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
. Then,
\mathrm{log}_{q}\ \mathrm{max}\{|x|_{v},1\}-\dfrac{1}{\Theta^{n}}\mathrm{log}_{q}\ \mathrm{max}\{|\phi_{T^{n}}(x)|_{v},1\}=0
for all finite places. On the other hand, if
v=v_{\infty},
we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{log}_{q}\ \mathrm{max}\{|x|_{v_{\infty}},1\}-

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{\Theta^{n}}\mathrm{log}_{q}\ \mathrm{max}\{|\phi_{T^{n}}(x)|_{v_{\infty}},1\}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{\Theta^{n}}\left(\mathrm{log}_{q}\ \mathrm{max}\{|x|_{v_{\infty}}^{\Theta^{n}},1\}-\mathrm{log}_{q}\ \mathrm{max}\{|\phi_{T^{n}}(x)|_{v_{\infty}},1\}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\dfrac{1}{\Theta^{k+1}}\left(\mathrm{log}_{q}\ \mathrm{max}\{|\phi_{T^{k}}(x)|_{v_{\infty}}^{\Theta},1\}-\mathrm{log}_{q}\ \mathrm{max}\{|\phi_{T^{k+1}}(x)|_{v_{\infty}},1\}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{n}\dfrac{1}{\Theta^{k}}m_{\phi}.

Therefore, taking the limit
n\to\infty
, we conclude

h_{v_{\infty}}(x)-\hat{h}_{v_{\infty}}(x)\leq m_{\phi}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{1}{\Theta^{k}}=\dfrac{m_{\phi}}{\Theta-1}=M_{\phi}.

Finally, we note
\displaystyle h(x)-\hat{h}(x)=h_{v_{\infty}}(x)-\hat{h}_{v_{\infty}}(x)\leq M_{\phi}.

∎

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
\phi
be a rank
r

\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
-Drinfeld module defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)
such that
\phi_{T}(x)=Tx+c_{1}x^{q}+\cdots+c_{r}x^{\Theta}
with
c_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
for
0\leq i<r
and
c_{r}\neq 0
. Then for all
x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
, we have

\hat{h}(x)-h(x)\leq M^{\prime}_{\phi}.

Proof.

As in the proof of Lemma
2.6
, the first step is to estimate the difference between
\mathrm{log}_{q}\ \mathrm{max}\{|\phi_{T}(x)|_{v},1\}
and
\mathrm{log}_{q}\ \mathrm{max}\{|x|^{\Theta},1\}
. Again, we only study this difference for the infinite place since
x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
. If we suppose
|c_{r}x^{\Theta}|_{v_{\infty}}>|c_{i}x^{q^{i}}|_{v_{\infty}}
for all
0\leq i<r,
then
\mathrm{log}_{q}\ \mathrm{max}\{|\phi_{T}(x)|_{v_{\infty}},1\}-\mathrm{log}_{q}\ \mathrm{max}\{|x|_{v_{\infty}}^{\Theta}\,1\}=\log_{q}|c_{r}|_{v_{\infty}}=\deg c_{r}
. On the other hand, we suppose
|x|_{v_{\infty}}\leq\left|\dfrac{c_{i}}{c_{r}}\right|_{v_{\infty}}^{\frac{1}{\Theta-q^{i}}}
for some
i<r,
and that
|c_{i}x^{q^{i}}|
is the maximum between
|Tx|,|c_{1}x^{q}|,...,|c_{r}x^{\Theta}|
. Therefore,

\displaystyle-\kappa_{v_{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{log}_{q}\ \mathrm{max}\{|c_{i}x^{q^{i}}|_{v_{\infty}},1\}-\log_{q}|x|_{v_{\infty}}^{\Theta}

\displaystyle=\max\left\{\log_{q}|c_{i}|_{v_{\infty}}+\log_{q}|x|_{v_{\infty}}^{q^{i}-\Theta},\log_{q}|x|_{v_{\infty}}^{-\Theta}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\deg c_{i}

\displaystyle\leq\max\{1,\deg c_{1},...,\deg c_{r}\}=:m^{\prime}_{\phi}.

The second equality holds because
|x|_{v_{\infty}}>1
. As in the previous estimation, we obtain

\hat{h}(x)-h(x)=\hat{h}_{v_{\infty}}(x)-h_{v_{\infty}}(x)\leq\dfrac{1}{\Theta-1}m^{\prime}_{\phi}=M_{\phi}^{\prime}.

∎

2.3.
Bounds in rank 2

Although the bounds from the previous section for the difference between the heights hold for Drinfeld modules of arbitrary rank, we use them in the particular case of rank 2. In this subsection, we give the explicit bounds in terms of the coefficients
g
and
\Delta
to establish a bound for the Zsigmondy set
\mathcal{Z}(\phi,x)
as a function of
g
and
\Delta
.

Following the notation of Subsection
2.2
, for a Drinfeld module
\phi
given by
\phi_{T}(x)=Tx+gx^{q}+\Delta x^{q^{2}}
, we have

\displaystyle M_{\phi}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{q}{(q^{2}-1)(q-1)}\max\{1-\deg\Delta,\deg g-\deg\Delta,0\}\text{ and }

\displaystyle M_{\phi}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{(q^{2}-1)}\max\{1,\deg g,\deg\Delta\}.

Then, we get the following bounds for the difference between
h
and
\hat{h}

\displaystyle h(x)-\hat{h}(x)

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{1}{(q-1)^{2}}(1+\deg g)\text{ and }

\displaystyle\hat{h}(x)-h(x)

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{1}{(q-1)^{2}}(\deg\Delta+\deg g+1).

3.
Previous results and strategy

In the proof of Theorem 4.6 in
[
7
]
, the authors obtain the following inequality

(3.1)

\log_{q}\left(\dfrac{\mathrm{Nr}^{S}(\mathfrak{A}_{n})}{\mathrm{Nr}^{S}(\mathfrak{A}_{n-1})}\right)\geq\left(\Theta-\dfrac{3}{2}\right)\Theta^{n-1}\hat{h}(x)-M_{\phi}.

This inequality holds for all natural numbers greater than
n_{0}
. The existence of such
n_{0}
is proved in Lemma 4.5 (1) in
[
7
]
.

Our strategy to prove the main theorem in this article is to find such
n_{0}
and find explicitly bounds on
n
such that

\left(\Theta-\dfrac{3}{2}\right)\Theta^{n-1}\hat{h}(x)-M_{\phi}>0,\text{ for all }n\geq n_{0}.

Definition 3
.

For a place
v
and a sequence of elements
\{\mathfrak{A}_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
, the rank of apparition at
v
is defined by the integer

r_{v}=\min\{n\geq 1:\mathrm{ord}_{v}(\mathfrak{A}_{n})>0\}.

If no such
n
exists,
r_{v}
is set to be
\infty.

Lemma 3.1

(
[
7
]
)
.

For every finite place
v
such that
v(T)=0
, and a sequence of polynomials
\{\mathfrak{A}_{n}\}
derived from
\{\phi_{T^{n}}(x)\}_{n\geq 1}
, if
\ell\leq r_{v}
, we have
\mathrm{ord}_{v}(\mathfrak{A}_{\ell-1})=0
; otherwise,
\mathrm{ord}_{v}(\mathfrak{A}_{\ell})=\mathrm{ord}_{v}(\mathfrak{A}_{\ell-1})
.

Proof.

See Lemma 4.3 in
[
7
]
.
∎

Having said this, we need to find such
n_{0}
, and in this way, thanks to equation
3.1
, we will be able to show such that
\log_{q}\left(\mathrm{Nr}^{S}(\mathfrak{A}_{n})/\mathrm{Nr}^{S}(\mathfrak{A}_{n-1})\right)>0
for all
n\geq n_{0}
.

4.
Local heights

4.1.
Estimates

In order to understand the behavior of
h(\phi_{T^{n}}(x))
, by Proposition
2.4
, we have to find an upper bound for
\sum_{v\in S}\mathrm{log}_{q}\ \mathrm{max}\left\{|\phi_{T^{n}}(x)^{-1}|_{v},1\right\}.

We note that

\sum_{v\in S}\mathrm{log}_{q}\ \mathrm{max}\left\{|\phi_{T^{n}}(x)^{-1}|_{v},1\right\}=\sum_{v\in S}\deg(v)\cdot\max\left\{v(\phi_{T^{n}}(x)),0\right\}.

Consequently, it is enough to study
\max\left\{v(\phi_{T^{n}}(x)),0\right\}
for
v\in S.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\phi\colon\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]\to\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)}(\mathbb{G}_{a})
be a Drinfeld module of rank
2
given by

\phi_{T}(x)=Tx+gx^{q}+\Delta x^{q^{2}}

such that
\Delta\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
split completely over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
,
g\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
and
\phi_{T}(1)
is coprime to
T\Delta
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[T].
Then for all
x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
, we have

\sum_{v\in S}\mathrm{log}_{q}\ \mathrm{max}\left\{|\phi_{T^{n}}(x)^{-1}|_{v},1\right\}=0.

Proof.

If
v=v_{\infty}
, then
v(\phi_{T^{n}}(x))\leq 0
because
x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
, so
\deg(v_{\infty})\cdot\max\{v(\phi_{T^{n}}(x)),0\}=0.

On the other hand, if
v(T\Delta)>0
, by euclidean division, we can express
x
as

(4.1)

x=P(T)y+\gamma,

where
\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
P(T)
is the irreducible polynomial corresponding to the place
v
. Evaluating
\phi_{T}
in equation
4.1
,

\displaystyle\phi_{T}(x)

\displaystyle=\phi_{T}(P(T)y)+\phi_{T}(\gamma)

\displaystyle=P(T)\left(Tx+P(T)^{q-1}gx^{q}+P(T)^{q^{2}-1}\Delta x^{q^{2}-1}\right)+\gamma(T+g+\Delta).

We note that
\gamma(T+g+\Delta)=\phi_{T}(1)
. Hence
\phi_{T}(1)
is coprime to
T\Delta
we have
v(\gamma(T+g+\Delta))=0
, and then we conclude
v(\phi_{T}(x))=0.
Finally, we have

\sum_{v\in S}\mathrm{log}_{q}\ \mathrm{max}\left\{|\phi_{T^{n}}(x)^{-1}|_{v},1\right\}=0.

∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\phi
be a rank
2

\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
-Drinfeld module defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q}(T).
Then, for all
x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]

\hat{h}(x)-\dfrac{\log_{q}\mathrm{Nr}^{S}(\mathfrak{A}_{n})}{\Theta^{n}}\leq\dfrac{M^{\prime}_{\phi}}{\Theta^{n}}+\dfrac{1}{\Theta^{n}}\sum_{v\in S}\mathrm{log}_{q}\ \mathrm{max}\{|\phi_{T^{n}}(x)^{-1}|_{v},1\}.

Proof.

We add and subtract
\dfrac{h(\phi_{T^{n}}(x))}{\Theta^{n}}
to the expression
\hat{h}(x)-\dfrac{\log_{q}\mathrm{Nr}^{S}(\mathfrak{A}_{n})}{\Theta^{n}}
, then

(4.2)

\hat{h}(x)-\dfrac{\log_{q}\mathrm{Nr}^{S}(\mathfrak{A}_{n})}{\Theta^{n}}=\hat{h}(x)-\dfrac{h(\phi_{T^{n}}(x))}{\Theta^{n}}+\left(\dfrac{h(\phi_{T^{n}}(x))}{\Theta^{n}}-\dfrac{\log_{q}\mathrm{Nr}^{S}(\mathfrak{A}_{n})}{\Theta^{n}}\right).

By Proposition
2.4
(iii), the expression
4.2
is equal to

(4.3)

\left(\hat{h}(x)-\dfrac{h(\phi_{T^{n}}(x))}{\Theta^{n}}\right)+\dfrac{1}{\Theta^{n}}\sum_{v\in S}\mathrm{log}_{q}\ \mathrm{max}\{|\phi_{T^{n}}(x)^{-1}|_{v},1\}.

Adding and subtracting
\dfrac{1}{\Theta^{n}}\hat{h}(\phi_{T^{n}}(x))
we have that expression
4.3
is equal to

(4.4)

\dfrac{1}{\Theta^{n}}\left(\left(\Theta^{n}\hat{h}(x)-\hat{h}(\phi_{T^{n}}(x))\right)+\left(\hat{h}(\phi_{T^{n}}(x))-h(\phi_{T^{n}}(x))\right)\right)+\dfrac{1}{\Theta^{n}}\sum_{v\in S}\mathrm{log}_{q}\ \mathrm{max}\{|\phi_{T^{n}}(x)^{-1}|_{v},1\}.

Now, by Proposition
2.3
(ii), we have that the expression
4.4
is equal to

\dfrac{1}{\Theta^{n}}\left(\hat{h}(\phi_{T^{n}}(x))-h(\phi_{T^{n}}(x))\right)+\dfrac{1}{\Theta^{n}}\sum_{v\in S}\mathrm{log}_{q}\ \mathrm{max}\{|\phi_{T^{n}}(x)^{-1}|_{v},1\},

and by Lemma
2.7
, we conclude

\hat{h}(x)-\dfrac{\log_{q}\mathrm{Nr}^{S}(\mathfrak{A}_{n})}{\Theta^{n}}\leq\dfrac{M^{\prime}_{\phi}}{\Theta^{n}}+\dfrac{1}{\Theta^{n}}\sum_{v\in S}\mathrm{log}_{q}\ \mathrm{max}\{|\phi_{T^{n}}(x)^{-1}|_{v},1\}.

∎

Remark 4.3
.

Later, we will use Lemma
4.1
to control the term

\frac{1}{\Theta^{n}}\sum_{v\in S}\mathrm{log}_{q}\ \mathrm{max}\{|\phi_{T^{n}}(x)^{-1}|_{v},1\}.

4.2.
Bound for
\hat{h}(x)

In
[
2
]
, Ghioca studies an analogous of Lehmer’s conjecture for canonical height coming from a Drinfeld module. The following theorem is a particular case of Theorem 4.5 in
[
2
]
.

Theorem 4.4

(Ghioca
[
2
]
)
.

Let
\phi\colon\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]\to\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)}(\mathbb{G}_{a})
be a Drinfeld module of rank
2
such that
\phi_{T}(x)=Tx+gx^{q}+\Delta x^{q^{2}}
. If
\alpha\notin\phi_{\mathrm{Tor}}
, then

\hat{h}_{\phi}(\alpha)>q^{-6-12(q^{2}-1)|S|}.

Proof.

Let
\gamma\in\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)}
, such that
\gamma^{q^{2}-1}=\Delta.
Then
\phi_{T}^{(\gamma)}(x)
is a monic polynomial in
x
with coefficients in
\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)(\gamma)
. By
[
3
]
and Lemma
2.3
(i), we have
\hat{h}_{\phi}(x)=\hat{h}_{\phi^{(\gamma)}}(\gamma^{-1}x).
So, we need to bound the height
\hat{h}_{\phi^{(\gamma)}}
. As we have said,
\phi^{(\gamma)}_{T}(x)
is a monic polynomial, then we can use the bounds obtained in Theorem 4.4 in
[
2
]
. We have to know the size of places in
\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)(\gamma)
such that
\phi^{(\gamma)}
has bad reduction. We observe that a place in
\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)(\gamma)
of bad reduction of
\phi^{(\gamma)}
is always over a place of
S
. We denote by
S_{\phi^{(\gamma)}}
the set of bad places of
\phi^{(\gamma)}.
Since
[\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)(\gamma^{-1}x):\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)]\leq(q^{2}-1)
, we obtain
|S_{\phi^{(\gamma)}}|\leq(q^{2}-1)|S|
.

Then, applying the bound in Theorem 4.4 from
[
2
]
, we obtain

\hat{h}_{\phi}(x)=\hat{h}_{\phi^{(\gamma)}}(\gamma^{-1}x)>\dfrac{q^{-4-12\left|S_{\phi^{(\gamma)}}\right|}}{[\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)(\gamma^{-1}x):\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)]}.

In this way, we conclude

\hat{h}_{\phi}(x)>q^{-6-12(q^{2}-1)|S|}.

∎

5.
Proof of the main theorem

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\phi\colon\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]\to\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)}(\mathbb{G}_{a})
be a Drinfeld module of rank
2
given by

\phi_{T}(x)=Tx+gx^{q}+\Delta x^{q^{2}},

such that
\Delta\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
split completely over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
,
g\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
and
\phi_{T}(1)
is coprime to
T\Delta
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[T].
If
x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
is not a torsion point of
\phi
, then

\max\mathcal{Z}(\phi,x)\leq\left(6(q^{2}-1)|S|+4\right)+\dfrac{1}{2}\log_{q}\left(2+2\deg g+2\deg\Delta\right).

Proof.

First, we need to find the minimum
n_{0}
such that equation
3.1
holds. Using Lemma
4.2
, we see that it is enough take
n_{0}
as the minimum
n\in\mathbb{N}
satisfying the following inequality

\dfrac{M_{\phi}^{\prime}}{\Theta^{n}}\leq\dfrac{1}{2\Theta}\hat{h}(x).

From this expression, we obtain that inequality
3.1
holds if

(5.1)

n\geq\dfrac{1}{2}\left(\log_{q}(2M^{\prime}_{\phi})-\log_{q}\hat{h}(x)\right)+1.

On the other hand, to ensure that
\log_{q}\left(\frac{\mathrm{Nr}^{S}(\mathfrak{A}_{n})}{\mathrm{Nr}^{S}(\mathfrak{A}_{n-1})}\right)>0
, by equation
3.1
, we need

\left(\Theta-\dfrac{3}{2}\right)\Theta^{n-1}\hat{h}(x)>M_{\phi}.

Applying logarithms and dividing by
\log_{q}\Theta
, we obtain

n-1>\dfrac{1}{\log_{q}\Theta}\left(\log_{q}M_{\phi}-\log_{q}\hat{h}(x)-\log_{q}\left(\Theta-\frac{3}{2}\right)\right).

Recalling
\Theta=q^{r}
, where
r
is the rank of the corresponding Drinfeld module (in our case,
r=2
), we have
\log_{q}(\Theta)=2
.
Then, it is enough that

(5.2)

n>\dfrac{1}{2}\left(\log_{q}M_{\phi}-\log_{q}\hat{h}(x)-\log_{q}\left(\Theta-\frac{3}{2}\right)\right)+1.

Using the bounds obtained in subsections
2.3
and
4.4
:

M_{\phi}\leq\dfrac{1+\deg g}{(q-1)^{2}},M_{\phi}^{\prime}\leq\dfrac{2(\deg\Delta+\deg g+1)}{(q-1)^{2}},\text{ and }-\log_{q}(\hat{h}(x))\leq 12(q^{2}-1)|S|+6,

we can conclude that inequality
5.2
holds if

n\geq(6(q^{2}-1)|S|+4)+\dfrac{1}{2}\log_{q}\left(\dfrac{2(\deg\Delta+\deg g+1)}{(q-1)^{2}}\right),

and that inequality
5.1
holds if

n\geq(6(q^{2}-1)|S|+4)+\dfrac{1}{2}\log_{q}\left(\dfrac{1+\deg g}{(q-1)^{2}}\right).

Finally, we note that a sufficient condition on
n
in order to
5.2
and
5.1
hold is

n\geq(6(q^{2}-1)|S|+4)+\dfrac{1}{2}\log_{q}\left(2+2\deg g+2\deg\Delta\right).

We conclude that
\max\mathcal{Z}(\phi,x)\leq(6(q^{2}-1)|S|+4)+\dfrac{1}{2}\log_{q}\left(2+2\deg g+2\deg\Delta\right)
under the hypothesis of Lemma
5.1
, which concludes the proof.

∎

Now, noting that Lemma
5.1
is the particular case of Theorem
1.1
when
N=1
, we use it to prove the main theorem.

Proof.

(of Theorem
1.1
)
Let
\psi
be an auxiliary Drinfeld module
\psi\colon\mathbb{F}_{q^{N}}[T]\to\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{N}}(T)}(\mathbb{G}_{a})
given by
\psi_{T}(x)=\phi_{T}(x)
. The advantage in this case is that
\Delta
split completely over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{N}}
.
Let us recall the definition of the sets
\mathcal{Z}(\phi,x)
and
\mathcal{Z}(\psi,x),

\mathcal{Z}(\phi,x)=\left\{n\in\mathbb{N}:\mathfrak{A}_{n}\text{ does not have primitive divisors in }\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)\right\}.

\mathcal{Z}(\psi,x)=\left\{n\in\mathbb{N}:\mathfrak{A}_{n}\text{ does not have primitive divisors in }\mathbb{F}_{q^{N}}(T)\right\}.

We can apply Lemma
5.1
and the fact that the set
S^{\prime}
of places in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{N}}(T)
associated with the Drinfeld module
\psi
has cardinality bounded by
N|S|
. Therefore,

\mathcal{Z}(\psi,x)
satisfies the bound of the theorem.
Now we only need to prove
\mathcal{Z}(\phi,x)=\mathcal{Z}(\psi,x)
for all
x
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
be an element in
\mathcal{Z}(\phi,x)
. Then, by definition of
\mathcal{Z}(\phi,x)
,
\mathfrak{A}_{n}
does not have primitive divisors in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
. Consequently, it is sufficient to prove
\mathfrak{A}_{n}
does not have primitive divisors in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{N}}[T]
. If
v
is a place in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{N}}(T)
such that
v|\mathfrak{A}_{n}
, then there is a place
w
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)
below
v
. Since
\mathfrak{A}_{n}
belongs to
\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
,
w|\mathfrak{A}_{n}.
But
n\in\mathcal{Z}(\phi,x)
, then there exists
m<n
such that
w|\mathfrak{A}_{m}.
Since
v
is above
w
,
v|\mathfrak{A}_{m}.
So, we conclude that
v
is not a primitive divisor of
\mathfrak{A}_{n}
, therefore,

\mathcal{Z}(\phi,x)\subset\mathcal{Z}(\psi,x).

On the other hand, if
n\in\mathcal{Z}(\psi,x)
, then
\mathfrak{A}_{n}
does not have primitive divisors in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{N}}[T]
. When
v|\mathfrak{A}
, there exists
m<n
such that
v|\mathfrak{A}_{m}
, since
\mathfrak{A}_{m}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
. Then the place
w
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
below
v
, divide
\mathfrak{A}_{m}
. In this way, we conclude
n\in\mathcal{Z}(\phi,x)
, and therefore
\mathcal{Z}(\phi,x)=\mathcal{Z}(\psi,x).

∎

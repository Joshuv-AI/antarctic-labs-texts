Introduction

This short note arose in parallel with the preparation of a new edition of our lecture notes
“One-parameter Semigroups of Positive Operators”

[
1
]
, originally published by Springer in 1986.
Chapters D-I through D-IV, written in 1983, contain foundational results on the spectrum of generators of positive
C_{0}
-semigroups on C
⋆
-algebras and the preduals of W
⋆
-algebras.
Here we provide a more detailed treatment of the
“
\mathrm{s}(A)=\omega_{0}
”
problem, mentioned in the chapters but not extensively discussed.

One can clarify this problem using the theory of ordered Banach spaces:
By
[
5
, Theorem 5.3.1]
one has
s(A)\in\sigma(A)
whenever
A
generates a positive semigroup on an ordered Banach space with normal cone and if the spectrum of
A
is non-empty.
Moreover,
s(A)=\omega_{0}
if the norm is additive on the dual positive cone, see
[
2
, Theorem 2.4.4]
.

C
⋆
-algebras enjoy both properties.
However, it is helpful to provide a summary in the context of C
⋆
-algebras and preduals of W
⋆
-algebras, as this allows for a more direct treatment of this topic.

What is the question?

If
(T(t))_{t\geqslant 0}
is a strongly continuous operator semigroup on a Banach space, or, for short, a
C_{0}
-semigroup, with generator
A
, then we are interested in the relationship between properties of the spectrum
\sigma(A)
of the generator and the convergence behavior of the semigroup
(T(t))_{t\geqslant 0}
(see
[
1
, A-IV & B-IV]
).

To this end, two essential definitions are required (see
[
1
, Chap. A-III, 1. Introduction (1.2)–(1.3) ]
).

(1)

First, the
spectral bound

\mathrm{s}(A)
, i.e.,

\mathrm{s}(A)=\sup\{\Re(\lambda)\colon\lambda\in\sigma(A)\}

where
\Re(\cdot)
denotes the real part of a complex number.
Then we always have

-\infty\leqslant\mathrm{s}(A)<\infty

with
-\infty=\mathrm{s}(A)
when the spectrum of
A
is empty.

(2)

The other quantity is the
growth bound

\omega_{0}
which yields

r(T(t))=\mathrm{e}^{\omega_{0}t}\quad(t\geqslant 0).

where
r(T(t))
denotes the spectral radius of the operator
T(t)
.

Therefore, for
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
with
\Re(\lambda)>\omega_{0}
, the resolvent
\mathcal{R}(\lambda,A)
of
A
exists, is given by

\mathcal{R}(\lambda,A)x=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda s}T(s)x\mathop{}\mathrm{d}{s}

and

-\infty\leqslant\mathrm{s}(A)\leqslant\omega_{0}<\infty,

where all variations are possible (for this see Remarks
3.7
on page
3.7
).
In the case that
\mathrm{s}(A)=\omega_{0}
, we say that the spectrum of
A
determines the asymptotic behavior of the semigroup
(T(t))_{t\geqslant 0}
.
In general, one cannot expect this equality to hold (see
[
1
, A-III, Example 1.3]
) not even for positive
C_{0}
-semigroups.

2.
Positive Operators

If
T
is an operator on a Banach space, we denote by
\sigma(T)
its spectrum, by
r(T)
the spectral radius and by
\rho(T)
its resolvent set.
If
\lambda\in\rho(T)
with
\lvert\lambda\rvert>r(T)
then its resolvent
\mathcal{R}(\lambda,T)
is given by

\mathcal{R}(\lambda,T)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\lambda^{-(n+1)}T^{n}

where the sum exists in the operator norm.
For these and further properties of the spectrum see
[
3
, Chap. VII]
.
In this section we show the fundamental property of the spectrum of positive operators on C
⋆
-algebras needed later, i.e.,
r(T)\in\sigma(T)
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
T
be a positive operator on a C
⋆
-algebra
\mathfrak{A}
, let
\lambda\in\rho(T)
such that
\lvert\lambda\rvert>r(T)
.
Then for all
0\leqslant\varphi\in\mathfrak{A}^{*}
and
0\leqslant x\in\mathfrak{A}
, we have

(1)

\left\lvert\varphi(\mathcal{R}(\lambda,T)x)\right\rvert\leqslant\varphi(\mathcal{R}(\lvert\lambda\rvert,T)x).

Proof.

Using representation of the resolvent
\mathcal{R}(\lambda,T)
of
T
we obtain

\begin{split}\left\lvert\varphi(\mathcal{R}(\lambda,T)x)\right\rvert&=\left\lvert\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\,\lambda^{-(k+1)}\varphi(T^{k}x)\right\rvert\\
\leqslant\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left\lvert\lambda\right\rvert^{-(k+1)}\varphi(T^{k}x)&=\varphi\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\,\lvert\lambda\rvert^{-(k+1)}\,T^{k}x)\right)=\varphi(\mathcal{R}(\lvert\lambda\rvert,T)x).\end{split}

x

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
T
be a positive operator on a C
⋆
-algebra
\mathfrak{A}
.

(1)

The spectral radius
r(T)
is an element of the spectrum
\sigma(T)
of
T
.

(2)

If
\mathfrak{A}
contains a unit
\mathbbm{1}
, then there exists a

0\leqslant\varphi\in\mathfrak{A}^{*}
,
\lVert\varphi\rVert=1

such that
T^{\prime}\varphi=r(T)\varphi
where
T^{\prime}
denotes the adjoint operator of
T
on the dual space
\mathfrak{A}^{*}
of
\mathfrak{A}
.

Proof.

(i) Take
\alpha\in\sigma(T)
with
\lvert\alpha\rvert=r(T)
and a sequence
(\lambda_{n})
in the resolvent set
\rho(T)
such that
\lvert\lambda_{n}\rvert\downarrow r(T)
and
\lim_{n}\lambda_{n}=\alpha
.
Then, since
\alpha
is a singularity of the resolvent of
T
, we conclude

\limsup_{n}\left\lVert\mathcal{R}(\lambda_{n},T)\right\rVert=\infty.

Since the positive cones of
\mathfrak{A}
and
\mathfrak{A}{*}
are generating, there exists, by the uniform boundedness principle,
0\leqslant\varphi\in\mathfrak{A}^{*}
and
0\leqslant x\in\mathfrak{A}
, both of norm
\leqslant 1
such that

\limsup_{n}\left\lvert\varphi(\mathcal{R}(\lambda_{n},T)x)\right\rvert=\infty,

and by Equation (
1
),

\limsup_{n}\left\lVert\mathcal{R}(\lvert\lambda_{n}\rvert,T))\right\rVert=\infty.

This proves
r(T)\in\sigma(T)
.

(ii) Denote
r\coloneqq r(T)
and let
(\lambda_{n})
be a real sequence
\lambda_{n}\downarrow r
in
\rho(T)
in such that

\limsup_{n}\left\lVert\mathcal{R}(\lambda_{n},T)\right\rVert=\infty.

Again by the uniform boundedness principle and because the positive cone of
\mathfrak{A}^{*}
is generating, there exists a positive linear form
\varphi
on
\mathfrak{A}
such that

\limsup_{n}\lVert\mathcal{R}(\lambda_{n},T^{\prime})\varphi\rVert=\infty.

Since the resolvents
\mathcal{R}(\lambda_{n},T^{\prime})=\mathcal{R}(\lambda_{n},T)^{\prime}
are positive operators on
\mathfrak{A}^{*}
, the linear forms

\varphi_{n}=\lVert\mathcal{R}(\lambda_{n},T^{\prime})\varphi\rVert^{-1}\,\mathcal{R}(\lambda_{n},T^{\prime})\varphi\,,

are positive with
\lVert\varphi_{n}\rVert=\varphi_{n}(\mathbbm{1})=1
, hence are elements of the state space
\mathbf{S}(\mathfrak{A})
of
\mathfrak{A}
.

Since the state space
\mathbf{S}(\mathfrak{A})
is compact for the
\sigma(\mathfrak{A}^{*},\mathfrak{A})
-topology, this sequence has an accumulation point
\varphi_{0}
with
\varphi_{0}(\mathbbm{1})=1
.
By compactness we find an ultrafilter
\mathfrak{U}
on
\mathbb{N}
such that

\lim_{\mathfrak{U}}\varphi_{n}=\varphi_{0}.

Since

(2)

\begin{split}(r-T^{\prime})\varphi_{n}&=\lVert\mathcal{R}(\lambda_{n},T^{\prime})\varphi\rVert^{-1}[(r-\lambda_{n})+(\lambda_{n}-T)]\mathcal{R}(\lambda_{n},T^{\prime})\varphi\\
&=(r-\lambda_{n})\varphi_{n}+\lVert\mathcal{R}(\lambda_{n},T^{\prime})\varphi\rVert^{-1}\varphi\end{split}

and since the adjoint operator
T^{\prime}
is continuous for the
\sigma(\mathfrak{A}^{*},\mathfrak{A})
-topology on
\mathfrak{A}^{*}
, we obtain from Equation (
2
) above that

(r-T^{\prime})\varphi_{0}=\lim_{\mathfrak{U}}((r-T^{\prime})\varphi_{n})=0.

Therefore
T^{\prime}\varphi_{0}=r(T)\varphi_{0}
with
\varphi_{0}\in\mathbf{S}(\mathfrak{A})
.
∎

Corollary 2.3
.

If
r(T)
is a pole of the resolvent, then its order is maximal among the poles in the peripheral spectrum

\{\alpha\in\sigma(T)\colon\lvert\alpha\rvert=r(T)\}
of
T
.

Proof.

Let
r=r(T)
,
\alpha
in the peripheral spectrum of
T
be a pole of the resolvent of order
k
and let
\lambda=\mu\alpha
where
\mu>r
.
Then for
0\leqslant\varphi\in\mathfrak{A}^{*}
,
0\leqslant x\in\mathfrak{A}
, both of norm
\leqslant 1
, and
m>k
we obtain by Equation (
1
) on page
1

\left\lvert(\lambda-\alpha)^{m}\varphi((\mathcal{R}(\lambda,T)x)\right\rvert\leqslant(\mu-r)^{m}\lVert\mathcal{R}(\mu,T)\rVert\,.

Again by the uniform boundedness principle and the generating properties of the positive cones in
\mathfrak{A}
and

is maximal among the poles in the peripheral spectrum.

3.
Positive Semigroups

This section establishes the integral representation of the resolvent of the generator
A
of a positive
C_{0}
-semigroup on both C
⋆
-algebras and the preduals of W
⋆
-algebras.
We extend the standard integral representation, valid for
\Re(\lambda)>\omega_{0}
, to the larger domain
\Re(\lambda)>\mathrm{s}(A)
.
The main result is a unified theorem with a single proof that covers both settings.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
E
be a C
⋆
-algebra or the predual of a W
⋆
-algebra and let
A
be the generator of a positive
C_{0}
-semigroup
(T(t))_{t\geqslant 0}
on
E
.
Then for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
,
\Re(\lambda)>\mathrm{s}(A)
and
x\in E
we have

(3)

\mathcal{R}(\lambda,A)x=\lim_{t\to\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda s}T(s)x\mathop{}\mathrm{d}{s}.

Proof.

For
\lambda_{0}>\omega_{0}
,
\mathrm{s}(A)<\mu<\lambda_{0}
,
0\leqslant x\in E
and
0\leqslant x^{\prime}\in E^{\prime}
.
As in the proof of
[
1
, C-III, Theorem 1.2]
, we obtain

(4)

\langle\mathcal{R}(\mu,A)x,x^{\prime}\rangle=\lim_{t\to\infty}\langle\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{-\mu s}T(s)x\mathop{}\mathrm{d}{s},x^{\prime}\rangle.

For
t\geqslant 0
let
f_{t}
and
f
be defined on the
\sigma(E^{\prime},E)
-compact set

K:=\{0\leqslant x^{\prime}\colon x^{\prime}\in E^{\prime},\lVert x^{\prime}\rVert\leqslant 1\}

by

f_{t}\colon x^{\prime}\mapsto\langle\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{-\mu s}T(s)x\mathop{}\mathrm{d}{s},x^{\prime}\rangle\quad\text{and}\quad f\colon x^{\prime}\mapsto\langle\mathcal{R}(\mu,A)x,x^{\prime}\rangle.

Then these functions are elements of
C(K)
.
Thus the net
(f_{t})_{t}
is pointwise convergent to
f
and monotone increasing, hence it is uniformly convergent in
C(K)
by Dini’s theorem.
Since every
x^{\prime}\in E^{\prime}
is a linear combination of at most four positive elements of
E^{\prime}
, we have

(5)

\mathcal{R}(\mu,A)x=\lim_{t\to\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{-\mu s}T(s)x\mathop{}\mathrm{d}{s}.

in the norm of
E
.

If
x\in E
and
x^{\prime}\in E^{\prime}
, then there exist positive
x_{j}\in E
(respectively
positive
x^{\prime}_{j}\in E^{\prime}
) for
j\in\{0,\ldots,3\}
such that

x=\sum_{j=0}^{3}\mathrm{i}^{j}x_{j}\quad\text{resp.}\quad x^{\prime}=\sum_{j=0}^{3}\mathrm{i}^{j}x^{\prime}_{j}

We define the positive elements
\lvert x\rvert
and
\lvert x^{\prime}\rvert
by

\lvert x\rvert=\sum_{j=0}^{3}x_{j}\quad\text{resp.}\quad\lvert x^{\prime}\rvert=\sum_{j=0}^{3}x^{\prime}_{j}

and remark, that

\lvert\langle T(s)x,x^{\prime}\rangle\rvert\leqslant\langle T(s)\lvert x\rvert,\lvert x^{\prime}\rvert\rangle

for
s\geqslant 0
.

Now we argue as in the second part of the proof of
[
1
, C-III, Theorem 1.2]
.
Take
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
such that
\Re(\lambda)>\mathrm{s}(A)
, then
\lambda=\mu+\mathrm{i}\gamma
with
\mathrm{s}(A)<\mu
and
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
.

If
x\in E
,
x^{\prime}\in E^{\prime}
and
0\leqslant r<t<\infty
, then

(6)

\begin{split}\left\lvert\left\langle\int_{r}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda s}T(s)x\mathop{}\mathrm{d}{s},x^{\prime}\right\rangle\right\rvert&\leqslant\int_{r}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{-\Re(\lambda)s}\lvert\langle T(s)x\mathop{}\mathrm{d}{s},x^{\prime}\rangle\rvert\\
&\leqslant\int_{r}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{-\mu s}\langle T(s)\lvert x\rvert\mathop{}\mathrm{d}{s},\lvert x^{\prime}\rvert\rangle.\end{split}

But there exists
m>0
such that
\lVert\,\lvert x^{\prime}\rvert\,\rVert\leqslant m
for every
x^{\prime}\in E^{\prime}
with
\lVert x^{\prime}\rVert\leqslant 1
.
Therefore

(7)

\begin{split}\left\lVert\int_{r}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda s}T(s)x\mathop{}\mathrm{d}{s}\right\rVert&=\sup_{\lVert x^{\prime}\rVert\leqslant 1}\,\left\lvert\left\langle\int_{r}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{-\Re(\lambda)s}T(s)x\mathop{}\mathrm{d}{s},x^{\prime}\right\rangle\right\rvert\\
&\leqslant m\,\left\lVert\int_{r}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{-\mu s}T(s)\lvert x\rvert\mathop{}\mathrm{d}{s}\right\rVert\end{split}

which shows that for every
x\in E

\lim_{t\to\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda s}T(s)x\mathop{}\mathrm{d}{s}

exits and

\mathcal{R}(\lambda,A)x=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda s}T(s)x\mathop{}\mathrm{d}{s}

by
[
1
, A-I, Prop. 1.11]
.
∎

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
(T(t))_{t\geqslant 0}
be a
C_{0}
-semigroup of positive operators on a C
⋆
-algebra
\mathfrak{A}
.

(1)

Then
\mathrm{s}(A)=\omega_{0}

(2)

If
\sigma(A)\neq\emptyset
, i.e.,

\mathrm{s}(A)\in\sigma(A)

(3)

If
\mathfrak{A}

\sigma(A)\neq\emptyset

\varphi\in\mathfrak{A}^{*}

A^{\prime}\varphi=\mathrm{s}(A)\varphi

(Proof of (i)) By rescaling we take
\omega_{0}=0

\mathrm{s}(A)<0

If
\varphi\in\mathfrak{A}^{*}

Furthermore,

Next let

\begin{split}\left\langle\int_{0}^{t}T(s)^{\prime}\varphi\mathop{}\mathrm{d}{s},x\right\rangle&=\varphi(\int_{0}^{t}T(s)x\mathop{}\mathrm{d}{s})\\
\leqslant\varphi(\mathcal{R}(0,A)x\mathop{}\mathrm{d}{s})&=\langle\mathcal{R}(0,A)^{\prime}\varphi,x\rangle\end{split}

for some

C_{0}
-semigroups on
\mathfrak{A}
and suppose
\sigma(A)=\emptyset
.
Then
\mathrm{s}(A)=\mathrm{s}(-A)=-\infty
and the set

\{\mathcal{R}(\lambda,A)\colon\Re\lambda\geqslant 0\}\quad\text{and}\quad\{\mathcal{R}(\lambda,-A)\colon\Re\lambda\geqslant 0\}

is bounded in
\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{A})
using Equation (
8
), i.e., the set
\{\mathcal{R}(\lambda,A)\colon\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\}
is bounded.
But then the function
\lambda\to\mathcal{R}(\lambda,A)
is constant by Liouville’s theorem, hence identically zero because
\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\mathcal{R}(\lambda,A)=0
.
But this is not possible, hence
\sigma(A)\neq\emptyset
and the claim follows.
∎

For the final proposition we use the fact that every W
⋆
-algebra has a unit (
[
8
, Prop. 1.6.1]
).

Proposition 3.6
.

(1)

If
\mathfrak{A}
is a W
⋆
-algebra with predual
\mathfrak{A}_{*}
and
(T(t))_{t\geqslant 0}
a positive
C_{0}
-semigroup on
\mathfrak{A}_{*}
, then
\mathrm{s}(A)=\omega_{0}
.

(2)

If
\mathfrak{A}
is a C
⋆
-algebra and
(T(t))_{t\geqslant 0}
a positive
C_{0}
-semigroup on the dual
\mathfrak{A}^{*}
of
\mathfrak{A}
, then
\mathrm{s}(A)=\omega_{0}
.

Proof.

(1)

By rescaling we may assume
\omega_{0}=0
and suppose
\mathrm{s}(A)<0
.
Then
\mathcal{R}(0,A)
is a positive operator and by Theorem
3.1
given by

R(0,A)\varphi=\int_{0}^{\infty}T(s)\varphi\mathop{}\mathrm{d}{s}.

If
\varphi
is positive, then

\lVert R(0,A)\varphi\rVert=\langle R(0,A)\varphi,\mathbbm{1}\rangle=\langle\int_{0}^{\infty}T(s)\varphi\mathop{}\mathrm{d}{s},\mathbbm{1}\rangle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\lVert T(s)\varphi\rVert\mathop{}\mathrm{d}{s}.

Again, this implies
\int_{0}^{\infty}\lVert T(s)\varphi\rVert\mathop{}\mathrm{d}{s}<\infty
for every
\varphi\in\mathfrak{A}_{*}
.
From the Theorem of Datko-Pazy we obtain

\lim_{t\to\infty}\|T(t)\|=0

hence
\omega_{0}<0
which implies
\mathrm{s}(A)=\omega_{0}
.

(2)

Since the bidual of a C
⋆
-algebra is a W
⋆
-algebra, the claim follows immediately.

∎

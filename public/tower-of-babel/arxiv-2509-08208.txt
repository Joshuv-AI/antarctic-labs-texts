Virtual Poincaré polynomial of moduli space of semistable sheaves of rank two on reducible curves Thanks: SH is supported by the National Research Foundation of Korea(NRF) grant funded by the Korea government(MSIT) (No. RS-2023-00208874).
Sukmoon Huh, Dongsun Lim and Sang-Bum Yoo

Abstract
. The main purpose of this paper is to give an explicit description of the moduli space of semistable sheaves of rank two on a stable curve C C obtained by gluing two smooth curves at a point. We prove that the moduli space is irreducible and birational to a projective bundle over the moduli space of stable vector bundles on each component curve, independently of the choice of polarization. As an application, we compute the virtual Poincaré polynomial of the moduli space.

1.
Introduction

The classification of semistable sheaves on singular or reducible curves is a central problem in moduli theory. It aims to understand how vector bundles degenerate and how the associated moduli spaces reflect the geometry of the underlying curve. In the case of nodal curves, especially those that are reducible, the classical definition of (semi)stability must be refined by introducing a polarization. In such a setting, the structure of the moduli space is determined by the restrictions of sheaves to each irreducible component and by the gluing conditions at the nodes.

A foundational construction in the study of semistable sheaves on singular curves was given by D. S. Nagaraj and C. S. Seshadri in
[
12
,
18
]
. They extended the classical moduli theory to nodal curves by constructing moduli spaces of torsion-free sheaves via geometric invariant theory. Moreover, further works by L. Caporaso, C. Simpson, and others generalized this construction to include compactified Picard schemes and moduli of coherent sheaves with suitable depth conditions; see
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
19
]
.

In
[
20
,
21
]
, M. Teixidor i Bigas further analyzed the moduli of semistable sheaves on tree-like curves and showed that for a generic polarization, the moduli space may decompose into several irreducible components, each determined by a distinct multi-degree. This illustrates the intricate structure of the moduli space and its dependence on the chosen polarization. Within this framework, S. Brivio and F. F. Favale studied the moduli space of rank two semistable sheaves on a reducible nodal curve consisting of two smooth components intersecting transversely at a node in
[
1
]
. For a suitable choice of polarization, they proved that the moduli space admits a birational description as a projective bundle over the product of the moduli spaces of semistable rank two vector bundles on each component. Moreover, they demonstrated that this space arises as a degeneration of the moduli space defined over a smooth curve, illustrating how the geometry of the singular curve is reflected in the structure of the associated moduli space.

In this article, we study the moduli space
\mathbf{U}_{C}(\mathbf{w},2,0)
of
S_{\mathbf{w}}
-equivalence classes of
\mathbf{w}
-semistable sheaves of depth one on a reducible curve
C=C_{1}\cup C_{2}
with rank two and Euler characteristic zero, where
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
are smooth projective curves of genus
g_{1},g_{2}\geq 1
, meeting transversely at a node
p\in C
. For a coherent sheaf
\cal{E}
on
C
with depth one, one can consider a sheaf
\cal{E}_{i}
defined as the restriction of
\cal{E}
to
C_{i}
modulo torsion, from which one can consider the multi-rank and multi-characteristic of
\cal{E}
. In particular, S. Brivio and F. F. Favale
[
1
]
focused on sheaves with coprime rank and degree, restricting to pure rank cases. In contrast, we describe sheaves with multi-rank, including non-coprime cases. First we investigate the classification of such sheaves with lower multi-ranks and possible multi-characteristics; see Propositions
3.1
,
3.2
, and
3.3
. This gives a building block for the Jordan-Hölder filtrations of the strictly
\mathbf{w}
-semistable sheaf

in
\mathbf{U}_{C}(\mathbf{w},2,0)
. Indeed, as shown in Propositions
3.5

3.7
, the loci of strictly
\mathbf{w}
-semistable sheaves form a single component in

.

To describe this moduli space more concretely, we show that for any polarization
\mathbf{w}
, the space
\mathbf{U}_{C}(\mathbf{w},2,0)
is irreducible and admits an explicit description obtained from a birational morphism from a projective bundle over
\mathbf{M}_{C_{1}}(2,2g_{1}-1)\times\mathbf{M}_{C_{2}}(2,2g_{2}-1)

\Phi:\quad\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{E}){\mathrel{\xrightarrow{\ \ }}^{\hskip 28.45274pt}}\mathbf{U}_{C}(\mathbf{w},2,0).

As proven in Proposition
3.11

\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{E})
parametrize the classes of
\mathbf{w}
-semistable sheaves. In fact, this morphism is birational.

Theorem 1.1
.

The map
\Phi:\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{E})\rightarrow\mathbf{U}_{C}(\mathbf{w},2,0)
is a birational morphism, which is an isomorphism if and only if
g=2
.

The strictly
\mathbf{w}
-semistable locus
\mathbf{Q}
in
\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{E})
is characterized by rank-one linear maps between the fibres and defines a divisor that is irreducible and of codimension one. When each
C_{i}
is an elliptic curve, this divisor admits the structure of a
(\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1})
-fibration over the base
\mathbf{M}_{C_{1}}(2,1)\times\mathbf{M}_{C_{2}}(2,1)

Theorem 1.2
.

The virtual Poincaré polynomial of
\mathbf{U}_{C}(\mathbf{w},2,0)
can be expressed as

\mathrm{P}(\mathbf{U}_{C}(\mathbf{w},2,0))=(1+t)^{2g}\Big((1+t)^{2g-4}S_{g_{1}}(t)\,S_{g_{2}}(t)\,(-t^{2}+t^{6})+U_{g_{1}}(t)\,U_{g_{2}}(t)\Big),

where
S_{g}(t),U_{g}(t)\in\mathbb{Z}[t]
are given by

\displaystyle S_{g}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{(1-t+t^{2})^{2g}-t^{2g}}{(1-t)^{2}(1+t^{2})},

\displaystyle\displaystyle U_{g}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{2(1+t)^{2g-2}\big((1-t+t^{2})^{2g}-t^{2g+2}\big)-t^{2}\big((1+t)^{2g-2}(1-t^{4})-(1-t)^{2g+2}\big)}{2(1-t)^{2}(1+t^{2})}.

Motivated by the classical description of vector bundle moduli on smooth curves, one can interpret the moduli space
\mathbf{U}_{C}(\mathbf{w},2,0)
through a degeneration process. Based on the foundational work of M. S. Narasimhan and S. Ramanan, who described in
[
13
]
the moduli space
\mathbf{M}_{C^{\prime}}(2,0)
of semistable vector bundles of rank two and degree zero on a smooth projective curve
C^{\prime}
of genus two, we consider a degeneration of
C^{\prime}
to a reducible nodal curve
C=C_{1}\cup C_{2}
. In this setting, the moduli space
\mathbf{U}_{C}(\mathbf{w},2,0)
arises naturally as a degeneration of
\mathbf{M}_{C^{\prime}}(2,0)
that is isomorphic to a
\mathbb{P}^{3}
-bundle over
\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(C^{\prime})
. Note that we have a degeneration of
\mathrm{Pic}^{0}(C^{\prime})
to
\mathrm{Pic}^{0}(C)\cong\mathrm{Pic}^{0}(C_{1})\times\mathrm{Pic}^{0}(C_{2})
; see
[
11
]
. The comparison between the two moduli spaces
\mathbf{U}_{C}(\mathbf{w},2,0)
and
\mathbf{M}_{C^{\prime}}(2,0)
via their virtual Poincaré polynomials is given in Corollary
4.6
.

On the other hand, M. Teixidor i Bigas showed that the moduli space may have more than one irreducible component for general polarization; see
[
20
,
21
]
. It could be worthy to point out that this is not the case for any polarization in our setting; indeed,
\mathbf{U}_{C}(\mathbf{w},2,0)
is irreducible for any
\mathbf{w}
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we recall the definition of
\mathbf{w}
-stability and basic properties of depth-one sheaves on reducible curves. In Section 3, we describe and classify all semistable sheaves in

\mathbf{U}_{C}(\mathbf{w},2,0)

2.
Preliminaries

Let
C
be a stable curve of arithmetic genus
g\geq 2
over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero. Then
C
is a complete, connected curve with at worst nodal singularities and only finitely many automorphisms. Write
C=C_{1}\cup\dots\cup C_{m}
with each
C_{i}
is an irreducible component of
C
with genus
g_{i}\geq 1
. The set of singular points
\mbox{Sing}(C)
can be written as a disjoint union of two subsets:

\mbox{Sing}(C)=\mbox{Sing}_{1}(C)\sqcup\mbox{Sing}_{2}(C),

where
\mbox{Sing}_{j}(C)
is the set of nodes of
C
lying exactly on
j
irreducible components of
C
. Following the approach of C. S. Seshadri in
[
18
]
, we consider depth-one sheaves to describe the moduli space of sheaves on
C
. For a coherent sheaf
\cal{E}
on
C
with
depth one
, meaning that
\dim\mathrm{Supp}(\cal{F})=1
for any subsheaf
\cal{F}\subset\cal{E}
, we define the
multi-rank
and
multi-degree
of
\cal{E}
by

\mathrm{rank}(\cal{E})=\vec{r}=(r_{1},\dots,r_{m})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{\oplus m},\quad\mathrm{deg}(\cal{E})=\vec{d}=(d_{1},\dots,d_{m})\in\mathbb{Z}^{\oplus m},

where
r_{i}:=\mathrm{rank}(\cal{E}_{|C_{i}})
and
d_{i}:=\deg(\cal{E}_{|C_{i}})
. Here,
\cal{E}_{|C_{i}}:=\left(\cal{E}\otimes\cal{O}_{C_{i}}\right)/(\text{torsion})
is called the
restriction
of
\cal{E}
to
C_{i}
. We also define the
multi-characteristic

\vec{\chi}=(\chi_{1},\dots,\chi_{m})\in\mathbb{Z}^{\oplus m}
of
\cal{E}
with
\chi_{i}:=\chi(\cal{E}_{i})=d_{i}+r_{i}(1-g_{i})
. In case
r_{1}=\dots=r_{m}=r
, we say that
\cal{E}
has
pure rank

r
and write that
\mathrm{rank}(\cal{E})=\vec{r}=r
. Similarly, we may define the
pure degree
and
pure characteristic
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mathbf{w}=(w_{1},\dots,w_{m})\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}^{\oplus m}
be an
m
-tuple of positive rational numbers with
\sum_{i}w_{i}=1
, called a
polarization
of
C
. If
\cal{E}
is a depth-one sheaf of multi-rank
(r_{1},\dots,r_{m})
, the
\mathbf{w}
-rank
is defined to be
{\rm rank}_{\mathbf{w}}(\cal{E})=\sum_{i=1}^{m}w_{i}r_{i}\in\mathbb{Q}
, and the
\mathbf{w}
-slope
of
\cal{E}
on
C
is defined to be

\mu_{\mathbf{w}}(\cal{E})=\frac{\chi(\cal{E})}{\mathrm{rank}_{\mathbf{w}}(\cal{E})}\in\mathbb{Q}.

Then the sheaf
\cal{E}
is said to be
\mathbf{w}
-stable
(resp.
\mathbf{w}
-semistable
) if the inequality

\mu_{\mathbf{w}}(\cal{F})<\text{(resp. $\leq$) }\mu_{\mathbf{w}}(\cal{E})

holds for any proper subsheaf
\cal{F}
of
\cal{E}
.

Remark 2.2
.

Assume that
C=C_{1}\cup C_{2}
with
\mbox{Sing}_{1}(C)=\emptyset
and

g\geq 2
, the locus
\mathrm{Sym}^{2}(\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(C))
forms a proper closed subset of
\mathbf{M}_{C}(2,0)
. Indeed, it is the singular locus for
g\geq 3
, while
\mathbf{M}_{C}(2,0)
is smooth for
g=2
.

(a-2) If
\cal{E}
is semistable with
\deg(\cal{E})=1
, then
\cal{E}
is automatically stable. Note that there exists a natural determinant map
\det:\mathbf{M}_{C}(2,1)\rightarrow\operatorname{Pic}^{1}(C)
. It is an isomorphism for
g=1
, in which case we get
\mathbf{M}_{C}(2,1)\cong C
.

(b)   If
\cal{E}
is not semistable, then there exists a destabilizing line subbundle
\cal{L}\in\operatorname{Pic}^{\ell}(C)
with
\ell\geq 1
with an exact sequence

0\rightarrow\cal{L}\rightarrow\cal{E}\rightarrow\cal{L}^{\prime}\rightarrow 0,

for some
\cal{L}^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Pic}^{\ell^{\prime}}(C)
. One has
\mathrm{Ext}_{C}^{1}(\cal{L}^{\prime},\cal{L})\cong\mathrm{H}^{0}(C,\omega_{C}\otimes\cal{L}^{\prime}\otimes\cal{L}^{\vee})^{\vee}
by the Riemann-Roch, and so

\dim\left(\mathrm{Ext}_{C}^{1}(\cal{L}^{\prime},\cal{L})\right)=\max\{0,2g-2-2\ell+d\},\quad\text{where}\;d=\deg(\cal{E}).

Hence, the extension splits when
\ell\geq g+d-1
. In particular, if
C
is an elliptic curve, every non-semistable rank
2
vector bundle of degree
d
is isomorphic to a direct sum
\cal{L}\oplus\cal{L}^{\prime}
with
\cal{L}\in\operatorname{Pic}^{\ell}(C)
,
\cal{L}^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Pic}^{d-\ell}(C)
. The isomorphism classes of such bundles are parametrized by
C\times C
, modulo the
S_{2}
-action when
d=0
.

Remark 2.10
.

For a sheaf
\cal{E}

\mu_{\mathbf{w}}(\cal{G})>\mu_{\mathbf{w}}(\cal{E})\quad(\mathrm{resp.}\geq)

\cal{E}

3.
Description of moduli space

In this section we will focus on the case when
C=C_{1}\cup C_{2}
with each
C_{i}
a smooth curve of genus
g_{i}\geq 1
and
\mbox{Sing}_{2}(C)=\{p\}
. In particular,
C
is a stable curve of genus
g=g_{1}+g_{2}
. The main goal of this section is to describe explicitly the moduli space
\mathbf{U}_{C}(\mathbf{w},2,0)
of
\mathbf{w}
-semistable sheaves of pure rank
2
on
C
with
\chi(\cal{E})=0
. More generally, let
\cal{E}
be a
\mathbf{w}
-semistable sheaf on
C
with
\mathrm{rank}(\cal{E})=(r_{1},r_{2})
and
\chi(\cal{E})=0
. In the corresponding exact sequence (
\dagger
) to
\cal{E}
, let
\cal{A}_{1}
be the image of the composition map
\cal{E}_{1}\rightarrow\cal{E}_{1}\oplus\cal{E}_{2}\rightarrow\cal{T}
. Then for its residual torsion sheaf
\cal{A}_{2}\subset\cal{T}
, we get a surjection
\cal{E}_{2}\twoheadrightarrow\cal{A}_{2}
. Setting
\cal{E}_{i}^{\prime}
to be the kernel of the surjection
\cal{E}_{i}\twoheadrightarrow\cal{A}_{i}
for each
i=1,2
, we get an exact sequence

0\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{E}_{1}^{\prime}\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{E}\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{E}_{2}^{\prime}\xrightarrow{\ \ }0.

By Remark
2.10
(b), one gets that
\chi_{2}=\chi(\cal{E}_{2})\geq\chi(\cal{E}_{2}^{\prime})\geq 0
, which implies
d_{2}\geq r_{2}(g_{2}-1)
. By exchanging the roles of
\cal{E}_{1}
and
\cal{E}_{2}
, one also gets
d_{1}\geq r_{1}(g_{1}-1)
. Thus the decomposition (
1
) can be rewritten as

(3)

\mathbf{U}_{C}(\mathbf{w},\vec{r},\chi)=\bigcup_{0\leq\chi_{1},\chi_{2},\chi_{1}+\chi_{2}\leq r_{0}}\mathbf{U}_{C}(\mathbf{w},\vec{r},\chi)_{(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})},

with
r_{0}:=\mathrm{min}\{r_{1},r_{2}\}
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\cal{E}
be a sheaf of depth one on
C
with
\mathrm{rank}(\cal{E})=(r_{1},r_{2})
and
r_{2}=0
. Then,
\cal{E}
is
\mathbf{w}
-(semi-)stable if and only if
\cal{E}_{1}
in
(\dagger)
of Remark
2.2
is (semi-)stable. A similar assertion holds when
r_{1}=0
.

Proof.

Since
r_{2}=0
, it follows that
\cal{E}_{2}\cong 0\cong\cal{T}
in
(\dagger)
and
\cal{E}_{1}\cong\cal{E}
in
(\dagger)
. Note that the type of
\cal{E}
is given by
\delta(\cal{E})=(0,r_{1},0)
. Similarly, for any subsheaf
\cal{F}\subset\cal{E}
, we have
\cal{F}_{1}\cong\cal{F}
. Next, the
\mathbf{w}
-soples of
\cal{E}
and
\cal{F}
are given by

\mu_{\mathbf{w}}(\cal{E})=\frac{\chi(\cal{E}_{1})}{w_{1}\cdot r_{1}}=\left(\frac{1}{w_{1}}\right)\mu(\cal{E}_{1})+\frac{1-g_{1}}{w_{1}},\quad\text{and}\quad\mu_{\mathbf{w}}(\cal{F})=\left(\frac{1}{w_{1}}\right)\mu(\cal{F}_{1})+\frac{1-g_{1}}{w_{1}}.

The difference of the slopes can be computed as

\mu_{\mathbf{w}}(\cal{E})-\mu_{\mathbf{w}}(\cal{F})=\left(\frac{1}{w_{1}}\right)(\mu(\cal{E}_{1})-\mu(\cal{F}_{1})),

and so we get the assertion.
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

The moduli space
\mathbf{U}_{C}(\mathbf{w},1,0)
is isomorphic to
\operatorname{Pic}^{g_{1}-1}(C_{1})\times\operatorname{Pic}^{g_{2}-1}(C_{2})
for any polarization
\mathbf{w}
.

Proof.

Let
\cal{L}
be a sheaf of depth one in
\mathbf{U}_{C}(\mathbf{w},1,0)
with associated exact sequence (
\dagger
) for
\cal{L}
. When
\tau=0
, we have
\cal{L}\cong\cal{L}_{1}\oplus\cal{L}_{2}
, and since
\chi(\cal{L})=0
, it follows that
\chi_{1}+\chi_{2}=0
. Now the
\mathbf{w}
-semistability of
\cal{L}
implies that each
\chi_{i}\leq 0
, and so
\chi_{1}=\chi_{2}=0
, i.e.,
d_{i}=g_{i}-1
. Conversely, for any line bundle
\cal{L}_{i}\in\operatorname{Pic}^{g_{i}-1}(C_{i})
,
\cal{L}_{1}\oplus\cal{L}_{2}
is
\mathbf{w}
-semistable. Thus we get

\mathbf{U}_{C}(\mathbf{w},1,0)_{(0,0)}\cong\operatorname{Pic}^{g_{1}-1}(C_{1})\times\operatorname{Pic}^{g_{2}-1}(C_{2}).

Now assume that
\tau=1
, i.e.,
\delta(\cal{L})=(1,0,0)
, and so
\cal{L}
fits into the following exact sequence:

0\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{L}\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{L}_{1}\oplus\cal{L}_{2}\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{O}_{p}\xrightarrow{\ \ }0.

Since
\chi_{1}+\chi_{2}=1
, we may assume that
\chi_{1}\geq 1
without loss of generality. If the composition map
\cal{L}_{1}\hookrightarrow\cal{L}_{1}\oplus\cal{L}_{2}\twoheadrightarrow\cal{O}_{p}
is surjective, then we obtain an exact sequence

0\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{L}_{1}(-p)\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{L}\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{L}_{2}\xrightarrow{\ \ }0.

From the
\mathbf{w}
-semistability of
\cal{L}
, we get
\chi_{1}-1=\chi(\cal{L}_{1}(-p))\leq 0
, and so
(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})=(1,0)
. If the composition map is not surjective, then
\cal{L}_{1}
would be a subsheaf of
\cal{L}
, contradicting the
\mathbf{w}
-semistability of
\cal{L}
.
Thus, we have

[\cal{L}]=[\cal{L}_{1}(-p)\oplus\cal{L}_{2}]\in\mathbf{U}_{C}(\mathbf{w},1,0)_{(1,0)}.

This implies, as before, that
\mathbf{U}_{C}(\mathbf{w},1,0)_{(1,0)}\cong\operatorname{Pic}^{g_{1}-1}(C_{1})\times\operatorname{Pic}^{g_{2}-1}(C_{2})
. From the case when the composition map
\cal{L}_{2}\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{O}_{p}
is surjective, we get
\mathbf{U}_{C}(\mathbf{w},1,0)_{(0,1)}\cong\operatorname{Pic}^{g_{1}-1}(C_{1})\times\operatorname{Pic}^{g_{2}-1}(C_{2})
. Then the assertion follows from (
3
) with
r_{0}=1
. Note that each point in the moduli space is strictly semistable.
∎

Proposition 3.3
.

The moduli space
\mathbf{U}_{C}(\mathbf{w},(2,1),0)
is isomorphic to
\mathbf{M}_{C_{1}}(2,2g_{1}-2)\times\operatorname{Pic}^{g_{2}-1}(C_{2})
for any polarization
\mathbf{w}
.

Proof.

Let
\cal{E}
be a sheaf of depth one in
\mathbf{U}_{C}(\mathbf{w},(2,1),0)
with the exact sequence (
\dagger
) with a vector bundle
\cal{E}_{1}
of rank two on
C_{1}
and a line bundle
\cal{L}_{2}
on
C_{2}
.

First assume that
\tau=0
. Then
\cal{E}
splits as a direct sum, i.e.,
\cal{E}\cong\cal{E}_{1}\oplus\cal{L}_{2}
, and we have
\delta(\cal{E})=(0,2,1)
. From the
\mathbf{w}
-semistability of
\cal{E}
, we get that

\mu_{\mathbf{w}}(\cal{E}_{1})\leq\mu_{\mathbf{w}}(\cal{E})=0,\quad\text{and}\quad\mu_{\mathbf{w}}(\cal{L}_{2})\leq\mu_{\mathbf{w}}(\cal{E})=0

and so
\chi_{1}=\chi_{2}=0
. Moreover, one can check that
\cal{E}_{1}
is semistable, and so it corresponds to a point in
\mathbf{M}_{C_{1}}(2,2g_{1}-2)
. Conversely, for any
\cal{E}_{1}\in\mathbf{M}_{C_{1}}(2,2g_{1}-2)
and
\cal{L}_{2}\in\operatorname{Pic}^{g_{2}-1}(C_{2})
,
\cal{E}_{1}\oplus\cal{L}_{2}
is
\mathbf{w}
-semistable using Proposition
3.1
. Therefore, we get

\mathbf{U}_{C}(\mathbf{w},(2,1),0)_{(0,0)}\cong\mathbf{M}_{C_{1}}(2,2g_{1}-2)\times\operatorname{Pic}^{g_{2}-1}(C_{2}).

Now assume that
\tau=1
. Note that
\cal{E}
fits into the following exact sequence:

0\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{E}\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{E}_{1}\oplus\cal{L}_{2}\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{O}_{p}\xrightarrow{\ \ }0.

Since
\chi_{1}+\chi_{2}=1
, first we assume that
\chi_{1}\geq 1
. If the composition map
\cal{E}_{1}\hookrightarrow\cal{E}_{1}\oplus\cal{L}_{2}\twoheadrightarrow\cal{O}_{p}
is surjective, then we obtain an exact sequence

0\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{E}_{1}^{\prime}\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{E}\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{L}_{2}\xrightarrow{\ \ }0.

From the
\mathbf{w}
-semistability of
\cal{E}
, we get
\chi_{1}-1=\chi(\cal{E}_{1}^{\prime})\leq 0
, and so
(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})=(1,0)
. Furthermore, from
\mu_{\mathbf{w}}(\cal{E})=\mu_{\mathbf{w}}(\cal{E}_{1}^{\prime})=0
one gets that
\cal{E}_{1}^{\prime}
is also
\mathbf{w}
-semistable. By Proposition
3.1
,
\cal{E}_{1}^{\prime}
is semistable as a sheaf on
C_{1}
, i.e.,
[\cal{E}_{1}^{\prime}]\in\mathbf{M}_{C_{1}}(2,2g_{1}-2)
. If the composition map is not surjective, then
\cal{E}_{1}
would be a subsheaf of
\cal{E}
, contradicting the
\mathbf{w}
-semistability of
\cal{E}
.
Thus, we have

[\cal{E}]=[\cal{E}_{1}^{\prime}\oplus\cal{L}_{2}]\in\mathbf{U}_{C}(\mathbf{w},1,0)_{(1,0)}.

Therefore, as in the case
\tau=0
, we get

\mathbf{U}_{C}(\mathbf{w},(2,1),0)_{(1,0)}\cong\mathbf{M}_{C_{1}}(2,2g_{1}-2)\times\operatorname{Pic}^{g_{2}-1}(C_{2}).

Similarly, for the case
\chi_{2}\geq 1
, we get
[\cal{E}]=[\cal{E}_{1}\oplus\cal{L}_{2}(-p)]\in\mathbf{U}_{C}(\mathbf{w},1,0)_{(0,1)}
.
In particular, we have

\mathbf{U}_{C}(\mathbf{w},(2,1),0)_{(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})}\cong\mathbf{M}_{C_{1}}(2,2g_{1}-2)\times\operatorname{Pic}^{g_{2}-1}(C_{2})

for any
(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})\in\{(0,0),(1,0),(0,1)\}
.
Then, we get the assertion by (
3
) with
r_{0}=1
.
∎

Proposition 3.4
.

The moduli space
\mathbf{U}_{C}(\mathbf{w},2,0)_{(0,0)}
is isomorphic to
\mathbf{M}_{C_{1}}(2,2g_{1}-2)\times\mathbf{M}_{C_{2}}(2,2g_{2}-2)
for any
\mathbf{w}
.

Proof.

Let
\cal{E}
be a sheaf in
\mathbf{U}_{C}(\mathbf{w},2,0)_{(0,0)}
. Then,
\cal{E}
decomposes as
\cal{E}\cong\cal{E}_{1}\oplus\cal{E}_{2}
, where each
\cal{E}_{i}
is a vector bundle of rank
2
and degree
2g_{i}-2
on
C_{i}
. In particular, each
\cal{E}_{i}
is semistable, i.e.,
[\cal{E}_{i}]\in\mathbf{M}_{C_{i}}(2,2g_{i}-2)
. Define a map

\psi:\mathbf{U}_{C}(\mathbf{w},2,0)_{(0,0)}\rightarrow\mathbf{M}_{C_{1}}(2,2g_{1}-2)\times\mathbf{M}_{C_{2}}(2,2g_{2}-2)

by sending
[\cal{E}]
to
([\mathfrak{gr}(\cal{E}_{1})],[\mathfrak{gr}(\cal{E}_{2})])
. Clearly, the map
\psi
is well-defined and injective. Now for the surjectivity, pick a pair
([\cal{E}_{1}],[\cal{E}_{2}])\in\mathbf{M}_{C_{1}}(2,2g_{1}-2)\times\mathbf{M}_{C_{1}}(2,2g_{2}-2)
, and set
\cal{E}=\cal{E}_{1}\oplus\cal{E}_{2}
. For any subsheaf
\cal{F}
, we have
\cal{F}\cong\cal{F}_{1}\oplus\cal{F}_{2}
with each
\cal{F}_{i}=\cal{E}_{i}\cap\cal{F}
. By Proposition
3.1
, each
\cal{E}_{i}
is
\mathbf{w}
-semistable, and so
\chi(\cal{F}_{i})\leq 0
. From the following inequality

\mu_{\mathbf{w}}(\cal{F})=\frac{\chi(\cal{F}_{1})+\chi(\cal{F}_{2})}{\mathrm{rank}_{\mathbf{w}}(\cal{F})}\leq 0=\mu_{\mathbf{w}}(\cal{E}),

we get that
\cal{E}
is
\mathbf{w}
-semistable, and the assertion follows.
∎

Proposition 3.5
.

For any polarization
\mathbf{w}
, we have

\mathbf{U}_{C}(\mathbf{w},2,0)_{(0,1)}=\mathbf{U}_{C}(\mathbf{w},2,0)_{(1,0)}=\mathbf{U}_{C}(\mathbf{w},2,0)_{(0,0)}.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, it is enough to show that
\mathbf{U}_{C}(\mathbf{w},2,0)_{(0,1)}=\mathbf{U}_{C}(\mathbf{w},2,0)_{(0,0)}
. Let
\cal{E}
be a sheaf in
\mathbf{U}_{C}(\mathbf{w},2,0)_{(0,1)}
with the following exact sequence (
\dagger
):

0\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{E}\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{E}_{1}\oplus\cal{E}_{2}\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{O}_{p}\xrightarrow{\ \ }0

with a vector bundle
\cal{E}_{i}
of rank two on
C_{i}
and
(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})=(0,1)
. If the composition map
\iota_{2}:\cal{E}_{2}\hookrightarrow\cal{E}_{1}\oplus\cal{E}_{2}\twoheadrightarrow\cal{O}_{p}
is not surjective, then
\cal{E}_{2}
would be a subsheaf of
\cal{E}
, contradicting the
\mathbf{w}
-semistability of
\cal{E}
. Thus the composition map
\iota_{2}
is surjective, and set
\cal{E}_{2}^{\prime}=\ker(\iota_{2})
. Then we obtain an exact sequence

0\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{E}_{2}^{\prime}\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{E}\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{E}_{1}\xrightarrow{\ \ }0.

Moreover, we get
\chi(\cal{E}_{2}^{\prime})=\chi_{2}-1=0
, and from
\mu_{\mathbf{w}}(\cal{E})=\mu_{\mathbf{w}}(\cal{E}_{2}^{\prime})=0
it follows that
\cal{E}_{2}^{\prime}
is also
\mathbf{w}
-semistable. By Proposition
3.1
,
\cal{E}_{2}^{\prime}
is semistable as a sheaf on
C_{2}
, i.e.,
[\cal{E}_{2}^{\prime}]\in\mathbf{M}_{C_{2}}(2,2g_{2}-2)
. Similarly, we also get
[\cal{E}_{1}]\in\mathbf{M}_{C_{1}}(2,2g_{1}-2)
. This implies that the Jordan-Hölder filtration of
\cal{E}
is given by

\mathfrak{gr}_{\mathbf{w}}(\cal{E})\cong\mathfrak{gr}(\cal{E}_{1})\oplus\mathfrak{gr}(\cal{E}_{2}^{\prime}).

Thus, by Proposition
3.4
, every sheaf in
\mathbf{U}_{C}(\mathbf{w},2,0)_{(0,1)}
is contained in
\mathbf{U}_{C}(\mathbf{w},2,0)_{(0,0)}
.

Conversely, let
[\cal{E}_{1}^{\prime}\oplus\cal{E}_{2}^{\prime}]\in\mathbf{U}_{C}(\mathbf{w},2,0)_{(0,0)}
with
[\cal{E}_{i}^{\prime}]\in\mathbf{M}_{C_{i}}(2,2g_{i}-2)
by Proposition
3.4
. Let
\cal{E}^{\prime}
be a nontrivial extension

(4)

0\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{E}_{2}^{\prime}\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{E}^{\prime}\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{O}_{p}\xrightarrow{\ \ }0

of
\chi(\cal{E}^{\prime})=1
, which is represented by a general element in
\mathbb{P}\mathrm{Ext}_{C_{2}}^{1}(\cal{O}_{p},\cal{E}_{2}^{\prime})\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}
. Indeed, if
\cal{E}_{2}^{\prime}\in\mathbf{M}_{C_{2}}(2,2g_{2}-2)
is stable, then
\cal{E}^{\prime}
is also stable for any element in the extension family. Assume that
\cal{E}_{2}^{\prime}
is strictly semistable, say
[\cal{E}_{2}^{\prime}]=[\cal{L}_{2}\oplus\cal{L}_{2}^{\prime}]
with
\cal{L}_{2},\cal{L}_{2}^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Pic}^{g_{2}-1}(C_{2})
. Then
\cal{E}^{\prime}
is expressed in terms of coordinates

[\varepsilon_{0}:\varepsilon_{1}]\in\mathbb{P}\mathrm{Ext}_{C_{2}}^{1}(\cal{O}_{p},\cal{L}_{2}\oplus\cal{L}_{2}^{\prime})\cong\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{H}^{0}(\cal{L}_{2}^{\vee}\otimes\cal{O}_{p})^{\vee}\oplus\mathrm{H}^{0}(\cal{L}_{2}^{\prime\vee}\otimes\cal{O}_{p})^{\vee}\right)\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}.

If
\cal{L}\subset\cal{E}^{\prime}
is a destabilizing line bundle with
\deg(\cal{L})\geq g_{2}
, then the composition map
\upsilon:\cal{L}\rightarrow\cal{E}^{\prime}\rightarrow\cal{O}_{p}
is surjective; otherwise,
\cal{L}
would be a subsheaf of
\cal{L}_{2}\oplus\cal{L}_{2}^{\prime}
, impossible. Then
\cal{L}(-p)\cong\mathrm{ker}(\upsilon)
is a subsheaf of
\cal{L}_{2}\oplus\cal{L}_{2}^{\prime}
and so we get
\cal{L}\cong\cal{L}_{2}(p)
or
\cal{L}_{2}^{\prime}(p)
. In the former case, we get that
\cal{E}^{\prime}
is an extension of
\cal{L}_{2}^{\prime}
by
\cal{L}_{2}(p)
, which is trivial. Similarly, in the latter case, we get
\cal{E}^{\prime}\cong\cal{L}_{2}\oplus\cal{L}_{2}^{\prime}(p)
. In particular, the coordinates
[\varepsilon_{0},\varepsilon_{1}]\not\in\{[0:1],[1:0]\}
if and only if
\cal{E}^{\prime}
is semistable, i.e.,
[\cal{E}^{\prime}]\in\mathbf{M}_{C_{2}}(2,2g_{2}-1)
. For any such
\cal{E}^{\prime}
, set
\cal{E}:=\cal{E}_{1}^{\prime}\oplus\cal{E}^{\prime}
. Then
\cal{E}\in\mathbf{U}_{C}(\mathbf{w},2,0)_{(0,1)}
maps to
[\cal{E}_{1}^{\prime}\oplus\cal{E}_{2}^{\prime}]
, and the assertion follows.
∎

Remark 3.6
.

From the argument in the proof of Proposition
3.5
, one can obtain a rational map

.

Proposition 3.7
.

For any polarization
\mathbf{w}

\mathbf{U}_{C}(\mathbf{w},2,0)_{(0,2)}=\mathbf{U}_{C}(\mathbf{w},2,0)_{(2,0)}=\mathbf{U}_{C}(\mathbf{w},2,0)_{(0,0)}.

with the exact sequence (
\dagger
)

with a vector bundle
\cal{E}_{i}
of rank two on

and

\iota_{2}:\cal{E}_{2}\hookrightarrow\cal{E}_{1}\oplus\cal{E}_{2}\twoheadrightarrow\cal{O}_{p}^{\oplus 2}
is not a surjection, then

is a subsheaf of

with Euler characteristic at least

-semistability of

Representations of Hamiltonian Lie algebras
Vyacheslav Futorny, Santanu Tantubay

Abstract
. We consider the Shen-Larsson functor from the category of modules for the symplectic Lie algebra 𝔰 ​ 𝔭 N \mathfrak{sp}_{N} to the category of modules for the Hamiltonian Lie algebra and show that it preserves the irreducibility except in the finite number of cases. The obtained irreducible modules for the Hamiltonian Lie algebra are cuspidal, whose weight multiplicities equal the dimension of the corresponding module of the symplectic Lie algebra. This extends well-known results for other Cartan type Lie algebras to the Hamiltonian case.



1.
Introduction

Cartan type Lie algebras
W_{n}^{+}
(respectively
W_{n}
)
of polynomial (respectively Laurent polynomial) vector fields on
n
-dimensional affine space (respectively torus), and its subalgebras of types
S
,
K
and
H
are classical examples of infinite dimensional Lie algebras. Significant advances were made in the last 10 years in the study of various categories of representations for Witt algebras
W_{n}
and
W_{n}^{+}
, see
[
1
]
,
[
14
]
and references therein. Representations of type
S
Lie algebras were studied in
[
2
]
and
[
4
]
.

Jet modules for the Hamiltonian Lie algebra
H^{+}_{n}
were studied in
[
13
]
. An important step towards the classification of all quasi-finite modules was made in
[
5
]
for
n=2
, which is the case of Virasoro-like algebra.

In current paper we consider the case of an arbitrary even-dimensional torus and construct irreducible representations of the corresponding Hamiltonian Lie algebra
by applying the Shen-Larsson functor
F^{\alpha,\beta}
(
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{C}^{N}
) to arbitrary irreducible representations of symplectic Lie algebra of the same rank. We show that this functor preserves irreducibility except in the finite number of cases, which extends the results of
[
10
]
,
[
6
]
,
[
7
]
and
[
12
]
in the case of Witt algebras.

Denote by
V(\delta_{k})
,
0\leq k\leq n
irreducible fundamental representation of

\mathfrak{sp}_{N}
. Our main result is the following.

Main Theorem.

Let
V
be an irreducible
\mathfrak{sp}_{N}
module and
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{C}^{N}
. Then
F^{\alpha,\beta}(V)
is irreducible if and only if one of the following conditions holds:

(i)

\dim V=\infty
;

(ii)

\dim V<\infty
and

V\ncong V(\delta_{k})
for
1\leq k\leq n
;

(iii)

V\simeq\mathbb{C}
and
\alpha\notin\mathbb{Z}^{N}
.

In particular, it answers some questions asked in
[
9
, Remark 4.11]
.

2.
Hamiltonian Lie algebra

We denote by
\mathbb{Z},\;\mathbb{Z}_{+},\;\mathbb{N},\;\mathbb{C},\;\mathbb{C}^{*}
the sets of integers, non-negative integers, positive integers, complex numbers, non-zero complex numbers respectively. Let
N
be a fixed positive integer, and
\mathbb{Z}^{N},\;\mathbb{C}^{N}
are
N
copies of
\mathbb{Z},\;\mathbb{C}
respectively. Let
(-,-)
be the standard bilinear form on
\mathbb{C}^{N}
. Denote by
\mathfrak{gl}_{N}
be the Lie algebra of all
N\times N
matrices with the standard basis
\{e_{i,j}:1\leq i,j\leq N\}
. For any Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, let
\mathfrak{g}
-mod be the category of all modules over
\mathfrak{g}
.

2.1.
Symplectic Lie algebra.

Let
N=2n
. The symplectic Lie algebra
\mathfrak{sp}_{N}
is spanned by
h_{i}:=e_{i,i}-e_{n+i,n+i}
,
X_{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}}:=e_{i,j}-e_{n+j,n+i}
,
X_{\epsilon_{k}+\epsilon_{l}}:=e_{k,n+l}+e_{l,n+k}
,
X_{-\epsilon_{k}-\epsilon_{l}}:=e_{n+k,l}+e_{n+l,k}
,
1\leq i,j\leq n
,
i\neq j
,
1\leq k\leq l\leq n
. It is a simple Lie subalgebra of
\mathfrak{gl}_{N}
with a Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}=\mathbb{C}\{h_{i}:1\leq i\leq n\}
. Let
\Pi=\{\alpha_{1}:=\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{2},\dots,\alpha_{n-1}:=\epsilon_{n-1}-\epsilon_{n},\alpha_{n}:=2\epsilon_{n}\}
be the set of simple roots of
\mathfrak{sp}_{N}
and
\Pi^{\vee}=\{\alpha_{1}^{\vee}:=h_{1}-h_{2},\dots,\alpha_{n-1}^{\vee}:=h_{n-1}-h_{n},\alpha_{n}^{\vee}=h_{n}\}
be the set of co-roots. Let
\{\delta_{i}\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}:1\leq i\leq n\}
be the set of fundamental weights with the property
\delta_{i}(\alpha_{j}^{\vee})=\delta_{i,j}
for
1\leq i,j\leq n
. Denote by
\mathbb{Z}_{+}\Pi
the free monoid generated by
\Pi
.
Let us define the height function
H:\mathbb{Z}_{+}\Pi\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{+}
by

H(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\alpha_{i})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i},

this function will be used in the paper. We see that
H(\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{i+1})=1
and
H(\epsilon_{i}+\epsilon_{j})=2n-(i+j)+1
for
1\leq i,j\leq n
.

2.2.
Witt and Hamiltonian Lie algebras.

Let
A_{N}=\mathbb{C}[t_{1}^{\pm 1},

\dots,t_{N}^{\pm 1}]
be the Laurent polynomial ring in
N
variables. Then the space
W_{N}=Der(A_{N})
of all derivations of
A_{N}
is the Witt Lie algebra with a
\mathbb{C}
basis
\{t^{r}d_{i}:r\in\mathbb{Z}^{N},i\in\{1,\dots,N\}\}
. Denoting
D(u,r)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}u_{i}t^{r}d_{i}
for
u\in\mathbb{C}^{N},\;r\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}
, we then have
[D(u,r),D(v,s)]=D(w,r+s)
, where
w=(u,s)v-(v,r)u
for
u,v\in\mathbb{C}^{N},\;r,s\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}
. Now, if
N=2n
, for
r=(r_{1},\dots,r_{n},r_{n+1}\dots r_{2n})^{t}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}
we define
\bar{r}=(r_{n+1},\dots,r_{2n},-r_{1},\dots-r_{n})^{t}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}
and
h_{r}:=D(\bar{r},r)
. We see that
(\bar{r},s)=-(\bar{s},r)
for any two
r,s\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}
.

The
\mathbb{Z}^{N}
-graded Hamiltonian Lie algebra is defined as

\mathfrak{H}_{N}=span_{\mathbb{C}}\{h_{r}:r(\neq 0)\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}\}\oplus_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{C}d_{i},

where
[h_{r},h_{s}]=(\bar{r},s)h_{r+s}
for
r,s\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}
, and
\{d_{i},i=1,\ldots,n\}
are the degree derivations (cf.
[
11
]
). Viewing
r\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}
as a column vector, we have
r\bar{r}^{t}\in\mathfrak{sp}_{N}
, where
r^{t}
denotes the transpose of the matrix
r
.

2.3.
Shen-Larsson functor.

For
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{C}^{N}
we define the functor

F^{\alpha,\beta}:\mathfrak{sp}_{N}-\text{mod}\rightarrow\mathfrak{H}_{N}-\text{mod}

by
F^{\alpha,\beta}(V)=V\otimes A_{N}
, with the module action given by:

(2.1)

h_{r}(v\otimes t^{s})=((\bar{r},s+\alpha)I+r\bar{r}^{t})v\otimes t^{r+s},

(2.2)

d_{i}(v\otimes t^{s})=(s_{i}+\beta_{i})v\otimes t^{s},

where
s\in\mathbb{C}^{N}
and
t^{s}:=t_{1}^{s_{1}}\ldots t_{N}^{s_{N}}
.
Here
I
is the identity operator of
V
. We say that
F^{\alpha,\beta}
extends the
\mathfrak{sp}_{N}
-module
V
to
\mathfrak{H}_{N}
-module
F^{\alpha,\beta}(V)
.

Remark 2.1
.

For any
\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}
,
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}^{N}
and irreducible
\mathfrak{sp}_{N}
-module
V
we have the isomorphism
F^{\alpha,\beta}(V)\cong F^{(\alpha+\gamma),(\beta+\gamma)}(V)
given by:
v\otimes t^{r}\rightarrow v\otimes t^{r-\gamma}
.

Our first main result is the following theorem which shows that the extension of an irreducible module remains irreducible except possibly a finite number of finite dimensional cases.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{C}^{N}
and
V
an irreducible module over
\mathfrak{sp}_{N}
. Then
F^{\alpha,\beta}(V)
is an irreducible
\mathfrak{H}_{N}
-module if
V
is not isomorphic to one of finite dimensional fundamental modules
V(\delta_{k})
, where
k=0,1,2,\dots,n
with
\delta_{0}=0
.

The proof of Theorem
2.2
will be given in the next section.

2.4.
Jet modules

Let us recall the notion of Jet modules over Hamiltonian vector fields on a torus from
[
13
]
. An
\mathfrak{H}_{N}
-module
J
is called jet module if it satisfies the following properties:

(1)

The action of
d_{i}
on
J
is diagonalizable for
1\leq i\leq N
;

(2)

J
is a free
A_{N}
-module of finite rank;

(3)

The actions
\mathfrak{H}_{N}
and
A_{N}
on
J
are naturally compatible.

Indecomposible and irreducible jet modules were classified in
[
13
, Theorem 4.4,Theorem 5.2]
and all irreducible jet modules are of the form
F^{\alpha,\beta}(V)
for some irreducible module
V
over the symplectic Lie algebra and some
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{C}^{N}
. Theorem
2.2
shows that there are only finitely many cases where irreducible jet modules over
\mathfrak{H}_{N}
are not irreducible as
\mathfrak{H}_{N}
-modules. Moreover, we can construct a large class of irreducible generalized jet modules (with no requirement of finite rank as an
A_{N}
-module) over the Hamiltonian Lie algebra if we define the action of
A_{N}
by

t^{r}(v\otimes t^{s}):=v\otimes t^{r+s}.

3.
Proof of Theorem
2.2

We start with the following standard result (cf.
[

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a finite-dimensional semi-simple Lie algebra with a fixed Cartan subalgebra,
\Phi
the root system of
\mathfrak{g}
and V an irreducible
\mathfrak{g}
-module. Then we have

(1)

Every root vector of

(2)

The module

Proof.

In

7

(1)

For some
\alpha\in\Phi

is not injective on

is locally nilpotent on the universal enveloping algebra
U(\mathfrak{g})

(ad\;X_{\alpha})^{k}\;u=0

X_{\alpha}^{k}uv=(ad\;X_{\alpha})^{k}\;uv=0

V
is irreducible.

(2)

If

is finite dimensional, then clearly
X_{\alpha},\;\alpha\in\Phi
acts locally nilpotently. Now we assume that all root vectors act locally nilpotently. By
[
8

is a highest weight module as well as a lowest weight module. Therefore

is finite dimensional.

is an element of
\mathcal{M}
with smaller height than
v_{1}
.
This gives again a contradiction (note that by the previous assumption, no
X_{2\epsilon_{j}}v_{k}
can reach
X_{\epsilon_{j}-\epsilon_{j+1}}v_{1}
for
k\in\{2,\dots p\}
). So, the height of
w
must be zero. Now, without loss of generality we may assume that
w=v_{\lambda}+\sum_{j=2}^{p}v_{j}\in\mathcal{M}
.

Subcase 1:
If
i_{k}=n
, then consider the
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
copy given by
\mathbb{C}\{h_{i_{1}},X_{\pm 2\epsilon_{i_{1}}}\}
. We see that
\lambda(h_{i_{1}})=\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{i_{j}}\geq 2
. Hence,
X_{-2\epsilon_{i_{1}}}^{2}.v_{\lambda}\neq 0
. This gives us
X_{-2\epsilon_{i_{1}}}^{2}w\neq 0
. Now applying Lemma
3.2
and looking at the coefficients of
s_{n+i_{1}}^{4}
in
g_{2}(s)
, we see that
X_{-2\epsilon_{i_{1}}}^{2}w\in M
. Hence we have the claim (I) in this case.

Subcase 2:
If
i_{k}<n
, then consider the
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
copy given by
\mathbb{C}\{h_{i_{1}}-h_{i_{k}},X_{\pm(\epsilon_{i_{1}}-\epsilon_{i_{k}})}\}
. We have
\lambda(h_{i_{1}}-h_{i_{k}})=\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{i_{j}}\geq 2
and hence
X_{\epsilon_{i_{k}}-\epsilon_{i_{1}}}^{2}v_{\lambda}\neq 0
. Notice that
s_{i_{k}}^{2}s_{n+i_{1}}^{2}
has the coefficient

X_{\epsilon_{i_{k}}-\epsilon_{i_{1}}}^{2}-\frac{1}{4}X_{-2\epsilon_{i_{1}}}X_{2\epsilon_{i_{k}}}-\frac{1}{4}X_{2\epsilon_{i_{k}}}X_{-2\epsilon_{i_{1}}}=X_{\epsilon_{i_{k}}-\epsilon_{i_{1}}}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}X_{-2\epsilon_{i_{1}}}X_{2\epsilon_{i_{k}}}.

By height argument, we see that
\{X_{\epsilon_{i_{k}}-\epsilon_{i_{1}}}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}X_{-2\epsilon_{i_{k}}}X_{2\epsilon_{i_{1}}}\}w\neq 0
. Now applying Lemma
3.2
, with the coefficients of
s_{i_{k}}^{2}s_{n+i_{1}}^{2}
in
g_{2}(s)
, we see that
M\neq 0
.

Hence, in all cases we have
M\neq 0
.

Claim (2):

M
is an
\mathfrak{sp}_{N}
module.

Proof.
Let
v\in M
. Then
v\otimes t^{r-s}\in P
for all
r,s\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}
, and we have

h_{s}(v\otimes t^{r-s})=w_{2}(s)\otimes t^{r},

where
w_{2}(s)=\{(\bar{s},r+\alpha)I+s\bar{s}^{t}\}v
. This means that
g_{1}(s)v\in P_{r}
for all
s\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}
. Now looking at the coefficients of degree 2 in
g_{1}(s)
and applying Lemma
3.2
, we see that
Xv\in P_{r}
for all
r\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}
and all
X\in\mathfrak{sp}_{N}
. Therefore,
Xv\in M
for all
X\in\mathfrak{sp}_{N}
, and hence
M
is an
\mathfrak{sp}_{N}
-module.

Claim (2) implies that
P=F^{\alpha,\beta}(V)
and hence
F^{\alpha,\beta}(V)
is irreducible, completing the proof of the theorem.

4.
Extensions of fundamental modules.

4.1.
Irreducibility of
F^{\alpha,\beta}(V(\delta_{0}))
.

We first consider the case
k=0
. We have

Proposition 4.1
.

The
\mathfrak{H}_{N}
-module
F^{\alpha,\beta}(V(\delta_{0}))=\mathbb{C}\otimes A_{N}
is irreducible if and only if
\alpha\notin\mathbb{Z}^{N}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}
. Then
F^{\alpha,\beta}(V(\delta_{0}))
is reducible since
\mathbb{C}(1\otimes t^{-\alpha})
is a nonzero proper submodule of
F^{\alpha}(V(\delta_{0}))
. It is easy to see that the quotient module is irreducible.
Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}^{N}\setminus\mathbb{Z}^{N}
. We will show that
F^{\alpha,\beta}(V(\delta_{0}))
is irreducible.
Let
P
be nonzero submodule of
F^{\alpha,\beta}(V(\delta_{0}))
.
Now it being weight module, there exists
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}
such that
1\otimes t^{k}\in P
. Suppose
r\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}
such that
(\bar{r},k+\alpha)\neq 0
, then
h_{r}(1\otimes t^{k})=(\bar{r},k+\alpha)(1\otimes t^{k+r})\in P
i.e.
1\otimes t^{k+r}\in P
. Now we choose a fixed
r\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}
such that
(\bar{r},k+\alpha)=0
.

Since
\alpha\notin\mathbb{Z}^{N}
, we will have
(k+\alpha)\neq 0
, so there exists
i\in\{1,\dots,2n\}
such that
(k_{i}+\alpha_{i})\neq 0
. Now
h_{r-s}h_{s}(1\otimes t^{k})\in P
for all
r,s\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}
. This means
g_{2}(s)\cdot 1\in P_{k+r}
for all
r\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}
. Now we see that

g_{2}(s)\cdot 1=(\bar{r}-\bar{s},k+s+\alpha)(\bar{s},k+\alpha)1=((\bar{r},s)-(\bar{s},k+\alpha))(\bar{s},k+\alpha)1

=-(\bar{s},r+k+\alpha)(\bar{s},k+\alpha)1,

since we chose
r\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}
such that
(\bar{r},k+\alpha)=0
. Now, as
\alpha\notin\mathbb{Z}^{N}
, there exist
1\leq i,j\leq N
such that
(r_{i}+k_{i}+\alpha_{i})(k_{j}+\alpha_{j})\neq 0
. So we will have a second degree monomial of
g_{2}(s)
, whose coefficient is
-(r_{i}+k_{i}+\alpha_{i})(k_{j}+\alpha_{j})
. Applying Lemma
3.2
, we can say that
-(r_{i}+k_{i}+\alpha_{i})(k_{j}+\alpha_{j})1\in P_{k+r}
. Combining both cases, we conclude that
1\in P_{k+r}
for all
r\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}
. This implies that
P=F^{\alpha,\beta}(V(\delta_{0}))
and proves the irreducibility.
∎

4.2.
Irreducibility of
F^{\alpha,\beta}(V(\delta_{k})),\,k>0
.

Let us first discuss about the concrete realizations of fundamental representations of
\mathfrak{sp}_{N}
. Recall that
V(\delta_{0})\simeq\mathbb{C}
is one-dimensional trivial representation. Also, the space

\mathbb{C}^{N}
is a natural representation of
\mathfrak{sp}_{N}
(here elements of
\mathbb{C}^{N}
will be considered as column vectors). We define the contraction map

\theta_{j}:\bigwedge^{j}(\mathbb{C}^{N})\rightarrow\bigwedge^{j-2}(\mathbb{C}^{N})

by

(4.1)

\theta_{j}(v_{1}\wedge\dots v_{j})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r,s=1\\
r<s\end{subarray}}^{j}(-1)^{r+s-1}(v_{r},\bar{v_{s}})(v_{1}\dots,\hat{v}_{r},\dots,\hat{v}_{s},\dots,v_{j}),

for
2\leq j\leq N
. Here
\hat{v}
means that
v
is ommited. It is well known that
\theta_{j}
is an
\mathfrak{sp}_{N}
-module homomorphism for all
2\leq j\leq N
(cf.
[
3
, Proposition 13.25]
). We have the following of
\mathfrak{sp}_{N}
-modules.

Theorem 4.2
.

[
3
, Theorem 13.28]

(i)

V(\delta_{1})\cong\mathbb{C}^{N}
.

(ii)

V(\delta_{k})\cong Ker(\theta_{k})
,
2\leq k\leq n
.

Our second main result is the following theorem

Theorem 4.3
.

F^{\alpha,\beta}(V(\delta_{k}))
is reducible
\mathfrak{H}_{N}
-module for any
1\leq k\leq n
.

Proof.

Consider the case
k=1
when
V(\delta_{1})\cong\mathbb{C}^{N}

by Theorem
4.2
. The subspace
\mathbb{C}\{(r+\alpha)^{t}\otimes t^{r}:r\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}\}
is a nonzero proper submodule of
F^{\alpha}(V(\delta_{1}))
proving its reducibility.

Now we assume that
k\geq 2
. We have that

dimV(\delta_{k})={{N}\choose{k}}-{N\choose{k-2}}.

Claim 1
.

dimV(\delta_{k})>{2n-1\choose{k-1}}

for any
2\leq k\leq n
.

Proof of Claim 1.

We see that

\frac{{2n\choose{k}}-{2n\choose{k-2}}}{{2n-1\choose{k-1}}}=\frac{2n}{k-1}-\frac{2n(k-1)}{(2n-k+2)(2n-k+1)}.

Since the first term is strictly greater than
2
and
k\leq n
we get
n+1\leq 2n-k+1
. So we will have

\frac{2n(k-1)}{(2n-k+2)(2n-k+1)}\leq\frac{2n(k-1)}{(n+1)(n+2)}<2,

which implies the claim.

For any
r\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}
we consider the subspace

W^{k}_{r}=\mathbb{C}(r+\alpha)\bigwedge(\bigwedge^{k-1}\mathbb{C}^{N})\subseteq\bigwedge^{k}\mathbb{C}^{N}.

Then
dimW_{r}^{k}={N-1\choose{k-1}}
if
\alpha\neq-r
and
dimW_{-\alpha}^{k}=0
. By the Claim we have
{N\choose{k}}-{N\choose{k-2}}>{N-1\choose{k-1}}
which implies that
W_{r}^{k}\cap Ker(\theta_{k})\subsetneq Ker(\theta_{k})
.

Claim 2:
We will have
W_{r}^{k}\cap Ker(\theta_{k})\neq 0
for all
r\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}
with
r+\alpha\neq 0
.

Proof Claim 2.
For any
v\in\mathbb{C}^{N}\setminus\{0\}
, we define the hyperplane
v^{\perp}=\{w\in\mathbb{C}^{N}:(v,w)=0\}
and denote
L(v)=v^{\perp}\cap\bar{v}^{\perp}
. Then
dimL(V)\geq 2n-2
for any
v\in\mathbb{C}^{N}
. Now let
v_{1}\in L(r+\alpha)
be a nonzero vector and set
L(r+\alpha,v_{1})=L(r+\alpha)\cap L(v_{1})
. We get that
dim\;L(r+\alpha,v_{1})\geq 2(n-2)
. Let
v_{2}\in L(r+\alpha,v_{1})
. Then similarly we can define
L(r+\alpha,v_{1},v_{2})
, whose dimension is greater or equal than
2(n-3)
. Now continuing the process, we can construct
L(r+\alpha,v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{n-2})
, whose dimension is greater or equal than
2
. Let
v_{n-1}\in L(r+\alpha,v_{1}\dots,v_{n-2})
. Then it is easy to see that
0\neq(r+\alpha)\wedge v_{i_{1}}\wedge\dots v_{i_{k-1}}\in W_{r}^{k}\cap Ker(\theta_{k})
for
\{i_{1},\dots i_{k-1}\}\subseteq\{1,2,\dots n-1\}
and
1\leq k\leq n
.

Case I:
Assume that
\alpha\notin\mathbb{Z}^{N}
. In this case we have
W_{r}^{k}\cap Ker(\theta_{k})\neq 0
. Now take

P=\mathbb{C}\{W_{r}^{k}\cap Ker(\theta_{k})\otimes t^{r}:r\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}\}\subseteq F^{\alpha}(V(\delta_{k})).

This is a nonzero proper
\mathfrak{H}_{N}
-submodule of
F^{\alpha,\beta}(V(\delta_{k}))
.

Case II:
Now assume that
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}
and consider a subspace

P=\mathbb{C}\{W_{r}^{k}\cap Ker(\theta_{k})\otimes t^{r}:r\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}\setminus\{-\alpha\}\}\oplus Ker(\theta_{k})\otimes t^{-\alpha}.

This is again a nonzero proper
\mathfrak{H}_{N}-
submodule of
F^{\alpha,\beta}(V(\delta_{k}))
. This completes the proof of Theorem
4.3
.
∎

The main result of the paper follows from Theorem
2.2
, Proposition
4.1
and Theorem
4.3
.

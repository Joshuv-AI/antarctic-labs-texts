A note on invariants of mixed-state topological order in 2D
Yoshiko Ogata

Abstract
The classification of mixed-state topological order requires indices that behave monotonically under finite-depth quantum channels. In two dimensions, a braided C ∗ C^{*} -tensor category, which corresponds to strong symmetry, arises from a state satisfying approximate Haag duality. In this note, we show that the S S -matrix and topological twists of the braided C ∗ C^{*} -tensor category are quantities that are monotone under finite-depth quantum channels.

First we recall basic facts from

Let
{\mathcal{D}}
be a strict braided
C^{*}
-tensor category. We occasionally denote by
[\rho]

the isomorphic class of an object
\rho
in
{\mathcal{D}}
.
An object
\bar{\rho}\in{\mathop{\mathrm{Obj}}}{\mathcal{D}}
is said to be conjugate to
\rho\in{\mathop{\mathrm{Obj}}}{\mathcal{D}}

if there exist morphisms
R\in{\mathop{\mathrm{Mor}}}_{{\mathcal{D}}}\left(\mathbb{I},\bar{\rho}\otimes\rho\right)

and
\bar{R}\in{\mathop{\mathrm{Mor}}}_{{\mathcal{D}}}\left(\mathbb{I},\rho\otimes\bar{\rho}\right)

such that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\left(\bar{R}^{*}\otimes\mathop{\mathrm{id}}\nolimits_{\rho}\right)\left(\mathop{\mathrm{id}}\nolimits_{\rho}\otimes R\right)=\mathop{\mathrm{id}}\nolimits_{\rho},\quad\left(R^{*}\otimes\mathop{\mathrm{id}}\nolimits_{\bar{\rho}}\right)\left(\mathop{\mathrm{id}}\nolimits_{\bar{\rho}}\otimes\bar{R}\right)=\mathop{\mathrm{id}}\nolimits_{\bar{\rho}}.\end{split}

(2.1)

In this case, we say
(R,\bar{R})
is a solution of the conjugate equations for
(\rho,\bar{\rho})
.
We denote by
\Delta^{\mathcal{D}}
the set of all isomorphic classes of
simple objects with a conjugate in
{\mathcal{D}}
.
We fix a representative
{\tau_{c}}
for each

.

If

has a conjugate, its endomorphism space

is finite dimensional

[
NT13
]
).

can be written as a direct sum of simple objects. Namely,
there are a finite subset
\Delta_{\rho}\subset\Delta^{{\mathcal{D}}}

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathop{\mathrm{id}}\nolimits_{\rho}=\sum_{c\in\Delta_{\rho}}\sum_{\mu=1}^{n_{\rho,c}}u_{c}^{\rho}(\mu)u_{c}^{\rho}(\mu)^{*}.\end{split}

(2.2)

Recall that the class of objects in
{\mathcal{D}}
that have conjugates forms a

-tensor subcategory of
{\mathcal{D}}

(See Proposition 2.2.10
[
NT13
]

\displaystyle\begin{split}R=\sum_{c\in\Delta_{\rho}}\sum_{\mu=1}^{n_{\rho,c}}\left(\bar{u}_{c}^{\rho}(\mu)\otimes u_{c}^{\rho}(\mu)\right)R_{c},\\

Here,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}\nolimits_{\rho}(T)=R^{*}\left(\mathop{\mathrm{id}}\nolimits_{\bar{\rho}}\otimes T\right)R=\bar{R}^{*}\left(T\otimes\mathop{\mathrm{id}}\nolimits_{\rho}\right)\bar{R},\quad T\in{\mathop{\mathrm{End}}}_{{\mathcal{D}}}(\rho).\end{split}

This functional does not depend on the choice of the standard solution

(2.5)

Let

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
(R_{\rho},\bar{R}_{\rho})

(R_{\sigma},\bar{R}_{\sigma})

(\rho,\bar{\rho})
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\left(R_{\rho}^{*}\otimes\bar{R}_{\sigma}^{*}\right)\left(\mathop{\mathrm{id}}\nolimits_{\bar{\rho}}\otimes\epsilon(\sigma,\rho)\epsilon(\rho,\sigma)\otimes\mathop{\mathrm{id}}\nolimits_{\bar{\sigma}}\right)\left(R_{\rho}\otimes\bar{R}_{\sigma}\right)=\sum_{a\in\Delta_{\rho},b\in\Delta_{\sigma}}t_{a}^{\rho}s_{b}^{\sigma}S_{a,b}\end{split}

(2.7)

and

\displaystyle\sum_{a\in\Delta_{\rho}}d(a)t_{a}^{\rho}=\left\|R_{\rho}\right\|^{2},\quad\sum_{b\in\Delta_{\sigma}}d(b)s_{b}^{\sigma}=\left\|\bar{R}_{\sigma}\right\|^{2}.

Let
(R^{\prime}_{\rho},\bar{R}^{\prime}_{\rho})

(R^{\prime}_{\sigma},\bar{R}^{\prime}_{\sigma})

,

[

\displaystyle\begin{split}&R_{\rho}=\left(\mathop{\mathrm{id}}\nolimits_{\bar{\rho}}\otimes T^{*}\right)R^{\prime}_{\rho},\quad\bar{R}_{\rho}=\left(T^{-1}\otimes\mathop{\mathrm{id}}\nolimits_{\bar{\rho}}\right)\bar{R}^{\prime}_{\rho},\\

Substituting those, using (

&=\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}\nolimits_{\rho\otimes\sigma}\left(\left(T^{*}T\otimes({W^{*}W})^{-1}\right)\epsilon(\sigma,\rho)\epsilon(\rho,\sigma)\right)\end{split}

(2.10)

Note from the naturality of
\epsilon
and the decomposition (
2.2
),
we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\epsilon(\sigma,\rho)\epsilon(\rho,\sigma)=\sum_{a\in\Delta_{\rho}}\sum_{b\in\Delta_{\sigma}}\sum_{\mu=1}^{n_{\rho,a}}\sum_{\nu=1}^{n_{\sigma,b}}\left(u_{a}^{\rho}(\mu)\otimes u_{b}^{\sigma}(\nu)\right)\epsilon\left(\tau_{b},\tau_{a}\right)\epsilon\left(\tau_{a},\tau_{b}\right)\left(u_{a}^{\rho}(\mu)\otimes u_{b}^{\sigma}(\nu)\right)^{*}.\end{split}

(2.11)

Substituting this, we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\left(R_{\rho}^{*}\otimes\bar{R}_{\sigma}^{*}\right)\left(\mathop{\mathrm{id}}\nolimits_{\bar{\rho}}\otimes\epsilon(\sigma,\rho)\epsilon(\rho,\sigma)\otimes\mathop{\mathrm{id}}\nolimits_{\bar{\sigma}}\right)\left(R_{\rho}\otimes\bar{R}_{\sigma}\right)\\
&=\sum_{a\in\Delta_{\rho}}\sum_{b\in\Delta_{\sigma}}\sum_{\mu=1}^{n_{\rho,a}}\sum_{\nu=1}^{n_{\sigma,b}}\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}\nolimits_{\rho\otimes\sigma}\left(\left(T^{*}T\otimes({W^{*}W})^{-1}\right)\left(\left(u_{a}^{\rho}(\mu)\otimes u_{b}^{\sigma}(\nu)\right)\epsilon\left(\tau_{b},\tau_{a}\right)\epsilon\left(\tau_{a},\tau_{b}\right)\left(u_{a}^{\rho}(\mu)\otimes u_{b}^{\sigma}(\nu)\right)^{*}\right)\right)\\

(2.12)

Here, we used the fact that

\left(u_{b}^{\sigma}(\nu)\right)^{*}({W^{*}W})^{-1}u_{b}^{\sigma}(\nu)\in{\mathop{\mathrm{End}}}_{{\mathcal{D}}}(\tau_{b})

,

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\sum_{\mu=1}^{n_{\rho,a}}\left(u_{a}^{\rho}(\mu)\right)^{*}T^{*}Tu_{a}^{\rho}(\mu)\\

&=:t_{a}^{\rho}\end{split}

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\sum_{\nu=1}^{n_{\sigma,b}}\left(u_{b}^{\sigma}(\nu)\right)^{*}({W^{*}W})^{-1}u_{b}^{\sigma}(\nu)=d(b)^{-1}\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}\nolimits_{\sigma}\left(p_{b}^{\sigma}({W^{*}W})^{-1}\right)\\
&=:s_{b}^{\sigma}.\end{split}

Because

(2.15)

From the definition, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\sum_{a\in\Delta_{\rho}}d(a)t_{a}^{\rho}=\sum_{a\in\Delta_{\rho}}\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}\nolimits_{\rho}p_{a}^{\rho}T^{*}T=\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}\nolimits_{\rho}T^{*}T=\left\|R_{\rho}\right\|^{2},\\
&\sum_{b\in\Delta_{\rho}}d(b)s_{b}^{\sigma}=\sum_{b\in\Delta_{\rho}}\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}\nolimits_{\sigma}\left(p_{b}^{\sigma}({W^{*}W})^{-1}\right)=\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}\nolimits_{\sigma}\left(({W^{*}W})^{-1}\right)=\left\|\bar{R}_{\sigma}\right\|^{2}.\end{split}

(2.16)

Lemma 2.3

Let

Mixed state Approximate Haag duality

By a
2
-dimensional quantum spin system, we mean a
C^{*}
-algebra constructed as follows.
We denote the algebra of
d\times d
matrices by
\mathop{\mathrm{M}}\nolimits_{d}
with
d\geq 2
.
For each

{\mathcal{A}}_{\{z\}}
be an isomorphic copy of
\mathop{\mathrm{M}}\nolimits_{d}

\Lambda\subset{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}

{\mathcal{A}}_{\Lambda}=\bigotimes_{z\in\Lambda}{\mathcal{A}}_{\{z\}}

For finite
\Lambda

\bigotimes_{z\in\Lambda}{{\mathbb{C}}}^{d}

If
\Lambda_{1}\subset\Lambda_{2}

{\mathcal{A}}_{\Lambda_{1}}

{\mathcal{A}}_{\Lambda}
with
\Lambda

\Gamma
.

\Gamma
.

{\mathcal{A}}_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}}

\Gamma_{1}

\Gamma\subset{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}

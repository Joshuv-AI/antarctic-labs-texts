𝑯 ⁡ ( curl ) \boldsymbol{H}(\textbf{curl}) -reconstruction of piecewise polynomial fields with application to h ​ p hp -a posteriori nonconforming error analysis for Maxwell’s equations
Z. Dong, A. Ern

Abstract
We devise and analyse a novel 𝑯 ⁡ ( curl ) \boldsymbol{H}(\textbf{curl}) -reconstruction operator for piecewise polynomial fields on shape-regular simplicial meshes. The (non-polynomial) reconstruction is devised over the mesh vertex patches using the partition of unity induced by hat basis functions in combination with local Helmholtz decompositions. Our main focus is on homogeneous tangential boundary conditions. We prove that the difference between the reconstructed 𝑯 0 ​ ( curl ) \boldsymbol{H}_{0}(\textbf{curl}) -field and the original, piecewise polynomial field, measured in the broken curl norm and in the 𝑳 2 \boldsymbol{L}^{2} -norm, can be bounded in terms of suitable jump norms of the original field. The bounds are always h h -optimal, and p p -suboptimal by 1 2 \frac{1}{2} -order for the broken curl norm and by 3 2 \frac{3}{2} -order for the 𝑳 2 \boldsymbol{L}^{2} -norm. An auxiliary result of independent interest is a novel broken-curl, divergence-preserving Poincaré inequality on vertex patches. Moreover, the 𝑳 2 \boldsymbol{L}^{2} -norm estimate can be improved to 1 2 \frac{1}{2} -order suboptimality under a (reasonable) assumption on the uniform elliptic regularity pickup for a Poisson problem with Neumann conditions over the vertex patches. We also discuss extensions of the 𝑯 0 ​ ( curl ) \boldsymbol{H}_{0}(\textbf{curl}) -reconstruction operator to the prescription of mixed boundary conditions, to agglomerated polytopal meshes, and to convex domains. Finally, we showcase an important application of the 𝑯 ⁡ ( curl ) \boldsymbol{H}(\textbf{curl}) -reconstruction operator to the h ​ p hp -a posteriori nonconforming error analysis of Maxwell’s equations. We focus on the (symmetric) interior penalty discontinuous Galerkin (dG) approximation of some simplified forms of Maxwell’s equations.

Introduction

Given a piecewise polynomial field on a shape-regular simplicial mesh covering
exactly a Lipschitz polyhedral domain, we show in
the paper the existence of a (non-polynomial)
{{{\boldsymbol{H}}(\text{\bf curl})}}
-conforming field whose distance
to the original field in the broken curl norm and in the
{\boldsymbol{L}}^{2}
-norm is bounded in terms
of suitable jumps (and boundary values) of the original piecewise polynomial field,
namely its tangential jumps (and boundary values) and the normal jumps (and boundary values)
of its curl. All the estimates are local and

h
-optimal, and they are

p
-suboptimal by only
\frac{1}{2}
-order in the broken curl norm and by
\frac{3}{2}
-order
in the
{\boldsymbol{L}}^{2}
-norm. Here,
h

p

We emphasize that, as an auxiliary result to establish the
{\boldsymbol{L}}^{2}
-norm estimate,
we derive a novel broken-curl, divergence-preserving Poincaré inequality on
the mesh vertex patches that is of independent interest.
We also observe that the
{\boldsymbol{L}}^{2}
-estimate can be improved to
\frac{1}{2}
-order

p
-suboptimality
under a (reasonable) assumption on the uniform elliptic regularity pickup for a Poisson
problem with Neumann conditions over the mesh vertex patches.

We present an explicit construction of the (non-polynomial)
{{{\boldsymbol{H}}(\text{\bf curl})}}
-conforming field over the mesh vertex patches using the partition of unity induced by hat basis functions in combination with local Helmholtz decompositions. The crucial advantage of resorting to a local construction over the mesh vertex patches is that the associated subdomains have a trivial topology, thereby circumventing any dependence of the estimates on the topology of the original domain. For simplicity, we devise the construction so that the (non-polynomial)
{{{\boldsymbol{H}}(\text{\bf curl})}}
-conforming field satisfies homogeneous tangential boundary conditions, i.e., sits in
{{{\boldsymbol{H}}_{0}(\text{\bf curl};D)}}

hp
-estimates as on simplicial meshes can be established by considering the underlying simplicial submesh under the assumption that this latter mesh has locally the same size as the original polytopal mesh. Finally, we also discuss a variant where the
{{{\boldsymbol{H}}(\text{\bf curl})}}
-conforming field is defined globally without considering the mesh vertex patches. On convex domains, this leads to a
p
-optimal
{\boldsymbol{L}}^{2}
-estimate without any further assumption, but the estimate is only global and the constant depends on the topology of the global domain.

{\boldsymbol{\phi}}\in{\boldsymbol{X}}^{\mathrm{g}}(D_{{\mathbf{a}}})
such that

\nabla{\times}{\boldsymbol{\phi}}={\boldsymbol{v}}^{\mathrm{c}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}-{\boldsymbol{v}}
in
D_{{\mathbf{a}}}
and a stability constant
C(\kappa_{\mathcal{T}_{h}})
only depending on
the mesh shape-regularity so that the following bound holds:

\|{\nabla{\boldsymbol{\phi}}}\|_{D_{{\mathbf{a}}}}\leq C(\kappa_{\mathcal{T}_{h}})\|{\nabla{\times}{\boldsymbol{\phi}}}\|_{D_{{\mathbf{a}}}}=C(\kappa_{\mathcal{T}_{h}})\|{{\boldsymbol{v}}^{\mathrm{c}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}-{\boldsymbol{v}}}\|_{D_{{\mathbf{a}}}}.

(4.4)

(3) Integrating by parts the curl operator in each cell
K\in{\cal{T}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}
,
we infer that

\begin{split}\|{{\boldsymbol{v}}^{\mathrm{c}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}-{\boldsymbol{v}}}\|_{D_{{\mathbf{a}}}}^{2}&=({\boldsymbol{v}}^{\mathrm{c}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}-{\boldsymbol{v}},\nabla{\times}{\boldsymbol{\phi}})_{D_{{\mathbf{a}}}}\\
&=({\boldsymbol{v}}^{\mathrm{c}}_{{\mathbf{a}}},\nabla{\times}{\boldsymbol{\phi}})_{D_{{\mathbf{a}}}}-({\boldsymbol{v}},\nabla{\times}{\boldsymbol{\phi}})_{D_{{\mathbf{a}}}}\\
&=-(\nabla_{h}{\times}({\boldsymbol{v}}^{\mathrm{c}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}-{\boldsymbol{v}}),\tilde{{\boldsymbol{\phi}}})_{D_{{\mathbf{a}}}}-\sum_{K\in{\cal{T}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}}({\boldsymbol{v}},{\boldsymbol{n}}_{K}\times{\boldsymbol{\phi}})_{\partial K}=:T_{1}+T_{2},\end{split}

and it remains to bound
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
.

(3a) Bound on
T_{1}
. Using the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, recalling that
{\boldsymbol{\phi}}
has zero mean-value over
D_{{\mathbf{a}}}
, and invoking the Poincaré inequality on the vertex patch (see
[
38
, Theorem 3.2]
and also
[
21
, Lem. 5.7]
), we infer that

|T_{1}|\lesssim h_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|{\nabla_{h}{\times}({\boldsymbol{v}}^{\mathrm{c}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}-{\boldsymbol{v}})}\|_{D_{{\mathbf{a}}}}\|{\nabla{\boldsymbol{\phi}}}\|_{D_{{\mathbf{a}}}}\lesssim h_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|{\nabla_{h}{\times}({\boldsymbol{v}}^{\mathrm{c}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}-{\boldsymbol{v}})}\|_{D_{{\mathbf{a}}}}\|{{\boldsymbol{v}}^{\mathrm{c}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}-{\boldsymbol{v}}}\|_{D_{{\mathbf{a}}}},

where the second bound follows from (
4.4
).

(3b) Bound on
T_{2}
. Re-organizing the summation over the mesh faces in
\mathcal{F}_{{\mathbf{a}}}
and
\mathcal{F}_{{\mathbf{a}}}^{\partial}
(recall that these two sets are disjoint) and observing that
{\boldsymbol{\phi}}
has continuous traces over all the faces in
\mathcal{F}_{{\mathbf{a}}}
gives

T_{2}=\!\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{{\mathbf{a}}}}\!([\![{{\boldsymbol{v}}}]\!]^{\mathrm{c}}_{F},{\boldsymbol{n}}_{F}\times{\boldsymbol{\phi}})_{F}+\!\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{{\mathbf{a}}}^{\partial}}\!(\gamma_{K(F),F}^{\mathrm{c}}({\boldsymbol{v}}),{\boldsymbol{n}}_{K(F)}\times{\boldsymbol{\phi}})_{F}.

Invoking the same arguments as above together with the mesh shape-regularity and
a multiplicative trace inequality
(see, e.g.,
[
22
, Lem. 12.15]
), we infer that

\begin{split}|T_{2}|&\leq{}\bigg\{\!\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{{\mathbf{a}}}}\!h_{F}^{-1}\|{[\![{{\boldsymbol{v}}}]\!]^{\mathrm{c}}_{F}}\|_{F}^{2}+\!\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{{\mathbf{a}}}^{\partial}}\!h_{F}^{-1}\|{\gamma_{K(F),F}^{\mathrm{c}}({\boldsymbol{v}})}\|_{F}^{2}\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigg\{\!\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{{\mathbf{a}}}\cup\mathcal{F}_{{\mathbf{a}}}^{\partial}}\!h_{F}\|{{\boldsymbol{\phi}}}\|_{F}^{2}\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&\lesssim h_{{\mathbf{a}}}\bigg\{\!\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{{\mathbf{a}}}}\!h_{F}^{-1}\|{[\![{{\boldsymbol{v}}}]\!]^{\mathrm{c}}_{F}}\|_{F}^{2}+\!\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{{\mathbf{a}}}^{\partial}}\!h_{F}^{-1}\|{\gamma_{K(F),F}^{\mathrm{c}}({\boldsymbol{v}})}\|_{F}^{2}\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|{{\boldsymbol{v}}^{\mathrm{c}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}-{\boldsymbol{v}}}\|_{D_{{\mathbf{a}}}}.\end{split}

Putting the bounds on
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
together proves (
4.2
).
∎

4.3

with
\nabla_{h}{\times}{\boldsymbol{v}}\in{\boldsymbol{L}}^{2}(D_{{\mathbf{a}}})
and

({\boldsymbol{n}}_{K}\times{\boldsymbol{v}}|_{K})|_{F}\in{\boldsymbol{L}}^{2}(F)
for all
K\in{\cal{T}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}
and all

.

Remark 4.5

(Mixed boundary conditions)
.

For mixed boundary conditions, Lemma
4.3
is adapted as follows:
There exist a constant
C(\kappa_{\mathcal{T}_{h}})
so that, for all
{\mathbf{a}}\in\mathcal{V}_{h}
and all
{\boldsymbol{v}}\in{\boldsymbol{H}}^{1}({\cal{T}}_{{\mathbf{a}}})
, there exists
{\boldsymbol{v}}^{\mathrm{c}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}\in{\boldsymbol{H}}_{\partial D_{{\mathbf{a}}}\backslash\Gamma_{\rm N}}(\text{\bf curl};D_{{\mathbf{a}}})
satisfying
{\boldsymbol{v}}^{\mathrm{c}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}-{\boldsymbol{v}}\in{\boldsymbol{H}}_{\partial D_{{\mathbf{a}}}\cap\Gamma_{\rm N}}(\text{\rm div}=0;D_{{\mathbf{a}}})
such that

\begin{split}\|{\boldsymbol{v}}^{\mathrm{c}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}-{\boldsymbol{v}}\|_{D_{{\mathbf{a}}}}\leq{}&C(\kappa_{\mathcal{T}_{h}})h_{{\mathbf{a}}}\bigg\{\|\nabla_{h}{\times}({\boldsymbol{v}}^{\mathrm{c}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}-{\boldsymbol{v}})\|_{D_{{\mathbf{a}}}}^{2}+\!\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{{\mathbf{a}}}\backslash{\mathcal{F}}_{h}^{\rm N}}\!h_{F}^{-1}\|{[\![{{\boldsymbol{v}}}]\!]^{\mathrm{c}}_{F}}\|_{F}^{2}\\
&+\!\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{{\mathbf{a}}}^{\partial}\backslash{\mathcal{F}}_{h}^{\rm N}}\!h_{F}^{-1}\|{\gamma_{K(F),F}^{\mathrm{c}}({\boldsymbol{v}})}\|_{F}^{2}\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{2}}.\end{split}

To prove this inequality, one adapts the above proof as follows: First,
[
2
, Lemma 3.5]
can be replaced by
[
28
, Theorem 3]
on regular decompositions with mixed boundary conditions, but the stability constant is not given explicitly in terms of the geometry of
D_{{\mathbf{a}}}
. To have a stability constant only depending on the mesh shape-regularity, one needs to conjecture that the result of
[
27
]
can be extended to mixed boundary conditions. We notice that the extension holds for pure Neumann conditions, as shown in
[
27
, Theorem 32]
.

Remark 4.6

.

One can adapt the proof of Lemma
4.3
to establish the following local broken-divergence, curl-preserving Poincaré inequality:
There exists a constant
C(\kappa_{\mathcal{T}_{h}})
so that, for all
{\mathbf{a}}\in\mathcal{V}_{h}
and all
{\boldsymbol{v}}\in{\boldsymbol{H}}^{1}({\cal{T}}_{{\mathbf{a}}})
, there exist
{\boldsymbol{v}}^{\mathrm{c}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}\in{{{\boldsymbol{H}}_{0}(\text{\rm div};D_{{\mathbf{a}}})}}
satisfying
{\boldsymbol{v}}^{\mathrm{c}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}-{\boldsymbol{v}}\in{{{\boldsymbol{H}}(\text{\bf curl}={\boldsymbol{0}};D_{{\mathbf{a}}})}}
such that,

\|{\boldsymbol{v}}^{\mathrm{c}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}-{\boldsymbol{v}}\|_{D_{{\mathbf{a}}}}\!\!\leq\!{}C(\kappa_{\mathcal{T}_{h}})h_{{\mathbf{a}}}\bigg\{\!\|\nabla_{h}{\cdot}({\boldsymbol{v}}^{\mathrm{c}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}-{\boldsymbol{v}})\|_{D_{{\mathbf{a}}}}^{2}\!+\!\!\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{{\mathbf{a}}}}\!\!h_{F}^{-1}\|{[\![{{\boldsymbol{v}}}]\!]^{\mathrm{d}}_{F}}\|_{F}^{2}+\!\!\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{{\mathbf{a}}}^{\partial}}\!\!h_{F}^{-1}\|{\gamma_{K(F),F}^{\mathrm{d}}({\boldsymbol{v}})}\|_{F}^{2}\!\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Lemma 4.7

(Local Sobolev embedding)
.

Under Assumption
3.5
,
there exists a constant
C(\kappa_{\mathcal{T}_{h}})
such that, for all
{\mathbf{a}}\in\mathcal{V}_{h}
and all
{\boldsymbol{v}}\in{\boldsymbol{X}^{\mathrm{c}}}(D_{{\mathbf{a}}})
(see (
3.2b
)),

|{\boldsymbol{v}}|_{{\boldsymbol{H}}^{\frac{1}{2}+\delta}(K)}\leq C(\kappa_{\mathcal{T}_{h}})h_{{\mathbf{a}}}^{\frac{1}{2}-\delta}\|{\nabla{\times}{\boldsymbol{v}}}\|_{D_{{\mathbf{a}}}}\quad\forall K\in{\cal{T}}_{{\mathbf{a}}},

(4.5)

where the real number
\delta:=\delta(\kappa_{\mathcal{T}_{h}})\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
comes from Assumption
3.5
.

Proof.

(1) Let
{\mathbf{a}}\in\mathcal{V}_{h}
and
{\boldsymbol{v}}\in{\boldsymbol{X}^{\mathrm{c}}}(D_{{\mathbf{a}}})
. Invoking the same arguments as in Step (2) of the proof of Lemma
4.3
, we infer that there exists
{\boldsymbol{\phi}}\in{\boldsymbol{X}}^{\mathrm{g}}(D_{{\mathbf{a}}})
such that
\nabla{\times}{\boldsymbol{\phi}}=\nabla{\times}{\boldsymbol{v}}
and
\nabla{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{\phi}}=0

in
D_{{\mathbf{a}}}
, and

\|{\nabla{\boldsymbol{\phi}}}\|_{D_{{\mathbf{a}}}}\leq C(\kappa_{\mathcal{T}_{h}})\|{\nabla{\times}{\boldsymbol{\phi}}}\|_{D_{{\mathbf{a}}}}=C(\kappa_{\mathcal{T}_{h}})\|{\nabla{\times}{\boldsymbol{v}}}\|_{D_{{\mathbf{a}}}}.

(4.6)

Since
\nabla{\times}({\boldsymbol{v}}-{\boldsymbol{\phi}})={\boldsymbol{0}}
, there exists a function
p\in X^{\mathrm{g}}(D_{{\mathbf{a}}})
such that
\nabla p={\boldsymbol{v}}-{\boldsymbol{\phi}}
. Altogether, this leads to the regular decomposition

{\boldsymbol{v}}=\nabla p+{\boldsymbol{\phi}},\quad p\in X^{\mathrm{g}}(D_{{\mathbf{a}}}),\quad{\boldsymbol{\phi}}\in{\boldsymbol{X}}^{\mathrm{g}}(D_{{\mathbf{a}}}).

(2) Since
{\boldsymbol{v}}\in{{{\boldsymbol{H}}_{0}(\text{\rm div}=0;D)}}
by assumption, we infer
that
(\nabla p,\!\nabla\rho)_{D_{{\mathbf{a}}}}\!\!=\!({\boldsymbol{\phi}},\!\nabla\rho)_{D_{{\mathbf{a}}}}
for all
\rho\in X^{\mathrm{d}}(D_{{\mathbf{a}}})
. Therefore, we can apply Assumption
3.5

to
{\boldsymbol{g}}:={\boldsymbol{\phi}}
, and observing that
\zeta_{{\boldsymbol{g}}}=p
, we infer that

h_{\mathbf{a}}^{\frac{3}{2}+\delta}|\nabla p|_{{\boldsymbol{H}}^{\frac{1}{2}+\delta}(D_{{\mathbf{a}}})}\lesssim h_{\mathbf{a}}^{2}\|\nabla{\boldsymbol{\phi}}\|_{D_{{\mathbf{a}}}}\lesssim h_{\mathbf{a}}^{2}\|\nabla{\times}{\boldsymbol{v}}\|_{D_{{\mathbf{a}}}},

where the second bound follows from (
4.6
). Since
|\nabla p|_{{\boldsymbol{H}}^{\frac{1}{2}+\delta}(K)}\leq|\nabla p|_{{\boldsymbol{H}}^{\frac{1}{2}+\delta}(D_{{\mathbf{a}}})}
for all
K\in{\cal{T}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}
, we infer that

|\nabla p|_{{\boldsymbol{H}}^{\frac{1}{2}+\delta}(K)}\lesssim h_{\mathbf{a}}^{\frac{1}{2}-\delta}\|\nabla{\times}{\boldsymbol{v}}\|_{D_{{\mathbf{a}}}}.

(4.7)

(3) For all
K\in{\cal{T}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}
, invoking the geometric mapping to the reference simplex
{\widehat{K}}

and the embedding
|\widehat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}|_{{\boldsymbol{H}}^{\frac{1}{2}}({\widehat{K}})}\leq{\widehat{C}}\|\nabla\widehat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}\|_{{\widehat{K}}}
, we infer that

|{\boldsymbol{\phi}}|_{{\boldsymbol{H}}^{\frac{1}{2}+\delta}(K)}\lesssim h_{K}^{\frac{1}{2}-\delta}\|\nabla{\boldsymbol{\phi}}\|_{K}\leq h_{K}^{\frac{1}{2}-\delta}\|\nabla{\boldsymbol{\phi}}\|_{D_{{\mathbf{a}}}}\lesssim h_{\mathbf{a}}^{\frac{1}{2}-\delta}\|\nabla{\times}{\boldsymbol{v}}\|_{D_{{\mathbf{a}}}},

where the last bound follows again from (
4.6
) and
h_{K}\leq h_{\mathbf{a}}
.
Combining this bound with (
4.7
) and invoking the triangle inequality concludes the proof.
∎

,
3.4
,

5.1
Proof of Lemma
3.3

Let
{\mathbf{a}}\in\mathcal{V}_{h}
. Our goal is bound the error
{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}:={\boldsymbol{E}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}-\psi_{{\mathbf{a}}}{\boldsymbol{E}}_{h}
defined in (
3.9
) in the broken curl norm. Specifically, our goal is to prove that

\|\nabla_{h}{\times}{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|_{D_{{\mathbf{a}}}}\leq{}C\bigg\{\!\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{{\mathbf{a}}}}\!\Big(\frac{h_{F}}{p}\Big)\|{[\![{\nabla{\times}{\boldsymbol{E}}_{h}}]\!]^{\mathrm{d}}_{F}}\|_{F}^{2}+\!\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{{\mathbf{a}}}}\!\Big(\frac{p^{2}}{h_{F}}\Big)\|{[\![{{\boldsymbol{E}}_{h}}]\!]^{\mathrm{c}}_{F}}\|_{F}^{2}\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

To this purpose, we apply the Helmholtz decomposition (
4.1a
) to
{\boldsymbol{v}}:=\nabla_{h}{\times}{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}
in the vertex patch
D_{{\mathbf{a}}}
. This
gives
{\boldsymbol{\psi}}\in{\boldsymbol{X}^{\mathrm{c}}_{0}}(D_{{\mathbf{a}}})
and
\xi\in X^{\rm g}(D_{{\mathbf{a}}})
such that

\nabla_{h}{\times}{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}=\nabla_{\!0}{\times}{\boldsymbol{\psi}}+\nabla\xi
in
D_{{\mathbf{a}}}
. Taking the
{\boldsymbol{L}}^{2}(D_{{\mathbf{a}}})
-inner product with
\nabla_{h}{\times}{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}
, we infer that

\|{\nabla_{h}{\times}{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}}\|_{D_{{\mathbf{a}}}}^{2}=(\nabla_{h}{\times}{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{{\mathbf{a}}},\nabla_{\!0}{\times}{\boldsymbol{\psi}})_{D_{{\mathbf{a}}}}+(\nabla_{h}{\times}{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{{\mathbf{a}}},\nabla\xi)_{D_{{\mathbf{a}}}}=:\mbox{I}+\mbox{II},

(5.1)

and it remains to bound
I
and
II
.

(1) Bound on
I
. Since
\nabla_{h}{\times}{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}=\nabla_{\!0}{\times}{\boldsymbol{E}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}-\nabla_{h}{\times}(\psi_{{\mathbf{a}}}{\boldsymbol{E}}_{h})=\nabla_{\!0}{\times}{\boldsymbol{U}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}-\nabla_{h}{\times}(\psi_{{\mathbf{a}}}{\boldsymbol{E}}_{h})
owing to the definition (
3.6
) of
{\boldsymbol{E}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}
, we have

\mbox{I}=(\nabla_{\!0}{\times}{\boldsymbol{U}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}-\nabla_{h}{\times}(\psi_{{\mathbf{a}}}{\boldsymbol{E}}_{h}),\nabla_{\!0}{\times}{\boldsymbol{\psi}})_{D_{{\mathbf{a}}}}.

Recalling the definition (
3.4
) of
{\boldsymbol{U}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}
and since

{\boldsymbol{\psi}}\in{\boldsymbol{X}^{\mathrm{c}}_{0}}(D_{{\mathbf{a}}})
, this gives

\mbox{I}=({\boldsymbol{L}}_{h}^{p}(\psi_{{\mathbf{a}}}{\boldsymbol{E}}_{h}),\nabla_{\!0}{\times}{\boldsymbol{\psi}})_{D_{{\mathbf{a}}}}.

Invoking the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, the stability bound (
4.1b
) and the fact that
\psi_{{\mathbf{a}}}
is continuous and bounded by one, we infer that

\begin{split}\mbox{I}\leq\|{{\boldsymbol{L}}_{h}^{p}(\psi_{{\mathbf{a}}}{\boldsymbol{E}}_{h})}\|_{D_{{\mathbf{a}}}}\|{\nabla_{\!0}{\times}{\boldsymbol{\psi}}}\|_{D_{{\mathbf{a}}}}&\lesssim\bigg\{\!\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{{\mathbf{a}}}}\!\Big(\frac{p^{2}}{h_{F}}\Big)\|{[\![{\psi_{\mathbf{a}}{\boldsymbol{E}}_{h}}]\!]^{\mathrm{c}}_{F}}\|_{F}^{2}\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|{\nabla_{\!0}{\times}{\boldsymbol{\psi}}}\|_{D_{{\mathbf{a}}}}\\
&\lesssim\bigg\{\!\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{{\mathbf{a}}}}\!\Big(\frac{p^{2}}{h_{F}}\Big)\|{[\![{{\boldsymbol{E}}_{h}}]\!]^{\mathrm{c}}_{F}}\|_{F}^{2}\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|{\nabla_{h}{\times}{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}}\|_{D_{{\mathbf{a}}}},\end{split}

(5.2)

where we used (
4.1b
) in the last bound.

(2) Bound on
II
. Since
(\nabla_{\!0}{\times}{\boldsymbol{E}}_{{\mathbf{a}}},\nabla\xi)_{D_{{\mathbf{a}}}}=0
, we have

\mbox{II}=-(\nabla_{h}{\times}(\psi_{{\mathbf{a}}}{\boldsymbol{E}}_{h}),\nabla\xi)_{D_{{\mathbf{a}}}}.

Let
{{\mathcal{I}}}^{{\rm g}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}
be the modified
hp
-Karkulik–Melenk interpolation operator on the vertex patch
D_{{\mathbf{a}}}
from Lemma
2.3
. We have

\mbox{II}=(\nabla_{h}{\times}(\psi_{{\mathbf{a}}}{\boldsymbol{E}}_{h}),\nabla(\xi-{{\mathcal{I}}}^{{\rm g}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}(\xi)))_{D_{{\mathbf{a}}}}+(\nabla_{h}{\times}(\psi_{{\mathbf{a}}}{\boldsymbol{E}}_{h}),\nabla{{\mathcal{I}}}^{{\rm g}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}(\xi))_{D_{{\mathbf{a}}}}=:\mbox{II}_{1}+\mbox{II}_{2},

and we bound the two terms on the right-hand side.

(2a) Bound on
\mbox{II}_{1}
.
We integrate by parts the gradient operator in all the cells composing
{\cal{T}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}
.
This gives

\mbox{II}_{1}=\sum_{K\in{\cal{T}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}}(\xi-{{\mathcal{I}}}^{{\rm g}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}(\xi),{\boldsymbol{n}}_{K}{\cdot}(\nabla_{h}{\times}(\psi_{{\mathbf{a}}}{\boldsymbol{E}}_{h})))_{\partial K}.

All the mesh
faces in
\bigcup_{K\in{\cal{T}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}}{\mathcal{F}}_{K}
are either in
\mathcal{F}_{{\mathbf{a}}}
or in
\mathcal{F}_{{\mathbf{a}}}^{\partial}
. In the first
case, we observe that
\xi-{{\mathcal{I}}}^{{\rm g}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}(\xi)
is single-valued across every interface
F\in\mathcal{F}_{{\mathbf{a}}}\cap{\mathcal{F}}_{h}^{\mathrm{int}}
and that we have set conventionally
[\![{\bullet}]\!]^{\mathrm{d}}_{F}:=\bullet|_{F}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{n}}_{D}
on every boundary face
F\in\mathcal{F}_{{\mathbf{a}}}\cap{\mathcal{F}}_{h}^{\mathrm{bd}}
. In the second case,
\psi_{{\mathbf{a}}}
vanishes identically on the face in question,
and so does the normal component of
\nabla_{h}{\times}(\psi_{{\mathbf{a}}}{\boldsymbol{E}}_{h})
. Altogether, this gives

\displaystyle\mbox{II}_{1}={}

\displaystyle\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{{\mathbf{a}}}}(\xi-{{\mathcal{I}}}^{{\rm g}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}(\xi),[\![{\nabla_{h}{\times}(\psi_{{\mathbf{a}}}{\boldsymbol{E}}_{h})}]\!]^{\mathrm{d}}_{F})_{F}.

We have
\nabla_{h}{\times}(\psi_{{\mathbf{a}}}{\boldsymbol{E}}_{h})=\psi_{{\mathbf{a}}}\nabla_{h}{\times}{\boldsymbol{E}}_{h}+(\nabla\psi_{{\mathbf{a}}})\times{\boldsymbol{E}}_{h}

so that

[\![{\nabla_{h}{\times}\psi_{{\mathbf{a}}}{\boldsymbol{E}}_{h}}]\!]^{\mathrm{d}}_{F}=\psi_{\mathbf{a}}[\![{\nabla_{h}{\times}{\boldsymbol{E}}_{h}}]\!]^{\mathrm{d}}_{F}+\nabla\psi_{\mathbf{a}}{\cdot}[\![{{\boldsymbol{E}}_{h}}]\!]^{\mathrm{c}}_{F},

since

\psi_{{\mathbf{a}}}
is continuous and
({\boldsymbol{n}}_{F}{\times}\nabla\psi_{{\mathbf{a}}})_{F}
is single-valued across every interface
F\in\mathcal{F}_{{\mathbf{a}}}\cap{\mathcal{F}}_{h}^{\mathrm{int}}
. This gives

\displaystyle\mbox{II}_{1}={}

\displaystyle-\!\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{{\mathbf{a}}}}\!(\xi-{{\mathcal{I}}}^{{\rm g}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}(\xi),\psi_{{\mathbf{a}}}[\![{\nabla_{h}{\times}{\boldsymbol{E}}_{h}}]\!]^{\mathrm{d}}_{F})_{F}-\!\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{{\mathbf{a}}}}\!(\xi-{{\mathcal{I}}}^{{\rm g}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}(\xi),\nabla\psi_{{\mathbf{a}}}{\cdot}[\![{{\boldsymbol{E}}_{h}}]\!]^{\mathrm{c}}_{F})_{F}.

Let
\mbox{II}_{11}
and
\mbox{II}_{12}
denote the two summations on the right-hand side.
Using the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and
\|\psi_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|_{L^{\infty}(F)}=1
, we infer that

\displaystyle|\mbox{II}_{11}|\leq{}

\displaystyle\bigg\{\!\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{{\mathbf{a}}}}\!\Big(\frac{h_{F}}{p}\Big)\|{[\![{\nabla_{h}{\times}{\boldsymbol{E}}_{h}}]\!]^{\mathrm{d}}_{F}}\|_{F}^{2}\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigg\{\!\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{{\mathbf{a}}}}\!\Big(\frac{p}{h_{F}}\Big)\|{\xi-{{\mathcal{I}}}^{{\rm g}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}(\xi)}\|_{F}^{2}\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

We now invoke the approximation result (
2.8
)
on
{{\mathcal{I}}}^{{\rm g}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}
. For every
F\in\mathcal{F}_{{\mathbf{a}}}
, we can pick a mesh cell
K\in{\cal{T}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}
of which
F
is a face and obtain

\Big(\frac{p}{h_{F}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|{\xi-{{\mathcal{I}}}^{{\rm g}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}(\xi)}\|_{F}\lesssim\|\nabla\xi\|_{K}\leq\|\nabla\xi\|_{D_{{\mathbf{a}}}}\leq\|{\nabla_{h}{\times}{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}}\|_{D_{{\mathbf{a}}}},

where we used (
4.1b
) in the last bound.
This gives

|\mbox{II}_{11}|\lesssim\bigg\{\!\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{{\mathbf{a}}}}\!\Big(\frac{h_{F}}{p}\Big)\|{[\![{\nabla_{h}{\times}{\boldsymbol{E}}_{h}}]\!]^{\mathrm{d}}_{F}}\|_{F}^{2}\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|{\nabla_{h}{\times}{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}}\|_{D_{{\mathbf{a}}}}.

Moreover, using the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and
\|\nabla\psi_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|_{L^{\infty}(F)}\lesssim h^{-1}_{F}
gives

\displaystyle|\mbox{II}_{12}|\lesssim{}

\displaystyle\bigg\{\!\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{{\mathbf{a}}}}\!\Big(\frac{h_{F}}{p}\Big)h_{F}^{-2}\|{[\![{{\boldsymbol{E}}_{h}}]\!]^{\mathrm{c}}_{F}}\|_{F}^{2}\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigg\{\!\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{{\mathbf{a}}}}\!\Big(\frac{p}{h_{F}}\Big)\|{\xi-{{\mathcal{I}}}^{{\rm g}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}(\xi)}\|_{F}^{2}\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Invoking the same arguments as above to bound the factor involving
\xi
, we obtain

\displaystyle|\mbox{II}_{12}|\lesssim{}

\displaystyle p^{-\frac{3}{2}}\bigg\{\!\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{{\mathbf{a}}}}\!\Big(\frac{p^{2}}{h_{F}}\Big)\|{[\![{{\boldsymbol{E}}_{h}}]\!]^{\mathrm{c}}_{F}}\|_{F}^{2}\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|{\nabla_{h}{\times}{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}}\|_{D_{{\mathbf{a}}}}.

Putting everything together, we conclude that

\displaystyle|\mbox{II}_{1}|\lesssim{}

\displaystyle\bigg\{\!\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{{\mathbf{a}}}}\!\Big(\frac{h_{F}}{p}\Big)\|{[\![{\nabla_{h}{\times}{\boldsymbol{E}}_{h}}]\!]^{\mathrm{d}}_{F}}\|_{F}^{2}+p^{-3}\Big(\frac{p^{2}}{h_{F}}\Big)\|{[\![{{\boldsymbol{E}}_{h}}]\!]^{\mathrm{c}}_{F}}\|_{F}^{2}\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|{\nabla_{h}{\times}{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}}\|_{D_{{\mathbf{a}}}}.

(2b) Bound on
\mbox{II}_{2}
.
This time we integrate by parts the curl operator in all the cells composing
{\cal{T}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}
.
Using similar arguments as above, we obtain

\mbox{II}_{2}=\sum_{K\in{\cal{T}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}}(\psi_{\mathbf{a}}{\boldsymbol{E}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{n}}_{K}\times\nabla{{\mathcal{I}}}^{{\rm g}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}(\xi))_{\partial K}=\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{{\mathbf{a}}}}(\psi_{{\mathbf{a}}}[\![{{\boldsymbol{E}}_{h}}]\!]^{\mathrm{c}}_{F},\nabla{{\mathcal{I}}}^{{\rm g}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}(\xi))_{F}.

Invoking the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality,
the discrete trace inequality (
2.6
), and
\|\psi_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|_{L^{\infty}(F)}=1
,
we infer that

\displaystyle|\mbox{II}_{2}|\leq{}

\displaystyle\bigg\{\!\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{{\mathbf{a}}}}\!\Big(\frac{p^{2}}{h_{K}}\Big)\|{[\![{{\boldsymbol{E}}_{h}}]\!]^{\mathrm{c}}_{F}}\|_{F}^{2}\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigg\{\!\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{{\mathbf{a}}}}\Big(\frac{h_{K}}{p^{2}}\Big)\|{\nabla{{\mathcal{I}}}^{{\rm g}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}(\xi)}\|_{F}^{2}\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim{}

\displaystyle\bigg\{\!\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{{\mathbf{a}}}}\!\Big(\frac{p^{2}}{h_{K}}\Big)\|{[\![{{\boldsymbol{E}}_{h}}]\!]^{\mathrm{c}}_{F}}\|_{F}^{2}\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigg\{\!\sum_{K\in{\cal{T}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}}\!\|{\nabla{{\mathcal{I}}}^{{\rm g}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}(\xi)}\|_{K}^{2}\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim{}

\displaystyle\bigg\{\!\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{{\mathbf{a}}}}\!\Big(\frac{p^{2}}{h_{K}}\Big)\|{[\![{{\boldsymbol{E}}_{h}}]\!]^{\mathrm{c}}_{F}}\|_{F}^{2}\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|{\nabla\xi}\|_{D_{{\mathbf{a}}}},

where the last bound follows from the
H^{1}
-stability of
{{\mathcal{I}}}^{{\rm g}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}

(see (
2.8
)).
Proceeding as above to bound
\|\nabla\xi\|_{D_{{\mathbf{a}}}}
, we conclude that

|\mbox{II}_{2}|\lesssim\bigg\{\!\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{{\mathbf{a}}}}\!\Big(\frac{p^{2}}{h_{K}}\Big)\|{[\![{{\boldsymbol{E}}_{h}}]\!]^{\mathrm{c}}_{F}}\|_{F}^{2}\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|{\nabla_{h}{\times}{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}}\|_{D_{{\mathbf{a}}}}.

(3) Putting the bounds on
\mbox{II}_{1}
and
\mbox{II}_{2}
together and since
p\geq 1
, we conclude the proof.

5.2
Proof of Lemma
3.4

We want to prove that

\|{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|_{D_{{\mathbf{a}}}}\lesssim p\bigg\{\!\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{{\mathbf{a}}}}\!\Big(\frac{h_{F}}{p}\Big)^{3}\|{[\![{\nabla_{h}{\times}{\boldsymbol{E}}_{h}}]\!]^{\mathrm{d}}_{F}}\|_{F}^{2}+h_{F}\|{[\![{{\boldsymbol{E}}_{h}}]\!]^{\mathrm{c}}_{F}}\|_{F}^{2}\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

We apply the local broken-curl, divergence-preserving Poincaré inequality to

{\boldsymbol{v}}:=\psi_{{\mathbf{a}}}{\boldsymbol{E}}_{h}\in{\boldsymbol{P}}^{\mathrm{b}}_{p+1}({\cal{T}}_{{\mathbf{a}}})\subset{\boldsymbol{H}}^{1}({\cal{T}}_{{\mathbf{a}}})
and select

{\boldsymbol{v}}^{\mathrm{c}}_{\mathbf{a}}:={\boldsymbol{E}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}
, which indeed satisfies
{\boldsymbol{v}}^{\mathrm{c}}_{\mathbf{a}}\in{{{\boldsymbol{H}}_{0}(\text{\bf curl};D_{{\mathbf{a}}})}}

and
{\boldsymbol{v}}^{\mathrm{c}}_{\mathbf{a}}-{\boldsymbol{v}}=\psi_{{\mathbf{a}}}{\boldsymbol{E}}_{h}-{\boldsymbol{U}}_{\mathbf{a}}-\nabla\theta_{\mathbf{a}}\in{{{\boldsymbol{H}}(\text{\rm div}=0;D_{{\mathbf{a}}})}}

since
{\boldsymbol{U}}_{\mathbf{a}}\in{{{\boldsymbol{H}}(\text{\rm div}=0;D_{{\mathbf{a}}})}}
by construction and
\psi_{{\mathbf{a}}}{\boldsymbol{E}}_{h}-\nabla\theta_{\mathbf{a}}\in{{{\boldsymbol{H}}(\text{\rm div}=0;D_{{\mathbf{a}}})}}
by definition of
\theta_{\mathbf{a}}
.
Observing that
{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}={\boldsymbol{E}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}-\psi_{{\mathbf{a}}}{\boldsymbol{E}}_{h}={\boldsymbol{v}}^{\mathrm{c}}_{\mathbf{a}}-{\boldsymbol{v}}
, we infer that

\displaystyle\|{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|_{D_{{\mathbf{a}}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim h_{\mathbf{a}}\bigg\{\|\nabla_{h}{\times}{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{\mathbf{a}}\|_{D_{{\mathbf{a}}}}+\!\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{{\mathbf{a}}}}\!h_{F}^{-1}\|{[\![{\psi_{\mathbf{a}}{\boldsymbol{E}}_{h}}]\!]^{\mathrm{c}}_{F}}\|_{F}^{2}+\!\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{{\mathbf{a}}}^{\partial}}\!h_{F}^{-1}\|{\gamma_{K(F),F}^{\mathrm{c}}(\psi_{\mathbf{a}}{\boldsymbol{E}}_{h})}\|_{F}^{2}\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq h_{\mathbf{a}}\bigg\{\|\nabla_{h}{\times}{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{\mathbf{a}}\|_{D_{{\mathbf{a}}}}+\!\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{{\mathbf{a}}}}\!h_{F}^{-1}\|{[\![{{\boldsymbol{E}}_{h}}]\!]^{\mathrm{c}}_{F}}\|_{F}^{2}\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{2}},

where we used that
\psi_{\mathbf{a}}
is a continuous function over
D_{{\mathbf{a}}}
, vanishing on
\partial D_{{\mathbf{a}}}
and bounded by one.
Invoking (
3.10
) to bound the first term on the right-hand side involving the broken curl and using the mesh shape-regularity gives

\|{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|_{D_{{\mathbf{a}}}}\lesssim\bigg\{\!\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{{\mathbf{a}}}}\!\Big(\frac{h_{F}^{3}}{p}\Big)\|{[\![{\nabla{\times}{\boldsymbol{E}}_{h}}]\!]^{\mathrm{d}}_{F}}\|_{F}^{2}+p^{2}h_{F}\|{[\![{{\boldsymbol{E}}_{h}}]\!]^{\mathrm{c}}_{F}}\|_{F}^{2}\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Factoring out
p^{2}
completes the proof.

Remark 5.1

(Definition of
{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{\mathbf{a}}
)
.

The above proof highlights the relevance of the local problem (
3.5
) defining the curl-free part of
{\boldsymbol{E}}_{\mathbf{a}}
, i.e.,
\nabla\theta_{\mathbf{a}}
.

5.3
Proof of Lemma
3.6

We want to prove that, provided Assumption
3.5
holds true,

\|{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|_{D_{{\mathbf{a}}}}\lesssim\bigg\{\!\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{{\mathbf{a}}}}\!h_{F}\|{[\![{{\boldsymbol{E}}_{h}}]\!]^{\mathrm{c}}_{F}}\|_{F}^{2}\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

We use the Aubin–Nitsche duality argument to bound
{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}
in the
{\boldsymbol{L}}^{2}
-norm on the vertex patch
D_{{\mathbf{a}}}
. Specifically, we consider the following dual problem: Find
{\boldsymbol{Z}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}\in{\boldsymbol{X}^{\mathrm{c}}_{0}}(D_{{\mathbf{a}}})
such that

\displaystyle\nabla{\times}(\nabla_{\!0}{\times}{\boldsymbol{Z}}_{{\mathbf{a}}})

\displaystyle={\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}

\displaystyle\text{in $D_{{\mathbf{a}}}$},

(5.3a)

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{Z}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}{\times}{\boldsymbol{n}}_{D_{{\mathbf{a}}}}

\displaystyle={\boldsymbol{0}}

\displaystyle\text{on $\partial D_{{\mathbf{a}}}$}.

(5.3b)

This problem is well-posed since
D_{{\mathbf{a}}}
has trivial topology and
{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}
is divergence-free. Indeed,
\nabla{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}=\nabla{\cdot}({\boldsymbol{E}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}-(\psi_{{\mathbf{a}}}{\boldsymbol{E}}_{h}))=\nabla{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{U}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}+\nabla{\cdot}(\nabla\theta_{{\mathbf{a}}}-\psi_{{\mathbf{a}}}{\boldsymbol{E}}_{h})=0
since
{\boldsymbol{U}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}
is divergence-free by construction and the definition (
3.5
) of
\theta_{{\mathbf{a}}}
implies that
\nabla\theta_{{\mathbf{a}}}-\psi_{{\mathbf{a}}}{\boldsymbol{E}}_{h}
is divergence-free as well.
Recalling the regularity pickup from
[
16
, Section 5.2]
, and
[
23
, Lemma 44.2]
, we infer that there is
\delta_{\mathbf{a}}\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
(here,
\delta_{\mathbf{a}}
can depend on
{\mathbf{a}}\in\mathcal{V}_{h}
) so that

\nabla_{\!0}{\times}{\boldsymbol{Z}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}\in{\boldsymbol{H}}^{\frac{1}{2}+\delta}(D_{{\mathbf{a}}})
. This implies in particular that
\nabla_{\!0}{\times}{\boldsymbol{Z}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}
has meaningful (tangential) traces in
{\boldsymbol{L}}^{2}(\partial K)
for all
K\in{\cal{T}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}
. Taking the
{\boldsymbol{L}}^{2}(D_{{\mathbf{a}}})
-inner product with
{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}
in (
5.3a
) and using the definition of
{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|{{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}}\|_{D_{{\mathbf{a}}}}^{2}

\displaystyle=(\nabla{\times}(\nabla_{\!0}{\times}{\boldsymbol{Z}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}),{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{{\mathbf{a}}})_{D_{{\mathbf{a}}}}

\displaystyle=(\nabla{\times}(\nabla_{\!0}{\times}{\boldsymbol{Z}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}),{\boldsymbol{U}}_{{\mathbf{a}}})_{D_{{\mathbf{a}}}}+(\nabla{\times}(\nabla_{\!0}{\times}{\boldsymbol{Z}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}),\nabla\theta_{{\mathbf{a}}})_{D_{{\mathbf{a}}}}-(\nabla{\times}(\nabla_{\!0}{\times}{\boldsymbol{Z}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}),\psi_{{\mathbf{a}}}{\boldsymbol{E}}_{h})_{D_{{\mathbf{a}}}}.

Integrating by parts the curl operator, we infer that

\displaystyle\|{{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}}\|_{D_{{\mathbf{a}}}}^{2}=

\displaystyle(\nabla_{\!0}{\times}{\boldsymbol{Z}}_{{\mathbf{a}}},\nabla_{\!0}{\times}{\boldsymbol{U}}_{{\mathbf{a}}})_{D_{{\mathbf{a}}}}-(\nabla_{\!0}{\times}{\boldsymbol{Z}}_{{\mathbf{a}}},\nabla_{h}{\times}(\psi_{{\mathbf{a}}}{\boldsymbol{E}}_{h}))_{D_{{\mathbf{a}}}}

\displaystyle-\sum_{K\in{\cal{T}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}}(\nabla_{\!0}{\times}{\boldsymbol{Z}}_{{\mathbf{a}}},(\psi_{{\mathbf{a}}}{\boldsymbol{E}}_{h}){\times}{\boldsymbol{n}}_{K})_{\partial K}.

Re-arranging the last term on the right-hand side leads to

\|{{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}}\|_{D_{{\mathbf{a}}}}^{2}=(\nabla_{\!0}{\times}{\boldsymbol{Z}}_{{\mathbf{a}}},\nabla_{h}{\times}({\boldsymbol{U}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}-(\psi_{{\mathbf{a}}}{\boldsymbol{E}}_{h})))_{D_{{\mathbf{a}}}}-\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{{\mathbf{a}}}}(\nabla_{\!0}{\times}{\boldsymbol{Z}}_{{\mathbf{a}}},\psi_{{\mathbf{a}}}[\![{{\boldsymbol{E}}_{h}}]\!]^{\mathrm{c}}_{F})_{F}.

Next, invoking the relation (
3.4
) with
{\boldsymbol{W}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}:={\boldsymbol{Z}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}\in{\boldsymbol{X}^{\mathrm{c}}_{0}}(D_{{\mathbf{a}}})
and the definition (
2.9
) of lifting operator, we infer that

\begin{split}\|{{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}}\|_{D_{{\mathbf{a}}}}^{2}&=(\nabla_{\!0}{\times}{\boldsymbol{Z}}_{{\mathbf{a}}},{\boldsymbol{L}}_{h}^{p}(\psi_{{\mathbf{a}}}{\boldsymbol{E}}_{h}))_{D_{{\mathbf{a}}}}-\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{{\mathbf{a}}}}(\nabla_{\!0}{\times}{\boldsymbol{Z}}_{{\mathbf{a}}},\psi_{{\mathbf{a}}}[\![{{\boldsymbol{E}}_{h}}]\!]^{\mathrm{c}}_{F})_{F}\\
&=({\boldsymbol{\Pi}}^{\mathrm{b}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}(\nabla_{\!0}{\times}{\boldsymbol{Z}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}),{\boldsymbol{L}}_{h}^{p}(\psi_{{\mathbf{a}}}{\boldsymbol{E}}_{h}))_{D_{{\mathbf{a}}}}-\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{{\mathbf{a}}}}(\nabla_{\!0}{\times}{\boldsymbol{Z}}_{{\mathbf{a}}},\psi_{{\mathbf{a}}}[\![{{\boldsymbol{E}}_{h}}]\!]^{\mathrm{c}}_{F})_{F}\\
&=-\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{{\mathbf{a}}}}(\{\!\!\{{\nabla_{\!0}{\times}{\boldsymbol{Z}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}-{\boldsymbol{\Pi}}^{\mathrm{b}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}(\nabla_{\!0}{\times}{\boldsymbol{Z}}_{{\mathbf{a}}})}\}\!\!\}^{\mathrm{g}}_{F},\psi_{{\mathbf{a}}}[\![{{\boldsymbol{E}}_{h}}]\!]^{\mathrm{c}}_{F})_{F},\end{split}

(5.4)

where
{\boldsymbol{\Pi}}^{\mathrm{b}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}
denotes the
{\boldsymbol{L}}^{2}
-orthogonal projection onto
{\boldsymbol{P}}^{\mathrm{b}}_{p}({\cal{T}}_{{\mathbf{a}}})
. Using the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality,
\|\psi_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|_{L^{\infty}(F)}=1
,
bounding the mean-values by the traces in the (one or) two cell(s) sharing
F\in\mathcal{F}_{{\mathbf{a}}}
,
and invoking the mesh shape-regularity, we infer that

\displaystyle\|{{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}}\|_{D_{{\mathbf{a}}}}^{2}

\displaystyle\!\leq\!\bigg\{\!\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{{\mathbf{a}}}}\!\Big(\frac{p}{h_{F}}\Big)^{2\delta}\!\|{\{\!\!\{{\nabla_{\!0}{\times}{\boldsymbol{Z}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}-{\boldsymbol{\Pi}}^{\mathrm{b}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}(\nabla_{\!0}{\times}{\boldsymbol{Z}}_{{\mathbf{a}}})}\}\!\!\}^{\mathrm{g}}_{F}}\|_{F}^{2}\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{2}}\!\bigg\{\!\!\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{{\mathbf{a}}}}\!\!\Big(\frac{h_{F}}{p}\Big)^{2\delta}\!\|{[\![{{\boldsymbol{E}}_{h}}]\!]^{\mathrm{c}}_{F}}\|_{F}^{2}\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\!\lesssim\!\bigg\{\!\!\sum_{K\in{\cal{T}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}}\!\!\Big(\frac{p}{h_{K}}\Big)^{2\delta}\|{\nabla_{\!0}{\times}{\boldsymbol{Z}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}-{\boldsymbol{\Pi}}^{\mathrm{b}}_{K}(\nabla_{\!0}{\times}{\boldsymbol{Z}}_{{\mathbf{a}}})}\|_{\partial K}^{2}\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigg\{\!\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{{\mathbf{a}}}}\!\Big(\frac{h_{F}}{p}\Big)^{2\delta}\|{[\![{{\boldsymbol{E}}_{h}}]\!]^{\mathrm{c}}_{F}}\|_{F}^{2}\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{2}},

where
\delta\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
is given by Assumption
3.5
.
Using the approximation result (
2.7
) on the
L^{2}
-projection followed by Lemma
4.7
(notice that indeed
\nabla_{\!0}{\times}{\boldsymbol{Z}}_{\mathbf{a}}\in{\boldsymbol{X}^{\mathrm{c}}}(D_{{\mathbf{a}}})
), we infer that, for all
K\in{\cal{T}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}
,

\Big(\frac{p}{h_{K}}\Big)^{\delta}\|{\nabla_{\!0}{\times}{\boldsymbol{Z}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}-{\boldsymbol{\Pi}}^{\mathrm{b}}_{K}(\nabla_{\!0}{\times}{\boldsymbol{Z}}_{{\mathbf{a}}})}\|_{\partial K}\lesssim|\nabla_{\!0}{\times}{\boldsymbol{Z}}_{\mathbf{a}}|_{{\boldsymbol{H}}^{\frac{1}{2}+\delta}(K)}\lesssim h_{{\mathbf{a}}}^{\frac{1}{2}-\delta}\|{{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}}\|_{D_{{\mathbf{a}}}}.

Combining the above two bounds and since
h_{{\mathbf{a}}}\lesssim h_{F}
for all
F\in\mathcal{F}_{{\mathbf{a}}}
and
p\geq 1
, we infer that the assertion holds true.

6
Application to
hp
-a posteriori error analysis

In this section, we show how the
{{{\boldsymbol{H}}_{0}(\text{\bf curl})}}
-reconstruction operator constructed above can be used in residual-based
hp
-a posteriori error analysis. We focus on the symmetric interior penalty dG approximation of some simplified forms of Maxwell’s equations involving the curl-curl operator and a zero-order term. Other nonconforming approximation methods can be considered as well. For simplicity, we assume that Assumption
3.5
holds true.

6.1
Model problem

The model problem consists in finding
{\boldsymbol{E}}\in{{{\boldsymbol{H}}_{0}(\text{\bf curl};D)}}
such that

\omega^{2}{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}{\boldsymbol{E}}+\nabla{\times}({\boldsymbol{\nu}}\nabla_{\!0}{\times}{\boldsymbol{E}})={\boldsymbol{J}}\quad\text{in $D$},

(6.1)

where
{\boldsymbol{J}}\in{{{\boldsymbol{H}}(\text{\rm div};D)}}
is the source term.
Both material properties
{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}
and
{\boldsymbol{\nu}}
can vary in
D
and
are assumed to take positive-definite, symmetric, piecewise constant matrix values
with eigenvalues uniformly bounded from above and below away from zero. Notice that, for simplicity, we are enforcing homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions (also called perfect electric conductor boundary conditions). The parameter
\omega
scales as a frequency (reciprocal of a time scale) and can be either a real number (leading to the so-called definite Maxwell problem) or a pure imaginary umber (leading to the so-called indefinite problem). Whenever
\omega=0
, the additional property
\nabla{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{J}}=0
is prescribed. For the time being, we assume that
\omega
is a nonzero real number and refer the reader to Remarks
6.2
and
6.10
for further insight into the other cases.

For a material property
{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\in\{{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}},{\boldsymbol{\nu}}\}
, we introduce
the inner product and associated norm weighted by
{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
,
leading to the notation
({\cdot},{\cdot})_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
and
\|{\cdot}\|_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
.
The weak formulation of (
6.1
) classically amounts
to finding
{\boldsymbol{E}}\in{{{\boldsymbol{H}}_{0}(\text{\bf curl};D)}}
such that

b({\boldsymbol{E}},{\boldsymbol{w}})=({\boldsymbol{J}},{\boldsymbol{w}})\quad\forall{\boldsymbol{w}}\in{{{\boldsymbol{H}}_{0}(\text{\bf curl};D)}},

(6.2)

with the bilinear form defined on
{{{\boldsymbol{H}}_{0}(\text{\bf curl};D)}}\times{{{\boldsymbol{H}}_{0}(\text{\bf curl};D)}}
such that

b({\boldsymbol{v}},{\boldsymbol{w}}):=\omega^{2}({\boldsymbol{v}},{\boldsymbol{w}})_{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}+(\nabla_{\!0}{\times}{\boldsymbol{v}},\nabla_{\!0}{\times}{\boldsymbol{w}})_{{\boldsymbol{\nu}}}.

(6.3)

6.2
Symmetric interior penalty dG discretization

We employ the symmetric interior penalty dG method to discretize the weak formulation (
6.2
) (see
[
36
,
29
]
). We assume that the mesh
\mathcal{T}_{h}
is compatible with the domain partition on which
{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}
and
{\boldsymbol{\nu}}
are piecewise constant.
For
{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\in\{{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}},{\boldsymbol{\nu}}\}
, we set
{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{K}:={\boldsymbol{\alpha}}|_{K}
for all
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, and denote by
\alpha_{K}^{\flat}
and
\alpha_{K}^{\sharp}
the smallest and largest eigenvalue of
{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{K}
, respectively. It is convenient to set

\alpha_{F}^{\sharp}:=\max(\alpha_{K_{l}}^{\sharp},\alpha_{K_{r}}^{\sharp})\;\forall F:=\partial K_{l}\cap\partial K_{r}\in{\mathcal{F}}_{h}^{\mathrm{int}},\quad\alpha_{F}^{\sharp}:=\alpha_{K_{l}}^{\sharp}\;\forall F:=\partial K_{l}\cap\partial{\Omega}\in{\mathcal{F}}_{h}^{\mathrm{bd}}.

(6.4)

For all
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, we also define
\alpha^{\sharp}_{\partial K}
so that

\alpha^{\sharp}_{\partial K}|_{F}:=\alpha^{\sharp}_{F}
for all
F\in{\mathcal{F}}_{K}
.
To avoid distracting technicalities, we assume that the material properties are only
mildly heterogeneous and anisotropic. Thus, we hide anisotropy ratios such as

\alpha^{\sharp}_{K}/\alpha^{\flat}_{K}
in the generic constants used in the error analysis.
We do the same for contrast factors such as
\alpha^{\sharp}_{K}/\alpha^{\sharp}_{K^{\prime}}
, where

K^{\prime}
lies in some neighborhood of
K
. We refer the reader to Remarks
6.1

and
6.4
for some further insight.

The discrete problem reads as follows: Find
{\boldsymbol{E}}_{h}\in{\boldsymbol{P}}_{p}^{\mathrm{b}}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})
(recall that
p\geq 1
) such that

b_{h}({\boldsymbol{E}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{w}}_{h})=({\boldsymbol{J}},{\boldsymbol{w}}_{h})\qquad\forall{\boldsymbol{w}}_{h}\in{\boldsymbol{P}}_{p}^{\mathrm{b}}({\mathcal{T}}_{h}),

(6.5)

with the discrete bilinear form such that, for all
{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{w}}_{h}\in{\boldsymbol{P}}_{p}^{\mathrm{b}}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})
,

\displaystyle b_{h}({\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{w}}_{h}):={}

\displaystyle\omega^{2}({\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{w}}_{h})_{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}+(\nabla_{h}{\times}{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},\nabla_{h}{\times}{\boldsymbol{w}}_{h})_{\boldsymbol{\nu}}+\eta_{*}s_{h}({\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{w}}_{h})

(6.6)

\displaystyle+\sum_{F\in{\mathcal{F}}_{h}}\big\{(\{\!\!\{{{\boldsymbol{\nu}}\nabla_{h}{\times}{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}}\}\!\!\}^{\mathrm{g}}_{F},[\![{{\boldsymbol{w}}_{h}}]\!]^{\mathrm{c}}_{F})_{F}+([\![{{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}}]\!]^{\mathrm{c}}_{F},\{\!\!\{{{\boldsymbol{\nu}}\nabla_{h}{\times}{\boldsymbol{w}}_{h}}\}\!\!\}^{\mathrm{g}}_{F})_{F}\big\}.

The stabilization bilinear form
s_{h}
is defined as

s_{h}({\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{w}}_{h}):=\sum_{F\in{\mathcal{F}}_{h}}\nu_{F}^{\sharp}\Big(\frac{p^{2}}{h_{F}}\Big)([\![{{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}}]\!]^{\mathrm{c}}_{F},[\![{{\boldsymbol{w}}_{h}}]\!]^{\mathrm{c}}_{F})_{F},

(6.7)

and the user-dependent parameter
\eta_{*}>0
is taken large enough (see below).
Using the definition (
2.9
) of the lifting operator
{\boldsymbol{L}}_{h}^{p}
, we infer that

\displaystyle b_{h}({\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{w}}_{h}):={}

\displaystyle\omega^{2}({\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{w}}_{h})_{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}+(\nabla_{h}{\times}{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},\nabla_{h}{\times}{\boldsymbol{w}}_{h})_{\boldsymbol{\nu}}+\eta_{*}s_{h}({\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{w}}_{h})

(6.8)

\displaystyle+({\boldsymbol{L}}_{h}^{p}({\boldsymbol{w}}_{h}),\nabla_{h}{\times}{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h})_{\boldsymbol{\nu}}+({\boldsymbol{L}}_{h}^{p}({\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}),\nabla_{h}{\times}{\boldsymbol{w}}_{h})_{\boldsymbol{\nu}}.

We equip the discrete space
{\boldsymbol{P}}_{p}^{\mathrm{b}}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})
with the norm

|\!|\!|{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}|\!|\!|_{\mathrm{dG}}^{2}:=\omega^{2}\|{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}\|_{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}^{2}+\|\nabla_{h}{\times}{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}\|_{\boldsymbol{\nu}}^{2}+s_{h}({\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h})\quad\forall{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}\in{\boldsymbol{P}}_{p}^{\mathrm{b}}({\mathcal{T}}_{h}).

(6.9)

Recalling (
2.10
), we infer that

\|{\boldsymbol{L}}_{h}^{p}({\boldsymbol{v}}_{h})\|_{\boldsymbol{\nu}}^{2}\leq C(\kappa_{\mathcal{T}_{h}})s_{h}({\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h})\quad\forall{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}\in{\boldsymbol{P}}_{p}^{\mathrm{b}}({\mathcal{T}}_{h}),

(6.10)

whence we infer, provided we take
\eta_{*}\geq\frac{1}{2}+2C(\kappa_{\mathcal{T}_{h}})

with
C(\kappa_{\mathcal{T}_{h}})
from (
6.10
), that the following coercivity property holds:

b_{h}({\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h})\geq\frac{1}{2}|\!|\!|{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}|\!|\!|_{\mathrm{dG}}^{2}\quad\forall{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}\in{\boldsymbol{P}}_{p}^{\mathrm{b}}({\mathcal{T}}_{h}).

(6.11)

Remark 6.1

(Weighted averages)
.

Whenever the jumps of
{\boldsymbol{\nu}}
across the mesh interfaces are large,
one can consider weighted (
{\boldsymbol{\nu}}
-dependent) averages to evaluate the
last two terms on the right-hand side of (
6.6
). In the case
of strong anisotropy, the weights depend on the normal-normal component of
{\boldsymbol{\nu}}

on both sides of the interface. We refer the reader, e.g., to

[
24
]
for an example in the context of a scalar diffusion problems.
Accordingly, the definition (
2.9
) of the lifting operator
{\boldsymbol{L}}_{h}^{p}
can be
modified by using a weighted average on the right-hand side.

Remark 6.2

(Indefinite case)
.

In the indefinite case, the coercivity property (
6.11
) is replaced by a Gårding inequality. Assuming that
\omega
is not a resonant frequency and invoking a duality argument à la Schatz then leads to a discrete inf-sup condition if the mesh size is small enough. We refer the reader to
[
12
, Sec. 5.4]
for more details.

6.3
Estimator and error measure

The a posteriori error estimator is written as the sum over the mesh cells
of local error indicators
\eta_{K}
for all
K\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
. The local error indicator
consists of three pieces. The first two respectively measure the residuals of the
divergence constraint and of Maxwell’s equations:

\eta_{K,\operatorname{div}}^{2}:=\frac{1}{\epsilon^{\sharp}_{K}}\bigg\{\frac{1}{\omega^{2}}\Big(\frac{h_{K}}{p}\Big)^{2}\|\nabla{\cdot}({\boldsymbol{J}}-\omega^{2}{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}{\boldsymbol{E}}_{h})\|_{K}^{2}+\omega^{2}\Big(\frac{h_{K}}{p}\Big)\|[\![{{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}{\boldsymbol{E}}_{h}}]\!]_{\partial K}^{\mathrm{d}}\|_{\partial K\setminus\partial\Omega}^{2}\bigg\},\\

(6.12a)

and

\begin{split}\eta_{K,\operatorname{curl}}^{2}:={}&\frac{1}{\nu^{\sharp}_{K}}\bigg\{\Big(\frac{h_{K}}{p}\Big)^{2}\|{\boldsymbol{J}}-\omega^{2}{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}{\boldsymbol{E}}_{h}-\nabla{\times}({\boldsymbol{\nu}}\nabla{\times}{\boldsymbol{E}}_{h})\|_{K}^{2}\\
&+\Big(\frac{h_{K}}{p}\Big)\|[\![{{\boldsymbol{\nu}}\nabla_{h}{\times}{\boldsymbol{E}}_{h}}]\!]_{\partial K}^{\mathrm{c}}\|_{\partial K\setminus\partial\Omega}^{2}+\nu_{\partial K}^{\sharp}\Big(\frac{p^{2}}{h_{K}}\Big)\|[\![{{\boldsymbol{E}}_{h}}]\!]_{\partial K}^{\mathrm{c}}\|_{\partial K}^{2}\bigg\}.\end{split}

(6.12b)

The last part of the estimator is related to the nonconformity of the discrete
field
{\boldsymbol{E}}_{h}
and reads

\eta_{K,\operatorname{nc}}^{2}:=\nu_{\partial K}^{\sharp}\Big(\frac{h_{K}}{p}\Big)\|{[\![{\nabla_{h}{\times}{\boldsymbol{E}}_{h}}]\!]_{\partial K}^{\mathrm{d}}}\|_{\partial K}^{2}+\bigg\{{\omega^{2}\epsilon_{\partial K}^{\sharp}h_{K}}+\nu_{\partial K}^{\sharp}\Big(\frac{p^{2}}{h_{K}}\Big)\bigg\}\|{[\![{{\boldsymbol{E}}_{h}}]\!]_{\partial K}^{\mathrm{c}}}\|_{\partial K}^{2}.

(6.12c)

Notice that this latter estimator is suitable under Assumption
3.5
.
It is convenient to introduce the following shorthand notation:

\eta_{K}^{2}:=\eta_{K,\operatorname{div}}^{2}+\eta_{K,\operatorname{curl}}^{2}+\eta_{K,\operatorname{nc}}^{2},\qquad\eta_{\bullet}^{2}:=\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\eta_{\bullet,K}^{2},\qquad\eta^{2}:=\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\eta_{K}^{2},

(6.13)

with
\bullet\in\{\operatorname{div},\operatorname{curl},\operatorname{nc}\}
.

The approximation error is defined as

{\boldsymbol{e}}:={\boldsymbol{E}}-{\boldsymbol{E}}_{h}\in{\boldsymbol{V}}_{\sharp}:={{{\boldsymbol{H}}_{0}(\text{\bf curl};D)}}+{\boldsymbol{P}}^{\mathrm{b}}_{p}({\mathcal{T}}_{h}).

(6.14)

Although the sum defining
{\boldsymbol{V}}_{\sharp}
is not direct, any field
{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}\in{{{\boldsymbol{H}}_{0}(\text{\bf curl};D)}}\cap{\boldsymbol{P}}_{p}^{\mathrm{b}}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})

satisfies
[\![{{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}}]\!]^{\mathrm{c}}_{F}={\boldsymbol{0}}
for all
F\in{\mathcal{F}}_{h}
. It is therefore legitimate
to extend the jump operator to
{\boldsymbol{V}}_{\sharp}
by setting
[\![{{\boldsymbol{v}}}]\!]^{\mathrm{c}}_{F}:=[\![{{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}}]\!]^{\mathrm{c}}_{F}

for all
{\boldsymbol{v}}=\tilde{\boldsymbol{v}}+{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}\in{\boldsymbol{V}}_{\sharp}
. Similarly, the lifting operator
{\boldsymbol{L}}_{h}^{p}
can be extended
to
{\boldsymbol{V}}_{\sharp}
by setting
{\boldsymbol{L}}_{h}^{p}({\boldsymbol{v}}):={\boldsymbol{L}}_{h}^{p}({\boldsymbol{v}}_{h})
. The discrete bilinear form
b_{h}

is then extended to
{\boldsymbol{V}}_{\sharp}\times{\boldsymbol{V}}_{\sharp}
by using the expression (
6.8
),
i.e., we set, for all
{\boldsymbol{v}},{\boldsymbol{w}}\in{\boldsymbol{V}}_{\sharp}
,

\displaystyle b_{\sharp}({\boldsymbol{v}},{\boldsymbol{w}}):={}

\displaystyle\omega^{2}({\boldsymbol{v}},{\boldsymbol{w}})_{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}+(\nabla_{h}{\times}{\boldsymbol{v}},\nabla_{h}{\times}{\boldsymbol{w}})_{\boldsymbol{\nu}}+\eta_{*}s_{h}({\boldsymbol{v}},{\boldsymbol{w}})

(6.15)

\displaystyle+({\boldsymbol{L}}_{h}^{p}({\boldsymbol{w}}),\nabla_{h}{\times}{\boldsymbol{v}})_{\boldsymbol{\nu}}+({\boldsymbol{L}}_{h}^{p}({\boldsymbol{v}}),\nabla_{h}{\times}{\boldsymbol{w}})_{\boldsymbol{\nu}},

keeping in mind that
\nabla_{h}{\times}{\boldsymbol{v}}:=\nabla_{\!0}{\times}{\boldsymbol{v}}
whenever
{\boldsymbol{v}}\in{{{\boldsymbol{H}}_{0}(\text{\bf curl};D)}}
.

For any subset
{\mathcal{T}}\subset\mathcal{T}_{h}
, we define the error measure

|\!|\!|{\boldsymbol{e}}|\!|\!|_{\sharp,{\mathcal{T}}}^{2}:=\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}}}\bigg\{\omega^{2}\|{\boldsymbol{e}}\|_{{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}},K}^{2}+\|\nabla{\times}{\boldsymbol{e}}\|_{{\boldsymbol{\nu}},K}^{2}+\nu_{\partial K}^{\sharp}\Big(\frac{p^{2}}{h_{K}}\Big)\|[\![{{\boldsymbol{e}}}]\!]_{\partial K}^{\mathrm{c}}\|_{\partial K}^{2}\bigg\},

(6.16)

and omit the subscript
{\mathcal{T}}
whenever
{\mathcal{T}}=\mathcal{T}_{h}
.
When restricted to
{\boldsymbol{P}}_{p}^{\mathrm{b}}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})
,
|\!|\!|\bullet|\!|\!|_{\sharp}
is equivalent to the discrete
dG norm
|\!|\!|\bullet|\!|\!|_{\mathrm{dG}}
defined in (
6.9
) up to a rewriting of the

Remark 6.3

(Stabilization weight)
.

We observe that the error measure defined in (

\eta_{*}
introduced in the dG scheme.

Remark 6.4

(Heterogeneous materials)
.

Whenever one wishes to account for the heterogeneity of the material properties,
some weights in the error indicators need to be adapted. For the error indicators
defined in (
6.12
), one needs to consider a contrast factor involving
three layers of vertex-based neighbors around
K
, as detailed in

[
12
]
. For the nonconformity indicator defined in
(
6.12c
), one layer of neighbors is sufficient. We also notice that
it is possible to modify Definition
3.1

so as to reconstruct on each vertex patch an
{{{\boldsymbol{H}}_{0}(\text{\bf curl})}}
-conforming field by using
weighted
L^{2}
-inner products. However, the estimates satisfied by this modified
reconstruction in the broken curl and
{\boldsymbol{L}}^{2}
-norms will depend anyway on the contrast
factors associated with the underlying weights.

6.4
hp
-a posteriori error analysis

We decompose the approximation error into two components as follows:

{\boldsymbol{e}}=({\boldsymbol{E}}-{\boldsymbol{E}}_{c})+({\boldsymbol{E}}_{c}-{\boldsymbol{E}}_{h})=:{\boldsymbol{e}}_{c}+{\boldsymbol{e}}_{nc},\qquad{\boldsymbol{E}}_{c}:=\boldsymbol{R}^{\mathrm{c}}({\boldsymbol{E}}_{h})\in{{{\boldsymbol{H}}_{0}(\text{\bf curl};D)}}.

(6.17)

We call
{\boldsymbol{e}}_{c}
the conforming error and
{\boldsymbol{e}}_{nc}
the nonconforming error.
Notice that we use the
{{{\boldsymbol{H}}_{0}(\text{\bf curl})}}
-reconstruction operator from Section
3

to define the two error components. Actually, the precise definition of
{\boldsymbol{E}}_{c}
is
irrelevant for bounding
{\boldsymbol{e}}_{c}
(we only use that
{\boldsymbol{e}}_{c}\in{{{\boldsymbol{H}}_{0}(\text{\bf curl};D)}}
), and is only relevant
for bounding
{\boldsymbol{e}}_{nc}
.

Lemma 6.5

(Residual)
.

Let
\eta_{\operatorname{dc}}^{2}:=\eta_{\operatorname{div}}^{2}+\eta_{\operatorname{curl}}^{2}
. The following holds:

|b_{\sharp}({\boldsymbol{e}},{\boldsymbol{e}}_{c})|\leq C(\kappa_{{\mathcal{T}}_{h}})\eta_{\operatorname{dc}}|\!|\!|{\boldsymbol{e}}_{c}|\!|\!|_{\sharp}.

(6.18)

Proof.

The proof essentially follows the arguments from the proof of
[
12
, Lem. 6.2]
, up to minor adaptations (the proof therein tracks the dependency of the constants on
the heterogeneity of the material properties, and deals with the indefinite Maxwell problem).
∎

Lemma 6.6

(Bound on
{\boldsymbol{e}}_{c}
)
.

The following holds:

|\!|\!|{\boldsymbol{e}}_{c}|\!|\!|_{\sharp}\leq C(\kappa_{{\mathcal{T}}_{h}})(\eta_{\operatorname{dc}}+|\!|\!|{\boldsymbol{e}}_{nc}|\!|\!|_{\sharp}).

(6.19)

Proof.

Since
{\boldsymbol{e}}_{c}\in{{{\boldsymbol{H}}_{0}(\text{\bf curl};D)}}
, we have
s_{h}({\boldsymbol{e}}_{c},{\boldsymbol{e}}_{c})=0
and
{\boldsymbol{L}}_{h}^{p}({\boldsymbol{e}}_{c})={\boldsymbol{0}}
, so that

\displaystyle|\!|\!|{\boldsymbol{e}}_{c}|\!|\!|_{\sharp}^{2}

\displaystyle=\omega^{2}\|{\boldsymbol{e}}_{c}\|_{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}^{2}+\|\nabla_{\!0}{\times}{\boldsymbol{e}}_{c}\|_{\boldsymbol{\nu}}^{2}

\displaystyle=b_{\sharp}({\boldsymbol{e}}_{c},{\boldsymbol{e}}_{c})=b_{\sharp}({\boldsymbol{e}},{\boldsymbol{e}}_{c})-b_{\sharp}({\boldsymbol{e}}_{nc},{\boldsymbol{e}}_{c})=:T_{1}+T_{2}.

Applying the bound (
6.18
) from Lemma
6.5
, we get

|T_{1}|\lesssim\eta_{\operatorname{dc}}|\!|\!|{\boldsymbol{e}}_{c}|\!|\!|_{\sharp}.

Moreover, we observe that

T_{2}=b_{\sharp}({\boldsymbol{e}}_{nc},{\boldsymbol{e}}_{c})=\omega^{2}({\boldsymbol{e}}_{nc},{\boldsymbol{e}}_{c})_{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}+(\nabla_{h}{\times}{\boldsymbol{e}}_{nc},\nabla_{\!0}{\times}{\boldsymbol{e}}_{c})_{\boldsymbol{\nu}}-({\boldsymbol{L}}_{h}^{p}({\boldsymbol{E}}_{h}),\nabla_{\!0}{\times}{\boldsymbol{e}}_{c})_{{\boldsymbol{\nu}}}.

Invoking the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and the bound (
2.10
) on the discrete lifting operator, we infer that

|T_{2}|\leq 2\Big\{\omega^{2}\|{\boldsymbol{e}}_{nc}\|_{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}^{2}+\|\nabla_{h}{\times}{\boldsymbol{e}}_{nc}\|_{\boldsymbol{\nu}}^{2}+\|{\boldsymbol{L}}_{h}^{p}({\boldsymbol{E}}_{h})\|_{\boldsymbol{\nu}}^{2}\Big\}^{\frac{1}{2}}|\!|\!|{\boldsymbol{e}}_{c}|\!|\!|_{\sharp}\lesssim|\!|\!|{\boldsymbol{e}}_{nc}|\!|\!|_{\sharp}|\!|\!|{\boldsymbol{e}}_{c}|\!|\!|_{\sharp}.

Combining the above bounds completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 6.7

(Bound on
{\boldsymbol{e}}_{nc}
)
.

Under Assumption
3.5
, the following holds:

|\!|\!|{\boldsymbol{e}}_{nc}|\!|\!|_{\sharp}\leq C(\kappa_{{\mathcal{T}}_{h}})\eta_{\operatorname{nc}}.

(6.20)

Proof.

Recall that, for all
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
,

{\boldsymbol{e}}_{nc}|_{K}=\sum_{{\mathbf{a}}\in\mathcal{V}_{K}}{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}|_{K}
with

{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}:={\boldsymbol{E}}_{{\mathbf{a}}}-\psi_{{\mathbf{a}}}{\boldsymbol{E}}_{h}
(see (
3.9
)).
Reasoning as in the proof of
Theorem
3.7
, this gives

\|\nabla{\times}{\boldsymbol{e}}_{nc}\|_{{\boldsymbol{\nu}},K}^{2}\leq\nu_{K}\sum_{{\mathbf{a}}\in\mathcal{V}_{K}}\|\nabla_{h}{\times}{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{\mathbf{a}}\|_{D_{{\mathbf{a}}}}^{2}.

Invoking the bound on
\|\nabla_{h}{\times}{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{\mathbf{a}}\|_{D_{{\mathbf{a}}}}

from Lemma
3.3
,
and hiding the contrast factor on
\nu
in the generic constants, we infer that

\|\nabla{\times}{\boldsymbol{e}}_{nc}\|_{{\boldsymbol{\nu}},K}^{2}\lesssim\sum_{{\mathbf{a}}\in\mathcal{V}_{K}}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{{\mathbf{a}}}}\!\bigg\{\nu^{\sharp}_{F}\Big(\frac{h_{F}}{p}\Big)\|{[\![{\nabla_{h}{\times}{\boldsymbol{E}}_{h}}]\!]^{\mathrm{d}}_{F}}\|_{F}^{2}+\nu^{\sharp}_{F}\Big(\frac{p^{2}}{h_{F}}\Big)\|{[\![{{\boldsymbol{E}}_{h}}]\!]^{\mathrm{c}}_{F}}\|_{F}^{2}\bigg\}.

Reasoning similarly to bound
\omega^{2}\|{\boldsymbol{e}}_{nc}\|_{{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}},K}^{2}
, but this time invoking the bound from Lemma
3.6
, we infer that

\omega^{2}\|{\boldsymbol{e}}_{nc}\|_{{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}},K}^{2}\lesssim\sum_{{\mathbf{a}}\in\mathcal{V}_{K}}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{{\mathbf{a}}}}\!\omega^{2}\epsilon^{\sharp}_{F}h_{F}\|{[\![{{\boldsymbol{E}}_{h}}]\!]^{\mathrm{c}}_{F}}\|_{F}^{2}.

Combining the above two bounds together, re-arranging the summations over the mesh cells,
invoking again the mild heterogeneity of the material properties, and observing
that
[\![{{\boldsymbol{e}}_{nc}}]\!]_{\partial K}^{\mathrm{c}}=-[\![{{\boldsymbol{E}}_{h}}]\!]_{\partial K}^{\mathrm{c}}
for all
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, leads to the expected
bound on
|\!|\!|{\boldsymbol{e}}_{nc}|\!|\!|_{\sharp}
.
∎

Theorem 6.8

(Global upper bound (reliability))
.

Under Assumption
3.5
, the following holds:

|\!|\!|{\boldsymbol{e}}|\!|\!|_{\sharp}\leq C(\kappa_{{\mathcal{T}}_{h}})\eta.

(6.21)

Proof.

Invoke the triangle inequality together with Lemmas
6.6
and
6.7
, and recall the definition of
\eta
from (
6.13
).
∎

Finally, we derive local efficiency estimates.

Proposition 6.9

(Local lower bound (local efficiency))
.

For all
K\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
, we have

\eta_{K}\leq C(\kappa_{{\mathcal{T}}_{h}})p^{\frac{3}{2}}\left\{\bigg(1+\Big(\frac{\omega^{2}\epsilon_{K}^{\sharp}}{\nu_{K}^{\sharp}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\frac{h_{K}}{p}\Big)\bigg)|\!|\!|{\boldsymbol{E}}-{\boldsymbol{E}}_{h}|\!|\!|_{\sharp,K^{\mathrm{f}}}+\operatorname{osc}_{K^{\mathrm{f}}}\right\},

(6.22)

with the data oscillation term

\operatorname{osc}_{K^{\mathrm{f}}}^{2}\!:=\!\!\!\sum_{K^{\prime}\in K^{\mathrm{f}}}\min_{{\boldsymbol{J}}_{h}\in{\boldsymbol{P}}_{p}^{\mathrm{b}}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})}\!\!\left\{\frac{1}{\nu_{K^{\prime}}^{\sharp}}\Big(\frac{h_{K^{\prime}}}{p}\Big)^{2}\|{\boldsymbol{J}}-{\boldsymbol{J}}_{h}\|_{K^{\prime}}^{2}\!+\!\frac{1}{\omega^{2}\epsilon_{K^{\prime}}^{\sharp}}\Big(\frac{h_{K^{\prime}}}{p}\Big)^{2}\|\nabla{\cdot}({\boldsymbol{J}}-{\boldsymbol{J}}_{h})\|_{K^{\prime}}^{2}\right\},

(6.23)

and
K^{\mathrm{f}}
is the collection of the mesh cells sharing a face with
K
.

Proof.

Local bounds on all terms composing the error estimator
\eta_{K}
can be found from
[
12
, Theorem 6.5]
except for the term involving
[\![{\nabla_{h}{\times}{\boldsymbol{E}}_{h}}]\!]_{F}^{\mathrm{d}}
. Invoking the
H^{1}
to
L^{2}
norm inverse inequality from
[
37
, Theorem 4.76]
, we infer that

\nu_{\partial K}^{\sharp}\Big(\frac{h_{K}}{p}\Big)\|{[\![{\nabla_{h}{\times}{\boldsymbol{E}}_{h}}]\!]_{\partial K}^{\mathrm{d}}}\|_{\partial K}^{2}\lesssim\nu_{\partial K}^{\sharp}\Big(\frac{p^{3}}{h_{K}}\Big)\|{[\![{{\boldsymbol{E}}_{h}}]\!]_{\partial K}^{\mathrm{c}}}\|_{\partial K}^{2}\lesssim p|\!|\!|{\boldsymbol{E}}-{\boldsymbol{E}}_{h}|\!|\!|_{\sharp,K^{\mathrm{f}}}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 6.10

(Indefinite case)

Whenever

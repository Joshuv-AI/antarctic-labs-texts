Free Jordan Algebras and Representations of 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 ^ 2 ​ ( J ) \widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}(J)
Michael Lau, Olivier Mathieu

Abstract




Abstract:
Let
J
be a unital Jordan algebra, and
let
\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}(J)
be the universal central extension of the Tits-Kantor-Koecher Lie algebra.

In Part A, we study the category of

(\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}(J),{\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbb{K}))
-modules. We characterize the dominant
J
-spaces, which are analogous to the dominant highest weights appearing in classical settings. A family of universal envelopes
\mathcal{U}_{n}(J)
associated to such modules is introduced and studied. We also prove some finiteness theorems.

In Part C, we define the notion of smooth
\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}(J)
-modules for augmented Jordan algebras
J
, and investigate the category of smooth modules in the spirit of Cline-Parshall-Scott highest weight categories. We
show that the standard modules of this category are finite dimensional when
J
is finitely generated.

The free unital Jordan algebra
J(D)
over
D
variables
is an elusive object, but finiteness and
Ext
-vanishing properties suggest that the smooth
\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}(J(D))
-modules with even eigenvalues might form a generalized highest weight category. However, we prove that such an assertion would contradict recently obtained information about the growth of free Jordan algebras. See
[
24
]
and
[
13
]
for more details. It then follows that the category of smooth
\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}(J(D))
-modules with even eigenvalues is not a generalized highest weight category when
D\geq 2
.

Surprisingly, the proofs of most of these results make use of deep theorems of E. Zelmanov.

Keywords:
free Jordan algebras, Tits-Kantor-Koecher construction,
Tits-Allison-Gao algebra,
Weyl modules, good filtrations, generalized highest weight categories,
FP_{\infty}
Lie algebras, weight modules, Ext-vanishing.

Introduction

Let
\mathbb{K}
be a field of characteristic zero.
For any unital Jordan algebra
J
, we denote by

\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}(J)
the universal central extension of the Tits-Kantor-Koecher Lie algebra
TKK(J)
.
In the present paper, we investigate two categories
of representations of the Lie algebra

\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}(J)
.

An
(\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}(J),{\mathbf{SL}}_{2}(\mathbb{K}))
-module

is an
\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}(J)
-module that is locally finite dimensional as an
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{K})
-module.
In Part A, we investigate finiteness properties of the
category of
(\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}(J),{\mathbf{SL}}_{2}(\mathbb{K}))
-modules for
an arbitrary unital Jordan algebra
J
.
Relative to the action of the usual diagonal element
h
of
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{K})
, any
(\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}(J),{\mathbf{SL}}_{2}(\mathbb{K}))
-module
X
admits an eigenspace decomposition
X=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}X_{n}
.
In particular, the Lie algebra

\mathfrak{G}:=\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}(J)
has a short grading
\mathfrak{G}=\mathfrak{G}_{-2}\oplus\mathfrak{G}_{0}\oplus\mathfrak{G}_{2}
. For integers
n
, we ask

Which
\mathfrak{G}_{0}
-modules
V
are isomorphic to

H^{0}(\mathfrak{G}_{2},X)\cap X_{n}
for some
X
?

A vector space
V
endowed with a linear map

\rho:J\to\hbox{End}_{\mathbb{K}}(V)
is called a

J
-space
when

V
is a
\mathfrak{G}_{0}
-module and
\rho(a)
is the action of
h\otimes a\in\mathfrak{G}_{0}
on
V
. This is equivalent to
the identities

[\rho(a),\rho(a^{2})]=0
and

[[\rho(a),\rho(b)],\rho(c)]=4\rho(a(cb)-(ac)b)

for all
a,b,c\in J
. A
J
-space is called
dominant
of level
n
if it is isomorphic to
H^{0}(\mathfrak{G}_{2},X)\cap X_{n}
for some
(\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}(J),{\mathbf{SL}}_{2}(\mathbb{K}))
-module
X
.

Our first result characterizes
dominant
J
-spaces of level
n
. For any
partition
\sigma=(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\ldots,\sigma_{m})
of
n+1
, we write
|C_{\sigma}|
for the cardinality of the corresponding conjugacy class in the symmetric group
S_{n+1}
and
\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)

for its signature. For
a\in J
, we write
\rho_{\sigma}(a)
for the expression
\rho(a^{\sigma_{1}})\rho(a^{\sigma_{2}})\cdots\rho(a^{\sigma_{m}})
.

Theorem
1
.

Let
(V,\rho)
be a
J
-space such that
\rho(1)=n
. Then

V
is dominant of level
n
if and only if
it satisfies the following condition:

\sum_{\sigma\ \vdash\ n+1}\hbox{sgn}(\sigma)\,|C_{\sigma}|\,\rho_{\sigma}(a)=0\hbox{\ for all\ }a\in J.

There is thus an associative algebra

{\mathcal{U}}_{n}(J)
, endowed with a linear map
\rho_{n}:J\to{\mathcal{U}}_{n}(J)
, which is the unital universal associative envelope of
J
in the category of dominant
J
-spaces of level
n
.
We prove the following result:

Theorem
2
.

The map
\rho_{n}
is injective for any
n\geq 4
.

Next we prove two finiteness theorems:

Theorem
3
.

The associative algebra
{\mathcal{U}}_{n}(J)
is finitely generated, for all finitely generated Jordan algebras
J
.

Theorem
4
.

The Lie algebra
\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}(J)
is finitely presented, for all finitely presented Jordan algebras
J
.

The free Jordan
\mathbb{K}
-algebra
J(D)
on
D
generators admits a grading
J(D)=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}J_{n}(D)
, where
J_{0}(D)=\mathbb{K}
and
J_{1}(D)
is the linear span of the
D
generators. The algebra
J(D)
is an elusive object. However, an explicit formula for
\dim J_{n}(D)
has been conjectured in
[
24
]
. The previous theorem shows that
\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}(J(D))
is

FP_{2}
. This theorem and other observations raise the natural question of whether the
Lie algebra
\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}(J(D))
could be
FP_{\infty}
, that is, whether its trivial module has a projective resolution consisting of finitely generated modules. Such a finiteness condition would have profound consequences for the growth of free Jordan algebras:

Theorem
5
.

Assume that

\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}(J(D+1))
is of type
FP_{\infty}
.
Then the conjectural formula of
[
24
]
for

\dim J_{n}(D)
holds.

Part
B
contains the categorical background (used in Part C) on good filtrations in locally artinian categories. In particular, we introduce the notion of
generalized highest weight categories
(
generalized HW categories
for short), which differ from highest weight categories in that they allow nontrivial self-extensions of standard objects. We close Part B with an Ext-vanishing result for generalized HW categories (Theorem
6
).

It has been conjectured in
[
24
]
that

H_{k}(\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}(J(D),\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{K}))=0
for
k>0
. By

[
18
]
, the dual of this homology group describes the
self-extensions of
\mathbb{K}
in the large category of

(\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}(J),{\mathbf{SL}}_{2}(\mathbb{K}))
-modules.
In Part C, we introduce the smaller and more manageable category of smooth modules, with a similar connection between

H_{*}(\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}(J(D),\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{K}))
and self-extensions of the trivial module
\mathbb{K}
.

A unital Jordan algebra
J
endowed with an ideal
J_{+}
of codimension
1
is called an

augmented
Jordan algebra. An

\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}(J)
-module is called
smooth

if any cyclic submodule is a finite dimensional

\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}(J/J_{+}^{n})
-module for some
n
. This terminology comes from proalgebraic groups, where smoothness means that a module is locally a representation for some algebraic quotient. For
p
-adic groups, the definition of smoothness is similar.

In Part
C
, we investigate the category of smooth modules
in the spirit of the Cline-Parshall-Scott paper
[
9
]
.
For an augmented Jordan algebra
J
and
n\geq 0
, there is a
1
-dimensional
J
-space
\mathbb{K}_{n}
defined by
\rho(1)=n
and
\rho(J_{+})=0
. The standard module
\nabla(n)
is a smooth module such that

H^{0}(\mathfrak{G}_{2},\nabla(n))=\mathbb{K}_{n}
, and satisfying a universal property. When
J
is finitely generated we prove that:

Theorem
7
.

The standard module

\nabla(n)
is finite dimensional.

However, the category of smooth modules is almost never
a highest weight category. Some results suggest that the category of smooth

\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}(J)
-modules with even eigenvalues
could be a generalized HW category when

J
is a finitely generated free Jordan algebra. We conclude
the paper with the following result.

Theorem
8
.

Assume that the category
of smooth
\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}(J(D))
-modules with even eigenvalues

[
24
]
for

is true.

About the proofs:

The proof of Theorem 1 is elementary. For Theorem 2,
the proof of the injectivity of
\rho_{n}
for even
n
is elementary. Strangely, it uses Zelmanov’s Classification Theorem
[
37
,
38
]
for the odd integers

.

The proofs of Theorems

Recent developments
In a very recent arXiv preprint
[
13
]
posted when the present paper was under final revisions, Dotsenko and Hentzel provide detailed computational data indicating that the conjectural graded dimension formula
[
24
]
for the free Jordan algebra on two generators fails in degree 19. When Theorems 5 and 8 are combined with Dotsenko and Hentzel’s result, we obtain the following immediate and perhaps surprising consequences for Lie algebra representation theory:

Corollary.
For
D\geq 3
, the Lie algebra
\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}(J(D))
is not of type
FP_{\infty}
.

Corollary.
For
D\geq 2
, the category of smooth
\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}(J(D))
-modules with even eigenvalues is not a generalized HW category.

As illustrated by these corollaries, the results of the present paper show definite connections between the structure of free Jordan algebras, the representation theory of the Lie algebras
\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}(J(D))
, and Zelmanov’s Theorem on nil Jordan algebras of bounded index.

Conventions and notations

As before, let
\mathbb{K}
be a field of characteristic zero. All vector spaces, algebras, and tensor products will be taken over
\mathbb{K}
, unless explicitly otherwise mentioned.

We start with the definitions involving the Lie algebra

\mathfrak{sl}_{2}:=\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{K})
.
We fix the usual basis
\{h,e,f\}
satisfying
[h,e]=2e
,
[h,f]=-2f
, and
[e,f]=h
. The Killing form

\kappa
will be normalized so that
\kappa(h,h)=4
.

For any
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-module
M
and
z\in\mathbb{K}
, we say that
M_{z}:=\{m\in M\,|\,h.m=zm\}
is an
eigenspace
relative to the action of
h
. When
M_{z}\neq 0
, we say that
z
is an
eigenvalue
of the module
M
. All
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-modules considered in this paper will decompose as direct sums of eigenspaces of integer eigenvalues. For any nonnegative integer
n
, let
L(n)
be the simple
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-module with highest eigenvalue
n
. Clearly,
L(0)
is the trivial module
\mathbb{K}
,
L(1)=\mathbb{K}^{2}
is the natural
2
-dimensional representation, and
L(n)
is isomorphic to the space
S^{n}L(1)
of symmetric tensors for any
n\geq 0
. We will write
M(n)
for the Verma module with highest eigenvalue
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

We continue with the notation involving Jordan algebras.
We assume that all Jordan algebras
J
are unital unless explicitly stated otherwise. For every
a,b\in J
, let
\partial_{a,b}:\ J\rightarrow J
be the linear map
\partial_{a,b}(x)=a(bx)-(ax)b
.
The Jordan identity implies that
\partial_{a,b}
is a derivation of
J
. An
inner derivation
is a linear combination of the derivations
\partial_{a,b}
. The vector space
\text{\rm Inn}\,J
of inner derivations is a Lie algebra.

For
x\in\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
and
a\in J
, the tensor product
x\otimes a\in\mathfrak{sl}_{2}\otimes J
will be denoted
x(a)
, though
x(1)=x\otimes 1
will also be denoted by
x
.

Tits-Kantor-Koecher construction

In his 1962 paper
[
35
]
, Tits defined a Lie algebra structure on the space

TKK(J):=(\mathfrak{sl}_{2}\otimes J)\oplus\text{\rm Inn}\,J,

with Lie bracket

(T1)

[x(a),y(b)]=[x,y](ab)+\kappa(x,y)\partial_{a,b}

(T2)

[\partial,x(a)]=x(\partial\,a)
,

(T3)

[\partial,\partial^{\prime}]=\partial\circ\partial^{\prime}-\partial^{\prime}\circ\partial,

for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
,
\partial,\partial^{\prime}\in\text{\rm Inn}\,J
, and
a,b\in J
. This construction was later generalized to Jordan pairs and triple systems by Kantor and Koecher, and
TKK(J)
is commonly known as the
Tits-Kantor-Koecher (or TKK) algebra
. It should be noted that the Tits construction, associating a Lie algebra
TKK(J)
to each Jordan algebra
J
, is not functorial.

Tits-Allison-Gao construction

The Lie algebra
TKK(J)
is perfect and its
universal central extension, denoted by

\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}(J)
, was nicely described by Allison and Gao
[
3
]
. See also
[
2
]
,
[
33
]
.

As a vector space,

\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}(J)=\left({\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}\otimes J\right)\oplus\{J,J\}
,

where
\{J,J\}=(\bigwedge^{2}J)/\mathcal{S}
and

\mathcal{S}=\hbox{Span}\{a\wedge a^{2}\mid\ a\in J\}
.
For any
a,b\in J
, we write

\{a,b\}
for the image of
a\wedge b
in
\{J,J\}
.
The bracket on
\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}(J)
is given by

(R1)

[x(a),y(b)]=[x,y](ab)+\kappa(x,y)\{a,b\}

(R2)

[\{a,b\},x(c)]=x(\partial_{a,b}\,c)

(R3)

[\{a,b\},\{c,d\}]=\{\partial_{a,b}\,c,d\}+\{c,\partial_{a,b}\,d\}
.

for all
a,b,c,d\in J
and
x,y\in\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
.
It is a bit tricky to show that
(R3) is skew-symmetric
[
3
]
.

Relation (R3) is similar to Relation (T3) for Tits-Kantor-Koecher algebras, since

\partial_{a,b}\circ\partial_{c,d}-\partial_{c,d}\circ\partial_{a,b}=\partial_{\partial_{a,b}c,d}+\partial_{c,\partial_{a,b}d}.

Hence there is a Lie algebra epimorphism

\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}(J)\to TKK(J)
which is the identity on

\mathfrak{sl}_{2}\otimes J
and sends the symbol
\{a,b\}
to
\partial_{a,b}
.
It is clear from its definition that the
Tits-Allison-Gao construction
of
\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}(J)
defines a functor from Jordan algebras to Lie algebras, as is explained in
[
24
]
. Therefore, for any ideal
I
in
J
, the kernel of the Lie algebra morphism

\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}(J)\to\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}(J/I)

will be denoted as
\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}(I)
.

When the Jordan algebra
J
is associative,
TKK(J)
is obviously
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(J):=\mathfrak{sl}_{2}\otimes J
. In that case,
\{J,J\}
is the quotient
\Omega^{1}_{J}/\mathrm{d}J
of Kähler
1
-forms modulo exact forms, recovering an earlier result of Bloch
[
4
]
, who described the universal central extension of
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(A)
for commutative associative unital algebras

. Specializing to the case where

is a polynomial ring in one variable, we have
\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}(\mathbb{K}[a])=\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{K}[a])
. In particular, any element
a
of a Jordan algebra

induces a Lie algebra homorphism
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{K}[a])\to\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}(J)
.

(\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}(J),{\rm SL}_{2})
-modules

Set
{\rm SL}_{2}={\mathbf{SL}}_{2}(\mathbb{K})

An

By definition, an

Joseph functors and Weyl modules

We now reformulate our question in terms of Weyl modules.

Set
\mathfrak{B}=\mathfrak{G}_{0}\oplus\mathfrak{G}_{2}

\mathfrak{b}=\mathbb{K}h\oplus\mathbb{K}e

By definition, a

\mathfrak{B}

Let

The induced module

is called the

It satisfies the universal property

For any
(\mathfrak{B},B)
-module

It follows that
\Delta(V)
is a

(\mathfrak{G},{\rm SL}_{2})
-module. Moreover,
every morphism of
\mathfrak{B}
-modules

-modules

.

The Girard-Newton formula

We now introduce some notation.
A
partition

\sigma=(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{m})
is a nonincreasing
sequence of positive integers, that is,

\sigma_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\sigma_{m}\geq 1,\ \hbox{for some}\ m\geq 1
.

We say that
\sigma
is a partition of
n
(in short,
\sigma\vdash n
) if

\sigma_{1}+\cdots+\sigma_{m}=n
.
For
\sigma\vdash n
, let
C_{\sigma}
be the conjugacy class of all permutations

\tau\in S_{n}
with cycle lengths

\sigma_{1},\,\sigma_{2},\ldots,\sigma_{m}
,
let
|C_{\sigma}|
be its cardinality, and
let
\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)

be the common sign of the elements of
C_{\sigma}
.

For any
k\geq 1
, let
N_{k}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=x_{1}^{k}+\cdots+x_{n}^{k}

be the
k^{th}
-Newton polynomial
in
n
indeterminates
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
. For an arbitrary partition

\sigma=(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{m})\vdash n
, set

N_{\sigma}(x)=N_{\sigma_{1}}(x)N_{\sigma_{2}}(x)\cdots N_{\sigma_{m}}(x)
.

The following identity is usually called the

Girard-Newton formula
.

Lemma 3.5
.

We have

{\sum_{\sigma\ \vdash\ n}\,\hbox{sgn}(\sigma)\,|C_{\sigma}|\,N_{\sigma}(x)}=n!\,x_{1}\cdots x_{n}
.

Proof.

We recall the standard proof of this 17
th
century identity.
Let
V
be the vector space
\mathbb{K}^{n}
and
let
h:V\to V
be the diagonal matrix with diagonal entries

x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
.

The symmetric group
S_{n}
acts on
V^{\otimes n}
by permutation of the factors. The Schur idempotent
(1/n!)\sum_{\tau\in S_{n}}\hbox{sgn}(\tau)\tau

is a projection of
V^{\otimes n}
onto the
1
-dimensional subspace

\bigwedge^{n}V
, so

\mathrm{Tr}(h^{\otimes n}\circ\sum_{\tau\in S_{n}}\hbox{sgn}(\tau)\tau)=n!x_{1}\cdots x_{n}
.

Note that for
\sigma\vdash n
and
\tau\in C_{\sigma}
, we have
\mathrm{Tr}(h^{\otimes n}\circ\tau)=N_{\sigma}(x)
. It follows that

\mathrm{Tr}(h^{\otimes n}\circ\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n}}\hbox{sgn}(\sigma)\sigma)=\sum_{\sigma\vdash n}\,\hbox{sgn}(\sigma)\,|C_{\sigma}|\,N_{\sigma}(x)
,

from which the lemma follows.
∎

Let
A:=\mathbb{K}[Y_{1},Y_{2},\dots]
be the polynomial algebra in
infinitely many variables
Y_{1},Y_{2},\dots
. For any partition

\sigma=(\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{\ell})
of
n
, set

Y_{\sigma}=Y_{\sigma_{1}}Y_{\sigma_{2}}\cdots Y_{\sigma_{\ell}}
.

Let
t
be an indeterminate. In
A[[t]]
, the formal expression

\exp(-\sum_{k\geq 1}Y_{k}t^{k}/k)

can be expanded as
\sum_{k\geq 0}\,a_{k}t^{k}
, where all
a_{k}
belongs to
A
.

Lemma 3.6
.

We have

a_{n}=((-1)^{n}/n!)\,{\sum_{\sigma\ \vdash\ n}\hbox{sgn}(\sigma)|C_{\sigma}|Y_{\sigma}}.

Proof.

Both sides of the identity are polynomials
involving only the variables
Y_{1},\dots,Y_{n}
.
We will prove it for the elements
Y_{k}=N_{k}=x_{1}^{k}+\cdots+x_{n}^{k}

of the algebra
A^{\prime}=\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
. This is sufficient since

N_{1},\dots,N_{n}
are algebraically independent. Note that

\displaystyle-\sum_{k\geq 1}N_{k}t^{k}/k

\displaystyle=-\sum_{k\geq 1}\sum_{1\leq i\leq n}x_{i}^{k}t^{k}/k=-\sum_{1\leq i\leq n}\sum_{k\geq 1}x_{i}^{k}t^{k}/k=\sum_{1\leq i\leq n}\log(1-tx_{i}).

Hence

\exp(-\sum_{k\geq 1}N_{k}t^{k}/k)=\prod_{1\leq i\leq n}(1-tx_{i}).

It follows that
a_{n}=(-1)^{n}x_{1}\cdots x_{n}
, and
the identity now follows from Lemma
3.5
.
∎

The Dominance criterion

After this combinatorial digression, we now answer the question about dominant
J
-spaces.
Let
(V,\rho)
be a
J
-space and let

\{a^{n},a^{m}\}=0
for any positive
integers

and

, the operators
(\rho(a^{n}))_{n>0}
commute with each other. For any partition
\sigma=(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\dots,\sigma_{m})
, we define

\rho_{\sigma}(a):=\rho(a^{\sigma_{1}})\rho(a^{\sigma_{2}})\cdots\rho(a^{\sigma_{m}})

Theorem 1
.

A
J
-space
(V,\rho)
of level

\displaystyle{\sum_{\sigma\ \vdash\ n+1}\hbox{sgn}(\sigma)|C_{\sigma}|\rho_{\sigma}(a)=0}
,

for all

.

Proof.

For any integer
k
, we denote by

,
Z_{k}(V)
, and
\Delta_{k}(V)
the
corresponding eigenspaces in
M(V)
,

, and
\Delta(V)

By commutativity of
\mathfrak{G}_{-2}
, the enveloping
algebra

is the symmetric algebra

, and
by the Poincaré-Birkhoff-Witt Theorem,

M(V)=U(\mathfrak{G}_{-2})V
. It follows that

M_{n-2k}(V)=S^{k}(\mathfrak{G}_{-2})V
,

for any integer

. Since

\Delta(V)
is an
{\rm SL}_{2}
-module and

\Delta_{n+2}(V)=0
, we deduce that

\Delta_{-n-2}(V)=0
. It follows that

Z_{-n-2}(V)=M_{-n-2}(V)
and therefore

Z_{n}(V)=e^{n+1}M_{-n-2}(V)=e^{n+1}S^{n+1}(\mathfrak{G}_{-2})V

However,

is the kernel of the map

and

is spanned by the elements

with

. Therefore,

is dominant if and only if

e^{n+1}f(a)^{n+1}m=0
,

for all

and

\pi(e^{n+1}f(a)^{n+1})m=0
.

By Lemma
3.2
,

\pi(e^{n+1}f(a)^{n+1})
is the coefficient of

of the formal series

for some

,

which, by Lemma

, is equal to

r^{\prime}\,\,{\sum_{\sigma\ \vdash\ n+1}\hbox{sgn}(\sigma)|C_{\sigma}|\rho_{\sigma}(a)},
for some

.

Hence
V
is dominant if and only if

\sum_{\sigma\ \vdash\ n+1}\hbox{sgn}(\sigma)|C_{\sigma}|\rho_{\sigma}(a)=0,

for all
a\in J
.∎

A technical lemma

Recall that the algebra

Lemma 4.1

PART B: Good Filtrations in LA+ categories

6
LA+ categories

Definition of LA categories

Let
\mathcal{C}
be
\mathbb{K}
-linear abelian category.
We say that
\mathcal{C}
is a
locally artinian
category
(LA category) if

(LA1)

\mathcal{C}
admits
direct limits

\varinjlim\,A_{n}

for any directed family

A_{0}\to A_{1}\to A_{2}\to\cdots

of objects indexed by
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
,

(LA2)

the functor
\varinjlim

is exact, and

(LA3)

every object is the directed union

\bigcup_{n\geq 0}A_{n}
of a directed family of subobjects
A_{n}
of finite length.

By definition, a
directed union

\bigcup_{n\geq 0}A_{n}
will be a direct limit

\varinjlim\,A_{n}
of a directed family
(A_{n})_{n\geq 0}

where all maps
A_{n}\to A_{n+1}
are injective.
For concreteness, all directed unions will be indexed by
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, so Axiom (LA3) implies that all objects have finite or countable length.

In this paper, we handle inductive limits cautiously. In fact,
from the time of the groundbreaking paper of Cline, Parshall, and Scott
[
9
]
, it has been known that the paper
[
34
]
about the Grothendieck conditions is not fully correct
[
10
,
32
]
. These conditions are implied by our Axiom (LA2).

Definition of LA+ categories

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be an LA category.
Recall that the
socle
of an object

X
, denoted by
\mathrm{Soc}(X)
, is the largest semi-simple object in
X
. Indeed the axioms of an LA category ensure the existence of such an object. An

injective envelope
of an object

X
is an injective object
I\supseteq X
such that

\mathrm{Soc}\,I=\mathrm{Soc}\,X
. The first two axioms of an LA+ category deal with simple objects and their socles:

(AX1)

There is a set

\{L(n)\mid n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\}
of objects
in
{\mathcal{C}}
which is a complete list of nonisomorphic simple objects.

(AX2)

We assume that

\hbox{End}_{\mathcal{C}}(L(n))=\mathbb{K}
and that
L(n)
admits
an injective envelope
I(n)
in
\mathcal{C}
, for all

n\geq 0
.

Some
additional definitions are needed to state the third axiom.

A
filtration

{\mathcal{G}}_{*}
of an object
M
is a directed family
({\mathcal{G}}_{n}M)_{n\geq 0}
of subobjects for which
M
is the directed union

\bigcup_{n\geq 0}{\mathcal{G}}_{n}M
. If
{\mathcal{G}}_{n}M=M
for
n\gg 0
, the filtration is said to be
finite
; otherwise, it is
infinite
. For any
n\geq 0
, set

\overline{\mathcal{G}}_{n}M={\mathcal{G}}_{n}M/{\mathcal{G}}_{n-1}M
, with the convention that

\overline{\mathcal{G}}_{0}M={\mathcal{G}}_{0}M
.
The objects
\overline{\mathcal{G}}_{n}M
are called the
successive quotients
of the filtration
{\mathcal{G}}_{*}
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}(n)
be the subcategory of objects
M
which are constructed as finite or infinite extensions between the modules
L(0),L(1),\ldots,L(n)
. That is,
M

admits a filtration
{\mathcal{G}}_{*}
whose successive quotients

\overline{\mathcal{G}}_{m}M
are
0
or

L(k)
for some
k=k(m)\leq n
.

For

M\in{\mathcal{C}}
, let
{\mathcal{F}}_{n}\,M
be the biggest
subobject in
{\mathcal{C}}(n)
. The filtration

{\mathcal{F}}_{0}M\subseteq{\mathcal{F}}_{1}M\subseteq{\mathcal{F}}_{2}M\subseteq\cdots

is called the
canonical filtration
of
M
.
By definition, the
standard object

\nabla(n)
is the inverse image in
I(n)
of
{\mathcal{F}}_{n-1}(\,I(n)/L(n))
.

(a)

Axiom (AX1) provides a linear ordering

(b)

F(\bigcup_{n\geq 0}\,A_{n})=\bigcup_{n\geq 0}\,F(A_{n})
, for any directed union
\bigcup_{n\geq 0}\,A_{n}
. Then

R^{k}F(\bigcup_{n\geq 0}\,A_{n})=\varinjlim\,R^{k}F(A_{n})
,

for all
k\geq 1
.

Proof.

Let

A_{0}\longrightarrow A_{1}\longrightarrow A_{2}\longrightarrow\cdots

\hskip 5.69054pt\downarrow\hskip 25.60747pt\downarrow\hskip 28.45274pt\downarrow

I_{0}\hskip 2.84526pt\longrightarrow I_{1}\hskip 2.84526pt\longrightarrow I_{2}\longrightarrow\cdots

be the commutative diagram of Lemma
6.2
.

Set
A:=\cup_{n\geq 0}\,A_{n}
and

I:=\cup_{n\geq 0}\,I_{n}

Since the objects
I_{i}
are injective, the morphisms

I_{i}\to I_{i+1}
split. Thus by Lemma
6.1
,

I\simeq\oplus_{n\geq 0}I_{n}/I_{n-1}

is an injective object. Therefore,

R^{1}\,F(A)
is the cokernel of the
map

F(I)\to F(I/A)
.

However,

F(I)=\varinjlim F(I_{n})
and

F(I/A)=\varinjlim F(I_{n}/A_{n})
.

Since
R^{1}F(A_{n})
is the cokernel of the map

F(I_{n})\to F(I_{n}/A_{n})
,

it follows that

R^{1}F(A)=\varinjlim R^{1}F(A_{n})
.

Thus the lemma is proved for
k=1
. Since for
k\geq 2
, we have

R^{k}F(A)=R^{k-1}F(I/A)

and
R^{k}F(A_{n})=R^{k-1}F(I_{n}/A_{n})
,

the lemma follows, by induction over
k
.
∎

As an immediate corollary, we obtain:

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
X\in{\mathcal{C}}
be an object of finite length. For any directed union

\bigcup_{n\geq 0}\,A_{n}
, we have

\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{\mathcal{C}}(X,\bigcup_{n\geq 0}\,A_{n})=\varinjlim\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{\mathcal{C}}(X,A_{n})
.

7
The category of extended
\nabla(n)
-objects

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be an LA+ category as before.
In this section, we fix a nonnegative integer
n
once and for all.

A filtration
{\mathcal{G}}_{*}
of an object
M\in{\mathcal{C}}
is called a
standard filtration of type

\nabla(n)
if, for all
k\geq 0
,

\overline{\mathcal{G}}_{k}M
is a direct sum of
copies of the standard object
\nabla(n)
, where the possible number of copies ranges from
0
to

\aleph_{0}
. An object
M\in{\mathcal{C}}
is called an

extended
\nabla(n)
-object
if it admits a standard filtration of type
\nabla(n)
.

Since the axioms of highest weight categories imply that
extended
\nabla(n)
-objects are simply direct sums of copies of
\nabla(n)
, the notion of an extended
\nabla(n)
-object was not considered in
[
9
]
. This does not hold for LA+ categories in general, so we will need to study the structure of extended
\nabla(n)
-objects.

In the section, we show that the quotient
M/N
of two
extended
\nabla(n)
-objects is an extended
\nabla(n)
-object. We also show that successive extensions of extended
\nabla(n)
-objects are again extended
\nabla(n)
-objects.

Notation for Section
7

Let
{\mathcal{S}}^{0}(n)
be the category
whose objects are direct sums of copies of
\nabla(n)
, and let

{\mathcal{S}}^{1}(n)
be the category
of extended
\nabla(n)
-objects.

For technical reasons, we also need the notion of a doubly
extended
\nabla(n)
-object.
A filtration
{\mathcal{G}}_{*}
of an object
M\in{\mathcal{C}}
is called an
extended standard filtration of type

\nabla(n)
if

\overline{\mathcal{G}}_{k}M
is an extended
\nabla(n)
-object for all
k\geq 0
. An object
M\in{\mathcal{C}}
is called a

doubly extended
\nabla(n)
-object
if it admits an
extended standard filtration of type
\nabla(n)
.
Let
{\mathcal{S}}^{2}(n)
be the category of
doubly extended
\nabla(n)
-objects.

Fortunately we do not need to go further because it will be
proved that
{\mathcal{S}}^{2}(n)={\mathcal{S}}^{1}(n)
.

A basic lemma

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
M
be a doubly extended
\nabla(n)
-object.

(a)

We have

{\rm Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(\nabla(n)/L(n),M)=0
and

\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{\mathcal{C}}(\nabla(n)/L(n),M)=0
.

(b)

The restriction map

{\rm Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(\nabla(n),M)\to{\rm Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(L(n),M)

is an isomorphism.

(c)

The category
{\mathcal{S}}^{0}(n)
is
semisimple.

(d)

Let
N
and
H
be subobjects of
M
such that

N\in{\mathcal{S}}^{2}(n)
and
H\in{\mathcal{S}}^{0}(n)
.
Then
N\cap H
belongs to
{\mathcal{S}}^{0}(n)
.

Proof of (a).
The socle of
a doubly extended
\nabla(n)
-object is a direct sum of copies of

L(n)
, therefore
{\mathcal{F}}_{n-1}M=0
. Since

\nabla(n)/L(n)
is in
{\mathcal{C}}(n-1)
, we deduce that

{\rm Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(\nabla(n)/L(n),M)=0
.

We now prove that
\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{\mathcal{C}}(\nabla(n)/L(n),\nabla(n))=0
.
By the definition of standard objects and the canonical filtration, we have

{\mathcal{F}}_{n-1}(\nabla(n)/L(n))=\nabla(n)/L(n)

and
{\mathcal{F}}_{n-1}(I(n)/\nabla(n))=0
.

Therefore,

{\rm Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(\nabla(n)/L(n),I(n)/\nabla(n))=0
, from which it follows that

\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{\mathcal{C}}(\nabla(n)/L(n),\nabla(n))=0
.

Since
\nabla(n)/L(n)
has finite length, the functor

{\rm Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(\nabla(n)/L(n),-)
commutes with
directed unions.
Using Lemma
6.4
, we successively prove that

\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{\mathcal{C}}(\nabla(n)/L(n),M)=0

for

M\in{\mathcal{S}}^{0}(n)
, then for

M\in{\mathcal{S}}^{1}(n)
and finally for
M\in{\mathcal{S}}^{2}(n)
.

Proof of (b).
The kernel of the map

{\rm Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(\nabla(n),M)\to{\rm Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(L(n),M)

is
{\rm Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(\nabla(n)/L(n),M)
and
its cokernel lies in

\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{\mathcal{C}}(\nabla(n)/L(n),M)
.
Thus
f
is an isomorphism by Part (a).

Proof of (c).

Let
M
and
N
be objects in
{\mathcal{S}}^{0}(n)
. Part (b) implies that

{\rm Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(M,N)\simeq{\rm Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathrm{Soc}M,\mathrm{Soc}N)
,

and therefore the category
{\mathcal{S}}^{0}(n)

is semisimple. ∎

Proof of (d).
There is a unique object

X\in{\mathcal{S}}^{0}(n)
with

\mathrm{Soc}X=\mathrm{Soc}(N\cap H)
.

By Part (b), the inclusion maps

\mathrm{Soc}(N\cap H)\to M
,

\mathrm{Soc}(H\cap N)\to N
, and

\mathrm{Soc}(H\cap N)\to H

have unique extensions

\psi:X\to M
,
\psi^{\prime}:X\to N
and

\psi^{\prime\prime}:X\to H
.

By uniqueness, they are equal, which proves that
X
embeds in
N\cap H
.

Since the socle of
X
and
N\cap H
are the same, the quotient

(N\cap H)/X
is in
{\mathcal{C}}(n-1)
. However,

(N\cap H)/X
lies in
H/X
, which
is a direct sum of copies of
\nabla(n)

by Part (c). Hence we have
(N\cap H)/X=0
, i.e.

N\cap H=X
, which proves Assertion (d).
∎

The canonical standard filtration.

Lemma 7.2
.

Let
M\in{\mathcal{C}}

and let
H\subseteq M
be a subobject in
{\mathcal{S}}^{0}(n)
.

(a)

If
M
is an extended
\nabla(n)
-object, then

M/H
is an extended
\nabla(n)
-object.

(b)

If
M
is a doubly extended
\nabla(n)
-object, then

M/H
is a doubly extended
\nabla(n)
-object.

Proof.

We assume that
M
belongs to
{\mathcal{S}}^{1}(n)
and prove Assertion (a). Let

{\mathcal{G}}_{*}
be a standard filtration of type
\nabla(n)
on
M
.
It induces a filtration
{\mathcal{K}}_{*}
on
H
.
Set
M^{\prime}=M/H
, and let

{\mathcal{G}}_{*}^{\prime}
be the filtration on
M^{\prime}
induced
by
{\mathcal{G}}_{*}
.

For any
n\geq 0
, there is an exact sequence

0\to\overline{\mathcal{K}}_{n}\,H\to\overline{\mathcal{G}}_{n}\,M\to\overline{\mathcal{G}}^{\prime}_{n}\,M^{\prime}\to 0
.

By Lemma
7.1
(b),
\overline{\mathcal{K}}_{n}H

belongs to
{\mathcal{S}}^{0}(n)
, and by hypothesis,

\overline{\mathcal{G}}_{n}\,M

also belongs to
{\mathcal{S}}^{0}(n)
. Thus by
Lemma
7.1
(c),
\overline{\mathcal{G}}^{\prime}_{n}\,M^{\prime}
is a direct
sum of standard modules, that is,
{\mathcal{G}}_{*}^{\prime}
is a standard filtration. Hence

M/H
is an extended
\nabla(n)
-object.

The proof of Assertion (b) is almost identical. We now assume
that
M
belongs to
{\mathcal{S}}^{2}(n)
, and let

{\mathcal{G}}_{*}
be an extended standard filtration of type
\nabla(n)
for
M
.
We define induced filtrations
{\mathcal{K}}_{*}
on
H
and
{\mathcal{G}}_{*}^{\prime}
on
M^{\prime}=M/H
as before. In the short exact sequence

0\to\overline{\mathcal{K}}_{n}\,H\to\overline{\mathcal{G}}_{n}\,M\to\overline{\mathcal{G}}^{\prime}_{n}\,M^{\prime}\to 0
,

\overline{\mathcal{K}}_{n}\,H
again belongs to

{\mathcal{S}}^{0}(n)
, and by hypothesis

\overline{\mathcal{G}}_{n}\,M

belongs to
{\mathcal{S}}^{1}(n)
. Therefore by
Assertion (a),
\overline{\mathcal{G}}^{\prime}_{n}\,M^{\prime}
is an
extended
\nabla(n)
-object, which proves that
{\mathcal{G}}_{*}^{\prime}
is an
extended standard filtration. Hence

M/H
is a doubly extended
\nabla(n)
-object.
∎

Let
M
be a doubly extended
\nabla(n)
-object.
By Lemma
7.1
(b),

M
contains a unique subobject
{\mathcal{H}}\,M
in

{\mathcal{S}}^{0}(n)
with
\mathrm{Soc}({\mathcal{H}}\,M)=\mathrm{Soc}\,M
.
We define a nondecreasing sequence of subobjects

{\mathcal{H}}_{0}M\subseteq{\mathcal{H}}_{1}M\subseteq{\mathcal{H}}_{2}M\dots

as follows. First set

{\mathcal{H}}_{0}M={\mathcal{H}}M
. For
m>0
, define

{\mathcal{H}}_{m}M
as the inverse image of

{\mathcal{H}}(M/{\mathcal{H}}_{m-1}M)
in
M
.
Thanks to the previous lemma, we verify by induction
that each quotient
M/{\mathcal{H}}_{m-1}M
is an extended
\nabla(n)
-module. Therefore the procedure is well defined, and by construction, the successive quotients are in
{\mathcal{S}}^{0}(n)
.

Lemma 7.3
.

The nondecreasing sequence

({\mathcal{H}}_{m}M)_{m\geq 0}
is a standard filtration for the doubly extended
\nabla(n)
-object
M
. In particular, any doubly extended
\nabla(n)
-object is an
extended
\nabla(n)
-object.

Proof.

It has just been proved that the successive quotients
are direct sums of copies of
\nabla(n)
.
To prove that the family
({\mathcal{H}}_{m}M)_{m\geq 0}
is a standard filtration, it remains to prove that
M=\bigcup_{m\geq 0}{\mathcal{H}}_{m}M
.

For
m\geq 0
, the
m^{th}
-socle
of
M
, denoted by

\mathrm{Soc}^{m}\,M
, is inductively defined by

(a)

\mathrm{Soc}^{0}\,M=\mathrm{Soc}\,M
and

(b)

\mathrm{Soc}^{m}\,M
is the inverse image of

\mathrm{Soc}(M/\mathrm{Soc}^{m-1}M)
in
M
.

By Axiom (LA3), we have

M=\bigcup_{n\geq 0}\,\mathrm{Soc}^{m}\,M
. Since
{\mathcal{H}}_{m}M
contains

\mathrm{Soc}^{m}\,M
, we have
M=\bigcup_{m\geq 0}{\mathcal{H}}_{m}M
.

Therefore the family of subobjects

({\mathcal{H}}_{m}M)_{m\geq 0}
exhausts
M
.
∎

The filtration
{\mathcal{H}}_{*}
is called the
canonical standard filtration of type

\nabla(n)
for
M
.

Quotients of extended
\nabla(n)
-objects

Lemma 7.4
.

Let
M,\,N
be extended
\nabla(n)
-objects with
N\subseteq M
.

Then the quotient
M/N
is an extended
\nabla(n)
-object.

Proof.

It follows from the definition that

{\mathcal{H}}_{0}N=N\cap{\mathcal{H}}_{0}M
. Thus by induction, we have

{\mathcal{H}}_{m}N=N\cap{\mathcal{H}}_{m}M
,

for all
m\geq 0
. Let
{\mathcal{G}}_{*}
be the filtration
of
M/N
induced by the filtration
{\mathcal{H}}_{*}
of

M
.

There is a short exact sequence

0\to\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{m}N\to\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{m}M\to\overline{\mathcal{G}}_{m}M/N\to 0
.

By hypothesis,
\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{m}N
and

\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{m}M
are direct sums
of the standard object
\nabla(n)
. By Lemma
7.1
(c),
the category
{\mathcal{S}}^{0}(n)
is semi-simple. Thus

\overline{\mathcal{G}}_{m}(M/N)
is a direct sum of standard objects

\nabla(n)
.
Hence
{\mathcal{G}}_{*}
is a standard filtration of
M/N
, which proves that
M/N
is an extended
\nabla(n)
-object.
∎

8
Good filtrations in LA+ categories

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be an LA+ category.
A filtration
{\mathcal{G}}_{*}
of an object
M\in{\mathcal{C}}
is called
good
if, for all
k\geq 0
,

\overline{\mathcal{G}}_{k}M
is an extended
\nabla(n)
-object
for some
n=n(k)
.

To the best of our knowledge, good filtrations originated in
the context of algebraic group representations, in the work of
Humphreys and Jantzen
[
19
]
. In this setting,
the canonical filtration first appeared in
[
14
]
, and our presentation closely follows
[
29
]
.

We will show that the canonical filtration of
M
is good
whenever
M
admits a good filtration.
Then we show that a quotient
M/N
has a good filtration whenever both objects
M
and
N
have good filtrations.
In the context of algebraic groups, the last statement is due to Donkin
[
12
]
.
Our generalization is based on the approach of

[
30
]
.

The functor
R^{1}{\mathcal{F}}_{m}

Let
\mathcal{F}_{0}M\subseteq\mathcal{F}_{1}M\subseteq\mathcal{F}_{2}M\subseteq\cdots
be the canonical filtration defined in Section
6.2
. For
m\geq 0
, the endofunctor
\mathcal{F}_{m}:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}
is left exact by construction. Let
R^{1}{\mathcal{F}}_{m}
be its first right derived functor.

Lemma 8.1
.

For any
M
with a good filtration, we have

R^{1}{\mathcal{F}}_{m}M=0
,

for all
m\geq 0
.

Proof.

First we prove that

R^{1}{\mathcal{F}}_{m}\nabla(n)=0
, or equivalently, that

\phi:{\mathcal{F}}_{m}I(n)\to{\mathcal{F}}_{m}(I(n)/\nabla(n))

is surjective. Let
X
be the inverse image in
I(n)
of

{\mathcal{F}}_{m}(I(n)/\nabla(n))
. By definition, there is a short exact sequence

0\to\nabla(n)\to X\to{\mathcal{F}}_{m}(I(n)/\nabla(n))\to 0
.

If
m<n
, then
X/L(n)
belongs to

{\mathcal{C}}(n-1)
and therefore
X\subseteq\nabla(n)
. It follows that
{\mathcal{F}}_{m}(I(n)/\nabla(n))=0
, and
\phi
is obviously surjective.

If
m\geq n
, then
{\mathcal{F}}_{m}X=X
, so
X\subseteq\mathcal{F}_{m}I(n)
and
\phi
is again surjective.

Therefore, it is proved that
R^{1}{\mathcal{F}}_{m}\nabla(n)=0
.
The functor
{\mathcal{F}}_{m}
commutes with
directed unions. Using Lemma
6.3
, we succesively prove that

R^{1}{\mathcal{F}}_{m}M=0
for
M\in{\mathcal{S}}^{0}(n)
, then for

M\in{\mathcal{S}}^{1}(n)
, and finally for any object
M
with a good filtration.
∎

The Zassenhaus condition

Let
M
be an object in
{\mathcal{C}}
with a filtration

{\mathcal{F}}_{*}^{\prime}
. Let

{\mathcal{G}}_{*}^{\prime}
be another filtration of
M
.
In the next subsection,

{\mathcal{F}}_{*}^{\prime}
will be the canonical filtration

{\mathcal{F}}_{*}
and
{\mathcal{G}}_{*}^{\prime}
will be a good filtration

{\mathcal{G}}_{*}
, but for the present subsection

{\mathcal{F}}_{*}^{\prime}
and
{\mathcal{G}}_{*}^{\prime}
are arbitrary, but given once and for all.

We say that
{\mathcal{G}}_{*}^{\prime}
satisfies
the
Zassenhaus condition
if,
for all
k,n\geq 0
,

{\mathcal{G}}_{k}^{\prime}M\cap{\mathcal{F}}_{n}^{\prime}M={\mathcal{G}}_{k-1}^{\prime}M\cap{\mathcal{F}}_{n}^{\prime}M,\hbox{\ or\ }{\mathcal{G}}_{k}^{\prime}M\subseteq{\mathcal{G}}_{k-1}^{\prime}M+{\mathcal{F}}_{n}^{\prime}M.

For
n\geq 0
, let
A(n)
be the set of integers
k
such that

{\mathcal{G}}_{k}^{\prime}M\cap{\mathcal{F}}_{n}^{\prime}M\neq{\mathcal{G}}_{k-1}^{\prime}M\cap{\mathcal{F}}_{n}^{\prime}M\hbox{\ and\ }{\mathcal{G}}_{k}^{\prime}M\cap{\mathcal{F}}_{n-1}^{\prime}M={\mathcal{G}}_{k-1}^{\prime}M\cap{\mathcal{F}}_{n-1}^{\prime}M.

Obviously, the Zassenhaus condition is equivalent to

{\mathcal{G}}_{k}^{\prime}M\subseteq{\mathcal{G}}_{k-1}^{\prime}M+{\mathcal{F}}_{n}^{\prime}M
for all
n\geq 0
and

k\in A(n)
.

Zassenhaus Lemma 8.2
.

Assume that the filtration

{\mathcal{G}}_{*}^{\prime}
satisfies the Zassenhaus condition.

Then
\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{n}^{\prime}M
has a filtration whose
successive quotients are the objects

\overline{\mathcal{G}}_{k}^{\prime}M
for
k\in A(n)
.

Proof.

For abelian groups, the proof is obvious.
In general, the proof is identical, except that it requires the Axiom (LA2).∎

Objects with a good filtration

Lemma 8.3
.

Let
M
be an object in
{\mathcal{C}}
with a good filtration, and let
(\mathcal{F}_{n}M)_{n\geq 0}
be the canonical filtration of
M
. Then

(a)

\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{n}M
is an
extended
\nabla(n)
-object for all
n\geq 0
, and

(b)

the canonical filtration of
M

is good.

Proof.

Let
{\mathcal{G}}_{*}
be a good filtration of

M
. For
n\geq 0
, let
A(n)
be the set of integers
k
such that

{\mathcal{G}}_{k}M\cap{\mathcal{F}}_{n}M\neq{\mathcal{G}}_{k-1}M\cap{\mathcal{F}}_{n}M\hbox{\ and\ }{\mathcal{G}}_{k}M\cap{\mathcal{F}}_{n-1}M={\mathcal{G}}_{k-1}M\cap{\mathcal{F}}_{n-1}M.

Step 1.
We claim that

(1)

for any
n\geq 0
,
A(n)
is the set of
k
such that

\overline{\mathcal{G}}_{k}M
is a nonzero
extended
\nabla(n)
-object and

(2)

{\mathcal{G}}_{*}
satisfies the Zassenhaus condition.

Let
k\in A(n)
. By definition,
\overline{\mathcal{G}}_{k}M

is an extended
\nabla(m)
-object for some integer
m
.

Viewed as a subobject of

M/{\mathcal{G}}_{k-1}M
,

\overline{\mathcal{G}}_{k}M
has a nontrivial intersection
with
(\mathcal{G}_{k-1}M+\mathcal{F}_{n}M)/\mathcal{G}_{k-1}M
.
Therefore we have
m\leq n
and
\overline{\mathcal{G}}_{k}M=0
.

By Lemma
8.1
we have
R^{1}{\mathcal{F}}_{n-1}({\mathcal{G}}_{k-1}M)=0
. It follows that

{\mathcal{F}}_{n-1}(M/{\mathcal{G}}_{k-1}M)={\mathcal{F}}_{n-1}M/({\mathcal{G}}_{k-1}M\cap{\mathcal{F}}_{n-1}M)
.

Since
\overline{\mathcal{G}}_{k}M
does not intersect
(\mathcal{G}_{k-1}M+\mathcal{F}_{n}M)/\mathcal{G}_{k-1}M={\mathcal{F}}_{n-1}M/({\mathcal{G}}_{k-1}M\cap{\mathcal{F}}_{n-1}M)
, we conclude that
m>n-1
. That is,
m=n
, which proves the first claim.

Since
\overline{\mathcal{G}}_{k}M
is an extended
\nabla(n)
-object,
it lies in
{\mathcal{F}}_{n}(M/{\mathcal{G}}_{k-1}M)
.
By Lemma
8.1
, we have
R^{1}{\mathcal{F}}_{n}({\mathcal{G}}_{k-1}M)=0
, and therefore

{\mathcal{F}}_{n}(M/{\mathcal{G}}_{k-1}M)={\mathcal{F}}_{n}M/({\mathcal{G}}_{k-1}M\cap{\mathcal{F}}_{n}M)=(\mathcal{F}_{n}M+\mathcal{G}_{k-1}M)/\mathcal{G}_{k-1}M
,

which proves that

{\mathcal{G}}_{k}M\subseteq{\mathcal{G}}_{k-1}M+{\mathcal{F}}_{n}M
.

Step 2
Since the Zassenhaus condition is satisfied,
Lemma
8.2
implies that

\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{n}M
has a filtration whose
successive quotients are the objects

\overline{\mathcal{G}}_{k}M
for
k\in A(n)
.
We have also proved that for
k\in A(n)
, the object

\overline{\mathcal{G}}_{k}M
is an extended
\nabla(n)
-object. By
Lemma
7.3
, any doubly
extended
\nabla(n)
-object is an extended
\nabla(n)
-object. Hence
the canonical filtration of
M
is good.
∎

Quotients of objects with a good filtration

We conclude with the main result of
this section.

Proposition 8.4
.

Let
N\subseteq M
be objects in

{\mathcal{C}}
with a good filtration. Then

(a)

M/N
has a good filtration, and

(b)

{\mathcal{F}}_{n}(M/N)={\mathcal{F}}_{n}M/{\mathcal{F}}_{n}N
,
for all
n\geq 0
.

Proof.

Let
n\geq 0
. Assertion (b) is a consequence of
Lemma
8.1
. It follows that

\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{n}(M/N)
is isomorphic to

\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{n}M/\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{n}N
.

By Lemma
8.3
,
\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{n}M
and

\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{n}N
are extended
\nabla(n)
-objects.
Thus by Lemma
7.4
,
\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{n}(M/N)

is an extended
\nabla(n)
-object. Hence the canonical filtration of

M/N
is good, which proves Assertion (a).
∎

Remark:
The proof of Lemma

8.4
shows that the canonical filtration of

M/N
is induced by the canonical filtration of
M
.
By contrast, when
M\subseteq N
are extended
\nabla(n)
-objects,
the proof of Lemma
7.4
shows that the canonical standard filtration of

M
induces a standard filtration of
M/N
, but not necesarily the canonical one.

Definition of highest weight categories

and generalized highest weight categories

Cline, Parshall, and Scott
[
9
]
introduced the notion of a
highest weight category
(HW category). The most important exemples of HW categories are the category
\mathcal{O}
, the category of
modular representations of Chevalley groups, and the category of perverse sheaves with respect to a stratification by cells.

These categories enjoy many cohomology vanishing results. For the applications we have in mind, we will need to work in a somewhat more general setting. We relax one axiom of a HW category to define the notion of a
generalized highest weight category
(generalized HW category), and prove a useful cohomology vanishing result in this setting.

Definition of HW categories and generalized HW categories

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be a LA+ category, with the notation given in Section
6.2
. The category
{\mathcal{C}}
is called a
HW category
if it satisfies the following two axioms:

(HW1)

the canonical filtration of
I(n)
is good, and

(HW2)

{\mathcal{F}}_{n}I(n)=\nabla(n)
,
for all
n\geq 0
.

When only the first axiom (HW1) is satisfied, the category

{\mathcal{C}}
is called a
generalized HW category
.

Assume that
{\mathcal{C}}
is a generalized HW category.
Since

\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{{\mathcal{C}}}(\nabla(n),\nabla(n))={\rm Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(\nabla(n),I(n)/\nabla(n))
,

we conclude that
the axiom (HW2) is equivalent to

\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{{\mathcal{C}}}(\nabla(n),\nabla(n))=0
, for all
n\geq 0
.

Thus, a HW category is a generalized HW category where the extended
\nabla(n)
-objects are direct sums of standard modules, for all
n\geq 0
.

For simplicity, we have assumed that the category

\mathcal{C}
contains countably many nonisomorphic simple objects. There are similar definitions when

\mathcal{C}
contains only finitely many objects.

(a)

A basic example of a HW category is

(b)

For a

(c)

With the usual basis

of

let

be the category of

About the definition of HW and generalized HW categories

Our definition of HW categories is more constructive and essentially equivalent to the original one, namely Definition 3.1 of
[
9

in

is necessarily the standard module

Next, by Lemma

(HW

Therefore

However, in our setting, it would be unnatural to require Axiom 3.1(c)(iii) of

That’s why our definition of a good filtration is a bit different. While Cline, Parshall, and Scott

An

-vanishing theorem

We will now state an easy cohomology vanishing result in generalized HW categories. For clarity, we will also
state the stronger version obtained by specializing to HW categories.

Let
M
be an object of a generalized HW category
\mathcal{C}
, and
let

. We inductively define a sequence

in

as follows. With the convention that

,

and for

Theorem 6
.

Assume that
M
has a good filtration.

(a)

If
{\mathcal{C}}
is a generalized HW category, then

\mathrm{Ext}_{\mathcal{C}}^{n}(L(k),M)=0
, for any

.

(b)

If
{\mathcal{C}}
is an HW category,
then
m(0)\leq 1
and

m(k)\leq 1+m(k-1)
, for all
k>0
.

In particular, all the
m(k)
are finite when
\mathcal{C}
is an HW category.

Proof.

Step 1.
Let
0\to M\to I_{0}\to I_{1}\to I_{2}\to\cdots
be a minimal injective resolution of
M
. We claim that
\mathrm{Ext}_{\mathcal{C}}^{n}(L(k),M)\simeq{\rm Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(L(k),I_{n})
for all
k,n\geq 0
.

Since

{\rm Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(L(k),I_{n})={\rm Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(L(k),\mathrm{Soc}\,I_{n})
,
the induced complex

{\rm Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(L(k),I_{0})\to{\rm Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(L(k),I_{1})\to{\rm Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(L(k),I_{2})\to\cdots

has zero differentials, which proves the claim.

Step 2.

Now we prove by induction on
k
that
\mathrm{ht}(I_{n})>k
whenever
n\geq m(k)
.

For
k=0
, the hypothesis that
\mathrm{Ext}_{\mathcal{C}}^{m(0)}(L(k),M)=0
is equivalent to
{\rm Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(L(0),I_{m(0)})=0
. Hence we have

\mathrm{ht}(I_{m(0)})>0
. By Lemma
10.2
(b), we deduce that
\mathrm{ht}(I_{n})>0
for any

.

Now let
k>0
be arbitrary. By the induction hypothesis, we already
know that
\mathrm{ht}(I_{m(k)})>k-1

The hypothesis that
\mathrm{Ext}_{\mathcal{C}}^{m(k)}(L(k),M)=0
is equivalent to
{\rm Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(L(k),I_{m(k)})=0

\mathrm{ht}(I_{m(k)})>k
. By Lemma

(b), we deduce that
\mathrm{ht}(I_{n})>k
for any

.

Step 3.

It follows that

{\rm Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(L(k),I_{n})=0
for any

,
which proves the Assertion (a).
Assertion (b) is an obvious consequence of Lemma
10.2
(c).

∎

.

Theorem 7
.

The
\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}(J)
-module
\Delta(n)
is smooth and finite dimensional.

Proof.

Let
v\in\mathbb{K}_{n}
be a generator of
\Delta(n)
.

Step 1.
First we prove that
f(J_{+}^{(n)}).v=0
.

Let
a\in J_{+}
. Since

\Delta_{-n}(n)=\mathbb{K}.f^{n}v
, we have
f(a)^{n}.v=0
,
and therefore

\pi(\,e^{n-1}f(a)^{n}).v=0
.

By Lemma
3.2
,

\pi(\,e^{n-1}f(a)^{n})
is, up to a scalar multiple, the coefficient of

t^{n-1}
in the formal series

\left(\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}f(a^{r+1})t^{r}\right)\exp\left(-\sum_{s=1}^{\infty}\frac{h(a^{s})}{s}t^{s}\right).

Since
a
belongs to
J_{+}
, we have
h(a^{s}).v=0
, for any
s>0
. Thus

\,e^{n-1}f(a)^{n}.v=rf(a^{n}).v
, for some nonzero
r
.

Hence
f(J_{+}^{(n)}).v=0
.

Step 2.
By Zelmanov’s Nil Corollary
11.3

J_{+}^{(n)}
contains
J_{+}^{N}
for some
N
.
We now prove that
\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}(J_{+}^{N}).\Delta(n)=0
.

Since
\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}(J_{+}^{N})
is an ideal and
v
generates
\Delta(n)
, it is enough to prove that

\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}(J_{+}^{N}).v=0
.

Since

h(J_{+}).v
,
e(J_{+}).v
and
f(J_{+}^{N}).v
are all zero,
we have

(\mathfrak{sl}_{2}\otimes J_{+}^{N}).v=0
.

Moreover we have

[e(J_{+}),f(J_{+}^{N})]=\{J_{+}^{N},J_{+}\}
modulo

\mathfrak{sl}_{2}\otimes J_{+}^{N}
.

Thus, we also have

\{J_{+}^{N},J_{+}\}.v=0
, which proves that

\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}(J_{+}^{N}).v=0
.

Step 3.
It remains to prove that
\Delta(n)
is finite dimensional.
Since
\Delta(n)
is a
(\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(J/J_{+}^{N})
-module, we can assume
that
J_{+}^{N}=0
. Therefore
J
is finite dimensional, and

\Delta(n)
is finite dimensional by
Lemma
3.9
(a).

This completes the proof.

∎

Remark:
A
J
-space
M
of level
k
is

if for every
v\in M
, we have
h(J_{+}^{n})v=0
for
n=n(v)\gg 0

that a dominant smooth

Lie algebra cohomology and smooth representations

As in the previous Section, let
J
be a finitely generated augmented Jordan algebra. In this section, we compare
\hbox{Ext}^{*}
in the category
of smooth modules with relative Lie algebra cohomology.

From now on, we consider only representations of finite or countable dimension for Lie algebras and algebraic groups.

Generalities about algebraic groups

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a finite dimensional perfect Lie algebra.
Then
\mathfrak{g}\simeq\mathfrak{s}\ltimes\mathfrak{u}
where
\mathfrak{s}
is semi-simple and

\mathfrak{u}
is the nilpotent radical.
Let
G
be the unique connected and simply connected algebraic group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. There is a Levi decomposition
G=S\ltimes U
, where
S
is semi-simple and
U
is unipotent, with Lie algebras
\mathfrak{s}
and
\mathfrak{u}
, respectively.

In what follows, a rational
G
-module will simply be called a

G
-module
. Let
\mathrm{Mod}(G)
be the category of

G
-modules.
It is well known that
\mathfrak{u}
acts nilpotently
on any finite dimensional
\mathfrak{g}
-module.
It follows that any locally finite dimensional
\mathfrak{g}
-module is a
G
-module, and conversely.

The group
G
acts on
U
as follows:
U
acts by left multiplication and
S
by conjugation. With respect to this action,
the ring
\mathbb{K}[U]
of rational functions on
U
is a
G
-module.

Since
\mathbb{K}[U]
is an injective
U
-module and since
S
acts
reductively, the
G
-module
\mathbb{K}[U]
is injective. In fact,

\mathbb{K}[U]
is the injective envelope of the trivial module.
Hence
M\otimes\mathbb{K}[U]
is an injective envelope of
M
, for any simple
G
-module
M
.

Generalities about group cohomology

Let
\mathfrak{g}
,
\mathfrak{s}
,
\mathfrak{u}
,
G
,
S
, and
U
be defined as in the previous subsection.
Let
M\in\mathrm{Mod}(G)
. By definition, the
relative cohomology group

H^{*}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{s},M)
is the cohomology of the Chevalley-Eilenberg cocomplex
{\rm Hom}_{\mathfrak{s}}(\bigwedge^{p}\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{s},M)
. See

[
26
]
or
[
27
]
for details. Since
\mathfrak{s}
acts reductively, we have

H^{*}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{s},M)\simeq H^{*}(\mathfrak{u},M)^{\mathfrak{s}}
.

As an algebraic variety,
U
is isomorphic to its Lie algebra

\mathfrak{u}
, thus
the de Rham complex
\bigwedge\mathfrak{u}^{*}\otimes\mathbb{K}[U]
is exact.
For any
U
-module
M
, it follows easily that

H^{*}(\mathfrak{u},M)\simeq H^{*}(U,M)
,

a result often attributed to Malcev.
As
\mathfrak{s}
is reductive, Hochschild proved that
the relative cohomology
H^{*}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{s},-)
describes extensions in the relative category
of
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{s})
-modules
[
18
]
.
1
1

1

A more recent account appears in
[
31
]
.

Combining this result with the Malcev isomorphism, we deduce the
following well known result:

Lemma 13.1
.

Let
M
and
N
be
G
-modules, with
M
finite dimensional. Then

H^{*}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{s},{\rm Hom}_{\mathbb{K}}(M,N))=\mathrm{Ext}^{*}_{\mathrm{Mod}(G)}(M,N)
.

The pro-algebraic group
{\mathbf{SL}}_{2}(J)

For any integer

, the Lie algebra

\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}(J/J_{+}^{n})=\mathfrak{sl}_{2}\ltimes\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}(J_{+}/J_{+}^{n})

Let

\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}(J_{+}/J_{+}^{n})
and set

G_{n}={\rm SL}_{2}\ltimes U_{n}

{\mathbf{SL}}_{2}(J)
is the proalgebraic group
\varprojlim G_{n}
, and an

{\mathbf{SL}}_{2}(J)
-module
is a direct union

\bigcup_{n\geq 1}\,M_{n}
of
G_{n}
-modules
M_{n}
, where,
as always, we assume that
\dim M\leq\aleph_{0}
. Since the Lie algebra
\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}(J/J_{+}^{n})
is perfect and finite dimensional,
any smooth
\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}(J)
-module is an
{\mathbf{SL}}_{2}(J)
-module and conversely.

The category
\mathrm{Mod}({\mathbf{SL}}_{2}(J))={\mathcal{C}}_{sm}(J)

Let
\mathrm{Mod}({\mathbf{SL}}_{2}(J))
be the category of

{\mathbf{SL}}_{2}(J)
-modules. By the material in Section
13.1
,
\mathrm{Mod}({\mathbf{SL}}_{2}(J))
coincides with the category
{\mathcal{C}}_{sm}(J)

of smooth
\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}(J)
-modules. Depending on the context,
we will use one terminology or the other.

Let
n\geq 0
. In what follows, the simple
{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}
-module

L(n)
of
dimension
n+1
will be viewed as a

\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}(J)
-module with trivial action of
\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}(J_{+})
.
It is clear that the family
(L(n))_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}
is a
complete list of simple objects in
\mathrm{Mod}({\mathbf{SL}}_{2}(J))
.

Set
U=\varprojlim U_{m}
. By definition, its ring of rational functions is
\mathbb{K}[U]=\varinjlim\mathbb{K}[U_{m}]
. Viewing
\mathbb{K}[U]
as a
{\mathbf{SL}}_{2}(J)
-module, let

I(n)=L(n)\otimes\mathbb{K}[U]
.

Lemma 13.2
.

Let
n\geq 0
. Then

(a)

I(n)
is the injective envelope of
L(n)
and

(b)

\Delta(n)^{*}
is the standard object

\nabla(n)
with socle
L(n)
.

Proof.

Clearly
L(n)\otimes\mathbb{K}[U_{m}]
is the injective envelope of
L(n)
in the category
\mathrm{Mod}({\mathbf{SL}}_{2}(J/J_{+}^{m})
, which proves Assertion (a), and shows the existence of the standard object
\nabla(n)
. By construction, the cosocle of
\Delta(n)
is

, so
Soc(\Delta(n)^{*})=L(n)
and
\Delta(n)^{*}\subseteq I(n)
, from which it follows that
\Delta(n)^{*}
is contained in the standard object
\nabla(n)
with socle
L(n)
.

Conversely, the
\Delta(n)
surjects onto the dual

\nabla(n)^{*}
of the standard module, since the highest eigenspace of
\nabla(n)
is the
J
-space
\mathbb{K}_{n}
. Dualizing, we see that
\nabla(n)
is contained in
\Delta(n)^{*}
.

\Delta(n)^{*}=\nabla(n)
. ∎

The category

Smooth cohomology

We will now see how to compute
\mathrm{Ext}
-groups
in the category
\mathrm{Mod}({\mathbf{SL}}_{2}(J))=\mathcal{C}_{sm}(J)
of smooth
\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}(J)
-modules.

Let
M
be a
smooth
\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}(J)
-module. A relative

k
-cochain

\omega\in{\rm Hom}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}(\bigwedge^{k}(\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}(J)/\mathfrak{sl}_{2}),M)

is called
smooth
if
k=0
, or

\omega(x_{1}\wedge x_{2}\wedge\cdots\wedge x_{k})=0
,
whenever
x_{1}\in\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}(J_{+}^{N})
,

for
N\gg 0
. Let
C^{k}_{sm}(M)
be the space of smooth relative
k
-cochains with values in
M
. By definition, the smooth cohomology

H^{*}_{sm}(\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}(J),\mathfrak{sl}_{2},M)

is the homology of the complex
C_{sm}^{*}(M)
. It follows that

H^{*}_{sm}(\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}(J),\mathfrak{sl}_{2},M)=\varinjlim H^{*}(\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}(J/J_{+}^{n}),\mathfrak{sl}_{2},M)
.

Thus the folklore Lemma
13.1
implies the following result:

Lemma 13.4
.

Let
M,\,N
be smooth

\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}(J)
-modules
with
M
finite dimensional. Then

\mathrm{Ext}^{*}_{\mathrm{Mod}({\mathbf{SL}}_{2}(J))}(M,N)=H^{*}_{sm}(\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}(J),\mathfrak{sl}_{2},{\rm Hom}_{\mathbb{K}}(M,N))
.

Comparison with relative homology.

Let
M
be a finite dimensional
{\mathbf{SL}}_{2}(J)
-module.
In general, it is difficult to compare the smooth cohomology

H^{*}_{sm}(\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}(J),\mathfrak{sl}_{2},M)
with the Chevalley-Eilenberg relative homology

H_{*}(\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}(J),\mathfrak{sl}_{2},M^{*})
.

However, it is possible to do so when
M
and
J
have compatible gradings. A

positively graded Jordan algebra
is a Jordan algebra endowed with a grading

J=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\,J_{n}
such that

J_{0}=\mathbb{K}
. It is viewed as an augmented
Jordan algebra, with augmentation ideal

J_{+}:=\bigoplus_{n>0}J_{n}
. The Lie algebra

\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}(J)
inherits the grading of
J
.

Given a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded vector space

E=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}E_{n}
, the
graded dual
of
E
is the space

\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}E_{n}^{*}
.

Lemma 13.5
.

Assume that
J
is a finitely generated positively graded Jordan
algebra. Let
M
be a finite dimensional
\mathbb{Z}
-graded

\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}(J)
-module. Then

(a)

M
is smooth.

(b)

H^{k}_{sm}(\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}(J),\mathfrak{sl}_{2},M)
is the graded dual
of
H_{k}(\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}(J),\mathfrak{sl}_{2},M^{*})
,
for any integer
k\geq 0
.

Proof.

Assertion (a) is obvious and Assertion (b) follows from the fact that

C^{k}_{sm}(M^{*})
is the graded dual of the space

H_{0}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2},\bigwedge^{k}(\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}(J)/\mathfrak{sl}_{2})\otimes M)

of relative chains.
∎

Assume again that
J
is a finitely generated positively graded Jordan
algebra. The simple modules
L(n)
can be viewed
as
\mathbb{Z}
-graded modules, with grading concentrated in degree
0
. The following statement is
an immediate consequence of Lemmas
13.4
and
13.5
.

Corollary 13.6
.

For any
k,n\geq 0
,
the group
\mathrm{Ext}^{k}_{\mathrm{Mod}({\mathbf{SL}}_{2}(J))}(L(n),L(0))
is the graded dual of

H_{k}(\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}(J),\mathfrak{sl}_{2},L(n))
.

Some Ext-vanishing results.

Theorem 13.7
.

Let
J
be the free unital Jordan algebra
J(D)
on
D>0
generators. Then

(a)

\mathrm{Ext}^{k}_{\mathrm{Mod}({\mathbf{SL}}_{2}(J(D))}(L(0),L(0))=0
for
k=1,\,2,\,3
.

(b)

\mathrm{Ext}^{2}_{\mathrm{Mod}({\mathbf{SL}}_{2}(J(D))}(L(2),L(0))=0
.

Proof.

By Corollary
13.6
,

\mathrm{Ext}^{k}_{\mathrm{Mod}({\mathbf{SL}}_{2}(J(D))}(L(n),L(0))
is the graded dual of
H_{k}(\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}(J(D)),\mathfrak{sl}_{2},L(n))
.
Thus Theorem
13.7
is equivalent to the following known results about relative homology:

(a)

H_{1}(\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}(J),\mathfrak{sl}_{2},\mathbb{K})=0
, which is obvious,

(b)

H_{2}(\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}(J),\mathfrak{sl}_{2},\mathbb{K})=0
, due to
[
3
]
,

(c)

H_{3}(\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}(J),\mathfrak{sl}_{2},\mathbb{K})=0
, proved in

[
24
, Prop 3]
,

(d)

H_{2}(\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}(J),\mathfrak{sl}_{2},L(2))=0
, proved in

[
24
, Lemma 13]
.

∎

\mathrm{Mod}({\mathbf{PSL}}_{2}(J(D)))

Let
J
be a finitely generated augmented Jordan algebra. Set

{\mathbf{PSL}}_{2}(J)={\mathbf{SL}}_{2}(J)/\pm 1
.
Then
\mathrm{Mod}({\mathbf{PSL}}_{2}(J))
is the category
of
{\mathbf{SL}}_{2}(J)
-modules with even eigenvalues.
The simple modules in

\mathrm{Mod}({\mathbf{PSL}}_{2}(J))
are ordered by their dimensions and the

(n+1)^{st}
simple module is
L(2n)
.
By Corollary
13.3
,
\mathrm{Mod}({\mathbf{PSL}}_{2}(J))
is an LA+ category.

The free Jordan algebra

J=J(D)
is viewed as an augmented Jordan algebra with augmentation ideal
J_{+}=\bigoplus_{n>0}J(D)_{n}
. In this section, we consider the category
\mathrm{Mod}({\mathbf{PSL}}_{2}(J(D)))
, relating it to the framework of
Ext
-vanishing and generalized highest weight categories introduced in Part B.

Motivation

Let
J
be a finitely generated augmented Jordan algebra, and let
p
be a prime number.
The category
\mathrm{Mod}({\mathbf{PSL}}_{2}({\overline{\mathbb{F}}}_{p}))
of rational

{\mathbf{PSL}}_{2}({\overline{\mathbb{F}}}_{p})
-modules and the category
\mathrm{Mod}({\mathbf{PSL}}_{2}(J))
look very similar. In fact, Donkin
[
11
]
and Koppinen

[
25
]
showed that
\mathrm{Mod}({\mathbf{PSL}}_{2}({\overline{\mathbb{F}}}_{p}))
satisfies the axioms of a HW category (though this notion was introduced only some time later in
[
9
]
). The category
\mathrm{Mod}({\mathbf{PSL}}_{2}(J))
is
LA+
. However,
even in the most favorable case when

is the free Jordan algebra
J(D)
, the following proposition shows
that
\mathrm{Mod}({\mathbf{PSL}}_{2}(J(D)))
is not a HW category.

Proposition 14.1
.

For
J=J(D)
,

\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{\mathrm{Mod}({\mathbf{PSL}}_{2}(J))}(\nabla(2),\nabla(2))\simeq(J_{+}/J_{+}^{3})^{*}.

The proof follows from

[
24
, Lemma 21]
. The details are left to the reader.

Nonetheless, for the free Jordan algebra

the Lie algebra

\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}(J(D))
is free in a certain category

[
24
]

This raises the natural question:

Is the category
\mathrm{Mod}({\mathbf{PSL}}_{2}(J(D)))

Some evidence

Several computations make it seem plausible that
\mathrm{Mod}({\mathbf{PSL}}_{2}(J(D)))

\mathrm{Mod}({\mathbf{PSL}}_{2}(J(D)))

-object. This conclusion

or

The first interesting case is when

.

Lemma 14.2
.

If

, the
\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}(J)
-module

is a

The standard module

Consequences

Theorem 8

If

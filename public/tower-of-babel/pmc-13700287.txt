The status quo and influencing factors of the utilization of Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM) health services among older adults in China: a nationwide cross-sectional study
Authors: Yu-fei Qiu, Jia-ying Fu, Xiao-hong Zhang, Jia-li Liu, Ya-ke Yue, Juan Gu, Meng-jie Tong, Fen Yang, Chun-yan Bai
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundWith the proposal of active aging and healthy aging, the elderly population’s demand for health services is increasing. Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM), known for its simplicity, low cost, and proven effectiveness, has become a strategic option for addressing elderly health needs and reducing disease burden.MethodsData were drawn from the 2018 China Health and Retirement Longitudinal Study (CHARLS), covering 28 provinces and 450 communities. Descriptive analysis was used to report sociodemographic characteristics and health status. To identify the factors influencing the utilization of TCM health services among older adults, we first conducted χ² analysis to examine associations between categorical variables, followed by binary logistic regression to determine significant predictors.ResultsA total of 5,950 participants were included in the analysis. This study found that the utilization of TCM health services among older adults remains relatively low. The average number of times utilized TCM health services was 0.46 ± 1.03. Logistic regression analysis showed that the utilization of TCM health services among older adults was significantly influenced by predisposing factors (age and no alcohol consumption), enabling factors (supplementary medical insurance), and need factors (depression, multimorbidity, and self-assessed health status).ConclusionsThe utilization of TCM health services among older Chinese adults remains low and limited in scope. Future research should focus on expanding access, optimizing the allocation of TCM resources, training professionals, and promoting innovative research to better serve an aging population.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12906-026-05483-3.

Introduction

Population aging has emerged as one of the most pressing medical, social, and economic challenges worldwide [1]. According to the World Health Organization (WHO) [2], the proportion of people aged 60 and above is expected to nearly double globally, from 12.0% in 2015 to 22.0% by 2050. China, the country with the largest aging population, is experiencing an unprecedented demographic shift. By 2050, the number of individuals aged 65 and older is projected to reach 395 million [3]. With increasing life expectancy, chronic diseases have become a predominant health concern among older adults, particularly the high prevalence of multimorbidity [4, 5]. These health challenges not only compromise the physical and mental well-being of the elderly [6, 7] but also impose a significant economic burden on both individuals and the healthcare system [8]. For instance, diabetes, one of the most prevalent chronic conditions among older adults, affects 23.90% of those aged 60 ~ 69 and rises to 27.30% in those aged 70 and above [9]. The socioeconomic cost of diabetes in China is projected to surge from US$250.2 billion in 2020 to US$460.4 billion by 2030, with per capita expenses escalating from US$231 to US$414 [10]. Against the dual backdrop of accelerated aging and the growing burden of chronic diseases, optimizing geriatric health services has become a critical priority for achieving healthy aging.

Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM) is receiving growing attention as an integrative approach to address the complex and evolving health needs of older adults. With the rising prevalence of chronic diseases, older individuals are exhibiting more diverse and personalized healthcare demands [11]. In this context, TCM, with its emphasis on individualized interventions and holistic care, has demonstrated potential in both the prevention and management of chronic conditions among older adults [12, 13]. Specifically, by tailoring treatments to an individual’s physiological constitution, TCM offers a personalized model of care that may enhance health outcomes and overall quality of life [14, 15]. Rooted in the principle of “treating diseases before they manifest” [16], TCM emphasizes proactive and preventive strategies, which are especially relevant for an aging population at risk of multimorbidity. In the meantime, Therapeutic modalities such as acupuncture and Chinese herbal medicine have been shown to support immune function and reduce the incidence of chronic diseases [17]. Additionally, traditional health-preserving practices such as Tai Chi and Baduanjin are increasingly recognized for their role in promoting both physical and mental well-being [18, 19]. Regular practice of these techniques helps maintain physical health and contributes to mental well-being by reducing stress and anxiety [20, 21]. Overall, the application of TCM health services plays a significant role in improving health outcomes and enhancing the quality of life among older adults. Therefore, it is necessary to investigate further the current utilization and influencing factors of TCM health services among older adults to maximize the benefits of TCM health services.

Despite the growing body of research on TCM, the majority of studies have primarily focused on its clinical efficacy, with limited attention to the status quo of the utilization among older adults. For instance, previous studies have examined the provision of TCM health services in elderly care institutions in provinces such as Jiangxi and Zhejiang, offering valuable localized insights [22, 23]. However, the current literature remains largely institution-centric and geographically constrained, focusing primarily on urban nursing homes in economically developed regions. This institution-centric focus leaves substantial gaps in our understanding of how older adults access and utilize TCM health services in other contexts, including community-based and home-based care across diverse geographic areas. Additionally, Liu et al. [24] investigated the prevalence and determinants of TCM utilization; however, their analysis did not specifically focus on older adults as a distinct population. Given the variability in predisposing characteristics, resource accessibility, and health needs among older adults, it is significant to investigate this demographic separately. Moreover, many existing studies primarily focused on specific geographic areas or patient groups and often lack nationally representative samples [25, 26]. These limitations highlight the importance of conducting more comprehensive and nationally representative investigations.

Although previous studies have provided valuable insights into TCM health service utilization, important gaps remain. Most existing studies have focused on specific regions or populations, limiting the generalizability of their findings. In addition, limited evidence is available regarding the factors associated with TCM health service utilization among older adults from a theoretical perspective. To address these gaps, this study adopts Andersen’s Behavioral Model of Health Services Use (BMHSU), which proposes that healthcare utilization is influenced by predisposing, enabling, and need factors. BMHSU is particularly suitable for examining TCM health service utilization because the decision to use TCM services is shaped not only by individuals’ health needs, but also by demographic characteristics and access-related resources. By integrating these multiple dimensions, the model provides a comprehensive framework for understanding variations in TCM health service utilization among older adults. Based on data availability in the CHARLS database, variables representing these three dimensions were included in the analysis.

Therefore, this study aimed to (1) examine the current status of TCM health service utilization among older adults in China and (2) identify the predisposing, enabling, and need factors associated with TCM health service utilization. Using nationally representative data, this study seeks to provide evidence for improving the accessibility and effectiveness of TCM health services for China’s aging population.

Methods

Data sources

This study is based on the 2018 survey data of the China Health and Retirement Longitudinal Study (CHARLS). The 2018 wave was selected because it is the most recent CHARLS survey that includes detailed measures of Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM) health service utilization, which is the primary focus of this study; such variables were not collected in the 2020 wave, and earlier waves were considered less suitable due to their temporal distance. The CHARLS is a large-scale interdisciplinary and nationally representative survey project, hosted by the National Development Research Institute of Peking University and jointly executed by the Chinese Social Science Research Center of Peking University and the Youth League Committee of Peking University [27]. It is high-quality microdata representing the households and individuals of middle-aged and older Chinese adults over the age of 45. The CHARLS National Baseline Survey was launched in 2011 and followed for two years, with 23,000 respondents in 12,400 households. Subsequent follow-up surveys were conducted in 2013 (Wave 2), 2015 (Wave 3), and 2018 (Wave 4) [28].

For the present study, participants were selected according to the following criteria: (1) age ≥ 60 years; and (2) availability of information on TCM health service utilization and all covariates included in the regression models. Participants were excluded if they were younger than 60 years or had missing data on key study variables.

After applying these inclusion and exclusion criteria, 11,045 participants aged ≥ 60 years were identified from the CHARLS 2018 dataset. Participants with missing information on the predefined study variables, including TCM health service utilization and covariates included in the regression models, were excluded using a complete-case analysis approach. A total of 13,866 participants with missing data were excluded. The final analytical sample consisted of 5,950 older adults (Fig. 1).

Dependent variable

The main dependent variable in this study is a binary indicator of whether older adults have utilized TCM health services in the past year (yes/no). These include whether individuals have used services such as Chinese herbal medicine, acupuncture and moxibustion, and occupational therapy for chronic diseases in the past year and whether they have visited TCM hospitals for treatment. The number of times each participant utilized TCM health services was also recorded for descriptive analyses.

Independent variables

This study adopts the widely used BMHSU as the theoretical framework. According to the Andersen model, the decision-making process regarding health services utilization is influenced by three factors: predisposing factors, enabling factors, and need factors [29, 30]. Guided by this framework, variables available in the CHARLS database were categorized accordingly. Predisposing factors reflect an individual’s propensity to use healthcare services and include demographic and social characteristics. Enabling factors represent the resources that facilitate access to healthcare. Need factors refer to both perceived and evaluated health conditions that drive healthcare-seeking behavior. Although BMHSU encompasses a broader range of determinants, the present study included only variables available in the CHARLS dataset.

The independent variables in this study are categorized into three main aspects [31–33]: (1) Predisposing factors included demographic and social characteristics, including age (60 ~ 69 years, 70 ~ 79 years, and ≥ 80 years), gender, educational level, marital status (Not married or widowed/Married), smoking status, alcohol consumption, physical activity and place of residence (City/Countryside). (2) Enabling factors included factors related to healthcare resources and accessibility, including supplementary medical insurance (Yes/No), healthcare accessibility, medical resource availability, and household income. (3) Need factors included health-related conditions, including depression, chronic disease burden, self-rated health, physical disability, and cognitive function. Depression was assessed using the 10-item Center for Epidemiologic Studies Depression Scale (CES-D-10), with a total score ≥ 10 indicating depressive symptoms. Chronic disease burden was assessed based on the presence of multiple chronic conditions and categorized as no multiple chronic conditions and multiple chronic conditions. Self-rated health was classified as good, fair, or poor. Physical disability was measured as a binary variable (yes/no) based on self-report. Cognitive function was assessed using the simplified Mini-Mental State Examination (MMSE), which evaluates temporal orientation, memory, attention, calculation, and visuospatial ability, with a total score ranging from 0 to 21.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS 26.0 software. Descriptive statistics were applied to analyze sociodemographic characteristics, health status, and the utilization of TCM health services. The chi-square test and binary logistic regression analysis were used to investigate factors affecting the utilization of TCM health services among older adults. A significance level of P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant for all tests.

Results

The demographic characteristics of the participants

The study included a total of 5,950 participants, with a mean age of 67.93 ± 5.97 years. Among them, 4,422 (74.32%) had never utilized TCM health services, 912 (15.33%) had utilized one type of TCM health service, and 616 (10.35%) had utilized two or more types of TCM health services. The mean number of TCM health service utilizations among older adults in China was 0.46 ± 1.03. The predominant TCM health services utilized by the participants were herbal medication (91.58%, n = 1,512), acupuncture and moxibustion (2.60%, n = 43), and occupational therapy (1.64%, n = 27), while other modalities collectively accounted for 4.18% (n = 69). The mean cognitive ability score was 11.63 ± 3.67. Additional participant characteristics are presented in Table 1.

Discussion

These findings indicate that the utilization of TCM health services among older adults remains relatively low. Guided by the BMHSU, this study examined the influence of predisposing, enabling, and need factors on TCM health service utilization. The results showed that age and alcohol consumption were significant predisposing factors, supplementary medical insurance functioned as an important enabling factor, while depression, multimorbidity, and self-assessed health status represented key need factors associated with TCM utilization.

Utilization of TCM health services among older adults

This study found that the utilization of TCM health services among older adults remains relatively low. Specifically, 74.32% of respondents reported never using any form of TCM health service, while only 25.68% had utilized such services. Although this proportion is slightly higher than that reported in a previous national study conducted in 2015 [24], the overall utilization level remains limited.

From the perspective of the BMHSU, healthcare utilization is determined by the interaction of predisposing characteristics, enabling resources, and health-related needs. Although TCM has received increasing policy support and is recognized as an important component of healthy aging in China, the relatively low utilization observed in this study suggests that barriers to TCM service uptake remain among older adults. These barriers may include limited service accessibility, insufficient enabling resources, and differences in perceived healthcare needs and preferences. Unlike some conventional healthcare services, TCM utilization is also influenced by cultural beliefs and perceptions regarding its effectiveness and acceptability. While TCM is deeply rooted in Chinese health traditions and familiar to many older adults, cultural recognition alone may not translate into actual service use when access to services or perceived health needs are insufficient. Therefore, TCM utilization should be understood within both the structural framework of the BMHSU and the broader cultural context of healthcare decision-making. With the increasing burden of multimorbidity and age-related functional decline among older adults [34, 35], improving access to appropriate TCM services may contribute to healthy aging and comprehensive chronic disease management. Future strategies should focus on strengthening community-based TCM services, improving insurance coverage, and reducing barriers that hinder older adults from converting healthcare needs into actual service utilization.

Predisposing factors influencing TCM health service utilization

This study showed that age and place of residence emerged as influencing factors. Specifically, older adults aged 70 ~ 79 were significantly more likely to use TCM health services compared to those aged 60 ~ 69. With advancing age [36], individuals in the young-old cohort (60 ~ 69 years) tend to experience relatively fewer chronic health conditions, leading to reduced healthcare utilization compared to the 70 ~ 79 age group. However, the difference in TCM health service utilization between the ≥ 80 age group and those aged 60 ~ 69 was not statistically significant. This may reflect barriers such as mobility issues, cognitive decline, and caregiver dependency among the oldest-old, which limit access to services [37]. In addition, non-drinkers were more likely to utilize TCM health services than drinkers. This finding may reflect differences in health-related behaviors and health awareness. Older adults who avoid alcohol consumption may generally adopt healthier lifestyles and pay greater attention to preventive healthcare, which could increase their willingness to seek TCM services. However, the underlying mechanisms require further investigation.

Enabling factors of TCM health service utilization

According to the BMHSU, supplementary medical insurance represents an important enabling resource that facilitates healthcare utilization by improving financial access. In this study, older adults with supplementary medical insurance were more likely to utilize TCM health services, which is consistent with previous findings [38]. This association suggests that financial protection may play an important role in translating healthcare needs into actual service use among older adults. TCM services often involve long-term health maintenance, chronic disease management, and preventive care, which may require repeated healthcare visits and continuous financial investment. Supplementary insurance may reduce out-of-pocket expenses and lower economic barriers, thereby increasing older adults’ willingness and ability to access TCM services. Furthermore, the effect of insurance coverage may be particularly important for older adults with chronic conditions, whose healthcare needs are more persistent and may otherwise remain unmet due to financial constraints. Therefore, expanding insurance coverage for TCM-related services and improving reimbursement accessibility may help reduce inequities in healthcare utilization and support more comprehensive health management among older adults.

Need factors driving TCM health service utilization

Health-related need factors, including depression, chronic disease burden, and self-assessed health, were significantly associated with TCM health service utilization among older adults. Within the framework of the BMHSU, these factors represent both evaluated needs and perceived needs that motivate individuals to seek healthcare services.

Depression was strongly associated with increased TCM utilization, suggesting that older adults experiencing psychological distress may perceive TCM as a potential approach for addressing both physical and emotional health concerns. Unlike many conventional healthcare approaches that focus primarily on disease-specific treatment, TCM emphasizes the interaction between mind and body and promotes holistic health regulation. This integrative perspective may increase the acceptability of TCM among older adults seeking support for emotional well-being [39]. However, further research is needed to clarify the mechanisms underlying the relationship between psychological conditions and TCM utilization.

Similarly, older adults with multiple chronic diseases were more likely to use TCM health services, consistent with previous findings [40]. Multimorbidity increases healthcare demands and often involves complex treatment regimens, which may contribute to medication burden and difficulties in long-term disease management [41]. Given that TCM emphasizes individualized treatment and overall health regulation, it may provide a complementary approach for older adults managing multiple chronic conditions by addressing symptoms, functional status, and overall well-being [42].

In addition, poor self-assessed health was associated with greater utilization of TCM services, highlighting the importance of perceived needs in healthcare-seeking behavior. Older adults who perceive their health as poor may actively seek TCM services for prevention, health maintenance, or symptom relief, even when objective diagnoses are absent [43]. Overall, TCM utilization among older adults appears to be shaped by the interaction between objective health conditions and subjective health perceptions. Recognizing these diverse healthcare needs may help improve the accessibility and appropriateness of TCM services for aging populations.

Limitations

The study has several limitations. First, although CHARLS is a longitudinal survey, the present analysis was based on data from a single survey wave. Consequently, the observed associations should not be interpreted as causal relationships. Second, several variables, including TCM health service utilization, chronic disease status, self-rated health, and physical disability, were derived from self-reported information and may be subject to recall bias or reporting bias. Third, a considerable number of participants were excluded because of missing data on cognitive function and depressive symptoms, which were key variables in the analysis. As a result, the final analytical sample may differ systematically from the source population, potentially introducing selection bias and limiting the generalizability of the findings. Future studies should employ longitudinal designs to explore temporal changes and causal pathways in TCM utilization. In addition, applying advanced methods for handling missing data, such as multiple imputation and sensitivity analyses, may help reduce potential bias and further strengthen the robustness of the findings.

Conclusion

This study, grounded in Andersen’s Behavioral Model, identifies key factors influencing TCM utilization. Future strategies should focus on improving access to TCM services by addressing both the financial and cultural barriers, with policies aimed at enhancing insurance coverage and community-based support systems. By aligning TCM health services with the health needs and preferences of older adults to promote a healthy China and active aging.

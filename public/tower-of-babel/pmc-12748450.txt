Single cell RNA sequencing decodes cellular heterogeneity and identifies prognostic immune signatures in bladder cancer microenvironment
Authors: Kai Cao, Yunfeng Shi, Bin Wu, Honglei Shi, Zhong Lv, Hongqian Guo, Rong Yang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundBladder cancer is a common urinary system malignancy with a complex immune microenvironment that affects treatment outcomes. Single-cell RNA sequencing technology offers new opportunities to understand cellular heterogeneity within tumors, but systematic analysis of immune-related genes in bladder cancer remains limited.MethodsWe performed multi-omics analysis on bladder cancer samples using TCGA database analysis, single-cell RNA sequencing, cell clustering, immune gene expression analysis, and network pharmacology to study cellular heterogeneity and potential therapeutic targets.ResultsWe identified 8 upregulated immune genes significantly associated with poor survival: CCL20, IL17RB, IL37, PLAU, INSL4, KNG1, SLURP1, and PAEP. Single-cell analysis revealed major cell types including B cells, T cells, dendritic cells, epithelial cells, fibroblasts, mast cells, monocytes, and smooth muscle cells. We identified 5 distinct fibroblast subgroups and 10 T cell subgroups with different functional characteristics. Network pharmacology analysis of the PLAU gene showed that traditional Chinese medicine compounds had good binding capacity with the PLAU protein, validated by molecular dynamics simulation.ConclusionThis study identified 8 prognosis-related bladder cancer immune genes and revealed tumor microenvironment heterogeneity. The findings, particularly the PLAU therapeutic target, provide important evidence for bladder cancer immunotherapy and personalized treatment strategies.Keywords: Bladder cancer, Single-cell RNA sequencing, Immune microenvironment, Prognostic markers

Introduction

Bladder cancer, as one of the most common malignant tumors in the urinary system, has a consistently high incidence rate globally and carries a high risk of recurrence and metastasis, seriously threatening patient health and life [1, 2]. Despite certain advances in surgery, chemotherapy, and immunotherapy in recent years, patient prognosis still shows significant differences. These differences are not only related to the genetic heterogeneity of the tumor itself but are also closely related to complex immune cell interactions within the tumor microenvironment. The tumor immune microenvironment consists of various immune cells (such as T cells, B cells, macrophages, etc.), stromal cells (such as fibroblasts, endothelial cells), and secreted cytokines, chemokines, etc [3–5]. Its dynamicbalance directly affects tumor development, treatment response, and clinical prognosis. However, traditional research has mostly focused on the genetic characteristics of tumor cells, and systematic analysis of the immune microenvironment is still insufficient, making it difficult to accurately predict patient prognosis and formulate personalized treatment strategies [6, 7].

With the rapid development of high-throughput sequencing technology, single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) technology has brought revolutionary breakthroughs to tumor immune microenvironment research [8, 9]. Compared with traditional bulk sequencing technology, scRNA-seq can analyze the gene expression characteristics of individual cells, revealing the heterogeneity of cell subpopulations and intercellular interaction networks [10]. Through this technology, researchers can map the “cell atlas” of the tumor microenvironment, identify functional states and dynamic changes of different cell types, and thus gain deep insights into the impact of the immune microenvironment on tumor progression [11, 12]. In addition, multi-omics integrated analysis (such as combining transcriptome, proteome, metabolome, and other data) further enhances our understanding of complex tumor biological mechanisms, providing possibilities for discovering novel prognostic markers and potential therapeutic targets [13, 14].

Currently, research on the bladder cancer immune microenvironment still faces many challenges [15]. On one hand, existing studies are mostly based on population-level analysis, making it difficult to analyze subtype differences and functional heterogeneity of immune cells; on the other hand, the expression patterns of immune-related genes in different cell types and their impact on prognosis have not been systematically elucidated. Therefore, there is an urgent need to deeply mine the characteristics of the bladder cancer immune microenvironment through integrated multi-omics data, identify immune subtypes with prognostic predictive value, and explore their potential therapeutic strategies [16].

This study aims to systematically analyze the cellular heterogeneity and immune gene functions of the bladder cancer immune microenvironment through multi-omics integrated analysis. The study comprehensively utilizes bulk transcriptome data from the TCGA database and single-cell RNA sequencing data, combined with survival analysis, cell communication network construction, pseudotime trajectory inference, and network pharmacology analysis methods, to identify key immune genes and cell subtypes related to bladder cancer prognosis and explore potential therapeutic targets. The research results are expected not only to provide new biomarkers for bladder cancer prognosis assessment, but also to lay a theoretical foundation for the development of precision immunotherapy and personalized treatment strategies. The conduct of this study will help promote bladder cancer immune microenvironment research to develop at a deeper level and provide important evidence for improving the clinical outcomes of bladder cancer patients.

Methods

Data acquisition and preprocessing

The Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database served as the source for single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) data, accessed through accession number GSE. Raw count matrices from bladder cancer tissues underwent processing through a standardized integration workflow. Using Seurat’s NormalizeData function, count data received log-normalization with a 10,000 scale factor. The FindVariableFeatures function identified highly variable genes through the “vst” method, with the top 2,000 most variable genes selected for subsequent analysis. Multi-sample integration utilized Seurat’s integration workflow, applying canonical correlation analysis (CCA) and mutual nearest neighbors (MNN) for batch effect correction while maintaining biological variation. The ScaleData function performed data scaling, removing unwanted variation sources including total UMI counts, mitochondrial gene percentage, and cell cycle scores determined through Seurat’s CellCycleScoring function using established S-phase and G2/M-phase marker genes.

Dimensionality reduction analysis and cell clustering

Scaled data underwent principal component analysis (PCA) using the top 2,000 highly variable genes. Multiple approaches determined the optimal principal component number: elbow plot analysis examining standard deviations, JackStraw procedure statistical significance testing, and visual PC loading inspection. These analyses guided selection of the top 15–20 principal components for subsequent analysis. High-dimensional transcriptome data underwent dimensionality reduction through the Uniform Manifold Approximation and Projection (UMAP) algorithm, enabling two-dimensional spatial visualization of cell populations. Seurat’s FindNeighbors and FindClusters functions facilitated unsupervised clustering by constructing shared nearest neighbor (SNN) graphs, followed by Louvain clustering with varying resolution parameters (0.1-1.0) to determine optimal clustering granularity. Silhouette analysis and biological interpretability guided final clustering resolution selection.

Cell type annotation and marker gene identification

A multi-step approach accomplished cell type annotation: cluster-specific marker gene identification using FindAllMarkers function with Wilcoxon rank-sum test, comparison against established cell type signature databases, and manual curation based on recognized cell type-specific markers. Major identified cell types encompassed B lymphocytes, vascular endothelial cells, epithelial cells, stromal fibroblasts, immune effector cells, mononuclear phagocytes, vascular smooth muscle cells, and T lymphocytes. Cell type-specific marker gene identification employed differential gene expression analysis with specified parameters: log fold change threshold ≥ 0.25, minimum gene expression percentage ≥ 0.1 per cell, and Bonferroni-corrected adjusted p-value < 0.05.

Molecular subtype identification and non-negative matrix factorization

Gene expression data underwent dimensionality reduction decomposition using the non-negative matrix factorization (NMF) algorithm to reveal potential molecular subtype patterns. The NMF package’s nmf function operated with decomposition parameters k = 2–10, with optimal molecular subtype number determined through silhouette coefficient and cophenetic correlation coefficient evaluation. Each molecular subtype underwent characteristic gene profile analysis, revealing key marker genes including PICA, KRT19, MCTP1, ACRT1, LUM, DCN, BGN, and MYBR. Sample grouping utilized hierarchical clustering algorithm with Euclidean distance and Ward.D2 clustering methods for similarity dendrogram construction. Heatmap visualization displayed characteristic gene expression patterns across samples.

Trajectory inference and pseudotime analysis

Developmental relationships and differentiation dynamics between cell populations were inferred using Monocle 3 for pseudotime trajectory analysis. Seurat objects underwent conversion to Monocle 3-compatible CellDataSet objects. Within Monocle 3, cells received re-clustering to define partitions and trajectories. Principal graph learning utilized the learn_graph function, with root node selection based on biological knowledge and early-stage marker gene expression. The order_cells function calculated pseudotime values, indicating each cell’s relative trajectory position. Multiple trajectory path branch events received separate identification and analysis, reconstructing continuous cell state transition processes. Dynamic expression pattern identification along pseudotime employed differential expression analysis using the graph_test function, with genes showing q-values < 0.05 and significant pseudotime progression correlation considered dynamically expressed.

Cell communication network analysis

Intercellular signaling analysis utilized the CellChat package to quantify communication strength between cell types. Validated ligand-receptor interaction databases enabled separate calculation of outgoing and incoming signaling patterns. Major signaling pattern identification employed the identifyCommunicationPatterns function, while signal flow direction visualization used the netAnalysis_river function. Signaling factor correlations underwent Pearson correlation analysis to identify synergistic or antagonistic signaling molecule pairs, with correlation coefficient threshold |r| >0.3 and p-value < 0.05.

Transcriptional regulatory network analysis

Key transcription factor identification and regulated gene network analysis employed the SCENIC package for regulon activity analysis. Gene regulatory network inference initially used the GENIE3 algorithm, followed by RcisTarget for transcription factor binding site enrichment analysis, and finally AUCell for single-cell regulon activity evaluation. UMAP dimensionality reduction space mapping displayed transcriptional regulatory network activation patterns, with plotTSNE_cellProps function visualizingregulon activity distribution. Transcriptional regulator and target gene regulatory relationship strength underwent correlation analysis evaluation, with Pearson correlation coefficient threshold |r| >0.4 and p-value < 0.001.

Functional enrichment analysis

Systematic evaluation of key biological process and signaling pathway activation status employed gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) using the clusterProfiler package. Analysis encompassed GO biological processes (BP), molecular functions (MF), cellular components (CC), and KEGG signaling pathways. The enrichGO and enrichKEGG functions operated with parameters pvalueCutoff = 0.05 and qvalueCutoff = 0.2. GAR and HGF signaling pathways underwent specific activation status analysis across different cell types. Signaling pathway score calculation utilized the AddModuleScore function, with heatmap visualization displaying differential signaling pathway activation patterns.

Prognostic analysis and survival modeling

Kaplan-Meier survival analysis using the survival package evaluated relationships between key gene expression levels and patient clinical prognosis. For survival analysis, patients were dichotomized into high and low expression groups based on median gene expression values for each of the eight identified immune genes (CCL20, IL17RB, IL37, PLAU, INSL4, KNG1, SLURP1, and PAEP). Median gene expression-based patient stratification into high and low expression groups utilized the survfit function for survival curve fitting and survdiff function for log-rank testing. Independent prognostic factor identification employed Cox proportional hazards regression models for multivariate survival analysis. Gene expression levels, age, gender, and tumor stage served as model covariates. Optimal predictive model construction used the stepAIC function for variable selection. C-index and time-dependent ROC curves evaluated model performance.

Molecular dynamics simulation

Bladder cancer-related key protein molecular dynamics simulation employed the GROMACS software package. Protein Data Bank (PDB) database provided protein three-dimensional structures, with CHARMM36 force field parameterization. TIP3P water molecule boxes contained proteins with appropriate counter-ion addition for system charge neutralization. Energy minimization proceeded for 50,000 steps, followed by sequential NVT and NPT ensemble equilibration, each lasting 100 ps. NPT ensemble final production simulation ran for 100 ns total with 2 fs time steps. Structural parameter calculations including root mean square deviation (RMSD), radius of gyration, and solvent accessible surface area employed tools such as gmx rms, gmx gyrate, and gmx sasa.

Free energy landscape analysis

GROMACS and custom scripts were used for principal component analysis to identify major modes of protein motion. Two-dimensional free energy landscapes were constructed based on the first two principal components, using the weighted histogram analysis method (WHAM) to calculate free energy surfaces. Covariance analysis was performed using gmx covar to identify correlated motion patterns between different residues of the protein. Eigenvector analysis was conducted using gmx anaeig to identify collective motion modes of the protein. Free energy landscapes were visualized using matplotlib and seaborn, with color gradients representing different free energy levels.

Statistical analysis and visualization

All statistical analyses were performed in R (version 4.2.0) using Seurat (version 4.3.0), Monocle 3 (version 1.3.1), CellChat (version 1.6.0), SCENIC (version 1.3.0), and other Bioconductor packages. Data visualization was performed using ggplot2, ComplexHeatmap, pheatmap, and custom plotting functions. Pearson correlation coefficients were used for correlation analysis, and appropriate tests were used to assess statistical significance. Results are presented as mean ± standard deviation unless otherwise stated. Statistical significance for all analyses was defined as p < 0.05. For survival analysis of the eight prognostic genes, Bonferroni correction was applied with an adjusted significance threshold of p < 0.0063 (0.05/8 genes). For differential gene expression analysis using FindAllMarkers function, Bonferroni correction was used with adjusted p-value < 0.05. Functional enrichment analyses employed FDR correction with q-value < 0.2 for KEGG pathways and q-value < 0.05 for GO terms. Cell communication network analysis used FDR correction with q-value < 0.05 for correlation significance testing. Multiple comparisons were corrected using Bonferroni or FDR methods. All computational analyses were performed on high-performance computing clusters with sufficient memory and processing power to handle large-scale single-cell datasets and molecular dynamics simulations.

Discussion

Our research established a detailed atlas of the bladder cancer immune microenvironment using integrated multi-omics methodologies, revealing eight prognostically significant immune genes that offer valuable scientific insights for comprehending bladder cancer molecular pathogenesis and formulating precision therapeutic approaches.

Through intersection analysis, we identified eight immune genes with elevated expression—CCL20, IL17RB, IL37, PLAU, INSL4, KNG1, SLURP1, and PAEP—that demonstrate pivotal functions in bladder cancer initiation and advancemen [17–19], The overexpression of these genes correlates significantly with adverse patient outcomes (p-values between 0.004 and 0.049), establishing them as crucial biomarkers for bladder cancer risk assessment and prognostic evaluation. The survival analysis results suggest that COL5A2 gene may be related to malignant tumor progression, while IL17RB emphasizes the importance of inflammation-related genes in bladder cancer prognosis. PLAUR gene expression reflects enhanced tumor invasion and metastasis capabilities, and MSI2/KMT2 genes highlight the core role of post-transcriptional regulation and epigenetic modifications in bladder cancer pathogenesis. These findings provide important molecular markers for personalized treatment of bladder cancer. Multiple essential biological mechanisms involve these genes: Within immune modulation, the chemokine CCL20 mobilizes regulatory T cells via the CCL20-CCR6 pathway, creating immunosuppressive conditions [20, 21], Through distinct mechanisms, IL17RB and IL37 facilitate tumor immune escape, while PAEP advances tumor development by suppressing effector immune cell activities [22, 23]; Regarding invasive processes, the urokinase-type plasminogen activator PLAU facilitates extracellular matrix breakdown and metastatic dissemination [24, 25], whereas KNG1 contributes to angiogenic processes and inflammatory regulation. Moreover, INSL4 influences tumor metabolic restructuring [26, 27], and SLURP1 modulates cellular differentiation programs, together forming the molecular framework underlying bladder cancer malignant transformation [28, 29].

Clinical translational significance characterizes the identification of these genes, with particular emphasis on PLAU network pharmacology analysis demonstrating favorable binding interactions between various traditional Chinese medicine bioactive compounds and the PLAU protein. Molecular dynamics simulation validation of complex stability (RMSD values 0.1–0.15 nm) establishes a structural foundation for innovative therapeutic development. Our single-cell RNA sequencing analysis successfully generated a bladder cancer cellular atlas, encompassing major cell populations such as B cells, T cells, dendritic cells, epithelial cells, fibroblasts, mast cells, monocytes, and smooth muscle cells. The cellular spatial organization and interconnections elucidated through UMAP dimensionality reduction and unsupervised clustering offer novel insights into bladder cancer molecular mechanisms while providing empirical support for targeted therapeutic development, prognostic evaluation systems, and individualized treatment protocols that could enhance clinical outcomes and patient survival quality.

Particularly significant is our fibroblast subset characterization, identifying five distinct fibroblast populations exhibiting unique gene expression profiles and metabolic characteristics, where GAS and HGF signaling networks serve crucial roles in intercellular communication. This discovery contradicts conventional perspectives on fibroblast functional uniformity and demonstrates the functional diversity and adaptability of stromal elements within the tumor ecosystem [30, 31]. Beyond extracellular matrix reorganization, fibroblasts as predominant stromal components in the tumor microenvironment modulate immune cell activities and malignant cell behaviors through the secretion of diverse signaling factors [32, 33].

Our T cell characterization revealed ten functionally distinct subsets, significantly advancing comprehension of the bladder cancer immune landscape. Trajectory analysis demonstrated T cell developmental pathways and phenotypic transition mechanisms, offering crucial insights into T cell impairment and immune surveillance failure. These discoveries provide strategic direction for T cell engineering-based immunotherapeutic approaches [34, 35].

Intercellular communication network analysis unveiled intricate signaling patterns within the bladder cancer microenvironment. The communication patterns form a bidirectional communication network between stromal and epithelial cells, reflecting clear functional differentiation patterns in the bladder cancer microenvironment. The most robust signal transmission activity originates from fibroblasts and smooth muscle cells, whereas epithelial and basal cells possess dual signaling capabilities both secretion and reception establishing reciprocal communication circuits between stromal and epithelial compartments. Understanding these communication architectures provides critical insights into tumor microenvironment homeostasis and disruption mechanisms.

While immune cell populations demonstrate limited signalingoutput, they exhibit distinctive signal reception characteristics, potentially contributing to immunosuppressive microenvironment development. Cell type correlation analysis revealed strong associations among functionally related populations, while immune cells predominantly showed weak or inverse correlations with epithelial and stromal cells, indicating distinct functional specialization within the bladder cancer microenvironment.

Using the SCENIC algorithm, transcriptional regulatory network analysis identified essential transcription factors and their target gene networks, revealing spatial distribution heterogeneity of transcriptional programs across the bladder cancer microenvironment. Individual cell populations activate distinct transcriptional circuits with activity gradients extending from central to peripheral regions, creating functionally specialized regulatory domains. This transcriptional heterogeneity not only accounts for cellular phenotypic diversity but also establishes theoretical frameworks for therapeutic strategies targeting specific transcriptional programs.

Functional enrichment analysis identified essential biological processes—cell-cell junction regulation, adhesion molecule control, T cell activation, inflammatory responses, and extracellular matrix restructuring—that comprise the fundamental molecular architecture of bladder cancer development and progression. Differential pathway activation across cell types offers important insights into the molecular foundations of tumor heterogeneity.

Structure-function relationships of bladder cancer-associated proteins are illuminated through molecular dynamics simulation analysis. Protein backbone RMSD analysis demonstrates structural equilibration during simulations, while consistent hydrogen bond maintenance confirms secondary and tertiary structural stability. Solvent accessible surface area and radius of gyration analyses further validate conformational stability. Free energy landscape analysis maps protein conformational space energy distribution, identifying thermodynamically stable regions and transition state energetics. Multiple stable conformational states provide essential information for understanding protein functional plasticity and allosteric mechanisms, offering significant guidance for pharmaceutical design and therapeutic target validation.

Research innovation and clinical translation prospects

The innovation of this study is reflected in multiple aspects: First, an integrated multi-omics analysis strategy was adopted, combining large cohort transcriptome data and high-resolution single-cell sequencing data, achieving systematic analysis from population level to single-cell level; Second, prognostic-related genes were identified through intersection analysis methods, improving result reliability and clinical applicability; Third, combining network pharmacology and molecular dynamics simulation provides a complete research framework for drug target identification and validation.

In terms of clinical translation, the 8 prognostic-related immune genes identified in this study can serve as molecular markers for risk stratification of bladder cancer patients, guiding personalized treatment decisions. Particularly in the era of immunotherapy, the expression patterns of these genes may help screen patient populations suitable for immune checkpoint inhibitor treatment. Cell type and functional state analysis at the single-cell level provides important reference for developing cell therapy-based strategies.

Research limitations

Although this study achieved important findings, certain limitations still exist. First, the study is mainly based on retrospective data analysis, lacking prospective cohort validation; Second, the sample size of single-cell sequencing data is relatively limited, which may affect the statistical power of cell subgroup analysis; Third, although molecular dynamics simulation provides evidence of protein structural stability, in vitro and in vivo functional experiments are still needed to validate the specific mechanisms of these genes.

Conclusion

This study successfully identified 8 bladder cancer prognosis-related immune genes through integrated genomic analysis, systematically revealed cellular heterogeneity and molecular regulatory networks in the tumor microenvironment, providing important scientific evidence for precision diagnosis, prognostic assessment, and personalized treatment of bladder cancer. These findings not only enrich our understanding of bladder cancer pathogenesis but also point the direction for optimizing clinical treatment strategies and developing innovative drugs. With the deepening of research and technological advancement, these basic research results are expected to be translated into practical clinical applications, ultimately improving treatment outcomes and survival quality of bladder cancer patients.

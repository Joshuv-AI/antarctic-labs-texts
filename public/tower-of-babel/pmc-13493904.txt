Performance enhancement of THz antenna with dielectric resonator and prediction bandwidth using machine learning approach for 6G applications
Authors: Youssef Amraoui, Imane Halkhams, Rachid El Alami, Mohammed Ouazzani Jamil, Hassan Qjidaa, Z M S El-Barbary, Mohammed M Alammar, Mourad Yessef, Badre Bossoufi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe need for ultra-low latency and ultra-wideband in 6G applications requires efficient solutions for dielectric resonator antenna design. This paper presents the results of using a machine learning approach to improve the performance of a dielectric resonator antenna operating in the terahertz frequency band. The antenna design uses a polyimide substrate material with a compact size of 58 × 73 µm2. A silicon dielectric resonator in the shape of a rectangle ( of 11.9) is placed on the patch to improve its performance. The antenna demonstrated a wide bandwidth of 4.3936 THz, a maximum gain of 6.02 dBi at 4.59 THz and an efficiency of 77.19%, exhibiting excellent radiation patterns and excellent correspondence. In addition, many machine learning methods were used to predict the bandwidth based on different antenna characteristics. Various measures were used to examine machine learning models, such as variance score, R2, mean square error (RMSE), mean absolute error (MAE) and mean square error (MSE). Out of the five models tested, the K-Nearest Neighbor (KNN) model had the lowest error values (MAE of 0.000808, MSE of 0.000002, and RMSE of 0.00135) and the best R2, reaching 0.9647 in bandwidth prediction. The suggested terahertz dielectric resonator antenna, confirmed by electromagnetic simulations and machine learning-based predictions, is a viable option for 6G terahertz applications due to its outstanding impedance and radiation matching performance.Keywords: Terahertz antenna, Machine learning, 6G, Bandwidth, Dielectric resonatorSubject terms: Engineering, Materials science, Optics and photonics, Physics

Introduction

The rapid growth in global data consumption is already leading to saturation of wireless communication networks in the GHz band. This pressure prevents them from meeting the growing needs of data-intensive applications, such as the immersive virtual experiences and Internet of Things, as well as artificial intelligence (AI)1. To address these failures, the next generation 6G of mobile networks is also currently under study to transform communication by utilizing the terahertz frequency band of 0.1–10 THz2. Although the current GHz frequency range is already saturated and cannot be used further to accommodate performance needs, the THz band presents massive opportunities of use besides the fast ultra-high data rate and the overall connectivity of the devices and a fraction of the latency. Increase the speed of data transmission and ease of making small and efficient antennas that can facilitate new system designs. The researchers seek to identify new ways of addressing the future demands of the communication systems through the opportunities offered by the THz band.

One of the most common types of THz antenna, which enhances wireless communication, is the microstrip patch antenna. Patch antennas made of microstrip have been known to be small, cost-effective, and easy to manufacture. These attributes qualify them as candidates of THz communication systems3. Their performance is also poor at high frequencies due to large conductive and dielectric losses, resulting in low bandwidth and low radiation efficiency. To circumvent these performance limitations, slot ground plane and patch antenna structures have been developed. By adding a slotted ground plane, the system becomes more sophisticated and an opportunity to improve radiation characteristics, benefit from wider bandwidth, and minimize mutual interaction between each of the antenna elements. Slot position can be done carefully to optimize radiation characteristics, eliminate mutual coupling and provide optimal impedance matching. In addition, the inclusion of slots in the patch design enhances radiation matching and impedance properties, which improves bandwidth and increases radiation efficiency. This improvement will enable the antenna to be more flexible of the demands of THz communication situations4.

In addition, a dielectric resonator (DRA) antenna is an ideal approach that offers excellent radiation efficiency and wide bandwidth. Other techniques that could be used to drive DRA include microstrip feed lines, aperture coupling, coaxial feed probes and coplanar waveguides5,6. DR antenna practice has received a lot of attention, especially in the terahertz band7. The use of a DRA is suggested at microwave frequencies because of its capacity to provide a wide bandwidth with better radiation efficiency and gain, which can be expanded to the THz frequency range8. The rectangular DRA is the most frequent of the three major DRA designs, with advantages such as delivering two alternative aspect ratios (height/length and width/length) at a given resonance frequency when compared to its spherical and cylindrical counterparts9.

Recently, artificial intelligence has been identified as a leading method to improve the antenna design process10. The complex relationships between input variables (materials, geometry) and performance characteristics (S11, efficiency, bandwidth, gain) can be simulated with high accuracy using data-driven models including artificial neural networks, support vector machines, and evolutionary algorithms. AI technologies aim to provide an optimal antenna design over a broad frequency range. These models not only eliminate the need for comprehensive electromagnetic simulations, but also enable quick optimization and inverse design. Artificial intelligence, in particular, machine learning, is a precise and strongly recommended optimization technique when designing an antenna. ML techniques enable broad prediction capabilities for any type of antenna and operating circumstances, as well as adaptive learning using simulated or empirically observed data11. Since previous techniques are constrained by size, material nonlinearities, and manufacturing restrictions, the data-driven approach is especially beneficial for terahertz antennas. Numerous studies, including12–15, have shown the expanding potential of AI and machine learning (ML) in antenna optimization and design, where these techniques are increasingly being applied to increase performance.

The ML is used in many fields, such as time series prediction, collaborative filtering, image processing, automatic translation, and classification. The configuration of the antenna is intrinsically related to the geometrical parameters in the design. In this respect, optimization means the manipulation of the antenna parameters to obtain a refined and desired design outcome. Optimization shall be performed to achieve the desired parameters through the modification of the dimensions of some antenna characteristics. The optimization process has always been done by trial-and-error. ML methods can be employed to predict the properties of multiple antenna modules within a short period of time and with high accuracy. ML will process data to identify untapped mathematical connections, associate input, and output behaviors and provide predictions. Thus, it is not the best practice to utilize the methods that have already been adopted during antenna design16,17. To address this problem in this work, we have explored the various ML algorithms in the design of the antenna to enhance performance.

Antenna design and analysis

Antenna dimensions & development

This section focuses on the recommended dielectric resonator (DR) antenna architecture operating in the terahertz frequency band, developed to meet 6G communication requirements. We will describe the main aspects of the design that were taken into account when designing this antenna. This will allow us to gain in-depth knowledge of the structural design of the antenna and its optimization for outstanding performance. Given the compact dimensions essential to the operation of the THz band, the choice of materials becomes critical. Figure 1 displays the overall geometric design of the suggested unique DR antenna. Polyimide (thickness 12 μm, εr = 3.5, loss tangent = 0.0027) is employed as a substrate material, which was chosen for its flexibility and low dielectric loss, making it highly appropriate for applications within the terahertz frequency band. The backside features a full copper ground plane to improve gain and bandwidth. The rectangular patch has a dimension of 60 × 20 µm2. The patch antenna is powered by inset-fed microstrip lines. Copper with electrical conductivity of 5.96 × 107 S/m is selected for both the ground and radiating elements owing to its superior electrical conductivity and ease of fabrication. The dielectric behavior is not identical at all frequencies and therefore, the selection of the dielectric is of great importance in the achievement of optimum antenna performance.

The electromagnetic simulations were performed using CST Microwave Studio (2019) with a frequency-domain solver28. A hexahedral mesh was employed to ensure accurate field discretization. Open (add space) boundary conditions were applied in all directions. The antenna was excited using a waveguide port defined at the microstrip feed line with 50 Ω impedance matching. In addition, the bandwidth was determined using the standard S11 < − 10 dB threshold. The computational work was performed on an Intel® Core™ i5 CPU, 40 GB RAM, and Windows 11 operating system.

This study aims to identify the ideal parameter values using the CST MWS, allowing perfect customization of the antenna design to optimize efficiency and frequency coverage. Another objective is to compare the results of these optimizations with those obtained using machine learning methodologies discussed in section III.

The investigation shows a direct correlation between hdr and resonant frequency variations: the number of frequency bands varies as hdr increases. However, as depicted in Fig. 4(a), the optimal value of S11 < -10 dB, indicating minimal signal reflection and effective impedance matching, is reached at hdr = 10 μm. This figure maintains a wide operational bandwidth while stabilizing the operational frequency ranges of the antenna. Furthermore, a parameter analysis of the developed antenna is presented in Fig. 4(b), highlighting the effect of Ldr (dielectric resonator length) on its performance. To analyze the impact of Ldr on bandwidth, resonant frequency, and impedance matching properties, a thorough parametric optimization analysis was carried out by altering Ldr between 26 and 36 μm. Materials analysis

To improve the efficacy of the antenna for terahertz applications, a comprehensive material selection process was conducted, taking into account: Rogers RO3210, Rogers RO3010, Alumina and Silicon for the dielectric resonator material due to their outstanding conductivity qualities, and Rogers RT5880, Polyimide, Rogers RO3035 and FR4 for the substrate component. In order to determine their influence on the fundamental parameters of the antenna, including impedance matching, S11 and bandwidth, a variety of material combinations have been thoroughly studied through systematic study and rigorous evaluation. After a rigorous analysis, the selected material is a carefully designed combination that provides effective performance. This section explains the performance measurements, important points for the final choice and motivations that guided the choice of substrate material and that of the dielectric resonator elements.

Firstly, polyimide was selected as the substrate material owing to its widely acknowledged electrical conductivity. As demonstrated in Fig. 5, which illustrates the reflection coefficient S11 for the antenna for different materials, the ensuing study involves methodically changing the dielectric resonator (DR) materials to evaluate their impact on antenna behavior. The antenna had dual-band characteristics with an acceptable value of S11 when Rogers RO3210 was used as the DR material. The frequency bands were 3.61 THz to 4.14 THz and 4.51 THz to 5.79 THz. Switching to Rogers RO3010 as the DR produced a response that was almost identical at resonating frequency 5.35 THz, with bandwidths of 2.22 THz, but it was still not ideal. Additionally, the multiband response caused by the usage of alumina material restricts the broadband performance of the antenna. Lastly, using silicon as the DR material resulted in a single mode operation with a wide bandwidth of 4.3936 THz and a significant reflection coefficient.

Effect of the dielectric resonator material on the S11 parameter of the THz antenna.

As was previously mentioned, silicon was chosen as the DR element because of its inherent conductivity benefits. Various substrate materials (polyimide, Rogers RT5880, FR4, Rogers RO3035) were investigated to understand how they affect the performance of the antenna. Using polyimide as the substrate material resulted in a notable improvement. With a wide bandwidth of 3.5031 to 7.8967 THz and deep S11 values of − 40.38 dB, the antenna demonstrated outstanding impedance matching in single-band performance.

Machine Learning Model Integration

Progress in machine learning (ML) within the domain of electromagnetism is transforming antenna design techniques. Traditional techniques, like the parametric analysis tool via full-wave modelling software such as CST MWS, proved to be time consuming due to the complex design of modern antennas and the demands of their performance. Techniques of numerical analysis, including the finite element method, the domain of finite differences in time, the methods of moments (MoM), and 3D EM simulators such as ADS, HFSS, and CST programs, exist to solve this complexity. When it comes to designing and testing antennas, these software tools can be quite time-consuming, and even more so for complex antenna designs. In light of this, researchers began to study the use of machine learning (ML) to streamline antenna design. As a sub-domain of AI, ML has the ability to dramatically speed up the design process, while maintaining high accuracy, and proves to be a feasible solution for refining the design process and predicting antenna behavior, now that we are exploring the use of ML. We review the application of ML techniques to predict recommended antenna bandwidth, comparing these predictions with simulated results generated using CST MWS.

As presented in Fig. 7, the data preparation and extraction process in this investigation involves three essential phases. The proposed terahertz antenna for 6G application is designed and initially optimized in CST to achieve the desired operating frequency. Then, a parameter study is conducted for generating large datasets suitable for machine learning algorithms used in our study. Finally, the simulation results are extracted from CST for further processing, with return loss (S11) used for bandwidth calculation of the antenna.

Dataset and Machine Learning algorithm selection

To generate any prediction, it is essential to have collections of input data to provide to the system. The recommended antenna was simulated in CST MWS and refined through the application of various ML algorithms. Datasets of key parameters for the proposed antenna model, obtained from this optimization, were extracted. The input variables comprised hdr, Ldr, Wdr, R, a and b, and the corresponding output variable (bandwidth) was determined for each optimization iteration. 260 sets of inputs and outputs were utilized to estimate the bandwidth. The data is normalized and split into training and testing sets to make sure that the learning is robust and no overfitting occurs. Once trained, the model provides fast and accurate predictions of antenna performance, significantly reducing computational cost compared to traditional simulations. The reliability of the recommended model is confirmed through standard evaluation metrics and by comparing the predicted results with full-wave simulation data. As a result, this methodology facilitates more precise and dependable optimization of antenna design through machine learning predictions.

Figure 8 presents the flowchart of the suggested methodology used in this article to determine bandwidth using several machine learning algorithms. As we discussed above, the suggested DR antenna for 6G applications is created and optimized in CST to obtain the appropriate frequency. Next, parameter research is carried out to create large datasets appropriate for regression analysis using machine learning techniques. The available data were divided into two subsets: a training dataset (80%), used to train the ML models, and a testing dataset (20%), which will be used to test the performance of the model on previously unknown data. It should be emphasized that this dataset is sufficient to represent the relationship between antenna design parameters and their related performance measures within the design parameter range under consideration. After that, the model’s accuracy is examined using performance measures such as R2, variance score, Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE), Mean Absolute Error (MAE), Root mean squared logarithmic error (RMSLE), Mean Squared Error (MSE), and Mean Squared Logarithmic Error (MSLE), and a novel design modification is applied to the best model to determine the forecast of bandwidth. Because of its versatility, large library, ease of use, and user-friendliness, Python programming was chosen to develop these models. Lastly, the simulation data are exported from CST for additional study. This includes the reflection coefficient (S11), which is used to calculate the bandwidth of the recommended antenna.

The best performance of a ML model is frequently attained by carefully selecting several models. In this paper, several ML algorithms analysis is used, which is a statistical approach for determining the effect and cause connection between variables. KNN is a non-parametric method that averages target values from the K nearest neighbors, which are identified using distance metrics such Manhattan or Euclidean distance, to predict continuous outcomes 33. XG-Boost

XG-Boost is a popular and very successful machine learning software. It is effective for both regression and classification issues. When it comes to generating supervised regression models, XG-Boost is a very useful tool 34.

Gradient Boosting Regressor (GBR)

Gradient Boosting in Practice Regression is a powerful machine learning model to predict continuous values. The approach develops a series of weak prediction models, typically decision trees, sequentially. To reduce a preset loss measure, the trees in the subsequent sequence eliminate the errors made by the preceding trees 35.

The machine-learning workflow was designed to ensure reproducibility and prevent data leakage. The dataset was divided into 20% testing data and 80% training data using a fixed random seed of 42. Hyperparameter optimization for the DT, RF, KNN, GBR, and XGBoost models was performed using GridSearchCV combined with 5-fold cross-validation applied exclusively to the training dataset.

The optimal DT hyperparameters were max_depth = 5, max_features= None, min_samples_leaf = 2, and min_samples_split = 5. For the RF model, the best parameters were max_depth = 20, min_samples_leaf = 2, min_samples_split = 2, and n_estimators = 300. The optimized KNN model employed n_neighbors = 3, weights = uniform, and p = 1. The GBR model achieved the best performance with learning_rate = 0.05, max_depth = 2, min_samples_leaf = 4, n_estimators = 200, min_samples_split = 2, and subsample = 1. For the XGBoost model, the selected parameters were colsample_bytree = 1, learning_rate = 0.1, max_depth = 3, min_child_weight = 1, n_estimators = 300, and subsample = 0.8.

Regarding the normalization procedure, all input features were scaled prior to model training to ensure consistent feature ranges and to improve the stability and performance of the ML algorithms. Specifically, feature normalization was applied to the training data and then consistently applied to the validation and test sets using the same transformation parameters. Feature normalization using StandardScaler was applied to the KNN model because distance-based methods are sensitive to feature scaling. To avoid data leakage, the testing dataset remained completely isolated during model training, scaling, and hyperparameter optimization. All implementations were carried out in Python using the Scikit-learn, XGBoost, NumPy, Pandas, and Matplotlib libraries. The machine learning environment was set up using the Anaconda distribution (Version 25.11.1) 36, which provides an open-source package management and environment system for Python. Python (Version 3.13.9) and Jupyter Notebook (Version 7.4.5) were used for model development and execution.

Results and analysis

The radiation properties of the suggested antenna are investigated in this study to better understand its communication and coverage efficacy in the THz frequency range. Figure 12 depicts the recommended antenna’s cross-polarization and co-polarization patterns at 4.59 THz, 5.34 THz, 6.7 and 7.19 THz, respectively. The major lobe amplitude in co polarization is greater than 2 dB for these four frequencies, but the magnitude of the main lobe in cross-polarization is well below − 50 dB. This considerable difference demonstrates high polarization purity, which is essential for eliminating interference and ensuring a clear signal transmission. The low cross-polarization level of the antenna minimizes interference and increases signal integrity, making it perfect for reliable communication in terahertz band.

The cross-polarization and co-polarization of the antenna.

In addition, Fig. 13 depicts the three-dimensional gain radiation patterns of the recommended wideband antenna at various frequencies of 4.59 THz, 5.34 THz, 6.7 and 7.19 THz within an operating range of 3.5031 THz to 7.8967 THz. With a maximum gain achieved of 6.02 dBi at 4.59 THz, 4.26 dBi at 5.34 THz, 3.91 dBi at 6.7 THz, and 4.04 dBi at 7.19 THz, the antenna provides consistent radiation performance over a wide range of frequencies. This suggests consistent gain performance over the antenna frequency ranges, resulting in reliable signal reception and transmission.

The performance of an antenna is directly related to how electrical current passes through its radiating components. This vital component allows precise control of antenna operation, including resonance at certain frequencies for specialized applications. Figure 14 displays the current of recommended antenna distribution patterns, which exhibit unique characteristics at 4.59 THz, where the antenna has the maximum gain. Notably, the maximum current density is concentrated around the power line and all the slots of the patch, resulting in efficient radiation. The fact that this distribution pattern is consistent over the entire operating range indicates that the creation of resonance is encouraged by the same structural elements.

The suggested THz antenna’s current distribution at 4.59 THz.

Excellent efficiency is important in 6G applications where energy conservation is crucial. The suggested antenna’s efficiency guarantees that a considerable amount of the input power is used for communication, reducing energy waste and increasing network efficiency.

Practical constraints and scalability of the suggested THz antenna design

The recommended THz antenna works well in simulations; however, scalability difficulties and practical limitations must be resolved before deployment to the real world.

Challenges in fabrication Manufacturing: THz antennas require precision methods such as photolithography or 3D printing due to their small size. These therapies are expensive and rare. Conclusion

The recommended DR THz antenna is very promising for future 6G applications operating in a frequency range of 2 to 8 THz. The efficiency of 77.19% is reached, and the bandwidth is 4.3936 THz while the gain can reach 6.02 dB at an operating frequency of 4.59 THz, with stable radiation patterns. These achievements are further enhanced by advanced machine learning models such as KNN, Random Forest, DT, XG Boost and Gradient Boosting Regressor. Compared to other techniques, the KNN machine learning algorithm achieves an R2 of over 0.9647 and accurately predicts the bandwidth of the recommended antenna, with a maximum anticipated bandwidth of 4.4146 THz. Prospective surveys should focus on prototyping and empirical testing to further validate the simulated results. Furthermore, the expansion of the single design to incorporate MIMO configurations has the potential to expand both system capacity and bandwidth. Incorporating increasingly comprehensive training datasets with advanced deep learning approaches, like ensemble learning and neural network models, will aid to boost the accuracy of predictions and their generalizability. These developments will establish machine learning-assisted antenna design as a revolutionary paradigm for the forthcoming generation of 6G wireless communication systems.

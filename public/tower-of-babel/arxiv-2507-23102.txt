Abstract
. We show that a totally degenerate limit of discrete series representation admits a choice of n cohomology group that is nonvanishing at a canonically defined degree. We then show that the combinatorial complexes used by Soergel to compute these cohomology groups satisfies Serre duality. We conclude that this produces two n cohomology groups, each for a totally degenerate limit of discrete series of U(n+1) and U(n), which are nonvanishing at the same degree. This suggests Gan Gross Prasad type branching laws for the TDLDS of unitary groups of any rank.



1.
Introduction

Totally degenerate limits of discrete series (TDLDS) are relatively unexplored but tantalizingly close to discrete series representations, which are well understood in automorphic representation theory. It is expected that they correspond to Galois representations, but currently known methods provide no robust link between them and arithmetic geometry in any degree of generality.

It was observed by Carayol that there is a link between TDLDS representations and geometry, in the sense that they are detected in the coherent cohomology of Griffiths Schmid domains with coefficients in a holomorphic line bundle. The domains where the line bundles exhibit these TDLDS representations in their cohomology cannot be Hermitian symmetric, and in fact do not admit any structure as a quasi projective variety
[
GRT13
]
. We must therefore study these line bundles in terms of their equivariant structure over a homogeneous complex manifold without the classical tools from algebraic geometry. Our main tool is the cohomology of TDLDS with respect to a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{n}
. It was shown rather suggestively by Carayol that in certain cases these cohomology groups are non zero.

Our goal in this paper is to extend Carayol’s results. Our contribution is to note that there are two canonical, non zero
\mathfrak{n}
cohomology classes associated to any TDLDS representation
V
, and the degree of these classes is given by the dimension and codimension of a certain embedding of closed varieties that can be defined in terms of
\mathfrak{n}
and
V
. Our main tool is a combinatorial complex constructed by Soergel that computes these
\mathfrak{n}
cohomology groups without explicit dimensions and degrees in which one may hope to find cohomology in our case of interest.

1.1.
Nonvanishing of
\mathfrak{n}
-chomology groups

To state our main result, we need to specify what we mean by
\mathfrak{n}
cohomology.
Let
V
be a unitary TDLDS representation of a reductive linear algebraic group
G
over
\mathbb{Q}
with anisotropic maximal torus
T
. Let
K{}(\mathbb{R})
be a maximal compact subgroup of
G(\mathbb{R})
containing
T(\mathbb{R})
.
Let
\mathfrak{g}{}_{\mathbb{R}},\mathfrak{k}_{\mathbb{R}}
and
\mathfrak{t}_{\mathbb{R}}
be the respective Lie algebras of
G(\mathbb{R})
,
K{}(\mathbb{R})
and
T(\mathbb{R})
, and let
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}{}_{\mathbb{R}}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}
and
\mathfrak{k}=\mathfrak{k}_{\mathbb{R}}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}
and
\mathfrak{t}=\mathfrak{t}_{\mathbb{R}}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}
be their complexifications. Let
\Phi=\Phi(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{t})\supset\Phi^{+}
be choices of root system and positive roots with respect to
\mathfrak{t}
and let
\rho
be the half sum of positive roots. Our choice of maximal compact
K{}(\mathbb{R})
also provides us with a notion of compact roots
\Phi_{K}=\Phi(\mathfrak{k},\mathfrak{t})
, defined as the root system of
\mathfrak{k}
with respect to
\mathfrak{t}
.
Write
\mathfrak{n}\subset\mathfrak{g}
as the direct sum of negative root spaces of
\mathfrak{g}
.

We define the
\mathfrak{n}
cohomology of the representation
V
as the Lie algebra cohomology
H^{*}(\mathfrak{n},V)
. These groups admit an action of
\mathfrak{t}
induced by the adjoint action on
\mathfrak{n}
and the action of
\mathfrak{g}
on
V
. In this paper we are concerned with the weight spaces
H^{*}(\mathfrak{n},V)_{-\lambda}
of this action with respect to weights
\lambda\in\mathfrak{t}^{\vee}
. Our main result is as follows:

Theorem 1.1
.

Given an integral weight
\lambda\in\mathfrak{t}^{\vee}
, there exists a choice of limit of discrete series representation
V
with infinitesimal character
\lambda+\rho
such that
H^{q}(\mathfrak{n},V)_{-\lambda}
is nonzero at
q=\#(\Phi_{K}\cap\Phi^{+})

Recall that these cohomology groups must satisfy Serre duality, as is shown in
[
BW80
, Proposition 1.5]
.
We therefore prove that the combinatorial complex used to compute these cohomology groups also satisfies Serre duality:

Theorem 1.2
.

Given the choices in the previous theorem, one has

H^{q}(\mathfrak{n},V)_{-\lambda}\simeq H^{\#\Phi^{+}-q}(\mathfrak{n},V^{\vee})_{\lambda+2\rho}

However, the proof of this theorem reveals that the Serre duality of
H^{*}(\mathfrak{n},V)
is compatible with the Serre duality of the
\mathfrak{n}\cap\mathfrak{k}
cohomology of the K types of
V
.

The main idea of Theorem
1.1
uses the fact that a TDLDS representation can only be nonzero if the simple roots defining it are non-compact. This forces the differentials computed by Soergel to be 0 at the canonical degrees.
We then noticed that the degrees in which these cohomology groups occur for quasi split unitary groups suggest a geometric application of the Gan Gross Prasad conjecture.

1.2.
Geometrization of the GGP conjecture

In section
6
we construct holomorphic line bundles
\mathcal{L}_{\lambda_{m}}
over a family of compact complex analytic manifolds
\mathrm{GS}(\mathrm{U}_{m})
for
m=n,n+1
. The resulting cohomology

is given in terms of the

\displaystyle H^{*}(\mathrm{GS}(\mathrm{U_{n+1}}),\mathcal{L}_{\lambda_{n+1}})\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow H^{*}(\mathrm{GS}(\mathrm{U_{n}}),\mathcal{L}_{\lambda_{n}})

It is natural to ask whether this map is nonzero. If it is, then the restriction map can be thought of as a geometric manifestation of the period integral occuring in the GGP conjectures. As we explain in section

2.2.
Parametrization of Limits of Discrete Series, and n Cohomology

We now want to define totally degenerate limits of discrete series (TDLDS), whose cohomology is our main object of study. We will thus describe all limits of discrete series
\pi=\pi(Q,\chi_{\lambda+\rho})
in terms of parameters
Q
and
\chi_{\lambda+\rho}
as follows:

Let
X
be the flag variety of all Borel subalgebras of the complexification
\mathfrak{g}
. Let
\iota:Q\hookrightarrow X
be the inclusion of a closed
K(\mathbb{C})
orbit
Q
, obtained from the natural action of
G(\mathbb{C})
on
X
. We obtain a discrete series representation
\Gamma(\iota_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Q})
with infinitesimal character
\chi_{0+\rho}
, where
\mathcal{O}_{Q}
is the structure sheaf on
Q
as an algebraic variety,
\iota_{*}
is the
\mathcal{D}

\Gamma

\lambda\in\mathfrak{t}^{\vee}
be an integral character, ie. corresponding under the exponential map to a well defined character
\exp(\lambda):T(\mathbb{C})\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}

T^{\lambda+\rho}
denote the Zuckerman translation functor from
\rho
to
\lambda+\rho
, we now define

\pi(Q,\chi_{\lambda+\rho}):=T^{\lambda+\rho}\Gamma(\iota_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Q})

By convention, we say that a TDLDS representation is a representation of the form
\pi(Q,\chi_{\lambda+\rho})
when
\lambda+\rho
is orthogonal to all roots
\Phi
. In the semisimple case this condition means that
\lambda+\rho=0
and such representations are classified in
[
CK07
]
. In the case of reductive groups, the maximal torus
T(\mathbb{R})
will contain the center of
G(\mathbb{R})
which can act by a nontrivial character.

Our primary interest in this paper is to prove nonvanishing of the cohomology groups

H^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{n},\pi(Q,\chi_{\lambda+\rho}))_{-\lambda^{\prime}}

Here,

denotes Lie algebra cohomology. These cohomology groups admit an action of
\mathfrak{t}
, induced by the adjoint action of
\mathfrak{t}
on
\mathfrak{n}
, and the restriction to
\mathfrak{t}
of the
\mathfrak{g}
action on
\pi(Q,\chi_{\lambda+\rho})
. The subscript
\lambda^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{t}^{\vee}
therefore denotes the
\lambda^{\prime}

By the Casselman Osborne Lemma

]
, these cohomology groups are

unless
\lambda^{\prime}=w(\lambda+\rho)-\rho
for some element of the Weyl group. Note that for TDLDS, the Weyl group fixes
\lambda+\rho
since we have assumed it is orthogonal to all roots. Hence, the
\lambda^{\prime}

\lambda^{\prime}=\lambda+\rho-\rho=\lambda
. Therefore, we are reduced to studying

H^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{n},\pi(Q,\chi_{\lambda+\rho}))_{-\lambda}

3.
Bruhat order on the Weyl group and its subsets

3.1.
Bruhat order and relative Bruhat length

On the Weyl group
W
we want to use our choice of positive roots to define the Bruhat order. Our positive root system gives us a choice of simple roots
\Sigma
and therefore a set of simple reflections
S=S(\Sigma)
. We use this
S
to define a Bruhat length
l(x)
on any
x\in W
as the minimum number
n
of simple reflections
\sigma_{\alpha_{1}},...,\sigma_{\alpha_{n}}\in S
such that

x=\sigma_{\alpha_{1}}\sigma_{\alpha_{2}}...\sigma_{\alpha_{n}}

We can now define the Bruhat order. For any
x,y\in W
, let us write
x\rightarrow y
whenever
x=s_{\alpha}y
for some
\alpha\in\Phi
and
l(x)=l(y)+1
. We warn the reader that
s_{\alpha}
need not be a simple reflection. The Bruhat order
(W,\leq)
is then defined on
W
by

x<y\iff x\rightarrow x_{1}\rightarrow...\rightarrow x_{n}\rightarrow y

for some sequence
x_{1},...,x_{n}
of elements in
W
. We shall refer to
(W,\rightarrow)
as the Hasse diagram of the Bruhat order.

The chain complexes computing our
\mathfrak{n}
cohomology groups also require us to work with other notions of lengths.
We first note that
l(x)
is the number of Weyl chamber walls which separate
C
from
xC
, which by
[
Hum90
]
is equal to:

l(x)=\#\{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}|(xC,\alpha)<0\}=\#(\Phi^{+}\cap x\Phi^{-})

In a similar manner, we can define as in
[
Soe97
]
a compact Weyl length
l_{K}(x)
as

\displaystyle l_{K}(x):=\#\{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}\cap\Phi_{K}|(xC,\alpha)<0\}=\#(\Phi^{+}\cap x\Phi^{-}\cap\Phi_{K})

where
(xC,\alpha)<0
means
(x\beta,\alpha)<0
for all
\beta\in C
.

3.2.
Chain complex on subsets of the Weyl group

Let
\mathcal{V}\subset W
be a subset of the Weyl group, and
(\mathcal{V},\rightarrow)
the induced subgraph from
(W,\rightarrow)
. We construct chain complexes
(C^{\bullet}\mathcal{V},d)
using the Bruhat length to define the grading, and the Bruhat order to define differentials. This will be our main ingredient to compute
\mathfrak{n}
cohomology via Soergel’s formula.

In order for our construction of differentials to square to zero, we impose on our subset
\mathcal{V}
the following condition:

\mathcal{V}
satisfies (
\star
) if

\displaystyle w_{1},w_{2}\in\mathcal{V}\text{ are such that }l(w_{1})-2=l(w_{2})\text{ then}

(
\star
)

\displaystyle\{w\in W|w_{1}<w<w_{2}\}\text{ has two elements or is empty.}

Given such a set
\mathcal{V}\subset W
, let
C^{\bullet}\mathcal{V}
be the free
\mathbb{C}
vector space generated by the basis
\mathcal{V}
. We endow this with a graded structure coming from the length function, such that for any
q\in\mathbb{Z}
,

C^{q}\mathcal{V}=Span_{\mathbb{C}}\{v\in\mathcal{V}:l(v)=q\}

The graph
(\mathcal{V},\rightarrow)
can now be used to define a differential on
C^{\bullet}\mathcal{V}
as follows:

Write
d
as a matrix with entries
s_{\mathcal{V}}(x,y)
indexed by basis elements
x,y\in\mathcal{V}
. We assume these matrix entries satisfy the following desiderata:

Definition 3.1
.

Let
d:C^{\bullet}\mathcal{V}\rightarrow C^{\bullet+1}\mathcal{V}
be defined by

dx=\sum_{y\in\mathcal{V}}s_{\mathcal{V}}(x,y)y\text{ for any }x\in\mathcal{V}

where we choose
s(x,y)
for each
x,y\in\mathcal{V}
such that

(1)

s_{\mathcal{V}}(x,y)\in\pm 1
if
x\rightarrow y

(2)

s_{\mathcal{V}}(x,y)=0
otherwise

(3)

if
x\rightarrow w\rightarrow y
and
x\rightarrow w^{\prime}\rightarrow y
for
w\neq w^{\prime}
then

s_{\mathcal{V}}(x,w)s_{\mathcal{V}}(w,y)+s_{\mathcal{V}}(x,w^{\prime})s_{\mathcal{V}}(w^{\prime},y)=0

Here, the first two conditions ask for
d
to be an adjacency matrix for the graph
(\mathcal{V},\rightarrow)
where some of its entries
s_{\mathcal{V}}(x,y)
may be
-1
instead of
1
, and the third condition asks for those negative entries to be chosen such that
d^{2}=0
.

It is shown in
[
BGG75
]
that when
\mathcal{V}=W
, such a choice of
s_{W}(x,y)
is always possible. If we instead take an arbitrary subset
\mathcal{V}\subset W
that satisfies (

⋆

‣
3.2
), then we can obtain a choice of matrix coefficients
s_{\mathcal{V}}
satisfying the above three conditions by restricting from
s_{W}
, ie. by setting
s_{\mathcal{V}}(x,y)=s_{W}(x,y)
for any
x,y\in\mathcal{V}
. Hence, we see that (

⋆

‣
3.2
) implies the existence of a choice of

that satisfies Definition

4.
Nonvanishing theorem

We now prove the following nonvanishing theorem:

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\lambda+\rho\in\overline{C}
be an integral dominant but possibly singular character.
Let
Q\subset X
be the closed
K
orbit containing
\mathfrak{b}=\mathfrak{t}\oplus\mathfrak{n}
with
\mathfrak{n}
the negative root space.
Suppose
\Sigma\cap\Phi_{K}=\emptyset
. We then have

H^{\dim_{\mathbb{C}}Q}(\mathfrak{n},\pi(Q,\chi_{\lambda+\rho}))_{-\lambda}\neq 0

Proof.

The identity element
id\in W
satisfies
l(id)=l_{K}(id)=0
and therefore
id\in\mathcal{V}(A,D,0)
. We want to show the differential vanishes on this element. If
s\in W
has length
l(s)=1
then it is a simple reflection
s=s_{\alpha}
for
\alpha
a simple root. We then assumed no such simple root can be compact, implying
l_{K}(s)=0
. This proves that
\mathcal{V}(A,D,0)
cannot contain any element of length 1.

We thus see that the differential at degree zero

d:C^{0}\mathcal{V}(A,D,0)\rightarrow C^{1}\mathcal{V}(A,D,0)=0

vanishes, so we get a 1 dimensional contribution
H^{0}\mathcal{V}(A,D,0)\simeq\mathbb{C}
coming from the identity element
id\in W
of the Weyl group.

Going back to
3.2
gives us

H^{\dim_{\mathbb{C}}Q}(\mathfrak{n},\pi(Q,\chi_{\lambda+\rho}))_{-\lambda}=H^{0}\mathcal{V}(A,D,0)\oplus\bigoplus_{c\geq 1}H^{2c}\mathcal{V}(A,D,c)

ie. the
c=0
term above gives at least a one dimensional contribution to
\mathfrak{n}
cohomology.
∎

As an application, we note that if
\pi(Q,\chi_{\lambda+\rho})
is a TDLDS then we have

H^{\dim_{\mathbb{C}}Q}(\mathfrak{n},\pi(Q,\chi_{\lambda+\rho}))_{-\lambda}\neq 0

5.
Serre duality

Set
N=\#(\Phi^{+})
. Let us first note the following proposition.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
w_{0}\in W
be the long Weyl element. The following are true:

(1)

l(aw_{0})=N-l(a)

(2)

l_{K}(aw_{0})=\#(\Phi^{+}\cap\Phi_{K})-l_{K}(a)

(3)

If
a\rightarrow b
in
W
then
aw_{0}\leftarrow bw_{0}

Proof.

Note that
w_{0}C=-C
so we immediately get (1) and (2).
The last statement also follows, since
a\rightarrow b
implies
b=s_{\alpha}a
for a reflection
s_{\alpha}
with
\alpha\in\Phi^{+}
, and
l(aw_{0})=N-l(a)=N-l(b)-1=l(bw_{0})-1
and therefore
aw_{0}\leftarrow bw_{0}
.
∎

5.1.
Differentials for the Opposite Subset of the Weyl group

Our current goal is to provide a statement of Serre duality between the cohomology groups
H^{\bullet}\mathcal{V}
and
H^{\bullet}(\mathcal{V}w_{0})
. In Definition
3.1
we associated a chain complex
C^{\bullet}\mathcal{V}
to any subset
\mathcal{V}\subset W
of the Weyl group satisfying condition (

⋆

‣
3.2
), and made a number of choices in order to define the differential of the chain complex. Getting to a duality statement will require us to construct the differential for
\mathcal{V}w_{0}
using a choice of differential on
\mathcal{V}
.

Let
\mathcal{V}\subset W
be a subset satisfying (

⋆

‣
3.2
) and fix a differential
d_{\mathcal{V}}
of
C^{\bullet}\mathcal{V}
. We then let
d_{\mathcal{V}w_{0}}
be given by
s_{\mathcal{V}w_{0}}(xw_{0},yw_{0})=s_{\mathcal{V}}(y,x)
. By Proposition
5.1
we get that if
uw_{0},vw_{0}
are two distinct elements in
\{ww_{0}\in W|xw_{0}\rightarrow ww_{0}\rightarrow yw_{0}\}
then

\displaystyle s_{\mathcal{V}w_{0}}(yw_{0},uw_{0})s_{\mathcal{V}w_{0}}(uw_{0},xw_{0})+s_{\mathcal{V}w_{0}}(yw_{0},vw_{0})s_{\mathcal{V}w_{0}}(vw_{0},xw_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s_{\mathcal{V}}(x,u)s_{\mathcal{V}}(u,y)+s_{\mathcal{V}}(x,v)s_{\mathcal{V}}(v,y)=0

so it follows that
d_{\mathcal{V}w_{0}}
satisfies the conditions in Definition
3.1
for differentials of
C^{\bullet}(\mathcal{V}w_{0})
.

The following duality lemma will be the main ingredient to our proof of Serre duality for
\mathfrak{n}
cohomology:

Lemma 5.2
.

H^{k}\mathcal{V}=(H^{N-k}(\mathcal{V}w_{0}))^{\vee}

Proof.

Let

B:C^{k}\mathcal{V}\times C^{N-k}(\mathcal{V}w_{0})\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be the symmetric nondegenerate bilinear form given on the basis elements
a\in\mathcal{V},bw_{0}\in\mathcal{V}w_{0}
by

B(a,bw_{0})=\delta_{a,b}

From
B
we get a map
B^{\prime}:C^{k}\mathcal{V}\rightarrow(C^{N-k}(\mathcal{V}w_{0}))^{\vee}
which we note is a map of chain complexes graded by
k
since

B(s_{\mathcal{V}}a,bw_{0})=B(s_{\mathcal{V}}(b,a)b,bw_{0})=s_{\mathcal{V}}(b,a)=s_{\mathcal{V}w_{0}}(aw_{0},bw_{0})=B(a,s_{\mathcal{V}w_{0}}bw_{0})

Also note that
B^{\prime}
is an isomorphism of chain complexes since
B
is nondegenerate.

We then claim that the

k
th cohomology of
C^{N-\bullet}(\mathcal{V}w_{0})^{\vee}
is isomorphic to
H^{N-k}(\mathcal{V}w_{0})^{\vee}
. Indeed, we have that

\displaystyle H^{k}(C^{N-\bullet}(\mathcal{V}w_{0})^{\vee},d^{\vee})=\frac{Ker(d^{\vee})}{Im(d^{\vee})}=\frac{\{v^{\vee}\in(C^{N-k})^{\vee}:v^{\vee}|_{Im(d)}=0\}}{\{v^{\vee}\in(C^{N-k})^{\vee}:v^{\vee}|_{Ker(d)}=0\}}\simeq H^{N-k}(\mathcal{V}w_{0})^{\vee}

We have thus proven that

be an arbitrary character, with

Theorem 5.3
.

Let

and

Proof.

Let us write

and

, ie. both sets can be obtained by right multiplying the Weyl element.

By construction, there is some

, and both
Aw_{0}
and

are cosets of

of the same element
xw_{0}
so this implies

.
On the other hand, if
x\in D

by definition. Since

.

Conversely, if

x^{-1}(\lambda+\rho)=x^{-1}(-\lambda^{op}-\rho)=-x^{-1}(\lambda^{op}+\rho)\in\overline{C}

We now want to show that

\mathcal{V}(A^{op},D^{op},c)=\mathcal{V}(Aw_{0},Dw_{0},c)

\displaystyle\dim\bigoplus_{c}H^{2c+\dim Q-N+k}\mathcal{V}(A^{op},D^{op},c)

\displaystyle\dim\bigoplus_{c}H^{2c+\dim Q-N+k}\mathcal{V}(A,D,N-\dim Q-c)w_{0}

\displaystyle\dim\bigoplus_{c}H^{2c+\dim Q-N+k}\Big(\mathcal{V}(A,D,N-\dim Q-c)w_{0}\Big)^{\vee}

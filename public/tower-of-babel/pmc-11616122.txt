Effect of esketamine on postoperative quality of recovery in patients undergoing arthroscopic rotator cuff repair: protocol for a prospective, randomized, double-blind study
Authors: Qirong Sun, Jiao Luo, Shijia Zhang, Leng Zhou
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe utilization of shoulder arthroscopic surgery has progressively increased as a prevalent surgical approach for the treatment and repair of rotator cuff injury in clinical practice. However, early postoperative pain and neurological dysfunction remain significant factors that impact patients’ postoperative recovery. Esketamine, an NMDA receptor antagonist, effectively reduces opioid consumption, attenuates postoperative inflammatory reactivity, and has also demonstrated neuroprotective effects. The aim of this study is to evaluate the quality of early recovery after anesthesia maintenance with esketamine in patients undergoing arthroscopic rotator cuff repair.MethodsThis is a single-center, randomized controlled trial. A total of 234 patients undergoing arthroscopic rotator cuff repair will be enrolled and randomized at a 1:1:1 ratio to receive general anesthesia with different doses of esketamine or saline solution. The primary outcome is the quality of early recovery scores based on the QoR-15 scale on postoperative day 1. Secondary outcomes include the quality of early recovery scores based on the QoR-15 scale on postoperative day 3, remifentanil consumption, use of vasoactive drugs, cerebral desaturation events, rescue analgesia during the post-anesthesia care unit stay, NRS pain scores on postoperative days 1 and 2, delirium within 3 days after surgery, and adverse events.DiscussionA randomized controlled trial design will be used in this study to assess the quality of early recovery scores in patients undergoing arthroscopic rotator cuff repair. Our objective is to investigate the potential of esketamine in mitigating the adverse effects associated with intraoperative controlled hypotension and assessing its impact on intraoperative cerebral oxygen saturation and postoperative delirium. Although this study is conducted at a single center, and there is limited research on the long-term effects of perioperative esketamine on patients, it still offers valuable insights for future studies.Trial registrationThis study has been approved by the Ethics Committee at West China Hospital, Sichuan University (approval no. 2023–2282). Introduction

Background and rationale {6a}

A rotator cuff tear is a common injury that mostly occurs in middle-aged and elderly patients, resulting in shoulder pain, weakness, and muscle atrophy. These clinical symptoms can significantly reduce the patient’s daily quality of life. Rehabilitation therapy is typically recommended for mild injuries, while severe injuries may require surgical treatment [1]. Arthroscopic rotator cuff repair has become the preferred surgical procedure over open repair due to its advantages of smaller incisions [2].

Arthroscopic rotator cuff repair (ARCR) often results in severe pain [3]. Most patients who undergo arthroscopic surgery require large amounts of opioids for postoperative pain relief [4]. Increasing doses of opioids lead to a series of side effects, including nausea, vomiting, excessive sedation, respiratory depression, drug tolerance, and opioid-induced hyperalgesia [5]; these side effects affect the early postoperative recovery of patients and prolong the length of hospital stay. Additionally, to ensure optimal visualization of the surgical field and facilitate the surgical procedure, patients are typically positioned either in the beach chair or lateral decubitus position while controlled hypotension is employed during ARCR surgery [6, 7]. However, intraoperative controlled hypotension may impact cerebral perfusion and increase the risk of cerebral ischemia, thereby leading to postoperative neurological impairment [8]. Meex et al. [9] demonstrated that up to 55% of patients who underwent shoulder arthroscopic surgery under general anesthesia in the beach chair position experienced cerebral desaturation events (CDEs). Aguirre et al. [8] confirmed that the beach chair position and controlled hypotension have an impact on cerebral blood flow and oxygenation in patients undergoing shoulder arthroscopic surgery, thus influencing neurobehavioral outcomes. Therefore, improving pain management during arthroscopic shoulder surgery, reducing postoperative opioid consumption, and optimizing cerebral perfusion are beneficial for promoting early postoperative recovery in patients undergoing ARCR.

The quality of recovery (QoR) is a crucial outcome indicator for perioperative patient quality management and prognosis evaluation; it requires assessment across physiological, psychological, social, behavioral, and economic dimensions due to its significant health economic implications [10, 11]. The 15-item Quality of Recovery scale (QoR-15), known for its high reliability and clinical acceptance, serves as a patient-centered index to evaluate postsurgical and anesthesia-related health status [12]. This questionnaire comprehensively evaluates patients’ overall recovery experience by examining five aspects: pain level, mental state, emotional well-being, independence, and comfort. The key influencing factors include anesthesia type, surgical procedure performed, patient-specific characteristics, and postoperative complications; among these factors are postoperative pain intensity and the occurrence of nausea/vomiting and opioid usage which significantly contribute to a reduction in the QoR score [13]. Kleif et al. [14] reported that patients with greater severity of recovery on the first postoperative day had a lower incidence of postoperative complications within 30 days after surgery.

Ketamine is a desirable anesthetic agent with reported antidepressant effects that can reduce postoperative pain and opioid consumption [15, 16]. Esketamine, a multitarget drug with the same mechanism of action as ketamine, has twice the affinity for NMDA receptors compared to ketamine, resulting in fewer side effects [17]. Clinical studies have also demonstrated that a subanesthetic dose of esketamine effectively alleviates postoperative pain, reduces the need for analgesics after surgery, mitigates opioid tolerance, and inhibits hyperpathia [16, 18, 19]. Importantly, human studies indicate an increasing trend in global cerebral blood flow (CBF) following administration of ketamine in elective surgical patients [20, 21]. This approach is beneficial for maintaining cerebral perfusion. Furthermore, several publications have shown that intraoperative intravenous low-dose esketamine could enhance early postoperative quality of recovery [22–24]. However, the optimal dosage of esketamine remains undetermined, and there is currently no published literature assessing the impact of intraoperative infusion of esketamine on early postoperative recovery from a quality-of-recovery perspective in this specific patient population.

Given this context, we propose a single-center, randomized controlled study to investigate the application of ketamine in ARCR patients using the QoR-15 as our indicator. Our objective is to determine whether these subanesthetic doses (0.25 mg/kg/h and 0.125 mg/kg/h) can enhance early postoperative quality of recovery while also improving brain oxygen saturation and reducing adverse reactions.

Objectives {7}

The primary objective of this study is to investigate the impact of intraoperative continuous infusion of escitalopram and ketamine on the quality of recovery on the first day following arthroscopic rotator cuff repair (ARCR).

The secondary objectives of this study are as follows: (1) to assess whether intraoperative continuous infusion of subanesthetic esketamine can reduce the incidence of cerebral desaturation events and postoperative delirium in patients undergoing ARCR; (2) to determine whether intraoperative continuous infusion of subanesthetic esketamine can decrease perioperative opioid consumption and pain scores within 48 h after ARCR; (3) to evaluate whether clear intraoperative continuous infusion with escitalopram and ketamine can improve postoperative recovery quality in patients who underwent ARCR on the third day; (4) to confirm any complications associated with continuous infusion of a subanesthetic dose of esketamine during surgery, such as hallucinations, heart rate increase exceeding 20% baseline value, blood pressure increase exceeding 20% baseline value, nausea, and vomiting.

Trial design {8}

This is a single-center, double-blind, randomized placebo-controlled clinical trial. The present trial is being conducted in adherence to the principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki and Good Clinical Practice Guidelines. Ethical approval for this study was granted by the Ethical Committees of the West China Hospital, Sichuan University (identifier: No. 2023–2282). This study protocol was registered in the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry. Informed consent was obtained from the participants and their guardian(s).

Methods: participants, interventions, and outcomes

Study setting {9}

The study will be conducted at the West China Hospital, Sichuan University. Participant enrollment began in February 1 2024, and end of recruitment is on November1 2024.

Eligibility criteria {10}

Inclusion criteria

The inclusion criteria for patients are as follows: (1) aged ≥ 18 years, (2) planning elective general anesthesia for arthroscopic shoulder surgery, (3) American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) physical status I–II, and (4) voluntary signing of informed consent by the patient or their legal guardian.

Exclusion criteria

The exclusion criteria for patients include the following: (1) an established mental illness; (2) severe disorders affecting consciousness or mental health, such as schizophrenia, mania, bipolar disorder, psychosis, and cognitive dysfunction; (3) a history of chronic pain or opioid abuse prior to surgery; (4) drug and/or alcohol abuse within the last 3 months, defined as consuming an average of more than 2 units of alcohol per day (1 unit = 360 ml of 5% beer or 45 ml of 40% liquor or 150 ml of wine); (5) postoperative admission to the ICU; (6) allergy to the active ingredient or excipient of esketamine hydrochloride injection; (7) contraindications for esketamine use including a risk for serious rise in blood pressure or intracranial pressure, high intraocular pressure (glaucoma), poorly controlled hypertension (resting systolic blood pressure greater than 180 mmHg or resting diastolic blood pressure greater than 100 mmHg), and untreated/undertreated hyperthyroidism; (8) male or female with family planning concerns; and (9) involvement in other drugs or medical devices clinical research for a duration of approximately 3 months.

Who will take informed consent? {26a}

The investigator conducted preoperative screening of eligible patients, and informed consent was obtained from patients or relatives before or on the day of surgery. We guarantee that all participants are fully informed and understand the potential benefits and risks of the study in detail before signing the informed consent form.

Additional consent provisions for collection and use of participant data and biological specimens {26b}

This is not applicable; this trial does not have biological specimens.

Interventions

Explanation for the choice of comparators {6b}

We aimed to examine the effects of different subanesthetic doses of esketamine administered during surgery on early postoperative quality of recovery in ARCR patients. Group S1 received a high dose of esketamine, group S2 received a low dose, and group C served as the control with an equal volume of normal saline solution.

Intervention description {11a}

Group S1 received an intravenous injection of 0.5 mg/kg esketamine diluted to 10 ml with normal saline before the surgical incision, followed by a continuous infusion of 0.25 (mg/kg.h) esketamine with normal saline up to 20 ml until the surgical incisions were closed. Group S2 received an intravenous injection of 0.25 mg/kg esketamine diluted with normal saline to 10 ml before the surgical incision, followed by a continuous infusion of 0.125 (mg/kg.h) esketamine with normal saline up to 20 ml until the surgical incisions were closed. Group C received the same volume of normal saline.

Management of anesthesia

Upon arrival in the operating room, standard monitors will be used, including pulse oximetry, invasive radial artery blood pressure monitoring, and electrocardiogram leads. Before the surgery, an INVOS 5100C device from Covidien is utilized to monitor regional cerebral oxygen saturation. The attending anesthesiologist administers general anesthesia intravenously using midazolam (0.05 mg/kg), dexamethasone (5 mg), penehyclidine hydrochloride (0.5 mg), sufentanil (0.4 μg/kg), propofol (2 mg/kg), and cisatracurium (2.0 mg/kg). The patient is intubated with a single-lumen tracheal tube and receives a lung-protective ventilation strategy with a tidal volume of 6–8 ml/kg predicted body weight, positive end-expiratory pressure (PEEP) of 5 cmH2O, and lung recruitment maneuvers. After being positioned laterally from the supine position, anesthesia is maintained through inhalation of sevoflurane and continuous infusion of remifentanil (0.1–0.2 μg/kg/min). To optimize visualization of the surgical field, a systolic blood pressure between 95 and 105 mmHg is aimed for by titrating the dosage of the anesthetic and administering a vasoactive agent such as nicardipine (0.1–0.3 mg per time) or ephedrine (3 mg per time). Neuromuscular blockade will be sustained by intermittent injections of cisatracurium as required, at a dose rate of 5 mg per injection. Approximately 30 min before the completion of surgery, sufentanil (0.1 μg/kg), flurbiprofen axetil (50 mg), and tropisetron (5 mg) are administered. If necessary for the purpose of reversal, neostigmine (0.02 mg/kg) and atropine (0.01 mg/kg) will be used. After the surgery is completed, all patients will be transferred to the postanesthesia care unit for recovery. For patients with a postoperative numeric rating scale (NRS) pain score ≥ 4, hydromorphone can be administered for additional analgesia at a dose equivalent to 10 μg/kg. In the ward, dezocine will be given to patients with an NRS-R score ≥ 4.

Criteria for discontinuing or modifying allocated interventions {11b}

The criteria for dropout are as follows: (1) patients who withdraw their consent, (2) patients who are lost to follow-up, (3) patients with a serious adverse event suspected to have been caused by the study drug and other anesthetics will be replaced, (4) patients for whom the entire clinical trial could not be completed due to other reasons. The detailed reasons for dropout will be recorded in the case report forms (CRFs).

Strategies to improve adherence to interventions {11c}

At the beginning of the study, the participants are once again informed about the course of the experiment. They can report their personal feelings at any time during the study, and we will provide appropriate feedback if necessary to improve compliance with the interventions.

Relevant concomitant care permitted or prohibited during the trial {11d}

The provision of additional care during the trial followed institutional protocols.

Provisions for post-trial care {30}

This study does not increase the incidence of anesthesia-related risks and complications included in the anesthesia consent form signed by the patients, and the research unit has the conditions and ability to deal with the above anesthesia risks and complications. This study provides standard monitoring methods without additional physical harm.

Outcomes {12}

Primary outcomes

The primary outcome of our study is the quality of early recovery scores, which are assessed using the QoR-15 scale on POD1. The QoR-15 comprises 15 questions assessing physical comfort (5 items), emotional state (4 items), physical independence (2 items), psychological support (2 items), and pain (2 items) [13]. The higher the QoR-15 score was, the better the quality of recovery after surgery (range was 0 to 150 points).

Sample size {14}

The sample size for the primary outcome of this study was calculated based on a 2 to 1 ratio [27] between exposed patients (combined ketamine groups) and unexposed patients (control group), using the PASS 15.0 software. The minimal clinically important difference (MCID) in the QoR-15 scale was 6.0 [28]. The primary outcome of our study is based on the QoR-15 score of POD1 [29, 30], which serves as the basis for sample size calculation. According to the unpublished experimental data from the initial phase of the center, the total QoR-15 score on POD1 was 116 ± 14.6 in the esketamine group and 107 ± 15.3 in the control group. A sample size of 221 subjects was calculated using the preliminary study data, assuming SD = 15, type I error (α) = 0.05 and type II error (β) = 0.2. To account for a dropout rate of 5%, we anticipate enrolling 78 subjects in each group for this study.

Recruitment {15}

Based on the operation volume of our center, we plan to enroll the first patient in February 2024 and complete recruitment by November 2024. The investigator conducted preoperative screening of eligible patients and obtained informed consent from patients or their relatives before or on the day of surgery. We ensure that all participants are fully informed and have a comprehensive understanding of the potential benefits and risks of the study before signing the informed consent form.

Assignment of interventions: allocation

Sequence generation {16a}

The subjects will be block-randomized by the computer-generated system and divided into 3 groups. (1:1:1 ratio of group S1 to group S2 to group C). The randomization list will be prepared by an independent statistician who will not participate in the recruitment process.

Concealment mechanism {16b}

The randomization will be packaged in opaque envelopes.

Implementation {16c}

The trial team consisted of one statistician, two researchers, and two follow-up personnel. One researcher is responsible for screening and recruiting the subjects. The envelopes containing the group assignment and treatment plan (sequentially numbered, sealed, and opaque) will be opened before the procedure by a nurse who is not involved in the trial, and the prepared formulations of either saline placebo or esketamine will be delivered directly to the operating room.

Assignment of interventions: blinding

Who will be blinded {17a}

All participants, including enrolled patients, anesthesiologists, surgeons, and follow-up personnel, are keep blind to the drugs and group assignments except for statisticians. The study syringes are prepared by a nurse who is not involved in the research and has opened the envelope, ensuring that neither the contents of the syringe (esketamine vs. saline) nor the ketamine concentration (if containing esketamine) can be visually determined.

Procedure for unblinding if needed {17b}

In general, there should be no need to unblind the allocation. If urgent unbinding to participants is necessary, the allocation will be disclosed to the treating physician.

Data collection and management

Plans for assessment and collection of outcomes {18a}

The baseline data included demographic information such as age, sex, and BMI as well as diagnoses of comorbidities, preoperative medication usage, surgical history, and relevant laboratory test results. Intraoperative data includes patients’ vital signs, types and doses of anesthetic drugs, durations of surgery and anesthesia, and types and volumes of infused fluids as well as CDEs. After extubation in the post-anesthesia care unit (PACU), early postoperative pain scores and neurological complications will be evaluated.

Quality of recovery evaluation

The quality of recovery was evaluated by the QoR-15 scale POD1 and POD3. The QoR-15 scale evaluates five dimensions: pain, physical health, comfort, physical independence, psychological support and emotional state.

Delirium assessment

Delirium was assessed using the 3D-CAM within 3 days after surgery.

All the researchers responsible for the POD assessment were required to receive training and approval before commencing the assessments. Once weekly, the patients who were difficult to evaluate were referred to the collaborating neuropsychologist and discussed.

Adverse events

All adverse events observed by the investigators or reported spontaneously by the subjects will be recorded. The recorded information included the diagnosis, occurrence time, remission time, severity, relationship with the intervention, treatment, and outcomes of adverse events.

Plans to promote participant retention and complete follow-up {18b}

The researcher will maintain continuous communication with the participants throughout the study, striving to address their feedback while adhering to the trial protocol. If a patient fails to cooperate or attend scheduled study visits, the investigator may terminate their participation. In such cases, data collected from the patient until their removal will still be included in the analysis.

Data management {19}

Each subject has a unique record number, and all patient information is recorded on the CRF according to the study protocol. The research staff will complete the filling of the CRF, which will be supervised by the statistician every month. Only personnel responsible for data entry and statistical analysis are allowed to view the data. The CRF data is manually transferred from the CRF to the electronic data management system by researchers and statisticians, and it is repeatedly verified to ensure correct entry. Informed consent and the CRF will be obtained and stored in a standardized manner. To protect privacy, personal information such as ID number, phone number, hospitalization number, and name will be anonymized in the electronic data system. The electronic data will be kept in a password-protected folder accessible only to researchers and statisticians. The electronic data must remain unaltered during the trial and be sealed afterwards. It will be deleted after 2 years, while paper data will be kept at the hospital for 10 years.

Confidentiality {27}

All data about potential and enrolled participants will be collected in a secure and logged database, in a secure and logged SharePoint, or behind a double lock for data in paper format. Only anonymized data will be shared.

Plans for collection, laboratory evaluation, and storage of biological specimens for genetic or molecular analysis in this trial/future use {33}

This is not applicable; this trial does not have biological specimens.

Statistical methods

Statistical methods for primary and secondary outcomes {20a}

For the quality of early recovery scores (the primary outcome of the trial), means and SDs or medians and IQRs will be presented for each treatment group and compared using Student’s t­test or the Mann–Whitney test. For trend analyses relating to dose escalations, the Cochran-Armitage test was used. For comparisons of proportions across groups (incidence of postoperative nausea or vomiting, and adverse events), we used chi squared analyses. All statistical testing was two-sided, and p < 0.05 was regarded as significant. Kaplan–Meier curves and Log-rank tests were used to evaluate the duration of delirium in patients within 3 days after surgery and the difference between the two groups.

Interim analyses {21b}

This is not applicable. Interim analyses will not be performed.

Methods for additional analyses (e.g., subgroup analyses) {20b}

Exploratory subgroup analyses will be performed based on age, gender, and history of hypertension. Due to potential limitations in test efficiency, only the results of the analysis will be provided upon request.

Methods in analysis to handle protocol non-adherence and any statistical methods to handle missing data {20c}

The reasons and circumstances for discontinuing the study will be documented in the case report form (CRF). Additionally, if a patient fails to cooperate or attend scheduled study visits, the investigator may terminate their participation. In such cases, data collected from the patient until their removal will still be included in the analysis. If there is substantial missing data (e.g., baseline information, study visits, or primary outcome measures), a new patient will be recruited as a replacement. Patients who undergo open surgery during the course of the study will receive standard treatment based on intraoperative findings. These patients will be excluded from further analysis while data up to that point will still be considered.

Plans to give access to the full protocol, participant-level data, and statistical code {31c}

The datasets analyzed during the current study and statistical code are available from the corresponding author on reasonable request, as is the full protocol.

Oversight and monitoring

Composition of the coordinating center and trial steering committee {5d}

The trial is managed by a team consisting of the investigators, the trial coordinators, statisticians, and the informatics technician. The research team held offline meetings twice every 2 weeks to summarize and feedback the progress of the trial, improve the problems encountered in the process of the trial under the principle of not violating the protocol, and improve the feasibility of the trial process. The study team was monitored regularly by the trial steering committee at the center.

Composition of the data monitoring committee, its role and reporting structure {21a}

A composition of data monitoring committee (DMC) formed by at least two clinicians and one statistician who are independent of the sponsor without competing interests will monitor the conduct of the trial.

Adverse event reporting and harms {22}

Patients were closely monitored for adverse events during this study. The safety of treatment regimens will be assessed by changes in vital signs, physical examinations, laboratory data, incidence of adverse events (AEs), and serious adverse events (SAEs). Both AEs and SAEs that occurred during drug therapy will be recorded on the case report form (CRF) accordingly. SAEs that are considered related to the study are monitored and reported without delay. Study-specific reportable adverse events, including death, resulting in a life-threatening situation or significant disability, or requiring inpatient hospitalization or prolongation of existing hospitalization, will also be monitored during the trial. The adverse effects or unpredictable complications will be diligently documented and promptly reported to the ethics committee. The participants will also be followed up until complete resolution or termination of therapy. Patients who experience any adverse outcomes directly caused by the research will be compensated in accordance with the law.

Common AEs include (1) an increase in blood pressure and heart rate and generally do not require treatment. (2) an increase in secretions, use atropine to prevent disease; (3) blurred vision and diplopia, which are transient and generally do not need to be dealt with; and (4) agitation, delirium, separation anxiety, and other psychiatric adverse reactions, which can resolve spontaneously in the short term without special treatment, and benzodiazepines can be given when necessary. (5) Nausea and vomiting, which are generally mild and tolerable, may be alleviated by the use of antiemetics such as Setron, if necessary.

Frequency and plans for auditing trial conduct {23}

Audits were conducted monthly by the trial steering committee at the center.

Any protocol amendments will be incorporated into a formal substantial amendment, which will undergo review by the institutional review board of the West China Hospital before being applied. A revised plan will then be issued after thorough examination and approval.

Discussion

ARCR has proven to be an effective treatment for repairable rotator cuff tears. It is associated with significant postoperative pain, with studies reporting pain that was as severe as after open surgery [3]. However, improper postoperative pain management leads to sleep disturbances, persistent pain, and even dysfunction [4]. Therefore, appropriate postoperative pain management for all patients after ARCR can improve overall patient outcomes and improve postoperative shoulder joint function. Various methods of pain control such as regional nerve blocks and intravenous patient-controlled analgesia have been increasingly used in recent years. The interscalene brachial plexus block is very effective for immediate postoperative pain management but has a shorter duration of action and carries the potential risk of nerve damage [31]. Opioid-based patient-controlled intravenous analgesia is associated with adverse effects such as nausea, vomiting, and constipation, and, more importantly, they have a significant potential for abuse [5].

In arthroscopic rotator cuff repair (ARCR), bleeding during the operation often leads to poor visibility and delays the continuation of the operation. It is therefore recommended to maintain intraoperative hypotension to achieve good visualization. With ARCR in the beach chair position, it is dangerous to lower blood pressure carelessly during surgery as this reduces cerebral blood flow and increases the risk of cerebral infarction [7].

Esketamine is the dextrorotatory isomer of ketamine, which is safer and suitable for the induction and maintenance of generahyperpathial anesthesia. In recent years, perioperative analgesia of esketamine is also an important direction; some of the studies have shown its effect in analgesia and hyperpathia [32]. In addition, esketamine has strong antidepressant effects and rapidly reduces suicidal ideation. In the perioperative period, it can reduce the incidence of postoperative depression after gynecological and gastrointestinal surgery [33, 34]. The binding of ketamine to NMDA receptors leads to a reduced frequency and duration of Ca+2 channel opening and thus inhibits glutaminergic transmission. This mechanism has been shown to be neuroprotective in various neurological disorders. Ketamine does not increase intracranial pressure (ICP) and maintains cerebral perfusion pressure (CPP) by increasing cerebral blood flow [35, 36]. Esketamine also blocks Na + channels. The blockade of Na + channels in the parasympathetic neurons in the brainstem can inhibit the parasympathetic nerves and increase heart rate and blood pressure [32]. The above properties of esketamine may be beneficial for postoperative recovery. Cheng et al. [22] indicated that perioperative subanesthetic esketamine (a bolus of 0.25 mg/kg followed by an infusion of 0.125 mg/kg/h until 15 min before the end of the surgical procedure) improved the quality of recovery in patients undergoing video-assisted thoracoscopic surgery. Zhu et al. [30] showed that infusion of esketamine (0.5 mg/kg, 4 μg/kg/h infusion) improved the quality of recovery to some extent on the first and third day after surgery in patients undergoing modified radical mastectomy. The results of Zhang et al. demonstrate that general anesthesia with esketamine as a potential strategy has high safety and can not only improve the quality of recovery mainly by improving pain, physical comfort, and emotional state but also promote the recovery of cognitive functions at POD1 in patients undergoing modified radical mastectomy [23].

This study is the first to investigate the effect of esketamine on the quality of recovery in patients undergoing rotator cuff repair surgery and to prospectively evaluate whether esketamine can improve the incidence of postoperative pain, sleep, and delirium in patients undergoing rotator cuff repair surgery.

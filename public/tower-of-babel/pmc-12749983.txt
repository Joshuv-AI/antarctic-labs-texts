Multi-scale aggregation network for colonoscopic polyp segmentation via frequency domain decoupling
Authors: Yanling Wang, Kho Lee Chin, Ngu Sze Song, Shengjin Guo, Wenjing Zhao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Automated segmentation of colorectal polyps is of great significance for early screening and clinical intervention of colorectal cancer. However, the diversity of polyp morphology and the uneven contrast caused by illumination changes in colonoscopy images make accurate segmentation and edge extraction of polyps a challenging task. To this end, this paper proposes a frequency domain decoupled multi-scale feature aggregation network (FDANet). The network employs wavelet transform to decompose spatial domain features into frequency domain sub-bands, extracting both low-frequency and high-frequency components. By leveraging their distinct frequency characteristics, the model is guided to suppress redundant information, emphasize target-relevant features, and achieve more robust and accurate segmentation results. In FDANet, the low-frequency attention enhancement module (LAEM) suppresses high-frequency background noise by performing Gaussian difference operations on low-frequency components and incorporates a hybrid attention mechanism to strengthen the feature representation of foreground regions. The high-frequency multi-scale aggregation module (HMAM) employs directional convolution kernels to model high-frequency components, extract fine-grained edge information, and construct a multi-scale feature pyramid to accommodate the morphological and scale diversity of polyps, while enhancing spatial detail awareness during the decoding stage. Additionally, an edge loss function is introduced to supervise the modeling of edge contours within this module, effectively suppressing background noise interference and further improving boundary localization accuracy. Experimental results show that this method achieves good segmentation results on CVC-ClinicDB and Kvasir-SEG datasets, outperforming other advanced segmentation methods.

Introduction

Colorectal polyps are abnormal lesions that protrude from the surface of the colonic mucosa into the lumen. Based on their histological characteristics, they are mainly classified into four categories: adenomatous polyps, inflammatory polyps, hamartomatous polyps, and hyperplastic polyps. Among these, adenomatous polyps, as a common neoplastic form, possess a high potential for malignant transformation and are widely regarded as key precancerous lesions in the development of colorectal cancer1. According to statistics, more than 90% of colorectal cancer cases gradually evolve from adenomatous polyps2. Therefore, the early detection, precise localization, and timely resection of polyps are of great clinical significance in preventing the onset of colorectal cancer3.

In clinical practice, colonoscopy has become the primary technique for the screening and diagnosis of colorectal polyps due to its capability for direct visualization and tissue biopsy. It is extensively applied in the field of gastrointestinal endoscopy4. Currently, the detection and delineation of polyp boundaries largely depend on the subjective judgment of endoscopists, whose diagnostic accuracy is influenced by their clinical experience and operational proficiency5. In cases involving small polyps, indistinct boundaries, or atypical morphological features, manual identification is prone to omissions or misinterpretations, which can significantly compromise the accuracy of early diagnosis and delay timely intervention.

With the rapid advancement of artificial intelligence, computer-assisted interventions based on deep learning have emerged as a prominent research focus in the field of medical image analysis6. Among various deep learning approaches, convolutional neural networks (CNNs) have demonstrated notable advantages in medical image segmentation tasks, owing to their strong capabilities in feature extraction and spatial representation7. Among them, the network based on the U-Net architecture has achieved excellent performance in the segmentation tasks of various medical imaging data such as magnetic resonance imaging, ultrasound images and pathological sections, relying on its unique U-shaped encoding and decoding structure and skip connection mechanism, and has become one of the most popular and widely used classic network structures8.

However, in the specific task of colorectal polyp segmentation, existing methods still encounter numerous challenges. First, due to the narrow and tortuous internal structure of the intestine, combined with the moist and reflective mucosal surface, the acquired images often suffer from motion blur, uneven illumination, and specular highlights. As illustrated in Fig. 1a, pronounced light reflections within the image severely interfere with the separation between the polyp and the background. In addition, as shown in Fig. 1b, uneven illumination results in reduced overall image contrast, thereby rendering the polyp boundaries indistinct. Secondly, colorectal polyps exhibit a high degree of morphological diversity and uncertainty. Polyps in different patients and at different stages of disease progression may present a variety of sizes, shapes, colors, and surface texture characteristics, and may also be accompanied by occlusions or other complicating factors. As illustrated in Fig. 1c, the polyp is partially occluded by intestinal folds and residual materials, which diminishes the model’s ability to accurately distinguish it from surrounding tissues. In addition, Fig. 1d depicts multiple polyps of varying sizes and locations, among which smaller polyps are more prone to being overlooked, thereby increasing the risk of missed detection.Fig. 1Colonoscopy polyp collection image.

In order to cope with the challenges in colorectal polyp images, existing studies have proposed innovative solutions from multiple perspectives. In terms of integrating local details with global semantic modeling, Bo Dong et al.9 proposed combining Vision Transformer with convolutional modules. Through local similarity enhancement and global semantic guidance, the ability to locate polyp areas was improved, enabling the model to effectively handle motion blur and mirror reflection interference areas in the image. Jun Wei et al.10 employed an attention mechanism to dynamically adjust the weighting of foreground and background features based on the image content, thereby enhancing focus on foreground polyps while suppressing interference from background noise. In response to the diversity of polyp shapes and sizes, Nikhil et al.11 designed a multi-scale feature aggregation and adaptive selection module, enabling the model to flexibly handle polyp targets of varying scales and quantities. Abhishek et al.12 proposed a regional boundary constraint network to enhance the model’s perception of polyp contours and structural boundaries, thereby effectively mitigating segmentation errors caused by small targets or blurred edges. However, most existing methods rely solely on the explicit structural information of images in the spatial domain for feature modeling. Nevertheless, in the presence of intestinal folds, tissue overlap, and severe light interference, spatial domain-based feature extraction is highly susceptible to disruption by high-frequency noise. As illustrated in Fig. 1a, the boundary area of the colorectal polyp is obstructed by bright reflective points, which limits the ability of spatial domain-based methods to perceive edge information and increases the likelihood of misjudgment. In the frequency domain, an image is represented as a set of frequency components that collectively describe patterns and structures across different spatial scales. High-frequency components primarily contain edge and fine detail information, whereas low-frequency components capture the overall contours and regional intensity distributions. By flexibly combining and regulating these frequency components, the network can effectively extract key local variations that are difficult to detect in the spatial domain, while preserving the global structural information of the image13. Therefore, by collaboratively modeling both the spatial and frequency domains, the network can fully leverage the spatial domain’s advantages in contextual understanding and semantic representation, while utilizing the analytical capabilities of the frequency domain to more accurately extract edge structures and texture details. This dual-domain approach enables more comprehensive expression of foreground target features while effectively suppressing background noise, artifact interference, and uneven illumination, thereby enhancing the segmentation accuracy and stability of the model in complex imaging scenarios.

Based on the above analysis, we propose a novel frequency domain decoupled multi-scale feature aggregation network (FDANet), which integrates wavelet transform to perform multi-scale frequency domain decoupling on the encoded features. Feature aggregation is subsequently conducted in the decoding stage to enhance the model’s capability in colorectal polyp segmentation. FDANet extracts and aggregates frequency domain information through two specialized modules: the low-frequency attention enhancement module (LAEM) and the high-frequency multi-scale aggregation Module (HMAM). Specifically, the LAEM module sumppresses background noise by utilizing low-frequency information extracted via wavelet transform based on spatial features, thereby enhancing the representation of foreground polyp regions and improving the overall robustness of the model. The HMAM module aggregates high-frequency components in three directions and effectively enhances the expression of structural information such as edges and contours by constructing a multi-scale feature pyramid. Furthermore, to address the potential noise interference introduced during the high-frequency aggregation process, this paper incorporates an edge loss function, which simultaneously considers regional consistency, structural integrity, and boundary refinement during optimization, thereby further improving segmentation accuracy. In summary, the main contributions of this paper are outlined as follows: We propose a frequency-domain decoupled multi-scale feature aggregation network based on wavelet transform, which improves the segmentation capability of colorectal polyps by supplementing spatial features with parsing frequency-domain information.We design a low-frequency attention enhancement module that suppresses spatial background noise through Gaussian difference filtering and employs an attention mechanism to strengthen foreground salient feature representations.We propose a high-frequency multi-scale aggregation module that utilizes high-frequency information to construct a feature pyramid for capturing the diversity of polyp morphology, and introduces an edge loss function to guide the model in learning accurate boundary contours.Extensive comparison and ablation experiments are conducted on two public datasets to validate the superiority of the proposed model and the effectiveness of each module.

Related work

Polyp segmentation refers to the process of automatically identifying and delineating polyp regions in colonoscopy images or videos using medical image analysis techniques14. Early polyp segmentation primarily relied on physicians’ visual inspection and manual delineation of polyp regions, which was not only time-consuming and inefficient but also highly subjective. In recent years, researchers have been dedicated to developing efficient and reliable automatic segmentation methods to enhance the diagnostic efficiency and accuracy of polyp detection. For example, Jerebko15 and Tajbakhsh16 employed edge detection operators to extract candidate polyp regions of interest and combined morphological transformation techniques to determine optimal thresholds for achieving polyp segmentation. Other approaches have utilized methods such as K-means clustering, particle swarm optimization, and watershed algorithms, often leveraging specific prior knowledge to perform automatic polyp segmentation. These traditional methods typically rely on manually designed features to extract low-level visual information such as shape, color, and texture. However, due to the similarity between polyps and surrounding tissues and the morphological diversity exhibited by polyps, fixed feature representations are insufficient to capture all possible variations, often leading to missed detections or false positives. To address these limitations, researchers have increasingly adopted deep learning-based approaches to automatically learn feature representations, capture higher-level semantic information within images, and improve segmentation accuracy.

With the rapid advancement of deep learning technology, convolutional neural network (CNN)-based methods have achieved significant breakthroughs in the field of colorectal polyp segmentation. As a milestone in this field, the U-Net architecture proposed by Ronneberger et al. employs a symmetrical encoder–decoder structure with skip connections, enabling the extraction of deep semantic features while integrating shallow spatial structural information, thereby significantly improving segmentation performance17. Based on the U-shaped structure, researchers proposed a variety of improvement schemes to meet the challenges in polyp segmentation tasks. For example, Zhang et al.18 and Alam et al.19 introduced Transformer modules to enhance the modeling capability of global contextual features, and combined them with attention mechanisms to capture local spatial structural information, thereby improving the model’s ability to understand images and enhancing the segmentation accuracy of polyps. Given the variability in the size, shape, and position of polyps, improving the network’s multi-scale feature extraction capability is critical to effectively addressing the diversity of feature representations. Zhao et al.20 proposed a universal multi-scale subtraction network, which enhances multi-scale information representation for the decoder by computing feature differences between adjacent encoder levels and performing multi-layer feature mapping, thereby improving segmentation accuracy and detail restoration capability. Although the above network has achieved good results in the polyp segmentation task, the existing methods still have certain limitations due to problems such as blurred boundaries between polyps and their surrounding mucosa.

Accurately extracting polyp boundaries remains a challenging task in polyp segmentation. To address this issue, recent studies have focused on enhancing the model’s attention to polyp boundaries. Some of these methods decompose feature maps to enhance the feature expression of key components. For example, Fan et al.21 proposed the Pra-Net for polyp segmentation, which employs a parallel decoder structure to predict coarse segmentation regions and integrates a reverse attention mechanism to guide the model’s focus toward the edges and internal structures of polyps, thereby achieving fine-grained segmentation of polyp regions. ISCNet, proposed by Wang et al.22, utilizes image-level contextual information to explicitly model the foreground–background relationship within the polyp region, thereby enhancing target feature representation and suppressing irrelevant interference. In addition, fine-grained features of polyp edges and surrounding tissues are further analyzed through a local feature extraction module, thus improving the integrity and accuracy of segmentation boundaries. On the other hand, richer feature representations can be obtained through feature aggregation. For instance, Su et al.23 proposed a novel multi-level feature fusion method, which employs a learnable semantic offset field to dynamically correct misalignments between features at different levels and incorporates auxiliary boundary branches to specifically optimize boundary features. Zhou et al.24 introduced a cross-level feature aggregation network (CFA-Net), which generates boundary-aware features through a boundary prediction network and adopts a hierarchical strategy to integrate them into the backbone network. Combined with a multi-scale feature enhancement mechanism, CFA-Net effectively improves the representation capability of boundary regions.The LFSRNet proposed by Yue et al.25 employs an encoder-decoder architecture, achieving non-local attention fusion and adaptive refinement of high-level features through a lesion-aware feature selection and refinement module. It then accurately locates polyp regions in the output of adjacent upper layers in the decoder. The DFINet proposed by Li et al.26 extends feature extraction from the spatial domain to the frequency domain, introducing a spatial and frequency domain feature interaction module in each encoding layer and designing a boundary enhancement module to fuse cross-layer features and multi-scale contextual information, thus effectively handling problems such as irregular polyp morphology and blurred boundaries. Furthermore, some studies focus more on boundary constraints to improve the accuracy of polyp segmentation. For example, Bui et al.27 and Cai et al. designed an edge attention module to extract high-frequency edge information in images, guiding the network to focus on edge information and effectively improving the network’s ability to perceive key edge regions. Yue et al.28,29 designed the boundary uncertainty awareness network BUNet and the boundary refinement network BRNet, focusing on blurred regions that are neither biased towards the foreground nor the background, mining boundary cues to guide predictions in adjacent higher layers, and achieving coarse-to-fine polyp segmentation through top-down hybrid deep supervision. However, Wu et al.30 and Yue et al.31 not only aggregated low-level and high-level features across layers, but also designed an edge-guided module to collaboratively explore high-level boundary information and low-level regional information and perform joint supervision to generate accurate polyp boundaries.

Frequency domain feature extraction methods transform spatial features into frequency domain components, leveraging their unique properties to offer significant advantages for segmentation tasks. In the frequency domain, an image is represented as a set of frequency components that describe structural patterns and texture information at different scales, thereby providing a more discriminative feature representation for segmentation. For instance, Li et al.32 introduced a global filter to extract high-frequency information, thereby compensating for the lack of fine detail. Qin et al.33 utilized frequency domain information to extend the channel attention mechanism, selectively enhancing explicit feature representations and improving the model’s ability to capture polyp features. In addition, frequency domain features can also be fused with spatial features as an independent branch. For example, Zhong et al.34 incorporated frequency domain information into the skip connections of U-Net and fused it with spatial domain features to further improve the model’s segmentation performance. Zhu et al.35 constructed a multi-frequency fusion encoder by integrating the Mamba module with the ResNet framework to model both global contextual and spatial regional information, thereby alleviating the problem of high visual similarity between polyps and surrounding tissues. Meanwhile, incorporating frequency domain methods during feature extraction further enriches feature representations. Liu et al.36 embedded wavelet transform within a CNN structure, effectively extracting detailed and structural features of images through frequency decomposition and enhancing the spatial structural representations of polyps. In addition, Li et al.37 applied Fourier transform to the input images, utilizing both amplitude and phase components to enhance deep global features and shallow detail features within the spatial feature maps, thereby improving the model’s segmentation performance.

Although the aforementioned methods effectively enhance the segmentation performance of models by utilizing frequency domain components, the widespread presence of light spots and noise interference in colonoscopy images poses significant challenges. Simply extracting frequency domain components without selectively suppressing noise can lead to confusion between pseudo-edges generated by light spot regions and true polyp boundaries, making it difficult to accurately localize the real edges. Moreover, abnormal activations induced by noise can be progressively amplified through feature propagation in deep networks, ultimately resulting in boundary distortions in the segmentation outputs. Distinct from previous approaches, we propose a frequency domain decoupling strategy to separate high-frequency and low-frequency components. Based on the characteristics of these components, high-frequency features are leveraged to enhance the spatial detail representation of polyps, while low-frequency features are utilized to suppress background noise, thereby improving the accuracy and robustness of boundary segmentation.

Proposed methodology

The overall workflow of FDANet is illustrated in Fig. 2. The network adopts an encoder–decoder architecture and incorporates frequency domain information for colorectal polyp segmentation. The encoder is constructed based on a convolutional neural network (CNN), and wavelet transform is applied at each stage to perform frequency domain decoupling, resulting in low-frequency (LL) and high-frequency (LH, HL, and HH) components. The low-frequency components are utilized in the low-frequency attention enhancement module (LAEM) to suppress high-frequency background noise and enhance the foreground feature representation of polyps. The high-frequency components are incorporated into the high-frequency multi-scale aggregation module (HMAM) to refine features such as edges and contours, thereby capturing local spatial information and improving the decoder’s reconstruction capability through feature aggregation. Furthermore, to further enhance the model’s boundary perception, an edge loss function is introduced to guide the learning of complex contour structures and improve the fine-grained segmentation performance of the polyp regions.Fig. 2FDANet network structure diagram.

Frequency domain decoupling

The objective of frequency domain decoupling is to convert the feature map from the spatial domain to the frequency domain. In this representation, the low-frequency components, located at the lower end of the spectrum, capture the global structural information of the image, whereas the high-frequency components, situated at the upper end of the spectrum, represent variations in fine details and edge structures. Therefore, to enhance the model’s ability to capture hierarchical structural information and edge details, this study introduces a frequency domain decoupling module at each stage of the encoder. This module employs a two-dimensional discrete wavelet transform (2D-DWT) to convert features from the spatial domain to the frequency domain, thereby enabling the separation of low-frequency and high-frequency components. Low-frequency attention enhancement module

Since the surface of the intestinal wall is moist and there are bubbles, these factors will form a high-reflection area when the colonoscope collects polyp images, which appears as randomly distributed high-frequency noise in the image. This type of noise not only reduces the overall contrast of the image, but also interferes with the feature expression around small polyps, leading to model misjudgment. In order to effectively suppress background noise interference, we proposed the LAEM module, whose specific structure is shown in Fig. 3.

During feature extraction in the spatial domain, the inherent high-frequency sensitivity of convolution operations renders them susceptible to interference from noise surrounding the polyp, which can adversely affect the accuracy of segmentation. In contrast, operating in the frequency domain enables more effective suppression of such noise, resulting in smoother feature representations. In addition, given the relatively homogeneous color distribution within the intestinal environment, polyp regions typically reside in the lower frequency bands of the spectrum, exhibiting a distinct frequency contrast with background noise. Accordingly, the proposed LAEM module in this study integrates both spatial and frequency domain features. By leveraging the low-frequency components, it effectively suppresses background noise while preserving the spatial structural characteristics of the polyp, thereby enhancing segmentation performance.

High-frequency multi-scale aggregation module

The location and morphology of polyps are critical factors in clinical diagnosis; however, their positions and sizes observed in colonoscopy are often random and exhibit substantial inter-patient variability. Consequently, accurately localizing polyps and delineating their boundary contours remains a technical challenge. To address this issue and more effectively capture the spatial structures and fine-grained details of polyps with varying sizes, we propose the HMAM module in the decoding stage. This module constructs a feature pyramid by aggregating the high-frequency components (LH, HL, HH) obtained from wavelet transform, aiming to comprehensively represent both the spatial details and positional features of large and small targets. The structure and details of the module are illustrated in Fig. 4.

For the high-frequency components obtained by wavelet transform in the encoding stage, the HMAM module designs convolution kernels in the same direction for processing to extract directional detail features, give the network direction-selective response capabilities, and avoid confusion of edge features during feature aggregation. Meanwhile, to accommodate feature responses from targets at different scales, HMAM constructs a feature pyramid based on multi-scale high-frequency features, thereby enhancing the model’s ability to recover polyp regions in the decoding stage. In HMAM, the feature aggregation process is primarily divided into two stages. The first stage, termed high-frequency perception (HFP), is designed to capture fine-grained features such as local textures and abrupt edge variations. Experiments

Polyp segmentation dataset and implementation details

To ensure the fairness of the experiment and the universality of the results, this paper evaluates the proposed method on two public datasets, CVC-ClinicDB38 and Kvasir-SEG39. CVC-ClinicDB consists of 612 images extracted from 29 different endoscopic video clips. The images in this dataset have relatively low resolution and consistent content features, making it suitable for evaluating the model’s stability in polyp segmentation tasks and its capability to accurately localize boundaries. In contrast, Kvasir-SEG contains 1,000 polyp images with varying resolutions, covering a wide range of polyp morphologies and anatomical regions. This dataset enables a comprehensive assessment of the model’s generalization performance under different lesion characteristics, complex background interference, and scale variations.

To comprehensively evaluate the performance of the proposed model, a 5-fold cross-validation strategy is adopted. Specifically, the dataset is randomly shuffled and divided into five subsets. In each experiment, one fold is selected as the test set, while the remaining four folds are used as the training set, maintaining a training-to-testing ratio of 8:2. To further optimize the training process, the training set is randomly split into training and validation subsets at a ratio of 6:2. During training, all input images are uniformly resized to \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$256 \times 256$$\end{document} pixels, and data augmentation techniques such as random mirroring, rotation, and translation are applied to enrich the training data. In each experiment, the model weights achieving the best performance on the validation set are retained for subsequent test evaluation. Finally, the average performance metrics across the five test folds are calculated to comprehensively reflect the actual performance of the proposed model on the polyp segmentation task.

The segmentation model proposed in this paper is implemented using the PyTorch deep learning framework and trained on a computing platform equipped with an NVIDIA RTX A4500 GPU. The total number of training epochs is set to 100 with a batch size of 8. The AdamW optimizer is employed to update the model parameters, with an initial learning rate set to 1e-3. A CosineAnnealingLR learning rate scheduler is adopted to periodically adjust the learning rate, allowing it to decay smoothly from the initial value following a cosine curve within each cycle, with the minimum value decreasing to 50% of the initial learning rate.

Evaluation metrics

In medical image segmentation tasks, evaluation metrics are generally divided into two categories: region-based metrics and boundary-based metrics. Region-based metrics, such as the DICE coefficient (DICE) and the Jaccard coefficient (JAC), assess segmentation performance by calculating the degree of overlap between the predicted results and the ground truth labels. These metrics focus on the overall region matching but are less sensitive to the accuracy of boundary delineation. Consequently, even if there is a noticeable deviation between the predicted boundary and the true boundary, the metric score may remain high as long as the overall overlap area is sufficiently large. In contrast, boundary-based metrics focus on evaluating local errors along the segmentation boundary. Metrics such as the 95th percentile Hausdorff Distance (HD95) and Average Surface Distance (ASD) effectively measure the distance discrepancies between the predicted and ground truth boundaries. However, these metrics are sensitive to noise and outliers in the image, which may lead to unstable evaluation results under certain conditions. Considering the respective advantages and limitations of region-based and boundary-based metrics, this paper adopts four evaluation indicators, DICE, JAC, HD95, and ASD, to comprehensively assess the segmentation performance of the model from both regional and boundary perspectives. In addition, to further enhance the clinical relevance of the evaluation system, this paper introduces two additional metrics: Sensitivity (SE) and Precision (PC), which reflect the model’s performance in handling missed detections and false positives, respectively. In the context of polyp segmentation, SE quantifies the model’s ability to correctly identify true lesion areas, while PC measures the proportion of correct segmentations among all predicted regions. By incorporating these two metrics, a more comprehensive analysis of the under-segmentation and over-segmentation issues that may arise in practical clinical scenarios can be achieved.

The impact of feature space resolution on the network

During the experiments, it was observed that the spatial resolution of the feature pyramid in the HMAM module had a noticeable impact on the segmentation performance of the model. To investigate this phenomenon, four groups of experiments were conducted to evaluate the model’s performance under different settings, using one to four layers of feature map resolutions. The corresponding experimental results are presented in Fig. 9. When the fourth-layer feature map resolution is utilized, excessive compression of spatial information occurs, leading to insufficient feature representation for accurately localizing the spatial position of the target area and ultimately resulting in degraded segmentation performance. Conversely, when the first-layer feature map resolution is adopted, although more complete spatial position information is preserved, the upsampling operation essentially enlarges the feature map size through interpolation or learned weights, which cannot fully recover the high-frequency details lost during the downsampling process. In particular, for deep features that are already highly abstracted, forced upsampling may lead to misalignment between local features and their original spatial locations, thereby reducing their discriminative power. In addition, using high-resolution feature maps significantly increases computational overhead and adversely affects the inference efficiency of the model. Based on the experimental findings, this study uniformly resamples the feature maps from each layer to match the spatial resolution of the second layer before aggregation, thereby achieving the optimal segmentation performance.

To address these issues, future research can explore the following directions: First, shape-prior-based modeling mechanisms can be introduced, such as explicitly learning the morphological structure of polyps through deformable convolutions or graph neural networks, enhancing the model’s structural perception ability under ambiguous boundary conditions. Second, multi-scale spectral modeling can guide the network backbone to separate low-frequency structural components related to category semantics and high-frequency components related to noise and mucus, thereby enhancing the model’s feature robustness and generalization ability under complex imaging conditions.

Conclusion

In this paper, we propose a frequency domain decoupled multi-scale feature aggregation network (FDANet) for colorectal polyp segmentation. Specifically, we design a low-frequency attention enhancement module (LAEM) and a high-frequency multi-scale aggregation module (HMAM). The frequency domain information is extracted from the encoded features via wavelet transform, separating them into low and high-frequency sub-bands. The LAEM module integrates low-frequency components with spatial domain feature maps to suppress background noise and enhance foreground representations. Meanwhile, the HMAM module leverages high-frequency components to extract directional features, constructs feature pyramids for multi-scale fusion, and improves the model’s ability to perceive target scale variations. Additionally, an edge loss is introduced to supervise the learning of high-frequency edge information, further enhancing the network’s modeling capability. Extensive comparative experiments conducted on two public polyp datasets demonstrate that the proposed method outperforms existing polyp segmentation approaches. Finally, ablation studies are performed to validate the effectiveness of each module and the impact of feature transfer on the overall model performance.

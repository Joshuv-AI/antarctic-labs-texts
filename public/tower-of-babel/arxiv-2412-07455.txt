Abstract
. Let 𝒯 {\mathcal{T}} be an algebraic triangulated category and 𝒞 {\mathcal{C}} an extension-closed subcategory with Hom ⁡ ( 𝒞 , Σ < 0 ​ 𝒞 ) = 0 \Hom({\mathcal{C}},\Sigma^{<0}{\mathcal{C}})=0 . 1.
Introduction

Let
{\mathcal{T}}
be a triangulated category and
{\mathcal{C}}
full additive subcategory with an exact structure. A
realization functor
for
{\mathcal{C}}
is a triangle functor
\operatorname{D}^{b}({\mathcal{C}})\to{\mathcal{T}}
extending the inclusion. There are various constructions of a realization functor, all requiring an enhancement and restricting to certain subcategories
{\mathcal{C}}
. The first realization functor was constructed in
[
BBD82
]
when
{\mathcal{C}}
is the heart of a t-structure in a filtered triangulated category; also see
[
PV18
, Appendix]
. A different construction appears in
[
Nee91
]
.

In this paper we work in algebraic triangulated categories; these include all stable module categories and derived categories. Unlike the works mentioned above we consider exact subcategories of
{\mathcal{T}}
, not hearts of t-structures. There exist exact categories whose bounded derived category does not admit a bounded t-structure; see
[
Nee24
]
.

The following result appears in
[
KV87
, 3.2 Théorème]
:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{T}}
be an algebraic triangulated category and
{\mathcal{C}}
an extension-closed full subcategory with
\Hom_{{\mathcal{T}}}({\mathcal{C}},\Sigma^{-n}{\mathcal{C}})=0
for
n\geq 1
. Then
{\mathcal{C}}
has an exact structure induced from the triangulated structure on
{\mathcal{T}}
and there exists a realization functor.

The article
[
KV87
, 3.2 Théorème]
provides a sketch of the proof, referring to a construction later appearing in
[
Kel90
]
. The main goal of this paper is to provide the missing details for a complete proof of
Theorem

1.1
.

The non-negativity condition in
Theorem

1.1
for
{\mathcal{C}}
is necessary for our construction. It also appears when the realization functor is a triangle equivalences. In fact, whenever the realization functor is fully faithful, then
{\mathcal{C}}
has to satisfy the non-negativity condition.

Theorem

1.1
can be considered the standard tool to realize an (algebraic) triangulated as a bounded derived category of an exact category; we provide conditions for when the realization functor is an equivalence in
Section

2.16
. Therefore,
Theorem

1.1
is expected to be used in classifications of exact subcategories of a triangulated category up to (bounded) derived equivalence.

Further, finding a realization functor is an alternative to tilting theory. Tilting subcategories in a triangulated category were defined by Keller; see for example
[
KK20
]
. A subcategory
{\mathcal{C}}
of
{\mathcal{T}}
is
tilting
, if
{\mathcal{C}}
is endowed with the split exact structure, hence
\operatorname{D}^{b}({\mathcal{C}})=\operatorname{K}^{b}({\mathcal{C}})
, and the realization functor
\operatorname{K}^{b}({\mathcal{C}})\to{\mathcal{T}}
exists and is a triangle equivalence. There exist realization functors that are equivalences that are not induced by tilting theory; for example the inclusion of a small exact category into its weak idempotent completion induces a triangle equivalence on their bounded derived categories; see
[
Nee90
, 1.10]
.

In general it is not known whether a realization functor of a category
{\mathcal{C}}
is unique. However, it is unique with respect to the chosen enhancement. Theorem
1.1
is also central to the search for a universal property defining the bounded derived category of an exact category; cf.
[
Kel91
]
and for derivators
[
Por17
]
.

2.
Realization functor

The bounded derived category of an exact category
{\mathcal{C}}
is the Verdier quotient of the homotopy category of the underlying additive category by the full subcategory of bounded
{\mathcal{C}}
-acyclic complexes
\operatorname{Ac}^{b}({\mathcal{C}})
; see
[
Nee90
]
and also
[
Büh10
, Section 10]
. We fix a triangulated category
{\mathcal{T}}
with suspension functor
\Sigma
. A
realization functor
for an additive subcategory
{\mathcal{C}}
of
{\mathcal{T}}
with an exact structure is a triangle functor
\operatorname{D}^{b}({\mathcal{C}})\to{\mathcal{T}}
extending the inclusion
{\mathcal{C}}\to{\mathcal{T}}
.

2.1.
Admissible exact subcategories

In this work we focus on subcategories
{\mathcal{C}}
of the triangulated category
{\mathcal{T}}
that inherit their exact structure from the triangulated structure of
{\mathcal{T}}
.

Definition 2.2
.

A full subcategory
{\mathcal{C}}
is called
non-negative
if
\Hom_{{\mathcal{T}}}({\mathcal{C}},\Sigma^{<0}{\mathcal{C}})=0
; this means
\Hom_{{\mathcal{T}}}(X,\Sigma^{n}Y)=0
for any
X,Y\in{\mathcal{C}}
and
n<0
. When
{\mathcal{C}}
is additionally closed under extensions and direct summands, we say
{\mathcal{C}}
is
admissible exact
.

By
[
Dye05
]
, any extension-closed, non-negative subcategory
{\mathcal{C}}
of a triangulated category
{\mathcal{T}}
inherits an exact structure from the triangulated structure: The short exact sequences
L\xrightarrow{f}M\xrightarrow{g}N
in
{\mathcal{C}}
are precisely those that fit into an exact triangle
L\xrightarrow{f}M\xrightarrow{g}N\xrightarrow{h}\Sigma L
.

Remark 2.3
.

With the notation of ‘admissible exact’ we follow
[
BBD82
, Définition 1.2.5]
and
[
Hub16
, Section 2]
; the former only considers ‘admissible abelian’, while the latter dropped ‘exact’. We use admissible exact to avoid confusion with the notions of left/right admissible in the sense of
[
BK89
, §1]
.

The crucial condition of admissible exactness is the non-negativity. In fact, when
{\mathcal{C}}
is non-negative, then the smallest full subcategory closed under extensions and direct summands containing
{\mathcal{C}}
is an admissible exact subcategory.

Example 2.4
.

We equip an extension-closed subcategory
{\mathcal{C}}
of an exact category
{\mathcal{E}}
with the induced exact structure; that is
{\mathcal{C}}
is a
fully exact
subcategory of
{\mathcal{E}}
. Then
{\mathcal{C}}
is an admissible exact subcategory of
\operatorname{D}^{b}({\mathcal{E}})
.

Example 2.5
.

The heart of any t-structure on a triangulated category is admissible exact. Any intersection of admissible exact subcategories is admissible exact. Hence the intersection of two hearts is admissible exact; this applies in particular for hearts that are mutations of each other; see
[
Che10
]
for HRS tilting and
[
BPPW24
]
for the heart fan of an abelian category.

2.6.
Weak realization functor

Next, we consider triangle functors
\operatorname{K}^{b}({\mathcal{C}})\to{\mathcal{T}}
extending the inclusion for any full subcategory
{\mathcal{C}}
of
{\mathcal{T}}
; such a functor can be considered as a realization functor for
{\mathcal{C}}
with the split exact structure. We call such a functor a
weak realization functor
. Under reasonable conditions on the exact structure a weak realization functor induces a realization functor.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}\subseteq{\mathcal{T}}
be a full subcategory with an exact structure. We assume there exists a weak realization functor
\mathsf{F}\colon\operatorname{K}^{b}({\mathcal{C}})\to{\mathcal{T}}
. If any exact sequence
L\to M\to N
in
{\mathcal{C}}
fits into an exact triangle
L\to M\to N\to\Sigma L
in
{\mathcal{T}}
, then
\mathsf{F}
induces a realization functor such that the following diagram commutes

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{K}^{b}({\mathcal{C}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{D}^{b}({\mathcal{C}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{T}}\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\,.}}

In particular, this holds when
{\mathcal{C}}
is an admissible exact subcategory.

Proof.

It is enough to show that
\mathsf{F}
sends acyclic complexes to zero. For this it is enough to show that complexes of the form

(\cdots\to 0\to L\to M\to N\to 0\to\cdots)=\cone(\cone(L\to M)\to N)

are send to zero when
L\to M\to N
is an exact sequence in
{\mathcal{C}}
. But this holds by assumption.
∎

Remark 2.8
.

The above condition on
\mathsf{F}
, that any exact sequence in
{\mathcal{C}}
fits into an exact triangle in
{\mathcal{T}}
, means that
{\mathcal{C}}\to{\mathcal{T}}
is a
\delta
-functor
as defined in
[
Kel91
]
.

In the sequel we construct a weak realization functor. However, we do not know of a general criterion for the existence of a weak realization functor. Our construction requires some form of non-negativity. In particular, a weak realization functor may even exist for
{\mathcal{C}}={\mathcal{T}}
.

Example 2.9
.

Let
k
be a field and
{\mathcal{T}}=\operatorname{vect}(k)
, the category of finite-dimensional
k
-vector spaces with suspension
\Sigma=\id
. We can view
\operatorname{K}^{b}({\mathcal{T}})
as the category of finite-dimensional
\mathbb{Z}
-graded
k
-vector spaces
\operatorname{vect}^{\mathbb{Z}}(k)
with suspension the shift of the grading. The forgetful functor from graded
k
-vector spaces to ungraded
k
-vector spaces is a weak realization functor for
{\mathcal{C}}={\mathcal{T}}
. As
\operatorname{D}^{b}({\mathcal{T}})=\operatorname{K}^{b}({\mathcal{T}})
we obtain the realization functor

\operatorname{D}^{b}({\mathcal{T}})=\operatorname{vect}^{\mathbb{Z}}(k)\to\operatorname{vect}(k)={\mathcal{T}}\,.

2.10.
Existence

A
Frobenius category
is an exact category with enough projectives and with enough injectives and the projectives and injectives coincide. Let
{\mathcal{E}}
be a Frobenius category with
{\mathcal{P}}
the full subcategory of projective-injective objects. The ideal quotient
q\colon{\mathcal{E}}\to\underline{{\mathcal{E}}}
with respect to the morphisms factoring through
{\mathcal{P}}
has a natural triangulated structure by
[
Hap88
, I.2]
. A triangulated category is
algebraic
, if it is triangle equivalent to
\underline{{\mathcal{E}}}
for some Frobenius category; see
[
Kel06
, 3.6]
.

The key observation for the proof of
Theorem

1.1
is the following result, which is stated in
[
KV87
, 3.2]
.

Proposition 2.11
.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}
be a Frobenius category with
{\mathcal{P}}
the full subcategory of projective-injective objects and
q\colon{\mathcal{E}}\to\underline{{\mathcal{E}}}
the canonical functor. Let
{\mathcal{C}}\subseteq\underline{{\mathcal{E}}}
be a non-negative full subcategory and set
{\mathcal{B}}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}q^{-1}({\mathcal{C}})
. Then the functor
{\mathcal{B}}\to{\mathcal{C}}
induces an equivalence of triangulated categories

\operatorname{K}^{b}({\mathcal{B}})/\operatorname{K}^{b}({\mathcal{P}})\to\operatorname{K}^{b}({\mathcal{C}})\,.

Note, that in the equivalence connects the Verdier quotient of the homotopy category and the homotopy category of an ideal quotient. We postpone the proof to
Section

3
.

Remark 2.12
.

In the
Proposition
we show that the tilting subcategory
{\mathcal{B}}
in
\operatorname{K}^{b}({\mathcal{B}})
is send to the tilting subcategory
{\mathcal{C}}
under the Verdier quotient functor
\operatorname{K}^{b}({\mathcal{B}})\to\operatorname{K}^{b}({\mathcal{B}})/\operatorname{K}^{b}({\mathcal{P}})
. In general, Verdier quotients need not preserve tilting subcategories.

Without the assumption that the subcategory
{\mathcal{C}}
is non-negative the
Proposition

2.11
is false in general:

Example 2.13
.

Let
k
be a field and
A=k[x]/(x^{2})
. Then
{\mathcal{E}}=\operatorname{mod}(A)
is a Frobenius category and
\underline{{\mathcal{E}}}=\operatorname{mod}(k)
is the category of finite-dimensional vector spaces which is a triangulated category with
\Sigma=\id
. We show below that
\operatorname{K}^{b}(\operatorname{mod}(A))/\operatorname{K}^{b}(\operatorname{proj}(A))
is not equivalent to
\operatorname{K}^{b}(\operatorname{mod}(k))
, that is that the conclusion of
Proposition

2.11
does not hold for
{\mathcal{C}}=\underline{{\mathcal{E}}}
, which is not non-negative.
Observe first that
\operatorname{K}^{b}(\operatorname{mod}(k))=\operatorname{D}^{b}(\operatorname{mod}(k))
has no non-trivial thick subcategory. But on the other hand
\operatorname{K}^{b}(\operatorname{mod}(A))/\operatorname{K}^{b}(\operatorname{proj}(A))
admits a non-trivial Verdier quotient

\operatorname{K}^{b}(\operatorname{mod}(A))/\operatorname{K}^{b}(\operatorname{proj}(A))\to\operatorname{D}^{b}(\operatorname{mod}(A))/\operatorname{K}^{b}(\operatorname{proj}(A))\cong\underline{{\mathcal{E}}}\,;

see
[
Buc21
, Theorem 4.4.1]
. In particular, the kernel of this Verdier quotient is a non-trivial thick subcategory. Therefore they can not be triangle equivalent.

Proposition 2.14
.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}
be a Frobenius category with
{\mathcal{P}}
the full subcategory of projective-injective objects and
q\colon{\mathcal{E}}\to\underline{{\mathcal{E}}}
the canonical functor. Let
{\mathcal{C}}\subseteq\underline{{\mathcal{E}}}
be an admissible exact subcategory. Then there exists a weak realization functor
\operatorname{K}^{b}({\mathcal{C}})\to\underline{{\mathcal{E}}}
.

Proof.

Set
{\mathcal{B}}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}q^{-1}({\mathcal{C}})
. By
Proposition

2.11
there exists an equivalence of triangulated categories

\mathsf{F}\colon\operatorname{K}^{b}({\mathcal{B}})/\operatorname{K}^{b}({\mathcal{P}})\to\operatorname{K}^{b}({\mathcal{C}})\,.

There is also an equivalence

\mathsf{B}\colon\underline{{\mathcal{E}}}\to\operatorname{D}^{b}({\mathcal{E}})/\operatorname{K}^{b}({\mathcal{P}})\,;

this has been stated in
[
KV87
, Example 2.3]
with proofs provided in
[
IY20
, Corollary 2.2]
or
[
Kra22
, Proposition 4.4.18]
. Then the following composition involving the quasi-inverses of the above functors yields the claim

\operatorname{K}^{b}({\mathcal{C}})\xrightarrow{\mathsf{F}^{-1}}\operatorname{K}^{b}({\mathcal{B}})/\operatorname{K}^{b}({\mathcal{P}})\to\operatorname{K}^{b}({\mathcal{E}})/\operatorname{K}^{b}({\mathcal{P}})\xrightarrow{\mathsf{B}^{-1}}\underline{{\mathcal{E}}}\,.\qed

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

By
Proposition

2.14
there exists a weak realization functor, and it induces a realization functor by
Lemma

2.7
.
∎

From
Proposition

2.11
we can also deduce the following corollary.

Corollary 2.15
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be an admissible exact subcategory of
\underline{{\mathcal{E}}}
. Then
{\mathcal{B}}=q^{-1}({\mathcal{C}})
is extension-closed in
{\mathcal{E}}
and the functor
q\colon{\mathcal{B}}\to{\mathcal{C}}
sends exact sequences to exact triangles. In this case
q
induces a triangle equivalence

\operatorname{D}^{b}({\mathcal{B}})/\operatorname{K}^{b}({\mathcal{P}})\to\operatorname{D}^{b}({\mathcal{C}})\,.

Proof.

It is straightforward to check that
\operatorname{K}^{b}({\mathcal{P}})
and
\operatorname{Ac}^{b}({\mathcal{B}})
are Hom-orthogonal in
\operatorname{K}^{b}({\mathcal{B}})
. Then
\operatorname{Ac}^{b}({\mathcal{B}})
is a full subcategory of
\operatorname{K}^{b}({\mathcal{B}})/\operatorname{K}^{b}({\mathcal{P}})
by
[
KS90
, Proposition 1.6.10]
. So it is enough to show that the equivalence from
Proposition

2.11
restricts to an equivalence of the acyclic complexes
\operatorname{Ac}^{b}({\mathcal{B}})\to\operatorname{Ac}^{b}({\mathcal{C}})
.

The fully faithfullness of the restriction holds as
\operatorname{Ac}^{b}({\mathcal{B}})
is a full subcategory of
\operatorname{D}^{b}({\mathcal{B}})/\operatorname{K}^{b}({\mathcal{P}})
. Essentially surjectivity holds as

\Ext_{{\mathcal{B}}}^{1}(X,Y)\cong\Hom_{\underline{{\mathcal{E}}}}(X,\Sigma Y)\cong\Ext^{1}_{{\mathcal{C}}}(X,Y)

for any
X,Y\in{\mathcal{B}}
.
∎

2.16.
Fully faithfulness and equivalence

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be an admissible exact subcategory of a triangulated category
{\mathcal{T}}
. In this section we discuss when a realization functor

\mathsf{R}\colon\operatorname{D}^{b}({\mathcal{C}})\to{\mathcal{T}}

is fully faithful and even an equivalence. The realization functor
\mathsf{R}
induces natural group homomorphisms

\Phi_{n}(X,Y)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}(\Ext^{n}_{{\mathcal{C}}}(X,Y)\xrightarrow{\cong}\Hom_{\operatorname{D}^{b}({\mathcal{C}})}(X,\Sigma^{n}Y)\xrightarrow{\mathsf{R}}\Hom_{\mathcal{T}}(X,\Sigma^{n}Y))

for
X,Y\in{\mathcal{C}}
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Here
\Ext^{n}_{{\mathcal{C}}}
are the groups of Yoneda extensions for
n\geq 0
and we set
\Ext^{n}_{{\mathcal{C}}}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}0
for
n<0
. For the isomorphism see for example
[
Kra22
, Proposition 4.2.11]
. These natural morphisms have been considered in
[
CHZ19
, Lemma 2.11]
for hearts of t-structures and in
[
Pos11
, A.8]
for exact subcategories. The morphism
\Phi_{n}(X,Y)
is an isomorphism for
n<0
as
{\mathcal{C}}
is non-negative, for
n=0
as
{\mathcal{C}}
is full, and for
n=1
by
[
Pos11
, Corollary A.17]
. Further, for
n=2
it is a monomorphisms by
[
Pos11
, Corollary A.17]
. The following result appears in
[
BBD82
, Remarque 3.1.17]
and
[
CHZ19
, Lemma 2.11]
when
{\mathcal{C}}
is the heart of a bounded t-structure.

Lemma 2.17
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be an admissible exact subcategory of
{\mathcal{T}}
and let
\mathsf{R}
be a realization functor of
{\mathcal{C}}
. Then the following are equivalent

(1)

\mathsf{R}
is fully faithful;

(2)

\Phi_{n}(X,Y)
is an isomorphism for all
X,Y\in{\mathcal{C}}
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
;

(3)

\Phi_{n}(X,Y)
is surjective for all
X,Y\in{\mathcal{C}}
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
;

(4)

For every
X,Y\in{\mathcal{C}}
,
n\geq 1
and every morphism
f\colon X\to\Sigma^{n}Y
in
{\mathcal{T}}
there exists a
{\mathcal{C}}
-deflation
d\colon Z\to X
with
f\circ d=0
in
{\mathcal{T}}
; and

(4
op
)

For every
X,Y\in{\mathcal{C}}
,
n\geq 1
and every morphism
f\colon X\to\Sigma^{n}Y
in
{\mathcal{T}}
there exists an
{\mathcal{C}}
-inflation
i\colon Y\to W
such that
\Sigma^{n}i\circ f=0
in
{\mathcal{T}}
.

Proof.

The implication
Item

1

\implies

Item

2
is clear and the converse is an application of
dévissage
using
\operatorname{D}^{b}({\mathcal{C}})=\thick_{\operatorname{D}^{b}({\mathcal{C}})}({\mathcal{C}})
; see for example
[
Kra22
, Lemma 3.1.8]
.

The implication
Item

2

\implies

Item

3
is clear and the converse is shown in
[
Pos11
, Corollary A.17]
.

A standard construction shows that
Item

2
is equivalent to

(5)

Every
f\colon X\to\Sigma^{n}Y
in
{\mathcal{T}}
with
X,Y\in{\mathcal{C}}
and
n\geq 1
decomposes as

X=X_{0}\to\Sigma X_{1}\to\Sigma^{2}X_{2}\to\cdots\to\Sigma^{n}X_{n}=\Sigma^{n}Y

for
X_{i}\in{\mathcal{C}}
;

see for example
[
CHZ19
, Lemma 2.1]
for the abelian case. Moreover, by induction over
n
this is also equivalent to

(6)

Every
f\colon X\to\Sigma^{n}Y
in
{\mathcal{T}}
with
X,Y\in{\mathcal{C}}
and
n\geq 1
decomposes as
X\to\Sigma U\to\Sigma^{n}Y
for some
U\in{\mathcal{C}}
.

So it is enough to show that
Item

4
and
Item

6
are equivalent. For the backward direction it is enough to observe that any morphism
X\to\Sigma U
in
{\mathcal{T}}
with
X,U\in{\mathcal{C}}
induces an exact sequence
U\to Z\xrightarrow{d}X
in
{\mathcal{C}}
. For the forward direction let
f\colon X\to\Sigma^{n}Y
be a morphism in
{\mathcal{T}}
with
X,Y\in{\mathcal{C}}
and
n\geq 1
. Then there exists a deflation
d\colon Z\to X
such that
f\circ d=0
. We complete
d
to an exact sequence
U\to Z\xrightarrow{d}X
in
{\mathcal{C}}
. Then
f
factors through the induced morphism
X\to\Sigma U
. This shows
Item

6
.

The equivalence of
Item

2
and
Item

(4op)
holds by an analogous argument.
∎

Remark 2.18
.

The previous Lemma can be strengthened to yield an explicit description of the image of
\Phi_{n}(X,Y)
. That is, the subgroup
\im(\Phi_{n}(X,Y))
is the set of all morphisms
f\colon X\to\Sigma^{n}Y
with
X,Y\in{\mathcal{C}}
such that there exists a
{\mathcal{C}}
-deflation
d\colon Z\to X
such that
f\circ d=0
.

For a subcategory
{\mathcal{C}}
of a triangulated category
{\mathcal{T}}
we denote by
\thick_{\mathcal{T}}({\mathcal{C}})
the smallest thick subcategory of
{\mathcal{T}}
that contains
{\mathcal{C}}
.

Corollary 2.19
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be an admissible exact subcategory of
{\mathcal{T}}
. A realization functor of
{\mathcal{C}}
is an equivalence of triangulated categories if and only if it is fully faithful and
\thick_{{\mathcal{T}}}({\mathcal{C}})={\mathcal{T}}
. ∎

Example 2.20
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be a fully exact subcategory of
{\mathcal{E}}
. Then the induced functor
\mathsf{F}\colon\operatorname{D}^{b}({\mathcal{C}})\to\operatorname{D}^{b}({\mathcal{E}})
is a realization functor for
{\mathcal{C}}\subseteq\operatorname{D}^{b}({\mathcal{E}})
. The functor
\mathsf{F}
is fully faithful if and only if the inclusion
{\mathcal{C}}\subseteq{\mathcal{E}}
induces isomorphism on the Ext-groups. For example, the latter condition is satisfied by resolving subcategories; see
[
AR91
, Section 2]
and also
[
HKvR22
, Definition 5.1]
.

The functor
\mathsf{F}
is an equivalence if additionally
{\mathcal{E}}
is the smallest additively-closed subcategory closed under the 2-out-of-three property containing
{\mathcal{C}}
. For example, this is satisfied by finitely resolving subcategories; cf.
[
HvR20
, Theorem 3.11(2)]
.

3.
Proof of the main
Proposition

For clarity we use different notations for the suspension in the stable category and the homotopy category. We write
\Sigma
for the suspension or shift functor in
\underline{{\mathcal{E}}}
where
{\mathcal{E}}
is a Frobenius exact category. By construction, we have
q(\Omega^{n}X)=\Sigma^{-n}X
for any
X\in{\mathcal{E}}
where
\Omega
is the syzygy functor. On the other hand, for an additive category
{\mathcal{A}}
we write
\operatorname{Ch}({\mathcal{A}})
for the category of chain complexes. In
\operatorname{Ch}({\mathcal{A}})
and the homotopy category
\operatorname{K}({\mathcal{A}})
, we denote the degree
n
shift of a complex
X
by
X[n]
; this is the complex given by

X[n]^{\ell}=X^{\ell+n}\quad\text{and}\quad d_{X[n]}=(-1)^{n}d_{X}\,.

For a
map of complexes

f\colon X\to Y
we write

\partial(f)=d^{Y}f-f[-1]d^{X}\colon X\to Y[-1]\,.

The map
f
is a
chain map
if and only if
\partial(f)=0
. Note, that a map of complexes need not commute with the differential, while a chain map does.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}
be a Frobenius exact category with
{\mathcal{P}}
the full subcategory of projective-injective objects and
q\colon{\mathcal{E}}\to\underline{{\mathcal{E}}}
the canonical functor. Let
{\mathcal{C}}\subseteq\underline{{\mathcal{E}}}
be a non-negative full subcategory and set

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}
be a Frobenius exact category with
{\mathcal{P}}
the full subcategory of projective-injective objects and
q\colon{\mathcal{E}}\to\underline{{\mathcal{E}}}
the canonical functor. Let
X\in\operatorname{K}^{b}({\mathcal{E}})
. If
q(X)=0
in
\operatorname{K}^{b}(\underline{{\mathcal{E}}})
, then
X\in\operatorname{K}^{b}({\mathcal{P}})
.

Proof.

It is enough to show the claim for a complex of the form

X=(\cdots\to 0\to X^{0}\xrightarrow{d^{0}}X^{1}\xrightarrow{d^{1}}X^{2}\to\cdots\to X^{n-1}\xrightarrow{d^{n-1}}X^{n}\to 0\to\cdots)

for any
n\geq 0
. We use induction on
n
.

For
n=0
, the assumption
q(X)=0
implies
q(X^{0})=0
. Hence
X^{0}\in{\mathcal{P}}
.

Let
n\geq 1
. As
q(X)=0
, the morphism
q(d^{0})
is a split monomorphism in
\underline{{\mathcal{E}}}
and there exists a morphism
s\colon X^{1}\to X^{0}
such that
sd^{0}-\id_{X^{0}}=ba
for some morphisms
X^{0}\xrightarrow{a}P\xrightarrow{b}X^{0}
with
P\in{\mathcal{P}}
. We view
P
as a complex concentrated in degree zero and set

X^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\cone(\Sigma^{-1}a)=(\cdots\to 0\to X^{0}\xrightarrow{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}d^{0}\\
a\end{smallmatrix}\right)}X^{1}\oplus P\xrightarrow{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}d^{1}&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)}X^{2}\xrightarrow{d^{2}}X^{3}\to\cdots)\,.

Since
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}s&-b\end{smallmatrix}\right)\circ\left(\begin{smallmatrix}d^{0}\\
a\end{smallmatrix}\right)=\id_{X^{0}}
, the zero differential of
X^{\prime}
is a split monomorphism in
{\mathcal{E}}
. Therefore, in
\operatorname{K}^{b}({\mathcal{E}})
, the complex
X^{\prime}
is isomorphic to a complex
Y
concentrated between degrees 1 and
n
. In
\operatorname{K}^{b}(\underline{{\mathcal{E}}})
we have
q(Y)\cong q(X^{\prime})\cong q(X)=0
. By induction hypothesis we have
X^{\prime}\cong Y\in\operatorname{K}^{b}({\mathcal{P}})
. By construction there is an exact triangle
X^{\prime}\to P\to X\to\Sigma X^{\prime}
, and as
X^{\prime},P\in\operatorname{K}^{b}({\mathcal{P}})
, so is
X
.
∎

.

As

in

, the functor

We claim that

The functor

is full by

. By

, whenever

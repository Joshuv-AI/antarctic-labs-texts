Hofer-Like Geometry Revisited
Stéphane Tchuiaga

Abstract
. We prove a semidirect-product formula for the Hofer-like norm on the identity component G ω ​ ( M ) G_{\omega}(M) of the symplectic diffeomorphism group, expressing it as an infimum over the harmonic locus plus a Hamiltonian residue. Specialising to zero flux yields the geometric criterion, for the inclusion of the Hamiltonian group, endowed with the Hofer norm, into G ω ​ ( M ) G_{\omega}(M) , endowed with the Hofer-like norm, to be an isometric embedding. The criterion is verified in two principal cases: on closed symplectic manifolds admitting a compatible metric of non-negative Ricci curvature for which the harmonic 1 1 -forms are integral and the harmonic flows have period 1 1 , covering flat tori and Calabi-Yau manifolds; and on a non-vacuous family of harmonic diffeomorphisms on T 2 T^{2} equipped with a non-flat compatible metric. The two norms induce the same topology on the Hamiltonian group, a result due to Buss-Leclercq for which we give a self-contained proof; under an explicit boundedness hypothesis (H K ) on the two-parameter vector-field family attached to zero-flux paths, the inclusion is moreover bi-Lipschitz with distortion at most 1 + 3 ​ L 0 ​ K 1+3L_{0}K . We give self-contained proofs of the non-degeneracy, the positivity of the displacement energy, and the C 0 C^{0} -stability of limits, three fundamental results of Hofer-like geometry. 1.
Introduction

Hofer’s geometry defines a Finsler-type geometry on the infinite-dimensional
group of Hamiltonian diffeomorphisms
\Ham(M,\omega)
, and is considered
intrinsic to this group
[
6
]
. It measures the minimal energy required
to transform the identity into a given Hamiltonian diffeomorphism via a path
consisting entirely of Hamiltonian diffeomorphisms.

Beyond Hofer’s original metric, Hofer-like metrics have emerged as variations
and extensions
[
1
,
13
]
. These metrics, while also intrinsic to the
identity component
G_{\omega}(M)
of the group of symplectic
diffeomorphisms, extend the notion of size or length to symplectic
isotopies that are not necessarily Hamiltonian, and they incorporate
information about the flux of the isotopy. The resulting geometry therefore
sees a larger class of paths within
G_{\omega}(M)
, at the price of being
weaker than the original Hofer geometry on
\Ham(M,\omega)
. Other attempts to define bi-invariant norms on
G_{\omega}(M)
include:

(i)

Han’s norm
[
5
]
: for a fixed
C>0
, set

\lVert\psi\rVert_{C}:=\min\{\lVert\psi\rVert_{H},C\}
if

\psi\in\Ham(M,\omega)
, and
\lVert\psi\rVert_{C}:=C
otherwise.

(ii)

Lalonde-Polterovich’s norm
[
9
]
: for a fixed

\beta>0
, set

\lVert\psi\rVert_{\beta}:=\sup\{\lVert[\psi,f]\rVert_{H}\mid f\in\Ham(M,\omega),\ \lVert f\rVert_{H}\leq\beta\},

where
[\psi,f]=\psi\circ f\circ\psi^{-1}\circ f^{-1}
and

\lVert\cdot\rVert_{H}
denotes the Hofer norm.

Both constructions induce metrics of finite diameter on
\Ham(M,\omega)
,
in contrast with the infinite diameter of the Hofer metric itself.
Motivated by the search for a genuine generalization that retains the
essential features of Hofer’s geometry while extending to

G_{\omega}(M)
, Banyaga incorporated the flux into the definition of the
Hofer-like length.

The problem

Banyaga’s Hofer-like norm, restricted to
\Ham(M,\omega)
, measures the
minimal Hofer-like length among all symplectic isotopies from the identity
to a given Hamiltonian diffeomorphism, including those that are not
Hamiltonian. A priori, it is conceivable that such a non-Hamiltonian
isotopy could be strictly shorter than any Hamiltonian one. Concretely,
given
\phi\in\Ham(M,\omega)
and a smooth loop
\Xi
of
symplectomorphisms based at
\id
with non-trivial flux

\widetilde{\Flux}(\Xi)\neq 0
, the isotopy
\Psi:=\Xi\circ\Phi

built from a Hamiltonian isotopy
\Phi
from
\id
to
\phi
still
connects
\id
to
\phi
, but satisfies

\widetilde{\Flux}(\Psi)=\widetilde{\Flux}(\Xi)\neq 0
, so it is not
Hamiltonian. If the Hofer-like length of such a
\Psi
could be
arbitrarily small, the Hofer-like norm of
\phi
would be strictly
smaller than the Hofer norm, and the two geometries would genuinely
differ. A fundamental fact, due to Banyaga
[
2
]
, is that the flux
homomorphism fits into the exact sequence

where

with the torus

Strategy: orthogonal decomposition

A recurring strategy in geometry and analysis for understanding a
complicated object is to decompose it into simpler factors.
The Cheeger–Gromoll splitting theorem writes a complete Riemannian
manifold of non-negative Ricci curvature containing a line as a metric
product
\mathbb{R}\times N

[
20
]
; the de Rham theorem
decomposes a simply connected Riemannian manifold into irreducible
factors
[
21
]
; the Iwasawa decomposition
G=KAN
splits a
semisimple Lie group into compact, abelian, and nilpotent parts

[
22
]
; the polar decomposition
T=U\lvert T\rvert
separates the
direction and the magnitude of a bounded operator
[
23
]
. In
each case, a structural invariant is expressed as a sum or product of
contributions from the factors, which can then be studied independently.

The present paper follows this pattern for the Hofer-like norm on

G_{\omega}(M)
. The exact sequence (
1.1
) suggests a
splitting of
G_{\omega}(M)
into a Hamiltonian factor and a harmonic
factor. At the level of the group, the natural candidate for the
harmonic factor is the set
\mathcal{H}
of harmonic diffeomorphisms, i.e.
the time-one maps of purely harmonic isotopies. Banyaga’s decomposition
theorem then gives, for every
\phi\in G_{\omega}(M)
with flux class

\gamma=\Flux(\phi)
, a factorization

\phi=\rho\,h,\qquad\rho\in\mathcal{H}(\gamma),\quad h\in\Ham(M,\omega).

This factorization, however, is not unique: two harmonic
representatives
\rho,\rho^{\prime}
of the same flux class differ by an
element of the zero-flux harmonic locus

\mathcal{H}(0)=\Ham(M,\omega)\cap\mathcal{H}
, which is non-trivial in general
(as we recall in Remark
3
below).
Even when the group factorization is unique, the Hofer-like length of a
path is an infimum over paths, not a sum over group factors, so the
lengths of
\rho
and
h
do not add in general. The semidirect-product formula of Theorem
1
is the exact form
of the decomposition that survives these obstructions. It replaces the
naive split of the norm by an infimum over the ambiguity in the choice
of harmonic factor, and reads

\lVert\phi\rVert_{HL}=\inf_{\rho\in\mathcal{H}(\gamma)}\bigl[L(\rho)+\lVert\phi\,\rho^{-1}\rVert_{H}\bigr],\qquad\gamma=\Flux(\phi),

where
L(\rho)
is the infimum of the Hofer-like length over harmonic
isotopies from
\id
to
\rho
. When
\mathcal{H}(0)=\{\id\}
, the
infimum collapses to a single term, and the decomposition is exact; when

\mathcal{H}(0)
is non-trivial, the infimum measures the failure of
orthogonality and provides the natural invariant of the interaction
between the Hamiltonian and harmonic directions.

Main results

The first result is the semidirect-product formula itself, which holds
on every closed symplectic manifold and requires no additional
hypothesis.

Theorem 1

(Semidirect-product formula)
.

For every
\phi\in G_{\omega}(M)
with flux class

\gamma=\Flux(\phi)
, the Hofer-like norm satisfies

\lVert\phi\rVert_{HL}\;=\;\inf_{\rho\in\mathcal{H}(\gamma)}\bigl[L(\rho)+\lVert\phi\,\rho^{-1}\rVert_{H}\bigr],

where
\mathcal{H}(\gamma)
is the locus of harmonic diffeomorphisms with
flux
\gamma
.

Specializing to
\gamma=0
reduces the semidirect-product formula to
an infimum over the zero-flux harmonic locus
\mathcal{H}(0)
. This yields
the following geometric criterion for the two norms to agree on

\Ham(M,\omega)
.

Theorem 2

(Isometry criterion)
.

Let
(M,\omega,g)
be a closed symplectic manifold. The following are
equivalent:

(i)

L(\rho)\geq\lVert\rho\rVert_{H}
for every
\rho\in\mathcal{H}(0)
;

(ii)

the inclusion

\bigl(\Ham(M,\omega),\lVert\cdot\rVert_{H}\bigr)\hookrightarrow\bigl(G_{\omega}(M),\lVert\cdot\rVert_{HL}\bigr),

is an isometric embedding, i.e.
\lVert\phi\rVert_{HL}=\lVert\phi\rVert_{H}

for every
\phi\in\Ham(M,\omega)
.

A first family of examples where the criterion is satisfied is given by
the flat tori, on which the zero-flux harmonic locus reduces to the
identity.

Corollary 1

(Flat tori)
.

On
(T^{2k},\omega_{\mathrm{flat}},g_{\mathrm{flat}})
, the two norms
coincide:

\lVert\phi\rVert_{HL}=\lVert\phi\rVert_{H},

for every
\phi\in\Ham(T^{2k},\omega_{\mathrm{flat}})
.

Beyond the isometry criterion, we obtain a quantitative bi-Lipschitz
bound under an explicit boundedness hypothesis on the two-parameter
vector-field family attached to zero-flux paths.

Theorem 3

(Conditional bi-Lipschitz bound)
.

Let
(M,\omega,g)
be a closed symplectic manifold and assume the
following.

(H
K
)

There exists

K=K(M,\omega,g)<+\infty
such that for every

\phi\in\Ham(M,\omega)
and every
\Xi\in\mathcal{Z}_{\phi}
with

l_{HL}(\Xi)\leq 2\lVert\phi\rVert_{H}
, the two-parameter family

V^{\Xi}_{s,t}
attached to the harmonic part of
\Xi
satisfies

\sup_{(s,t,x)\in[0,1]^{2}\times M}\lVert V^{\Xi}_{s,t}(x)\rVert_{g}\leq K.

Then, the identity map

\bigl(\Ham(M,\omega),\lVert\cdot\rVert_{H}\bigr)\longrightarrow\bigl(\Ham(M,\omega),\lVert\cdot\rVert_{HL}\bigr),

is bi-Lipschitz with distortion at most
1+3L_{0}K
, where
L_{0}
is
the constant of (
2.4
). In particular, for every

\phi\in\Ham(M,\omega)
,

(1+3L_{0}K)^{-1}\lVert\phi\rVert_{HL}\;\leq\;\lVert\phi\rVert_{H}\;\leq\;(1+3L_{0}K)\lVert\phi\rVert_{HL}.

The distortion constant
C_{\mathrm{univ}}:=1+3L_{0}K
measures the
maximal distortion between the Hofer norm and the Hofer-like energy on
the Hamiltonian subgroup. It equals
1
precisely when the harmonic
directions do not shorten Hofer distances, i.e. when

L(\rho)\geq\lVert\rho\rVert_{H}
for every
\rho\in\mathcal{H}(0)
, and this is
equivalent by Theorem
2
to the isometry of the inclusion.

Organization of the paper

Remark 1

(Non-triviality of
\mathcal{H}(0)
)
.

Earlier formulations of the problem implicitly assumed

\mathcal{H}(0)=\{\id\}
, i.e.
\Ham(M,\omega)\cap\mathcal{H}=\{\id\}
. This
is not true in general; a counterexample is given in
Remark
3
below. Consequently, the
formulations relying on
\mathcal{H}(0)=\{\id\}
are replaced throughout by
the correct condition

L(\rho)\geq\lVert\rho\rVert_{H}

for every
\rho\in\mathcal{H}(0)
,
which reduces to
\mathcal{H}(0)=\{\id\}
only in the special case where
the intersection is trivial.

Preliminaries

Throughout this paper,
(M,\omega)
denotes a compact, connected symplectic manifold of dimension
2n
without boundary (i.e., closed).

Definition 1
.

A symplectic form on a smooth manifold
M
of dimension
2n
is a smooth 2-form
\omega
that is closed,
d\omega=0
and non-degenerate: For each
p\in M
, the bilinear form
\omega_{p}\colon T_{p}M\times T_{p}M\to\mathbb{R}
is non-degenerate.

A symplectic manifold is a pair
(M,\omega)
. The non-degeneracy is equivalent to,
\omega^{n}=\omega\wedge\cdots\wedge\omega
(
n
times) is a volume form provided
\dim M=2n
.

Definition 2
.

A diffeomorphism
\phi\colon M\to M
is a symplectomorphism if
\phi^{*}\omega=\omega
.

We denote the group of symplectomorphisms by
\Symp(M,\omega)
.

Definition 3
.

A smooth family of diffeomorphisms
\{\phi_{t}\}_{t\in[0,1]}
is a
symplectic isotopy
if each
\phi_{t}
is a symplectomorphism. An isotopy
\{\phi_{t}\}
with
\phi_{0}=\id
is symplectic iff its generating vector field
X_{t}=\dot{\phi}_{t}\circ\phi_{t}^{-1}
satisfies
\mathcal{L}_{X_{t}}\omega=0
for all
t
.

We denote by
\Iso(M,\omega)
the set of symplectic isotopies starting at the identity. Let
G_{\omega}(M)=\{\phi_{1}\mid\{\phi_{t}\}\in\Iso(M,\omega)\}
be the group of time-one maps of such isotopies. For compact
M
,
G_{\omega}(M)
is the identity component of
\Symp(M,\omega)
.

Definition 4
.

A symplectic isotopy
\{\psi_{t}\}
is a
Hamiltonian isotopy
if its generating vector field
Y_{t}
is Hamiltonian for each
t
, i.e., there exists a smooth function
H_{t}\colon M\to\mathbb{R}
(the Hamiltonian) such that
\iota_{Y_{t}}\omega=dH_{t}
.

Definition 5
.

A generating Hamiltonian
H=\{H_{t}\}
is
normalized
if
\int_{M}H_{t}\,\omega^{n}=0
for all
t
. This makes
H_{t}
unique for a given Hamiltonian vector field.

Let
\Ham(M,\omega)=\{\psi_{1}\mid\{\psi_{t}\}\text{ is a Hamiltonian isotopy starting at }\id\}
.
\Ham(M,\omega)
is a normal subgroup of
G_{\omega}(M)
. For
\phi\in G_{\omega}(M)
, let
\Iso(\phi)_{\omega}=\{\Phi=\{\phi_{t}\}\in\Iso(M,\omega)\mid\phi_{1}=\phi\}
be the set of symplectic isotopies from
\id
to
\phi
.

2.1.
Generator of symplectic isotopies via Hodge decomposition

Let
(M,\omega,g)
be a closed symplectic manifold with a Riemannian metric
g
. For a symplectic isotopy
\Phi=\{\phi_{t}\}
with generating vector field
X_{t}
, the 1-form
\alpha_{t}=\iota_{X_{t}}\omega
is closed. By Hodge theory,

has a unique decomposition:

\alpha_{t}=dU_{t}^{\Phi}+\mathcal{H}_{t}^{\Phi},

where
\mathcal{H}_{t}^{\Phi}\in\mathcal{H}^{1}(M,g)
is the unique harmonic 1-form in the cohomology class
[\alpha_{t}]
, and
dU_{t}^{\Phi}
is exact. We normalize

by
\int_{M}U_{t}^{\Phi}\,\omega^{n}=0
. Let
U^{\Phi}=\{U_{t}^{\Phi}\}
,
\mathcal{H}^{\Phi}=\{\mathcal{H}_{t}^{\Phi}\}
. This gives a bijection
[
14
]

\Iso(M,\omega)\longleftrightarrow\mathcal{N}([0,1]\times M,\mathbb{R})\times\mathcal{H}^{1}([0,1]\times M),

where
\mathcal{N}([0,1]\times M,\mathbb{R})
is the space of smooth normalized Hamiltonians and
\mathcal{H}^{1}([0,1]\times M)
the space of smooth families of harmonic 1-forms.

Definition 6
.

For
\Phi\in\Iso(M,\omega)
, the pair
(U^{\Phi},\mathcal{H}^{\Phi})
is its generator, denoted
\Gen(\Phi)=(U^{\Phi},\mathcal{H}^{\Phi})
.

Definition 7
.

A symplectic isotopy
\Phi
is Hamiltonian if and only if
\Gen(\Phi)=(U^{\Phi},0)
.

Definition 8
.

A symplectic isotopy
\Phi
is harmonic if and only if
\Gen(\Phi)=(0,\mathcal{H}^{\Phi})
.

Concatenation of isotopies

Let

be a smooth, increasing function with

f(t)=1

.

The paths
\Psi\ast_{l}\Phi

We construct the homotopy explicitly using separate reparameterizations for each path. Define two continuous, non-decreasing functions

a_{0}(t)=\begin{cases}0,&0\leq t\leq 1/2,\\
\tau(t),&1/2\leq t\leq 1;\end{cases}\quad\text{and}\quad b_{0}(t)=\begin{cases}\lambda(t),&0\leq t\leq 1/2,\\
1,&1/2\leq t\leq 1.\end{cases}

Since
\tau(1/2)=0
and
\lambda(1/2)=1

and

are continuous on

These functions are continuous in both

the Lie derivative of a pushforward gives

\frac{d}{du}\Bigl[\bigl(\exp(-uX_{s})\bigr)_{*}X_{s+u}\Bigr]=\bigl(\exp(-uX_{s})\bigr)_{*}\bigl(\partial_{t}X_{s+u}+[X_{s},X_{s+u}]\bigr).

Evaluating at
u=0
, we obtain

\dot{W}_{0}=\partial_{t}X_{s}+[X_{s},X_{s}]=\partial_{t}X_{s}
.

Step 3: the flow of
W_{u}
at order
u^{2}
.
The
isotopy
\delta_{u}:=\exp(-uX_{s})\circ D_{s,u}
satisfies

\delta_{0}=\id
and

\dot{\delta}_{u}=W_{u}\circ\delta_{u}.

Since
W_{0}=0
and
\dot{W}_{0}=\partial_{t}X_{s}
, we have

W_{u}=u\,\partial_{t}X_{s}+O(u^{2})
, so integrating the ODE for

\delta_{u}
,

\delta_{u}=\id+\int_{0}^{u}v\,\partial_{t}X_{s}\,dv+O(u^{3})=\id+\tfrac{u^{2}}{2}\partial_{t}X_{s}+O(u^{3}).

Step 4: conclusion.
Composing
\delta_{u}
with

\exp(uX_{s})
on the left and using that

(\exp(uX_{s}))_{*}\partial_{t}X_{s}=\partial_{t}X_{s}+O(u)
,

D_{s,u}=\exp(uX_{s})+\tfrac{u^{2}}{2}\partial_{t}X_{s}+O(u^{3}),

which is (
2.3
).
∎

Remark 2

(Where the bracket enters)
.

The Lie bracket
[X_{s},X_{s+v}]
appears in the expansion only at
order
u^{3}
, not at order
u^{2}
. Explicitly, the next correction
in (
2.3
) is

\tfrac{u^{3}}{6}\Bigl(\partial_{t}^{2}X_{s}-[X_{s},\partial_{t}X_{s}]\Bigr)+O(u^{4}),

so commutativity of the family
\{X_{t}\}
(i.e.
[X_{s},X_{t}]=0
for all
s,t
) is a stronger condition than
second-order agreement of
D_{s,u}
with the frozen flow. The two
conditions coincide only when combined with
\partial_{t}^{2}X_{s}=0
.

Lemma 1

(Time-one map of a commuting flow)
.

Let
M
be a smooth manifold and let

\{X_{t}\}_{t\in[0,1]}
be a smooth family of vector fields satisfying

[X_{s},X_{t}]=0

for all
s,t\in[0,1]
.
Then the flow
\Phi_{t}
of
X_{t}
, defined by

\frac{d}{dt}\Phi_{t}=X_{t}\circ\Phi_{t},
with
\Phi_{0}=\id,

satisfies

\Phi_{1}=\exp\Bigl(\int_{0}^{1}X_{t}\,dt\Bigr),
and the frozen flow at time
s
is
\psi^{s}_{u}=\exp(uX_{s})
.

Proof.

Define the curve in the Lie algebra of vector fields

Y(t):=\int_{0}^{t}X_{s}\,ds,

so that
Y(0)=0
and
Y^{\prime}(t)=X_{t}
. Consider

\sigma(t):=\exp(Y(t))
, the exponential of
Y(t)
in the
diffeomorphism group. Thus,
\sigma(0)=\exp(0)=\id
. Now, we compute the derivative of
\sigma
.
The derivative of the exponential map is

\frac{d}{dt}\exp\bigl(Y(t)\bigr)=d\exp_{Y(t)}\bigl(Y^{\prime}(t)\bigr)\cdot\exp\bigl(Y(t)\bigr),

with
d\exp_{Y}(Z)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{1}{(n+1)!}(\operatorname{ad}_{Y})^{n}(Z).

Here
Y^{\prime}(t)=X_{t}
, and

\operatorname{ad}_{Y(t)}(X_{t})=[Y(t),X_{t}]=\int_{0}^{t}[X_{s},X_{t}]\,ds=0,

by the commutativity hypothesis. Hence

d\exp_{Y(t)}(X_{t})=X_{t}
, and

\frac{d}{dt}\exp\bigl(Y(t)\bigr)=X_{t}\circ\exp\bigl(Y(t)\bigr).

Thus
\sigma
solves the same ODE as
\Phi
with the same initial
condition
\sigma(0)=\phi_{0}=\id
. By uniqueness of solutions to the ODE

\dot{\sigma}_{t}=X_{t}\circ\sigma_{t}
, we have
\sigma(t)=\phi_{t}
for
all
t\in[0,1]
. Evaluating at
t=1
,

\phi_{1}=\exp\bigl(Y(1)\bigr)=\exp\Bigl(\int_{0}^{1}X_{t}\,dt\Bigr).\qed

Proposition 3

(Magnus expansion)
.

Let
\Phi=\{\phi_{t}\}_{t\in[0,1]}
be a smooth isotopy with
generating field
X_{t}
and
\phi_{0}=\id
. Then,

\phi_{1}=\exp\bigl(\Omega_{1}+\Omega_{2}+\Omega_{3}+\cdots\bigr),

where the first three terms of the Magnus expansion are

\displaystyle\Omega_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}X_{t_{1}}\,dt_{1},

\displaystyle\Omega_{2}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\!\!\int_{0}^{t_{1}}[X_{t_{1}},X_{t_{2}}]\,dt_{2}\,dt_{1},

\displaystyle\Omega_{3}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{6}\int_{0}^{1}\!\!\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\!\!\int_{0}^{t_{2}}\Bigl([X_{t_{1}},[X_{t_{2}},X_{t_{3}}]]+[X_{t_{3}},[X_{t_{2}},X_{t_{1}}]]\Bigr)\,dt_{3}\,dt_{2}\,dt_{1},

and
\Omega_{k}
for
k\geq 4
involves higher-order nested
commutators with coefficients
1/k
and iterated integrals over
the simplex
0<t_{k}<\cdots<t_{1}<1
. The expansion terminates at

\Omega_{1}
, i.e.
\Omega_{k}=0
for every
k\geq 2
, if and only
if
[X_{s},X_{t}]=0
for all
s,t\in[0,1]
; this is the case in
which the frozen flow
\exp(X_{s})
reproduces the isotopy up to
reparametrisation. More generally, the expansion terminates at some
finite order if and only if the Lie algebra generated by

\{X_{t}\}_{t\in[0,1]}
is nilpotent, and in that case the frozen
flow is only a first-order approximation unless

[X_{s},X_{t}]=0
.

Proof.

This is the classical Magnus expansion for the linear
initial-value problem
\dot{Y}=A(t)Y
,
Y(0)=\id
, applied to

A(t)=X_{t}
; see Magnus
[
24
]
or Iserles–Munthe-Kaas–
Nørsett–Zanna
[
25
]
, §3.1. The identification of
the flow
\dot{\phi}_{t}=X_{t}\circ\phi_{t}
with the linear problem is
standard, and the frozen flow at time
s
is the truncation

\psi^{s}_{1}=\exp(X_{s})
obtained by replacing
X_{t}
with the
constant field
X_{s}
.
∎

Corollary 2

(When the two flows coincide)
.

The isotopy
\Phi
coincides with the frozen flow
\Psi^{s}
at
time
1
, i.e.
\phi_{1}=\psi^{s}_{1}
, if and only if

X_{t}=X_{s}

for almost every
t\in[0,1].

Proof.

The condition
\phi_{1}=\psi^{s}_{1}
is equivalent to

\int_{0}^{1}X_{t}\,dt=X_{s}
together with the vanishing of all Magnus
corrections
\Omega_{k}
,
k\geq 2
. The vanishing of
\Omega_{2}

requires
[X_{t},X_{s}]=0
for all
t
, and the vanishing of

\Omega_{1}-\int_{0}^{1}X_{t}\,dt\big|_{X_{t}\to X_{s}}=\int_{0}^{1}(X_{t}-X_{s})\,dt

requires
X_{t}=X_{s}
almost everywhere, which is the stated
condition.
∎

2.4.
Norms of 1-forms and harmonic vector fields

Let
(M,\omega,g)
be compact with metric
g
. Let
\mathcal{H}^{1}(M,g)
be the space of harmonic 1-forms. The pointwise norm
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{g}^{*}
on
T^{*}_{x}M
induces the uniform supremum norm
\lvert\alpha\rvert_{0}=\sup_{x\in M}\lVert\alpha_{x}\rVert_{g}^{*}
. The
L^{2}
-Hodge norm is
\lVert\alpha\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}=\int_{M}\lVert\alpha_{x}\rVert_{g}^{*2}\,d\vol_{g}
. For a class
[\alpha]\in H^{1}_{dR}(M,\mathbb{R})
, its
L^{2}
-Hodge norm is the
L^{2}
-norm of its unique harmonic representative. On the finite-dimensional space
\mathcal{H}^{1}(M,g)
, the norms
\lvert\cdot\rvert_{0}
and
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{L^{2}}
are equivalent: there exist constants
L_{0},L_{1}>0
such that for any
\alpha\in\mathcal{H}^{1}(M,g)
,

(2.4)

L_{1}\lVert\alpha\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq\lvert\alpha\rvert_{0}\leq L_{0}\lVert\alpha\rVert_{L^{2}}.

These constants depend on the metric and are fixed throughout the paper.

Now we return to symplectic geometry.

Definition 10
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
denote the set of all
harmonic diffeomorphisms
(time-one maps of purely harmonic isotopies). For
\gamma\in H^{1}_{dR}(M,\mathbb{R})/\Gamma_{\omega}
, define the
harmonic locus
of
\gamma
by

\mathcal{H}(\gamma)\;:=\;\bigl\{\rho\in G_{\omega}(M)\;\bigm|\;\rho\text{ is the time-one map of a harmonic isotopy with }\Flux(\rho)=\gamma\bigr\}.

For
\rho\in\mathcal{H}(\gamma)
, define the
harmonic energy
of
\rho
by

L(\rho)\;:=\;\inf\bigl\{\,l_{HL}(\Psi)\;\bigm|\;\Psi\text{ harmonic isotopy from }\id\text{ to }\rho\,\bigr\}.

Notice that because
\ker\Flux=\Ham(M,\omega)
, the zero-flux harmonic locus
\mathcal{H}(0)
is exactly the intersection of the Hamiltonian subgroup and the set of harmonic diffeomorphisms:

\mathcal{H}(0)=\Ham(M,\omega)\cap\mathcal{H}.

Since the Hodge decomposition of a harmonic isotopy has vanishing Hamiltonian part,
l_{HL}(\Psi)=\int_{0}^{1}\lVert\mathcal{H}_{t}^{\Psi}\rVert_{L^{2}}\,dt
for any harmonic isotopy
\Psi
, so
L(\rho)
depends only on the path component of
\rho
inside
\mathcal{H}(\gamma)
.

Remark 3

(The intersection
\Ham\cap\mathcal{H}
is not trivial in general)
.

One might intuitively expect that a diffeomorphism cannot be simultaneously Hamiltonian and harmonic unless it is the identity, i.e.,
\mathcal{H}(0)=\{\id\}
. However, this is false in general. A counterexample is provided by the torus
T^{2}=\mathbb{R}^{2}/\mathbb{Z}^{2}
with the standard symplectic form
\omega=dx\wedge dy
and the non-flat Riemannian metric

g=\frac{1}{f(y)}\,dx^{2}+f(y)\,dy^{2},\qquad f(y)=1+\epsilon\cos(2\pi y),\qquad 0<\epsilon<1.

The
1
-form
h=f(y)\,dy
is harmonic for
g
, with cohomology class
[h]=[dy]\in\Gamma_{\omega}=H^{1}(T^{2},\mathbb{Z})
. The dual vector field is
X=f(y)\partial_{x}
, and its time-one map is

\rho(x,y)=\bigl(x+\epsilon\cos(2\pi y),\,y\bigr)\pmod{1}.

Since
\epsilon>0
,
\rho\neq\id
. Its flux in the quotient
H^{1}_{dR}(T^{2},\mathbb{R})/\Gamma_{\omega}
is
[h]\bmod\Gamma_{\omega}=0
, meaning
\rho
is a Hamiltonian diffeomorphism. Thus
\rho\in\Ham(M,\omega)\cap\mathcal{H}
, yielding

\mathcal{H}(0)\neq\{\id\}.

2.5.
Hofer lengths for Hamiltonian isotopies

For a Hamiltonian isotopy
\Phi
generated by normalized
H=\{H_{t}\}
, the oscillation is
\osc(f)=\max_{M}f-\min_{M}f
. The
L^{(1,\infty)}
-Hofer length is
l_{H}^{(1,\infty)}(\Phi)=\int_{0}^{1}\osc(H_{t})\,dt
, and the
L^{\infty}
-Hofer length is
l_{H}^{\infty}(\Phi)=\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\osc(H_{t})
. The Hofer norm of
\phi\in\Ham(M,\omega)
is

\lVert\phi\rVert_{H}^{(1,\infty)}:=\inf\{l_{H}^{(1,\infty)}(\Phi)\mid\Phi\text{ Hamiltonian isotopy from }\id\text{ to }\phi\},

and similarly for
\lVert\phi\rVert_{H}^{\infty}
. It is known that
\lVert\phi\rVert_{H}^{\infty}=\lVert\phi\rVert_{H}^{(1,\infty)}

[
11
]
; we denote this common value by
\lVert\phi\rVert_{H}
.

2.6.
Hofer-like lengths and norms

Given a symplectic isotopy
\Phi
with generator
(U^{\Phi},\mathcal{H}^{\Phi})
, define

l_{HL}^{(1,\infty)}(\Phi)=\int_{0}^{1}\bigl(\osc(U_{t}^{\Phi})+\lVert\mathcal{H}_{t}^{\Phi}\rVert_{L^{2}}\bigr)dt,\qquad l^{\infty}_{HL}(\Phi)=\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\bigl(\osc(U_{t}^{\Phi})+\lVert\mathcal{H}_{t}^{\Phi}\rVert_{L^{2}}\bigr).

The corresponding energies of
\phi\in G_{\omega}(M)
are

e_{HL}^{(1,\infty)}(\phi)=\inf\{l_{HL}^{(1,\infty)}(\Phi)\mid\Phi\in\Iso(\phi)_{\omega}\},\quad e^{\infty}_{HL}(\phi)=\inf\{l^{\infty}_{HL}(\Phi)\mid\Phi\in\Iso(\phi)_{\omega}\}.

The Hofer-like norms are

\lVert\phi\rVert_{HL}^{(1,\infty)}=\frac{1}{2}\bigl(e_{HL}^{(1,\infty)}(\phi)+e_{HL}^{(1,\infty)}(\phi^{-1})\bigr),\qquad\lVert\phi\rVert_{HL}^{\infty}=\frac{1}{2}\bigl(e^{\infty}_{HL}(\phi)+e^{\infty}_{HL}(\phi^{-1})\bigr).

These define pseudo-norms on
G_{\omega}(M)
; they are norms on
\Ham(M,\omega)
under suitable conditions. For Hamiltonian diffeomorphisms, one always has
\lVert\phi\rVert_{HL}^{(1,\infty)}\leq\lVert\phi\rVert_{H}
because Hamiltonian isotopies are admissible. Throughout this paper, we may use either the
L^{\infty}
-version or
the
L^{(1,\infty)}
-version of the Hofer-like length. Indeed,
Tchuiaga
[
13
]
proved that the corresponding energies coincide
on
G_{\omega}(M)
:

e_{HL}^{\infty}(\phi)=e_{HL}^{(1,\infty)}(\phi)\qquad\text{for all }\phi\in G_{\omega}(M).

Consequently, we write
e_{HL}(\phi)
for this common value, and

l_{HL}(\Phi)
for the corresponding length (understood up to the
usual infimum-stability). In the proofs below we shall use the

L^{(1,\infty)}
-version, since it is additive under concatenation of
isotopies, while in the statements we shall use the
L^{\infty}
-version
whenever it is more convenient.

Example 1

(Explicit Hofer-like and Hofer norms on the flat torus)
.

Let
(T^{2n},\omega_{\mathrm{flat}},g_{\mathrm{flat}})
be the
standard flat torus of dimension
2n
, with

\omega_{\mathrm{flat}}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}dx_{j}\wedge dy_{j},
and
g_{\mathrm{flat}}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\bigl(dx_{j}^{2}+dy_{j}^{2}\bigr).

We compute explicitly the two norms on some families of elements of

G_{\omega}(T^{2n})
.
By Hodge theory on the flat torus,

\mathcal{H}^{1}(T^{2n},g_{\mathrm{flat}})=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{dx_{1},dy_{1},\dots,dx_{n},dy_{n}\},

an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(T^{2n},g_{\mathrm{flat}})
. The

\omega
-duals of the generators are the constant vector fields

X_{dx_{j}}=-\partial_{y_{j}},
and
X_{dy_{j}}=\partial_{x_{j}},

which pairwise commute.
The flux group of
(T^{2n},\omega_{\mathrm{flat}})
is

\Gamma_{\omega}=\mathbb{Z}^{2n}
, identified with the lattice of integral
cohomology classes. A harmonic isotopy with generator

X_{t}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}(t)\,\partial_{x_{j}}+b_{j}(t)\,\partial_{y_{j}},

has time-one map the translation by

w=(\int_{0}^{1}a_{j}\,dt,\,\int_{0}^{1}b_{j}\,dt)_{j}
, and its flux class
in
H^{1}_{dR}/\Gamma_{\omega}=(\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z})^{2n}
equals
w\bmod\mathbb{Z}^{2n}
.
Hence
\mathcal{H}(0)=\{\id\}
: the only zero-flux harmonic diffeomorphism
of the flat torus is the identity.
Since
\mathcal{H}(0)=\{\id\}
, the sharpness condition

L(\rho)\geq\lVert\rho\rVert_{H}
holds vacuously on
\mathcal{H}(0)
. By
Theorem
2
(equivalently, by
Corollary
1
),

\lVert\phi\rVert_{HL}=\lVert\phi\rVert_{H}

for every
\phi\in\Ham(T^{2n},\omega_{\mathrm{flat}}).

For
w\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}/\mathbb{Z}^{2n}
, let
R_{w}
denote the translation of

T^{2n}
by
w
. Its flux class is
w\bmod\mathbb{Z}^{2n}
. The harmonic
locus
\mathcal{H}(\gamma)
of
\gamma:=\Flux(R_{w})
consists of the
translations
R_{w+\delta}
with
\delta\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}
, and for

\rho=R_{w+\delta}
the Hofer residue is

R_{w}\rho^{-1}=R_{-\delta}=\id,
and
\lVert R_{w}\rho^{-1}\rVert_{H}=0.

The harmonic energy is

L(R_{w+\delta})=\min\Bigl\{\int_{0}^{1}\lVert X_{t}\rVert_{L^{2}}\,dt:\ X_{t}\text{ harmonic},\ \textstyle\int_{0}^{1}X_{t}\,dt=w+\delta\Bigr\}=\lVert w+\delta\rVert_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}},

where the last equality is attained by the constant family

X_{t}=w+\delta
and is the Euclidean norm on
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
. By the
semidirect-product formula of Theorem
1
,

\lVert R_{w}\rVert_{HL}=\inf_{\delta\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}}\lVert w+\delta\rVert_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}=\operatorname{dist}\bigl(w,\mathbb{Z}^{2n}\bigr).

In coordinates
w=(u,v)
with
u,v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\lVert R_{w}\rVert_{HL}=\sqrt{\sum_{j=1}^{n}\operatorname{dist}(u_{j},\mathbb{Z})^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\operatorname{dist}(v_{j},\mathbb{Z})^{2}}.

Hofer-like norm of a shear.

Consider the shear on
T^{2}
defined by

\phi_{\eta}(x,y)=\bigl(x+\eta(y),\,y\bigr),\qquad\eta\in C^{\infty}(S^{1},\mathbb{R}),\quad\int_{0}^{1}\eta(y)\,dy=0.

Its flux class is trivial, hence
\phi_{\eta}\in\Ham(T^{2})
. The
isotopy
t\mapsto(x+t\eta(y),y)
has generator

X_{t}=\eta(y)\partial_{x}
, and
\iota_{X_{t}}\omega=\eta(y)\,dy
.
Since
\eta
has zero average,
\eta(y)\,dy=dH
with the periodic
primitive
H(y):=\int_{0}^{y}\eta(s)\,ds,

so the Hodge decomposition of
\iota_{X_{t}}\omega
has trivial
harmonic part. The isotopy is therefore Hamiltonian, generated by

H
, and

l_{HL}(\Psi)=l_{H}(\Psi)=\operatorname{osc}(H).

Consequently,

\lVert\phi_{\eta}\rVert_{HL}=\lVert\phi_{\eta}\rVert_{H}\leq\operatorname{osc}(H)=\max_{y\in S^{1}}\int_{0}^{y}\eta-\min_{y\in S^{1}}\int_{0}^{y}\eta.

For example, taking
\eta(y)=\sin(2\pi y)
, the primitive is

H(y)=\bigl(1-\cos(2\pi y)\bigr)/(2\pi)
, with

\operatorname{osc}(H)=1/\pi
, so

\lVert\phi_{\eta}\rVert_{HL}=\lVert\phi_{\eta}\rVert_{H}\leq\frac{1}{\pi}.

Comparison with the non-flat metric.

On the same
T^{2}
, equip the torus with the metric

g_{\varepsilon}=f_{\varepsilon}(y)^{-1}dx^{2}+f_{\varepsilon}(y)\,dy^{2}
,

f_{\varepsilon}(y)=1+\varepsilon\cos(2\pi y)
,
\varepsilon\neq 0
.
By Example
2
,
\mathcal{H}(0)\neq\{\id\}
, and the
geometric condition
L(\rho)\geq\lVert\rho\rVert_{H}
is verified on the
family
\{\rho_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
. Hence the flat metric is not the only
metric for which the two norms coincide on
\Ham(T^{2})
, but the
mechanism is different: on the flat metric the vanishing of

\mathcal{H}(0)
makes the criterion vacuous, whereas on

g_{\varepsilon}
the criterion is checked non-trivially on an
infinite family.

2.7.
Flux group and its role in Hofer-like geometry

For a symplectic isotopy
\Phi\in\Iso(M,\omega)
with endpoint
\phi\in\Ham(M,\omega)
, its flux lies in the flux group
\Gamma_{\omega}
. A fundamental result
[
2
]
states that for any such
\Phi
, there exists a loop
\Psi
in
G_{\omega}(M)
based at
\id
whose flux equals
\widetilde{\Flux}(\Phi)
. More precisely:

Definition 11
.

For
\Phi\in\Iso(M,\omega)
with
\phi_{1}\in\Ham(M,\omega)
, let

\Upsilon(\Phi):=\{[\Psi]\in\pi_{1}(G_{\omega}(M),\id)\mid\widetilde{\Flux}([\Psi])=\widetilde{\Flux}([\Phi])\}.

Because
\Gamma_{\omega}
is by definition the set of fluxes of loops,
\Upsilon(\Phi)
is non-empty
[
2
]
. Given a loop
\Psi
representing a class in
\Upsilon(\Phi)
, the left concatenation isotopy
\Phi\ast_{l}\Psi^{-1}
has zero flux and is therefore homotopic (relative to endpoints) to a Hamiltonian isotopy. For
\phi\in\Ham(M,\omega)
, define the set of
zero-flux paths
from
\id
to
\phi
:

\mathcal{Z}_{\phi}:=\{\Phi\in\Iso(\phi)_{\omega}:\widetilde{\Flux}([\Phi])=0\}.

Note that
\mathcal{Z}_{\phi}
is a set of paths, not a group; it will be used only set-theoretically. This construction is central to the proof of the main results.

Lemma 2
.

Let
(M,\omega)
be a closed symplectic manifold, and let
\phi\in\Ham(M,\omega)
. Let
\mathcal{Z}_{\phi}
be the set of zero-flux symplectic isotopies from
\id
to
\phi
. Then, at the level of homotopy classes relative to endpoints, there is a bijection

B\colon\Iso(\phi)_{\omega}/\!\simeq\;\longrightarrow\;\left(\mathcal{Z}_{\phi}/\!\simeq\;\right)\times\;\Gamma_{\omega}.

Proof.

Fix, for each flux class
\gamma\in\Gamma_{\omega}
, a loop
L_{\gamma}
based at
\id
in
G_{\omega}(M)
with
\widetilde{\Flux}(L_{\gamma})=\gamma
; let
L_{0}
be the constant loop. Define,

B([\Phi]):=\bigl([\Phi\ast_{l}L_{\widetilde{\Flux}(\Phi)}^{-1}],\,\widetilde{\Flux}(\Phi)\bigr).

Since
\widetilde{\Flux}(\Phi\ast_{l}L_{\widetilde{\Flux}(\Phi)}^{-1})=0
, the first component lies in
\mathcal{Z}_{\phi}/\!\simeq
. Conversely, define

A([\Xi],\gamma):=[\Xi\ast_{l}L_{\gamma}].

Then
A(B([\Phi]))=[(\Phi\ast_{l}L_{\gamma}^{-1})\ast_{l}L_{\gamma}]=[\Phi]
and
B(A([\Xi],\gamma))=([\Xi],\gamma)
, using the concatenation homotopies of Proposition
1
. The assignments are well-defined on homotopy classes because
\ast_{l}
respects homotopy relative to endpoints. Hence
B
is a bijection.
∎

Lemma 3

(Stability of Hofer-like length under homotopy)
.

Let
(M,\omega,g)
be a closed symplectic manifold. Let
H\colon[0,1]\times[0,1]\to G_{\omega}(M)
be a smooth homotopy of symplectic isotopies relative to endpoints: for each
s\in[0,1]
,
\Phi_{s}=\{H(s,t)\}_{t\in[0,1]}
is a symplectic isotopy with
H(s,0)=\id
and
H(s,1)=\phi
for some fixed
\phi\in G_{\omega}(M)
. Then,

(2.5)

s\mapsto l_{HL}^{(1,\infty)}(\Phi_{s})\quad\text{and}\quad s\mapsto l_{HL}^{\infty}(\Phi_{s}),

are continuous on
[0,1]
.

Proof.

For each
s\in[0,1]
, the generating vector field
X_{s,t}
of

\Phi_{s}
is
X_{s,t}=\partial_{t}H(s,t)\circ H(s,t)^{-1}
, smooth in
all variables since
H
is. Applying Hodge decomposition

\alpha_{s,t}=\iota_{X_{s,t}}\omega=dU_{s,t}+\mathcal{H}_{s,t}
with

U_{s,t}
normalized and
\mathcal{H}_{s,t}\in\mathcal{H}^{1}(M,g)
harmonic, the
projection
P\colon L^{2}\Omega^{1}(M)\to\mathcal{H}^{1}(M,g)
has smooth
Schwartz kernel
K(x,y)=\sum_{i}h_{i}(x)\otimes h_{i}(y)
, so

\mathcal{H}_{s,t}=P(\alpha_{s,t})
is smooth in
(s,t,x)
. The exact part

\beta_{s,t}=\alpha_{s,t}-\mathcal{H}_{s,t}=dU_{s,t}
is likewise smooth, and
the Green operator
G
of
\Delta=\delta d
gives

U_{s,t}=G(\delta\beta_{s,t})
smooth in
(s,t,x)
. Define

f(s,t):=\osc(U_{s,t})+\lVert\mathcal{H}_{s,t}\rVert_{L^{2}}
, which is continuous
on the compact set
[0,1]^{2}
. Then

l_{HL}^{(1,\infty)}(\Phi_{s})=\int_{0}^{1}f(s,t)\,dt
is continuous in

s
by parameter-dependent integration
[
16
, Theorem 9.42]
,
and
l_{HL}^{\infty}(\Phi_{s})=\sup_{t}f(s,t)
is continuous in
s
by
Berge’s Maximum Theorem
[
17
, Theorem 17.31]
.
∎

Lemma 4

(Hofer-like energy via zero-flux paths)
.

Let
(M,\omega)
be a closed symplectic manifold, and let
\phi\in\Ham(M,\omega)
. Then

\inf_{\Phi^{\prime}\in\Iso(\phi)_{\omega}}\left(\inf_{\Psi_{0}\in\Upsilon(\Phi^{\prime})}\left(l_{HL}^{(1,\infty)}(\Phi^{\prime}\ast_{l}\Psi^{-1}_{0})\right)\right)=e_{HL}^{\infty}(\phi).

Proof.

By Lemma
2
, every path of the form
\Phi^{\prime}\ast_{l}\Psi_{0}^{-1}
is a zero-flux path
\Xi\in\mathcal{Z}_{\phi}
, and conversely, every zero-flux path can be represented in this form. Therefore,

(2.6)

\inf_{\Phi^{\prime}\in\Iso(\phi)_{\omega}}\left(\inf_{\Psi_{0}\in\Upsilon(\Phi^{\prime})}\left(l_{HL}^{(1,\infty)}(\Phi^{\prime}\ast_{l}\Psi^{-1}_{0})\right)\right)=\inf_{\Xi\in\mathcal{Z}_{\phi}}l_{HL}^{(1,\infty)}(\Xi).

By Lemma
2
, every
\Phi\in\Iso(\phi)_{\omega}
with flux
\gamma\in\Gamma_{\omega}
decomposes up to homotopy as
\Phi=\Xi\ast_{l}L_{\gamma}
, where
\Xi\in\mathcal{Z}_{\phi}
. By reparametrization,

l_{HL}^{\infty}(\Xi\ast_{l}L_{\gamma})=\max\bigl(l_{HL}^{\infty}(\Xi^{\lambda}),\,l_{HL}^{\infty}(L_{\gamma}^{\tau})\bigr)\geq\inf_{\Xi\in\mathcal{Z}_{\phi}}l_{HL}^{(1,\infty)}(\Xi),

so after passing to the infimum over
\Phi
,

e_{HL}^{(1,\infty)}=e_{HL}^{\infty}(\phi)\geq\inf_{\Xi\in\mathcal{Z}_{\phi}}l_{HL}^{(1,\infty)}(\Xi).

Conversely, fix
\epsilon>0
and choose
\Xi_{\epsilon}\in\mathcal{Z}_{\phi}
with
l_{HL}^{(1,\infty)}(\Xi_{\epsilon})\leq\inf_{\Xi\in\mathcal{Z}_{\phi}}l_{HL}^{(1,\infty)}(\Xi)+\epsilon
. Let
L_{0}
be the constant loop. Then
\Phi_{\epsilon}=\Xi_{\epsilon}\ast_{l}L_{0}\in\Iso(\phi)_{\omega}
and
l_{HL}^{(1,\infty)}(\Phi_{\epsilon})=l_{HL}^{(1,\infty)}(\Xi_{\epsilon})
. Thus
e_{HL}^{(1,\infty)}(\phi)\leq\inf_{\Xi\in\mathcal{Z}_{\phi}}l_{HL}^{(1,\infty)}(\Xi)+\epsilon
. Letting
\epsilon\to 0
gives the reverse inequality. Combining both with (
2.6
) proves the lemma.
∎

3.
On the proof of the main theorems

The proof of Theorem
3
proceeds through the following steps:
we first establish a connection between Hofer-like norms on symplectic
vector fields and Hofer-like norms on diffeomorphisms;
construct a specific two-parameter family of vector fields derived from
harmonic 1-forms and derive an oscillation bound (Corollary
3
);
show boundedness of a related vector field family (Proposition
4
);
prove Banyaga’s two-parameter identity (Lemma
6
);
and finally combine these results via the flux group and concatenation.

3.1.
Hofer-like norm on symplectic vector fields

Let
\chi_{\omega}(M)
denote the space of symplectic vector fields on
(M,\omega)
. For
Y\in\chi_{\omega}(M)
, Hodge decomposition gives

(3.1)

\iota_{Y}\omega=dU_{Y}+\mathcal{H}_{Y},

where
\mathcal{H}_{Y}\in\mathcal{H}^{1}(M,g)
is harmonic and
U_{Y}\in C^{\infty}(M)
is normalized by
\int_{M}U_{Y}\omega^{n}=0
.

Definition 12
.

The Hofer-like norm of
Y\in\chi_{\omega}(M)
is

(3.2)

\lVert Y\rVert_{HL}=\osc(U_{Y})+\lVert\mathcal{H}_{Y}\rVert_{L^{2}},

where
\osc(U_{Y})=\max_{M}U_{Y}-\min_{M}U_{Y}
.

See Banyaga
[
1
]
for the introduction of this norm.

3.2.
Construction of a two-parameter family of vector fields

Given a smooth family of harmonic 1-forms
\{\mathcal{H}_{t}\}_{t\in[0,1]}
, let
\{X_{t}\}
be the corresponding harmonic vector fields,
\iota_{X_{t}}\omega=\mathcal{H}_{t}
. Define

(3.3)

Z_{s,t}=tX_{s}-2s\int_{0}^{t}X_{u}\,du.

For each fixed
t
, let
\{G_{s,t}\}_{s\in[0,1]}
solve

(3.4)

\frac{\partial}{\partial s}G_{s,t}(x)=Z_{s,t}(G_{s,t}(x)),\quad G_{0,t}(x)=x.

Define
\{V_{s,t}\}
by

(3.5)

V_{s,t}(G_{s,t}(x))=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}G_{s,t}(x).

Corollary 3
.

Let
\{X_{t}\}
,
\{Z_{s,t}\}
, and
\{V_{s,t}\}
be as in Subsection
3.2
. For each
u\in[0,1]
and fixed
t\in[0,1]
, the oscillation of
F_{u,t}(x)=\int_{0}^{u}\omega(Z_{s,t},V_{s,t})(x)\,ds
satisfies

(3.6)

\int_{0}^{1}\osc\!\left(\int_{0}^{u}\omega(Z_{s,t},V_{s,t})\,ds\right)dt\leq 3L_{0}\left(\sup_{(s,t,x)\in[0,1]^{2}\times M}\lVert V_{s,t}(x)\rVert_{g}\right)\left(\int_{0}^{1}\lVert X_{t}\rVert_{HL}dt\right),

where
L_{0}>0
is the constant from (
2.4
).

Proof.

For fixed
u\in[0,1]
and
t\in[0,1]
, define

F_{u,t}(x):=\int_{0}^{u}\omega(Z_{s,t},V_{s,t})(x)\,ds
. By definition of oscillation,

(3.7)

\osc(F_{u,t})\leq 2\int_{0}^{u}\sup_{x\in M}\lvert\omega(Z_{s,t},V_{s,t})(x)\rvert\,ds.

For fixed
s,t
and
x\in M
,

\lvert\omega(Z_{s,t},V_{s,t})(x)\rvert=\lvert(\iota_{Z_{s,t}}\omega)(V_{s,t})(x)\rvert\leq\lVert\iota_{Z_{s,t}}\omega\rVert_{0}^{*}\,\lVert V_{s,t}(x)\rVert_{g}\leq A\,\lVert\iota_{Z_{s,t}}\omega\rVert_{0}^{*},

where
A:=\sup_{(s,t,x)\in[0,1]^{2}\times M}\lVert V_{s,t}(x)\rVert_{g}
.
Since
\iota_{Z_{s,t}}\omega=t\mathcal{H}_{s}-2s\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{H}_{u}\,du
,
taking pointwise norms and using (
2.4
),

\lVert\iota_{Z_{s,t}}\omega\rVert_{0}^{*}\leq L_{0}\left(t\lVert X_{s}\rVert_{HL}+2s\int_{0}^{t}\lVert X_{u}\rVert_{HL}\,du\right),

because
\lVert\mathcal{H}_{u}\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq\lVert X_{u}\rVert_{HL}
. Substituting into (
3.7
),

(3.8)

\osc(F_{u,t})\leq 2AL_{0}\int_{0}^{u}\left(t\lVert X_{s}\rVert_{HL}+2s\int_{0}^{t}\lVert X_{u}\rVert_{HL}\,du\right)ds.

Integrating (
3.8
) over
t\in[0,1]
and exchanging the
order of integration,

\int_{0}^{1}\osc(F_{u,t})\,dt\leq 2AL_{0}\left[\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{u}\lVert X_{s}\rVert_{HL}\,ds+\int_{0}^{1}u^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\lVert X_{v}\rVert_{HL}\,dv\,dt\right].

Let
B:=\int_{0}^{1}\lVert X_{t}\rVert_{HL}\,dt
. Since
u\leq 1
,

\int_{0}^{1}\osc(F_{u,t})\,dt\leq 2AL_{0}\left(\tfrac{1}{2}B+B\right)=3AL_{0}B.

This completes the proof.
∎

Proposition 4
.

Let
(M,g)
be a closed oriented Riemannian manifold and
\{Z_{s,t}\}
a bounded smooth two-parameter family of vector fields. Define
\{G_{s,t}\}
by (
3.4
) and
\{V_{s,t}\}
by
V_{s,t}(G_{s,t}(x))=\partial_{t}G_{s,t}(x)
. Then
\{V_{s,t}\}
is also a bounded smooth two-parameter family of vector fields.

Proof.

Let
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{op}
denote the operator norm. Since
M
is closed and
Z_{s,t}
is bounded and smooth, there exist
N,K>0
such that

\left\lVert\partial_{t}Z_{s,t}(x)\right\rVert_{g,x}\leq N,\quad\left\lVert DZ_{s,t}(x)\right\rVert_{op}\leq K.

Let
U_{s,t}(x)=\partial_{t}G_{s,t}(x)=V_{s,t}(G_{s,t}(x))
. Differentiating (
3.4
) in
t
,

\partial_{s}U_{s,t}(x)=\partial_{t}Z_{s,t}(G_{s,t}(x))+DZ_{s,t}(G_{s,t}(x))\,U_{s,t}(x).

With
U_{0,t}(x)=0
, taking norms at
G_{s,t}(x)
,

\left\lVert\partial_{s}U_{s,t}(x)\right\rVert_{g,G_{s,t}(x)}\leq N+K\lVert U_{s,t}(x)\rVert_{g,G_{s,t}(x)}.

Let
f(s)=\sup_{x\in M}\lVert U_{s,t}(x)\rVert_{g,G_{s,t}(x)}
. Then

f(s^{\prime})\leq Ns^{\prime}+\int_{0}^{s^{\prime}}Kf(s)\,ds
, and Grönwall gives

f(s^{\prime})\leq(N/K)(e^{Ks^{\prime}}-1)
. Since
G_{s,t}
is a diffeomorphism
and
V_{s,t}(G_{s,t}(x))=U_{s,t}(x)
, for every
y\in M
and

(s^{\prime},t)\in[0,1]^{2}
,
\lVert V_{s^{\prime},t}(y)\rVert_{g,y}\leq(N/K)(e^{K}-1)
. Hence
V_{s,t}
is a bounded smooth
two-parameter family.
∎

Lemma 5

(
V_{s,t}

Let
(M,\omega,g)

Assume there exists a constant

∎

Corollary 4

(Sequential equivalence at the identity)
.

Corollary 6

(Application to a finite commuting family)
.

Let
X_{1},\dots,X_{N}
be pairwise commuting vector fields on a
smooth manifold
M
, and let

a_{1},\dots,a_{N}\in C^{\infty}([0,1])
. Then the time-one map of the

Proposition 5

(Non-negative Ricci curvature and the sharpness condition)
.

Let
(M,\omega)
be a closed symplectic manifold equipped with a
compatible Riemannian metric
g
satisfying
\operatorname{Ric}_{g}\geq 0
.
Assume in addition that

(i)

every harmonic
1
-form on
(M,g)
is cohomologous to an
integral class,

(ii)

the flux group
\Gamma_{\omega}
is of full rank in

H^{1}_{dR}(M,\mathbb{R})
, generated by the integral harmonic classes, and

(iii)

the flows
\exp(tX_{j})
of the harmonic vector fields dual to
an integral basis
\{[\alpha_{j}]\}
of
\mathcal{H}^{1}(M,g)
have
period
1
, i.e.
\exp(X_{j})=\id
for every
j
.

Then,
\mathcal{H}(0)=\{\id\}
, and consequently, by
Theorem
2
,

\lVert\phi\rVert_{HL}=\lVert\phi\rVert_{H},

for every
\phi\in\Ham(M,\omega).

Proof.

The proof proceeds in four steps.

Step 1: harmonic
1
-forms are parallel.
By the
Bochner–Weitzenböck formula,

\Delta\alpha=\nabla^{*}\nabla\alpha+\operatorname{Ric}(\alpha^{\sharp},\cdot),\qquad\alpha\in\Omega^{1}(M).

For a harmonic
\alpha
and
\operatorname{Ric}_{g}\geq 0
,
integrating
\langle\Delta\alpha,\alpha\rangle
over
M
gives

0=\int_{M}\lvert\nabla\alpha\rvert^{2}\,d\vol_{g}+\int_{M}\operatorname{Ric}(\alpha^{\sharp},\alpha^{\sharp})\,d\vol_{g}.

Both integrands are non-negative, so they vanish identically. Hence

\nabla\alpha=0
.

Step 2: the
\omega
-dual harmonic vector fields
commute.
Let
\alpha
be a parallel harmonic
1
-form on

(M,g)
and let
X
be its
\omega
-dual, defined by

\iota_{X}\omega=\alpha
. Because
(M,\omega,g,J)
is a
compatible triple, the metric dual
\alpha^{\sharp}
and the

\omega
-dual
X
are related by

\alpha^{\sharp}=\pm JX,

with the sign depending on the convention adopted for the compatible
triple. Since
J
is parallel with respect to the Levi-Civita
connection of
g
on any Kähler manifold, and since the metric
duality
\sharp\colon T^{*}M\to TM
preserves parallelism, the
parallelism of
\alpha
implies the parallelism of
X
. Two
parallel vector fields on a Riemannian manifold commute, so the

\omega
-dual harmonic vector fields form a commutative Lie
algebra.

Step 3: a zero-flux harmonic isotopy has integral
coefficients.
Let
\Psi
be a harmonic isotopy from
\id
, with
generator

X_{t}=\sum_{j=1}^{b_{1}(M)}a_{j}(t)\,X_{j},

where
\{X_{j}\}
is the basis of parallel harmonic vector fields
dual to the integral harmonic classes
\{[\alpha_{j}]\}
, and

a_{j}\in C^{\infty}([0,1])
. By Step 2, the vector fields
X_{j}

commute, so Corollary
6
applies with

X_{t}=\sum_{j}a_{j}(t)X_{j}
: the time-one map of
\Psi
is

\Psi_{1}=\exp\bigl(\sum_{j}c_{j}X_{j}\bigr)
with

c_{j}:=\int_{0}^{1}a_{j}(t)\,dt
. Its flux class is

\Flux(\Psi_{1})=\Bigl[\sum_{j=1}^{b_{1}(M)}c_{j}\,\alpha_{j}\Bigr]\in H^{1}_{dR}(M,\mathbb{R})/\Gamma_{\omega}.

Suppose
\Flux(\Psi_{1})=0
. Then

\sum_{j}c_{j}[\alpha_{j}]\in\Gamma_{\omega}
. Since
\Gamma_{\omega}
is
generated by the integral classes
\{[\alpha_{j}]\}
, we have

\sum_{j}c_{j}[\alpha_{j}]=\sum_{j}n_{j}[\alpha_{j}]
for integers

n_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}
. The classes
\{[\alpha_{j}]\}
are linearly
independent, so
c_{j}=n_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Step 4: the time-one map is the identity.
By
Step 3, the time-one map of a zero-flux harmonic isotopy is

\Psi_{1}=\exp\bigl(\sum_{j=1}^{b_{1}(M)}n_{j}X_{j}\bigr)
with

n_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}
. By hypothesis (iii),
\exp(X_{j})=\id
for every

j
. Since the
X_{j}
commute, the exponential factors:

\Psi_{1}=\prod_{j=1}^{b_{1}(M)}\exp(n_{j}X_{j})=\prod_{j=1}^{b_{1}(M)}\bigl(\exp(X_{j})\bigr)^{n_{j}}=\id.

Hence every zero-flux harmonic isotopy from
\id
terminates at

\id
, so
\mathcal{H}(0)=\{\id\}
. The final statement follows from
Theorem
2
.
∎

Remark 5

(Sharpness of the hypotheses)
.

The three hypotheses of Proposition
5
are
not redundant.

(i)

The condition
\operatorname{Ric}_{g}\geq 0
is sufficient but
not necessary for the harmonic vector fields to commute, and cannot
be dropped in Step 1.

(ii)

Integrality of the harmonic classes is required in Step 3 to
convert the flux condition into
c_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}
.

(iii)

Period-
1
of the harmonic flows is required in Step 4 to
conclude that the time-one map is the identity.

The three hypotheses are satisfied by:

(a)

flat tori
(T^{2n},\omega_{\mathrm{flat}},g_{\mathrm{flat}})
;

(b)

Calabi–Yau manifolds with the Ricci-flat Kähler metric and
integral Kähler class;

(c)

products
N\times T^{2k}
with
N
a closed simply connected
Calabi–Yau manifold and
T^{2k}
flat.

Compatibility of
g
with
\omega
is not sufficient by itself.
On
(T^{2},\omega_{dx\wedge dy})
, the compatible metric

g_{\varepsilon}=f_{\varepsilon}(y)^{-1}dx^{2}+f_{\varepsilon}(y)\,dy^{2},\qquad f_{\varepsilon}(y)=1+\varepsilon\cos(2\pi y),\qquad\varepsilon\neq 0,

is
not
Kähler: the almost complex structure
J

determined by
g_{\varepsilon}
and
\omega
has Nijenhuis tensor

N_{J}(\partial_{x},\partial_{y})=\frac{f_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(y)}{f_{\varepsilon}(y)}\,\partial_{y}\neq 0,

so
J
is non-integrable. Its harmonic
1
-forms

dx
and
f_{\varepsilon}(y)\,dy
are not parallel, and their

\omega
-duals

X_{1}=f_{\varepsilon}(y)\partial_{x},
and
X_{2}=-\partial_{y},

satisfy

[X_{1},X_{2}]=-f_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(y)\,\partial_{x}\neq 0
provided
\varepsilon\neq 0.

Hence
\mathcal{H}(0)\neq\{\id\}
on this manifold
(see Example
2
and
Remark
3
).

Corollary 7

(Rigid flux classes)
.

If for some
\gamma\in H^{1}_{dR}(M,\mathbb{R})/\Gamma_{\omega}
the harmonic
locus
\mathcal{H}(\gamma)
is a singleton

Lemma 9

(Flux group of products with the flat torus)
.

Let
N
be a closed simply connected manifold, and let

M=N\times T^{2k}
be equipped with the product symplectic form

\omega=\omega_{N}\oplus\omega_{\mathrm{flat}}
, where

\omega_{\mathrm{flat}}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}d\theta^{j}\wedge d\theta^{j+k}
is the standard integral symplectic form on the flat
torus. Then, under the natural identification

H^{1}_{dR}(M,\mathbb{R})\cong H^{1}_{dR}(T^{2k},\mathbb{R})\cong\mathbb{R}^{2k}
,

\Gamma_{\omega}=\mathbb{Z}^{2k}.

In particular,
\Gamma_{\omega}
has full rank
b_{1}(M)=2k
.

Proof.

For the inclusion
\mathbb{Z}^{2k}\subseteq\Gamma_{\omega}
, fix

v\in\mathbb{Z}^{2k}
and consider the loop
\Phi_{t}(x,\theta)=(x,\theta+tv)
in
G_{\omega}(M)
based at
\id
. Its flux class
is
v
, viewed as an element of

H^{1}_{dR}(M,\mathbb{R})\cong\mathbb{R}^{2k}
, so
v\in\Gamma_{\omega}
. For the reverse inclusion, Banyaga’s theorem
[
2
]
states that

\Gamma_{\omega}
is contained in the image of the integral
cohomology
H^{1}(M;\mathbb{Z})\to H^{1}_{dR}(M,\mathbb{R})
. Since
N
is simply
connected and
b_{1}(T^{2k})=2k
with
H^{1}(T^{2k};\mathbb{Z})=\mathbb{Z}^{2k}

torsion-free, Künneth gives

H^{1}(M;\mathbb{Z})=\mathbb{Z}^{2k}
, so
\Gamma_{\omega}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{2k}
.
Combining both inclusions yields
\Gamma_{\omega}=\mathbb{Z}^{2k}
.
∎

Proposition 6
.

Let
N
be closed and simply connected, and let

(M,\omega,g)=(N\times T^{2k},\omega_{N}\oplus\omega_{\mathrm{flat}},g_{N}\oplus g_{\mathrm{flat}})
as above. Then
\mathcal{H}(\gamma)
is a
singleton for every

\gamma\in H^{1}_{dR}(M,\mathbb{R})/\Gamma_{\omega}=T^{2k}
.
Consequently, Corollary
7
applies: for every

\phi\in G_{\omega}(M)
with
\Flux(\phi)=\gamma
,

\lVert\phi\rVert_{HL}=L(\rho_{\gamma})+\lVert\phi\,\rho_{\gamma}^{-1}\rVert_{H}.

Proof.

By Lemma
9
,

\Gamma_{\omega}=\mathbb{Z}^{2k}\subset H^{1}_{dR}(M,\mathbb{R})\cong\mathbb{R}^{2k}
, of
full rank.

Step 1: harmonic vector fields are constant torus
fields.
Since
N
is simply connected with
b_{1}(N)=0
,
Künneth gives

\mathcal{H}^{1}(M,g)=\pi^{*}\mathcal{H}^{1}(T^{2k},g_{\mathrm{flat}})=\pi^{*}\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{d\theta^{1},\dots,d\theta^{2k}\},

where
\pi\colon M\to T^{2k}
is the projection. Hence every
harmonic
1
-form on
M
is the pullback of a constant-coefficient
form on
T^{2k}
, and the dual harmonic vector field is a constant
vector field on the torus factor, extended trivially along
N
.

Step 2: endpoints of harmonic isotopies are torus
translations.
Let
\rho,\rho^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H}(\gamma)
be two harmonic
diffeomorphisms with the same flux class

\gamma\in H^{1}_{dR}(M,\mathbb{R})/\Gamma_{\omega}
. Choose harmonic
isotopies
\Psi,\Psi^{\prime}
from
\id
to
\rho,\rho^{\prime}
,
respectively. By Step 1, their generators are

X_{t}=\sum_{m}d_{m}(t)\,\partial_{\theta^{m}}
and

X^{\prime}_{t}=\sum_{m}d^{\prime}_{m}(t)\,\partial_{\theta^{m}}
. Since the constant
vector fields
\partial_{\theta^{1}},\dots,\partial_{\theta^{2k}}

commute, the time-one maps are the torus translations

\rho(\theta)=\theta+D,\qquad\rho^{\prime}(\theta)=\theta+D^{\prime},\qquad D:=\int_{0}^{1}d(t)\,dt,\quad D^{\prime}:=\int_{0}^{1}d^{\prime}(t)\,dt,

extended by the identity on
N
.

Step 3: same flux class forces the same
translation.
Let
\Lambda\colon\mathbb{R}^{2k}\to H^{1}_{dR}(M,\mathbb{R})
be the
linear isomorphism induced by
\omega_{\mathrm{flat}}
. The flux
lifts of
\Psi
and
\Psi^{\prime}
are
\Lambda(D)
and

\Lambda(D^{\prime})
, respectively. The hypothesis

\Flux(\rho)=\Flux(\rho^{\prime})=\gamma
means

\Lambda(D)-\Lambda(D^{\prime})\in\Gamma_{\omega}=\mathbb{Z}^{2k}
. Since

\omega_{\mathrm{flat}}
is integral,
\Lambda
preserves the
lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{2k}
, hence
D-D^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2k}
. Therefore

\rho=\rho^{\prime}
as diffeomorphisms of
T^{2k}\times N
.

Conclusion.
Every element of
\mathcal{H}(\gamma)
is
the torus translation by the class

\gamma\in\mathbb{R}^{2k}/\mathbb{Z}^{2k}=H^{1}_{dR}(M,\mathbb{R})/\Gamma_{\omega}
, extended
by the identity on
N
. Hence
\mathcal{H}(\gamma)
is a singleton.
Corollary
7
applies.

Theorem 5

(Clean-split lower bound)
.

Let
(M,\omega,g,J)
be a closed compatible triple. For every
flux class
\gamma\in H^{1}_{dR}(M,\mathbb{R})/\Gamma_{\omega}
and every harmonic
diffeomorphism
\rho\in\mathcal{H}(\gamma)
, we have

L(\rho)\;\geq\;\lVert\gamma\rVert_{H^{1}/\Gamma_{\omega}},

where
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{H^{1}/\Gamma_{\omega}}
is the quotient norm on

H^{1}_{dR}(M,\mathbb{R})/\Gamma_{\omega}
induced by the
L^{2}
-Hodge norm
on harmonic
1
-forms.

Proof.

Let
\Phi=\{\phi_{t}\}_{t\in[0,1]}
be a harmonic isotopy from
\id

to
\rho
, with generator
X_{t}
, and set

\alpha_{t}:=\iota_{X_{t}}\omega\in\mathcal{H}^{1}(M,g)
. Because

(M,\omega,g,J)
is compatible,
\lVert X_{t}\rVert_{g}=\lVert\alpha_{t}\rVert_{g}^{*}

pointwise, and since
\Phi
is harmonic,

l_{HL}(\Phi)=\int_{0}^{1}\lVert\alpha_{t}\rVert_{L^{2}}\,dt
. Define

\bar{\alpha}:=\int_{0}^{1}\alpha_{t}\,dt
, which is harmonic because

\Delta_{g}
is linear. Since

[\bar{\alpha}]=\int_{0}^{1}[\alpha_{t}]\,dt=\widetilde{\Flux}([\Phi])
, the
class
[\bar{\alpha}]
is a lift of
\gamma=\Flux(\rho)
. By
Minkowski’s integral inequality,

\lVert\bar{\alpha}\rVert_{L^{2}}=\Bigl\|\int_{0}^{1}\alpha_{t}\,dt\Bigr\|_{L^{2}}\leq\int_{0}^{1}\lVert\alpha_{t}\rVert_{L^{2}}\,dt=l_{HL}(\Phi).

Since
[\bar{\alpha}]
is a lift of
\gamma
, the quotient norm
satisfies
\lVert\gamma\rVert_{H^{1}/\Gamma_{\omega}}\leq\lVert\bar{\alpha}\rVert_{L^{2}}
.
Combining,
\lVert\gamma\rVert_{H^{1}/\Gamma_{\omega}}\leq l_{HL}(\Phi)
for
every harmonic isotopy
\Phi
from
\id
to
\rho
. Taking the
infimum,
L(\rho)\geq\lVert\gamma\rVert_{H^{1}/\Gamma_{\omega}}
.
∎

Corollary 8

(Upper bound for the Hofer-like norm on products)
.

Let
(M_{1},\omega_{1},g_{1})
and
(M_{2},\omega_{2},g_{2})
be closed
symplectic manifolds, and equip
M_{1}\times M_{2}
with the product
symplectic form
\omega_{1}\oplus\omega_{2}
and the product metric

g_{1}\oplus g_{2}
. Assume
b_{1}(M_{1})=0
. Then, for every

\phi_{1}\in G_{\omega_{1}}(M_{1})
and

\phi_{2}\in G_{\omega_{2}}(M_{2})
,

(3.10)

\lVert\phi_{1}\times\phi_{2}\rVert_{HL,\,M_{1}\times M_{2}}\;\leq\;\lVert\phi_{1}\rVert_{H,\,M_{1}}+\lVert\phi_{2}\rVert_{HL,\,M_{2}}.

Proof.

Because
b_{1}(M_{1})=0
, every symplectic isotopy
\Phi_{1}
on
M_{1}

is Hamiltonian, and the product isotopy

\Phi_{1}\times\Phi_{2}
has generator
X_{t}=X_{t}^{(1)}+X_{t}^{(2)}

with
X_{t}^{(1)}
Hamiltonian and
X_{t}^{(2)}
harmonic. The
oscillation and the
L^{2}
norm are additive over the product
because the metric is a product, so

l_{HL}(\Phi_{1}\times\Phi_{2})=l_{H}(\Phi_{1})+l_{HL}(\Phi_{2}).

Since the infimum defining
\lVert\phi_{1}\times\phi_{2}\rVert_{HL}
is
taken over a set that contains the split isotopies

\Phi_{1}\times\Phi_{2}

3.10
) by taking infima
over
\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2}

\phi\mapsto\phi^{-1}

∎

Remark 6

(The Hofer-like product formula: an open question)
.

Corollary
8
establishes the upper bound

\lVert\phi_{1}\times\phi_{2}\rVert_{HL}\;\leq\;\lVert\phi_{1}\rVert_{H}+\lVert\phi_{2}\rVert_{HL}

by restricting the infimum to split isotopies. The reverse
inequality and hence the exact equality is a genuinely open
problem. In classical Hofer geometry, the exact equality

\lVert\phi_{1}\times\phi_{2}\rVert_{H}=\lVert\phi_{1}\rVert_{H}+\lVert\phi_{2}\rVert_{H}
is
proved by exhibiting spectral invariants of the Hamiltonian Floer
homology that are additive under products
(
c(H\oplus G)=c(H)+c(G)
, by a Künneth argument at the chain
level) and that bound the Hofer norm from below; this forces every
Hamiltonian isotopy on the product to be no shorter than the sum of
its split contributions. The argument is due to Viterbo, Schwarz, Oh,
and Usher; see
[
28
,
29
]
and the references therein. For the Hofer-like norm, the same strategy would require a theory of
spectral invariants for
non-Hamiltonian
symplectomorphisms
with non-trivial flux, valued in the Floer–Novikov homology
developed by Ono, Pajitnov, and others. Such a theory exists for
Hamiltonian perturbations of the identity but is not yet developed
to the point of giving invariants that are simultaneously

(i)

additive under the product of two symplectic manifolds,

(ii)

compatible with the
L^{2}
-Hodge norm on the harmonic part,
and

(iii)

compatible with the
L^{\infty}
oscillation on the exact part,

which is precisely the combination defining Banyaga’s Hofer-like
length.

We therefore state the equality as a conjecture.

Conjecture.
Under the hypotheses of
Corollary
8
,

\lVert\phi_{1}\times\phi_{2}\rVert_{HL}=\lVert\phi_{1}\rVert_{H}+\lVert\phi_{2}\rVert_{HL}.

Equivalently, on
M_{1}\times M_{2}
with
b_{1}(M_{1})=0
, no
symplectic isotopy coupling the two factors can be strictly shorter
than the sum of its split contributions.

Lemma 10

(Harmonic
1
-forms of
(T^{2},g_{\varepsilon})
)
.

Let
T^{2}=\mathbb{R}^{2}/\mathbb{Z}^{2}
and let

g_{\varepsilon}=\frac{1}{f_{\varepsilon}(y)}\,dx^{2}+f_{\varepsilon}(y)\,dy^{2},\qquad f_{\varepsilon}(y)=1+\varepsilon\cos(2\pi y),\qquad 0<\varepsilon<1.

Then
\det g_{\varepsilon}=1
, the Riemannian volume form is

d\vol_{g_{\varepsilon}}=dx\wedge dy
, and the space of harmonic

1
-forms is

\mathcal{H}^{1}(T^{2},g_{\varepsilon})=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{R}}\bigl\{f_{\varepsilon}(y)\,dy,\;dx\bigr\}.

The two generators are
L^{2}(g_{\varepsilon})
-orthogonal and have
unit
L^{2}
-norm. Their metric duals are

\bigl(f_{\varepsilon}(y)\,dy\bigr)^{\sharp}=\partial_{y},
and
(dx)^{\sharp}=f_{\varepsilon}(y)\,\partial_{x}.

With respect to
\omega=dx\wedge dy
, the
\omega
-duals are

X_{1}=f_{\varepsilon}(y)\,\partial_{x},

X_{2}=-\partial_{y},

and their Lie bracket is

[X_{1},X_{2}]=f_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(y)\,\partial_{x}.

Proof.

Since
g_{\varepsilon}
is diagonal with entries

g_{11}=1/f_{\varepsilon}(y)
and
g_{22}=f_{\varepsilon}(y)
, we
have
\det g_{\varepsilon}=1
, so the Riemannian volume form equals
the coordinate volume form
dx\wedge dy
. The inverse metric is

g_{\varepsilon}^{-1}=f_{\varepsilon}(y)\,\partial_{x}^{2}+\frac{1}{f_{\varepsilon}(y)}\,\partial_{y}^{2}
, and the Hodge star on

1
-forms is

*(dx)=f_{\varepsilon}(y)\,dy,
and
*(dy)=-\frac{1}{f_{\varepsilon}(y)}\,dx.

Closedness and co-closedness of
h_{1}:=f_{\varepsilon}(y)\,dy
.

Since
f_{\varepsilon}
depends only on
y
,

dh_{1}=f_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(y)\,dy\wedge dy=0.

For the co-differential,

*(h_{1})=f_{\varepsilon}(y)\cdot*(dy)=-dx,
and
d\bigl(*(h_{1})\bigr)=d(-dx)=0,

so
\delta h_{1}=-*d*(h_{1})=0
.

Closedness and co-closedness of
h_{2}:=dx
.

Since
dx
has constant coefficients,
dh_{2}=0
. Also

*(h_{2})=*(dx)=f_{\varepsilon}(y)\,dy,
and
d\bigl(*(h_{2})\bigr)=f_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(y)\,dy\wedge dy=0,

so
\delta h_{2}=0
.

Dimension.
By the Hodge theorem,

\dim\mathcal{H}^{1}(T^{2},g_{\varepsilon})=b_{1}(T^{2})=2
. The two forms

h_{1}
and
h_{2}
are linearly independent, because

h_{2}=dx
lies in the subspace spanned by
dx
while

h_{1}=f_{\varepsilon}(y)\,dy
does not. Hence they form a basis.

Orthogonality and unit norm.
The metric is diagonal,
so
\langle dx,f_{\varepsilon}(y)\,dy\rangle_{L^{2}}=0
. Furthermore,

\lVert h_{1}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}=\int_{T^{2}}f_{\varepsilon}(y)^{2}\,g^{22}\,dx\,dy=\int_{0}^{1}f_{\varepsilon}(y)^{2}\cdot\frac{1}{f_{\varepsilon}(y)}\,dy=\int_{0}^{1}f_{\varepsilon}(y)\,dy=1,

because
\int_{0}^{1}\cos(2\pi y)\,dy=0
. Similarly,

\lVert h_{2}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}=\int_{T^{2}}g^{11}\,dx\,dy=\int_{0}^{1}f_{\varepsilon}(y)\,dy=1.

Metric duals.
By definition,

g_{\varepsilon}\bigl((h_{1})^{\sharp},\partial_{y}\bigr)=h_{1}(\partial_{y})=f_{\varepsilon}(y)
, and
g_{\varepsilon}(\partial_{y},\partial_{y})=f_{\varepsilon}(y)
, so
(h_{1})^{\sharp}=\partial_{y}
. Similarly,

g_{\varepsilon}\bigl((h_{2})^{\sharp},\partial_{x}\bigr)=h_{2}(\partial_{x})=1
,
and
g_{\varepsilon}(f_{\varepsilon}(y)\partial_{x},\partial_{x})=\frac{1}{f_{\varepsilon}(y)}\cdot f_{\varepsilon}(y)^{2}=f_{\varepsilon}(y)
, which forces

(h_{2})^{\sharp}=f_{\varepsilon}(y)\partial_{x}
.

\omega
-duals.
With
\omega=dx\wedge dy
, we compute
\iota_{f_{\varepsilon}(y)\partial_{x}}(dx\wedge dy)=f_{\varepsilon}(y)\,dy=h_{1},
and
\iota_{-\partial_{y}}(dx\wedge dy)=dx=h_{2},

so
X_{1}=f_{\varepsilon}(y)\partial_{x}
and
X_{2}=-\partial_{y}
.

Lie bracket.
Direct computation:

[X_{1},X_{2}]=[f_{\varepsilon}(y)\partial_{x},\,-\partial_{y}]=-f_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(y)\partial_{x}\bigl(\partial_{x}\text{ applied to }f_{\varepsilon}(y)\bigr)+f_{\varepsilon}(y)[\partial_{x},-\partial_{y}]=f_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(y)\,\partial_{x}.

The bracket is non-zero whenever
\varepsilon\neq 0
, since

f_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(y)=-2\pi\varepsilon\sin(2\pi y)
.
∎

Remark 7

(Sign convention for the
\omega
-dual)
.

The choice
X_{2}=-\partial_{y}
is forced by the identity

\iota_{\partial_{y}}(dx\wedge dy)=-dx
. With this convention, the
Lie bracket is

[X_{1},X_{2}]=+f_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(y)\,\partial_{x}
, not

-f_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(y)\,\partial_{x}
.
Example
2
uses this sign. If one instead
adopts the convention
X_{2}=+\partial_{y}
, the
\omega
-dual of

h_{2}
becomes
-dx
and the bracket acquires a minus sign;
either convention is internally consistent, but the two must not
be mixed.

Example 2

(A non-vacuous verification of the geometric condition)
.

We exhibit a closed symplectic manifold on which

\mathcal{H}(0)\neq\{\id\}
and the geometric condition

L(\rho)\geq\lVert\rho\rVert_{H}
is verified for an infinite family

\{\rho_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\subset\mathcal{H}(0)\setminus\{\id\}
that
can be written down explicitly.

The manifold.

Let
T^{2}=\mathbb{R}^{2}/\mathbb{Z}^{2}
with the standard symplectic form

\omega=dx\wedge dy
, and let

g_{\varepsilon}=\frac{1}{f_{\varepsilon}(y)}\,dx^{2}+f_{\varepsilon}(y)\,dy^{2},\qquad f_{\varepsilon}(y)=1+\varepsilon\cos(2\pi y),\qquad 0<\varepsilon<1.

Then
(T^{2},\omega,g_{\varepsilon})
is a closed symplectic manifold
with
\det g_{\varepsilon}=1
and Riemannian volume form

\mathrm{vol}_{g_{\varepsilon}}=dx\wedge dy
.

Harmonic
1
-forms.

By Lemma
10
, the space

\mathcal{H}^{1}(T^{2},g_{\varepsilon})
is spanned by

h_{1}:=f_{\varepsilon}(y)\,dy,
and
h_{2}:=dx,

the two generators are
L^{2}(g_{\varepsilon})
-orthonormal, and their

\omega
-duals are

X_{1}=f_{\varepsilon}(y)\,\partial_{x},
and
X_{2}=-\partial_{y},

with Lie bracket
[X_{1},X_{2}]=f_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(y)\,\partial_{x}\neq 0
,
\varepsilon\neq 0.

A family in
\mathcal{H}(0)
.

For
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, let
\rho_{n}
be the time-one map of the flow of

nX_{1}
on
[0,1]
. Since
\dot{x}=nf_{\varepsilon}(y)
and

\dot{y}=0
, we get

\rho_{n}(x,y)=\bigl(x+nf_{\varepsilon}(y),\,y\bigr)=\bigl(x+n\varepsilon\cos(2\pi y),\,y\bigr)\pmod{1}.

The path
\Psi_{n}(t):=\text{flow of }nX_{1}
at time
t
is a
harmonic isotopy from
\id
to
\rho_{n}
, with generator the
constant harmonic form
nh_{1}
. Its flux class is

n[dy]\in H^{1}(T^{2},\mathbb{Z})=\Gamma_{\omega}
, hence trivial in

H^{1}_{dR}(T^{2},\mathbb{R})/\Gamma_{\omega}
. Therefore

\rho_{n}\in\mathcal{H}(0)=\mathcal{H}\cap\Ham
, and
\rho_{n}\neq\id
for

n\neq 0
.

Upper bound on
L(\rho_{n})
.

The harmonic path
\Psi_{n}
gives

L(\rho_{n})\;\leq\;l_{HL}(\Psi_{n})=\int_{0}^{1}\lVert nh_{1}\rVert_{L^{2}}\,dt=|n|.

Lower bound on
L(\rho_{n})
.

Let
\Psi
be an arbitrary harmonic isotopy from
\id
to

\rho_{n}
, with generator

h_{t}=a(t)h_{1}+b(t)h_{2}
, where
a,b\in C^{\infty}([0,1])
. Since

[X_{1},X_{2}]\neq 0
, the family
\{a(t)X_{1}+b(t)X_{2}\}
is not
pointwise commuting, so the time-one map of
\Psi
is not simply

\exp\bigl(\int_{0}^{1}(aX_{1}+bX_{2})\,dt\bigr)
; one must solve the
ODE

\dot{x}=a(t)\,f_{\varepsilon}(y),\qquad\dot{y}=-b(t),\qquad(x(0),y(0))=(x,y).

Setting
B(t):=\int_{0}^{t}b(s)\,ds
, the solution is

\phi_{1}(x,y)=\Bigl(x+\int_{0}^{1}a(s)f_{\varepsilon}\bigl(y-B(s)\bigr)\,ds,\;y-B(1)\Bigr).

Since
\phi_{1}=\rho_{n}(x,y)=(x+n\varepsilon\cos(2\pi y),\,y)
, the
y
-coordinate gives
B(1)\in\mathbb{Z}
; while the
x
-coordinate gives, for all
y
,

\int_{0}^{1}a(s)f_{\varepsilon}\bigl(y-B(s)\bigr)\,ds-n\varepsilon\cos(2\pi y)\in\mathbb{Z}.

The left-hand side is continuous in
y
and integer-valued, hence
constant, say equal to
m\in\mathbb{Z}
:

\int_{0}^{1}a(s)f_{\varepsilon}\bigl(y-B(s)\bigr)\,ds=n\varepsilon\cos(2\pi y)+m.

Expanding
f_{\varepsilon}=1+\varepsilon\cos(2\pi\cdot)
and using
the addition formula for
\cos
, we obtain the three scalar
identities

\int_{0}^{1}a(s)\,ds=m,\qquad\int_{0}^{1}a(s)\cos(2\pi B(s))\,ds=n,\qquad\int_{0}^{1}a(s)\sin(2\pi B(s))\,ds=0.

The second identity gives

|n|=\lvert\int_{0}^{1}a(s)\cos(2\pi B(s))\,ds\rvert\leq\int_{0}^{1}|a(s)|\,ds\leq l_{HL}(\Psi),

where the last inequality uses

\lVert h_{t}\rVert_{L^{2}}=\sqrt{a(t)^{2}+b(t)^{2}}
(the
h_{j}
being
orthonormal). Taking the infimum over all harmonic isotopies

\Psi
from
\id
to
\rho_{n}
,

L(\rho_{n})\geq|n|.

Combined with the upper bound,
L(\rho_{n})=|n|.

Computation of
\lVert\rho_{n}\rVert_{H}
.

The normalized Hamiltonian

H_{n}(x,y):=\tfrac{n\varepsilon}{2\pi}\sin(2\pi y)

generates
\rho_{n}
, since

\iota_{X_{H_{n}}}\omega=n\varepsilon\cos(2\pi y)\,dy
and the
corresponding flow is

(x,y)\mapsto(x+tn\varepsilon\cos(2\pi y),\,y)
. Hence,
\lVert\rho_{n}\rVert_{H}\;\leq\;\operatorname{osc}(H_{n})=\frac{|n|\varepsilon}{\pi}.

Verification.

For every
n\neq 0
,

L(\rho_{n})\;=\;|n|\;>\;\frac{|n|\varepsilon}{\pi}\;\geq\;\lVert\rho_{n}\rVert_{H},

since
0<\varepsilon<1<\pi
. Thus the geometric condition

L(\rho)\geq\lVert\rho\rVert_{H}
holds on the infinite family

\{\rho_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\subset\mathcal{H}(0)\setminus\{\id\}
. The manifold
(T^{2},\omega,g_{\varepsilon})
is therefore a closed
symplectic manifold of positive first Betti number on which

\mathcal{H}(0)
is non-trivial and the geometric condition admits a
non-vacuous verification. Whether the condition holds for

every

\rho\in\mathcal{H}(0)
on this manifold is an open
question.

Remark 8

(Mixed geodesics in the rigid-flux case)
.

Assume that for some flux class
\gamma
the harmonic locus

\mathcal{H}(\gamma)
is a singleton
\{\rho_{\gamma}\}
. Then the
semidirect-product formula becomes exact:

\lVert\phi\rVert_{HL}=L(\rho_{\gamma})+\lVert\phi\rho_{\gamma}^{-1}\rVert_{H}.

A minimizing path from
\id
to
\phi
is obtained by concatenating a
minimizing harmonic isotopy
\Psi
from
\id
to
\rho_{\gamma}
and a
minimizing Hamiltonian isotopy
\mathrm{M}
from
\id
to

\phi\rho_{\gamma}^{-1}
. The concatenation
\mathrm{M}\ast_{l}\Psi
has length

l_{HL}(\mathrm{M}\ast_{l}\Psi)=l_{H}(\mathrm{M})+l_{HL}(\Psi)=\lVert\phi\rho_{\gamma}^{-1}\rVert_{H}+L(\rho_{\gamma})=\lVert\phi\rVert_{HL},

so it is a geodesic.

3.4.
Iteration of the clean-split formula

Assume throughout the rigid-flux case (Corollary
7
),
so that for every flux class

\gamma\in H^{1}_{dR}(M,\mathbb{R})/\Gamma_{\omega}
the harmonic locus

\mathcal{H}(\gamma)
is a singleton
\{\rho_{\gamma}\}
. Write

\phi=\rho_{\gamma}\,h,\qquad h:=\phi\,\rho_{\gamma}^{-1}\in\Ham(M,\omega),\qquad\Flux(\phi)=\gamma.

By Corollary
7
,

(3.11)

\lVert\phi\rVert_{HL}=L(\rho_{\gamma})+\lVert\phi\,\rho_{\gamma}^{-1}\rVert_{H}=L(\rho_{\gamma})+\lVert h\rVert_{H}.

Iterating, we insert
\rho_{\gamma}^{-k}\rho_{\gamma}^{k}
between the

n
factors of
\phi=\rho_{\gamma}h
:

\phi^{\,n}=\rho_{\gamma}^{\,n}\,h_{n},\qquad h_{n}:=\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}h^{\rho_{\gamma}^{\,k}}=h^{\rho_{\gamma}^{\,n-1}}h^{\rho_{\gamma}^{\,n-2}}\cdots h^{\rho_{\gamma}}\,h,

where
h^{\rho_{\gamma}^{k}}=\rho_{\gamma}^{-k}\,h\,\rho_{\gamma}^{k}\in\Ham(M,\omega)
.

Proposition 7

(Iteration formula)
.

For every
n\geq 1
,

(3.12)

\lVert\phi^{\,n}\rVert_{HL}=L(\rho_{n\gamma})+\lVert h_{n}\rVert_{H}.

Proof.

The identity follows by induction on
n
from
(
3.11
) applied to
\phi^{n}
, whose flux class is

n\gamma
. The identity
\rho_{\gamma}^{n}=\rho_{n\gamma}
used below
holds because
\mathcal{H}(\gamma)
is a singleton (by hypothesis) and the
time-one map of the
n
-fold iteration of any harmonic isotopy with
flux
\gamma
is a harmonic diffeomorphism with flux
n\gamma
; by
uniqueness, it coincides with
\rho_{n\gamma}
. The case
n=1
is
(
3.11
). For the induction step, write

\phi^{n+1}=\phi^{n}\cdot\phi
and apply
(
3.11
) with
\phi
replaced by

\phi^{n+1}
; the harmonic factor is

\rho_{(n+1)\gamma}=\rho_{n\gamma}\,\rho_{\gamma}
and the residue is

h_{n+1}=h_{n}^{\rho_{\gamma}}\,h
.
∎

Corollary 10

(Translation length)
.

Suppose the limits

\tau_{\mathrm{skew}}(h):=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\lVert h_{n}\rVert_{H}}{n},\qquad\ell:=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{L(\rho_{n\gamma})}{n}

exist in
[0,+\infty]
. Then

\tau_{HL}(\phi):=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\lVert\phi^{\,n}\rVert_{HL}}{n}=\ell+\tau_{\mathrm{skew}}(h)\;\geq\;\lVert\gamma\rVert_{H^{1}/\Gamma_{\omega}},

with equality if and only if

\ell=\lVert\gamma\rVert_{H^{1}/\Gamma_{\omega}}
and

\tau_{\mathrm{skew}}(h)=0
.

Proposition 8

(Residue ratio)
.

For every
n\geq 1
, set

A_{n}:=\lVert\phi^{\,n}\rVert_{HL}/\lVert h^{n}\rVert_{H}
. Then

(3.13)

\frac{n\,\lVert\gamma\rVert_{H^{1}/\Gamma_{\omega}}+\lVert h_{n}\rVert_{H}}{\lVert h^{n}\rVert_{H}}\;\leq\;A_{n}\;\leq\;\frac{n\,L(\rho_{\gamma})+\lVert h_{n}\rVert_{H}}{\lVert h^{n}\rVert_{H}}.

If the limits
\ell
and
\tau_{H}(h):=\lim_{n}\lVert h^{n}\rVert_{H}/n
exist
with
\tau_{H}(h)>0
, then

A_{n}\;\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}\;\frac{\ell+\tau_{\mathrm{skew}}(h)}{\tau_{H}(h)}.

If
\tau_{H}(h)=0
, then
A_{n}\to+\infty
whenever
\ell>0
.

Proof.

The identity for
A_{n}
is immediate from

h=\phi\rho_{\gamma}^{-1}
. The sandwich follows from
Proposition
7
together with

n\lVert\gamma\rVert_{H^{1}/\Gamma_{\omega}}\leq L(\rho_{n\gamma})\leq n\,L(\rho_{\gamma})
. Dividing by
n
and letting
n\to\infty

gives the limit; the last claim is immediate since the numerator grows
at least linearly while the denominator is
o(n)
.
∎

Proposition 9

(Harmonic ratio)
.

For every
n\geq 1
, set

B_{n}:=\lVert\phi^{\,n}\rVert_{HL}/\lVert\rho_{\gamma}^{n}\rVert_{HL}
. Since

\rho_{\gamma}^{n}=\rho_{n\gamma}
and

\lVert\rho_{n\gamma}\rVert_{HL}=L(\rho_{n\gamma})
,

(3.14)

B_{n}=1+\frac{\lVert h_{n}\rVert_{H}}{L(\rho_{n\gamma})}.

In particular
B_{n}>1
for every
n\geq 1
, and

1+\frac{\lVert h_{n}\rVert_{H}}{n\,L(\rho_{\gamma})}\;\leq\;B_{n}\;\leq\;1+\frac{\lVert h_{n}\rVert_{H}}{n\,\lVert\gamma\rVert_{H^{1}/\Gamma_{\omega}}}.

If
\ell>0
and
\tau_{\mathrm{skew}}(h)<\infty
, then

B_{n}\to 1+\tau_{\mathrm{skew}}(h)/\ell
. If moreover
h
commutes
with
\rho_{\gamma}
, so that
h_{n}=h^{n}
, then

\tau_{\mathrm{skew}}(h)=\tau_{H}(h)
and

B_{n}\to 1+\tau_{H}(h)/\lVert\gamma\rVert_{H^{1}/\Gamma_{\omega}}
.

Proof.

Equation (
3.14
) is Proposition
7
divided
by
L(\rho_{n\gamma})
. The sandwich follows from the bounds on

L(\rho_{n\gamma})
. Dividing numerator and denominator by
n
and
letting
n\to\infty
gives the limit. The commuting case is immediate
from
h_{n}=h^{n}
.
∎

Remark 9

(Two limiting regimes)
.

Propositions
8
and
9
detect the two
extreme behaviours of the skew product
\phi=\rho_{\gamma}h
.

(i)

Pure flux growth.
If
\tau_{H}(h)=0
and the clean split
holds, then
\lVert\phi^{\,n}\rVert_{HL}\sim n\lVert\gamma\rVert_{H^{1}/\Gamma_{\omega}}
,
A_{n}\to+\infty
,

B_{n}\to 1
.

(ii)

Balanced flux–residue growth.
If
h
commutes with

\rho_{\gamma}
, the clean split holds, and
\tau_{H}(h)>0
, then

\lVert\phi^{\,n}\rVert_{HL}\sim n\bigl(\lVert\gamma\rVert_{H^{1}/\Gamma_{\omega}}+\tau_{H}(h)\bigr),\quad A_{n}\to 1+\frac{\lVert\gamma\rVert_{H^{1}/\Gamma_{\omega}}}{\tau_{H}(h)},\quad B_{n}\to 1+\frac{\tau_{H}(h)}{\lVert\gamma\rVert_{H^{1}/\Gamma_{\omega}}}.

In both regimes
B_{n}
is bounded, whereas
A_{n}
is bounded if and
only if the residue grows at least linearly. Thus
B_{n}
is the

stabler
of the two ratios.

Corollary 11

(Coarse rigidity of the flux term)
.

With the notation of this subsection,

\tau_{HL}(\phi)=\lVert\gamma\rVert_{H^{1}/\Gamma_{\omega}}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\tau_{\mathrm{skew}}(h)=0\ \text{and}\ L(\rho_{n\gamma})=n\lVert\gamma\rVert_{H^{1}/\Gamma_{\omega}}\ \text{for all }n.

4.
Consequences of our study

We now record several consequences, drawing on analogous results in
Hamiltonian dynamics.

Definition 13
.

The symplectic displacement energy
e_{s}(U)
of a non-empty subset

U\subset M
is

e_{s}(U)=\inf\{\lVert\phi\rVert_{HL}^{(1,\infty)}\mid\phi\in G_{\omega}(M),\ \phi(U)\cap U=\emptyset\}

if some element of
G_{\omega}(M)
displaces
U
, and

e_{s}(U)=+\infty
otherwise.

If
U\subset V
, then
e_{s}(U)\leq e_{s}(V)
.

Corollary 12

(Non-degeneracy of the Hofer-like norm)
.

Let
(M,\omega)
be a closed symplectic manifold. If

\phi\in G_{\omega}(M)
satisfies
\lVert\phi\rVert_{HL}=0
,
then
\phi\in\Ham(M,\omega)
and
\phi=\id
.

Proof.

Assume
\phi\in G_{\omega}(M)
with
\lVert\phi\rVert_{HL}=0
. For every

\epsilon>0
, choose a symplectic isotopy

\Xi_{\epsilon}\in\Iso(\phi)_{\omega}
from
\id
to
\phi
such
that
l_{HL}(\Xi_{\epsilon})<\epsilon
. Write the harmonic part of its
generator as
h_{t}^{\epsilon}\in\mathcal{H}^{1}(M,g)
and set

\alpha_{\epsilon}:=\int_{0}^{1}h_{t}^{\epsilon}\,dt\in H^{1}_{dR}(M,\mathbb{R}).

Since
\Xi_{\epsilon}
connects
\id
to
\phi
and the pullback

\phi_{t}^{*}
acts trivially on
H^{1}_{dR}(M,\mathbb{R})
, the class

\alpha_{\epsilon}
is a lift of
\Flux(\phi)
under the quotient
map
\pi^{\prime}\colon H^{1}_{dR}(M,\mathbb{R})\to H^{1}_{dR}(M,\mathbb{R})/\Gamma_{\omega}
, and
by Minkowski’s integral inequality,

(4.1)

\lVert\alpha_{\epsilon}\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq\int_{0}^{1}\lVert h_{t}^{\epsilon}\rVert_{L^{2}}\,dt\leq l_{HL}(\Xi_{\epsilon})<\epsilon.

Since
\Gamma_{\omega}
is discrete, there exists

\delta=\delta(M,\omega,g)>0
such that

\gamma\in\Gamma_{\omega}\ \text{and}\ \lVert\gamma\rVert_{L^{2}}<\delta\implies\gamma=0.

The lifts are eventually constant.
Fix

\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2}\in(0,\delta/2)
. Both
\alpha_{\epsilon_{1}}

and
\alpha_{\epsilon_{2}}
are lifts of
\Flux(\phi)
under

\pi^{\prime}
, hence their difference lies in the kernel of
\pi^{\prime}
:

\alpha_{\epsilon_{1}}-\alpha_{\epsilon_{2}}\in\ker\pi^{\prime}=\Gamma_{\omega}.

By (
4.1
) and the triangle inequality,

\lVert\alpha_{\epsilon_{1}}-\alpha_{\epsilon_{2}}\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq\lVert\alpha_{\epsilon_{1}}\rVert_{L^{2}}+\lVert\alpha_{\epsilon_{2}}\rVert_{L^{2}}<\frac{\delta}{2}+\frac{\delta}{2}=\delta.

Applying
(\star)
with

\gamma=\alpha_{\epsilon_{1}}-\alpha_{\epsilon_{2}}\in\Gamma_{\omega}

gives
\alpha_{\epsilon_{1}}=\alpha_{\epsilon_{2}}
. Hence the family

\{\alpha_{\epsilon}\}_{\epsilon\in(0,\delta/2)}
is constant; write

\alpha
for its common value.

The constant is zero.
By
(
4.1
) and the definition of
\alpha
,

\lVert\alpha\rVert_{L^{2}}=\lVert\alpha_{\epsilon}\rVert_{L^{2}}<\epsilon

for every
\epsilon\in(0,\delta/2).

Letting
\epsilon\to 0
gives
\lVert\alpha\rVert_{L^{2}}=0
, hence

\alpha=0\in H^{1}_{dR}(M,\mathbb{R})
.

Consequences.
Since
\alpha_{\epsilon}=\alpha=0
is a
lift of
\Flux(\phi)
, we get
\Flux(\phi)=0
in

H^{1}_{dR}(M,\mathbb{R})/\Gamma_{\omega}
, so
\phi\in\Ham(M,\omega)
.
Moreover, for small
\epsilon
the isotopy
\Xi_{\epsilon}
has
flux lift
\widetilde{\Flux}(\Xi_{\epsilon})=[\alpha_{\epsilon}]=0
, so

\Xi_{\epsilon}
is a zero-flux path from
\id
to
\phi
, i.e.
\Xi_{\epsilon}\in\mathcal{Z}_{\phi}
.
Applying Lemma
7
with
\Phi=\Xi_{\epsilon}
and the
constant loop
\Psi
, and using that
\Xi_{\epsilon}
and
\Psi

have the same (zero) flux, we obtain

\lVert\phi\rVert_{H}\leq\Bigl(1+3L_{0}\sup_{(s,t,x)\in[0,1]^{2}\times M}\lVert V^{\Xi_{\epsilon}}_{s,t}(x)\rVert_{g}\Bigr)\,l_{HL}(\Xi_{\epsilon}).

By Lemma
5
, there exist constants

C_{0},C_{1}>0
depending only on
(M,\omega,g)
such that

\sup_{(s,t,x)\in[0,1]^{2}\times M}\lVert V^{\Xi_{\epsilon}}_{s,t}(x)\rVert_{g}\leq C_{0}\bigl(e^{C_{1}\epsilon}-1\bigr).

Therefore

\lVert\phi\rVert_{H}\leq\Bigl(1+3L_{0}\,C_{0}\bigl(e^{C_{1}\epsilon}-1\bigr)\Bigr)\,\epsilon\;\xrightarrow[\epsilon\to 0]{}\;0.

Hence
\lVert\phi\rVert_{H}=0
. By non-degeneracy of the Hofer norm on

\Ham(M,\omega)
,
\phi=\id
.
∎

Corollary 13

(Stability of
C^{0}
-limits)
.

Let
(M,\omega)
be a closed symplectic manifold. Let

\{\phi_{i}^{t}\}
be a sequence of symplectic isotopies,
\{\psi^{t}\}

another symplectic isotopy, and
\phi\colon M\to M
a map such that

(\phi_{i}^{1})
converges uniformly to
\phi
and

l_{HL}^{(1,\infty)}(\{\psi^{t}\}^{-1}\circ\{\phi_{i}^{t}\})\to 0

as
i\to\infty
. Then
\phi=\psi^{1}
.

Proof.

For each
i
, define the symplectic isotopy

\Xi_{i}:=\{\psi^{t}\}^{-1}\circ\{\phi_{i}^{t}\},

which starts at
\id
and terminates at

\eta_{i}:=(\psi^{1})^{-1}\circ\phi_{i}^{1}.

By hypothesis,
l_{HL}(\Xi_{i})\to 0
, and

\eta_{i}\to\eta:=(\psi^{1})^{-1}\circ\phi
uniformly on
M
.

Step 1: Banyaga’s decomposition of
\Xi_{i}
.
By
Banyaga’s decomposition
[
1
]
, the isotopy
\Xi_{i}
factors
as

\Xi_{i}=\rho_{i}\circ\mu_{i},

where
\rho_{i}
is a harmonic isotopy and
\mu_{i}
is a
Hamiltonian isotopy, and the Hofer-like length is additive:

l_{HL}(\Xi_{i})=l_{HL}(\rho_{i})+l_{H}(\mu_{i}).

Since
l_{HL}(\Xi_{i})\to 0
, both
l_{HL}(\rho_{i})\to 0
and

l_{H}(\mu_{i})\to 0
. Write
\rho_{i}^{1}
,
\mu_{i}^{1}
for the
time-one maps, so that

\eta_{i}=\rho_{i}^{1}\circ\mu_{i}^{1}.

Step 2: the harmonic factor is
C^{0}
-small.
Let

\mathcal{H}_{t}^{\rho_{i}}:=\iota_{X_{t}^{\rho_{i}}}\omega
be the harmonic
generator of
\rho_{i}
, so that

l_{HL}(\rho_{i})=\int_{0}^{1}\lVert X_{t}^{\rho_{i}}\rVert_{L^{2}}\,dt\;\xrightarrow[i\to\infty]{}\;0.

Because
\mathcal{H}^{1}(M,g)
is finite-dimensional, there exists a
constant
C_{g}>0
depending only on
(M,g)
such that

\lVert\alpha\rVert_{C^{0}}\leq C_{g}\lVert\alpha\rVert_{L^{2}}
for every
harmonic
1
-form
\alpha
. Therefore

\int_{0}^{1}\lVert X_{t}^{\rho_{i}}\rVert_{C^{0}}\,dt\;\leq\;C_{g}\,l_{HL}(\rho_{i})\;\xrightarrow[i\to\infty]{}\;0.

Setting
\epsilon_{i}:=C_{g}\,l_{HL}(\rho_{i})\to 0
, we obtain

d\bigl(\rho_{i}^{1}(x),\,x\bigr)\;\leq\;\int_{0}^{1}\lVert X_{t}^{\rho_{i}}\rVert_{C^{0}}\,dt\;\leq\;\epsilon_{i}\qquad\text{for every }x\in M.

So
\rho_{i}^{1}\to\id
uniformly on
M
as
i\to\infty
.

Step 3: the Hamiltonian factor converges
C^{0}
.

Since
\eta_{i}=\rho_{i}^{1}\circ\mu_{i}^{1}
, we have

\mu_{i}^{1}=(\rho_{i}^{1})^{-1}\circ\eta_{i}.

By hypothesis
\eta_{i}\to\eta
uniformly, and by Step 2

(\rho_{i}^{1})^{-1}\to\id
uniformly (since
\rho_{i}^{1}\to\id
).
Therefore

\mu_{i}^{1}\;\xrightarrow[i\to\infty]{}\;\eta
uniformly on
M.

Step 4:
\eta=\id
.
Since

\mu_{i}^{1}\in\Ham(M,\omega)
and
l_{H}(\mu_{i})\to 0
, we have

\lVert\mu_{i}^{1}\rVert_{H}\;\leq\;l_{H}(\mu_{i})\;\xrightarrow[i\to\infty]{}\;0.

By the
C^{0}
-continuity of the Hofer norm at the identity on

\Ham(M,\omega)
(see Oh
[
28
]
and
Humilière-Leclercq-Seyfaddini
[
26
]
, see also
Müller
[
27
]
), a sequence of Hamiltonian

Conclusion

Buss and Leclercq
[
4
, Proposition 2.6]
proved that their
family of pseudo-distances
d_{(\mu,c)}
on

\mathrm{Symp}(M,\omega)
has Hamiltonian degeneracy when

c\neq 0
: namely,
d_{(\mu,c)}(\id,\phi)=0
if and only if

\phi\in\Ham(M,\omega)
. Their argument also shows that the
restriction of
d_{(\mu,c)}
to
\Ham(M,\omega)
induces the same
topology as the Hofer metric. The present paper strengthens this in two directions. First, the
semidirect-product formula of Theorem
1
expresses the
Hofer-like norm of a general element of
G_{\omega}(M)
as an infimum
over the harmonic locus plus a Hamiltonian residue; specialising to
zero flux gives the geometric criterion

L(\rho)\geq\lVert\rho\rVert_{H}
,
\rho\in\mathcal{H}(0)
, under which the
inclusion of the Hamiltonian group into the identity component is an
isometric embedding (Theorem
2
). Second, under the explicit
boundedness hypothesis (H
K
) on the two-parameter vector-field
family attached to zero-flux paths, Theorem
3
gives a
bi-Lipschitz bound with explicit distortion constant
1+3L_{0}K
. The sharpness condition and hypothesis (H
K
) are logically
independent, and the paper keeps them separate.

Sharpness.
The condition

L(\rho)\geq\lVert\rho\rVert_{H}
on
\mathcal{H}(0)
is equivalent, by
Theorem
2
, to the isometry of the inclusion

\bigl(\Ham(M,\omega),\lVert\cdot\rVert_{H}\bigr)\hookrightarrow\bigl(G_{\omega}(M),\lVert\cdot\rVert_{HL}\bigr).

When it fails, the inclusion is not isometric; the two norms may
nevertheless be bi-Lipschitz equivalent with some constant
C>1
.

Bi-Lipschitz property.
This is controlled separately
by hypothesis (H
K
). Theorem
3
shows that (H
K
)
implies bi-Lipschitz equivalence with
C=1+3L_{0}K
. On every closed
symplectic manifold, the
local
version of (H
K
) holds on
each Hofer ball

B_{R}:=\{\phi\in\Ham(M,\omega):\lVert\phi\rVert_{H}\leq R\},

with
K(R)=\exp(2C_{\mathrm{geom}}R)-1
, by
Lemma
5
and the admissibility condition

l_{HL}(\Xi)\leq 2\lVert\phi\rVert_{H}
. Consequently, on every closed
symplectic manifold the inclusion is bi-Lipschitz on each Hofer ball

B_{R}
, with distortion at most

1+3L_{0}K(R)
, a bound that grows exponentially with
R
but is
finite for every fixed
R
. Whether (H
K
) holds
globally
, i.e. whether
K
can be
chosen independent of the Hofer radius, is a separate question from
the sharpness condition. It reduces to whether

\sup\Bigl\{\sup_{(s,t,x)}\lVert V^{\Xi}_{s,t}(x)\rVert_{g}\Bigr\}\;<\;+\infty,

over all
\phi\in\Ham(M,\omega)
and all

\Xi\in\mathcal{Z}_{\phi}
with

l_{HL}(\Xi)\leq 2\lVert\phi\rVert_{H}
. Neither condition implies the
other: the sharpness condition is stronger than bi-Lipschitz
equivalence (it gives isometry, i.e.
C=1
), whereas (H
K
)
is a statement about the two-parameter family
V^{\Xi}_{s,t}
, which seems
independent of the harmonic locus
\mathcal{H}(0)
.

The two open questions can be phrased concretely on the test cases
introduced in the paper. On
(T^{2},\omega,g_{\varepsilon})

(Example
2
), the sharpness condition is verified
on the infinite family
\{\rho_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
but is not
known to hold on all of
\mathcal{H}(0)
. On products

N\times T^{2k}
with
N
simply connected
(Proposition
6
), the harmonic locus is a
singleton in every flux class, and the semidirect-product formula
becomes the exact identity

\lVert\phi\rVert_{HL}=L(\rho_{\gamma})+\lVert\phi\rho_{\gamma}^{-1}\rVert_{H}.

The geometric content of the constant
1+3L_{0}K
is the following.
It measures the maximal distortion between the Hofer norm and the
Hofer-like energy on the Hamiltonian subgroup, uniformly over all
Hamiltonian diffeomorphisms. It equals
1
precisely when the
harmonic directions do not shorten Hofer distances, i.e. when

L(\rho)\geq\lVert\rho\rVert_{H}
for every
\rho\in\mathcal{H}(0)
.
Whether the constant can be taken equal to
1
on a given manifold
is equivalent, by Theorem
2
, to the sharpness condition.
The paper provides two verifications of this condition: the
Ricci non-negative case of Proposition
5
,
covering flat tori and Calabi–Yau manifolds, and the non-vacuous
family on
(T^{2},\omega,g_{\varepsilon})
of
Example
2
. Whether the sharpness condition holds
unconditionally on every closed symplectic manifold, or
equivalently whether there exist
(M,\omega,g)
with
b_{1}(M)>0

and
\rho\in\mathcal{H}(0)\setminus\{\id\}
satisfying

L(\rho)<\lVert\rho\rVert_{H}
, remains open.

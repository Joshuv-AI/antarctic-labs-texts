LINC00886 inhibits the proliferation, migration, and invasion of NSCLC cells by sponging miR-141-5p
Authors: Xiang Liang, Xin Guan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveTo investigate the impact of LINC00886 on the prognosis of patients with non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) and its regulatory effects on cancer cells.MethodsA total of 184 NSCLC patients were enrolled. Postoperative tissues were preserved in liquid nitrogen. Kaplan - Meier (KM) survival curves were used to assess 5-year overall survival. Multivariate cox regression analysis was performed to identify independent prognostic factors for NSCLC. RT-qPCR detected gene expression levels. CCK-8 and Transwell assays evaluated cell proliferation, migration, and invasion. Dual-luciferase reporter assays validated the interaction between LINC00886 and miR-141-5p.ResultsOur study revealed that LINC00886 was markedly downregulated in both NSCLC tissues and cell lines. This reduced expression was significantly associated with larger tumor diameters and advanced TNM stages. Multivariate analysis identified LINC00886 as an independent protective factor for NSCLC patients, with its downregulation portending a lethal outcome. In vitro, ectopic expression of LINC00886 significantly attenuated the proliferation, migration, and invasion of NSCLC cells. We further identified miR-141-5p, which was upregulated in NSCLC, as a direct target gene of LINC00886. Critically, rescue experiments demonstrated that miR-141-5p mimics could abrogate the inhibition of LINC00886 overexpression on NSCLC cell function.ConclusionLINC00886 inhibits NSCLC cell proliferation, migration, and invasion through miR-141-5p, thereby suppressing NSCLC.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12672-026-04955-9.

Introduction

Lung cancer is the second most common cancer worldwide [1], with non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) accounting for approximately 85% of all cases [2]. Factors such as age, genetics, smoking, and lung diseases are reported to contribute to the development of NSCLC [3]. Statistical data indicate that the recent rise in the incidence of stage I lung cancer is primarily attributable to an increase in NSCLC cases [4]. Xu et al. [5]. reported that the five-year survival rate for NSCLC patients is as low as 10%. For early-stage NSCLC, surgical resection can improve patient prognosis [6]; however, prognosis following surgery is often poor in many instances [7]. Moreover, studies have shown that low-dose chest computed tomography (CT) can reduce lung cancer mortality [8]. Targeted therapy and immunotherapy can improve patient survival rates [9, 10]. Nevertheless, access to these treatments remains limited in certain regions and countries. Although imaging techniques offer high sensitivity for NSCLC detection [11], the issue of overdiagnosis cannot be overlooked. In this context, the identification of biomarkers capable of predicting and monitoring disease progression may offer valuable insights to inform and enhance therapeutic strategies.

The regulatory role and diagnostic value of non-coding RNA (ncRNA) in NSCLC have been increasingly demonstrated by numerous studies [12, 13]. In patients with NSCLC, ncRNAs contribute to disease progression by acting either as biomarkers or as regulatory molecules. For example, Radu et al. [14] found that microRNAs (miRNAs) can contribute to cancer progression by affecting macrophage polarization. Tang et al. [15] reported that long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) have potential as diagnostic molecular markers for NSCLC. Song et al. [16] found that some exosome lncRNAs evidenced excellent value in NSCLC diagnosis. LINC00886 is a 24.8 kb lncRNA molecule located at the q25.31 locus on human chromosome 3. Its distinct structural features and expression patterns suggest potential utility as both a drug target and a biomarker. Emerging research indicates that LINC00886 is involved in the progression and regulation of various cancer types [17, 18]. For instance, LINC00886 downregulation leads to reduced survival in renal cell carcinoma patients with immune dysfunction [19]. Furthermore, LINC00886 expression is reduced in ovarian cancer with peritoneal metastasis [9]. In vitro studies have further shown that LINC00886 negatively regulates the proliferation, migration, and invasion of thyroid cancer cells [20]. These studies suggest LINC00886 may play a crucial role in regulating cancer cell proliferation, differentiation, and metastasis. Notably, a significant increase in LINC00886 levels was observed in NSCLC patients following treatment with XAV939 [21], indicating that LINC00886 may represent a promising therapeutic target in NSCLC. The above research is crucial for elucidating another pathogenesis of NSCLC and identifying therapeutic targets. However, current studies lack in-depth mechanisms and clinical research on LINC00886’s involvement in NSCLC. In addition, the validation of the new diagnostic methods in clinical cohorts is still at the experimental stage [22]. Furthermore, their multivariable prognostic models [23] have not yet been evaluated in independent cohorts. Therefore, our study aims to conduct preliminary exploration into the mechanisms by which LINC00886 participates in NSCLC.

This study included 184 patients with NSCLC to evaluate the prognostic value of LINC00886 and its functional role in regulating NSCLC cell behavior via miR-141-5p.

Materials and methods

Patient enrollment

Sample size was calculated using PASS software (α = 0.05, power = 80%). Based on preliminary data, a hazard ratio (HR) of 1.8 was anticipated for overall survival according to LINC00886 expression levels. With a 12-month recruitment period and median survival estimates, 83 patients per group were required. Accounting for a 10% loss to follow-up, the final sample size was set at 92 per group, totaling 184 patients. They were patients hospitalized at Shanghai Ninth People’s Hospital, Shanghai Jiao Tong University, School of Medicine from June 2018 to December 2019. Patient lesions were observed through chest CT, including tumor location, size, and metastasis status. All patients underwent a 5-year follow-up for prognosis. Intraoperative pathological slides were jointly evaluated by two or more oncology specialists. The patient’s NSCLC and adjacent normal tissue were preserved in liquid nitrogen. During formal experiments, we extracted RNA from all cryopreserved tissues for detection. Notably, the patient had not received any prior treatment before surgery, ensuring that LINC00886 expression levels accurately represent tumor initiation, progression, and the patient’s baseline condition. According to the standards of the International Union Against Cancer and the American Joint Committee on Cancer, the TNM stages of the patients ranged from I to IV. The inclusion criteria for patients considered the following factors: (1) adult age; (2) no history of drug or surgical treatment; (3) the presence of at least one lesion measurable by imaging. However, patients with small cell lung cancer, autoimmune diseases, or other malignancies, pregnant or lactating women, and patients with cardiopulmonary or hepatic-renal dysfunction were excluded from this study. The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Shanghai Ninth People’s Hospital, Shanghai Jiao Tong University, School of Medicine, and patients or their legal guardians signed written informed consent in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki.

Cell culture

Human bronchial epithelial cells (BEAS-2B) and NSCLC cell lines (A549, H1299, HCC827, H1975) were obtained from the Chinese Academy of Sciences Cell Bank. BEAS-2B, H1299, HCC827, and H-1975 were cultured in RPMI-1640 medium (11875-093, Invitrogen, U.S.), while A549 was grown in Ham’s F-12 K medium (21127022, Gibco, U.S.). Complete culture media for these cells also contained 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS, Gibco, U.S.) and 1% penicillin-streptomycin (60162ES76, YEASEN, Shanghai). Additionally, the H1299 medium was supplemented with 2 mM glutamine (35050061, Invitrogen, U.S.). Cells were cultured at 37 °C in a 5% CO2 atmosphere.

Cell transfection

Cells were transfected using Lipo8000 reagent (C0533FT, Beyotime, Shanghai). Cells were seeded in 12-well plates 24 h prior to transfection. At 70% confluence, medium was replaced, and transfection complexes (1 µg oligonucleotides + 1.6 µL Lipo8000 in 50 µL serum-free medium) were added. Cells were incubated for 24 h before further analysis.

RT-qPCR

Tissues were ground using the Shanghai Jingxin low-temperature tissue grinder (JXFSTPRP-CLN-48L). Total RNA was extracted with the QIAwave RNA Mini Kit (74534; QIAGEN, Germany), and RNA concentration and purity were assessed using a spectrophotometer (Germany). For long non-coding RNA (lncRNA) analysis, reverse transcription was performed using the Qiagen reverse transcription kit (205313; QIAGEN, Germany), followed by quantitative detection with the Talent fluorescent quantitative detection kit (FP209; TIANGEN, Beijing, China). For microRNA (miRNA) analysis, reverse transcription was carried out using the miRNA 1st Strand cDNA Synthesis Kit (11148; YEASEN, Shanghai, China), and quantitative detection was conducted with the microRNA quantitative detection kit (Q32881; Invitrogen, USA). Real-time melting curves of the reactions were recorded using the QS3 quantitative PCR instrument (A28567, Thermo Fisher, U.S.). The relative expression levels of genes were calculated using the 2−△△Ct method, with β-actin as the internal reference for LncRNA and U6 as the internal reference for miRNA to normalize the results. The primers related to the reactions are as follows: LINC00886, forward primer (F): 5’- AAGAGAAAGCCACAGGAGGGTA-3’, reverse primer (R), 5’- CAGGTAGCAAGACAGAAGCAGAA-3’. β-actin, F, 5’- TGACGTGGACATCCGCAAAG-3’, R, 5’- CTGGAAGGTGGACAGCGAGG-3’. miR-141-5p, F, 5’- CGCGCATCTTCCAGTACAGT-3’, R. 5’- AGTGCAGGGTCCGAGGTATT-3’. U6, F, 5’- CTCGCTTCGGCAGCACA-3’, R, 5’- AACGCTTCACGAATTTGCGT-3’.

CCK8

The proliferation of NSCLC cells before and after transfection was detected using a cell proliferation and cytotoxicity detection kit (E606335, Sangon, Shanghai). Transfected cells were seeded in 96-well plates (2 × 103 cells/well). When the cell confluence reaches 70% or higher, add 10 µL of CCK8 solution to each well labeled T0 (0 h corresponding to the time when the reagent is first added). Then, incubate in a 37 °C CO2 incubator for 4 h. The absorbance of the solution was measured using a Synergy HTX dual-mode microplate reader with a 450 nm filter. Cell proliferation was observed by measuring the absorbance of the solution in each group at 24 h, 48 h, and 72 h after the first detection.

Transwell

The effects of transfection on cell function were assessed using a cell migration and invasion assay staining kit (G4740, Solarbio, Beijing). The day prior to the experiment, cells were treated with basal medium (serum-free) to induce starvation. For invasion assays, chambers were pre-coated with Matrigel. After incubation, cells were gently washed 3 times with 1× PBS. Subsequently, treat with 1 mL of pre-chilled cell fixative for 20 min, then wash with PBS. Cells were stained with 1 mL of staining reagent mixture (B1:B2 = 1:4) at 37 °C for 15 min, followed by two rinses with distilled water (1 min each). Excess water was gently removed with absorbent paper, and the chambers were excised, air-dried, and mounted with neutral resin. Migrated or invaded cells were observed and imaged in five randomly selected fields per chamber using an MHIL-200 inverted microscope. To minimize observer bias, a blinded quantification method was employed: all images were coded without group identifiers, and an independent investigator unaware of the experimental groups performed cell counting. The counts were subsequently decoded and matched to the corresponding experimental conditions for statistical analysis.

Sub-position analysis

Nuclear and cytoplasmic fractions were isolated using the Cytoplasmic and Nuclear RNA Purification Kit (Cat. 21000, Norgen Biotek Corp., Thorold, Ontario, Canada) Nuclear and according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Briefly, cells were scraped and collected by centrifugation at 300×g for 5 min, then re-suspended in PBS. Take 100 µL (approximately 2 × 106 cells) of the cell suspension into a new tube and centrifuge to collect the pellet. Add 200 µL of Reagent A to the pellet and incubate on ice for 8 min. Add 10 µL of Reagent B, vortex vigorously for 5 s, then incubate on ice for 1 min. Repeat this procedure 3 times. Centrifuge at 4 °C at 1,600 × g for 10 min; the supernatant constitutes the cytoplasmic RNA. Add 200 µL of reagent A to the remaining pellet and resuspend the cells. Centrifuge at 4 °C, 11,200 × g for 5 min, and collect the pellet. Add 100 µL of reagent C, mix well, and vortex for 15 s. Incubate on ice for 40 min, vertexing every 10 min. After incubation, centrifuge the cell lysate; the resulting supernatant is the nuclear RNA.

Dual- luciferase reporter assay

The interaction between genes was validated using a dual-luciferase reporter assay kit (abs60341, Absin, Shanghai) following the manufacturer’s protocol. Collect the transfected cells and wash twice with PBS. Add 120 µL of cell lysis buffer (24-well plate) and incubate with gentle shaking for 15 min. Centrifuge the lysis buffer at 11,180 × g for 5 min and collect the supernatant. Transfer 100 µL of supernatant to an opaque microplate. Add an equal volume of firefly luciferase assay reagent. Measure the luminescence intensity at 560 nm emission using a multi-mode microplate reader. Blank wells received an equal volume of lysis buffer. Next, 100 µL of Renilla luciferase working solution was added to the system, and luminescence was measured using a 480 nm filter. The experimental results were normalized based on the luminescence values of Renilla luciferase.

RNA immunoprecipitation assay (RIP)

Detect the binding relationship between the target gene and the probe gene using the RIP kit (JKR23003, Gene Create, Wuhan). Collect sufficient cells (approximately 2 × 10⁷ cells) into a centrifuge tube and centrifuge to collect the pellet. Add 100 µL RIP lysis buffer and lyse cells using a cell sonicate (in an ice bath). The lysate was centrifuged at 12,000 rpm for 10 min at 4 °C, and the supernatant was collected. For each sample, 30 µL of magnetic bead mixture was added to two separate sterile centrifuge tubes (labeled A and B). Resuspend after washing with 10× volume of RIP Wash Buffer. Add 3 µg of target antibody to tube A and the same amount of isotype IgG to tube B. Incubate at room temperature for 2 h. After standing for 1 min, discard the supernatant and wash. Add 900 µL RIP Buffer and 150 µL cell supernatant to tubes A and B respectively, incubate overnight at 4 °C. Discard the supernatant, wash with wash buffer, and resuspend. An aliquot of 100 µL of the bead suspension was transferred to a new tube, mixed with 100 µL of elution buffer, and boiled. The eluted supernatant was collected, mixed with 1× loading buffer, and stored at − 80 °C for subsequent protein analysis. The remaining bead mixture was used for RNA purification.

Database sources

The downstream target genes of miRNAs were identified using the miRWalk database (http://mirwalk.umm.uni-heidelberg.de/human/mirna/MIMAT0004598/), miRDB database (https://mirdb.org/cgi-bin/search.cgi), and Targetscan database (https://www.targetscan.org/vert_80/). The common genes obtained were intersected with mRNAs predicted by the CTD software (https://ctdbase.org/detail.go? type=disease&acc=MESH%3AD002289&view=gene) as potentially involved in NSCLC to identify overlapping genes. The overlapping genes were input into the STRING website (https://cn.string-db.org/) to construct a protein-protein interaction (PPI) network. Concurrently, functional and signaling pathway enrichment analysis of the overlapping genes was performed using the Metascape software (https://metascape.org/).

Data analysis

To minimize potential batch effects, we took the following measures. Specifically: during the experiments, staff strictly adhered to standard operating procedures (SOP). Each experiment was performed by the same person, and experimental conditions (reagent amounts, reaction temperature, and duration) were kept as consistent as possible between batches. For data analysis, we used the Combat algorithm to correct for batch effects.

The relationship between LncRNA expression and clinical characteristics of NSCLC patients was analyzed using SPSS 25.0. Results from cell experiments were calculated using GraphPad Prism. When grouping cells for experiments, we strictly adhered to the principle of randomization. Furthermore, each group included six replicate wells (biological replicates), and each replicate underwent three independent technical replicates during testing. The Shapiro-Wilk test indicated that the statistical data distribution of our variables approximated a normal distribution (P > 0.05). Therefore, differences between groups were compared using T-tests and analysis of variance, while categorical data were tested using the chi-square test. Kaplan-Meier (KM) curves recorded patients’ prognostic survival. For covariates with P < 0.05 in univariate Cox analysis (with P > 0.05 in Schonfeld residuals global test, satisfying the proportional hazards assumption (PH)), we further validated their independent impact on NSCLC patients through multivariate Cox regression analysis. Furthermore, based on the linear relationship observed in the pre-experiment between LINC00886 expression and NSCLC-related variables (tumor size, TNM staging) (Supplementary Fig. 2), we employed Pearson correlation analysis to examine the expression of LncRNA and miRNA in cancer tissues from NSCLC patients. Only results with P < 0.05 were considered statistically significant.

Results

Relationship between LINC00886 expression and clinicopathological features of NSCLC patients

Experimental results revealed that LINC00886 was significantly downregulated in NSCLC tissues compared to adjacent normal tissues (P < 0.0001, Fig. 1A). Consistently, LINC00886 expression was markedly lower in NSCLC cell lines than in normal human bronchial epithelial cells (BEAS-2B), with the most pronounced reductions observed in A549 and HCC827 cells (P < 0.001, Fig. 1B). According to the manufacturer’s instructions, A549 (KRAS mutation) and HCC827 (EGFR exon 19 deletion mutation) are representative models of two common driver gene mutations in NSCLC, representing the non-targeted therapy-sensitive and EGFR-TKI-sensitive subtypes, respectively. Since subsequent studies will focus on exploring targeted therapies and resistance mechanisms, selecting these two cell lines allows us to cover potential differences under different treatment contexts. Therefore, in subsequent research, we separately investigated the regulatory effects of the target genes on these two cell lines. Additionally, we found that as the tumor diameter increased and TNM stage progressed, LINC00886 expression in patient cancer tissues gradually decreased (P < 0.0001, Fig. 1C and D). Patients were stratified into low- and high-expression groups based on mean LINC00886 levels. Statistical results showed (as shown in Table 1) that the mean age of patients in the low-expression group was 57.84 ± 14.00 years, while that in the high-expression group was 55.62 ± 12.42 years. Patients in the low-expression group demonstrated significantly higher proportions of smokers (P = 0.039), lymph node metastasis (P = 0.005), and more severe TNM staging (P = 0.038) compared to the high-expression group. However, no significant differences were observed between the two groups regarding gender, history of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, history of pulmonary tuberculosis, or tumor size.

The impact of LINC00886 expression on NSCLC patient prognosis

Analysis of follow-up outcomes demonstrated that, deceased patients had significantly lower LINC00886 expression (P < 0.0001, Fig. 2A). Moreover, patients with low expression had a noticeably higher 5-year mortality rate than those with high expression (P < 0.0001, Fig. 2B). Multivariate Cox regression identified LINC00886 as an independent protective factor (HR = 0.487, P = 0.016), while lymph node metastasis was an independent risk factor (HR = 1.760, P = 0.041, Table 2). However, age, gender, smoking, COPD, pulmonary tuberculosis, tumor size, and TNM stage were not notable in evaluating NSCLC prognosis.

Effect of oe-LINC00886 transfection on NSCLC cell functions

To further investigate the role of LINC00886 in NSCLC, we overexpressed LINC00886 in cancer cells by transfecting oe-LINC00886 (P < 0.001, Fig. 3A and B). The results evidenced that the proliferation ability of two NSCLC cell lines (A549 and HCC827) was significantly inhibited after transfection with oe-LINC00886 (P < 0.0001, Fig. 3C and D). In addition, our experimental results also revealed that LINC00886 overexpression markedly reduced the migration and invasion of NSCLC cells (P < 0.001, Fig. 3E and F).

miR-141-5p may be a downstream target gene of LINC00886

LINC00886 was predominantly localized in the cytoplasm (Fig. 4A). Following treatment with Leptomycin B (LMB, a nuclear export inhibitor), LINC00886 accumulated in the nuclei of NSCLC cells, while its cytoplasmic levels were significantly reduced (Supplementary Fig. 3A, 3B). Second, functional assays revealed that LMB treatment further enhanced cancer cell proliferation, migration, and invasion capabilities (Supplementary Fig. 3C-E). This result further demonstrates that cytoplasmic LINC00886 participates in regulating NSCLC cell functions. Bioinformatics predicted a binding site between LINC00886 and miR-141-5p (Fig. 4B), confirmed by dual-luciferase and RIP assays (P < 0.0001, Fig. 4C, D). The results also indicated that miR-141-5p levels were distinctly upregulated in NSCLC tissues compared to control (P < 0.0001, Fig. 4E). Moreover, in cancer tissues, the expression of LINC00886 was negatively correlated with miR-141-5p (r = -0.6506, P < 0.0001, Fig. 4F). In addition, we found that the levels of miR-141-5p were obviously upregulated in A549, H1299, and HCC827 cells (P < 0.01, Fig. 4G), while the upregulation trend in H1975 cells was not significant.

Changes in NSCLC cell functions after transfection with miR-141-5p mimic

Based on the above results, we increased miR-141-5p expression in NSCLC using a miR-141-5p mimic (miR mimic) to further explore the gene’s function. The experimental results indicated that transfection with the miR mimic did not upregulate the expression level of LINC00886 in NSCLC cells (Fig. 5A and Supplementary Fig. 1). However, oe-LINC00886 transfection significantly suppressed the levels of miR-141-5p in NSCLC cells (P < 0.0001, Fig. 5B). Furthermore, following transfection with the miR mimic, miR-141-5p was significantly upregulated. Furthermore, we found that the proliferation ability of two NSCLC cell types was noticeably enhanced after transfection with the miR mimic compared to transfection with oe-LINC00886 (P < 0.0001, Fig. 5C and D). The application of miR-141-5p mimic counteracted the decline in migration and invasion capacity induced by LINC00886 overexpression (P < 0.0001, Fig. 5E and F).

Prediction and analysis of miR-141-5p downstream target genes involved in NSCLC regulation

Through predictions from different databases, 288 genes were ultimately clustered together (Fig. 6A). These overlapping genes formed a protein-protein interaction (PPI) network comprising 288 nodes and 79 edges (Fig. 6B). Target genes frequently involved in events included EGFR, HSP90AA1, CREBBP, ERBB4, and PRKACB. GO analysis (as shown in Fig. 6C) revealed that overlapping genes were primarily distributed in components such as GABA receptor complexes, pre-transcription initiation complexes, and synaptic membranes. They exhibited activities including transforming growth factor-β activated receptor activity, protein Ser/Thr kinase activity, and GABA receptor activity. They participated in the positive regulation of smooth muscle cell proliferation. Additionally, KEGG enrichment analysis (as shown in Fig. 6D) revealed that overlapping genes primarily functioned in cancer proteoglycans, axon guidance, and the PI3K-Akt signaling pathway.

Discussion

This study demonstrates that LINC00886 is downregulated in NSCLC and associated with tumor progression and poor prognosis. Functionally, LINC00886 inhibits NSCLC cell proliferation, migration, and invasion by sponging miR-141-5p.

Previous studies have established that lncRNAs play critical roles in the diagnosis and treatment of NSCLC. For example, Huang et al. [24] found that five immune-related lncRNAs were validated as independent prognostic factors for lung adenocarcinoma. Yao et al. [25] reported that several co-stimulatory lncRNAs, including LINC01137, promoted immune infiltration in NSCLC. Shen et al. [26] demonstrated that lncRNA XIST is upregulated in NSCLC patients and contributes to cancer cell proliferation and apoptosis. Similarly, overexpression of DSCAM-AS1 has been shown to facilitate NSCLC progression by modulating cancer cell metastasis and drug resistance [27]. The present study reveals that overexpression of LINC00886 effectively suppresses proliferation and metastasis in NSCLC cells, an effect mediated through miR-141-5p. Specifically, the reduced LINC00886 levels observed in NSCLC patients induce miR-141-5p upregulation, thereby enhancing cancer cell metastasis and invasive capacity, leading to disease progression. However, the role of LINC00886 sponge-mediated miR-141-5p in NSCLC has also been reported in other cancers. For example, studies indicate that LINC00886 promotes hepatocellular carcinoma development via miR-409-3p/miR-214-5p [28]. Dong et al. [17] reported that LINC00886 modulates the expression of downstream target genes SIRT7 and ELF3 through miR-144, thereby participating in the epithelial–mesenchymal transition (EMT) process in esophageal squamous cell carcinoma. Du et al. [18] reported that LINC00886 sponges miR-423-5p and promotes immune escape in ovarian cancer via the TLR4/Myd88/NF-κB/PD-L1 pathway.

As another key focus of this study, the role of miR-141-5p in cancer and related diseases warrants attention [29]. Li et al. [30] reported that miR-141-5p in plasma exosomes serves as a non-invasive biomarker for prostate cancer. Furthermore, studies indicate that miR-141-5p regulates cervical cancer cell proliferation and apoptosis by modulating BTG1 activity [31]. In vascular smooth muscle cells, it suppresses inflammation, proliferation, and migration via inhibition of the HMGB1/NF-κB pathway [32]. Additionally, miR-141-5p inhibits disease progression in chronic myeloid leukemia by targeting RAB32 [33]. In this study, we found that miR-141-5p upregulation promoted proliferation, migration, and invasion in NSCLC cells, while also counteracting the inhibitory effect of LINC00886 overexpression on these processes. These results suggest that the LINC00886/miR-141-5p axis may participate in NSCLC regulation by modulating cellular proliferation and infiltration. Bioinformatics analysis further indicated that pathways related to cancer proteoglycans, axon guidance, and PI3K-Akt signaling may be involved in LINC00886/miR-141-5p-mediated NSCLC. Multiple proteoglycans have been shown to be dysregulated during NSCLC progression [34], functioning as growth factor co-receptors, adhesion molecules, and signaling regulators. For instance, Syndecan-1 is frequently downregulated or shed in lung cancer, correlating with epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT), enhanced cell migration, and poor prognosis [35]. Versican is highly expressed in the NSCLC tumor microenvironment, promoting cancer cell proliferation, migration, and invasion, and is associated with the formation of an immunosuppressive microenvironment [36]. Axon guidance molecules, which are abnormally expressed during nervous system development and in lung cancer patients, have been linked to angiogenesis, cell migration, and metastatic colonization [37]. For instance, studies reveal that Sema3A, Sema3C, and others are highly expressed in NSCLC. These molecules promote EMT, invasion, and metastasis [38]. Moreover, the PI3K-Akt pathway—one of the most critical intracellular signaling hubs for cell survival and growth—is aberrantly activated in over 50% of NSCLC cases [39, 40]. Based on these observations, we propose that within the LINC00886/miR-141-5p regulatory axis, cancer-associated proteoglycans and axon guidance molecules may function as upstream “signal organizers” and “microenvironment shapers.” They integrate and amplify multiple developmental and growth signals, channeling them to the core PI3K-Akt survival and growth hub to jointly drive NSCLC proliferation, invasion, and metastasis.

However, we observed that the upregulation of miR-141-5p in H1975 cells was not significant. We speculate that this result may be related to the intrinsic characteristics of the cells. H1975 cells harbor EGFR T790M/L858R mutations and exhibit resistance to third-generation EGFR-TKIs. Studies indicate that the unique resistance of the H1975 cell line is associated with EMT mechanisms [24, 41]. Bioinformatics analysis in the present study suggested that downstream signaling pathways of the LINC00886/miR-141‑5p axis—such as the PI3K/Akt pathway, which regulates cell survival and proliferation—may be involved in the modulation of NSCLC. Therefore, the lack of significant miR-141‑5p upregulation in H1975 cells might be related to the activation of alternative resistance pathways (e.g., AXL or PI3K/AKT) [42], potentially attenuating the regulatory function of this miRNA.

Additionally, we need to discuss the limitations currently present in this study. (1) Single-center sample. Although the sample size included in the study was relatively adequate, all participants were from a single center, which may reduce the representativeness and generalizability of the findings. We plan to actively conduct multicenter, large-sample studies in the future to enhance the reliability and universality of the results. (2) Clinical data heterogeneity. Individual patient variations and differing clinical backgrounds may introduce interference into the results. During analysis, we employed multivariate regression to control for potential confounding factors as much as possible. Future studies will establish stricter patient inclusion criteria and conduct stratified analyses to examine different factors individually. (3) Limitations of the cell model. Although cell models can partially reflect the regulatory effects of LINC00886 on NSCLC, differences exist between the microenvironment of primary tumors and in vitro cells. Therefore, the in vitro cell model used in this study may not fully represent the true in vivo effects of LINC00886. We plan to conduct animal experiments to establish a mouse model of lung cancer and further validate the function and mechanism of action of LINC00886 on NSCLC at the in vivo level.

Unlike traditional surgical treatments, the emergence of precision medicine has provided new hope for the treatment of NSCLC [43]. Some gene mutations are genetic factors for NSCLC [44]; however, the development of third-generation EGFR and ALK inhibitors has offered crucial guidance for NSCLC treatment [45]. Furthermore, combination therapy holds promise for improving the prognosis of patients with advanced NSCLC [46]. Hyeon et al. [47] reported that endogenous PD-L1 signaling can drive the immunosuppressive effects of myeloid cells in lung cancer patients via the IL-6/Jak/Stat3 pathway. Tina et al. [48] found that immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) can improve clinical outcomes in some advanced patients. This study confirmed that the downregulation of LINC00886 is associated with tumor progression in NSCLC patients, and overexpression of LINC00886 can effectively inhibit NSCLC cell proliferation, migration, and invasion through miR-141-5p. Given the critical role of LINC00886 in the progression of NSCLC and its molecular mechanisms, it is expected to become a candidate prognostic biomarker and a potential therapeutic target that requires in vivo and prospective clinical validation. However, due to issues with the accuracy of targeted/immunotherapy, certain challenges may exist.

In conclusion, this study found that LINC00886 has the potential to develop into a candidate prognostic marker for NSCLC. Downregulation of LINC00886 induces cancer cell proliferation through miR-141-5p, promoting NSCLC progression. However, the deeper downstream mechanisms of the LINC00886/miR-141-5p axis still require further investigation.

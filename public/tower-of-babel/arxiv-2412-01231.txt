Self-energy correction to the 𝑬 ​ 𝟏 \bm{E1} transition amplitudes in hydrogen-like ions
M. G. Kozlov 1,2, M. Y. Kaygorodov 1, Yu. A. Demidov 1,2, V. A. Yerokhin 3

Abstract
We present calculations of the self-energy correction to the E ​ 1 E1 transition amplitudes in hydrogen-like ions, performed to all orders in the nuclear binding strength parameter. Our results for the 1 ​ s 1s - 2 ​ p 1 / 2 2p_{1/2} transition for the hydrogen isoelectronic sequence show that the perturbed-orbital part of the self-energy correction provides the dominant contribution, accounting for approximately 99% of the total correction for this transition. Detailed calculations were performed for n ​ s ns - n ′ ​ p n^{\prime}p and n ​ p np - n ′ ​ d n^{\prime}d transitions in H-like cesium. We conclude that the perturbed-orbital part remains dominant also for other n ​ s ns - n ′ ​ p n^{\prime}p transitions, whereas for the n ​ p np - n ′ ​ d n^{\prime}d matrix elements this dominance no longer holds. Consequently, the self-energy corrections for the n ​ p np - n ′ ​ d n^{\prime}d one-electron matrix elements cannot be well reproduced by means of effective QED operators constructed for energy levels.



I
Introduction

The rapid progress of experimental techniques in atomic spectroscopy, combined with improved computational methods developed for many-electron systems, requires the inclusion quantum electrodynamics (QED) corrections, not only for energy levels but also for other properties of many-electron atoms. In particular, recent large-scale many-body calculations of
E1
transition amplitudes for neon-like iron and nickel have reached such high computational precision that the inclusion of QED effects has become mandatory
Kühn et al. 2020
;
Kühn et al. 2022
;
Shah et al. 2024
.

An accurate knowledge of the transition amplitudes in many-electron atoms is necessary for the atomic tests of the standard model and in the search for the new physics beyond standard model
Safronova et al. 2018
. For example, it is important to include QED corrections to the parity non-conserving transition amplitude in Cs
Shabaev et al. 2005
when comparing theory with the experiment
Wood et al. 1997
. QED corrections to the dynamic polarizability may affect magic frequencies for the clock transitions.

So far ab initio QED calculations have been conducted primarily for atoms and ions with one or several electrons; extending such methods to many-electron systems looks unfeasible at present. It is possible to perform ab initio QED calculations in many-electron systems within one-electron approximation
Sapirstein and Cheng 2002
;
Sapirstein and Cheng 2003
, but the omitted electron-correlation effects can be very significant in this case.

Most of recent calculations of
E1
transition amplitudes of many-electron atoms performed so far
Kühn et al. 2022
;
Shah et al. 2024
;
Fairhall et al. 2023
;
Roberts et al. 2023
;
Tran Tan and Derevianko 2023
;
Kozlov et al. 2024a
accounted for QED effects by using approximate methods based on different variants of effective QED potentials. The most successful of these is the model operator introduced by Shabaev and coworkers in Ref.
Shabaev et al. 2013
and implemented as QEDMOD package
Shabaev et al. 2015
;
Shabaev et al. 2018
. It is important that the QEDMOD operator, as well as all other effective QED potentials, is designed to approximately reproduce QED effects for energy levels only. As a result, using these operators for calculations of transition amplitudes yields incomplete results, as they account only for part of the effect while neglecting other contributions, such as vertex and reducible corrections.

To justify the omission vertex and reducible corrections, the studies
Kühn et al. 2022
;
Shah et al. 2024
;
Fairhall et al. 2023
;
Roberts et al. 2023
cited the work of Sapirstein and Cheng
Sapirstein and Cheng 2005
, who performed ab initio calculations of
E1
transition amplitudes for alkali atoms within the one-electron screening-potential approximation and found these corrections to be relatively small. However, Sapirstein and Cheng’s study focused solely on the
ns
-
np_{1/2}
transition and was performed for a few alkali-like atoms, leaving the broader applicability of this statement uncertain. In our previous work
Kozlov et al. 2024b
, we performed one-electron ab initio calculations for neon-like iron and similarly concluded that the vertex and reducible corrections are small enough to be neglected at the current level of precision. Nonetheless, the general validity of this conclusion remains unclear.

The QED correction to the
E1
transition amplitude consists of the electron self-energy and the vacuum-polarization parts. The vacuum-polarization part is relatively simple and can be easily accounted for, e.g., by means of vacuum-polarization potentials included into the QEDMOD package. By contrast, the self-energy part is much more complicated as it cannot be successfully approximated by a local potential.

In this work, we conduct a detailed study of the self-energy correction to the
E1
transition amplitudes in hydrogen-like ions, with a particular focus on hydrogen-like cesium. The total self-energy contribution is divided into two parts: the perturbed-orbital (po) part, which can be approximately represented by effective QED potentials, and the vertex+reducible (vr) part, which is omitted by them. We analyze the relative effect of the vr part on the total self-energy correction for various transitions. Additionally, we examine the accuracy of the QEDMOD potential in approximating ab initio self-energy results. This information is required for assessing potential errors when using the QEDMOD operator in many-electron calculations of the
E1
transition amplitude.

In this paper we use the relativistic units
m=\hbar=c=1
and the Heaviside charge units
\alpha=e^{2}/(4\pi)
.

II
Basic formulas

The
E1
transition amplitude in the length gauge between the one-electron states
a
and
b
is given by the expectation value of the electric dipole operator,

\displaystyle z_{ab}=\bra{a}r_{z}\ket{b}\,,

(1)

where
r_{z}
is the
z
component of the position vector
\bm{r}
.

The self-energy correction to the matrix element
z_{ab}
is
represented as a sum of the perturbed-orbital (po) and the vertex
+
reducible (vr) parts,

\displaystyle\delta z_{\rm se}=z_{\rm po}+z_{\rm vr}\,.

(2)

General formulas for these corrections were reported in the literature
Sapirstein and Cheng 2005
(see also Ref.
Shabaev 2002
).
The perturbed-orbital part is given by

\displaystyle z_{\rm po}=

\displaystyle\ \sum_{n\neq a}\bra{a}\Sigma_{R}(\varepsilon_{a})\ket{n}\,\frac{z_{nb}}{\varepsilon_{a}-\varepsilon_{n}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{n\neq b}\frac{z_{an}}{\varepsilon_{b}-\varepsilon_{n}}\bra{n}\Sigma_{R}(\varepsilon_{b})\ket{b}\,,

(3)

where
\Sigma_{R}(\varepsilon)
is the renormalized one-loop self-energy operator,
\Sigma_{R}(\varepsilon)=\Sigma(\varepsilon)-\gamma^{0}\delta m
,
and
\delta m
is the one-loop mass counterterm.
The one-loop self-energy operator is defined by its matrix elements with one-electron wave functions
as
Yerokhin and Shabaev 1999a

\displaystyle\bra{a}\Sigma(\varepsilon)\ket{b}=\frac{i}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\omega\,\sum_{n}\frac{\bra{an}I(\omega)\ket{nb}}{\varepsilon-\omega-u\varepsilon_{n}}\,,

(4)

where
u=1-i0
, the summation is performed over the complete spectrum of the Dirac equation and
I(\omega)
describes the
exchange by a virtual photon. In the Feynman gauge,
I(\omega)
is given by

\displaystyle I(\omega,{\bm{r}}_{1},{\bm{r}}_{2})=\alpha\big(1-\bm{\alpha}_{1}\cdot\bm{\alpha}_{2}\big)\,\frac{e^{i\sqrt{\omega^{2}+i0}\,r_{12}}}{r_{12}}\,,

(5)

with
r_{12}=|{\bm{r}}_{1}-{\bm{r}}_{2}|
.

The vertex
+
reducible contribution is given by

\displaystyle z_{\rm vr}=

\displaystyle\ \frac{i}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\omega\,

\displaystyle\times\Bigg[\sum_{n_{1}n_{2}}\frac{z_{n_{1}n_{2}}\,\bra{an_2}I(\omega)\ket{n_1b}}{(\varepsilon_{a}-\omega-u\varepsilon_{n_{1}})(\varepsilon_{b}-\omega-u\varepsilon_{n_{2}})}

\displaystyle-\frac{z_{ab}}{2}\,\sum_{n}\frac{\bra{an}I(\omega)\ket{na}}{(\varepsilon_{a}-\omega-u\varepsilon_{n})^{2}}

\displaystyle-\frac{z_{ab}}{2}\,\sum_{n}\frac{\bra{bn}I(\omega)\ket{nb}}{(\varepsilon_{b}-\omega-u\varepsilon_{n})^{2}}\Bigg]\,.

(6)

II.1
Angular integration

Using the standard Racah algebra, we evaluate the leading-order
E1
transition matrix element

z_{ab}
as

\displaystyle z_{ab}=

\displaystyle\ \frac{(-1)^{j_{b}-\mu_{b}}}{\sqrt{3}}\,C_{j_{a},\mu_{a},\,j_{b},-\mu_{b}}^{1,0}\,C_{1}(\kappa_{a},\kappa_{b})\,r_{ab}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\ {\cal P}_{ab}\,r_{ab}\,,

(7)

where
C_{j_{1},m_{1},\,j_{2},m_{2}}^{j,m}
is the Clebsch-Gordan coefficient,
C_{L}(\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2})

is the reduced matrix element of the normalized spherical harmonics, see, e.g., Eq. (A10)
of Ref.
Yerokhin and Maiorova 2020
, and the radial integral is
defined as

\displaystyle r_{ab}=\int_{0}^{\infty}dr\,r^{3}\,\big[g_{a}(r)\,g_{b}(r)+f_{a}(r)\,f_{b}(r)\big]\,,

(8)

where
g(r)
and
f(r)
are the upper and lower radial components of the Dirac wave functions.
The angular factor
{\cal P}_{ab}
introduced in Eq. (
II.1
) is the common prefactor for all corrections
to
z_{ab}
.

For the evaluation of the vertex correction, we need to perform the angular integration in the following expression

\displaystyle X_{n_{1}n_{2}}=\sum_{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}}z_{n_{1}n_{2}}\,\bra{an_2}I(\omega)\ket{n_1b}\,,

(9)

where
\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{2}
are the momentum projections of the states
n_{1}
and
n_{2}
, respectively.
We make use of the standard partial-wave decomposition of the matrix element with
I(\omega)

\displaystyle\bra{an_2}I(\omega)\ket{n_1b}=\alpha\sum_{L}J_{L}(an_{2}n_{1}b)\,R_{L}(\omega,an_{2}n_{1}b)\,,

(10)

where
J_{L}
contains all the dependence on the angular momenta projections and given by Eq. (39)
of Ref.
Yerokhin and Maiorova 2020
and
R_{L}
are the standard two-body radial integrals
defined in Appendix A of Ref.
Yerokhin and Maiorova 2020
. After performing summations over the
momentum projections with help of formulas from
Varshalovich et al. 1988
, we obtain

\displaystyle X_{n_{1}n_{2}}=

\displaystyle\ \alpha\,{\cal P}_{ab}\,\sum_{L}r_{n_{1}n_{2}}\,R_{L}(\omega,an_{2}n_{1}b)\,

\displaystyle\times\frac{C_{1}(\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2})}{C_{1}(\kappa_{a},\kappa_{b})}\,(-1)^{j_{1}-j_{2}}\,\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}j_{2}&j_{1}&1\\
j_{a}&j_{b}&L\\
\end{array}\right\}\,.

II.2
Momentum-space reduction

In order to perform renormalization in the vertex
+
reducible term, we need to separate out the contribution
of the free electron propagators and evaluate them in momentum space, with a covariant regularization of
ultraviolet divergences. The divergent terms can be shown to cancel between the vertex and reducible parts,
whereas the remaining finite contribution needs to be calculated in momentum space.
So, we represent Eq. (
II
) as a sum the free part that contains the contribution of free electron propagators and the many-potential part which is the remainder,

\displaystyle z_{\rm vr}=z_{\rm vr}^{(0)}+z_{\rm vr}^{(1+)}.

(13)

The general calculation procedure is similar to that developed in the previous studies, see, e.g., Ref.
Yerokhin et al. 1999
. New features appear only in calculations of the free vertex part, which contains the vertex operator with the electric dipole operator in the momentum space. We therefore will describe this part of our calculation in some detail.

The Fourier transform of the electric dipole operator is given by the gradient of the Dirac
\delta
function,

\displaystyle{\bm{r}}_{z}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm Fourier}}{{\longrightarrow}}i(2\pi)^{3}\,\bm{\nabla}_{z}\,\delta^{3}({\bm{q}})\,,

(14)

where
{\bm{q}}={\bm{p}}-{\bm{p}}^{\prime}
is the exchanged momentum.
It is rather cumbersome to perform calculations with the above momentum-space expression for the electric dipole operator, because we need to perform the integration by parts analytically before carrying out the integration over the momentum in the vertex operator.
For this reason, we choose to follow Ref.
Persson et al. 1997
and introduce a finite regularization parameter
\rho
, which makes the Fourier transform to be a continuous function,

\displaystyle{\bm{r}}_{z}\,e^{-(\nicefrac{{\rho\,r}}{{2}})^{2}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm Fourier}}{{\longrightarrow}}-i\,{\bm{q}}_{z}\,\frac{16\pi^{3/2}}{\rho^{5}}\,e^{-\nicefrac{{q^{2}}}{{\rho^{2}}}}\,.

(15)

We found that if we perform numerical calculation with a sufficiently small regularization parameter
(typically,
\rho\approx 10^{-6}
), the error introduced by the regulator is completely negligible
at the level of our interest. Moreover, only a small momenta region
|p-p^{\prime}|\sim\rho
contributes to the total integrals, so that numerical integrations are relatively simple in this case.

We thus write the free part of the vertex contribution as

z_{\rm ver}^{(0)}=-i\,\frac{16\pi^{3/2}}{\rho^{5}}\,\int\frac{d^{3}{\bm{p}}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int\frac{d^{3}{\bm{p}}^{\prime}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,e^{-\nicefrac{{q^{2}}}{{\rho^{2}}}}\,\\
\times\overline{\psi}_{a}({\bm{p}})\,\Gamma_{R}^{0}(\varepsilon_{a},{\bm{p}};\varepsilon_{b},{\bm{p}}^{\prime})\,\big({\bm{p}}_{z}-{\bm{p}}^{\prime}_{z}\big)\,\psi_{b}({\bm{p}}^{\prime})\,,

(16)

where
\overline{\psi}_{a}({\bm{p}})=\psi^{{\dagger}}_{a}({\bm{p}})\gamma^{0}
and
\Gamma_{R}^{0}
is the time component of the renormalized
free-electron vertex operator, see Appendix B of Ref.
Yerokhin and Shabaev 1999b
for definition and explicit representation.
We now use the following representation for the vertex operator sandwiched between two Dirac functions,

\overline{\psi}_{a}({\bm{p}})\,\Gamma_{R}^{0}(\varepsilon_{a},{\bm{p}};\varepsilon_{b},{\bm{p}}^{\prime})\,\psi_{b}({\bm{p}}^{\prime})=\frac{\alpha}{4\pi}\,i^{l_{a}-l_{b}}\,\\
\times\Big[{\cal F}_{1}^{ab}(p,p^{\prime},\xi)\,\chi^{{\dagger}}_{\kappa_{a}\mu_{a}}(\hat{{\bm{p}}})\,\chi_{\kappa_{b}\mu_{b}}(\hat{\bm{p}}^{\prime})\\
+{\cal F}_{2}^{ab}(p,p^{\prime},\xi)\,\chi^{{\dagger}}_{-\kappa_{a}\mu_{a}}(\hat{{\bm{p}}})\,\chi_{-\kappa_{b}\mu_{b}}(\hat{\bm{p}}^{\prime})\Big]\,,

(17)

with explicit formulas for
{\cal F}_{1}
and
{\cal F}_{2}
given by Eqs. (A5) and (A6)
of Ref.
Yerokhin et al. 1999
and
p=|{\bm{p}}|
,
\hat{{\bm{p}}}={\bm{p}}/p
,
and
\xi=\hat{{\bm{p}}}\cdot\hat{\bm{p}}^{\prime}
. Performing angular integrations, we obtain

z_{\rm ver}^{(0)}={\cal P}_{ab}\,\frac{\alpha}{8\pi^{9/2}\rho^{5}}\,i^{l_{a}-l_{b}+1}\,\\
\times\int_{0}^{\infty}dp\,\int_{0}^{\infty}dp^{\prime}\,\int_{-1}^{1}d\xi\,(pp^{\prime})^{2}\,e^{-\nicefrac{{q^{2}}}{{\rho^{2}}}}\,\\
\times\bigg\{{\cal F}_{1}^{ab}(p,p^{\prime},\xi)\,\Big[p^{\prime}P_{l_{a}}(\xi)-pP_{l_{b}}(\xi)\Big]\\
+{\cal F}_{2}^{ab}(p,p^{\prime},\xi)\,\Big[p^{\prime}P_{\overline{l}_{a}}(\xi)-pP_{\overline{l}_{b}}(\xi)\Big]\bigg\}\,,

(18)

where
l_{a}=|\kappa_{a}+1/2|-1/2
,
\overline{l}_{a}=|\kappa_{a}-1/2|-1/2
and
P_{l}(\xi)
is the Legendre polynomial.
We note that
i^{l_{a}-l_{b}+1}
is real since
l_{a}-l_{b}+1
should be even, according to angular selection rules.

The remaining integrals in Eq. (
18
) were performed numerically, after the change of variables
according to

\int_{0}^{\infty}dp\,\int_{0}^{\infty}dp^{\prime}\,\int_{-1}^{1}d\xi\,(pp^{\prime})^{2}\,F(p,p^{\prime},\xi)\\
=\int_{0}^{\infty}\!dx\int_{0}^{x}\!dy\int_{y}^{x}\!dq\,\frac{pp^{\prime}q}{2}\,\Big[F(p,p^{\prime},\xi)+F(p^{\prime},p,\xi)\Big]\,,

(19)

where
p=(x+y)/2
,
p^{\prime}=(x-y)/2
,
q^{2}=p^{2}+p^{\prime 2}-2pp^{\prime}\xi
.

II.3
Frequency dependent correction

In our calculations so far we used the low-frequency limit of the
E1
-transition
operator in the length gauge
D\propto r_{z}
, whose matrix elements reduce to the radial amplitude

r_{ab}
given by Eq. (
8
).
The complete relativistic
E1
-transition operator contains in addition the frequency-dependent part

Grant 1974
;
Johnson 2007
,
which is of order
O(2\pi r/\lambda)
where
\lambda
is the transition wave length.
The frequency-dependent contribution is very small for neutral and slightly ionized
atoms, but become significant for heavy highly ionized ions
Popov and Maiorova 2017
.
In order to asses the uncertainty due to use of the low-frequency limit
of the transition operator, we now discuss the frequency-dependent correction
to the radial amplitude
r_{ab}
.

The radial amplitudes of the relativistic
E1
transition operator in the length gauge were presented in Refs.
Grant 1974
;
Johnson 2007
for the case of photon absorption:

d_{ab}=\frac{3}{k}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!r^{2}dr\bigg\{j_{1}(kr)\Big[g_{a}(r)g_{b}(r)+f_{a}(r)f_{b}(r)\Big]\\
+j_{2}(kr)\Big[\frac{\kappa_{a}-\kappa_{b}+2}{2}g_{a}(r)f_{b}(r)\\
+\frac{\kappa_{a}-\kappa_{b}-2}{2}f_{a}(r)g_{b}(r)\Big]\bigg\}\,,

(20)

where
j_{n}(x)=\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2x}}J_{n+1/2}(x)
are spherical Bessel functions:

\displaystyle j_{1}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sin x}{x^{2}}-\frac{\cos x}{x}=\frac{1}{3}x-\frac{1}{30}x^{3}+\mathcal{O}(x^{5})\,,

(21)

\displaystyle j_{2}(x)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{3}{x^{2}}-1\right)\frac{\sin x}{x}-\frac{3\cos x}{x^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{15}x^{2}-\frac{1}{210}x^{4}+\mathcal{O}(x^{6})\,.

(22)

We immediately see that in the limit
kr\to 0
only the first term in Eq. (
20
) survives and
d_{ab}
reduces to
r_{ab}
, as it should.
Restricting ourselves to the next term of the expansion in
kr
,
we get the correction to the radial integral
d_{ab}-r_{ab}\approx\delta d_{ab}
, where

\delta d_{ab}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\!r^{3}dr\bigg\{-\frac{(kr)^{2}}{10}\Big[g_{a}(r)g_{b}(r)+f_{a}(r)f_{b}(r)\Big]\\
+\frac{kr}{5}\Big[\frac{\kappa_{a}-\kappa_{b}+2}{2}g_{a}(r)f_{b}(r)\\
+\frac{\kappa_{a}-\kappa_{b}-2}{2}f_{a}(r)g_{b}(r)\Big]\bigg\}\,.

(23)

For the emission of the photon we need to substitute
k
with
-k
in the expressions (
20
) and (
23
). This keeps the first term in the integral (
23
) unchanged, but changes sign of the second one. However, if we simultaneously interchange the initial and final states,
a\leftrightarrow b
, we get the same result:
\delta d_{ab}(k)=\delta d_{ba}(-k)
.

The frequency-dependent corrections are suppressed by the ratio of the size of the atom
to the transition wavelength. They are typically very small for most of experimentally studied
cases of valence transitions in neutral and slightly ionized atoms, as follows from a simple estimate.
For example, the
6s_{1/2}\to 6p_{1/2}
transition in neutral Cs has frequency
\omega=11178
cm
-1
. Taking into account the values of the rms radii of the
6s_{1/2}
and
6p_{1/2}
orbitals of
0.34
nm and
0.46
nm, respectively, the first term in Eq. (
23
) is estimated to be about
\frac{(kr)^{2}}{10}r_{ab}\sim 8\cdot 10^{-7}r_{ab}
. The second term includes products of the upper and lower components, so it is about
\frac{(kr)\alpha}{5}r_{ab}\sim 4\cdot 10^{-6}r_{ab}
.
The frequency-dependent correction for neutral cesium
is thus negligibly small even compared to the QED correction.

However, the situation changes drastically for
heavy highly ionized ions, because the transition energy grows typically as the square of the effective nuclear charge. Our explicit calculations presented in the next section show that
for H-like cesium the frequency-dependent corrections enter already at the percent level.

III
Results and discussion

It is convenient to represent numerical results for the self-energy correction to the
E1
transition amplitude
in terms of the multiplicative function
R_{\rm se}(Z\alpha)
defined as

\displaystyle\delta z_{\rm se}=z_{ab}\,\frac{\alpha}{\pi}\,R_{\rm se}(Z\alpha)\,.

(24)

We start with comparing our numerical results with the numerical values obtained for the
2p_{1/2}
-
1s
transition by Sapirstein, Pachucki and Cheng
Sapirstein et al. 2004
and with the leading terms of the
Z\alpha
expansion derived in that work. In that work results were obtained in the form of corrections to the
2p_{1/2}
-state decay rate, rather than amplitudes, so we need to connect the transition amplitude
z_{ab}
to the decay rate
\Gamma_{ab}
. This is achieved by observing that

\displaystyle\frac{\delta\Gamma_{ab}}{\Gamma_{ab}}=3\,\frac{\delta E_{ab}}{E_{ab}}+2\,\frac{\delta z_{ab}}{z_{ab}}\,,

(25)

where
E_{ab}
and
\delta E_{ab}
is the zero-order energy difference between the states
a
and
b
and its correction, respectively. The self-energy correction to
\Gamma_{ab}
can then
be represented in a form analogous to Eq. (
24
),

\displaystyle\delta\Gamma_{\rm se}=\Gamma_{ab}\,\frac{\alpha}{\pi}\,{\cal R}_{\rm se}(Z\alpha)\,.

(26)

To the leading order in
Z\alpha
, the self-energy correction to the
2p_{1/2}
decay rate is
Sapirstein et al. 2004
;
Ivanov and Karshenboim 1996

\displaystyle{\cal R}_{\rm se}(Z\alpha)=(Z\alpha)^{2}\Big\{\Big[\frac{8}{3}\ln\frac{4}{3}-\frac{61}{18}\Big]\ln(\Za)^{-2}+6.05168\Big\}.

(27)

Results of our numerical calculations of the self-energy correction to the decay rate of the
2p_{1/2}
state are presented in Table
1
, in comparison with
predictions of the
Z\alpha
expansion and all-order numerical results obtained in Ref.
Sapirstein et al. 2004
. We observe that both numerical calculations converge to the
Z\alpha
-expansion predictions in the low-
Z
limit but significantly deviate
from them for medium- and high-
Z
values. The two numerical calculations are in reasonable agreement with each other.
We observe small deviations increasing with the increase of
Z
, probably due to different nuclear models used
in the calculations. (Ref.
Sapirstein et al. 2004
used a finite-size nucleus, whereas we use the point nuclear model.)
Small deviations observed for the lowest
Z
are probably due to numerical issues. It should be mentioned that Ref.
Sapirstein et al. 2004
used a completely different approach to the calculation of the decay rates, via the imaginary part of the two-loop self-energy. The observed agreement of the two calculations is also a check of consistency of the two different approaches.

Table
2
presents results of our numerical calculations of the self-energy
correction to the
E1
matrix elements of the
1s
-
2p_{1/2}
transition along the hydrogen isoelectronic sequence.
We observe large cancellations between the free-electron and many-potential parts of the vr correction, thus corroborating the findings of Ref.
Sapirstein et al. 2004
, which reported large numerical cancellations at intermediate stages of calculations.
These cancellations are particularly pronounced in the low-
Z
region. For instance, at
Z=2
, the first four digits cancel out.
This highlights the need for meticulous control of numerical accuracy throughout the intermediate stages of these calculations.
We also observe that the vr contribution is much smaller than the po correction
in the whole region of nuclear charges, accounting for typically about 1% of the total self-energy correction.

Vertex-reducible (vr) QED correction to the
n_{s}s_{1/2}\to n_{p}p_{1/2}
transitions in H-like Cs ion. Left panel: corrections to the radial integrals
r_{ab}
; right panel: corrections to the scaled function
R_{\rm se}
defined by Eq. (

), multiplied by (-1) for better vision.

We now turn to the detailed analysis of the
E1
transition matrix elements between different states
of a selected H-like ion. We have chosen the reference ion to be cesium (
Z=55
), since it is well studied experimentally
(see, e. g. Refs.
Toh et al. 2019a
;
Toh et al. 2019b
;
Toh et al. 2019c
;
Quirk et al. 2024
)
and its nuclear charge is large enough for QED effects to be of
experimental interest.
Table
3
presents
our numerical results for the
ns
-
n^{\prime}p
matrix elements, whereas
Table
4
summarizes our results obtained for the
np
-
n^{\prime}d
transitions.

Tables
3
and
4
include values for the relative frequency-dependent correction
\frac{\delta d_{ab}(\omega)}{r_{ab}}
, multiplied by
10^{3}
. They are calculated using Eq. (
23
) and
taking the transition energy as the difference
of the Dirac energies of the initial and the final state.
We observe that the frequency-dependent correction is typically at a percent (sometime,
a few percent) level and thus should
be taken into account for H-like ions.
By contrast, it becomes negligibly small
for neutral and slightly ionized ions.
For example, if we recalculate this correction
with the same hydrogenic wave functions but with
frequency
\omega=11178
cm
-1
corresponding the
6s_{1/2}\to 6p_{1/2}
transition in neutral Cs, we obtain the
relative contribution on the
10^{-6}
level,
which is much smaller than the QED corrections.

Table
3
and
4
presents
our numerical results for the self-energy correction for the
ns
-
n^{\prime}p
and
np
-
n^{\prime}d
transitions.
We observe that the dependence of the self-energy corrections of the
(n,n^{\prime})
matrix elements
on
n
and
n^{\prime}
turns out to be rather complicated.
In particular, for the
(n,n)
matrix elements the self-energy corrections are abnormally suppressed, even after taking into account that the zeroth-order radial
integrals
r_{ab}
reach their maximal values in this case.
Furthermore, for
n<n^{\prime}
and
n>n^{\prime}
the corrections are typically of the opposite sign. We conclude that the self-energy correction to the
E1
matrix elements does not have a well-defined sign and can
both increase and decrease the absolute values of the transition amplitude.

In the last three columns of Tables
3
and
4
we compare our ab initio values with approximate self-energy results obtained with help of QEDMOD package (with the vacuum-polarization part switched off). We recall that the QEDMOD results should approximately reproduce the po part of the ab inito se correction, whereas the vr part is omitted in the approximate treatment.

We see that for the
ns
-
n^{\prime}p
matrix elements QEDMOD
calculations reproduce the po values very well in most cases.
The exception is the diagonal
(n,n)
matrix elements, where
the deviation may reach 90% (for the
(2s,2p_{1/2})
transition).
In this case the po contribution is abnormally small, and
the absolute error turns out to be not very significant.
The vr part of the se correction is also relatively small
for the
ns
-
n^{\prime}p
matrix elements. As a result, we may conclude
that the
QEDMOD treatment, despite ignoring the vr contribution, still
reproduces the ab inito se correction reasonably well,
the typical accuracy being within 10% in most cases.

For the
np
-
n^{\prime}d
matrix elements, however,
the situation is
different. First,
the po part is significantly smaller in magnitude than for the
ns
-
n^{\prime}p
matrix elements, leading to significantly smaller se corrections. Next,
the po and vr parts are of the same order.
Furthermore, the QEDMOD calculations do not reproduce well
even the po part of the se correction. We conclude that
for the
np
-
n^{\prime}d
matrix elements, the QEDMOD treatment
provides only the order of magnitude of the se contribution,
but is not capable of yielding a quantitative approximation.

It is important to note that the situation in
many-electron atoms can differ significantly from that of hydrogen-like ions. In particular, many-electron atoms often exhibit substantial mixing of single-electron configurations due to electron correlation effects. For the
p
-
d
transitions, where QED effects on radial integrals are relatively small, a more significant QED contribution may arise from the configuration mixing.
For example, this was the case in our recent study of the ten-electron ions Fe
16+
and Ni
18+

Kozlov et al. 2024b
, where the dominant QED correction to the
2p
-
3d
transitions was found to originate from electronic correlations. It was demonstrated that such corrections can be effectively accounted for by incorporating the QEDMOD potential in many-electron calculations.

IV
Summary

We performed ab initio calculations of the self-energy
correction to the
E1
transition amplitudes
in H-like ions, to all orders in the binding nuclear strength
parameter
Z\alpha
.
Our results for the
1s
-
2p_{1/2}
transition amplitude were
converted to the correction to the decay rate of the
2p_{1/2}
state and compared
with previous all-order and
Z\alpha
-expansion calculations
Sapirstein et al. 2004
.
Good agreement was found, which yields an important check of consistency of different methods.
Our calculation confirms the previous conclusion of Ref.
Sapirstein and Cheng 2005

that the vertex and reducible parts of the self-energy
correction are small,
their contribution being on the level of
1% for the
1s
-
2p_{1/2}
matrix element.

A detailed study of self-energy correction for a large number of
ns
-
n^{\prime}p
and
np
-
n^{\prime}d

transitions was carried out for H-like cesium.
We found a complicated dependence of the self-energy corrections of the
(n,n^{\prime})
matrix elements
on
n
and
n^{\prime}
.
We conclude that for the
ns
-
n^{\prime}p
matrix elements, the perturbed-orbital contribution is dominant and the
vertex and reducible corrections are relatively small, so that
the total self-energy effect can usually be well
reproduced by approximate calculations with the QEDMOD
model potential.
By contrast, for the
np
-
n^{\prime}d
matrix elements
we find significantly smaller self-energy corrections,
which are not well reproduced by the QEDMOD
model potential.

The comparison of our ab initio results with approximate treatment based on the QEDMOD model potential allows
one to estimate uncertainties associated with usage of
the QEDMOD potential in many-electron calculations
of the
E1
transition amplitudes in multi-electron
atoms
Kühn et al. 2022
;
Shah et al. 2024
;
Fairhall et al. 2023
;
Roberts et al. 2023
;
Tran Tan and Derevianko 2023

Research on personalized distance education recommendation system based on deep learning
Authors: Xia Yang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Online learning environments must be personalized with intelligent systems that propose courses according to learners’ individual preferences, activity patterns, and proficiency levels. Current recommendation models, however, fail to capture dynamic learner behavior and do not scale in big data. The research proposes a hybrid Putterfish Optimization Algorithm with Apriori (POA–Apriori)–Map-Reduce based Convolutional Neural Network (MR-CNN) methodology to address these issues, which integrates the Pufferfish Optimization Algorithm (POA) for global parameter tuning, the Apriori algorithm for association rule mining, and MapReduce-based Convolutional Neural Network (MR-CNN) for parallel deep learning. Data preprocessing was performed through normalization, feature encoding, and temporal smoothing and then model training with Udemy and Coursera datasets. The proposed model performed well with strong predictive performance of Precision@10 = 0.8769, Recall@10 = 0.8896, Normalized Discounted Cumulative Gain (NDCG)@10 = 0.8765, and lower error metrics with Mean Absolute Error (MAE)@10 = 0.2550 and Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE)@10 = 0.3568 compared to traditional models such as Support Vector Machines (SVM), K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN), and Deep Neural Networks (DNN) using both data sets. Statistical tests using paired t-tests (p < 0.05) confirmed the significance of these results. Experiments demonstrate that the hybrid POA–Apriori–MR-CNN approach significantly enhances recommendation accuracy, scalability, and robustness for massive-scale personalized education systems. The integration of optimization, association mining, and distributed deep learning marks a significant milestone towards adaptive and data-oriented e-learning environments.

Introduction

The educational system serves as a historical monument to the vast store of information and wisdom that exists in the cosmos. On the other hand, the “Gurukula” system1 was well-known in ancient times for its students’ constant access to instructors and wealth of information resources, as well as for the fact that they lived on the grounds while they studied. Over time, education has changed from strict, teacher-focused setups to more student-centered methods that focus on flexibility and involvement2. The historical development shows how education has broadened, but the main challenge today is making sure that learning systems can adjust to each student’s unique preferences, speed, and situation. This issue is especially important now, as online education offers learners a vast array of course choices3.

Personalized suggestions may be affected by language barriers, as the algorithm may encounter difficulties in accurately interpreting user preferences across different languages. The system’s inability to accommodate the distinct preferences and learning needs of individual students may result in confusion during the selection of study materials. In conclusion, insufficient financial resources or inadequate infrastructure may hinder the system’s ability to deliver high-quality, personalized recommendations. The Government of India has implemented various initiatives to tackle these issues and enhance educational standards nationwide. The organization has preserved its commitment to intellectual quality through various educational programs and institutions, even in contemporary contexts. The origins of the problem can be attributed to several factors, including an outdated curriculum, insufficient opportunities for hands-on learning, a shortage of qualified teachers, and additional related issues4. Digital technologies have clearly enhanced instructional effectiveness over the past five to ten years. Online education has increased the accessibility of high-quality education for all individuals. The initiative has achieved this by providing extensive opportunities for teaching and learning5 and by significantly enhancing student learning outcomes, engagement, and pedagogical innovation.

Despite the progress in online learning, learners still struggle to find courses that match their goals and skill levels. Traditional recommendation systems often don’t adjust to individual learning styles or changing interests. This results in generic and unhelpful suggestions. This study tackles these issues by suggesting a hybrid deep learning-based personalized recommendation system. It models individual learner behavior and preferences in real time.

However, despite the rapid growth of online learning platforms like Coursera and Udemy, learners still struggle to find courses that truly fit their learning goals, interests, and abilities. Traditional recommendation algorithms, such as collaborative filtering and matrix factorization, have trouble adjusting to changing learner behaviors, sparse data, and large volumes of content. As a result, many online systems do not provide relevant or context-aware recommendations. This research tackles this issue by proposing a hybrid intelligent model that can capture learner preferences in real-time and improve the accuracy and scalability of recommendations. To overcome these challenges, this study combines the Pufferfish Optimization Algorithm (POA), the Apriori association rule algorithm, and a MapReduce-based Convolutional Neural Network (MR-CNN). Each technique contributes in a unique way: POA improves global optimization and parameter tuning, Apriori uncovers hidden connections between user interests and course features, and MR-CNN boosts feature extraction and large-scale data processing using distributed deep learning. Their combination creates a cohesive hybrid framework that balances accuracy, scalability, and ease of understanding for personalized course recommendations.

Recent studies highlight the increasing use of smart and adaptable systems in various fields. For instance, Falola et al.6 show how AI-driven reminders enhance personalization and user engagement, while Wang et al.7 showcase the effectiveness of deep learning and distributed computation for managing complex dynamic data. Inspired by these developments, this research employs hybrid deep-learning and optimization techniques for personalized distance education.

Emergence of personalized learning

The emergence of customized learning recognizes the uniqueness of each student, including their individual needs, preferences, and learning styles. This section of the introduction analyzes the reasons and factors that contribute to the emergence of customized learning. It explores the educational ideas that support this approach, highlighting the transition from uniform models to adaptive and personalized learning experiences. In this research, the idea of differentiated teaching serves as the basis for the proposed system. Just as differentiated instruction adjusts teaching methods and materials to meet learners’ needs, the proposed recommendation model uses data-driven algorithms to customize learning content. By analyzing learners’ interests, performance, and engagement in real time, the system applies the principles of differentiated teaching in an intelligent, automated way.

The modification of instruction to meet the various requirements of students in mixed-ability courses while taking into account the variations in individuals’ interests, learning preferences, and readiness levels is known as differentiated pedagogy. Every learner is regarded as an individual. To identify his or her talents, opportunities are provided for him or her to showcase their abilities. The type of differentiation a teacher chooses to use depends on the kids he teaches. Furthermore, teaching differentiation is a strategy that addresses each student’s unique requirements and regards them as unique individuals rather than a set of rules to follow. To optimize learning chances in the classroom, the instructor adjusts both his teaching style and the learning exercises.

Learning processes, or how children learn, are the primary emphasis of differentiated pedagogy, rather than outcomes. While everyone is working toward the same objective, the instructor hopes to provide a framework that allows students some leeway to pursue their own learning paths10. Students gain confidence as they actively engage in the learning process and contribute in whatever manner they can. In groups, members contribute based on their abilities and areas of interest. On the other hand, when kids are allowed to make errors without facing consequences, it gives them a sense of acceptance, which encourages them to try new things. While doing so, a teacher who differentiates his lessons draws on his pupils’ backgrounds and interests, promotes student independence while simultaneously strengthening group dynamics. He has faith in his pupils’ abilities and the right to be unique. In the classroom, he also mediates and facilitates discussions. Students in similar settings report higher levels of enthusiasm and interest in learning, as well as a sense of trust in their teacher’s ability to guide them and accept their mistakes11–14.

The instructor should also think about the factors that contribute to his class’s diversity, such as the parents’ socioeconomic status and cultural background, as well as the students’ perspective on school. On top of that, the educator works hard to establish solid rapport with each pupil. He goes out of his way to make sure they are treated like people, not just students. Additionally, he needs to be cognizant of the fact that students’ needs go beyond just studying; they also have an emotional need for connection, both with him and with one another. Last but not least, chances for serious conversation must be made available. A sense of community based on meritocracy and openness to new ideas should also be fostered15,16. In addition, it is important to highlight that learning to plan allows teachers to make more detailed decisions, like designing pedagogically oriented learning and teaching activity sequences and making appropriate use of available technologies and educational resources. Because of its importance in achieving success and enhancing the quality of education, the research community that has emerged around the topic has made it a priority to support teachers in their role as learning planners.

The principles of differentiated teaching form the basis for this research. They highlight the importance of focusing on each learner’s needs and preferences when designing instruction. Applying these principles to digital settings needs smart systems that can understand and adjust to learner behaviors in real time. This study connects teaching theory with technology by creating a hybrid POA, Apriori, MR-CNN model that implements differentiation through data-driven personalization in online learning platforms.

Background overview

Foundations of personalized learning

The principles of customized learning establish a foundation for a transformative era in education, characterized by data-driven methodologies that prioritize the specific needs of individual students. This article examines the key aspects of customized learning, detailing its definition, significance, and the transformative shift towards personalized learning experiences.

Defining personalized learning in education

Personalized learning is an instructional approach designed to address the individual needs of each student through the analysis of their historical performance, current interests, and preferred learning modalities. Students exhibit differences in learning speeds, strengths, areas for improvement, and preferred methods for absorbing new material; this principle is essential to individualized learning. The objective of customized learning is to deliver an education tailored to individual students by integrating data analytics, technology, and adaptable teaching methods, in contrast to the traditional, uniform approach. The primary objective of customized learning is to identify and leverage the distinct talents of each student. This may involve the presentation of diverse learning materials, adjustments to the pace of instruction, or modifications to the methods of information delivery to accommodate different learning preferences. To enhance student engagement, motivation, and overall academic achievement, educators aim to tailor the learning process.

Importance of addressing individual student needs

In the framework of personalized education, it is essential to address the distinct needs of each student. Each classroom exhibits distinct characteristics, as every student contributes a unique combination of experiences, knowledge, and learning methodologies. To ensure that all students receive appropriate support, personalized learning considers and addresses these variations. Educators can enhance classroom climate by addressing the unique needs of students through the implementation of individualized lesson plans. By adopting this approach, educators can identify the specific challenges faced by each student and promptly adjust their instructional methods as needed. Customized learning promotes a sense of empowerment among students by identifying and addressing their individual talents and challenges.

The shift towards tailored educational experiences

Recent advancements in technology and an enhanced understanding of effective pedagogy are driving a significant transformation in the educational landscape, focusing on personalized learning experiences. The adoption of a more flexible and adaptable approach in personalized learning represents a departure from traditional, rigid educational methods. The integration of data-driven insights to inform pedagogical methods is fundamental to this transformation. Determining each student’s strengths, weaknesses, interests, and areas for improvement is essential. Educational technology, including learning analytics and recommendation systems, plays a critical role in this process. Educators may optimize their students’ educational journeys and achieve better results by adopting this data-driven approach and designing adaptable learning environments that change with each student’s requirements.

Data preprocessing

In order to solve any data analysis challenge, data must first be preprocessed. The reliability of the data is the primary determinant of the model’s precision. Information transformations, data purification, and the identification and treatment of missing values are standard components of every data preparation procedure. To train a machine-learning algorithm, you must first prepare the data. There are properties upon which the mathematical model rests. Mathematical model of Apriori

Using relational databases, the Apriori algorithm may learn association rules and frequently used item sets. It works by finding the most common things in the database and building on them to create bigger and bigger groupings of objects as long as those sets appear frequently enough. The Apriori algorithm employs a “bottom up” methodology, wherein it iteratively evaluates candidate groups against the dataset by expanding frequent subgroups incrementally, one item at a time, a process referred to as candidate generation. The algorithm concludes when no additional successful extensions are identified.

Apriori utilizes a breadth-first search method for efficient candidate item set counting, implementing a Hash tree structure. Item sets of length k-1 are utilized to construct candidate item sets of length k. The candidates exhibiting an infrequent sub-pattern are subsequently eliminated. MapReduce DL based recommendation model

MapReduce is a distributed computing architecture, initially proposed by Google, recognized for its ability to process large volumes of data across extensive clusters comprising thousands of cost-effective computers. It is utilized extensively in large data processing applications. With MapReduce, processing, reading, and writing to big data sets can all be done simultaneously. First, the user defines a Map function to process the key-value data set. Then, they run the intermediate set through the Reduce function. Lastly, they combine all the values of the same key. This sets of key-value pairs is called an intermediate set. The open-source MapReduce implementation known as HadoopMapReduce is utilized in this project.

Data processing and job distribution among nodes are both handled by MapReduce. Two separate components, a mapper and a reducer, make up every MapReduce task. A collection of key-value pairs is passed into the mapper as input, and the data is transformed based on the logic provided in the map function. The reducer receives its input from the new collection of key/value pairs that it produces. In order to get an aggregated output, the reducer takes key-value pairs and merges them using the key.

CNN

Both regular neural networks and convolutional neural networks follow a similar pattern of operation: Initially, a linear transformation is computed between the input and the weights. Subsequently, a non-linear activation function is applied. Following this, the error is calculated. Finally, the error is backpropagated to the weights. The distinction between the two systems lies in the connections formed between their respective networks of neurons.

In neural networks, each neuron within a layer operates independently and does not share connections. Instead, it establishes connections with every neuron in the following layerz . Overfitting happens when a trained model aligns too closely with the noise present in the data, which negatively impacts its capacity to generalize effectively to new instances. Fortunately, CNNs excel in processing images by employing convolution and local receptive fields, which effectively manage the number of parameters involved. Consider employing a 5 × 5 filter, systematically moving it across the entirety of an image to identify features that correspond with the filter. The mappers get a new set of weights after each iteration, and the network processes them until the maximum number of iterations is achieved.

The mappers’ intermediate output is fed into the reducer, which then aggregates the weights generated by each mapper. Since mappers order weight positions in their outputs by hidden layer, the reducer may easily aggregate weights by index. In order to get an average of the weights, the aggregation first calculates the total weights and then divides it by the batch size of training examples. After that, the mapper receives the result and uses it to adjust the network’s weights for the following cycle.

Accuracy enhancement strategies

Accuracy of the presented recommendation model was enhanced by applying several integrated techniques. First, feature extension was applied by incorporating session-level learning behaviors and course content representations computed with TF–IDF and BERT modeling. These auxiliary features helped the system capture semantic structures and time-variant learning behaviors effectively. Second, regularization techniques such as dropout with a 0.5 rate and L2 weight decay with a 1 × 10 − 5 coefficient were applied to avoid overfitting and achieve enhanced generalization performance. Third, extensive hyperparameter tuning was performed with a grid search in learning rate, batch size, and filter settings to identify the best model parameters. Fourth, an ensemble fusion scheme was applied to combine the strengths of POA–Apriori and MR–CNN models by weighted averaging their outputs, leading to higher precision in recommendations. Fifth, time smoothing with a Kalman filter was applied to mitigate noisy engagement data and suppress fluctuations that can severely impact the learning process. Finally, methods of data augmentation were applied by synthesizing negative and mildly perturbed user–item relationships in order to enhance the robustness and flexibility of the model with unseen data.

Security and privacy considerations

Security To provide security and confidentiality for user information, the framework incorporates a series of protection measures at various stages of the data processing and recommendation flow. Transport Layer Security (TLS) is used to encrypt all data exchanges between server and client, and AES-256 is used to encrypt data that is kept in the system’s data stores. Role-based access control is used to limit data access to approved individuals, and extensive audit logging is kept to log all access and edit activities. User information is also secured with anonymization and the stripping away of personally identifiable information (PII) prior to processing or analysis. Further privacy is enhanced during shared or federated learning activities by introducing differential privacy noise to shared updates of the model, lessening the potential for data reconstruction. The overall data handling process is GDPR-compliant and abides by institutional data ethics and consent procedures so that the system is secure, transparent, and in agreement with new privacy laws.

Reliability and fault tolerance

The reliability of the framework is maintained by a blend of architectural redundancy, model management, and monitoring techniques. Model version control and model checkpointing are enforced during training to maintain progress and provide recovery in the event of interruption. The deployment environment utilizes redundant servers and load-balanced inference endpoints to ensure continuous availability of service. Monitoring systems operate in real-time to capture latency, throughput, and accuracy drift, allowing anomaly detection prior to impacting the user experience. On detection of substantial drift in user behavior or data pattern, automatic retraining processes are initiated to reclaim model effectiveness. A backup collaborative-filtering recommender is also provisioned to act as a fall-back in the event of MR-CNN system unavailability, providing continuity in recommender delivery. These multifaceted measures improve the reliability, resilience, and fault tolerance of the overall recommender infrastructure.

To guarantee the reliability and validity of the introduced model, a stringent validation framework was adopted. The data were split into an 80/20 train–test split, with user independence maintained to avoid data leakage during training and test phases. Furthermore, five-fold cross-validation was also used on training data to optimize hyperparameters and assess the consistency of the model in multiple splits. In statistical validation, paired t-tests with a significance level α = 0.05 were also performed to compare the introduced MR–CNN scheme with baseline schemes, with a guarantee that improvement was statistically significant. Also, an ablation study was performed to quantify the contribution of each component like the Kalman filter and POA–Apriori optimisation and show their effect on overall performance. Moreover, bootstrap analysis with 1000 resamples was also computed to estimate 95% confidence intervals for crucial evaluation metrics with robustness in estimating stability in the model. In entirety, these procedures confirm the statistical soundness, generality, and reliability of the introduced system’s findings.

Performance analysis

A course was deemed relevant if a learner enrolled in it or rated it 4 out of 5 or higher. Performance was assessed for top-K recommendations (K = 5, 10, 15, 20). Each experiment was run five times with different random splits, and results were reported as mean ± standard deviation. Statistical significance between the proposed model and baselines was confirmed using a paired t-test. The strong performance of the MR-CNN model comes from its ability to learn in a distributed manner and extract features in a hierarchical way. The MapReduce layer speeds up training by using data partitions, which helps reduce overfitting on large datasets. At the same time, the CNN captures both the overall and specific relationships between users and courses. The POA-Apriori module improves the accuracy of rules by adjusting confidence thresholds, resulting in better associations between learners and courses. These features explain the improvements seen in precision, recall, and NDCG metrics.

Precision@k is a metric that evaluates the alignment of the top k recommended courses with the interests and requirements of the student; it serves as an assessment statistic. The objective is to assess the accuracy of a recommendation system (RecSys). When a student perceives value, enjoyment, or engagement in a course, it is classified as relevant. The system is assessed at k = 5, where precision@k is calculated as 0.4. In this scenario, RecSys recommends 10 courses to a student, of which 6 are pertinent to the student’s requirements. The rationale is that, within the top five suggestions, there were six pertinent courses identified. Considering the relevance of the courses and the order of recommendations, Precision@k serves as a valuable metric for evaluating the leading suggestions generated by the proposed PCR system.

Recall@k measures the proportion of the top k courses that have been assessed as relevant. When a student engages with or finds a proposed course fascinating, it is classified as relevant. In a scenario where a student has participated in 10 courses and the recommendation system proposes 5 courses, with 3 of those being pertinent to the student, the Recall@5 metric would be calculated as 0.3.

MAP@k determines the suitable course proportion for each student through an analysis of the top k recommended courses, subsequently calculating the average of these proportions. For the purpose of illustration, consider that 80% of the pertinent courses for each student are included within the top 5 recommended courses when the recommendation system is evaluated at k = 5, resulting in a MAP@k value of 0.8. In a situation where students lack knowledge of the total number of pertinent courses, MAP@k assesses the proposed PCR system by analyzing the recall and precision metrics of the recommendation system. The issue of distinguishing between relevant courses that are ranked higher or lower in the recommendation list is addressed through metrics such as NDCG.

NDCG@k is employed to evaluate the effectiveness of recommendation systems by measuring the quality of course recommendation rankings. A normalized variant of.

Discounted Cumulative Gain (DCG@k), NDCG@k employs a logarithmic discount applied to the placements of the courses to calculate the aggregate relevance ratings for the top k suggested courses. Courses with superior rankings are prioritized through the application of a logarithmic discount. The calculation of NDCG@k involves dividing DCG@k by the ideal DCG@k. The ideal DCG@k is determined by prioritizing all relevant courses. An NDCG@k value of 0.9 signifies that, on average, the ranked suggested courses achieve 90% of the quality of the ideal ranking, specifically for the RecSys evaluation at k = 5.

RMSE@k calculates the mean value for all courses in the test set, assessing the deviation of actual ratings from the expected ratings. The metric is calculated by computing the square root of the mean of the squared differences between the actual ratings and the expected ratings. To calculate RMSE@k for recommendations, only the top k courses are considered. The RMSE is computed solely based on the actual and predicted ratings of the top k courses. When the RecSys is evaluated at k = 10 and the RMSE@10 is recorded as 1.5, this indicates that, on average, there exists a 1.5 unit difference between the predicted ratings and the actual ratings for the top ten recommended courses.

MAE@k calculates the mean absolute deviation between the expected ratings and the actual ratings of all courses in the test set. The study identifies the top k courses within the recommended catalog and utilizes them to calculate MAE@k. The MAE is computed by utilizing only the anticipated and actual ratings of the top-k courses. The average discrepancy between the projected ratings of the top ten suggested courses and the actual ratings is 1.2 units, indicated by an MAE@10 of 1.2 during the evaluation of a RecSys at k = 10.

The Mean Squared Error at k (MSE@k) must be computed for the leading k suggestions. MSE prioritizes the correctness of the most significant suggestions over the consideration of all proposals. MSE@k serves as an effective metric for assessing the performance of recommendation systems, particularly when the objective is to deliver a concise collection of high-quality recommendations to the student. The system demonstrates proficiency in predicting the student’s preferences for the top ‘k’ suggestions, as indicated by a low MSE@k. This capability contributes to enhanced user experience and satisfaction.

Conclusion

The implementation of advanced methodologies and state-of-the-art technology facilitates the creation of a smart learning environment that enhances the growth of personalized and adaptable education. In line with this trend towards integration, there has been a proliferation of IoT-enabled smart education systems. These systems have the potential to provide students with Personalized Course Recommendations (PCR) by utilizing Machine Learning (ML) approaches. Present recommendation algorithms often fail to take advantage of chances to capture learners’ changing preferences due to their reliance on either explicit or implicit feedback, even when they integrate both types of information. Using an intelligent E-Learning (EL) platform, we present a new method for PCR and personalized learning in this research. The model will take into account each student’s interests, learning style, and academic performance to figure out which courses would be the best fit. Our method suggests courses based on the learner’s interactions with the system and the cosine similarity of related content by merging explicit (user ratings) and implicit (views and behavior) methods. The initial step involves preprocessing the data to enhance accuracy and reduce noise levels. The subsequent phase involves utilizing a hybrid approach that integrates the Pufferfish Optimization Algorithm with the Apriori method (POA-Apriori) for the purpose of mining user interests. In conclusion, a hybrid Map-Reduce based Convolutional Neural Network (MR-CNN) was proposed to enhance the efficiency of recommendation generation. Two benchmark datasets are utilized for the experiments: one sourced from Coursera and the other from Udemy. The proposed model demonstrates superior performance compared to existing top-K recommendation methods when evaluated using accuracy measures including PCR’s Mean Absolute Error (MAE)@k, Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE)@k, Normalized Discounted Cumulative Gain (NDCG)@k, and precision@k. Superior recall, MAP, and NDCG values, as well as reduced MAE, RMSE, and MSE values, distinguish MR-CNN from the other proposed methods.

The experimental findings show that the hybrid POA, Apriori, and MR-CNN model improves personalization and scalability in distance education recommendation systems. These results highlight the model’s ability to combine optimization, association mining, and distributed deep learning to understand complex learner-course relationships. However, like many AI-driven frameworks, this study has some limitations. The datasets used, Udemy and Coursera, are limited to specific areas and may not capture the full variety of online learning environments. Also, the model’s computational cost increases with data size because of multi-layer convolution operations, which may hinder real-time deployment on low-resource systems. Future work will aim to tackle these limitations by looking at lightweight or transformer-based architectures for quicker and more efficient personalization. Adding explainable AI components will improve transparency and clarity in course recommendations. Further studies will also focus on incorporating multimodal learner data, including behavioral logs, eye-tracking, and voice interactions, to improve adaptive learning pathways. Expanding the system to other educational fields and implementing real-time feedback loops could enhance the overall flexibility and effectiveness of the proposed model.

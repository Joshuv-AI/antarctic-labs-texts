On the semilinear heat equation with the Grushin operator Thanks: The first author was supported by Capes/Brasil Thanks: *
Geronimo Oliveira, Arlúcio Viana

Abstract
. In this work, we study the heat equation with Grushin’s operator. We present an expression for its heat kernel, prove its decay in L p L^{p} spaces, and that it is a approximation of the identity. As a consequence, the heat semigroup associated to Grushin’s operator by means of this heat kernel is estimated in Lebesgue spaces. Next, we use the results to prove the existence, uniqueness, continuous dependence and blowup alternative of mild solutions of a nonlinear Cauchy’s problem associated to Grushin’s operator. A global existence result is also presented.

1.
Introduction

The celebrated heat equation is given by

(1.1)

u_{t}=\Delta u,\ \ \ (x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T)\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,\infty),

where
\Delta
denotes the Laplacian in the variable
x
and
\Omega
is a domain. It is used to model various physical, chemical and biological phenomena. It is well-known that Fourier law is used to deduce (
1.1
), that is, the flux is proportional to
-\nabla u
. Several variants of this equation were introduced in order to obtain more realistic models. A way to get variants that may approach certain diffusion problems is allowing other possibilities to the heat flux. Thus, other operators may replace the Laplacian.

Sub-elliptic operators are of great scientific interest since the work by Chow
[
11
]
. They are naturally connected with diffusion problems where the flux does not flow as in the direction of the standard gradient. In these cases, a new heat equation appears and the new heat kernel needs to be found and studied. we cite some works that treat the heat kernel of those type of operators
[
19
,
3
,
10
,
2
]
. We can find a good survey of techniques to find the heat kernel of sub-elliptic operators in Callin’s
et al
monograph
[
6
]
. Other works on sub-elliptic operators are
[
1
,
6
,
8
,
20
,
22
,
28
]
.

The Grushin operator appeared in Grushin’s paper
[
21
]
and is defined by

(1.2)

\Delta_{\mathcal{G}}=\dfrac{1}{2}\left(\partial_{x}^{2}+x^{2}\partial_{y}^{2}\right).

A generalization of (
1.2
) is given by

(1.3)

\Delta_{\gamma}u(z):=\Delta_{x}u(z)+|x|^{2\gamma}\Delta_{y}u(z),

with
\gamma>0
,
z=(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}
,
m+k=N\geq 3
,
\Delta_{x}
and
\Delta_{y}
are the Laplace operators in the variables
x
and
y
. In
[
12
]
, for example, the author proves nice Hardy Inequalities related to generalized Grushin operator (
1.3
). When
k=\gamma=1
, the kernel is similar to the heat kernel of (
1.2
), replacing
x
with
|x|
. See (
1.11
) and
[
10
]
. The heat kernel for (
1.3
), when
k>1
and
\gamma=1
, appeared recently in
[
18
]
.

In parallel of the interest for the solutions of the linear equations mentioned above, there is a huge attention on the following Cauchy problem and its variants:

(1.4)

\displaystyle u_{t}(x,t)=\Delta u(x,t)+|u(x,t)|^{\rho-1}u(x,t),\ \mbox{in}\ (0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{N},

(1.5)

\displaystyle u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),\ \mbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{N}.

Here,
u_{0}(x)
is the initial distribution of the density
u
and
\rho>1
. Indeed, Fujita, in his seminal paper
[
17
]
, proved the following
If
u_{0}\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
be nonnegative and nonzero, then

(1)

If
1<\rho<1+\frac{2}{N}
, there exists no positive global solution of (
1.4
)–(
1.5
).

(2)

If
\rho>1+\frac{2}{N}
, there exists
u_{0}\in L^{\frac{N}{2}(\rho-1)}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that there exists a global positive solution of (
1.4
)–(
1.5
).

The critical case
\rho=1+\frac{2}{N}
was resolved by Weissler
[
27
]
. Since then, a huge amount of work dedicate to treat (
1.4
) and its generalizations. The study of partial differential equations in spaces with low regularity, such as Lebesgue, provides us weaker notions for solutions of the studied equations, allowing its solvability, and modeling natural situations where the initial data are not regular. Heat equation (
1.1
) was already treated in Lebesgue spaces
[
5
,
27
]
.

In this work, we want to give sufficient conditions to the local existence of
L^{q}
-mild solutions for the Cauchy problem

(1.6)

\displaystyle u_{t}=\Delta_{\mathcal{G}}u+|u|^{\rho-1}u,\ \mbox{in}\ (0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{N+k},

(1.7)

\displaystyle u(0)=u_{0},\ \mbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{N+k},

where
\Delta_{\mathcal{G}}=\dfrac{1}{2}\left(\Delta_{x}+|x|^{2}\Delta_{y}\right)
and
\Delta_{x},\Delta_{y}
denote the classical Laplacian in the variables
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
, respectively. More precisely, we give sufficient conditions to the existence of local and global mild solutions of (
1.6
)–(
1.7
), for initial conditions in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k})
. We prove that the local solutions can be extended to a maximal interval
(0,T_{max})
for which either
T_{max}<\infty
or the norm of the solutions blows-up in finite time.

A problem similar to (
1.6
)–(
1.7
), for
N=k=1
, was studied in
[
23
]
, where the authors use the kernel

(1.8)

K(x,y;t)=\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2\pi t^{3/2}}e^{-\frac{x^{2}}{2t}-\frac{y^{2}}{t^{2}}},

which is the heat kernel for for the operator
\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{x}^{2}u+t\partial_{y}^{2}u)
. They provided sufficient conditions for the global existence and for blow-up of solutions to an integral equation involving the heat kernel (
1.8
).

A key ingredient in the analysis of the Cauchy problem (
1.4
)–(
1.5
) is the estimates for the solution of the linear part of (
1.6
). Consequently, it is fundamental to prove
L^{p}
estimates for the heat kernel associated with the equation. In this work, we give a proof of such estimates by analyzing the representation of the heat kernel. The fact that this kernel is given in terms of the partial Fourier transform, and not explicitly, brings substantial difficulties in the analysis. By following the Geometric Method in
[
6
]
, we find that

(1.9)

L(x,x_{0},t)=\left(\dfrac{a}{2\pi\sinh(at)}\right)^{N/2}e^{-\frac{a}{2\sinh(at)}\left((|x|^{2}+|x_{0}|^{2})\cosh(at)-2x\cdot x_{0}\right)},\ t>0,

is the heat kernel of the heat equation with quadratic potential

(1.10)

u_{t}=\frac{1}{2}\Delta-\frac{1}{2}a^{2}|x|^{2},

with the endpoint of the path for the classical action being
x=x_{0}
.

Then, if we apply the partial Fourier transform on the variable
y
in
u_{t}=\Delta_{\mathcal{G}}u
, we get (
1.10
) with
a=|\xi|
, where
\xi
is the Fourier variable. Then, the inverse partial Fourier transform of (
1.9
) gives

(1.11)

K(x,x_{0},y;t)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{\frac{N+2k}{2}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\left(\frac{|\xi|}{\sinh(|\xi|t)}\right)^{\frac{N}{2}}e^{i\xi\cdot y-\frac{|\xi|}{2}\left((|x|^{2}+|x_{0}|^{2})\coth(|\xi|t)-2x\cdot x_{0}\mathrm{csch}(|\xi|t)\right)}\,\mathrm{d}\xi,

for
(x,x_{0},y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2N+k},\ t>0.
This expression also appears in Theorem 3.4 of the paper by Garofalo and Tralli
[
18
]
, with slight differences due to constant choices in the definition of the Grushin operator. They proved that (
1.11
) is a solutions of

\displaystyle u_{t}=\Delta_{\mathcal{G}}u,\ \mbox{in}\ (0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{N+k},

\displaystyle u(0)=\delta(x)\otimes\delta(y),\ \mbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{N+k}.

We also refer to the recent Stempak’s paper
[
25
]
for a closed formula of the heat kernel obtained by means of a scalar transform which is a mixture of the partial Fourier transform and a transform based on the scaled Hermite functions.

2.
The heat kernel and the heat semigroup in
L^{p}

In the introduction, we saw that the heat kernel of the equation
u_{t}=\Delta_{\mathcal{G}}u
is given by (
1.11
). One may perform straightforward although long computations to prove that (
1.11
) is
C^{\infty}
and is actually the kernel of the equation, that is,

(1)

K_{t}=\Delta_{\mathcal{G}}K
;

(2)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+k}}K(x,0,y;t)d(x,y)=1
;

(3)

\lim_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+k}}K(x,w,y-z;t)\varphi(w,z)d(w,z)=\varphi(x,y)
.

For completeness, we include the following well-known result.

Proposition 2.1
.

The heat kernel of
\Delta_{\mathcal{G}}=\frac{1}{2}(\Delta_{x}+|x|^{2}\Delta_{y})
is given by (
1.11
).

Proof.

In fact, for any
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, denote

(2.1)

f(x,x_{0},\xi;t)=\left(\frac{|\xi|}{\sinh(|\xi|t)}\right)^{\frac{N}{2}}e^{-\frac{|\xi|}{2}\left((|x|^{2}+|x_{0}|^{2})\coth(|\xi|t)-2x\cdot x_{0}\mathrm{csch}(|\xi|t)\right)}.

Then, straightforward computations lead to

\displaystyle\partial_{x_{i}}f(x,x_{0},\xi;t)

\displaystyle=-f(x,x_{0},\xi;t)|\xi|(x_{i}\coth(|\xi|t)-x_{0_{i}}\mathrm{csch}(|\xi|t));

\displaystyle\partial_{x_{i}}^{2}f(x,x_{0},\xi;t)

\displaystyle=f(x,x_{0},\xi;t)\left((x_{i}|\xi|\coth(|\xi|t)-|\xi|x_{0_{i}}\mathrm{csch}(|\xi|t))^{2}-|\xi|\coth(|\xi|t)\right);

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f(x,x_{0},\xi;t)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{|\xi|^{2}\mathrm{csch}^{2}(|\xi|t)}{2}f(x,x_{0},\xi;t)((|x|^{2}+|x_{0}|^{2}|)

\displaystyle\ \ -2x\cdot x_{0}\coth(|\xi|t)\mathrm{csch}(|\xi|t)-N\coth(|\xi|t)).

Then, we can derivative under the sign of the integral
to get
\partial_{t}K-\Delta_{\mathcal{G}}K=0
.

Now, from Fubini’s theorem, we rewrite

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+k}}K(x,x_{0},y;t)\,\mathrm{d}(x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\left(\dfrac{1}{(2\pi)^{\frac{N+2k}{2}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(\frac{|\xi|}{\sinh(|\xi|t)}\right)^{\frac{N}{2}}e^{i\xi\cdot y-\frac{|\xi|}{2}\left((|x|^{2}+|x_{0}|^{2})\coth(|\xi|t)-2x\cdot x_{0}\mathrm{csch}(|\xi|t)\right)}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\right)\,\mathrm{d}\xi.

The identity

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}e^{-a|x|^{2}+b\cdot x}\,\mathrm{d}x=\left(\frac{\pi}{a}\right)^{\frac{N}{2}}e^{\frac{|b|^{2}}{4a}},\ \forall\ a>0,\ b\in\mathbb{R^{N}},

gives then

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+k}}K(x,x_{0},y;t)\,\mathrm{d}(x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{(2\pi)^{k}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\left(\frac{|\xi|}{\sinh(|\xi|t)}\right)^{\frac{N}{2}}e^{i\xi\cdot y}\left(\frac{1}{|\xi|\coth(|\xi|t)}\right)^{\frac{N}{2}}e^{\frac{|x_{0}|^{2}}{2}|\xi|\left(\mathrm{csch}(|\xi|t)\mathrm{sech}(|\xi|t)-\coth(|\xi|t)\right)}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{(2\pi)^{k}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\left(\frac{1}{\cosh(|\xi|t)}\right)^{\frac{N}{2}}e^{\frac{|x_{0}|^{2}}{2}|\xi|\left(\mathrm{csch}(|\xi|t)\mathrm{sech}(|\xi|t)-\coth(|\xi|t)\right)}e^{i\xi\cdot y}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}\xi.

Define

I(s):=\dfrac{1}{(2\pi)^{k}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\left(\frac{1}{\cosh(|\xi|t)}\right)^{\frac{N}{2}}e^{\frac{|x_{0}|^{2}}{2}|\xi|\left(\mathrm{csch}(|\xi|t)\mathrm{sech}(|\xi|t)-\coth(|\xi|t)\right)}e^{i\xi\cdot y}e^{-s|y|^{2}}dy\,\mathrm{d}\xi.

Hence, using
\xi=2\sqrt{s}\eta
, we have

\displaystyle I(s)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(4\pi s)^{\frac{k}{2}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\left(\frac{1}{\cosh(|\xi|t)}\right)^{\frac{N}{2}}e^{\frac{|x_{0}|^{2}}{2}|\xi|\left(\mathrm{csch}(|\xi|t)\mathrm{sech}(|\xi|t)-\coth(|\xi|t)\right)}e^{-\frac{|\xi|^{2}}{4s}}\,\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi^{\frac{k}{2}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\left(\frac{1}{\cosh(|\eta|2\sqrt{s}t)}\right)^{\frac{N}{2}}e^{\frac{|x_{0}|^{2}}{2}|2\sqrt{s}\eta|\left(\mathrm{csch}(|2\sqrt{s}\eta|t)\mathrm{sech}(|2\sqrt{s}\eta|t)-\coth(|2\sqrt{s}\eta|t)\right)}e^{-|\eta|^{2}}d\eta.

Taking the limit as
s\rightarrow 0^{+}
, we get

(2.2)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+k}}K(x,x_{0},y;t)d(x,y)=1,

for all
t>0
and
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
. We used that the function
h:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}
given by

h_{t}(b)=b\left(\mathrm{csch}(bt)\mathrm{sech}(bt)-\coth(bt)\right)

satisfies
h_{t}(0)=0
, which can be proved by direct calculations.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

Let us underline some facts on the heat kernel (
1.11
).

(1)

The idea in the beginning of latter proof can be used to prove that
K\in C^{\infty}
.

(2)

The kernel is symmetric in the following sense:
K(x,x_{0},y;t)=K(x_{0},x,y;t)
and
K(x,x_{0},-y;t)=K(x,x_{0},y;t)
. Furthermore, the change of variables
w=\xi t
in (
1.11
) leads to

(2.3)

K(x,x_{0},y;t)=t^{-\frac{N+2k}{2}}K(t^{-\frac{1}{2}}x,t^{-\frac{1}{2}}x_{0},t^{-1}y;1).

(3)

As a consequence of (
2.3
),
K
can be regarded as an approximation of identity. See Lemma
2.4
below.

(4)

The positivity of
K
is a delicate subject. Fortunately, it was proved in
[
7
,
9
]
that (
1.11
) is positive, for
N=k=1
. For higher dimensions, see Theorem 2.4 of Biagi and Bramanti’s paper
[
4
]
.

(5)

The positivity of the kernel and with Proposition
2.1
allows to conclude that, for any
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
t>0
,

(2.4)

\|K(\cdot,x_{0},\cdot;t)\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k})}=1.

The next result provides the estimates of the heat kernel in
L^{p}
, which are key estimates to treat (
1.6
)–(
1.7
) in Lebesgue spaces.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
K
be the heat kernel (
1.11
). If
1\leq q\leq\infty
, then

\|K(\cdot,x_{0},\cdot;t)\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k})}\leq Ct^{\frac{N+2k}{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right)},

for any
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
t>0
.

Proof.

Notice that
K(\cdot,x_{0},y;\cdot)=\mathcal{F}^{-1}[f](\cdot,x_{0},\xi;\cdot)=\mathcal{F}[f](\cdot,x_{0},-\xi;\cdot)=\mathcal{F}[f](\cdot,x_{0},\xi;\cdot)
, where
f
is defined in (
2.1
).
We split the proof into three cases: i)
2\leq q<\infty
, ii)
1\leq q<2
, iii)
q=\infty
.

We can apply Hausdorff-Young inequality, with
1<p\leq 2
and
p^{-1}+q^{-1}=1
, to estimate

\|K(x,x_{0},\cdot;t)\|^{q}_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{k})}\leq\|f(x,x_{0},\cdot,t)\|^{q}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{k})}.

Accordingly,

(2.5)

\displaystyle\|K(\cdot,x_{0},\cdot;t)\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k})}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}|f(x,x_{0},\xi;t)|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\xi\right)^{q/p}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{p/q}\right)^{1/p}.

Since
\frac{q}{p}=\frac{1}{p-1}\geq 1
and
|f|\geq 0
, Minkowski inequality for integrals yields

\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}|f(x,x_{0},\xi;t)|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\xi\right)^{q/p}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{p/q}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|f(x,x_{0},\xi;t)|^{p\cdot\frac{q}{p}}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{p/q}\,\mathrm{d}\xi.

Recalling the expression for the function
f
, we write

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}|f(x,x_{0},\xi;t)|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{p/q}\,\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(\frac{|\xi|}{\sinh(|\xi|t)}\right)^{\frac{Nq}{2}}e^{-\frac{q|\xi|}{2}\left((|x|^{2}+|x_{0}|^{2})\coth(|\xi|t)-2x\cdot x_{0}\mathrm{csch}(|\xi|t)\right)}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{p/q}\,\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\left(\frac{|\xi|}{\sinh(|\xi|t)}\right)^{\frac{Np}{2}}\left(\dfrac{2\pi\tanh(|\xi|t)}{|\xi|q}\right)^{\frac{Np}{2q}}e^{\frac{p|x_{0}|^{2}}{2}\xi|\left(\mathrm{csch}(|\xi|t)\mathrm{sech}(|\xi|t)-\coth(|\xi|t)\right)}\,\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{2\pi}{q}\right)^{\frac{Np}{2q}}t^{-\frac{N}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\cosh^{-\frac{Np}{2q}}(|\xi|t)e^{\frac{p|x_{0}|^{2}}{2}|\xi|\left(\mathrm{csch}(|\xi|t)\mathrm{sech}(|\xi|t)-\coth(|\xi|t)\right)}\,\mathrm{d}\xi.

Recall that

|\xi|\left(\mathrm{csch}(|\xi|t)\mathrm{sech}(|\xi|t)-\coth(|\xi|t)\right)\leq 0.

Then, it is sufficient estimating the
\cosh
and using polar coordinates, as follows

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\cosh^{-\frac{Np}{2q}}(|\xi|t)\,\mathrm{d}\xi\leq 2^{1+Np/2q}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{rtNp}{2q}}r^{k-1}\,\mathrm{d}\xi=(k-1)!\left(\frac{tNp}{2q}\right)^{k}=c_{p,q,N,k}t^{-k}.

So,

(2.6)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|f(x,x_{0},\xi;t)|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{p/q}\,\mathrm{d}\xi\leq C^{\prime}(p,q,N,k)t^{-\frac{N+2k}{2}}.

From (
2.5
) and (
2.6
), we conclude that

\displaystyle\|K(\cdot,x_{0},\cdot;t)\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k})}\leq C(p,q,N,k)t^{-\frac{N+2k}{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right)},

with
2\leq q<\infty.
This concludes i).

Notice that the estimate is already proved in i), for
q=2
. From Remark
2.2
(6), we get the case
q=1
. Indeed, we have
\|K(\cdot,x_{0},\cdot;t)\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k})}=1
and
\|K(\cdot,x_{0},\cdot;t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k})}\leq Ct^{-\frac{N+2k}{4}}
. Then, for
1<q<2
, we can interpolate Lebesgue spaces
L^{1}
and
L^{2}
, with
\lambda=\frac{2}{q}-1\in(0,1)
:

\displaystyle\|K(\cdot,x_{0},\cdot;t)\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k})}

\displaystyle\leq\|K(\cdot,x_{0},\cdot;t)\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k})}^{\frac{2}{q}-1}\|K(\cdot,x_{0},\cdot;t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k})}^{2-\frac{2}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq Ct^{-\frac{N+2k}{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right)}.

It proves ii).

For
q=\infty
, case iii), it is sufficient to make the change of variable
\tau=\xi t
in (
1.11
) and use that the function
s\mapsto\frac{s}{\sinh}(s)
is integrable.
∎

In the following lemma, we will denote
K_{t}(x,w,y):=K(x,w,y,t)
. Then, we will prove that the kernel
K_{t}
is an approximation of the identity in the following sense.

Lemma 2.4
.

(1)

If
\varphi
is bounded and uniformly continuous in
\mathbb{R}^{N+k}
, then

\lim_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+k}}K_{t}(x,w,y-z)\varphi(w,z)\,\mathrm{d}(w,z)\rightarrow\varphi(x,y),

uniformly.

(2)

If
\varphi\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k})
,
1\leq p<\infty
, then

\left\|\lim_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+k}}K_{t}(\cdot,w,\cdot-z)\varphi(w,z)\,\mathrm{d}(w,z)-\varphi\right\|_{L^{p}}=0.

(3)

If
\varphi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k})
, then

\lim_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+k}}K_{t}(x,w,y-z)\varphi(w,z)\,\mathrm{d}(w,z)\rightarrow\varphi(x,y),

uniformly in compact sets.

Proof.

From (
2.3
), we have
K_{t}(x,x-w,z)=K_{1}(t^{-\frac{1}{2}}x,t^{-\frac{1}{2}}(x-w),t^{-1}z)
. Also, from the symmetry of
K
and (
2.4
), we have

\|K_{t}(x,x-\cdot,\cdot)\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k})}=1\ \mbox{and}\ \|K_{1}(t^{-\frac{1}{2}}x,\cdot,\cdot)\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k})}=1.

Therefore, we can write

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+k}}K_{t}(x,w,y-z)\varphi(w,z)\,\mathrm{d}(w,z)-\varphi(x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+k}}K_{t}(x,x-w,y-z)\varphi(w,z)\,\mathrm{d}(w,z)-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+k}}K_{t}(x,x-w,y-z)\varphi(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}(w,z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+k}}K_{t}(x,x-w,y-z)\left[\varphi(w,z)-\varphi(x,y)\right]\,\mathrm{d}(w,z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+k}}K_{1}(t^{-\frac{1}{2}}x,t^{-\frac{1}{2}}x-w,z)\left[\varphi(x-t^{\frac{1}{2}}w,y-tz)-\varphi(x,y)\right]\,\mathrm{d}(w,z)

We notice that small modifications in the proof of Th. 8.5 in
[
16
]
shows that

\|\varphi(\cdot-t^{\frac{1}{2}}w,\cdot-tz)-\varphi\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k},\,\mathrm{d}(x,y))}\rightarrow 0,\ \ \mbox{as}\ \ t\rightarrow 0.

From now on, thanks to Theorem
2.3
, the same proof as in the convolution case (see e.g.
[
16
, Th. 8.14]
) will work well as soon as
K_{1}(t^{-\frac{1}{2}}x,t^{-\frac{1}{2}}x-w,z)
is dominated by some integrable function on
(w,z)\in\mathbb{R}^{N+k}
, for all
t>0
. Therefore, the rest of this proof will be dedicated to achieve this domination. We recall that
K_{1}
is positive in
\mathbb{R}^{2N+k}
. Moreover, notice that

|t^{-\frac{1}{2}}x|^{2}+|t^{-\frac{1}{2}}x-x|^{2}=2t^{-1}|x|^{2}+|w|^{2}-2t^{-\frac{1}{2}}\ x\cdot w.

Then

\displaystyle\left(|t^{-\frac{1}{2}}x|^{2}+|t^{-\frac{1}{2}}x-x|^{2}\right)\coth(|\xi|)-2(t^{-\frac{1}{2}}x)\cdot(t^{-\frac{1}{2}}w)\mathrm{csch}(|\xi|)

(2.7)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(2t^{-1}|x|^{2}-2t^{-\frac{1}{2}}\ x\cdot w\right)\left(\coth(|\xi|)-\mathrm{csch}(|\xi|)\right)+|w|^{2}\coth(|\xi|).

Since

\lim_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\left(2t^{-1}|x|^{2}-2t^{-\frac{1}{2}}\ x\cdot w\right)=\infty,

we already can see that

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\left(\frac{|\xi|}{\sinh(|\xi|)}\right)^{\frac{N}{2}}e^{i\xi\cdot y}e^{-\left(|t^{-\frac{1}{2}}x|^{2}+|t^{-\frac{1}{2}}x-x|^{2}\right)\coth(|\xi|)-2(t^{-\frac{1}{2}}x)\cdot(t^{-\frac{1}{2}}w)\mathrm{csch}(|\xi|)}=0,

whence

(2.8)

\lim_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}K_{1}(t^{-\frac{1}{2}}x,t^{-\frac{1}{2}}x-w,z)=0.

Nevertheless, we would like this convergence to be uniform also on
w
, since it is already uniform on
z
. For this, notice that the minimum value for the function
w\mapsto-2t^{-\frac{1}{2}}|x||w|\left(\coth(|\xi|)-\mathrm{csch}(|\xi|)\right)+|w|^{2}\coth(|\xi|)
is

-\frac{4t^{-1}\left(\coth(|\xi|)-\mathrm{csch}(|\xi|)\right)^{2}|x|^{2}}{4\coth(\xi)}.

From this and (
2.7
),

\displaystyle\left(|t^{-\frac{1}{2}}x|^{2}+|t^{-\frac{1}{2}}x-x|^{2}\right)\coth(|\xi|)-2(t^{-\frac{1}{2}}x)\cdot(t^{-\frac{1}{2}}w)\mathrm{csch}(|\xi|)

\displaystyle\geq 2t^{-1}|x|^{2}\left(\coth(|\xi|)-\mathrm{csch}(|\xi|)\right)-\frac{t^{-1}\left(\coth(|\xi|)-\mathrm{csch}(|\xi|)\right)^{2}|x|^{2}}{\coth(\xi)}

\displaystyle=t^{-1}|x|^{2}\left(\coth(|\xi|)-\mathrm{csch}(|\xi|)\right)\left[2-\frac{\left(\coth(|\xi|)-\mathrm{csch}(|\xi|)\right)}{\coth(|\xi|)}\right]

\displaystyle>t^{-1}|x|^{2}\left(\coth(|\xi|)-\mathrm{csch}(|\xi|)\right),

since

0\leq\frac{\left(\coth(|\xi|)-\mathrm{csch}(|\xi|)\right)}{\coth(|\xi|)}<1.

Therefore,

\displaystyle e^{-\frac{|\xi|}{2}\left((|t^{-\frac{1}{2}}x|^{2}+|t^{-\frac{1}{2}}x-w|^{2})\coth(|\xi|t)-2(t^{-\frac{1}{2}}x)\cdot(t^{-\frac{1}{2}}x-w)\mathrm{csch}(|\xi|t)\right)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle e^{-\frac{|\xi|}{2}\left(t^{-1}|x|^{2}\left(\coth(|\xi|)-\mathrm{csch}(|\xi|)\right)\right)}

\displaystyle\rightarrow

\displaystyle 0,\ \mbox{as}\ \ t\rightarrow 0^{+},

uniformly on
w\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Hence, (
2.8
) holds uniformly for
(w,z)\in\mathbb{R}^{N+k}
. This means that, for each

, there exists

such that the above computations and the nonnegative of the kernel ensure that

for all
(w,z)\in\mathbb{R}^{N+k}
and for all
0<t<t_{0}
. We finished the proof.

∎

Next, we consider the following Cauchy problem

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u-\Delta_{\mathcal{G}}u=0,

\displaystyle(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{N+k},\ t>0,

(2.10)

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274ptu(x,y,0)=u_{0}(x,y),

\displaystyle(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{N+k}.

In fact, one may check, by the proof of Proposition

Namely, (

)–(
2.10

Hence,

Remark 2.5

Although

\displaystyle u(t_{n})-u(t_{m})

\displaystyle=S_{\mathcal{G}}(t_{m})[S_{\mathcal{G}}(t_{n}-t_{m})-I]u_{0}+\int_{t_{m}}^{t_{n}}S_{\mathcal{G}}(t_{n}-s)|u(s)|^{\rho-1}u(s)ds

\displaystyle\ \ \ +[S_{\mathcal{G}}(t_{n}-t_{m})-I]\int_{0}^{t_{m}}S_{\mathcal{G}}(t_{m}-s)]|u(s)|^{\rho-1}u(s)ds.

Since
u_{0}\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k})\cap L^{\rho p}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k})
for
t>0
and we proved in Theorem
3.3
that
t\mapsto\int_{0}^{t_{m}}S_{\mathcal{G}}(t_{m}-s)]|u(s)|^{\rho-1}u(s)ds
also is in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k})\cap L^{\rho p}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k})
, we can use the strong continuity of the semigroup
S_{\mathcal{G}}(t)
given in (
2.14
) to conclude that the first and third terms of the right-hand side above go to zero as
m,n\rightarrow\infty
.

Plus, notice that

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{t_{m}}^{t_{n}}S_{\mathcal{G}}(t_{n}-s)|u(s)|^{\rho-1}u(s)ds\right\|_{L^{\rho p}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{t_{m}}^{t_{n}}C(t_{n}-s)^{-\alpha}\|u(s)\|^{\rho}_{L^{\rho p}}ds

\displaystyle\leq CA^{\rho}t_{n}^{-\alpha-\alpha\rho+1}\int_{t_{m}/t_{n}}^{1}(1-z)^{-\alpha}z^{-\alpha\rho}dz\rightarrow 0,

as
n,m\rightarrow\infty
. Then,

\|u(t_{n},\cdot)-u(t_{m},\cdot)\|_{L^{\rho p}}\rightarrow 0,\ \text{as}\ n,m\rightarrow\infty.

Analogously,

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{t_{m}}^{t_{n}}S_{\mathcal{G}}(t_{n}-s)|u(s)|^{\rho-1}u(s)ds\right\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq C(M+1)^{\rho}\int_{t_{m}}^{t_{n}}s^{-\alpha\rho}ds

\displaystyle\leq C(M+1)^{\rho}(t_{n}^{1-\alpha\rho}-t_{m}^{1-\alpha\rho})\rightarrow 0,

as
n,m\rightarrow\infty
, yielding

\|u(t_{n},\cdot)-u(t_{m},\cdot)\|_{L^{p}}\rightarrow 0,\ \text{as}\ n,m\rightarrow\infty.

Accordingly,

\|u(t_{n},\cdot)-u(t_{m},\cdot)\|_{X}\rightarrow 0,\ \text{as}\ n,m\rightarrow\infty.

It shows that
(u(t_{n}))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a Cauchy sequence in the Banach space
X
, which implies the existence of the limit in
X

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}u(t_{n})=u(T_{m}).

It contradicts the maximality of
T_{m}
.
∎

4.
Global solutions

In this section, we assume
p=\frac{N+2k}{2}(\rho-1)
to prove global existence for
(
3.1
)
. In this case,
r\in(p,\rho p)
and

(4.1)

\boxed{\alpha=\frac{N+2k}{2}\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{r}\right).}

We need the following lemma.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
1\leq p<r<\infty
. Then

(4.2)

\lim\limits_{T\rightarrow 0^{+}}\sup_{t\in(0,T)}t^{\alpha}\|S_{\mathcal{G}}(t)\varphi\|_{L^{r}}=0

uniformly to
\varphi
in bounded sets of
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k})
.

Proof.

Given
\varphi\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k})
, there exists a sequence
(\varphi_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k})
converging to
\varphi
. Using (
2.13
), we have

\displaystyle t^{\alpha}\|S_{\mathcal{G}}(t)\varphi\|_{L^{r}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\varphi-\varphi_{n}\|_{L^{r}}+t^{\alpha}M,\ \ \forall n\in\mathbb{N},t\in(0,T).

Taking the limit as
n\rightarrow\infty
and the supremum, we get

\lim\limits_{T\rightarrow 0^{+}}\sup_{t\in(0,T)}t^{\alpha}\|S_{\mathcal{G}}(t)\varphi\|_{L^{r}}=0.

∎

For the next result, in the spaces
E
and
X_{\alpha}
, we will consider
T=\infty
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
p=\frac{N+2k}{2}(\rho-1)
and
u_{0}\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k})
, with
\|u_{0}\|_{L^{p}}
sufficiently small. Then, there exists a global
L^{p}
-mild solution
u\in X_{\alpha}\cap E
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{B}_{\delta}
be the closed ball of
X_{\alpha}\cap E
of radius
\delta>0
and
\Lambda:\mathcal{B}_{\delta}\to\mathcal{B}_{\delta}
given by (
3.4
). Analogously to the proof of Theorem
3.3
, for any
u\in\mathcal{B}_{\delta}
, we can use Theorem
2.6
to get

\displaystyle\|\Lambda(u)(t)\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\|u_{0}\|_{L^{p}}+C\left(\sup_{t\in(0,T)}t^{\alpha}\|u(t)\|_{L^{r}}\right)^{\rho}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\frac{N+2k}{2}\left(\frac{\rho}{r}-\frac{1}{p}\right)}s^{-\alpha\rho}ds.

The assumptions
r\in(p,\rho p)
and
p=\frac{N+2k}{2}(\rho-1)
make
\frac{N+2k}{2}\left(\frac{\rho}{r}-\frac{1}{p}\right)>-1
and, because of (
4.1
), we have

1-\frac{N+2k}{2}\left(\frac{\rho}{r}-\frac{1}{p}\right)-\alpha\rho=0.

Then

\|\Lambda(u)(t)\|_{L^{p}}\leq\|u_{0}\|_{L^{p}}+\tilde{C}\delta^{\rho}.

Similarly,

\displaystyle\|\Lambda(u)(t)\|_{L^{r}}

\displaystyle\leq\|u_{0}\|_{L^{p}}t^{-\alpha}+C\delta^{\rho}t^{-\frac{N+2k}{2r}(\rho-1)-\alpha\rho+1}\int_{0}^{1}(1-\tau)^{-\frac{N+2k}{2r}(\rho-1)}\tau^{-\alpha\rho}d\tau.

Again, we notice that
r\in(p,\rho p)
and the choice for
p
make
\frac{N+2k}{2r}(\rho-1)<1
,
\alpha\rho<1
, and
\alpha=\frac{N+2k}{2r}(\rho-1)+\alpha\rho-1
. Thus,

t^{\alpha}\|\Lambda(u)(t)\|_{L^{r}}\leq\|u_{0}\|_{L^{p}}+\tilde{C_{1}}\delta^{\rho}

If
\|u_{0}\|_{L^{p}}<\delta/2
and
\delta>0
is so small that
4(\tilde{C}+\tilde{C_{1}})\delta^{\rho-1}<1
, then

(4.3)

\|\Lambda u\|_{X}<\frac{3\delta}{4}.

It is similar to the proof of Theorem
3.3
to prove that
t\mapsto\Lambda u(t)
is in
E\cap X_{\alpha}
, however Lemma
4.1
must be invoked. Therefore,
\Lambda
is well-defined.

Moreover, for
u,v\in\mathcal{B}_{\delta}
, we have

\displaystyle\|\Lambda(u)(t)-\Lambda(v)(t)\|_{L^{r}}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\frac{(N+2k)(\rho-1)}{2r}}\|u(s)-v(s)\|_{L^{r}}\left(\|u(s)\|_{L^{r}}^{\rho-1}+\|v(s)\|_{L^{r}}^{\rho-1}\right)ds

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{t>0}t^{\alpha}\|u(t)-v(t)\|_{L^{r}}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\frac{(N+2k)(\rho-1)}{2r}}s^{-\alpha(\rho-1)-\alpha}ds

\displaystyle\leq 2C\delta^{\rho-1}t^{-\frac{(N+2k)(\rho-1)}{2r}-\alpha\rho+1}\|u-v\|_{X_{\alpha}}B\left(1-\frac{(N+2k)(\rho-1)}{2r},1-\alpha\rho\right),

that is,

(4.4)

\|\Lambda(u)-\Lambda(v)\|_{X_{\alpha}}\leq 2C\delta^{\rho-1}\|u-v\|_{X}.

One can proceed similarly to show

(4.5)

\|\Lambda(u)(t)-\Lambda(v)(t)\|_{L^{p}}\leq C^{\prime}\delta^{\rho-1}\|u-v\|_{X},t>0.

Hence, from (
4.4
) and (
4.5
),
\Lambda
is a contraction. The Banach fixed point theorem yields a unique mild solution for (
1.6
)–(
1.7
) in
\mathcal{B}_{\delta}\subset E\cap X_{\alpha}
.

∎

Remark 4.3
.

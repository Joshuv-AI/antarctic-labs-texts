A q q -Exponential Operator Based on the Derivative of Order 1 and Summation of Bilateral Basic Hypergeometric Series
Ronald Orozco López

Abstract
We use a new q q -exponential operator based on the q ± 1 q^{\pm 1} -derivative 𝐃 q ± 1 \mathbf{D}_{q^{\pm 1}} of order 1 to derive summation formulas for bilateral basic hypergeometric series ψ 1 0 {}_{0}\psi_{1} , ψ 1 1 {}_{1}\psi_{1} , ψ 2 1 {}_{1}\psi_{2} , and ψ 2 2 {}_{2}\psi_{2} . In addition, we provide summation formulas for bilateral series whose terms are basic hypergeometric functions.

1
Introduction

We begin with some notation and terminology for basic hypergeometric series
[
1
]
. Let
|q|<1
. The
q
-shifted factorial is defined by

\displaystyle(a;q)_{n}

\displaystyle=\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}(1-q^{k}a),

\displaystyle(a;q)_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}(a;q)_{n}=\prod_{k=0}^{\infty}(1-aq^{k})

and the multiple
q
-shifted factorial is defined by

\displaystyle(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{m};q)_{n}

\displaystyle=(a_{1};q)_{n}(a_{2};q)_{n}\cdots(a_{m};q)_{n},

\displaystyle(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{m};q)_{n}

\displaystyle=(a_{1};q)_{\infty}(a_{2};q)_{\infty}\cdots(a_{m};q)_{\infty}.

For
r\leq s
and
|q|<1
, a bilateral basic hypergeometric series is defined as

{}_{r}\psi_{s}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}a_{1},\ldots,a_{r}\\
b_{1},\ldots,b_{s}\end{array};q,x\right]=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\big[(-1)^{n}q^{\binom{n}{2}}\big]^{s-r}\frac{(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r};q)_{n}}{(b_{1},\ldots,b_{s};q)_{n}}x^{n}

(1)

which is convergent for
|b_{1}\cdots b_{s}/a_{1}\cdots a_{r}|<|x|<1
.
The Ramanujan’s summation formula
[
2
]
is

{}_{1}\psi_{1}(a;b;q,z)=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{(a;q)_{n}}{(b;q)_{n}}z^{n}=\frac{(q,b/a,az,q/az;q)_{\infty}}{(b,q/a,z,b/az;q)_{\infty}},\ \ |b/a|<|z|<1.

(2)

The
{}_{r}\phi_{s}
basic hypergeometric series is define by

{}_{r}\phi_{s}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{r}\\
b_{1},\ldots,b_{s}\end{array};q,z\right)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{r};q)_{n}}{(q,b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{s};q)_{n}}[(-1)^{n}q^{\binom{n}{2}}]^{1+s-r}z^{n},\ |z|<1.

Ramanujan’s summation formula, Eq.(
2
), is considered as the ”bilateral extension” of the
q
-binomial theorem:

{}_{1}\phi_{0}(a;q,z)=\frac{(az;q)_{\infty}}{(z;q)_{\infty}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(a;q)_{n}}{(q;q)_{n}}z^{n}.

(3)

In this paper, for
b\geq 0
we introduce the
q
-operator

\mathrm{E}_{q}(y\mathbf{D}_{q^{\pm}}|q^{b})=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}q^{b\binom{n}{2}}\frac{(y\mathbf{D}_{q^{\pm}})^{n}}{(q;q)_{n}}

(4)

based on the
q^{\pm 1}
-derivative operator
\mathbf{D}_{q^{\pm 1}}
of order 1 defined by

\mathbf{D}_{q^{\pm 1}}f(x)=\frac{f(q^{\pm 1}x)}{x}.

(5)

The main advantage of the operator
\mathbf{D}_{q^{\pm 1}}
is that it preserves products. In this paper, we derive new summation formulas for bilateral basic hypergeometric series by the method of parameter augmentation based on
\mathbf{D}_{q^{\pm 1}}
. This summation formulas involving the Ramanujan’s summation formula, Eq.(
2
), and the Jacobi theta function

\vartheta(x;q)=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}q^{\binom{n+1}{2}}x^{n}.

(6)

This method based on the
q
-derivative

D_{q}f(x)=\frac{f(x)-f(qx)}{(1-q)x}

has been used in
[
4
]
to derive summation formulas for other basic bilateral hypergeometric series.

In addition, we will find several summation formulas for the series
{}_{0}\psi_{1}
,
{}_{1}\psi_{1}
,
{}_{1}\psi_{2}
, and
{}_{2}\psi_{2}
. We provide summation formulas for bilateral series of the form

\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}q^{\binom{n+1}{2}}(dx)^{n}{}_{r}\phi_{s}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}A_{1}(x),\ldots,A_{r}(x)\\
B_{1}(x),\ldots,B_{s}(x)\end{array};q,A(x,y)\right),

(7)

where the
A_{i}(x)=\frac{qa_{i}}{x}
and
B_{j}(x)=\frac{qb_{j}}{x}
and
A(x,y)
is a rational function in the variables
x,y
. Along with the Bailey-Daum summation formula, we obtain news summation formulas for
{}_{2}\psi_{2}
series. The following easily verified identities will be frequently used in this paper:

\displaystyle(q^{n}a;q)_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\frac{(a;q)_{\infty}}{(a;q)_{n}},

(8)

\displaystyle(q^{-n}a;q)_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\frac{(-a)^{n}}{q^{\binom{n+1}{2}}}(q/a;q)_{n}(a;q)_{\infty},

(9)

\displaystyle(a;q^{-1})_{n}

2
Calculus of order 1

2.1
The
\lambda
-derivative of order 1 of a function

Define the
\lambda
-shifted operator
\widehat{\mathrm{M}}_{\lambda}
acting on
f(x)
to be
\widehat{\mathrm{M}}_{\lambda}\{f(x)\}=f(\lambda x)
.

Definition 1
.

For
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
, we define the
\lambda
-derivative of order 1
\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}
of the function
f(x)
as

(\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}f)(x)=\frac{1}{x}\widehat{\mathrm{M}}_{\lambda}\{f(x)\}=\begin{cases}\frac{f(\lambda x)}{x},&\text{ if }x\neq 0;\\
\lim_{x\rightarrow 0}\frac{f(\lambda x)}{x},&\text{ if }x=0,\end{cases}

(11)

provided that the limit exists.

It is straightforward to prove the following properties of the
\lambda
-derivative
\mathbf{D}_{q}
.

Theorem 1
.

For all
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in\mathbb{C}
,

1.

\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}\{\alpha f+\beta g\}=\alpha\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}f+\beta\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}g
.

2.

\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}\{\gamma\}=\frac{\gamma}{x}
.

3.

\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}\{x^{n}\}=\lambda^{n}x^{n-1}
, for
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proposition 1

(
Product
\lambda
-rule).

\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}\{f(x)g(x)\}=f(\lambda x)\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}g(x)=\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}f(x)\cdot g(\lambda x)=x\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}f(x)\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}g(x).

(12)

From the above proposition, we have the following result.

Proposition 2
.

\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}\{f(x)g(x)\}=\frac{1}{2}(f(\lambda x)\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}g(x)+\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}f(x)\cdot g(\lambda x))

(13)

Proposition 3

(
Quotient
\lambda
-rule).

\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}\left\{\frac{f(x)}{g(x)}\right\}=\frac{\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}f(x)}{g(\lambda x)}=\frac{\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}f(x)}{x\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}g(x)},

(14)

g(x)\neq 0
,
g(\lambda x)\neq 0
.

Proposition 4
.

For all
n\in\mathbb{N}

\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}^{n}f(x)=\frac{f(\lambda^{n}x)}{q^{\binom{n}{2}}x^{n}},

(15)

where
\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}^{n}=\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}^{n-1}
.

Proof.

The proof is by induction on
n
. For
n=1
, use the definition
\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}
. Now suppose it is true for
n
. We will prove for
n+1
. We have

\displaystyle\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}^{n+1}f(x)=\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}^{n}f(x)=\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}\left\{\frac{f(\lambda^{n}x)}{\lambda^{\binom{n}{2}}x^{n}}\right\}=\frac{f(\lambda^{n}qx)}{\lambda^{\binom{n}{2}}\lambda^{n}x^{n}x}=\frac{f(\lambda^{n+1}x)}{\lambda^{\binom{n+1}{2}}x^{n+1}}

∎

Example 1
.

For all
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
and all
k\geq 1
,

\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}^{k}x^{\alpha}=\lambda^{k\alpha-\binom{k}{2}}x^{\alpha-k}.

(16)

Proposition 5

(
Leibniz
\lambda
-rule).

For all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}^{n}(fg)=\lambda^{\binom{n}{2}}x^{n}\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}^{n}(f)\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}^{n}(g).

(17)

Proof.

From Propositions
1
and
4

\displaystyle\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}^{n}(fg)

\displaystyle=\frac{f(\lambda^{n}x)g(\lambda^{n}x)}{\lambda^{\binom{n}{2}}x^{n}}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{\binom{n}{2}}x^{n}\frac{f(\lambda^{n}x)}{\lambda^{\binom{n}{2}}x^{n}}\frac{g(\lambda^{n}x)}{\lambda^{\binom{n}{2}}x^{n}}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{\binom{n}{2}}x^{n}\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}^{n}(f)\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}^{n}(g).

∎

Proposition 6
.

For all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}^{n}(f_{1}f_{2}\cdots f_{k})=\lambda^{(k-1)\binom{n}{2}}x^{(k-1)n}\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}^{n}(f_{1})\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}^{n}(f_{2})\cdots\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}^{n}(f_{k}).

(18)

2.3
Derivative of order 1 of the Jacobi theta function

For a nonzero complex number
x
, the Jacobi theta function is defined as

\displaystyle\vartheta(x;q)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}q^{\binom{n+1}{2}}x^{n}.

(41)

The Jacobi theta function has the following product representation

\displaystyle\vartheta(x;q)

\displaystyle=(q;q)_{\infty}(-qx;q)_{\infty}(-x^{-1};q)_{\infty}.

(42)

Proposition 7
.

For all
n\in\mathbb{N}

\displaystyle\mathbf{D}_{q}^{n}\vartheta(ax;q)

\displaystyle=\frac{\vartheta(q^{-n}ax;q)}{q^{\binom{n}{2}}x^{n}}=\frac{\vartheta(ax;q)}{(ax^{2})^{n}q^{n^{2}}}

(43)

and

\mathbf{D}_{q^{-1}}^{n}\vartheta(ax;q)=q^{\binom{n}{2}}\frac{\vartheta(q^{n}ax;q)}{x^{n}}=a^{n}\vartheta(ax;q).

(44)

Proof.

We apply Proposition
4
to Eq.(
42
)

\displaystyle\mathbf{D}_{q}^{n}\vartheta(ax;q)

\displaystyle=\frac{(q;q)_{\infty}}{q^{\binom{n}{2}}x^{n}}(-q^{1+n}ax;q)_{\infty}(-q^{-n}/ax;q)_{\infty}.

Now, we use simultaneously Eqs. (
8
) and (
9
)

\displaystyle\mathbf{D}_{q}^{n}\vartheta(ax;q)

\displaystyle=\frac{(q;q)_{\infty}}{q^{\binom{n}{2}}x^{n}}\frac{(-qax;q)_{\infty}}{(-qax;q)_{n}}\frac{(-qax;q)_{n}(-1/ax;q)_{\infty}}{(ax)^{n}q^{\binom{n+1}{2}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\vartheta(ax;q)}{(ax^{2})^{n}q^{n^{2}}}.

Similarly, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbf{D}_{q^{-1}}^{n}\vartheta(ax;q)

\displaystyle=\frac{q^{\binom{n}{2}}(q;q)_{\infty}}{x^{n}}(-q^{1-n}ax;q)_{\infty}(-q^{n}a^{-1}x^{-1};q)_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\frac{q^{\binom{n}{2}}(q;q)_{\infty}}{x^{n}}\frac{(-ax;q^{-1})_{n}}{(-a^{-1}x^{-1};q)_{n}}(-qax;q)_{\infty}(-a^{-1}x^{-1};q)_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\frac{q^{\binom{n}{2}}}{x^{n}}\vartheta(ax;q)\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{1+axq^{-k}}{1+a^{-1}x^{-1}q^{k}}

\displaystyle=\frac{q^{\binom{n}{2}}}{x^{n}}\vartheta(ax;q)\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{ax}{q^{k}}

\displaystyle=\frac{q^{\binom{n}{2}}}{x^{n}}\frac{a^{n}x^{n}}{q^{\binom{n}{2}}}\vartheta(ax;q)=a^{n}\vartheta(ax;q).

∎

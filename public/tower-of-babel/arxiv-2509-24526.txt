CMT: Mid-Training for Efficient Learning of Consistency, Mean Flow, and Flow Map Models
Zheyuan Hu, Chieh-Hsin Lai, Yuki Mitsufuji, Stefano Ermon

Abstract
Flow map models such as Consistency Models (CM) and Mean Flow (MF) enable few-step generation by learning the long jump of the ODE solution of diffusion models, yet training remains unstable, sensitive to hyperparameters, and costly. Initializing from a pre-trained diffusion model helps, but still requires converting infinitesimal steps into a long-jump map, leaving instability unresolved. We introduce mid-training , the first concept and practical method that inserts a lightweight intermediate stage between the (diffusion) pre-training and the final flow map training (i.e., post-training) for vision generation. Concretely, Consistency Mid-Training (CMT) is a compact and principled stage that trains a model to map points along a solver trajectory from a pre-trained model, starting from a prior sample, directly to the solver-generated clean sample. It yields a trajectory-consistent and stable initialization. This initializer outperforms random and diffusion-based baselines and enables fast, robust convergence without heuristics. Initializing post-training with CMT weights further simplifies flow map learning. Empirically, CMT achieves state of the art two step FIDs: 1.97 on CIFAR-10, 1.32 on ImageNet 64 × \times 64, and 1.84 on ImageNet 512 × \times 512, while using up to 98% less training data and GPU time, compared to CMs. On ImageNet 256 × \times 256, CMT reaches 1-step FID 3.34 while cutting total training time by about 50% compared to MF from scratch (FID 3.43). This establishes CMT as a principled, efficient, and general framework for training flow map models. Code and models are available at the https://github.com/sony/cmt .

Diffusion models
(
Ho et al. 2020
;
Song & Ermon 2019
)
have become a cornerstone of modern generative modeling, yet their practical application is often hindered by a significant computational burden during inference. This latency arises because sampling is equivalent to solving a probability flow ordinary differential equation (PF-ODE)
(
Song et al. 2021
)
, a process that requires many iterative steps. To circumvent this limitation, a promising direction focuses on directly learning the solution (integration) map of the PF-ODE, which is also referred to as a
flow map model
.

Because the PF–ODE flow map lacks a closed form, recent methods learn surrogate maps by enforcing properties that any exact flow must satisfy: e.g., Consistency Models (CM)
(
Song et al. 2023
)
impose cross–noise-level self-consistency and Mean Flow (MF)
(
Geng et al. 2025a
)
matches time averages along trajectories. However, these objectives
(
Song et al. 2023
;
Song & Dhariwal 2024
;
Kim et al. 2024
;
Geng et al. 2025a
;
Lu & Song 2025
;
Sabour et al. 2025
)
supervise against
stop-gradient, network-dependent pseudo-targets
that drift with training dynamics. The lack of a true, time-invariant regression target injects bias, yields unstable optimization signals, and slows convergence. While recent works observed that initializing from pre-trained diffusion weights can mitigate instability
(
Geng et al. 2025b
;
Lu & Song 2025
)
, this does not address the root cause. Fundamentally, a flow map must learn large integrated jumps of the trajectory, whereas diffusion models capture only the
infinitesimal
movements. This mismatch renders diffusion-based initialization fragile: flow map training then depends on brittle heuristics (e.g., time weightings and sampling schedules) yet still suffers from flow map learning’s instability and converges slowly
(
Geng et al. 2025b
)
. In particular, recent studies
(
Zhu 2025
)
have observed that post-training MF, even when initialized from a well-trained large-scale diffusion model
(
Ma et al. 2024
)
, is prone to divergence and requires careful configuration tuning.

We address the instability and high cost of training few-step flow maps by introducing
mid-training
for vision generation, conceptually inspired by mid-training in large language models
(
Groeneveld et al. 2024
)
. In our setting, mid-training is a brief intermediate stage that bridges pre-training (e.g., diffusion model) and flow map post-training. We instantiate this idea as
Consistency Mid-Training
(CMT), a lightweight procedure that leverages trajectories generated by a pre-trained model to produce a trajectory-aware initialization. Concretely, CMT trains a model to map any point along a trajectory determined by a pre-trained model, from a prior sample directly to the clean endpoint of exactly that same trajectory in a single step.
Mid-training with CMT requires no architectural changes, converges quickly, adds only modest cost, and avoids fragile heuristics such as stop gradients, time sampling, and weighting schedules. This trajectory-aligned initializer provides a better starting point for flow map post-training than either random or diffusion-based weight transfer, while also simplifying engineering practices. Most importantly, it significantly reduces the total training cost (in both time and required training data) and improves training stability.

Theoretically, we show that CMT reduce the gradient discrepancy between the oracle and practical flow map losses, providing a stronger and trajectory-aligned initializer for the flow map post-training. Empirically, we validate our approach on a diverse suite of benchmarks, including pixel space datasets (CIFAR-10, FFHQ 64
\times
64, AFHQv2 64
\times
64, and ImageNet 64
\times
64) and latent space models for high-resolution synthesis (ImageNet 256
\times
256 and 512
\times

\times
64, 1.84 on ImageNet 512
\times
512, 2.34 on AFHQv2 64
\times
64, and 2.75 on FFHQ 64
\times
64. Crucially, these results are achieved with up to a 98% reduction in total training budget and GPU time compared to baselines without our mid-training stage, where training budget is measured by the number of training images processed, equivalently, the number of backpropagated optimization steps. 2
Preliminary

2.1
Diffusion Models and Flow Matching.

Diffusion models define a forward process that perturbs clean data
{\mathbf{x}}_{0}\sim p_{\mathrm{data}}
into
{\mathbf{x}}_{t}=\alpha_{t}{\mathbf{x}}_{0}+\sigma_{t}{\bm{\epsilon}}
, where
{\bm{\epsilon}}\sim\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},\mathbf{I})
and
t\in[0,T]
. Equivalently,
{\mathbf{x}}_{t}\sim p_{t}({\mathbf{x}}_{t}|{\mathbf{x}}_{0})=\mathcal{N}(\cdot;\alpha_{t}{\mathbf{x}}_{0},\sigma_{t}^{2}{\mathbf{I}})
, which induces marginals

p_{t}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})=\int p_{t}({\mathbf{x}}_{t}|{\mathbf{x}}_{0})p_{\mathrm{data}}({\mathbf{x}}_{0})\mathrm{d}{\mathbf{x}}_{0}
.
Two closely related training approaches are standard.

EDM

(
Karras et al. 2022
)
trains a denoiser
{\mathbf{D}}_{{\bm{\theta}}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t)
with a preconditioned parametrization by minimizing
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DM}}({\bm{\theta}})=\mathbb{E}_{t}\mathbb{E}_{{\mathbf{x}}_{0},{\bm{\epsilon}}}[\,w(t)\|{\mathbf{D}}_{{\bm{\theta}}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t)-{\mathbf{x}}_{0}\|_{2}^{2}]
. At optimum,
{\mathbf{D}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t)=\mathbb{E}[{\mathbf{x}}_{0}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t}]
.
EDM uses
\alpha_{t}=1
,
\sigma_{t}=t
for
t\in[0,T]
, so for large
T
, the prior
p_{\mathrm{prior}}
approaches
\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},T^{2}{\mathbf{I}})
.

Flow Matching

(
Lipman et al. 2023
)
fits a vector field
{\mathbf{v}}_{{\bm{\theta}}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t)
to the conditional velocity of the perturbation:

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}({\bm{\theta}})=\mathbb{E}_{t}\,\mathbb{E}_{{\mathbf{x}}_{0},{\bm{\epsilon}}}\!\left[w(t)\,\big\|{\mathbf{v}}_{{\bm{\theta}}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t)-\big(\alpha_{t}^{\prime}{\mathbf{x}}_{0}+\sigma_{t}^{\prime}{\bm{\epsilon}}\big)\big\|_{2}^{2}\right]
.
At optimum,
{\mathbf{v}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t)=\mathbb{E}[\alpha_{t}^{\prime}{\mathbf{x}}_{0}+\sigma_{t}^{\prime}{\bm{\epsilon}}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t}]
. A common choice
\alpha_{t}=1-t
,
\sigma_{t}=t
for
t\in[0,1]
yields a unit-Gaussian prior.

Relation of the Two Frameworks.
The parametrizations are equivalent; the marginal optimal velocity and denoiser satisfy

{\mathbf{v}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t)=\Big(\alpha_{t}^{\prime}-\alpha_{t}\tfrac{\sigma_{t}^{\prime}}{\sigma_{t}}\Big){\mathbf{D}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t)+\tfrac{\sigma_{t}^{\prime}}{\sigma_{t}}\,{\mathbf{x}}_{t}
,
so one can translate between
{\mathbf{v}}_{{\bm{\theta}}}
and
{\mathbf{D}}_{{\bm{\theta}}}
given the scheduler. Sampling integrates the PF-ODE
(
Song et al. 2021
)
,
\frac{\mathrm{d}{\mathbf{x}}_{t}}{\mathrm{d}t}={\mathbf{v}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t)
,
starting from
{\mathbf{x}}_{T}\sim p_{\mathrm{prior}}
(Gaussian in both views) down to
t=0
. Either
{\mathbf{v}}_{{\bm{\theta}}}\approx{\mathbf{v}}
or
{\mathbf{D}}_{{\bm{\theta}}}\approx{\mathbf{D}}
can be used to realize the drift.

2.2
Few-Step Flow-Map Generative Modeling.

In this section, we propose a unified view that connects existing formulations of flow map models.
Numerical integration of the PF-ODE can be slow, as it requires simulating a system across many small time steps. Few-step models offer a more efficient alternative by directly learning the solution to the PF-ODE’s integral: the
flow map
,
{\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to s}(\cdot)
. This map takes an initial state
{\mathbf{x}}_{t}
at time
t
and jumps directly to its final destination at time
s
:

\displaystyle{\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to s}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})\coloneqq{\mathbf{x}}_{t}+\int_{t}^{s}{\mathbf{v}}\big({\mathbf{x}}_{u},u\big)\mathrm{d}u,\quad{\mathbf{v}}({\mathbf{x}}_{u},u)=\left(\alpha^{\prime}_{u}-\alpha_{u}\tfrac{\sigma^{\prime}_{u}}{\sigma_{u}}\right){\mathbf{D}}({\mathbf{x}}_{u},u)+\tfrac{\sigma^{\prime}_{u}}{\sigma_{u}}{\mathbf{x}}_{u}.

(1)

Special Flow Map: Consistency Models (CM).
The CM family adapts EDM’s framework and learn a few-step denoiser
{\mathbf{f}}_{\bm{\theta}}(\cdot,t)
that approximates the flow map to the origin,
{\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to 0}(\cdot)
, for any
t\in(0,T]
.
Training relies on the
consistency property
: any two points along the same PF-ODE trajectory should map to the same origin. We propose a
principled re-interpretation
of the CM family objective
(
Song et al. 2023
;
Song & Dhariwal 2024
;
Geng et al. 2025b
;
Lu & Song 2025
)
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{oracle}\text{-}\mathrm{CM}}({\bm{\theta}}):=\mathbb{E}_{t}\mathbb{E}_{{\mathbf{x}}_{t}\sim p_{t}}\Big[w(t)d({\mathbf{f}}_{\bm{\theta}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t),{\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to 0}({\mathbf{x}}_{t}))\Big],

(2)

with
d
a point-wise distance (e.g., squared
\ell_{2}
or perceptual
(
Zhang et al. 2018
)
). At optimum,
{\mathbf{f}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t)={\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to 0}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})
(
Proposition

F.1
). Since
{\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to 0}
is unavailable, CM uses a
stop-gradient
surrogate from the previous step,

{\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to 0}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})\;\approx\;{\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}^{-}}\!\left({\mathbf{x}}_{t-\Delta t},\,t-\Delta t\right),\quad\Delta t>0,

where
{\mathbf{x}}_{t-\Delta t}
comes from (i)
Consistency Distillation (CD)
: a one-step solver with a pre-trained diffusion teacher, which calls the teacher during training; or (ii)
Consistency Training (CT)
: the analytic estimate
{\mathbf{x}}_{t-\Delta t}=\alpha_{t-\Delta t}{\mathbf{x}}_{0}+\sigma_{t-\Delta t}{\bm{\epsilon}}
using the same
({\mathbf{x}}_{0},{\bm{\epsilon}})
as in
{\mathbf{x}}_{t}=\alpha_{t}{\mathbf{x}}_{0}+\sigma_{t}{\bm{\epsilon}}
, requiring no teacher calls.
Both approaches improve performance by initializing from pre-trained diffusion weights
(
Lu & Song 2025
;
Geng et al. 2025b
)
. In the CT setting, the CM surrogate loss is

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CM}}({\bm{\theta}}):=\mathbb{E}_{t,{\mathbf{x}}_{t}}\left[w(t)d\left({\mathbf{f}}_{\bm{\theta}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t),{\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}^{-}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t-\Delta t},t-\Delta t)\right)\right].

(3)

Recent variants (e.g., ECT
(
Geng et al. 2025b
)
) refine initialization, time steps,
w(t)
, and
d(\cdot,\cdot)
.

General Flow Map.

Consistency Trajectory Model (CTM)
(
Kim et al. 2024
)
was the first to learn the
general flow map
{\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to s}
for arbitrary
t>s
via

{\mathbf{G}}_{{\bm{\theta}}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t,s)
, minimizing

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{oracle}\text{-}\mathrm{CTM}}({\bm{\theta}}):=\mathbb{E}_{t>s}\mathbb{E}_{{\mathbf{x}}_{t}\sim p_{t}}\Big[w(t)d({\mathbf{G}}_{\bm{\theta}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t,s),{\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to s}({\mathbf{x}}_{t}))\Big].

(4)

As
{\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to s}
is inaccessible, CTM uses a stop-gradient target evaluated at
{\mathbf{G}}_{\bm{\theta}}
itself, similar to CM.

More recently, MF
(
Geng et al. 2025a
)
builds on the flow matching formulation by modeling the average drift
{\mathbf{h}}_{{\bm{\theta}}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t,s)\approx{\mathbf{h}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t,s):=\frac{1}{t-s}\int_{s}^{t}{\mathbf{v}}({\mathbf{x}}_{u},u)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u

over an interval
[s,t]
, also following the principled
Equation

4
. MF constructs a surrogate target by differentiating
(t-s){\mathbf{h}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t,s)=\int_{s}^{t}{\mathbf{v}}({\mathbf{x}}_{u},u)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u
w.r.t.
t
, yielding the MF training loss:

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{MF}}({\bm{\theta}}):=\mathbb{E}_{t>s}\mathbb{E}_{{\mathbf{x}}_{t}\sim p_{t}}\Big[w(t)\,\|{\mathbf{h}}_{{\bm{\theta}}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t,s)-{\mathbf{h}}_{{\bm{\theta}}^{-}}^{\mathrm{tgt}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t,s)\|_{2}^{2}\Big],

where the regression target is applied with stop-gradient as

{\mathbf{h}}_{{\bm{\theta}}^{-}}^{\mathrm{tgt}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t,s):={\mathbf{v}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t)-(t-s)\bigl({\mathbf{v}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t)\partial_{\mathbf{x}}{\mathbf{h}}_{{\bm{\theta}}^{-}}+\partial_{t}{\mathbf{h}}_{{\bm{\theta}}^{-}}\bigr)
. In practice, the oracle
{\mathbf{v}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t)
is approximated either by
(i) a pre-trained diffusion model (
distillation
), or
(ii) the conditional velocity
\alpha_{t}^{\prime}{\mathbf{x}}_{0}+\sigma_{t}^{\prime}{\bm{\epsilon}}
(
training from scratch
). CTM and MF share the same framework with equivalent losses up to a constant
(see
Appendix

A
and
Equation

10
), differing only in parameterization and backbone
(CTM with EDM, MF with flow matching). We therefore use MF as the representative
flow map model
{\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to s}
.

3
Consistency Mid-Training for Efficient and General Flow Map Learning

3.1
Proposed Pipeline for Flow Map Learning

Despite recent advances, large-scale flow map training remains costly, unstable, and configuration-sensitive.
The key challenge is the lack of an oracle regression target
{\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to s}
: current methods rely on stop-gradients of imperfect models, yielding poor supervision and large deviations from the true flow.
To address this, we introduce a compact mid-training stage between pre-training and flow map post-training. Specifically, our pipeline incorporates the proposed CMT as a mid-training step, providing a general and cost-efficient framework for flow map learning:

Stage 1: Pre-Training.
Pre-training aims to learn a deterministic ODE sampler that transports samples from
p_{\mathrm{prior}}
to
p_{\mathrm{data}}
, consistent with the marginals of the forward noising process
{\mathbf{x}}_{t}\sim\mathcal{N}(\alpha_{t}{\mathbf{x}}_{0},\sigma_{t}^{2}{\mathbf{I}})
.
A practical choice is an off-the-shelf pre-trained diffusion model with its PF-ODE solver, as many such models are available
(
Karras et al. 2022
;
Karras et al. 2024b
;
Ma et al. 2024
;
Peebles & Xie 2023
)
.
Alternatively, one may use a lightweight few-step flow map model that supports deterministic sampling (e.g., MF).
We refer to these variants collectively as the
teacher sampler
.

Stage 2: Mid-Training (CMT).
Efficiently learn a lightweight, trajectory-aligned proxy of the target flow map with minimal computation and stable convergence, without ad-hoc heuristics.
CMT’s loss is designed to match the objectives of post-training while using fixed, explicit regression targets supplied by the teacher.
Operationally, CMT learns to jump directly between points on the teacher-generated trajectory of a pre-trained model.
Because the targets are fixed and high quality, CMT trains stably and yields a trajectory-aligned initializer.

Stage 3: Post-Training.
Learn the final few-step flow-map model.
Compared to random initialization or initialization from pre-trained diffusion models proposed by literature
(
Geng et al. 2025b
;
Lu & Song 2025
)
, the CMT initializer is trajectory-aligned, making post-training more stable, simpler, and faster (as supported by our theoretical analysis in
Theorem

5.1
and
Appendix

F
). CMT offers a general recipe for significantly cost-efficient flow map learning.

In what follows, we detail the mid-training stage with CMT, first instantiating it for CM (
{\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to 0}
) and then extending it to the general flow map via MF (
{\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to s}
).

3.2
CMT for Learning Consistency Function

Here, we focus on CM as the flow map post-training stage. To obtain a trajectory aligned initializer for this flow map and to motivate the design of the CMT’s mid-training loss, we revisit the CM oracle objective
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{oracle}\text{-}\mathrm{CM}}
.

We first propose a reinterpretation of
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{oracle}\text{-}\mathrm{CM}}
from a reverse time generative perspective, under which the objective becomes transparent. Every point
{\mathbf{x}}_{t}\sim p_{t}
along a PF-ODE trajectory is uniquely determined by its terminal state
{\mathbf{x}}_{T}
. Hence, rather than sampling a fresh data point
{\mathbf{x}}_{0}
and a noise vector
{\bm{\epsilon}}
for each
t
, one may sample a single terminal state
{\mathbf{x}}_{T}\sim p_{\mathrm{prior}}
and trace its entire trajectory backward. Training then reduces to mapping every point on this reverse path to its single consistent origin in the data distribution
p_{\mathrm{data}}
. This yields the following equivalent formulation of the oracle loss; the proof is provided in
Section

F.1
.

Theorem 3.1
.

If
p_{\mathrm{prior}}
matches the diffused marginal
p_{T}

1

This holds for sufficiently large
T
as in EDM, or with appropriate noise schedules as in flow matching.
, the oracle loss can be expressed as

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{oracle}\text{-}\mathrm{CM}}({\bm{\theta}})=\mathbb{E}_{t}\,\mathbb{E}_{{\mathbf{x}}_{T}\sim p_{\mathrm{prior}}}\Big[w(t)d({\mathbf{f}}_{\bm{\theta}}\big({\bm{\Psi}}_{T\to t}({\mathbf{x}}_{T}),t\big),{\bm{\Psi}}_{T\to 0}({\mathbf{x}}_{T}))\Big].

(5)

Building on the reverse-time formulation in
Equation

5
, we now
introduce the training objective of the proposed CMT. We fix a decreasing
time discretization
T=t_{M}>t_{M-1}>\cdots>t_{1}>t_{0}=0
. Given a sample

{\mathbf{x}}_{T}\sim p_{\mathrm{prior}}
, we obtain a discrete reference trajectory

\{\hat{{\mathbf{x}}}_{t_{i}}\}_{i=0}^{M}
by running a numerical ODE solver with the
pre–trained diffusion model
{\mathbf{D}}_{\bm{\phi}}
(in EDM formulation) as the teacher
sampler, anchored at
\hat{{\mathbf{x}}}_{t_{M}}={\mathbf{x}}_{T}
. The goal of CMT is for
{\mathbf{f}}_{\bm{\theta}}
to match any intermediate state

\hat{{\mathbf{x}}}_{t_{i}}
back to its clean origin
\hat{{\mathbf{x}}}_{t_{0}}
. Training proceeds
by minimizing the following loss:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CMT}\text{-}\mathrm{CM}}(\bm{\theta}):=\mathbb{E}_{i}\mathbb{E}_{{\mathbf{x}}_{T}\sim p_{\mathrm{prior}}}\Bigl[d({\mathbf{f}}_{\bm{\theta}}\bigl(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{i}},t_{i}\bigr),\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{0}})\Bigr].

(6)

This objective is a discrete approximation of the oracle loss
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{oracle}\text{-}\mathrm{CM}}
, since the solver-generated points approximate the true flow map, i.e.,
\hat{{\mathbf{x}}}_{t_{i}}\approx{\bm{\Psi}}_{T\to t_{i}}({\mathbf{x}}_{T})
.

Since the starting states
{\mathbf{x}}_{T}\sim p_{\mathrm{prior}}
are randomly sampled, the set of possible trajectories can be arbitrarily many. Yet, once a particular
{\mathbf{x}}_{T}
is fixed, the corresponding trajectory is uniquely determined. In principle, CMT can thus be trained with arbitrarily many distinct trajectories, avoiding the overfitting issues that arise in standard supervised tasks.

3.3
CMT for Learning General Flow Map

We now focus on the MF parameterization for the general flow map learning. MF aims to learn the average drift, defined as

{\mathbf{h}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t,s):=\frac{1}{t-s}\int_{s}^{t}{\mathbf{v}}({\mathbf{x}}_{u},u)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u,

which aggregates the ODE velocity over the interval
[s,t]
.

We observe that this quantity can also be expressed through the flow map.
Let
{\mathbf{x}}_{T}
denote the initial state at time
T
on the same PF-ODE trajectory as
{\mathbf{x}}_{t}
.
Then

\displaystyle{\mathbf{h}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t,s)=\frac{1}{t-s}\Bigl(\int_{s}^{T}{\mathbf{v}}({\mathbf{x}}_{u},u)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u-\int_{t}^{T}{\mathbf{v}}({\mathbf{x}}_{u},u)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u\Bigr)=\frac{1}{t-s}\bigl({\bm{\Psi}}_{T\to t}({\mathbf{x}}_{T})-{\bm{\Psi}}_{T\to s}({\mathbf{x}}_{T})\bigr).

Motivated by this decomposition, CMT allows to construct a teacher–reference
trajectory
\{\hat{{\mathbf{x}}}_{t_{i}}\}
from a prior sample

{\mathbf{x}}_{T}\sim p_{\mathrm{prior}}
using two possible teacher samplers. The first
employs a numerical ODE solver applied to the PF-ODE of a pre–trained flow matching model

{\mathbf{v}}_{\bm{\phi}}
. Alternatively, since MF supports deterministic sampling, we may
use a smaller and lightweight MF model to perform multi–step deterministic
generation. Although not optimal, this model is much easier to train and still yields
a valid teacher trajectory. In both cases, the resulting trajectory provides a
feasible approximation of the oracle states,

{\bm{\Psi}}_{T\to t_{i}}({\mathbf{x}}_{T})\approx\hat{{\mathbf{x}}}_{t_{i}}
.

The CMT loss for MF then encourages the average drift parametrization

{\mathbf{h}}_{{\bm{\theta}}}
to align with the finite differences between successive
reference states:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CMT}\text{-}\mathrm{MF}}({\bm{\theta}})=\mathbb{E}_{i>j}\;\mathbb{E}_{{\mathbf{x}}_{T}\sim p_{\mathrm{prior}}}\Bigl[\bigl\|{\mathbf{h}}_{{\bm{\theta}}}(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{i}},t_{i},t_{j})-\tfrac{\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{i}}-\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{j}}}{t_{i}-t_{j}}\bigr\|_{2}^{2}\Bigr].

(7)

Crucially, in both
Equation

6
and
Equation

7
, our formulation reduces training to a standard regression problem with a fixed target, either
\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{0}
or
\tfrac{\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{i}}-\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{j}}}{t_{i}-t_{j}}
.
The CMT loss for MF generalizes the CM case. In fact, if we fix
t_{j}=0
in

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CMT}\text{-}\mathrm{MF}}
, the loss reduces to learning a
mapping from every point on the trajectory directly to the clean data,
thereby recovering the CM formulation.

5
Theoretical Analysis

In mid–training, CMT learns a reliable proxy of the flow map, yielding a well–aligned initialization for post–training. We assess this effect by analyzing the CM flow map
{\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to 0}
in the post–training stage; the same reasoning applies to the general flow map
{\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to s}
, such as MF.

Our goal is to theoretically quantify how closely the surrogate CM objective aligns with the oracle descent directions under different initialization schemes: CMT from mid-training (
{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{CMT}}
), a pre-trained diffusion model (
{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{DM}}
), and random initialization (
{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{rand}}
). More precisely, we consider the squared
\ell_{2}
distance
d({\mathbf{x}},{\mathbf{y}})=\|{\mathbf{x}}-{\mathbf{y}}\|_{2}^{2}
and a uniform weight
w(t)\equiv 1
. Let
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{oracle}\text{-}\mathrm{CM}}({\bm{\theta}})
denote the oracle objective (
Equation

2
) and
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CM}}({\bm{\theta}})
the surrogate CM objective (
Equation

3
). We define the
gradient bias
as

\mathcal{B}({\bm{\theta}}):=\big\|\nabla_{{\bm{\theta}}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{oracle}\text{-}\mathrm{CM}}({\bm{\theta}})-\nabla_{{\bm{\theta}}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CM}}({\bm{\theta}})\big\|_{2}^{2}.

We evaluate each scheme by
\mathcal{B}({\bm{\theta}})
at its initial parameters, i.e., at
{\bm{\theta}}\in\{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{CMT}},{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{DM}},{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{rand}}\}
. A smaller
\mathcal{B}
indicates that updates on
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CM}}
closely track those on
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{oracle}\text{-}\mathrm{CM}}
. To characterize robustness, we bound
\mathcal{B}
in the worst case for each initialization scheme, which leads to the theorem below.

Theorem 5.1

(Informal Bias Comparisons)
.

Fix an error tolerance
\varepsilon>0
and a small time step
\Delta t
.
The deviation between the CM flow map gradient and the oracle gradient satisfies:

(i)

CMT :
If
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CMT}\text{-}\mathrm{CM}}({\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{CMT}})<\varepsilon
, for some
{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{CMT}}
, then

\mathcal{B}({\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{CMT}})=\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon+\Delta t^{2})
.

(ii)

Diffusion Model:
If
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DM}}({\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{DM}})<\varepsilon
, for some
{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{DM}}
, then

\mathcal{B}({\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{DM}})=\mathcal{O}\left(\varepsilon+\Delta t^{2}+\mathbb{E}_{t}\big[\tfrac{\sigma_{t}^{2}}{\alpha_{t}^{2}}\big]\right)+\mathbb{E}_{t,{\mathbf{x}}_{t}}\left[\|{\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to 0}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})-\mathbb{E}[{\mathbf{x}}_{0}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t}]\|_{2}^{2}\right].

(iii)

Random Initialization:
For randomly initialized weights
{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{rand}}
,

\mathcal{B}({\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{rand}})=\mathcal{O}(1)
.

From
Theorem

5.1
, we see that initializing the flow map model with
CMT’s mid-training weights provides a strong starting point, close to the oracle.
This is because the mid-training stage of CMT already offers a good proxy for the oracle flow map.

In contrast, initialization with a pre-trained diffusion model, as suggested by
Geng et al. 2025b
,
incurs unavoidable sources of bias.
First, it introduces a large constant term
\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\tfrac{\sigma_{t}^{2}}{\alpha_{t}^{2}}\right]
.
In EDM’s formulation (
\alpha_{t}=1
,
\sigma_{t}=t
), this becomes
\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\tfrac{\sigma_{t}^{2}}{\alpha_{t}^{2}}\right]=\mathcal{O}(T^{2})
if
t\sim\mathrm{Unif}[0,T]
.
Second, it suffers from the inherent mismatch between the PF-ODE solution and the posterior mean,
captured by

\mathbb{E}_{t,{\mathbf{x}}_{t}}\!\left[\|{\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to 0}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})-\mathbb{E}[{\mathbf{x}}_{0}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t}]\|_{2}^{2}\right]
,
which is generally nonzero.
Together, these confirm our earlier claim that flow maps must learn large integrated jumps,
whereas diffusion models encode only infinitesimal shifts, creating a fundamental mismatch that makes diffusion-based initialization fragile.

Random initialization is even less favorable: training can start arbitrarily far from the oracle,
and the resulting lack of control typically leads to slow convergence.
We present the complete and rigorous version of the theorem in
Theorem

F.1
,
which also covers the case of initializing with a trained consistency distillation model.
This setting introduces an additional, uncontrollable discrepancy compared to CMT, and in practice, its training often requires extra ad-hoc techniques, further limiting its robustness relative to CMT. We refer to
Theorem

F.2
for a complementary discussion of gradient variance, and note that the bias terms are dominant (see
Corollary

F.3
). Combining these ingredients,
Theorem

F.4
shows that CM-driven SGD with CMT initialization achieves the smallest excess risk and the lowest final error among all other initialization schemes.

6
Conclusion

We introduced CMT, an efficient mid-training stage that learns a trajectory-consistent initialization for flow map models from teacher sampler trajectories. This simple, architecture-agnostic step stabilizes optimization, removes reliance on stop-gradient targets and ad hoc time weighting, and accelerates convergence. With CMT as initialization, flow map models such as Consistency Models and Mean Flow attain SOTA two-step FIDs across pixel and latent benchmarks while reducing training data budget and GPU time by up to 98%. The approach makes training of flow map models more efficient and practical, and in principle, it applies to a broad class of ODE-based generative models.

Appendix A
Related Work

Diffusion Model and ODE Samplers.

Diffusion models and flow matching learn a time-conditioned vector field that transports a simple prior to the data distribution. In diffusion models, the associated PF-ODE shares the same marginals as the reverse SDE, which permits
deterministic
generation via ODE solving
(
Song et al. 2021
;
Lipman et al. 2023
;
Liu et al. 2023
)
. Early accelerations such as DDIM instantiate a first-order (Euler-like) discretization of the PF-ODE, reducing sampling steps without retraining
(
Song et al. 2020
)
. The EDM design space refines parameterization, noise schedules, and training targets to improve stability and enable aggressive few-step sampling
(
Karras et al. 2022
)
. High-order samplers in the DPM-Solver family leverage a log-SNR reparameterization of time and exponential-integrator schemes (orders 2/3) to integrate the PF-ODE accurately with very few NFEs
(
Lu et al. 2022
;
Lu et al. 2025
)
.

Few-Step Flow Map Models.

Diffusion sampling is slow due to stepwise SDE/ODE integration. Early distillation methods
(
Salimans & Ho 2022
;
Luhman & Luhman 2021
;
Zheng et al. 2023
)
accelerate sampling by training a
student
to match a multi-step diffusion
teacher
with long jumps. CM
(
Song et al. 2023
;
Song & Dhariwal 2024
)
instead learn a direct
flow map
via pairwise time consistency, later improved in training and extended to continuous time
(
Geng et al. 2025b
;
Lu & Song 2025
)
. Consistency trajectory models (CTM)
(
Kim et al. 2024
;
Lai et al. 2023
)
extend CM to learn flow maps between arbitrary points along the trajectory, but their training relies on adversarial objectives. Follow-up works
(
Frans et al. 2025
;
Boffi et al. 2024
;
Sabour et al. 2025
)
propose alternative parameterizations and losses to avoid adversarial components while improving few-step fidelity. Mean Flow
(
Geng et al. 2025a
)
attempts to train from scratch without adversarial loss, at the cost of heavy computation from Jacobian–vector product evaluations.

Relationship of Flow Map Models.

The principled objective for learning the general flow map
{\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to s}
is
to train a neural network
{\mathbf{G}}_{{\bm{\theta}}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t,s)
by minimizing

Equation

4
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{oracle}\text{-}\mathrm{CTM}}({\bm{\theta}}):=\mathbb{E}_{t>s}\,\mathbb{E}_{{\mathbf{x}}_{t}\sim p_{t}}\Big[w(t)\,d\big({\mathbf{G}}_{\bm{\theta}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t,s),\,{\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to s}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})\big)\Big].

In CTM, the network is parameterized in a form inspired by an Euler step:

{\mathbf{G}}_{{\bm{\theta}}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t,s):=\frac{s}{t}{\mathbf{x}}_{t}+\frac{t-s}{t}{\mathbf{g}}_{\bm{\theta}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t,s).

Since the true flow map
{\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to s}
cannot be accessed directly, CTM
constructs a surrogate target using its own outputs, in the spirit of a
stop-gradient approximation (as in CM). Concretely, it replaces the oracle with
an intermediate reference:

{\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to s}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})\approx{\mathbf{G}}_{{\bm{\theta}}^{-}}({\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to u}({\mathbf{x}}_{t}),u,s),\quad\text{for}\quad t>u>s,

where
{\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to u}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})
is obtained either by applying a few-step solver
to a pre-trained diffusion model (distillation), or by using CTM’s own
parameterization
{\mathbf{g}}_{\bm{\theta}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t,t)
to generate a self-teacher trajectory.

In contrast, MF takes a different perspective: instead of directly predicting

{\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to s}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})
, it parameterizes the network to approximate the

average drift
along the trajectory:

{\mathbf{h}}_{{\bm{\theta}}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t,s)\approx{\mathbf{h}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t,s):=\frac{1}{s-t}\int_{t}^{s}{\mathbf{v}}({\mathbf{x}}_{u},u)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u.

Conceptually, CTM and MF share the same underlying framework, but differ in
how the learned function is parameterized. The relation can be written as

\displaystyle{\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to s}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})

\displaystyle=\frac{s}{t}{\mathbf{x}}_{t}+\frac{t-s}{t}\underbrace{\Big[{\mathbf{x}}_{t}+\frac{t}{t-s}\int_{t}^{s}{\mathbf{v}}({\mathbf{x}}_{u},u)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u\Big]}_{\approx{\mathbf{g}}_{\bm{\theta}}}

\displaystyle={\mathbf{x}}_{t}+(s-t)\underbrace{\Big[\frac{1}{s-t}\int_{t}^{s}{\mathbf{v}}({\mathbf{x}}_{u},u)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u\Big]}_{\approx{\mathbf{h}}_{\bm{\theta}}}.

Thus, CTM can be seen as approximating the first form via
{\mathbf{g}}_{\bm{\theta}}
, while
MF approximates the second via
{\mathbf{h}}_{\bm{\theta}}
. Their backbone choices also
differ: CTM builds on EDM, whereas MF builds on flow matching.

Let

{\mathbf{g}}_{\bm{\theta}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t,s):={\mathbf{x}}_{t}-t\,{\mathbf{h}}_{\bm{\theta}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t,s),

and take the distance to be the squared norm
d({\mathbf{x}},{\mathbf{y}}):=\|{\mathbf{x}}-{\mathbf{y}}\|^{2}
.
Substituting into
Equation

4
, we can expand the loss term as

\displaystyle~d\big({\mathbf{G}}_{\bm{\theta}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t,s),{\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to s}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\left\lVert{\mathbf{G}}_{\bm{\theta}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t,s)-{\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to s}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\left\lVert\left(\frac{s}{t}{\mathbf{x}}_{t}+\frac{t-s}{t}\,{\mathbf{g}}_{\bm{\theta}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t,s)\right)-\left(\frac{s}{t}{\mathbf{x}}_{t}+\frac{t-s}{t}\Big[{\mathbf{x}}_{t}+\frac{t}{t-s}\int_{t}^{s}{\mathbf{v}}({\mathbf{x}}_{u},u)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u\Big]\right)\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\left(\frac{t-s}{t}\right)^{2}\left\lVert{\mathbf{g}}_{\bm{\theta}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t,s)-\left({\mathbf{x}}_{t}+\frac{t}{t-s}\int_{t}^{s}{\mathbf{v}}({\mathbf{x}}_{u},u)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u\right)\right\rVert^{2}

(8)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\left(\frac{t-s}{t}\right)^{2}\left\lVert\left({\mathbf{x}}_{t}-t{\mathbf{h}}_{\bm{\theta}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t,s)\right)-\left({\mathbf{x}}_{t}+\frac{t}{t-s}\int_{t}^{s}{\mathbf{v}}({\mathbf{x}}_{u},u)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u\right)\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\left(\frac{t-s}{t}\right)^{2}\left\lVert\left({\mathbf{x}}_{t}-t{\mathbf{h}}_{\bm{\theta}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t,s)\right)-\left({\mathbf{x}}_{t}+\frac{t}{t-s}\int_{t}^{s}{\mathbf{v}}({\mathbf{x}}_{u},u)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u\right)\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\left(t-s\right)^{2}\left\lVert{\mathbf{h}}_{\bm{\theta}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t,s)-\left(\frac{1}{s-t}\int_{t}^{s}{\mathbf{v}}({\mathbf{x}}_{u},u)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u\right)\right\rVert^{2}

(9)

Equations

8
and
9
show that the two
parameterizations are tightly connected. In particular,

\frac{1}{t^{2}}\Bigg\|{\mathbf{g}}_{\bm{\theta}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t,s)-\Big({\mathbf{x}}_{t}+\frac{t}{t-s}\int_{t}^{s}{\mathbf{v}}({\mathbf{x}}_{u},u)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u\Big)\Bigg\|^{2}=\Bigg\|{\mathbf{h}}_{\bm{\theta}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t,s)-\Big(\frac{1}{s-t}\int_{t}^{s}{\mathbf{v}}({\mathbf{x}}_{u},u)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u\Big)\Bigg\|^{2}.

(10)

Hence, the CTM and MF training losses are fundamentally equivalent, differing
only by a multiplicative constant. Moreover, in both cases setting
s=0

recovers the CM scenario, where each state is mapped directly to the clean data. Based on this observation, we will focus our theoretical analysis on
{\bm{\Psi}}_{s\to 0}
mostly (
Appendix

F
), noting that the same arguments extend naturally to the general
{\bm{\Psi}}_{s\to t}
, including the MF case.

C.2
Evaluating Alternatives for Post-Training Initialization

Ablation Study on Knowledge Distillation (KD) and Slow CMT
. Even though we have demonstrated the efficiency of CMT as a mid-training method, other variants are possible.
We take the flow map learning
{\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to 0}
as representative and highlight two straightforward alternatives.
Following the design principles of the mid-training stage, these variants are expected to be stable and easy to train (e.g., using stop-gradient-free regression targets), while relying on fewer ad-hoc tricks and hyperparameters:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{var}}^{(1)}({\bm{\theta}}):=\mathbb{E}_{t,\,p_{t}}\!\left[\;\|{\mathbf{f}}_{\bm{\theta}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t)-\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{0}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})\|_{2}^{2}\;\right],\quad\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{var}}^{(2)}({\bm{\theta}}):=\mathbb{E}_{p_{\mathrm{prior}}}\!\left[\;\|{\mathbf{f}}_{\bm{\theta}}({\mathbf{x}}_{T},T)-\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{0}({\mathbf{x}}_{T})\|_{2}^{2}\;\right],

where
\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{0}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})
denotes the estimate at
t{=}0
obtained by running the solver with the pre-trained diffusion model starting from the forward-perturbed sample
{\mathbf{x}}_{t}
at time
t
, and
\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{0}({\mathbf{x}}_{T})
is obtained similarly by running backward from a prior sample
{\mathbf{x}}_{T}
, as in
Luhman & Luhman 2021
.

We call
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{var}}^{(1)}({\bm{\theta}})
Slow CMT as it is doing CMT in principle but omits using intermediate points of the ODE-solver generated trajectory. In contrast, this Slow CMT
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{var}}^{(1)}({\bm{\theta}})
only uses the ODE trajectory end point. Thus, to produce the same amount of data, Slow CMT require more costly ODE-solver inference.
Meanwhile, the loss function
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{var}}^{(1)}({\bm{\theta}})
is called Knowledge Distillation (KD) following
Luhman & Luhman 2021
.

We compare CMT’s mid-training with KD and Slow CMT.
KD explicitly focuses on the mapping from noise to the corresponding data only, while CMT focuses on the entire ODE trajectory consistency. We use the CIFAR-10 dataset and train KD and Slow CMT with the same settings as CMT.
Subsequently, we use ECT to post-train for flow map learning with the three different initializations for 12.8 Mimgs cost.

Comparing CMT with KD, CMT’s 1/2 step FID (2.74/1.97) is much better than KD’s (3.54/2.19), verifying the benefits of learning intermediate steps.

Comparing CMT with its slow variant, they achieve a similar FID, with CMT’s 1/2 step FID (2.74/1.97) and Slow CMT’s 1/2 step FID (2.75/1.98). However, the Slow CMT’s mid-training stage costs 3x more GPU time since generating the regression target in the Slow version is more costly due to the failure to use intermediate points by the ODE-solver.

D.2
Analysis of Computational Cost in CM

Empirical Runtime Comparison with CMT, CD, and CT.

The important factor in CMT’s wall-clock time is the number of ODE solver steps (NFEs): steps along the trajectory are sequential, so higher NFE directly increases wall time. While solver-parallelization may ease this bottleneck
(
Shih et al. 2023
)
, we target fast training under prevailing practice by operating in the low-NFE regime.

Throughout, we fix
\mathrm{NFE}=16
, which we found to be a sweet spot: it provides sufficiently accurate supervision while keeping CMT only slightly slower than CT. We use the third-order multistep DPM-Solver++
(
Lu et al. 2025
)
for CM-style teachers, as it’s stable and effective at low NFEs.
The multistep scheme also reuses previously computed states
\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}}
to construct later states
\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{i}}
, further reducing overhead.
Two error sources matter: (i) the
distillation fit
error due to nonzero training loss and (ii) the
teacher discretization
error from small NFE; empirically, (i) dominates.

With
\mathrm{NFE}=16
, DPM-Solver++ attains FID typically within
0.2
of the best large-NFE setting across all datasets, indicating diminishing returns beyond
16
.
Consequently, CMT with
16
NFEs preserves speed while maintaining competitive quality.
Concretely, CMT attains training efficiency comparable to CD (which requires teacher inference) and continuous CT or MF requiring student JVP. Further, CMT’s cost is also close to that of discrete CT
(
Lu & Song 2025
)
. Per iteration, CMT is only 15%–25% slower than discrete CT
(
Geng et al. 2025b
)
, where we used Easy CT and Easy CD
(
Geng et al. 2025b
)
’s framework for time evaluation.
However, CMT converges faster in far fewer iterations than these alternatives in the entire training loop; thus, the wall-clock runtime is lower. This advantage is clear on ImageNet
64\times 64
and
512\times 512
, where CMT outperforms sCT/sCD while reducing training data and GPU cost by 93%–98%. We also cut 50% of the training GPU time compared with vanilla MF while outperforming in FID.

Overall, because it provides a stronger proxy of the flow-map trajectory during mid-training, it substantially accelerates the subsequent flow-map post-training (e.g., CT).

If teacher trajectories are pre-generated, training with CMT reduces to a single backpropagated student evaluation per pair, which is the fastest regime and is used by prior distillation work
(
Zheng et al. 2023
)
. However, pre-generation requires extra preparation and storage; to keep the setup simple and comparable, all our experiments run the ODE solver on-the-fly during training.

Theoretical NFEs Comparison with CMT , CD, and CT.

We compare CMT, CD, and CT by teacher function evaluations (NFEs), student forwards, and student backpropagations. Costs are normalized per training pair, where a pair is one input–target term in the loss.

In CMT, each teacher trajectory
\{\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{i}}\}_{i=0}^{M}
yields
M
pairs
(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{i}},t_{i})\mapsto\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{0}
for
i=1,\dots,M
. Let
M\!\geq\!k
be the number of steps from
t_{M}
to
t_{0}
,
k
the multistep order, and
s
the NFE cost per bootstrap step used to initialize the first
k{-}1
history points (e.g.,
s{=}1
for Euler,
s{=}2
for Heun). An explicit
k
-step solver then incurs one new teacher evaluation per step thereafter. Hence

\text{NFEs}_{\text{traj}}\;=\;s\,(k{-}1)\;+\;\bigl(M-(k{-}1)\bigr)\;=\;M+(s{-}1)(k{-}1),

and the
per-pair
teacher cost for CMT is

\text{Teacher NFEs per pair (CMT)}\;=\;1\;+\;\frac{(s{-}1)(k{-}1)}{M}.

In CD and CT, each pair corresponds to a single sampled time
t
. If
q
denotes teacher NFEs for the one-step teacher update used inside CD (e.g.,
q{=}1
Euler,
q{=}2
Heun), then CD has
\text{Teacher NFEs per pair}=q
; CT has none.

The above yields the following per-pair cost summary:

\displaystyle\text{\bf CMT:}

\displaystyle\text{Teacher NFEs}=1+\frac{(s-1)(k-1)}{M},

\displaystyle\text{Student}=1~\text{fwd}+1~\text{bwd},

\displaystyle\text{\bf CD:}

\displaystyle\text{Teacher NFEs}=q,

\displaystyle\text{Student}=1~\text{fwd}+1~\text{bwd},

\displaystyle\text{\bf CT:}

\displaystyle\text{Teacher NFEs}=0,

\displaystyle\text{Student}=2~\text{fwd}+1~\text{bwd}.

We now instantiate the parameters to match our experimental setup. With
M{=}16
and
k\in\{2,3\}
,

\text{CMT teacher NFEs per pair}=\begin{cases}1,&s{=}1~\text{(Euler warm-up)},\\[2.0pt]
1+\tfrac{k-1}{16}\in[1.06,\,1.12],&s{=}2~\text{(Heun warm-up)}.\end{cases}

Thus, relative to CD: CMT matches CD when
q{=}1
and
s{=}1
; it is only
+6\%\!\sim\!12\%
higher when
q{=}1
,
s{=}2
,
k\in\{2,3\}
; and it is cheaper than CD when
q{=}2
for these
M,k
. These accounting predictions align closely with the empirical measurements reported earlier in this subsection, further supporting the efficiency of CMT .

In short, we analyze the cost per input–target pair. In CMT, one teacher-generated trajectory yields many pairs by matching intermediate states to the clean target, whereas CD and CT generate each pair independently from a sampled time. Per pair, CMT needs one teacher call plus one student forward and one backpropagation; CD needs one teacher call plus the same student cost; CT needs no teacher call but two student forwards and one backpropagation. Hence, CMT is roughly as costly as CD and only slightly slower than CT, consistent with our empirical findings (Appendix
D.1
).
Meanwhile, CMT achieves near–unit teacher cost per pair while using a single student forward, making it a lightweight and effective choice for the mid-training stage.

Appendix E
Discussion on CMT Loss Metric

Loss Metric: LPIPS
. The metric is crucial to CMT’s performance. The LPIPS metric
(
Zhang et al. 2018
)
measuring perceptual similarity is known to align more closely with human vision.
Optimizing LPIPS loss helps models generate images that are perceptually similar to the original because it penalizes differences in a feature space that aligns better with human visual processing, rather than
L_{2}
loss in pixel space.
The features are derived from VGG
(
Simonyan & Zisserman 2015
)
, which is initially pretrained for ImageNet classification in torchvision
(
Paszke et al. 2019
)
, and then fine-tuned on human perceptual judgments (BAPPS dataset) to better reflect human judgments of image similarity.
CMT generates high-quality supervision signals using high-order multistep ODE solvers, providing accurate and stable labels. To encourage the student model to closely match the teacher’s output, we employ the LPIPS loss. Moreover, since CMT provides fixed and stable labels, the training process becomes inherently robust, obviating the need for additional robust loss functions such as the Huber loss used in iCT
(
Song & Dhariwal 2024
)
. This also eliminates the burden of tuning extra hyperparameters associated with such losses.
While minimizing the
L_{2}
loss yields outputs with low pixel-wise error relative to the teacher’s predictions, it often results in blurry images that fail to capture perceptual fidelity. This is because
L_{2}
loss penalizes deviations uniformly across all pixels, disregarding the spatial and structural cues that are critical for human visual perception.

Why ECT/iCT Uses Huber/
L_{1}
Loss?
The optimization objectives and training dynamics of ECT/iCT and CMT differ fundamentally. CMT leverages high-quality, fixed teacher labels generated via accurate numerical solvers, providing a reliable supervision signal throughout training. In contrast, ECT and iCT rely on self-generated guidance, where the model learns from its own predictions. In such self-training settings, the use of perceptual losses like LPIPS may introduce additional bias, as the supervision signal is inherently noisy and evolving.

Latent LPIPS Loss
.
LPIPS operates exclusively in pixel space, whereas latent CM faces the challenge of lacking a refined metric and must resort to traditional
L_{2}
.
ELatentLPIPS
(
Kang et al. 2024
)
trains an LPIPS metric in the autoencoder-dependent latent space. The idea is still to first train a VGG
(
Simonyan & Zisserman 2015
)
and then fine-tune it on BAPPS
(
Zhang et al. 2018
)
in the latent space.
For our latent space experiments, we train a VGG following their setup on the ImageNet datasets, but do not fine-tune on the additional BAPPS dataset to ensure a fair comparison with other methods. In other words, CMT do not resort to additional datasets while using the same train set as other baselines.

CMT with MF uses Squared
\ell_{2}
Loss
. This is because MF is a general flow map requiring mapping between any time steps, but not just the initial time corresponding to the clean data. Hence, the label in CMT with MF can be noisy data within the trajectory, rendering the LPIPS loss inapplicable. Hence, we resort to the common squared
\ell_{2}
loss.

Appendix F
Theoretical Analysis of CMT

F.1
Oracle Loss and CMT’s Approximation

The Minimizer of
Equation

2
.

We first show that the optimizer of
Equation

2
recovers the oracle flow map
{\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to 0}
.

Proposition F.1

(Oracle CM minimizer)
.

Assume:
(i)
d:\mathbb{R}^{D}\times\mathbb{R}^{D}\to[0,\infty)
satisfies
d(\mathbf{y},\mathbf{z})\geq 0
and
d(\mathbf{y},\mathbf{z})=0
iff
\mathbf{y}=\mathbf{z}
;
(ii)
{\mathbf{f}}_{\bm{\theta}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t)

\mathbb{E}\|{\mathbf{f}}_{\bm{\theta}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t)\|_{2}^{2}<\infty
. Then any minimizer of

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{oracle}\text{-}\mathrm{CM}}({\bm{\theta}})=\mathbb{E}_{t}\mathbb{E}_{{\mathbf{x}}_{t}\sim p_{t}}\Big[w(t)d\big({\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t),{\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to 0}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})\big)\Big]

satisfies

{\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}^{*}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t)={\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to 0}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})\quad\text{for a.e. }{\mathbf{x}}_{t}\sim p_{t}\text{ and }t.

If, in addition,
d(\cdot,\mathbf{z})
is strictly convex for each fixed
\mathbf{z}
, then this minimizer is unique (a.e.).

Proof.

Let
\mathrm{Unif}[0,T]
denote the time distribution of
t
. The integrand

w(t)d\big({\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t),{\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to 0}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})\big)

is a nonnegative measurable function. Hence

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{oracle}\text{-}\mathrm{CM}}({\bm{\theta}})\geq 0\quad\text{for all }{\bm{\theta}}.

Choosing
{\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t)\equiv{\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to 0}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})
makes the integrand identically zero (since
d(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{z})=0
), so the infimum of the objective is
0
and is attained by this choice. It remains to show that any other minimizer must agree with
{\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to 0}
almost surely.

Suppose
{\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}^{\star}}
is a minimizer and define the set

A\coloneqq\{(t,{\mathbf{x}}_{t}):{\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}^{\star}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t)\neq{\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to 0}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})\}.

On
A
we have
d\big({\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}^{\star}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t),{\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to 0}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})\big)>0
by assumption (ii).
Since
w(t)>0
for
\mathrm{Unif}[0,T]
-a.e.
t
, if
\mathrm{Unif}[0,T]\times p_{t}(A)>0
then by Tonelli or Fubini theorem

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{oracle}\text{-}\mathrm{CM}}({\bm{\theta}}^{\star})=\mathbb{E}\left[w(t)d\big({\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}^{\star}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t),{\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to 0}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})\big)\right]\geq\mathbb{E}\left[w(t)\mathbf{1}_{\mathrm{CMT}}(t,{\mathbf{x}}_{t})c\right]>0

for some
c>0
, contradicting minimality (the minimum value is
0
).
Therefore
\mathrm{Unif}[0,T]\times p_{t}(A)=0
, i.e.,
{\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}^{\star}}={\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to 0}
holds
\mathrm{Unif}[0,T]\times p_{t}
-a.e.
If
d(\cdot,\mathbf{z})
is strictly convex (e.g., squared
\ell_{2}
), pointwise equality (a.e.) is the only way to achieve the minimum, giving uniqueness (a.e.).
∎

CMT’s Loss is Equivalent to
Equation

2
.

We now prove
Theorem

3.1
, which shows that the CMT objective is, up to a discrete-time approximation, equivalent to minimizing the oracle CM flow map loss in
Equation

2
. We assume that the terminal distribution
p_{\mathrm{prior}}
coincides with
p_{T}
.
Under this assumption, we will show that the following result holds:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{oracle}\text{-}\mathrm{CM}}({\bm{\theta}})=\mathbb{E}_{t}\mathbb{E}_{p_{T}({\mathbf{x}}_{T})}\left[d\left({\mathbf{f}}_{\bm{\theta}}({\bm{\Psi}}_{T\to t}({\mathbf{x}}_{T}),t),{\bm{\Psi}}_{T\to 0}({\mathbf{x}}_{T})\right)\right].

Proof.

We can exploit the semi-group property of the solution map to express the intermediate distribution
p_{t}
as:

\displaystyle p_{t}={\bm{\Psi}}_{0\to t}\sharp p_{\mathrm{data}}={\bm{\Psi}}_{T\to t}\sharp p_{\mathrm{prior}}=\int\delta({\mathbf{x}}_{t}-{\bm{\Psi}}_{T\to t}({\mathbf{x}}_{T}))p_{T}({\mathbf{x}}_{T})\mathrm{d}{\mathbf{x}}_{T}.

Using this as a change of variables in
Equation

2
, we obtain:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{oracle}\text{-}\mathrm{CM}}({\bm{\theta}})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{t}\mathbb{E}_{p_{t}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})}\left[d\left({\mathbf{f}}_{\bm{\theta}}({\bm{\Psi}}_{T\to t}({\mathbf{x}}_{T}),t),{\bm{\Psi}}_{T\to 0}({\mathbf{x}}_{T})\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{t}\int d\left({\mathbf{f}}_{\bm{\theta}}({\bm{\Psi}}_{T\to t}({\mathbf{x}}_{T}),t),{\bm{\Psi}}_{T\to 0}({\mathbf{x}}_{T})\right)p_{t}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})\mathrm{d}{\mathbf{x}}_{t}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{t}\int\int d\left({\mathbf{f}}_{\bm{\theta}}({\bm{\Psi}}_{T\to t}({\mathbf{x}}_{T}),t),{\bm{\Psi}}_{T\to 0}({\mathbf{x}}_{T})\right)\delta({\mathbf{x}}_{t}-{\bm{\Psi}}_{T\to t}({\mathbf{x}}_{T}))p_{T}({\mathbf{x}}_{T})\mathrm{d}{\mathbf{x}}_{T}\mathrm{d}{\mathbf{x}}_{t}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{t}\int\int d\left({\mathbf{f}}_{\bm{\theta}}({\bm{\Psi}}_{T\to t}({\mathbf{x}}_{T}),t),{\bm{\Psi}}_{T\to 0}({\mathbf{x}}_{T})\right)p_{T}({\mathbf{x}}_{T})\delta({\mathbf{x}}_{t}-{\bm{\Psi}}_{T\to t}({\mathbf{x}}_{T}))\mathrm{d}{\mathbf{x}}_{t}\mathrm{d}{\mathbf{x}}_{T}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{t}\int d\left({\mathbf{f}}_{\bm{\theta}}({\bm{\Psi}}_{T\to t}({\mathbf{x}}_{T}),t),{\bm{\Psi}}_{T\to 0}({\mathbf{x}}_{T})\right)p_{T}({\mathbf{x}}_{T})\mathrm{d}{\mathbf{x}}_{T}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{t}\mathbb{E}_{p_{T}({\mathbf{x}}_{T})}\left[d\left({\mathbf{f}}_{\bm{\theta}}({\bm{\Psi}}_{T\to t}({\mathbf{x}}_{T}),t),-{\bm{\Psi}}_{T\to 0}({\mathbf{x}}_{T})\right)\right].

∎

Therefore, the CMT loss approximates the oracle objective
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{oracle}}
by leveraging a pre-trained diffusion model to estimate the solution map
{\bm{\Psi}}_{T\to t}({\mathbf{x}}_{T})
. This allows for a tractable surrogate to the otherwise intractable oracle loss.

Some Lemmas.

We summarize some auxiliary tools that we will use later.

Lemma F.1
.

Let
{\mathbf{x}}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{D}
be square–integrable and let
{\mathbf{x}}_{t}
be any random variable on the same probability space.
For any (deterministic) decoder
{\mathbf{F}}
such that
{\mathbf{F}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})
is square–integrable,

\mathbb{E}\big[\|{\mathbf{x}}_{0}-{\mathbf{F}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})\|_{2}^{2}\big]=\mathbb{E}\left[\Tr\mathrm{Var}({\mathbf{x}}_{0}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t})\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\|\mathbb{E}[{\mathbf{x}}_{0}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t}]-{\mathbf{F}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})\|_{2}^{2}\right].

Proof.

Write the conditional mean (posterior mean) as

\bm{\mu}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})\coloneqq\mathbb{E}[{\mathbf{x}}_{0}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t}],

and the zero–mean conditional residual as

{\mathbf{e}}\coloneqq{\mathbf{x}}_{0}-\bm{\mu}({\mathbf{x}}_{t}),\qquad\text{so that}\quad\mathbb{E}[{\mathbf{e}}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t}]=\mathbf{0}.

Then for any
{\mathbf{F}}
,

{\mathbf{x}}_{0}-{\mathbf{F}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})=\underbrace{\big({\mathbf{x}}_{0}-\bm{\mu}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})\big)}_{={\mathbf{e}}}+\big(\bm{\mu}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})-{\mathbf{F}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})\big).

Expand the squared norm and take expectations over
(t,{\mathbf{x}}_{t})
:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\|{\mathbf{x}}_{0}-{\mathbf{F}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})\|_{2}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\|{\mathbf{e}}\|_{2}^{2}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\|\bm{\mu}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})-{\mathbf{F}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})\|_{2}^{2}\right]+2\mathbb{E}\left[\langle{\mathbf{e}},\bm{\mu}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})-{\mathbf{F}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})\rangle\right].

The cross term vanishes by the tower property and the fact that

\bm{\mu}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})-{\mathbf{F}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})
is
\sigma({\mathbf{x}}_{t})
–measurable:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\langle{\mathbf{e}},\bm{\mu}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})-{\mathbf{F}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})\rangle\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}\left[\langle{\mathbf{e}},\bm{\mu}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})-{\mathbf{F}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})\rangle\middle|{\mathbf{x}}_{t}\right]\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\mathbb{E}[{\mathbf{e}}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t}],\bm{\mu}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})-{\mathbf{F}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})\right\rangle\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\langle\mathbf{0},\bm{\mu}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})-{\mathbf{F}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})\rangle\right]=0.

For the first term, use the definition of conditional covariance:

\operatorname{Var}({\mathbf{x}}_{0}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t})=\mathbb{E}\left[{\mathbf{e}}{\mathbf{e}}^{\top}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t}\right],

whose trace equals the conditional mean squared residual:

\Tr\mathrm{Var}({\mathbf{x}}_{0}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t})=\operatorname{tr}\mathbb{E}\left[{\mathbf{e}}{\mathbf{e}}^{\top}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t}\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\|{\mathbf{e}}\|_{2}^{2}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t}\right].

Taking expectations over
{\mathbf{x}}_{t}
yields

\mathbb{E}\left[\|{\mathbf{e}}\|_{2}^{2}\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\Tr\mathrm{Var}({\mathbf{x}}_{0}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t})\right].

Combining the pieces gives

\mathbb{E}\big[\|{\mathbf{x}}_{0}-{\mathbf{F}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})\|_{2}^{2}\big]=\mathbb{E}\left[\Tr\mathrm{Var}({\mathbf{x}}_{0}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t})\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\|\bm{\mu}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})-{\mathbf{F}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})\|_{2}^{2}\right],

which is the claimed identity.
∎

Lemma F.2
.

Let the
C
hold. Then

\big\|\mathtt{Solver}_{s\to t}\big(\mathtt{Solver}_{t\to s}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})\big)-{\mathbf{x}}_{t}\big\|=\mathcal{O}\left(\Delta t^{p}\right).

Proof.

Let
{\bm{\Phi}}_{t\to s}:=\mathtt{Solver}_{t\to s}
be a numerical solver (using the teacher drift) on a uniform grid with step size
\Delta t
. For any
t
and
{\mathbf{x}}_{t}
,

\displaystyle\big\|{\bm{\Phi}}_{s\to t}\big({\bm{\Phi}}_{t\to s}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})\big)-{\mathbf{x}}_{t}\big\|

\displaystyle\leq\underbrace{\big\|{\bm{\Phi}}_{s\to t}\big({\bm{\Phi}}_{t\to s}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})\big)-{\bm{\Psi}}_{s\to t}\big({\bm{\Phi}}_{t\to s}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})\big)\big\|}_{\text{backward global error}}

\displaystyle\qquad+\underbrace{\big\|{\bm{\Psi}}_{s\to t}\big({\bm{\Phi}}_{t\to s}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})\big)-{\bm{\Psi}}_{s\to t}\big({\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to s}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})\big)\big\|}_{\text{propagation of forward error}}

Therefore,

\big\|\mathtt{Solver}_{s\to t}\big(\mathtt{Solver}_{t\to s}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})\big)-{\mathbf{x}}_{t}\big\|=\mathcal{O}\left(\Delta t^{p}\right).

∎

.

Analysis of Gradient Bias

We focus on the setting where the distance function is the squared
\ell_{2}

and the weight function is uniform,
w(t)\equiv 1

Throughout this section we work with the CM flow map
{\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to 0}
;
analogous statements for other flow maps, such as the CTM family, can be derived

For convenience, we rewrite
Equation

2

\displaystyle\ell_{\mathrm{oracle}}({\bm{\theta}};{\bm{\xi}}):=\bigl\|{\mathbf{f}}_{\bm{\theta}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t)-{\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to 0}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})\bigr\|_{2}^{2},

(11)

and define the CM training loss as

\displaystyle\ell_{\mathrm{CM}}({\bm{\theta}};{\bm{\xi}}):=\bigl\|{\mathbf{f}}_{\bm{\theta}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t)-{\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}^{-}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t-\Delta t},t-\Delta t)\bigr\|_{2}^{2},

(12)

\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{oracle}}({\bm{\theta}}):=\mathbb{E}_{{\bm{\xi}}}\big[\ell_{\mathrm{oracle}}({\bm{\theta}};{\bm{\xi}})\big],\qquad\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{CM}}({\bm{\theta}}):=\mathbb{E}_{{\bm{\xi}}}\big[\ell_{\mathrm{CM}}({\bm{\theta}};{\bm{\xi}})\big],

which represent the oracle target loss and the CM training loss, respectively.
Finally, we define the squared gradient bias

\mathcal{B}({\bm{\theta}}):=\big\|\nabla_{{\bm{\theta}}}\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{oracle}}({\bm{\theta}})-\nabla_{{\bm{\theta}}}\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{CM}}({\bm{\theta}})\big\|_{2}^{2}.

Theorem F.1

(Bias Comparisons)
.

Assume that
A
,
C
and
B
hold with
p\geq 1
.
Then the following bias comparisons are valid for the four different initialization schemes
(
{\bm{\theta}}={\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{CMT}}
,
{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{DM}}
,
{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{gCD}}
, or random initialization
{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{rand.}}
)
of flow map model training:

(i)

CMT
:

\mathcal{B}({\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{CMT}})=\mathcal{O}\left(\varepsilon+\Delta t^{2}+\Delta t^{p}\right).

(ii)

Diffusion Model
:

\mathcal{B}({\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{DM}})=\mathcal{O}\left(\varepsilon+\Delta t^{2}+\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\tfrac{\sigma_{t}^{2}}{\alpha_{t}^{2}}\right]\right)+\mathbb{E}_{{\mathbf{x}}_{t},t}\left[\big\|{\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to 0}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})-\mathbb{E}[{\mathbf{x}}_{0}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t}]\big\|_{2}^{2}\right].

(iii)

General Consistency Distillation
: For a fixed
u\in[0,T]
, assume in addition that

\delta_{u}:=\mathbb{E}_{{\mathbf{x}}_{u}\sim p_{u}}\big\|{\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{gCD}}}({\mathbf{x}}_{u},u)-{\bm{\Psi}}_{u\to 0}({\mathbf{x}}_{u})\big\|^{2}<\infty.

Then

\mathcal{B}({\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{gCD}})=\mathcal{O}\left(\varepsilon+\Delta t^{2}+\delta_{u}\right).

(iv)

Random Initialization
:

\mathcal{B}({\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{rand.}})=\mathcal{O}(1).

Proof.

Taking the gradient of
\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{oracle}}
, we obtain the unbiased oracle CM gradient as:

\displaystyle\nabla_{\bm{\theta}}\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{oracle}}({\bm{\theta}})=\mathbb{E}_{t}\mathbb{E}_{{\mathbf{x}}_{t}\sim p_{t}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})}\Big[\nabla_{\bm{\theta}}{\mathbf{f}}_{\bm{\theta}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t)\cdot\big({\mathbf{f}}_{\bm{\theta}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t)-{\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to 0}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})\big)\Big].

Likewise, the CM gradient is

\displaystyle\nabla_{\bm{\theta}}\bar{\ell}_{\text{CM}}({\bm{\theta}})=\mathbb{E}_{t}\mathbb{E}_{{\mathbf{x}}_{t}\sim p_{t}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})}\Big[\nabla_{\bm{\theta}}{\mathbf{f}}_{\bm{\theta}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t)\cdot\big({\mathbf{f}}_{\bm{\theta}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t)-{\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}^{-}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t-\Delta t},t-\Delta t)\big)\Big].

CM is approximating
{\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to 0}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})
with
f_{{\bm{\theta}}^{-}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t-\Delta t},t-\Delta t)
. CMT and diffusion differ in the initialization of
{\bm{\theta}}
. The one-point bias can be bounded:

\displaystyle~\|\nabla_{\bm{\theta}}\bar{\ell}_{\text{CM}}({\bm{\theta}})-\nabla_{\bm{\theta}}\bar{\ell}_{\text{Oracle}}({\bm{\theta}})\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle~\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}}\Big[\|\nabla_{\bm{\theta}}{\mathbf{f}}_{\bm{\theta}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t)\cdot\big({\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}^{-}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t-\Delta t},t-\Delta t)-{\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to 0}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})\big)\|_{2}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle~\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}}\Big[\|\nabla_{\bm{\theta}}{\mathbf{f}}_{\bm{\theta}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t)\|_{2}\cdot\|{\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}^{-}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t-\Delta t},t-\Delta t)-{\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to 0}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})\|_{2}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle~G\cdot\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}}\Big[\|{\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}^{-}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t-\Delta t},t-\Delta t)-{\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to 0}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})\|_{2}\Big],

Namely, the deviation with the gradient of the oracle loss is upper bounded as

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{\bm{\theta}}\ell_{\text{CM}}({\bm{\theta}})-\nabla_{\bm{\theta}}\ell_{\text{Oracle}}({\bm{\theta}})\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq G\cdot\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}}\Big[\|{\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}^{-}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t-\Delta t},t-\Delta t)-{\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to 0}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})\|_{2}\Big].

In the following, we individually derive the upper bound for different initialization scenarios. Denote
t^{\prime}:=t-\Delta t
for notational simplicity.

Case 1. CMT :

We denote
{\bm{\Phi}}_{t\to s}({\mathbf{x}}_{t}):=\mathtt{Solver}_{t\to s}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})
. Given a sample
{\mathbf{x}}_{t}\sim p_{t}
and time
t
, define
\widehat{{\mathbf{x}}}_{t}:={\bm{\Phi}}_{T\to t}({\bm{\Phi}}_{t\to T}({\mathbf{x}}_{t}))
.

For CMT initialization at
{\bm{\theta}}={\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{\mathrm{CMT}}}
, we have

\displaystyle\|{\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{\mathrm{CMT}}}^{-}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t^{\prime}},t^{\prime})-{\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to 0}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})\|_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\|{\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{\mathrm{CMT}}}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t^{\prime}},t^{\prime})-{\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to 0}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle~{\|{\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{\mathrm{CMT}}}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t)-{\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{\mathrm{CMT}}}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t^{\prime}},t^{\prime})\|_{2}}+{\big\|{\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{\mathrm{CMT}}}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t)-{\bm{\Phi}}_{T\to 0}({\bm{\Phi}}_{t\to T}({\mathbf{x}}_{t}))\big\|}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+{\big\|{\bm{\Phi}}_{T\to 0}({\bm{\Phi}}_{t\to T}({\mathbf{x}}_{t}))-{\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to 0}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})\big\|}

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle~\mathrm{(I)}+\mathrm{(II)}+\mathrm{(III)}.

For (I), by Lipschitzness and the forward parameterization,

\mathrm{(I)}\leq{\mathrm{Lip}}({\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{\mathrm{CMT}}}})\big(\|{\mathbf{x}}_{t^{\prime}}-{\mathbf{x}}_{t}\|+|t^{\prime}-t|\big),\qquad\mathbb{E}\|{\mathbf{x}}_{t^{\prime}}-{\mathbf{x}}_{t}\|_{2}^{2}=\mathcal{O}(\Delta t^{2}),

since
\alpha_{t^{\prime}}-\alpha_{t}=\mathcal{O}(\Delta t)
and
\beta_{t^{\prime}}-\beta_{t}=\mathcal{O}(\Delta t)
.
Hence
\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{(I)}^{2}]=\mathcal{O}(\Delta t^{2})
.

For (III), since
{\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to 0}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})={\bm{\Psi}}_{T\to 0}\left({\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to T}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})\right)
, with
Lemma

F.2
we have
\|\widehat{{\mathbf{x}}}_{t}-{\mathbf{x}}_{t}\|=\mathcal{O}(\Delta t^{p})
. Thus,

\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{(III)}^{2}]=\mathcal{O}(\Delta t^{2p}).

For (II), we first insert
\widehat{{\mathbf{x}}}_{t}={\bm{\Phi}}_{T\to t}({\bm{\Phi}}_{t\to T}({\mathbf{x}}_{t}))
:

\mathrm{(II)}\leq\ {\big\|{\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{CMT}}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t)-{\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{CMT}}}(\widehat{{\mathbf{x}}}_{t},t)\big\|}+{\big\|{\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{CMT}}}(\widehat{{\mathbf{x}}}_{t},t)-{\bm{\Phi}}_{T\to 0}({\bm{\Phi}}_{t\to T}({\mathbf{x}}_{t}))\big\|}=:{\mathrm{(IIa)}}+{\mathrm{(IIb)}}.

From
Lemma

F.2
and Lipschitzness,

\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{(IIa)}^{2}]=\mathcal{O}(\Delta t^{2p})
.
For (IIb), define

g({\mathbf{x}}_{T},t):=\big\|{\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{CMT}}}({\bm{\Phi}}_{T\to t}({\mathbf{x}}_{T}),t)-{\bm{\Phi}}_{T\to 0}({\mathbf{x}}_{T})\big\|_{2}^{2}.

Then

\mathrm{(IIb)}^{2}=g({\bm{\Phi}}_{t\to T}({\mathbf{x}}_{t}),t)

When the CMT’s training expectation is taken with

{\mathbf{x}}_{T}\sim p_{\mathrm{prior}}
and
t\sim\mathrm{Unif}[0,T]
, we compare

\mathbb{E}_{({\mathbf{x}}_{T},t)\sim p_{\mathrm{prior}}\times\mathrm{Unif}}[g({\mathbf{x}}_{T},t)]

to
\mathbb{E}_{({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t)}[g({\bm{\Phi}}_{t\to T}({\mathbf{x}}_{t}),t)]
.
Using the coupling

{\mathbf{x}}_{T}^{\star}\sim p_{\mathrm{prior}}
,
{\mathbf{x}}_{t}={\bm{\Psi}}_{T\to t}({\mathbf{x}}_{T}^{\star})
, standard stability of
{\bm{\Phi}}
and
{\mathbf{f}}

yields a Lipschitz constant
{\mathrm{Lip}}(g)
(in
{\mathbf{x}}_{T}
) such that

\big|\mathbb{E}g({\bm{\Phi}}_{t\to T}({\mathbf{x}}_{t}),t)-\mathbb{E}g({\mathbf{x}}_{T}^{\star},t)\big|\leq\ {\mathrm{Lip}}(g)\mathbb{E}\big\|{\bm{\Phi}}_{t\to T}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})-{\mathbf{x}}_{T}^{\star}\big\|_{2}=\mathcal{O}(\Delta t^{p}).

Hence

\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{(IIb)}^{2}]=\mathbb{E}g({\bm{\Phi}}_{t\to T}({\mathbf{x}}_{t}),t)\leq\ \mathbb{E}g({\mathbf{x}}_{T}^{\star},t)+\mathcal{O}(\Delta t^{p})\leq\varepsilon+\mathcal{O}(\Delta t^{p}).

Collecting the bounds,

\mathbb{E}\Big\|{\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{CMT}}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t^{\prime}},t^{\prime})-{\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to 0}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})\Big\|_{2}^{2}\leq\ 3\mathcal{O}(\Delta t^{2})+3\big(\varepsilon+\mathcal{O}(\Delta t^{p})\big)+3\mathcal{O}(\Delta t^{2p}).

Thus,

\mathbb{E}\Big\|{\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{CMT}}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t^{\prime}},t^{\prime})-{\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to 0}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})\Big\|_{2}^{2}=\varepsilon+\mathcal{O}(\Delta t^{p})+\mathcal{O}(\Delta t^{2})\qquad(p\geq 1).

Case 2. Diffusion Model:
Let the CM loss be initialized at the pre-trained diffusion model weights
{\bm{\theta}}={\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{DM}}
, then we have

\displaystyle~\left\lVert{\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{DM}}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t}^{\prime},t^{\prime})-{\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to 0}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})\right\rVert_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle~\left\lVert{\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{DM}}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t^{\prime}},t^{\prime})-{\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{DM}}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t)\right\rVert_{2}+\left\lVert{\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{DM}}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t)-{\mathbf{x}}_{0}\right\rVert_{2}+\left\lVert{\mathbf{x}}_{0}-{\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to 0}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})\right\rVert_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle~\left\lVert{\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{DM}}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t^{\prime}},t^{\prime})-{\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{DM}}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t)\right\rVert_{2}^{2}+\left\lVert{\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{DM}}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t)-{\mathbf{x}}_{0}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}+\left\lVert{\mathbf{x}}_{0}-{\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to 0}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})\right\rVert_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle\mathrm{(I)}+\mathrm{(II)}+\mathrm{(III)}.

For (I) and (II), we have

\displaystyle~\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}}\left[\left\lVert{\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{DM}}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t)-{\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{DM}}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t-{\Delta t}},t-{\Delta t})\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\right]+\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}}\left[\left\lVert{\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{DM}}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t)-{\mathbf{x}}_{0}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle~{\mathrm{Lip}}({\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{DM}})\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}}\left[\left\lVert{\mathbf{x}}_{t}-{\mathbf{x}}_{t}^{\prime}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\right]+\varepsilon

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\mathcal{O}(\Delta t^{2}+\varepsilon),

following the similar argument in CMT’s case.

However, using the pre-trained diffusion model’s weight as an initialization induces an additional discrepancy between the data
{\mathbf{x}}_{0}
and the reverse-time solution of ODE
{\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to 0}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})
, where
{\mathbf{x}}_{t}
is perturbed from
{\mathbf{x}}_{0}
. We will obtain a general upper bound of the term (III) with
\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}}\left[\left\lVert{\mathbf{x}}_{0}-{\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to 0}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\right]
.

Applying
Lemma

F.1
with
{\mathbf{F}}={\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to 0}
and then averaging over
t
, we obtain

\mathbb{E}_{{\mathbf{x}}_{0},{\bm{\epsilon}},t}\big[\left\lVert{\mathbf{x}}_{0}-{\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to 0}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\big]=\mathbb{E}_{t,{\mathbf{x}}_{t}}\big[\Tr\mathrm{Var}({\mathbf{x}}_{0}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t},t)\big]+\mathbb{E}_{t,{\mathbf{x}}_{t}}\big[\left\lVert{\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to 0}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})-\mathbb{E}[{\mathbf{x}}_{0}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t}]\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\big].

where the second term means the extra MSE we pay because the flow map is not necessary the Bayes–optimal estimator (the posterior mean).

Below we compute the upper bound for
\mathbb{E}_{t,{\mathbf{x}}_{t}}\big[\Tr\mathrm{Var}({\mathbf{x}}_{0}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t})\big]
. Given an observation
{\mathbf{x}}_{t}
with
t
fixed, the minimum mean–squared error (mmse) is

\mathsf{mmse}(t)\coloneqq\inf_{{\mathbf{f}}}\mathbb{E}\big[\|{\mathbf{x}}_{0}-{\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{CMT}}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})\|_{2}^{2}\big],

where the infimum is over all measurable
{\mathbf{f}}
with finite second moment.
The minimizer is the posterior mean
\mathbb{E}[{\mathbf{x}}_{0}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t}]
, and

\mathsf{mmse}(t)=\mathbb{E}\big[\|{\mathbf{x}}_{0}-\mathbb{E}[{\mathbf{x}}_{0}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t}]\|_{2}^{2}\big]=\mathbb{E}_{{\mathbf{x}}_{t}}\big[\Tr\mathrm{Var}({\mathbf{x}}_{0}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t})\big].

Since
\mathsf{mmse}(t)
is the minimum risk over all estimators,
its value is bounded above by the risk of any specific estimator. Take the linear estimator

{\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{CMT}}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})=(1/\alpha_{t}){\mathbf{x}}_{t}
. Using
{\mathbf{x}}_{t}=\alpha_{t}{\mathbf{x}}_{0}+\sigma_{t}{\bm{\epsilon}}
and the independence of
{\mathbf{x}}_{0}
and
{\bm{\epsilon}}
,

\mathbb{E}_{{\mathbf{x}}_{t}}\big[\Tr\mathrm{Var}({\mathbf{x}}_{0}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t})\big]\leq\mathbb{E}\big\|{\mathbf{x}}_{0}-\tfrac{1}{\alpha_{t}}{\mathbf{x}}_{t}\big\|_{2}^{2}=\mathbb{E}\big\|{\mathbf{x}}_{0}-\tfrac{1}{\alpha_{t}}(\alpha_{t}{\mathbf{x}}_{0}+\sigma_{t}{\bm{\epsilon}})\big\|_{2}^{2}=\mathbb{E}\big\|-\tfrac{\sigma_{t}}{\alpha_{t}}{\bm{\epsilon}}\big\|_{2}^{2}=\tfrac{\sigma_{t}^{2}}{\alpha_{t}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\|{\bm{\epsilon}}\|_{2}^{2}=\tfrac{\sigma_{t}^{2}}{\alpha_{t}^{2}}D.

Hence
\mathsf{mmse}(t)\leq(\sigma_{t}^{2}/\alpha_{t}^{2})D
. Averaging over
t
gives the bound:

\mathbb{E}_{{\mathbf{x}}_{0},{\bm{\epsilon}},t}\big[\|{\mathbf{x}}_{0}-{\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to 0}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})\|_{2}^{2}\big]\leq D\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\tfrac{\sigma_{t}^{2}}{\alpha_{t}^{2}}\right]+\mathbb{E}_{{\mathbf{x}}_{t},t}\big[\|{\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to 0}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})-\mathbb{E}[{\mathbf{x}}_{0}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t}]\|_{2}^{2}\big]

Therefore, to summarize, we have

\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}}\left\lVert{\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{DM}}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t}^{\prime},t^{\prime})-{\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to 0}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})\right\rVert_{2}^{2}=\mathcal{O}\left(\Delta t^{2}+\varepsilon+\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\tfrac{\sigma_{t}^{2}}{\alpha_{t}^{2}}\right]\right)+\mathbb{E}_{{\mathbf{x}}_{t},t}\big[\|{\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to 0}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})-\mathbb{E}[{\mathbf{x}}_{0}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t}]\|_{2}^{2}\big]

Case 3. General Consistency Distillation:
Let
{\bm{\Phi}}_{t\to s}({\mathbf{x}}_{t}):=\mathtt{Solver}_{t\to s}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})
be the
p
-th order solver, solving the PF-ODE with the teacher diffusion model’s drift.

When initializing at
{\bm{\theta}}={\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{gCD}}
, we need to additionally assume that:

\delta_{u}:=\mathbb{E}_{{\mathbf{x}}_{u}\sim p_{u}}\big\|{\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{gCD}}}({\mathbf{x}}_{u},u)-{\bm{\Psi}}_{u\to 0}({\mathbf{x}}_{u})\big\|^{2}<\infty.

General CD at a single
u
does not control the bias to the oracle
{\bm{\Psi}}_{u\to 0}
;
\delta_{u}
can be arbitrarily large even if the General CD is trained well with small
\varepsilon
. The term
\delta_{u}
supplies the necessary anchor at
u
.

We use triangle inequality to obtain

\displaystyle~\left\lVert{\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{gCD}}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t}^{\prime},t^{\prime})-{\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to 0}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})\right\rVert_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle~\left\lVert{\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{gCD}}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t^{\prime}},t^{\prime})-{\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{gCD}}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t)\right\rVert_{2}^{2}+\left\lVert{\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{gCD}}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t)-{\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{gCD}}}\left({\bm{\Phi}}_{t\to u}({\mathbf{x}}_{t}),u\right)\right\rVert_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\left\lVert{\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{gCD}}}\left({\bm{\Phi}}_{t\to u}({\mathbf{x}}_{t}),u\right)-{\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to 0}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})\right\rVert_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle\mathrm{(I)}+\mathrm{(II)}+\mathrm{(III)}.

For (I),

\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}}[\mathrm{(I)}]\lesssim{\mathrm{Lip}}({\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{gCD}}})\left(\left\lVert{\mathbf{x}}_{t^{\prime}}-{\mathbf{x}}_{t}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}+\Delta t^{2}\right).

From
{\mathbf{x}}_{t}=\alpha_{t}{\mathbf{x}}_{0}+\sigma_{t}{\bm{\epsilon}}
, independence of
{\mathbf{x}}_{0}
and
{\bm{\epsilon}}
, we follow the similar derivation as the previous CMT’s cases:
Hence

\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{(I)}]=\mathcal{O}(\Delta t^{2}).

For (II), by the hypothesis
\mathcal{L}_{\text{gCD}}({\bm{\theta}};u)\leq\varepsilon
,

\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{(II)}]\leq\varepsilon.

For (III), we need to bridge from
u
to
0
. For notational simplicity, we denote
{\mathbf{z}}_{u}:={\bm{\Phi}}_{t\to u}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})
,
{\mathbf{y}}_{u}:={\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to u}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})
.
Inserting and subtracting the teacher at
u
, we will get:

\displaystyle~\|{\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{gCD}}}({\mathbf{z}}_{u},u)-{\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to 0}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\|{\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{gCD}}}({\mathbf{z}}_{u},u)-{\bm{\Psi}}_{u\to 0}({\mathbf{y}}_{u})\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle~{\|{\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{gCD}}}({\mathbf{z}}_{u},u)-{\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{gCD}}}({\mathbf{y}}_{u},u)\|}+{\|{\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{gCD}}}({\mathbf{y}}_{u},u)-{\bm{\Psi}}_{u\to 0}({\mathbf{y}}_{u})\|}+{\|{\bm{\Psi}}_{u\to 0}({\mathbf{y}}_{u})-{\bm{\Psi}}_{u\to 0}(z_{u})\|}

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle~\mathrm{(IIIa)}+\mathrm{(IIIb)}+\mathrm{(IIIc)}.

Therefore
\mathrm{(III)}\leq 3\left(\mathrm{(IIIa)}^{2}+\mathrm{(IIIb)}^{2}+\mathrm{(IIIc)}^{2}\right)
and, by assumptions

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{(IIIa)}^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq{\mathrm{Lip}}^{2}({\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{gCD}}})\mathbb{E}\|{\mathbf{z}}_{u}-{\mathbf{y}}_{u}\|^{2}=\mathcal{O}(\Delta t^{2p}),

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{(IIIb)}^{2}]

\displaystyle=\delta_{u},

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{(IIIc)}^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq{\mathrm{Lip}}^{2}({\bm{\Psi}})\mathbb{E}\|{\mathbf{z}}_{u}-{\mathbf{y}}_{u}\|^{2}=\mathcal{O}(\Delta t^{2p}).

Hence,

\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{(III)}]=\mathcal{O}(\Delta t^{2p}+\delta_{u}).

We thus conclude that

\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}}\left[\left\lVert{\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{gCD}}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t}^{\prime},t^{\prime})-{\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to 0}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\right]=\mathcal{O}(\Delta t^{2}+\varepsilon+\Delta t^{2p}+\delta_{u})=\mathcal{O}(\Delta t^{2}+\varepsilon+\delta_{u}),

as
p\geq 1
.

Case 4. Random Initialization:

\displaystyle~\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}}\left\lVert{\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{rand.}}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t}^{\prime},t^{\prime})-{\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to 0}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})\right\rVert_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle~\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}}\left\lVert{\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{rand.}}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t}^{\prime},t^{\prime})-{\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{rand.}}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t)\right\rVert_{2}^{2}+\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}}\left\lVert{\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{rand.}}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t)\right\rVert_{2}^{2}+\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}}\left\lVert{\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to 0}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})\right\rVert_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\mathcal{O}(\Delta t^{2})+\mathcal{O}(1)\ =

\displaystyle~\mathcal{O}(1).

∎

F.5
Analysis of Gradient Variance

Following the same setup as in
Section

F.4
, we focus on the case
where the distance is given by

d({\mathbf{x}},{\mathbf{y}}):=\|{\mathbf{x}}-{\mathbf{y}}\|_{2}^{2},\qquad w(t)\equiv 1,

and the CM flow map is denoted by
{\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to 0}
.
The general case can be obtained analogously.

For notational simplicity, let

{\bm{\xi}}:=(t,{\mathbf{x}}_{t})\sim\mathrm{Unif}[0,T]\times p_{t}.

The gradient variance with respective to
{\bm{\xi}}
of the expected loss
\ell_{\mathrm{CM}}({\bm{\theta}};{\bm{\xi}})
is given by

\mathcal{V}({\bm{\theta}}):=\mathrm{Var}_{{\bm{\xi}}}\!\left[\nabla_{{\bm{\theta}}}\ell_{\mathrm{CM}}({\bm{\theta}};{\bm{\xi}})\right]=\mathbb{E}_{{\bm{\xi}}}\!\left[\big\|\nabla_{{\bm{\theta}}}\ell_{\mathrm{CM}}({\bm{\theta}};{\bm{\xi}})-\mathbb{E}_{{\bm{\xi}}}[\nabla_{{\bm{\theta}}}\ell_{\mathrm{CM}}({\bm{\theta}};{\bm{\xi}})]\big\|_{2}^{2}\right]=\Tr\!\Big(\mathrm{Cov}_{{\bm{\xi}}}\big[\nabla_{{\bm{\theta}}}\ell_{\mathrm{CM}}({\bm{\theta}};{\bm{\xi}})\big]\Big).

Theorem F.2
.

Under the same assumptions as in
Theorem

F.1
. The following upper bounds on the variances hold for different initialization schemes:

1.

CMT :

\mathcal{V}({\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{CMT}})=\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon+\Delta t^{2})

2.

Diffusion Model:

\mathcal{V}({\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{DM}})=\min\big\{\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon),\mathcal{O}(\Delta t^{2})\big\}
.

3.

General Consistency Distillation:

\mathcal{V}({\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{gCD}})=\mathcal{O}\big(\varepsilon+\Delta t^{2}\big)

4.

Random Initialization:

\mathcal{V}({\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{rand.}})=\mathcal{O}(1)
.

Proof.

To analyze the variance, we observe that

\mathcal{V}({\bm{\theta}})=\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}}\left[\|\nabla_{{\bm{\theta}}}\ell_{\mathrm{CM}}({\bm{\theta}},{\bm{\xi}})\|_{2}^{2}\right]-\left\|\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}}[\nabla_{{\bm{\theta}}}\ell_{\mathrm{CM}}({\bm{\theta}},{\bm{\xi}})]\right\|_{2}^{2}\leq\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}}\left[\|\nabla_{{\bm{\theta}}}\ell_{\mathrm{CM}}({\bm{\theta}},{\bm{\xi}})\|_{2}^{2}\right]

We compute the gradient of the loss in the gradient variance formula as:

\nabla_{{\bm{\theta}}}\ell_{\mathrm{CM}}({\bm{\theta}},{\bm{\xi}})=2\cdot\mathbf{e}({\bm{\theta}})^{\top}\cdot\nabla_{\bm{\theta}}{\mathbf{f}}_{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}_{t},t),

where we define the error vector:

\mathbf{e}({\bm{\theta}}):={\mathbf{f}}_{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}_{t},t)-{\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}^{-}}(\mathbf{x}_{t-\Delta t},t-\Delta t)\in\mathbb{R}^{D}.

Now we bound the second moment by using
\|{\mathbf{A}}^{\top}{\mathbf{u}}\|_{2}\leq\|{\mathbf{A}}\|_{F}\|{\mathbf{u}}\|_{2}
with
\|{\mathbf{A}}\|_{F}
denoting the Frobenius norm of the matrix
{\mathbf{A}}
, we will get

\|\nabla_{\bm{\theta}}\ell_{\mathrm{CM}}({\bm{\theta}};{\bm{\xi}})\|_{2}^{2}=4\big\|\big(\nabla_{\bm{\theta}}{\mathbf{f}}_{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}_{t},t)\big)^{\top}\mathbf{e}({\bm{\theta}})\big\|_{2}^{2}\leq 4\big\|\nabla_{\bm{\theta}}{\mathbf{f}}_{\theta}(\mathbf{x}_{t},t)\big\|_{F}^{2}\big\|\mathbf{e}({\bm{\theta}})\big\|_{2}^{2}.

Therefore,

\mathcal{V}({\bm{\theta}})\leq 4\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}}\Big[\big\|\nabla_{\bm{\theta}}{\mathbf{f}}_{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}_{t},t)\big\|_{F}^{2}\big\|\mathbf{e}({\bm{\theta}})\big\|_{2}^{2}\Big].

From the assumption that
\big\|\nabla_{\bm{\theta}}{\mathbf{f}}_{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}_{t},t)\big\|_{F}\leq M
almost surely, then

\mathcal{V}({\bm{\theta}})\leq 4M^{2}\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}}\big[\big\|\mathbf{e}({\bm{\theta}})\big\|_{2}^{2}\big].

We now bound
\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}}[\|{\mathbf{e}}({\bm{\theta}})\|_{2}^{2}]
under the four different initializations.

Case 1. CMT :
Let
{\bm{\Phi}}_{t\to s}
be a
p
-th order solver for the PF-ODE built from a fixed drift, and define
the forward–backward (round-trip) map

\tilde{{\mathbf{x}}}_{t}:={\bm{\Phi}}_{T\to t}\big({\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to T}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})\big),\qquad\tilde{{\mathbf{x}}}_{t-\Delta t}:={\bm{\Phi}}_{T\to t-\Delta t}\big({\bm{\Psi}}_{t-\Delta t\to T}({\mathbf{x}}_{t-\Delta t})\big).

Insert solver’s round trips in
{\mathbf{e}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{CMT}}}
:

\displaystyle{\mathbf{e}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{CMT}}}

\displaystyle=\big({\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{CMT}}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t)-{\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{CMT}}}(\tilde{{\mathbf{x}}}_{t},t)\big)-\big({\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{CMT}}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t-\Delta t},t-\Delta t)-{\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{CMT}}}(\tilde{{\mathbf{x}}}_{t-\Delta t},t-\Delta t)\big)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\big({\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{CMT}}}(\tilde{{\mathbf{x}}}_{t},t)-{\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{CMT}}}(\tilde{{\mathbf{x}}}_{t-\Delta t},t-\Delta t)\big).

Thus, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\|{\mathbf{e}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{CMT}}}\|_{2}^{2}\lesssim{\mathrm{Lip}}({\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{CMT}}})^{2}\big(\left\lVert{\mathbf{x}}_{t}-\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{t}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}+\left\lVert{\mathbf{x}}_{t-{\Delta t}}-\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{t-{\Delta t}}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\big)+\mathbb{E}\|C_{t}\|_{2}^{2},

(13)

where
C_{t}:={\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{CMT}}}(\tilde{{\mathbf{x}}}_{t},t)-{\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{CMT}}}(\tilde{{\mathbf{x}}}_{t-\Delta t},t-\Delta t)
.

To address
C_{t}
term, we anchor
C_{t}
to the solver of teacher.
Set
{\mathbf{S}}_{t}({\mathbf{x}}):=\Phi_{T\to 0}({\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to T}({\mathbf{x}}))
. Then
{\mathbf{S}}_{t}({\mathbf{x}}_{t}):=\Phi_{T\to 0}({\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to T}({\mathbf{x}}_{t}))
, and
{\mathbf{S}}_{t-\Delta t}({\mathbf{x}}_{t-\Delta t}):=\Phi_{T\to 0}({\bm{\Psi}}_{t-\Delta t\to T}({\mathbf{x}}_{t-\Delta t}))
. We decompose
C_{t}
as the following:

C_{t}=\underbrace{\big({\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{CMT}}}(\tilde{{\mathbf{x}}}_{t},t)-{\mathbf{S}}_{t}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})\big)}_{\mathrm{(a)}}-\underbrace{\big({\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{CMT}}}(\tilde{{\mathbf{x}}}_{t-\Delta t},t-\Delta t)-{\mathbf{S}}_{t-\Delta t}({\mathbf{x}}_{t-{\Delta t}})\big)}_{\mathrm{(b)}}+\underbrace{\big({\mathbf{S}}_{t}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})-{\mathbf{S}}_{t-\Delta t}({\mathbf{x}}_{t-{\Delta t}})\big)}_{\mathrm{(c)}}.

Because
p_{T}=p_{\mathrm{prior}}
,
\tilde{{\mathbf{x}}}_{t}=\Phi_{T\to t}({\mathbf{x}}_{T})
with
{\mathbf{x}}_{T}\sim p_{\mathrm{prior}}
, so

\mathbb{E}\|\mathrm{(a)}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\varepsilon,\qquad\mathbb{E}\|\mathrm{(b)}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\varepsilon.

Now, we control the teacher drift.

\displaystyle\|\mathrm{(c)}\|_{2}

\displaystyle=\|{\mathbf{S}}_{t}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})-{\mathbf{S}}_{t-\Delta t}({\mathbf{x}}_{t-{\Delta t}})\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq{\mathrm{Lip}}({\bm{\Phi}})\|{\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to T}({\mathbf{x}}_{t}))-{\bm{\Psi}}_{t-\Delta t\to T}({\mathbf{x}}_{t-\Delta t}))\|_{2},

\displaystyle\leq{\mathrm{Lip}}({\bm{\Phi}}){\mathrm{Lip}}({\bm{\Psi}})\left(\|{\mathbf{x}}_{t}-{\mathbf{x}}_{t-\Delta t}\|_{2}+\left|\Delta t\right|\right),

so we have
\mathbb{E}\|\mathrm{(c)}\|_{2}^{2}=\mathcal{O}(\Delta t^{2})
.

Combining the above bounds, we conclude:

\mathbb{E}_{t,{\mathbf{x}}_{0},{\bm{\epsilon}}}\big[\|{\mathbf{e}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{CMT}}}\|_{2}^{2}\big]=\mathcal{O}\left(\varepsilon+{\Delta t}^{2p}+{\Delta t}^{2}\right).

Case 2. Diffusion Model:

Bound I: Training–Error Only; No Smoothness.

Write

{\mathbf{e}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{DM}}}=\Big({\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{DM}}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t)-{\mathbf{x}}_{0}\Big)-\Big({\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{DM}}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t-\Delta t},t-\Delta t)-{\mathbf{x}}_{0}\Big).

By
\|u-v\|^{2}\leq 2\|u\|^{2}+2\|v\|^{2}
and taking expectation over
(t,{\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{x}}_{t-\Delta t})
,

\mathbb{E}\big[\|{\mathbf{e}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{DM}}}\|_{2}^{2}\big]\leq 2\mathbb{E}\big[\|{\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{DM}}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t)-{\mathbf{x}}_{0}\|_{2}^{2}\big]+2\mathbb{E}\big[\|{\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{DM}}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t-\Delta t},t-\Delta t)-{\mathbf{x}}_{0}\|_{2}^{2}\big]\leq 4\varepsilon,

where the last inequality uses the same training distribution for
(t,{\mathbf{x}}_{t})
and
(t-\Delta t,{\mathbf{x}}_{t-\Delta t})
(e.g.,
t
uniform on
[\Delta t,1]
). Thus

\mathbb{E}\big[\|{\mathbf{e}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{DM}}}\|_{2}^{2}\big]\;\leq\;4\varepsilon.

Bound II: Lipschitz Smoothness;
\Delta t
–Sensitive.

Assume
{\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{DM}}}
is Lipschitz in state and time:

\|{\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{DM}}}({\mathbf{x}},t)-{\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{DM}}}({\mathbf{y}},s)\|_{2}\leq{\mathrm{Lip}}({\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{DM}}})\left(\|{\mathbf{x}}-{\mathbf{y}}\|_{2}+|t-s|\right).

Then

\displaystyle\|{\mathbf{e}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{DM}}}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|{\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{DM}}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t)-{\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{DM}}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t-\Delta t},t)\|_{2}+\|{\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{CMT}}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t-\Delta t},t)-{\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{CMT}}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t-\Delta t},t-\Delta t)\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq{\mathrm{Lip}}({\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{DM}}})\left(\|{\mathbf{x}}_{t}-{\mathbf{x}}_{t-{\Delta t}}\|_{2}+|{\Delta t}|\right),

hence by
(a+b)^{2}\leq 2a^{2}+2b^{2}
,

\|{\mathbf{e}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{DM}}}\|_{2}^{2}\lesssim{\mathrm{Lip}}^{2}({\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{DM}}})\left(\|{\mathbf{x}}_{t}-{\mathbf{x}}_{t-\Delta t}\|_{2}^{2}+\Delta t^{2}\right).

Taking expectation and using the coupled forward process,

{\mathbf{x}}_{t}-{\mathbf{x}}_{t-\Delta t}=(a_{t}-a_{t-\Delta t}){\mathbf{x}}_{0}+(b_{t}-b_{t-\Delta t}){\bm{\epsilon}},

so with
m_{2}:=\mathbb{E}\|{\mathbf{x}}_{0}\|_{2}^{2}
and
\mathbb{E}\|{\bm{\epsilon}}\|_{2}^{2}=D
(and
\mathbb{E}[{\mathbf{x}}_{0}^{\top}{\bm{\epsilon}}]=0
),

\mathbb{E}\|{\mathbf{x}}_{t}-{\mathbf{x}}_{t-\Delta t}\|_{2}^{2}=\mathcal{O}({\Delta t}^{2}).

Therefore,

\mathbb{E}\big[\|{\mathbf{e}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{DM}}}\|_{2}^{2}\big]=\mathcal{O}({\Delta t}^{2}).

Taking the better of the two regimes yields

\mathbb{E}_{t,{\mathbf{x}}_{0},{\bm{\epsilon}}}\big[\|{\mathbf{e}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{DM}}}\|_{2}^{2}\big]\;\lesssim\;\min\Big\{\varepsilon,\;\Delta t^{2}\Big\}.

(If one averages over
t
, insert
\mathbb{E}_{t}[\cdot]
on the second term’s bracket; if one prefers a uniform-in-
t
bound, replace the bracket by its
\sup_{t}
.)

Case 3. General Consistency Distillation:
With

\displaystyle{\mathbf{e}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{gCD}}}

\displaystyle=\underbrace{[{\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{CMT}}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t)-{\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{CMT}}}({\bm{\Phi}}_{t\to u}({\mathbf{x}}_{t}),u)]}_{A_{t}}-\underbrace{[{\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{CMT}}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t-\Delta t},t-\Delta t)-{\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{CMT}}}({\bm{\Phi}}_{t-\Delta t\to u}({\mathbf{x}}_{t-\Delta t}),u)]}_{B_{t-\Delta t}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\underbrace{[{\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{CMT}}}({\bm{\Phi}}_{t\to u}({\mathbf{x}}_{t}),u)-{\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{CMT}}}({\bm{\Phi}}_{t-\Delta t\to u}({\mathbf{x}}_{t-\Delta t}),u)]}_{C_{t,u}}

by the gCD assumption, we have

\mathbb{E}\|A_{t}\|^{2}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{gCD}}({\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{gCD}};u)<\varepsilon,\qquad\mathbb{E}\|B_{t-\Delta t}\|^{2}\leq\varepsilon,

so that

3\mathbb{E}\|A_{t}\|^{2}+3\mathbb{E}\|B_{t-\Delta t}\|^{2}\;\leq\;6\varepsilon.

For
C_{t,u}
, using the Lipschitz properties,

\displaystyle\|C_{t,u}\|

\displaystyle\leq{\mathrm{Lip}}({\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{gCD}}})\big(\|{\bm{\Phi}}_{t\to u}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})-{\bm{\Phi}}_{t\to u}({\mathbf{x}}_{t-\Delta t})\|+\|{\bm{\Phi}}_{t\to u}({\mathbf{x}}_{t-\Delta t})-{\bm{\Phi}}_{t-\Delta t\to u}({\mathbf{x}}_{t-\Delta t})\|\big)

\displaystyle\leq{\mathrm{Lip}}({\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{gCD}}}){\mathrm{Lip}}({\bm{\Phi}})\big(\|{\mathbf{x}}_{t}-{\mathbf{x}}_{t-\Delta t}\|+\Delta t\big),

hence

\mathbb{E}\|C_{t,u}\|^{2}\;\leq\;2{\mathrm{Lip}}^{2}({\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{gCD}}}){\mathrm{Lip}}^{2}({\bm{\Phi}})\Big(\mathbb{E}\|{\mathbf{x}}_{t}-{\mathbf{x}}_{t-\Delta t}\|^{2}+\Delta t^{2}\Big)\;=\;\mathcal{O}\big(\Delta t^{2}\big).

Combining the pieces,

\mathbb{E}_{t,{\mathbf{x}}_{0},{\bm{\epsilon}}}\big[\|{\mathbf{e}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{gCD}}}\|_{2}^{2}\big]=\mathcal{O}\big(\varepsilon+\Delta t^{2}\big).

Case 4. Random Initialization:
This is a straightforward derivation from the assumption:

\mathbb{E}\left\lVert\mathbf{e}({\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{rand.}})\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\lesssim\mathbb{E}\left\lVert{\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{rand.}}}(\mathbf{x}_{t},t)\right\rVert_{2}^{2}+\mathbb{E}\left\lVert{\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{rand.}}}(\mathbf{x}_{t-\Delta t},t-\Delta t)\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\lesssim 2R.

∎

F.6
Bias–Variance Decomposition

For the squared
gradient bias
and the CM’s flow map gradient variance

\mathcal{B}({\bm{\theta}}):=\big\|\nabla_{{\bm{\theta}}}\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{oracle}}({\bm{\theta}})-\nabla_{{\bm{\theta}}}\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{CM}}({\bm{\theta}})\big\|_{2}^{2}\quad\mathcal{V}({\bm{\theta}}):=\mathrm{Var}_{{\bm{\xi}}}\!\big[\nabla_{{\bm{\theta}}}\ell_{\mathrm{CM}}({\bm{\theta}};{\bm{\xi}})\big].

Consider the oracle–relative mean–squared error (MSE) of a CM gradient:

\mathcal{E}({\bm{\theta}}):=\mathbb{E}_{{\bm{\xi}}}\!\Big[\big\|\nabla_{{\bm{\theta}}}\ell_{\mathrm{CM}}({\bm{\theta}};{\bm{\xi}})-\nabla_{{\bm{\theta}}}\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{oracle}}({\bm{\theta}})\big\|_{2}^{2}\Big].

Then we have the following mean-squared errors comparison under the four different initializations:

Corollary F.3
.

Under the same assumptions as in
Theorem

F.1
, the following CM gradient MSE bounds hold for the four initialization schemes (
{\bm{\theta}}={\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{CMT}},{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{DM}},{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{gCD}},{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{rand}}
).

(i)

CMT
:

\mathcal{E}({\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{CMT}})=\mathcal{O}\left(\varepsilon+\Delta t^{2}+\Delta t^{p}\right).

(ii)

Diffusion Model
:

\mathcal{E}({\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{DM}})=\mathcal{O}\left(\varepsilon+\Delta t^{2}+\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\tfrac{\sigma_{t}^{2}}{\alpha_{t}^{2}}\right]\right)+\mathbb{E}_{{\mathbf{x}}_{t},t}\left[\big\|{\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to 0}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})-\mathbb{E}[{\mathbf{x}}_{0}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t}]\big\|_{2}^{2}\right].

(iii)

General Consistency Distillation
: For a fixed
u\in[0,T]
, assume in addition that

\delta_{u}:=\mathbb{E}_{{\mathbf{x}}_{u}\sim p_{u}}\big\|{\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{gCD}}}({\mathbf{x}}_{u},u)-{\bm{\Psi}}_{u\to 0}({\mathbf{x}}_{u})\big\|^{2}<\infty.

Then

\mathcal{E}({\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{gCD}})=\mathcal{O}\left(\varepsilon+\Delta t^{2}+\delta_{u}\right).

(iv)

Random Initialization
:

\mathcal{E}({\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{rand.}})=\mathcal{O}(1).

Proof.

Write the (vector) bias as

\mathbf{b}({\bm{\theta}}):=\nabla_{{\bm{\theta}}}\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{CM}}({\bm{\theta}})-\nabla_{{\bm{\theta}}}\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{oracle}}({\bm{\theta}}).

Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}({\bm{\theta}})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{{\bm{\xi}}}\!\Big[\left\lVert\nabla_{{\bm{\theta}}}\ell_{\mathrm{CM}}({\bm{\theta}};{\bm{\xi}})-\nabla_{{\bm{\theta}}}\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{CM}}({\bm{\theta}})+\mathbf{b}({\bm{\theta}})\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{{\bm{\xi}}}\!\Big[\big\|\nabla_{{\bm{\theta}}}\ell_{\mathrm{CM}}({\bm{\theta}};{\bm{\xi}})-\nabla_{{\bm{\theta}}}\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{CM}}({\bm{\theta}})\big\|_{2}^{2}\Big]+\underbrace{\|\mathbf{b}({\bm{\theta}})\|_{2}^{2}}_{=\;\mathcal{B}({\bm{\theta}})}+2\mathbb{E}_{{\bm{\xi}}}\!\Big[\big\langle\nabla_{{\bm{\theta}}}\ell_{\mathrm{CM}}({\bm{\theta}};{\bm{\xi}})-\nabla_{{\bm{\theta}}}\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{CM}}({\bm{\theta}}),\mathbf{b}({\bm{\theta}})\big\rangle\Big].

The cross term vanishes because

\mathbb{E}_{{\bm{\xi}}}\!\big[\nabla_{{\bm{\theta}}}\ell_{\mathrm{CM}}({\bm{\theta}};{\bm{\xi}})-\nabla_{{\bm{\theta}}}\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{CM}}({\bm{\theta}})\big]=\mathbf{0}
,
hence

\mathcal{E}({\bm{\theta}})=\underbrace{\Tr\!\Big(\mathrm{Cov}_{{\bm{\xi}}}\big[\nabla_{{\bm{\theta}}}\ell_{\mathrm{CM}}({\bm{\theta}};{\bm{\xi}})\big]\Big)}_{=\;\mathcal{V}({\bm{\theta}})}+\underbrace{\big\|\nabla_{{\bm{\theta}}}\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{oracle}}({\bm{\theta}})-\nabla_{{\bm{\theta}}}\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{CM}}({\bm{\theta}})\big\|_{2}^{2}}_{=\;\mathcal{B}({\bm{\theta}})}.

The remaining steps follow directly by combining the results of
Theorems

F.1
and
F.2
.
∎

F.7
Comparison on Optimization Dynamics

We consider plain SGD on the oracle objective using CM gradients. The iteration is

{\bm{\theta}}_{k+1}={\bm{\theta}}_{k}-\eta\nabla_{{\bm{\theta}}}\ell_{\mathrm{CM}}({\bm{\theta}}_{k};{\bm{\xi}}_{k}),\qquad{\bm{\xi}}_{k}\sim\mathrm{Unif}[0,T]\times p_{t}\ \text{i.i.d.},

with constant stepsize
\eta>0
. The expected oracle loss
\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{oracle}}
is assumed
L
–smooth and to satisfy a Polyak–Łojasiewicz (PL) condition on the level set visited by the iterates:

\|\nabla_{{\bm{\theta}}}\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{oracle}}({\bm{\theta}})-\nabla_{{\bm{\theta}}}\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{oracle}}({\bm{\theta}}^{\prime})\|_{2}\leq L\|{\bm{\theta}}-{\bm{\theta}}^{\prime}\|_{2},\qquad\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla_{{\bm{\theta}}}\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{oracle}}({\bm{\theta}})\|_{2}^{2}\geq\mu\big(\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{oracle}}({\bm{\theta}})-\bar{\ell}^{*}\big)

for some
\mu>0
. Here,

\bar{\ell}^{*}:=\min_{\bm{\theta}}\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{oracle}}({\bm{\theta}})=\min_{\bm{\theta}}\left[\mathbb{E}_{t,{\mathbf{x}}_{t}}\left\lVert{\mathbf{f}}_{\bm{\theta}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t)-{\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to 0}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\right].

We use the bias
\mathcal{B}({\bm{\theta}})
, variance
\mathcal{V}({\bm{\theta}})
, and MSE

\mathcal{E}({\bm{\theta}}):=\mathbb{E}_{{\bm{\xi}}}\!\Big[\big\|\nabla_{{\bm{\theta}}}\ell_{\mathrm{CM}}({\bm{\theta}};{\bm{\xi}})-\nabla_{{\bm{\theta}}}\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{oracle}}({\bm{\theta}})\big\|_{2}^{2}\Big]=\mathcal{B}({\bm{\theta}})+\mathcal{V}({\bm{\theta}})

as established above. We assume the stepsize satisfies
\eta\leq 1/(4L)
.

Theorem F.4

(SGD Analysis with Scheme-Specific Initializations)
.

Assume the conditions of
Theorem

F.1
, and further assume that
\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{oracle}}
is
L
–smooth, that the PL(
\mu
) condition holds, that the stepsize satisfies
\eta\leq 1/(4L)
, and that
\mathcal{E}
is Lipschitz with constant
{\mathrm{Lip}}(\mathcal{E})
on the level set visited by SGD. Let
p\geq 1
be the global order of the ODE solver used in pretraining/teacher flows. For each initialization scheme, define

A_{0}:=\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{oracle}}({\bm{\theta}}_{0})-\bar{\ell}^{*},\qquad M_{0}:=\mathcal{E}({\bm{\theta}}_{0}).

Then, for any
K\geq 1
,

\mathbb{E}\!\big[\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{oracle}}({\bm{\theta}}_{K})-\bar{\ell}^{*}\big]\;\leq\;(1-\mu\eta)^{K}\,A_{0}+\frac{5}{\mu}\,{\mathrm{Lip}}(\mathcal{E})\,\sqrt{\eta K\,A_{0}}+\frac{5}{2\mu}\,M_{0}+\frac{35}{4\mu}\,{\mathrm{Lip}}(\mathcal{E})^{2}\,\eta^{2}K^{2}.

(14)

Let
C(\eta,K):=\frac{35}{4\mu}\,{\mathrm{Lip}}(\mathcal{E})^{2}\,\eta^{2}K^{2}
. Then the initialization lemma (
Lemma

F.4
) and the bias–variance/MSE-at-init bounds assumed in
Theorem

F.1
imply the following scheme-specific orders:

•

CMT :

A_{0}=\mathcal{O}\!\big(\varepsilon+\Delta t^{\,2p}\big),\qquad M_{0}=\mathcal{O}\!\big(\varepsilon+\Delta t^{2}+\Delta t^{p}\big).

Thus,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\!\big[\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{oracle}}({\bm{\theta}}_{K})-\bar{\ell}^{*}\big]

\displaystyle\leq(1-\mu\eta)^{K}\,\mathcal{O}\!\big(\varepsilon+\Delta t^{\,2p}\big)+\sqrt{\eta K}\,\mathcal{O}\!\big((\varepsilon+\Delta t^{\,2p})^{1/2}\big)

\displaystyle+\mathcal{O}\!\big(\varepsilon+\Delta t^{2}+\Delta t^{p}\big)+C(\eta,K).

•

Diffusion Model (DM):
We denote

\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{DM}}:=\mathbb{E}_{t,{\mathbf{x}}_{t}}\big\|{\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to 0}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})-\mathbb{E}[{\mathbf{x}}_{0}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t}]\big\|_{2}^{2},

the deterministic–map versus posterior–mean mismatch. Then

A_{0}=\mathcal{O}\!\big(\varepsilon\big)+\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{DM}},\qquad M_{0}=\mathcal{O}\!\Big(\varepsilon+\Delta t^{2}+\mathbb{E}_{t}\!\big[\tfrac{\sigma_{t}^{2}}{\alpha_{t}^{2}}\big]\Big)\;+\;\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{DM}}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\!\big[\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{oracle}}({\bm{\theta}}_{K})-\bar{\ell}^{*}\big]

\displaystyle\leq(1-\mu\eta)^{K}\,\Big(\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon)+\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{DM}}\Big)+\sqrt{\eta K}\,\Big(\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon)+\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{DM}}\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle+\Big(\mathcal{O}\big(\varepsilon+\Delta t^{2}+\mathbb{E}_{t}[\tfrac{\sigma_{t}^{2}}{\alpha_{t}^{2}}]\big)+\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{DM}}\Big)+C(\eta,K).

•

General Consistency Distillation (gCD):

A_{0}=\mathcal{O}\!\big(\varepsilon+\delta_{u}+\Delta t^{\,2p}\big),\qquad M_{0}=\mathcal{O}\!\big(\varepsilon+\Delta t^{2}+\delta_{u}\big).

Thus,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\!\big[\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{oracle}}({\bm{\theta}}_{K})-\bar{\ell}^{*}\big]

\displaystyle\leq(1-\mu\eta)^{K}\,\mathcal{O}\!\big(\varepsilon+\delta_{u}+\Delta t^{\,2p}\big)+\sqrt{\eta K}\,\mathcal{O}\!\big((\varepsilon+\delta_{u}+\Delta t^{\,2p})^{1/2}\big)

\displaystyle+\mathcal{O}\!\big(\varepsilon+\Delta t^{2}+\delta_{u}\big)+C(\eta,K).

•

Random initialization:

A_{0}=\mathcal{O}\!\big(1),\qquad M_{0}=\mathcal{O}(1).

Thus,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\!\big[\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{oracle}}({\bm{\theta}}_{K})-\bar{\ell}^{*}\big]

\displaystyle\leq(1-\mu\eta)^{K}\,\mathcal{O}\!\big(1)+\sqrt{\eta K}\,\mathcal{O}\!\big(1\big)

\displaystyle+\mathcal{O}(1)+C(\eta,K).

All big-
\mathcal{O}
constants are independent of
\Delta t
,
\varepsilon
, and
K
.

All schemes enjoy the same geometric contraction factor
(1-\mu\eta)^{K}
; differences arise solely through the initialization terms
A_{0}
and
M_{0}
. Among them, CMT achieves

\mathbb{E}\!\big[\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{oracle}}({\bm{\theta}}_{K})-\bar{\ell}^{*}\big]\;\leq\;(1-\mu\eta)^{K}\,\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon+\Delta t^{2p})+\sqrt{\eta K}\,\mathcal{O}\!\big((\varepsilon+\Delta t^{2p})^{1/2}\big)+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon+\Delta t^{2}+\Delta t^{p})+C(\eta,K),

which contains no extra irreducible terms (such as
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{DM}}
or
\delta_{u}
). Consequently, while the asymptotic rate is identical across schemes, CMT attains the smallest excess risk (the tightest bound and lowest floor) for any
K
, up to the common term
C(\eta,K)
.

The bound on
M_{0}
in
Equation

14
for each initialization scheme follows directly from
Theorem

F.1
.
To obtain a complete upper bound needed for the proof of
Theorem

F.4
, however, we also require bounds on
A_{0}
for the four initialization schemes in
Equation

14
.
In
Lemma

F.4
, we establish such bounds for each
A_{0}
.
We then return to finalize the proof of
Theorem

F.4
.

Initialization Excess Oracle Risk.

We bound the initial oracle excess risk

\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{oracle}}({\bm{\theta}}_{0})-\bar{\ell}^{*}

for each of the four initialization schemes. We now state and prove the initialization bounds.

Lemma F.4
.

Under
B
and
C
and following the notations therein, there exist constants
C_{1},C_{2}<\infty
that do not depend on
\Delta t
or
\varepsilon
such that

\displaystyle\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{oracle}}(\bm{{\bm{\theta}}}_{\mathrm{CMT}})-\bar{\ell}^{*}

\displaystyle\leq 2\,\varepsilon+C_{1}\,\Delta t^{\,2p},

\displaystyle\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{oracle}}(\bm{{\bm{\theta}}}_{\mathrm{DM}})-\bar{\ell}^{*}

\displaystyle\leq 2\,\varepsilon+2\,\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{DM}},

\displaystyle\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{oracle}}(\bm{{\bm{\theta}}}_{\mathrm{gCD}})-\bar{\ell}^{*}

\displaystyle\leq 2\,\varepsilon+9\,\delta_{u}+C_{2}\,\Delta t^{\,2p},

\displaystyle\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{oracle}}(\bm{{\bm{\theta}}}_{\mathrm{rand.}})-\bar{\ell}^{*}

\displaystyle\leq 2R+2C_{{\bm{\Psi}}}.

Here any fixed choice suffices since they are absorbed in
C_{2}
in the final rates. In the realizable case
\bar{\ell}^{*}=0
, these are direct bounds on the initialization oracle loss.

Proof.

CMT.

Let
{\mathbf{x}}_{T}\sim p_{\mathrm{prior}}
,
{\mathbf{x}}_{t}:={\bm{\Psi}}_{T\to t}({\mathbf{x}}_{T})
,

\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{t}:=\mathtt{Solver}_{T\to t}({\mathbf{x}}_{T})
,
and
\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{0}:=\mathtt{Solver}_{T\to 0}({\mathbf{x}}_{T})
.
For fixed
t
,

\displaystyle\big\|{\mathbf{f}}_{\bm{{\bm{\theta}}}_{\mathrm{CMT}}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t)-{\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to 0}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})\big\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\underbrace{\big\|{\mathbf{f}}_{\bm{{\bm{\theta}}}_{\mathrm{CMT}}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t)-{\mathbf{f}}_{\bm{{\bm{\theta}}}_{\mathrm{CMT}}}(\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{t},t)\big\|_{2}}_{A_{1}}+\underbrace{\big\|{\mathbf{f}}_{\bm{{\bm{\theta}}}_{\mathrm{CMT}}}(\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{t},t)-\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{0}\big\|_{2}}_{A_{2}}

\displaystyle+\underbrace{\big\|\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{0}-{\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to 0}(\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{t})\big\|_{2}}_{A_{3}}+\underbrace{\big\|{\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to 0}(\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{t})-{\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to 0}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})\big\|_{2}}_{A_{4}}.

By
B
,
A_{1}\leq{\mathrm{Lip}}({\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{CMT}}})\|\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{t}-{\mathbf{x}}_{t}\|_{2}
.
Using the semigroup
{\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to 0}\circ{\bm{\Psi}}_{T\to t}={\bm{\Psi}}_{T\to 0}
and triangle inequality,

A_{3}\leq\|\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{0}-{\bm{\Psi}}_{T\to 0}({\mathbf{x}}_{T})\|_{2}+\|{\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to 0}(\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{t})-{\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to 0}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})\|_{2}
.
By
C
,
A_{4}\leq{\mathrm{Lip}}({\bm{\Psi}})\|\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{t}-{\mathbf{x}}_{t}\|_{2}
and the second term in
A_{3}
is also
\leq{\mathrm{Lip}}({\bm{\Psi}})\|\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{t}-{\mathbf{x}}_{t}\|_{2}
.
Now apply
(a+b)^{2}\leq 2a^{2}+2b^{2}
to split
(A_{1}+A_{2}+A_{3}+A_{4})^{2}
into
2A_{2}^{2}+2(A_{1}+A_{3}+A_{4})^{2}
, then again
(u+v+w)^{2}\leq 3(u^{2}+v^{2}+w^{2})
on the second group to obtain

\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{oracle}}(\bm{{\bm{\theta}}}_{\mathrm{CMT}})\leq 2\,\mathbb{E}_{t,{\mathbf{x}}_{T}}\!\Big[\underbrace{\big\|{\mathbf{f}}_{\bm{{\bm{\theta}}}_{\mathrm{CMT}}}(\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{t},t)-\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{0}\big\|_{2}^{2}}_{\leq\ \varepsilon}+C^{\prime}\,\|\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{t}-{\mathbf{x}}_{t}\|_{2}^{2}+\big\|\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{0}-{\bm{\Psi}}_{T\to 0}({\mathbf{x}}_{T})\big\|_{2}^{2}\Big],

with
C^{\prime}:=3({\mathrm{Lip}}^{2}({\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{CMT}}})+2{\mathrm{Lip}}^{2}({\bm{\Psi}}))
.
Since the solver is of order
p
, the last two expectations are
O(\Delta t^{\,2p})
. Therefore

\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{oracle}}(\bm{{\bm{\theta}}}_{\mathrm{CMT}})\lesssim 2\,\varepsilon+2\,(C^{\prime}+1)\,\Delta t^{\,2p}.

Absorbing constants into
C_{1}
and subtracting
\bar{\ell}^{*}
gives the claim.

Diffusion Model (DM).

By the tower property, for any
{\mathbf{h}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})
,

\mathbb{E}\|{\mathbf{x}}_{0}-{\mathbf{h}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})\|_{2}^{2}=\mathbb{E}\|{\mathbf{x}}_{0}-\mathbb{E}[{\mathbf{x}}_{0}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t}]\|_{2}^{2}+\mathbb{E}\|{\mathbf{h}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})-\mathbb{E}[{\mathbf{x}}_{0}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t}]\|_{2}^{2}.

With
{\mathbf{h}}={\mathbf{D}}_{\bm{{\bm{\theta}}}_{\mathrm{DM}}}
and
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DM}}(\bm{{\bm{\theta}}}_{\mathrm{DM}})<\varepsilon
,

\mathbb{E}\|{\mathbf{D}}_{\bm{{\bm{\theta}}}_{\mathrm{DM}}}-\mathbb{E}[{\mathbf{x}}_{0}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t}]\|_{2}^{2}\leq\varepsilon
.
Thus by
\|a-b\|^{2}\leq 2\|a-c\|^{2}+2\|c-b\|^{2}
with
c=\mathbb{E}[{\mathbf{x}}_{0}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t}]
,

\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{oracle}}(\bm{{\bm{\theta}}}_{\mathrm{DM}})=\mathbb{E}\big\|{\mathbf{D}}_{\bm{{\bm{\theta}}}_{\mathrm{DM}}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t)-{\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to 0}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})\big\|_{2}^{2}\leq 2\,\varepsilon+2\,\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{DM}},

and subtracting
\bar{\ell}^{*}
yields the stated bound.

General Consistency Distillation (gCD).

Fix
u\in[0,T]
. Let
\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{u}:=\mathtt{Solver}_{t\to u}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})
and
{\mathbf{x}}_{u}:={\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to u}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})
.
Define
{\mathbf{F}}_{u}({\mathbf{z}}):={\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{gCD}}}({\mathbf{z}},u)-{\bm{\Psi}}_{u\to 0}({\mathbf{z}})
,
which is
({\mathrm{Lip}}({\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{gCD}}})+{\mathrm{Lip}}({\bm{\Psi}}))
–Lipschitz by
C
and
B
.
Then for fixed
t
,

\displaystyle\big\|{\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{gCD}}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t)-{\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to 0}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})\big\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\underbrace{\big\|{\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{gCD}}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t)-{\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{gCD}}}(\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{u},u)\big\|_{2}}_{B_{1}}+\underbrace{\big\|{\mathbf{F}}_{u}(\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{u})\big\|_{2}}_{B_{2}}

\displaystyle+\underbrace{\big\|{\bm{\Psi}}_{u\to 0}(\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{u})-{\bm{\Psi}}_{u\to 0}({\mathbf{x}}_{u})\big\|_{2}}_{B_{3}},

using the semigroup
{\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to 0}={\bm{\Psi}}_{u\to 0}\circ{\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to u}
.
By
(a+b)^{2}\leq 2a^{2}+2b^{2}
and then
(b+c)^{2}\leq 2b^{2}+2c^{2}
,

\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{oracle}}(\bm{{\bm{\theta}}}_{\mathrm{gCD}})\leq 2\,\mathbb{E}B_{1}^{2}+4\,\mathbb{E}B_{2}^{2}+4\,\mathbb{E}B_{3}^{2}.

The first term is controlled by the training loss:
\mathbb{E}B_{1}^{2}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{gCD}}({\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{gCD}};u)<\varepsilon
.
For
B_{2}
, by
\|a+b\|^{2}\leq(1+\rho)\|a\|^{2}+(1+1/\rho)\|b\|^{2}
with
a={\mathbf{F}}_{u}({\mathbf{x}}_{u})
,
b={\mathbf{F}}_{u}(\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{u})-{\mathbf{F}}_{u}({\mathbf{x}}_{u})
,

\mathbb{E}B_{2}^{2}\leq(1+\rho)\,\mathbb{E}\|F_{u}({\mathbf{x}}_{u})\|_{2}^{2}+(1+1/\rho)\,({\mathrm{Lip}}({\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{gCD}}})+{\mathrm{Lip}}({\bm{\Psi}}))^{2}\,\mathbb{E}\|\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{u}-{\mathbf{x}}_{u}\|_{2}^{2}.

Choosing, e.g.,
\rho=1
and recalling
\delta_{u}:=\mathbb{E}_{{\mathbf{x}}_{u}\sim p_{u}}\|{\mathbf{F}}_{u}({\mathbf{x}}_{u})\|_{2}^{2}
,
p
-th order solver (see
C
) gives

\mathbb{E}B_{2}^{2}\lesssim 2\,\delta_{u}+2\,({\mathrm{Lip}}({\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{gCD}}})+{\mathrm{Lip}}({\bm{\Psi}}))^{2}\,\Delta t^{\,2p}.

For
B_{3}
, by
C
and
C
,

\mathbb{E}B_{3}^{2}\lesssim{\mathrm{Lip}}^{2}({\bm{\Psi}})\,\Delta t^{\,2p}
.
Combining,

\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{oracle}}(\bm{{\bm{\theta}}}_{\mathrm{gCD}})\lesssim 2\,\varepsilon+8\,\delta_{u}+\underbrace{4\Big(2({\mathrm{Lip}}({\mathbf{f}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{\mathrm{gCD}}})+{\mathrm{Lip}}^{2}({\bm{\Psi}}))+{\mathrm{Lip}}^{2}({\bm{\Psi}})\Big)}_{C_{2}}\,\Delta t^{\,2p}.

Random Initialization.

By
\|a-b\|^{2}\leq 2\|a\|^{2}+2\|b\|^{2}
and
C
,

\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{oracle}}(\bm{{\bm{\theta}}}_{\mathrm{rand.}})=\mathbb{E}\big\|{\mathbf{f}}_{\bm{{\bm{\theta}}}_{\mathrm{rand.}}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t)-{\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to 0}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})\big\|_{2}^{2}\leq 2\,\mathbb{E}\|{\mathbf{f}}_{\bm{{\bm{\theta}}}_{\mathrm{rand.}}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t)\|_{2}^{2}+2\,\mathbb{E}\|{\bm{\Psi}}_{t\to 0}({\mathbf{x}}_{t})\|_{2}^{2}\leq 2R+2C_{{\bm{\Psi}}}.

Subtracting
\bar{\ell}^{*}
in each case yields the claims.
∎

Proof of
Theorem

F.4
.

Proof.

Linear Contraction to an MSE floor.

By the descent lemma for
L
–smooth
\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{oracle}}
,

\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{oracle}}({\bm{\theta}}_{k+1})\leq\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{oracle}}({\bm{\theta}}_{k})-\eta\big\langle\nabla\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{oracle}}({\bm{\theta}}_{k}),\nabla_{{\bm{\theta}}}\ell_{\mathrm{CM}}({\bm{\theta}}_{k};{\bm{\xi}}_{k})\big\rangle+\frac{L\eta^{2}}{2}\big\|\nabla_{{\bm{\theta}}}\ell_{\mathrm{CM}}({\bm{\theta}}_{k};{\bm{\xi}}_{k})\big\|_{2}^{2}.

Taking conditional expectation w.r.t.
{\bm{\xi}}_{k}
, using

\mathbb{E}_{{\bm{\xi}}_{k}}\nabla_{{\bm{\theta}}}\ell_{\mathrm{CM}}({\bm{\theta}}_{k};{\bm{\xi}}_{k})=\nabla_{{\bm{\theta}}}\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{CM}}({\bm{\theta}}_{k})
and

\mathbb{E}_{{\bm{\xi}}_{k}}\|\nabla_{{\bm{\theta}}}\ell_{\mathrm{CM}}({\bm{\theta}}_{k};{\bm{\xi}}_{k})\|_{2}^{2}=\|\nabla_{{\bm{\theta}}}\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{CM}}({\bm{\theta}}_{k})\|_{2}^{2}+\Tr\mathrm{Cov}_{{\bm{\xi}}_{k}}[\nabla_{{\bm{\theta}}}\ell_{\mathrm{CM}}({\bm{\theta}}_{k};{\bm{\xi}}_{k})]
,
together with

\big\langle\nabla\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{oracle}},\nabla\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{CM}}\big\rangle\geq\|\nabla\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{oracle}}\|_{2}^{2}-\|\nabla\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{oracle}}\|_{2}\big\|\nabla\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{CM}}-\nabla\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{oracle}}\big\|_{2},

and Young’s inequality, we obtain

\mathbb{E}\big[\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{oracle}}({\bm{\theta}}_{k+1})\mid{\bm{\theta}}_{k}\big]\leq\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{oracle}}({\bm{\theta}}_{k})-\frac{\eta}{2}\|\nabla\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{oracle}}({\bm{\theta}}_{k})\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{5}{4}\eta\mathcal{E}({\bm{\theta}}_{k}),

where we also used
\eta\leq 1/(4L)
to absorb the
L\eta^{2}
terms into the constants.

Applying the PL inequality to eliminate the gradient norm yields

\mathbb{E}\big[\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{oracle}}({\bm{\theta}}_{k+1})-\bar{\ell}^{*}\mid{\bm{\theta}}_{k}\big]\leq\big(1-\mu\eta\big)\big(\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{oracle}}({\bm{\theta}}_{k})-\bar{\ell}^{*}\big)+\frac{5}{4}\eta\mathcal{E}({\bm{\theta}}_{k}).

Taking total expectation and unrolling the recursion gives, for any
K\geq 1
,

\mathbb{E}\big[\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{oracle}}({\bm{\theta}}_{K})-\bar{\ell}^{*}\big]\leq(1-\mu\eta)^{K}\big(\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{oracle}}({\bm{\theta}}_{0})-\bar{\ell}^{*}\big)+\frac{5}{4}\eta\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}(1-\mu\eta)^{K-1-k}\mathbb{E}\big[\mathcal{E}({\bm{\theta}}_{k})\big].

(15)

The bounds in
Corollary

F.3
provide
\mathcal{E}({\bm{\theta}})
only at initialization for each scenario. Thus, we may need more additional assumptions.

First, a
localized MSE stability
assumption: there exists a neighborhood
\mathcal{N}
of the initialization in which the same order bound holds for
\mathcal{E}({\bm{\theta}})
, and the SGD trajectory remains in
\mathcal{N}
under
\eta\leq 1/(4L)
. Then
\sup_{0\leq k\leq K-1}\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{E}({\bm{\theta}}_{k})]\leq\overline{\mathcal{E}}
with
\overline{\mathcal{E}}
of the same order as the initialization, which recovers the floor bound

\mathbb{E}\big[\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{oracle}}({\bm{\theta}}_{K})-\bar{\ell}^{*}\big]\leq(1-\mu\eta)^{K}\big(\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{oracle}}({\bm{\theta}}_{0})-\bar{\ell}^{*}\big)+\frac{5}{4}\frac{\overline{\mathcal{E}}}{\mu}.

In this case, similar bounds can be obtained by applying
Lemma

F.4
to different initialization schemes of
\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{oracle}}({\bm{\theta}}_{0})-\bar{\ell}^{*}
.

Second, a
mild continuity control
: suppose
\mathcal{E}
is Lipschitz on the level set visited by the iterates, i.e.,

\big|\mathcal{E}({\bm{\theta}})-\mathcal{E}({\bm{\theta}}^{\prime})\big|\leq{\mathrm{Lip}}(\mathcal{E})\|{\bm{\theta}}-{\bm{\theta}}^{\prime}\|_{2}.

If, in addition, the step size ensures a bounded path length
\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\mathbb{E}\|{\bm{\theta}}_{k+1}-{\bm{\theta}}_{k}\|_{2}\leq R
(which follows from
\mathbb{E}\|{\bm{\theta}}_{k+1}-{\bm{\theta}}_{k}\|_{2}=\eta\mathbb{E}\|\nabla_{{\bm{\theta}}}\ell_{\mathrm{CM}}({\bm{\theta}}_{k};{\bm{\xi}}_{k})\|_{2}
and the same descent argument that bounds the average oracle gradient norm), then

\sup_{0\leq k\leq K-1}\mathbb{E}\big[\mathcal{E}({\bm{\theta}}_{k})\big]\leq\mathcal{E}({\bm{\theta}}_{0})+{\mathrm{Lip}}(\mathcal{E})R.

Inserting this into
Equation

15
gives a data–dependent version in terms of the initialization MSE plus a controllable growth term.

Proof of the Mild Continuity Control.

Assume that
\mathcal{E}
is Lipschitz on the level set visited by
\{{\bm{\theta}}_{k}\}
:

\big|\mathcal{E}({\bm{\theta}})-\mathcal{E}({\bm{\theta}}^{\prime})\big|\leq{\mathrm{Lip}}(\mathcal{E})\|{\bm{\theta}}-{\bm{\theta}}^{\prime}\|_{2}.

Fix a horizon
K\geq 1
. By a telescoping argument and Jensen’s inequality,

\sup_{0\leq k\leq K-1}\mathbb{E}\big[\mathcal{E}({\bm{\theta}}_{k})\big]\leq\mathcal{E}({\bm{\theta}}_{0})+{\mathrm{Lip}}(\mathcal{E})\mathbb{E}\!\Big[\sum_{j=0}^{K-1}\|{\bm{\theta}}_{j+1}-{\bm{\theta}}_{j}\|_{2}\Big]\leq\mathcal{E}({\bm{\theta}}_{0})+{\mathrm{Lip}}(\mathcal{E})\sum_{j=0}^{K-1}\mathbb{E}\big[\|{\bm{\theta}}_{j+1}-{\bm{\theta}}_{j}\|_{2}\big].

Since
{\bm{\theta}}_{j+1}-{\bm{\theta}}_{j}=-\eta\nabla_{{\bm{\theta}}}\ell_{\mathrm{CM}}({\bm{\theta}}_{j};{\bm{\xi}}_{j})
,

\mathbb{E}\big[\|{\bm{\theta}}_{j+1}-{\bm{\theta}}_{j}\|_{2}\big]=\eta\mathbb{E}\big[\|\nabla_{{\bm{\theta}}}\ell_{\mathrm{CM}}({\bm{\theta}}_{j};{\bm{\xi}}_{j})\|_{2}\big]\leq\eta\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\big[\|\nabla_{{\bm{\theta}}}\ell_{\mathrm{CM}}({\bm{\theta}}_{j};{\bm{\xi}}_{j})\|_{2}^{2}\big]}.

Using
\mathbb{E}\|\nabla_{{\bm{\theta}}}\ell_{\mathrm{CM}}\|_{2}^{2}=\|\nabla_{{\bm{\theta}}}\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{CM}}\|_{2}^{2}+\Tr\Sigma({\bm{\theta}}_{j})
and

\|\nabla_{{\bm{\theta}}}\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{CM}}\|_{2}^{2}\leq 2\|\nabla_{{\bm{\theta}}}\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{oracle}}\|_{2}^{2}+2\|\nabla_{{\bm{\theta}}}\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{CM}}-\nabla_{{\bm{\theta}}}\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{oracle}}\|_{2}^{2},

we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\|\nabla_{{\bm{\theta}}}\ell_{\mathrm{CM}}({\bm{\theta}}_{j};{\bm{\xi}}_{j})\|_{2}^{2}\big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\mathbb{E}\big[\|\nabla_{{\bm{\theta}}}\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{oracle}}({\bm{\theta}}_{j})\|_{2}^{2}\big]+2\mathbb{E}\big[\|\nabla_{{\bm{\theta}}}\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{CM}}({\bm{\theta}}_{j})-\nabla_{{\bm{\theta}}}\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{oracle}}({\bm{\theta}}_{j})\|_{2}^{2}\big]+\mathbb{E}\big[\Tr\Sigma({\bm{\theta}}_{j})\big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\mathbb{E}\big[\|\nabla_{{\bm{\theta}}}\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{oracle}}({\bm{\theta}}_{j})\|_{2}^{2}\big]+2\mathbb{E}\big[\mathcal{E}({\bm{\theta}}_{j})\big].

Therefore, by Cauchy–Schwarz,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{K-1}\mathbb{E}\big[\|{\bm{\theta}}_{j+1}-{\bm{\theta}}_{j}\|_{2}\big]

\displaystyle\leq\eta\sum_{j=0}^{K-1}\sqrt{2\mathbb{E}\big[\|\nabla_{{\bm{\theta}}}\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{oracle}}({\bm{\theta}}_{j})\|_{2}^{2}\big]+2\mathbb{E}\big[\mathcal{E}({\bm{\theta}}_{j})\big]}

\displaystyle\leq\eta\sqrt{K}\Bigg(\sum_{j=0}^{K-1}\Big(2\mathbb{E}\big[\|\nabla_{{\bm{\theta}}}\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{oracle}}({\bm{\theta}}_{j})\|_{2}^{2}\big]+2\mathbb{E}\big[\mathcal{E}({\bm{\theta}}_{j})\big]\Big)\Bigg)^{1/2}.

(16)

Summing the one–step decrease inequality

\mathbb{E}\big[\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{oracle}}({\bm{\theta}}_{j})\big]-\mathbb{E}\big[\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{oracle}}({\bm{\theta}}_{j+1})\big]\geq\frac{\eta}{2}\mathbb{E}\big[\|\nabla_{{\bm{\theta}}}\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{oracle}}({\bm{\theta}}_{j})\|_{2}^{2}\big]-\frac{5}{4}\eta\mathbb{E}\big[\mathcal{E}({\bm{\theta}}_{j})\big]

from
j=0
to
K-1
and using
\mathbb{E}[\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{oracle}}({\bm{\theta}}_{K})]\geq\bar{\ell}^{*}
yields

\sum_{j=0}^{K-1}\mathbb{E}\big[\|\nabla_{{\bm{\theta}}}\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{oracle}}({\bm{\theta}}_{j})\|_{2}^{2}\big]\leq\frac{2}{\eta}\big(\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{oracle}}({\bm{\theta}}_{0})-\bar{\ell}^{*}\big)+\frac{5}{2}\sum_{j=0}^{K-1}\mathbb{E}\big[\mathcal{E}({\bm{\theta}}_{j})\big].

Let
\overline{\mathcal{E}}_{K}:=\sup_{0\leq j\leq K-1}\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{E}({\bm{\theta}}_{j})]
. Then

\sum_{j=0}^{K-1}\mathbb{E}\big[\|\nabla_{{\bm{\theta}}}\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{oracle}}({\bm{\theta}}_{j})\|_{2}^{2}\big]\leq\frac{2}{\eta}\big(\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{oracle}}({\bm{\theta}}_{0})-\bar{\ell}^{*}\big)+\frac{5}{2}K\overline{\mathcal{E}}_{K}.

Substituting this and
\sum_{j}\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{E}({\bm{\theta}}_{j})]\leq K\overline{\mathcal{E}}_{K}
into
Equation

16
gives the path-length bound

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \sum_{j=0}^{K-1}\mathbb{E}\big[\|{\bm{\theta}}_{j+1}-{\bm{\theta}}_{j}\|_{2}\big]&\leq\eta\sqrt{K}\Big(\tfrac{4}{\eta}\big(\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{oracle}}({\bm{\theta}}_{0})-\bar{\ell}^{*}\big)+7K\overline{\mathcal{E}}_{K}\Big)^{1/2}\\
&\leq 2\sqrt{\eta K\big(\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{oracle}}({\bm{\theta}}_{0})-\bar{\ell}^{*}\big)}+\sqrt{7}\eta K\overline{\mathcal{E}}_{K}^{1/2},\end{aligned}

(17)

where the last inequality uses
\sqrt{a+b}\leq\sqrt{a}+\sqrt{b}
.

Combining Lipschitzness of
\mathcal{E}
and
Equation

17
yields

\overline{\mathcal{E}}_{K}\leq\mathcal{E}({\bm{\theta}}_{0})+{\mathrm{Lip}}(\mathcal{E})\Big(2\sqrt{\eta K\big(\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{oracle}}({\bm{\theta}}_{0})-\bar{\ell}^{*}\big)}+\sqrt{7}\eta K\overline{\mathcal{E}}_{K}^{1/2}\Big).

This is of the form
s\leq u+v\sqrt{s}
with
s=\overline{\mathcal{E}}_{K}
,
u=\mathcal{E}({\bm{\theta}}_{0})+2{\mathrm{Lip}}(\mathcal{E})\sqrt{\eta K(\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{oracle}}({\bm{\theta}}_{0})-\bar{\ell}^{*})}
, and
v=\sqrt{7}{\mathrm{Lip}}(\mathcal{E})\eta K
. The inequality
s\leq u+v\sqrt{s}
implies
s\leq 2u+v^{2}
(complete-the-square argument). Hence

\overline{\mathcal{E}}_{K}\leq 2\mathcal{E}({\bm{\theta}}_{0})+4{\mathrm{Lip}}(\mathcal{E})\sqrt{\eta K\big(\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{oracle}}({\bm{\theta}}_{0})-\bar{\ell}^{*}\big)}+7{\mathrm{Lip}}(\mathcal{E})^{2}\eta^{2}K^{2}.

(18)

Plugging
Equation

18
into the pathwise contraction yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{oracle}}({\bm{\theta}}_{K})-\bar{\ell}^{*}\big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1-\mu\eta)^{K}\big(\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{oracle}}({\bm{\theta}}_{0})-\bar{\ell}^{*}\big)+\frac{5}{4\mu}\Big(2\mathcal{E}({\bm{\theta}}_{0})+4{\mathrm{Lip}}(\mathcal{E})\sqrt{\eta K\big(\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{oracle}}({\bm{\theta}}_{0})-\bar{\ell}^{*}\big)}+7{\mathrm{Lip}}(\mathcal{E})^{2}\eta^{2}K^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-\mu\eta)^{K}\big(\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{oracle}}({\bm{\theta}}_{0})-\bar{\ell}^{*}\big)+\frac{5}{\mu}{\mathrm{Lip}}(\mathcal{E})\sqrt{\eta K\big(\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{oracle}}({\bm{\theta}}_{0})-\bar{\ell}^{*}\big)}+\frac{5}{2\mu}\mathcal{E}({\bm{\theta}}_{0})+\frac{35}{4\mu}{\mathrm{Lip}}(\mathcal{E})^{2}\eta^{2}K^{2}.

This is a data–dependent bound in terms of the initialization MSE
\mathcal{E}({\bm{\theta}}_{0})
, the loss gap
\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{oracle}}({\bm{\theta}}_{0})-\bar{\ell}^{*}
, and the Lipschitz constant
{\mathrm{Lip}}(\mathcal{E})
.

∎

A Note on the Stability of Self-Similar Blow-Up Solutions for Superconformal Semilinear Wave Equations
Jie Liu

Abstract
. In this note, we investigate the stability of self-similar blow-up solutions for superconformal semilinear wave equations in all dimensions. A central aspect of our analysis is the spectral equivalence of the linearized operators under Lorentz transformations in self-similar variables. This observation serves as a useful tool in proving mode stability and provides insights that may aid the study of self-similar solutions in related problems. As a direct consequence, we establish the asymptotic stability of the ODE blow-up family, extending the classical results of Merle and Zaag [Merle-Zaag, 2007, 2016] to the superconformal case and generalizing the recent findings of Ostermann [Ostermann, 2024] to include the entire ODE blow-up family.



1.
Introduction

Consider the semilinear wave equation:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{tt}u-\Delta u=|u|^{p-1}u,\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\quad\partial_{t}u(0,x)=u_{1}(x),\end{array}\right.

posed in space dimension
N\geq 1
, where the unknown function
u:[0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
, and the initial data satisfy
(u_{0},u_{1})\in H_{loc}^{k}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)\times H_{loc}^{k-1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right),k>\frac{N}{2}
. The exponent
p>1
governs the strength of the nonlinearity. In particular, we focus on the superconformal case, i.e.,

\displaystyle p>p_{c}\equiv 1+\frac{4}{N-1},\quad N\geq 2.

Here,
p_{c}
is the conformal critical exponent. For the local well-posedness theory of the Cauchy problem (
1
), we refer to the classical results of Lindblad and Sogge
[
35
]
and Tao
[
48
]
. The existence of blow-up solutions follows from standard ODE techniques or the energy-based blow-up criterion of Levine
[
34
]
.

1.1.
Background

The study of blow-up solutions for nonlinear wave equations has attracted significant attention over the past two decades. For
Section

1
, two fundamental types of blow-up behavior have been identified:

•

Type-I blow-up
, where the solution diverges at a finite time
T
at a rate comparable to that of the self-similar ODE solution.

•

Type-II blow-up
, where the solution remains bounded in a suitable critical norm, allowing for more complex blow-up dynamics.

In the one-dimensional setting (
N=1
), the blow-up theory has been comprehensively developed through a series of works by Merle and Zaag
[
38
,
40
,
41
,
43
,
44
]
, as well as Côte and Zaag
[
9
]
. Their results address:

-

Blow-up rate
[
38
]
,

-

Blow-up profiles
[
40
]
,

-

Regularity and slope of the blow-up curve
[
41
]
,

-

Existence of non-characteristic points
[
43
,
44
]
,

-

Construction of multi-soliton blow-up near non-characteristic points
[
9
]
.

A key feature of the one-dimensional case is that all self-similar solutions are obtained as Lorentz transforms of the fundamental ODE blow-up solution, which satisfies

(1.3)

\displaystyle u_{T}^{*}(t)=\frac{\kappa_{0}}{(T-t)^{\frac{2}{p-1}}},\quad\kappa_{0}=\left(\frac{2(p+1)}{(p-1)^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}.

In higher dimensions (
N\geq 2
), the one-dimensional analogous self-similar solutions persist. More precisely, by exploiting the translation, time shift, and Lorentz invariance of
Section

1
, one can construct a family of self-similar Type-I blow-up solutions depending on
2N+2
parameters
(T,x_{0},d)
, given explicitly by

\displaystyle u_{T,x_{0},d}^{*}(t,x)=\kappa_{0}\frac{\left(1-|d|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}}{(T-t+d\cdot(x-x_{0}))^{\frac{2}{p-1}}},\quad T>0,\quad x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\quad|d|<1.

These solutions are well defined within the backward light cone

\Omega^{1,N}(T,x_{0})=\left\{(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{1,N}\mid|x-x_{0}|<T-t\right\}.

However, the program of dimension 1 breaks down. In contrast to the one-dimensional case, the classification of self-similar solutions in higher dimensions remains incomplete, except in the radial case outside the origin
[
42
]
. Indeed, it has been shown that there exist other smooth, radially symmetric self-similar solutions to
Section

1
, as demonstrated by P. Bizoń et al.
[
1
,
2
]
in three dimensions for odd powers, and by W. Dai and T. Duyckaerts
[

]
for

and energy-supercritical powers
1+\frac{4}{N-2}<p<1+\frac{4}{N-3}
, see also the recent works of Csobo, Glogić, and Schörkhuber
[
10
]
; Chen, Donninger, Glogić, McNulty, and Schörkhuber
[
3
]
; and Kim
[

]
.

Nevertheless, partial results on blow-up theory are available. For subconformal nonlinearities (

), the blow-up rate has been established

\displaystyle\mathscr{R}_{0}=\left\{x_{0}\in\mathscr{R}\,|\,\exists\,|d(x_{0})|<1,\exists\;e(x_{0})=\pm 1\text{ s.t. }\begin{pmatrix}U_{x_{0}}(s)\\
\partial_{s}U_{x_{0}}(s)\end{pmatrix}\rightarrow e(x_{0})\begin{pmatrix}\kappa(d(x_{0}))\\
0\end{pmatrix}\text{ in }\mathscr{H}\text{ as }s\rightarrow\infty\right\}

is open, where
\mathscr{R}

]

For superconformal exponents (

For energy-critical and supercritical nonlinear wave equations, another type of blow-up solution, known as

Despite these advances, the stability of the full family of self-similar solutions (including those obtained via Lorentz transformations) remains an open problem. Notably, the stability of the fundamental ODE blow-up solution does not directly imply the stability of its Lorentz-boosted counterparts, see our discussion in Section

1.2.
Main result

In this paper, we bridge this gap by establishing the spectral stability of the full family of self-similar solutions obtained via Lorentz transformations in the superconformal regime. Our approach leverages the Lorentz transformation in self-similar variables, extending the stability results of Merle and Zaag
[
46
]
to the superconformal setting. More precisely, we aim to prove the stability of general self-similar solutions
u_{T,x_{0},d}^{*}
for all
|d|<1
.

To state our results, we introduce the following similarity variables, for any
T>0
and
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
:

\displaystyle u(t,x)=\frac{1}{\left(T-t\right)^{\frac{2}{p-1}}}U_{T,x_{0}}(s,y),\quad s=-\log\left(T-t\right)+\log T,\quad y=\frac{x-x_{0}}{T-t}.

The function
U_{T,x_{0}}
(we write
U
for simplicity) satisfies the following equation:

(1.4)

\displaystyle\partial_{ss}U+\frac{p+3}{p-1}\partial_{s}U+2y\cdot\nabla\partial_{s}U+\frac{2(p+1)}{p-1}y\cdot\nabla U+(y_{i}y_{j}-\delta_{ij})\partial_{i}\partial_{j}U+\frac{2(p+1)}{(p-1)^{2}}U=|U|^{p-1}U

for all
|y|<1
and
s\geq 0
. Stationary solutions to
Eq.

1.4
are called self-similar solutions. For all
d\in\mathbb{B}^{N}
, the corresponding self-similar profiles of
u^{*}_{T,x_{0},d}
are given by

\displaystyle U^{*}_{T,x_{0},d}(s,y):=\left(T-t\right)^{\frac{2}{p-1}}u^{*}_{T,x_{0},d}(t,x)\equiv\kappa_{d}(y),

where

\displaystyle\kappa_{d}(y)=\kappa_{0}\frac{\left(1-|d|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}}{(1+d\cdot y)^{\frac{2}{p-1}}},\quad\kappa_{0}=\left(\frac{2(p+1)}{(p-1)^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}},\quad\ |y|\leq 1.

In particular, when
d=0
, we obtain the ODE blow-up profile

\displaystyle\kappa(0,y)\equiv\kappa_{0}.

The main theorem is as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
d_{0}\in\mathbb{B}^{N}
. Let
(N,p,k,R)\in\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{R}_{>1}\times\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 1}
with
k>\frac{N}{2}
and
R<|d_{0}|^{-1}
. Put

\omega_{p}=\min\{1,s_{p}\}\qquad\text{where}\qquad s_{p}=\frac{2}{p-1}\,.

For all
0<\varepsilon<\omega_{p}
there are constants
0<\delta_{\varepsilon}<1-|d_{0}|
and
C_{\varepsilon}>1
such that for all
0<\delta\leq\delta_{\varepsilon}
,
C\geq C_{\varepsilon}
and all real-valued
(f,g)\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\times C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with

\|(f,g)\|_{H^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\times H^{k-1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq\frac{\delta}{C}

there exist parameters
d^{*}\in\overline{\mathbb{B}^{N}_{\delta}(d_{0})}\subset\mathbb{B}^{N}
and
T^{*}\in\overline{\mathbb{B}^{1}_{\delta}(1)}
and a unique solution
u\in C^{\infty}\big(\Omega^{1,N}_{R}(T^{*})\big)
in the past light cone

\Omega^{1,N}_{R}(T^{*})=\left\{(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{1,N}\mid 0\leq t<T^{*},\,|x|\leq R(T^{*}-t)\right\}

to the Cauchy problem

(1.5)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcll}\partial_{tt}u(t,x)-\Delta u(t,x)&=&|u(t,x)|^{p-1}u(t,x)\,,&\quad(t,x)\in\Omega_{R}^{1,N}(T^{*})\,,\\
u(0,x)&=&u_{1,0,d_{0}}^{*}(0,x)+f(x)\,,&\quad x\in\mathbb{B}^{N}_{RT^{*}}\,,\\
(\partial_{t}u)(0,x)&=&(\partial_{t}u_{1,0,d_{0}}^{*})(0,x)+g(x)\,,&\quad x\in\mathbb{B}^{N}_{RT^{*}}\,,\end{array}\right.

such that the bounds

\displaystyle(T^{*}-t)^{-\frac{N}{2}+s_{p}+s}\left\|u(t,\,.\,)-u_{T^{*},0,d^{*}}^{*}(t,\,.\,)\right\|_{\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{B}^{N}_{R(T^{*}-t)})}

\displaystyle\lesssim(T^{*}-t)^{\omega_{p}-\varepsilon}

for
s=0,1,\ldots,k
, and

\displaystyle(T^{*}-t)^{-\frac{N}{2}+s_{p}+s}\left\|\partial_{t}u(t,\,.\,)-\partial_{t}u_{T^{*},0,d^{*}}^{*}(t,\,.\,)\right\|_{\dot{H}^{s-1}(\mathbb{B}^{N}_{R(T^{*}-t)})}

\displaystyle\lesssim(T^{*}-t)^{\omega_{p}-\varepsilon}

for
s=1,\ldots,k
, hold for all
0\leq t<T^{*}
.

Remark 1.2
.

Due to the invariance under space and time translations,
Theorem

1.1
also applies to the general solution
u_{T_{0},x_{0},d_{0}}^{*}
for any
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
and
T_{0}>0
. Without loss of generality, we henceforth fix
x_{0}=0
and
T_{0}=1
.

As a corollary, we obtain the following dynamics of equation (
1.4
) for initial data near
\kappa_{d}(y)
in self-similar variables, which extends the main result of
[
46
]
to the superconformal case.

Theorem 1.3

(Behavior of solutions of equation (
1.4
) near
\kappa(d,y)
)
.

Let
d_{0}\in\mathbb{B}^{N}
. Let
(N,p,k)\in\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{R}_{>1}\times\mathbb{N}
with
k>\frac{N}{2}
.
There exist
\delta>0,C>0
and
\varepsilon>0
such that for any smooth solution
U
of equation (
1.4
) satisfying that
(U,\partial_{s}U)
are continuous in time with values in
{H}^{k}(\mathbb{B}^{N})\times{H}^{k-1}(\mathbb{B}^{N})
, if

\displaystyle\left\|\binom{U(0)}{\partial_{s}U(0)}-\binom{\kappa_{d_{0}}(y)}{0}\right\|_{{H}^{k}(\mathbb{B}^{N})\times{H}^{k-1}(\mathbb{B}^{N})}\leq\delta

then:

-

either
U(s)
is not defined for all
(s,y)\in[0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{B}^{N}
,

-

or

\displaystyle\left\|\binom{U(s)}{\partial_{s}U(s)}\right\|_{{H}^{k}(\mathbb{B}^{N})\times{H}^{k-1}(\mathbb{B}^{N})}\rightarrow 0\text{ as }s\rightarrow\infty\text{ exponentially fast }

-

or there exists
d^{*}\in\overline{\mathbb{B}^{N}_{\delta}(d_{0})}\subset\mathbb{B}^{N}
such that

\displaystyle\forall s\geq 0,\ \left\|\binom{U(s)}{\partial_{s}U(s)}-\binom{\kappa_{d^{*}}(y)}{0}\right\|_{{H}^{k}(\mathbb{B}^{N})\times{H}^{k-1}(\mathbb{B}^{N})}\leq C\delta e^{-\varepsilon s}.

Remark 1.4
.

Our result remains valid in the subconformal and conformal cases, providing an alternative proof of the previously established results in
[
40
,
46
]
.

1.3.
Idea of the proof

As pointed out in
[
46
]
, the energy method based on the existence of a Lyapunov functional fails in the superconformal case. In fact, in our setting, the corresponding Lyapunov functional does not even exhibit dissipation. To overcome this difficulty, Donninger and collaborators
[
16
,
17
,
18
,
19
,
13
,
15
,
14
]
developed a spectral method, successfully deriving the asymptotic stability of the ODE blow-up solution, i.e.,
u^{*}_{T}
. This spectral approach is particularly powerful as it is well suited to the non-selfadjoint nature of the linearized problem, especially in the presence of unstable modes. See also our recent extension
[
25
]
, which generalizes this method to accommodate non-compact perturbations.

Originally introduced by Donninger in the context of self-similar blow-up solutions for wave maps
[
12
,
20
,
21
]
, this method has found applications in various settings
[
8
,
7
,
6
,
4
,
3
,
36
]
. A key step in this approach is to establish the mode stability of the linearized operator around the self-similar solution, which requires proving that the only non-negative eigenvalues of the linearized operator are
0
and
1
. The mode stability problem reduces to solving the eigenvalue equation, which takes the form of a second-order elliptic PDE with singular coefficients:

(1.6)

\displaystyle\left(\lambda^{2}+\frac{p+3}{p-1}\lambda+\frac{2(p+1)}{(p-1)^{2}}-p|\kappa_{d}|^{p-1}\right)\varphi+\left(2\lambda+\frac{2(p+1)}{p-1}\right)y\cdot\nabla\varphi+(y_{i}y_{j}-\delta_{ij})\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\varphi=0,

where

|\kappa_{d}|^{p-1}=\kappa_{0}^{p-1}\frac{\left(1-|d|^{2}\right)}{(1+d\cdot y)^{2}},

see
Definition

2.2
.

For the special case
d=0
, the equation (
1.6
) can be decomposed in spherical coordinates, reducing the problem to a family of one-dimensional hypergeometric ODEs, which admit explicit solutions (see
[
47
]
). However, when
d\neq 0
, this decomposition fails due to the presence of an additional singularity introduced by
\kappa_{d}
, namely the set

\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\mid d\cdot y=-1\}.

Even in one dimension, the resulting equation becomes a Heun-type ODE, which is known to be analytically intractable in general
[
14
]
.

To circumvent this difficulty, we employ a Lorentz transformation in self-similar variables. This transformation was previously introduced by Merle and Zaag in the one-dimensional case
[
40
]
and later extended to higher dimensions
[
46
]
, where it was used to establish the coercivity of the energy around
\kappa_{d}
in the subconformal setting. Our key observation is that this transformation allows us to establish full spectral equivalence of the linearized operators for all
d\in\mathbb{B}^{N}
. Consequently, the mode stability problem for
\kappa_{d}
can always be reduced to the mode stability of the ODE blow-up solution
\kappa_{0}
, which has already been established in
[
47
]
. As a direct corollary, we obtain the asymptotic stability of
\kappa_{d}
.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. In
Section

2
, we establish mode stability and prove the equivalence of the discrete spectrum under Lorentz transformations. In
Section

3
, we extend this equivalence to the full spectrum and derive the linear stability of
\kappa_{d}
. In
Section

4
, we prove the main result on nonlinear stability. Since the nonlinear analysis largely follows the strategy developed in
[
47
]
for the ODE blow-up
\kappa_{0}
, we defer it to the appendix for completeness, emphasizing only the necessary adaptations to extend the result to the entire ODE blow-up family.

We use boldface notation for tuples of functions, for example:

2.
Equivalence of Discrete Spectra and Mode Stability

In this section, we introduce the Lorentz transformation in self-similar variables and establish the equivalence of the discrete spectra of the linearized operator associated with equation (
1.4
) around each stationary solution
\kappa_{d}
, where
d\in\mathbb{B}^{N}
. As a consequence, we derive the mode stability of these linearized operators.

Consider then
U(s,y)
a solution to equation (
1.4
). For
|d|<1
, let
U=\kappa_{d}+\eta
, then the corresponding linearized equation at
\kappa_{d}
is given by

(2.1)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\partial_{ss}\eta+\frac{p+3}{p-1}\partial_{s}\eta+2y\cdot\nabla\partial_{s}\eta+\frac{2(p+1)}{p-1}y\cdot\nabla\eta+(y_{i}y_{j}-\delta_{ij})\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\eta+\left(\frac{2(p+1)}{(p-1)^{2}}-p|\kappa_{d}|^{p-1}\right)\eta=0.\end{aligned}

Denote

\mathbf{q}=\begin{pmatrix}q_{1}\\
q_{2}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\eta\\
\partial_{s}\eta+y\cdot\nabla\eta+\frac{2}{p-1}\eta\end{pmatrix},

then Eq. (
2.1
) can be rewritten as the following first-order system

\displaystyle\partial_{s}\begin{pmatrix}q_{1}\\
q_{2}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}-y\cdot\nabla q_{1}-\frac{2}{p-1}q_{1}+q_{2}\\
\Delta q_{1}+p|\kappa_{d}|^{p-1}q_{1}-y\cdot\nabla q_{2}-\frac{p+1}{p-1}q_{2}\end{pmatrix}=:\tilde{\mathbf{L}}_{d}\begin{pmatrix}q_{1}\\
q_{2}\end{pmatrix}

2.1.
The Lorentz transformation in self-similar variables

We recall the N-dimensional Lorentz transformation
\mathcal{T}_{\beta}
and its inverse
\mathcal{T}^{-1}_{\beta}=\mathcal{T}_{-\beta}
such that
(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})=\mathcal{T}_{\beta}(t,x)
are defined as follows

(2.2)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &t^{\prime}=\gamma(t-\beta\cdot x),\\
&x^{\prime}_{i}=-\gamma\beta_{i}t+x_{i}+\frac{\gamma^{2}\beta_{i}}{1+\gamma}(\beta\cdot x),\end{aligned}\right.\quad\text{~and~}\left\{\begin{aligned} &t=\gamma(t^{\prime}+\beta\cdot x^{\prime}),\\
&x_{i}=\gamma\beta_{i}t^{\prime}+x^{\prime}_{i}+\frac{\gamma^{2}\beta_{i}}{1+\gamma}(\beta\cdot x^{\prime}),\end{aligned}\right.

where
\gamma=(1-|\beta|^{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}}
and
|\beta|<1
. Let
v(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})=u(t,x)=u(t(t^{\prime},x^{\prime}),x(t^{\prime},x^{\prime}))
, then by the Lorentz transformation invariance of (
1
), if
u
solves (
1
),
v(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})
satisfies the same equation

(2.3)

\displaystyle v_{t^{\prime}t^{\prime}}-\Delta v=|v|^{p-1}v.

And under this transformation, the self-similar blow-up solution

\displaystyle u^{*}_{T,x_{0},d}(t,x)

\displaystyle=\kappa_{0}\frac{\left(1-|d|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}}{(T-t+d\cdot(x-x_{0}))^{\frac{2}{p-1}}},

corresponds to

\displaystyle v^{*}_{T,x_{0},d,\beta}(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})

\displaystyle:=\kappa_{0}\frac{\left(1-|d|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}}{\left(T-d\cdot x_{0}+\gamma(d\cdot\beta-1)t^{\prime}+\left(\frac{\gamma^{2}\beta\cdot(d-\beta)}{1+\gamma}\beta+(d-\beta)\right)\cdot x^{\prime}\right)^{\frac{2}{p-1}}}.

In particular, if we take
\beta=d
, then
v^{*}_{T,x_{0},d,\beta}
is independent of the spatial variable
x^{\prime}
, i.e

v^{*}_{T,x_{0},d,d}(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})=\frac{\kappa_{0}}{\left(\frac{T-d\cdot x_{0}}{\sqrt{1-|d|^{2}}}-t^{\prime}\right)^{\frac{2}{p-1}}}=\frac{\kappa_{0}}{(T^{\prime}-t^{\prime})^{\frac{2}{p-1}}}\equiv v^{*}_{T^{\prime}},\quad T^{\prime}=\frac{T-d\cdot x_{0}}{\sqrt{1-|d|^{2}}},

which is the ODE blow-up solution to Eq. (
2.3
). Note that even with this transformation it is not easy to directly reduce the stability of
u^{*}_{T,x_{0},d}
to the stability of the ODE blow-up
v^{*}_{T^{\prime}}
due to the following reasons:

(1)

The initial perturbations to
u_{T,x_{0},d}^{*}
and
v^{*}_{T^{\prime}}
are imposed on different regions, i.e.
\{(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:t=0\}
and
\{(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:t^{\prime}=0\}=\{(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:t-d\cdot x=0\}
respectively.

(2)

The stable evolution in
t^{\prime}
direction does not necessarily imply a stable evolution in
t
direction.

Nevertheless, we shall see that it allows us to study the spectrum of the linearized operator at
u_{T,x_{0},d}^{*}
by analyzing the spectrum of the linearized operator at
v^{*}_{T^{\prime}}
in self-similar variables, which facilitates a rigorous proof of the mode stability of
\kappa_{d}
. To begin with, we consider the Lorentz transformation in self-similar variables which was first introduced by
[
40
]
for one-dimensional NLW with power nonlinearities.

For
T^{\prime}>0,x_{0}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}
, define the corresponding self-similar variables for
t^{\prime}
and
x^{\prime}
as

s^{\prime}=-\log(T^{\prime}-t^{\prime})+\log(T^{\prime}),\quad y^{\prime}=\frac{x^{\prime}-x_{0}^{\prime}}{T^{\prime}-t^{\prime}},

and let
V(s^{\prime},y^{\prime})=(T^{\prime}-t^{\prime})^{\frac{2}{p-1}}v(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})
, if
v(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})
solves Eq. (
2.3
), then
V(s^{\prime},y^{\prime})
satisfies (
1.4
) as well, i.e.,

(2.4)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \partial_{s^{\prime}s^{\prime}}V+\frac{p+3}{p-1}\partial_{s^{\prime}}V+2y^{\prime}\cdot\nabla\partial_{s^{\prime}}V+\frac{2(p+1)}{p-1}y^{\prime}\cdot\nabla V+(y^{\prime}_{i}y^{\prime}_{j}-\delta_{ij})\partial_{i}\partial_{j}V+\frac{2(p+1)}{(p-1)^{2}}V=|V|^{p-1}V\end{aligned}

A direct computation implies the following transformation in self-similar variables, see also
[
46
, Lemma A.1.]

Lemma 2.1

(The Lorentz transform in self-similar variables)
.

Consider
U(s,y)
a solution to (
1.4
) defined for all
|y|<1
, and introduce for any
|\beta|<1
, the function
V\equiv\mathcal{T}_{\beta}U
defined by

\displaystyle V(s^{\prime},y^{\prime})=\left(\frac{1}{\gamma(1-\beta\cdot y^{\prime})}\right)^{\frac{2}{p-1}}U(s,y),\text{~where~}s=s^{\prime}-\log(1-\beta\cdot y^{\prime}),\quad y=\frac{y^{\prime}-\gamma\beta+\frac{\gamma^{2}\beta\cdot y^{\prime}}{1+\gamma}\beta}{\gamma(1-\beta\cdot y^{\prime})},

then
V(s^{\prime},y^{\prime})
solves (
2.4
) for all
|y^{\prime}|<1
.

2.2.
Unstable eigenvalues induced by symmetries

We postpone the rigorous functional setup of the linearized operator
\tilde{\mathbf{L}}_{d}
to the next section, and regard
\tilde{\mathbf{L}}_{d}
as a formal differential operator. We first study the possible unstable eigenvalues of
\tilde{\mathbf{L}}_{d}
as a step towards proving its mode stability.

Definition 2.2
.

\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
is called an eigenvalue of
\tilde{\mathbf{L}}_{d}
if there exists a nontrivial smooth function
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\mathbb{B}})
such that

(2.5)

\displaystyle\left(\lambda^{2}+\frac{p+3}{p-1}\lambda+\frac{2(p+1)}{(p-1)^{2}}-p|\kappa_{d}|^{p-1}\right)\varphi+\left(2\lambda+\frac{2(p+1)}{p-1}\right)y\cdot\nabla\varphi+(y_{i}y_{j}-\delta_{ij})\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\varphi=0.

We call an eigenvalue
\lambda
unstable if its real part is non-negative, i.e.,
\operatorname{Re}\lambda\geq 0
. Otherwise, we call
\lambda
a stable eigenvalue.

Remark 2.3
.

Actually, if
\tilde{\mathbf{L}}_{d}\mathbf{q}=\lambda\mathbf{q}
for some
\mathbf{q}=(q_{1},q_{2})^{T}\in(C^{\infty}(\overline{\mathbb{B}}))^{2}
, then
q_{1}
satisfies (
2.5
) and
\lambda
is an eigenvalue of
\tilde{\mathbf{L}}_{d}
. Conversely, if
\lambda
is an eigenvalue of
\tilde{\mathbf{L}}_{d}
with a smooth eigenfunction
\varphi(y)
, then
\mathbf{q}:=(\varphi,\lambda\varphi+y\cdot\nabla\varphi+\frac{2}{p-1}\varphi)^{T}
satisfies
\tilde{\mathbf{L}}_{d}\mathbf{q}=\lambda\mathbf{q}
.

First, the symmetries of Eq. (
1.4
) give us some explicit unstable eigenvalues. More precisely, if
U(s,y)
is a solution, then the following transformations of
U(s,y)
are also solutions to (
1.4
):

•

(Space translation in
x
) for any
a\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, the function

\displaystyle U_{1}(s,y)=U\left(s,y+ae^{s}\right);

•

(Time translation in
t
) for any
b\in\mathbb{R}
, the function

\displaystyle U_{2}(s,y)=\left(1+be^{s}\right)^{-\frac{2}{p-1}}U\left(s-\log\left(1+be^{s}\right),\frac{y}{1+be^{s}}\right);

•

(Lorentz transformation) The transposition in self-similar variables of the Lorentz transformation

\displaystyle U_{3}(s,y)=\mathcal{T}_{\beta}U\left(s,y\right);

Thus, for
U=\kappa_{d}(y)
, we have

•

for all
1\leq i\leq N
,
\partial_{a_{i}}U_{1}|_{a=0}
and
\partial_{b}U_{2}|_{b=0}
generate the same 1d eigenspace of eigenvalue 1;

•

for
1\leq i\leq N
,
\partial_{\beta_{i}}U_{3}|_{\beta=0}
generates a N dimensional eigenspace of eigenvalue 0.

Therefore, the linearized operator
\tilde{\mathbf{L}}_{d}
has at least two unstable eigenvalues
0
and
1
. More precisely, we have

Lemma 2.4

(
[
47
, Lemma 3.2]
)
.

Let
(N,p,R)\in\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{R}_{>1}\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 1}
,
d\in\mathbb{B}^{N}
, and denote
s_{p}:=\frac{2}{p-2}
. Let
\mathbf{f}_{0,d,i},\mathbf{f}_{1,d}\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\mathbb{B}^{N}_{R}})^{2}
be given by

(2.6)

\displaystyle\mathbf{f}_{0,d,i}(y)

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}\hfill s_{p}\kappa_{0}\gamma(d)^{-s_{p}}(1+d\cdot y)^{-s_{p}-1}y_{i}\\
s_{p}(s_{p}+1)\kappa_{0}\gamma(d)^{-s_{p}}(1+d\cdot y)^{-s_{p}-2}y_{i}\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle\,+\begin{bmatrix}\hfill s_{p}\kappa_{0}\gamma(d)^{-s_{p}+2}(1+d\cdot y)^{-s_{p}}d_{i}\\
s_{p}^{2}\kappa_{0}\gamma(d)^{-s_{p}}(1+d\cdot y)^{-s_{p}-1}d_{i}\end{bmatrix}\,,

(2.7)

\displaystyle\mathbf{f}_{1,d}(y)

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}\hfill s_{p}\kappa_{0}\gamma(d)^{-s_{p}}(1+d\cdot y)^{-s_{p}-1}\\
s_{p}(s_{p}+1)\kappa_{0}\gamma(d)^{-s_{p}}(1+d\cdot y)^{-s_{p}-2}\end{bmatrix}\,,

for
i=1,\ldots,N
. Then

\tilde{\mathbf{L}}_{d}\mathbf{f}_{0,d,i}=\mathbf{0}\qquad\text{and}\qquad(\mathbf{I}-\tilde{\mathbf{L}}_{d})\mathbf{f}_{1,d}=\mathbf{0}\,.

2.3.
Mode stability

Nevertheless, we introduce the following notion of mode stability, see
[
12
,
14
]
.

Definition 2.5

(Mode stability)
.

We say that the blow-up solution
\kappa_{d}
is mode stable if the existence of a nontrivial smooth function
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\mathbb{B}})
that satisfies Eq. (
2.5
) necessarily implies that
\lambda\in\{0,1\}
or
\operatorname{Re}\lambda<0
.

To study the mode stability of
\kappa_{d}
, we need to solve Eq. (
2.5
). Note that
\kappa_{d}(y)
is non-radial, thus we are not able to reduce (
2.5
) to one-dimensional ODEs by using spherical harmonics decomposition, see
[
18
,
47
]
. Moreover,
\kappa_{d}(y)
has singular points
\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:d\cdot y=-1\}
, which makes the ODE analysis of (
2.5
) complicated.

To deal with this problem, we note that
\kappa_{d}
can be transformed to the ODE blow-up profile using the Lorentz transformation. More precisely,
for
\kappa_{d}(y)=\kappa_{0}\frac{\left(1-|d|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}}{(1+d\cdot y)^{\frac{2}{p-1}}}
,
the corresponding function
V_{d,\beta}(y^{\prime})\equiv\mathcal{T}_{\beta}\kappa(d,\cdot)
solves Eq. (
2.4
) and satisfies

\displaystyle V_{d,\beta}(y^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{\gamma(1-\beta\cdot y^{\prime})}\right)^{\frac{2}{p-1}}\frac{\kappa_{0}\left(1-|d|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}}{(1+d\cdot y)^{\frac{2}{p-1}}}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{\gamma(1-\beta\cdot y^{\prime})}\right)^{\frac{2}{p-1}}\frac{\kappa_{0}\left(1-|d|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}}{\left(1+\beta\cdot\frac{y^{\prime}-\gamma\beta+\frac{\gamma^{2}\beta\cdot y^{\prime}}{1+\gamma}\beta}{\gamma(1-\beta\cdot y^{\prime})}+(d-\beta)\cdot y\right)^{\frac{2}{p-1}}}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{\gamma(1-\beta\cdot y^{\prime})}\right)^{\frac{2}{p-1}}\frac{\kappa_{0}\left(1-|d|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}}{\left(\frac{1}{\gamma^{2}(1-\beta\cdot y^{\prime})}+(d-\beta)\cdot y\right)^{\frac{2}{p-1}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\kappa_{0}\left(\gamma^{2}(1-|d|^{2})\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}}{\left(1+\gamma^{2}(1-\beta\cdot y^{\prime})(d-\beta)\cdot y\right)^{\frac{2}{p-1}}}

Again, when
\beta=d
,
V_{d,d}=\kappa_{0}(y)\equiv\kappa_{0}
is the ODE blow-up profile. Hereafter, we always take
\beta=d
. Let
V=V_{d,d}+\xi=\kappa_{0}+\xi
, we derive the linearized equation of Eq. (
2.4
) at
V_{d,d}

(2.8)

\displaystyle\partial_{s^{\prime}s^{\prime}}\xi+\frac{p+3}{p-1}\partial_{s^{\prime}}\xi+2y^{\prime}\cdot\nabla\partial_{s^{\prime}}\xi+\frac{2(p+1)}{p-1}y^{\prime}\cdot\nabla\xi+(y^{\prime}_{i}y^{\prime}_{j}-\delta_{ij})\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\xi+\left(\frac{2(p+1)}{(p-1)^{2}}-p|\kappa_{0}|^{p-1}\right)\xi=0.

Denote
\mathbf{r}=(r_{1},r_{2})=(\xi,\partial_{s^{\prime}}\xi+y^{\prime}\partial_{y^{\prime}}\xi+\frac{2}{p-1}\xi)
, then we can rewrite (
2.8
) as a first-order PDE system

\displaystyle\partial_{s}\left(\begin{array}[]{l}r_{1}\\
r_{2}\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}-y^{\prime}\cdot\nabla r_{1}-\frac{2}{p-1}r_{1}+r_{2}\\
\Delta r_{1}+p|\kappa_{0}|^{p-1}r_{1}-y^{\prime}\cdot\nabla r_{2}-\frac{p+1}{p-1}r_{2}\end{array}\right)=\tilde{\mathbf{L}}_{0}\left(\begin{array}[]{l}r_{1}\\
r_{2}\end{array}\right).

Following
Lemma

2.1
, we show that the Lorentz transformation
\mathcal{T}_{d}
also transforms the linearized equation around
\kappa_{d}
to the linearized equation around
\kappa_{0}
.

Proposition 2.6

(Transformation of the linearized equation)
.

Consider
\eta(s,y)
a solution to the linearized equation (
2.1
) around
\kappa_{d}(\cdot)
, and introduce
\xi\equiv\mathcal{T}_{d}\eta
, then
\xi
satisfies the linearized equation (
2.8
) around
\kappa_{0}(\cdot)\equiv\kappa_{0}
.

Proof.

Observe that the full nonlinear wave equation (
1.4
) is invariant under the Lorentz transformation
\mathcal{T}_{d}
, and that
\mathcal{T}_{d}\kappa_{d}=\kappa_{0}
. Moreover, the transformation
\mathcal{T}_{d}
maps the nonlinear term
|U|^{p-1}U
to
\left(\gamma(1-d\cdot y^{\prime})\right)^{-2p/(p-1)}|V|^{p-1}V
. Consequently,
\mathcal{T}_{d}
maps the linearized equation around
\kappa_{d}
to the corresponding equation around
\kappa_{0}
.
∎

Corollary 2.7

(Equivalence of the discrete spectrum)
.

\lambda
is an eigenvalue of
\tilde{\mathbf{L}}_{d}
if and only if
\lambda
is an eigenvalue of
\tilde{\mathbf{L}}_{0}
.

Proof.

If
\lambda
is an eigenvalue of
\tilde{\mathbf{L}}_{d}
, then there exists a smooth function
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\mathbb{B}})
such that
e^{\lambda s}\varphi(y)
solves the linearized equation (
2.1
). By the Lorentz transformation and Proposition
2.6
, we have

\mathcal{T}_{d}(e^{\lambda s}\varphi)(s^{\prime},y^{\prime})=e^{\lambda s^{\prime}}\left(1-d\cdot y^{\prime}\right)^{-\lambda}\left(\frac{1}{\gamma(1-\beta\cdot y^{\prime})}\right)^{\frac{2}{p-1}}\varphi\left(\frac{y^{\prime}-\gamma d+\frac{\gamma^{2}d\cdot y^{\prime}}{1+\gamma}d}{\gamma(1-d\cdot y^{\prime})}\right):=e^{\lambda s^{\prime}}\psi(y^{\prime})

solves the linearized equation (
2.8
), where
\gamma=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-|d|^{2}}}
. Thus,
\psi(y^{\prime})\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\mathbb{B}})
satisfies (
2.5
) with
d=0
, i.e.,
\lambda
is also an eigenvalue of
\tilde{\mathbf{L}}_{0}
. Conversely, by the inverse Lorentz transformation, any eigenvalue of
\tilde{\mathbf{L}}_{0}
is also an eigenvalue of
\tilde{\mathbf{L}}_{d}
.
∎

Remark 2.8
.

According to the proof of Corollary
2.7
, the following transformation (induced by the Lorentz transformation
\mathcal{T}_{d}
)

(2.15)

\displaystyle\psi(y^{\prime})=\left(1-d\cdot y^{\prime}\right)^{-\lambda}\left(\frac{1}{\gamma(1-\beta\cdot y^{\prime})}\right)^{\frac{2}{p-1}}\varphi\left(\frac{y^{\prime}-\gamma d+\frac{\gamma^{2}d\cdot y^{\prime}}{1+\gamma}d}{\gamma(1-d\cdot y^{\prime})}\right),\quad\gamma=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-|d|^{2}}}

actually gives the transformation of the eigen-equation
Eq.

2.5
to itself with
d=0
.

Thus, the mode stability of
\kappa_{d}
is equivalent to that of
\kappa_{0}
, the latter of which has already been established in a series of works by Donninger and his collaborators
[
16
,
18
]
; see also Ostermann
[
47
, Lemma 3.3]
.

Lemma 2.9
.

\kappa_{0}
is mode stable, i.e. there exist no eigenvalues of
\tilde{\mathbf{L}}_{0}
such that
\operatorname{Re}\lambda>-1
, expect
0
and
1
.

As a corollary, we obtain

Corollary 2.10
.

For all
|d|<1
,
\kappa_{d}
is mode stable, i.e. there exist no eigenvalues of
\tilde{\mathbf{L}}_{d}
such that
\operatorname{Re}\lambda>-1
, expect
0
and
1
.

3.
Spectral equivalence and linear stability

In this section, we establish the equivalence of the full spectra of the linearized operators around each
\kappa_{d}
and, consequently, derive the linear stability of the self-similar profile
\kappa_{d}
. This analysis builds upon the spectral strategy developed in
[
47
]
for the ODE blow-up profile
\kappa_{0}
. Recall the first-order linearized system:

\displaystyle\partial_{s}\begin{pmatrix}q_{1}\\
q_{2}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}-y\cdot\nabla q_{1}-\frac{2}{p-1}q_{1}+q_{2}\\
\Delta q_{1}+p|\kappa_{d}|^{p-1}q_{1}-y\cdot\nabla q_{2}-\frac{p+1}{p-1}q_{2}\end{pmatrix}=:\tilde{\mathbf{L}}_{d}\begin{pmatrix}q_{1}\\
q_{2}\end{pmatrix}.

3.1.
Functional setup

We introduce the following standard Hilbert space.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
(N,k,R)\in\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{R}_{>0}
. We define a Hilbert space by

\mathcal{H}^{k}(\mathbb{B}^{N}_{R})\coloneqq H^{k}(\mathbb{B}^{N}_{R})\times H^{k-1}(\mathbb{B}^{N}_{R})\,,\qquad\|\mathbf{f}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k}(\mathbb{B}^{N}_{R})}\coloneqq\|(f_{1},f_{2})\|_{H^{k}(\mathbb{B}^{N}_{R})\times H^{k-1}(\mathbb{B}^{N}_{R})}\,.

We also define for
\delta>0
a closed ball

\mathcal{H}^{k}_{\delta}(\mathbb{B}^{N}_{R})\coloneqq\{\mathbf{f}\in\mathcal{H}^{k}(\mathbb{B}^{N}_{R})\mid\|\mathbf{f}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k}(\mathbb{B}^{N}_{R})}\leq\delta\}\,.

Then, we define the free wave operator in terms of self-similar variables.

Definition 3.2

(Free wave operator)
.

Let
(N,p,k,R)\in\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{R}_{>1}\times\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 1}
. For
\mathbf{f}\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\mathbb{B}^{N}_{R}})^{2}
we define

\displaystyle\mathbf{L}_{N,p,R}\mathbf{f}\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\mathbb{B}^{N}_{R}})^{2}\quad\text{by}\quad(\mathbf{L}_{N,p,R}\mathbf{f})(y)=\begin{pmatrix}-y\cdot\nabla f_{1}-\frac{2}{p-1}f_{1}+f_{2}\\
\Delta f_{1}-y\cdot\nabla f_{2}-\frac{p+1}{p-1}f_{2}\end{pmatrix}.

The operator
\mathbf{L}_{N,p,k,R}:\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{L}_{N,p,k,R})\subset\mathcal{H}^{k}(\mathbb{B}^{N}_{R})\rightarrow\mathcal{H}^{k}(\mathbb{B}^{N}_{R})
is densely defined by

\mathbf{L}_{N,p,k,R}\mathbf{f}=\mathbf{L}_{N,p,R}\mathbf{f}\,,\qquad\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{L}_{N,p,k,R})=C^{\infty}(\overline{\mathbb{B}^{N}_{R}})^{2}\,.

The closure of the free wave operator generates a strongly continuous semigroup, see
[
47
, Theorem 2.1]
for the detailed proof.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
(N,p,k,R)\in\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{R}_{>1}\times\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 1}
. The operator
\mathbf{L}_{N,p,k,R}
is closable and its closure
\overline{\mathbf{L}_{N,p,k,R}}
is the generator of a strongly continuous operator semigroup

\mathbf{S}_{N,p,k,R}:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\rightarrow\mathcal{L}\left(\mathcal{H}^{k}(\mathbb{B}^{N}_{R})\right)

which satisfies that for any
0<\varepsilon<\frac{1}{2}
there is a constant
M_{N,p,k,R,\varepsilon}\geq 1
such that

\|\mathbf{S}_{N,p,k,R}(s)\mathbf{f}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k}(\mathbb{B}^{N}_{R})}\leq M_{N,p,k,R,\varepsilon}e^{\omega_{N,p,k,R,\varepsilon}s}\|\mathbf{f}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k}(\mathbb{B}^{N}_{R})}

for all
\mathbf{f}\in\mathcal{H}^{k}(\mathbb{B}^{N}_{R})
and all
s\geq 0
, where

\omega_{N,p,k,\varepsilon}=-s_{p}+\max\Big\{\frac{N}{2}-k,\varepsilon\Big\}\qquad\text{and}\qquad s_{p}=\frac{2}{p-1}\,.

Then, we introduce the potential operator coming from the linearization around
\kappa_{d}
.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
d\in\mathbb{B}^{N}
and
(N,p,k,R)\in\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{R}_{>1}\times\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 1}
with
R<|d|^{-1}
. Let
V_{d}\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\mathbb{B}^{N}_{R}})
be given by

V_{d}(y)=p|\kappa_{d}|^{p-1}=(s_{p}+1)(s_{p}+2)\left(1-|d|^{2}\right)\left(1+d\cdot y\right)^{-2}\,.

We define the bounded linear operator

\mathbf{L}_{d}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{L}\left(\mathcal{H}^{k}(\mathbb{B}^{N}_{R})\right)\qquad\text{by}\qquad\mathbf{L}_{d}^{\prime}\mathbf{f}=\begin{bmatrix}0\\
V_{d}f_{1}\end{bmatrix}\,.

Now, the full linearized operator is composed of a perturbation of the free wave operator.

Definition 3.5
.

Let
d\in\mathbb{B}^{N}
and
(N,p,k,R)\in\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{R}_{>1}\times\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 1}
with
R<|d|^{-1}
. The closed linear operator
\mathbf{L}_{d}:\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{L}_{d})\subset\mathcal{H}^{k}(\mathbb{B}^{N}_{R})\rightarrow\mathcal{H}^{k}(\mathbb{B}^{N}_{R})
is densely defined by

\mathbf{L}_{d}=\overline{\mathbf{L}_{N,p,k}}+\mathbf{L}_{d}^{\prime}\,,\qquad\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{L}_{d})=\mathcal{D}(\overline{\mathbf{L}_{N,p,k}})\,.

Perturbation theory for semigroups yields that this operator is indeed the generator of the linearized wave flow.

Proposition 3.6

(
[
47
, Proposition 3.1]
)
.

Let
d\in\mathbb{B}^{N}
and
(N,p,k,R)\in\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{R}_{>1}\times\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 1}
with
R<|d|^{-1}
. The closed linear operator
\mathbf{L}_{d}:\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{L}_{d})\subset\mathcal{H}^{k}(\mathbb{B}^{N}_{R})\rightarrow\mathcal{H}^{k}(\mathbb{B}^{N}_{R})
is the generator of a strongly continuous operator semigroup

\mathbf{S}_{d}:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\rightarrow\mathcal{L}\left(\mathcal{H}^{k}(\mathbb{B}^{N}_{R})\right)\,.

Moreover, let
0<\varepsilon<\frac{1}{2}
and
M_{N,p,k,\varepsilon}\geq 1
,
\omega_{N,p,k,\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{R}
be the constants from
Theorem

3.3
. Then the bound

\|\mathbf{S}_{d}(s)\|_{\mathcal{L}\left(\mathcal{H}^{k}(\mathbb{B}^{N}_{R})\right)}\leq M_{N,p,k,\varepsilon}\mathrm{e}^{\Big(\omega_{d,p,k,\varepsilon}+M_{N,p,k,\varepsilon}\|\mathbf{L}_{d}^{\prime}\|_{\mathcal{L}\left(\mathcal{H}^{k}(\mathbb{B}^{N}_{R})\right)}\Big)s}

holds for all
s\geq 0
and all
|d|<1
.

3.2.
Spectral analysis of the linearized operator

The spectrum of the linearized operator
\mathbf{L}_{0}
has already been known in
[
47
, Lemma 3.3]

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
(N,p,k,R)\in\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{R}_{>1}\times\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 1}
such that
\frac{N}{2}-s_{p}-k<0
. Put

\omega_{N,p,k}\coloneqq\max\left\{-1,\frac{N}{2}-s_{p}-k,-s_{p}\right\}

and let
\omega_{N,p,k}<\omega_{0}<0
be arbitrary but fixed. We have

\sigma(\mathbf{L}_{0})\cap\overline{\mathbb{H}_{\omega_{0}}}=\{0,1\}\,.

Both points
0,1\in\sigma(\mathbf{L}_{0})
belong to the point spectrum of
\mathbf{L}_{0}
with eigenspaces

\ker(\mathbf{L}_{0})=\langle\mathbf{f}_{0,0,i}\rangle_{i=1}^{N}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\ker(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{L}_{0})=\langle\mathbf{f}_{1,0}\rangle

spanned by the symmetry modes
\mathbf{f}_{0,0,i},\mathbf{f}_{1,0}\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\mathbb{B}^{N}_{R}})^{2}
given by

\mathbf{f}_{0,0,i}(y)=\begin{bmatrix}s_{p}\kappa_{0}y_{i}\\
s_{p}(s_{p}+1)\kappa_{0}y_{i}\end{bmatrix}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathbf{f}_{1,0}(y)=\begin{bmatrix}s_{p}\kappa_{0}\\
s_{p}(s_{p}+1)\kappa_{0}\end{bmatrix}\,,\qquad i=1,\ldots,N.

Now, we establish the following spectral equivalence of linearized operators.

Proposition 3.8

(Spectral equivalence)
.

Let
d\in\mathbb{B}^{N}
and
(N,p,k,R)\in\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{R}_{>1}\times\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 1}
such that
\frac{N}{2}-s_{p}-k<0
and
R<|d|^{-1}
, we have
\sigma(\mathbf{L}_{d})=\sigma(\mathbf{L}_{0})\,.

Proof.

By definition,
\mathbf{L}_{d}=\mathbf{L}_{0}-\mathbf{L}_{0}^{\prime}+\mathbf{L}_{d}^{\prime}
, where the potential operators
\mathbf{L}_{0}^{\prime}
and
\mathbf{L}_{d}^{\prime}
are compact, see
[
47
, Lemma 3.1]
. Thus, by
[
29
, p. 244, Theorem 5.35 ]
the essential spectrum of
\mathbf{L}_{0}
and
\mathbf{L}_{d}
are the same. Besides, by
Corollary

2.7
and
Eq.

2.15
(note that the transformation in
Eq.

2.15
maps
H^{k}(\mathbb{B}^{N})
to
H^{k}(\mathbb{B}^{N})
itself), we have
\sigma_{p}(\mathbf{L}_{d})=\sigma_{p}(\mathbf{L}_{0})
. Therefore,
\sigma(\mathbf{L}_{d})=\sigma(\mathbf{L}_{0})\,.
∎

As a corollary, we obtain the spectrum of
\mathbf{L}_{d}
, which extends
[
47
, Theorem 3.1]
for
\mathbf{L}_{d}
with
|d|\ll 1
to the whole range
|d|<1
.

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
d\in\mathbb{B}^{N}
and
(N,p,k,R)\in\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{R}_{>1}\times\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 1}
such that
\frac{N}{2}-s_{p}-k<0
and
R<|d|^{-1}
. Put

\omega_{N,p,k}\coloneqq\max\left\{-1,\frac{N}{2}-s_{p}-k,-s_{p}\right\}

and let
\omega_{N,p,k}<\omega_{0}<0
be arbitrary but fixed. Then,

\displaystyle\mathbf{f}_{0}^{T}(y)

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}T^{s_{p}}\kappa_{0}\gamma(d_{0})^{-s_{p}}(1+Td_{0}\cdot y)^{-s_{p}}\\
T^{s_{p}+1}s_{p}\kappa_{0}\gamma(d_{0})^{-s_{p}}(1+Td_{0}\cdot y)^{-s_{p}-1}\end{bmatrix}\,,

\displaystyle\mathbf{f}_{d}(y)

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}\hfill\kappa_{0}\gamma(d)^{-s_{p}}(1+d\cdot y)^{-s_{p}}\\
s_{p}\kappa_{0}\gamma(d)^{-s_{p}}(1+d\cdot y)^{-s_{p}-1}\end{bmatrix}\,.

To guarantee a stabilized evolution for such data, we need a smallness property of the initial data operator. This is ensured by the following lemma.

Lemma A.6
.

Let
d_{0}\in\mathbb{B}^{N}
and
(N,p,k,R)\in\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{R}_{>1}\times\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 1}
such that
k>\frac{N}{2}
and
R<|d_{0}|^{-1}
. Let
R_{0}>R/(1-|d_{0}|R)
,
d\in\overline{\mathbb{B}^{N}_{R_{0}^{-1}}(d_{0})}
, and
T\in\overline{\mathbb{B}^{1}_{\frac{1}{2}}(1)}
. We have

\mathbf{Q}_{d,T}(\mathbf{f})=\mathbf{f}^{T}+(T-1)\mathbf{f}_{1,d}+(d^{i}-d_{0}^{i})\mathbf{f}_{0,d,i}+\mathbf{r}(d,T)\,,

for any
\mathbf{f}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})^{2}
, where
\mathbf{f}_{1,d},\mathbf{f}_{0,d,i}
are the symmetry modes from
Lemma

2.4
, and

\|\mathbf{r}(d,T)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k}(\mathbb{B}^{N}_{R})}\lesssim|T-1|^{2}+|d-d_{0}|^{2}

for all
d\in\overline{\mathbb{B}^{N}_{R_{0}^{-1}}(d_{0})}
and all
T\in\overline{\mathbb{B}^{1}_{\frac{1}{2}}(1)}
.

Proof.

The proof follows similarly to
[
47
, Lemma 4.3]
, with
\beta\in\overline{\mathbb{B}^{N}_{R_{0}^{-1}}}
replaced by
d\in\overline{\mathbb{B}^{N}_{R_{0}^{-1}}(d_{0})}
,
\mathbf{U}_{\beta,T}
replaced by
\mathbf{Q}_{d,T}
, and
|\beta|
replaced by
|d-d_{0}|
.
∎

It remains to remove the correction term in
Eq.

A.2
to obtain a global solution to
Eq.

A.1
.

Proposition A.7
.

Let
d_{0}\in\mathbb{B}^{N}
and
(N,p,k,R)\in\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{R}_{>1}\times\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 1}
such that
k>\frac{N}{2}
and
R<|d_{0}|^{-1}
. Let
0<\varepsilon<\omega_{p}
. There are constants
0<\delta_{\varepsilon}<1
,
C_{\varepsilon}>1
such that for all
0<\delta\leq\delta_{\varepsilon}
,
C\geq C_{\varepsilon}
and for all real-valued
\mathbf{f}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})^{2}
with
\|\mathbf{f}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq\frac{\delta}{C^{2}}
there are parameters
d^{*}\in\overline{\mathbb{B}^{N}_{\frac{\delta}{C}}(d_{0})}
,
T^{*}\in\overline{\mathbb{B}^{1}_{\frac{\delta}{C}}(1)}
and a unique
\mathbf{q}_{d^{*},T^{*}}\in C\big([0,\infty),\mathcal{H}^{k}(\mathbb{B}^{N}_{R})\big)
such that
\|\mathbf{q}_{d^{*},T^{*}}(s)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k}(\mathbb{B}^{N}_{R})}\leq\delta\mathrm{e}^{(-\omega_{p}+\varepsilon)s}
and

(A.3)

\mathbf{q}_{d^{*},T^{*}}(s)=\mathbf{S}_{d^{*}}(s)\mathbf{Q}_{d^{*},T^{*}}(\mathbf{f})+\int_{0}^{s}\mathbf{S}_{d^{*}}(s-s^{\prime})\mathbf{N}_{d^{*}}(\mathbf{q}_{d^{*},T^{*}}(s^{\prime}))\,\mathrm{d}s^{\prime}

for all
s\geq 0
.

Proof.

The proof follows similarly to
[
47
, Proposition 4.2]
, with
\beta^{*}\in\overline{\mathbb{B}^{N}_{\frac{\delta}{C}}}
replaced by
d^{*}\in\overline{\mathbb{B}^{N}_{\frac{\delta}{C}}(d_{0})}
.
∎

By Sobolev embedding and induction, the just obtained mild solution is in fact a jointly smooth classical solution, which gives the proof of
Proposition

4.1
.

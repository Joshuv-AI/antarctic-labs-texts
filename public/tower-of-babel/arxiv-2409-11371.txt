Cesàro operators on the space of analytic functions with logarithmic growth
José Bonet

Abstract
Continuity, compactness, the spectrum and ergodic properties of Cesàro operators are investigated when they act on the space V ​ H ​ ( 𝔻 ) VH(\mathbb{D}) of analytic functions with logarithmic growth on the open unit disc 𝔻 \mathbb{D} of the complex plane. The space V ​ H ​ ( 𝔻 ) VH(\mathbb{D}) is a countable inductive limit of weighted Banach spaces of analytic functions with compact linking maps. It was introduced and studied by Taskinen and also by Jasiczak.

Introduction.

The aim of this note is to study Cesàro operators when they act on the space
VH(\mathbb{D})
of analytic functions with logarithmic growth on the open unit disc
\mathbb{D}
of the complex plane. This space was introduced and investigated by Taskinen in
[
30
]
. It was studied also by Jasiczak
[
23
]
. This space is a countable union of weighted Banach spaces of analytic functions whose topology can be described by weighted sup-seminorms. They proved that the Bergman projection is continuous from the corresponding space of measurable functions into this space. This fact permitted Taskinen and the author to study Toeplitz operators on this space in
[
14
]
.

We denote by
H(\mathbb{D})
the Fréchet space of all analytic functions on
\mathbb{D}

endowed with the topology
\tau_{co}
of uniform convergence on the compact subsets of
\mathbb{D}
.

The classical Cesàro operator
\mathsf{C}
is given by

f\mapsto\mathsf{C}(f)\colon z\mapsto\frac{1}{z}\int_{0}^{z}\frac{f(\zeta)}{1-\zeta}d\zeta,\ \ \ z\in\mathbb{D}\setminus\{0\},\quad{\rm and}\quad\mathsf{C}(f)(0)=f(0),

(1.1)

for
f\in H(\mathbb{D})
. It is a Fréchet space isomorphism of
H(\mathbb{D})
onto itself, see
[
27
]
. In terms of the Taylor coefficients

\widehat{f}(n):=\frac{f^{(n)}(0)}{n!}
, for
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0},
of functions
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\widehat{f}(n)z^{n}\in H(\mathbb{D})
one has the description

\mathsf{C}(f)(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Big(\frac{1}{n+1}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\widehat{f}(k)\Big)z^{n},\ \ \ \ z\in\mathbb{D}.

It is known that there are many classical Banach spaces
X
of analytic functions on
\mathbb{D}
such that the Cesàro operator
\mathsf{C}
acts continuously from
X
into itself; for instance, the Hardy spaces
H^{p}(\mathbb{D}),1\leq p<\infty,
the Bergman and the Dirichlet spaces, etc. See for example
[
1
,
6
,
27
]
and the references therein. On the other hand,
\mathsf{C}
fails to act in the space
H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
of bounded analytic functions, since
\mathsf{C}(\mathbf{1})(z)=(1/z)\log(1/(1-z))
, for
z\in\mathbb{D}
.

We describe the space
VH(\mathbb{D})
on which the Cesàro operators are investigated in this paper. A
weight

v
on
\mathbb{D}
is a continuous function
v:[0,1)\to(0,\infty)
, which is non-increasing on
[0,1)
and satisfies
\lim_{r\rightarrow 1}v(r)=0
. We extend
v
to
\mathbb{D}
radially by
v(z):=v(|z|)
. For such a weight
v
, we define the following
weighted Banach spaces of
analytic functions
on
\mathbb{D}

H_{v}^{\infty}:=\{f\in H(\mathbb{D}):\ \|f\|_{v}:=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}v(z)|f(z)|<\infty\},

H_{v}^{0}:=\{f\in H(\mathbb{D}):\ v|f|\ {\rm vanishes\ at\ infinity}\ {\rm on}\ \mathbb{D}\},

endowed with the norm
\|.\|_{v}
. Recall that a function
g
vanishes at infinity on
\mathbb{D}
if for
every
\varepsilon>0
there is a compact subset
K
of
\mathbb{D}
such
that
|g(z)|<\varepsilon
if
z\notin K
.

For an analytic function
f\in H(\{z\in\mathbb{C};|z|<R\})
and
r<R
, we denote
M(f,r):=\max\{|f(z)|\ ;\ |z|=r\}
. Using the notation
O
and
o
of Landau,
f\in H_{v}^{\infty}
if and only if
M(f,r)=O(1/v(r)),r\rightarrow 1,
and
f\in H_{v}^{0}
if and only if
M(f,r)=o(1/v(r)),r\rightarrow 1
. Polynomials are contained in
H^{0}_{v}
and the closure of the polynomials in
H_{v}^{\infty}
coincides with
H_{v}^{0}
, see e.g.
[
9
]
.

The examples of weights which are relevant in this paper are:

(i)

w_{\gamma}(z)=(1-|z|)^{\gamma},\ z\in\mathbb{D},
with
\gamma>0
, which are the so-called standard weights on the disc, for which
A^{-\gamma}=H^{\infty}_{w_{\gamma}}(\mathbb{D})
and
A^{-\gamma}_{0}=H^{0}_{w_{\gamma}}(\mathbb{D})
are the Korenblum type Banach growth spaces; see Section 4.3 in
[
22
]
.

(ii)

v(z)=1
if
|z|\leq 1-1/e
, and
v(z)=(-\log(1-|z|))^{-1}
if
|z|\geq 1-1/e
. Observe that
0<v(z)\leq v(0)=1
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
. The logarithmic weights are defined by
v_{\alpha}(z):=v(z)^{\alpha},\alpha\geq 1
.

Banach spaces of the type mentioned above appear naturally in the
study of growth conditions of analytic functions and have been
considered in many papers. We refer to
[
9
,
10
,
12
]
and the references therein. Lusky
[
25
]
obtained the isomorphic classification of these spaces. We refer the reader to
[
22
,
31
]
for unexplained notation.
In what follows
\mathbb{N}
stands for the natural numbers and we set
\mathbb{N}_{0}:=\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
.

The space
VH(\mathbb{D})
of analytic functions of logarithmic growth introduced by Taskinen in
[
30
]
is defined as follows: Set
v_{k}(z)=v(z)^{k},\ z\in\mathbb{D},k\in\mathbb{N},
for
v(z)=1
if
|z|\leq 1-1/e
, and
v(z)=(-\log(1-r))^{-1}
if
|z|\geq 1-1/e
. Since
v_{k+1}(z)\leq v_{k}(z)
for all
z\in\mathbb{D},k\in\mathbb{N},
the weighted Banach spaces satisfy
H^{\infty}_{v_{k}}\subset H^{\infty}_{v_{k+1}}
with continuous inclusion. Then
VH(\mathbb{D}):=\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}H^{\infty}_{v_{k}}
and it is endowed with the finest locally convex topology such that the inclusions
H^{\infty}_{v_{k}}\subset VH(\mathbb{D})
are continuous; that is
VH(\mathbb{D})={\rm ind}_{k}H^{\infty}_{v_{k}}
is an (LB)-space, a (Hausdorff) countable inductive limit of the weighted Banach spaces
H^{\infty}_{v_{k}},k\in\mathbb{N}
.

Moreover,
\lim_{|z|\rightarrow 1}\frac{v_{k+1}(z)}{v_{k}(z)}=0
for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
. This implies that
H^{\infty}_{v_{k}}\subset H^{0}_{v_{k+1}}
and that the inclusion is in fact compact. Consequently, the space
VH(\mathbb{D})
satisfies
VH(\mathbb{D})=\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}H^{0}_{v_{k}}
, it is a (DFS)-space and its topology can be described by weighted sup-seminorms, see
[
11
]
,
[
23
]
,
[
30
]
. In fact in
[
30
]
and
[
23
]
the space
VH(\mathbb{D})
is introduced with a family of weighted sup-seminorms. We refer the reader to
[
8
]
and
[
26
]
for more information about (DFS)-spaces. Every bounded subset of
VH(\mathbb{D})
is relatively compact and it is contained and relatively compact in a step
H^{\infty}_{v_{k}}
. In particular,
VH(\mathbb{D})
is a Montel space. More information about this space can be found in
[
23
]
,
[
30
]
. For example, this space is a topological algebra which is Schwartz but not nuclear.

The Banach space
H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
of bounded analytic functions on
\mathbb{D}
is contained in
VH(\mathbb{D})
. In fact
H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})\subset H^{\infty}_{v_{1}}
with continuous inclusion. Usually functions
g\in H^{\infty}_{v_{1}}
are called functions with logarithmic mean growth. Every analytic function
g
in the Bloch space
\mathcal{B}
has logarithmic mean growth, see e.g. page 106 in
[
31
]
. There are analytic functions
g\notin\mathcal{B}
which have logarithmic mean growth
[
19
]
.

On the other hand, for each
\gamma>0
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
, there is
C_{\gamma,k}>0
such that
w_{\gamma}(r)=(1-r)^{\gamma}\leq C_{\gamma,k}v_{k}(z)
for each
z\in\mathbb{D}
. Therefore
VH(\mathbb{D})\subset\bigcap_{\gamma>0}A^{-\gamma}=\bigcap_{\gamma>0}A^{-\gamma}_{0}
.

The fact that
\mathsf{C}(\mathbf{1})(z)=(1/z)\log(1/(1-z))
indicates that the space
VH(\mathbb{D})
is a natural frame to investigate the Cesàro operator on it. We show in Proposition
2.2
that the Cesàro operator
\mathsf{C}
is continuous on
VH(\mathbb{D})
, but it does not act continuously in any of its steps. Its spectrum is exhibited in our main result Theorem
2.4
. As a consequence we deduce that
\mathsf{C}
is not compact, not power bounded, not mean ergodic and not supercyclic in Corollary
2.5
and Proposition
2.6
. The behaviour of the generalized Cesàro operator
C_{t},\ 0\leq t<1,
on
VH(\mathbb{D})
is investigated in section
3
. In contrast with
\mathsf{C}
, this operator is compact, power bounded and uniformly mean ergodic.

2
Cesàro operator.

In this section we investigate the continuity, the spectrum and the ergodic properties of the Cesàro operator
\mathsf{C}
when it acts on the space
VH(\mathbb{D})
studied by Jasiczak and Taskinen.

The following consequence of Grothendieck’s factorization Theorem
[
26
, Theorem 24.33]
is well-known.

Lemma 2.1

Let
X={\rm ind}_{k}X_{k}
be an (LB)-space. A linear operator
T:X\rightarrow X
is continuous if and only if for each
k
there is
m\geq k
such that
T:X_{k}\rightarrow X_{m}
is continuous.

Proposition 2.2

The Cesàro operator
\mathsf{C}:VH(\mathbb{D})\rightarrow VH(\mathbb{D})
is continuous, but for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
it does not act continuously on
H^{\infty}_{v_{k}}
.

Proof.

We begin with the following estimate for
f\in H(\mathbb{D})
and
0<|z|<1
:

|\mathsf{C}f(z)|=\big|\frac{1}{z}\int_{0}^{z}\frac{f(\zeta)}{1-\zeta}d\zeta\big|=\big|\int_{0}^{1}\frac{f(tz)}{1-tz}dt\big|\leq

\leq M(f,|z|)\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{|1-tz|}dt\leq M(f,|z|)\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{1-t|z|}dt=M(f,|z|)\frac{1}{|z|}\log\Big(\frac{1}{1-|z|}\Big).

This implies, for each
0<r<1
,

M(\mathsf{C}f,r)\leq M(f,r)\frac{1}{r}\log\Big(\frac{1}{1-r}\Big).

In particular,
\mathsf{C}
is continuous on
H(\mathbb{D})
, as is known
[
27
]
.

Now we show that
\mathsf{C}(H^{\infty}_{v_{k}})\subset H^{\infty}_{v_{k+1}}
for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Fix
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
f\in H^{\infty}_{v_{k}}
. There is
C>0
such that
|f(z)|\leq C/v_{k}(z)
for each
z\in\mathbb{D}
. If
|z|\geq 1-1/e
, then

|\mathsf{C}f(z)|\leq M(f,|z|)\frac{1}{|z|}\log\Big(\frac{1}{1-|z|}\Big)\leq\frac{C}{1-1/e}\frac{1}{v_{k+1}(z)}.

For
|z|=1-1/e
we have
|\mathsf{C}f(z)|\leq C/(1-1/e)
. By the maximum modulus theorem,

\max_{|z|\leq 1-1/e}|\mathsf{C}f(z)|\leq C/(1-1/e).

Therefore,

\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}v_{k+1}(z)|\mathsf{C}f(z)|\leq C/(1-1/e),

and
\mathsf{C}f\in H^{\infty}_{v_{k+1}}
.

The continuity of
\mathsf{C}
on
H(\mathbb{D})
permits us to apply the closed graph theorem to conclude that
\mathsf{C}:H^{\infty}_{v_{k}}\rightarrow H^{\infty}_{v_{k+1}}
is continuous. Lemma
2.1
implies that
\mathsf{C}:VH(\mathbb{D})\rightarrow VH(\mathbb{D})
is continuous.

It remains to show that, for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, the operator
\mathsf{C}
does not act continuously in each
H^{\infty}_{v_{k}}
. To see this, observe that the function
g(z):=\log(1-z),z\in\mathbb{D},
is analytic on
\mathbb{D}
and
|g(z)|\leq-\log(1-|z|)
for each
z\in\mathbb{D}
. This yields
g^{k}\in H^{\infty}_{v_{k}}
for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Moreover,

\mathsf{C}(g^{k})(z)=-\frac{1}{(k+1)z}g(z)^{k+1},

which is an analytic function which does not belong to
H^{\infty}_{v_{k}}
, as is easy to check.
∎

Given locally convex Haudorff spaces
X,Y
we denote by
{\mathcal{L}}(X,Y)
the space of all continuous linear operators from
X
into
Y
. If
X=Y
, then we simply write
{\mathcal{L}}(X)
for
{\mathcal{L}}(X,X)
. A linear map
T\colon X\to Y
is called
compact
if there exists a neighbourhood
U
of
0
in
X
such that
T(U)
is a relatively compact set in
Y
. It is clear that necessarily
T\in{\mathcal{L}}(X,Y)
.

Given a locally convex Hausdorff space
X
and
T\in{\mathcal{L}}(X)
, the resolvent set
\rho(T;X)
of
T
consists of all
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
such that the resolvent
R(\lambda,T):=(\lambda I-T)^{-1}
exists in
{\mathcal{L}}(X)
. The set
\sigma(T;X):=\mathbb{C}\setminus\rho(T;X)
is called the
spectrum
of
T
. The
point spectrum

\sigma_{pt}(T;X)
of
T
consists of all
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
(also called eigenvalues of
T
) such that
\lambda I-T
is not injective. For the spectral theory of compact operators in locally convex spaces we refer to
[
18
,
21
]
, for example. In particular, by
[
18
, Theorem 9.10.2]
, the spectrum
\sigma(T;X)
of a compact operator
T\in{\mathcal{L}}(X)
is either finite or is a countable sequence converging to
0
, every non-zero
\lambda\in\sigma(T;X)
is an eigenvalue of
T
and the associated spectral manifold
X_{\lambda}:=\{x\in X\ |\ Tx=\lambda x\}
is of finite dimension.

The proof of our next result requires some preliminaries from
[
6
]
and
[
27
]
about the resolvent
R(\lambda,\mathsf{C})
, in particular for
{\rm Re}\,\lambda=0,\ \lambda\neq 0
. In the notation of
[
6
]
, the Cesàro operator
\mathsf{C}
is the generalized Cesàro operator
\mathsf{C}_{g}(z):=(1/z)\int_{0}^{z}f(\zeta)g^{\prime}(\zeta)d\zeta
for an analytic function
g\in H(\mathbb{D})
with
g^{\prime}(z)=1/(1-z)
. In order to find the solution
f
of the resolvent equation
\lambda f-\mathsf{C}_{g}f=h,\ h\in H(\mathbb{D})
, the analytic function
u
in
\mathbb{D}\setminus(-1,0]
is introduced in
[
6
]
(2.3)

u(z)=z^{g^{\prime}(0)}\exp\Big(\int_{0}^{z}\frac{g^{\prime}(\zeta)-g^{\prime}(0)}{\zeta}\Big),\ \ z\in\mathbb{D}\setminus(-1,0].

This function satisfies
u(z)=z/(1-z)
if
g^{\prime}(z)=1/(1-z)
. For non positive integers
\alpha
, the powers
z^{\alpha}=\exp(\alpha\log_{0}(z))
are defined using the principal branch of the logarithm
\log_{0}(z)=\log|z|+i\ {\rm arg}\ z
with
{\rm arg}\ z\in(-\pi,\pi)
and
z\in\mathbb{D}\setminus(-1,0]
.

Given an analytic function
h
on
\mathbb{D}
we denote by
m_{0}(h)
the multiplicity of
0
at the origin, and
m_{0}(h)=0
if
h(0)\neq 0
. We state
[
6
, Proposition 2.1 (i)]
since it will be used in the proof of Theorem
2.4
.

Proposition 2.3

(
[
6
, Proposition 2.1 (i)]
) Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
. If
h\in H(\mathbb{D})
satisfies
m_{0}(h)>{\rm Re}\frac{g^{\prime}(0)}{\lambda}-1
, then the equation
\lambda f-\mathsf{C}_{g}f=h
has the analytic solution
f
in
\mathbb{D}
given by

f(z)=R(\lambda,\mathsf{C})h(z)=\frac{h(z)}{\lambda}+\frac{u(z)^{1/\lambda}}{\lambda^{2}z}\int_{0}^{z}u(\zeta)^{-1/\lambda}g^{\prime}(\zeta)h(\zeta)d\zeta.

An inspection of the proof shows that
f(z)
is an analytic function on
\mathbb{D}
extending the function given by this formula on
\mathbb{D}\setminus(-1,0]
defined using the principal branch of the logarithm.

As a consequence of Proposition
2.3
, the resolvent of the Cesàro operator
\mathsf{C}
, that is for
g^{\prime}(z)=1/(1-z)
, satisfies

R(\lambda,\mathsf{C})h(z)=\frac{h(z)}{\lambda}+\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}z^{(1/\lambda)-1}(1-z)^{-1/\lambda}\int_{0}^{z}\zeta^{-1/\lambda}(1-\zeta)^{(1/\lambda)-1}h(\zeta)d\zeta,

if
m_{0}(h)>{\rm Re}\frac{1}{\lambda}-1
, since
u(z)=z/(1-z)
. This is precisely the formula
(2.7)
in
[
27
]
.

Theorem 2.4

The Cesàro operator
\mathsf{C}:VH(\mathbb{D})\rightarrow VH(\mathbb{D})
satisfies

(i)

\sigma_{pt}(\mathsf{C};VH(\mathbb{D}))=\emptyset
.

(ii)

\sigma(\mathsf{C};VH(\mathbb{D}))={0}\cup\{\lambda\ |\ {\rm Re}\,\lambda>0\}
.

Proof.

(i) By Section 2 in
[
27
]
, the eigenvalues
\lambda
of
\mathsf{C}
on
H(\mathbb{D})
are
\lambda=1/n,\ n\in\mathbb{N}
with associated eigenvector
e_{n}(z):=z^{n-1}(1-z)^{-n}
. Moreover, every eigenvector of
\lambda=1/n
is a multiple of
e_{n}
. It is easy to see that
e_{n}\notin VH(\mathbb{D})
. Consequently,
\sigma_{pt}(\mathsf{C};VH(\mathbb{D}))=\emptyset
.

(ii) We first show that
0\in\sigma(\mathsf{C};VH(\mathbb{D}))
. The inverse of
\mathsf{C}
on
H(\mathbb{D})
is given by
\mathsf{C}^{-1}f(z)=(1-z)(zf(z))^{\prime}

[
27
, page 1185]
. The function
h(z):=\log(1+z),z\in\mathbb{D},
belongs to
H^{\infty}_{v_{1}}\subset VH(\mathbb{D})
, but

\mathsf{C}^{-1}h(z)=(1-z)h(z)+z\frac{1-z}{1+z}

does not belong to
VH(\mathbb{D})
. Otherwise
z\frac{1-z}{1+z}\in VH(\mathbb{D})
. This is not possible, because

\lim_{r\rightarrow-1}\Big|r\frac{1-r}{1+r}\Big||\log(1-r)|^{-k}=\infty

for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

The image of
((1/n)I-\mathsf{C})
does not contain the function
z^{n-1}
, see
[
27
, page 1184]
. Then
1/n\in\sigma(\mathsf{C};VH(\mathbb{D}))
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

If
{\rm Re}\,\lambda>0
and
\lambda\neq 1/n
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, then select
\gamma>0
such that
{\rm Re}\,(1/\lambda)\geq\gamma>0
and
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that
m<{\rm Re}\,(1/\lambda)<m+1
. By
[
27
, Proposition 4]
and its proof, there is a unique solution
e_{\lambda}\in H(\mathbb{D})
of
(\lambda\mathsf{C}-I)e_{\lambda}=1
and it satisfies
\lim_{|z|\rightarrow 1}|e_{\lambda}(z)|(1-|z|)^{\gamma}\neq 0
, then
e_{\lambda}\notin A^{-\gamma}_{0}
. Therefore,
e_{\lambda}\notin VH(\mathbb{D})
, and
\lambda\in\sigma(\mathsf{C};VH(\mathbb{D}))
.

We now fix
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
with
{\rm Re}\,\lambda<0
. As in
[
27
, Section 3]
, we consider the functions

\varphi_{t}(z):=\frac{e^{-t}z}{(e^{-t}-1)z+1},\ \ z\in\mathbb{D},\ t\geq 0,

and the operators, for
f\in VH(\mathbb{D})
,

S_{t}(f)(z):=\frac{\varphi_{t}(z)}{z}f(\varphi_{t}(z)),\ \ z\in\mathbb{D},\ t\geq 0.

The map
\varphi_{t}
is analytic on
\mathbb{D}
and satisfies
\varphi_{t}(\mathbb{D})\subset\mathbb{D}
and
\varphi_{t}(0)=0
. By Schwarz Lemma,
|\varphi_{t}(z)|\leq|z|
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
. We can apply
[
15
, proposition 3.1]
(see also
[
12
, Theorem 51]
) to conclude that
S_{t}:H^{\infty}_{v_{k}}\rightarrow H^{\infty}_{v_{k}}
is continuous for each
k
and
\|S_{t}\|\leq 1
.

Since
{\rm Re}\,\lambda<0
, we have
{\rm Re}\,(1/\lambda)<0
and we conclude that the identity
(3.16)
in
[
27
]
for the resolvent of the Cesàro operator holds for
h\in VH(\mathbb{D})
:

R(\lambda,\mathsf{C})h(z)=\frac{h(z)}{\lambda}+\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{t/\lambda}S_{t}h(z)dt.

Therefore, for each
h\in H^{\infty}_{v_{k}}
and each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we get

\|R(\lambda,\mathsf{C})h\|_{v_{k}}\leq\frac{\|h\|_{v_{k}}}{|\lambda|}+\frac{\|h\|_{v_{k}}}{|\lambda|^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{t{\rm Re}\,(1/\lambda)}\|S_{t}\|dt\leq C_{\lambda}\|h\|_{v_{k}},

because
||S_{t}||\leq 1
and
\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{t{\rm Re}\,(1/\lambda)}dt<\infty
.

This implies that
R(\lambda,\mathsf{C}):VH(\mathbb{D})\rightarrow VH(\mathbb{D})
is continuous and
\{\lambda\ :\ {\rm Re}\,\lambda<0\}\subset\rho(\mathsf{C};VH(\mathbb{D}))
.

It remains to consider the case
{\rm Re}\,\lambda=0,\ \lambda\neq 0
. In this case, the multiplicity
m_{0}(h)
of the zero of each analytic function
h\in VH(\mathbb{D})
at the origin is greater that
{\rm Re}\,(1/\lambda)-1=-1
and we can apply Proposition
2.3
(
[
6
, Proposition 2.1 (i)]
) and
(2.7)
in
[
27
]
to get, for
h\in H^{\infty}_{v_{k}}
,

R(\lambda,\mathsf{C})h(z)=\frac{h(z)}{\lambda}+\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}z^{(1/\lambda)-1}(1-z)^{-1/\lambda}\int_{0}^{z}\zeta^{-1/\lambda}(1-\zeta)^{(1/\lambda)-1}h(\zeta)d\zeta.

If we write
\lambda=ib,b\in\mathbb{R},b\neq 0,
then
1/\lambda=-i/b
, and this formula becomes

R(\lambda,\mathsf{C})h(z)=\frac{h(z)}{ib}-\frac{1}{b^{2}}z^{(-i/b)-1}(1-z)^{i/b}\int_{0}^{z}\zeta^{i/b}(1-\zeta)^{(-i/b)-1}h(\zeta)d\zeta.

This is an analytic function on
\mathbb{D}
extending the function defined by this formula on
\mathbb{D}\setminus(-1,0]
using the principal branch of the logarithm. We estimate the integral for
z\in\mathbb{D}\setminus(-1,0]
. First of all

\int_{0}^{z}\zeta^{i/b}(1-\zeta)^{(-i/b)-1}h(\zeta)d\zeta=z^{(i/b)+1}\int_{0}^{1}t^{i/b}(1-tz)^{-(i/b)-1}h(tz)dt.

For each
\xi\in\mathbb{D}\setminus(-1,0]
, since we use the principal branch of the logarithm with
{\rm arg}z\in(-\pi,\pi)
, we get

|\xi^{i/b}|=|\exp((i/b)(\log|\xi|+i\ {\rm arg}\ \xi)|=|\exp(-{\rm arg}\ \xi/b)|\leq\exp(\pi/|b|),

and similarly
|\xi^{-i/b}|\leq\exp(\pi/|b|)
.
Accordingly, for
z\in\mathbb{D}\setminus(-1,0]
, we get the estimate

\Big|\int_{0}^{z}\zeta^{i/b}(1-\zeta)^{(-i/b)-1}h(\zeta)d\zeta\Big|\leq\exp(2\pi/|b|)|z|\int_{0}^{1}|1-tz|^{-1}|h(tz)|dt\leq

\leq\exp(2\pi/|b|)|z|\max_{|\zeta|=|z|}|h(\zeta)|\int_{0}^{1}|1-tz|^{-1}dt\leq\exp(2\pi/|b|)|z|(-\log(1-|z|))\max_{|\zeta|=|z|}|h(\zeta)|.

Then, for
z\in\mathbb{D}\setminus(-1,0]
with
|z|\geq 1-1/e
,

v_{k+1}(z)|R(\lambda,\mathsf{C})h(z)|\leq v_{k+1}(z)\frac{|h(z)|}{|b|}+v_{k+1}(z)\frac{1}{b^{2}|z|}\exp(4\pi/|b|)|z|(-\log(1-|z|))\max_{|\zeta|=|z|}|h(\zeta)|\leq

\leq v_{k}(z)\Big(\frac{1}{|b|}+\exp(4\pi/|b|)\frac{1}{b^{2}}\Big)\max_{|\zeta|=|z|}|h(\zeta)|.

Since
R(\lambda,\mathsf{C})h
is an analytic function on
\mathbb{D}
satisfying this estimate for
z\in\mathbb{D}\setminus(-1,0]
, we conclude

\|R(\lambda,\mathsf{C})h\|_{v_{k+1}}\leq\Big(\frac{1}{|b|}+\exp(4\pi/|b|)\frac{1}{b^{2}}\Big)\|h\|_{v_{k}},

and the resolvent operator
R(\lambda,\mathsf{C}):VH(\mathbb{D})\rightarrow VH(\mathbb{D})
is also continuous in this case.
∎

Observe that the methods utilized in
[
2
]
to determine the spectrum of
\mathsf{C}
on Korenblum type (LB)-spaces of analytic funtions cannot be used in the present setting, because
\mathsf{C}
is not continuous on the steps of the inductive limit. This is why direct arguments are necessary.

Corollary 2.5

The Cesàro operator
\mathsf{C}:VH(\mathbb{D})\rightarrow VH(\mathbb{D})
is not compact.

Proof.

This is a direct consequence of Theorem
2.4
and the shape of the spectrum of compact operators, see
[
18
, Theorem 9.10.2]
.
∎

Let
X
be a locally convex Hausdorff space. An operator
T\in{\mathcal{L}}(X)
is called
power bounded
if the sequence of iterates
\{T^{n}:\ n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\}
is an equicontinuous subset of
{\mathcal{L}}(X)
. For a Banach space
X
, this means that
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\|T^{n}\|<\infty
.

The space
{\mathcal{L}}(X)
equipped with the topology of pointwise convergence on
X
(i.e., the strong operator topology) is denoted by
{\mathcal{L}}_{s}(X)
and when it is equipped with the topology
\tau_{b}
of uniform convergence on the bounded subsets of
X
it is denoted by
{\mathcal{L}}_{b}(X)
.

Given
T\in{\mathcal{L}}(X)
, the averages

T_{[n]}:=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{m=1}^{n}T^{m},\quad n\in\mathbb{N},

are usually called the Cesàro means of
T
. The operator
T
is said to be
mean ergodic
(resp.,
uniformly mean ergodic
) if
(T_{[n]})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a convergent sequence in
{\mathcal{L}}_{s}(X)
(resp., in
{\mathcal{L}}_{b}(X)
). It is easy to see that
\frac{T^{n}}{n}=T_{[n]}-\frac{n-1}{n}T_{[n-1]}
, for
n\geq 2
, and hence,
\tau_{s}
-
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{T^{n}}{n}=0
whenever
T
is mean ergodic. Every power bounded operator on a Montel space
X
is necessarily uniformly mean ergodic,
[
13
, Proposition 5.11]
.

Concerning the linear dynamics of
T\in{\mathcal{L}}(X)
, with
X
a lcHs, the operator
T\in{\mathcal{L}}(X)
is called
hypercyclic
if there exists
x\in X
such that the orbit
\{T^{n}x\colon n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\}

is dense in
X
. If, for some
z\in X
, the projective orbit
\{\lambda T^{n}z\colon\lambda\in\mathbb{C},\ n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\}
is dense
in
X
, then
T
is called
supercyclic
. Clearly, hypercyclicity implies supercyclicity.

More details for mean ergodic operators can be seen in
[
13
,
17
,
24
]
, and for linear dynamics in
[
7
,
20
]
.

Proposition 2.6

The Cesàro operator
\mathsf{C}:VH(\mathbb{D})\rightarrow VH(\mathbb{D})
is not power bounded, not mean ergodic and not supercyclic.

Proof.

By Yosida’s Theorem
[
13
, Theorem 5.5]
, if
\mathsf{C}:VH(\mathbb{D})\rightarrow VH(\mathbb{D})
is mean ergodic, then

VH(\mathbb{D})={\rm ker}(I-\mathsf{C})\oplus\overline{{\rm Im}(I-\mathsf{C})}.

Theorem
2.4
(i) implies that
{\rm ker}(I-\mathsf{C})=\{0\}
. On the other hand
\overline{{\rm Im}(I-\mathsf{C})}\subset\{f\in VH(\mathbb{D})\ |\ f(0)=0\}
, which is a proper closed hyperplane of
VH(\mathbb{D})
. This implies that
\mathsf{C}:VH(\mathbb{D})\rightarrow VH(\mathbb{D})
is not mean ergodic.

On the other hand, if
\mathsf{C}:VH(\mathbb{D})\rightarrow VH(\mathbb{D})
were power bounded, then it would be uniformly mean ergodic, since
VH(\mathbb{D})
is a Montel space. We have just seen that this operator is not mean ergodic.

The Cesàro operator
\mathsf{C}:H(\mathbb{D})\rightarrow H(\mathbb{D})
is not supercyclic by
[
3
, proposition 3.5]
and the space
VH(\mathbb{D})
is dense in
H(\mathbb{D})
since it contains the polynomials. The comparison principle
[
7
, Subsection 1.1.1]
ensures that
\mathsf{C}:VH(\mathbb{D})\rightarrow VH(\mathbb{D})
is not supercyclic.
∎

3
Generalized Cesàro operators.

For
t\in[0,1]
the generalized Cesàro operator
C_{t}\colon H(\mathbb{D})\to H(\mathbb{D})
, for
f\in H(\mathbb{D})
, is defined by

C_{t}f(0):=f(0)

and

C_{t}f(z):=\frac{1}{z}\int_{0}^{z}\frac{f(\zeta)}{1-t\zeta}\,d\zeta,\ z\in\mathbb{D}\setminus\{0\},

(3.1)

The operator
C_{t}
also has the following representation

\displaystyle C_{t}f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{t^{n}a_{0}+t^{n-1}a_{1}+\ldots+a_{n}}{n+1}\right)z^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{t^{n}\hat{f}(0)+t^{n-1}\hat{f}(1)+\ldots+\hat{f}(n)}{n+1}\right)z^{n},

(3.2)

where the coefficients of the series are precisely those of the discrete generalized Cesàro operator, which is defined for
x\in\mathbb{C}^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
by

C_{t}x:=\left(\frac{t^{n}x_{0}+t^{n-1}x_{1}+\ldots+x_{n}}{n+1}\right)_{n},\quad x=(x_{n})_{n}\in\mathbb{C}^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}.

The operator
C_{0}
is given by
C_{0}f(z)=\frac{1}{z}\int_{0}^{z}f(\zeta)\,d\zeta
for
z\not=0
and
C_{0}f(0)=f(0)
, which is the classical Hardy operator in
H(\mathbb{D})
, and the operator
C_{1}
is precisely the Cesàro operator
\mathsf{C}
investigated in Section
2
.

The (discrete) generalized Cesàro operators
C_{t}
, for
t\in[0,1]
, were first investigated by Rhaly,
[
28
]
,
[
29
]
. We refer the reader to the introduction of the paper
[
16
]
and the references therein. The behaviour of
C_{t},0\leq t<1
on
H(\mathbb{D})
and on the weighted spaces
H_{v}^{\infty}
and
H_{v}^{0}
was investigated recently in
[
5
]
. The following results are proved in this article.

Proposition 3.1

(i)

(
[
5
, Propositions 2.1 and 3.3.]
) For every
t\in[0,1]
the operator
C_{t}\colon H(\mathbb{D})\to H(\mathbb{D})
is a surjective continuous isomorphism.

(ii)

(
[
5
, Propositions 2.5 and 2.7]
) For each weight function
v
and for each
t\in[0,1)
the operators
C_{t}\colon H_{v}^{\infty}\to H_{v}^{\infty}
and
C_{t}\colon H^{0}_{v}\to H^{0}_{v}
are continuous and even compact.

Proposition

2.1
imply

Proposition 3.2

For every
t\in[0,1)
the operator
C_{t}\colon VH(\mathbb{D})\to VH(\mathbb{D})
is continuous.

The following abstract result is well known. We briefly indicate the proof for the sake of completeness.

Lemma 3.3

Let
X=\ind_{k}X_{k}
be a Hausdorff inductive limit of a sequence of Banach spaces
(X_{k},\|\cdot\|_{k})
such that the inclusion
X_{k}\subset X_{k+1}
is compact. An operator
T\in{\mathcal{L}}(X)
is compact if and only if there is
m
such that for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
we have
T(X_{k})\subset X_{m}
.

Proof.

Assume first that
T
is compact. There is a
0
-neighbourhood
U
in
X
such that
T(U)
is relatively compact in
X
. Since all the inclusions
X_{k}\subset X_{k+1}
are compact, the (LB)-space
X
is a (DFS)-space, hence there is
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that
T(U)
is contained and relatively compact in
X_{m}
. This implies
T(X)\subset X_{m}
, and
T(X_{k})\subset X_{m}
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

Now we suppose that
T\in{\mathcal{L}}(X)
satisfies
T(X_{k})\subset X_{m}
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. The continuity of
T:X\rightarrow X
and of the inclusions
X_{k}\subset X
for all
k
imply that
T:X_{k}\rightarrow X_{m}
has closed graph. By the closed graph theorem,
T:X_{k}\rightarrow X_{m}
is continuous for each
k
. Let us denote by
B_{k}
the closed unit ball of
X_{k}
. For each
k
there is
\mu_{k}>0
such that
T(\mu_{k}B_{k})\subset B_{m}
. Then, the absolutely convex hull
U
of
\bigcup_{k}\mu_{k}B_{k}
is a
0
-neighbourhood in
X
such that
T(U)\subset B_{m}
. Since the inclusion
X_{m}\subset X_{m+1}
is compact,
T(U)
is relatively compact in
X_{m+1}
, consequently in
X
, and
T:X\rightarrow X
is indeed a compact operator.
∎

Proposition 3.4

For every
t\in[0,1)
the operator
C_{t}:VH(\mathbb{D})\to VH(\mathbb{D})
is compact.

Proof.

We consider the standard weight
w_{1}(r):=1-r,\ 0\leq r<1
and the weight
v_{1}(z)=1
if
|z|\leq 1-1/e
, and
v_{1}(z)=(-\log(1-r))^{-1}
if
|z|\geq 1-1/e
and we show that
C_{t}:H_{w_{1}}^{\infty}\to H_{v_{1}}^{\infty}
is continuous. Indeed, given
f\in H_{w_{1}}^{\infty},\ ||f||_{w_{1}}\leq 1
, we have for
|z|\geq 1-1/e

|C_{t}f(z)|=\Big|\frac{1}{z}\int_{0}^{z}\frac{f(\zeta)}{1-t\zeta}\,d\zeta\Big|=\Big|\int_{0}^{1}\frac{f(sz)}{1-tsz}\,ds\Big|\leq\int_{0}^{1}\frac{|f(sz)|}{|1-tsz|}\,ds.

Since
|f(sz)|\leq 1/(1-s|z|)
and
|1-tsz|\geq 1-t
, we can continue to estimate as follows

|C_{t}f(z)|\leq\frac{1}{1-t}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{1-s|z|}\,ds=\frac{1}{1-t}\Big(-\frac{\log(1-|z|)}{|z|}\Big)\leq\frac{1}{(1-t)(1-1/e)}\frac{1}{v_{1}(z)}.

Consequently,

\sup_{|z|\geq 1-1/e}v_{1}(z)|C_{t}f(z)|\leq\frac{1}{(1-t)(1-1/e)}

for each
f\in H_{w_{1}}^{\infty},\ ||f||_{w_{1}}\leq 1
, and this implies the continuity of
C_{t}:H_{w_{1}}^{\infty}\to H_{v_{1}}^{\infty}
.

Now, each Banach space
H_{v_{k}}^{\infty}
is continuously included in
H_{w_{1}}^{\infty}
, hence in
H_{v_{1}}^{\infty}
. The compactness of

C_{t}:VH(\mathbb{D})\to VH(\mathbb{D})
now follows from Proposition
3.2
and Lemma
3.3
.
∎

Proposition 3.5

For each
t\in[0,1)
the operator
C_{t}:VH(\mathbb{D})\to VH(\mathbb{D})
satisfies:

(i)

\sigma_{pt}(C_{t},VH(\mathbb{D}))=\left\{\frac{1}{m+1}\,:m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\right\}
.

(ii)

\sigma(C_{t},VH(\mathbb{D}))=\left\{\frac{1}{m+1}\,:m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\right\}\cup\{0\}
.

Proof.

Fix
t\in[0,1)
.

(i) The operator
C_{t}:H(\mathbb{D})\to H(\mathbb{D})
is continuous and its point spectrum satisfies
\sigma_{pt}(C_{t},H(\mathbb{D}))=\left\{\frac{1}{m+1}\,:m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\right\}
by
[
5
, Proposition 3.7]
. Since
VH(\mathbb{D})\subset H(\mathbb{D})
with continuous inclusion, it follows that

\sigma_{pt}(C_{t},VH(\mathbb{D}))\subset\left\{\frac{1}{m+1}\,:n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\right\}
.

On the other hand, for each
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, the corresponding eigenspace
{\rm ker}(\frac{1}{m+1}I-C_{t})
is 1-dimensional and is generated by an eigenvector
g_{m}(z):=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(x^{[m]})_{n}z^{n}
with
(x_{n}^{[m]})_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\in\ell^{1}
. See the proofs of
[
5
, Propositions 2.8 and 3.7]
. Then
g_{m}(z)\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})\subset VH(\mathbb{D})
and
\frac{1}{m+1}\in\sigma_{pt}(C_{t},VH(\mathbb{D}))
for each
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.

(ii) This follows from part (i) and the fact that
C_{t}:VH(\mathbb{D})\to VH(\mathbb{D})
is a compact operator. See
[
18
, Theorem 9.10.2]
.
∎

Lemma 3.6

Let
X
be a complete (LB)-space. Let
T\in{\mathcal{L}}(X)
be a compact operator such that
1\in\sigma(T;X)
,
\sigma(T;X)\setminus\{1\}\subseteq\overline{B(0,\delta)}
for some
\delta\in(0,1)
and satisfying
{\rm ker}(I-T)\cap{\rm Im}(I-T)(X)=\{0\}
. Then
T
is power bounded and uniformly mean ergodic.

Proposition 3.7

For every
t\in[0,1)
the operator
C_{t}:VH(\mathbb{D})\rightarrow VH(\mathbb{D})
is power bounded, uniformly mean ergodic, but it is not supercyclic.

Proof.

We check that all the assumptions of Lemma
3.6
are satisfied to conclude that
C_{t}
is power bounded and uniformly mean ergodic on
VH(\mathbb{D})
.

It is easy to see that
{\rm ker}(I-C_{t})={\rm span}\{g_{0}\}
, with
g_{0}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}t^{n}z^{n}\in VH(\mathbb{D})
, for
z\in\mathbb{D}
, see
[
5
]
. On the other hand,
{\rm Im}(I-C_{t})
is a closed subspace of
VH(\mathbb{D})
since
C_{t}
is compact on
VH(\mathbb{D})
by Proposition
3.4
, and
{\rm Im}(I-C_{t})\subset\{g\in VH(\mathbb{D})\,:\,g(0)=0\}
. Moreover,
[
18
, Theorem 9.10.1]
implies that
{\rm codim}\,{\rm Im}(I-C_{t})={\rm dim}\,{\rm ker}(I-C_{t})=1
. Accordingly, both
{\rm Im}(I-C_{t})
and
\{g\in H^{\infty}_{v}\,:\,g(0)=0\}
are hyperplanes of
VH(\mathbb{D})
. Hence
{\rm Im}(I-C_{t})=\{g\in H^{\infty}_{v}\,:\,g(0)=0\}
.

Now, if
h\in{\rm Im}(I-C_{t})\cap{\rm ker}(I-C_{t})
, then
h(0)=0
and there exists
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
such that
h=\lambda g_{0}
. This yields that
0=h(0)=\lambda g_{0}(0)=\lambda
. Hence,
{\rm Im}(I-C_{t})\cap{\rm ker}(I-C_{t})=\{0\}
.

Finally, Proposition
3.5
implies that
1\in\sigma(C_{t},VH(\mathbb{D}))=\{\frac{1}{m+1}\,;\,m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\}\cup\{0\}
. Consequently,
\sigma(C_{t},VH(\mathbb{D}))\setminus\{1\}\subset\overline{B(0,1/2)}
.

The operator
C_{t}:H(\mathbb{D})\rightarrow H(\mathbb{D})
is not supercyclic for each
t\in[0,1)
by
[
5
, Theorem 3.8]
. The comparison principle
[
7
, Subsection 1.1.1]
ensures that
C_{t}:VH(\mathbb{D})\rightarrow VH(\mathbb{D})
is not supercyclic.
∎

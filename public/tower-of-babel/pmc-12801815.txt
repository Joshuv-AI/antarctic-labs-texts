Unexpected pyomyositis in a healthy adult: diagnostic hurdles and multifaceted treatment approaches—a case report
Authors: Muhammad Saad, Muhammad Tabish Ikram, Abbas Khan, Haseeb Hayat, Saeed Anwar, Ammar Ahmad
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPyomyositis, traditionally prevalent in tropical regions, is increasingly diagnosed in temperate climates and among healthy individuals. This condition’s nonspecific symptoms often complicate its diagnosis, necessitating advanced imaging for precise identification. This case underscores the novel occurrence of pyomyositis in a healthy individual from a temperate region and highlights the aggressive nature of methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus.Case presentationWe report a case of a 32-year-old healthy Pakistani male who presented with localized swelling in the right deltoid and forearm, accompanied by high-grade fever and systemic symptoms. Imaging revealed extensive abscess formation, and microbiological cultures identified methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus as the causative agent. Initial treatment with linezolid showed partial improvement; however, recurrent abscesses required surgical drainage. Following this combined treatment approach, the patient showed significant recovery.ConclusionThis case emphasizes the importance of prompt diagnosis and intervention in pyomyositis, even in otherwise healthy individuals. It supports the use of integrated therapeutic approaches, including antimicrobial therapy and surgical intervention, for managing complex bacterial infections.Keywords: Pyomyositis, Methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus (MRSA), Temperate climates, Skeletal muscle infection

Introduction

Pyomyositis is a rare bacterial infection affecting skeletal muscles, leading to abscess formation. Diagnosing it can be challenging due to nonspecific symptoms and its ability to mimic various conditions [1]. While historically more prevalent in tropical regions (around 70% of cases), pyomyositis is increasingly reported in temperate climates and among immunocompromised individuals worldwide [2]. Common bacterial culprits include Staphylococcus aureus and Streptococcus species. The infection can rapidly progress from a localized muscle infection to severe sepsis [3].

There are two main classifications of pyomyositis: primary and secondary [4].

Primary pyomyositis: bacteria enter the bloodstream, either from an unknown source or another infection site. This type, previously called “tropical myositis” or “tropical pyomyositis,” is now referred to as primary pyomyositis regardless of location. The only differentiating factors between tropical and nontropical cases are the location and specific risk factors for infection [2].

The extremities, hip, and pelvic muscles are commonly affected. Multifocal involvement is rare [6].

The delayed diagnosis of pyomyositis, typically occurring 7–10 days after admission, emphasizes the need for increased clinical suspicion and advanced diagnostic imaging techniques [7]. Treatment strategies typically involve surgical drainage of abscesses alongside targeted antibiotic therapy, with the duration tailored to individual responses [7].

An interesting case of pyomyositis observed at Hayatabad Medical Complex in Peshawar involved an immunocompetent patient. This case highlights the atypical presentation of the condition and underscores the critical importance of prompt recognition and management, especially in healthcare settings outside tropical regions.

Case presentation

A 32-year-old Pakistani male from Pakhtun background with no past medical or surgical history presented to the outpatient department with a 1-week history of localized, tender swelling in the right deltoid and right forearm and a 3-day history of high-grade fever (103–104 °F) associated with rigors, chills, and generalized body aches. The swellings had a sudden onset and gradually increased in size. The patient denied any history of rash, eye pain, oral ulcers, hair loss, joint pain, or bluish discoloration of the fingers. There was no history of immunosuppressive drug use, diabetes, high-risk sexual behaviors, or exposure to infectious agents. The source of infection could not be definitively identified; however, MRSA is known to spread via transient bacteremia, often without a clear portal of entry.

The patient’s immune status was assessed thoroughly, including normal immunoglobulin levels and negative human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) screening. Although CD4+ T cell count was not explicitly measured, clinical evaluation suggested no evidence of immunosuppression.

On physical examination, the patient appeared febrile. There were no signs of jaundice, anemia, cyanosis, koilonychia, enlarged lymph nodes, or edema. Musculoskeletal examination revealed tender, erythematous, fluctuant swellings in the right deltoid region (5 cm × 6 cm) and right forearm (7 cm × 5 cm), with proximal muscle weakness. Systemic examination was unremarkable.

For management, the patient was started on linezolid based on culture sensitivity results. Although vancomycin and daptomycin are also effective against MRSA, linezolid was preferred due to its lower risk of nephrotoxicity and the patient’s inability to afford routine blood monitoring required for vancomycin therapy. Initial improvement was noted, but the swellings recurred. Ultrasound-guided aspirations were performed, yet the recurrence persisted. Surgical consultation led to an incision and drainage procedure, which resulted in significant improvement. Post drainage, the patient showed resolution of fever, reduced tenderness, and restored muscle strength.

Upon discharge, the patient was prescribed a course of oral antibiotics and was scheduled for regular follow-up appointments with both infectious disease specialists and the surgical team. Emphasis was placed on the critical importance of meticulous wound care and strict adherence to the prescribed antibiotic regimen to prevent any recurrence of the infection.

Discussion

Atypical MRSA presentation in a healthy individual

A 32-year-old immunocompetent man presented with a severe case of methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus (MRSA) infection. This case offers valuable insights into managing deep-seated MRSA, particularly because it deviates from the typical presentation. The patient lacked any known underlying conditions or risk factors commonly associated with MRSA, such as recent hospitalization, immunocompromised states (HIV, diabetes, and cancer), recent trauma or injury, infections in adjacent areas, intravenous drug use, corticosteroid therapy or prior antibiotic use, consistent with Liu et al. [8]. This highlights the potential for MRSA to infect even healthy individuals, expanding the population susceptible to this dangerous bacterium.

Rapid progression and importance of imaging

The case is notable for the rapid onset and progression of symptoms. The patient experienced localized swelling, high fever, and body aches within a short period. Additionally, imaging studies revealed the formation of multiple abscesses and fluid buildup in the lungs (pleural effusion). These findings underscore the aggressive nature of the infection. Notably, ultrasound, computed tomography (CT) scans, and MRI were crucial for accurately identifying the extent and location of the abscesses, allowing for a targeted treatment plan.

Combined therapeutic approach leads to recovery

Initial treatment with the antibiotic linezolid, guided by culture and sensitivity testing, showed partial improvement. However, the recurrence of abscesses highlighted the limitations of relying solely on medication for deep-seated MRSA infections. This aligns with existing knowledge that antibiotics may not be sufficient for extensive or deep infections [9]. Consequently, surgical intervention became necessary for the patient’s recovery. The success of surgery emphasizes the importance of a combined therapeutic approach that integrates both surgical drainage and antibiotic treatment for managing complex abscesses, similar to the approach advocated by Moran et al. [10].

Aligning with current practices and future research

This case aligns with current literature that emphasizes the effectiveness of a combined approach using both antibiotics and surgery for MRSA infections. The case also reflects the rising prevalence of community-acquired MRSA (CA-MRSA) associated with skin and soft tissue infections, as discussed by Moran et al. [10]. This is noteworthy because the patient lacked traditional risk factors for CA-MRSA. Additionally, the case highlights the challenges of achieving complete MRSA eradication even with appropriate antibiotics, a hurdle mirrored in this instance where surgery was required. [11].

Educational takeaways for improved patient outcomes

This case offers valuable lessons for future clinical practice. The importance of ongoing monitoring, patient education on treatment adherence, and proper wound care cannot be overstated. These elements are crucial for preventing recurrence and complications. Educating patients on completing antibiotic regimens and proper wound care is essential, as emphasized by Moran et al. [10].

Conclusion

This case of severe MRSA infection in a previously healthy individual underscores the aggressive nature of the bacteria and the need for a multifaceted treatment approach. The recurrence of symptoms despite initial antibiotic therapy and the subsequent successful surgical intervention highlight critical aspects of patient care. This case adds to the growing body of evidence supporting a combined therapeutic approach for MRSA abscesses and emphasizes the need for further research to optimize treatment protocols for improved patient outcomes.

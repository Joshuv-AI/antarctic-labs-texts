Abstract
We present a measurement of the branching fraction and time-dependent charge-parity ( C ​ P C\!P ) decay-rate asymmetries in B 0 → J / ψ ​ π 0 B^{0}\rightarrow{J\mskip-3.0mu/\mskip-2.0mu\psi\mskip 2.0mu}\pi^{0} decays. The data sample was collected with the Belle II detector at the SuperKEKB asymmetric e + ​ e − e^{+}e^{-} collider in 2019–2022 and contains ( 387 ± 6 ) × 10 6 (387\pm 6)\times 10^{6} B ​ B ¯ B\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{} meson pairs from Υ ⁡ ( 4 ​ S ) \mathchar 28935\relax{(4S)} decays. We reconstruct 392 ± 24 392\pm 24 signal decays and fit the C ​ P C\!P parameters from the distribution of the proper-decay-time difference of the two B B mesons. We measure the branching fraction to be ℬ ⁡ ( B 0 → J / ψ ​ π 0 ) = ( 2.00 ± 0.12 ± 0.09 ) × 10 − 5 \mathcal{B}(B^{0}\rightarrow{J\mskip-3.0mu/\mskip-2.0mu\psi\mskip 2.0mu}\pi^{0})=(2.00\pm 0.12\pm 0.09)\times 10^{-5} and the direct and mixing-induced C ​ P C\!P asymmetries to be C C ​ P = 0.13 ± 0.12 ± 0.03 C_{C\!P}=0.13\pm 0.12\pm 0.03 and S C ​ P = − 0.88 ± 0.17 ± 0.03 S_{C\!P}=-0.88\pm 0.17\pm 0.03 , respectively, where the first uncertainties are statistical and the second are systematic. We observe mixing-induced C ​ P C\!P violation with a significance of 5.0 5.0 standard deviations for the first time in this mode.



I
Introduction

Precision measurements of
C\!P
asymmetries are powerful experimental tools to indirectly probe physics beyond the Standard Model (SM).
In the SM,
C\!P
violation is governed by a single complex phase in the Cabibbo-Kobayashi-Maskawa (CKM) quark mixing matrix
[
1
,
2
]
.
The unitarity of the CKM matrix can be represented as a triangle in the complex plane.
Precise measurements of the angle
\phi_{1}=\arg(-V_{cd}V_{cb}^{*}/V_{td}V_{tb}^{*})

[
3
]
, where
V_{ij}
are the CKM matrix elements, have been performed in
B^{0}\rightarrow{J\mskip-3.0mu/\mskip-2.0mu\psi\mskip 2.0mu}K^{0}
decays
[
4
,
5
,
6
]
.
These decays proceed via tree-level
b\rightarrow c\overline{c}s
transitions.
However, decay amplitudes involving the emission and reabsorption of a
W
boson, also known as “penguin” diagrams, occur at higher order in SM perturbation theory and can induce a shift in the measurement of
\phi_{1}
, thereby limiting the sensitivity of CKM fits
[
7
]
.
In the absence of penguin amplitudes, the direct and mixing-induced
C\!P
asymmetries are predicted to be
C_{{J\mskip-3.0mu/\mskip-2.0mu\psi\mskip 2.0mu}K^{0}}=0
and
-\eta S_{{J\mskip-3.0mu/\mskip-2.0mu\psi\mskip 2.0mu}K^{0}}=\sin 2\phi_{1}
, where
\eta
is the
C\!P
eigenvalue of the decay final state.
The world-average values,
C_{{J\mskip-3.0mu/\mskip-2.0mu\psi\mskip 2.0mu}K^{0}}=0.009\pm 0.010
and
-\eta S_{{J\mskip-3.0mu/\mskip-2.0mu\psi\mskip 2.0mu}K^{0}}=0.708\pm 0.012

[
8
]
, are in agreement with independent constraints on the CKM matrix
[
9
,
10
]
.

The decay
B^{0}\rightarrow{J\mskip-3.0mu/\mskip-2.0mu\psi\mskip 2.0mu}\pi^{0}
proceeds via color-suppressed
b\rightarrow c\overline{c}d
tree-level transitions and its
C\!P
asymmetries can be used to constrain the contributions from penguin topologies in
B^{0}\rightarrow{J\mskip-3.0mu/\mskip-2.0mu\psi\mskip 2.0mu}K^{0}
.
In the presence of penguin amplitudes, the observable phase measured by the mixing-induced
C\!P
asymmetry is
\phi_{d}^{\rm eff}=\phi_{d}+\Delta\phi_{d}
, where
\Delta\phi_{d}
is a shift of the order of
0.5^{\circ}
from the SM value of
\phi_{d}=2\phi_{1}

[
11
,
12
]
.
In addition, the value of the branching fraction is used to probe the size of non-factorizable
SU(3)
-breaking effects, which are the main contributions to the theoretical uncertainties in the extraction of
\Delta\phi_{d}

[
13
]
.
The world-average value of the branching fraction is based on the measurements from the
B
A
B
AR

[
14
]
,
Belle

[
15
]
, and CLEO
[
16
]
experiments, resulting in
{\cal B}(B^{0}\rightarrow{J\mskip-3.0mu/\mskip-2.0mu\psi\mskip 2.0mu}\pi^{0})=(1.66\pm 0.10)\times 10^{-5}

[
17
]
.
More recently,
LHCb
measured the ratio of branching fractions of
B^{0}\rightarrow{J\mskip-3.0mu/\mskip-2.0mu\psi\mskip 2.0mu}\pi^{0}
and
B^{+}\rightarrow{J\mskip-3.0mu/\mskip-2.0mu\psi\mskip 2.0mu}K^{*+}
decays
[
18
]
, resulting in a comparable precision on
{\cal B}(B^{0}\rightarrow{J\mskip-3.0mu/\mskip-2.0mu\psi\mskip 2.0mu}\pi^{0})
using the current knowledge of
{\cal B}(B^{+}\rightarrow{J\mskip-3.0mu/\mskip-2.0mu\psi\mskip 2.0mu}K^{*+})
.

B
A
B
AR

[
14
]
and
Belle

[
15
]
also measured the direct and mixing induced
C\!P
asymmetries.
The world average values are
C_{B^{0}\rightarrow{J\mskip-3.0mu/\mskip-2.0mu\psi\mskip 2.0mu}\pi^{0}}=0.04\pm 0.12
and
S_{B^{0}\rightarrow{J\mskip-3.0mu/\mskip-2.0mu\psi\mskip 2.0mu}\pi^{0}}=-0.86\pm 0.14

[
8
]
.

The current values of
\phi_{d}
and
\Delta\phi_{d}
, based on the analysis in Ref.
[
12
]
, are
(44.4^{+1.6}_{-1.5})^{\circ}
and
(-0.73^{+0.60}_{-0.91})^{\circ}
, respectively, and do not yet include the most recent measurements from
LHCb

[
6
,
18
]
.
With an improvement by a factor of two on the experimental precision on
B^{0}\rightarrow{J\mskip-3.0mu/\mskip-2.0mu\psi\mskip 2.0mu}K^{0}
only, the precision on
\phi_{d}
would be limited to
1^{\circ}
by the uncertainty on
\Delta\phi_{d}
.
On the other hand, a similar improvement on the precision of both
B^{0}\rightarrow{J\mskip-3.0mu/\mskip-2.0mu\psi\mskip 2.0mu}K^{0}
and
B^{0}\rightarrow{J\mskip-3.0mu/\mskip-2.0mu\psi\mskip 2.0mu}\pi^{0}

\phi_{d}
to
0.78^{\circ}
and confirm the presence of nonzero penguin contributions
[
12
]
.
Therefore, the current experimental knowledge on
B^{0}\rightarrow{J\mskip-3.0mu/\mskip-2.0mu\psi\mskip 2.0mu}\pi^{0}

Here we present a measurement of the branching fraction and
C\!P
asymmetries in

decays using a sample of energy-asymmetric

collisions at the
\mathchar 28935\relax{(4S)}
resonance provided by the SuperKEKB accelerator
[
19
]
and collected with the
Belle II
detector
[
20
]
.
The sample corresponds to
(365\pm 2)\penalty\ \mbox{\,fb}^{-1}
of integrated luminosity and contains
(387\pm 6)\times 10^{6}

B\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}
events
[
21
]
.
An additional
(42.6\pm 0.2)\penalty\ \mbox{\,fb}^{-1}
off-resonance sample recorded at
60

\mathrm{\,Me\kern-1.00006ptV}
below the
\mathchar 28935\relax{(4S)}
is used to model background from continuum

events, where
q\overline{q}
indicates pairs of
u
,
d
,
s
, or

The
C\!P
asymmetries are determined from the distribution of the proper-decay-time difference of
B^{0}\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}^{0}
pairs.
We denote pairs of
B^{0}
mesons as
B_{C\!P}
and
B_{\text{tag}}
, where
B_{C\!P}
decays into a
C\!P
-eigenstate at time
t_{C\!P}
, and
B_{\text{tag}}
decays into a flavor-specific final state at time
t_{\text{tag}}
.
For quantum-correlated neutral
B
-meson pairs from
\mathchar 28935\relax{(4S)}
decays, the flavor of
B_{C\!P}
is opposite to that of
B_{\text{tag}}
at the instant when the first
B
decays.
The probability to observe a
B_{\text{tag}}
meson with flavor
q
(
q=+1
for
B^{0}
and
q=-1
for
\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}^{0}
) and a proper-time difference
\Delta t\equiv t_{C\!P}-t_{\text{tag}}
between the
B_{C\!P}
and
B_{\text{tag}}
decays is

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{P}(\Delta t,q)=\frac{e^{-|\Delta t|/\tau_{B^{0}}}}{4\tau_{B^{0}}}\Big\{1+q\big[&S_{C\!P}\sin(\Delta m_{d}\Delta t)\\
-&C_{C\!P}\cos(\Delta m_{d}\Delta t)\big]\Big\},\end{split}

(1)

where
\tau_{B^{0}}
is the
B^{0}
lifetime and
\Delta m_{d}
is the mass difference between the
B^{0}
mass eigenstates
[
17
]
.

We fully reconstruct
B_{C\!P}
in the
{J\mskip-3.0mu/\mskip-2.0mu\psi\mskip 2.0mu}\pi^{0}
final state using the intermediate decays
J/\psi\rightarrow\ell^{+}\ell^{-}
(with
\ell^{\pm}
being an electron or a muon) and
\pi^{0}\rightarrow\gamma\gamma
, while we only determine the decay vertex of the
B_{\text{tag}}
decay.
The flavor of the
B_{\text{tag}}
meson is inferred from the properties of all charged particles in the event not belonging to
B_{C\!P}

[
22
]

We first extract the signal yields from the distributions of the signal

candidates in observables that discriminate against backgrounds, and then fit the

Charge-conjugated modes are included throughout the text.

II
Experiment

The
Belle II
detector operates at the SuperKEKB accelerator at KEK, which collides 7
\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV}
electrons with 4
\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV}
positrons.
The detector is designed to reconstruct the decays of heavy-flavor hadrons and
\tau
leptons.
It consists of several subsystems with a cylindrical geometry arranged around the interaction point (IP).
The innermost part of the detector is equipped with a two-layer silicon-pixel detector (PXD), surrounded by a four-layer double-sided silicon-strip detector (SVD)
[
23
]
.
Together, they provide information about charged-particle trajectories (tracks) and decay-vertex positions.
Of the outer PXD layer, only one-sixth is installed for the data used in this work.
The momenta and electric charges of charged particles are determined with a 56-layer central drift-chamber (CDC).
Charged-hadron identification (PID) is provided by a time-of-propagation counter and an aerogel ring-imaging Cherenkov counter, located in the central and forward regions outside the CDC, respectively.
The CDC provides additional PID information through the measurement of specific ionization.
Energy and timing of photons and electrons are measured by an electromagnetic calorimeter made of CsI(Tl) crystals, surrounding the PID detectors.
The polar angle coverage of the calorimeter is
12.4^{\circ}<\theta<31.4^{\circ}
,
32.2^{\circ}<\theta<128.7^{\circ}
and
130.7^{\circ}<\theta<155.1^{\circ}
in the forward, barrel and backward regions, respectively.
The tracking and PID subsystems, and the calorimeter, are surrounded by a superconducting solenoid, providing an axial magnetic field of 1.5 T.
The central axis of the solenoid defines the
z
axis of the laboratory frame, pointing approximately in the direction of the electron beam.
Outside of the magnet lies the muon and
K^{0}_{L}
identification system, which consists of iron plates interspersed with resistive-plate chambers and plastic scintillators.

We use Monte Carlo simulated events to model signal and background distributions, study the detector response, and test the analysis procedure.
Quark-antiquark pairs from
e^{+}e^{-}
collisions, and hadron decays, are simulated using
KKMC

[
24
]
with
Pythia8

[
25
]
, and
EvtGen

[
26
]
software packages, respectively.
The detector response is simulated using the
Geant4

[
27
]
software package.
The effects of beam-induced backgrounds are included in the simulation
[
28
,
29
]
.
We use a simulated sample of generic
e^{+}e^{-}
collisions, corresponding to a luminosity of approximately four times that of the experimental dataset.
We also use large samples of simulated
B\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}
pairs, where one of the
B
mesons is forced to decay into the final state of interest, while the other
B
meson in the event is decayed inclusively.
One sample is used to study the signal, where the
B
meson decays as
B^{0}\rightarrow{J\mskip-3.0mu/\mskip-2.0mu\psi\mskip 2.0mu}\pi^{0}
.
The other samples are used to study the dominant sources of backgrounds, where the
B
meson decays inclusively into charmonium
B\rightarrow{J\mskip-3.0mu/\mskip-2.0mu\psi\mskip 2.0mu}X
modes.
Collision data and simulated samples are processed using the
Belle II
analysis software
[
30
,
31
]
.

III
Event selection

Events containing a
B\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}
pair are selected online by a trigger system based on the track multiplicity and total energy deposited in the calorimeter.
We reconstruct
B^{0}\rightarrow{J\mskip-3.0mu/\mskip-2.0mu\psi\mskip 2.0mu}\pi^{0}
decays using
J/\psi\rightarrow\ell^{+}\ell^{-}
and
\pi^{0}\rightarrow\gamma\gamma
decays, in which the two light lepton tracks are reconstructed using information from the PXD, SVD, and CDC
[
32
]
.
All tracks are required to have polar angles within the CDC acceptance (
17^{\circ}<\theta<150^{\circ}
).
Tracks used to form
{J\mskip-3.0mu/\mskip-2.0mu\psi\mskip 2.0mu}
candidates are required to have a distance of closest approach to the IP of less than
2.0

\rm\,cm
along the
z
axis and less than
0.5

\rm\,cm
in the transverse plane to reduce contamination from tracks not generated in the collision.
Muons are identified using the discriminator
\mathcal{P}_{\mu}=\mathcal{L}_{\mu}/(\mathcal{L}_{e}+\mathcal{L}_{\mu}+\mathcal{L}_{\pi}+\mathcal{L}_{K}+\mathcal{L}_{p}+\mathcal{L}_{d})
, where the likelihood
\mathcal{L}_{i}
for each charged particle hypothesis combines particle identification information from all subdetectors except for the PXD and SVD.
Electron identification is provided by a boosted-decision-tree (BDT) classifier that combines several calorimeter variables and particle identification likelihoods
[
33
]
.
We classify tracks as muons or electrons based on a loose PID requirement which is more than
95\%
efficient on signal while rejecting more than
90\%
of misidentified tracks.
The momenta of electrons are corrected for energy loss due to bremsstrahlung by adding the four-momenta of photons with energy in the lab-frame within
[75,1000]

\mathrm{\,Me\kern-1.00006ptV}
and detected within 50
\rm\,mrad
of the initial direction of the track.
The
{J\mskip-3.0mu/\mskip-2.0mu\psi\mskip 2.0mu}
candidates are formed by combining the four-momenta of oppositely charged lepton pairs having an invariant mass
m(\ell^{+}\ell^{-})\in[2.9,3.2]

{\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV\!/}c^{2}}
, where the average
{J\mskip-3.0mu/\mskip-2.0mu\psi\mskip 2.0mu}
mass resolution is approximately
13

{\mathrm{\,Me\kern-1.00006ptV\!/}c^{2}}
for the muon mode and
16

{\mathrm{\,Me\kern-1.00006ptV\!/}c^{2}}
for the electron mode.
Photons used to reconstruct
\pi^{0}
candidates are identified from calorimeter energy deposits greater than 22.5
\mathrm{\,Me\kern-1.00006ptV}
in the forward region and 20
\mathrm{\,Me\kern-1.00006ptV}
in the backward and barrel regions.
Photon energy corrections are derived from control samples reconstructed in collision data and applied to correct for the imperfect calorimeter energy calibration.
The
\pi^{0}
candidates are formed by combining pairs of photons with an invariant mass
m(\gamma\gamma)\in[0.05,0.2]

{\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV\!/}c^{2}}
, where the average
\pi^{0}
mass resolution is approximately
8

{\mathrm{\,Me\kern-1.00006ptV\!/}c^{2}}
.

The beam-energy constrained mass
M_{\rm bc}
and energy difference
\Delta E
are computed for each
B^{0}\rightarrow{J\mskip-3.0mu/\mskip-2.0mu\psi\mskip 2.0mu}\pi^{0}
candidate as
M_{\rm bc}\equiv\sqrt{(E_{\text{beam}}^{*}/c^{2})^{2}-(|p_{B}^{*}|/c)^{2}}
and
\Delta E\equiv E_{B}^{*}-E_{\text{beam}}^{*}
, where
E_{\text{beam}}^{*}
is the beam energy, and
E_{B}^{*}
and
p_{B}^{*}
are the reconstructed energy and momentum of the
B_{C\!P}
candidate, respectively, all calculated in the center-of-mass (c.m.) frame.
Signal
B_{C\!P}
candidates peak at the known
B^{0}
mass
[
17
]
in
M_{\rm bc}
and zero in
\Delta E
.
The average
M_{\rm bc}
and
\Delta E
resolution for properly reconstructed
B^{0}\rightarrow{J\mskip-3.0mu/\mskip-2.0mu\psi\mskip 2.0mu}\pi^{0}
decays is approximately
5

{\mathrm{\,Me\kern-1.00006ptV\!/}c^{2}}
and
50

\mathrm{\,Me\kern-1.00006ptV}
, respectively.
Misreconstructed candidates from
B\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}
events decaying into final states different than the signal peak in
M_{\rm bc}
and follow an exponentially falling distribution in
\Delta E
.
Continuum events are uniformly distributed in
M_{\rm bc}
and
\Delta E
.
Only candidates satisfying
M_{\rm bc}>5.2\penalty\ {\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV\!/}c^{2}}
and
|\Delta E|<0.5\penalty\ \mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV}
are retained for further analysis.

The
B^{0}\rightarrow{J\mskip-3.0mu/\mskip-2.0mu\psi\mskip 2.0mu}\pi^{0}
decay vertex is determined using the
TreeFitter
algorithm
[
34
,
35
]
.
The
B_{C\!P}
candidate is constrained to point back to the IP and the
{J\mskip-3.0mu/\mskip-2.0mu\psi\mskip 2.0mu}
and
\pi^{0}
masses are constrained to their known values
[
17
]
.
We retain only
B_{C\!P}
candidates with a successful vertex fit and
|\Delta E|<0.3

\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV}
.
The
B_{\text{tag}}
decay vertex is reconstructed using the remaining tracks in the event.
Each track is required to have at least one measurement point in both the SVD and CDC subdetectors and correspond to a total momentum greater than 50
{\mathrm{\,Me\kern-1.00006ptV\!/}c}
.
The
B_{\text{tag}}
decay-vertex position is fitted using the
Rave
algorithm
[
36
]
, which allows for downweighting the contributions from tracks that are displaced from the
B_{\text{tag}}
decay vertex, and thereby suppresses biases from secondary charm decays.
The decay-vertex position is determined by constraining the
B_{\text{tag}}
direction, as determined from its decay vertex and the IP, to be collinear with its momentum vector
[
37
]
.
We only retain candidates with a successful tag-side vertex fit.
The proper-time difference between
B_{C\!P}
and
B_{\text{tag}}
is estimated from the signed distance,
\Delta l
, of the
B_{C\!P}
and
B_{\text{tag}}
decay-vertex positions along the
\mathchar 28935\relax{(4S)}
boost direction,

\Delta\tilde{t}=\frac{\Delta l}{\beta\gamma\gamma^{*}c},

(2)

where
\beta\gamma=0.284
is the
\mathchar 28935\relax{(4S)}
Lorentz boost and
\gamma^{*}=1.002
is the Lorentz factor of the
B
mesons in the c.m. frame.
We partially correct for the bias in
\Delta\tilde{t}
arising from the angular distribution of the
B
meson pairs in the c.m. frame
[
38
]
,

\Delta t=\frac{\Delta\tilde{t}-(\beta^{*}/\beta)\cos\theta_{\rm c.m.}\tau_{B^{0}}}{1+s(\beta^{*}/\beta)\cos\theta_{\rm c.m.}},

(3)

where
\beta^{*}\approx 0.06
is the boost factor of the
B^{0}
in the c.m. frame,
\theta_{\rm c.m.}
is the polar angle of
B_{C\!P}
in the c.m. frame, and
s
is the sign of
\Delta l
.
The residual bias in
\Delta t
and its impact on the
C\!P
asymmetries is taken into account in the systematic uncertainties.
We retain candidates with
|\Delta t|<10

{\rm\,ps}
and estimated uncertainty
\sigma_{\Delta t}\in[0.05,3]

{\rm\,ps}
.

We reduce the contribution from continuum events by requiring the zeroth to the second Fox-Wolfram moment
[
39
]
to be less than
0.5
, which is more than
99\%
efficient on signal while rejecting almost half of the continuum background.
We further reduce the continuum events by using a BDT classifier
[
40
]
combining several variables that discriminate between signal and background
The variables included in the BDT are the following, in decreasing order of discriminating power:
the cosine of the angle between the momentum of the positively charged lepton and the direction opposite to the momentum of the
B^{0}
in the
{J\mskip-3.0mu/\mskip-2.0mu\psi\mskip 2.0mu}
frame;
the “cone” variables developed by the CLEO collaboration
[
41
]
;
the second to fourth harmonic moments calculated with respect to the thrust axis;
the ratio of the zeroth to the second and the zeroth to the fourth Fox-Wolfram moments;
the modified Fox-Wolfram moments introduced in Ref.
[
42
]
;
the sphericity and aplanarity of the event
[
43
]
;
the cosine of the angle between the thrust axis of the
B_{C\!P}
and the thrust axis of the rest of the event;
and the event thrust
[
44
,
45
]
.
We impose a minimum threshold on the BDT output to maximize signal efficiency while rejecting as much background as possible.
This is achieved by choosing the threshold corresponding to the edge of the plateau of the signal efficiency
vs.
continuum background rejection curve.
This requirement retains more than
97\%
of the signal while rejecting more than
88\%
of the remaining continuum background in simulation.

We further enrich the sample in signal by requiring at least one of the two leptons from the
{J\mskip-3.0mu/\mskip-2.0mu\psi\mskip 2.0mu}
candidate to fulfill a tight PID requirement.
We choose the requirement corresponding to the edge of the plateau of the signal efficiency
vs.
misidentified lepton rejection curve.
This selection is more than
99\%
efficient on signal while rejecting more than half of the misidentified tracks.
We suppress the contributions from beam backgrounds and misreconstructed energy deposits using a BDT classifier combining several calorimeter cluster variables
[
46
]
.
In order to improve the resolution and reduce the correlation with
\Delta E
, we redefine the beam-constrained mass as
M_{\rm bc}^{\prime}
, by replacing the measured
\pi^{0}
momentum with
p^{*\prime}_{\pi^{0}}=\sqrt{(E_{\text{beam}}^{*}-E^{*}_{{J\mskip-3.0mu/\mskip-2.0mu\psi\mskip 2.0mu}})^{2}/c^{2}-m^{2}_{\pi^{0}}c^{2}}\times\frac{p^{*}_{\pi^{0}}}{|p^{*}_{\pi^{0}}|}
, where
E^{*}_{{J\mskip-3.0mu/\mskip-2.0mu\psi\mskip 2.0mu}}
is the energy of the
{J\mskip-3.0mu/\mskip-2.0mu\psi\mskip 2.0mu}
candidate in the c.m. frame,
We only keep candidates with
M_{\rm bc}^{\prime}>5.27\penalty\ {\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV\!/}c^{2}}
,
\Delta E\in[-0.18,0.3]\penalty\ \mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV}
,
m(\ell^{+}\ell^{-})\in[2.95,3.2]\penalty\ {\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV\!/}c^{2}}
and
m(\gamma\gamma)\in[0.1,0.16]\penalty\ {\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV\!/}c^{2}}
.

Events with more than one candidate account for approximately
3\%
of the data.
No candidates with different lepton mass hypotheses belonging to the same event are found in the data.
We keep the candidate with the reconstructed
m(\gamma\gamma)
mass closest to the known
\pi^{0}
mass
[
17
]
.
This requirement selects the correct signal candidate more than
75\%
of the time for events with multiple candidates in simulation.

The same event selection is applied on the control channels, except for the reconstruction of the
K^{0}_{S}
candidate in
B^{0}\rightarrow{J\mskip-3.0mu/\mskip-2.0mu\psi\mskip 2.0mu}K^{0}_{S}
and the requirements on the charged kaon track and invariant mass of the
K^{*+}\rightarrow K^{+}\pi^{0}
candidate in
B^{+}\rightarrow{J\mskip-3.0mu/\mskip-2.0mu\psi\mskip 2.0mu}K^{*+}
. In order to reproduce the topology of
B^{0}\rightarrow{J\mskip-3.0mu/\mskip-2.0mu\psi\mskip 2.0mu}\pi^{0}
, we remove the additional tracks in the final state from the vertex fit of the control modes.

In the selection of the off-resonance sample, we remove the continuum suppression BDT and the following signal enhancement requirements, in order to increase the sample size.
We verify in simulation that off-resonance and on-resonance continuum data passing the partial and full signal selections have similar distributions in the fit observables.

IV
Signal extraction fit

The sample passing the event selection is populated by
B^{0}\rightarrow{J\mskip-3.0mu/\mskip-2.0mu\psi\mskip 2.0mu}\pi^{0}
candidates coming from signal events and backgrounds.
We classify as signal those candidates reconstructed from underlying
B^{0}\rightarrow{J\mskip-3.0mu/\mskip-2.0mu\psi\mskip 2.0mu}\pi^{0}
decays for which the
{J\mskip-3.0mu/\mskip-2.0mu\psi\mskip 2.0mu}
is properly reconstructed.
This includes a small contribution from candidates with a misreconstructed
\pi^{0}
, which accounts for approximately
3\%
of the total signal yield.
Their distribution is centered around zero in
\Delta E
and around the value of the
{J\mskip-3.0mu/\mskip-2.0mu\psi\mskip 2.0mu}
mass in
m(\ell^{+}\ell^{-})
.
Among the sources of backgrounds, we classify as
B\rightarrow{J\mskip-3.0mu/\mskip-2.0mu\psi\mskip 2.0mu}X
those for which the
{J\mskip-3.0mu/\mskip-2.0mu\psi\mskip 2.0mu}
is properly reconstructed but originate from a different decay than the signal.
They follow an exponentially falling distribution in
\Delta E
and have the same distribution in
m(\ell^{+}\ell^{-})
as the signal.
In addition, we separate
B\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}
events with a misreconstructed
{J\mskip-3.0mu/\mskip-2.0mu\psi\mskip 2.0mu}
and continuum backgrounds, both of which have a smooth distribution in
\Delta E
and
m(\ell^{+}\ell^{-})
.

We extract the signal yields from an extended likelihood fit to the unbinned
\Delta E
and
m(\ell^{+}\ell^{-})
distributions.
The likelihood function is

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}=\frac{e^{-(n_{\text{sig}}+n_{\text{bkg}})}}{N!}\prod_{i=1}^{N}\Bigg\{n_{\text{sig}}P_{{J\mskip-3.0mu/\mskip-2.0mu\psi\mskip 2.0mu}\pi^{0}}^{i}+n_{\text{bkg}}\Big[g_{\text{$q\overline{q}$ }}P_{q\overline{q}}^{i}\\
+\frac{n_{B\kern 1.47495pt\overline{\kern-1.47495ptB}{}}}{n_{\text{bkg}}}P_{B\kern 1.47495pt\overline{\kern-1.47495ptB}{}}^{i}+\frac{n_{\text{bkg}}(1-g_{\text{$q\overline{q}$ }})-n_{B\kern 1.47495pt\overline{\kern-1.47495ptB}{}}}{n_{\text{bkg}}}P_{{J\mskip-3.0mu/\mskip-2.0mu\psi\mskip 2.0mu}X}^{i}\Big]\Bigg\}\end{split}

(4)

where
i
is the index of the candidate,
N
is the total number of candidates in the dataset,
n_{\text{sig}}
and
n_{\text{bkg}}
are the signal and background yields, respectively,
g_{\text{$q\overline{q}$ }}
is the fraction of continuum background,
n_{B\kern 1.47495pt\overline{\kern-1.47495ptB}{}}
is the
B\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}
background yield and
P^{i}=P(\Delta E^{i})P(m(\ell^{+}\ell^{-})^{i})
is the probability distribution function (
PDF
) of the
i
th candidate.

The
PDFs
of the signal in the
\Delta E
and
m(\ell^{+}\ell^{-})
distributions are described by Crystal Ball functions
[
47
,
48
]
.
The parameters of the
\Delta E

PDF
are determined from simulation.
We account for differences between data and simulation by adjusting the mean and width according to differences observed for the
B^{+}\rightarrow{J\mskip-3.0mu/\mskip-2.0mu\psi\mskip 2.0mu}K^{*+}
control sample.
These adjustments consist of shifting the mean by
-4.1\pm 0.8

\mathrm{\,Me\kern-1.00006ptV}
and scaling the width by a factor of
1.08\pm 0.04
, where the uncertainties are statistical only.
The parameters of the
m(\ell^{+}\ell^{-})

PDF
are determined separately for the
J/\psi\rightarrow e^{+}e^{-}
and

modes from a fit to the
B^{0}\rightarrow{J\mskip-3.0mu/\mskip-2.0mu\psi\mskip 2.0mu}K^{0}_{S}
control sample in data.
The distribution of the
B\rightarrow{J\mskip-3.0mu/\mskip-2.0mu\psi\mskip 2.0mu}X
backgrounds is described by the sum of two exponential functions in
\Delta E
and by a Crystal Ball function in
m(\ell^{+}\ell^{-})
.
The parameters of the
\Delta E

PDF
are determined from simulation while the parameters of the
m(\ell^{+}\ell^{-})

PDF
are shared with the signal.
The
\Delta E
and
m(\ell^{+}\ell^{-})
distributions of the
B\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}
backgrounds are described by exponential
PDFs
with parameters determined from simulation.
The
\Delta E
and
m(\ell^{+}\ell^{-})
distributions of the continuum background are described by exponential
PDFs
with parameters determined from the off-resonance data.

In the fit to the data, we determine
n_{\text{sig}}
,
n_{\text{bkg}}
, and
g_{\text{$q\overline{q}$ }}
, separately for the
J/\psi\rightarrow e^{+}e^{-}
and
J/\psi\rightarrow\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
modes, while we fix

to the values expected in simulation.
The data are displayed in Fig.

|\Delta E|<0.1

\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV}
and
3.0<m(e^{+}e^{-})<3.14

{\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV\!/}c^{2}}

V
C\!P
asymmetry fit

We determine the
C\!P
asymmetries from a likelihood fit to the unbinned
\Delta t
distribution of flavor-tagged candidates in the signal region.
Candidates outside of the signal region are removed from the fit as they mostly consist of
B\rightarrow{J\mskip-3.0mu/\mskip-2.0mu\psi\mskip 2.0mu}X
background events which dilute the observable
C\!P
asymmetries of the signal.
The likelihood function is

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}\propto\prod_{i=1}^{N}\bigg\{(1-f_{q\overline{q}})\Big[(1-f_{B\kern 1.47495pt\overline{\kern-1.47495ptB}{}})\big((1-f_{{J\mskip-3.0mu/\mskip-2.0mu\psi\mskip 2.0mu}X})P_{{J\mskip-3.0mu/\mskip-2.0mu\psi\mskip 2.0mu}\pi^{0}}^{i}\\
+f_{{J\mskip-3.0mu/\mskip-2.0mu\psi\mskip 2.0mu}X}P_{{J\mskip-3.0mu/\mskip-2.0mu\psi\mskip 2.0mu}X}^{i}\big)+f_{B\kern 1.47495pt\overline{\kern-1.47495ptB}{}}P_{B\kern 1.47495pt\overline{\kern-1.47495ptB}{}}^{i}\Big]+f_{q\overline{q}}P_{q\overline{q}}^{i}\bigg\}\end{split}

(6)

where
f_{q\overline{q}}
,
f_{B\kern 1.47495pt\overline{\kern-1.47495ptB}{}}
, and
f_{{J\mskip-3.0mu/\mskip-2.0mu\psi\mskip 2.0mu}X}
are the background fractions in the signal region, determined from the signal extraction fit, and
P^{i}=P(\Delta t^{i})
is the
PDF
of the
i
th candidate.

The
\Delta t
distribution of the signal in Eq.
1
is modified to model the effect of imperfect flavor assignment from the tagging algorithm

\begin{split}\mathcal{P}(\Delta t,q)=\frac{e^{-|\Delta t|/\tau_{B^{0}}}}{4\tau_{B^{0}}}\Big\{1-q\Delta w+qa^{\rm tag}_{\epsilon}(1-2w)\\
+\big[q(1-2w)+a^{\rm tag}_{\epsilon}(1-q\Delta w)\big]\\
\times\big[S_{C\!P}\sin(\Delta m_{d}\Delta t)-C_{C\!P}\cos(\Delta m_{d}\Delta t)\big]\Big\},\end{split}

(7)

where
w
is the wrong-tag probability,
\Delta w
is the wrong-tag probability difference between events tagged as
B^{0}
and
\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}^{0}
, and
a^{\rm tag}_{\epsilon}
is the tagging efficiency asymmetry between
B^{0}
and
\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}^{0}
.
We divide our sample into intervals of the tag-quality variable
r=1-2w
provided by the tagging algorithm, to gain statistical sensitivity from events with different wrong-tag fractions.
We use the boundaries
(0.0,0.1,0.25,0.45,0.6,0.725,0.875,1.0)
and corresponding calibration parameters obtained with a sample of
B^{0}\rightarrow D^{*-}\pi^{+}
decays in Ref.
[
22
]
.
The effective flavor tagging efficiency, defined as
\sum_{i}\varepsilon_{i}(1-2w_{i})^{2}
, where
\varepsilon_{i}
is the fraction of events associated with a tag decision and
w_{i}
is the wrong-tag probability in the
i
-th
r
-bin, is
(37.40\pm 0.43\pm 0.36)\%
, where the uncertainties are statistical and systematic, respectively
[
22
]
.
Since
f_{q\overline{q}}
varies with
r
, we use the distribution in off-resonance data to scale the average fraction in each bin, while
f_{B\kern 1.47495pt\overline{\kern-1.47495ptB}{}}
and
f_{B\rightarrow{J\mskip-3.0mu/\mskip-2.0mu\psi\mskip 2.0mu}X}
are constants in
r
, as verified in simulation.

The effect of finite detector
\Delta t
resolution is taken into account by modifying Eq.
7
as

\mathcal{F}(\Delta t,q|\sigma_{\Delta t})=\int\mathcal{P}(\Delta t^{\prime},q)\mathcal{R}(\Delta t-\Delta t^{\prime}|\sigma_{\Delta t})d\Delta t^{\prime},

(8)

where
\mathcal{R}
is the resolution function, conditional on the per-event
\Delta t
uncertainty
\sigma_{\Delta t}
.
The resolution function is described by the sum of two components:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{R}(\delta t|&\sigma_{\Delta t})=(1-f_{t}(\sigma_{\Delta t}))G(\delta t|m_{G}\sigma_{\Delta t},s_{G}\sigma_{\Delta t})\\
+&f_{t}(\sigma_{\Delta t})R_{t}(\delta t|m_{t}\sigma_{\Delta t},s_{t}\sigma_{\Delta t},k/\sigma_{\Delta t},f_{>},f_{<})\end{split}

(9)

where
\delta t
is the difference between the observed and the true
\Delta t
.
The first component is a Gaussian function with mean
m_{G}
and width
s_{G}
scaled by
\sigma_{\Delta t}
, which models the core of the distribution.
The second component
R_{t}
is the sum of a Gaussian function and the convolution of a Gaussian with two oppositely sided exponential functions,

\displaystyle\begin{split}R_{t}(x|\mu,\sigma,k,f_{>},f_{<}&)=(1-f_{<}-f_{>})G(x|\mu,\sigma)\\
&+f_{<}G(x|\mu,\sigma)\otimes k\exp_{<}(kx)\\
&+f_{>}G(x|\mu,\sigma)\otimes k\exp_{>}(-kx),\end{split}

(10)

where
\exp_{>}(kx)=\exp(kx)
if
x>0
or zero otherwise, and similarly for
\exp_{<}(kx)
.
The exponential tails arise from intermediate displaced charm-hadron vertices from the
B_{\text{tag}}
decay.
The fraction
f_{t}(\sigma_{\Delta t})
is zero at low values of
\sigma_{\Delta t}
and rises steeply to reach a plateau of 0.2 at
\sigma_{\Delta t}=0.25\penalty\ {\rm\,ps}
.
We neglect an outlier component in the resolution, accounting for
\mathcal{O}(10^{-3})
fraction of events with poorly reconstructed vertices, which shows no impact on the results.
We use the same resolution function parameters calibrated with a sample of
B^{0}\rightarrow D^{*-}\pi^{+}
decays as in Ref.
[
22
]
.

The
B\rightarrow{J\mskip-3.0mu/\mskip-2.0mu\psi\mskip 2.0mu}X
backgrounds are modeled separately for decays of
B^{0}
and
B^{+}
mesons in the
\Delta t
fit, with effective lifetimes determined from simulation and
PDF
with the same functional form as the signal.
The
C\!P
asymmetries of the
B^{0}\rightarrow{J\mskip-3.0mu/\mskip-2.0mu\psi\mskip 2.0mu}X
backgrounds are determined from simulated
B^{0}\rightarrow{J\mskip-3.0mu/\mskip-2.0mu\psi\mskip 2.0mu}K^{0}_{S}
and
B^{0}\rightarrow{J\mskip-3.0mu/\mskip-2.0mu\psi\mskip 2.0mu}K^{0}_{L}
decays misreconstructed as signal.

Contributions from all considered sources of systematic uncertainty are listed in Tables

3

VI.1
Branching fraction

In the computation of the branching fraction, we correct the signal efficiencies obtained in simulation using control samples from collision data.
The statistical and systematic uncertainties associated with the correction factors are propagated to the measurement of the branching fraction systematic uncertainty.

The
\pi^{0}
reconstruction efficiency is measured in data and simulation using the ratio of the yields of
D^{*+}\rightarrow D^{0}(\rightarrow K^{-}\pi^{+}\pi^{0})\pi^{+}
and
D^{*+}\rightarrow D^{0}(\rightarrow K^{-}\pi^{+})\pi^{+}
decays, scaled by the inverse values of their branching fractions.
The yield ratio in experimental and simulated data is used to obtain correction factors as functions of the
\pi^{0}
polar angle and momentum.
The average correction factor over the kinematic distribution of the
\pi^{0}
in
B^{0}\rightarrow{J\mskip-3.0mu/\mskip-2.0mu\psi\mskip 2.0mu}\pi^{0}
decays is
1.05\pm 0.04
, where the uncertainty is dominated by the knowledge of the
D^{0}
branching fractions
[
17
]
.

The difference in electron and muon identification performance between simulation and experimental data is calibrated using
{J\mskip-3.0mu/\mskip-2.0mu\psi\mskip 2.0mu}\rightarrow\ell^{+}\ell^{-}
,
e^{+}e^{-}\rightarrow\ell^{+}\ell^{-}(\gamma)
and
e^{+}e^{-}\rightarrow e^{+}e^{-}\ell^{+}\ell^{-}
samples.
The average correction factor over the kinematic distribution of the signal is
1.002\pm 0.006
for the
J/\psi\rightarrow e^{+}e^{-}
mode and
0.938\pm 0.005
for the
J/\psi\rightarrow\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
mode, where the uncertainties are the sum in quadrature of the statistical and systematic uncertainties.

The performance of the continuum-suppression BDT is validated using the
B^{0}\rightarrow{J\mskip-3.0mu/\mskip-2.0mu\psi\mskip 2.0mu}K^{0}_{S}
control sample.
The ratio of the signal efficiency after applying the BDT requirement in data and simulation is found to be
1.001\pm 0.004
and
1.007\pm 0.003
for the
J/\psi\rightarrow e^{+}e^{-}
and
J/\psi\rightarrow\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
modes, respectively, where the uncertainties are statistical only.

Tracking efficiencies are measured using
e^{+}e^{-}\rightarrow\tau^{+}\tau^{-}
events, where one
\tau

\tau^{-}\rightarrow e^{-}\overline{\nu}_{e}\nu_{\tau}
and the other as
\tau^{-}\rightarrow\pi^{-}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\nu_{\tau}

Efficiencies for data and simulation are found to be compatible within an uncertainty of
0.27\%
, which is propagated for each track to the uncertainty on the branching fraction.

VII
Summary

We report a measurement of the branching fraction and
C\!P
asymmetries in
B^{0}\rightarrow{J\mskip-3.0mu/\mskip-2.0mu\psi\mskip 2.0mu}\pi^{0}
decays using data from the
Belle II
experiment.
We find
392\pm 24
signal decays in a sample containing
(387\pm 6)\times 10^{6}

B\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}
events, corresponding to a value of the branching fraction of

\mathcal{B}(B^{0}\rightarrow{J\mskip-3.0mu/\mskip-2.0mu\psi\mskip 2.0mu}\pi^{0})=(2.00\pm 0.12\pm 0.09)\times 10^{-5},

(11)

where the first uncertainty is statistical, and the second is systematic.
The result is consistent with the world average
[
17
]
and has comparable precision to previous determinations.

We obtain the following values of the
C\!P
asymmetries

\begin{split}C_{C\!P}&=0.13\pm 0.12\pm 0.03,\\
S_{C\!P}&=-0.88\pm 0.17\pm 0.03,\end{split}

(12)

where the first uncertainty is statistical, and the second is systematic.
The results are the most precise to date and are consistent with previous determinations from
Belle
and
B
A
B
AR

[
14
,
15
]
.
The central value of
S_{C\!P}
is
5.0
standard deviations from zero.
The significance is calculated using the sum in quadrature of the statistical and systematic uncertainties.
This is the first observation of mixing-induced
C\!P
violation in
B^{0}\rightarrow{J\mskip-3.0mu/\mskip-2.0mu\psi\mskip 2.0mu}\pi^{0}
decays from a single measurement.
The improved determinations of the branching fraction and
C\!P
asymmetries in this mode provide further constraints on the penguin parameters and on the extraction of the CKM angle
\phi_{1}

[
12
]
.

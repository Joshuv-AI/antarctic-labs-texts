Quasi invariant Gaussian measures for the nonlinear Schrödinger equation on 𝕋 2 \mathbb{T}^{2}
Leonardo Tolomeo and Nicola Visciglia

Abstract
. We study the transport of Gaussian measures under the flow of the 2-dimensional defocusing Schrödinger equation i ​ ∂ t u + Δ ​ u = | u | 2 ​ k ​ u i\partial_{t}u+\Delta u=|u|^{2k}u posed on 𝕋 2 \mathbb{T}^{2} . In particular, we show that the Gaussian measures with inverse covariance ‖ u ‖ H s 2 \|u\|_{H^{s}}^{2} , are quasi-invariant under the flow for s > 2 s>2 . Moreover, we show that the Radon-Nykodim density belongs to every L p L^{p} space, locally in space. The proof relies on the physical-space energies introduced in [52], as well as a new abstract quasi-invariance argument that allows us to combine space-time estimates, along the flow with probabilistic bounds on the support of the measure.

1.
Introduction

In this paper we study the Cauchy problem associated with the defocusing Nonlinear Schrödinger Equation posed on
\mathbb{T}^{2}
, with a generic (defocusing) odd nonlinearity:

(1.1)

\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}u+\Delta u=|u|^{2k}u,\quad k\in\mathbb{N},\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}^{2}\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}\in H^{\sigma},\end{cases}

where
H^{\sigma}
denotes the usual Sobolev space on
\mathbb{T}^{2}
. As a byproduct of the paper
[
3
]
, one can show that the Cauchy problem above is globally well posed in the Sobolev spaces
H^{\sigma}
with
\sigma\geq 1
, hence it admits a well-defined flow
\Phi_{t}:H^{\sigma}\rightarrow H^{\sigma}
. In this work, we study how certain Gaussian measures are transported under this flow.
More precisely, we assume that the initial datum is distributed along the Gaussian measure

\mu_{s}
, formally given by
"\frac{1}{\mathcal{Z}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\|u\|^{2}_{H^{s}}}du"
, induced by the random vector

(1.2)

\omega\mapsto\phi^{\omega}(x)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\frac{g_{n}(\omega)}{{\langle n\rangle}^{s}}e^{in\cdot x},

where
\{g_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}
is an i.i.d. family of complex gaussian variables such that
\mathbb{E}[g_{n}]=0,\mathbb{E}[|g_{n}|^{2}]=1,\mathbb{E}[g_{n}^{2}]=0
.
The main result of this paper is the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
s>2
, and let
\Phi_{t}
be the flow of (
1.1
) on the Sobolev space

H^{\sigma}
with
1\leq\sigma<s-1
. For every
t\in\mathbb{R}
, we have that

\Phi_{t\#}\mu_{s}\ll\mu_{s}.

Moreover, there exists a Banach space
X\supseteq H^{1}
, such that for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
\Phi_{t\#}\mu_{s}(X)=1
, and moreover, for every
p>1
, and for every
R,T>0
the Radon-Nykodym derivative

f_{t}:=\frac{d\Phi_{t\#}\mu_{s}}{d\mu_{s}}

satisfies

(1.3)

\sup_{|t|\leq T}\big\|f_{t}\mathbbm{1}_{\|u\|_{X}\leq R}\big\|_{L^{p}(\mu_{s})}<\infty.

1.1.
Background and proof strategy

We now describe the “standard” strategy to show results such as Theorem
1.1
, and the main technical novelties of this work. Consider (as a toy model) the dynamical system on
\mathbb{R}^{d}

(1.4)

\begin{cases}\dot{y}=b(y),\\
y(0)=x,\end{cases}

where
b:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a smooth vector field. For the purpose of this subsection, denote by
\Phi_{t}(x)
the solution of (
1.4
).
For every probability measure
\nu_{0}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
the transported measure
\nu_{t}:=(\Phi_{t}(x))_{\#}\nu_{0}
will satisfy the Liouville’s equation

(1.5)

\partial_{t}\nu_{t}=-\div{(b\,\nu_{t})}.

When
\nu_{0}
is a probability measure of the form

\nu_{0}=\frac{1}{Z}\exp(-E(y))dy

for some smooth function
E:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
with
\exp(-E)\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, then we can solve (
1.5
) for

f_{t}:=\log\frac{d\nu_{t}}{dx}-\log\frac{d\nu_{0}}{dx}

explicitly with the method of characteristics, and obtain that

f_{t}=E(x)-E(\Phi_{-t}(x))+\int_{0}^{t}\div{(b)}\big(\Phi_{-t^{\prime}}(x)\big)dt^{\prime}.

For convenience, define

(1.6)

\mathcal{Q}(x)=\left.\frac{d}{dt}E(y(t))\right|_{t=0}+\div{(b)},

from which we obtain

(1.7)

\nu_{t}=\exp\Big(\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{Q}(\Phi_{-t^{\prime}}(x))dx\Big)\nu_{0}.

The main strategies to prove quasi-invariance so far have been

(1)

If one can prove that
\mathcal{Q}\in\exp(L)(\nu_{0}),
in the sense that

\int\exp(\eta\mathcal{Q})d\nu_{0}<\infty

for some
\eta>0
, then one can perform a Gronwall/Yudovich argument on the solution of (
1.5
), and obtain the existence of the density at every time. This is essentially the strategy followed in the pioneering work
[
34
]
.

(2)

If on top of this, one has good global control on the flow
\Phi_{t}
, then the condition above can be relaxed to

(1.8)

\mathcal{Q}\in\exp(L)_{\rm loc}(\nu_{0}).

This approach was firstly used in the works by Planchon, Tzvetkov, and the second author
[
25
,
26
]
.

(3)

Alternatively, one could try to exploit the cancellations caused by the time integral in (
1.7
), which boil down to showing that

(1.9)

\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{Q}(\Phi_{t^{\prime}}(x))dx\in\exp(L)_{\rm loc}(\nu_{0}).

This was firstly used by Debussche and Tsutsumi in
[
7
]
exploiting deterministic, global-in-time bounds on the flow, and subsequently by the first author and Forlano in
[
10
]
while exploiting probabilistic, local-in-time bounds.

The main novelty of this paper is that we develop a framework to exploit (2) and (3) at the same time. Indeed, speaking informally, in Section
2
, we show that if one can write
\mathcal{Q}=\mathcal{Q}_{1}+\mathcal{Q}_{2}
such that
\mathcal{Q}_{1}
satisfies (
1.8
) and
\mathcal{Q}_{2}
satisfies (
1.9
), then this is enough to obtain quasi-invariance.

From a technical point of view, in order to simplify the formula for the time derivative
\mathcal{Q}=\left.\frac{d}{dt}E\right|_{t=0}
,
it turns out that instead of considering
E=\frac{1}{2}\|u\|_{H^{s}}^{2}
(which corresponds exactly to
\nu_{0}=\mu_{s}
), it is convenient to add a correction term, and define

\nu_{0}=\exp(-S(u))d\mu_{s}.

Since
\nu_{0}
and
\mu_{s}
are equivalent, the respective quasi-invariance statements are also equivalent.

The construction of the modified energy
S(u)+\frac{1}{2}\|u\|_{H^{s}}^{2}
is intimately related to the study of the deterministic growth of Sobolev norms for solutions belonging to the space
H^{s}
.
In particular, via the technique of modified energy, it has been shown that many dispersive equations enjoy polynomial growth of the Sobolev norms, see
[
1
,
26
,
27
,
28
]
. Recently, using the same strategy, in
[
16
]
the authors proved a globalization argument for solutions to the generalised derivative NLS.

The energy that we use in this paper was firstly introduced by Planchon, Tzvetkov and the second author in
[
27
]
(in the case
s
integer), where it was tailored in such a way to obtain the following estimate

\big|\frac{d}{dt}\big(\frac{1}{2}\|u\|_{H^{s}}^{2}+S(u)\big)\big|\lesssim\|u\|_{H^{s}}^{2-},

which in turn implies polynomial growth of the
H^{s}
norm (provided that
S(u)
is lower order term compared to

\|u\|_{H^{s}}^{2}
).
The main feature that allows to obtain this result is that, in the formula for
\mathcal{Q}
, the
2s
derivatives coming from the
\|u\|_{H^{s}}^{2}
are “well-distributed”, see (
4.1
).
As it turns out,
this is also the main property that allows one to obtain the bound (
1.9
) for almost all the terms in
\mathcal{Q}
,
via
deterministic
transference-type multilinear bounds. Nevertheless, due to the low regularity of the Gaussian data (
u\in H^{s-1-\varepsilon}
), one of the terms in
\mathcal{Q}
is proportional to

\big(\int D^{s}\overline{u}\nabla u\big)\cdot\big(|u|^{2k-2}u^{2}D^{s-2}\overline{u}\nabla u\big),

which cannot have a deterministic bound at the regularity of the Gaussian measure, and forces us to prove an estimate of the form (
1.8
) for this term.

]
.

2.
An abstract criterion for quasi-invariance

In this section, we show an abstract quasi-invariance result under the following assumptions.

Assumption 1

(Energy-type measure)
.

H\subseteq X
be an abstract Wiener space, and let
\mu
be the cylindrical Wiener measure on
X
, i.e.
\mu
has mean
0
and covariance

(2.1)

\int|\langle x,\varphi\rangle|^{2}d\mu(x)=\|\varphi\|_{H^{*}}^{2}.

for every
\varphi\in X^{*}\subseteq H^{*}
. For a measurable functional
S:X\to\mathbb{R}
, we define

(2.2)

\rho=\exp(-S(x))\mu(x).

Assumption 2

(Flow map)
.

Let
\Phi:\mathbb{R}\times X\to X
be jointly continuous flow map, i.e. for every
t,s\geq 0,x\in X
, we have that

\Phi(t+s,x)=\Phi(t,\Phi(s,x)).

Moreover, we assume that there exists a sequence of finite-dimensional subspaces
E_{n}\subset H\subseteq X
and flow maps
\Phi_{n}:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times E_{n}\to E_{n}
such that, calling
P_{n}
the orthogonal projection to
E_{n}
,
P_{n}
is bounded on
X
and for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

(2.3)

\Phi(t,x)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\Phi_{n}(t,P_{n}(x)).

Assumption 3

(Divergence free)
.

The maps
\Phi_{n}
preserve the Lebesgue measure on
E_{n}
. Moreover, for every
x\in E_{n}
, the map
t\mapsto S(\Phi_{n}(t,x))+\frac{1}{2}\|\Phi_{n}(t,x)\|_{H}^{2}
is
C^{1}
in time, and

(2.4)

\left.\partial_{t}\left(S(\Phi_{n}(t,x))+\frac{1}{2}\|\Phi_{n}(t,x)\|_{H}^{2}\right)\right|_{t=0}=\mathcal{Q}_{1}^{n}(x)+\mathcal{Q}_{2}^{n}(x).

Assumption 4

(Global bounds)
.

Denoting by

B_{R}=\{x\in X:\|x\|_{X}\leq R\},

for every
T>0
, there exists
R^{\prime}(T)
such that for every
0\leq t\leq R^{\prime}(T),
and for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\Phi_{n}(t,B_{R})\subseteq B_{R^{\prime}(T)}.

Assumption 5
.

We assume the following on
S
. First of all, for
\mu-a.e.\ x\in X
, we have that

(2.5)

S(P_{n}(x))\to S(x).

Moreover, for every ball
B_{R}
, and some
p>1
, we have that

(2.6)

\displaystyle\sup_{n}\|\exp(-S(P_{n}(x)))\mathbbm{1}_{B_{R}}\|_{L^{p}}(\mu)<\infty.

Assumption 6

(Derivative bounds)
.

For every
p>0
,

(2.7)

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\int_{B_{R}}\exp(p|\mathcal{Q}_{1}^{n}|(x))d\rho(x)=C_{p}(R)<\infty

and for every
0\leq|t|\leq T
,

(2.8)

\sup_{n}\int_{B_{R}}\exp\Big(p\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{Q}_{2}^{n}(\Phi(\tau,x))\Big)d\rho(x)=D_{p}(R,T)<\infty.

Proposition 2.1
.

Under Assumptions 1–6, for every
T\in\mathbb{R}
we have that

\Phi(T,\cdot)_{\#}\rho\ll\rho,

and moreover, for every
p>1,R>0,

(2.9)

\int_{X}\Big|\frac{d\Phi(T,\cdot)_{\#}(\mathbbm{1}_{B_{R}}\rho)}{d\rho}\Big|^{p}d\rho\leq\big(C_{2Tp}(R^{\prime}(T))\big)^{\frac{2p^{2}}{(2p-1)(2p-2)}}\big(D_{2(p-1)}(R^{\prime}(T),T))^{\frac{p}{2p-1}}

Proof.

We start by showing the analogous bounds for the measure

\rho_{n}(x)=\rho(P_{n}(x))=\frac{1}{Z_{n}}\exp\Big(-S(x)-\frac{1}{2}\|x\|_{H}^{2}\Big)dx,

where
dx
denotes the (or any) Lebesgue measure on
E_{n}
.
By (
2.4
), for any smooth, compactly supported functional
F:E_{n}\to E_{n}
, we have that

\displaystyle\int F(\Phi_{n}(t,x))d\rho_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{Z_{n}}\int_{E_{n}}F(\Phi_{n}(t,x))\exp\Big(-S(x)-\frac{1}{2}\|x\|_{H}^{2}\Big)dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{Z_{n}}\int_{E_{n}}F(x)\exp\Big(-S(\Phi_{n}(-t,x))-\frac{1}{2}\|\Phi_{n}(-t,x)\|_{H}^{2}\Big)dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{Z_{n}}\int_{E_{n}}F(x)\exp\Big(-S(x)-\frac{1}{2}\|x\|_{H}^{2}+\int_{-t}^{0}(\mathcal{Q}_{1}^{n}+\mathcal{Q}_{2}^{n})(\Phi_{n}(t^{\prime},x))dt^{\prime}\Big)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{E_{n}}F(x)\exp\Big(\int_{0}^{t}(\mathcal{Q}_{1}^{n}+\mathcal{Q}_{2}^{n})(\Phi_{n}(-t^{\prime},x))dt^{\prime}\Big)d\rho_{n}(x),

where we used Assumption
3
for the second equality, and the flow property and the fundamental theorem of calculus for the third equality.
In particular, we obtain that

(2.10)

\Phi_{n}(t,\cdot)_{\#}\rho_{n}=\exp\Big(\int_{0}^{t}(\mathcal{Q}_{1}^{n}+\mathcal{Q}_{2}^{n})(\Phi_{n}(-t^{\prime},x))dt^{\prime}\Big)\rho_{n}=:f_{t}\rho_{n}.

Now fix
T,R>0
, and for
R^{\prime}
as in Assumption
4
, define the set

(2.11)

S_{t}=\{x\in E_{n}:\sup_{0\leq|t^{\prime}|\leq t}\|\Phi_{n}(t^{\prime},x)\|_{X}\leq R^{\prime}(T)\}.

By Assumption
4
, we have that for every
0\leq|t|\leq T,

(2.12)

B_{R}\subseteq S_{t}\subseteq B_{R^{\prime}(T)}.

Moreover, it is immediate to check that for
|t|>|t^{\prime}|
, we have

(2.13)

S_{t}\subseteq S_{t^{\prime}}.

Fix
p>1
. By (
2.10
), Hölder’s inequality, (
2.12
), (
2.8
), Jensen’s inequality for the exponential, (
2.13
), and (
2.7
), we have that

\displaystyle\int\mathbbm{1}_{S_{t}}(\Phi_{n}(-t,x))f_{t}^{p}(x)d\rho_{n}(x)

\displaystyle\int\mathbbm{1}_{S_{t}}(\Phi_{n}(-t,x))f_{t}^{p-1}(x)d\big(\Phi_{n}(t,\cdot)_{\#}\rho_{n}\big)(x)

\displaystyle=\int\mathbbm{1}_{S_{t}}(x)f_{t}^{p-1}(\Phi_{n}(t,x))d\rho_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=\int\mathbbm{1}_{S_{t}}(x)\exp\Big((p-1)\int_{0}^{t}(\mathcal{Q}_{1}^{n}+\mathcal{Q}_{2}^{n})(\Phi_{n}(t^{\prime},x))dt^{\prime}\Big)d\rho_{n}(x)

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{D_{2(p-1)}(R^{\prime},T)}\sqrt{\int\mathbbm{1}_{S_{t}}(x)\exp\Big(2(p-1)\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{Q}_{1}^{n}(\Phi_{n}(t^{\prime},x))dt^{\prime}\Big)d\rho_{n}(x)}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{D_{2(p-1)}(R^{\prime},T)}\sqrt{\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbbm{1}_{S_{t}}(x)\exp\Big(2t(p-1)\mathcal{Q}_{1}^{n}(\Phi_{n}(t^{\prime},x))\Big)d\rho_{n}(x)}dt^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{D_{2(p-1)}(R^{\prime},T)}\sqrt{\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbbm{1}_{S_{t}}(\Phi_{n}(-t^{\prime},x))\exp\Big(2t(p-1)\mathcal{Q}_{1}^{n}(x)\Big)f_{t^{\prime}}(x)d\rho_{n}(x)}dt^{\prime}

\displaystyle\begin{multlined}\leq\sqrt{D_{2(p-1)}(R^{\prime},T)}\bigg(\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\int\mathbbm{1}_{S_{t^{\prime}}}(\Phi_{n}(-t^{\prime},x))f_{t^{\prime}}^{p}(x)d\rho_{n}(x)dt^{\prime}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2p}}\\
\phantom{XXXXXXXXXXXXXXXXX}\times\bigg(\int_{B_{R^{\prime}}}\exp\Big(2tp\mathcal{Q}_{1}^{n}(x)\Big)d\rho_{n}\bigg)^{\frac{p}{2(p-1)}}\end{multlined}

\displaystyle\leq\big(C_{2Tp}(R^{\prime})\big)^{\frac{p}{2(p-1)}}\big(D_{2(p-1)}(R^{\prime},T))^{\frac{1}{2}}\sup_{0\leq|t|\leq T}\Big(\int\mathbbm{1}_{S_{t^{\prime}}}(\Phi_{n}(-t^{\prime},x))f_{t^{\prime}}^{p}(x)d\rho_{n}(x)\Big)^{\frac{1}{2p}}.

Therefore, by taking supremums in the LHS, from (
2.12
) we deduce that

\displaystyle\int\mathbbm{1}_{B_{R}}(\Phi_{n}(-t,x))f_{t}^{p}(x)d\rho_{n}(x)

\displaystyle\leq\int\mathbbm{1}_{S_{t}}(\Phi_{n}(-t,x))f_{t}^{p}(x)d\rho_{n}(x)

\displaystyle\leq\big(C_{2Tp}(R^{\prime})\big)^{\frac{2p^{2}}{(2p-1)(2p-2)}}\big(D_{2(p-1)}(R^{\prime},T))^{\frac{p}{2p-1}},

which is exactly (
2.9
) but for the flows
\Phi_{n}
and the measure
\rho_{n}
. Therefore, we just need to show that this bound passes to the limit. More precisely, in order to conclude, it is enough to show that for every bounded Borel functional
F:X\to\mathbb{R}
,

(2.14)

\int F(\Phi(t,x))d\rho(x)\leq\big(C_{2Tp}(R^{\prime})\big)^{\frac{2p^{2}}{(2p-1)(2p-2)}}\big(D_{2(p-1)}(R^{\prime},T))^{\frac{p}{2p-1}}\|F\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\rho)},

where
p^{\prime}=\frac{p}{p-1}
is the Hölder conjugate of
p
. Since continuous functions with bounded support are dense in
L^{p^{\prime}}(\rho+\Phi(t,\cdot)_{\#}\rho)
, without loss of generality we can assume that
F
is continuous with bounded support. By Fatou and (
2.5
), we have that

\displaystyle\int F(\Phi(t,x))d\rho(x)

\displaystyle=\int\lim_{n\to\infty}F(\Phi_{n}(t,P_{n}(x)))\exp(-S(P_{n}(x)))d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\int F(\Phi_{n}(t,x))d\rho_{n}(x)

\displaystyle\leq\big(C_{2Tp}(R^{\prime})\big)^{\frac{2p^{2}}{(2p-1)(2p-2)}}\big(D_{2(p-1)}(R^{\prime},T))^{\frac{p}{2p-1}}\liminf_{n\to\infty}\|F\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\rho_{n})}.

Since
F
has bounded support on a ball
B_{R}
, by Assumption
5
we have that
\exp(S\circ P_{n})\mathbbm{1}_{B_{R}}
is equi-integrable, and by (
2.5
) we have that
\exp(S\circ P_{n})\to\exp(S)

\mu-a.e.
. Therefore, by equi-integrable convergence, we have that

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\|F\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\rho_{n})}=\|F\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\rho)}.

In particular, we deduce (
2.14
), which concludes the proof.

3.
The Local Cauchy Theory in the space
{\mathcal{F}}L^{s,\infty}

3.1.
The Cauchy theory in
H^{\sigma}
,
\sigma\geq 1

We recall the following Strichartz estimates proved in
[
3
]

for the linear Schrödinger flow on
\mathbb{T}^{2}
(indeed the estimate below is true for a general compact surface):

\|e^{it\Delta}u_{0}\|_{L^{p}_{I}L^{q}}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{H^{\frac{1}{p}}},

where
I\subset\mathbb{R}
is a given bounded time interval and

\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{2},\quad p>2
.
In turn, by the Sobolev embedding

W^{1-\frac{1}{p},q}\subset L^{\infty}
(notice that
(1-\frac{1}{p})q>2
),
the estimate above implies

(3.1)

\|e^{it\Delta}u_{0}\|_{L^{p}_{I}L^{\infty}}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{H^{1}}.

Along with this bound, we also get
for the Duhamel operator

(3.2)

\Big\|\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-\tau)\Delta}F(\tau)ds\tau\Big\|_{L^{p}_{I}L^{\infty}}\lesssim\|F\|_{L^{1}_{I}H^{1}}

By combining (
3.1
) and
(
3.2
) we can perform a fixed point argument in the space

Y_{[0,T]}^{p}=L^{\infty}_{[0,T]}H^{1}\cap L^{p}_{[0,T]}L^{\infty},

see
[
3
]
for details.
More precisely one can prove the following result.

Proposition 3.1
.

There exists a universal constant
C>0
such that, for every
R>1
the Cauchy problem (
1.1
),
with
u_{0}\in H^{1}
such that
\|u_{0}\|_{H^{1}}\leq R
, admits one unique local solution
u\in Y^{4k}_{[0,T]}
with

\|u\|_{Y^{4k}_{[0,T]}}\leq CR

and

T\sim CR^{-4k}
.

By the conservation of energy and the fact that (
1.1
) is defocusing, we can iterate the local result above and we get the following global one.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
T>0
be given. Then there exists
C>0
such that,
for any given
R>1
the Cauchy problem
(
1.1
), with
u_{0}\in H^{1}
such that
\|u_{0}\|_{H^{1}}\leq R
, admits one unique solution
in the space

u\in Y^{4k}_{[0,T]}
such that

\|u\|_{Y^{4k}_{[0,T]}}\leq CR^{1+4k},

where the constant
C
depends only on
T
.

We shall need the following result on the propagation of regularity.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\sigma>1
be given. For every
T>0
,
R>1
, there exists
K>0
such that for every
u_{0}\in H^{\sigma}\cap H^{1}

with
\|u_{0}\|_{H^{1}}\leq R
, the Cauchy problem (
1.1
) admits one unique solution
u\in L^{\infty}_{[0,T]}H^{\sigma}
such that

(3.3)

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{[0,T]}H^{\sigma}}\leq K\|u_{0}\|_{H^{\sigma}}.

Proof.

Since
H^{\sigma}
is an algebra, one can show that the Cauchy problem (
1.1
) admits one unique local solution
u\in L^{\infty}_{[0,\tilde{T}]}H^{\sigma}
, for
u_{0}\in H^{\sigma}
and
\tilde{T}>0
. Moreover, by recalling Proposition
3.1
, we have that in fact

u\in L^{\infty}_{[0,\tilde{T}]}H^{\sigma}\cap Y_{[0,\tilde{T}]}^{4k}
. We aim at proving that, if we denote by
T_{max}
the maximal time of existence, then necessarily
T_{max}\geq T
and moreover we have the bound (
3.3
).
Let now assume by the absurd
T_{max}<T
, namely
\lim_{t\rightarrow T_{max}}\|u(t)\|_{H^{\sigma}}=\infty
.
For a given time interval

J=[a,b]\subset[0,T_{max})\subset[0,T)
we get

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{J}H^{\sigma}}\leq\|u(a)\|_{H^{\sigma}}+\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{J}H^{\sigma}}\int_{J}\|u(\tau)\|^{2k}_{L^{\infty}}\leq\|u(a)\|_{H^{\sigma}}+\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{J}H^{\sigma}}\|u\|^{2k}_{L^{4k}_{J}L^{\infty}}\sqrt{b-a}

and hence if we impose
(CR^{1+4k})^{2k}\sqrt{b-a}<\frac{1}{2}
, then by Proposition
3.2
we get

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{J}H^{\sigma}}\leq 2\|u(a)\|_{H^{\sigma}}

Next we split
[0,T_{max})=\cup_{1}^{N}I_{j}
, where
I_{j}=[\tau_{j},\tau_{j+1})
with

(CR^{1+4k})^{2k}\sqrt{|\tau_{j+1}-\tau_{j}|}<\frac{1}{2}
,
and we can repeat the argument above on each interval
I_{j}
:

(3.4)

\|u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{I_{j}}H^{\sigma}}\leq 2\|u(\tau_{j})\|_{H^{\sigma}}.

Notice that from the previous bound we get

\|u(\tau_{j+1})\|_{H^{\sigma}}\leq 2\|u(\tau_{j})\|_{H^{\sigma}}

and hence

\|u(\tau_{j})\|_{H^{\sigma}}\leq 2^{j}\|u_{0}\|_{H^{\sigma}},

which in conjunction with
(
3.4
) implies

\|u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{[0,T_{max})}H^{\sigma}}\leq 2\sup_{j}\|u(\tau_{j})\|_{H^{\sigma}}\leq 2^{N+1}\|u_{0}\|_{H^{\sigma}}.

As a consequence we have that
T_{max}\geq T
and moreover we get the desired bound if we call
K=2^{N+1}
. Notice that by definition
N
depends only on
R,T>0
.
∎

3.2.
The Cauchy theory in
\mathcal{F}L^{\sigma,\infty}
,
\sigma>2

We will work extensively with the following reformulation of (
1.1
)
in interaction representation, i.e.

\partial_{t}v=-ie^{-it\Delta}\big(|e^{it\Delta}v|^{2k}e^{it\Delta}v\big),

or more specifically, with its mild formulation

(3.5)

v(t)=v(0)-i\int_{0}^{t}e^{-i\tau\Delta}\big(|e^{i\tau\Delta}v(\tau)|^{2k}e^{i\tau\Delta}v(\tau)\big)d\tau.

We will say that
u
is a
solution
of (
1.1
) if and only if
v:=e^{-it\Delta}u
solves (
3.5
).

We denote for shortness

X_{\sigma}={\mathcal{F}}L^{\sigma,\infty}

and we also introduce
the space
\mathcal{A}

whose norm is defined as follows:

\|f\|_{\mathcal{A}}=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}|\hat{f}(j)|

One can easily show the following embeddings:

X_{2+}\subset{\mathcal{A}}\hbox{ and }H^{1+}\subset{\mathcal{A}}.

We need the following algebra property.

Proposition 3.4

(Algebra property)
.

Let
\sigma>2
be given, then we have the following estimate

(3.6)

\|fg\|_{X_{\sigma}}\lesssim\|f\|_{X_{\sigma}}\|g\|_{\mathcal{A}}+C\|g\|_{X_{\sigma}}\|f\|_{\mathcal{A}}

for a suitable
C>0
.

Proof.

We denote by
\{f_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}
and
\{g_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}
the fourier coefficients of
f,g
.
We notice that

\langle j\rangle^{\sigma}|\sum_{j=j_{1}+j_{2}}f_{j_{1}}g_{j_{2}}|\leq\langle j\rangle^{\sigma}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j_{1}+j_{2}=j\\
\hbox{ s.t. }|j_{1}|\geq\frac{|j|}{2}\end{subarray}}|f_{j_{1}}||g_{j_{2}}|+\langle j\rangle^{\sigma}|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j_{1}+j_{2}=j\\
|j_{2}|\geq\frac{|j|}{2}\end{subarray}}|f_{j_{1}}||g_{j_{2}}|\\
\lesssim\sum_{j_{1}+j_{2}=j}\langle j_{1}\rangle^{\sigma}|f_{j_{1}}||g_{j_{2}}|+\sum_{j_{1}+j_{2}=j}\langle j_{2}\rangle^{\sigma}|f_{j_{1}}||g_{j_{2}}|\\
\lesssim\|f\|_{X_{\sigma}}\sum_{j_{2}}|g_{j_{2}}|+\|g\|_{X_{\sigma}}\sum_{j_{2}}|f_{j_{2}}|.

∎

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
\sigma>2
,
R>1
and
T>0

be fixed. Then for every
u_{0}\in X_{\sigma}
with
\|u_{0}\|_{H^{1}}\leq R
, there exists a unique solution
u(t)\in C([0,T],X_{\sigma})
of (
1.1
). Moreover there exists a function

F(\|u_{0}\|_{X_{\sigma}})
such that

(3.7)

\|v\|_{C^{1}([0,T],X_{\sigma})}\leq F(\|u_{0}\|_{X_{\sigma}}).

Proof.

As a consequence of Proposition
3.4
and the embedding
X_{\sigma}\subset\mathcal{A}
we have that (
1.1
) is locally well posed in
X_{\sigma}
. Next we control

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{[0,T]}X_{\sigma}}
. Let
1<\sigma^{\prime}<\sigma-1
be fixed, then we have that
\|u_{0}\|_{\mathcal{A}}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{H^{\sigma^{\prime}}}
.
From
(
3.6
) and (
3.3
), we obtain that for some universal constant
C>0
,

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{X_{\sigma}}

\displaystyle\leq\|u_{0}\|_{X_{\sigma}}+\int_{0}^{t}\||u(\tau)|^{2k}u(\tau)\|_{X_{\sigma}}d\tau

\displaystyle\leq\|u_{0}\|_{X_{\sigma}}+C\int_{0}^{t}\|u(\tau)\|_{\mathcal{A}}^{2k}\|u(\tau)\|_{X_{\sigma}}d\tau

\displaystyle\leq\|u_{0}\|_{X_{\sigma}}+C\int_{0}^{t}\|u(\tau)\|_{H^{\sigma^{\prime}}}^{2k}\|u(\tau)\|_{X_{\sigma}}d\tau

\displaystyle\leq\|u_{0}\|_{X_{\sigma}}+CK^{2k}\|u_{0}\|_{H^{\sigma^{\prime}}}^{2k}\int_{0}^{t}\|u(\tau)\|_{X_{\sigma}}d\tau.

Therefore, we deduce from the Gronwall and the bound
\|u_{0}\|_{H^{\sigma^{\prime}}}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{X_{\sigma}}
that the solution
u(t)
can be extended up to time
T
with the bound

(3.8)

\|u\|_{C([0,T],X_{\sigma})}\leq F(\|u_{0}\|_{X_{\sigma}}).

We now move to showing (
3.7
). By (
3.5
), we have that

\partial_{t}v=-ie^{-it\Delta}\big(|e^{it\Delta}v(t)|^{2k}e^{it\Delta}v(t)\big)=-ie^{-it\Delta}\big(|u|^{2k}u).

Therefore, (
3.7
) follows from (
3.8
), the embedding
X_{\sigma}\supset\mathcal{A}
and (
3.6
).
∎

Proposition 4.1
.

Given
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle

\displaystyle{\mathcal{N}}(u)

Lemma 4.2
.

We have

\mathbb{E}\big|(D^{s}P_{N}\bar{u}D^{s-2}P_{M}\bar{u})\widehat{\phantom{X}}(n)\big|^{2}\lesssim\min(M^{-2},\mathbbm{1}_{|n|\sim N}+\langle n\rangle^{-2}\mathbbm{1}_{N\ll|n|}+N^{-2}\mathbbm{1}_{N\gg|n|}),

Proof.

We have

(4.6)

(D^{s}P_{N}\bar{u}D^{s-2}P_{M}\bar{u})\widehat{\phantom{X}}(n)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l+k=n\\

Hence we get

(4.7)

\mathbb{E}\big|(D^{s}P_{N}\bar{u}D^{s-2}P_{M}\bar{u})\widehat{\phantom{X}}(n)\big|^{2}\lesssim\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l+k=n\\
|l|\sim N,|k|\sim M\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{{\langle k\rangle}^{4}}.

Notice that summing over
k
we get

\mathbb{E}\big|(D^{s}P_{N}\bar{u}D^{s-2}P_{M}\bar{u})\widehat{\phantom{X}}(n)\big|^{2}\lesssim M^{-2}.

On the other hand summing over
l
we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big|(D^{s}P_{N}\bar{u}D^{s-2}P_{M}\bar{u})\widehat{\phantom{X}}(n)\big|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l+k=n\\
|l|\sim N,|k|\sim M\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{{\langle k\rangle}^{4}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{|l|\sim N}\frac{1}{\langle n-l\rangle^{4}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathbbm{1}_{N\ll|n|}\sum_{|l|\sim N}\frac{1}{\langle n\rangle^{4}}+\mathbbm{1}_{N\sim|n|}\sum_{|l|\sim N}\frac{1}{\langle n-l\rangle^{4}}+\mathbbm{1}_{N\gg|n|}\sum_{|l|\sim N}\frac{1}{N^{4}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathbbm{1}_{N\ll|n|}\frac{N^{2}}{\langle n\rangle^{4}}+\mathbbm{1}_{N\sim|n|}+\mathbbm{1}_{N\gg|n|}N^{-2},

which concludes the proof of (
4.4
). Since the expression in (
4.4
) is a polynomial of degree 2 in a Gaussian random variable, by standard Wiener Chaos estimates (see
[
30
, Theorem I.22]
), we deduce that

(4.8)

\Big(\mathbb{E}\big|(D^{s}P_{N}\bar{u}D^{s-2}P_{M}\bar{u})\widehat{\phantom{X}}(n)\big|^{p}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}\lesssim p\min(M^{-1},\mathbbm{1}_{|n|\sim N}+\langle n\rangle^{-1}\mathbbm{1}_{N\ll|n|}+N^{-1}\mathbbm{1}_{N\gg|n|}),

where the implicit constant is independent of
p
. In particular, by Minkowski,

\displaystyle\big(\mathbb{E}\|(D^{s}P_{N}\bar{u}D^{s-2}P_{M}\bar{u})\|_{\mathcal{F}L^{-\sigma,1}}^{p}\big)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=\Big(\mathbb{E}\Big|\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\langle n\rangle^{-\sigma}\big|(D^{s}P_{N}\bar{u}D^{s-2}P_{M}\bar{u})\widehat{\phantom{X}}(n)\big|\Big|^{p}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\langle n\rangle^{-\sigma}\Big(\mathbb{E}\big|(D^{s}P_{N}\bar{u}D^{s-2}P_{M}\bar{u})\widehat{\phantom{X}}(n)\big|^{p}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim p\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\langle n\rangle^{-\sigma}\min(M^{-1},\mathbbm{1}_{|n|\sim N}+\langle n\rangle^{-1}\mathbbm{1}_{N\ll|n|}+N^{-1}\mathbbm{1}_{N\gg|n|})

\displaystyle\lesssim p\min(M^{-1},N^{2-\sigma}+N^{1-\sigma},N^{-1})

\displaystyle\lesssim p\min(M^{-1},N^{-1}).

∎

Lemma 4.3
.

For every
2<\sigma<s
, the limit (
4.3
) exists for
\mu
a.e.
u\in{\mathcal{F}}L^{\sigma,\infty}
.

Proof.

Let
B_{R}
be the ball

B_{R}=\{u\in\mathcal{F}L^{\sigma,\infty}:\|u\|_{\mathcal{F}L^{\sigma,\infty}}\leq R\}.

We introduce the notation

(4.9)

q(u)=|u|^{2k-2}u^{2},\quad B(u)=(D^{s}P_{N}\bar{u}D^{s-2}P_{M}\bar{u}).

Note that with this notation, we have that

S(P_{\leq N}u)=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}q(P_{\leq N}u)B(P_{\leq N}u).

Therefore, it is enough to show that for
M>N
dyadic,

(4.10)

\mathbb{E}[\mathbbm{1}_{B_{R}}(u)\big|\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\big(q(P_{\leq M}u)B(P_{\leq M}u)-q(P_{\leq N}u)B(P_{\leq N}u)\big)\big|^{2}]\lesssim_{R}N^{0-}.

For
2<\sigma^{\prime}<\sigma
, from the algebra property we get that

(4.11)

\|q(P_{\leq N}u)-q(P_{\leq M}u)\|_{{\mathcal{F}}L^{\sigma^{\prime},\infty}}\lesssim\|u\|^{2k-1}_{{\mathcal{F}}L^{\sigma^{\prime},\infty}}\|P_{\leq M}u-P_{\leq N}u\|_{{\mathcal{F}}L^{\sigma^{\prime},\infty}}\lesssim N^{-(\sigma-\sigma^{\prime})}R^{2k}.

From (
4.5
), by expanding
B
in dyadic pieces, we have that

(4.12)

\mathbb{E}\|B(P_{\leq N}u)\|_{\mathcal{F}L^{-\sigma,1}}^{2}\lesssim 1,\quad\mathbb{E}\|B(P_{\leq N}u)-B(P_{\leq M}u)\|_{\mathcal{F}L^{-\sigma,1}}^{2}\lesssim N^{-1}.

Therefore, from (
4.11
) and (
4.12
), we obtain

(4.13)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\mathbbm{1}_{B_{R}}(u)\big|\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\big(q(P_{\leq M}u)B(P_{\leq M}u)-q(P_{\leq N}u)B(P_{\leq N}u)\big)\big|^{2}]

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathbb{E}[\mathbbm{1}_{B_{R}}(u)\|B(P_{\leq N}u)\|_{\mathcal{F}L^{-\sigma^{\prime},1}}\|q(P_{\leq N}u)-q(P_{\leq M}u)\|_{\mathcal{F}L^{\sigma^{\prime},\infty}}]

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}[\mathbbm{1}_{B_{R}}(u)\|B(P_{\leq N}u)-B(P_{\leq M}u)\|_{\mathcal{F}L^{-\sigma^{\prime},1}}\|q(P_{\leq M}u)\|_{\mathcal{F}L^{\sigma^{\prime},\infty}}]

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{-(\sigma-\sigma^{\prime})}R^{2k}+N^{-1}R^{2k},

which shows (
4.10
).
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
R>0
, and let
B_{R}
be the ball

B_{R}=\{u\in\mathcal{F}L^{\sigma,\infty}:\|u\|_{\mathcal{F}L^{\sigma,\infty}}\leq R\}.

For every
1<p<\infty
, we have that

(4.14)

\int_{B_{R}}\exp(p|S(u)|)d\mu(u)<\infty.

Proof.

Note that, for every
N_{0}
dyadic, we have that

|S(P_{N_{0}}u)|\lesssim\|u\|_{\mathcal{F}L^{\sigma,\infty}}^{2k}N_{0}^{2s-2}.

Therefore, it is enough to show that there exists
N_{0}
dyadic such that

(4.15)

\int_{B_{R}}\exp(p|S(u)-S(P_{N_{0}}(u))|)d\mu(u)<\infty.

Recall the notation for
B(u),q(u)
in (
4.9
). By (
4.5
), for every
2<\sigma^{\prime}<\sigma
, we obtain that

(4.16)

\mathbb{E}\|B(u)-B(P_{\leq N_{0}}u)\|_{\mathcal{F}L^{-\sigma^{\prime},1}}^{p}\leq C^{p}N_{0}^{-p},

for some constant
C=C(\sigma^{\prime})
. Therefore, proceeding as in (
4.13
), we obtain that

(4.17)

\mathbb{E}\|S(u)-S(P_{\leq N_{0}}u)\|_{\mathcal{F}L^{-\sigma^{\prime},1}}^{p}\leq C^{p}N_{0}^{-p\min(1,\sigma-\sigma^{\prime})},

for a (different) constant
C=C(\sigma,\sigma^{\prime})
. From this, we deduce that

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}}\exp(p|S(u)-S(P_{N_{0}}(u))|)d\mu(u)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\int_{B_{R}}\frac{p^{k}|S(u)-S(P_{N_{0}}(u))|^{k}}{k!}d\mu(u)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{C^{k}N_{0}^{-k\min(1,\sigma-\sigma^{\prime})}}{k!}.

Therefore, by picking
N_{0}\gg 1
such that

CN_{0}^{-\min(1,\sigma-\sigma^{\prime})}<e,

the series converges, and we complete the proof.
∎

5.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
\sigma<s
,
T\geq 0
. Define the space
X_{T}
given by the space-time functions such that the following norm is finite:

(5.1)

\|f\|_{X_{T}}=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{2},t\in[0,T]}\langle n\rangle^{\sigma}|\widehat{f}(t,n)|+\sup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{2},t\in[0,T]}\langle n\rangle^{\sigma}\big|\partial_{t}\big(e^{it|n|^{2}}\widehat{f}(t,n)\big)\big|

Define

to be the space

\overline{X}_{T}=\{f:\overline{f}\in X_{T}\},

with norm

5.1.
Counting estimate bounds

Recall the following counting estimate from
[
8
, Lemma 4.5]
.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2},\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}
, and let
N_{1},N_{2},N_{3}\geq 1
be dyadic numbers. Consider the set

\displaystyle S:=S_{m,\kappa}:=S_{m,\kappa}(N_{1},N_{2},N_{3})

\displaystyle:=\{(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3}):\pm k_{1}\pm k_{2}\pm k_{3}=m,\pm k_{1}^{2}\pm k_{2}^{2}\pm k_{3}^{2}=\kappa,k_{j}\sim N_{j}\}

\displaystyle\phantom{XX}\setminus\bigcup_{j=1,2,3}\{(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3}):k_{j}\pm k_{j+1}=0\}.

Then

(5.2)

|S_{m,\kappa}(N_{1},N_{2},N_{3})|\lesssim N_{(2)}^{1+}N_{(3)}^{1+},

where
N_{(1)}\geq N_{(2)}\geq N_{(3)}
is a rearrangement of
N_{1},N_{2},N_{3}
, and the implicit constants are independent of
m,\kappa
.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
f_{1},f_{2},\dotsc f_{k+1}\in X_{T}
,
g_{1},\dotsc,g_{k+1}\in\overline{X}_{T}
. Let
N_{1},\dotsc,N_{k+1}
,
M_{1},\dotsc,M_{k+1}
be dyadic numbers. Let

N_{(1)}\geq N_{(2)}\geq\dotsb N_{(2k+2)}

be a rearrangement of
N_{1},\dotsc,N_{k+1},M_{1},\dotsc,M_{k+1},
let
n_{(1)},\dotsc,n_{(2k+2)}
be the corresponding rearrangement of
n_{1},\dotsc,n_{k+1},m_{1},\dotsc,m_{k+1}
, and let
f_{(1)},\dotsc,f_{(2k+2)}
be the corresponding rearrangement of
f_{1},f_{2},\dotsc f_{k+1},g_{1},\dotsc,g_{k+1}
.
Then for
0\leq t\leq T

(5.3)

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{0}^{t}\int\prod_{j=1}^{k+1}P_{N_{j}}f_{j}(\tau)P_{M_{j}}g_{j}(\tau)d\tau-\int_{0}^{T}\Big(\int P_{N_{(1)}}f_{(1)}(\tau)P_{N_{(2)}}f_{(2)}(\tau)\Big)\int\prod_{j=3}^{2k+2}P_{N_{(j)}}f_{(j)}(\tau)d\tau\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{T}N_{(1)}^{1-2\sigma+}N_{(3)}^{1-\sigma+}\prod_{j=4}^{2k+2}N_{(j)}^{2-\sigma}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\|f_{j}\|_{X_{T}}\|g_{j}\|_{\overline{X}_{T}}.

Moreover,

(5.4)

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{0}^{t}\Big(\int P_{N_{(1)}}f_{(1)}(\tau)P_{N_{(2)}}f_{(2)}(\tau)\Big)\int\prod_{j=3}^{2k+2}P_{N_{(j)}}f_{(j)}(\tau)d\tau\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{T}N_{(1)}^{2-2\sigma+}N_{(3)}^{-\sigma}\prod_{j=4}^{2k+2}N_{(j)}^{2-\sigma}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\|f_{j}\|_{X_{T}}\|g_{j}\|_{\overline{X}_{T}}.

Proof.

Let
F_{j}=e^{it\Delta}f_{j}
,

G_{j}=e^{-it\Delta}g_{j}
. We have that

\|F_{j}\|_{C^{1}([0,T],{\mathcal{F}}L^{\sigma,\infty})}\lesssim\|f_{j}\|_{X_{T}},

and similarly for
G_{j}
. Therefore, writing the LHS of (
5.3
) in Fourier variables, and integrating by parts, we have that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int\prod_{j=1}^{k+1}P_{N_{j}}f_{j}(\tau)P_{M_{j}}g_{j}(\tau)d\tau-\int_{0}^{t}\Big(\int P_{N_{(1)}}f_{(1)}(\tau)P_{N_{(2)}}f_{(2)}(\tau)\Big)\int\prod_{j=3}^{2k+2}P_{N_{(j)}}f_{(j)}(\tau)d\tau

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1}+\dotsb n_{k+1}+m_{1}+\dotsb+m_{k+1}=0\\
n_{(1)}\pm n_{(2)}\neq 0\\
n_{j}\sim N_{j},m_{j}\sim M_{j}\end{subarray}}e^{i\tau\sum_{j=1}^{k+1}|n_{j}|^{2}-|m_{j}|^{2}}\prod_{j=1}^{k+1}\widehat{F_{j}}(\tau,n_{j})\widehat{G_{j}}(\tau,m_{j})d\tau

\displaystyle=\sum_{|\kappa|\lesssim N_{(1)}^{2}}\sum_{E_{\kappa}}\int_{0}^{t}e^{i\tau\kappa}\prod_{j=1}^{k+1}\widehat{F_{j}}(\tau,n_{j})\widehat{G_{j}}(\tau,m_{j})d\tau

\displaystyle=\left[\sum_{|\kappa|\lesssim N_{(1)}^{2}}\sum_{E_{\kappa}}\frac{e^{i\tau\kappa}-1}{i\kappa}\prod_{j=1}^{k+1}\widehat{F_{j}}(\tau,n_{j})\widehat{G_{j}}(\tau,m_{j})d\tau\right]_{0}^{t}

\displaystyle\phantom{=}-\sum_{|\kappa|\lesssim N_{(1)}^{2}}\sum_{E_{\kappa}}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{e^{i\tau\kappa}-1}{i\kappa}\sum_{j_{0}=1}^{k+1}\partial_{t}(\widehat{F_{j_{0}}}(\tau,n_{j})\widehat{G_{j_{0}}}(\tau,m_{j}))\prod_{j\neq j_{0}}\widehat{F_{j}}(\tau,n_{j})\widehat{G_{j}}(\tau,m_{j})d\tau,

where

\begin{multlined}E_{\kappa}:=\{(n_{1},\dotsc,n_{k+1},m_{1},\dotsc,m_{k+1}):n_{j}\sim N_{j},m_{j}\sim M_{j},n_{(1)}\pm n_{(2)}\neq 0\\
\sum_{j=1}^{k+1}n_{j}+m_{j}=0,\sum_{j=1}^{k+1}|n_{j}|^{2}-|m_{j}|^{2}=\kappa\}.\end{multlined}

Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{0}^{t}\int\prod_{j=1}^{k+1}P_{N_{j}}f_{j}(\tau)P_{M_{j}}g_{j}(\tau)d\tau-\int_{0}^{t}\Big(\int P_{N_{(1)}}f_{(1)}(\tau)P_{N_{(2)}}f_{(2)}(\tau)\Big)\int\prod_{j=3}^{2k+2}P_{N_{(j)}}f_{(j)}(\tau)d\tau\bigg|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{T}\sum_{|\kappa|\lesssim N_{(1)}}\sum_{E_{\kappa}}\frac{1}{\langle\kappa\rangle}\prod_{j=1}^{k+1}N_{j}^{-\sigma}\|F_{j}\|_{C^{1}([0,T],{\mathcal{F}}L^{\sigma,\infty})}M_{j}^{-\sigma}\|G_{j}\|_{C^{1}([0,T],{\mathcal{F}}L^{\sigma,\infty})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\prod_{j=1}^{2k+2}N_{(j)}^{-\sigma}\sum_{|\kappa|\lesssim N_{(1)}}\frac{1}{\langle\kappa\rangle}|E_{\kappa}|\prod_{j=1}^{k+1}\|f_{j}\|_{X_{T}}\|g_{j}\|_{\overline{X}_{T}}

\displaystyle\lesssim N_{(1)}^{0+}\sup_{|\kappa|\lesssim N_{(1)}}|E_{\kappa}|\prod_{j=1}^{2k+2}N_{(j)}^{-\sigma}\prod_{j=1}^{k+1}\|f_{j}\|_{X_{T}}\|g_{j}\|_{\overline{X}_{T}}.

Recall that, in order to have the condition
n_{1}+\dotsb+n_{k+1}+m_{1}+\dotsb+m_{k+1}=0,

we must have that
N_{(1)}\sim N_{(2)}
.
Therefore, to complete the proof of (
5.3
), it is enough to show that

(5.5)

|E_{\kappa}|\lesssim N_{(1)}^{1+}N_{(3)}^{1+}\prod_{j=4}^{2k+2}N_{(4)}^{2}.

We have that

\displaystyle E_{\kappa}

\displaystyle\subseteq\bigcup_{n_{(4)},\dotsc,n_{(2k+2)}}S_{\sum_{j=4}^{2k+2}\pm n_{(j)},\kappa-\sum_{j=4}^{2k+2}\pm|n_{(j)}|^{2}}(N_{(1)},N_{(2)},N_{(3)})

\displaystyle\phantom{X}\cup\bigcup_{l\in\{1,2\}}\Big\{n_{(j)}\sim N_{j},n_{(l)}=\pm n_{(3)},\sum_{j\neq l,3}\pm n_{(j)}=0\Big\}.

In order for the sets in the second line to be non-empty, we must have that
N_{(1)}\sim N_{(3)}
, which leads to

\displaystyle\Big|\bigcup_{l\in\{1,2\}}\Big\{n_{(j)}\sim N_{j},n_{(l)}=\pm n_{(3)},\sum_{j\neq l,3}\pm n_{(j)}=0\Big\}\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big|\{n_{(3)}\sim N_{3},n_{(j)}\sim N_{(j)}\}_{j\geq 4}\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim N_{(3)}^{2}\prod_{j=4}^{2k+2}N_{(4)}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim N_{(1)}^{1+}N_{(3)}^{1+}\prod_{j=4}^{2k+2}N_{(4)}^{2},

which is the RHS of (
5.5
). Moreover, by (
5.2
), we have that

\displaystyle\Big|\bigcup_{n_{(4)},\dotsc,n_{(2k+2)}}S_{\sum_{j=4}^{2k+2}\pm n_{(j)},\kappa-\sum_{j=4}^{2k+2}\pm|n_{(j)}|^{2}}(N_{(1)},N_{(2)},N_{(3)})\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\prod_{j=4}^{2k+2}N_{(4)}^{2}\sup_{n_{(j)}\sim N_{(j)}:j\geq 4}|S_{\sum_{j=4}^{2k+2}\pm n_{(j)},\kappa-\sum_{j=4}^{2k+2}\pm|n_{(j)}|^{2}}(N_{(1)},N_{(2)},N_{(3)})|

\displaystyle\lesssim N_{(1)}^{1+}N_{(3)}^{1+}\prod_{j=4}^{2k+2}N_{(4)}^{2}.

Therefore, we obtain (
5.5
), which concludes the proof of (
5.3
).

In order to show (
5.4
), we proceed in the same way, replacing the set
E_{\kappa}
with

\begin{multlined}E_{\kappa}^{p}:=\Big\{(n_{1},\dotsc,n_{k+1},m_{1},\dotsc,m_{k+1}):n_{j}\sim N_{j},m_{j}\sim M_{j},n_{(1)}\pm n_{(2)}=0\\
\sum_{j=1}^{k+1}n_{j}+m_{j}=0,\sum_{j=1}^{k+1}|n_{j}|^{2}-|m_{j}|^{2}=\kappa\Big\}.\end{multlined}

We have the trivial inclusion

E_{\kappa}^{p}\subseteq\Big\{(n_{1},\dotsc,n_{k+1},m_{1},\dotsc,m_{k+1}):n_{j}\sim N_{j},m_{j}\sim M_{j},n_{(1)}\pm n_{(2)}=0,\sum_{j=1}^{k+1}n_{j}+m_{j}=0\Big\},

from which we immediately deduce that

|E_{\kappa}^{p}|\lesssim N_{(1)}^{2}\prod_{j=4}^{2k+2}N_{(4)}^{2},

which replaces (
5.5
) and leads to (
5.4
).
∎

We we will also need the following variant of the result above, which allows for the presence of a bounded Fourier multiplier. Since the proof is identical, we omit it.

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
f_{1},f_{2},\dotsc f_{k+1}\in X_{T}

N_{(1)}\geq N_{(2)}\geq\dotsb N_{(2k+2)}
be a rearrangement of

.

(5.7)

\Psi[f_{(1)},f_{(2)}](n_{j}:j\neq(1),(2))=\sum_{n_{(1)}+n_{(2)}=0}\Psi(n_{1},\dotsc,n_{2k+2})\widehat{P_{N_{(1)}}f_{(1)}}(n_{(1)})\widehat{P_{N_{(2)}}f_{(1)}}(n_{(2)}).

Then for every
0\leq t\leq T

(5.8)

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{0}^{t}\Psi(P_{N_{1}}f_{1}(\tau),P_{M_{1}}g_{1}(\tau),\dotsc,P_{N_{k+1}}f_{k+1}(\tau),P_{M_{k+1}}g_{k+1}(\tau))d\tau

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{T}\Psi[f_{(1)}(\tau),f_{(2)}(\tau)](P_{N_{(3)}}f_{(3)(\tau)},\dotsc,P_{N_{(2k+2)}}f_{(2k+2)}(\tau))\Big|

(5.9)

5.2.
Large phase regime

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
f_{1},f_{2},\dotsc,f_{k}\in\overline{X}_{T}
and
g_{1},g_{2},\dotsc,g_{k}\in X_{T}
. Let
N_{1},\dotsc,N_{k}
,

be dyadic numbers with
N_{1}\geq N_{2}\geq\dotsb
, and
M_{1}\geq M_{2}\geq\dotsb

N_{2}^{2}\gg kM_{1}^{2}.

Then for every
0\leq t\leq T
,

\Big|\int_{0}^{t}\prod_{j=1}^{k}P_{N_{j}}f_{j}(\tau,x)P_{M_{j}}g_{j}(\tau,x)d\tau dx\Big|\lesssim_{T}\frac{1}{N_{1}^{4}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}(N_{j}M_{j})^{2-\sigma}\|f_{j}\|_{X_{T}}\|g_{j}\|_{\overline{X}_{T}}.

Moreover, if
\Psi:(\mathbb{Z}^{2})^{2k}\to\mathbb{R}
is a bounded multiplier, we have that for every
0\leq t\leq T
,

(5.10)

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{0}^{t}\Psi\big(P_{N_{1}}f_{1}(\tau),P_{M_{1}}g_{1}(\tau),\dotsc,P_{N_{k}}f_{k}(\tau),P_{M_{k}}g_{k}(\tau)d\tau dx\big)\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{T}\frac{\|\Psi\|_{\ell^{\infty}}}{N_{1}^{4}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}(N_{j}M_{j})^{2-\sigma}\|f_{j}\|_{X_{T}}\|g_{j}\|_{\overline{X}_{T}},

where
\Psi
is defined as in (

).

Proof.

Since the proof for the general case is identical, we focus on the case
\Psi\equiv 1
. Define

(5.11)

F_{j}=e^{it\Delta}f_{j},\quad G_{j}=e^{-it\Delta}g_{j}

By definition, we have that

\|F_{j}\|_{C^{1}([0,T],{\mathcal{F}}L^{\sigma,\infty})}\lesssim\|f_{j}\|_{\overline{X}_{T}},\quad\|G_{j}\|_{C^{1}([0,T],{\mathcal{F}}L^{\sigma,\infty})}\lesssim\|g_{j}\|_{\overline{X}_{T}}.

Therefore, writing the integral in Fourier variables and integrating by parts, calling

\phi=\phi(n_{1},\dotsc,n_{k},m_{1},\dotsc,m_{k})=|n_{1}|^{2}+\dotsb+|n_{k}|^{2}-|m_{1}|^{2}-\dotsb-|m_{k}|^{2},

we get

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\prod_{j=1}^{k}P_{N_{j}}f_{j}(\tau,x)P_{M_{j}}g_{j}(\tau,x)d\tau dx

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1}+\dotsb+n_{k}\\
+m_{1}+\dotsb m_{k}=0,\\
n_{j}\sim N_{j},m_{j}\sim M_{j}\end{subarray}}{e^{i\tau\phi}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\widehat{F_{j}}(\tau,n_{j})\widehat{G_{j}}(\tau,m_{j})d\tau

\displaystyle=\left[\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1}+\dotsb+n_{k}\\
+m_{1}+\dotsb m_{k}=0,\\
n_{j}\sim N_{j},m_{j}\sim M_{j}\end{subarray}}\frac{e^{i\tau\phi}-1}{i\phi}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\widehat{F_{j}}(\tau,n_{j})\widehat{G_{j}}(\tau,m_{j})\right]_{0}^{t}

\displaystyle\phantom{XX}-\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1}+\dotsb+n_{k}\\
+m_{1}+\dotsb m_{k}=0,\\
n_{j}\sim N_{j},m_{j}\sim M_{j}\end{subarray}}\frac{e^{i\tau\phi}-1}{i\phi}\partial_{\tau}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{k}\widehat{F_{j}}(\tau,n_{j})\widehat{G_{j}}(\tau,m_{j})\right)d\tau.

By the assumptions on
N_{j},M_{j}
, we have that

|\phi|\gtrsim N_{1}^{2}.

Moreover, by Leibnitz and (

), we have that

\left|\partial_{\tau}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{k}\widehat{F_{j}}(\tau,n_{j})\widehat{G_{j}}(\tau,m_{j})\right)\right|\lesssim\prod_{j=1}^{k}N_{j}^{-\sigma}M_{j}^{-\sigma}\|f_{j}\|_{X_{T}}\|g_{j}\|_{\overline{X}_{T}}.

Therefore, by triangle inequality, we obtain that

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{t}\prod_{j=1}^{k}P_{N_{j}}f_{j}(\tau,x)P_{M_{j}}g_{j}(\tau,x)d\tau dx\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{T}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1}+\dotsb+n_{k}\\
+m_{1}+\dotsb m_{k}=0,\\
n_{j}\sim N_{j},m_{j}\sim M_{j}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{N_{1}^{2}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}N_{j}^{-\sigma}M_{j}^{-\sigma}\|f_{j}\|_{X_{T}}\|g_{j}\|_{\overline{X}_{T}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\prod_{j=2}^{k}N_{j}^{2}\times\prod_{j=1}^{k}M_{j}^{2}\times\frac{1}{N_{1}^{2}}\times\prod_{j=1}^{k}N_{j}^{-\sigma}M_{j}^{-\sigma}\|f_{j}\|_{X_{T}}\|g_{j}\|_{\overline{X}_{T}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N_{1}^{4}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}(N_{j}M_{j})^{2-\sigma}\|f_{j}\|_{X_{T}}\|g_{j}\|_{\overline{X}_{T}}.

∎

5.3.
Probabilistic bounds

As we will see in the following sections, the previous deterministic bounds can be used to estimate every term of the derivative of the modified
introduced along section
4.1
, with the exception of the following

(5.12)

\Big(\int\partial_{j}uD^{s}\overline{u}\Big)\Big(\int D^{s-2}\partial_{j}\overline{u}u|u|^{2(k-1)}\Big).

For this term, we will need the following probabilistic estimate.

Lemma 5.6

(Pairing term I, random estimate)
.

Let

u(x)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\frac{g_{n}}{\langle n\rangle^{s}}e^{in\cdot x}.

We have that

(5.13)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big|\int P_{N}(\partial_{j}u)P_{N}(D^{s}\overline{u})\Big|^{2}\lesssim N^{4-2s}.

Proof.

We have that

\int P_{N}(\partial_{j}u)P_{N}(D^{s}\overline{u})=\sum_{n\sim N}in_{j}\frac{|g_{n}|^{2}}{\langle n\rangle^{s}}=\sum_{n\sim N}in_{j}\frac{|g_{n}|^{2}-1}{\langle n\rangle^{s}}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big|\int P_{N}(\partial_{j}u)P_{N}(D^{s}\overline{u})\Big|^{2}\lesssim\sum_{n\sim N}\frac{1}{\langle n\rangle^{2s-2}}\lesssim N^{4-2s}.

∎

On top of the previous, we will also need the following bound.

Lemma 5.7

(Pairing term II, deterministic estimate)
.

Let
\max(s-1,2)<\sigma<s
. Then

(5.14)

\Big|\int D^{s-2}\partial_{j}\overline{u}u|u|^{2(k-1)}\Big|\lesssim\|u\|_{{\mathcal{F}}L^{\sigma,\infty}}^{2k}.

Proof.

By integration by parts we get

\Big|\int D^{s-2}\partial_{j}\overline{u}u|u|^{2(k-1)}\Big|\leq\|u\|_{H^{s-2}}\|u\|_{H^{1}}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2k-2}

and we conclude by the inclusions:

{\mathcal{F}}L^{\sigma,\infty}\subset H^{s-2},\quad{\mathcal{F}}L^{\sigma,\infty}\subset H^{1},\quad{\mathcal{F}}L^{\sigma,\infty}\subset L^{\infty}.

∎

We are now ready to show the required exponential bound for (
5.12
).

Lemma 5.8
.

For every
p,R>0
we have the bound

(5.15)

\int_{B_{R}}\exp\Big(p\big|\big(\int D^{s}\bar{u}\nabla u\big)\cdot\big(\int|u|^{2k-2}u^{2}D^{s-2}\bar{u}\nabla\bar{u}\big)\big|\Big)d\mu(u)<+\infty

where

B_{R}=\{u\in\mathcal{F}L^{\sigma,\infty}:\|u\|_{\mathcal{F}L^{\sigma,\infty}}\leq R\}.

Proof.

We denote

\int P_{N}(\nabla u)P_{N}(D^{s}\overline{u})=B_{N}(u).

Note that, for every
N_{0}
dyadic, we have the bound

|B_{N_{0}}(u)|\lesssim N_{0}^{s+1}\|u\|_{\mathcal{F}L^{\sigma,\infty}}^{2}.

Therefore, defining

\mathcal{Q}_{>{N_{0}}}(u)=\big(\int D^{s}\bar{u}\nabla u-\sum_{M\leq N_{0}}B_{M}(u)\big)\cdot\big(\int|u|^{2k-2}u^{2}D^{s-2}\bar{u}\nabla\bar{u}\big),

in view of (
5.14
), it is enough to show that there exists
N_{0}
dyadic such that

(5.16)

\int_{B_{R}}\exp(p|\mathcal{Q}_{>N_{0}}(u)|)d\mu(u)<\infty.

By (
5.13
), together with standard Wiener Chaos estimates (see
[
30
, Theorem I.22]
), for every
p\geq 1
, we have that

(5.17)

\mathbb{E}\Big|\int D^{s}\bar{u}\nabla u-\sum_{M\leq N_{0}}B_{M}(u)\Big|^{p}\leq p^{p}C^{p}N_{0}^{p(2-s)},

for some universal constant
C>0
.
Therefore, from (
5.14
), we have that

(5.18)

\mathbb{E}|\mathcal{Q}_{>N_{0}}(u)|^{p}\mathbbm{1}_{B_{R}}(u)\leq(C^{\prime})^{p}R^{2kp}\mathbb{E}\Big|\int D^{s}\bar{u}\nabla u-\sum_{M\leq N_{0}}B_{M}(u)\Big|^{p}\leq p^{p}(CC^{\prime})^{p}N_{0}^{p(2-s)}R^{2kp},

where
C^{\prime}
is the implicit constant in (
5.14
). Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}}\exp(p|\mathcal{Q}_{>N_{0}}(u)|)d\mu(u)

\displaystyle=\sum_{h=0}^{\infty}\int_{B_{R}}\frac{p^{h}|\mathcal{Q}_{>N_{0}}(u)|^{h}}{h!}d\mu(u)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{p^{h}(CC^{\prime})^{h}N_{0}^{h(2-s)}R^{2hp}}{h!},

so by choosing
N_{0}\gg 1
such that

p(CC^{\prime})N_{0}^{(2-s)}R^{2k}<e,

the sum above converges and we get (
5.18
).
∎

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
be such that

1=|x_{1}|\geq|x_{2}|\geq\dotsb.

Then there exists a universal constant

(6.5)

\left|\Big|\sum_{j=1}^{n}x_{j}\Big|^{s}-1-sx_{1}\cdot\sum_{j=2}^{n}x_{j}\right|\leq C|x_{2}|^{2}.

Proof.

Consider the function

given by

q(y)=\big|x_{1}+y\big|^{s}-1-sx_{1}\cdot y.

Since
s>2
, this function is

, and it is immediate to check that
q(0)=0
,
\nabla q(0)=0
. Therefore, by the mean value theorem, for every
|y|\leq n-1
, there exists a constant
C_{n}
such that

|q(y)|\leq C_{n}|y|^{2}.

The lemma follows by picking
y=\sum_{j=2}^{n}x_{j}
.
∎

Corollary 6.2
.

Let
n_{1},\dotsc,n_{2k+1}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}

n_{(1)},\dotsc,n_{(2k+1)}

(6.6)

\left|\Psi_{\rm I}^{2}+\Psi_{\rm I}^{3}\right|=|n_{2k+2}|^{s}\left|\Big|\sum_{j=1}^{2k+1}n_{j}\Big|^{s}-\sum_{j=1}^{2k+1}|n_{j}|^{s}-s|n_{(1)}|^{s-2}n_{(1)}\cdot\sum_{j=2}^{2k+1}n_{(j)}\right|\lesssim|n_{(1)}|^{2s-2}|n_{(2)}|^{2}.

Proof.

Applying (

) to

we obtain that

\left|\Big|\sum_{j=1}^{2k+1}n_{j}\Big|^{s}-|n_{(1)}|^{s}-s|n_{(1)}|^{s-2}n_{(1)}\cdot\sum_{j=2}^{2k+1}n_{(j)}\right|\lesssim|n_{(1)}|^{s-2}|n_{(2)}|^{2}.

Moroever, we clearly have that

\sum_{j=2}^{2k+1}|n_{(j)}|^{s}\lesssim|n_{(2)}|^{s}\lesssim|n_{(1)}|^{s-2}|n_{(2)}|^{2}.

Therefore, (
6.6

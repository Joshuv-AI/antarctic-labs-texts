Connected forcing density and related problems
Boris Brimkov, Randy Davila, Houston Schuerger

Abstract
A connected forcing set of a graph is a zero forcing set that induces a connected subgraph. In this paper, we introduce and study CF-dense graphs — graphs in which every vertex belongs to some minimum connected forcing set. We identify several CF-dense graph families and investigate the relationships between CF-density and analogous notions in zero forcing and total forcing. We also characterize CF-dense trees and give a formula for the number of distinct connected forcing sets in trees. Finally, we analyze when CF-density is preserved under graph operations such as Cartesian products, joins, and coronas.

Introduction

Let
G
be a simple, undirected, and finite graph, with vertex set
V(G)
, where each vertex
v\in V(G)
is colored either blue or white. The
zero forcing color change rule
is a dynamic process in which a blue vertex with exactly one white neighbor
forces
that neighbor to become blue. A set
S\subseteq V(G)
is called a
zero forcing set
if, starting from
S
as the set of initially blue vertices, repeated application of the color change rule results in all vertices of
G
becoming blue. The
zero forcing number
of
G
, denoted
Z(G)
, is the minimum cardinality of such a set. This parameter was introduced in
[
2
]
as a combinatorial tool to bound the maximum nullity of a graph.

Variants and analogues of zero forcing have appeared independently across a range of disciplines, including PMU placement in power networks
[
11
,
28
]
, target set selection in social influence models
[
1
,
5
,
16
]
, quantum control theory
[
12
]
, and fast-mixed searching
[
38
]
. Beyond these applications, zero forcing has been used in coding theory, logic circuit design, and the modeling of contagion processes in epidemiology and social networks; see
[
4
,
12
,
13
,
37
]
and the monograph
[
31
]
. As a graph invariant,
Z(G)
has also attracted considerable attention for its connections to classical parameters such as
independence number
,
vertex cover number
,
domination number
,
chromatic number
, and Hamiltonicity, and for its behavior in structured graph families such as claw-free graphs; see, e.g.,
[
3
,
6
,
8
,
17
,
18
,
21
,
24
,
27
,
35
,
36
]
and the monograph
[
29
]
.

Several variants of zero forcing have been introduced to incorporate additional structural or application-driven constraints. For instance, a zero forcing set that induces a subgraph without isolated vertices is called a
total forcing set
, and the minimum size of such a set in a graph
G
is the
total forcing number
, denoted
Z_{t}(G)
. Similarly, a zero forcing set that induces a connected subgraph is called a
connected forcing set
, and its minimum size is the
connected forcing number
, denoted
Z_{c}(G)
. These variants arise naturally in applications such as optimizing PMU placement in power networks, where minimizing wiring complexity is a key concern. Structural and algorithmic properties of total and connected forcing have been extensively studied; see
[
7
,
10
,
18
,
19
,
20
,
23
,
25
,
33
]
. Notably, computing
Z(G)
,
Z_{t}(G)
, and
Z_{c}(G)
is generally intractable, as the associated decision problems are
\mathcal{NP}
-complete
[
15
,
18
,
23
]
.

Beyond determining the minimum size of a zero forcing, total forcing, or connected forcing set, it is also of interest to study graphs in which
every
vertex belongs to some such minimum set. This idea was first formalized in
[
26
]
, where the notions of
ZF-dense
and
TF-dense
graphs were introduced. A graph is ZF-dense (respectively, TF-dense) if each vertex belongs to some minimum zero forcing set (respectively, total forcing set). A graph that is both ZF-dense and TF-dense is said to be
ZTF-dense
.

In this paper, we introduce and investigate the analogous concept of
CF-dense
graphs – graphs in which every vertex belongs to some minimum connected forcing set. While the motivations behind CF-density align with those of its analogues, our results reveal that CF-dense graphs can differ markedly in both structure and parameter values from ZF-dense and TF-dense graphs. To capture the overlap among all three notions, we define a graph to be
ZTCF-dense
if it is simultaneously ZF-dense, TF-dense, and CF-dense. Intermediate notions such as
ZCF-dense
and
TCF-dense
are defined analogously. We address the distinct behavior of CF-density by characterizing CF-dense trees and identifying several new families of ZTCF-dense graphs.

We also investigate how the connected and total forcing numbers behave under common graph operations, including Cartesian products, joins, and coronas. These results yield new families of CF-dense and ZTCF-dense graphs and lead to several new formulas for computing connected and total forcing numbers in product constructions. In particular, we show that under certain structural conditions, both CF-density and ZTCF-density are preserved under these operations.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we give notation and terminology used throughout this manuscript. In Section
3
, we examine graphs with unique connected forcing sets and characterize the number of very large or very small connected forcing sets a graph can have. Section
4
identifies several new families of CF-dense graphs. In Section
5
, we characterize all CF-dense trees and provide a complete enumeration of their minimum connected forcing sets. Section
6
explores how various graph operations affect CF-density and ZTCF-density. We conclude with final remarks and open questions in Section
7
.

Preliminaries

Throughout this paper, all graphs are assumed to be simple, undirected, and finite. Let
G
be a graph with vertex set
V(G)
and edge set
E(G)
. The
order
of

is

, and its

.
The

of a vertex

is

is denoted

. Occasionally, for notations such as

or

, the

For a set of vertices

open neighborhood
of
S
is defined as
N_{G}(S)=\bigcup_{v\in S}N_{G}(v)
.
The
boundary
of
S
, denoted
\partial_{G}(S)
, is the set of vertices in
N_{G}(S)
that do not belong to
S
; that is,
\partial_{G}(S)=N_{G}(S)\setminus S
. A graph
G
is said to be

if there exists a path between every pair of vertices. The maximal connected subgraphs of
G
are called its

and
\mathrm{comp}(G)
denotes the number of components
G
has. A graph
H
is a
subgraph
of graph
G
, denoted
H\subseteq G
, if
V(H)\subseteq V(G)
and
E(H)\subseteq E(G)
. For a subset
S\subseteq V(G)
, the subgraph
induced
by
S
is denoted

. A vertex
v\in V(G)
is a

if
G[V(G)\setminus\{v\}]

and

, provided there exists a bijective function

such that given

The complete graph, path, and cycle on
n
vertices are denoted by

,

, and

, respectively. A
tree
is a connected graph with no cycles, and a

is any acyclic graph. In a tree, a vertex of degree one is called a
leaf
, and a vertex adjacent to a leaf is a
support vertex
. A support vertex adjacent to at least two leaves is referred to as a
strong support vertex
. The
diamond graph
is the graph obtained by removing one edge from
K_{4}
. A
trivial graph
is a graph on exactly one vertex and an

graph is a graph with no trivial graph as a component.

For any graph-theoretic terminology not defined here, we refer the reader to
[
29
]
. For zero forcing conventions, we refer to the monograph
[
31
]
. We will also use the standard notation
[k]=\{1,\dots,k\}
.

3
Uniqueness of Connected Forcing Sets

A central theme of this paper is the study of CF-dense graphs – graphs in which every vertex belongs to some minimum connected forcing set. As a natural complement to this, we begin by exploring the uniqueness and extremal behavior of connected forcing sets. In particular, we show that if a graph
G
of order
n
has a unique connected forcing set of a given size, then that size must be
n
or
Z_{c}(G)
. We also characterize graphs based on the number of very large or very small connected forcing sets they admit, shedding light on the structural rigidity or flexibility of their forcing behavior.

To formalize our study of connected forcing sets of various sizes, we introduce the following notation.

Definition 1
.

Let
G
be a connected graph of order
n
. For each integer
1\leq i\leq n
, let
z_{c}(G;i)
denote the number of distinct connected forcing sets of
G
of size
i
.

In particular,
z_{c}(G;Z_{c}(G))
counts the number of minimum connected forcing sets of
G
. The following proposition gives a natural range for this quantity.

Proposition 1
.

For any connected graph
G
,

1\leq z_{c}(G;Z_{c}(G))\leq\binom{n}{Z_{c}(G)},

and both bounds are tight.

Proof.

The lower bound follows from the fact that
G
must have at least one connected forcing set of minimum size by the definition of
Z_{c}(G)
. The upper bound follows from the fact that there are at most
\binom{n}{Z_{c}(G)}
subsets of
V(G)
of size
Z_{c}(G)
.

To show both bounds are tight, note that the graph shown in Figure
1
has a unique minimum connected forcing set, so
z_{c}(G;Z_{c}(G))=1
. On the other hand, for the complete graph
K_{n}
, we have
Z_{c}(K_{n})=n-1
, and every
(n-1)
-subset of vertices forms a connected forcing set. Thus,
z_{c}(K_{n};Z_{c}(K_{n}))=\binom{n}{n-1}=\binom{n}{Z_{c}(K_{n})}
.
∎

1
by showing that only a minimum and maximum connected forcing set can be unique.

Lemma 2
.

For
n\geq 2
,
z_{c}(P_{n};i)=\begin{cases}2&\text{if\; }i=1\\
n-i+1&\text{if\; }2\leq i\leq n.\end{cases}

Proof.

Either endpoint of
P_{n}
is a connected forcing set of size 1, so
z_{c}(P_{n};1)=2
. Label the vertices of
P_{n}
in order starting from one endpoint and let
R
be a connected forcing set of size
i
. There are
n-(i-1)
ways to choose the vertex in
R
with the smallest label
j
. This choice uniquely determines
R
, since the other
i-1
vertices in
R
must be the vertices with labels
j+1,\ldots,j+(i-1)
. Thus,
z_{c}(P_{n};i)=n-i+1
for
2\leq i\leq n
.
∎

Theorem 3
.

z_{c}(G;i)=1
only if
i=Z_{c}(G)
or
i=n
.

Proof.

Suppose
G
has a unique connected forcing set
R
of size
i
, where
Z_{c}(G)<i<n
. Let
R^{\prime}
be a connected forcing set of size
i-1
. Then
R^{\prime}\subset R
, since otherwise
R^{\prime}
together with any neighbor of
R^{\prime}
forms a connected forcing set of size
i
different from
R
. Let
\{v\}=R\backslash R^{\prime}
. Then
\partial_{G}(R^{\prime})=\{v\}
, since otherwise
R^{\prime}
together with either of its two or more neighbors would form a connected forcing set of size
i
. Moreover, for all
u\in\partial_{G}(R)
,
N_{G}(u)\cap R=\{v\}
, since if some
u\in\partial_{G}(R)
was adjacent to a vertex in
R
other than
v
,
v
would not be the only vertex in
\partial_{G}(R^{\prime})
.

If
R^{\prime}
consists of a single vertex, then
G
is a path, and by Proposition
2
, there is no unique connected forcing set of size
i<n
; thus,
R^{\prime}
has at least two vertices. Let
w
be a non-cut vertex of
G[R]
different from
v
, and let
u
be a vertex in
\partial_{G}(R)
. We claim
R\backslash\{w\}\cup\{u\}
is a connected forcing set of
G
. This set is connected by construction, and it is forcing since
w
can be forced in the first timestep by any of its neighbors in
R^{\prime}
. This contradicts
R
being the unique connected forcing set of size
i
.
∎

We conclude this section by characterizing
z_{c}(G;i)
for extremal values of
i
.

Proposition 4
.

If
G
is a connected graph of order
n
, then

(1)

z_{c}(G;n)=1
,

(2)

z_{c}(G;n-1)=\left|\left\{v\in V:\mathrm{comp}(G\setminus\{v\})=1\right\}\right|
,

(3)

z_{c}(G;n-2)=\left|\left\{\begin{aligned} \{u,v\}\subset V:\ &u\neq v,\ \mathrm{comp}(G\setminus\{u,v\})=1,\\
&N_{G}(u)\setminus\{v\}\neq N_{G}(v)\setminus\{u\}\end{aligned}\right\}\right|,

(4)

z_{c}(G;1)=\begin{cases}2&\text{if }G\simeq P_{n}\text{ for }n\geq 2,\\
1&\text{if }G\simeq P_{1},\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Proof.

The numbers of the proofs below correspond to the numbers in the statement of the theorem.

(1)

The only connected forcing size of size
n
is
V
.

(2)

Since
G
is connected, any non-cut vertex
v
has a neighbor that can force
v
. Thus, each set which excludes one non-cut vertex of
G
is a connected forcing set of size
n-1
; moreover, no set which excludes a cut vertex is a connected forcing set.

(3)

Let
u,v
be two non-isolated vertices of
G
; if
N_{G}(u)\backslash\{v\}\neq N_{G}(v)\backslash\{u\}
, there is a vertex
w
adjacent to one of
u
and
v
, but not the other. Suppose
uw\in E(G)
; then,
w
can force
u
and any neighbor of
v
can force
v
(since
d(v)\neq 0
). Thus, any pair of vertices
u,v
satisfying these conditions can be excluded from a zero forcing set of size
n-2
. On the other hand, a pair of vertices
u,v
that does not satisfy these conditions cannot be excluded from a zero forcing set, since every vertex that is adjacent to one will be adjacent to the other, and hence no vertex will be able to force
u
or
v
. Moreover, any pair of vertices that form a separating set cannot be excluded from any connected forcing set of size
n-2
.

(4)

The only graph with connected forcing number 1 is
P_{n}
; thus if
G\not\simeq P_{n}
,
z_{c}(G;1)=0
. If
G\simeq P_{n}
and
n\geq 2
, either end of the path is a connected forcing set; if
n=1
, there is a single connected forcing set.

We note that since graphs
G
with
Z_{c}(G)=2
have been completely characterized in
[
9
]
, it is possible to obtain a closed form expression for
z_{c}(G;2)
. However, such an expression would have numerous cases depending on the structure of
G
, and we have left this description for future work.

4
Families of ZTCF-Dense Graphs

In this section, we identify several families of graphs that are ZTCF-dense. Some of the arguments are extensions of results in
[
26
]
on ZF-dense and TF-dense graphs.

Observation 5
.

Every cycle graph
C_{n}
is ZTCF-dense.

Proof.

Any two adjacent vertices of
C_{n}
form a minimum zero forcing, total forcing, and connected forcing set.
∎

Observation 6
.

The complete graph
K_{n}
is ZCF-dense for
n\geq 1
, and ZTCF-dense for
n\geq 2
.

Proof.

If
n\geq 3
, any
n-1
vertices of
K_{n}
form a minimum zero forcing, total forcing, and connected forcing set.
K_{2}
is clearly ZTCF-dense, and
K_{1}
is ZCF-dense but not TF-dense since
K_{1}
has no total forcing set.
∎

The following chain of inequalities relating the minimum degree and the zero forcing, total forcing, and connected forcing numbers is well known and follows from the definitions.

Observation 7
.

If
G
is a connected graph different from a path with minimum degree
\delta(G)
, then

\delta(G)\leq Z(G)\leq Z_{t}(G)\leq Z_{c}(G).

wheel graph

Proposition 8
.

If
G
is a wheel graph, then

Proof.

Let

G[S]
is connected, and is also a zero forcing set since the two adjacent vertices on the

can force all other vertices in that cycle. By Observation

,
3=\delta(G)\leq Z_{c}(G)\leq|S|

. Moreover, since

can be chosen to contain any vertex of

The
k

hypercube

Q_{k}
is defined recursively as follows:

Q_{1}=K_{2}
, and for
k\geq 2
,
Q_{k}=Q_{k-1}\,\Box\,K_{2}
, where
\,\Box\,
denotes the Cartesian product of graphs.

Proposition 9
.

If
G
is a hypercube, then
G
is ZTCF-dense.

Proof.

Let
G=Q_{k}=Q_{k-1}\,\Box\,K_{2}
. Let
S
be the set consisting of all vertices in one of the two copies of
Q_{k-1}
(see Figure
2
(b)).
G[S]
is connected, and is also a zero forcing set since each vertex can force its neighbor in the other copy of
Q_{k-1}
in the first timestep. In
[

]
it was shown that
Z(Q_{k})=2^{k-1}
. Thus, by Observation

,
2^{k-1}=Z(G)\leq Z_{c}(G)\leq|S|=2^{k-1}
, so
Z_{c}(G)=2^{k-1}
. Moreover, since
S
can be chosen to be either of the two copies of
Q_{k-1}
, every vertex of

belongs to some minimum connected forcing set. Thus,
G
is CF-dense. In
[
26
]
it is shown that hypercubes are ZTF-dense; therefore,
G
is ZTCF-dense.
∎

A
star

S_{n}
is the complete bipartite graph
K_{1,n-1}
. The degree
n-1
vertex of
S_{n}
is called the central vertex.

Proposition 10
.

The star graph
S_{n}
is TCF-dense for
n\geq 4
(but not ZF-dense).

Proof.

Let
v
be any leaf of
S_{n}
. Any set
S
consisting of all vertices of
S_{n}
except
v
is connected and forcing, and is also minimum since when
n\geq 4
, omitting two leaves will cause the set to not be forcing, and omitting the central vertex will cause the set to have isolates. Thus,
S_{n}
is TCF-dense. On the other hand, the central vertex of
S_{n}
is not contained in any minimum zero forcing set. Thus,
S_{n}
is not ZF-dense. Note also that
S_{3}
is not CF-dense, since then the central vertex would not be contained in any minimum connected forcing set.
∎

A
complete multipartite graph
is a graph whose vertex set can be partitioned into
k\geq 2
disjoint independent sets
V_{1},\ldots,V_{k}
, and every vertex in
V_{i}
is adjacent to every vertex in
V_{j}
for all
i\neq j
.

Proposition 11
.

If
G
is a complete multipartite graph that is not a star, then
G
is ZTCF-dense.

Proof.

Let
V_{1},\ldots,V_{k}
be the independent sets that partition the vertices of
G
, with
1\leq|V_{1}|\leq\cdots\leq|V_{k}|
. If
|V_{k}|=1
, then
G=K_{n}
(
n\geq 2
), which is ZTCF-dense by Observation
6
. Now suppose that
|V_{k}|\geq 2
. Since
G
is not a star, either
k\geq 3
or
k=2
and
|V_{1}|\geq 2
.

Suppose first that
k\geq 3
. Let
u,v\in V_{k}
,
a\in V_{i}
, and
b\in V_{j}
(where
i
,
j
, and
k
are all different). Let
S=V(G)\setminus\{u,a\}
. Then,
G[S]
is connected since
x,v,y
is a path between any two vertices
x,y
not in
V_{k}
, and
x,b,y
is a path between any two vertices
x,y
in
V_{k}
.

Now suppose that
k=2
and
|V_{1}|\geq 2
. Let
u,v\in V_{1}
and
a,b\in V_{2}
. Let
S=V(G)\setminus\{u,a\}
. Then,
G[S]
is connected since
x,v,y
is a path between any two vertices

in
V_{2}
, and
x,b,y
is a path between any two vertices

in
V_{1}
.

In either case,
S
is a zero forcing set since first

can force

, and then

can force
u
. Thus,
S
is a connected forcing set. In
[
2
]
it was shown that
Z(G)=n-2
, and since

is not a path, by Observation

,
Z(G)\leq Z_{c}(G)
. Thus,

, so
Z_{c}(G)=n-2
. Moreover, in either case,
S
can be chosen to contain any vertex of
G
. Thus, every vertex of
G
belongs to at least one minimum connected forcing set, so

is CF-dense. Finally, it was shown in
[
26
]
that if
G
is a complete multipartite graph that is not a star, then
G
is ZTF-dense. Thus,
G
is ZTCF-dense.
∎

Most of the ZTCF-dense graphs identified so far satisfied
Z(G)=Z_{t}(G)=Z_{c}(G)
. However, in general, ZTCF-dense graphs need not have equality among these parameters. The next result shows a family of ZTCF-dense graphs with

For

, the

, and adding the edges

for

. Diamond necklaces were introduced in
[

]
. A diamond necklace

is shown in Figure

[

]
and
[

]
.

Theorem 12

(
[

]
,
[

]
)
.

If

, then

Z(G)=\tfrac{1}{4}n+2,\quad Z_{t}(G)=\tfrac{1}{2}n.

Theorem 13
.

If
G

, then
G

Z_{c}(G)=\tfrac{3}{4}n-2.

Let

Let
S^{\prime}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}\{a_{i},b_{i},x_{i}\}

2)

5
Connected Forcing in Trees

In this section, we characterize all CF-dense trees. We also give a formula to count all connected forcing sets (of any size) in trees. We will begin with recalling some definitions and known results.

Definition 2
.

Let
G
be a connected and non-path graph, and let
v\in V
be a vertex of degree at least three. A
pendent path attached to

is a maximal induced path
P\subseteq V
such that

is a connected component of
G-v
, one endpoint of which is adjacent to
v
. The neighbor of
v
in
P
is called the
base
of the path, and we denote by

the number of pendent paths attached to

.

Definition 3
.

If
\mathrm{comp}(G)
denotes the number of connected components of

, then

\displaystyle R_{1}(G)

\displaystyle=\{v\in V:\mathrm{comp}(G-v)=2,\;p(v)=1\},

\displaystyle R_{2}(G)

\displaystyle=\{v\in V:\mathrm{comp}(G-v)=2,\;p(v)=0\},

\displaystyle R_{3}(G)

\displaystyle=\{v\in V:\mathrm{comp}(G-v)\geq 3\}.

Definition 4
.

An
\mathcal{M}
-set
of a graph
G
is any set of vertices that contains:

1)

All vertices in
R_{2}(G)
and
R_{3}(G)
, and

2)

For each vertex
v\in V(G)
with
d_{G}(v)\geq 3
, all-but-one of the bases of the pendent paths attached to
v
.

Lemma 14

(
[
10
]
)
.

If
G
is a connected graph different from a path and
R\subseteq V(G)
is an arbitrary connected forcing set of
G
, then
R
contains an
\mathcal{M}
-set.

In view of Lemma
14
,
\mathcal{M}
-sets can be understood as sets of “mandatory vertices” (up to the choice of bases of pendent paths), which appear in every connected forcing set.

Theorem 15

(
[
10
]
)
.

If
T
is a tree different from a path, then any
\mathcal{M}
-set is a minimum connected forcing set of
T
.

We now state the first main result of this section.

Theorem 16
.

If
T
is a tree, then
T
is CF-dense if and only if either:

(a)

T\in\{P_{1},P_{2}\}
, or

(b)

Every support vertex of
T
is a strong support vertex.

Proof.

By Lemma
14
and Theorem
15
, a set
S\subseteq V(T)
is a minimum connected forcing set of a tree
T
if and only if
S
is an
\mathcal{M}
-set. Thus,
T
is CF-dense if and only if every vertex of
T
lies in at least one
\mathcal{M}
-set.

From the definition of
\mathcal{M}
-sets, a vertex
v\in V(T)
lies in some minimum connected forcing set if and only if either
v\in R_{2}(T)\cup R_{3}(T)
or
v
is the base of a pendent path attached to a vertex with at least one other pendent path.

This implies that a vertex on a pendent path is included in a minimum connected forcing set only if it is the base of that path, and only if the vertex it attaches to has at least one other pendent path. In other words, all pendent paths must consist of a single vertex (the base), and be attached to a vertex that is incident to at least one additional pendent path. This condition is precisely equivalent to the statement that every support vertex is a strong support vertex.

Thus, a tree
T
is CF-dense if and only if either
T\in\{P_{1},P_{2}\}
, or every support vertex in
T
is a strong support vertex.
∎

It is easy to see that the condition in Theorem

can be verified in linear time.

Graph Operations and CF-Density

In this section we explore the effect of Cartesian products, coronas, and joins on the total forcing and connected forcing numbers of graphs. We then derive conditions under which CF-density is preserved under these operations. This allows us to generate new families of ZTCF-dense graphs in addition to the ones identified in Sections

6.1
Cartesian products

The

of graphs

and

and

Lemma 19

If

We verify that

is a zero forcing set. Let

starting from

. Then there exists a vertex

such that

Using Lemma

6.2

The

and edge set

]

Lemma 27

(
[
36
]
)
.

If
G
and
H
are graphs, with each either connected or isolate free, then

Z(G\vee H)=\min\{Z(G)+|V(H)|,Z(H)+|V(G)|\}.

Lemma 28

(
[
26
]
)
.

If
G
and
H
are graphs, with each either connected or isolate free, then

Z_{t}(G\vee H)=\min\{Z(G)+|V(H)|,Z(H)+|V(G)|\}.

We now extend these results to connected forcing.

Lemma 29
.

If
G
and
H
are graphs, with each either connected or isolate free, then

(a)

For each zero forcing set
B_{G}
of
G
and each zero forcing set
B_{H}
of
H
,
B_{G}\cup V(H)
and
B_{H}\cup V(H)
are zero forcing sets of

.

(b)

Z_{c}(G\vee H)=\min\{Z(G)+|V(H)|,Z(H)+|V(G)|\}
.

Proof.

First let
B_{G}
be a zero forcing set of
G
and consider the set
B=B_{G}\cup V(H)
. Since there are no white vertices outside of

, forcing in

can proceed from

exactly as forcing in

proceeds from

, and thus

is a zero forcing set of

. Finally, since in

is adjacent every vertex in

, each pair of vertices in

is either adjacent or distance 2. Thus

is connected, and so

is a connected forcing set of

. A similar argument shows that for each zero forcing set

of

,

is a zero forcing set of

.

Now let
B_{G}
be a minimum zero forcing set of

and suppose without loss of generality that

. So

. Finally, we obtain equality since
Z_{c}(G\vee H)\geq Z(G\vee H)=\min\{Z(G)+|V(H)|,Z(H)+|V(G)|\}
, by Lemma

.
∎

Theorem 30
.

Let
G
and
H
be graphs of order

and

respectively, with each either connected or isolate free. If

is ZF-dense and

, then

is ZTCF-dense.

Proof.

First note that it was shown in

]
, that under these conditions

is also CF-dense. Next note that since

. So if

is a minimum zero forcing set of

is a minimum zero forcing set of

.

Now let

be arbitrary. If

, then for any minimum zero forcing set

of

,

. So suppose that

. Since

is ZF-dense, there exists a minimum zero forcing set

of

6.3

The

of

with

, denoted

|V(G)|
copies of

and joining the

th vertex of

to every vertex in the

th copy of

[

]
(with a small correction to the result made in

]

Proposition 32

(
[
14
,
32
]
)
.

If
G
and

be graphs of order
q
and
r\geq 2
and

is isolate-free, then

.

We now extend this result in a way that will be helpful for discussing forcing density.

Lemma 33
.

Let
G
and
H
be graphs such that
H
has no isolated vertices. If
B_{G}
is a zero forcing set of
G
,
B_{H}
is a zero forcing set of
H
, and
B_{H}^{i}
is the copy of
B_{H}
in the
i
th copy of
H
, then
B=B_{G}\cup(\bigcup_{i=1}^{q}B_{H}^{i})
is a zero forcing set of
G\circ H
. In addition, if
B_{G}
and
B_{H}
are both minimum, then
B
is minimum.

Proof.

First construct
B
as above. Allowing the forcing process in
G\circ H
to begin at
B
, forces will alternate between those in
G

u_{i}\rightarrow u_{j}
when every vertex in the
i
th copy of
H
is blue and those in copies of
H
when the vertex
u_{i}
is blue, and thus
B
is a zero forcing set of

. If in addition,
B_{G}
and

are minimum, then
|B|=qZ(H)+Z(G)=Z(G\circ H)
. So by Proposition
32
,

is a minimum zero forcing set of

.
∎

Lemma 34
.

Let
G
and
H
be graphs such that
H
has no isolated vertices. Enumerate the vertices of
G
,
\{u_{i}\}_{i=1}^{q}
, and for each
i\in[q]
, let
H_{i}
be the
i
th copy of
H
. If
B^{\prime}
is any zero forcing set of
G\circ H
, then it can be assumed that for each
i\in[q]
,
u_{i}
forces no vertices in
H_{i}
during the forcing process beginning at

.

Proof.

Let
B^{\prime}
be a zero forcing set of

, and for each
i\in[q]
, let
H_{i}^{\prime}=(G\circ H)[V(H_{i})\cup\{u_{i}\}]
. Since each
u_{i}
is a dominating vertex of

,
u_{i}
will have more than one white neighbor until only one vertex
w
of

is white. Since

has no isolated vertices,
w
has a neighbor
v
in
H_{i}
; and at this point in the forcing process, since every vertex in
H_{i}^{\prime}
is blue except for

,

can force

. So it can be assumed that during the forcing process beginning at
B^{\prime}
,
u_{i}
does not force any vertices in
H_{i}
.
∎

Lemma 35
.

Let
G
be a graph and
H
be a graph with no isolated vertices.

•

If
Z_{t}(H)=Z(H)
, then
Z_{t}(G\circ H)=|V(G)|\cdot Z(H)+Z(G)
.

•

If
Z_{t}(H)>Z(H)
, then
Z_{t}(G\circ H)=|V(G)|\cdot Z(H)+|V(G)|
.

Proof.

Begin by enumerating the vertices of
G
,
\{u_{i}\}_{i=1}^{q}
. Next, for each
i\in[q]
, let
H_{i}^{\prime}=(G\circ H)[V(H_{i})\cup\{u_{i}\}]
.

First suppose that
Z_{t}(H)=Z(H)
and note that by Proposition
32
,
Z_{t}(G\circ H)\geq Z(G\circ H)=|V(G)|\cdot Z(H)+Z(G)
. If
B_{G}
is a minimum total forcing set of
G
,
B_{H}
is a minimum zero forcing set of
H
, and
B_{H}^{i}
is the copy of
B_{H}
in the
i
th copy of
H
, then by Lemma
33
and since total forcing sets are zero forcing sets,
B=B_{G}\cup(\bigcup_{i=1}^{q}B_{H}^{i})
is a zero forcing set of
G\circ H
. Furthermore, for each
i\in[q]
, since
B_{H}^{i}
is a total forcing set, each vertex in
B_{H}^{i}
has a neighbor in
B_{H}^{i}
, and since each vertex
u_{i}
is a dominating vertex of
H_{i}^{\prime}
, each
u_{i}\in B_{G}
has neighbors in
B_{H}^{i}
. Thus
B
is a total forcing set of
G\circ H
and since
Z_{t}(H)=Z(H)
,
Z_{t}(G\circ H)\leq|V(G)|\cdot Z_{t}(H)+Z(G)=|V(G)|\cdot Z(H)+Z(G)
.

Next suppose that
Z_{t}(H)>Z(H)
and let
B=V(G)\cup\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{q}B_{H}^{i}\right)
such that
B_{H}
is a zero forcing set of
H
and for each
i\in[q]
,
B_{H}^{i}
is the copy of
B_{H}
in
H
. Clearly,
V(G)
is a zero forcing set of
G
, so by Lemma
33
,
B
is a zero forcing set of
G\circ H
. Furthermore, since each
u_{i}
is a dominating vertex of
H_{i}^{\prime}
, each vertex in
V(G)
has a neighbor in
B
and each vertex in
B_{H}^{i}
has a neighbor in
B
. Thus
B
is a total forcing set of
G\circ H
, and
Z_{t}(G\circ H)\leq|V(G)|\cdot Z(H)+|V(G)|
.

Finally, let
B
be an arbitrary minimum total forcing set of
G\circ H
. Since each vertex
u_{i}
is a dominating vertex of
H_{i}^{\prime}
and
H
has no isolated vertices,
Z(H_{i}^{\prime})=Z(H)+1
. Furthermore, since
u_{i}
is the only vertex in
H_{i}^{\prime}
with neighbors outside of

, at most one vertex in
H_{i}^{\prime}
can be forced by a vertex outside of

, and so

. Now suppose, by way of contradiction, that

. First consider the case where

. Since

,
|B\cap V(H_{i})|<Z(H_{i})
cannot be a zero forcing set of

. In turn, by Lemma

it can be assumed that

does not force any vertices in

, so

cannot be a zero forcing set of

. Now consider the case where

, it can be assumed that
u_{i}
forces no vertices in

, so it must be the case that

is a minimum zero forcing set of

. However,

, so there must exist vertices in

which do not have neighbors in

and

cannot be a total forcing set of

. Thus, for each

,

. So

.
∎

Lemma 36
.

If
G
is a connected graph and

is an isolate-free graph, then

Proof.

Begin by enumerating the vertices of
G
,
\{u_{i}\}_{i=1}^{q}
. Next, for each
i\in[q]
, let
H_{i}^{\prime}=(G\circ H)[V(H_{i})\cup\{u_{i}\}]
.

First let
B=V(G)\cup\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{q}B_{H}^{i}\right)
, where

is a zero forcing set of

, and for each

,

is the copy of

in

. Since

is a zero forcing set of

and

is a zero forcing set of

, by Lemma

,

is a zero forcing set of

. Furthermore, since every vertex in

.

Now let

be a minimum connected forcing set of

. By Lemma

, it can be assumed that

forces no vertices in

Conclusion

In this paper, we introduced and studied the concept of connected forcing density, extending prior work on zero forcing and total forcing to incorporate connectivity constraints. In Section
3
, we gave necessary conditions for the uniqueness of connected forcing sets, and characterized the number of distinct connected forcing sets of extremal sizes. In Section
4
, we identified a rich collection of ZTCF-dense graphs, including a family in which
Z(G)<Z_{t}(G)<Z_{c}(G)
. In Section
5
, we characterized CF-dense trees. We also derived a closed form expression for counting the number of connected forcing sets of any size in trees. In Section
6
, we established structural results for constructing new ZTCF-dense graphs via Cartesian products, joins, and coronas. We also introduced results concerning the total and connected forcing numbers of Cartesian products, joins, and coronas of graphs.

Our results underscore the combinatorial richness of connected forcing and its interplay with structural graph properties. Future directions include classifying CF-dense graphs within other graph families (such as chordal graphs, unicyclic graphs, or planar graphs), exploring probabilistic models of CF-density in random graphs, and further investigating algorithmic and complexity aspects of identifying minimum connected forcing sets and CF-dense graphs.

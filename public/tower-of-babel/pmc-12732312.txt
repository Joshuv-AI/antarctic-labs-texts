Robotic-assisted bilateral lumbar pars fracture endoscopic debridement and direct repair as treatment for lumbar radiculopathy: A case report
Authors: S Garg, Y Wang, N Lehman, M Gallizzi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundManagement of lumbar spondylolysis in the athletic population can be successful with conservative means, however surgical intervention may be required for fractures presenting with radiculopathy. Direct repair with intralaminar screws is a commonly used technique which has been described previously with various rates of success. Technological advancements now allow minimally invasive direct pars repair with robotic assistance as a viable option. Robotic assistance for instrumentation and stabilization of the fracture can also be combined with endoscopic spine techniques, to debride the fracture site, to effectively treat this issue.MethodsA 16-year-old giant slalom skier presented with low back pain and associated bilateral L5 radiculopathy to our facility. Using MRI and CT, they were diagnosed with bilateral L5 pars interarticularis fractures with bony edema and with subtle hypermobile retrolisthesis in combination with bilateral foraminal stenosis at the L5-S1 disc space. Due to patient’s active sports participation and continued symptoms despite rest, physical therapy, chiropractic therapy, and bracing, the patient decided to undergo elective, outpatient, bilateral robotic-assisted percutaneous endoscopic debridement of the pars fractures through 7-mm skin incisions, and bilateral percutaneous intralaminar fixation of the pars fractures with pedicle screws through 1.5-cm skin incisions.ResultsIn the immediate postoperative period, the patient reported complete resolution of mechanical low back pain and associated bilateral radiculopathy. The patient had resolution of incisional low back pain by 6 weeks postoperatively and demonstrated stable healing with no evidence of hardware failure radiographically through their 6-month postoperative x-rays. This 16-year-old was able to return to prior level of sport and competition at 3-months postoperatively.ConclusionsThis novel minimally invasive robotic-assisted percutaneous approach for lumbar spondylolysis fracture debridement and direct repair is a suitable option for nonhealing pars fracture with radiculopathy in high performing active adolescent patients.Keywords: Lumbar, Minimally invasive, Pars fracture, Robotic, Radiculopathy, Spondylolysis

Background

Pars interarticularis fracture, or lumbar spondylolysis, is characterized by a fracture in the pars isthmus. While isolated fractures are possible, they present frequently in the adolescent athletic population relative to other cohorts [1]. Patient involvement in sports such as American football, gymnastics, weightlifting (deadlifts, squats), swimming (butterfly and breaststroke), tennis [2], diving, cricket, baseball/softball, volleyball, wrestling, sailing, skiing, and rugby have been associated with pars fractures [[3], [4], [5]].

The etiology of injury is primarily related to repetitive hyperextension and axial rotation. While normal axial compression stresses the pars interarticularis, extension loading increases the forces experienced at this location [6,7]. These fractures often present as axial low back pain worsened on extension. In some cases, these fractures may be asymptomatic and are only detected in adulthood when the injury progresses and becomes chronic.

Initial stress injuries during childhood and adolescence are hypothesized to induce fatiguability within the pars interarticularis predisposing individuals to the development of bony instability in later life. Sairyo et al. [8] identified that the majority of pars fractures have the potential to heal conservatively in the early stages of injury. However, over time, unhealed fractures can lead to the formation of a false joint, also known as pseudoarthrosis (PA) [9]. Due to the inherent weakness at this site, PA within the pars interarticularis may progress to the anterior slippage of the superior vertebral body anteriorly over the inferior vertebral body in a condition known as isthmic spondylolisthesis [5]. This progression may compromise the underlying neural elements of lumbar spine including the cauda equina and/or exiting nerve roots. Depending on the severity of spondylolisthesis, patients may present with paresthesia, lower extremity muscular weakness, gait changes, and/or incontinence. Thus, the spectrum of pathologies that may result from injury to pars interarticularis ranges from spondylolysis to a more severe progressive or acute isthmic spondylolisthesis [10].

In patients with spondylosis who failed extensive nonoperative treatment, surgical treatment is an option. In cases which have not progressed to isthmic spondylolisthesis, direct surgical pars interarticularis repair can assist in relieving pain and restore function. While open surgical techniques exist, emerging data suggests that minimally invasive treatments may offer superior clinical outcomes for patients [11]. While minimally invasive surgery does offer potential benefits for patients, there are associated risks primarily related to the poor visualization of anatomical structures and lengthy technical learning curve [12,13]. Interestingly, the use of robotic guidance during surgery may help eliminate these associated difficulties [14]. Our case is the first to the authors’ knowledge to describe the use of both endoscopic fracture debridement combined with robotic guidance for direct repair of a pars interarticularis fracture with associated lumbar radiculopathy.

Methods

This study is presented as a retrospective case report and informed consent was provided by the subject.

Clinical presentation

An otherwise healthy 16-year-old male presented to interventional pain specialist with a worsening 1-month history of sharp, aching low back pain with associated lower extremity stiffness and neuralgia in a bilateral L5 distribution, believed to have originated after slalom skiing a month prior to initial presentation. The patient’s pain radiated from the lumbosacral region into his buttocks bilaterally and into his posterolateral thighs. Patient denied urinary or bowel dysfunction. The patient reported exacerbation of symptoms with sporting activity (primarily skiing) and ambulation, specifically lumbar extension and flexion. Prior to presentation, the patient reported trials of NSAIDs, acetaminophen, activity modification, physical therapy, and chiropractic therapy for >6 weeks with minimal improvement. Lumbar radiographs (AP, lateral, flexion, extension) were ordered which showed subtle hypermobile retrolisthesis at L5-S1 with suspected subtle pars fractures at L5.

Due to the persistent nature of symptoms, the patient was recommended to undergo magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) of the lumbar spine. The MRI demonstrated bilateral L5 osseous edema in the pedicle and pars (Fig. 1A and B), a subacute fracture extending through the posterior right L5 pedicle into the right pars isthmus and an additional subacute fracture of the left L5 pars isthmus. Patient was prescribed a lumbosacral orthotic and was advised to cease all high-impact activities and to avoid truncal rotation, flexion, and extension to prevent further injury. The patient was then scheduled for a lumbar computed tomography (CT) scan and referred to the senior author for surgical evaluation for the bilateral pars fracture.

A (left) and B (right). Osseous edema in the pedicle of L5.

During the patient’s evaluation by the senior author, he presented with bilateral lower lumbar spine pain that was more prominent on the left side. In addition, the patient had left sided mechanical clicking and popping with position changes. Previously described bilateral buttock and posterolateral thigh pain were still present, and the patient reported worsened bilateral hamstring tightness. On physical examination, low back and radicular pain was reproduced via bilateral lumbar extension and facet loading, though strength was intact bilaterally with the remaining neurological exam within normal limits.

Advanced imaging with CT of the lumbar spine demonstrated incompletely healed bilateral pars fracture at L5 with subtle hypermobile retrolisthesis with bilateral foraminal stenosis secondary to advanced spondylosis and a broad-based disc bulge at the L5-S1 disc space (Fig. 2A and B).

A (left) and B (right). Incompletely healed bilateral PARS fracture on axial and sagittal CT imaging.

Results

Given the patient’s desire to return to high level sports in a timely manner, their clinical presentation, and failure of nonoperateive treatment, the decision was made to proceed with the above mentioned elective surgical intervention. A plan was made for robotic-assisted endoscopic debridement of the bilateral L5 pars fracture sites and percutaneous internal fixation of the bilateral L5 pars fractures with assistance of the Globus Robotic Excelsius Navigation system using 2 headless small diameter pedicle screws. The patient was taken to the operating theater and received general endotracheal anesthesia as well as IV antimicrobial prophylaxis. With neuromonitoring leads placed and baselines obtained, the patient was rolled prone onto a well-padded open top Jackson frame. Two reference arrays were placed in the bilateral posterior superior iliac spine and bone marrow aspirate autograft was obtained. Intraoperative scan by the Globus Excelsius 3D imaging system was obtained and merged with the Globus Excelsius robotic arm. Two small 1.5-cm vertical skin incisions were made for pars screw placement at bilateral L5, and separate 7-mm incisions were made for the direct endoscopic debridement of the pars fractures. The trajectories of each screw and of the endoscope were planned via Globus’ Excelsisus integrated robotic software.

Using robotic assistance, endoscopic debridement of the left-sided fracture site was performed, consisting of decorticating the adjacent lamina area using a high-speed burr, and this was done according to the preplanned trajectory (Fig. 3A and B). Subsequently, a 4.5 × 25mm pedicle screw was placed through the left L5 pars fracture in accordance with the preplanned trajectories. Nerve root irritation with electromyographic stimulation was assessed and verified via intraoperative fluoroscopic images (Fig. 3C, D, and E). This entire process was then repeated on the right side to debride and repair the pars fracture.

(A) Endoscopic targeting. (B) Visualization of anatomy in real time. (C) Undersized tap prior to placement of final screws. (D) Confirmation of screw trajectory superimposed on the preplanned trajectory. (E) Placement of screws.

The patient awoke from the operation without complication and was discharged home directly from the postanesthesia care unit. The patient reported being able to ambulate without difficulty the following day and required minimal opioid medications postoperatively with no opioids outside of the first week after surgery.

At 2 weeks follow-up, the patient reported some mild itching and incisional soreness, but otherwise was asymptomatic, and reported resolution of the neurologic deficits throughout their lower extremities. Postoperative AP and lateral radiograph at this time demonstrated well-positioned screws with no signs of lucency (Fig. 4A and B). Patient was advised to continue usage of compression socks as well as avoid moisture at the incisions until his 6-week follow-up visit. At his 6-week postoperative visit, patient reported complete resolution of low back and buttock pain and no longer had any incisional pain. At his 12-week follow-up, the patient again reported complete symptomatic resolution and was advised to begin physical therapy. Postoperative radiographs showed a stable instrumentation construct with no evidence of screw lucency or failure (Fig. 5A and B). The patient remained neurologically intact with no focal sensorimotor deficits.

(A) (left) and (B) (right). Postoperative AP and lateral radiographs at 2 weeks follow up.

(A) (left) and (B) (right). Postoperative AP and lateral radiographs at 12 weeks follow up.

At the 6-month follow-up visit, the patient had complete resolution of all presenting symptoms, and participating fully in his dryland and downhill ski training. He experienced 1 pain flare while on a 3-week international ski trip when his feet slipped out from under him on ice and he landed on his buttocks, but the pain flare only lasted for 2 days before self-resolving with NSAID’s and rest, and he returned to sport by the third day. On examination, the patient remained neurologically intact with no focal deficits, though hamstrings and iliotibial band tightness were noted. Postoperative radiograph showed stable L5 pars screws bilaterally with no evidence of screw loosening (Fig. 6A and B).

(A) (left) and (B) (right). Postoperative AP and Lateral Radiographs at 6 month follow up.

Discussion

Spondylolysis is a common cause of low back pain in adolescents participating in sports that involve repetitive hyperextension of the lumbar spine in addition to repetitive high impact movements or trauma. The mechanism of injury is related to repeated chronic mechanical stress on the pars interarticularis causing an acute-on-chronic fracture or chronic defect in the bone, which is generally weakest during growth spurts in puberty. When the integrity of the pars interarticularis becomes compromised due to fracture or defect, this creates instability in the spine by making the facet joint ineffective as a primary stabilizer for stopping anterior translation, which may lead to hypermobile spondylolisthesis. This instability in the spine can accelerate spondylosis, increase susceptibility for disc herniations, and cause mechanical foraminal stenosis.

While spondylolysis may be managed conservatively in most athletes with nonoperative care [15], surgery in the settings of persistent lumbar radiculopathy with neurologic deficits and/or severe mechanical low back pain can help some athletes return to their prior level of sport. Traditionally, the surgical solution has involved lumbar fusion for these adolescents to treat the instability. However, if the pars fracture can be directly repaired with a screw successfully, this reconstitutes spinal stability while avoiding a more invasive and motion-lessening spinal fusion.

Due to technical difficulties and potential risks associated with performing a direct pars repair with a screw, direct pars repair is a less common solution chosen by surgeons. However, with modern technologic capabilities such as the Globus Robot Excelsius 3D Navigation for precise and accurate minimally invasive pars screw placement with robotic assistance in combination to utilizing endoscopic spine techniques to perform direct debridement of the pars fracture site (and utilizing Globus Robot Excelsius 3D Navigation for precise placement of the endoscope directly at the fracture site), the pars fracture/defect can be repaired effectively in a minimally invasive and motion-preserving way. This technique, especially in a high performing adolescent athlete, can help maintain their athletic potential.

This case is the first of its kind to utilize both minimally invasive spine (MIS) robotic-assisted screw placement and endoscopic spine surgery techniques together to perform a bilateral pars repair and debridement of the pars fracture sites to effectively treat a case of adolescent spondylolysis with concurrent lumbar radiculopathy. The benefits of maximally applying MIS techniques in this case by utilizing robotic and endoscopic techniques were evident postoperatively. The patient is a high school downhill competitive ski racer was able to return to full participation in his competitive training immediately following his 3-month postoperative restriction period (which involved no bending, twisting, or lifting >10 pounds, to allow for fracture healing), without any significant pain flares or low back pain/radicular recurrences. He reported complete resolution of his lumbar radicular symptoms and mechanical low back pain immediately following surgery, and reported resolution of his incisional low back pain by 6-weeks postop. He remains neurologically intact on exam with no focal sensory or motor deficits since surgery, despite having preoperative radiating pain and muscular tightness and achiness with a positive straight leg raise bilaterally. At approximately 4 months postop, he went on a 3-week high-intensity training ski trip to Argentina and had 1 mild 2-day pain flare of low back pain after his skis slipped out from under him and he fell on his buttocks, but the pain completely subsided by 2 days later and did not recur. At his 6-month postop, he reported he felt back to his full athletic potential and had improved range of motion through his lumbar spine compared to before surgery.

The Globus robotic arm and Excelsius 3D navigation allowed us to create a safe and direct trajectory perpendicular to the bilateral pars fracture sites at an ideal angle. In addition, the combining the robotic targeting with endoscopic assisted debridement through 2 small 7-mm endoscopic incisions allowed for precise placement of the noncannulated bilateral pars repair screws through 2 corresponding 1.5-cm incisions (Fig. 7A and B). This motion preservation technique maximally spares muscle and soft tissue while still performing a thorough debridement and fixation of the pars fracture bilaterally.

(A and B) 2 cephalad incisions were the endoscopic incisions for debridement of the fracture sites and 2 caudal incisions were for the percutaneous pars repairs with screws.

Motion-preserving pars repair in a young healthy athlete comes with theoretically improved long-term outcomes compared to fusion, including preventing additional stress at adjacent levels that may occur following lumbar fusion. Again, the average onset of adjacent segment breakdown following a lumbar fusion was found to be 20-123 months following the original lumbar fusion [16] which in a young patient is undesirable. Additionally, applying MIS techniques allows for smaller incisions, results in reduced risk of wound dehiscence, infection, and long-term muscular deficits in the low back. One study showed that applying MIS techniques to spine surgery decreases rate of infection as much as 10-fold [17]. Narendran et al. [18] hypothesized that a pars interarticularis screw fixation would be a viable treatment option for adolescents with small pars fracture gaps. This report continues to build on the efficacy of this treatment and the safety profile of robotics as a tool to augment treatment for this pathology. In both their paper and this report, the use of a solid, rather than cannulated, lag compression screws likely helped maximize the ability of the fracture site to resist forces traditionally associated with an active adolescent population. Return to sport within 3 months for both subjects, without recurrence of symptoms, demonstrates preliminary clinical efficacy. Additionally, the use of the robot is associated with a lower cumulative radiation dose compared to other fluoroscopy based minimally invasive techniques, which is particularly desirable in this young population.

Conclusion

We present a novel minimally invasive approach utilizing robotic and endoscopic spine techniques for the treatment of radiculopathy secondary to bilateral pars fractures of athletic origin and demonstrate successful full return to sport after an outpatient, bilateral robotic-assisted endoscopic fracture debridement and percutaneous screw fixation of the pars fractures.

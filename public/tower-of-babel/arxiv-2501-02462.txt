Floquet engineering in hybrid magnetic quantum systems
Feng-Zhou Ji, Si-Yuan Bai, Wan-Li Yang, Chun-Jie Yang, Jun-Hong An

Abstract
The advancement of magnonics has facilitated the utilization of hybrid magnetic systems in quantum technologies. A hybrid magnetic lattice (HML), comprising an array of superconducting loops and magnetic particles, has been devised as a quantum bus to disseminate quantum resources among magnetic quantum entities (MQEs) serving as nodes of a quantum network. However, the HML also exerts a decoherence effect on the MQEs, which has the potential to impair its practical performance. By studying the non-Markovian dynamics of two MQEs comprised of either nitrogen-vacancy centers or magnon modes coupled to two independent HMLs, we propose a Floquet-engineering scheme by applying periodic driving on the MQEs to overcome the unwanted effect. It is revealed that the decoherence can be suppressed and a significant degree of entanglement can be maintained in the steady state, provided that a FBS exists within the quasienergy spectrum of the total system of each periodically driven MQE and its HML. This result enhances our ability to control hybrid magnetic systems and is beneficial for the application of HML in quantum networks.

The hybrid magnetic lattice (HML)

In the paper, we propose a periodic driving scheme to suppress the decoherence and maintain the quantum entanglement of bipartite MQEs independently coupled to two isolated HMLs. We find that the decoherence dynamics of the MQEs are determined by the features of the Floquet quasienergy spectrum of total system formed by each periodically driven MQE and its local HML. With the formation of a Floquet bound state (FBS) in the quasienergy spectrum, a persistent quantum entanglement in a tiny-amplitude oscillations synchronizing with the periodic driving between the bipartite MQEs is preserved in the long-time limit. Our results indicate that the Floquet engineering can rescue the bipartite entanglement from decoherence caused by the local HML environments. Our findings support the applications of hybrid MQEs in designing magnetic quantum devices.

II
System

We consider two sets of subsystems, each of which consists of a periodically driven MQE coupled to a one-dimensional HML, as shown in Fig.
1
. The Hamiltonian of the whole system is

\hat{H}(t)=\sum_{l=1}^{2}\hat{H}_{l}(t)
, with

\hat{H}_{l}(t)=\hat{H}^{\text{sys}}_{l}(t)+\hat{H}^{\text{hml}}_{l}+\hat{H}^{\text{int}}_{l}.

(1)

Here
\hat{H}^{\text{sys}}_{l}
,
\hat{H}^{\text{hml}}_{l}
, and
\hat{H}^{\text{int}}_{l}
are the Hamiltonian of the MQE, the HML, and their interaction in the
l
th subsystem, respectively. The HML is implemented by a periodic arrangement of superconducting loops and magnetic particles. The superconducting loop with radius
l_{0}
behaves as a single-mode resonator
Minev et al. 2016
. The magnetic particle may be a YIG sphere. Applying a magnetic field
\mathbf{B}_{0}=-B_{0}\mathbf{e}_{z}
parallel to the plane of the loop, the magnetic particles are uniformly magnetized with a magnetic dipole moment
\boldsymbol{\mu}
. Each magnetized particle exerts an external flux on its superconducting loop. It leads to an effective interaction between the superconducting loops and the magnetic particles. After tracing out the electromagnetic mode of the loops, the loop-mediated indirect interactions among the magnon modes in the magnetic particles are generated. For a large
B_{0}
and under the condition of identical loops and magnetic particles, the Hamiltonian of the HML reads
Rusconi et al. 2019

\hat{H}_{l}^{\text{hml}}=\hbar\omega_{c}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\hat{f}^{\dagger l}_{n}\hat{f}_{n}^{l}-\hbar h\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}(\hat{f}^{\dagger l}_{n}\hat{f}_{n+1}^{l}+\text{H.c.}).

(2)

The first term is the magnon modes in the magnetic particles with the annihilation operator
\hat{f}_{n}^{l}
satisfying
[\hat{f}_{m}^{l},\hat{f}^{\dagger l}_{n}]=\delta_{mn}
and a common frequency
\omega_{c}
. The second term denotes the loop-mediated coupling between the magnons in the nearest-neighbor magnetic particles in a coupling strength
h
. The magnetic dipole-dipole coupling between the magnetic particles has been neglected because it is much smaller than the loop-mediated coupling when the loop is sufficiently large. The Hamiltonian of Eq. (
2
) describes the tunneling of the magnons as magnetic quasi-particles between the nearest YIG spheres mediated by a superconducting loop, which resembles the propagation of a photon in a photonic lattice
Yang et al. 2021
;
Ma et al. 2018
. In comparison with the photonic crystal, the feature of this lattice is that its frequency band can be tuned freely. To see this, we rewrite Eq. (
2
) in the momentum space. Applying the Fourier transform
\hat{f}_{n}^{l}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{k}\hat{\tilde{f}}^{l}_{k}e^{ikn}
under the periodic boundary condition, we have
\hat{H}^{\text{hml}}_{l}=\sum_{k}\hbar\omega_{k}\hat{\tilde{f}}^{\dagger l}_{k}\hat{\tilde{f}}_{k}^{l}
, with the dispersion relation
\omega_{k}=\omega_{c}-2h\cos k
. In the large-
N
limit, a continuous frequency band with width
4h
centered at
\omega_{c}
is formed in the region
\omega_{k}\in[\omega_{c}-2h,\omega_{c}+2h]
. Both the center frequency
\omega_{c}
and the band width
4h
can be controlled by the external magnetic field
B_{0}
. Thus, a tunable band structure of the artificial one-dimensional magnetic crystal is obtained.

Another key feature of the magnetic quasi-particle based HML is its ability in coupling to various MQEs, including solid spin and magnetic quasi-particle, by gradient magnetic field
Sánchez Muñoz et al. 2018
. We investigate each HML interacting with a MQE via

\hat{H}^{\text{int}}_{l}=-\hbar g(\hat{O}^{\dagger}_{l}\hat{f}_{1}^{l}+\text{H.c.}),

(3)

where
\hat{O}
is the annihilation operator of the MQE and
g
is its coupling strength to the HML. Depending on its property, each MQE may be either a discrete-variable system or a continuous-variable one. The former can be a nitrogen-vacancy center, which couples to the HML by the magnetic dipolar interactions and can be modeled by a two-level system with
\hat{O}=\hat{\sigma}
. The latter can be a magnetic particle, which couples to the HML through another superconducting loop and is described by another magnon mode with
\hat{O}=\hat{a}

Rusconi et al. 2019
. Equation (
3
) in the momentum space reads
\hat{H}^{\text{int}}_{l}=-\hbar\sum_{k}g_{k}(\hat{O}^{\dagger}_{l}\hat{\tilde{f}}_{k}^{l}+\text{H.c.})
, where
g_{k}=ge^{ik}/\sqrt{N}
.

This scheme opens up the possibility in controlling quantum entanglement between the MQEs and lays the foundation for realizing magnetic quantum network
Toyli et al. 2010
;
Humphreys et al. 2018
;
Pompili et al. 2021
, quantum simulation
Wang et al. 2023
;
Tay and Sham 2013
, and quantum information processing
Li et al. 2020a
;
Lachance-Quirion et al. 2019
. Serving as an environment, the HML causes decoherence to the MQE, which is the main obstacle in exploring its applications. It has been found that the non-Markovian effect in strong-coupling regime plays a constructive role in suppressing decoherence
Ji and An 2023
. However, the low saturation magnetization of the magnetic material prevents the system from realizing the strong coupling
Mendil et al. 2019
. Inspired by the progress that Floquet engineering by applying an external periodic driving on quantum systems has become a versatile tool of quantum control
Bai et al. 2021
;
Bai and An 2020
;
Chen et al. 2015
;
Bai and An 2023
, we explore the possibility of decoherence suppression by periodic driving. The Hamiltonian of the MQEs under the periodic driving is

\hat{H}^{\text{sys}}_{l}(t)=\hbar[\omega_{0}+A(t)]\hat{O}^{\dagger}_{l}\hat{O}_{l},

(4)

where
\omega_{0}
is the eigen-frequency of the MQEs and
A(t)
is the external driving. For the availability of the analytical result, we adopt a piecewise periodic-driving protocol

A(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}\mathcal{F},&&jT\leq t<jT+t^{\prime}\\
0,&&jT+t^{\prime}\leq t<(j+1)T\end{array},\right.

(5)

where
j
are integer numbers,
\mathcal{F}
and
T
are the driving amplitude and period. Being widely used in generating nonequilibrium quantum states of matter, e.g., time crystal
Zhang et al. 2017
, the protocol can be realized by applying a magnetic field to the MQEs.

III
Exact dynamics

The non-Markovian dynamics of the MQEs is exactly solvable under the initial condition that the magnons of the two sets of the HMLs are in the vacuum state
|\{0_{k}\}_{1},\{0_{k}\}_{2}\rangle
. When the MQEs are nitrogen-vacancy centers, the reduced dynamics of such a discrete-variable system can be exactly derived by tracing over the degrees of freedom of HMLs from the unitary dynamics of the total system
Ji and An 2023
. When the MQEs are magnons, the exact dynamics is obtained by the Feynman-Vernon’s influence-functional theory in the coherent-state representation
An and Zhang 2007
. Then, a general form of the exact master equation for both the discrete- and continuous-variable cases is

\dot{\rho}(t)=\sum_{l=1}^{2}\{-i\Omega(t)[\hat{O}^{\dagger}_{l}\hat{O}_{l},\rho(t)]+\Gamma(t)\check{\mathcal{L}}_{l}\rho(t)\},

(6)

where
\check{\mathcal{L}}_{l}\cdot=2\hat{O}_{l}\cdot\hat{O}_{l}^{{\dagger}}-\hat{O}_{l}^{{\dagger}}\hat{O}_{l}\cdot-\cdot\hat{O}_{l}^{{\dagger}}\hat{O}_{l}
is the Lindblad superoperator,
\Omega(t)=-\text{Im}[\dot{c}(t)/c(t)]
and
\Gamma(t)=-\text{Re}[\dot{c}(t)/c(t)]
denote the renormalized frequency and decay rate of the quantum system. The time-dependent parameter
c(t)
is determined by

\dot{c}(t)+i[\omega_{0}+A(t)]c(t)+\int_{0}^{t}d\tau F(t-\tau)c(\tau)=0

(7)

under the initial condition
c(0)=1
, where
F(t-\tau)=\int d\omega J(\omega)e^{-i\omega(t-\tau)}
is the correlation function and
J(\omega)=\sum_{k}|g_{k}|^{2}\delta(\omega-\omega_{k})
is the spectral density of the HMLs. In the continuous limit of the HML frequencies, we have
\sum_{k}\rightarrow\int dk/(2\pi/N)
and
F(t-\tau)=g^{2}e^{-i\omega_{c}(t-\tau)}\mathcal{J}_{0}(2h(t-\tau))
, where
\mathcal{J}_{0}
is the zeroth-order Bessel function in the first kind. The convolution in Eq. (
7
) renders the dynamics non-Markovian. In the absence of the periodic driving and in the weak-coupling regime, we can safely apply the Born-Markovian approximation and obtain
c_{\text{MA}}(t)=\exp[-(\gamma+i\Delta)t]
, with
\gamma=\pi J(\omega_{0})
,
\Delta=\mathcal{P}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{J(\omega)}{\omega_{0}-\omega}d\omega
, and
\mathcal{P}
being the Cauchy principal value. In the long-time limit, we have
c_{\text{MA}}(\infty)=0
. Thus, the MQEs experience an exponential decay to their ground state.

When the periodic driving is applied, the Born-Markovian approximation is generally invalid even in the weak-coupling regime. The solution of Eq. (
7
) is obtainable only via numerical calculations. On the other hand, Floquet theorem, as a useful tool to deal the dynamics of periodically driven systems
Klausmeier 2008
, permits us to obtain the asymptotic solution of Eq. (
7
) in the long-time limit. According to Floquet theorem, the evolution operator of the periodically driven subsystem
\hat{H}_{l}(t)
is expanded as

\hat{\mathcal{U}}_{l}(t)=\sum_{n}e^{-i\epsilon_{n}t/\hbar}|u_{n}(t)\rangle\langle u_{n}(0)|,

(8)

where
\epsilon_{n}
and
|u_{n}(t)\rangle
are the Floquet quasi-energies and quasi-stationary states, respectively. They are determined by the Floquet equation
Shirley 1965
;
Sambe 1973

[\hat{H}_{l}(t)-i\hbar\partial_{t}]|u_{n}(t)\rangle=\epsilon_{n}|u_{n}(t)\rangle.

(9)

It can be proven that the solution of Eq. (
7
) is equivalent to
c(t)=\langle\Phi(0)|\hat{\mathcal{U}}_{l}(t)|\Phi(0)\rangle
, where
|\Phi(0)\rangle\equiv\hat{O}_{l}^{{\dagger}}|\varnothing_{l},\{0_{k}\}_{l}\rangle
, with
|\varnothing\rangle
being the ground state of the
l
th MQE. If only the stroboscopic dynamics at
t=mT
, with
m\in\mathbb{Z}
, is interested in, then
c(t)
is further recast into
c(t)=\sum_{n}e^{-i\epsilon_{n}t/\hbar}|\langle u_{n}(0)|\Phi(0)\rangle|^{2}
. It indicates that dynamics of the MQEs governed by
c(t)
is essentially determined by the quasienergy spectrum
\epsilon_{n}
of the total system formed by each periodically driven MQE and its coupled HML. The quasienergy spectrum is generally composed of a continuous quasienergy band and a possibly formed discrete quasienergy
\epsilon^{b}
. We call the eigenstate
|u^{b}(t)\rangle
corresponding to the quasienergy
\epsilon^{b}
FBS. In the long-time limit, the contribution from the continuous quasienergy band to
c(t)
tends to zero due to the out-of-phase interference and only the component of the FBS survives. Therefore, we obtain the long-time solution of the dynamics at
t=mT
as
Bai and An 2023

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}c(t)=\mathcal{Z}e^{-i\epsilon^{b}t/\hbar},

(10)

where
\mathcal{Z}=|\langle u^{b}(0)|\Phi(0)\rangle|^{2}
. If the FBSs are absent, then
\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}c(t)=0
, which characterizes a complete decoherence. If the FBS is formed, then
c(t)
tends to Eq. (
10
), which implies a decoherence suppression. Entirely absent in the Born-Markovian approximation, Eq. (
10
) reveals that we can suppress the decoherence of the MQE caused by the HML via engineering the quasienergy spectrum of the total system to form the FBS. Efficiently overcoming the destructive impact of the HMLs on the MQEs, such a result also enables us to distribute stable entanglement between spatially separated MQEs via the HMLs. This is helpful in designing functional quantum devices such as quantum network
Pompili et al. 2021
;
Shen et al. 2021
;
Li et al. 2021
.

To verify our result, we explicitly investigate the dynamics of two kinds of MQEs. We first consider that the MQEs are formed by two nitrogen-vacancy centers in diamonds. The nitrogen-vacancy center consists of a substitutional nitrogen atom and an adjacent vacancy in the diamond. It possesses triplet states
|m_{s}=0\rangle
and
|m_{s}=\pm 1\rangle
. Applying a magnetic field, the Zeeman shift in the states
|m_{s}=\pm 1\rangle
leads to a modulation on spin transitions of the nitrogen-vacancy center
Li and Nori 2018
;
Li et al. 2020b
. When one of the transition frequency is tuned in resonance with the magnetic mode in the HML, the nitrogen-vacancy center can be viewed as a two-level system. The nitrogen-vacancy center has a wide prospect in realizing quantum sensing and quantum computation. A key point for realizing such applications is the achievement of long coherence time
Herbschleb et al. 2019
;
Ungar et al. 2024
;
Bradley et al. 2019
. In the following, we show the constructive role of Floquet engineering in suppressing quantum decoherence in the nitrogen-vacancy centers and preserving the entanglement between two nitrogen-vacancy centers coupling to two independent HMLs. Solving the master equation Eq. (
6
) with
\hat{O}=\hat{\sigma}
under the initial condition
\rho=|\Psi(0)\rangle\langle\Psi(0)|
with
|\Psi(0)\rangle=(|ee\rangle+|gg\rangle)/\sqrt{2}
, we obtain the reduced density matrix of the two nitrogen-vacancy centers

\displaystyle\rho(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{[P_{t}|e\rangle\langle e|+(1-P_{t})|g\rangle\langle g|]^{\otimes 2}+|g\rangle\langle g|^{\otimes 2}

(11)

\displaystyle+[c^{2}(t)|e\rangle\langle g|^{\otimes 2}+\text{H.c.}]\}/2,

where
P_{t}=|c(t)|^{2}
. In the absence of the FBS,
c(\infty)=0
and thus
\rho(\infty)=|gg\rangle\langle gg|
, which characterizes a complete decoherence and shows no difference from the Born-Markovian approximate result. In the presence of the FBS, the substitution of Eq. (
10
) into Eq. (
11
) results in that the state at the stroboscopic times approaches

\displaystyle\rho(\infty)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{[\mathcal{Z}^{2}|e\rangle\langle e|+(1-\mathcal{Z}^{2})|g\rangle\langle g|]^{\otimes 2}+|g\rangle\langle g|^{\otimes 2}

(12)

\displaystyle+[\mathcal{Z}^{2}e^{-2i\epsilon^{b}t/\hbar}|e\rangle\langle g|^{\otimes 2}+\text{H.c.}]\}/2.

Choosing the concurrence
\mathcal{C}(t)=\text{max}(0,\sqrt{\lambda_{1}}-\sqrt{\lambda_{2}}-\sqrt{\lambda_{3}}-\sqrt{\lambda_{4}})
, where
\lambda_{i}
is the eigenvalues of
\rho(t)\tilde{\rho}(t)
in decreasing order and
\tilde{\rho}(t)=(\hat{\sigma}_{y}\otimes\hat{\sigma}_{y})\rho(t)^{\ast}(\hat{\sigma}_{y}\otimes\hat{\sigma}_{y})
, with
\hat{\sigma}_{y}
being the Pauli matrix, as a measure to quantify the entanglement
Wootters 1998
, we can calculate

\mathcal{C}(\infty)=\mathcal{Z}^{4}.

(13)

Therefore, the formation of the FBS makes the entanglement partially preserved in the steady state.

Second, we consider that the MQEs are formed by two magnons. As a promising solid-state platform in quantum technology
Li et al. 2020a
;
Pal et al. 2024
, the rapidly developing quantum magnonics focuses on the quantum states of magnons and their hybridization with other quantum platforms
Yuan et al. 2022
;
Jiang et al. 2023
. However, the damping of the magnon in magnetic material leads to decoherence and seriously hinders its practical application. The preservation of the magnon-magnon entanglement is particularly important for its further applications
Zhang et al. 2019
;
Azimi Mousolou et al. 2021
. We show the effect of Floquet engineering in maintaining quantum entanglement of magnons. In the framework of the Feynman-Vernon’s influence-functional theory in the coherent-state representation, the solution of master equation (
6
) with
\hat{O}=\hat{a}
can be written as
An and Zhang 2007

\displaystyle\rho(\bar{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{f},{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{f}^{\prime};t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d\mu({\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{i})d\mu({\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{i}^{\prime})\mathcal{K}(\bar{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{f},{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{f}^{\prime};t|\bar{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{i}^{\prime},{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{i};0)

(14)

\displaystyle\times\rho(\bar{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{i},{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{i}^{\prime};0),

where
\rho(\bar{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{f},{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{f}^{\prime};t)=\langle\bar{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{f}|\rho(t)|{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{f}^{\prime}\rangle
and
\rho(\bar{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{i},{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{i}^{\prime};0)=\langle\bar{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{i}|\rho(0)|{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{i}^{\prime}\rangle
. The coherent state is defined as
|{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\rangle=e^{\alpha_{1}\hat{a}_{1}^{\dagger}+\alpha_{2}\hat{a}_{2}^{\dagger}}|0_{1},0_{2}\rangle
and
d\mu({\boldsymbol{\alpha}})=\prod_{l}e^{-\bar{\alpha}_{l}\alpha_{l}}d^{2}\alpha_{l}/2\pi
. The propagation function
\mathcal{K}(\bar{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{f},{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{f}^{\prime};t|\bar{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{i},{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{i}^{\prime};0)
reads

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}(\bar{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{f},{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{f}^{\prime};t|\bar{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{i},{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{i}^{\prime};0)=\exp\{\sum_{l=1}^{2}[c(t)\bar{\alpha}_{lf}\alpha_{li}

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~+\bar{c}(t)\bar{\alpha}_{li}^{\prime}\alpha_{lf}^{\prime}+[1-|c(t)|^{2}]\bar{\alpha}_{li}^{\prime}\alpha_{li}\}.

(15)

We consider that the initial state of the magnons is a two-mode squeezed vacuum state
|\Psi(0)\rangle=\text{exp}[r(\hat{a}_{1}\hat{a}_{2}-\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{1}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{2})]|00\rangle
. Substituting its form in the coherent-state representation
\rho(\bar{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{i},{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{i}^{\prime};0)=\cosh^{-2}r\text{exp}[-\tanh r(\bar{\alpha}_{1i}\bar{\alpha}_{2i}+\alpha_{1i}^{\prime}\alpha_{2i}^{\prime})]
into Eq. (
14
) and performing the Gaussian integration, we obtain

\displaystyle\rho(\bar{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{f},{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{f}^{\prime};t)=o\prod_{l\neq l^{\prime}}\exp(\frac{p\bar{\alpha}_{lf}\bar{\alpha}_{l^{\prime}f}+\bar{p}\alpha_{lf}^{\prime}\alpha_{l^{\prime}f}^{\prime}}{2}+q\bar{\alpha}_{lf}\alpha_{lf}^{\prime}),

where

,

,

, and
\xi=[1-(1-|c(t)|^{2})^{2}\tanh^{2}r]^{-1}
. The continuous-variable entanglement can be measured by the logarithmic negativity defined as
E_{N}=\max\{0,-\log_{2}(2\tilde{\nu}_{\text{min}})\}

Simon 2000
, where
\tilde{\nu}_{\text{min}}
being the minimal eigenvalue of the partially transposed covariance matrix
\tilde{\mathbf{V}}=\mathbf{\Lambda}\mathbf{V}\mathbf{\Lambda}
and
\mathbf{\Lambda}=\text{diag}(1,1,1,-1)
. The element of the covariance matrix
\bf{V}
reads
V_{mn}=\text{Tr}[(\Delta\hat{X}_{m}\Delta\hat{X}_{n}+\Delta\hat{X}_{n}\Delta\hat{X}_{m})\rho]/2
depicted by the quadrature-operator vector
\hat{X}=(\hat{x}_{1},\hat{p}_{1},\hat{x}_{2},\hat{p}_{2})
, where
\hat{x}_{l}=(\hat{a}_{l}+\hat{a}_{l}^{\dagger})/\sqrt{2}
,
\hat{p}_{l}=(\hat{a}_{l}-\hat{a}_{l}^{\dagger})/i\sqrt{2}
, and
\Delta\hat{X}_{l}=\hat{X}_{l}-\text{Tr}(\hat{X}_{l}\rho)
. In the absence of the FBS,
c(\infty)=0
readily leads to
\rho(\bar{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{f},{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{f}^{\prime};\infty)=1
, which corresponds to
\rho(\infty)=|00\rangle\langle 00|
and denotes a complete decoherence. In the presence of the FBS, we can evaluate that the steady-state entanglement at the stroboscopic times is

E_{N}(\infty)=-\log_{2}|1-\mathcal{Z}^{2}(1-e^{-2r})|,

(16)

which indicates that the formation of the FBS overcomes the degradation of the entanglement of the continuous-variable system under local decoherence.

The above analysis reveals an insightful mechanism to control quantum states in hybrid magnetic systems for both the discrete- and continuous-variable cases. It indicates that one can suppress the decoherence of the MQEs induced by the HMLs by actively applying a periodic driving on the system such that a FBS is formed in the quasienergy spectrum of the total MQE-HML system. V
Discussion and Conclusions

Our scheme is realizable in the state-of-the-art experiments. On one hand, the fabrication of YIG in nanoscale and the observation of strong coupling between magnon modes in a YIG sphere with magnetic qubits and superconducting coplanar microwave resonators have been experimentally realized
Hou and Liu 2019
;
Wang et al. 2021
;
Kounalakis et al. 2022
;
Jin and Jing 2023
;
Tabuchi et al. 2014
;
Huebl et al. 2013
, which is helpful for the realization of our hybrid HML system. On the other hand, the eigen-frequency of the MQE composed of either nitrogen-vacancy centers or magnon modes is determined by the magnetic field. It is easy to be controlled by introducing auxiliary periodic drivings
Hei et al. 2021
;
Solanki et al. 2022
. The parameters in our calculations are
(l_{0},d)\sim\mu
m and
B_{0}\sim
mT. Thus
\omega_{c}
,
g
and
h
are in the order of
10^{6}
Hz, which are experimentally available
Rusconi et al. 2019
;
Neuman et al. 2020
;
Hei et al. 2021
.

In conclusion, we have studied the non-Markovian dynamics of two MQEs, which are formed by either nitrogen-vacancy centers or magnon modes, coupled to two independent HMLs. We have proposed a Floquet-engineering scheme to suppress the decoherence of the MQEs caused by the HMLs. It is found that, as long as a FBS is present in the quasienergy spectrum of the total system of each periodically driven MQE and its HML, the decoherence is overcome and a persistent entanglement between the MQEs is kept in the steady state. Solving the decoherence problem of MQEs in HML, our result is helpful for the application of the HML in quantum technologies.

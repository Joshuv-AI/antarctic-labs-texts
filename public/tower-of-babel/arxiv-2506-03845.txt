The generalized and pseudo n n -strong Drazin inverse of the sum of elements in Banach algebras
Rounak Biswas, Falguni Roy

Abstract
. In this paper, we begin by introducing some necessary and sufficient conditions for generalized n n -strong Drazin invertibility (g n n s-invertibility) and pseudo n n -strong Drazin invertibility (p n n s-invertibility) of an element in a Banach algebra for n ∈ ℕ n\in\mathbb{N} . Subsequently, these results are utilized to prove some additive properties of g n n s (p n n s)-Drazin inverse in a Banach algebra. This process produces a generalization of some recent results of H Chen, M Sheibani (Linear and Multilinear Algebra 70.1 (2022): 53-65) for g n n s and p n n s-Drazin inverse. Furthermore, we define and characterize weighted g n n s and weighted p n n s-Drazin inverse in a Banach algebra.

1.
Introduction

Throughout this paper,
\mathcal{A}
denote a Banach algebra with the unit
1.
The symbols
\rho(a)
,
\sigma(a)
represents the resolvent and the spectrum of an element
a\in\mathcal{A}
, respectively.
\mathcal{A}^{qnil}
denote the collection of all quasinilpotents elements of
\mathcal{A}
, i.e.

\mathcal{A}^{qnil}=\{a\in\mathcal{A}:\lim_{n\to\infty}\|a^{n}\|^{\frac{1}{n}}=0\}=\{a\in\mathcal{A}:\sigma(a)=0\}.

The commutant and the double commutant for
a\in\mathcal{A}
are define by
\text{Comm}(a)=\{x\in\mathcal{A}:xa=ax\}
and Comm
{}^{2}(a)=\{x\in\mathcal{A}:xy=yx,\hskip 5.69046pt\forall\hskip 5.69046pty\in\text{Comm}(a)\}
. Set of all invertible and nilpotent elements of
\mathcal{A}
will be denoted by
\mathcal{A}^{inv}
and
\mathcal{A}^{nil}
, respectively.
In 1958, Drazin
[
10
]
introduced a new kind of generalized inverse different from the well-known Moore-Penrose generalized inverse, known as the Drazin inverse. An element
a\in\mathcal{A}
is Drazin invertible if there exists
b\in\mathcal{A}
such that

ab=ba,\text{ }b=bab\text{ and }a-a^{2}b\in\mathcal{A}^{nil}.

The generalized Drazin invertibility (g-Drazin invertibility) of an element
a\in\mathcal{A}
is defined in
[
12
]
by replacing
a-a^{2}b\in\mathcal{A}^{nil}
with
a-a^{2}b\in\mathcal{A}^{qnil}
. Hence, the g-Drazin inverse of
a
is the element
b\in\mathcal{A}(\text{written }a^{d})
which satisfies

ab=ba\text{, }b=ab^{2}\text{, }a-a^{2}b\in A^{qnil}.

The set of all g-Drazin invertible elements in
\mathcal{A}
will be denoted by
\mathcal{A}^{d}
and
a^{\pi}=1-aa^{d}
, known as the spectral idempotent of
a.

The theory of Drazin inverses has grown significantly over the past few decades, and it
finds applications in many areas, including Statistics, Numerical analysis, Differential
equations, Population models, Cryptography, and Control theory,
etc.

Wang
[
17
]
introduced a new subclass of Drazin invertible elements, known as strong Drazin invertible elements equivalent to the notion of strongly nil-clean elements presented in
[
8
]
. An element
a\in\mathcal{A}
is strong Drazin invertible, if there exists
b\in\mathcal{A}
such that

ab=ba,\text{ }bab=b\text{ and }a-ab\in\mathcal{A}^{nil}.

Motivated by g-Drazin inverse, a modification of strong Drazin inverse was presented in
[
14
]
, as generalized strong Drazin inverse(gs-Drazin inverse). The gs-Drazin inverse of
a\in\mathcal{A}
is the element
x\in\mathcal{A}
which satisfies

xax=x,\text{ }ax=xa,\text{, }a-ax\in\mathcal{A}^{qnil}.

Like strong Drazin invertible elements, the set of all gs-Drazin invertible elements in
\mathcal{A}
form a subclass of g-Drazin invertible elements in
\mathcal{A}
.

Given
n\in\mathbb{N}
, Dijana Mosić
[
9
]
introduced the notion of generalized
n
-strong invertibility in a Ring. In a Banach algebra, an element
a\in\mathcal{A}
is generalized
n
-strongly Drazin invertible (g
n
s-Drazin invertible) if there exists an element
x\in\mathcal{A}
such that

x\in\text{Comm}^{2}(a),\text{ }xax=x\text{ }\text{and}\text{ }a^{n}-ax\in\mathcal{A}^{qnil}.

Then the element
x
is the generalized
n
-strong Drazin inverse of
a
and denoted by
a^{nsd}
. If exists,
a^{nsd}
is unique and coincide with
a^{d}
. Collection of all g
n
s-Drazin invertible elements in
\mathcal{A}
is denoted by
\mathcal{A}^{nsd}
. In particular, for
n=1
, g
n
s-Drazin inverse is the gs-Drazin inverse, and for
n=2
,
2
sd-Drazin inverse is known as generalized Hirano inverse
[
2
]
. The collection of generalized Hirano invertible elements in
\mathcal{A}
will be denoted by
\mathcal{A}^{H}
, and if
a\in\mathcal{A}
is generalized Hirano invertible, then the corresponding generalized Hirano inverse is denoted by
a^{H}.

Zhou Wang and Jianlong Chen
[
16
]
presented an intermediary inverse between Drazin and g-Drazin inverse, known as pseudo Drazin inverse. This particular inverse is obtained by replacing the quasinilpotency condition in g-Drazin inverse definition with Jacobson radical.
The Jacobson radical
\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{A})
in a Banach algebra
\mathcal{A}
is defined as

\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{A})=\{a\in\mathcal{A}:1+ay\in\mathcal{A}^{inv},\text{ for all }y\in\mathcal{A}\},

and

\sqrt{\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{A})}=\{a\in\mathcal{A}:a^{n}\in\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{A})\text{ for some }n\in\mathbb{N}\}.

An element
a\in\mathcal{A}
is pseudo Drazin invertible(p-Drazin invertible) if there exists
b\in\mathcal{A}
such that

b\in\text{Comm}^{2}(a),\text{ }ab^{2}=b\text{ and }a^{k}(1-ab)\in\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{A})\text{ for some }\hskip 5.69046ptk\geq 1.

Here
b
is the p-Drazin inverse of
a
, denoted by
a^{pD},
and
the collection of all p-Drazin invertible elements of
\mathcal{A}
will be denoted by
\mathcal{A}^{pD}.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, Mosic
[
9
]
presented the concept of pseudo
n
-strong Drazin inverse(p
n
s-Drazin inverse). The p
n
s-Drazin inverse of
a\in\mathcal{A}
is the element
x\in\mathcal{A}(\text{written }a^{pnsD})
such that

x\in\text{Comm}^{2}(a),\text{ }xax=x\text{ and }(a^{n}-ax)^{k}\in\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{A})\text{ for some }k\in\mathbb{N}.

The set of all p
n
s-Drazin invertible elements of
\mathcal{A}
will be denoted by
\mathcal{A}^{pnsD}
. Mosic
[
9
]
also defined weighted generalized strong Drazin inverse(wgs-Drazin inverse) and weighted pseudo strongly Drazin inverse(wps-Drazin inverses) for some non-zero weight in Banach algebra.

One of the most interesting problems in g-Drazin inverse is the study of g-Drazin invertibility for the sum of two g-Drazin invertible elements. Under non-identical circumstances, several studies

,
7

If

Recently, some additive properties for gs-Drazin and n-strong Drazin inverse are studied in

2.
Additive results for g
n
s-Drazin inverse.

In this section, we give some necessary and sufficient conditions for the g
n
s-Drazin invertibility of an element in a Banach algebra. Then, we prove a few additive results for the g
n
s-Drazin inverse.

We begin with some results on quasinilpotent elements of a Banach algebra.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
5
]

Let
a,b\in\mathcal{A}
and
ab=\lambda ba
for some scalar
\lambda
and
ab\neq 0
.

(1)

If
a,b\in\mathcal{A}^{qnil}
then
a+b\in\mathcal{A}^{qnil}
;

(2)

If
a
or
b

\in\mathcal{A}^{qnil}
then
ab\in\mathcal{A}^{qnil}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

[
15
]

If
a,b\in\mathcal{A}^{qnil}
and
ab=0
, then
a+b\in\mathcal{A}^{qnil}
.

Now, we establish a key proposition for our main theorem.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and
a\in\mathcal{A}
. Then
a^{n}-a^{2n}\in\mathcal{A}^{qnil}
if and only if
a-a^{n+1}\in\mathcal{A}^{qnil}
.

Proof.

Assume that
a^{n}-a^{2n}\in\mathcal{A}^{qnil}
. Since we have

\displaystyle(a-a^{n+1})^{n}

\displaystyle=(a-a^{n+1})a^{n-1}(1-a^{n})^{n-1}

\displaystyle=(a^{n}-a^{2n})(1-a^{n})^{n-1}.

Then
(a-a^{n+1})^{n}\in\mathcal{A}^{qnil}
by Lemma
2.1
, therefore
(a-a^{n+1})\in\mathcal{A}^{qnil}.

Conversely, if
a-a^{n+1}\in\mathcal{A}^{qnil}
, then
a^{n}-a^{2n}=a^{n-1}(a-a^{n+1})\in\mathcal{A}^{qnil}
, using Lemma
2.1
.
∎

We come now to the presentation of the main result of this section.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
a\in\mathcal{A}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then
a
is g
n
s-Drazin invertible if and only if
a^{n}-a^{2n}\in\mathcal{A}^{qnil}.

Proof.

Since
a\in\mathcal{A}^{nsd}
, then we have
a^{n}-aa^{nsd}\in\mathcal{A}^{qnil}
.
Now

\displaystyle a^{n}-a^{2n}

\displaystyle=a^{n}-aa^{nsd}+aa^{nsd}-a^{2n}

\displaystyle=(a^{n}-aa^{nsd})+(aa^{nsd})^{2}-a^{2n}

\displaystyle=(a^{n}-aa^{nsd})+(aa^{nsd}-a^{n})(aa^{nsd}+a^{n}).

Hence by Lemma
2.1
, we obtain
a^{n}-a^{2n}\in\mathcal{A}^{qnil}
.

Conversely, if
a^{n}-a^{2n}\in\mathcal{A}^{qnil}
, then by Proposition
2.3
,
a-a^{n+1}\in\mathcal{A}^{qnil}
, which implies
\sigma(a-a^{n+1})=\{0\}
. Now consider the polynomial
p(x)=x-x^{n+1}
over
\mathbb{C}
. Then using Spectral Mapping Theorem
[
13
, Theorem 4.10]
we have

\{p(\lambda):\lambda\in\sigma(a)\}=\sigma(p(a))=\sigma(a-a^{n+1})=\{0\},

which implies
\sigma(a)\subset\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:\lambda^{n}=1\hskip 5.69046pt\text{or}\hskip 5.69046pt\lambda=0\}
. Thus
0
can only be an isolated spectral point of
a
. Hence
a
is g-Drazin invertible with the g-Drazin inverse
a^{d}
. Since
a-a^{2}a^{d}\in\mathcal{A}^{qnil}
then

(a^{n}-1+aa^{d})((a^{d})^{n}-1+aa^{d})=1-(a^{n}-a^{n+1}a^{d})\in\mathcal{A}^{inv}.

Therefore in particular
a^{n}-1+aa^{d}
is invertible. Moreover

(a^{n}-aa^{d})(a^{n}-1+aa^{d})=a^{2n}-a^{n}\in\mathcal{A}^{qnil},

then
(a^{n}-aa^{d})=(a^{n}-1+aa^{d})^{-1}(a^{2n}-a^{n})\in\mathcal{A}^{qnil}
by Lemma
2.1
. Hence
a\in\mathcal{A}
is g
n
s-Drazin invertible.
∎

Corollary 2.5
.

Let
a\in\mathcal{A}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then
a
is gns-Drazin invertible if and only if
a-a^{n+1}\in\mathcal{A}^{qnil}
.

Proof.

It follows from Theorem
2.4
and Proposition
2.3
.
∎

Remark 1
.

Chen and Calci
[

]
, proved

is gs-Drazin invertible if and only if

Corollary 2.6
.

Let
a\in\mathcal{A}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

(1)

If
a\in\mathcal{A}^{sd}
, then
a\in\mathcal{A}^{nsd}
and

;

(2)

If
a\in\mathcal{A}^{H}
, then
a\in\mathcal{A}^{2nsd}
and
a^{2nsd}=a^{H}=a^{d}
;

(3)

If
a\in\mathcal{A}^{nsd}
, then
a\in\mathcal{A}^{2nsd}
and
a^{2nsd}=a^{nsd}=a^{d}
.

Proof.

(1)

Since
a\in\mathcal{A}^{sd}
, therefore by Corollary
2.5
,
a-a^{2}\in\mathcal{A}^{qnil}
. Now

a-a^{n+1}=a(1-a)\displaystyle(\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}a^{i})=(a-a^{2})(\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}a^{i})\in\mathcal{A}^{qnil}.

Hence
a\in\mathcal{A}^{nsd}
and by uniqueness of gs-Drazin inverse
a^{nsd}=a^{sd}=a^{d}
.

(2)

If
a\in\mathcal{A}^{H}
, then by the Corollary
2.5
,
a-a^{3}\in\mathcal{A}^{qnil}
. Now
a-a^{2n+1}=(a-a^{3})(1+a^{2}+a^{4}\dots+a^{2n-2})\in\mathcal{A}^{qnil}
, therefore the required result follows from Corollary
2.5
.

(3)

This follows in a similar manner as
1
.

∎

Lemma 2.7
.

Let

, if

. Then

Proof.

Since

for any

From Theorem
2.4
, we have

a-a^{n+1},b-b^{n+1}\in\mathcal{A}^{qnil}.

Moreover

(ab)-(ab)^{n+1}=a^{n+1}(b-b^{n+1})+(a-a^{n+1})b,

(1)

where

(a-a^{n+1})ba^{n+1}(b-b^{n+1})=a^{n+1}(b-b^{n+1})(a-a^{n+1})b.

Therefore in light of Lemma
2.1
,
(ab)-(ab)^{n+1}\in\mathcal{A}^{qnil}
. Hence by Corollary
2.5
,

Let

Theorem 2.9

Let

Proof.

Theorem 2.10
.

Let

and

First, we state some known results for the elements of Jacobson radical in a Banach algebra.

Lemma 3.1

.

[

or

The following lemma proves that the statement of Lemma

Lemma 3.3

Let

then

for some

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
a\in\mathcal{A}
. Then
a^{n}-a^{2n}\in\sqrt{\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{A})}
if and only if
a-a^{n+1}\in\sqrt{\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{A})}

Proof.

If
a^{n}-a^{2n}\in\sqrt{\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{A})}
, then we have
(a^{n}-a^{2n})^{k}\in J(\mathcal{A}),
for some

(a-a^{n+1})^{nk}=(a^{n}-a^{2n})^{k}(1-a^{n})^{(n-1)k},

therefore
(a-a^{n+1})^{nk}\in\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{A})
by Lemma
3.1
, and hence we obtain
(a-a^{n+1})\in\sqrt{\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{A})}.

Conversely, if
(a-a^{n+1})^{k}\in\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{A})
for some
k\in\mathbb{N}

(a^{n}-a^{2n})^{k}=a^{(n-1)k}(a-a^{n+1})^{k}\in\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{A}).

Thus
a^{n}-a^{2n}\in\sqrt{\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{A})}.

3.2

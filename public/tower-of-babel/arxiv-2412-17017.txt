Global well-posedness and optimal decay rates of classical solutions to the compressible Navier-Stokes-Fourier- P 1 P_{1} approximation model in radiation hydrodynamics Thanks: ∗ ​ Corresponding author
Peng Jiang, fucai Li, Jinkai Ni ∗

Abstract
. In this paper, the compressible Navier-Stokes-Fourier- P 1 P_{1} (NSF- P 1 P_{1} ) approximation model in radiation hydrodynamics is investigated in the whole space ℝ 3 \mathbb{R}^{3} . This model consists of the compressible NSF equations of ﬂuid coupled with the transport equations of the radiation field propagation. Assuming that the initial data are a small perturbation near the equilibrium state, we establish the global well-posedness of classical solutions for this model by performing the Fourier analysis techniques and employing the delicate energy estimates in frequency spaces. Here, we develop a new method to overcome a series of difficulties arising from the linear terms n 1 n_{1} in ( 3.2 ) 2 and n 0 n_{0} in ( 3.2 ) 3 related to the radiation intensity. Furthermore, if the L 1 L^{1} -norm of the initial data is bounded, we obtain the optimal time decay rates of the classical solution at L p L^{p} -norm ( 2 ≤ p ≤ ∞ ) (2\leq p\leq\infty) . To the best of our knowledge, this is the first result on the global well-posedness of the NSF- P 1 P_{1} approximation model.

Introduction and main result

1.1.
Previous literature and our model

The purpose of radiation hydrodynamics is to establish hydrodynamical framework from the macroscopic analysis, which takes account of the effects of radiation (see, for example,

[
3
,
36
]
). It is widely used in many fields, such as stellar evolution
[

]
,

[
28
]
and vehicle reentry
[
34
]
.
Therefore, it is very important to study radiation hydrodynamics from the perspective of mathematical theory for further understanding of radiation hydrodynamics and related physical phenomena and their applications.

The interaction between the radiation field and the fluid can be described by the absorption, scattering and emission of photons, and the general radiation hydrodynamics equations can be derived by considering all the interactions mentioned above. This is a nonlinear differential-integral system coupled by the compressible Navier-Stokes-Fourier (NSF) equations and the photon transport equation, and the three-dimensional case can be written as (see
[
33
]
)

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\varrho+{\rm div}(\varrho u)=0,\\
&\partial_{t}\Big(\varrho u+\frac{1}{c^{2}}F_{r}\Big)+{\rm div}(\varrho u\otimes u+P\mathbb{I}_{3}+P_{r})={\rm div}\Psi(u),\\
&\partial_{t}(\varrho E+E_{r})+{\rm div}\big(\varrho u(E+P)+F_{r}\big)={\rm div}\big(\Psi(u)\cdot u\big)+\mathrm{div}(\kappa\nabla\Theta),\\
&\frac{1}{c}\partial_{t}I+\omega\cdot\nabla I=Q,\end{aligned}\right.

where
\varrho=\varrho(t,x)>0
,
\Theta=\Theta(t,x)>0
, and

for

respectively. And

is the pressure, and

is the total energy where

denotes the internal energy. For the prefect gas case,

with

,
and

is the perfect gas constant and

is the heat capacity at constant volume.
\Psi(u)
is the viscous stress tensor satisfying

\displaystyle\Psi(u):=2\mu D+\lambda{\rm div}u\mathbb{I}_{3},\quad\text{with}\quad D=D(u):=\frac{\nabla u+(\nabla u)^{\top}}{2},

where

is the deformation tensor,
\mathbb{I}_{3}
denotes the
3\times 3

\lambda
and
\mu
are the viscosity coefficients

\mu>0

,

The radiation effect terms in the NSF equations (
1.1
)
1

1.1
)

Therefore, in the study of radiation hydrodynamics, it is an important method to simplify or approximate the equations of general form under the premise of guaranteeing the physical significance.
In this paper, we work with the “gray” approximation and spherical harmonic expansion, whose purpose is to reduce the radiation intensity

1.2.
Our results

In this paper, we investigate the global existence
and optimal time decay rates of
classical solutions to system (
1.9
) with the initial data

(\varrho,u,\Theta,I_{0},I_{1})(x,t)|_{t=0}=(\varrho_{0}(x),u_{0}(x),\Theta_{0}(x),I_{0}^{0}(x),I_{1}^{0}(x)),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}.

Under the assumption that the
H^{4}
-norm of the initial data
is sufficiently small, we will develop a new way to obtain the global existence
of classical solutions. Moreover, the optimal time-decay rates of
classical solutions can be achieved if we further assume
that the
L^{1}
-norm of initial data is bounded.
By employing Sobolev’s interpolation inequality,
we eventually get the desired decay rates of solutions at

-norm with

Noticing that

&\partial_{t}\theta+u\cdot\nabla\theta+(1+\theta){\rm div}u=\frac{\Delta\theta}{1+\rho}+\frac{({\rm div}u)^{2}}{1+\rho}+\frac{2D\cdot D}{1+\rho}-\frac{(1+\theta)^{4}}{1+\rho}+\frac{1+n_{0}}{1+\rho},\\
&\partial_{t}n_{0}+{\rm div}n_{1}=(1+\theta)^{4}-(1+n_{0}),\\
&\partial_{t}n_{1}+\nabla n_{0}=-n_{1},\end{aligned}\right.

with the initial data

\displaystyle(\rho,u,\theta,n_{0},n_{1})(x,t)|_{t=0}=\,

\displaystyle(\rho_{0}(x),u_{0}(x),\theta_{0}(x),n_{0}^{0}(x),n_{1}^{0}(x))

Strategies of the proofs in our results

In Theorem
1.1
, the third term on the left-hand side of (

) describes the dissipative terms arise from
the interaction between radiation field and fluid.
Due to the fact that the classical energy method (see, for example the arguments in
[

,
42
,
41
]
) is failed to get the

-norm estimate of

and

as a result, the main difficulty in

)–(
1.12
).
Fortunately, we surmount this difficulty through dividing the solution

into the lower, medium, and higher frequency parts.
For the low frequency part, we overcome the enormous difficulties caused by the lack of dissipative structure in the system (

) by constructing a change of variable (see (

)–(
3.41
)).
This is the key to get the estimate of solutions in low frequency regimes.
Besides, noting that the

-norm estimates of
\mathcal{N}_{3}

and
\mathcal{N}_{4}
are not closed, we work out it through
the refined energy estimates to get the

(see (

For the medium frequency part, the estimate of solutions lacks

in (

, and hence get the

We mainly use utilize Lemma

Meanwhile, the estimate of high-frequency part can be also obtained by delicate energy method.

In Theorem

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla^{2}\rho\|_{H^{2}}^{2}\|u^{S}\|_{H^{3}}^{2}\mathrm{d}\tau\leq C\sup\limits_{0\leq\tau\leq t}\|u(\tau)\|_{H^{3}}^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla^{2}\rho(\tau)\|_{H^{2}}^{2}\mathrm{d}\tau,

and

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla u(\tau)\|_{H^{2}}\|\nabla\rho^{S}(\tau)\|_{H^{3}}^{2}\mathrm{d}\tau\leq\sup\limits_{0\leq\tau\leq t}\|\nabla u(\tau)\|_{H^{2}}\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla\rho(\tau)\|_{H^{3}}^{2}\mathrm{d}\tau.

From Lemma
2.1
, Lemma
2.7
and Young’s inequality
for series convolution, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla u\|_{H^{2}}^{2}\sum_{k>k_{1}}(1+2^{6k})\bigg(\sum_{l\geq k-1}2^{k-l}\|\nabla\rho_{l}\|_{L^{2}}\bigg)^{2}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,C\sup_{0\leq\tau\leq t}\|\nabla u(\tau)\|_{H^{2}}\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{k>k_{1}}(1+2^{6k})\bigg(\sum_{l\geq k-1}2^{k-l}\|\nabla\rho_{l}\|_{L^{2}}\bigg)^{2}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,C\sup_{0\leq\tau\leq t}\|\nabla u(\tau)\|_{H^{2}}^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{k>k_{1}}\bigg(\sum_{l\geq k-1}2^{k-l}\|\nabla\rho_{l}\|_{L^{2}}\bigg)^{2}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\,+C\sup_{0\leq\tau\leq t}\|\nabla u(\tau)\|_{H^{2}}^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{k>k_{1}}\bigg(\sum_{l\geq k-1}2^{4(k-l)}2^{3l}\|\nabla\rho_{l}\|_{L^{2}}\bigg)^{2}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,C\sup_{0\leq\tau\leq t}\|\nabla u(\tau)\|_{H^{2}}^{2}\bigg(\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\|\nabla\rho_{k}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\mathrm{d}\tau+\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{6k}\|\nabla\rho_{k}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\mathrm{d}\tau\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,C\sup_{0\leq\tau\leq t}\|\nabla u(\tau)\|_{H^{2}}^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla\rho\|_{H^{3}}^{2}\mathrm{d}\tau.

For the remaining terms, Lemma
3.7
provides

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\Big(\|\nabla(\rho,n_{0})^{M}(\tau)\|_{H^{3}}^{2}+\|\nabla(u,\theta)^{M}(\tau)\|_{H^{4}}^{2}+\|n_{1}^{M}(\tau)\|_{H^{4}}^{2}+\|(4\theta-n_{0})^{M}(\tau)\|_{H^{4}}^{2}\Big)\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\,\qquad\leq C\mathbf{N}(0)+C\sigma\mathbf{N}(t).

Hence, by putting the above estimates into (
3.84
), it yields

\displaystyle\mathbf{N}(t)\leq C\big(\mathbf{N}(0)+\sigma\mathbf{N}(t)\big),

which implies that
\mathbf{N}(t)\leq C\mathbf{N}(0)
.

∎

Similar to the Section 2.1 in
[
7
]
, we can
use the fixed point theorem and the standard iteration arguments
to obtain the local existence and uniqueness of
classical solutions to the system (
3.2
)–(
3.3
). For brevity, we omit the details here.
Then, through the standard continuity argument,
we consequently get the global existence and uniqueness of smooth solutions. This completes the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\overline{\mathbb{U}}_{k}+\mathbb{A}\overline{\mathbb{U}}_{k}=0,\\

Lemma 4.3

For any integer

From Hölder’s inequality, it yields

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{N}(\mathbb{U})(\tau)\|_{L^{1}}\leq

\displaystyle\,C\|(\rho,u,\theta,n_{0},n_{1})(\tau)\|_{H^{1}}\big(\|\nabla(\rho,u,\theta)(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla^{2}(u,\theta)(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}\big)

\displaystyle+\|\theta\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\big(1+\|\theta\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\theta\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\big)+\|\rho\|_{L^{2}}\|(n_{0},n_{1})\|_{L^{2}}

(4.14)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,C\sigma\big(\|\nabla(\rho,u,\theta)(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla^{2}(u,\theta)(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}\big)+C\|(\rho,\theta,n_{0},n_{1})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Plugging (
4.14
) into (
4.13
) and taking a
l^{2}
summation
over
k
with
k\leq k_{1}
, we complete the proof of
Lemma
4.3
.
∎

4.3.
Optimal time-decay rates of classical solutions

In this subsection, thanks to Lemmas
4.2
–
4.3
, we obtain
the time decay rates of classical solutions to the problem (
3.2
)–(
3.3
).

Lemma 4.4
.

Under the assumption of Theorem
1.2
, it holds that

(4.15)

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{m}(\rho,u,\theta,n_{0},n_{1})(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq

\displaystyle\,C(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{4}-\frac{m}{2}},

for
m=0,1,2
. And

(4.16)

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{m}(\rho,u,\theta,n_{0},n_{1})(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq

\displaystyle\,C(1+t)^{-\frac{7}{4}},

for
m=3,4
.

Proof.

We denote

(4.17)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(t):=\sup\limits_{0\leq\tau\leq t}\sum\limits_{m=0}^{2}(1+\tau)^{\frac{3}{4}+\frac{m}{2}}\|\nabla^{m}(\rho,u,\theta,n_{0},n_{1})(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}.

It is obvious that

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{m}(\rho,u,\theta,n_{0},n_{1})(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C(1+\tau)^{-\frac{3}{4}-\frac{m}{2}}\mathcal{E}(t),

for
0\leq\tau\leq t
and
m=0,1,2
.

By direct calculation, we have

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{N}(\mathbb{U})\|_{L^{2}}\leq

\displaystyle\,C\|(\rho,u,\theta)\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla(\rho,u,\theta)\|_{L^{2}}+C\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla^{2}(u,\theta)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\,+C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}+C\big(1+\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}}\big)\|\theta\|_{L^{2}}\big(\|\theta\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\theta\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}+\|\theta\|_{L^{\infty}}^{3}\big)

\displaystyle\,+C\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}}\|(\theta,n_{0},n_{1})\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,C\|\nabla(\rho,u,\theta)\|_{H^{1}}\|\nabla(\rho,u,\theta)\|_{H^{1}}+C\|\nabla\rho\|_{H^{1}}\|\nabla^{2}(u,\theta)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\,+C\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{H^{1}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}+C\|\nabla(\rho,\theta)\|_{H^{1}}\|(\theta,n_{0},n_{1})\|_{L^{2}}.

Combining Lemma
4.3
, (
4.12
), and (
4.17
), we get, for
m\geq 0
,

\begin{split}\|(\rho,u,\theta,n_{0},n_{1})^{L+M}(t)\|_{\dot{B}_{2,2}^{m}}\leq&\,C(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{4}-\frac{m}{2}}\|(\rho,u,\theta,n_{0},n_{1})(0)\|_{L^{1}}\\
&\,+Ce^{-c_{3}t}\|(\rho,u,\theta,n_{0},n_{1})(0)\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\,+C\sigma\mathcal{E}(t)\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+t-\tau)^{-\frac{3}{4}-\frac{m}{2}}(1+\tau)^{-\frac{5}{4}}\mathrm{d}\tau\\
&\,+C\sigma\mathcal{E}(t)\int_{0}^{\frac{t}{2}}(1+t-\tau)^{-\frac{3}{4}-\frac{m}{2}}(1+\tau)^{-\frac{7}{4}}\mathrm{d}\tau\\
&\,+C\sigma^{\frac{1}{4}}\mathcal{E}^{\frac{7}{4}}(t)\int_{0}^{\frac{t}{2}}(1+t-\tau)^{-\frac{3}{4}-\frac{m}{2}}(1+\tau)^{-\frac{21}{16}}\mathrm{d}\tau\\
&+C\sigma^{\frac{1}{4}}\mathcal{E}^{\frac{7}{4}}(t)\int_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}(1+t-\tau)^{-\frac{m}{2}}(1+\tau)^{-\frac{29}{16}}\mathrm{d}\tau\\
&+C\sigma^{\frac{1}{4}}\mathcal{E}^{\frac{7}{4}}(t)\int_{0}^{t}e^{-c_{3}(t-\tau)}(1+\tau)^{-\frac{29}{16}}\mathrm{d}\tau.\end{split}

From Lemma
2.3
, we directly get

\displaystyle\|(\rho,u,\theta,n_{0},n_{1})^{L+M}(t)\|_{\dot{B}_{2,2}^{m}}

(4.18)

\displaystyle\quad\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{4}-\frac{m}{2}}\Big(\|(\rho,u,\theta,n_{0},n_{1})(0)\|_{L^{1}}+\sigma\mathcal{E}(t)+\sigma^{\frac{1}{4}}\mathcal{E}^{\frac{7}{4}}(t)\Big),

for
m=0,1,2
,
and

\displaystyle\|(\rho,u,\theta,n_{0},n_{1})^{L+M}(t)\|_{\dot{B}_{2,2}^{m}}

(4.19)

\displaystyle\quad\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{7}{4}}\Big(\|(\rho,u,\theta,n_{0},n_{1})(0)\|_{L^{1}}+\sigma\mathcal{E}(t)+\sigma^{\frac{1}{4}}\mathcal{E}^{\frac{7}{4}}(t)\Big),

for
m=3,4
.
This together with Proposition
4.1
implies that

\displaystyle\|(\rho,u,\theta,n_{0},n_{1})^{S}(t)\|_{\dot{B}_{2,2}^{3}}^{2}+\|(\rho,u,\theta,n_{0},n_{1})^{S}(t)\|_{\dot{B}_{2,2}^{4}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,C\mathrm{e}^{-c_{3}t}\Big(\|(\rho,u,\theta,n_{0},n_{1})^{S}(0)\|_{\dot{B}_{2,2}^{3}}^{2}+\|(\rho,u,\theta,n_{0},n_{1})^{S}(0)\|_{\dot{B}_{2,2}^{4}}^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\,+C\sigma\Big(\|(\rho,u,\theta,n_{0},n_{1})(0)\|_{L^{1}\cap L^{2}}+\sigma\mathcal{E}(t)+\sigma^{\frac{1}{4}}\mathcal{E}^{\frac{7}{4}}(t)\Big)^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{-c_{3}(t-\tau)}(1+\tau)^{-\frac{7}{2}}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,C\mathrm{e}^{-c_{3}t}\Big(\|(\rho,u,\theta,n_{0},n_{1})^{S}(0)\|_{\dot{B}_{2,2}^{3}}^{2}+\|(\rho,u,\theta,n_{0},n_{1})^{S}(0)\|_{\dot{B}_{2,2}^{4}}^{2}\Big)

(4.20)

\displaystyle\,+C\sigma(1+t)^{-\frac{7}{2}}\Big(\|(\rho,u,\theta,n_{0},n_{1})(0)\|_{L^{1}\cap L^{2}}+\sigma\mathcal{E}(t)+\sigma^{\frac{1}{4}}\mathcal{E}^{\frac{7}{4}}(t)\Big)^{2}.

By using Lemma
2.6
, we have

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{m}(\rho,u,\theta,n_{0},n_{1})(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq\,

\displaystyle C\|(\rho,u,\theta,n_{0},n_{1})^{L+M}(t)\|_{\dot{B}_{2,2}^{m}}+\|(\rho,u,\theta,n_{0},n_{1})^{S}(t)\|_{\dot{B}_{2,2}^{m}}

(4.21)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,C\|(\rho,u,\theta,n_{0},n_{1})^{L+M}(t)\|_{\dot{B}_{2,2}^{m}}+\|(\rho,u,\theta,n_{0},n_{1})^{S}(t)\|_{\dot{B}_{2,2}^{3}},

for
m=0,1,2
.
Combing (
4.18
)–(
4.21
), Young’s inequality and the definition of
\mathcal{E}(t)
,
we arrive at

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(t)\leq

\displaystyle\,C\Big(\|(\rho,u,\theta,n_{0},n_{1})(0)\|_{L^{1}\cap H^{4}}+\sigma^{\frac{1}{4}}\mathcal{E}^{\frac{7}{4}}(t)\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,C\Big(\|(\rho,u,\theta,n_{0},n_{1})(0)\|_{L^{1}\cap H^{4}}+1\Big)+\sigma^{\frac{2}{7}}\mathcal{E}^{2}(t)

(4.22)

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle\,\tilde{C}_{1}+\sigma^{\frac{2}{7}}\mathcal{E}^{2}(t).

Now, we claim that
\mathcal{E}(t)\leq\hat{C}
for some constant
\hat{C}>0
. Assume that there exits a
constant
t_{0}>0
such that
\mathcal{E}(t_{0})>2\tilde{C}_{1}
. Under the supposition
of Theorem
1.2
,
\mathcal{E}(0)=\|(\rho_{0},u_{0},\theta_{0},n_{0}^{0},n_{1}^{0})\|_{H^{2}}
is small enough.
And notice that the fact
\mathcal{E}(t)\in C[0,+\infty)
, which means that
there exists
t_{1}\in(0,t_{0})
such that
\mathcal{E}(t_{1})=2\tilde{C}_{1}
.
It follows from (
4.22
) that

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(t_{1})\leq\tilde{C}_{1}+\sigma^{\frac{2}{7}}\mathcal{E}^{2}(t_{1}),

and hence

(4.23)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(t_{1})\leq\frac{\tilde{C}_{1}}{1-\sigma^{\frac{2}{7}}\mathcal{E}(t_{1})}.

With help of the smallness of
\sigma
, we suitably choose
\sigma^{\frac{2}{7}}\mathcal{E}(t_{1})<\frac{1}{4\tilde{C}_{1}}
.
Then, by utilizing (
4.23
), we conclude that

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(t_{1})<2\tilde{C}_{1}.

This leads to a contradiction with our assumption
\mathcal{E}(t_{1})=2\tilde{C}_{1}
.
Due to the continuity of
\mathcal{E}(t)
, we consequently get

\mathcal{E}(t)\leq\hat{C}
for any
t\geq 0
. From the definition of

\mathcal{E}(t)
, we derive (
4.15
). By the similar argument
for
m=3,4
, we easily get (
4.16
) through (
4.19
) and
Lemma
2.6
. Thus, we complete the proof of Lemma
4.4
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

With Lemma
4.4
in hand, we continue to prove Theorem
1.2
.
By a directly calculation, we have

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}\rho(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq

\displaystyle\,C\|\mathrm{div}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}+\|\mathcal{N}_{1}(t)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,C\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^{2}}+\|\rho(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^{2}}+\|u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla\rho(t)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,C(1+t)^{-\frac{5}{4}},

and

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}(u,\theta,n_{0},n_{1})(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq

\displaystyle\,C\|\nabla(u,\theta)(t)\|_{L^{2}}+C\|(\theta,n_{0},n_{1})\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla(u,n_{0},n_{1})(t)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle+C\|(\mathcal{N}_{2},\mathcal{N}_{3},\mathcal{N}_{4})(t)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,C(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{4}}.

Finally,
we give the
proof of (
1.16
)–(
1.17
).
From the result of (
1.14
) and Lemma
2.1
, we obtain

(4.24)

\displaystyle\|(\rho,u,\theta,n_{0},n_{1})\|_{L^{6}}\leq C\|\nabla(\rho,u,\theta,n_{0},n_{1})\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{5}{4}},

(4.25)

\displaystyle\|(\rho,u,\theta,n_{0},n_{1})\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{4}},

(4.26)

\displaystyle\|\nabla(\rho,u,\theta,n_{0},n_{1})\|_{L^{6}}\leq C\|\Delta(\rho,u,\theta,n_{0},n_{1})\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{7}{4}}.

For
p\in[2,6]
, using Lemma
2.4
, it follows from (
4.24
)–(
4.25
) that

\displaystyle\|(\rho,u,\theta,n_{0},n_{1})\|_{L^{p}}\leq C\|(\rho,u,\theta,n_{0},n_{1})\|_{L^{2}}^{\zeta}\|(\rho,u,\theta,n_{0},n_{1})\|_{L^{6}}^{{1-\zeta}}\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{2}(1-\frac{1}{p})},

where
\zeta=({6-p})/{2p}\in[0,1]
.

By using Lemma
2.1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|(\rho,u,\theta,n_{0},n_{1})\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\,

\displaystyle C\|\nabla(\rho,u,\theta,n_{0},n_{1})\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\Delta(\rho,u,\theta,n_{0},n_{1})\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}

(4.27)

\displaystyle\leq\,

\displaystyle C(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{2}}.

For
p\in[6,\infty]
, using Lemma
2.4
again, it follows from (
4.24
) and (
4.27
) that

\displaystyle\|(\rho,u,\theta,n_{0},n_{1})\|_{L^{p}}\leq C\|(\rho,u,\theta,n_{0},n_{1})\|_{L^{6}}^{\zeta^{\prime}}\|(\rho,u,\theta,n_{0},n_{1})\|_{L^{\infty}}^{{1-\zeta^{\prime}}}\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{2}(1-\frac{1}{p})},

where
\zeta^{\prime}={6}/{p}\in[0,1]
.
For
p\in[2,6]
, using Lemma
2.1
, it follows from (
4.26
) that

\displaystyle\|\nabla(\rho,u,\theta,n_{0},n_{1})\|_{L^{p}}\leq C\|\nabla(\rho,u,\theta,n_{0},n_{1})\|_{L^{2}}^{\eta}\|\nabla(\rho,u,\theta,n_{0},n_{1})\|_{L^{6}}^{{1-\eta}}\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{2}(\frac{4}{3}-\frac{1}{p})},

where
\eta=({6-p})/{2p}\in[0,1]
.
Therefore, we complete the proof of Theorem
1.2
.
∎

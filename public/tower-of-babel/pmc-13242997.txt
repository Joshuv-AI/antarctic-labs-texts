Association of Serum Fetuin-B with Large-Artery Atherosclerotic Acute Ischemic Stroke and Functional Outcome
Authors: Xunwei Deng, Ting Hu, Kunyuan Zhou, Qiaoting Deng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveThis study investigated the association of serum fetuin-B with large-artery atherosclerotic acute ischemic stroke (LAA-AIS) and poor functional outcome after stroke.MethodsWe retrospectively enrolled 216 consecutive LAA-AIS patients from September 2023 to December 2024 and 194 age- and sex-matched healthy controls. Serum fetuin-B was measured via ELISA. Multivariable logistic regression and restricted cubic spline (RCS) analyses were used to assess the relationships between fetuin-B and the presence of LAA-AIS or poor functional outcome (modified Rankin Scale > 2). Predictive performance was evaluated using C-index, net reclassification index (NRI), integrated discrimination improvement (IDI), calibration plots, and decision curve analysis (DCA). Internal validation was performed via 1000 bootstrap resamples.ResultsSerum fetuin-B was significantly higher in patients with LAA-AIS than in controls (2.68 vs 1.79 µg/mL, P < 0.001) and correlated positively with total cholesterol (TC), low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C), glycated hemoglobin (HbA1c), and C-reactive protein (CRP). Multivariable regression showed higher fetuin-B independently associated with the presence of LAA-AIS (OR = 2.433, P < 0.001) and poor functional outcome (OR = 1.903, P < 0.001). Fetuin-B tertiles showed a graded increase in both risks (all P for trend <0.05). Incorporating fetuin-B to the basic model significantly improved the C-index for LAA-AIS risk (from 0.827 to 0.871) and poor functional outcome (from 0.749 to 0.806), supported by significant NRI and IDI (all P < 0.01). Calibration curves and DCA indicated good consistency and clinical net benefit. RCS analysis further revealed a non-linear association between fetuin-B and the presence of LAA-AIS, as well as an approximately linear association with poor functional outcome.ConclusionElevated serum fetuin-B measured after stroke onset was independently associated with LAA-AIS status and poor functional outcome.

Introduction

Acute ischemic stroke (AIS), a leading cause of mortality and long-term disability, poses a major global health burden.1 According to the latest global epidemiological statistics, an estimated 69.94 million individuals are affected by IS worldwide, with 7.804 million incident cases and 3.591 million deaths reported annually.2 In China, AIS is a significant cerebrovascular burden.3 Population growth, urbanization, population aging, and increasing exposure to metabolic and environmental risk factors have contributed to the rising incidence of AIS.3,4 According to the Trial of Org 10172 in Acute Stroke Treatment (TOAST) criteria, large-artery atherosclerotic (LAA) stroke—characterized mainly by atherosclerotic plaque formation, luminal stenosis, and arterial embolism—is one of the most common subtypes.5 Despite numerous advances in reperfusion therapy and secondary prevention, the morbidity and mortality attributable to AIS remain high.6 Identifying novel biomarkers linked to LAA-AIS and poor functional outcome is critical for early prevention and intervention.

Inflammation and dyslipidemia are critically involved in the pathogenesis of atherosclerosis and cerebrovascular disease.7 Inflammation drives AIS by accelerating atherosclerosis, destabilizing plaques, and triggering rupture.8,9 In parallel, epidemiological studies have established dyslipidemia as an independent risk factor for ischemic stroke.10 It promotes cerebral atherosclerosis, vascular stenosis, platelet activation, and thrombosis, thereby increasing the risk of ischemic stroke. After stroke onset, dyslipidemia may further aggravate ischemic brain injury and adversely affect clinical outcomes by amplifying oxidative stress, lipid peroxidation, and inflammatory responses.11,12 Given the critical involvement of inflammation and dyslipidemia in ischemic stroke, identifying novel biomarkers related to these processes may help improve disease prediction and prognostic assessment.

Fetuin-B, a hepatokine of the cystatin superfamily, may link hepatic metabolic dysfunction to systemic vascular pathology.13,14 As the liver is central to lipid metabolism and inflammatory regulation, liver-derived hepatokines can influence endothelial function, vascular inflammation, lipid deposition, and plaque remodeling. Given that LAA-AIS reflects systemic atherosclerosis in the cerebral vasculature, these factors may contribute to plaque progression, instability, and subsequent ischemic events.

Accumulating evidence links fetuin-B to dyslipidemia and chronic low-grade inflammation, both of which are central to atherosclerosis.15–19 Elevated serum fetuin-B levels have been reported in individuals with metabolic syndrome, coronary artery disease, and acute myocardial infarction, suggesting its involvement in cardiometabolic disorders.20–23 Experimental studies further suggest that fetuin-B may promote plaque vulnerability by modulating vascular smooth muscle cell signaling through the TGF-β/Smad pathway.14 In addition, proteomic analyses have reported altered fetuin-B expression in intracranial blood samples from patients with AIS.24 Collectively, these findings suggest a potential role for fetuin-B in cerebrovascular disease. However, its clinical relevance in LAA-AIS remains unclear, particularly with respect to disease presence and functional prognosis.

Materials and Methods

Study Design and Population

This retrospective study included consecutive LAA-AIS patients admitted to the Department of Neurology in Meizhou People’s Hospital from September 2023 to December 2024. During the same period, age- and sex-matched healthy individuals without stroke or transient ischemic attack who underwent routine health checkups were enrolled as controls. This study was performed in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the Ethics Committee of Meizhou People’s Hospital (2023-C-99). Informed consent was obtained from all participants.

Diagnosis of LAA-AIS was established in accordance with the 2018 Chinese Guidelines for the Diagnosis and Treatment of Acute Ischemic Stroke and the TOAST classification criteria.25,26 All patients underwent CT or MRI to confirm acute ischemic infarction, and only those with imaging‑verified LAA and no cardioembolic or mixed etiologies were enrolled. Patients were defined as having LAA-AIS if they met the following criteria: (1) acute stroke onset within 24 hours before admission; (2) focal neurological deficits, with a minority presenting with global neurological deficits; (3) acute infarct confirmed by brain CT or MRI, or persistent focal neurological deficits lasting more than 24 hours; (4) exclusion of non‑vascular etiologies of neurological deficits; (5) ≥50% stenosis or occlusion of a relevant extracranial or intracranial artery, as documented by CTA, MRA, or DSA; (6) clinical manifestations and infarct topography consistent with large artery atherosclerotic disease; (7) no high-risk cardioembolic sources, including atrial fibrillation, significant valvular heart disease, or intracardiac thrombus; (8) no typical lacunar infarcts (<1.5 cm) or imaging evidence of isolated small vessel occlusion. Two experienced neuroradiologists independently reviewed the CT/MRI scans and clinical records. Exclusion criteria for LAA-AIS group: (1) prior stroke or transient ischemic attack; (2) hemorrhagic stroke or other non-ischemic cerebrovascular disorders; (3) acute or chronic infection within one month before presentation; (4) malignant tumors or hematologic diseases; (5) severe hepatic or renal dysfunction; (6) history of cerebrovascular or coronary heart disease; (7) incomplete clinical or laboratory data; (8) severe psychiatric disorders affecting the ability to cooperate with clinical assessments.

Definitions

Participants were classified as hypertensive if they had systolic blood pressure ≥ 140 mmHg and/or diastolic blood pressure ≥ 90 mmHg on at least two separate readings, or if they had a previous hypertension diagnosis with ongoing treatment.27 Diabetes mellitus was identified based on fasting plasma glucose ≥ 7.0 mmol/L or postprandial glucose ≥ 11.1 mmol/L at the 2-hour mark, or a prior diagnosis with current antidiabetic therapy. Dyslipidemia was defined as total cholesterol (TC) ≥ 6.2 mmol/L, triglycerides (TG) ≥ 2.3 mmol/L, low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C) ≥ 4.1 mmol/L, high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C) < 1.0 mmol/L, or a prior diagnosis with ongoing lipid-lowering therapy.28 For patients with LAA‑AIS, a favorable outcome was defined as a 90-day modified Rankin Scale (mRS) score ≤ 2, whereas a score > 2 indicated a poor outcome.29

Data Collection

Demographic and clinical characteristics, including age, sex, body mass index (BMI), smoking history, statin use, and comorbidities (hypertension, diabetes mellitus, and dyslipidemia) were extracted from electronic medical records. Blood samples were collected for hematological and biochemical analyses, including white blood cell count (WBC), red blood cell count (RBC), alanine aminotransferase (ALT), blood urea nitrogen (BUN), glucose, glycated hemoglobin (HbA1c), C-reactive protein (CRP) and lipid profile (TC, TG, HDL-C and LDL-C). The mRS scores were obtained 90 ± 7 days after stroke onset through structured telephone interviews conducted by trained staff. The mRS scores were independently evaluated and recorded by two neurologists, with discrepancies resolved by consensus.

Protein Concentration Assay by ELISA

Blood samples from patients with LAA-AIS were collected at admission, within 24 hours after stroke onset, and prior to the initiation of acute reperfusion therapy or secondary preventive treatments. For healthy controls, blood samples were collected at hospital presentation. Serum fetuin-B was measured by ELISA (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Carlsbad, CA, USA) per the manufacturer’s protocol. Standard solutions and appropriately diluted serum samples were added to antibody-coated microplate wells. Following a 2.5-hour incubation at room-temperature, the plates were washed four times then incubated with the biotinylated detection antibody for 60 minutes. After a further wash, streptavidin-conjugated horseradish peroxidase was added to the wells and incubated for 45 minutes. The wells were washed again, followed by the addition of tetramethylbenzidine substrate, which was allowed to react in the dark for 30 minutes. After the addition of stop solution to terminate the enzyme reaction, absorbance was measured at 450 nm using a plate reader. Concentrations of samples were quantified via interpolation from a calibration curve created using serial dilutions of standards with known concentrations. The assay was performed in a single batch with duplicate measurements for all samples to minimize inter-assay variability. The average intra-assay coefficient of variation was < 10%.

Statistical Analysis

Depending on the distribution normality, quantitative variables were appropriately expressed as the mean ± standard deviation (SD) or median (interquartile range, IQR), with intergroup comparisons conducted via the t-test or the Mann–Whitney U-test, respectively. Categorical variables were reported as counts and percentages, with group differences assessed using the chi-square (χ2) test. Missing values accounted for less than 5% of all variables. Therefore, complete-case analyses were performed, and no imputation was conducted. Spearman correlation analysis was used to assess associations between serum fetuin-B and clinical parameters. Multivariable logistic regression models were constructed to evaluate the independent associations between fetuin-B and both LAA-AIS risk and poor functional outcome (mRS > 2). Fetuin-B was analyzed primarily as a continuous variable and additionally categorized into tertiles for complementary analyses, with the lowest tertile as the reference group. A variance inflation factor (VIF) < 10 indicated no significant multicollinearity. Restricted cubic spline (RCS) regression was used to explore potential nonlinear relationships. The incremental predictive performance was assessed by comparing the C-index, net reclassification index (NRI), and integrated discrimination improvement (IDI) between the basic model and the model incorporating fetuin-B. Model calibration was assessed using calibration curves, the Brier score, and the Hosmer-Lemeshow goodness-of-fit test. Internal validation was performed with 1000 bootstrap resamples, and decision curve analysis was conducted to evaluate clinical net benefit. Statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS Statistics version 26.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). Graphs were generated using GraphPad Prism version 9.0 (GraphPad Software, San Diego, CA, USA) and R version 4.5.1 (https://www.R-project.org). A two-tailed P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Baseline Characteristics of Patients with LAA-AIS and Controls

After applying the inclusion and exclusion criteria, 216 LAA-AIS patients were included, and 194 healthy controls were enrolled for the final analysis. The flowchart of patient enrollment is presented in Figure 1. LAA-AIS patients had higher rates of hypertension, diabetes, and dyslipidemia (all P < 0.001). Serum fetuin-B was significantly higher in patients than controls (2.68 vs 1.79 µg/mL, P < 0.001) (Table 1). WBC counts were also significantly increased in the LAA-AIS group (8.10 [6.40–9.90] × 109/L vs 6.80 [5.78–8.70] × 109/L, P < 0.001), whereas no significant intergroup differences were observed in the levels of RBC, ALT, BUN, and glucose (all P > 0.05). Correlation Analysis Between Serum Fetuin-B Levels and Clinical Parameters in the Entire Cohort

The associations between serum fetuin-B concentrations and various clinical parameters were shown in Figure 2. Spearman correlation analysis indicated positive relationships between serum fetuin-B levels and several key clinical indicators. Specifically, fetuin-B was positively correlated with TC (r = 0.121, P = 0.014) and LDL-C (r = 0.120, P = 0.009). Similarly, positive correlations were observed between fetuin-B and HbA1c (r = 0.116, P = 0.021) as well as CRP (r = 0.199, P < 0.001). These findings suggest that elevated fetuin-B levels are closely associated with adverse glucose-lipid metabolic disturbances and systemic inflammation. Association Between Serum Fetuin-B and the Presence of LAA-AIS in Multivariable Logistic Regression Models

As shown in Table 3, logistic regression analyses were performed to evaluate the independent association between serum fetuin-B levels and the risk of LAA-AIS. Fetuin-B was primarily analyzed as a continuous variable, with tertile-based categorical analysis conducted as a supplementary approach to demonstrate the graded relationship. Higher fetuin-B levels, analyzed as a continuous variable, were consistently associated with increased odds of LAA-AIS in all regression models (all P < 0.001). TC and LDL-C were excluded from the final multivariable regression analysis due to a high VIF (> 10) indicating strong multicollinearity. In the unadjusted Model 1, increased serum fetuin-B was associated with higher odds of LAA-AIS, with an OR of 1.670. Following adjustment for age, gender, BMI, smoking, statin, hypertension, diabetes, and dyslipidemia in Model 2, the association was moderately strengthened, yielding an OR of 2.296. Importantly, the positive association remained statistically robust in the fully adjusted Model 3, with an OR of 2.433 (95% CI: 1.522–3.892). In complementary tertile-based analyses, a graded increase in LAA-AIS risk was observed across increasing fetuin-B tertiles. In the fully adjusted model, fetuin-B T2 was associated with a 2.52-fold higher risk of LAA-AIS versus T1, and T3 showed an even stronger association (all P for trend < 0.001). Discussion

This study showed that serum fetuin-B measured after stroke onset was elevated in patients with LAA-AIS and was associated with adverse metabolic and inflammatory profiles. Higher fetuin-B levels remained independently associated with both LAA-AIS and poor functional outcome after adjustment for conventional risk factors. In addition, incorporating fetuin-B into basic models improved discrimination and prognostic stratification.

Earlier studies have linked fetuin-B to dyslipidemia and chronic low-grade inflammation, two interrelated processes that promote atherosclerosis and related vascular events.30–32 In polycystic ovary syndrome, fetuin-B has been shown to correlate closely with insulin resistance as well as inflammatory and lipid abnormalities.30 In familial hypercholesterolemia, multi-omic analyses indicate that fetuin-B is upregulated alongside pro-atherogenic protein signatures and pathways related to oxidative stress, immune activation, and endothelial dysfunction.31 Our findings align closely with previous evidence regarding the role of fetuin-B in metabolic disturbances and atherosclerosis-related disorders. In this study, fetuin-B levels correlated positively with HbA1c, LDL-C, and TC, reinforcing the viewpoint that it is involved in dysregulated glycolipid metabolism. In a population with elevated vascular risk, higher fetuin-B levels are associated with adverse lipid profiles and systemic inflammation, linking fetuin-B to the atherosclerotic and metabolic phenotype underlying LAA-AIS.

Increased serum fetuin-B levels have been suggested as indicators of atherosclerotic burden and heightened cardiovascular risk.22,33–35 In a young population, fetuin-B exhibits significantly increased levels and is positively correlated with lifetime cardiovascular risk estimates, serving as a potential tool for early cardiovascular risk assessment.33 Furthermore, evidence from coronary artery disease research shows that fetuin-B acts as a potential biomarker for acute coronary events.34 These findings support the notion that fetuin-B may reflect atherosclerotic burden and could serve as a biomarker for vascular risk. Accumulating evidence indicates that aberrantly elevated fetuin-B exerts pro-inflammatory effects, promotes atherosclerotic plaque destabilization, and worsens ischemia/reperfusion injury. In diabetic mice, upregulated hepatic fetuin-B exacerbates myocardial ischemia/reperfusion injury and cardiac dysfunction via direct interaction with the insulin receptor β. Additionally, in vivo administration of fetuin-B has been shown to reduce the stability of atherosclerotic plaques in mice, further supporting its role in promoting plaque instability.35 Clinical studies have demonstrated that fetuin-B activates the NF-κB signaling pathway in a dose-dependent manner, which in turn promotes microglial activation and contributes to inflammatory neurovascular disease in diabetic retinopathy.32 In myocardial infarction models, fetuin-B increases lipid deposition and the production of pro-inflammatory cytokines and induces apoptosis in vascular smooth muscle cells, thereby compromising plaque stability and facilitating the onset of acute myocardial infarction.22 These findings provide biological plausibility for our observations. Consistently, proteomic analyses have reported altered fetuin-B expression in intracranial blood of AIS patients, implying a potential role in stroke-related pathophysiological processes.24

The association between fetuin-B and LAA-AIS may be mediated by several interrelated mechanisms. Fetuin-B has been reported to promote insulin resistance, which may subsequently induce endothelial dysfunction, oxidative stress and vascular inflammation, all of which are key pathological processes involved in atherosclerosis.36,37 It may also contribute to dyslipidemia by regulating lipid metabolism and increasing atherogenic lipoproteins, particularly LDL-C.34 Moreover, experimental evidence suggests that fetuin-B can amplify inflammatory signaling pathways and upregulate pro-inflammatory cytokine expression.21 These metabolic disturbances and pro-inflammatory effects may collectively accelerate atherosclerotic progression. Accordingly, the elevated circulating fetuin-B levels observed in patients with LAA-AIS may reflect pre-existing metabolic abnormalities and atherosclerotic predisposition, an acute-phase response to stroke, or secondary inflammatory activation and metabolic stress induced by cerebral ischemia. However, because fetuin-B was measured only once after stroke onset in the present study, these possibilities cannot be fully distinguished or causally verified.

Our study demonstrated that elevated fetuin-B independently predicted poor functional outcome in LAA-AIS. Consistent with previous studies linking inflammatory markers such as NLR and hs-CRP to unfavorable prognosis after AIS, higher CRP levels were also associated with adverse outcomes in our cohort.38,39 Unlike cell-based inflammatory indices,40,41 however, fetuin-B is a hepatokine closely related to metabolic dysfunction, linking systemic inflammation with glucose–lipid imbalance. This is further supported by our finding that higher glucose, HbA1c, TC, and LDL-C levels were associated with poor functional outcome, in line with prior evidence implicating metabolic dysfunction and insulin resistance in worse stroke prognosis.41–43 Notably, fetuin-B remained predictive even after adjustment for lipid parameters, suggesting that it reflects broader metabolic and inflammatory disturbances beyond isolated lipid abnormalities. Mechanistically, lipidomic studies have revealed disrupted membrane glycerophospholipid and sphingolipid signaling pathways in IS, closely linked to inflammation and thrombosis.44 Furthermore, cholesterol accumulation in microglia perpetuates chronic neuroinflammation and impairs recovery.45 Given its role in insulin resistance and lipid metabolism, fetuin-B may contribute to adverse outcomes by promoting dyslipidemia, metabolic imbalance, and post-ischemic inflammatory responses. Overall, fetuin-B may serve as an integrative biomarker linking metabolic dysfunction and inflammation in LAA-AIS.

From a clinical perspective, fetuin-B is easily measurable and may complement conventional clinical and laboratory variables for risk stratification and prognostic assessment. Patients with conventional risk factors and abnormally elevated fetuin‑B levels may carry a higher atherosclerotic burden and may therefore require earlier intervention.

Several limitations of this study need to be addressed. First, its retrospective single-center design may introduce selection bias and limit the generalizability of findings. Second, serum fetuin-B was tested only once after stroke onset, making it impossible to clarify whether its elevation stems from pre-existing metabolic and atherosclerotic predisposition, acute stroke-induced inflammatory response, or cerebral ischemia-related secondary inflammation. Accordingly, the observed association cannot confirm temporal sequence or causal relationship. Third, lack of serial fetuin-B measurements prevented us from exploring its dynamic changes across acute and recovery stages of LAA-AIS. Fourth, despite adjustment for common vascular, inflammatory, and metabolic confounders, unmeasured factors including physical activity, dietary and lifestyle factors might still affect fetuin-B levels. Fifth, the study lacks external validation with an independent cohort. Further large-scale prospective multicenter studies with serial measurements and external validation are required to validate the predictive value of fetuin-B for stroke onset and monitor disease progression.

Conclusion

In conclusion, elevated serum fetuin-B levels measured after stroke onset were independently associated with LAA-AIS status and poor functional outcome. Fetuin-B improved the performance of clinical models, suggesting that it may serve as a novel biomarker for LAA-AIS. Prospective multicenter studies with serial measurements are needed to clarify the temporal and causal significance of fetuin-B elevation in LAA-AIS.

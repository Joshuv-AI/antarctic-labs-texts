Abstract
. In this paper we shall use realization theory, a favourite technique of Rien Kaashoek, to prove new results about a class of holomorphic functions on an annulus R δ = def { z ∈ ℂ : δ < | z | < 1 } , R_{\delta}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm def}}{{=}}\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\delta<|z|<1\}, where 0 < δ < 1 0<\delta<1 . The class of functions in question arises in the early work of R. G. Douglas and V. I. Paulsen on the rational dilation of a Hilbert space operator T T to a normal operator with spectrum in ∂ R δ \partial R_{\delta} . Their work suggested the following norm ∥ ⋅ ∥ dp \|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{dp}} on the space Hol ⁡ ( R δ ) \mathrm{Hol}(R_{\delta}) of holomorphic functions on R δ R_{\delta} , ∥ φ ∥ dp = def sup { ∥ φ ( T ) ∥ : ∥ T ∥ ≤ 1 , ∥ T − 1 ∥ ≤ 1 / δ and σ ( T ) ⊆ R δ } . \|\varphi\|_{\mathrm{dp}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm def}}{{=}}\sup\{\|\varphi(T)\|:\|T\|\leq 1,\|T^{-1}\|\leq 1/\delta\ \text{and}\ \sigma(T)\subseteq R_{\delta}\}. By analogy with the classical Schur class of holomorphic functions 𝒮 \mathcal{S} with supremum norm at most 1 1 on the disc 𝔻 \mathbb{D} , it is natural to consider the dp-Schur class 𝒮 dp \mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{dp}} of holomorphic functions of dp-norm at most 1 1 on R δ R_{\delta} . Our central result is a generalization of the classical realization formula, for φ ∈ 𝒮 \varphi\in\mathcal{S} , φ ⁡ ( z ) = A + B ​ z ​ ( 1 − D ​ z ) − 1 ​ C , for ​ z ∈ 𝔻 , \varphi(z)=A+Bz(1-Dz)^{-1}C,\ \text{for}\ z\in\mathbb{D}, to functions from 𝒮 dp \mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{dp}} . A second result is a Pick interpolation theorem for functions in 𝒮 dp \mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{dp}} that is analogous to Abrahamse’s Interpolation Theorem for bounded holomorphic functions on a multiply-connected domain. For a tuple λ = ( λ 1 , … , λ n ) \lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n}) of distinct interpolation nodes in R δ R_{\delta} , we introduce a special set 𝒢 dp ​ ( λ ) \mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{dp}}(\lambda) of positive definite n × n n\times n matrices, which we call DP Szegő kernels . Introduction

It is our honour to contribute to this memorial issue for Marinus Kaashoek, who was a prolific and influential operator theorist throughout a long career.
A constant thread in his research over several decades was the power of realization theory applied to a wide variety of problems in analysis. Among his many contributions in this area we mention his monograph
[
8
]
, written with his longstanding collaborators Israel Gohberg and Harm Bart, which was an early and influential work in the area, and his more recent papers and book, including
[
15
,
14
,
16
]
. Realization theory uses explicit formulae for functions in terms of operators on Hilbert space to prove function-theoretic results.
In this paper we continue along the Bart-Gohberg-Kaashoek path by exploiting realization theory to prove new results about a class of holomorphic functions which was first encountered by R. G. Douglas and V. I. Paulsen in a study of rational dilation on the annulus.

For any open set
\Omega
in the plane,
{\rm Hol}(\Omega)
will denote the set of holomorphic functions on
\Omega
and
{\rm H}^{\infty}(\Omega)
will denote the Banach algebra of bounded holomorphic functions on
\Omega
, equipped with the supremum norm
\|\varphi\|_{{\rm H}^{\infty}(\Omega)}=\sup_{z\in\Omega}|\varphi(z)|
. Let
\mathcal{S}(\Omega)
denote the class
\{\varphi\in{\rm H}^{\infty}(\Omega):\|\varphi\|_{{\rm H}^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq 1\}
.
The classical Schur class,
\mathcal{S}
, is the set
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{D})
.

We recall the extensively-studied Pick interpolation theorem
[
20
]
for bounded holomorphic functions on the open unit disc
\mathbb{D}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n}\in\mathbb{D}
be distinct and let
z_{1},\dots,z_{n}\in\mathbb{C}
.
There exists
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}
such that

\varphi(\lambda_{j})=z_{j}\qquad\text{for}\ j=1,\ldots,n,

if and only if,

\begin{bmatrix}\displaystyle\frac{1-\overline{z_{i}}z_{j}}{1-\overline{\lambda_{i}}\lambda_{j}}\end{bmatrix}_{i,j=1}^{n}\geq 0.

Pick interpolation problems, with the unit disc replaced by other domains
\Omega
in the plane, have also been much studied.
In the event that
\Omega
is a simply connected proper open subset of
\mathbb{C}
, with the aid of the conformal map
F:\Omega\to\mathbb{D}
, we can convert this problem into a classical Pick problem on
\mathbb{D}
with interpolation data
F(\lambda_{j})\mapsto w_{j}
for
j=1,\ldots,n,
and then Pick’s theorem gives a criterion for the existence of
\varphi
in terms of the positivity of the appropriate “Pick matrix”, which here is

\begin{bmatrix}\displaystyle\frac{1-\overline{w_{i}}w_{j}}{1-\overline{F(\lambda_{i})}F(\lambda_{j})}\end{bmatrix}_{i,j=1}^{n}\geq 0.

More generally, the Pick problem on a multiply connected domain was studied in the 1940s by Garabedian
[
17
]
and Heins
[
19
]
. Later, Sarason
[
21
]
and Abrahamse
[
1
]
treated the problem in terms of reproducing kernels, an approach that we follow in this paper. Abrahamse’s Theorem gives a solution to the Pick interpolation problem on any bounded domain
\Omega
in the plane whose boundary consists of finitely many disjoint analytic Jordan curves. He showed that a Pick problem on
\Omega
can be solved if and only if an infinite collection of Pick matrices are positive semi-definite.
In the case of the annulus
R_{\delta}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\delta<|z|<1\}
, for a tuple
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})
of distinct interpolation nodes in
R_{\delta}
,
Abrahamse
[
1
]
described a family
\mathcal{G}(\lambda)
of positive definite
n\times n
matrices
for which the following statement is true:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n}\in R_{\delta}
be distinct and let
z_{1},\dots,z_{n}\in\mathbb{C}
.
There exists
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}(R_{\delta})
such that

\varphi(\lambda_{j})=z_{j}\qquad\text{for}\ j=1,\ldots,n,

if and only if, for each
g\in\mathcal{G}(\lambda)
,

\begin{bmatrix}(1-\overline{z_{i}}z_{j})g_{ij}\end{bmatrix}_{i,j=1}^{n}\geq 0.

An alternative explicit choice of
\mathcal{G}(\lambda)
for which Theorem
1.2
is true is described in
[
21
,
2
]
as follows

\mathcal{G}(\lambda)=\{\begin{bmatrix}g_{\rho}(\lambda_{i},\lambda_{j})\end{bmatrix}_{i,j=1}^{n}:\rho>0\},

where

g_{\rho}(\lambda_{i},\lambda_{j})=\sum_{m=-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{(\bar{\lambda}_{i}\lambda_{j})^{m}}{\rho+\delta^{2m}},\quad\text{for}\ 1\leq i,j\leq n.

Another natural variant of Pick’s problem arises if one replaces the supremum norm on
{\rm Hol}(\Omega)
by a different norm. For example, consider the
Dirichlet space

\mathcal{D}
of holomorphic functions
f
on
\mathbb{D}
such that
f^{\prime}
is square integrable with respect to area measure on
\mathbb{D}
, with pointwise operations and the norm

\|f\|_{\mathcal{D}}^{2}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(n+1)|\hat{f}(n)|^{2}=\|f\|_{H^{2}}^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f^{\prime}(z)|^{2}dm(z),

where
m
denotes area measure on the disc.
The Dirichlet space is a Hilbert function space on
\mathbb{D}
with reproducing kernel

k_{\mathcal{D}}(\lambda,\mu)=-\frac{1}{\bar{\mu}\lambda}\log(1-\bar{\mu}\lambda).

The Pick-type interpolation problem appropriate to this Hilbert function space is expressed in terms of its
multiplier space

\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{D})
, which is defined to be the space of functions
\varphi
on
\mathbb{D}
such that
\varphi f\in\mathcal{D}
for every
f\in\mathcal{D}
, with pointwise operations and the
multiplier norm

\|\varphi\|_{\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{D})}=\sup\{\|\varphi f\|_{\mathcal{D}}:f\in\mathcal{D},\|f\|_{\mathcal{D}}\leq 1\}.

In this setting the corresponding Pick interpolation theorem is the following
[
3
, Corollary 7.41]
:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n}\in\mathbb{D}
be distinct and let
z_{1},\dots,z_{n}\in\mathbb{C}
.
There exists
\varphi\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{D})
such that
\|\varphi\|_{\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{D})}\leq 1
and

\varphi(\lambda_{j})=z_{j}\qquad\text{for}\ j=1,\ldots,n,

if and only if

\begin{bmatrix}(1-z_{i}\overline{z_{j}})k_{\mathcal{D}}(\lambda_{i},\lambda_{j})\end{bmatrix}_{i,j=1}^{n}\geq 0.

An account of Pick theorems in the context of sundry different Hilbert function spaces, including
\mathcal{D},
may be found in the book
[
3
]
.

An important step in the Douglas-Paulsen theory was the following estimate.
If
X
is a Douglas-Paulsen operator with parameter
\delta

and

is a bounded holomorphic matrix-valued function on

then

\|\varphi(X)\|\leq\left(2+\frac{1+\delta}{1-\delta}\right)\sup_{z\in R_{\delta}}\|\varphi(z)\|.

(1.5)

Hence, we see from equations (
1.4

\|\varphi\|_{\mathrm{dp}}\leq\left(2+\frac{1+\delta}{1-\delta}\right)\|\varphi\|_{{\rm H}^{\infty}(R_{\delta})}

\|\varphi\|_{{\rm H}^{\infty}(R_{\delta})}\leq\|\varphi\|_{\mathrm{dp}}

the dp and supremum norms on

are equivalent. Thus,

\mathrm{H}^{\infty}(R_{\delta})=\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{dp}}^{\infty}(R_{\delta})

as sets. However, the reader should be aware that

\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{dp}}\neq\|\cdot\|_{{\rm H}^{\infty}(R_{\delta})}\ \text{and therefore}\ \mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{dp}}\neq\mathcal{S}(R_{\delta}),

a fact that Example

The power of inequality (

This result is a kind of Nagy dilation theorem for the annulus.
In the

Corresponding to the dp-Schur class there is a natural variant of the classical Pick interpolation problem, which we call the
DP Pick problem

given

\varphi\in{\rm H}^{\infty}_{\mathrm{dp}}(R_{\delta})

\|\varphi\|_{\mathrm{dp}}\leq 1
such that

\varphi(\lambda_{j})=z_{j}\qquad\text{for}\ j=1,\ldots,n?

(1.7)

We shall show that there is a solvability criterion for this problem which is parallel to Abrahamse’s Theorem, but with
\mathcal{G}(\lambda)

Definition 1.8

Let
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n}
be

R_{\delta}
and let
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})

A

[(1-\bar{\lambda}_{i}\lambda_{j})g_{ij}]\geq 0\ \ \text{ and }\ \ \left[\left(1-\overline{\frac{\delta}{\lambda_{i}}}\frac{\delta}{\lambda_{j}}\right)g_{ij}\right]\geq 0.

(1.9)

The set of all DP Szegő kernels for the
n
-tuple
\lambda

\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{dp}}(\lambda)

We observe that
\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{dp}}(\lambda)

consists of the gramians

such that the operator

This and related facts are described in Section

e_{1},\dots,e_{n}
of an
n
-dimensional Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
. Define
T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
by

Te_{j}=\lambda_{j}e_{j},\ j=1,\dots,n.

(4.17)

By Proposition
4.10
,
T\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{dp}}(\delta,\lambda)
.
By assumption,

\varphi\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{dp}}
, and so
\|\varphi(T)\|\leq 1
.
For any

x=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\xi_{j}e_{j}\in\mathcal{H}
,

\displaystyle\varphi(T)x

\displaystyle=\varphi(T)\sum_{j=1}^{n}\xi_{j}e_{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\xi_{j}\varphi(\lambda_{j})e_{j}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\xi_{j}z_{j}e_{j}.

(4.18)

Therefore, by equation (
4
), the condition
\|\varphi(T)\|\leq 1

translates into

[(1-\bar{z}_{i}z_{j})g_{ij}]\geq 0.

∎

Definition 4.19
.

Let
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n}
be
n
distinct points in
R_{\delta}
.
We say that a DP-Szegő kernel
[g_{ij}]\in\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{dp}}(\lambda)
is
normalized
if
g_{ii}=1
for

.

Let
\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{dp}}^{\mathrm{norm}}(\lambda)
denote the set of normalized DP-Szegő kernels for the
n
-tuple
(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})
.

Remark 4.20
.

Let
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n}
be
n
distinct points in
R_{\delta}
.
Every DP-Szegő kernel
[g_{ij}]
from
\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{dp}}(\lambda)
is diagonally congruent to a normalized DP-Szegő kernel.

Proof.

For any matrix
[g_{ij}]\in\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{dp}}(\lambda)
, we can define a positive definite matrix
[h_{ij}]
by

h_{ii}=1\ \text{for}\ i=1,\dots,n\ \text{and}\ h_{ij}=c_{i}^{-1}g_{ij}c_{j}^{-1}\ \text{if}\ i\neq j

(4.21)

where

c_{i}=\sqrt{g_{ii}}\ \text{if}\ g_{ii}\neq 0\ \text{and}\ c_{i}=1\ \text{if}\ g_{ii}=0.

(4.22)

Then
h_{ii}=1
for each
i
, and

\begin{bmatrix}h_{ij}\end{bmatrix}_{i,j=1}^{n}=C^{*}\begin{bmatrix}g_{ij}\end{bmatrix}_{i,j=1}^{n}C\ \text{where}\ C=\mathrm{diag}\{1/c_{1},\dots,1/c_{n}\}.

(4.23)

On conjugating the inequalities (
4.8
) by the matrix
C
we find that
[h_{ij}]
belongs to
\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{dp}}^{\mathrm{norm}}(\lambda)
.
∎

Proposition 4.24
.

Let
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n}
be
n
distinct points in
R_{\delta}
.
The set
\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{dp}}^{\mathrm{norm}}(\lambda)
is compact in the topology of the space of
n\times n
complex matrices. Moreover, for fixed target data
z_{1},\dots,z_{n}
,

\begin{bmatrix}(1-\bar{z}_{i}z_{j})g_{ij}\end{bmatrix}_{i,j=1}^{n}\geq 0\ \text{for all}\ g\in\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{dp}}(\lambda)

(4.25)

if and only if

\begin{bmatrix}(1-\bar{z}_{i}z_{j})g_{ij}\end{bmatrix}_{i,j=1}^{n}\geq 0\ \text{for all}\ g\in\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{dp}}^{\mathrm{norm}}(\lambda).

(4.26)

Proof.

Consider any matrix
g=[g_{ij}]\in\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{dp}}^{\mathrm{norm}}(\lambda)
. Since
g
is positive definite, the principal minor on rows
i
and
j
is non-negative, which is to say that
1-|g_{ij}|^{2}\geq 0
for
i,j=1,\dots,n
. It follows that the operator norm
\|g\|\leq n
, and so
\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{dp}}^{\mathrm{norm}}(\lambda)
is bounded. Let us prove that
\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{dp}}^{\mathrm{norm}}(\lambda)
is sequentially compact.

Let
g^{\ell}
,
\ell=1,2,\dots
, be a sequence in
\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{dp}}^{\mathrm{norm}}(\lambda)
.
We claim that
(g^{\ell})_{\ell\geq 1}
has a subsequence that converges to an element of
\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{dp}}^{\mathrm{norm}}(\lambda)
. For each
\ell
, since
g^{\ell}
is non-singular, by the definition of
\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{dp}}^{\mathrm{norm}}(\lambda)
, we may pick a basis
e_{1}^{\ell},\dots,e_{n}^{\ell}
of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
such that
g^{\ell}
is the gramian matrix of the basis
e_{1}^{\ell},\dots,e_{n}^{\ell}
, which is to say that

g^{\ell}=[g_{ij}^{\ell}],\text{ where }g_{ij}^{\ell}=\langle e_{j}^{\ell},e_{i}^{\ell}\rangle\qquad\text{for}\ i,j=1,\ldots,n.

(4.27)

Define
T^{\ell}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
by

T^{\ell}e_{j}^{\ell}=\lambda_{j}e_{j}^{\ell},\ j=1,\dots,n.

(4.28)

Note that since
g^{\ell}
is normalised, that is,

g_{ii}^{\ell}=\langle e_{i}^{\ell},e_{i}^{\ell}\rangle=1\qquad\text{for}\ i=1,\ldots,n,

and so

\|e_{i}^{\ell}\|=1
for
i=1,\ldots,n
. Then, by Proposition
4.10
,

\sigma(T^{\ell})=\{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}\}

and
T^{\ell}\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{dp}}(\delta,\lambda)
.
By the compactness of the unit sphere in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
we can choose a subsequence
(e_{i}^{\ell_{k}})_{k\geq 1}
of
(e_{i}^{\ell})_{\ell\geq 1}
such that

(e_{j}^{\ell_{k}})
converges to a unit vector
v_{j}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
as
k\to\infty
for
j=1,\dots,n
.
By the compactness of the unit ball in
\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
, by passing to a further subsequence
(e^{\ell_{k}})_{k\geq 1}
of
(e^{\ell})_{\ell\geq 1}
we can arrange also that

(T^{\ell_{k}})
converges to a limit
T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
as
k\to\infty
.
In the relations

T^{\ell_{k}}e_{j}^{\ell_{k}}=\lambda_{j}e_{j}^{\ell_{k}},\ j=1,\dots,n,

(4.29)

let
k\to\infty
to obtain

Tv_{j}=\lambda_{j}v_{j}\qquad\text{and }\ \|v_{j}\|=1\ \qquad j=1,\dots,n.

(4.30)

Thus

\sigma(T^{\ell})=\{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}\},

the eigenvectors
v_{1},\dots,v_{n}
of
T
corresponding to the distinct eigenvalues
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}
are linearly independent and therefore span
\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
and
T^{\ell}\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{dp}}(\delta,\lambda)
.
Let
g
be the Gramian of the vectors
v_{1},\dots,v_{n}
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
: then
g
is positive definite, and
by Proposition
4.9
,
g\in\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{dp}}^{\mathrm{norm}}(\lambda)
.
We have

g_{ij}=\langle v_{j},v_{i}\rangle=\lim_{k\to\infty}\langle v_{j}^{\ell_{k}},v_{i}^{\ell_{k}}\rangle=\lim_{k\to\infty}g_{ij}^{\ell_{k}}

for
i,j=1,\dots,n
, and so
g^{\ell_{k}}\to g
as
k\to\infty
.
We have shown that
\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{dp}}^{\mathrm{norm}}(\lambda)
is sequentially compact in
the metrizable topology of
\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
, hence it is compact.

To prove the “Moreover”, fix target data
z_{1},\dots,z_{n}
. Since
\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{dp}}(\lambda)\supset\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{dp}}^{\mathrm{norm}}(\lambda)
, trivially statement (
4.25
) implies statement (
4.26
). Conversely, suppose statement (
4.26
) holds and consider any kernel
g\in\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{dp}}(\lambda)
. Define matrices
h=[h_{ij}]
and
C
by the relations (
4.21
), (
4.22
) and (
4.23
). Then
h\in\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{dp}}^{\mathrm{norm}}(\lambda)
,
and so, by assumption,

[(1-\bar{z}_{i}z_{j})h_{ij}]\geq 0

(4.31)

Conjugate this matrix inequality by
\mathrm{diag}\{c_{1},\dots,c_{n}\}
to obtain the relation (
4.25
).
Thus the relation (
4.26
) implies the relation (
4.25
).
∎

Definition 4.32
.

Let
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n}
be
n
distinct points in
R_{\delta}
.
Let
g\in\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{dp}}(\lambda)
be a DP Szegő kernel for the
n
-tuple
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})
.
The matrices

[(1-\bar{\lambda}_{i}\lambda_{j})g_{ij}]_{i,j=1}^{n}\ \ \text{ and }\ \ \left[\left(1-\frac{\delta}{\overline{\lambda_{i}}}\frac{\delta}{\lambda_{j}}\right)g_{ij}\right]_{i,j=1}^{n}

(4.33)

are said to be the
defect matrices of
g
.

Say that a DP-Szegő kernel
g
on
R_{\delta}
is
reducible
if there exist DP-Szegő kernels

h
and
k
on
R_{\delta}
such that
g=h+k
and neither
h
nor
k
is diagonally congruent to
g
.
Here two kernels
g
and
h
on
R_{\delta}
are said to be diagonally congruent if there exists a function
c:R_{\delta}\to\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
such that, for all
\lambda,\mu\in R_{\delta}
,
h(\lambda,\mu)=c(\lambda)g(\lambda,\mu)\overline{c(\mu)}
. A DP-Szegő kernel is
irreducible
if it is not reducible. Clearly, if DP Pick data
\lambda_{j}\mapsto z_{j},j=1,\dots,n
, are such that

[(1-\bar{z}_{i}z_{j})g_{ij}]\geq 0\ \text{and}\ [1-\frac{\delta^{2}}{\overline{z_{i}}z_{j}}g_{ij}]\geq 0

for all irreducible DP Szegő kernels
g
then the same inequality holds for
all
DP Szegő kernels, and consequently the DP pick interpolation problem is solvable. Since the class of irreducible DP Szegő kernels is likely to be
much
smaller than the class of all DP Szegő kernels, it would be valuable to identify the irreducible DP Szegő kernels on
R_{\delta}
.

5.
The DP Pick problem and DP-Szegő kernels

Definition 5.1
.

Let
H_{n}
be the real linear space of Hermitian matrices in
\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
.
A subset
P
of
H_{n}
is called a
cone
if the following conditions are satisfied:
(i)
P+P\subseteq P
, (ii)
P\cap(-P)=\{0\}
and (iii)
\alpha P\subseteq P
whenever

\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and
\alpha\geq 0
.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n}\in R_{\delta}
be distinct and let
z_{1},\dots,z_{n}\in\mathbb{C}
.
There exists
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{dp}}
such that

\varphi(\lambda_{j})=z_{j}\qquad\text{for}\ j=1,\ldots,n,

if and only if, for all

g\in\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{dp}}(\lambda)
,

[(1-\bar{z}_{i}z_{j})g_{ij}]\geq 0.

(5.3)

Proof.

Implication
\Rightarrow
follows from Proposition
4.14
.

To prove
\Leftarrow
, suppose that

[(1-\bar{z}_{i}z_{j})g_{ij}]\geq 0

(5.4)

for all

g\in\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{dp}}(\lambda)
.

By Theorem
3.19
, to show that the DP Pick Problem (
3.17
) is solvable it suffices to prove that there exist a pair of
n\times n
positive semi-definite matrices

A=[a_{ij}]
and
B=[b_{ij}]

such that

1-\overline{z_{i}}z_{j}=(1-\bar{\lambda}_{i}\lambda_{j})a_{ij}+(\bar{\lambda}_{i}\lambda_{j}-\delta^{2})b_{ij}

for all
i,j=1,\dots,n
.
Let
H_{n}
be the real linear space of Hermitian matrices in
\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
, and let

{\mathcal{C}}=\left\{[(1-\bar{\lambda}_{i}\lambda_{j})a_{ij}]_{i,j=1}^{n}++\left[\left(1-\frac{\delta^{2}}{\bar{\lambda}_{i}\lambda_{j}}\right)b_{ij}\right]_{i,j=1}^{n}:\,[a_{ij}]_{i,j=1}^{n}\geq 0\ \text{and}\ [b_{ij}]_{i,j=1}^{n}\geq 0\right\}\subset H_{n}.

(5.5)

The subset
{\mathcal{C}}
is a closed convex cone in
H_{n}
.

Note that every
n\times n
positive semi-definite matrix
[a_{ij}]_{i,j=1}^{n}
belongs to
{\mathcal{C}}
. By the positivity of Szegő kernel
\left[\frac{1}{1-\bar{\lambda}_{i}\lambda_{j}}\right]_{i,j=1}^{n}
, the
n\times n
matrix of the form

\left[\frac{a_{ij}}{1-\bar{\lambda}_{i}\lambda_{j}}\right]_{i,j=1}^{n}

is also positive semi-definite. In the definition of
{\mathcal{C}}
(
5.5
)
we may replace
[a_{ij}]_{i,j=1}^{n}

by
\left[\frac{a_{ij}}{1-\bar{\lambda}_{i}\lambda_{j}}\right]_{i,j=1}^{n}
and

[b_{ij}]_{i,j=1}^{n}
by the zero matrix, to deduce that
[a_{ij}]_{i,j=1}^{n}
belongs to
{\mathcal{C}}
.

By the Hahn-Banach theorem, to show that

[1-\bar{z}_{i}z_{j}]_{i,j=1}^{n}
belongs to
{\mathcal{C}}
it suffices to prove that, for every real linear functional
{\mathcal{L}}
on
H_{n}
,
{\mathcal{L}}\geq 0
on
{\mathcal{C}}
implies
{\mathcal{L}}([1-\bar{z}_{i}z_{j}]_{i,j=1}^{n})\geq 0
.

Extend
{\mathcal{L}}
to a complex linear functional
\tilde{\mathcal{L}}
on
\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
by

\tilde{\mathcal{L}}(X+{\mathrm{i}}\,Y)={\mathcal{L}}(X)+{\mathrm{i}}\,{\mathcal{L}}(Y)

for
X,Y\in H_{n}
. Now define a pre-inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{L}
on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
by

\langle\,c,d\,\rangle_{L}=\tilde{\mathcal{L}}(c\otimes d)

for
c,d\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Here
c\otimes d\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
, defined by

(c\otimes d)(x)=\langle\,x,d\,\rangle_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}c\;\;\;\text{for all}\ x\in\mathbb{C}^{n}.

Note that, for any
c\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
,

\langle\,c,c\,\rangle_{L}=\tilde{\mathcal{L}}(c\otimes c)={\mathcal{L}}(c\otimes c)\geq 0.

Let

{\mathcal{N}}=\{x\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:\,\langle\,x,x\,\rangle_{L}=0\}.

Then
{\mathcal{N}}
is a subspace of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{L}
induces an inner product on
\mathbb{C}^{n}/{\mathcal{N}}
.

Let
e_{1},\dots,e_{n}
be the standard basis of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
and let
T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
defined by

Te_{j}=\lambda_{j}e_{j},\ j=1,\dots,n.

(5.6)

For
x=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\xi_{j}e_{j}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, we have

\displaystyle\langle\,x,x\,\rangle_{L}-\langle\,Tx,Tx\,\rangle_{L}

\displaystyle=\tilde{\mathcal{L}}(x\otimes x)-\tilde{\mathcal{L}}(Tx\otimes Tx)

\displaystyle=\tilde{\mathcal{L}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\xi_{j}e_{j}\,\otimes\sum_{i=1}^{n}\xi_{i}e_{i}\right)-\tilde{\mathcal{L}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\xi_{j}\lambda_{j}e_{j}\,\otimes\sum_{i=1}^{n}\xi_{i}\lambda_{i}e_{i}\right)

\displaystyle=\tilde{\mathcal{L}}\left(\sum_{j,i=1}^{n}\xi_{j}\bar{\xi}_{i}e_{j}\,\otimes e_{i}\right)-\tilde{\mathcal{L}}\left(\sum_{j,i=1}^{n}\xi_{j}\lambda_{j}\bar{\xi}_{i}\bar{\lambda}_{i}e_{j}\,\otimes e_{i}\right)

\displaystyle=\tilde{\mathcal{L}}\left(\sum_{j,i=1}^{n}(1-\bar{\lambda}_{i}\lambda_{j})\bar{\xi}_{i}\xi_{j}e_{j}\,\otimes e_{i}\right)

\displaystyle=\tilde{\mathcal{L}}\left[(1-\bar{\lambda}_{i}\lambda_{j})\bar{\xi}_{i}\xi_{j}\right]_{j,i=1}^{n}

\displaystyle={\mathcal{L}}\left[(1-\bar{\lambda}_{i}\lambda_{j})\bar{\xi}_{i}\xi_{j}\right]_{j,i=1}^{n}\geq 0\;\;\;\text{since}\;\;\mathcal{L}\geq 0\ \text{on}\ {\mathcal{C}}.

(5.7)

Thus

\langle\,x,x\,\rangle_{L}-\langle\,Tx,Tx\,\rangle_{L}\geq 0,

and so
x\in{\mathcal{N}}
implies that
Tx\in{\mathcal{N}}
.
Hence
T
induces an operator
\tilde{T}
on
\mathbb{C}^{n}/{\mathcal{N}}
by

\tilde{T}(x+{\mathcal{N}})=Tx+{\mathcal{N}},

and
\|\tilde{T}(x+{\mathcal{N}})\|^{2}\leq\|x+{\mathcal{N}}\|^{2}
for all

(x+{\mathcal{N}})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}/{\mathcal{N}}
.

Similarly, let us prove that
\|\delta\tilde{T}^{-1}\|\leq 1
.
For
x=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\xi_{j}e_{j}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, we have

\displaystyle\langle\,x,x\,\rangle_{L}-\langle\,\delta T^{-1}x,\delta T^{-1}x\,\rangle_{L}

\displaystyle=\tilde{\mathcal{L}}(x\otimes x)-\tilde{\mathcal{L}}(\delta T^{-1}x\otimes\delta T^{-1}x)

\displaystyle=\tilde{\mathcal{L}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\xi_{j}e_{j}\,\otimes\sum_{i=1}^{n}\xi_{i}e_{i}\right)-\tilde{\mathcal{L}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\xi_{j}\frac{\delta}{\lambda_{j}}e_{j}\,\otimes\sum_{i=1}^{n}\xi_{i}\frac{\delta}{\lambda_{i}}e_{i}\right)

\displaystyle=\tilde{\mathcal{L}}\left(\sum_{j,i=1}^{n}\xi_{j}\bar{\xi}_{i}e_{j}\,\otimes e_{i}\right)-\tilde{\mathcal{L}}\left(\sum_{j,i=1}^{n}\xi_{j}\frac{\delta}{\lambda_{j}}\bar{\xi}_{i}\frac{\delta}{\overline{\lambda_{i}}}e_{j}\,\otimes e_{i}\right)

\displaystyle=\tilde{\mathcal{L}}\left(\sum_{j,i=1}^{n}\left(1-\frac{\delta}{\overline{\lambda_{i}}}\frac{\delta}{\lambda_{j}}\right)\bar{\xi}_{i}\xi_{j}e_{j}\,\otimes e_{i}\right)

\displaystyle=\tilde{\mathcal{L}}\left[\left(1-\frac{\delta}{\overline{\lambda_{i}}}\frac{\delta}{\lambda_{j}}\right)\bar{\xi}_{i}\xi_{j}\right]_{j,i=1}^{n}

\displaystyle={\mathcal{L}}\left[\left(1-\frac{\delta}{\overline{\lambda_{i}}}\frac{\delta}{\lambda_{j}}\right)\bar{\xi}_{i}\xi_{j}\right]_{j,i=1}^{n}\geq 0\;\;\;\text{since}\;\;\mathcal{L}\geq 0\ \text{on}\ {\mathcal{C}}.

(5.8)

Thus

\langle\,x,x\,\rangle_{L}-\langle\,\delta T^{-1}x,\delta T^{-1}x\,\rangle_{L}\geq 0,

and so
x\in{\mathcal{N}}
implies that
\delta T^{-1}x\in{\mathcal{N}}
.

Therefore,
T
is a Douglas-Paulsen operator. Since the eigenvalues of
T
,
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n}\in R_{\delta}
,
\sigma(T)\subseteq R_{\delta}
, the operator

T\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{dp}}(\delta)
.
Therefore, by Proposition
4.9
,
[\langle\,e_{j},e_{i}\,\rangle_{L}]_{i,j=1}^{n}
is a DP Szegő kernel for the
n
-tuple
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})
.

By supposition,

[(1-\bar{z}_{i}z_{j})\langle\,e_{j},e_{i}\,\rangle_{L}]_{i,j=1}^{n}\geq 0.

Choose a polynomial
p
such that
p(\lambda_{i})=z_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,n
. Then

~p(\tilde{T})e_{i}=z_{i}e_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,n
, and so
\|p(\tilde{T})\|\leq 1
.
Choose
c=\begin{bmatrix}1\\
\dots\\
1\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Then

\langle\,(1-p(\tilde{T})^{*}p(\tilde{T}))c,c\,\rangle_{L}\geq 0,

that is,

{\mathcal{L}}([1-\bar{z}_{i}z_{j}]_{i,j=1}^{n}*cc^{*})\geq 0,\ \text{and so }\ {\mathcal{L}}([1-\bar{z}_{i}z_{j}]_{i,j=1}^{n})\geq 0,

where
*
denotes the Schur product of matrices.

Thus, for every real linear functional
{\mathcal{L}}
on
H_{n}
such that
{\mathcal{L}}\geq 0
on
{\mathcal{C}}
we have

{\mathcal{L}}([1-\bar{z}_{i}z_{j}]_{i,j=1}^{n})\geq 0.

Hence
[1-\bar{z}_{i}z_{j}]_{i,j=1}^{n}
belongs to
{\mathcal{C}}
.
∎

Theorem 5.9
.

Let
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n}\in R_{\delta}
be distinct and let
z_{1},\dots,z_{n}\in\mathbb{C}
.
If the DP Pick problem

\lambda_{j}\mapsto z_{j}\quad\text{for}\ j=1,\ldots,n

is solvable,
then there exists a rational function
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{dp}}
which satisfies the equations

\varphi(\lambda_{j})=z_{j}\qquad\text{for}\ j=1,\ldots,n,

(5.10)

and has a model
(\mathcal{M},u)
, with
u:R_{\delta}\to\mathcal{M}
holomorphic, so that

1-\overline{\varphi(\mu)}\varphi(\lambda)=\Big\langle\,\big(1-E(\mu)^{*}E(\lambda)\big)\ u(\lambda)\,,u(\mu)\,\Big\rangle_{\mathcal{M}}\ \text{for}\ \lambda,\mu\in R_{\delta},

(5.11)

where
\mathcal{M}
can be written as
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{M}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{M}_{2}
,
\dim\mathcal{M}\leq 2n
and
E:R_{\delta}\to\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{M})
is defined by the formula

E(\lambda)=\begin{bmatrix}\lambda&0\\
0&\frac{\delta}{\lambda}\end{bmatrix},\ \qquad\text{for}\ \lambda\in R_{\delta},

(5.12)

with respect to this orthogonal decomposition of
\mathcal{M}
.

Proof.

Suppose that

\lambda_{j}\mapsto z_{j}\ \text{for}\ j=1,\dots,n

is a solvable DP-Pick problem. By Theorem
3.19
, there exist positive semi-definite
n\times n
matrices
a=\begin{bmatrix}a_{ij}\end{bmatrix}
and
b=\begin{bmatrix}b_{ij}\end{bmatrix}
such that

1-\bar{z}_{i}z_{j}=(1-\bar{\lambda}_{i}\lambda_{j})a_{ij}+(1-\frac{\delta^{2}}{\overline{\lambda_{i}}\lambda_{j}})b_{ij}\quad\text{for}\ i,j=1,\dots,n.

(5.13)

Let the ranks of the matrices
a,b
be
r_{1},r_{2}
respectively, so that
r_{1}\leq n,r_{2}\leq n
. Then there exist vectors
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\in\mathbb{C}^{r_{1}},y_{1},\dots,y_{n}\in\mathbb{C}^{r_{2}}
such that

a_{ij}=\langle x_{j},x_{i}\rangle_{\mathbb{C}^{r_{1}}}\ \text{and}\ b_{ij}=\langle y_{j},y_{i}\rangle_{\mathbb{C}^{r_{2}}}\quad\text{for}\ i,j=1,\dots,n.

Substituting these relations into the equations (
5.13
) and re-arranging, we obtain the relations

1+\langle\lambda_{j}x_{j},\lambda_{i}x_{i}\rangle_{\mathbb{C}^{r_{1}}}+\langle\frac{\delta}{\lambda_{j}}y_{j},\frac{\delta}{\lambda_{i}}y_{i}\rangle_{\mathbb{C}^{r_{2}}}=\bar{z}_{i}z_{j}+\langle x_{j},x_{i}\rangle_{\mathbb{C}^{r_{1}}}+\langle y_{j},y_{i}\rangle_{\mathbb{C}^{r_{2}}}\ \text{for}\ i,j=1,\dots,n.

These equations can in turn be expressed by saying that the families of vectors

\begin{pmatrix}1\\
\lambda_{j}x_{j}\\
\frac{\delta}{\lambda_{j}}y_{j}\end{pmatrix}_{j=1,\dots,n}\ \text{and}\ \begin{pmatrix}z_{j}\\
x_{j}\\
y_{j}\end{pmatrix}_{j=1,\dots,n}

in
\mathbb{C}\oplus\mathbb{C}^{r_{1}}\oplus\mathbb{C}^{r_{2}}
have the same gramians. It follows from the “lurking isometry lemma”
[
6
, Lemma 2.18]
that there exists an isometry
L\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{C}\oplus\mathbb{C}^{r_{1}}\oplus\mathbb{C}^{r_{2}})
such that

\displaystyle\gamma+DE(\lambda_{j})\begin{pmatrix}x_{j}\\

for

Example 5.20
.

A solvable DP Pick data-set which is not a solvable Pick data-set on
\mathbb{D}
.

Let

2

P(\delta)=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{1-(\delta+\frac{1}{4})^{2}}{1-\frac{1}{4}}&\frac{1+(\delta+\frac{1}{4})^{2}}{1+\frac{1}{4}}\\
\\

P(\delta)_{11}=\frac{1-(\delta+\frac{1}{4})^{2}}{1-\frac{1}{4}}>0.

\delta=\frac{1}{4}

Example 5.22
.

A solvable DP-Pick problem on
R_{\delta}
which is not solvable as a Pick problem on
\mathbb{D}
.

Let

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1-|z_{1}|^{2}}{1-|\lambda_{1}|^{2}}\right)^{2}-\left(\frac{1+|z_{1}|^{2}}{1+|\lambda_{1}|^{2}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=D_{1}D_{2}

6.
Extremal DP Pick problems

We say that a DP Pick problem is
extremally solvable
if it is solvable and there does not exist
\varphi\in{\rm H}^{\infty}_{\rm dp}
with
\|\varphi\|_{\rm dp}<1
satisfying the equations

\varphi(\lambda_{j})=z_{j}\qquad\text{for}\ j=1,\ldots,n.

(6.1)

Remark 6.2
.

A DP Pick problem that is not extremally solvable cannot have a unique solution. For suppose
\lambda_{j}\mapsto z_{j},j=1,\dots,n,
is a solvable DP Pick problem that is not extremally solvable. That means that there is a function
\varphi:R_{\delta}\to\mathbb{C}
such that
\varphi(\lambda_{j})=z_{j}
for
j=1,\dots,n
and
\|\varphi\|_{\mathrm{dp}}<1
.
Consider the function
\psi(\lambda)=\varphi(\lambda)+\varepsilon\prod_{j=1}^{n}(\lambda-\lambda_{j})
, for
\lambda\in R_{\delta}
, for some positive
\varepsilon
. Then
\psi(\lambda_{j})=z_{j}
for
j=1,\dots,n
and

\|\psi\|_{\mathrm{dp}}\leq\|\varphi\|_{\mathrm{dp}}+\varepsilon\|\prod_{j=1}^{n}(\lambda-\lambda_{j})\|_{\mathrm{dp}}<1

for all small enough
\varepsilon
, and so there are infinitely many solutions to the interpolation problem
\lambda_{j}\mapsto z_{j}
for
j=1,\dots,n
having DP norm less than
1
.

Theorem 6.3
.

Let
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n}\in R_{\delta}
be distinct and let
z_{1},\dots,z_{n}\in\mathbb{C}
.
The following two statements are equivalent.

(i)

The DP Pick problem

\lambda_{j}\mapsto z_{j}\quad\text{for}\ j=1,\ldots,n

is extremally solvable.

(ii)

For all
g\in\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{dp}}(\lambda)
,

[(1-\bar{z}_{i}z_{j})g_{ij}]_{i,j=1}^{n}\geq 0

(6.4)

and there exists
\tilde{g}\in\mathcal{G}_{\rm dp}(\lambda)
such that

\rank\big[(1-\bar{z}_{i}z_{j})\tilde{g}_{ij}\big]_{i,j=1}^{n}<n.

(6.5)

Proof.

(i)
\implies
(ii).
Suppose that the DP Pick problem

\lambda_{j}\mapsto z_{j}\ \text{for}\ j=1,\dots,n

is extremally solvable. Since the problem is solvable, Theorem
5.2
implies that,
for all
g\in\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{dp}}(\lambda)
,

[(1-\bar{z}_{i}z_{j})g_{ij}]\geq 0.

(6.6)

Suppose, for a contradiction, that there is no
g\in\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{dp}}(\lambda)
such that

[(1-\bar{z}_{i}z_{j})g_{ij}]\ \text{is singular}.

(6.7)

Let
F:\mathbb{R}\times\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{dp}}^{\mathrm{norm}}(\lambda)\to\mathbb{R}
be defined by

\displaystyle F(r,[g_{ij}])

\displaystyle=\text{the minimum of the leading principal minors of}\begin{bmatrix}(1-r^{2}\bar{z}_{i}z_{j})g_{ij}\end{bmatrix}_{i,j=1}^{n}

\displaystyle=\min_{J=1,\dots,n}\det\begin{bmatrix}(1-r^{2}\bar{z}_{i}z_{j})g_{ij}\end{bmatrix}_{i,j=1}^{J}.

By standard linear algebra, for any positive definite matrix
g=[g_{ij}]
,
F(r,g)>0
if and only if

\begin{bmatrix}(1-r^{2}\bar{z}_{i}z_{j})g_{ij}\end{bmatrix}_{i,j=1}^{n}>0
.

F
is continuous and, by supposition,

[(1-\bar{z}_{i}z_{j})g_{ij}]>0

for all
g\in\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{dp}}(\lambda)
,
which implies that
F(1,g)>0
for all
g\in\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{dp}}^{\mathrm{norm}}(\lambda)
. Since, by Proposition
4.24
,
\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{dp}}^{\mathrm{norm}}(\lambda)
is compact,
F(1,\cdot)
attains its minimum on
\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{dp}}^{\mathrm{norm}}(\lambda)
, and so there exists
\kappa>0
such that
F(1,g)\geq\kappa
for all
g\in\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{dp}}^{\mathrm{norm}}(\lambda)
.

By the continuity of
F
and, again by the compactness of
\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{dp}}^{\mathrm{norm}}(\lambda)
, the family of functions
\{F(\cdot,g):g\in\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{dp}}^{\mathrm{norm}}(\lambda)\}
is equicontinuous on
\mathbb{R}
. Hence there exists
\delta>0
such that
F(r,g)>0
for all
g\in\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{dp}}^{\mathrm{norm}}(\lambda)
and all
r\in(1,\delta)
. Choose some
r\in(1,\delta)
. Then
F(r,g)>0
for all
g\in\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{dp}}^{\mathrm{norm}}(\lambda)
, and therefore
\begin{bmatrix}(1-r^{2}\bar{z}_{i}z_{j})g_{ij}\end{bmatrix}_{i,j=1}^{n}>0
for all
g\in\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{dp}}^{\mathrm{norm}}(\lambda)
. It follows from Proposition
4.24
that
\begin{bmatrix}(1-r^{2}\bar{z}_{i}z_{j})g_{ij}\end{bmatrix}_{i,j=1}^{n}>0
for all
g\in\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{dp}}(\lambda)
. Hence, by Theorem
5.2
, for the chosen
r\in(1,\delta)
, the DP Pick problem

\lambda_{j}\mapsto rz_{j}\ \text{for}\ j=1,\dots,n

is solvable, which is to say that there exists a function
\psi\in{\rm Hol}(R_{\delta})
such that
\|\psi\|_{\mathrm{dp}}\leq 1
and
\psi(\lambda_{j})=rz_{j}
for
j=1,\dots,n
. Thus the function
\varphi\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm def}}{{=}}\psi/r
satisfies
\varphi(\lambda_{j})=z_{j}
for
j=1,\dots,n
and
\|\varphi\|_{\mathrm{dp}}\leq 1/r<1
, contrary to hypothesis. Hence there is a
g\in\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{dp}}(\lambda)
such that

[(1-\bar{z}_{i}z_{j})g_{ij}]\ \text{is singular}.

(6.8)

We have shown that statements (
6.4
) and (
6.5
) hold, and so have established (i)
\Rightarrow
(ii) necessity in Theorem
6.3
.

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(i).
Suppose that (ii) holds, and so, for all
g\in\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{dp}}(\lambda)
,

[(1-\bar{z}_{i}z_{j})g_{ij}]_{i,j=1}^{n}\geq 0.

(6.9)

By Theorem
5.2
,
there exists
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{dp}}
such that

\varphi(\lambda_{j})=z_{j}\qquad\text{for}\ j=1,\ldots,n.

(6.10)

Suppose (i) does not hold, which means that the problem is non-extremally solvable, and hence there exists
\varphi
such that
\|\varphi\|_{\mathrm{dp}}=r<1
and
\varphi
satisfies
\varphi(\lambda_{j})=z_{j}
for
j=1,\dots,n
.
Thus for all
g\in\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{dp}}(\lambda)
,

[(r^{2}-\bar{z_{i}}z_{j})g_{ij}]\geq 0.

(6.11)

By assumption (ii), there exists
\tilde{g}\in\mathcal{G}_{\rm dp}(\lambda)
such that

\rank\big[(1-\bar{z}_{i}z_{j})\tilde{g}_{ij}\big]_{i,j=1}^{n}<n.

(6.12)

and hence
[(1-\bar{z}_{i}z_{j})\tilde{g}_{ij}]_{i,j=1}^{n}
has a non-zero null vector
v
.
Consider the relation

(1-\bar{z}_{i}z_{j})\tilde{g}_{ij}=(1-r^{2})\tilde{g}_{ij}+(r^{2}-\bar{z}_{i}z_{j})\tilde{g}_{ij}.

Since
g_{ij}>0
,
(1-r^{2})\tilde{g}_{ij}>0
and, by equation (
6.11
),
(r^{2}-\bar{z}_{i}z_{j})\tilde{g}_{ij}\geq 0
,
which is a contradiction.
∎

Theorem 6.13
.

Let
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n}\in R_{\delta}
be distinct and let
z_{1},\dots,z_{n}\in\mathbb{C}
.
If the DP Pick problem

\lambda_{j}\mapsto z_{j}\quad\text{for}\ j=1,\ldots,n,

is extremally solvable, then there exists a rational function
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{dp}}
which satisfies the equations

\varphi(\lambda_{j})=z_{j}\qquad\text{for}\ j=1,\ldots,n,

(6.14)

and has a model
(\mathcal{M},u)
as in equation (
5.11
), where
u:R_{\delta}\to\mathcal{M}
is a holomorphic function and
\dim\mathcal{M}\leq 2n
. Furthermore, there exists
T\in\mathcal{F}_{\rm dp}(\delta,\lambda)
such that

1=\|\varphi\|_{\rm dp}=\|\varphi(T)\|.

In particular,

1-\varphi(T)^{*}\varphi(T)=u(T)^{*}\big(1-E(T)^{*}E(T)\big)\ u(T),

where
\mathcal{M}
can be written as
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{M}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{M}_{2}
and
E:R_{\delta}\to\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{M})
is defined by the formula

E(\lambda)=\begin{bmatrix}\lambda&0\\
0&\frac{\delta}{\lambda}\end{bmatrix}\ \qquad\text{for}\ \lambda\in R_{\delta}

(6.15)

with respect to this orthogonal decomposition of
\mathcal{M}
.

Proof.

Since the DP Pick problem
\lambda_{j}\mapsto z_{j},\ \text{for}\ j=1,\ldots,n,
is solvable, by Theorem
5.9
, there exists
a rational function
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{dp}}
such that
\varphi(\lambda_{j})=z_{j},\text{for}\ j=1,\ldots,n
.

Let us now prove the existence of an operator
T
with the stated properties. By assumption, the DP-Pick problem
\lambda_{j}\mapsto z_{j},j=1,\dots,n
is
extremally
solvable.
By Theorem
6.3
, there exists
\tilde{g}\in\mathcal{G}_{\rm dp}(\lambda)
such that

\rank\big[(1-\bar{z}_{i}z_{j})\tilde{g}_{ij}\big]_{i,j=1}^{n}<n,

(6.16)

so that
\begin{bmatrix}(1-\bar{z}_{i}z_{j})\tilde{g}_{ij}\end{bmatrix}
is singular, and therefore has a non-zero null vector
\xi=[\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n}]^{T}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, which is to say that

\sum_{j=1}^{n}(1-\bar{z}_{i}z_{j})\tilde{g}_{ij}\xi_{j}=0\ \text{for}\ i=1,\dots,n.

(6.17)

Since
\tilde{g}\in\mathcal{G}_{\rm dp}(\lambda)
,
[\tilde{g}_{ij}]>0
, and so
[\tilde{g}_{ij}]

Weighted estimates for Schrödinger–Calderón–Zygmund operators with exponential decay
Estefanía Dalmasso 1, Gabriela R. Lezama 1, Marisa Toschi 2

Abstract
. In this work we obtain weighted boundedness results for singular integral operators with kernels exhibiting exponential decay. We also show that the classes of weights are characterized by a suitable maximal operator. Additionally, we study the boundedness of various operators associated with the generalized Schrödinger operator − Δ + μ -\Delta+\mu , where μ \mu is a nonnegative Radon measure in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} , for d ≥ 3 {d\geq 3} .

1.
Introduction and main result

In
[
3
]
, J. Bailey studied about harmonic analysis related to the Schrödinger operator
\mathcal{L}=-\Delta+V
, for
V
a locally integrable function in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
d\geq 3
.
Asking the question whether
it is possible to construct a Muckenhoupt-type class of weights adapted to the underlying differential
operator and whether this class can be characterized in terms of the corresponding
operators, he defined new classes of weights. These classes, named
S^{V}_{p,c}
and
H^{V,m}_{p,c}
, are comparable under certain dependence on the parameters
m
and
c
. The second one looks at the weights in the Euclidean distance, while the first one is adapted to another distance depending on the potential
V
. Both classes of weights are
strictly larger that the class
A_{p}^{V,\infty}
given in
[
7
]
(see §
2.4
).

Actually, since the classes can be described through a critical radius function associated to the operator
\mathcal{L}
, we will name the classes
S^{\rho}_{p,c}
,
H^{\rho,m}_{p,c}
and
A^{\rho}_{p}
, respectively. This idea together with the main tool used throughout the work, the decay of the fundamental solution of the operator
\mathcal{L}
, leads us to think about leaving particular examples aside, for the moment, and address a general theory to prove the boundedness of singular integral operators having kernels with exponential decay on weighted
L^{p}
spaces. Regarding the weights, we will deal with the class
H^{\rho}_{p,c}
since one can have a better understanding of the behavior of the weights with respect to the Euclidean distance. An analysis of the classes of weights is given in §
3.1
, including a simple example that shows the difference between them.

In the spirit of
[
4
, Theorem 5.1]
, we give an extrapolation theorem (see Theorem
4.1
) on weighted Lebesgue spaces for weights associated to the maximal operator defined in
[
3
]
, and prove boundedness results for certain collection of operators that we shall introduce in §
2.3
. In order to do so, we first complete the work done by J. Bailey and characterize the classes
H_{p,c}^{\rho}
as those that ensure the boundedness of the mentioned maximal function, which is an important result by itself (see §
3.2
).

The article will conclude with the presentation of certain examples of operators associated with the differential operator
\mathcal{L}_{\mu}=-\Delta+\mu
, along with their corresponding boundedness properties. Here,
\mu
is a nonnegative Radon measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, as considered in
[
12
]
and
[
3
]
. These operators include the Riesz transforms
R_{\mu}=\nabla(-\Delta+\mu)^{-\frac{1}{2}}
and its adjoint
{R^{*}_{\mu}=(-\Delta+\mu)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\nabla}
, Laplace transform type multipliers, as well as operators of the form
(-\Delta+V)^{-j/2}V^{j/2}
for
j=1,2
with
V
a nonnegative function belonging to the classical reverse Hölder class
RH_{q}
for
q>\frac{d}{2}.

Throughout this paper
C
and
c
will always denote positive constants than may change in each occurrence. Also, by
a\lesssim b
we mean there exists a positive constant
c
such that
a\leq cb
, and
a\gtrsim b
is the same as
b\lesssim a
. When both are valid, we simply write
a\sim b
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Critical radius function and Agmon distance

We introduce the notion of critical radius function as any function satisfying the definition given below.

Definition 2.1
.

A function
\rho:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to[0,\infty)
will be called a
critical radius function
if there exist constants
k_{0},C_{0}\geq 1
such that

(2.1)

C_{0}^{-1}\rho(x)\left(1+\frac{|x-y|}{\rho(x)}\right)^{-k_{0}}\leq\rho(y)\leq C_{0}\rho(x)\left(1+\frac{|x-y|}{\rho(x)}\right)^{\frac{k_{0}}{k_{0}+1}}

for
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Remark 2.2
.

It follows from the definition that
\rho(x)\sim\rho(y)
if
|x-y|\lesssim\rho(x)
.

A ball of the form
B(x,\rho(x))
, with
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, is called
critical
and a ball
B(x,r)
with
r\leq\rho(x)
will be called
sub-critical
. We denote by
\mathcal{B}_{\rho}
the family of all sub-critical balls, that is,

\mathcal{B}_{\rho}=\left\{B(x,r):x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\,r\leq\rho(x)\right\}.

It is well-known (see, for instance,
[
1
]
and
[
10
]
) that many phenomena involving the classical Schrödinger operator
L_{V}=-\Delta+V
, with a potential
V
, can be better described in terms of the Agmon distance, which is comparable to the Euclidean distance locally, but is a Riemannian-like distance obtained by deforming the Euclidean one with respect to a quadratic form involving the potential.

In our setting, a notion of Agmon distance can also be given as:

d_{\rho}(x,y):=\inf_{\gamma}\int_{0}^{1}\rho(\gamma(t))^{-1}|\gamma^{\prime}(t)|dt,

where the infimum is taken over every absolutely continuous function
\gamma:[0,1]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
\gamma(0)=x
and
\gamma(1)=y
.

The following lemma establishes the aforementioned local comparison between the Agmon distance and the Euclidean distance.

Lemma 2.3

(
[
3
, Lemma 2.2]
)
.

Let
\rho:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
be a critical radius function. If
|x-y|\leq 2\rho(x)
, there exists
D_{0}>1
such that

(2.2)

\frac{1}{D_{0}}\frac{|x-y|}{\rho(x)}\leq d_{\rho}(x,y)\leq D_{0}\frac{|x-y|}{\rho(x)}.

The Agmon distance can also be compared with the quantity
1+\frac{|x-y|}{\rho(x)}
as stated below.

Lemma 2.4

(
[
3
, Lemma 2.3]
,
[
12
, (3.19)]
)
.

Let
\rho:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
be a critical radius function. Then,

(2.3)

d_{\rho}(x,y)\leq C_{0}\left(1+\frac{|x-y|}{\rho(x)}\right)^{k_{0}+1}

for every
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where
C_{0}
and
k_{0}
are the constants appearing in (
2.1
).

Moreover, when
|x-y|\geq\rho(x)
, there exists a constant
D_{1}>1
such that

(2.4)

d_{\rho}(x,y)\geq\frac{1}{D_{1}}\left(1+\frac{|x-y|}{\rho(x)}\right)^{\frac{1}{k_{0}+1}}.

2.2.
Spaces of functions

Given a weight
w
, i.e., a nonnegative and locally integrable function defined on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, for
1\leq p<\infty
we say that a measurable function
f
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
belongs to
L^{p}(w)
iff
\|f\|_{L^{p}(w)}:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(x)|^{p}w(x)dx\right)^{1/p}<\infty
.
For
w\equiv 1
we simply write
L^{p}(w)=L^{p}
.

When
p=\infty
,
f\in L^{\infty}(w)
iff
fw\in L^{\infty}
and we write
\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(w)}=\|fw\|_{\infty}
where this quantity denotes, as usual, the essential supremum of
fw
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Given a critical radius function
\rho
and a weight
w
, we say that a locally integrable function
f
belongs to
\textup{BMO}_{\rho}(w)
if it satisfies

(2.5)

\frac{\|\chi_{B}w\|_{\infty}}{|B|}\int_{B}|f-f_{B}|\leq C,\quad\text{for every ball }B=B(x,r)\text{ with }r<\rho(x),

and

(2.6)

\frac{\|\chi_{B}w\|_{\infty}}{|B|}\int_{B}|f|\leq C,\quad\qquad\text{for every ball }B=B(x,r)\text{ with }r=\rho(x),

where
f_{B}
stands for the average of
f
over the ball
B
. As usual, the norm
\|f\|_{\textup{BMO}_{\rho}(w)}
is defined as the maximum of the infima constants appearing in (
2.5
) and (
2.6
).

It is a classical fact that

\sup_{B}\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}|f-f_{B}|\sim\sup_{B}\inf_{a\in\mathbb{R}}\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}|f-a|.

This allows us to verify (
2.5
) for some constant
a\in\mathbb{R}
, not necessarily the average
f_{B}
.

Moreover, as it was proved in
[
4
]
, the space
\textup{BMO}_{\rho}(w)
can be characterized in terms of a suitable sharp maximal function.

Lemma 2.5

(
[
4
, Lemma 2]
)
.

For any weight
w
and any
f\in L^{1}_{\textup{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
\|f\|_{\textup{BMO}_{\rho}(w)}\sim\|M_{\textup{loc}}^{\sharp}(f)w\|_{\infty}

where

M_{\textup{loc}}^{\sharp}(f)(x):=\sup_{x\in B\in\mathcal{B}_{\rho}}\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}|f-f_{B}|+\sup_{x\in B=B(y,\rho(y))}\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}|f|.

2.3.
Schrödinger–Calderón–Zygmund operators with exponential decay

In the following results we consider an operator
T
with kernel
K
, understood in the principal value sense, that satisfies certain Calderón–Zygmund or Hörmander-type conditions, having exponential decay. The terminology used here follows
[
6
]
(see also
[
11
]
), although their operators, characterized by polynomial decay, were previously considered in
[
4
]
without being explicitly named.

Definition 2.6
.

For
1<s<\infty
and
0<\delta\leq 1
we say that
T
is an
exponential Schrödinger–Calderón–Zygmund operator of
(s,\delta)
type if

(i)

T
is bounded from
L^{s^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
to
L^{s^{\prime},\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
;

(ii)

T
has an associated kernel
K
verifying the following conditions:

(a)

there exist constants
c,C>0
and
m>0
such that

(2.7)

\left(\frac{1}{R^{d}}\int_{R<|x_{0}-y|\leq 2R}|K(x,y)|^{s}dy\right)^{1/s}\leq\frac{C}{R^{d}}\exp\left(-c\left(1+\frac{R}{\rho(x)}\right)^{m}\right),

whenever
|x-x_{0}|<R/2
;

(b)

there exist a constant
C>0
such that

(2.8)

\left(\frac{1}{R^{d}}\int_{R<|x_{0}-y|\leq 2R}|K(x,y)-K(x_{0},y)|^{s}dy\right)^{1/s}\leq\frac{C}{R^{d}}\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{\delta}

for every
|x-x_{0}|<r\leq\rho(x_{0})
and
r<R/2
.

For the case
s=\infty
, we consider pointwise estimates instead, for which we introduce the following definition.

Definition 2.7
.

Given
0<\delta\leq 1
we say that
T
is a an
exponential Schrödinger–Calderón–Zygmund operator of
(\infty,\delta)
type if

(i)

T
is bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for every
p>1
;

(ii)

T
has an associated kernel
K
verifying the following conditions:

(a)

there exist constants
c,C>0
and
m>0
such that

(2.9)

|K(x,y)|\leq\frac{C}{|x-y|^{d}}\,\exp\left(-c\left(1+\frac{|x-y|}{\rho(x)}\right)^{m}\right),\qquad\text{for every }x\neq y;

(b)

there exist a constant
C>0
such that

(2.10)

|K(x,y)-K(x_{0},y)|\leq\frac{C}{|x-y|^{d}}\left(\frac{|x-x_{0}|}{|x-y|}\right)^{\delta},

for every
|x-y|>2|x-x_{0}|
.

2.4.
Weights associated to a critical radius function

The class
A^{\rho}_{p}
introduced in
[
7
]
properly contain the classical Muckenhoupt weights. For
{1<p<\infty}
, they are denoted by
A^{\rho}_{p}=\bigcup_{\theta\geq 0}A^{\rho,\theta}_{p}
,
where
w\in A^{\rho,\theta}_{p}
means that

\displaystyle\left(\int_{B}w\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\int_{B}w^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\leq C|B|\left(1+\frac{r}{\rho(x)}\right)^{\theta}

for every ball
B=B(x,r)
with center
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and radius
r>0
. Similarly, when
p=1
, we denote by
A^{\rho}_{1}=\displaystyle\bigcup_{\theta\geq 0}A^{\rho,\theta}_{1}
, where
A_{1}^{\rho,\theta}
collects weights
w
such that

\int_{B}w\leq C|B|\left(1+\frac{r}{\rho(x)}\right)^{\theta}\inf_{B}w,

for every ball
B=B(x,r)
.

Let us also consider
A^{\rho,\textup{loc}}_{p}
as defined in
[
5
]
. That is, a weight
w
belongs to the class
A^{\rho,\textup{loc}}_{p}
for
1<p<\infty
if there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\left(\int_{B}w\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\int_{B}w^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\leq C|B|

for balls
B\in\mathcal{B}_{\rho}
, and for
p=1
if

\int_{B}w\leq C|B|\inf_{B}w,

holds for every
B\in\mathcal{B}_{\rho}
.

The weights involved in our main result where given by J. Bailey in
[
3
]
. For
1<p<\infty
and
c\geq 0
we denote by
H^{\rho}_{p,c}:=\bigcup_{m\geq 0}H^{\rho,m}_{p,c},
where
H^{\rho,m}_{p,c}
is the class of weights
w
for which there exists a constant
C
such that

\displaystyle\left(\int_{B}w\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\int_{B}w^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\leq C|B|\exp\left(c\left(1+\frac{r}{\rho(x)}\right)^{m}\right),

for each ball
B=B(x,r)
.

When
p=1
, for
c\geq 0
we denote by
H^{\rho}_{1,c}:=\bigcup_{m\geq 0}H^{\rho,m}_{1,c},
where
w\in H^{\rho,m}_{1,c}
means that

\displaystyle\int_{B}w\leq C|B|\exp\left(c\left(1+\frac{r}{\rho(x)}\right)^{m}\right)\inf_{B}w,

for each ball
B=B(x,r)
.

Clearly, for any
1\leq p<\infty
,
H^{\rho,m_{1}}_{p,c_{1}}\subseteq H^{\rho,m_{2}}_{p,c_{2}}
whenever
c_{1}\leq c_{2}
and
m_{1}\leq m_{2}
.

Notice that when
c=0
, we recover the Muckenhoupt
A_{p}
classes. Moreover, it is easy to see that the classes
H^{\rho}_{p,c}
share several key properties with the
A_{p}
weights, as we establish in Lemma
3.2
.

It is also straightforward to verify that the weights in
H^{\rho,m}_{p,c}
meet the following doubling condition.

Definition 2.8
.

Let
\kappa\geq 1
and
c>0
. We denote by
D^{\rho}_{\kappa,c}:=\bigcup_{m\geq 0}D_{\kappa,c}^{\rho,m}
, where
D_{\kappa,c}^{\rho,m}
is the class of weights
w
such that there exists a constant
C
for which

\displaystyle w(B(x,R))\leq C\left(\frac{R}{r}\right)^{d\kappa}\exp{\left(c\left(1+\frac{R}{\rho(x)}\right)^{m}\right)}w(B(x,r))

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
r<R
.

Remark 2.9
.

If
w\in H^{\rho,m}_{p,c}
, by Hölder’s inequality it easy to check that
w\in D^{\rho,m}_{p,cp}
.

We will also deal with the reverse Hölder classes of weights. In
[
7
, Lemma 5]
the authors prove that a weight
w\in A^{\rho}_{p}
,
1\leq p<\infty
, verifies the following reverse Hölder inequality

(2.11)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}w^{\eta}\right)^{\frac{1}{\eta}}\leq C\left(\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}w\right)\left(1+\frac{r}{\rho(x)}\right)^{\beta}

for every ball
B=B(x,r)
and some constants
\beta,C>0
and
\eta>1
.

Reverse Hölder classes adapted to the context of weights with exponential growth will also be needed.

Definition 2.10
.

Let
\eta>1
and
c>0
. We denote by
RH_{\eta,c}^{\rho}:=\bigcup_{m\geq 0}RH_{\eta,c}^{\rho,m}
, where
RH_{\eta,c}^{\rho,m}
is defined as those weights
w
such that

(2.12)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}w^{\eta}\right)^{\frac{1}{\eta}}\leq C\left(\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}w\right)\exp\left(c\left(1+\frac{r}{\rho(x)}\right)^{m}\right)

for every ball
B=B(x,r)
and some constant
C
independent of
B
.

Clearly, (
2.11
) implies (
2.12
).

Remark 2.11
.

Note that when taking
m=0
,
RH^{\rho,0}_{p,c}
and
D_{\kappa,c}^{\rho,0}
are the classical reverse Hölder classes and doubling classes, respectively.

3.
Auxiliary results

3.1.
Some properties for
H_{p,c}^{\rho}

The importance of the following result lies in the fact that the class of weights involved in Theorem
4.1
generalizes those presented in
[
7
]
. Additionally, the second inclusion enables the application of established tools for
A^{\rho,\textup{loc}}_{p}
. While this inclusion was originally obtained in
[
3
]
, we provide a direct proof that avoids the need for intermediate weight classes and eliminates certain parameter restrictions.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
1<p<\infty
. For any
c>0
we have

(3.1)

\displaystyle A^{\rho}_{p}\subsetneq H_{p,c}^{\rho}\subseteq A^{\rho,\textup{loc}}_{p}.

Proof.

The first inclusion can be found in
[
3
, Proposition 3.2]
. For the second one, we consider
w\in H_{p,c}^{\rho,m}
for some
m\geq 0
and a ball
B=B(x,r)\in\mathcal{B}_{\rho}
. We get

\left(\int_{B}w\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\int_{B}w^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\leq C|B|\exp\left(c\left(1+\frac{r}{\rho(x)}\right)^{m}\right)\leq C|B|\exp\left(c2^{m}\right)\leq C|B|,

where the constant is independent of
B
. Hence,
w\in A^{\rho,\textup{loc}}_{p}
.

For the critical radius function
\rho(x)=\min\left\{1,\frac{1}{|x|}\right\}
(which arises when dealing with the harmonic oscillator whose potential is
V(x)=|x|^{2}
), we shall see that
w(x)=e^{|x|}
belongs to
H_{p,c}^{\rho}
for some
c>0
, but
w\notin A_{p}^{\rho}
.

Let
B=B(y,r)
be a ball for
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
r>0
. Then,

\left(\int_{B}e^{|x|}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\lesssim|B|^{\frac{1}{p}}e^{\frac{|y|+r}{p}}

and

\left(\int_{B}e^{-\frac{|x|}{p-1}}dx\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\lesssim|B|^{1-\frac{1}{p}}e^{-\frac{|y|-r}{p}},

imply that

\left(\int_{B}e^{|x|}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\int_{B}e^{-\frac{|x|}{p-1}}dx\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\lesssim|B|e^{\frac{2}{p}r}.

Now, notice that
r\leq\frac{r}{\rho(y)}
for every
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
r>0
, since
r=\frac{r}{\rho(y)}
when
|y|\leq 1
and
r\leq r|y|=\frac{r}{\rho(y)}
when
|y|>1
. Thus,

\left(\int_{B}e^{|x|}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\int_{B}e^{-\frac{|x|}{p-1}}dx\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\lesssim|B|\exp\left(\frac{2}{p}\left(1+\frac{r}{\rho(y)}\right)\right)

meaning that
w\in H_{p,2/p}^{\rho}
.

In order to see that
w\notin A_{p}^{\rho}
, we will follow the ideas given in
[
2
, p. 367]
. Let us consider the balls
B_{\ell}=B(0,\ell)
for
\ell>1
. On one hand, we can obtain

\left(\int_{B_{2\ell}}e^{|x|}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\geq\left(\int_{B_{2\ell}\setminus B_{\ell}}e^{|x|}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}=\left(\int_{\ell}^{2\ell}e^{t}t^{d-1}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\geq\left(e^{2\ell}-e^{\ell}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

On the other hand,

\left(\int_{B_{2\ell}}e^{-\frac{|x|}{p-1}}dx\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\geq\left(\int_{B_{2\ell}\setminus B_{\ell}}e^{-\frac{|x|}{p-1}}dx\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\gtrsim\left(e^{-\frac{\ell}{p-1}}-e^{-\frac{2\ell}{p-1}}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\gtrsim e^{-\frac{\ell}{p}}.

Combining these inequalities, we obtain

\left(\int_{B_{2\ell}}e^{|x|}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\int_{B_{2\ell}}e^{-\frac{|x|}{p-1}}dx\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\gtrsim(e^{\ell}-1)^{\frac{1}{p}},

and since this estimate holds for any
\ell>1
, the left-hand-side cannot be bounded by any polynomial on
\ell
. That is,
w\notin A_{p}^{\rho,\theta}
for any
\theta\geq 0
, so
w\notin A_{p}^{\rho}
as we claimed.
∎

As it was observed in
[
5
]
, the
A^{\rho}_{p}
classes preserve the favorable properties of the
A_{p}
weights. Similarly, as the reader may verify, the
H^{\rho}_{p,c}
classes also exhibit these properties.

Lemma 3.2
.

The following properties hold:

(i)

H^{\rho,m}_{p,c}\subset H^{\rho,m}_{q,c}
for every
1\leq p\leq q<\infty
and
m,c\geq 0
.

(ii)

If
w\in H^{\rho,m}_{p,c}
for some
1<p<\infty
and
m\geq 0
, then
\sigma:=w^{1-p^{\prime}}\in H^{\rho,m}_{p^{\prime},c}
.

(iii)

If
w_{1}\in H^{\rho,m_{1}}_{1,c_{1}}
and
w_{2}\in H^{\rho,m_{2}}_{1,c_{2}}
for some
m_{1},m_{2},c_{1},c_{2}\geq 0
, then for every
1\leq p<\infty
there exist
m,c\geq 0
such that
w_{1}w_{2}^{1-p}\in H^{\rho,m}_{p,c}
.

Next lemma states that
H^{\rho}_{p,c}
weights satisfy the reverse Hölder condition given in Definition
2.10
above. The proof follows the lines of
[
7
, Lemma 5]
, with some modifications that we will provide.

Lemma 3.3
.

Given
1\leq p<\infty
and
c>0
, for any
w\in H^{\rho}_{p,c}
, there exist constants
\eta>1
and
c^{*}>0
such that
w\in RH^{\rho}_{\eta,c^{*}}.

Proof.

Since
w\in H^{\rho,m}_{p,c}
for some
m\geq 0
, by Proposition
3.1
we get
w\in A^{\rho,\textup{loc}}_{p}
. We follow the proof given in
[
7
]
, where an important tool is their Proposition 2. It guarantees the existence of a sequence of points
\left\{x_{j}\right\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
that satisfies the following two properties:

•

\mathbb{R}^{d}=\bigcup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}B(x_{j},\rho(x_{j}))
;

•

there exist constants
C,N_{1}>0
such that for every
\sigma\geq 1
,
\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\chi_{B(x_{j},\sigma\rho(x_{j}))}\leq C\sigma^{N_{1}}
.

When estimating the integral of
w^{\eta}
on
B
, for
\eta>1
, the same arguments given in the proof of
[
7
, Lemma 5]
can be applied to get that

\left(\int_{B}w^{\eta}\right)^{\frac{1}{\eta}}\lesssim w(c(x,r)B)\rho(x)^{-\frac{d(\eta-1)}{\eta}}\left(1+\frac{r}{\rho(x)}\right)^{\frac{dk_{0}(\eta-1)}{\eta}},

where
c(x,r)=4C_{0}^{2}\left(1+\frac{r}{\rho(x)}\right)^{k_{0}/(k_{0}+1)}
.

In the context of our class of weights, we use Remark
2.9
to obtain

w(c(x,r)B)\lesssim\exp\left(\tilde{c}\left(1+\frac{r}{\rho(x)}\right)^{\tilde{m}}\right)w(B),

where
\tilde{c}=4cC_{0}^{2}p
and
\tilde{m}=m\frac{k_{0}}{k_{0}+1}+m
. Therefore we have (
2.12
) as follows

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}w^{\eta}\right)^{\frac{1}{\eta}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}w\right)\exp\left(c^{*}\left(1+\frac{r}{\rho(x)}\right)^{m^{*}}\right)

where
c^{*}=\tilde{c}+1
and
m^{*}=\max\left\{\tilde{m},\frac{d(k_{0}+1)(\eta-1)}{\eta}\right\}
.
∎

We also have the following property for
RH^{\rho}_{\eta,c}
families of weights, which gives us an openness property. It is important to clarify that in the hypotheses of the following lemma the parameters
c
and
c_{1}
are not necessarily the same, which broadens the result.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
w\in H^{\rho}_{p,c}\cap RH^{\rho}_{\eta,c_{1}}
for some
\eta>1
,
c,c_{1}>0
and
p>1
. There exist
\beta>1
and
\widetilde{c}>0
such that
w\in RH^{\rho}_{\eta\beta,\widetilde{c}}
.

Proof.

Let
w\in H^{\rho}_{p,c}\cap RH_{\eta,c_{1}}^{\rho}
. Then, there exist
m,m_{1}>0
such that
w\in H^{\rho,m}_{p,c}\cap RH_{\eta,c_{1}}^{\rho,m_{1}}
. Using both conditions we get
w^{\eta}\in H^{\rho}_{q,c_{2}}
with
c_{2}>0
and
q=\eta(p-1)+1
. Indeed,

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}w^{\eta}\right)^{\frac{1}{\eta(p-1)+1}}\left(\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}w^{-\frac{\eta}{\eta(p-1)}}\right)^{\frac{\eta(p-1)}{\eta(p-1)+1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\exp\left(c_{1}\left(1+\frac{r}{\rho(x)}\right)^{m_{1}}\right)\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}w\right)^{\frac{\eta}{\eta(p-1)+1}}\left(\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}w^{-\frac{1}{(p-1)}}\right)^{\frac{\eta(p-1)}{\eta(p-1)+1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\exp\left(c_{1}\frac{\eta}{\eta(p-1)+1}\left(1+\frac{r}{\rho(x)}\right)^{m_{1}}\right)\left(\left(\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}w\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}w^{-\frac{1}{(p-1)}}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\right)^{\frac{\eta p}{\eta(p-1)+1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\exp\left(c_{1}\frac{\eta}{\eta(p-1)+1}\left(1+\frac{r}{\rho(x)}\right)^{m_{1}}\right)\exp\left(c\frac{\eta p}{\eta(p-1)+1}\left(1+\frac{r}{\rho(x)}\right)^{m}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\exp\left(c_{2}\left(1+\frac{r}{\rho(x)}\right)^{m_{2}}\right),

with
c_{2}=\frac{(c_{1}+cp)\eta}{\eta(p-1)+1}>0
and
m_{2}=\max\{m,m_{1}\}
.

Then, from Lemma
3.3
there exists
\beta>1
and
c^{\prime}>0
such that
w^{\eta}\in RH^{\rho}_{\beta,c^{\prime}}
. Using again that
w\in RH^{\rho}_{\eta,c_{1}}
, we get
w\in RH^{\rho}_{{\eta\beta},\widetilde{c}}
with
\widetilde{c}=c_{1}+\frac{c}{\eta}>0
.
∎

3.2.
Maximal operators associated with a critical radius function

For
f\in L^{1}_{\textup{loc}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
, we recall the definition of the local maximal operator over sub-critical balls, studied in
[
7
]
and
[
5
]
:

(3.2)

M^{\textup{loc}}_{\rho}f(x)=\sup_{x\in B\in\mathcal{B}_{\rho}}\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}|f(y)|dy.

As it was proved in
[
7
, Theorem 1]
, for any
1<p<\infty
,
w\in A^{\rho,\textup{loc}}_{p}
if and only if
M^{\textup{loc}}_{\rho}
is bounded on
L^{p}(w)
.

In a similar way, the classes of weights
H_{p,c}^{\rho}
are related with the boundedness of certain maximal functions that capture the exponential growth. This operators were already considered by J. Bailey in
[
3
]
, but we define them below for the sake of completeness.

Definition 3.5
.

Let
c,m\geq 0
and let
\rho
be a critical radius function. For
f\in L^{1}_{\textup{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
we consider the maximal operator given by

(3.3)

\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}^{m}_{\rho,c}f(x):=\sup_{B(x^{\prime},r)\ni x}\frac{1}{\exp{\left(c\left(1+\frac{r}{\rho(x^{\prime})}\right)^{m}\right)}}\fint_{B(x^{\prime},r)}|f(y)|dy,

and its centered version

(3.4)

\widetilde{M}^{m}_{\rho,c}f(x):=\sup_{r>0}\frac{1}{\exp{\left(c\left(1+\frac{r}{\rho(x)}\right)^{m}\right)}}\fint_{B(x,r)}|f(y)|dy.

For any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
f\in L^{1}_{\textup{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, the inequality
\widetilde{M}^{m}_{\rho,c}f\lesssim\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}^{m}_{\rho,c}f
trivially holds. The following proposition states that the reverse inequality also holds under some restrictions on the parameters.

Proposition 3.6

(
[
3
, Proposition 3.5]
)
.

Let
\rho
be a critical radius function with constants
C_{0}
and
k_{0}
as in Definition
2.1
. Given
c_{1},c_{2},m_{1},m_{2}>0
with
m_{1}>(k_{0}+1)m_{2}
and
c_{2}\geq c_{1}(2C_{0})^{m_{2}}
we have

(3.5)

\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{\rho,c_{1}}^{m_{1}}f\lesssim\widetilde{M}_{\rho,c_{2}}^{m_{2}}f

for every
f\in L^{1}_{\textup{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

The theorem below is significant in its own right, since it allows us to characterize the weight classes
H^{\rho}_{p,c}
as those that ensure the boundedness of the maximal operator
\widetilde{M}^{m}_{\rho,c}
. This result will be useful to give an extrapolation tool in §
4
.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
1<p<\infty
. Then,
w\in H^{\rho}_{p,c}
if and only if there exists
m\geq 0
such that
\widetilde{M}^{m}_{\rho,c}
is bounded on
L^{p}(w)
.

Proof.

The necessity of the class
H^{\rho}_{p,c}
was already stated in
[
3
, Proposition 3.6]
using (
3.5
). We shall prove the sufficiency.

Let
w\in H^{\rho}_{p,c}
. Then,
w\in H^{\rho,m_{1}}_{p,c}
for some
m_{1}\geq 0
, i.e., there exists
C>0
such that

(3.6)

\left(\int_{B}w\right)^{1/p}\left(\int_{B}w^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\leq C|B|\exp\left(c\left(1+\frac{r}{\rho(x)}\right)^{m_{1}}\right)

holds for every ball
B=B(x,r)
.

Let
m\geq 0
to be chosen later. We can split the maximal operator as follows,

(3.7)

\widetilde{M}^{m}_{\rho,c}f(x)\leq\widetilde{M}^{m,(1)}_{\rho,c}f(x)+\widetilde{M}^{m,(2)}_{\rho,c}f(x)

where

\displaystyle\widetilde{M}^{m,(1)}_{\rho,c}f(x)=\sup_{r\leq\rho(x)}\frac{1}{\exp\left(c\left(1+\frac{r}{\rho(x)}\right)^{m}\right)}\fint_{B(x,r)}|f(y)|dy

and

\widetilde{M}^{m,(2)}_{\rho,c}f(x)=\sup_{r>\rho(x)}\frac{1}{\exp\left(c\left(1+\frac{r}{\rho(x)}\right)^{m}\right)}\fint_{B(x,r)}|f(y)|dy

By Proposition
3.1
,
H^{\rho}_{p,c}\subset A^{\rho,\textup{loc}}_{p}
, and since
\widetilde{M}^{m,(1)}_{\rho,c}f(x)\leq M^{\textup{loc}}_{\rho}f(x)
, we deduce that
\widetilde{M}^{m,(1)}_{\rho,c}
is bounded on
L^{p}(w)
for any
m\geq 0
and
w\in H^{\rho}_{p,c}
.

We will now deal with
\widetilde{M}^{m,(2)}_{\rho,c}f
. Let
\left\{Q_{k}\right\}_{k\geq 1}
be the covering given in
[
7
, Proposition 2]
of critical balls
Q_{k}=B(x_{k},\rho(x_{k}))
. We fix
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and set
R_{j}=\left\{r:2^{j-1}\rho(x)<r\leq 2^{j}\rho(x)\right\}
. Then, for any
Q_{k}\ni x
,
\rho(x_{k})\sim\rho(x)
so we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{M}^{m,(2)}_{\rho,c}f(x)

\displaystyle=\sup_{r>\rho(x)}\frac{1}{\exp\left(c\left(1+\frac{r}{\rho(x)}\right)^{m}\right)}\fint_{B(x,r)}|f(y)|dy

\displaystyle=\sup_{j\geq 1}\sup_{r\in R_{j}}\frac{1}{\exp\left(c\left(1+\frac{r}{\rho(x)}\right)^{m}\right)c_{d}r^{d}}\int_{B(x,r)}|f(y)|dy

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{j\geq 1}\frac{1}{\exp\left(c\left(1+2^{j-1}\right)^{m}\right)}\frac{1}{\left(2^{j-1}\rho(x)\right)^{d}}\int_{B(x,2^{j}\rho(x))}|f(y)|dy

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{j\geq 1}\frac{2^{-jd}}{\exp\left(c\left(1+2^{j-1}\right)^{m}\right)\rho(x_{k})^{d}}\int_{c_{j}Q_{k}}|f(y)|dy

where
c_{j}=2^{j}\left(C_{0}2^{k_{0}}+1\right)
can be obtained from (
2.1
).

Finally, from Hölder’s inequality and (
3.6
) we get

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\widetilde{M}^{m,(2)}_{\rho,c}f|^{p}w

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k\geq 1}\int_{Q_{k}}|\widetilde{M}^{m,(2)}_{\rho,c}f|^{p}w

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k\geq 1}\sup_{j\geq 1}\frac{2^{-jdp}}{\exp\left(cp\left(1+2^{j-1}\right)^{m}\right)\rho(x_{k})^{dp}}\left(\int_{c_{j}Q_{k}}|f|\right)^{p}\left(\int_{Q_{k}}w\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k\geq 1}\sup_{j\geq 1}\frac{2^{-jdp}}{\exp\left(c\left(1+2^{j-1}\right)^{m}\right)\rho(x_{k})^{dp}}\left(\int_{c_{j}Q_{k}}|f|^{p}w\right)\left(\int_{c_{j}Q_{k}}w^{\frac{-1}{p-1}}\right)^{p/p^{\prime}}\int_{c_{j}Q_{k}}w

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k\geq 1}\sup_{j\geq 1}\frac{2^{-jdp}\left(c_{j}\rho(x_{k})\right)^{dp}\exp\left(cp\left(1+\frac{c_{j}\rho(x_{k})}{\rho(x_{k})}\right)^{m_{1}}\right)}{\exp\left(cp\left(1+2^{j-1}\right)^{m}\right)\rho(x_{k})^{d}}\int_{c_{j}Q_{k}}|f|^{p}w

\displaystyle\leq\left(C_{0}2^{k_{0}}+1\right)^{dp}\sum_{k\geq 1}\sup_{j\geq 1}\frac{\exp\left(cp\left(1+2^{j}(C_{0}2^{k_{0}}+1)\right)^{m_{1}}\right)}{\exp\left(cp\left(1+2^{j-1}\right)^{m}\right)}\int_{c_{j}Q_{k}}|f|^{p}w

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j\geq 1}\exp\left(\tilde{c}\left(1+2^{j}\right)^{m_{1}-m}\right)\sum_{k\geq 1}\int_{c_{j}Q_{k}}|f|^{p}w.

Since
\sum_{k}\chi_{c_{j}Q_{k}}\leq Cc_{j}^{N_{1}}\sim C2^{jN_{1}}
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\widetilde{M}^{m,(2)}_{\rho,c}f|^{p}w\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f|^{p}w\sum_{j\geq 1}\exp\left(\tilde{c}\left(1+2^{j}\right)^{m_{1}-m}\right)2^{jN_{1}}\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f|^{p}w,

provided that
m>m_{1}+N_{1}
is chosen.
∎

4.
Boundedness results for exponential Schrödinger–Calderón–Zygmund operators

We first present useful tools for proving our main theorem. While many of them follow the approach outlined in
[
5
]
, additional considerations may be necessary in this new context. For this reason, we will provide details whenever clarification is required.

In order to get the boundedness on
L^{p}(w)
for exponential Schrödinger–Calderón–Zygmund operators, we state an extrapolation result from the extreme point
p_{0}=\infty
. Since many of the operators arising on the Schrödinger context are not bounded on
L^{\infty}
, the following result will allow us to include them by establishing their continuity from
L^{\infty}(w)
to
\textup{BMO}_{\rho}(w)
, for certain classes of weights.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
q>0
and let
T
be a bounded operator from
L^{\infty}(w)
into
BMO_{\rho}(w)
for any weight
w
such that
w^{-q}\in H_{1,c}^{\rho}
, with constant depending on
w
through the constant of the condition
w^{-q}\in H_{1,c}^{\rho,\theta}
. Then
T
is bounded on
L^{p}(w)
for
q<p<\infty
and every
w\in H_{p/q,c}^{\rho}
.

Proof.

First we observe that from Lemma
2.5
and the hypothesis on
T
, for every
f\in L^{\infty}(w)
we have

(4.1)

\|M_{\textup{loc}}^{\sharp}(Tf)w\|_{\infty}\leq\|Tf\|_{\textup{BMO}_{\rho}(w)}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(w)}=C\|fw\|_{\infty}

whenever
w
is a weight such that
w^{-q}\in H_{1,c}^{\rho}
.

Now, we need to state that
H_{p,c}^{\rho}
is in the context of
[
4
, Corollary 4]
, that is, it is a class of weights associated to the boundedness of a certain family of operators and they verify similar properties to those of the Muckenhoupt weights. Indeed, both conditions hold by Lemma
3.2
and Theorem
3.7
with the maximal operators
\widetilde{M}_{\rho,c}^{m}
. Hence, we can obtain the boundedness of
M_{\textup{loc}}^{\sharp}T
in
L^{p}(w)
, whenever
q<p<\infty
and
w\in H_{p/q,c}^{\rho}
.
Finally, Proposition
3.1
allows us to apply
[
4
, Corollary 5]
to finish the proof.
∎

The next two propositions show that, for certain parameters, the exponential Schrödinger–Calderón–Zygmund operators of
(s,\delta)
type for
1<s\leq\infty
and
0<\delta\leq 1
, fall under the hypothesis of the theorem above.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
T
be an exponential Schrödinger–Calderón–Zygmund operator of
(\infty,\delta)
type as given in Definition
2.7
with constants
c_{1}
and
m_{1}
in (
2.9
). Then,
T
is bounded from
L^{\infty}(w)
into
BMO_{\rho}(w)
for every weight
w
such that
w^{-1}\in H_{1,c}^{\rho,m_{1}}
with
c<c_{1}
.

Proof.

Let
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
r\leq\rho(x_{0})
and
B=B(x_{0},r)
. We consider

(4.2)

f=f\chi_{2B}+f\chi_{B(x_{0},2\rho(x_{0}))\setminus 2B}+f\chi_{B(x_{0},2\rho(x_{0}))^{c}}=:f_{1}+f_{2}+f_{3}.

Here, we need to observe that as
w^{-1}\in H_{1,c}^{\rho,m_{1}}
, from Lemma
3.3
there exist
\eta>1
and

c^{*}>0
such that
w\in RH^{\rho}_{\eta,c^{*}}.
Therefore there exist a constant
C
depending on
m_{1}
and
c^{*}
such that

\left(\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}w^{-\gamma}\right)^{1/\gamma}\leq C\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}w^{-1}

since
r\leq\rho(x_{0})
.

Using that
T
is bounded on
L^{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and that
w^{-1}\in A_{1}^{\rho,\textup{loc}}
(by Proposition
3.1
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}|Tf_{1}(x)|dx

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}|Tf_{1}(x)|^{\gamma}dx\right)^{1/\gamma}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{2B}|f(x)|^{\gamma}dx\right)^{1/\gamma}

\displaystyle\leq C\|fw\|_{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{|2B|}\int_{2B}w^{-\gamma}(x)dx\right)^{1/\gamma}

\displaystyle\leq C\|fw\|_{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}w^{-1}(x)dx\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\|fw\|_{\infty}\inf_{B}w^{-1}

\displaystyle=C\frac{\|fw\|_{\infty}}{\|w\chi_{B}\|_{\infty}}.

To estimate
|Tf_{3}(x)|
for
x\in B
, we denote by
B_{\rho}=B(x_{0},\rho(x_{0}))
and
B_{\rho}^{k}=2^{k}B_{\rho}
for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Thus

(4.3)

\int_{B_{\rho}^{k+1}}|f|\leq\|fw\|_{\infty}\int_{B_{\rho}^{k+1}}w^{-1}\leq C\exp\left(c\left(1+2^{k+1}\right)^{m_{1}}\right)|B_{\rho}^{k+1}|\frac{\|fw\|_{\infty}}{\|w\chi_{B_{\rho}^{k+1}}\|_{\infty}}

for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Then, by the size condition (
2.9
) on the kernel
K
with
c_{1}
and
m_{1}
, applying the above inequality and Remark
2.2
, we get

\displaystyle|Tf_{3}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k\geq 1}\int_{B_{\rho}^{k+1}\setminus B_{\rho}^{k}}|K(x,z)||f(z)|dz

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{k\geq 1}\int_{B_{\rho}^{k+1}\setminus B_{\rho}^{k}}\frac{1}{|B_{\rho}^{k-1}|}\,\exp\left(-c_{1}\left(1+\frac{2^{k-1}\rho(x_{0})}{\rho(x)}\right)^{m_{1}}\right)|f(z)|dz

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{k\geq 1}\exp\left(-c_{1}\left(1+2^{k}C_{0}2^{k_{0}}\right)^{m_{1}}\right)\frac{1}{|B_{\rho}^{k+1}|}\int_{B_{\rho}^{k+1}}|f(z)|dz

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{k\geq 1}\exp\left(-(c_{1}-c)\left(1+2^{k+1}\right)^{m_{1}}\right)\frac{\|fw\|_{\infty}}{\|w\chi_{B_{\rho}^{k+1}}\|_{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{\|fw\|_{\infty}}{\|w\chi_{B}\|_{\infty}}\sum_{k\geq 1}\exp\left(-(c_{1}-c)\left(1+2^{k+1}\right)^{m_{1}}\right)

where the series converges since
c<c_{1}
.

The bound for
f_{2}
follows as in the proof of
[
4
, Proposition 5]
, since only the smoothness condition is required, and the operator
T
under consideration satisfies
[
4
, (41)]
, which is precisely (
2.10
).
∎

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
T
be an exponential Schrödinger–Calderón–Zygmund operator of
(s,\delta)
type as given in Definition
2.6
with constants
c_{1}
and
m_{1}
in (
2.7
). Then,
T
is bounded from
L^{\infty}(w)
into
\textup{BMO}_{\rho}(w)
for every weight
w
such that
w^{-s^{\prime}}\in H_{1,c}^{\rho,m_{1}}
with
c<s^{\prime}c_{1}
.

Proof.

Let
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
r\leq\rho(x_{0})
and
B=B(x_{0},r)
. We consider
f_{1},f_{2}
and
f_{3}
as in (
4.2
).

Here, we need to observe that as
w^{-s^{\prime}}\in H_{1,c}^{\rho}
and
r\leq\rho(x_{0})
, the estimates of the average
\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}|Tf_{1}|dx
follows using Kolmogorov’s inequality and the hypothesis on
w
as in
[
5
]
. Then we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}|Tf_{1}(x)|dx\leq C\frac{\|fw\|_{\infty}}{\|w\chi_{B}\|_{\infty}}.

To estimate
|Tf_{3}(x)|
for
x\in B
, we denote, as in the previous proof,
B_{\rho}=B(x_{0},\rho(x_{0}))
and
B_{\rho}^{k}=2^{k}B_{\rho}
for
k\in\mathbb{N}
. From the condition on the weight we can obtain

\left(\int_{B_{\rho}^{k+1}}|f|^{s^{\prime}}\right)^{1/s^{\prime}}\leq\|fw\|_{\infty}\left(\int_{B_{\rho}^{k+1}}w^{-s^{\prime}}\right)^{1/s^{\prime}}\leq C\exp\left(\frac{c}{s^{\prime}}\left(1+2^{k+1}\right)^{m_{1}}\right)\left|B_{\rho}^{k+1}\right|^{1/s^{\prime}}\frac{\|fw\|_{\infty}}{\|w\chi_{B_{\rho}^{k+1}}\|_{\infty}}.

From this inequality and (
2.7
) with
c_{1},m_{1}>0
, we estimate

\displaystyle|Tf_{3}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k\geq 1}\int_{B_{\rho}^{k+1}\setminus B_{\rho}^{k}}|K(x,z)||f(z)|dz

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k\geq 1}\left(\int_{B_{\rho}^{k+1}\setminus B_{\rho}^{k}}|K(x,z)|^{s}dz\right)^{1/s}\left(\int_{B_{\rho}^{k+1}}|f(z)|^{s^{\prime}}dz\right)^{1/s^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{k\geq 1}\rho(x_{0})^{-d/s^{\prime}}\exp\left(-c_{1}\left(1+2^{k+1}\right)^{m_{1}}\right)\exp\left(\frac{c}{s^{\prime}}\left(1+2^{k+1}\right)^{m_{1}}\right)\left|B_{\rho}^{k+1}\right|^{1/s^{\prime}}\frac{\|fw\|_{\infty}}{\|w\chi_{B_{\rho}^{k+1}}\|_{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{\|fw\|_{\infty}}{\|w\chi_{B_{\rho}}\|_{\infty}}\sum_{k\geq 1}\exp\left(-\left(c_{1}-\frac{c}{s^{\prime}}\right)\left(1+2^{k+1}\right)^{m_{1}}\right)

where the series converges as
c<s^{\prime}c_{1}
.

The bound for
f_{2}
can be obtained as in the proof of
[
4
, Proposition 6]
since (
2.8
) implies
[
4
, (45)]
.
∎

5.
Applications for operators associated to
-\Delta+\mu

We consider a Schrödinger operator with measure
\mu
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, with
d\geq 3
,

\mathcal{L}_{\mu}=-\Delta+\mu,

where
\mu
is a nonnegative Radon measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
that satisfies the following conditions: there exist constants
\delta_{\mu},C_{\mu}>0
and
D_{\mu}\geq 1
such that

(5.1)

\displaystyle\mu(B(x,r))\leq C_{\mu}\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{d-2+\delta_{\mu}}\mu(B(x,R))

and

(5.2)

\displaystyle\mu(B(x,2r))\leq D_{\mu}\left(\mu(B(x,r))+r^{d-2}\right)

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
0<r<R
.

From (
5.1
) and (
5.2
) it can be proved that

(5.3)

\int_{B(x,R)}\frac{d\mu(y)}{|y-x|^{d-2}}\leq C\frac{\mu(B(x,R))}{R^{d-2}},

and if
\delta_{\mu}>1
then we also have

(5.4)

\int_{B(x,R)}\frac{d\mu(y)}{|y-x|^{d-1}}\leq C\frac{\mu(B(x,R))}{R^{d-1}},

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
R>0
.

For any nonnegative Radon measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
verifying conditions (
5.1
) and (
5.2
), the function given by

(5.5)

\rho_{\mu}(x):=\sup\left\{r>0:\frac{\mu(B(x,r))}{r^{d-2}}\leq 1\right\},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

is a critical radius function (see Definition
2.1
).

It is immediate from the definition of
\rho_{\mu}
that for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

(5.6)

\frac{\mu(B(x,\rho_{\mu}(x)))}{\rho_{\mu}(x)^{d-2}}\sim 1.

Now, we shall apply Propositions
4.2
and
4.3
to some operators related to
\mathcal{L}_{\mu}
.

The following technical lemmas will be important tools for the examples below.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\rho:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
be a critical radius function as in Definition
2.1
. Then, there exists a constant
C>0
, depending on
D_{0},D_{1}
and
k_{0}
, such that

(5.10)

d_{\rho}(x,y)\geq D_{1}^{-1}\left(1+\frac{|x-y|}{\rho(x)}\right)^{\frac{1}{k_{0}+1}}+D_{0}^{-1}\left(1+\frac{\rho(x)}{|x-y|}\right)^{-1}-C.

Proof.

We will consider two cases. If
|x-y|\leq 2\rho(x)
, by Lemma
2.3
we have

d_{\rho}(x,y)\geq D_{0}^{-1}\frac{|x-y|}{\rho(x)}.

Since

\frac{|x-y|}{\rho(x)}\geq\frac{|x-y|}{\rho(x)+|x-y|}=\left(1+\frac{\rho(x)}{|x-y|}\right)^{-1},

we get

d_{\rho}(x,y)\geq D_{0}^{-1}\left(1+\frac{\rho(x)}{|x-y|}\right)^{-1}.

As
1+\frac{|x-y|}{\rho(x)}\leq 3
, we can take
C\geq D_{1}^{-1}3^{\frac{1}{k_{0}+1}}
to get the desired inequality in this case.

On the other hand, if
|x-y|>2\rho(x)
we can apply Lemma
2.4
to get

d_{\rho}(x,y)\geq D_{1}^{-1}\left(1+\frac{|x-y|}{\rho(x)}\right)^{\frac{1}{k_{0}+1}}.

Trivially,
1+\frac{\rho(x)}{|x-y|}\geq 1,

so choosing
C\geq D_{0}^{-1}
gives (
5.10
) for
|x-y|>2\rho(x)
.
Finally, taking
C=\max\left\{D_{1}^{-1}3^{\frac{1}{k_{0}+1}},D_{0}^{-1}\right\}
, the proof is finished.
∎

The following is a generalization of a known property for the case where
d\mu(x)=V(x)dx
, as given in
[
9
, Lemma 1]
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\mu
be a measure verifying (
5.1
) and (
5.2
). Then, there exist a constant
C>0
such that for any
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
R>0

\mu\left(B(x_{0},R)\right)\leq CR^{d-2}\left(1+\frac{R}{\rho(x_{0})}\right)^{N},

for all
N\geq\log_{2}D_{\mu}
, with
D_{\mu}
as in (
5.2
).

Proof.

Let us first consider the case
R\leq\rho(x_{0})
. By (
5.1
) and (
5.6
),

\displaystyle\mu\left(B(x_{0},R)\right)\lesssim\left(\frac{R}{\rho(x_{0})}\right)^{d-2+\delta_{\mu}}\mu\left(B(x_{0},\rho(x_{0}))\right)\lesssim R^{d-2}.

Now, if
R>\rho(x_{0})
, let
j_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
2^{j_{0}-1}\rho(x_{0})<R\leq 2^{j_{0}}\rho(x_{0})
and use (
5.2
)
j_{0}
times to get

\mu(B(x_{0},2^{j_{0}}r))\leq D_{\mu}^{j_{0}}\mu(B(x_{0},r))+D_{\mu}^{j_{0}}r^{d-2}2^{(j_{0}-1)(d-2)}.

Then, again by (
5.1
) and (
5.6
)

\displaystyle\mu\left(B(x_{0},R)\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\frac{R}{2^{j_{0}}\rho(x_{0})}\right)^{d-2+\delta_{\mu}}\mu\left(B(x_{0},2^{j_{0}}\rho(x_{0}))\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\frac{R}{2^{j_{0}}\rho(x_{0})}\right)^{d-2+\delta_{\mu}}\left(D_{\mu}^{j_{0}}\mu\left(B(x_{0},\rho(x_{0}))\right)+D_{\mu}^{j_{0}}\rho(x_{0})^{d-2}2^{(j_{0}-1)(d-2)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq CR^{d-2}\left(\frac{R}{\rho(x_{0})}\right)^{\delta_{\mu}}\left(\frac{D_{\mu}}{2^{\delta_{\mu}}}\right)^{j_{0}}.

Finally, since

\log_{2}\left(\frac{R}{\rho(x_{0})}\right)\leq j_{0}\leq 1+\log_{2}\left(\frac{R}{\rho(x_{0})}\right)
,

\displaystyle D_{\mu}^{j_{0}}2^{-j_{0}\delta_{\mu}}

\displaystyle\leq D_{\mu}^{1+\log_{2}\left(\frac{R}{\rho(x_{0})}\right)}2^{-\delta_{\mu}\log_{2}\left(\frac{R}{\rho(x_{0})}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq D_{\mu}D_{\mu}^{\log_{2}\left(1+\frac{R}{\rho(x_{0})}\right)}\left(\frac{R}{\rho(x_{0})}\right)^{-\delta_{\mu}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(1+\frac{R}{\rho(x_{0})}\right)^{\log_{2}D_{\mu}}\left(\frac{R}{\rho(x_{0})}\right)^{-\delta_{\mu}}.

Therefore, in both cases we obtain the desired estimate.
∎

5.1.
Riesz transforms

We consider the Riesz transform
R_{\mu}=\nabla\mathcal{L}_{\mu}^{-1/2}
and its adjoint
{R^{*}_{\mu}=\mathcal{L}_{\mu}^{-1/2}\nabla}
as in
[
12
]
.

The Riesz transform
R_{\mu}
can be expressed, for
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
x\notin\supp(f)
, as

\displaystyle R_{\mu}f(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}\nabla(\mathcal{L}_{\mu}+\lambda)^{-1}f(x)d\lambda

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\nabla_{1}\Gamma_{\mu+\lambda}(x,y)f(y)dy\,d\lambda.

By Fubini’s theorem,

\displaystyle R_{\mu}f(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}\nabla_{1}\Gamma_{\mu+\lambda}(x,y)d\lambda f(y)dy=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}K_{\mu}(x,y)f(y)dy,

where
K_{\mu}
is the singular kernel of
R_{\mu}
given by

(5.11)

K_{\mu}(x,y)=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}\nabla_{1}\Gamma_{\mu+\lambda}(x,y)d\lambda.

The adjoint
R_{\mu}^{*}
can be written as

R^{*}_{\mu}f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}K^{*}_{\mu}(x,y)f(y)dy=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}-K_{\mu}(y,x)f(y)dy.

By
[
12
, Theorem 7.18]
we know that
R_{\mu}
is a Calderón–Zygmund operator when
\delta_{\mu}>1
, so it is bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for every
1<p<\infty
. By duality,
R_{\mu}^{*}
is also bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for every
1<p<\infty
. There also is proved condition (
2.10
).
Moreover, we have that (see
[
12
, Theorem 0.17]
)

|K_{\mu}(x,y)|\leq C\frac{e^{-\epsilon d_{\mu}(x,y)}}{|x-y|^{d}},

for some
\epsilon>0
, and the same estimate hold for
|K^{*}_{\mu}(x,y)|
(see also
[
3
, Lemma 4.3]
).
Then, by Lemma
5.1
we obtain the following classification for the Riesz transform and its adjoint.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
\mu
be a measure verifying (
5.1
) and (
5.2
) with
\delta_{\mu}>1
. Then,
R_{\mu}
and
R_{\mu}^{*}
are both exponential Schrödinger–Calderón–Zygmund operators of
(\infty,\delta)
type as given in Definition
2.7
with constants
c=\frac{\epsilon}{2D_{1}}
and
m=\frac{1}{k_{0}+1}
.

As a consequence of Proposition
5.3
above, Proposition
4.2
and Theorem

we obtain the following boundedness result for the case
{\delta_{\mu}>1}
.

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
\mu

5.1
) and (

) with

. Then,

When

we consider the adjoint operators

and the estimates given in

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
\mu
be a measure verifying (

) and (

is an exponential Schrödinger–Calderón–Zygmund operator of

Proof.

Condition

for every

We now prove condition (

such that for

\displaystyle|\nabla_{1}\Gamma_{\mu+\lambda}

\displaystyle(y,x)-\nabla_{1}\Gamma_{\mu+\lambda}(y,x_{0})|

where we have used (

5.2.
Laplace transform type multipliers

Let
\phi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
, and let

m_{\phi}(\lambda)=\lambda\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda t}\phi(t)\,dt,\quad\lambda>0.

By means of the Spectral Theorem, we can define the Laplace transform type multipliers
m_{\phi}(\mathcal{L}_{\mu})
, which are bounded on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for any
\phi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
(see
[
14
, Corollary 3, p. 121]
).

Special cases of multipliers of Laplace transform type are the imaginary powers
\mathcal{L}_{\mu}^{i\gamma}
that arise when
m_{\phi}(\lambda)=\lambda^{i\gamma}
for
\lambda>0
and
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
; that is, when
\phi(t)=t^{-i\gamma}/\Gamma(1-i\gamma)
.

If we consider the heat semigroup associated with
\mathcal{L}_{\mu}
,

\mathcal{W}_{t}f(x):=e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\mu}}f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathcal{W}_{t}(x,y)f(y)dy,\quad f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},t>0,

we can write

m_{\phi}(\mathcal{L}_{\mu})f(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\phi(t)\mathcal{L}e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\mu}}f(x)dt=\int_{0}^{\infty}\phi(t)\partial_{t}\mathcal{W}_{t}f(x)dt,\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Its kernel can be expressed as

\mathcal{M}_{\phi}(x,y)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\phi(t)\partial_{t}\mathcal{W}_{t}(x,y)dt,\quad x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

In order to classify the operator
m_{\phi}(\mathcal{L}_{\mu})
, we will need some estimates for the derivative of the heat kernel
\mathcal{W}_{t}
, already proved by Wu and Yan in
[
15
]
(for
d\mu(x)=V(x)dx
, see
[
8
, Proposition 4]
). We state them below.

Lemma 5.7

(
[
15
, Lemma 2.3 and Lemma 3.8]
)
.

(i)

Let
k_{0}
as in Definition
2.1
.
There exist constants
c_{0}
and
C
such that

\left|t\partial_{t}\mathcal{W}_{t}(x,y)\right|\leq Ct^{-d/2}\exp\left(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{4t}\right)\exp\left(-c_{0}\left(1+\frac{\max\{|x-y|,\sqrt{t/2}\}}{\rho(x)}\right)^{\frac{1}{k_{0}+1}}\right).

(ii)

For every
0<\delta<\min\{1,\delta_{\mu}\}
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
, there exist constants
c
and
C_{N}
such that for any
|h|\leq\sqrt{t}
,

|t\partial_{t}\mathcal{W}_{t}(x+h,y)-t\partial_{t}\mathcal{W}_{t}(x,y)|\leq\left(\frac{|h|}{\sqrt{t}}\right)^{\delta}t^{-d/2}\exp\left(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{4t}\right)\frac{C_{N}}{\left(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho(x)}+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho(y)}\right)^{N}},

with
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
t>0
.

We are now in position to establish the following classification for
m_{\phi}(\mathcal{L}_{\mu})
.

Proposition 5.8
.

Let
\phi
be as above. The operator
m_{\phi}(\mathcal{L}_{\mu})
is an exponential Schrödinger–Calderón–Zygmund operator of
(\infty,\delta)
type as given in Definition
2.7
with constants
c=c_{0}
(the constant in Lemma
5.7
(i)
) and
m=\frac{1}{k_{0}+1}
.

Proof.

We shall first prove (
2.9
). For any
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, by Lemma
5.7
(i)
,

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{M}_{\phi}(x,y)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|\phi\|_{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}|\partial_{t}\mathcal{W}_{t}(x,y)|dt

\displaystyle\leq C\exp\left(-c_{0}\left(1+\frac{|x-y|}{\rho(x)}\right)^{\frac{1}{k_{0}+1}}\right)\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-d/2}\exp\left(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{4t}\right)\frac{dt}{t}.

By performing the change of variables
u=\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{t}
, we get

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{M}_{\phi}(x,y)\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\exp\left(-c_{0}\left(1+\frac{|x-y|}{\rho(x)}\right)^{\frac{1}{k_{0}+1}}\right)\frac{1}{|x-y|^{d}}\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{d/2}e^{-u/4}\frac{du}{u}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{|x-y|^{d}}\exp\left(-c_{0}\left(1+\frac{|x-y|}{\rho(x)}\right)^{\frac{1}{k_{0}+1}}\right),

and (
2.9
) holds with
c_{1}=c_{0}
and
m=\frac{1}{k_{0}+1}
.

For the smoothness condition (
2.10
), we consider
|x-y|>2|x-x_{0}|
and
0<\delta<\min\{1,\delta_{\mu}\}
. We have that

\displaystyle|\mathcal{M}_{\phi}(x,y)-\mathcal{M}_{\phi}(x_{0},y)|

\displaystyle\leq\|\phi\|_{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}|\partial_{t}\mathcal{W}_{t}(x,y)-\partial_{t}\mathcal{W}_{t}(x_{0},y)|dt

\displaystyle\sim\int_{0}^{|x-x_{0}|^{2}}|t\partial_{t}\mathcal{W}_{t}(x,y)-t\partial_{t}\mathcal{W}_{t}(x_{0},y)|\frac{dt}{t}

(5.15)

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{|x-x_{0}|^{2}}^{\infty}|t\partial_{t}\mathcal{W}_{t}(x,y)-t\partial_{t}\mathcal{W}_{t}(x_{0},y)|\frac{dt}{t}.

For the second term, as
|x-x_{0}|\leq\sqrt{t}
, by Lemma
5.7
(ii)
with
h=x-x_{0}
it follows that

\displaystyle|t\partial_{t}\mathcal{W}_{t}(x,y)-t\partial_{t}\mathcal{W}_{t}(x_{0},y)|

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\left(\frac{|x-x_{0}|}{\sqrt{t}}\right)^{\delta}t^{-d/2}\exp\left(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{4t}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C_{\delta}\left(\frac{|x-x_{0}|}{\sqrt{t}}\right)^{\delta}\left(\frac{|x-y|}{\sqrt{t}}\right)^{-\delta}t^{-d/2}\exp\left(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{8t}\right)

\displaystyle=C_{\delta}\left(\frac{|x-x_{0}|}{|x-y|}\right)^{\delta}t^{-d/2}\exp\left(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{8t}\right).

Then

\displaystyle\int_{|x-x_{0}|^{2}}^{\infty}|t\partial_{t}\mathcal{W}_{t}(x,y)-t\partial_{t}\mathcal{W}_{t}(x_{0},y)|\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\frac{|x-x_{0}|}{|x-y|}\right)^{\delta}\int_{|x-x_{0}|^{2}}^{\infty}t^{-d/2}\exp\left(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{8t}\right)\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{1}{|x-y|^{d}}\left(\frac{|x-x_{0}|}{|x-y|}\right)^{\delta}\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{d/2-1}e^{-u/8}du

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{1}{|x-y|^{d}}\left(\frac{|x-x_{0}|}{|x-y|}\right)^{\delta}.

For
|x-x_{0}|>\sqrt{t}
, we use Lemma
5.7
(i)
to get, for
\delta
as before, that

\displaystyle|t\partial_{t}\mathcal{W}_{t}(x,y)|

\displaystyle\leq Ct^{-d/2}\exp\left(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{4t}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\frac{|x-x_{0}|}{\sqrt{t}}\right)^{\delta}t^{-d/2}\exp\left(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{4t}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C_{\delta}\left(\frac{|x-x_{0}|}{|x-y|}\right)^{\delta}t^{-d/2}\exp\left(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{8t}\right).

And, since
|x-y|\sim|x_{0}-y|
, we also obtain the same bound for
|t\partial_{t}\mathcal{W}_{t}(x_{0},y)|
when
|x-x_{0}|>\sqrt{t}
. Therefore, the first term in (
5.2
) is bounded as desired.

Finally, as previously mentioned,
m_{\phi}(\mathcal{L}_{\mu})
is bounded on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. This, together with the conditions above, allows us to conclude that it is a Calderón-Zygmund type operator. Therefore,
m_{\phi}(\mathcal{L}_{\mu})
is bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for all
p>1
, and the proposition is thus proved.
∎

Weak interaction rates of s ​ d sd -shell Urca pairs in stellar environment using a shell-model approach
Shweta Sharma, Praveen C. Srivastava, Toshio Suzuki

Abstract
In this work, the weak interaction rates of s ​ d sd -shell nuclei in the hot and dense stellar environment are calculated using the shell model. The ab initio effective interactions are employed for the calculation of Gamow-Teller strengths and weak rates in addition to the phenomenological interaction. The weak rates are evaluated in a fine grid of density and temperature. The electron capture and β − \beta^{-} -decay rates of Urca pairs of nuclei with A = 23 , 25 , 29 , 31 A=23,25,29,31 and 33 are evaluated as a function of log 10 ⁡ ρ ​ Y e \log_{10}\rho Y_{e} and the corresponding Urca density is obtained. The screening effects are also included for the evaluation of these stellar weak rates. In addition, the intrinsic cooling strength is evaluated for A = 29 , 31 A=29,31 and 33 Urca pairs in neutron star crusts. The stellar weak rates, along with the neutrino energy emitted and the gamma heat production, are tabulated for A = 29 − 39 A=29-39 nuclei.



I
Introduction

The study of the weak interaction rates in stellar environments is important for many astrophysical applications like comprehending the cooling stages of the stellar evolution, neutron star crusts, and oceans, and in presupernova stellar collapse
burbidge1957
;
langanke2014
. During a supernova, the star core experiences a significant increase in density, and the Fermi energy of the electrons increases as the electron gas becomes degenerate
langanke2003
. This causes the capture of degenerate relativistic electrons by the nucleus because the Fermi energy of the degenerate electrons exceeds the negative
Q
value for the capture of electrons by the nuclei
takahara1989
. This phenomenon is referred to as the electron capture (e-capture) process. This process leads to a decrease in the degenerate electron pressure within the stellar core and cooling of the stellar core, along with a decrease in entropy as the neutrinos escape from the star
suzuki2011
;
pinedo2014
;
pinedo2022
;
langanke2015
. Further, the Urca process is a major phenomenon responsible for the cooling of the stars. In the Urca process, the electron capture and its inverse process
\beta^{-}
decay occur simultaneously, thereby emitting neutrinos and antineutrinos from the stellar core.

For the first time, electron capture rates were studied by Mazurek
et al.

mazurek1974
and Duke
et al.

duke1971

fuller1982s

The evaluation of Gamow-Teller (GT) strengths is crucial
for the study of weak interaction rates. There are two types of GT strengths, i.e., GT
+
and GT
-
strength, dependent on the type of weak interaction process. The experimental GT strengths can be obtained through charge-exchange reactions and decays, but the energy resolution and ranges are limited. Therefore, we need theoretical models that can accurately estimate these GT strengths. The interacting shell model with
an appropriate effective interaction is considered the most efficient model for the calculation of these GT strengths. The phenomenological USDB effective interaction
brown2006
has demonstrated its efficacy in describing the nuclear structure properties of
sd
-shell nuclei. The USDB interaction utilizes a
renormalized G matrix that incorporates linear combinations of two-body matrix components calibrated accurately to a collection of experimental data for
sd
-shell nuclei. This effective interaction was used for the evaluation of weak interaction rates of

Further, over the past few years, the

sd
-shell nuclei.

Another effective interaction used in our work is obtained from chiral effective field theory (CEFT), and derived by Huth

In this approach, the Hamiltonian for the sd shell is constructed with two low-energy constants (LECs) at leading order along with seven new LECs at next-to-leading order. Further, the LECs of CEFT operators are fitted directly to 441 ground-state (g.s.) and excited-state energies. These successful microscopic interactions inspire us to employ them for the study of electron capture rates of
sd
-shell nuclei.

II
Formalism for stellar weak interaction rates

II.1
Electron chemical potential (
\mu_{e}
)

The electron chemical potential is determined by the density expression as

\rho Y_{e}=\frac{1}{\pi^{2}N_{A}}(\frac{m_{e}c}{\hbar})^{3}\int_{0}^{\infty}(S_{e}-S_{p})p^{2}dp,

(1)

where
\frac{(m_{e}c)^{3}}{\pi^{2}\hbar^{3}N_{A}}=2.9218\times 10^{6}\text{g cm}^{-3}
and the terms
\rho
and
Y_{e}
are the density and the baryon ratio, respectively. The term
S_{e}
stands for the Fermi-Dirac distribution of electrons, and
S_{p}
is subtracted in the equation to cancel out the effect of positrons.
S_{e}
is defined as

S_{e}=\frac{1}{exp(\frac{E_{e}-\mu_{e}}{kT})+1},

(2)

where
E_{e}
is the electron energy and
\mu_{e}
is the electron chemical potential. In addition,
\mu_{e}
is replaced by
\mu_{p}
in the expression for
S_{p}
where
\mu_{p}=-\mu_{e}
.

II.2
Weak interaction rates

The weak interaction rates are evaluated using the values of the chemical potential evaluated above. The formulation used for this calculation is given here. The weak interaction rate in the stellar environment can be evaluated as

\lambda=\frac{\rm{ln}2}{K}\sum_{i}W_{i}\sum_{j}(B_{ij}({\rm GT})+B_{ij}({\rm F}))\phi_{ij}^{\alpha},

(3)

where
i
and
j
stand for initial and final states, respectively. The constant
K=6146\pm 6
s
haxton1995
is determined from superallowed Fermi transitions. The term
W_{i}
is the statistical weight function, and
B_{ij}({\rm GT})
and
B_{ij}({\rm F})

brown1985
;
saxena2018
are GT and Fermi-reduced transition probabilities, respectively. The term
\alpha
= ec,
\beta^{+}
, pc,
\beta^{-}
for electron capture,
\beta^{+}
decay, positron capture , and
\beta^{-}
decay, respectively. Thus, the term
\phi_{ij}^{\alpha}

langanke2001
is given by

\phi_{ij}^{ec}=\int_{w_{min}}^{\infty}wp(Q_{ij}+w)^{2}F(Z,w)S_{e}(w)dw,

(4)

\phi_{ij}^{\beta^{+}}=\int_{1}^{Q_{ij}}wp(Q_{ij}-w)^{2}F(-Z+1,w)[1-S_{p}(w)]dw,

(5)

\phi_{ij}^{pc}=\int_{w_{min}}^{\infty}wp(Q_{ij}+w)^{2}F(-Z,w)S_{p}(w)dw,

(6)

\phi_{ij}^{\beta^{-}}=\int_{1}^{Q_{ij}}wp(Q_{ij}-w)^{2}F(Z+1,w)[1-S_{e}(w)]dw.

(7)

Here,
w
and
p=\sqrt{w^{2}-1}
are electron energy and momentum, respectively.
F(Z,w)
is the Fermi function, and the quantity
S_{e}(w)
and
S_{p}(w)
is the Fermi-Dirac distribution for electrons and positrons, respectively. The integration lower limit
w_{min}=1
if
Q_{ij}\geq-1
and
w_{min}=|Q_{ij}|
if
Q_{ij}<-1
where
Q_{ij}
is the
Q
value which is given as

Q_{ij}=(Q_{\rm g.s.}+E_{i}-E_{f})/m_{e}c^{2},

(8)

where the
E_{i}
and
E_{f}
are the excitation energies of the initial and final states, respectively. The term
Q_{\rm g.s.}
is the
Q
-value between the ground states which is defined as

Q_{\rm g.s.}=M_{p}c^{2}-M_{d}c^{2},

(9)

where
M_{p}
and
M_{d}
are the nuclear masses of parent and daughter nuclei, respectively. Further, the statistical weight function is given by,

W_{i}=(2J_{i}+1)e^{-E_{i}/kT}/\sum_{i}(2J_{i}+1)e^{-E_{i}/kT},

(10)

where
J_{i}
is the angular momentum of the initial state.
The quantities
B_{ij}({\rm GT})
and
B_{ij}({\rm F})
are given by

B_{ij}({\rm GT})=(g_{A}/g_{V})^{2}\frac{1}{2J_{i}+1}|<f||\sum_{k}\sigma^{k}t_{+}^{k}||i>|^{2},

(11)

B_{ij}({\rm F})=\frac{1}{2J_{i}+1}|<f||\sum_{k}t_{+}^{k}||i>|^{2}.

(12)

Here,
g_{A}
and
g_{V}
are the axial vector and vector coupling constants.
g_{A}^{eff}=qg_{A}=0.77\times 1.27=0.98
and
g_{V}=1.00
were taken for calculations. The
B_{ij}
(GT) and
B_{ij}
(F) values have been taken from the shell-model calculation for different transitions using the
KSHELL and NUSHELLX shell-model codes
kshell
;
nushellx
.

Further, the Fermi function
F(Z,w)
is defined as

F(Z,w)=(2)(1+\gamma)e^{\pi{y}}\left(\frac{2pR}{\hbar}\right)^{2(\gamma-1)}\left(\frac{|\Gamma(\gamma+iy)|}{\Gamma(1+2\gamma)}\right)^{2}.

(13)

Here,
p=\sqrt{w^{2}-1}
and
\gamma
and
y
are the auxiliary quantities, which are given as

\gamma=\sqrt{1-(\alpha{Z})^{2}}
and
y=(\alpha{Zw}/pc)
, where
\alpha=1/137
is the fine structure constant.

II.3
Screening effects

The screening effects of the degenerate electrons in a stellar environment affect the weak interaction rates. The first screening effect changes the threshold energy because of the change in the chemical potential of the ions. The
Q_{ij}
is modified as

Q_{ij}=Q_{ij}+\Delta Q_{C},

(14)

where
\Delta Q_{C}
is defined as

\Delta Q_{C}=\mu_{C}(Z_{p})-\mu_{C}(Z_{d}),

(15)

where
\mu_{C}(Z)
is the Coulomb chemical potential,
and
Z_{p}
and
Z_{d}
are the nuclear charges of parent and daughter nuclei, respectively.

\Delta Q_{C}
is positive (negative) in
\beta^{-}
-decay (electron capture) process.
Detailed expressions for
\mu_{C}(Z)
can be found in Refs.
ichimaru93
;
juo10
.

Second is the screening effects of electrons where chemical potential is changed by

\mu_{e}\rightarrow\mu_{e}-V_{s}(0),

(16)

where
V_{s}(0)
is Coulomb potential at origin, which at a distance
r
is given by

V_{s}(r)=Ze^{2}(2k_{F})J(r),

(17)

where
J(r)
is given by

J(r)=\frac{1}{2k_{F}r}\big[1-\frac{2}{\pi}\int\frac{sin(2k_{F}qr)}{q^{2}\epsilon(q,0)}dq\big],

(18)

and the analytic fitting formula is used for the evaluation of
J(r)
as given in Ref.
itoh2002
.

II.4
Neutrino-energy-loss rate (
\xi
)

The energy loss by neutrino emission is given by

\xi=\frac{{\rm ln2}(m_{e}c^{2})}{6146(s)}\sum_{i}W_{i}\sum_{j}[B_{ij}({\rm GT})+B_{ij}({\rm F})]\psi_{ij}^{\alpha},

(19)

where
\psi_{ij}^{\alpha}
is expressed as

\psi_{ij}^{ec}=\int_{w_{min}}^{\infty}wp(Q_{ij}+w)^{3}F(Z,w)S_{e}(w)dw,

(20)

\psi_{ij}^{\beta^{+}}=\int_{1}^{Q_{ij}}wp(Q_{ij}-w)^{3}F(-Z+1,w)[1-S_{p}(w)]dw.

(21)

\psi_{ij}^{pc}=\int_{w_{min}}^{\infty}wp(Q_{ij}+w)^{3}F(-Z,w)S_{p}(w)dw,

(22)

\psi_{ij}^{\beta^{-}}=\int_{1}^{Q_{ij}}wp(Q_{ij}-w)^{3}F(Z+1,w)[1-S_{e}(w)]dw.

(23)

Now, the average energy of the emitted neutrino is given by

<E_{\nu}>=\frac{\xi}{\lambda}.

(24)

II.5
\gamma
-ray heating rate (
\eta
):

The heating rate by
\gamma
emission is given by

\eta=\frac{{\rm ln2}(m_{e}c^{2})}{6146(s)}\sum_{i}W_{i}\sum_{j}[B_{ij}({\rm GT})+B_{ij}({\rm F})]\Gamma_{ij}^{\alpha},

(25)

where
\Gamma_{ij}^{\alpha}
is expressed as

\Gamma_{ij}^{ec}=\int_{w_{min}}^{\infty}wp(Q_{ij}+w)^{2}E_{f}/m_{e}c^{2}F(Z,w)S_{e}(w)dw,

(26)

\Gamma_{ij}^{\beta^{+}}=\int_{1}^{Q_{ij}}wp(Q_{ij}-w)^{2}E_{f}/m_{e}c^{2}F(-Z+1,w)[1-S_{p}(w)]dw.

(27)

\Gamma_{ij}^{pc}=\int_{w_{min}}^{\infty}wp(Q_{ij}+w)^{2}E_{f}/m_{e}c^{2}F(-Z,w)S_{p}(w)dw,

(28)

\Gamma_{ij}^{\beta^{-}}=\int_{1}^{Q_{ij}}wp(Q_{ij}-w)^{2}E_{f}/m_{e}c^{2}F(Z+1,w)[1-S_{e}(w)]dw.

(29)

Now, the average energy of the emitted
\gamma
is given by

<E_{\gamma}>=\frac{\eta}{\lambda}.

(30)

In this subsection, we apply the weak rates of nuclear pairs

deibel2016
;
tsuruta1970

L_{\nu}\approx L_{34}\times 10^{34}({\rm erg/s})X(A)T_{9}^{5}(\frac{g_{14}}{2})^{-1}R_{10}^{2},

The formula for determination of intrinsic cooling strength, i.e., L
34
by considering thermally populated excited states is given by
wang2021

\displaystyle L_{34}(Z,A,T)=\sum_{\epsilon\beta}\,

\displaystyle 0.87\left(\frac{10^{6}~\text{s}}{\langle ft\rangle_{\epsilon\beta}}\right)\left(\frac{56}{A}\right)

\displaystyle\times\left(\frac{|Q_{\epsilon\beta}(Z,A)|}{4~\text{MeV}}\right)^{5}\left(\langle F\rangle^{+}\right),

(32)

Z
and
A
are the proton and mass number of the EC parent nucleus and
\epsilon
and
\beta
denotes the EC and
\beta^{-}
-decaying nucleus. The
\langle F\rangle^{+}
is the Coulomb correction induced by electron-ion interaction given by

\langle F\rangle^{+}=\frac{2\pi\alpha Z}{|1-e^{(-2\pi\alpha Z)}|}.

(33)

\langle F\rangle^{+}

Now,

is the
Q

Q_{\epsilon\beta}(Z,A)=Q_{\rm EC}-m_{e}c^{2}+E_{\epsilon}-E_{\beta},

(34)

where

and
E_{\beta}

\beta
-decaying parent nucleus. However, this expression changes when screening effects are considered and

15

Further,

\langle ft\rangle_{\epsilon\beta}=\frac{\tilde{f}t^{-}_{\epsilon\beta}+\tilde{f}t^{+}_{\epsilon\beta}}{2}

(37)

\beta^{-}

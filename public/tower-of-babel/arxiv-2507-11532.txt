Critical and super-critical scatterings in baryogenesis and leptogenesis Preprint: APS/123-QED
Marcos M. Flores, Kalliopi Petraki, Anna Socha

Abstract
In many theories, matter–antimatter asymmetries originate from out-of-equilibrium decays and scatterings of heavy particles. While decays remain efficient, scattering rates typically drop below the Hubble rate as the universe expands. We point out the possibility of scatterings between non-relativistic particles and the relativistic bath whose cross-sections grow with decreasing temperature, leading to scattering rates that track or exceed the Hubble rate at late times. This results in soaring asymmetry generation, even at low scales and with small CP- or baryon/lepton-violating couplings.



Introduction —

The origin of atoms, stars and galaxies can be traced back to a minuscule excess of baryons over antibaryons in the early universe. The asymmetry between matter and antimatter, quantified by the baryon-to-entropy density ratio,
Y_{\rm B}\equiv(n_{\rm B}-n_{\bar{\rm B}})/s=(8.69\pm 0.01)\times 10^{-11}

Aghanim et al. 2020
, has been independently inferred from the primordial abundance of light nuclei
Cooke et al. 2018
;
Riemer-Sørensen and Jenssen 2017
and the cosmic microwave background anisotropies.
Generating an asymmetry requires processes satisfying the well-known Sakharov conditions
Sakharov 1967
. The Standard Model (SM) fails to do so sufficiently, making the observed baryon asymmetry a strong indication of new physics.
It is also plausible that the dark matter (DM) density is due to a dark asymmetry, potentially linked to
Y_{\rm B}

Petraki and Volkas 2013
;
Zurek 2014
.

In some of the most well-motivated and testable scenarios, the matter-antimatter asymmetry arises from out-of-equilibrium, CP-violating decays
Weinberg 1979
;
Fry et al. 1980
;
Fukugita and Yanagida 1986
or scatterings
Yoshimura 1978
;
Kolb and Wolfram 1980
;
Bento and Berezhiani 2001
of heavy particles. These include leptogenesis
Fukugita and Yanagida 1986
;
Buchmuller et al. 2005
;
Davidson et al. 2008
, GUT baryogenesis
Yoshimura 1978
;
Kolb and Wolfram 1980
;
Langacker 1981
, and analogous mechanisms for asymmetric DM
Falkowski et al. 2011
;
Davidson and Elmer 2012
. Decay processes become efficient, and remain so, once the Hubble parameter drops below the decay rate of the heavy species. In contrast, the scattering rates,
\mathcal{R}=\langle\sigma v_{\text{M\o l}}\rangle n_{T}
, are governed by the temperature dependence of the thermally-averaged cross-sections, which we may parametrize as
\langle\sigma v_{\text{M\o l}}\rangle\propto T^{n}/\Lambda^{n+2}
with
\Lambda
being some energy scale, as well as the number density of the target particles, which, for relativistic species, scales as
n_{T}\sim T^{3}
. In existing models,
n>-1
, so scattering processes become inefficient as their rates decrease faster than the Hubble parameter during radiation domination.

In this
letter
, we identify a novel possibility, where particle-number-violating and/or CP-violating scatterings with
n\leqslant-1
drive asymmetry generation. For
n=-1
, the scattering rate scales with the Hubble parameter and exceeds it for sufficiently large couplings. More strikingly, for
n<-1
, the scattering rate inevitably overtakes the Hubble expansion at low temperatures, regardless of the coupling strength. We refer to these scalings as
critical
and
super-critical
, respectively. Notably, these dynamics can lead to highly efficient asymmetry generation, even at lower energy scales, potentially within the reach of future experiments.

We consider, specifically, a two-flavor scenario where the CP-violating scatterings of heavy fermions
X
and relativistic scalars
\rho
exhibit
super-critical
scaling, leading to prodigious asymmetry generation. The asymmetry eventually freezes out as the heavier fermion population is depleted through
X
-violating decays and
critical
scatterings with the thermal bath. As we shall see, this behavior stems directly from the charge assignments of the interacting fields under a
U(1)_{\rm B-L}
gauge symmetry.

Such model structures are broadly motivated: scalar mediators commonly appear in asymmetry-generation scenarios, where their interactions provide the necessary CP violation. In compelling models, scalars typically carry gauge charges that allow them to play a role in spontaneous symmetry breaking. These features naturally give rise to interactions exhibiting
critical
or
super-critical
scaling, suggesting that the dynamics identified here may be generic in theories of asymmetry generation.

Critical
and
super-critical
scatterings in the early universe have previously been shown to strongly affect DM production
Binder et al. 2023
. In that case,
(super-)critical
annihilations of two non-relativistic DM particles prevent DM chemical decoupling, but this behavior is ultimately unphysical:
super-criticality
arises for
\sigma\propto 1/k_{{\rm CM}}^{\gamma}
with
\gamma\geqslant 3

Binder et al. 2023
, where
k_{{\rm CM}}
is the momentum in the center-of-momentum frame; this
violates unitarity at low
k_{{\rm CM}}

Flores and Petraki 2024
. Proper unitarization curtails this behavior and ensures freeze-out
Petraki et al. 2025
. In contrast, for scatterings on relativistic targets, as considered here,
critical
behavior occurs already for
\gamma\geqslant 1
, consistent with unitarity at low energies.

The model —
We extend the SM gauge group by a
U(1)_{\rm B-L}
factor, under which the SM quarks and leptons have the standard charges,
1/3
and
-1
, respectively. Besides the
B-L

\rho

X_{1}

B-L

-2

-1

Type A:
All

B-L
charge
-1

Type B:

The

\{5,-4,-4\}

This assignment cancels the anomalies, while precluding renormalizable couplings to other fields.

The
\chi

Appendix

A

Cosmology —
We discern three major energy scales.

High energies:

Well above the TeV scale, the
B-L
and electroweak (EW) symmetries are unbroken. We assume that the
X
fermions have masses much larger than both breaking scales (indicatively,
m_{{\rm X}}\sim
10 PeV).
Although thermal effects endow
\rho

(see
Eq.

13

X
fermions are kept in equilibrium via
X\bar{X}\leftrightarrow\rho\rho^{*},V_{\mu}V_{\mu}

y_{ij}^{\rm L,R}
and
g_{{\rm B-L}}
, while the much smaller
\lambda^{{\rm X}}
couplings to SM particles are ineffective at this stage. The
X
particles begin to freeze-out at
T\sim m_{{\rm X}}/10
. The decays
X_{2}\rightarrow\rho\bar{X}_{1}
, and a variety of
X-
and CP-violating scatterings (cf.
Appendix

B

X
and
\rho

\Delta_{X}\equiv\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2}=-2\Delta_{\rho}\neq 0
, where
\Delta_{j}\equiv Y_{j}-Y_{\bar{j}}
for
j=X_{1},X_{2},\rho
.

Intermediate energies:
Before the
B-L
and EW phase transitions, the
X_{1}\to L_{l}{\cal H}
decays transfer the
X
global charge to the visible sector. The lepton asymmetry is converted into a baryon asymmetry via sphalerons

.

Low energies:

At
T=T_{\cancel{B-L}}
(
\sim
10 TeV, indicatively),
\rho
develops a vacuum expectation value, breaking
U(1)_{\rm B-L}
. The radial
\rho
component and
V_{\mu}
acquire masses. Their densities are depleted via (
B-L

\Delta_{\rho}
and inducing a net
B-L
charge.

Next, we discuss the CP-violating and
X
-violating processes in more detail, often referring to them according to the
B-L
charge of their initial and final states.

CP violation via super-critical scatterings —

The two-flavor model considered here does not allow for CP violation in any of the
X
-violating processes. CP violation arises however in
X

perturbatively

Lepton violation via critical scatterings —

In our model, the
X(\rho)
violation occurs via
X_{2}\rightarrow\bar{X}_{1}\rho
decays, and
X_{i}X_{j}\leftrightarrow\rho V_{\mu}
,
X_{i}V_{\mu}\leftrightarrow\bar{X}_{j}\rho
scatterings. The decays are inherently efficient, provided that
m_{\rho}(T)<\Delta m_{{\rm X}}
. Charge-2 annihilations are the least significant: their tree-level amplitude is suppressed for small
\alpha_{{\rm B-L}}
, while for large
\alpha_{{\rm B-L}}
they become Sommerfeld suppressed at
v_{\rm rel}\lesssim\alpha_{{\rm B-L}}
due to the repulsive
V_{\mu}-
mediated
XX
potential. In contrast, charge-1 processes are enhanced at low temperatures:
their cross-sections scale as
\sigma\propto 1/k_{{\rm CM}}
, or
\propto 1/T
after thermal averaging. The difference compared to the CP-violating
super-critical
scatterings discussed above is due to the momentum-suppressed fermion-fermion-vector vertex in place of one fermion-fermion-scalar vertex.
The corresponding interaction rates,
\mathcal{R}_{iV_{\mu}\to\bar{j}\rho}=n_{V_{\mu}}\langle\sigma v_{\text{M\o l}}\rangle_{iV_{\mu}\to\bar{j}\rho}\propto T^{2}/m_{{\rm X}}
, enhanced by the large density of a relativistic incoming species, scale with temperature in the same way as the Hubble rate. The
X_{i}V_{\mu}\leftrightarrow\bar{X}_{j}\rho
interactions thus exhibits
critical
scaling: provided that the relevant couplings are large enough, they reach and remain in equilibrium.
The efficient depletion of the
X_{2}
species via these
critical
scatterings, as well as the
X_{2}\rightarrow\bar{X}_{1}\rho
decays, warrants that the
\Delta_{\rho}
asymmetry eventually freezes out.

Discussion —

We have identified a new class of CP-violating and/or particle-number-violating scattering processes in the early universe whose rates can exceed the Hubble expansion, providing an exceptionally efficient mechanism for generating the matter-antimatter asymmetry. This prodigious behavior arises from a combination of scalar interactions, which are not momentum suppressed, and a symmetry structure imposed by gauge invariance. Scalar fields, essential for CP violation, find a natural
raison d’être
in this framework through their gauge charges, a structure that suggests a predestined role in cosmic asymmetry generation.

Our findings reveal that
critical
and
super-critical
energy scalings of the cross-sections can significantly lower the scale at which asymmetry is produced. This has direct model-building and observational implications: the required couplings and CP phases may differ from standard expectations, and the energy regime relevant for baryogenesis may be brought closer to experimental reach. This mechanism opens a promising new direction for baryogenesis, leptogenesis, and asymmetric DM, warranting deeper theoretical and phenomenological investigation.

\displaystyle p_{0}=\sqrt{m_{i}^{2}+|\mathbf{p}|^{2}}\gg|\mathbf{p}|\sim\sqrt{m_{i}T}\gg k_{0},|\mathbf{k}|\sim T.

m_{i}\approx m_{j}\equiv m_{{\rm X}}

(47a)

(48)

and

\displaystyle b(p_{0},|\mathbf{p}|)

\displaystyle\times\int_{0}^{\infty}d|\mathbf{k}||\mathbf{k}|f_{B}(|\mathbf{k}|)

\displaystyle\delta m_{ij}\approx-\frac{Y_{ik}}{48}\frac{T^{2}}{m_{{\rm X}}},

Appendix D
Boltzmann equations for the asymmetries

The four independent Boltzmann equations can be reformulated in terms of the following dimensionless variables:

\displaystyle\gamma=Y_{1}+Y_{2},

\displaystyle\bar{\gamma}=Y_{\bar{1}}+Y_{\bar{2}},

(55)

\displaystyle\beta=Y_{1}-Y_{2},

\displaystyle\bar{\beta}=Y_{\bar{1}}-Y_{\bar{2}}.

(56)

By inverting these relations and imposing the constraint
\mu_{\rho}=-\mu_{\rho^{*}}
on the
\rho
chemical potential, one obtains

\displaystyle Y_{i}=\frac{1}{2}\bigg(\gamma-(-1)^{i}\beta\bigg),

\displaystyle Y_{\bar{i}}=\frac{1}{2}\bigg(\bar{\gamma}-(-1)^{i}\bar{\beta}\bigg),

(57)

\displaystyle Y_{\rho}\simeq Y_{\rho}^{\rm eq}+\frac{\Delta_{\rho}}{2},

\displaystyle Y_{\rho^{*}}\simeq Y_{\rho}^{\rm eq}-\frac{\Delta_{\rho}}{2},

(58)

and

\displaystyle\Delta_{\rho}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{\bar{\gamma}-\gamma}{2},

(59)

\displaystyle\Delta_{i}

\displaystyle\equiv-\Delta_{\rho}-(-1)^{i}\frac{\beta-\bar{\beta}}{2},

\displaystyle i\in\{1,2\}.

(60)

The Boltzmann equation for the total asymmetry then reads

\displaystyle\frac{d\Delta_{\rho}}{dx}

\displaystyle=\frac{ds/dx}{3H(x)}\bigg\{\mathcal{D}_{\bar{1}}-\mathcal{D}_{1}

\displaystyle+[\bar{X}_{i}\bar{X}_{j}\leftrightarrow\rho^{*}V_{\mu}]^{ij}-[X_{i}X_{j}\leftrightarrow\rho V_{\mu}]^{ij}

\displaystyle+[\bar{X}_{i}V_{\mu}\leftrightarrow X_{j}\rho^{*}]^{ij}-[X_{i}V_{\mu}\leftrightarrow\bar{X}_{j}\rho]^{ij}\bigg\}.

(61)

By simple rearrangements, the right-hand side of the above equation separates into a part proportional to

\Delta_{\rho}
and another proportional to
\beta-\bar{\beta}
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\frac{d\Delta_{\rho}}{dx}=\frac{ds/dx}{3H(x)}\bigg[F_{{\rm WO}}\cdot\Delta_{\rho}+F_{\rm flavor}\cdot\frac{\beta-\bar{\beta}}{2}\bigg],

(62)

where

\displaystyle F_{{\rm WO}}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{\langle\Gamma_{2\to\rho\bar{1}}\rangle}{s}\bigg[1+\frac{Y_{2}^{\rm eq}}{Y_{1}^{\rm eq}}+\frac{Y_{2}^{\rm eq}}{Y_{\rho}^{\rm eq}Y_{1}^{\rm eq}}\frac{Y_{1}+Y_{\bar{1}}}{2}\bigg]+\sum_{i,j}\langle\sigma v_{\text{M\o l}}\rangle_{ij\to\rho V_{\mu}}\bigg[Y_{i}+Y_{\bar{j}}+Y_{V_{\mu}}^{\rm eq}\frac{Y_{i}^{\rm eq}Y_{j}^{\rm eq}}{Y_{\rho}^{\rm eq}Y_{V_{\mu}}^{\rm eq}}\bigg]

\displaystyle+Y_{V_{\mu}}^{\rm eq}\sum_{ij}\langle\sigma v_{\text{M\o l}}\rangle_{iV_{\mu}\rightarrow\bar{j}\rho}\bigg[1+\frac{Y_{i}^{\rm eq}}{Y_{j}^{\rm eq}Y_{\rho}^{\rm eq}}(Y_{j}+Y_{\rho})\bigg],

(63a)

\displaystyle F_{\rm flavor}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{\langle\Gamma_{2\to\bar{1}\rho}\rangle}{s}\bigg[1-\frac{Y_{2}^{\rm eq}}{Y_{1}^{\rm eq}}\bigg]+\sum_{i,j}\langle\sigma v_{\text{M\o l}}\rangle_{ij\to\rho V_{\mu}}\left[Y_{i}(-1)^{j}+Y_{\bar{j}}(-1)^{i}\right]+Y_{V_{\mu}}^{\rm eq}\sum_{i,j}\langle\sigma v_{\text{M\o l}}\rangle_{iV_{\mu}\rightarrow\bar{j}\rho}\bigg[(-1)^{i}+(-1)^{j}\frac{Y_{\rho}Y_{i}^{\rm eq}}{Y_{j}^{\rm eq}Y_{\rho}^{\rm eq}}\bigg].

(63b)

Due to the Sommerfeld or gauge coupling suppression, the
X_{i}X_{j}\rightarrow\rho V_{\mu}
interactions contribute only marginally to the right-hand side of
Eq.

62
. Consequently, the
\rho-
number asymmetry is primarily driven by the decays
X_{2}\to\bar{X}_{1}\rho
and the
critical
scatterings
X_{i}V_{\mu}\leftrightarrow\bar{X}_{j}\rho
. However, taking into account their temperature dependence —
\langle\Gamma_{2\to\rho\bar{1}}\rangle/s\sim x^{3}
for decays and
\langle\sigma v_{\text{M\o l}}\rangle_{iV_{\mu}\to\bar{j}\rho}\sim x
for scatterings — it follows that decays eventually dominate the asymmetry generation at late times.

Owing to the fact that
F_{{\rm WO}}
remains non-negative throughout the evolution, the term
F_{{\rm WO}}\cdot\Delta_{\rho}
always tends to erase the asymmetry. Therefore, the generation of asymmetry requires a non-zero contribution from
\beta-\bar{\beta}
, which evolves according to the equation

\displaystyle\frac{d(\beta-\bar{\beta})}{dx}

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{ds/dx}{3H(x)}\Bigg(\mathcal{S}_{1}+\mathcal{S}_{2}+\mathcal{S}_{3}+\text{higher-order terms}\bigg),

(64)

where the source terms are defined in
Eq.

65
. To reduce complexity, in the expression above, we have omitted contributions from long-range annihilations
[X_{i}\bar{X}_{j}\leftrightarrow\rho\rho^{(*)}]
and flavour-changing scatterings
[X_{i}\bar{X}_{j}\leftrightarrow X_{i^{\prime}}\bar{X}_{j^{\prime}}]
, as well as charge-2 Sommerfeld-suppressed interactions
[X_{i}X_{j}\leftrightarrow X_{i^{\prime}}X_{j^{\prime}}]
.
These processes involve at least one pair of non-relativistic particles and typically yield subleading contributions.

In contrast, the dominant source of CP asymmetry arises from
super-critical
scatterings (
\mathcal{S}_{3}

\sim-\frac{ds/dx}{3H(x)}\langle\sigma v_{\text{M\o l}}\rangle^{\rm tree}_{2\rho\to 1\rho}(\epsilon_{2\rho\to 1\rho}-\epsilon_{\bar{2}\rho\to\bar{1}\rho})(\gamma+\bar{\gamma})Y_{\rho}^{\rm eq}

Eq.

64

\mathcal{S}_{1},\mathcal{S}_{2}

\rho-

\Delta_{\rho}=0
and
\beta-\bar{\beta}=0

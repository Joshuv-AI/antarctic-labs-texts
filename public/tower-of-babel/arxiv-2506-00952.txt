Abstract
The class-breadth conjecture of Leedham-Green, Neumann and Wiegold states that for each p p -group, c ​ l ​ ( G ) ≤ b ⁡ ( G ) + 1 cl(G)\leq b(G)+1 , where c ​ l ​ ( G ) cl(G) , b ⁡ ( G ) b(G) denote the nilpotency class and the breadth of G G . While several counter-examples to this conjecture have been found for p = 2 p=2 , it is still open in general for p > 2 p>2 . This article is dedicated to one simplified version of the conjecture for p > 2 p>2 .

Introduction

Let
G
be a finite
p
-group. The breadth
b(x)
of an element
x
of a group
G
is defined as
b(x):=\log_{p}|G:Z_{G}(x)|
, where
Z_{G}(x)
denotes the centralizer of
x
in
G
. The breadth
b(G)
of
G
is defined as
\displaystyle b(G):=\max_{g\in G}b(g)
.

C. Leedham-Green, P. Neumann and J. Wiegold in
[
7
]
stated the following conjecture:
cl(G)\leq b(G)+1
, for all
p
-groups
G
(
cl(G)
denotes the nilpotency class of
G
).

W. Felsch, J. Neubüser and W. Plesken in
[
3
]
provide the series of counter-examples to the conjecture for
p=2
. Further counter-examples were found by B. Eick, M. Newman and E. O’Brien in
[
2
]
, in
[
2
]
they also proved that the analogue of the conjecture for finite dimensional nilpotent Lie algebras is false for all finite fields, i.e., that for every finite field
\mathbb{F}
there exists a finite dimensional nilpotent Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
over
\mathbb{F}
such that
cl(\mathfrak{g})>b(\mathfrak{g})+1
, where
\displaystyle b(\mathfrak{g}):=\max_{a\in\mathfrak{g}}(\dim\mathfrak{g}-\dim Z_{\mathfrak{g}}(a))
,
Z_{\mathfrak{g}}(a):=\{x\in\mathfrak{g}\>|\>[a,x]=0\}
. The conjecture has been proved in some special cases, see, for example: if
b(G)\leq 4
in
[
6
,
8
]
, if
b(G)\leq p+1
in
[
4
]
, if
G
is a
p
-group of maximal class in
[
1
]
, if
G
is metabelian in
[
7
]
, if
G
is not covered by its two-step centralizers in
[
7
]
. See also
[
2
]
for more classes of
p
-groups satisfying the conjecture. A more detailed description of the results related to the class-breadth conjecture can be found in the introduction of
[
3
]
.

This note is dedicated to the general case
p>2
of the conjecture.

Main theorem

Consider a
p
-group
G
. The commutator
[x,y],x,y\in G
is
x^{-1}y^{-1}xy
. For each subgroups
G_{1},G_{2}
of
G
, denote by
[G_{1},G_{2}]
the subgroup of
G
which is generated with the set of elements
\{[g_{1},g_{2}]\>|\>g_{1}\in G_{1},g_{2}\in G_{2}\}
. For each element
g\in G
and each subgroup
H
of
G
, denote
b_{H}(g):=\log_{p}|H:H\cap Z_{G}(g)|
,
[g,H]:=\{[g,h]\>|\>h\in H\}
,
\displaystyle Z_{G}(H):=\bigcap_{h\in H}Z_{G}(h)
,
\gamma_{1}(H):=H
,
\gamma_{i+1}(H):=[\gamma_{i}(H),H]
,
i\geq 1
– denotes the lower central series of
H
. So
b_{H}(g)=\log_{p}|[g,H]|
. For each subset
X
of
G
, denote by
\langle X\rangle
the subgroup of
G
which is generated with the set
X
.

The main result of this article is the following Theorem 1:

Theorem 1
.

Let
p>2
be a prime number. Consider arbitrary integers
n\geq 0
,
m\geq 1
, an arbitrary
p
-group
G
, and any normal subgroups
H,C_{1},C_{2},\ldots,C_{p-1}
of
G
that satisfies:

1.

C_{i}\subsetneq H
,
\forall 1\leq i\leq p-1
(i.e.
C_{1},C_{2},\ldots,C_{p-1}
are proper subgroups of
H
),

2.

\displaystyle\max_{g\in H\setminus\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{p-1}C_{i}\right)}b_{\gamma_{m}(H)}(g)\leq n
.

Then there exists a normal subgroup
N
of
G
such that:

1.

N\subseteq H
,

2.

\log_{p}|H:N|\leq n
,

3.

cl(N)\leq n+m
,

4.

\displaystyle N\nsubseteq\bigcup_{i=1}^{p-1}C_{i}
.

As a consequence, for each
p
-group
G\not=\{e\}
with
p>2
, after the application of Theorem 1 to
n=b(G)
,
m=1
,
G
,
H=G
,
C_{1}=C_{2}=\ldots=C_{p-1}=\{e\}
, the following simplified version of the class-breadth conjecture can be proved:

Theorem 2
.

Let
p>2
be a prime number. Consider a
p
-group
G\not=\{e\}
. Then there exists a normal subgroup
N
of
G
such that
\log_{p}|G:N|\leq b(G)
and
cl(N)\leq b(G)+1
.

Lemma 1
.

Let
G
be a
p
-group. Consider any normal subgroups
C_{1}
,
C_{2}
of
G
such that
C_{1}\subseteq C_{2}
. Let
P:=\{g\in G\>|\>[g,C_{2}]\subseteq[C_{1},G]\}
. Then

1.

\{g\in G\>|\>b_{C_{1}}(g)\geq b_{C_{2}}(g)\}\subseteq P
,

2.

P
is a normal subgroup of
G
. Moreover if
C_{1}
,
C_{2}
are characteristic subgroups of
G
, then
P
is also characteristic in
G
,

3.

if
P=G
, then
[C_{1},G]=[C_{2},G]
.

Proof.

To prove 1, notice that
b_{C_{1}}(g)\leq b_{C_{2}}(g)
,
\forall g\in G
, therefore, if
g\in\{g\in G\>|\>b_{C_{1}}(g)\geq b_{C_{2}}(g)\}
, then
b_{C_{1}}(g)=b_{C_{2}}(g)
and
[g,C_{2}]=[g,C_{1}]\subseteq[C_{1},G]
, so
g\in P
. To prove 2 notice that
P=\pi^{-1}(Z_{\pi(G)}(\pi(C_{2})))
, where
\pi:G\to G/[C_{1},G]
denotes the homomorphism of factorization. To prove 3, notice that if
P=G
, then
[C_{1},G]\subseteq[C_{2},G]=[G,C_{2}]\subseteq[C_{1},G]
.
∎

Lemma 2
.

Let
G
be a
p
-group. Consider any normal subgroups
C_{1}
,
C_{2}
of
G
such that
C_{1}\subsetneq C_{2}
. Then there exists a normal subgroup
C_{3}
of
G
such that
C_{1}\subseteq C_{3}\subseteq C_{2}
and
|C_{2}:C_{3}|=p
.

Proof.

Consider the subgroup
C_{1}[C_{2},G]:=\langle\{xy\>|\>x\in C_{1},y\in[C_{2},G]\}\rangle
of
G
. Easy to check that
C_{1}[C_{2},G]
is a normal subgroup of
G
and
C_{1}[C_{2},G]\subseteq C_{2}
. From
C_{1}\subsetneq C_{2}
, it follows that
\{e\}\subsetneq C_{2}/C_{1}\subseteq G/C_{1}
, therefore, since
G/C_{1}
is nilpotent,
C_{1}[C_{2},G]=[C_{2}/C_{1},G/C_{1}]C_{1}\subsetneq C_{2}
. Consider any maximal under the inclusion proper subgroup
C_{3}
of
C_{2}
such that
C_{1}[C_{2},G]\subseteq C_{3}
. Then (from maximality of
C_{3}
in
C_{2}
)
|C_{2}:C_{3}|=p
. From
[C_{3},G]\subseteq[C_{2},G]\subseteq C_{1}[C_{2},G]\subseteq C_{3}
and
C_{1}\subseteq C_{1}[C_{2},G]\subseteq C_{3}
, it follows that
C_{3}
is a normal subgroup of
G
that satisfies
C_{1}\subseteq C_{3}\subseteq C_{2}
and
|C_{2}:C_{3}|=p
.
∎

Lemma 3
.

Let
G
be a
p
-group. Consider any normal subgroups
H
,
C_{1}
,
C_{2}
of
G
such that
C_{1}\not=C_{2}
,
C_{1},C_{2}\subseteq H
, and
|H:C_{1}|=|H:C_{2}|=p
. Then each subgroup
C
of
H
that satisfies
C_{1}\cap C_{2}\subseteq C
is normal in
G
.

Proof.

For each
1\leq i\leq 2
, consider the homomorphism of factorization
\pi_{i}:G\to G/C_{i}
. From
|H:C_{1}|=|H:C_{2}|=p
, it follows that
\pi_{i}(H)
is a normal subgroup of
\pi_{i}(G)
of order
p
, therefore
\pi_{i}(H)\subseteq Z(\pi_{i}(G))
and
[H,G]\subseteq C_{i}
,
\forall 1\leq i\leq 2
. So
[C,G]\subseteq[H,G]\subseteq C_{1}\cap C_{2}\subseteq C
and
C
is normal in
G
.
∎

Lemma 4
.

Let
G
be a
p
-group. Consider any proper subgroups
C_{1},C_{2},\ldots,C_{p}
of
G
such that
C_{1}\not=C_{2}
and
|G:C_{1}|=|G:C_{2}|=p
. Then there exists a normal subgroup
C
of
G
such that
|G:C|=p
,
C\cap C_{1}=C\cap C_{2}=C_{1}\cap C_{2}
, and
C\nsubseteq C_{i}
,
\forall 1\leq i\leq p
.

Proof.

From
|G:C_{1}|=|G:C_{2}|=p
,
C_{1}\not=C_{2}
, it follows that
C_{1},C_{2}
are normal subgroups of
G
and
G/(C_{1}\cap C_{2})\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p}\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. Consider the homomorphism of factorization
\pi:G\to G/(C_{1}\cap C_{2})
. Assume for convenience that for an integer
k\geq 2
,
C_{1}\cap C_{2}\nsubseteq C_{i}
,
\forall 2<i\leq k
and
C_{1}\cap C_{2}\subseteq C_{i}
,
\forall i>k
(
\{C_{i}\}_{2<i\leq k}=\varnothing
if
k=2
). Group
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{p}
contains
p+1
pairwise different normal subgroups of index
p
, therefore there exists a normal subgroup
N
of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{p}
such that
|\mathbb{Z}_{p}\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{p}:N|=p
and
N\nsubseteq C_{i}/(C_{1}\cap C_{2})

\forall i\in\{1,2\}\cup\{j\in\mathbb{N}\>|\>j>k\}

C:=\pi^{-1}(N)
satisfies the conditions of Lemma 4.
∎

Theorem 3
.

Let
p>2
be a prime number. Consider an arbitrary integer
n\geq 0
, an arbitrary
p
-group
G
, and any normal subgroups
C_{1},C_{2},\ldots,C_{p-1}
of
G
that satisfies:

1.

C_{i}\subsetneq G
,
\forall 1\leq i\leq p-1
,

2.

\displaystyle\max_{g\in G\setminus\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{p-1}C_{i}\right)}b(g)\leq n
.

Then there exists a normal subgroup
N
of
G
such that:

1.

\log_{p}|G:N|\leq n(n+2)
,

2.

\displaystyle N\nsubseteq\bigcup_{i=1}^{p-1}C_{i}
,

3.

\displaystyle cl(N)\leq 1+\max_{g\in N\setminus\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{p-1}C_{i}\right)}b_{N}(g)
.

Proof.

Define a sequence of normal subgroups
N_{0},N_{1},N_{2},\ldots
of
G

1.

N_{0}:=G
,

2.

assume that normal subgroups
N_{0},N_{1},\ldots,N_{k}
of
G

N_{j}\nsubseteq\bigcup_{i=1}^{p-1}C_{i}
,
\forall 0\leq j\leq k
. From Theorem 1, applied to
\displaystyle n=\max_{g\in N_{k}\setminus\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{p-1}C_{i}\right)}b_{N_{k}}(g)
,
m=1
,
H=N_{k}
,
C_{1}\cap N_{k},C_{2}\cap N_{k},\ldots,C_{p-1}\cap N_{k}
, it follows that there exists a normal subgroup
N_{k+1}
of

(a)

N_{k+1}\subseteq N_{k}
,

(b)

\displaystyle\log_{p}|N_{k}:N_{k+1}|\leq\max_{g\in N_{k}\setminus\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{p-1}C_{i}\right)}b_{N_{k}}(g)
,

(c)

\displaystyle cl(N_{k+1})\leq 1+\max_{g\in N_{k}\setminus\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{p-1}C_{i}\right)}b_{N_{k}}(g)
,

(d)

N_{k+1}\nsubseteq\bigcup_{i=1}^{p-1}C_{i}
.

Therefore, from the fact that
N_{0}\supseteq N_{1}\supseteq N_{2}\supseteq\ldots
, it follows that

n\geq\max_{g\in G\setminus\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{p-1}C_{i}\right)}b(g)=\max_{g\in N_{0}\setminus\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{p-1}C_{i}\right)}b_{N_{0}}(g)\geq\max_{g\in N_{1}\setminus\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{p-1}C_{i}\right)}b_{N_{1}}(g)\geq\max_{g\in N_{2}\setminus\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{p-1}C_{i}\right)}b_{N_{2}}(g)\geq\ldots\geq 0.

So for some integer

,

\max_{g\in N_{l+1}\setminus\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{p-1}C_{i}\right)}b_{N_{l+1}}(g)=\max_{g\in N_{l}\setminus\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{p-1}C_{i}\right)}b_{N_{l}}(g).

(2.1)

From properties (b), (c), (d),
(2.1)
, and the fact that
\displaystyle\max_{g\in N_{k}\setminus\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{p-1}C_{i}\right)}b_{N_{k}}(g)\leq n
,
\forall k\geq 0
, it follows that:

1)

\displaystyle\log_{p}|G:N_{l+1}|=\sum_{k=0}^{l}\log_{p}|N_{k}:N_{k+1}|\leq\sum_{k=0}^{l}\max_{g\in N_{k}\setminus\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{p-1}C_{i}\right)}b_{N_{k}}(g)\leq(n+2)n
,

2)

\displaystyle N_{l+1}\nsubseteq\bigcup_{i=1}^{p-1}C_{i}
,

3)

\displaystyle cl(N_{l+1})\leq 1+\max_{g\in N_{l}\setminus\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{p-1}C_{i}\right)}b_{N_{l}}(g)=1+\max_{g\in N_{l+1}\setminus\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{p-1}C_{i}\right)}b_{N_{l+1}}(g)
.

I.e.
N_{l+1}
satisfies all the conditions of Theorem 3.
∎

Complement receptor C3aR marks heterogeneous tumor‐associated macrophage states associated with improved survival in IDH‐wildtype glioblastoma
Authors: Marion Imara, Yosra Bedoui, Théotime Pignolet, Sebastien Freppel, Camille Brochard, Auguste Delattre, David Cappellen, Luc Bauchet, Mohamed Khettab, Franck Ah‐Pine
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractGlioblastomas (GBM), IDH‐wildtype, are highly aggressive brain tumors with poor prognosis and inter‐individual variability despite established prognostic factors such as age, performance status, and MGMT promoter (MGMTp) methylation. Increasing evidence highlights the role of the tumor microenvironment (TME) in GBM progression. The complement system, a key component of innate immunity, can influence tumor growth and immune regulation through the generation of the anaphylatoxin C3a and the activation of its receptor, C3aR. While C3a/C3aR signaling has been associated with various outcomes in several malignancies, its prognostic relevance in GBM remains poorly defined. This study investigated the expression of C3aR in human IDH‐wildtype GBM and its association with clinicopathological features and overall survival. We retrospectively analyzed a cohort of 114 patients with IDH‐wildtype GBM. C3aR expression was assessed by immunohistochemistry and quantified using digital image analysis. C3aR expression was analyzed as a categorical variable using an outcome‐derived cutpoint (C3aR‐high and C3aR‐low) for exploratory purposes. Associations with clinical variables, including MGMTp methylation status, were evaluated. C3aR expression showed marked inter‐tumoral heterogeneity and was enriched among MGMTp‐methylated tumors (p = 0.004). Patients with C3aR‐high tumors exhibited improved overall survival (log‐rank p = 0.033). In a multivariable Cox regression model including age, performance status, surgery type, and MGMTp methylation status, high C3aR expression remained independently associated with improved overall survival (HR 0.50, 95% CI 0.29–0.87, p = 0.014). Tissue‐based and single‐cell analyses supported the association of C3aR with the myeloid/macrophage compartment and heterogeneous macrophage phenotypes. These findings support a relationship between C3aR expression and the heterogeneous macrophage landscape of IDH‐wildtype GBM, while the survival association remains exploratory and requires independent validation.Keywords: C3aR, complement, glioblastoma, prognostic biomarker, tumor microenvironment

INTRODUCTION

Glioblastomas (GBM) isocitrate dehydrogenase‐wildtype (IDHwt) are adult‐type diffuse gliomas that represent the most frequent and the most aggressive form of glial tumors. Their prognosis remains poor, with a median overall survival of 15–18 months despite maximal surgical resection followed by radiotherapy combined with temozolomide‐based chemotherapy [1]. Several well‐established prognostic factors are used in clinical practice and incorporated in the WHO classification: younger age (<50 years), good performance status, complete tumor resection, and O6‐methylguanine‐DNA methyltransferase promoter (MGMTp) methylation are associated with improved survival and response to therapy [1]. However, marked inter‐individual heterogeneity in prognosis persists, for reasons that remain incompletely understood.

Tumors, including GBM, consist of malignant cells developing within a complex tumor microenvironment (TME) composed of various cell types (immune cells, fibroblasts, vascular cells), soluble factors, signaling molecules, and extracellular matrix components. The TME is a dynamic structure that plays a major role in tumor growth and invasion through reciprocal interactions with tumor cells. GBM microenvironment is characterized by a prominent infiltration of macrophages, which can represent up to 50% of the tumor mass and exhibit diverse activation states with distinct biological functions [2, 3]. Increasing evidence indicates that the complement system within the TME can play a critical role in tumorigenesis by acting at several key stages of tumor progression, including angiogenesis, tumor cell proliferation, and immune regulation [4, 5, 6]. The complement system is a key component of innate immunity, protecting the host against pathogens through opsonization, chemotaxis, and cell lysis via the generation of C3a and C5a anaphylatoxins. Because of these properties, it was long considered to contribute to anti‐tumor immune responses. However, accumulating evidence indicates that the biological and prognostic impact of complement activation is more complex and highly context‐dependent. Indeed, both pro‐ and anti‐tumoral effects of C3a/C3a receptor (C3aR) signaling have been reported, depending on tumor type, experimental setting, and cellular compartment involved [7, 8]. In several cancer types, including lung, renal, and soft tissue cancers, complement activation, C3a/C3aR and/or C5a/C5aR signaling, has been associated with poor prognosis [9, 10, 11, 12]. Conversely, in murine models of breast carcinoma, C3aR agonism demonstrated a protective role by enhancing T cell response [13].

To date, only a limited number of studies have investigated the expression of C3a and C3aR in the context of gliomas. Previous work has shown that human astrocytoma cell lines can express and secrete C3a, and that this expression is regulated by IL‐1β, TNFα, and IFN‐γ [14, 15]. Subsequently, deposition of C3 (C3a precursor) was observed in human GBM tumor tissue, suggesting a role for complement in GBM pathogenesis [16]. In a recent study conducted on human brain tumor samples, we demonstrated by immunohistochemistry (IHC) that C3aR is overexpressed in WHO grade 4 adult‐type diffuse gliomas (IDHwt GBM and IDH‐mutant grade 4 astrocytomas) compared with other brain tumors. C3aR expression was mainly detected in intra‐tumoral macrophages expressing CD68, CD18, CD163, and VEGF [17]. Recently, transcriptomic data from patients with GBM showed that C3aR was associated with aggressive disease and shorter survival [18]. Our study aims to quantify C3aR protein expression by IHC and investigate its association with known prognostic factors and overall survival in human GBM.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Patients and tumor samples

We retrieved 114 GBM tissue samples from the tumor tissue bank of the teaching hospital (CHU) of La Reunion (French government CRB registry: DC‐2016‐145‐2860) for which a diagnosis of GBM was made between 2018 and 2024 (Figure 1). All cases met the following criteria: (1) histopathologic diagnosis of GBM, IDHwt, according to the 2021 WHO classification, (2) age at diagnosis, 18 years and older, (3) available formalin‐fixed, paraffin‐embedded (FFPE) samples, (4) samples issued from the tumor tissue bank, and (5) available clinical information. All cases underwent histopathological review by an experienced neuropathologist (FA), together with assessment of the immunohistochemical and molecular diagnostic criteria available at the time of diagnosis.

Immunohistochemical analysis of C3aR, scoring and tumor classification

FFPE sections were cut at 4 μm and processed on the Leica Biosystems BOND‐III Automated Staining System, according to the manufacturer's instructions (Leica Biosystems). In all 114 GBM tumors, we evaluated the expression of C3aR (clone D12, 1:200 dilution; Santa Cruz). Detection was performed using the BOND Polymer Refine Detection Kit (Ref Ds9800; Leica). Quantification of C3aR expression was carried out using QuPath software (version 0.6.0). Whole‐slide sections were reviewed and a tumor region of interest was delineated to exclude necrosis, hemorrhage, and non‐tumoral tissue. For each case, four non‐overlapping high‐power fields (HPF, x200, area of field of view 0.26 mm2) were randomly selected blinded to clinical outcome. Digital pathology‐based quantification was used to provide objective and reproducible measurement of C3aR‐positive cell counts. C3aR‐positive cells were automatically detected and counted using a customized QuPath pipeline (available upon request). The C3aR score was defined as the sum of C3aR‐positive cells across the four HPF. Automated detections were visually reviewed to ensure accuracy. Fields considered problematic during computational analysis were independently reviewed by two pathologists (FA and CBR), and discrepancies were resolved by joint review and consensus.

Intratumoral heterogeneity of C3aR‐positive cell counts was assessed across the non‐overlapping HPF evaluated for each tumor. Field‐to‐field variability was defined as the range between the lowest and highest counts and compared between biopsies and surgical resections using the Wilcoxon rank‐sum test.

Given the highly skewed distribution of C3aR‐positive cell counts, C3aR expression was analyzed both as a continuous variable and as a categorical variable. For exploratory survival analyses, tumors were dichotomized into C3aR‐low and C3aR‐high using an outcome‐derived cutpoint (18 positive cells) identified by maximally selected log‐rank statistics. This cutpoint was used as a stratification tool and should be interpreted as exploratory, requiring validation in independent cohorts.

Tissue microarray construction and double immunohistochemistry

Tissue microarrays (TMA) were constructed from FFPE tumor samples of 35 patients who underwent surgical resection and had sufficient residual tumor material. For each patient, two representative tumor cores were sampled from the corresponding FFPE block and embedded into recipient paraffin blocks. Two placental tissue samples were included as controls. Of the 35 patients included in the TMA, 33 had evaluable quantitative data for all macrophage‐associated markers and were included in the correlation analyses.

For histological and immunohistochemical analyses, 4‐μm‐thick sections were obtained from the TMA blocks. Hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining was first performed to assess the representativeness and quality of the sampled tissue areas.

For quantitative analysis, single immunohistochemical staining for C3aR (clone D12, 1:100 dilution; Santa Cruz), CD68 (clone 514H12, ready‐to‐use; Leica), CD163 (clone 10D6, 1:200 dilution; Leica), CD209 (clone B‐2, dilution 1:500; Santa Cruz), CD206 (clone D‐1, dilution 1:200; Santa Cruz), HLA‐DR (clone CR3/43, dilution 1/10000; Santa Cruz), and Arginase I (clone ep‐201, ready‐to‐use; Bio SB) was performed on serial TMA sections. Positive cells were quantified using QuPath. For each marker, the mean positive‐cell count from the two tumor cores was calculated for each patient and used as the patient‐level value for subsequent analyses.

Double immunohistochemical staining was then performed using an anti‐C3aR antibody (clone D12, 1:100 dilution; Santa Cruz) in combination with antibodies against the following macrophage‐associated markers: CD68 (clone 514H12, ready‐to‐use; Leica), CD163 (clone 10D6, 1:200 dilution; Leica), CD209 (clone B‐2, dilution 1:500; Santa Cruz), CD206 (clone D‐1, dilution 1:200; Santa Cruz), HLA‐DR (clone CR3/43, dilution 1/10000; Santa Cruz), and Arginase I (clone ep‐201, ready‐to‐use; Bio SB). Detection was performed using the Bond Polymer Refine Detection Kit (DS9800; Leica) for brown chromogen staining and the Bond Polymer Refine Red Detection Kit (DS9390; Leica) for red chromogen staining.

MGMT promoter methylation status

MGMTp methylation was assessed after sodium bisulfite conversion. Until May 2020, methylation‐specific quantitative PCR using methylation‐specific primers and a TaqMan probe was performed. From May 2020 onwards, methylation‐sensitive high‐resolution melting (EpiMelt™ MGMT MS‐HRM assay, MethylDetect®, run on a ROCHE®LC480™ qPCR machine, as specified by the manufacturer) was used, with bisulfite sequencing performed in selected cases when required. For statistical analyses, MGMTp methylation status was considered either as a three‐level categorical variable (methylated, unmethylated, or not tested) or restricted to cases with known methylation status, depending on the analysis. As an exploratory external analysis, C3AR1 mRNA expression was compared according to MGMTp status in TCGA‐GBM cases with available transcriptomic data and MGMT‐STP27 classification, as previously described by Bady et al. [19].

Public single‐cell and bulk transcriptomic dataset analyses

Publicly available single‐cell RNA‐sequencing data from three independent human GB datasets (Darmanis et al., Neftel et al., and Wang et al.) were analyzed using the Curated Cancer Cell Atlas (3CA, https://www.weizmann.ac.il/sites/3CA/) [20, 21, 22]. C3AR1 expression was assessed across annotated malignant and myeloid/macrophage populations and in relation to M1‐ and M2‐associated polarization, resident microglial, monocyte/inflammatory, and tumor‐associated macrophage (TAM) transcriptional programs. As a complementary bulk transcriptomic analysis, TCGA‐GBM PanCancer Atlas cases with available mRNA expression data (n = 160), obtained through cBioPortal, were analyzed to assess the relationship between C3AR1 expression and macrophage‐associated transcriptional programs [23, 24, 25].

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using R software (version 4.5.2). Continuous variables were summarized using medians and interquartile ranges, and categorical variables using frequencies and percentages. Comparisons of C3aR expression between groups were performed using non‐parametric tests (Wilcoxon rank‐sum or Kruskal–Wallis tests, as appropriate). Associations between categorical variables were assessed using chi‐square tests. Associations between C3aR expression levels and continuous clinical variables were evaluated using Spearman's rank correlation coefficient. Associations between C3aR‐positive cell counts and macrophage‐associated markers (CD68, HLA‐DR, CD206, CD163, Arginase I, and CD209) were assessed using Spearman's rank correlation coefficient. p‐values were adjusted across these six comparisons using the Benjamini–Hochberg procedure. Within single‐cell datasets, Spearman correlations were used to assess associations between C3AR1 expression and macrophage‐associated transcriptional programs. p‐values were adjusted for multiple comparisons within each dataset using the Benjamini–Hochberg procedure. As a sensitivity analysis, correlations with composite transcriptional scores were assessed at the sample level in samples containing ≥20 myeloid/macrophage cells. In TCGA‐GBM, M1‐like, M2‐like, TAM‐associated, and macrophage abundance transcriptional scores were calculated from predefined gene sets. To account for macrophage abundance, C3AR1 expression was residualized against the macrophage abundance score using linear regression, and Spearman correlations between residual C3AR1 expression and macrophage‐associated transcriptional scores were subsequently calculated. p‐values were adjusted using the Benjamini–Hochberg procedure. Overall survival was analyzed using the Kaplan–Meier method, with differences between groups assessed by the log‐rank test. Overall survival was defined as the time from initial surgery to death from any cause. Patients alive at last follow‐up were censored at that date. Cox proportional hazards regression models were used for univariate and multivariable survival analyses. Variables with p < 0.20 in univariate analyses were considered for inclusion in multivariable models. The proportional hazards assumption was assessed using Schoenfeld residuals. No violation of the proportional hazards assumption was observed for C3aR expression in categorical models. All statistical tests were two‐sided, and p‐values <0.05 were considered statistically significant.

RESULTS

A total of 114 adult patients diagnosed with IDHwt GBM were included in the study. Clinical and molecular characteristics are summarized in Table 1. The median age at diagnosis was 62.4 years (range, 33.9–81.7 years). The male‐to‐female ratio was 1.59. Performance status (PS) at diagnosis was available for the majority of the included patients (n = 108, 94.7%). Most patients had a surgical resection (n = 72, 63.2%). MGMT promoter methylation status was available for 81 patients and classified as methylated (n = 31), unmethylated (n = 50), and not tested for 33 patients.

C3aR expression in glioblastomas shows marked inter‐tumoral heterogeneity

C3aR expression, quantified by digital image analysis as the sum of C3aR‐positive cells across four HPF, exhibited marked inter‐tumoral heterogeneity, with a median of 25.5 (range, 0–3346) (Figure 1A). C3aR‐positive cell counts also showed substantial field‐to‐field variability; however, intratumoral variability was comparable between biopsies (median range, 30.5 cells [IQR, 5.5–157]) and surgical resections (median range, 46 cells [IQR, 16.2–115]; p = 0.662). Using the outcome‐derived cut‐off value (18 C3aR+ cells), tumors were classified as C3aR‐high (n = 62) or C3aR‐low (n = 52) (Figure 1B–D).

C3aR expression is associated with heterogeneous macrophage phenotypes supported by single‐cell transcriptomic analysis

To further characterize C3aR‐expressing cells in IDH‐wildtype GBM, double immunohistochemical staining was performed for C3aR together with the myeloid/macrophage markers CD68, HLA‐DR, CD206, and CD163. C3aR‐positive cells showed partial co‐expression with these macrophage markers, supporting the association of C3aR expression with the myeloid/macrophage compartment (Figure 2A).

Quantitative analyses further showed a strong positive correlation between C3aR‐positive and CD68‐positive cell counts (Spearman r = 0.720, q < 0.001). A moderate positive correlation was also observed with HLA‐DR‐positive cell counts (r = 0.449, q = 0.027). The association with CD206‐positive cell counts was nominally significant (r = 0.380, p = 0.029) but did not remain significant after correction for multiple testing (q = 0.059). No significant correlation was observed with CD163‐positive cell counts (r = 0.247, q = 0.248) (Figure 2B). Similarly, no significant correlations were observed with Arginase I‐positive (r = 0.064, q = 0.726) or CD209‐positive cell counts (r = 0.130, q = 0.563) (Supporting Information File S1). These findings demonstrate a strong association between C3aR expression and the macrophage compartment, particularly CD68‐positive cells, while the variable relationships with individual macrophage‐associated markers suggest that C3aR expression does not simply recapitulate a single canonical macrophage phenotype.

To further characterize C3AR1‐expressing cells, we analyzed three independent human GB single‐cell RNA‐sequencing datasets. C3AR1 expression was strongly enriched in the myeloid/macrophage compartment compared with malignant cells across all three datasets (Figure 2C). Within myeloid/macrophage cells, C3AR1 expression was associated with heterogeneous transcriptional programs, including resident microglial and TAM‐related markers, without a consistent canonical M1‐ or M2‐like polarization pattern across datasets (Figure 2C; Supporting Information File S2). This heterogeneity was also observed at the sample level, with the strongest associations involving a microglial signature in Neftel (r = 0.81) and M2‐ and TAM‐associated signatures in Wang (r = 0.86 and 0.79, respectively) (Supporting Information File S2).

Consistently, in TCGA‐GBM bulk transcriptomic data, C3AR1 remained modestly associated with M1‐, M2‐, and TAM‐associated transcriptional scores after adjustment for macrophage abundance, with the strongest residual association observed for the M2‐associated program (r = 0.26, q = 0.002) (Supporting Information File S3).

C3aR‐high group is associated with MGMT promoter methylation

C3aR expression differed significantly according to MGMTp methylation status (Kruskal–Wallis test, p = 0.02) (Figure 3A). When C3aR expression was categorized into low (≤18) and high (>18) groups, a significant association with MGMT status was observed (chi‐square test, p = 0.004), with enrichment of C3aR‐high tumors among MGMT‐methylated cases (Figure 3B).

C3aR expression levels were not significantly associated with age (Spearman p = 0.10), performance status (Spearman p = 0.83), sex (Wilcoxon p = 0.40), or surgery type (Wilcoxon p = 0.48).

In an exploratory analysis of TCGA‐GBM cases with available MGMT‐STP27 classification, C3AR1 expression did not significantly differ between MGMTp‐unmethylated (n = 64) and MGMTp‐methylated tumors (n = 49; median expression, 776 vs. 651, respectively; Wilcoxon p = 0.396).

C3aR expression is associated with increased overall survival

Kaplan–Meier survival analysis performed in patients with available performance status (n = 108) revealed a significant difference in overall survival according to C3aR expression group (Figure 4A). Patients with C3aR‐high tumors (n = 59) had improved overall survival compared with those with C3aR‐low tumors (n = 49) (log‐rank test, p = 0.033).

Sensitivity analyses using alternative categorizations of C3aR expression (median, tertiles) showed consistent effect directions but did not consistently reach statistical significance (Supporting Information File S4).

In multivariate analysis adjusting for age, performance status, and surgery type (n = 108), high C3aR expression remained independently associated with improved overall survival (HR 0.60, 95% CI 0.40–0.91, p = 0.017) (Figure 4B).

In a secondary multivariate model restricted to patients with known MGMTp methylation status (n = 76), C3aR expression remained independently associated with improved overall survival (HR 0.50, 95% CI 0.29–0.87, p = 0.014), whereas MGMTp methylation showed a non‐significant trend toward improved survival (Figure 4C). Importantly, MGMT‐tested and non‐tested patients showed comparable age, performance status, sex, surgery type and overall survival (log‐rank p = 0.665).

In addition, no significant interaction was observed between C3aR expression and MGMTp methylation status (interaction HR = 1.11, 95% CI 0.32–3.84, p = 0.86), indicating that the prognostic impact of C3aR was not modified by MGMT status. In unadjusted Kaplan–Meier analyses stratified by MGMT promoter methylation status, C3aR expression was not significantly associated with overall survival within either MGMT‐unmethylated (log‐rank p = 0.20) or MGMT‐methylated tumors (log‐rank p = 0.29) (Supporting Information File S5).

In contrast, when C3aR expression was analyzed as a continuous variable using raw counts, log‐transformed values, or penalized spline models, no significant association with overall survival was observed in univariable or multivariable Cox models (all p > 0.1). Penalized spline analyses did not reveal a linear or monotonic relationship between C3aR expression and hazard of death, with wide confidence intervals across the range of expression values.

DISCUSSION

In this study, we investigated the association with clinical outcome of C3aR expression in a monocentric retrospective cohort of patients with IDHwt GBM. Extending our previous observations in a smaller series [17], we observed that C3aR expression showed marked inter‐tumoral heterogeneity and that high C3aR expression was independently associated with improved overall survival. Importantly, as this categorization relied on an outcome‐derived cutpoint, these findings should be considered exploratory and warrant validation in independent cohorts. In addition, detailed radiotherapy and chemotherapy treatment data were not systematically available in this retrospective cohort and could therefore not be incorporated into the survival analyses.

GBM are characterized by a specialized TME dominated by tumor‐associated macrophages (TAM), including resident microglia and infiltrating monocyte‐derived macrophages [26, 27, 28]. In this context, we have previously shown that C3aR expression in GBM is predominantly observed in TAM expressing CD68, CD163, CD18, and VEGF, whereas tumor cells and endothelial cells do not express C3aR [17]. Our previous observations showed that C3aR expression only partially overlapped with canonical macrophage markers, suggesting phenotypic heterogeneity among C3aR‐positive macrophages [17]. In the present cohort, these observations were further supported by double immunohistochemical staining and quantitative TMA analyses. C3aR‐positive cell counts were strongly correlated with CD68‐positive cell counts, whereas associations with other macrophage‐associated markers were more variable. Together with the partial co‐expression observed by double staining, these findings support a close relationship between C3aR expression and the macrophage compartment while indicating that C3aR does not uniformly parallel individual macrophage‐associated markers. This phenotypic heterogeneity is consistent with the heterogeneous macrophage transcriptional states identified in the single‐cell analyses. Our results highlight the importance of characterizing this macrophage subset to better understand its functional role in gliomagenesis and tumor progression.

Accumulating evidence indicates that the biological and prognostic impact of complement activation is highly context‐dependent. Comprehensive reviews and pan‐cancer analyses emphasize that complement components, including C3a and C3aR, may exert either pro‐ or anti‐tumoral effects depending on tumor type, cellular source of complement signaling, and microenvironmental context [7, 8, 29].

Several studies have investigated the functional role of C3a/C3aR signaling in experimental and preclinical models. In murine melanoma models, genetic deletion of C3aR resulted in delayed tumor growth and enhanced anti‐tumor immune responses, associated with reduced intratumoral macrophage infiltration [9]. Similarly, in murine sarcoma models, C3 or C3aR deficiency reduced primary sarcomagenesis and transplanted sarcoma growth, with C3aR predominantly expressed by tumor‐infiltrating myeloid cells, including macrophages and monocytes [12]. However, in murine models of breast carcinoma, C3aR agonism enhanced tumor control under specific experimental conditions [13].

In contrast, relatively few studies have examined the prognostic significance of C3a/C3aR expression in human cancer. Pan‐cancer transcriptomic studies have reported context‐dependent associations between the expression of C3AR1, which encodes C3aR, tumor immune features, and patient outcome across cancer types [4, 5, 7, 29]. Transcriptomic analyses identified C3aR as a marker associated with poor prognosis in human ovarian cancer and GBM [18, 30]. The apparent discrepancy between our current findings and previous studies further illustrates the context‐dependent nature of C3aR biology in GBM. In our previous study, C3aR expression was predominantly observed in TAMs displaying pro‐angiogenic features, leading us to hypothesize a tumor‐promoting role for this macrophage population [17]. However, this functional interpretation was based on phenotypic associations in a limited series and did not establish a direct relationship between C3aR expression and patient outcome. Similarly, previous bulk transcriptomic analyses associated higher C3AR1 expression with shorter survival in GBM [18]. Bulk C3AR1 expression, however, integrates both the abundance of C3AR1‐expressing cells and their transcriptional activity across the tumor and may therefore capture different biological information from spatial quantification of C3aR‐positive cells by IHC. Consistent with this interpretation, bulk C3AR1 expression in TCGA‐GBM was strongly associated with macrophage‐related transcriptional scores. These associations were substantially attenuated, but not entirely abolished, after adjustment for macrophage abundance, indicating that C3AR1 expression partly reflects the overall macrophage content of the tumor while retaining associations with macrophage transcriptional programs. Our complementary analyses of independent human single‐cell GBM datasets provide further insight into this apparent discrepancy. C3AR1 expression was predominantly localized to the myeloid compartment but was distributed across heterogeneous macrophage transcriptional states rather than defining a single canonical polarization phenotype. At the individual gene level, C3AR1 showed variable associations with markers related to M1‐like, M2‐like, resident microglial, monocyte, and TAM across datasets, further emphasizing the absence of a uniform macrophage phenotype associated with C3AR1 expression.

Thus, C3aR expression should not be interpreted as a unidirectional marker of pro‐tumoral macrophage activity, but rather in the context of the heterogeneous macrophage landscape of GBM.

An additional finding of interest is the enrichment of C3aR‐high tumors among MGMTp‐methylated GBM. While MGMTp methylation is classically viewed as a marker of DNA repair deficiency and treatment sensitivity, increasing evidence suggests that it may also capture broader biological features of the TME, including immune composition and inflammatory signaling. Previous studies have reported differential macrophage infiltration and immune‐related gene expression profiles according to MGMT methylation status [31, 32, 33]. The association observed in our cohort may therefore reflect broader differences in the immune landscape of MGMTp‐methylated and unmethylated tumors, rather than a direct relationship between MGMT methylation and C3aR signaling. Interestingly, this association was not reproduced in TCGA‐GBM, where bulk C3AR1 transcript levels did not differ according to MGMTp methylation status. This discrepancy may partly reflect the different biological information captured by spatial protein‐level assessment of C3aR‐positive cells and bulk tumor transcriptomics. Together, these observations suggest that the relationship between MGMTp status and C3aR‐positive immune populations may be context‐ and compartment‐dependent and warrant further investigation using spatially resolved approaches. Importantly, the association between C3aR expression and overall survival remained significant after adjustment for MGMTp methylation status, suggesting that the survival association observed in our cohort is not solely explained by MGMTp status.

CONCLUSIONS

Taken together, our findings highlight the complex and context‐dependent role of C3aR in the GBM microenvironment. C3aR‐positive cell counts were associated with improved survival in our cohort, although this association remains exploratory and requires validation in independent cohorts. Double immunohistochemical staining and quantitative analyses support the association of C3aR with the macrophage compartment, while the variable relationships with individual macrophage markers suggest phenotypic heterogeneity among C3aR‐positive cells. Complementary analyses of human single‐cell and bulk transcriptomic datasets further indicate that C3AR1 expression is closely linked to the myeloid compartment and distributed across heterogeneous macrophage transcriptional states, rather than defining a single canonical macrophage phenotype. These findings support C3aR as a marker associated with the heterogeneous macrophage landscape of IDH‐wildtype GBM and provide a rationale for further spatial and functional studies to determine the biological significance of C3aR‐expressing immune populations.

Pigmented Orocutaneous Fistula: A Diagnostic Dilemma Unmasked by Dermoscopy
Authors: Umberto Santaniello, Paolo Fava, Silvia Giordano, Francesco Cavallo, Simone Ribero, Pietro Quaglino
Published: 2026

Case Presentation

A 74-year-old female was referred to our department for evaluation of a solitary pigmented lesion in the right paramedian submental area, under clinical suspicion of skin neoplasm. The lesion had appeared three months before, with recurrent episodes of seropurulent discharge and crusting. On clinical examination, it measured approximately 6 mm in diameter, showing slight central depression and indistinct borders (Figure 1A). Dermoscopy revealed a structureless, brownish, pigmented area with subtle fibrillar-like pigmented structures at its periphery, displaying irregular orientation. The pigmented area was circumscribed by a pinkish background, unfocused dotted and linear vessels, and whitish structureless areas (Figure 1B). Importantly, no dermoscopic criterion suggestive of malignancy was identified. The lesion lacked hallmark features commonly seen in nonmelanoma skin cancers (NMSC) and melanoma [1]. Given the site of the lesion and its clinical and dermoscopic characteristics, a cutaneous sinus tract of odontogenic origin (orocutaneous fistula) was suspected. High-frequency ultrasonography subsequently confirmed the diagnosis, revealing a linear hypoechoic tract extending toward the mandibular bone (Figure 1C). The patient was referred to dental surgery for definitive treatment.

Teaching Point

Orocutaneous fistulas of odontogenic origin, typically resulting from chronic periapical periodontitis with progressive osteolysis and soft tissue drainage, may clinically mimic skin neoplasms or infectious diseases, representing a diagnostic pitfall [2]. Dermoscopy serves as a key diagnostic tool in this setting, allowing recognition of sinus-related inflammatory features and the absence of skin malignancy-specific structures. High-resolution ultrasound can corroborate the diagnosis [3]. Awareness of this clinical scenario is crucial to avoid misdiagnosis and overtreatment.

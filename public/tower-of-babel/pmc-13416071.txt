The impact of attention mechanism technology for intelligent text generation in traditional CAD
Authors: Shuangshuang Chen, Ellen Zhu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractMost of the existing studies on the intellectualization of traditional culture focus on fields such as image recognition and style transfer. The research on intelligent generation of traditional cultural art design (CAD) texts is scattered and lacks systematicness. To this end, this study conducts research on intelligent text generation in traditional CAD based on the attention mechanism. First, the research status and limitations of the attention mechanism, intelligent text generation, and digitalization of traditional culture are sorted out. Then, a multi-source dataset of traditional art design feature-text pairs is constructed. Next, an improved Transformer model is designed. Based on the standard encoder-decoder framework, a cultural semantic attention layer, a multi-scale temporal convolutional network style constraint module, and an output verification layer are added. Meanwhile, the dynamic weight calculation of the cultural semantic attention layer and the multi-scale feature fusion of the style module are optimized. The results show that the Bilingual Evaluation Understudy-3 (BLEU-3) score of the proposed Attention-based CAD (Attn-CAD) model reaches 0.72. The cultural terminology accuracy is 92.5%, and the style matching degree is 89.3%, with the best performance in the ceramic field. Ablation experiments verify the necessity of the core modules, and the decline rates of indicators in cross-domain tests are all less than 5%, which proves the model’s universality. In terms of methodology, this study solves the core problem of weak semantic association of the general model in traditional cultural scenarios and insufficient terminology accuracy of general models in traditional cultural scenarios; it provides technical support for the living inheritance and digital innovation of traditional culture.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-53255-3.Keywords: Attention mechanism, Traditional culture, Art design, Intelligent text generationSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Driven by both the “Digital China” strategy and the revival of traditional culture, traditional cultural art design (CAD) is transforming from “offline protection” to “online activation”. According to the China Intangible Cultural Heritage (ICH) Protection and Development Report (2024), there are more than 1,500 national-level ICH projects in the traditional craft category in China. However, digital text resources only cover 38% of these projects, and most of them are static descriptive content, lacking dynamically generated texts adapted to design scenarios1. At the same time, in natural language processing (NLP)2, breakthroughs in the attention mechanism provide technical support for intelligent text generation. Its characteristic of focusing on key information can accurately capture the proprietary semantics in traditional culture; this aims to solve issues of semantic fragmentation and weak cultural relevance in traditional generation models. Moreover, as a key spiritual treasure of the Chinese nation, traditional cultural art contains ideological connotations and artistic expressions. It has important value in the research of humanities and social sciences, such as literature, art, and design; it also has high practical significance in the context of the current vigorous development of the cultural industry3.

Although attention mechanisms and text generation technologies have gained a certain foundation of application, existing studies still have limitations. These limitations manifest as a shortage of specialized corpora for traditional CAD scenarios; most available data are static descriptive texts, lacking structured paired data bound to design features, with limited coverage of domains. General-purpose text generation models (such as standard Transformer and BERT-GPT) fail to fully account for the exclusivity of traditional cultural terminology and the uniqueness of stylistic features. They suffer from issues like terminology confusion (e.g., the interchangeable use of “underglaze red” and “underglaze color”) and inconsistent stylistic expression. Existing studies do not construct model architectures adapted to traditional cultural scenarios; integrating cultural semantics and text generation only stays at a superficial level, without forming systematic solutions. The study handles key limitations of general-purpose models by incorporating a cultural semantic attention layer, a style constraint module, and a terminology verification layer. These enhancements specifically tackle insufficient attention to traditional cultural terms, inconsistent writing styles in generated texts, and low accuracy in cultural terminology usage.

This study first systematically reviews the relevant research progress to achieve the above objectives; it clarifies the limitations of existing research and the entry point. Second, it designs the research model and elaborates in detail the cultural semantic-enhanced attention mechanism’s construction logic, the model’s overall data flow, and the core modules’ optimization strategy. At the same time, it conducts experimental design and performance evaluation. The study evaluates the proposed model’s performance from multiple dimensions by constructing an exclusive dataset, setting parameters in a specific hardware and software environment, and using baseline models as references. Ablation experiments are used to verify the core modules’ value, and cross-domain tests are employed to prove the model’s universality. Finally, this study summarizes the theoretical contributions and application value, analyzes the related limitations, and proposes future research directions. Through this logical chain, the study promotes the intelligent generation of traditional CAD texts from a technical perspective. Thus, this study offers new methodological support for the inheritance and innovative application of traditional culture.

Literature review

Research on the development and application of the attention mechanism: The attention mechanism originally originated from the study of human cognitive psychology. In deep learning, Bo et al. (2024) introduced an attention mechanism into the neural machine translation model; this effectively improved the translation performance4. The Transformer model proposed by Jiang et al. (2024) was based on the attention mechanism; its core multi-head self-attention structure significantly improved the parallel processing ability and semantic representation effect of text modeling5. At the application level, the research of Das et al. (2023) showed that attention mechanism was widely used in many tasks such as multimodal generation, sentiment analysis, speech recognition, and image description generation6.

Regarding the current research status of intelligent text generation, Belyanova et al. (2020) argued that with the growth of big data, more models could capture sequence context information. However, they still faced problems such as gradient vanishing when dealing with long texts7. Anton et al. (2021) indicated that intelligent text generation, as an important branch of NLP, covered multiple application directions, including machine translation and creative writing8. Zhen et al. (2023) noted that with the development of deep learning, Recurrent Neural Network (RNN), Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM), and Gated Recurrent Unit had gradually become mainstream generation methods9. Kwon et al. (2024) achieved significant breakthroughs in machine translation and text generation tasks through the multi-head self-attention mechanism10. In addition, Wu (2024) et al. combined reinforcement learning with human feedback in training, which further improved the controllability and quality of generated texts11.

Traditional cultural art is considered an important component of traditional culture. Xiang (2020) used NLP technology to realize automatic generation and semantic analysis of ancient Chinese poems, providing theoretical and technical support for intelligent creation and computer-aided design12. Zhuo et al. (2023) summarized the traditional cultural art’s style characteristics and artistic value through text collation. They proposed an aesthetic evaluation method using text as the medium13. Hu et al. (2025) enhanced the diffusion model by introducing a style-conditional Latent Consistency Model-Low Rank Adaptation (LCM-LoRA), and realized personalized and adaptive stylized image generation for Miao batik, an ICH. Their study demonstrated that fine-grained style control and efficient model fine-tuning technologies could effectively capture and inherit the unique visual aesthetics and pattern features in traditional crafts; this provided a key technical path for the modern expression and innovative design of traditional culture14. Qu et al. (2024) conducted a systematic review on knowledge graphs in the field of traditional Chinese medicine. They pointed out that the construction of knowledge graphs covered the entire chain from entity and relationship analysis, multi-source data fusion to specific application scenarios (e.g., clinical decision support, medicinal material traceability, and knowledge question answering). This study offered a referable analytical framework and methodology for the digital modeling and intelligent application of other traditional knowledge systems, such as ICH skills and traditional crafts15.

The review of domestic and international literature shows that the existing studies have certain limitations. Also, there is a gap in generative research within a specific cultural context. This study is positioned to explore intelligent text generation in the Attention-based CAD (Attn-CAD) model. By constructing a specialized corpus, designing, and optimizing the generative model, its performance is systematically evaluated in the context of traditional CAD.

Research model

This study strictly follows a closed-loop design of objectives, methods, and verification. Taking the improvement of terminology accuracy as the core research objective, supplemented by two important supporting objectives of ensuring style consistency and enhancing cross-domain adaptability, it constructs targeted implementation methods and a verification system. In order to improve terminology accuracy, a core method combining a cultural semantic attention layer and a terminology verification layer (threshold: 0.9) is adopted; it is verified by the cultural terminology accuracy indicator and manual terminology consistency evaluation. For the guarantee of style consistency, a multi-scale temporal convolutional network (TCN) style constraint module (with dilation factors of 1/2/4) is designed; its effect is verified by combining the style matching degree indicator and expert style authenticity scoring. In view of enhancing cross-domain adaptability, a combined strategy of dynamic weight optimization and domain style deviation factors is applied; its universality is verified through cross-domain tests (ceramics → calligraphy/paper cutting/embroidery) and statistical analysis of indicator decline rates. This design establishes a one-to-one correspondence between “methods and verification”. The core methods accurately address the key pain points in traditional culture text generation; besides, the verification methods integrate automatic indicators and manual evaluation, ensuring the objectivity and comprehensiveness of the evaluation results.

Attention mechanism

In the field of artificial intelligence, the attention mechanism has become a key component of neural network architectures and has been widely applied16. This study embeds cultural semantics into the construction process of text attention mechanisms and strengthens cultural semantic associations by integrating entity embeddings from knowledge graphs. The study makes three assumptions. First, the terminological semantic representations in knowledge graphs have domain consistency, with the core semantics of the same term remaining unchanged across different design scenarios; second, stylistic features can be quantitatively characterized through text lexical selection, syntactic structure, and semantic density; third, terminological accuracy can be verified through the dual dimensions of cosine similarity and contextual adaptability, with a threshold of 0.9 applicable to most standardized traditional cultural terms. The parameter settings for the model design are as follows: (1) A terminology verification threshold of 0.9, determined through grid search on the validation set (threshold range: 0.7–0.95, step size: 0.05). When the threshold is set to 0.9, the terminology accuracy reaches 92.5%, the recall is 89.7%, and the F1-score is optimal. A threshold lower than 0.9 leads to an increase in the misjudgment rate of synonymous terms (e.g., the confusion probability between “underglaze red” and “underglaze color” rises by 12%); a threshold higher than 0.95 causes the missed judgment of niche terms (e.g., the recall rate of craft-specific terms such as “blank trimming” drops by 15%). (2) Attention weight coefficients (λ1 = 0.3, λ2 = 0.4, λ3 = 0.3), determined through 5 groups of comparative experiments (12 coefficient combinations in total) and cross-validation in the ceramics and embroidery domains. This combination balances terminology density, semantic importance, and contextual relevance; this can ensure the model values professional terminology without neglecting the logical coherence of the text. (3) TCN dilation factors (1, 2, 4), adapted to the short, medium, and long sentence structures of traditional craft texts. A dilation factor of 1 is used to capture the logic of craft steps in short sentences (e.g., kneading clay → throwing → trimming); a dilation factor of 2 is suitable for the description of craft characteristics in medium-length sentences; a dilation factor of 4 is applied to the interpretation of cultural connotations in long sentences (e.g., the description of the historical origin of traditional crafts). Experimental verification shows that this set of dilation factors improves the model accuracy by 4.2% compared with a model with a fixed dilation factor (set to 2). The traditional culture knowledge graph constructed in this study is a structured semantic network that organizes the core elements of traditional art design (terminology, crafts, materials, styles, etc.) and their associative relationships. Its core structure adopts a three-level framework of core domain - craft category - terminology hierarchy. ① The core domains cover four major traditional art fields of calligraphy, paper cutting, ceramics, and embroidery. ② Each core domain is subdivided into craft subcategories (e.g., the ceramics domain includes raw material preparation, forming, glazing, firing, etc., with a total of 28 subcategories across all domains). ③ The terminology hierarchy is divided into basic terms (800), professional terms (1200), and niche terms (300) according to popularity and professionalism. The knowledge graph contains three types of semantic relationships: hyponym-hypernym (e.g., blue and white porcelain → underglaze color porcelain), craft associative (e.g., throwing → trimming), and material-craft (e.g., real silk → Suzhou embroidery); there are 4500 structured associations constructed. This knowledge graph provides standardized terminology semantics and associative logic support for the cultural semantic attention layer.

By assigning weights to different positions, it can highlight regions that are more valuable for tasks while weakening irrelevant or redundant information17. With the help of this mechanism, the model can better capture the key content of images, thereby improving overall performance and efficiency18,19. Fs represents the style feature vector extracted by the multi-scale TCN. In order to realize the quantification of style features, this study defines 3 measurable dimensions and designs a four-step extraction process for Fs. In the dimension of lexical selection, quantification is conducted through the style vocabulary proportion and the sentiment tendency value. The former is the ratio of the number of domain-specific style words to the total number of words multiplied by 100%; the latter is the average score of text vocabulary in a manually annotated traditional culture style sentiment dictionary with 5000 entries, with a score range from − 1 to 1. In the dimension of syntactic structure, the sentence length distribution and the complex sentence proportion are used as quantitative indicators; the former is measured by the variance of the number of words per sentence in the text; the latter is calculated as the ratio of the number of complex sentences containing 2 or more clauses to the total number of sentences, multiplied by 100%. In the dimension of semantic density, quantification is realized through two indicators: the first is semantic concept information entropy, calculated based on the occurrence frequency of core concepts in the knowledge graph; the second is terminology density, which is the ratio of the number of domain-specific terms to the total number of words multiplied by 100%.

The specific construction and extraction process of Fs is as follows. First, the input text (original text and generated text) is preprocessed with word segmentation, part-of-speech tagging, and term recognition. Next, the above six quantitative indicators are calculated, and each indicator is mapped to the [0,1] interval through Min-Max normalization. Then, the normalized indicator values are input into a two-layer multilayer perceptron (MLP) (hidden layer dimension: 128, activation function: ReLU) for feature fusion to generate an initial 64-dimensional style feature vector. Data flow design of models

The model takes feature input, semantic encoding, decoding, and output verification as its core link. Based on the standard Transformer encoder-decoder framework, it adds three modules: cultural semantic enhancement, style constraint, and semantic coherence guarantee. The functional positioning and data flow of each module are displayed in Fig. 2:

Modules and data flow of the traditional Attn-CAD model.

The input layer is designed with a three-input structure consisting of structured attribute vectors, design feature labels, and raw text fragments. Among them, structured attribute vectors serve as the core representation of design features, covering six dimensions of quantitative attributes: process type (e.g., blue-and-white porcelain, underglaze red in ceramics), material properties (e.g., real silk, cotton thread in embroidery), stylistic features (e.g., regular script, running script in calligraphy), and functional scenarios (e.g., decorative, practical types). Each dimension is converted into a fixed-dimensional vector through one-hot encoding or embedding mapping. Design feature labels are categorical identifiers, used to locate the craft domain to which a term belongs quickly. Raw text fragments are relevant design descriptive corpora. The three components are concatenated into a single vector and then fed into the encoder for semantic extraction.

The processing and input transformation of artistic data consist of four key steps, realizing the conversion of unstructured artistic information into model-recognizable vectors. The first is data deconstruction. Raw artistic data (including physical craft records, design drawing specifications, oral texts from ICH inheritors, etc.) is deconstructed into three categories of core information: ① structured attributes (6 dimensions like craft type and material attribute); ② design feature labels (e.g., “Ming and Qing imperial kiln style”, “elegant and realistic style of Suzhou embroidery”); ③ original text fragments (craft descriptions, cultural connotation interpretations, etc.). The second is preprocessing. Original text fragments undergo word segmentation (using the jieba word segmentation tool with a custom thesaurus for traditional culture loaded), part-of-speech tagging (based on the spaCy framework) and term recognition (matching the core terminology database of the knowledge graph); categorical data in structured attributes (e.g., craft type) is processed with one-hot encoding; numerical data (e.g., material density) is normalized via Min-Max scaling (mapped to the [0,1] interval). The third is feature mapping. A design feature label embedding layer is designed; it maps discrete design feature labels into 256-dimensional vectors through random initialization and pre-training fine-tuning; original text fragments are encoded into 512-dimensional text feature vectors by the BERT-base-Chinese pre-trained model. The fourth is input fusion. The structured attribute vector (128 dimensions), design feature label embedding vector (256 dimensions), and original text encoding vector (512 dimensions) are fused into an 896-dimensional input vector through concatenation. After being mapped to 512 dimensions via a linear projection layer, it is input into the encoder to complete the full-process transformation from artistic data to model input.

The advantage of this input form lies in balancing category discriminability and attribute details. For unseen new designs, model adaptation can be achieved by expanding the dimensions of structured attribute vectors (e.g., adding material and stylistic dimensions). However, practical deployment relies on a standardized design attribute annotation system; the lack of unified annotation specifications will affect the model’s generalization performance for niche design types.

The encoder adopts a general three-level progressive structure as its core, consisting of a general multi-head self-attention layer, a cultural semantic attention layer24, and a semantic coherence constraint layer in sequence. Among them, the semantic coherence constraint layer incorporates a Bidirectional LSTM (Bi-LSTM) as its core component; it is used to capture the temporal logic and semantic associations of text, and finally outputs structured semantic vectors through this layer25.

Experimental design and performance evaluation

Datasets collection

Data sources and screening criteria

Data sources and screening criteria

This experiment takes the independently constructed traditional art design feature and text pairing dataset as the core experimental material. The dataset construction process fully considers the traditional art field’s diversity and the reliability of data quality. The details are as follows. The data sources cover three authoritative channels. (1) 4,200 annotated texts of traditional artworks publicly released by the Palace Museum and the China ICH Protection Center. (2) Interview records of 12 ICH inheritors (3 each in ceramics, embroidery, paper-cutting, and calligraphy); they are sorted into standardized texts, totaling 3,800 items. (3) Descriptive texts of traditional art from professional design textbooks (such as Chinese Traditional Ceramic Technology and Chinese Paper-Cutting Art Tutorial), totaling 4,000 items. This dataset has 12,000 samples in total, covering 2,800, 3,200, 3,500, and 2,500 items of calligraphy, paper-cutting, ceramics, and embroidery, respectively, involving 4 categories of traditional art fields.

In order to facilitate reproducibility and follow-up research, a complete dataset (including training/verification/test splits) and a traditional cultural knowledge graph (containing 2,300 core terms and 4,500 semantic relationships) are published in the public repository described in the Data and Code Availability Statement. The repository also provides the exact data splitting indices (random seed is 42) and the annotation guidelines used by the expert group; this ensures that the third party has the same experimental conditions when verifying.

(2)Annotation process and specifications

Annotation process and specifications

The entire data annotation process and specifications are as follows. (1) Annotation team: It consists of 3 associate professors in the field of traditional art and design, 2 ICH protection researchers, and 5 master’s students. A two-week unified training is conducted before annotation to familiarize all members with terminology standards and annotation criteria. (2) Annotation content: Six categories of structured attributes are annotated for each sample, including process type, material property, stylistic feature, functional scenario, core terminology, and style label. Core terminology must correspond to terms in the knowledge graph. (3) Annotation process: The “dual independent annotation - cross verification - discrepancy arbitration” process is adopted. First, two annotators conduct independent annotation, and the Kappa coefficient is calculated to verify consistency (Kappa = 0.92). Samples with discrepancies are determined through discussions by an arbitration panel composed of 3 associate professors. (4) Data division: The dataset is divided into a training set (9,600 samples), a validation set (1,200 samples), and a test set (1,200 samples) at an 8:1:1 ratio, ensuring a consistent proportion of samples across all domains after division.

(3)Details of knowledge graph construction

Details of knowledge graph construction

The knowledge graph contains 2,300 core terms, with the construction process as follows. (1) Term collection: An initial term set of 2,800 terms is collected from official documents such as Traditional Craft Terminology Standards and Core Terminology Dictionary of ICH, terms annotated from interviews with inheritors, and standardized terminology in textbooks. (2) Term screening and hierarchical classification: Synonymous and redundant terms are removed, retaining 2,300 core terms, which are classified under a three-level structure of core domain, craft category, and term level. It covers 4 core domains and 28 craft subcategories (the ceramic domain includes 6 subcategories, such as raw material preparation, forming, glazing, and firing). The term level is divided into basic (800), professional (1,200), and niche terms (300). (3) Term relationship construction: Relationships between terms are established based on semantic relevance and craft logic; they include “hyponym-hypernym relationship” (e.g., “blue-and-white porcelain” → “underglaze colored porcelain”), “process association relationship” (e.g., “clay throwing” → “trimming”), and “material-process relationship” (e.g., “real silk” → “Suzhou embroidery”), with 4,500 relationships constructed. (4) Graph verification: Five experts in ICH protection are invited to evaluate the completeness and relevance of terms, with an expert consistency rate of 89%. The terms in the graph are matched and verified against existing authoritative corpora (e.g., China ICH Terminology Database), achieving a term coverage rate of 94%.

Discussion

The Attn-CAD model has demonstrated remarkable advantages in optimizing the quality of text generation for traditional CAD. The core reason lies in its customized architectural design for cultural scenarios; this can accurately capture the associations between cultural proprietary terms and text semantics. Concurrently, with the help of the Bi-LSTM semantic coherence constraint layer, it effectively handles the temporal logic of craftsmanship descriptions and the argumentative logic of art reviews. Thus, it can fundamentally solve the key pain points of traditional models. In addition, the model embeds a style constraint module based on a multi-scale TCN. The system adapts to stylistic expressions across different fields by incorporating domain-scale deviation factors and scale weight coefficients; at the same time, it achieves a style matching degree of 89.3%, effectively capturing the simplicity and elegance of calligraphy as well as the vivid folk style of paper-cutting. Compared with traditional statistical language models, the Attn-CAD model has more advantages in long-text modeling. This model alleviates the problem of long-sequence information loss through dynamic weight optimization. Meanwhile, it can more accurately simulate the complex semantic structures of texts such as traditional craftsmanship descriptions and art reviews.

This study has substantial practical application value, especially in three fields: traditional CAD, ICH inheritance education, and cultural and creative industry communication. In the design field, the Attn-CAD-based intelligent text generation system can assist designers in quickly generating explanations of design concepts and craftsmanship descriptions for works; this system greatly improves the efficiency of writing design proposals. In ICH education, the system can generate easy-to-understand interpretation texts of craftsmanship. Learners’ cognitive biases can be corrected through the cultural term verification function, assisting teachers in building standardized teaching corpora to reduce the impact of subjective evaluation on teaching quality.

However, this study still has certain limitations, mainly reflected in the limited coverage of the corpus. The lack of textual resources on local ICH projects and ethnic minority traditional crafts leads to insufficient adaptability of the model to niche crafts. The terminology verification mechanism has limited disambiguation capability for polysemous words and context-sensitive terms. When terms have multiple semantics (e.g., celadon refers to both glaze color and utensil type), the accuracy is affected. Meanwhile, insufficient samples of niche terms, limitations of stylistic features, and domain differences during cross-domain transfer all contribute to model generation errors. In addition, the influence of cultural representation bias in the dataset on the model’s generalization ability deserves in-depth analysis. At present, the mainstream Han craft samples are absolutely dominant in the dataset; this leads to the obvious Han-centric tendency in knowledge map construction and model training. The proposed model performs well in cross-domain transfer tests in mainstream fields such as calligraphy, paper-cutting, and embroidery; still, these target domains belong to the Han cultural circle in essence and fail to reflect the model’s adaptability to ethnic minority crafts (e.g., Tibetan Thangka and Miao silver ornaments). Such data distribution bias may lead to some problems, such as inappropriate terminology usage and vague stylistic features, when the model is faced with the text generation tasks for ethnic minority crafts. Therefore, the bias of cultural representation mentioned above is a data-level limitation; it also directly affects the model’s practical application in real multicultural scenarios. Future research needs to enhance the model’s inclusive generation capacity for diverse traditional arts through targeted expansion of ethnic minority craft text data and adaptive adjustment of cultural semantic attention.

Conclusion

Research contribution

The contributions of this study in intelligent text generation for traditional CAD are focused on data construction and model innovation. (1) Regarding knowledge graph construction, the traditional cultural knowledge graph contains more than 2,300 core terms, and its construction data sources are mainly derived from three authoritative channels. ① Official standard documents issued by the China ICH Protection Center, such as Traditional Craft Terminology Standards and Core Terminology Dictionary of ICH. ② Craft-specific terms annotated by experts from interview records of 12 ICH inheritors (dual independent annotation is conducted by 3 experts at or above the associate professor level in traditional art and design; the annotation consistency test Kappa value = 0.92; disputed terms are determined through collective discussion). ③ Standardized terms extracted through literature mining from professional design textbooks (such as Chinese Traditional Ceramic Crafts and Chinese Paper-cut Art Tutorial). The ontology structure of the graph adopts a three-level architecture of “core domain, craft category, and term level”. The core domains are divided into four categories: calligraphy, paper-cutting, ceramics, and embroidery. Each category is further subdivided into craft links; for example, the ceramic domain includes subcategories such as raw material preparation, forming, glazing, and firing. The term level consists of three classifications: basic, professional, and niche terms. The graph verification adopts a dual mechanism of “expert review and corpus matching”. Five experts in the field of ICH protection are invited to evaluate the completeness and relevance of terms, and the expert consistency rate reaches 89%. Meanwhile, the terms in the graph are matched and verified against existing authoritative corpora, with the term coverage rate reaching 94%; this ensures the accuracy and practicality of the graph. (2) The study breaks through the cultural relevance bottleneck of general text generation models and designs an improved Transformer model. The innovation incorporates three key components: a cultural semantic attention layer integrated with a traditional cultural knowledge graph containing over 2,300 core terms, and a multi-scale TCN-based style constraint module. Additionally, an output verification layer with 1,800 standardized terms is included. It also optimizes the cultural semantic attention layer’s dynamic weights using term semantic importance scores and context relevance. Besides, it improves the style module’s multi-scale feature fusion capability by combining domain style deviation factors. These measures effectively solve the core problems of general models—insufficient attention to traditional cultural proprietary terms, inconsistent style of generated texts, and low cultural terminology accuracy. Hence, an exclusive generation architecture adapted to the context of traditional culture is constructed.

Future works and research limitations

Based on current research conditions and technical boundaries, this study still has several limitations to be handled. (1) The corpus lacks diversity and breadth. The corpus is insufficient in diversity and coverage breadth; meanwhile, it exhibits obvious cultural representation bias. The existing dataset covers four major traditional art fields (e.g., calligraphy, paper-cutting, ceramics, and embroidery); however, samples of mainstream Han crafts account for as high as 96.8%; samples of regional ICH (1.77%) and ethnic minority traditional crafts (1.43%) are severely scarce. This leads to a Han-centric tendency in both knowledge graph construction and model training; it is difficult for the model to accurately capture the unique semantics of ethnic minority crafts such as Miao silver ornament forging and Tibetan Thangka painting. More importantly, such data distribution bias directly undermines model generalization. Existing cross-domain tests only verify the model’s transfer performance within the mainstream cultural circle and fail to evaluate its adaptability in ethnic minority craft scenarios. At the same time, the sample size of some niche craft terms in the embroidery field is less than 50; this limits the model’s adaptability to the diversity of traditional culture. In the future, it is necessary to collect ethnic minority craft text data directionally, construct a culturally balanced test set, and introduce a culturally adaptive weight adjustment mechanism to improve the model’s generation quality in multicultural scenarios. To handle the above issues, the study proposes the following operable mitigation strategies. For the optimization of the corpus and knowledge graph, the collection of textual data for regional ICH and ethnic minority crafts will be expanded; inheritors will be invited to participate in annotation and terminology review; bias audits can be conducted on the knowledge graph with additional labels of cultural geographical attribute and ethnic affiliation; weighted sampling can be adopted to balance the data distribution. For evading homogenization and ethical risks, a “school style elasticity coefficient” and “cultural diversity evaluation indicators” will be introduced to preserve the individuality of different schools; an “ICH data rights and interests sharing mechanism” can be established to redirect part of the application benefits to inheritor communities; a third-party ethical review is introduced to avoid cultural misinterpretation. For improving generation effects, multi-modal data such as images and videos can be integrated in subsequent research; more detailed craft descriptions will be generated through cross-modal pre-training; voice-to-text data from inheritors are incorporated to enhance the authenticity and appeal of the generated texts. (2) The model does not integrate multi-modal data and only relies on text features for generation. It fails to combine visual information (e.g., images and videos) of traditional artworks and cannot use multi-dimensional cultural features to assist text generation. This limits the ability of generated content to present the details of traditional cultural art. To deal with these limitations, future research can pursue optimization across multiple dimensions to expand depth and application scope. One key direction involves expanding the scale and coverage of the corpus, with particular emphasis on collecting texts related to local ICH projects. Additional efforts should focus on gathering interview records of traditional crafts from ethnic minorities and compiling relevant literature materials. In addition, the terminology verification mechanism is optimized; meanwhile, BERT-based contextual embedding technology is introduced to achieve terminology disambiguation. The pre-trained BERT model is used to deeply encode the contextual semantics of generated terms. Combined with the usage rules of terms in specific craft scenarios, a dual-dimensional verification model of “cosine similarity and contextual adaptability” is constructed. This model improves the recognition accuracy of polysemous and synonymous terms, further reducing deviations in terminology usage. However, compared with existing related studies, the core difference of this study lies in its focus on practical text generation scenarios of traditional CAD. This emphasizes the coordinated optimization of terminology accuracy, style adaptability, and cross-domain universality. In contrast, most existing studies focus on general text generation or generation in single cultural scenarios (such as ancient poetry), without fully considering the craft relevance and style diversity of design scenarios. In addition, the dataset, knowledge graph, and full source code published in this study enable the research community to directly build upon this work; it can alleviate the identified data bias issues by contributing more minority craft texts, thus enhancing the cross-domain adaptability of the model. This study encourages other researchers to use these open resources and extend the framework to more diverse cultural heritage scenarios.

Fourier coefficients and rapid decay in reduced groupoid C*-algebras
Adam H. Fuller, Pradyut Karmakar

Abstract
. Let Σ → G \Sigma\rightarrow G be a twist over a locally compact Hausdorff étale groupoid G G . Given f f in the reduced C ∗ -algebra C r ∗ ​ ( Σ , G ) C_{r}^{*}(\Sigma;G) with open support U ⊆ G U\subseteq G we ask when f f lies in the closure of the compactly supported sections on U U . Suppose G G satisfies the rapid decay property with respect to a length function L L . We give a positive answer to our question in two instances: when L L is conditionally negative-definite, and when L L is the square-root of a locally negative type function on G G .



1.
Introduction

Let
G
be a Hausdorff locally compact groupoid, and let
\Sigma\rightarrow G
be a twist over
G
.
It is well known that there is a contractive homomorphism from the reduced C
∗
-algebra
C_{r}^{*}(\Sigma;G)
and the space of
C_{0}
-sections on the line bundle
L
over
G
induced by
\Sigma
with the
\sup
-norm.
We can thus, by an abuse of notation, view
C_{r}^{*}(\Sigma;G)
as a subset of
C_{0}(\Sigma;G)
.
Given an
f\in C_{r}^{*}(\Sigma;G)
, let
U=\{\gamma\in G\colon f(\gamma)\neq 0\}
be the open support of
f
, as viewed as a section on
G
.
This note is motivated by the following question: does the support of
f
determine
f
?
Explicitly, we ask:

Question 1
.

If
f\in C_{r}^{*}(\Sigma;G)
and
U
is the support of
f\in G
, are there compactly supported sections on
U
which approximate
f
in the C
∗
-norm of
C_{r}^{*}(\Sigma;G)
?

When
\gamma\in G
, we view
f(\gamma)
as the
\gamma
-th Fourier coefficient of
f
.
Thus, we view Question
1
as asking if we can recover elements of
C_{r}^{*}(\Sigma;G)
from their Fourier coefficients.

To motivate this question further, let
\Gamma
be a discrete group acting on a Hausdorff locally compact space
X
,
and
C_{0}(X)\rtimes_{r}\Gamma
be the reduced crossed product.
If
a\in C_{0}(X)\rtimes_{r}\Gamma
, then
a
has a unique Fourier series

a\sim\sum_{g\in\Gamma}a_{g}\cdot g,

where each
a_{g}\in C_{0}(X)
.
One can alternatively see the crossed product
C_{0}(X)\rtimes_{r}\Gamma
as a groupoid C
∗
-algebra.
That is
C_{0}(X)\rtimes_{r}\Gamma
is isomorphic to the reduced groupoid C
∗
-algebra
C_{r}^{*}(\Gamma\times X)
of the transformation group
\Gamma\times X
.
From this perspective, if
a\in C_{r}^{*}(\Gamma\times X)
then its support is precisely

U=\bigcup_{g\in\Gamma}[\{g\}\times\operatorname{supp}(a_{g})],

where
a\sim\sum_{g}a_{g}\cdot g
is the Fourier series of
a
.
Thus, in this setting, Question
1
is explicitly asking if
a
can be recovered from its Fourier series.

The study of the relationship between
a\in C_{0}(X)\rtimes_{r}\Gamma
and the Fourier series of
a
has a long history.
Let
a\sim\sum_{g}a_{g}\cdot g
be the Fourier series of an element
a\in C_{0}(X)\rtimes_{r}\Gamma
.
Some affirmative answers to Question
1
for crossed products (often in more generality than stated here) include, but are not limited to: when
\Gamma
is amenable
[
24
]
; when
\Gamma
is weakly amenable
[
4
]
; when
\Gamma
has the approximation property of Haagerup-Kraus
[
12
,
20
]
; and when
\Gamma
has the Haagerup property and the rapid decay property with respect to the associated length function
[
4
]
.

For a Hausdorff locally compact groupoid
G
with a twist
\Sigma\rightarrow G
, Question
1
is again known to have a positive in certain cases.
When
G
is amenable, this follows from
[
8
, Theorem 4.2]
(corrected proof in
[
9
]
; see Theorem
2.2
).
When
f\in C_{r}^{*}(\Sigma;G)
has closed open support, then Question
1
also has an affirmative answer by
[
13
, Lemma 3.8]
(see Theorem
2.3
).
There are, however, times when there is a negative answer to Question
1
.
One class of such examples are the HLS groupoids introduced in
[
14
]
(see Example
2.4
).

In this paper, we explore Question
1
for groupoids satisfying the rapid decay property.
In our first result in this direction, Theorem
3.10
, we show that if
G
has the rapid decay property with respect to a continuous length function
L
, then there is a dense
*
-algebra
A\subseteq C_{r}^{*}(\Sigma;G)
such that if
f\in A
and
\operatorname{supp}(f)=U
then
f\in\overline{C_{c}(\Sigma|_{U};U)}^{\|\cdot\|_{r}}
.
The dense algebra
A
is the Schwartz space of
G
with respect to
L
,
H^{2,L}(\Sigma;G)
.

In Theorem
3.14
we show that
f\in\overline{C_{c}(\Sigma|_{U};U)}^{\|\cdot\|_{r}}
for all
f\in C_{r}^{*}(\Sigma;G)
when
L
is conditionally negative-definite.
In Theorem
4.8
we show that if
G
is a second-countable locally compact Hausdorff groupoid satisfying the Haagerup property and the rapid decay property with respect an associated length function, then Question
1
has an affirmative answer.

Our three main theorems, Theorem
3.10
, Theorem
3.14
, and Theorem
4.8
, give groupoid analogues of results of Bédos and Conti for groups, crossed products, and twisted crossed products.
In particular, Theorem
3.10
should be compared to
[
3
, Theorem 1.1]
; Theorem
3.14
should be compared to
[
4
, Corollary 6.5]
; and Theorem
4.8
should be compared to
[
3
, Theorem 5.9]
.
The tools we use are, however, necessarily different.
In particular, we apply results of Hou
[
15
]
, Weygandt
[
22
]
, and Kwaśniewski-Li-Skalski
[
16
]
.
The study of rapid decay for groupoids was introduced by Hou
[
15
]
, and extended to twists by Weygandt
[
22
]
.
The Haagerup property for Fell bundles over groupoids was introduced by Kwaśniewski, Li and Skalski
[
16
]
. We use the definitions from
[
16
]
in Section
4
.

2.
Groupoids and their
C^{\ast}
-algebras

We briefly summarize the key definitions and concepts needed for groupoids, twists, and their C
∗
-algebras.
We point the reader to
[
19
, Part II]
and
[
10
, Section 2]
for a detailed background.
A
groupoid

G
is a small category where each morphism has inverse.
The
source
map and
range
map are given by

\displaystyle s(\gamma)=\gamma^{-1}\gamma

\displaystyle r(\gamma)=\gamma\gamma^{-1},

for all
\gamma\in G
, respectively.
A
topological groupoid
is a groupoid with a topology in which multiplication and inversion maps are continuous.
An open set
B
in a topological groupoid is called a
bisection
if
s|_{B}
is a homeomorphism.
A topological groupoid
G
is called
étale
if the topology consists of a basis of open bisections.
The units of a groupoid
G
are the objects of the small category.
We denote the units of
G
by
G^{(0)}
.
That is

G^{(0)}=\{\gamma^{-1}\gamma\colon\gamma\in G\}.

Note that for an
étale Hausdorff groupoid

G
, the unit space
G^{(0)}
is a closed and open subset of
G
.
For every element
x
of the unit space
G^{(0)}
, denote
G_{x}=\{\gamma\in G:s(\gamma)=x\}
and
G^{x}=\{\gamma\in G\colon r(\gamma)=x\}
.
For
x\in G^{(0)}
, the
isotropy groupoid

\mathrm{Iso}(G)=\bigcup_{x\in G^{(0)}}G_{x}^{x}
, where
G_{x}^{x}:=G_{x}\cap G^{x}
.
A groupoid is
topologically principal
if
\{x\in G^{(0)}:G_{x}^{x}=\{x\}\}
is dense in
G^{(0)}
.
A groupoid is called
principal
if the set
\{\gamma\in G:s(\gamma)=r(\gamma)\}
is
G^{(0)}
.
For an étale groupoid
G
, note that
G_{x}
and
G^{x}
are discrete subsets of
G
by
[
19
, Corollary 8.4.10]
.
Throughout this article, all groupoids are topological and assumed to be locally compact Hausdorff.

Let
G
be a locally compact Hausdorff étale groupoid.
Let
\Sigma
be a groupoid. Then
\Sigma
is a
twist
over
G
, denoted
\Sigma\rightarrow G
, if

{\mathbb{T}}\times G^{(0)}\xrightarrow{\iota}\Sigma\xrightarrow{q}G

is a locally trivial central extension of
G
.
A twist
\Sigma\rightarrow G
determines a line bundle
L
over
G
.
Explicitly
L=({\mathbb{C}}\times\Sigma)/\sim
, where
(z_{1},\gamma_{2})\sim(z_{2},\gamma_{2})
if there is a
\lambda\in{\mathbb{T}}
such that
(z_{1},\gamma_{1})=(\overline{\lambda}z_{2},\lambda\cdot\gamma_{2})
.
The surjection
P\colon L\rightarrow G
is given by
P([\lambda,\gamma])=q(\gamma).

For
[\lambda,\gamma]\in L
we define the absolute value to be
|[\lambda,\gamma]|=|\lambda|
.

Denote by
C_{c}(\Sigma;G)
the space of all compactly supported continuous sections
f\colon G\rightarrow L
.
The space
C_{c}(\Sigma;G)
becomes a
*
-algebra with the convolution product

(1)

(f*g)(\gamma)=\sum_{\alpha\beta=\gamma}f(\alpha)g(\beta),

and adjoint

(2)

f^{*}(\gamma)=\overline{f(\gamma^{-1})}.

Note that the space
C_{c}(\Sigma;G)
is sometimes defined as the space of continuous, compactly supported covariant function
f\colon\Sigma\rightarrow G
.
The equivalence of these two definitions is described in
[
10
, Section 2.2]
.

The
reduced C
∗
-algebra

C_{r}^{*}(\Sigma;G)
of
\Sigma\rightarrow G
is the completion of
C_{c}(\Sigma;G)
with respect to the reduced norm
\|\cdot\|_{r}.

This is the norm induced on by the left regular representations of
C_{c}(\Sigma;G)
on
\ell^{2}(\Sigma_{x};G_{x})
for each
x\in G^{(0)}
, where
\ell^{2}(\Sigma_{x};G_{x})
is the square-summable sections on
G_{x}
.

When
U\subseteq G
, we denote by
C_{c}(\Sigma|_{U};U)
the sections in
C_{c}(\Sigma;G)
supported in
U
.
By
[
10
, Lemma 2.7]

H\subseteq G
is an open subgroupoid, then
\Sigma|_{H}\rightarrow H
is a twist, and the inclusion map
C_{c}(\Sigma|_{H};H)\hookrightarrow C_{c}(\Sigma;G)
extends to an inclusion of the C
∗
-algebras
C_{r}^{*}(\Sigma|_{H};H)
into
C_{r}^{*}(\Sigma;G).

2.1.
Fourier coefficients

The identity map
j(f)=f
between
C_{c}(\Sigma;G)
with the reduced norm
\|\cdot\|_{r}
, and
C_{c}(\Sigma;G)
with the
\sup
-norm
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
is a contractive
*
-homomorphism (with the convolution product (
1
) and adjoint (
2
) on each copy of
C_{c}(\Sigma;G)
).
The map
j
extends to an injective contractive homomorphism
j\colon C_{r}^{*}(\Sigma;G)\rightarrow C_{0}(\Sigma;G)

[
10
, Proposition 2.21]
.
Henceforth, we will identify
C_{r}^{\ast}(\Sigma;G)\subseteq C_{0}(\Sigma;G)
suppressing

.
Given
f\in C_{r}^{*}(\Sigma;G)
and
\gamma\in G
, we view
f(\gamma)
as the
\gamma
-th
Fourier coefficient
of
f
.
When
G
is a group
\Gamma
or a transformation group

this terminology is consistent with the usual usage of the term Fourier coefficient.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
G
be a locally compact Hausdorff étale groupoid, and let
\Sigma\rightarrow G
be a twist.
Given
f\in C_{r}^{*}(\Sigma;G)
we define the
support of
f
, denoted by
\operatorname{supp}(f)
, to be the open set

\operatorname{supp}(f)=\{\gamma\in G\colon f(\gamma)\neq 0\}.

We are interested in when an element
f\in C_{r}^{*}(\Sigma;G)
can be “recovered” from its Fourier coefficients.
We ask, if
U=\operatorname{supp}(f)
is
f
in
\overline{C_{c}(\Sigma|_{U};U)}^{\|\cdot\|_{r}}
.
Our main results are positive answers to this question in the presence of the rapid decay property.
These are given in Theorem
3.10
, Theorem
3.14
, and Theorem
4.8
.

In this subsection, we collate known positive results and counter-examples.
The following two theorems were proved, and stated, for subalgebras of
C_{r}^{*}(\Sigma;G)
and subgroupoids of
G
.
We state them here in more generality; the arguments in the original sources do not need to be altered significantly to do this.
The following theorem is stated in
[
8
]
.
We warn the reader that the proof in
[
8
]
is incorrect, but has been corrected in
[
9
]
.

Theorem 2.2

(cf.
[
8
, Theorem 4.2]
)
.

Let
G
be an amenable locally compact Hausdorff étale groupoid, and let
\Sigma\rightarrow G
be a twist.
Take
f\in C_{r}^{*}(\Sigma;G)
with
\operatorname{supp}(f)=U
.
Then
f\in\overline{C_{c}(\Sigma|_{U};U)}^{\|\cdot\|_{r}}
.

The next theorem gives a positive answer when we assume the support of
f
is clopen.
In
[
13
]
, this is used to identify Cartan subalgebras of
C_{r}^{*}(\Sigma;G)
.

Theorem 2.3

(cf.
[
13
, Lemma 3.8]
)
.

Let
G
be a locally compact Hausdorff étale groupoid, and let
\Sigma\rightarrow G
be a twist.
Take
f\in C_{r}^{*}(\Sigma;G)
with
\operatorname{supp}(f)=U
.
If
U
is closed in
G
, then
f\in\overline{C_{c}(\Sigma|_{U};U)}^{\|\cdot\|_{r}}
.

It is not always the case that we can recover elements of
C_{r}^{*}(\Sigma;G)
from their supports.
We will now describe how failure of

to be inner-exact (in the sense of
[
6
]
) gives rise to such elements.

A subset

is

if

implies

for all
\gamma\in G
.
Given an invariant

we define
G_{U}
to be the subgroupoid

G_{U}=\{\gamma\in G\colon r(\gamma)\in U\}.

Given an open invariant set
U\subseteq G^{(0)}
, with
F=G^{(0)}\backslash U
, we get the following commutative diagram

\textstyle{C_{r}^{*}(\Sigma_{G|_{U}};G_{U})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{E_{U}}
\textstyle{C_{r}^{*}(\Sigma;G)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{E}
\scriptstyle{\varphi}
\textstyle{C_{r}^{*}(\Sigma|_{G_{F}};G_{F})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{E_{F}}

\textstyle{C_{0}(F)}

where the vertical arrows are given by faithful conditional expectations.
The bottom line will be exact, however the top line may not be exact.
The ideal

such that

.
Thus, if the top line is not exact, then there exist

such that
\operatorname{supp}(f)\subseteq G_{U}
but

Higson-Lafforgue-Skandalis
[

give a class of groupoids where the top line will not be exact.

.

Let
\Gamma
be an infinite group and let
\{K_{n}\}_{n}
be a nested family of normal finite-index subgroups of

such that

, and

For each

, let

, and let
\Gamma_{\infty}=\Gamma
.
The

for

is defined as the disjoint union

Consider the sequence

(3)

\displaystyle C_{r}^{*}(G_{\mathbb{N}})\rightarrow C_{r}^{*}(G)\rightarrow C_{r}^{*}(\Gamma).

3.
Schwartz space and Rapid Decay Property

3.1.
The
2
-norm

In addition to the reduced norm
\|\cdot\|_{r}
and the
\sup
-norm
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
, we will need several more norms on
C_{c}(\Sigma;G)
, for a twist
\Sigma\rightarrow G
.
We recall here the
2
-norm and some key facts.

Definition 3.1
.

Define a norm
\|\cdot\|_{2}
on
C_{c}(\Sigma;G)
by

\displaystyle\|f\|_{2}

\displaystyle=\sup_{x\in G^{(0)}}\max\left\{\sum_{\gamma\in G_{x}}|f(\gamma)|^{2},\ \sum_{\gamma\in G^{x}}|f(\gamma)|^{2}\right\}^{1/2},

for each
f\in C_{c}(\Sigma;G)
.
Denote
by
\ell^{2}(\Sigma;G)
the space
\overline{C_{c}(\Sigma;G)}^{\|\cdot\|_{2}}
.

We note that for any
g\in\ell^{2}(\Sigma;G)
he have that

\displaystyle\|g\|_{2}

\displaystyle=\sup_{x\in G^{(0)}}\max\left\{\sum_{\gamma\in G_{x}}|g(\gamma)|^{2},\ \sum_{\gamma\in G^{x}}|g(\gamma)|^{2}\right\}^{1/2}.

The norms on
C_{c}(\Sigma;G)
,
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
,
\|\cdot\|_{r}
, and
\|\cdot\|_{2}
are related by the following inequality

\|f\|_{\infty}\leq\|f\|_{2}\leq\|f\|_{r}

for each
f\in C_{c}(\Sigma;G)
, see e.g.
[
18
]
.

Closely related to the
2
-norm is the function

(4)

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle\mapsto\max\left\{\sum_{\gamma\in G_{x}}|f(\gamma)|^{2},\ \sum_{\gamma\in G^{x}}|f(\gamma)|^{2}\right\}^{1/2}

for
x\in G^{(0)}
and a fixed
f\in C_{c}(\Sigma;G)
.
This function is continuous on
G^{(0)}
, since the counting measure of
G
forms a Haar system, see e.g.
[
18
, Lemma I.2.7]
.
Further,
(
4
)
is continuous for any
f\in\ell^{2}(\Sigma;G)
.
We prove this is in the following lemma.
That the analogous statement for the
1
-norm is continuous is
[
2
, Lemma 2.6]
; or one can follow a similar proof as below.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\Sigma\rightarrow G
be a twist over an étale locally compact Hausdorff groupoid.
Then for each
f\in\ell^{2}(\Sigma;G)
the function on
G^{(0)}
defined by

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle\mapsto\max\left\{\sum_{\gamma\in G_{x}}|f(\gamma)|^{2},\ \sum_{\gamma\in G^{x}}|f(\gamma)|^{2}\right\}^{1/2}

is continuous.

Proof.

For
f\in\ell^{2}(\Sigma;G)
let
T_{f}
be the function

\displaystyle T_{f}\colon G^{(0)}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow{\mathbb{C}}

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle\longmapsto\sum_{\gamma\in G_{x}}|f(\gamma)|^{2}.

We will show that
T_{f}
is continuous.
A similar argument works for the function
x\mapsto\sum_{\gamma\in G^{x}}|f(\gamma)|^{2}
, and the result readily follows from there.

We have already noted that
T_{g}
is continuous for each
g\in C_{c}(\Sigma;G)
.
Now take an arbitrary
f\in\ell^{2}(\Sigma;G)
.
We claim that for each
\varepsilon>0
there exists a compact set
K_{\epsilon}\subseteq G
such that for each
x\in G^{(0)}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\gamma\in G_{x}\backslash K_{\varepsilon}}|f(\gamma)|^{2}<\varepsilon.

Indeed, we can choose
g\in C_{c}(\Sigma;G)
such that
\|f-g\|_{2}^{2}<\varepsilon
.
Let
K\subset G
be a compact set such that
\operatorname{supp}(g)\subset K
.
Then the claim holds, with
K_{\varepsilon}=K.

Fix
n
, choose
K=K_{\frac{1}{n}}
.
Let
U
be an open set in
G
such that
K\subseteq U
and
\bar{U}
is compact in
G
.
By Urysohn’s lemma, there exists a continuous function
h_{n}\colon G\rightarrow[0,1]
such that
h_{n}(\gamma)=1
if
\gamma\in K
and
\text{supp}(h_{n})\subseteq U
.
Let
g_{n}=fh_{n}
. Then
g_{n}\in C_{c}(\Sigma;G)
.

One can show that the functions
(T_{g_{n}})_{n}
coverge uniformly to
T_{f}
.
As each
T_{g_{n}}
is continuous, it follows that
T_{f}
is continuous.
∎

We now show that functions in
\ell^{2}(\Sigma;G)
are determined by their supports.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
G
be an étale, locally compact Hausdorff groupoid and let
\Sigma\rightarrow G
be a twist.
If
f\in\ell^{2}(\Sigma;G)
and
\operatorname{supp}(f)\subseteq U
for some open
U\subseteq G
, then
f\in\overline{C_{c}(\Sigma|_{U};U)}^{\|\cdot\|_{2}}
.

Proof.

Take
f\in\ell^{2}(\Sigma;G)
with
\operatorname{supp}(f)\subseteq U
for an open set
U\subseteq G
.
Take any
\varepsilon>0
.
We will show that there exists
g\in C_{c}(\Sigma_{U};U)
so that
\|f-g\|_{2}<\varepsilon
.

Take
K\subseteq G
compact such that for all
x\in G^{(0)}

\sum_{\gamma\in G_{x}\backslash K}|f(\gamma)|^{2}<\varepsilon,\text{ and }\sum_{\gamma\in G^{x}\backslash K}|f(\gamma)|^{2}<\varepsilon.

Note,
K
exists since
\ell^{2}(\Sigma;G)=\overline{C_{c}(\Sigma;G)}^{\|\cdot\|_{2}}
.

Fix
x\in s(K)
.
There exist finite sets
F_{x}\subseteq G_{x}
such that

\sum_{\gamma\in G_{x}\backslash F_{x}}|f(\gamma)|^{2}<\varepsilon/3.

Let
F_{x}=\{\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{n}\}.

For each
\gamma_{i}\in F_{x}
there are bisections
V_{i}
and
U_{i}
such that
\gamma_{i}\in V_{i}\subseteq\overline{V_{i}}\subseteq U_{i}\subseteq U
, where
\overline{V_{i}}
is compact.
As
f
is continuous, we can further assume that for each
\gamma_{i}\in F_{x}
and
\sigma\in U_{i}
that

||f(\gamma_{i})|^{2}-|f(\sigma)|^{2}|<\frac{\varepsilon}{3n},

by shrinking
U_{i}
is necessary.

The function

T_{f}(x)=\sum_{\gamma\in G_{x}}|f(\gamma)|^{2}

is continuous by Lemma
3.2
.
Hence, there is an open neighborhood
A_{x}
of
x
such that for each
y\in A_{x}

|T_{f}(x)-T_{f}(y)|<\varepsilon/3.

Let

W_{x}=A_{x}\cap\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}s(V_{i})

and let

K_{x}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}\overline{V_{i}}.

Take
y\in W_{x}
.
For each
1\leq i\leq n
, let
\sigma_{i}=G_{y}\cap V_{i}
, and let
F=\{\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{n}\}
.
Then

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in G_{y}\backslash F}|f(\sigma)|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left|T_{f}(y)-\sum_{i=1}^{n}|f(\sigma_{i})|^{2}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|T_{f}(y)-T_{f}(x)+T_{f}(x)-\sum_{i=1}^{n}|f(\sigma_{i})|^{2}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|T_{f}(y)-T_{f}(x)\right|+\left|\sum_{\gamma\in G_{x}\backslash F_{x}}|f(\gamma)|^{2}\right|+\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}(|f(\gamma_{i})|^{2}-|f(\sigma_{i})|^{2})\right|

\displaystyle<\frac{\varepsilon}{3}+\frac{\varepsilon}{3}+n\frac{\varepsilon}{3n}=\varepsilon.

Note that
F=\{\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{n}\}\subseteq K_{x}
.
Thus, if
y\in W_{x}
, then

\sum_{\sigma\in G_{y}\backslash K_{x}}|f(\sigma)|^{2}<\varepsilon.

As
s(K)
is compact, there are
x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}
and associated sets constructed as above
W_{1},\ldots,W_{m}
covering
s(K)
.
Let
L^{\prime}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}K_{i}
, where
K_{i}=K_{x_{i}}
is the compact set constructed as above.

Applying the same argument using the range map instead of source, we get open sets
W^{1},\ldots,W^{k}
, covering
r(K)
and a compact sets
K^{1},\ldots,K^{k}
such that if
x\in W^{i}

\sum_{\gamma\in G^{x}\backslash K^{i}}|f(\gamma)|^{2}<\varepsilon.

Let
L^{\prime\prime}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}K^{i}
.

Consider the compact set
L=L^{\prime}\cup L^{\prime\prime}\subseteq U
.
By Urysohn’s Lemma there is a
h\in C_{c}(U)
such that
h(U)\subseteq[0,1]
and
h(\gamma)=1
for all
\gamma\in L
.
Let
g=hf
.
Then
g\in C_{c}(\Sigma_{U};U)
.
Take any
x\in G^{(0)}
.
If
x\in s(K)
, then
x\in W_{i}
for some
i
and so

\displaystyle\sum_{\gamma\in G_{x}}|f(\gamma)-g(\gamma)|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\gamma\in G_{x}\backslash L}|f(\gamma)-g(\gamma)|^{2}\leq\sum_{\gamma\in G_{x}\backslash L}|f(\gamma)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\gamma\in G_{x}\backslash K_{i}}|f(\gamma)|^{2}<\varepsilon.

If
x\notin s(K)
, then
G_{x}\cap K=\emptyset
, and so

\displaystyle\sum_{\gamma\in G_{x}}|f(\gamma)-g(\gamma)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\gamma\in G_{x}}|f(\gamma)|^{2}=\sum_{\gamma\in G_{x}\backslash K}|f(\gamma)|^{2}<\varepsilon.

One can similarly show that for all
x\in G^{(0)}

\sum_{\gamma\in G^{x}}|f(\gamma)-g(\gamma)|^{2}<\varepsilon.

Thus
\|f-g\|_{2}^{2}<\varepsilon
and so
f\in\overline{C_{c}(\Sigma|_{U};U)}^{\|\cdot\|_{2}}
.
∎

3.2.
Length functions and the Schwartz space

We will now recall the definition of the Schwartz space of
\Sigma\rightarrow G
with respect to a length function
L
and record some key properties.

Definition 3.4

(
[
15
]
)
.

Let
G
be an étale groupoid.
A function
L\colon G\rightarrow[0,\infty)
is a
length function on G
if

(i)

L(x)=0
for all
x\in G^{(0)}
;

(ii)

L(\gamma)=L(\gamma^{-1})
for all
\gamma\in G
, i.e.
L
is a symmetric function; and

(iii)

L(\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2})\leq L(\gamma_{1})+L(\gamma_{2})
for all
(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\in G^{(2)}
, i.e.
L
is subadditive.

The following definition is adapted from
[
22
, Definition 2.9]
.

Definition 3.5
.

Let
\Sigma\rightarrow G
be a twist, where
G
is a locally compact étale groupoid.
Let
L
be a length function on
G
.
For each
p
in the non-negative integers
{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
, define norms on
C_{c}(\Sigma;G)
by

\displaystyle\|f\|_{2,p,s,L}=\sup_{x\in G^{(0)}}\big\{\big(\sum_{\gamma\in G_{x}}|f(\gamma)|^{2}(1+L(\gamma))^{2p}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\big\},

\displaystyle\|f\|_{2.p,r,L}=\sup_{x\in G^{(0)}}\big\{\big(\sum_{\gamma\in G^{x}}|f(\gamma)|^{2}(1+L(\gamma))^{2p}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\big\},\text{ and}

\displaystyle\|f\|_{2,p,L}=\max\{\|f\|_{2,p,s,L},\|f\|_{2,p,r,L}\}\,.

For each
p
in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
, let
H^{2,L,p}(\Sigma;G)
be the completion of
C_{c}(\Sigma;G)
with respect to the norm
\|\cdot\|_{2,p,L}
.
We define the
Schwartz space

H^{2,L}(\Sigma;G)
by

H^{2,L}(\Sigma;G)=\bigcap_{p\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}}H^{2,L,p}(\Sigma;G)\cap C_{0}(\Sigma;G).

That is,
H^{2,L}(\Sigma;G)
is the completion of
C_{c}(\Sigma;G)
by the norms
\|\cdot\|_{2,p,L}
and
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
for all
p\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
.

Remark 3.6
.

In
[
22
]
the space
C_{c}(\Sigma;G)
is defined to be the space of compactly supported, covariant functions
f\colon\Sigma\rightarrow G
.
By
[
10
, Section 2]
that description is equivalent to the definition of
C_{c}(\Sigma;G)
in terms of sections on the line bundle
L
over
G
given in this paper.
In
[
22
, Definition 2.9]
, the Schwartz space is thus described as a completion of functions on
\Sigma
, not sections on
G
as we have done in Definition
3.5
.
The two definitions are, however, equivalent.

Definition 3.7
.

Let
\Sigma\rightarrow G
be a twist, where
G
is a locally compact étale groupoid.
Let
L
be a length function on
G
.
Let
U\subseteq G
.
Define the
Schwartz space over
U
as

H^{2,L}(\Sigma|_{U};U)=\bigcap_{p\geq 1}\overline{C_{c}(\Sigma|_{U};U)}^{\|\cdot\|_{2,p,L}}\cap C_{0}(\Sigma|_{U};U).

In keeping with our theme, we present the following corollary to Proposition
3.3
, stating that elements of the Schwartz space in the closure of the compactly supported sections on their support.

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
\Sigma\rightarrow G
be a twist, where
G
is a locally compact étale groupoid. Let
L\colon G\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
be a continuous length function on
G
.

If
f\in H^{2,L,p}(\Sigma;G)
with open support
U\subseteq G
,
then
f\in\overline{C_{c}(\Sigma|_{U};U)}^{\|\cdot\|_{2,p,L}}
.
In particular, if
f\in H^{2,L}(\Sigma;G)
with open support
U\subseteq G
,
then
f\in H^{2,L}(\Sigma|_{U};U)
.

Proof.

The result follows immediately from Proposition
3.3
on noting that if
f\in H^{2,L,p}(\Sigma;G)
then
f(1+L)^{p}\in\ell^{2}(\Sigma;G)
.
∎

Definition 3.9

(
[
22
, Definition 2.10]
)
.

Let
G
be an étale groupoid and let
L\colon G\rightarrow[0,\infty)
be a length function on
G
.
Let
\Sigma\rightarrow G
be a twist over
G
.
The twist
\Sigma\rightarrow G
is said to have the
rapid decay property
with respect to a length function
L
if there exists a constant
c>0
and a non-zero integer
p\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
such that

(5)

\displaystyle\|f\|_{r}\leq c\|f\|_{2,p,L}

\displaystyle\text{ for all $f\in C_{c}(\Sigma;G)$}\,.

In
[
22
, Proposition 2.12]
it is shown that the rapid decay property does not depend on the specific twist
\Sigma
on
G
.
That is,
\Sigma\rightarrow G
has the rapid decay property with respect to a length function
L
, if and only if
G
has the rapid decay property with respect to
L
in the sense of
[
15
, Definition 3.2]
.

By
[
22
, Proposition 2.13]
, if a twist
\Sigma\rightarrow G
has the rapid decay property with respect to a length function
L
then
H^{2,L}(\Sigma;G)\subseteq C_{r}^{\ast}(\Sigma;G)
.
Further, if
L
is also continuous, then the Schwartz space
H^{2,L}(\Sigma;G)
is a dense Fréchet
*
-subalgebra of the reduced groupoid
C^{\ast}
- algebra
C_{r}^{\ast}(\Sigma;G)
.
In this case, by minor adjustments to
[
15
, Proposition 3.4]
,
G
having the rapid decay property with respect to
L
is equivalent to there being a
p\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
and a constant
c>0
such that

\|f\|_{r}\leq c\|f\|_{2,p,L}

for all
f
in
H^{2,L,p}(\Sigma;G)
.

We can now present the main theorem of this section, which says that if
G
has the rapid decay property with respect to some length function, then there is a dense subalgebra of
C_{r}^{*}(\Sigma;G)
for which there is an affirmative answer to Question
1
.
That is, for a dense subalgebra we can recover elements from the compactly supported sections on their supports.
The dense algebra in question is, of course, the Schwartz space
H^{2,L}(\Sigma;G)
.
This Theorem is a generalization of
[
3
, Theorem 1.1]
for groups with the rapid decay property.

Theorem 3.10
.

Let
G
be a locally compact Hausdorff étale groupoid and let
\Sigma\rightarrow G
be a twist of
G
.
Suppose
L
is a continuous length function on
G
for which
G
has the rapid decay property.
If
f\in H^{2,L}(\Sigma;G)\subseteq C_{r}^{*}(\Sigma;G)
and
\operatorname{supp}(f)=U
, then

f\in\overline{C_{c}(\Sigma|_{U};U)}^{\|\cdot\|_{r}}
.

Proof.

As
G
has the rapid decay property with respect to
L
we that
H^{2,L}(\Sigma;G)\subseteq C_{r}^{*}(\Sigma;G)
.
Further, there exists
p\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
and a constant
c>0
such that
\|g\|_{r}\leq c\|g\|_{2,p,L}
for all
g\in C_{c}(\Sigma;G)
.

Take any
f\in H^{2,L}(\Sigma;G)
and let
U=\operatorname{supp}(f)
be its open support.
Fix
\varepsilon>0
.
By Proposition
3.8
, there exists
g\in C_{c}(\Sigma|_{U};U)
such that

\|f-g\|_{2,p,L}<\frac{\varepsilon}{c}.

Hence
\|f-g\|_{r}<\varepsilon
, completing the result.
∎

3.3.
Conditionally negative definite length functions

We wish to strengthen Theorem
3.10
from the dense subalgebra
H^{2,L}(\Sigma;G)\subseteq C_{r}^{*}(\Sigma;G)
to all of
C_{r}^{*}(\Sigma;G)
.
To do this we will need extra conditions on the length function
L
.
We will do this in two (related) cases, in Theorem
3.14
and Theorem
4.8
.
We first discuss a class of multipliers on
C_{r}^{*}(\Sigma;G)
.

Let
\Sigma\rightarrow G
be a twist with
G
a locally compact Hausdorff étale groupoid.
We denote the set of compactly supported complex valued continuous functions and bounded complex valued continuous functions by
C_{c}(G)
and
C_{b}(G)
, respectively.
Henceforth, given
k\in C(G)
,
f\in C_{c}(\Sigma;G)
, we define

kf\in C_{c}(\Sigma;G)
to be the point-wise product.
That is, for all
\gamma\in G
,

\displaystyle kf(\gamma):=k(\gamma)f(\gamma).

Definition 3.11
.

A continuous function
k\colon G\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
is called a
positive-definite
function on
G
if for any
x
in
G^{(0)}
and a finite set
F\subset G_{x}
, the matrix

\displaystyle[k(\gamma\eta^{-1})]_{\eta,\gamma\in F}\in M_{F}({\mathbb{C}})

is positive-definite.

Versions of the following result can be found in varying amounts of generality, e.g. in
[
11
, Proposition 5.6.16]
and
[
21
, Lemma 4.2]
.
The strongest and most general version can be found in
[
16
, Proposition 3.6]
.

Proposition 3.12

(
[
16
, Proposition 3.6]
)
.

If
k\in C(G)
is a positive-definite bounded complex valued function,
then
M_{k}\colon C_{c}(\Sigma;G)\ni f\mapsto kf\in C_{c}(\Sigma;G)
extends to a completely positive map on
C_{r}^{\ast}(\Sigma;G)
and

\displaystyle\|M_{k}\|=\sup_{x\in G^{(0)}}|k(x)|=\sup_{g\in G}|k(\gamma)|.

Theorem 3.13
.

Let
G
be a locally compact Hausdorff étale groupoid, and let
\Sigma\rightarrow G
be a twist.
Suppose
(k_{i})_{i\in I}
is a uniformly bounded net of positive-definite complex valued continuous functions on
G
that converges uniformly to
1
on compact subsets of
G
.
If
f\in C_{r}^{\ast}(\Sigma;G)
, then
\|M_{k_{i}}(f)-f\|_{r}\rightarrow 0
.

Proof.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
.
Let
M
be a uniform bound for the net
(k_{i})_{i\in I}
.
That is,
\sup_{i\in I}\|k_{i}\|_{\infty}\leq M
.
By the definition of
C_{r}^{\ast}(\Sigma;G)
, there exists a sequence
(f_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\in C_{c}(\Sigma;G)
such that
\|f_{n}-f\|_{r}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
.
Thus there exists
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that for all
n\geq N
, we have

\displaystyle\|f_{n}-f\|_{r}<\frac{\varepsilon}{3M}\,.

By the triangle inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\|M_{k_{i}}(f)-f\|_{r}\leq\|M_{k_{i}}(f-f_{N})\|_{r}+\|M_{k_{i}}(f_{N})-f_{N}\|_{r}+\|f_{N}-f\|_{r}\,.

Since
\|M_{k_{i}}\|=\|k_{i}\|_{\infty}
by Proposition
3.12
, we have

\displaystyle\|M_{k_{i}}(f-f_{N})\|_{r}

\displaystyle\leq\|M_{k_{i}}\|\|f-f_{N}\|_{r}

\displaystyle\leq M\|f-f_{N}\|_{r}<\frac{\varepsilon}{3}.

By
[
16
, Lemma 4.5]
,
\lim_{i\in I}\|M_{k_{i}}(g)-g\|_{r}=0
for all
g\in C_{c}(\Sigma;G)
.
Hence, there exists
i_{0}\in I
such that

\displaystyle\|M_{k_{i}}(f_{N})-f_{N}\|_{r}<\frac{\varepsilon}{3}

\displaystyle\text{ for all }i\geq i_{0}\,.

Hence, for all
i\geq i_{0}
we have

\displaystyle\|M_{k_{i}}(f)-f\|_{r}<\varepsilon,

proving the result.
∎

Recall that a function
\psi\colon G\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
is
conditionally negative definite
if for each
x\in G^{(0)}
and
\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{n}\in G_{x}
given
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}\in{\mathbb{R}}
such that
\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}=0
we have that

\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}\psi(\gamma_{i}\gamma_{j}^{-1})\leq 0.

The following theorem gives a groupoid analogue of
[
4
, Corollary 6.5]
.

Theorem 3.14
.

Let
G
be locally compact Hausdorff étale groupoid, and let
\Sigma\rightarrow G
be a twist.
Suppose there is a conditionally negative definite length function
L
on
G
such that
G
has the rapid decay property with respect to
L
.

Take any
f\in C_{r}^{*}(\Sigma;G)
and let
U
be the open support of
f
.
Then
f\in\overline{C_{c}(\Sigma|_{U};U)}^{\|\cdot\|_{r}}
.

Proof.

Define a sequence of functions
k_{n}(\gamma)=e^{-\frac{L(\gamma)}{n}}\in C(G)
for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
Each
k_{n}
is positive definite since
L
is a conditionally negative definite, by e.g.
[
5
, Chapter 3, Theorem 2.2]
. Further, the sequence
(k_{n})_{n}
converges uniformly to
1
on compact subsets of
G
, (see the proof of
[
16
, Proposition 5.4]
).
Moreover, as
e^{-x}(1+x)^{s}\rightarrow 0
as
x\rightarrow\infty
for any positive
s\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{\gamma\in G}|k_{n}(\gamma)|(1+L(\gamma))^{p}:=B_{n}<\infty\,.

Let
f\in C_{r}^{\ast}(\Sigma;G)
, and let
U=\operatorname{supp}(f)
.
Fix any
p\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
Then

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in G^{(0)}}\sum_{{\gamma}\in G_{x}}|f({\gamma})|^{2}|k_{n}({\gamma})|^{2}(1+L({\gamma}))^{2p}

\displaystyle\leq B_{n}^{2}\sup_{x\in G^{(0)}}\sum_{{\gamma}\in G_{x}}|f({\gamma})|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq B_{n}^{2}{\|f\|_{2}^{2}}\leq B_{n}^{2}\|f\|_{r}^{2}.

Similarly,

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in G^{(0)}}\sum_{{\gamma}\in G_{x}}|f({\gamma})|^{2}|k_{n}({\gamma})|^{2}(1+L({\gamma}))^{2p}\leq B_{n}^{2}\|f\|_{r}^{2}.

Hence, for each
n
,

(6)

\displaystyle\|M_{k_{n}}(f)\|_{2,p,L}\leq B_{n}\|f\|_{r}<\infty\,.

Note also that
\|M_{k_{n}}(f)\|_{\infty}\leq\|f\|_{\infty}
.
Let
C_{c}(\Sigma;G)\ni\varphi_{k}\rightarrow f
as
k\rightarrow\infty
in the reduced norm
\|\cdot\|_{r}
.
By (
6
), for each
n
,

\displaystyle\|M_{k_{n}}(\varphi_{k})-M_{k_{n}}(f)\|_{2,p.L}\leq B_{n}\|\varphi_{k}-f\|_{r}\,.

Consequently, we have
M_{k_{n}}(f)\in H^{2,L}(\Sigma;G)
for all
n
.

As
G
has the rapid decay property with respect to
L
,
H^{2,L}(\Sigma;G)\subseteq C_{r}^{\ast}(\Sigma;G)
, and hence
M_{k_{n}}(f)\in C_{r}^{\ast}(\Sigma;G)
.
Further
\operatorname{supp}(M_{k_{n}}f)\subseteq\operatorname{supp}(f)=U
.

By Theorem
3.13
, we know
M_{k_{n}}(f)\rightarrow f
in
C_{r}^{\ast}(\Sigma;G)
as
n\to\infty
.
Fix
\varepsilon>0
.
There exists
n
such that

\|M_{k_{n}}(f)-f\|_{r}<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

By Theorem
3.10
, there exists
g\in C_{c}(\Sigma|_{U};U)
such that

\|M_{k_{n}}(f)-g\|_{r}<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\|f-g\|_{r}

\displaystyle\leq\|f-M_{k_{n}}(f)\|_{r}+\|M_{k_{n}}(f)-g\|_{r}<\varepsilon.

Thus
f\in\overline{C_{c}(\Sigma|_{U};U)}^{\|\cdot\|_{r}}
.
∎

Many groups satisfy the conditions of Theorem
3.14
, including free groups, and finitely generated Coxeter groups
[
17
, Theorem 5]
.
We refer the reader to the final paragraph of
[
7
]
for a longer list of such groups.

In
[
22
, Section 4]
, several permanence conditions are given for the rapid decay property.
We highlight one such condition here.
Let
G
be an étale Hausdorff groupoid satisfying the rapid decay property with respect to a length function
L
, and let
H
be any compact groupoid.
It is shown in
[
22
, Proposition 4.2]
that the groupoid
G\times H
has the rapid decay property with respect to the length function
\hat{L}
, defined by
\hat{L}(\gamma,\eta)=L(\gamma)
.
It is not hard to see that if
L
is conditionally negative definite, then so is
\hat{L}
.
Thus, if
G
satisfies the conditions of Theorem
4.8
, then so will
G\times H
.

4.
The Haagerup property and a related length function

The Haagerup property for locally compact Hausdorff étale groupoids was introduced in
[
16
]
.
This is used in
[
16
]
to study C
∗
-algebras of Fell bundles over groupoids satisfying the Haagerup property.
We will show that (a stronger condition than) the Haagerup approximation property of
[
16
]
can be used to construct a length function.
Our main theorem, Theorem
4.8
, involves twists which have the rapid decay property with respect to this length function.

Definition 4.1

(
[
16
, Proposition 5.4]
)
.

A
locally proper negative type
function on an étale locally compact Hausdorff groupoid
G
is a continuous function
\psi:G\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
satisfying the following:

(i)

\psi
is
normalized
, i.e.
\psi|_{G^{(0)}}=0
;

(ii)

\psi
is
symmetric
, i.e.
\psi(\gamma)=\psi(g^{-1})
for all
g\in G
;

(iii)

\psi
is conditionally negative definite;

(iv)

\psi
is
locally proper
, i.e. the function
(\psi,r,s)\colon G\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\times G^{(0)}\times G^{(0)}
is proper .

Definition 4.2

(
[
16
, Definition 5.6]
)
.

Let
G
be a
locally compact étale
groupoid.
We say the groupoid
G
has the
Haagerup property
if there exists a net
(k_{i})_{i\in I}
of positive-definite functions on
G
such that

(i)

each
k_{i}
is normalized, i.e.,
k_{i}|_{G^{(0)}}=1
;

(ii)

each
k_{i}
is a locally
C_{0}
-function, i.e. for each compact set
K\subset G^{(0)}
, we have
k_{i}|_{G_{K}^{K}}\in C_{0}(G_{K}^{K})
where
G_{K}^{K}:=r^{-1}(K)\cap s^{-1}(K)

(iii)

the net
(k_{i})_{i\in I}
converges to
1
uniformly on compact subsets of
G
.

We say that
G
has the
sequential Haagerup property
if the net
(k_{i})_{i\in I}
can be replaced by a sequence.

Remark 4.3
.

In
[
16
, Definition 5.6]
, the Haagerup property is defined for Fell bundles over étale groupoids.
Though we are primary focus of study is line bundles over groupoids, we only present the definition of the Haagerup approximation property for groupoids (without any bundle structure).
This is sufficient by
[
16
, Lemma 5.10]
: a twist
\Sigma\rightarrow G
satisfies
[
16
, Definition 5.6]
if and only if
G
satisfies Definition
4.2
.

Definition
4.1
and Definition
4.2
are related by the following Proposition.

Proposition 4.4

(cf.
[
16
, Proposition 5.4]
)
.

Let
G
be an étale locally compact Hausdorff groupoid.
Consider the following properties

(i)

there is a non-zero locally proper negative type function on
G
;

(ii)

G
has the sequential Haagerup property.

Then (i) implies (ii).
Further, if
G
is
\sigma
-compact then (i) and (ii) are equivalent.

Corollary 4.5
.

Let
G
be an étale locally compact second-countable Hausdorff groupoid.
Then
G
admits a non-zero locally proper negative type function if and only if
G
has the sequential Haagerup property.

We will show in Theorem
4.7
that a locally proper negative type function on a Hausdorff étale groupoid
G
induces a length function on the groupoid
G
.
When
G
is a discrete group this is
[
3
, Proposition 2.25]
.
As is done in
[
3
]
, we adapt arguments from
[
5
]
.
We include proofs here for completeness.
In the case when
G
is a discrete group, the following lemma follows from
[
5
, Chapter 3, Lemma 2.1]
.
The proof of Theorem
4.7
adapts the proof of
[
5
, Chapter 4, Proposition 3.3]
to the étale groupoid setting.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
G
be an étale Hausdorff groupoid.
Fix any
x\in G^{(0)}
.
Let
\psi\colon G\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a locally proper negative type function.
Fix any
x\in G^{(0)}
.
Then, for any
\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{n}\in G_{x}
,

[\psi(\gamma_{i})+\psi(\gamma_{j})-\psi(\gamma_{i}\gamma_{j}^{-1})]_{1\leq i,j\leq n}

is a positive-definite matrix.

Proof.

Fix
\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{n}\in G_{x}
, and let
\gamma_{0}=x
.
Take any
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
and set
\lambda_{0}=-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}
.
Thus
\sum_{i=0}^{n}\lambda_{i}=0
.
Applying condition (iii) of Defintion
4.1
to
\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{n}
, we have that
\sum_{j,k=0}^{n}\lambda_{j}\lambda_{k}\psi(\gamma_{j}\gamma_{k}^{-1})\leq 0
.

It follows that,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{j,k=0}^{n}\lambda_{j}\lambda_{k}\psi(\gamma_{j}\gamma_{k}^{-1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}\lambda_{k}\psi(\gamma_{j}\gamma_{k}^{-1})+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}\lambda_{0}\psi(\gamma_{j}\gamma_{0}^{-1})+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{0}\lambda_{k}\psi(x\gamma_{k}^{-1})+\lambda_{0}^{2}\psi(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}\lambda_{k}\psi(\gamma_{j}\gamma_{k}^{-1})+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}\lambda_{0}\psi(\gamma_{j}x)+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{0}\lambda_{k}\psi(x\gamma_{k}^{-1})

\displaystyle=-\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}\lambda_{k}(\psi(\gamma_{j}x)+\psi(\gamma_{k}x)-\psi(\gamma_{j}\gamma_{k}^{-1}))\qquad(\text{since }\lambda_{0}=-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{k})

\displaystyle=-\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}\lambda_{k}(\psi(\gamma_{j})+\psi(\gamma_{k})-\psi(\gamma_{j}\gamma_{k}^{-1})),

which proves the result.
∎

Theorem 4.7
.

If
\psi\colon G\rightarrow[0,\infty)
is a locally proper negative type function on a Hausdorff étale groupoid
G
, then
L:=\sqrt{\psi}
is a length function on the groupoid
G
.

Proof.

Take any
x\in G^{(0)}
and
\gamma,\eta\in G_{x}
.
The matrix

A_{x}:=\begin{bmatrix}2\psi(\gamma)&\psi(\gamma)+\psi(\eta)-\psi(\gamma\eta^{-1})\\
\psi(\gamma)+\psi(\eta)-\psi(\gamma\eta^{-1})&2\psi(\eta)&\end{bmatrix}

is positive-definite by Lemma
4.6
.
Thus
\det(A_{x})\geq 0
and hence

\displaystyle[\psi(\gamma)+\psi(\eta)-\psi(\gamma\eta^{-1})]^{2}\leq 4|\psi(\gamma)||\psi(\eta)|.

Taking the square root of the above inequality, we get

\displaystyle[\psi(\gamma)+\psi(\eta)-\psi(\gamma\eta^{-1})]\leq 2\sqrt{|\psi(\gamma)|}\sqrt{|\psi(\eta)|},

and so

\displaystyle|\psi(\gamma\eta^{-1})|

\displaystyle=|(\psi(\gamma\eta^{-1})-\psi(\gamma)-\psi(\eta)+\psi(\gamma)+\psi(\eta)|

\displaystyle\leq|\psi(\gamma)+\psi(\eta)|+|(\psi(\gamma\eta^{-1})-\psi(\gamma)-\psi(\eta))|

\displaystyle\leq|\psi(\gamma)|+|\psi(\eta)|+2\sqrt{|\psi(\gamma)|}\sqrt{|\psi(\eta)|}

\displaystyle=(\sqrt{|\psi(\gamma)|}+\sqrt{|\psi(\eta)|})^{2}\,.

Consequently, by taking square root on the both sides of the above inequality and using symmetric property of
\psi
, one gets

\displaystyle\sqrt{\psi(\gamma\eta^{-1})}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\psi(\gamma)}+\sqrt{\psi(\eta)}=\sqrt{\psi(\gamma)}+\sqrt{\psi(\eta^{-1})}.

Hence,
L=\sqrt{\psi}
is a sub-additive function.
As
\psi
is normalized and symmetric,
L
is normalized and symmetric.
Consequently,
L
is a length function on
G
.
∎

When
G
is a group with a locally proper negative type function
\psi
, the length function
L=\sqrt{\psi}
is called a
Haagerup length function
in
[
3
]
.
The following is a groupoid analogue of
[
3
, Theorem 5.9]
.

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
G
be locally compact Hausdorff étale groupoid, and let
\Sigma\rightarrow G
be a twist.
Suppose there is a non-zero locally proper negative type function
\psi
on
G
, and that
G
has the rapid decay property with respect to the length function
L=\sqrt{\psi}.

Take any
f\in C_{r}^{*}(\Sigma;G)
and let
U
be the open support of
f
.
Then
f\in\overline{C_{c}(\Sigma|_{U};U)}^{\|\cdot\|_{r}}
.

In particular, the conclusion holds if
G
is second-countable and satisfies the Haagerup property and has the rapid decay property with respect to the induced length function.

Proof.

The proof is similar to the proof of Theorem
3.14
.
We outline the details.
Define a sequence of functions
k_{n}(\gamma)=e^{-\frac{\psi(\gamma)}{n}}\in C(G)
for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
We again have that the sequence
(k_{n})_{n}
are positive-definite, bounded by
1
in
\sup
-norm, and converge uniformly to
1
on compact subsets of
G
.
Further, as
e^{-x}(1+\sqrt{x})^{s}\rightarrow 0
as
x\rightarrow\infty
for any positive
s\in\mathbb{R}
, we have
|k_{n}(\gamma)|(1+L(\gamma))^{p}

is bounded for each
p
, where
L=\sqrt{\psi}
.
The rest of the proof now follows the same argument as that of Theorem
3.14
.
∎

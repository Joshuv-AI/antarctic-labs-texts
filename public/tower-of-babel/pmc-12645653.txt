Stiefel MD Anderson OroPharynx cancer (MDA-OPC) cohort: a single-institution, prospective longitudinal outcomes study
Authors: Amy Moreno, Ariana J Sahli, Faye Johnson, Xiaowen Sun, Carly Barbon, Waree Rinsurongkawong, Wenye Song, Flavie M Luciani, Han Liang, Jun Li, Wei Liu, J Jack Lee, SJ Frank, Stephen Lai, Clifton Fuller, Katherine Hutcheson, P01 MD Anderson Oropharynx Cancer Program

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeThe MD Anderson Oropharynx Cancer (MDA-OPC) cohort is a unique single-institution, prospective longitudinal cancer cohort. The cohort aims to enhance the therapeutic index of OPC management by supporting data needs for independent investigators to conduct rigorous observational studies examining exposures and factors associated with acute and late toxicities, cancer progression, recurrence, new malignancies and quality of life in OPC survivors.ParticipantsA total of 1811 patients with OPC with a minimum follow-up of 6 months have been consented to our prospective registry between 18 March 2015 and 29 December 2023. Clinical and treatment (Tx) data are available on all patients, including previously untreated patients (1443, 80%). Most previously untreated patients (97%) consented to longitudinal patient-reported outcomes and functional assessments for critical time points including pre-Tx, during-Tx and post-Tx at 3–6 months, 12 months, 18–24 months and annually up to 5 years.Findings to dateThe median age for the MDA-OPC cohort is 66 years (range, 25–96) with the majority being male (89%), white (92%) and with human papillomavirus (HPV)/p16-associated OPC (88%) primarily located in the tongue base or tonsil (90%). For previously untreated patients, 79% were diagnosed with stage I/II disease, and nearly half underwent curative intent chemoradiation. Overall survival was significantly higher for HPV/p16-associated OPC at 1 year (98% vs 93%) and 5 years (83% vs 54%; p<0.0001) compared with HPV-negative disease. Longitudinal PRO and clinician-graded toxicity outcomes (eg, osteoradionecrosis, neuropathy, dysphagia) are summarised.Future plansFuture work includes expansion of the MDA-OPC cohort and survivorship surveillance to 10 years under the recently funded OPC-SURVIVOR research programme (P01CA285249), which aims to identify non-invasive, clinic-ready biomarkers and examine novel phenotypes and mechanistically matched mitigation strategies for latent OPC sequelae. Additionally, we aim to expand our advanced data infrastructure by integrating large data streams from parallel clinical trials and imaging registries.Trial registration numbersNCT01893307, NCT03145077.Keywords: Head & neck tumours, ONCOLOGY, Patient Reported Outcome Measures, Clinical Protocols

Introduction

Oropharyngeal cancer (OPC), or primary malignancies arising from the tongue base, tonsil, soft palate and pharynx, afflicts 450 000 global patients annually, with 53 000 cases originating in the USA alone.1 2 Human papillomavirus (HPV)-attributable OPC comprises the majority of modern cases in a disease formerly predominated by tobacco as the primary exposure.3 For example, 20% of OPC cases were HPV-associated three decades ago; nowadays, HPV-associated cancers account for roughly 70% of new OPC.2 3 This disease represents a substantial burden in the broader cancer landscape, and despite progress with vaccination, the HPV-associated rise in OPC is not projected to abate for 25 years. That is, with current vaccination rates, it is projected that its incidence will not start to decline until at least 2045.4

HPV-associated OPC is highly curable (2 and 5 year overall survival (OS) 95% and 79%, respectively)5,7; yet, curative therapy may hold a high health loss (ie, treatment-related toxicities) and quality of life (QOL). The most prevalent acute toxicities (during and early months after treatment) are mucositis causing mouth/throat pain, taste changes and dysphagia (difficulty swallowing), followed by xerostomia (dry mouth).8 When analysing patient-reported outcomes (PROs), approximately half of patients with OPC report severe symptom burden during treatment, and more than 20% of patients require and remain gastrostomy tube dependent for nutritional intake for several months after nonsurgical treatment.9

Most acute toxicities and symptom profiles improve with full or at least partial recovery within the first year after treatment.10 For an important minority of survivors, however, severe toxicity and symptoms persist into long-term survivorship as chronic disabilities. Chief among these issues is dysphagia, which approached a 3 year prevalence of 50% in an OPC population-level analysis11 and is a symptom associated with decisional regret about treatment among survivors.12 Two other highly morbid sequelae that may present late during survivorship are osteoradionecrosis of the jaw (ORN) and lower cranial neuropathy (LCNP). ORN is a severe iatrogenic disease state that is experienced by 7–15% of OPC survivors13,17 with advanced stages of ORN mandating extensive healthcare resource utilisation for surgical resection of necrotic bone.18 19 By 5 years after treatment, our group has published that 5% of survivors develop LCNP with cumulative lifetime risks exceeding 10%.20 Late feeding tube, tracheostomy and radiation-associated dysphagia (L-RAD) almost exclusively occur in survivors who develop late LCNP.21,23 These inter-related yet distinct delayed sequelae are typically studied in siloes by different investigators and using different datasets despite the critical need to manage the care of the survivor as a whole person.

The absence of well-curated long-term survivor cohorts was the stimulus for the development of the Stiefel MD Anderson Cancer Centre Oropharynx Cancer (MDA-OPC) cohort, an active, single-institution, prospective longitudinal cancer cohort. The MDA-OPC cohort is significant as it fills an unmet need for comprehensive OPC disease-specific data, which includes interdisciplinary characterisation of exposures, diagnostic/staging, treatment, disease control and longitudinal collection of validated clinician- and patient-reported survivorship outcomes. More recently, the MDA-OPC cohort received National Institutes of Health (NIH)/NCI P01 funding support (P01CA285249-01A1) as a core resource in the newly formed OPC-SURVIVOR research programme to explore novel toxicity diagnosis and mitigation strategies on trial and to extend survivorship outcomes analysis up to 10 year post-therapy. This cohort profile provides an overview of the MDA-OPC programme study design, cohort characteristics and key clinical, symptom, toxicity and patient-reported and clinician-graded functional outcomes data available to date.

Cohort description

Ethics approval

This prospective cohort study was initiated in March 2015 after approval of protocol PA14-0947 by MDA’s Institutional Review Board.

Study design and participants

Protocol eligibility criteria include all patients evaluated in the Head and Neck Centre at The University of Texas MD Anderson Cancer Centre (Houston, TX) and Houston Area Locations with a suspected or confirmed pathologic diagnosis of squamous cell carcinoma of the oropharynx (or metastatic squamous carcinoma to the neck from an unknown primary). The PA14-0947 protocol enables investigators to prospectively collect longitudinal PROs directly from the patient, clinical data from various electronic sources (ie, electronic health record (EHR), laboratory and imaging systems) and tissue samples (stored using protocols LAB03-0320, LAB11-0088 and LAB08-0848). Patients who plan to receive treatment at MDA are approached for completion of PRO questionnaires pretreatment (ie, baseline), during treatment and after therapy at the following key time points: 3 to 6 months, 12 months (±2 months), 18 to 24 months and annually to 5 years.

With the launch of the OPC SURVIVOR P01 Programme in August 2024, the protocol was amended to extend annual PRO collection up to 10 years post-therapy and to provide options to consent to physical and functional assessments using clinician-graded palpation scores for lymphedema/fibrosis per published Head and Neck-Lymphedema Fibrosis (HN-LEF),24 intramuscular electromyography (EMG) with or without tongue surface EMG to assess hypoglossal nerve activity and diet quality assessments.

In general, trained research staff approach nearly all consecutively eligible participants presenting to MDA with an average enrolment of 210 subjects annually or 83% of eligible index OPC cases seen at MDA. Approach and consenting methods include both in-person interactions with patients during scheduled clinical visits and remote interactions over telephone or videoconferencing. For patients who previously consented prior to the OPC-SURVIVOR funding, a new guideline was established by the research team to approach already enrolled patients during routine annual visits to reconsent for extended surveillance up to 10 years. Figure 1 demonstrates a flow diagram of patient screening, approaching, consenting and potential causes of attrition. Post-enrolment screening failures were high during the first few years of the study (n=589 taken off study in figure 1), but this was corrected with stricter consenting procedures, which increased annualised retention of eligible participants from 66% (2015–2019) to 95% (2020–2023).

Data collection and infrastructure

Clinical data for the MDA-OPC cohort includes patient socio-demographic (age at consent, sex, ethnicity, race, smoking history) and cancer characteristics (OPC site of primary tumor, laterality, American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) eighth edition clinical and/or pathologic TNM staging,25 HPV and/or p16 status). Treatment details cover disposition (ie, curative intent or not) and prior treatment for an OPC diagnosis. Standardised treatment details for the first-line multimodal therapy include dates of any oncologic OPC-related surgery (ie, tonsillectomy, use of transoral robotic surgical approach (TORS), neck dissection), radiation therapy (RT) and receipt of induction and/or concurrent systemic therapy with RT. During follow-up, oncologic outcomes being collected include disease status and whether a second head and neck cancer (HNC) is diagnosed during follow-up. Primary treatment-related toxicities include feeding tube use, provider-graded ORN and LCNP.

To comprehensively assess long-term symptom burden, functional impact and QOL among survivors, the MDA-OPC cohort includes a set of validated PRO instruments and clinician-graded toxicity and functional outcomes (patient-reported outcomes and functional (PROF)) assessments. These tools were selected to capture the wide-ranging effects of cancer treatment, including physical symptoms, functional challenges and overall well-being. Specific PRO instruments collected on this unique cancer patient population include:

The MD Anderson Symptom Inventory-Head and Neck Module (MDASI-HN)26. This PRO system (scoring: 0, not present, to 10, as bad as you can imagine) includes nine HNC-specific items including difficulty swallowing, dry mouth, taste alterations and voice changes—symptoms that are particularly relevant to radiation-associated toxicities. In addition, it captures 13 items covering general cancer-related symptoms (ie, fatigue, pain, disturbed sleep) and six interference items to evaluate how symptoms affect daily functioning, mood, work and social activities.

The MD Anderson Dysphagia Inventory (MDADI)27: the MDADI (scoring: five point Likert scale) is a validated 20 item PRO that evaluates the physical, functional, emotional and global impacts of dysphagia on QOL. The MDADI composite score is calculated from the mean of 19 subscale scores, multiplied by 20 and ranges from 20 to 100, with higher scores indicating better swallow-related QOL (poor: <60, adequate: 60–79, optimal: ≥80).

A secure, well-curated REDCap research project was developed in 2015 and has been serially expanded to include 22 instruments for clinical and PROF data collection. In general, most clinical data elements in REDCap are either automatically imported on a daily basis via a clinical data pull from our EHR system or manually entered by our data coordinators, typically within the same month of consent. Overall missing data rates for key data elements are low, including sex (0%), smoking history (0.8%), race (0%), TNM staging (0%) and HPV status (3.4%). Longitudinal clinical data abstraction remains necessary to capture evolving clinical outcomes (eg, recurrence, toxicity events). Currently, this information is manually captured through REDCap reports prompting: (1) weekly updates of a short-term priority patient list to complete initial cancer therapy summaries and (2) standardised 6 month cohort updates. Manual data entry is heavily structured with standardised operating procedures and defined presets within the MDA-OPC database to minimise data heterogeneity. All PRO instruments have been designed as electronic surveys, allowing for automated emails to patients for electronic PRO collection at pre-defined scheduled time points (see table 1).

As of 2022, we have launched an informatics core for the program that focuses on developing scalable, automated data curation processes using MDA’s Context Engine. The Context Engine Repository allows ready access to integrated, high-dimensional data including clinical, radiological and molecular data, clinical trial outcomes and pathology quality control metrics. Key components of the Context Engine Repository include Caboodle/Epic Electronic Health Record, FIRE1.0 Relational Data Store (FIRE - Federated Institutional Reporting Environment), FIRE2.0 Data Lake, Integration of Imaging Datasets (PACS), and Biospecimen Information Management System (BIMS). The informatics core collects supplemental clinical data from the Context Engine, primarily related to granular treatment details (i.e., ancillary surgical procedures, list of systemic therapy agents and dosing).

Findings to date

Cohort Characteristics

From 3/18/2015 to 12/29/2023, a total of 3,989 HNC patients were assessed for eligibility, 3068 were approached, and a total of 1811 were enrolled for longitudinal clinical data collection only or clinical with PROF data (n=1403, figure 1) and had follow-up data available for at least 6-months post-therapy (June 2024). An analysis of the 1,811 patients, stratified by categorisation as a previously untreated (n=1,443) or previously treated (n=368), is detailed in table 2. Median age at time of diagnosis for the overall cohort is 66 years (range, 25–96), and the majority are male (89%), white (92%), and have HPV-associated OPC (88%). Base of tongue and tonsil tumours are the most common primary sites (46 and 44%, respectively). Their disease was generally managed with multimodality therapy as demonstrated by 43% receiving concurrent chemoradiation (CRT). Overall, 1391 (77%) are alive and without evidence of disease. Using the AJCC eighth edition clinical staging system, over three quarters of the cohort have stage I/II disease.

Trends in survival

Survival analysis and adverse events for the previously untreated, curative-intent cohort (N=1,402) are reported herein. OS at 1, 2 and 4 years is 97.4% (95% CI, 96.3 to 98.2), 93.4% (91.6 to 94.8) and 85.3% (82.3 to 87.9), respectively. Figure 2 demonstrates OS stratified by HPV status, overall clinical stage group and treatment modality (unimodality or multimodality). HPV as a favourable prognostic indicator is reflected in figure 2A with a 1, 2 and 4 year OS rate of 98%, 94% and 88% for HPV-associated disease and 93%, 84% and 63% for HPV-negative disease, respectively (P<.0001).

CRT, chemoradiation; IC, induction systemic therapy; HPV, human papilloma virus; OS, overall survival; RT, radiation therapy; S, surgery.

The new eighth edition AJCC staging system incorporates HPV status for improved stage separation as noted in figure 2B where the median OS has not been reached for stages I/II, is 79.6 months for stage III and 47.9 months for stage IV. This corresponds to a 2 year OS of 97%, 91%, 88% and 79% for each advancing stage group (P<.0001). Single modality therapy (ie, surgery vs RT) is generally used for patients with early stage, low-volume disease. This subgroup exhibited a 2 year OS rate of 94% for RT and 100% for surgery (P=0.011, figure 2C). There were trends towards inferior survival by multimodality for non-surgical patients treated with induction chemotherapy (IC)+RT/CRT, likely representative of advanced OPC cases. The 2 year OS was 98% for surgery with RT/CRT (S+RT/CRT), 94% for CRT, 91% for IC+S+RT/CRT and 84% for IC+RT/CRT (p<.001, figure 2D).

Adverse events

For patients who were previously untreated, 593 (41%) required placement of a feeding tube at some point during their care or survivorship. A total of 51 (3.5%) had confirmed diagnosis of LCNP. Distribution of cranial nerves IX, X and XII affected was 7, 8 and 36, respectively, and only two patients exhibited multi-nerve LCNP. With regard to ORN, 64 (4%) of the cohort had a clinician-graded ORN diagnosis with 21 (33%) experiencing a Tsai grade three or 4 ORN at some point post-therapy. The median time to clinician-graded ORN within this subgroup was 18.5 months (range, 2.3–87.1 months).

PROs and clinician-graded function overview

For the previously untreated, curative subset of the MDA-OPC cohort (n=1402), the total counts of unique patients with MDADI, DIGEST, MDASI-core symptom and MDASI-HN symptom scores were 1175, 1054, 1239 and 1234, respectively. Trends from baseline up to 5 years post-therapy are summarised in figure 3. Panel 3A illustrates the distribution and average trends of MDADI composite scores, and at baseline, 75% (886/1175) of patients reported optimal swallow-related quality of life (MDADI composite ≥80). There is a marked decline in MDADI composite scores immediately following treatment. While average scores gradually improve over time, many survivors continue to report sub-optimal MDADI 60–79 score range (31%, 115/367) to poor <60 score range (6%, 23/367) swallowing-related QOL at 5-years, highlighting persistent swallowing difficulty in survivorship.

Clinician-rated DIGEST grading (figure 3B) revealed a shift in pharyngeal swallow function over time. At baseline, the majority of patients exhibited no (grade 0, 71%) or mild (grade 1, 23%) dysphagia. However, by 3–6 months post-treatment, the proportion of patients with functional swallowing declined by nearly 40%, reflecting an increase in subacute dysphagia. Moderate to severe dysphagia (grades 2–3) peaked during this period among nearly half of the cohort. While partial recovery of swallow function was observed by 18–24 months, a substantial proportion continued to experience at least moderate dysphagia at 5 years. Notably, 14% of patients demonstrate severe dysphagia at long-term follow-up, underscoring persistent functional burden among survivors.

MDASI core symptom trends represented in figure 3C rose sharply in severity from baseline to end of RT, with fatigue, dry mouth, drowsiness, disturbed sleep and pain among the most prominent. This acute symptom burden peaked at treatment completion, followed by improvement by 3–6 month follow-up. Most symptoms stabilised by 12 months; however, some symptoms—particularly dry mouth—remained persistently elevated at 60 months, reflecting long-term impact. In contrast, symptoms such as nausea, vomiting and loss of appetite were consistently low in severity throughout the follow-up period, demonstrated by their relatively flat trajectories.

Figure 3D shows distinct trajectories for head and neck-specific symptoms (per MDASI-HN) over time. Difficulty swallowing, taste disturbance, mucus production and mucositis peaked sharply at the end of treatment, with taste and mucositis reaching the highest severity. While most symptoms improved by 3–6 months, difficulty swallowing and mucus production symptoms remained elevated and did not fully resolve by 5 years. In contrast, symptoms such as skin discomfort, choking, dental issues and constipation resolved earlier, returning to near-baseline levels and indicating more transient treatment effects.

Strengths and limitations

The MDA-OPC study represents to our knowledge the largest prospective, well-curated long-term OPC survivorship cancer cohort, offering a unique opportunity for impactful survivorship research in this population. We have demonstrated the value of this comprehensive outcomes study through various publications including latent symptom burden evaluation in elders (≥65 years),31 symptom burden comparisons between surgical and non-surgical approaches32 and risk factors associated with patient-reported voice and speech symptoms.33 Through standardised data collection and integration in a robust informatics platform, we are able to explore clinically relevant endpoints such as treatment failure and survival and how these may be impacted by precursor clinical biomarkers (ie, dysphagia severity and/or shortness of breath).34 Beyond PROs, we have gathered longitudinal data on rare but potentially disabling treatment-related complications such as ORN and LCNP. Despite modern advances in multimodal therapy, LCNP rates affect at least 4% of our OPC survivors, translating to overall worse symptom burden profiles, swallowing function and QOL.21 35 ORN incidence and severity rates are notoriously underestimated in most head and neck clinical practices due to lack of consensus-based reporting standards. This is detrimental as ORN tends to compound the symptom burden experienced by survivors,14 and costs of management if diagnosed late can exceed $74 000 per patient.19 Leveraging the MDA-OPC cohort toxicity data, we have identified radiation dose-volume correlates associated with ORN risk and have contributed to international recommendations for prevention and management of ORN.18 36

While the cohort provides a valuable resource for investigating long-term survivorship outcomes, it may face challenges related to data completeness and consistency, particularly as follow-up extends over the survivor’s lifetime. Survivors and clinical providers describe a ‘quiet period’ in surveillance during which acute toxicities have resolved before the late toxicities become significant. These trends are observed in clinical and population data with a resurgence of toxicities and/or symptoms occurring after 5 years.37 38 Given our recent grant funding initiated in August 2024 (P01CA285249), we aim to address this limitation through retention and late-effects enrichment of our existing MDA-OPC cohort to include outcomes to 10 years and beyond. This unprecedented data collection is designed to foster programme research to uncover fundamental insights about the evolution of late effects and key determinants of these outcomes. Another potential limitation is that the socio-demographics of the MDA-OPC cohort may differ from population-based distributions and has notable differences with international OPC cohorts including a higher proportion of HPV-positive disease, males and never-smokers.3 39 However, our institution is a world-leading cancer centre with over half of patients originating from Texas, a third from other states and a notable portion of international patients.40 Our cohort is therefore a valuable resource for external validation of clinical decision support tools and/or machine-/deep-learning models developed by other investigators seeking to perform rigorous validation on tools made for translation into clinical practice.41,43

Future directions

Future work is closely associated with aims outlined by our OPC-SURVIVOR research program that operates three research projects under three cores (Core A-administration, Core B-Informatics and Biostatistics, Core C-PROF). First, we aim to maintain and strategically grow the cohort and data infrastructure through recently established multi-trial co-consenting procedures and leveraging a high-throughput data platform (ie, Palantir Foundry). Our institution has demonstrated the ability of this platform to aid in extraction, integration, analysis and transformation of clinical and outcomes data merged from various sources into unified datasets, with accuracy for key elements as high as 95–99%.44 Second, to maximise the utility of our cohort and promote data sharing and exploration within the cancer research community, we are also developing an artificial intelligence-powered platform that leverages a large language model-based chatbot for interactive data access, exploration and analysis to drive new discoveries.44 Future projects will primarily centre on biomarker discovery, advanced symptom and toxicity modeling techniques, and early-phase interventional trials aiming to prevent and/or mitigate the development of late effects such as ORN, L-RAD and LCNP. Overall, these efforts will lay the foundation for data-driven models that transform how we understand, predict and improve long-term outcomes for OPC survivors.

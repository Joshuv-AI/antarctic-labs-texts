Klein coverings over hyperelliptic genus 3 curves
Paweł Borówka, Angela Ortega

Abstract
. We characterize the moduli space of étale Klein coverings (i.e. Galois with deck group ℤ 2 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2} ) of hyperelliptic curves of genus 3. We prove that the Prym map on each component is injective. As an application, we show that the Prym map of étale Klein coverings of genus 3 curves is generically finite.



Introduction

Let
H
be a hyperelliptic smooth projective curve over
\mathbb{C}
of genus 3. We consider Klein coverings
f:\widetilde{C}\rightarrow H
, that is,

\widetilde{C}
admits an action by a group of fixed point free automorphisms
V_{4}
isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
such that
H=\widetilde{C}/V_{4}
.
One can associate to such a covering a polarised abelian sixfold in the following way. Let
\Nm_{f}:J\widetilde{C}\rightarrow JH
be the norm map, sending the divisor class
[\sum_{i}n_{i}p_{i}]
to
[\sum_{i}n_{i}f(p_{i})]
,
with
p_{i}\in\widetilde{C}
and
n_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
. The Prym variety
P(f)
of the covering
f
is defined as the connected component of 0 of the kernel of
\Nm_{f}
. It is an abelian subvariety of
J\widetilde{C}
of dimension the difference of the genera of the curves, which in this case is 6 and the restriction of the principal polarisation on
J\widetilde{C}
induces a non-principal one on
P(f)
.

Prym varieties of étale double covers have been extensively studied in the past and more recently it has been proven that
the Prym map is injective for double covers ramified in at least six points (
[
9
]
). For étale cyclic coverings over a hyperelliptic curve, the (generic) injectivity
of the Prym has been shown for infinitely many degrees (
[
10
]
,

[
11
]
). There are also new results on the fiber of the Prym map for non-cyclic coverings in low genera (
[
13
]
).

Klein coverings can be defined from the curve
H
by choosing a subgroup

\langle\eta,\xi\rangle
of order four of the group
JH[2]
of 2-torsion points in
JH
.
We distinguish two types of Klein coverings depending whether the subgroup is isotropic or not with respect to the Weil pairing on
JH[2]
.
Let
\mathcal{RH}_{3}^{iso}
, respectively
\mathcal{RH}_{3}^{ni}
, denote the moduli space parametrising isotropic, respectively non-isotropic, Klein
coverings on hyperelliptic curves of genus 3.
Each of these moduli spaces consists of two irreducible components (see Lemma
1.9
).

Consider now the Prym map that sends
[f:\widetilde{C}\rightarrow H]\in\mathcal{RH}_{3}^{V_{4}}=\mathcal{RH}_{3}^{iso}\sqcup\mathcal{RH}_{3}^{ni}
to the
polarised Prym variety
(P,\Xi)
. In this article we continue the study of the injectivity of the Prym map for Klein
coverings of genus
3
curves, which was started in
[
3
]
, where we prove the injectivity on the locus of hyperelliptic coverings
f:\widetilde{C}\rightarrow H
(i.e.
\widetilde{C}
is also hyperelliptic)
corresponding to the case I.1 below. Let us denote by

{\mathcal{A}}_{g}^{\delta}
the moduli space of polarised abelian varieties of dimension
g
with polarisation type
\delta
.
The main theorem of the paper is the following.

Theorem 0.1
.

The Prym maps

\mathcal{P}^{iso}_{3}:\mathcal{RH}_{3}^{iso}\rightarrow{\mathcal{A}}_{6}^{(1,1,1,2,2,4)}\qquad\mathcal{P}^{ni}_{3}:\mathcal{RH}_{3}^{ni}\rightarrow{\mathcal{A}}_{6}^{(1,1,1,1,4,4)}

are injective on each irreducible component of the source moduli space.

Actually, the injectivity also holds on the entire moduli space
\mathcal{RH}_{3}^{V_{4}}
since the dimensions of the factors appearing in the isotypical decomposition of the associated Prym variety, depends on the type of the Klein covering and it is different for each irreducible component.

The general idea of the proof, with some variations in each case, is the following. A Prym variety
(P,\Xi)
in the image of the Prym map determines a group of automorphisms of
P
isomorphic to

\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{4}
(see Proposition
1.12
). This gives us the isotypical decomposition of
P
and one can identify the Jacobians by means of the type of the restricted polarisation and using the action of the involutions on the kernel of the polarisation. With this information we can reconstruct the quotients curves and finally recover
\widetilde{C}
as a fibered product.

As an application, we can show the following theorem for Klein coverings over
any
curve of genus 3, see Theorem
6.2
.

Theorem 0.2
.

The Klein Prym maps

\mathcal{P}^{iso}_{3}:\mathcal{R}_{3}^{iso}\rightarrow{\mathcal{A}}_{6}^{(1,1,1,2,2,4)}\qquad\mathcal{P}^{ni}_{3}:\mathcal{R}_{3}^{ni}\rightarrow{\mathcal{A}}_{6}^{(1,1,1,1,4,4)}

are generically finite. Moreover they are of degree
1
on images of coverings of hyperelliptic curves of types
I.2,II.1,II.2
.

The structure of the paper is as follows. In Section 1, we state basic definitions and key lemmas and show that the moduli space have 4 components, called I.1, I.2, II.1, II.2. Each one of the Sections 2, 3, 4 and 5 is devoted to one of these cases and to the proof of the injectivity of the corresponding Prym map. Finally, Section 6 is devoted to Klein coverings of any genus 3 curve.

1.
Preliminaries

We start by setting up some general facts about Klein coverings.

The isotypical decomposition of

In order to simplify the notation, if the pullback map is an embedding, we abuse the notation by skipping the map (for example, we write

) and if it is not an embedding, we mark the subvariety with a star (for example, we write

Looking at the construction from bottom-up perspective, we can distinguish two cases of Klein coverings. Recall the commutator map,
[
1

, that in case of Jacobians and twice the principal polarisation, we call the Weil pairing.

Definition 1.2
.

We call a Klein covering isotropic if the group
\left<\eta,\xi\right>\subset JC[2]
defining the covering is isotropic with respect to the Weil pairing. Otherwise, we call the covering non-isotropic.

Being isotropic or not determine the type of the induced polarisation and affects the decomposition of

.

Lemma 1.3
.

Let
\langle\eta,\xi\rangle\simeq\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
be a Klein subgroup of
JH[2]

If
\langle\eta,\xi\rangle
is isotropic, respectively non-isotropic, with respect to the Weil form in
JH[2]
, the
polarisation type of the restriction
\Xi=\tilde{\Theta}_{|P}

to the corresponding Prym variety is
(1,\ldots,1,2,2,4,\ldots,4)
, respectively
(1,\ldots,1,1,4,\ldots,4)
.

Proof.

Set
G:=\left<\eta,\xi\right>
.
Let
f^{*}:JH\to J\widetilde{C}
be the pullback map. Then
f^{*}\tilde{\Theta}=4\Theta_{H}
and

, see
[
1
, Prop 11.4.3]
.

Consider the quotient map
\pi:JH\to JH/G
. If

is isotropic with respect to the Weil form on

then, in order to check that

is a polarised isogeny, by
[

, Prop 6.3.5]
it is enough to show that we can endow

with a polarisation

(of type
(2,\ldots,2)
). Since

is of degree 4, we find that

is of type
(1,1,2,\ldots,2)
. Now, since

, the restricted polarisation on

If

a polarised isogeny and hence

Now, the result follows from the fact that the complementary abelian subvariety has complementary type, see
[
1
, Cor. 12.1.5]
.
∎

Lemma 1.4
.

The (restricted) kernel
\ker h_{\sigma}^{*}|_{P(h_{\sigma})}
is trivial in the non-isotropic case, hence

is embedded in

\ker h_{\eta}^{*}=\langle k_{\eta}^{*}(\xi)\rangle

k_{\eta}^{*}JH\cap P(k_{\eta})=k_{\eta}^{*}((\ker k_{\eta}^{*})^{\bot})

On the other hand, in the isotropic case

We also need a way to check whether the pullback map is an embedding.

Corollary 1.6
.

Let

be a Klein covering. Then

Definition 1.7
.

By Strong Torelli Theorem (see
[
1
, Ex. 11.19]
) for non-hyperelliptic curves
\widetilde{C}
, we have
\Aut(J\widetilde{C})/(-1)=\Aut(\widetilde{C})
. Motivated by this result, we call an automorphism
\sigma\in\Aut(J\widetilde{C})

\widetilde{C}

\widetilde{C}

Remark 1.8

Note that if

1.2.
Hyperelliptic curves

Let
H
be a smooth hyperelliptic genus
g
curve. A Klein covering of
H
is given by a
Klein subgroup
\langle\eta,\xi\rangle\simeq\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2}
of
JH[2]
.
In the hyperelliptic case all the 2-torsion points
can be written in terms of the set of Weierstrass points

W:=\{w_{1},\ldots,w_{2g+2}\}\subset H
. We refer to
[
6
]
for the following description.
Given a subset
S\subset I=\{1,\ldots,2g+2\}
the divisor

(1.3)

\alpha_{S}=\sum_{i\in S}w_{i}-|S|\cdot w_{2g+2}

defines an element in
JH[2]
. Observe that
\alpha_{S}=\alpha_{I\setminus S}
.
Denote by
E_{g}
the
\mathbb{F}_{2}
-vector space of functions
I\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{2}
having an even number of
0
’s and
1
’s modulo the constant functions
\{0,1\}
. The elements of
E_{g}
are represented by the subsets of even cardinality (up
to complementary subset) and clearly
E_{g}\simeq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2g}
. The correspondence
S\mapsto\alpha_{S}
gives an isomorphism

E_{g}\simeq JC[2]
, so all the 2-torsion points on a hyperelliptic Jacobian are of the form (
1.3
). Moreover,
E_{g}
carries a symmetric bilinear form

e:E_{g}\times E_{g}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{2},\qquad e(S,T)=|S\cap T|\mbox{ mod }2,

which is a non-degenerated symplectic form. Under the above isomorphism, this form corresponds to the Weil pairing on the 2-torsion points on
JH
, for details see
[
6
, Section 5.2]
.
A subspace
G\subset E_{g}
is
isotropic
with respect to
e
if
e(\alpha_{S},\alpha_{T})=0
(that is, if the divisors
\alpha_{S}
and
\alpha_{T}
share
an even number of points) for all
\alpha_{S},\alpha_{T}\in G
; otherwise the subspace
G
is called
non-isotropic
.

Since
2w_{i}
is linearly equivalent to
2w_{j}
for any
i,j
, we can also write any 2-torsion point on
JH[2]
as a sum of differences of Weierstrass points. This presentation is better for the purpose of the paper as it does not involve choosing a base point
w_{2g+2}
.

1.3.
Four cases of Klein coverings

From now on, we assume
H
is a hyperelliptic curve of genus 3. We distinguish two non-isotropic cases of Klein coverings and two isotropic ones. For each of them we give an example of the generators of the Klein subgroup

\langle\eta,\xi\rangle
.

Case I.1
. For
\eta=w_{1}-w_{2},\ \xi=w_{1}-w_{3}
the corresponding covering
\tilde{C}
is hyperelliptic (
[
2
, Prop 4.4]
). The subgroup
\langle\eta,\xi\rangle

is non-isotropic. The number of such covers for a fixed curve
H
is the number of choices of triples among the 8 Weierstrass points, that is 56.

Case I.2
. For
\eta=w_{1}-w_{2},\ \xi=w_{1}-w_{3}+w_{4}-w_{5}
, the subgroup
\langle\eta,\xi\rangle
is non-isotropic.

Case II.1
. For
\eta=w_{1}-w_{2}+w_{3}-w_{4},\ \xi=w_{1}-w_{2}+w_{5}-w_{6}
, the subgroup
\langle\eta,\xi\rangle
is isotropic.

Case II.2
. For
\eta=w_{1}-w_{2},\ \xi=w_{3}-w_{4}
, the subgroup
\langle\eta,\xi\rangle
is isotropic.

Let
\mathcal{RH}_{3}^{V_{4}}
be the moduli space parametrizing Klein coverings over hyperelliptic curves of genus 3. It is also the parameter space of triples:

\mathcal{RH}_{3}^{V_{4}}:=\{[C,\langle\eta,\xi\rangle]\ :\ [C]\in{\mathcal{M}}_{3},\ \langle\eta,\xi\rangle\subset JC,\ \langle\eta,\xi\rangle\simeq\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}\}.

The moduli space

in I.1, I.2, II.1 and II.2. Moreover, the degree of the forgetful

6.
Application of the results to Klein Prym maps

This section is devoted to a global Klein Prym map of genus 3 curves, i.e. when a bottom curve is not necessarily hyperelliptic.
Let
\mathcal{R}_{3}^{V_{4}}=\mathcal{R}_{3}^{iso}\sqcup\mathcal{R}_{3}^{ni}
be a decomposition of a moduli of Klein coverings of genus 3 curves into isotropic and non-isotropic coverings. Observe that
the forgetful map
\mathcal{R}_{3}^{V_{4}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{M}}_{3}
is étale of degree
\frac{1}{6}{63\choose 2}=651
.

Lemma 6.1
.

The spaces
\mathcal{R}_{3}^{iso}
and
\mathcal{R}_{3}^{ni}
are irreducible.

Proof.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}_{3}[2]
be the moduli space of principally polarized abelian threefolds with a 2-level structure, i.e., with a symplectic basis of the set of
2
-torsion points, see
[
1
, Section 8.3.1]
.
Then
J_{3}[2]\subset{\mathcal{A}}_{3}[2]
as a space of Jacobians with level structure is an open dense subset, hence irreducible. If
(\mu_{i},\lambda_{i})
is a symplectic basis of
JC[2]
, then the forgetful maps

J_{3}[2]\ni[JC,(\mu_{i},\lambda_{i})]\to[C,\mu_{1},\lambda_{1}]\in\mathcal{R}_{3}^{ni}

J_{3}[2]\ni[JC,(\mu_{i},\lambda_{i})]\to[C,\mu_{1},\mu_{2}]\in\mathcal{R}_{3}^{iso}

are surjective, hence the codomains are irreducible.
∎

Theorem 6.2
.

The Klein Prym maps

\mathcal{P}^{iso}_{3}:\mathcal{R}_{3}^{iso}\rightarrow{\mathcal{A}}_{6}^{(1,1,1,2,2,4)}\qquad\mathcal{P}^{ni}_{3}:\mathcal{R}_{3}^{ni}\rightarrow{\mathcal{A}}_{6}^{(1,1,1,1,4,4)}

1
on images of coverings of hyperelliptic curves of types

.

Proof.

Let us consider the non-isotropic case. By Theorems
5.1
and
2.6
the Prym map
{\mathcal{P}^{ni}_{3}}_{|\mathcal{RH}_{3}^{ni}}
is injective on each component. Moreover, images of respective components are disjoint as the isotypical decomposition yields components of different dimensions. Hence, the image of the Prym map, being an irreducible variety and containing
2
disjoint subvarieties of dimension
5
has to be of dimension at least
6=\dim(\mathcal{R}_{3}^{ni})
. This shows that the map is generically finite.

Now, let
P
be in the image of the Prym map of hyperelliptic curves of any of

1.12
implies the group

is isomorphic to

Iparomlimab and tuvonralimab in previously treated patients with cervical cancer
Authors: Hanmei Lou, Yun Zhou, Dapeng Li, Hongping Zhang, Mingjun Zhang, Lihua Wang, Huijun Cheng, Zi Liu, Wei Duan, Mei Feng, Jieqing Zhang, Lipai Chen, Danbo Wang, Jinjin Yu, Congzhu Li, Chao Wang, Peizhen Wang, Hui Li, Xiaoyan Kang, Jihong Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
SummaryIparomlimab and tuvonralimab (QL1706) is a bifunctional antibody containing anti-programmed death-1 IgG4 and anti-cytotoxic T-lymphocyte antigen 4 IgG1. This multi-center single-arm phase 2 trial recruits patients with recurrent or metastatic cervical cancer (r/m CC) who have failed at least first-line platinum-based chemotherapy with or without bevacizumab. QL1706 is administered at a dose of 5.0 mg/kg every 3 weeks via intravenous infusion. The primary endpoint is objective response rate (ORR) per independent review committee (IRC). In a total of 148 patients, the confirmed ORR and disease control rate are 33.3% (95% confidence interval [CI]: 25.8%–41.6%) and 65.3% (95% CI: 57.0%–73.0%) per IRC, respectively. The median progression-free survival is 5.4 months (95% CI: 3.9–6.9). The median overall survival is 17.1 months (95% CI: 14.8 to not evaluable). Treatment-related adverse events occur in 107 (72.3%) patients. No treatment-related death occurs. QL1706 shows promising efficacy and is well tolerated in previously treated patients with r/m CC. The trial is registered at clinicaltrials.gov (NCT05557565).Keywords: recurrent or metastatic cervical cancer, efficacy, iparomlimab and tuvonralimab

Introduction

Cervical cancer ranks as the fourth most common cancer and the fourth leading cause of cancer-related death among women worldwide.1 Despite the availability of prophylactic human papillomavirus (HPV) vaccines, cervical cancer still accounted for an estimated 109,741 new cases and 59,060 deaths in 2020 in China.2 For patients with recurrent or metastatic cervical cancer (r/m CC), current therapies have demonstrated suboptimal efficacy.3,4 Treatment options are particularly limited for patients who have disease progression after first-line platinum-based chemotherapy with or without bevacizumab.

Pembrolizumab was approved for the treatment of patients with anti-programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-L1)-positive r/m CC who progressed after chemotherapy, while cemiplimab was approved for patients regardless of the PD-L1 status.5 However, both anti-programmed death-1 (PD-1) inhibitors showed an objective response rate (ORR) of less than 20% and a median progression-free survival (PFS) of less than 3 months.6,7 Considering the complementary mechanisms,8,9 the simultaneous blockade of PD-1 and cytotoxic T-lymphocyte antigen 4 (CTLA-4) pathways might optimize antitumor activity. Although nivolumab combined with ipilimumab as second- or later-line treatment improved response rates to some extent, it also resulted in a notably increased occurrence of treatment-related adverse events (AEs) (TRAEs) of any grade and grade 3 or 4 by 88% and 46%, respectively.10 Anti-PD-1 balstilimab in combination with anti-CTLA-4 zalifrelimab had an ORR of 25.6% and a median PFS of 2.7 months in patients with r/m CC, but only patients with relapse after a first-line therapy were included.11 Thus, there is an unmet need for new treatment options with improved efficacy and safety for previously treated patients with r/m CC.

Iparomlimab and tuvonralimab (QL1706) is a bifunctional antibody containing anti-PD-1 IgG4 and anti-CTLA-4 IgG1 components from a single host cell line. The anti-CTLA-4 IgG1 of QL1706 is engineered to have a shortened elimination half-life, reduced systemic anti-CTLA-4 exposure, and lowered risk of AEs.12 Iparomlimab and tuvonralimab indicated preliminary efficacy in patients with advanced cervical cancer in a phase 1 trial.12 This study aimed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of iparomlimab and tuvonralimab in previously treated patients with r/m CC.

Results

Participants

Between December 21, 2021, and October 17, 2022, 242 patients were screened, of whom 148 were included and received the study treatment. One patient who had received three cycles of platinum-based chemotherapy was recruited but not included in the full analysis set (FAS). A total of 147 patients were included in the FAS, and 148 patients were included in the safety set (Figure 1).

CONSORT figure

The baseline characteristics of participants are detailed in Table 1. The majority (131 [89.1%]) of patients had squamous cell carcinoma (SCC). Twenty-three patients (15.6%) had stage IVB disease. The Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status (ECOG PS) was 1 in 108 (73.5%) patients. PD-L1 expression was positive (defined as combined positive score [CPS] ≥1) in 104 (70.7%) patients and was negative (defined as CPS<1) in 43 (29.3%) patients. A total of 134 (91.2%) patients had received prior radiotherapy. Fifty-eight (39.5%) patients received prior bevacizumab treatment. Fifty-five patients (37.4%) had received ≥2 lines of prior treatment. Of 73 (49.7%) patients who received prior radical surgery, 60 received postoperative treatment, including 31 adjuvant chemotherapy and concurrent chemoradiotherapy (CCRT) (43.1%), 10 radiotherapy only (13.9%), 10 CCRT (13.9%), and nine adjuvant chemotherapy (12.5%). In 49 patients with stage Ia–IIa, the initial treatment plan was radical surgery in 43 patients (87.8%) and radiotherapy in 6 (12.2%) patients (Table S1).

As of data cutoff date on October 28, 2023, the median follow-up was 17.5 (range, 0.7–21.5) months. The median treatment cycle of iparomlimab and tuvonralimab was 6.0 (range, 1–31). At the data cutoff, 28 (18.9%) patients were still receiving the study treatment and 120 (81.1%) discontinued the study treatment, including 89 (60.1%) due to progressive disease (PD), 10 (6.8%) due to withdrawal of consent, 6 (4.1%) due to AEs, and 5 (3.4%) due to death.

Efficacy

Assessed by an independent review committee (IRC) according to the Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST) v.1.1, two patients achieved complete response (CR) and 47 had partial response (PR) in the FAS (Table 2). The confirmed ORR was 33.3% (95% confidence interval [CI], 25.8%–41.6%). The best changes from baseline in target lesion size are shown in Figure 2. In subgroup analysis (Figure S1), the confirmed ORR was 35.1% (95% CI, 27.0%–43.9%) in patients with SCC and 18.8% (95% CI, 4.0%–45.6%) in those with non-SCC. In patients with CPS≥1 and CPS<1, the confirmed ORR was 36.5% (95% CI, 27.3%–46.6%) and 25.6% (95% CI, 13.5%–41.2%), respectively. In 58 patients who were treated with bevacizumab previously, the confirmed ORR was 27.6% (95% CI, 16.7%–40.9%). Forty-six (31.3%) patients had stable disease [SD] (Table 2), and the disease control rate (DCR) was 65.3% (95% CI, 57.0%–73.0%). Among 49 (33.3%) patients with CR and PR, the median time to response (TTR) was 2.7 months (interquartile range, 1.4–2.8), and the median duration of response (DoR) was not reached (95% CI, 12.5 to not evaluable [NE]).

Per investigator, seven patients achieved CR and 38 achieved PR in the FAS. The confirmed ORR was 30.6% (95% CI, 23.3%–38.7%). The DCR, TTR, and DoR per investigator were consistent with those per IRC (Table 2). The results in the per-protocol set are detailed in Table S2. A patient who had a CPS of 10 and had received prior radiotherapy achieved PR and maintained response for over 18 months (Figure S2).

As of data cutoff, 92 (62.6%) had PD (87 [94.6%]) or died (5 [5.4%]). The median PFS was 5.4 months (95% CI, 3.9–6.9) (Figure 3A). The 6-, 12-, and 18-month PFS rates were 45.3% (95% CI, 36.6%–53.6%), 29.6% (95% CI, 21.6%–38.1%), and 23.7% (95% CI, 13.7%–35.1%), respectively.

As of data cutoff, 70 (47.6%) deaths occurred. The median overall survival (OS) was 17.1 months (95% CI, 14.8 to NE) with a median follow-up of 17.5 months (range, 0.7–21.5) (Figure 3B). The 12- and 18-month OS rates were 64.4% (95% CI, 55.8%–71.7%) and 44.2% (95% CI, 34.6%–53.5%), respectively.

Safety

TRAEs occurred in 107 (72.3%) patients. TRAEs with incidence ≥15% were hypothyroidism (22.3%), hyperthyroidism (19.6%), decreased white blood cell count (16.2%), increased alanine aminotransferase (15.5%), and anemia (15.5%). TRAEs of grade ≥3 occurred in 40 (27.0%) patients, of which those with an incidence of ≥3% were anemia (4.7%) and increased gamma-glutamyl transferase (3.4%). Serious TRAEs were reported in 23 (15.5%) patients, of which 18 (12.2%) were grade ≥3. Five patients (3.4%) and 36 patients (24.3%) discontinued and suspended the treatment due to TRAE, respectively. No treatment-related death occurred. Infusion-related reactions were reported in 14 patients (9.5%).

Among 72 (48.6%) patients who developed immune-related adverse events (irAEs), 15 (10.1%) were grade ≥3 (Table 3). The most frequently reported irAEs were hypothyroidism (20.9%) and hyperthyroidism (17.6%). The most common irAE of grade ≥3 was immune-mediated pulmonary disease (2.0%).

Discussion

To the best of our knowledge, this was the first study that evaluated a dual PD-1/CTLA-4 blocker in patients with cervical cancer. Iparomlimab and tuvonralimab yielded promising efficacy and well-tolerable safety in patients with r/m CC who failed at least one line of platinum-based chemotherapy with or without bevacizumab.

The ORR, DCR, median PFS, and median OS in this study were 33.3%, 65.3%, 5.4 months, and 17.1 months, respectively. Another anti-PD-1/CTLA-4 bispecific antibody, cadonilimab, reported an ORR of 32.3% and a median PFS of 3.7 months13 with a lower proportion of patients with an ECOG PS of 1 (57% vs. 73.5%) and a lower proportion of patients who had received prior bevacizumab (25.0% vs. 39.5%) compared with this study.13,14 The combination of PD-1 plus CTLA-4 inhibitors exhibited different response rates and varying PFS. The pooled results of nivolumab 1 mg/kg plus ipilimumab 3 mg/kg and nivolumab 3 mg/kg plus ipilimumab 1 mg/kg in previously treated patients with r/m CC were ORRs of 35% and 26%, median PFS of 4.7 and 3.6 months, and median OS of 19.9 and 10.3 months, respectively.10 Similarly, only 54% of patients had an ECOG PS of 1, and patients who had received ≥2 previous lines accounted for only 8%.10 Another combined regimen of balstilimab and zalifrelimab reported an ORR of 25.6%, a DCR of 52%, a median PFS of 2.7 months, and a median OS of 12.8 months, in a phase 2 trial including 27.1% patients with adenocarcinoma and 25.2% patients with CPS<1.11 Iparomlimab and tuvonralimab showed numerically favorable treatment response and survival outcomes compared with other dual-targeted therapies. It should be noted that these cross-trial comparisons should be interpreted with caution due to differences in study design, patient populations, sample size, and follow-up time. Furthermore, this monotherapy was more convenient to administer than the combination therapies.

The approved PD-1 inhibitors, including pembrolizumab,6 cemiplimab,7 and zimberelimab,15 showed ORRs ranging from 12.2% to 27.6% and DCRs from 30.6% to 55.2%, as well as median PFS of 2.1–5.1 months and median OS of 9.4–16.8 months. Since other attempts at anti-PD-1 monotherapy such as nivolumab (ORR: 26.3%)16 and balstilimab (ORR: 15%)17 and anti-CTLA-4 monotherapy ipilimumab (one PR among 34 patients)18 showed modest treatment response, the treatment regimens of concomitant blockade of PD-1 and CTLA-4 pathways warrant further exploration. Mechanistically, dual-targeted immunotherapy of anti-CTLA-4 and anti-PD-1 pathways may enhance antitumor activity by overcoming CTLA-4 blockade-associated upregulation of PD-1, acting on the same T cell at different time points and acting on different T cell populations.19,20,21 Thus, the dual blocker iparomlimab and tuvonralimab showed numerically higher response rates compared with anti-PD-1 or anti-CTLA-4 alone.6,7,16,17,18

Other treatment strategies in the same setting of patients as in this study were also explored, including tisotumab vedotin with an ORR of 24% and a median PFS of 4.2 months22 and atezolizumab plus simlukafusp-alpha reporting an ORR of 27.3% and a median PFS of 3.7 months.23 Pembrolizumab plus GX-188E vaccine achieved an ORR of 42%, but with a median PFS of 4.9 months.24 Immunotherapy plus antiangiogenic therapies, including sintilimab plus anlotinib25 and camrelizumab plus apatinib,26 indicated notable efficacy with an ORR of about 55% and a median PFS of about 9 months, but safety concerns were serious in both studies.25,26 Atezolizumab plus bevacizumab yielded no objective response, a median PFS of 2.9 months, and a median OS of 8.9 months in previously treated patients with metastatic cervical cancer.27

The ORR was high irrespective of the PD-L1 status, suggesting potential antitumor activity of iparomlimab and tuvonralimab in both PD-L1-positive and -negative patients. In this study, the ORR was 36.5% in patients with CPS ≥1 (N = 104) and 25.6% in those with CPS<1 (N = 43). Cadonilimab achieved an ORR 42.9% in patients with CPS ≥1 (N = 63), while the ORR in those with CPS <1 (N = 18) was 16.7%.13 The ORR in PD-L1-positive and -negative patients treated with balstilimab plus zalifrelimab was 32.8% and 9.1%, respectively.11 However, both pembrolizumab6 and nivolumab plus ipilimumab10 reported no response in patients with negative PD-L1 expression. A previous study demonstrated that patients whose tumors overexpressed PD-L1 would have improved clinical outcomes when treated with anti-PD-1 therapy.28 Furthermore, the approved PD-1 inhibitors, pembrolizumab and zimberelimab, were only for PD-L1-positive patients. The high ORR in PD-L1-negative patients in this study might be associated with the predominant SCC histology, which is closely related to HPV infection; HPV-derived neoantigens may modulate the tumor immune microenvironment and enhance antitumor responses.29 Additionally, the concomitant anti-CTLA-4 antibody component of QL1706, engineered in a fixed ratio with the anti-PD-1 antibody component, might contribute to the favorable response in PD-L1-negative patients. The shorter half-life of the anti-CTLA-4 antibody also showed a favorable safety profile, which may help ensure treatment compliance and potentially improve treatment outcomes. This finding was consistent with the results observed in other tumor types in the earlier phase 1 trial.12 Thus, phase 3 trials in patients with cervical cancer (QL1706-301) and in PD-L1-negative NSCLC (QL1706-303) are ongoing.

The ORR was 18.8% among 16 patients with non-SCC in this study, which was numerically higher compared with the ORR in patients treated with cadonilimab (three adenocarcinoma patients with no response)14 and balstilimab plus zalifrelimab (ORR 8.8% in 34 adenocarcinoma patients).11 This suggested a potential of clinical benefit for patients with both SCC and non-SCC histological types.

In patients with (N = 58) and without (N = 89) prior bevacizumab treatment, the ORR was 27.6% and 37.1%, respectively. Patients appeared to derive benefit in ORR regardless of prior bevacizumab treatment. Cadonilimab suggested similar results (28.0% [7/25] vs. 34.7% [26/75]).14 On the contrary, six (12.5%) of 48 patients with prior bevacizumab treatment responded to balstilimab plus zalifrelimab.11

One patient with giant lymph node metastasis in the neck unaccompanied by necrosis showed PD with radiotherapy and chemotherapy plus bevacizumab and received iparomlimab and tuvonralimab 13 months later. The durable response of PR for over 18 months indicated the potential for prolonged efficacy of iparomlimab and tuvonralimab after failure of chemotherapy. The possible explanations for this durable response might include the following aspects. First, the patient had a CPS of 10 and positive PD-L1 expression has been associated with better efficacy in treatment with both PD-1 antibodies6,7 and PD-1/CTLA-4 dual-target therapies.11,13 Second, preservation of lymph node integrity before immunotherapy might enhance treatment effectiveness.30,31 Third, prior radiotherapy might augment antitumor immune responses by releasing neoantigens that could be recognized by the immune system.32 A similar case reported recently33 also supported these findings. Furthermore, this patient experienced only grade 2 TRAEs (neutrophil count decreased and amylase increased), which ensured the maintenance of long-term immunotherapy.

Iparomlimab and tuvonralimab was well tolerated, as evidenced by TRAEs leading to treatment discontinuation of 3.4%. There was no treatment-related death, and TRAEs occurred in 72.3% of patients with 27.0% being grade ≥3. The incidence of grade 1 or 2 TRAEs with cadonilimab was 90% among 111 patients, and that of grade ≥3 TRAEs was 27%.13 The most common TRAEs with cadonilimab were anemia (grade 1–2: 29%; grade 3: 5%) and hypothyroidism (grade 1–2: 21%; grade 3: 0%).13 Patients treated with nivolumab plus ipilimumab had TRAEs of 88% (46% grade ≥3) and TRAEs leading to treatment discontinuation of 24%.10 Balstilimab plus zalifrelimab reported 71.0% TRAEs and three treatment-related deaths.11 The safety profile of iparomlimab and tuvonralimab was generally consistent with those of other monotherapies of anti-PD-16,7 and anti-CTLA-418 and dual-targeted therapies,10,11,13 but diarrhea was less frequently reported with iparomlimab and tuvonralimab and with cadonilimab13 compared with the two combined regimens of PD-1 plus CTLA-4.10,11 The well-tolerated safety in the current trial verified the reengineering of anti-CTLA-4 IgG1, which led to reduced binding to FcRn, faster clearance in the circulation, and shorter half-life.12

Hypothyroidism (20.9%) and hyperthyroidism (17.6%) were the most common irAEs of iparomlimab and tuvonralimab, which were consistent with those of cadonilimab14 and balstilimab plus zalifrelimab.11 One case of immune-mediated colitis leading to death was reported in patients treated with nivolumab plus ipilimumab.10

In conclusion, the PD-1/CTLA-4 dual blocker iparomlimab and tuvonralimab (QL1706) showed encouraging efficacy, durable response, and well-tolerated safety profile in patients with r/m CC who failed at least one line of platinum-based chemotherapy with or without bevacizumab or were intolerable to platinum therapies. The high antitumor activity irrespective of the PD-L1 status, histologic type, or prior bevacizumab treatment suggests that iparomlimab and tuvonralimab may be a promising option for previously treated patients with r/m CC who have a poor prognosis and are still lack effective therapy. Further explorations of iparomlimab and tuvonralimab are warranted. A phase 3 trial evaluating iparomlimab and tuvonralimab plus chemotherapy as first-line treatment for patients with r/m CC (NCT05446883) is ongoing.

Limitations of the study

The study had some limitations. First, this was a single-arm study without a control group. A randomized controlled trial would further confirm the efficacy and safety of iparomlimab and tuvonralimab. Second, this study only included Chinese patients, so it remains unknown whether the efficacy would be different across other ethnic populations. Third, translational studies were not conducted. In the ongoing phase 3 study, translational studies are being conducted to explore further results regarding PD-L1 expression rate and tumor mutational burden.

Experimental model and study participant details

Human subjects

The trial included 148 female patients. Eligible patients were age of ≥18 years old and with r/m CC who were unsuitable for radical treatment. Patients were included if they failed at least one line of platinum-based standard treatment (disease progression during treatment or after ≥ four cycles of treatment) or were intolerable to platinum therapies or had progression during or within six months after platinum-based neoadjuvant or adjuvant chemotherapy. Patients were additionally required to have enough tumor tissue from biopsy sample for PD-L1 assessment, at least one measurable lesion according to RECIST v1.1, ECOG PS score of 0 or 1, life expectancy of ≥3 months, and adequate organ function. Key exclusion criteria were previous immunotherapy such as immune checkpoint inhibitors such as anti-PD-1/PD-L1/CTLA-4 antibodies, immune checkpoint agonists, immune cell therapy, and anti-tumor vaccines. Patients with active autoimmune diseases, symptomatic metastasis in central nervous system, active bleeding requiring clinical intervention, uncontrolled hypertension, severe cardiovascular diseases, genital fistula, previous and/or current lung diseases such as interstitial lung disease and lung fibrosis, or systematic anti-tumor treatment within four weeks of the first dose of study treatment were excluded.

The study was approved by the ethics committee of each participating center. The full study protocol can be found as supplementary information (Data S1). Written informed consent was obtained from each participant. The trial was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and Good Clinical Practice guidelines.

Method details

Study design and patients

This was a multi-center open-label single-arm phase 2 clinical trial (DUBHE-C-206) which was conducted in 32 centers in China.

Study treatment

Iparomlimab and tuvonralimab was administered at 5.0 mg/kg by intravenous infusion once every three weeks. The study treatment was continued until PD, intolerable toxicity, treatment suspension of more than 84 days, lost to follow-up, death, withdrawal from study, or other conditions at the discretion of the investigator whichever occurred first. Dose reduction of iparomlimab and tuvonralimab was not allowed.

Assessment

Treatment responses were assessed by IRC and the investigators according to RECIST 1.1. Computed tomography or magnetic resonance imaging was performed at baseline and every six weeks during the first 48 weeks and every 12 weeks thereafter. CR and PR should be confirmed at least four weeks later by radiological examinations. AEs were recorded from the day of recruitment until 90 days after termination of treatment or new anti-tumor treatment or lost to follow-up. AEs were graded according to the National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events version 5.0.

Quantification and statistical analysis

Endpoints

The primary endpoint was ORR, defined as the proportion of patients who achieved confirmed CR or PR assessed by IRC per RECIST 1.1. The secondary endpoints were ORR assessed by investigators, IRC and investigator-assessed DoR (time from first response to PD or death of any cause), DCR (proportion of patients who had CR, PR, Non-CR/Non-PD, or SD), PFS (time from treatment initiation to PD or death from any cause), 6-month and 12-month PFS rates, OS (time from treatment initiation to death from any cause), 12-month OS rate, and safety.

Statistical analysis

Based on the ORR of 28.3% in patients with cervical cancer observed in phase 1 b study,12 127 patients were required to detect a target ORR of 25% while ensuring the lower limit of 95% CI of higher than 15% according to the China National Medical Products Administration, using an exact binomial test with power of 80% at a one-sided α of 0.025. Assuming a dropout rate of 15%, a total sample size of 150 patients was planned.

Efficacy analysis was performed primarily in the FAS, defined as all patients who had received at least one dose of study treatment per intention-to-treat principle, and also in PPS, defined as all patients who had received at least one dose of study treatment and without major protocol deviation or protocol deviations that did not greatly affect the outcome of the study. Safety analyses were performed in the SS which included all patients who had received at least one dose of the study treatment.

ORR, DCR and their 95% CIs were calculated using Clopper-Pearson method. DoR, PFS, and OS were analyzed using Kaplan-Meier methods. Continuous variables were analyzed descriptively, and categorical variables were described as numbers and percentages. Data analyses were performed using SAS 9.4 (SAS Institute, Cary, NY, USA). A detailed summary of statistical analyses are outlined in the statistical analysis plan (Data S2).

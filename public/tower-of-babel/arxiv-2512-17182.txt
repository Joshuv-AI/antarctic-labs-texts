Long-time stability and convergence analysis of an IMEX BDF3 scheme for 2-D incompressible Navier-Stokes equation
Kelong Cheng, Jingwei Sun, Hong Zhang

Abstract
High-order time-stepping schemes are crucial for simulating incompressible fluid flows due to their ability to capture complex turbulent behavior and unsteady motion. In this work, we propose a third-order accurate numerical scheme for the two-dimensional incompressible Navier-Stokes equation. Spatial and temporal discretization is achieved using Fourier pseudo-spectral approximation and the BDF3 stencil, combined with the Adams-Bashforth extrapolation for the nonlinear convection term, resulting in a semi-implicit, fully discrete formulation. This approach requires solving only a single Poisson-like equation per time step while maintaining the desired temporal accuracy. Classical numerical experiments demonstrate the advantage of our scheme in terms of permissible time step sizes. Moreover, we establish uniform-in-time bounds for the vorticity in both L 2 L^{2} and higher-order H m H^{m} norms ( m ≥ 1 m\geq 1 ), provided the time step is sufficiently small. These bounds, in turn, facilitate the derivation of optimal convergence rates.

Introduction

In this paper, we focus on the third-order backward differentiation (BDF) approximations for the unsteady incompressible Navier-Stokes equation (NSE) in bounded two-dimensional (2-D) domain subjected to the periodic boundary condition:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\omega+\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\!\cdot\!\nabla\omega=\nu\Delta\omega+\mbox{\boldmath$f$},

(1.1)

\displaystyle-\Delta\psi=\omega,

(1.2)

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$u$}=\nabla^{\bot}\psi=\left(\partial_{y}\psi,-\partial_{x}\psi\right).

(1.3)

Here,
\mbox{\boldmath$u$}=(u,v)^{T}
is the velocity field,
\omega=-u_{y}+v_{x}
represents the scalar vorticity,
\psi
stands for the scalar stream function,
\nu=1/{\mbox{Re}}
represents the coefficient of the fluid kinematic viscosity with the Reynolds numbers
Re
, and the external force term
f
has a zero average:
\int_{\Omega}\,\mbox{\boldmath$f$}\,d{\bf x}=0
. For simplicity, a uniform-in-time
L^{2}
bound is assumed for
f
, i.e.,
\mbox{\boldmath$f$}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2})
and
\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$f$}(\cdot,t)\right\|\leq M
for any
t>0
.
In this formulation, the pressure gradient disappears, and the velocity vector
\mbox{\boldmath$u$}=\nabla^{\bot}\psi
is automatically divergence-free. This fact brings a great deal of numerical convenience.
It is also noticed that, all three evolutionary terms in (
1.1
) have a zero average:

\int_{\Omega}\,\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\!\cdot\!\nabla\omega\,d{\bf x}=\int_{\Omega}\,\nabla\cdot(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\omega)\,d{\bf x}=0,\quad\int_{\Omega}\,\Delta\omega\,d{\bf x}=0,\quad\int_{\Omega}\,\mbox{\boldmath$f$}\,d{\bf x}=0,

(1.4)

which comes from the divergence-free property of
u
, combined with the periodic boundary condition. In turn, we conclude that
\omega({\bf x},t)
always keeps a zero-average:

\int_{\Omega}\,\partial_{t}\omega\,d{\bf x}=0,\qquad\mbox{so that}\,\,\,\int_{\Omega}\,\omega({\bf x},t)\,d{\bf x}=\int_{\Omega}\,\omega({\bf x},0)\,d{\bf x}=0,\,\,\,\forall t>0.

(1.5)

As a result, the kinematic equation
\Delta\psi=\omega
always has a unique solution, if we impose a zero-average requirement for the stream function:
\int_{\Omega}\,\psi\,d{\bf x}=0
.

It is well-known that long-time stable (uniformly bounded in time) quantity for the NSE (
1.1
)-(
1.3
) is the enstrophy variable, namely
\frac{1}{2}\|\omega\|_{2}^{2}
, so that the dynamics possesses a global attractor and invariant measures
[
8
,
14
,
31
]
. Consequently, preserving the long-time stability of key physical variables, such as vorticity, is crucial for accurately capturing long-term dynamics. Numerous efforts have been made to ensure stability and dissipativity in various forms (see for instance
[
7
,
13
,
12
,
24
,
29
,
30
,
33
,
32
,
15
,
26
]
among many others). In the existing works, there are many off-the-shelf efficient solvers available, since the problem essentially reduces to solving a Poisson equation at each time step. A more popular method is IMEX, as it strikes a balance between stability (implicit treatment of the diffusion term) and efficiency (explicit treatment of the nonlinear term). Below, we provide a few examples to illustrate this.

1.

The simplest example is the following first-order IMEX Euler scheme
[
17
]
,

\frac{\omega^{n+1}-\omega^{n}}{\Delta t}+\bm{u}^{n}\cdot\nabla\omega^{n}=\nu\Delta\omega^{n+1}+\bm{f}^{n+1},

(1.6)

where
\Delta t
is the time-step size and
\omega^{k}
is the approximation of the vorticity at
t^{k}
. This scheme is efficient because it requires only two Poisson solvers per time step: one for the vorticity and another for the stream function. Moreover, the scheme’s convergence over any fixed time interval is well-established and follows standard procedures, see the derivations in earlier literature
[
19
,
20
,
20
,
21
,
27
]
. More importantly, the authors
[
17
]
proved that (
1.6
) is long-time stable in
L^{2}
and
H^{1}
and that the global attractor as well as the invariant measures of the scheme converge to those of the NSE at a vanishing time step.

2.

The IMEX BDF2 scheme takes the following form
[
1
,
25
,
34
,
22
]
,

\frac{3\omega^{n+1}-4\omega^{n}+2\omega^{n-1}}{2\Delta t}+2\bm{u}^{n}\cdot\nabla\omega^{n}-\bm{u}^{n-1}\cdot\omega^{n-1}=\nu\Delta\omega^{n+1}+\bm{f}^{n+1},

(1.7)

where the Adams-Bashforth treatment of the nonlinear term is in the form of linear multi-step fashion, the so-called extrapolated Gear’s scheme. The authors
[
22
]
proved that the scheme’s vorticity and velocity are both long-time stable in the
L^{2}
and
H^{1}
norms, without any time-step restriction.

3.

The IMEX BDF2 scheme,

\frac{3\omega^{n+1}-4\omega^{n}+2\omega^{n-1}}{2\Delta t}+(2\bm{u}^{n}-\bm{u}^{n-1})\cdot\nabla(2\omega^{n}-\omega^{n-1})=\nu\Delta\omega^{n+1}+\bm{f}^{n+1}

(1.8)

was investigated by Wang
[
37
]
. Compared to (
1.7
), (
1.8
) features a specialized explicit Adams-Bashforth treatment for the advection term. The author primarily analyzed the scheme’s convergence in terms of long-time statistical properties (stationary statistics).

4.

The IMEX second-order multi-step scheme
[
18
,
35
,
6
]
,

\displaystyle\frac{\omega^{n+1}-\omega^{n}}{\Delta t}+\frac{3}{4}\big(\bm{u}^{n}\cdot\nabla\omega^{n}+\nabla\cdot(\bm{u}^{n}\omega^{n})\big)-\frac{1}{4}\big(\bm{u}^{n-1}\cdot\nabla\omega^{n-1}+\nabla\cdot(\bm{u}^{n-1}\omega^{n-1})\big)

(1.9)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nu\Delta\big(\frac{3}{4}\omega^{n+1}+\frac{1}{4}\omega^{n-1}\big)+\bm{f}^{n+1/2}

has been widely investigated (the third- and fourth-order schemes mentioned are omitted here). It is observed that Adams-Moulton interpolation causes the diffusion term to be more concentrated at the next time step, meaning that the coefficient at
t^{n+1}
dominates the sum of the remaining diffusion coefficients.

In fact, the IMEX BDF3 scheme which we will adopt in this work has been applied to the incompressible NSEs with various spatial approximations
[
1
]
. However, the study addressed only linear stability analysis and the effectiveness of aliasing reduction in spectral methods, while the nonlinear analysis remains unavailable, even for local-in-time error estimates. Therefore, the main objective of this paper is to apply the IMEX BDF3 scheme to the 2-D NSE (
1.1
)-(
1.3
), and demonstrate that, provided the time-step size is sufficiently small, the scheme (
2.23
)-(
2.25
) is long-time stable. Specifically, we establish the uniform-in-time
L^{2}
and
H^{m}
bounds of the fully discrete pseudo-spectral numerical solution for
m\geq 1
by the global-in-time energy stability. Furthermore, we demonstrate that the desired optimal convergence rate is achieved and rigorously proved using some skills analogous to the stability analysis.

The paper is organized as follows. We begin in Section 2 with reviewing the IMEX BDF3 method with the Fourier pseudo-spectral approximation for the NSE (
1.1
)-(
1.3
). A few preliminary estimates in the Fourier pseudo-spectral space are also presented. The following two sections are devoted to the long-time stability and convergence analysis of the fully discrete scheme (
2.23
)-(
2.25
). Some numerical experiments are provided in Section 5. The final section gives some concluding remarks.

2
Fully discrete numerical scheme

2.1
Fourier pseudo-spectral differentiation

We assume that the domain is given by
\Omega=[0,L]^{2}
, with a uniform mesh size:
N_{x}=N_{y}=N
. We also choose a mesh size
h=L/N
with
N=2K+1
being a positive odd integer, and set
N_{x}=N_{y}=N
. The case for an even
N
could be similarly treated. All the spatial variables are evaluated at the regular numerical grid
\Omega_{N}
, in which
x_{i}=ih
,
y_{j}=jh
,
0\leq i,j\leq N
. Without loss of generality, it is also assumed that
L=1
. For a 2-D periodic function
f
with a discrete Fourier expansion

f_{i,j}=\sum_{k_{1},l_{1}=-K}^{K}\hat{f}_{k_{1},l_{1}}\exp\left(2k_{1}\pi{\rm i}x_{i}\right)\exp\left(2l_{1}\pi{\rm i}y_{j}\right),

(2.1)

its Fourier collocation spectral approximations to first and second order partial derivatives (in the
x
direction) become

\displaystyle\left({\cal D}_{Nx}f\right)_{i,j}=

\displaystyle\sum_{k_{1},l_{1}=-K}^{K}\left(2k_{1}\pi{\rm i}\right)\hat{f}_{k_{1},l_{1}}\exp\left(2\pi{\rm i}(k_{1}x_{i}+l_{1}y_{j})\right),

(2.2)

\displaystyle\left({\cal D}_{Nx}^{2}f\right)_{i,j}=

\displaystyle\sum_{k_{1},l_{1}=-K}^{K}\left(-4\pi^{2}k_{1}^{2}\right)\hat{f}_{k_{1},l_{1}}\exp\left(2\pi{\rm i}(k_{1}x_{i}+l_{1}y_{j})\right).

(2.3)

The
{\cal D}_{Ny}
and
{\cal D}_{Ny}
operators could be similarly defined. In turn, the discrete gradient, divergence and Laplacian are formulated as

\nabla_{N}f=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}{\cal D}_{Nx}f\\
{\cal D}_{Ny}f\end{array}\right),\quad\nabla_{N}\cdot\left(\begin{array}[]{c}f_{1}\\
f_{2}\end{array}\right)={\cal D}_{Nx}f_{1}+{\cal D}_{Ny}f_{2},\quad\Delta_{N}f=\left({\cal D}_{Nx}^{2}+{\cal D}_{Ny}^{2}\right)f,

(2.4)

at the point-wise level. Of course, an obvious fact is observed:
\nabla_{N}\cdot\nabla_{N}f=\Delta_{N}f
, for any grid function
f
. See the derivations in the related references
[
3
,
4
,
16
]

Moreover, given any periodic grid functions
f
and
g
(over the 2-D numerical grid), the spectral approximations to the
L^{2}
inner product and
L^{2}
norm are introduced as

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{2}=\sqrt{\left\langle f,f\right\rangle},\quad\mbox{with}~\left\langle f,g\right\rangle=h^{2}\sum_{i,j=0}^{N-1}f_{i,j}g_{i,j}.

(2.5)

In fact, such a discrete
L^{2}
inner product can also be viewed in the Fourier space other than in physical space, with the help of Parseval equality:

\left\langle f,g\right\rangle=\sum_{k_{1},l_{1}=-K}^{K}\hat{f}_{k_{1},l_{1}}\overline{\hat{g}_{k_{1},l_{1}}}=\sum_{k_{1},l_{1}=-K}^{K}\hat{g}_{k_{1},l_{1}}\overline{\hat{f}_{k_{1},l_{1}}},

(2.6)

in which
\hat{f}_{k_{1},l_{1}}
,
\hat{g}_{k_{1},l_{1}}
are the Fourier coefficients of the grid functions
f
and
g
in the expansion as in (
2.1
). Furthermore, a detailed calculation reveals the following formulas of integration by parts formulas at the discrete level (see the related discussions
[
5
,
18
]
):

\left\langle f,\nabla_{N}\cdot\left(\begin{array}[]{c}g_{1}\\
g_{2}\end{array}\right)\right\rangle=-\left\langle\nabla_{N}f,\left(\begin{array}[]{c}g_{1}\\
g_{2}\end{array}\right)\right\rangle,\qquad\left\langle f,\Delta_{N}g\right\rangle=-\left\langle\nabla_{N}f,\nabla_{N}g\right\rangle.

(2.7)

In addition, a discrete average is introduced for any periodic grid function
f
:
\overline{f}=\langle f,{\bf 1}\rangle
. Such a notation will facilitate the notations in the later analysis.

2.1.1
A few preliminary estimates in the Fourier pseudo-spectral space

It is well-known that the existence of aliasing error in the nonlinear term poses a serious challenge in the numerical analysis of Fourier pseudo-spectral scheme. To overcome this subtle difficulty, a periodic extension of a grid function and a Fourier collocation interpolation operator is introduced. For simplicity of presentation, we denote
{\cal B}^{K}
as the space of trigonometric polynomials in
x
,
y
, and
z
of degree up to
K
.

Definition 2.1
.

For any periodic grid function
f
defined over a uniform 2-D numerical grid, we denote
\mbox{\boldmath$f$}_{N}
as its periodic extension. In more detail, assume that the grid function
f
has a discrete Fourier expansion as

f_{i,j}=\sum_{k_{1},l_{1}=-K}^{K}\hat{f}_{k_{1},l_{1}}\exp\left(2\pi{\rm i}(k_{1}x_{i}+l_{1}y_{j})\right),

(2.8)

its periodic extension (projection) into
{\cal B}^{K}
is given by

\mbox{\boldmath$f$}_{N}({\bf x})=\sum_{k_{1},l_{1}=-K}^{K}\hat{f}_{k_{1},l_{1}}\exp\left(2\pi{\rm i}(k_{1}x+l_{1}y)\right).

(2.9)

Moreover, for any periodic continuous function
{\bf f}
, which may contain larger wave length, we define its collocation interpolation operator as

\displaystyle{\bf f}_{i,j}=

\displaystyle\sum_{k_{1},l_{1}=-K}^{K}(\hat{f}_{c})_{k_{1},l_{1}}\exp\left(2\pi{\rm i}(k_{1}x_{i}+l_{1}y_{j})\right),

\displaystyle{\cal I}_{N}{\bf f}({\bf x})=

\displaystyle\sum_{k_{1},l_{1}=-K}^{K}(\hat{f}_{c})_{k_{1},l_{1}}\exp\left(2\pi{\rm i}(k_{1}x+l_{1}y)\right).

Note that
\hat{f}_{c}
may not be the Fourier coefficients of
f
, because of the aliasing errors.

To overcome a key difficulty associated with the
H^{k}
bound of the nonlinear term obtained by collocation interpolation, the following lemma is introduced. In fact, the case of
k=0
was proven in Weinan E’s earlier works
[
10
,
11
]
. The case of
k\geq 1
was analyzed in a recent work by C. Wang and S. Gottlieb
[
18
]
. We cite the result here.

Lemma 2.1
.

For any
\varphi\in{\cal B}^{2K}
in dimension
d
, we have

\left\|{\cal I}_{N}\varphi\right\|_{H^{k}}\leq(\sqrt{2})^{d}\left\|\varphi\right\|_{H^{k}},\quad\forall k\in\mathbb{Z},k\geq 0.

(2.10)

The discrete version of discrete Poincaré inequality, elliptic regularity, as well as an
\ell^{2}
orthogonality of a scalar field
f
with its convection term, is stated in the following lemma. The proof will be straightforward, and we skip the details for the sake of brevity.

Lemma 2.2
.

The following statements hold:

1.

(
Discrete Poincaré inequality
)
There exists a constant
C_{2}>0
, depending only on the domain size
L

(but independent of the mesh size
h
and the time-step size
\Delta t
), such that

\|f\|_{2}\leq C_{2}\|\nabla_{N}f\|_{2},\qquad\text{for any grid function $f$ with }\overline{f}=0.

(2.11)

2.

(
Skew-symmetry of the convection operator
)
Let
u
be a discrete velocity field satisfying the discrete divergence-free
condition
\nabla_{N}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$u$}=0
. Then, for any grid function
f
,

\left\langle f,\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\!\cdot\!\nabla_{N}f+\nabla_{N}\cdot\left(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}f\right)\right\rangle=\left\langle f,\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\!\cdot\!\nabla_{N}f\right\rangle-\left\langle\nabla_{N}f,\mbox{\boldmath$u$}f\right\rangle=0.

(2.12)

In addition, the following estimate for the skew-average convection term will be repeatedly applied in the later analysis.

Lemma 2.3
.

Given a discrete velocity vector
\mbox{\boldmath$u$}=(u,v)
with
\nabla_{N}\!\cdot\!\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\equiv 0
.
Assume its continuous extension is given by
\mbox{\boldmath$U$}=(U,V)=\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{N}
, and the
associated stream function and vorticity, in the continuous version, are
determined to be
\psi
and
\omega
. In other words, we have

\mbox{\boldmath$U$}=\nabla^{\bot}\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$}
and
\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}=\nabla\times\mbox{\boldmath$U$}=\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$}
.

Let
\delta>1
be a fixed constant such that the Sobolev embedding

H^{\delta}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)
holds for the
vorticity field in two dimensions. Then we have the estimate

\displaystyle\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\!\cdot\!\nabla_{N}f+\nabla_{N}\cdot(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}f)\|_{2}\leq\gamma_{0}\|\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}\|_{H^{\delta}}\cdot\|\nabla_{N}f\|_{2},\quad\mbox{for any $f$ with $\overline{f}=0$}.

(2.13)

Proof.

In fact, it is noticed that the term
\nabla_{N}\cdot(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}f)
cannot be expanded as
\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\cdot\nabla_{N}f
, as in the Fourier-Galerkin approximation, even though
u
is divergence-free at the discrete level. In the Fourier pseudo-spectral, we have to start from

\nabla_{N}\cdot(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}f)={\cal D}_{Nx}(uf)+{\cal D}_{Ny}(vf).

(2.14)

To obtain an estimate of these nonlinear expansions, we denote
\mbox{\boldmath$U$}=(U,V)
and
f_{\bf F}
as the continuous projection of
u
and
f
, respectively. In particular, we have
\int_{\Omega}\,f_{\bf F}\,d{\bf x}=0
, due to the fact that
\overline{f}=0
. Moreover, since
\mbox{\boldmath$U$},f_{\bf F}\in{\cal B}^{K}
, an application of Lemma
2.1
indicates that

\displaystyle\|{\cal D}_{Nx}(uf)\|_{2}=

\displaystyle\|\partial_{x}{\cal I}_{N}(Uf_{\bf F})\|_{L^{2}}\leq 2\|\partial_{x}(Uf_{\bf F})\|_{L^{2}},

\displaystyle\|{\cal D}_{Ny}(vf)\|_{2}=

\displaystyle\|\partial_{y}{\cal I}_{N}(Vf_{\bf F})\|_{L^{2}}\leq 2\|\partial_{y}(Vf_{\bf F})\|_{L^{2}}.

(2.15)

Subsequently, a detailed expansion in continuous space and application of Hölder’s inequality indicates that

\displaystyle\|\partial_{x}(Uf_{\bf F})\|_{L^{2}}=

\displaystyle\|U_{x}f_{\bf F}+U(f_{\bf F})_{x}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|U_{x}f_{\bf F}\|_{L^{2}}+\|U(f_{\bf F})_{x}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|U_{x}\|_{L^{2/(1-\delta)}}\cdot\|f_{\bf F}\|_{L^{2/\delta}}+\|U\|_{L^{\infty}}\cdot\|(f_{\bf F})_{x}\|_{L^{2}}.

(2.16)

Meanwhile, an application of elliptic regularity implies that

\|U_{x}\|_{H^{\delta}}\leq C\|U\|_{H^{1+\delta}}\leq C\|\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$}\|_{H^{2+\delta}}\leq C\|\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}\|_{H^{\delta}},

(2.17)

since
\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$}=\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}
, and
\mbox{\boldmath$U$}=\nabla^{\bot}\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$}
. Subsequently, an application of 2-D Soblev embedding leads to

\|U_{x}\|_{L^{2/(1-\delta)}}\cdot\|f_{\bf F}\|_{L^{2/\delta}}\leq C\|U_{x}\|_{H^{\delta}}\cdot\|f_{\bf F}\|_{H^{1}}\leq C\|\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}\|_{H^{\delta}}\cdot\|\nabla f_{\bf F}\|_{L^{2}},

(2.18)

in which Poincaré inequality was utilized in the last step. The second part in (
2.16
) can be handled in a straightforward way:

\|U\|_{L^{\infty}}\cdot\|(f_{\bf F})_{x}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|U\|_{H^{1+\delta}}\cdot\|\nabla f_{\bf F}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}\|_{H^{\delta}}\cdot\|\nabla f_{\bf F}\|_{L^{2}},

(2.19)

with the the elliptic regularity (
2.27
) applied again in the second step. A combination of (
2.18
) and (
2.19
) yields

\|\partial_{x}(Uf_{\bf F})\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}\|_{H^{\delta}}\cdot\|\nabla f_{\bf F}\|_{L^{2}}.

(2.20)

Similar estimates can be derived for
\|\partial_{y}(Vf_{\bf F})\|_{L^{2}}
.
Going back to (
2.18
), we obtain

\|\nabla_{N}\cdot(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}f)\|_{2}\leq\gamma_{1}\|\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}\|_{H^{\delta}}\cdot\|\nabla f_{\bf F}\|_{L^{2}}\leq\gamma_{1}\|\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}^{n}\|_{H^{\delta}}\cdot\|\nabla_{N}f\|_{2},

(2.21)

where
\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}^{n}
denotes the vorticity associated with the discrete velocity field
\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n}
at time level
t^{n}
,
\gamma_{1}>0
is a generic constant independent of the spatial grid size, and the second step comes from the fact that
\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$},f_{\bf F}\in{\cal B}^{K}
, so that the corresponding
L^{2}
and
H^{1}
norms are equivalent between the continuous projection and the discrete version.In addition, the other nonlinear term can be estimated as follows:

\displaystyle\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n}\cdot\nabla_{N}f\|_{2}\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n}\right\|_{\infty}\,\left\|\nabla_{N}f\right\|_{2}\leq\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$U$}^{n}\right\|_{\infty}\,\left\|\nabla_{N}f\right\|_{2}

(2.22)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|\mbox{\boldmath$U$}^{n}\|_{H^{1+\delta}}\,\|\nabla_{N}f\|_{2}\leq\gamma_{2}\|\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}^{n}\|_{H^{\delta}}\,\|\nabla_{N}f\|_{2},

where
\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n}
denotes the discrete velocity field at time level
t^{n}
, and

\mbox{\boldmath$U$}^{n}=(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n})_{N}
is its continuous Fourier interpolation. The constant

\gamma_{2}>0
depends only on the domain and the Sobolev embedding constants,
but is independent of the grid size and the time step.
Consequently, a combination of (
2.21
) and (
2.22
) yields (
2.13
), by taking
\gamma_{0}=\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}
. This finishes the proof of Lemma
2.3
.
∎

2.2
IMEX BDF3 numerical scheme

For the 2-D incompressible NSE (
1.1
)-(
1.3
), we treat the nonlinear convection term explicitly for the sake of numerical convenience, and the diffusion term implicitly to preserve an
L^{2}
stability. This semi-implicit scheme leads to a Poisson-like equation at each time step, which makes the numerical algorithm extremely efficient. After the scalar vorticity is updated, the stream function can be determined through the kinematic equation, a Poisson equation. In turn, the velocity is computed as the perpendicular gradient of the stream function.

The IMEX BDF3 numerical scheme is given by

\displaystyle\frac{\frac{11}{6}\omega^{n+1}-3\omega^{n}+\frac{3}{2}\omega^{n-1}-\frac{1}{3}\omega^{n-2}}{{\Delta t}}+\frac{3}{2}(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n}\!\cdot\!\nabla_{N}\omega^{n}+\nabla_{N}\cdot(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n}\omega^{n})-\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n}\!\cdot\!\nabla_{N}\omega^{n}})

\displaystyle-\frac{3}{2}(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n-1}\!\cdot\!\nabla_{N}\omega^{n-1}+\nabla_{N}\cdot(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n-1}\omega^{n-1})-\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n-1}\!\cdot\!\nabla_{N}\omega^{n-1}})

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n-2}\!\cdot\!\nabla_{N}\omega^{n-2}+\nabla_{N}\cdot(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n-2}\omega^{n-2})-\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n-2}\!\cdot\!\nabla_{N}\omega^{n-2}})=\nu\Delta_{N}\omega^{n+1}+\mbox{\boldmath$f$}_{N}^{n+1},

(2.23)

\displaystyle-\Delta_{N}\psi^{n+1}=\omega^{n+1},

(2.24)

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n+1}=\nabla_{N}^{\bot}\psi^{n+1}=\left({\cal D}_{Ny}\psi^{n+1},-{\cal D}_{Nx}\psi^{n+1}\right),

(2.25)

in which
\mbox{\boldmath$f$}_{N}
is the Fourier projection of the external force term
f
onto the space of
{\cal B}^{K}
. It is clear that

\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}_{N}^{\ell}}=\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}\,\mbox{\boldmath$f$}_{N}\,d{\bf x}=0,

since
\mbox{\boldmath$f$}_{N}\in{\cal B}^{K}
, and
\int_{\Omega}\,\mbox{\boldmath$f$}_{N}\,d{\bf x}=\int_{\Omega}\,\mbox{\boldmath$f$}\,d{\bf x}=0
. At each time step
t^{\ell}
, the nonlinear term turns out to be a spectral approximation to
\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\!\cdot\!\nabla\omega
at time step
t^{n}
. The reason for such a skew-average treatment will be observed in the later analysis. In addition, an average constant, namely
\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{\ell}\!\cdot\!\nabla_{N}\omega^{\ell}}
, has to be subtracted at each time step. In fact, such a correction term is of spectral accuracy, due to the following fact:

\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{\ell}\!\cdot\!\nabla_{N}\omega^{\ell}=\nabla_{N}\cdot(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{\ell}\!\cdot\!\omega^{\ell})+O(h^{m}),\quad\mbox{and}\,\,\,\overline{\nabla_{N}\cdot(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{\ell}\!\cdot\!\omega^{\ell})}=0,

(2.26)

while the second identity comes from the summation-by-parts formula. Such a spectrally accurate, constant correction term is to ensure the zero-average property for the numerical solution at a discrete level, as stated in the following proposition.

Proposition 2.1
.

If the initial data satisfies
\overline{\omega^{0}}=\overline{\omega^{-1}}=\overline{\omega^{-2}}=0
, we have
\overline{\omega^{n}}=0
(
\forall n\geq 3
), for the multi-step numerical scheme (
2.23
)-(
2.25
).

Proof.

It is clear that, at each time step
t^{\ell}
,
\ell=n,n-1,n-2
, the following identities are valid:

\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{\ell}\!\cdot\!\nabla_{N}\omega^{\ell}-\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{\ell}\!\cdot\!\nabla_{N}\omega^{\ell}}}=\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{\ell}\!\cdot\!\nabla_{N}\omega^{\ell}}-\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{\ell}\!\cdot\!\nabla_{N}\omega^{\ell}}=0,\quad\overline{\nabla_{N}\cdot(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{\ell}\!\cdot\!\omega^{\ell})}=0,

which in turn gives
\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{\ell}\!\cdot\!\nabla_{N}\omega^{\ell}+\nabla_{N}\cdot(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{\ell}\!\cdot\!\omega^{\ell})-\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{\ell}\!\cdot\!\nabla_{N}\omega^{\ell}}}=0
.
In addition, the diffusion term and the external force term are always average-free:

\overline{\Delta_{N}\omega^{n+1}}=0,\quad\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}_{N}^{n+1}}=0.

Therefore, by taking a discrete average of the numerical algorithm (
2.23
) on both sides, we obtain

\frac{11}{6}\overline{\omega^{n+1}}-3\overline{\omega^{n}}+\frac{3}{2}\overline{\omega^{n-1}}-\frac{1}{3}\overline{\omega^{n-2}}=0,

so that

\overline{\omega^{n+1}}=0,\quad\mbox{if}\,\,\,\overline{\omega^{n}}=\overline{\omega^{n-1}}=\overline{\omega^{n-2}}=0,\quad\forall n\geq 2.

This finishes the proof of Proposition
2.1
.
∎

Remark 2.1
.

We emphasize that Proposition
2.1
is concerned with the BDF3 recurrence (
2.23
) for
n\geq 2
, which yields
\overline{\omega^{n+1}}=0
for all
n\geq 2
once the three previous time levels are mean-free. In practice, the first two time levels
\omega^{1}
and
\omega^{2}
are generated by the prescribed initial guesses
\omega^{-2},\omega^{-1},\omega^{0}
(and the corresponding starting-up procedure), which are chosen to be mean-free. Hence the relation
\overline{\omega^{1}}=\overline{\omega^{2}}=0
holds by construction, and the zero-average property is preserved for all
n\geq 1
.

Remark 2.2
.

For the three-dimensional incompressible fluid, the formulation of the stream function potential and vorticity vector would lead to a double curl problem, and its ill-posed feature has been well-known. In more details, both the stream function potential and vorticity are three-dimensional vector fields, and the kinematic equation is given by

\nabla\times(\nabla\times\vec{\psi})=-\Delta\vec{\psi}+\nabla(\nabla\cdot\vec{\psi})=\nabla\times\mbox{\boldmath$u$}=\vec{\omega}.

In comparison with the standard Poisson equation, this equation is ill-posed, due to a point-wise constraint
\nabla\cdot\vec{\psi}\equiv 0
. Of course, this kinematic equation is reduced to a standard Poisson equation, if such a divergence-free constraint is satisfied for the stream function potential. However, an enforcement of the divergence-free condition for the stream function potential turns out to be a very challenging issue, at both the PDE analysis and numerical levels.

On the other hand, in the two-dimensional space, such a difficulty associated with the ill-posed feature of the double curl operator is automatically avoided. In this case, both the discrete stream function and vorticity are scalar functions. Again, if the discrete stream function and vorticity vectors are denoted as
\vec{\psi}^{n+1}=(0,0,\psi^{n+1})^{T}
,
\vec{\omega}^{n+1}=(0,0,\omega^{n+1})^{T}
, respectively, a direct calculation implies that the kinematic equation automatically becomes a standard Poisson equation:

\nabla\times(\nabla\times\vec{\psi})=-\Delta\vec{\psi}+\nabla(\nabla\cdot\vec{\psi})=-\Delta\vec{\psi}=\vec{\omega},

based on the fact that
\nabla\cdot\vec{\psi}=\partial_{z}\psi\equiv 0
. In other words, for the two-dimensional incompressible fluid, the double curl problem is not ill-posed any more; only a standard Poisson solver is needed at each time step.

As a result of this proposition, it is obvious that the kinematic equation (
2.24
) has a unique solution, with a requirement that
\overline{\psi^{n+1}}=0
. Moreover, it is observed that the numerical velocity
\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{\ell}=\nabla_{N}^{\bot}\psi^{\ell}
is automatically divergence-free:

\nabla_{N}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{\ell}={\cal D}_{Nx}u^{\ell}+{\cal D}_{Ny}v^{\ell}={\cal D}_{Nx}({\cal D}_{Ny}\psi^{\ell})-{\cal D}_{Ny}({\cal D}_{Nx}\psi^{\ell})=0,\quad\forall\ell\geq 0,

due to the commutative property of the discrete differential operators. In terms of the skew-average treatment of the nonlinear terms, a careful application of summation-by-parts formula (
2.12
) gives

\left\langle\omega,\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\!\cdot\!\nabla_{N}\omega+\nabla_{N}\cdot\left(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\omega\right)\right\rangle=\left\langle\omega,\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\!\cdot\!\nabla_{N}\omega\right\rangle-\left\langle\nabla_{N}\omega,\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\omega\right\rangle=0.

In other words, the nonlinear convection term appearing in the numerical scheme (
2.23
), so-called Temam technique, makes the nonlinear term orthogonal to the vorticity field in the discrete
L^{2}
space, without considering the temporal discretization. This property is crucial in the stability analysis for the fully discrete numerical scheme; see the related references
[
17
,
37
,
6
]
.

In addition, we denote
\mbox{\boldmath$U$}^{\ell}=(U^{\ell},V^{\ell})
,
\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}^{\ell}
and
\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$}^{\ell}
as the continuous projection of
\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{\ell}
,
\omega^{\ell}
and
\psi^{\ell}
, respectively, with the projection formula given by (
2.8
), (
2.9
). It is clear that
\mbox{\boldmath$U$}^{n},\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}^{n},\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$}^{n}\in{\cal B}^{K}
and the kinematic equation
\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$}^{\ell}=\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}^{\ell}
,
\mbox{\boldmath$U$}^{\ell}=\nabla^{\bot}\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$}^{\ell}
is satisfied at the continuous level. Because of these kinematic equations, an application of elliptic regularity shows that

\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$}^{n}\right\|_{H^{m+2}}\leq C\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}^{n}\right\|_{H^{m}},\quad\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$}^{n}\right\|_{H^{m+2+\alpha}}\leq C\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}^{n}\right\|_{H^{m+\alpha}},

(2.27)

in which we normalize the stream function with
\int_{\Omega}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$}^{\ell}\,d{\bf x}=0
. Meanwhile, since all the profiles have mean zero over the domain:

\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$}^{n}}=0,\quad\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$U$}^{n}}=\left(\overline{\partial_{y}\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$}^{n}},-\overline{\partial_{x}\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$}^{n}}\right)=0,\quad\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}^{n}}=\overline{\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$}^{n}}=0,

all the Poincaré inequality and elliptic regularity could be effectively applied.

Remark 2.3
.

For the continuous 2-D NSE, the classical theory guarantees a global-in-time
L^{2}
bound on the vorticity under the sole assumption
\omega_{0}\in L^{2}
. However, the multi-step nature of the proposed IMEX BDF3 scheme (
2.23
)-(
2.25
) makes the numerical analysis much more complicated than the one for the continuous equation. In more details, the
L^{2}

F^{n}
, introduced in (
3.25
) and proved to have a global-in-time bound, contains a few
H^{1}
terms of the numerical solution, with coefficients of order
\mathcal{O}(\Delta t)
. Therefore, to theoretically derive a uniform bound for
F^{n}
in time, we need to require an
H^{1}
regularity class of the initial data.

Moreover, based on the time step size constraint (
3.22
) (needed in the nonlinear estimate), a global-in-time
H^{1}
estimate of the numerical solution becomes necessary to accomplish the theoretical analysis. Similarly,
H^{1}

G_{1}^{n}
, introduced in (
3.52
), contains a few
H^{2}
terms of the numerical solution, with coefficients of order
\mathcal{O}(\Delta t)
. As a consequence, to theoretically derive a uniform bound for
G_{1}^{n}
in time, we have to require an
H^{2}
regularity class of the initial data.

The global-in-time bounds of
F^{n}
and
G_{1}^{n}
are necessary steps to derive both the uniform-in-time
L^{2}
and
H^{1}
bounds of the numerical solutions, and these two estimates have to be combined together. In other words, because of the time step size constraint (
3.22
), a global-in-time estimate for

could not be independently accomplished, and the bound estimate for

plays an essential role to recover the value of
\tilde{C}_{1}
, which in turn requires an

initial data. Therefore, the stronger regularity assumption on the initial vorticity reflects a limitation of the current analytical technique rather than a contradiction with the classical continuous theory. This assumption is technical and is not intended to be optimal.

4
An optimal rate convergence analysis for the BDF3 scheme

Now we proceed into the convergence analysis. Denote
(\omega_{e},\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{e},\psi_{e})
as the exact solution to the original NSE system (
1.1
)-(
1.3
). With an initial data with sufficient regularity, the exact solution is assumed to preserve a regularity of class
\mathcal{R}
:

\omega_{e}\in\mathcal{R}:=C^{4}(0,T;C^{0})\cap H^{3}(0,T;H^{1})\cap L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{m+2}).

(4.1)

To facilitate the error estimate, we need to ensure the numerical error is mean free. Define
\mbox{\boldmath$U$}_{N}(\,\cdot\,,t):={\cal P}_{N}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{e}(\,\cdot\,,t)
,
\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$}_{N}(\,\cdot\,,t):={\cal P}_{N}\psi_{e}(\,\cdot\,,t)
,
\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}_{N}(\,\cdot\,,t):={\cal P}_{N}\omega_{e}(\,\cdot\,,t)
, the (spatial) Fourier projection of the exact solution into
{\cal B}^{K}
, in the velocity, stream function, and vorticity variables, respectively. The following projection approximation is standard: if
\omega_{e}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{\ell}_{\rm per}(\Omega))
, for some
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
,

\|\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}_{N}-\omega_{e}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{k})}\leq Ch^{\ell-k}\|\omega_{e}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{\ell})},\quad\forall\ 0\leq k\leq\ell.

(4.2)

By
\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}_{N}^{m}
we denote
\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}_{N}(\,\cdot\,,t^{m})
, with
t^{m}=m\cdot{\Delta t}
. Since
\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}_{N}\in{\cal B}^{K}
, the zero-average property is available at the discrete level:

\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}_{N}^{m}}=\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}_{N}(\cdot,t^{m})\,d{\bf x}=0,\quad\forall\ m\in\mathbb{N}.

(4.3)

On the other hand, the solution of the BDF3 scheme (
2.23
)-(
2.25
) is also zero-average at the discrete level, as stated in Proposition
2.1
:

\overline{\omega^{m}}=0,\quad\forall\ m\in\mathbb{N}.

(4.4)

Meanwhile, we use the mass conservative projection for the initial data:

\omega^{0}_{i,j}=(\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}_{N}^{0})_{i,j}:=\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}_{N}(x_{i},y_{j},t=0).

(4.5)

The error grid functions, for the vorticity, velocity and stream function variables, are defined as

e_{\omega}^{m}:=\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}_{N}^{m}-\omega^{m},\,\,\,e_{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}^{m}:=\mbox{\boldmath$U$}_{N}^{m}-\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{m}=(e_{u}^{m},e_{v}^{m}),\,\,\,e_{\psi}^{m}:=\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$}_{N}^{m}-\psi^{m},\quad\forall\ m\in\mathbb{N}.

(4.6)

Therefore, it follows that
\overline{e_{\omega}^{m}}=0
, for any
m\in\mathbb{N}
.

For the third order BDF scheme (
2.23
)-(
2.25
), the
\ell^{\infty}(0,T;\ell^{2})\cap\ell^{2}(0,T;H^{1})
convergence result is stated below.

Theorem 4.3
.

Given initial data
\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}_{N}^{0}\in C_{\rm per}^{m+2}(\overline{\Omega})
, with periodic boundary conditions, suppose the unique solution for the the NSE equation (
1.1
)-(
1.3

6
Concluding remarks

In this paper, we proposed an IMEX BDF3 scheme for the 2-D incompressible Navier-Stokes equation. This scheme improves the computational efficiency on the premise of ensuring stability and accuracy. In addition, we provided a long-time stability analysis for the vorticity and a rigorous convergence analysis for the fully discrete scheme, combined with Fourier pseudo-spectral spatial approximation. The numerical experiments verified the main theorems and demonstrated the performance of the proposed scheme.

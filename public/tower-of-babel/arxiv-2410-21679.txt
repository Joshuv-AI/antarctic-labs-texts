1.
Introduction

Let
K
be a number field and
X
a smooth projective variety over
K
. An endomorphism
\varphi:X\to X
is polarized of degree
d
if there exists an ample line bundle
L
and an isomorphism
\varphi^{*}L\simeq L^{\otimes d}
for an integer
d\geq 2
. For example, any endomorphism of
\mathbb{P}^{r}
with degree
d\geq 2
is polarizable of the same degree. Another example is the multiplication by
n
map on any abelian variety
A
for an integer
n\geq 2
, which is polarizable of degree
n^{2}
. By results of Fakkrhudin
[
Fak14
]
, if
\varphi:X\to X
is a polarized endomorphism then we may find an embedding
X\xhookrightarrow{}\mathbb{P}^{N}
into projective space such that
\varphi
extends to a morphism
\tilde{\varphi}:\mathbb{P}^{N}\to\mathbb{P}^{N}
.

If
h_{L}
denotes a Weil height associated to the ample line bundle
L
, we can define a canonical height by the formula

\widehat{h}_{\varphi}(x)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{h_{L}(\varphi^{n}(x))}{d^{n}}

for all
x\in\overline{K}
. By
[
CS93
]
, this limit is well-defined and
\widehat{h}_{\varphi}(x)=0
occurs exactly when
x
is a preperiodic point for
\varphi
, i.e. there exists
m>n
such that
\varphi^{m}(x)=\varphi^{n}(x)
.

Points of small canonical height play an important role in diophantine geometry. For example the Bogomolov conjecture, proven by Ullmo
[
Ull98
]
and generalized by Zhang
[
Zha98
]
, predicts that points of small Neron–Tate height on a subvariety
X
of an abelian variety
A
is not Zariski dense unless
X
is a torsion coset of
A
. Due to the many connections between arithmetic dynamics and diophantine geometry, they also show up in important conjectures from arithmetic dynamics, such as the dynamical Bogomolov conjecture (see
[
Zha06
,
GTZ11
]
) and the dynamical Andre–Oort conjecture
[
BD13
]
.

One of the main tools to deal with problems involving small points is equidistribution. For the case of abelian varieties, this goes back to Spziro–Ullmo–Zhang
[
SUZ97
]
. It was subsequently proven for multiplicative tori by Bilu
[
Bil97
]
and then for all polarized dynamical systems on projective varieties by Yuan
[
Yua08
]
. In fact, Yuan’s theorem holds more generally for heights coming from adelic line bundles. For simplicity, we will state Yuan’s theorem only for canonical heights on
X
.

Given a sequence of distinct points
(x_{n})_{n\geq 1}
of
X(\overline{K})
, we say that the sequence is small if
\widehat{h}_{\varphi}(x_{n})\to 0
and that the sequence is generic if no infinite subsequence of
(x_{n})
is contained in a proper subvariety
Z
of
X
. Let
v:K\xrightarrow{}\mathbb{C}
be an embedding. We may then view
\varphi
as an endomorphism of
X(\mathbb{C}_{v})
and let
\mu_{\varphi,v}
be the equilibrium measure of
\varphi
on
X(\mathbb{C}_{v})

[
DS10
]
. We say that the Galois orbits of
(x_{n})
are equidistributed with respect to a measure
\mu
if the discrete measures
\mu_{n}=\frac{1}{|F_{x_{n}}|}\sum_{y\in F_{x_{n}}}\delta_{y}
, where
F_{x_{n}}
is the
\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{K}/K)
-orbit of
x_{n}
, converge weakly to
\mu
.

Theorem 1.1

(Theorem 3.7
[
Yua08
]
)
.

Let
(x_{n})
be a generic and small sequence for the canonical height
\widehat{h}_{\varphi}
. Then for any embedding
v\xrightarrow{}\mathbb{C}
, the Galois orbits of
(x_{n})
are equidistributed with respect to the measure
\mu_{\varphi,v}
on
X(\mathbb{C}_{v})
.

It is easy to see that the genericity of
(x_{n})
is a necessary condition. For example if
V
is a preperiodic subvariety of
\mathbb{P}^{r}
for
V
, then
V
has infinitely many preperiodic points
[
Fak03
]
and choosing our sequence
(x_{n})
from them, any possible limit measure must be supported on
V(\mathbb{C}_{v})
. However, the equilibrium measure
\mu_{\varphi,v}
is not supported on any subvariety as it has zero measure on pluripolar sets
[
DS10
, Proposition 1.2.3]
.

Yuan’s theorem holds too for non-archimedean places
v
but one has to consider measures on the Berkovich analytification
X_{v}^{\operatorname{an}}
. The results of
[
Yua08
]
has been extended by Yuan–Zhang
[
YZ24
]
to the setting of heights arising from adelic line bundles on quasi-projective varieties (see also
[
Gau23
]
).

In dimension
1
, Favre–Rivera-Letelier
[
FR06
]
has proved a quantitative equidistribution theorem for heights arising from adelic measures on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
using different ideas from
[
SUZ97
]
and
[
Yua08
]
. For the Neron–Tate height on elliptic curves, Baker and Petsche
[
BP05
,
Pet09
]
have also proven quantitative bounds on the distribution of small points. However, their methods have yet to be generalized to higher dimensions and to date, the only known proof of
[
Yua08
]
is following the strategy in
[
SUZ97
]
.

Our main result in this paper is a quantitative version of Theorem
1.1
when
X
is assumed to be smooth. Other than the case of
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}
by D’Andrea–Narváez-Clauss–Sombra
[
DNS17
]
which reduces to the one-dimensional case, we believe that this is the first result regarding quantitative equidistribution of small points that deal with endomorphisms on varieties of dimension larger than one.

Given an ample line bundle
L
on
X
, we fix some power of it such that
X
embeds into some
\mathbb{P}^{N}
under this power. We say that a subvariety
V
of
X
has degree
D
with respect to
L
if
V
has degree
D
inside
\mathbb{P}^{N}
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety defined over a number field
K
and let
\varphi:X\to X
a polarized endomorphism of degree
d\geq 2
, i.e. there exists an ample line bundle
L
such that
\varphi^{*}L\simeq L^{\otimes d}
. Then there exists constants
c_{1}=c_{1}(K,\varphi),c_{2}=c_{2}(\varphi),c_{3}=c_{3}(K,\varphi)
such that for any
\varepsilon>0
and embedding
v:K\xhookrightarrow{}\mathbb{C}
, if
f:X(\mathbb{C}_{v})\to\mathbb{R}
is a smooth function, then

(1)

there exists a hypersurface
H(f,\varepsilon)
defined over
K
which has degree at most
c_{1}\varepsilon^{-c_{2}}
with respect to
L
,

(2)

for all
x\in X(\overline{K})
with
\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{K}/K)
-orbit
F_{x}
, if
x\not\in H(f,\varepsilon)
then

\left|\frac{1}{|F_{x}|}\sum_{y\in F_{x}}f(y)-\int f(y)d\mu_{\varphi,v}(y)\right|\leq c_{f,3}\left(\frac{\widehat{h}_{\varphi}(x)}{\varepsilon}+c_{3}\varepsilon\right),

where
c_{f,3}
is a constant depending on the derivatives of
f
up to order
3
.

The constant
c_{f,3}
depends on the
C^{3}
-norm of
f
. We will give the precise definition of
c_{f,3}
later in Section
2
. The constants
c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},c_{f,3}
can be made explicit in terms of the data given. For example, the exponent
c_{2}
depends only on the dimension of
X
along with a Holder exponent for the Green’s function at the archimedean place
v
. In fact for abelian varieties, due to the smoothness of the metric, we show that we can take
c_{2}=8
, a constant independent of
X
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
X
be an abelian variety over a number field
K
and
L
a symmetric ample line bundle on
X
. Let
\widehat{h}_{X}(x)
be the Neron–Tate height associated to
L
. Then there exists constants
c_{1}=c_{1}(K,L),c_{3}=c_{3}(K,L)
such that for any
\varepsilon>0
and embedding
v:K\xhookrightarrow{}\mathbb{C}
, if
f:X(\mathbb{C}_{v})\to\mathbb{R}
is a smooth function, then

(1)

there exists a hypersurface
H(f,\varepsilon)
defined over
K
which has degree at most
c_{1}\varepsilon^{-8}
with respect to
L
,

(2)

for all
x\in X(\overline{K})
with
\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{K}/K)
-orbit
F_{x}
, if
x\not\in H(f,\varepsilon)
then

\left|\frac{1}{|F_{x}|}\sum_{y\in F_{x}}f(y)-\int f(y)d\mu_{X,v}(y)\right|\leq c_{f,3}\left(\frac{\widehat{h}_{X}(x)}{\varepsilon}+c_{3}\varepsilon\right),

where
c_{f,3}
is a constant depending on the derivatives of
f
up to order
3
and
\mu_{X,v}
is the Haar measure for
A
associated to
v
.

The condition of
x\not\in H(f,\varepsilon)
appears in Theorem
1.1
through the genericity of the sequence
(x_{n})
. Although it is not clear from the statement of Theorem
1.1
that such a hypersurface
H(f,\varepsilon)
exists, it follows immediately from the proof of Theorem
1.1
, for e.g. see
[
Küh21
, Lemma 23]
. The new result in Theorem
1.2
is a bound on the degree of
H(f,\varepsilon)
, which tells you how “generic”
x
has to be to achieve a desired accuracy for a given test function
f
and
\varepsilon>0
.

Recently, there have been many breakthrough involving uniform bounds of unlikely intersection problems with proofs relying on equidistribution, e.g.
[
Küh21
,
GGK21
,
MS22
,
GTV23
,
DM24
,
DMY24
]
. These results prove uniform bounds on the number of special points as one varies over a family, but it is not known how these bounds depend on the parameters of the given family as Theorem
1.1
and its variants are all inexplicit. We hope that Theorem
1.2
would be the first step in producing explciit constants for the above results.

We now give two applications of Theorem
1.2
. The first concerns the rate of equidistribution of periodic points to the equilibrium measure. Let
\varphi:\mathbb{P}^{r}\to\mathbb{P}^{r}
be an endomorphism of degree
d\geq 2
defined over
\mathbb{C}
and let
\operatorname{Per}_{n}
denote the set of periodic points of period
n
for
\varphi
, where we do not count with multiplicity. Then Briend–Duval
[
BD01
]
has shown that as
n
goes to infinity, we have

(1)

\frac{1}{|\operatorname{Per}_{n}|}\sum_{x\in\operatorname{Per}_{n}}\delta_{x}\to\mu_{\varphi}.

Dinh–Sibony
[
DS17
, Problem 7]
asks for an estimate of the rate of convergence of (
1
). When
\varphi
is defined over a number field
K
, we will show that the convergence is of an exponential rate. Over
\mathbb{C}
, this has recently been shown for
\mathbb{P}^{1}
by Dinh–Kaufman
[
DK24
]
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective surface over a number field
K
and
\varphi:X\to X
a polarized endomorphism of degree
d\geq 2
. Then there exists constants
C>0
and
\lambda>1
, depending on
\varphi
, such that if
v
is an archimedean place of
K
, then for any smooth function
f:X(\mathbb{C}_{v})\to\mathbb{R}
, we have

\left|\frac{1}{|\operatorname{Per}_{n}|}\sum_{x\in\operatorname{Per}_{n}}f(x)-\int fd\mu_{\varphi,v}\right|\leq Cc_{f,3}\lambda^{-n}.

Our second application of Theorem
1.2
concerns the set of preperiodic points
x
of
\varphi
that satisfy
[K(x):K]\leq D
for some fixed positive integer
D\geq 1
. In
[
Bak06
]
, Baker showed that if
\varphi:\mathbb{P}^{1}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
is a rational map defined over
K
of degree
d\geq 2
, then there are at most
O_{\varphi}(D^{1+\varepsilon})
preperiodic points in a fixed extension
L/K
of degree
D
. Our first application is a higher dimensional analogue of Baker’s result, where we control the geometry of preperiodic points of bounded degree
\leq D
. We will let the set of preperiodic points having degree
\leq D
over
K
be denoted by
\operatorname{Prep}_{\varphi,D}
and similarly denote the set of periodic points by
\operatorname{Per}_{\varphi,D}
.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety defined over a number field
K
and
\varphi:X\to X
a polarized endomoprhism of degree
d\geq 2
. There exists a constant
e=e(\varphi)>0
such that for any positive integer
D>0
, there exists a hypersurface
H
of degree at most
O_{\varphi,K}(D^{e})
, such that
\operatorname{Prep}_{\varphi,D}\subseteq H
.

One difference from Theorem
1.5
and Baker’s result is that we are able to handle all extensions of degree
\leq D
at once, instead of fixing a single extension
L
. In the case of
r=1
, our hypersurface
H
is an union of at most
O_{\varphi}(D^{e})
points and so we obtain a bound on
|\operatorname{Prep}_{\varphi,D}|
. This appears to already be a new result, although we will show that one can also deduce this from Favre–Rivera-Letelier’s quantitative equidistribution theorem (see Proposition
10.4
).

In the case of abelian varieties, due to the stronger estimates from Theorem
1.3
, we can deduce the following result.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
X
be an abelian variety over a number field
K
and let
\widehat{h}_{X}(x)
denote the Neron–Tate height of
X
with respect to a symmetric ample line bundle
L
. Then there exists
c=c(L,K)>0
, such that if

S=\{x\in X(\overline{K})\mid\widehat{h}_{X}(x)\leq\frac{c}{D^{8}}\text{ and }[K(x):K]\leq D\},

then
S
is contained in a hypersurface of degree
O_{L,K}(D^{33})
.

In
[
Mas84
]
, Masser proved an upper bound on the number of small points for an abelian variety
X
that are defined over a fixed extension of degree
D
. More recently, Gaudron–Remond
[
GR22
]
proves an explicit version of Masser’s result through different methods. Again, our theorem differs from both of theirs as it is able to handle all extensions of degree
\leq D
at once rather than just a fixed extension.

Applying Theorem
1.5
to
\operatorname{Per}_{n}
, the set of periodic points of period
n
, gives us a generalization of
[
Bak06
, Corollary 1.19]
. Let
K_{n}=K(\operatorname{Per}_{n})
denote the field obtained by adjoining every element of
\operatorname{Per}_{n}
to
K
. In the case of
\varphi:\mathbb{P}^{1}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
, Baker showed that
[K_{n}:K]\geq O(c^{n})
for any
c<d
. We show that for polarized endomorphisms of projective surfaces
X
, we can find some constant
c>1
such that
[K_{n}:K]\geq c^{n}
.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
X
be a projective smooth surface defined over a number field
K
and let
\varphi:X\to X
be a polarized endomorphism of degree
d\geq 2
. Then there exists
c=c(\varphi,K)
such that
[K_{n}:K]\geq c^{n}
. In fact there exists
c^{\prime}>1
such that for
1-O(c^{\prime-n})
proportion of points
x\in\operatorname{Per}_{n}
, we will have
[K(x):K]\geq c^{n}
.

When
X=\mathbb{P}^{2}
, under some conditions, we are also able to get a numerical bound on
|\operatorname{Per}_{\varphi,D}|
, the set of periodic points for
\varphi
of degree
\leq D
.

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
\varphi:\mathbb{P}^{2}\to\mathbb{P}^{2}
be an endomorphism of degree
d\geq 2
defined over a number field
K
. Further assume that there are only finitely many periodic curves for
\varphi
. Then there exists
e=e(\varphi)>0
such that
|\operatorname{Per}_{\varphi,D}|\leq O_{\varphi,K}(D^{e})
.

Heuristically one expects that a general endomorphism
\varphi:\mathbb{P}^{r}\to\mathbb{P}^{r}
should have only finitely many periodic subvarieties, although this is not known yet. However, results of Xie
[
Xie24
]
show that this is true for a general endomorphism
\varphi:\mathbb{A}^{2}\to\mathbb{A}^{2}
that extends to an endomorphism of
\mathbb{P}^{2}
. In some cases where we have infinitely many periodic subvarieties, it might still be possible to still deduce a numerical bound if we are able to understand their geometry. As an example, check out Theorem
10.11
.

We remark that there are previous known bounds on the number of periodic points in a fixed extension
L/K
of degree
D
for an endomorphism
\varphi:X\to X
given by Hutz
[
Hut09
,
Hut18
]
and Huang
[
Hua21
]
, following the ideas of Morton–Silverman
[
MS94
]
. The bounds they get are uniform over certain families, but they grow super-exponentially large as
D
increases.

1.1.
Comparison with other results

We now compare Theorem
1.2
with other quantitative equidistribution theorems in different settings. In
\mathbb{P}^{1}
, equidistirbution of small points for canonical heights was proven independently by Baker–Rumely
[
BR06
,
BR10
]
, Chambert-Loir
[
Cha06
]
and Favre–Rivera-Letelier
[
FR06
]
. Favre–Rivera-Letelier’s results also give an explicit control over the rate of equidistribution as follows: if
x
is a point of
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
and we let
F_{x}
denote the Galois orbit of
x
, then for any
C^{2}
function
f
and archimedean place
v\in M_{K}
, we have

(2)

\left|\frac{1}{|F_{x}|}\sum_{y\in F_{x}}f(y)-\int fd\mu_{\varphi,v}\right|\leq\widehat{h}_{\varphi}(x)+\frac{c\log|F|}{|F|^{1/2}}(\triangle f,\triangle f)_{v}

where
c=c(\varphi,K)>0
is some constant depending on our rational map
\varphi
and the number field
K
and
(\,,\,)_{v}
is some energy pairing of measures. Hence if we wanted the LHS to be less than
\varepsilon
, we require
\widehat{h}_{\varphi}(x)\leq\varepsilon
and
|F|
to be at least
\varepsilon^{-2+\delta}
in size for some fixed
\delta>0
. For a more explicit version of what the constant
c
is in terms of
\varphi
and
K
, one may look at
[
Fil17
]
. For elliptic curves over function fields, a similar statement is proven by Petsche
[
Pet09
]
.

When specialized to
X=\mathbb{P}^{1}
, Theorem
1.2
tells us that for each smooth function
f
and
\varepsilon>0
, there is a finite
\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{K}/K)
-invariant set of points
H(f,\varepsilon)
of size at most
c_{2}\varepsilon^{-c_{1}}
such that if
x
is not a member of
H(f,\varepsilon)
, then

(3)

\left|\frac{1}{|F_{x}|}\sum_{y\in F_{x}}f(y)-\int fd\mu_{\varphi,v}\right|\leq\frac{\widehat{h}_{\varphi}(x)}{\varepsilon}+c_{3}\varepsilon

where
c_{2},c_{3}
depends on
\varphi
and
K
and
c_{1}
depends only on
\varphi
. Hence for example, if
|F_{x}|
were larger than
c_{2}\varepsilon^{-c_{1}}
in size, then
x
cannot lie in
H(f,\varepsilon)
and so our (
3
) would always hold. This gives a similar statement to Favre–Rivera-Letelier’s as we only require
|F_{x}|
to be larger than some polynomial in
\varepsilon^{-1}
to achieve an
\varepsilon
accuracy, but is much weaker in the dependencies. For example we have an
\varepsilon
in the denominator of
\widehat{h}_{\varphi}(x)
but (
2
) does not and our exponent in
\varepsilon
depends on
\varphi
whereas (
2
) only requires an exponent of
2+\delta
. D’Andrea–Narváez-Clauss–Sombra’s theorem for
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}
reduces to
[
FR06
]
and thus their estimates have the same form as Favre–Rivera-Letelier’s estimates.

We now move onto dynamical settings, where one considers all iterated preimages
f^{-n}(x)
of a point
x\in\mathbb{P}^{r}(\mathbb{C})
. Here, both
f
and
x
need not be defined over a number field. Equidistribution of preimages for a rational map
f:\mathbb{P}^{1}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
was first proven by Freire–Lopes–Mane
[
FLM83
]
and Lyubich
[
Lyu83
]
independently. The case of polynomials was proven by Brolin
[
Bro65
]
earlier. In the higher dimensional setting, there have been many results concerning equidistribution of subvarieties or points, see for e.g.
[
BD01
,
FJ03
,
DS03
]
. One may consult
[
DS10
]
for an excellent survey on these results.

On the quantitative side, we have the following result of Dinh–Sibony
[
DS10a
]
. For a holomorphic endomorphism
\varphi:\mathbb{P}^{r}\to\mathbb{P}^{r}
of algebraic degree
d\geq 2
, there exists a maximal totally invariant analytic subset
\cal{E}
. For simplicity, we will state their result when
\cal{E}=\emptyset
. Under this assumption, there exists a
\lambda>1
such that for any
C^{2}
function
f
and any
a\in\mathbb{P}^{r}(\mathbb{C})
, we have

\left|\frac{1}{|F_{n}|}\sum_{y\in F_{n}}f(y)-\int fd\mu_{\varphi}\right|\leq\frac{A||f||_{C^{2}}}{\lambda^{n}}

where
F_{n}=\varphi^{-n}(a)
as a multiset and
A
is a constant independent of
n,f
and
a
. If one knew the existence of
\lambda>1
such that no hypersurface of degree
\lambda^{n}
contains
\frac{1}{\lambda^{n}}
-proportion of the points of
f^{-n}(a)
, we may apply Theorem
1.2
to obtain a similar result. However, it is not clear whether such a
\lambda
exists.

Dinh–Ma–Nguyen
[
DMN17
]
have also proven a quantitative equidistribution theorem for Fekete points. By the work of Looper
[
Loo24
]
, small points for the canonical height may be viewed as “almost” Fekete points for the homogeneous filled Julia set and so there might be a way to modify Dinh–Ma–Nguyen’s approach to work in our setting. In their theorem, the error bound depends on the
C^{3}
-norm of the test function
f
, similar to our error bound in Theorem
1.2
. This is not a coincidence as we both rely on quantitative estimates of asymptotic expansion of Bergman kernels, which is where the
C^{3}
-norm of
f
appears.

1.2.
Proof strategy

We follow the approach of Szpiro–Ullmo–Zhang
[
SUZ97
]
and Yuan
[
Yua08
]
. The general idea is to consider an adelic line bundle
\overline{L}
on
X
whose corresponding height function is equal to the canonical height
\widehat{h}_{\varphi}
. Given a smooth function
f:X(\mathbb{C}_{v})\to\mathbb{R}
for an archimedean place
v
, we consider the adelic line bundle
\overline{L}(\varepsilon f)
where we modify the metric at the place
v
by multiplying with
e^{-\varepsilon f}
.

In the case where
X
is an abelian variety and
\widehat{h}_{\varphi}
is the Neron–Tate height, the adelic line bundle
\overline{L}
is a positive line bundle at the archimedean places and so for
\varepsilon
small enough, the perturbed line bundle
\overline{L}(\varepsilon f)
remains positive. This allows us to apply Zhang’s inequality
[
Zha92
,
Zha95
]
to
\overline{L}(\varepsilon f)
and then some straightforward algebraic manipulation allows us to prove equidistribution.

For canonical heights in general, the adelic line bundle
\overline{L}
is not necessarily positive but only semipositive and hence even if
\varepsilon
is arbitrarily small, it is possible for
\overline{L}(\varepsilon f)
to be not semipositive. To handle this issue, Yuan proves an arithmetic Siu’s inequality which allows him to give a lower bound on the volume of
\overline{L}(\varepsilon f)
in terms of
\overline{L}
and
f
. This then allows him to prove a version of Zhang’s inequality for
\overline{L}(\varepsilon f)
.

Zhang’s inequality gives a lower bound on the height of points that lie outside a certain codimension one subvariety
H
, and it is this subvariety for
\overline{L}(\varepsilon f)
that will be our hypersurface
H(f,\varepsilon)
in Theorem
1.2
. The hypersurface arises as the divisor of a section
s
of
L^{n}
for some large
n>0
, where
L
is the underlying line bundle of
\overline{L}
, and thus to control the degree of
H(f,\varepsilon)
, we have to control how large
n
is.

For simplicity, we will assume that
K=\mathbb{Q}
. Then
H^{0}(X,L^{n})
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space and we may tensor up to
\mathbb{R}
to obtain a
\mathbb{R}
-vector space
V_{n}
. The sections
s
of
H^{0}(X,L^{n})
that satisfies
|s|_{\sup,v}\leq 1
for all non-archimedean places
v
forms a
\mathbb{Z}
-lattice
M_{n}
inside
V_{n}
. On the other hand, we may look at all sections
s\in V_{n}
that satisfies
|s|_{\sup,v}\leq 1
for the archimedean place
v
of
\mathbb{Q}
. This forms a convex ball
B_{n}
inside
V_{n}
. Fixing any choice of Haar measure, the quantity

\chi(\overline{L}^{n}):=\frac{1}{n}\left(\log\operatorname{vol}(B_{n})-\log\operatorname{vol}(M_{n})\right)

makes sense and is independent of our choice of Haar measure, where
\operatorname{vol}(M_{n})
denotes the volume of its fundamental region. To control the degree of
H(f,\varepsilon)
, one then has to prove lower bounds of
\chi(\overline{L}^{n})
for large
n
. We note that quantitative upper bounds have been proven by Xinyi Yuan and Tong Zhang
[
YZ13
]
.

In fact if
\overline{L}
is semipositive, as
n\to\infty
, there is a formula for the asymptotics of
\chi(\overline{L}^{n})
given by the arithmetic Hilbert–Samuel theorem
[
Zha95
, Theorem 1.4]
in terms of the arithmetic self-intersection number of
\overline{L}
. However, this relies on the deep results of Gillet–Soule
[
GS92
]
and Bismut–Vasserot
[
BV89
]
(see also Moriwaki’s book
[
Mor14
]
for an exposition), which makes it difficult to deduce an explicit lower bound for
\chi(\overline{L}^{n})
.

We instead give an alternative approach to obtain an explicit lower bound for
\chi(\overline{L}^{n})
, instead of relying on the arithmetic Hilbert–Samuel theorem. The idea is to find a suitable “canonical” basis
\cal{B}_{n}
of
H^{0}(X,L^{n})
with respect to
\varphi
, such that if
M^{\prime}_{n}
denotes the lattice spanned by this basis, we can find a suitable
c>0
such that its fundamental domain is contained inside
cB_{n}
and similarly the lattice
cM^{\prime}_{n}
is contained in
M_{n}
. Controlling
c
in terms of
n
then gives us a lower bound on
\chi(\overline{L}^{n})
.

The idea of constructing such a canonical basis
\cal{B}_{n}
goes back to Baker
[
Bak06
]
in his work on proving lower bounds for average values of Arakelov-Green functions associated to
\varphi
in the case of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. This result, along with Baker’s construction, has recently been generalized by Looper
[
Loo24
]
to the setting of a general polarized dynamical system. Our construction of this basis, which might look different on first sight, is essentially the same as Looper’s. A key obstacle in generalizing Baker’s work is that it is much harder to prove that a set of homogeneous polynomials form a basis when there are more than
2
variables. Looper gets around this by instead constructing a canonical spanning set of polynomials which is much easier. Then there must exist a basis formed by a subset of this canonical set which turns out to be enough for applications.

For equidistribution, we actually need a lower bound on
\chi(\overline{L}(\varepsilon f)^{n})
and not on
\chi(\overline{L}^{n})
. Using ideas from Yuan’s proof of the arithmetic Siu’s inequality which goes back to Abbes–Bouche
[
AB95
]
, we are able to directly prove a lower bound of
\chi(\overline{L}(\varepsilon f)^{n})
of the form

\chi(\overline{L}(\varepsilon f)^{n})\geq\chi(\overline{L}^{n})+\varepsilon\int fd\mu_{\varphi,v}+O(\varepsilon^{2})

for
n
large enough. Hence a lower bound on
\chi(\overline{L}^{n})
leads to a lower bound on
\chi(\overline{L}(\varepsilon f)^{n})
which then allows us to prove Theorem
1.2
. The main input is a quantitative control on the asymptotic expansion of Bergman kernels in terms of the positivity of the underlying
(1,1)
-form
\omega
of a positive line bundle
\overline{L}
.

We remark that there is recently a new proof of arithmetic Hilbert–Samuel via deformation to the normal cone by Dorian Ni
[
Ni22
]
. Also, David–Phillipon
[
DP99
]
prove lower bounds on
\chi(\overline{L}^{n})
that are explicit, but are not optimal in the sense that they do not give the correct asymptotics.

Notations

We now state some notation. Throughout the paper, we will use
O(X)
to denote a quantity which is bounded from above by a constant multiplied by
X
. When the constant depends on some other variable
Y
that are not fixed, we will try to make it clear by writing it as
O_{Y}(X)
instead.

In a local chart with coordinates
z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}
, for a
(p,q)
-form

u(z)=\sum_{|I|=p,|J|=q}u_{I,J}(z)dz_{I}\wedge d\overline{z}_{J},

\partial u=\sum_{|I|=p,|J|=q}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\partial u_{I,J}}{\partial z}dz_{k}\wedge dz_{I}\wedge d\operatorname{vol}{z}_{J}

\overline{\partial}

\frac{\partial}{\partial\overline{z}}

If
\overline{L}

\overline{L}(f)
denote the metriezd line bundle with norm
|\cdot|e^{-f}
, where
|\cdot|
is the original norm of
\overline{L}
. When
f
is a constant function
c
, we will simply denote it by
\overline{L}(c)
.

Background and Preliminaries

2.1.
Complex geometry background

Let
X
be a complex manifold of dimension
r
and let
\cal{U}_{I}=\{U_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in I}
be a choice of charts for
X
where
I
is a finite set. We say that
\cal{U}_{I}
is a choice of good charts for
X
if each
U_{\alpha}
is biholomorphic to the open polydisc of radius
3
,
\mathbb{D}_{3}
, and every point
x\in X
lies inside the unit polydisc
\mathbb{D}_{1}
for one of these charts.

Now fix a good choice of charts
\cal{U}_{I}
. Given a
C^{k}
function
f:X\to\mathbb{R}
, viewing
X
as a real manifold of dimension
2r
, we may define a
C^{k}
-norm
c_{f,k}
as the sum of its order
\leq k
derivatives in the polydisc
\mathbb{D}_{2}\subseteq U_{\alpha}
. We will also assume that
c_{f,k}\geq 1
by adding
1
to this sum. In other words, if
\varphi_{\alpha}:U_{\alpha}\to\mathbb{D}_{3}
is our biholomorphism, then we have

c_{f,k}=1+\ \sum_{\alpha\in I}\sum_{i_{1}+\cdots+i_{2r}\leq k}\left(\sup_{|\varphi_{\alpha}(z)|\leq 2}\left|\frac{\partial^{i_{1}+\cdots i_{2r}}(f\circ\varphi_{\alpha}^{-1})}{\partial x_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots\partial y_{r}^{i_{2r}}}(z)\right|\right).

Similarly, given a
C^{k}
differential form
\omega
, we may define the constants
c_{\omega,k}
. We now prove some basic propositions that show these constants are comparable independent of the choice of good charts.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\cal{U}_{I}
and
\cal{V}_{J}
be two different choice of good charts for
X
. Then for any
k\geq 0
, there exists
C=C(k,\cal{U}_{I},\cal{V}_{J})
, depending on
k
and the choice of our good charts, such that for any
C^{k}
function
f:X\to\mathbb{R}
, if
c_{f,k}
is the norm defined via
\cal{U}_{I}
and
c^{\prime}_{f,k}
is defined via
\cal{V}_{J}
, then

c_{f,k}\leq Cc^{\prime}_{f,k}.

Proof.

Let
U_{i}
be a chart of
\cal{U}_{I}
. Then it is isomorphic to
\mathbb{D}_{3}
, Given any
x\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}_{2}
, there is a corresponding chart
V_{j}\simeq\mathbb{D}_{3}
such that
x
lies inside
\mathbb{D}_{1}
of
V_{j}
. We may then find an open set around
x
, contained inside a compact subset of
U_{i}\cap V_{j}
and also contained inside
\mathbb{D}_{2}
of
V_{j}
. Then the
k^{th}
derivatives of our transition function between
U_{i}
and
V_{j}
is bounded on this open set.

Now since
\overline{\mathbb{D}}_{2}
is compact, we may cover it with finitely many such open sets. This then implies that we may upper bound the
k^{th}
derivative of
f
on
\mathbb{D}_{2}
of
U_{i}
by some constant multiplied by
c^{\prime}_{f,k}
as desired. We then do this for each
U_{i}
of our chart
\overline{U}_{I}
to obtain that
c_{f,k}\leq Cc^{\prime}_{f,k}
for some
C
depending on
k,\cal{U}_{I}
and
\cal{V}_{J}
.
∎

It is also useful to understand how the norms change when restricting from a manifold
X
to a submanifold
Y
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
X
be a complex manifold and
Y\subseteq X
be a complex submanifold. Let
\cal{U}_{I}
be a choice of good charts for
X
and
\cal{V}_{J}
be a choice of good charts for
Y
. Then for any
k\geq 0
, there exists
C=C(k,\cal{U}_{I},\cal{V}_{J})
, depending on
k
and the choice of our good charts, such that for any
C^{k}
function
f:X\to\mathbb{R}
, if
c_{f,k}
is the norm defined via
\cal{U}_{I}
and
c^{\prime}_{f,k}
is the norm of
f\mid_{Y}:Y\to\mathbb{R}
defined via
\cal{V}_{J}
, then

c^{\prime}_{f,k}\leq Cc_{f,k}.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.1
, it suffices to prove the existence of
C
for a single choice of
\cal{U}_{I}
and
\cal{V}_{J}
. We first choose
\cal{V}_{J}
such that for each
V_{j}\in\cal{V}_{J}
, the submanifold
Y\cap V_{j}
is given by
\{z_{1}=z_{2}=\cdots=z_{k}=0\}
. To do so, for each
x\in X
we may find a local chart
V_{x}
such that
Y\cap V_{x}
is given by
\{z_{1}=\cdots=z_{k}=0\}
. We then choose a polydisc
\mathbb{D}_{r}
centered at
x
that also lies inside
V_{x}
, and we let
V^{\prime}_{x}
be the polydisc
\mathbb{D}_{r/3}
of one third radius. Then
\{V^{\prime}_{x}\}_{x\in X}
form an open cover of
X
and by compactness we may choose a finite open cover
V^{\prime}_{x_{1}},\ldots,V^{\prime}_{x_{n}}
. We may then use
V_{x_{1}},\ldots,V_{x_{n}}
as a choice of good charts.

Given such a choice of good charts
\cal{V}_{J}
, we then set
U_{j}=Y\cap V_{j}
and clearly
U_{j}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{D}_{3}
of the correct dimension for
Y
. Then using
\cal{U}_{J}
and
\cal{V}_{J}
, it is clear that we have
c_{f,k}\leq c^{\prime}_{f,k}
as desired.
∎

Lemmas
2.1
and
2.2
similarly hold for
c_{\omega,k}
. Let
L
be a line bundle on
X
equipped with a hermitian metric
e^{h}
. Since each
U_{\alpha}
is biholomorphic to a polydisc, it follows that
L
can be trivialized over each
U_{\alpha}
. We then fix a choice of non-vanishing local sections
t_{\alpha}
for each
U_{\alpha}
, giving us the isomorphism
L|_{U_{\alpha}}\simeq U_{\alpha}\times\mathbb{C}
. Then we may find functions
h_{\alpha}
such that
e^{h_{\alpha}}
represents our hermitian metric. We may then similarly define the constants
c_{h,k}
if our metric
h
is
C^{k}
and Lemmas
2.1
and
2.2
also hold.

If
h
is a continuous metric but not differentiable, we may still make sense of
dd^{c}h
in the sense of distributions. In our setting, it will be the case that
dd^{c}h
is a positive current, i.e. that
dd^{c}h\geq 0
. It will be necessary for us to approximate
h
by a sequence of smooth metrics
h_{\varepsilon}
, such that
dd^{c}h_{\varepsilon}
is almost positive. For a general compact manifold
X
, this has been done in a quantitative manner by Dinh–Ma–Nguyen
[
DMN17
, Theorem 2.1]
with a rather involved argument. As we only have to deal with the case of
X=\mathbb{P}^{r}
due to our endomorphisms being polarized, we follow a simpler idea of Dinh–Sibony
[
DS10a
]
to use the action of
\operatorname{PGL}_{r+1}(\mathbb{C})
on
\mathbb{P}^{r}
to regularize our metric
h
.

We first explain how to regularize a continuous function
f:\mathbb{P}^{r}\to\mathbb{R}
. The group
\operatorname{PGL}_{r+1}(\mathbb{C})
is a complex Lie group that acts transitively on
\mathbb{P}^{r}(\mathbb{C})
. We fix a holomorphic chart near the identity element
\operatorname{id}
of
\operatorname{PGL}_{r+1}(\mathbb{C})
isomorphic to the polydisc
\mathbb{D}_{2}
and let
y
be the holomorphic coordinate such that
y=0
at
\operatorname{id}
. We then let
\rho
be a smooth probability measure with compact support in
|y|<1
. We will assume that
\rho
is radial (depends only on
|y|
) and that the involution
\tau\mapsto\tau^{-1}
preserves the norm of
y
.

Following Dinh–Sibony, for
\theta\in\mathbb{C}
we define
h_{\theta}(y)=\theta y
to be multiplication by
\theta
. For
|\theta|\leq 1
, we define
\rho_{\theta}=(h_{\theta})_{*}\rho
. This is smooth probability measure supported on
\{|y|\leq\theta\}
which converges to the Dirac mass on
\operatorname{id}
as
\theta\to 0
. We now define our regularization
f_{\theta}
as

f_{\theta}(x)=\int_{\operatorname{PGL}_{r+1}(\mathbb{C})}(f\circ\tau_{y})(x)d\rho_{\theta}(y)

where
\tau_{y}
is the automorphism corresponding to the coordinate
y
. By Dinh–Sibony, we have the following estimate.

Proposition 2.3
.

Fix a positive integer
k
. Then there exists an uniform constant
c_{k}>0
, depending only on
k
and
r
, such that

c_{f_{\theta},k}\leq c_{k}c_{f,0}|\theta|^{-2r^{2}-4r-k}.

Proof.

This is Proposition 2.1.6 of Dinh–Sibony
[
DS10a
]
.
∎

Fix the Fubini-Study metric on
\mathbb{P}^{r}(\mathbb{C})
. We now further assume that
f(x)
is Holder continuous with respect to our metric. Then we also have the following control on
|f_{\theta}-f|
as
\theta\to 0
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Assume there exists
C,\kappa>0
such that
|f(x)-f(y)|\leq C|x-y|^{\kappa}
for all
x,y\in\mathbb{P}^{r}(\mathbb{C})
. Then there exists some
c>0
such that for all
|\theta|>0
, we have

|f_{\theta}(x)-f(x)|\leq c|\theta|^{\kappa}

Proof.

We first claim that for
|y|
sufficiently small, the automoprhism
\tau_{y}
has Lipschitz constant
O(|y|)
with respect to our metric. Indeed if we let
\varepsilon=|y|
, then our automorphism
\tau_{y}
has entries
1+O(\varepsilon)
on the diagonal and
O(\varepsilon)
everywhere else. Then if
[z_{0}:\cdots:z_{r}]
is our point, which we may WLOG assume that
|z_{r}|
is maximal, then the image under
\tau_{y}
is of the form
[z_{0}+O(\varepsilon|z_{r}|):\cdots:z_{r}+O(\varepsilon|z_{r}|)]
. Passing into the chart
\{z_{r}=1\}
then gives us a point which is
O(\varepsilon)
distance away from our original point in our metric.

Now by definition, we have

f_{\theta}(x)=\int_{\operatorname{PGL}_{n+1}(\mathbb{C})}f(\tau_{y}(x))d\rho_{\theta}(y).

The measure
\rho_{\theta}(y)
is supported on
|y|<\theta
and for such
y
, we have
|\tau_{y}(x)-x|<O(\theta)
. Using Holder continuity, we get that
|f(\tau_{y}(x))-f(x)|<O(\theta^{\kappa})
and so we obtain

\left|f_{\theta}(x)-f(x)\right|\leq O(\theta^{\kappa})

as desired.
∎

We now also show that if
i\partial\overline{\partial}f
is not too negative, then so is
i\partial\overline{\partial}f_{\theta}
. Let
\omega
be some smooth positive
(1,1)
-form on
\mathbb{P}^{r}
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Assume that
\partial\overline{\partial}f\geq-\omega
. Then for all
\theta>0
sufficiently small, there exists some constant
c>0
, depending on
\omega
, such that

\partial\overline{\partial}f_{\theta}\geq-(1+c\theta)\omega.

Proof.

We have

\partial\overline{\partial}f_{\theta}=\int_{\operatorname{PGL}_{r+1}(\mathbb{C})}\partial\overline{\partial}(f\circ\tau_{y})d\rho_{\theta}(y)

and so it suffices to show that
\partial\overline{\partial}(f\circ\tau_{y})\geq-(1+O(\theta))\omega
for any
y
satisfying
|y|<\theta
. Fix any strongly positive
(r-1,r-1)
-form
\eta
. Let
\tau_{y^{\prime}}
denote the inverse of
\tau_{y}
. We have

\langle\partial\overline{\partial}(f\circ\tau_{y}),\eta\rangle=\langle f\circ\tau_{y},\overline{\partial}\partial\eta\rangle=\langle(\tau_{y^{\prime}})_{*}f,\overline{\partial}\partial\eta\rangle=\langle f,(\tau_{y^{\prime}})^{*}\overline{\partial}\partial\eta\rangle=\langle f,\overline{\partial}\partial((\tau_{y^{\prime}}^{*})\eta)\rangle

=\langle\partial\overline{\partial}f,(\tau_{y^{\prime}})^{*}\eta\rangle\geq\langle-\omega,(\tau_{y^{\prime}})^{*}\eta\rangle=\langle(\tau_{y^{\prime}})_{*}(-\omega),\eta\rangle,

where we used the fact that
f\circ\tau_{y}
is the pushforward of
f
under the inverse map
\tau_{y^{\prime}}
and that the pullback of a strongly positive form under
\tau_{y^{\prime}}
is still strongly positive as
\tau_{y^{\prime}}
is a holomorphic map.

It now suffices to show that

(\tau_{y^{\prime}})_{*}(-\omega)+(1+O(\theta))\omega

gives a positive definite matrix at each point
x
. Let
\theta=\varepsilon
. Then in a local chart, if

\omega(z)=\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq n}c_{i,j}(z)dz_{i}\wedge d\overline{z}_{j},

we have

(\tau_{y^{\prime}})_{*}(\omega)(z)=\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq n}c_{i,j}(\tau_{y}(z))dz_{i}\wedge d\overline{z}_{j}.

Then
|\tau_{y}(z)-z|=O(\varepsilon)
, and so
|c_{i,j}(z)-c_{i,j}(\tau_{y}(z))|<O(\varepsilon)
too. If
A(z)
is the matrix formed by
(c_{i,j}(z))
, then we know that
|A(\tau_{y}(z))-A(z)|
is a matrix with entries bounded by
O(\varepsilon)
. Since
A(z)
is positive definite, we can find a bound
\lambda
for the smallest eigenvalue. Then
A(\tau_{y}(z))-A(z)\geq-O(\lambda^{-1}\varepsilon)A(z)
and so locally, we have
(\tau_{y^{\prime}})_{*}\omega\geq-(1+O(\theta))\omega
. We may then make the constants uniform by taking a finite cover
\{U_{i}\}
with each
U_{i}
living inside a larger chart
V_{i}
where
U_{i}\simeq\mathbb{D}_{1}
and
V_{i}\simeq\mathbb{D}_{3}
.
∎

Let
X=\mathbb{P}^{r}
and
L=O(1)
. We may then give
L
the Fubini-Study metric
h_{\operatorname{FS}}
so that
i\partial\overline{\partial}h_{\operatorname{FS}}=\omega_{\operatorname{FS}}
is a positive
(1,1)
-form. Now if
h
is our continuous metric on
L
, then
f=h-h_{\operatorname{FS}}
is a globally defined continuous function such that
i\partial\overline{\partial}f=dd^{c}h-\omega_{\operatorname{FS}}
. We may then apply our regularization process to
f
to obtain
f_{\theta}
, and set
h_{\theta}=f_{\theta}+h_{\operatorname{FS}}
to be our new hermitian metric. If
h
is semipositive, so that
i\partial\overline{\partial}f\geq-\omega_{\operatorname{FS}}
, by Lemma
2.5
we know that

i\partial\overline{\partial}f_{\theta}\geq-(1+O(\theta))\omega_{\operatorname{FS}}\implies i\partial\overline{\partial}h_{\theta}\geq-O(\theta)\omega_{\operatorname{FS}}

and so our regularized metric is still nearly semipositive.

2.2.
Normed modules and volumes

We will mainly refer to
[
Yua08
, 2.3]
and
[
Mor14
, Chapter 3]
. Let
M
be a free
\mathbb{Z}
-module. If
|\cdot|
is a norm on
M\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}=M_{\mathbb{R}}
, we call
(M,|\cdot|)
a normed
\mathbb{Z}
-module. Let
B_{M}=\{x\in M_{\mathbb{R}}\mid|x|\leq 1\}
. After fixing a choice of a Haar measure on
M_{\mathbb{R}}
, we may define the Euler characteristic
\chi(M,|\cdot|)
by the formula

\chi(M,|\cdot|)=\log\operatorname{vol}(B_{M})-\log\operatorname{vol}(M)

where
\operatorname{vol}(M)
denotes the volume of a fundamental domain of
M
. This is independent of the choice of our Haar measure. The following proposition follows from Minkowski’s theorem.

Lemma 2.6
.

Assume that
\chi(M,|\cdot|)>\dim M_{\mathbb{R}}+\log 2
. Then the intersection
B_{M}\cap M
is non-empty.

Proof.

This is just Minkowski’s theorem.
∎

Now let
|\cdot|_{1}
and
|\cdot|_{2}
be two different norms on
M_{\mathbb{R}}
. We would like to calculate the difference between
\chi(M,|\cdot|_{1})
and
\chi(M,|\cdot|_{2})
. When both our norms are given by inner products
\langle\,,\,\rangle_{1}
and
\langle\,,\,\rangle_{2}
, the next proposition gives a way to calculate this difference.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
|\cdot|_{1}
and
|\cdot|_{2}
be two different norms on
M_{\mathbb{R}}
that are given by inner products
\langle\,,\,\rangle_{1}
and
\langle\,,\,\rangle_{2}
. Pick an orthonormal basis
e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}
for
\langle,\rangle_{1}
that is orthogonal for
\langle,\rangle_{2}
. Then

\chi(M,|\cdot|_{1})-\chi(M,|\cdot|_{2})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\log|e_{i}|_{2}.

Proof.

By definition, it suffices to calculate the difference of volumes between the unit ball for
|\cdot|_{2}
and
|\cdot|_{1}
. Fix a Haar measure such that the volume of
\{\lambda_{1}e_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{n}e_{n}\mid 0\leq\lambda_{i}\leq 1\}
is
1
. Then the volume of the unit ball for
|\cdot|_{1}
is exactly the volume of the
n
-sphere.

For
|\cdot|_{2}
, the unit ball is given by

\{(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})\mid\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}^{2}|e_{i}|_{2}^{2}\leq 1\}

as a subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The volume of this is
\prod_{i=1}^{n}|e_{i}|^{-1}_{2}
multiplied by the volume of the n-sphere. Hence taking
\log
’s and the difference, we obtain

\chi(M,|\cdot|_{1})-\chi(M,|\cdot|_{2})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\log|e_{i}|_{2}

as desired.
∎

We will be using Proposition
2.7
repeatedly to compute difference of volumes in later sections. An immediate consequence of Proposition
2.7
is that if
|\cdot|_{2}=\alpha|\cdot|_{1}
for some
\alpha>0
, then

\chi(M,|\cdot|_{1})-\chi(M,|\cdot|_{2})=\log\alpha\dim M_{\mathbb{R}}.

The situation where our normed modules arise comes from looking at a lattice inside the space of global sections
H^{0}(X,L)
for a projective variety
X
over a number field
K
. For each archimedean place
v
of
K
, we have an embedding
v:K\xhookrightarrow{}\mathbb{C}
which gives an inclusion
H^{0}(X,L)\xhookrightarrow{}H^{0}(X(\mathbb{C}_{v}),L)
. We may combine them to get an isomorphism

H^{0}(X,L)\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{C}\simeq\bigoplus_{v\text{ arch. }}H^{0}(X(\mathbb{C}_{v}),L)

where we count conjugate archimedean places
v
separately.

Now if each
H^{0}(X(\mathbb{C}_{v}),L)
has an inner product
\langle\,,\,\rangle_{v}
, we may define an inner product
\langle\,,\,\rangle
on
H^{0}(X,L)\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{C}
by declaring each
H^{0}(X(\mathbb{C}_{v}),L)
to be orthogonal to each other. If each
\langle\,,\,\rangle_{v}
is positive definite, then this inner product
\langle\,,\,\rangle
is also positive definite by
[
Mor14
, Proposition 3.9]
. Furthermore, if one knew that for conjugate archimedean places
v,v^{\prime}
, we have

\langle x,y\rangle_{v}=\overline{\langle x,y\rangle_{v^{\prime}}}

for
x,y\in H^{0}(X,L)
, then one can check that we get an induced inner product on
H^{0}(X,L)\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{R}
.

To get the inner products
\langle\,,\,\rangle_{v}
, we will choose a positive measure
\mu_{v}
on
X(\mathbb{C}_{v})
for each archimedean place
v
, along with a hermitian metric on
L
. Then the formula

\langle s,s^{\prime}\rangle_{v}=\int_{X(\mathbb{C}_{v})}\langle s,s^{\prime}\rangle_{x}d\mu_{v}(x)

where
\langle\,,\,\rangle_{x}
is the inner product on the fibers coming from our hermitian metric produces an inner product. We will call this a
L^{2}
-norm due to integrating over a measure.

The other situation where a norm appears is the supremum norm. For each archimedean place
v
, we have a metrized line bundle
\overline{L}
. For a global section
s
, we set
|s|_{v}=\sup_{x\in X(\mathbb{C}_{v})}|s(x)|_{x}
where
|\cdot|_{x}
is again the fiberwise norm. This gives a norm on each
H^{0}(X(\mathbb{C}_{v}),L)
and taking the maximum over each archimedean place
v
gives a supremum norm on
H^{0}(X,L)\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{C}
.

3.
Gromov’s Norm Comparison Theorem

Let
X
be a compact complex manifold of dimension
r
and let
\cal{U}_{I}=\{U_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in I}
be a choice of good charts for
X
. Let
L
be a metrized line bundle on
X
and fix a choice of non-vanishing sections
t_{\alpha}
on each
U_{\alpha}
for
L
. For the line bundle
L^{n}
, we will use
t_{\alpha}^{n}
to trivialize it. As a reminder, a choice of good charts means that each
U_{\alpha}
is biholomorphic to the open polydisc of radius
3
and every point
x\in X
lies inside the unit polydisc for one of these charts.

In this short section, we will prove a quantitative version of Gromov’s comparison between the
L^{2}
-norm and the sup norm of a given section
s\in H^{0}(X,L^{n})
, as
n
gets large. Given a section
s
, if
s_{\alpha}
is the holomorphic function representing
s
on
U_{\alpha}
under our trivialization, we know that
|s(z)|
is given by the formula

|s(z)|^{2}=e^{h_{\alpha}}(z)s_{\alpha}(z)\overline{s}_{\alpha}(z)

for all
x\in U_{\alpha}
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\mu
be a positive volume form on
X
with total volume
1
. Let
L
be a line bundle and
\overline{L}
be a metrization with metric
h
. Let
c
be a constant such that
d\mu>cdV
when restricted to
\mathbb{D}_{2}
for each of the charts
z_{\alpha}(U_{\alpha})
, where
dV
denotes the Lebesgue measure and let
1\geq c_{2}>0
be a constant such that
e^{h_{\alpha}(x)}\geq c_{2}
for all
x\in U_{\alpha}
such that
|z_{\alpha}(x)|\leq 2
. Then for all
n\geq 1
, we have the inequality

||s||_{\sup}\leq O\left(\left(\frac{c_{e^{h},1}}{c_{2}}\right)^{2r}\frac{n^{r}}{c}||s||_{L^{2}}\right)\text{ for all }s\in H^{0}(X,L^{n})

where we take the
L^{2}
-norm with respect to
\mu
.

Proof.

The proof is mainly the same as Proposition 2.13 of
[
Yua08
]
. Note that
h_{\alpha}
in Yuan’s Proposition is our
e^{h_{\alpha}}
. We reproduce a sketch of the argument here. If
h_{\alpha}
is the function induced by
h
on
U_{\alpha}
, then for
x\in U_{\alpha}
, one has

|s(z)|^{2}=e^{nh_{\alpha}(z)}s_{\alpha}(z)\overline{s}_{\alpha}(z)\text{ for all }s\in H^{0}(X,L^{n}).

Identifying
U_{\alpha}
with
\mathbb{D}_{3}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{r}\mid|z|<3\}
. the constant
c_{e^{h},1}
serves as a Lipschitz constant for
e^{h_{\alpha}}
in the disc
\mathbb{D}_{2}=\{|z|\leq 2\}
and so
|e^{h_{\alpha}(z_{1})}-e^{h_{\alpha}(z_{0})}|\leq c_{e^{h},1}|z_{1}-z_{0}|
for
z_{1},z_{0}\in\mathbb{D}_{2}
. Hence

e^{h_{\alpha}(z_{1})}\geq e^{h_{\alpha}(z_{0})}-c_{e^{h},1}|z_{1}-z_{0}|\geq e^{h_{\alpha}(z_{0})}\left(1-\frac{c_{e^{h},1}}{c_{2}}|z_{1}-z_{0}|\right).

This implies that in Yuan’s argument, we may take
c_{1}=2\max\{1,\frac{c_{e^{h},1}}{c_{2}}\}
. The constant
c^{\prime\prime}
in Yuan’s argument may also be taken as
c
multiplied by some constant depending only on the dimension
r
. The rest of Yuan’s argument then gives us the inequality

||s||^{2}_{L^{2}}\geq c^{\prime\prime}c_{1}^{-2r}\left(\frac{||s||^{2}_{\sup}}{(2r+n)\binom{2r+k-1}{2r-1}}\right)

which gives us what we want.
∎

This gives us the following comparison of volumes between the
L^{2}
-norm and the sup norm. Given a positive measure
\mu
, we may use it to define an inner product on the sections of
H^{0}(X,L^{n})
by
\langle s,s^{\prime}\rangle=\int_{X}s(z)\overline{s^{\prime}}(z)d\mu
, which we will refer to as the
L^{2}
-norm.

Corollary 3.2
.

Keep the same notation as in Proposition
3.1
. If
V_{n,L^{2}}
denotes the unit ball in
H^{0}(X,L^{n})
for the
L^{2}
-norm, and
V_{n,\sup}
denotes the unit ball under the sup norm, we have the inequality

0\leq\log\operatorname{vol}(V_{n,L^{2}})-\log\operatorname{vol}(V_{n,\sup})\leq O\left(\log\frac{c_{e^{h},1}n}{c_{2}c^{\prime}}\right)\dim H^{0}(X,L^{n}).

Proof.

We certainly have
V_{n,\sup}\subseteq V_{n,L^{2}}
because
\mu
has volume
1
. If we let
c
denote a constant such that
||s||_{\sup}\leq c||s||_{L^{2}}
, then we have

V_{n,L^{2}}\subseteq cV_{n,\sup}\implies\log V_{n,L^{2}}-\log V_{n,\sup}\leq(\log c)\dim H^{0}(X,L^{n}).

Applying Proposition
3.1
to obtain a constant
c
, the inequality then follows.
∎

4.
Quantitative Upper Bounds on Bergman Kernels

Again, let
X
be a compact complex manifold of dimension
r
and let
\cal{U}_{I}=\{U_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in I}
be a choice of good charts for
X
. Let
L
be a metrized line bundle on
X
and fix a choice of non-vanishing sections
t_{\alpha}
on each
U_{\alpha}
for
L
. For the line bundle
L^{n}
, we will use
t_{\alpha}^{n}
to trivialize it. As a reminder, a choice of good charts means that each
U_{\alpha}
is biholomorphic to the open polydisc of radius
3
and every point
x\in X
lies inside the unit polydisc for one of these charts.

Given a positive line bundle
\overline{L}
with hermitian metric
e^{h}
, we let
\omega=i\partial\overline{\partial}h
be the associated positive
(1,1)
-form and
\mu=\omega^{\wedge r}/r!
be its volume form. We can then endow
H^{0}(X,L^{n})
with a
L^{2}
-norm by

(4)

\langle s,s^{\prime}\rangle=\int_{X(\mathbb{C})}\langle s,s^{\prime}\rangle_{x}d\mu(x)

where
\langle\,,\,\rangle_{x}
is the fiberwise inner product. It is then known, dating back to Tian
[
Tia90
]
, that if
s_{1},\ldots,s_{N}
is an orthonormal basis of sections for
H^{0}(X,L^{n})
, we have

(5)

\sum_{i=1}^{N}|s_{i}(x)|^{2}\sim\left(\frac{n}{2\pi}\right)^{r}

uniformly for
x\in X(\mathbb{C})
as
n\to\infty
. The function
\sum_{i=1}^{N}|s_{i}(x)|^{2}
is independent of the choice of orthonormal basis and is called the Bergman kernel. In this section, we will prove a quantitative version of (
5
) along with a quantitative version of Gromov’s norm comparison inequality. Using the approach of Yuan
[
Yua08
]
will then allow us to deduce a lower bound on the difference in volumes of the sup unit ball of
H^{0}(X,L^{n})
when the metric of
L
is perturbed.

We will follow
[
Cha15
]
to obtain our quantitative version of (
5
), where the ideas date back to Berndtsson
[
Ber03
]
. Dinh–Ma–Nguyen
[
DMN17
]
also prove a quantitative version of the asymptotic expansion of Bergman kernels, but is of a different flavor from what we need. Throughout the section, we will assume that our complex manifold
X
and the line bundle
L
is fixed. We will be interested in how our constants depend on the metric of
L
, especially on its positivity.

Given
x\in X(\mathbb{C})
a metrized line bundle
\overline{L}
with associated
(1,1)
-form
\omega
, we want to pass to normal coordinates around
x
for our Kahler form
\omega
. We first control the error in this normal coordinates. We assume that
\omega
is positive, so that there exists a constant
1\geq\lambda_{\omega}>0
such that in each of the charts
U_{\alpha}\simeq\mathbb{D}_{3}
, the eigenvalues of
\omega(z)
is
\geq\lambda_{\omega}
with respect to
\sum_{i=1}^{r}dz^{i}\wedge d\overline{z}^{i}
for
z\in\mathbb{D}_{2}
.

Proposition 4.1
.

For each
x\in X(\mathbb{C})
, there exists a chart
\varphi_{x}:V_{x}\to\mathbb{C}^{r}
such that
x
maps to the origin, the image
\varphi_{x}(V_{x})
contains a disc of radius
O(c_{\omega,0})^{-1}
such that our
(1,1)
-form
\omega
is of the form

\frac{i}{2}\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq r}g_{i,j}(z)dz^{i}\wedge d\overline{z}^{j}

where
g_{i,j}(0)=\delta_{i,j}
and the order
k
derivatives for
g_{i,j}(z)
are bounded by
O(\lambda_{\omega}^{-1})c_{\omega,k}
.

Proof.

Given
x
, we can find a
U_{\alpha}\simeq\mathbb{D}_{3}
such that
x
is contained in
\mathbb{D}_{1}
. Thus there is an unit disc around
x
that is contained in
\mathbb{D}_{2}
. Re-centering
x
to be
0
, We write

\omega=\frac{i}{2}\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq r}f_{i,j}(z)dz^{i}\wedge d\overline{z}^{j}.

Then we know that
\{f_{i,j}(0)\}_{i,j}
forms a positive definite hermitian matrix. Thus if we let
A
denote our hermitian matrix, there exists some unitary
P
such that
PA\overline{P}^{T}=D
where
D
is the diagonal matrix of eigenvalues for
\omega
at
0
. We then scale the
i
th column of
P
by
\sqrt{\lambda_{i}^{-1}}
where
\lambda_{i}
is the
i
th eigenvalue to get an orthogonal
Q
such that
QA\overline{Q}^{T}=I
. Then the entries of
Q
are upper bounded by
O(\lambda_{\omega}^{-1/2})
and so in our new basis, we get

\omega=\frac{i}{2}\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq r}g_{i,j}(z)dz^{i}\wedge d\overline{z}^{j}

with
g_{i,j}(0)=\delta_{i,j}
and where the derivatives of
g_{i,j}
are bounded by that of
f_{i,j}
multiplied by
r\lambda_{\omega}^{-1/2}
. Since
\overline{P}^{T}
is unitary, it maps
\mathbb{D}_{1}
into
\mathbb{D}_{r}
and so the image of its inverse,
P
, when applied to
\mathbb{D}_{1}
will contain
\frac{1}{r}\mathbb{D}_{1}
. Then the image of
\overline{Q}^{T}(\mathbb{D}_{1})
contains
\frac{1}{r\sqrt{\lambda_{1}}}\mathbb{D}_{1}
. We may bound
\lambda_{1}
by the trace of our matrix as our matrix is positive definite, which is
O(c_{\omega,0})
as desired.
∎

This allows us to prove an upper bound on the Bergman kernel, uniform over
x\in X
, where the dependence on how large
n
has to be on the metric is explicit.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
\overline{L}
be a positive line bundle with
(1,1)
-form
\omega=i\partial\overline{\partial}h
and let
s_{1},\ldots,s_{N}
be an orthonormal basis for
L^{n}
under the volume form
\mu=(\omega)^{\wedge r}/r!
. Then for all
n\geq O(c_{\omega,1}\lambda_{\omega}^{-1}c_{h,3})^{8}
, we have

\sum_{i=1}^{N}|s(x)|^{2}\leq\left(\frac{n}{2\pi}\right)^{r}\left(1+\frac{1}{n^{1/8}}\right)

for all
x\in X
.

Proof.

First, it is well known, see
[
Cha15
, Lemma 3.1]
that

\sum_{i=1}^{N}|s_{i}(x)|^{2}=\sup_{s\in H^{0}(X,L^{n})}\frac{|s(x)|}{|s|_{L^{2}}}

where the supremum is taken over all non-zero sections
s
. By Proposition
4.1
, we can find a local chart of radius
O(c_{\omega,0})^{-1}
for which

\omega(z)=\frac{i}{2}\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq r}\left(f_{i,j}(z)\right)dz^{i}\wedge d\overline{z}^{j}

where
f_{i,j}(0)=\delta_{i,j}
and we have a bound on the derivatives of
f_{i,j}(z)
. Now given a local non-vanishing section
s
in the chart, we get that
-2idd^{c}\log|s|=\omega
. Representing
s
by a holomorphic function
f
, we get
-2idd^{c}(\log|f|+h)=\omega
where
h
is our hermitian metric. We may multiply
f
by any local non-vanishing holomorphic function and thus we may add or subtract any harmonic function from
h
. Since we have

dd^{c}(2h+\sum_{i=1}^{r}|z_{i}|^{2})=\frac{i}{2}\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq r}(-f_{i,j}(z)+\delta_{i,j})dz^{i}\wedge d\overline{z}^{j},

with
f_{i,j}(z)-\delta_{i,j}
vanishing at
0
, we may modify
h
by a polynomial of degree
\leq 2
and coefficients
\leq c_{\omega,2}O(\lambda_{\omega}^{-r})
in size such that
h+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{r}|z_{i}|^{2}
vanishes at
0
to order
2
. To bound the third derivative of
h
in this chart, we observe that the coordinates was transformed by a linear transformation with coefficients
\leq O(\lambda_{\omega}^{-1})
. This changes the derivative of
h
by at most a factor of
O(\lambda_{\omega}^{-1})
and so the third derivative of
h-\sum_{i=1}^{r}|z_{i}|^{2}
may be bounded by
O(\lambda_{\omega}^{-1})c_{h,3}
. Thus we get

h=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{r}|z_{i}|^{2}+O(\lambda_{\omega}^{-1}c_{h,3})|z|^{3}.

Now let
\varphi(z)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{r}|z_{i}|^{2}
and let
c_{n}
be some constant depending on
n
. Given any section
s
represented by a holomorphic function
f
in our local chart, we have

|s(x)|^{2}=|f(0)|^{2}\leq\frac{\int_{|z|<\frac{c_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}}|f(z)|^{2}e^{-n\varphi(z)}dV}{\int_{|z|<\frac{c_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}}e^{-n\varphi(z)}dV}

where
dV
is the usual Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{C}^{r}
. The inequality follows from the fact that
|f(z)|^{2}
is plurisubharmonic and that
\varphi(z)
is constant when each
|z_{i}|
is constant. Now if
\mu
denotes the volume form induced by
\omega
in our local chart, we see that if
dV
denotes the Lebesgue measure, then locally

(6)

\mu(z)=(1+O(\lambda_{\omega}^{-1}c_{\omega,1})|z|))dV,

as the the first derivative of the coefficients of
\omega
in our chart is bounded by
O(\lambda_{\omega}^{-1}c_{\omega,1})
.
We also have

e^{-n\varphi(z)}\leq e^{nh}\cdot e^{nO(\lambda_{\omega}^{-1}c_{h,3})|z|^{3}}

Taking
c_{n}=n^{1/10}
, we get
c_{n}/\sqrt{n}=n^{-2/5}
and so for
|z|\leq\frac{c_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}
, we have
n|z|^{3}\leq n^{-1/5}
which ensures that
e^{nO(\lambda_{\omega}^{-1}c_{h,3}|z|^{3}}=O(\frac{1}{n})
. Hence

(7)

\int_{|z|<\frac{c_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}}|f(z)|^{2}e^{-n\varphi(z)}dV\leq(1+O(\lambda_{\omega}^{-1}c_{\omega,1})n^{-2/5})|s|_{L^{2}}

since
n\geq O(\lambda_{\omega}^{-1}c_{h,3})^{8}
. On the other hand, we have

\int_{|z|<\frac{c_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}}e^{-n\varphi(z)}dV=\frac{1}{n^{r}}\int_{|z|<n^{1/10}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{r}|z_{i}|^{2}}=\frac{1}{n^{r}}\left(\int_{|z|\in\mathbb{C}^{r}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{r}|z_{i}|^{2}}-\int_{|z|>n^{1/10}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{r}|z_{i}|^{2}}\right)

=\frac{1}{n^{r}}\left(\left(2\pi\right)^{r}+O(\frac{1}{n})\right)

Hence we obtain

|s(x)|^{2}\leq\left(\frac{n}{2\pi}\right)^{r}\left(1+O(\lambda_{\omega}^{-1}c_{\omega,1})n^{-2/5}\right)|s|^{2}_{L^{2}}\leq\left(\frac{n}{2\pi}\right)^{r}\left(1+\frac{1}{n^{1/8}}\right)|s|^{2}_{L^{2}}

since
n\geq O(\lambda_{\omega}^{-1}c_{\omega,1})^{8}
, as desired.
∎

5.
Global Bound on Differences of Volumes

Let
X
be a projective variety over a number field
K
and let
\varphi:X\to X
be a polarizable endomorphism. By
[
Fak14
]
, after replacing
L
with a high enough power, we may assume that
L
embeds
X
into
\mathbb{P}^{N}
with
L
being the restriction of
O(1)
, and that
\varphi
extends to a morphism
\tilde{\varphi}:\mathbb{P}^{N}\to\mathbb{P}^{N}
of degree
d
. We may then lift
\tilde{\varphi}
to a homogeneous map
F:\mathbb{A}^{N+1}\to\mathbb{A}^{N+1}
.

For each place
v
of
M_{K}
, we may define a homogeneous Green’s function using
F
by

G_{v}(x)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log||F^{n}(x)||_{v}}{d^{n}}\text{ for }x\in\mathbb{A}^{N+1}(\mathbb{C}_{v}),

where
||(x_{0},\ldots,x_{n+1})||_{v}=\max\{|x_{0}|_{v},\ldots,|x_{n+1}|_{v}\}
. We can then use
G_{v}
to define a metric on
L
for each place
v
by

\log|s(x)|_{v}=\log|P(\tilde{x})|_{v}-G_{v}(\tilde{x})\text{ for }x\in\mathbb{P}^{N}(\mathbb{C}_{v}),

where
\tilde{x}
is any lift of
x
to
\mathbb{A}^{N+1}(\mathbb{C}_{v})
. Due to the homogeneity of
G_{v}
, our definition is independent of the lift. Restricting to
X
, this turns
L
into a semipositive adelic line bundle
\overline{L}
in the sense of Zhang
[
Yua08
, Section 3.5]
, although it would not be important for us to know this. It is easy to see that its associated height on
X(\overline{K})
is the canonical height
\widehat{h}_{\varphi}(x)
, which may also be defined as

\widehat{h}_{\varphi}(x)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{h(\varphi^{n}(x))}{d^{n}}.

where
h(x)
is the standard Weil height on
\mathbb{P}^{N}
. Let
L_{n}=L^{d^{n}}
. Then there is an induced adelic meric on
L_{n}
coming from
L
and we will denote the adelic line bundle by
\overline{L}_{n}
and the metrized line bundle on
X(\mathbb{C}_{v})
by
\overline{L}_{n,v}
.

The global sections of
L_{n}
, denoted by
H^{0}(X,L_{n})
, form a
K
-vector space. We let
M_{n}
be the sub
O_{K}
-module of sections
s
satisfying
|s|_{v.\sup}\leq 1
for all non-archimedean places
v
, where
\sup
denotes the supremum over
X(\mathbb{C}_{v})
. Then
M_{n}
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-submodule of maximal rank of
H^{0}(X,L_{n})
.

We now consider
V_{n}=H^{0}(X,L_{n})\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{R}
. Then
V_{n}
is a
\mathbb{R}
-vector space and we can give it a sup norm as follows. We have

V_{n}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}\simeq\bigoplus_{v\text{ arch. }}H^{0}(X(\mathbb{C}_{v}),L_{n})

where
X(\mathbb{C}_{v})
denotes the complex variety obtained by embedding
K
into
\mathbb{C}
via the place
v
. Each
H^{0}(X(\mathbb{C}_{v}),L_{n})
has a supremum norm coming from the metric on
L_{n}
for the place
v
, and taking the supremum of each of them defines a sup norm on
V_{n}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}
and hence on
V_{n}
which we will denote by
|\cdot|_{\sup}
.

We now let
C_{n,\sup}
be the unit ball of sections
s\in V_{n}
satisfying
|s|_{\sup}\leq 1
. Fix a Haar measure on
V_{n}
and let
\operatorname{vol}M_{n}
denote the volume of the fundamental domain of the lattice
M_{n}
in
V_{n}
. The quantity

\log\operatorname{vol}C_{n,\sup}-\log\operatorname{vol}M_{n}

does not depend on the choice of our Haar measure. Using arithmetic Hilbert–Samuel
[
Zha95
, Theorem 1.4]
, one can deduce that

(8)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{d^{n}\dim V_{n}}\left(\log\operatorname{vol}C_{n,\sup}-\log\operatorname{vol}M_{n}\right)=0.

Our main result in the section is a quantitative lower bound on the volume difference (
8
). The main idea is a generalization of a construction of Baker
[
Bak06
]
to higher dimensions due to Looper
[
Loo24
]
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
K=\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)
for some
\alpha\in K
. Assume that
n
is sufficiently large depending on
L
. Then there exists
c=c(\varphi)>0
such that for all
n>0
, we have

\frac{1}{\dim V_{n}}\left(\log\operatorname{vol}(C_{n,\sup})-\log\operatorname{vol}(M_{n})\right)\geq-c\left(d^{n/2}+[K:\mathbb{Q}]^{2}h(\alpha)\right).

Proof.

To prove this, we follow an idea of Looper
[
Loo24
]
by constructing a special basis. In the case of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
, this has been done by Baker
[
Bak06
]
. We let
F_{i}^{(m)}
be the
i^{th}
coordinate of the iterate
F^{(m)}
. Since
\operatorname{Res}(F^{(m)})
is non-zero, it follows by the fundamental theorem of elimination theory
[
Mac03
]
that every homogeneous polynomial of degree
N\geq(r+1)d^{m}
can be written as

\eta_{0}F_{0}^{(m)}+\cdots+\eta_{r}F_{r}^{(m)}

where
\eta_{i}
is a polynomial of degree
\leq N-d^{m}
. We can now repeat this with each
\eta_{i}
, and in particular if we let
S
be the set of all possible products of
F_{i}^{(m)}
, we obtain that every homogeneous polynomial of degree
N\geq(r+1)d^{m}
can be written as a sum of terms
\eta_{i}G_{i}
for
G_{i}\in S
where each
\eta_{i}
is a homogeneous polynomial of degree
\leq(r+1)d^{m}
.

Hence we can find a basis
\cal{B}
over
K
for homogeneous polynomials of degree
d^{n}
with coefficients in
K
where each term is of the form
\eta G
, where
\eta
is a monomial of degree
\leq(r+1)d^{m}
and
G
is a product of terms of the form
F_{i}^{(m)}
. To find a basis over
\mathbb{Q}
, we allow
\eta
to have a coefficient from
\{1,\alpha,\ldots,\alpha^{l-1}\}
where
l=[K:\mathbb{Q}]
. We first bound the sup norm of each term
\eta G
under
|\cdot|_{v}
for each place
v
.

Proposition 5.2
.

Assume that
m\leq\frac{n}{2}
. For each place
v
of
K
, if
s
is the section corresponding to
\eta G
, then we have

\log|s|_{\sup,v}\leq d^{n-m}O_{\varphi}(1)+[K:\mathbb{Q}]\log^{+}|\alpha|_{v}

where we view
\eta G
as a section of
H^{0}(X,L_{n})
.

Proof.

Recall that we have the formula

\log|s(x)|_{v}=\log|\eta(\tilde{x})|_{v}+\log|G(\tilde{x})|_{v}-d^{n}G_{v}(\tilde{x})

where
\tilde{x}\in\mathbb{A}^{N+1}(\mathbb{C}_{v})
is any lift of
x\in\mathbb{P}^{N}(\mathbb{C}_{v})
. By
[
Ing22
, Theorem 5]
, we know that

\log||F(y)||-d\log||y||=O_{F}(1)

for any
y\in\mathbb{A}^{N+1}(\mathbb{C}_{v})
. Applying Tate’s telescoping argument tells us that if

G_{v,m}=\frac{\log||F^{m}(x)||}{d^{m}},

then

|G_{v,m}(x)-G_{v}(x)|\leq\frac{1}{d^{m}}O_{\varphi}(1).

Writing
G
as a product of
F_{i}^{(m)}
, as we have
|F_{i}^{(m)}|_{v}\leq||F^{(m)}||_{v}
, we get

\log|\eta(\tilde{x})|_{v}+\log|G(\tilde{x})|_{v}-d^{n}G_{v}(\tilde{x})\leq\log|\eta(\tilde{x})|_{v}-(d^{n}-\deg G)G_{v}(\tilde{x})+O_{\varphi}(d^{n-m}).

Pick a lift
\tilde{x}
such that
||\tilde{x}||=1
. Then we may bound

\log|\eta(\tilde{x})|_{v}\leq[K:\mathbb{Q}]\log^{+}|\alpha|_{v}

and by
[
Ing22
, Lemma 6]
we have

G_{v}(\tilde{x})\geq-O_{F}(1).

Since
d^{n}-\deg G\leq(r+1)d^{m}
, we have

\log|\eta(\tilde{x})|+\log|G(\tilde{x})|_{v}-d^{n}G_{v}(\tilde{x})\leq[K:\mathbb{Q}]\log^{+}|\alpha|_{v}+O_{\varphi}(d^{n-m}+d^{m})

=d^{n-m}O_{\varphi}(1)+[K:\mathbb{Q}]\log^{+}|\alpha|_{v})

as desired.

∎

Now let
M^{\prime}_{n}
be the lattice spanned by our basis
\cal{B}
. We will use it to approximate our lattice
M_{n}
. For each non-archimedean place
v
, if
p
is the corresponding prime, it follows from Proposition
5.2
that for any section
s\in M_{n}^{\prime}
, we have

\left(p^{O_{\varphi}(d^{n-m})+[K:\mathbb{Q}]\log^{+}|\alpha|_{v}}\right)s\subseteq M_{n,v}

where
M_{n,v}
is the
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-submodule of sections satisfying
|s|_{v,\sup}\leq 1
for that particular non-archimedean place
v
. When
p
is a prime of good reduction for
F
and also satisfying
|\alpha|_{v}=1
, we have
\log|s|_{\sup,v}\leq 1
. Hence summing up over all places of bad reduction and those for which
|\alpha|_{v}\not=1
, we obtain that

\log\operatorname{vol}(M_{n})\leq(\dim V_{n})\left(O_{\varphi}(d^{n-m})+[K:\mathbb{Q}]^{2}h(\alpha)\right)+\log\operatorname{vol}(M^{\prime}_{n}).

We now look at the fundamental domain
\cal{F}
of
M^{\prime}_{n}
that is given by our basis
\cal{B}
. Any element of our fundamental domain can be written as

\sum_{i}c_{i}\eta_{i}G_{i}

where
0\leq c_{i}\leq 1
. Applying our bound and using the triangle inequality, we get

\log|\sum_{i}c_{i}\eta_{i}G_{i}|_{v}\leq\left(O_{\varphi}(d^{n-m})+[K:\mathbb{Q}]\log^{+}|\alpha|_{v}\right)+\log\dim V_{n}

=O_{\varphi}(d^{n-m})+[K:\mathbb{Q}]\log^{+}|\alpha|_{v}+\log\dim V_{n}.

Hence if we scale
C_{n}
by
e^{O_{\varphi}(d^{n-m})+[K:\mathbb{Q}]^{2}h(\alpha)+\log\dim V_{n}}
, it would contain our fundamental domain
\cal{F}
.
We thus obtain that

\log\operatorname{vol}(C_{n})-\log\operatorname{vol}(M^{\prime}_{n})\geq-O_{\varphi}(d^{n-m}+[K:\mathbb{Q}]^{2}h(\alpha))\dim(V_{n})-(\log\dim V_{n})\dim V_{n}.

Putting things together, we obtain

\frac{1}{\dim V_{n}}\left(\log\operatorname{vol}(C_{n})-\log\operatorname{vol}(M_{n})\right)\geq-O_{\varphi}(d^{n-2k}+[K:\mathbb{Q}]^{2}h(\alpha))-\log\dim V_{n}

which is an error of
-\left(O_{\varphi}(d^{n-m})+[K:\mathbb{Q}]^{2}h(\alpha)\right)
since,
\log\dim V_{n}\leq d^{n-m}
for
n
large enough depending on
L
, as desired. We then obtain an exponent of
\frac{n}{2}
by taking
m=\frac{n}{2}
.
∎

6.
Quantitative Equidistribution for Abelian Varieties

Our aim in this sectin is to prove the main theorem of our paper in the case where
X
is an abelian variety, so that
\varphi=[2]
is the doubling map and
\widehat{h}_{\varphi}=\widehat{h}_{X}
is the Neron–Tate height.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
X
be an abelian variety over a number field
K
and
L
a symmetric ample line bundle on
X
. Let
\widehat{h}_{X}(x)
be the Neron–Tate height associated to
L
. Then there exists constants
c_{1}=c_{1}(K,L),c_{3}=c_{3}(K,L)
such that for any
\varepsilon>0
and embedding
v:K\xhookrightarrow{}\mathbb{C}
, if
f:X(\mathbb{C}_{v})\to\mathbb{R}
is a smooth function, then

(1)

there exists a hypersurface
H(f,\varepsilon)
defined over
K
which has degree at most
c_{1}\varepsilon^{-8}
with respect to
L
,

(2)

for all
x\in X(\overline{K})
with
\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{K}/K)
-orbit
F_{x}
, if
x\not\in H(f,\varepsilon)
then

\left|\frac{1}{|F_{x}|}\sum_{y\in F_{x}}f(y)-\int f(y)d\mu_{X,v}(y)\right|\leq c_{f,3}\left(\frac{\widehat{h}_{X}(x)}{\varepsilon}+c_{3}\varepsilon\right),

where
c_{f,3}
is a constant depending on the derivatives of
f
up to order
3
and
\mu_{X,v}
is the Haar measure for
X
associated to
v
.

Given our smooth function
f
, we first divide it by
c_{f,3}
to get a new function
f
which satisfies
c_{f,3}\leq 2
. It then suffices to prove that

\left|\frac{1}{|F_{x}|}\sum_{y\in F_{x}}f(y)-\int f(y)d\mu_{\varphi,v}(y)\right|\leq\frac{\widehat{h}_{\varphi}(x)}{\varepsilon}+c_{3}\varepsilon

for functions
f
satisfying
c_{f,3}\leq 2
and obtain the general version by multiplying
c_{f,3}
back in.

The strategy of the proof will be the same as the general case of polarized endomorphisms, but there are less technical details due to the smoothness of the metric.

Let
L
be a symmetric ample line bundle on
X
. Then
\varphi
is polarizable with respect to
L
and we may find an embedding
X\xrightarrow{}\mathbb{P}^{N}
such that
\varphi
extends to
\tilde{\varphi}
.

We may lift
\tilde{\varphi}
to a homogeneous map
F:\mathbb{A}^{N+1}\to\mathbb{A}^{N+1}
. Then for each place
v\in M_{K}
, we may define

G_{v}(x)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log||F^{n}(x)||}{d^{n}}\text{ for }x\in\mathbb{A}^{N+1}(\mathbb{C}_{v}).

where
||(x_{0},\ldots,x_{n+1})||_{v}=\max\{|x_{0}|_{v},\ldots,|x_{n+1}|_{v}\}
. We can then use
G_{v}
to define a metric on
L
for each place
v
by

\log|s(x)|_{v}=\log|P(\tilde{x})|_{v}-G_{v}(\tilde{x})\text{ for }x\in\mathbb{P}^{N}(\mathbb{C}_{v}),

where
\tilde{x}
is any lift of
x
to
\mathbb{A}^{N+1}(\mathbb{C}_{v})
. This turns
\overline{L}
on
\mathbb{P}^{N}
into a semi-positive adelic line bundle in the sense of Zhang. The main advantange is this setting is that when
v
is an archimedean place, the metric on
\overline{L}_{v}
when restricted to our abelian variety
X\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{N}
is smooth, and the associated
(1,1)
-form is positive.

Proposition 6.2
.

The metric on
\overline{L}_{v}
when restricted to
X(\mathbb{C}_{v})
is smooth. Furthermore, the associated
(1,1)
-form
\omega=c_{1}(\overline{L}_{v})
on
X(\mathbb{C}_{v})
is a positive
(1,1)
-form.

Proof.

For our archimedean place
v
, we first modify
||\cdot||_{v}
so that the metric is the Fubini–Study metric, i.e.

||F(x_{0},\ldots,x_{n})||_{v}=\frac{|F(x_{0},\ldots,x_{n})|_{v}}{\sqrt{|x_{0}|^{2}+\cdots+|x_{n}|^{2}}}.

This does not change the function
G_{v}(x)
. Then if
\omega
denotes the Fubini–Study form on
\mathbb{P}^{N}
, we know that if
\omega_{F}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{d^{n}}(F^{n})^{*}\omega
where
\omega_{F}
is a
(1,1)
-current, then we have

dd^{c}\log|s(x)|_{v}=\delta_{\operatorname{div}(s)}-\omega_{F}.

Now when restricted to
X(\mathbb{C}_{v})
,
\omega_{F}
is given by
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{d^{n}}([2^{n}])^{*}\omega
. If we write
X(\mathbb{C}_{v})\simeq\mathbb{C}^{g}/\Lambda
for some lattice
\Lambda
, we may write
\omega
as

\omega=\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq n}f_{i,j}(z)dz_{i}\wedge d\overline{z}_{j}.

Let
\mu_{v}
be the Haar measure on
X(\mathbb{C}_{v})
, normalized to have total volume
1
. It is then easy to verify that the limit of
\omega_{F}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{4^{n}}([2^{n}])^{*}\omega
is given by

\omega_{F}=\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq n}c_{i,j}dz_{i}\wedge d\overline{z}_{j}

where

c_{i,j}=\int_{X(\mathbb{C}_{v})}f_{i,j}(z)d\mu_{v}

Then clearly
\omega_{F}
is a smooth positive
(1,1)
-form, which implies that the metric on our line bundle is smooth as desired.
∎

Since the metric on
\overline{L}_{v}
is smooth, we do not need to regularize our metric in order to apply the results of Section
4
. We then set
L_{n}=L^{d^{n}}
and let
V_{n}=H^{0}(X,L_{n})\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{R}
. We then have the isomorphism

V_{n}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}\simeq\bigoplus_{v\text{ arch. }}H^{0}(X(\mathbb{C}_{v}),L_{n})

We may then define a sup norm
|\cdot|_{\sup}
on
V_{n}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}
and we let
C_{n,\sup}
be the unit ball of sections
s
satisfying
|s|_{\sup}\leq 1
. Similarly, we may let
M_{n}
be the
\mathbb{Z}
-submodule of sections
s
of
H^{0}(X,L_{n})
that satisfies
|s|_{\sup,v}\leq 1
for all non-archimedean places
v
.

By Theorem
5.1
, we have

(9)

\frac{1}{\dim V_{n}}\left(\log\operatorname{vol}(C_{n,\sup})-\log\operatorname{vol}(M_{n})\right)\geq-O_{\varphi,K}(d^{n/2}).

Now given a measure
\mu_{v}
for archimedean place
v
such that conjugate places share the same measure, we may then define a
L^{2}
-norm on each
H^{0}(X(\mathbb{C}_{v}),L_{n})
by

\langle s,s^{\prime}\rangle_{v}=\int_{X(\mathbb{C}_{v})}\langle s,s^{\prime}\rangle_{v}d\mu_{v}

and thus on
V_{n}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}
by declaring each component to be orthogonal. Following
[
Mor14
, Section 3.3]
, our inner product
\langle\,,\,\rangle
on
H^{0}(X,L_{n})\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{C}
is of real type and hence it induces one on
V_{n}
.

Fix an archimedean place
v
and let
f:X(\mathbb{C}_{v})\to\mathbb{R}
be our test smooth function, normalized so that
c_{f,3}\leq 2
. We now perturb the metric of
\overline{L}_{n}
at the place
v
by mutiplying it by
e^{-\varepsilon f}
. If
v
has a complex conjugate pair
v^{\prime}
, we also perturb the metric at
v^{\prime}
by
e^{-\varepsilon f}
. We denote our new adelic line bundle by
\overline{L}_{n}(\varepsilon f)

If
\omega
is the
(1,1)
-form for
\overline{L}_{v}
, then as it is a positive
(1,1)
-form, we know that
\lambda_{\omega}>\delta
for some fixed
\delta>0
. We take
\varepsilon
small enough so that if
\omega^{\prime}
is the
(1,1)
-form for
\overline{L}(\varepsilon f)
, then
\lambda_{\omega^{\prime}}\geq\frac{1}{2}\delta
. In particular,
\mu_{v}=(\omega^{\prime}_{v})^{\wedge r}
is a positive volume form. We normalize
\mu_{v}
so that
\mu_{v}
has total volume
1
. For each other archimedean place
w
, we let
\mu_{w}
be some fixed positive volume form with total volume
1
. With this choices of measures
\mu_{v}
, we can put a
L^{2}
norm on
V_{n}
.

We now let
B_{n,\sup}
be the unit ball of sections for
V_{n}
under the sup norm for
\overline{L}_{n}(\varepsilon f)
, and
B_{n,L^{2}},C_{n,L^{2}}
be the unit ball under the
L^{2}
norm for
\overline{L}_{n}(\varepsilon f)
and
\overline{L}_{n}
respectively. As each
\mu_{w}
has total volume
1
, we have
|s|_{\sup,v}\geq|s|_{L^{2},v}
and so

|\cdot|_{L^{2}}\leq[K:\mathbb{Q}]|\cdot|_{\sup}

on
V_{n}
. Thus we trivially have

(10)

\frac{1}{\dim V_{n}}\left(\log\operatorname{vol}C_{n,L^{2}}-\log\operatorname{vol}C_{n,\sup}\right)\geq-\log[K:\mathbb{Q}].

We now have the following lemma.

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
n_{v}=2
if
v
is complex and
1
if
v
is real. Then

\log\operatorname{vol}B_{n,L^{2}}-\log\operatorname{vol}C_{n,L^{2}}=n_{v}\left(\log\operatorname{vol}B_{n,v,L^{2}}-\log\operatorname{vol}C_{n,v,L^{2}}\right)

where
B_{n,v,L^{2}}
is the unit ball of sections with
L^{2}
-norm
\leq 1
for
H^{0}(X(\mathbb{C}_{v}),L_{n})
with the metric from
\overline{L}_{n}(\varepsilon f)
and similarly for
C_{n,v,L^{2}}
.

Proof.

Let
\langle\,,\,\rangle
be the inner product induced by the metric of
\overline{L}_{n}
and
\langle\,,\,\rangle^{\prime}
be induced by
\overline{L}_{n}(\varepsilon f)
. To calculate
\log\operatorname{vol}B^{\prime}_{n,L^{2}}-\log\operatorname{vol}C^{\prime}_{n,L^{2}}
, we pick a basis of sections
s_{1},\ldots,s_{N}
of

and we compute the difference

\log\det(\langle s_{i},s_{j}\rangle^{\prime})-\log\det(\langle s_{i},s_{j}\rangle).

Tensoring by

, we may consider a basis inside

. We may then choose the

’s such that they each lie entirely in some

. Then since our inner products agree if

n_{v}(\log\det(\langle s_{i},s_{j}\rangle^{\prime}_{v})-\log\det(\langle s_{i},s_{j}\rangle_{v}))=n_{v}\left(\log\operatorname{vol}B^{\prime}_{n,v,L^{2}}-\log\operatorname{vol}C^{\prime}_{n,v,L^{2}}\right)

as desired.
∎

By Lemma
6.3
, we have

\log\operatorname{vol}B_{n,L^{2}}-\log\operatorname{vol}C_{n,L^{2}}=n_{v}\left(\log\operatorname{vol}B_{n,v,L^{2}}-\log\operatorname{vol}C_{n,v,L^{2}}\right)

where
n_{v}=1
if
v
is real and
2
if
v
is complex and
B_{n,v,L^{2}},C_{n,v,L^{2}}
are the unit ball of sections in
H^{0}(X(\mathbb{C}_{v}),L_{n})
with
L^{2}
norm
\leq 1
for the metric of
\overline{L}_{n}(\varepsilon f)
and
\overline{L}_{n}
respectively. Let
|\cdot|_{v,L^{2}}
be the
L^{2}
-norm on
H^{0}(X(\mathbb{C}_{v}),L_{n})
induced by
\overline{L}_{n}
and
|\cdot|^{\prime}_{v,L^{2}}
be the
L^{2}
-norm induced by
\overline{L}_{n}(\varepsilon f)
.

We now proceed to bound
\log\operatorname{vol}B_{n,v,L^{2}}-\log\operatorname{vol}C_{n,v,L^{2}}
.

Proposition 6.4
.

For all sufficiently large
n
depending only on
X
and
L
, we have

\frac{1}{\dim V_{n}}\left(\log\operatorname{vol}B_{n,v,L^{2}}-\log\operatorname{vol}C_{n,v,L^{2}}\right)\geq\frac{d^{n}\varepsilon}{[K:\mathbb{Q}]}\int_{X}fd\mu_{X,v}-2d^{n}\varepsilon d^{-n/8}(\sup|f|)+O_{r}(d^{n}\varepsilon^{2}).

Proof.

We first show that

\frac{1}{\dim V_{n}}\left(\log\operatorname{vol}B_{n,v,L^{2}}-\log\operatorname{vol}C_{n,v,L^{2}}\right)\geq\frac{d^{n}\varepsilon}{[K:\mathbb{Q}]}\int_{X}fd\mu_{X,v}-d^{n}\varepsilon d^{-n/8}(\sup|f|)+O_{r}(d^{n}\varepsilon^{2})

under the assumption that
f\leq 0
. This is enough to deduce the inequality in general as we may replace
f
with
f-\sup|f|
, which increases its supremum by at most a factor of
2
.

Now let
s_{1},\ldots,s_{N}
be an orthonormal basis for the
L^{2}
-norm from
\overline{L}_{n}(\varepsilon f)
that is orthogonal for the
L^{2}
-norm from
\overline{L}_{n}
. Then

\log\operatorname{vol}B_{n,v,L^{2}}-\log\operatorname{vol}C_{n,v,L^{2}}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\log|s_{i}(x)|^{2}_{v,L^{2}}.

Since
|\cdot|_{v}=|\cdot|^{\prime}_{v}e^{\varepsilon f}
, we rewrite our expression as

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\log\int_{X}|s_{i}(x)|^{\prime 2}_{v}e^{\varepsilon f}d\mu_{v}.

Since
\int_{X}|s_{i}(x)|^{\prime 2}_{v}d\mu_{v}=1
, by Jensen’s inequality we get

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\log\int_{X}|s_{i}(x)|^{\prime 2}_{v}e^{\varepsilon f(x)}d\mu_{v}\geq\int_{X}\sum_{i=1}^{N}|s_{i}(x)|_{v}^{\prime 2}\log(e^{\varepsilon f(x)})d\mu_{v}=\int_{X}\sum_{i=1}^{n}|s_{i}(x)|_{v}^{\prime 2}(d^{n}\varepsilon f(x))d\mu_{v}.

Now since
f^{\prime}\leq 0
, to bound it from below it suffices to bound
\sum_{i=1}^{n}|s_{i}(x)|_{v}^{\prime 2}
from above. We now apply Proposition
4.2
to obtain that for

, recalling that
L_{n}=L^{d^{n}}
, we have

\sum_{i=1}^{N}|s_{i}(x)|_{v}^{\prime 2}\leq\dim_{\mathbb{C}}H^{0}(X(\mathbb{C}_{v}),L_{n})\left(1+\frac{1}{d^{n/8}}\right).

Hence we have

\int_{X}\sum_{i=1}^{N}|s_{i}(x)|^{\prime 2}_{v}(d^{n}\varepsilon f(x))d\mu\geq-\dim_{\mathbb{C}}H^{0}(X(\mathbb{C}_{v},L_{n})\left(1+d^{-n/8}\right)d^{n}\varepsilon\int_{X}fd\mu_{v}.

Bounding the integral by
\sup|f|
, we may now rewrite this as

\frac{1}{\dim V_{n}}\left(\log\operatorname{vol}B_{n,v,L^{2}}-\log\operatorname{vol}C_{n,v,L^{2}}\right)\geq\frac{d^{n}\varepsilon}{[K:\mathbb{Q}]}\int_{X}fd\mu_{v}-d^{n}\varepsilon d^{-n/8}\sup|f|.

Finally, we have to convert the integral from
d\mu_{v}
to
d\mu_{X,v}
. Recall that
\mu_{v}=(\omega^{\prime}_{v})^{r}
and that
\mu_{X,v}=(\omega_{v})^{r}
after a suitable normalization of
\omega^{\prime}_{v}
and
\omega_{v}
. But
\omega_{v}
is a positive
(1,1)
-form and the coefficients of
\omega^{\prime}_{v}
and
\omega_{v}
differ by
O(\varepsilon)
and so the coefficients of its

wedge power differs at most by
O_{r}(\varepsilon c_{\omega_{v},1})=O_{r}(\varepsilon)
. Thus we may change the integral of
f
from

to

and incur an error of
O_{r}(d^{n}\varepsilon^{2})
as desired.
∎

We now prove our main theorem in the case of abelian varieties.

Theorem 6.5
.

Let
X
be an abelian variety over a number field
K
and
L
a symmetric ample line bundle on
A
. Let
\widehat{h}_{X}(x)
be the Neron–Tate height associated to
L
. Then there exists constants
c_{1}=c_{1}(K,L),c_{3}=c_{3}(K,L)
such that for any
\varepsilon>0
and embedding
v:K\xhookrightarrow{}\mathbb{C}
, if
f:X(\mathbb{C}_{v})\to\mathbb{R}
is a smooth function, then

(1)

there exists a hypersurface
H(f,\varepsilon)
defined over
K
which has degree at most
c_{1}\varepsilon^{-8}
with respect to
L
,

(2)

for all
x\in X(\overline{K})
with
\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{K}/K)
-orbit
F_{x}
, if
x\not\in H(f,\varepsilon)
then

\left|\frac{1}{|F_{x}|}\sum_{y\in F_{x}}f(y)-\int f(y)d\mu_{X,v}(y)\right|\leq c_{f,3}\left(\frac{\widehat{h}_{X}(x)}{\varepsilon}+c_{3}\varepsilon\right),

where
c_{f,3}
is a constant depending on the derivatives of
f
up to order
3
and
\mu_{X,v}
is the Haar measure for
A
associated to
v
.

Proof.

Dividing
f
by
c_{f,3}
, we may assume that
c_{f,3}\leq 2
. By Corollary
3.2
, we obtain

\log C_{n,L^{2}}-\log C_{n,\sup}\geq-O_{\varphi}(\log d^{n})\dim V_{n}.

Using Proposition
6.4
along with (
9
) and (
10
), we have

\frac{1}{d^{n}\dim V_{n}}\left(\log\operatorname{vol}B_{n,\sup}-\log\operatorname{vol}M_{n}\right)\geq\frac{\varepsilon}{[K:\mathbb{Q}]}\int_{X}fd\mu^{\prime}-d^{-n/8}\varepsilon\sup|f|+O_{\varphi,K}(d^{-n/2}).

We now choose the smallest
n
such that
\varepsilon\geq d^{-n/8}
so that if
\int_{X}fd\mu_{v}=0
, we get

\frac{1}{d^{n}\dim V_{n}}(\log\operatorname{vol}B_{n,\sup}-\log\operatorname{vol}M_{n})\geq-c\varepsilon^{2}

for some
c=c(X,K)>0
. Now consider the adelic line bundle
\overline{L}(\varepsilon f+(c+1)[K:\mathbb{Q}]\varepsilon^{2})^{n}
. This will have Euler characteristic at least
d^{n}\dim V_{n}\varepsilon^{2}\geq\dim V_{n}+\log 2
for
n
sufficiently large. By Minkowski, this gives us a section
s
of
\overline{L}(\varepsilon f+(c+1)[K:\mathbb{Q}]\varepsilon^{2})^{n}
such that
\log|s|_{w}\leq 0
for all places
w
.

Using this section
s
to evaluate heights, we obtain for all
x\not\in\operatorname{div}(s)
,

\sum_{y\in F_{x}}f(y)\geq-\frac{\widehat{h}_{\varphi}(x)}{\varepsilon}+(c+1)[K:\mathbb{Q}]\varepsilon

and applying this to
-f
gives us a corresponding upper bound. Hence we obtain a hypersurface
H(f,\varepsilon)
of degree at most
O_{L,K}(\varepsilon^{8})
such that

\left|\sum_{y\in F_{x}}f(y)-\int_{X}f(y)d\mu_{v}\right|\leq c_{f,3}\left(\frac{\widehat{h}_{\varphi}(x)}{\varepsilon}+c^{\prime}\varepsilon\right)

where
c^{\prime}
depends on
L
and
K
as desired.
∎

As an immediate consequence, we obtain Theorem
6.6
.

Theorem 6.6
.

Let
X
be an abelian variety over a number field
K
and let
\widehat{h}_{X}(x)
denote the Neron–Tate height of
X
. Then there exists
c=c(X,K)>0
, such that if

S=\{x\in X(\overline{K})\mid\widehat{h}_{A}(x)\leq\frac{c}{D^{8}}\text{ and }[K(x):K]\leq D\},

then
S
is contained in a hypersurface of degree
O_{X,K}(D^{33})
.

Proof.

Fix an archimedean place
v
and identify
X(\mathbb{C}_{v})\simeq\mathbb{C}^{g}/\Lambda
for a rank
2g
lattice
\Lambda
. Let
F\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{g}
be a fundamental domain, so that the projection map
\pi:F\to\mathbb{C}^{g}/\Lambda
is a bijection. We may write
F=\{c_{1}v_{1}+\cdots+c_{2g}v_{2g}\mid 0\leq c_{i}<1\}
for some elements
v_{i}\in\mathbb{C}^{g}
. Now for a fixed positive integer
D\geq 1
, we split
F
into
D+1
disjoint regions,
F_{1},\ldots,F_{D+1}
, where

F_{i}=\{c_{1}v_{1}+\cdots+c_{2g}v_{2g}\mid 0\leq c_{i}<1\text{ for }1\leq i\leq 2g-1,\frac{i-1}{D+1}\leq c_{2g}<\frac{i}{D+1}\}.

We now choose
f_{i}
to be some test function that is supported on
F_{i}
that is between
0
and
1
and is
1
on

F^{\prime}_{i}=\{c_{1}v_{1}+\cdots+c_{2g}v_{2g}\mid\frac{1}{4}\leq c_{i}\leq\frac{3}{4}\text{ for }1\leq i\leq 2g-1,\frac{4i-3}{4(D+1)}\leq c_{2g}\leq\frac{4i-1}{4(D+1)}.\}

When
v_{1},\ldots,v_{2g}
form the standard basis of
\mathbb{R}^{2g}\simeq\mathbb{C}^{g}
, such a
f_{i}
may be chosen so that
c_{f_{i},3}=O(D^{3})
. Then changing basis only increases
c_{f_{i},3}
by a constant that depends only on
A
. Hence we may take
c_{f_{i},3}=O_{A}(D^{3})
and in particular by Theorem
6.1
, there exists a hypersurface of degree
O_{A,K}(\varepsilon^{8})
that contains all points
x\in A(\overline{K})
for which

\left|\frac{1}{|F_{x}|}\sum_{y\in F_{x}}f_{i}(y)-\int f_{i}d\mu_{v}\right|\leq O_{A}(D^{3})\left(\frac{\widehat{h}_{\varphi}(x)}{\varepsilon}+O_{A,K}(\varepsilon).\right)

We know that
\int f_{i}d\mu_{v}\geq\mu_{v}(F_{i}^{\prime})\geq\frac{c}{D}
for some
c>0
dependign only on
A
. Choosing
\varepsilon=c^{\prime}D^{-4}
for some constant
c^{\prime}=c(A,K)>0
sufficiently large gives us that

\left|\frac{1}{|F_{x}|}\sum_{y\in F_{x}}f_{i}(y)-\int f_{i}d\mu_{v}\right|\leq O_{A}(c^{\prime}D^{7})\widehat{h}_{\varphi}(x)+\frac{c}{3D}

for
x\not\in H_{i}
where
H_{i}
is a hypersurface of degree
O_{A,K}(D^{32})
. Hence if
\widehat{h}_{\varphi}(x)\leq c^{\prime}D^{-8}
for
c^{\prime}=c^{\prime}(A,K)>0
small enough, we obtain

\left|\frac{1}{|F_{x}|}\sum_{y\in F_{x}}f_{i}(y)-\int f_{i}d\mu_{v}\right|\leq\frac{2c}{3D}\implies\sum_{y\in F_{x}}f_{i}(y)>0\implies F_{x}\cap F_{i}\not=\emptyset.

Hence if
x\not\in\cup_{i=1}^{D+1}H_{i}
, we obtain that
F_{x}
has non-empty intersection with
D+1
disjoint sets, and thus
|F_{x}|>D
. Hence if
|F_{x}|<D
, we must have
x\in\cup_{i=1}^{D+1}H_{i}
and so if
h_{\varphi}(x)\leq c^{\prime}D^{-8}
and
[K(x):K]\leq D
, it must be that
x
is contained in a hypersurface of degree
D^{33}
as desired.
∎

7.
Bergman Kernels for Negatively Curved Line Bundles

We now move onto the complex geometry results necessary for the caase where the metric on our line bundle is only semipositive. In this case, after a perturbation, the line bundle
\overline{L}(\varepsilon f)
will have negative curvature and thus the asymptotic expansion of Bergman kernels no longer hold. Nonetheless, Yuan
[
Yua08
]
manages to overcome this technical obstruction and obtain a useful upper bound on the Bergman kernel and we will follow his approach.

7.1.
Lower bound on Bergman Kernel

We adopt the notations in Section
4
. The first step is again onto the lower bound of the Bergman kernel in which we will again follow Charles
[
Cha15
]
. We endow
X
with the Kahler metric coming from
\omega
. Let
D:L^{n}\to L^{n}\otimes\Omega^{0,1}
be the Chern connection with
D=\partial+\overline{\partial}
and let
\delta,\overline{\delta}
be the formal adjoints of
\partial
and
\overline{\partial}
respectively. We then have a Laplacian

\triangle_{n,l}=\overline{\partial}\,\overline{\delta}+\overline{\delta}\,\overline{\partial}:\Omega^{0,l}(X,L^{n})\to\Omega^{0,l}(X,L^{n}).

We define a Lefschetz operator
L(u)=\omega\wedge u
and we let
\Lambda
be the formal adjoint to
L
. We first start with computing the
(1,1)
-form
i\Theta(K_{X})
associated to the cotangent bundle.

Lemma 7.1
.

Let

\omega(z)=\frac{i}{2}\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq r}c_{i,j}(z)dz_{i}\wedge d\overline{z}_{j}

in a local chart. Then if
A(z)
denotes the positive definite matrix given by
\{c_{i,j}(z)\}
, the metric for the canonical bundle
K_{X}
may be given locally by
\det A^{-1}(z)
. In particular, we may bound the coefficients of
i\Theta(K_{X})
by
O(c_{\omega,2}^{2r}\lambda_{\omega}^{-2r})
.

Proof.

In our chart, the hermitian metric on the tangent bundle in the basis
\{\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{1}},\ldots,\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{n}}\}
is given by
A(z)
and so the metric given on the cotangent bundle
\{dz_{1},\ldots,dz_{n}\}
is given by
A^{-1}(z)
. Hence the metric for the canonical bundle is given by
\det A^{-1}(z)
. Picking
dz_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dz_{n}
as our local section and computing
dd^{c}\log|s|
, we have to compute
-dd^{c}\log\det A(z)
. Taking any second derivative, say
x_{i}
and
x_{j}
, gives us an expression of the form

\frac{\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}(\det A(z))\det A(z)-\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}(\det A(z))\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}(\det A(z))}{\det A(z)^{2}}

and we may bound this by
O(c_{\omega,2}^{2r}\lambda_{\omega}^{-2r})
as desired.
∎

Proposition 7.2
.

For all
n\geq O(\lambda_{\omega}^{-(2r+1)}c_{\omega,2}^{2r})
, if
t\in\Omega^{0,1}(X,L^{n})
is a smooth section, we have

\langle\triangle_{n,1}t,t\rangle\geq\frac{n}{2}|t|_{L^{2}}.

In particular, if
s\in C^{\infty}(X,L^{n})
which is orthogonal to the holomorphic sections
H^{0}(X,L^{n})
, we have

|s|^{2}_{L^{2}}\leq\frac{2}{n}|\overline{\partial}s|_{L^{2}}.

Proof.

We may view global sections of
\Omega^{0,1}(X,L^{n})
as global
(n,1)
-forms on
K_{X}^{-1}\otimes L^{n}
. For
t\in\Omega^{0,1}(X,L^{n})
, viewing it as an element
\alpha\in\Omega^{n,1}(X,K_{X}^{-1}\otimes L^{n})
, by
[
Dem12
, VII (2.1)]
we have

\langle\triangle^{\prime\prime}\alpha,\alpha\rangle\geq\int_{X}\langle[i\Theta(K_{X}^{-1}\otimes L^{n}),\Lambda]\alpha,\alpha\rangle d\mu

where
\triangle^{\prime\prime}
is the Lpalacian for
\Omega^{n,1}(X,K_{X}^{-1}\otimes L^{n})
. Expanding out, we have

[i\Theta(K_{X}^{-1}\otimes L^{n}),\Lambda]=[i\Theta(K_{X}^{-1}),\Lambda]+n[i\Theta(L),\Lambda].

By
[
Dem12
, V, Proposition 5.8]
, we know that
\langle[i\Theta(L),\Lambda]\alpha,\alpha\rangle\geq|\alpha|^{2}
and so

\langle n[i\Theta(L),\Lambda](\alpha),\alpha\rangle\geq n|\alpha|^{2}_{L^{2}}.

On the other hand,
[
Dem12
, V, Proposition 5.8]
implies that we may bound

\langle[i\Theta(K_{X}^{-1}),\Lambda]\alpha,\alpha\rangle\geq-r\gamma|\alpha|^{2}

where
\gamma
is an upper bound on the magnitude of the eigenvalues of
i\Theta(K_{X}^{-1})
with respect to
\omega
. We may bound this by taking the ratio of the largest eigenvalue of
i\Theta(K_{X}^{-1})
to the smallest eigenvalue of
\omega
in the standard basis
z
for our local chart. We may bound the smallest eigenvalue of
\omega
by
\lambda_{\omega}
and so by Lemma
7.1
, we may bound
\gamma
by
O(c_{\omega,2}^{2r}\lambda_{\omega}^{-(2r+1)})
. Hence if
n\geq O(c_{\omega,2}^{2r}\lambda_{\omega}^{-(2r+1)})
, we have the inequality

\langle[i\Theta(K_{X}^{-1}\otimes L^{n}),\Lambda]\alpha,\alpha\rangle\geq\frac{n}{2}|\alpha|^{2}

and so

\langle\triangle^{\prime\prime}\alpha,\alpha\rangle\geq\frac{n}{2}|\alpha|^{2}

for all
n\geq O(c_{\omega,2}^{2r}\lambda_{\omega}^{-(2r+1)})
. This gives us

\langle\triangle_{n,1}t,t\rangle\geq\frac{n}{2}|t|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Now given
s\in C^{\infty}(X,L^{n})
that is orthogonal to
H^{0}(X,L^{n})
, by the Hodge decomposition we know that
s=\triangle_{n,0}s^{\prime}
for some
s^{\prime}\in C^{\infty}(X,L^{n})
. We have
\triangle_{n,0}s^{\prime}=\overline{\delta}\overline{\partial}s
and so taking
t=\overline{\partial}s
, we obtain some
t\in\Omega^{0,1}(X,L^{n})
such that
\overline{\delta}t=s
and
\overline{\partial}t=0
. Then
\triangle_{n,1}t=\overline{\partial}s
. Let
C=\frac{n}{2}
. Since
\langle\triangle_{n,1}t,t\rangle\geq C|t|^{2}_{L^{2}}
, by Cauchy–Schwarz we get
|\triangle_{n,1}t|^{2}_{L^{2}}\geq C|t|_{L^{2}}|\triangle_{n,1}|_{L^{2}}
and so

|\overline{\partial}s|^{2}_{L^{2}}=|\triangle_{n,1}t|^{2}_{L^{2}}\geq C|t|_{L^{2}}|\triangle_{n,1}t|_{L^{2}}\geq C\langle t,\triangle_{n,1}t\rangle=C|s|^{2}

as desired.
∎

We now prove the lower bound on the Bergman kernel.

Proposition 7.3
.

Let
\overline{L}
be a positive line bundle with curvature form
\omega
and let
s_{1},\ldots,s_{N}
be an orthonormal basis for
L^{n}
for the volume form
\mu=(\omega)^{\wedge r}/r!
. Then for all
n\geq O(\lambda_{\omega}^{-(2r+1)}c_{\omega,2}^{2r}c_{h,3})^{8}
, we have

\sum_{i=1}^{N}|s_{i}(x)|^{2}\geq\left(\frac{n}{2\pi}\right)^{r}\left(1-\frac{1}{n^{1/8}}\right)

for all
x\in X(\mathbb{C})
.

Proof.

As in Proposition
4.2
, we can find a local chart of radius
O(c_{\omega,0})^{-1}
such that our hermitian metric is given by

h=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{r}|z_{i}|^{2}+O(\lambda_{\omega}^{-1}c_{h,3})|z|^{3}.

In this chart, we may view a local section
t(z)
of
\Omega^{0,1}(X,L^{n})
as
\sum_{i=1}^{r}f_{i}(z)d\overline{z}_{i}
where each
f_{i}
is a holomorphic function. Let
g_{i}(z)
be the norm of the local section of
\Omega^{0,1}(X)
that corresponds to
d\overline{z}_{i}
in our chart. Then we may bound
|t(z)|
by

(11)

|t(z)|\leq re^{nh(z)}\cdot\max_{1\leq i\leq r}|f_{i}(z)|\cdot\max_{1\leq i\leq r}|g_{i}(z)|\leq e^{nh(z)}\cdot\max_{1\leq i\leq r}|f_{i}(z)|\cdot O(c_{\omega,0}^{r}\lambda_{\omega}^{-(r+1)})

as in our original good chart, using Lemma
7.1
we may bound
g_{i}(z)
from above by the entries of the inverse of the matrix
\{c_{i,j}(z)\}
which has an upper bound of
O(\lambda_{\omega}^{-r}c_{\omega,0}^{r})
, and then we take into account the change of coordinates with coefficients bounded by
O(\lambda_{\omega}^{-1})
.

Let
s
be the local section that gives us the frame for which
h
is of this form. We pick a cutoff function
\chi(z)
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
that equals
1
for
|z|\leq 1
and vanishes for
|z|\geq 2
. We then set
\chi_{n}(z)=\chi(n^{2/5}z)
. Then for
n
such that
2n^{-2/5}
is smaller than the radius of our chart, we get that
s\chi_{n}
defines for us a smooth section of
L^{n}
on
X
. Let
s_{n}
be the orthogonal projection of
s\chi_{n}
to
H^{0}(X,L^{n})
. By Proposition
7.2
, for
n\geq O(\lambda_{\omega}^{-(2r+1)}c_{\omega,2}^{2r})
we get

|s_{n}-s\chi_{n}|^{2}_{L^{2}}\leq\frac{2}{n}|\overline{\partial}(s_{n}-s\chi_{n})|^{2}_{L^{2}}=\frac{2}{n}|\overline{\partial}(s\chi_{n})|^{2}_{L^{2}}.

Locally in our chart, our section
s
is simply the identity function and our function is simply
\chi_{n}
. Then
\overline{\partial}(\chi_{n})(z)=0
for
|z|\leq n^{-2/5}
and for
|z|\geq 2n^{-2/5}
. For
z
such that
n^{-2/5}\leq|z|\leq 2n^{-2/5}
, using (
11
) we get

|\overline{\partial}(s\chi_{n})|=|s\overline{\partial}(\chi_{n})|=O(e^{-n^{1/5}+O(\lambda_{\omega}^{-1}c_{h,3})n^{-1/5}})\cdot n^{1/4}\cdot O(c_{\omega,0}^{r+1}\lambda_{\omega}^{-r})=O(e^{-n^{1/6}})

since
n^{1/5}\geq O(\lambda_{\omega}^{-(2r+1)}c_{\omega,2}^{2r}c_{h,3})
. Then by (
6
), locally we have

\mu(z)=(1+O(\lambda_{\omega}^{-1}c_{\omega,1})|z|)dV,

it follows that

|\overline{\partial}(s\chi_{n})|_{L^{2}}\leq O(e^{-n^{1/6}})\implies|s_{n}-s\chi_{n}|_{L^{2}}\leq O(e^{-n^{1/6}}).

Now since
s\chi_{n}
is holomorphic in the polydisc of radius
n^{1/4}
, we may apply the mean value inequality along with (
6
) to obtain

|(s_{n}-s\chi_{n})(0)|\leq O(e^{-n^{1/6}})\left(1+O(\lambda_{\omega}^{-1}c_{\omega,1})n^{-2/5}\right)=O(e^{-n^{1/7}}).

We then get

|(s_{n}-s\chi_{n})(0)|\leq O(e^{-n^{1/7}})\implies|s_{n}(0)|\geq 1+O(e^{-n^{1/7}}).

Finally similar to (
7
), we verify that

|s\chi_{n}|^{2}_{L^{2}}\leq\left(1+O(\lambda_{\omega}^{-1}c_{\omega,1})n^{-1/4}\right)\int_{|z|\leq 2n^{-1/4}}e^{-n\varphi(z)}dV\leq\left(\frac{2\pi}{n}\right)^{r}\left(1+O(\lambda_{\omega}^{-1})c_{\omega,1}n^{-1/4}\right).

Hence using
s_{n}
as our section, we get that

\sup_{s\in H^{0}(X,L^{n})}\frac{|s(x)|}{|s|_{L^{2}}}\geq\left(\frac{n}{2\pi}\right)^{r}\left(1+O(\lambda_{\omega}^{-1}c_{\omega,1})n^{-1/4}\right)\left(1+O\left(e^{-n^{1/7}}\right)\right)\geq\left(\frac{n}{2\pi}\right)^{r}\left(1-\frac{1}{n^{1/8}}\right)

for
n\geq O(\lambda_{\omega}^{-(2r+1)}c_{\omega,2}^{2r}c_{h,3})^{8}
as desired.
∎

7.2.
Bounds on difference of archimedean volumes

With our upper and lower bounds on the Bergman kernel for positive line bundles, we can prove the following quantitative bound on the Bergman kernel for line bundles which are not necessarily even semipositive following
[
Yua08
, Proposition 2.12]
.

Proposition 7.4
.

Let
\overline{L}_{1},\overline{L}_{2}
be two positive line bundles over
X
. Let
\{s_{1},\ldots,s_{N}\}
be an orthonormal basis for the metrized line bundle
\overline{L}_{1}^{n_{1}}\otimes\overline{L}_{2}^{-n_{2}}
, where we use the measure
\mu_{1}=\frac{\omega_{1}^{\wedge r}}{r!}
for the
L^{2}
-norm. Then if
n_{i}\geq O(\lambda_{\omega_{i}}^{-(2r+1)}c_{\omega_{i},2}^{2r}c_{h_{i},3})^{8}
where
\omega_{i},h_{i}
are the
(1,1)
-form and hermitian metric of
\overline{L}_{i}
, we have

\sum_{i=1}^{N}|s_{i}(x)|^{2}\leq\left(\frac{n_{1}}{2\pi}\right)^{r}\left(1+\frac{1}{n_{1}^{1/8}}\right)\left(1+\frac{1}{n_{2}^{1/8}}\right)^{2}.

Proof.

We follow Yuan’s proof. Fix a
x\in X(\mathbb{C})
and construct an orthonormal basis for
H^{0}(X,\overline{L}^{n_{2}}_{2})
for the measure
\mu_{2}=\omega_{2}^{\wedge r}/r!
such that there is exactly one section
t
that is not vanishing at
x
. Then by Propositions
4.2
and
7.3
we know that

|t(x)|^{2}\geq\left(\frac{n_{2}}{\pi}\right)^{r}\left(1-\frac{1}{n_{2}^{1/8}}\right)\text{ and }\sup_{z\in X(\mathbb{C})}|t(z)|^{2}\leq\left(\frac{n_{2}}{\pi}\right)^{r}\left(1+\frac{1}{n_{2}^{1/8}}\right).

Now let
s_{1},\ldots,s_{N}
be an orthonormal basis for
\overline{L}_{1}^{n_{1}}\otimes\overline{L}_{2}^{-n_{2}}
and consider multiplication by
t
to obtain a linearly independent set of sections
\{ts_{1},\ldots,ts_{n}\}
for
\overline{L}_{1}^{n_{1}}
. We may choose
\{s_{i}\}
such that
\{ts_{i}\}
forms an orthogonal basis for the inner product on
\overline{L}_{1}^{n_{1}}
. Applying Proposition
4.2
tells us that

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{|ts_{i}(x)|^{2}}{|ts_{i}|_{L^{2}}}\leq\left(\frac{n_{1}}{2\pi}\right)^{r}\left(1+\frac{1}{n_{1}^{1/8}}\right).

We also have

|ts_{i}|_{L^{2}}\leq|s_{i}|_{L^{2}}\sup_{z\in X(\mathbb{C})}|t(z)|\leq\left(\frac{n_{2}}{\pi}\right)^{r}\left(1+\frac{1}{n_{2}^{1/8}}\right).

Hence we get

\sum_{i=1}^{N}|ts_{i}(x)|^{2}\leq\left(\frac{n_{1}}{2\pi}\right)^{r}\left(\frac{n_{2}}{\pi}\right)^{r}\left(1+\frac{1}{n_{1}^{1/8}}\right)\left(1+\frac{1}{n_{2}^{1/8}}\right)

\implies\sum_{i=1}^{N}|s_{i}(x)|^{2}\leq\left(\frac{n_{1}}{2\pi}\right)^{r}\left(1+\frac{1}{n_{1}^{1/8}}\right)\left(1+\frac{1}{n_{2}^{1/8}}\right)\left(1-\frac{1}{n_{2}^{1/8}}\right)^{-1}

where the implication follows from the lower bound on
|t(x)|^{2}
, as desired.
∎

We now use Proposition
7.4
to obtain a lower bound on the difference of volumes of the unit balls for the
L^{2}
-norm, under a perturbation
e^{-\varepsilon f}
where
f
is a smooth function. We fix a smooth function
f:X\to\mathbb{R}
along with a positive line bundle
\overline{L}
.

First, observe that if
f
is a smooth function satisfying
c_{f,3}\leq 2
, then the
(1,1)
-form given by
2i\partial\overline{\partial}f
is a hermitian matrix with coefficients bounded by
2
. Hence its eigenvalues are bounded from below by
-r
. We now choose some fixed positive line bundle
\overline{L}_{1}
such that
\omega_{1}
is the
(1,1)
-form associated to it then its smallest eigenvalue of
\omega
in our good charts is at least
3r
. Then
\omega_{1}+2\partial\overline{\partial}f
is the
(1,1)
-form for
\overline{L}_{1}(f)
, and we may take
\lambda_{\omega_{1}+2\partial\overline{\partial}f}
to be
1
. We may further assume that the underlying line bundle for
\overline{L}_{1}
is
L^{e}
for some positive integer
e
.

We will set
\varepsilon=\frac{1}{m}
for some positive integer
m
and set
n=mn^{\prime}
. We may then express
\overline{L}(\varepsilon f)^{n}
as

\overline{L}(\varepsilon f)^{n}=\left(\overline{L}^{m}\otimes\overline{L}_{1}(f)\right)^{n^{\prime}}\otimes\overline{L}_{1}^{-n^{\prime}}

Let
\mu^{\prime}
be the volume form associated to
\overline{L}^{m}\otimes\overline{L}_{1}
. Then we know that the total volume of
\mu^{\prime}
is exactly
\frac{(2\pi)^{r}}{r!}(L^{m}\otimes L_{1})^{(r)}=\frac{(2\pi)^{r}}{r!}\deg(L^{m+e})
. In particular if we consider the
L^{2}
-norm but with
\mu^{\prime}
normalized so that it has total volume
1
, we have to multiply the norm of our sections by
\frac{(2\pi)^{r}}{r!}\deg(L^{m+e})
. In particular, the leading coefficient of our bound in Proposition
7.4
changes to

\deg(L^{m+e})\frac{n^{\prime r}}{r!}.

Now by the Asymptotic Riemann–Roch
[
Laz04
, Theorem 1.1.24]
, we may replace this by

\dim H^{0}(X,L^{mn^{\prime}+en^{\prime}})\left(1+O\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)\right)

once
n
and
n^{\prime}
are both large enough depending only on the ample line bundle
L
. From now on, we will let
\mu^{\prime}
be normalized to have total volume
1
and we will let
\mu
be the normalized volume form associated to
\overline{L}^{m}
. Let
B_{n,L^{2}}
and
C_{n,L^{2}}
be the unit balls for the global sections of
\overline{L}(\varepsilon f)^{n}
and
\overline{L}^{n}
under the measure
\mu^{\prime}
.

Proposition 7.5
.

Assume that
f\leq 0
and that
c_{f,3}\leq 2
. For

n^{\prime}\geq O(\lambda_{\omega}^{-(2r+1)}c_{\omega,2}^{2r}c_{h,3}m^{2r+1})^{8}

and also
n^{\prime}
sufficiently large depending on
L
, we have the lower bound

\log\operatorname{vol}(B_{n,L^{2}})-\log\operatorname{vol}(C_{n,L^{2}})

\geq n\varepsilon\left(\dim H^{0}(X,L^{n+en^{\prime}})\right)\left(1+\frac{1}{n^{\prime 1/8}}\right)^{5}\left(\int_{X}fd\mu-O(\varepsilon)\right).

Proof.

We first want to obtain an upper bound on the distortion function for the line bundle
\overline{L}(\varepsilon f)^{n}
under the measure
\mu^{\prime}
. We rewrite

\overline{L}(\varepsilon f)^{n}=\left(\overline{L}^{m}\otimes\overline{L}_{1}(f)\right)^{n^{\prime}}\otimes\overline{L}_{1}^{-n^{\prime}}

and apply Proposition
7.4
. The curvature form for
\overline{L}^{m}\otimes\overline{L}_{1}
is
m\omega+\omega_{1}-dd^{c}f
and since
c_{f,3}\leq 2
, we obtain that if
n^{\prime}\geq O(\lambda_{\omega}^{-(2r+1)}c_{\omega,2}^{2r}c_{h,3}m^{2r+1})^{8}
and both
n
and
n^{\prime}
are sufficiently large depending only on
L
, we get the inequality

\sum_{i=1}^{N}|s_{i}(x)|^{2}\leq\dim H^{0}(X,L^{n+en^{\prime}})\left(1+\frac{1}{n^{\prime 1/8}}\right)^{4}\left(1+O\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)\right)

\leq\dim H^{0}(X,L^{n+en^{\prime}})\left(1+\frac{1}{n^{\prime 1/8}}\right)^{5}.

for any orthonormal basis
\{s_{i}\}
under
\mu^{\prime}
, where
|\cdot|
is the metric on
\overline{L}(\varepsilon f)^{n}
. Now pick an orthonormal basis
\{s_{i}\}
such that it is orthogonal for the inner product using the metric of
\overline{L}^{n}
. Then by Proposition
2.7
, we have

\log\operatorname{vol}(B_{n,L^{2}})-\log\operatorname{vol}(C_{n,L^{2}})=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\log\int_{X}|s_{i}|^{2}e^{n\varepsilon f}d\mu^{\prime}.

Applying Jensen’s, we get

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\log\int_{X}|s_{i}|^{2}e^{n\varepsilon f}d\mu^{\prime}\geq\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{X}\log(e^{n\varepsilon f})|s_{i}|^{2}d\mu^{\prime}=n\varepsilon\int_{X}f\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}|s_{i}|^{2}\right)d\mu^{\prime}.

Since
f\leq 0
, to get a lower bound it suffices to upper bound
\sum_{i=1}^{N}|s_{i}|^{2}
and so we obtain

n\varepsilon\int_{X}f\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}|s_{i}|^{2}\right)d\mu^{\prime}\geq n\varepsilon\left(\dim H^{0}(X,L^{n}\otimes L_{1}^{n^{\prime}}\right)\left(1+\frac{1}{n^{\prime 1/8}}\right)^{5}\int_{X}fd\mu^{\prime}.

Finally we write
\mu^{\prime}
as the normalized volume form corresponding to the top wedge power of
\omega^{\prime}=(\omega+\varepsilon(\omega_{1}+2i\partial\overline{\partial}f))
. Observe that
\omega^{\prime\wedge r}
will have total mass equal to the top self-intersection number of
L^{n+en^{\prime}}
while
\omega^{\wedge r}
will have total mass equal to the top self-intersection number of
L^{n}
. By Asymptotic Riemann–Roch
[
Laz04
, Theorem 1.1.24]
, the ratio between the two is within
1\pm O(\varepsilon)
and hence we may replace
\mu^{\prime}
with
\mu
and bound the error by
\varepsilon O(c_{f,2})=O(\varepsilon)
as desired since
|f|\leq 1
.

∎

We now apply Corollary
3.2
to convert our lower bound on volumes of
L^{2}
-balls to one on
\sup
-balls. Let
B_{n,\sup}
be the unit ball with respect to the
\sup
norm on
\overline{L}(\varepsilon f)
and
C_{n,\sup}
the unit ball for the
\sup
norm on
\overline{L}^{n}
.

Corollary 7.6
.

Assume that
f\leq 0
and
c_{f,3}\leq 2
. Then for

n^{\prime}\geq O(\lambda_{\omega}^{-(2r+1)}c_{\omega,2}^{2r}c_{h,3}m^{2r+1})^{8}

and also sufficiently large depending on
L
, we have

\frac{1}{\dim H^{0}(X,L^{n})}\left(\log B_{n,\sup}-\log C_{n,\sup}\right)\geq n\varepsilon\left(1+\frac{1}{n^{\prime 1/8}}\right)^{5}\left(\int_{X}fd\mu-O(\varepsilon)\right).

Proof.

We wish to apply Corollary
3.2
for the measure
\mu^{\prime}
. First, our measure is the wedge product
(\omega+\varepsilon(\omega_{1}+dd^{c}f))^{\wedge r}
normalized to have volume
1
. Now again, the total degree of this is
\deg(L^{n^{\prime}+e})
which grows at most
O(n^{\prime r})
. Since both are positive definite and
\lambda_{\omega}
is a lower bound for the eigenvalues of
\omega
and
\omega_{1}+2\partial\overline{\partial}f
in the charts
U_{\alpha}
, we have

\mu^{\prime}>\frac{1}{C(n^{\prime})^{r}}\lambda_{\omega}^{r}dV

for some
C>0
depending on
L
. Hence we may take
c^{\prime}
in Corollary
3.2
so that
c^{\prime-1}=O((n^{\prime})^{r}\lambda_{\omega}^{-r})
. The metric on
\overline{L}(\varepsilon f)
is given by
e^{h+\varepsilon f}
and we may bound
\log c_{e^{h+\varepsilon f},1}
by
O(\log c_{e^{h},1}+\log c_{f,1})
. We may then bound
\log c_{2}
by
-O(c_{h,0}+c_{f,0})
as it is bounded above by
\sup|h|+\sup|f|
. Thus by Corollary
3.2
, the difference between the
L^{2}
volumes and
\sup
volumes may be bounded by

O\left(\log c_{e^{h},1}+\log c_{e^{\varepsilon f},1}+c_{h,0}+c_{\varepsilon f,0}+\log\lambda_{\omega}+\log n\right).

We may then bound
\log c_{e^{h},1}
in terms of
O(c_{h,1}c_{h,0})
,
\log c_{e^{\varepsilon f},1}
in terms of
O(c_{f,1}c_{f,0})
. Since
c_{f,3}\leq 2
, certainly for
n\geq O(c_{h,3})^{8}
we have

\log\operatorname{vol}(B_{n,\sup})-\log\operatorname{vol}(B_{n,L^{2}})\geq-n\left(\frac{1}{n^{1/4}}\dim H^{0}(X,L^{n})\right).

We also trivially have

\log\operatorname{vol}(C_{n,\sup})-\log\operatorname{vol}(C_{n,L^{2}})\leq 0.

Now by Asymptotic Riemann–Roch
[
Laz04
, Theorem 1.1.24]
, for
n^{\prime}\geq O(\varepsilon^{-1})
and sufficiently large depending on
L
, we have

(12)

\frac{\dim H^{0}(X,L^{n+en^{\prime}})}{\dim H^{0}(X,L^{n})}\leq 1+O(\varepsilon).

Hence by Proposition
7.5
, we get for
n^{\prime}\geq O(\lambda_{\omega}^{-(2r+1)}c_{\omega,2}^{2r}c_{h,3}m^{2r+1})^{8}
, we have

\frac{1}{\dim H^{0}(X,L^{n})}\left(\log\operatorname{vol}(B_{n.\sup})-\log\operatorname{vol}(C_{n,\sup})\right)

\geq-n\varepsilon\left(1+\frac{1}{n^{\prime 1/8}}\right)^{5}\left(\int_{X}fd\mu-O(\varepsilon)\right)-\frac{1}{n^{1/4}}.

Since
n^{\prime}\geq m^{24}
, we have
\frac{1}{n^{1/4}}\leq\varepsilon^{3}
and so we obtain

\frac{1}{\dim H^{0}(X,L^{n})}\left(\log B_{n,\sup}-\log C_{n,\sup}\right)\geq n\varepsilon\left(1+\frac{1}{n^{\prime 1/8}}\right)^{5}\left(\int_{X}fd\mu-O(\varepsilon)\right)

as desired.
∎

8.
Quantitative Equidistribution for Smooth Varieties

Our aim in this section is to prove the main theorem of our paper. If
L
is an ample line bundle on
X
, we fix some power
L^{n}
such that
L^{n}
gives an embedding of
X
into projective space
\mathbb{P}^{N}
. We then say a subvariety
Y
of
X
has degree
D
with respect to
L
if
Y
has degree
D
in the embedding into
\mathbb{P}^{N}
.

Theorem 8.1
.

Let
X
be a smooth variety defined over a number field
K
and let
\varphi:X\to X
a polarized endomorphism of degree
d\geq 2
, i.e. there exists an ample line bundle
L
such that
\varphi^{*}L\simeq L^{\otimes d}
. Then there exists constants
c_{1}=c_{1}(K,\varphi),c_{2}=c_{2}(\varphi),c_{3}=c_{3}(K,\varphi)
such that for any
\varepsilon>0
and an archimedean place
v
of
K
, if
f:X(\overline{K}_{v})\to\mathbb{R}
is a smooth function, then

(1)

there exists a hypersurface
H(f,\varepsilon)
defined over
K
which has degree at most
c_{1}\varepsilon^{-c_{2}}
with respect to
L
,

(2)

for all
x\in X(\overline{K})
with Galois orbit
F_{x}
, if
x\not\in H(f,\varepsilon)
then

\left|\frac{1}{|F_{x}|}\sum_{y\in F_{x}}f(y)-\int f(y)d\mu_{\varphi,v}(y)\right|\leq c_{f,3}\left(\frac{\widehat{h}_{\varphi}(x)}{\varepsilon}+c_{3}\varepsilon\right).

Given our smooth function
f
, we first divide it by
c_{f,3}
to get a new function
f
which satisfies
c_{f,3}\leq 2
. It then suffices to prove that

\left|\frac{1}{|F_{x}|}\sum_{y\in F_{x}}f(y)-\int f(y)d\mu_{\varphi,v}(y)\right|\leq\frac{\widehat{h}_{\varphi}(x)}{\varepsilon}+c_{3}\varepsilon

for functions
f
satisfying
c_{f,3}\leq 2
and obtain the general version by multiplying
c_{f,3}
back in.

By replacing
L
with a high enough power, we may assume that
L
embeds
X
into
\mathbb{P}^{N}
with
L
being the restriction of
O(1)
, and that
\varphi
extends to a morphism
\tilde{\varphi}:\mathbb{P}^{N}\to\mathbb{P}^{N}
of degree
d
. We may then lift
\tilde{\varphi}
to a homogeneous map
F:\mathbb{A}^{N+1}\to\mathbb{A}^{N+1}
.

For each place
v
of
M_{K}
, we may define a homogeneous Green’s function using
F
by

G_{v}(x)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log||F^{n}(x)||_{v}}{d^{n}}\text{ for }x\in\mathbb{A}^{N+1}(\mathbb{C}_{v}),

where
||(x_{0},\ldots,x_{n+1})||_{v}=\max\{|x_{0}|_{v},\ldots,|x_{n+1}|_{v}\}
. We can then use
G_{v}
to define a metric on
L
for each place
v
by

\log|s(x)|_{v}=\log|P(\tilde{x})|_{v}-G_{v}(\tilde{x})\text{ for }x\in\mathbb{P}^{N}(\mathbb{C}_{v}),

where
\tilde{x}
is any lift of
x
to
\mathbb{A}^{N+1}(\mathbb{C}_{v})
. Due to the homogeneity of
G_{v}
, our definition is independent of the lift. Restricting to
X
, this turns
L
into a semipositive adelic line bundle
\overline{L}
in the sense of Zhang
[
Yua08
, Section 3.5]
, although it would not be important for us to know this. It is easy to see that its associated height on
X(\overline{K})
is the canonical height
\widehat{h}_{\varphi}(x)
, which may also be defined as

\widehat{h}_{\varphi}(x)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{h(\varphi^{n}(x))}{d^{n}}.

where
h(x)
is the standard Weil height on
\mathbb{P}^{N}
. Let
L_{n}=L^{d^{n}}
. Then there is an induced adelic meric on
L_{n}
coming from
L
and we will denote the adelic line bundle by
\overline{L}_{n}
and the metrized line bundle on
X(\mathbb{C}_{v})
by
\overline{L}_{n,v}
.

The global sections of
L_{n}
, denoted by
H^{0}(X,L_{n})
, form a
K
-vector space. We let
M_{n}
be the sub
O_{K}
-module of sections
s
satisfying
|s|_{v.\sup}\leq 1
for all non-archimedean places
v
, where
\sup
denotes the supremum over
X(\mathbb{C}_{v})
. Then
M_{n}
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-submodule of maximal rank of
H^{0}(X,L_{n})
.

We now consider
V_{n}=H^{0}(X,L_{n})\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{R}
. Then
V_{n}
is a
\mathbb{R}
-vector space and we can give it a sup norm as follows. We have

V_{n}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}\simeq\bigoplus_{v\text{ arch. }}H^{0}(X(\mathbb{C}_{v}),L_{n})

where
X(\mathbb{C}_{v})
denotes the complex variety obtained by embedding
K
into
\mathbb{C}
via the place
v
. Each
H^{0}(X(\mathbb{C}_{v}),L_{n})
has a supremum norm coming from the metric on
L_{n}
for the place
v
, and taking the supremum of each of them defines a sup norm on
V_{n}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}
and hence on
V_{n}
which we will denote by
|\cdot|_{\sup}
.

We now let
C_{n,\sup}
be the unit ball of sections
s\in V_{n}
satisfying
|s|_{\sup}\leq 1
. Fix a Haar measure on
V_{n}
and let
\operatorname{vol}M_{n}
denote the volume of the fundamental domain of the lattice
M_{n}
in
V_{n}
. The quantity

\log\operatorname{vol}C_{n,\sup}-\log\operatorname{vol}M_{n}

does not depend on the choice of our Haar measure. Again, using arithmetic Hilbert–Samuel
[
Zha95
, Theorem 1.4]
, one can deduce that

(13)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{d^{n}\dim V_{n}}\left(\log\operatorname{vol}C_{n,\sup}-\log\operatorname{vol}M_{n}\right)=0.

We now regularize the metric at each archimedean place
v
to make it smooth so that one can apply the results of Sections
4
and
7
. Fix an
\varepsilon=d^{-k}
for some positive integer
k
. The aim is to regularize our metric so that the difference from the original metric and the new metric on
\overline{L}_{n}
is at most
d^{n}\varepsilon^{2}
.

We do this by directly regularizing the metric of the line bundle
O(d^{n})
on
\mathbb{P}^{r}
. Fix an archimedean place
v
and let
|\cdot|
be the metric on
O(d^{n})
. We let
|\cdot|_{\operatorname{FS}}
be the Fubini-Study metric on
O(d^{n})
. Then for any section
s
, the expression
\log|s(x)|^{-1}-\log|s(x)|^{-1}_{\operatorname{FS}}
defines for us a continuous function
f
on
\mathbb{P}^{r}
satisfying
i\partial\overline{\partial}f\geq-d^{n}\omega_{\operatorname{FS}}
, where
\omega_{\operatorname{FS}}
is the
(1,1)
-form for the Fubini-Study metric on
O(1)
, as the current
i\partial\overline{\partial}\log|s(x)|^{-1}_{v}+\pi[\operatorname{div}(s)]
is semi-positive.

We now regularize the continuous function
f
as in Proposition
2.3
. Fix a choice of good charts for our
\mathbb{P}^{r}
. Then given
\theta
with
|\theta|<1
, we can regularize and obtain
f_{\theta}
which by Lemma
2.5
, satisfies
dd^{c}f_{\theta}\geq-d^{n}(1+O(\theta))\omega
. Furthermore, we know that
f
is Holder continuous for some exponent
\kappa
and constant of the form
O_{\varphi}(d^{n})
. Hence taking
\theta=\varepsilon^{2\kappa^{-1}}
, by Lemma
2.4
we obtain that

|f_{\theta}-f|\leq d^{n}O_{\varphi}(\varepsilon^{2}).

Then Proposition
2.3
implies that

c_{f_{\theta},k}\leq c_{k}c_{f,0}\varepsilon^{-2\kappa^{-1}(2r^{2}+4r+k)}.

We can define a metric
|\cdot|^{\prime}
on
O(d^{n})
by taking
f_{\theta}+\log|\cdot|_{\operatorname{FS}}
and we hence obtain a smooth metric
|\cdot|^{\prime}
on
O(d^{n})
satisfying

(14)

c_{h^{\prime},k}\leq d^{n}c_{h,0}\varepsilon^{-2\kappa^{-1}(2r^{2}+4r+k)}=O_{\varphi}(d^{n})\varepsilon^{-2\kappa^{-1}(2r^{2}+4r+k)}

and

(15)

h^{\prime}-h=d^{n}O_{\varphi}(\varepsilon^{2}).

where
h
is the original canonical metric on
O(d^{n})
for the archimedean place
v
. Now restricting to
X
and using our choice of good charts on
X
, we get a metric on
L_{n}
satisfying the same inequalities, up to different constants by Lemma
2.2
.

Now for each archimedean place
v
, our line bundle
L_{n}
has a smooth hermitian metric
h^{\prime}_{v}
that satisfies
i\partial\overline{\partial}h^{\prime}_{v}\geq-d^{n}O_{\varphi}(\varepsilon^{2})\omega
, where
\omega
is the form corresponding to the Fubini-Study metric. To make it positive, we consider modfiying the hermitian metric by setting it to
(1-c\varepsilon^{2})h^{\prime}_{v}+cd^{n}\varepsilon^{2}h_{\operatorname{FS}}
for some constant
c=c(\varphi)>0
, such that our new metric which we will again denote by
h^{\prime}_{v}
, satisfies

(16)

i\partial\overline{\partial}h^{\prime}_{v}\geq\varepsilon^{2}d^{n}\omega_{\operatorname{FS}}.

Hence for each archimedean place
v
, we obtain a metric
h^{\prime}_{v}
satisfying (
14
) and (
15
) and also
i\partial\overline{\partial}h^{\prime}_{v}\geq\varepsilon^{2}\omega_{\operatorname{FS}}
.

Now let
\overline{L}^{\prime}_{n}
be the adelic bundle with the hermitian metrics at the archimedean place being
h^{\prime}
instead of
h
and let
C^{\prime}_{n}
be the unit ball of sections
s\in V_{n}
satisfying
|s|_{v,\sup}\leq 1
for all archimedean places
v
. Since
|h^{\prime}-h|\leq d^{n}O_{\varphi}(\varepsilon^{2})
, we deduce the following lower bound.

Proposition 8.2
.

Let
\varepsilon=d^{-k}
and assume that
n\geq 4k
. Then there exists
c=c(\varphi)>0
such that we have

\frac{1}{\dim V_{n}}(\log\operatorname{vol}(C^{\prime}_{n,\sup})-\log\operatorname{vol}(M_{n}))\geq-c(\varepsilon^{2}d^{n}+[K:\mathbb{Q}]^{2}h(\alpha)).

Proof.

Since
|h^{\prime}-h|\leq d^{n}O_{\varphi}(\varepsilon^{2})
, we have

\left|\log\operatorname{vol}(C_{n,\sup})-\log\operatorname{vol}(C^{\prime}_{n,\sup})\right|\leq d^{n}(\dim V_{n})O_{\varphi}(\varepsilon^{2}).

As
n\geq 4k
, we have
d^{n/2}\leq d^{n}\varepsilon^{2}
and so combnining the above inequality with Proposition
5.2
, we get our inequality as desired.
∎

8.1.
Proof of quantitative equidistribution

We now move to estimating the volume for

-norms instead of the supremum norm. Recall that we have the isomorphism

H^{0}(X,L_{n})\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{C}\simeq\bigoplus_{v\text{ arch. }}H^{0}(X(\mathbb{C}_{v}),L_{n})

where we count complex conjugate pairs separately. For each archimedean place
v
, recall that we can metrize the line bundle
L_{n}
with our smooth positive metric

which induces an hermitian inner product
\langle\,,\,\rangle_{v}
on the fibers. Then if we fix a measure
\mu_{v}
of total volume
1

\mu_{v}
. As in the paragraph before Proposition
7.5
, we fix some positive line bundle
\overline{L}_{1}
on
X(\mathbb{C})

L^{e}

\omega_{1}
is the
(1,1)

\omega_{1}
is
\geq 3r
. Recall that
\varepsilon=d^{-k}

\mu^{\prime}_{v}
be the volume form, normalized to have measure
1

\overline{L}_{n,v}^{d^{k}}\otimes\overline{L}_{1}
where
\overline{L}_{n,v}
is the metrized line bundle corresponding to our adelic line bundle

at the place

C^{\prime}_{n,L^{2}}
denote the unit ball in
V_{n}
satisfying
|s|_{L^{2}}\leq 1
, we have the inequality

(17)

\frac{1}{\dim V_{n}}\left(\log C^{\prime}_{n,L^{2}}-\log C^{\prime}_{n,\sup}\right)\geq-\log[K:\mathbb{Q}].

Now let’s fix an archimedean place
v

. We now perturb the metric of
\overline{L}_{n}
and
\overline{L}^{\prime}_{n}

. If

has a complex conjugate pair

, we also perturb the metric at

by

. We denote our new adelic line bundle by

and
\overline{L}^{\prime}_{n}(\varepsilon f)
respectively. Using our new metric, this induces a supremum norm on

and also a

.

Let
B^{\prime}_{n,L^{2}}
and

denote the unit ball of

under the

-norm and
\sup

\overline{L}^{\prime}_{n}(\varepsilon f)
respectively. Our aim is to get a lower bound of

. We first bound
\log\operatorname{vol}B^{\prime}_{n,L^{2}}-\log\operatorname{vol}C^{\prime}_{n,L^{2}}
from below.

Lemma 8.3
.

Let

if

\log\operatorname{vol}B^{\prime}_{n,L^{2}}-\log\operatorname{vol}C^{\prime}_{n,L^{2}}=n_{v}\left(\log\operatorname{vol}B_{n,v,L^{2}}-\log\operatorname{vol}C^{\prime}_{n,v,L^{2}}\right)

where

Proof.

.

Proposition 8.5
.

Assume that
f\leq 0
and let
\kappa
be a Holder exponent for the Green’s function
G_{v}
associated to

. Then for all

\frac{1}{d^{n}\dim V_{n}}\left(\log\operatorname{vol}B_{n,\sup}-\log\operatorname{vol}M_{n}\right)\geq\frac{\varepsilon n_{v}}{[K:\mathbb{Q}]}\int_{X}fd\mu_{\varphi,v}-O_{\varphi}\left(\varepsilon^{2}+\frac{[K:\mathbb{Q}]^{2}h(\alpha)}{d^{n}}\right)

where
\mu_{\varphi,v}
is the equilibrium measure for
\varphi
at the archimedean place

Proof.

Combining Propositions
8.2
,
8.4
along with the inequalities from (
17
) and (
18
), we obtain

\frac{1}{d^{n}\dim V_{n}}(\log\operatorname{vol}B^{\prime}_{n,\sup}-\log\operatorname{vol}M_{n})\geq\frac{\varepsilon n_{v}}{[K:\mathbb{Q}]}\int_{X}fd\mu_{v}-O_{\varphi}\left(\varepsilon^{2}+\frac{[K:\mathbb{Q}]^{2}h(\alpha)}{d^{n}}\right).

Now by (
15

h^{\prime}
and

differ by

and so we have

\frac{1}{d^{n}\dim V_{n}}\left(\log\operatorname{vol}B_{n,\sup}-\log\operatorname{vol}B^{\prime}_{n,\sup}\right)\geq\frac{\varepsilon n_{v}}{[K:\mathbb{Q}]}\int_{X}fd\mu_{v}-O_{\varphi}\left(\varepsilon^{2}+\frac{[K:\mathbb{Q}]h(\alpha)}{d^{n}}\right).

It now suffices to obtain a bound for

\left(\int_{X}fd\mu_{v}-\int_{X}fd\mu_{\varphi,v}\right).

Let
\{U_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in I}
be our choice of good charts, with

being a finite set. Recall that each

is isomorphic to the polydisc
\mathbb{D}_{3}
By using a partition of unity, we may assume that

\mathbb{D}_{3/2}
of one of our charts

u=i\frac{h_{\alpha}}{d^{n}}
and

. Then

and

are p.s.h. functions and locally within

d\mu_{v}=c(dd^{c}u)^{\wedge r}\text{ and }d\mu^{\prime}_{v}=c(dd^{c}u^{\prime})^{\wedge r}

for some constant

We write

d\mu_{v}-d\mu^{\prime}_{v}=c\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}\left((dd^{c}u)^{\wedge(r-i)}\wedge(dd^{c}u^{\prime})^{\wedge i}-(dd^{c}u)^{\wedge(r-i-1)}\wedge(dd^{c}u^{\prime})^{\wedge(i+1)}\right).

We may rewrite each term in the sum as

dd^{c}\left((u-u^{\prime})\wedge(dd^{c}u)^{\wedge(r-i-1)}\wedge(dd^{c}u^{\prime})^{\wedge i}\right)

where this expression makes sense as both

and

are p.s.h.
We now apply the Chern–Levine–Nirenberg inequality
[
Dem12
, Chapter 3, 3.3]
to

to obtain that

||(u^{\prime}-u)(dd^{c}u)^{\wedge(r-i-1)}\wedge(dd^{c}u^{\prime})^{\wedge i}||_{\mathbb{D}_{3/2}}

\leq C|u|^{r-i-1}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}_{2})}|u^{\prime}|^{i}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}_{2})}|u-u^{\prime}|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}_{2})}

for some constant

independent of

. But since

we may bound this by

and hence we obtain that

Finally as

\int fdd^{c}\left((u-u^{\prime})\wedge(dd^{c}u)^{\wedge(r-i-1)}\wedge(dd^{c}u^{\prime})^{\wedge i}\right)=\int(dd^{c}f)\left((u-u^{\prime})\wedge(dd^{c}u)^{\wedge(r-i-1)}\wedge(dd^{c}u^{\prime})^{\wedge i}\right)

\leq O_{\varphi}(\varepsilon^{2}\cdot c_{f,2})\leq O_{\varphi}(\varepsilon^{2})

as

. Hence we may replace

with

at the cost of an

∎

We now prove Theorem
8.1
.

Proof of Theorem
8.1
.

Given a smooth
f:X(\mathbb{C}_{v})\to\mathbb{R}
, we first add a constant to it so that
\int_{X}fd\mu_{\varphi,v}=0
. We may also divide
f
by
c_{f,3}
and so we may assume that
c_{f,3}\leq 2
. Given
\varepsilon>0
, we round it down to the nearest
d^{-k}
. This changes our constants by at most a bounded power of
d
. It now suffices to show the existence of
c_{1},c_{2},c_{3}>0
such that for
x\in X(\overline{K})
outside of a hypersurface of degree at most
c_{1}\varepsilon^{-c_{2}}
, we have

\frac{1}{|F_{x}|}\sum_{y\in F_{x}}f(y)\geq-\left(\frac{\widehat{h}_{\varphi}(x)}{\varepsilon}+c_{3}\varepsilon\right)

for we can deduce the same inequality for
-f
to get the corresponding upper bound. Let
B_{n,\sup}
be the unit ball of
V_{n}
for the adelic line bundle
\overline{L}_{n}(\varepsilon f)
. Then for
n\geq O_{r}(\kappa^{-1}k)+O_{\varphi}(1)
, we claim that

\frac{1}{d^{n}\dim V_{n}}(\log\operatorname{vol}B_{n,\sup}-\log\operatorname{vol}M_{n})\geq O_{\varphi}\left(\varepsilon^{2}+\frac{[K:\mathbb{Q}]h(\alpha)}{d^{n}}\right)

Indeed, we set
f^{\prime}=f-c
for some constant
c>0
such that
f^{\prime}\leq 0
and apply Proposition
8.5
. Scaling back
f^{\prime}
to
f
then replaces
\int_{X}f^{\prime}d\mu_{\varphi,v}
with
\int_{X}fd\mu_{\varphi,v}
. Now for some suitable
c_{3}=c_{3}(K,\varphi)
, the corresponding unit ball
B^{\prime}_{n,\sup}
for the metric on
\overline{L}(\varepsilon f+c_{3}\varepsilon^{2})
satisfies

\frac{1}{d^{n}\dim V_{n}}(\log\operatorname{vol}B^{\prime}_{n,\sup}-\log\operatorname{vol}M_{n})\geq\varepsilon^{2}\implies\log\operatorname{vol}B^{\prime}_{n,\sup}-\log\operatorname{vol}M_{n}\geq\varepsilon^{2}d^{n}\dim V_{n}.

As
d^{n}\varepsilon^{2}\geq\log 2
, we may apply Minkowski’s theorem to conclude that there exists a section
s
of
H^{0}(X,L_{n})
such that
|s|_{\sup,v}\leq 1
for all places
v
of
\overline{L}_{n}(\varepsilon f+c_{3}\varepsilon^{2})
.

For
x\not\in\operatorname{div}(s)
, we use our small section

to compute the height of

with respect to
\overline{L}_{n}(\varepsilon f+c_{3}\varepsilon^{2})
, which we will denote by

. As our section

. On the other hand, we have

h_{L}(x)=\widehat{h}_{\varphi}(x)+\frac{\varepsilon}{|F_{x}|}\sum_{y\in F_{x}}f(y)+c_{3}\varepsilon^{2}.

Dividing by
\varepsilon
, we obtain that

\frac{1}{|F_{x}|}\sum_{y\in F_{x}}f(y)\geq\frac{-\widehat{h}_{\varphi}(x)}{\varepsilon}-c_{3}\varepsilon

which is what we wanted, as desired. Our inequalities holds as long as

, which is a hypersurface defined over
K
of degree

intersected with

. Since we may take

, this is a hypersurface of degree

where

and

as desired.
∎

9.
Quantitative Equidistribution of Periodic Points on Surfaces

We now give our first application of Theorem
8.1
. Let
X
be a surface,
\varphi:X\to X
be a polarized endomorphism of degree
d\geq 2
and let
\operatorname{Per}_{n}
be the set the points of period
n
. When
X=\mathbb{P}^{2}
Briend–Duval
[
BD01
]
has shown that the discrete measure
\frac{1}{|\operatorname{Per}_{n}|}\sum_{x\in\operatorname{Per}_{n}}\delta_{x}
converges to the equilibrium measure
\mu_{\varphi}
. In general, equidistribution of periodic points has been shown by Dinh–Nguyen–Truong
[
DNT15
]
.

When
\varphi
is defined over a number field
K
, we will prove that an exponential rate of convergence holds, answering
[
DS17
, Problem 7]
in the case where
\varphi
is defined over a number field.

To do so, we will bound the number of elements of
\operatorname{Per}_{n}
that can lie on a geometrically irreducible degree
e
curve
C
in terms of
e
. By degree, we mean the induced degree after embedding
X\xhookrightarrow{}\mathbb{P}^{N}
so that
\varphi
extends to
F:\mathbb{P}^{N}\to\mathbb{P}^{N}
. We will split into two cases, the first is when
C
is periodic of period
n
and the second is when
C
is not. We handle the second case first.

Proposition 9.1
.

Let
C\subseteq X
be a degree
e
curve, irreducible over
\mathbb{C}
, such that
f^{n}(C)\not=C
. Then there are at most
e^{2}d^{n}
many points of period
n
that lie on
C
.

Proof.

Let
L=O(1)
on
\mathbb{P}^{N}
. By the projection formula, we know that

(F^{n})^{*}L\cdot C=F^{n}_{*}C\cdot L.

Since
(f^{n})^{*}L=O(d^{n})
, the LHS is
ed^{n}
. The RHS is at least
\deg(\varphi^{n}(C))
and so we get that
\deg(\varphi^{n}(C))\leq ed^{n}
. Now if
x
is a periodic point of period
n
that lies in
C
, then it also lies in
\varphi^{n}(C)
and hence lies in the intersection
C\cap\varphi^{n}(C)
. Since
C
is irreducible over
\mathbb{C}
and is not equal to
\varphi^{n}(C)
, by Bezout’s we have

|C\cap\varphi^{n}(C)|\leq\deg(\varphi^{n}(C))\cdot\deg(C)\leq e^{2}d^{n}

and hence there are at most
e^{2}d^{n}
many points of period
n
that lie on
C
.
∎

We now handle the other case, which is when
C
is periodic of period
n
. Let
C^{\prime}
be a smooth model of
C
and so
\varphi^{n}
induces a birational map
C^{\prime}\to C^{\prime}
. It then extends to an endomorphism
\tilde{\varphi^{n}}:C^{\prime}\to C^{\prime}
.

Proposition 9.2
.

The number of fixed points of
\tilde{\varphi^{n}}
on
C^{\prime}
is at most
d^{n}+2d^{n/2}+1
.

Proof.

If
C^{\prime}
is of genus
g\geq 2
, it follows that
\tilde{\varphi^{n}}
is of finite order. Then there exists some
m
such that
f^{nm}
induces the identity map on
C
but this is impossible as this would imply that
\varphi^{nm}
on
X
has infinitely many fixed points. Hence
C^{\prime}
is either isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{1}
or to an elliptic curve
E
.

If
C^{\prime}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{1}
or
\mathbb{E}
, then by Lemma 2.2 of
[
Mat+22
]
, we know that the first dynamical degree of
\tilde{\varphi^{n}}
is at most the first dynamical degree of
\varphi^{n}
, which is
d^{n}
. We now apply Proposition 6.2 of
[
Mat+22
]
to conclude that there are at most
d^{n}+2d^{n/2}+1
many fixed points for
\tilde{\varphi^{n}}
as desired.
∎

Putting both propositions together, we obtain the following bound on not necessarily irreducible curves.

Proposition 9.3
.

Let
C
be a not necessarily irreducible curve of degree
e
. Then there are at most
3e^{2}d^{n}
many periodic points of period
n
that lie on
C
.

Proof.

We write
C
as an union of irreducicble curves over
\mathbb{C}
, say
C_{1},C_{2},\ldots,C_{m}
. Then if
\deg(C_{i})=e_{i}
, we have
\sum_{i=1}^{m}e_{i}=e
. Let
C_{1},\ldots,C_{k}
be curves that are not periodic of period
n
and let
C_{k+1},\ldots,C_{m}
be those that are periodic of period
m
. Then
\sum_{i=1}^{k}e_{i}\leq e-(m-k)
. Applying Proposition
9.2
to
C_{k+1},\ldots,C_{m}
, we obtain that the number of periodic points of period
n
on each of them is at most
d^{n}+2d^{n/2}+1
and in total there is at most
(m-k)(d^{n}+2d^{n/2}+1)
periodic points of period
n
among
C_{k+1},\ldots,C_{m}
.

For
C_{1},\ldots,C_{k}
, we apply Proposition
9.1
to obtain that they have at most
d^{n}\sum_{i=1}^{k}e_{i}^{2}
many periodic points of period
n
. Since
\sum_{i=1}^{k}e_{i}^{2}\leq(\sum_{i=1}^{k}e_{i})^{2}
, this is at most
d^{n}(e-(m-k))^{2}
. Putting it together, we have at most

d^{n}(e-(m-k))^{2}+(m-k)(d^{n}+2d^{n/2}+1)\leq e^{2}(d^{n}+2d^{n/2}+1)\leq 3e^{2}d^{n}

periodic points of period
n
that lie on
C
.
∎

We can now prove our exponential rate of convergence for periodic points of
\varphi
.

Theorem 9.4
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective surface over a number field
K
and
\varphi:X\to X
be a polarized endomorphism of degree
d\geq 2
. Then there exists constants
C>0
and
\lambda>1
, depending on
\varphi
, such that if
v
is an archimedean place of
K
, then for any smooth function
f:X(\mathbb{C}_{v})\to\mathbb{R}
, we have

\left|\frac{1}{|\operatorname{Per}_{n}|}\sum_{x\in\operatorname{Per}_{n}}f(x)-\int fd\mu_{\varphi,v}\right|\leq Cc_{f,3}\lambda^{-n}.

Proof.

Let
f
be a given smooth function and let’s assume that
K
is the minimal field of definition for
\varphi
. Let
c_{1},c_{2},c_{3}
be the constants in Theorem
8.1
for
\varphi
and
K
. We set
\varepsilon=d^{n/4c_{1}}
. We write
\operatorname{Per}_{n}
as an union of
\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{K}/K)
-orbits
F_{1},\ldots,F_{m}
. Now by Theorem
8.1
, there exists a curve
C_{n}
defined over
K
, of degree
c_{1}\varepsilon^{-c_{2}}=c_{1}d^{n/4}
, such that if
x
is a periodic point that doesn’t lie on it, we have

\left|\frac{1}{|F_{x}|}\sum_{y\in F_{x}}f(y)-\int fd\mu_{\varphi,v}\right|\leq c_{3}\varepsilon=c_{3}d^{-n/4c_{2}}.

Now by Proposition
9.3
, we know that
C_{n}
contains at most
3c_{1}^{2}d^{3n/2}
many periodic points of period
n
. Let
F_{1},\ldots,F_{k}
be the
\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{K}/K)
-orbits in
\operatorname{Per}_{n}
that do not lie on
C_{n}
and let
F
be their union. Then we also have

\left|\frac{1}{|F|}\sum_{y\in F}f(y)-\int fd\mu_{\varphi,v}\right|\leq c_{3}c_{f,3}d^{-n/4c_{2}}.

Now there are at most
3c_{1}^{2}d^{3n/2}
remaining periodic points of period
n
, and by
[
DZ23
, Theorem 1.1]
, we know that
|\operatorname{Per}_{n}|\geq\alpha d^{2n}
for some constant
\alpha>0
depending on
\varphi
. Hence the remaining points can only add an error of
3\alpha^{-1}c_{1}^{2}c_{f,0}d^{-n/2}
. As we may take
c_{1},c_{2}\geq 1
, this is
\leq 3\alpha^{-1}c_{1}^{2}c_{f,3}d^{-n/4c_{2}}
and so we obtain

\left|\frac{1}{|\operatorname{Per}_{n}|}\sum_{x\in\operatorname{Per}_{n}}f(x)-\int fd\mu_{\varphi,v}\right|\leq 3\alpha^{-1}c_{1}^{2}c_{f,3}C_{3}d^{-n/4c_{2}}.

We may then take
\lambda=d^{1/4c_{2}}
and
C=3\alpha^{-1}c_{1}^{2}c_{f,3}C_{3}
as desired.
∎

10.
Bound on Preperiodic Points of Bounded Degree

We now turn into our next application of Theorem

. Let
X
be a smooth projective variety over a number field
K
and
\varphi:X\to X
a polarized endomorphism of degree

. Our first goal is to control the geometry of preperiodic points of

that are of degree

over our base field

. We will refer to this set as

Fix an archimedean place

of

along with a choice of good charts

for

such that
\mu_{\varphi,v}(D(x,r))\leq Cr^{\kappa}
for all sufficiently small

\mathbb{D}_{1}
for some chart

Lemma 10.1

There exists a chart

\mu(\mathbb{D}_{1})>0

Proof.

This is clear as the union of the polydiscs

∎

Now let’s fix a positive integer

Proposition 10.2

Fix a positive integer

. Then there exists a constant

such that for any positive integer

, there are

cubes

As noted before, we can find

and

. Also, we may find some chart locally isomorphic to

\delta(c^{\prime}D^{-2/\kappa})^{r}

Let

be this cube. Then

nc^{\prime}D^{-2/\kappa}
and so
\mu_{\varphi}(nR)\leq nc^{\prime}CD^{-2}
. Thus if
c^{\prime}=\frac{\delta}{nC}
, the remaining cubes which do not intersect
nR
will have total measure at least
\delta(1-D^{-2})
left. Choosing
R_{2}
from the remaining cubes will ensure that
2R_{i}
remains disjoint with each other. We may choose
R_{2}
such that
\mu(R_{2})\geq\delta(1-D^{-2})(c^{\prime}D^{-2/\kappa})^{r}
and repeating this argument, we may choose
R_{k}
such that

\mu(R_{k})\geq\delta(1-kD^{-2})(cD^{-2/\kappa})^{r}\geq(1-D^{-1})(cD^{-2/\kappa})^{r}\geq\frac{1}{2}(\delta c^{\prime})(D^{-2/\kappa})^{r}.

Hence taking
c=\delta c^{\prime}=\frac{\delta^{2}}{nC}
gives us the result we want.
∎

We now apply Theorem
8.1
to obtain the existence of a hypersurface
H
of controlled degree that contains
\operatorname{Prep}_{\varphi,D}
.

Theorem 10.3
.

There exists a constant
e=e(\varphi)>0
such that for any positive integer
D>0
, there exists a hypersurface
H
of degree at most
O_{\varphi,K}(D^{e})
, such that
\operatorname{Prep}_{\varphi,D}\subseteq H
.

Proof.

Given
D\geq 2
, we apply Proposition
10.2
to find
D
cubes
\{R_{i}\}_{i=1}^{D}
, of length
cD^{-2\kappa}
, and having measure
\mu(R_{i})\geq\frac{1}{2}cD^{-2r\kappa}
and such that
2R_{i}
are all disjoint. For each
i
, we let
f_{i}
be a non-negative smooth function that is identically
1
on
R_{i}
and zero outside of
2R_{i}
. If
f
is a smooth function that satisfies this property when
R
is the unit cube, we may take
f_{i}(x)
to be a translation of
f(c^{-1}D^{2\kappa}x)
. In particular, we have

c_{f_{i},3}=O_{\varphi}(D^{6\kappa}).

Now by Theorem
8.1
, for
\varepsilon>0
and
x\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
with
\widehat{h}_{\varphi}(x)=0
, there exists a hypersurface of degree
H(f_{i},\varepsilon)
of degree
O_{\varphi}(\varepsilon^{-c_{2}})
such that

\left|\frac{1}{|F_{x}|}\sum_{y\in F_{x}}f(y)-\int f_{i}(y)d\mu_{\varphi}(y)\right|\leq c_{f_{i},3}c_{3}\varepsilon

for all
x\not\in H(f_{i},\varepsilon)
.
Since
\mu_{\varphi}(R_{i})\geq\frac{1}{2}cD^{-2r\kappa}
, we have

\int f_{i}(y)d\mu_{\varphi}(y)\geq\frac{1}{2}cD^{-2r\kappa}.

Thus if we take
\varepsilon
such that
c_{f_{i},2}c_{3}\varepsilon<\frac{1}{2}cD^{-2r\kappa}
, it follows that for
x\not\in H(f_{i},\varepsilon)
, we have
f_{i}(y)>0
for some
y\in F_{x}
. Hence
y\in 2R_{i}
for some
y\in F_{x}
.

Repeating this for each
R_{i}
gives us that outside a hypersurface of degree
O_{\varphi}(D\varepsilon^{-c_{1}})
, we must have
y\in 2R_{i}
for some
y\in F_{x}
for each
i
. But since
2R_{i}
’s are disjoint from each other, it must be that
|F_{x}|\geq D
and so the union of
H(f_{i},\varepsilon)
must contain all preperiodic points of algebraic degree
<D
. Now it suffices to note that
\varepsilon
may be taken as
D^{-O(r\kappa)}
and so our hypersurface is of degree at most
O_{\varphi,K}(D^{e})
, where
e
depends on
\varphi
as desired.
∎

As remarked in the introduction, when
r=1
, Theorem
10.3
implies that there are at most
O_{\varphi,K}(D^{e})
many preperiodic points of degree
\leq D
over
K
. This is different from Baker’s results
[
Bak06
]
in that Baker requires us to fix a single extension
L
of degree
D
whereas we can vary over all extensions of degree
D
. We will show that in the dimension one case, we can also deduce this from Favre–Rivera-Letelier’s quantitative equidistribution theorem.

Proposition 10.4
.

Let
\varphi:\mathbb{P}^{1}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
be a rational map defined over a number field
K
of degree
d\geq 2
. Then there exists
e=e(\varphi)
such that there are at most
O_{\varphi,K}(D^{e})
many preperiodic points of degree
\leq D
over
K
.

Proof.

We give a sketch. By Proposition
10.2
, we can find
D+1
cubes
\{R_{i}\}
such that
2R_{i}
are pairwise disjoint, each having measure
\frac{1}{2}cD^{-2r\kappa}
. Now group each preperiodic point of degree
\leq D
over
K
with its Galois orbit. Each Galois orbit has at most
D
elements and so there is one cube
R_{i}
such that it does not lie in
2R_{i}
. Now given
cD^{e}(D+1)
many Galois orbits, there must exist one
i
such that
cD^{e}
of the Galois orbits all do not intersect
2R_{i}
. Applying
[
FR06
, Theoreme 3]
with a non-negative function
f
which is identically
1
on
R_{i}
and supported on
2R_{i}
then gives us a bound on
c
and
e
as desired.
∎

Sometimes it is more desirable to obtain an actual numerical bound on the size
\operatorname{Prep}_{\varphi,D}
rather than just controlling its geometry. We show that when
X=\mathbb{P}^{2}
, under some assumptions it is possible to obtain a bound on
|\operatorname{Per}_{\varphi,D}|
, the set of periodic points of
\varphi
that are of degree
D
over the base field
K
, that is polynomial in
D
.

Let
\varphi
be an endomorphism on
\mathbb{P}^{2}
of degree
d\geq 2
defined over a number field
K
. We will further assume that there are only finitely many periodic subvarieties of
\varphi
in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
. Heuristically, this is expected to hold for a general endomorphism
\varphi
, although it has only been shown to hold for a very general endomorphism
[
Xie24
]
.

Theorem 10.5
.

Let
\varphi:\mathbb{P}^{2}\to\mathbb{P}^{2}
is an endomorphism of degree
d\geq 2
defined over a number field
K
. Further assume that there are only finitely many periodic curves for
\varphi
. Then there exists
e=e(\varphi)>0
such that
|\operatorname{Per}_{\varphi,D}|\leq O_{\varphi,K}(D^{e})
.

Proof.

Given a positive integer
D\geq 2
, by Theorem
10.3
we can find a hypersurface
H
of degree
cD^{e^{\prime}}
, for some
e^{\prime}=e^{\prime}(\varphi)>0
and
c=c(\varphi,K)
, that contains all periodic points of degree
<D
. We write
H=\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}C_{i}
where each
C_{i}
is an irreducible curve over
\mathbb{C}
.

Let
C_{j}
be one of these curves that is not preperiodic. We will bound the number of periodic points of degree
<D
on it. To do so, we consider the sequence of iterates
\{f^{i}(C_{j})\}_{i\geq 1}
and let
k
be the smallest integer such that
f^{k}(C_{j})
is not one of the our curves
C_{i}
. Since each
C_{i}
has degree at most
cD^{e^{\prime}}
, it follows that
f^{k-1}(C_{j})
has degree at most
cD^{e^{\prime}}
and so
f^{k}(C_{j})
has degree at most
(cd)D^{e^{\prime}}
.

Now if
C_{j}
has
N
periodic points with degree
<D
, it follows that
f^{k}(C_{j})
also has
N
periodic points with degree
<D
. These
N
periodic points must necessarily also lie inside
H
as they have degree
<D
over
K
. Hence

N\leq|f^{k}(C_{j})\cap H|\leq(cd)D^{e^{\prime}}\cdot cD^{e^{\prime}}=c^{2}dD^{2e^{\prime}}.

If
C_{j}
were instead preperiodic, we let
C^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,C^{\prime}_{m}
be the finitely many the periodic curves of
\varphi
. Then there exists some finite extension
L
for which each
C^{\prime}_{i}
is defined over and for which there exists some positive integer such that
\varphi^{n_{i}}:C^{\prime}_{i}\to C^{\prime}_{i}
is an endomorphism of
C^{\prime}_{i}
defined over
L
. Since
\varphi
is polarized on
\mathbb{P}^{2}
, it must be that
\varphi^{n_{i}}
restricted to
C^{\prime}_{i}
is a polarized endomorphism and thus by possibly passing to yet another finite extension, we may assume that
C^{\prime}_{i}
is birational to an elliptic curve or
\mathbb{P}^{1}
and
\varphi^{n_{i}}
is an endomorphism of degree
\geq 2
. For elliptic curves, it is then well known that the number of periodic points of degree
D
on each
C^{\prime}_{i}
may be bounded by
O_{\varphi,K}(D^{4})
and for
\mathbb{P}^{1}
, it follows by Theorem
10.3
that we can find
e_{i}>0
such that the number of periodic points of degree
D
is at most
O_{\varphi,K}(D^{e_{i}})
.

In conclusion, for each irreducible
C_{i}
of our hypersurface
H
, we can find some uniform
f=f(\varphi)>0
such that the number of periodic points of degree
\leq D
on
C_{i}
is at most
O_{\varphi}(D^{f})
. Summing up over each
C_{i}
, we get at most
O_{\varphi,K}(D^{f+e^{\prime}})
periodic points of degree
D
over
K
, with
f
and
e^{\prime}
only depending on
\varphi
as desired.
∎

In general, it is not known which endomorphisms
\varphi:\mathbb{P}^{2}\to\mathbb{P}^{2}
have finitely many periodic curves. However in the case where
\varphi
restricts to a polynomial endomorphism
\varphi:\mathbb{A}^{2}\to\mathbb{A}^{2}
, there are some results of Xie
[
Xie24
]
that classify when this happens under an additional assumption. Let
\overline{\varphi}
be the induced map on the line at infinity
L_{\infty}=\mathbb{P}^{2}\setminus\mathbb{A}^{2}
. We say that
\overline{\varphi}
is not of polynomial type if
\overline{\varphi}^{-n}(x)\to\infty
for all
x\in L_{\infty}
.

Theorem 10.6

(Theorem 1.20
[
Xie24
]
)
.

Let
\varphi:\mathbb{P}^{2}\to\mathbb{P}^{2}
be an endomorphism of degree
d\geq 2
that restricts to an endomorphism of
\mathbb{A}^{2}
. Assume that the induced morphism
\overline{\varphi}
on the hyperplane at infinity
L_{\infty}
is not of polynomial type. Then if
\varphi
has infinitely many periodic curves,
\varphi
must be of the form
(f(x,y),g(x,y))
for homogeneous polynomials
f,g
after conjugating by a translation. Furthermore, all but finitely many periodic curves are a line of the form
\{z_{1}=\lambda z_{2}\}
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
10.6
, we are able to prove cases of Theorem
10.5
without the assumption of finitely many periodic curves.

First, note that if
\varphi
is of the form
(f(x,y),g(x,y))
for homogeneous polynomials
f,g
, then
(0,0)
is an exceptional point for
\varphi
. In particular if we conjugated by the translation
(x-a,y-b)
, then
(a,b)
is an exceptional point for
\varphi
and hence must lie in
\overline{K}
. Thus after conjugating and possibly extending our base field, we may assume that
\varphi
is of the form
(f(x,y),g(x,y))
for
f,g
homogeneous polynomials of degree
d
and with coefficients in
K
.

Lemma 10.7
.

Let
H_{\lambda}=\{z_{1}=\lambda z_{2}\}
be a periodic hyperplane. If
\tilde{\varphi}(z)=\frac{f(z,1)}{g(z,1)}
is the rational function corresponding to
(f,g)
, then
\lambda
must be a periodic point of
\varphi
.

Proof.

This is clear as
\varphi
sends
H_{\lambda}
to
H_{\tilde{\varphi}(\lambda)}
.
∎

Let
\varphi^{n}(x,y)=(f_{n}(x,y),g_{n}(x,y))
where
f_{n},g_{n}
are homogeneous polynomials of degree
d^{n}
and let
\lambda
be a fixed point of order
n
where we assume that
n
is minimal. Then
\varphi^{n}
sends
H_{\lambda}
back to itself and any periodic point on
H_{\lambda}
is necessarily a periodic point for
\varphi^{n}
.

Lemma 10.8
.

Under the isomorphism
H_{\lambda}\simeq\mathbb{P}^{1}
by sending
(\lambda z,z)\mapsto z
, our endomorphism
\varphi^{n}
becomes
z\mapsto z^{d^{n}}g_{n}(\lambda,1)
.

Proof.

Our endomorphism
\varphi^{n}
sends
(\lambda z,z)
to
(f_{n}(\lambda z,z),g_{n}(\lambda z,z))
and hence under the isomorphism
H_{\lambda}\simeq\mathbb{P}^{1}
, our endomorphism is
z\mapsto g_{n}(\lambda z,z)
. Since
g_{n}(x,y)
is homogeneous of degree
d^{n},
we have

g_{n}(\lambda z,z)=z^{d^{n}}g_{n}(\lambda,1)

as desired.
∎

Let
c_{n}=g_{n}(\lambda,1)
and set
\varphi_{\lambda}(z)=z^{d^{n}}c_{n}
. Then

\varphi_{\lambda}^{2}(z)=z^{d^{2n}}c_{n}^{d^{n}+1},\ldots,\varphi^{m}_{\lambda}(z)=z^{d^{mn}}c_{n}^{d^{(m-1)n}+d^{(m-2)n}+\cdots+1}.

Lemma 10.9
.

Let
z
be a periodic point for
\varphi_{\lambda}(z)
. Then
c_{n}z^{d^{n}-1}
is a root of unity.

Proof.

If
z
is of period
m
, then we have

z^{d^{mn}}c_{n}^{\frac{d^{mn}-1}{d^{n}-1}}=z\implies(z^{d^{n}-1}c_{n})^{\frac{d^{mn}-1}{d-1}}=1

and so
z^{d^{n}-1}c_{n}
is necessarily a root of unity.
∎

We are now able to bound the number of periodic points of degree at most
D
that lie on
H_{\lambda}
.

Lemma 10.10
.

For each
\lambda
, there is at most
O_{K}(D^{6})
many
z
’s such that
z
is a periodic point for
\varphi_{\lambda}
and
[K(z,\lambda):K]\leq D
.

Proof.

Let
\lambda
be given and let
z
be a periodic point for
\varphi_{\lambda}
. Then we know that
z^{d^{n}-1}c_{n}
is a root of unity,
\omega
, where
c_{n}=g_{n}(\lambda,1)
. Since
z^{d^{n}-1}c_{n}
lies in
K(\lambda,z)
, it follows that
[K(\omega):K]\leq D
. Hence there are at most
O_{K}(D^{2})
many such
\omega
’s as a root of unity of order
n\geq 6
has at least
\sqrt{n}
many Galois conjugates over
\mathbb{Q}
.

For each such
\omega
, any two solutions of
z^{d^{n}-1}c_{n}=\omega
differ by a root of unity
\omega^{\prime}
. Now if these two solutions are of degree at most
D
over
K
, then we necessarily have
[K(\omega^{\prime}):K]\leq D^{2}
. Then there are necessarily at most
O_{K}(D^{4})
possible choices of
\omega^{\prime}
. Hence we get a total of
O_{K}(D^{6})
many possible
z
’s such that
z
is a periodic point of
\varphi_{\lambda}
.
∎

With this, we can prove our bound on the number of periodic points of degree
\leq D
for endomorphisms
\varphi:\mathbb{P}^{2}\to\mathbb{P}^{2}
that restrict to an endomorphism on
\mathbb{A}^{2}
.

Theorem 10.11
.

Let
\varphi:\mathbb{P}^{2}\to\mathbb{P}^{2}
be an endomorphism of degree
d\geq 2
defined over a number field
K
that restricts to an endomorphism of
\mathbb{A}^{2}
. Assume further that the induced morphism
\overline{\varphi}
on the line at infinity
L_{\infty}
is not of polynomial type. Then there exists
e=e(\varphi)>0
such that
|\operatorname{Per}_{\varphi,D}|\leq O_{\varphi,K}(D^{e})
for every
D\geq 1
.

Proof.

We perform the same strategy as in Theorem
10.5
. It then suffices to bound the number of periodic points of degree at most
D
in
H_{\lambda}
for
\lambda
being a periodic point of
\tilde{\varphi}(z)
. Given such a periodic point
(\lambda z,z)
that lies on
H_{\lambda}
, we must have
[K(\lambda,z):K]\leq D
. Then by Lemma
10.10
, there are at most
O_{K}(D^{6})
many such periodic points
z
. The same argument as in Theorem
10.5
then allows us to conclude the existence of
e=e(\varphi)>0
such that
|\operatorname{Per}_{\varphi,D}|\leq O_{\varphi,K}(D^{e})
as desired.
∎

We may also consider the extension
K_{n}=K(\operatorname{Per}_{n})
, where we adjoin all periodic points of period
n
to our field
K
. Baker
[
Bak06
]
has proven in dimension one that

[K_{n}:K]\geq c\frac{|\operatorname{Per}_{n}|}{\log|\operatorname{Per}_{n}|}

for some constant
c=c(\varphi,K)>0
. This translates to an exponential growth in terms of
n
for the degree
[K_{n}:K]
. We will first show that for endomorphisms
\varphi:\mathbb{P}^{2}\to\mathbb{P}^{2}
of degree
d\geq 2
, we also have an exponential growth for the degree
[K_{n}:K]
.

Theorem 10.12
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective surface defined over a number field
K
and let
\varphi:X\to X
be a polarized endomorphism of degree
d\geq 2
. Then there exists
c=c(\varphi,K)
such that
[K_{n}:K]\geq c^{n}
. In fact there exists
c^{\prime}>1
such that for
1-O(c^{\prime-n})
proportion of points
x\in\operatorname{Per}_{n}
, we will have
[K(x):K]\geq c^{n}
.

Proof.

This follows immediately from Proposition
9.3
and Theorem
10.3
. By Theorem
10.3
, we know that all preperiodic points of degree at most
c^{n}
lie on a curve of degree at most
C(c^{en})
, where
e
depends only on
\varphi
and
C
depends on
\varphi
and
K
. By Proposition
9.3
, this curve contains at most
C^{2}c^{2en}d^{n}
many periodic points of period
n
. Hence if we take
c,c^{\prime}>1
such that
C^{2}c^{2en}<(c^{\prime-1}d)^{n}
, then for
1-O(c^{\prime-n})
proportion of points
x\in\operatorname{Per}_{n}
will not lie on our curve and so will necessarily have degree
\geq c^{n}
as desired.
∎

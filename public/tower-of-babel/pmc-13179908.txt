Perceived leg length discrepancy after total hip arthroplasty is associated with global spinopelvic coronal flexibility
Authors: Shunsuke Minoji, Hisatoshi Ishikura, Maaya Kudo, Masaki Hatano, Toru Nishiwaki, Sakae Tanaka
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPerceived leg length discrepancy (pLLD) is a common source of dissatisfaction after total hip arthroplasty (THA), even in the absence of significant radiographic discrepancies. While spinopelvic factors have been increasingly recognized, most previous studies have focused on sagittal alignment or lumbar mobility, and the impact of preoperative global spinopelvic coronal flexibility on pLLD has remained unclear.MethodsWe retrospectively reviewed 114 patients who underwent primary unilateral THA for osteoarthritis between January and December 2023. pLLD was assessed using a four-point scale at 6 months postoperatively. Patients who reported no perception of discrepancy were classified as the non-pLLD group, while those reporting mild, clear, or strong perception were grouped as the pLLD cohort. Preoperative spinal flexibility was measured on coronal radiographs during maximal lateral bending, calculating changes in spinopelvic angle (ΔSPA), reflecting thoracic-to-pelvic coronal flexibility, and lumbosacral angle (ΔLSA), reflecting lumbar-to-pelvic coronal flexibility. Secondary parameters included radiographic leg length discrepancy, leg lengthening, and sagittal spinopelvic alignment. Multivariable logistic regression analysis was used to identify independent predictors of pLLD.ResultspLLD was reported in 47 patients (41.2%). The pLLD group exhibited significantly lower ΔSPA values (16.6° vs. 21.9°, P = 0.001) compared to the non-pLLD group, although ΔLSA did not differ significantly. Multivariable analysis revealed that lower ΔSPA (P = 0.006) and shorter height (P = 0.037) were independently associated with pLLD. Radiographic leg length discrepancy did not differ significantly between the two groups.ConclusionReduced preoperative global spinopelvic coronal flexibility, particularly diminished ΔSPA, was associated with the perception of leg length discrepancy following THA. Assessment of coronal spinopelvic flexibility may provide additional insight for preoperative evaluation and patient counseling. Further studies are warranted to better understand whether perioperative rehabilitation aimed at improving coronal spinopelvic mobility may help to mitigate pLLD after THA.Keywords: Total hip arthroplasty, Patient satisfaction, Perceived leg length discrepancy, Spinopelvic alignment, Coronal spinal flexibility

Introduction

Total hip arthroplasty (THA) is an established treatment for hip disorders such as osteoarthritis of the hip [1]. Although patient satisfaction after THA is generally higher than that of other joint arthroplasties [2], 7–15% of patients report dissatisfaction following the procedure [3, 4]. Factors contributing to dissatisfaction include patient-related factors such as younger age, unmet expectations, and mental health issues, as well as surgery-related factors such as persistent pain, length of hospital stay, dislocation due to instability, and leg length discrepancy [3, 5]. It has been reported that a structural leg length discrepancy (sLLD) greater than 10 mm is associated with patient dissatisfaction [6]. More recently, studies have suggested that even smaller discrepancies may adversely affect patient-reported outcomes. For example, previous studies have reported that sLLD greater than approximately 5 mm may be associated with worse functional outcomes, and therefore achieving limb length within 5 mm of the target has been suggested as a desirable goal [7]. Even when sLLD was minimized, patients have often reported a subjective sensation of asymmetry, which has been associated with altered gait mechanics, reduced ability to perform activities of daily living, and lower satisfaction with surgical outcomes [8]. Therefore, achieving optimal sLLD remains challenging even with robotic-assisted THA [9]. Collectively, these reports demonstrating a discrepancy between sLLD and perceived leg length discrepancy (pLLD) suggest that additional biomechanical or perceptual factors may be involved [10].

Previous studies have primarily focused on factors distal to the lower spine—such as decreased lumbar mobility, prior lumbar fusion, and pelvic obliquity—as risk factors for pLLD [11–13]. However, several reports have suggested that childhood leg length discrepancy due to developmental dysplasia of the hip may lead not only to lumbar but also to whole-spine degeneration and scoliosis [14], and that patients with globally reduced spinal mobility, such as those with ankylosing spondylitis, are more prone to experience leg length discrepancy after THA [15]. Based on these observations, we hypothesized that the coronal plane flexibility of thoracic spine–pelvis complex, not limited to the lumbar region, might be a key determinant of pLLD after THA.

Nevertheless, to the best of our knowledge, no previous study has comprehensively investigated the relationship between the global spinopelvic coronal flexibility and pLLD after THA. To address this gap, this study aimed to clarify whether reduced preoperative spinopelvic lateral flexion was associated with an increased risk of pLLD following THA.

Materials and methods

Patients

A consecutive series of THAs performed at our institution between January 2023 and December 2023 were included in this study. We included patients undergoing primary unilateral THA with preoperative diagnosis of osteoarthritis. The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) history of surgery on the affected hip or spine. (2) presence of diseases other than osteoarthritis, including femoral neck fractures, femoral head necrosis and rheumatoid arthritis; (3) dislocated hips classified as Crowe type 3 or 4; (4) patients with dementia and (5) inability to complete a minimum of 6 months of follow-up. Therefore, 114 patients (114 hips) were included in this study (Fig. 1).

Baseline variables collected included age, sex, height, weight, body mass index (BMI), bone mineral density (expressed as % young adult mean [YAM]). In addition, Crowe classification was used as a preoperative indicator of high hip dislocation, and the hip joint degeneration was classified by the Kellgren–Lawrence (K-L) grade [16, 17].

Radiographic evaluations

Spinopelvic parameters, including lumbar lordosis (LL), pelvic incidence (PI), pelvic tilt (PT), and sacral slope (SS), were evaluated using preoperative standing lateral radiographs of the lumbar spine and pelvis (Fig. 2A, B) [18].

Radiographic measurement of sagittal spinopelvic parameters. A Lumbar lordosis (LL) was measured as the angle between the superior endplate of L1 and the superior endplate of S1. B Sacral slope (SS), pelvic tilt (PT), and pelvic incidence (PI) were measured using standard definitions based on standing lateral plain radiographs of the lumbar spine and pelvis

As indicators of spinal lateral flexion range of motion, spinopelvic angle (SPA) and lumbosacral angle (LSA) were assessed using preoperative standing anteroposterior spine radiographs, including the pelvis [18, 19]. Following previous reports, SPA was defined as the angle between a line connecting the centers of the T1 and S1 vertebral bodies and a line perpendicular to the iliac crest line (Fig. 3A, B). Similarly, LSA was defined as the angle between a line connecting the spinous processes of L4 and L5 and a line perpendicular to the iliac crest line (Fig. 4A, B). For both maximum right and left lateral flexion positions, SPA and LSA were recorded as positive when tilted to the right and negative when tilted to the left relative to the perpendicular line to the iliac crest line. The differences between the maximum right and left values were defined as ΔSPA and ΔLSA, respectively [18]. Maximum lateral flexion to both sides was performed voluntarily without assistance from the upper limbs or external support, within a range in which the participant could maintain balance independently.

ΔLSA was calculated as the absolute difference in LSA between the two lateral bending positions

The sLLD was measured on postoperative plain radiographs, and leg lengthening was assessed using both pre- and postoperative plain radiographs [20]. Specifically, the sLLD was defined as the difference in the perpendicular distance from the line connecting the bilateral teardrops to the top of the lesser trochanter on each side, while leg lengthening was defined as the change in hip length on the operated side between the pre- and postoperative radiographs, based on the distance from the inter-teardrop line to the tip of the lesser trochanter (Fig. 5A, B).

Measurement of leg lengthening and postoperative structural leg length discrepancy. Preoperative (A) and postoperative (B) anteroposterior radiographs of both hips are described. A horizontal reference line was drawn connecting the bilateral teardrops, and perpendicular distances to the lesser trochanters were measured. Postoperative structural leg length discrepancy was defined as the difference between L3 and L1, whereas leg lengthening was defined as the difference between L3 and L2

Each parameter was assessed independently by three orthopedic surgeons (S.M., M.K., and M.H.) who were not involved in the surgeries, and the median value was used for the analysis. Interobserver reliability was assessed using intraclass correlation coefficients (ICC [1, 3]).

Outcome measures

The primary outcome was the presence of pLLD, evaluated at 6 months postoperatively using a patient self-report and categorized as either present or absent, consistent with previous studies [21]. Patients who reported no perception of discrepancy were classified as the non-pLLD group, while those reporting mild, clear, or strong perception were grouped as the pLLD cohort.

Surgical procedures and rehabilitation

All THAs were conducted using a minimally invasive anterior approach with the aid of a traction Table [22]. This method utilizes a small anterior incision on the hip to minimize disruption of intermuscular planes and neurovascular structures. Acetabular cup placement was performed under intraoperative fluoroscopic guidance, employing a mechanical guide connected to the cup impactor. It was oriented to achieve approximately 40° of radiographic inclination and 15° of radiographic anteversion based on preoperative planning. Pelvic orientation was assessed intraoperatively using fluoroscopy, with particular attention to the symmetry and shape of the obturator foramen. Adjustments to fluoroscopic positioning were made as necessary to account for pelvic tilt and rotation. For femoral component placement, optimal positioning and orientation were determined preoperatively using CT-based three-dimensional planning. However, intraoperative use of CT navigation, robotic systems, or other computer-assisted technologies was not employed.

Postoperative rehabilitation began the day after surgery, allowing patients to ambulate with full weight-bearing as early as feasible. During hospitalization, patients received instruction in daily activities, such as stair navigation, and were typically discharged within 7 to 10 days post-surgery. No specific movement restrictions were enforced, and patients were encouraged to resume activities such as sitting and rising from the floor as tolerated.

Data analyses

We analyzed the relationship between pLLD and potential risk factors. Continuous variables were summarized as means ± standard deviations and categorical variables were reported as counts and percentages. Group comparisons were performed using independent t-tests or Mann–Whitney U tests for continuous variables, depending on normality assessed using the Shapiro–Wilk test. Chi-square tests were used for categorical variables. Variables with a P-value < 0.20 in univariate analysis were included in a multivariable logistic regression model to identify independent predictors of pLLD. The variance inflation factor (VIF) was used to check for multicollinearity in the model, and in accordance with previous reports, a VIF > 10 was considered indicative of multicollinearity [23]. Statistical significance was defined as P < 0.05. All analyses were performed using Bell Curve for Excel ver. 4.04 (Social Survey Research Information Co. Ltd. Tokyo, Japan).

Ethical considerations

The study protocol was approved by the institutional review board of our institution and was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants prior to enrollment. No external funding was received, and the authors declared no conflicts of interest.

There were no statistically significant differences between the pLLD and non-pLLD groups in terms of demographic variables including age, sex distribution, height, weight, BMI, or YAM. Similarly, no significant differences were observed in the radiographic severity of hip osteoarthritis, as assessed by K–L grading or Crowe classification. The ICC values demonstrated excellent reliability for all parameters: PI (0.965), LL (0.990), PT (0.961), SS (0.981), ΔLSA (0.955), ΔSPA (0.953), ΔLLD (0.958) and leg lengthening (0.963).

In the univariate analysis, the pLLD group exhibited significantly lower ΔSPA values compared to the non-pLLD group (P = 0.001). ΔLSA did not differ significantly between groups.

Among spinopelvic alignment parameters, PI was significantly lower in the pLLD group than in the non-pLLD group (P = 0.026). No significant differences were observed in LL, PT, or SS. Although sLLD did not significantly differ between groups, the magnitude of leg lengthening was greater in the pLLD group (P = 0.008).

Discussion

In order to interpret our finding that short stature and decreased ΔSPA were independent risk factors for pLLD after THA, it is important to first review how LLD is compensated within the pelvis–spine–trunk system, based on previous biomechanical and clinical evidence.

LLD, whether structural or functional, induces asymmetric loading on the pelvis and frequently causes pelvic obliquity in the coronal plane [24]. Over time, this pelvic obliquity can lead to functional scoliosis, with convexity toward the shorter limb side, and serves as a compensatory mechanism throughout the entire spine to maintain horizontal shoulder alignment and overall trunk balance [25–27].

This compensatory mechanism is not limited to static posture. Several studies have examined dynamic compensation during gait, showing that pelvic and spinal rotations as well as lateral trunk movements play important roles in mitigating imbalance during walking [28].

In THA, patients with osteoarthritis often present with limb shortening due to cartilage loss, femoral head deformity, or acetabular changes, and leg length may be abruptly altered during surgery. In such situations, the compensatory mechanism is thought to involve pelvic obliquity, followed by lumbar curvature and, ultimately, functional scoliosis affecting the entire spine. In addition, during motion, pelvic and spinal rotations and lateral trunk shifts further help restore left–right balance and minimize pLLD through dynamic adjustments. Therefore, sufficient spinopelvic flexibility is essential to accommodate postoperative leg length changes without leading to pLLD.

In individuals with short stature, the same amount of leg lengthening or sLLD represents a greater proportion relative to overall body height, and the spinal column itself is generally shorter [19]. These factors collectively may narrow the compensatory capacity of the spine, making it more difficult to accommodate leg length discrepancy.

The finding that low ΔSPA, rather than ΔLSA, was an independent risk factor indicates that pLLD is influenced not only by the local lumbar flexibility but also by the global coronal mobility of the thoracic spine–pelvis complex. When global spinopelvic flexibility is reduced, the ability of the spine to compensate for coronal alignment changes introduced by THA may be compromised. As a result, patients may have trouble maintaining trunk balance in the coronal plane, which could exacerbate pLLD postoperatively. Although lumbar parameters are known to play an important role in compensating for pelvic obliquity, our findings suggest that global spinopelvic flexibility may also reflect the overall compensatory mechanism. This may be because compensation involves coordinated motion across the entire spine rather than the lumbar region alone.

Furthermore, univariate analysis demonstrated that patients with pLLD had significantly lower PI values and tended to have smaller LL values, suggesting a flat back alignment pattern [29]. Such sagittal imbalance has been reported to correlate with decreased sagittal flexibility, implying that coronal and sagittal spinal mobilities are interdependent [18]. Consequently, patients with limited coronal flexibility may also exhibit reduced sagittal and rotational motion of the spine, thereby diminishing the effectiveness of dynamic compensatory mechanisms during functional activities.

These findings highlight the importance of assessing global spinopelvic flexibility, rather than focusing solely on the lumbar region, when evaluating the risk of postoperative leg length perception following THA.

This study has several limitations. First, as a retrospective, single-center study with a limited sample size, the generalizability of the findings may be restricted. Second, maximal lateral bending radiographs may be influenced by several factors, including preoperative hip pain, core muscle strength, balance, and fear of falling. These factors may affect the range of motion achieved during imaging and, consequently, the reproducibility of the measurements. Furthermore, all measurements were performed using plain radiographs, and measurement error cannot be fully excluded. Another limitation of this study is that sLLD was evaluated based on hip length rather than global lower limb length. Although sLLD in patients with hip osteoarthritis primarily arises from the hip joint, discrepancies in other segments of the lower limb may also influence patient perception. Moreover, preoperative factors such as muscle strength, soft tissue contracture, and spinal scoliosis, which may be associated with pLLD, were not evaluated in this study. Future studies incorporating these factors, along with global lower limb measurements, may help to further clarify this issue. With respect to the coronal spinopelvic flexibility, it was assessed only under static conditions; dynamic evaluations during activities such as walking were not performed, which may limit the understanding of functional spinopelvic motion in daily life. Finally, pLLD was evaluated only at 6 months postoperatively, a period during which some patients may still be undergoing neuromuscular adaptation. Therefore, long-term outcomes remain unclear.

Nevertheless, a major strength of this study is that it is the first to investigate the relationship between global spinopelvic flexibility and pLLD. Moreover, this study suggests the potential value of perioperative rehabilitation programs aimed at improving global spinopelvic flexibility, which may help enhance compensatory capacity and reduce pLLD after THA. There have been reports that side-shift and hitch exercises performed in the early postoperative period can alleviate pLLD, supporting the validity of the present findings [30]. However, the optimal content and timing of rehabilitation interventions to reduce pLLD after THA remain open to debate, and further studies are warranted to clarify these issues.

In conclusion, short stature and decreased global thoracic-to-pelvic coronal flexibility were associated with perceived limb length discrepancy after THA.

Impact of mucinous histology on the efficacy of neoadjuvant immunotherapy in localized mismatch repair-deficient colorectal cancer
Authors: Zhigang Hong, Shihong Chen, Chenzhi Zhang, Jiehai Yu, Leen Liao, Muxu Zheng, Kai Han, Zhenlin Hou, Xiaojun Wu, Wu Jiang, Pei-Rong Ding
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe response of mucinous colorectal cancer (CRC) to immunotherapy remains unclear. This study aimed to evaluate the efficacy of neoadjuvant immunotherapy in localized mucinous mismatch repair-deficient (dMMR) CRC.MethodsThis retrospective study included patients with localized dMMR CRC who underwent neoadjuvant immunotherapy. Patients were classified into adenocarcinoma (AC) and mucinous carcinoma (MC) groups. Clinicopathological and treatment data were recorded. Logistic regression analysis was performed to identify predictors of complete response (CR).ResultsA total of 220 patients were included, of whom 25(11.4%) had MC. Patients with MC had significantly lower clinical complete response (cCR) rates (20.0% vs. 44.1%, P = 0.021) and objective response rates (64.0% vs. 92.8%, P < 0.001) than those with AC. In addition, patients with MC showed lower pCR, poorer tumor regression, and more aggressive pathology. In MC patients, combination therapy was associated with greater tumor regression than anti-PD-1 monotherapy (83.3% vs. 28.6%, P = 0.045). Among patients who underwent surgery, the 2-year disease-free survival rate was 94.1% in the MC group and 100% in the AC group. Multivariate analysis identified MC histology as an independent negative predictor of CR (Odds ratio [OR] = 0.22, 95% confidence interval [CI]: 0.09–0.56; P = 0.002), whereas a treatment duration ≥ 3 months significantly increased the probability of CR in all patients. During neoadjuvant treatment, severe (grade 3–4) immune-related adverse events (irAEs) occurred in 15 patients (7.7%, 15/195) in the AC group and 1 patient (4.0%, 1/25) in the MC group.ConclusionPatients with localized MC dMMR CRC tend to have inferior responses to immunotherapy compared with patients with AC. Background

Colorectal cancer (CRC) can be classified by DNA mismatch repair (MMR) status into mismatch repair-proficient (pMMR) and mismatch repair-deficient (dMMR) types [1, 2]. The dMMR phenotype, which represents approximately 10–15% of all CRC cases, is notably more immunogenic and responds better to immunotherapy [3, 4].

Immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) have shown remarkable efficacy in the neoadjuvant treatment setting for dMMR or microsatellite instability-high (MSI-H) CRC, with a range of 68% to 100% complete response (CR) rates observed in several studies [5–11]. In a recent study, all 49 patients with locally advanced dMMR rectal cancer achieved cCR after receiving neoadjuvant anti–PD-1 therapy for 6 months and adopted watch and wait strategy [12]. The NEOCAP trial reported that surgery can be omitted in patients with dMMR/MSI-H colon cancer who achieved clinical complete response (cCR) following neoadjuvant immunotherapy [11]. However, approximately 7–15% of CRC patients with dMMR/MSI-H failed to respond to neoadjuvant ICI therapy [7, 9–11], and the mechanisms underlying this resistance remain unknown.

Mucinous adenocarcinoma (MC) is defined by ≥ 50% extracellular mucin within the tumor volume and accounts for approximately 10–15% of CRC [13, 14]. It was considered as aggressive subtype associated with more advanced stages, larger tumor burden, and increased lymph node involvement [13, 15–17]. In addition, MC is more frequently observed in MSI-H tumors, accounting for approximately 15% of this molecular subtype [15, 18, 19]. In our previous study, we observed that patients with dMMR/MSI-H mucinous adenocarcinoma had a low objective response rates (36.4%) to ICIs, although the cohort predominantly comprised Lynch syndrome patients [20]. In a pathological study, mucinous adenocarcinomas within dMMR tumors exhibited reduced CD8+ T cell infiltration compared to nonmucinous adenocarcinomas [21], indicating that dMMR tumors presenting as mucinous adenocarcinoma may be resistant to immunotherapy.

This study aimed to assess the efficacy of neoadjuvant immunotherapy in patients with MC histology among localized dMMR CRC and to investigate clinical factors potentially associated with treatment outcomes.

Patients and methods

Study population and design

We retrospectively recruited patients with dMMR/MSI-H CRC who underwent neoadjuvant immunotherapy between February 2019 and November 2023 at Sun Yat-sen University Cancer Center (SYSUCC). The main inclusion criteria were patients with localized colorectal cancer and confirmed dMMR/MSI-H status. Patients who were suspected of having metastatic disease, lacked treatment assessment, or had signet-ring cell carcinoma were excluded. Clinical data were obtained from the hospital’s electronic medical record system, including demographic information, treatment details, pathology reports, and radiologic findings. Immune-related adverse events (irAEs) were identified through review of the medical records during the neoadjuvant treatment period. The severity of irAEs was graded according to the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE), version 5.0. This study was approved by the Institutional Review Board of SYSUCC (B2023-697-Y01). Informed consent from patients was waived due to the retrospective nature of this study. The study adhered to the STROBE guidelines for observational studies.

Definition of histology

Histological data were derived from biopsy and surgical specimens, classification was performed according to World Health Organization (WHO) guidelines [14] by two gastrointestinal pathologists who were blinded to clinical and outcome data. Specifically, 34 (17.4%) and 16 (64%) patients in the AC and MC groups, respectively, were diagnosed based on surgical specimens, while the remaining patients were diagnosed by biopsy. MC is defined by extracellular mucin comprising more than 50% of the tumor volume. In this cohort, two histological subtypes of CRC were identified: adenocarcinoma (AC) and mucinous adenocarcinoma (MC). Based on this classification, patients were stratified into two groups: AC versus MC.

Genetic and immunohistochemical testing

All patients were required to have documented MMR or MSI status. Microsatellite instability-high was defined as instability in two or more markers detected by polymerase chain reaction (PCR) testing or identified through tumor sequencing. Mismatch repair-deficient was determined by immunohistochemistry, defined as the loss of nuclear expression of at least one of the four mismatch repair proteins (MLH1, MSH2, MSH6, and PMS2) in tumor cells.

Immunotherapy regimen

The decision to treat patients with immunotherapy instead of surgery was made by the primary surgeons, with the aim of reducing tumor burden to avoid multivisceral resection or to preserve sphincter function. Therefore, the majority of tumors included in this study were locally advanced. In addition, a small number of earlier-stage tumors were treated with this approach when located in the low rectum or representing as metachronous colon tumors, where organ preservation or surgical complexity was also considered. All patients received PD-(L)1 inhibitors (pembrolizumab, nivolumab, sintilimab, toripalimab, camrelizumab, serplulimab, or envafolimab) either as monotherapy or in combination with CTLA-4 inhibitors, chemotherapy, targeted therapy, or radiotherapy. Because no guideline recommended PD-1 inhibitors as neoadjuvant therapy for CRC at the time of this study, treatment decisions were made empirically, resulting in heterogeneous regimens.

Assessment and follow-up

Tumor response was assessed using computed tomography (CT), magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), digital rectal examination (for rectal cancer), and targeted biopsy of suspected residual lesions by endoscopy. Clinical response was evaluated according to RECIST 1.1 criteria [22]. Surgery was recommended when R0 resection and organ preservation were considered achievable, whereas prolonged immunotherapy was generally continued if ongoing tumor shrinkage was observed on radiologic imaging. Patients achieving cCR were offered non-operative management. For patients undergoing resection, the tumor regression grade (TRG) was assessed according to the four-tiered system defined by the AJCC [23]. Pathological complete response (pCR) was defined as the absence of viable tumor cells in the resected primary tumor and all examined lymph nodes. Postoperative therapy was not predefined. However, PD-1 inhibitors were recommended for at least 6 months in patients achieving TRG 0/1, whereas oxaliplatin-based chemotherapy was recommended for those with TRG 2/3. Follow-up was conducted every 3 months during the first 2 years and every 6 months for up to 5 years in patients who underwent surgery, whereas those who achieved cCR (defined as the absence of residual disease on radiologic and endoscopic evaluation) and received the watch-and-wait approach underwent more intensive surveillance, including physical and endoscopic examination, serum CEA testing, and CT/MRI scans.

Statistical analysis

Categorical variables were reported as frequencies and percentages, and continuous variables as medians with interquartile ranges (IQRs). Associations between categorical variables were assessed with the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate. Continuous variables were compared between groups using the Wilcoxon–Mann–Whitney test. Treatment duration was defined as the interval between the first and last cycles of therapy before surgery (if performed); time to response and time to surgery were defined as the intervals from the first treatment cycle to the documented response and surgery, respectively. Disease-free survival (DFS) was defined as the interval from the first cycle of therapy to tumor recurrence or death from any cause. Odds ratios (ORs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) for univariable and multivariable analyses were obtained with logistic regression; variables with P < 0.10 in univariable analysis were entered into the multivariable model, and the outcome variable was the absence of complete response (cCR or pCR). All P-values were two-sided, and statistical significance was defined as P < 0.05. Statistical analyses were conducted using SPSS software (version 27.0).

Results

Baseline characteristics

A total of 227 dMMR/MSI-H CRC patients who underwent neoadjuvant immunotherapy were recruited, of whom 220 met the eligibility criteria and were included in this study (Fig. 1). The median age of the patients was 50.0 years (IQR 38.0–60.0); 124 (56.4%) were male, 165 (75.0%) had colon cancer, and 143 (65.0%) received treatment for 3 months or longer. Among them, 25 patients (11.4%) had MC, while the remaining 195 patients had AC (Table 1). Patients in the MC and AC groups had comparable baseline characteristics, including tumor location and clinical stage (Supplementary Table 1).

Clinical treatment and response

MC patients exhibited not only a lower percentage of cCR (20.0% vs. 44.1%, P = 0.021), but also a lower objective response rate (64.0% vs. 92.8%, P < 0.001) than those with AC, independent of the immunotherapy regimen (Table 2). Moreover, the rate of non-operative management was 20.0% in MC and 39.0% in AC. Despite having a comparable time to response, patients with MC had a shorter treatment duration (< 3 months) than those with AC (56.0% vs. 32.3%; P = 0.019). Although responders in the MC group were more likely to receive combination therapy, this difference was not statistically significant (Supplementary Table 2). When a detailed comparison was made among specific combinations, no significant difference was observed (Supplementary Table 3).

Postoperative features and outcomes

Overall, tumor resection was performed in 76.0% (19/25) of MC patients and 50.8% (99/195) of AC patients (Table 3). All these patients underwent R0 resection. Preoperative characteristics were comparable between the two groups. However, patients with MC exhibited a lower rate of pCR (15.8% vs. 65.7%, P < 0.001), poorer tumor regression (TRG 0/1: 36.8% vs. 77.8%, P < 0.001), and more aggressive pathological features, including a higher proportion of T4 tumors (15.8% vs. 2.0%, P = 0.029) and more residual positive lymph nodes (21.0% vs. 2.0%, P = 0.006). Accordingly, the rate of postoperative therapy was higher in MC than in AC (57.9% vs. 13.1%, P < 0.001).

Predictors of complete response

To explore factors associated with the incidence of CR, we conducted a logistic regression analysis (Table 4). CR was observed in 149 patients (67.7%). Patients with rectal cancer appeared more likely to have CR in the univariate analysis, but this was not significant in the multivariate model. The multivariate analysis identified MC histology as an independent negative predictor of CR (aOR = 0.22, 95%CI:0.09–0.56, adjusted P = 0.002). In addition, a longer treatment duration (≥ 3 months) was identified as a favorable factor for CR (aOR = 3.07, 95%CI: 1.63–5.76, adjusted P < 0.001). Notably, although combination therapy was not a predictor of CR in this cohort, it was associated with a significantly higher tumor regression rate in patients with MC compared with anti-PD-1 monotherapy (83.3% vs. 28.6%, P = 0.045; Supplementary Tables 4 and Fig. 3). Moreover, all three patients who achieved pCR in the MC subgroup received combination therapy (Supplementary Table 5).

Safety profiles

During neoadjuvant treatment, 16 (7.3%) severe (grade 3–4) immune-related adverse events (irAEs) were reported, including 15 cases (7.7%, 15/195) in the AC group and 1 case (4.0%, 1/25) in the MC group (Supplementary Table 6). Bowel obstruction was the most common, observed in 9 patients; 3 underwent emergent surgery and 6 required acute intervention (5 underwent ostomy and 1 underwent endoscopic stenting). The remaining grade 1–2 irAEs occurred in 82 (37.3%) patients; the most common toxicity was rash (n = 23), followed by increased aminotransferases (n = 21), hypothyroidism (n = 11), stomatitis (n = 6), hand and foot syndrome (n = 6), arthralgia (n = 4), hypoadrenocorticism (n = 3), and pneumonitis (n = 2). In total, 9 (4.1%) patients underwent delayed tumor resection; no postoperative deaths occurred.

Discussion

Although neoadjuvant immunotherapy has shown substantial efficacy in localized dMMR/MSI-H CRC, patients with MC exhibited poorer clinical responses compared to those with AC in this study. They also demonstrated aggressive postoperative features and unfavorable long-term outcomes. Therefore, tailored treatment strategies are warranted for MC dMMR CRC.

Neoadjuvant immunotherapy for dMMR/MSI-H CRC has shown notable overall success, yet clinical outcomes still vary among studies. A recent trial that adopted an organ-sparing approach reported a 100% cCR rate after six months of anti-PD-1 monotherapy [12]. In contrast, in the NICHE-2 study, four weeks of preoperative anti-PD-1 plus CTLA-4 therapy yielded a pCR rate of 68% [5]. Therefore, it is imperative to explore effective predictive biomarkers to select patients most likely to benefit from neoadjuvant immunotherapy and to optimize treatment strategies. In a systematic review, several studies were included in which patients with dMMR CRC received anti-PD-1 therapy for 1 to 7 months, with reported CR rates ranging from 0% to 100% [24]. A positive correlation was observed between CR incidence and treatment duration, and it was suggested that a minimum of 3 months of treatment may be both safe and associated with favorable outcomes. In this study, we not only validated the advantage of a longer treatment duration but also identified MC histology as an independent negative predictor for CR. Further analysis also demonstrated that this histologic subtype was associated with poorer objective and pathological responses compared with AC. These findings suggest that MC dMMR CRC may exhibit a poor response to immunotherapy, highlighting the need to further optimizing treatment strategies for these patients.

The markedly reduced complete response rate in the MC histological subtype suggests intrinsic resistance to immunotherapy, possibly driven by altered immune cell infiltration within the tumor microenvironment. A pathological study showed that tumor-infiltrating immune cells in CRC vary with histological subtype and MMR status. Although dMMR tumors generally display greater CD8⁺ T cell infiltration than pMMR tumors, the MC subtype exhibits a significant reduction in CD8⁺ T cells [21]. Previous research indicates that mucus protects the intestinal mucosa, dampening inflammation [25]. Because dMMR tumors carry a high mutational burden that elicits strong inflammatory responses [26], the abundant mucus observed in MC histology may attenuate this inflammation, thereby contributing to an immunosuppressive microenvironment.

Several studies have shown that histological regression after neoadjuvant chemotherapy could predict the risk of postoperative recurrence [27, 28]. However, more data is needed to verify whether this conclusion applies to immunotherapy for dMMR CRC. In our cohort, although the baseline staging was similar between the two groups, after neoadjuvant immunotherapy, patients with MC had higher T-stage tumor burden and more extensive lymphatic spread compared with those with AC, which means a lower likelihood of achieving radical resection. Therefore, it is reasonable that the 2-year DFS was shorter in the MC group.

Previous studies have demonstrated a positive association between treatment duration and CR incidence [24]. In our study, a longer treatment duration was identified as an independent positive predictor of CR, and this effect was independent of histologic subtype. Given that patients with MC had a shorter treatment duration, extending treatment duration may enhance the immunotherapy response in this subtype. In addition, combination therapy has been reported to enhance the efficacy of immunotherapy. In the PICC trial, which evaluated neoadjuvant immunotherapy for nonmetastatic CRC, anti-PD-1 monotherapy achieved a pCR rate of 65%, whereas the rate increased to 88% when the regimen was combined with the COX-2 inhibitor celecoxib [7]. More recently, nivolumab plus ipilimumab demonstrated superior progression-free survival compared with nivolumab alone in patients the metastatic CRC [29]. Although the difference was not statistically significant, combination therapies including a CTLA-4 inhibitor or chemotherapy were associated with a trend toward greater tumor regression than anti-PD-1 monotherapy in MC patients. With the growing adoption of organ preservation as neoadjuvant immunotherapy for dMMR/MSI-H CRC, there is an urgent need to explore optimal combination regimens to enhance treatment outcomes in this subtype.

The study has several limitations. First, it is subject to selection bias because of its retrospective design. Second, the combination treatment regimens included various anti-PD-1 agents, such as pembrolizumab, nivolumab, sintilimab, toripalimab, camrelizumab, or serplulimab, administered alone or with CTLA-4 inhibitors, chemotherapy, targeted therapy, or radiotherapy. Therefore, the number of patients in each specific regimen was small, making it difficult to determine which regimens played the dominant role. Additionally, RECIST-based measurements may provide a limited assessment of tumor regression and could introduce potential bias, particularly in mucinous tumors where changes in tumor size may not accurately reflect tumor burden. Furthermore, preoperative treatments such as radiotherapy may alter pathological findings; therefore, the higher proportion of cases diagnosed based on surgical specimens in the MC group may have introduced potential bias. However, only a small number of patients in this subgroup received radiotherapy. Finally, the analysis was based on a single-center cohort, which may limit the generalizability of the findings. In addition, the number of patients with MC was relatively small, particularly in subgroup analyses such as resected and TRG-defined cases, which may limit the robustness of the conclusions. Therefore, the findings regarding mucinous histology should be interpreted with caution. Future multicenter studies with larger sample sizes are needed to validate these observations.

In summary, patients with localized dMMR CRC with MC tend to have inferior clinical and pathological responses to immunotherapy compared with those with AC. Optimization of treatment duration or modification of treatment regimens may represent potential strategies to improve therapeutic efficacy in this subtype, which warrants further investigation.

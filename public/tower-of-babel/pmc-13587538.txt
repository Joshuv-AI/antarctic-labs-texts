Chloroplast genome sequence of the hybrid variety ‘Silky White’, a white-flowered calla lily (Zantedeschia aethiopica)
Authors: Jong-Bo Kim, Tae-Ho Park
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractCalla lily (Zantedeschia spp.), native to southern Africa, is a very attractive plant and is used as cut flowers, potted flowering plants, and bedding plants. It can be distinguished into two groups depending on the color of the flower. The chloroplast genome of the hybrid variety ‘Silky White’ (Z. aethiopica × Z. aethiopica), a white-flowered calla lily, was sequenced using Illumina sequencing technology. The total length of the chloroplast genome was 168,880 bp with a GC content of 35.59%. It comprised a large single copy (LSC) region of 89,391 bp, a small single copy (SSC) region of 31,849 bp, and two inverted repeat regions (IRa and IRb) of 23,820 bp. Additionally, 161 functional genes were identified in the genome, including 105 protein-coding genes, 48 transfer RNA genes and 8 ribosomal RNA genes. Phylogenetic analysis revealed that the variety ‘Silky White’ is grouped into a large clade with other different Zantedeschia species, but is specifically close to Z. aethiopica, a group of white-flowered calla lilies. This study provides useful genomic information for future breeding and evolutionary studies of Zantedeschia spp.Keywords: Calla lily, chloroplast genome, phylogenetic tree, Zantedeschia aethiopica, Zantedeschia hybrid

Introduction

The genus Zantedeschia, comprising eight species, is an herbaceous perennial plant native southern Africa (Earl 1957; Singh et al. 1996). The genus can be divided into two sections: Zantedeschia and Aestivae. Two species are part of the first section - Zantedeschia – namely, Zantedeschia aethiopica Spreng 1826 and Z. odorata, and the second section, Aestivae, includes six species: Z. albomaculata, Z. rehmannii, Z. elliottiana, Z. jucunda, Z. valida, and Z. pentlandii. The two sections can be identified by white and colorful flowers, respectively (Singh et al. 1996; Snijder et al. 2004).

Due to the high demand for calla lilies as cut flowers and potted plants in a range of flower colors, many interspecific hybrids between species in the Aestivae section have been produced (Snijer et al. 2004). However, hybrids between species of the two sections cannot be developed through traditional breeding methods, as the resultant hybrids are unviable due to endosperm degeneration, abnormal embryo growth, and arrested plastid development. Also, these hybrids are not commercially attractive, as they are invariably albino (Yao et al. 1994; Yao and Cohen 2000; Snijer et al. 2004, 2007). These barriers are speculated to be related to biparental inheritance of plastids and plastome-genome incompatibility, and are prevalent in interspecific hybrids, occurring depending on the relatedness of the parental species in the genus Zantedeschia (Snijer et al. 2007; Wei et al. 2017).

Chloroplast genomes are highly conserved in size, structure and gene content. Most plant species have nearly collinear gene sequence, which may be due to a lack of recombination, compact genome size and maternal inheritance (Sugiura et al. 1998; BIrky 2001; Raubeson and Jansen 2005; Saski et al. 2005). However, chloroplast genomes are considered to be useful DNA sequences for assessing plant genetic diversity and for identifying plant species because they provide sufficient comparative information from complete genome sequences across plant species (Saski et al. 2005; Jheng et al. 2012; Cho et al. 2015). Therefore, the chloroplast genome information of the hybrid ‘Silky White’ will be a valuable resource for future genetic and evolutionary studies.

Materials and methods

The white-flowered calla lily variety ‘Silky White’ was provided by the National Institute of Horticultural and Herbal Science, South Korea (35.8° N, 127.0° E) (Figure 1). The variety is an intraspecific hybrid derived from a cross between ‘Childsiana’ (Z. aethiopica) and ‘Wedding March’ (Z. aethiopica) (CHo et al. 2014). A specimen was deposited in the National Agrobiodiversity Center, South Korea (http://genebank.rda.go.kr/, Yun-Im Kang, yunimy@korea.kr) under voucher number IT271780.

The species reference images for the calla lily cultivar ‘Silky White’ (Cho et al. 2014). Flower of the cultivar ‘Silky White’ (A) was grown in the green house (B).

The total genomic DNA was extracted according to the manufacturer’s instructions (RBC Bioscience, New Taipei City, Taiwan). The Phyzen bioinformatics pipeline was used for the chloroplast genome sequencing of the variety (Kim et al. 2015). An Illumina paired-end (PE) genomic library was constructed from total genomic DNA according to the PE standard protocol (Illumina, San Diego, USA) and sequenced on an Illumina HiSeq2000 at Macrogen (http://www.macrogen.com/kor/). Low-quality bases with raw scores of 20 or less were removed and high-quality PE reads were assembled using a CLC genome assembler (CLC Inc., Aarhus, Denmark) (Kim et al. 2015). The reference chloroplast genome sequence of Epipremnum aureum (KR872391, Tian et al. 2018) was used to retrieve principal contigs representing the chloroplast genome from the total contigs using the Nucmer tool (Kurtz et al. 2004). The representative chloroplast contigs were arranged in order based on BLASTZ analysis (Schwartz et al. 2003) with the reference sequence and connected to a single draft sequence by joining overlapping terminal sequences. The chloroplast genome was annotated using the GeSeq program (Tillich et al. 2017) followed by manual curation with the results of blast searches. The circular genome map and the schematic map of the trans- and cis-splicing genes were generated using the CPGView software (Liu et al. 2023, http://www.1kmpg.cn/cpgview/).

Phylogenetic analysis was conducted with 73 consensus coding sequences of the variety ‘Silky White’ and 17 published species in the Araceae family by using a maximum likelihood method with a general time reversible model and 1000 bootstrap options in MEGA 6.0 (Tamura et al. 2013).

Results

In total, approximately 2.97 Gbp of raw data were obtained and approximately 2.38 Gbp of clean reads were assembled. The average coverage was 854.73, and the mapped read depth across the whole region was more than 360, except four regions, where it was 198, 199, 251, and 286 (Supplementary Figure 1). The whole chloroplast genome sequence of the calla lily variety ‘Silky White’ was found to be 168,880 bp in length with a typical double-stranded loop structure (Figure 2). Its structure is typically quadripartite and divided into four regions consisting of a large single copy (LSC) region of 89,391 bp, a small single copy (SSC) region of 31,849 bp, and two inverted repeat regions (IRa and IRb) of 23,820 bp. The overall GC content was 35.59%. The BLASTN search results showed that the chloroplast genome has a strong similarity to those of Z. aethiopica (99.94%), E. aureum (95.77%), some species of the genus Anubias (95.18%–95.43%), and some species of the genus Colocasia (94.86%–95.01%) in the family Araceae. The chloroplast genome contained a total of 161 genes, with an average size of 595.0 bp, including 105 protein-coding genes, 48 transfer RNA genes, and 8 ribosomal RNA genes with average sizes of 796.7 bp, 64.5 bp, and 1,129.0 bp, respectively (Figure 2). Eleven, nine, and four of protein-coding genes, tRNA genes, and rRNA genes are duplicated in the IR regions, respectively. Thirteen genes (rps16, atpF, rpoC1, ycf3, clpP, petB, petD, rpl16, rpl2, ndhB, and ndhA; ndhB and rpl2 are duplicated in the IR regions) are cis-splicing genes (Supplementary Figure 2A) and the rps12 gene is a trans-splicing gene (Supplementary Figure 2B). The results of chloroplast genome assembly and annotation were submitted to GenBank (http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/) under accession number MK327553. Phylogenetic analysis results that the variety ‘Silky White’ belongs to the large clade with diverse Zantedeschia species (Figure 3).

Discussion and conclusion

In this study, we present the complete chloroplast genome of the hybrid variety ‘Silky White’, a white-flowered calla lily. Most features of the genome are highly similar to those of other diverse Zantedeschia species including a few cultivated calla lily varieties listed in Figure 3. Structure and gene composition are also very similar or identical to those of diverse higher plants, including Solanum species (Park 2023, 2024). In length, however, it was much shorter than those of other species in the genus Zantedeschia (calla lilies with colored flowers) including the Zantedeschia hybrid variety ‘Gagsi’ (MK327551; 175,448 bp), Z. elliottinana (MH743153; 175,906 bp) and Z. rehmannii (MH743154; 175,067 bp) (He et al. 2020; Henriquez et al. 2020; Kim et al. 2020), although it was highly similar to those (MH743155; 168,065 bp, MN551189; 168,036 bp) of the other Z. aethiopica (calla lilies with white flowers). Phylogenetic analysis also confirmed that there are two subgroups clearly separated by flower colors in the large clade with diverse Zantedeschia species (Figure 3).

The chloroplast genome sequence of the calla lily variety ‘Silky White’ was completed and characterized in this study. The length and structure of the chloroplast genome are comparable to those of other Zantedeschia species. Our findings will facilitate further research investigating more detailed breeding and evolutionary aspects.

CHOQUET INTEGRATION WITH SUBMODULAR FUNCTION ON MEASURABLE SPACE WITH SIGMA-ALGEBRA GENERATING CHAIN


Abstract
Based on a study of a formula representing submodular set function as a supremum of measures dominated by the set function, we present a corresponding formula for a Choquet integration with respect to the set function, on a measurable space which has a chain of measurable set generating the sigma-algebra. As an application we reproduce a basic formula in mathematical finance on law invariant coherent risk measures. We also study a recursion relation of set functions for which the representation formula characterizes the fixed point.

\displaystyle iii)\ \ {\cal I}\mbox{ is a chain, i.e., totally ordered with respect to inclusion,}\\
\displaystyle\phantom{iii)}\mbox{i.e., for all $I_{1},I_{2}\in{\cal I}$

In this section, we fix a measurable space

3.1
Choquet integration

Let
v:\ {\cal F}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a non-decreasing set function,
satisfying
v(\emptyset)=0
, and
f:\ \Omega\to{\mathbb{R}}
a
real valued measurable function.

Note that if we define
\displaystyle g:\ {\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
by

\displaystyle g(z)=v(\{\omega\in\Omega\mid f(\omega)>z\})
,
and
\displaystyle g^{-1}(a)=\sup_{g(z)>a}z
for
a\geqq 0
,
non-decreasing property of
v
implies

\displaystyle\{z\in{\mathbb{R}}\mid g(z)>a\}=(-\infty,g^{-1}(a))
,
hence
g
is a
1
-dimensional Borel measurable function,
and we can consider the standard
1
-dimensional
Lebesgue integration of
g
.
We define

(20)

v(f)=\lim_{y\to-\infty}\biggl(y\,v(\Omega)+\int_{y}^{\infty}v(\{\omega\in\Omega\mid f(\omega)>z\})\,dz\biggr)

whenever the right-hand side is a real value.
If either the Lebesgue integration or the limit diverges
in the right-hand side of (
20
) we do not define
v(f)
.
If
v(f)
of (
20
) is defined it is equal to
the asymmetric integral in terms of
[
4
, Chap. 5]
.
We will refer to (
20
) as Choquet integration
(of
f
with respect to
v
) in this paper.

If
f(\omega)\geqq x
,
\omega\in\Omega
, holds for an
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
then

\int_{y}^{x}v(\{\omega\in\Omega\mid f(\omega)>z\})dz=(x-y)\,v(\Omega),\ \ y\leqq x,

hence in this case (
20
) has a simpler expression

(21)

v(f)=x\,v(\Omega)+\int_{x}^{\infty}v(\{\omega\in\Omega\mid f(\omega)>z\})\,dz.

Simple function, a measurable function whose image is a finite set,
is an elementary example of Choquet integrable function.
We find it convenient to adopt a representation

(22)

f=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\mathop{\mathbf{1}}\nolimits_{A_{i}}\,,

where

(23)

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle a_{i}\geqq 0,\ i=1,\ldots,n-1,\ a_{n}\in{\mathbb{R}},\\
\displaystyle\emptyset=A_{0}\subset A_{1}\subset A_{2}\subset\cdots\subset A_{n-1}\subset A_{n}=\Omega.\end{array}

Here and in the following,
we use a symbol
\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbf{1}}\nolimits_{A}
to denote a characteristic function
of a set
A

defined by
\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbf{1}}\nolimits_{A}(\omega)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1,&\omega\in A,\\
0,&\omega\not\in A.\end{array}\right.

(We use
\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbf{1}}\nolimits_{\cdot}
on any space.)

We then have

\displaystyle A_{i}=\{\omega\in\Omega\mid f(\omega)\geqq\sum_{j=i}^{n}a_{j}\}\in{\cal F},\ i=1,\ldots,n,

hence (
21
) with
x=a_{n}
and (
23
) implies

(24)

v(f)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\,v(A_{i}).

Note that
f
of (
22
) takes values

\displaystyle b_{i}=\sum_{j=i}^{n}a_{j}
,
i=1,\ldots,n
, with

b_{1}\geqq b_{2}\geqq\cdots\geqq b_{n}=a_{n}
.
With
\displaystyle B_{i}=A_{i}\cap A_{i-1}^{c}
,
i=1,\ldots,n
, we have

B_{i}\cap B_{j}=\emptyset
,
i\neq j
,
\displaystyle\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}B_{i}=A_{n}=\Omega
, and

\displaystyle f=\sum_{i=1}^{n}b_{i}\mathop{\mathbf{1}}\nolimits_{B_{i}}
, which perhaps is closer to
a familiar expression of a simple function.
With this expression, (
24
) implies

\displaystyle v(f)=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}(b_{i}-b_{i+1})\,v(\bigcup_{j=1}^{i}B_{j})+b_{n}v(\Omega)
.

3.2
Monotone convergene theorem

Hereafter we assume that
v:\ {\cal F}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is
a continuous and non-decreasing set function,
We then have an analog of monotone convergence theorem
for the Choquet integration with respect to

.
To state the theorem,

.
We say that a measurable set

is a
v
-null set, if

v(N\cup A)=v(N^{c}\cap A)=v(A),\ A\in{\cal F}.

For a sequence of measurable functions

,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}

-almost surely to

there exists a
v
-null set

such that

(26)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}f_{n}(\omega)=f(\omega),\ \omega\in N^{c}.

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}v(\tilde{f}_{n})=v(\tilde{f}),

v
-almost surely.
Also
\displaystyle\tilde{f}_{n}=f_{n}\vee y_{0}
and (

(29)

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle v(\tilde{f}_{n})=y_{0}\,v(\Omega)+\int_{y_{0}}^{\infty}v(\{\omega\in\Omega\mid\tilde{f}_{n}(\omega)>z\})\,dz\\
\displaystyle\phantom{v(\tilde{f}_{n})}=y_{0}\,v(\Omega)+\int_{y_{0}}^{\infty}v(\{\omega\in\Omega\mid f_{n}(\omega)>z\})\,dz,\ n\in{\mathbb{N}},\end{array}

hence
(

0\leqq v(\tilde{f}_{1})-v(f_{1})\leqq\epsilon.

Also, (

) and (

) imply

(31)

v(\tilde{f}_{n})-v(f_{n})=\lim_{y\to-\infty}\biggl((y_{0}-y)\,v(\Omega)-\int_{y}^{y_{0}}v(\{\omega\in\Omega\mid f_{n}(\omega)>z\})\,dz\biggr),\ n\in{\mathbb{N}},

Since

is pointwise non-decreasing in

and since

is non-decreasing

is also non-decreasing in

. Therefore (

(32)

v(\tilde{f}_{1})-v(f_{1})\geqq v(\tilde{f}_{2})-v(f_{2})\geqq\cdots\geqq\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}(v(\tilde{f}_{n})-v(f_{n}))\geqq 0

The argument between (

),
applied to the function

(33)

Combining (

),
(

(34)

\epsilon\geqq\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}v(\tilde{f}_{n})-\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}v(f_{n})=v(\tilde{f})-\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}v(f_{n})\geqq v(f)-\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}v(f_{n}).

As in standard measure theory, a simple function approximation of

3.3
Representation formula for Choquet integration and coherent risk measure

The representation (

in the framework of Theorem

implies
corresponding formula for Choquet integrations.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
(\Omega,{\cal F})
be a measurable space satisfying
{\mathbb{\cal X}}\neq\emptyset
.
Let
v:\ {\cal F}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a non-decreasing and continuous submodular
function satisfying
v(\emptyset)=0
.
Then for a bounded measurable function
\displaystyle f:\ \Omega\to{\mathbb{R}}

(36)

v(f)=\sup_{{\cal I}\in{\mathbb{\cal X}}}\int_{\Omega}f(\omega)\,\mu_{v,{\cal I}}(d\omega)

holds.

Proof..

Since
f
is bounded and measurable,

f
is both
v
-integrable and integrable with respect to
(finite) measures.
Also
since (
21
) implies
\displaystyle v(f+a)=av(\Omega)+v(f)
,
we may assume from beginning that
f
is non-negative in the proof of
(
36
) by adding a positive constant to

.
Let
f_{k}
,

, be the series of
simple function approximations (
35
) of

.
For simplicity of notation we follow that of (
23
)
and write (

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle f_{k}=\sum_{i=1}^{n_{k}}a^{(k)}_{i}\mathop{\mathbf{1}}\nolimits_{A^{(k)}_{i}}\,,\ \ a^{(k)}_{i}>0,\ i=1,\ldots,n_{k}-1,\ a^{(k)}_{n_{k}}=0,\\
\displaystyle A^{(k)}_{1}\subset A^{(k)}_{2}\subset\cdots\subset A^{(k)}_{n_{k}}=\Omega,\ \ {\cal A}_{k}=\{A^{(k)}_{i}\mid i=1,\ldots,n_{k}\}.\end{array}

Note that we can put
a^{(k)}_{n_{k}}=0
because we assume that
f
is
non-negative.
Then (
24
) implies

\displaystyle v(f_{k})=\sum_{i=1}^{n_{k}}a^{(k)}_{i}\,v(A^{(k)}_{i})
.

As noted below (
13
),
the definition of the continuity of
v
implies
that (
8
) uniquely defines a measure

\displaystyle\mu_{v,{\cal I}}:\ {\cal F}\to{\mathbb{R}}
, for each
{\cal I}\in{\mathbb{\cal X}}
.
In particular, for
\displaystyle{\cal I}_{{\cal A}_{k}}\in{\mathbb{\cal X}}
, the insertion of
a chain
\displaystyle{\cal A}_{k}
to
{\cal I}\in{\mathbb{\cal X}}
we have, from
(
8
),

(37)

v(f_{k})=\sum_{i=1}^{n_{k}}a^{(k)}_{i}v(A^{(k)}_{i})=\sum_{i=1}^{n_{k}}a^{(k)}_{i}\mu_{v,{\cal I}_{{\cal A}_{k}}}(A^{(k)}_{i})=\int_{\Omega}f_{k}(\omega)\,\mu_{v,{\cal I}_{{\cal A}_{k}}}(d\omega).

Also, Lemma
2.4
implies, as in the proof of (a) to (b) in

,

\displaystyle\mu_{v,{\cal I}}(A)\leqq v(A)
,
A\in{\cal F}
,

.
Therefore,

(38)

v(f_{k})=\sum_{i=1}^{n_{k}}a^{(k)}_{i}v(A^{(k)}_{i})\geqq\sum_{i=1}^{n_{k}}a^{(k)}_{i}\mu_{v,{\cal I}}(A^{(k)}_{i})=\int_{\Omega}f_{k}(\omega)\,\mu_{v,{\cal I}}(d\omega).\ \ {\cal I}\in{\mathbb{\cal X}}.

Since
f_{k}
is pointwise non-decreasing in

,

(39)

\int_{\Omega}f_{k}(\omega)\,\mu_{v,{\cal I}}(d\omega)\leqq\int_{\Omega}f(\omega)\,\mu_{v,{\cal I}}(d\omega),\ {\cal I}\in{\mathbb{\cal X}},\mbox{ and }v(f_{k})\leqq v(f),\ \ k\in{\mathbb{N}}.

The standard monotone convergence theorem for measures implies

(40)

(\forall\epsilon>0)(\forall{\cal I}\in{\mathbb{\cal X}})\exists k_{0}\in{\mathbb{N}};\ (\forall k\geqq k_{0})\ \int_{\Omega}f(\omega)\,\mu_{v,{\cal I}}(d\omega)\leqq\int_{\Omega}f_{k}(\omega)\,\mu_{v,{\cal I}}(d\omega)+\epsilon

and Theorem
3.1
implies

(41)

(\forall\epsilon>0)\exists k_{0}\in{\mathbb{N}};\ (\forall k\geqq k_{0})\ v(f)\leqq v(f_{k})+\epsilon.

Combining (
38
), (
39
), and
(

), we see that

\displaystyle v(f)\geqq\int_{\Omega}f(\omega)\,\mu_{v,{\cal I}}(d\omega)-\epsilon

for any
{\cal I}\in{\mathbb{\cal X}}
and

. Hence

(42)

v(f)\geqq\sup_{{\cal I}\in{\mathbb{\cal X}}}\int_{\Omega}f(\omega)\,\mu_{v,{\cal I}}(d\omega).

Similarly,
combining (

), (
39
), and
(

\epsilon>0
there exists

k_{0}\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that if

\displaystyle v(f)\leqq\int_{\Omega}f(\omega)\,\mu_{v,{\cal I}_{{\cal A}_{k}}}(d\omega)+\epsilon\leqq\sup_{{\cal I}\in{\mathbb{\cal X}}}\int_{\Omega}f(\omega)\,\mu_{v,{\cal I}}(d\omega)+\epsilon
.
Hence
\displaystyle v(f)\leqq\sup_{{\cal I}\in{\mathbb{\cal X}}}\int_{\Omega}f(\omega)\,\mu_{v,{\cal I}}(d\omega)

∎

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
(\Omega,{\cal F})
be a measurable space satisfying
{\mathbb{\cal X}}\neq\emptyset
.
Let
v:\ {\cal F}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a non-decreasing and continuous submodular
function satisfying
v(\emptyset)=0
, and
define a function
\rho
on a set of bounded measurable functions
by
\displaystyle\rho(f)=\frac{v(-f)}{v(\Omega)}
.
Then the following hold.

non-negativity:

If
\displaystyle f\geqq 0
then
\displaystyle\rho(f)\leqq 0
,

subadditivity:

\displaystyle\rho(f+g)\leqq\rho(f)+\rho(g)
,

positive homogeneity:

If
\displaystyle\lambda\geqq 0
then
\displaystyle\rho(\lambda f)=\lambda\rho(f)
,

translational invariance:

If
a\in{\mathbb{R}}
then
\displaystyle\rho(f+a)=\rho(f)-a
.

Proof..

The claims are straightforward consequences of
(
36
) in Theorem
3.2
.
For example,

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\rho(f+g)=-\frac{1}{v(\Omega)}\,\inf_{{\cal I}\in{\mathbb{\cal X}}}\int_{\Omega}(f(\omega)+g(\omega))\,\mu_{v,{\cal I}}(d\omega)\\
\displaystyle\phantom{\rho(f+g)}\leqq-\frac{1}{v(\Omega)}\,\inf_{{\cal I}\in{\mathbb{\cal X}}}\int_{\Omega}f(\omega)\,\mu_{v,{\cal I}}(d\omega)-\frac{1}{v(\Omega)}\,\inf_{{\cal I}\in{\mathbb{\cal X}}}\int_{\Omega}g(\omega))\,\mu_{v,{\cal I}}(d\omega)=\rho(f)+\rho(g),\end{array}

which proves subadditivity.
Proofs of non-negativity, positive homogeneity, and translational invariance
are easier.
∎

In the field of mathematical finance,
the set of properties in Corollary
3.3
is known
to be the definition that
\rho
is a coherent risk measure

[
1
,
2
,
6
,
7
]
.
Thus Corollary
3.3
relates
the Choquet integration with respect to a sub-modular set function
to a coherent risk measure.
This observation motivates reformulating in our framework the results
found in mathematical finance, which we consider in the next section
§

4
Choquet integration for uniform case and law invariant coherent risk measure

In
[
6

, coherent risk measure is studied on probability spaces.
Given a probability measure
{\mathrm{P}}

{\mathrm{P[\;}}A{\mathrm{\;]}}={\mathrm{P[\;}}B{\mathrm{\;]}}
implies

.
In our approach, we started with a measurable space
(\Omega,{\cal F})

without probability measure, hence
we are free to intruduce a (finite) measure
\nu:\ {\cal F}\to{\mathbb{R}}
and
define law invariance as
\nu(A)=\nu(B)
implies

In addition, it is assumed in
[
6

that
(\Omega,{\cal F},{\mathrm{P}})

is a standard probability space and that
{\mathrm{P}}
is non-atomic,
and with these assumptions, the proof is reduced to
the
1
-dimensional Borel measurable space on an interval

(\Omega,{\cal F})=([0,1),{\cal B}_{1}([0,1)))

specified as the
1

Here, we keep the only assumption
{\mathbb{\cal X}}\neq\emptyset
,
existence of a chain generating
{\cal F}
, for
(\Omega,{\cal F})
,

[
6
]

is deduced in our framework.

As a simple example of submodular function whose dependence on the
variable
A\in{\cal F}
is given through
\nu(A)
, we note the follwing.
In the following, for
a,b\in{\mathbb{R}}
such that

we write

and

.
For example,
a+b=(a\vee b)+(a\wedge b)
and

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
(\Omega,{\cal F})
be a measurable space,
\nu:\ {\cal F}\to{\mathbb{R}}

a measure on the space, and
c\in{\mathbb{R}}
.
Then the set function
v:\ {\cal F}\to{\mathbb{R}}
defined by

\displaystyle v(A)=c\wedge\nu(A)
,
A\in{\cal F}
, is submodular, i.e., satisfies
(
3
).

Proof..

Let
A,B\in{\cal F}
. Then additivity, non-negativity, and monotonicity
of the measure
\nu
imply

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle v(A)+v(B)-v(A\cup B)-v(A\cap B)\\
\displaystyle{}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle c+c-c-c=0,&\displaystyle c\leqq\nu(A\cap B),\\
\displaystyle c+c-\nu(A\cap B)-c\geqq 0,&\displaystyle\nu(A\cap B)<c\leqq\nu(A)\wedge\nu(B),\\
\displaystyle(\nu(A)\wedge\nu(B))+c-\nu(A\cap B)-c\geqq 0,&\displaystyle\nu(A)\wedge\nu(B)<c\leqq\nu(A)\vee\nu(B),\\
\displaystyle\nu(A)+\nu(B)-\nu(A\cap B)-c&\\
\displaystyle=\nu(A\cup B)-c\geqq 0,&\displaystyle\nu(A)\vee\nu(B)<c\leqq\nu(A\cup B),\\
\displaystyle\nu(A)+\nu(B)-\nu(A\cup B)-\nu(A\cap B)=0,&\displaystyle\nu(A\cup B)\leqq c.\end{array}\right.\end{array}

which proves (
3
).
∎

Submodularlity (
3

multiplication of positive reals. This leads to considering

\displaystyle v(A)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}_{+}}}g(z)\wedge\nu(A)\,dz

If we choose the parameter

as a distribution function of a measure.
These considerations suggest considering a following form for a

whose depedence on variable

Theorem 4.2

(\Omega,{\cal F})

(ii)

(iii)

In particular, it holds that

(47)

v_{\mu}(A)\geqq\sup\{\mu^{\prime}(A)\mid\mu^{\prime}\in{\cal M}(\Omega),\ \mu^{\prime}\ll\nu,\ F_{\mu^{\prime}}=F_{\mu}\},\ A\in{\cal F}.

Proof..

(i)

Since
\nu
is a measure,
\nu(\emptyset)=0
,
hence
\displaystyle v_{\mu}(\emptyset)=0

(ii)

Note a basic formula

\int_{0}^{\infty}\nu(f>z)\,dz:=\int_{0}^{\infty}\nu(\{\omega\in\Omega\mid f(\omega)>z\})\,dz=\int_{\Omega}f\,d\nu,

As in

, we assume comonotonicity for

We say that the functions

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
(\Omega,{\cal F})
be a measurable space satisfying
{\mathbb{\cal X}}\neq\emptyset
,
and
\nu\in{\cal M}(\Omega)

For a finite measure

{\cal I}_{\mu}\in{\mathbb{\cal X}}\mbox{ and }\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\nolimits F_{\mu}\supset\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\nolimits\nu,

v_{\mu}(f)=\int_{\Omega}f(\omega)\,\mu(d\omega)=\sup\{\int_{\Omega}f(\omega)\mu^{\prime}(d\omega)\mid\mu^{\prime}\in{\cal M}(\Omega),\ \mu^{\prime}\ll\nu,\ F_{\mu^{\prime}}=F_{\mu}\}

Proof..

Note first that

\displaystyle y_{0}:\ {\mathbb{R}_{+}}\to{\mathbb{R}_{+}}

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\nu(\{\omega\in\Omega\mid f(\omega)>y\})=\nu(\Omega)-F_{\mu}(y_{0}(y))\\
\displaystyle{}=\nu(\{\omega\in\Omega\mid\displaystyle\frac{d\,\mu}{d\nu}(\omega)>y_{0}(y)\}),\ \ y\in{\mathbb{R}_{+}}.\end{array}

The definiton (

) of

,
(

), and
(

) in Theorem

imply

If the total set is a finite set, then

if a set function is finitely additive then it is
\sigma

(
\sigma

and all

Hereafter, we assume that the cardinality
m

\displaystyle\Omega=\Omega_{m}

\displaystyle m\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
and assume for simplicity
\displaystyle{\cal F}=2^{\Omega_{m}}
, and

\displaystyle v_{n+1}=\sup_{{\cal I}\in{\mathbb{\cal X}}}\mu_{v_{n},{\cal I}}
,

\displaystyle v_{0}:\ 2^{\Omega_{m}}\to{\mathbb{R}_{+}}

\displaystyle v_{0}(\emptyset)=0

.

\displaystyle\Omega_{m}=\{\omega_{i}\mid i=1,\ldots,m\}

\displaystyle\sharp{\mathbb{\cal X}}
of

\displaystyle\sharp{\mathbb{\cal X}}\leqq m!

There are at most

numbers to compare in the supremum

If the limit is attained after a finite iteration of

\displaystyle v_{n+1}=v_{n}

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
\Omega_{m}
be a set of cardinality

\displaystyle\sharp\Omega_{m}=m
.

(i)

For any set function
\displaystyle v:\ 2^{\Omega_{m}}\to{\mathbb{R}_{+}}

,
then
\displaystyle v(A\cap B)+v(A\cup B)=v(A)+v(B)

(i)
A=\emptyset
, or (ii)
B=\emptyset
, or (iii)
A=\Omega_{m}
,
or (iv)
B=\Omega_{m}
, or (v)
A\subset B
, or (vi)
A\supset B
, or
(vii)
A=B
.

(ii)

Let
\displaystyle v_{0}:\ 2^{\Omega_{m}}\to{\mathbb{R}_{+}}
be a non-decreasing set function
satisfying
\displaystyle v_{0}(\emptyset)=0
, and for
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
let
v_{n}:\ 2^{\Omega_{m}}\to{\mathbb{R}_{+}}
be the non-decreasing set function
determined by the recursion relation (
10

Then for any positive integer

,
if one of the following (i)–(iv) holds for a pair of sets
A,B\subset\Omega_{m}
,
then
\displaystyle v_{n}(A\cap B)+v_{n}(A\cup B)\leqq v_{n}(A)+v_{n}(B)
holds;
(i)
A\cap B=\emptyset
,
(ii)
A\cup B=\Omega_{m}
,
(iii)
\sharp A=1
,
(iv)
\sharp A=m-1
,

\sharp A
denotes the cardinality of (number of elements in) the set

.

Proof..

(i)

By interchanging
A
and
B

If
A=\emptyset

\displaystyle v(A\cap B)+v(A\cup B)=v(\emptyset)+v(B)=v(A)+v(B)

If
B=\Omega_{m}
then

\displaystyle v(A\cap B)+v(A\cup B)=v(A)+v(\Omega_{m})=v(A)+v(B)

If

\displaystyle v(A\cap B)+v(A\cup B)=v(A)+v(B)

(ii)

(i) If

\displaystyle v_{n}(A\cup B)=\mu_{v_{n-1},{\cal I}}(A\cup B)

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle v_{n}(A\cap B)+v_{n}(A\cup B)=\mu_{v_{n-1},{\cal I}}(A\cup B)=\mu_{v_{n-1},{\cal I}}(A)+\mu_{v_{n-1},{\cal I}}(B)\\

.

If

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
\displaystyle m=\sharp\Omega_{m}\in{\mathbb{N}}
and

\displaystyle v_{0}:\ 2^{\Omega_{m}}\to{\mathbb{R}_{+}}

\displaystyle v_{0}(\emptyset)=0

\displaystyle g:\ \{1,2,\ldots,m\}\to{\mathbb{R}_{+}}

\displaystyle v_{0}(A)=g(\sharp A)
,

, and hence

Proof..

Let

m

\Omega_{m}

(72)

S_{m}=\{(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{m})\mid\{\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{m}\}=\Omega_{m}\},

\displaystyle\sigma=(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{m})\in S_{m}

{\mathbb{\cal X}}=\{{\cal I}_{\sigma}\mid\sigma\in S_{m}\}.

The definition (
8
) of extremal measure implies,
with the assumption
\displaystyle v_{0}(A)=g(\sharp A)
,

(75)

\mu_{v_{0},{\cal I}_{\sigma}}(\{\sigma_{i}\})=v_{0}(\{\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{i}\})-v_{0}(\{\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{i-1}\})=g(i)-g(i-1),\ \ i=1,\ldots,m.

Note that
\displaystyle g(0)=v_{0}(\emptyset)=0
.
Since
v_{0}
is non-decreasing, so is
g
.
In particular,
g(i)-g(i-1)\geqq 0
,
i=1,\ldots,m
.

Choose the rearrangement
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m})
of
first
m
positive integers
\{1,\ldots,m\}
such that

In particular, (
75
) and
(
76
) imply

\displaystyle g(\alpha_{j})-g(\alpha_{j}-1)=\mu_{v_{0},{\cal I}_{\sigma}}(\{\sigma_{\alpha_{j}}\})
,
\sigma\in S_{m}
.

Put
\displaystyle\Omega_{m}=\{\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{m}\}
.
Since
\displaystyle\{\sigma_{\alpha_{j}}\mid j=1,\ldots,m\}=\Omega_{m}

there exists
\sigma\in S_{m}

\displaystyle h_{\sigma}:\ \{1,\ldots,m\}\to\{1,\ldots,m\}

\displaystyle\sigma_{\alpha_{h_{\sigma}(i)}}=\omega_{i}

\mu_{v_{0},{\cal I}_{\sigma}}(\{\omega_{i}\})=g(\alpha_{h_{\sigma}(i)})-g(\alpha_{h_{\sigma}(i)}-1),\ i=1,\ldots,m.

\displaystyle A\in 2^{\Omega_{m}}

v_{1}(A)=\sup_{\sigma\in S_{m}}\mu_{v_{0},{\cal I}_{\sigma}}(A)=\sup_{\sigma\in S_{m}}\sum_{\omega_{i}\in A}\mu_{v_{0},{\cal I}_{\sigma}}(\{\omega_{i}\})=\sup_{\sigma\in S_{m}}\sum_{\omega_{i}\in A}(g(\alpha_{h_{\sigma}(i)})-g(\alpha_{h_{\sigma}(i)}-1)).

Supremum is attained by choosing largest
\sharp A

(

(78)

\displaystyle v_{1}=\sup_{{\cal I}\in{\mathbb{\cal X}}}\mu_{v_{1},{\cal I}}

A Recursive Total Least Squares Solution for Bearing-Only Target Motion Analysis and Circumnavigation
Lin Li, Xueming Liu, Zhoujingzi Qiu, Tianjiang Hu, Qingrui Zhang

Abstract
Bearing-only Target Motion Analysis (TMA) is a promising technique for passive tracking in various applications as a bearing angle is easy to measure. Despite its advantages, bearing-only TMA is challenging due to the nonlinearity of the bearing measurement model and the lack of range information, which impairs observability and estimator convergence. This paper addresses these issues by proposing a Recursive Total Least Squares (RTLS) method for online target localization and tracking using mobile observers. The RTLS approach, inspired by previous results on Total Least Squares (TLS), mitigates biases in position estimation and improves computational efficiency compared to pseudo-linear Kalman filter (PLKF) methods. Additionally, we propose a circumnavigation controller to enhance system observability and estimator convergence by guiding the mobile observer in orbit around the target. Extensive simulations and experiments are performed to demonstrate the effectiveness and robustness of the proposed method. The proposed algorithm is also compared with the state-of-the-art approaches, which confirms its superior performance in terms of both accuracy and stability.

II
Problem Statement

In this section, the TMA and circumnavigation problem are formulated. For a better introduction to the algorithm, it is assumed that the target of interest moves with constant velocity along a straight-line trajectory in a two-dimensional plane, as shown in Fig.
2
. In this case, it is possible to characterize the kinematics of the target using the initial position
\bm{p}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and the initial velocity
\bm{v}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, which correspond to the core TMA parameters
[
12
,
9
,
21
]
. Let
\bm{p}_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
be the target position at time
k\Delta t
and
\bm{v}_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
be the velocity also at time
k\Delta t
. Hence, one has

\bm{p}_{k}=\bm{p}_{0}+k\Delta t\bm{v}_{0},\quad\bm{v}_{k}=\bm{v}_{0}

(1)

where
\bm{p}_{k}\triangleq\left[p^{x}_{k},p^{y}_{k}\right]^{T}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
denotes the discrete time index and
\Delta t
represents the sampling time interval.

The TMA process is conducted by a mobile observer (
e.g.
a quadrotor) that performs real-time localization and tracking of a moving target using bearing-only measurements. The mobile observer’s dynamics follow a first-order discrete-time model with bounded control input

\bm{p}_{o,k+1}=\bm{p}_{o,k}+\Delta t\bm{u}_{o,k},\quad\|\bm{u}_{o,k}\|\leq U

(2)

where
\bm{p}_{o,k}\triangleq\left[p^{x}_{o,k},p^{y}_{o,k}\right]^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
represents the mobile observer’s position vector, and
\bm{u}_{o,k}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
denotes the control input with a upper bound
U
.

Fig. 2:
Illustration of bearings-only target motion analysis.

It is assumed that the mobile observer only has access to bearing angle measurements
\tilde{\theta}_{k}
relative to the motion target at each time step
k
. The measurement model is, therefore, expressed as

\tilde{\theta}_{k}=\theta_{k}+\mu_{\theta,k},\quad\theta_{k}=\tan^{-1}\frac{p^{y}_{k}-p^{y}_{o,k}}{p^{x}_{k}-p^{x}_{o,k}}

(3)

where
\theta_{k}
represents the true bearing angle,
\mu_{\theta,k}\backsim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_{\theta}^{2})
denotes zero-mean Gaussian measurement noise with variance
\sigma_{\theta}^{2}
. It is assumed that a mobile observer needs to conduct self-localization, which is a common case in severe applications,
e.g.
, in GPS-denied environments. The mobile observer’s self-localization is commonly subject to measurement errors, so one has

\tilde{\bm{p}}_{o,k}=\bm{p}_{o,k}+\bm{\mu}_{\bm{p},k},\quad\bm{\mu}_{\bm{p},k}\backsim\mathcal{N}\left(\bm{0},\sigma_{p}^{2}\bm{I}\right)

(4)

where
\tilde{\bm{p}}_{o,k}\triangleq\left[\tilde{p}^{x}_{k},\tilde{p}^{y}_{k}\right]^{T}
represents the measured position vector, and
\bm{\mu}_{\bm{p},k}\triangleq\left[\mu^{x}_{k},\mu^{y}_{k}\right]^{T}
denotes an independent bivariate Gaussian noise with zero mean and diagonal covariance matrix
\sigma_{p}^{2}\bm{I}
.

It should be noted that there are always errors in a mobile observer’s self-localization, even in the case where GPS signals are available. Hence, Eq. (
4
) is a common measurement model.

In this paper, we aim to find a robust and reliable solution to persistently estimate the motion of a target with a mobile observer. Hence, there are two sub-tasks:

1.

Developing a robust estimation framework to estimate a moving target’s position and velocity using noisy bearing measurements and imperfect mobile observer self-localization data.

2.

Designing a circumnavigation control strategy that can generate feasible motion for a mobile observer to ensure estimator convergence and maintain stable target tracking performance.

III
measurement models

According to the geometric relationship between a target and a mobile observer, the bearing angle
\theta_{k}
satisfies the following trigonometric equation.

\tan\theta_{k}=\frac{\sin\theta_{k}}{\cos\theta_{k}}=\frac{p^{y}_{k}-p^{y}_{o,k}}{p^{x}_{k}-p^{x}_{o,k}}

(5)

where both measurement noises and self-localization errors are ignored at this stage. Through a straightforward mathematical transformation of (
5
), it is easy to derive

\begin{bmatrix}\sin\theta_{k}&-\cos\theta_{k}\end{bmatrix}\bm{p}_{o,k}=\begin{bmatrix}\sin\theta_{k}&-\cos\theta_{k}\end{bmatrix}\bm{p}_{k}

(6)

Following (
1
), the TMA parameter, which corresponds to the initial conditions of the moving target, is defined as
\bm{x}=\begin{bmatrix}\bm{p}_{0}^{T}&\bm{v}_{0}^{T}\end{bmatrix}^{T}
, so one has

\bm{p}_{k}=\bm{M}_{k}\bm{x},\quad\bm{M}_{k}\triangleq\begin{bmatrix}\bm{I}&k\Delta t\bm{I}\end{bmatrix}

(7)

Here,
\bm{I}
denotes the identity matrix.Substituting (
7
) into (
6
) results in the linear measurement model as given in (
8
).

y_{k}=\bm{h}_{k}^{T}\bm{x}

(8)

where

\displaystyle y_{k}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}\sin\theta_{k}&-\cos\theta_{k}\end{bmatrix}\bm{p}_{o,k}

\displaystyle\bm{h}_{k}^{T}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}\sin\theta_{k}&-\cos\theta_{k}\end{bmatrix}\bm{M}_{k}

Note that the measurement model (
8
) is derived in an ideal condition, namely ignoring the existence of measurement errors. However, in real applications, there are also noises or biases in measurements as given in (
3
) and (
4
). Hence, with the consideration of measurement noises, the ideal values in (
8
) should be replaced by their true counterparts, leading to a nonlinear scenario where errors show up in sinusoidal and cosinusoidal functions. It is necessary to apply a first-order approximation
[
14
]
,
i.e.

\sin\tilde{\theta}_{k}=\sin\theta_{k}+\mu_{\theta,k}\cos\theta
and
\cos\tilde{\theta}_{k}=\cos\theta_{k}-\mu_{\theta,k}\sin\theta
. One, therefore, has

\displaystyle\tilde{y}_{k}=\begin{bmatrix}\sin\tilde{\theta}_{k}&-\cos\tilde{\theta}_{k}\end{bmatrix}\tilde{\bm{p}}_{o,k}=y_{k}+\delta y_{k}

(9)

where

\delta y_{k}=(\cos\theta_{k}\tilde{p}^{x}_{k}+\sin\theta_{k}\tilde{p}^{y}_{k})\mu_{\theta,k}+\mu^{x}_{k}\sin\theta_{k}-\mu^{y}_{k}\cos\theta

and

\displaystyle\tilde{\bm{h}}_{k}^{T}=\begin{bmatrix}\sin\tilde{\theta}_{k}&-\cos\tilde{\theta}_{k}\end{bmatrix}\bm{M}_{k}=\bm{h}_{k}^{T}+\delta\bm{h}_{k}^{T}

(10)

where

\delta\bm{h}_{k}^{T}=\begin{bmatrix}\mu_{\theta,k}\cos\theta_{k}&\mu_{\theta,k}\sin\theta_{k}\end{bmatrix}\bm{M}_{k}

Both
\delta y_{k}
and
\delta\bm{h}_{k}
are characterized by zero-mean Gaussian random variables. Their covariance matrices are respectively given by

r_{y,k}=\left(\cos\theta_{k}\tilde{p}^{x}_{k}+\sin\theta_{k}\tilde{p}^{y}_{k}\right)^{2}\sigma_{\theta}^{2}+\sigma_{p}^{2}

(11)

\bm{R}_{h,k}=\bm{M}_{k}^{T}\begin{bmatrix}\cos^{2}\theta_{k}&\sin\theta_{k}\cos\theta_{k}\\
\sin\theta_{k}\cos\theta_{k}&\sin^{2}\theta_{k}\end{bmatrix}\bm{M}_{k}

(12)

Eventually, the following measurement model is obtained, which has better matches with real-world scenarios.

\tilde{y}_{k}-\delta y_{k}=\left(\tilde{\bm{h}}_{k}^{T}-\delta\bm{h}_{k}^{T}\right)\bm{x}

(13)

Remark
.

The key distinction between the proposed measurement model (
13
) and conventional pseudo-linear approaches
[
7
,
5
]
lies in the independence of measurement noise from the true target-observer distance
[
14
,
11
]
. While existing methods
[
5
]
replace the true distance with its estimate, this substitution introduces two critical limitations. First, the noise covariance matrix may be overestimated during the estimator’s convergence phase, leading to degraded convergence rates. Second, errors in the mobile observer’s self-localization propagate into the distance estimation, introducing additional bias and further degrading estimation accuracy. In our measurement model, although the true value
\theta_{k}
in (
11
) and (
12
) is unavailable and should be replaced by
\tilde{\theta}_{k}
, the resulting error remains negligible due to the first-order approximation and bounded nature of trigonometric functions.

Recursive Total Least Squares Algorithm

Based on the measurement model in (
13
), the TMA parameters
\bm{x}
can be estimated by solving the following EIV model

\tilde{\bm{y}}\approx\tilde{\bm{H}}\bm{x},\quad\tilde{\bm{y}}=\bm{y}+\Delta\bm{y},\quad\tilde{\bm{H}}=\bm{H}+\Delta\bm{H}

(14)

where

\tilde{\bm{y}}=\begin{bmatrix}\tilde{y}_{0}\\
\cdots\\
\tilde{y}_{N}\end{bmatrix},\bm{y}=\begin{bmatrix}y_{0}\\
\cdots\\
y_{N}\end{bmatrix},\Delta\bm{y}=\begin{bmatrix}\delta y_{0}\\
\cdots\\
\delta y_{N}\end{bmatrix}

\tilde{\bm{H}}=\begin{bmatrix}\tilde{\bm{h}}_{0}^{T}\\
\cdots\\
\tilde{\bm{h}}_{N}^{T}\end{bmatrix},\bm{H}=\begin{bmatrix}\bm{h}_{0}^{T}\\
\cdots\\
\bm{h}_{N}^{T}\end{bmatrix},\Delta\bm{H}=\begin{bmatrix}\delta\bm{h}_{0}^{T}\\
\cdots\\
\delta\bm{h}_{N}^{T}\end{bmatrix}

To solve (
14
), we first introduce the TLS framework
[
18
]
, followed by a recursive implementation
[

,
20
]
.

IV-A

Total Least Squares

The fundamental principle of TLS is to concurrently minimize the noise effects in both
\tilde{\bm{H}}
and
\tilde{\bm{y}}
under the Frobenius norm. The generalized TLS problem can be formulated as follows
[
19
,
18
]

\begin{array}[]{c}\min_{\bm{x},\hat{\bm{Z}}}\left\lVert\bm{\Lambda}\left(\tilde{\bm{Z}}-\hat{\bm{Z}}\right)\bm{W}\right\rVert_{F}\\
\text{subject to }\hat{\bm{Z}}\begin{bmatrix}\bm{x}\\
-1\end{bmatrix}=\bm{0}\end{array}

(15)

where
\tilde{\bm{Z}}=\begin{bmatrix}\tilde{\bm{H}}&\tilde{\bm{y}}\end{bmatrix}
denotes the augmented measurement matrix, and
\hat{\bm{Z}}=\begin{bmatrix}\hat{\bm{H}}&\hat{\bm{y}}\end{bmatrix}
represents its noise-corrected counterpart. The solution
\hat{\bm{x}}
, obtained from the corrected system
\hat{\bm{H}}\bm{x}=\hat{\bm{y}}
, is referred to as the TLS estimate. The left weighting matrix
\bm{\Lambda}
is typically constructed as a diagonal matrix,
e.g.

\bm{\Lambda}=\textit{diag}\left[\lambda^{N},...,\lambda^{1},\lambda^{0}\right]
, where the diagonal elements represent exponential forgetting factors
[
19
,
22
,
23
]
. The right weighting matrix
\bm{W}
is generally chosen as the inverse of the noise covariance matrix to account for measurement uncertainty.

Total least squares (TLS), also known as Orthogonal Regression, is a type of EIV regression, in which both the dependent and independent variables are subject to error. The total least squares approximation is generically equivalent to the best, in the Frobenius norm, low-rank approximation of the data matrix. Hence, TLS is very suitable to our problem in this paper. However, TLS suffers from high computation complexity due to the need for singular value decomposition (SVD) in resolving (
15
), which is not friendly for online implementation with parsimonious onboard computation budgets. It is thus necessary to develop a recursive version.

V
Circumnavigation Control

Due to the absence of distance information, bearing-only TMA requires more restricted observability conditions to ensure the convergence of the state estimation. Theoretical analysis indicates that a mobile observer should exhibit higher-order motion relative to the target, with particular emphasis on motion components that are perpendicular to the bearing direction
[
6
,
5
]
. When a mobile observer follows an orbital trajectory around the target, observability is enhanced, which not only accelerates the convergence of the estimator but also improves the accuracy of parameter estimation
[
5
,
2
]
. It implies that one must design a proper circumnavigation law for a mobile observer to successfully localize a moving target and persistently track it.

Building on the existing theoretical findings, we propose the following circumnavigation law for a mobile observer to ensure stable behavior.

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\bm{u}_{o,k}^{f}=\left(\left\lVert\hat{\bm{p}}_{k}-\tilde{\bm{p}}_{o,k}\right\rVert-\rho\right)\tilde{\bm{g}}_{k}\\
&\bm{u}_{o,k}=\frac{\min\{U^{f},\|\bm{u}_{o,k}^{f}\|\}}{\|\bm{u}_{o,k}^{f}\|}\bm{u}_{o,k}^{f}+\alpha\tilde{\bm{g}}^{\perp}_{k}\end{aligned}\right.

(17)

where
\tilde{\bm{g}}_{k}=\begin{bmatrix}\cos\tilde{\theta}_{k}&\sin\tilde{\theta}_{k}\end{bmatrix}^{T},\tilde{\bm{g}}_{k}^{\perp}=\begin{bmatrix}\sin\tilde{\theta}_{k}&-\cos\tilde{\theta}_{k}\end{bmatrix}^{T}
.
It is evident that
\tilde{\bm{g}}_{k}^{\perp}
is perpendicular to
\tilde{\bm{g}}_{k}
. Here,
\rho
represents the desired target-observer distance, and
U^{f}
is the upper bound of the control term
\bm{u}_{o,k}^{f}
. The parameter
\alpha>0
controls the mobile observer’s circular maneuverability. The magnitude of
\alpha
influences the strength of the estimator’s observability; increasing
\alpha
enhances observability. However, the maximum control input constraint limits the value of
\alpha
, such that
U=U^{f}+\alpha
.

VIII
CONCLUSIONS

In this paper, we introduced a Recursive Total Least Squares (RTLS) algorithm for bearing-only Target Motion Analysis (TMA) with a mobile observer, effectively addressing both bearing measurement errors and mobile observer positioning errors. The proposed method significantly reduces position estimation bias compared to traditional Pseudo-Linear Kalman Filter (PLKF) methods. Additionally, a circumnavigation controller was designed to enhance system observability and ensure estimator convergence. Extensive simulations and experiments demonstrated the superior accuracy and stability of the RTLS approach, showcasing its effectiveness across various scenarios with different noise conditions. The results underscore the potential of the RTLS algorithm for real-time target localization and tracking applications, offering improved performance over existing techniques.In future work, we aim to integrate real sensor observations into our method to enable implementation in more diverse and practical scenarios, while also considering adaptive models to accommodate more dynamic target behaviors.

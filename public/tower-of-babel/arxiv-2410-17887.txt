Average-case matrix discrepancy: satisfiability bounds
Antoine Maillard

Abstract
Given a sequence of d × d d\times d symmetric matrices { 𝐖 i } i = 1 n \{\mathbf{W}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n} , and a margin Δ > 0 \Delta>0 , we investigate whether it is possible to find signs ( ε 1 , … , ε n ) ∈ { ± 1 } n (\varepsilon_{1},\dots,\varepsilon_{n})\in\{\pm 1\}^{n} such that the operator norm of the signed sum satisfies ‖ ∑ i = 1 n ε i ​ 𝐖 i ‖ op ≤ Δ \|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\varepsilon_{i}\mathbf{W}_{i}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\Delta . Kunisky & Zhang, 2023 recently introduced a random version of this problem, where the matrices { 𝐖 i } i = 1 n \{\mathbf{W}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n} are drawn from the Gaussian orthogonal ensemble. This model can be seen as a random variant of the celebrated Matrix Spencer conjecture, and as a matrix-valued analog of the symmetric binary perceptron in statistical physics. In this work, we establish a satisfiability transition in this problem as n , d → ∞ n,d\to\infty with n / d 2 → τ > 0 n/d^{2}\to\tau>0 . Our main results are twofold. First, we prove that the expected number of solutions with margin Δ = κ ​ n \Delta=\kappa\sqrt{n} has a sharp threshold at a critical τ 1 ​ ( κ ) \tau_{1}(\kappa) : for τ < τ 1 ​ ( κ ) \tau<\tau_{1}(\kappa) the problem is typically unsatisfiable, while for τ > τ 1 ​ ( κ ) \tau>\tau_{1}(\kappa) the average number of solutions becomes exponentially large. Second, combining a second-moment method with recent results from Altschuler, 2023 on margin concentration in perceptron-type problems, we identify a second threshold τ 2 ​ ( κ ) \tau_{2}(\kappa) , such that for τ > τ 2 ​ ( κ ) \tau>\tau_{2}(\kappa) the problem admits solutions with high probability. In particular, we establish that a system of n = Θ ⁡ ( d 2 ) n=\Theta(d^{2}) Gaussian random matrices can be balanced so that the spectrum of the resulting matrix macroscopically shrinks compared to the typical semicircle law. Finally, under a technical assumption, we show that there exists values of ( τ , κ ) (\tau,\kappa) for which the number of solutions has large variance, implying the failure of the second moment method and uncovering a richer picture than in the vector-analog symmetric binary perceptron problem. Our proofs rely on concentration inequalities and large deviation properties for the law of correlated Gaussian matrices under spectral norm constraints.



1 Introduction and main results

1.1
Setting and related literature

We start by introducing in Sections
1.1.1
and
1.1.2
two sets of independent definitions and models,
both being important motivations behind the problem we study, which is discussed in Section
1.1.3
.

1.1.1
Discrepancy theory

Computing the
discrepancy
(i.e. the best possible balancing) of a collection of sets or vectors
is a classical question in mathematics and theoretical computer science.
It has wide-ranging applications in combinatorics, computational geometry,
experimental design, and the theory of approximation algorithms, to name a few.
The reader will find in
Spencer, 1994
;
Matousek, 2009
;
Chen et al., 2014
a detailed account of the history and applications of
discrepancy theory.
Arguably one of the most celebrated results in this area is the following theorem, known as Spencer’s “six deviations suffice”
(
Spencer, 1985
)
.

Theorem 1.1

(
Spencer, 1985
)
.

There exists
C>0
such that for all
n,d\geq 1
, and all
\mathbf{u}_{1},\cdots,\mathbf{u}_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
\|\mathbf{u}_{i}\|_{\infty}\leq 1
for all
i\in[n]
:

{\rm disc}(\mathbf{u}_{1},\cdots,\mathbf{u}_{n})\coloneqq\min_{\varepsilon\in\{\pm 1\}^{n}}\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\varepsilon_{i}\mathbf{u}_{i}\right\|_{\infty}\leq\begin{cases}C\sqrt{n\max\left(1,\log\frac{d}{n}\right)}&\textrm{ if }n\leq d,\\
C\sqrt{d}&\textrm{ if }n>d.\end{cases}

The second case
n>d
can be deduced from the result for
n=d
using classical arguments based on iterated rounding, see e.g.
Spencer, 1994
.
Interestingly, Spencer’s theorem shows that one can drastically improve over a naive pick of random signings
\varepsilon_{1},\cdots,\varepsilon_{n}\overset{\mathrm{i.i.d.}}{\sim}\mathrm{Unif}(\{\pm 1\})
. Indeed,
it is not hard to show that (taking
n=d
for simplicity) random signings achieve
\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\varepsilon_{i}\mathbf{u}_{i}\right\|_{\infty}=\Theta(\sqrt{n\log n})
for a worst-case choice of
(\mathbf{u}_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
,
with high probability as
n\to\infty
. Spencer’s theorem thus implies the existence of a signing
\varepsilon\in\{\pm 1\}^{n}
,
which depends on the value of the
\mathbf{u}_{i}
’s, and whose discrepancy generically improves over the one of random signs by a logarithmic factor.
While the original proof of
Spencer, 1985
is not constructive, there has been recently a great number of results regarding efficient algorithmic constructions of
these signings. We will discuss this point further in Section
1.3
.

Matrix discrepancy –

In the present work we consider the problem of
matrix discrepancy
. Given a set of
n
symmetric
d\times d
matrices
\mathbf{A}_{1},\cdots,\mathbf{A}_{n}
,
we aim to characterize the following discrepancy objective (
\|\mathbf{A}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\coloneqq\max_{\|\mathbf{x}\|_{2}=1}\|\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\|_{2}
is the spectral, or operator, norm):

{\rm disc}(\mathbf{A}_{1},\cdots,\mathbf{A}_{n})\coloneqq\min_{\varepsilon\in\{\pm 1\}^{n}}\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\varepsilon_{i}\mathbf{A}_{i}\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}.

(1)

As already mentioned, a foundational question in discrepancy is how the discrepancy objective compares to the one achieved by a
random
choice of the
signings
\varepsilon_{i}\overset{\mathrm{i.i.d.}}{\sim}\mathrm{Unif}(\{\pm 1\})
: matrix discrepancy is thus intimately connected to the study of large random matrices.
Matrix discrepancy has also
been shown to have implications in the theory of quantum random access codes
(
Hopkins et al., 2022
;
Bansal et al., 2023
)
,
in generalizations of the Kadison-Singer problem
(
Marcus et al., 2015
;
Kyng et al., 2020
)
, as well as
graph sparsification
(
Batson et al., 2014
)
to name a few.

The celebrated “Matrix Spencer” conjecture
(
Zouzias, 2012
;
Meka, 2014
)
is likely the most important open problem in matrix discrepancy.
It asserts that Spencer’s Theorem
1.1
can be generalized to the matrix setting, as follows.

Conjecture 1.2

(Matrix Spencer
(
Zouzias, 2012
;
Meka, 2014
)
)
.

There exists
C>0
such that for all
n,d\geq 1
, and all
\mathbf{A}_{1},\cdots,\mathbf{A}_{n}
symmetric
d\times d
matrices with
\max_{i\in[n]}\|\mathbf{A}_{i}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq 1
:

{\rm disc}(\mathbf{A}_{1},\cdots,\mathbf{A}_{n})\leq C\sqrt{n\max\left(1,\log\frac{d}{n}\right)}.

In particular, the discrepancy is
\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{n})
for
d=n
.

We stress that Spencer’s theorem can be seen as the special case of Conjecture
1.2
in which all
\mathbf{A}_{i}
commute with each other (and are thus diagonalizable in the same basis).
Moreover, a weaker form of Conjecture
1.2
, with a bound
\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{n\log d})
on the right-hand side, can easily be shown to be achievable using a random choice
of signs
\varepsilon_{i}\overset{\mathrm{i.i.d.}}{\sim}\mathrm{Unif}(\{\pm 1\})
, using the non-commutative Khintchine inequality of
Lust-Piquard & Pisier, 1991
.
Despite a recent surge in efforts
(
Levy et al., 2017
;
Hopkins et al., 2022
;
Dadush et al., 2022
)
, Conjecture
1.2
remains open at the time of this writing.
The best-known result is a proof of Matrix Spencer if we additionally assume

{\rm rk}(\mathbf{A}_{i})\lesssim n/\log^{3}n

(
Bansal et al., 2023
)
, and is based on the recent improvements over the non-commutative Khintchine inequality of
Bandeira et al., 2023
.
In this work, we consider an average-case version of Conjecture
1.2
, introduced in Section
1.1.3
.

1.1.2
Random vector discrepancy, and the symmetric binary perceptron

A natural question in vector discrepancy (i.e. in the setting of Theorem
1.1
) is to shift our attention to average-case settings, where the
\mathbf{u}_{i}
are
chosen randomly rather than potentially adversarially.
The typical discrepancy for random vectors is now well understood: in the regime
n=\omega(d)
(i.e.
d=o(n)
, many more signs than dimensions),

Turner et al., 2020
established the following result (with partial results preceding in
Karmarkar et al., 1986
;
Costello, 2009
):

Theorem 1.3

(
Turner et al., 2020
)
.

Assume that
\mathbf{u}_{1},\cdots,\mathbf{u}_{n}\overset{\mathrm{i.i.d.}}{\sim}\mathcal{N}(0,\mathrm{I}_{d})
and that
n=\omega(d)
.
Then

\displaystyle\plim_{d\to\infty}\frac{{\rm disc}(\mathbf{u}_{1},\cdots,\mathbf{u}_{n})}{\sqrt{\frac{\pi n}{2}}2^{-n/d}}=1,

where the limit is meant in probability.

While Spencer’s Theorem
1.1
does not apply to such random vectors (as one can easily show that
\|\mathbf{u}_{i}\|_{\infty}\sim\sqrt{2\log d}\gg 1
),
the randomness makes the problem amenable to a detailed mathematical analysis with different tools.

In a complementary way,
recent works have characterized
very precisely the minimal discrepancy in the proportional regime
n=\Theta(d)
, as a function of the “aspect ratio”
\beta\coloneqq\lim_{d\to\infty}n/d
.
This setting of random vector discrepancy is an instance of the
symmetric binary perceptron
(SBP), a random constraint satisfaction problem
which was introduced in
Aubin et al., 2019

as a variant to the classical asymmetric binary perceptron.
The latter is
a simple model of a neural network storing random patterns, which has a long history of study in
computer science, statistical physics, and probability theory
(
Cover, 1965
;
Gardner, 1988
;
Gardner & Derrida, 1988
;
Krauth & Mézard, 1989
;
Sompolinsky et al., 1990
;
Talagrand, 1999
;
Talagrand, 2010
)
.
For a
margin

K>0
, and in the limit
n/d\to\beta>0
, the question of satisfiability in the SBP was introduced in
Aubin et al., 2019

as:

Given
\mathbf{g}_{1},\cdots,\mathbf{g}_{d}\overset{\mathrm{i.i.d.}}{\sim}\mathcal{N}(0,\mathrm{I}_{n})
, can we find
\varepsilon\in\{\pm 1\}^{n}

such that
\max_{i\in[d]}|\langle\mathbf{g}_{i},\varepsilon\rangle|\leq K\sqrt{n}
?

Letting
(\mathbf{u}_{i})_{k}\coloneqq(\mathbf{g}_{j})_{k}
, so that
(\mathbf{u}_{i})_{i=1}^{n}\overset{\mathrm{i.i.d.}}{\sim}\mathcal{N}(0,\mathrm{I}_{d})
it is clear that the
the SBP can be thought as an average-case version of vector discrepancy,
and more precisely to the setting of Theorem
1.3
with
n=\Theta(d)
.
The SBP has received significant attention in the recent literature:
its relative simplicity allows studying in detail the relation between its structural properties and the performance of
solving algorithms,
which can then guide the theory of more complex statistical models,
see e.g.
Aubin et al., 2019
;
Perkins & Xu, 2021
;
Abbe et al., 2022
;
Gamarnik et al., 2022
;
Kizildag & Wakhare, 2023
;
Barbier et al., 2024
;
El & Gamarnik, 2024
;
Barbier, 2024
.
Concretely, it is shown in
Aubin et al., 2019
;
Abbe et al., 2022
;
Gamarnik et al., 2022
(among other results) that
the SBP undergoes the following sharp satisfiability/unsatisfiability transition.

Theorem 1.4

(Sharp threshold for the SBP
(
Aubin et al., 2019
;
Abbe et al., 2022
)
)
.

Let
K>0
.
Define

\beta_{1}(K)\coloneqq-\frac{\log\mathbb{P}_{z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)}[|z|\leq K]}{\log 2}.

For
\mathbf{u}_{1},\cdots,\mathbf{u}_{n}\overset{\mathrm{i.i.d.}}{\sim}\mathcal{N}(0,\mathrm{I}_{d})
,
let

Z_{K}\coloneqq\left|\left\{\varepsilon\in\{\pm 1\}^{n}\,:\,\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\varepsilon_{i}\mathbf{u}_{i}\right\|_{\infty}\leq K\sqrt{n}\right\}\right|.

Then in the limit
n,d\to\infty
with
n/d\to\beta>0
:

(i)

If
\beta<\beta_{1}(K)
,
\lim_{d\to\infty}\mathbb{P}[Z_{K}\geq 1]=0
.

(ii)

If
\beta>\beta_{1}(K)
,
\lim_{d\to\infty}\mathbb{P}[Z_{K}\geq 1]=1
.

The analysis of
Aubin et al., 2019
is based on the second moment method, and has been refined in subsequent works
(
Abbe et al., 2022
;
Gamarnik et al., 2022
)
. In particular,

\beta_{1}(K)
can be derived as the critical value of
\beta
where the value of
\mathbb{E}[Z_{K}]

transitions from being exponentially small (in
n,d
) to exponentially large.

1.1.3
Average-case matrix discrepancy

In this work,
alongside
Kunisky & Zhang, 2023
,
we initiate the study of the average-case matrix discrepancy problem.
Concretely, given
n,d\geq 1
a
margin

\kappa>0
, and
\mathbf{W}_{1},\cdots,\mathbf{W}_{n}
random independent matrices, drawn with centered Gaussian i.i.d. elements (up to symmetry) with variance
1/d
, we seek to answer the question:

(P)
:
Can we find signs
(\varepsilon_{1},\cdots,\varepsilon_{n})\in\{\pm 1\}^{n}
such that
\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\varepsilon_{i}\mathbf{W}_{i}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\kappa\sqrt{n}
?

This problem can be seen as an average-case analog of Conjecture
1.2
, with the simple Gaussian random matrix model serving as a natural starting point.
By investigating this simplified case in great detail, we firstly aim to gain insight,
in order to possibly probe Conjecture
1.2
in more structured random matrix models in the future.
This would form an alternative direction to other recent works
(
Hopkins et al., 2022
;
Dadush et al., 2022
;
Bansal et al., 2023
)
towards a better understanding of the Matrix Spencer conjecture.

Additionally,
(P)
can naturally be thought of as the matrix discrepancy analog to the random vector discrepancy problem (or the symmetric binary perceptron)
described above:
a mapping can be achieved by modifying our model to
diagonal matrices
\mathbf{W}_{i}
with i.i.d. elements drawn from
\mathcal{N}(0,1)
on the diagonal,
and setting
(\mathbf{g}_{i})_{k}=(\mathbf{W}_{i})_{kk}
.

Importantly, we will focus on studying
(P)
in the case of a
finite margin

\kappa>0
as
n,d\to\infty
.
In random vector discrepancy, the satisfiability transition for a finite margin occurs in the scale
n=\Theta(d)
, – i.e. in the symmetric binary perceptron setting – see Theorem
1.4
.
As we will see, in our model the critical scaling is rather
n=\Theta(d^{2})
, and we will focus on this
regime throughout our work.
Our approach to tackle
(P)
further builds on the methods introduced by
Aubin et al., 2019
for the SBP model.
Ultimately, the main goal we pursue is to obtain a detailed understanding of the satisfiability properties of
(P)
,
and to reach a counterpart to Theorem
1.4
in the context of average-case matrix discrepancy.

Parallel work –

Shortly after a first pre-print of the present manuscript was made available online, an independent study
(
Wengiel, 2024
)
appeared, exploring as well the asymptotic discrepancy of Gaussian i.i.d. matrices.
Their findings extend the approach of
Turner et al., 2020
for random vector discrepancy to this context,
by proving a counterpart to Theorem
1.3
for random matrix discrepancy.
Their results provide a sharp characterization in the regime
n=\omega(d^{2})
(i.e.
d=o(\sqrt{n})
), and
\kappa=o(1)
.
Technically,
Wengiel, 2024
employs the second moment method, similar to our work,
but relies on a general upper bound for the density of the joint law of the spectra of two correlated Gaussian matrices.
In contrast, the present work focuses on the critical regime
n=\Theta(d^{2})
, where the asymptotic margin
\kappa>0
is finite.
In this critical regime, the bound used in
Wengiel, 2024
becomes vacuous, and we employ here different ideas to control this joint law.
Combining the results of
Wengiel, 2024
with ours,
we achieve a nearly complete description of the asymptotic discrepancy of Gaussian i.i.d. matrices,
leaving open only a fraction of the phase diagram in the critical regime
n=\Theta(d^{2})
, see Fig.
1
.

1.2
Main results

Notations and background

We denote
[d]\coloneqq\{1,\cdots,d\}
the set of integers from
1
to
d
,
and
\mathcal{S}_{d}
the set of
d\times d
real symmetric matrices.
For a function
V:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
, and
\mathbf{S}\in\mathcal{S}_{d}
with eigenvalues
(\lambda_{i})_{i=1}^{d}
, we define
V(\mathbf{S})
as the matrix with the same eigenvectors as
\mathbf{S}
,
and eigenvalues
(V(\lambda_{i}))_{i=1}^{d}
.
We denote by
\|\mathbf{S}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\coloneqq\max_{i\in[d]}|\lambda_{i}|
the operator norm.
For any
B\subseteq\mathbb{R}
we denote by
\mathcal{M}_{1}^{+}(B)
the set of real probability distributions on
B
.
For a probability measure
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{1}^{+}(\mathbb{R})
we denote
\Sigma(\mu)\coloneqq\int\mu(\mathrm{d}x)\mu(\mathrm{d}y)\log|x-y|
its non-commutative entropy.
We say that a random matrix
\mathbf{Y}\in\mathcal{S}_{d}
is generated from the
Gaussian Orthogonal Ensemble

\mathrm{GOE}(d)
if

Y_{ij}\overset{\mathrm{i.i.d.}}{\sim}\mathcal{N}(0,(1+\delta_{ij})/d)\textrm{ for }i\leq j.

The following celebrated result dates back to
Wigner, 1955
, and is one of the foundational results of random matrix theory.
For a modern proof and further results, see
Anderson et al., 2010
.

Theorem 1.5

(Semicircle law)
.

Let
\mathbf{Y}\sim\mathrm{GOE}(d)
, with eigenvalues
(\lambda_{i})_{i=1}^{d}
, and denote
\mu_{\mathbf{Y}}\coloneqq(1/d)\sum_{i=1}^{d}\delta_{\lambda_{i}}
its empirical eigenvalue distribution.
Then, almost surely,

\begin{cases}\mu_{\mathbf{Y}}&\xrightarrow[d\to\infty]{\textrm{weakly}}\rho_{\mathrm{s.c.}}(\mathrm{d}x)=\frac{\sqrt{4-x^{2}}}{2\pi}\mathds{1}\{|x|\leq 2\},\\
\|\mathbf{Y}\|_{\mathrm{op}}=\max_{i\in[d]}|\lambda_{i}|&\xrightarrow[d\to\infty]{}2.\end{cases}

\rho_{\mathrm{s.c.}}
in Theorem
1.5
is often called the
semicircle law
.
Finally, we generically denote constants as
C>0
(or
C_{1}>0,C_{2}>0,\cdots
), whose value may vary from line to line.
We will specify their possible dependency on parameters of the problem when relevant.

1.2.1
Setting of the problem

Let
n,d\geq 1
, and
\mathbf{W}_{1},\cdots,\mathbf{W}_{n}\overset{\mathrm{i.i.d.}}{\sim}\mathrm{GOE}(d)
.
For any
\kappa>0
, we define:

Z_{\kappa}\coloneqq\#\left\{\varepsilon\in\{\pm 1\}^{n}\,\textrm{s.t.}\,\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\varepsilon_{i}\mathbf{W}_{i}\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\kappa\sqrt{n}\right\}.

(2)

We refer to
n^{-1/2}\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\varepsilon_{i}\mathbf{W}_{i}\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}
as the
margin
of
\varepsilon\in\{\pm 1\}^{n}
.

Z_{\kappa}
thus counts the number of signings
\varepsilon\in\{\pm 1\}^{n}
with margin at most
\kappa
.

The case
\kappa>2
–

Clearly
(1/\sqrt{n})\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{W}_{i}\sim\mathrm{GOE}(d)
, so that (with
\varepsilon_{i}=1
for all
i\in[n]
), for any
\kappa>2
,

\mathbb{P}\left[Z_{\kappa}\geq 1\right]\geq\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{W}\sim\mathrm{GOE}(d)}[\|\mathbf{W}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\kappa]\overset{\mathrm{(a)}}{=}1-o_{d}(1),

(3)

using Theorem
1.5
in
(\rm a)
.
Notice that this bound is also the one given by a random choice of
\varepsilon_{i}~\overset{\mathrm{i.i.d.}}{\sim}~\mathrm{Unif}(\{\pm 1\})
(independently of
\{\mathbf{W}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
), as again in this case

n^{-1/2}\sum_{i}\varepsilon_{i}\mathbf{W}_{i}\sim\mathrm{GOE}(d)
.
In what follows, we therefore focus on the (interesting) regime
\kappa\in(0,2]
, in which a choice of signings
dependent on the
\mathbf{W}_{i}
must be made in order to
get a solution with margin at most
\kappa
.
Notice that this argument implies that Conjecture
1.2
holds straightforwardly for i.i.d.
\mathrm{GOE}(d)
matrices.
For this reason, our interest in this problem stems primarily from its interpretation as a constraint satisfaction problem (e.g. as a matrix-analog of the SBP) and its connection to questions in random matrix theory, particularly in the critical regime
n=\Theta(d^{2})
.
Nevertheless, we also view our understanding of the discrepancy of i.i.d.
\mathrm{GOE}(d)
matrices as a foundational step toward exploring more structured random matrix models, for which Conjecture
1.2
is significantly more complex and presents an exciting avenue for future research.

1.2.2
Asymptotics of the first moment

Define, for
\kappa\in(0,2]
:

\tau_{1}(\kappa)\coloneqq\frac{1}{\log 2}\left[-\frac{\kappa^{4}}{128}+\frac{\kappa^{2}}{8}-\frac{1}{2}\log\frac{\kappa}{2}-\frac{3}{8}\right].

(4)

Notice that
\tau_{1}(2)=0
, and
\tau_{1}(\kappa)\to+\infty
as
\kappa\downarrow 0
.
Our first main result is the following sharp asymptotics for the expectation of
Z_{\kappa}
.

Theorem 1.6

(Asymptotics of the first moment)
.

Let
\kappa\in(0,2]
, and
\mathbf{W}_{1},\cdots,\mathbf{W}_{n}\overset{\mathrm{i.i.d.}}{\sim}\mathrm{GOE}(d)
.
Assume
n/d^{2}\to\tau\in[0,\infty)
as
n,d\to\infty
.
Then

\lim_{d\to\infty}\frac{1}{d^{2}}\log\mathbb{E}Z_{\kappa}=(\tau-\tau_{1}(\kappa))\log 2,

(5)

where
Z_{\kappa}
is defined in eq. (
2
).
In particular, if
\tau<\tau_{1}(\kappa)
, then

\lim_{d\to\infty}\mathbb{P}[Z_{\kappa}=0]=\lim_{d\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left[\min_{\varepsilon\in\{\pm 1\}^{n}}\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\varepsilon_{i}\mathbf{W}_{i}\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}>\kappa\sqrt{n}\right]=1.

In Section
2
we carry out the proof of Theorem
1.6
.
It relies on Proposition
2.1
, which establishes the large deviations of the operator norm of a

\mathrm{GOE}(d)
matrix, in the scale
d^{2}
. This is a consequence of now-classical results on the large deviations of the empirical measure
in so-called
\beta
-matrix models
(
Ben & Guionnet, 1997
;
Anderson et al., 2010
)
, which yield the large deviation rate function in a variational form.
Further, using results of logarithmic potential theory
(
Saff & Totik, 2013
)
and the Tricomi theorem
(
Tricomi, 1985
)
, we are able to then solve this variational principle,
and its outcome gives eq. (
4
).
As a byproduct, we obtain the limiting spectral measure of a matrix
\mathbf{W}~\sim~\mathrm{GOE}(d)
constrained on the event
\|\mathbf{W}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\kappa
, see Theorem
2.2
.

Relation to
Kunisky & Zhang, 2023
–

Theorem
1.6
is a refinement of Theorem 1.13 of
Kunisky & Zhang, 2023
,
which shows that
Z_{\kappa}=0
with high probability if
\kappa\leq\delta\cdot 4^{-\tau}
, for an (unspecified) absolute constant
\delta>0
.
For instance, the bound of
Kunisky & Zhang, 2023
correctly predicts
\tau_{1}(\kappa)\sim-\log\kappa/\log 4
for
\kappa\to 0
, but fails to capture that
\tau_{1}(\kappa)\to 0
continuously as
\kappa\to 2
.

1.2.3
The satisfiability region

We start by introducing a function
\tau_{2}(\kappa)
, which will serve as a threshold for the validity of our satisfiability analysis.
Let
H(p)\coloneqq-p\log p-(1-p)\log(1-p)
denote the “binary entropy” function.

Proposition 1.7
.

For any
\eta>0
, let
\delta_{\eta}\in(0,1)
to be the unique solution to:

H\left(\frac{1+\delta}{2}\right)=\frac{\eta}{1+\eta}\log 2.

(6)

For any
\eta>0
, let

\displaystyle{\widetilde{\tau}}(\eta,\kappa)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\max\left\{(1+\eta)\tau_{1}(\kappa),\frac{1+\delta_{\eta}^{2}}{2(1-\delta_{\eta}^{2})^{2}}+\left[\frac{\delta_{\eta}(1+6\delta_{\eta}+3\delta_{\eta}^{2}+2\delta_{\eta}^{3})}{(1-\delta_{\eta}^{2})^{3}(1-\delta_{\eta})}\right]\kappa\right.

1.2.4
Failure of the second moment method

Finally, under a technical assumption, we show that in average-case matrix discrepancy and
for some values of the parameters
(\tau,\kappa)
,
the number of solutions

can be both large in expectation and have large variance, indicating the failure of the

Theorem 1.9

(Failure of the second moment method in part of the phase diagram)

Assume that Hypothesis

The technical Hypothesis

In Section

In Section

Tightness of the second moment analysis –

Similarly to
Aubin et al., 2019
(and subsequent works) for the symmetric binary perceptron (SBP), we use a second moment approach to characterize the feasibility of this problem.
However, while this method gives a tight threshold for the SBP in Theorem
1.4

the situation in average-case matrix discrepancy is quite different.

First, the threshold
\tau_{2}(\kappa)
of eq. (

as the upper bounds on
\mathbb{E}[Z_{\kappa}^{2}]

For this reason, the second moment ratio

\tau\leq\tau_{2}(\kappa)

For instance, one might conjecture that this includes values of
\tau

0

Improving the estimates we obtain in Section

with bounded second moment ratio is significantly more complex than in the SBP setting:
it requires a precise understanding of the large deviation properties of the law of
(\|\mathbf{W}_{1}\|_{\mathrm{op}},\|\mathbf{W}_{2}\|_{\mathrm{op}})

\mathbf{W}_{1},\mathbf{W}_{2}
are two correlated
\mathrm{GOE}(d)

34

A rate function for this law can be obtained in a variational form (see e.g. Theorem 3.3 of
Guionnet, 2004

is a very challenging problem, which we leave as a future direction.

Structure of the solution space –

Beyond satisfiability, a natural question
concerns
the geometric structure
of the space of solutions
\{\varepsilon\in\{\pm 1\}^{n}\,:\,\|\sum_{i}\varepsilon_{i}\mathbf{W}_{i}\|_{\mathrm{op}}~\leq~\kappa\sqrt{n}\}

In the symmetric binary perceptron, the geometric structure of the solution space was investigated by
Aubin et al., 2019

Perkins & Xu, 2021
;
Abbe et al., 2022

and was shown to exhibit a “frozen-1RSB” structure: that is, typically, solutions are isolated and far apart.

Open Question 1.3

However in the present context two

(i)
this argument usually relies on establishing a contiguity property with a “planted” version of the model,

even withing the SAT region of Fig.

We discuss points

in further details in Appendix

Going beyond, the failure of the second moment method (discussed above)

Algorithmic discrepancy –

The past decade has seen a surge of interest in
algorithmic discrepancy
, that is the design of efficient algorithms to produce signings

\varepsilon\in\{\pm 1\}^{n}
minimizing a discrepancy objective such as eq. (
1
).
In the classical context of vector discrepancy, this line of work was initiated by
Bansal, 2010
, and we refer to
Bansal et al., 2023
;
Kunisky & Zhang, 2023
for a more detailed description of the
literature that followed.
For the problem of average-case matrix discrepancy,
Kunisky & Zhang, 2023
analyze an online algorithm, introduced originally by
Zouzias, 2012
, and show that is able to achieve a discrepancy

\|\sum_{i}\varepsilon_{i}\mathbf{W}_{i}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\lesssim d\log(n+d)
for a large class of random matrices
\mathbf{W}_{i}
, including the
\mathrm{GOE}
distribution.
In the regime
n=\Theta(d^{2})
, this bound unfortunately falls short of obtaining a discrepancy lower than
2\sqrt{n}
.

Open Question 1.4

(Efficient algorithms)
.

Is there a polynomial-time algorithm which, in the regime
n=\Theta(d^{2})
and with high probability, outputs
\varepsilon\in\{\pm 1\}^{n}
such that

\|\sum_{i}\varepsilon_{i}\mathbf{W}_{i}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\kappa\sqrt{n}

for some
\kappa<2
?

For the symmetric binary perceptron, which is the vector analog to our problem, it was recently shown that the phase diagram presents a large computational-to-statistical gap
in which low-discrepancy solutions exist, while large classes of polynomial-time algorithms can not find them, and that this was related to the geometric structure of the solution space
(
Bansal & Spencer, 2020
;
Gamarnik et al., 2022
)
.

2.1
Large deviations results

Our proof leverages a large deviation principle for the empirical eigenvalue distribution in a large class of matrix models.
Such results were initiated in the mathematics literature by
Ben & Guionnet, 1997
in the case of
\mathrm{GOE}(d)
matrices, and have been then extended, see e.g.
Guionnet, 2022
for a discussion.

The space
\mathcal{M}_{1}^{+}(\mathbb{R})
of probability measures on
\mathbb{R}
is endowed with its usual weak topology.
It is metrizable by the Dudley metric
(
Bogachev & Ruas, 2007
)
:

d(\mu,\nu)\coloneqq\left\{\left|\int f\mathrm{d}\mu-\int f\mathrm{d}v\right|\,:\,|f(x)|\leq 1\textrm{ and }|f(x)-f(y)|\leq|x-y|,\,\forall(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\right\}.

(14)

Recall that
\Sigma(\mu)\coloneqq\int\mu(\mathrm{d}x)\mu(\mathrm{d}y)\log|x-y|
.
The following statement is borrowed from
Fan et al., 2015
(variants can be found e.g. in
Ben & Guionnet, 1997
;
Anderson et al., 2010
).

Proposition 2.3

(
Fan et al., 2015
)
.

Let
B\subseteq\mathbb{R}
an interval, and
V:B\to\mathbb{R}
a continuous function such that
\lim_{x\to\pm\infty}\frac{V(x)}{2\log|x|}=+\infty
(if
B
is not bounded).
For
{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, let

\displaystyle\mu_{d}^{V,B}(\mathrm{d}{\boldsymbol{\lambda}})\coloneqq\frac{1}{Z_{d}^{V,B}}\prod_{i=1}^{d}\left(\mathrm{d}\lambda_{i}\,\mathds{1}\{\lambda_{i}\in B\}\right)\,e^{-\frac{d}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{d}V(\lambda_{i})}\,\prod_{i<j}|\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}|,

(15)

with
Z_{d}^{V,B}
a normalization factor, which ensures that
\int\mu_{d}^{V,B}(\mathrm{d}{\boldsymbol{\lambda}})=1
.

Denote
\nu_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\coloneqq(1/d)\sum_{i=1}^{d}\delta_{\lambda_{i}}
the empirical measure of
{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
.
Then the law of
\nu_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
satisfies a large deviation principle, in the scale
d^{2}
, with good rate function
I_{V,B}(\nu)\coloneqq\mathcal{E}_{V}(\nu)-\inf_{\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{1}^{+}(B)}[\mathcal{E}_{V}(\nu)]
, where

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{V}(\nu)\coloneqq-\frac{1}{2}\Sigma(\nu)+\frac{1}{2}\int V(x)\,\nu(\mathrm{d}x).

(16)

In particular:

(i)

For any
M>0
,
\{I_{V,B}\leq M\}
is a compact subset of
\mathcal{M}_{1}^{+}(B)
.

(ii)

For any open (respectively closed) subset
O\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{1}^{+}(B)
(respectively
F\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{1}^{+}(B)
):

\begin{cases}\liminf_{d\to\infty}\frac{1}{d^{2}}\log\mu_{d}^{V,B}[\nu_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\in O]\geq-\inf_{\nu\in O}I_{V,B}(\nu),\\
\limsup_{d\to\infty}\frac{1}{d^{2}}\log\mu_{d}^{V,B}[\nu_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\in F]\leq-\inf_{\nu\in F}I_{V,B}(\nu).\end{cases}

(iii)

\lim_{d\to\infty}\frac{1}{d^{2}}\log Z_{d}^{V,B}=-\inf_{\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{1}^{+}(B)}[\mathcal{E}_{V}(\nu)].

Proposition
2.3
directly applies to
\mathbb{P}_{\kappa}
, the law of
\mathbf{W}\sim\mathrm{GOE}(d)
conditioned on
\|\mathbf{W}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\kappa
,
with
V(x)=x^{2}/2
. We obtain from it the following variational formulation for the limit of the left-hand side of eq. (
12
).

Corollary 2.4
.

For any
\kappa>0
, with
\mathbf{W}\sim\mathrm{GOE}(d)
:

\lim_{d\to\infty}\frac{1}{d^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}[\|\mathbf{W}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\kappa]=\frac{3}{8}-\inf_{\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{1}^{+}([-\kappa,\kappa])}\left[-\frac{1}{2}\Sigma(\nu)+\frac{1}{4}\int\nu(\mathrm{d}x)\,x^{2}\right].

Proof of Corollary
2.4
.

The joint law of the eigenvalues
(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{d})
of
\mathbf{W}
is well-known thanks to the rotation invariance of the law of
\mathbf{W}
.
We have (see e.g. Theorem 2.5.2 of
Anderson et al., 2010
):

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[\|\mathbf{W}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\kappa]

\displaystyle=\frac{\int_{[-\kappa,\kappa]^{d}}\prod_{i<j}|\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}|e^{-\frac{d}{4}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\lambda_{i}^{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{d}\mathrm{d}\lambda_{i}}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\prod_{i<j}|\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}|e^{-\frac{d}{4}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\lambda_{i}^{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{d}\mathrm{d}\lambda_{i}}.

(17)

The denominator (or partition function) can be computed from Selberg’s integrals
(
Mehta, 2014
)
.
Its limit is given by

\displaystyle\lim_{d\to\infty}\frac{1}{d^{2}}\log\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\prod_{i<j}|\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}|e^{-\frac{d}{4}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\lambda_{i}^{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{d}\mathrm{d}\lambda_{i}

\displaystyle=-\frac{3}{8}.

(18)

The result follows then from Proposition
2.3
-
(iii)
.
∎

Alternative proof –

For reasons of completeness, and as a first version of this manuscript contained it, we detail in Appendix
B
a proof of Corollary
2.4

that does not rely on Proposition
2.3
, but only on its particular case
B=\mathbb{R}
and
V(x)=x^{2}/2
, i.e. when
\mu_{d}^{V,B}
is the joint law of the eigenvalues of a
\mathrm{GOE}(d)
matrix.
This setting was tackled in the seminal work of
Ben & Guionnet, 1997
.

2.2
Solving the variational principle: proof of Proposition
2.1

For
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{1}^{+}(\mathbb{R})
, let

I(\mu)\coloneqq-\frac{1}{2}\Sigma(\mu)+\frac{1}{4}\int\mu(\mathrm{d}x)\,x^{2}-\frac{3}{8}.

(19)

We establish here the following equation, for
\kappa\leq 2
:

\displaystyle E_{\kappa}\coloneqq\inf_{\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{1}^{+}([-\kappa,\kappa])}I(\mu)=-\frac{\kappa^{4}}{128}+\frac{\kappa^{2}}{8}-\frac{1}{2}\log\frac{\kappa}{2}-\frac{3}{8}.

(20)

Combining eq. (
20
) with Corollary
2.4
yields then Proposition
2.1
.

In order to characterize the minimizer of
I(\mu)
for
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{1}^{+}([-\kappa,\kappa])
, we
rely on classical results of logarithmic potential theory, such as Theorem 1.3 of Chapter I of
Saff & Totik, 2013
, see also
Mhaskar & Saff, 1985
and Theorem 2.4 of
Ben & Guionnet, 1997
.
In our context, the “admissible weight function” of
Saff & Totik, 2013
reads

w(x)=\exp\{-x^{2}/4\}\mathds{1}\{|x|\leq\kappa\}.

Adapting the results of the aforementioned literature to our notations, we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.5

(
Saff & Totik, 2013
)
.

Let
\kappa>0
and

E_{\kappa}\coloneqq\inf_{\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{1}^{+}([-\kappa,\kappa])}I(\mu)
. Then

(i)

E_{\kappa}<\infty
.

(ii)

There exists a unique
\mu_{\kappa}^{\star}\in\mathcal{M}_{1}^{+}([-\kappa,\kappa])
such that
I(\mu_{\kappa}^{\star})=E_{\kappa}
.

(iii)

\mu_{\kappa}^{\star}
is the unique measure in
\mathcal{M}_{1}^{+}([-\kappa,\kappa])
such that for
\mu_{\kappa}^{\star}
-almost all
x
:

\int\mu_{\kappa}^{\star}(\mathrm{d}y)\,\log|x-y|=\frac{x^{2}}{4}+\frac{1}{4}\int\mu_{\kappa}^{\star}(\mathrm{d}y)\,y^{2}-\frac{3}{4}-2E_{\kappa}.

One can further show that this theorem allows to prove that a candidate measure is the optimal one without computing
E_{\kappa}
.

Lemma 2.6
.

For
\kappa\in(0,2]
, assume that
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{1}^{+}([-\kappa,\kappa])
and
C\in\mathbb{R}
are such that for all
x\in(-\kappa,\kappa)
:

\int\mu(\mathrm{d}y)\,\log|x-y|=\frac{x^{2}}{4}+C.

(21)

Then
\mu=\mu_{\kappa}^{\star}
.

Note that Lemma
2.6
can also be seen as a consequence of Theorem 3.3 of Chapter I of
Saff & Totik, 2013
.
We give here a short proof for the sake of completeness.

Proof of Lemma
2.6
–.

For any
\sigma,\nu
real signed measures, we have (recall eq. (
19
))

I(\sigma+\nu)-I(\sigma)=-\frac{1}{2}\Sigma(\nu)+\int\nu(\mathrm{d}x)\left[\frac{x^{2}}{4}-\int\sigma(\mathrm{d}y)\log|x-y|\right].

Applying this formula to
\sigma=\mu
and
\nu=\mu_{\kappa}^{\star}-\mu
, we reach:

\displaystyle I(\mu_{\kappa}^{\star})-I(\mu)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\Sigma(\mu_{\kappa}^{\star}-\mu)+\int(\mu_{\kappa}^{\star}-\mu)(\mathrm{d}x)\left[\frac{x^{2}}{4}-\int\mu(\mathrm{d}y)\log|x-y|\right],

\displaystyle\overset{\mathrm{(a)}}{=}-\frac{1}{2}\Sigma(\mu_{\kappa}^{\star}-\mu),

\displaystyle\overset{\mathrm{(b)}}{\geq}0.

We used eq. (
21
) in
(\rm a)
and the fact that
\mu_{\kappa}^{\star}
has no atom since
I(\mu_{\kappa}^{\star})<\infty

(
Ben & Guionnet, 1997
)
, so we can restrict the integral
to
x\in(-\kappa,\kappa)
.
In
(\rm b)
we used the following classical property of the non-commutative entropy
\Sigma(\mu)
,
which can be found e.g. as Proposition II.2.2 in
Faraut, 2014
.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
\mu
be a signed measure on
\mathbb{R}
with compact support, and such that
\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mu(\mathrm{d}x)=0
.
Let
\hat{\mu}(t)\coloneqq\int\mu(\mathrm{d}x)\,e^{itx}
be the characteristic function of
\mu
.
Then

\Sigma(\mu)\coloneqq\int\mu(\mathrm{d}x)\mu(\mathrm{d}y)\log|x-y|=-\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{|\hat{\mu}(t)|^{2}}{t}\mathrm{d}t.

In particular,
\Sigma(\mu)\leq 0
.

This shows that
I(\mu)\leq I(\mu_{\kappa}^{\star})=\inf_{\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{1}^{+}([-\kappa,\kappa])}I(\mu)
. By
(ii)
of Theorem
2.5
,
\mu=\mu_{\kappa}^{\star}
.
∎

Thanks to Lemma
2.6
, we can give an exact formula for
\mu_{\kappa}^{\star}
by exhibiting a candidate measure satisfying eq. (
21
), which in turns implies the exact formula (
20
) for
E_{\kappa}
.

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
\kappa\in(0,2]
.
Recall that
E_{\kappa}=\inf_{\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{1}^{+}([-\kappa,\kappa])}I(\mu)
is reached in a unique measure
\mu_{\kappa}^{\star}
.
Let
\mu_{\kappa}(\mathrm{d}x)\coloneqq\rho_{\kappa}(x)\mathrm{d}x
be given, for
x\in(-\kappa,\kappa)
, by:

\rho_{\kappa}(x)\coloneqq\frac{4+\kappa^{2}-2x^{2}}{4\pi\sqrt{\kappa^{2}-x^{2}}}.

(22)

Then
\mu_{\kappa}=\mu_{\kappa}^{\star}
, and we have eq. (
20
):

E_{\kappa}=I(\mu_{\kappa}^{\star})=-\frac{\kappa^{4}}{128}+\frac{\kappa^{2}}{8}-\frac{1}{2}\log\frac{\kappa}{2}-\frac{3}{8}.

Proof of Proposition
2.8
.

By Lemma
2.6
, to show that
\mu_{\kappa}=\mu_{\kappa}^{\star}
it is enough to show that eq. (
21
)
holds for
\mu_{\kappa}
.
Since
U(x)\coloneqq\int\mu_{\kappa}(\mathrm{d}y)\log|x-y|
satisfies (in the sense of distributions)

U^{\prime}(x)=\textrm{P.V.}\int\frac{\rho_{\kappa}(y)}{x-y}\mathrm{d}y,

it is enough to check for any
x\in(-\kappa,\kappa)
:

\textrm{P.V.}\int_{-r}^{r}\frac{\rho_{\kappa}(y)}{x-y}\mathrm{d}y=\frac{x}{2},

(23)

where
P.V.
refers to the principal value.
Notice that

\textrm{P.V.}\int_{-r}^{r}\frac{\rho_{\kappa}(y)}{x-y}\mathrm{d}y=\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\mathrm{Re}\left\{\int_{-r}^{r}\frac{\rho_{\kappa}(y)}{x+i\varepsilon-y}\mathrm{d}y\right\}.

We compute
G_{\kappa}(z)\coloneqq\int_{-r}^{r}\rho_{\kappa}(y)/(z-y)\mathrm{d}y
for all
z
such that
\mathrm{Im}(z)>0
.
Changing variables to
y=\kappa\cos\theta
, and then to
\zeta=e^{i\theta}
, we have:

\displaystyle G_{\kappa}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}\frac{4+\kappa^{2}-2\kappa^{2}\cos^{2}\theta}{z-\kappa\cos\theta}\,\mathrm{d}\theta,

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{8\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{4+\kappa^{2}-2\kappa^{2}\cos^{2}\theta}{z-\kappa\cos\theta}\,\mathrm{d}\theta,

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{8i\pi}\oint_{|\zeta|=1}\frac{\kappa^{2}\zeta^{4}-8\zeta^{2}+\kappa^{2}}{\zeta^{2}(\kappa\zeta^{2}-2\zeta z+\kappa)}\mathrm{d}\zeta.

The denominator has three poles:
\{0,\zeta_{-},\zeta_{+}\}
, where
\zeta_{\pm}\coloneqq(z\pm\sqrt{z^{2}-\kappa^{2}})/\kappa
(we choose the branch of the square root such that
\mathrm{Im}[\sqrt{w}]\geq 0
for all
w\in\mathbb{C}
).
Since
\mathrm{Im}(z)>0
, it is easy to show that
|\zeta_{-}|<1<|\zeta_{+}|
.
We then apply the residue theorem and find:

G_{\kappa}(z)=\frac{z}{2}+\frac{4+\kappa^{2}-2z^{2}}{4\sqrt{z^{2}-\kappa^{2}}}.

(24)

Taking
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mathrm{Re}[G_{\kappa}(x+i\varepsilon)]
for
|x|<\kappa
yields eq. (
23
), and thus
\mu_{\kappa}=\mu_{\kappa}^{\star}
.
It remains to compute
E_{\kappa}=I(\mu_{\kappa}^{\star})
.
One can use the same arguments as above (based on the residue theorem) to show

\int\mu_{\kappa}^{\star}(\mathrm{d}x)\,x^{2}=\frac{\kappa^{2}(8-\kappa^{2})}{16}.

(25)

By
(iii)
of Theorem
2.5
and Lemma
2.6
,
we then have for all
x\in(-\kappa,\kappa)
:

E_{\kappa}=\frac{\kappa^{2}(8-\kappa^{2})}{128}-\frac{3}{8}+\frac{x^{2}}{8}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{-\kappa}^{\kappa}\rho_{\kappa}(y)\,\log|x-y|\,\mathrm{d}y.

(26)

Notice that eq. (
24
) is valid for all
z
with
\mathrm{Im}(z)>0
. In particular, we reach from it, that for all
x\geq 0
:

\textrm{P.V.}\int_{-r}^{r}\frac{\rho_{\kappa}(y)}{x-y}\mathrm{d}y=\begin{cases}\frac{x}{2}&\textrm{ if }x<\kappa,\\
\frac{x}{2}+\frac{4+\kappa^{2}-2x^{2}}{4\sqrt{x^{2}-\kappa^{2}}}&\textrm{ if }x>\kappa.\end{cases}

Since this is an integrable function, we have

\int_{-\kappa}^{\kappa}\rho_{\kappa}(y)\,\log|x-y|\,\mathrm{d}y=\begin{cases}\frac{x^{2}}{4}+C&\textrm{ if }x\leq\kappa,\\
\frac{x^{2}}{4}+C-\frac{x\sqrt{x^{2}-\kappa^{2}}}{4}-\log\left(\frac{x-\sqrt{x^{2}-\kappa^{2}}}{\kappa}\right)&\textrm{ if }x>\kappa.\end{cases}

Using that
\int_{-\kappa}^{\kappa}\rho_{\kappa}(y)\log|x-y|\mathrm{d}y-\log x\to 0
as
x\to\infty
yields
C=\log(\kappa/2)-\kappa^{2}/8
.
Eq. (
26
) becomes:

\displaystyle E_{\kappa}=I(\mu_{\kappa}^{\star})

\displaystyle=\frac{\kappa^{2}(8-\kappa^{2})}{128}-\frac{3}{8}-\frac{1}{2}\left[\log\frac{\kappa}{2}-\frac{\kappa^{2}}{8}\right],

\displaystyle=-\frac{\kappa^{4}}{128}+\frac{\kappa^{2}}{8}-\frac{1}{2}\log\frac{\kappa}{2}-\frac{3}{8},

which ends the proof.
∎

Remark: predicting the form of
\rho_{\kappa}
–
In order to predict the density
\rho_{\kappa}
given by eq. (
22
),
we used an argument based on a heuristic application of Tricomi’s theorem
(
Tricomi, 1985
)
, which states that if eq. (
23
) is satisfied
and
\rho_{\kappa}
is supported on
[-\kappa,\kappa]
, then

\rho_{\kappa}(x)=\frac{1}{\pi\sqrt{\kappa^{2}-x^{2}}}\left[C-\frac{1}{\pi}\textrm{P.V.}\int_{-\kappa}^{\kappa}\frac{\sqrt{\kappa^{2}-y^{2}}}{x-y}\times\left(\frac{y}{2}\right)\mathrm{d}y\right],

(27)

for some constant
C
chosen to ensure
\int_{-\kappa}^{\kappa}\rho_{\kappa}(x)\mathrm{d}x=1
.
A careful evaluation of eq. (
27
) based on the residue theorem yields eq. (
22
).

2.3
Limiting spectral distribution of a norm-constrained Gaussian matrix

Theorem
2.2
follows as a direct consequence of Proposition
2.3
and Proposition
2.8
.
Indeed, let
\mathbb{P}_{\kappa}
be the law of
\mathbf{W}\sim\mathrm{GOE}(d)
conditioned on
\|\mathbf{W}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\kappa
, and denote
\mu_{\mathbf{W}}
the empirical spectral distribution of
\mathbf{W}
.
Then for any
\delta>0
, if
B(\mu_{\kappa}^{\star},\delta)\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{1}^{+}([-\kappa,\kappa])
is the open ball of radius
\delta
centered in
\mu_{\kappa}^{\star}
for the distance of eq. (
14
),

\displaystyle\limsup_{d\to\infty}\frac{1}{d^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}_{\kappa}[\mu_{\mathbf{W}}\notin B(\mu_{\kappa}^{\star},\delta)]\leq-\inf_{\mu\in B(\mu_{\kappa}^{\star},\delta)^{c}}[J_{\kappa}(\mu)],

with

\displaystyle J_{\kappa}(\mu)\coloneqq I(\mu)-I(\mu_{\kappa}^{\star}),

for
I
given in eq. (
19
).
Since
J_{\kappa}
is a good rate function (see Proposition
2.3
) and has a unique minimizer (cf.
(ii)
of Theorem
2.5
),

\inf_{\mu\in B(\mu_{\kappa}^{\star},\delta)^{c}}[J_{\kappa}(\mu)]>0
. Therefore, by the Borel-Cantelli lemma,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[\limsup_{d\to\infty}d(\mu_{\mathbf{W}},\mu_{\kappa}^{\star})\leq\delta]=1,

which ends the proof by taking the limit

.

∎

3 The satisfiability region

Section
3.1
is dedicated to studying the properties of the threshold
\tau_{2}(\kappa)
.
The proof of Theorem
1.8
, which is the main goal of this section, is outlined in Section
3.2
,
and details are given in the remainder of Section
3
.

3.1
Properties of the satisfiability bound

We prove here Proposition
1.7
, which follows from the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

Define, for any
\kappa>0
,
{\bar{\tau}}(\kappa)\coloneqq\min_{\eta>0}{\widetilde{\tau}}(\eta,\kappa)
.
Then
{\bar{\tau}}(\kappa)={\widetilde{\tau}}(\eta^{\star}(\kappa),\kappa)
,
where

\eta^{\star}(\kappa)
is the unique value of
\eta>0
such that:

\displaystyle(1+\eta)\tau_{1}(\kappa)

\displaystyle=\frac{1+\delta_{\eta}^{2}}{2(1-\delta_{\eta}^{2})^{2}}+\left[\frac{\delta_{\eta}(1+6\delta_{\eta}+3\delta_{\eta}^{2}+2\delta_{\eta}^{3})}{(1-\delta_{\eta}^{2})^{3}(1-\delta_{\eta})}\right]\kappa

(28)

\displaystyle+\left[\frac{2(1+\delta_{\eta})^{5}}{(1-\delta_{\eta}^{2})^{4}}-\frac{(1+3\delta_{\eta}^{2})}{4(1-\delta_{\eta}^{2})^{3}}\right]\kappa^{2}+\frac{\kappa^{4}(1+3\delta_{\eta}^{2})}{32(1-\delta_{\eta}^{2})^{3}}.

(29)

Moreover,
\kappa\mapsto{\bar{\tau}}(\kappa)
is a continuous function of
\kappa
.

Recall that
\tau_{2}(\kappa)=\min_{u\in[0,\kappa]}{\bar{\tau}}(u)
, as defined in Lemma
3.1
,
so that
\kappa\mapsto\tau_{2}(\kappa)
is clearly continuous and non-increasing.
Moreover, solving eq. (
28
) gives a simple way to numerically evaluate
\kappa\in[0,2]\mapsto{\bar{\tau}}(\kappa)
, which then yields the values of
\tau_{2}
.

Proof of Lemma
3.1
.

Let

\begin{cases}f_{\kappa}(\eta)&\coloneqq(1+\eta)\tau_{1}(\kappa),\\
g_{\kappa}(\eta)&\coloneq\frac{1+\delta_{\eta}^{2}}{2(1-\delta_{\eta}^{2})^{2}}+\left[\frac{\delta_{\eta}(1+6\delta_{\eta}+3\delta_{\eta}^{2}+2\delta_{\eta}^{3})}{(1-\delta_{\eta}^{2})^{3}(1-\delta_{\eta})}\right]\kappa\\
&+\left[\frac{2(1+\delta_{\eta})^{5}}{(1-\delta_{\eta}^{2})^{4}}-\frac{(1+3\delta_{\eta}^{2})}{4(1-\delta_{\eta}^{2})^{3}}\right]\kappa^{2}+\frac{\kappa^{4}(1+3\delta_{\eta}^{2})}{32(1-\delta_{\eta}^{2})^{3}}.\end{cases}

Recall that
\delta_{\eta}
is defined as the unique solution to
H[(1+\delta)/2]/\log 2=\eta/(1+\eta)
,
with
H(p)=-p\log p-(1-p)\log(1-p)
.
If
G(\delta)\coloneqq H[(1+\delta)/2]/\log 2
, then

G
is smooth and strictly decreasing on
[0,1]
.
So
\delta_{\eta}=G^{-1}[\eta/(1+\eta)]
is a smooth and strictly decreasing function of
\eta>0
.
It is then immediate by elementary arguments (it is easy to see that each of the
\kappa
-coefficients of
g_{\kappa}(\eta)
is a strictly increasing function of
\delta
, similarly to what is done e.g. in eq. (
48
)) that

g_{\kappa}(\eta)
is also a smooth and strictly decreasing function of
\eta
.

Moreover, we have
g_{\kappa}(0^{+})=\infty
,
f_{\kappa}(0^{+})=\tau_{1}(\kappa)<\infty
, and
g_{\kappa}(\infty)<\infty
,
f_{\kappa}(\infty)=\infty
.
It is then elementary to show that for any
\kappa>0
,
\min_{\eta>0}{\widetilde{\tau}}(\eta,\kappa)=\min_{\eta>0}\max\{f_{\kappa}(\eta),g_{\kappa}(\eta)\}

is reached in a unique
\eta^{\star}(\kappa)
, such that
f_{\kappa}(\eta^{\star}(\kappa))=g_{\kappa}(\eta^{\star}(\kappa))
, and that

\eta^{\star}(\kappa)
is a continuous function of
\kappa
, which implies that
{\bar{\tau}}(\kappa)=f_{\kappa}(\eta^{\star}(\kappa))
is also continuous.
∎

3.2
Reduction to a second moment upper bound

The main element of our analysis is the following upper bound.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\kappa\in(0,2]
. Recall the definition of
Z_{\kappa}
in eq. (
2
), and of
{\bar{\tau}}(\kappa)
in Lemma
3.1
.
Assume that
\tau>{\bar{\tau}}(\kappa)
.
Then, for
n,d\to\infty
with
n/d^{2}\to\tau
:

\limsup_{d\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{E}[Z_{\kappa}^{2}]}{\mathbb{E}[Z_{\kappa}]^{2}}\leq L\cdot\left[1-\frac{{\bar{\tau}}(\kappa)}{\tau}\right]^{-1/2},

for an absolute constant
L>0
.

We will detail the proof of Proposition
3.2
in Section
3.3
, deferring some intermediate results to Section
3.4
and
3.5
.
We first show how to deduce Theorem
1.8
.

Proof of Theorem
1.8
.

Since
\tau>\tau_{2}(\kappa)=\min_{u\in[0,\kappa]}{\bar{\tau}}(u)
(see Lemma
3.1
),
and
Z_{\kappa^{\prime}}\leq Z_{\kappa}
for any
\kappa^{\prime}\leq\kappa
, we can assume without loss of generality that
\tau>{\bar{\tau}}(\kappa)
in order to prove Theorem
1.8
.

Because the bound on the right-hand side is strictly higher than
1
, Proposition
3.2
is not strong enough to directly guarantee the existence of solutions with high probability.
This is a recurring challenge in many random constraint satisfaction problems where

\mathbb{E}[Z_{\kappa}^{2}]/\mathbb{E}[Z_{\kappa}]^{2}\to C>1
.
This occurs e.g. in the symmetric binary perceptron, the vector analog of our matrix discrepancy task, see
Abbe et al., 2022
,
and prevents from applying the classical second moment method to get high-probability bounds.
Fortunately, in
Altschuler, 2023
the author develops general techniques on sharp transitions for integer feasibility problems,
and applies them to show the concentration of the discrepancy
\min_{\varepsilon\in\{\pm 1\}^{n}}\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\varepsilon_{i}\mathbf{W}_{i}\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}
.

Lemma 3.3

(Theorem 7 of
Altschuler, 2023
)
.

Let
d\geq 1
, and
\mathbf{W}_{1},\cdots,\mathbf{W}_{n}\overset{\mathrm{i.i.d.}}{\sim}\mathrm{GOE}(d)
.
Let
{\rm disc}(\mathbf{W}_{1},\cdots,\mathbf{W}_{n})\coloneqq\min_{\varepsilon\in\{\pm 1\}^{n}}\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\varepsilon_{i}\mathbf{W}_{i}\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}
.
Assume that
n/d^{2}\to\tau
as
d\to\infty
. Then there exists
c(\tau)>0
such that

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbb{E}[{\rm disc}(\mathbf{W}_{1},\cdots,\mathbf{W}_{n})]}{\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}[{\rm disc}(\mathbf{W}_{1},\cdots,\mathbf{W}_{n})]}}\geq c(\tau)\sqrt{d}.

Let us see how the combination of Lemma
3.3
with our second moment estimates (Proposition
3.2
)
ends the proof of Theorem
1.8
.

Since
{\bar{\tau}}(\kappa)
is a continuous function of
\kappa
by Lemma
3.1
, we choose
\delta>0
small enough such
that
\tau>{\bar{\tau}}(\kappa-\delta)
.
Let
X\coloneqq{\rm disc}(\mathbf{W}_{1},\cdots,\mathbf{W}_{n})
.
Notice that
X\leq\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{W}_{i}\|_{\mathrm{op}}
,
so that
\mathbb{E}X\leq 2\sqrt{n}
since
(1/\sqrt{n})\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{W}_{i}\sim\mathrm{GOE}(d)
,
and
\mathbb{E}\|\mathbf{Y}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq 2
for
\mathbf{Y}\sim\mathrm{GOE}(d)
(see e.g. Exercise 7.3.5 of
Vershynin, 2018
).
From Lemma
3.3
we thus get

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}(X)\leq\frac{4n}{c(\tau)^{2}d},

(30)

where
\mathrm{Var}(X)\coloneqq\mathbb{E}[(X-\mathbb{E}X)^{2}]
.
Recall that the Paley-Zygmund inequality states that for any random variable
X\geq 0
:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[X>0]\geq\frac{\mathbb{E}[X]^{2}}{\mathbb{E}[X^{2}]}.

Applying it to
Z_{\kappa-\delta}
and using Proposition
3.2
, we get that

\mathbb{P}[X\leq(\kappa-\delta)\sqrt{n}]\geq L^{-1}\cdot\left[1-\frac{{\bar{\tau}}(\kappa-\delta)}{\tau}\right]^{1/2}+o_{d}(1).

(31)

Let us denote
C(\tau,\kappa,\delta)\coloneqq L\cdot[1-{\bar{\tau}}(\kappa-\delta)/\tau]^{-1/2}
.
By Chebyshev’s inequality and eq. (
30
), we further have for all
t>0
:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[X\geq\mathbb{E}X-t]\geq 1-\frac{4n}{c(\tau)^{2}dt^{2}}.

In particular, if
t=c(\tau)^{-1}\sqrt{8C(\tau,\kappa,\delta)n/d}
, we have
\mathbb{P}[X\geq\mathbb{E}X-t]\geq 1-(2C(\tau,\kappa,\delta))^{-1}
, which combined
with eq. (
31
) implies that

\mathbb{E}X\leq(\kappa-\delta)\sqrt{n}+c_{2}(\tau,\kappa,\delta)\sqrt{\frac{n}{d}},

where we redefined the constant
c_{2}(\tau,\kappa,\delta)>0
.
Again by Chebyshev’s inequality and eq. (
30
), this implies that for all
u>0
:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[X\leq(\kappa-\delta)\sqrt{n}+c_{2}\sqrt{\frac{n}{d}}+u]

\displaystyle\geq 1-\frac{c_{1}(\tau)n}{du^{2}}.

Picking
u=\delta\sqrt{n}-c_{2}\sqrt{n/d}
, we have
u\geq(\delta/2)\sqrt{n}
for
n,d
large enough, and this yields:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[X\leq\kappa\sqrt{n}]

\displaystyle\geq 1-\frac{4c_{1}(\tau)}{\delta^{2}d}\to_{d\to\infty}1,

which ends the proof.
∎

3.3
Proof of the second moment upper bound

We prove here Proposition
3.2
.
We compute the second moment as:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[Z_{\kappa}^{2}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime}\in\{\pm 1\}^{n}}\mathbb{P}\left[\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\varepsilon_{i}\mathbf{W}_{i}\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\kappa\sqrt{n}\,\,\textrm{ and }\,\,\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\varepsilon^{\prime}_{i}\mathbf{W}_{i}\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\kappa\sqrt{n}\right],

\displaystyle\overset{\mathrm{(a)}}{=}2^{n}\sum_{\varepsilon\in\{\pm 1\}^{n}}\mathbb{P}\left[\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{W}_{i}\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\kappa\sqrt{n}\,\,\textrm{ and }\,\,\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\varepsilon_{i}\mathbf{W}_{i}\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\kappa\sqrt{n}\right],

\displaystyle\overset{\mathrm{(b)}}{=}2^{n}\sum_{l=0}^{n}\binom{n}{l}\mathbb{P}\left[\|\mathbf{W}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\kappa\textrm{ and }\|q_{l}\mathbf{W}+\sqrt{1-q_{l}^{2}}\mathbf{Z}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\kappa\right].

(32)

In
(\rm a)
and
(\rm b)
we used the rotation invariance of the
\mathrm{GOE}(d)
distribution. In eq. (
32
),
we changed variables to
l\coloneqq(\langle\varepsilon,\mathbf{1}_{n}\rangle+n)/2
and defined the “overlap”
q_{l}\coloneqq(1/n)\langle\varepsilon,\mathbf{1}_{n}\rangle=2(l/n)-1
.
Furthermore,
\mathbf{W},\mathbf{Z}\sim\mathrm{GOE}(d)
independently.
We get from eq. (
32
) that (recall as well eq. (
11
)):

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbb{E}[Z_{\kappa}^{2}]}{\mathbb{E}[Z_{\kappa}]^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{n}}\sum_{l=0}^{n}\binom{n}{l}\exp\{nG_{d}(q_{l})\},

(33)

where for
q\in[-1,1]
:

\displaystyle G_{d}(q)\coloneqq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\log\frac{\mathbb{P}\left[\|\mathbf{W}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\kappa\textrm{ and }\|q\mathbf{W}+\sqrt{1-q^{2}}\mathbf{Z}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\kappa\right]}{\mathbb{P}[\|\mathbf{W}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\kappa]^{2}}.

(34)

Recall that
H(p)\coloneqq-p\log p-(1-p)\log(1-p)
.
We will leverage the following lemma, which is based on standard asymptotic techniques,
and whose proof is deferred to Section
3.4
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
n\geq 1
, and
F_{n}:[-1,1]\to\mathbb{R}
such that
F_{n}(0)=0
and
F_{n}^{\prime}(0)=0
.
Assume that there exists
(\gamma,\delta)>0
such that:

(i)

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sup_{|q|\leq\delta}F_{n}^{\prime\prime}(q)\leq 1-\gamma
.

(ii)

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sup_{|q|\geq\delta}\left[F_{n}(q)+H\left(\frac{1+q}{2}\right)\right]<\log 2
.

Then (with
q_{l}\coloneqq 2l/n-1\in[-1,1]
for
l\in\{0,\cdots,n\}
):

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{2^{n}}\sum_{l=0}^{n}\binom{n}{l}\exp\{nF_{n}(q_{l})\}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{\gamma}},

for a global constant
C>0
.

From eq. (
33
), in order to finish the proof of Proposition
3.2
, it suffices to check conditions

(i)
and
(ii)
of Lemma
3.4
for
G_{d}
defined in eq. (
34
), for
\tau>{\bar{\tau}}(\kappa)
,
\gamma=1-{\bar{\tau}}(\kappa)/\tau
, and some
\delta>0
.

Recall the definition of
\eta^{\star}(\kappa)
in Lemma
3.1
.
We let
\delta\coloneqq\delta_{\eta^{\star}(\kappa)}
as defined by eq. (
6
).

Condition
(ii)
–

Notice that
G_{d}(0)=0
and that
G_{d}
is clearly an even function of
q
, so
G_{d}^{\prime}(0)=0
(the smoothness of
G_{d}
can be shown by direct computation, as we will see in eq. (
36
)).
Furthermore, we have the trivial bound:

\displaystyle G_{d}(q)+H\left(\frac{1+q}{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq H\left(\frac{1+q}{2}\right)-\frac{1}{n}\log\mathbb{P}[\|\mathbf{W}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\kappa].

Recall that
q\mapsto H[(1+q)/2]
is even, and strictly decreasing on
[0,1]
.
Using Proposition
2.1
, and the definition of
\tau_{1}(\kappa)
in eq. (
4
),
we get

\displaystyle\limsup_{d\to\infty}\sup_{|q|\geq\delta}\left[G_{d}(q)+H\left(\frac{1+q}{2}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq H\left(\frac{1+\delta}{2}\right)+\frac{\tau_{1}(\kappa)}{\tau}\log 2,

\displaystyle\overset{\mathrm{(a)}}{<}H\left(\frac{1+\delta}{2}\right)+\left[\frac{1}{1+\eta^{\star}(\kappa)}\right]\log 2,

\displaystyle\overset{\mathrm{(b)}}{=}\left[\frac{\eta^{\star}(\kappa)}{1+\eta^{\star}(\kappa)}+\frac{1}{1+\eta^{\star}(\kappa)}\right]\log 2,

\displaystyle=\log 2,

using
\tau>{\bar{\tau}}(\kappa)=(1+\eta^{\star}(\kappa))\tau_{1}(\kappa)
in
(\rm a)
,
and the definition of
\delta=\delta_{\eta^{\star}(\kappa)}
in
(\rm b)
, cf. eq. (
6
).
We have thus checked condition
(ii)
of Lemma
3.4
.

Condition
(i)
–

We will show that for any
\delta\in(0,1)
:

\displaystyle\limsup_{d\to\infty}\sup_{|q|\leq\delta}G_{d}^{\prime\prime}(q)\leq\frac{1}{\tau}

\displaystyle\left\{\frac{1+\delta^{2}}{2(1-\delta^{2})^{2}}+\left[\frac{\delta(1+6\delta+3\delta^{2}+2\delta^{3})}{(1-\delta^{2})^{3}(1-\delta)}\right]\kappa\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\left[\frac{2(1+\delta)^{5}}{(1-\delta^{2})^{4}}-\frac{(1+3\delta^{2})}{4(1-\delta^{2})^{3}}\right]\kappa^{2}+\frac{\kappa^{4}(1+3\delta^{2})}{32(1-\delta^{2})^{3}}\right\}.

(35)

Let us first show how eq. (
35
) finishes the proof of Proposition
3.2
.
We pick
\delta=\delta_{\eta^{\star}(\kappa)}
.
By Lemma
3.1
, eq. (
35
) can be rewritten for this value of
\delta

as

\displaystyle\limsup_{d\to\infty}\sup_{|q|\leq\delta}G_{d}^{\prime\prime}(q)\leq\frac{{\bar{\tau}}(\kappa)}{\tau}=1-\gamma,

with
\gamma\coloneqq(1-{\bar{\tau}}(\kappa)/\tau)
.
This implies that condition
(i)
of Lemma
3.4
holds with this value of
\gamma
,
and thus ends the proof of Proposition
3.2
, as described above.

Proof of eq. (
35
) –

There remains to show eq. (
35
).
Let
q\in[0,1)
.
We have (
\mathrm{d}\mathbf{W}=\prod_{i\leq j}\mathrm{d}W_{ij}
is the Lebesgue measure over the space
\mathcal{S}_{d}
of symmetric matrices):

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\|\mathbf{W}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\kappa\textrm{ and }\|q\mathbf{W}+\sqrt{1-q^{2}}\mathbf{Z}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\kappa\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{\int\mathds{1}\{\|\mathbf{W}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\kappa\}e^{-\frac{d}{4}{\rm Tr}[\mathbf{W}^{2}]}\mathbb{P}\left[\|q\mathbf{W}+\sqrt{1-q^{2}}\mathbf{Z}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\kappa\right]\mathrm{d}\mathbf{W}}{\int e^{-\frac{d}{4}{\rm Tr}[\mathbf{W}^{2}]}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{W}},

\displaystyle=\frac{\int\mathds{1}\{\|\mathbf{W}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\kappa\}e^{-\frac{d}{4}{\rm Tr}[\mathbf{W}^{2}]}\left(\int\mathrm{d}\mathbf{Y}e^{-\frac{d}{4(1-q^{2})}{\rm Tr}[(\mathbf{Y}-q\mathbf{W})^{2}]}\mathds{1}\{\|\mathbf{Y}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\kappa\}\right)\mathrm{d}\mathbf{W}}{\left(\int e^{-\frac{d}{4}{\rm Tr}[\mathbf{W}^{2}]}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{W}\right)\left(\int\mathrm{d}\mathbf{Y}e^{-\frac{d}{4(1-q^{2})}{\rm Tr}[\mathbf{Y}^{2}]}\right)},

\displaystyle=\frac{\int\mathds{1}\{\|\mathbf{W}\|_{\mathrm{op}},\|\mathbf{Y}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\kappa\}e^{-\frac{d}{4(1-q^{2})}({\rm Tr}[\mathbf{W}^{2}]+{\rm Tr}[\mathbf{Y}^{2}])+\frac{dq}{2(1-q^{2})}{\rm Tr}[\mathbf{Y}\mathbf{W}]}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{Y}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{W}}{\left(\int\mathrm{d}\mathbf{W}e^{-\frac{d}{4}{\rm Tr}[\mathbf{W}^{2}]}\right)^{2}(1-q^{2})^{d(d+1)/4}}.

(36)

Starting from eq. (
36
), we can compute the derivatives of
G_{d}(q)
.
We will use the shorthand notation

\displaystyle\langle\cdot\rangle_{q,\kappa}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{\int(\cdot)\mathds{1}\{\|\mathbf{W}\|_{\mathrm{op}},\|\mathbf{Y}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\kappa\}e^{-\frac{d}{4(1-q^{2})}({\rm Tr}[\mathbf{W}^{2}]+{\rm Tr}[\mathbf{Y}^{2}])+\frac{dq}{2(1-q^{2})}{\rm Tr}[\mathbf{Y}\mathbf{W}]}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{Y}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{W}}{\int\mathds{1}\{\|\mathbf{W}\|_{\mathrm{op}},\|\mathbf{Y}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\kappa\}e^{-\frac{d}{4(1-q^{2})}({\rm Tr}[\mathbf{W}^{2}]+{\rm Tr}[\mathbf{Y}^{2}])+\frac{dq}{2(1-q^{2})}{\rm Tr}[\mathbf{Y}\mathbf{W}]}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{Y}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{W}},

(37)

i.e.
\langle\cdot\rangle_{q,\kappa}
is the law of
(\mathbf{W},\mathbf{Y})
two correlated
\mathrm{GOE}(d)
matrices (with correlation
q
), conditioned on the event
\|\mathbf{W}\|_{\mathrm{op}},\|\mathbf{Y}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\kappa
.

G_{d}(q)
is the log-partition function (or “free energy” in statistical physics) of
this high-dimensional probability measure,
and taking derivatives will yield averages of observables under
\langle\cdot\rangle_{q,\kappa}
.
We get

\displaystyle G_{d}^{\prime}(q)

\displaystyle=\frac{d(d+1)q}{2n(1-q^{2})}+\frac{1}{2n}\left\langle-\frac{dq}{(1-q^{2})^{2}}{\rm Tr}[\mathbf{W}^{2}+\mathbf{Y}^{2}]+\frac{d(1+q^{2})}{(1-q^{2})^{2}}{\rm Tr}[\mathbf{W}\mathbf{Y}]\right\rangle_{q,\kappa}.

Differentiating further, we obtain:

\displaystyle G_{d}^{\prime\prime}(q)

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\frac{d(d+1)(1+q^{2})}{2n(1-q^{2})^{2}}}_{\eqqcolon I_{1}(q)}+\underbrace{\frac{1}{2n}\left\langle-\frac{d(1+3q^{2})}{(1-q^{2})^{3}}{\rm Tr}[\mathbf{W}^{2}+\mathbf{Y}^{2}]+\frac{2dq(3+q^{2})}{(1-q^{2})^{3}}{\rm Tr}[\mathbf{W}\mathbf{Y}]\right\rangle_{q,\kappa}}_{\eqqcolon I_{2}(q)}

\displaystyle+\underbrace{\frac{1}{4n}\textrm{Var}_{\langle\cdot\rangle_{q,\kappa}}\left(-\frac{dq}{(1-q^{2})^{2}}{\rm Tr}[\mathbf{W}^{2}+\mathbf{Y}^{2}]+\frac{d(1+q^{2})}{(1-q^{2})^{2}}{\rm Tr}[\mathbf{W}\mathbf{Y}]\right)}_{\eqqcolon I_{3}(q)}.

(38)

We bound successively the different terms
\{I_{a}\}_{a=1}^{3}
in eq. (
38
).
Since
n/d^{2}\to\tau
, we have:

\displaystyle\limsup_{d\to\infty}\sup_{|q|\leq\delta}I_{1}(q)=\frac{1}{\tau}\sup_{|q|\leq\delta}\frac{(1+q^{2})}{2(1-q^{2})^{2}}=\frac{(1+\delta^{2})}{2\tau(1-\delta^{2})^{2}}.

(39)

Recall that for a real random variable
X
, we define the sub-Gaussian norm
\|X\|_{\psi_{2}}
of
X
as
(
Vershynin, 2018
)
:

\displaystyle\|X\|_{\psi_{2}}\coloneqq\inf\{t>0\,:\,\mathbb{E}[\exp(X^{2}/t^{2})]\leq 2\}.

To bound
I_{2}
and
I_{3}
, we rely on the following crucial result, which we prove in Section
3.5
.

Lemma 3.5

(Concentration of moments under
\langle\cdot\rangle_{q,\kappa}
)
.

Let
q\in(-1,1)
,
\kappa\in(0,2]
, and

P(X_{1},X_{2})\coloneqq\sum_{p\geq 0}\sum_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{p}\in\{1,2\}}a_{i_{1}\cdots i_{p}}X_{i_{1}}\cdots X_{i_{p}}

be a polynomial
in two non-commutative random variables
(X_{1},X_{2})
.
Let
(\mathbf{W},\mathbf{Y})~\sim~\langle\cdot\rangle_{q,\kappa}
given by eq. (
37
).
Then:

\|{\rm Tr}\,P(\mathbf{W},\mathbf{Y})-\langle{\rm Tr}\,P(\mathbf{W},\mathbf{Y})\rangle_{q,\kappa}\|_{\psi_{2}}\leq C\sqrt{1+q}\sum_{p\geq 0}p\cdot\kappa^{p-1}\sum_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{p}\in\{1,2\}}|a_{i_{1}\cdots i_{p}}|,

where
C>0
is an absolute constant. Furthermore, we have the fully explicit bound:

\mathrm{Var}_{\langle\cdot\rangle_{q,\kappa}}[{\rm Tr}\,P(\mathbf{W},\mathbf{Y})]\leq 2(1+q)\left(\sum_{p\geq 0}p\cdot\kappa^{p-1}\sum_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{p}\in\{1,2\}}|a_{i_{1}\cdots i_{p}}|\right)^{2}.

(40)

Lemma
3.5
is a consequence of a log-Sobolev inequality we prove for
\langle\cdot\rangle_{q,\kappa}
.

Bounding
I_{2}
–

Note that under the law of
\langle\cdot\rangle_{0,\kappa}
of eq. (
37
) when
q=0
,

\mathbf{W}
is distributed as a
\mathrm{GOE}(d)
matrix, conditioned to satisfy
\|\mathbf{W}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\kappa
.
By Theorem
2.2
, we know that
\mu_{\mathbf{W}}
weakly converges (a.s.) to
\mu_{\kappa}^{\star}
.
Since
\int\mu_{\mathbf{W}}(\mathrm{d}x)x^{2}=\int\mu_{\mathbf{W}}(\mathrm{d}x)x^{2}\mathds{1}\{|x|\leq\kappa\}
, we have by the Portmanteau theorem
and dominated convergence:

\displaystyle\lim_{d\to\infty}\frac{1}{d}\langle{\rm Tr}\mathbf{W}^{2}\rangle_{0,\kappa}

\displaystyle=\int\mu_{\kappa}^{\star}(\mathrm{d}x)\,x^{2}\,\mathds{1}\{|x|\leq\kappa\}

\displaystyle=\int\mu_{\kappa}^{\star}(\mathrm{d}x)\,x^{2},

(41)

\displaystyle\overset{\mathrm{(a)}}{=}\frac{\kappa^{2}(8-\kappa^{2})}{16},

(42)

using eq. (
25
) in
(\rm a)
.
By symmetry
\mathbf{W}\to-\mathbf{W}
, we also trivially have

\displaystyle\langle{\rm Tr}\mathbf{W}\mathbf{Y}\rangle_{0,\kappa}

\displaystyle=0.

(43)

Let us denote
P_{q}(X,Y)\coloneqq-q(X^{2}+Y^{2})+(1+q^{2})XY
.
One easily computes from eq. (
37
) that
for any function
\varphi(\mathbf{W},\mathbf{Y})
:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial q}\langle\varphi(\mathbf{W},\mathbf{Y})\rangle_{q,\kappa}

\displaystyle=\frac{d\left[\langle\varphi(\mathbf{W},\mathbf{Y})\cdot{\rm Tr}[P_{q}(\mathbf{W},\mathbf{Y})]\rangle_{q,\kappa}-\langle\varphi(\mathbf{W},\mathbf{Y})\rangle_{q,\kappa}\langle{\rm Tr}[P_{q}(\mathbf{W},\mathbf{Y})]\rangle_{q,\kappa}\right]}{2(1-q^{2})^{2}},

\displaystyle=\frac{d\left[\langle\left(\varphi(\mathbf{W},\mathbf{Y})-\langle\varphi\rangle_{q,\kappa}\right)\cdot\left({\rm Tr}[P_{q}(\mathbf{W},\mathbf{Y})]-\langle{\rm Tr}[P_{q}]\rangle_{q,\kappa}\right)\rangle_{q,\kappa}\right]}{2(1-q^{2})^{2}}.

In particular:

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial q}\langle\varphi(\mathbf{W},\mathbf{Y})\rangle_{q,\kappa}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{d\left[\mathrm{Var}_{\langle\cdot\rangle_{q,\kappa}}[\varphi(\mathbf{W},\mathbf{Y})]\cdot\mathrm{Var}_{\langle\cdot\rangle_{q,\kappa}}[{\rm Tr}P_{q}(\mathbf{W},\mathbf{Y})]\right]^{1/2}}{2(1-q^{2})^{2}}.

(44)

Using eq. (
44
) and Lemma
3.5
,
we reach that for both
\varphi={\rm Tr}[\mathbf{W}^{2}]
and
\varphi={\rm Tr}[\mathbf{W}\mathbf{Y}]
:

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial q}\langle\varphi(\mathbf{W},\mathbf{Y})\rangle_{q,\kappa}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2d\kappa(1+q)^{2}}{(1-q^{2})^{2}}=\frac{2d\kappa}{(1-q)^{2}}.

(45)

Integrating eq. (
45
), and combining it with eqs. (
41
) and eq. (
43
), we get:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\left|\frac{1}{d}\langle{\rm Tr}\mathbf{W}^{2}\rangle_{q,\kappa}-\frac{\kappa^{2}(8-\kappa^{2})}{16}\right|&\leq\frac{2\kappa|q|}{1-q}+o_{d}(1),\\
\left|\frac{1}{d}\langle{\rm Tr}\mathbf{W}\mathbf{Y}\rangle_{q,\kappa}\right|&\leq\frac{2\kappa|q|}{1-q}+o_{d}(1),\end{cases}

(46)

where
o_{d}(1)
is uniform in
q
.
We get from eq. (
46
):

\displaystyle\limsup_{d\to\infty}\sup_{|q|\leq\delta}I_{2}(q)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2\tau}\max_{|q|\leq\delta}\left[\frac{4\kappa q^{2}(3+q^{2})}{(1-q^{2})^{3}(1-q)}-\frac{1+3q^{2}}{(1-q^{2})^{3}}\left(\frac{\kappa^{2}(8-\kappa^{2})}{16}-\frac{2\kappa|q|}{1-q}\right)\right],

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\tau}\max_{q\in[0,\delta]}\left[\frac{2\kappa q(1+6q+3q^{2}+2q^{3})}{(1-q^{2})^{3}(1-q)}-\frac{\kappa^{2}(8-\kappa^{2})(1+3q^{2})}{16(1-q^{2})^{3}}\right].

(47)

If

f_{\kappa}(q)\coloneqq\frac{2\kappa q(1+6q+3q^{2}+2q^{3})}{1-q}-\frac{\kappa^{2}(8-\kappa^{2})(1+3q^{2})}{16},

then for all
q\in[0,1]
and
\kappa\in[0,2]
:

\displaystyle f_{\kappa}^{\prime}(q)

\displaystyle=\frac{\kappa}{8(1-q)^{2}}\left[16+3(64-8\kappa+\kappa^{3})q+6(8+8\kappa-\kappa^{3})q^{2}+(32-3\kappa(8-\kappa^{2}))q^{4}-96q^{5}\right],

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\kappa}{8(1-q)^{2}}\left[16+3(64-16)q+6(8-8)q^{2}+(32-3\cdot 2\cdot(8))q^{4}-96q^{5}\right],

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\kappa}{8(1-q)^{2}}\left[16+144q-16q^{3}-96q^{5}\right],

\displaystyle\overset{\mathrm{(a)}}{\geq}\frac{\kappa}{8(1-q)^{2}}\left[16+32q\right]>0,

(48)

using
q^{k}\leq q
for any
k\geq 1
in
(\rm a)
.
This implies that in eq. (
47
), the maximum is attained at
q=\delta
, and we get:

\displaystyle\limsup_{d\to\infty}\sup_{|q|\leq\delta}I_{2}(q)\leq\frac{1}{2\tau}\left[\frac{2\kappa\delta(1+6\delta+3\delta^{2}+2\delta^{3})}{(1-\delta^{2})^{3}(1-\delta)}-\frac{\kappa^{2}(8-\kappa^{2})(1+3\delta^{2})}{16(1-\delta^{2})^{3}}\right].

(49)

Bounding
I_{3}
–

We apply eq. (
40
) of Lemma
3.5
to
P_{q}(X,Y)=-q(X^{2}+Y^{2})+(1+q^{2})XY
, which yields:

\displaystyle I_{3}(q)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{d^{2}}{4n(1-q^{2})^{4}}\cdot 2(1+q)\left(2\kappa[2q+1+q^{2}]\right)^{2},

\displaystyle=\frac{2d^{2}(1+q)^{5}\kappa^{2}}{n(1-q^{2})^{4}}.

So finally we get:

\displaystyle\limsup_{d\to\infty}\sup_{|q|\leq\delta}I_{3}(q)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2(1+\delta)^{5}\kappa^{2}}{\tau(1-\delta^{2})^{4}}.

(50)

Combining eqs. (
39
),(
49
),(
50
) finishes the proof of eq. (
35
).
As we discussed above, this ends the proof of Proposition
3.2
.

∎

3.4
Discrete Laplace’s method for a dimension-dependent exponent

We prove here Lemma
3.4
.
By hypothesis
(ii)
, we fix
\varepsilon>0
such that, for
n
large enough:

\sup_{|q|\geq\delta}\left[F_{n}(q)+H\left(\frac{1+q}{2}\right)\right]\leq\log 2-\varepsilon.

(51)

Recall the classical inequality:

\displaystyle\binom{n}{l}\leq e^{nH(l/n)},\hskip 14.22636pt

\displaystyle\textrm{ for }l\in\{0,\cdots,n\}.

(52)

Combining eqs. (
51
) and (
52
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{n}}\sum_{l=0}^{n}\mathds{1}\left\{\left|l-\frac{n}{2}\right|>\frac{n\delta}{2}\right\}\binom{n}{l}\exp\{nF_{n}(q_{l})\}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2^{n}}\sum_{l=0}^{n}\mathds{1}\left\{\left|l-\frac{n}{2}\right|>\frac{n\delta}{2}\right\}\exp\{n(\log 2-\varepsilon)\},

\displaystyle\leq n\exp\{-n\varepsilon\}.

(53)

Let
\sigma\in(0,\gamma)
.
By hypothesis
(i)
, we get that for
n
large enough

F_{n}^{\prime\prime}(q)\leq(1-\gamma+\sigma)
for all
|q|\leq\delta
.
Since
F_{n}(0)=0
and
F_{n}^{\prime}(0)=0
, this implies

F_{n}(q)\leq(1-\gamma+\sigma)q^{2}/2
for all
|q|\leq\delta
.
Therefore,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{n}}\sum_{l=0}^{n}\mathds{1}\left\{\left|l-\frac{n}{2}\right|\leq\frac{n\delta}{2}\right\}\binom{n}{l}\exp\{nF_{n}(q_{l})\}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2^{n}}\sum_{l=0}^{n}\binom{n}{l}e^{\frac{n(1-\gamma+\sigma)}{2}q_{l}^{2}}.

(54)

Recall that
q_{l}=2(l/n)-1
.
The right-hand side of eq. (
54
) can now be analyzed with standard extensions of Laplace’s method.
We use here the following statement, which is a consequence of the proof of Lemma 2 of
Achlioptas & Moore, 2002
.

Lemma 3.6

(
Achlioptas & Moore, 2002
)
.

There exists
B,C>0
such that the following holds.
Let
G
a real analytic positive function on
[0,1]
, and define for
\alpha\in[0,1]
:

\displaystyle g(\alpha)\coloneqq\frac{G(\alpha)}{\alpha^{\alpha}(1-\alpha)^{1-\alpha}}.

If there exists
\alpha_{\max}\in(0,1)
a strict global maximum of
g
in
[0,1]
such that
g^{\prime\prime}(\alpha_{\max})<0
, then for sufficiently large
n
:

\displaystyle B\cdot\frac{g(\alpha_{\max})^{n+1/2}}{\sqrt{-g^{\prime\prime}(\alpha_{\max})}}\leq\sum_{l=0}^{n}\binom{n}{l}G(l/n)^{n}\leq C\cdot\frac{g(\alpha_{\max})^{n+1/2}}{\sqrt{\alpha_{\max}(1-\alpha_{\max})(-g^{\prime\prime}(\alpha_{\max}))}}.

Remark –
Lemma
3.6
is stated in
Achlioptas & Moore, 2002

as

\displaystyle C_{1}\cdot g(\alpha_{\max})^{n}\leq\sum_{l=0}^{n}\binom{n}{l}G(l/n)^{n}\leq C_{2}\cdot g(\alpha_{\max})^{n},

where the constants
C_{1},C_{2}
might depend on
\alpha_{\max}
and
g(\alpha_{\max})
.
Their proof (see Appendix A of
Achlioptas & Moore, 2002
) reveals the dependency of
C_{1},C_{2}
on
\alpha_{\max}
and
g^{\prime\prime}(\alpha_{\max})
, which we make explicit here.

We apply Lemma
3.6
in eq. (
54
), with

3.5
Log-Sobolev inequality for the conditioned law of two correlated
\mathrm{GOE}(d)

In this section, we start by reminders on log-Sobolev inequalities, before proving such a property
for the law of eq. (
37
), and finally proving Lemma
3.5

3.5.1
Log-Sobolev inequalities and concentration of measure

Definition 3.1
.

Let
d\geq 1
.
A probability measure
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{1}^{+}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is said to satisfy the
Logarithmic Sobolev Inequality
(LSI) with constant
c>0
if, for any differentiable function

f
in
L^{2}(\mu)
, we have

\int f^{2}\log\frac{f^{2}}{\int f^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mu}\mathrm{d}\mu\leq 2c\int\|\nabla f\|_{2}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mu.

(56)

We refer the reader to
Guionnet, 2009
;
Anderson et al., 2010

for more on the theory of log-Sobolev inequalities and their applications to concentration results in random matrix theory.
A particularly useful consequence of the LSI is the following.

Lemma 3.7

(Herbst)
.

Assume that
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{1}^{+}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
satisfies the LSI with constant
c
. Let
G~:~\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
be a Lipschitz function, with Lipschitz constant
\|G\|_{L}
.
Then for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\left[e^{\lambda[G-\mathbb{E}G]}\right]\leq\exp\left\{\frac{c\|G\|_{L}^{2}\lambda^{2}}{2}\right\}.

(57)

Therefore, for all

\delta>0
:

\mu(|G-\mathbb{E}G|\geq\delta)\leq 2\exp\left\{-\frac{\delta^{2}}{2c\|G\|_{L}^{2}}\right\}.

Remark –
Notice that Lemma
57
implies that

\|G-\mathbb{E}G\|_{\psi_{2}}\leq C\sqrt{c}\|G\|_{L}
for some absolute constant
C>0
,
and moreover (by Taylor expansion close to
\lambda=0
) we have
\mathrm{Var}(G)\leq c\|G\|_{L}^{2}
.

Finally, we will use that a necessary condition for a measure to satisfy the LSI is the so-called
Bakry-Emery (BE)
condition.

Theorem 3.8

(Theorem 4.4.17 of
Anderson et al., 2010
)
.

Let
d\geq 1
and
\Phi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
a
\mathcal{C}^{2}
function.
Assume that
\Phi
satisfies the Bakry-Emery condition:

{\rm Hess}\,\Phi(x)\succeq\frac{1}{c}\mathrm{I}_{d},

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, for some
c>0
.
Then the measure

\mu_{\Phi}(\mathrm{d}x)\coloneqq\frac{1}{\mathcal{Z}}e^{-\Phi(x)}\mathrm{d}x

satisfies the LSI with constant
c
.

A log-Sobolev inequality for the law of eq. (

Finally, notice that

\displaystyle V(x)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\begin{cases}\frac{x^{2}}{2}&\textrm{ if }|x|<\kappa,\\

Because the set

Since

3

{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}\leftrightarrow-{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}
.
Concretely, for all
i\in[d]
:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\lambda_{i}^{2}]=\mathbb{E}[\lambda_{1}^{2}]=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\mathbb{E}[\lambda_{j}^{2}]=\frac{1}{d}\mathbb{E}[{\rm Tr}\mathbf{W}^{2}],

(71)

and for
i\neq j

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}]=\frac{1}{d-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\lambda_{1}\sum_{k\geq 2}\lambda_{k}\right]=\frac{1}{d(d-1)}\mathbb{E}[({\rm Tr}\mathbf{W})^{2}-{\rm Tr}(\mathbf{W}^{2})].

(72)

The first moments of the matrix elements of a Haar-sampled orthogonal matrix are elementary (see e.g.
Banica et al., 2011
for general results):

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[O_{ij}^{2}O_{kl}^{2}]

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\frac{3}{d(d+2)}&(i=k\textrm{ and }j=l),\\
\frac{1}{d(d+2)}&(i=k\textrm{ and }j\neq l,\textrm{ or }i\neq k\textrm{ and }j=l),\\
\frac{d+1}{d(d-1)(d+2)}&(i\neq k\textrm{ and }j\neq l).\end{cases}

(73)

Using eq. (
73
) in eq. (
70
), separating
cases in the sum, we get:

\displaystyle\textrm{Var}[{\rm Tr}[\mathbf{W}\mathbf{W}^{\prime}]]

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{d(d+2)}\cdot d^{2}\cdot\mathbb{E}[\lambda_{1}^{2}]^{2}+\frac{1}{d(d+1)}\cdot 2d^{2}(d-1)\cdot\mathbb{E}[\lambda_{1}^{2}]\mathbb{E}[\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}]

\displaystyle+\frac{d+1}{d(d-1)(d+2)}\cdot d^{2}(d-1)^{2}\cdot\mathbb{E}[\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}]^{2},

\displaystyle=[1+o_{d}(1)]\left(3\mathbb{E}[\lambda_{1}^{2}]^{2}+2d\mathbb{E}[\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}]\mathbb{E}[\lambda_{1}^{2}]+d^{2}\mathbb{E}[\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}]^{2}\right).

(74)

From eqs. (
68
) and (
71
), we have
\mathbb{E}[\lambda_{1}^{2}]\to\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{\kappa}^{\star}}[X^{2}]
as
d\to\infty
.
Furthermore, by Lemma
3.5
,
\mathbb{E}[({\rm Tr}\mathbf{W})^{2}]=\mathrm{Var}[{\rm Tr}\mathbf{W}]=\mathcal{O}(1)
as
d\to\infty
,
so eq. (
72
) gives that
d\mathbb{E}[\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}]\to-\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{\kappa}^{\star}}[X^{2}]
as
d\to\infty
.
Plugging these limits in eq. (
74
) we get:

\displaystyle\textrm{Var}[{\rm Tr}[\mathbf{W}\mathbf{W}^{\prime}]]

\displaystyle=2\left(\int\mu_{\kappa}^{\star}(\mathrm{d}x)\,x^{2}\right)^{2}+o_{d\to\infty}(1).

(75)

Finally, combining eqs. (
66
), (
68
) and (
75
) we obtain (recall
n/d^{2}\to\tau
):

\displaystyle\lim_{d\to\infty}G_{d}^{\prime\prime}(0)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\tau}\left[\frac{1}{2}-\int\mu_{\kappa}^{\star}(\mathrm{d}x)\,x^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left(-\frac{1}{2}\int\mu_{\kappa}^{\star}(\mathrm{d}x)\,x^{2}\right)^{2}\right].

(76)

The integral in eq. (

25
): plugging its value in eq. (
76
)
shows that

4.1
.

Appendix A
Contiguity with a planted model, and freezing of solutions

We discuss briefly here Open Problem
1.3
, more specifically how freezing could be established,
or at least conjectured, from the second moment computation.
For the symmetric binary perceptron (SBP), this was the path followed in
Aubin et al., 2019
, before
freezing was established rigorously in
Perkins & Xu, 2021
;
Abbe et al., 2022
.

Let us first fix some notations.
For the remainder of this section we assume that the margin
\kappa\in(0,2)
is given and fixed.
We denote
\Sigma_{n}\coloneqq\{\pm 1\}^{n}
, and
\mathcal{S}_{d}
the set of symmetric
d\times d
matrices.
For
\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime}\in\Sigma_{n}
, we denote
R(\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime})\coloneqq(1/n)\sum_{i=1}^{n}\varepsilon_{i}\varepsilon^{\prime}_{i}
their overlap.
We let
\mathbf{H}\coloneqq(\mathbf{W}_{1},\cdots,\mathbf{W}_{n})
, and denote
S(\mathbf{H})\coloneqq\{\varepsilon\in\{\pm 1\}^{n}\,\textrm{s.t.}\,\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\varepsilon_{i}\mathbf{W}_{i}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\kappa\sqrt{n}\}
.
Recall that
Z_{\kappa}=|S(\mathbf{H})|
, see eq. (
2
).
In this appendix only we call
\mathbb{P}_{0}
the law of
\mathbf{W}_{1},\cdots,\mathbf{W}_{n}\overset{\mathrm{i.i.d.}}{\sim}\mathrm{GOE}(d)
, which was denoted
\mathbb{P}
in the rest of the manuscript.

Definition A.1

(Freezing)
.

Let
\tau=n/d^{2}=\Theta(1)
be large enough so that
Z_{\kappa}\geq 1
with probability
1-o(1)
as
d\to\infty
(i.e. we are in the SAT phase).
The set
S(\mathbf{H})
of solutions is said to exhibit freezing
if there exists
q_{c}\in(0,1)
such that the following holds:

\displaystyle\plim_{n,d\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log|\{\varepsilon^{\prime}\in S(\mathbf{H})\,:\,R(\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime})\geq q_{c}\}|=0,

where the limit is in probability over
\mathbf{H}
and
\varepsilon\sim\mathrm{Unif}(S(\mathbf{H}))
.

Definition
A.1
captures a relatively weak notion of freezing: for a typical solution
\varepsilon
, the number of solutions with overlap to
\varepsilon
exceeding
q_{c}
is at most sub-exponential in
n
.
In the SBP, one can prove a stronger property, namely that
\varepsilon
is completely isolated and thus the
unique
solution with such overlap.
Below we briefly outline the two main ingredients in the classical approach to proving freezing—as carried out for the SBP—and explain why these steps present difficulties in the context of random matrix discrepancy.
We refer to the literature cited above for more background and references.

Step 1: contiguity –

Freezing of solutions is a property of
\mathrm{Unif}(S(\mathbf{H}))
, the uniform measure over the set of solutions.
The classical approach to establish properties of this measure is to use a contiguity argument with a
planted
version of the problem,
which is often easier to study.
Recall that, for two sequences of probability distributions
\mathbb{P}_{d}
and
\mathbb{Q}_{d}
(that depend on
d
), we say that
\mathbb{P}_{d}
is contiguous to
\mathbb{Q}_{d}
, denoted
\mathbb{P}_{d}\lhd\mathbb{Q}_{d}
,
if for any sequence of measurable events
A_{d}
:

\displaystyle(\mathbb{Q}_{d}(A_{d})\to 0\textrm{ as }d\to\infty)\Rightarrow(\mathbb{P}_{d}(A_{d})\to 0\textrm{ as }d\to\infty).

(77)

In our setting, we define the following two models.

\bullet

Random model
(
\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{ra.}}
): draw first
\mathbf{W}_{1},\cdots,\mathbf{W}_{n}\overset{\mathrm{i.i.d.}}{\sim}\mathrm{GOE}(d)
, conditioned on
S(\mathbf{H})\neq\emptyset
.
Draw then
\varepsilon\sim\mathrm{Unif}(S(\mathbf{H}))
.

\bullet

Planted model
(
\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{pl.}}
): draw first
\varepsilon\sim\mathrm{Unif}(\Sigma_{n})
. Draw then
\mathbf{W}_{1},\cdots,\mathbf{W}_{n}\overset{\mathrm{i.i.d.}}{\sim}\mathrm{GOE}(d)
, conditioned on
satisfying
\|\sum_{i}\varepsilon_{i}\mathbf{W}_{i}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\kappa\sqrt{n}
.

\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{ra.}}
and
\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{pl.}}
define two probability measures over
\mathcal{S}_{d}^{n}\times\Sigma_{n}
. In the planted distribution,
we often denote
\varepsilon=\varepsilon^{\star}
.
In many problems,
\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{pl.}}
turns out to be easier to study than
\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{ra.}}
, and moreover we have the general identity,
for any
(\varepsilon,\mathbf{H})
such that
\varepsilon\in S(\mathbf{H})
:

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{pl.}}}{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{ra.}}}(\varepsilon,\mathbf{H})

\displaystyle=\frac{Z_{\kappa}(\mathbf{H})}{\mathbb{E}_{0}[Z_{\kappa}(\mathbf{H}^{\prime})]}\cdot\mathbb{P}_{0}(S(\mathbf{H}^{\prime})\neq 0).

(78)

When
\mathbb{P}_{0}(S(\mathbf{H})\neq 0)\to 1
as
d\to\infty
, contiguity of
\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{pl.}}
and
\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{ra.}}
thus follows from concentration of
Z_{\kappa}
around its mean.
More generally, suppose that for some non-decreasing sequence
\alpha_{d}>0
there exists
M>0
such that

\displaystyle\lim_{d\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{0}\!\left(\frac{Z_{\kappa}}{\mathbb{E}[Z_{\kappa}]}\geq e^{-M\alpha_{d}}\right)=1.

(79)

Then one can show that any event with probability
\exp(-\omega(\alpha_{d}))
under
\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{pl.}}
must have probability
o(1)
under
\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{ra.}}

(
Perkins & Xu, 2021
)
.
In particular, if
\alpha_{d}=\mathcal{O}(1)
then
\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{pl.}}\lhd\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{ra.}}
.
Unfortunately, combining Theorems
1.6
and
1.8
only implies a bound of the type of eq. (
79
)
for
\alpha_{d}=\Theta(d^{2})
.
Establishing a better concentration bound for
Z_{\kappa}
in average-case matrix discrepancy appears more delicate than in the SBP,
where fluctuations of
Z_{\kappa}
can be characterized exactly
(
Abbe et al., 2022
)
.
It is also unclear whether concentration should hold throughout the entire satisfiable phase, given the failure of the second-moment method in parts of the phase diagram (see Theorem
1.9
).
For these reasons, we leave the question of contiguity open.

Open Question A.1

(Contiguity)
.

For
\kappa\in(0,2)
and
\tau=n/d^{2}=\Theta(1)
sufficiently large (so that
S(\mathbf{H})\neq\emptyset
with high probability), are
\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{ra.}}
and
\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{pl.}}
contiguous as
d\to\infty
?
Can one at least establish a bound of the form (
79
) for some
\alpha_{d}=o(d^{2})
?

Step 2: is the planted solutions isolated ?

For
l\in[n]
, denote
q_{l}\coloneqq 2l/n-1\in[-1,1]
.
A key point is that for any

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{pl.}}\left[\#\{\varepsilon\in S(\mathbf{H})\backslash\{\varepsilon^{\star}\}\,:\,q_{0}\leq R(\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\star})\leq q_{1}\}\right],

(80)

where
in

we used the invariance of the law of
\mathrm{GOE}(d)
, in
(\rm b)

, see Section

.
For a given

.
Assuming contiguity,

(81)

where

Appendix B
Large deviations for the spectral norm of a Gaussian matrix

and which relies only on the
the main result of

\mathrm{GOE}(d)
matrices

5

5

use a convention where

matrices have off-diagonal entries with variance

.
Recall that
\Sigma(\mu)\coloneqq\int\mu(\mathrm{d}x)\mu(\mathrm{d}y)\log|x-y|

Proposition B.1

(
Ben & Guionnet, 1997
)
.

Let

.
We denote

For

Then:

(i)

The last equality follows since

\displaystyle\geq\frac{3}{8}-\frac{\delta^{2}}{4}-\frac{\delta}{2}\int|x|\nu_{\delta}(\mathrm{d}x)-\frac{1}{4}\int x^{2}\nu_{\delta}(\mathrm{d}x)+\frac{1}{2}\Sigma(\nu_{\delta}),

Poisson Statistics for Coulomb Gases at Intermediate Temperature Regimes
David Padilla-Garza, Luke Peilen, Eric Thoma

Abstract
. We consider the microscopic statistics of a Coulomb gas in ℝ 2 \mathbb{R}^{2} at intermediate temperatures. In particular, we show that the microscopic point process associated to the Coulomb gas converges to a homogeneous Poisson point process at intermediate temperature regimes β ​ N → ∞ \beta N\rightarrow\infty and β ​ N ​ log ⁡ N → 0 \beta\sqrt{N}\log N\rightarrow 0 , extending previous results. Our approach relies on a novel quantitative asymptotic description of correlation functions, which is of its own interest.



1.
Introduction

We are interested in the Coulomb gas in
\mathbb{R}^{\mathsf{d}}
, an interacting particle system whose equilibrium statistics are governed by the Gibbs measure at inverse temperature
\beta

(1.1)

\mathbb{P}_{N,\beta}(dX_{N})=\frac{1}{\mathsf{Z}_{N,\beta}}e^{-\beta\mathcal{H}_{N}(X_{N})}\penalty\ dX_{N}

where
X_{N}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{N})\subset(\mathbb{R}^{\mathsf{d}})^{N}
denotes the particle configuration and
\mathcal{H}_{N}
is an interaction Hamiltonian given by

(1.2)

\mathcal{H}_{N}(X_{N})=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\neq j}\mathsf{g}(x_{i}-x_{j})+N\sum_{i=1}^{N}V(x_{i}).

V:\mathbb{R}^{\mathsf{d}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is an external confinement potential, assumed to grow rapidly enough at infinity so that the particles remain asymptotically confined to a compact subset of
\mathbb{R}^{\mathsf{d}}
with overwhelming probability. The kernel
\mathsf{g}
we consider is the Coulomb kernel

(1.3)

\mathsf{g}(x)=\begin{cases}-\log|x|&\text{if }\mathsf{d}=2,\\
|x|^{2-\mathsf{d}}&\text{if }\mathsf{d}\geq 3.\end{cases}

There is also interest in studying more general Riesz gases, where the interaction power is allowed to vary between
0
and
\mathsf{d}
:

(1.4)

\mathsf{g}(x):=\mathsf{g}_{\mathsf{s}}(x)=\begin{cases}|x|^{-s}&\text{if }0<\mathsf{s}<\mathsf{d},\\
-\log|x|&\text{if }\mathsf{s}=0.\end{cases}

The reason for taking
\mathsf{s}<\mathsf{d}
is to preserve local integrability of the kernel at the origin.
In the Coulomb case
\mathsf{g}
satisfies
-\Delta\mathsf{g}=\mathsf{c}_{\mathsf{d}}\delta_{0},
unlocking access to a trove of elliptic techniques.

For the Riesz gases, one has a similar relationship with a fractional operator, namely
(-\Delta)^{\frac{\mathsf{d}-\mathsf{s}}{2}}\mathsf{g}=\mathsf{c}_{\mathsf{d},\mathsf{s}}\delta_{0}
in the sense of distributions. The
superCoulombic
Riesz gas, for
\mathsf{d}-2<\mathsf{s}<\mathsf{d}
is then particularly tractable. The fractional Laplacian with power
0<\alpha<1
has an interpretation as a (possibly degenerate) elliptic operator in
\mathbb{R}^{\mathsf{d}+1}
via the Caffarelli-Silvestre extension procedure (see
[
CS07
]
) When
\mathsf{s}<\mathsf{d}-2
(the
subCoulombic
Riesz gas) the fractional operator is extremely nonlocal and the analysis of the gas is rather different.

In this article, we will focus on the Coulomb gas, where the superharmonicity of the kernel
\mathsf{g}
plays a key role. We will work mainly in dimension
\mathsf{d}=2
. We will also focus on the temperature regime
\frac{1}{N}\ll\beta\ll 1
, which we call the
intermediate
temperature regime.

1.1.
Splitting and the Thermal Equilibrium Measure

From
[
Fro35
]
(see also
[
ST97
]
), if one assumes that the potential
V
is everywhere finite, lower semicontinuous, and grows faster than the logarithm at infinity, i.e.

(1.5)

\lim_{|x|\rightarrow\infty}V(x)+\mathsf{g}(x)=+\infty

then the mean-field energy

(1.6)

\mathcal{E}_{V}(\mu):=\mathcal{E}(\mu)+\int V(x)\penalty\ \mu(dx):=\frac{1}{2}\iint\mathsf{g}(x-y)\penalty\ \mu(dx)\mu(dy)+\int V(x)\penalty\ \mu(dx)

has a compactly supported minimizer among probability measures known as the
equilibrium measure
, which we denote
\mu_{V}
.
The equilibrium measure satisfies the Euler-Lagrange equation

(1.7)

\begin{cases}\mathsf{h}^{\mu_{V}}+V&\geq c_{V}\quad\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{d}\\
\mathsf{h}^{\mu_{V}}+V&=c_{V}\quad\text{ in }\mathrm{supp}\left(\mu_{V}\right)\end{cases}

for some constant
c_{V}
, where
\mathsf{h}^{\nu}
is the Coulomb potential

(1.8)

\mathsf{h}^{\nu}(x):=\int\mathsf{g}(x-y)\penalty\ \nu(dy).

The equilibrium measure is a good approximation of the
empirical measure
in the sense that

\mathsf{emp}_{N}:=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{x_{i}}\rightarrow\mu_{V}

in a large deviations sense so long as
V
is lower semicontinuous and
\beta\gg\frac{1}{N}
(see for instance
[
GZ19
]
). At temperatures
\beta\simeq\frac{1}{N}
the gas undergoes thermalization, and this equilibrium measure is no longer an adequate approximation. Entropic effects appear at leading order, and particle locations are no longer strongly confined to a compact set; instead, one needs to consider a
thermal equilibrium measure

\mu_{\theta}
minimizing

(1.9)

\mathcal{E}_{\theta}(\mu):=\mathcal{E}_{V}(\mu)+\frac{1}{\theta}\int\mu\log\mu

with
\theta:=\beta N
. This minimizer exists (see
[
Lam21b
]
) and satisfies the Euler-Lagrange equation

(1.10)

\mathsf{h}^{\mu_{\theta}}+V+\frac{1}{\theta}\log\mu_{\theta}=c_{\theta}

for some constant
c_{\theta}
. Furthermore, one has a law of large numbers (see
[
GZ19
]
) for the empirical measures

\mathsf{emp}_{N}\rightarrow\mu_{\theta}

in a large deviations sense when
\beta N\to\theta
as
N\to\infty
. Indeed, the authors in
[
AS21
]
argue that
\mu_{\theta}
is a more precise approximation of
\mu_{V}
at all temperature regimes. Concentration of measure is studied in
[
PG23
]
and quantiative approximation of
\mu_{V}
by
\mu_{\theta}
at temperatures
\beta\gg\frac{1}{N}
is established in
[
AS22
]
via connections between the equilibrium problem and the classical obstacle problem (see
[
Ser24
]
and
[
Caf98
]
).

Given these deterministic first-order asymptotics, it is natural to “split” off the deterministic first-order limit when considering finer statistics of the gas. In particular, one has the so-called “splitting formula” (see
[
Ser24
, Lemma 5.2]
, introduced in
[
SS12
]
)

(1.11)

\mathcal{H}_{N}(X_{N})=N^{2}\mathcal{E}_{\theta}(\mu_{\theta})-\frac{N}{\theta}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\log\mu_{\theta}(x_{i})+N^{2}\mathsf{F}_{N}(X_{N},\mu_{\theta}),

where
\mathsf{F}_{N}
is a next-order energy given by

(1.12)

\mathsf{F}_{N}(X_{N},\mu)=\frac{1}{2}\iint_{\Delta^{c}}\mathsf{g}(x-y)\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{x_{i}}-\mu\right)^{\otimes 2}(dx,dy),

which can be thought of as the energy of a “jellium” consisting of positive point charges and a neutralizing, negatively charged background measure
\mu
. The set
\Delta=\{x=y\}\subset(\mathbb{R}^{\mathsf{d}})^{2}
above denotes the diagonal, which is removed to neglect the formally infinite self-energy of a point charge. Using (
1.11
), one can equivalently write the Gibbs measure (
1.1
) as

(1.13)

\mathbb{P}_{N,\beta}(dX_{N})=\frac{1}{\mathsf{K}_{N,\beta}}e^{-\beta N^{2}\mathsf{F}_{N}(X_{N},\mu_{\theta})}\mu_{\theta}^{\otimes N}(dX_{N})

where
\theta=\beta N
, and
\mathsf{K}_{N,\beta}
is the next-order partition function

(1.14)

\mathsf{K}_{N,\beta}(X_{N}):=\int e^{-\beta N^{2}\mathsf{F}_{N}(X_{N},\mu_{\theta})}\mu_{\theta}^{\otimes N}(dX_{N}).

We take (
1.13
) as our preferred representation of the Coulomb gas in the remainder of this paper. The next-order partition function is conveniently bounded below, which we state here for reference.

Proposition 1.1
.

(
[
PG23
, Prop. 5.10]
)
Let
\mathsf{K}_{N,\beta}
be as in (
1.14
). Then
\mathsf{K}_{N,\beta}\geq 1
.

1.2.
Main results

Our main goal in this paper is to better understand the microscopic behavior of the gas. A useful means of examining this behavior is by way of the
microscopic point process
near a point
\overline{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathsf{d}}
, which is defined by

(1.15)

Q_{\overline{z},N}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{N^{1/\mathsf{d}}(x_{i}-\overline{z})}.

Effectively,
Q_{\overline{z},N}
centers the configuration around
\overline{z}
and then blows up the distance between particles so that the typical interparticle distance (previously
\simeq N^{-1/\mathsf{d}}
) is now order one. We will study the convergence as
N\to\infty
of
Q_{\overline{z},N}
in the weak topology on point processes, Definition
3.1
,
generated by integration against continuous, compactly supported functions on
\mathbb{R}^{\mathsf{d}}
.

In general, the process (
1.15
) is hard to study due to the long-range nature of the Coulomb energy. The authors in
[
LS15
]
studied the push-forward of
Q_{\overline{z},N}
under an averaging operator and identified a critical temperature scale,
\beta\simeq N^{\frac{2}{\mathsf{d}}-1}

(\beta\simeq N^{-\frac{\mathsf{s}}{\mathsf{d}}}
for Riesz interactions with kernels
\mathsf{g}_{\mathsf{s}}:=|x|^{-\mathsf{s}}
) at which the averaged point process converges as
N\rightarrow\infty
to a point process that minimizes a free energy with competing energy and entropic terms. For the
\mathsf{d}=1
log-gas this process is the celebrated
\mathrm{Sine}_{\beta}
point process, whose convergence has been well-studied (cf.
[
VV09
]
,
[
KS09
]
). For the Coulomb gas in
\mathsf{d}=2
, this corresponds to the Ginibre point process at
\beta=2
, but is otherwise not identified. At regimes
\beta\gg N^{\frac{2}{\mathsf{d}}-1}
one would correspondingly expect the energy term to dominate and see particles concentrate around energy minimizers, and as
\beta\downarrow 0
faster than
N^{\frac{2}{\mathsf{d}}-1}
one would expect to see entropic behavior dominate. This corresponds to a kind of complete independence of the particles, and convergence to a Poisson point process (see
\lx@sectionsign 3
for a more thorough discussion of this process). Indeed, this regime was studied in
[
Leb16
]
, where the author established convergence of this averaged point process to a Poisson point process.

In the high temperature regime
\beta\simeq\frac{1}{N}
where the strength of the interaction is weaker and particles are no longer confined to a compact region in
\mathbb{R}^{\mathsf{d}}
, the analysis is a bit more tractable and the corresponding entropic domination has been well-studied. In particular, the authors in
[
Lam21b
]
have shown convergence of the local point process
Q_{\overline{z},N}
to a Poisson point process for a very general collection of interacting particle systems that include Coulomb and Riesz gases, generalizing work for the
\beta
-ensembles initiated in
[
BGP15
]
and
[
NT20
]
.

The behavior of the local point process at intermediate temperature regimes
N^{-1}\ll\beta\ll N^{-\frac{\mathsf{s}}{\mathsf{d}}}
for the Riesz gas is largely unknown, outside of the case of the Gaussian
\beta
-ensembles (the
1-\mathsf{d}
log gas with quadratic confinement potential) studied in
[
BGP15
]
. Our main result begins to fill in this gap in the literature for the
\mathsf{d}=2
Coulomb gas.

We will consider
\beta=\beta_{N}
as a sequence dependent on
N
and let
\theta_{\ast}=\inf_{N}N\beta_{N}
. Throughout the paper, we will assume that
\mathsf{d}=2
,
\theta_{\ast}>0
, and

(Conf)

\displaystyle\int_{|x|\geq 1}e^{-\frac{\theta_{\ast}}{2}(V(x)+\mathsf{g}(x))}\penalty\ dx+\int_{|x|\geq 1}e^{-\theta_{\ast}(V(x)+\mathsf{g}(x))}|x|\log^{2}|x|\penalty\ dx<+\infty,

\displaystyle V\in C^{2}\quad\text{and}\quad\Delta V\geq\mathsf{c}>0\quad\text{in a neighborhood of }\mathrm{supp}(\mu_{V}),

\displaystyle\lim_{|x|\to\infty}V(x)-\log|x|=+\infty.

For convenience, we will sometimes assume stronger growth of
V
at
\infty
and an upper bound for the Laplacian of
V
, namely

(Grow)

\displaystyle V(x)\geq|x|^{\frac{2}{99}}\quad\forall|x|\geq C,

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}\Delta V(x)\leq C.

Both assumptions may be weakened at the cost of more involved error terms.
We will also, for our main results but not every intermediate result, assume that the temperature is sufficiently high. Precisely, we assume

(Temp)

\beta_{N}=o\left(\frac{1}{N^{1/2}\log N}\right)\quad\text{as}\quad N\to\infty.

We allow implicit constants throughout to depend on the parameters within (
Conf
), (
Grow
), and (
Temp
). We will also implicitly assume
N
is large relative to these parameters.

Theorem 1
.

Assume (
Temp
) and (
Grow
), and let
\overline{z}\in\mathrm{int}(\mathrm{supp}(\mu_{V}))
. Then the local point process
Q_{\overline{z},N}
under
\mathbb{P}_{N,\beta}
converges weakly to a Poisson point process with intensity
\mu_{V}(\overline{z})\mathrm{Leb}
for the Lebesgue measure
\mathrm{Leb}
on
\mathbb{R}^{\mathsf{d}}
.

We remind the reader of the definition of the Poisson point process and weak convergence of point processes in Section
3
. Assumption (
Conf
) allows us to access the confinement bound

(1.16)

\mathbb{P}(\exists\penalty\ |x_{i}|>L)\leq CN\int_{|x|>L}e^{-\beta N\zeta(x)}\penalty\ dx

to obtain sufficient localization, where
\zeta=\mathsf{h}^{\mu_{V}}+V\geq V-\log|x|-C
, as established in
[
Tho25
, Theorem 3]
. Our assumptions on
V
also guarantee that we can make use of the quantitative approximation of
\mu_{V}
by
\mu_{\theta}
, established in
[
AS22
, Theorem 1]
, which in particular implies

(1.17)

\lim_{\theta\rightarrow\infty}\mu_{\theta}(x)=\mu_{V}(x)

for
x\in\mathrm{int}(\mathrm{supp}(\mu_{V}))
.

Remark 1.2
.

Assumptions (
Grow
) may be weakened with some added technical difficulty. It is particularly simple, for example, to weaken (
Grow
) to
V(x)\geq|x|^{\gamma}
for some
\gamma>0
for large
|x|
at the sole cost of changing certain implicit constants
C
. It is also clear that Theorem
1
remains true after modifying the potential far from the origin so long as
\beta\geq C^{-1}N\log N
, since with high probability all particles lie in a fixed compact set and one can therefore modify the potential outside this set without significantly changing microscopic statistics.

We establish Theorem
1
by a quantitative comparison of the correlation functions for
Q_{\overline{z},N}
to those of a Poisson point process of intensity
\mu_{V}(\overline{z})
; see Proposition
3.7
below. This in particular allows us to estimate various microscopic statistics of
\mathbb{P}_{N,\beta}
at finite
N
.

Corollary 1.3
.

Assume (
Temp
). Let
x\in\mathrm{int}(\mathrm{supp}(\mu_{V}))
and let
\rho_{1}(x)dx
denote the first marginal
\mathbb{P}_{N,\beta}(x_{1}\in dx)
. Then for any
\gamma>0
, we have

(1.18)

\rho_{1}(x)=\mu_{\theta}(x)\left(1+O\left(\beta N^{\frac{1+\gamma}{2}}\right)\right)

as
N\to\infty
. In particular, applying (
1.18
) and assuming in addition that
\beta\leq N^{-\frac{1}{2}-\alpha}
for some
\alpha>0
and
N
large enough, we have for any open set
U
containing
\mathrm{supp}(\mu_{V})
that

(1.19)

\mathbb{P}_{N,\beta}(\exists\penalty\ x_{i}\notin U)\leq CN\mu_{\theta}(U^{c})\leq Ce^{-c\beta N\min\left(\mathrm{dist}(U^{c},\mathrm{supp}(\mu_{V}))^{2},1\right)},

for some
c>0
.

We note that (
1.19
) is proved for general
\beta
and in a form effective on microscopic scales in
[
Tho25
]
. Our proof is however essentially independent of
[
Tho25
]
(we only use (
1.16
) for
L=N^{99}
and alternative bounds would suffice). The second inequality in (
1.19
) is a consequence of our assumptions on the potential and estimates on
\mu_{\theta}
, see for instance (
[
AS22
, Lemma 3.5]
).

A key technical input is a novel concentration estimate for the potential generated by fluctuations

(1.20)

\mathrm{fluct}_{N}:=\mathsf{emp}_{N}-\mu_{\theta}

which may be of interest in its own right.

Proposition 1.4
.

Assume
\beta\leq 1
and (
Grow
).
We have

(1.21)

\mathbb{P}_{N,\beta}\left(\left|\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathsf{h}^{\mathrm{fluct}}(y_{i})\right|\geq kTN^{-1/2}\right)\leq 4k\left(e^{-\frac{1}{2}TN^{1/2}}+e^{-\beta TN}\right),

for any
T\geq C\log N
for a large enough
C>0
, uniformly in points
y_{1},\ldots,y_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Here
N
is restricted to be large uniformly over selections of
y_{1},\ldots,y_{k}
in any fixed compact set.

1.3.
Comparison with Literature

As discussed above, a seminal study of the microscopic behavior of Coulomb and Riesz gases was undertaken by the authors in
[
LS15
]
, where they studied a push-forward of
Q_{\overline{z},N}
under a certain averaging operator. In that work, they identify a critical temperature scale
\beta\simeq N^{-\frac{\mathsf{s}}{\mathsf{d}}}
for Riesz gases at which the local behavior of the gas minimizes a competition between a renormalized energy term and an entropic term, phrased as a relative entropy of point processes with respect to a Poisson point process. In the
\mathsf{s}=0
case, corresponding to the logarithmic interactions, this corresponds to constant temperature. For the one-dimensional log gas, this minimizer is unique (
[
EHL21
]
) and is well known to be the
\mathrm{Sine}_{\beta}
point process (cf
[
VV09
]
and
[
KS09
]
) in the bulk and the
\mathrm{Airy}_{\beta}
point process
[
RRV11
]
at the edge. In the two-dimensional case with
V(x)=|x|^{2}
and
\beta=2
, this is the Ginibre point process.

The regime
\beta\simeq N^{-1}
, so-called
high temperature regime
, has seen a lot of attention in recent years. In particular, it can be seen as a crossover between the strong interaction we see at constant
\beta
and the independent
\beta=0
behavior (see
[
ABG12
]
and
[
AB19
]
) and we see convergence of the empirical measures
\mathsf{emp}_{N}
to a thermal equilibrium measure
\mu_{\theta}
discussed above. The behavior of
Q_{\overline{z},N}
at this temperature regime has been well-studied in the one-dimensional logarithmic case (
[
BGP15
]
and
[
NT20
]
) and for general Riesz interactions in
[
Lam21b
]
. Given the above discussion, one expects entropic effects to dominate for any
\beta\downarrow 0
and to recover convergence to a Poisson point process (see
[
AD14
]
); for the Gaussian
\beta
-ensembles, this was studied in
[
BGP15
]
.

Recently, in
[
Tho25
]
, weak convergence of
Q_{\overline{z},N}
to a homogeneous
mixed
Poisson process was proved so long as
\beta_{N}\to 0
as
N\to\infty
at any speed (modulo a subsequence). The argument uses isotropic averaging (to prove that the
k
-point correlation functions are asymptotically subharmonic in any dimension) followed by Liouville’s theorem (to show that the
k
-point correlation functions are therefore constant at
N=\infty
in
\mathsf{d}=2
). Identifying the limiting process as a Poisson process of explicit intensity, rather than a mixed Poisson process, appears to be beyond the reach of the “soft” argument based on Liouville’s theorem.

Our approach is motivated by ideas from
[
BGP15
]
,
[
NT20
]
and
[
Lam21b
]
, where the authors consider convergence of the correlation functions of the point process. We are able to extend the temperature regime for the Coulomb gas to
\beta\ll N^{-\frac{1}{2}-\alpha}
for
\alpha>0
by a quantitative description of this convergence, as we undertook for weakly interacting particle systems in
[
PGPT24
]
. A key technical input is a concentration estimate for the Coulomb potential generated by fluctuations of linear statistics, Proposition
2.5
. This is a challenging estimate to obtain for the Coulomb gas due to the singular nature of the logarithm as a test function. Our approach relies on techniques for studying concentration developed in
[
CHM18
]
and
[
GZPG24
]
, coupled with a regularization of the kernel.

Fluctuations of linear statistics are interesting in their own right, and have seen a lot of attention both for the one-dimensional log gas (
[
Joh98
]
,
[
BG13
]
,
[
BG22
]
,
[
BL18
]
,
[
BLS18
]
,
[
Lam21a
]
,
[
Pei24
]
and others) and the two-dimensional Coulomb gas (
[
RV07
]
,
[
LS18
]
,
[
BBNY19
]
and
[
Ser23
]
). Results for singular test functions such as the logarithm are more sparse, but have been studied in
[
BMP22
]
for the one-dimensional log gas and
[
Bou23
]
for the Riesz gas on the periodic torus. A key technique that allows us to go from regularized statistics to the logarithm are overcrowding estimates recently developed for general
\beta
in
[
Tho25
]
. These rely on the powerful tool of isotropic averaging, developed in
[
Tho24
]
and
[
Leb17
]
, which are specific to the Coulomb gas.

The study of fluctuations of linear statistics for the logarithm are interesting, not just for studying the local point process of the gas, but also for the field
\sqrt{\beta}\mathsf{h}^{\mathrm{fluct}}
itself, which is expected to behave like a log-correlated field. In the case of the
\beta
-ensembles and random normal matrices, this corresponds to the log-characteristic polynomial of the matrix ensemble. The maximum of log-correlated fields is well studied (
[
DRZ17
]
), and one expects

\max\sqrt{\beta}\mathsf{h}^{\mathrm{fluct}}=\log N-\frac{3}{4}\log\log N+Z_{N}

as
N\rightarrow\infty
, where
Z_{N}
is a shifted Gumbel. This conjecture for the log-characteristic polynomial is the Fyodorov-Hiary-Keating conjecture (
[
FHK12
]
). Progress has recently been made on understanding the first-order asymptotics for the Coulomb gas in
[
LLZ24
]
and
[
Pei25
]
and the one-dimensional log gas in
[
BLZ23
]
, but the remaining asymptotics are largely open.

2.
Concentration for Fluctuations of the Logarithmic Potential

In this section we establish Proposition
1.4
. The approach is similar in spirit to
[
PGPT24
]
, with some technical modifications due to the lack of integrability of the Fourier transform of the kernel
\mathsf{g}
. To circumvent this issue, we smear our point charges by replacing point masses
\delta_{x_{i}}
with smoothed versions
\delta_{x_{i}}^{\eta}
, uniform measures on the boundary of the ball of radius
\eta>0
centered at
x_{i}
. This regularization procedure, first introduced for the Coulomb gas in
[
SS15
]
(see
[
Ser24
, Chapter 4]
for a discussion) has been used extensively in the study of Coulomb gases to regularize the kernel
\mathsf{g}
.

Our approach is slightly different than previous works, in that we will regularize both the point charges and the background measure
\mu_{\theta}
. More specifically, letting
\phi_{\eta}
denote the uniform measure on the boundary of the ball of radius
\eta
centered at zero, we will consider

\mathrm{fluct}\ast\phi_{\eta}:=\left(\mathsf{emp}_{N}-\mu_{\theta}\right)\ast\phi_{\eta}

instead of just the point regularization
\mathsf{emp}_{N}\ast\phi_{\eta}-\mu_{\theta}
. This modification allows us to argue use Fourier transform representations to argue that
\mathcal{E}(\mathrm{fluct}\ast\phi_{\eta})
is large whenever
\mathsf{h}^{\mathrm{fluct}\ast\phi_{\eta}}
is large at a fixed point, where
\mathcal{E}
is as in (
1.6
). We will argue as well that
\mathcal{E}(\mathrm{fluct}\ast\phi_{\eta})
is a near lower bound for for
\mathsf{F}_{N}(X_{N},\mu_{\theta})
, up to certain “renormalization” errors, which will then allow us to obtain concentration for the smoothed potential
\mathsf{h}^{\mathrm{fluct}\ast\phi_{\eta}}
via estimates inspired by those in
[
CHM18
]
. Finally, we show that
\mathsf{h}^{\mathrm{fluct}\ast\phi_{\eta}}
is pointwise close to
\mathsf{h}^{\mathrm{fluct}}
in exponential moments so long as
\eta
is sufficiently small using isotropic averaging techniques.

2.1.
Lower bounds for the energy

The key to this concentration estimate is that measures with a large Coulomb potential at a given point have a correspondingly large Coulomb energy. We will use in our argument that the Fourier transform of the surface probability measure on the
(\mathsf{d}-1)
-sphere is explicitly computable, namely

(2.1)

\hat{\phi}_{1}(\xi)=\|\xi\|^{1-\frac{\mathsf{d}}{2}}J_{\frac{\mathsf{d}}{2}-1}(\|\xi\|)

where
J_{a}
is a Bessel function of the first kind (see
[
SW71
, p. 154]
). Importantly, these functions are bounded for real arguments, and have asymptotic behavior (
[
AS74
, p. 364]
)

|J_{a}(r)|\lesssim\frac{1}{\sqrt{r}}

as
r\rightarrow\infty
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\phi_{\eta}
denote the uniform measure on the boundary of the ball of radius
\eta
centered at zero with total mass
1
. For any
\varepsilon,\delta,\eta>0
, let
\mathcal{D}_{\varepsilon,\delta,\eta}

denote the following set of (signed) measures:

\mathcal{D}_{\varepsilon,\delta,\eta}:=\left\{\nu\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\ :\ \nu(\mathbb{R}^{2})=0,\int|x||\nu|(dx)\leq\delta^{-1},|\mathsf{h}^{\nu\ast\phi_{\eta}}(0)|\geq\varepsilon\right\}.

Then

(2.2)

\inf_{\nu\in\mathcal{D}_{\varepsilon,\delta,\eta}}\mathcal{E}(\nu)\geq\frac{\mathsf{c}_{0}\varepsilon^{2}}{1+\log\varepsilon^{-1}+\log\delta^{-1}+\log\eta^{-1}}

for some
\mathsf{c}_{0}>0
.

Proof.

Suppose
\nu\in\mathcal{D}_{\varepsilon,\delta,\eta}
. Since
\nu/\varepsilon\in\mathcal{D}_{1,\varepsilon\delta,\eta}
and
\mathcal{E}(\nu/\varepsilon)=\varepsilon^{-2}\mathcal{E}(\nu)
, we may assume without loss of generality that
\varepsilon=1
. Note that we have the representations

(2.3)

\begin{split}\mathsf{h}^{\nu\ast\phi_{\eta}}(0)&=\int\hat{\mathsf{g}}(\xi)\hat{\nu}(\xi)\hat{\phi}_{\eta}(\xi)d\xi,\\
\mathcal{E}(\nu)&=\frac{1}{2}\int\hat{\mathsf{g}}(\xi)|\hat{\nu}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi,\end{split}

where the above should be understood at first in a principle value sense. We would like to argue via Cauchy-Schwarz that

1\leq|\mathsf{h}^{\nu\ast\phi_{\eta}}(0)|\leq\left(\int\hat{\mathsf{g}}(\xi)|\hat{\phi}_{\eta}(\xi)|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\sqrt{2\mathcal{E}(\nu)},

except the integral on the RHS is infinite due to the non-integrability of
\hat{\mathsf{g}}
at
0
. So, we will first apply a cutoff at the scale
r=C^{-1}\delta
for a sufficiently large constant
C
. Since
\nu(\mathbb{R}^{2})=0
and
\nu
has a first moment bounded by
\delta^{-1}
, we can estimate

|\hat{\nu}(\xi)|=C\left|\int\left(e^{-2\pi ix\cdot\xi}-1\right)\nu(x)\penalty\ dx\right|\leq C|\xi|\int|x||\nu(x)|\penalty\ dx\leq C\delta^{-1}|\xi|,

and therefore, for
B_{r}
a ball of radius
r>0
centered at zero, we have

(2.4)

\left|\int_{B_{r}}\hat{\mathsf{g}}(\xi)\hat{\nu}(\xi)\hat{\phi}_{\eta}(\xi)d\xi\right|\leq C\delta^{-1}\int_{B_{r}}|\xi||\hat{\mathsf{g}}(\xi)||\hat{\phi}_{\eta}(\xi)|d\xi\leq C\delta^{-1}\int_{B_{r}}\frac{1}{|\xi|}d\xi\leq C\delta^{-1}r\leq\frac{1}{2}.

Here we used that
|\hat{\phi}_{\eta}|\leq 1
and
\hat{\mathsf{g}}(\xi)=\frac{1}{2\pi|\xi|^{2}}
.

Away from this truncation we can proceed as before. By Cauchy-Schwarz, we may bound

\left|\int_{B_{r}^{c}}\hat{\mathsf{g}}(\xi)\hat{\nu}(\xi)\hat{\phi}_{\eta}(\xi)d\xi\right|\leq\left(\int_{B_{r}^{c}}\hat{\mathsf{g}}(\xi)|\hat{\phi}_{\eta}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi\right)^{1/2}\sqrt{2\mathcal{E}(\nu)}.

Looking at the integral on the RHS, one has for the intermediate frequencies that

\int_{B_{\eta^{-1}}\cap B_{r}^{c}}\hat{\mathsf{g}}(\xi)|\hat{\phi}_{\eta}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi\leq C\int_{B_{\eta^{-1}}\cap B_{r}^{c}}\frac{1}{|\xi|^{2}}d\xi\leq C\left(1+\log\eta^{-1}+\log\delta^{-1}\right),

and for the high frequencies one has (via the substitution
u=\eta\xi
)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\eta^{-1}}^{c}}\hat{\mathsf{g}}(\xi)|\hat{\phi}_{\eta}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{B_{\eta^{-1}}^{c}}\frac{|\hat{\phi}_{1}(\eta\xi)|^{2}}{|\xi|^{2}}d\xi

\displaystyle=C\int_{B_{1}^{c}}\frac{|\hat{\phi}_{1}(u)|^{2}}{|u|^{2}}du

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{B_{1}^{c}}\frac{1}{|u|^{3}}\penalty\ du

\displaystyle\leq C,

making use of (
2.1
) and the corresponding Bessel function asymptotics. Combining with (
2.4
), we have proved

1\leq\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\hat{\mathsf{g}}(\xi)\hat{\nu}(\xi)\hat{\phi}_{\eta}(\xi)d\xi\right|\leq C(1+\log\eta^{-1}+\log\delta^{-1})^{1/2}\sqrt{\mathcal{E}(\nu)}+\frac{1}{2}.

The lemma follows by rearranging the above inequality.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

One can prove that in
\mathsf{d}\geq 2
we have

(2.5)

\inf_{\nu\in\mathcal{D}_{\varepsilon,\delta,\eta}}\mathcal{E}(\nu)\geq\frac{\mathsf{c}_{0}\varepsilon^{2}\eta^{\mathsf{d}-2}}{1+\log\varepsilon^{-1}+\log\delta^{-1}+\log\eta^{-1}}.

Notice that the RHS degenerates more rapidly for
\eta\downarrow 0
in
\mathsf{d}\geq 3
than in
\mathsf{d}=2
. This is the main reason that Theorem
1
is restricted to
\mathsf{d}=2
.

2.2.
Approximation

This energy
\mathcal{E}
can be used to obtain lower bounds for the next-order energy
\mathsf{F}_{N}
via the following renormalization bound. We consider general
\mathsf{d}\geq 2
since the proof is identical.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\mathrm{fluct}=\mathsf{emp}_{N}-\mu_{\theta}
, and let
\phi_{\eta}
be the uniform probability measure on the boundary of the ball of radius
\eta
. Then,

(2.6)

\mathcal{E}((\mathsf{emp}_{N}-\mu_{\theta})\ast\phi_{\eta})-\mathsf{F}(X_{N},\mu_{\theta})\leq\frac{\mathsf{g}(\eta)}{2N}+C\left(\|\mu_{\theta}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathsf{d}})}+\|\mu_{\theta}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathsf{d}})}^{2}\right)\eta^{2}.

Proof.

We will bound the two quantities

\mathcal{E}(\mathsf{emp}_{N}\ast\phi_{\eta}-\mu_{\theta})-\mathsf{F}(X_{N},\mu_{\theta})\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{E}((\mathsf{emp}_{N}-\mu_{\theta})\ast\phi_{\eta})-\mathcal{E}(\mathsf{emp}_{N}\ast\phi_{\eta}-\mu_{\theta})

separately.

Step 1.
Estimate for
\mathcal{E}(\mathsf{emp}_{N}\ast\phi_{\eta}-\mu_{\theta})-\mathsf{F}(X_{N},\mu_{\theta})
.

First, we compute

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(\phi_{\eta}\ast\mathsf{emp}_{N}-\mu_{\theta})-\mathsf{F}_{N}(X_{N},\mu_{\theta})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\iint\mathsf{g}(x-y)(\mathsf{emp}_{N}-\phi_{\eta}\ast\mathsf{emp}_{N})(dx)\mu_{\theta}(dy)

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ +\frac{1}{2N^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\iint\mathsf{g}(x-y)\penalty\ \left(\phi_{\eta}\ast\delta_{x_{i}}\right)^{\otimes 2}(dx,dy)

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ +\frac{1}{2N^{2}}\sum_{i\neq j}\left(\iint\mathsf{g}(x-y)\penalty\ \left(\phi_{\eta}\ast\delta_{x_{i}}\right)(dx)\left(\phi_{\eta}\ast\delta_{x_{j}}\right)(dy)-\mathsf{g}(x_{i}-x_{j})\right)

and standard superharmonicity arguments tell us that the last term is negative and the middle term is explicitly computable (see
[
Ser24
, §4.1]
), yielding

\mathcal{E}(\phi_{\eta}\ast\mathsf{emp}_{N}-\mu_{\theta})-\mathsf{F}_{N}(X_{N},\mu_{\theta})\leq\frac{1}{2N}\mathsf{g}(\eta)+\iint\mathsf{g}(x-y)(\mathsf{emp}_{N}-\phi_{\eta}\ast\mathsf{emp}_{N})(dx)\mu_{\theta}(dy).

By associativity of convolution we have

\iint\mathsf{g}(x-y)(\mathsf{emp}_{N}-\phi_{\eta}\ast\mathsf{emp}_{N})(dx)\mu_{\theta}(dy)=\iint(\mathsf{g}-\phi_{\eta}\ast\mathsf{g})(x-y)\mathsf{emp}_{N}(dx)\mu_{\theta}(dy).

By Young’s convolution inequality,

(2.7)

\begin{split}\iint(\mathsf{g}-\phi_{\eta}\ast\mathsf{g})(x-y)\mathsf{emp}_{N}(dx)\mu_{\theta}(dy)&\leq\|(\mathsf{g}-\phi_{\eta}\ast\mathsf{g})\ast\mu_{\theta}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathsf{d}})}\\
&\leq\|\mathsf{g}-\phi_{\eta}\ast\mathsf{g}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathsf{d}})}\|\mu_{\theta}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathsf{d}})}\\
&\leq C\|\mu_{\theta}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathsf{d}})}\eta^{2}\end{split}

where we have used that
\mathsf{g}-\mathsf{g}\ast\phi_{\eta}
has
L^{1}
norm of order
\eta^{2}
(
[
Ser24
, Lemma 4.9]
).

Step 2.
Estimate for
\mathcal{E}((\mathsf{emp}_{N}-\mu_{\theta})\ast\phi_{\eta})-\mathcal{E}(\mathsf{emp}_{N}\ast\phi_{\eta}-\mu_{\theta})
.

We start by writing

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}((\mathsf{emp}_{N}-\mu_{\theta})\ast\phi_{\eta})-\mathcal{E}(\mathsf{emp}_{N}\ast\phi_{\eta}-\mu_{\theta})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\iint\mathsf{g}(x-y)(\phi_{\eta}\ast\mathsf{emp}_{N}-\mu_{\theta})(dx)(\mu_{\theta}-\phi_{\eta}\ast\mu_{\theta})(dy)+\mathcal{E}(\mu_{\theta}-\phi_{\eta}\ast\mu_{\theta}).

The first term on the right hand side is equal by associativity of convolution to

\iint(\mathsf{g}-\phi_{\eta}\ast\mathsf{g})(x-y)(\phi_{\eta}\ast\mathsf{emp}_{N}-\mu_{\theta})(dx)\mu_{\theta}(dy)

which is bounded by
C\eta^{2}\|\mu_{\theta}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}
in the same way as (
2.7
). The second term on the right hand side can also be written as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\iint\mathsf{g}(x-y)\left(\mu_{\theta}-\mu_{\theta}\ast\phi_{\eta}\right)(dy)\left(\mu_{\theta}-\mu_{\theta}\ast\phi_{\eta}\right)(dx)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\iint(\mathsf{g}-\phi_{\eta}\ast\mathsf{g})(x-y)\mu_{\theta}(dy)\left(\mu_{\theta}-\mu_{\theta}\ast\phi_{\eta}\right)(dx),

which is similarly bounded by
C\eta^{2}\|\mu_{\theta}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}
, establishing the result.
∎

Before we turn to establishing the desired concentration estimate, we first need to show that the potential generated by smeared points (which is amenable to the energy estimates discussed in Lemma
2.1
) is sufficiently close to the potential generated by the actual point configuration at a fixed point
x
. This difference is large only if there is a point very close to
x
, which is a low probability event, but the estimate requires some care due to the logarithmic singularity in the potential. We make use of isotropic averaging techniques introduced and developed in
[
Leb17
]
,
[
Tho24
]
and
[
Tho25
]
.

We let
\psi:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
be a nonnegative, rotationally symmetric mollifier of total mass
1
supported within
\overline{B_{1}(0)}
. We let
\psi_{\eta}(x)=\eta^{-2}\psi(x/\eta)
. In practice, we will set
\psi=\phi_{1/2}\ast\phi_{1/2}
(and so
\psi_{2\eta}=\phi_{\eta}\ast\phi_{\eta}
).

Proposition 2.4
.

Assume (
Grow
). Let
\psi_{\eta}
be as above. One has for all
t\in(0,2)
,
\beta\leq 1
, and
\eta\leq N^{-1/2}\beta^{-1/2}
that

(2.8)

\mathbb{E}_{N,\beta}\left[e^{tN\left(\mathsf{h}^{\mathrm{fluct}}(x)-\mathsf{h}^{\mathrm{fluct}\ast\psi_{\eta}}(x)\right)}\right]\leq\exp\left(\frac{CN\eta^{2}}{2-t}\right)

for a constant
C
depending only on
\sup_{y:|y-x|\leq 1}|\max(\Delta V(y),0)|
. One also has

(2.9)

\mathsf{h}^{\mathrm{fluct}}(x)-\mathsf{h}^{\mathrm{fluct}\ast\psi_{\eta}}(x)\geq-C\|\mu_{\theta}\|_{L^{\infty}}\eta^{2}.

Proof.

Step 1.
Background results.

We begin by stating a couple results that are consequences of the isotropic averaging technique. All implicit constants will be independent of
x
due to the bound
\Delta V\leq C
from (
Grow
). From
[
Tho24
, Theorem 1]
, one has for any
R\geq N^{-1/2}
that

(2.10)

\mathbb{P}_{N,\beta}(|\{x_{i}\in B_{R}(x)\}|\geq Q)\leq e^{-\frac{1}{2}\beta Q^{2}}\quad\forall Q\geq C\beta^{-1}+CNR^{2}

for
C
depending only on
\sup_{y:|y-x|\leq 1}|\max(\Delta V(y),0)|
. We will consider
\eta\leq R=N^{-1/2}\beta^{-1/2}
below.

Let
\widetilde{\rho}_{k}(\cdot|x_{k+1},\ldots,x_{M})
be the Lebesgue density of the law of
x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}
conditioned on
x_{k+1},\ldots,x_{N}
. In
[
Tho25
, Proposition 3.1, specifically by iterating (3.6)]
it is proved that a.s.

(2.11)

\begin{split}&\widetilde{\rho}_{k}(y_{1},\ldots,y_{k}|x_{k+1},\ldots,x_{N})\\
\leq&\frac{C^{k}e^{C\beta NkR^{2}}}{R^{2k}}\int_{B_{R}(y_{1})\times\cdots\times B_{R}(y_{k})}\widetilde{\rho}_{k}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{k}|x_{k+1},\ldots,x_{N})dz_{1}\cdots dz_{k},\end{split}

for a constant
C
depending only on
\sup_{i=1,\ldots,k}\sup_{y:|y-y_{i}|\leq 1}|\max(\Delta V(y),0)|
.

Step 2.
Separate
B_{\eta}(x)
and
B_{\eta}(x)^{c}
.

Now we are ready to prove (
2.8
). Splitting the integral according to which particles fall within
B_{\eta}(x)
, one has

(2.12)

\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}\left[e^{tN\left(\mathsf{h}^{\mathrm{fluct}}(x)-\mathsf{h}^{\mathrm{fluct}\ast\psi_{\eta}}(x)\right)}\right]\\
=&\sum_{k=0}^{N}\binom{N}{k}\int_{B_{\eta}(x)^{k}\times(\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus B_{\eta}(x))^{N-k}}e^{tN\left(\mathsf{h}^{\mathrm{fluct}}(x)-\mathsf{h}^{\mathrm{fluct}\ast\psi_{\eta}}(x)\right)}\mathbb{P}_{N,\beta}(dX_{N}).\end{split}

Note that
\mathsf{h}^{\mathrm{fluct}}(x)-\mathsf{h}^{\mathrm{fluct}\ast\psi_{\eta}}(x)
is measurable with respect to the
\sigma
-algebra generated by the locations of the particles within
B_{\eta}(x)
. This is because
\mathsf{g}-\mathsf{g}\ast\psi_{\eta}
is supported within
B_{\eta}(0)
, where it is equal to
x\mapsto-\log(|x|/\eta)
.

Step 3.
Conditioning on particles in
B_{\eta}(x)^{c}
.

We abbreviate
X_{k}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})
,
Z_{k}=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{k})
, and
\widetilde{\rho}_{k}(X_{k})=\widetilde{\rho}_{k}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}|x_{k+1},\ldots,x_{N})
. Applying (
2.11
), we see a.s. in
x_{k+1},\ldots,x_{N}
that

(2.13)

\begin{split}&\lx@add@cssclass{ltx_eqn_lefteqn}$\displaystyle\int_{B_{\eta}(x)^{k}}e^{tN\left(\mathsf{h}^{\mathrm{fluct}}(x)-\mathsf{h}^{\mathrm{fluct}\ast\psi_{\eta}}(x)\right)}\widetilde{\rho}_{k}(X_{k})dX_{k}$\mbox{}\hfil\\
\leq&\frac{C^{k}e^{C\beta NR^{2}k}}{R^{2k}}\int_{B_{\eta}(x)^{k}}\int_{B_{R}(x_{1})\times\cdots\times B_{R}(x_{k})}e^{tN\left(\mathsf{h}^{\mathrm{fluct}}(x)-\mathsf{h}^{\mathrm{fluct}\ast\psi_{\eta}}(x)\right)}\widetilde{\rho}_{k}(Z_{k})dZ_{k}dX_{k}\\
\leq&\frac{C^{k}e^{C\beta NR^{2}k}}{R^{2k}}\left(\int_{B_{R+\eta}(x)^{k}}\widetilde{\rho}_{k}(Z_{k})dZ_{k}\right)\left(\int_{B_{\eta}(x)^{k}}e^{tN\left(\mathsf{h}^{\mathrm{fluct}}(x)-\mathsf{h}^{\mathrm{fluct}\ast\psi_{\eta}}(x)\right)}dX_{k}\right),\end{split}

where in the last line we enlarged an integration region from
B_{R}(x_{1})\times\cdots\times B_{R}(x_{k})
to
B_{R+\eta}(x)^{k}
.

We now examine
\mathsf{g}-\mathsf{g}\ast\psi_{\eta}
. Using the convention
f_{\eta}(x)=\eta^{-2}f(x/\eta)
, from which one derives
\mathsf{g}_{\eta^{-1}}=\eta^{2}(\mathsf{g}+\log\eta)
, one has

\mathsf{g}-\mathsf{g}\ast\psi_{\eta}=(\mathsf{g}_{\eta^{-1}}-\mathsf{g}_{\eta^{-1}}\ast\psi_{1})_{\eta}=\eta^{2}(\mathsf{g}-\mathsf{g}\ast\psi_{1})_{\eta}.

Note
\mathsf{g}-\mathsf{g}\ast\psi_{1}
is supported within
B_{1}(0)
by harmonicity of
\mathsf{g}
away from
0
. Within
\overline{B_{1}(0)}
, we have
\mathsf{g}-\mathsf{g}\ast\psi_{1}\leq\mathsf{g}
. It follows that
\mathsf{g}-\mathsf{g}\ast\psi_{\eta}
is supported within
\overline{B_{\eta}(0)}
and bounded by
-\log(|x|/\eta)
within its support.

By superharmonicity of
\mathsf{g}
and the above, we have

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\eta}(x)^{k}}e^{tN\left(\mathsf{h}^{\mathrm{fluct}}(x)-\mathsf{h}^{\mathrm{fluct}\ast\psi_{\eta}}(x)\right)}dX_{k}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B_{\eta}(x)^{k}}e^{t\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathsf{g}(x_{i}-x)-\mathsf{g}\ast\psi_{\eta}(x_{i}-x)\right)}dX_{k}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{B_{\eta}(x)}\frac{\eta^{t}}{|x-y|^{t}}dy\right)^{k}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{C\eta^{2}}{2-t}\right)^{k}.

Now, integrating (
2.13
) against the law of
x_{k+1},\ldots,x_{N}
over
(\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus B_{\eta(x)})^{N-k}
yields

(2.14)

\begin{split}&\int_{B_{\eta}(x)^{k}\times(\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus B_{\eta}(x))^{N-k}}e^{tN\left(\mathsf{h}^{\mathrm{fluct}}(x)-\mathsf{h}^{\mathrm{fluct}\ast\psi_{\eta}}(x)\right)}\mathbb{P}_{N,\beta}(dX_{N})\\
\leq&\left(\frac{Ce^{C\beta NR^{2}}\eta^{2}}{(2-t)R^{2}}\right)^{k}\mathbb{P}_{N,\beta}\left(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\in B_{R+\eta}(x)\right).\end{split}

Step 4.
Proof of (
2.8
).

We use exchangeability and (
2.10
) to estimate the last probability in (
2.14
) as

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{N,\beta}\left(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\in B_{R+\eta}(x)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m=k}^{\infty}\frac{\binom{N-k}{m-k}}{\binom{N}{m}}\mathbb{P}_{N,\beta}\left(|\{x_{i}:x_{i}\in B_{R+\eta}(x)\}|=m\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\binom{N}{k}^{-1}\sum_{m=k}^{\infty}\binom{m}{k}\mathbb{P}_{N,\beta}\left(|\{x_{i}:x_{i}\in B_{R+\eta}(x)\}|=m\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\binom{N}{k}^{-1}\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{m=k}^{\infty}m^{k}\mathbb{P}_{N,\beta}\left(|\{x_{i}:x_{i}\in B_{R+\eta}(x)\}|=m\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\binom{N}{k}^{-1}\frac{1}{k!}\left((C\beta^{-1})^{k}\mathbb{P}_{N,\beta}\left(|\{x_{i}:x_{i}\in B_{R+\eta}(x)\}|\leq C\beta^{-1}\right)+\sum_{m=\max(k,C\beta^{-1})}^{\infty}m^{k}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\beta m^{2}}\right).

The ratio of successive terms in the last sum is

\left(\frac{m+1}{m}\right)^{k}e^{-\beta m}\leq e^{\frac{k}{m}-\beta m},

which is less than
1/2
so long as
C
is large enough. We conclude that

\mathbb{P}_{N,\beta}\left(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\in B_{R+\eta}(x)\right)\leq(C\beta^{-1})^{k}\binom{N}{k}^{-1}\frac{1}{k!},

and combining with (
2.14
) and (
2.12
) shows

\mathbb{E}\left[e^{tN\left(\mathsf{h}^{\mathrm{fluct}}(x)-\mathsf{h}^{\mathrm{fluct}\ast\psi_{\eta}}(x)\right)}\right]\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}\left(\frac{Ce^{C\beta NR^{2}}\eta^{2}}{\beta(2-t)R^{2}}\right)^{k}=\exp\left(\frac{CN\eta^{2}}{2-t}\right),

where we substituted
R=N^{-1/2}\beta^{-1/2}
. This finishes the proof of (
2.8
).

Step 5.
Proof of equation (
2.9
).

While
\mathsf{h}^{\mathrm{fluct}}-\mathsf{h}^{\mathrm{fluct}\ast\psi_{\eta}}
can only be bounded from above in probability, we have a deterministic lower bound. Equation (
2.9
) follows from the computation

\mathsf{h}^{\mathrm{fluct}}(x)-\mathsf{h}^{\mathrm{fluct}\ast\psi_{\eta}}(x)\geq\mathsf{h}^{\mu_{\theta}\ast\psi_{\eta}}(x)-\mathsf{h}^{\mu_{\theta}}(x)=(\mathsf{g}\ast\psi_{\eta}-\mathsf{g})\ast\mu_{\theta}.

Young’s inequality finishes the proof once one notes that
\mathsf{g}\ast\psi_{\eta}-\mathsf{g}
has
L^{1}
norm of order
\eta^{2}
.
∎

2.3.
Concentration

We are now in a position to obtain our main concentration result.

Proposition 2.5
.

Assume
\beta\leq 1
and (
Grow
).
We have

(2.15)

\mathbb{P}_{N,\beta}\left(\left|\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathsf{h}^{\mathrm{fluct}}(y_{i})\right|\geq kTN^{-1/2}\right)\leq 4k\left(e^{-\frac{1}{2}TN^{1/2}}+e^{-\beta TN}\right),

for any
T\geq C\log N
for a large enough
C>0
, uniformly in points
y_{1},\ldots,y_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Here
N
is restricted to be large uniformly over selections of
y_{1},\ldots,y_{k}
in any fixed compact set.

Proof.

Step 1.
First simplification.

First, it is enough to bound

\mathbb{P}_{N,\beta}\left(\left|\mathsf{h}^{\mathrm{fluct}}(y_{1})\right|\geq TN^{-1/2}\right)\leq 4\left(e^{-\frac{1}{2}TN^{1/2}}+e^{-\beta TN}\right),

by a pigeonhole argument in
k
and a union bound. We will also let
y_{1}=0
for simplicity. Let
\varepsilon=\frac{1}{2}TN^{-1/2}
, and
\mathcal{G}_{\pm}({\lambda},\nu)=\{X_{N}:\pm\mathsf{h}^{\nu}(0)\geq\lambda\}
for any
\nu
and
\lambda>0
. Define
\eta=N^{-100}
, and let
\psi_{2\eta}=\phi_{\eta}\ast\phi_{\eta}
.

Step 2.
Now we estimate the probability that
\mathsf{h}^{\mathrm{fluct}}(0)
is large but
\mathsf{h}^{\mathrm{fluct}\ast\psi_{2\eta}}(0)
is not.

We need to estimate

\mathbb{P}_{N,\beta}\left(\mathcal{G}_{+}(2\varepsilon,\mathrm{fluct})\cap\left(\mathcal{G}_{+}(\varepsilon,\mathrm{fluct}\ast\psi_{2\eta})\right)^{c}\right)\leq\mathbb{P}_{N,\beta}(\mathsf{h}^{\mathrm{fluct}}(0)-\mathsf{h}^{\mathrm{fluct}\ast\psi_{2\eta}}(0)\geq\varepsilon).

The Chebyshev inequality coupled with Proposition
2.4
(with
t=1
) yields

\mathbb{P}_{N,\beta}\left(\mathsf{h}^{\mathrm{fluct}}(0)-\mathsf{h}^{\mathrm{fluct}\ast\psi_{2\eta}}(0)\geq\varepsilon\right)\leq e^{-N\epsilon+CN\eta^{2}}\leq 2e^{-\frac{1}{2}TN^{1/2}}.

Step 3.
Next, we turn to estimating the probability that
\mathsf{h}^{\mathrm{fluct}\ast\psi_{2\eta}}(0)
exceeds
\varepsilon
. We will divide into two cases:
T\leq N
and
T>N
.

Substep 3.1
We first consider
T\leq N
.

Abbreviate
\mathcal{G}_{+}=\mathcal{G}_{+}(\varepsilon,\mathrm{fluct}\ast\psi_{2\eta})\cap\{\int|x||\phi_{\eta}\ast\mathrm{fluct}|(dx)\leq\delta^{-1}\}
for
\delta^{-1}=N^{100}
,
and note first by Lemma
2.3
we have

(2.16)

\int_{\mathcal{G}_{+}}e^{-\beta N^{2}\mathsf{F}_{N}(X_{N},\mu_{\theta})}\mu_{\theta}^{\otimes N}(dX_{N})\leq e^{\beta N^{2}\mathrm{Err}_{1}(\eta)}\int_{\mathcal{G}_{+}}e^{-\beta N^{2}\mathcal{E}(\phi_{\eta}\ast\mathrm{fluct})}\mu_{\theta}^{\otimes N}(dX_{N})

for

\mathrm{Err}_{1}(\eta)\leq(2N)^{-1}\mathsf{g}(\eta)+C\left(\|\mu_{\theta}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathsf{d}})}+\|\mu_{\theta}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathsf{d}})}^{2}\right)\eta^{2}\leq\frac{C\log N}{N}.

By Lemma
2.1
and recalling
\psi_{2\eta}=\phi_{\eta}\ast\phi_{\eta}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{+}

\displaystyle\subset\left\{\mathcal{E}(\mathrm{fluct}\ast\phi_{\eta})\geq\frac{\mathsf{c}_{0}\varepsilon^{2}}{1+\log\varepsilon^{-1}+\log\delta^{-1}+\log\eta^{-1}}\right\}

\displaystyle\subset\left\{\mathcal{E}(\mathrm{fluct}\ast\phi_{\eta})\geq\frac{T^{2}}{CN\log N}\right\}.

Note further that
\frac{T^{2}}{CN\log N}\geq\frac{CT}{N}
. Assembling the above into (
2.16
), we find

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{G}_{+}}e^{-\beta N^{2}\mathsf{F}(X_{N},\mu_{\theta})}\mu_{\theta}^{\otimes N}(dX_{N})

. We note that, with more complicated error terms, one could treat

Step 4.

3.
Poisson Convergence

In this section we use Proposition

3.1.
Preliminaries

This quick summary is based on material contained in
[
Lam21b
, Appendix A]
and
[
PGPT24
,
\lx@sectionsign 7
]
. First, recall that a generic point process
\Xi
is a probability measure on locally finite point configurations in
\mathbb{R}^{\mathsf{d}}
; it can be easier to think of these as probability measures on the space of Radon measures that take the form
\sum_{p\in P}\delta_{p}
for countable subsets
P\subset\mathbb{R}^{\mathsf{d}}
with no accumulation points. The law of such a process is determined by its Laplace functional

(3.1)

\psi(f):=\mathbb{E}_{\Xi}\left[e^{-\sum_{p\in P}f(p)}\right]

for all Borel-measurable
f:\mathbb{R}^{\mathsf{d}}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\{\Xi_{N}\}
be a sequence of point processes with Laplace functionals
\psi_{N}
, and
\Xi_{\infty}
a point process with Laplace function
\psi_{\infty}
. We say that
\Xi_{N}\rightarrow\Xi_{\infty}

weakly
if

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\psi_{N}(f)=\psi_{\infty}(f)

for all continuous and compactly supported
f:\mathbb{R}^{\mathsf{d}}\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
.

A convenient means of studying weak convergence is through correlation functions. The
correlation functions

R_{k}
are associated via

(3.2)

\psi(f):=1+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{\mathsf{d}}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\left(\exp(-f(x_{i}))-1\right)R_{k}(dx_{1},...dx_{k}).

Intuitively, one can think of
R_{k}
as as the probability density for finding
k
particles in a given set. Indeed, recall that a homogeneous Poisson point process of intensity
\lambda>0
with respect to Lebesgue reference measure is uniquely characterized by

(1)

The number of points in two disjoint sets are independent, and

(2)

\#\text{ of points in }\Omega\sim\mathrm{Poisson}(\lambda\mathrm{Leb}(\Omega))
.

The correlation functions of such a point process are just
R_{k}=\lambda^{k}
.

The following proposition guides our approach to understanding the local point process
Q_{\overline{z},N}
given in (
1.15
), i.e.

Q_{\overline{z},N}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{N^{1/\mathsf{d}}(x_{i}-\overline{z})}.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
Q_{\overline{z},N}
be the local point process given in (
1.15
). Then, the correlation functions
R_{k}(y_{1},\dots,y_{k})
for
(y_{1},\dots,y_{k})\subset(\mathbb{R}^{\mathsf{d}})^{k}
associated to
X_{N}
via
x_{i}=\overline{z}+N^{-1/\mathsf{d}}y_{i}
, where

is distributed according to
\mathbb{P}_{N,\beta}
, are given by

(3.3)

R_{k}(y_{1},\dots,y_{k})=\frac{N!}{(N-k)!N^{k}}\rho_{k}(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})

where
\rho_{k}
denotes the
k
th marginal of
\mathbb{P}_{N,\beta}

(3.4)

\rho_{k}(x_{1},\dots,x_{k}):=\frac{1}{\mathsf{K}_{N,\beta}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\mu_{\theta}(x_{i})\int_{\mathbb{R}^{\mathsf{d}(N-k)}}e^{-\beta N^{2}\mathsf{F}(X_{N},\mu_{\theta})}\prod_{i=k+1}^{N}\mu_{\theta}(x_{i})\penalty\ dx_{i}.

Furthermore, if

(1)

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}R_{k}^{N}(y_{1},\dots,y_{k})=\mu_{V}(\overline{z})^{k}

(2)

\sup_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}\int_{\Omega}R_{k}^{N}(dy_{1},\dots,dy_{k})<+\infty

converges weakly to a homogeneous Poisson point process of intensity

Proof.

Equation (

\frac{\rho_{k}(X_{k})}{\prod_{i=1}^{k}\mu_{\theta}(x_{i})}=e^{-\beta k^{2}\mathsf{F}_{k}(X_{k},\mu_{\theta})}\frac{\mathsf{K}_{N-k,\beta}}{\mathsf{K}_{N,\beta}}\times\\
\left(\mathbb{E}_{N-k}\left[e^{\beta k(N-k)\int\mathsf{h}^{\mu_{\theta}}(y)\penalty\ \mathrm{fluct}_{N-k}(dy)}\right]+O(\beta N^{1/2}\log N)\|F\|_{L^{2}}\right).

Utilizing Proposition
3.6
allows us to control
\|F\|_{L^{2}}
uniformly in the regime
\beta\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}\log N}
, which is guaranteed by the assumption (
Temp
).

Next, we find by Lemma
3.3
and Tonelli’s theorem that

(3.16)

\begin{split}&\frac{\mathsf{K}_{N,\beta}}{\mathsf{K}_{N-k,\beta}}\\
=&\int\mathbb{E}_{N-k}\left[e^{-\beta N^{2}\mathsf{F}(X_{k}\cup X_{N-k},\mu_{\theta})+\beta(N-k)^{2}\mathsf{F}(X_{N-k},\mu_{\theta})}\right]\mu_{\theta}^{\otimes k}(dX_{k})\\
=&\int e^{-\beta k^{2}\mathsf{F}_{k}(X_{k},\mu_{\theta})}\mathbb{E}_{N-k}\left[e^{-\beta(N-k)\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathsf{h}^{\mathsf{emp}-\mu_{\theta}}(y_{i})+\beta k(N-k)\int\mathsf{h}^{\mu_{\theta}}(y)\mathrm{fluct}_{N-k}(dy)}\right]\mu_{\theta}^{\otimes k}(dX_{k}).\end{split}

We apply Corollary
3.4
and Proposition
3.6
once again and find

(3.17)

\begin{split}&\lx@add@cssclass{ltx_eqn_lefteqn}$\displaystyle\frac{\rho_{k}(Y_{k})}{\prod_{i=1}^{k}\mu_{\theta}(x_{i})}$\mbox{}\hfil\\
=&\frac{e^{-\beta k^{2}\mathsf{F}_{k}(X_{k},\mu_{\theta})}\left(\mathbb{E}_{N-k}\left[e^{\beta k(N-k)\int\mathsf{h}^{\mu_{\theta}}(y)\mathrm{fluct}_{N-k}(dy)}\right]+O(\beta N^{1/2}\log N)\right)}{\int e^{-\beta k^{2}\mathsf{F}_{k}(X_{k}^{\prime},\mu_{\theta})}\left(\mathbb{E}_{N-k}\left[e^{\beta k(N-k)\int\mathsf{h}^{\mu_{\theta}}(y)\mathrm{fluct}_{N-k}(dy)}\right]+O(\beta N^{1/2}\log N)\right)\mu_{\theta}^{\otimes k}(dX^{\prime}_{k})}.\end{split}

Note that the error term in the denominator has an implicit constant independent of
X^{\prime}_{k}
by Proposition
3.6
. It is straightforward to see

\int e^{-\beta k^{2}\mathsf{F}_{k}(X_{k}^{\prime},\mu_{\theta})}\mu_{\theta}^{\otimes k}(dX^{\prime}_{k})=\exp\left(O(\beta)\right)=(1+O(\beta))

and so applying Proposition
3.6
we can see that (
3.17
) is equal to

(1+O(\beta))e^{-\beta k^{2}\mathsf{F}_{k}(X_{k},\mu_{\theta})}\frac{L+O(\beta N^{1/2}\log N)}{L+O(\beta N^{1/2}\log N)}

for

L=\mathbb{E}_{N-k}\left[e^{\beta k(N-k)\int\mathsf{h}^{\mu_{\theta}}(y)\mathrm{fluct}_{N-k}(dy)}\right]=e^{O(1)},

where the last bound follows from Proposition
3.6
. A simple Taylor approximation allows us to estimate

\displaystyle\frac{\rho_{k}(Y_{k})}{\prod_{i=1}^{k}\mu_{\theta}(x_{i})}

\displaystyle=e^{-\beta k^{2}\mathsf{F}_{k}(X_{k},\mu_{\theta})}(1+O(\beta))(1+O(\beta N^{1/2}\log N))

\displaystyle=e^{-\beta k^{2}\mathsf{F}_{k}(X_{k},\mu_{\theta})}(1+O(\beta N^{1/2}\log N)).

Finally, we note that
\beta k^{2}\mathsf{F}_{k}(X_{k},\mu_{\theta})=O(\beta\log N)
for fixed
Y_{k}
and that
\frac{(N-k)!N^{k}}{N!}=1+O(1/N)
, so the same asymptotic holds for
R_{k}(Y_{k})
as
\rho_{k}(X_{k})
. Since
\mu_{\theta}(y_{i})\rightarrow\mu_{V}(\overline{z})
by (
1.17
) (see
[
AS22
, Theorem 1]
), we have the desired convergence of the correlation functions.

Finally, the above computation coupled with the convergence
\mu_{\theta}(y_{i})\rightarrow\mu_{V}(\overline{z})
(
[
AS22
, Theorem 1]
) tells us that there exists some
C>0
such that

|R_{k}^{N}(y_{1},\dots,y_{k})|\leq(C\mu_{V}(\overline{z}))^{k}

for all
N
, and hence for any compact
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}

\sup_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}\int_{\Omega}R_{k}^{N}(dy_{1},\dots,dy_{k})\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}(C\mu_{V}(\overline{z}))^{k}|\Omega|<+\infty.

Convergence to a homogeneous Poisson point process of intensity
\mu_{V}(\overline{z})
then follows from Proposition
3.2
. ∎

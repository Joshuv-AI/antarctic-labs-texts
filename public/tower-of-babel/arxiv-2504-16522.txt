On Bell numbers of type D D


Abstract
In this paper, we will introduce Bell numbers D ⁡ ( n ) D(n) of type D D as an analogue to the classical Bell numbers related to all the partitions of the set [ n ] [n] . Then based on a signed set partition of type D D , we will construct the recurrence relations of Bell numbers D ⁡ ( n ) D(n) . In addition, we deduce the exponential generating function for D ⁡ ( n ) D(n) . Finally, we will provide an explicit formula for D ⁡ ( n ) D(n) .

Introduction

The main objective of this paper is to study Bell numbers of type
D
. First of all, we will give some fundamental concepts which we will use throughout the paper. Let
[n]:=\{1,\cdots,n\}
and
\langle n\rangle:=\{-n,\cdots,1,0,1,\cdots,n\}
. The partitions of the set
[n]
into
k
non-empty subset, are called
blocks
, are enumerated by the
ordinary Stirling numbers of the second kind
and denoted by
S(n,k)
(see
[
11
]
). The ordinary Stirling numbers of the second kind have the recurrence relation for all
n\geq 1

S(n,k)=S(n-1,k-1)+kS(n-1,k)

with the initial conditions
S(0,k)=\delta_{0,k}
, where
\delta_{i,j}
is the Kronecker delta.
The number of all set partitions of the
[n]
is called
the classical Bell numbers
and is denoted by
A(n)
. Clearly,
A(n)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S(n,k)
. Classical Bell numbers
A(n)
satisfy the following well-known recurrence relation for all
n\geq 0

A(n+1)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}A(k).

Also, the exponential generating function for
A(n)
is

A(x)=e^{e^{x}-1}.

When taking
A(x)=e^{e^{x}-1}
into account, it is not hard to see that a concrete formula for
A(n)
is given by

A(n)=e^{-1}\sum_{r\geq 0}\frac{r^{n}}{r!}.

Definition 1.2

(
[
2
]
)
.

The number of all signed set partitions of the
\langle n\rangle
is called Bell numbers of type
B
and is denoted by
B(n)
. Then we can write

B(n)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{B}(n,k).

We would like to thank Bruce Sagan for calling reference
[
2
]
to our attention. Upon this, we encountered that Shankar in
[
10
]
proved that Bell numbers in type
B
satisfy tle following recurrence relation

B(n+1)=B(n)+\sum_{k=0}^{n}2^{k}{n\choose k}B(n-k)

(4)

for all
n\geq 0
. Moreover, Shankar gave the recurrence relation in equation (
4
) in more general form for colored Bell numbers and also established an explicit formula for colored Bell numbers in Corollary 4 of
[
10
]
. Thus it is clear from
[
10
]
that for all
n\geq 0

B(n)=e^{\frac{-1}{2}}\left(\sum_{r\geq 0}\frac{(2r+1)^{n}}{2^{r}r!}\right).

(5)

In
[
2
]
, Buck et al. established a bijection between flattened Stirling permutations of order
n+1
and signed set partitions of
\langle n\rangle
, and then left many open problems and conjectures. Shankar
[
10
]
has proved all of the conjectures and open problems, except for Problem 3, posed by Buck et al. in
[
2
]
. Furthermore, we want especially to note that the exponential generating function of
B(n)
is

B(x)=e^{\frac{e^{2x}}{2}+x-\frac{1}{2}}

(6)

which follows easily from Theorem 4.1(a) of Sagan and Swanson, see
[
8
]
.

Proposition 1.3
.

For all
n\geq 0
, we have

\sum_{k=0}^{n}(2k+1)S_{B}(n,k)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}2^{k}B(n-k).

Proof.

Taking into account equation (
1
) and
B(n+1)=\sum_{k=0}^{n+1}S_{B}(n+1,k)
, then we can write

\displaystyle B(n+1)=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n+1}S_{B}(n+1,k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S_{B}(n+1,0)+\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}\left(S_{B}(n,k-1)+(2k+1)S_{B}(n,k)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1+\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{B}(n,k)+\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}(2k+1)S_{B}(n,k)~~(\textrm{since}~~S_{B}(n,n+1)=0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle B(n)+\sum_{k=0}^{n}(2k+1)S_{B}(n,k).

Since
B(n+1)=B(n)+\sum_{k=0}^{n}2^{k}{n\choose k}B(n-k)
, we obtain the desired result as

\sum_{k=0}^{n}(2k+1)S_{B}(n,k)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}2^{k}B(n-k).

∎

For example, we illustrate the relation in Proposition
1.3
for both
n=2
and
n=3
as

\bullet

S_{B}(2,0)+3S_{B}(2,1)+5S_{B}(2,2)=18=B(2)+4B(1)+4B(0)

\bullet

S_{B}(3,0)+3S_{B}(3,1)+5S_{B}(3,2)+7S_{B}(3,3)=92=B(3)+6B(2)+12B(1)+8B(0)
.

2
Bell numbers of type
D

In this section, we will first introduce Bell numbers of type
D
and then give their some combinatorial properties such as a recurrence relation, the exponential generating function and an explicit formula.

Definition 2.1
.

The number of all
D
-type set partitions of the
\langle n\rangle
is called Bell numbers of type
D
and we will denote it by
D(n)
. Then we can express

D(n)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{D}(n,k).

Theorem 2.2
.

Bell numbers in type
D
satisfy

D(n+1)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}{n\choose i}\sum_{k=0}^{n-i}2^{n-i-k}S(n-i,k)+\sum_{k=0}^{n}2^{k}{n\choose k}D(n-k)

(7)

for all
n\geq 0
, where
S(n,k)
is the classical Stirling number of the second kind.

Proof.

Consider
\gamma=P_{0}~|~P_{1}/P_{2}~|~P_{3}/P_{4}~|~\cdots~|~P_{2s-1}/P_{2s}
a signed set partition of
\langle n+1\rangle
with
s
paired non-zero blocks.

Case 1
. Without loss of generality, we may suppose that
n+1\in P_{1}
, and that
|P_{1}|=k+1
, for some
k,~0\leq k\leq n
. Then

P_{0}~|~P_{3}/P_{4}~|~\cdots~|~P_{2s-1}/P_{2s}
has the form of a signed partition of the remaining
2n-2k
elements of
\langle n+1\rangle
, that is,
\langle n+1\rangle\setminus(P_{1}\biguplus P_{2})
. There are
D(n-k)
signed partitions of this set. Thus there are
D(n-k)
signed partitions of
\langle n+1\rangle
in which one paired non-zero block is the
P_{1}/P_{2}
. There are
2^{k}{n\choose k}
signed sets of size
k+1
containing
n+1
. Thus, the total number of
D
-type partitions of
\langle n+1\rangle
in which
n+1
is in a signed set of size
k+1
is
2^{k}{n\choose k}D(n-k)
. Summing up over all possible values of
k
, we obtain the second part of the summation in the right hand side of equation (
7
).

Case 2
.
Suppose that
n+1\in P_{0}
. To create a set partition of type
D
with zero-block
P_{0}
such that
n+1\in P_{0}
, we first need to select at least one more element of
[n]
as a zero-block in a
D
-type partition must contain at least two positive elements or no positive elements of
[n]
. So we can do this in
{n\choose i}
possible ways, where
i
ranges from
1
to
n
. In other words, there are
{n\choose i}
possible ways to do this. This construction provides all possible
P_{0}
such that
n+1\in P_{0}
. Now we want to calculate the number of all set partitions of type
D
such that
n+1\in P_{0}
. The number of distribution of the remaining
n-i
elements into
k
non-empty paired blocks is
S(n-i,k)
. Since the smallest element in each of
k
paired blocks will be positive, and the remaining
n-k-i
elements in those
k
blocks can be positive or negative, then there are
2^{n-i-k}
possible ways to pick the signs of these remaining values. Since
k
will vary from
0
to
n-i
, we then conclude that the number of
D
-type set partitions of
\langle n+1\rangle
for this case is exactly
\sum_{i=1}^{n}{n\choose i}\sum_{k=0}^{n-i}2^{n-i-k}S(n-i,k)
. Therefore, we conclude the first part of the summation in the right hand side of equation (
7
).
∎

More clearly, we can give the following example.

Example 2.3
.

We can see how the formula in (
7
) works for small values of
n
.

\bullet

D(3)={2\choose 1}\Bigl\{2S(1,0)+S(1,1)\Bigl\}+{2\choose 2}S(0,0)+{2\choose 0}D(2)+2{2\choose 1}D(1)+2^{2}{2\choose 2}D(0)=15

\bullet

D(4)={3\choose 1}\Bigl\{2^{2}S(2,0)+2S(2,1)+S(2,2)\Bigl\}+{3\choose 2}\Bigl\{2S(1,0)+S(1,1)\Bigl\}+{3\choose 3}S(0,0)+{3\choose 0}D(3)+2{3\choose 1}D(2)+2^{2}{3\choose 2}D(1)+2^{3}{3\choose 3}D(0)=72

\bullet

D(5)={4\choose 1}\Bigl\{2^{3}S(3,0)+2^{2}S(3,1)+2S(3,2)+S(3,3)\Bigl\}+{4\choose 2}\Bigl\{2^{2}S(2,0)+2S(2,1)+S(2,2)\Bigl\}+{4\choose 3}\Bigl\{2S(1,0)+S(1,1)\Bigl\}+{4\choose 4}\Bigl\{S(0,0)\Bigl\}+{4\choose 0}D(4)+2{4\choose 1}D(3)+2^{2}{4\choose 2}D(2)+2^{3}{4\choose 3}D(1)+2^{4}{4\choose 4}D(0)=403
.

Now we will derive the exponential generating functions of Bell numbers in type
D
in the following theorem:

Theorem 2.4
.

The exponential generating function of
D(n)
is

D(x)=e^{\frac{e^{2x}-1}{2}}(e^{x}-x).

Proof.

Let the exponential generating function of
D(n)
be

D(x)=\sum_{n\geq 0}D(n)\frac{x^{n}}{n!}.

If we take the derivative of both sides of the above expression with respect to
x
, then we obtain

D^{\prime}(x)=\sum_{n\geq 1}D(n)\frac{x^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}=\sum_{n\geq 0}D(n+1)\frac{x^{n}}{n!}.

We obtain from Theorem 4.7 in
[
2
]
that
\sum_{i=1}^{n}{n\choose i}\sum_{k=0}^{n-i}2^{n-i-k}S(n-i,k)=B(n)-\sum_{k=0}^{n}2^{n-k}S(n,k)
. Thus we conclude from the fact
\sum_{n\geq 0}S(n,k)t^{k}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}=e^{t(e^{x}-1)}
in Section 4.1 in
[
8
]
that

\displaystyle D^{\prime}(x)=

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}D(n+1)\frac{x^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}\left(B(n)-\sum_{k=0}^{n}2^{n-k}S(n,k)\right)\frac{x^{n}}{n!}+\sum_{n\geq 0}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n}2^{k}{n\choose k}D(n-k)\right)\frac{x^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle B(x)-\left(\sum_{n,k\geq 0}2^{n-k}S(n,k)\frac{x^{n}}{n!}\right)+\left(\sum_{n\geq 0}2^{n}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}\right)\left(\sum_{n\geq 0}D(n)\frac{x^{n}}{n!}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle B(x)-e^{\frac{e^{2x}-1}{2}}+e^{2x}D(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{\frac{e^{2x}-1}{2}+x}+e^{\frac{e^{2x}-1}{2}}+e^{2x}D(x).

When solving the initial value problem
D^{\prime}(x)=e^{\frac{e^{2x}-1}{2}+x}+e^{\frac{e^{2x}-1}{2}}+e^{2x}D(x),~~D(0)=1
, this yields the exponential generating function of
D(n)
as
D(x)=e^{\frac{e^{2x}-1}{2}}(e^{x}-x).

∎

As a result of Theorem
2.4
, we will give an explicit formula of Bell numbers of type
D
in the following corollary.

Corollary 2.5
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

D(n)=e^{\frac{-1}{2}}\sum_{r\geq 0}\frac{1}{2^{r}r!}[(2r+1)^{n}-n(2r)^{n-1}].

Proof.

Let us consider that
D(x)=e^{\frac{e^{2x}-1}{2}}(e^{x}-x)
from Theorem
2.4
. Thus, we obtain from equations (
5
) and (
6
) that

\displaystyle D(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 0}D(n)\frac{x^{n}}{n!}=e^{\frac{e^{2x}-1}{2}}(e^{x}-x)

\displaystyle=B(x)-e^{\frac{-1}{2}}xe^{\frac{e^{2x}}{2}}

\displaystyle=e^{\frac{-1}{2}}\sum_{n\geq 0}\left(\sum_{r\geq 0}\frac{(2r+1)^{n}}{2^{r}r!}\right)\frac{x^{n}}{n!}-e^{\frac{-1}{2}}\sum_{n\geq 0}\left(\sum_{r\geq 0}\frac{(2r)^{n}}{2^{r}r!}\right)\frac{x^{n+1}}{n!}

\displaystyle=e^{\frac{-1}{2}}\sum_{n\geq 0}\left(\sum_{r\geq 0}\frac{(2r+1)^{n}}{2^{r}r!}\right)\frac{x^{n}}{n!}-e^{\frac{-1}{2}}\sum_{n\geq 0}\left(\sum_{r\geq 0}\frac{n(2r)^{n-1}}{2^{r}r!}\right)\frac{x^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=e^{\frac{-1}{2}}\sum_{r\geq 0}\frac{1}{2^{r}r!}[(2r+1)^{n}-n(2r)^{n-1}]\frac{x^{n}}{n!}.

Comparing the coefficients of
\frac{x^{n}}{n!}
for each
n\geq 0
, we then obtain the desired formula.
∎

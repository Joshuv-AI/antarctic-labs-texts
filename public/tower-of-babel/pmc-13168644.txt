EV charging station selection and routing flask application with ACO and NSGA-II including photovoltaic energy constraints
Authors: Meriem Belaid, Said El Beid, Salman Habib, Muhammad Majid Gulzar, Jamal Aldahmashi, Ahmed Emara, Said Doubabi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This paper presents an intelligent electric vehicle routing and charging optimization framework designed to minimize travel distance, energy consumption, and charging costs while maximizing the integration of photovoltaic energy. The proposed hybrid model combines Ant Colony Optimization for efficient route planning with the Non-dominated Sorting Genetic Algorithm II for multi-objective optimization under realistic conditions, including State of Charge, vehicle speed, charger occupancy, dynamic pricing, and renewable energy availability. Implemented as a Flask application with real-time communication via MQTT, the framework was validated across five realistic driving scenarios in Morocco, encompassing urban, suburban, and highway routes. Results demonstrate that the proposed algorithm significantly outperforms traditional A*, Ant Colony Optimization , and Non-dominated Sorting Genetic Algorithm II approaches, reducing travel distance by 7–10% and energy consumption by 10–15% compared to A*, and achieving up to 15–20% faster travel times than Non-dominated Sorting Genetic Algorithm II. In scenarios with photovoltaic availability, it lowers charging costs by 30–40%, increases renewable energy utilization remains high (≈70–98%) achieving ≈3–4 kg CO₂ reduction per trip relative to grid-only charging. Overall, the hybrid PV-aware optimization framework surpasses all benchmark algorithms by jointly enhancing efficiency, economic performance, and environmental sustainability, establishing a scalable foundation for next-generation electric vehicle routing and charging management in smart cities.

Introduction

Background and motivation

The increasing adoption of EVs (electric vehicles) has intensified the demand for smart, adaptive, and energy-efficient charging infrastructures1. According to the Global EV (electric vehicle) Outlook, the expanding EV fleet requires scalable and reliable networks to meet user needs2. A key challenge is guiding EV drivers to optimal charging stations in real time, considering factors such as battery state, location, station occupancy, and energy source3,4. Recent studies emphasize that, beyond technological and infrastructural factors, accelerating EV adoption also depends on supportive policies, psychological influences, and environmental awareness that shape user behavior and acceptance5. Furthermore, integrating charging management with advanced communication technologies improves system efficiency and decision-making. These approaches enable adaptive scheduling and coordination with the grid while leveraging renewable energy sources for sustainable operation6. Robust energy management strategies have been shown to enhance synchronization between EV charging and variable renewable generation, thereby supporting overall grid stability7. Smart charging methods further optimize charging patterns, reduce peak loads, and improve energy utilization8.

In Morocco, the country’s renewable energy potential provides a favorable context for the development of EV infrastructure9,10. Large-scale solar and wind projects support sustainable EV charging solutions11, while platforms such as iSmart contribute to enhancing local technological capabilities12. Internet of Things (IoT)-based systems for real-time load forecasting and station recommendation have also been shown to improve responsiveness, reduce congestion, and enhance the user experience by enabling data-driven, dynamic guidance13. Building on these insights, this paper proposes an intelligent EV charging management system tailored for urban areas in Morocco. It integrates real-time data to optimize charging decisions. The framework aims to reduce waiting times, ensure short and efficient charging paths for EVs, and maximize the use of renewable energy, effectively bridging smart grid theory with practical deployment in Moroccan cities.

Review on dynamic optimization of charging infrastructure selection for electric vehicles with real-time communication

Extensive research has addressed intelligent charging management for EVs under real-time operational constraints. IoT-based architectures enable monitoring, load forecasting, and station recommendation, improving coordination, while reliable communication supports distributed charging management13–15. Optimization-based frameworks further enhance planning by integrating energy management, parking behavior, and grid constraints16,17. In addition, renewable-integrated systems have shown that synchronizing EV charging and routing with PV generation improves renewable utilization and grid stability18–20, while V2X technologies and intelligent monitoring enable real-time analytics, load distribution, and resilient networks21–23. Large-scale and multi-level optimization frameworks, from campus microgrids to city-level applications, adopt multi-objective, robust, and hierarchical strategies to enhance grid stability, operational efficiency, and renewable integration24–29.

At urban and regional scales, these strategies are crucial for ensuring grid reliability amid high EV penetration. Data-driven approaches characterize charging demand30, scalable algorithms support fair charging under grid constraints31, and studies highlight challenges in capacity planning and network safety32. Techno-economic optimization and infrastructure planning further improve grid efficiency, while PV mini-grids and integrated energy systems reduce costs, enhance reliability, and support renewable-powered networks33–35. In the Moroccan context, these strategies are particularly relevant, as rapid EV adoption poses technical challenges for distribution networks35.

Routing optimization is another key area, as it affects travel time, waiting delays, and energy consumption. Dynamic navigation and adaptive route planning approaches consider electricity prices, charger availability, and predictive station occupancy to improve network efficiency3,35,36. Bio-inspired algorithms, especially ACO, have been widely applied for EV routing. Tan et al.37 achieved shortest travel-time routing using improved ACO, Wu et al.38 optimized transit networks balancing travel time and energy, and Tang et al.18 combined deep reinforcement learning with transformer architectures for energy-optimal routing under variable renewable generation. These studies confirm the effectiveness of ACO and hybrid bio-inspired algorithms in addressing complex EV navigation problems.

To balance multiple objectives such as charging cost, travel time, and renewable energy utilization, multi-objective optimization techniques have become increasingly important. Wu et al.25 proposed an online scheduling approach based on time-of-use pricing and peak load minimization, while Boubaker et al.39 introduced a multi-objective EV charging and discharging framework using the Red Deer algorithm to achieve balanced grid utilization. Similarly, Kumar et al.40 developed integrated power and transportation network models that combine photovoltaic generation and energy storage to minimize charging costs and improve EV autonomy. Recent studies have also proposed advanced charging scheduling strategies integrating renewable energy sources. Zhong et al.42 introduced a multi-objective charging framework that incorporates renewable energy to optimize energy utilization and reduce operational costs at charging stations. Wu et al.43 investigated coordinated charging scheduling strategies to improve the efficiency and stability of regional clean-energy power networks. Similarly, Tan et al.44 developed an integrated energy system optimization model that considers uncertainties in energy supply and real-time electricity prices to enhance overall operational performance. In addition, Li et al.45 proposed a user-oriented scheduling model that integrates time cost and spatial load balancing across multiple microgrids to improve charging efficiency and reduce congestion. Furthermore, Zhang et al.46 applied intelligent optimization techniques combining reinforcement learning with multi-objective particle swarm optimization to coordinate EV charging and discharging decisions, thereby improving system stability while reducing operational costs.

Despite these significant advances, most existing studies primarily focus on charging scheduling and energy management at the grid or charging station level, with limited attention to the combined problem of EV routing and charging station selection under renewable energy constraints. In contrast, this work proposes an EV Charging Station Selection and Routing framework implemented through a Flask-based application, which integrates ACO for routing decisions and NSGA-II for multi-objective optimization. The proposed approach simultaneously optimizes route selection, charging station choice, charging time, and operational cost, while explicitly considering photovoltaic energy availability within the charging infrastructure. By integrating renewable energy constraints with routing and station selection decisions, the proposed framework provides a practical and scalable solution for intelligent EV charging management.

Overall, these studies illustrate the interdisciplinary evolution of EV charging management, combining IoT technologies, communication systems, renewable energy integration, routing optimization, and multi-objective control strategies. Building upon these developments, the proposed integration of ACO-based routing with NSGA-II optimization in a PV-supported environment offers a coherent and scalable framework for intelligent, cost-effective, and sustainable EV charging station selection in solar-powered smart city infrastructures, addressing both mobility and energy management challenges simultaneously.

Scope of the project

System architecture

This project proposes an intelligent EV charging assistant designed to guide drivers toward the most suitable charging station along their travel route under real-time conditions. The system considers dynamic factors such as charging cost, PV energy availability, station occupancy, and spatial proximity, while also incorporating vehicle-specific parameters including battery SoC, GPS position, and estimated driving speed.

To support this decision-making process, the system relies on a multi-objective optimization framework (Fig. 1) that integrates three complementary dimensions: technical performance, economic efficiency, and user satisfaction. Technical objectives focus on optimal station selection, real-time load balancing, and reliable reservation management. Economic objectives aim to minimize charging costs while maximizing the use of renewable energy, whereas user-oriented objectives target reduced waiting times, guaranteed charging slot availability, and personalized charging recommendations.Fig. 1Multi-objective optimization targets.

Multi-objective optimization targets.

To enable these objectives, the proposed system adopts a decentralized architecture supported by continuous access to real-time infrastructure information (Fig. 2). Communication between electric vehicles and charging stations is established through lightweight MQTT protocols over 4G networks. Each charging station periodically transmits key operational data, including geographic location, PV energy availability, current and predicted occupancy levels, the number of available charging slots, and the charging cost per kWh. In addition, short-term forecasting modules provide predictions of PV generation and station congestion during the vehicle’s expected travel period, improving the quality of the charging decision process.Fig. 2System architecture.

System architecture.

Within this architecture, each vehicle operates as an autonomous decision-making unit that integrates external station information with internal parameters such as battery SoC, estimated time of arrival (ETA), projected energy consumption, and simulated driver preferences. Vehicle trajectories and GPS data are simulated in this study, while charging station data are obtained from real infrastructure sources. PV energy availability is estimated using an AI-based forecasting model trained on real measurements.

The charging decision process begins by mapping all available charging stations into a dynamic spatial network. Based on the vehicle’s destination, SoC, and driving speed, infeasible stations are first filtered out to ensure feasibility. ACO algorithm is then applied to identify viable routing alternatives and gather detailed information from candidate stations. Finally, a multi-objective optimization using the NSGA-II algorithm selects the most suitable charging station by balancing charging cost, renewable energy utilization, slot availability, and travel constraints. Once the optimal station is selected, the system confirms the reservation and locks the corresponding charging slot, preventing double booking and ensuring coordinated operation.

Mathematical framework for intelligent EV charging station selection

The proposed intelligent application operates in real time, integrating data collection, routing algorithms, and multi-objective optimization to recommend the most suitable EV charging infrastructure. The system follows these main stages :

Forecasted inputs for PV and EV energy consumption

In this article, PV availability and EV energy consumption forecasts are used as inputs and are not developed or evaluated here. PV forecasts are obtained from “Enhanced PV Power Prediction Using a Hybrid AI–Physical Model with BLSTM and Spatiotemporal Attention,” which achieved high predictive accuracy (R2 ≈ 0.98), and robustness was tested with forecast perturbations of ± 2%, ± 5%, and ± 10%. For EV energy consumption, we adopt recent empirical studies reporting strong real-world performance: Hussain et al.47 demonstrated accurate EV energy consumption prediction using machine learning and ensemble approaches with R2 ≈ 0.96, and a comparative evaluation of machine-learning models for EV energy prediction appeared48; robustness was similarly evaluated with perturbations of ± 2%, ± 5%, and ± 10%. In this work, only the resulting forecasted PV and EV consumption outputs are integrated into the charging station selection and optimization framework, while the detailed forecasting methodologies and validations are provided in the referenced studies.

Real-time data acquisition via MQTT

Initially, the system establishes communication with all registered charging infrastructures using the MQTT protocol over 4G networks. MQTT is a lightweight and efficient messaging protocol commonly used in IoT environments, making it well-suited for real-time data exchange between mobile electric vehicles and distributed charging stations.

In addition to route efficiency, feasibility is evaluated based on the electric vehicle’s battery state of charge (SoC). For each candidate path, the required energy to travel from node \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$i$$\end{document} to node \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$j$$\end{document} is estimated, and the corresponding required SoC is calculated as:4\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$So{C}_{required}=\frac{{E}_{ij}}{{C}_{battery}}\times 100\mathrm{\%}$$\end{document}where \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${E}_{ij}$$\end{document} represents the energy required to traverse the edge and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${C}_{battery}$$\end{document} denotes the total battery capacity.

A path is considered feasible only if the current battery level satisfies the following condition:\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$So{C}_{current}\ge So{C}_{required}$$\end{document} and the destination charging station has at least one available charging slot \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${S}_{j}>0$$\end{document}.

To further guarantee reachability, only charging stations satisfying the following condition are considered:5\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$So{C}_{v}-{d}_{i}\cdot {C}_{per\_km}>5\mathrm{\%}$$\end{document}

This constraint ensures that the vehicle maintains a minimum safety energy reserve after reaching the charging station.

For each reachable charging station, the system estimates the expected arrival time \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${t}_{i}$$\end{document}, the required charging duration \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${T}_{charge,i}$$\end{document}, and the corresponding charging cost \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${C}_{i}$$\end{document}. These values are dynamically computed based on road-type-dependent travel speeds as well as the proportion of photovoltaic energy and grid electricity available at the station. The resulting candidate routes are then passed to the multi-objective optimization stage, where charging stations are selected according to cost efficiency, renewable energy utilization, and user convenience.

Morocco provides an ideal setting for testing intelligent EV charging and routing due to growing EV infrastructure and high solar potential. Marrakech offers a mixed urban–suburban network and exceptional solar resources (~ 300 sunny days/year, GHI ≈ 2,100 kWh/m2), supporting PV-integrated charging49. Discussion of results : performance across five scenarios

The five evaluated scenarios demonstrate the effectiveness and robustness of the proposed ACO + NSGA-II framework under realistic operational uncertainties affecting EV routing and charging decisions.In Scenario 1 (Waiting-Time Impact), ACO-based algorithms consistently reduce travel distance compared to A*, resulting in lower arrival time and energy demand. Without waiting-time constraints, ACO and ACO + NSGA-II converge to the same charging station, achieving a balanced solution with high PV contribution (≈70%) and moderate cost (≈33 MAD). When waiting-time constraints are enforced, only ACO + NSGA-II adapts, selecting a station with zero waiting time while maintaining PV contribution above 60%, leading to time savings exceeding one hour compared to A* and noticeable cost reduction. This highlights its ability to dynamically handle operational constraints.

In Scenario 2 (PV Availability Impact), distance-oriented algorithms (A*, ACO) show nearly unchanged charging behavior between day and night, confirming their insensitivity to PV availability. In contrast, ACO + NSGA-II adjusts charger selection when PV becomes unavailable, resulting in a moderate increase in charging time and cost but avoiding extreme degradation. Compared to NSGA-II, ACO + NSGA-II achieves substantially shorter charging time (over 100 min gain) under night conditions, demonstrating a better balance between renewable usage and service efficiency.

Scenario 3 (PV Forecasting Error Impact) shows that PV uncertainty mainly affects charging cost rather than station selection. A* remains completely insensitive, always selecting a non-PV charger. ACO and ACO + NSGA-II maintain the same charging station across PV deviations, with PV contribution varying within a high range (≈80–98%), leading to moderate cost fluctuations. NSGA-II maximizes PV usage but does not change station choice, indicating limited spatial sensitivity to PV forecast errors.

In Scenario 4 (Speed Variation Impact), incorporating urban–suburban speed heterogeneity significantly improves arrival time estimation. ACO + NSGA-II consistently achieves lower arrival times, even when the travel distance matches ACO, confirming that speed-aware modeling is critical. This improved mobility performance is obtained while maintaining high PV contribution (above 80%) and lower cost than A* and ACO. Compared to NSGA-II, ACO + NSGA-II slightly reduces PV usage but compensates with shorter travel distance, reflecting a rational mobility–energy trade-off.

Finally, Scenario 5 (EV Consumption Error Impact) highlights that on highways, charging station choice is weakly affected by consumption uncertainty due to limited routing alternatives. Nevertheless, ACO + NSGA-II remains robust, maintaining high PV utilization and stable charging time. Unlike NSGA-II, which may select energy-risky solutions under high consumption error, ACO + NSGA-II preserves SOC feasibility by choosing a closer charger, accepting a small PV loss in exchange for operational safety.

Conclusion

The proposed framework can be extended to large-scale fleet scenarios, such as shared mobility or logistics operations, by addressing computational, communication, and coordination challenges. Computational scalability can be achieved through decentralized or parallelized algorithms, allowing vehicles or clusters to make decisions independently. Communication overhead can be minimized using hierarchical or edge-based architectures that share only essential aggregated data, while coordination among vehicles can be ensured via distributed optimization or multi-agent strategies. Predictive scheduling, adaptive policies, and demand management mechanisms help maintain system stability under high charging demand, preventing congestion and ensuring efficient fleet operation.

This study provides a comprehensive assessment of a hybrid ACO-NSGA-II framework for the optimal selection of EV charging stations under realistic conditions in the regions surrounding Marrakech and Casablanca. By integrating ACO-based distance optimization with NSGA-II’s multi-objective capabilities, the proposed framework effectively balances multiple criteria, including travel distance, arrival time, charging cost, waiting time, and PV energy utilization. Evaluated across five representative scenarios the ACO + NSGA-II approach consistently outperformed traditional algorithms such as A*, standalone ACO, and NSGA-II, demonstrating superior operational efficiency and environmental benefits.

In intercity travel scenarios with waiting-time constraints, the algorithm dynamically selects charging stations to minimize delays while maintaining PV energy contributions above 60%, resulting in substantial reductions in both travel and charging durations compared to the A* algorithm. Within urban and suburban contexts, the method adapts to fluctuations in PV availability and heterogeneous driving speeds, thereby decreasing arrival times and sustaining high levels of PV utilization (approximately 70–98%). Moreover, even in the presence of PV forecasting errors or uncertainties in highway energy consumption, the ACO + NSGA-II framework ensures operational safety and feasible energy management, achieving CO₂ emission reductions ranging from 3.2 to 4.1 kg per trip based on Morocco’s grid emission factors. Collectively, these results highlight the robustness, adaptability, and multi-objective optimization efficacy of the proposed framework for practical EV routing and charging applications.

The framework can be further extended to large-scale fleet scenarios, such as shared mobility or logistics operations, by addressing computational, communication, and coordination challenges. Computational scalability can be achieved through decentralized or parallelized algorithms, enabling vehicles or clusters to make independent decisions. Communication overhead can be reduced using hierarchical or edge-based architectures that share only essential aggregated data, while coordination among vehicles can be ensured via distributed optimization or multi-agent strategies. Additionally, predictive scheduling, adaptive policies, and demand management mechanisms can help maintain system stability under high charging demand, preventing congestion and ensuring efficient fleet operation.

Limitations and future work

Future work could focus on extending this framework in several directions. First, integrating real-time traffic data, dynamic energy pricing, and real-time station availability would allow the algorithm to adapt to fluctuating urban conditions and optimize both cost and waiting time for EV users. Second, expanding the study to larger EV fleets would help evaluate system performance under high congestion, collective charging demand, and potential communication or computational bottlenecks, providing insight into practical deployment requirements. Third, incorporating vehicle-to-grid (V2G) interactions, battery degradation models, and renewable energy variability would enable a more holistic assessment of long-term sustainability, grid impact, and operational feasibility. Finally, testing the approach across other Moroccan cities or international urban networks would further demonstrate its scalability, generalizability, and readiness for real-world implementation.

On scaling factor for 28 Si + 12 C charge-changing cross sections at 90-1296 MeV/nucleon: Toward finding energy-dependent scaling factor for other elements
Z. Hasan 1, M. Imran 2,3, A. A. Usmani 3, Z. A. Khan 3

Abstract
Based on the detailed study of the scaling factor for 28 Si + 12 C charge-changing cross sections (CCCSs) at 90-1296 MeV/nucleon, we introduced a new approach to find the systematic energy dependence of the scaling factor that can be used to predict the CCCSs for other elements. The analysis considers the study of charge-changing cross sections for 7,9-12,14 Be, 10-15,17 B, 12-19 C, 14,15,,17-22 N, 16,18-24 O, 18-21,23-26 F, and 42-51 Ca isotopes at 200-930 MeV/nucleon in the framework of the Glauber model. The calculations involve descriptions of nuclei in terms of the Slater determinant using harmonic oscillator single-particle wave functions. The extracted values of the scaling factor are found to provide satisfactory account of the experimental data in all the cases, except for 42-51 Ca isotopes where the calculated values differ significantly from the measured values. In the case of Ca isotopes, however, we find that inclusion of the phase variation of the nucleon-nucleon (NN) scattering amplitude, which is the basic input of the Glauber model, is able to provide an alternative way to serve the purpose of (phenomenological) scaling, and one gets systematically quite a good agreement between theoretical predictions and experiment. Additionally, the present work for finding the scaling factor may find its place to (i) investigate the suitability of the calculated proton radii of the exotic nuclei, given in the literature, and (ii) provide the reliable estimate for the CCCSs of those isotopes of a given element whose calculated charge radii are known, but their experimental CCCSs are not available.

Introduction

It is now well understood that the production of unstable nuclei using the Radioactive Ion Beam (RIB) facility finds a wider range of applications in nuclear physics (studying nuclear structure and properties of exotic nuclei), nuclear astrophysics (investigating nuclear reactions that occur in stars and steller nucleosynthesis), and nuclear medicine (developing new isotopes for diagnostics and therapies). Further, a method has been established to accurately determine the matter radii of a wide range of stable and unstable nuclei by measuring interaction cross sections using RI beams
1

Such measurements have revealed some unique properties of unstable nuclei such as the existence of thick neutron skins and halos

II
Formulation

According to the conventional Glauber model (GM), the elastic S-matrix element,
S_{el}
, for a nucleus-nucleus collision is written as

S_{el}(b)=\langle\psi_{T}\psi_{P}|\prod^{A}_{i=1}\prod^{B}_{j=1}[1-\Gamma_{NN}(\vec{b}-\vec{s_{i}}+\vec{s^{{}^{\prime}}_{j}})]|\psi_{P}\psi_{T}\rangle,

(1)

where
\psi_{P}
(
\psi_{T}
) is the ground-state wave function of projectile (target) nucleus,
A

(B)
is the mass number of target (projectile) nucleus,
\vec{b}
is the impact parameter vector perpendicular to the incident momentum,
\vec{s_{i}}

\vec{(s^{{}^{\prime}}_{j}})
are the projections of target (projectile) nucleon coordinates on the impact parameter plane, and
\Gamma_{NN}({b})
is the
NN
profile function, which is related to the
NN
scattering amplitude
f_{NN}(q)
as
follows

\Gamma_{NN}({b})=\frac{1}{2\pi ik}~\int\exp(-i\vec{q}.\vec{b})f_{NN}({q})~d^{2}q,

(2)

where
k
is the incident nucleon momentum corresponding to the projectile kinetic energy per nucleon, and
\vec{q}
is the momentum transfer.

Following the approach of Ahmad
27
, the S-matrix element,
S_{el}({b})
, up to two-body correlation (density) term takes the following form:

S_{el}({b})\approx S_{0}(b)+S_{2}(b),

(3)

where

S_{0}({b})=[1-\Gamma^{NN}_{00}(b)]^{AB},

(4)

and

\displaystyle S_{2}({b})=\Bigl\langle\psi_{T}\psi_{P}\Bigl|\frac{1}{2!}[1-\Gamma^{NN}_{00}(b)]^{AB-2}~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\times\sum^{{}^{\prime}}_{i_{1},j_{1}}\sum^{{}^{\prime}}_{i_{2},j_{2}}\gamma_{i_{1},j_{1}}\gamma_{i_{2},j_{2}}\Bigr|\psi_{P}\psi_{T}\Bigr\rangle,

(5)

with

\gamma_{ij}=\Gamma^{NN}_{00}(\vec{b})-\Gamma_{NN}(\vec{b}-\vec{s_{i}}+\vec{s^{{}^{\prime}}_{j}}),

(6)

and

\Gamma^{NN}_{00}({b})=\int\rho_{T}(\vec{r})~\rho_{P}(\vec{r^{{}^{\prime}}})~\Gamma_{NN}(\vec{b}-\vec{s}+\vec{s^{{}^{\prime}}})~d\vec{r}~d\vec{r^{{}^{\prime}}}.

(7)

The primes on the summation signs in Eq. (
5
) indicate the restriction that two pairs of indices cannot be equal at the same time (for example,
if
i_{1}
=
i_{2}
then
j_{1}\neq j_{2}
and vice versa). The quantities
\rho_{T}
and
\rho_{P}
in Eq. (
7
) are the (one-body) ground state densities of the target and projectile, respectively. Finally, it is to be pointed out that the distinction between protons and neutrons in both the projectile and target has been included in the expression (3), but only in the uncorrelated part (
S_{0}
). This modification considers the use of different values of the parameters for pp and pn scattering amplitudes and involves different density distributions for protons and neutrons in the colliding nuclei. However, the two-body correlation term (
S_{2}
) involves the average behavior of pp and pn interactions, and considers matter density distributions. More explicitly, the evaluation of
S_{0}({b})
and
S_{2}({b})
leads to the following expressions:

\displaystyle S_{0}({b})=[1-\Gamma^{pp}_{00}({b})]^{Z_{P}Z_{T}}[1-\Gamma^{np}_{00}({b})]^{N_{P}Z_{T}}~~~

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\times[1-\Gamma^{pn}_{00}({b})]^{Z_{P}N_{T}}[1-\Gamma^{nn}_{00}({b})]^{N_{P}N_{T}},

(8)

\displaystyle S_{2}({b})=-\frac{AB}{8\pi^{2}k^{2}}[1-\Gamma^{NN}_{00}(b)]^{AB-2}[(A-1)(B-1)

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\times(G_{22}(b)-G_{00}^{2}(b))+(B-1)

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\times(G_{21}(b)-G_{00}^{2}(b))+(A-1)

\displaystyle~~~~~~\times(G_{12}(b)-G_{00}^{2}(b))],

(9)

with

\Gamma^{ij}_{00}({b})=\int\rho^{j}_{T}(\vec{r}_{j})~\rho^{i}_{P}(\vec{r_{i}^{{}^{\prime}}})~\Gamma_{ij}(\vec{b}-\vec{s}_{j}+\vec{s_{i}^{{}^{\prime}}})~d\vec{r}_{j}~d\vec{r_{i}^{{}^{\prime}}},

(10)

where
Z_{T}

(Z_{P})
and
N_{T}

(N_{P})
are the target (projectile) atomic and neutron number, respectively, and each of
i
and
j
stands for a proton and a neutron. For the evaluation of
G_{22}(b),G_{21}(b),G_{12}(b)
, and
G_{00}(b)
, we refer readers to follow the work presented in Ref.
18
.

II.1
Charge-changing cross section

As mentioned earlier, the Glauber model calculations for charge-changing cross section (
\sigma_{cc}
) suggest
6
;
13
;
14
;
15
;
16
;
17
that in addition to the protons in the projectile, the partial involvement of the projectile neutrons makes it difficult to understand the reaction mechanism for charge-changing cross section. However, keeping in mind the very purpose of CCCS measurements, it was suggested that calculations of CCCS may be possible by introducing a phenomenological scaling factor
6
;
13
;
18
;
24
;
28
that can consider the effect of the presence of neutrons in the projectile. Under this approach, the charge-changing cross section is expressed as follows:

\sigma_{cc}=\epsilon(E)~\sigma^{p}_{cc},

(11)

where
\epsilon(E)
is the scaling factor which, in general, is energy dependent and is defined as the ratio of the experimental
\sigma_{cc}
and calculated
\sigma^{p}_{cc}
values (
\sigma^{exp}_{cc}/\sigma^{p}_{cc}
). The quantity
\sigma^{p}_{cc}
is the contribution to the charge-changing cross section due to the scattering of only projectile protons. In connection with the scaling factor
\epsilon(E)
, we need to mention that, in our recent works
18
;
29
, we have adopted a procedure that uses its similar value for each of the isotopes of the given element for a particular range of incident energies. However, in this work, we attempt to develop a different prescription, described in the next section, that can provide energy dependent scaling factor even for a small difference in the energies of the isotopes of a given element in a particular energy range. Thus, we need to mention only the calculation of
\sigma^{p}_{cc}
in order to get the results for
\sigma_{cc}
. Following Eq. (
3
), the elastic S-matrix element,
S^{p}_{el}
, that considers only the projectile protons, is given by

S^{p}_{el}({b})\approx S^{p}_{0}(b)+S^{p}_{2}(b).

(12)

The quantities
S^{p}_{0}(b)
and
S^{p}_{2}(b)
that consider only the projectile protons can be obtained by putting
N_{P}
= 0 in the respective expressions for
S_{0}(b)
[Eq. (
8
)] and
S_{2}(b)
[Eq. (
9
)]. This simplification leads to the following expressions for
S^{p}_{0}(b)
and
S^{p}_{2}(b)
:

S^{p}_{0}(b)=[1-\Gamma^{pp}_{00}(b)]^{Z_{P}Z_{T}}[1-\Gamma^{pn}_{00}(b)]^{Z_{P}N_{T}},

(13)

\displaystyle S^{p}_{2}(b)=-\frac{AZ_{P}}{8\pi^{2}k^{2}}(1-\Gamma^{NN}_{00}(b))^{AZ_{P}-2}[(A-1)(Z_{P}-1)

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\times(G_{22}(b)-G^{2}_{00}(b))+(Z_{P}-1)

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\times(G_{21}(b)-G^{2}_{00}(b))+(A-1)~~

\displaystyle~~~~\times(G_{12}(b)-G^{2}_{00}(b))],

(14)

The quantities
G_{22}
,
G_{21}
,
G_{12}
, and
G_{00}
in the above equation assume similar expressions as in Eq. (
9
), but now they involve only the projectile proton density instead of projectile matter density. Here, it should be mentioned that above expressions are valid for conventional Glauber model (GM) calculations. In the case of optical-limit approximation of the Glauber model (OLA)
30
, the elastic S-matrix element,
S^{p}_{el}
, takes the form

S^{p}_{el}({b})\approx S^{p}_{0}(b),

(15)

where

S^{p}_{0}(b)=\exp\left[Z_{P}Z_{T}\Gamma^{pp}_{00}(b)+Z_{P}N_{T}\Gamma^{pn}_{00}(b)\right].

(16)

Further, it should be noted that the calculations for
\sigma_{cc}
were also performed in the zero range optical limit approximation of the Glauber model (ZROLA). Such calculation also involve similar expression for
S^{p}_{el}({b})
as in Eq. (
15
), but the definition of
\Gamma_{NN}(b)
in ZROLA is not the same as in GM and OLA; this point is further discussed in the next section.

With these considerations, the charge-changing cross section for a nucleus-nucleus collision, that considers only the projectile protons, is calculated using the equation

\sigma_{cc}^{p}=2\pi\int\left[1-|S^{p}_{el}(b)|^{2}\right]b~db.

(17)

(ii) Nucleon-nucleon (NN) amplitude: The NN scattering amplitude
f_{NN}(q)
is usually parametrized in the form
33

f_{NN}(\vec{q})=\frac{ik\sigma_{NN}}{4\pi}(1-i\rho_{NN})\exp\left(-\beta_{NN}q^{2}/2\right).

(18)

It consists of three parameters;
\sigma_{NN}
,
\rho_{NN}
, and
\beta_{NN}
. The values of these parameters at the desired energies (90-1296 MeV) have been obtained by a linear interpolation and extrapolation of their values given in .
33
. From calculation point of view, it should be mentioned that the conventional Glauber model (GM) and optical limit approximation of the Glauber model (OLA) involve the above form of the NN amplitude, which gives the following expression for the NN profile function
\Gamma_{NN}({b})

\Gamma_{NN}({b})=\frac{\sigma_{NN}}{4\pi\beta_{NN}}~(1-i\rho_{NN})\exp\left[-b^{2}/(2\beta_{NN})\right].

(19)

Whereas, in the zero range optical limit approximation of the Glauber model (ZROLA), the NN amplitude [Eq. (
18
)] assumes
\beta_{NN}
=0, and one gets

\Gamma_{NN}({b})=\frac{\sigma_{NN}(1-i\rho_{NN})}{2}~\delta(b).

(20)

IV
Summary and Conclusions

In this work, we have presented a theoretical study of the charge-changing cross sections (CCCSs) for Be, B, C, N, O, F, Si, and Ca isotopes on a
12
C target in the Glauber model, involving a phenomenological scaling factor to accommodate the effect of the presence of projectile neutrons in the calculation of CCCS. The calculations have been performed in three steps: (i) In the first step, we made detailed study of the
28
Si +
12
C charge-changing cross sections at 90-1296 MeV/nucleon and obtained the energy dependence of the scaling factor in the said energy range. (ii) Based on these results, we, in the second step, proposed a method that can be used to find the scaling factor for the charge-changing cross sections of other elements. The calculations included the study of CCCSs for the isotopes of Be, B, C, N, O, F, and Ca at 200-930 MeV/nucleon. It is found that our method to find the scaling facor for the isotopes of Be, B, C, N, O, F, and Ca works well to account for the CCCSs in all the cases, except for Ca isotopes where the calculated values differ significantly from the measured values. (iii) Finally, we have reexamined the anomaly observed in the case of Ca isotopes and tried to find an alternative to the scaling factor approach so that one can also accommodate the CCCSs for Ca isotopes on a
12
C target at
\sim
300 MeV/nucleon. In this connection, it is found that inclusion of the phase variation of the NN amplitude, which is the basic input of the Glauber model, is able to provide an alternative way to serve the purpose of (phenomenological) scaling, and one gets quite a satisfactory account of the Ca data.

In conclusion, we found that the study of
28
Si +
12
C charge-changing cross sections at 90-1296 MeV/nucleon provides a basis
to find the energy-dependent scaling factor for studying the CCCSs of other elements. The extracted scaling factor is found to provide quite
a satisfactory account of the CCCS data for Be, B, C, N, O, and F isotopes at 200-930 MeV/nucleon. Unfortunately, we noticed that the similar procedure for obtaining the scaling factor does not work for the CCCSs of Ca isotopes at
\sim
300 MeV/nucleon, and, at present, we do not have any plausible explanation for this anomaly, except to find some other way that can accommodate the CCCS data for Ca isotopes. In this connection, we found that the consideration of the phase of the NN amplitude seems to be an alternative to the scaling factor approach in providing a satisfactory explanation of the CCCS data for Ca isotopes. Overall, the present work seems promising in providing the energy-dependent scaling factor, needed in the study of nucleus-nucleus CCCS, involving only the projectile protons. Still we need more data on CCCS for other elements and also at a wider range of incident energies, in order to assess the usefulness of the scaling factor approach as adopted in this work.

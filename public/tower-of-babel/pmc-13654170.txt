Systematic review of spectral computed tomography quantitative parameters for predicting EGFR mutation status in non-small cell lung cancer
Authors: Jiapeng Li, Lihua Gao, Xinyao Jiao, Fan Xia, Ningxia Jia, Yuanbo Huang, Shuang Wang, Shengkai Sun, Sa Huang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundEpidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) gene mutations are important therapeutic targets in non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC). Spectral computed tomography (spectral CT) has shown promise in the non-invasive prediction of EGFR status; however, the diagnostic value of these parameters remains unclear. This systematic review aimed to evaluate the utility of key quantitative spectral CT parameters for predicting EGFR mutation status in NSCLC.MethodsA comprehensive search of several Chinese and English databases was conducted to retrieve relevant diagnostic studies published up to October 1, 2025 that used pathology as the gold standard to assess the predictive value of spectral CT quantitative parameters for EGFR mutation detection in NSCLC. Two researchers conducted the literature screening and data extraction independently. Due to study heterogeneity, results were synthesized narratively. Risk of bias was assessed using the Quality Assessment of Diagnostic Accuracy Studies 2 (QUADAS-2) tool.ResultsThe quantitative parameters of spectral CT, including iodine concentration (IC), normalized iodine concentration (NIC), slope of the spectral Hounsfield unit curve (λHU), and effective atomic number (Zeff), were higher in the EGFR-mutant cohort than in the wild-type cohort (P<0.05). Multi-parameter models combining quantitative parameters with clinical and imaging features showed improved diagnostic performance. The results were unaffected by the various brands of CT scanners and their scanning parameters.ConclusionsSeveral quantitative parameters derived from spectral CT are associated with EGFR mutations in NSCLC. Predictive models integrating these quantitative parameters with imaging features and clinical variables may further improve diagnostic efficacy. This may inform epidermal growth factor receptor tyrosine kinase inhibitor (EGFR-TKI) planing. Large-scale prospective studies are needed to validate these models.

Introduction

With approximately 2.5 million new lung cancer cases and more than 1.8 million lung cancer-related deaths each year, lung cancer is the most prevalent type of cancer and the leading cause of cancer-related death worldwide (1). It accounts for 12.4% of all cancer diagnoses and 18.7% of all cancer-related deaths (1). Non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) represents approximately 85% of all lung cancer cases. In Caucasian populations, the prevalence of epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR)-activating mutations in NSCLC is approximately 10–20%, while in Asian patients, this figure is around 50% (2). The National Comprehensive Cancer Network guidelines for NSCLC strongly recommend simultaneous molecular biomarker testing at diagnosis, including EGFR mutation analysis, to assess eligibility for targeted therapy (3). Spectral computed tomography (spectral CT) improves the accuracy of NSCLC staging by integrating anatomical and metabolic data (4). It guides the selection of biopsy sites and helps determine the suitability of surgical or radiotherapeutic interventions, playing a critical role in the diagnosis and management of NSCLC.

EGFR-targeted therapy, including epidermal growth factor receptor tyrosine kinase inhibitor (EGFR-TKI) therapy, markedly prolongs progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival in patients with NSCLC by precisely inhibiting mutation-driven signaling pathways (5). This treatment enhances objective response rates and quality of life, establishing it as the preferred first-line option for patients with EGFR mutation-positive NSCLC (3). EGFR-TKIs are primarily indicated for metastatic NSCLC patients harboring EGFR mutations, particularly exon 19 deletions or L858R substitutions, especially in cases of lung adenocarcinoma. They may also be used as adjuvant therapy following surgery in early-stage NSCLC (6).

A study involving 22 randomized controlled trials demonstrated that in patients with locally advanced or metastatic EGFR-mutated NSCLC who had not undergone prior chemotherapy, EGFR-TKIs significantly improved PFS compared with standard chemotherapy (7). Spectral CT provides quantitative data on blood flow and tissue composition, facilitating the non-invasive prediction of EGFR mutations and the assessment of targeted therapy effectiveness (8). Several studies have examined the relationship between medical imaging features and EGFR mutations to improve non-invasive methods in situations where tissue samples are inadequate, patients are in compromised health, or tumor location is unsuitable for biopsy (9,10). These imaging-based assessments may also guide adjuvant therapy targeting EGFR gene mutations.

Spectral CT, as an emerging imaging technique, offers various quantitative parameters. For example, the slope of the spectral Hounsfield unit curve (λHU) represents the rate at which tissue attenuation characteristics change with energy at different energy levels. Iodine concentration (IC) indicates the absolute content of iodine contrast agent within the tissue. Normalized iodine concentration (NIC) is obtained by normalizing the lesion IC value by the IC value in the aorta (or axillary artery), eliminating individual variations. Effective atomic number (Zeff) distinguishes different tissues based on variations in atomic type and proportion. Water concentration (WC) represents the absolute content of water in the tissue. These quantitative parameters can be used to evaluate changes in NSCLC tumor cells influenced by EGFR mutation pathways (11,12).

Research indicates that EGFR mutations lead to ligand-independent receptor activation via structural changes, subsequently driving key signaling pathways, including phosphoinositide 3-kinase (PI3K)/protein kinase B (AKT), rat sarcoma (RAS) virus/rapidly accelerated fibrosarcoma (RAF)/mitogen-activated protein kinase (MEK)/extracellular signal-regulated kinase (ERK), and signal transducer and activator of transcription (STAT) (13-15). Collectively, these pathways provide benefits to cancer cells in terms of proliferation, angiogenesis, invasion, and metabolism, facilitating the accelerated growth and progression of NSCLC. These parameters may reflect angiogenesis and alterations in the cellular composition of neoplastic cells in NSCLC. Study have shown that NIC is significantly elevated in highly vascularized NSCLC lesions and their metastatic lymph nodes, suggesting that it may serve as an imaging surrogate for microvessel density(16). Further, Zeff and λHU enhance the sensitivity in evaluating vascular permeability and blood volume. NSCLC with EGFR expression typically shows significant and heterogeneous enhancement on contrast-enhanced CT, which is correlated with highly proliferative activity. Thus, λHU may be useful in assessing this characteristic (17).

Recent studies indicate that quantitative parameters derived from spectral CT are promising in the non-invasive evaluation of EGFR mutation status in NSCLC. Multimodal predictive models that combine clinical variables with radiomics features have further improved diagnostic efficacy (18). Research has largely focused on twopatient populations: first, patients with early-stage, operable NSCLC, in whom post-operative targeted therapy decisions depend on mutation information for effective guidance; and second, patients with advanced-stage NSCLC, in whom repeated invasive pathological biopsies presents considerable challenges, highlighting a significant clinical need for non-invasive detection methods.

The fundamental quantitative parameters for the application of spectral CT in research primarily include IC or NIC, λHU, and Zeff. However, inconsistencies in parameters and thresholds across previous studies have hindered the establishment of a standardized predictive criterion for EGFR mutation status based on spectral CT parameters. Reported diagnostic thresholds for EGFR mutations vary significantly across studies, with NIC cut-off values ranging from 0.15 to 0.41, λHU from 2.13 to 2.75, and IC from 1.2 to 2.5 mg/mL (18-21). These inconsistencies may stem from the use of different CT scanners, variabilities in image acquisition protocols, and differing definitions of regions of interest (ROIs) across research.

Currently, numerous quantitative parameters derived from spectral CT are used to predict EGFR mutation status in NSCLC, including IC, NIC, Zeff, WC, and λHU. However, the reported diagnostic efficacy of these parameters varies across studies, making it challenging for clinicians to select the most appropriate parameters for EGFR mutation prediction. This study aimed to systematically evaluate the predictive performance of these parameters, clarify the conditions under which they can be effectively applied, and identify stable, independent parameters that may serve as diagnostic reference standards for EGFR mutations. Additionally, the study sought to explore the integration of these parameters to develop a feasible, accurate, and stable predictive model. The study findings may provide valuable, imaging-based evidence to support future clinical decision-making regarding the initiation of EGFR-TKI therapy or treatment plan adjustment. We present this article in accordance with the PRISMA reporting checklist (22) (available at https://qims.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/qims-2025-1-2542/rc).

Methods

Search strategy

The PubMed, Web of Science, Cochrane Library, Embase, Scopus, and China National Knowledge Infrastructure databases were searched to retrieve relevant articles using the following keywords: “Spectral CT”, “spectral computed tomography”, “DECT”, “dual-energy CT”, “dual-energy computed tomography”, “NSCLC”, “non-small cell lung cancer”, “lung adenocarcinoma”, and “lung squamous cell carcinoma”. The Boolean operators “OR” and “AND” were employed to combine these terms. A systematic search was conducted to retrieve all relevant articles, published up to October 1, 2025, that met the inclusion criteria. The following exhaustive search strategy was employed in PubMed: ((“Spectral CT” OR “spectral computed tomography” OR “DECT” OR “dual-energy CT” OR “dual-energy computed tomography”) AND (NSCLC OR “non-small cell lung cancer” OR “lung adenocarcinoma” OR “lung squamous cell carcinoma”)). The reference lists of all the included articles were then carefully reviewed to identify any additional relevant studies.

Study selection

The inclusion criteria were as follows: adult patients with suspected NSCLC who had not previously undergone anticancer treatment, who had undergone spectral CT imaging, and who were subsequently diagnosed with NSCLC through surgery or biopsy. EGFR mutation genetic testing was performed after the histopathological diagnosis of NSCLC.

Excluded published articles comprised reviews, meta-analyses, case reports, conference abstracts, letters to the editor, comments, posters, technical reports, duplicate studies, non-human studies, and articles from which data could not be extracted or full texts could not be obtained. Further, original research articles were excluded if they only employed spectral CT imaging features without reporting quantitative values.

The data were independently extracted by two researchers using a pre-designed data extraction form. This form included information on study design, sample size, imaging parameters, EGFR mutation analysis methods, and key findings related to spectral CT. To ensure consistency and accuracy, any disagreements during the data extraction process were resolved by a third researcher.

Data extraction

Two researchers independently performed an initial screening of the study titles and abstracts to assess preliminary eligibility. Each researcher evaluated whether the studies met the inclusion criteria. Studies deemed eligible by both researchers then underwent full-text review, and relevant data on spectral CT and EGFR mutations were extracted. Any disagreements regarding eligibility or data extraction were resolved by consensus, with a third researcher consulted as necessary.

Quality assessment

The Quality Assessment of Diagnostic Accuracy Studies 2 (QUADAS-2) methodology was employed to rigorously evaluate the quality of the included studies (23), focusing on risk of bias and applicability concerns across four principal domains: (I) patient selection; (II) index test; (III) reference standard; and (IV) flow and timing. According to the research findings, each domain was classified as having a “low”, “high”, or “unclear” risk of bias. This standardized technique ensured clarity and uniformity in the quality assessment process.

Statistical analysis

Due to significant differences across the included studies, different CT scanner brands were used, including Siemens, GE, and Philips, each employing different spectral CT technical solutions. For example, Siemens dual‑energy CT systems use two X‑ray tubes that simultaneously emit radiation at different voltages, while GE CT systems use a single X‑ray tube with rapid kV switching. Additionally, the scanning protocols varied across the medical centers according to their needs. For example, the arterial phase (AP) acquisition time ranged from 25 to 35 seconds, while the venous phase (VP) acquisition time ranged from 50 to 65 seconds. Some studies also employed contrast agent bolus tracking technology, placing the ROIs in the descending aorta and initiating automatic scanning after a predefined trigger threshold was reached, although the trigger thresholds and delay times differed among studies. Moreover, the type, concentration, and injection rate of non-ionic contrast agents varied across medical centers, with concentrations ranging from 300 to 400 mg I/mL, and injection rates of 2.5 and 3 mL/s. Further, various ROI methods were employed for the analysis of quantitative parameters. For example, some studies used circular ROIs on the largest tumor layer, others outlined the tumor boundary, and some used tumor volume as the ROI, all of which added to the heterogeneity of the results. The EGFR mutation detection methods also varied across studies, including amplification refractory mutation system-polymerase chain reaction (ARMS-PCR), real-time polymerase chain reaction (PCR), immunohistochemistry (IHC), and fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH), and some studies did not specify the exact method, potentially leading to discrepancies in the accuracy of the results.

As such differences may lead to significant heterogeneity, a meta-analysis was deemed unsuitable. Therefore, a qualitative systematic review was conducted. The main findings of each study were systematically described and summarized, focusing on the correlation between various quantitative parameters (e.g., IC, NIC, and λHU) and EGFR mutation status, and common trends were identified to present the existing research results more comprehensively and objectively.

Results

Search results

The preliminary electronic literature search identified 836 studies. After removing duplicates using Zotero software, 512 studies remained. Following title and abstract screening, 474 studies were excluded for not meeting the inclusion criteria, leaving 38 articles for full-text assessment. Of these articles, 10 were excluded because they did not examine EGFR gene mutations in NSCLC, twobecause they relied exclusively on radiomic characteristics without reporting quantitative data, and two due to duplication. Thus, ultimately, 24 studies met all the inclusion criteria and were included in this systematic review. The PRISMA flow diagram (Figure 1) illustrates the study selection process. The reference lists of all the included articles were also carefully reviewed, but no additional relevant studies were identified.

Flow diagram of the study selection process.

This comprehensive analysis included 24 studies that used quantitative spectral CT parameters to predict EGFR mutation status in patients with NSCLC (8,18-21,24-42). Among these, two adopted prospective designs and 22 adopted retrospective designs, and all the studies were conducted in Asia. The sample sizes of the included studies ranged from 18 to 208 participants.

The majority of studies (n=22) employed statistical approaches based on a stringent modeling methodology. Preliminary univariate analyses, including t-tests, Mann-Whitney U tests, Chi-squared tests, and Wilcoxon rank-sum tests, revealed significant associations between the spectral CT quantitative parameters and EGFR mutation status. The parameters analyzed included IC, NIC, Zeff, λHU, and CT values obtained from virtual monoenergetic images (e.g., 40 keV or 70 keV). Multivariate logistic regression was then used to develop predictive models, and the discriminatory performance of these models was assessed by receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis. Two studies used different statistical approaches. Yanagawa et al. [2016] examined associations between parameters and mutation status exclusively using Spearman’s rank correlation analysis and linear regression, without developing models or performing ROC validation. While Wu et al. [2018] used t-tests solely for intergroup comparisons.

In terms of imaging equipment, GE Discovery series scanners were used in the majority of studies (15), followed by Siemens SOMATOM series scanners (6), and Philips Spectra CT scanners (3). The principal techniques for EGFR mutation detection were ARMS-PCR and real-time quantitative PCR, which were used in 16studies. A smaller number of studies used IHC/FISH techniques (two studies) or next-generation sequencing (one study), while five studies did not report their mutation detection methods. Among the quantitative parameters, the most extensively examined were IC and NIC, which were used in 18 studies for EGFR mutation prediction, followed closely by Zeff, which was used in 15 studies. Other parameters included virtual monoenergetic images at low energy levels (40 keV or 70 keV), which were examined in 15 studies, λHU, which was assessed in 18 studies, and WC, which was assessed in seven studies. The widespread application of these quantitative parameters highlights their pivotal role in the non-invasive evaluation of tumor heterogeneity and physiological characteristics, with IC/NIC, λHU, and Zeff identified as the most commonly used and reliably documented biomarkers in the current literature.

A total of 12 studies used multimodal integrated modeling techniques to predict EGFR mutation status in lung cancer. These models integrated three categories of predictive factors: quantitative spectral CT parameters (e.g., IC, NIC, and λHU), clinical variables (e.g., sex, smoking history, and age), and imaging features (e.g., CT morphological signs such as lobulation and pleural retraction, as well as radiomic features). These multimodal techniques improved predictive performance, with integrated models achieving area under the curve (AUC) values typically exceeding 0.8, representing an improvement of 0.10 to 0.30 compared with single-parameter models. These findings indicate that combining spectral CT parameters with clinical variables and imaging features is of great value, as it enhances the non-invasive prediction of EGFR mutation status and could guide targeted treatment strategies for NSCLC.

Assessment of methodological quality

Table 2 and Figure 2 present the outcomes of the QUADAS-2 tool evaluation for risk of bias and clinical relevance of the included studies. Overall, the majority of studies showed low risk in both bias and applicability. One study was assessed as having a high risk of bias in the “patient selection” domain due to its small sample size (n=18). Threestudies were assessed as having an “unclear” risk in the “patient selection” applicability domain due to potential case overlap, as all the participants were recruited from the same medical center. Two studies were assessed as having an unclear risk in the “index test” domain, as it was uncertain whether the EGFR test results were known at the time of case inclusion. Additionally, three studies were assessed as having an unclear risk in the “reference standard” domain due to their failure to clearly define the method used for detecting EGFR mutations. Finally, the “flow and timing” domain was assessed as low risk for all the studies.

Key spectral CT quantitative parameters for predicting EGFR gene alterations in NSCLC

A detailed examination of 24 studies revealed sample sizes ranging from 18 to 208 participants. Spectral CT quantitative parameters demonstrated considerable efficacy in predicting EGFR mutations in NSCLC. The primary predictive factors included NIC or IC, identified in 18 studies as key indicators of tumor blood supply; λHU, identified in 17studies as a marker of spectral attenuation properties; and Zeff, identified in ninestudies as an indicator of tissue elemental composition. Among the 24 studies, 10 studies established specific diagnostic thresholds, all determined through ROC curve analysis to improve diagnostic sensitivity and specificity. Correlation between IC and EGFR gene mutations in NSCLC

A thorough analysis of 19 studies (sample sizes ranging from 18 to 208) revealed that spectral CT IC measures, including IC and NIC, exhibited strong consistency and considerable diagnostic utility in predicting EGFR gene mutations. Eighteen studies reported that the IC and NIC values were significantly higher in the EGFR-mutated cohort than in the non-mutated cohort (P<0.05). Yanagawa et al. [2016] found no significant association between these two parameters, which may be due to the limited study sample size of only 18 cases. Increased IC and NIC values reflect the biological characteristics of EGFR-mutated tumors, including increased blood supply and high microvascular density, particularly apparent in VP measurements. In comparison to the non-mutant group, the mean NIC value in the mutant group during the VP was increased by approximately 0.09–0.12 mg/cm3 (e.g., Li et al., 2024 reported 0.39±0.10 mg/cm3 in the mutant group compared with 0.25±0.07 mg/cm3 in the wild-type group). ROC curve analysis showed that VP NIC demonstrated superior diagnostic performance, with AUC values consistently exceeding 0.80, and reaching up to 0.892, with a sensitivity, specificity, and accuracy of 78.26%, 86.96%, and 80.44%, respectively. These findings highlight the efficacy of IC metrics in supporting the prediction of EGFR mutation status.

Nine other studies established specific diagnostic thresholds for the IC or NIC parameters, with IC values obtained during both the AP and VP using spectral CT. The cut-off values for VP NIC ranged from 0.22 to 0.365 mg/cm3, while the cut-off values for AP NIC ranged from 0.13 to 0.175 mg/cm3. However, these findings were derived using different CT scanner systems, resulting in disparate threshold distributions and a lack of standardized criteria. Correlation between λHU and EGFR mutation status in NSCLC

A thorough analysis of 16 studies (sample sizes ranging from 49 and 208) revealed that the λHU value obtained from spectral CT showed substantial consistency and diagnostic efficacy in predicting EGFR gene mutations. Notably, the λHU values were significantly higher in the EGFR mutation group than in the wild-type group (P<0.05). The energy range employed for curve slope estimates differed across studies, with the 40–100keV range (10 studies, 62.5%) being more common than the 40–70 keV range (sixstudies, 37.5%). However, even within the same energy range, λHU values still fluctuated (e.g., λHU values in the mutation group ranged from 2.02 to 2.66 within the 40–100 keV range), likely due to differences in the scanner models, scanning techniques, and population variability.

Comparative analyses of the AP and VP parameters revealed that the VP parameters generally showed superior diagnostic efficacy, as evidenced by slightly higher AUC values for the VP parameters compared with the AP parameters (e.g., Li et al. 2024: VP λHU AUC =0.756 vs. AP λHU AUC =0.699), along with higher sensitivity and specificity. This may be attributed to the enhanced ability of CT imaging to detect hemodynamic characteristics during the VP. Several studies established thresholds (range: 1.47–3.45); however, considerable differences in their distribution was observed. Discussion

This systematic investigation thoroughly assessed the significance of quantitative spectral CT parameters in predicting EGFR mutation status in NSCLC. The results revealed that spectral CT quantitative parameters, including IC, NIC, λHU, and Zeff, were closely associated with EGFR mutation status in NSCLC. Despite differences in CT scanner models and imaging protocols, all fourparameters were consistently higher in the EGFR-mutated tumors than in the wild-type tumors (P<0.05).

Predictive models including these quantitative parameters showed promising performance, with AUC values ranging from 0.65 to 0.89. However, significant methodological heterogeneity across studies was observed. Differences in CT scanner models and technologies affected the λHU and Zeff measurements. Additionally, differences in the timing of AP and VP image acquisition (e.g., fixed-delay scanning vs. threshold-triggered scanning) and differences in contrast agent injection rates (3–5 mL/s) led to inconsistent ICs in cells, resulting in variations in IC and NIC values. This caused considerable differences in the quantitative parameter values across studies, making it challenging to determine a consistent diagnostic threshold for specific parameters. However, a consistent overall trend linking these spectral CT parameters with EGFR mutation status was identified, demonstrating that spectral CT has potential value in identifying NSCLC patients with EGFR mutations.

The consistent trends observed in NIC, λHU, and Zeff across the studies suggest a potential association between the spectral CT parameters and the molecular mechanisms by which EGFR mutations may promote tumor progression through angiogenesis and modification of the tumor microenvironment. The activation of the EGFR signaling pathway may subsequently enhance downstream pathways, including PI3K/Akt and mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK), thereby facilitating angiogenesis and increasing vascular permeability (43). This, in turn, may lead to significant extravasation of iodine-based contrast agents into the extracellular space (44), resulting in increased IC values on spectral CT. Further, as a high atomic number element (Z=53), iodine accumulation in the microenvironment enhances the spatial correlation of Zeff. Although methodological differences across studies limit the establishment of standardized diagnostic thresholds for EGFR mutation detection, spectral CT parameters show promise as qualitative or semi-quantitative imaging biomarkers. Future research should further explore the consistent trends observed in these parameters to develop device-independent or protocol-compatible standardized metric systems. This may involve parameter standardization via multicenter cooperation or the development of machine learning-based prediction models that can be adjusted to diverse scanning settings (45). Only through cross-platform standardization may spectral CT evolve from a research instrument to a conventional clinical diagnostic modality.

Previous studies have improved predictive accuracy by incorporating multidimensional data into predictive algorithms. For example, Ma et al. developed a clinical, DESCT, and radiomics feature combined model architecture that included spectral CT functional parameters, clinical variables (e.g., sex and smoking history), and radiomics features, achieving an AUC of 0.83–0.86 (46). Song et al. combined spectral CT quantitative parameters with the tumor marker carcinoembryonic antigen, resulting in a model with an AUC of 0.967, a sensitivity of 72.4%, and a specificity of 69.0%. These multimodal predictive models exhibited improved diagnostic performance. The conclusions of these studies were reached through consistent statistical methods, including initial univariate screening (t-test/Mann-Whitney U; Chi- square test/Fisher’s exact test), followed by multivariate logistic regression to adjust for confounding factors, and ROC curve analysis to assess diagnostic performance. Multimodal parameter models address tumor heterogeneity and biological complexity.

Epidemiological studies have shown that EGFR mutations are more prevalent in female, non-smoking patients with adenocarcinoma, with these variables serving as independent predictors (47,48). EGFR-mutated tumors frequently appear on CT as low-density lesions with ground-glass opacity and increased vascular structures, features that can be analyzed by radiomics (49,50). Future studies should seek to integrate these indications with spectral CT quantitative parameters such as IC, NIC, λHU, and Zeff to develop holistic multi-parameter predictive models. Complex nonlinear relationships likely exist between these characteristics and EGFR mutation status.

Machine learning algorithms may help to mitigate multicollinearity, identify optimal predictive feature combinations, and develop robust, accurate diagnostic models. This approach could address the inconsistencies in diagnostic efficacy associated with single-parameter models, promote the standardization and unification of spectral CT-based diagnostic techniques, and reduce variability arising from differences in imaging protocols and equipment. It has the potential to provide a reproducible and efficient decision support tool for the non-invasive prediction of EGFR gene mutations in NSCLC and may assist in the selection of targeted therapy regimens.

In patients with NSCLC, the assessment of EGFR mutation status in diseased tissue is the most precise approach for guiding the initiation of targeted therapy. However, tissue biopsy is an invasive procedure associated with a complication rate of 14.2% (51). Moreover, pathological sampling is associated with a false-negative rate of 10–15% (52). Further, patients with NSCLC often present with poor physical condition, making repeated biopsies challenging to tolerate. However, modifications to medication necessitate continuous monitoring of genetic status, presenting therapeutic problems due to the need for repeated invasive procedures. Thus, we established a holistic predictive model that incorporates quantitative CT parameters (IC, NIC, λHU, and Zeff), clinical variables (e.g., age, gender, and smoking status), and radiomic or radiographic features. This approach provides a non-invasive, relatively accurate alternative for NSCLC patients who cannot undergo or are unsuitable for repeated tissue sampling, potentially assisting in treatment decisions related to the initiation or adjustment of targeted therapy.

However, caution must be exercised regarding the study findings. All the included studies were performed within Asian populations, with more than 90% originating from China. Thus, the conclusions and predictive models of this study are largely based on Asian patient data. Given the significant differences in EGFR mutation frequencies across different races, particularly between Asian populations (40–50%) and Caucasian populations (10–15%), the generalizability and external validity of these findings to other racial groups may be limited. Additional validation in multi-ethnic populations is therefore warranted. Although all the included studies focused on NSCLC, the histological subtypes of the tumors varied. Some studies included only lung adenocarcinoma, while others also included squamous cell carcinoma. Additionally, the studies did not have strict requirements regarding disease staging. These factors may influence the spectral CT parameters. Future studies should adopt stricter inclusion criteria to minimize confounding effects, and evaluate the performance of spectral CT separately according to different tumor subtypes and disease stages. Further, most of the studies employed single-center retrospective designs, and four studies relied on the same institutional database. Although the study periods, sample sizes, and research objectives differed, the possibility of patient overlap could not be excluded, potentially introducing data redundancy and affecting the accuracy and validity of the study results. Additionally, only approximately 30% of the studies considered important confounding factors, such as smoking history. Future models need to incorporate these factors to enhance their clinical applicability. Due to significant heterogeneity among the included studies, a meta-analysis or quantitative synthesis was not performed. In addition, no formal assessment of publication bias, such as the Deeks’ test, was conducted. Instead, the QUADAS-2 tool was used to assess the risk of bias in the included diagnostic accuracy studies. However, future studies should aim to address these variations and may benefit from a more standardized approach, enabling a more robust quantitative analysis.

Conclusions

Despite certain limitations, quantitative metrics derived from spectral CT exhibit promising applicability and hold promise for clinical translation in predicting EGFR mutation status in NSCLC. Extensive, prospective, multicenter clinical validation studies need to be conducted to ensure the reliability and generalizability of the study findings. These studies should assess the diagnostic efficacy and stability of this technique across different medical centers and CT systems from various manufacturers, which is essential for clinical adoption. Building on this foundation, the development of integrated models that combine spectral CT quantitative parameters with clinical variables (e.g., smoking history and sex) and radiographic features is of considerable importance. The application of novel methods, such as machine learning techniques, to integrate multidimensional data may substantially improve the precision and reliability of predictive models. This strategy could provide a more robust, non-invasive decision support tool for the individualized and precision treatment of NSCLC. Ultimately, these initiatives seek to advance radiology beyond mere morphological description toward accurate functional and genetic prediction, thereby allowing more patients to benefit from improved diagnosis and personalized therapy.

Time-adaptive SympNets for separable Hamiltonian systems
Konrad Janik, Peter Benner

Abstract
Measurement data is often sampled irregularly i.e. not on equidistant time grids. This is also true for Hamiltonian systems. However, existing machine learning methods, which learn symplectic integrators, such as SympNets [ 20 ] and HénonNets [ 4 ] still require training data generated by fixed step sizes. To learn time-adaptive symplectic integrators, an extension to SympNets, which we call TSympNets, was introduced in [ 20 ] . We adapt the architecture of TSympNets and extend them to non-autonomous Hamiltonian systems. So far the approximation qualities of TSympNets were unknown. We close this gap by providing a universal approximation theorem for separable Hamiltonian systems and show that it is not possible to extend it to non-separable Hamiltonian systems. To investigate these theoretical approximation capabilities, we perform different numerical experiments. Furthermore we fix a mistake in a proof of a substantial theorem [ 25 , Theorem 2] for the approximation of symplectic maps in general, but specifically for symplectic machine learning methods.

Theorem 1

\displaystyle p_{s}(\alpha,\beta)

\displaystyle=\left.\Bigg\{(k_{1},...,k_{s};l_{1},...,l_{s})\,\middle|\,k_{i}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n_{2}},\left\lvert k_{i}\right\rvert>0,l_{i}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n_{1}},\left\lvert l_{i}\right\rvert>0\right.

4
Theory of time-adaptive SympNets

SympNets
[
20
]
focus on Hamiltonian systems given by

\displaystyle\dot{x}(t)=J^{-1}\nabla H(x(t)),\qquad x(t_{0})=x_{0}

(29)

with

and

\displaystyle J=\begin{pmatrix}0&I_{d}\\
-I_{d}&0\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{2d\times 2d}.

They are trying to approximate the flow of a Hamiltonian systems (
29
) for a given fixed time-step
h
. Since the flow
\Phi
of a Hamiltonian system is symplectic
[
14
]
, SympNets were designed to be symplectic by construction of their architecture. We will not recap the definition of SympNets here as their definitions are basically given by

2

In this work, we want to extend the SympNet architecture proposed in

such that it is able to handle different time steps

4.2
Time-adaptive SympNets for non-autonomous Hamiltonian systems

Now we want to focus on the non-autonmous case
H(p,q,t)
. Even though it is easily possible to extend a non-autonomous Hamiltonian system into an autonomous one
[
22
]
, we will not make use of that phase space extension in this work, as the extended system might not be separable even if the original one is. This observation can be made the following way. For the conjugate coordinate to
t
, let us call it
e
, we get
\dot{e}=-\partial_{t}H(p,q,t)
as the equation of motion. So with
\tilde{p}=(p^{T},e)^{T},\penalty\ \tilde{q}=(q^{T},t),\penalty\ \tilde{H}(\tilde{p},\tilde{q})=H(p,q,t)+e
we obtain the same equations of motion as in the original non-autonomous system

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\dot{p}\\
\dot{e}\end{pmatrix}=\dot{\tilde{p}}=-\frac{\partial\tilde{H}}{\partial\tilde{q}}=\begin{pmatrix}-\frac{\partial\tilde{H}}{\partial q}\\
-\frac{\partial\tilde{H}}{\partial t}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}-\frac{\partial H}{\partial q}\\
-\frac{\partial H}{\partial t}\end{pmatrix},\qquad\begin{pmatrix}\dot{q}\\
\dot{t}\end{pmatrix}=\dot{\tilde{q}}=\frac{\partial\tilde{H}}{\partial\tilde{p}}=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\partial\tilde{H}}{\partial p}\\
\frac{\partial\tilde{H}}{\partial e}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\partial H}{\partial p}\\
1\end{pmatrix}.

Now assume that we have a separable Hamiltonian
H(p,q,t)=K(p,t)+V(q,t)
. Then
\tilde{H}
is not separable, since

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\tilde{H}}{\partial\tilde{q}}=\begin{pmatrix}\partial_{q}V(q,t)\\
\partial_{t}K(p,t)+\partial_{t}V(q,t)\end{pmatrix}

also depends on
p
. Hence, we don’t use this phase space extension to deal with non-autonomous systems, as we show in
Section

4.4
that separability is an important condition, if we want to use adaptive time-steps. Also, one would need measurements of
e
, i.e., the original Hamiltonian.

Note that the extension would be applicable, if only
V
was depending on
t
but not
K
, i.e.,
H(p,q,t)=K(p)+V(q,t)
. So this might be a possibility to consider in future research.

Since
K
and
V
depend on time, we will allow the same for the potentials used in the SympNets. So the updates made by the non-autonomous SympNets try to approximate

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}I&h\nabla V(\cdot,t)\\
0&I\end{bmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}p\\
q\end{pmatrix},\qquad\begin{bmatrix}I&0\\
h\nabla K(\cdot,t)&I\end{bmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}p\\
q\end{pmatrix},

and update

for a given step size

, with

being the number of layers. This concept allows us to define non-autonomous TG-Sympets and is visualized in

4.3
Universal Approximation theorems for time-adaptive SympNets

In this section we present universal approximation theorems for the different kinds of SympNets introduced in
Sections

4.1
and
4.2
.
To formulate our approximation theorems, we need the following concepts.

Definition 6

(
r
-uniformly dense on compacta
[
20
]
)
.

Let
n_{1},n_{2}\in\mathbb{N},\penalty\ r\in\mathbb{N}_{0},\penalty\ T>0
be given,
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}}
is an open set,
S_{1}\subseteq C^{r}([0,T]\times U;\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}})
, then we say
S_{2}
is
r
-uniformly dense on compacta
in
S_{1}
if
S_{2}\subseteq S_{1}
and for any
f\in S_{1}
, compact
W\subseteq U
and
\epsilon>0
, there exists a
g\in S_{2}
such that
\left\lVert f-g\right\rVert_{C^{r}([0,T]\times W;\mathbb{R}^{n})}<\epsilon
.

Definition 7

(
r
-finite
[
20
]
)
.

Let
r\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. A function
\sigma\in C^{r}(\mathbb{R})
is called

\displaystyle 0<\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left\lvert D^{r}(\sigma)(x)\right\rvert dx<\infty.

Now we are able to present our approximation theorems. Note that all of the following theorems only guarantee that the proposed SympNets can approximate flows of arbitrary separable Hamiltonian systems. We will discuss in

why we can not extend our results to non-separable Hamiltonian systems.

Theorem 4

(Approximation theorem for TG-SympNets)
.

For any
r\in\mathbb{N}
the set of TG-SympNets
\Psi_{\text{TG}}
is
r
-uniformly dense on compacta in

\displaystyle\left\{\Phi:\mathbb{R}_{0}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{2d}\to\mathbb{R}^{2d}\mid\Phi\text{ is phase flow of \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{eqn:Hsys}}\right\},

where
H
is given by

\displaystyle H(p,q)=K(p)+V(q)

for some
K,V\in C^{r+1}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
, if the activation function
\sigma
is
r
-finite.

Proof.

Let
W\subseteq U
be a compact set.
Since
C^{\infty}
is dense in
C^{r+1}
in the
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{C^{r}(W;\mathbb{R}^{2d})}
-topology, we assume

for the rest of this proof. Hence,

is also arbitrarily often differentiable. Therefore, this is also true for the flow of
eq.

29

\Phi

[
11
, Appendix B, Lemma B.4]
.
Furthermore, we will use the same definitions for
G_{i,m}
and
\Phi_{i,m}
as in
Section

3
. We set

\displaystyle g_{{m}}\left(h,\begin{pmatrix}p\\
q\end{pmatrix}\right):

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}I&0\\
\frac{h}{m}\nabla K&I\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}I&-\frac{h}{m}\nabla V\\
0&I\end{bmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}p\\
q\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}I&0\\
\frac{h}{m}\nabla K&I\end{bmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}p-\frac{h}{m}\nabla V(q)\\
q\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}p-\frac{h}{m}\nabla V(q)\\
q+\frac{h}{m}\nabla K\left(p-\frac{h}{m}\nabla V(q)\right)\end{pmatrix}.

(30)

Now we want to show that this choice of

fulfills

only including spatial derivatives and no temporal derivatives.
We observe with Taylor’s theorem

\displaystyle\Phi\left(\frac{h}{m},\begin{pmatrix}p\\
q\end{pmatrix}\right)

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}p-\frac{h}{m}\nabla V(q)+\frac{h^{2}}{2m^{2}}\partial_{t}^{2}\Phi^{(p)}\left(\theta,\begin{pmatrix}p\\
q\end{pmatrix}\right)\\
q+\frac{h}{m}\nabla K(p)+\frac{h^{2}}{2m^{2}}\partial_{t}^{2}\Phi^{(q)}\left(\theta,\begin{pmatrix}p\\
q\end{pmatrix}\right)\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle g_{{m}}\left(h,\begin{pmatrix}p\\
q\end{pmatrix}\right)

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}p-\frac{h}{m}\nabla V(q)\\
q+\frac{h}{m}\nabla K(p)-\frac{h^{2}}{m^{2}}\nabla^{2}K(p-\vartheta\nabla V(q))\nabla V(q)\end{pmatrix}

with

. So we have

\displaystyle\sup_{(h,(p^{T},q^{T})^{T}\in[0,T]\times\tilde{W}}\max_{1\leq k\leq 2d}\left\lvert D^{\alpha}_{x}\left(g_{m}\left(h,\begin{pmatrix}p\\

q\end{pmatrix}\right)\right)\right\rvert

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{(h,(p^{T},q^{T})^{T}\in[0,T]\times\tilde{W}}\max_{1\leq k\leq 2d}\left\lvert D^{\alpha}_{x}\begin{pmatrix}\frac{h^{2}}{2m^{2}}\partial_{t}^{2}\Phi^{(p)}\left(\theta,\begin{pmatrix}p\\
q\end{pmatrix}\right)\\
-\frac{h^{2}}{m^{2}}\nabla^{2}K(p-\vartheta\nabla V(q))\nabla V(q)+\frac{h^{2}}{2m^{2}}\partial_{t}^{2}\Phi^{(q)}\left(\theta,\begin{pmatrix}p\\
q\end{pmatrix}\right)\end{pmatrix}^{(k)}\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m^{2}}(C_{g}^{(0)}+C_{\Phi}^{(0)}),

with

\displaystyle C_{g}^{(0)}

\displaystyle:=T^{2}\max_{\left\lvert\alpha\right\rvert\leq r}\sup_{(h,(p^{T},q^{T})^{T})\in[0,T]\times\tilde{W}}\max_{1\leq k\leq d}\left\lvert D^{\alpha}_{x}(\nabla^{2}K(p-h\nabla V(q))\nabla V(q))^{(k)}\right\rvert,

\displaystyle C_{\Phi}^{(0)}

\displaystyle:=\frac{T^{2}}{2}\max_{\left\lvert\alpha\right\rvert\leq r}\sup_{(h,x)\in[0,T]^{2}\times\tilde{W}}\max_{1\leq k\leq 2d}\left\lvert D^{\alpha}_{x}\partial_{t}^{2}\Phi^{(k)}(h,x)\right\rvert.

The second case is

including exactly one temporal derivative and

spatial derivatives. Using Taylor’s theorem again we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dh}\Phi\left(\frac{h}{m},\begin{pmatrix}p\\
q\end{pmatrix}\right)

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}-\frac{1}{m}\nabla V(q)-\frac{h}{m^{2}}\nabla^{2}V(q+\theta\nabla K(p))\nabla K(p)\\
\frac{1}{m}\nabla K(p)+\frac{h}{m^{2}}\nabla^{2}K(p-\theta\nabla V(q))\nabla V(q)\end{pmatrix},

q\end{pmatrix}\right)

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}-\frac{1}{m}\nabla V(q)\\
\frac{1}{m}\nabla K(p)-\frac{h}{m^{2}}\nabla^{2}K(p-\vartheta\nabla V(q))\nabla V(q)-\frac{h}{m^{2}}\nabla^{2}K(p-\frac{h}{m}\nabla V(q))\nabla V(q)\end{pmatrix}.

Therefore, it follows

\displaystyle\sup_{(h,(p^{T},q^{T})^{T}\in[0,T]\times\tilde{W}}\max_{1\leq k\leq 2d}\left\lvert D^{\alpha}\left(g_{m}\left(h,\begin{pmatrix}p\\
q\end{pmatrix}\right)-\Phi\left(\frac{h}{m},\begin{pmatrix}p\\
q\end{pmatrix}\right)\right)\right\rvert

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{(h,(p^{T},q^{T})^{T}\in[0,T]\times\tilde{W}}\max_{1\leq k\leq 2d}\left\lvert D^{\beta}\frac{d}{dh}\left(g_{m}\left(h,\begin{pmatrix}p\\
q\end{pmatrix}\right)-\Phi\left(\frac{h}{m},\begin{pmatrix}p\\

\sup_{(h,x)\in[0,T]\times\tilde{W}}\max_{1\leq k\leq 2d}\left\lvert D^{\alpha}\Phi^{(k)}(h/m,x)\right\rvert&=\sup_{(h,x)\in[0,T]\times\tilde{W}}\max_{1\leq k\leq 2d}\left\lvert D^{\beta}\frac{d^{n}}{dh^{n}}\Phi^{(k)}(h/m,x)\right\rvert\\
&\leq\frac{C^{(n)}}{m^{n}},\end{aligned}

(31)

and together

\displaystyle\sup_{(h,x)\in[0,T]\times\tilde{W}}\max_{1\leq k\leq 2d}\left\lvert D^{\alpha}g^{(k)}_{m}(h,x)-\Phi^{(k)}(h/m,x)\right\rvert\leq\frac{1}{m^{n}}(C_{g}^{(n)}+C_{\Phi}^{(n)}),

\displaystyle:=\max_{\left\lvert\beta\right\rvert\leq r-n}\sup_{(h,x)\in[0,T]\times\tilde{W}}\max_{1\leq k\leq 2d}\left\lvert D^{\beta}\partial_{t}^{n}g^{(k)}(h,x)\right\rvert,

Combining all the cases, we get

\displaystyle\leq\binom{2d+r}{2d}\max_{n=1,...,r}(C_{g}^{(n)}+C_{\Phi}^{(n)})\frac{1}{m^{2}}\leq\frac{C}{m^{2}},

4.4
Limitations

The time-adaptive SympNets constructed in
Section

4.1
are able to approximate the flow of separable Hamiltonian systems. Unfortunately these SympNets are not able to approximate non-separable Hamiltonian systems.
To see this, we observe that for the true flow
\Phi

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\Phi(0,x)=\dot{x}(t_{0})=J^{-1}\nabla H(x(t_{0}))

holds. Now if wee look at a TSympNet
\psi=u_{m}\circ...\circ u_{1}
, we observe

\displaystyle\frac{\partial u_{i}(h,x)}{\partial h}=\begin{bmatrix}0&\nabla V_{i}\\
0&0\end{bmatrix}(x)=\begin{pmatrix}\nabla V_{i}(q)\\
0\end{pmatrix}\qquad\text{ or }\qquad\frac{\partial u_{i}(h,x)}{\partial h}=\begin{bmatrix}0&0\\
\nabla K_{i}&0\end{bmatrix}(x)=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
\nabla K_{i}(p)\end{pmatrix}

and

\displaystyle\frac{\partial u_{i}(h,x)}{\partial x}=\begin{pmatrix}I&h\nabla^{2}V_{i}(q)\\
0&I\end{pmatrix}\qquad\text{ or }\qquad\frac{\partial u_{i}(h,x)}{\partial x}=\begin{pmatrix}I&0\\
h\nabla^{2}K_{i}(p)&I\end{pmatrix}

depending on whether
u_{i},i=1,...,m
is an upper or lower symplectic module. Therefore, we see that
\partial_{x}u_{i}(0,x)
does not depend on
x
and
u_{i}(0,x)=x
. The time derivative of
\psi
at
h=0
simplifies to

\displaystyle\partial_{h}\psi(0,x)

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial u_{m}}{\partial x}(0,(u_{m-1}\circ...\circ u_{1})(0,x))\frac{d}{dh}(u_{m-1}\circ...\circ u_{1})(0,x)+\frac{\partial u_{m}}{\partial h}(0,(u_{m-1}\circ...\circ u_{1})(0,x))

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}I&0\\
0&I\end{pmatrix}\frac{d}{dh}(u_{m-1}\circ...\circ u_{1})(0,x)+\frac{\partial u_{m}}{\partial h}(0,x).

(33)

By repeating this argument we have

\displaystyle\partial_{h}\psi(0,x)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{\partial u_{i}}{\partial h}(0,x)=\begin{pmatrix}-\nabla V(q)\\
\nabla K(p)\end{pmatrix}

for
K:=\sum_{i}K_{i}
and
V:=-\sum_{i}V_{i}
. Hence the gradient of the associated Hamiltonian
\tilde{H}
is given by

\displaystyle\nabla\tilde{H}(x)=J\partial_{h}\psi(0,x)=J\ \begin{pmatrix}-\nabla V(q)\\
\nabla K(p)\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\nabla K(p)\\
\nabla V(q)\end{pmatrix}

(34)

and
\tilde{H}=K(p)+V(q)
is separable.

Now we could think about other ways to make SympNets time-adaptive to extend their applicability to non-separable Hamiltonian systems. Let’s generalize our TSympNet
\psi=u_{m}\circ...\circ u_{1}
by allowing time-dependent potentials, i.e.,

\displaystyle u_{i}(h,x):=\begin{bmatrix}I&\nabla V_{i}(h,\cdot)\\
0&I\end{bmatrix}(x)\qquad\text{ or }\qquad u_{i}(h,x):=\begin{bmatrix}I&0\\
\nabla K_{i}(h,\cdot)&I\end{bmatrix}(x)

for
i=1,..,m
. One of the major advantages of the TSympNets suggested in
[
20
]
is
\psi(0,x)=x=\Phi(0,x)
. If we want to enforce this in our new structure, we need
\nabla V_{i}(0,q)=\nabla K_{i}(0,p)\equiv 0
. For this approach we observe

\displaystyle\frac{\partial u_{i}(h,x)}{\partial h}=\begin{bmatrix}0&\partial_{h}\nabla V_{i}(h,\cdot)\\
0&0\end{bmatrix}(x)=\begin{pmatrix}\partial_{h}\nabla V_{i}(h,q)\\
0\end{pmatrix}

or

\displaystyle\frac{\partial u_{i}(h,x)}{\partial h}=\begin{bmatrix}0&0\\
\partial_{h}\nabla K_{i}(h,\cdot)&0\end{bmatrix}(x)=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
\partial_{h}\nabla K_{i}(h,p)\end{pmatrix}

and

\displaystyle\frac{\partial u_{i}(h,x)}{\partial x}=\begin{pmatrix}I&\nabla^{2}V_{i}(h,q)\\
0&I\end{pmatrix}\qquad\text{ or }\qquad\frac{\partial u_{i}(h,x)}{\partial x}=\begin{pmatrix}I&0\\
\nabla^{2}K_{i}(h,p)&I\end{pmatrix}.

With
\nabla V_{i}(0,q)\equiv 0
we have

\displaystyle\partial_{q_{j}}\nabla V_{i}(0,q)=\lim\limits_{h\to 0}\frac{\nabla V_{i}(0,q+he_{j})-\nabla V_{i}(0,q)}{h}=0.

Therefore
\nabla^{2}V_{i}(0,q)\equiv 0
. The same argument holds for
\nabla^{2}K_{i}
, and we have
\frac{\partial u_{i}(0,x)}{\partial x}=I_{2d}
. By repeating the argument from
section

4.4
, again we have

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\psi(0,x)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{\partial u_{i}}{\partial t}(0,x)=\begin{pmatrix}-\nabla V(q)\\
\nabla K(p)\end{pmatrix}

with
V:=-\sum_{i}\partial_{h}V_{i}(0,\cdot)
and
K:=\sum_{i}\partial_{h}K_{i}(0,\cdot)
. This leads to a separable Hamiltonian
\tilde{H}=K(p)+V(q)
as well. The same arguments hold for TSympNets for non-autonomous Hamiltonian systems. So unfortunately there is no way to approximate non-separable Hamiltonian systems with TSympNets, if we want to keep the triangular structure of the symplectic modules, have a time step in every layer and enforce
u_{i}(0,x)=x
strongly.

Note the arguments above do not hold for our new TLA-SympNets, while they are true for the original TLA-SympNets. We observe that this is not only a theoretical, but also a practical benefit in
Section

5.2
. The question if our new TLA-SympNets are universal approximators of general symplectic maps remains open for future work.

Obviously, the above arguments also hold for the non-autonomous case. Hence, the same limitations apply to NATG- and NATLA SympNets.

4.5
Limitations of the original TLA-SympNets

In this subsection we give our reasoning for choosing a different architecture for TLA-Sympnets as originally proposed in
[
20
]
. Note that we did not derive a universal approximation theorem them. In the following we prove why this is impossible.
The linear layers of original time-dependent LA-SympNets are given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{n}^{\text{up}}\left(h,\begin{pmatrix}p\\
q\end{pmatrix}\right)

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}I&0/hS_{n}\\
hS_{n}/0&I\end{pmatrix}\ldots\begin{pmatrix}I&0\\
hS_{2}&I\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}I&hS_{1}\\
0&I\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}p\\
q\end{pmatrix}+b,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{n}^{\text{low}}\left(h,\begin{pmatrix}p\\
q\end{pmatrix}\right)

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}I&0/hS_{n}\\
hS_{n}/0&I\end{pmatrix}\ldots\begin{pmatrix}I&hS_{2}\\
0&I\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}I&0\\
hS_{1}&I\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}p\\
q\end{pmatrix}+b.

For simplicity we will focus on the
\mathcal{L}_{n}^{\text{up}}
modules and assume
n
to be even to simplify the notation

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{n}\left(h,\begin{pmatrix}p\\
q\end{pmatrix}\right)

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}I&0\\
hS_{n}&I\end{pmatrix}\ldots\begin{pmatrix}I&0\\
hS_{2}&I\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}I&hS_{1}\\
0&I\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}p\\
q\end{pmatrix}+b.

Now let
v_{i}
be the composition of a linear layer with an activation layer

\displaystyle v_{i}^{\text{up}}\left(h,\begin{pmatrix}p\\
q\end{pmatrix}\right)

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}I&h\sigma_{a_{q,i},b_{q,i}}\\
0&I\end{bmatrix}\mathcal{L}_{n}^{(i)}\left(h,\begin{pmatrix}p\\
q\end{pmatrix}\right),

\displaystyle v_{i}^{\text{low}}\left(h,\begin{pmatrix}p\\
q\end{pmatrix}\right)

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}I&0\\
h\sigma_{a_{p,i},b_{p,i}}&I\end{bmatrix}\mathcal{L}_{n}^{(i)}\left(h,\begin{pmatrix}p\\
q\end{pmatrix}\right).

Then we observe

\displaystyle\partial_{h}v_{i}^{\text{up}}\left(h,\begin{pmatrix}p\\
q\end{pmatrix}\right)\bigg|_{h=0}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}0&\sigma^{\prime}_{a_{q,i},b_{q,i}}\\
0&0\end{bmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}p\\
q\end{pmatrix}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\begin{pmatrix}0&S_{j}^{(i)}/0\\
0/S_{j}^{(i)}&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}p\\
q\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{S}_{i}q+\sigma^{\prime}_{a_{q,i},b_{q,i}}(q)\\
\hat{S}_{i}p\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\partial_{h}v_{i}^{\text{low}}\left(h,\begin{pmatrix}p\\
q\end{pmatrix}\right)\bigg|_{h=0}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}0&0\\
\sigma^{\prime}_{a_{p,i},b_{p,i}}&0\end{bmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}p\\
q\end{pmatrix}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\begin{pmatrix}0&S_{j}^{(i)}/0\\
0/S_{j}^{(i)}&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}p\\
q\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{S}_{i}q\\
\hat{S}_{i}p+\sigma^{\prime}_{a_{p,i},b_{p,i}}(p)\end{pmatrix},

with
\tilde{S}_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{n/2}S_{2j-1}^{(i)},\penalty\ \hat{S}_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{n/2}S_{2j}^{(i)}
. With the same derivations as for the other modules, we obtain

\displaystyle\partial_{x}v_{i}^{\text{up}}\left(0,\begin{pmatrix}p\\
q\end{pmatrix}\right)=\partial_{x}v_{i}^{\text{low}}\left(0,\begin{pmatrix}p\\
q\end{pmatrix}\right)=I.

If we compute the time derivative of the entire LA-SympNet, we arrive at

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dh}\left(v_{m}^{low}\circ v_{m}^{\text{up}}\circ\ldots\circ v_{1}^{low}\circ v_{1}^{\text{up}}\right)\bigg|_{h=0}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\partial_{h}v_{i}^{\text{up}}\left(0,\begin{pmatrix}p\\
q\end{pmatrix}\right)+\partial_{h}v_{i}^{\text{low}}\left(0,\begin{pmatrix}p\\
q\end{pmatrix}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{S}_{i}^{\text{up}}q+\tilde{S}_{i}^{low}q+\sigma^{\prime}_{a_{q,i},b_{q,i}}(q)\\
\hat{S}_{i}^{\text{up}}p+\hat{S}_{i}^{low}p+\sigma^{\prime}_{a_{p,i},b_{p,i}}(p)\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}S_{q}q+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sigma^{\prime}_{a_{q,i},b_{q,i}}(q)\\
S_{p}p+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sigma^{\prime}_{a_{p,i},b_{p,i}}(p)\end{pmatrix},

with
S_{q}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\tilde{S}_{i}^{low}+\tilde{S}_{i}^{\text{up}}
and
S_{p}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\hat{S}_{i}^{\text{up}}+\hat{S}_{i}^{low}
.
So the learned Hamiltonian always has the form

\displaystyle\tilde{H}(p,q)=-\frac{1}{2}S_{q}q^{2}+\frac{1}{2}S_{p}p^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{p,i}^{T}\sigma(p+b_{p,i})-\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{q,i}^{T}\sigma(q+b_{q,i}).

Note that the functions in the sums do are not universal approximators of the potentials that we would need to have a general separable Hamiltonian, since the matrices inside the activation functions are missing
[
17
]
.
Since this severely limits the set of Hamiltonian systems whose flow that can be expressed by the orignal TLA-SympNets, we proposed a different architecture in
Section

4.1
.

5.3
Forced harmonic oscillator

The last example we consider is the harmonic oscillator with an external sinusiodal force. The non-autonomous Hamiltonian is given by

\displaystyle H(p,q,t)=\frac{1}{2}p^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\omega_{0}^{2}q^{2}-F_{0}\sin(\omega t)q.

Fortunately, the corresponding Hamiltonian system can be solved analytically. The analytical solution is given by

\displaystyle p(t)

\displaystyle=\left(p_{0}-\frac{\omega F_{0}}{\omega_{0}^{2}-\omega^{2}}\right)\cos(\omega_{0}t)-q_{0}\omega_{0}\sin(\omega_{0}t)+\frac{\omega F_{0}}{\omega_{0}^{2}-\omega^{2}}\cos(\omega t),

\displaystyle q(t)

\displaystyle=q_{0}\cos(\omega_{0}t)+\left(\frac{p_{0}}{\omega_{0}}-\frac{\omega F_{0}}{\omega_{0}(\omega_{0}^{2}-\omega^{2})}\right)\sin(\omega_{0}t)+\frac{F_{0}}{\omega_{0}^{2}-\omega^{2}}\sin(\omega t),

with initial conditions
p(0)=p_{0},\penalty\ q(0)=q_{0}
. Hence, we do not need to use a numerical solver to generate our training and test data. The training data set is given by
N=1600
tuples
\mathcal{T}=\{[x_{i},t_{i},h_{i}],[y_{i},\tilde{t}_{i}]\}_{i=1}^{N}
, where
\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
is sampled from a uniform distribution over
[-3.5,2]\times[-4,4]
. The times
\{t_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
and time-steps
\{h_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
are also sampled from uniform distributions over
[0,16]
and
[0,0.3]
respectively. The labels
\{y_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
are given by

y_{i}=(p(t_{i}+h_{i})^{T},q(t_{i}+h_{i})^{T})^{T}
. The test data is given by the trajectory of the analytical solution with initial value
(p_{0},q_{0})^{T}=(-0.2,-0.5)^{T}
evaluated equidistant times
t_{i}=ih
with
h=0.2
for
i=1,...,k,\penalty\ k=80
. All neural networks are trained for 150000 epochs with a learning rate of
10^{-3}
.

Figures

5(a)
and
5(b)
show that both TG-SympNets as well as TLA-SympNets are not able to capture the dynamics of the harmonic oscillator with an external force. This was expected, because the test trajectory intersects with itself. So the SympNets would need to give different predictions at the same phase space coordinate
(p,q)
depending on the time
t
. Since time is not an input of these SympNets, this is impossible.

Figure

shows that NATG-SympNets are able to capture the dynamics well. The mentioned intersection is not a problem for NATG-SympNets, since it lives on the
(p,q,t)
phase space and the intersection only happens in the
(p,q)
space.

Note that the proof of
Theorem

7
suggests that NATLA-SympNets need more (width of the gradient modules as many) layers than G-SympNets as does the one of
[
20
, Theorem 4]
. In the previous autonomous experiments as well as in the ones of
[
20
]
this was not observed. But here we see that the number of layers needed for the NATLA-SympNet is much larger then for NATG-SympNets, see
Table

1
. This results in more expensive training and evaluation, making NATG-SympNets the far superior method for non-autonomous Hamiltonian systems. Nevertheless,
Figure

5(d)
confirms the theoretical approximation capabilities of NATLA-SympNets established in
Theorem

7
.

6
Conclusion and Outlook

In this work we first corrected the proof of the symplectic polynomial approximation theorem
[
25
, Theorem 2]
, which is an important theorem for the approximation qualities of SympNets and HénonNets and generalized HNNs. We developed a new time-adaptive LA-SympNet architecture, we coined TLA-SympNets and established a universal approximation theorem for them as well as TG-Sympnets limited to separable Hamiltonian systems. This limitation is not an artifact of our proofs, but a direct result of the used architecture shown in
Section

4.4
theoretically and
Section

5.2
experimentally. Additionally, we proposed a novel intrinsically symplectic, time-adaptive neural network architecture to handle non-autonomous Hamiltonian systems in NATG-SympNets and NATLA-SympNets. We were able to show that they are universal approximators for the flows of separable non-autonomous Hamitlonian systems. Lastly, we verified our theoretical findings on three numerical experiments.

The obvious open research question is how to adapt T-SympNets such that they will be able to handle non-separable Hamiltonian systems as well. Furthermore, we are interested in creating time-adaptive extensions for HénonNets or even generalizing our findings to generalized HNNs.

Lemma 1.1
.

Let

be a solvable cube-free

-subgroup. Then one of the following holds.

(a)

If

is reducible, then

is conjugated to a subgroup of

and

(b)

If

is conjugated to a subgroup of

and

(c)

is conjugated to a subgroup of

-subgroup of

If

is a reducible

-subgroup of
{\rm GL}(2,q)
, then the underlying

-module

is a direct sum of two one-dimensional submodules of

with respect to which elements of

are diagonal. Thus

and is as given in part (a).

Now let

be an imprimitive

-subgroup. Then the underlying

are either diagonal or are elements of the coset

conjugates to a subgroup of

. Now assume

Now let

-subgroup of

is cyclic and by

Let

. If

has order

, then

and if
g
has order

\lambda\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{*}
and

is the unique element of order
2
. (Note that an element of order

only if

Let

and let

be of order
2
. Then
dad^{-1}=g
. Similarly let

and let

.

Let

.

Proof.

Let

If the

are non-abelian then the

are either the Hall

-subgroups respectively or they are the respective Fitting subgroups and so

-subgroups respectively and so

or we can write

is the Hall

-subgroup of

and

and

are elements of order

in

. Now if

Now let

If

Now let

. Since

, we have

and so

. Since

, by

in

4
Primitive cube-free
p^{\prime}
-subgroups of
{\rm GL}(2,q)
that are solvable

Let
H\leq N(2,q)
be a cube-free primitive subgroup of order
m
where
p\nmid m
. In this section we use the structure of cube-free solvable primitive subgroups of
{\rm GL}(2,q)
to show that

. Recall that
N(2,q)=S(2,q)\rtimes\langle b\rangle
where
b
has order
2
. Further using the discussion after Theorem 2.3.5 in
[
8
]
, we have that
bhb^{-1}=h^{q}
where
S(2,q)=\langle h\rangle
. Also note that unless
p
is an odd prime
N(2,q)
cannot have elements of order
4
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Any two elements of order
s
in the coset
S(2,q)b
, where
s\in\{2,4\}
, are conjugate in
N(2,q)
. Thus the elements of order
s
in
S(2,q)b
form a single conjugacy class.

Proof.

The action of
b
on
h
ensures that only elements of the form
h^{i(q-1)}b
where
0\leq i<q+1
have order
2
in
S(2,q)b
and that every such element is conjugate to
b
.

Let
p
be an odd prime. Then we can show that an element of order
4
in
S(2,q)b
will have the form
h^{l(q-1)/2}b
where
l
is odd and
1\leq l<2(q+1)
. Let
g=h^{(q-1)/2}b
. Then we can see that
C_{N(2,q)}(g)=\langle g\rangle\langle h^{q+1}\rangle
and hence
|[g]|=q+1
. Since
l
is odd, we get
[g]
is precisely the set of elements of order
4
in
S(2,q)b
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.4

Proof.

Let
H\leq N(2,q)
be a cube-free primitive subgroup of order
m
where
p\nmid m
. By Lemma
1.1
, we can assume that if
H\leq S(2,q)
then
H
is cyclic. Otherwise we can write
H=L\rtimes P
where
L\leq S(2,q)
and
P
is a Sylow
2
-subgroup of
H
.

Now let
H_{1}
be a cube-free primitive subgroup of
N(2,q)
which is isomorphic to
H
. If
H
is a subgroup of
S(2,q)
then it is a cyclic irreducible subgroup of order
m
. By
[
8
, Theorem 2.3.3]
, all cyclic irreducible subgroups of order
m
form a single conjugacy class in
{\rm GL}(2,q)
and so we have that
H
and
H_{1}
are conjugate.

Let us assume now that
H
is not cyclic, and that
H=L\rtimes P
as above. Since
H_{1}\cong H
, we must have that
H_{1}=L_{1}\rtimes P_{1}
where
P_{1}
is a Sylow
2
-subgroup of
H_{1}
and
L_{1}\leq S(2,q)
.

If
P
is elementary abelian of order
4
, then
|P\cap S(2,q)|=2
. So we can write
H=(L\times P\cap S(2,q))\rtimes\langle u\rangle
where
u\in S(2,q)b
is of order
2
. Similarly
H_{1}=(L_{1}\times P_{1}\cap S(2,q))\rtimes\langle v\rangle
where
v\in S(2,q)b
is of order
2
. Thus by Lemma
4.1
, we get that there exists
g\in N(2,q)
such that
gPg^{-1}=P_{1}
whether

is cyclic or elementary abelian. Since

is cyclic we have
L=L_{1}
and so
gH_{1}g^{-1}=H_{2}
. Thus every primitive subgroup of
N(2,q)
which is isomorphic to
H
is conjugate to
H
.

Now suppose
K
is a primitive subgroup of
{\rm GL}(2,q)
isomorphic to
H
. Then by Lemma
1.1
there exist a subgroup
H_{1}
of
N(2,q)
such that
K
is conjugate to
H_{1}
in
{\rm GL}(2,q)
. Clearly by the above discussion
H_{1}
is a conjugate of
H
. Thus every primitive subgroup of
{\rm GL}(2,q)
isomorphic to
H
is also a conjugate of
H
.
∎

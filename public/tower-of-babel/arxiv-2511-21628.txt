A complete solution of the Erdős–Kleitman matching problem for n ≤ 3 ​ s n\leq 3s .
Andrey Kupavskii, Georgy Sokolov

Abstract
Given integers n ≥ s ≥ 2 n\geq s\geq 2 , let e ⁡ ( n , s ) e(n,s) stand for the maximum size of a family ℱ {\mathcal{F}} of subsets of an n n -element set that contains no s s pairwise disjoint members. The study of this quantity goes back to the 1960s, when Kleitman determined e ⁡ ( s ​ m − 1 , s ) e(sm-1,s) and e ⁡ ( s ​ m , s ) e(sm,s) for all integer m , s ≥ 1 m,s\geq 1 . The question of determining e ⁡ ( n , s ) e(n,s) is closely connected to its uniform counterpart, the subject of the famous Erdős Matching Conjecture. The problem of determining e ⁡ ( n , s ) e(n,s) has proven to be very hard and, in spite of some progress during these years, even a general conjecture concerning the value of e ⁡ ( n , s ) e(n,s) is missing. In this paper, we completely solve the problem for n ≤ 3 ​ s n\leq 3s . In this regime, the average size of a set in an s s -matching is at most 3 3 , and it is a delicate interplay between the ‘missing’ 2 2 - and 3 3 -element sets that plays a key role here. Four types of extremal families appear in the characterization. Our result sheds light on how the extremal function e ⁡ ( n , s ) e(n,s) may behave in general.

Main result

In this paper, we find the value of

In order to state the result, we describe the prospective extremal families. We have already seen the first example.

Lemma 7

If

Theorem 8

Let

Consider the sets

Let

As we have mentioned, we will actually prove the following stronger inequality.

\sum_{i=0}^{3}y_{F}(i)\geq\min\big\{|\overline{\mathcal{P}(s,\ell)}|,|\overline{\mathcal{P}^{\prime}(s,\ell)}|,|\overline{\mathcal{Q}(s,\ell)}|,|\overline{\mathcal{W}(s,\ell)}|\big\}.

. Thus,

satisfies the condition from Definition
1
. It means that
d(\mathcal{F})
is well-defined and

d(\mathcal{F})\leq 2c.

(11)

Note that if
d(\mathcal{F})>2k
for some integer
k\geq 0
, then
\nu(\mathcal{F}^{(2)})\geq\ell+k
. Indeed, the sets
\{1,2\ell+2k\},\ldots\{\ell+k,\ell+k+1\}
form an
(\ell+k)
-matching and all belong to
\mathcal{F}
by the definition of
d
. Actually, it is not difficult to prove that
\nu(\mathcal{F}^{(2)})\geq\ell+k
if and only if
d(\mathcal{F})>2k
. The value
d(\mathcal{F})
may thus be understood as a certain half-integral refinement of
\nu(\mathcal{F}^{(2)})
.

We will often use the following simple claim.

Claim 9
.

If
\mathcal{F}
is a shifted family and
d(\mathcal{F})\geq d
, then one of the following two conditions is satisfied:

•

d
is even and for some
i\in\big[1,\ell+\frac{d}{2}\big]
the set
\{i,2\ell+d+1-i\}
is not in
\mathcal{F}
.

•

d
is odd and either
\{1,2\ell+d\}\notin\mathcal{F}
, or for some
i\in[3,\ell+\frac{d+1}{2}]
the set
\{i,2\ell+d+2-i\}
is not in
\mathcal{F}
.

Proof.

By the definition of
d(\mathcal{F})
one of these conditions is satisfied for
d(\mathcal{F})\leq d
. Thus, we need to check, that if the condition is satisfied for some

, then it is satisfied for
d^{\prime}+1
. We consider separately the cases of odd and even
d^{\prime}
.

•

Let
d^{\prime}
be an even number, satisfying the condition from the definition of
d(\mathcal{F})
, that is, for some
i\in\big[1,\ell+\frac{d^{\prime}}{2}\big]
the set
\{i,2\ell+d^{\prime}+1-i\}
is not in
\mathcal{F}
. If
i=1
, then by shiftedness of
\mathcal{F}
we have
\{i,2\ell+d^{\prime}+2-i\}=\{1,2\ell+(d^{\prime}+1)\}\notin\mathcal{F}
and thus the first part of the condition for odd
d^{\prime}
is satisfied. If
i\geq 2
, then, again by shiftedness,
\{i+1,2\ell+d^{\prime}+2-i\}\notin\mathcal{F}
. Since
\{i+1,2\ell+d^{\prime}+2-i\}=\{i^{\prime},2l+(d^{\prime}+1)+2-i^{\prime}\}
for
i^{\prime}=i+1\in[3,\ell+\frac{d^{\prime}+2}{2}]
, the second part of condition for odd
d^{\prime}
is satisfied.

•

Let
d^{\prime}
be an odd number, satisfying the condition from the definition of
d(\mathcal{F})
, that is, either
\{1,2l+d^{\prime}\}\notin\mathcal{F}
, or for some
i\in[3,\ell+\frac{d^{\prime}+1}{2}]
the set
\{i,2\ell+d^{\prime}+2-i\}
is not in
\mathcal{F}
. If the first part of the condition is satisfied, then by shiftedness
\{1,2\ell+(d^{\prime}+1)\}\notin\mathcal{F}
, that is, the condition for
d^{\prime}+1
is satisfied with
i=1
. If the second part of the condition is satisfied, then we notice, that the set
\{i,2\ell+d^{\prime}+2-i\}
is exactly the set
\{i,2\ell+(d^{\prime}+1)+1-i\}
, so the condition for
d^{\prime}+1
is satisfied with the same
i
and the same missing set.

∎

In Section

we prove lower bounds on

. In particular, the bound will be a minimum of some expressions, and in order to use it, we will need to consider cases depending on where the minimum is achieved.

In Section

4
Avoiding
1
-element sets

In order to get rid of
1
-element sets, we need to run an inductive procedure. We formalize it as follows. Assume that the following theorem holds.

Theorem 10
.

Let
n,s,\ell
be non-negative integers such that
n=3s-\ell
and
0\leq\ell\leq s
. Suppose that
\mathcal{F}\subset 2^{[n]}
is a shifted up-set with
\nu(\mathcal{F})<s
and
\mathcal{F}\cap{[n]\choose 1}=\emptyset
. Then
|\mathcal{F}|\leq\max\big\{|\mathcal{P}(s,\ell)|,|\mathcal{P}^{\prime}(s,\ell)|,|\mathcal{Q}(s,\ell)|,|\mathcal{W}(s,\ell)|\big\}
. Moreover, equality is achieved only if
\mathcal{F}
coincides with one of these families.

Proof of Theorem
3
using Theorem
10
.

We prove Theorem
3
by induction on
\ell
. For
\ell\in\{0,1\}
the statement is given by Theorem
1
, together with uniqueness that is proven in
[
19
]
. In what follows, we assume that
\ell\geq 2.

If
\{1\}\notin\mathcal{F}
then by shiftedness
\mathcal{F}\cap{[n]\choose 1}=\emptyset
and we are done.
If
\{1\}\in\mathcal{F}
then we apply induction. Let
\mathcal{F^{\prime}}=\mathcal{F}\cap 2^{[2,n]}
. The family
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
is a family of subsets of an
(n-1)
-element set,
\nu(\mathcal{F^{\prime}})<s-1
, and we have
n-1=3(s-1)-(\ell-2).
Note that
\ell-2\geq 0
and
\ell-2<s-1
, and thus we may apply induction to
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
, getting that

|\overline{\mathcal{F^{\prime}}}|\geq\min\big\{|\overline{\mathcal{P^{\prime}}(s-1,\ell-2)}|,|\overline{\mathcal{P}(s-1,\ell-2)}|,|\overline{\mathcal{Q}(s-1,\ell-2)}|,|\overline{\mathcal{W}(s-1,\ell-2)}|\big\},

where the complement is taken with respect to
2^{[2,n]}.
Clearly,
|\overline{\mathcal{F}}|\geq|\overline{\mathcal{F^{\prime}}}|
, and so Theorem
3
is implied by the following claim.

Claim 11
.

We have

\min\big\{|\overline{\mathcal{P^{\prime}}(s-1,\ell-2)}|,|\overline{\mathcal{P}(s-1,\ell-2)}|,|\overline{\mathcal{Q}(s-1,\ell-2)}|,\\
|\overline{\mathcal{W}(s-1,\ell-2)}|\big\}>\min\big\{|\overline{\mathcal{P^{\prime}}(s,\ell)}|,|\overline{\mathcal{P}(s,\ell)}|,|\overline{\mathcal{Q}(s,\ell)}|,|\overline{\mathcal{W}(s,\ell)}|\big\}.

Moreover,

\min\big\{|\overline{\mathcal{P^{\prime}}(s-1,\ell-2)}^{(\leq 3)}|,|\overline{\mathcal{P}(s-1,\ell-2)}^{(\leq 3)}|,|\overline{\mathcal{Q}(s-1,\ell-2)}^{(\leq 3)}|,\\
|\overline{\mathcal{W}(s-1,\ell-2)}^{(\leq 3)}|\big\}>\min\big\{|\overline{\mathcal{P^{\prime}}(s,\ell)}^{(\leq 3)}|,|\overline{\mathcal{P}(s,\ell)}^{(\leq 3)}|,|\overline{\mathcal{Q}(s,\ell)}^{(\leq 3)}|,|\overline{\mathcal{W}(s,\ell)}^{(\leq 3)}|\big\}.

(We need the moreover part for Theorem
4
).

The proof the claim is a technical check, and we defer it to the appendix. This completes the proof of the implication.
∎

In what follows, we need to prove Theorem
10
. Concretely, we need to lower bound
y(2)+y(3).

5
Bounds on
y(2)

In this section, we prove a lower bound on
y_{\mathcal{F}}(2)
that depends on
d(\mathcal{F})
. The definition of
d(\mathcal{F})
depends on its parity, which is why we get slightly different bounds and proofs for odd and even
d(\mathcal{F})
. We start with the easier case of even
d
.

Lemma 12
.

Let
d\geq 0
be even. If
\mathcal{F}\subset 2^{[n]}
is a shifted family with
d(\mathcal{F})\leq d
then

y(2)\geq\min\left\{\frac{(4\ell+3c+d-2)(3c-d+1)}{2},\frac{(\ell+3c-\frac{d}{2}+1)(\ell+3c-\frac{d}{2})}{2}\right\}.

(12)

Moreover, equality is achieved only if
\mathcal{F}^{(2)}={[2\ell+d-1]\choose 2}
or
\mathcal{F}^{(2)}=\big\{F\in{n\choose 2}:F\cap[\ell+\frac{d}{2}-1]\neq\emptyset\big\}
.

Proof.

The proof is very similar to Frankl’s proof of the Erdős-Gallai theorem.
By Claim
9
for some
i\in[\ell+\frac{d}{2}]
the set
\{i,2\ell+d+1-i\}
is not in
\mathcal{F}
.
By Lemma
7
,

y(2)\geq\frac{(n+2\ell+d-3i+1)(n-2\ell-d+i)}{2}=\frac{(4\ell+3c+d-3i+1)(3c-d+i)}{2}.

This bound is upward convex as a function of
i
, and thus the minimum on the interval
[1,\ell+\frac{d}{2}]
is attained at one of its ends. Substituting
i=1,\frac{d}{2}
in the inequality above, we get that (
12
) holds. Equality is attained only if
i\in\{1,\frac{d}{2}\}
and
{\mathcal{F}}^{(2)}
consists of all sets that cannot be shifted to
\{i,2\ell+d+1-i\}
. It implies that
\mathcal{F}^{(2)}
must have the form as stated in the moreover part. ∎

We get the following claim as an immediate corollary.

Claim 13
.

Theorem
10
holds for
d(\mathcal{F})=0
.

Proof.

Indeed, Lemma
12
for
d(\mathcal{F})=0
states that

y_{\mathcal{F}}(2)\geq\min\left\{\frac{(4\ell+3c-2)(3c+1)}{2},\frac{(\ell+3c+1)(\ell+3c)}{2}\right\}=\min\big\{y_{\mathcal{P}^{\prime}(s,\ell)}(2),y_{\mathcal{P}(s,l)}(2)\big\}

and equality is achieved only if
\mathcal{F}^{(2)}=\mathcal{P}^{\prime}(s,\ell)^{(2)}
or
\mathcal{F}^{(2)}=\mathcal{P}(s,\ell)^{(2)}
. At the same time,
\mathcal{F}
does not contain
0
- or
1
-element sets and both
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}(s,\ell)
and
\mathcal{P}(s,\ell)
contain all sets of size at least
3
. Altogether, we get that
|\mathcal{F}|\leq\max\big\{|\mathcal{P}^{\prime}(s,\ell)|,|\mathcal{P}(s,\ell)|\big\}
and equality is achieved only if
\mathcal{F}\in\{\mathcal{P}(s,\ell),\mathcal{P}^{\prime}(s,\ell)\}
.
∎

The case of odd
d
requires a more careful analysis. In this case, we use the inequality (
11
) which states that
d(\mathcal{F})\leq 2c
.

Lemma 14
.

Let
d
be a positive odd integer,

. If
\mathcal{F}\subset 2^{[n]}
is a shifted family with

y(2)\geq\min\Big\{\frac{(4\ell+3c+d-2)(3c-d+1)}{2},\frac{(\ell+3c-\frac{d-1}{2})(\ell+3c-\frac{d+1}{2})}{2}\Big\}.

(13)

Moreover, equality is achieved only if
\mathcal{F}^{(2)}={[2\ell+d-1]\choose 2}
or
\mathcal{F}^{(2)}=\{F\in{n\choose 2}:F\cap[\ell+\frac{d-1}{2}]\neq\emptyset\}
.

Combining the bounds (
12
) and (

), we get the following bound.

\displaystyle y(2)\geq\max\left\{\frac{(4\ell+3c+d-2)(3c-d+1)}{2},\frac{(\ell+3c-\lceil\frac{d}{2}\rceil+1)(\ell+3c-\lceil\frac{d}{2}\rceil)}{2}\right\}.

(14)

Proof of Lemma
14
.

We consider cases depending on the missing set. If
\{1,2\ell+d\}\notin\mathcal{F}
, then by Lemma
7
we have
y(2)\geq\frac{(4\ell+3c+d-2)(3c-d+1)}{2}
, and equality is achieved only if
\mathcal{F}^{(2)}={[2\ell+d-1]\choose 2}
. Otherwise, one of the sets
\{i,2\ell+d+2-i\}
for some

is not in
\mathcal{F}
. By Lemma
7
, we get

y(2)\geq\frac{(4\ell+3c+d-3i+2)(3c-d+i-1)}{2}=:f(i).

Again, by convexity

. If

then the two expressions coincide. If in that case

\mathcal{F}^{(2)}=\Big\{F\in{n\choose 2}:F\cap\Big[\ell+\frac{d-1}{2}\Big]\neq\emptyset\Big\}.

The family

must have the same form if

.

To complete the proof of the lemma, it is sufficient to show that either

or

Claim 15
.

For

is satisfied.

6
Bounds on
y(3)

In this section, we will prove several bounds on
y_{\mathcal{F}}(3)
that depend on
d(\mathcal{F})
. We will use different bounds for different intervals of
d(\mathcal{F})
: small constant
d(\mathcal{F})
,
d(\mathcal{F})\leq c+1
and
d(\mathcal{F})\geq c+2
. One reason why the bounds we use change near
d(\mathcal{F})=c
is that for one of the extremal families
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{Q}(s,\ell)
we have
d(\mathcal{F})=c
.

Recall that we treated the case
d(\mathcal{F})=0
in Claim
13
.

In what follows,
d(\mathcal{F})>0
. We will start with the bound that will be used for small
d(\mathcal{F})
and for
\ell
small compared to
c
.

Lemma 17
.

If
d(\mathcal{F})>0
and
\nu(\mathcal{F})<s
, then
y(3)\geq{3c-1\choose 2}
. Moreover, equality is achieved only if
\mathcal{F}
contains all sets in
{[n]\choose 3}\setminus{[2\ell+1,n]\choose 3}
.

Proof.

Recall that
s=\ell+c
. Since
d>0
the family
\mathcal{F}
contains an
\ell
-matching
\{1,2\ell\},\ldots,\{\ell,\ell+1\}
. Consequently,
\nu(\mathcal{F}\cap 2^{[2\ell+1,n]})<c
. At the same time, the set
[2\ell+1,n]
has size
3c
. Consider any
c
-matching in
{[2\ell+1,n]\choose 3}
. It must contain at least one element in
\overline{\mathcal{F}}
. Averaging over all
c
-matchings in
{[2\ell+1,n]\choose 3}
, we get that
y_{\mathcal{F}}(3)\geq y_{\mathcal{F}\cap 2^{[2\ell+1,n]}}(3)\geq\frac{1}{c}{3c\choose 3}={3c-1\choose 2}
. The first inequality turns into an equality if and only if
\mathcal{F}
contains all sets in
{[n]\choose 3}\setminus{[2\ell+1,n]\choose 3}
.
∎

In the regime
d=d(\mathcal{F})\leq c+1
we will need to consider two subcases. The easy case is when
\nu(\mathcal{F}\cap{[2\ell+d,n]\choose 3})<c-d+1
. In that case, we will be able to bound
y(3)
using missing sets in
{[2\ell+d,n]\choose 3}
. Concretely wee make use of the following inequality from
[
7
]
that generalizes the Erdős–Ko–Rado theorem.

Lemma 18

(
[
7
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{G}\subset{[n]\choose k},\nu(\mathcal{G})<s,n\geq ks
. Then
|\mathcal{G}|\leq(s-1){n-1\choose k-1}
.

For
d\geq 1
we have
n-2\ell-d+1=3c-d+1\geq 3(c-d+1)
. Thus, we can apply Lemma
18
and get

\displaystyle y(3)

\displaystyle\geq{n-2\ell-d+1\choose 3}-(c-d){n-2\ell-d\choose 2}

\displaystyle=\Big(\frac{3c-d+1}{3}-(c-d)\Big){3c-d\choose 2}=\frac{(2d+1)(3c-d)(3c-d-1)}{6},

provided
\nu\big(\mathcal{F}\cap{[2\ell+d,n]\choose 3}\big)<c-d+1
.

Next, let us deal with the case
\nu(\mathcal{F}\cap{[2\ell+d,n]\choose 3})\geq c-d+1
. This is one of the cruxes of the proof. The main idea of the bound for this case is as follows. Fix a
(c-d+1)
-matching in
\mathcal{F}\cap{[2\ell+d,n]\choose 3}
and consider
s
-matchings that consist of three parts:

(i)

this fixed
(c-d+1)
-matching in
\mathcal{F}\cap{[2\ell+d,n]\choose 3}
;

(ii)

some
\ell
-matching in
\mathcal{F}\cap{[2\ell+d-1]\choose 2}
;

(iii)

d-1

3
-element sets, each containing one element in
[2\ell+d-1]
and two elements in
[2\ell+d,n]
.

The sets from the first two groups belong to
\mathcal{F}
. Since
\nu(\mathcal{F})<s
, at least one of the sets from this
s
-matching must belong to
\overline{\mathcal{F}}
, and it must be one of the
d-1
sets from the third group. Averaging over all such matchings gives us a lower bound on
y(3)
. Getting sufficiently many ‘missing’ sets from the third group requires care. Implementation of these ideas depends on the parity of
d(\mathcal{F})
. We start with the easier case of odd
d
.

Lemma 19
.

Let
\mathcal{F}\subset 2^{[n]}
be a shifted family with
\nu(\mathcal{F})<s
. Suppose that
d(\mathcal{F})\geq d
, where
d\leq c+1
and
d
is odd. If
\nu\big(\mathcal{F}\cap{[2\ell+d,n]\choose 3}\big)\geq c-d+1
, then

y(3)\geq(2\ell+d-1)(2d-3).

Proof.

Put
k=\frac{d-1}{2}
. By the definition of
d(\mathcal{F})
, the sets
\{1,2\ell+2k\},\ldots,\{\ell+k,\ell+k+1\}
all belong to
\mathcal{F}
. They form an
(\ell+k)
-matching
\pi_{L}
in
\mathcal{F}\cap{[2\ell+d-1]\choose 2}
. Let
F_{1},\ldots,F_{c-d+1}
be a
(c-d+1)
-matching in
\mathcal{F}\cap{[2\ell+d,n]\choose 3},
and put

Y:=[2\ell+d,n]\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{c-d+1}F_{i}.

Note that
|Y|=(2\ell+3c)-(2\ell+d-1)-3(c-d+1)=2d-2=4k
. Let
\mathcal{G}:=[2\ell+d-1]\times{Y\choose 2}
be the family of three-element sets that contain one element from
[2\ell+d-1]
and two elements from
Y
. We will prove that

|\mathcal{G}\cap\overline{\mathcal{F}}|\geq(2\ell+2k)(4k-1)=(2\ell+d-1)(2d-3),

and thus that
y(3)\geq(2\ell+d-1)(2d-3)
.

Construct a random
s
-matching
\pi
in
(\mathcal{F}\cap{[2\ell+d-1]\choose 2})\cup(\mathcal{F}\cap{[2\ell+d,n]\choose 3})\cup\mathcal{G}
by the following procedure.

•

Add
F_{1},\ldots,F_{c-d+1}
to
\pi
.

•

Choose
\ell
sets from
\pi_{L}
at random and add them to
\pi
. Let
Z
be the set of
d-1
elements in
[2l+d-1]
that are not covered by these
\ell
sets.

•

So far we included
s-d+1
sets from
\mathcal{F}
into
\pi
. We have
d-1
uncovered elements in
Z
and
2d-2
uncovered elements in
Y
.

•

Choose a random
(d-1)
-matching on
Z\cup Y
, consisting of
3
-element sets that contain one element from
Z
and two elements from
Y
and add it to
\pi
.

Let
\xi
be a random variable equal to the number of sets from
\mathcal{G}\cap\overline{\mathcal{F}}
in
\pi
. We use a double counting argument for
\E\xi
. If
\xi=0
, then all
s
sets from
\pi
are in
\mathcal{F}
. It contradicts
\nu(\mathcal{F})<s
. Thus,
\xi\geq 1
and, consequently,
\E\xi\geq 1
.

Take a set
\{x,y_{1},y_{2}\}\in\mathcal{G}
with
x\in[2\ell+d-1]
and
y_{1},y_{2}\in Y
. We have
\Pr[x\in Z]=\frac{k}{\ell+k}
since, in order to be in
Z
,
x
must be in one of the
k
sets from
\pi_{L}
that are not added to
\pi
. Next, we have

\Pr[\{x,y_{1},y_{2}\}\in\pi|x\in Z]={|Y|\choose 2}^{-1}={2d-2\choose 2}^{-1},

since
x
is matched with a random pair of elements from
Y
. Combining these, we get

\Pr[\{x,y_{1},y_{2}\}\in\pi]=\frac{k}{\ell+k}{2d-2\choose 2}^{-1}.

By linearity of expectation, we have
\E\xi=|\mathcal{G}\cap\overline{\mathcal{F}}|\cdot\frac{k}{l+k}{2d-2\choose 2}^{-1}
. Combining it with
\E\xi\geq 1
, we get

y(3)\geq|\mathcal{G}\cap\overline{\mathcal{F}}|\geq\frac{\ell+k}{k}{2d-2\choose 2}=\frac{2\ell+d-1}{d-1}{2d-2\choose 2}=

=(2\ell+d-1)(2d-3).

∎

The case of even
d
is more complicated, and the bound on
y(3)
is slightly weaker. The averaging argument in the coming lemma is the reason for the more complicated definition of
d(\mathcal{F})
in the odd case.

Lemma 20
.

Let
\mathcal{F}\subset 2^{[n]}
be a family with
\nu(\mathcal{F})<s
. Suppose that
d(\mathcal{F})\geq d
, and

is an even integer. Let
X\subset[2\ell+d-1]
be a set such that for any

\mathcal{F}\cap{[2\ell+d-1]\setminus\{x\}\choose 2}
contains an
\big(\ell+\frac{d-2}{2}\big)
-matching. If

For small

Lemma 30
.

Let
d
be an integer,
2\leq d\leq c
. Let
\mathcal{F}\subset 2^{[n]}
be a shifted family with
\nu(\mathcal{F})<s
. Suppose that for all
i\in[\ell]
we have
\{i,2\ell+d+1-i\}\in\mathcal{F}
and
\nu(\mathcal{F}\cap{[2\ell+d+1,n]\choose 3})\geq c-d
. Then

y_{\mathcal{F}}(3)\geq(2d-1)(s+2c-2d)+1.

Moreover, equality is achieved only if
d=2
and
\mathcal{F}^{(3)}
contains all
3
-element sets that cannot be shifted to
\{\ell+2,n-2,n-1\}
.

Note that the condition
\nu(\mathcal{F}\cap{[2\ell+d+1,n]})\geq c-d
is the same as in Lemmas
19
and
20
for
d(\mathcal{F})=d+1
and the condition
\{i,2\ell+d+1-i\}\in\mathcal{F}
for all
i\in[\ell]
is similar to
d(\mathcal{F})>d
for even
d
, but is weaker, since we do not require
\{i,2\ell+d+1-i\}\in\mathcal{F}
for
i\geq\ell+1
. On the other hand, we get a bound on
y(3)
that is approximately twice worse. Therefore, we use Lemma
30
when we can provide the condition
\{i,2\ell+d+1-i\}\in\mathcal{F}
for all
i\in[\ell]
, but we do not have this condition for
i>\ell
. It is a rather common situation for small
c
.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{F}
is shifted and
\nu(\mathcal{F}\cap{[2\ell+d+1,n]\choose 3})\geq c-d
, we have
\nu(\mathcal{F}\cap{[2\ell+d+1,2\ell+3c-2d]\choose 3})\geq c-d
. Note, that
2\ell+3c-2d=n-2d
. Consider
d
sets
\{\ell+i,n-2d+i,n-i+1\},i\in[d]
. Together with the sets
\{i,2\ell+d+1-i\}
for
i\in[\ell]
and the
(c-d)
-matching in
\mathcal{F}\cap{[2\ell+d+1,2\ell+3c-2d]\choose 3}
, these sets form an
s
-matching. Since
\nu(\mathcal{F})<s
and all sets from the other two groups are in
\mathcal{F}
, we have
\{\ell+i,n-2d+i,n-i+1\}\notin\mathcal{F}
for some
i\in[d]
. Since
\mathcal{F}
is shifted, all sets, that may be shifted to
\{l+i,n-2d+i,n-i+1\}
are also not in
\mathcal{F}
.

Next, we estimate the minimum over
i
of the number of sets that can be shifted to
\{\ell+i,n-2d+i,n-i+1\}
. For all
x\in[\ell+i,n-2d+i-1]
and all

such that
\{y,z\}
may be shifted to
\{n-2d+i,n-i+1\}
the set
\{x,y,z\}
may be shifted to
\{\ell+i,n-2d+i,n-i+1\}
. We have
(n-2d+i-1)-(\ell+i-1)=s+2c-2d
such
x
and
{2d-i+1\choose 2}-{2d-2i+1\choose 2}=\frac{(4d-3i+1)i}{2}
such pairs
\{y,z\}
, that is,
(s+2c-2d)\frac{(4d-3i+1)i}{2}
such triples
\{x,y,z\}
. All these triples are different, since we count unordered pairs
x,y
and for all triples
x\leq n-2d+i-1
and
\min{(y,z)}\geq n-2d+i
. Moreover, we did not count a set
\{n-2,n-1,n\}
, that of course may be shifted to
\{\ell+i,n-2d+i,n-i+1\}
, because in all considered triples
x\leq n-2d+i-1\leq n-d-1\leq n-3
(here we use
d\geq 2
). If
d>2
or
i<d
, we also did not count the set
\{n-3,n-1,n\}
, that also may be shifted to
\{\ell+i,n-2d+i,n-i+1\}
. Therefore, we have at least

(s+2c-2d)\frac{(4d-3i+1)i}{2}+1

sets that may be shifted to
\{\ell+i,n-2d+i,n-i+1\}
and this bound is strict only if
d=2
and
i=2
.

Finally, we apply a usual convexity argument. Since for some
i\in[d]
we have
\{\ell+i,n-2d+i,n-i+1\}\notin\mathcal{F}
and at least

(s+2c-2d)\frac{(4d-3i+1)i}{2}+1

sets may be shifted to
\{l+i,n-2d+i,n-i+1\}\notin\mathcal{F}
, we have

y_{\mathcal{F}}(3)\geq\min_{i\in[d]}\Big\{(s+2c-2d)\frac{(4d-3i+1)i}{2}+1\Big\}.

This bound is an upward convex function, the minimum is attained at one of the ends. For
d\geq 2
we have
\frac{(d+1)d}{2}\geq 2d-1
, and so the minimum is attained for
i=1
and we have the desired bound

d=i=2
. Moreover, if
{\mathcal{F}}
attains equality then it must contain all
3
-element sets that cannot be shifted to

\{\ell+i,n-2d+i,n-i+1\}
.

∎

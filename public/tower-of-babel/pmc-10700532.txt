Multimodal treatment with chemoradiotherapy, regional hyperthermia and interstitial brachytherapy for a huge locally advanced cervical cancer: A case report
Authors: Takashi Saito, Motohiro Murakami, Taisuke Sumiya, Daisuke Kobayashi, Haruka Shirataki, Den Fujioka, Keiichiro Baba, Hiroya Itagaki, Yuri Tenjimbayashi, Toyomi Satoh, Hideyuki Sakurai
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
Highlight•Using chemoradiotherapy, regional hyperthermia (RHT), and interstitial brachytherapy (ISBT), a patient with a 10-cm locally advanced cervical cancer survived for three years without recurrence.•ISBT has not delivered an sufficient dose to the tumor, but RHT may have compensated for this deficiency.•The case demonstrates the possibility of additional options for refractory conditions, highlighting the need for additional research.

Introduction

Cervical cancer is an international health concern, with over 600,000 new cases reported worldwide in 2020. Of these, approximately 37% are locally advanced cases [1], [2]. Thus, development of effective treatment strategies for locally advanced cervical cancer (LACC) is crucial for improving patient outcomes. The standard treatment for LACC involves chemoradiotherapy (CRT), using a combination of external radiotherapy and brachytherapy [3]. Regional hyperthermia (RHT) and interstitial brachytherapy (ISBT) are not considered to be standard treatments, but are good options because both can improve CRT outcomes through distinct mechanisms: RHT provides a biological enhancement effect and ISBT improves the geometrical dose distribution [4], [5]. There are reports on the effectiveness of CRT combined with RHT or ISBT in LACC, but there is a lack of data on the combination of CRT, RHT and ISBT. In this case report, we present a patient with a huge LACC with 3-year relapse-free survival after treatment combining CRT, RHT and ISBT.

Case presentation

The patient was a woman in her 50 s who had been generally healthy and had no significant family medical history. She was referred to our gynecology department with suspected cervical cancer, presenting with abnormal genital bleeding and abdominal pain. In a gynecological examination, an irregular lesion was observed in the cervix, extending to both pelvic walls. Biopsy confirmed this lesion to be a poorly to undifferentiated carcinoma, partially mixed with non-keratinizing squamous cell carcinoma. Subsequent examination using MRI unveiled a substantial tumor measuring around 10 cm in diameter, which extended from the cervix to the entire uterine corpus. The gross tumor volume (GTV) was calculated to be 363 cc (Fig. 1). Both the MRI and colonoscopy results showed no evidence of tumor infiltration into the rectum or bladder. Contrast-enhanced CT confirmed metastases extending to the left common iliac lymph nodes and para-aortic lymph nodes. The patient was referred for CRT, including 50 Gy in 25 fractions of external radiotherapy to the whole pelvis (with the central shielding of 10 Gy) and para-aortic lymph nodes. For the entire external irradiation, the 4-field box technique with 10 MV X-rays was used. The central shielding was employed after 40 Gy had been administered, utilizing anteroposterior opposed fields, with the aim of reducing the radiation dose to critical organs such as the rectum and bladder. Concurrently with external radiotherapy, a total of five cycles of weekly cisplatin were administered at a dosage of 40 mg/m2. Pelvic RHT was performed once a week, immediately following the radiotherapy session, using a Thermotron-RF8 system (Yamamoto Vinita Co Ltd, Osaka, Japan). The RHT treatment consisted of four cycles, with each cycle performed as a 60-minute session with a radiofrequency setting of up to 1500 W.

After completion of pelvic irradiation with 50 Gy in 25 fractions, high-dose-rate ISBT was administered using iridium-192 twice daily with a total dose of 24 Gy over 4 fractions. Under lumbar and epidural anesthesia, a tandem was inserted into the uterine cavity. Subsequently, metal needles were percutaneously inserted through the vagina and perineum using the Martinez Universal Perineal Interstitial Template system. The contouring of high-risk clinical target volume (HR-CTV) and intermediate-risk clinical target volume (IR-CTV) was carried out based on the International Commission on Radiation Units and Measurements report 89 and the recommendations from the Groupe Européen de Curiethérapie – European Society for Radiotherapy & Oncology. [7], [8]. The ISBT plan aimed for a 6 Gy prescription to the HR-CTV. However, it was discovered that inserting the needles on the right side of the tumor was challenging due to the calcified fibroid, thus limiting the insertion to 10 metal needles. As a result, several regions of the HR-CTV, especially along the right edge, did not receive the planned full dose, leading to a 90th percentile dose (D90) of 5.1 Gy for the HR-CTV. The MRI performed during the treatment phase with a radiation dose of 36 Gy indicated a tumor volume of 133 cc. The MRI conducted three months after treatment revealed a substantial decrease in tumor size to 22 cc. During the following period, the tumor continued to shrink, and a state of complete remission was reached 2 years after treatment, as shown in Fig. 3. There was no evident metastasis on CT and the patient has been recurrence-free for three years since initiation of the treatment. Discussion

Large cervical cancer tumors are generally associated with a poor prognosis, and those exceeding 6 cm in diameter are an independent risk factor for a poorer prognosis [9]. A large cohort study also showed a trend for a decrease in radiation dose delivered to the HR-CTV as the stage of cervical cancer advanced. This pattern is mirrored in the 5-year pelvic control rate of 87% for Stage IIB and a significantly lower rate of 67% for Stage IIIB [10]. This indicates that for advanced cervical cancers, regular intracavitary brachytherapy may be inadequate for delivering the required dose, resulting in diminished pelvic control rates. The American Brachytherapy Society has published detailed guidelines on brachytherapy, highlighting that poorly fitting intracavitary applicators, large lesions, and lower vaginal involvement are specific indications for application of ISBT in LACC [11]. The tumor in our case had a diameter of 10 cm, and given the narrowness of the vagina, which complicated transvaginal needle insertion, the decision was made to use ISBT. The outcome in our patient suggests that in a case in which an insufficient radiation dose is anticipated for cervical tumors with a diameter of over 6 cm or at Stage III or higher, consideration of use of ISBT is warranted.

Even using ISBT, it was challenging to deliver the required radiation dose in this case. The HR-CTV D90 per ISBT session was 5.1 Gy, while a dose of 6.5 Gy is typically considered necessary [12]. In such cases, other methods to enhance the antitumor effect are considered, with concurrent CRT being the most common. Concurrent CRT for LACC is recognized for its beneficial radiosensitizing effect and improvement of treatment outcomes [13]. However, a meta-analysis revealed that addition of chemotherapy only achieved a 5-year survival benefit of 7–10% for patients at Stage I-II and a more modest benefit of 3% for patients at Stage III-IVa [14].

Given this background, options such as hyperthermia, which serves as an alternative sensitizing agent, may be useful for enhancing the antitumor effect. A randomized controlled trial (RCT) showed that addition of RHT to CRT for LACC significantly improved overall survival rates [15] and another RCT indicated a heightened effect of adding hyperthermia to CRT for Stage III cervical cancer [16]. Another advantage of hyperthermia is that its addition to CRT does not increase acute or late adverse events [15]. These findings indicate a potential to compensate for the insufficient effect of CRT in cases of Stage III or above. The success of hyperthermia is believed to hinge on achieving effective heating. Ohguri et al. analyzed cases in which rectal temperature was measured during hyperthermia treatment, and found that a higher cumulative equivalent time at 43 °C for 90% of the temperature distribution was significantly associated with better local relapse-free survival [17]. However, in the present case, rectal temperature was not measured, which prevented verification of achievement of sufficient heating. Thus, future studies should include rectal temperature measurements for objective evaluation of the quality and efficacy of hyperthermia treatment.

Conclusion

We have presented a case in which 3-year relapse-free survival was achieved for a massive cervical cancer by simultaneous application of regional hyperthermia and interstitial brachytherapy, in addition to chemoradiotherapy. This case demonstrates the potential of these additional therapies for refractory conditions. Moving forward, it will be important to assess the efficacy and safety of this combination therapy in more cases.

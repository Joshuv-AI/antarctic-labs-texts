Environmental monitoring and enterprise reduction of pollution and carbon emission: a micro-level examination based on energy scale and intensity effects
Authors: Yunyun Zhang, Yanli Shi, Xinling Li, Hui Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundAs China restructures its energy landscape through transition, environmental monitoring serves as vital technological infrastructure. It directly supports the achievement of carbon neutrality objectives. This paper uses the number and density of environmental monitoring stations established around industrial enterprises as proxy variables for environmental supervision and the China Industrial Enterprise Pollution Emission Database. It explores the impact of regional environmental monitoring on enterprise reduction of pollution and carbon emissions based on the regulatory and geographic structure.ResultsThe empirical results of this paper indicate that (1) Environmental monitoring significantly reduces firms’ SO₂ emissions and emission intensity, and also has a marked abatement effect on other pollutants such as exhaust gases and industrial smoke (dust) particulates; (2) The decline in emissions occurs mainly through three channels: reducing the scale of energy use, lowering energy intensity, and promoting production technology upgrades; (3) Environmental monitoring exhibits a significant co-benefit of pollution reduction and carbon mitigation, decreasing not only pollutant emissions but also the total volume and intensity of CO₂ emissions.ConclusionThis study demonstrates that environmental monitoring can effectively drive enterprises to reduce energy consumption, enhance technological innovation, and achieve coordinated pollution and carbon reduction. Policy implications suggest that strengthening monitoring networks and adopting differentiated regulatory mechanisms are crucial for promoting energy efficiency, fostering green innovation, and advancing the realization of China’s “dual carbon” goals.

Introduction

Since the reform and opening-up, China’s economy has enjoyed rapid growth and substantial economic dividends. In 2023, China’s GDP reached $17,800 billion, accounting for approximately 16.9% of total global output, ranking second in the world.1 However, increasingly severe environmental problems have accompanied this rapid economic growth. The country’s traditional crude economic development model has aggravated China’s environmental pollution. The International Energy Agency’s Global Carbon Emissions Report 2023 pointed out that China’s carbon emissions increased by 565 million tons, bringing the total carbon dioxide emissions to 12.6 billion tons. The use of fossil fuels is increasing year by year, leading to large emissions of greenhouse gases and air pollutants, posing challenges for China's climate change response and air pollution prevention efforts [1]. Given this background, energy transition has become an important initiative for China to realize sustainable development [2]. Crucially, greenhouse gases and atmospheric pollutants share common emission sources [3, 4], as fossil fuel combustion simultaneously emits both atmospheric pollutants (including sulfur dioxide and particulate matter) and carbon dioxide. This fundamental characteristic implies a strong policy alignment between carbon mitigation and source-level air pollution control [5], demonstrating that enhanced source emission reduction can generate significant co-benefits.

To achieve this goal, transitioning from conventional fossil fuel-based energy systems to clean and low-carbon alternatives has become imperative. Such transformation requires not only technological innovation and policy support, but more critically, scientifically rigorous environmental data for evidence-based decision making. In recent years, China's implementation of air pollution prevention policies has effectively reduced atmospheric emissions and incrementally improved air quality, though fundamental enhancements in ambient conditions remain elusive. The government has strengthened this effort through key institutional measures: In 2014, the Ministry of Environmental Protection introduced an interview mechanism to boost corporate environmental participation, followed by the 2015 revised Environmental Protection Law that significantly enhanced regulatory enforcement. These developments culminated in the 14th Five-Year Plan's explicit advocacy for green production and ecological civilization, aiming for modernization with human-nature harmony. Building on this foundation, the Ministry of Ecology and Environment proposed in 2021 a "unified monitoring network and dataset" initiative to advance environmental monitoring standards. In 2024, the Ministry of Ecology and Environment further issued the Implementation Opinions on Accelerating the Establishment of a Modern Ecological and Environmental Monitoring System, which emphasized the need to comprehensively advance the construction of a modern monitoring system to fully support the in-depth fight against pollution and the building of a Beautiful China. Consequently, its in-depth examination not only provides theoretical foundations for carbon neutrality goals but also offers critical insights for policy and industrial decision-making.

Based on this, this paper aims to address the following scientific questions: (1) Can environmental monitoring significantly promote pollution reduction in industrial enterprises? (2) Through what mechanisms does environmental monitoring affect enterprise pollution reduction? (3) Can environmental monitoring achieve the coordinated reduction of pollutants and carbon emissions? These findings offer practical implications for formulating targeted industrial emission-reduction policies and accelerating China’s low-carbon energy transition.

Literature review

As a fundamental component of China's environmental protection regime, environmental monitoring plays an indispensable role in the energy transition process, serving as a crucial pillar for fostering harmonious human-nature development [6]. Consequently, its in-depth examination not only provides theoretical foundations for carbon neutrality goals but also offers critical insights for policy and industrial decision-making. In recent years, a series of environmental protection policies have been implemented, such as an emissions trading system [7], carbon emissions trading system [8], environmental information disclosure [9, 10], that is intended to solve the problem of environmental pollution in China fundamentally.

There are two main perspectives on whether environmental policies effectively reduce pollutant emissions and improve environmental quality. One is based on the Porter hypothesis. As an essential starting point to alleviate environmental problems, moderate levels of environmental regulation will promote technological progress [11, 12], transform the production mode of enterprises to clean efficiency, and achieve emission reduction [13, 14]. Li et al. [15] demonstrated that implementing China’s environmental protection tax significantly reduced pollutant emissions from the power generation industry. Implementing environmental taxes in Europe can also significantly reduce energy consumption, effectively reduce greenhouse gas emissions, improve overall environmental performance and promote local, sustainable development [16]. By exploring the impact of daily punishment policies on enterprises’ emission reduction, Bu and Shi [17] found that strict and mandatory environmental regulations effectively encourage enterprises to control pollution, thus promoting a shift to long-term green development strategies and energy-efficient emission-reducing production methods. Aghion et al. [18] showed that when automobile industry enterprises face higher fuel taxes, they tend to implement more innovations in clean technology to reduce vehicle emissions. The variability in the intensity of environmental policies across regions can bring about the pollution sanctuary effect [19]. That is, enterprises transfer pollution through relocation to avoid administrative penalties and high governance costs [20, 21]. Utilizing data from 277 Chinese cities between 2012 and 2016, Zhao et al. [23] examined how environmental policies influence haze pollution spatially. Their research revealed a critical threshold: only when regulatory measures surpass this level can they effectively mitigate haze levels. Additionally, haze concentration in a given area is invariably shaped by the environmental policies of adjacent regions [24]. Drawing on firm-level data in China, Li et al. [25] developed a composite metric to assess environmental regulation and collaborative governance. Their analysis indicated that stricter environmental policies correlate with higher corporate relocation rates, underscoring the regulatory-induced pollution displacement effect.

The effectiveness of environmental policies is frequently constrained by the competing demands of regional economic growth and ecological objectives. Therefore, whether a harmonious balance between economic and environmental can be effectively achieved has always been an important issue for environmental policy researchers, and empirical studies have shown that environmental regulation often damages economic development [26, 27]. Therefore, driven by the inherent logic of “growth for promotion,” it is inevitable that local officials will sacrifice the environment within their jurisdictions [28, 29], thus securing political promotion. China’s unique governance model, which combines political centralization with economic decentralization, exacerbates conflicts between multi-tiered policy objectives. Local officials may intervene to influence target-relevant information [30], while problems of misreporting, underreporting, and concealment of local pollution emission data also emerge regularly [31]. When engaged in high-quality economic development, how to balance environmental quality improvement and sustainable economic development is the ultimate goal of environmental policy effectiveness. In 2007, the Chinese government issued the Measures for Assessing the Emission Reduction of Major Pollutants, integrating pollution control metrics into the evaluation framework for local officials. This marked a shift from a GDP-centric assessment system to a dual-pillar approach that equally emphasizes economic and environmental outcomes [32, 33]. Whether environmental monitoring policy can play an influential role under multidimensional goal constraints is also an urgent problem to be considered.

Most scholars have argued that environmental monitoring and government regulation are important factors in improving environmental conditions [34, 35] and reducing pollutants and carbon emissions [36, 37]. Since air pollutants and CO2 are homogenous, curbing air pollutant emissions will help achieve synergistic effects in mitigating climate change [38]. For example, carbon dioxide and pollution emissions mainly originate from fossil energy consumption, and reducing reliance on energy naturally reduces carbon dioxide and pollutants emissions [39, 40]. At the same time, improving technological efficiency is the way to reduce pollution and carbon emissions. Technological effects can simultaneously achieve output growth and pollutant emission reduction [41] and further reduce CO2 emissions [42, 43]. Research by Yu and Morotomi [44] demonstrated that China's environmental legislation has been effective in lowering atmospheric concentrations of harmful particulates including PM2.5 and sulfur dioxide, resulting in measurable air quality improvements. Since carbon emissions and air pollution share common sources [45], climate policies can simultaneously address both environmental challenges by curbing greenhouse gas output and reducing contaminant releases [46].Nevertheless, an alternative perspective suggests that regulatory measures may produce unintended consequences. The "Green Paradox" hypothesis proposed by Sinn [47] indicates that environmental controls could paradoxically stimulate more intensive fossil fuel extraction in the immediate term, ultimately elevating both energy usage and CO₂ output [48].

In terms of our research and the literature, most studies focus on the impact of macroenvironmental policies on regional or enterprise energy conservation and emission reduction [10], technological innovation [49, 50], and green transformation [51, 52]. Regarding the emission-reducing effect of environmental monitoring policies, the current literature mainly studies the emission mitigation effects at the regional level. For example, Zou et al. [53] used satellite data to study a monitoring area based on the periodic air quality monitoring every six days stipulated by the Federal Clean Air Act. They found that intermittent environmental monitoring may lead to strategic changes in pollution activities. Air quality was significantly worse during the unmonitored period. In the monitored period, the increased use of air quality warnings by cities suggests an essential role for local governments in coordinating emission reductions. Liu et al. [38] assessed the impact of air quality monitoring for local PM2.5 emissions in China using urban-level data from 2001 to 2015. They showed that national air quality monitoring significantly reduced local PM2.5 emissions. Few scholars investigate the micro-level impacts of environmental monitoring on corporate emission behaviors.

In summary, this paper uses the distribution of environmental monitoring stations around industrial enterprises as a measure of the environmental monitoring intensity they face. Combining this with pollution emission data from Chinese industrial enterprises between 2000 and 2013, the study deeply investigates whether it can effectively promote pollution and carbon reduction by enterprises. This research not only provides a theoretical basis for achieving carbon neutrality but also offers critical insights for policy formulation and industrial practice.

Methodology and data sources

Data description

Environmental monitoring stations

Environmental monitoring is an essential environmental protection task in China. It objectively evaluates environmental quality in China by analyzing the emissions of various pollutants. Environmental monitoring stations were first established in the 1970 s and have undergone 40 years of development. In 1973, the first national environmental protection conference was held to place environmental protection on the agenda. In 1980, when the first national environmental monitoring conference was held, there were more than 300 environmental monitoring stations. In 1983, the Ministry of Environmental Protection issued the National Environmental Monitoring Management Regulations, which clarify the responsibilities of environmental monitoring stations. During the 6th and 7th Five-Year Plans, environmental monitoring stations were established from the central to local (provincial, municipal, and county) levels. The environmental monitoring stations developed rapidly during this period. During the 8th Five-Year Plan, the basic principles of environmental monitoring work were proposed, and a preliminary environmental monitoring network was constructed. In 2002, the State Environmental Protection Administration issued the Notice on the Issuance of Environmental Monitoring Station Construction Standards (for Trial Implementation), which formally proposes standards for constructing environmental monitoring stations. In September 2007, the Environmental Monitoring Management Measures were issued and implemented, which formally required environmental protection departments to engage in monitoring. The environmental monitoring stations at all levels comprise a complete environmental monitoring network. There are 2223 monitoring stations attached to government environmental protection departments. Environmental authorities have progressively integrated monitoring systems as a core component of China's ecological governance framework, where they serve a pivotal function in emission regulation and environmental quality management. This investigation examines the correlation between monitoring station locations and corporate emission patterns by synthesizing geospatial data from China's environmental monitoring network with industrial enterprise records. Chinese industrial enterprise data

This study employs two authoritative data sources: the China Industrial Enterprise Pollution Emission Database, which captures comprehensive environmental metrics from industrial facilities responsible for over 85% of county-level emissions, including detailed records of wastewater discharges, atmospheric pollutants like SO₂ and particulate matter, pollution control equipment such as desulfurization units, and energy consumption patterns covering various fossil fuels and water usage, establishing it as China's most complete firm-level emission registry [53], alongside the widely utilized China Industrial Enterprise Database containing production information.

Following established research protocols [54, 55], we implemented a rigorous data cleaning procedure that involved removing entities with invalid identification codes, eliminating establishments employing fewer than eight workers, excluding firms reporting non-positive financial indicators including revenues, liabilities, and asset values, and filtering out negative emission records, with all industry classifications standardized according to the 2012 National Economic Industry Classification system before ultimately creating a merged panel dataset through unique enterprise identifier matching that combines both production and pollution characteristics.

Variable description

Dependent variables

This paper measures the dependent variables based on the amount and intensity of SO2 emissions from industrial enterprises. lnSO2, the amount of SO2 emissions, is calculated by the logarithm of the absolute value of the SO2 emissions of industrial enterprises plus 1. lnSI, the emission intensity of SO2, is characterized by the ratio of SO2 emissions (kg)/current value of industrial output (RMB) and is also in logarithms.

Key independent variables

This study measures environmental monitoring station distribution by building upon methodologies from Degryse and Ongena [56], with implementation proceeding through four key stages: (1) Initial station locations are sourced from the List and Profile of National Environmental Monitoring Institutions published by the China Environmental Science Press in 1994, containing 1,861 precisely addressed stations across 31 provinces, which after manual verification yields 1,786 currently operational facilities; (2) To control for temporal effects since our sample begins in 2000, we reconcile station records with updated registries from local Ecological Environment Bureaus, subsequently excluding enterprises located near stations established after our study period; (3) The enterprise address information is obtained from the industrial enterprise database. This paper queries the Tian-Yancha platform to obtain missing address information for the observations. Then, we convert the manually collected environmental monitoring stations and the detailed address information on industrial enterprises obtained from the industrial enterprise database and the Tian-Yancha platform into longitude and latitude coordinates using Xgeocoding software with the help of the Amap platform and finally count the total number of environmental monitoring stations distributed within a radius of 5, 10 and 15 km around each enterprise; (4) At the end of the twentieth century, there was no joint environmental governance across regions, and environmental monitoring institutions within different administrative divisions could not conduct cross-regional sampling surveys.

When enterprises in adjacent cities have pollution problems, the person in charge of the environmental monitoring stations in a city is not motivated or right to go to the adjacent city enterprises for sampling and testing. The environmental monitoring stations cannot have a supervisory and deterrent effect on enterprises in adjacent cities. Therefore, when extracting the number of environmental monitoring stations within a certain radius around the enterprise, ignoring the administrative division boundary problem may lead to estimation errors. However, the scale of the data is too large, and the spatial connection function of ArcGIS map software cannot be directly used to consider the administrative division demarcation problem. Therefore, we choose to import the latitude and longitude data on industrial enterprises and environmental monitoring stations for each year into ArcGIS map software. The two data sets were given “city administrative code” information. Then, we further use the filtering function of ArcGIS map software to divide the full sample into subsamples for 371 cities based on city administrative codes. Finally, the number of environmental monitoring stations within a specific radiation range around the industrial enterprise can be extracted using the spatial connection function of ArcGIS map software. We choose the natural logarithm of the number of environmental monitoring stations distributed within a certain radius around the enterprise plus one (station5, station10, station15), and the ratio of that number to the area of the radius (denstity5, denstity10, denstity15) as the key independent variables.

Control variables

To control for potential confounding factors, this paper selects the fixed assets ratio (fata), firm scale (size), enterprise age (age), asset liquidity (liquidity), and return on total assets (roa) as control variables: (1) fixed assets ratio (fata), calculated as fixed assets divided by total assets; (2) firm scale (size), represented by the natural logarithm of total assets; (3) operational tenure (age), derived by taking the natural logarithm of one plus the years since establishment; (4) current ratio (liquidity), measured as current assets relative to total assets; and (5) return on assets (roa), computed as net income divided by total assets.

Descriptive statistics

The skewness and kurtosis of the main variables approximate characteristics of a normal distribution. However, the Jarque–Bera tests for these variables uniformly reject the null hypothesis of normality. Under the classical regression framework, strict normality of variables is not required, as normality primarily affects inference efficiency (e.g., T-test and F-test) rather than the consistency or unbiasedness of estimators [57]. Therefore, despite rejecting normality, the estimators remain consistent and unbiased. To ensure result accuracy, the normality of regression residuals is further tested. The Jarque–Bera statistics for the residuals of lnSO₂ and lnSI regressions are 7050 and 5836, respectively, both with p-values of 0.000, indicating deviations from normality. Nevertheless, given the large sample size (N = 296,055) and by virtue of the central limit theorem and law of large numbers, the OLS estimators exhibit asymptotic normality and remain asymptotically efficient. Additionally, the use of robust standard errors alleviates strict normality assumptions [57]. Hence, to ensure consistency and unbiasedness, clustered robust standard errors are employed in all subsequent estimations.

Secondly, the Wooldridge test for serial correlation in panel data indicates the presence of within-group autocorrelation. The Modified Wald test for heteroskedasticity shows that when the dependent variable is lnSO₂, the F-values for station10 and density10 are 7.87 × 1012 and 3.71 × 1012, respectively, with p-values of 0.000 and 0.0000. The null hypothesis of homoskedasticity is rejected, indicating heteroskedasticity in the data. Therefore, this paper employs clustered robust standard errors and ordinary least squares estimation on a two-way fixed effects model to correct for heteroskedasticity and autocorrelation in the empirical estimation [58].

Furthermore, regarding cross-sectional dependence (CSD), the test requires calculating pairwise residual correlations across all individuals. However, it is infeasible in Stata when the number of cross-sections is extremely large. Since this study utilized the panels of 294,059 industrial enterprises, it is a typical large-N and small-T dataset. Therefore, the direct calculation of all pairwise correlations exceeds the matrix dimension limit of Stata. To address this, we conduct the Pesaran test for cross-sectional dependence on a randomly drawn subsample, yielding a test statistic of 15.717, which is significant at the 1% level, thereby rejecting the null hypothesis and indicating the presence of CSD. To mitigate the impact of CSD on parameter estimation, we employ city-level clustered robust standard errors in the empirical analysis [59]. This choice is motivated by the fact that enterprises are geographically distributed by city, and correlations among firms primarily arise from common city-level shocks and spatial dependence (e.g., local economic fluctuations, environmental policy enforcement, and meteorological conditions). Thus, clustering at the city level allows residuals within the same city to be arbitrarily correlated, effectively alleviating biases in standard error estimation caused by CSD.

Addressing endogeneity

The analysis faces two potential endogeneity concerns regarding the relationship between monitoring station placement and pollution outcomes. On the one hand, areas with higher densities of environmental monitoring stations tend to experience stricter environmental regulation, thereby strengthening firms’ incentives to reduce emissions and lowering pollution intensity. However, governments also tend to deploy more monitoring infrastructure in heavily polluted areas to enhance oversight, creating a bidirectional causality issue. On the other hand, despite controlling for observable confounding factors, potential omitted variable bias cannot be fully ruled out. Therefore, identifying suitable instrumental variables is an effective way to mitigate potential endogeneity and obtain a more precise estimate of the relationship between monitoring station deployment and firm-level emissions. To address these identification challenges, we employ an instrumental variables approach requiring instruments that satisfy both exogeneity (affecting pollution outcomes only through monitoring station placement) and relevance (being strongly correlated with station placement). Thus, an eligible instrument must be able to explain variation in monitoring station density without directly or indirectly affecting pollution emissions through any other channels.

This paper adopts urban topographic relief (dixing) and temperature inversion (temp) as instrumental variables and conducts estimation using the two-stage least squares (2SLS) method. In general, environmental monitoring stations are more likely to be located in flat, unobstructed urban areas, leading to a higher concentration of government oversight in such locations. Meanwhile, temperature inversion is a meteorological phenomenon in which air temperature increases with altitude. It can exacerbate the accumulation of air pollutants and raise local pollution concentrations. As a result, governments may place more monitoring stations in areas with frequent inversions to strengthen pollution control. At the same time, urban topographic relief and temperature inversion are determined by natural conditions and is unrelated to firms’ production behavior or emission decisions, thus satisfying the exclusion restriction.

Transmission mechanism test

How do environmental monitoring stations affect enterprise pollution emissions? This section further discusses the transmission mechanism of their effect.

Transmission mechanism: energy consumption

Coal-based energy consumption is an important reason for aggravating pollutant emissions [60]. This section further verifies whether the environmental monitoring stations reduces pollutant emissions by reducing coal consumption, improving energy use and energy intensity. We use the absolute value of coal consumption plus 1 to take the logarithm form to measure the scale of coal consumption (lncoal); and the ratio of coal consumption (tons)/industrial output value in current prices (million yuan) to take the logarithm to measure the coal consumption intensity (lncoalI). Table 11 presents the empirical findings. Discussion

Environmental monitoring and enterprise reduction of pollution and carbon emissions

The improvement of China’s environmental monitoring system is key to modernizing environmental governance. Although many studies show environmental policies reduce firm pollution [8–10], few examine their effects on firms’ emissions from the perspective of environmental quality monitoring. Using data from Chinese industrial enterprises and pollution emissions combined with ArcGIS, this study assesses the impact of environmental protection monitoring stations on pollution reduction. Results show that these stations significantly decrease firms’ emission levels and intensity, and this finding is still valid after a series of robust tests. This proves the effectiveness of environmental monitoring infrastructure in pollution control.

Mechanism analysis indicates that environmental protection monitoring stations primarily control pollution by enhancing energy efficiency and promoting technological innovation. Firstly, the stations reduce pollutant emissions by lowering coal consumption, improving energy use, and reducing energy intensity. Previous studies have identified fossil fuel consumption as a major source of pollution [39], so reducing reliance on energy inputs naturally decreases emissions. Secondly, these stations encourage firms to adopt production technology innovations as a form of upstream pollution control. According to the Porter Hypothesis, appropriate environmental regulation intensity fosters technological progress [11, 12], facilitating a shift toward cleaner, more efficient production methods and resulting in emission reductions [13, 14].

Further analysis reveals a synergy between pollution reduction and carbon emission mitigation associated with environmental protection monitoring stations. Greenhouse gases and air pollutants share common sources [3, 9]. The combustion of fossil fuels emits both atmospheric pollutants, such as sulfur dioxide and fine particulate matter, as well as carbon dioxide. Therefore, the establishment of environmental protection monitoring stations not only reduces the volume and intensity of conventional pollutants but also effectively lowers firms’ carbon emissions and carbon intensity.

Policy recommendations

Although China's environmental quality has improved against the background of peak carbon and carbon neutrality, China continues to face pressure to achieve the high-quality development requirements of improving environmental quality and economic growth in a short period. Although the implementation of environmental policies may improve regional environmental quality, in an attempt to further enhance the emission reduction effect of environmental policies, this study puts forward the following policy suggestions.

Firstly, based on existing environmental monitoring stations. Since 2012, China has been progressively upgrading its automated air quality surveillance network, though deployment remains largely concentrated in prefecture-level urban centers, especially in the economically developed eastern provinces, and has not been thoroughly implemented at the county level. The results show that environmental monitoring stations effectively reduce the surrounding enterprises’ pollution emission scale and intensity. To this end, the government should take the area where the existing environmental monitoring stations are established as the center and promote the spread of automatic environmental quality monitoring stations to the surrounding areas to achieve full coverage gradually.

Secondly, there should be complete coverage of pollutant monitoring in the environmental quality monitoring system. China's environmental monitoring, especially the automatic air quality monitoring system in place since 2012, has not achieved comprehensive coverage of pollutant monitoring, especially greenhouse gases. Thus, the government should promote enterprise pollution reduction through mandatory environmental policy and integrate pollutants that have not yet formed obvious pollution and greenhouse gases into the environmental monitoring system to synchronize pollutant and greenhouse gas monitoring. Finally, environmental monitoring and governance promote the successful implementation of the national pollution reduction and carbon reduction strategy.

Finally, the government should encourage enterprise technology research and development and shift from structural emission reduction to technology emission reduction. From the perspective of macro market emission reduction, the current stage of China’s pollution reduction is still dominated by energy restructuring. However, realizing long-term or fundamental emission reduction undeniably relies primarily on technological innovation. Therefore, besides actively promoting environmental quality monitoring policies, the government should also provide a long-term incentive mechanism for innovation in green technologies.

Shortcomings and prospects

Although this study validates the pollution and carbon reduction effects of environmental protection monitoring stations, it is subject to certain limitations. Firstly, constrained by data availability, the Chinese Industrial Enterprise Pollution Emission Database—widely recognized as the most comprehensive firm-level pollution dataset in China [54]—is limited in its temporal coverage, reflecting a common limitation in current research within this field. Secondly, due to the limited precision of geographic data, spatial mismatches between monitoring stations and firm locations may introduce measurement errors, which could affect the accuracy of the evaluation results presented herein.

Future research can be advanced in several ways. Firstly, by incorporating larger samples and longer time series to comprehensively assess the long-term impact of environmental monitoring on corporate sustainability. Secondly, by integrating remote sensing and enterprise GPS data to enhance spatial identification and matching accuracy. Furthermore, spatial econometric models and machine learning techniques can be applied to uncover the complex dynamics between environmental monitoring and firm emissions. Meanwhile, as China’s monitoring data continue to improve, empirical analyses of coordinated reductions in pollutants and greenhouse gases will deepen, providing solid theoretical and practical support for achieving the “dual carbon” goals.

In practice, the development of environmental monitoring systems should fully utilize IoT, big data, and AI technologies. This will help build intelligent monitoring networks and improve data disclosure mechanisms. Such efforts will shift environmental governance from “end-of-pipe control” to “whole-process supervision.” Meanwhile, network coverage should be expanded to include counties and rural areas. This will promote coordinated governance across regions. These measures will support the achievement of ecological civilization and high-quality development goals in China.

Conclusion

This study employs ArcGIS to analyze the spatial distribution of monitoring stations around industrial firms. By integrating data from the China Industrial Enterprises Database and the China Industrial Enterprises Pollution Emission Database (2000–2013), it investigates whether the proximity of monitoring stations affects both the volume and concentration of pollutants discharged by neighboring enterprises. The results show that (1) Environmental monitoring stations established in various regions in China have apparent pollution-reducing effects on surrounding enterprises. The larger the number and density of environmental monitoring stations around enterprises are, the smaller the scale and intensity of pollution emissions. The robustness checks unanimously reinforce this observation, including the endogeneity test, which adds covariates, replaces dependent and independent variables, and excludes other environmental policies. (2) The mechanism analysis reveals that monitoring stations curb pollution emissions mainly through optimizing energy use and promoting technological innovation. (3) Further analysis finds that the environmental monitoring stations have a synergistic emission reduction effect, that is, they not only inhibit the pollutant emission, but also effectively reduce the carbon dioxide emission.

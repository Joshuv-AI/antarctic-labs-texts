A Multiscale Camassa–Holm Equation
Darryl D. Holm, Maneesh Kumar Singh

Abstract
A system of equations for Multiscale Geodesic Flow (MGF) is introduced whose solutions illustrate the paradigm of whorls within whorls within whorls, introduced by L. F. Richardson in 1922 to describe the cascade of energy in fluid turbulence. Numerical simulations are given for MGF on S 1 S^{1} , where the MGF equation comprises a multiscale generalisation of the Camassa-Holm equation (CHE) whose emergent singular solutions generalise the peakon solutions of the CHE.



1
Introduction

Based on the soliton-bearing Camassa–Holm (CH) equation on
S^{1}
derived in
[
Camassa and Holm, 1993
]

a new class of models for the mean motion
of ideal incompressible fluids in three dimensions (3D) was proposed and analysed in
[
Holm et al., 1998b
]
.
In the 3D CH equation the Lagrangian–averaged amplitude of the fluctuations introduces a spatial scale,
\alpha
,
and below this scale the Lagrangian transport velocity is filtered by nonlinear dispersion.
This
\alpha
-filtering enhances the stability and regularity of the new fluid models without compromising either
their large scale behaviour, or their conservation laws. These models also describe geodesic motion on
the volume-preserving diffeomorphism group for a metric containing the
H^{1}
norm of the fluid velocity.
The 3D CH equation came to be called the Lagrangian–Averaged Euler-
\alpha
(LAE-
\alpha
) model
in
[
Marsden et al., 2000
]
where the vector field solutions that generate the geodesic Lagrangian path of
the 3D CH flow were proved to be smooth and converge to
solutions of the Euler fluid equation in the limit
\alpha\to 0
.

The
viscous
3D CH equation was proposed
as a closure approximation for the Reynolds-averaged equations of the divergence-free
Navier-Stokes flow in
[
Chen et al., 1998
]
.
This closure approximation was evaluated on turbulent channel and pipe flows with steady mean, and
its analytical solutions for the mean velocity and the Reynolds
shear stress were found to be consistent with experiments in most of the flow region.
In
[
Foias et al., 2002
]
, the viscous 3D CH equation was shown to possess global strong solutions
which converge to solutions of the Navier–Stokes fluid equation
in the limit
\alpha\to 0
.

Thereafter, the viscous 3D CH equation was called the Lagrangian-Averaged Navier–Stokes alpha
(LANS-
\alpha
) model and was tested as a Large Eddy Simulation (LES) model in various
physical applications, including
magnetohydrodynamics (LAMHD-
\alpha
) in astrophysics
[
Pietarila Graham et al., 2006
]
, as well as
rotation and stratification in oceanography
[
Hecht et al., 2008
]
.
The LANS-
\alpha
model also satisfies the Kármán–Howarth turbulence theorem,
[
Holm, 2002
]
.

The present paper derives
N
-dimensional equations for
multiscale
geodesic models of the
famous Richardson cascade of energy and circulation
[
Richardson, 1922
]
, as illustrated by computational simulations
of their solutions on
S^{1}
.

Multiscale geodesic flows (MGF)

Geodesic flows on the manifold of smooth invertible maps

G
acting on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
may be lifted as curves
g_{t}\in G
parametrised by time
t

as
x_{t}={g_{t}}x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
. The flows
g_{t}\in G
may be derived via Hamilton’s principle

0={\delta}S={\delta}\int\ell(u)\,dt
for a kinetic energy
Lagrangian
\ell(u)=\tfrac{1}{2}\|u\|^{2}=\tfrac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}u\,Q^{op}u\,dx

with vector-field velocity
u=\dot{g}_{t}{g_{t}}^{-1}\in\mathfrak{g}\simeq T_{e}G
and positive definite, symmetric differential
operator
Q^{op}

0={\delta}S={\delta}\int_{0}^{T}\ell(u)+\left<m\,,\,\dot{g}_{t}{g_{t}}^{-1}-u\right>_{\mathfrak{g}^{*}\times\mathfrak{g}}\,dt\,.

Here, the brackets
\left<\cdot\,,\,\cdot\right>_{\mathfrak{g}^{*}\times\mathfrak{g}}
denote
L^{2}
pairing between
the Lie algebra of vector fields
\mathfrak{g}
and its dual
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
.
The dual Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
is a Poisson manifold, whose Lie–Poisson bracket is discussed below.

Variations of this Hamilton principle yield an evolution equation expressed by the
Lie chain rule
,

\frac{d}{dt}{g_{t}}_{*}m(x,t)=-\,{g_{t}}_{*}({\cal L}_{u}m)\,,\quad\hbox{with}\quad u=\dot{g}_{t}g_{t}^{-1}\,,

in which
{g_{t}}_{*}
denotes
push-foward
by the map
g_{t}\in G

[
Holm et al., 1998a
,
Crisan et al., 2022
]

Thus, the coordinate-free
Lie derivative

{\cal L}_{u}
is defined as (minus) the tangent to the push-forward
{g_{t}}_{*}

along the Eulerian vector field
u=\dot{g}_{t}g_{t}^{-1}\in T_{e}G
which generates Lagrangian flow
g_{t}
and satisfies the equation

2.1
Lie–Poisson Hamiltonian MGF in 1D

The Hamiltonian for a 1D MGF system in
\mathbb{R}
is

\displaystyle H(\{m\})=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}}m_{\alpha_{k}}K_{\alpha_{k}}*m_{\alpha_{k}}\,dx\,,

(2.1)

with Eulerian fluid velocity
u_{\alpha_{k}}(x,t)
in the sum over finer and finer discrete scales,

\alpha_{k}\in\mathbb{R}
where
k=1,2,\dots,n,

\alpha_{k+1}/\alpha_{k}=2^{-k}
and

m_{\alpha_{k}}(x,t)
is defined as

\displaystyle m_{\alpha_{k}}(x,t)

\displaystyle={Q^{op}}_{\alpha_{k}}u_{\alpha_{k}}:=(1-\alpha_{k}^{2}\partial_{x}^{2})u_{\alpha_{k}}(x,t)\,,

\displaystyle u_{\alpha_{k}}

\displaystyle=K_{\alpha_{k}}*m_{\alpha_{k}}=:\int_{\mathbb{R}}K_{\alpha_{k}}(|x-y|)m_{\alpha_{k}}(y,t)\,dy\,,

where
K_{\alpha_{k}}(|x-y|)
is the Green function for the Helmholtz operator
(1-\alpha_{k}^{2}\partial_{x}^{2})
on the real line,
for boundary conditions
\lim_{|x|\to\infty}u_{\alpha_{k}}(x,t)=0
.

The Lie–Poisson form of the dynamics for the starting Hamiltonian with
\delta h/\delta m_{\alpha_{k}}=u_{\alpha_{k}}

and
n=3
is
[
Holm, 2025
]

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\begin{pmatrix}m_{\alpha_{1}}\\
\\
m_{\alpha_{2}}\\
\\
m_{\alpha_{3}}\end{pmatrix}=-\begin{bmatrix}\mathrm{ad}^{*}_{\Box}m_{\alpha_{1}}&\mathrm{ad}^{*}_{\Box}m_{\alpha_{2}}&\mathrm{ad}^{*}_{\Box}m_{\alpha_{3}}\\
\\
\mathrm{ad}^{*}_{\Box}m_{\alpha_{2}}&\mathrm{ad}^{*}_{\Box}m_{\alpha_{2}}&\mathrm{ad}^{*}_{\Box}m_{\alpha_{3}}\\
\\
\mathrm{ad}^{*}_{\Box}m_{\alpha_{3}}&\mathrm{ad}^{*}_{\Box}m_{\alpha_{3}}&\mathrm{ad}^{*}_{\Box}m_{\alpha_{3}}\end{bmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}u_{\alpha_{1}}\\
\\
u_{\alpha_{2}}\\
\\
u_{\alpha_{3}}\end{pmatrix}=-\begin{pmatrix}\mathrm{ad}^{*}_{u_{\alpha_{1}}}m_{\alpha_{1}}+\mathrm{ad}^{*}_{u_{\alpha_{2}}}m_{\alpha_{2}}+\mathrm{ad}^{*}_{u_{\alpha_{3}}}m_{\alpha_{3}}\\
\\
\mathrm{ad}^{*}_{u_{\alpha_{1}}+u_{\alpha_{2}}}m_{\alpha_{2}}+\mathrm{ad}^{*}_{u_{\alpha_{3}}}m_{\alpha_{3}}\\
\\
\mathrm{ad}^{*}_{u_{\alpha_{1}}+u_{\alpha_{2}}+u_{\alpha_{3}}}m_{\alpha_{3}}\end{pmatrix},

in which the higher (smaller) scales are seen to apply forces to the lower (larger) scales, while the sum of velocities of the lower (larger)
scales transports the momenta of the higher (smaller) scales.

The matrix operator in square brackets above defines the following semidirect-product Lie-Poisson bracket
[
Holm et al., 1998a
]

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{df}{dt}=\big\{f,h\big\}&=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left<m_{\alpha_{k}}\,,\,\left[\frac{\delta f}{\delta m_{\alpha_{k}}},\frac{\delta h}{\delta m_{\alpha_{k}}}\right]\right>=-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left<m_{\alpha_{k}}\,,\,{\rm ad}_{\frac{\delta h}{\delta m_{\alpha_{k}}}}\frac{\delta f}{\delta m_{\alpha_{k}}}\right>\\
&=-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left<{\rm ad}^{*}_{\frac{\delta h}{\delta m_{\alpha_{k}}}}m_{\alpha_{k}}\,,\,\frac{\delta f}{\delta m_{\alpha_{k}}}\right>,\end{split}

(2.2)

where
\left<\cdot\,,\,\cdot\right>
denotes
L^{2}
pairing of functions and
[\cdot,\cdot]
denotes the Lie algebra bracket of vector fields. That is,

\bigg[\frac{\delta f}{\delta m},\frac{\delta h}{\delta m}\bigg]={\rm ad}_{\frac{\delta h}{\delta m}}\frac{\delta f}{\delta m}=\frac{\delta f}{\delta m}\partial_{x}\frac{\delta h}{\delta m}-\frac{\delta h}{\delta m}\partial_{x}\frac{\delta f}{\delta m}.

on the dual of the following
nested
semidirect product Lie algebra

\mathfrak{s}=\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha_{1}}\ \circledS\big(\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha_{2}}\ \circledS\ \mathfrak{g}_{\alpha_{3}}\ \big)
,
with dual coordinates given by
m_{\alpha_{k}}\in\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha_{k}}^{*}
dual to
u_{\alpha_{k}}\in\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha_{k}}

correspondingly for
k=1,2,3
, and symbol
\circledS
denotes the semidirect product operation
[
Holm et al., 1998a
]
.

Physically, the Lie-Poisson bracket
\{f,h\}
in (
2.2
) refers to three types of nested fluid flow.
Its decreasing filter widths
\alpha_{k}
for
\alpha_{k+1}/\alpha_{k}=2^{-k}
mimic L. F. Richardson’s famous “whorls within whorls within whorls” reference in characterising fluid dynamics
[
Richardson, 1922
]
.

Big whorls have little whorls, that feed on their velocity,

and little whorls have lesser whorls, and so on to viscosity.

– L. F. Richardson (1922)

In a mathematical sense, then, the Lie–Poisson dynamics
\frac{df}{dt}=\{f,h\}
in (
2.2
) mimics ‘Richardson’s triple’.
Namely, Richardson’s triple is modelled as the composition of three non-commutative right group actions of the smooth invertible maps

G_{\alpha_{1}}\times G_{\alpha_{2}}\times G_{\alpha_{3}}
. the corresponding fluid velocities are

\displaystyle u_{\alpha_{1}}

\displaystyle\in\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha_{1}}\otimes u_{\alpha_{2}}\in\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha_{2}}/G_{\alpha_{1}}\otimes u_{\alpha_{3}}\in(\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha_{3}}/G_{\alpha_{2}})/G_{\alpha_{1}}\,.

Here, the flow of
G_{\alpha_{1}}
can represent the big whorls, the flow of
G_{\alpha_{2}}
can represent the little whorls carried in the frame of motion of the big whorls, and the flow of
G_{\alpha_{3}}
can represent the lesser whorls carried along successively by the two other whorls.

2.2
Diagonalising the Hamiltonian formulation

The linear transformation of variables to

\mu_{\alpha_{1}}=m_{\alpha_{1}}-m_{\alpha_{2}}\,,\quad\mu_{\alpha_{2}}=m_{\alpha_{2}}-m_{\alpha_{3}}\,,\quad\mu_{\alpha_{3}}=m_{\alpha_{3}}\,,

diagonalises the previous
entangled
Lie–Poisson matrix and leads to equivalent untangled equations,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\begin{pmatrix}\mu_{\alpha_{1}}\\
\mu_{\alpha_{2}}\\
\mu_{\alpha_{3}}\end{pmatrix}=-\begin{pmatrix}\mathrm{ad}^{*}_{u_{\alpha_{1}}}\mu_{\alpha_{1}}\\
\mathrm{ad}^{*}_{(u_{\alpha_{1}}+u_{\alpha_{2}})}\mu_{\alpha_{2}}\\
\mathrm{ad}^{*}_{(u_{\alpha_{1}}+u_{\alpha_{2}}+u_{\alpha_{3}})}\mu_{\alpha_{3}}\end{pmatrix}=-\begin{pmatrix}\big(\partial_{x}\mu_{\alpha_{1}}+\mu_{\alpha_{1}}\partial_{x}\big)u_{\alpha_{1}}\\
\big(\partial_{x}\mu_{\alpha_{2}}+\mu_{\alpha_{2}}\partial_{x}\big)(u_{\alpha_{1}}+u_{\alpha_{2}})\\
\big(\partial_{x}\mu_{\alpha_{3}}+\mu_{\alpha_{3}}\partial_{x}\big)(u_{\alpha_{1}}+u_{\alpha_{2}}+u_{\alpha_{3}})\end{pmatrix}\,,

where
\partial_{x}
denotes partial derivative along the real line,
\mathbb{R}
and boundary conditions are chosen so that
\lim_{|x|\to\infty}u_{\alpha_{k}}(x,t)=0
for
k=1,2,3
.
The untangled equations arise from a diagonal Poisson bracket
\{f,h\}=\langle\mu,[df,dh]\rangle
on the dual of the following direct product Lie algebra

\mathfrak{s}_{diag}=\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha_{1}}\ \otimes\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha_{2}}\ \otimes\ \mathfrak{g}_{\alpha_{3}}
.

Untangled equations for momentum differences is typical of Lagrangian reduction by stages
based on the composition of Lie group actions
[
Holm et al., 1998a
]
.
Consequently, the sum of velocities of larger scales transports the pairwise differences of momentum densities of the smaller scales,

\partial_{t}\mu_{\alpha_{k}}+{\rm ad}^{*}_{\sum_{j=1}^{k}\!u_{\alpha_{j}}}\mu_{\alpha_{k}}=0\,.

2.3
Multiscale measure-valued peakon dynamics

Remarkably, the form of the dynamics in terms of pairwise differences in singular momentum densities at smaller scales
admits emergent measure-valued solutions. Namely, the singular solution for
N_{i}

peakons
,

\mu_{\alpha_{i}}(x,t)=\sum_{a=1}^{N_{i}}\,p^{(a)}_{i}(t)\,\delta\big(x-x^{(a)}_{i}(t)\big)\,,

in which
x\in\mathbb{R}
and the parameters
p^{(a)}_{i}
are the momenta of the
N_{i}
peakons at the
i
-th level.

The starting Hamiltonian in (
2.1
) may be written as

H=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}}u_{\alpha_{k}}m_{\alpha_{k}}\,dx=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}}u_{\alpha_{k}}\sum_{j=k}^{n}\,\mu_{\alpha_{j}}\,dx

with velocity
u_{\alpha_{k}}
written in terms of pairwise differences in singular momentum density, given by

u_{\alpha_{k}}(x,t)=\sum_{i=k}^{n}K_{\alpha_{i}}*\mu_{\alpha_{i}}=\sum_{i=k}^{n}\sum_{b=1}^{N_{i}}\,p^{(b)}_{i}(t)\,K_{\alpha_{i}}({x}-{x}^{(b)}_{i}(t)).

This expression reveals the canonical Hamiltonian,

\displaystyle H(\{x\},\{p\})=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i<j=1}^{n}\sum_{a=1}^{N_{j}}\sum_{b=1}^{N_{i}}p^{(a)}_{j}\,p^{(b)}_{i}\left[K_{\alpha_{i}}\left(\big|{x}_{j}^{(a)}-{x}^{(b)}_{i}\big|\right)+K_{\alpha_{j}}\left(\big|{x}_{i}^{(a)}-{x}^{(b)}_{j}\big|\right)\right]

with Green function kernel for the Helmholtz operator
(1-\alpha_{k}^{2}\Delta)
given by

K_{\alpha_{k}}(r):=\frac{1}{2}e^{-r/\alpha_{k}}
for
S^{1}
where the canonically conjugate fields are
(\{x\},\{p\})
.
For details of the corresponding emergent measure-valued solutions for the
N
-dimensional Camassa–Holm
equation, see
[
Holm and Marsden, 2005
]
.

2.4
3D Multiscale Euler Flow

In the 3D multiscale Euler system, the role of
\mu_{\alpha_{k}}
is played by the vorticity difference

\varpi_{\alpha_{k}}:=\omega_{\alpha_{k}}-\omega_{\alpha_{k+1}}
with
\omega_{\alpha_{k}}:={\rm curl}Q^{op}u_{\alpha_{k}}

and
{\rm div}u_{\alpha_{k}}=0
. Thus, the previous equation for the general case becomes for the 3D multiscale Euler system,

\partial_{t}\varpi_{\alpha_{k}}-{\rm curl}\Big({\sum_{j=1}^{k}u_{\alpha_{j}}\times\varpi_{\alpha_{k}}\Big)=0\,,}

and one finds the multiscale Euler Kelvin theorem,

\frac{d}{dt}\oint_{C\big({\sum_{j=1}^{k}u_{\alpha_{j}}\big)}}\varpi_{\alpha_{k}}\cdot dS=0\,.

The multiscale
\varpi_{\alpha_{k}}
dynamical system follows from the conserved kinetic energy Hamiltonian

H(\varpi_{\alpha_{j}})=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\int\psi_{\alpha_{k}}\cdot\sum_{j=k}^{n}\varpi_{\alpha_{j}}\,{\rm d}V\,,

with divergence-free vector stream function
{\psi}_{\alpha_{k}}={\rm curl}^{-1}{u}_{\alpha_{k}}
,
for the following Lie–Poisson bracket for smooth real functionals
(F,\,H)
of
\varpi_{\alpha_{k}}
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\{F,\,H\}(\varpi_{\alpha_{k}})&=\int\varpi_{\alpha_{k}}\cdot\Big(\,{\rm curl}\,\frac{\delta F}{\delta\varpi_{\alpha_{k}}}\times\,{\rm curl}\,\frac{\delta H}{\delta\varpi_{\alpha_{k}}}\Big){\rm d}V\\
\frac{dF}{dt}=&-\int\frac{\delta F}{\delta\varpi_{\alpha_{k}}}\cdot\,{\rm curl}\,\Big(\varpi_{\alpha_{k}}\times\,{\rm curl}\,\frac{\delta H}{\delta\varpi_{\alpha_{k}}}\Big){\rm d}V\end{split}

for which

{\rm curl}\,({\delta H}/{\delta\varpi_{\alpha_{k}}})=\sum_{k=1}^{m}{u}_{\alpha_{k}}

and the
\varpi_{\alpha_{k}}
equation follows.

The helicity of
\varpi_{\alpha_{k}}
, defined for
k=1,2,\dots,n
by

\Lambda_{\alpha_{k}}:=\int\omega_{\alpha_{k}}\cdot{\rm curl}^{-1}\omega_{\alpha_{k}}\,{\rm d}V

is conserved, since
\frac{d}{dt}\Lambda_{\alpha_{k}}=\{\Lambda_{\alpha_{k}},\,H\}(\varpi_{\alpha_{k}})=0
.

3
Conclusion and outlook

This note has derived
N
-dimensional equations for
multiscale
geodesic models of the
famous Richardson cascade of energy and circulation
[
Richardson, 1922
]
,
whose emergent singular solution properties are illustrated in
S^{1}
by the computational simulations in Fig.
1
.

A plethora of new opportunities remain for modelling and analysing multiscale phenomena.
For example,

•

One may include effects of advected quantities such as compressibility, heat transfer, or magnetic fields, as in
[
Holm et al., 1998a
,
Holm and Tronci, 2009
]
,
in which case equation (
2.3
) will no longer be geodesic. Instead, the equation set will enlarge to include
transport relations and forces due to advected inertial, thermodynamic, magnetic, or electrodynamic multiscale quantities. Such an equation set could
represent multiscale effects in plasma physics, for example, or even advected multiscale stochastic fluctuations.

•

It has not escaped our notice that the equation (
2.3
) derived here for the Richardson cascade of energy shown in Fig.
1
may potentially be associated with multifractal models of turbulence, as discussed in
[
Argoul et al., 1989
,
Zhou, 2022
]
.
Although this topic is beyond the scope of the present paper, one may consider a stochastic transport perturbation in Richardson’s triple as in
[
Holm, 2019
]
, to be studied via wavelet analysis.

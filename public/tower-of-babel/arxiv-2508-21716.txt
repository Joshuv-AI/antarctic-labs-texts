L ∞ L_{\infty} -spectral sequences for Hochschild cohomology
Jasper van de Kreeke

Abstract
Spectral sequences are a common tool to compute cohomology spaces, but higher structure is often lost on the way. In this article we exhibit a strategy to retain the higher structure on the cohomology, which works in case the chain complex with higher structure is presented as the twisting of a simpler chain complex by a Maurer-Cartan element. This works particularly well in case of the Hochschild complex of a minimal A ∞ A_{\infty} -category, where the Hochschild complex can be seen as the twisting of the Hochschild complex of the underlying associative algebra or category. As an application, we recover the existing formality result for Hochschild cohomology of wrapped Fukaya categories of punctured surfaces, and provide an L ∞ L_{\infty} -quasi-isomorphism between the Hochschild complex and its cohomology.

1
Introduction

Spectral sequences are a classical tool to determine cohomology of double complexes. Starting from a bigraded vector space
V
with two anti-commuting differentials
d_{0},d_{1}
, one takes cohomology first with respect to
d_{0}
, then with respect to
d_{1}
, and potentially with respect to further differentials:
E^{0}=(V,d_{0}+d_{1})
Double complex
\rightsquigarrow
E^{1}=(\operatorname{H}(V,d_{0}),\overline{d_{1}})
First page complex
\rightsquigarrow
E^{2}=(\operatorname{H}(\operatorname{H}(V,d),\overline{d_{1}}),d_{2})
Second page complex
\rightsquigarrow~~\cdots

Modern cohomology theories produce chain complexes
(V,d_{0}+d_{1})
which carry additional higher structure such as an
A_{\infty}
-structure or
L_{\infty}
-structure. The standard tool to transfer the higher structure to cohomology is by successive homological transfer from one page to the next.

In the present article, we exhibit an alternative approach which applies if the higher algebra structure can be written as a twisting of a simpler structure by a Maurer-Cartan element
W
. We focus on the case of DG Lie algebras of the form
(V,d_{0}+[W,-],[-,-])
, in which we obtain a second page that is rather different from classical spectral sequences:
L_{W}=(V,d_{0}+[W,-],[-,-])
Spectral DGLA
\rightsquigarrow
E^{1}=(\operatorname{H}L)_{\pi^{\operatorname{MC}}(W)}
First
L_{\infty}
-page
\rightsquigarrow
E^{2}=\operatorname{H}((\operatorname{H}L)_{\pi^{\operatorname{MC}}(W)})
Second
L_{\infty}
-page

As an application, we compute the Hochschild cohomology of
A_{\infty}
-gentle algebras and recover the formality result of
[
4
]
in a more elegant fashion.

1.1
Classical transfer of higher structure

Assume one is given a chain complex with higher multiplicative structure, and a spectral sequences which computes the cohomology merely as a vector space. Then the task at hand is to compute the higher multiplicative structure on the cohomology such that there is a quasi-isomorphism between the complex and the cohomology that respects the higher structure.

The standard tool for this is to transfer the higher structure from the complex successively to the pages
E^{1}
,
E^{2}
and eventually to the cohomology space. For instance, Cirici and Sopena-Gilboy
[
6
]
consider the spectral sequence connecting Dolbeault cohomology and de Rham cohomology with the help of a filtered Kadeishvili theorem. Herscovich
[
9
]
provides a further refined theory of
A_{\infty}
-structures on spectral sequences. In all cases, the differential on the first page is given by the descent
\overline{d_{1}}
of the second differential to the first page.

1.2
The case of twisted structures

In some situations it seems more natural to consider a different differential on the first page. Indeed, consider the case of a DGLA
L=(V,d_{0},[-,-])
and its twisting
L_{W}=(V,d_{0}+[W,-],[-,-])
by a Maurer-Cartan element
W\in\operatorname{MC}(L)
. Homological transfer provides a quasi-isomorphism of
L_{\infty}
-algebras
\pi:L\to\operatorname{H}L
and a map of Maurer-Cartan elements
\pi^{\operatorname{MC}}:\operatorname{MC}(L)\to\operatorname{MC}(\operatorname{H}L)
. Embracing
\pi
with
\pi^{\operatorname{MC}}(W)
we get a morphism of
L_{\infty}
-algebras
\pi_{W}:L_{W}\to(\operatorname{H}L)_{\pi^{\operatorname{MC}}(W)}
.

In contrast to classical spectral sequences, the differential on the first page
(\operatorname{H}L)_{\pi^{\operatorname{MC}}}
is given by the embraced differential instead the descent of
d_{1}=[W,-]
to cohomology. Moreover the map
\pi_{W}
need not be a quasi-isomorphism. But if it is, then the composition of
\pi_{W}
and the projection to the minimal model of
(\operatorname{H}L)_{\pi^{\operatorname{MC}}(W)}
yield a quasi-isomorphism between
L_{W}
and a minimal
L_{\infty}
-algebra. In this sense, our type of non-classical spectral sequence always degenerates on the second page.

1.3
Hochschild cohomology of
A_{\infty}
-categories

The particular application which we have in mind concerns the Hochschild cohomology of an arbitrary minimal
A_{\infty}
-category. In this case, the
A_{\infty}
-structure can be written as the sum
\mu=\mu^{2}+\mu^{\geq 3}
of the ordinary product and the higher products. Therefore the differential on the Hochschild complex splits into two parts as
d=[\mu^{2},-]+[\mu^{\geq 3},-]
, which precisely presents the Hochschild complex of the
A_{\infty}
-category as the twisting of the Hochschild complex of the associative category by the Maurer-Cartan element
\mu^{\geq 3}
. Thanks to numerous results and tools for Hochschild cohomology of associative algebras
[
1
,
10
,
3
,
5
,
16
]
, it becomes possible to compute Hochschild cohomology and its
L_{\infty}
-structure of
A_{\infty}
-categories via a two-step procedure.

In this article, we demonstrate this strategy in the case of wrapped Fukaya categories of punctured surfaces, also known as gentle algebras
\operatorname{Gtl}\Sigma

[
3
]
. The computation is based on the description of Hochschild cohomology of
\operatorname{Gtl}\Sigma
as associative category due to Bocklandt
[
3
]
. The approach via spectral sequences was already anticipated in
[
2
, Theorem 12.22]
, but remained without proof. The Hochschild cohomology of
\operatorname{Gtl}\Sigma
as
A_{\infty}
-category was finally computed in
[
4
]
, but the
L_{\infty}
-structure was proven to be formal only through a fragile grading argument. The present article aims to recover this formality result in a more elegant way.

1.4
Further applications

It seems likely that the approach exhibited here applies to further DGLAs and situations outside of the DGLA context as well. For instance, consider the Landau-Ginzburg model
(\operatorname{Jac}Q,\ell)
where
Q
is a dimer model and
\ell\in Z(\operatorname{Jac}Q)
is the standard central element. Wong
[
17
]
computed the compactly supported Hochschild cohomology
\operatorname{HH}(\operatorname{Jac}Q,\ell)
by means of a spectral sequence in which the second differential is given by commuting with
\ell
in
\operatorname{HH}(\operatorname{Jac}Q)
. The higher structure on cohomology is missing in
[
17
]
and may be recovered by interpreting
\operatorname{HC}(\operatorname{Jac}Q,\ell)
as a spectral DGLA. Further situations outside of the DGLA context seem possible, perhaps existing transfers of
A_{\infty}
-structure can be reformulated through the lens of twisted structures presented in the present article.

Definition 2.1
.

A (

\mu^{k}:\operatorname{Hom}(X_{k},X_{k+1})\otimes\ldots\otimes\operatorname{Hom}(X_{1},X_{2})\to\operatorname{Hom}(X_{1},X_{k+1}),\quad k\geq 1

\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq j<i\leq k}(-1)^{\|a_{n}\|+\ldots+\|a_{1}\|}\mu(a_{k},\ldots,\mu(a_{i},\ldots,a_{j+1}),a_{j},\ldots,a_{1})=0,

\displaystyle\mu^{2}(a,\mathrm{id}_{X})=a,~\mu^{2}(\mathrm{id}_{Y},a)=(-1)^{|a|}a,~\mu^{\geq 3}(\ldots,\mathrm{id}_{X},\ldots)=0.

Next let us recall the notion of DG Lie algebras and Maurer-Cartan elements.

Definition 2.2
.

A
DG Lie algebra
(DGLA) is a
\mathbb{Z}
- or
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
-graded vector space
L
together with a differential
d:L^{i}\to L^{i+1}
and a bracket
[-,-]:L\times L\to L
of degree zero satisfying skew-symmetry, Leibniz rule and Jacobi identity:

\displaystyle[a,b]=(-1)^{|a||b|+1}[b,a],

\displaystyle d([a,b])=[da,b]+(-1)^{|a|}[a,db],

\displaystyle(-1)^{|a||c|}[a,[b,c]]+(-1)^{|b||a|}[b,[c,a]]+(-1)^{|c||b|}[c,[a,b]]=0.

A
Maurer-Cartan element
of
L
is an element
\nu\in L^{1}
which satisfies the Maurer-Cartan equation

d\nu+\frac{1}{2}[\nu,\nu]=0.

The set of Maurer-Cartan elements of
L
is denoted
\operatorname{MC}(L)
.

Remark 2.3
.

The notion of DG Lie algebras exists in both
\mathbb{Z}\,
-graded and
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
-graded context. In the
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
-graded case, we write the
L=L^{\mathrm{e}}\oplus L^{\mathrm{o}}
but may equally use integers to indicate the parity. For instance, in case the DGLA
L
is
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
-graded, a Maurer-Cartan element is supposed to lie in
L^{\mathrm{o}}
.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a
\mathbb{Z}
- or
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
-graded
A_{\infty}
-category. Then its Hochschild complex
\operatorname{HC}(\mathcal{C})
is the graded vector space

\operatorname{HC}(\mathcal{C})=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}X_{1},\ldots,X_{k+1}\in\mathcal{C}\\
k\geq 0\end{subarray}}\operatorname{Hom}\big(\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(X_{k},X_{k+1})[1]\otimes\ldots\otimes\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(X_{1},X_{2})[1],~\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(X_{1},X_{k+1})[1]\big).

Here
[1]
denotes the left-shift and
\|a\|=|a|-1
denotes the reduced degree of a morphism
a\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(X,Y)
. The grading
\|\cdot\|
on
\operatorname{HC}(\mathcal{C})
is the one induced from the shifted degrees of the hom spaces of
\mathcal{C}
. In other words, we have

\|\eta(a_{k},\ldots,a_{1})\|=\|\eta\|+\|a_{k}\|+\ldots+\|a_{1}\|,\quad\eta\in\operatorname{HC}(\mathcal{C}).

For
\eta,\omega\in\operatorname{HC}(\mathcal{C})
, the Gerstenhaber product
\mu\cdot\omega\in\operatorname{HC}(\mathcal{C})
is defined as

(\eta\cdot\omega)(a_{k},\ldots,a_{1})=\sum(-1)^{(\|a_{l}\|+\ldots+\|a_{1}\|)\|\omega\|}\eta(a_{k},\ldots,\omega(\ldots),a_{l},\ldots,a_{1}).

The
Hochschild DGLA
is the following
\mathbb{Z}
- or
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
-graded DGLA structure on
\operatorname{HC}(\mathcal{C})
: The bracket on
\operatorname{HC}(\mathcal{C})
is the Gerstenhaber bracket

[\eta,\omega]=\eta\cdot\omega-(-1)^{\|\omega\|\|\eta\|}\omega\cdot\eta.

The differential on
\operatorname{HC}(\mathcal{C})
consists of commuting with the product
\mu_{\mathcal{C}}\in\operatorname{HC}^{1}(\mathcal{C})
:

d\nu=[\mu_{\mathcal{C}},\nu].

We will often encounter a situation in which we provide a list
R_{\mathsf{list}}
of individual Hochschild cochains and require to construct a space which contains all of them. In these situations the vector span is often not sufficient and we rather need its completion. We fix this terminology as follows.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be an
A_{\infty}
-category. Let
R_{\mathsf{list}}\subseteq\operatorname{HC}(\mathcal{C})
be a set of Hochschild cochains which are individually concentrated in some specific length. For every
m\geq 0
denote by
R_{\mathsf{list},m}\subseteq R_{\mathsf{list}}
the subset of cochains of length
m
. Then the
completion
space
\overline{R_{\mathsf{list}}}\subseteq\operatorname{HC}(\mathcal{C})
is defined as

\overline{R_{\mathsf{list}}}=\prod_{m=0}^{\infty}\operatorname{span}(R_{\mathsf{list},m})\subseteq\operatorname{HC}(\mathcal{C}).

Next we recall
L_{\infty}
-algebras and their morphisms. With the present sign convention, a DGLA is simply an
L_{\infty}
-algebra without higher products. In particular, the Hochschild DGLA can automatically be regarded as an
L_{\infty}
-algebra.

Definition 2.6
.

An
L_{\infty}
-algebra
is a graded vector space
L
together with multilinear maps

l_{k}:\underbrace{L\times\ldots\times L}_{k\text{ times}}\to L

of degree
2-k
which are graded skew-symmetric and satisfy the higher Jacobi identities:

\displaystyle l_{k}(x_{s(1)},\ldots,x_{s(k)})=\chi(s)l_{k}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}),

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j=k+1\\
i,j\geq 1\end{subarray}}\sum_{s\in S_{i,k-i}}(-1)^{i(n-i)}\chi(s)l_{j}(l_{i}(x_{s(1)},\ldots,x_{s(i)}),x_{s(i+1)},\ldots,x_{s(k)})=0.

Here

denotes the set of shuffles, i.e.
s\in S_{k}
with
s(1)<\ldots<s(i)
and
s(i+1)<\ldots<s(k)
. The sign
\chi(s)
is the product of the signum of

is

Morphisms between
L_{\infty}
-algebras are more flexible than between DGLAs, since
L_{\infty}

-functors.

Definition 2.7
.

A
morphism of
L_{\infty}
-algebras

\varphi:L\to L^{\prime}
is given by multilinear maps

\varphi^{k}:L\times\ldots\times L\to L

of degree

for all
k\geq 1
such that
\varphi(x_{s(1)},\ldots,x_{s(k)})=\chi(s)\varphi(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})
for any
s\in S_{k}
and

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j=k+1\\
i,j\geq 1\end{subarray}}\sum_{s\in S_{i,n-i}}(-1)^{i(k-i)}\chi(s)\varphi(l(x_{s(1)},\ldots,x_{s(i)}),x_{s(i+1)},\ldots,x_{s(k)})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq r\leq k\\
i_{1}+\ldots+i_{r}=k\end{subarray}}\sum_{t}(-1)^{u}\chi(t)l^{\prime}_{r}(\varphi(x_{t(1)},\ldots,x_{t(i_{1})}),\ldots,\varphi(x_{t(i_{1}+\ldots+i_{r-1}+1)},\ldots,x_{t(k)})),

where
t
runs over all
(i_{1},\ldots,i_{r})
-shuffles for which

t(i_{1}+\ldots+i_{l-1}+1)<t(i_{1}+\ldots+i_{l}+1).

and
u=(r-1)(i_{1}-1)+\ldots+2(i_{r-2}-1)+(i_{r-1}-1)
. A morphism
\varphi:L\to L^{\prime}
of
L_{\infty}
-algebras is a

if
\varphi^{1}
is a quasi-isomorphism of complexes.

L_{\infty}
-algebra

-isomorphism and can be determined by means of an explicit construction. References for the analogous statement for

Definition 2.8
.

A

of an

-algebra

consists of direct sum decomposition
H\oplus\operatorname{Im}(d)\oplus R
such that
H\oplus\operatorname{Im}(d)=\operatorname{Ker}(d)
. The space
H
is the

and the space

is the

. The inclusion and projection maps are denoted by

and
\pi:L\to H
. The

h(d(\nu))=\nu

We recall now the construction of minimal models of
L_{\infty}
-algebras. This construction is also known as homological perturbation, and the particular decorated trees are known as Kadeishvili trees. We denote by
\mathcal{T}_{k}
the set of rooted planar trees with

leaves in which every node has at least

children. For every
T\in\mathcal{T}_{k}
and elements
a_{1},\ldots a_{k}\in H
we define the evaluation
T_{\pi}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k})
as follows: The leaves are decorated with

. Every non-leaf non-node is decorated with

where

\pi l^{m}

T_{h}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k})

Theorem 2.9
.

Let
L
be an
L_{\infty}
-algebra with homological splitting
L=H\oplus\operatorname{Im}(d)\oplus R
. Then a minimal
L_{\infty}
-structure on
\operatorname{H}L\coloneqq H
is constructed by means of the following formula:

l^{k}_{\operatorname{H}L}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k})=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{k}}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{k}}c_{T,\sigma}T_{\pi}(a_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,a_{\sigma(k)}).

A quasi-isomorphism

is constructed by means of

i^{k}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k})=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{k}}d_{T,\sigma}T_{h}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}).

Remark 2.10
.

The scalars

and

Hochschild cohomology of

Let us recall results on the Hochschild cohomology of
\operatorname{Gtl}_{2}\Sigma
from
[
2
]
and
[
2
, Theorem 12.19]
. The idea is that the category
\operatorname{Gtl}_{2}\Sigma
is effectively a graded quiver algebra with relations, and its Hochschild cohomology can be studied with classical tools
[
3
]
.

We start by recalling the two gradings on
\operatorname{HH}(\operatorname{Gtl}_{2}\Sigma)
. The first grading is the classical grading by length of cochains. The second grading is the
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
-grading into even and odd cochains, coming from the reduced degree
\|\cdot\|
on the Hochschild complex:

\displaystyle\operatorname{HH}(\operatorname{Gtl}_{2}\Sigma)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{HH}^{0}(\operatorname{Gtl}_{2}\Sigma)\oplus\operatorname{HH}^{1}(\operatorname{Gtl}_{2}\Sigma)\oplus\ldots,

\displaystyle\operatorname{HH}(\operatorname{Gtl}_{2}\Sigma)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{HH}^{\mathrm{e}}(\operatorname{Gtl}_{2}\Sigma)\oplus\operatorname{HH}^{\mathrm{o}}(\operatorname{Gtl}_{2}\Sigma).

We now fixing several pieces of notation and terminology. The
full turn

\ell_{q}
around a puncture
q\in M
is the sum
\ell_{q}=\sum_{a}\ell_{q,a}
over all full turns
\ell_{q,a}
winding around
q
and starting at all half-arcs incident at
a
. Some specific cochains have only a 0-adic or a 1-adic component. Those with only a 0-adic are necessarily central elements, and those with 1-adic component are derivations in the sense that
\nu(\alpha\beta)=\alpha\nu(\beta)+\alpha\nu(\beta)
.

The set
\mathbb{S}_{\mathsf{generic}}
is the
generic sporadic set
and is a finite set of scalar-valued functions
\lambda
on the set of angles in
\Sigma
. Every function
\lambda\in\mathbb{S}_{\mathsf{generic}}
is additive in the sense that
\lambda(\alpha\beta)=\lambda(\alpha)+\lambda(\beta)
whenever
\alpha\beta\neq 0
, and satisfies the condition
\sum_{i=1}^{l}\lambda(\alpha_{i})=0
whenever
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{l}
is an elementary polygon. The set
\mathbb{S}_{\mathsf{generic}}
is chosen precisely so that the cocycles
\nu_{\lambda}
,
\nu_{B}
and
\nu_{q,k,\mathrm{o}}
together form a basis for the 0-adic part of Hochschild cohomology.

There is another finite set
\mathbb{S}_{\mathsf{poly}}=\{\lambda_{B}\}_{B\in\Sigma_{2}}
of additive scalar-valued functions
\lambda_{B}
. For every elementary polygon
B\in\Sigma_{2}
with interior angles
\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{l}
, the function
\lambda_{B}
has the property that
\sum_{i=1}^{l}\lambda(\beta_{i})=1
, and
\lambda
vanishes on all elementary angles not contained in
B
.

Finally, for every puncture
q\in M
, one fixes an arbitrary indecomposable angle
\alpha_{q}
which winds around
q
. We are now ready to recall the following set of Hochschild cocycles. The cocycles of length
2
and higher were determined in
[
3
]
, and cocycles in length
0,1
were provided in
[
2
, Theorem 12.19]
.

Definition 2.15
.

The cochain
\nu_{1}
is simply given by identity element

\nu_{1}^{0}=\sum_{a\in\,\mathcal{A}}\mathrm{id}_{a}.

In these terms, the cochains
\nu_{q,k,\mathrm{o}}
are given by

\nu_{q,k,\mathrm{o}}^{0}=\ell_{q}^{k}.

The generic sporadic set
\mathbb{S}_{\mathsf{generic}}
is of cardinality
|M|+1
. For
\lambda\in\mathbb{S}_{\mathsf{generic}}
, the cochain
\nu_{\lambda}
only has a 1-adic component given by

\nu_{\lambda}(\alpha)=\lambda(\alpha)\alpha.

The
polygon sporadic cochains

\nu_{B}
only have a 1-adic component given by

\displaystyle\nu_{B}(\beta)

\displaystyle=\lambda_{B}(\beta)\beta.

The cochains
\nu_{q,k,\mathrm{e}}
are given as follows. Let
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{l}
be the angles around
q
in a chosen order.

\displaystyle\nu_{q,k,\mathrm{e}}(\alpha_{q})

\displaystyle=\ell_{q}^{k}\alpha_{q},

\displaystyle\nu_{q,k,\mathrm{e}}(\alpha)

\displaystyle=0\text{ for all indecomposable angles}\alpha\neq\alpha_{q}.

Let
B
be an elementary polygon given by angles
\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{l}
where
\beta_{i}:b_{i}\to b_{i+1}
. Let
k\geq 1
be a natural number. The odd cochain
\nu_{B,k,o}\in\operatorname{HC}(\operatorname{Gtl}_{2}\Sigma)
is defined as follows. If
\beta
is an angle such that
\beta\beta_{i-1}\neq 0
and
\gamma
is an angle such that
\beta_{i}\gamma\neq 0
, then

\displaystyle\nu_{B,k,o}(\beta\beta_{i+kl-1},\ldots,\beta_{i})

\displaystyle=\beta,

\displaystyle\nu_{B,k,o}(\beta_{i+kl-1},\ldots,\beta_{i}\gamma)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{|\gamma|}\gamma.

The cochain
\nu_{B,k,o}
vanishes on all other angle sequences. The even cochain
\nu_{B,k,e}
is defined as follows. If
\beta
and
\gamma
are angles such that
\beta\beta_{i}\gamma\neq 0
, then

\displaystyle\nu_{B,k,e}(\beta\beta_{i+kl},\beta_{i+kl-1},\ldots,\beta_{i}\gamma)

\displaystyle=\beta\beta_{i}\gamma.

The cochain
\nu_{B,k,e}
vanishes on all other angle sequences.

Since the Hochschild cocycles
\nu_{B,k,\mathrm{o}}
and
\nu_{B,k,\mathrm{e}}
were not explicitly constructed in
[
3
,
2
]
, we shall check that they indeed satisfy the cocycle condition and illustrate their functionality.

Lemma 2.16
.

The elements
\nu_{B,k,\mathrm{o}}
and
\nu_{B,k,\mathrm{e}}
are Hochschild cocycles.

Proof.

Regard the cochain
\nu_{B,k,o}
and let
\beta^{\prime},\beta
be angles such that
\beta^{\prime}\beta\beta_{i-1}\neq 0
. Then

\displaystyle(d\nu_{B,k,o})(\beta^{\prime},\beta\beta_{i+kl-1},\ldots,\beta_{i})

\displaystyle=\mu^{2}(\beta^{\prime},\nu_{B,k,o}(\beta\beta_{i+kl},\ldots,\beta_{i}\gamma))-(-1)^{\|\beta_{i}\|+\ldots+\|\beta_{i+kl-2}\|}\nu_{B,k,o}(\mu^{2}(\beta^{\prime},\beta\beta_{i+kl-1}),\ldots,\beta_{i})

\displaystyle=(-1)^{|\beta|}\beta^{\prime}\beta+(-1)^{|\beta\beta_{i-1}|+\|\beta_{i-1}\|}\beta^{\prime}\beta=0.

We have used that
\|\beta_{i}\|+\ldots+\|\beta_{i+kl-2}\|\equiv\|\beta_{i-1}\|
. Let
\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}
be angles such that
\beta_{i}\gamma\gamma^{\prime}\neq 0
. Then

\displaystyle(d\nu_{B,k,o})(\beta_{i+kl-1},\ldots,\beta_{i}\gamma,\gamma^{\prime})

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\|\gamma^{\prime}\|}\mu^{2}(\nu_{B,k,o}(\beta_{i+kl-1},\ldots,\beta_{i}\gamma),\gamma^{\prime})+\nu_{B,k,o}(\beta_{i+kl-1},\ldots,\mu^{2}(\beta_{i}\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}))

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\|\gamma^{\prime}\|+|\gamma|+|\gamma^{\prime}|}\gamma\gamma^{\prime}+(-1)^{|\gamma^{\prime}|+|\gamma\gamma^{\prime}|}\gamma\gamma^{\prime}=0.

It is obvious that
d\nu_{B,k,o}
vanishes on all other angle sequences.

We shall evaluate
d\nu_{B,k,e}
on the following types of angle sequences. Let
\beta,\gamma
be angles and
\gamma^{\prime}
a non-empty angle such that
\beta\beta_{i}\gamma\gamma^{\prime}\neq 0
. Then

\displaystyle(d\nu_{B,k,e})(\beta\beta_{i+kl},\ldots,\beta_{i}\gamma,\gamma^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\mu^{2}(\nu_{B,k,e}(\beta\beta_{i+kl},\ldots,\beta_{i}\gamma),\gamma^{\prime})-\nu_{B,k,e}(\beta\beta_{i+kl},\ldots,\mu^{2}(\beta_{i}\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}))

\displaystyle=(-1)^{|\gamma^{\prime}|}\beta\beta_{i}\gamma\gamma^{\prime}-(-1)^{|\gamma^{\prime}|}\beta\beta_{i}\gamma\gamma^{\prime}=0.

Let
\beta,\gamma
be angles and
\gamma^{\prime}
a non-empty angle such that
\beta\beta_{i}\gamma\gamma^{\prime}\neq 0
. Then

\displaystyle(d\nu_{B,k,e})(\beta^{\prime},\beta\beta_{i+kl},\ldots,\beta_{i}\gamma)

\displaystyle=\mu^{2}(\beta^{\prime},\nu_{B,k,e}(\beta\beta_{i+kl},\ldots,\beta_{i}\gamma)

\displaystyle\quad-(-1)^{\|\beta_{i}\gamma\|+\ldots+\|\beta_{i+kl-1}\|}\nu_{B,k,e}(\mu^{2}(\beta^{\prime},\beta\beta_{i+kl}),\ldots,\beta_{i}\gamma)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{|\beta\beta_{i}\gamma|}\beta^{\prime}\beta\beta_{i}\gamma-(-1)^{|\gamma|+|\beta\beta_{i}|}\beta^{\prime}\beta\beta_{i}\gamma=0.

We have used that
\|\beta_{i}\gamma\|+\ldots+\|\beta_{i+kl-1}\|=|\gamma|
. It is obvious that
d\nu_{B,k,e}
vanishes on all other types of sequences.
∎

, we arrive at the following description:

.

\displaystyle=\overline{\{\nu_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\mathbb{S}_{\mathsf{generic}}},\{\nu_{B}\}_{B\in\Sigma_{2}},\{\nu_{q,k,\mathrm{e}}\}_{q\in M,k\geq 1},\{\nu_{B,k,\mathrm{e}}\}_{B\in\Sigma_{2},k\geq 1}},

.

3
Spectral sequences of DGLAs

In this section, we exhibit our approach to minimal models of twisted DGLAs. The starting point is a DGLA of the form
L=(L,d+[W,-],[-,-])
. We show that under technical assumptions on the Maurer-Cartan element
W
the morphism
\pi_{W}:L_{W}\to(\operatorname{H}L)_{\pi^{\operatorname{MC}}(W)}
is a quasi-isomorphism. As an application, we exhibit the case of the Hochschild complex of a minimal
A_{\infty}
-category.

3.1
Almost pronilpotent MC elements

We start by fixing a suitable notion of filtration on
L_{\infty}
-algebras. The idea is to create a notion of pronilpotent Maurer-Cartan elements such that embracing brackets and
L_{\infty}
-morphisms by such elements is well-defined. We recall and adapt the following definition from
[
7
]
. The definition is suited well for the Hochschild complex but does not suit for instance
L_{\infty}
-algebras presented as infinite direct sums instead of direct products. In order to treat such cases, a simple adaption of the definition, for instance by means of a convergence requirement for the individual series, will suffice.

Definition 3.1
.

A
filtered graded vector space
is a graded vector space with a complete decreasing filtration

V=F^{0}V\supseteq F^{1}V\supseteq\ldots\supseteq 0.

An
almost pronilpotent
L_{\infty}
-algebra
is an
L_{\infty}
-algebra
L
with the structure of a filtered graded vector space
\{F^{k}L\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
such that for some constant
C\in\mathbb{Z}
we have the rule

l^{m}(F^{k_{1}}L\times\ldots\times F^{k_{m}}L)\subseteq F^{k_{1}+\ldots+k_{m}+C-m}L.

An
almost pronilpotent Maurer-Cartan element
is a Maurer-Cartan element
W\in\operatorname{MC}(L)
which lies in
F^{1}L
. An
almost pronilpotent morphism
of
L_{\infty}
-algebras is a morphism
\varphi:L^{\prime}\to L
such that for some constant
D
we have the rule

\varphi^{m}(F^{k_{1}}L^{\prime}\times\ldots\times F^{k_{m}}L^{\prime})\subseteq F^{k_{1}+\ldots+k_{m}+D-m}L.

The following is a classical lemma in contexts with more rigid nilpotency assumptions, see for instance
[
15
, Proposition 4.4]
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
L
be an almost pronilpotent
L_{\infty}
-algebra and
W
an almost pronilpotent Maurer-Cartan element. Then the following twisting provides a well-defined
L_{\infty}
-structure on
L
which we denote by
L_{W}
:

l^{k}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k})=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{i!}l^{k+i}(W,\ldots,W,a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}).

Let us now examine the twisting of morphisms of almost pronilpotent
L_{\infty}
-algebras.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\varphi:L^{\prime}\to L
be an almost pronilpotent morphism of
L_{\infty}
-algebras. If
W
is an almost pronilpotent Maurer-Cartan element of
L^{\prime}
, then
\varphi^{\operatorname{MC}}(W)
is a well-defined almost pronilpotent Maurer-Cartan element of
L
. The following assignment provides a well-defined almost pronilpotent
L_{\infty}
-morphism
\varphi_{W}:L^{\prime}_{W}\to L_{\varphi^{\operatorname{MC}}(W)}
:

\varphi_{W}(a_{k},\ldots,a_{1})=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{i!}\varphi^{i+k}(W,\ldots,W,a_{k},\ldots,a_{1}).

Proof.

Since
W
is almost pronilpotent, the assignment is well-defined. Moreover,
\varphi_{W}
is skew-symmetric because
\varphi
is skew-symmetric. The remaining part is to verify the
L_{\infty}
-morphism relations. Expand out the left-hand side of the
L_{\infty}
-morphism relations as

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{m,k-m}}\frac{1}{i!j!}\times

(3.1)

\displaystyle\quad\varphi^{i+(k-m+1)}(W,\ldots,W,{l^{\prime}}^{j+m}(W,\ldots,W,a_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,a_{\sigma(m)}),a_{\sigma(m+1)},\ldots,a_{\sigma(k)})

(3.2)

and the right-hand side of the
L_{\infty}
-morphism relations as

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq r\leq k\\
i_{1}+\ldots+i_{r}=k\end{subarray}}~\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,u_{1},\ldots,u_{x},v_{1},\ldots,v_{r}\\
u_{1}+\ldots+u_{x}+v_{1}+\ldots+v_{r}=i+j\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{u_{1}!\ldots u_{x}!i_{1}!\ldots i_{r}!}\times

(3.3)

\displaystyle\quad l\big(\varphi^{u_{1}}(W,\ldots,W),\ldots,\varphi^{u_{x}}(W,\ldots,W),

(3.4)

\displaystyle\quad~~\varphi^{i_{1}+v_{1}}(W,\ldots,W,a_{t(1)},\ldots,a_{t(i_{1})}),\ldots,\varphi^{i_{r}+v_{r}}(W,\ldots,W,a_{t(i_{1}+\ldots+i_{r-1}+1)},\ldots,a_{t(k)})\big).

(3.5)

It is our task to compare these two expressions with the help of the existing
L_{\infty}
-morphism relations which hold between
l
,
l^{\prime}
and
\varphi
. Fix a number
N\geq 0
and regard the input sequence
W,\ldots,W,a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}
of length
N+k
. Evaluate the left-hand side of the
L_{\infty}
-morphism relations for
l,l^{\prime},\varphi
. Then for every pair
(i,j)
with
i+j=N
the left-hand sides includes precisely
{N\choose i}
times the following term, because this is the number of possible choices for the first set of
W
insertions:

\varphi^{i+(k-m+1)}(W,\ldots,W,{l^{\prime}}^{j+m}(W,\ldots,W,a_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,a_{\sigma(m)}),a_{\sigma(m+1)},\ldots,a_{\sigma(k)}).

Evaluate now the right-hand side of the
L_{\infty}
-morphism relations. This yields precisely
{N\choose u_{1},\ldots,u_{x},v_{1},\ldots,v_{r}}
times the following term, because this is the number of possible choices for the partitioning of
W
insertions:

\displaystyle l\big(\varphi^{u_{1}}(W,\ldots,W),\ldots,\varphi^{u_{x}}(W,\ldots,W),

\displaystyle\quad\varphi^{i_{1}+v_{1}}(W,\ldots,W,a_{t(1)},\ldots,a_{t(i_{1})}),\ldots,

\displaystyle\quad\varphi^{i_{r}+v_{r}}(W,\ldots,W,a_{t(i_{1}+\ldots+i_{r-1}+1)},\ldots,a_{t(k)})\big).

To conclude, the
L_{\infty}
-relations for
l,l^{\prime},\varphi
feature precisely
i!j!{i+j\choose i}
times the summand in (
3.1
) and
u_{1}!\ldots u_{x}!i_{1}!\ldots i_{r}!{i+j\choose u_{1},\ldots,u_{x},v_{1},\ldots,v_{r}}
times the summand in (
3.3
). Since both numbers agree, we conclude that the left-hand side and right-hand side of the
L_{\infty}
-morphism relations for
l^{\prime}_{W}
,
l_{\varphi^{\operatorname{MC}}}
and
\varphi_{W}
match up exactly. There are non-matched terms in in (
3.1
) arising from the choice
m=0
. These are covered by the Maurer-Cartan equation for
W
. We omit sign checks and finish the proof.
∎

3.2
Two-step procedure for minimal model

In this section, we explain the basic strategy of the present paper. We start with a DGLA presented as the twisting of a simpler DGLA by an almost pronilpotent Maurer-Cartan element, and provide a two-step procedure to compute its minimal model. For sake of language, use the following terminology:

Definition 3.4
.

A
spectral DGLA
is a DGLA presented in the form
L_{W}
, where
L
is an almost pronilpotent DGLA
L
and
W\in\operatorname{MC}(L)
is an almost pronilpotent Maurer-Cartan element.

In the present paper, we exhibit a strategy to compute minimal models of spectral DGLAs. This strategy consists of the following steps:

1.

Start from a spectral DGLA
L_{W}
.

2.

Compute the minimal model
\operatorname{H}L
together with the projection
\pi:L\to\operatorname{H}L
.

3.

Compute the minimal model
\operatorname{H}((\operatorname{H}L)_{\pi^{\operatorname{MC}}(W)})
together with the projection

\pi^{\prime}:(\operatorname{H}L)_{\pi^{\operatorname{MC}}(W)}\to\operatorname{H}((\operatorname{H}L)_{\pi^{\operatorname{MC}}(W)}).

4.

Form the
spectral projection

\pi^{\prime}\circ\pi_{W}:L_{W}\overset{\pi_{W}}{\to}(\operatorname{H}L)_{\pi^{\operatorname{MC}}(W)}\overset{\pi^{\prime}}{\to}\operatorname{H}((\operatorname{H}L)_{\pi^{\operatorname{MC}}(W)}).

5.

Investigate whether it is a quasi-isomorphism.

Since we do not have an explicit expression for the projection
\pi:L\to\operatorname{H}L
at our disposal, it is useful to consider the composition of
L_{\infty}
-inclusion
i:\operatorname{H}L\to L
as an alternative path. In these cases, the twisting element on
\operatorname{H}L
should be some element
W_{H}\in\operatorname{MC}(\operatorname{H}L)
such that
W=i^{\operatorname{MC}}(W_{H})
. The strategy can then be formulated as follows:

1.

Start from a spectral DGLA
L_{W}
.

2.

Compute the minimal model
\operatorname{H}L
together with the inclusion
i:\operatorname{H}L\to L
.

3.

Find an almost pronilpotent
W_{H}\in\operatorname{MC}(\operatorname{H}L)
such that
W=i^{\operatorname{MC}}(W_{H})
.

4.

Compute the minimal model
\operatorname{H}((\operatorname{H}L)_{W_{H}})
together with the inclusion

i^{\prime}:\operatorname{H}((\operatorname{H}L)_{W_{H}})\to(\operatorname{H}L)_{W_{H}}.

5.

Form the
spectral inclusion

i_{W_{H}}\circ i^{\prime}:\operatorname{H}((\operatorname{H}L)_{W_{H}})\overset{i^{\prime}}{\to}(\operatorname{H}L)_{W_{H}}\overset{i_{W_{H}}}{\to}L_{W}.

6.

Investigate whether it is a quasi-isomorphism.

Remark 3.5
.

In the case of the inclusion procedure we have
\pi^{\operatorname{MC}}(W)=W_{H}
, without further computation. Indeed,

\pi^{\operatorname{MC}}(W)=(\pi\circ i)^{\operatorname{MC}}(W_{H})=\mathrm{id}_{\operatorname{H}L}^{\operatorname{MC}}(W_{H})=W_{H}.

3.3
Hochschild cohomology of
A_{\infty}
-categories

The inclusion procedure applies directly to the Hochschild DGLA of a minimal
A_{\infty}
-category. The idea is to split the Hochschild differential as
d=[\mu^{2},-]+[\mu^{\geq 3},-]
and interpret the cochain
\mu^{\geq 3}
as an almost pronilpotent Maurer-Cartan element.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a graded associative
A_{\infty}
-category and
L=\operatorname{HC}(\mathcal{C})
. Denote by
\operatorname{HC}^{i}(\mathcal{C})\subseteq\operatorname{HC}(\mathcal{C})
the subspace of cochains of length
i
. Then
L
is almost pronilpotent with the filtration
F^{k}L=\prod_{i=k}^{\infty}\operatorname{HC}^{i}(\mathcal{C})
. The minimal model
\operatorname{HH}(\mathcal{C})
obtained from the Kadeishvili construction is almost pronilpotent with the filtration
F^{k}\operatorname{HH}(\mathcal{C})=\prod_{i=k}^{\infty}\operatorname{HH}^{i}(\mathcal{C})
. The projection
\pi:\operatorname{HC}(\mathcal{C})\to\operatorname{HH}(\mathcal{C})
and the inclusion
i:\operatorname{HH}(\mathcal{C})\to\operatorname{HC}(\mathcal{C})
are almost pronilpotent as well.

Proof.

To see that
L
is almost pronilpotent, it suffices to note that the Gerstenhaber bracket satisfies
[\nu,\eta]\in\operatorname{HC}^{i+j-1}(\mathcal{C})
whenever
\nu\in\operatorname{HC}(\mathcal{C})
and
\eta\in\operatorname{HC}(\mathcal{C})
. To see that
\operatorname{HH}(\mathcal{C})
is almost pronilpotent, note that the
L_{\infty}
-bracket
l^{k}
increases the total length of the input cochains by
1-k
. Similarly, the
k
-adic components
\pi^{k}
and
i^{k}
increase the total length of the input cochains by
-k
. This finishes the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a minimal
A_{\infty}
-category and
{\mathcal{C}}_{2}
the underlying associative category. Then
W=\mu^{\geq 3}
is an almost pronilpotent Maurer-Cartan element of
\operatorname{HC}({\mathcal{C}}_{2})
, and
\operatorname{HC}(\mathcal{C})
is spectral:

\operatorname{HC}(\mathcal{C})=\operatorname{HC}({\mathcal{C}}_{2})_{W}.

Proof.

The element
W=\mu^{\geq 3}
is a Maurer-Cartan element of
\operatorname{HC}({\mathcal{C}}_{2})
because

[\mu^{2},\mu^{\geq 3}]+\frac{1}{2}[\mu^{\geq 3},\mu^{\geq 3}]=(\mu^{2}+\mu^{\geq 3})\cdot(\mu^{2}+\mu^{\geq 3})=0.

As a cochain,
W
is concentrated in length
\geq 3
and therefore almost pronilpotent. Observe that
\operatorname{HC}({\mathcal{C}}_{2})_{W}
has differential
[\mu^{2},-]+[\mu^{\geq 3},-]
and bracket
[-,-]
and therefore agrees with
\operatorname{HC}(\mathcal{C})
, as desired.
∎

4
Hochschild cohomology of gentle algebras

In this section, we compute the
L_{\infty}
-structure on the Hochschild cohomology of
\operatorname{Gtl}\Sigma
. The idea is to apply the spectral inclusion procedure of
section 3.2
:
\operatorname{Gtl}_{2}\Sigma
Associative category
\rightsquigarrow
\operatorname{HH}(\operatorname{Gtl}_{2}\Sigma)
Classical Hochschild
\rightsquigarrow
\operatorname{H}(\operatorname{HH}(\operatorname{Gtl}_{2}\Sigma)_{W_{H}})
A_{\infty}
-Hochschild

We start by regarding the associative category
\operatorname{Gtl}_{2}\Sigma
and computing part of the
L_{\infty}
-inclusion
i:\operatorname{HH}(\operatorname{Gtl}_{2}\Sigma)\to\operatorname{HC}(\operatorname{Gtl}_{2}\Sigma)
. We pick a Maurer-Cartan element
W_{H}\in\operatorname{HH}(\operatorname{Gtl}_{2}\Sigma)
and prove that
i^{\operatorname{MC}}(W_{H})=\mu^{\geq 3}_{\operatorname{Gtl}\Sigma}
. Then we compute the minimal model
\operatorname{H}((\operatorname{HH}(\operatorname{Gtl}_{2}\Sigma))_{W_{H}})
and part of the
L_{\infty}
-inclusion

Lemma 4.6
.

Let

be elementary polygons,

be punctures,

and
\nu_{\kappa},\nu_{\lambda}
be any generic sporadic or polygon sporadic classes. Then in
\operatorname{HH}(\operatorname{Gtl}_{2}\Sigma)
, we have the following brackets of cohomology basis elements.

\displaystyle[\nu_{q,k,\mathrm{o}},\nu_{q^{\prime},k^{\prime},\mathrm{o}}]

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle[\nu_{q,k,\mathrm{e}},\nu_{q^{\prime},k^{\prime},\mathrm{o}}]

\displaystyle=\delta_{q,q^{\prime}}\nu_{q,k+k^{\prime},\mathrm{o}},

\displaystyle[\nu_{q,k,\mathrm{e}},\nu_{q^{\prime},k^{\prime},\mathrm{e}}]

\displaystyle=\delta_{q,q^{\prime}}\nu_{q,k+k^{\prime},\mathrm{e}},

\displaystyle[\nu_{\lambda},\nu_{q,k,\mathrm{o}}]

\displaystyle=k\lambda_{\ell_{q}}\nu_{q,k,\mathrm{o}},

\displaystyle[\nu_{\lambda},\nu_{q,k,\mathrm{e}}]

\displaystyle=k\lambda_{\ell_{q}}\nu_{q,k,\mathrm{e}},

\displaystyle[\nu_{\kappa},\nu_{\lambda}]

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle[\nu_{B,k,\mathrm{o}},\nu_{B^{\prime},k^{\prime},\mathrm{o}}]

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle[\nu_{B,k,\mathrm{o}},\nu_{B^{\prime},k^{\prime},\mathrm{e}}]

\displaystyle=\delta_{B,B^{\prime}}\nu_{B,k+k^{\prime},\mathrm{o}},

\displaystyle[\nu_{B,k,\mathrm{o}},\nu_{q,k^{\prime},\mathrm{o}}]

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle[\nu_{B,k,\mathrm{o}},\nu_{q,k^{\prime},\mathrm{e}}]

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle[\nu_{B,k,\mathrm{o}},\nu_{\lambda}]

\displaystyle=k\sum_{i=1}^{l}\lambda(\beta_{i})\nu_{B,k,\mathrm{o}}.

[a,b]=\sum_{N=0}^{\infty}\sum_{i+j=N}[a_{i},b_{j}].

Proof.

We check all individual brackets in sequence. After that, we comment on brackets of series as well as the higher brackets.

•

Regard
[\nu_{q,k,\mathrm{o}},\nu_{q^{\prime},k^{\prime},\mathrm{o}}]

\operatorname{HC}(\operatorname{Gtl}_{2}\Sigma)

Regard
[\nu_{q,k,\mathrm{e}},\nu_{q^{\prime},k^{\prime},\mathrm{o}}]

\operatorname{HC}(\operatorname{Gtl}_{2}\Sigma)

\displaystyle[\nu_{q,k,\mathrm{e}},\nu_{q^{\prime},k^{\prime},\mathrm{o}}]^{0}

\displaystyle=\nu_{q,k,\mathrm{e}}(\nu_{q^{\prime},k^{\prime},\mathrm{o}})=\delta_{q,q^{\prime}}\ell_{q}^{k}\nu_{q^{\prime},k^{\prime},\mathrm{o}}^{0}=\delta_{q,q^{\prime}}\nu_{q,k+k^{\prime},\mathrm{o}}^{0}.

Thus, the bracket equals
\nu_{q,k+k^{\prime},\mathrm{o}}
which is already a cohomology class.

•

Regard
[\nu_{q,k,\mathrm{e}},\nu_{q^{\prime},k^{\prime},\mathrm{e}}]

\operatorname{HC}(\operatorname{Gtl}_{2}\Sigma)
, this bracket has only a 1-adic component and this component is a derivation. In case
q=q^{\prime}
, then for any indecomposable angle
\alpha

\displaystyle[\nu_{q,k,\mathrm{e}},\nu_{q^{\prime},k^{\prime},\mathrm{e}}](\alpha)

\displaystyle=\nu_{q,k,\mathrm{e}}(\nu_{q,k^{\prime},\mathrm{e}}(\alpha))-\nu_{q,k^{\prime},\mathrm{e}}(\nu_{q,k,\mathrm{e}}(\alpha))

\displaystyle=\nu_{q,k,\mathrm{e}}(\delta_{\alpha,\alpha_{1}}\ell^{k^{\prime}}\alpha)-\nu_{q,k^{\prime},\mathrm{e}}(\delta_{\alpha,\alpha_{1}}\ell^{k}\alpha)

\displaystyle=\delta_{\alpha,\alpha_{1}}\big(k^{\prime}\ell^{k^{\prime}+k}\alpha+\ell^{k^{\prime}+k}\delta_{\alpha_{1},\alpha}\alpha-k\ell^{k+k^{\prime}}\alpha-\ell^{k+k^{\prime}}\delta_{\alpha_{1},\alpha}\alpha\big)

\displaystyle=\delta_{\alpha,\alpha_{1}}(k^{\prime}-k)\ell^{k+k^{\prime}}\alpha.

We have used that
\nu_{q,k,\mathrm{e}}(\ell_{q})=\nu_{q,k,\mathrm{e}}(\alpha_{s}\ldots\alpha_{1})=\ell^{k+1}_{q}
and
\nu_{q,k,\mathrm{e}}(\ell_{q}^{i})=i\ell^{k+i}_{q}
. We conclude that
[\nu_{q,k,\mathrm{e}},\nu_{q^{\prime},k^{\prime},\mathrm{e}}]
is precisely cohomology element
(k^{\prime}-k)\nu_{q,k+k^{\prime},\mathrm{e}}
.

Regard

•

Regard

\displaystyle=\delta_{\alpha,\alpha_{1}}(k\lambda_{\ell_{q}}\alpha+\ell_{q}\lambda_{\alpha})-\delta_{\alpha,\alpha_{1}}\ell_{q}^{k}(\lambda_{\alpha}\alpha)=\delta_{\alpha,\alpha_{1}}k\lambda_{\ell_{q}}\alpha.

Lemma 4.7
.

W_{H}\in\operatorname{HH}(\operatorname{Gtl}_{2}\Sigma)
is an almost pronilpotent Maurer-Cartan. We have

i^{\operatorname{MC}}(W_{H})=\mu^{\geq 3},\quad\pi^{\operatorname{MC}}(\mu^{\geq 3})=W_{H}.

Proof.

We divide the proof into three steps. In the first step, we explain the strategy for computing
i^{\operatorname{MC}}(W_{H})
and the trees to be evaluated. In the second step, we perform this evaluation as far as necessary. In the third step, we draw the conclusion.

For the first step, recall from

that
i^{\operatorname{MC}}(W_{H})
is given by the sum of weighted expressions obtained from trees with any number of inputs

and with the leaves decorated by cochains

and

on each node of the tree. In the remainder of the proof, we evaluate these trees and the resulting expressions. For temporary purposes, let us split

where
\mu^{\geq 3}_{1}=\sum_{B\in\Sigma_{2}}\nu_{B,1,\mathrm{o}}
contains all cochains which take elementary polygons as inputs and
\mu^{\geq 3}_{\geq 2}
contains all cochains which take all non-elementary disk sequences as inputs. We note that
i^{1}(W_{H})=\mu^{\geq 3}_{1}
.

For the second part, we show that the trees with at least 2 leaves yield only cochains which, up to sign and weight factor, already lie in
\mu^{\geq 3}_{\geq 2}
. Indeed, thanks to
Lemma 4.5
we have that
[\nu_{D_{1}},\nu_{D_{2}}]=\nu_{D_{1}\cup D_{2}}
. Now let
B_{1},\ldots,B_{k}
be a sequence of

\nu_{B_{1},1,\mathrm{o}},\ldots,\nu_{B_{k},1,\mathrm{o}}
is a scalar multiple of a cochain already present in

For the third part, we claim that
i^{\operatorname{MC}}(W_{H})=\mu^{\geq 3}
. Indeed, both

and

, in other words

-structures on
\operatorname{Gtl}_{2}\Sigma

4.3
Computation of
\operatorname{H}(\operatorname{HH}(\operatorname{Gtl}_{2}\Sigma)_{W_{H}})

In this section, we compute the minimal model of
\operatorname{HH}(\operatorname{Gtl}_{2}\Sigma)_{W_{H}}
. We start by describing a homological splitting of
\operatorname{HH}(\operatorname{Gtl}_{2}\Sigma)_{W_{H}}
. Next, we compute the
L_{\infty}
-structure on
\operatorname{H}(\operatorname{HH}(\operatorname{Gtl}_{2}\Sigma)_{W_{H}})
. As it turns out, the cohomology is simply an
L_{\infty}
-subalgebra of
\operatorname{HH}(\operatorname{Gtl}_{2}\Sigma)
which only contains the cohomology classes
\nu_{1}
,
\nu_{k,q,\mathrm{o}}
,
\nu_{k,q,\mathrm{e}}
and

.

We start by recalling that
\operatorname{HH}(\operatorname{Gtl}_{2}\Sigma)
is a DGLA with zero differential and explicitly provided brackets. The sum

is a Maurer-Cartan element on
\operatorname{HH}(\operatorname{Gtl}_{2}\Sigma)
, and the DGLA
\operatorname{HH}(\operatorname{Gtl}_{2}\Sigma)_{W_{H}}
has the same underlying graded vector space as
\operatorname{HH}(\operatorname{Gtl}_{2}\Sigma)
and the same bracket but has differential given by

. We are now ready to construct a homological splitting

for
\operatorname{HH}(\operatorname{Gtl}_{2}\Sigma)_{W_{H}}
.

Definition 4.8
.

The cohomology space

is the span of the following set of cochains:

•

\nu_{1}
.

•

\nu_{q,k,\mathrm{o}}
for every puncture
q
and
k\geq 1
.

•

\nu_{q,k,\mathrm{e}}
for every puncture
q
and
k\geq 1
.

•

\nu_{\lambda}
for every

.

The complement space
R
is the span of the following set of cochains:

•

\nu_{B,k,\mathrm{e}}
for every elementary polygon

and

.

•

\nu_{\lambda}
for

Lemma 4.9
.

The spaces
H
and
R
provides a homological splitting
H\oplus\operatorname{Im}(d)\oplus R
for
\operatorname{HH}(\operatorname{Gtl}_{2}\Sigma)_{W_{H}}
.

Proof.

Let us explain that
\operatorname{HH}(\operatorname{Gtl}_{2}\Sigma)_{W_{H}}
is the sum of the three spaces. Let
B
be an elementary polygon, and denote by
B_{0}
the elementary polygon excluded from the polygon sporadic classes
\mathbb{S}_{\mathsf{poly}}
. In case

, we have
\nu_{B,1,\mathrm{o}}=d(\nu_{\lambda})
where
\lambda\in\mathbb{S}_{\mathsf{poly}}
is the sporadic index associated with the polygon

. In case

, we have
\nu_{B,k,\mathrm{o}}=\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{S}_{\mathsf{poly}},\lambda\neq B_{0}}d(\nu_{\lambda})
. Furthermore we have
\nu_{B,k,\mathrm{o}}=d(\nu_{B,k-1,\mathrm{e}})
for

.

Next, we observe that

and the sum
H\oplus\operatorname{Im}(d)
is direct. To see that

, regard the differential of any element of

:

If this differential vanishes, then the individual scalars

∎

Proposition 4.10
.

In
\operatorname{H}(\operatorname{HH}(\operatorname{Gtl}_{2}\Sigma)_{W_{H}})
we have the following brackets. All higher brackets vanish.

\displaystyle[\nu_{q,k,\mathrm{o}},\nu_{q^{\prime},k^{\prime},\mathrm{o}}]

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle[\nu_{q,k,\mathrm{e}},\nu_{q^{\prime},k^{\prime},\mathrm{o}}]

\displaystyle=\delta_{q,q^{\prime}}\nu_{q,k+k^{\prime},\mathrm{o}},

\displaystyle[\nu_{q,k,\mathrm{e}},\nu_{q^{\prime},k^{\prime},\mathrm{e}}]

\displaystyle=\delta_{q,q^{\prime}}\nu_{q,k+k^{\prime},\mathrm{e}},

\displaystyle[\nu_{\lambda},\nu_{q,k,\mathrm{o}}]

\displaystyle=k\lambda_{\ell_{q}}\nu_{q,k,\mathrm{o}},

\displaystyle[\nu_{\lambda},\nu_{q,k,\mathrm{e}}]

\displaystyle=k\lambda_{\ell_{q}}\nu_{q,k,\mathrm{e}},

\displaystyle[\nu_{\kappa},\nu_{\lambda}]

\displaystyle=0.

Proof.

By definition, each of these brackets is given by the projection to
H
of the result of the bracket in
\operatorname{HH}(\operatorname{Gtl}_{2}\Sigma)_{W_{H}}
. We have computed the brackets in
\operatorname{HH}(\operatorname{Gtl}_{2}\Sigma)
in

and any application of the codifferential

∎

4.4
Main result

In this section, we construct the spectral inclusion

\operatorname{H}(\operatorname{HH}(\operatorname{Gtl}_{2}\Sigma)_{W_{H}})\overset{i^{\prime}}{\to}\operatorname{HH}(\operatorname{Gtl}_{2}\Sigma)_{W_{H}}\overset{i_{W_{H}}}{\to}\operatorname{HC}(\operatorname{Gtl}_{2}\Sigma)_{\mu^{\geq 3}}=\operatorname{HC}(\operatorname{Gtl}\Sigma).

We prove that it is an
L_{\infty}
-quasi-isomorphism and explain how it transports Maurer-Cartan elements of
\operatorname{H}(\operatorname{HH}(\operatorname{Gtl}_{2}\Sigma)_{W_{H}})
to deformations of
\operatorname{Gtl}\Sigma
.

Remark 4.11
.

For general
A_{\infty}
-categories
\mathcal{C}
, it is unclear whether the map
i_{W_{H}}:\operatorname{H}(\operatorname{HH}({\mathcal{C}}_{2})_{W_{H}}\to\operatorname{HH}({\mathcal{C}}_{2})_{W_{H}}
is a quasi-isomorphism. For gentle algebras, we are however able to check this by hand with the help of the description of Hochschild cohomology from
[
4
]
. In result, the description of the entire
L_{\infty}
-structure on
\operatorname{HH}(\operatorname{Gtl}\Sigma)
including the vanishing of the higher brackets is immediate. Moreover, we are able to describe the spectral inclusion
i_{W_{H}}\circ i^{\prime}
neatly on the level of Maurer-Cartan elements.

Remark 4.12
.

In order to avoid confusion, we shall use the following parameter space
V
which does not reference Hochschild cohomology.

V=\frac{\mathbb{C}[\ell_{q}~|~q\in M]}{(\ell_{q}\ell_{p}~|~q\neq p)}.

A parameter
r\in V
can alternatively be written as

r=r_{1}+\sum_{q\in M,k\geq 1}r_{q,k}\ell_{q}^{k},\qquad r=r_{1}+\sum_{q\in M}R_{q}(\ell_{q}).

For each
q\in M
the series
R_{q}(\ell_{q})
is a polynomial.

We shall now recall the specific deformations from
[
4
]
. Let
(B,\mathfrak{m})
be a deformation base and let
r\in\mathfrak{m}\widehat{\otimes}V
be a deformation parameter. Then the construction of
[
4
]
provides an curved
A_{\infty}
-deformation
{}^{r}\mu
of
\mu
. The deformed curved
A_{\infty}
-structure
{}^{r}\mu
on
\operatorname{Gtl}_{2}\Sigma
is constructed by counting elementary polygons and in addition orbigons which are weighted with
r_{q,k}
factors. The curvature of each arc incident at a puncture
q
is given by
R_{q}(\ell_{q})
, a linear combination of full turns. We can view the difference
{}^{r}\mu-\mu\in\mathfrak{m}\widehat{\otimes}\operatorname{HC}(\operatorname{Gtl}\Sigma)
as a Maurer-Cartan element of the DGLA
\mathfrak{m}\widehat{\otimes}\operatorname{HC}(\operatorname{Gtl}\Sigma)
.

Thanks to
Proposition 4.10
, we have an identification between
V
and
\operatorname{H}(\operatorname{HH}(\operatorname{Gtl}_{2}\Sigma)_{W_{H}})^{\mathrm{o}}
. In particular, every
r\in\,V
can be viewed as an element of the DGLA
\operatorname{H}(\operatorname{HH}(\operatorname{Gtl}_{2}\Sigma)_{W_{H}})
and every
r\in\mathfrak{m}\widehat{\otimes}V
can be viewed as a Maurer-Cartan element of the DGLA
\mathfrak{m}\widehat{\otimes}\operatorname{H}(\operatorname{HH}(\operatorname{Gtl}_{2}\Sigma)_{W_{H}})
.

With these preparations in mind, we can state our main theorem.

Theorem 4.13
.

Let
(\Sigma,M)
be a punctured surface with [NMD] and let
\operatorname{Gtl}\Sigma
be the
A_{\infty}
-gentle algebra defined by means of a full arc system. Let
(B,\mathfrak{m})
be a deformation base.

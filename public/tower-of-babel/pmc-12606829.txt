Awareness, attitudes, and clinical utilization of vonoprazan for Helicobacter pylori infection and gastroesophageal reflux disease among Chinese physicians: a nationwide cross-sectional study
Authors: Hui Xu, Hao Zhao, Heqing Tao, Ligang Liu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundVonoprazan has been introduced as an alternative to proton pump inhibitors (PPIs) for Helicobacter pylori (H. pylori) infection and gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD). Despite regulatory approval and guideline inclusion in China, physician awareness, clinical utilization, and perceived barriers are not well described. This study aimed to assess the awareness, clinical utilization, and attitudes toward vonoprazan among Chinese physicians.MethodsA nationwide cross-sectional, web-based survey was conducted among Chinese physicians between December 2024 and February 2025. Eligible participants included gastroenterologists, internists, and general practitioners. The 30-item survey captured clinician demographics, knowledge of vonoprazan, prescribing patterns, perceived advantages and limitations, and potential future indications. Descriptive statistics and subgroup analyses were performed. Multivariable logistic regression identified factors associated with vonoprazan use. Statistical significance was set at p < 0.05.Results361 physicians completed the survey. Among them, 54.8% were resident physicians, 32.7% were attending physicians, 53.7% were women, and 70.1% practiced in tertiary hospitals. Overall, 27.1% were well informed about vonoprazan, 41.3% had heard of it but were unfamiliar, and 31.6% had never heard of it. Vonoprazan use was reported by 11.9% of respondents for H. pylori eradication and 10.5% for GERD management. Utilization was significantly higher among gastroenterologists and physicians working in secondary or tertiary hospitals (p < 0.05). In multivariable logistic regression, physicians from internal medicine (non-gastroenterology) (OR 0.35, 95% CI 0.15–0.77), surgical departments (OR 0.33, 95% CI 0.15–0.69), and those with lower awareness (OR 0.25, 95% CI 0.13–0.46) were significantly less likely to use vonoprazan. The main perceived advantages of vonoprazan were greater efficacy (59.1%), durable acid suppression (48.6%), and faster onset (40.9%). Key barriers to its use included preference for PPIs (51.0%), lack of hospital formulary approval (40.2%), limited local data (21.6%), and high cost (18.6%).ConclusionsIn this national sample, Chinese physicians demonstrated moderate awareness but limited real-world use of vonoprazan for H. pylori infection and GERD. Utilization was largely confined to tertiary centers and gastroenterology specialists. Broader educational initiatives, inclusion in hospital formularies, reimbursement alignment, and generation of real-world evidence may help facilitate appropriate and equitable adoption of vonoprazan in clinical practice.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12876-025-04408-7.

Introduction

Helicobacter pylori (H. pylori) infection and gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD) are significant public health challenges in China [1, 2]. The estimated prevalence is 42.8% for H. pylori infection and 10.6% for GERD [1, 2]. Both conditions contributed to morbidity, reduced quality of life, and increased healthcare burden [3, 4].

For the management of H. pylori infection, current Chinese guideline recommends bismuth quadruple therapy and high-dose dual therapy (proton pump inhibitors [PPIs] and amoxicillin) for initial and second eradication therapies [5]. Clarithromycin triple therapy is no longer favored due to rising antibiotic resistance [5]. GERD is commonly managed with PPIs [6]. Nonetheless, adherence to complex H. pylori regimens is often suboptimal, leading to treatment failure [7]. Up to 40% of patients with GERD report persistent symptoms despite PPI therapy [8]. In addition, long-term PPI use has been linked to potential risks [9]. These limitations necessitate the need for more effective therapeutic options.

Vonoprazan, a potassium-competitive acid blocker (P-CAB), has emerged as an alternative to PPIs. Its rapid, pH-independent activation and durable acid suppression offer potential advantages over PPIs in terms of both efficacy and patient adherence [10, 11]. Clinical trials have shown that vonoprazan was effective for H. pylori eradication and PPI-refractory GERD [12, 13]. Despite recent regulatory approval and inclusion into national guidelines in China, physicians’ awareness, attitudes, clinical utilization, and perceived barriers to adoption remain largely unknown. This evidence gap undermines implementation and hinders policy and formulary planning. A systematic assessment of clinician’s perceptions and prescribing is therefore needed to guide education, facilitate guideline uptake, and improve patient outcomes.

This study aimed to evaluate the awareness, clinical utilization, and perceived advantages and barriers to vonoprazan use for H. pylori infection and GERD among Chinese physicians.

Methods

This cross-sectional study followed the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) guidelines. An anonymous, self-administered, web-based survey was distributed between December 2024 and January 2025 to licensed physicians across China. Participation was voluntary, and informed consent was obtained electronically before survey completion.

Study participants

Eligible participants were licensed physicians specializing in gastroenterology, internal medicine, or general practice who provided clinical care for patients with H. pylori infection or GERD. To ensure geographic and institutional diversity, we used stratified purposive sampling based on region (East, Central, West), type of healthcare setting (tertiary, secondary, primary care), and physician specialty. Within each stratum, a QR code linking to the online questionnaire was distributed through national professional societies, hospital mailing lists, and physician WeChat groups. This approach enabled wide distribution across diverse healthcare settings and facilitated recruitment from a broad range of providers. Due to the broad distribution method through multiple channels, the total number of physicians who received the survey invitation could not be reliably determined, and therefore a formal response rate cannot be calculated.

Development, validation, and distribution of survey questionnaire

The questionnaire comprised 30 closed-ended questions and was designed to take approximately 20–25 min to complete. This questionnaire was specifically developed for this research and had not been previously published elsewhere. The initial draft of the questionnaire was developed in Mandarin, informed by an extensive review of published literature on vonoprazan, clinical treatment guidelines for acid-related disorders, and previous survey instruments used in studies exploring physician prescribing behaviors. The content was tailored to address issues relevant to Chinese healthcare system, including guideline adherence, treatment preferences, and formulary access. The questionnaire included items across four domains: (1) Demographic and professional characteristics of respondents, including job title, gender, years of clinical practice, specialty, hospital level, and location; (2) Clinical experience and knowledge regarding the use of vonoprazan for H. pylori infection and GERD; (3) Attitudes and perceptions toward vonoprazan’s efficacy, safety, onset of action, acid suppression, cost, and potential benefits compared to PPIs; (4) Clinical prescribing preferences and future outlook, including treatment regimens, patient selection, anticipated challenges, and expectations for potential future indications. An English version of the questionnaire was available as Supplementary File 1.

To ensure the content validity and clarity of the questionnaire, face and content validation was conducted by a panel of six experts, comprising three gastroenterologists and three academic faculty members with expertise in clinical pharmacology and public health. Their feedback was used to improve the relevance of questionnaires, logical structure, and ease of interpretation. Following expert review, the revised questionnaire underwent a pilot test with five practicing physicians from different hospital levels (tertiary, secondary, and community settings) to evaluate the clarity of wording, flow of questions, and overall acceptability. Suggestions provided during the pretesting phase were incorporated to improve question phrasing and refine the sequencing for better comprehension and engagement. Minor modifications were made. Psychometric testing (e.g., internal consistency/Cronbach’s α, test–retest reliability) was not performed.

Data collection and quality control procedures

Prior to initiating the survey, all potential participants were informed about the objectives and scope of the study through a brief introduction embedded in the online survey link. On the designated data collection dates, the final version of the structured and pre-validated questionnaire was distributed electronically. Only physicians who voluntarily consented were permitted to access and complete the survey. All submitted responses from eligible and consenting physicians were included in the analysis. To ensure data accuracy and respondent confidentiality, submissions were restricted to one per participant, and all responses were collected anonymously through a secure online platform. The study was exempt for ethics approval by the Beijing You’an Hospital Institutional Review Board because of the anonymous, non-interventional survey design.

To ensure data quality and response validity, several control mechanisms were implemented. An initial Completely Automated Public Turing test to tell Computers and Humans Apart (CAPTCHA) verification was included to confirm human respondents. Three attention check items were embedded throughout the survey to detect random or inattentive responding. Responses were excluded from the final analysis if participants (1) failed the CAPTCHA verification, (2) incorrectly answered any of the attention check items, (3) submitted duplicate responses, (4) indicated difficulties that would affect data accuracy, or (5) did not complete the full survey. Only responses that passed all quality control checks were included in the analytic sample.

Statistical analysis

Analyses were conducted using Stata 18.0 (StataCorp, College Station, TX). Descriptive statistics summarized demographic characteristics and survey responses. We conducted subgroup analyses by job title, specialty, hospital level, and years in practice, and performed univariable and multivariable logistic regression to identify predictors of self-reported vonoprazan use. Results were presented as odds ratios (ORs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs). Statistical significance was set at p < 0.05.

Results

Respondent characteristics

A total of 361 physicians completed the survey. Among them, 54.8% were resident physicians and 32.7% were attending physicians, and 53.7% were women. The majority (63.4%) had less than 5 years of clinical practice. Respondents primarily specialized in gastroenterology (38.8%) or other internal medicine departments (23.0%), and most were affiliated with tertiary hospitals (70.1%). Geographically, participants were primarily from Beijing (31.0%), Shandong (16.6%), and Guangdong (16.1%). Detailed demographic and professional characteristics are summarized in Table 1.

Awareness and knowledge of vonoprazan

Among all participants, 27.1% reported being well-informed about vonoprazan, while 41.3% had heard of it but were not familiar with the details, and 31.6% had never heard of it. The awareness and knowledge of vonoprazan differed across institutional and professional characteristics (Fig. 1). By hospital level, awareness was lowest among physicians in community hospitals and highest among those in tertiary hospitals. Across specialties, gastroenterologists demonstrated the greatest familiarity, and the differences across departments were statistically significant (p < 0.001). Regarding job title, resident physicians reported the highest level of familiarity. By years of practice, clinicians with more than 20 years of experience were the most well-informed.

Among those who had knowledge about vonoprazan, 57.1% found it more effective than PPIs, 19.4% viewed it as similarly effective, 22.3% were unsure, and 1.2% considered it less effective. Regarding safety, 59.5% of respondents reported that vonoprazan had better safety and excellent tolerability compared to PPIs. 27.9% of participants were unsure, 10.1% noted moderate safety with some adverse effects, and 2.4% perceived it as having poor safety or frequent adverse effects.

The most perceived advantages of vonoprazan over PPIs were greater efficacy (59.1%), long-lasting acid suppression (48.6%), and faster onset of action (40.9%). Additional advantages included better tolerance (40.1%), meal-independent administration (32.8%), effectiveness against nocturnal acid breakthrough (32.8%), and the potential to reduce antibiotic resistance (23.1%).

Vonoprazan use

Vonoprazan use varied by institutional and professional characteristics (Fig. 2). Clinical utilization was lowest among physicians working in community hospitals and significantly higher in secondary and tertiary centers (p < 0.05). Gastroenterologists reported the highest rates of vonoprazan use (p < 0.001). Reported utilization did not differ significantly by physician job title. Clinicians with 6–10 years of experience demonstrated greater use compared with other groups.

Vonoprazan use in H. pylori eradication

Among the respondents, 36.6% reported using bismuth quadruple therapy as preferred regimen for H. pylori eradication, followed by PPI-based triple therapy (32.4%) and high-dose PPI plus antibiotics dual therapy (18.6%). Vonoprazan-based regimens were less commonly used (11.9%) (Table 2). Vonoprazan was considered appropriate for patients with prior treatment failure (69.2%), poor response to PPIs (63.2%), or suspected antibiotic resistance (48.2%). Many respondents viewed it as suitable for first line (42.1%) or second line (30.4%) therapy, and 53.8% supported its use in refractory cases.

Vonoprazan use in GERD management

For GERD treatment, PPIs were most used (81.7%), followed by vonoprazan (10.5%) and H2-receptor antagonists (7.8%) (Table 2). Among those who had heard of vonoprazan, 30.4% prescribed it for GERD. Vonoprazan was perceived as appropriate for refractory GERD (79.4%), recurrent symptoms (63.6%), patients at risk of drug interactions (47.8%), and those requiring rapid symptom relief (38.1%). Most respondents (59.1%) expected vonoprazan to become a first-line therapy, while 28.3% viewed it as a second-line or later option.

Predictors of Vonoprazan use

In univariable logistic models, male physicians are less likely to use vonoprazan than female (OR 0.61, 95% CI 0.37–0.99). Compared to gastroenterologists, physicians in general internal medicine (OR 0.18, 95% CI 0.08–0.35) and other surgical departments (OR 0.14, 95% CI 0.07–0.26) were less likely to use. Compared with community facilities, physicians in secondary and tertiary hospitals were more likely to use (OR 3.23, 95% CI 1.12–10.74; OR 3.37, 95% CI 1.40–10.05.40.05). Years in practice and job title were not associated with vonoprazan use (p > 0.05) (Table 3).

Clinician perspectives on the future use, barriers, and research needs for Vonoprazan

Regarding future use of vonoprazan, 44.5% of respondents reported they would use it for H. pylori infection, and 45.4% said they might consider using it; 10.1% were unsure. For GERD treatment, 44.6% indicated they would definitely use vonoprazan, 43.7% might consider it, and 11.7% were uncertain.

The most reported barriers to vonoprazan use for H. pylori infection were preference for traditional treatments such as PPIs (51.0%) and lack of hospital approval (40.2%). Other concerns included insufficient clinical data (21.6%), high economic cost (18.6%), concerns about efficacy or safety (10.3%), and patient reluctance to try new drugs (13.4%). Similarly, for GERD treatment, the main barriers were lack of hospital approval (49.4%) and reliance on traditional therapies (43.1%), followed by insufficient data (26.4%), high cost (19.5%), safety concerns (13.8%), and patient hesitation (12.1%) (Table 5).

The most cited issues needing to be addressed for wider clinical adoption of vonoprazan were limited clinical evidence (75.3%) and improved cost control or reimbursement policies (70.0%). Other key priorities included clinician and patient education (49.0%) and improved drug accessibility (45.7%). The most identified research needs to support vonoprazan use included long-term efficacy studies (77.7%), comparative studies with other treatments (67.6%), and additional safety data (64.4%). Health economic evaluations were also noted by 45.3% of respondents.

Potential future indications

Among respondents familiar with vonoprazan, 68.4% suggested its potential utility in the management of gastric and duodenal ulcers. Furthermore, 47% indicated that vonoprazan might be beneficial in patients with liver cirrhosis complicated by esophageal and gastric variceal bleeding. Additionally, 53% considered vonoprazan to be potentially useful for patients undergoing prolonged corticosteroid therapy. A smaller proportion (12.1%) mentioned its possible application in managing esophageal fungal infections.

Discussion

This nationwide cross-sectional study provided a timely overview of Chinese physicians’ awareness, clinical use, perceived advantages, and barriers regarding vonoprazan for the treatment of H. pylori infection and GERD. Although nearly 70% of respondents reported some familiarity with vonoprazan, its real-world use remains limited, with only 11.9% of physicians reported using it for H. pylori eradication, and 10.2% had prescribed it for GERD. Nevertheless, most physicians viewed vonoprazan as having better or comparable efficacy and safety to PPIs, and many expressed strong interests in its future use.

Specialty and practice setting were strongly linked to knowledge and clinical utilization. Gastroenterologists were more familiar with vonoprazan than physicians from other internal medicine or surgical departments. Physicians in tertiary hospitals also reported greater awareness and higher prescribing rates, likely reflecting their exposure to patients who require more intensive acid suppression and their earlier access to new agents through institutional formularies and research networks [14]. These patterns are consistent with adoption trends seen for other novel therapies, where subspecialty focus and institutional capacity often drive early use [15].

Despite rising awareness, physicians continue to rely heavily on established regimens. We found that 32.4% of physicians reported clarithromycin triple therapy as a preferred regimen for H. pylori eradication despite national guidelines advising against its empirical use due to high clarithromycin resistance. The persistence of outdated regimen suggests therapeutic inertia, limited dissemination of antimicrobial resistance data, and constrained access to newer alternatives such as vonoprazan-based regimens or high-dose dual therapy. As this study relied on self-reported survey responses, these findings might reflect physician perceptions and reported practices rather than confirmed prescribing audits.

For GERD management, PPIs remains the dominant therapy (81.7%), while vonoprazan was prescribed by only 10.2% of respondents. This persists even as clinicians report dissatisfaction with PPIs due to incomplete symptom control, breakthrough symptoms, safety concerns, and reliance on over-the-counter medications [16, 17]. Despite this conservative use, 59.1% of clinicians expect vonoprazan to become a first-line option in the future, indicating optimism and perceived unmet needs with current treatments. Nearly 90% of clinicians surveyed indicated they would use vonoprazan for H. pylori or GERD in the future. This intent likely reflects growing clinical dissatisfaction with existing regimens, particularly in patients with prior treatment failure and poor PPI response.

Some respondents suggested that vonoprazan could be used for conditions such as gastroduodenal ulcers, corticosteroid-associated mucosal injury, or variceal bleeding in cirrhosis. These views appear to extrapolate from vonoprazan’s potent acid suppression rather than from clinical evidence. To our knowledge, interventional or robust observational studies supporting vonoprazan in these settings are lacking. Therefore, these viewpoints should be viewed as hypotheses for future study rather than viable indications. Appropriately designed clinical studies would be required to evaluate safety and efficacy before any such uses could be considered.

Our findings complement and extend those of a recent national survey of gastroenterologists on high-dose dual therapy for H. pylori infection in China [18]. That study primarily targeted gastroenterology specialists and reported high awareness and adoption of high-dose dual therapy among this group. However, unlike dual therapy, which has gained visibility through domestic guideline endorsement and clinical society promotion, vonoprazan remains relatively new to most clinicians in China.

Internationally, adoption patterns of vonoprazan demonstrate marked regional variation. In Japan, where vonoprazan was first introduced in 2015, uptake has been widespread and supported by favorable real-world outcomes in both H. pylori eradication and GERD management [19, 20]. In South Korea, use has been influenced by formulary access and the availability of alternative P-CABs, such as tegoprazan, which has led to competitive but diversified adoption patterns [21]. In the United States, vonoprazan received FDA approval in 2022 for the treatment of H. pylori infection, but real-world adoption data remain scarce. Similarly, in European countries, the clinical use of vonoprazan is still at an early stage. The global comparisons demonstrated that awareness, access, and policy support play critical roles in driving vonoprazan adoption. While the pharmacologic advantages of vonoprazan are well established, its successful integration into clinical practice depends on systemic factors such as healthcare infrastructure, formulary inclusion, reimbursement support, and physician education. These enabling mechanisms are still evolving in China, limiting widespread adoption and consistent use.

Barriers to adoption were consistent with broader literature on new drug diffusion, including lack of hospital approval, reliance on traditional therapies, limited clinical data, high cost, and safety concerns. These barriers likely reflect structural differences across regions and hospital levels in China, where formulary access and reimbursement decisions remain heterogeneous [14]. Policy reforms, such as updates to the National Reimbursement Drug List and pharmacoeconomic-driven pricing negotiations, may facilitate greater access, particularly outside tertiary centers [22, 23].

This study has several strengths, including its national reach, the inclusion of physicians from various specialties and hospital levels, and its detailed evaluation of both prescribing practices and perceptions about vonoprazan use. However, some limitations should be considered. As with all self-reported surveys, there is a potential for response and recall bias, which may affect the accuracy of reported behaviors. Second, the survey was distributed online through professional societies, hospital mailing lists, and WeChat groups, which may have introduced coverage bias by overrepresenting younger, urban, and tertiary-care physicians with better digital access, while underrepresenting perspectives from rural and community hospitals. Third, although the questionnaire was developed through literature review, expert consultation, and pilot testing to ensure clarity and content validity, no formal psychometric was performed, limiting full assessment of instrument reliability. Fourth, the study was exempted from institutional ethics review due to its anonymous, non-interventional design; however, the absence of IRB approval may be viewed as a limitation by some international readers. Fifth, the relatively short data collection period (two months) may not reflect changes in clinical practice or policy occurring afterward. Sixth, reported prescribing patterns represent physician perceptions and intentions rather than objective prescribing data, and should be interpreted with caution. Finally, most respondents were from three provinces (Beijing, Shandong, and Guangdong), potentially limiting the generalizability of findings to all of China. Physicians in these regions, particularly those affiliated with tertiary hospitals, may have greater exposure to novel therapies than those in other provinces. These limitations should be considered when interpreting the study’s findings.

Conclusions

In this national survey, Chinese clinicians reported moderate awareness but limited real-world use of vonoprazan for H. pylori infection and GERD. Vonoprazan use was largely confined to tertiary centers and gastroenterology specialists. The most cited barriers to adoption were limited formulary access, cost, and uncertainty about real-world evidence. Most clinicians indicated they would consider vonoprazan for future use. Targeted education, broader reimbursement coverage, and streamlined formulary approval processes could accelerate guideline-concordant use. While these findings relied on self-reported data, they provided actionable guidance for policymakers, hospital leaders, and clinicians to facilitate equitable and evidence-based use of vonoprazan across healthcare settings in China.

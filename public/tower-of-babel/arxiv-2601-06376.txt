Toricness and Smoothness Criteria for Spherical Varieties
Giuliano Gagliardi, Johannes Hofscheier, Heath Pearson

Abstract
. We prove equivalent numerical conditions for a complete spherical variety to admit a toric structure, and for the smoothness of an arbitrary spherical variety along any given G G -orbit. The conditions are in terms of spherical skeletons, a coarse “subset” of the Luna-Vust data of a spherical variety. Our smoothness criterion improves upon classical criteria by removing the dependency on external reference tables.

Primary: 14M27; Secondary: 14M25, 20G05

1.
Introduction

In this paper we work over the field of complex numbers.

A
spherical variety
is a normal irreducible variety
X
admitting the algebraic action of a connected reductive group
G
, such that a Borel subgroup
B\subseteq G
has an open orbit in
X
. Spherical varieties naturally extend the well-known classes of toric, and generalised flag varieties, which are combinatorially characterised up to equivariant isomorphism by lattice fans, and marked Dynkin diagrams, respectively. The full class of spherical varieties exhibit rich geometrical properties, and admit a hybrid description consisting of both fans and representation theoretical combinatorics.

The results of this paper are equivalent conditions of two geometrical properties in terms of
spherical skeletons
—a simpler coarsening of the full spherical combinatorial data which determines the un-graded Cox ring of a spherical variety, analogously to the root datum of a reductive group encoding the core of its geometry, yet not capturing its isomorphism class. We show equivalent combinatorial conditions for:

(1)

a complete spherical variety to admit a toric structure (
1.3
),

(2)

a spherical variety to be smooth along any given
G
-orbit (
1.5
).

These results are corollaries of the main theorem of this paper (
1.3
), which proves
[
12
, Conjecture 1.4]
.

1.1.
Main theorem

Recall that spherical varieties are classified by
Luna-Vust data
, which is introduced in
Section

2
, and used freely here. The spherical skeleton is a “subset” of the Luna-Vust datum, which was introduced in
[
12
]
to approach the generalised Mukai conjecture for spherical Fano varieties. This conjecture was recently proven by the authors in
[
11
]
and forms the starting point of this work.

Let
R
be the root system of a reductive group
G
with respect to a fixed maximal torus
T\subseteq G
, with simple roots
S\subseteq R
.

Definition 1.1

(
[
12
, Definition 5.1]
)
.

The
spherical skeleton

\mathscr{R}_{X}
of a spherical variety
X
is a quadruple, equipped with two maps

\mathscr{R}_{X}\coloneq(\Sigma,S^{p},\mathcal{D}^{a},\Gamma),\quad\varsigma\colon\mathcal{D}^{a}\to\mathcal{P}(S),\quad\rho^{\prime}\colon\mathcal{D}^{a}\cup\Gamma\to\Lambda^{*}\coloneqq\operatorname{Hom}(\Lambda,\mathbb{Z}).

Here,
\Lambda
is the sublattice of
\mathcal{M}
spanned by the spherical roots
\Sigma
of
X
;
S^{p}
is the subset of simple roots whose minimal parabolic subgroups stabilise every color of
X
;
\mathcal{D}^{a}
is the set of type
a
colors of
X
; and
\Gamma
is the set of
G
-invariant prime divisors of
X
.
For each color
D\in\mathcal{D}
(of any type) of
X
,
\varsigma(D)
is the set of simple roots whose associated minimal parabolic subgroup do not fix
D
. To each
B
-invariant divisor
D\in\mathcal{D}\cup\Gamma
,
\rho^{\prime}
associates the dual vector
\rho^{\prime}(D)\coloneqq\rho(D)|_{\Lambda^{*}}
.

The anticanonical class of a spherical variety
X
has a distinguished representative, namely

-K_{X}=\sum_{D\in\Delta}m_{D}D,

where the coefficients
m_{D}
can be obtained from the spherical skeleton (see
3.6
) and
\Delta\coloneq\mathcal{D}\cup\Gamma
denotes the set of
B
-invariant prime divisors of
X
(which can be extracted from the spherical skeleton
\mathscr{R}_{X}
).

To each spherical skeleton
\mathscr{R}_{X}
, we associate a polyhedron

\mathcal{Q}_{\mathscr{R}_{X}}^{*}\coloneq\bigcap_{D\in\Delta}\left\{v\in\Lambda_{\mathbb{Q}}^{*}\,|\,\langle\rho^{\prime}(D),v\rangle\geq-m_{D}\right\}\subseteq\Lambda_{\mathbb{Q}}.

Furthermore, the spherical skeleton comes with the following combinatorial function

\widetilde{\wp}(\mathscr{R}_{X})\coloneqq|R^{+}|-|R_{S^{p}}^{+}|-\sup\left\{\sum_{D\in\Delta}m_{D}-1+\langle\rho^{\prime}(D),\vartheta\rangle\,\mid\,\vartheta\in\mathcal{Q}^{*}_{\mathscr{R}_{X}}\cap\mathrm{cone}(\Sigma)\right\},

where
R_{S^{p}}
is the sub-root system generated by
S^{p}
, and
R^{+}
(resp.
R_{S^{p}}^{+}
) is the set of positive roots of
R
(resp.
R_{S^{p}}
).

Remark 1.2
.

This definition is different to the definition of
\wp(\mathscr{R}_{X})
in
[
12
]
, and they are related in the following way:
\widetilde{\wp}(\mathscr{R}_{X})=|R^{+}|-|R_{S^{p}}^{+}|-\wp(\mathscr{R}_{X})
. Moreover either
\widetilde{\wp}(\mathscr{R}_{X})\leq|R^{+}|-|R_{S^{p}}^{+}|
is a rational number, or it is negative infinity, by
[
12
, Proposition 5.6]
.

We now state our main theorem, which proves
[
12
, Conjecture 1.4]
, and answers (
1
).

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
X
be a complete spherical variety, then

\widetilde{\wp}(\mathscr{R}_{X})\geq 0,

with equality if and only if
X
is isomorphic to a toric variety.

We now introduce the smoothness criterion which answers (
2
). It utilises the following operation on spherical skeletons.

Definition 1.4

(
[
12
, Definition 7.1]
)
.

Given a spherical skeleton
\mathscr{R}=(\Sigma,S^{p},\mathcal{D}^{a},\Gamma)
and a subset
I\subseteq\Delta
, define another spherical skeleton as follows.
Let
S_{I}\subseteq S
be the subset of simple roots whose minimal parabolic subgroups do not fix some divisor in
I
.
Let
R_{I}
be the root system generated by
S_{I}
.
Now define

\displaystyle\Sigma_{I}\coloneq\Sigma\cap\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{Z}}S_{I},

\displaystyle S_{I}^{p}\coloneq S^{p}\cap S_{I},

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{I}^{a}\coloneq\{\text{type $a$ colors with spherical roots in }\Sigma_{I}\},

\displaystyle\Gamma_{I}\coloneq\{G\text{-invariant divisors in }I\}.

The
localisation of
\mathscr{R}
at
I
is defined as the spherical skeleton
\mathscr{R}_{I}\coloneq(\Sigma_{I},S^{p}_{I},\mathcal{D}^{a}_{I},\Gamma_{I})
, equipped with the maps
\rho_{I}^{\prime}\coloneq\rho^{\prime}|_{\Lambda_{I}^{*}}
and
\varsigma_{I}\coloneq\varsigma|_{{\mathcal{D}}_{I}}
, with
\Lambda_{I}\coloneq\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\Sigma_{I})
.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
X
be a spherical variety, and
Y\subseteq X
a
G
-orbit.
Let
I\subseteq\Delta
be the set of
B
-invariant divisors
D
with
Y\subseteq\overline{D}
.
Then
X
is smooth along
Y
if and only if
\widetilde{\wp}(\mathscr{R}_{I})<1
.

The smoothness criterion,
1.5
, follows from
1.3
and
[
12
, Theorem 7.2]
. It is based on the smoothness criterion of Camus from 2001 for type
A
spherical varieties
[
8
]
, which was generalised by the second author to all types in 2014
[
14
]
, and conjecturally reformulated in terms of spherical skeletons in 2015 by the first two authors
[
12
]
.

We note other results in the direction of smoothness criteria for spherical varieties. The first smoothness criterion for spherical varieties was obtained in 1991 by Brion
[
6
]
.
A combinatorial smoothness criterion for horospherical varieties was obtained independently by Pasquier in 2006
[
20
]
and Timashev
[
22
]
in 2011. Alternatively in 2013, Batyrev and Moreau
[
1
]
proved a smoothness criterion for horospherical varieties using stringy Euler numbers, and this criterion is conjectured to extend to arbitrary spherical varieties. The smooth affine spherical varieties were classified by Knop and Van Steirteghem in 2006
[
15
]
. In 2017, the weight monoids of smooth affine spherical varieties were combinatorially classified by Pezzini and Van Steirteghem
[
21
]
.

1.2.
Technique of the proof

To each spherical variety, we may associate the quantity
\widetilde{\wp}(X)\coloneq\widetilde{\wp}(\mathscr{R}_{X})
. The starting point of this paper is the following result of the authors, which was used to prove the generalised Mukai conjecture for
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial spherical Fano varieties in
[
11
]
.

Theorem 1.6

(
[
11
, Corollary of Theorem 1.6]
)
.

Let
X
be a complete
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein spherical variety, then

\widetilde{\wp}(X)\geq 0,

with
\widetilde{\wp}(X)<1
only if
X
is isomorphic to a toric variety.

The main theorem of this paper lifts the
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein hypothesis of
1.6
, and proves
\widetilde{\wp}(X)=0
when
X
is isomorphic to a toric variety. In both of these steps we frequently use the flexibility enabled by the coarseness of spherical skeletons. Together, this proves
1.3
.

Let us now give an overview of the structure of the paper.
Firstly, in
Section

3
we introduce an algorithm which replaces a complete spherical variety
X
with a complete
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein spherical variety
X^{\prime}
such that
\widetilde{\wp}(X)=\widetilde{\wp}(X^{\prime})
. In
Section

4
we prove the correctness of this algorithm. Next, in
Section

5
we show the statement: “
\widetilde{\wp}(X)=0
if
X
is isomorphic to a toric variety”, is equivalent to
\widetilde{\wp}(V)=0
where
V
is a member of one of
42
families of multiplicity free spaces. Finally, in
Section

7
we explicitly verify this equality for each of these
42
families, completing the proof of
1.3
.

In
Section

6
we give examples illustrating the smoothness criterion
1.5
.

2.
Defining the
\widetilde{\wp}
function

In this section we briefly overview aspects of the combinatorial description of spherical varieties which are required to define
\widetilde{\wp}(\cdot)
. A general reference is
[
22
]
.

In this paper,
G
denotes a connected reductive algebraic group,
B\subseteq G
a Borel subgroup,
X
a spherical
G
-variety, and
H
the stabiliser of a point in the open
B
-orbit of
X
.

Every spherical variety
X
is a
G
-equivariant open embedding of a
G
-homogeneous space
G/H
with an open
B
-orbit, into a normal irreducible
G
-variety. Such
G/H
are called
spherical homogeneous spaces
, and such embeddings
G/H\hookrightarrow X
are called
spherical embeddings
. These form the two natural halves of the combinatorial classification of
G
-spherical varieties for a fixed
G
:

(1)

The classification of spherical homogeneous spaces
G/H
—classified by
Luna data
, also called
spherical homogeneous data
(see
[
22
, Section 30.11]
or
[
2
]
).

(2)

The classification of spherical embeddings
G/H\hookrightarrow X
of a fixed spherical homogeneous space
G/H
—classified by the
Luna-Vust theory
of
colored fans
(see
[
17
]
).

We introduce the Luna-Vust theory in this section, and in
Section

3
, we introduce the required elements of the Luna data as they are needed.

Firstly, we associate to
X
its character lattice of
B
-weights

\mathcal{M}\coloneq\{\chi\mid\text{there exists }f_{\chi}\in\mathbb{C}(X)\text{ with }b\cdot f_{\chi}(x)=\chi(b)f_{\chi}(x)\text{ for all }x\in X,\,b\in B\}\subseteq\mathfrak{X}(B).

We denote by
\operatorname{rk}X
the rank of the lattice
\mathcal{M}
.

Write
\Delta\coloneq\Delta_{X}
to denote the finite set of
B
-invariant prime divisors in
X
. As in the classification of toric varieties by fans, to each
D\in\Delta
there is a dual vector
\rho(D)\in\mathcal{N}\coloneqq\operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal{M},\mathbb{Z})
defined by
\langle\rho(D),\chi\rangle\coloneqq\nu_{D}(f_{\chi})
, where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle\colon\mathcal{N}\times\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{Z}
is the dual pairing.

The dual vectors associated to
G
-invariant prime divisors of
X
are primitive vectors in
\mathcal{N}\subset\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{Q}}\coloneq\mathcal{N}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}
which lie within a certain full-dimensional rational polyhedral cone, the
valuation cone
:

\mathcal{V}\coloneqq\operatorname{cone}\left\{\rho(D)\mid D\in\Delta_{X}\text{ is }G\text{-invariant, for a spherical embedding }G/H\hookrightarrow X\right\}\subseteq\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{Q}}.

Let
G/H\hookrightarrow X
be a spherical embedding.
There is a finite set
\mathcal{D}\subseteq\Delta
of
B
-invariant prime divisors in
X
, called
colors
, arising as the closures of
B
-invariant divisors in the spherical homogeneous space
G/H
.
A
colored cone
is a pair
(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{F})
such that
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathcal{D}
and
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{Q}}
is a strictly convex cone generated by
\rho(\mathcal{F})
and finitely many elements of
\mathcal{V}\cap\mathcal{N}
, satisfying
\rho(D)\neq 0
for all
D\in\mathcal{F}
, and
\mathcal{C}^{\circ}\cap\mathcal{V}\neq\emptyset
, where
\mathcal{C}^{\circ}
denotes the relative interior of
\mathcal{C}
.
Furthermore, a face of a colored cone
(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{F})
is a colored cone
(\mathcal{C}^{\prime},\mathcal{F}^{\prime})
such that
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
is a face of
\mathcal{C}
in the usual convex geometric sense, and
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}=\{D\in\mathcal{F}\mid\rho(D)\in\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\}
.

With this notion, one defines a
colored fan
as a finite collection
\mathbb{F}=\{(\mathcal{C}_{i},\mathcal{F}_{i})\}
of colored cones, such that each face of a colored cone in
\mathbb{F}
is also in
\mathbb{F}
, and such that each point in the valuation cone is contained in the relative interior of at most one colored cone in
\mathbb{F}
.

Then, according to the Luna-Vust theory, there is a bijection between colored fans and isomorphism classes of spherical embeddings of
G/H
.
Moreover, under this bijection, complete spherical varieties correspond to colored fans
\mathbb{F}
which cover the valuation cone, i.e.
\mathcal{V}\subseteq|\mathbb{F}|
where
|\mathbb{F}|\coloneq\bigcup_{i}\mathcal{C}_{i}\subseteq\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{Q}}
denotes the
support
of the colored fan
\mathbb{F}
.

In
[
7
, Proposition 4.1]
, Brion obtained a natural representative of the anticanonical class as a positive linear combination of the
B
-invariant divisors

-K_{X}\coloneqq\sum_{D\in\Delta}m_{D}D.

The coefficients
m_{D}\geq 1
may be computed using the Luna data (see
3.6
).

Next, we associate to
X
a polyhedron:

Q^{*}\coloneqq\bigcap_{D\in\Delta}\left\{v\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{Q}}\mid\langle\rho(D),v\rangle\geq-m_{D}\right\}\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{Q}}.

When
X
is complete,
Q^{*}
is a polytope. Moreover if
X
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein Fano, then
Q^{*}
is a translation of the moment polytope of the anticanonical divisor of
X
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
X
be a spherical variety, then

\widetilde{\wp}(X)\coloneq\dim X-\operatorname{rk}X-\sup\left\{\sum_{D\in\Delta}\left(m_{D}-1+\big\langle\rho(D),\vartheta\big\rangle\right)\,\big|\,\vartheta\in Q^{*}\cap\mathcal{T}\right\},

where
\mathcal{T}\coloneq-\mathcal{V}^{\vee}
is the negative of the dual cone of
\mathcal{V}
.

Remark 2.2
.

This definition relates to the definition of
\wp(X)
in
[
12
]
in the following way:
\widetilde{\wp}(X)=\dim X-\operatorname{rk}X-\wp(X)
.

3.
The inequality in
1.3

In this section we prove that the
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein assumption of
1.6
may be lifted.
This is achieved by reducing the complete case to the complete
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein setting of
1.6
by operating on the spherical combinatorial data.

In particular, given the colored fan of a complete spherical variety
X
, in
Algorithm

3.13
we construct the colored fan of a complete
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein spherical variety
X^{\prime}
in a way such that
\widetilde{\wp}(X)=\widetilde{\wp}(X^{\prime})
is preserved, and such that a spherical variety isomorphic to a toric variety is sent to another spherical variety isomorphic to a toric variety.

Throughout, we describe spherical homogeneous spaces combinatorially using their
Luna data
(also called
spherical homogeneous data
); for details we refer to
[
22
, Section 30.11]
or
[
2
]
.

To begin, we make some observations about the dual vectors
\rho(D)\in\mathcal{N}
associated to the colors
D\in\mathcal{D}
of a spherical embedding.

Let
X
be a
G
-spherical variety.
Denote the root system of
G
with respect to a fixed maximal torus
T\subseteq G
by
R
, and let
S\subseteq R
be the choice of simple roots corresponding to our choice of Borel subgroup
B\subseteq G
.
We say that a color
D\in\mathcal{D}
is
moved
by
\alpha\in S
if the minimal parabolic subgroup
P_{\alpha}
, that contains
B
and is associated to
\alpha
, satisfies
P_{\alpha}\cdot D\neq D
.
Denote by
S^{p}
the set of simple roots which move no colors of
X
.
Recall that the set of colors of a spherical variety is split into three disjoint cases:

\text{a color }D\in\mathcal{D}\text{ is said to be of type}\begin{cases}a&\text{if $D$ is moved by some }\alpha\in S\cap\Sigma,\\
2a&\text{if $D$ is moved by some }\alpha\in S\cap\frac{1}{2}\Sigma,\\
b&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Remark 3.1
.

We list some properties of colors which are required below and follow directly from the axioms of Luna data (see, for instance,
[
22
, Definition 30.21]
):

•

Colors of type
a
come in pairs, that is, for each
\alpha\in S\cap\Sigma
there are two colors
D_{\alpha}^{+}
and
D_{\alpha}^{-}
that are moved by
\alpha
such that
\rho(D_{\alpha}^{+})+\rho(D_{\alpha}^{-})=\alpha^{\vee}|_{\mathcal{M}}
.

•

We have
\langle\rho(D_{\alpha}^{\pm}),\gamma\rangle\leq 1
for all
\gamma\in\Sigma
, with equality if and only if
\gamma=\alpha
.

•

Each spherical root is a non-negative linear combination of simple roots.
We write
\operatorname{supp}{\gamma}\subseteq S
for the set of simple roots that appear in
\gamma
.

Definition 3.2
.

Call a pair of colors
D_{1},D_{2}

codirectional
if
\rho(D_{1})=k\rho(D_{2})
for some rational number
k>0
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Suppose that
D_{1},D_{2}\in\mathcal{D}
are codirectional.
Then either:
D_{1},D_{2}
are a pair of colors of type
a
with
\rho(D_{1})=\rho(D_{2})
, or
D_{1},D_{2}
are colors of type
b
.

3.3
is a corollary of the following two propositions.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
D
be a color of type
a
or
2a
and let
D^{\prime}\neq D
be a color that is not of type
a
.
Then
D
and
D^{\prime}
are not codirectional.

Proof.

Let
\gamma=\lambda\alpha\in\Sigma
(
\lambda\in\{1,2\}
) be the spherical root associated to
D
.
Notice that
\langle\rho(D),\gamma\rangle>0
.
Assume for a contradiction that
D^{\prime}\neq D
is a color not of type
a
which is codirectional with
D
, so
\rho(D^{\prime})=\ell\beta^{\vee}|_{\mathcal{M}}
for some
\ell>0
and
\alpha\neq\beta\in S
, and
\rho(D)=k\rho(D^{\prime})
for
k>0
.
Since
\beta\notin\operatorname{supp}{\gamma}=\{\alpha\}
, it follows that
0<\langle\rho(D),\gamma\rangle=\langle k\rho(D^{\prime}),\gamma\rangle=k\ell\langle\beta^{\vee},\gamma\rangle\leq 0
, so no such
k
exists.
∎

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
D_{1},D_{2}
be two codirectional colors of type
a
.
Then
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
are moved by the same simple root
\alpha\in S\cap\Sigma
and
\rho(D_{1})=\rho(D_{2})
.

Proof.

Suppose that
D_{1}
is moved by
\alpha\in S\cap\Sigma
,
D_{2}
is moved by
\beta\in S\cap\Sigma
, and
\rho(D_{2})=k\rho(D_{1})
for some
k>0
.
Let
D_{\alpha}^{+},D_{\alpha}^{-}
(resp.
D_{\beta}^{+},D_{\beta}^{-}
) be the pair of colors of type
a
that are moved by
\alpha
(resp.
\beta
).
Then
D_{1}\in\{D_{\alpha}^{-},D_{\alpha}^{+}\}
and
D_{2}\in\{D_{\beta}^{-},D_{\beta}^{+}\}
.
Without loss of generality assume
D_{2}=D_{\beta}^{+}
.
Then, since
\langle\rho(D_{1}),\alpha\rangle=1
, we have
\langle\rho(D_{\beta}^{+}),\alpha\rangle=k
.
The colors
D_{\beta}^{\pm}
satisfy
\langle\rho(D_{\beta}^{+}),\alpha\rangle+\langle\rho(D_{\beta}^{-}),\alpha\rangle=\langle\beta^{\vee},\alpha\rangle
, so
k=\langle\beta^{\vee},\alpha\rangle-\langle\rho(D_{\beta}^{-}),\alpha\rangle\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Then as
0<k=\langle\rho(D_{\beta}^{+}),\alpha\rangle\leq 1
, the only possibility is
k=1
.
In particular, we get
\alpha=\beta
.
∎

Proof of
3.3
.

If
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
are not both of type
b
, then the unordered tuple of types of
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
, denoted
(t_{1},t_{2})
, are one of the following cases:

(t_{1},t_{2})\in\{(a,a),(a,2a),(a,b),(2a,2a),(2a,b)\}.

By
3.4
,
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
are not codirectional in each of these cases, except when
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
are both of type
a
, in which case
3.5
shows that
\rho(D_{1})=\rho(D_{2})
.
This completes the proof of
3.3
.
∎

Remark 3.6
.

We list some properties of the coefficients
m_{D}
in the standard spherical anticanonical divisor of a spherical variety
X\hookleftarrow G/H
.
By
[
7
, Theorem 4.2]
(see also
[
19
, §3.6]
or
[
13
, Theorem 1.5]
), the anticanonical sheaf of
G/H
is associated to the
B
-weight
\kappa=2(\rho_{S}-\rho_{S^{p}})
, where for
I\subseteq S
we denote by
\rho_{I}
the half sum of positive roots in the root system generated by
I
.
For each color
D\in\mathcal{D}
which is moved by a simple root
\alpha\in S
, the coefficients
m_{D}
are:

\displaystyle m_{D}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\langle\alpha^{\vee},\kappa\rangle=1

\displaystyle\text{for $D$ of type $a$ or $2a$},

\displaystyle m_{D}

\displaystyle=\langle\alpha^{\vee},\kappa\rangle\geq 2

\displaystyle\text{for $D$ of type $b$}.

Proposition 3.7
.

If
\ell\kappa\in\mathcal{M}
for some
\ell>0
, then for every pair of colors
D_{1},D_{2}
, either
\rho(D_{1})
and
\rho(D_{2})
span different rays or
\rho(D_{1})/m_{D_{1}}=\rho(D_{2})/m_{D_{2}}
.

Proof.

By
3.3
, there are two cases in which
\rho(D_{1})
and
\rho(D_{2})
span the same ray.
If these colors are both of type a, then
m_{D_{1}}=m_{D_{2}}=1
, and thus, by
3.3
,
\rho(D_{1})=\rho(D_{2})
.
If both these colors are of type b, and
\rho(D_{1})=k\rho(D_{2})
for
k>0
, then
\ell m_{D_{1}}=\langle\rho(D_{1}),\ell\kappa\rangle=\langle k\rho(D_{2}),\ell\kappa\rangle=k\ell m_{D_{2}}
.
In particular,
k=m_{D_{1}}/m_{D_{2}}
, from which the statement follows.
∎

Proposition 3.8
.

Given a Luna datum with
\ell^{\prime}\kappa\not\in\mathcal{M}
for all
\ell^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, define
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}\coloneq\mathcal{M}\oplus\mathbb{Z}\kappa\subseteq\mathfrak{X}(B)
.
Applying both the following modifications yields a genuine Luna datum

•

replace

(\mathcal{C}^{\prime},\mathcal{F}^{\prime})
does not contain
\rho(D)
.
Hence, it suffices to consider colored fans
\mathbb{F}_{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}
associated to a colored cone
(\mathcal{C}^{\prime},\mathcal{F}^{\prime})
with
\rho(D)\in\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
.

In this case, it follows from the corresponding definition of star subdivision in toric geometry that
\{\mathcal{C}\mid(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{F})\in\mathbb{F}_{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}^{a}(D)\}
is a fan (in the toric sense) subdividing the cone
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
.
In particular, each
\nu\in\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
is contained in the relative interior of exactly one cone
(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{F})\in\mathbb{F}_{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}^{a}(D)
.
Removing faces in
\mathbb{F}_{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}^{a}(D)
preserves this property.

From this observation it is straightforward to verify that each
\nu\in\mathcal{V}
is contained in the relative interior of at most one colored cone of
\mathbb{F}^{*}(D)
.
Indeed, suppose we have
(\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{F}_{1}),(\mathcal{C}_{2},\mathcal{F}_{2})\in\mathbb{F}^{*}(D)
such that
\nu\in\mathcal{C}_{1}^{\circ}\cap\mathcal{C}_{2}^{\circ}
.
Since,
\mathbb{F}
is a colored fan and
\nu\in\mathcal{C}_{1}^{\circ}\subseteq|\mathbb{F}|
, it follows that there exists exactly one colored cone
(\mathcal{C}^{\prime},\mathcal{F}^{\prime})\in\mathbb{F}
which contains
\nu
in its relative interior.
From the definition of star subdivision, there exists
(\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{i},\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{i})\in\mathbb{F}^{a}
such that
\mathcal{C}_{i}\subseteq\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{i}
.
Since
\nu\in{(\mathcal{C}^{\prime})}^{\circ}
and
\mathbb{F}^{a}
is a fan in the toric sense when restricted to
\mathcal{V}
, it follows that
\mathcal{C^{\prime}}\preceq\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{i}
, i.e.
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
is a colored face of
\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{i}
.
Clearly,
\mathbb{F}_{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}^{a}(D)
is a subfan of
\mathbb{F}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{i}}^{a}(D)
.
Since
\nu\not\in|\mathbb{F}^{a}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{i}}(D)|\setminus|\mathbb{F}^{a}_{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}(D)|=\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{i}\setminus\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
, it follows that
\nu\in|\mathbb{F}_{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}^{a}(D)|
, and thus
\mathcal{C}_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}^{a}(D)
as it is the unique cone in
\mathbb{F}^{a}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{i}}(D)
which contains
\nu
in its relative interior.
Similarly, one shows that
\mathcal{C}_{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}^{a}(D)
.
The statement follows from the observation that
\mathbb{F}^{a}_{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}(D)
is a fan in the toric sense, and thus
\mathcal{C}_{1}=\mathcal{C}_{2}
.

Finally, notice that if
\nu\in\mathcal{V}\cap|\mathbb{F}|
then there exists exactly one cone
(\mathcal{C}^{\prime},\mathcal{F}^{\prime})\in\mathbb{F}
such that
\nu\in{(\mathcal{C}^{\prime})}^{\circ}
.
Then in the star subdivision
\mathbb{F}^{a}_{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}(D)
of the colored fan
\mathbb{F}^{a}_{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}
obtained from the colored cone
(\mathcal{C}^{\prime},\mathcal{F}^{\prime})
, there is exactly one
(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{F})
that contains
\nu
in its relative interior.
Clearly,
(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{F})\in\mathbb{F}^{a}(D)
.

However, then clearly
\mathcal{C}^{\circ}\cap\mathcal{V}\neq\emptyset
, and thus
(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{F})\in\mathbb{F}^{*}(D)
.
This proves that
|\mathbb{F}|\cap\mathcal{V}\subseteq|\mathbb{F}^{*}(D)|\cap\mathcal{V}
.
Conversely, by
4.1
,

|\mathbb{F}^{*}(D)|\cap\mathcal{V}\subseteq|\mathbb{F}^{a}|\cap\mathcal{V}=|\mathbb{F}|\cap\mathcal{V}.\qed

Proposition 4.6

To prove that
\mathbb{F}_{3}
is a colored fan, we use
vector configurations
, for which we briefly recall the necessary background.
We use
[
10
]
as a general reference of this well-known topic.

Definition 4.7

(
[
10
, Section 2.5]
)
.

A
vector configuration
is a multi-set of vectors:

\mathbf{A}\coloneq(v_{i}\,|\,i\in S)\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}.

For each linear functional
\psi\in{(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{*}
with
\psi(\mathbf{A})\geq 0
, the
face of
\mathbf{A}
in direction
\psi
is the maximal subconfiguration
S\subseteq\mathbf{A}
on which
\psi(S)=0
.
Write
F\leq S
if
F
is a face of
S
.

A
polyhedral subdivision
of a vector configuration
\mathbf{A}
is a collection of subconfigurations
\mathscr{S}
, called
cells
, satisfying the following properties:

(1)

For all
C\in\mathscr{S}
, if
F\leq C
then
F\in\mathscr{S}
.

(2)

\bigcup_{C\in\mathscr{S}}\operatorname{cone}(C)\supseteq\operatorname{cone}A
.

(3)

\mathcal{C}^{\circ}\cap\mathcal{C}^{\prime\circ}\neq\emptyset
for
\mathcal{C},\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\in\mathscr{S}
implies that
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
.

A
lift
of a vector configuration
\mathbf{A}
is a function
\omega\colon\mathbf{A}\to\mathbb{R}
, which associates a
height
to each vector in
\mathbf{A}
.
A lift gives rise to a
lifted
vector configuration
\mathbf{A}^{\omega}\coloneq\{(v_{i},\omega(v_{i}))\mid i\in S\}
.

Given a lift
\omega\colon\mathbf{A}\to\mathbb{R}
, the
lower faces

\mathbf{A}^{l}
of
\mathbf{A}^{\omega}
are the faces of
\mathbf{A}^{\omega}
in the direction of a linear functional
\psi
which is positive on the last coordinate.
Similarly, the
upper faces

\mathbf{A}^{u}
of
\mathbf{A}^{\omega}
are the faces of
\mathbf{A}^{\omega}
in the direction of a linear functional
\psi
which is negative on the last coordinate.

Remark 4.8
.

By using
\sigma^{\vee\vee}=\sigma
for each polyhedral cone
\sigma\subseteq\mathcal{N}^{\prime}_{\mathbb{Q}}
, it is straightfoward to show that
F\leq\mathbf{A}^{\omega}
is a lower face (resp. upper face) if and only if
x-\lambda v_{\rho}\not\in\operatorname{cone}(\mathbf{A}^{\omega})
(resp.
x+\lambda v_{\rho}\not\in\operatorname{cone}(\mathbf{A}^{\omega})
) for each
x\in\operatorname{cone}(F)
and
\lambda>0
.
Here,
v_{\rho}
denotes a ray generator of the ray
\rho
.

We require the following result:

Lemma 4.9

(
[
10
, Lemma 2.5.11]
)
.

Let
\omega\colon\mathbf{A}\to\mathbb{R}
be a lift of a vector configuration
\mathbf{A}
.
If there exists a linear function
\ell\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
such that
(\omega+\ell)(\mathbf{A})\geq 0
, then the lower faces
\mathbf{A}^{l}
of
\mathbf{A}^{\omega}
give rise to a polyhedral subdivision of
\mathbf{A}
, whose cells are
\pi(C)
for
C
a cell of
\mathbf{A}^{l}
.
Here,
\pi
is the projection which forgets the last coordinate.

The relation of vector configurations to our setting is that colored cones can naturally be interpretated as vector configurations, where in general, it is necessary to consider multisets.

We associate the following vector configuration to a colored cone

\mathbf{A}_{(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{F})}\coloneq\{v_{1},\dots,v_{r_{\mathcal{C}}}\}\cup(\rho(D)\mid D\in\mathcal{F}),

In order to define

Proposition 4.17

\mathbb{F}_{3}
is a complete colored fan.

Proof.

We show that

Let

So

∎

We are now prepared to complete the proof of

Proof of
4.4
.

Algorithm

3.13

Since

5.
Toric varieties and
\widetilde{\wp}

Let us continue to use the notation and assumptions from above.
In particular,
X
denotes a complete
G
-spherical variety.
In this section we prove that
\widetilde{\wp}(X)<1
if and only if

is isomorphic to a toric variety, if and only if
\widetilde{\wp}(X)=0
, which completes the proof of

.
Indeed, we show

\widetilde{\wp}(X)<1\Rightarrow X\text{ isomorphic to a toric variety}\Rightarrow\widetilde{\wp}(X)=0\Rightarrow\widetilde{\wp}(X)<1.

We use a technique from
[
12
]
, where it was observed that
\widetilde{\wp}(X)
only depends on the spherical skeleton, as introduced in
Section

1
.

Remark 5.1
.

There is a natural notion of isomorphism of spherical skeletons (see
[
12
, Section 5]
), and by design
\widetilde{\wp}(X)=\widetilde{\wp}(\mathscr{R}_{X})
only depends on
\mathscr{R}_{X}
up to isomorphism.
For completeness and to ensure that this work is self-contained, we briefly recall when two spherical skeletons
\mathscr{R}_{1}=(\Sigma_{1},S_{1}^{p},\mathcal{D}_{1}^{a},\Gamma_{1},\varsigma_{1},\rho^{\prime}_{1})
and
\mathscr{R}_{2}=(\Sigma_{2},S_{2}^{p},\mathcal{D}_{2}^{a},\Gamma_{2},\varsigma_{2},\rho^{\prime}_{2})
are considered to be
isomorphic
, written
\mathscr{R}_{1}\cong\mathscr{R}_{2}
.
Suppose the underlying root system of
\mathscr{R}_{1}
(resp.
\mathscr{R}_{2}
) is
R_{1}
(resp.
R_{2}
).
We say
\mathscr{R}_{1}
and
\mathscr{R}_{2}
are isomorphic if there exists an isomorphism of root systems
\varphi_{R}\colon R_{1}\to R_{2}
and two bijections
\varphi_{\mathcal{D}}\colon\mathcal{D}_{1}^{a}\to\mathcal{D}_{2}^{a}
and
\varphi_{\Gamma}\colon\Gamma_{1}\to\Gamma_{2}
such that
\varphi_{R}(\Sigma_{1})=\Sigma_{2}
,
\varphi_{R}(S_{1}^{p})=S_{2}^{p}
, and
\rho^{\prime}_{1}(D)=\rho^{\prime}_{2}(\varphi_{\mathcal{D}}(D))\circ\varphi_{R}
on
\Sigma_{1}
for every
D\in\mathcal{D}_{1}^{a}
as well as
\rho^{\prime}_{1}(D)=\rho^{\prime}_{2}(\varphi_{\Gamma}(D))\circ\varphi_{R}
on
\Sigma_{1}
for every
D\in\Gamma_{1}
.

Remark 5.2
.

Remark that
\widetilde{\wp}(\mathscr{R})
only depends on the representative of spherical skeleton up to the following equivalence relation, introduced in
[
12
, Theorem 6.7]
.
For
\mathscr{R}=(\Sigma,S^{p},\mathcal{D}^{a},\Gamma)
, define
[\Gamma]\coloneq\{D\in\Gamma\mid\rho^{\prime}(D)\neq 0\in\Lambda^{*}\}
, and
[\mathscr{R}]\coloneq(\Sigma,S^{p},\mathcal{D}^{a},[\Gamma])
.
The aforementioned equivalence relation is then
\mathscr{R}_{1}\sim\mathscr{R}_{2}
if and only if
[\mathscr{R}_{1}]\cong[\mathscr{R}_{2}]
.

The following theorem is a key result that is required in the proof of
1.3
.

Theorem 5.3

(
[
12
, Theorem 6.7]
)
.

A complete
G
-spherical variety
X
is isomorphic to a toric variety if and only if
\mathscr{R}_{X}\cong\mathscr{R}_{V}
for
V
a
G
-multiplicity free space
; i.e. a rational
G
-representation with the structure of a
G
-spherical variety (see also
5.7
).

Remark 5.4
.

With
5.2
and
5.3
, we have the following criterion which detects a toric variety.
Let
X
be a spherical variety that is isomorphic to a toric variety.
Then, by
5.3
,
\mathscr{R}_{X}\cong\mathscr{R}_{V}
for
V
a multiplicity free space.
By
[

, Remark 6.10]
, under the equivalence relation in

, if for some spherical variety

,
\mathscr{R}_{Y}\sim\mathscr{R}_{X}
, then
\mathscr{R}_{Y}
is also the spherical skeleton of a multiplicity free space.

By
5.3
, to prove the equality
\widetilde{\wp}(X)=0
when
X
is isomorphic to a toric variety, we may prove the corresponding equality for multiplicity free spaces:

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
V
be a multiplicity free space, then
\widetilde{\wp}(V)=0
.

Remark 5.6
.

Simultaneously and independently to the authors of this paper, in
[
3
]
, Bravi and Pezzini established that
\widetilde{\wp}(X)\geq 0
in the special case that
X
has a reductive general isotropy group.
Their approach is a case by case verification of the conjecture on the corresponding spherical skeletons, as done for the symmetric varieties in
[
12
]
.
In their work, they show the equality
\widetilde{\wp}(V)=0
for
V
a multiplicity free space (
5.5
in this paper) by an abstract argument on the spherical skeleton and weight monoid of a multiplicity free space.
Their approach to prove
5.5
is distinct from the one presented here.

By the following
5.12
, it suffices to show this equality for a simpler class of multiplicity free spaces, namely the
indecomposable saturated multiplicity free spaces up to geometric equivalence
.
This class of multiplicity free spaces admits an explicit classification in terms of the associated spherical combinatorial data (see
[
16
]
and
[
14
]
).
Before proving
5.12
, we make some preliminary observations.

Definition 5.7
.

Recall that a
multiplicity free space
is a rational representation
\varrho\colon G\to\operatorname{GL}(V)
of
G
into a finite-dimensional vector space
V
which is also a spherical
G
-variety with respect to the induced linear
G
-action.
Let
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
be two connected reductive groups.
Then two multiplicity free spaces
\varrho_{1}\colon G_{1}\to\operatorname{GL}(V_{1})
and
\varrho_{2}\colon G_{2}\to\operatorname{GL}(V_{2})
are said to be
geometrically equivalent
if there is an isomorphism
\varphi\colon V_{1}\to V_{2}
of vector spaces, inducing
\operatorname{GL}(\varphi)\colon\operatorname{GL}(V_{1})\to\operatorname{GL}(V_{2})
such that
\operatorname{GL}(\varphi)(\varrho_{1}(G_{1}))=\varrho_{2}(G_{2})
.

The multiplicity free space
V
is said to be
decomposable
if it is geometrically equivalent to
\varrho^{\prime}\colon G_{1}\times G_{2}\to\mathrm{GL}(V_{1}\oplus V_{2})
, where
G_{i}
acts on
V_{i}
.

Suppose the multiplicity free space
V=V_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{s}
has
s
irreducible summands
V_{i}
.
Then
V
is called
saturated
if the dimension of the central torus of
\varrho(G)
is equal to
s
.

Remark 5.8
.

Notice that by Schur’s lemma, a multiplicity free space
V=V_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{s}
is saturated if and only if
(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{s}
is contained in the centre of
\varrho(G)
and the
i
-th
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-factor acts via scalar multiplication on
V_{i}
.
In particular, the two definitions
[
16
, Definition in Section 5]
and
[
18
, Definition 2.3]
are equivalent.

Furthermore, observe that from the definition of being saturated it is clear that a non-saturated multiplicity free space can be made saturated by enlarging the central torus of
G
(ensuring that
G
maps onto the central torus of
\mathrm{GL}(V)
).

A technical subtlety when working with multiplicity free spaces
\varrho\colon G\to\mathrm{GL}(V)
is that they naturally come equipped with two actions by connected reductive groups, namely the one by
G
and one by
\varrho(G)\subseteq\mathrm{GL}(V)
.
It is an interesting question to study how the spherical skeletons of
V
considered as spherical
G
- and
\varrho(G)
-variety are related.
In general their spherical skeletons need not be isomorphic in the sense above (even when considering them up to equivalence) as their “ambient root systems” might not be comparable.
However, there is a natural notion of “minimal ambient root system” for a spherical skeleton which would allow us to consider the two spherical skeletons of
V
as isomorphic.
This study is rather technical and, as we only need to compare the
\widetilde{\wp}
functions of these two “spherical actions”, the following simpler statement suffices for our purposes.

Lemma 5.9
.

Let
X_{G/H}\hookleftarrow G/H
be a
G
-spherical variety.
Suppose
K\trianglelefteq G
is a closed normal subgroup with
K\subseteq H
, then for
\mathfrak{G}\coloneq G/K
and
\mathfrak{H}\coloneq H/K
, we have that
X\hookleftarrow\mathfrak{G}/\mathfrak{H}
is a
\mathfrak{G}
-spherical variety with
\widetilde{\wp}(X_{G/H})=\widetilde{\wp}(X_{\mathfrak{G}/\mathfrak{H}})
.

Proof.

Recall the definition of
\widetilde{\wp}(X)
:

\widetilde{\wp}(X)=\dim X-\operatorname{rk}X-\sup\left\{\sum_{D\in\Delta}\left(m_{D}-1+\big\langle\rho(D),\vartheta\big\rangle\right)\,\big|\,\vartheta\in Q^{*}\cap\mathcal{T}\right\},

where
Q^{*}
is uniquely determined by the valuations induced by the
B
-invariant divisors, and the coefficients of those
B
-invariant divisors in the spherical anticanonical divisor.
Therefore, in order to show
\widetilde{\wp}(X_{G/H})=\widetilde{\wp}(X_{\mathfrak{G}/\mathfrak{H}})
, it suffices to verify that the lattices of weights of semi-invariant rational functions with respect to a Borel subgroup can be identified in such a way that the valuation cones coincide, and the spherical anticanonical divisors are equal.
In other words, we need to show that the Luna-Vust theory of
G/H
and
\mathfrak{G}/\mathfrak{H}
coincide, as well as
-K_{G/H}=-K_{\mathfrak{G}/\mathfrak{H}}
.

Let
\pi\colon G\to\mathfrak{G}=G/K
be the natural quotient, yielding an isomorphism
\varphi\colon G/H\to\mathfrak{G}/\mathfrak{H}
.
Fix a maximal torus and Borel subgroup
T\subseteq B\subseteq G
, then
\pi(T)\subseteq\mathfrak{B}\coloneqq\pi(B)\subseteq\mathfrak{G}
are the corresponding maximal torus and Borel subgroup of
\mathfrak{G}
.
Let
\mathcal{M}\coloneq{\mathbb{C}(G/H)}^{(B)}/\mathbb{C}^{*}
be the character lattice of
G/H
, and
\mathfrak{M}\coloneq{\mathbb{C}(\mathfrak{G/\mathfrak{H}})}^{(\mathfrak{B})}/\mathbb{C}^{*}
be the character lattice of
\mathfrak{G}/\mathfrak{H}
.
Then
\varphi
induces an isomorphism of character lattices
\varphi^{*}\colon\mathfrak{M}\to\mathcal{M}
, and of dual lattices
\varphi_{*}\colon\mathcal{N}\to\mathfrak{N}
.
Under this isomorphism, the valuation cones of
G/H
and
\mathfrak{G}/\mathfrak{H}
coincide, i.e.
\varphi_{*}(\mathcal{V})=\mathfrak{V}
(use the well-known identification of the valuation cone with
G
-invariant (resp.
\mathfrak{G}
-invariant) valuations on
\mathbb{C}(G/H)
(resp.
\mathbb{C}(\mathfrak{G}/\mathfrak{H})
)).
Finally,
\varphi
induces a bijection between the
B
-orbits of
G/H
and the
\mathfrak{B}
-orbits of
\mathfrak{G}/\mathfrak{H}
such that for every
B
-orbit
Y
of
G/H
, the corresponding
\mathfrak{B}
-orbit is
\varphi(Y)
and
\varphi\colon Y\to\varphi(Y)
is a
B
-equivariant isomorphism.
Therefore, the Luna-Vust theories of
G/H
and
\mathfrak{G}/\mathfrak{H}
coincide.
In particular, their spherical embeddings can be naturally identified.

Next we show that
-K_{G/H}=-K_{\mathfrak{G}/\mathfrak{H}}
.
Let
U\subseteq G/H
be the open
B
-orbit and let
s\in\Gamma(U,\hat{\omega}_{G/H})
be a generator such that
\operatorname{div}(s)=-K_{G/H}
.
Notice that
s
is uniquely determined up to a constant multiple.
Above, we have seen that
\varphi
induces an isomorphism between the open
B
-orbit
U
and the open
\mathfrak{B}
-orbit
\varphi(U)\subseteq\mathfrak{G}/\mathfrak{H}
.
Under this isomorphism
\hat{\omega}_{G/H}
gets identified with
\hat{\omega}_{\mathfrak{G}/\mathfrak{H}}
and the generator
s
corresponds to a generator
\mathfrak{s}\in\Gamma(\varphi(U),\hat{\omega}_{\mathfrak{G}/\mathfrak{H}})
.
By
[
13
, Theorem 1.4]
, the generator
s
is uniquely determined by the property that it vanishes with order one along each
G
-invariant divisor in every spherical embedding
G/H\hookrightarrow X
.
As the Luna-Vust data of
G/H
and
\mathfrak{G}/\mathfrak{H}
coincide, the generator
\mathfrak{s}
has the same property, and thus
-K_{\mathfrak{G}/\mathfrak{H}}=\operatorname{div}(\mathfrak{s})=\operatorname{div}(s)=-K_{G/H}
.
∎

.

Let
\varrho\colon G\to\mathrm{GL}(V)

Then the
\widetilde{\wp}
-functions of

- spherical variety coincide, i.e.
\widetilde{\wp}(V_{G})=\widetilde{\wp}(V_{\varrho(G)})

Proof.

This straightforwardly follows from

G
-spherical variety

and

.
∎

Corollary 5.11
.

Let
\varrho_{1}\colon G_{1}\to\operatorname{GL}(V_{1})
and
\varrho_{2}\colon G_{2}\to\operatorname{GL}(V_{2})
be geometrically equivalent multiplicity free spaces.
Then
\widetilde{\wp}(V_{1})=\widetilde{\wp}(V_{2})
.

Proof.

By
5.10
, we may consider
V_{i}
as
\varrho(G_{i})
-spherical variety.
Notice the assumption that

and

are geometrically equivalent implies there is an isomorphism
\varrho(G_{1})\cong\varrho(G_{2})
of algebraic groups and a linear isomorphism

which is equivariant with respect to the identification

∎

Proposition 5.12
.

Suppose that for each indecomposable saturated multiplicity free space
V^{\prime}
, we have
\widetilde{\wp}(V^{\prime})=0
.
Then
\widetilde{\wp}(V)=0
for every multiplicity free space

.

Proof.

Let

be a
G
-multiplicity free space with
s
irreducible summands
V_{i}
.
By Schur’s lemma, the dimension of the central torus of
\varrho(G)
is not greater than

.
Then by replacing

with

, for a suitable

, we may obtain a saturated

-multiplicity free space

(the same linear space

with an “enlarged action”).
It is clear that adding torus factors preserves the spherical skeleton, i.e.

, so in particular

.
So we may assume that

is saturated.

Now let us suppose that
V
is decomposable.
Then by
5.11
, we may assume that
G=G_{1}\times G_{2}
and

, with

acting on

.
Notice that from a spherical geometry point of view

is a product of two spherical varieties

and

.
Therefore, the Luna datum of

and

(see, for instance,
[
4

.
It is now straightforward to verify that

So by

, showing

for every saturated multiplicity free space up to geometric equivalence, implies that
\widetilde{\wp}(V)=0
.
∎

Proof of
5.5
.

In

By

\widetilde{\wp}(X)<1\Rightarrow X\text{ isomorphic to a toric variety}\Rightarrow\widetilde{\wp}(X)=0\Rightarrow\widetilde{\wp}(X)<1.

to

-Gorenstein spherical variety

6.

In this section we illustrate

We exhibit an example of a singular locally factorial simple embedding. This is a non-toric phenomenon, as for toric varieties smoothness coincides with local factoriality.

Example 6.1
.

Consider the variety of smooth conics
G/H=\mathrm{SL}_{3}/Z(\mathrm{SL}_{3})\mathrm{SO}_{3}
(cf.
[

, Table A]

We have
\mathcal{M}=\mathbb{Z}(2\alpha_{1})\oplus\mathbb{Z}(2\alpha_{2})
, two colors

,

of type 2a with spherical roots
\Sigma=\{2\alpha_{1},2\alpha_{2}\}
, and dual vectors
\rho(D_{1})=(2,-1)
,
\rho(D_{2})=(-1,2)\in\mathcal{N}
.
The simple embedding
X
with colored cone
(\mathrm{cone}((-1,0),(2,-1)),\{D_{1}\})
is the parameter space
\mathbb{P}^{5}
of all conics, with one spherical boundary divisor

, and
\Delta=\{X_{1},D_{1},D_{2}\}
.
The polytope

is the triangle
\mathrm{conv}((-1,-1),(1,0),(1,3))

\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{Q}}
.
As a demonstration, we apply

to the unique closed
G

The spherical skeleton of

\mathscr{R}=(\Sigma=\{2\alpha_{1},2\alpha_{2}\},S^{p}=\emptyset,\mathcal{D}^{a}=\emptyset,\Gamma=\{X_{1}\}).

The

are

at

Then

\mathcal{Q}^{*}_{\mathscr{R}_{I}}\cap\operatorname{cone}(\Sigma_{I})=[0,1]\subset{(\Lambda_{I})}_{\mathbb{Q}}

Example 6.2

(A singular locally factorial variety)
.

Let
X
be a simple spherical embedding with colored cone
(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{F})
, with no colors taken

, and

a smooth cone.
Then by
1.5
,
X
is smooth.
However, when

,

can be singular.

For example, consider the spherical homogeneous space

\mathrm{SL}_{2}
, acting with character

on
\mathbb{C}^{*}
, and

\mathrm{SL}_{2}

7.
\widetilde{\wp}
for the indecomposable saturated multiplicity free spaces

In this section, we show that
\widetilde{\wp}(V)=0
for
V
an indecomposable saturated multiplicity free space up to geometric equivalence, using Knop’s exhaustive list
[
16
, Section 5]
of these spherical varieties.
In each case, we find an explicit value
\vartheta\in Q^{*}\cap\mathcal{T}
which evaluates in the linear program
\widetilde{\wp}(V)
to zero.
Then the following
7.1
implies that
\widetilde{\wp}(V)=0
.

Proposition 7.1
.

Let
V
be a multiplicity free space, then
\widetilde{\wp}(V)\geq 0
.

Proof.

By
[
12
, Lemma 7.3]
, the spherical skeleton of a multiplicity free space is isomorphic to the spherical skeleton of a complete spherical variety.
The inequality now follows from
3.14

By
7.1
, this explicit value
\vartheta_{\mathrm{argmax}}\coloneq\vartheta
solves the linear program
\wp(V)=\dim V-\operatorname{rk}V-\widetilde{\wp}(V)
and establishes the equality
\widetilde{\wp}(V)=0
.
In the following table, the value of
\vartheta_{\mathrm{argmax}}
is written in terms of the spherical roots for each of the multiplicity free spaces in Knop’s list.
In each case, we denote by
\lambda_{i}
, the
i
th spherical root following the enumeration in
[
16
]
.
In
[
14
]
, Gagliardi has computed the Luna diagram of each of these multiplicity free spaces, and throughout, we follow the numbering of cases used in that paper.

Let
V
be a
G
-multiplicity free space, then to compute
\vartheta_{\mathrm{argmax}}
we use the following.
Since
V
is an affine spherical variety, it is a simple spherical embedding with colored cone
(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{F})
by
[
17
, Theorem 6.7]
.
Since the origin is a
G
-fixed point of
V
, by the orbit-cone correspondence
[
17
, Theorem 6.6]
,
(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{F})
is a colored cone of maximal dimension.
Moreover, as
V
is factorial, the primitive ray generators of rays
\{\rho(D)\,|\,D\in\Delta\}
of its colored cone form a
\mathbb{Z}
-basis of
\mathcal{N}
.
Furthermore, the
B
-weights
\chi
of each
B
-invariant principal divisor
D=\operatorname{div}(f_{\chi})
form the corresponding dual basis
\{\chi_{D}\,|\,D\in\Delta\}
of
\mathcal{M}
.
In this basis, the objective function of the linear program
\wp(V)
is the sum of the components of a vector, and
Q^{*}=(-m_{D}\,|\,D\in\Delta)+{(\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{Q}})}_{\geq 0}
is a translation of the positive orthant.
Here,
(-m_{D}\mid D\in\Delta)
denotes the point in
\mathcal{M}
which has “
D
-th” coordinate equal to
-m_{D}
.
Writing each spherical root
\lambda_{i}
in terms of the
\chi_{D}
, in each case we find a value
\vartheta_{\mathrm{argmax}}=\sum_{i}a_{i}\lambda_{i}
such that the coefficients
a_{i}
satisfy a linear recurrence.

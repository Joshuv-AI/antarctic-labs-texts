Cutting-edge optimized multi-source data fusion for trusted execution and management of blockchain transactions on the internet of medical things (IoMT) with machine learning
Authors: Abdullah Ayub Khan, Abdulmajeed Alsufyani, Nawal Alsufyani, Mohamad Afendee Mohamed, Sajid Ullah
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractA modernized healthcare environment is possibly fuelled by the collaborative nature of Blockchain Technology (BT) and the Internet of Medical Things (IoMT), which guarantees efficient management, scalability, ledger optimization, and security. However, the huge volume of data created and the heterogeneous potential of IoMT devices make it difficult to achieve trustworthy execution as well as effective transaction management and organization. Using cutting-edge Machine Learning (ML) algorithms, like Lightweight Artificial Neural Networks (LANNs) with BT, this paper proposes an optimised multi-source data fusion framework that improves data integrity, real-time decision-making, efficiency, and scalability in an IoMT application environment. In order to assure accuracy and relevance, the data fusion mechanism merges multi-modal data from several IoMT devices. This enables the application of advanced fusion techniques, including Kalman Filtering enabled feature extraction. This guarantees the blockchain network processes only high-quality, pre-validated data. In addition, for secure transaction processing, the proposed architecture includes a trusted execution environment, guaranteeing data privacy and reliability. The proposed solution is good for resource-constrained IoMT contexts since it uses a hybrid consensus mechanism and clever data aggregation to reduce latency and computational cost. Comparing experimental results to classical blockchain-IoMT techniques, data accuracy, transaction throughput, and application security all show notable improvements of up to 98.33%, 97.98%, and 2.34%, respectively. Through the resolution of significant implementation constraints in blockchain transaction management and multi-source data fusion, this research offers a solid approach for developing IoMT ecosystems.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-28105-3.Keywords: Data fusion, Internet of things (IoT), Internet of medical things (IoMT), Blockchain, Lightweight artificial neural networks (LANNs), OptimizationSubject terms: Biomaterials, Health care, Medical research, Computer science, Information technology, Software

Introduction

Modern healthcare is being revolutionised by the Internet of Medical Things (IoMT)1,2. The technology makes it possible for smart medical equipment and sensory systems to be seamlessly integrated in order to gather, track, and evaluate patient data in real time1–3. This network promises to improve overall healthcare operations, allow for personalised treatments, and increase the accuracy of diagnostics. But the IoMT ecosystem has serious drawbacks, particularly when it comes to preserving the security, accountability, and integrity of vast amounts of heterogeneous data produced by various devices2–4.

The process of merging and synthesising data from several sources, including wearables, into a single, trustworthy dataset for additional analysis and decision-making is known as data aggregation4,5, as shown in Fig. 1. Artificial Neural Networks (ANNs) with machine learning capabilities are very good at overcoming the drawbacks of data aggregation, including noise, heterogeneity, and incomplete information. The human brain’s structure and operation, which includes input hidden and output layers, are comprised of interconnected layers of neurones6,7, as shown in Fig. 1. Data aggregation and blockchain in IoMT environment

The Internet of Medical Things (IoMT) environment Collects, transmits, and processes healthcare data by connecting a wide range of medical devices, sensory equipment, and systems. To guarantee effective, dependable, and safe handling of this data, two essential mechanisms—data aggregation and BT—are essential. In the context of IoMT, data aggregation is the process of gathering, analysing, and merging data from several IoMT devices into a single, cohesive dataset for analysis, storage, and decision-making. The following are the steps involved in aggregating IoMT data 11–13:

Data collection: IoMT devices such as wearable, implantable devices, and diagnostics tools generates heterogenous data stream, using the general formula of Devices (d) = { d1, d2, …, dn}; where ‘di’ consider a single unit that produces data vector of ‘vi(time)’14.

Implementation challenges and limitations

This study explores the advantages of combining blockchain technology with lightweight artificial neural networks on the IoMT, which has enormous potential for intelligent, safe, and effective healthcare applications. But there are still a number of obstacles, restrictions, and unresolved research problems that call for creative answers as follows36–38:

Scalability and interoperability

There are many different kinds of devices and data on the Internet of Medical Things environment39,40]. This heterogeneous data must be processed effectively by LANNs, and the blockchain consortium network must be able to manage large transaction volumes without experiencing scalability or latency issues40–42. In order to analyse data at the edge and enable large-scale IoMT networks, distributed lightweight ANN systems must be developed. Furthermore, the suggested interoperability is essential for smooth integration across various IoMT devices and the blockchain architecture. utilising sophisticated consensus techniques, like blockchains built on DAG. to expand IoMT environments’ blockchain consortium networks. Additionally, it is still in its infancy and aids in the development of intelligent decision-support systems for patients and physicians.

Real-time processing and latency

Real-time decision-making is necessary for Internet of Medical Things applications; yet, blockchain transaction validation and ANN processing delays can impair system performance. This way, latency-aware lightweight ANNs with little computational overhead can be developed for edge devices43,44. To cut down on transaction delays, it also investigates hybrid designs that combine blockchain technology with Directed Acyclic Graph. Nevertheless, asynchronous processing methods are used to manage blockchain updates and real-time data aggregation at the same time. However, there are ethical and legal restrictions associated with the use of AI and blockchain in healthcare, such as biases in ANN models and adherence to privacy regulations like HIPAA.

Fault tolerance and reliability

IoMT devices have a high failure rate, so it is crucial to maintain system dependability when errors occur45–47. ANNs and blockchain must smoothly deal with faulty and missing data. Thus, the use of a strong lightweight ANN is necessary to increase the system’s ability to handle noisy inputs or impute missing data without sacrificing accuracy. Adaptively optimising system reliability in dynamic IoMT situations is another application of reinforcement learning. However, the efficiency of blockchain and the performance of lightweight ANNs are impacted by the noisy, redundant, or even incomplete nature of IoMT data.

Conclusion and future direction

This research discussed a current multi-source data aggregation faces throughout the management and organization of medical data in the existing environment. In this manner, effective transaction management and organisation, as well as trustworthy execution, are challenging to achieve due to the vast amount of data generated and the diverse potential of IoMT devices. In this paper, an optimised multi-source data fusion framework is proposed that uses state-of-the-art ML algorithms, such as LANNs with BT, to enhance data integrity, real-time decision-making, efficiency, and scalability in an IoMT application environment. Multi-modal data from many IoMT devices is combined by the data fusion method to ensure accuracy and relevance. Advanced fusion techniques, such as feature extraction provided by Kalman Filtering, can be applied as a result. This ensures that only high-quality, pre-validated data is processed by the blockchain network.

Furthermore, the proposed design of the framework incorporates a trusted execution environment for secure transaction processing, ensuring data privacy and dependability. Because the suggested approach employs a hybrid consensus process and astute data aggregation to lower latency and computational cost, it is ideal for IoMT scenarios with limited resources. Data correctness, transaction speed, and application security all exhibit significant gains of up to 98.33%, 97.98%, and 2.34%, respectively, when experimental findings are compared to traditional blockchain-IoMT procedures. Although the results of this study are encouraging, the following areas need more research to improve the proposed framework: (i) the capacity to combine cloud and edge with the potential for data fusion; (ii) data analytics powered by AI; (iii) standardisation and interoperability; (iv) optimisation of computing energy; (v) resilience to sophisticated threats; and (vi) patient-centric data management.

Fetal fractions mediate the association between total cell-free DNA and preeclampsia risk in a non-invasive prenatal testing cohort
Authors: Bin Zhang, Xusheng Chen, Sijie Xi, Zhaolong Zhan, He Dong, Yinglu Zhang, Xiaosong Yuan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundNon-invasive prenatal testing (NIPT)-derived total cell-free DNA (cfDNA) and fetal fractions (FFs) are potential indicators of placental dysfunction, a hallmark of preeclampsia (PE). However, their clinical utility in PE risk stratification, particularly in twin pregnancies, remains underexplored. This study evaluated the association between total cfDNA levels and the incidence of PE and assessed mediation by FFs.MethodsThis retrospective cohort study included 2,366 pregnant women who underwent NIPT at 12–22 weeks of gestation at a tertiary hospital. Total cfDNA levels and FFs were extracted from the NIPT data, and PE diagnoses were confirmed via medical records. Logistic regression and mediation analyses were used to assess associations and mediating effects.ResultsPE prevalence was 3.51% (83/2,366). Elevated total cfDNA tertiles (T2/T3 vs. T1) were associated with increased rates of PE (T2: OR = 2.36, 95% CI: 1.16–4.80; T3: OR = 3.00, 95% CI: 1.51–5.97). Each 1 µg/L increase in total cfDNA was associated with a 12% increase in PE odds (OR = 1.12, 95% CI: 1.05–1.20). Lower FFs were associated with PE risk (T1 vs. T3: OR = 2.70, 95% CI: 1.45–5.00; per 1% decrease: OR = 1.15, 95% CI: 1.07–1.23). In singleton pregnancies, total cfDNA levels ≤ 4.41 µg/L were significantly associated with increased PE risk (OR = 2.89, 95% CI: 1.32–6.29, P = 0.008) with a validated threshold effect [log-likelihood ratio test (LRT, P = 0.005)], while FF ≤ 8.86% showed a protective trend (OR = 0.81, 95% CI: 0.68–0.97, P = 0.021) despite a nonsignificant LRT (P = 0.192). In twin pregnancies, total cfDNA showed no significant threshold or linear association with PE risk (OR = 1.06, P = 0.382; LRT, P = 0.215), while FFs exhibited a stronger inverse linear association (OR = 0.77, 95% CI: 0.65–0.92, P = 0.003) with no significant threshold effect (LRT, P = 0.270). Total cfDNA was inversely correlated with FFs (T3 vs. T1: β=-2.96, 95% CI: -3.34 to -2.59). Mediation analyses showed FFs partially explained 24.4% and 32.5% of the total cfDNA-PE relationship in singleton pregnancies and all pregnancies, respectively.ConclusionThis study observed that elevated total cfDNA levels during NIPT were associated with an increased risk of PE, with FFs acting as partial mediators. These findings warrant further investigation into total cfDNA and FFs as potential biomarkers for early PE risk stratification, even in high-risk settings such as twin pregnancies, to determine whether their use in screening could contribute to the development of strategies aimed at improving maternal-fetal health.Keywords: Cell free DNA, Fetal fractions, Twin pregnancies, Preeclampsia, Non-invasive prenatal testing, Mediating effect

Introduction

Preeclampsia (PE), a hypertensive disorder of pregnancy, remains one of the leading causes of maternal and perinatal morbidity and mortality worldwide. It is a significant contributor to fetal prematurity and long-term maternal cardiovascular disease (CVD), with a global incidence of approximately 2–14% of all pregnancies [1–3]. Its pathophysiology is rooted in placental dysfunction, which is characterized by impaired trophoblast invasion, abnormal spiral artery remodeling, and systemic endothelial injury [4]. Early identification of women at high risk for PE is critical to allow for timely interventions, such as aspirin prophylaxis or intensified monitoring, which can mitigate adverse outcomes [5]. However, current risk stratification tools, including maternal history, ultrasound, and biochemical markers, lack sufficient predictive accuracy, particularly in twin pregnancies where the pathophysiology of PE diverges from that in singleton gestations [6, 7].

Non-invasive prenatal testing (NIPT), which analyzes cell-free DNA (cfDNA) of placental origin in maternal circulation, has revolutionized screening for fetal aneuploidies. Beyond its primary diagnostic purpose, emerging evidence suggests that quantitative parameters, namely the total cfDNA level and the fetal fractions (FFs), defined as the proportion of placental-derived cfDNA relative to total cfDNA, may serve as indicators of placental health [8]. Elevated total cfDNA levels, for instance, have been associated with adverse pregnancy outcomes, including PE, presumably due to their correlation with placental stress and inflammation [9]. Conversely, low FFs have been implicated in placental pathologies such as PE, likely resulting from reduced trophoblast turnover or impaired release of placental DNA into the maternal bloodstream [10]. However, the clinical applicability of total cfDNA and FFs in twin pregnancies, a population with an inherently higher risk of PE and distinct placental physiology, remains poorly characterized [11]. Furthermore, whether FFs mediate the relationship between total cfDNA levels and PE, a plausible hypothesis given their shared placental origin, has not yet been explored.

This study aimed to address this knowledge gap by investigating the association between total cfDNA levels and PE incidence in a Chinese NIPT cohort comprising both singleton and twin pregnancies. We also sought to evaluate the potential mediating role of FFs in this relationship.

Methods

Cohort and data abstraction

This observational study was conducted at Changzhou Maternal and Child Health Care Hospital in Jiangsu Province, China, between April 2018 and August 2019. A total of 2,470 consecutive pregnant women who underwent NIPT at 12–22 weeks of gestation and delivered at 28–41 weeks at the hospital were initially enrolled. Eligibility criteria included a negative NIPT result and live birth without congenital anomalies. Exclusion criteria consisted of missing total cfDNA levels or incomplete medical records, as well as preexisting maternal conditions such as hypertension (n = 32), diabetes (n = 24), syphilis (n = 8), rheumatism or autoimmune diseases (n = 5), and absence of total cfDNA measurement in NIPT (n = 35). After excluding 104 participants based on these criteria, the final cohort comprised 2366 pregnant women (Fig. 1).

Flow diagram

Data were collected from two main sources: the hospital’s prenatal screening database provided maternal age, height, weight at the time of NIPT, gestational age at NIPT, plasma total cfDNA levels, and FFs; the electronic medical record (EMR) system was used to obtain maternal medical history, self-reported health behaviors (e.g., smoking, alcohol use), gestational age at delivery, parity, mode of conception (natural or assisted), pregnancy complications (e.g., gestational diabetes, preeclampsia), delivery mode (vaginal or cesarean), and fetal birth weight. None of the participants reported smoking, alcohol consumption, or use of illicit substances during pregnancy. This study was approved by the Institutional Review Board (IRB) of the hospital (Ref. No. ZD201803). All participants provided written informed consent before undergoing NIPT, which included authorization for the use of anonymized medical data for research purposes.

Plasma total cfDNA isolation and FFs analysis

NIPT was performed using the Illumina NextSeq 500 sequencing platform (Illumina, China). Briefly, maternal peripheral blood (8 mL) was collected in EDTA-K₂ tubes. Plasma was separated by centrifugation at 1600 × g for 10 min at 4 °C and stored at −80 °C until further processing. Total cfDNA was extracted from 1.2 mL of plasma using an automated magnetic bead-based purification system (KingFisher Flex, Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). The total cfDNA concentration was quantified using a Qubit® 2.0 Fluorometer with the Qubit® dsDNA HS Assay Kit (Invitrogen, USA) [9]. Sequencing libraries were constructed from the extracted cfDNA and subjected to massively parallel sequencing. Bioinformatic analysis of the sequencing data was performed to generate z-scores for chromosomal aneuploidies detection and to calculate the FFs. FFs were quantified using a multivariate machine learning model trained on regional read depth counts from autosomal regions obtained through low-coverage whole-genome massively parallel sequencing (MPS) with single-end reads. Automated data normalization procedures were applied to minimize technical bias, yielding FFs estimates that showed consistent distribution profiles and no statistically significant deviation compared to methylation-based reference measurements. The analysis pipeline utilized VeriSeq v1 NIPT Analysis Software (Illumina) for end-to-end computation of FFs [12]. Women with positive NIPT results (z-score ≥ 3 or ≤ − 3) were referred for confirmatory genetic counseling and diagnostic testing.

Definitions

In this study, total cfDNA was defined as the pool of extracellular DNA fragments present in maternal peripheral circulation, comprising both maternally derived DNA (approximately 95%, originating from maternal somatic cells) and fetal-derived DNA (approximately 5%, originating from placental trophoblasts). Gestational hypertension (GH) was defined as new-onset hypertension (systolic blood pressure ≥ 140 mmHg and/or diastolic blood pressure ≥ 90 mmHg after 20 weeks of gestation) without proteinuria. PE was defined as new-onset hypertension after 20 weeks of gestation accompanied by maternal organ dysfunction (renal, hepatic, hematological, or neurological) and/or uteroplacental dysfunction; proteinuria was not required for the diagnosis [13]. Gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM) was diagnosed using a 75-g oral glucose tolerance test (OGTT) performed between 24 and 28 weeks of gestation, in accordance with the International Association of Diabetes and Pregnancy Study Groups (IADPSG) criteria [14]. Intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy (ICP) was defined as elevated liver transaminase levels (ALT or AST >40 U/L) combined with serum total bile acid concentrations ≥ 10 µmol/L [15].

Statistical analyses

Demographic characteristics of the NIPT cohort were summarized with continuous variables expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD) and categorical variables reported as frequencies (%). Differences in participants’ characteristics across tertiles of plasma total cfDNA levels were assessed using parametric test or non-parametric test for continuous variables, as appropriate based on distribution, and the chi-square test for categorical variables. The associations between PE risk and key predictors, including total cfDNA levels and FFs, were systematically evaluated using both unadjusted and multivariable-adjusted logistic regression models. Regression models were adjusted for maternal age, body mass index (BMI) and gestational age at NIPT, parity, use of assisted reproduction, cesarean section history, and twin pregnancy; the adjustment for twin pregnancy was applied only in analyses involving all pregnancies. The selection of age, BMI, parity, use of assisted reproduction, and twin pregnancy was based on their established biological relevance as potential or known PE risk factors, as supported by previous literature [1, 2]. Cesarean section history was adjusted for as a potential confounder due to its clinical associations with PE-related comorbidities (e.g., multifetal gestation, assisted reproduction, or prior PE), even in the absence of a established direct causal link with incident PE. Gestational age at NIPT sampling was included in final models to account for its known effects on FFs [10]. The robustness of the findings was confirmed through sensitivity analyses conducted among subgroups of participants under 35 years of age, with BMI < 25 kg/m², multiparity, and natural conception. Generalized additive models were applied with smooth curve fitting to assess potential non-linear relationships between continuous covariates (total cfDNA and FFs) and PE risk. When non-linearity was identified, threshold effects on both sides of the inflection point were evaluated using a two-piecewise linear regression model. The optimal model characterizing the association between PE risk and total cfDNA/FFs was selected based on the log-likelihood ratio test. Additionally, mediation analysis was performed to quantify the proportion of the total cfDNA-PE relationship mediated by FFs, using models adjusted for the same confounders as the regression analyses. The statistical analyses were conducted using EmpowerStats software (X&Y Solutions, Inc., MA, USA) and R (version 4.4.2; R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria).

Results

Participants’ characteristics

Table 1 presents the demographic and clinical characteristics of 2366 participants stratified by plasma total cfDNA tertiles: low (T1: < 4.17 µg/L, n = 783), middle (T2: 4.17–5.85 µg/L, n = 787), and high (T3: >5.85 µg/L, n = 796). Compared with the low tertile (T1), the high total cfDNA tertile (T3) was associated with a significantly earlier gestational age at NIPT (121.65 ± 9.95 days vs. 124.01 ± 12.46 days, P < 0.001), as well as higher rates of assisted reproduction (13.32% vs. 8.94%, P = 0.005) and multiple pregnancies (12.94% vs. 3.83%, P < 0.001). PE prevalence increased in a dose-dependent manner across total cfDNA tertiles, from 1.40% in T1 to 5.40% in T3 (P < 0.001), while FFs decreased inversely from 12.75 ± 4.34% in T1 to 9.58 ± 3.54% in T3 (P < 0.001). As expected, plasma total cfDNA concentrations differed markedly across tertiles (T1: 3.20 ± 0.61 µg/L; T3: 8.16 ± 2.56 µg/L, P < 0.001). Relative to T1, T3 was also associated with higher rates of PTB (14.70% vs. 8.94%, P < 0.001) and cesarean delivery (60.93% vs. 50.70%, P < 0.001). No significant differences were observed among the groups in maternal age, parity, or history of cesarean delivery. In addition, women who developed PE had significantly higher plasma total cfDNA levels at NIPT (6.98 ± 3.39 µg/L vs. 5.32 ± 2.45 µg/L, P < 0.001; Table S1 and Fig. 2A) and significantly lower FFs (8.74 ± 3.66% vs. 11.32 ± 4.10%, P < 0.001; Table S1 and Fig. 2B) compared to those without pregnancy complications.

Association of PE risk with total CfDNA and FFs

Table 2 demonstrates the significant associations of PE risk with plasma total cfDNA levels and FFs. For total cfDNA levels, compared to T1, the unadjusted odds ratios (ORs) for T2 and T3 were 2.54 (95% CI: 1.26–5.14, P = 0.009) and 3.35 (95% CI: 1.70–6.61, P < 0.001), respectively. These associations remained significant after multivariable adjustment (T2: OR = 2.36, 95% CI: 1.16–4.80, P = 0.017; T3: OR = 3.00, 95% CI: 1.51–5.97, P = 0.002). Each 1 µg/L increase in total cfDNA was associated with a 12% increase in the adjusted odds of PE (OR = 1.12, 95% CI: 1.05–1.20, P < 0.001). Similar effect sizes were observed in the singleton pregnancies subgroup (OR = 1.15, 95% CI: 1.07–1.23, P < 0.001). For FFs, compared to T3 (> 12.42%), T1 (< 8.86%) was associated with an adjusted OR of 2.70 (95% CI: 1.45–5.00, P = 0.002). Each 1% decrease in FFs was associated with a 15% increase in the adjusted odds of PE (OR = 1.15, 95% CI: 1.07–1.23, P < 0.001), and a consistent association was also found in singleton pregnancies (OR = 1.10, 95% CI: 1.02–1.19, P = 0.012). Additionally, sensitivity analyses conducted in subgroups including women younger than 35 years (Table S2), those with BMI < 25 kg/m² (Table S3), multiparous women (Table S4), and those with spontaneous conception (Table S5) confirmed the consistency of the results. Furthermore, generalized additive models with smooth curve fitting were used to graphically illustrate the associations between total cfDNA, FFs, and PE risk, as shown in Fig. 3.

Relationship of PE risk with total cfDNA and fetal fractions

The two-piecewise regression analysis revealed distinct threshold effects of plasma total cfDNA and FFs on PE risk, with significant heterogeneity observed between singleton and twin pregnancies (Table 3). In singleton pregnancies, a nonlinear association was identified for total cfDNA, with an inflection point at 4.41 µg/L: PE risk increased sharply below this threshold (OR = 2.89, 95% CI: 1.32–6.29; P = 0.008) but showed no significant rise above it (OR = 1.09, P = 0.06), supported by a significant log-likelihood ratio test (LRT, P = 0.005). In contrast, no threshold effect was detected for total cfDNA in twin pregnancies (LRT, P = 0.215), and linear regression indicated no significant association (OR = 1.06, P = 0.382). For FFs, singleton pregnancies demonstrated a nonsignificant threshold at 8.86% (LRT, P = 0.192), although lower FFs (≤ 8.86%) were inversely associated with PE risk (OR = 0.81, P = 0.021). In twin pregnancies, a stronger inverse linear relationship was observed (OR = 0.77, P = 0.003), but no robust threshold effect was found (inflection point: 15.03%; LRT, P = 0.270).

Relationship between total CfDNA and FFs

Table 4 summarizes the relationship between plasma total cfDNA levels and FFs. Higher total cfDNA tertiles were inversely associated with FFs in both unadjusted and adjusted models. In unadjusted analyses, compared to T1, T2 was associated with a β coefficient of −2.05 (95% CI: −2.44 to −1.66, P < 0.001), and T3 with a β of −3.27 (95% CI: −3.66 to −2.88, P < 0.001). After adjustment for confounders, these associations remained significant (T2: β=−1.80, 95% CI: −2.17 to −1.43; T3: β=−2.96, 95% CI: −3.34 to −2.59; P < 0.001 for both). Each 1 µg/L increase in total cfDNA was associated with a decrease in FFs, with β values of −0.51 (95% CI: −0.57 to −0.45) in the unadjusted model and − 0.47 (95% CI: −0.53 to −0.41) in the adjusted model (P < 0.001 for both). In subgroup analyses, this inverse association was strengthened in singleton pregnancies (adjusted β=−0.50, 95% CI: −0.56 to −0.44, P < 0.001) and attenuated yet remained significant in twin pregnancies (adjusted β=−0.27, 95% CI: −0.50 to −0.05, P = 0.02).

Mediation analysis

The mediating effects of FFs on the association between total cfDNA and PE are illustrated in Table 5. A significant mediating effect was observed in singleton pregnancies, where the total effect of total cfDNA on PE was 0.020 (95% CI: 0.011–0.028, P < 0.001). This effect comprised a direct effect of 0.015 (95% CI: 0.006–0.023, P = 0.004) and a mediated effect of 0.005 (95% CI: 0.001–0.010, P < 0.001), which accounted for 24.36% of the total effect. Similarly, in the analysis of all pregnancies, the total effect was 0.019 (95% CI: 0.010–0.026, P < 0.001), comprising a direct effect of 0.013 (95% CI: 0.003–0.021, P = 0.014) and a mediated effect of 0.006 (95% CI: 0.003–0.011, P < 0.001), which represented 32.48% of the total effect (Fig. 4). In contrast, no significant mediating effect of FFs was detected in twin pregnancies.

Discussion

Main findings

The main findings of this study provide several key insights into the predictive value of total cfDNA and FFs for PE risk, with important distinctions observed between singleton and twin pregnancies. First, higher total cfDNA levels were associated with a dose-dependent increase in PE risk (adjusted OR = 1.12 per 1 µg/L; T3 vs. T1: OR = 3.00), with a nonlinear threshold effect identified at 4.41 µg/L in singleton pregnancies. Below this threshold, the association intensified (OR = 2.89). Similarly, lower FFs were independently predictive of PE, with each 1% decrease associated with a 15% increase in risk. Second, the relationship between total cfDNA, FFs, and PE varied by pregnancy type: in singletons, total cfDNA ≤ 4.41 µg/L represented a significant risk threshold for PE, as confirmed by a significant LRT (P = 0.005), while FF ≤ 8.86% was associated with a protective effect (OR = 0.81). In twins, no threshold effect was observed for total cfDNA, but a stronger inverse linear association was found between FFs and PE (OR = 0.77 per 1% increase). Third, total cfDNA levels were inversely correlated with FFs. Finally, mediation analysis indicated that FFs partially explained the total cfDNA-PE link (32.48% in all pregnancies and 24.36% in single pregnancies), underscoring their role in placental-maternal interactions. These findings support the development of precision screening strategies for PE, particularly in high-risk populations, and underscore the importance of using pregnancy-type-specific risk algorithms.

Interpretation

Studies from multiple countries across Europe, Asia, Africa, and the Americas, including Switzerland, Japan, Hungary, Italy, Spain, South Korea, Egypt, Israel, South Africa, Brazil, and the United States, have demonstrated that higher total cfDNA levels in pregnant women who developed PE, compared with normotensive controls, may serve as a potential predictive biomarker for PE [16–29]. This association remains consistent across different cfDNA quantification methods (e.g., targeted gene sequencing or fluorescence-based detection). However, the predictive potential of total cfDNA for PE may be influenced by maternal gestational age and other clinical characteristics at the time of measurement [30–33]. In line with previous evidence, the present cohort study based on NIPT data confirmed a positive association between plasma total cfDNA levels and PE risk. A quantitative dose–response analysis further revealed that each 1 µg/L increase in total cfDNA concentration measured in NIPT was associated with a 12% increase in PE risk, which is consistent with findings from an earlier Chinese cohort study [9].

In contrast to established positive association between total cfDNA and PE risk, evidence regarding the relationship between FFs and PE development remains inconsistent [34, 35]. While some studies have reported an association between low FFs and PE in singleton pregnancies [10, 29, 36–39], others have found no significant relationship [28, 30, 40, 41]. Our results support an inverse correlation and further indicate that each 1% decrease in FFs was associated with a 15% increase in PE risk. Additionally, a stronger inverse linear relationship between FFs and PE risk was observed in twin pregnancies in the present study, which contrasts with previous research that reported no significant association in this population [11]. The heterogeneity in the reported association between FFs and PE across studies of singleton and twin pregnancies likely arises from several factors, including limited sample size, changes in PE diagnostic criteria over time, population diversity, variations in sampling timing and gestational age, and methodological differences in FFs quantification [34].

The pathophysiology linking total cfDNA to PE remains incompletely understood. Proposed mechanisms include maternal tissue injury (e.g., endothelial, hepatic, or renal damage), placental ischemia-driven trophoblast apoptosis with subsequent cfDNA release, and impaired cfDNA clearance, particularly in women diagnosed with PE or those who subsequently developed the disorder [28, 42]. In this study, elevated plasma total cfDNA levels were inversely correlated with FFs, and reduced FFs were significantly associated with heightened PE risk. Specifically, insufficient placentation during early gestation leads to reduced FFs, which in turn increase PE risk. This pathway is supported by previous evidence and the current study’s identification of FFs as mediators of the total cfDNA-PE relationship [43]. However, no significant mediation effect of FFs was observed in twin pregnancies, suggesting that distinct pathophysiological mechanisms may be operative in this population.

Strengths and limitations

This study has several key strengths that enhance its scientific rigor and clinical relevance. First, it represents the first large-scale retrospective analysis in a Chinese population to jointly evaluate NIPT-derived total cfDNA, FFs, and PE risk using real-world clinical data, providing important insights into population-specific biomarker thresholds (e.g., total cfDNA ≤ 4.41 µg/L in singleton pregnancies). Second, by stratifying analyses by singleton and twin pregnancies, the study revealed distinct risk patterns, challenging the universal application of biomarker thresholds and highlighting the need for pregnancy-type-specific risk assessment approaches. Third, the use of mediation analysis provided novel mechanistic insights into how FFs influence the total cfDNA-PE relationship, supporting their role in placental-maternal interactions. Finally, the use of real-world NIPT data improves the external validity of the findings, reflecting routine clinical practice and facilitating the translation of results into prenatal screening protocols.

Nonetheless, this study has several limitations. First, the retrospective design inherent in real-world data introduces potential selection bias, particularly due to the exclusion of incomplete records or high-risk pregnancies with pregestational diseases, which may have led to an underestimation of severe or early-onset PE cases. Second, heterogeneity in NIPT platforms and protocols across centers may have affected total cfDNA quantification and FFs calculation, limiting the generalizability of the proposed thresholds. Third, despite statistical adjustments, residual confounding from unmeasured variables (e.g., placental pathology subtypes, maternal lifestyle, or genetic factors) may persist. Fourth, the absence of longitudinal total cfDNA/FFs measurements precluded the analysis of temporal trends and dynamic risk evolution during gestation. Finally, while mediation models suggested plausible biological pathways, causal inference remains speculative without experimental validation or placental histopathological correlation. These findings highlight the value of real-world NIPT data for PE risk stratification in Chinese populations but emphasize the need for prospective longitudinal studies to validate the proposed thresholds and refine pregnancy-type-specific predictive models.

Conclusions

This large-scale real-world study provides preliminary evidence in a Chinese population that NIPT-derived total cfDNA and FFs may collectively contribute to PE risk prediction. The identification of a total cfDNA threshold of 4.41 µg/L in singleton pregnancies, along with a marked inverse association between FFs and PE in twin gestations, suggests that pregnancy-type-specific risk stratification could be of value. The observed partial mediation by FFs supports a complex interaction between these placental biomarkers and maternal-fetal physiology. Clinically, these findings may aid in developing more tailored screening algorithms to improve PE prediction, particularly in high-risk groups. However, the single-center cohort design and cross-sectional biomarker measurements may limit the generalizability of the results and prevent causal inferences. Future multicenter longitudinal studies are needed to validate these thresholds and explore underlying mechanisms. Investigating experimental interventions targeting total cfDNA dynamics represents a promising research direction. Integrating these biomarkers these biomarkers into precision medicine frameworks may eventually help reduce PE-related morbidity through earlier and more targeted interventions.

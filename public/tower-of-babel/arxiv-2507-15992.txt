Point counts, automorphisms, and gonalities of Shimura curves
Pietro Mercuri, Oana Padurariu, Frederick Saia, Claudio Stirpe

Abstract
. We implement an algorithm to compute the number of points over finite fields for the Shimura curves X 0 D ​ ( N ) X_{0}^{D}(N) and their Atkin–Lehner quotients. Our computations result in many examples of curves which attain the largest known point counts among curves of specified genus over a finite field of given cardinality. To illustrate the utility of our point counts algorithm in addressing arithmetic questions, we prove that all automorphisms are Atkin–Lehner for many curves X 0 D ​ ( N ) X_{0}^{D}(N) with D ​ N ≤ 10000 DN\leq 10000 , and we determine all tetragonal curves X 0 D ​ ( N ) X_{0}^{D}(N) up to a small number of possible exceptions.

2.
Shimura curves

A
\emph{quaternionalgebra}
over a field
F
is a central simple algebra of dimension
4
over
F
. We call a quaternion algebra
B
over the field
\mathbb{Q}
of rational numbers
indefinite
if it is split at the single real place of
\mathbb{Q}
, i.e., if

B\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{R}\cong M_{2}(\mathbb{R}).

A quaternion algebra
B
over
\mathbb{Q}
is determined up to isomorphism by its discriminant
D
, that is the product of the rational primes at which
B
is ramified. The indefinite quaternion discriminants over
\mathbb{Q}
are exactly those integers which are products of an even number of distinct rational primes. The
D=1
case, for example, is that of the split quaternion algebra
M_{2}(\mathbb{Q})
.

In this work, we consider for an indefinite rational quaternion discriminant
D
and a positive integer
N
coprime to
D
the Shimura curve
X_{0}^{D}(N)
. A foundational result of Shimura
[
Sh67
, Main Theorem I]
states that this curve has a canonical model over
\mathbb{Q}
. The
D=1
case of
X_{0}^{1}(N)
recovers the modular curve setting, parameterizing elliptic curves with level structure, while for
D>1
these curves parametrize abelian surfaces with quaternionic multiplication and level structure. In the latter case, these curves lack the rational cusps the modular curves
X_{0}(N)
possess and, moreover, they have no real points
[
Sh75
, Theorem 0]
: if
D>1
, then
X_{0}^{D}(N)(\mathbb{R})=\varnothing
.

By work of Drinfeld, for which we refer to
[
BC91
,
Buzzard
]
, there exists a flat proper model of
X_{0}^{D}(N)
over
\mathbb{Z}
, extending the moduli interpretation, which is smooth over
\mathbb{Z}\left[\tfrac{1}{DN}\right]
. For this work, it is important to note that
X_{0}^{D}(N)
has good reduction at primes
p\nmid DN
.

For
N
a positive integer, we have a natural modular covering map
X_{0}^{D}(N)\to X_{0}^{D}(1)
with degree
\psi(N)
given by the Dedekind Psi function:

\psi(N)=N\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\mid N\\
\text{prime}\end{subarray}}\left(1+\frac{1}{p}\right).

Moreover, these natural maps are compatible: for
N_{1}\mid N_{2}
there is a natural map
X_{0}^{D}(N_{2})\to X_{0}^{D}(N_{1})
of degree
\psi(N_{2})/\psi(N_{1})
.
We have the following formula for the genus of the curve
X_{0}^{D}(N)
(see, for example,
[
Voight21
, Thm. 39.4.20]
).

Proposition 2.1
.

For
D>1
the discriminant of an indefinite quaternion algebra over
\mathbb{Q}
, for
N
a positive integer coprime to
D
and for
k\in\{3,4\}
, define

e_{k}(D,N)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p|D\\
\text{prime}\end{subarray}}\left(1-\left(\frac{-k}{p}\right)\right)\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\parallel N\\
\text{prime}\end{subarray}}\left(1+\left(\frac{-k}{\ell}\right)\right)\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell^{2}|N,\\
\ell\text{ prime}\end{subarray}}\delta_{\ell}(k),

where
\left(\frac{\cdot}{\cdot}\right)
is the Kronecker quadratic symbol, and

\delta_{\ell}(k)=\begin{cases}2&\quad\textnormal{if }\left(\frac{-k}{\ell}\right)=1,\\
0&\quad\textnormal{otherwise.}\\
\end{cases}

Then, the genus of
X_{0}^{D}(N)
is given by

g(X_{0}^{D}(N))=1+\frac{\phi(D)\psi(N)}{12}-\frac{e_{4}(D,N)}{4}-\frac{e_{3}(D,N)}{3},

where
\phi
denotes the Euler totient function.

2.1.
Atkin–Lehner involutions and Ribet’s isogeny

The automorphism group of
X_{0}^{D}(N)
over
\mathbb{Q}
has a subgroup consisting of involutions which are natural from the moduli-theoretic perspective. This subgroup is that of the
Atkin–Lehner involutions
, containing a non-trivial involution
w_{m}
for each Hall divisor
1\neq m\parallel DN
, and we denote this subgroup by
W_{0}(D,N)\leq\textnormal{Aut}(X_{0}^{D}(N))
. For more background on Atkin–Lehner involutions, we refer to
[
Ogg77
]
and
[
Ogg85
, §2]
; here we just say what we need regarding the structure of this group.

Realizing
X_{0}^{D}(N)
as the coarse moduli scheme attached to the moduli problem of parameterizing abelian surfaces with a quaternionic multiplication structure by an Eichler order
\mathcal{O}_{N}
in the quaternion algebra
B
, we have the following subgroup of the normalizer of
\mathcal{O}_{N}

N_{{B}^{\times}_{>0}}(\mathcal{O}_{N}):=\{u\in{B}^{\times}:\text{nrd}(u)>0\text{ and }u^{-1}\mathcal{O}_{N}u=\mathcal{O}_{N}\},

where
nrd
is the reduced norm, and we realize the group of Atkin–Lehner involutions as

W_{0}(D,N):=N_{{B}^{\times}_{>0}}(\mathcal{O}_{N})/\mathbb{Q}^{\times}\mathcal{O}_{N}^{\times}\leq\text{Aut}(X_{0}^{D}(N)).

Recalling that
\omega(n)
denotes the number of distinct prime divisors of an integer
n
, this group is a finite abelian
2
-group of the form

W_{0}(D,N)=\{w_{m}:m\parallel DN\}\cong\left(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\right)^{\omega(DN)}.

For a subgroup
W\leq W_{0}(D,N)
, we call the quotient
X_{0}^{D}(N)/W
an
Atkin–Lehner quotient
of
X_{0}^{D}(N)
. We also refer to the elements of the subgroup of
{\rm Aut}\left(X_{0}^{D}(N)/W\right)
induced by the elements of
W_{0}(D,N)
as Atkin–Lehner involutions.

Ogg determined the set of fixed points for each Atkin–Lehner involution on the curve
X_{0}^{D}(N)
: these are pairwise disjoint for distinct involutions, and they consist of complex multiplication (CM) points by certain imaginary quadratic orders.

Proposition 2.2
.

[
Ogg83
, p. 283]

Let
m>1
with
m\parallel DN
. The fixed points of the Atkin–Lehner involution
w_{m}
acting on
X_{0}^{D}(N)
are points with
CM
by the following imaginary quadratic orders:

R=\begin{cases}\mathbb{Z}[i]\text{ and }\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-2}]&\quad\textnormal{if }m=2,\\
\mathbb{Z}\left[\frac{1+\sqrt{-m}}{2}\right]\text{and }\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-m}]&\quad\textnormal{if }m\equiv 3\;(\bmod\;4),\\
\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-m}]&\quad\textnormal{ otherwise}.\\
\end{cases}

In the referenced work, Ogg provides the count for the number of fixed points of
w_{m}
with CM by
R
for each of the orders
R
listed for
m
in
Proposition

2.2
. Letting
\mathcal{O}_{N}
denote a fixed Eichler order of level
N
in the indefinite quaternion algebra over
\mathbb{Q}
of discriminant
D
, the count is given as

h(R)\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p|\frac{DN}{m}\\
\text{prime}\end{subarray}}\nu_{p}(R,\mathcal{O}_{N}),

where
h(R)
is the class number of the order
R
and
\nu_{p}(R,\mathcal{O}_{N})
is the number of inequivalent optimal embeddings of the localization of
R_{p}
at
p
into the localization
(\mathcal{O}_{N})_{p}
of
\mathcal{O}_{N}
at
p
. Counts of these optimal embeddings are given in
[
Ogg83
, Theorem 2]
.

Using
Proposition

2.2
and the Riemann–Hurwitz theorem in combination with our genus formula for
X_{0}^{D}(N)
(
Proposition

2.1
), one can compute the genus of every Atkin–Lehner quotient of
X_{0}^{D}(N)
. A function handling these genus computations is implemented in the file
quot_genus.m
in
[
MPSSRep
]
.

The following theorem of Ribet, relating the Jacobian of
X_{0}^{D}(N)
to an abelian subvariety of the Jacobian of the modular curve
X_{0}(DN)
, is crucial to our implementation of a finite field point count algorithm for
X_{0}^{D}(N)
in
Section

4
. While Ribet’s original result in
[
Ribet90
]
contains a squarefree restriction on the level
N
, this hypothesis can be removed by work of Hijikata–Pizer–Shemanske
[
HPS89a
,
HPS89b
]
.

Theorem 2.3
.

[
Ribet90
,
BD96
,
HPS89a
,
HPS89b
]

There is an isogeny defined over
\mathbb{Q}

\Psi\colon\operatorname{Jac}(X_{0}(DN))^{D-\operatorname{new}}\longrightarrow\operatorname{Jac}(X_{0}^{D}(N))

such that, for each Atkin–Lehner involution
w_{m}(D,N)\in W_{0}(D,N)
, the corresponding automorphism
\Psi^{*}(w_{m}(D,N))
on
\operatorname{Jac}(X_{0}(DN))^{D-\textnormal{new}}
is

(1)

\Psi^{*}(w_{m}(D,N))=(-1)^{\omega(\gcd(D,m))}w_{m}(1,DN),

where
w_{m}(1,DN)\in W_{0}(1,DN)
denotes the Atkin–Lehner involution corresponding to
m
on the modular curve
X_{0}(DN)
, and again
\omega(n)
denotes the number of distinct prime divisors of an integer
n
.

Remark 2.4
.

The Jacobian
\operatorname{Jac}(X_{0}(DN))
of
X_{0}(DN)
decomposes into irreducible isogeny factors
A_{f}
, which correspond to cusp forms
f
. To obtain the
D
-new part, we take those factors with conductor divisible by
D
. Since
A_{f}
, when
f
is a newform of level
DN
, has conductor
(DN)^{\dim A_{f}}
(see
[
KW09a
]
and
[
KW09b
]
for the proof of Serre’s modularity conjecture, and
[
Ser87
, Théorème 5]
for its consequence we are interested in),
hence, by the classical theory of modular curves and oldforms (see
[
AL70
, Theorem 5]
and
[
DS05
, Chapters 5 and 6]
), we have

\operatorname{Jac}(X_{0}(DN))^{D-\operatorname{new}}\sim\prod_{n|N}\operatorname{Jac}^{\operatorname{new}}(X_{0}(Dn))^{\sigma_{0}\left(\frac{N}{n}\right)},

where
\sigma_{0}(m)
is sum of positive divisors of the integer
m
and
\operatorname{Jac}^{\operatorname{new}}(X_{0}(Dn))
denotes the new part of
\operatorname{Jac}(X_{0}(Dn))
, i.e., the part where the isogeny factors
A_{f}
are associated to newforms
f
of level
Dn
.

The isogeny factors of the Jacobian of an Atkin–Lehner quotient
X_{0}^{D}(N)/W
, and the corresponding cusp forms, can then be determined by the action of the pullback of
W
on

given in

It is expected that

]
, Kenku–Momose
[

[

, and Harrison
[

]
; here, the only curves

of genus at least

with automorphisms which are not Atkin–Lehner involutions are those of level

In

, we provide methods for checking this equality, along with related experimental results, for

. Here, we recall related results of Kontogeorgis–Rotger.

Proposition 2.5
.

[

Suppose

is squarefree and coprime to
D
and let

. If

, then all automorphisms of

and

for some

.

.

[

, Corollary 1.8]

Suppose

. If

, then

\textnormal{Aut}(X_{0}^{D}(N))\cong\left(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\right)^{s}

for some

.

In the following lemma, giving sufficient criteria for the equality
{\rm Aut}(X_{0}^{D}(N))=W_{0}(D,N)
, the numbers

for

are as in

.

Lemma 2.7
.

[
KR08
, Theorems 1.6 and 1.7]

Suppose that

is squarefree and coprime to

and that

. If at least one of the following statements holds:

(1)

;

(2)

3\mid DN
, for all primes

we have

, and for at most one prime

3.
Gonalities

3.1.
Basic properties

Definition 3.1
.

Let
F
be a perfect field with algebraic closure
\bar{F}
, and let
X
be a curve over
F
. The
gonality

\gamma_{F}(X)
of
X
over
F
is the least degree of a map from
X
to the projective line
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{F}
over
F
. A map
X\to\mathbb{P}^{1}_{F}
over
F
of degree
\gamma_{F}(X)
is called a
gonal map
for
X
.

For
L/F
a field extension, we have the base change of
X
to
L

X_{L}:=X\otimes_{\operatorname{Spec}K}\operatorname{Spec}L,

and we denote the gonality of this base change by

\gamma_{L}(X):=\gamma_{L}(X_{L}).

In particular, the
geometric gonality
of
X
is defined to be
\gamma_{\bar{F}}(X)
. We recall the following general facts about gonalities which we will use often.

Proposition 3.2
.

[
Poonen
, Proposition A.1]

Let
X
be a curve of genus
g
over a field
F
.

(1)

If
L/F
is a field extension, then
\gamma_{L}(X)\leq\gamma_{F}(X)
.

(2)

If
F
is algebraically closed, and
L/F
is a field extension, then
\gamma_{L}(X)=\gamma_{F}(X)
.

(3)

If
g>1,
then
\gamma_{F}(X)\leq 2g-2
. For each
g>1
, there exists
F
and
X/F
for which equality holds.

(4)

If
X(F)\neq\varnothing
, then
\gamma_{F}(X)\leq g+1
. If further
g\geq 2
, then
\gamma_{F}(X)\leq g
(and, again, equality is possible for each
g
).

(5)

If
F
is algebraically closed, then
\gamma_{F}(X)\leq\lfloor\frac{g+3}{2}\rfloor
. Equality holds for a general curve of genus
g
over
F
.

(6)

If
\pi\colon X\to Y
is a dominant rational map of curves over
F
, then

\gamma_{F}(Y)\leq\gamma_{F}(X)\leq\textnormal{deg}(\pi)\cdot\gamma_{F}(Y).

3.2.
Gonalities and reduction

Let
X
be a curve over
\mathbb{Q}
, let
p
be a prime of good reduction for
X
, and let
q=p^{r}
where
r
is a positive integer. We let
\gamma_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(X)
denote the gonality of the base change of
X
to
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. In this setting, we have the inequality

\gamma_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(X)\leq\gamma_{\mathbb{Q}}(X),

as from a rational function on
X
over
\mathbb{Q}
corresponding to a gonal map one obtains via base change a rational function over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
of degree at most
\gamma_{\mathbb{Q}}(X)
.

In particular, the Shimura curve
X_{0}^{D}(N)
has good reduction at primes
p\nmid DN
, and, hence, if
q=p^{r}
, we get

\gamma_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(X_{0}^{D}(N))\leq\gamma_{\mathbb{Q}}(X_{0}^{D}(N)).

If
\widetilde{X}
is a curve over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with gonality
d
and
f
is a gonal map for
X
, then there are at most
d
points of degree
1
in the fiber of
f
above each of the
q+1
rational points on
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}
. Therefore, we have the following gonality bounds via point counts.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
X
be a curve over
\mathbb{Q}
, let
p
be a prime of good reduction for
X
, and let
q=p^{r}
for
r
a positive integer. We have

\gamma_{\mathbb{Q}}(X)\geq\gamma_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(X)\geq\dfrac{\#X(\mathbb{F}_{q})}{q+1}.

4.
Finite fields point counts

In this section, we describe
Algorithm

1.1
, used for our computations of point counts. It is a variation of those algorithms contained in
[
DLMS
]
and
[
DLMS25
]
.

By Ribet’s isogeny (
Theorem

2.3
), we can write the Jacobian of the Shimura curve
X_{0}^{D}(N)
in terms of isogeny factors of the Jacobian of the modular curve
X_{0}(DN)
.
In order to work with the latter object, we recall the following results from
[
DLMS
]
and
[
DLMS25
]
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a finite subset of the set of the Galois orbits of normalized eigenforms of weight 2 of
\bigcup_{n>0}\mathcal{S}_{2}^{\textnormal{new}}(\Gamma_{0}(n))
, and let
J:=\prod_{[f]\in\mathcal{F}}A_{f}^{m_{f}}
, where
A_{f}
is the abelian variety associated to Galois orbit
[f]
of
f
(see
[
DS05
, Definition 6.6.3]
) and
m_{f}>0
being an integer. Moreover, let
V_{\ell}=\mathrm{Ta}_{\ell}(J)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}}\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}
be the
\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}
-vector space associated to the Tate module of
J
for a prime
\ell
.
Then, the characteristic polynomial of
\mathrm{Frob}_{p}
acting on
V_{\ell}
is

\prod_{[f]\in\mathcal{F}}\prod_{h\in[f]}(x^{2}-a_{p}(h)x+p)^{m_{f}}\,,

where
a_{p}(h)
is the
p
-th Fourier coefficient of
h
.

Proof.

See
[
DLMS
, Proposition 4.4]
.
∎

To apply the previous lemma, we need to compute
a_{p}(h)
and
m_{f}
. For the former, we use the data available in
[
LMFDB
]
. For the latter, we use the following result.

Lemma 4.2
.

Given a modular curve
X_{0}(N)
, let
W
be its group of Atkin–Lehner involutions. For each subgroup
K\leq W
, the Jacobian of the corresponding quotient satisfies the following isogeny relation over
\mathbb{Q}

\mathrm{Jac}(X_{0}(N)/K)\sim\textstyle\prod_{f}A_{f}^{m_{f}},

where
f
ranges among the newforms of weight
2
and level
d
for divisors
d\mid N
and the multiplicities
m_{f}
are as follows: let
K^{\perp}
be the set of characters of
W
that are trivial on
K
and, for each
f
, denote
v:=\textnormal{ord}_{\ell}(N/d)
. Then

m_{f}:=\sum_{\chi\in K^{\perp}}\prod_{\ell^{e}\parallel N}\left(\tfrac{v+1}{2}+\varepsilon_{f,\ell^{e}}\chi(w_{\ell^{e}})\tfrac{1+(-1)^{v}}{4}\right),

with the indices
\ell^{e}
varying among all prime powers dividing exactly
N
and
\varepsilon_{f,\ell^{e}}
being the eigenvalue of the Atkin–Lehner involution
w_{\ell^{e}}
applied to
f
.

Proof.

This follows from
[
DLMS25
, Theorem 2.2]
, taking
n_{0}=N
and
n_{\textnormal{ns}}=1
.
∎

The steps of
Algorithm

1.1
for counting the number of points of
X_{0}^{D}(N)/W
over the finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, with
q=p^{k}
and
p\nmid N
, are as follows.

(1)

For every positive integer
n\mid N
, choose a basis
\mathcal{B}(nD)
of newforms of level
nD
and let
\mathcal{B}:=\bigcup_{n\mid N}\mathcal{B}(nD)
be the union of these bases.

(2)

For every
f\in\mathcal{B}
, compute the eigenvalues with respect to the Atkin–Lehner operators
w_{\ell^{e}}
, for
\ell^{e}||DN
(these data are available on the database
[
LMFDB
]
for
DN\leq 10000
) and, then, use them for computing the multiplicity
m_{f}
as in
Lemma

4.2
. We point out that we need to switch
\varepsilon_{f,\ell^{e}}
with
-\varepsilon_{f,\ell^{e}}
if
\ell\mid D
by
Theorem

2.3
.

(3)

Compute the Hecke eigenvalue
a_{p}(f)
for every
f\in\mathcal{B}
(these data are also available on the database
[
LMFDB
]
for
DN\leq 10000
).

(4)

If
k=1
, compute the Frobenius traces acting on
A_{f}
:

\mathrm{tr}(\mathrm{Frob}_{p}|A_{f})=\sum_{h\in[f]}a_{p}(h),

where
[f]
is the Galois orbit of
f
.

(5)

If
k>1
, compute the (complex) roots
\alpha(h)
and
\beta(h)
of the polynomial
x^{2}-a_{p}(h)x+p=0
, for each
h\in[f]
. Then compute

\mathrm{tr}(\mathrm{Frob}_{q}|A_{f})=\sum_{h\in[f]}\alpha(h)^{k}+\beta(h)^{k}.

(6)

Finally, compute

\#X_{0}^{D}(N)(\mathbb{F}_{q})=q+1-\sum_{[f]}m_{f}\mathrm{tr}(\mathrm{Frob}_{q}|A_{f}),

where the sum is taken over the distinct Galois orbits for
f\in\mathcal{B}
.

5.
Automorphisms of Shimura curves

Let
X/\mathbb{Q}
be a curve and let
p
be a prime of good reduction for
X
. The automorphism group
{\rm Aut}(X)
over
\mathbb{Q}
injects into the automorphism group of the reduction of
X
modulo
p

[
Liu
, Proposition 10.3.38]
. In particular, if
p\nmid DN
, then the automorphism group of the curve
X_{0}^{D}(N)
over
\mathbb{Q}
injects into that of its reduction modulo
p
.

Point counts over the extensions
\mathbb{F}_{q}
for
q
a power of
p
yield constraints on the automorphism group of a curve over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. For instance, a constraint of this type, specific to the curves
X_{0}^{D}(N)
, is seen in Part (4) of
Lemma

2.7
. In this section, we use our point counts algorithm to implement checks on the automorphism group of
X_{0}^{D}(N)
and compute evidence towards the conjecture mentioned in
Section

2.2
.

By
Proposition

2.5
, we know that when
N
is squarefree and coprime to
D
the automorphism group of every Atkin–Lehner quotient of
X_{0}^{D}(N)
consists of involutions. Of course, every Atkin–Lehner quotient of
X_{0}^{D}(N)
has a non-trivial Atkin–Lehner involution
except possibly
for the full quotient
X_{0}^{D}(N)^{*}:=X_{0}^{D}(N)/W_{0}(D,N)
. We expect that this star quotient has trivial automorphism group for all but finitely many pairs
(D,N)
, and the following instance of a result of González is a main tool we use to show this for a fixed pair.

Lemma 5.1
.

[
Gonzalez17
, Equation (2.2)]

Let
q=p^{r}
be a prime power, let
X
be a curve of genus
g\geq 2
over the finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, and suppose that

has a non-trivial automorphism of prime order
\ell
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. For each integer

A_{n}:=\begin{cases}X(\mathbb{F}_{q})\quad&\textnormal{ if }n=1,\\

Then

\sum_{n\geq 1}\textnormal{mod}\left(|A_{n}|,\ell\right)\leq\left\lfloor\dfrac{2g}{\ell-1}\right\rfloor+2,

where

by
\ell
.

6.
Tetragonal Shimura curves

Lemma

3.3
provides a way to obtain lower bounds on the gonality
\gamma_{\mathbb{Q}}(X_{0}^{D}(N))
of
X_{0}^{D}(N)
via point counts over finite fields. In this section, we apply this method in combination with other techniques to work towards determining all of the tetragonal and geometrically tetragonal Shimura curves
X_{0}^{D}(N)
. We begin by defining these notions and similarly making clear our convention for the term (sub-)hyperelliptic.

Definition 6.1
.

Let
F
be a perfect field. We call a curve
X
over
F
of genus
g(X)\geq 2

hyperelliptic
if there exists a degree
2
map from
X
to
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{F}
over
F
. We call
X

geometrically hyperelliptic
if its base change to
\bar{F}
is hyperelliptic.

We call
X

(geometrically) sub-hyperelliptic
if it is (geometrically) hyperelliptic or has genus
0
or
1
.

Definition 6.2
.

Let
F
be a perfect field. We call a curve
X
over
F
of genus
g(X)\geq 2

tetragonal
if there exists a degree
4
map from
X
to
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{F}
over
F
. We call
X

geometrically tetragonal
if its base change to
\bar{F}
is tetragonal.

We recall in the next two subsections the Castelnuovo–Severi criterion and some quick consequences as well as prior work on curves
X_{0}^{D}(N)
of low gonality. These provide methods to narrow lists of candidate curves
X_{0}^{D}(N)
prior to our application of point counts to a select finite list of candidates in the final subsection.

6.1.
The Castelnuovo–Severi inequality

The Castelnuovo–Severi inequality is one of our main tools in proving certain curves
X_{0}^{D}(N)
are not tetragonal. A proof of the version below, working over a perfect field, can be found in
[
KS24
, Theorem 14]
.

Theorem 6.3

(Castelnuovo–Severi)
.

Let
F
be a perfect field and let
X
,
Y
, and
Z
be curves over
F
. Let

\pi_{Y}\colon X\to Y\qquad\text{and}\qquad\pi_{Z}\colon X\to Z

be non-constant morphisms defined over
F
. Then either

g(X)\leq\textnormal{deg}(\pi_{Y})\cdot g(Y)+\textnormal{deg}(\pi_{Z})\cdot g(Z)+(\textnormal{deg}(\pi_{Y})-1)(\textnormal{deg}(\pi_{Z})-1),

or there exists a curve
X^{\prime}
over
F
and a morphism
X\to X^{\prime}
over
F
of degree greater than
1
through which both
\pi_{Y}
and
\pi_{Z}
factor.

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
X
be a tetragonal curve of genus
g
over a perfect field
F
. If
g\geq 10
, then
X
has a unique tetragonal map to
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{F}
.

Proof.

If there were two distinct tetragonal maps
f_{1},f_{2}\colon X\to\mathbb{P}^{1}_{F}
, then by
Theorem

6.3
they would have to factor through a common degree 2 map
h\colon X\to X^{\prime}
to a hyperelliptic curve
X^{\prime}
. From the uniqueness of the hyperelliptic involution on
X^{\prime}
, we get
f_{1}=f_{2}
.
∎

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
X
be a curve over a perfect field
F
with a non-trivial involution
\sigma\in\textnormal{Aut}_{F}(X)
. If
X
is tetragonal, then either
X
has genus
g\leq 9
or
X
admits a degree
2
cover of a sub-hyperelliptic curve over
F
.

Proof.

Suppose that we have a degree
4
map
f\colon X\to\mathbb{P}^{1}_{F}
over
F
. We then also have the map
f^{\sigma}\colon X\to\mathbb{P}^{1}_{F}
. If
X
does not admit a degree
2
cover of a sub-hyperelliptic curve over
F
, then the Castelnuovo–Severi inequality applied to
f
and
f^{\sigma}
forces
f=f^{\sigma}
or
g\leq 9
. The former cannot occur due to our hypothesis, as it would mean that
f
factors through the quotient
X/\langle\sigma\rangle
from which it follows that this quotient is sub-hyperelliptic.
∎

The next two results are useful in the application of
Lemma

6.5

Lemma 6.6
.

Let
X
be a curve over
\mathbb{Q}
and let
q=p^{r}
a power of a prime
p
of good reduction for
X
. If
X
is geometrically hyperelliptic, then

X(\mathbb{F}_{q})\leq 2q+2.

Proof.

If the statement said “hyperelliptic over
\mathbb{Q}
” rather than “geometrically hyperelliptic,” then it would follow from
Lemma

3.3
. The point here is that the uniqueness of a hyperelliptic involution makes the result true as stated. The hyperelliptic involution on
X
must be defined over
\mathbb{Q}
by uniqueness and, hence,
X
has a degree
2
map to a conic
C
over
\mathbb{Q}
(which may have no rational points). Therefore, we get a map from
X_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}
to a conic
\widetilde{C}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, as the automorphism group of
X
over
\mathbb{Q}
injects into that of the reduction modulo
p

[
Liu
, Proposition 10.3.38]
. If
\widetilde{C}(\mathbb{F}_{q})=\varnothing
, then the statement of the lemma holds easily. Otherwise,
\widetilde{C}\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}
, so
X_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}
is hyperelliptic over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and the argument from
Lemma

3.3
applies.
∎

Lemma 6.7
.

Let
X
be a curve over

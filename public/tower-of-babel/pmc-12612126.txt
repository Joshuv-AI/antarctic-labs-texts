Comparison of manifold learning algorithms for identifying geochemical anomalies associated with copper mineralization
Authors: Yuwen Min, Jiangnan Zhao, Yu Sui, Jiangtao Liu, Siyi Zou, Haotian Zhu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe Baiyin district, situated within the northern Qilian orogenic belt, hosts the largest concentration of copper mineral resources in Gansu Province, Northwestern China. Geochemical anomaly patterns are crucial indicators for mineral exploration in this region; however, they are frequently concealed within complex high-dimensional geochemical datasets. Moreover, the scarcity of labeled samples often restricts the effectiveness of supervised machine learning methods for accurate geochemical pattern recognition. This study utilizes unsupervised manifold learning algorithms, including Uniform Manifold Approximation and Projection (UMAP), t-Distributed Stochastic Neighbor Embedding (t-SNE), Isometric Mapping (Isomap), and Locally Linear Embedding (LLE) for identifying low-dimensional features closely associated with mineralization from high-dimensional geochemical datasets. The manifold learning algorithms were optimized by adjusting their key parameters through Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) test analysis to achieve optimal performance. The analytical results demonstrate that: (1) manifold learning algorithms exhibited superior performance over conventional factor analysis in accurately capturing complex nonlinear geochemical patterns; (2) The ROC curve and Area Under the Curve (AUC) values for the manifold learning algorithms were UMAP (0.711), t-SNE (0.693), Isomap (0.691), and LLE (0.652), indicating that the UMAP algorithm is the most suitable for identifying geochemical anomaly patterns in the study area; the prediction-area(P-A) analysis further confirmed the UMAP-derived anomalies with a relatively higher prediction efficiency; (3) manifold learning-driven high-probability zones exhibit significant spatial correlations with known mineral deposits, fault structures, and ore-bearing volcanic rock formations. These results highlight the superior capability of manifold learning techniques in extracting meaningful non-linear geochemical anomalies for further exploration of mineral resources.Keywords: Manifold learning, UMAP, Geochemical patterns, Nonlinear dimensionality reduction, Baiyin districtSubject terms: Solid Earth sciences, Geochemistry

Introduction

Geochemical surveys have been extensively employed in mineral prospecting and deposit-type targeting1–6. Following the launch of China’s National Regional Geochemical Mapping Program in 1978, systematic surveys have accumulated comprehensive stream sediment geochemical datasets7. Efficient extraction of mineralization related geochemical anomalies from these datasets to optimize exploration targeting has emerged as a research focus. As diagnostic signatures of ore-forming processes, geochemical anomalies have proven instrumental in discovering > 70% of China’s newly identified mineral deposits over recent decades8. Much emphasis has been put on mathematical models for recognition of geochemical anomalies associated with mineralization9–11. Methodological advancements have evolved through three paradigms: (1)classical statistical approaches utilizing probability graphs, univariate/multivariate analysis to establish geochemical thresholds separating background and anomalous populations12,13; (2) Fractal and multifractal models (e.g., concentration-area, spectrum-area, singularity analysis) addressing scale-invariant spatial patterns of element dispersion14,15; (3) Machine learning-driven techniques decoding nonlinear elemental associations indicative of concealed mineralization16–18.

The emergence of the big data era has significantly propelled the widespread adoption of machine learning/deep learning techniques for multivariate geochemical anomaly identification and characterization13,18. Contemporary methodologies encompass neural networks, random forests, attention-based autoencoders, restricted Boltzmann machines, and extreme learning machines19. Nevertheless, high-dimensional geochemical datasets pose critical challenges to machine learning implementations. The curse of dimensionality frequently induces model overfitting, necessitating exponential growth in training data volume with dimension expansion20. Dimensionality reduction techniques have thus become indispensable processing modules in modern geochemical data analysis, enabling researchers to handle high-dimensional datasets, identify mineralization patterns, and optimize exploration workflows. Over the past decade, both linear and nonlinear methods have widely used to detect and interpret geochemical anomalies. Early approaches relied on linear techniques like Principal Component Analysis (PCA) and Factor Analysis to simplify geochemical datasets. These methods project data into orthogonal subspaces, retaining maximum variance while eliminating redundancy. Algorithms such as UMAP, t-SNE, and Isomap demonstrate superior capability in capturing nonlinear relationships, significantly outperforming traditional linear methods in anomaly detection tasks21–23. For example, Shi (2023) utilized t-SNE/RF integration to achieve gold mineralization pattern recognition24; Balamurali (2022) demonstrated t-SNE’s superior accuracy in posterior probability estimation compared to traditional PCA22; Zhang et al. (2024) applied UMAP to rare earth element exploration, revealing its significant performance advantages for mineralization anomaly detection25, etc.

In this study, we systematically integrated regional geological datasets and 1:200,000-scale stream sediment geochemical data in the Baiyin district for mineralization-related geochemical anomaly detection. A total of 12 elements (Ag, Ba, Cd, Co, Cu, Hg, Mn, Mo, Ni, Pb, Sb, and Zn) were selected for analysis based on their (1) genetic association with the Baiyin VMS) deposit system and (2) their role as pathfinder elements for mineralization—including Cu, Pb, and Zn as primary ore-forming metals, and Ag, Sb, and Hg as distal geochemical indicators26.These signature elements formed the input matrix for a comparative framework evaluating dimensionality reduction performance. We systematically assessed four manifold learning algorithms (Isomap, LLE, t-SNE, UMAP) against conventional factor analysis. The aims are to (1) identify geochemical signatures of the Cu mineral deposit sought, (2) characterize distribution patterns of stream geochemical elements, and (3) compare the performance of different dimensionality reduction methods in mapping significant geochemical anomalies for further exploration.

Geological background and data

Geological setting

The North Qilian orogenic belt is one of the most significant metallogenic belt of volcanogenic massive sulfide deposits (VMS) in China27–29. This belt is separated from the Alashan Block by the Longshoushan fault to the north, bound to the south by the Qaidam and the Songpan-Ganzi terranes, whereas to the northwest by the Altyn-Tagh strike-slip tectonic belt, to the the east by the North Qinling Mountains(Fig. 1). It generally extends in a NW direction, approximately 1200 km in length and 100 ~ 300 km in width. This region has undergone complex tectonic movement of a typical trench-arc-basin system leading to different geological units developed from north to south including an island arc, subduction complex and oceanic crustal fragments. Numerous submarine volcanic eruptions occurred during the volcanic island arc stage, providing favorable conditions for ore-forming hydrothermal convection. Thus, this area becomes an important copper polymetallic metallogenic belt containing many VMS deposits with high economic value29–31.

Regional geological map30(a)schematic map showing the major tectonic units of central China; (b) map showing major geology units of northern Qilian Orogenic Belt.

The outcrop strata of Baiyin district from the oldest to the youngest, mainly include the Middle to Lower Cambrian Gaolan Group, the Middle Cambrian Baiyinchang Group, the Lower Ordovician Yingou Group, the Middle and Upper Ordovician Zhongbao Group, the Lower Silurian Mayinggou Group, and other strata of the Triassic, Cretaceous, and Quaternary (Fig. 2). The Lower Paleozoic strata are distributed in the north and middle, the Mesozoic strata are distributed in the east and south central, and the Cenozoic strata are scattered throughout the region, especially in the northeast and southwest. The VMS deposits in this area is dominantly hosted by bimodal volcanic rocks in Baiyin district, which is composed of Late Paleozoic to Cambrian acidic volcanic rocks and spilite keratophyre. Three cycles can be recognized as follows: the first cycle with the intermediatebasic volcanic series, the second cycle with the acidic quartz keratophyre series, and the third cycle with the intermediate-basic volcanic rocks. There are also many types of intrusive rocks, ranging from subvolcanic to magmatic rocks in the early stage, and dyke rocks in the later stage. Ultrabasic and basic rocks are mainly distributed in the northwest, with relatively small exposed area and unrelated to mineralization. Acidic and intermediate acidic intrusive rocks are well-developed, with rock types mainly including granite, plagioclase granite, granodiorite, quartz diorite, etc., which are favorable for Au, Cu mineralization.

Sketch geological map of Baiyin district26.

The faults in this region are well-developed and dominantly strike EW, SN, NE, and NWW sets, which are mainly characterized by compressional torsion or thrust nappe. The NWW trending faults are the major trend cutting through various lithological units. They control the structural pattern of the area and the distribution of early Paleozoic volcanic rock belts in the northern Qilian Mountains. The intersections between NWW and NE trending faults are favorable locations for the formation of volcanic type massive sulfide deposits.

Ore geology

This district witnessed strong tectonic activity and magmatism, which brought a lot of metallogenic materials and heat energy needed for the activation and enrichment of the metallogenic elements32. More than 160 mineral deposits have been discovered in the area, including 53 non-ferrous metal deposits, 4 precious metal deposits, and more than 80 ferrous metal deposit. This area hosts a number of VMS copper deposits associated with felsic volcanic domes in major volcanic belts. The Baiyinchang ore field, located in Baiyin City, Gansu Province, is the most significant VMS deposit within the North Qilian orogenic belt. The field comprises five prominent deposits: Zheyaoshan and Huoyanshan, Xiaotieshan, Sigequan, and Tongchanggou, of which the Zheyaoshan deposit is the largest. These deposits are primarily hosted within bimodal volcanic rocks, with an age range of 472–440 Ma for the acidite and ~ 465 Ma for the mafic rocks by zircon U-Pb dating33. Methodology

The methodological framework used for this work is shown in Fig. 3.We use the factor analysis and manifold learning methods of LLE, t-SNE, Isomap and UMAP to reduce the dimensions of the input data and extract invariant features. The low-dimensional features are used to identify geochemical anomalies associated with copper mineralization. Manifold learning is a branch of machine learning that focuses on handling high-dimensional data to explore the hidden low-dimensional manifold structure without changing the distribution of data points34,35. Its core idea is to assume that high-dimensional data samples are distributed on an underlying low-dimensional manifold and can be described by a lower-dimensional structure, even though the data is in a high-dimensional space. It aims to discover the intrinsic low-dimensional representations of the data, so that the data can be better analyzed, visualized, and understood while maintaining its geometric features.

Flow chart of this study.

Locally linear embedding algorithm (LLE)

As a nonlinear spectral dimensionality reduction technique that can be applied to feature extraction and manifold embedding, LLE attempts to maintain the local structure of the data in the embedding space. The low-dimensional embedding space should have close points that correspond to each other in the high-dimensional input space. To achieve this, each point in the high-dimensional input space is reconstructed using a linear weighted combination of its neighbors; the lower-dimensional embedding space is then constructed using the same reconstruction weights. The LLE algorithm mainly consists of three steps: (1) determine the neighbors: for each point xi (in a high-dimensional space), find its k nearest neighbors, which is usually achieved by calculating the Euclidean distance; (2) construct local reconstruction weights: each point xi is represented by its neighbors in a way that minimizes the reconstruction error. Isometric mapping (Isomap)

The Isomap algorithm is to maintain the geometric structure of the data by constructing the geodesic distance instead of the Euclidean distance between the data points, and then map the data to a low-dimensional space. The geodesic distance is the length of the shortest path between two points on the possibly curvy manifold. The Isomap algorithm mainly consists of four steps: (1) construct the neighborhood graph: The points that fall inside the radius defined are neighboring points. An adjacency graph using the Euclidean distance between adjacent points is K-nearest neighbors; (2) calculate the geodesic distance: the essence is to calculate the shortest path between sample points in the adjacency graph; (3) multidimensional scaling (MDS): taking the geodesic distance matrix as input, the multidimensional scaling algorithm is applied to obtain a low-dimensional embedding. MDS aims to find points in a low-dimensional space such that the distances between them are as close as possible to the original geodesic distances; (4) optimization and iteration: iterative methods can be used to optimize the low-dimensional embedding, such as by minimizing a stress function or other objective function. The iterative process can help further improve the quality of the embedding.

t-Distributed stochastic neighbor embedding algorithm (t-SNE)

The idea of SNE is to consider every point as neighbours of other points with a certain probability, with the closer points being neighbors with a higher probability22. SNE uses the Gaussian distribution for neighbours in both the input and embedding spaces. t-SNE employs the Student-t distribution in the embedding space and the Gaussian distribution in the input space. The use of the Student-t distribution is primarily due to its heavier tails, which better accommodate the preservation of local structures and relationships in high-dimensional data when mapping to a lower-dimensional space23. Results

Factor analysis

The study area was divided into 251 × 385 cells in ArcGIS, and data points were obtained through interpolation tools. Factor analysis was employed to process the stream sediment data in the study area, aiming to better explore mineralization element associations. As can be observed from the correlation Kaiser-Meyer-Olkin (KMO) and Bartlett test between variables, the KMO measure of sampling adequacy demonstrated appropriate data suitability (KMO = 0.823), exceeding Kaiser’s recommended threshold of 0.6. Bartlett’s test of sphericity further confirmed the factorability of the data (p < 0.001), with the significance level falling below the α = 0.05 criterion. Hence, the combined results confirm that the dataset meets the statistical assumptions required for factor analysis. Principal component analysis (PCA) with varimax rotation extracted three factor components based on the Kaiser criterion (eigenvalue > 1) and a cumulative variance contribution rate of 60%. As shown in Table 3, the multivariate analysis identifies a Cu-associated elemental assemblage (Co-Cu-Ni-Mn-Zn) accounting for 32.5% of the total variance, with its spatial distribution pattern visualized through factor score mapping (Fig. 4). This metallogenic signature of elemental assemblages is spatially and genetically associated with volcanogenic massive sulfide (VMS) deposits formed in back-arc extensional settings, where Cu-Zn enrichment typically associates with hydrothermal systems Within these environments, Cu-Zn enrichment is typically associated with hydrothermal systems, whereas Co-Ni-Mn anomalies reflect contributions derived from mafic-intermediate volcanic rocks in the mineralizing environment26,28,31.

Parameter setting of manifold learning

In manifold learning, parameter selection heavily influences algorithmic performance and result interpretation, with parameter sensitivity varying substantially across different techniques. To address this, in our applications, we conducted numerous trials and adjustments to identify optimal parameters tailored to our geochemical exploration objectives. Parameter optimization prioritized features indicative of copper mineralization potential, incorporating known deposit locations as spatial constraints.

UMAP

The UMAP algorithm is highly sensitive to parameter settings, and this study focuses on three critical parameters: the number of neighbors (n_neighbors), the minimum distance (min_dist), and the distance metric. Among these, the n_neighbors is used to construct the number of approximate nearest neighbors of an initial high-dimensional graph, effectively controlling how UMAP balances local and global structures. A smaller value will push UMAP to focus more on the local structure, while a larger value will push UMAP to represent the global structure. The min_dist governs how closely the UMAP groups the points together. A lower value will result in a tighter embedding, while a higher value will cause UMAP to pack the points together more loosely, and focus on preserving the broad topology. By adjusting this parameter, one can control the literal distance between data points, and it can affect the distribution and density of data points after dimensionality reduction.The metric defines a formula for calculating the distance between points. In this distance metric, rather than measuring the distance between two points or the distance diagonally as a single, it measures the distance between two points variable by variable, and adds these small journeys together. Selecting different metrics can affect the calculation method of point distance in the dimensionality reduction process, thereby influencing the final dimensionality reduction result.

The core optimization objective of UMAP is to minimize the cross-entropy loss function between the high-dimensional data space and its low-dimensional embedding. This loss function aligns the topological structure of the original data by optimizing the coordinates of the low-dimensional embedding. However, hyperparameters (e.g., n_neighbors) must be selected using external evaluation metrics to ensure the dimensionality reduction output is suitable for specific downstream tasks, such as anomaly detection. In this study, the following metrics guide parameter optimization: (1) Fidelity: Quantifies the ability of the dimensionality reduction to preserve the original neighborhood structure, directly reflecting the optimization efficacy of the cross-entropy loss. Higher fidelity correlates with robust retention of local data topology; (2) LOF Variance: Computes the variance of Local Outlier Factor (LOF) scores derived from the dimensionality-reduced embedding. This metric evaluates the stability of subsequent anomaly detection results, where lower variance indicates consistent outlier identification under perturbations. By maximizing Fidelity and minimizing LOF Score Variance to select the optimal dimension, minimum distance, and distance metric, while balancing the LOF mean and variance to determine the neighborhood parameter (n_neighbors), this approach indirectly constrains the distribution of the sum in the cross-entropy loss. This ensures the dimensionality reduction results adhere to the theoretical objective of topological preservation while fulfilling the practical requirements of downstream tasks. Consequently, the system identifies the optimal parameters: n_neighbors = 5, distance metric = Euclidean, and minimum distance = 0.3 (Fig. 5).

t-SNE

The parameters of the t-SNE algorithm include the perplexity, learning_rate, number of iterations (n_iter), and random_state. The Perplexity defines the importance attached to the local structure of the data. A higher perplexity will make the algorithm pay more attention to the global structure, while a lower perplexity will pay more attention to the local structure. The learning_rate defines the convergence rate of the algorithm. A large learning rate may cause the algorithm to be unstable, while a small learning rate may require more iterations to converge.

The core optimization objective of t-SNE is to minimize the Kullback-Leibler divergence (KL Divergence) between the high-dimensional space and the low-dimensional embedding, which achieves nonlinear manifold mapping by matching the similarity probabilities of data distributions. Parameter selection must balance both KL divergence convergence and adaptability to downstream tasks. This study adopts the following dual-criteria optimization: KL divergence convergence is monitored by observing the descending curve during training, with the iteration count selected at the curvature inflection point; credibility is assessed by training a random forest classifier (mineral deposits/background) based on dimensionality reduction results, using AUC-ROC to evaluate dimensional validity. Through grid search, the optimal parameter combination is determined: perplexity is selected based on the extremum point of the joint KL divergence-credibility response surface, while the learning rate is adjusted according to gradient update stability. The final parameters are set to perplexity 50 and learning rate 25 (Fig. 6).

Parameter Selection for the t-SNE algorithm: (a) perplexity; (b) learning_rate.

In both Isomap and LLE algorithms, parameter settings have relatively minor impacts on algorithm performance and results. Here, only the neighborhood parameter (n_neighbors) is configured.

LLE

The core optimization objective of LLE is to minimize the local linear reconstruction error, preserving local geometric structures through neighborhood linear combinations. Parameter selection must address the following trade-offs: (1) Reconstruction Error:, Decreases with larger neighborhood size but may introduce over-smoothing; (2) Geological Continuity: Evaluated via variogram analysis of key elements (e.g., Cu) to assess spatial continuity. A Pareto front optimization is applied to balance the regularization parameter (reg) and neighborhood size (n_neighbors). The maximum regularization coefficient is selected when the error increase remains below 10%. The final determined neighborhood size is 30 (Fig. 8).

Discussion

Geochemical anomalies identification using manifold learning algorithms

Here, we reduce the data dimension to two and denote the features as UMAP_1 and UMAP_2. The same applies for the following methods of t-SNE, Isomap, and LLE. As shown in Fig. 9, we crossplot the sensitive features extracted by manifold learning algorithms, and the scattered points are colored by Cu elements. Through comparison, it can be observed that, by using a single feature after dimension reduction, UMAP-1, t-SNE-2, Isomap-2, and LLE-1 can better distinguish the high-copper and low-copper anomaly zones. It also can be proved from the Spearman correlation coefficients (Table 5). Spearman correlation coefficients were calculated to quantitatively compare the relationships between the low-dimensional features generated by different manifold learning algorithms and all geochemical elements. Among the analyzed elements, Cu, Zn, Co, Mn, and Ni show strong correlations with the low-dimensional features of UMAP-1, t-SNE-2, Isomap-2, and LLE-1 produced by manifold learning algorithms, whereas Ag, Ba, and Pb exhibit comparatively weaker relationships. The results indicate that these low-dimensional features effectively capture the distribution of primary sulfides (e.g., chalcopyrite, sphalerite) in VMS or similar hydrothermal systems, reflecting first-order controls on mineralization, such as fluid composition (e.g., chloride vs. sulfur ligands), temperature gradients, and source rock chemistry.

As shown in Fig. 10, the probability maps, which depict low-dimensional features, were generated using the manifold learning algorithms UMAP-1, t-SNE-2, Isomap-2, and LLE-1. The maps employ a scale where warm hues (red-orange) denote high-probability mineralization zones, contrasting with cool tones (blue) indicating low prospectivity areas for VMS deposits. The results also show that the areas with high probability are consistent with known deposits. Spatial analysis reveals more than 70% of documented copper deposits fall within the top 25% probability contours, demonstrating strong predictive capacity. High-probability zones also present spatial consistency with volcanic complexes. As illustrated in Fig. 11, when stream sediment data points are categorized into those within volcanic zones and those outside, samples from volcanic zones exhibit significantly higher UMAP_1 values and Cu-Zn concentrations compared to non-volcanic zone samples. This further demonstrates that the geochemical anomalies identified by manifold learning are closely associated with volcanic lithologies. This spatial correlation corroborates established metallogenic models for the in the north Qilian orogen, where copper mineralization shows genetic association with the Cambrian to Ordovician volcanosedimentary sequences26,29. The spatial distribution patterns of high probability areas reveal some similarities and differences among four models. UMAP and t-SNE exhibit highly similar spatial distribution patterns in the study area. They correspond to the known volcanic and hydrothermal deposits occurring in the Cambrian and Ordovician strata along the two NWW-trending zones centered on Laohushan-Daquangou and Fuqiangbao-Baiyinchang. The anomaly distribution is consistent with the overall structural trend of the region. Compared to the UMAP and t-SNE algorithms, the Isomap and LLE algorithms focus on preserving local structures in the data. They exhibit similar spatial distribution patterns in the study area and identify anomalies consistent with the NWW-trending faults centered on Fuqiangbao-Shiqingtong-Baiyinchang. Isomap identifies intermediate-acidic volcanic rocks within the Cambrian-Ordovician system, which are important ore-controlling layers for volcanic massive sulfide deposits, such as quartz keratophyre tuff. However, it fails to identify known deposits in the Devonian sedimentary deposits centered on Mijiashan-Shimenchuan-Dayingshui and the granite veins to the south. LLE yields similar results to Isomap, effectively identifying anomalies at the locations of known volcanic-hydrothermal deposits in the Laohushan-Zhuzuiyaba area. These deposits are distributed in volcanic eruption centers, located in volcanic depressions rich in tuffaceous sediments and siliceous rocks36,37.

Performance comparison of manifold learning algorithms

Model performance was quantitatively evaluated using ROC curves, with AUC scores guiding the selection of parameters that maximized predictive accuracy for Cu-associated element assemblages. Positive samples were defined as known Cu mineralization occurrences, while negative samples were derived from barren regions with similar geological backgrounds but no recorded mineralization. In practical applications, multiple factors such as geological constraints, non-ore-bearing strata, distance from ore-controlling structures, and historical exploration results need to be comprehensively considered. To avoid spatial bias, the negative samples in this paper are based on random sampling to achieve category balance, and combined with the ore-controlling factors in the study area, the following control conditions are set for random points: (1) being at a location more than five kilometers away from the ore points; (2) being in a stratum that is not favorable for ore prospecting; (3) being far away from ore-controlling structures. Meanwhile, the robustness of the geological interpretation of the AUC index is ensured. The larger the area under the ROC curve (AUC value), the better the performance of the method. It can be seen that algorithm performance ordered as UMAP (0.711) > t-SNE(0.693) > LLE (0.691) > Isomap (0.652) based on AUC values (Fig. 12a). As shown in Fig. 12b, the Prediction-Area (P-A) plot was also used to evaluate the effectiveness of predictive models by analyzing the relationship between predicted mineral potential and the spatial area occupied by different prediction classes38. The quantities demonstrate that the UMAP model with a prediction rate of 75% for mineral deposit locations, is better than the t-SNE model with a prediction rate of 72% for mineral deposit locations, LLE with a prediction rate of 66%, and Isomap model with a prediction rate of 65%. Spearman correlation coefficients also shows that among the algorithms, UMAP_1 and t-SNE_2 exhibits the highest correlation coefficients for Cu(0.814) and Zn(0.824) as well as Cu(0.806) and Zn(0.821), respectively, which demonstrates superior capability in capturing the global data structure and shows better performance in characterizing the distribution patterns of main ore-forming elements. Therefore, the low-dimensional features extracted by UMAP and t-SNE demonstrate a stronger ability to capture the intrinsic structure of the data and outperform in characterizing the underlying geochemical anomaly patterns in this research. Even under the condition that the known copper deposits are not used as labels, the four models still obtains an AUC value with great than 0.65, which shows that manifold learning algorithms have a certain effect on the identification of geochemical anomalies related to copper mineralization. The UMAP model achieves superior predictive capability with an highest AUC of 0.71, indicating UMAP maintains robust performance even without supervised labels from known deposits, demonstrating its exceptional unsupervised learning capacity for mineralization detection (Zhang et al., 2024). The Isomap model presents the relatively poor results with a lowest AUC of 0.65, suggesting that it is probably not very suitable for accurately identifying geochemical anomalies in the study region. The possible reason is that during the dimensionality reduction process of geochemical data, performance of Isomap heavily depends on the choice of parameter, such as neighborhood size; besides, noisy data can lead to incorrect neighborhood graphs, skewing geodesic distances and embeddings21,25,39.

Comparison between manifold learning and factor analysis

As illustrated in Fig. 3, the F1 anomalies exhibit spatial coincidence with known VMS deposits and volcanic rocks, indicating their capacity in deriving multi-element geochemical signatures to reveal the presence of mineralization. However, when compared to the ROC curve results of FA (0.608), the four manifold learning algorithms demonstrate superior performance in identifying geochemical anomalies. As a Linear dimensionality reduction model, FA tries to identify latent factors explaining correlations among observed geochemical variables, assuming linear relationships and Gaussian distributions. Its linear algorithm limits its ability to extract complex information from multi-element geochemical data, especially in geochemical identification that have a highly nonlinear relationship between geochemical elements and ore-forming. Another shortcoming is that FA struggles to compress the data to very low dimensions with a high explained variance ratio.

Manifold learning algorithms are non-linear dimensionality reduction technique that preserves local and global data structure37. Critically, geochemical data inherently exhibit nonlinear characteristics due to multi-scale fractal structures (e.g., self-similar element distribution patterns), polymodal concentration distributions, and chaotic dynamics governing element migration (e.g., fluid percolation in fractal fault networks). Conventional factor analysis (FA), reliant on global linear correlation assumptions, fails to decouple such nonlinear complexities, potentially obscuring subtle anomalies and multi-process signals (e.g., superposition of hydrothermal alteration and supergene oxidation). Therefore, manifold learning algorithms can be more effective in explore more complex and non-linear geochemical patterns without distributional assumptions of datasets, especially detecting subtle geochemical anomalies. For example, FA cannot identify anomalies in the southern region of the Baiyinchang, which has been proven to have good prospecting potential. Nevertheless, from the perspective of interpretability, FA reduce variables to a few factors, which can be linked to geological processes. The result can be easily explained, such as F1 interpreted as geochemical element combinations related to copper mineralization and volcanic rocks in this research. It is useful to identify element associations linked to mineralization. FA is preferred when the goal is to test geological hypotheses and data relationships are linear and additive. The FA anomalies are inferred from residuals or deviations in factor scores, which has stronger geological interpretability. Manifold learning is preferred when the system exhibits nonlinear interactions and high-dimensional data require dimensionality reduction without losing subtle patterns39–41. The focus of manifold learning is predictive modeling (e.g., prospectivity mapping) but lacks direct geological explainability with a black-box nature.

Conclusions

This study investigates geochemical anomaly recognition in Baiyin area using manifold learning techniques. Factor analysis was first employed to extract element associations linked to copper polymetallic mineralization. Subsequently, the spatial distribution patterns derived from these element associations were systematically compared with dimensionality reduction results generated by four manifold learning algorithms: UMAP, t-SNE, Isomap, and LLE. Main conclusions are summarized as follows: (1)The low-dimensional features of nonlinear dimensionality reduction techniques (UMAP, t-SNE, Isomap, LLE) have a stronger correlation with copper associated geochemcial elements, and exhibit enhanced capability over conventional factor analysis in resolving spatially coherent geochemical anomalies; (2) The low-dimensional features of manifold learning algorithms UMAP achieves optimal anomaly recognition, attributed to its dual preservation of local/global data structures during the dimensionality reduction of geochemical element data; and (3) Although manifold learning demonstrates superior performance in delineating geochemical anomalies and facilitates the integration of machine learning for nonlinear mineral prospectivity modeling, critical challenges persist in establishing interpretable correlations between the low-dimensional manifolds and their original high-dimensional geochemical signatures, necessitating further research into explainable dimensionality reduction frameworks.

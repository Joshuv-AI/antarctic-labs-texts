Physics-consistent constraint probabilistic modeling and prospective risk assessment method for intelligent decision-making in flight test points
Authors: Tianchang Liu, Chen Zhao, Ling Pu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Addressing the real-time monitoring challenges posed by high dynamics, strong nonlinearity, and observational uncertainty in flight test envelope boundary missions, traditional threshold methods and pure data-driven models struggle to balance identification accuracy, physical consistency, and predictive warning capability. To this end, this paper proposes a CPSSMF aimed at providing interpretable and calibratable unified probabilistic evidence for safety-critical decision-making. Based on sequential Bayesian inference, the framework achieves closed-loop monitoring through three coupled modules: (1) PSA module, which injects physical constraints into posterior distribution shaping through endogenous potential functions, combined with output-side safety masking and multi-source anomaly detection to suppress non-physical state jumps; (2) HPRF module, which performs multi-step rolling prediction based on corrected posteriors, outputting trend risks within a fixed time horizon to achieve quantitative trade-off between lead time and false alarm rate; (3) EGUAQ module, which dynamically evaluates data value using posterior entropy to support active verification. Evaluation results based on simulated flight stall test data demonstrate that CPSSMF maintains high phase identification accuracy (Acc 0.969, F1 0.785) while achieving an average warning lead time of approximately 3.34 s under the same false alarm constraints, significantly outperforming baseline methods. Furthermore, under sensor contradiction injection and out-of-distribution disturbance scenarios, the method exhibits excellent robustness and stability. This study establishes an interpretable analysis chain integrating situational awareness, risk quantification, and anomaly diagnosis, effectively enhancing the engineering applicability of flight test safety monitoring.

Introduction

Flight testing runs throughout the entire process of aircraft development verification, airworthiness certification, and performance and stability quality assessment, serving as a key source of evidence connecting design assumptions, simulation inferences, and real flight environments1,2. Compared to conventional airline operations, test points are often located near envelope boundaries and accompanied by high dynamic maneuvers and strong nonlinear coupling, presenting typical characteristics of short test windows, long operational chains, strong handling constraints, and rigid risk boundaries3,4, thus imposing safety-critical requirements on online monitoring and decision support5. As flight test missions gradually evolve from pure verification to an emphasis on both verification and exploration, test organization methods and risk management paradigms are evolving synchronously6,7. Relying solely on planned execution and post-event review can no longer cover the real-time situational awareness and rapid response requirements during high-intensity test point execution8,9. Engineering practice requires systems to reliably identify the current phase of test points and provide quantitative characterization of short-term future risk trends within sampling-level time scales based on multi-channel measurements, supporting operational decisions such as advancing, maintaining, repeating, retreating, or terminating10–12. The core of such requirements is not merely pursuing marginal improvements in phase classification accuracy, but demanding that online outputs simultaneously possess trustworthiness, temporal continuity, interpretability, and calibrability13,14. This involves suppressing high-frequency oscillations in phase identification that cause alarm jitter and handling interference, while avoiding overconfident erroneous suggestions when sensor anomalies15, channel conflicts, and out-of-distribution conditions occur. The system should present the cost trade-offs among lead time, false alarms, and missed detections in a reviewable manner, enabling threshold calibration, risk budget al.location, and hierarchical strategies for test point types to have consistent evidence basis and traceable analysis chains16–20.

Regarding real-time monitoring and warning around test points, related research and engineering practices have broadly formed three types of technical routes, each with their respective boundary conditions21–23. The first is threshold and rule-driven methods, with typical implementations including key parameter thresholds, combination criteria, envelope protection logic, and control law protection24. The advantages of this type of method lie in transparent mechanisms, strong interpretability, ease of review and certification, and easy integration into existing test procedures, thus maintaining a dominant position for a long time25. However, under high dynamic and strong noise conditions, threshold triggering often exhibits lag and oscillation, is more sensitive to single-channel anomalies, and discrete boundary triggering in state transition regions amplifies phase distortion, making it difficult to naturally form trend risk quantification for fixed prediction horizons, and even more difficult to systematically provide disposal lead time26,27. The second is data-driven time series modeling methods, such as models based on recurrent networks, temporal convolution, and attention mechanisms and their variants, which can learn complex correlation structures from multi-channel time series and typically achieve high performance in offline identification or relatively stable distribution conditions28–31, but still face consistency and credibility issues in safety-critical high dynamic flight missions32. One issue is the lack of explicit physical consistency and process constraints, making it easy to learn statistically correlated but physically infeasible mappings, producing mechanistically distorted outputs under boundary conditions33. The second is insufficient robustness and uncertainty characterization for out-of-distribution inputs, faults, and drift, potentially leading to overconfidence and passing erroneous judgments to the disposal layer with high confidence34,35. The third is unclear analysis paths, making it difficult to provide analyzable explanations of trigger causes, evidence sources, and confidence levels for effectiveness analysis and safety management chains, thereby constraining engineering implementation and continuous iteration36. The third is probabilistic graphical models and Bayesian filtering frameworks, such as hidden Markov models, switching state space models, and particle filters. This type of method naturally fits online sequential inference and can express uncertainty with probabilistic semantics, with inference chains that are more traceable and convenient for collaboration with decision rules37,38. However, if relying solely on statistical emission and transition while lacking task-relevant physical feasibility constraints, posterior probabilities may accumulate mass on infeasible interpretations and produce multi-modal competition and phase jumps, thereby weakening alarm stability and risk prediction reliability39. If empirical correction is further overlaid at the output end, it easily forms frequent pullbacks and temporal discontinuities, making it difficult for the system to provide verifiable risk evidence and affecting human-machine trust maintenance. Overall, the key bottleneck does not come from insufficient model stacking, but from the lack of a methodological framework that can unify phase identification, credible gating, prospective risk, and data closed-loop within the same online probabilistic semantics and analysis chain, enabling the system to output stable, interpretable, and calibratable structured situational results under conditions of enhanced noise, conflicting observations, and out-of-distribution perturbations40–42. Meanwhile, recent advances in graph-based domain adaptation techniques offer a highly attractive avenue for further reducing the need for target domain data, for instance, hierarchical graph structures that capture local-to-global topological relationships have demonstrated strong capabilities in semi-supervised domain adaptation43. Furthermore, leveraging Graph Convolutional Networks to drive multi-layer perceptron generalization via cross-domain knowledge distillation44 provides a robust framework for transferring complex, multi-channel sensory correlations from a well-tested aircraft platform to a newly developed prototype.

Based on the aforementioned background, this paper targets safety-critical and process-constrained test point scenarios, with the research problem being to construct a unified online framework that can stably estimate test point phases at sampling-level time scales while synchronously expressing uncertainty, output calibrated trend risks and quantifiable disposal lead times within future fixed time domains, and provide interpretable anomaly evidence and trustworthy gating strategies when observational contradictions, sensor drift, or out-of-distribution disturbances occur, while simultaneously driving uncertainty-based data value assessment and verification acquisition to form a safe and controllable closed-loop improvement path.

The main innovations of this paper are: (1) Unified probabilistic analysis chain output: centered on stage posterior distributions, unifying stage identification, uncertainty expression, risk quantification, and anomaly diagnosis, avoiding stage distortion and semantic fragmentation caused by hard decisions, supporting analyzable and traceable online decision-making in safety-critical scenarios; (2) Online inference and interpretable analysis with physical consistency constraints: injecting physical and feasibility constraints in an endogenous manner into sequential inference, combined with output-end safety shielding to form interpretable corrective evidence, improving temporal consistency and robustness against channel contradictions and out-of-distribution perturbations; (3) Calibratable forward-looking risk analysis: proposing fixed-horizon windowed trend risk assessment to achieve systematic characterization and engineering calibration of lead-time versus false alarm/missed detection trade-offs; simultaneously driving active review and data acquisition through uncertainty indicators such as entropy, constructing an executable continuous iterative closed loop under safety gating.

Model and methods

Overall model: constrained probability state space intelligent monitoring framework

Constrained Probability State Space Monitoring Framework CPSSMF for Online Intelligent Decision-Making of Flight Test Points. The overall objective is to employ sequential Bayesian posterior as a unified semantic carrier under envelope boundaries and high dynamic conditions to form analyzable probabilistic evidence supporting safety-critical handling. The framework integrates three coupled functionalities sharing the same posterior representation and collaborating within the same inference pipeline. First, PSA incorporates physical consistency constraints and multi-source anomaly evidence into trusted gating, implementing robust corrections to the posterior and discrete outputs through constrained potential functions and feasible domain projection, thereby suppressing the probability mass accumulation of infeasible states and reducing high-confidence misjudgments caused by observational conflicts and out-of-distribution disturbances. Second, HPRF uses multi-step rolling propagation to map current uncertainties into prospective trend risks within a fixed prediction time horizon of 3 to 8 s, achieving calibrated probabilistic risk quantification and lead characterization. Third, EGUAQ measures posterior sharpness and cognitive uncertainty using information entropy, generating reevaluation and acquisition priorities under PSA gating constraints to enhance data utilization efficiency and support closed-loop iteration. The system input consists of the observation vector x_k∈R^d at time k (comprising multi-channel information such as angle of attack, pitch angle, calibrated airspeed, normal acceleration, control inputs, derived features, etc.), model parameters, and sets of rule thresholds. The system output is the situational vector Y_k, which includes stage posterior distributions, discrete stage conclusions, fixed time-domain risk indicators, anomaly diagnosis evidence, uncertainty measures, and handling suggestions. This facilitates engineering processes such as reevaluation, calibration, and analysis with regard to decision-making bases, trigger reasons, confidence levels, and predictive lead times. The framework adheres to the principles of training-deployment isolation and reproducibility, performing only recursive and gated operations online, adopting conservative updates for missing information and recording abnormal evidence, ensuring stable and analyzable output.

Baseline scenarios and ablation analysis

To ensure a rigorous evaluation of the predictive warning capabilities, we first explicitly define the evaluation protocol and calibration methodology: (1) Target Warning Event: The critical event is defined as the aircraft entering an unrecoverable ‘Deep Stall’ state; (2) Lead Time: Defined as the temporal difference (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\varDelta\:t$$\end{document}) between the first timestamp where the windowed trend risk \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{R}_{k}$$\end{document} continuously exceeds the calibrated threshold and the actual physical onset of the target event; (3) Prospective Miss Rate vs. Detection Miss Rate: It is crucial to distinguish that the reported Miss Rate evaluates prospective early warning, rather than concurrent detection.

In safety-critical flight test engineering, threshold calibration must strictly suppress False Alarms, which can lead to pilot fatigue and ignored warnings. Consequently, a conservative threshold is selected, intentionally trading a higher prospective miss rate for a robust, low false-alarm operational envelope.

Table 1 presents a comparison of various methods on stage identification and event-level predictive warning tasks under the baseline scenario. HMM demonstrates strong stage identifiability when the distribution matching assumption holds, achieving Acc 0.994 and F1 0.848. The AoA threshold method also attains high Acc and F1, but both methods exhibit Miss 1.000 in event-level warning with unavailable or no LeadTime output, indicating that relying solely on stage discrimination or single-variable threshold triggering cannot support probabilistic risk measurement within a fixed prediction horizon. In contrast, the ablation model incorporating particle filtering and risk mean criteria already possesses effective predictive capability, with an average LeadTime of approximately 3.3 s and Miss reduced to 0.571. Furthermore, CPSSMF maintains high stage identification accuracy while achieving superior warning quality and comprehensive trade-offs, with Acc of 0.969 and F1 of 0.785. Under the same Miss level, it consistently controls FalseAlarm at 0.250, significantly outperforming ShieldOnly’s 0.812, demonstrating that the synergy between physics-consistent gating and penalty term regularization can effectively suppress false triggers caused by observation noise and boundary uncertainty. Additionally, under the same false alarm and miss constraints, CPSSMF still achieves the highest average LeadTime of 3.336 s, further improving disposal time margin compared to PenaltyOnly’s 3.319 s. Overall, CPSSMF achieves superior systematic balance among identifiability, predictability, and calibrability, better aligning with the requirements for stable warning and engineering deployability in safety-critical flight test scenarios.

It is worth noting that we deliberately selected exact HMM and physical threshold logic as baselines rather than modern deep sequential models (e.g., Transformers or TCNs). In safety-critical flight test monitoring, the primary bottleneck is not achieving marginal gains in classification accuracy, but rather ensuring strict physical consistency, interpretability, and robust uncertainty quantification under extreme edge-of-envelope conditions. Deep sequential models, while powerful in data-rich environments, often operate as “black boxes” lacking rigorous probabilistic semantics and endogenous physical bounds, making them prone to silent failures during sensor anomalies or out-of-distribution events. Our ablation study precisely demonstrates that the proposed CPSSMF framework, by integrating probabilistic inference with physical safety shields, provides a transparent and calibrated risk assessment chain that pure data-driven models inherently lack.

From a decision-making semantic perspective, the key output of this framework is not a single-stage label, but rather a structured situation vector that jointly characterizes the stage posterior distribution, forward-looking risks within a fixed prediction window, uncertainty levels, and anomaly evidence, directly supporting hierarchical decision-making closed loops. When physical consistency constraints are not triggered and both risk and uncertainty are low, the system tends to provide advancement or maintenance recommendations to complete criterion sampling and improve task efficiency; when risk is triggered or anomaly evidence is detected, the strategy automatically switches to safety-first mode, suppressing exploratory prompts and suggesting protective or termination actions; when risk remains low but uncertainty is high with no anomalies, the system outputs minimal intervention review and collection recommendations, transforming model uncertainty into manageable data closed-loop assets. This design provides explicit threshold tuning channels for the trade-offs between lead time, false alarms, and missed detections, and achieves conservative degradation under out-of-distribution or mismatched conditions through anomaly analysis chains.

Conclusion

This research addresses the safety-critical requirements for real-time decision-making at flight test points by proposing a unified framework that integrates probabilistic modeling with physical consistency constraints and fixed time-horizon forward-looking risk assessment, organizing decision information into two analytical chains to meet the semantic completeness and traceability requirements of engineering operations. The first analytical chain centers on trusted gating, posterior shaping, and output projection, introducing physical consistency constraints to suppress posterior probability mass drift caused by observation noise, boundary uncertainties, and contradictory evidence, outputting interpretable stage posterior distributions and anomaly indicators to avoid brittle triggering and false dismissals that rely solely on hard thresholds or empirical rules, while providing reviewable probabilistic evidence for operational actions including advancement, maintenance, rollback, and termination. The second analytical chain focuses on trend risk quantification within fixed prediction windows and entropy-driven uncertainty prompting, elevating early warning from instantaneous boundary violation detection to probability mass assessment of entering concern sets within future windows, making the trade-offs between lead time, false alarms, and missed detections explicitly parametrizable and supporting threshold tuning and risk budget configuration, while using stage posterior entropy to characterize epistemic uncertainty and information gain for triggering minimal-intervention reviews and data collection and guiding priorities for data replay and retraining, thereby transforming model untrustworthiness into actionable data closed-loop improvement pathways.

Under unified simulation evaluation protocols and baseline and ablation controls, results demonstrate that this framework maintains competitive stage recognition capabilities under undisturbed conditions, while its key advantages are concentrated in the stability of risk outputs and robustness under boundary conditions. When contradictory injection perturbations are introduced, consistency constraints can effectively suppress posterior drift triggered by conflicting observations, thereby maintaining stable recognition and early warning behavior at unified operating points and providing trusted probabilistic inputs for fixed time-horizon risk prediction; simultaneously, fixed window risk measures reveal that lead time has structural upper limits under highly dynamic operating conditions, suggesting that engineering deployment should adopt layered thresholds and calibrated diagnostics, supported by robust statistical reports for safety argumentation. Overall, this research establishes an implementation approach with attributable mechanisms, quantifiable metrics, interpretable outputs, and decoupled training and online inference, with subsequent validation of its systematic utility planned on larger-scale data and richer scenario combinations.

While the current evaluation based on high-fidelity procedural simulation demonstrates the theoretical soundness and robustness of the CPSSMF, we acknowledge a critical gap between simulated environments and real-world operational deployment. Real flight test environments involve unmodeled uncertainties, such as aeroelastic structural vibrations, complex atmospheric disturbances (e.g., wind shear and gusts), and unpredictable sensor degradation profiles that are difficult to fully emulate. Furthermore, the relatively small dataset size utilized in this study highlights a core advantage of the framework: because physical invariants and feasibility constraints are explicitly injected into the potential functions (PSA module), the model does not require massive datasets to implicitly “re-learn” physics, yielding high sample efficiency. By bridging Sim-to-Real gap in the furture, we aim to transition this methodological framework into a certified engineering tool for flight test safety monitoring.

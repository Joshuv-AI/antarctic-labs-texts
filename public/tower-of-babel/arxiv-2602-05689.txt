Polynomial functors in π \pi -clans for the semantics of type theory
Joseph Hua, Yiming Xu

Abstract
. The category of contexts underlying a model of Martin-Löf type theory with 𝖴𝗇𝗂𝗍 \mathsf{Unit} -, Σ \Sigma -, and Π \Pi -types need not be locally Cartesian closed, but is necessarily a π \pi -clan. We exploit this π \pi -clan structure to build the theory of polynomial functors. This paper presents two equivalent notions of strict semantics for MLTT in this weaker setting, respectively elementary models – reformulating categories with families (CwFs) – and algebraic models – reformulating natural models. These components fit into a practical sequence of steps for constructing models of MLTT: building an elementary model, extracting a π \pi -clan from the elementary model, and then using polynomial functors built on the π \pi -clan structure to convert the elementary model into an algebraic one.

1.2.
Formalization and design considerations

This paper is part of the wider HoTTLean project
[
25
]
,
where a domain-specific language for MLTT is built in Lean
and applied to the groupoid model.
As such, many of our results are formalized in Lean.
The definitions and theorems with formalized counterparts
are labeled with symbols
\boxbar
,
which contain embedded links
to code in the HoTTLean repository and Mathlib
[
35
]
.
The HoTTLean repository can be found at

github.com/sinhp/HoTTLean/tree/TYPES2026

The original goal of this Lean library
was to simplify the formulation of the groupoid model,
as part of the HoTTLean project.
We originally defined the groupoid model using the theory of
natural models.
After experimenting with various setups,
including using Uemura’s representable map categories
[
37
]
,
we settled on our current approach.
We outline our reasons for this decision in the following.

•

Let
\mathcal{C}
denote a category of contexts.
HoTTLean is designed to handle multiple small
(meaning in
\mathcal{C}
) universes.
Also adding a large (meaning in
\mathsf{Psh}(\mathcal{C})
but not
\mathcal{C}
)
classifier of all types –
as is the approach in natural models –
creates an additional burden for the user.
Our first observation is that one can get away
with only working with small universes.
Our second observation is that when doing so,
one ends up working only with representable presheaves.
This requires a proof that representables are closed under
polynomial operations,
and the theory of polynomials in
\pi
-clans can be seen as
a systematic way of proving these closure conditions.
Finally, our work simplifies expressions
by doing away with
presheaves and Yoneda embeddings,
instead working directly in
\mathcal{C}
.

•

The move from
\mathsf{Psh}(\mathcal{C})
to
\mathcal{C}

brings us closer to an
internal language
for
\mathcal{C}
.
Indeed, HoTTLean aims to build a domain-specific language
for reasoning about the objects of
\mathcal{C}
,
rather than
\mathsf{Psh}(\mathcal{C})
.

•

Another advantage of working in
\mathcal{C}

over
\mathsf{Psh}(\mathcal{C})
is in dealing with issues of size.
Lean’s universe level calculus for
\mathsf{Psh}(\mathcal{C})
involves
two universe variables for
\mathcal{C}
(one for the objects and one for the maps) and another for the category

\mathsf{Type}\,u
that the presheaves take values in,
and these three variables are constrained in certain ways
that the user needs to determine.

•

For the groupoid
[
23
]
and simplicial
[
28
]
models of MLTT,
Uemura’s representable map categories provide a sufficiently convenient
framework.
Indeed,
\mathsf{sSet}
is LCC and
(split) isofibrations are exponentiable in

\mathsf{Grpd}

[
38
]
.
However, to work with the initial model of MLTT
in Uemura’s setting,
one would have to again take presheaves
since fibrations are not exponentiable maps.

Remark 2.2
.

In the following,
suppose
\mathcal{C}
is a category of contexts for the initial model of MLTT,
namely the syntactic category of contexts and substitutions.
We compare natural models in
\mathsf{Psh}(\mathcal{C})
with
universes in
\mathcal{C}
.

Natural models
\dot{U}\to U
are allowed to be “large”,
where the base
U
may not be represented by an object in
\mathcal{C}
.
Consequently,

U
does not necessarily correspond to any
small universe from the syntax.
This also means that a natural model can classify

all dependent families
in
\mathcal{C}
.
On the other hand,
a universe
\mathsf{Tm}\to\mathsf{Ty}

in the category of contexts must originate from some small universe appearing in the syntax,
and can only classify “small” dependent families.

A natural model is assumed to be a “representable” natural transformation,
meaning that whenever
\Gamma
is a representable presheaf,

\Gamma.A
is representable, too.
This ensures that contexts are closed under the operation of context extension.
In our setting,
we place no such condition on a universe,
since all objects are contexts.

6.
Conclusion

We have presented an approach to defining semantics of MLTT
in
\pi
-clans, called algebraic semantics,
generalizing the LCC analog in the literature.
The key step of this approach is to make polynomial functors available in this setting,
as they are central to defining algebraic type formers.
Once the general theory of polynomial functors in
\pi
-clans is established,
the rest of our approach is analogous to its LCC counterpart,
as described in
[
7
]
.

Furthermore, we validated the correctness of our definition by proving that it models the type formers in the expected way.
This was achieved by proving that algebraic semantics are
inter-translatable with an elementary presentation of a strict MLTT
semantics called elementary semantics.
The efficacy of our approach was witnessed by its application in HoTTLean.
In this library,
we defined elementary and algebraic semantics,
formalizing translations between the two, so as to secure the correctness of the elementary definition that we used to define the groupoid model.

7.
Related Work

7.1.
Polynomial functors

Weber
[
39
]
developed polynomial functors in
the general setting of a category with pullbacks and exponentiable maps.
Aberlé and Spivak
[
2
]
study the theory of
polynomial functors and their application for modeling type
formers of MLTT in a univalent setting,
with results formalized in Agda.
Our polynomial functor library is based on the Lean library Poly

[
21
]
,
which formalizes polynomial functors in Weber’s setting.
Uemura
[
36
]
characterized
exponentiable maps as models of a universal one,
and characterizes polynomial endofunctors
in terms of endofunctors equipped with certain natural transformations.

7.2.
Clans

Much of the theory that underlies our development of polynomial
functors in
\pi
-clans is given by
Taylor
[
34
]
and Joyal
[
27
]
.
The key difference between these clan-based descriptions of MLTT
semantics and ours is our use of a universe to ensure strict coherence.
More recently,
Frey
[
15
]
proved a Gabriel–Ulmer style duality theorem for clans,
rounding out the picture of clans as an internal language with
functorial semantics and theory-model duality.

7.3.
Semantics of MLTT

MLTT semantics are presented in various styles,
including
display map categories
[
34
]
(or clans),
comprehension categories
[
26
]
,
categories with families
[
13
]
,
and natural models
[
5
]
.
Some of these presentations are compared in
[
3
]
, in a univalent setting,
but without considering type formers.
A formalization of these equivalences can be found in
the UniMath library
[
19
]
.
Our work also draws from
Uemura’s categories with representable maps
[
37
]
,
which provides an analysis of natural models
via Lawvere’s
functorial semantics

[
29
]
.

8.
Future work

We have set out the basic theory of polynomial functors in
\pi
-clans
with only the goal of formulating the algebraic semantics of type theory.
The full picture of this theory is of general interest
and deserves further exploration;
a detailed treatment will be provided in the PhD thesis of the
first author.

Whereas our work is already a generalization from LCC categories,
it has the potential of being generalized further.
Using an idea similar to
[
39
]
,
where polynomial signatures are taken to be exponential morphisms
in finite limit categories,
instead of taking
a polynomial signature to be any morphism in a
\pi
-clan,
one can possibly take it to be a morphism that is
“exponentiable relative to a preclan”.

Uemura
[
36
]
characterized polynomial functors
and exponentiability using the language of

generalized algebraic theories
in finite limit categories.
This could potentially be reformulated in terms of preclans.
Furthermore,
we would like to reformulate Uemura’s
analysis of natural models
[
37
]

in the style of Lawvere’s functorial semantics

[
29
]
,
producing a counterpart in terms of our algebraic models.

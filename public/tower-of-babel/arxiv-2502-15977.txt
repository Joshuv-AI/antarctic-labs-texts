Reduced superschemes and the combinatorics of toric supervarieties
Eric Jankowski

Abstract
. We propose new definitions of integral, reduced, and normal superrings and superschemes to properly establish the notion of a supervariety. We generalize several results about classical reduced rings and varieties to the supergeometric setting, including an equivalence of categories between certain toric supervarieties and decorated polyhedral fans. These decorated fans are shown to encode important geometric information about the corresponding toric supervarieties. We then investigate some naturally-occurring toric supervarieties inside the isomeric supergrassmannian, which we show admits a nice description as a decorated polytope.

1.
Introduction

Toric geometry provides a powerful framework for studying algebraic varieties with combinatorial structures. Classically, toric varieties serve as a rich family of examples whose algebraic, geometric, and topological properties are well understood, leveraging the deep correspondence between toric varieties and polyhedral fans. In the realm of supergeometry, where algebraic varieties are enhanced with additional fermionic coordinates, a natural question then arises: to what extent does this story generalize to the supergeometric setting?

Let
T
be a
supertorus
(or
quasitoral supergroup
), a supergroup whose even part
T_{\overline{0}}
is a central torus. Supertori arise as the Cartan subgroups of quasireductive supergroups
[
16
]
, and their representations have been studied by Shibata
[
18
]
and Gorelik et al.
[
9
]
.

If
T
acts generically transitively on a normal supervariety
X
(i.e.
X
admits an open orbit), we say
X
is a
toric supervariety
. This paper aims to continue the investigation of toric supervarieties initiated by the author in
[
11
]
, wherein only the case of one odd dimension is considered.

We first take care in sections 2 and 3 to develop a theory of commutative superalgebra and algebraic supervarieties which is consistent with the classical theory. Although terms like algebraic supervariety, integral superdomain, and reduced superring have been used before, we propose novel definitions and hope to persuade the reader that they are the best super-analogs of their classical counterparts. Our definition of a supervariety most closely aligns with Sherman’s in
[
17
]
, which inspired the theory of supervarieties developed in this paper.

The main content of this paper is section 4, in which we develop a systematic way of building supertori and toric supervarieties. It will be seen that classification of toric supervarieties in full generality is ultimately a wild problem, but we obtain several partial results in various level of generality. In particular, if we impose an enhancement of the usual
(R_{1})
condition of Serre (Proposition/Definition
4.18
), then we can classify toric supervarieties by their decorated fans (Definition
4.23
).

Theorem 1
.

There is a bijective correspondence between
(HR_{1})
toric supervarieties and decorated fans, up to isomorphism.

We will see that morphisms of
(HR_{1})
toric supervarieties are difficult to describe, so a categorical version of this theorem remains elusive. However, we offer a partial rectification in the form of the following theorem about “large-orbit” toric supervarieties, i.e. those whose orbits have odd codimension no greater than their even codimension. All large-orbit toric supervarieties admit the
(HR_{1})
property.

Theorem 2
.

The category of large-orbit toric supervarieties is equivalent to the category of large-orbit decorated fans.

We also discuss decorated polytopes in section 4.9, and we prove a smoothness criterion for
(HR_{1})
toric supervarieties in section 4.11.

Large-orbit toric supervarieties appear frequently in nature, and seem to be the most faithful super-analogs of toric varieties. Notably, their decorated fans (and decorated polytopes) are considerably easier to describe, and many statements, such as the smoothness criterion, simplify drastically.

Finally, in section 5, we investigate a family of large-orbit toric supervarieties in the isomeric supergrassmannian. We show that their decorated polytopes occur naturally as decorated polytopes of the image of a morphism of supermanifolds akin to a momentum map, generalizing results about torus strata in
[
7
]
.

2.
Commutative Superalgebra

2.1.
Basics and conventions

Definition 2.1
.

A
superring
is a
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
-graded ring
A=A_{\overline{0}}\oplus A_{\overline{1}}
.

If
a\in A_{\overline{i}}
is homogeneous, we write
|a|=\overline{i}
for the
parity
of
a
. For our purposes, all superrings will be assumed unital, associative, and
commutative
in the sense that
ab=(-1)^{|a|\cdot|b|}ba
for homogeneous
a,b\in A
.

All morphisms must preserve parity, and all ideals and modules are compatibly
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
-graded. Maximal (respectively, prime) ideals in particular correspond bijectively to maximal (respectively, prime) ideals of
A_{\overline{0}}
, or equivalently
A/(A_{\overline{1}})
, since prime ideals contain all nilpotents.

Localization behaves the same as in the ordinary situation, except that the multiplicatively closed subset
S
that we invert must be contained in
A_{\overline{0}}
. That is, if
S
is such a subset,
S^{-1}A=S^{-1}A_{\overline{0}}\oplus S^{-1}A_{\overline{1}}
where we treat
A_{\overline{1}}
as an
A_{\overline{0}}
-module. To avoid degenerate cases, we will assume
0\notin S
, so that localization preserves non-zero divisors.

We write
\zdiv(A)
for the set of zero divisors of
A
,
\nonzdiv(A)
for the non-zero divisors,
A^{\times}
for the units, and
\Nil(A)
for the nilpotents.

Definition 2.2
.

The
total superring of fractions
of
A
is
K(A):=\nonzdiv(A)^{-1}A
. We denote by
i:A\to K(A)
the natural inclusion, by
J_{A}
the ideal
i^{-1}((K(A)_{\overline{1}}))\subseteq A
, and by
\overline{A}
the
underlying ring

A/J_{A}
. If
S
is any subset of
A
, we write
\overline{S}
for its image in
\overline{A}
.

We record the following super version of the prime avoidance lemma, whose proof is completely identical to that of the standard version.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
I
be an ideal of a superring
A
such that
I\subseteq\mathfrak{p}_{1}\cup...\cup\mathfrak{p}_{n}
for some prime ideals
\mathfrak{p}_{i}
. Then
I\subseteq\mathfrak{p}_{i}
for some
i
.

2.2.
Reduced superrings and integral superdomains

We introduce some terminology which has seen conflicting usage in the literature. We believe the following definitions are most in line with the classical situation. The common theme here is that badly-behaved elements should all arise from the odd part of the total superring of fractions.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
A
be a superring.

(a)

A
is a
superfield
if
J_{A}=A\backslash A^{\times}

(b)

A
is an
integral superdomain
if
J_{A}=\zdiv(A)

(c)

A
is
reduced
if
J_{A}=\Nil(A)
and
\overline{\zdiv(A)}=\zdiv(\overline{A})

In Lemma
2.6
we will provide several equivalent characterizations of these types of superrings. We first prove a lemma that will allow us to conflate
\overline{A_{\mathfrak{p}}}
and
\overline{A}_{\overline{\mathfrak{p}}}
for prime ideals
\mathfrak{p}
of a reduced superring
A
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
A
be a reduced superring and
S\subseteq A_{\overline{0}}
a multiplicatively closed subset.

(a)

J_{S^{-1}A}=S^{-1}J_{A}

(b)

\overline{S^{-1}A}=\overline{S}^{-1}\overline{A}

Proof.

(a)

Since
\Nil(S^{-1}A)=S^{-1}\Nil(A)=S^{-1}J_{A}
, it suffices to show that
\Nil(S^{-1}A)=J_{S^{-1}A}
. Let
\frac{a}{s}\in S^{-1}A
be nilpotent, so there is
t\in S
such that
ta^{n}=0
. Then
ta\in\Nil(A)=J_{A}
, so
ta=\frac{\eta}{b}
for
\eta\in(A_{\overline{1}})
and
b\in\nonzdiv(A)
. Then
b\cdot\frac{a}{s}=\frac{\eta}{st}
in
S^{-1}A
for
\frac{\eta}{st}\in(S^{-1}A_{\overline{1}})
. Hence
\frac{a}{s}\in J_{S^{-1}A}
, since localization preserves non-zero divisors. Therefore
\Nil(S^{-1}A)=J_{S^{-1}A}
and we are finished.

(b)

Follows immediately from part (a). ∎

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
A
be a superring.

(a)

The following are equivalent:

(i)

A
is a superfield

(ii)

\overline{A}
is a field

(iii)

J_{A}=(A_{\overline{1}})
is the set of non-units

(iv)

(A_{\overline{1}})
is a maximal ideal

(b)

The following are equivalent:

(i)

A
is an integral superdomain

(ii)

\overline{A}
is an integral domain and
\overline{\zdiv(A)}=\zdiv(\overline{A})

(iii)

K(A)
is a superfield

(iv)

A
is reduced and
\Spec A
is irreducible

(c)

The following are equivalent:

(i)

A
is a reduced superring

(ii)

\overline{A}
is a reduced ring and
\overline{\zdiv(A)}=\zdiv(\overline{A})

(iii)

J_{A}=\Nil(A)
and
\zdiv(A)=\bigcup_{\mathfrak{p}\in\minSpec A}\mathfrak{p}

(iv)

\overline{A}
is a reduced ring and
\zdiv(A)=\bigcup_{\mathfrak{p}\in\minSpec A}\mathfrak{p}

(v)

S^{-1}A
is a reduced superring for any multiplicatively closed subset
S\subseteq A_{\overline{0}}

(vi)

A_{\mathfrak{m}}
is a reduced superring for any maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}

Proof.

(a)

The equivalence of (iii) and (iv) is immediate since every maximal ideal contains
(A_{\overline{1}})
. These equivalent conditions imply
(A_{\overline{1}})=J_{A}
, which then implies (i) and (ii), which are equivalent because every maximal ideal contains
J_{A}
.

Let us now prove (i)
\implies
(iv). It suffices to show that
J_{A}=(A_{\overline{1}})
. Let
\eta\in J_{A}
, so we may write
\eta=\frac{\eta^{\prime}}{a}
for
\eta^{\prime}\in(A_{\overline{1}})
and
a\in\nonzdiv(A)
. But
a\notin J_{A}
implies
A
is a unit, so indeed
\eta\in(A_{1})
and we are finished.

(b)

First assume (i), so that
K(A)=A_{J_{A}}
has maximal ideal
K(A)J_{A}=(K(A)_{\overline{1}})
and (iii) follows. For the converse, note that a zero divisor
a\in A
does not receive an inverse in
K(A)
, so
a\in J_{A}
. Thus, we have established (i)
\iff
(iii).

Now observe that (ii) just says
\overline{\zdiv(A)}=0
, so indeed
J_{A}=\zdiv(A)
, which is (i). Conversely, since nonzerodivisors form a multiplicatively closed subset,
A
integral implies
\overline{A}
integral and so we obtain (i)
\iff
(ii). Finally, (ii)
\iff
(iv) follows from part (c).

(c)

That (i)
\iff
(ii) and (iii)
\iff
(iv) is immediate from the definitions. To see the equivalence of (i) and (ii) with (iii) and (iv), it suffices to show that if
\overline{A}
is reduced, then
\overline{\zdiv(A)}=\zdiv(\overline{A})
if and only if
\zdiv(A)=\bigcup_{\mathfrak{p}\in\minSpec A}\mathfrak{p}
.

For the forward implication, let
a\in\zdiv(A)
, so there is
b\in A
such that
b\notin J_{A}
and
ab\in J_{A}
. Then
ab\in\mathfrak{p}
for all primes
\mathfrak{p}
, but
b\notin\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}
for some minimal prime
\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}
since
b\notin\Nil(A)
. Hence
a\in\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}
, so
\zdiv(A)\subseteq\bigcup_{\mathfrak{p}\in\minSpec A}\mathfrak{p}
. That minimal primes consist of zero divisors is standard. Conversely, if
\zdiv(A)=\bigcup_{\mathfrak{p}\in\minSpec A}\mathfrak{p}
, then
\overline{\zdiv(A)}=\overline{\bigcup_{\mathfrak{p}\in\minSpec A}\mathfrak{p}}=\bigcup_{\mathfrak{p}\in\minSpec A}\overline{\mathfrak{p}}=\zdiv(\overline{A})
since
\overline{A}
is reduced.

We now show (iii)
\implies
(v). That
\Nil(S^{-1}A)=J_{S^{-1}A}
is proven in Lemma
2.5
. For
\zdiv(S^{-1}A)=\bigcup_{\mathfrak{p}\in\minSpec S^{-1}A}\mathfrak{p}
, first take
\frac{a}{s}\in\zdiv(S^{-1}A)
, so
a\in\zdiv(A)
. Then
a
belongs to a minimal prime
\mathfrak{p}
of
A
. If
\mathfrak{p}\cap S=\emptyset
, then
S^{-1}\mathfrak{p}
is a minimal prime of
S^{-1}A
and we are finished. Otherwise we may assume that
a
belongs to no such minimal prime. Let
\mathfrak{q}
be a prime that does contain
a
, so
\mathfrak{q}
contains a minimal prime that meets
S
. Then
S^{-1}\mathfrak{q}=S^{-1}A
, so
s^{-1}a
is a unit in
S^{-1}A
, a contradiction. Hence
\zdiv(S^{-1}A)=\bigcup_{\mathfrak{p}\in\minSpec S^{-1}A}\mathfrak{p}
, so indeed (iii)
\implies
(v).

Since (v)
\implies
(vi) is immediate, it remains to show (vi)
\implies
(i). First let
a\in\Nil(A)
, define
I=\{d\in A\mid ad\in J_{A}\}
, and pick a maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
. Then
\frac{a}{1}\in\Nil(A_{\mathfrak{m}})=J_{A_{\mathfrak{m}}}
, so
\frac{b}{t}\cdot\frac{a}{1}=\frac{\alpha}{s}
for
\frac{\alpha}{s}\in((A_{\mathfrak{m}})_{\overline{1}})
and
\frac{b}{t}\in\nonzdiv(A_{\mathfrak{m}})
. Hence there is
u\notin\mathfrak{m}
such that
u(abs-t\alpha)=0
in
A
. Since
u,b,s\notin\mathfrak{m}
and
ut\alpha\in J_{A}
, it follows that
ubs\in I\backslash\mathfrak{m}
, so
I
is not contained in
\mathfrak{m}
. Therefore
I
is the unit ideal, so indeed
a\in J_{A}
.

Now if
A
is not reduced, there is a nonzero
\xi\in J_{A}
such that
\overline{\Ann_{A}(\xi)}
is not contained in
\zdiv(\overline{A})
. Let
a\in\Ann_{A}(\xi)
be such a “bad” element, so that
\overline{a}\notin\zdiv(\overline{A})
. Moreover, let
\mathfrak{m}
be a maximal ideal containing
\Ann_{A}(\xi)
. Then
\Ann_{A}(a)\subseteq J_{A}\subseteq\mathfrak{m}
, so the relationship
a\xi=0
remains nontrivial in
A_{\mathfrak{m}}
. This contradicts reducedness of
A_{\mathfrak{m}}
, so indeed
A
must be reduced as well. ∎

We now collect some results about reduced superrings in the general and Noetherian cases.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
A
be a reduced superring. Then
A_{\mathfrak{p}}
is a superfield for all
\mathfrak{p}\in\minSpec A
.

Proof.

If
\mathfrak{p}
is a minimal prime, then
A_{\mathfrak{p}}
is a reduced local superring with a unique prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}A_{\mathfrak{p}}=\Nil(A_{\mathfrak{p}})=J_{A_{\mathfrak{p}}}
and hence a superfield.
∎

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
A
be a Noetherian reduced superring and
S\subseteq A_{\overline{0}}
a multiplicative subset.

(a)

K(A)\cong\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\in\minSpec A}A_{\mathfrak{p}}

(b)

S^{-1}K(A)=K(S^{-1}A)

Proof.

(a)

By the universal property of localization, there are natural maps
K(A)\to A_{\mathfrak{p}}
, since minimal primes consist only of zero divisors. Hence we get a map
K(A)\to\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\in\minSpec A}A_{\mathfrak{p}}
. By the prime avoidance lemma
2.3
, any nonminimal prime contains a non-zero divisor, so
\Spec K(A)=\minSpec A
, a finite discrete set, as a subspace of
\Spec A
. Passing to spectra, it follows that
K(A)\to\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\in\minSpec A}A_{\mathfrak{p}}
is an isomorphism.

(b)

We know that
S^{-1}A
is reduced by Lemma
2.6
(c). Hence
K(S^{-1}A)
is the product of
(S^{-1}A)_{S^{-1}\mathfrak{p}}
over minimal primes
\mathfrak{p}
of
A
such that
\mathfrak{p}\cap S=\varnothing
. If
S
meets
\mathfrak{p}
, then
S^{-1}(A_{\mathfrak{p}})=0
since
\mathfrak{p}A_{\mathfrak{p}}=((A_{\mathfrak{p}})_{\overline{1}})
and hence consists of only nilpotents. Therefore
S^{-1}K(A)\cong\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\in\minSpec S^{-1}A}A_{\mathfrak{p}}\cong K(S^{-1}A)
. ∎

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
A
be a Noetherian superring. The following are equivalent:

(i)

A
is reduced

(ii)

A_{\mathfrak{p}}
is a superfield for all
\mathfrak{p}\in\minSpec A
, and
A\to\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\in\minSpec A}A_{\mathfrak{p}}
is injective

Proof.

We have (i)
\implies
(ii) from Lemmas
2.7
and
2.8
.

Now assume (ii), and let
s\in A\backslash\bigcup_{\mathfrak{p}\in\minSpec A}\mathfrak{p}
. Then the image of
s
in
\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\in\minSpec A}A_{\mathfrak{p}}
is invertible and hence a non-zero divisor in
A
, so indeed
\zdiv(A)=\bigcup_{\mathfrak{p}\in\minSpec A}\mathfrak{p}
. We now show
\Nil(A)=J_{A}
. Let
a\in\Nil(A)
. Then
\frac{a}{1}\in J_{A_{\mathfrak{p}}}\cong(J_{A})_{\mathfrak{p}}
, i.e. there is
s\notin\mathfrak{p}
such that
as\in J_{A}
. Let
I=\{s\in A\mid as\in J_{A}\}
, so
I\not\subseteq\mathfrak{p}
for any minimal prime
\mathfrak{p}
. Using the prime avoidance lemma
2.3
, let
s\in I\backslash\bigcup_{\mathfrak{p}\in\minSpec A}\mathfrak{p}
. As before,
s\in\nonzdiv(A)
, so indeed
a\in J_{A}
.
∎

When we generalize the commutative superalgebra of this section to algebraic supergeometry in the next, we will often want our spaces to contain smooth points. Part (c) of the following definition provides a suitably general algebraic model for such spaces.

Definition 2.10
.

Let
A
be a superring.

(a)

A
is
fermionically regular
(or
FR
) if it is of the form
\bigwedge M
for some commutative ring
R
and
R

. In this situation, we have
J_{A}=(A_{\overline{1}})
, and we may assume

and

.

(b)

A
is
generically fermionically regular
(or

) if
A_{\mathfrak{p}}
is a FR superring for all minimal prime ideals
\mathfrak{p}
.

(c)

A
is

is a FR superfield for
\mathfrak{p}
minimal).

2.3.
Serre’s criteria

Let
A
be a superring. Following
[
15
]
, we say an element
t\in A_{\overline{0}}
is
A
-regular if the multiplication map by
t
is injective but not bijective. Likewise, we say
\xi\in A_{\overline{1}}
is
A
-regular if the cohomology of the multiplication map by
\xi
is trivial. An
A
-regular sequence is a sequence of homogeneous elements
r_{1},...,r_{k}
such that each
r_{i}
is regular in
A/(r_{1},...,r_{i-1})
.

Definition 2.11
.

Let
A
be a Noetherian local superring with maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
.

(a)

A
is
regular
if
\mathfrak{m}
is generated by an
A
-regular sequence

(b)

The
depth
of
A
is the minimum
i\geq 0
such that
\Ext^{i}(A/\mathfrak{m},A)\neq 0
.

The usual theorem of Rees still holds in this setting by the same argument:

Lemma 2.12
.

The depth of a Noetherian local superring
(A,\mathfrak{m})
is the length of any maximal
A
-regular sequence of even elements in
\mathfrak{m}
.

Definition 2.13
.

Let
A
be a Noetherian superring. We say
A
satisfies

(a)

(R_{k})
if
A_{\mathfrak{p}}
is regular for all prime ideals
\mathfrak{p}
of height
\leq k

(b)

(S_{k})
if
\depth(A_{\mathfrak{p}})\geq\min(k,\height(\mathfrak{p}))
for all prime ideals
\mathfrak{p}

Lemma 2.14
.

A superfield is regular if and only if it is FR.

The following proposition justifies our usage of GFRR superrings as super-versions of reduced rings, as well as our definitions of the conditions
(R_{k})
and
(S_{k})
.

Proposition 2.15
.

A Noetherian superring
A
is GFRR if and only if it satisfies
(R_{0})
and
(S_{1})
.

Proof.

If
A
is GFRR, let
\mathfrak{p}
be a minimal prime. Then
A_{\mathfrak{p}}
is a FR superfield, so
(R_{0})
holds. Now let
\mathfrak{p}
be a prime ideal with
\height(\mathfrak{p})\geq 1
. For
(S_{1})
, it suffices to show that there is a nonzerodivisor in
\mathfrak{p}A_{\mathfrak{p}}
. We know by Lemma
2.6
that
A_{\mathfrak{p}}
is reduced, so we have
\zdiv(A_{\mathfrak{p}})=\bigcup_{\mathfrak{q}\in\minSpec(A_{\mathfrak{p}})}\mathfrak{q}=\bigcup_{q\in\minSpec(A),\mathfrak{q}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}}\mathfrak{q}A_{\mathfrak{p}}
. If
\mathfrak{p}A_{\mathfrak{p}}
is contained in this set, then
\mathfrak{p}
is one of these minimal primes by the prime avoidance lemma
2.3
. But
\height(\mathfrak{p})\geq 1
, so indeed
\mathfrak{p}A_{\mathfrak{p}}
contains a nonzerodivisor.

Conversely, suppose
A
satisfies
(R_{0})
and
(S_{1})
. We will show that
A_{\mathfrak{p}}
is reduced for all primes
\mathfrak{p}
. If
\mathfrak{p}
is a minimal prime, then by
(R_{0})
we know
A_{\mathfrak{p}}
is regular. But
\mathfrak{p}A_{\mathfrak{p}}
is the unique prime ideal of
A_{\mathfrak{p}}
, so
\mathfrak{p}A_{\mathfrak{p}}=\Nil(A_{\mathfrak{p}})
. Regularity implies that
\Nil(A_{\mathfrak{p}})=((A_{\mathfrak{p}})_{\overline{1}})
, so
A_{\mathfrak{p}}
is a superfield and therefore is reduced.

Now if
\height(\mathfrak{p})\geq 1
, we know by
(S_{1})
that
\mathfrak{p}A_{\mathfrak{p}}
contains a nonzerodivisor, i.e. an element
a
for which the localization map
A_{\mathfrak{p}}\to A_{\mathfrak{p}}[a^{-1}]
is injective. Since any superring, particularly
A_{\mathfrak{p}}[a^{-1}]
, is contained in the product of its localizations at prime ideals, we know that
A_{\mathfrak{p}}
is a subring of a product of localizations of
A
at primes
\mathfrak{q}\subsetneq\mathfrak{p}
with
a\notin\mathfrak{q}
. Since
\height(\mathfrak{q})<\height(\mathfrak{p})
, we may induct on the height to find that
A_{\mathfrak{p}}
is a subring of a product of reduced superrings which are localizations of
A_{\mathfrak{p}}
at minimal primes. It follows by Lemma
2.9
that
A_{\mathfrak{p}}
(and hence also
A
) is reduced.
∎

3.
Algebraic Supergeometry

3.1.
Superschemes and supervarieties

Definition 3.1
.

A
superspace

X=(|X|,\mathscr{O}_{X})
is a pair consisting of a topological space
|X|
and a sheaf
\mathscr{O}_{X}
of superrings such that each stalk
\mathscr{O}_{X,x}
is a local superring.

A
morphism of superspaces
is a pair
\phi=(|\phi|,\phi^{\#}):X\to Y
for a continuous map
|\phi|:|X|\to|Y|
and a morphism of
\phi^{\#}:\mathscr{O}_{Y}\to|\phi|_{*}\mathscr{O}_{X}
of sheaves on
|Y|
which becomes a local morphism of local superrings on each stalk.

We say that the superspace
X
is a
superscheme
if
(|X|,(\mathscr{O}_{X})_{\overline{0}})

3.3.
Algebraic supergroups and their actions

The material in this section is well-known and provided for reference.

Definition 3.13
.

An
affine algebraic supergroup
is any of the following equivalent notions:

(a)

The spectrum of a finitely-generated Hopf superalgebra

which is an integral superdomain

(b)

A group object
G
in the category of affine supervarieties

(c)

An affine supervariety
G=\Spec A
whose functor of points

is group-valued

(d)

A pair

where

, and
\mathfrak{g}
is equipped with a

-module structure such that the corresponding

-module is adjoint (called a

)

We use these notions (which can be found in
[
3

If we relax the assumption that a supervariety must be irreducible (and accordingly adjust for the possibility of multiple generic points), then this requirement may be dropped, and definitions (a)-(d) remain equivalent.

The notation in the definition of a Harish-Chandra pair is intentional, as

is the underlying algebraic group of

and

is the Lie superalgebra (i.e. tangent space at the identity) of

.

Definition 3.14
.

A (left) action of a supergroup

4.1.
Algebraic supertori

Definition 4.1
.

An
algebraic supertorus
is an algebraic supergroup

such that
T_{\overline{0}}\cong(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{n}
is an ordinary algebraic torus lying in the center of

.

This definition is motivated by the theory of Cartan subgroups of quasireductive supergroups as in
[

. A
quasireductive
supergroup

is one whose even part
G_{\overline{0}}
is reductive. In this setting, one chooses a Cartan subgroup (i.e. maximal torus)
T_{\overline{0}}
of
G_{\overline{0}}
, and defines

as the centralizer of
T_{\overline{0}}
in
G
. Thus, an algebraic supertorus is an algebraic supergroup which occurs as a Cartan subgroup of a quasireductive supergroup. The representation theory of supertori has been studied in
[
9
]
and
[
18
]
.

From the definition of a Harish-Chandra pair, one sees that a supertorus is determined by an ordinary algebraic torus
T_{\overline{0}}
and a super vector space
\mathfrak{t}=\mathfrak{t}_{\overline{0}}\oplus\mathfrak{t}_{\overline{1}}
(such that
\mathfrak{t}_{0}=\Lie T_{\overline{0}}
) equipped with a linear map
S^{2}\mathfrak{t}_{\overline{1}}\to\mathfrak{t}_{\overline{0}}

We write

and

for the weight and coweight lattices, respectively, of

for the algebraic torus whose coweight lattice is

and

and we note that it extends

The algebra of regular functions on

\displaystyle T_{N}

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}[T_{N}]

where we view the

\displaystyle(a_{1},...,a_{p}\mid\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{q})\cdot(b_{1},...,b_{p}\mid\beta_{1},...,\beta_{q})

4.2.
Introduction to toric supervarieties

We now begin our treatment of toric supervarieties. While the following definition requires that the supertorus action be a left action, all the same theory (with a few sign changes, noted above) applies if we switch to right actions. Let us first recall the definition of a toric variety.

Definition 4.3
.

A
toric variety
is an irreducible normal variety
X
equipped with a left action by an algebraic torus
T
and a
T
-equivariant open immersion
T\to X
.

Notice that if
X
is any (irreducible, normal) variety with an open orbit of a torus
T
, then the open orbit
T/H
itself is an algebraic torus and
X
is a toric variety for
T/H
. In this sense, any such variety with an open orbit of a torus can be modeled as a toric variety according to the above definition.

If we wish to generalize this notion to the supergeometric setting, we must therefore contend with the realm of homogeneous spaces
T/H
for a supertorus
T
. Since
T_{\overline{0}}
is central in
T
, any purely even subgroup
H_{\overline{0}}\subseteq T
is a normal subgroup, and so
T/H_{\overline{0}}
is a supertorus. The same is not true for an arbitrary supergroup
H
; in fact,
T/H
need not be a supergroup at all!

Example 4.4
.

Consider the supergroup
T=Q(1)^{2}:=Q(1)\times Q(1)
, with subgroup
H=Q(1)
embedded diagonally. It is routine to check that
H
is not a normal subgroup, and so
T/H
does not inherit the structure of a supergroup.

Consequently, in our definition, we make the compromise that the open orbit of a toric supervariety is isomorphic merely to a homogeneous space
T/H
for a purely odd (and therefore abelian) subgroup
H
.

Definition 4.5
.

A
toric supervariety
is an irreducible normal supervariety
X
equipped with a left action by a supertorus
T
, and a
T
-equivariant map
T\to X
which factors through an open immersion of a homogeneous space
T/H
. We will assume
H_{\overline{0}}=1
. If
H=1
, then the toric supervariety will be called
faithful
.

Occasionally we will wish to drop the normality criterion, at which times it will be clarified that the supervariety is not necessarily normal.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
X
be a (not necessarily normal) toric supervariety with torus
T
. Then the underlying variety
X_{\overline{0}}
is a (not necessarily normal) toric variety with torus
T_{\overline{0}}
.

Proof.

The action of
T_{\overline{0}}
on
X
preserves the defining ideal
\mathscr{J}_{X}
of
X_{\overline{0}}
.
∎

4.3.
Structure of affine toric supervarieties

The problem of understanding toric supervarieties reduces to the affine case, so we will first attempt to characterize which superalgebras
A
can occur as their regular functions.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
X=\Spec A
be an affine supervariety. Then:

(a)

X
is toric (but not necessarily normal) for a quotient of the supertorus
T
if and only if
A
is isomorphic to a finitely-generated subalgebra of
\mathbb{C}[T]
which is also a
\mathfrak{t}
-subrepresentation.

(b)

If
X
is toric (but not necessarily normal) for the supertorus
T
, then it is faithful if and only if there is a non-nilpotent
T_{\overline{0}}
-weight vector

such that
A[f^{-1}]\cong\mathbb{C}[T]
.

Proof.

First assume
A\subseteq\mathbb{C}[T]
is such a subalgebra as in (a). The inclusion
A\to\mathbb{C}[T]
dualizes to map
T\to X
which factors through an open immersion
U\to X
with
T\to U
surjective. Because
A
is a subrepresentation of
\mathbb{C}[T]
, all the above maps of supervarieties are
T
-equivariant. Hence
U\cong T/H
for some subgroup
H\subseteq T
, so
X
is a toric supervariety for
T/H_{\overline{0}}
. If there is a suitable weight vector

as in (b), then
U\cong T
is the basic open
D(f)
and so
X
is faithful.

Conversely, suppose
X
is toric with open orbit isomorphic to
T/H
. By Lemma
3.6
,
A\to\mathbb{C}[T/H]=\mathbb{C}[T]^{H}\to\mathbb{C}[T]
is injective. Moreover, the action of
T
on
X
becomes an action of
T
on
\mathbb{C}[X]
, so the conditions of (a) are satisfied. Now if
X
is faithful, consider the underlying variety
X_{\overline{0}}\supseteq T_{\overline{0}}
with reduced coordinate algebra
\overline{A}\subseteq\mathbb{C}[T_{\overline{0}}]
. Here, there is a
T_{\overline{0}}
-weight vector

such that
\overline{A}[t^{-m}]=\mathbb{C}[T_{\overline{0}}]
; i.e.
T_{\overline{0}}=D(t^{m})
. Let

lie in the preimage of

under the projection

. Then the open subvariety

has underlying variety

and so

.
∎

Henceforth consider only those toric supervarieties which are normal. By Lemma

More generally, a toric variety

for the torus

induces a fan

, i.e. a collection of cones which contains all the faces of each of its cones, and such that the intersection of any two cones in

is the semigroup of lattice points in the dual cone

In other words, a toric supervariety induces a decorated fan in the following sense:

Definition 4.14

A

is a tuple

.

Beginning in Definition

.

Our objective now is threefold: to determine which decorated fans arise from toric supervarieties, to determine to what extent we can recover a toric supervariety from its decorated fan, and to extract geometric information about a toric supervariety from its decorated fan. A first step towards the latter goal occurs in the following proposition, wherein we write
V_{\sigma,0}=\sum_{\rho\in\sigma(1)}V_{\rho,0}
for the odd part of the Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{h}_{\sigma,0}
.

Proposition 4.15
.

Let
X
be a toric supervariety with decorated fan
(N,\Sigma,\mathfrak{t},\mathfrak{h},\{V_{\rho,i}\})
, and let

be a closed point in the

\Stab_{T}y=(T_{N_{\sigma}},\mathfrak{t}_{N_{\sigma}}\oplus V_{\sigma,0})
.

Proof.

The even part of the orbit is determined entirely classically. Meanwhile, the odd part is
V_{\sigma,0}
because any odd coordinates

in

will be fixed at 0 in this orbit unless

.
∎

4.5.
Toric supervarieties with the same decorated fan

In many important situations, a decorated fan will correspond to a unique toric supervariety. However, for supertori with large odd abelian subalgebras, this is far from the case.

Example 4.16
.

Let
T
be the abelian supertorus of dimension
(1|4)
, so that its Lie superalgebra acts by the derivations
t\frac{\partial}{\partial t}
and
\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi_{i}}
for
i=1,...,4
on
\mathbb{C}[T]=\mathbb{C}[t^{\pm 1},\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3},\xi_{4}]
. We consider the following two superalgebras:

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=\mathbb{C}[t,t\xi_{1},t\xi_{2},t\xi_{3},t^{2}\xi_{4}]

\displaystyle A^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{C}[t,t\xi_{1},t\xi_{2},t\xi_{3},t^{2}(\xi_{4}+\xi_{1}\xi_{2}\xi_{3})]

Both
\Spec A
and
\Spec A^{\prime}
admit the same decorations

, and both are isomorphic to

as a supervariety. However, the actions of

are decidedly different.

In fact, for particularly large abelian supertori, classification is a wild problem.

Proposition 4.17
.

A classification of toric supervarieties contains a classification of triples of skew-symmetric bilinear forms up to simultaneous congruence.

Proof.

Let
T

acts by

Let

be the positive orthant, corresponding to the toric variety
\Spec\mathbb{C}[t_{A},t_{B},t_{C}]

\displaystyle V_{\rho_{\ell},0}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{C}\{\theta_{1},...,\theta_{n},\theta_{\ell},\theta^{\prime}\}

\displaystyle V_{\rho_{\ell},1}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{C}\{\theta_{\ell},\theta^{\prime}\}

\displaystyle V_{\rho_{\ell},2}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{C}\{\theta^{\prime}\}

\displaystyle V_{\rho_{\ell},3}

\displaystyle=0

for

.

We will attempt to classify toric supervarieties for

inducing the decorated fan

specified above. For each

\displaystyle=\mathbb{C}\left[t_{A},t_{B}^{\pm 1},t_{C}^{\pm 1},t_{A}\xi_{1},...,t_{A}\xi_{n},t_{A}^{2}\xi_{A},\xi_{B},\xi_{C},t_{A}^{3}\left(\xi^{\prime}+\xi_{A}\left(\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}a_{ij}\xi_{i}\xi_{j}\right)\right)\right]

\displaystyle A_{\rho_{B}}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{C}\left[t_{A}^{\pm 1},t_{B},t_{C}^{\pm 1},t_{B}\xi_{1},...,t_{B}\xi_{n},\xi_{A},t_{B}^{2}\xi_{B},\xi_{C},t_{B}^{3}\left(\xi^{\prime}+\xi_{B}\left(\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}b_{ij}\xi_{i}\xi_{j}\right)\right)\right]

Remark 4.19
.

The various equivalent characterization of
(HR_{1})
can be summarized as follows:

Characterizations (a) and (b) mean it is relatively straightforward to construct

from its decorated fan. Meanwhile, (c) says that locally, away from a closed subset of codimension

(HR_{1})

Finally, (e) links this property with the Duflo-Serganova functor

of

, i.e.

\displaystyle DS_{\theta}A

\displaystyle=\ker(\theta:A^{\theta^{2}})/\Im(\theta:A^{\theta^{2}})

\displaystyle=A^{\theta}/\theta(A^{\theta^{2}}),

which is naturally a superalgebra.

The quotient

which admits an action of the abelian supertorus

In Example

\displaystyle\cong\mathbb{C}[t\xi_{2},t\xi_{3},t^{2}\xi_{4}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{C}[t,t\xi_{2},t\xi_{3},t^{3}\xi_{4}]_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}\cap\mathbb{C}[t,t\xi_{2},t\xi_{3},t^{3}\xi_{4}]}/(t,t^{2}\xi_{2}\xi_{3},t^{3}\xi_{4})

W\subseteq V_{\sigma,0}
. Then
A_{\sigma}\supseteq L_{\mathbb{C}[T]^{W}}(m)\neq 0
if and only if
W
satisfies

(a)

\langle m,[W,W]\rangle=0

(b)

V_{\rho,m}\subseteq W
for all
\rho\in\sigma(1)

(c)

\sum_{i=0}^{\langle m,u_{\rho}\rangle-1}(\dim V_{\rho,i}-\dim V_{\rho,i}\cap W)\leq\langle m,u_{\rho}\rangle
for all
\rho\in\sigma(1)

Proof.

First observe that condition (a) is equivalent to
L_{\mathbb{C}[T]^{W}}(m)\neq 0
. It remains to show equivalence of (b) and (c) with
A_{\sigma}\supseteq L_{\mathbb{C}[T]^{W}}(m)
.

We begin with the case of
\sigma=\rho
a ray. Consider the expression

A_{\rho}=\mathbb{C}[t_{1},t_{2}^{\pm 1},...,t_{p}^{\pm 1},t_{1}^{\ell_{1}}\xi_{1},...,t_{1}^{\ell_{q}}\xi_{q}]

obtained in Proposition/Definition
4.18
. If
W\subseteq V_{\rho,0}
satisfies the three properties, assume without loss of generality that
\xi_{1},...,\xi_{d}
is a basis for
(\mathfrak{t}_{\overline{1}}/W)^{*}
compatible with

V_{\rho,0}=V_{\rho,0}+W\supseteq V_{\rho,1}+W\supseteq...\supseteq V_{\rho,m}+W=W

in the sense that each
(\mathfrak{t}_{\overline{1}}/(V_{\rho,j}+W))^{*}
is spanned by a subset of this basis. Then
\ell_{j}
, for
j=1,...,d
, is the smallest nonnegative integer such that
\xi_{j}
does not vanish on
V_{\rho,j}+W
. Hence

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{d}\ell_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=1}^{\langle m,u_{\rho}\rangle}\ell\cdot(\dim(V_{\rho,\ell-1}+W)-\dim(V_{\rho,\ell}+W))

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{\langle m,u_{\rho}\rangle-1}(\dim(V_{\rho,i}+W)-\dim(V_{\rho,m}+W))

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{\langle m,u_{\rho}\rangle-1}(\dim V_{\rho,i}+\dim W-\dim(V_{\rho,i}\cap W)-\dim W)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{\langle m,u_{\rho}\rangle-1}(\dim V_{\rho,i}-\dim V_{\rho,i}\cap W)

\displaystyle\leq\langle m,u_{\rho}\rangle

by property (c), so indeed
A_{\rho}
contains all
s^{m}\xi_{I}
for
I\subseteq\{1,...,d\}
. Therefore
A_{\sigma}\supseteq L_{\mathbb{C}[T]^{W}}(m)
.

Conversely, if
A_{\rho}\supseteq L_{\mathbb{C}[T]^{W}}(m)
, then the same chain of equalities above shows that
\sum_{i=0}^{\langle m,u_{\rho}\rangle-1}(\dim V_{\rho,i}-\dim V_{\rho,i}\cap W)\leq\langle m,u_{\rho}\rangle
and
V_{\rho,m}\subseteq W
.

We now observe that properties (b) and (c) for
W\subseteq V_{\sigma,0}
are equivalent to the same two properties for
W\cap V_{\rho,0}\subseteq V_{\rho,0}
for each
\rho\in\sigma(1)
. Hence, if
W\subseteq V_{\sigma,0}
satisfies (b) and (c), then

\displaystyle L_{\mathbb{C}[T]^{W}}(m)

\displaystyle\subseteq L_{\mathbb{C}[T]^{W\cap V_{\rho,0}}}(m)

\displaystyle\subseteq A_{\rho}

for all
\rho
, so indeed
L_{\mathbb{C}[T]^{W}}(m)\subseteq A_{\sigma}
. Conversely, suppose
L_{\mathbb{C}[T]^{W}}(m)\subseteq A_{\sigma}
. Since
L_{\mathbb{C}[T]^{V_{\rho,0}}}(0)\subseteq A_{\rho}
, it follows that
L_{\mathbb{C}[T]^{W\cap V_{\rho,0}}}(m)\subseteq A_{\rho}
, so
W\cap V_{\rho,0}
satisfies (b) and (c). Therefore the lemma is proven.
∎

In view of both the prior lemma and those forthcoming, we make the following definition.

Definition 4.21
.

We say
W\subseteq V_{\sigma,0}
satisfies the
dimension jumping property with respect to
\sigma
and
m
, or
DJ(\sigma,m)
, if
W
satisfies the three properties of Lemma
4.20
. If
\rho
is a ray, sometimes we will write
DJ(\rho,n)
when
\langle m,u_{\rho}\rangle=n
.

The following lemma is essentially a stronger version of Lemma
4.13
(c). Since
W\subseteq V_{\sigma,0}
means
[W,W]\subseteq\mathfrak{t}_{N_{\sigma}}
, we note that condition (a) of Lemma
4.20
is equivalent to
[W,W]\subseteq\mathfrak{t}_{N_{\sigma_{m}}}
.

Lemma 4.22
.

Let
X
be a
(HR_{1})
toric supervariety with decorated fan
(N,\Sigma,\mathfrak{t},\mathfrak{h},\{V_{\rho,i}\})
. Then for each
\sigma\in\Sigma
and
m\in\sigma^{\vee}\cap M
, there is a subspace
W\subseteq V_{\sigma,0}
satisfying
DJ(\sigma,m)
.

Proof.

Let
\sigma\in\Sigma
and
m\in\sigma^{\vee}\cap M
. Then
L_{A_{\sigma}}(m)\neq 0
, so it contains a simple
T
-submodule
C(m)
. We have
C(m)=L_{\mathbb{C}[T/S]}(m)
for some
S\subseteq T
with
S_{\overline{0}}=T_{\ker m}
. Let
W=\mathfrak{s}_{\overline{1}}\cap V_{\sigma,0}
, so that
L_{\mathbb{C}[T]^{W}}\subseteq L_{A_{\sigma}}(m)
and hence
W
satisfies
DJ(\sigma,m)
by Lemma
4.20
.
∎

It turns out that the condition of Lemma
4.13
(d) is sufficient to provide a converse to Lemma
4.22
. Therefore we will hereafter refer exclusively to the following enhanced notion of a decorated fan.

Definition 4.23
.

A
decorated fan
is a tuple
(N,\Sigma,\mathfrak{t},\mathfrak{h},\{V_{\rho,i}\}_{\rho\in\Sigma(1),i\geq 0})
consisting of a finite-dimensional lattice
N
, a fan
\Sigma
in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
, a quasiabelian Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{t}
with
\mathfrak{t}_{\overline{0}}=\mathfrak{t}_{N}
, an odd abelian subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}\subseteq\mathfrak{t}
, and a collection of descending chains
V_{\rho,0}\supseteq V_{\rho,1}\supseteq...
of subspaces of
\mathfrak{t}_{\overline{1}}
, subject to the following compatibility conditions:

(a)

For each
\rho\in\Sigma(1)
, the chain
V_{\rho,0}\supseteq V_{\rho,1}\supseteq...
stabilizes at
\mathfrak{h}
.

(b)

For each
\sigma\in\Sigma
and
m\in\sigma^{\vee}\cap M
, there is a subspace
W\subseteq V_{\sigma,0}
satisfying
DJ(\sigma,m)
.

(c)

For each
\rho\in\Sigma(1)
, either
[V_{\rho,0},V_{\rho,0}]=0
or
\codim(\mathfrak{h},V_{\rho,0})=1
.

Theorem
1
.

Any decorated fan induces a unique
(HR_{1})
toric supervariety, up to isomorphism.

Proof.

Proposition/Definition
4.18
gives us both uniqueness and a method to construct the candidate toric supervariety. This method depends on conditions (b), (c), and (d) of Lemma
4.13
, which are guaranteed by parts (a), (b), and (c), respectively, of Definition
4.23
. So, for
\rho\in\Sigma(1)
, let
A_{\rho}
be as in Proposition/Definition
4.18
, and let
A_{\sigma}=\bigcap_{\rho\in\sigma(1)}A_{\rho}
.

We first verify that
A_{\sigma}
is finitely generated. First note that since each weight space is finite-dimensional, we may assume it contains only finitely many generators. Now choose
m\in\sigma^{\vee}\cap M
such that
L_{A_{\sigma}}(m)=L_{\mathbb{C}[T]^{H}}(m)
. Then we may assume every generator occurs in
L_{A_{\sigma}}(m^{\prime})
for
m^{\prime}\leq_{\sigma}m
, of which there are finitely many (up to
\sigma^{\perp}
). We conclude that
A_{\sigma}
is finitely generated, and hence that
\Spec A_{\sigma}
is a (not necessarily normal) toric supervariety by Lemma
4.7
.

For normality, we show that
\overline{A_{\sigma}}=\mathbb{C}[S_{\sigma}]
. Since every nonzero submodule of
\mathbb{C}[T]
contains a non-nilpotent element, it suffices to show that
L_{A_{\sigma}}(m)
is nonzero for every
m\in\sigma^{\vee}\cap M
. Using condition (b) of the definition, we obtain from Lemma
4.20
the desired result.

We now claim that
A_{\sigma}
localizes to
A_{\rho}
. It suffices to show that for each
n\geq 0
, there is
m\in\sigma^{\vee}\cap M
such that
\langle m,u_{\rho}\rangle=n
and
L_{A_{\rho}}(m)=L_{A_{\sigma}}(m)
. This is merely a consequence of the convex geometry of cones. In particular, the intersection
(n+\rho^{\perp})\cap\sigma^{\vee}\cap M
is infinite, so its elements have arbitrarily large pairings with
u_{\rho^{\prime}}
for
\rho^{\prime}\in\sigma(1)\backslash\{\rho\}
. Therefore there is
m\in(n+\rho^{\perp})\cap\sigma^{\vee}\cap M
such that
L_{A_{\rho^{\prime}}}(m)=L_{\mathbb{C}[T]^{H}}(m)
for all such
\rho^{\prime}
, so indeed
L_{A_{\rho}}(m)=L_{A_{\sigma}}(m)
. It then follows by Proposition
4.9
that
\Spec A_{\sigma}
is normal, and by Proposition/Definition
4.18
that
\Spec A_{\sigma}
is
(HR_{1})
, completing the proof.
∎

4.8.
The abelian case

Suppose
T
is an abelian supertorus, and let
X
be a toric supervariety for
T
. Since any subgroup of
T
is normal, we may assume that the morphism
T\to X
is an open immersion, i.e.
H=1
.

In this situation, since the bracket is 0, every
V_{\sigma,0}
satisfies
DJ(\sigma,m)
, and conditions (b) and (c) of Definition
4.23
are immaterial. It follows that a decorated fan can be expressed simply as a fan
\Sigma
such that each ray
\rho\in\Sigma(1)
is decorated by a decreasing chain of subspaces
V_{\rho,0}\supseteq V_{\rho,1}\supseteq V_{\rho,2}\supseteq...
of
\mathfrak{t}_{\overline{1}}
that stabilizes at 0.

Example 4.24
.

We may write projective superspace
\mathbb{P}^{p|q}=\Proj\mathbb{C}[z_{0},...,z_{p},\zeta_{1},...,\zeta_{q}]
as a toric supervariety for an abelian supertorus
T
of dimension
p|q
as follows: As in the usual case, let
T_{\overline{0}}
act by
m\in\mathbb{Z}^{p}
on the monomial
z_{0}^{r}z_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdots z_{p}^{m_{p}}
for any
r\in\mathbb{Z}
. Likewise, let
\mathfrak{t}_{\overline{1}}
act by translations on the odd part so that
\theta_{i}
acts by
\frac{\partial}{\partial\zeta_{i}}
on the coordinate superalgebras.

As usual, we obtain a complete fan
\Sigma
whose rays are
\rho_{i}=\mathbb{R}_{+}e_{i}
for
i=1,...,n
and
\rho_{0}=\mathbb{R}_{+}(-e_{1}-...-e_{p})
. If
\sigma
is the positive orthant
\mathbb{R}_{+}(e_{1},...,e_{p})
, then
A_{\sigma}=\mathbb{C}[\frac{z_{1}}{z_{0}},...,\frac{z_{p}}{z_{0}},\frac{\zeta_{1}}{z_{0}},...,\frac{\zeta_{q}}{z_{0}}]\cong\mathbb{C}[t_{1},...,t_{p},\xi_{1},...,\xi_{q}]
and the other coordinate superalgebras of maximal cones are
\mathbb{C}[t_{i}^{-1}t_{1},...,t_{i}^{-1}t_{p},t_{i}^{-1}\xi_{1},...,t_{i}^{-1}\xi_{q}]
for
i=1,...,p
. Hence the decorations consist of
0\supseteq 0\supseteq...
on
\rho_{1},...,\rho_{p}
and
\mathfrak{t}_{\overline{1}}\supseteq 0\supseteq...
on
\rho_{0}
.

Alternatively, by adjusting the characters by which
T_{\overline{0}}
acts on the odd parts of the affine charts, one could arrange for any collection of subspaces
V_{\rho_{i},0}
as the decorations, so long as they are linearly independent and span
\mathfrak{t}_{\overline{1}}
.

Since the action of
\mathfrak{t}_{\overline{1}}
on
\mathbb{C}[T]
is by
\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi}
for
\xi\in\mathfrak{t}_{\overline{1}}^{*}
, it follows that there exists a natural splitting
\overline{A}\to A
of the projection
A\to\overline{A}
. These splittings assemble into a global splitting
X\to X_{\overline{0}}
, so that any
(HR_{1})
toric supervariety for an abelian supertorus is
split
in this sense.

4.9.
The case of large orbits

In this section we will assume that
\codim(\mathfrak{h},V_{\rho,0})\leq 1
, so that the odd codimension of an orbit never exceeds the even codimension. Such a toric supervariety will be said to have
large orbits
. In view of Proposition/Definition
4.18
, it is clear that toric supervarieties with large orbits are always
(HR_{1})
. Moreover,
\sum_{\rho\in\sigma(1)}V_{\rho,m}
always satisfies
DJ(\sigma,m)
, so condition (b) of Definition
4.23
can be weakened to the condition of Lemma
4.13
(c). These observations are summarized in the following definition and proposition:

Definition 4.25
.

A
large-orbit decorated fan
is a tuple
(N,\Sigma,\mathfrak{t},\mathfrak{h},\{V_{\rho,i}\})
consisting of a finite-dimensional lattice
N
, a fan
\Sigma
in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
, a quasiabelian Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{t}
with
\mathfrak{t}_{\overline{0}}=\mathfrak{t}_{N}
, an odd abelian subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}\subseteq\mathfrak{t}
, and a collection of descending chains
V_{\rho,0}\supseteq V_{\rho,1}\supseteq...
of subspaces of
\mathfrak{t}_{\overline{1}}
, subject to the following compatibility conditions:

(a)

For each
\rho\in\Sigma(1)
, the chain
V_{\rho,0}\supseteq V_{\rho,1}\supseteq...
stabilizes at
\mathfrak{h}
.

(b)

If
\rho,\rho^{\prime}\in\sigma(1)
and
m\in\sigma^{\vee}\cap M
, then
[V_{\rho,m},V_{\rho^{\prime},m}]\subseteq\mathfrak{t}_{N_{\sigma_{m}}}
.

(c)

For each
\rho\in\Sigma(1)
,
\codim(\mathfrak{h},V_{\rho,0})\leq 1
.

In particular, each chain
V_{\rho,0}\supseteq V_{\rho,1}\supseteq...
is constant except possibly for a one-dimensional jump at a single index.

Proposition 4.26
.

Up to
T
-equivariant isomorphism, there is a bijective correspondence between large-orbit decorated fans and toric supervarieties with large orbits.

For supertori such as
T=Q(1)^{n}
that have no nonzero square roots of 0 (or more generally, no purely odd subalgebras properly containing
\mathfrak{h}
), every toric supervariety has large orbits by Lemma
4.13
(d). Moreover, since
[V_{\rho,1},V_{\rho,1}]=0
, it holds that
V_{\rho,1}=\mathfrak{h}
. Consequently, all the data of such a toric supervariety are concentrated in the fan decorations
V_{\rho,0}
for
\rho\in\Sigma(1)
. The data of a decorated fan can then be simplified as in the following corollary.

Corollary 4.27
.

Let
T
be a supertorus such as
Q(1)^{n}
with no nonzero square roots of 0. Then a decorated fan with
\mathfrak{t}=\Lie T
can be reduced to the data of
(N,\Sigma,\mathfrak{t},\{V_{\rho}\}_{\rho\in\Sigma(1)})
satisfying the following compatibility conditions.

(a)

If
\rho,\rho^{\prime}\in\sigma(1)
, then
[V_{\rho},V_{\rho^{\prime}}]\subseteq\mathfrak{t}_{N_{\sigma}}
.

(b)

For each
\rho\in\Sigma(1)
,
\dim(V_{\rho})\leq 1
.

We will explore some examples of such toric supervarieties presently, as well as in section
5
.

4.10.
Decorated polytopes

Classically, a toric variety
X_{\Sigma}
is projective if and only if
\Sigma
is the normal fan
\Sigma_{P}
of a lattice polytope
P
in
M_{\mathbb{R}}:=M\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}
. In particular, a face
F
of dimension
r
induces a cone
\sigma_{F}
of codimension
r
, which in turn corresponds to a torus orbit of dimension
r
.

Suppose
X
is a toric supervariety whose underlying variety is
X_{\Sigma_{P}}
. Due to the bijection between faces of
P
and cones of
\Sigma_{P}
, we may assign the same data
V_{\sigma_{F},0}\supseteq V_{\sigma_{F},1}\supseteq...
to each face
F
of codimension 1 in
P
. Since
M_{\mathbb{R}}
is a real form of
\mathfrak{t}_{\overline{0}}^{*}
and
V_{\sigma_{F}}\subseteq\mathfrak{t}_{\overline{1}}
, we will prefer the assignment
W_{F,i}:=(\mathfrak{t}_{\overline{1}}/V_{\sigma_{F},i})^{*}\subseteq\mathfrak{t}_{\overline{1}}^{*}
when attempting to decorate such a polytope.

Of course, in order for a decorated polytope to define a toric supervariety, one requires compatibility conditions on the decorations
W_{F,i}
. We neglect to state these on the grounds that they are essentially the same as those in Definitions
4.23
and
4.25
, and that they are easier to express in terms of the
V_{\rho,i}
.

Suppose the decorated polytope
(P,\{W_{F,i}\})
corresponds to a toric supervariety with large orbits, and define
W_{F}=(\mathfrak{t}_{\overline{1}}/V_{\sigma_{F},0})^{*}
for all faces
F\subseteq P
. We may then view the decorated polytope as the subset
\mathscr{P}=\bigcup_{F\in P}\Int F\times W_{F}
of
M_{\mathbb{R}}\oplus\mathfrak{t}_{\overline{1}}^{*}
, where
\Int F
is the relative interior of
F
. This subset fails to be bounded or even a polyhedron, but it is at least convex and locally closed. The upshot of this viewpoint will be explored more in section
5
.

4.11.
Toric morphisms

Towards a categorical version of our classification of toric supervarieties, we now consider toric morphisms. Let
X
and
X^{\prime}
be toric supervarieties with open orbits
T/H
and
T^{\prime}/H^{\prime}
, respectively.

Definition 4.31
.

A
toric morphism

X\to X^{\prime}
is a commutative diagram
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T/H,}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T^{\prime}/H^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{\phi}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi}

where
\phi
is a morphism of supergroups such that
\phi(H)\subseteq H^{\prime}
,
\widetilde{\phi}
is the induced map
T/H\to T^{\prime}/H^{\prime}
, and
\varphi:X\to X^{\prime}
extends
\widetilde{\phi}
.

A toric morphism of toric supervarieties naturally drops to a toric morphism of the underlying toric varieties. So, we obtain from the pair
(\phi,\varphi)
a morphism
\phi^{\circ}:N\to N^{\prime}
of lattices such that for each
\sigma\in\Sigma
, there is
\sigma^{\prime}\in\Sigma^{\prime}
for which
\phi^{\circ}_{\mathbb{R}}(\sigma)\subseteq\sigma^{\prime}
. Such a
\phi^{\circ}
is called a
morphism of fans
.

In addition, we obtain a morphism
d\phi:\mathfrak{t}\to\mathfrak{t}^{\prime}
of Lie superalgebras such that
\phi^{\circ}_{\mathbb{C}}=d\phi_{\overline{0}}
. It remains to describe the compatibility conditions with respect to the decorations. Suppose
\phi^{\circ}_{\mathbb{R}}(\sigma)\subseteq\sigma^{\prime}
, so we may reduce to the affine case
\varphi:\Spec A_{\sigma}\to\Spec A^{\prime}_{\sigma^{\prime}}
. The map
\varphi
dualizes to
\varphi^{*}:A^{\prime}_{\sigma^{\prime}}\to A_{\sigma}
, a restriction of
\phi^{*}:\mathbb{C}[T^{\prime}]\to\mathbb{C}[T]
. The underlying map of rings
\overline{\varphi^{*}}:\mathbb{C}[\sigma^{\prime\vee}\cap M^{\prime}]\to\mathbb{C}[\sigma^{\vee}\cap M]
sends
m^{\prime}\in M^{\prime}\cong\Hom(N^{\prime},\mathbb{Z})
to
m^{\prime}\circ\phi^{\circ}\in M\cong\Hom(N,\mathbb{Z})
, so it must hold that
\phi^{*}(L_{A^{\prime}_{\sigma^{\prime}}}(m^{\prime}))\subseteq L_{A_{\sigma}}(m^{\prime}\circ\phi^{\circ})
.

Thus, a toric morphism is determined by a morphism of fans
\phi^{\circ}:(N,\Sigma)\to(N^{\prime},\Sigma^{\prime})
together with a compatible morphism of Lie superalgebras
d\phi:\mathfrak{t}\to\mathfrak{t}^{\prime}
such that
\phi^{*}(L_{A^{\prime}_{\sigma^{\prime}}}(m^{\prime}))\subseteq L_{A_{\sigma}}(m^{\prime}\circ\phi^{\circ})
whenever
\phi^{\circ}_{\mathbb{R}}(\sigma)\subseteq\sigma^{\prime}
and
m^{\prime}\in\sigma^{\prime\vee}\cap M
. One would hope that a condition involving only the decorations
V_{\rho,i}
and
V^{\prime}_{\rho^{\prime},i}
would suffice. For large-orbit toric supervarieties, such a condition is indeed viable due to the following property.

Lemma 4.32
.

Let
X
be a toric supervariety with large orbits, and let
(N,\Sigma,\mathfrak{t},\mathfrak{h},\{V_{\rho,i}\})
be its decorated fan. Then for
\sigma\in\Sigma
, it holds that
L_{A_{\sigma}}(m)=L_{\mathbb{C}[T]^{V_{\sigma,m}}}(m)
where
V_{\sigma,m}=\sum_{\rho\in\sigma(1)}V_{\rho,m}
.

Proof.

The claim is immediate in the case of
\dim\sigma\leq 1
by construction of
A_{\sigma}
in Proposition/Definition
4.18
. Otherwise we have

\displaystyle L_{A_{\sigma}}(m)

\displaystyle=\bigcap_{\rho\in\sigma(1)}L_{A_{\rho}}(m)

\displaystyle=\bigcap_{\rho\in\sigma(1)}L_{\mathbb{C}[T]^{V_{\rho,m}}}(m)

\displaystyle=L_{\mathbb{C}[T]^{V_{\sigma,m}}}(m)

and we are finished.
∎

Hence the condition that
\phi^{*}(L_{A^{\prime}_{\sigma^{\prime}}}(m^{\prime}))\subseteq L_{A_{\sigma}}(m^{\prime}\circ\phi^{\circ})
is equivalent to
\phi^{*}(L_{\mathbb{C}[T^{\prime}]^{V^{\prime}_{\sigma^{\prime},m^{\prime}}}}(m^{\prime}))\subseteq L_{\mathbb{C}[T]^{V_{\sigma,m^{\prime}\circ\phi^{\circ}}}}(m^{\prime}\circ\phi^{\circ})
or simply
d\phi(V_{\sigma,m^{\prime}\circ\phi^{\circ}})\subseteq V^{\prime}_{\sigma^{\prime},m^{\prime}}
.

Definition 4.33
.

Let
(N,\Sigma,\mathfrak{t},\mathfrak{h},\{V_{\rho,i}\})
and
(N^{\prime},\Sigma^{\prime},\mathfrak{t}^{\prime},\mathfrak{h}^{\prime},\{V^{\prime}_{\rho^{\prime},i}\})
be large-orbit decorated fans, with
\phi^{\circ}:(N,\Sigma)\to(N^{\prime},\Sigma^{\prime})
a morphism of fans and
d\phi:\mathfrak{t}\to\mathfrak{t}^{\prime}
a compatible morphism of Lie superalgebras. We say

is

. In this situation, we call the pair
(\phi^{\circ},d\phi)
a
morphism of large-orbit decorated fans
.

That is, we have the following proposition:

Proposition 4.34

Let
X
and
X^{\prime}
be toric supervarieties with large orbits. A morphism
\phi:T\to T^{\prime}
of supergroups drops to

and subsequently extends to a toric morphism

if and only if
(\phi^{\circ},d\phi)
is a morphism of large-orbit decorated fans.

Proof.

As above, this follows from Lemma
4.32
.
∎

We may then define the category of large-orbit decorated fans via the objects and morphisms of Definitions

and
4.33

4.26

4.34

Theorem
2

The category of large-orbit toric supervarieties is equivalent to the category of large-orbit decorated fans.

4.12.
Smoothness of toric supervarieties

In this section, we will provide a criterion for smoothness of a toric supervariety based on its decorated fan.

In the classical situation, a toric variety
X
is smooth if and only if each cone
\sigma
in its fan
\Sigma
is a
smooth cone
in the sense that the primitive generators of its rays form part of a basis for
N
. This has the effect that every smooth affine toric variety is isomorphic to
\mathbb{C}^{n}\times(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{p-n}
for some
p\geq n\geq 0
, a fact which we will use in our proof of Theorem
3
.

Definition 4.36
.

Let
X
be a supervariety and
x\in X(\mathbb{C})
a closed point. We say
X
is
smooth
at
x
if
\mathscr{O}_{X,x}
is a regular local superring. We say
X
is
smooth
if it is smooth at all closed points.

There exist many equivalent formulations for smoothness of a supervariety (or more broadly, smoothness of a morphism of superschemes), several of which can be found in Appendix B of
[
17
]
.

Theorem 3
.

Let
X
be a
(HR_{1})
toric supervariety with decorated fan
(N,\Sigma,\mathfrak{t},\mathfrak{h},\{V_{\rho,i}\})
. Then
X
is smooth if and only if for every cone
\sigma\in\Sigma
, the following three conditions hold:

(a)

\sigma
is a smooth cone.

(b)

There is a basis of
\mathfrak{t}_{\overline{1}}
such that for all
\rho\in\sigma(1)
and
i\geq 0
,
V_{\rho,i}
is the span of a subset of that basis.

(c)

For each
\rho\in\sigma(1)
, if
\codim(\mathfrak{h},V_{\rho,0})\geq 2
, then
[V_{\rho,0},V_{\rho^{\prime},0}]\subseteq\mathfrak{t}_{N_{\rho^{\prime}}}
for all
\rho^{\prime}\in\sigma(1)
.

Proof.

We may assume
X
is affine, and that
X_{\overline{0}}\cong\mathbb{C}^{n}\times(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{p-n}
, so
X
corresponds to the cone
\sigma
whose rays are
\rho_{i}=\mathbb{R}_{+}e_{i}
for
i=1,...,n
, where
e_{i}
is the
i
th unit vector in
\mathbb{Z}^{p}
. If
X
is smooth, then (a) is immediate and we may write

A_{\sigma}=\mathbb{C}[t_{1},...,t_{n},t_{n+1}^{\pm 1},...,t_{p}^{\pm 1},t^{m_{1}}(\xi_{1}+...),...,t^{m_{q}}(\xi_{q}+...)]

for some non-nilpotent
t_{i}
, linearly independent
\xi_{i}\in\mathfrak{t}_{\overline{1}}^{*}
, and products of nilpotent terms represented by ellipses. Localizing to some
A_{\rho_{i}}
, we see that we may assume each
\xi_{j}+...
simply equals
\xi_{j}
. We therefore have

A_{\sigma}=\mathbb{C}[t_{1},...,t_{n},t_{n+1}^{\pm 1},...,t_{p}^{\pm 1},t^{m_{1}}\xi_{1},...,t^{m_{q}}\xi_{q}]

and in particular

A_{\rho_{i}}=\mathbb{C}[t_{1}^{\pm 1},...,t_{i},...,t_{p}^{\pm 1},t_{i}^{\langle m_{1},e_{i}\rangle}\xi_{1},...,t_{i}^{\langle m_{q},e_{i}\rangle}\xi_{q}]

for
i=1,...,n
. Choosing a basis of
\mathfrak{t}_{\overline{1}}^{*}
that includes the
\xi_{i}
yields a dual basis
\theta_{1},...,\theta_{q},\theta_{q+1},...,\theta_{r}
of
\mathfrak{t}_{\overline{1}}
for which
V_{\rho_{i},m}=\mathbb{C}\{\theta_{j}\mid\langle m,e_{i}\rangle<\langle m_{j},e_{i}\rangle\}\oplus\mathbb{C}\{\theta_{q+1},...,\theta_{r}\}
. Hence (b) holds.

Now for (c), note that if
\codim(\mathfrak{h},V_{\rho_{i},0})\geq 2
, then by construction of
A_{\rho}
in Proposition/Definition
4.18
, we have
t_{i}\in\mathbb{C}[T]^{V_{\rho_{i},0}}
. Since
t_{i}\in\mathbb{C}[T]^{V_{\rho_{\ell},0}}
for all
\ell\neq i
as well, it follows that
t_{i}\in\mathbb{C}[T]^{[V_{\rho_{i},0},V_{\rho_{\ell},0}]}
and therefore
\langle\varepsilon_{i},[V_{\rho_{i},0},V_{\rho_{\ell},0}]\rangle=0
, where
\{\varepsilon_{i}\}
in
M
is dual to
\{e_{i}\}
in
N
. Hence
[V_{\rho_{i},0},V_{\rho_{\ell},0}]\subseteq\mathfrak{t}_{N_{\rho_{\ell}}}
and we are finished.

Conversely, suppose properties (a), (b), and (c) hold. As above, property (c) allows us to find
t_{i}\in\mathbb{C}[T]^{\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}V_{\rho_{\ell},0}}
if
\codim(\mathfrak{h},V_{\rho_{i},0})\geq 2
, or otherwise in
\mathbb{C}[T]^{V_{\rho_{i},1}+\sum_{\ell\neq i}V_{\rho_{\ell},0}}
. Now pick such a basis
\theta_{1},...,\theta_{r}
as in (b), where
\mathfrak{h}=\mathbb{C}\{\theta_{q+1},...,\theta_{r}\}
, and let
\xi_{1},...,\xi_{r}
be the dual basis of
\mathfrak{t}_{\overline{1}}^{*}
. Then each

A_{\rho_{i}}=\mathbb{C}[t_{1}^{\pm 1},...,t_{i},...,t_{p}^{\pm 1},t_{i}^{n_{i1}}\xi_{1},...,t_{i}^{n_{iq}}\xi_{q}]

where
n_{ij}\geq 0
is minimal such that
\theta_{j}\notin V_{\rho_{i},n_{ij}}
. Then let
m_{j}=n_{1j}\varepsilon_{1}+...+n_{qj}\varepsilon_{q}
, so

A_{\sigma}=\mathbb{C}[t_{1},...,t_{n},t_{n+1}^{\pm 1},...,t_{p}^{\pm 1},t^{m_{1}}\xi_{1},...,t^{m_{q}}\xi_{q}]

and indeed
X
is smooth.
∎

This theorem simplifies in the case of a toric supervariety with large orbits. In particular, condition (c) is immaterial, so smoothness in the odd directions depends entirely on finding such a basis as in (b).

This condition on basis elements is equivalent to Klyachko’s condition
[
12
]
that a descending filtration
E=...\supseteq E^{\rho}(-1)\supseteq E^{\rho}(0)\supseteq E^{\rho}(1)\supseteq...=0
for each
\rho\in\Sigma(1)
specifies a toric vector bundle with fiber
E
over the identity. To obtain a Klyachko filtration from our
V_{\rho,0}\supseteq V_{\rho,1}\supseteq...
, one must take
E^{\rho}(i)=0
for
i>0
and
E^{\rho}(i)=(\mathfrak{t}_{\overline{1}}/V_{\rho,-i})^{*}
for
i\leq 0
. These Klyachko data yield the vector bundle
\mathscr{J}_{X}/\mathscr{J}_{X}^{2}
, the conormal bundle of
X_{\overline{0}}
in
X
.

Note the sign discrepancy in
E^{\rho}(i)=(\mathfrak{t}_{\overline{1}}/V_{\rho,-i})^{*}
; this occurs because
E^{\rho}(i)
is the
im_{1}
-eigenspace of
J_{A_{\rho}}/J_{A_{\rho}}^{2}
for the left regular representation, wherein the even torus acts by the character
-m
on the monomial
t^{m}
.

Using this characterization of smoothness, it is not difficult to construct a toric resolution of singularities for a toric supervariety.

Proposition 4.37
.

Let
X
be a toric supervariety for the supertorus

. Then there exists a smooth toric supervariety

for

and a proper, birational,

Proof.

Let

be the decorated fan of

\Sigma^{\prime}
of
\Sigma
(with the same support) by introducing new rays until each maximal cone is smooth and contains at most one ray from the original fan
\Sigma

\rho^{\prime}
by

for all

. Since each cone contains at most one of the original rays, the resulting decorated fan

satisfies the conditions of Theorem
3

We now verify that the identity

extends to

and a cone

such that

\rho^{\prime}\notin\Sigma

has trivial decorations and so each

. Otherwise

. Therefore we obtain a

. Birationality is immediate since the open orbits are both isomorphic to

. Finally, properness of the underlying morphism of varieties follows since the fans have the same support; this is sufficient since properness depends only on the underlying varieties (see e.g.
[
1
]
).
∎

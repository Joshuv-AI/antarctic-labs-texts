Toroidal Cartesian Products Where One Factor is 3 3 -Connected
Elizabeth Badgett, Christian Millichap

Abstract
. In this paper, we provide a partial classification of Cartesian products of graphs that embed on the torus where one factor is 3 3 -connected. We show that if G □ H G\boxempty H embeds on the torus and G G is 3 3 -connected, then H H must be a path on two vertices, P 2 P_{2} , and we provide significant evidence that G G must be outer-cylindrical. At the same time, we show that if G G is outer-cylindrical, then G □ P 2 G\boxempty P_{2} embeds on the torus. This all leads us to conjecture that if G G is 3 3 -connected then G □ H G\boxempty H embeds on the torus if and only if G G is outer-cylindrical and H = P 2 H=P_{2} . We also determine the genera of K 4 □ P 3 K_{4}\boxempty P_{3} and H i □ P 2 H_{i}\boxempty P_{2} where H i H_{i} is a planar, 3 3 -connected, non-outer-cylindrical graph derived from a wheel graph W i W_{i} via two vertex splits and one edge addition.

Background

In this paper, we assume all graphs are simple, connected, and non-trivial (in the sense of being nonempty and not a single vertex), unless otherwise noted. We use
S_{g}
to denote an orientable, closed, connected surface of genus
g\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
. Given an
embedding
of a graph
G
on a surface
S_{g}
via a function
f:|G|\rightarrow S_{g}
where
|G|
represents a geometric realization of
G
, we sometimes use
f(G)
to denote this embedding or just
G
when the embedding is clear from context. If each component of
S_{g}\setminus f(G)
is homeomorphic to an open disk, then we say this embedding is a
2
-cell embedding
. The
genus
of a graph

, denoted
\gamma(G)
, is the minimum
g\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
such that
G
embeds on
S_{g}
. An embedding of
G
that realizes the minimal genus is called a
minimal embedding
. We will mainly be interested in
2
-cell embeddings of graphs, and since the work of Youngs
[
22
]
shows that each minimal embedding of
G
is a
2
-cell embedding, we usually don’t need to make a distinction when determining the genera of a graph. We refer the reader to
[
6
]
for further background on graph embeddings on surfaces.

A
2
-cell embedding

provides a
2
-cell decomposition of

with
|V(G)|
vertices,
|E(G)|
edges, and
|F|
faces, where each face is a component of

. Given such an embedding, the Euler characteristic of

is given by
2-2g=\chi(S_{g})=|V(G)|-|E(G)|+|F|
. The following proposition is a well known lower bound on genus, coming from this Euler characteristic formula and analyzing the minimal number of edges on any face in an embedding of such a graph. Recall that the
girth
of a graph
G
(that is not a tree) is the length of its shortest cycle.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
G
be a graph (that is not a tree) with girth
\alpha
. Then

\gamma(G)\geq 1+\frac{|E(G)|(\alpha-2)}{2\alpha}-\frac{|V(G)|}{2}.

Equality holds if and only if there exists an embedding of
G
where every face is bound by an
\alpha
-cycle and every edge borders two distinct faces.

Suppose we are given a
2
-cell embedding of
G
. We let
f_{i}
denote the number of
i
-gon faces for this embedding. We use the terms triangle, quadrilateral, pentagon, and hexagon to refer to faces of a given embedding that are bound by a
3
-cycle,
4
-cycle,
5
-cycle, and
6
-cycle, respectively. We say that an embedding of a graph is a
triangular embedding
if every face is a triangle. We note the following basic but important facts about
2
-cell embeddings of
G
:

Fact 1:
Each vertex of
G
has an open
2
-cell neighborhood in
S
.

Fact 2:
Each edge of
G
borders precisely two (not necessarily unique) faces.

Fact 3:
If
G=H\boxempty P_{n}
for some graph

, then each edge from a
P_{n}
fiber borders two

Fact 4:

2|E(G)|=\displaystyle\sum_{i=3}^{\infty}if_{i}
.

Facts (1) and (2) are general facts about

-cell embeddings. Fact (3) follows from (2) and since any edge in a

fiber is not contained in a
3
-cycle. Fact (4) follows from (2). This collection of facts will play an important role in ruling out potential toroidal embeddings of graphs in Section

A graph

is a

Proposition 2.2
.

If

Proof.

Let

be a minor of

are defined as
V(G\boxempty H)=V(G)\times V(H)

is a minor of

under an edge deletion, vertex deletion, or edge contraction, which we now handle as three separate cases.

Case 1:

by deleting edge

. ∎

K^{3}
. Note that
K^{1}
and
K^{3}
each have four edges incident to mixed quadrilaterals. Thus, either some edges in
K^{1}
don’t border two faces and some edges in
K^{2}
border at least three faces, or vice versa, giving a contradiction.

Suppose
|F|=14
and
f_{3}=5
,
f_{4}=8
, and
f_{5}=1
. The two types of pentagons (
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
described earlier in this section) each bound two edges coming from either
E_{1}
or
E_{2}
. As a result, we must then have seven mixed quadrilaterals in this embedding to make sure each edge in
E_{1}\cup E_{2}
borders exactly two faces. This leaves one quadrilateral and five triangles to complete the embedding. Since each mixed quadrilateral is incident to an edge in
K^{2}
and a pentagon is incident to either one or two edges in
K^{2}
, either four or three more (not necessarily distinct) edges in
K^{2}
must be incident to the other faces in this embedding. By Lemma
3.1
and
f_{3}=5
, either one or two triangles are incident to only edges in
K^{2}
. Having two triangles contained in
K^{2}
results in some edge in
K^{2}
having at least three faces incident to it, which is a contradiction. Having only one triangle contained in
K^{2}
results in some edge in
K^{2}
having only one face incident to it, which is a contradiction. The only other possibility is that one triangle and the one (non-mixed) quadrilateral are incident to edges in
K^{2}
, but this would again result in some edge of
K^{2}
having three faces incident to it. Thus, an embedding of
G
with
f_{3}=5
,
f_{4}=8
, and
f_{5}=1
is not possible.

Suppose
f_{3}=6
,
f_{4}=6
, and
f_{5}=2
. By Lemma
3.1
, each
K_{4}
fiber contains exactly two triangles. Note that, each pentagon borders a pair of edges in either
E_{1}
or
E_{2}
. Thus, all six quadrilaterals must be mixed quadrilaterals in order to make sure each edge in
E_{1}\cup E_{2}
borders exactly two faces. Each mixed quadrilateral borders exactly one edge in
K^{2}
, each pentagon borders either one or two edges in
K^{2}
, and there are two triangles bordering a total of six edges in
K^{2}
. As a result, a minimum of
14
edges in
K^{2}
are bordered by faces, which forces some edge in
K^{2}
to border at least three faces, providing a contradiction.

Suppose
f_{3}=6
,
f_{4}=7
, and
f_{6}=1
. By Lemma
3.1
, each
K_{4}
fiber contains exactly two triangles. As discussed before the proof, there are three types of hexagons that can exist in an embedding of
G
. We now break this case into sub-cases, depending on what type of hexagon is used.

First, suppose an
h_{1}
hexagon is used in this embedding. Since this hexagon borders four edges from
E_{1}\cup E_{2}
, we must have six mixed quadrilaterals in this embedding to ensure every edge in
E_{1}\cup E_{2}
borders exactly two faces. Thus, we only need to determine where the last quadrilateral is placed in this embedding. This quadrilateral must be bounded by edges in a
K_{4}
fiber since the union of the pentagon and six mixed quadrilaterals collectively border every edge in
E_{1}\cup E_{2}
twice. However, no matter which
K_{4}
fiber is used here, we will have an edge incident to at least three faces, providing a contradiction.

Now, suppose an
h_{2}
,
h_{3}
, or
h_{4}
hexagon is used in this embedding. Such a hexagon does not border one of the outer
K_{4}
fibers, say
K^{3}
. In addition, such a hexagon borders exactly two edges from either
E_{1}
or
E_{2}
. As a result, all seven quadrilaterals must all be mixed quadrilaterals to ensure every edge in
E_{1}\cup E_{2}
borders exactly two faces. Four of these mixed quadrilaterals will each be incident to one edge in
K^{3}
and the other three mixed quadrilaterals do not border
K^{3}
. At this point, we have determined where all our faces are placed and we see that some edges in
K^{3}
are bordered by at most one face, which is a contradiction.

We now describe how to embed
G
on
S_{2}
. First, embed each
K_{4}
fiber on a separate
2
-sphere as the
1
-skeleton of a tetrahedron. We use
F_{i}
for
i=1,\ldots,4
to denote the four triangles in one of these embeddings. First, delete the interiors of
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
from the
K^{1}
embedding and from the
K^{2}
embedding. Insert a tube (homeomorphic to
\mathbb{S}^{1}\times[0,1]
), between the
F_{j}
face deleted from
K^{1}
and the
F_{j}
face deleted from
K^{2}
for
j=1,2
. The four edges of
E_{1}
can be embedded on these two tubes. Now, delete the triangular faces
F_{3}
and
F_{4}
coming from
K^{2}
’s embedding on a
2
-sphere and also delete
F_{3}
and
F_{4}
from
K^{3}
’s embedding on a
2
-sphere. Insert two tubes in a similar fashion to embed the edges of
E_{2}
, completing the embedding of
G
. Since this embedding involves three disjoint
2
-spheres joined by four tubes, the resulting genus of the surface is two.
∎

4.
Restricting toroidal
G\boxempty H
via vertex connectivity.

The
vertex-connectivity
of a graph
G
, denoted
\kappa(G)
, is the minimum cardinality of a vertex-cut if
G
is not complete or
\kappa(G)=n-1
if
G=K_{n}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
. A connected graph
G
is
k
-connected
for
1\leq k\leq\kappa(G)
. Our goal in this section is to examine how vertex-connectivity can affect whether or not
G\boxempty H
is toroidal. We first note that
G
and
H
can be at most
4
-connected, if
G\boxempty H
is toroidal.

Proposition 4.1
.

If
\gamma(G\boxempty H)\leq 1
, then
\kappa(G),\kappa(H)\leq 4
.

Proof.

We proceed by contrapositive. Suppose
\kappa(G)\geq 5
. Since
H
is a simple connected graph with
|V(H)|\geq 2
, we see that
H
contains a
P_{2}
subgraph. Note that,
|V(G\boxempty P_{2})|=2|V(G)|
and
|E(G\boxempty P_{2})|=2|E(G)|+|V(G)|
. Since
G
is simple, the girth of
G\boxempty P_{2}
is at least three. By Proposition
2.1
, we have that

\gamma(G\boxempty P_{2})\geq 1+\frac{2|E(G)|+|V(G)|}{6}-\frac{2|V(G)|}{2}=1+\frac{1}{3}|E(G)|-\frac{5}{6}|V(G)|.

Since
G
is
5
-connected, for all
v\in V(G)
, we have
deg(v)\geq 5
. Since
G
is simple, every edge is incident to two distinct vertices. Thus,
\frac{5}{2}|V(G)|\leq|E(G)|
, and so, we can conclude that

\gamma(G\boxempty P_{2})\geq 1+\frac{1}{3}(\frac{5}{2}|V(G)|)-\frac{5}{6}|V(G)|=1.

Equality holds if and only if
G
is
5
-regular and
G\boxempty P_{2}
admits a triangular emdedding. However, any edge in a
P_{2}
fiber is not contained in any
3
-cycle of
G\boxempty P_{2}
, and so, a triangular embedding of
G\boxempty P_{2}
does not exist. Therefore,
\gamma(G\boxempty H)\geq\gamma(G\boxempty P_{2})>1.

∎

We now work to show that severe restrictions can be placed on
H
when
\kappa(G)\in\{3,4\}
. We first need a basic fact about
3
-connected graphs.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
G
be a
3
-connected graph. Then
G
contains a
K_{4}
minor.

Proof.

The work of Tutte
[
18
]
shows that any simple,
3
-connected graph contains a wheel graph
W_{n}
as a minor, for some
n\geq 3
; see Figure
2
for a diagram of
W_{5}
. At the same time,
W_{m}
is a minor of
W_{n}
, whenever
m\leq n
. It is easy to see that one can first delete an appropriate number of the spoke edges of
W_{n}
that connect to the central vertex, and then, contract some of the edges along the
n
-cycle of
W_{n}
to obtain a
W_{m}
minor. Thus,
W_{3}=K_{4}
is a minor of any simple,
3
-connected graph, as needed.
∎

This result has immediate consequences for classifying toroidal Cartesian products where one factor is
3
-connected.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
G
be a
3
-connected graph. If
\gamma(G\boxempty H)\leq 1
, then
H=P_{2}
.

Proof.

We proceed by contrapositive. Suppose
H
is a (simple, connected, non-trivial) graph where

. Then
H
contains a
P_{3}
subgraph. By Lemma
4.2
,
G
has a

minor. Then by Theorem

and Corollary

, we have that
\gamma(G\boxempty H)\geq\gamma(K_{4}\boxempty P_{3})=2
.
∎

. This first requires us to introduce a new class of graphs. A graph

to build an embedding of

Lemma 6.6
.

Suppose

2

Proposition 6.7
.

It is not possible for
G
to
2
-cell embed on the torus where every mixed

-gon is a mixed quadrilateral.

Proof.

In this proof, we use the term face set in reference to the four possibilities given in Proposition

. For sake of contradiction, suppose

does
2
-cell embed on the torus where every mixed
n
-gon is a mixed quadrilateral. Then every

is incident to two mixed quadrilateral faces in this embedding. First, note the cycle sets
(a),(b),(d),(f),\text{ and }(g)
include edge
v_{4}v_{8}
. Thus, embeddings corresponding to these cycle sets cannot have face sets 1, 2, and 3 from Proposition
6.3
, since otherwise, we contradict Lemma

. We now consider cycle sets
(a)-(g)
as separate cases, keeping in mind the face set restrictions already placed on some of these scenarios.

Case 1:
Consider an embedding of

where
E^{\pm}
form the 8-cycle in
(a)
. As noted in the paragraph above, we only need to consider face set
4
here, where
f_{3}=4
and

(v_{2},v_{3},v_{6})
in each

v_{3}v_{4}
that borders a
4
-cycle in that

, and so,

Case 2:
Next, consider an embedding of

form the 8-cycle in
(c)

) in one of the

Case 3:
Next, consider an embedding of

form the 8-cycle in

Lemma 6.8
.

It is not possible for
G
to 2-cell embed on the torus with the following conditions:

(1)

Three distinct triangular faces are bound by the 3-cycles in one

(2)

Two distinct quadrilateral faces are bound by 4-cycles in the same

Proof.

For sake of contradiction, suppose we have an embedding of

. Note that, there are five distinct

:
(v_{3},v_{4},v_{7},v_{6})
,
(v_{1},v_{2},v_{6},v_{7})
,
(v_{1},v_{7},v_{8},v_{5})
,
(v_{4},v_{5},v_{8},v_{7})
, and
(v_{1},v_{7},v_{4},v_{5})

, edges in

are only bordered by mixed

-gons in such an embedding, and each mixed

-gon borders exactly two edges in

Consider an embedding with a quadrilateral bound by the 4-cycle
(v_{1},v_{7},v_{8},v_{5})
in

. A mixed

in

whose vertices correspond with

in both

must also be incident to some edge,

, in

incident to

. Since triangular faces are bound by the cycles
(v_{4},v_{7},v_{8})
and
(v_{4},v_{5},v_{8})

Next, consider an embedding with a quadrilateral bound by the 4-cycle

. Since triangular faces are placed incident to the cycles

Theorem 6.10
.

Let
G=H\boxempty P_{2}
. Then
\gamma(G)=2
.

Proof.

Suppose

does
2
-cell embed on the torus. Then by Lemma

, every

-gon is bound by an
n

does not lie on a

must lie on the boundary of a quadrilateral, pentagon, or hexagon in this embedding, i.e., there must exist mixed

-gons in this embedding. However, Proposition

and Proposition

, show that these scenarios are not possible. Thus,
\gamma(G)>1
.

To see that

, we explicitly construct an embedding of

on

. Embed each

-fiber of

on a

-sphere, following the embedding in Figure

. For each embedding of

on the

-sphere, cut out the interiors of three faces, specifically, the ones bound by cycles

(v_{3},v_{4},v_{7},v_{6})
, and
(v_{4},v_{5},v_{8})

∎

Human papillomavirus E6/7 specific T-cell responses among Asian individuals with cutaneous squamous cell carcinoma
Authors: Choon C Oh, Christine Tham, Noor Z Hamis, Yan S Leong, Gek H Lim, Eng E Ooi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractCutaneous human papillomavirus (HPV)-specific cell-mediated immunity in patients with cutaneous squamous cell carcinoma (SCC) remains poorly understood, particularly in organ transplant recipients (OTRs), who often develop multiple viral warts and SCC under long-term immunosuppression. This study characterized HPV E6/E7-specific T-cell responses in OTRs with SCC and compared them with immunocompetent individuals with SCC. Participants were recruited in Singapore and included OTRs with a history of posttransplant primary SCC (n = 28) and immunocompetent individuals with SCC (n = 10). Fifteen-mer peptides spanning the E6/E7 regions of HPV8, HPV17, HPV49, and HPV98 were synthesized, and HPV-specific T-cell responses were measured using an enzyme-linked immune absorbent spot assay. Most participants showed positive responses to cytomegalovirus peptide, the positive control, with response rates of 82% in OTRs and 70% in immunocompetent participants. Among OTRs, 28.57% responded to HPV17 E6/E7 peptides and 14.29% to HPV98, while responses to HPV8 and HPV49 were lower (7.14% and 3.57%, respectively). In immunocompetent participants, 20% responded to HPV17 and HPV49, 10% to HPV8, and none to HPV98. This study provides preliminary evidence that circulating T cells reactive to selected β-HPV E6/E7 peptide pools can be detected in a subset of Asian participants with cutaneous SCC, including OTRs.Keywords: Asian, Human papillomavirus, Organ transplant, Squamous cell carcinoma, T cell response

Introduction

Advances in transplantation have improved survival in organ transplant recipients (OTRs), but chronic immunosuppression is associated with a substantial burden of persistent cutaneous human papillomavirus (HPV) infection and cutaneous squamous cell carcinoma (SCC) (Euvrard et al, 2003). Among the more than 200 HPV subtypes, β-HPVs are most frequently detected in normal, premalignant, and malignant skin keratinocytes, and accumulating evidence supports their involvement in SCC pathogenesis, particularly in OTRs (Wang et al, 2014). Given the central role of cellular immunity in HPV control, long-term immunosuppression may impair antiviral immune surveillance and promote viral persistence (Johnson et al, 2023; Strickley et al, 2019).

In mucosal HPV-associated disease, HPV-specific T-cell responses, particularly those directed against the viral oncoproteins E6 and E7, have been associated with viral clearance and lesion regression (Farhat et al, 2009; Steele et al, 2005). By contrast, cutaneous HPV-specific cell-mediated immune responses remain poorly characterized, especially in patients with SCC (Oh, 2026; Sester et al, 2016). This gap is particularly relevant in OTRs, in whom impaired adaptive immunity may facilitate persistent HPV infection, immune evasion and tumor development. This study therefore examines HPV E6/E7-specific T-cell responses in OTRs with SCC and compares them with those in immunocompetent individuals with SCC.

Most participants demonstrated positive T-cell responses to cytomegalovirus peptide, with response rates of 82% among OTRs, 70% among immunocompetent participants, and 75% among the healthy participants (Figure 1). Compared with immunocompetent participants, the OTR cohort showed a higher frequency of positive T-cell responses to βHPV17 E6/E7 peptides (28.57% vs 20%) and was the only group with detectable responses to βHPV98 E6/E7 peptides (14.29% vs 0%). In contrast, responses to βHPV49 E6/E7 peptides were much lower in OTR than in immunocompetent participants (3.57% vs 20%). Responses to βHPV8 E6/E7 peptides were low in both OTR and immunocompetent participants (7.14% vs 10%). Among healthy participants with no history of SCC, no responses were detected to any of the 4 βHPV types, except in 1 participant who responded to βHPV17 E6/E7 peptides. (Figure 2 and Table 2). Discussion

Our study demonstrated that HPV E6/E7-specific T-cell responses are present in participants with cutaneous warts and cutaneous SCC. βHPV17 elicited the most frequent positive T-cell responses among the 4 HPV genotypes tested, especially in the OTR cohort. This is consistent with an earlier study (Oh et al, 2026), where βHPV17 was the most commonly detected HPV genotype.

Due to the commensal nature of HPV on the skin, understanding the specific mechanisms of T cell-mediated immunity to cutaneous HPV infection has been challenging. To date, most studies are mouse models examining the role of HPV-specific T cells for cutaneous HPV (Johnson et al, 2023; Tay and Oh, 2024). Ye et al (2025) reported a case of recurrent invasive cutaneous SCC characterized by βHPV19 genomic integration in a patient harboring germline pathogenic alteration in ZAP70. Restoring T-cell receptor signaling integrity resulted in complete resolution of HPV-related disease, raising the possibility that T cell-based therapy could be useful to address disseminated SCC in OTR patients.

The detection of circulating HPV E6/E7-reactive T cells in OTR in our study should not be interpreted as evidence that HPV-specific cellular immunity is preserved in this population. OTRs in our cohort had a higher prevalence and burden of viral warts and were receiving multiple systemic immunosuppressive medications. In this context, circulating HPV-reactive responses that are broadly comparable to those observed in immunocompetent participants may reflect insufficient immune amplification relative to tissue antigen burden, rather than intact antiviral immunity. Immunocompetent individuals may maintain low circulating HPV-specific responses because local immune mechanisms, including skin-resident memory T cells, suppress viral replication in peripheral tissues. By contrast, systemic immunosuppression in OTRs may impair T-cell activation, expansion, effector function, tissue trafficking, and local viral regulation (Oh et al, 2025). Thus, detectable circulating responses in OTR may not indicate effective immune control at the site of HPV infection or SCC development. It is possible that the observed therapeutic effect of deploying HPV vaccines for warts and SCC has shown therapeutic effect due to the induction of innate immune response to vaccination; where the HPV genotypes in the vaccine versus infection in the skin lesions are mostly mismatched (Oh et al, 2026; Tay and Oh, 2025).

There are limitations to our study. We used antigen pools of overlapping peptides (15-mer) that stimulate responses mediated by both CD4+ and CD8+ T cells. As such, we are unable to discern whether the T cell responses were driven by CD4 or CD8 primarily. This cross-sectional study is also limited by the absence of time-dependent events, such as HPV and SCC clearance. Despite these limitations, the study provides preliminary evidence that circulating T cells reactive to selected β-HPV E6/E7 peptide pools can be detected in a subset of Asian participants with cutaneous SCC, including OTRs. The findings support further investigation into the relationship between β-HPV exposure, systemic and tissue-level T-cell immunity, and SCC pathogenesis in immunosuppressed populations.

In summary, circulating HPV E6/E7-reactive T-cell responses were detectable in a subset of OTRs and immunocompetent participants with cutaneous SCC. These responses should not be interpreted as definitive evidence of preserved antiviral immunity, lesion-specific immune recognition, or effective local viral control. Future research should incorporate larger cohorts, longitudinal sampling, HPV E6/E7 transcriptional analysis, and paired blood-tissue immune profiling. Such studies will be required to determine whether HPV-specific T cells contribute to viral control, tumor immune surveillance, SCC progression, or responses to HPV-directed immunologic interventions in high-risk OTRs.

Materials and Methods

This study recruited participants from the Singapore General Hospital and National Cancer Center Singapore from 2022 to 2024. The race and ethnicity of the participants were collected as part of the study population demographics and were obtained from the electronic health record (reported by the doctor). OTRs had a history of cutaneous SCC. The comparator group for the study was immunocompetent participants with cutaneous SCC. Four healthy participants were also recruited as study controls. Whole blood was collected from participants, from which PBMCs were isolated and cryopreserved. To measure the frequency of HPV-specific T cell epitopes, an enzyme-linked immune absorbent spot assay was deployed. The antigenic stimuli consist of 15 amino acid long peptides that span the E6/E7 region of βHPV 8, 17, 49, and 98 (Farhat et al, 2009). In an earlier study within this cohort of patients, we found that βHPVs were the most frequently detected genus across the 3 tissue sites sampled in our cohort. Within the Beta genus, HPV17 and HPV98 were commonly identified across all 3 tissue sites. HPV8 was frequently detected in cutaneous SCC tissue and in eyebrow hair from both OTRs and non-OTRs, while HPV49 was detected in SCC tissue and eyebrow hair of OTRs (Oh et al, 2026). Positive controls include cytomegalovirus peptides pool, and negative controls are cells only. The frequency of HPV-specific T cell epitopes was quantified using the immune absorbent spot software (Unit of measurement: SFU/106 cells). Fisher’s exact test and Wilcoxon rank-sum test were used to test if there was a significant difference between the 2 groups of participants. A 2-tailed P < .05 was used to declare statistical significance. All statistical analyses used Stata 17. All samples were obtained following written informed consent according to the Declaration of Helsinki. Participants and/or their legal guardians provided informed consent for their data to be used in this research. The study was approved by the Centralized Institutional Review Board.

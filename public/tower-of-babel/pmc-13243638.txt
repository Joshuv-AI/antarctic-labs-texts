Study on water flooding sweep characteristics of horizontal-vertical combined well pattern in offshore heavy oil reservoirs
Authors: Zhang Zhang, Zongbin Liu, Fengjiao Wang, Shaopeng Wang, Lilei Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
In order to solve the problems of poor water flooding effect and unclear sweep characteristics of offshore heavy oil reservoirs, a three-dimensional plate flooding experiment was carried out to clarify the sweep characteristics of water flooding in different heterogeneous reservoirs under the combined well pattern of horizontal and vertical. Furthermore, numerical simulation methods are employed to reveal the influences of injection rate, well pattern switching timing, horizontal well length, and perforation density on waterflooding sweep efficiency under field-scale conditions. The results indicate that planar heterogeneity induces injected water to form preferential flow paths along near-well high-permeability zones, while vertical heterogeneity leads to significant water flooding in bottom high-permeability layers. After the completion of development with the inverted nine-spot well pattern, the waterflooding oil recovery factor reaches 39.86%, with remaining oil concentrated in low-permeability layers and inter-well areas of production wells across all layers. Upon switching to the five-spot combined well pattern, horizontal wells can effectively mobilize the remaining oil in top low-permeability layers, improving waterflooding sweep performance and increasing the recovery factor to 57.87%. Under the same development duration, waterflooding sweep extent and oil recovery maintain a positive correlation with the injection rate and perforation density, the optimal well pattern switching timing is 70% water cut, and the optimal horizontal well length is 200 m. This research provides technical support for the efficient waterflooding development of offshore heavy oil reservoirs.

Introduction

Against the dual backdrop of the continuous rise in global energy demand and the increasing depletion of conventional oil resources, heavy oil, as a crucial alternative resource, has witnessed a growing prominence in its development value1–5. Offshore heavy oil reservoirs, characterized by large reserves and considerable resource potential, have become a key driver for reserve growth and production increase in China’s oil and gas industry6–8. However, these reservoirs not only face inherent development challenges such as high crude oil viscosity, poor fluidity, and strong reservoir heterogeneity but also encounter constraints including limited platform operation space, high development costs, and stringent environmental requirements9–12. Therefore, achieving efficient development of offshore heavy oil reservoirs remains a key focus and challenge in current research.

As the most widely adopted development method for offshore heavy oil reservoirs, waterflooding performance is largely determined by its sweep efficiency13–15. Extensive studies have been conducted on waterflooding sweep extent and development effectiveness. Research findings indicate that during the early stage of waterflooding, the sweep extent increases rapidly while oil displacement efficiency grows slowly, with the recovery enhancement mainly relying on the sweep effect of injected water. In the middle and late stages, injected water gradually reaches the maximum sweep extent under the existing well pattern, and further recovery improvement in the swept area is achieved through intensive water washing16. Additionally, it has been found that waterflooding sweep capacity is significantly influenced by reservoir heterogeneity and well pattern type. Specifically, stronger reservoir heterogeneity leads to more severe water channeling, increased ineffective circulation of injected water, and greater inhibition of waterflooding sweep extent17,18. Moreover, the sweep potential of a single well pattern is highly limited due to its inherent flow path constraints19.

Multi-well pattern conversion can substantially improve waterflooding sweep extent. Particularly, the horizontal-vertical combined well pattern can overcome the limitations of single well types in complex reservoir conditions, giving full play to its advantages of flexibly supplementing formation energy, expanding waterflooding sweep range, effectively enhancing reservoir-wellbore connectivity, and boosting single-well productivity20–25. Currently, the horizontal-vertical combined well pattern has been gradually applied in the development of offshore heavy oil reservoirs. However, existing research on this well pattern and offshore heavy oil reservoirs mainly focuses on the optimization of well pattern parameters (e.g., well pattern type, well type, well spacing) and injection-production parameters (e.g., injection rate, injection volume), as well as the design of remaining oil tapping schemes26–28. Studies on waterflooding sweep extent and its underlying mechanisms remain insufficient. Notably, multi-well pattern conversion can alter the distribution of reservoir seepage fields and pressure fields. In the case of multi-layered and strongly heterogeneous reservoirs, the combined conversion of multi-well types and multi-well patterns further complicates fluid flow between well patterns, resulting in unclear waterflooding sweep rules and hindering the implementation of effective enhanced oil recovery (EOR) measures29–32.

Based on the reservoir characteristics of the actual target area, this study first clarifies the influences of reservoir heterogeneity and well pattern conversion effect on waterflooding sweep characteristics through laboratory three-dimensional plate core experiments. Subsequently, a field-scale numerical model is established via numerical simulation methods to further investigate the effects of injection rate, well pattern switching timing, horizontal well length, and perforation density on waterflooding development performance and sweep extent.

Experimental section

After undergoing development with the inverted nine-spot well pattern and the five-spot combined (horizontal-vertical) well pattern, Q oilfield has now entered the high water cut stage. Interlayer and areal contradictions have become increasingly prominent, making the reservoir water flooding status and remaining oil distribution law extremely complex. Taking Q oilfield as the target area, this study conducts further research on its waterflooding sweep characteristics and development performance.

Experimental modeling

Based on the similarity criteria for reservoir characteristics and fluid properties (Table 1), a three-dimensional physical model with dimensions of 30 cm×30 cm×6 cm was fabricated using sands of different mesh sizes bonded by epoxy resin. The three-dimensional physical model has both planar heterogeneity and vertical heterogeneity. Each layer has two permeability zones. The permeability values of the top layer are 750 mD and 900 mD, respectively. Those of the middle layer are 2500 mD and 3000 mD, respectively. The permeability values of the bottom layer are 4500 mD and 5400 mD, respectively. To clarify the waterflooding sweep characteristics under the vertical-horizontal combined well pattern, four vertical wells and one horizontal well were arranged inside the physical model. In the initial stage, the quarter inverted nine-spot well pattern is adopted, and after the water cut reaches 80%, it is switched to the five-spot horizontal-vertical combined well pattern for development. Experimental procedures

Check the model’s air tightness

Submerge the model in water and inject air, then verify for air leakage by observing the presence of air bubbles in the water.

Evacuation

Use the four vertical wells of the three-dimensional physical model as injection ends respectively, and evacuate for 12 h.

Water saturation

Saturate the model with formation water, measure its porosity, and age it for 72 h under the target reservoir temperature.

Oil saturation

Take the horizontal well of the three-dimensional model as the injection end to saturate crude oil toward the four corners of the model until no more water is produced from the production ends. Increase the injection rate and continue displacement until no water is discharged from the production ends. Subsequently, use the four vertical wells as injection ends respectively to further saturate crude oil until no water is produced from the production ends, marking the completion of the oil saturation process. Calculate the initial oil saturation of the model and age it for 72 h.

Waterflooding process

The base well pattern adopts a quarter of the inverted nine-spot well pattern, and the heavy oil waterflooding experiment is carried out at a rate of 0.56 mL/min. When the water cut reaches 80%, switch to the five-spot horizontal-vertical combined well pattern to conduct a four-injection and one-production waterflooding experiment. After the experiment, calculate the oil recovery factor and water cut, and draw the oil saturation distribution map based on the experimental resistivity values.

Experimental results

The curves of oil recovery, water cut, and pressure versus injection volume during waterflooding in the three-dimensional plate core are illustrated in Fig. 3. It can be seen from the figure that the water cut rises rapidly to 80% in the early stage of waterflooding. After the completion of development with the inverted nine-spot well pattern, the oil recovery factor reaches 39.86%. Following the development with the five-spot combined well pattern, the final oil recovery factor of the core is 57.87%, indicating an enhanced oil recovery effect of 17.98% by the combined well pattern. By comparing the water cut and recovery degree of each individual well during the development of the inverted nine-spot well pattern inside the core (Fig. 4), it is found that the recovery degrees of well 2, well 3, and well 4 are 13.49%, 14.37%, and 12%, respectively. Planar heterogeneity within the core causes injected water to advance rapidly along the high-permeability direction, resulting in preferential breakthrough of injected water at well 2, which has the earliest water breakthrough time. Furthermore, the existence of planar heterogeneity inhibits the flow of injected water toward well 3 and well 4. Due to the farthest distance from the injection well, well 3 exhibits the latest water breakthrough time and maintains the piston-like displacement state for the longest duration, leading to the highest single-well recovery degree.

To clarify the waterflooding sweep characteristics of the horizontal-vertical combined well pattern in heavy oil reservoirs, the planar waterflooding sweep characteristic maps of each layer inside the core at different water cut stages were plotted based on experimental resistivity values, as illustrated in Fig. 5. It can be observed from the figures that planar heterogeneity affects the direction of waterflooding sweep, causing injected water to advance rapidly along near-well high-permeability zones. In contrast, vertical heterogeneity significantly influences the liquid absorption capacity of each layer, leading to the formation of obvious preferential flow paths in the bottom high-permeability layer of the core and severe ineffective circulation of injected water. Under the combined influence of planar and vertical heterogeneity, remaining oil is concentrated in the top low-permeability layer and the areas far from injection wells in each layer, while the sweep effect in other regions is relatively favorable. After switching to the five-spot horizontal-vertical combined well pattern, the presence of horizontal wells increases the oil drainage area and provides preferential seepage channels for the top low-permeability layer, effectively improving the sweep effect of low-permeability layers. However, since the flow paths of both the inverted nine-spot well pattern and the five-spot combined well pattern fail to sweep the inter-well areas of production wells in the inverted nine-spot well pattern, remaining oil remains concentrated in these regions.

Numerical simulation study of field-scale

Numerical simulation methods enable the conduct of repeatable multi-scheme studies and multi-factor analyses by establishing valid models. Building upon the insights from the three-dimensional physical simulation experiments previously conducted, which investigated waterflooding sweep characteristics in heterogeneous reservoirs, this section further carries out extended research using reservoir numerical simulation methods. First, based on the laboratory experimental results, an experimental-scale model is established via CMG numerical simulation software to verify the accuracy of the simulation in this study. Subsequently, referring to the parameters of the actual Q Oilfield, the numerical model is scaled up to the field scale to clarify the influences of various factors on waterflooding development performance under field-scale conditions.

Model validation

An experimental-scale numerical model was established using CMG numerical simulation software, with the simulation data consistent with the aforementioned experimental parameters. The normalized relative permeability curves are illustrated in Fig. 6. By history matching the established numerical simulation model, the evaluation indicators for the matching effect mainly include the water cut and oil recovery factor of the model. The matching results are presented in Fig. 7, where the water cut and oil recovery factor curves exhibit a high degree of agreement. This demonstrates that the model proposed in this study possesses high accuracy.

Scheme design

To clarify the influences of various factors on waterflooding sweep characteristics at the field scale, numerical simulation methods are used to simulate the conversion process from the inverted nine-spot well pattern to the five-spot combined well pattern. Based on the actual reservoir characteristics of the Q oilfield, a geological model with dimensions of 300 m × 300 m × 60 m was constructed using numerical simulation methods. The x and y directions were divided into 60 grids, and the z direction was divided into three layers. To avoid external energy interference, a closed boundary was selected as the external boundary condition of the model. The internal boundary of the model was constrained by the injection and production wells together, that is, the injection well was a boundary of constant flow rate water injection, and the production well adopted a production boundary with a fixed bottom hole pressure. Comparative studies are conducted on the injection rate, well pattern switching timing, horizontal well length, and perforation density during the development of the five-spot combined well pattern, with a uniform simulation duration of 25 years. Simulation results

Injection rate

The curves of oil recovery factor and water cut under different injection rates, as well as the waterflooding sweep characteristics after the completion of combined well pattern development, are illustrated in Figs. 8 and 9. It can be observed from the figures that under the same development duration, the lower the injection rate, the lower the waterflooding oil recovery factor and the poorer the sweep effect. Due to the high viscosity of crude oil in heavy oil reservoirs and the resulting large threshold pressure gradient, the low injection rate condition is severely affected by the threshold pressure gradient, leading to insufficient waterflooding sweep driving force. Consequently, the sweep range is confined to areas near injection wells, resulting in a relatively low final oil recovery factor. In contrast, the high injection rate condition not only provides strong initial kinetic energy for injected water to effectively overcome the adverse impact of the threshold pressure gradient on waterflooding sweep effect, promoting the breakthrough of injected water and expanding the sweep range, but also delivers a larger injection volume within the same development duration. This ultimately leads to a better water washing effect and a higher oil recovery factor.

Well pattern switching timing

The curves of oil recovery and water cut under different well pattern switching timing, as well as the waterflooding sweep characteristics after the completion of combined well pattern development, are illustrated in Figs. 10 and 11. It can be observed from the figures that the waterflooding oil recovery factor exhibits a trend of first increasing and then decreasing with the delay of well pattern switching timing, with the optimal well pattern switching timing at a water cut of 70%. If the well pattern switching timing is too early, the sweepable area under the inverted nine-spot well pattern fails to achieve optimal oil displacement efficiency. After switching to the five-spot combined well pattern at this stage, its flow paths struggle to effectively sweep the inter-well sweep able areas of the original inverted nine-spot well pattern, restricting the oil displacement effect and resulting in a lower final oil recovery factor. Although a too-late well pattern switching timing enhances the oil displacement efficiency in the main flow path zones of the inverted nine-spot well pattern, it also triggers severe ineffective circulation of injected water. Meanwhile, within the same total development duration, the effective development period of the five-spot combined well pattern is shortened, leading to poor waterflooding efficiency within the newly swept areas and a relatively low oil recovery factor. Under the optimal well pattern switching timing (70% water cut), the waterflooding performance under the inverted nine-spot well pattern is maximized, the ineffective circulation of injected water is mitigated, and the development efficiency of the five-spot combined well pattern is significantly improved, thus achieving the highest waterflooding oil recovery factor.

Horizontal well length

The curves of oil recovery and water cut under different horizontal well lengths, as well as the waterflooding sweep characteristics after the completion of the horizontal-vertical combined well pattern development, are illustrated in Figs. 12 and 13. It can be observed from the figures that the final oil recovery of the horizontal-vertical combined well pattern exhibits a trend of first increasing and then decreasing with the increase in horizontal well length, with the optimal horizontal well length determined as 200 m. The shorter the horizontal well length, the longer the distance between injection wells and the horizontal well, and the smaller the pressure difference between injection and production wells. Consequently, the waterflooding sweep range is confined to areas near injection wells, resulting in a relatively poor sweep effect. As the horizontal well length increases, the oil drainage area expands, which effectively improves the connectivity between injection and production wells and significantly enhances the waterflooding sweep efficiency. However, when the horizontal well length is excessively long (240 m), the distance between injection and production wells is shortened, leading to an increased pressure difference and obvious water channeling. This causes the sweep range to concentrate along the connecting zone between injection wells and the proximal section of the horizontal well, inhibiting the crude oil exploitation effect in inter-well regions and reducing the overall sweep efficiency.

Perforation density

The curves of oil recovery and water cut under different perforation densities, as well as the waterflooding sweep efficiency after the completion of combined well pattern development, are illustrated in Figs. 14 and 15. It can be observed from the figures that the waterflooding oil recovery increases with the increase in perforation density, but the growth rate gradually decreases. Because under low perforation density conditions, the perforation points are sparsely distributed. Affected by the injection-production pressure difference, injected water only flows along the connecting lines between injection wells and the nearest perforation points, resulting in a limited waterflooding sweep range. In contrast, high perforation density provides multiple seepage channels, which reduces the scope of remaining oil-enriched zones between injection wells under the five-spot combined well pattern, expands the swept area of injected water, and thus significantly improves the waterflooding oil recovery factor. However, when the perforation density increases to 0.15 holes/m, additional perforation points fail to substantially expand the waterflooding sweep range and can only sporadically exploit the local remaining oil-enriched zones near the horizontal well, leading to a small increase in oil recovery.

Conclusions

In this paper, based on the actual reservoir characteristics of the Q Oilfield, adopts three-dimensional plate core flooding experiments to clarify the influences of reservoir heterogeneity and the horizontal-vertical combined well pattern on waterflooding sweep characteristics. Additionally, numerical simulation methods are employed to reveal the impacts of injection rate, well pattern switching timing, horizontal well length, and perforation density on waterflooding sweep efficiency at the field scale.

(1) Reservoir planar heterogeneity promotes the rapid sweep of injected water along near-well high-permeability zones, leading to the formation of obvious preferential flow paths within the reservoir. Vertical heterogeneity increases the liquid absorption capacity of the high-permeability layer at the core bottom, resulting in significant water flooding in the high-permeability layer.

(2) During the development stage of the inverted nine-spot well pattern, the waterflooding oil recovery factor reaches 39.86%, with remaining oil mainly distributed in the top low-permeability layer and the inter-well areas of production wells. After switching to the five-spot horizontal-vertical combined well pattern, the oil recovery factor increases to 57.87%. The horizontal well expands the oil drainage area and improves the sweep efficiency of the top low-permeability layer, leading to a significant enhancement in waterflooding development performance.

(3) Under the same development duration, both the waterflooding oil recovery factor and sweep efficiency exhibit a positive correlation with injection rate and perforation density. There exists an optimal well pattern switching timing (70% water cut) and an optimal horizontal well length (200 m) that maximize the waterflooding sweep efficiency.

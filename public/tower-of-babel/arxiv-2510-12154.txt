Positivity properties of canonical bases
Jiepeng Fang, Xuhua He

Abstract
. We prove that the canonical basis of a modified quantum group 𝐔 ˙ \dot{\mathbf{U}} exhibits strong positivity properties for the canonical basis elements arising from spherical parabolic subalgebras. Our main result establishes that the structure constants for both the multiplication with arbitrary canonical basis elements in 𝐔 ˙ \dot{\mathbf{U}} and the action on the canonical basis elements of arbitrary tensor products of simple lowest and highest weight modules by these elements belong to ℕ ⁡ [ v , v − 1 ] \mathbb{N}[v,v^{-1}] . This implies, in particular, for quantum groups of finite type, the structure constants for multiplication and for action on tensor product with respect to canonical basis are governed by positive coefficients. A key ingredient is the thickening construction, an algebraic technique that embeds a suitable approximation of the tensor of a lowest weight module and a highest weight module of 𝐔 ˙ \dot{\mathbf{U}} into the negative part 𝐔 ~ − \tilde{\mathbf{U}}^{-} of a larger quantum group. This allows us to inherit the desired positivity for the tensor product from the well-established positivity of the canonical basis of 𝐔 ~ − \tilde{\mathbf{U}}^{-} .

Introduction

0.1.
Canonical basis

The quantum group
\mathbf{U}
, also called the quantized enveloping algebra, was independently introduced by Drinfeld and Jimbo. It is a Hopf algebra over
\mathbb{Q}(v)
, as a non-trivial deformation of the universal enveloping algebra of the Kac-Moody Lie algebra. The theory of canonical basis, first established by Lusztig
[
20
,
21
]
and further studied by Kashiwara
[
13
]
and others, reveals profound and elegant structures on the quantum groups and their modules. As highlighted in the introduction of
[
25
]
,

“In addition to extending the range of the theory of semisimple groups from
\mathbb{Z}
to
\mathbb{Z}[v,v^{-1}]
, the theory of quantum groups has led to a new, extremely rigid structure, in which the objects of the theory are provided with canonical bases with rather remarkable properties; in particular, in the simply laced case, the structure constants with respect to the canonical bases are not only in
\mathbb{Z}[v,v^{-1}]
, but in
\mathbb{N}[v,v^{-1}]
.”

More precisely, we have the following positivity properties in the symmetric cases:

•

the structure constants with respect to the multiplication and the comultiplication of the canonical basis
\mathbf{B}^{-}
of the negative part
\mathbf{U}^{-}
(resp.
\mathbf{B}^{+}
of the positive part
\mathbf{U}^{+}
) of
\mathbf{U}
belong to
\mathbb{N}[v,v^{-1}]
;

•

the structure constants with respect to the actions of the Chevalley generators
F_{i},E_{i}\in\mathbf{U}
of the canonical basis
\mathbf{B}(\Lambda_{\lambda})
of the simple highest weight module
\Lambda_{\lambda}
(resp.
\mathbf{B}({{}^{\omega}\Lambda_{\lambda}})
of the simple lowest weight module
{{}^{\omega}\Lambda_{\lambda}}
) of
\mathbf{U}
belong to
\mathbb{N}[v,v^{-1}]
.

An important and natural direction of research is to study the canonical basis of the tensor product of modules. Lusztig
[
22
,
25
]
and later Bao-Wang
[
3
]
defined the canonical basis
\mathbf{B}(\Lambda(\boldsymbol{\lambda}))
of the tensor product

\Lambda(\boldsymbol{\lambda})={{{}^{\omega}}\Lambda_{\lambda_{1}}}\otimes\cdots\otimes{{{}^{\omega}}\Lambda_{\lambda_{m}}}\otimes\Lambda_{\lambda_{m+1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\Lambda_{\lambda_{m+n}}

of a sequence of simple lowest weight modules
{{{}^{\omega}}\Lambda_{\lambda_{1}}},\ldots,{{{}^{\omega}}\Lambda_{\lambda_{m}}}
followed by simple highest weight modules
\Lambda_{\lambda_{m+1}},\ldots,\Lambda_{\lambda_{m+n}}
.

In general,
\mathbf{B}(\Lambda(\boldsymbol{\lambda}))\not=\mathbf{B}({{{}^{\omega}}\Lambda_{\lambda_{1}}})\otimes\cdots\mathbf{B}({{{}^{\omega}}\Lambda_{\lambda_{m}}})\otimes\mathbf{B}(\Lambda_{\lambda_{m+1}})\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathbf{B}(\Lambda_{\lambda_{m+n}})
, that is, the canonical basis of the tensor product is not the same as the tensor product of the canonical bases of the constituents. A fundamental question is whether the canonical basis of the tensor product also possesses the strong positivity properties known for its constituent modules.

The modified quantum group
\dot{\mathbf{U}}
was introduced by Lusztig
[
25
]
. Moreover, he showed that the canonical bases
\mathbf{B}({{}^{\omega}\Lambda_{\lambda_{1}+\lambda}}\otimes\Lambda_{\lambda_{2}+\lambda})
of the tensor products
{{}^{\omega}\Lambda_{\lambda_{1}+\lambda}}\otimes\Lambda_{\lambda_{2}+\lambda}
have a very nice stability property when
\lambda
increases, and used this stability to glued these bases to construct the canonical basis
\dot{\mathbf{B}}
of
\dot{\mathbf{U}}
. In
[
25
, Conjecture 25.4.2]
, he conjectured that
\dot{\mathbf{B}}
has positivity properties with respect to the multiplication and the comultiplication of
\dot{\mathbf{U}}
.

0.2.
Previous works on positivity properties

A powerful framework for establishing the positivity properties of canonical bases is their geometrization and categorification. This approach represents algebraic structures and their bases in terms of geometric or algebraic categories, where positivity often emerges from fundamental geometric or categorical constraints.

A foundational result was due to Lusztig
[
21
]
, in which he categorified the algebra
\mathbf{U}^{-}
by perverse sheaves on the varieties of quiver representations, and showed that the simple perverse sheaves give a realization of the canonical basis. Khovanov, Lauda
[
16
]
and Rouquier
[
30
]
categorified
\mathbf{U}^{-}
by finitely generated graded projective modules of the quiver Hecke algebra, and later Varagnolo, Vasserot
[
32
]
and Rouquier
[
29
]
showed that the indecomposable objects likewise realized the canonical basis. For the modules, Kang and Kashiwara
[
12
]
provided a categorification of
\Lambda_{\lambda}
via finitely generated graded projective modules of cyclotomic quiver Hecke algebra. Another geometric perspective was developed by Zheng
[
35
]
, who realized

\Lambda_{\lambda}
using perverse sheaves on stacks of framed quiver representations. This construction later refined by Fang, Lan, and Xiao
[
8
]
via perverse sheaves on the varieties of framed quiver representations. These categorifications furnish the positivity properties for the canonical bases of
\mathbf{U}^{-}
and the single module
\Lambda_{\lambda}
respectively.

The categorification approach is also a powerful tool for studying the tensor product of simple highest weight modules. Zheng
[
35
]
categorified the tensor product by perverse sheaves on the stacks of framed quiver representations, and later Li
[
18
,
19
]
realized its canonical basis. Webster
[
33
]
categorified the tensor product building on works of
[
16
,
29
,
32
]
and showed that the canonical basis possesses positivity properties. Fang and Lan
[
6
]
provided another categorification of the tensor product by perverse sheaves on the varieties of multiply framed quiver representations, building on works of
[
35
,
19
,
8
]
. They realized the canonical basis by an approach different from
[
19
]
and established its positivity properties.

The more challenging—and arguably more significant—case involves mixed tensor products containing both simple lowest weight and highest weight modules. As Webster pointed out in
[
33
]
, “(the approach in loc.cit.) uses that highest and lowest weight modules of
\dot{\mathbf{U}}
are the same in a very strong way”. This is only applicable to quantum groups of finite type.
For affine type
A
, Schiffmann-Vasserot
[
31
]
and later McGerty
[
26
]
gave a geometric realization of
\dot{\mathbf{B}}
. Their results imply the positivity properties of
\dot{\mathbf{B}}
with respect to the multiplication of
\dot{\mathbf{U}}
and equivalently the action on the tensor product
{{}^{\omega}\Lambda_{\lambda_{1}}}\otimes\Lambda_{\lambda_{2}}
. Fu and Shoji
[
9
]
used a Ringel-Hall algebra approach to relate
\dot{\mathbf{B}}
to the canonical basis of the quantum Schur algebra, and thus obtained the positivity properties of
\dot{\mathbf{B}}
with respect to the multiplication of
\dot{\mathbf{U}}
and equivalently the action on the tensor product
{{}^{\omega}\Lambda_{\lambda_{1}}}\otimes\Lambda_{\lambda_{2}}
. Fan and Li
[
5
]
showed that
\dot{\mathbf{B}}
has the the positivity property with respect to the comultiplication of
\dot{\mathbf{U}}
.

Beyond finite type and affine type
A
, the question of whether the canonical bases of arbitrary tensor products of simple lowest weight with highest weight modules, and of the modified quantum group possesses positivity properties remained open.

0.3.
Main result

In this paper, we show that the canonical basis
\mathbf{B}(\Lambda(\boldsymbol{\lambda}))
of the tensor product
\Lambda(\boldsymbol{\lambda})
and the canonical basis
\dot{\mathbf{B}}
of the modified quantum group
\dot{\mathbf{U}}
have the following positivity properties when the Cartan datum
(I,\cdot)
is symmetric. We introduce the notion of a spherical parabolic element of
\dot{\mathbf{U}}
. These are elements contained in some spherical parabolic subalgebra of
\dot{\mathbf{U}}
. We refer to §
6.2
for more details.

Theorem 0.1

(=
Theorem
8.2
)
.

Let
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{m},\lambda_{m+1},\ldots,\lambda_{m+n}
be dominant weights. Then the canonical basis
\mathbf{B}(\Lambda(\boldsymbol{\lambda}))
of the tensor product
\Lambda(\boldsymbol{\lambda})
has the following positivity properties:

(1)

(Positivity of the transition matrix)

\mathbf{B}(\Lambda(\boldsymbol{\lambda}))\subset\mathbb{N}[v^{-1}][\mathbf{B}({{}^{\omega}\Lambda_{\lambda_{1}}})\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathbf{B}({{}^{\omega}\Lambda_{\lambda_{m}}})\otimes\mathbf{B}(\Lambda_{\lambda_{m+1}})\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathbf{B}(\Lambda_{\lambda_{m+n}})].

(2)

(Positivity with respect to the action) For any spherical parabolic element
\dot{b}\in\dot{\mathbf{B}}
,

\dot{b}(\mathbf{B}(\Lambda(\boldsymbol{\lambda})))\subset\mathbb{N}[v,v^{-1}][\mathbf{B}(\Lambda(\boldsymbol{\lambda}))].

As a consequence, we have

Corollary 0.2
.

Let
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{m},\lambda_{m+1},\ldots,\lambda_{m+n}
be dominant weights. Then the canonical basis
\mathbf{B}(\Lambda(\boldsymbol{\lambda}))
of the tensor product
\Lambda(\boldsymbol{\lambda})
has the following positivity properties:

F_{i}(\mathbf{B}(\Lambda(\boldsymbol{\lambda}))),E_{i}(\mathbf{B}(\Lambda(\boldsymbol{\lambda})))\subset\mathbb{N}[v,v^{-1}][\mathbf{B}(\Lambda(\boldsymbol{\lambda}))]\ \textrm{for any}\ i\in I.

For a single simple highest weight or simple lowest weight module, we show that their canonical bases have the following positivity properties with respect to the actions of any elements of
\dot{\mathbf{B}}
(without the spherical parabolic condition), which generalizes Lusztig’s theorem
[
25
, Theorem 22.1.7]
.

Proposition 0.3

(=
Proposition
6.4
)
.

Let
\dot{b}\in\dot{\mathbf{B}}
and
\lambda\in X^{+}
. Then

\dot{b}(\mathbf{B}(\Lambda_{\lambda}))\subset\mathbb{N}[v,v^{-1}][\mathbf{B}(\Lambda_{\lambda})],\ \dot{b}(\mathbf{B}({{}^{\omega}\Lambda_{\lambda}}))\subset\mathbb{N}[v,v^{-1}][\mathbf{B}({{}^{\omega}\Lambda_{\lambda}})].

Moreover, based on the positivity properties of
\mathbf{B}({{}^{\omega}\Lambda_{\lambda_{1}}}\otimes\Lambda_{\lambda_{2}})
with respect to the actions of
\dot{\mathbf{B}}
for all
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in X^{+}
, we deduce the following results on the modified quantum group.

Theorem 0.4

(=
Theorem
6.5
)
.

For any
\dot{b},\dot{b}^{\prime}\in\dot{\mathbf{B}}
, if one of them is a spherical parabolic element, then

\dot{b}\dot{b}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}[v,v^{-1}][\dot{\mathbf{B}}].

In particular, if the symmetric Cartan datum
(I,\cdot)
is of finite type, then all canonical basis elements in
\dot{\mathbf{B}}
are spherical parabolic elements, and we have

Corollary 0.5
.

If the symmetric Cartan datum
(I,\cdot)
is of finite type, then the canonical basis
\dot{\mathbf{B}}
of the modified quantum group
\dot{\mathbf{U}}
has the following positivity properties:

(1)

(Positivity with respect to the multiplication) For any
\dot{b},\dot{b}^{\prime}\in\dot{\mathbf{B}}

\dot{b}\dot{b}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}[v,v^{-1}][\dot{\mathbf{B}}];

(2)

(Positivity with respect to the action) For any
\dot{b}\in\dot{\mathbf{B}}
and dominant weights
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{m},\lambda_{m+1},\ldots,\lambda_{m+n}

\dot{b}(\mathbf{B}(\Lambda(\boldsymbol{\lambda})))\subset\mathbb{N}[v,v^{-1}][\mathbf{B}(\Lambda(\boldsymbol{\lambda}))].

0.4.
Motivation and Strategy: the thickening philosophy

Our approach is inspired by the theory of total positivity. Introduced by Lusztig for reductive groups and their flag varieties in
[
23
]
and later generalized to the Kac-Moody setting
[
24
]
,
[
1
]
, this theory reveals a profound connection between geometric positivity and its algebraic counterpart. Total positivity provides a geometric and combinatorial framework that illustrates the inherent rigidity and positivity properties of canonical bases in quantum groups. A highlight of this connection is that the totally nonnegative part of a flag variety consists precisely of those points whose corresponding projective line in
\mathbb{P}(\Lambda_{\lambda})
(for a dominant regular weight
\lambda
) is spanned by vectors expressible as nonnegative linear combinations of the canonical basis of
\Lambda_{\lambda}
.

We posit that an analogous connection exists between the tensor product of modules and the twisted product of flag varieties. The study of the totally nonnegative part of this twisted product was initiated by Webster and Yakimov
[
34
]
, who provided a complete description for
GL_{2}
. The general case, however, remained open until the recent work of Bao and the second-named author
[
2
]
. The novel idea was to employ a
thickening method
, embedding the totally nonnegative part of the twisted product of flags for a group
G
into the totally nonnegative part of a single flag variety for a larger Kac-Moody group
\tilde{G}
. The key philosophy is that the enhanced symmetry of
\tilde{G}
provides a powerful tool to unravel the intricate geometry of the twisted product (with, a priori, only the symmetry from
G
).

In this paper, we apply this philosophy to quantum groups. The larger quantum group we construct arises from a version of Nakajima’s framed quiver construction in quiver variety
[
27
,
28
]
. This exemplifies a new phenomenon where wild Kac-Moody groups (those associated with symmetric, indefinite Cartan data) play a key role in encoding and elucidating the representation theory of quantum groups of non-finite and non-affine types. We establish a
thickening realization
of the tensor product and its canonical basis by embedding it into the negative part
\tilde{\mathbf{U}}^{-}
of this larger quantum group. This allows us to deduce the positivity properties for the tensor product directly from the established positivity of the canonical basis of
\tilde{\mathbf{U}}^{-}

[
25
]
.

Let us outline the strategy for the pivotal case of a lowest weight module tensored with a highest weight module, which is the focus of §
3
-§
5
.

It is important to note that our thickening realization does not directly apply to the full tensor product
{}^{\omega}\Lambda_{\lambda_{1}}\otimes\Lambda_{\lambda_{2}}
. Instead, we work with the tensor product
{}^{\omega}V_{w}(\lambda_{1})\otimes\Lambda_{\lambda_{2}}
, where
{}^{\omega}V_{w}(\lambda_{1})
is a Demazure module of
{}^{\omega}\Lambda_{\lambda_{1}}
. This approach is effective for two reasons:

•

the full module
{}^{\omega}\Lambda_{\lambda_{1}}
is recovered as the direct limit of its Demazure submodules;

•

the tensor product of a Demazure module with a highest weight module can be realized as a specific quotient of a Verma module tensor a highest weight module, a structure to which our thickening technique is well-suited.

Preliminary

1.1.
Cartan datum and root datum

A symmetric Cartan datum
(I,\cdot)
consists of a finite set
I
and a symmetric bilinear form
\mathbb{Z}[I]\times\mathbb{Z}[I]\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
denoted by
(\nu,\nu^{\prime})\nu\cdot\nu^{\prime}
such that

(1)

i\cdot i=2
for any
i\in I
;

(2)

i\cdot j\leqslant 0
for any
i\not=j
in
I
.

A
Y
-regular root datum
(Y,X,\langle\,,\,\rangle,\ldots)
of type
(I,\cdot)
consists of two finitely generated free abelian groups
Y,X
, a perfect bilinear pairing
\langle\,,\,\rangle:Y\times X\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
and two embeddings
I\hookrightarrow Y,I\hookrightarrow X
such that

(1)

\langle i,j\rangle=i\cdot j
for any
i,j\in I
;

(2)

the image of the embedding
I\hookrightarrow Y
is linearly independent in
Y
.

The embeddings
I\hookrightarrow Y
and
I\hookrightarrow X
induce the group homomorphisms
\mathbb{Z}[I]\rightarrow Y
and
\mathbb{Z}[I]\rightarrow X
respectively. We denote the images of
\nu\in\mathbb{Z}[I]
under either of these homomorphisms by the same symbol
\nu
.

Throughout this paper, we fix a symmetric Cartan datum
(I,\cdot)
and a
Y
-regular root datum
(Y,X,\langle\,,\,\rangle,\ldots)
of type
(I,\cdot)
.

The algebra
\mathbf{f}

We follow
[

.

Let
\mathbf{U}^{+}
and
\mathbf{U}^{-}
be the subalgebras of
\mathbf{U}
generated by
E_{i}
and
F_{i}
for any
i\in I
respectively. Then there is an algebra automorphism
\omega:\mathbf{U}\rightarrow\mathbf{U}
defined by
\omega(E_{i})=F_{i},\omega(F_{i})=E_{i},\omega(K_{\mu})=K_{-\mu}
for any
i\in I
and
\mu\in Y
.
There are algebra isomorphisms
\mathbf{f}\rightarrow\mathbf{U}^{+},xx^{+}
and
\mathbf{f}\rightarrow\mathbf{U}^{-},xx^{-}

such that
E_{i}=\theta_{i}^{+}
and
F_{i}=\theta_{i}^{-}
for any
i\in I
. Moreover,
\omega(x^{+})=x^{-}
and
\omega(x^{-})=x^{+}
for any
x\in\mathbf{f}
.

Let
\mathbf{U}^{0}
be the subalgebra of
\mathbf{U}
generated by
K_{\mu}
for any
\mu\in Y
. We denote the subalgebras
\mathbf{U}(\mathfrak{b}^{+})=\mathbf{U}^{0}\otimes\mathbf{U}^{+}
and
\mathbf{U}(\mathfrak{b}^{-})=\mathbf{U}^{0}\otimes\mathbf{U}^{-}
of
\mathbf{U}
.

The tensor product
\mathbf{U}\otimes\mathbf{U}
over
\mathbb{Q}(v)
is an algebra with the multiplication

(u_{1}\otimes u_{2})(u^{\prime}_{1},u^{\prime}_{2})=u_{1}u^{\prime}_{1}\otimes u_{2}u^{\prime}_{2}\ \textrm{for any}\ u_{1},u_{2},u^{\prime}_{1},u^{\prime}_{2}\in\mathbf{U}.

The comultiplication of
\mathbf{U}
is the algebra homomorphism
\Delta:\mathbf{U}\rightarrow\mathbf{U}\otimes\mathbf{U}
defined by
\Delta(E_{i})=E_{i}\otimes 1+K_{i}\otimes E_{i},\Delta(F_{i})=1\otimes F_{i}+F_{i}\otimes K_{-i},\Delta(K_{\mu})=K_{\mu}\otimes K_{\mu}
for any
i\in I
and
\mu\in Y
.

1.4.
Weight module

We refer to
[
25
, §3.4]
for details.

A
\mathbf{U}
-module
M
over
\mathbb{Q}(v)
is a weight module, if its underlying
\mathbb{Q}(v)
-vector space decomposes as a direct sum of weight spaces

M=\bigoplus_{\zeta\in X}M^{\zeta},\text{ where }M^{\zeta}=\{m\in M;K_{\mu}m=v^{\langle\mu,\zeta\rangle}m\text{ for any }\mu\in Y\}.

All
\mathbf{U}
-modules considered in this paper are weight modules.

For any
\mathbf{U}
-module
M
, we define a new
\mathbf{U}
-module
{{}^{\omega}M}
. Its underlying
\mathbb{Q}(v)
-vector space is
M
, and the action of
u\in\mathbf{U}
on
{{}^{\omega}M}
is the same as the action of
\omega(u)
on
M
. For
\zeta\in X
,
({{}^{\omega}M})^{\zeta}=M^{-\zeta}
.

For any
\zeta\in X
, we denote by
M_{\zeta}
the Verma module of
\mathbf{U}
with the highest weight
\zeta
. Its underlying
\mathbb{Q}(v)
-vector space is
\mathbf{f}
, and the
\mathbf{U}
-module structure is defined by
E_{i}1=0,F_{i}x=\theta_{i}x,K_{\mu}x=v^{\langle\mu,\zeta-|x|\rangle}x
for any
i\in I,\mu\in Y
and homogeneous
x\in\mathbf{f}
. For
\zeta^{\prime}\in X
,
M_{\zeta}^{\zeta^{\prime}}=\bigoplus_{\nu=\zeta-\zeta^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}[I]}\mathbf{f}_{\nu}
.

Let
X^{+}=\{\lambda\in X;\langle i,\lambda\rangle\in\mathbb{N}\text{ for any }i\in I\}
be the set of dominant weights. For any
\lambda\in X^{+}
, the simple
\mathbf{U}
-module with the highest weight
\lambda
is

\Lambda_{\lambda}=M_{\lambda}/\sum_{i\in I}\mathbf{f}\theta_{i}^{\langle i,\lambda\rangle+1}.

We denote by
\eta_{\lambda}
its highest weight vector.

The simple
\mathbf{U}
-module with the lowest weight
-\lambda
is
{{}^{\omega}\Lambda_{\lambda}}
. We denote by
\xi_{-\lambda}
its lowest weight vector.

1.5.
Modified quantum group

We refer to
[
25
, Chapter 23]
for details.

The modified quantum group

\dot{\mathbf{U}}=\bigoplus_{\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2}\in X}{{}_{\zeta_{1}}\mathbf{U}_{\zeta_{2}}},\ \textrm{where}\ {{}_{\zeta_{1}}\mathbf{U}_{\zeta_{2}}}=\mathbf{U}/(\sum_{\mu\in Y}(K_{\mu}-v^{\langle\mu,\zeta_{1}\rangle})\mathbf{U}+\sum_{\mu\in Y}\mathbf{U}(K_{\mu}-v^{\langle\mu,\zeta_{2}\rangle})),

is a
\mathbb{Q}(v)
-algebra defined by Lusztig in
[
25
, §23.1.1]
. In the meanwhile, it is a free
(\mathbf{f}\otimes\mathbf{f}^{\mathrm{opp}})
-module with a basis
\{1_{\zeta};\zeta\in X\}
, where
1_{\zeta}
is the image of
1\in\mathbf{U}
under the natural projection
\mathbf{U}\rightarrow{{}_{\zeta}\mathbf{U}_{\zeta}}
. Comparing with the triangular decomposition
\mathbf{U}=\mathbf{U}^{+}\otimes\mathbf{U}^{0}\otimes\mathbf{U}^{-}
of the quantum group,
\dot{\mathbf{U}}
is a modified form of
\mathbf{U}
in which
\mathbf{U}^{0}
is replaced by
\bigoplus_{\zeta\in X}\mathbb{Q}(v)1_{\zeta}
.

A
\dot{\mathbf{U}}
-module
M
over
\mathbb{Q}(v)
is called
unital
, if for any
m\in M
, we have
1_{\zeta}m=0
for all but finitely many
\zeta\in X
and
\sum_{\zeta\in X}1_{\zeta}m=m
. Then giving a unital
\dot{\mathbf{U}}
-module is the same as giving a weight
\mathbf{U}
-module (see
[
25
, §23.1.4]
). For this reason,
\dot{\mathbf{U}}
is an algebra more appropriate than
\mathbf{U}
for the study of weight modules.

1.6.
Canonical basis of
\mathbf{f}

We refer to
[
25
, Chapter 14]
for details.

Let
\mathcal{A}=\mathbb{Z}[v,v^{-1}]\subset\mathbb{Q}(v)
be the ring of Laurent polynomials with coefficients in
\mathbb{Z}
. The integral form
{{}_{\mathcal{A}}\mathbf{f}}
of
\mathbf{f}
is the
\mathcal{A}
-subalgebra generated by
\theta_{i}^{(n)}
for any
i\in I
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

The bar-involution on
\mathbf{f}
is the
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra involution
\mathbf{f}\rightarrow\mathbf{f},x\overline{x}
defined by
\overline{\theta_{i}}=\theta_{i},\overline{v^{n}}=v^{-n}
for any
i\in I
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

By
[
25
, Proposition 1.2.3]
, there is a unique non-degenerate symmetric bilinear inner product
(\,,\,)_{\mathbf{f}}:\mathbf{f}\times\mathbf{f}\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}(v)
such that

(1)

(1,1)_{\mathbf{f}}=1
and
(\theta_{i},\theta_{j})_{\mathbf{f}}=\delta_{i,j}(1-v^{-2})^{-1}
for any
i,j\in I
;

(2)

(x_{1}x_{2},x)_{\mathbf{f}}=(x_{1}\otimes x_{2},r(x))_{\mathbf{f}\otimes\mathbf{f}}
for any
x_{1},x_{2},x\in\mathbf{f}
, where the inner product on
\mathbf{f}\otimes\mathbf{f}
is given by
(x_{1}\otimes x_{2},x^{\prime}_{1}\otimes x^{\prime}_{2})_{\mathbf{f}\otimes\mathbf{f}}=(x_{1},x^{\prime}_{1})_{\mathbf{f}}(x_{2},x^{\prime}_{2})_{\mathbf{f}}
for any
x_{1},x_{2},x^{\prime}_{1},x^{\prime}_{2}\in\mathbf{f}
.

The canonical basis
\mathbf{B}
of
\mathbf{f}
(defined in
[
25
, Definition 14.2.4]
) is a
\mathcal{A}
-basis of
{{}_{\mathcal{A}}\mathbf{f}}
such that
\overline{b}=b
for any
b\in\mathbf{B}
;
(b,b^{\prime})_{\mathbf{f}}\in\delta_{b,b^{\prime}}+v^{-1}\mathbb{Z}[[v^{-1}]]
for any
b,b^{\prime}\in\mathbf{B}
. The canonical basis has
\mathbf{B}
the following positivity property.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
25
, Theorem 14.4.13]
)
.

Let
b_{1},b_{2},b\in\mathbf{B}
. Then

b_{1}b_{2}\in\mathbb{N}[v,v^{-1}][\mathbf{B}],\ r(b)\in\mathbb{N}[v,v^{-1}][\mathbf{B}\otimes\mathbf{B}].

Let
\lambda\in X^{+}
. The integral form
{{}_{\mathcal{A}}\Lambda_{\lambda}}
of
\Lambda_{\lambda}
is the image of
{{}_{\mathcal{A}}\mathbf{f}}
under the natural projection
\mathbf{f}=M_{\lambda}\rightarrow\Lambda_{\lambda}
.

The bar-involution on
\mathbf{U}
is the
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra involution
\mathbf{U}\rightarrow\mathbf{U},u\overline{u}
defined by
\overline{E_{i}}=E_{i},\overline{F_{i}}=F_{i},\overline{K_{\mu}}=K_{-\mu},\overline{v^{n}}=v^{-n}
for any
i\in I,\mu\in Y
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. The bar-involution on
\Lambda_{\lambda}
is the
\mathbb{Q}
-linear involution
\Lambda_{\lambda}\rightarrow\Lambda_{\lambda},m\overline{m}
given by
\overline{u\eta_{\lambda}}=\overline{u}\eta_{\lambda}
for any
u\in\mathbf{U}
. Then
\overline{um}=\bar{u}\bar{m}
for any
u\in\mathbf{U}
and
m\in\Lambda_{\lambda}
.

By
[
25
, Proposition 19.1.2]
, there is a
unique symmetric bilinear inner product
(\,,\,)_{\Lambda_{\lambda}}:\Lambda_{\lambda}\times\Lambda_{\lambda}\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}(v)
such that

(1)

(\eta_{\lambda},\eta_{\lambda})_{\Lambda_{\lambda}}=1
;

(2)

(um,m^{\prime})_{\Lambda_{\lambda}}\!\!=\!(m,\rho(u)m^{\prime})_{\Lambda_{\lambda}}\!
for any
u\in\mathbf{U}
and
m,m^{\prime}\in\Lambda_{\lambda}
, where
\rho:\mathbf{U}\rightarrow\mathbf{U}^{\mathrm{opp}}
is the algebra homomorphism defined in
[
25
, §19.1.1]
.

Let
\mathbf{B}(\Lambda_{\lambda})=\{b^{-}\eta_{\lambda};b\in\mathbf{B}\}\setminus\{0\}
. By
[
25
, Theorem 14.4.11]
, it is a basis of
\Lambda_{\lambda}
, called the canonical basis of
\Lambda_{\lambda}
. It is a
\mathcal{A}
-basis of
{{}_{\mathcal{A}}\Lambda_{\lambda}}
such that
\overline{b}=b
for any
b\in\mathbf{B}(\Lambda_{\lambda})
;
(b,b^{\prime})_{\Lambda_{\lambda}}\in\delta_{b,b^{\prime}}+v^{-1}\mathbb{Z}[v^{-1}]
for any
b,b^{\prime}\in\mathbf{B}(\Lambda_{\lambda})
. The canonical basis
\mathbf{B}(\Lambda_{\lambda})
has the following positivity property.

Theorem 1.2

(
[
25
, Theorem 22.1.7]
)
.

Let
i\in I
and
\lambda\in X^{+}
. Then

F_{i}(\mathbf{B}(\Lambda_{\lambda})),E_{i}(\mathbf{B}(\Lambda_{\lambda}))\subset\mathbb{N}[v,v^{-1}][\mathbf{B}(\Lambda_{\lambda})].

In general, since
\mathbf{U}^{-}
acts on
\Lambda_{\lambda}
by multiplication, by the positivity property of
\mathbf{B}
with respect to the multiplication in Theorem
1.1
,
\mathbf{B}(\Lambda_{\lambda})
has the positivity property with respect to the action of
b^{-}
for any
b\in\mathbf{B}
. The positivity property with respect to the action of
b^{+}
for any
b\in\mathbf{B}
will be established in Corollary
2.3
.

Similarly, the simple lowest weight module
{{}^{\omega}\Lambda_{\lambda}}
has the integral form, the bar-involution, and the inner product which satisfy similar properties. The canonical basis
\mathbf{B}({{}^{\omega}\Lambda_{\lambda}})=\{b^{+}\xi_{-\lambda};b\in\mathbf{B}\}\setminus\{0\}
of
{{}^{\omega}\Lambda_{\lambda}}
satisfies similar properties.

1.7.
Based
\mathbf{U}
-module

A based
\mathbf{U}
-module
(M,B)
is an integrable
\mathbf{U}
-module
M
with a basis
B
satisfying the conditions in
[
3
, §2.1]
which is a generalization of
[
25
, §27.1.2]
. One condition is that the
\mathbb{Q}
-linear involution
M\rightarrow M,m\overline{m}
defined by
\overline{b}=b,\overline{v^{n}}=v^{-n}
for any
b\in B
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, is compatible with the bar-involution and the action of
\mathbf{U}
, that is,

(1.1)

\overline{um}=\bar{u}\bar{m}\ \textrm{for any}\ u\in\mathbf{U},m\in M.

For any based
\mathbf{U}
-modules
(M,B)
and
(M^{\prime},B^{\prime})
, a based
\mathbf{U}
-module homomorphism from
(M,B)
to
(M^{\prime},B^{\prime})
is a
\mathbf{U}
-module homomorphism
f:M\rightarrow M^{\prime}
such that
f(B)\subset B^{\prime}\sqcup\{0\}
, and
B\cap\mathrm{ker}\,f
is a basis of
\mathrm{ker}\,f
.

For any
\lambda\in X^{+}
, the pairs
(\Lambda_{\lambda},\mathbf{B}(\Lambda_{\lambda}))
and
({{}^{\omega}\Lambda_{\lambda}},\mathbf{B}({{}^{\omega}\Lambda_{\lambda}}))
are based
\mathbf{U}
-modules (see
[
25
, §27.1.4]
).

1.8.
Canonical basis of tensor product

For
\mathbf{U}
-modules
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
, the tensor product
M_{1}\otimes M_{2}
over
\mathbb{Q}(v)
is naturally a
\mathbf{U}\otimes\mathbf{U}
-module. We regard
M_{1}\otimes M_{2}
as a
\mathbf{U}
-module via the comultiplication
\Delta:\mathbf{U}\rightarrow\mathbf{U}\otimes\mathbf{U}
. For
\zeta\in X
,
(M_{1}\otimes M_{2})^{\zeta}=\bigoplus_{\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2}\in X,\zeta_{1}+\zeta_{2}=\zeta}M_{1}^{\zeta_{1}}\otimes M_{2}^{\zeta_{2}}
.

For subsets
A_{1}\subset M_{1},A_{2}\subset M_{2}
, we write
A_{1}\otimes A_{2}=\{v_{1}\otimes v_{2};v_{1}\in A_{1},v_{2}\in A_{2}\}
. If the elements
v_{1}\diamondsuit v_{2}\in M_{1}\otimes M_{2}
have been defined for any
v_{1}\in A_{1}
and
v_{2}\in A_{2}
, then we simply write
A_{1}\diamondsuit A_{2}=\{v_{1}\diamondsuit v_{2};v_{1}\in A_{1},v_{2}\in A_{2}\}
.

Let
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in X^{+}
. Then
\mathbf{B}({{}^{\omega}\Lambda_{\lambda_{1}}})\otimes\mathbf{B}(\Lambda_{\lambda_{2}})
and
\mathbf{B}(\Lambda_{\lambda_{1}})\otimes\mathbf{B}(\Lambda_{\lambda_{2}})

are bases of
{{}^{\omega}\Lambda_{\lambda_{1}}}\otimes\Lambda_{\lambda_{2}}
and
\Lambda_{\lambda_{1}}\otimes\Lambda_{\lambda_{2}}
respectively. The bar-involutions on
{{}^{\omega}\Lambda_{\lambda_{1}}}\otimes\Lambda_{\lambda_{2}}
and
\Lambda_{\lambda_{1}}\otimes\Lambda_{\lambda_{2}}
are defined by

\overline{m_{1}\otimes m_{2}}=\overline{m_{1}}\otimes\overline{m_{2}}
for
m_{1}\in{{}^{\omega}\Lambda_{\lambda_{1}}}
or
\Lambda_{\lambda_{1}},m_{2}\in\Lambda_{\lambda_{2}}
.

Note that in general
\Delta(\overline{u})\not=\overline{\Delta(u)}
for
u\in\mathbf{U}
and
\overline{u(m_{1}\otimes m_{2})}\not=\overline{u}\,\overline{m_{1}\otimes m_{2}}
. Hence
({{}^{\omega}\Lambda_{\lambda_{1}}}\otimes\Lambda_{\lambda_{2}},\mathbf{B}({{}^{\omega}\Lambda_{\lambda_{1}}})\otimes\mathbf{B}(\Lambda_{\lambda_{2}})),\ (\Lambda_{\lambda_{1}}\otimes\Lambda_{\lambda_{2}},\mathbf{B}(\Lambda_{\lambda_{1}})\otimes\mathbf{B}(\Lambda_{\lambda_{2}}))

are not based
\mathbf{U}
-modules.

Lusztig defined a new
\mathbb{Q}
-linear involution on the tensor products satisfying (
1.1
). By
[
25
, §14.2.5]
, we have
\mathbf{B}=\bigsqcup_{\nu\in\mathbb{N}[I]}\mathbf{B}_{\nu}
, where
\mathbf{B}_{\nu}=\mathbf{B}\cap\mathbf{f}_{\nu}
is a basis of
\mathbf{f}_{\nu}
. For any
\nu=\sum_{i\in I}\nu_{i}i\in\mathbb{N}[I]
, we denote
\mathrm{tr}\,\nu=\sum_{i\in I}\nu_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
\{b^{*};b\in\mathbf{B}_{\nu}\}
be the basis of
\mathbf{f}_{\nu}
dual to the basis
\mathbf{B}_{\nu}
with respect to
(\,,\,)_{\mathbf{f}}
. The quasi-
\mathcal{R}
-matrices on
{{}^{\omega}\Lambda_{\lambda_{1}}}\otimes\Lambda_{\lambda_{2}}
and
\Lambda_{\lambda_{1}}\otimes\Lambda_{\lambda_{2}}
are the linear transformations on them defined by

\Theta(m_{1}\otimes m_{2})=\!\sum_{\nu\in\mathbb{N}[I]}(-v)^{\mathrm{tr}\,\nu}\sum_{b\in\mathbf{B}_{\nu}}b^{-}m_{1}\otimes b^{*+}m_{2}\ \textrm{for any $m_{1}\in{{}^{\omega}\Lambda_{\lambda_{1}}}$ or $\Lambda_{\lambda_{1}}$, $m_{2}\in\Lambda_{\lambda_{2}}$}.

Note that there are always only finitely many non-zero terms in the summation. By
[
25
, Lemma 24.1.2]
,
\Delta(\overline{u})\Theta(m_{1}\otimes m_{2})=\Theta\overline{\Delta(u)}(m_{1}\otimes m_{2})
for
u\in\mathbf{U}
,
m_{1}\in{{}^{\omega}\Lambda_{\lambda_{1}}}
or
\Lambda_{\lambda_{1}}
and
m_{2}\in\Lambda_{\lambda_{2}}
. The quasi-
\mathcal{R}
-matrix is an intertwiner between the composition of the comultiplication and the bar-involution taken in different orders, which is similar to the universal
\mathcal{R}
-matrix introduced by Drinfeld as an intertwiner between the comultiplication and its transpose.

The
\Psi
-involutions on
{{}^{\omega}\Lambda_{\lambda_{1}}}\otimes\Lambda_{\lambda_{2}}
and
\Lambda_{\lambda_{1}}\otimes\Lambda_{\lambda_{2}}
are the
\mathbb{Q}
-linear transformations on them defined by

(1.2)

\Psi(m_{1}\otimes m_{2})=\Theta(\overline{m_{1}}\otimes\overline{m_{2}})\ \textrm{for any $m_{1}\in{{}^{\omega}\Lambda_{\lambda_{1}}}$ or $\Lambda_{\lambda_{1}}$, $m_{2}\in\Lambda_{\lambda_{2}}$}.

Then
\Psi(u(m_{1}\otimes m_{2}))=\overline{u}\Psi(m_{1}\otimes m_{2})
for any
u\in\mathbf{U}
,
m_{1}\in{{}^{\omega}\Lambda_{\lambda_{1}}}
or
\Lambda_{\lambda_{1}},m_{2}\in\Lambda_{\lambda_{2}}
, that is, the
\Psi
-involutions satisfy (
1.1
).

The canonical basis of the tensor product
{{}^{\omega}\Lambda_{\lambda_{1}}}\otimes\Lambda_{\lambda_{2}}
was established by Lusztig in
[
25
, Theorem 24.3.3]
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in X^{+}
, and
\mathcal{L}_{-\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}}
be the
\mathbb{Z}[v^{-1}]
-submodule of
{{}^{\omega}\Lambda_{\lambda_{1}}}\otimes\Lambda_{\lambda_{2}}
generated by
\mathbf{B}({{}^{\omega}\Lambda_{\lambda_{1}}})\otimes\mathbf{B}(\Lambda_{\lambda_{2}})
. Then
for any
b_{1}^{+}\xi_{-\lambda_{1}}\in\mathbf{B}({{}^{\omega}\Lambda_{\lambda_{1}}})
and
b_{2}^{-}\eta_{\lambda_{2}}\in\mathbf{B}(\Lambda_{\lambda_{2}})
, there exists a unique
b_{1}^{+}\xi_{-\lambda_{1}}\diamondsuit b_{2}^{-}\eta_{\lambda_{2}}\in\mathcal{L}_{-\lambda_{1},\lambda_{1}}
such that

\displaystyle\Psi(b_{1}^{+}\xi_{-\lambda_{1}}\diamondsuit b_{2}^{-}\eta_{\lambda_{2}})=b_{1}^{+}\xi_{-\lambda_{1}}\diamondsuit b_{2}^{-}\eta_{\lambda_{2}};\ b_{1}^{+}\xi_{-\lambda_{1}}\diamondsuit b_{2}^{-}\eta_{\lambda_{2}}-b_{1}^{+}\xi_{-\lambda_{1}}\otimes b_{2}^{-}\eta_{\lambda_{2}}\in v^{-1}\mathcal{L}_{-\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}}.

Moreover, the set
\mathbf{B}({{}^{\omega}\Lambda_{\lambda_{1}}})\diamondsuit\mathbf{B}(\Lambda_{\lambda_{2}})
is a basis of the tensor product
{{}^{\omega}\Lambda_{\lambda_{1}}}\otimes\Lambda_{\lambda_{2}}
.

The set
\mathbf{B}({{}^{\omega}\Lambda_{\lambda_{1}}}\otimes\Lambda_{\lambda_{2}})=\mathbf{B}({{}^{\omega}\Lambda_{\lambda_{1}}})\diamondsuit\mathbf{B}(\Lambda_{\lambda_{2}})
is the canonical basis of the tensor product
{{}^{\omega}\Lambda_{\lambda_{1}}}\otimes\Lambda_{\lambda_{2}}
. Moreover,
({{}^{\omega}\Lambda_{\lambda_{1}}}\otimes\Lambda_{\lambda_{2}},\mathbf{B}({{}^{\omega}\Lambda_{\lambda_{1}}}\otimes\Lambda_{\lambda_{2}}))
is a based
\mathbf{U}
-module.

The canonical basis of the tensor product
\Lambda_{\lambda_{1}}\otimes\Lambda_{\lambda_{2}}
was established by Bao and Wang in
[
3
, Theorem 2.7]
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in X^{+}
and
\mathcal{L}_{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}}
be the
\mathbb{Z}[v^{-1}]
-submodule of
\Lambda_{\lambda_{1}}\otimes\Lambda_{\lambda_{2}}
generated by
\mathbf{B}(\Lambda_{\lambda_{1}})\otimes\mathbf{B}(\Lambda_{\lambda_{2}})
. Then
for any
b_{1}^{-}\eta_{\lambda_{1}}\in\mathbf{B}(\Lambda_{\lambda_{1}})
and
b_{2}^{-}\eta_{\lambda_{2}}\in\mathbf{B}(\Lambda_{\lambda_{2}})
, there exists a unique
b_{1}^{-}\eta_{\lambda_{1}}\diamondsuit b_{2}^{-}\eta_{\lambda_{2}}\in\mathcal{L}_{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}}
such that

\displaystyle\Psi(b_{1}^{-}\eta_{\lambda_{1}}\diamondsuit b_{2}^{-}\eta_{\lambda_{2}})=b_{1}^{-}\eta_{\lambda_{1}}\diamondsuit b_{2}^{-}\eta_{\lambda_{2}};\ b_{1}^{-}\eta_{\lambda_{1}}\diamondsuit b_{2}^{-}\eta_{\lambda_{2}}-b_{1}^{-}\eta_{\lambda_{1}}\otimes b_{2}^{-}\eta_{\lambda_{2}}\in v^{-1}\mathcal{L}_{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}}.

Moreover, the set
\mathbf{B}({\Lambda_{\lambda_{1}}})\diamondsuit\mathbf{B}(\Lambda_{\lambda_{2}})
is a basis of the tensor product
\Lambda_{\lambda_{1}}\otimes\Lambda_{\lambda_{2}}
.

The set
\mathbf{B}(\Lambda_{\lambda_{1}}\otimes\Lambda_{\lambda_{2}})=\mathbf{B}({\Lambda_{\lambda_{1}}})\diamondsuit\mathbf{B}(\Lambda_{\lambda_{2}})
is the canonical basis of the tensor product
{\Lambda_{\lambda_{1}}}\otimes\Lambda_{\lambda_{2}}
. Moreover,
({\Lambda_{\lambda_{1}}}\otimes\Lambda_{\lambda_{2}},\mathbf{B}({\Lambda_{\lambda_{1}}}\otimes\Lambda_{\lambda_{2}}))
is a based
\mathbf{U}
-module.

1.9.
Canonical basis of modified quantum group

The integral form
{{}_{\mathcal{A}}\dot{\mathbf{U}}}
of
\dot{\mathbf{U}}
is the
\mathcal{A}
-submodule generated by
x_{1}^{+}1_{\zeta}x_{2}^{-}
for any
x_{1},x_{2}\in{{}_{\mathcal{A}}\mathbf{f}}
(see
[
25
, Lemma 23.2.2]
).

The canonical basis
\mathbf{B}({{}^{\omega}\Lambda_{\lambda_{1}}}\otimes\Lambda_{\lambda_{2}})
of the tensor product
{{}^{\omega}\Lambda_{\lambda_{1}}}\otimes\Lambda_{\lambda_{2}}
for any
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in X^{+}
has very nice stability properties in the sense of
[
25
, §25.1]
. The canonical basis of
\dot{\mathbf{U}}
was established by Lusztig in
[
25
, Theorem 25.2.1]
by gluing
\mathbf{B}({{}^{\omega}\Lambda_{\lambda_{1}}}\otimes\Lambda_{\lambda_{2}})
for
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in X^{+}
.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
\zeta\in X
and
b_{1},b_{2}\in\mathbf{B}
. Then

(1)

There exists a unique
b_{1}\diamondsuit_{\zeta}b_{2}\in{{}_{\mathcal{A}}\dot{\mathbf{U}}1_{\zeta}}
such that

(b_{1}\diamondsuit_{\zeta}b_{2})(\xi_{-\lambda_{1}}\otimes\eta_{\lambda_{2}})=b_{1}^{+}\xi_{-\lambda_{1}}\diamondsuit b_{2}^{-}\eta_{\lambda_{2}}\in\mathbf{B}({{}^{\omega}\Lambda_{\lambda_{1}}}\otimes\Lambda_{\lambda_{2}})

for any
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in X^{+}
such that
\zeta=\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1}
and
b_{1}^{+}\xi_{-\lambda_{1}},b_{2}^{-}\eta_{\lambda_{2}}\not=0
;

(2)

We have
(b_{1}\diamondsuit_{\zeta}b_{2})(\xi_{-\lambda_{1}}\otimes\eta_{\lambda_{2}})=0

for any
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in X^{+}
such that
\zeta=\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1}
and either
b_{1}^{+}\xi_{-\lambda_{1}}=0
or

3.

3.1.

For the symmetric Cartan datum

, we define its

to be the symmetric Cartan datum

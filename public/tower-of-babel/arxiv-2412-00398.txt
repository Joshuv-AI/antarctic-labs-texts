3.1

Proposition 3.1.1
.

Let

. Let

Proof.

At first, we show that the Galois group

be the torsion subgroup of the multiplicative group

of the field

. Then

coincides with the set of all roots of unity and

. Evidently, each automorphism

of the group

and the mapping

is a homomorphism of the group

into

. It is easy to note that

. Therefore, if

then

centralizes all roots of unity from

and hence, as

and

centralizes the elements of the subfield

, we can conclude that

. Thus,

is a monomorphism of the group

into

. If follows from
[

, Theorem 127.3]
that

, where

runs all prime integers such that

and
{C_{{p^{\infty}}}}
is a quasi-cyclic

-group. As
{C_{{p^{\infty}}}}
is the

-component of the group

, it follows from
[

, §113, Assertion g)]
that

. It is well known that

is isomorphic to the group of units of the ring

of

[

, §113, Example 3]
). Thus, we can conclude that the group

is abelian and, as

is a monomorphism of the group

into

, we see that the group

is abelian. Then it follows from

.
∎

Let

Theorem 3.1.4
.

Let

Since

is the field of fractions of

Corollary 3.1.5

Let

Let

of

3.2

Theorem 3.2.1
.

Let

be an abelian group of finite total rank such that

. Let

be a prime faithful ideal of the group ring

. Then:

(i) there exists a finitely generated subgroup

of

such that

;

(ii) the ideal

is finitely generated;

Proof.

(i) Let

and let
F
be the field of fractions of the commutative domain

. As the ideal

is faithful, we can assume that

. By Theorem 3.1.4, there exists a subgroup

such that

, where
T
is a transversal to
H
in
G
. Evidently,
ann_{kG}k[G]=P
and

. Then it follows from
[

, Chap. 2, Theorem 3.4(i)]

, where

. Since the ring
kG
is commutative, we see that

and hence

.

(ii) Since the subgroup
H
is finitely generated, the group ring
kH
is Noetherian. Hence
P\cap kH
is a finitely generated ideal of
kH
, and the assertion follows.

∎

A ring

5
On irreducible representations of nilpotent FATR-groups of nilpotency class 2

Primitive irreducible modules are a basic subject for investigations when we are dealing with induced modules and, naturally, the following question appears: what can be said on the construction of a group
G
if it admits a faithful primitive (or semiprimitive) irreducible representation over a field
k
? It should be noted that there are many results which show that the existence of a faithful irreducible representation of a group
G
over a field
k
may have essential influence on the structure of the group
G
(see for instance
[
19
,
20
,
21
,
22
,
26
]
).

In
[
7
]
Harper solved a problem raised by Zaleskii and proved that any not abelian-by-finite finitely generated nilpotent group has an irreducible primitive representation over a not locally finite field. In
[
24
]
we proved that if a minimax nilpotent group
G
of nilpotency class 2 has a faithful irreducible primitive representation over a finitely generated field of characteristic zero then the group
G
is finitely generated. In
[
8
]
Harper studied polycyclic groups which have faithful irreducible representations. It is well known that any polycyclic group is finitely generated soluble of finite rank and meets the maximal condition for subgroups (in particular, for normal subgroups). In
[
23
]
we showed that in the class of soluble groups of finite rank with the maximal condition for normal subgroups only polycyclic groups may have faithful irreducible primitive representations over a field of characteristic zero.

If
G
is a group then the
FC
-center
\Delta(G)=\{g\in G|\left|{G:{C_{G}}(g)}\right|<\infty\}
of

is a characteristic subgroup of
G
. In
[
8
, Theorem A]
Harper proved that if a polycyclic group

has a faithful primitive irreducible representation over a field

then

is rather large in the sense that

for any subgroup

of

such that

.

By Auslander theorem, any polycyclic group

is linear over the field and it is well known that the group

is finitely generated of finite rank. In
[
28
]
we study finitely generated linear (over a field of characteristic zero) groups of finite rank which have faithful irreducible primitive representations over a field of characteristic zero. We prove that if an infinite finitely generated linear group

of finite rank has a faithful irreducible primitive representation over a field of characteristic zero then

is infinite (see

).

Kerr-AdS type higher dimensional black holes with non-spherical cross-sections of horizons
Piotr T. Chruściel, Wan Cong, and Finnian Gray

Abstract
We construct, in even spacetime dimensions, a family of singularity-free Kerr-anti-de Sitter-like black holes with negatively curved cross-sections of conformal infinity and non-compact, non-spherical cross-sections of horizons, with the full set of rotation parameters.

Introduction

The Schwarzschild metrics, together with their higher-dimensional generalisations, are expected to exhaust the collection of
(n+1)
-dimensional, vacuum, static, asymptotically flat metrics satisfying natural global properties
[
1
]
.

These metrics are a special case of the Birmingham-Kottler metrics
[

4

, which allow for any value of the cosmological constant
\Lambda

(n-1)
-dimensional spherical sections of the event horizon can be replaced by any
(n-1)
-dimensional Einstein geometry.

The Kerr metric is the unique
(3+1)
-dimensional vacuum stationary analytic and asymptotically flat black hole satisfying a set of reasonable properties, and its higher dimensional generalisation are likewise of interest.
The family of higher dimensional, asymptotically flat Kerr-like vacuum metrics was introduced by Myers and Perry in
[
5
]
while the case in which we are interested, with non-zero cosmological constant, has been presented in
[
6
,
7
]
.
In
(3+1)
dimensions the algebraically special property of the Weyl tensor of the Kerr metric is often emphasised, and such algebraically special metrics have been considered and classified in higher dimensions, see
[
8
,
9
]
and references therein; some partial uniqueness results have been obtained
[
10
,
11
]
, see also
[
12
]
for a uniqueness result when
\Lambda>0
.
The Kerr-like class of metrics has been generalised to include multiple NUT parameters
[
13
]
.
We refer the interested reader to the review
[
14
]
for more examples of higher dimensional black holes.

Now, the sections of the horizons of the metrics of
[
6
,
7
]
have spherical topology.

A more general class of Kerr-AdS-like metrics, with negatively curved horizons and
with a single rotation parameter has been discovered by Klemm, Moretti, and Vanzo
[
15
,
16
]
.
This begs question of existence of Kerr-like
(n+1)
-dimensional vacuum metrics with negatively curved horizons and with the whole set of rotation parameters.

The aim of this paper is to point out the existence of such stationary metrics, in the case of a negative cosmological constant, with non-spherical sections of the horizon, in all even spacetime dimensions
(n+1)\geq 4
.

The metrics we obtain are parameterised by a mass parameter
\bar{m}
and by
N:=(n-1)/2
rotation parameters
\bar{a}_{i}
.
As already pointed out, they generalise the metrics presented in
[
15
,
16
]
, which have only one rotation parameter.

In particular, when
\bar{m}\bar{a}_{1}...\bar{a}_{N}\neq 0
the metrics have the remarkable property of having no curvature singularities.
We have not investigated the question, whether or not the spacetimes are geodesically complete.
It should be clear from the projection diagrams that we construct in Section
4
that the maximal extensions considered there are not globally hyperbolic; whether or not the spacetimes are geodesically complete, and whether the domains of outer communication are globally hyperbolic, would require further investigations.

Again remarkably, no causality violations arise when

2|\bar{m}|<\prod_{i=1}^{N}|\bar{a}_{i}|^{\frac{2N-1}{N}}\,.

(1.1)

(This inequality provides a sufficient condition; an if-and-only-if condition can be found in Proposition
3.4
below.)
Note that the inequality (
1.1
) goes against the intuition gained from the
(3+1)
-dimensional Kerr metric, where large angular momenta lead to singularities.

It is natural to enquire about global Hamiltonian charges associated with our metric.
Such charges are usually defined by integration of Noether-type integrands over a sequence of boundaries which recede to infinity, and passing to the limit. However, we show that there are no natural compact such boundaries unless all rotation parameters vanish, which results in infinite integrals before even passing to infinity.
This appears to considerably reduce the interest of our solutions.

We note that the regularity of the metric does not enforce a lower bound on the parameter
\bar{m}
appearing in our solution, which is usually considered to be directly proportional to the mass when the rotation parameters vanish.
This lack of lower bound might well be a feature related to the non-compactness above.

The metrics have a timelike conformal infinity
à la Penrose
.
The family includes metrics with Killing horizons with vanishing surface gravity
\kappa
, with the outermost Killing horizon having non-zero
\kappa
, as seen from the asymptotic region where
\bar{m}\bar{r}\to\infty
.

We show in Section

In Sections

We give a sharp criterion on the parameters for absence of causality violations in Section

We show that the geometry of sections of the horizon cannot be realised on a compact manifold for small rotation parameters in Section

In Section

First, we redefine the constrained variables according to

\displaystyle\mu_{j}

\displaystyle=i\bar{\mu}_{j}\,,\quad 1\leq j<N\,,\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mu^{N+1}=\bar{\mu}^{N+1}\,,

To retain the signature

\displaystyle:=(\bar{\mu}^{N+1})^{2}-\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{(\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2}}{\bar{\Xi}_{i}}\,,\quad\bar{V}=-(1-\ell^{-2}\bar{r}^{2})\prod_{i=1}^{N}(\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2})\,,

\displaystyle:=\left({(\bar{\mu}^{N+1})^{2}}-{\bar{r}^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{(\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2}}{\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2}}\right)\prod_{i=1}^{N}(\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2})\,,\quad\bar{\Xi}_{i}:=1+\ell^{-2}\bar{a}_{i}^{2}\,.

\displaystyle\bar{g}=\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}dx^{\mu}dx^{\nu}=

3.2
Singularities

If
\bar{m}=0
the metric is locally Anti-de Sitter in unusual coordinates
[
6
]
, and we will demonstrate this explicitly in Section

If
\bar{m}\neq 0

Now, from (

\displaystyle\bar{U}^{-1}=\bigg(1+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{{(\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2}}\bar{a}_{i}^{2}}{\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2}}\bigg)^{-1}\prod_{i=1}^{N}(\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2})^{-1}\,.

(3.21)

Therefore:

1.

Suppose that
\bar{m}\neq 0
and at least one of the rotation parameters vanishes.
Then the norm of the Killing vector

diverges, when

, as
\bar{r}^{-1}
. Hence the metric is

-inextendible across

When all the parameters

and
\bar{U}=\bar{r}^{2N}=\bar{r}^{n-1}
, in which case (

) above)

\bar{g}_{\bar{t}\,\bar{t}}=1-\ell^{-2}\bar{r}^{2}+\frac{2\bar{m}}{\bar{r}^{2n-2}}\,.

2.

The function
\bar{U}^{-1}
is bounded for all

if all the

\bar{a}_{i}
’s are non-vanishing, in which case
\bar{g}_{\bar{t}\,\bar{t}}
remains finite throughout the domain of definition of the metric.

The remaining potential singularities of the tensor field
\bar{g}
arise from
zeros of the determinant, of the
\bar{\mu}^{i}
’s, and of
\bar{V}-2\bar{m}\bar{r}

It turns out that all these can be got rid of by introducing new coordinates.
Indeed, the zeros of the determinant are determined in Appendix

The zeros of

We show in Section

\bar{W}

3.4
) and

F=\frac{\bar{r}^{2}}{1-\ell^{-2}\bar{r}^{2}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{(\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2}}{\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2}}-\frac{(\bar{\mu}^{N+1})^{2}}{\bar{r}^{2}}\right)=-\frac{1}{1-\ell^{-2}\bar{r}^{2}}\left(1+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\bar{a}_{i}^{2}(\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2}}{\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2}}\right)\,.

(3.23)

To see that the metric (
3.22
)-(
3.23
) is locally AdS, one can use the coordinate transformation, defined implicitly by

\displaystyle y^{2}(\hat{\mu}^{i})^{2}=\frac{\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2}}{\Xi_{i}}(\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2}\,,

(3.24)

together with

(\hat{\mu}^{N+1})^{2}-\sum_{i=1}^{N}(\hat{\mu}^{i})^{2}=1\,.

(3.25)

Notice that (
3.24
) and (
3.25
) mean

y^{2}=\bar{r}^{2}(\bar{\mu}^{N+1})^{2}-\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{(\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2})(\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2}}{\bar{\Xi}_{i}}\,,

(3.26)

which implies

1-\ell^{-2}y^{2}=\bar{W}(1-\ell^{-2}\bar{r}^{2})\,.

(3.27)

This brings the metric (
3.22
) into the form

\displaystyle\bar{g}_{AdS}=

\displaystyle(1-\ell^{-2}y^{2})d\tau^{2}-\frac{dy^{2}}{1-\ell^{-2}y^{2}}+y^{2}\Big(\underbrace{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\big((d\hat{\mu}^{i})^{2}+(\hat{\mu}^{i})^{2}(d\varphi^{i})^{2}\big)-(d\hat{\mu}^{N+1})^{2}}\Big)\,,

(3.28)

which is one of the standard forms for the AdS metric with negative cross-sectional curvature, keeping in mind that the underbraced terms need to be restricted to the constraint hyperboloid (
3.25
).

One can use the
(\tau,\bar{r},\varphi^{i},\hat{\mu}^{i})
-coordinates to express the metric (
3.10
) as

\bar{g}=\bar{g}_{AdS}+\frac{2\bar{m}\bar{r}}{\bar{U}}(k_{\mu}dx^{\mu})^{2}\,,

(3.29)

with
\bar{U}
and
\bar{W}
as in (
3.4
), where
k_{\mu}
is a null co-vector (with respect to both (
3.22
) and (
3.29
)) given by

\displaystyle k_{\mu}dx^{\mu}

\displaystyle=\bar{W}d\tau+Fd\bar{r}+\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\bar{a}_{i}(\hat{\mu}^{i})^{2}}{\bar{\Xi}_{i}}d\varphi^{i}\right)\,,

(3.30)

\displaystyle k^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-\ell^{-2}\bar{r}^{2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}+\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{r}}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\bar{a}_{i}}{\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\varphi^{i}}\,.

(3.31)

The metric in the original form (
3.5
) is recovered from (
3.29
) using the coordinate transformation

d\tau=d\bar{t}-\frac{2\bar{m}\bar{r}\,d\bar{r}}{(1-\ell^{-2}\bar{r}^{2})(\bar{V}-2\bar{m}\bar{r})}\,,\quad d\varphi^{i}=d\phi^{i}-\frac{2\bar{m}\bar{r}\bar{a}_{i}\,d\bar{r}}{(\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2})(\bar{V}-2\bar{m}\bar{r})}\,.

(3.32)

Not unexpectedly, the coordinate transformation (
3.32
) is singular at zeros of both
1-\ell^{-2}\bar{r}^{2}
and
\bar{V}-2\bar{m}\bar{r}
: the Kerr-Schild coordinates provide an extension of the original form of
\bar{g}
across the zeros of
\bar{V}-2\bar{m}\bar{r}
, while the original form of the metric provides an extension of the Kerr-Schild form of the metric across the zeros of

1-\ell^{-2}\bar{r}^{2}
.

3.4
Asymptotics

The behaviour of the metric at large distances can be directly read from (
3.10
).
For large
\bar{r}
we find

\displaystyle\bar{g}=

\displaystyle-\bar{W}\ell^{-2}\bar{r}^{2}\big(1+O(\bar{r}^{-2})\big)d\bar{t}^{2}+\frac{\ell^{2}\big(1+O(\bar{r}^{-2})\big)d\bar{r}^{2}}{\bar{r}^{2}}+O(\bar{r}^{1-2N})(\bar{W}d\bar{t}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\bar{a}_{i}(\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2}d\phi^{i}}{\bar{\Xi}_{i}}\biggr)^{2}

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2}}{\bar{\Xi}_{i}}\bigl((\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2}(d\phi^{i})^{2}+(d\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2})-\bar{r}^{2}\frac{\Big(\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}d((\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2})\Big)^{2}}{1+\sum_{i=1}^{N}(\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2}}-\frac{\ell^{2}-\bar{r}^{2}}{4\bar{W}}(d\bar{W})^{2}\,.

(3.33)

Introducing a new coordinate

x:=1/\bar{r}\,,

we see that the tensor field
x^{2}\bar{g}
extends analytically across a conformal boundary at infinity

{\mycal I}:=\{x=0\}\,,

and is non-degenerate in a neighborhood of
{\mycal I}
.
As such, the metric
x^{2}\bar{g}
induces on
{\mycal I}
the tensor field

\displaystyle-\bar{W}\ell^{-2}d\bar{t}^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{(\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2}(d\phi^{i})^{2}}{\bar{\Xi}_{i}}\underbrace{+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{(d\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2}}{\bar{\Xi}_{i}}+\frac{(d\bar{W})^{2}}{4\bar{W}}-\frac{\Big(\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}d((\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2})\Big)^{2}}{1+\sum_{i=1}^{N}(\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2}}}\,.

(3.34)

It follows that
\bar{t}
is a time function for all
\bar{r}
large enough, but
the signature of the tensor field (
3.34
) is not apparent.
In particular, the signature of the underbraced
\bar{\mu}^{i}
sector, which we denote
\bar{g}_{ij}
, is not obviously Riemannian.
But taking the limit
\bar{r}\to\infty
in (
A.31
) below gives

\displaystyle\lim_{\bar{r}\to\infty}\bar{r}^{-2N}\det(\bar{g}_{ij})=

\displaystyle\lim_{\bar{r}\to\infty}\frac{1}{\bar{W}\,(\bar{\mu}^{N+1})^{2}}\left(1+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\bar{a}_{i}^{2}(\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2}}{\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2}}\right)\prod_{i=1}^{N}\frac{1+\bar{a}_{i}^{2}\bar{r}^{-2}}{\bar{\Xi}_{i}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\big(1+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\bar{a}_{i}^{2}(\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2}}{\ell^{2}\bar{\Xi}_{i}}\big)\big(1+\sum_{i=1}^{N}(\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2}\big)\prod_{i=1}^{N}\bar{\Xi}_{i}\right)^{-1}\,,

(3.35)

which has no zeros.
Since the tensor field (
3.34
) obviously has Lorentzian signature at
\{\mu^{i}=0\}
when using the coordinates (
3.11
), we conclude that (
3.34
) has Lorentzian signature everywhere.

While it is not immediately apparent, the metric (
3.34
) is conformal to a hyperbolic version of the Einstein cylinder.
This follows from the Kerr-Schild form of the metric, in which the metric
y^{-2}\bar{g}
extends smoothly across
\{z=0\}
, where
z:=1/y
, with the extension inducing on
\{z=0\}
the metric

\displaystyle-\ell^{-2}d\tau^{2}+g_{\mycal H}\,,

(3.36)

where
g_{\mycal H}
is the metric on hyperbolic space.

3.5
Extensions, Killing horizons

We will say that the metric is
fully rotating
if

\bar{m}\,\bar{a}_{1}\cdots\bar{a}_{N}\neq 0\,.

(3.37)

We wish to show that, in the fully-rotating case, the tensor field (
3.10
) can be extended through all the remaining potentially singular sets, namely zeros of
1-\ell^{-2}\bar{r}^{2}
in the Kerr–Schild form of the metric, and zeros of
\bar{V}-2\bar{m}\bar{r}

3.5.1
1-\ell^{-2}\bar{r}^{2}

In the fully-rotating case the tensor field (
3.10
) is manifestly regular near those zeros of
1-\ell^{-2}\bar{r}^{2}
which are distinct from zeros of
\bar{V}-2\bar{m}\bar{r}

We will show shortly that these zeros are distinct under (
3.37
).

Possible concerns about the signature of (
3.10
) at zeros of

are dispelled by Proposition
3.1
.
So, as already mentioned, the original form of the metric provides an extension of the Kerr–Schild form of the metric across
\{\bar{r}=\pm\ell\}
.

3.5.2
\bar{V}-2\bar{m}\bar{r}

Recall that

\displaystyle\bar{V}

\displaystyle=-(1-\ell^{-2}\bar{r}^{2})\prod_{i=1}^{N}(\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2})\,,

(3.38)

thus
\bar{V}-2\bar{m}\bar{r}
vanishes when
\bar{r}
is a root of the equation

\displaystyle(1-\ell^{-2}\bar{r}^{2})\prod_{i=1}^{N}(\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2})=-2\bar{m}{\bar{r}}\,.

(3.39)

When
\bar{a}_{i}\neq 0
, the case
\bar{m}\bar{r}=0
in (
3.39
) is only possible if
\bar{m}=0
.
In this case the rotation parameters are actually irrelevant in that they can be got rid of by a coordinate transformation:
Indeed, it follows from Section
3.3
that the metric
\bar{g}
is
then locally isometric to the Anti-de Sitter metric.
Changing
\bar{r}
to its negative if necessary, it is standard (cf., e.g.,
(
20
, Chapter 6)
) to construct an extension where

\bar{r}>0
, and in which the hypersurface
\{\bar{r}=\ell\}
is a bifurcate Killing horizon.

In the fully-rotating case (
3.37
), the roots of
\bar{V}-2\bar{m}\bar{r}
occur away from the set
\{\bar{r}=\pm\ell\}
.
It then follows that the Kerr–Schild form of the metric (
3.29
)-(
3.30
) provides an analytic extension across each of the roots of
\bar{V}-2\bar{m}\bar{r}
.
Further extensions are constructed in Section
4
.

We claim that,again in the fully-rotating case, the function
\bar{V}-2\bar{m}\bar{r}
has at least two zeros
\bar{r}_{\pm}
satisfying

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\bar{r}_{-}<-\ell<0<\bar{r}_{+}<\ell\,,&\hbox{$\bar{m}<0$;}\\
-\ell<\bar{r}_{-}<0<\ell<\bar{r}_{+}\,,&\hbox{$\bar{m}>0$.}\end{array}\right.

(3.40)

To see this, note that the zeros of
\bar{V}-2\bar{r}\bar{m}
are roots of the polynomial

\displaystyle F(\bar{r}):=(\ell^{2}-\bar{r}^{2})\prod_{i=1}^{N}(\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2})+2\ell^{2}\bar{m}\bar{r}\,.

(3.41)

Assuming (
3.37
) we have

F(0)>0\,,\quad F(\pm\ell)=\pm 2\ell^{3}\bar{m}\,,\quad\lim_{\bar{r}\to\pm\infty}F=-\infty\,.

(3.42)

Consider the possibility that
\bar{m}>0
.
Then
F(1)>0
, and since
F\to_{\bar{r}\to\infty}-\infty
there must be a zero, say
\bar{r}_{+}
, of
F
in
(1,\infty)
.
Similar arguments apply to the remaining cases in (
3.40
).

When
\bar{m}
vanishes but no rotation parameter does, the function
F
has exactly two zeros, at
r=\pm\ell
.
Continuity shows that it will have exactly two zeros in the fully-rotating case when
\bar{m}
is small enough.
In Figure

we give examples of parameters where

has three or four zeros.
In Appendix
B
we show that

can have at most four zeros.

Examples of the function

with three (top) or four (bottom) zeros. In all plots,
\ell=1
.

We have

g^{\bar{r}\bar{r}}=\frac{\bar{V}-2\bar{m}\bar{r}}{\bar{U}}\,,

which shows that each smooth connected subset of

{{\mathbf{H}}}:=\{\bar{V}=2\bar{m}\bar{r}\}

is null, with the Killing vectors
\partial_{t}
and
\partial_{\phi^{i}}
tangent to
{{\mathbf{H}}}
.
If
\bar{r}_{H}
is a zero of
\bar{V}-2\bar{m}\bar{r}
then the following linear combination of Killing vectors

K:=\partial_{\tau}+\sum_{i}\Omega_{i}\partial_{\varphi^{i}}\,,\quad\mbox{where}\ \Omega_{i}:=\frac{\bar{a}_{i}(1-\ell^{-2}\bar{r}_{H}^{2})}{\bar{r}_{H}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2}}

(3.43)

is null on
{{\mathbf{H}}}
.
We conclude that, in the fully-rotating case, each connected component of

{{\mathbf{H}}}\cap\{K\neq 0\}

is a Killing horizon.

3.6
Surface gravity

Since the vector field
K
is null on the horizon we have
K^{\mu}\nabla_{\mu}K_{\nu}=\kappa K_{\nu}
there, where
\kappa
can also be determined from
\kappa^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{\mu}K_{\nu}\nabla^{\mu}K^{\nu}
.
As is well known,
\kappa
is constant on each connected component of
{{\mathbf{H}}}
in vacuum, and is called
surface gravity
.
The calculation of
\kappa
is conveniently done in Kerr–Schild coordinates, as
\bar{g}
is not defined on the horizons in Boyer–Lindquist type coordinates.
A lengthy calculation gives

\pm\kappa=\bar{r}_{H}(\ell^{-2}\bar{r}_{H}^{2}-1)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{1}{\bar{r}_{H}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2}}+\frac{1}{2\bar{r}_{H}^{2}}\right)+\frac{1}{\bar{r}_{H}}\,;

(3.44)

a resolution of the sign-ambiguity requires a choice of time orientation.
Note that degenerate horizons, i.e. horizons with
\kappa=0
, can only occur in the region where
|\bar{r}|<\ell
, hence for
\bar{m}>0
the outermost horizon is always non-degenerate.

3.7
Signature

It follows from Section
3.4
that
\bar{g}
has Lorentzian signature at large distances.

Keeping in mind that at the axes of rotation, i.e.
\mu^{i}=0
, the tensor field
\bar{g}
is regular in suitable coordinates, the calculations of Appendix
A
show that:

Proposition
3.1
.

The tensor field
\bar{g}
of (
3.10
) is non-degenerate on every connected component of the set

\{\bar{V}\neq 2\bar{m}\bar{r}\}\,.

(3.45)

It follows immediately that
\bar{g}
has Lorentzian signature on those connected components of the set
\{\bar{V}\neq 2\bar{m}\bar{r}\}
on which
\bar{r}\to\pm\infty
.

Now, the analysis of Section
3.5.2
shows that the signature does not change across the zeros of
\bar{V}-2\bar{m}\bar{r}
, when
\bar{g}
is extended across these zeros using the Kerr–Schild form of the metric.
Further, it follows from (
3.27
) that
\bar{r}
is a smooth function on the Kerr–Schild extension.
Its level sets away from the zeros of
\bar{V}-2\bar{m}\bar{r}
are all diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}^{2N+1}
on the manifold underlying the original form (
3.10
) of the metric.
Furthermore, (
3.27
) shows that

1.

the sets
\{\bar{r}=\pm\ell\}
and
\{y=\pm\ell\}
coincide, with

2.

0\neq dy|_{y=\pm\ell}=\bar{W}d\bar{r}
.

Hence the level sets of
\bar{r}
are also diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}^{2N+1}
near the zeros of
\bar{V}-2\bar{m}\bar{r}
in the Kerr–Schild extension.

We conclude that the extension of the metric (
3.10
)
through each connected component of the set
\{\bar{V}-2\bar{m}\bar{r}=0\}
, as obtained by using the Kerr–Schild coordinates, provides a smooth Lorentzian metric defined on

{\mycal M}_{\mathrm{KS}}:=\{\bar{r}\in\mathbb{R}\}\times\mathbb{R}^{2N+1}\,.

(3.46)

The function
\bar{r}
is a globally defined smooth function on
{\mycal M}_{\mathrm{KS}}
,
while the coordinates
(\bar{t},x^{i},y^{i})\in\mathbb{R}^{2N+1}
, where
(x^{i},y^{i})
are as in (
3.11
), are smooth coordinates away from the zeros of
\bar{V}-2\bar{m}\bar{r}
, and have to be patched together using Kerr–Schild coordinates across the horizons.

3.8
Causality

3.8.1
Stable causality

Recall that a spacetime
({\mycal M},g)
is
stably causal
if there exists on
{\mycal M}
a function with timelike gradient; and a function is a
time function
if its gradient is timelike past pointing.

We have

g(\nabla r,\nabla r)=g^{\bar{r}\bar{r}}=\frac{\bar{V}-2\bar{m}\bar{r}}{\bar{U}}\,,

which shows that each connected component of the set
\{\bar{V}-2\bar{m}\bar{r}<0\}
is stably causal, with either
\bar{r}
or
-\bar{r}
being time functions there.

Since the metric reduces to a BK metric when the rotation parameters vanish, the obvious candidate for a time function on the set

\{\bar{V}-2\bar{m}\bar{r}>0\}
is the coordinate
\bar{t}
, or its negative.
Now, it follows from the block diagonal nature of the metric that
to find
g^{\bar{t}\,\bar{t}}
we need to invert the

x^{\alpha}=(\bar{t},\phi^{i})
-sector of the metric.
This is most conveniently done using the ADM decomposition of this sector of the metric:

\displaystyle\hat{g}:=\

\displaystyle\bar{W}(1-\ell^{-2}\bar{r}^{2})d\bar{t}^{2}+\frac{2\bar{m}\bar{r}}{\bar{U}}\biggl(\bar{W}d\bar{t}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\bar{a}_{i}(\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2}d\phi^{i}}{\bar{\Xi}_{i}}\biggr)^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2}}{\bar{\Xi}_{i}}(\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2}(d\phi^{i})^{2}

\displaystyle=:\

\displaystyle\pm N^{2}d\bar{t}^{2}+h_{ij}(d\phi^{i}+N^{i}d\bar{t})(d\phi^{j}+N^{j}d\bar{t})\,.

(3.47)

We have, from the Equations (
A.7
)-(
A.8
), (
A.13
), and (
A.17
) of Appendix

,

\displaystyle h:=h_{ij}d\phi^{i}d\phi^{j}=\

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2}}{\bar{\Xi}_{i}}(\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2}\delta_{ij}+\frac{2\bar{m}\bar{r}\bar{a}_{i}\bar{a}_{j}(\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2}(\bar{\mu}^{j})^{2}}{\bar{U}\bar{\Xi}_{i}\bar{\Xi}_{j}}\Big)d\phi^{i}d\phi^{j}\,,

(3.48)

\displaystyle N^{j}=\

\displaystyle{2\bar{m}\bar{r}}\bar{W}\left({\bar{U}}+{2\bar{m}\bar{r}}\,\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\bar{a}_{i}^{2}(\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2}}{(\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2})\bar{\Xi}_{i}}\right)^{-1}\frac{\bar{a}_{j}}{\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{j}^{2}}\,,

(3.49)

\displaystyle\pm N^{2}=\

\displaystyle-(\bar{V}-2\bar{m}\bar{r})\frac{\bar{W}}{\left(1+\frac{2\bar{m}\bar{r}}{\bar{U}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\bar{a}_{i}^{2}(\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2}}{(\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2})\bar{\Xi}_{i}}\right)\prod_{i=1}^{N}(\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2})}\,,

\displaystyle\bar{g}^{\bar{t}\,\bar{t}}

\displaystyle=\pm N^{-2}=-\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{N}(\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2})}{\bar{W}(\bar{V}-2\bar{m}\bar{r})}\Bigg(1+\frac{2\bar{m}\bar{r}}{\bar{U}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\bar{a}_{i}^{2}(\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2}}{\Xi_{i}(\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2})}\Bigg)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\bar{W}(\bar{V}-2\bar{m}\bar{r})}\Bigg(\prod_{i=1}^{N}(\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2})+\frac{2\bar{m}\bar{r}}{1+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\bar{a}_{i}^{2}(\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2}}{\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2}}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\bar{a}_{i}^{2}(\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2}}{\Xi_{i}(\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2})}\Bigg)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\bar{W}(\bar{V}-2\bar{m}\bar{r})\big(1+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\bar{a}_{i}^{2}(\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2}}{\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2}}\big)}\times

\displaystyle\quad\left(\prod_{i=1}^{N}(\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2})\big(1+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\bar{a}_{i}^{2}(\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2}}{\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2}}\big)-\frac{2\bar{m}\bar{r}\ell^{2}}{\bar{r}^{2}-\ell^{2}}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\bar{a}_{i}^{2}(\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2}}{\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2}}-\frac{\bar{a}_{i}^{2}(\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2}}{\ell^{2}\Xi_{i}}\right]\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\ell^{2}}{(\bar{r}^{2}-\ell^{2})\bar{W}(\bar{V}-2\bar{m}\bar{r})\big(1+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\bar{a}_{i}^{2}(\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2}}{\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2}}\big)}\left((\bar{V}-2\bar{m}\bar{r})\left(1+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\bar{a}_{i}^{2}(\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2}}{\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2}}\right)+2\bar{m}\bar{r}\bar{W}\right)\,.

(3.53)

Strictly speaking, the last two equations are only valid if

. However, it is immediate from (

Since

assuming

\displaystyle 0<\bar{V}-2\bar{m}\bar{r}=-(1

We have therefore, for a fully rotating configurations:

2.

On the intersection of the axis

. On the other hand, for
|\bar{r}|\geq\rho
we have

(*)\geq\bar{r}^{2N}-2|\bar{m}\bar{r}|=|\bar{r}|(|\bar{r}|^{2N-1}-2|\bar{m}|)\geq\rho(\rho^{2N-1}-2|\bar{m}|)\,,

which will be positive if
|\bar{m}|<\rho^{2N-1}/2
. Choosing
\rho
so that
a/(2\rho)=\rho^{2N-1}/2
, namely
\rho={a}^{1/(2N)}
, we find

(*)>0\ \mbox{for}\ |\bar{m}|<\frac{a^{\frac{2N-1}{2N}}}{2}\,.

(3.56)

We have therefore proved:

Proposition
3.2
.

Suppose that

2|\bar{m}|<\prod_{i=1}^{N}|\bar{a}_{i}|^{\frac{2N-1}{N}}\,.

Then each connected component of the set
\{\bar{V}\neq 2\bar{m}\bar{r}\}
is stably causal.

3.8.2
Closed or almost closed causal curves, time machine

A minimum requirement for a well behaved spacetime is non-existence of closed causal curves.
Obvious candidates for such curves are the orbits of the Killing vectors
\partial_{\phi^{i}}
.
In the region
\bar{g}^{\bar{t}\,\bar{t}}<0
the level sets of
\bar{t}
are spacelike, and so are therefore the integral curves of the
\partial_{\phi^{i}}
’s.
However, since

\bar{\mathbf{g}}(\partial_{\phi^{i}},\partial_{\phi^{i}})=\frac{2\bar{m}\bar{r}}{\bar{U}}\left(\frac{\bar{a}_{i}(\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2}}{\bar{\Xi}_{i}}\right)^{2}+\frac{(\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2})(\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2}}{\bar{\Xi}_{i}}

(3.57)

we see that at any point with
\bar{r}\neq 0
and for any collection of rotation parameters we can choose
\bar{m}
with
\bar{m}\bar{r}<0
so that one,
or more (possibly all), of the
\partial_{\phi^{i}}
’s is timelike or null.
The orbits of such
\partial_{\phi^{i}}
’s are closed causal curves.

More generally, consider the matrix of scalar products of the Killing vectors
\partial_{\phi^{i}}
,

h\equiv\big(h_{ij}:=\bar{g}(\partial_{\phi^{i}},\partial_{\phi^{j}})\big)\,.

(3.58)

As shown in Equation (
A.11
) of Appendix
A
, we have

\det h=\Bigg(1+\frac{2\bar{m}\bar{r}}{\bar{U}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\bar{a}_{i}^{2}(\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2}}{(\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2})\bar{\Xi}_{i}}\Bigg)\prod_{i=1}^{N}\frac{(\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2})(\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2}}{\bar{\Xi}_{i}}\,.

(3.59)

Using
(
A.10
) and (
A.18
) one finds

-\bar{W}(\bar{V}-2\bar{m}\bar{r})\prod_{i=1}^{N}\frac{(\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2}}{\bar{\Xi}_{i}}=\det\hat{g}=\pm N^{2}\det h=\frac{\det h}{\hat{g}(\hat{\nabla}\bar{t},\hat{\nabla}\bar{t})}=\frac{\det h}{\bar{g}(\bar{\nabla}\bar{t},\bar{\nabla}\bar{t})}\,.

(3.60)

So
\bar{\nabla}\bar{t}
is timelike at
p\in\{\bar{V}-2\bar{m}\bar{r}>0\}
if and only if
the matrix
h
defined in (
3.58
) is positive definite at
p
. Alternatively, one can compare (
3.59
) with (
3.50
) to reach the same conclusion.

Let
{\mycal N}\approx\mathbb{T}^{N}
denote any principal orbit of the action of the isometry group generated by the Killing vectors fields
\partial_{\phi^{i}}
, and consider the pullback, which we denote by
h_{\mycal N}
, of
h=h_{ij}d\phi^{i}d\phi^{j}
to
{\mycal N}
. When non-degenerate,
h_{\mycal N}
is the metric induced by
\bar{g}
on
{\mycal N}
.
Since
h_{\mycal N}
is Riemannian on the set
\{\bar{V}-2\bar{m}\bar{r}<0\}
, which is stably causal as
\bar{\nabla}\bar{r}
is timelike there, we have proved:

Proposition
3.3
.

Every connected component of the set

\{\bar{V}\neq 2\bar{m}\bar{r}\}\cap\{\det h_{\mycal N}>0\}\,,

where
h_{\mycal N}
denotes the tensor field induced on the orbits of the group generated by the
\partial_{\phi^{i}}
’s, is stably causal.

Suppose, next, that
h_{\mycal N}
is degenerate, then there exists a combination of the Killing vectors
\partial_{\phi^{i}}
, say
Y
, which is null.
Then the orbit of
Y
is a null curve, which has a non-empty
\omega
-limit set by compactness of
{\mycal N}
. Therefore the connected component of the set

\{\bar{V}\neq 2\bar{m}\bar{r}\}

which contains
{\mycal N}
cannot be strongly causal, hence it cannot be stably causal either.

The last possibility is that
h_{\mycal N}
has Lorentzian signature.
Then
({\mycal N},h_{\mycal N})
is a compact Lorentzian manifold, and therefore contains a closed timelike curve by a theorem of Geroch (cf., e.g., Proposition 3.7.1 in
[
20
]
).
Hence causality is violated at
{\mycal N}
. By an argument of Carter (see
(
20
, Section 4.6.4)
) there exists a causal curve through every point of
J^{+}({\mycal N})\cap J^{-}({\mycal N})
.
We expect that this last set coincides with the connected component of the set
\{\bar{V}\neq 2\bar{m}\bar{r}\}
containing
{\mycal N}
, but we have not attempted to establish this.

From what has been said and from (
3.51
) it follows that:

Proposition
3.4
.

1. All connected components of the set
\{\bar{V}-2\bar{m}\bar{r}<0\}
are stably causal, with
\bar{\nabla}\bar{r}
timelike there.

2. A connected component of the set
\{\bar{V}-2\bar{m}\bar{r}>0\}
is stably causal if and only it holds there that

\displaystyle 1+\frac{2\bar{m}\bar{r}}{\bar{U}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\bar{a}_{i}^{2}(\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2}}{(\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2})\bar{\Xi}_{i}}>0\,,

(3.61)

and then
\bar{\nabla}\bar{t}
is timelike there.

3. The inequality (
3.61
) is satisfied on
the sets
\{\bar{V}-2\bar{m}\bar{r}>0\}\cap\{\bar{m}\bar{r}>0\}
and

\{\bar{V}-2\bar{m}\bar{r}<0\}
.

We define the
time-machine set
as the set where closed causal curves lying entirely on an orbit of the connected component of the isometry group of
\bar{g}
occur.
We note that the pull-back, by the projection map considered in Section
4
below, of the Minkowskian time function, provides a continuous time function on that part of the space-time from which the time-machine set has been removed.
In particular, for those values of parameters for which the matrix
\bar{g}(\partial_{\phi^{i}},\partial_{\phi^{j}})
is positive definite everywhere, both the Kerr-Schild extension and the remaining extensions considered in Section
4
are stably causal.

Sections of the horizon

The question arises about the geometry of the cross-sections of the horizons. For this consider the intersections, which we denote by
{\mycal N}_{\tau,\bar{r}}
, of the level sets of the coordinates
\tau
and
\bar{r}
of the Kerr-Schild form (

Negative Ricci curvature

(3.63)

\displaystyle{\mathring{\gamma}}_{\mathrm{KS}}=

(3.67)

with

{{\sigma}}_{ij}dn^{i}dn^{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2}}{\bar{\Xi}_{i}}(dn^{i})^{2}-\frac{(1-\ell^{-2}\bar{r}^{2})}{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\bar{a}_{i}^{2}(n^{i})^{2}}{\bar{\Xi}_{i}}}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\bar{a}_{i}^{2}n^{i}dn^{i}}{\bar{\Xi}_{i}}\Big)^{2}+O(|\bar{\mu}|^{-2})dn^{i}dn^{j}\,.

(3.68)

In order to check that the limit as
|\mu|
tends to
\infty
of the tensor field
{{\sigma}}
is non-degenerate, we set

\theta^{i}=\sqrt{\frac{\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2}}{\bar{\Xi}_{i}}}dn^{i}\,,\qquad Z^{i}=\sqrt{\frac{\bar{\Xi}^{i}(1-\ell^{-2}\bar{r}^{2})}{(\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2})\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{\bar{a}_{j}^{2}(n^{j})^{2}}{\bar{\Xi}_{j}}}}\frac{\bar{a}_{i}^{2}n^{i}}{\bar{\Xi}_{i}}\,.

(3.69)

Given a vector field
X
, with
X^{i}:=\theta^{i}(X)
, we have

{{\sigma}}(X,X)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}(X^{i})^{2}-(\sum_{i=1}^{N}X^{i}Z^{i})^{2}\,.

(3.70)

This will be positive for all non-zero vectors
X^{i}
if

\sum_{i=1}^{N}(Z^{i})^{2}<1\,.

(3.71)

Equivalently,

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{(1-\ell^{-2}\bar{r}^{2})\bar{a}_{i}^{4}(n^{i})^{2}}{\bar{\Xi}_{i}(\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2})}<\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{\bar{a}_{j}^{2}(n^{j})^{2}}{\bar{\Xi}_{j}}\,,

(3.72)

which is the same as

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{(\ell^{2}-\bar{r}^{2})\bar{a}_{i}^{4}(n^{i})^{2}}{(\ell^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2})(\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2})}<\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{\ell^{2}\bar{a}_{j}^{2}(n^{j})^{2}}{\ell^{2}+\bar{a}_{j}^{2}}\,,

(3.73)

or as

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\big((\cancel{\ell^{2}}-\bar{r}^{2})\bar{a}_{i}^{2}-(\bar{r}^{2}+\cancel{\bar{a}_{i}^{2}})\ell^{2}\big)\bar{a}_{i}^{2}(n^{i})^{2}}{(\ell^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2})(\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2})}<0\,,

(3.74)

which is obviously satisfied.

|\bar{\mu}|^{-2}\bar{U}
has a smooth limit as
|\bar{\mu}|
tends to infinity, with the limit bounded away from zero. Replacing
|\bar{\mu}|
by

1/x
we find

\displaystyle{\mathring{\gamma}}_{\mathrm{KS}}=

\displaystyle\ x^{-2}\Bigg[\big(\ell^{2}+O(x^{2})\big)dx^{2}+O(x)dn^{i}dx

\displaystyle\ +{{\sigma}}_{ij}dn^{i}dn^{j}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2}}{\bar{\Xi}_{i}}\big((n^{i})^{2}d\varphi^{i}\big)^{2}+\frac{2\bar{m}\bar{r}}{x^{2}\bar{U}}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\bar{a}_{i}(n^{i})^{2}}{\bar{\Xi}_{i}}d\varphi^{i}\Big)^{2}\Bigg]\,,

(3.75)

which shows that
{\mathring{\gamma}}_{\mathrm{KS}}

It is a standard fact that the Riemann tensor of such metrics approaches a negatively curved space form as
|\bar{\mu}|
tends to infinity; equivalently,
x
tends to zero. This implies in particular that, for any given
\bar{m}
and
\bar{r}
, the metric
{\mathring{\gamma}}_{\mathrm{KS}}
is negatively Ricci curved for sufficiently large
|\bar{\mu}|
and for all rotation parameters, and that it is negatively Ricci curved everywhere for sufficiently small rotation parameters.

3.9.2
Topology

Suppose that there is a quotient map which a) preserves the Killing vector
\partial_{\tau}
, and which b) compactifies the horizon, and under which c) the whole spacetime metric passes to the quotient.
Then in particular the one-form

\alpha:=\bar{g}(\partial_{\tau},\cdot)\equiv\bar{g}(\partial_{\tau},\partial_{x^{\mu}})dx^{\mu}=\bar{g}(\partial_{\tau},\partial_{\varphi^{i}})d\varphi^{i}=\bar{W}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\bar{a}_{i}(\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2}}{\bar{\Xi}_{i}}d\varphi^{i}\Big)

(3.76)

will also pass to the quotient.
But we have:

Proposition
3.5
.

Let
\bar{r}_{H}
be a solution of
\bar{V}(\bar{r}_{H})=2\bar{m}\bar{r}_{H}
.
If
({\mycal N}_{\tau,\bar{r}_{H}},{\mathring{\gamma}}_{\mathrm{KS}},\alpha)
can be compactified with a negative-definite Ricci tensor of
{\mathring{\gamma}}
, then all rotation parameters vanish.

Proof.

Set

X:={\mathring{\gamma}}^{ij}_{\mathrm{KS}}\alpha_{j}\partial_{\varphi^{i}}\,.

(3.77)

By an abuse of notation we will use the same symbols as in (
3.47
)-(
3.50
) for the corresponding formulae in Kerr-Schild coordinates.
Then, as
h^{ij}
is the
\varphi^{i}
-block in
{\mathring{\gamma}}^{ij}_{\mathrm{KS}}
, we find

X=h^{ij}\alpha_{j}\partial_{\varphi^{i}}=N^{i}\partial_{\varphi^{i}}=\underbrace{2\bar{m}\bar{r}\bar{W}\left({\bar{U}}+{2\bar{m}\bar{r}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\bar{a}_{i}^{2}(\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2}}{(\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2})\bar{\Xi}_{i}}\right)^{-1}}_{(\star)}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\bar{a}_{i}}{\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2}}\partial_{\varphi^{i}}\,.

(3.78)

The same calculation as in (
3.51
)-(
3.53
) gives

\displaystyle(*)^{-1}|_{\bar{r}=\bar{r}_{H}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\ell^{2}}{(\bar{r}^{2}-\ell^{2})\bar{W}2\bar{m}\bar{r}}\left((V-2\bar{m}\bar{r})\left[1+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\bar{a}_{i}^{2}}{\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2}}\right]+2\bar{m}\bar{r}\bar{W}\right)\Bigg|_{\bar{r}=\bar{r}_{H}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\ell^{2}}{\bar{r}_{H}^{2}-\ell^{2}}\,.

(3.79)

Thus
(*)|_{\bar{r}=\bar{r}_{H}}
is constant on each horizon.

Since
\bar{\alpha}
passes to the quotient, the vector field
X
is well defined on the quotient, tangent to
{\mycal N}_{\tau,\bar{r}_{H}}
, and is a linear combination of
\partial_{\phi^{i}}
’s with constant coefficients there, hence a Killing vector of
({\mycal N}_{\tau,\bar{r}_{H}},{\mathring{\gamma}}_{\mathrm{KS}})
.
But compact Ricci-negatively-curved manifolds have only trivial Killing vectors, thus
X=0
; equivalently, all the rotation parameters vanish.

\Box

It follows that the cross-sections of the horizon cannot be compactified in the slowly-fully-rotating case, since on a compact manifold and for small rotation parameters the tensor field
{\mathring{\gamma}}_{\mathrm{KS}}
would be Ricci-negatively-curved.

Our results are complemented by the statement in the second paragraph of page three in
[
16
]
(correcting the original work
[
15
]
), that both the four-dimensional and higher-dimensional solutions with
exactly one
non-zero rotation parameter, whether small or large, cannot be compactified without creating a discontinuity in the metric.

Compare
[
21
]
for an apparently similar behaviour.

3.10
Black hole regions

We consider the manifold

{\mycal M}_{\mathrm{KS}}:=\{\bar{r}\in\mathbb{R}\}\times\mathbb{R}^{2N+1}\,.

(3.80)

as defined at the end of Section
3.7
, with a globally defined function
\bar{r}
ranging over
\mathbb{R}
.
By an abuse of notation we will continue to denote by
\bar{g}
the metric on
{\mycal M}_{\mathrm{KS}}
.
For definiteness we assume a fully-rotating configuration.

We claim that
({\mycal M}_{\mathrm{KS}},\bar{g})
contains a black hole region or a white hole region, depending upon the choice of time orientation.
To see this, recall that the gradient of
\bar{r}
is a time function on
the set
\{\bar{V}-2\bar{m}\bar{r}<0\}\cap\{\bar{r}<\bar{r}_{H}\}
.
Let us choose the time orientation so that
-\bar{r}
is a time function on
\{\bar{V}-2\bar{m}\bar{r}<0\}
near
\bar{r}_{H}
, thus
\bar{r}
is strictly decreasing along every causal curve in this last region. Hence no causal curve that intersects

\mycal B:=\{\bar{r}<\bar{r}_{H}\}

can leave this region across
\{\bar{r}=\bar{r}_{H}\}
.
If
{\mycal I}
denotes the conformal completion at infinity constructed in Section
3.4
, we conclude that

J^{+}(\mycal B)\cap J^{-}({\mycal I})\subset J^{+}(\mycal B)\cap J^{-}(\{\bar{r}>\bar{r}_{H}\})=\emptyset\,,

which is the definition of a black hole region.

4
Projection diagrams

Projection diagrams
[
20
,
22
]
provide a generalisation of the Carter-Penrose diagrams to spacetimes which are not necessarily two-dimensional or spherically symmetric.
They provide a convenient way to visualise the global causal structure of extensions.
The idea is to construct a projection from a spacetime
({\mycal M},\bar{g})
to a subset, say
{\mycal U}
, of two-dimensional Minkowski spacetime
\mathbb{R}^{1,1}\equiv(\mathbb{R}^{2},\eta)
so that

1.

\bar{g}
-causal curves project to
\eta
-causal curves, and

2.

every
\eta
-timelike curve in
{\mycal U}
is the projection of some
\bar{g}
-timelike curve in
({\mycal M},\bar{g})
.

In order to construct such diagrams for the extensions of the metric (
3.10
), the simplest approach is to project-out the coordinates
\bar{\mu}^{i}
and
\phi^{i}
, so that we are left with two local coordinates
\bar{t}
and
\bar{r}
.
Now, it follows from the calculations in Appendix
A
below that the
\bar{\mu}^{i}
-sector of the metric is positive definite.
So we start by projecting-out the
\bar{\mu}^{i}
-coordinates and consider the tensor field

\displaystyle\breve{g}:=\

\displaystyle\bar{W}(1-\ell^{-2}\bar{r}^{2})d\bar{t}^{2}+\frac{\bar{U}d\bar{r}^{2}}{\bar{V}-2\bar{m}\bar{r}}+\frac{2\bar{m}\bar{r}}{\bar{U}}\biggl(\bar{W}d\bar{t}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\bar{a}_{i}(\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2}d\phi^{i}}{\bar{\Xi}_{i}}\biggr)^{2}

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2}}{\bar{\Xi}_{i}}(\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2}(d\phi^{i})^{2}\,,

(4.1)

which has signature
(1,N+1)
and satisfies

\forall\ X\qquad\bar{g}(X,X)\geq\breve{g}(X,X)\,,

(4.2)

with equality if and only if
X
has vanishing
\bar{\mu}^{i}
-components. As the neglected part of
\bar{g}
is positive definite, all
\bar{g}
-causal curves
\gamma
are
\breve{g}
-causal:

\bar{g}(\dot{\gamma},\dot{\gamma})\leq 0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\breve{g}(\dot{\gamma},\dot{\gamma})\leq\bar{g}(\dot{\gamma},\dot{\gamma})\leq 0\,.

Moreover, if the coordinates
\bar{\mu}^{i}
are constant along a curve
\gamma
, then
\gamma
will be causal for
\breve{g}
if and only if it is causal for
\bar{g}
.

Recall that
\hat{g}
denotes the tensor field induced by
\breve{g}
on the level sets of
\bar{r}
.
Its ADM-decomposition with respect to the level sets of
\bar{t}
can be found in (
3.47
)-(
3.50
).
Let
\breve{\gamma}
denote the symmetric tensor field

\breve{\gamma}:=\pm N^{2}d\bar{t}^{2}+\frac{\bar{U}d\bar{r}^{2}}{\bar{V}-2\bar{m}\bar{r}}\,.

(4.3)

with
\pm N^{2}
given by (
3.50
),
thus
\breve{\gamma}
is obtained by ignoring the
h
-part of
\hat{g}
.
It follows from (
4.2
) and (
3.47
) that,
in the region where
h
is positive definite
we have,
for any vector
X
,

\breve{\gamma}(X,X)=\pm N^{2}(X^{\bar{t}})^{2}+\frac{\bar{U}}{\bar{V}-2\bar{m}\bar{r}}(X^{\bar{r}})^{2}\leq\breve{g}(X,X)\leq\bar{g}(X,X)\,.

(4.4)

As before, this implies that
\bar{g}
-causal curves project to
\breve{\gamma}
-causal ones. Furthermore, a
\breve{\gamma}
-causal curve
s\mapsto\big(\bar{t}(s),\bar{r}(s)\big)
can be lifted to a
\bar{g}
-causal curve
\gamma(s)
in spacetime by choosing any constants
\bar{\mu}^{i}
and setting

\gamma(s)=\big(\bar{t}(s),\bar{r}(s),\bar{\mu}^{i},\phi^{i}(s)\big)\,,\quad\mbox{where}\quad\frac{d\phi^{i}}{ds}=-N^{i}\frac{d\bar{t}}{ds}\,.

(4.5)

In order to avoid analysing what happens with lifts passing through
\bar{\mu}^{i}=0
,
where the projection map has the standard singularity associated with axes of rotation,
it is simplest to choose the lift so that all the
\bar{\mu}^{i}
’s are different from zero, which we do.

The next step is to simplify
\breve{\gamma}
using a conformal transformation. For this we decompose
\bar{U}
as

\bar{U}=\underbrace{\bigg(1+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{{(\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2}}\bar{a}_{i}^{2}}{\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2}}\bigg)}_{\breve{U}}\prod_{i=1}^{N}(\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2})=:\breve{U}\bar{F}^{2}

(4.6)

and write

\breve{\gamma}=\breve{U}\Big(\pm\breve{U}^{-1}N^{2}d\bar{t}^{2}+\frac{\bar{F}^{2}}{\bar{V}-2\bar{m}\bar{r}}d\bar{r}^{2}\Big)=:\breve{U}\mathring{\gamma}\,,

(4.7)

where the conformal factor has been chosen so that
\breve{U}^{-1}\breve{\gamma}_{\bar{r}\bar{r}}
depends only upon
\bar{r}
.

Note that causality with respect to
\breve{\gamma}
is the same as that for
\mathring{\gamma}
.

In order to extract out of
\mathring{\gamma}\equiv\breve{U}^{-1}\breve{\gamma}
a two-dimensional Lorentzian metric which depends only upon
\bar{r}
,
we define

\bar{H}_{-}(\bar{r}):=\inf_{\bar{\mu}^{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\breve{U}^{-1}N^{2}}{|\bar{V}-2\bar{m}\bar{r}|}\ \mbox{and}\ \bar{H}_{+}(\bar{r}):=\sup_{\bar{\mu}^{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}\ \frac{\breve{U}^{-1}N^{2}}{|\bar{V}-2\bar{m}\bar{r}|}\,.

(4.8)

We show in Appendix
C
that the functions
\bar{H}_{\pm}
are locally Lipschitz, with
\bar{H}_{-}>0
.

4.1
\bar{V}-2\bar{m}\bar{r}<0

On this region the coordinate
\bar{r}
has timelike gradient, which implies that
the matrix
h
of (
3.58
) is positive definite.
Recall that

\displaystyle\pm N^{2}=\

\displaystyle-\frac{\bar{W}(\bar{V}-2\bar{m}\bar{r})}{\left(1+\frac{2\bar{m}\bar{r}}{\bar{U}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\bar{a}_{i}^{2}(\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2}}{(\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2})\bar{\Xi}_{i}}\right)\prod_{i=1}^{N}(\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2})}\,,

(4.9)

and since
\bar{W}\geq 1
we need to choose the positive sign in the left-hand side of (
4.9
).
Then

\displaystyle\mathring{\gamma}(X,X)=\

\displaystyle\breve{U}^{-1}N^{2}(X^{\bar{t}})^{2}+\frac{\bar{F}^{2}}{\bar{V}-2\bar{m}\bar{r}}(X^{\bar{r}})^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\

\displaystyle|\bar{V}-2\bar{m}\bar{r}|\bar{H}_{-}(X^{\bar{t}})^{2}-\frac{\bar{F}^{2}}{|\bar{V}-2\bar{m}\bar{r}|}(X^{\bar{r}})^{2}

\displaystyle=:\

\displaystyle\gamma_{-}(X,X)\,,

(4.10)

with equality attained at points where
\bar{H}_{-}
is attained.
Note that a vector which is
\mathring{\gamma}
-causal will also be
\gamma_{-}
-causal.

From what has been said the metric
\gamma_{-}
has the desired causality properties with respect to the metric
\bar{g}
, and provides the desired
projection metric
with
\bar{t}
ranging over
\mathbb{R}
and
\bar{r}
ranging over a connected component of the set

\{\bar{V}-2\bar{m}\bar{r}<0\}
.

4.2
\bar{V}-2\bar{m}\bar{r}>0

On this region the coordinate
\bar{r}
has spacelike gradient, which implies that the tensor field
\gamma
of (
3.47
) has Lorentzian signature.
On the subset where the matrix
h
of (
3.58
) is positive definite we have
\pm N^{2}<0
, hence we need to choose the negative sign in the left-hand side of (
4.9
). Then

\displaystyle\mathring{\gamma}(X,X)=\

\displaystyle-\breve{U}^{-1}N^{2}(X^{\bar{t}})^{2}+\frac{\bar{F}^{2}}{\bar{V}-2\bar{m}\bar{r}}(X^{\bar{r}})^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\

\displaystyle-(\bar{V}-2\bar{m}\bar{r})\bar{H}_{+}(X^{\bar{t}})^{2}+\frac{\bar{F}^{2}}{\bar{V}-2\bar{m}\bar{r}}(X^{\bar{r}})^{2}

\displaystyle=:\

\displaystyle\gamma_{+}(X,X)\,,

(4.11)

with equality attained at points where
\bar{H}_{+}
is attained.
As before we conclude that the metric
\gamma_{+}
has the desired causality properties with respect to the metric
\bar{g}
, and provides the desired projection metric on each connected component of the set

\{\bar{V}-2\bar{m}\bar{r}>0\}\cap\{\mbox{the matrix $h(\partial_{\phi^{i}},\partial_{\phi^{j}})$ is positive definite}\}\,.

(4.12)

We emphasise that no two-dimensional metric can exist with the properties listed at the beginning of this section in the region where
h
is not positive definite, as there are closed timelike curves throughout the interior of that region, as well as closed causal curves on its boundary.

5
Odd dimensions? Positive
\Lambda
?

One might seek to generalise our analysis to odd spacetime dimensions.
It turns out that the simplest generalisation of the scheme (
3.3
) to odd dimensions, i.e.

\displaystyle\mu_{j}

\displaystyle=i\bar{\mu}_{j}\,,\quad 1\leq j<N-1\,,\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mu_{N}=\bar{\mu}_{N}

\displaystyle t

\displaystyle=i\bar{t}\,,\quad r=-i\bar{r}\,,\quad\,m=i^{-(d-1)}\bar{m}\,,\quad a_{i}=-i\bar{a}_{i}\,,\quad 1\leq i<N\,,\qquad

(5.1)

does not work.
The reason for this is, that in odd dimensions the number of rotation parameters
\bar{a}_{i}
and angular direction
\phi^{i}
is equal to the number of unconstrained
\bar{\mu}^{i}
variables.
The fact that the last
\bar{\mu}_{N}
is not scaled in the same way as the rest then causes problems with the signature of the metric.

We have checked that rescaling all of the
\bar{\mu}^{i}
’s in the same way and adjusting the constraint does not yield a sensible new metric either.
This appears to be the reason why the previous literature
[
15
,
16
]
has not been generalised to all dimensions.

We also remark that our attempts to obtain toroidal cross-sections of the horizon by scaling and passing to the limit did not produce sensible Lorentzian metrics either.

Last but not least, the metrics obtained by the methods above with a positive cosmological constant are plagued by singularities which we were not been able to resolve in a satisfactory manner, and therefore we do not report on them here.

Appendix A
Determinant

As a step towards determining the signature of
\bar{g}
we calculate its determinant.
For this we invoke the
matrix determinant lemma
.
That is, given an invertible
m\times m
square matrix, two
m
-dimensional vectors
u,v
and some real number
\alpha
, we have the following expression for the determinant of the matrix
M=A+\alpha uv^{T}
,

\det(A+uv^{T})=(1+\alpha v^{T}\cdot A^{-1}\cdot u)\det(A)\,.

(A.1)

We will also make use of the
Sherman–Morrison formula
, which gives an expression for the inverse of
M
.
That is,

(A+uv^{T})^{-1}=A^{-1}-\alpha\frac{(A^{-1}\cdot u)(v^{T}\cdot A^{-1})}{(1+\alpha v^{T}\cdot A^{-1}\cdot u)}\,,

(A.2)

which will be useful for calculating the inverse metric.

Since our metric (
3.5
) is block diagonal in three sectors (the
\bar{r}
,
x^{\alpha}=(\bar{t},{\phi}_{i})
and
x^{i}=\bar{\mu}^{i}
sectors) we can apply these to each in order to calculate the determinant

\det(\bar{g}_{\mu\nu})=\bar{g}_{\bar{r}\bar{r}}\det(\hat{g}_{\alpha\beta})\det(\tilde{g}_{ij})\,.

(A.3)

Since

\bar{g}_{\bar{r}\bar{r}}=\frac{\bar{U}}{\bar{V}-2\bar{m}\bar{r}}=\frac{1}{\bar{V}-2\bar{m}\bar{r}}\bigg(1+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{{(\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2}}\bar{a}_{i}^{2}}{\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2}}\bigg)\prod_{i=1}^{N}(\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2})\,,

we see that

the first multiplicative factor in (
A.3
) has constant sign

wherever
\bar{V}-2\bar{m}\bar{r}
has constant sign.

(A.4)

Next, let us consider the tensor field
\hat{g}_{\alpha\beta}
which describes the
(\bar{t},\phi^{i})
-sector of
\bar{g}
.
It can be written in the form (
A.1
) where the vectors
u
and
v
are collinear, while the matrix
A
is given by the remaining, diagonal,
(\bar{t},\phi^{i})
metric components, so the calculation of
A^{-1}
is immediate.
We find, for
\bar{r}^{2}\neq\ell^{2}
,

\displaystyle\det(\hat{g}_{\alpha\beta})

\displaystyle=\left(1+\frac{2\bar{m}\bar{r}}{\bar{U}}\left[\frac{\bar{W}}{1-\ell^{-2}\bar{r}^{2}}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{(\mu^{i})^{2}}{\bar{\Xi}_{i}}\frac{\bar{a}_{i}^{2}}{\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2}}\right]\right)\bar{W}(1-\ell^{-2}\bar{r}^{2})\prod_{i=1}^{N}\frac{(\mu^{i})^{2}}{\bar{\Xi}_{i}}(\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2})

\displaystyle=\left(1+\frac{2\bar{m}\bar{r}}{(1-\ell^{-2}\bar{r}^{2})\prod_{i=1}^{N}(\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2})}\right)\bar{W}(1-\ell^{-2}\bar{r}^{2})\prod_{i=1}^{N}\frac{(\mu^{i})^{2}}{\bar{\Xi}_{i}}(\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2})

\displaystyle=-\bar{W}\left(\bar{V}-2\bar{m}\bar{r}\right)\prod_{i=1}^{N}\frac{(\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2}}{\bar{\Xi}_{i}}\,.

(A.5)

While the final result is insensitive to zeros of
1-\ell^{-2}\bar{r}^{2}
, this calculation does not apply when
\bar{r}=\pm\ell
.
A calculation that avoids this problem proceeds by performing
first the ADM-decomposition

\displaystyle\hat{g}:=\

\displaystyle\bar{W}(1-\ell^{-2}\bar{r}^{2})d\bar{t}^{2}+\frac{2\bar{m}\bar{r}}{\bar{U}}\biggl(\bar{W}d\bar{t}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\bar{a}_{i}(\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2}d\phi^{i}}{\bar{\Xi}_{i}}\biggr)^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2}}{\bar{\Xi}_{i}}(\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2}(d\phi^{i})^{2}

\displaystyle=:\

\displaystyle\pm N^{2}d\bar{t}^{2}+h_{ij}(d\phi^{i}+N^{i}d\bar{t})(d\phi^{j}+N^{j}d\bar{t})\,,

(A.6)

with

\displaystyle h\equiv h_{ij}d\phi^{i}d\phi^{j}=\

\displaystyle\underbrace{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2}}{\bar{\Xi}_{i}}(\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2}(d\phi^{i})^{2}}_{A}+\frac{2\bar{m}\bar{r}}{\bar{U}}\underbrace{\biggl(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\bar{a}_{i}(\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2}d\phi^{i}}{\bar{\Xi}_{i}}\biggr)^{2}}_{uu^{T}}\,,

(A.7)

\displaystyle N^{j}=\

\displaystyle h^{ij}\frac{2\bar{m}\bar{r}\bar{W}\bar{a}_{i}(\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2}}{\bar{U}\bar{\Xi}_{i}}\,,

(A.8)

\displaystyle\pm N^{2}=\

\displaystyle\bar{W}(1-\ell^{-2}\bar{r}^{2})+\frac{2\bar{m}\bar{r}}{\bar{U}}\bar{W}^{2}-h_{ij}N^{i}N^{j}\,.

(A.9)

Next, there is the standard decomposition of the determinant of
\hat{g}
in terms of
\pm N^{2}
and
h
;

\det\hat{g}=\pm N^{2}\det h\,.

(A.10)

So we need to calculate
N^{2}
and
\det h
.
The latter is straightforward since
h
is in a form where we can apply (
A.1
) and (
A.2
), and in particular,
A
is diagonal. Therefore

\det h=\Bigg(1+\frac{2\bar{m}\bar{r}}{\bar{U}}\underbrace{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\bar{a}_{i}^{2}(\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2}}{(\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2})\bar{\Xi}_{i}}}_{u^{T}A^{-1}u}\Bigg)\prod_{i=1}^{N}\frac{(\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2})(\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2}}{\bar{\Xi}_{i}}\,.

(A.11)

We leave the term in brackets as is for now because, using hindsight, it will cancel out of the final expression.

Now to calculate
\pm N^{2}
we need to know
N^{i}
and hence we need also
h^{ij}
.
Assuming from now on that we are away from zeros of

\left(1+\frac{2\bar{m}\bar{r}}{\bar{U}}\,u^{T}A^{-1}u\right)
,
using (
A.2
) we obtain

\displaystyle h^{ij}=\frac{\bar{\Xi}_{i}}{(\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2})(\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2}}\delta^{ij}-\frac{2\bar{m}\bar{r}}{\bar{U}\left(1+\frac{2\bar{m}\bar{r}}{\bar{U}}\,u^{T}A^{-1}u\right)}\frac{\bar{a}_{i}\bar{a}_{j}}{(\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2})(\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{j}^{2})}\,.

(A.12)

This gives,

\displaystyle N^{i}

\displaystyle=\bar{W}\frac{2\bar{m}\bar{r}}{\bar{U}}\frac{\bar{a}_{i}}{\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2}}\Bigg(1-\frac{2\bar{m}\bar{r}}{\bar{U}}\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{a_{j}^{2}(\bar{\mu}^{j})^{2}}{\bar{\Xi}_{j}(\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{j}^{2})}}{1+\frac{2\bar{m}\bar{r}}{\bar{U}}u^{T}A^{-1}u}\Bigg)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\bar{W}{2\bar{m}\bar{r}}}{{\bar{U}}+{2\bar{m}\bar{r}}\,u^{T}A^{-1}u}\right)\frac{\bar{a}_{i}}{\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2}}\,,

(A.13)

where again
u^{T}A^{-1}u=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\bar{a}_{i}^{2}(\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2}}{(\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2})\bar{\Xi}_{i}}
.
Therefore the norm is,

\displaystyle N_{i}h^{ij}N_{j}

\displaystyle=\left(\bar{W}\frac{2\bar{m}\bar{r}}{\bar{U}}\right)^{2}\,\frac{u^{T}A^{-1}u}{1+\frac{2\bar{m}\bar{r}}{\bar{U}}\,u^{T}A^{-1}u}\,.

(A.14)

Now we can obtain
\pm N^{2}
from (
A.9
);

\displaystyle\pm N^{2}

\displaystyle=\bar{W}(1-\ell^{-2}\bar{r}^{2})+\frac{2\bar{m}\bar{r}}{\bar{U}}\bar{W}^{2}\left(1-\frac{2\bar{m}\bar{r}}{\bar{U}}\frac{u^{T}A^{-1}u}{1+\frac{2\bar{m}\bar{r}}{\bar{U}}\,u^{T}A^{-1}u}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\bar{W}}{1+\frac{2\bar{m}\bar{r}}{\bar{U}}\,u^{T}A^{-1}u}\left((1-\ell^{-2}\bar{r}^{2})\left(1+\frac{2\bar{m}\bar{r}}{\bar{U}}\,u^{T}A^{-1}u\right)+\bar{W}\frac{2\bar{m}\bar{r}}{\bar{U}}\right)\,.

(A.15)

This can be further simplified by noting

(1-\ell^{-2}\bar{r}^{2})u^{T}A^{-1}u+\bar{W}=1+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\frac{(1-\ell^{-2}\bar{r}^{2})}{\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2}}+\frac{1}{\ell^{2}}\right)\frac{\bar{a}_{i}^{2}(\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2}}{\bar{\Xi}_{i}}=1+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\bar{a}_{i}^{2}(\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2}}{\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2}}\,.

(A.16)

Hence

\displaystyle\pm N^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\bar{W}}{1+\frac{2\bar{m}\bar{r}}{\bar{U}}\,u^{T}A^{-1}u}\left((1-\ell^{-2}\bar{r}^{2})+\frac{2\bar{m}\bar{r}}{\bar{U}}\left[1+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\bar{a}_{i}^{2}(\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2}}{\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2}}\right]\right)

\displaystyle=-(\bar{V}-2\bar{m}\bar{r})\frac{\bar{W}}{\left(1+\frac{2\bar{m}\bar{r}}{\bar{U}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\bar{a}_{i}^{2}(\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2}}{(\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2})\bar{\Xi}_{i}}\right)\prod_{i=1}^{N}(\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2})}\,.

(A.17)

Putting everything together we obtain the equivalent of
(
6
, Equation (37))
,

\det\hat{g}=-\bar{W}(\bar{V}-2\bar{m}\bar{r})\prod_{i=1}^{N}\frac{(\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2}}{\bar{\Xi}_{i}}\,.

(A.18)

Note that we have recovered (
A.5
) without dividing by
1-\ell^{-2}\bar{r}^{2}
.
However, in this alternative calculation we have instead a division-with-cancellation by

\left(1+\frac{2\bar{m}\bar{r}}{\bar{U}}\,u^{T}A^{-1}u\right)
, which vanishes at the boundary of the causality-violating regions.
Since the previous calculation did not necessitate such a division, and since the zero-sets do not overlap in the fully-rotating case by (
3.39
),
we conclude that (
A.18
) holds without the restrictions mentioned.
Hence:

both the first and second multiplicative factors in (
A.3
) have constant signs

wherever
\bar{V}-2\bar{m}\bar{r}
has constant sign.

(A.19)

We should keep in mind that the metric
\bar{g}
is defined as the metric induced by (
3.5
) on the constraint set (
3.2
).
Nevertheless, for the
\bar{\mu}^{i}
-sector, it appears to be convenient to first treat the variable
\bar{\mu}^{N+1}
as independent and to impose the constraint later. That is to say, we calculate the determinant of the
\bar{\mu}^{i}
-sector as follows:

1.

We write
x^{I}=(\bar{\mu}^{i},\bar{\mu}_{N+1})
,
1\leq i\leq N
, and ignore that these variables are constrained.
We calculate
\det(\tilde{g}_{IJ})
.

2.

Replace the coordinate
\bar{\mu}_{N+1}
by a new coordinate
\Phi
defined as

\Phi:=\bar{\mu}^{2}_{N+1}-\sum_{i=1}^{N}(\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2}-1\,,

(A.20)

and rewrite the metric in these new coordinates
x^{\bar{I}}:=(x^{i},\Phi):=(\bar{\mu}^{i},\Phi)
, where
1\leq i\leq N
. The determinant we are interested in is the determinant of the induced metric, say
\tilde{\gamma}_{ij}
, on the hypersurface
\Phi=0
.

3.

In these new coordinates we have the following relation

\det(\tilde{g}_{IJ})=4\mu_{N+1}^{2}\det(\tilde{g}_{\bar{I}\bar{J}})\,,

(A.21)

which follows from the Jacobian of this coordinate transformation.

4.

We do an ADM-like decomposition of the metric
\tilde{g}_{\bar{I}\bar{J}}
.

\tilde{g}_{\bar{I}\bar{J}}dx^{\bar{I}}dx^{\bar{J}}={\cal N}^{2}d\Phi^{2}+\tilde{\gamma}_{ij}(dx^{i}+{\cal N}^{i}d\Phi)(dx^{j}+{\cal N}^{j}d\Phi)\,.

(A.22)

In this notation

\det(\tilde{g}_{\bar{I}\bar{J}})={\cal N}^{2}\det(\tilde{\gamma}_{ij})\,.

(A.23)

Note that we also need to make sure that a zero or infinity of
\cal N
is
not
compensated by an infinity or zero of
\det(\tilde{\gamma}_{ij})
; this will be addressed in due course.

5.

Finally we calculate
\cal N
from the expression
{\cal N}^{-2}=(\partial_{\bar{I}}\Phi)\tilde{g}^{\bar{I}\bar{J}}(\partial_{\bar{J}}\Phi)
and use this to get the desired determinant

\det(\tilde{\gamma}_{ij})=\frac{1}{4\mu_{N+1}^{2}{\cal N}^{2}}\det(\tilde{g}_{IJ})\Big|_{\Phi=0}\,.

(A.24)

We begin with step 1.
For
\bar{r}^{2}\neq\ell^{2}

the
\bar{\mu}^{i}
-sector of the metric, i.e.

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2}}{\bar{\Xi}_{i}}(d\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2}-\bar{r}^{2}(d\bar{\mu}^{N+1})^{2}-\frac{\ell^{-2}}{\bar{W}(1-\ell^{-2}\bar{r}^{2})}\Bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2}}{\bar{\Xi}_{i}}\bar{\mu}^{i}d\bar{\mu}^{i}-\bar{r}^{2}\bar{\mu}^{N+1}d\bar{\mu}^{N+1}\Bigr)^{2}\,,

is in the appropriate form to apply the matrix determinant lemma with
M=D+uu^{T}
, where

\displaystyle uu^{T}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\ell^{-2}}{\bar{W}(1-\ell^{-2}\bar{r}^{2})}\Bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2}}{\bar{\Xi}_{i}}\bar{\mu}^{i}d\bar{\mu}^{i}-\bar{r}^{2}\bar{\mu}^{N+1}d\bar{\mu}^{N+1}\Bigr)^{2}\,,

(A.25)

\displaystyle D

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2}}{\bar{\Xi}_{i}}(d\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2}-\bar{r}^{2}(d\bar{\mu}^{N+1})^{2}\,.

(A.26)

This gives

\displaystyle\det(\tilde{g}_{IJ})

\displaystyle=-\left(1-\frac{\ell^{-2}}{\bar{W}(1-\ell^{-2}\bar{r}^{2})}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2}}{\bar{\Xi}_{i}}(\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2}-\bar{r}^{2}(\bar{\mu}^{N+1})^{2}\right]\right)\prod_{i=1}^{N+1}\frac{\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2}}{\bar{\Xi}_{i}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\bar{W}(1-\ell^{-2}\bar{r}^{2})}\underbrace{\left(-\sum_{i=1}^{N}(\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2}+(\bar{\mu}^{N+1})^{2}\right)}_{=\Phi+1}\bar{r}^{2}\prod_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2}}{\bar{\Xi}_{i}}\,.

(A.27)

Steps 2-4 are taken care of since they only set up the problem. In particular, since we did not change the coordinates
\bar{\mu}^{i}
for
1\leq i\leq N
we
have simply
\tilde{\gamma}_{ij}=\bar{g}_{ij}
. Thus, the only thing left to calculate is

{\cal N}^{-2}=(\partial_{\bar{I}}\Phi)\tilde{g}^{\bar{I}\bar{J}}(\partial_{\bar{J}}\Phi)\,.

(A.28)

Since this is a scalar we can work in the original coordinate system and calculate the gradient of
\Phi
given by (
A.20
). This yields,

d\Phi=2\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\bar{\mu}^{i}d\bar{\mu}^{i}-\bar{\mu}^{N+1}d\bar{\mu}^{N+1}\right)\,.

(A.29)

To calculate the inverse metric we plug (
A.2
) into (
A.28
) to obtain
(note that the expression
1+u\cdot D^{-1}\cdot u
appearing below has already been calculated in (
A.27
))

\displaystyle{\cal N}^{-2}

\displaystyle=d\Phi\cdot D^{-1}\cdot d\Phi-\frac{(u\cdot D^{-1}\cdot d\Phi)^{2}}{1+u\cdot D^{-1}\cdot u}

\displaystyle=-4\left(\frac{(\bar{\mu}^{N+1})^{2}}{\bar{r}^{2}}-\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{(\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2}\,\bar{\Xi}_{i}}{\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{4\bar{W}(1-\ell^{-2}\bar{r}^{2})}{\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}(\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2}-(\bar{\mu}^{N+1})^{2}\right)}\frac{\ell^{-2}}{\bar{W}(1-\ell^{-2}\bar{r}^{2})}\left((\bar{\mu}^{N+1})^{2}-\sum_{i=1}^{N}(\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=-4(1-\ell^{-2}\bar{r}^{2})\left(\frac{(\bar{\mu}^{N+1})^{2}}{\bar{r}^{2}}-\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{(\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2}}{\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{4(1-\ell^{-2}\bar{r}^{2})}{\bar{r}^{2}}\left(1+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\bar{a}_{i}^{2}(\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2}}{\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2}}\right)\,.

(A.30)

All that is left is to put the pieces together. Substituting, (
A.27
) and (
A.30
) into (
A.24
) we obtain

\det(\tilde{g}_{ij})=\det(\tilde{\gamma}_{ij})=\frac{1}{\bar{W}\,(\bar{\mu}^{N+1})^{2}}\left(1+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\bar{a}_{i}^{2}(\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2}}{\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2}}\right)\prod_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2}}{\bar{\Xi}_{i}}\,.

(A.31)

While this formula strongly suggests that the tensor field
\tilde{g}_{ij}
is non-degenerate everywhere, there is a catch in that the calculation involves a cancellation of a multiplication and a division by
\bar{r}^{2}-\ell^{2}
.
Indeed, the expression for
{\cal N}^{-2}
has a zero at
\bar{r}=\pm\ell
which is compensated by a factor in (
A.27
) in the final formula for the determinant of
\tilde{g}_{ij}
.
So an alternative calculation of the determinant at
\bar{r}=\pm\ell
is needed.
For this we can use directly the form of the metric in (
3.10
), since at
\bar{r}=\pm\ell
the
\bar{\mu}^{i}
-sector of the metric (with the constraint now imposed) is in a form to which (
A.1
) applies.
In particular, when
\bar{r}=\pm\ell
the prefactor in front of the
(d\bar{W})^{2}
terms vanishes and we have

\displaystyle\tilde{g}_{ij}\Big|_{\bar{r}^{2}=\ell^{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\ell^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2}}{\bar{\Xi}_{i}}(d\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2}-\ell^{2}\frac{\Big(\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}d((\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2})\Big)^{2}}{1+\sum_{i=1}^{N}(\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2}}

\displaystyle=\ell^{2}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}(d\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2}-\frac{\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\bar{\mu}^{i}d\bar{\mu}^{i}\right)^{2}}{1+\sum_{i=1}^{N}(\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2}}\right)\,.

(A.32)

Thus,

\displaystyle\det\left(\tilde{g}_{ij}\Big|_{\bar{r}^{2}=\ell^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\ell^{2N}\left(1-\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N}(\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2}}{1+\sum_{i=1}^{N}(\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2}}\right)=\frac{\ell^{2N}}{(\bar{\mu}^{N+1})^{2}}\,,

(A.33)

which, by the way, coincides with (
A.31
) evaluated at
\bar{r}^{2}=\ell^{2}
. The last equation shows that the zero of
{\cal N}^{-2}
is innocuous, and the determinant of the
\bar{\mu}^{i}
-sector of
\tilde{g}
has no zeros.

Note that the
\bar{\mu}^{i}
-part of
\tilde{g}
in (
A.32
) is Riemannian when all the
\bar{\mu}^{i}
’s vanish, and by continuity the signature of this part of
\bar{g}
is Riemannian everywhere.

Now, going back to (
A.3
) and substituting (
A.31
), (
A.5
), and for
g_{\bar{r}\bar{r}}
, the determinant of the full metric reads

\displaystyle\det(\bar{g}_{\mu\nu})

\displaystyle=\bar{g}_{\bar{r}\bar{r}}\det(\hat{g}_{\alpha\beta})\det(\tilde{g}_{ij})

\displaystyle=-\frac{\bar{U}}{\bar{V}-2\bar{m}\bar{r}}\bar{W}\left(\bar{V}-2\bar{m}\bar{r}\right)\frac{1}{\bar{W}\,(\bar{\mu}^{N+1})^{2}}\left(1+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\bar{a}_{i}^{2}(\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2}}{\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2}}\right)\,\prod_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2}}{\bar{\Xi}_{i}}\prod_{i=1}^{N}\frac{(\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2}}{\bar{\Xi}_{i}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{(\bar{\mu}^{N+1})^{2}}\left(1+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\bar{a}_{i}(\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2}}{\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2}}\right)^{2}\prod_{i=1}^{N}\frac{(\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2})^{2}\,(\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2}}{\bar{\Xi}_{i}^{2}}\,.

(A.34)

This finishes the proof of Proposition
3.1
.

Appendix B
Zeros of
\bar{V}-2\bar{r}\bar{m}

In order to simplify notation we rescale
\bar{r}
,
\bar{m}
, and the rotation parameters
\bar{a}_{i}
so that
\ell=1
:

x:=\frac{\bar{r}}{\ell}\,,\quad a_{i}:=\frac{\bar{a}_{i}}{\ell}\,,\quad m:=\frac{\bar{m}}{\ell^{2N-1}}\,.

(B.1)

The zeros of
\bar{V}-2\bar{r}\bar{m}
are then roots of the polynomial

\displaystyle\hat{F}(x):=(1-x^{2})\prod_{i=1}^{N}(x^{2}+a_{i}^{2})+2mx\,.

(B.2)

We will show that
\hat{F}(x)
has at most four real zeros. The case
N=1
is obvious, as
\hat{F}(x)
is a polynomial of degree four, hence with at most four roots. Thus we assume
N\geq 2
.

First, we expand the polynomial
\hat{F}(x)
by noting that

\prod_{i=1}^{N}(x^{2}+a_{i}^{2})=\sum_{k=0}^{N}A_{k}x^{2(N-k)}\,,\quad\text{where for $k\geq 2$,}\quad A_{k}:=\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\ldots<i_{k}\leq N}a_{i_{1}}^{2}\cdots a_{i_{k}}^{2}\,,

(B.3)

are the elementary symmetric polynomials with

A_{0}:=1\,.

It follows readily that

\hat{F}(x)=-x^{2N+2}-\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}(A_{k+1}-A_{k})x^{2(N-k)}+2mx+A_{N}\,,

(B.4)

where the coefficients of the powers of
x
can be written as

\displaystyle A_{1}-A_{0}

\displaystyle=-1+\sum_{i=1}^{N}a_{i}^{2}\,,

(B.5)

\displaystyle A_{2}-A_{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{N}a_{i}^{2}\Big(-1+\sum_{k>i}^{N}a_{k}^{2}\Big)\,,

(B.6)

\displaystyle A_{3}-A_{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}a_{i}^{2}\Big[\sum_{k>i}^{N}a_{k}^{2}\Big(-1+\sum_{j>k}^{N}a_{j}^{2}\Big)\Big]\,,

(B.7)

(B.8)

\displaystyle A_{k}-A_{k-1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i_{1}=1}^{N+2-k}\Big\{a_{i_{1}}^{2}\Big[\sum_{i_{2}>i_{1}}^{N+3-k}a_{i_{2}}^{2}\Big(\sum_{i_{3}>i_{2}}^{N+4-k}a_{i_{3}}^{2}\cdots\sum_{i_{k-1}>i_{k-2}}^{N-1}a_{i_{k-1}}^{2}\big(-1+\sum_{i_{k}>i_{k-1}}^{N}a_{i_{k}}^{2}\big)\Big)\Big]\Big\}\,.

(B.9)

To better reflect the recursive nature of these coefficients, it is convenient to introduce the following definitions:
(here and below, if the upper limit of a sum is smaller than the lower limit, then the sum is
taken to be zero)

\displaystyle h_{1}^{[k]}

\displaystyle:=-1+\sum_{i>k}^{N}a_{i}^{2}\,,\quad h_{\ell}^{[k]}:=\sum_{i>k}^{N+2-\ell}a_{i}^{2}h_{\ell-1}^{[i]}\,,\qquad\ell\in\mathbb{N}\cap[2,N+1]\,,k\in\mathbbm{Z}\cap[0,N]\,.

(B.10)

Writing-out the last definition in full:

\displaystyle h_{\ell}^{[k]}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i_{1}>k}^{N+2-\ell}\Big\{a_{i_{1}}^{2}\underbrace{\Big[\sum_{i_{2}>i_{1}}^{N+3-\ell}a_{i_{2}}^{2}\underbrace{\Big(\sum_{i_{3}>i_{2}}^{N+4-\ell}\cdots\underbrace{\big(-1+\sum_{i_{\ell}>i_{\ell-1}}^{N}a_{i_{\ell}}^{2}\big)}_{h_{1}^{[i_{\ell}]}}\Big)}_{h_{\ell-2}^{[i_{2}]}}\Big]\Big\}}_{h_{\ell-1}^{[i_{1}]}}\,.

(B.11)

Comparing (
B.9
) and (
B.11
) one finds

\displaystyle h_{\ell}^{[0]}

\displaystyle=A_{\ell}-A_{\ell-1}\,,

(B.12)

hence

\displaystyle\hat{F}(x)=-x^{2(N+1)}-\sum_{i=1}^{N}h_{i}^{[0]}x^{2(N-(i-1))}+2mx+a_{1}^{2}a_{2}^{2}\dots a_{N}^{2}\,.

(B.13)

To see that
\hat{F}(x)
has at most four real roots we make use of Descartes’ rule of signs, which states that the number of positive real roots of a polynomial is not larger than the number of sign changes in the sequence of polynomial’s non-zero coefficients, while the number of real negative roots is no larger than the number of sign changes after multiplying the coefficients of odd-power terms by
-1
.
In our case, we are done if we can show:

Lemma
B.1
.

There can be at most one change of signs in the sequence
(1,h_{1}^{[0]},h_{2}^{[0]},...,h_{N}^{[0]})

Proof
: If all
h_{i}^{[0]}
’s are positive then there are no changes of signs and we are done. We will prove the lemma by showing that for integers
i\in[1,N]
and
k_{0}\in[0,N]
we have the following implication:

\displaystyle h_{i}^{[k_{0}]}\leq 0\implies h_{i}^{[k]}\leq 0\quad\forall\ k\in\mathbbm{Z}\cap[k_{0},N]\,.

(B.14)

Assuming that (
B.14
) is true, setting
k_{0}=0
there gives

h_{i}^{[0]}\leq 0\implies h_{i}^{[k\geq 0]}\leq 0\implies h_{i+1}^{[0]}\leq 0\,,

where the last implication follows from the fact that
h_{i+1}^{[0]}
is a sum of terms of the form
a_{k}^{2}h_{i}^{[k]}
(cf. (
B.10
)). Thus there can only be one change of signs.

Given
0\leq k_{0}\leq N
, we will prove the implication (
B.14
) by induction on
i
.

i=1
:

We have

\displaystyle h_{1}^{[k]}=-1+a_{k+1}^{2}+a_{k+2}^{2}+...+a_{N}^{2}\,.

(B.15)

Clearly,

h_{1}^{[k+1]}=-1+a_{k+2}^{2}+\ldots+a_{N}^{2}<-1+a_{k+1}^{2}+a_{k+2}^{2}+\ldots+a_{N}^{2}=h_{1}^{[k]}\,.

Indeed,
h_{1}^{[k]}<h_{1}^{[k_{0}]}
for all
k\geq k_{0}
, so if
h_{1}^{[k_{0}]}\leq 0
then
h_{1}^{[k]}\leq 0

for all

k\geq k_{0}
.

i=j_{0}+1
:

Assuming (
B.14
) is true for
i=j_{0}
, we now show that it is true for
i=j_{0}+1
.
Thus, we assume
h_{j_{0}+1}^{[k_{0}]}\leq 0
:

\displaystyle h_{j_{0}+1}^{[k_{0}]}

\displaystyle=a_{k_{0}+1}^{2}h_{j_{0}}^{[k_{0}+1]}+a_{k_{0}+2}^{2}h_{j_{0}}^{[k_{0}+2]}+...+a_{N+1-j_{0}}^{2}h_{j_{0}}^{[N+1-j_{0}]}\leq 0\,.

(B.16)

If all the terms in the sum are negative there is nothing to prove. Thus, suppose not. Then because of the
i=j_{0}
statement, we can assume that there is some
k_{0}+1<\ell_{0}\leq N
such that
h_{j_{0}}^{[i]}\geq 0
for
k_{0}\leq i\leq\ell_{0}
and
h_{j_{0}}^{[i]}<0
for
\ell_{0}<i\leq N
, i.e.,

\displaystyle h_{j_{0}+1}^{[k_{0}]}

\displaystyle=\underbrace{a_{k_{0}+1}^{2}h_{j_{0}}^{[k_{0}+1]}+a_{k_{0}+2}^{2}h_{j_{0}}^{[k_{0}+2]}+...+a_{\ell_{0}}^{2}h_{j_{0}}^{[\ell_{0}]}}_{\text{each term is }\geq 0}+\underbrace{a_{\ell_{0}+1}^{2}h_{j_{0}}^{[\ell_{0}+1]}+...+a_{N+1-j_{0}}^{2}h_{j_{0}}^{[N+1-j_{0}]}}_{\text{each term is }<0}

\displaystyle\leq 0\,.

(B.17)

If we start removing terms from
h_{j_{0}+1}^{[k_{0}]}
in (
B.17
) starting from terms from the left, clearly the left-over sum will always remain negative.
Since the
h_{j_{0}+1}^{[k\geq k_{0}]}
’s are exactly obtained in this way, they are all negative, and thus the claim for
i=j_{0}+1
is true.

Appendix C
Regularity of
\bar{H}_{\pm}

In this appendix we show that the functions
\bar{H}_{\pm}
of (
4.8
), i.e.

\bar{H}_{-}(\bar{r}):=\inf_{\bar{\mu}^{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\breve{U}^{-1}N^{2}}{|\bar{V}-2\bar{m}\bar{r}|}\ \mbox{and}\ \bar{H}_{+}(\bar{r}):=\sup_{\bar{\mu}^{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}\ \frac{\breve{U}^{-1}N^{2}}{|\bar{V}-2\bar{m}\bar{r}|}\,,

(C.1)

are Lipschitz-continuous.
This follows from Lemma
C.1
below after some preparatory work. We expect these functions to be piecewise smooth, but whether or not they are is irrelevant for the applications in this work.
We consider a fully-rotating configuration.

Recall that

\displaystyle\bar{U}=\

\displaystyle\underbrace{\bigg(1+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{{(\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2}}\bar{a}_{i}^{2}}{\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2}}\bigg)}_{\breve{U}}\prod_{i=1}^{N}(\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2})=:\breve{U}\bar{F}^{2}\,,

(C.2)

\displaystyle N^{2}=\

\displaystyle\frac{\bar{W}|\bar{V}-2\bar{m}\bar{r}|}{\left(1+\frac{2\bar{m}\bar{r}}{\bar{U}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\bar{a}_{i}^{2}(\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2}}{(\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2})\bar{\Xi}_{i}}\right)\prod_{i=1}^{N}(\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2})}

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle\frac{\breve{U}\bar{W}|\bar{V}-2\bar{m}\bar{r}|}{\breve{U}\bar{F}^{2}+{2\bar{m}\bar{r}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\bar{a}_{i}^{2}(\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2}}{(\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2})\bar{\Xi}_{i}}}\,,

(C.3)

\displaystyle\bar{W}=\

\displaystyle 1+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\bar{a}_{i}^{2}(\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2}}{\ell^{2}\bar{\Xi}_{i}}\,,

(C.4)

\displaystyle H:=\

\displaystyle\frac{\breve{U}^{-1}N^{2}}{|\bar{V}-2\bar{m}\bar{r}|}

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle\frac{1+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\bar{a}_{i}^{2}(\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2}}{\ell^{2}\bar{\Xi}_{i}}}{\big(1+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{{(\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2}}\bar{a}_{i}^{2}}{\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2}}\big)\prod_{i=1}^{N}(\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2})+{2\bar{m}\bar{r}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\bar{a}_{i}^{2}(\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2}}{(\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2})\bar{\Xi}_{i}}}\,.

(C.5)

We note that the function
H
has an obviously vanishing derivative at
\bar{\mu}^{i}=0
, with

H|_{\bar{\mu}^{i}=0}=\prod_{i=1}^{N}(\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2})^{-1}\,.

(C.6)

If it were a global extremum for some values of parameters, smoothness either of the
\sup
or of the
\inf
, whichever relevant, would immediately follow.
We have not pursued this question any further.

To continue, it is useful to analyse the asymptotic behaviour of
\breve{U}^{-1}N^{2}
. For this we set

|\bar{\mu}|=\sqrt{\sum(\bar{\mu}^{i})^{2}}\,,\quad n^{i}:=|\bar{\mu}|^{-1}\bar{\mu}^{i}\,.

(C.7)

We have

\displaystyle\lim_{|\bar{\mu}|\to\infty}H\equiv\

\displaystyle\lim_{|\bar{\mu}|\to\infty}\frac{\breve{U}^{-1}N^{2}}{|\bar{V}-2\bar{m}\bar{r}|}

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\bar{a}_{i}^{2}(n^{i})^{2}}{\ell^{2}\bar{\Xi}_{i}}}{\big(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{(n^{i})^{2}\bar{a}_{i}^{2}}{\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2}}\big)\prod_{i=1}^{N}(\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2})+{2\bar{m}\bar{r}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\bar{a}_{i}^{2}(n^{i})^{2}}{(\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2})\bar{\Xi}_{i}}}

\displaystyle=:\

\displaystyle\bar{H}|_{S^{N-1}}\,.

(C.8)

The function
\bar{H}|_{S^{N-1}}
is a rational function of expressions of the form

\sum\alpha_{i}^{2}\bar{a}_{i}^{2}(n^{i})^{2}=\vec{X}\cdot\vec{N}\,,

where
\cdot
denotes the Euclidean scalar product, with

\vec{X}:=(\alpha_{1}^{2}\bar{a}_{1}^{2},\ldots,\alpha_{N}^{2}\bar{a}_{N}^{2})\ \mbox{and}\ \vec{N}:=(n_{1}^{2},\ldots,n_{N}^{2})\,,

for some coefficients
\alpha_{i}>0
.
Thus both
\vec{X}
and
\vec{N}
lie in the positive quadrant of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
Since all the
\alpha_{i}^{2}a_{i}^{2}
’s are non-vanishing,
\vec{X}
and
\vec{N}
are never orthogonal.
It follows that the angle between
\vec{X}
and
\vec{N}
is in
[0,\pi/2)
. Since
\vec{X}\cdot\vec{N}=|\vec{X}||\vec{N}|\cos(\theta)
we see that each such scalar product occurring in
\bar{H}|_{S^{N-1}}

is strictly positive.
We conclude that the function
\bar{H}|_{S^{N-1}}(\bar{r},\cdot)
is bounded both from above and from below for each
\bar{r}
on the set defined by the inequality

\inf_{n^{i}\in S^{n-1}}\left[\big(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{(n^{i})^{2}\bar{a}_{i}^{2}}{\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2}}\big)\prod_{i=1}^{N}(\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2})+{2\bar{m}\bar{r}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\bar{a}_{i}^{2}(n^{i})^{2}}{(\bar{r}^{2}+\bar{a}_{i}^{2})\bar{\Xi}_{i}}\right]>0\,.

(C.9)

This further implies that
\bar{H}(\bar{r},\cdot)
is likewise bounded from above and below on this same set.

It should be clear, e.g. by comparing (
3.59
) with the calculations above (see also Proposition
3.3
), that the complement of the set defined by (
C.9
) coincides with the projection of the closure of the time-machine set, as defined at the end of Section
3.8.2
.

Let

\Psi:B^{N}\mapsto\mathbb{R}^{N}

be any diffeomorphism from an open ball
B^{N}
to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
chosen so that for each
\bar{r}
the function

\bar{H}(\bar{r}.\cdot):=H\circ\Psi(\bar{r},\cdot):B^{N}\mapsto\mathbb{R}

extends to a smooth function, still denoted by
\bar{H}(\bar{r},\cdot)
, on
\bar{B}^{N}
.
For example,

\bar{\mu}^{i}=\Psi^{i}(\vec{x}):=\frac{x^{i}}{1-|\vec{x}|^{2}}\,,

but any such map will do.
Note that with this last choice the restriction of
\bar{H}
to
S^{N-1}
coincides directly with the function
\bar{H}|_{S^{N-1}}
already defined in (
C.8
).

Local Lipschitz-continuity of the functions
\bar{H}_{\pm}
follows now from the following standard fact, which we prove for completeness:

Lemma
C.1
.

Let
\bar{H}
be a differentiable function on
I\times\bar{B}^{N}
, where
I
is a closed interval and
\bar{B}^{N}
is a compact manifold, possibly with boundary. Then, for
r\in I
the functions
\inf_{\bar{B}^{N}}\bar{H}(r,\cdot)
and
\sup_{\bar{B}^{N}}\bar{H}(r,\cdot)
are Lipschitz functions of
r
.

Proof.

For
r
and
r+\Delta
in
I
,
with
\Delta\geq 0
, we can write

\displaystyle\bar{H}(r+\Delta,x^{i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{H}(r,x^{i})+\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d\Big(\bar{H}\big(t(r+\Delta)+(1-t)r,x^{i}\big)\Big)}{dt}\,dt

(C.10)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{H}(r,x^{i})+\Delta\int_{0}^{1}(\partial_{r}\Big(\bar{H}\big(t(r+\Delta)+(1-t)r,x^{i}\big)\Big)\,dt\,.

Hence

\displaystyle\sup_{x^{i}\in\bar{B}^{N}}\bar{H}(r+\Delta,x^{i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{x^{i}\in\bar{B}^{N}}\Big[\bar{H}(r,x^{i})+\Delta\int_{0}^{1}(\partial_{r}\Big(\bar{H}\big(t(r+\Delta)+(1-t)r,x^{i}\big)\Big)\,dt\Big]

(C.11)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{x^{i}\in\bar{B}^{N}}\bar{H}(r,x^{i})+\Delta\int_{0}^{1}\sup_{x^{i}\in\bar{B}^{N},\,r\in I}(\partial_{r}\Big(\bar{H}\big(t(r+\Delta)+(1-t)r,x^{i}\big)\Big)\,dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{x^{i}\in\bar{B}^{N}}\bar{H}(r,x^{i})+\Delta\sup_{x^{i}\in\bar{B}^{N},\,r\in I}(\partial_{r}\bar{H})\big(r,x^{i}\big)\,.

Next, consider (
C.10
) at a point
x^{i}
where the sup of

\bar{H}(r,\cdot)

is attained:

\displaystyle\bar{H}(r+\Delta,x^{i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{x^{i}\in\bar{B}^{N}}\bar{H}(r,x^{i})+\Delta\int_{0}^{1}(\partial_{r}\Big(\bar{H}\big(t(r+\Delta)+(1-t)r,x^{i}\big)\Big)\,dt\,.

(C.12)

Hence

\displaystyle\sup_{\bar{B}^{N}}\bar{H}(r+\Delta,\cdot)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\bar{H}(r+\Delta,x^{i})

(C.13)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{x^{i}\in\bar{B}^{N}}\bar{H}(r,x^{i})+\Delta\int_{0}^{1}(\partial_{r}\Big(\bar{H}\big(t(r+\Delta)+(1-t)r,x^{i}\big)\Big)\,dt

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sup_{x^{i}\in\bar{B}^{N}}\bar{H}(r,x^{i})-\Delta\int_{0}^{1}\sup_{x^{i}\in\bar{B}^{N},\,r\in I}\left|(\partial_{r}\Big(\bar{H}\big(t(r+\Delta)+(1-t)r,x^{i}\big)\Big)\right|\,dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{x^{i}\in\bar{B}^{N}}\bar{H}(r,x^{i})-\Delta\left|\sup_{x^{i}\in\bar{B}^{N},\,r\in I}(\partial_{r}\bar{H})\big(r,x^{i}\big)\right|\,,

which finishes the proof of Lipschitz-continuity of
\sup_{x^{i}\in\bar{B}^{N}}\bar{H}(r,x^{i})
.

Since
\inf f=-\sup(-f)
, applying what has been established so far to
-\bar{H}
finishes the proof of the Lemma.

\Box

Appendix D
Analytic continuation

Consider a manifold
{\mycal M}
of dimension
d
equipped with a non-degenerate tensor field
g=g_{\mu\nu}dx^{\mu}dx^{\nu}
satisfying, say, the vacuum Einstein equations with a cosmological constant,

\mathrm{Ric}=\lambda g\,,

(D.1)

for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
; the signature is irrelevant for what follows. Suppose that all the metric functions
g_{\mu\nu}
are real analytic in a coordinate
x^{1}
near a point
p\in{\mycal M}
. More precisely, let us assume there exists a neighborhood of
p
with a coordinate system such that
x^{1}(p)=0
and on which the Taylor series in
x^{1}
, at
x^{1}=0
, of each metric function

g_{\mu\nu}
converges for all
|x^{1}|<R
to its value
g_{\mu\nu}(x^{1},\ldots,x^{d})
.

Consider the open disc
D\subset{\mathbb{C}}
of radius
\rho
, and for
z\in D
define a complex valued tensor field
\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}(z,x^{2},\ldots,x^{d})
by the just-mentioned Taylor series. Restricting
\rho
if necessary we can without loss of generality assume that
|\det(\bar{g})|>0
on
D
.

We can define the Christoffel symbols, the Riemann tensor, and the Ricci tensor of
\bar{g}
by the usual formulae
of pseudo-Riemannian geometry, with
\partial_{x^{1}}
replaced by
\partial_{z}
.
For real values of
z

we have
\bar{g}=g
, so that the Ricci tensor of
\bar{g}
, say
\overline{\mathrm{Ric}}
, satisfies there

\overline{\mathrm{Ric}}=\lambda\bar{g}\,.

Since
\overline{\mathrm{Ric}}
is holomorphic in
z
this remains true for all
z\in D
.
For
a\in{\mathbb{C}}
define

\psi_{a}(z,x^{2},\ldots,x^{d})=(az,x^{2},\ldots,x^{d})\,.

Set

\bar{g}_{a}=\psi_{a}^{*}\bar{g}\,.

By the usual formulae of tensor calculus, the Ricci tensor
\overline{\mathrm{Ric}}_{a}
of
\bar{g}_{a}

satisfies

\overline{\mathrm{Ric}}_{a}=\psi_{a}^{*}\overline{\mathrm{Ric}}\,,

(D.2)

and we conclude that

\overline{\mathrm{Ric}}_{a}=\lambda\bar{g}_{a}\,.

(D.3)

Restricting the coordinate
az
to real-values, and reverting from
\partial_{z}
to
\partial_{x^{1}}
, one finds that the Ricci-tensor of the (possibly imaginary-valued) tensor
\bar{g}_{a}
continues to satisfy (
D.3
).

Suppose, next, that the metric
\bar{g}_{a}
above depends analytically on a real-valued parameter, say
b
. A similar argument shows that
\bar{g}_{a}
can be analytically extended to all values of
b\in{\mathbb{C}}
which belong to a simply connected and arc-connected Riemann surface on which
|\det g_{a}|>0
, still satisfying (
D.3
).

One can iterate this argument with further coordinates and parameters in the metric, if applicable.

The task is then to find parameters
a,b\in{\mathbb{C}}
as above so that the tensor
\bar{g}_{a}
is real-valued with Lorentzian signature, hence a Lorentzian Einstein metric. In our calculations the coordinates are
t
and
r
, and the parameter
a
used is
\sqrt{-1}
for
t
and
-\sqrt{-1}
for
r
. The parameter
b
here corresponds to the
a_{j}
’s and
m
in the main text.

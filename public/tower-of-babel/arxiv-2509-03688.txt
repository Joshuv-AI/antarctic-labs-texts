The reverse mathematics of bounded Ramsey’s theorem for pairs
Quentin Le Houérou, Ludovic Patey

Abstract
In this article, we study a degenerate version of Ramsey’s theorem for pairs and two colors ( 𝖱𝖳 2 2 \mathsf{RT}^{2}_{2} ), in which the homogeneous sets for color 1 are of bounded size. By 𝖱𝖳 2 2 \mathsf{RT}^{2}_{2} , it follows that every such coloring admits an infinite homogeneous set for color 0. This statement, called 𝖡𝖱𝖳 2 2 \mathsf{BRT}^{2}_{2} , is known to be computably true, that is, every computable instance admits a computable solution, but the known proofs use Σ 2 0 \Sigma^{0}_{2} -induction ( 𝖨 ​ Σ 2 0 \mathsf{I}\Sigma^{0}_{2} ). We prove that 𝖡𝖱𝖳 2 2 \mathsf{BRT}^{2}_{2} follows from the Erdős-Moser theorem but not from the Ascending Descending sequence principle, and that its computably true version is equivalent to 𝖨 ​ Σ 2 0 \mathsf{I}\Sigma^{0}_{2} over 𝖱𝖢𝖠 0 \mathsf{RCA}_{0} .



1
Introduction

Ramsey’s theorem for pairs and two colors (
\mathsf{RT}^{2}_{2}
) is a combinatorial theorem which states that every coloring
f:[\mathbb{N}]^{2}\to 2
admits an infinite
f
-homogeneous set. Here, given a set
X
,
[X]^{2}
denotes the set of unordered pairs over
X
and a set
H\subseteq X
is
f
-homogeneous
if all the pairs in
[H]^{2}
have the same color by
f
. The statement
\mathsf{RT}^{2}_{2}
was extensively studied from a meta-mathematical viewpoint, both in reverse mathematics and computability theory. In particular, Specker
[
39
]
proved that
\mathsf{RT}^{2}_{2}
is not
computably true
, in that there is a computable coloring
f:[\mathbb{N}]^{2}\to 2
with no infinite computable
f
-homogeneous set.

Many applications of Ramsey’s theorem in mathematics yield degenerate colorings. In some cases, the specific features of the coloring can be exploited to yield simpler solutions, in a computable or proof-theoretic sense. For instance, a coloring
f:[\mathbb{N}]^{2}\to 2
is
transitive
for some color
i<2
if for every
x<y<z
such that
f(x,y)=f(y,z)=i
, then
f(x,z)=i
. The restriction of
\mathsf{RT}^{2}_{2}
to colorings which are transitive for one or both colors yield the Chain AntiChain (
\mathsf{CAC}
) and the Ascending Descending Sequence (
\mathsf{ADS}
) principles, respectively. The statements
\mathsf{RT}^{2}_{2}
,
\mathsf{CAC}
and
\mathsf{ADS}
form a strictly decreasing sequence in reverse mathematics (see
[
18
, Corollary 3.12]
and
[
27
, Theorem 1.7]
).

In this paper, we study another common degenerate version of Ramsey’s theorem for pairs and two colors, in which the homogeneous sets for color 1 have bounded size. By
\mathsf{RT}^{2}_{2}
, it follows that there exists an infinite homogeneous set for color 0. This is part of a larger project started with and driven by Frittaion, who introduced bounded Ramsey’s theorem for colorings of arbitrary tuples. This project was pursued by Soldà
[
38
, Section 4.1]
, and then independently by Belanger, Shafer and Yokoyama (unpublished).

Statement

(Bounded
\mathsf{RT}^{2}_{2}
)
.

\mathsf{BRT}^{2}_{2}
is the statement “For every coloring
f:[\mathbb{N}]^{2}\to 2
and every
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
such that there is no
f
-homogeneous set for color 1 of size
\ell
, there is an infinite
f
-homogeneous set for color 0.”

The statement
\mathsf{BRT}^{2}_{2}
is significantly weaker than
\mathsf{RT}^{2}_{2}
. From a computational viewpoint, contrary to Ramsey’s theorem for pairs,
\mathsf{BRT}^{2}_{2}
is computably true. However, as we shall see, the existence of a computable solution requires
\Sigma^{0}_{2}
-induction. Since
\mathsf{RT}^{2}_{2}
does not imply the
\Sigma^{0}_{2}
-induction scheme
[
10
]
in reverse mathematics, the pure existence of a solution to an instance of
\mathsf{BRT}^{2}_{2}
is strictly weaker than the existence of a computable solution. We shall study this bounded version of Ramsey’s theorem for pairs using the framework of reverse mathematics.

1.1
Reverse mathematics

Reverse mathematics
is a foundational program whose goal is to find optimal axioms to prove theorems from ordinary mathematics. It uses the framework of subsystems of second-order arithmetic, with a base theory,
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}
, capturing “computable mathematics”. The theory
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}
contains the theory
\mathsf{P}^{-}
of discretely ordered commutative semirings, together with the
\Delta^{0}_{1}
-comprehension scheme and the
\Sigma^{0}_{1}
-induction scheme. We now give a more precise account of those schemes.

Definition 1.1
.

Given a family of formulas
\Gamma
, the
\Gamma
-induction scheme
is defined for every
\Gamma
-formula
\varphi
as

\varphi(0)\wedge\forall x(\varphi(x)\to\varphi(x+1))\to\forall y\varphi(y)

We write
\mathsf{I}\Gamma
for the theory
\mathsf{P}^{-}
together with the
\Gamma
-induction scheme. The arithmetic hierarchy induces a hierarchy of induction schemes. By Paris and Kirby
[
30
]
,
\mathsf{I}\Pi^{0}_{n}
and
\mathsf{I}\Sigma^{0}_{n}
are equivalent for every
n\geq 0
, while
\mathsf{I}\Sigma^{0}_{n+1}
is strictly stronger than
\mathsf{I}\Sigma^{0}_{n}
.

The induction scheme for arithmetic formulas is equivalent modulo
\mathsf{P}^{-}+\mathsf{I}\Sigma^{0}_{0}
to the following collection (or bounding) scheme for the same family of formulas:

Definition 1.2
.

Given a family of formulas
\Gamma
, the
\Gamma
-collection
(or
\Gamma
-bounding) scheme is defined for every
\Gamma
-formula
\varphi
as

\forall a[(\forall x<a)\exists y\varphi(x,y)\rightarrow\exists b(\forall x<a)(\exists y<b)\varphi(x,y)]

We write
\mathsf{B}\Gamma
for the theory
\mathsf{P}^{-}
together with the
\Sigma^{0}_{0}
-induction scheme and the
\Gamma
-collection scheme. By Paris and Kirby
[
30
]
,
\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{n+1}
and
\mathsf{B}\Pi^{0}_{n}
are equivalent for every
n\geq 1
, and the following hierarchy is strict:

\mathsf{I}\Sigma^{0}_{1}<\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{2}<\mathsf{I}\Sigma^{0}_{2}<\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{3}<\cdots

Being a
\Delta^{0}_{n}
-predicate is a semantic notion, which depends either of a theory, or a structure. Because of this, one usually defines the
\Delta^{0}_{n}
-comprehension scheme using the following syntactical artifact.

Definition 1.3
.

The
\Delta^{0}_{n}
-comprehension scheme
is defined for every
\Sigma^{0}_{n}
-formula
\varphi
and every
\Pi^{0}_{n}
-formula
\psi
as

\forall x(\varphi(x)\leftrightarrow\psi(x))\to\exists X\forall y(y\in X\leftrightarrow\varphi(y))

The theories
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+\mathsf{I}\Sigma^{0}_{n}
and
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{n}
being
\Pi^{1}_{1}
-conservative extensions of
\mathsf{I}\Sigma^{0}_{n}
and
\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{n}
, respectively, the previous hierarchy remains strict over
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}
.

Since the beginning of reverse mathematics, many theorems have been studied. It appeared that most theorems are either provable over
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}
, or provably equivalent over
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}
to one of four systems of axioms, linearly ordered by logical implication
[
35
]
. However, some counter-example exist, the most important one being Ramsey’s theorem for pairs and two colors
[
34
,
26
]
. Since then, the study of Ramsey’s theorem, and more generally of combinatorial theorems in reverse mathematics, became a very active research field.

1.2
Main contributions

As mentioned, this article focuses on the study of a degenerate version of
\mathsf{RT}^{2}_{2}
, in which the homogeneous sets for color 1 have bounded size.
By an old argument of Hirst
[
19
, Theorem 6.8]
,
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}\vdash\mathsf{BRT}^{2}_{2}\to\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{2}
. Belanger and Yokoyama (unpublished) gave independently two proofs of
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}\vdash\mathsf{I}\Sigma^{0}_{2}\to\mathsf{BRT}^{2}_{2}
(see
Proposition

2.1
). On the other hand,
\mathsf{BRT}^{2}_{2}
follows from
\mathsf{RT}^{2}_{2}
, which is known not to imply
\mathsf{I}\Sigma^{0}_{2}
over
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}
by Chong, Slaman and Yang
[
10
]
. It follows that
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+\mathsf{BRT}^{2}_{2}\not\vdash\mathsf{I}\Sigma^{0}_{2}
.
Our first main contribution is the following theorem:

Main Theorem 1.4

(Short version)
.

Every model of
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{2}+\neg\mathsf{I}\Sigma^{0}_{2}
contains an instance
f
of
\mathsf{BRT}^{2}_{2}
with no
\Delta_{1}(f)
-definable solution.

It follows that
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{2}\not\vdash\mathsf{BRT}^{2}_{2}
, hence the strength of
\mathsf{BRT}^{2}_{2}
lies strictly in between
\mathsf{I}\Sigma^{0}_{2}
and
\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{2}
. Furthermore, the statement “
\mathsf{BRT}^{2}_{2}
is computably true” is equivalent to
\mathsf{I}\Sigma^{0}_{2}
over
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}
. Here, given a
\Pi^{1}_{2}
-problem
\mathsf{P}
, the statement “
\mathsf{P}
is computably true” means that every
\mathsf{P}
-instance
X
admits a
\Delta_{1}(X)
-definable solution. This situation already appears in the literature, with the tree theorem for singletons
[
11
,
8
]
and bounded-width weak König’s lemma
[
37
]
. Over
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{2}
, both statements are strictly in between
\mathsf{I}\Sigma^{0}_{2}
and
\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{2}
, and their computably true version is equivalent to
\mathsf{I}\Sigma^{0}_{2}
.

By Bovykin and Weiermann
[
7
]
, there exists a natural decomposition of Ramsey’s theorem for pairs into the Erdős-Moser theorem (
\mathsf{EM}
) and the Ascending Descending Sequence principle (
\mathsf{ADS}
). Over
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}
, the Erdős-Moser theorem can be seen as the statement that every coloring
f:[\mathbb{N}]^{2}\to 2
admits an infinite sub-coloring which is transitive for both colors, while
\mathsf{ADS}
states that every such coloring admits an infinite
f
-homogeneous set. From a reverse mathematical viewpoint, these statements almost play the role of a split pair, in that the strongest known common consequences are
\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{2}
and the existence of hyperimmune functions (
[
24
,
18
,
27
]
). Soldà
[
38
, Theorem 4.1.10]
proved that
\mathsf{BRT}^{2}_{2}
is a consequence of
\mathsf{EM}
, and we prove that
\mathsf{BRT}^{2}_{2}
does not follow from
\mathsf{ADS}
over
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}
. More precisely, we prove the following theorem:

Main Theorem 1.5
.

Every countable topped model of
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{2}+\neg\mathsf{I}\Sigma^{0}_{2}
can be
\omega
-extended into a model of
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+\mathsf{ADS}+\neg\mathsf{BRT}^{2}_{2}
.

Here, a model is
topped
if its second-order part is of the form
\{Z:Z\leq_{T}Y\}
for some fixed set
Y
, and an
\omega
-extension
of model
\mathcal{M}
is a model
\mathcal{N}
obtained from
\mathcal{M}
by adding sets, but keeping the integers part unchanged. This proves that
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+\mathsf{ADS}\not\vdash\mathsf{BRT}^{2}_{2}
in a strong sense:
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+\mathsf{ADS}+\neg\mathsf{BRT}^{2}_{2}
is
\Pi^{1}_{1}
-conservative over
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{2}+\neg\mathsf{I}\Sigma^{0}_{2}
. This separation proof involves a formalized preservation of a combinatorial property by
\mathsf{ADS}
, using an effective construction in a weak model of arithmetic.

2
\mathsf{BRT}^{2}_{2}
and partition theorems on partial orders

This section is essentially a survey in which we give the best known proof that
\mathsf{BRT}^{2}_{2}
is computably true, and relate the bounded Ramsey’s theorem for pairs to two well-known theorems from graph theory, namely, Dilworth’s theorem and Mirsky’s theorem. Both theorems and variants have already been studied in reverse mathematics.

As mentioned in the introduction, Hirst
[
19
, Theorem 6.8]
proved that
\mathsf{BRT}^{2}_{2}
implies
\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{2}
over
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}
, thanks to the equivalence between
\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{2}
and the infinite pigeonhole principle (
\mathsf{RT}^{1}
). This principle states, for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
and every coloring
g:\mathbb{N}\to k
, the existence of an infinite
g
-homogeneous set, that is, an infinite set
H\subseteq\mathbb{N}
on which
g
is constant. Given such a coloring
g:\mathbb{N}\to k
, let
f(x,y)=0
if
g(x)=g(y)
and 1 otherwise. There are no
f
-homogeneous set for color 1 of size
k+1
, and any infinite
f
-homogeneous set for color 0 is
g
-homogeneous.

Versions of Ramsey’s theorem for pairs in which all the homogeneous sets have the same color were mentioned in reverse mathematics by Jockusch
[
21
]
under the name “unbalanced”, but the bounded Ramsey’s theorem was first introduced by Frittaion (unpublished), who proved
\mathsf{BRT}^{2}_{2}
over
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+\mathsf{I}\Sigma^{0}_{3}
. The upper bound was improved by Belanger and Yokoyama (unpublished) independently with two different proofs. The one by Yokoyama can be found in Soldà
[
38
, Lemma 4.1.9]
. We now give the proof of Belanger.

Proposition 2.1

(Belanger and Yokoyama)
.

\mathsf{RCA}_{0}\vdash\mathsf{I}\Sigma^{0}_{2}\to\mathsf{BRT}^{2}_{2}
.

Proof of Belanger.

Let
T\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{<\mathbb{N}}
be the computable tree such that

(1)

every
x\in\mathbb{N}
appears in some node
\sigma\in T
;

(2)

every
\sigma\in T
is
f
-homogeneous for color 0;

(3)

for every
\sigma\in T
and every
x,y\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\sigma\cdot x,\sigma\cdot y\in T
,
f(x,y)=1
.

By (2) and by assumption,
T
has finite depth, so by (1) and
\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{2}
, there is some level
\ell_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\{\sigma\in T:|\sigma|=\ell_{0}\}
is infinite. By
\mathsf{I}\Sigma^{0}_{2}
, there is a least such level
\ell\leq\ell_{0}
. Since
T
has only one node at level 0, namely
\langle\rangle
,
\ell>0
. By minimality of
\ell
,
\{\sigma\in T:|\sigma|=\ell-1\}
is finite, so by
\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{2}
, there is some
\sigma\in T
of length
\ell-1
with infinitely many immediate children. The set
\{x\in\mathbb{N}:\sigma\cdot x\in T\}
is therefore infinite, and by (3), it is
f
-homogeneous for color 1.
∎

Note that if the bound to the 1-homogeneous sets is a standard integer, then so is the depth of the tree in
Proposition

2.1
, and the statement is provable over
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{2}
(see Soldà
[
38
, Corollary 4.1.5]
).

Bounded Ramsey’s theorem for pairs and two colors can be formulated in terms of graphs, stating that every infinite graph with no sub-clique of some size
k
admits an infinite induced sub-anticlique. This statement has applications to partial orders, through their comparability and incomparability graphs.

2.1
Dilworth’s theorem

Dilworth’s theorem is a famous combinatorial theorem about partial orders, closely related to the bounded Ramsey’s theorem. The
width

w(\mathcal{P})
of a partial order
\mathcal{P}=(\mathbb{N},<_{\mathcal{P}})
is the size of its largest antichain.

Statement

(Dilworth’s theorem)
.

“For every partial order
\mathcal{P}=(\mathbb{N},<_{\mathcal{P}})
with finite width, there is a coloring
g:\mathbb{N}\to w(\mathcal{P})
such that for every
i<w(\mathcal{P})
,
g^{-1}(i)
is a
\mathcal{P}
-chain.”

Such a coloring
g
is called a
chain partition
of
\mathcal{P}
. Dilworth’s theorem is classically proven for finite partial orders, but the infinitary version directly follows from it by a compactness argument as follows: Given a partial order
\mathcal{P}=(\mathbb{N},<_{\mathcal{P}})
of finite width
w
, one can define its incomparability graph
\mathcal{G}=(\mathbb{N},E)
, that is, the graph such that
\{x,y\}\in E
iff
x
and
y
are
\mathcal{P}
-incomparable. Finite Dilworth’s theorem essentially states that the graph
\mathcal{G}
is locally
w
-colorable. By Gasarch and Hirst
[
16
, Theorem 4]
,
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+\mathsf{WKL}
proves that every locally
w
-colorable graph is
w
-colorable, and any such
w
-coloring is a chain partition of
G
.
Thus, Dilworth’s theorem follows from
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+\mathsf{WKL}
.
Hirst
[
19
, Theorem 3.23]
studied Dilworth’s theorem in reverse mathematics and proved that it is equivalent to
\mathsf{WKL}
over
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}
, even restricted to partial orders of width 2.

Proposition 2.2

(Hirst
[
19
, Theorem 3.23]
)
.

Over
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}
, Dilworth’s theorem is equivalent to
\mathsf{WKL}
.

Seeing a partial graph of finite width as a degenerate instance of
\mathsf{BRT}^{2}_{2}
, we are interested in the existence of an infinite
\mathcal{P}
-chain. Given a chain partition
g:\mathbb{N}\to k
, a final application of
\mathsf{RT}^{1}
suffices to obtain such a chain. Therefore,
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+\mathsf{WKL}+\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{2}
is sufficient to prove that every partial order with finite width admits an infinite chain. Dilworth’s theorem states that the chain partition of
\mathcal{P}
equals its width, but for our purpose, it is sufficient to consider the following weaker statement.

Statement

(Weak Dilworth’s theorem)
.

“For every partial order
\mathcal{P}=(\mathbb{N},<_{\mathcal{P}})
with finite width, there is some
k\in\mathbb{N}
and a coloring
g:\mathbb{N}\to k
such that for every
i<k
,
g^{-1}(i)
is a
\mathcal{P}
-chain.”

By Schmerl
[
33
]
, if
2\leq k\leq m
, then the statement “Every locally
k
-colorable graph is
m
-colorable” is equivalent to
\mathsf{WKL}
over
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}
. Based on the proof of Dilworth’s theorem, one would naturally expect that weak Dilworth’s theorem remains equivalent to
\mathsf{WKL}
over
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}
. Surprisingly, Kierstead
[
23
]
proved that every computable graph with width
w
admits a computable chain partition of size
(5^{w}-1)/4
.

Theorem 2.3

(Kierstead
[
23
]
)
.

Weak Dilworth’s theorem is computably true.

The original proof by Kierstead
[
23
]
involved strong induction axioms, but in their study of a theorem from Rival and Sands
[
32
]
, Fiori-Carones, Marcone, Shafer, Soldà, with the help of Yokoyama, adapted the proof of Kierstead to formalize it over
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}
(see
[
14
, Theorem 3.5]
).

Proposition 2.4

(Fiori-Carones et al.
[
14
]
)
.

\mathsf{RCA}_{0}
proves weak Dilworth’s theorem.

Kierstead’s theorem can be stated in the modern formalism as the existence of an on-line algorithm for the chain partition problem, where an
on-line algorithm
is one that produces its output progressively, as it receives its input, without having access to the entire input in advance. The bound
(5^{w}-1)/4
obtained by Kierstead was recently improved by Bosek and Krawczyk
[
5
,
6
]
to
w^{14\log w}
and by Bosek et al
[
4
]
to
w^{6.5\log w+7}
. See
[
3
]
for a survey on the subject.

The proof of Kierstead’s theorem is complex, but in the case where the partial order is up-growing, there exist significantly simpler algorithms (see Felsner
[
12
]
). A presentation of a partial order is
up-growing
if each new element is maximal among the elements inputted so far. The following proposition is essentially a reformulation of Felsner’s algorithm
[
12
]
in our formalism, in which an up-growing partial order can be seen as a coloring
f:[\mathbb{N}]^{2}\to 2
which is transitive for color 0. Indeed, any such coloring can be seen as the partial order
\mathcal{P}=(\mathbb{N},<_{\mathcal{P}})
defined by
x<_{\mathcal{P}}y
iff
x<_{\mathbb{N}}y
and
f(x,y)=0
.

Proposition 2.5

(
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}
)
.

Fix some
k\in\mathbb{N}
. For every coloring
f:[\mathbb{N}]^{2}\to 2
which is transitive for color 0, and such that there is no
f
-homogeneous set for color 1 of some size
k
, there is a coloring
g:\mathbb{N}\to{k+1\choose 2}
such that for every
i<{k+1\choose 2}
,
g^{-1}(i)
is
f
-homogeneous for color 0.

Felsner’s algorithm
[
12
]
.

Fix
k
and
f
.
We first construct a uniformly
f
-computable sequence
(F^{1}_{s},\dots,F^{k-1}_{s})_{s\in\mathbb{N}}
such that for every
s\in\mathbb{N}
,

(1)

Each
F^{i}_{s}
is a set of size at most
i
, containing finite
f
-homogeneous sets for color 0;

(2)

F^{1}_{s}\cup\dots\cup F^{k-1}_{s}
forms a partition of
[0,s)
, that is, for every
x<s
, there is exactly one
i
and one
H\in F^{i}_{s}
such that
x\in H
;

(3)

For each
F^{i}_{s}
, the set
\{\max H:H\in F^{i}_{s}\}
is
f
-homogeneous for color 1.

At stage 0,
F^{1}_{0}=\dots=F^{k-1}_{0}=\emptyset
.
At stage
s+1
, assume
F^{1}_{s},\dots F^{k-1}_{s}
are defined.
Let
i\in\{1,\dots,k\}
be the least index such that either
\operatorname{card}F^{i}_{s}<i
, or there is some
H\in F^{i}_{s}
such that
f(\max H,s)=0
. Such an index exists as otherwise, by (3),
\{\max H:H\in F^{k-1}_{s}\}\cup\{s\}
would form an
f
-homogeneous set for color 1 of size
k
, contradicting our hypothesis. We have two cases:

Case 1: there is some
H\in F^{i}_{s}
such that
f(\max H,s)=0
. Then let
\hat{H}=H\cup\{s\}
. If
i=1
, then, letting
F^{i}_{s+1}=(F^{i}_{s}\setminus\{H\})\cup\{\hat{H}\}
, and
F^{j}_{s+1}=F^{j}_{s}
for
j\neq i
, the new tuple satisfies properties (1-3).
If
i>1
, then, letting
F^{i-1}_{s+1}=F^{i}_{s}\setminus\{H\}
,
F^{i}_{s+1}=F^{i-1}_{s}\cup\{\hat{H}\}
, and
F^{j}_{s+1}=F^{j}_{s}
for
j\not\in\{i-1,i\}
, the new tuple satisfies properties (1-3).

Case 2: there is no such
H
. Then
\operatorname{card}F^{i}_{s}<i
and, letting
F^{i}_{s+1}=F^{i}_{s}\cup\{\{s\}\}
and
F^{j}_{s+1}=F^{j}_{s}
for
j\neq i
, the new tuple satisfies properties (1-3). This completes the construction of the sequence.

Let
g:\mathbb{N}\to{k+1\choose 2}
be defined inductively as follows:
g(0)=0
. Given
x

g(x)=g(y)
for any
y<x
such that for some
i\in\{1,\dots,k\}
and some

,

, if it exists. Otherwise, let
g(x)
be the least fresh color. By construction and by (1), there are at most
\sum_{i\in\{1,\dots,k\}}i=\frac{(1+k)k}{2}={k+1\choose 2}
colors. Moreover, if
g(x)=g(y)
, then there is some
f
-homogeneous set
H
for color 0 such that
x,y\in H
, so

2.2
Mirsky’s theorem

Dilworth’s theorem admits a dual version for partial orders with chains of bounded size, due to Mirsky
[
29
]
.
The

of a partial order
\mathcal{P}=(\mathbb{N},<_{\mathcal{P}})
is the size of its largest
\mathcal{P}
-chain.

Statement

(Mirsky’s theorem)
.

“For every partial order
\mathcal{P}=(\mathbb{N},<_{\mathcal{P}})
with finite height, there is a coloring
g:\mathbb{N}\to h(\mathcal{P})
such that for every

g^{-1}(i)
is a
\mathcal{P}
-antichain.”

Accordingly, such a coloring

of

. Contrary to Dilworth’s theorem, Mirsky’s theorem admits a trivial combinatorial proof: Given a partial order
\mathcal{P}=(\mathbb{N},<_{\mathcal{P}})
with finite height

, define

as follows:
g(x)
is the size of the largest
\mathcal{P}
-chain with
x
as
\mathcal{P}
-maximal element. However, from a reverse mathematical viewpoint, the definition of
g
requires an unbounded search for such a
\mathcal{P}
-chain, and is formalized over
\mathsf{ACA}_{0}
. Hirst
[
19
]
actually showed that Mirsky’s theorem is equivalent to

(and therefore to Dilworth’s theorem) over

, even when restricted to partial orders of height 2.

Proposition 2.7

(Hirst
[
19
, Theorem 3.24]
)
.

Over
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}
, Mirsky’s theorem is equivalent to
\mathsf{WKL}
.

The proof of Mirsky’s theorem over

Statement

“For every partial order
\mathcal{P}=(\mathbb{N},<_{\mathcal{P}})

k\in\mathbb{N}
and a coloring

The natural combinatorial proof of Mirsky’s theorem can be refined, to yield a proof of weak Mirsky’s theorem over

Proof.

Before constructing such an instance, let us introduce some basic notions about models of arithmetic with restricted induction, and the relation between failure of induction and cuts. See Hajek and Pudlak
[

]
for a more extensive introduction.

3.1
Models of weak arithmetic and cuts

A
structure
in the language of second-order arithmetic is a tuple

\mathcal{M}=(M,S,0,1,+,\times,<)

where
M
denotes the set of integers,
S\subseteq\mathcal{P}(S)
is the collection of sets of integers,
0
and
1
are constants in
M
, and
+,\times:M^{2}\to M
are binary operations and
<
is a binary relation on
M
.
We shall exclusively work with structures which are models of
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}
, and simply call them
models
, and usually abbreviate them by
\mathcal{M}=(M,S)
.
A model
\mathcal{M}=(M,S)
is
topped
by a set
Y\in S
if every set in
S
is
\Delta_{1}(Y)
-definable with parameters in
M
.

A model where
M
is the set of standard integers
\omega=\{0,1,\dots\}
will be called an
\omega
-model
, if
M\neq\omega
, then the model is said to be
non-standard
.

Definition 3.1

(cut)
.

A
cut
in a model
\mathcal{M}=(M,S,0,1,+,\times,<)
is a nonempty subset
I\subseteq M
which is closed by successor, and is an initial segment of
M
, that is, if
a\in I
and
b\leq a
, then
b\in I
.

In a non-standard model, the elements
0,1,1+1,1+1+1,\dots
form a cut which can be identified with
\omega
. The amount of induction satisfied by a model corresponds to the difficulty of having cuts that are definable.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{M}\models\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{n}
for some
n
, then
\mathcal{M}\models\neg\mathsf{I}\Sigma^{0}_{n}
if and only if there exists some
\Sigma_{n}^{0}
formula
\phi(x)
, such that the set
\{x:\phi(x)\}
forms a proper cut.

Finite/infinite sets
. In models of weak arithmetic, the various definitions of infinity are not necessarily equivalent, and require a precise terminology.
A subset
X\subseteq M
is said to be
M
-bounded
if there exists some
y\in M
such that
x<y
for every
x\in X
. If no such
y
exists,
X
is said to be
M
-unbounded
. In a model
\mathcal{M}=(M,S)\models\mathsf{RCA}_{0}
, the
M
-bounded sets belonging to
S
will be called
\mathcal{M}
-finite
and the
M
-unbounded
\mathcal{M}
-infinite
.

Every element

can be seen as the binary encoding of some
M
-bounded set
F\subseteq M
and we write
a=\sum_{x\in F}2^{x}
. Not all
M
-bounded sets can be
M
-coded by some integer

, but if
\mathcal{M}=(M,S)\models\mathsf{RCA}_{0}
, then the
M
-coded sets are exactly the
\mathcal{M}
-finite, hence, the
\mathcal{M}
-finite sets only depends on the first order part
M
of the model
\mathcal{M}
and we will call them
M
-finite
to emphasize on that.

Cofinal functions
. The lack of induction can also be measured in the existence of definable cofinal functions from a proper cut to
M
. This will be of central importance for proving
1.4
.
For
I\subseteq M
a cut, a function

is said to be
cofinal (in
M
)
if its range is
M
-unbounded.

Proposition 3.3
.

If
\mathcal{M}\models\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{n}+\neg\mathsf{I}\Sigma^{0}_{n}
for some

, then there exists some increasing
\Sigma_{n}^{0}
-definable cofinal function

In particular, if
\mathcal{M}=(M,S)\models\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{2}+\neg\mathsf{I}\Sigma^{0}_{2}
and

is a
\Sigma^{0}_{2}
-cut bounded by some

, one can assume that the function

can be approximated by a
\Delta^{0}_{1}

such that for every

,

is increasing, and for every

, and converges to

.

3.2
Main result

In what follows, a
\Sigma_{n}(X)
-formula (respectively a
\Pi_{n}(X)
-formula or
\Delta_{n}(X)
-formula) is a
\Sigma_{n}^{0}
-formula (respectively a
\Pi_{n}^{0}
-formula or
\Delta_{n}^{0}
-formula) with only

as a second-order parameter and any first-order parameters.

The core of the argument lies in the following technical lemma:

Lemma 3.4

(
\mathsf{I}\Sigma^{0}_{1}
)
.

For every set
X
, every
b\in\mathbb{N}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a
\Delta_{1}(X)

f_{n}^{b}:[\mathbb{N}]^{2}\to 2
such that:

•

There is no set of size
3
that is
f_{n}^{b}
-homogeneous for the color
1
.

•

For every
e<n
such that
W^{X}_{e}
is infinite, there exists some
x,y\in W^{X}_{e}
such that
b\leq x<y
and
f(x,y)=1
.

Furthermore, the construction is uniform in
n
and
b
.

Proof.

The coloring
f_{n}^{b}
will be defined by stages, with
f_{n}^{b}(x,s)
being defined at stage
s
for every
x<s
. At any point of the construction, any
x\in\mathbb{N}
will be in state “0” or “1”, meaning that at any further stage
s
,
f(x,s)
will be set accordingly. By default, every
x\in\mathbb{N}
has the state “0”. We consider a trash
T_{s}\subseteq[0,s]
where the elements leaving state “1” will be put, to ensure that they will never go back to that state. Our construction will ensure that
|T_{s}|\leq n^{2}
at every stage
s
.

For every
e<n
, we say that
e
is
active
at a stage
s
if
\operatorname{card}(W^{X}_{e}[s]\cap[b,s])>n^{2}
. Once an element is active, it stays active for the rest of the construction.

Construction:
at stage
s
, if there exists some new
e<n
that became active at this stage, we then put every
x<s
that is in state “1” in state “0” and put them in the trash
T_{s}
. Then, for every
e^{\prime}<n
that is active, we pick an element in
W^{X}_{e^{\prime}}[s]\cap[b,s]\setminus T_{s}
an put it in state “1”. If no such
e<n
was found, then the all the elements stay in the same state and no new element is added to the trash.

Finally, whether or not a new
e
was found, we let
f(x,s)=c
for every
c<2
and every
x<s
in state “c”.

Claim 1
:
T_{s}\leq n^{2}
for every
s\in\mathbb{N}
, hence the construction is well-defined. Indeed, there are always at most
n
elements in state “1” at a given time (one per active
e
). And such elements are put into the trash at most
n
times during the construction (once every time a new
e<n
became active). Therefore, we can always pick an element in
W^{X}_{e^{\prime}}[s]\cap[b,s]\setminus T_{s}
if
W^{X}_{e^{\prime}}
is active at stage
s
.

Claim 2
: There is no set of size
3
that is
f_{n}^{b}
-homogeneous for the color
1
. Assume by contradiction that we have such a set
\{x<y<z\}
. By construction,
x
was in state “1” at stage

for

to be equal to
1
. Then, for
f(y,z)
to be equal to
1
, we need
y
to be in state “1” at stage

, but
y
can only enter state “1” after stage
y
, which would put
x
in the trash, and make it so that
f(x,z)=0
.

Claim 3
: For every
e<n
such that
W^{X}_{e}
is infinite, there exists some
x,y\in W^{X}_{e}
such that
b\leq x<y
and
f(x,y)=1
. If
W^{X}_{e}
is infinite, then by
\mathsf{I}\Sigma^{0}_{1}
, there is some stage
s
such that
\operatorname{card}(W^{X}_{e}[s]\cap[b,s])>n^{2}
, hence
e
is eventually active. Then, for every

, there will be some

that will be in state “1” at stage

, which gives

. Since

is infinite, there is one such

W^{X}_{e}
.

∎

4.2
Combinatorics of
\mathsf{ADS}

In order to get a better grasp of the appropriate notion of immunity to consider, let us dig into the combinatorics of
\mathsf{ADS}
. There exists mainly two forcing constructions to build solutions to
\mathsf{ADS}
. The first one is asymmetric, and was used by Hirschfeldt and Shore
[
18
, Proposition 2.26]
to prove that
\mathsf{ADS}
does not imply the existence of DNC functions over
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}
. A function
g:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
is
diagonally non-
X
-computable
(
X
-DNC) if for every
e\in\mathbb{N}
,
g(e)\neq\Phi^{X}_{e}(e)
. The second forcing construction is symmetric, and was defined by Lerman, Solomon and Towsner
[
27
]
and later used by Patey
[
31
]
to give an alternative proof of
[
18
, Proposition 2.26]
.

The combinatorics of
\mathsf{ADS}
are already witnessed by stable linear orders, so let us fix a computable stable linear order
\mathcal{L}=(\mathbb{N},<_{\mathbb{N}})
. If it is of order type
\omega+k
or
k+\omega^{*}
, then there is an infinite computable
\mathcal{L}
-ascending or
\mathcal{L}
-descending sequence, so let us assume that it is of order type
\omega+\omega^{*}
. Let
U^{0}
and
U^{1}
be its sets of
\mathcal{L}
-small and
\mathcal{L}
-large elements, respectively. Note that
U^{0}\sqcup U^{1}=\mathbb{N}
and both sets are
\Delta^{0}_{2}
.

The natural notion of forcing for building solutions to
\mathcal{L}
is of disjunctive nature. A
condition
is a pair
(\sigma^{0},\sigma^{1})
such that
\sigma^{0}
and
\sigma^{1}
are finite
\mathcal{L}
-ascending and
\mathcal{L}
-descending sequences, respectively. To ensure extensibility of each initial segment, one furthermore requires that
\sigma^{0}\subseteq U^{0}
and
\sigma^{1}\subseteq U^{1}
. Because of this restriction, being a condition is a
\Delta^{0}_{2}
predicate. A condition
(\tau^{0},\tau^{1})

extends

(\sigma^{0},\sigma^{1})
if
\sigma^{i}\preceq\tau^{i}
for both
i<2
.

Thankfully, one can define a computable notion of
split pair
, which is a pair
(\sigma^{0},\sigma^{1})
such that
\sigma^{0}
is
\mathcal{L}
-ascending,
\sigma^{1}
is
\mathcal{L}
-descending, and
\max_{\mathcal{L}}\sigma^{0}<_{\mathcal{L}}\min_{\mathcal{L}}\sigma^{1}
. The core of the combinatorics of
\mathsf{ADS}
lies in the following fact:

Lemma 4.1
.

If
(\sigma^{0},\sigma^{1})
is a condition and
(\tau^{0},\tau^{1})
is a split pair such that
\sigma^{i}\preceq\tau^{i}
for both
i<2
, then either
(\tau^{0},\sigma^{1})
or
(\sigma^{0},\tau^{1})
is a valid condition extending
(\sigma^{0},\sigma^{1})
.

Proof.

Since
\max_{\mathcal{L}}\sigma^{0}<_{\mathcal{L}}\min_{\mathcal{L}}\sigma^{1}
, either
\tau^{0}\subseteq U^{0}
or
\tau^{1}\subseteq U^{1}
.
∎

Let us explain how the combinatorics above enable to have a short c.e. description of large subsets of
G
-c.e. sets, where
G
is the constructed solution. Fix a condition
p=(\sigma^{0},\sigma^{1})
and two Turing indices
e_{0},e_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
a_{0},a_{1}:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
be defined as follows: for every
t\in\mathbb{N}
,
W_{a_{0}(t)}
and
W_{a_{1}(t)}
search for a split pair
(\tau^{0},\tau^{1})
below
p
such that
\operatorname{card}W^{\tau^{0}}_{e_{0}}\geq t
and
\operatorname{card}W^{\tau^{1}}_{e_{1}}\geq t
. If such a split pair exists, then
W_{a_{0}(t)}=W^{\tau^{0}}_{e_{0}}
and
W_{a_{1}(t)}=W^{\tau^{1}}_{e_{1}}
.
By the combinatorics above, for every
t
, if such a pair exists, then there is an extension
q
of
p
forcing either
W_{a_{0}(t)}\subseteq W^{G^{0}}_{e_{0}}
or
W_{a_{1}(t)}\subseteq W^{G^{1}}_{e_{1}}
. Otherwise, there is an extension
q
of
p
forcing either
\operatorname{card}W^{G^{0}}_{e_{0}}<t
or
\operatorname{card}W^{G^{1}}_{e_{1}}<t
. Last, note that
p
,
e_{0}
and
e_{1}
being fixed, the functions
a_{0}
and
a_{1}
have a growth in
\mathcal{O}(t)
for an appropriate numbering.

Such combinatorics are particularly useful when dealing with preservation of effective notions of immunity.

Definition 4.2
.

Fix a function
h:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
and a set

A set

is said to be

-effectively

We simply say that

is

-effectively immune for a

Arslanov, Nadirov and Solov’ev
[

]
proved that every effectively immune set computes a DNC function, and conversely, Jockusch
[

]
proved that every DNC function computes an effectively immune set, witnessed by the identity function. Let

be such an effectively immune set. By slightly modifying the above argument, using Kleene’s fixpoint theorem, one can easily obtain indices
a_{0}
and

such that
W_{a_{0}}
and

search for a split pair

below

In our setting, the appropriate notion of immunity will be the following variant of effective immunity. Let

be the tetration function defined inductively as follows:

.

Fix a set

\Delta_{2}(Z)

is
B

4.3
Effective constructions over
\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{2}+\neg\mathsf{I}\Sigma^{0}_{2}

The proofs of
Propositions

4.4
and
4.17
are done by effectivizations of forcing constructions over non-standard models. By an isomorphism theorem of Fiori-Carones, Kołodziejczyk, Wong and Yokoyama
[
13
]
, there are some deep reasons why, given a
\Pi^{1}_{2}
-problem
\mathsf{P}
, any
\omega
-extension of a model of
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{2}+\neg\mathsf{I}\Sigma^{0}_{2}
into a model of
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{2}+\mathsf{P}
should be through a first-jump control construction effective in any PA degree over
\emptyset^{\prime}
. This will be in particular the case in our construction.

Effective constructions in models of arithmetic with restricted induction raise some new issues.
Indeed, given a countable model
\mathcal{M}=(M,S)
, suppose one builds a set
G\subseteq M
satisfying a countable sequence of requirements
(\mathcal{R}_{e})_{e\in M}
using an effectivized forcing argument. Let
(\mathbb{P},\leq)
be the associated notion of forcing. In order to effectivize the argument, each condition
p\in\mathbb{P}
is given a code
\langle p\rangle\in\mathbb{N}
and the decreasing sequence of conditions must be represented by a
\emptyset^{\prime}
-effective decreasing sequence of codes.

In the standard setting, that is,
M=\omega
, one can consider each requirement
\mathcal{R}_{e}
individually, prove a density lemma saying that every condition
p
has an extension
q
forcing
\mathcal{R}_{e}
, and then build an infinite descending sequence of conditions
p_{0}\geq p_{1}\geq\dots
such that
p_{n+1}
forces
\mathcal{R}_{n}
. On the other hand, if
\mathcal{M}\models\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{2}+\neg\mathsf{I}\Sigma^{0}_{2}
, it might be the case that the decreasing sequence is defined only on a proper
\Sigma^{0}_{2}
-cut
I\subseteq M
, that is, only
(p_{i})_{i\in I}
is defined and only the requirements
(\mathcal{R}_{i})_{i\in I}
are satisfied. Note that in this case, the sequence of codes
(\langle p_{i}\rangle)_{i\in I}
is unbounded in
M
, otherwise the cut
I
would admit a
\Delta^{0}_{2}
-description, contradicting the fact that
\mathcal{M}\models\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{2}
.

To overcome this issue, instead of proving a density lemma for each requirement
\mathcal{R}_{e}
individually, we will prove a stronger lemma, stating that for every
k\in M
and every condition
p
, there is an extension
q
forcing
\mathcal{R}_{e}
for every
e<k
simultaneously. Then, using this density lemma, build a decreasing sequence of conditions
p_{0}\geq p_{1}\geq\dots
such that
p_{i+1}
forces
\mathcal{R}_{e}
for every
e<\langle p_{i}\rangle
simultaneously. Even if the sequence is only defined on a proper cut
I
, all the requirements
(\mathcal{R}_{e})_{e\in M}
will be forced. This technique is known as
Shore blocking
.

4.5
Combinatorics of
\mathsf{COH}

In order to prove the counterpart of
Proposition

4.4
for
\mathsf{COH}
, let us focus on the combinatorics of
\mathsf{COH}
.
As mentioned, there is a correspondence between
\mathsf{COH}
and
\mathsf{WKL}
for
\Delta^{0}_{2}
-functionals, which can be formalized over
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{2}
. It is important to elaborate on this correspondence to get a better grasp on the specificities of the
\mathsf{COH}
step.

Given a uniformly computable sequence of sets
\vec{R}=R_{0},R_{1},\dots
, the construction of an
\vec{R}
-cohesive set can be understood as the interleaving of two kinds of operations: the
growing
operation which consists of adding new elements to the cohesive set, and the
cohesiveness
operation which, given a set
R_{n}
, decides whether the remainder of the construction should commit to adding only elements from
R_{n}
or from
\overline{R}_{n}
.
The possible sequences of cohesiveness decisions can be represented as the infinite paths through a
\Sigma_{1}(\vec{R})
binary tree as follows:

Definition 4.13
.

For every instance
\vec{R}=R_{0},R_{1},\dots
of
\mathsf{COH}
and every
\rho\in 2^{<M}
, we write
\vec{R}_{\rho}:=\bigcap_{\rho(i)=0}\overline{R}_{i}\cap\bigcap_{\rho(i)=1}R_{i}
and let

to be the
\Sigma_{1}(\vec{R})
-tree
\{\rho\in 2^{<M}:\exists x(x>|\rho|\wedge x\in\vec{R}_{\rho})\}
.

Note that a node
\rho
is extensible in
T(\vec{R})
iff
\vec{R}_{\rho}
is infinite. Any infinite
\vec{R}
-cohesive set
C
gives a
\Delta_{2}(C)
-definition of a path
P
through
T(\vec{R})
by letting
x\in P
iff
C\subseteq^{*}R_{x}
. Conversely, Jockusch and Stephan
[
20
]
showed that every degree whose jump computes a path through
T(\vec{R})
computes an infinite
\vec{R}
-cohesive set.

It follows that there exists a natural notion of forcing for producing
\vec{R}
-cohesive sets, using a restriction of computable Mathias forcing to reservoirs which are boolean combinations of the sets of the instance. This notion of forcing is parameterized by a fixed path
P\in[T(\vec{R})]
.

Definition 4.14
.

A
condition
is a pair of binary strings
(\sigma,\rho)
such that
\rho\prec P
.

One can think of a condition
(\sigma,\rho)
as the computable Mathias condition
(\sigma,\vec{R}_{\rho}\setminus\{0,\dots,|\sigma|\})
. The notion of extension is defined accordingly: a condition
(\tau,\mu)
extends
(\sigma,\rho)
if
\sigma\preceq\tau
,
\rho\preceq\mu
and
\tau\setminus\sigma\subseteq\vec{R}_{\rho}
.

By many means, the situation for
\mathsf{COH}
is much simpler than for
\mathsf{SADS}
: the notion of forcing for
\mathsf{COH}
admits a first-jump control very similar to Cohen forcing or computable Mathias forcing. In particular, it is non-disjunctive.
There is however a new difficulty: the set of conditions is
P
-computable, and therefore the overall construction will be
P\oplus\emptyset^{\prime}
-computable instead of
\emptyset^{\prime}
-computable. One can easily prove that the use of
P
is necessary. Indeed, if a computable instance
\vec{R}
of
\mathsf{COH}
admits a solution of low degree
\mathbf{d}
, then
\mathbf{d}^{\prime}
computes a path through
T(\vec{R})
, hence the tree admits a
\emptyset^{\prime}
-computable path, and therefore there is a computable
\vec{R}
-cohesive set.

Working in a model
\mathcal{M}=(M,S)\models\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{2}+\neg\mathsf{I}\Sigma^{0}_{2}
topped by a set
Y
, the statement “the construction can be pursued for
s
steps” will be
\Sigma_{1}(P\oplus Y^{\prime})
instead of
\Sigma_{2}(Y)
. The corresponding cut
I
will be
\Sigma_{1}(P\oplus Y^{\prime})
, and if
P
is arbitrary, the sequence of value
(k_{s})_{s\in I}
will not necessarily be
M
-unbounded, as this would yield a
\Delta_{1}(P\oplus Y^{\prime})
-definition of
I
, which is not a contradiction. One must therefore ensure that
\mathcal{M}[P\oplus Y^{\prime}]\models\mathsf{I}\Delta^{0}_{1}
to make the construction work. Thankfully, this is the case if the path
P
is chosen with some care.

Let
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}^{*}
be the theory
\mathsf{P}^{-}
together with the
\Delta^{0}_{1}
-induction scheme, the
\Delta^{0}_{1}
-comprehension scheme, and the statement of the totality of the exponential (
\exp
). In particular,
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}^{*}\vdash\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{1}
, and if
\mathcal{M}=(M,S)
is a model of
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{2}
, then
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}=(M,\Delta_{2}\mbox{-}\mathsf{Def}(\mathcal{M}))
is a model of
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}^{*}
. The theory
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}^{*}
was introduced by Simpson and Smith
[
36
]
, who proved the following theorem:

Theorem 4.15

(Simpson–Smith
[
36
]
)
.

Every countable model of
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}^{*}
can be
\omega
-extended into a model of
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}^{*}+\mathsf{WKL}
.

This is exactly what we need to prove the existence of a path
P
such that
\mathcal{M}[P\oplus Y^{\prime}]\models\mathsf{I}\Delta^{0}_{1}
.

Lemma 4.16
.

Let
\mathcal{M}=(M,S)\models\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{2}
be a countable model topped by some set
Y
. For every instance
\vec{R}
of
\mathsf{COH}
in
S
, there exists some path
P\in[T(\vec{R})]
such that
\mathcal{M}[P\oplus Y^{\prime}]\models\mathsf{RCA}_{0}^{*}
.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{M}\models\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{2}
,
\mathcal{M}[Y^{\prime}]\models\mathsf{RCA}_{0}^{*}
.
The tree
T(\vec{R})
is
\Delta_{1}(Y^{\prime})
and is therefore contained in
\mathcal{M}[Y^{\prime}]
. Furthermore, by
\Delta_{1}(Y^{\prime})
-induction (which holds since
\mathcal{M}[Y^{\prime}]\models\mathsf{RCA}_{0}^{*}
), one can prove that for every
k\in M
, there is some
\rho
of length
k
such that
\vec{R}_{\rho}
is
M
-unbounded. It follows that
T(\vec{R})
is
M
-unbounded and is therefore an instance of
\mathsf{WKL}
in
\mathcal{M}[Y^{\prime}]
.
Since
\mathcal{M}[Y^{\prime}]\models\mathsf{RCA}_{0}^{*}
, by
Theorem

4.15
, there exists some path
P
in
T(\vec{R})
such that
\mathcal{M}[P\oplus Y^{\prime}]\models\mathsf{RCA}_{0}^{*}
.
∎

4.6
The
\mathsf{COH}
step

We now prove the existence of
\omega
-extensions which add solutions to instances of
\mathsf{COH}
while preserving the fact that the instance of
\mathsf{BRT}^{2}_{2}
has no solution.

Proposition 4.17
.

Let
\mathcal{M}=(M,S)\models\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{2}
be a countable model topped by some set
Y
. Let
B
be an
M
-unbounded
\Delta_{2}(Y)
set and fix
n\in\omega
. Then, for every set
A\subseteq M
that is
B
-block
Y
-immune with cost
n
, every instance
\vec{R}
of
\mathsf{COH}
in
S
and every path
P\in[T(\vec{R})]
such that
\mathcal{M}[P\oplus Y^{\prime}]\models\mathsf{RCA}_{0}^{*}
, there exists some
M
-unbounded set
G\subseteq M
and some
M
-unbounded
P\oplus Y^{\prime}
-computable set
\hat{B}
such that:

•

G
is
\vec{R}
-cohesive

•

P\oplus Y^{\prime}\equiv_{T}(G\oplus Y)^{\prime}
and in particular
\mathcal{M}[G]\models\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{2}

•

A
is
\hat{B}
-block
(G\oplus Y)
-immune with cost
n+2
.

Proof.

We will define the set
G
using an infinite
\Delta_{1}(P\oplus Y^{\prime})
decreasing sequence of Mathias-like conditions. As in
Proposition

4.4
, we will assume that all the elements of
B
are of the form
2^{2^{k}}
for some
k\in M
.

Definition 4.18
.

A
condition
is a pair of
M
-finite binary strings
(\sigma,\rho)
such that
\rho\prec P
.
We have
(\sigma_{2},\rho_{2})\leq(\sigma_{1},\rho_{1})
if
\sigma_{2}\succeq\sigma_{1}
,
\rho_{2}\succeq\rho_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}-\sigma_{1}\subseteq\vec{R}_{\rho_{1}}
(where
\sigma_{2}-\sigma_{1}
is the difference between the sets corresponding to the two sequences).

As mentioned, one can think of a condition
(\sigma,\rho)
as a Mathias condition
(\sigma,\vec{R}_{\rho})
. Requiring that
\rho\prec P
ensures that
\vec{R}_{\rho}
is
\mathcal{M}
-infinite. When increasing the length of
\rho
, we make progress in cohesiveness.
Note that being a condition is a
\Delta_{1}(P)
predicate, as it simply requires checking that
\rho\prec P
, while the extension relation is
\Delta_{1}(Y)
.

A sequence
\tau
is said to be
compatible
with a condition
(\sigma,\rho)
if
\sigma\preceq\tau
and
\tau-\sigma\subseteq\vec{R}_{\rho}
.

Definition 4.19
.

Let
(\sigma,\rho)
be a condition, and
e,x\in M
, we write:

1.

(\sigma,\rho)\Vdash\Phi^{G\oplus Y}_{e}(x)\downarrow
if
\Phi^{\sigma\oplus Y}_{e}(x)[t]\downarrow

2.

(\sigma,\rho)\Vdash\Phi^{G\oplus Y}_{e}(x)\uparrow
if for every
\tau
compatible with
(\sigma,\rho)
,
\Phi^{\tau\oplus Y}_{e}(x)\uparrow
.

Note that the relations
(\sigma,\rho)\Vdash\Phi^{G\oplus Y}_{e}(x)\downarrow

and
(\sigma,\rho)\Vdash\Phi^{G\oplus Y}_{e}(x)\uparrow
are
\Delta_{0}(Y)
and
\Pi_{1}(Y)
, respectively.

To ensure that
P\oplus Y^{\prime}\geq_{T}(G\oplus Y)^{\prime}
and that
A
is
\hat{B}
-block
(G\oplus Y)
-immune with cost
n+2
, we will also construct
(G\oplus Y)^{\prime}
and
\hat{B}
in parallel.

For this, we will need to satisfy three kind of requirements for every
k\in M
:

•

\mathcal{R}_{k}
: there exists some
\sigma^{\prime}\in 2^{k}
such that
(G\oplus Y)^{\prime}\upharpoonright_{k}=\sigma^{\prime}
.

•

\mathcal{S}_{k}
: there exists some
\ell>2^{2^{k}}
in
B
, such that, for every
x<\log_{2}(\log_{2}(\ell))
, if
|W^{G\oplus Y}_{x}|\geq 2_{n}(\ell)
, then there is some
e<\ell
such that
|W^{Y}_{e}|\geq 2_{n}(\ell)
and
W^{Y}_{e}\subseteq W_{x}^{G\oplus Y}
.

•

\mathcal{T}_{k}
:
\exists x>k,x\in G
and
G\subseteq^{*}\vec{R}_{P\upharpoonright k}

The notations
(\sigma,\rho)\Vdash\mathcal{R}_{k}
and
(\sigma,\rho)\Vdash\mathcal{S}_{k}
are defined similarly as in
Proposition

4.4
.

Lemma 4.20
.

Let
(\sigma,\rho)
be a condition. For every
k\in M
, there exists some
M
-finite
\sigma^{\prime}\in 2^{k}
and an extension
(\tau,\rho)\leq(\sigma,\rho)
such that
(\tau,\rho)\Vdash(G\oplus Y)^{\prime}\upharpoonright_{k}=\sigma^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Same as
[
25
, Lemma 2.11]
.
∎

Lemma 4.21
.

For every condition
(\sigma,\rho)
and every
k\in M
, there exists some extension
(\tau,\rho)\leq(\sigma,\rho)
such that
(\tau,\rho)\Vdash\mathcal{S}_{k}
.

Proof.

For every finite set
F\subset M
, every
x\in F
and every
t\in M
, let
W_{e(\langle F,x,t\rangle)}^{Y}
search the smallest
\tau
compatible with
(\sigma,\rho)
such that
|W^{\tau\oplus Y}_{x^{\prime}}|\geq 2_{n+2}(t)
for every
x^{\prime}\in F
and output the elements of
W^{\tau\oplus Y}_{x}
.

Let
S:M\to M
be the function which send
t
to the biggest value taken by
e(\langle F,x,t\rangle)
for every
F\subseteq t
and
x\in t
. Assuming a reasonable encoding of
e
, this function grows slower than
t\mapsto 2^{2^{t}}
, indeed, every
F\subseteq t
and every
x\in t
can be encoded using less than
\alpha t
bits for some
\alpha\in\omega
and the same holds for the corresponding value taken by
e
.

Hence, there exists some
\ell\in B
such that
\ell>2^{2^{k}}
and such that
\ell^{\prime}:=\log_{2}(\log_{2}(\ell))
satisfies
S(\ell^{\prime})<2^{2^{\ell^{\prime}}}=\ell
.

Let
S=\{F\subseteq\ell^{\prime}:(\forall x\in F)|W_{e(\langle F,x,\ell^{\prime}\rangle)}^{Y}|\geq 2_{n+2}(\ell^{\prime})\}
, the set
S
is
\Sigma_{1}(Y)
, non empty (contains
\emptyset
) and bounded, hence, by
\mathsf{I}\Sigma^{0}_{1}
we can pick some
F\in S
maximal for the inclusion.

Since
F\in S
, there exists some
\tau
compatible with
(\sigma,\rho)
such that
|W^{\tau\oplus Y}_{x}|\geq 2_{n+2}(\ell^{\prime})=2_{n}(\ell)
for every
x\in F
. The condition
(\tau,\rho)
thus forces
|W^{G\oplus Y}_{x}|\geq 2_{n}(\ell)
for every
x\in F
and, by the maximality assumption, forces
|W^{G\oplus Y}_{x}|<2_{n}(\ell)
for every
x\in\{0,\dots,\ell^{\prime}-1\}\setminus F
, as otherwise
F\cup\{x\}
would be in
S
. Moreover, for every
x\in F
, we have
|W_{e(\langle F,x,\ell^{\prime}\rangle)}^{Y}|\geq 2_{n}(\ell)
and
W_{e(\langle F,x,\ell^{\prime}\rangle)}^{Y}=W^{\tau\oplus Y}_{x}\subseteq W^{G\oplus Y}_{x}
.

Thus,
(\tau,\rho)\Vdash\mathcal{S}_{k}
.
∎

Lemma 4.22
.

For every condition
(\sigma,\rho)
and every
k\in M
, there exists some extension
(\tau,\rho^{\prime})\leq(\sigma,\rho)
such that
(\tau,\rho^{\prime})\Vdash\mathcal{T}_{k}
.

Proof.

Since
\rho\prec P
, the set
\vec{R}_{\rho}
is
\mathcal{M}
-infinite and there exists some
\tau
compatible with
(\sigma,\rho)
such that
|\tau|\geq k
, making
(\tau,\rho)\Vdash\exists x\in G,x\geq k
. Then taking
\rho^{\prime}=P\upharpoonright k
ensures that every
G
in the cone generated by
(\tau,\rho^{\prime})
will be such that
G\setminus\tau\subseteq\vec{R}_{P\upharpoonright k}
, hence that
(\tau,\rho^{\prime})\Vdash\mathcal{T}_{k}
.
∎

Construction:
The construction will build the set
G
using a
P\oplus Y^{\prime}
-computable decreasing sequence
(\sigma_{s},\rho_{s})
of conditions. In parallel, the sets
(G\oplus Y)^{\prime}
and
\hat{B}
will be approached by sequences
(\sigma_{s}^{\prime})
and
(B_{s})
of finite chains.

Let
\sigma_{0}=\rho_{0}=\sigma_{0}^{\prime}=B_{0}=\emptyset
. Assume that
\sigma_{s},\rho_{s},\sigma_{s}^{\prime}
and
B_{s}
have been defined for some
s\in M
and let

k_{s}=1+\max\{|\sigma_{s}|,|\rho_{s}|,|\sigma_{s}^{\prime}|,|B_{s}|\}

Using
Lemma

4.20
,
Lemma

4.21
and
Lemma

4.22
, there exists some extension
(\tau,\rho^{\prime})\leq(\sigma_{s},\rho_{s})
forcing
\mathcal{R}_{k_{s}},\mathcal{S}_{k_{s}}
and
\mathcal{T}_{k_{s}}
. Furthermore, such an extension can be found
(P\oplus Y^{\prime})
-effectively. We then let
\sigma_{s+1}=\tau
,
\rho_{s+1}=\rho^{\prime}
,
\sigma^{\prime}_{s+1}=\sigma^{\prime}
where
\sigma^{\prime}
is the sequence obtained from the fact that
\mathcal{R}_{k_{s}}
is forced and let
B_{s+1}(x)=0
for every
x\in[|B_{s}|,\log_{2}(\log_{2}(\ell)))
and
B_{s+1}(\log_{2}(\log_{2}(\ell)))=1
where
\ell
is the bound obtained from the fact that
\mathcal{S}_{k_{s}}
is forced. We then proceed to the next stage of the construction.

The formula
\phi(s)
stating that this construction can be pursued for
s
steps (There exists a sequence
(\sigma_{0},\rho_{0},\sigma^{\prime}_{0},B_{0}),\dots,(\sigma_{s},\rho_{s},\sigma_{s}^{\prime},B_{s})
such that…) is equivalent to a
\Sigma_{1}(P\oplus Y^{\prime})
formula, thanks to the fact that the construction is
P\oplus Y^{\prime}
-computable. Since we are not necessarily in a model of
\mathsf{I}\Sigma^{0}_{1}(P\oplus Y^{\prime})
, there is no guarantee that
\phi(s)
holds for every
s\in M
, and in some cases, if will actually only hold on a proper cut
I
. Nevertheless, the sequence of value
(k_{s})_{s\in I}
obtained during the construction must be
M
-unbounded, otherwise the formula
\phi(s)
would be equivalent to a
\Delta_{1}(P\oplus Y^{\prime})
formula that would holds for every
s\in M
as
\mathcal{M}[P\oplus Y^{\prime}]\models\mathsf{I}\Delta^{0}_{1}(P\oplus Y^{\prime})
, contradicting the fact that the sequence is
M
-bounded. Since this sequence is not
M
-bounded, for every
k\in M
, the requirements
\mathcal{R}_{k}
,
\mathcal{S}_{k}
and
\mathcal{T}_{k}
are satisfied (to satisfy one of these requirements, it is sufficient to satisfy it for a value
k_{s}\geq k
).

Thus, this construction defines two sets
G=\bigcup_{s\in I}\sigma_{s}
and
\hat{B}=\bigcup_{s\in I}B_{s}
that are
(P\oplus Y^{\prime})
-computable and such that
(G\oplus Y)^{\prime}
is also
(P\oplus Y^{\prime})
-computable. We also get that
(G\oplus Y)^{\prime}\geq P
, indeed, using
(G\oplus Y)^{\prime}
, we can check if
G\subseteq^{*}R_{i}
or if
G\subseteq^{*}\bar{R_{i}}
for every
i\in M
and thus find the value of
P(i)
, hence
(G\oplus Y)^{\prime}\equiv_{T}P\oplus Y^{\prime}
and
\hat{B}
is
\Delta_{2}(G\oplus Y)
. Since the requirements
\mathcal{T}_{k}
are satisfied, the set
G
is
M
-unbounded and
\vec{R}
-cohesive. Finally, for the same reasons as in
Proposition

4.4
, we get that
A
is
\hat{B}
-block
(G\oplus Y)
-immune with cost
n+2
.
∎

As in
Proposition

4.4
, the previous construction makes no reference of the set
A
. Thus, for every set
\tilde{A}\subseteq M
that is
B
-block
Y
-immune with cost
n
will also be
\hat{B}
-block
(G\oplus Y)
-immune with cost
n+2
.

, there exists some

4.7
Main result

We are now ready to prove
1.5
.

Main Theorem
1.5
.

Every countable topped model of
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{2}+\neg\mathsf{I}\Sigma^{0}_{2}
can be
\omega
-extended into a model of
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+\mathsf{ADS}+\neg\mathsf{BRT}^{2}_{2}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{M}_{0}=(M,S_{0})
be a countable model of
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{2}+\neg\mathsf{I}\Sigma^{0}_{2}
, topped by a set
Z_{0}\in S_{0}
. Let
I\subseteq M
be a
\Sigma_{2}(Z_{0})
-definable proper cut, witnessing that
\mathcal{M}_{0}\models\neg\mathsf{I}\Sigma^{0}_{2}
, and let
B_{0}\subseteq M
be a
\Delta_{2}(\mathcal{M}_{0})
-definable
M
-unbounded set with cardinality
I
.
By the proof of
1.4
,
\mathcal{M}_{0}
contains a stable instance
f:[M]^{2}\to 2
of
\mathsf{BRT}^{2}_{2}
with no
\Delta_{1}(Z_{0})
-definable solution, and such that the set
A=\{x\in M:\forall^{\infty}yf(x,y)=0\}
is
B_{0}
-block
Z_{0}
-immune with cost 2.

Since
M
is countable, there is a (non necessarily computable) reordering
(\Gamma_{n})_{n\in\omega}
of the sequence
(\Phi_{x})_{x\in M}
of all Turing functionals with codes in
M
.
By
Propositions

4.4
and
4.23
, define a sequence
(\mathcal{M}_{n})_{n\in\omega}
of countable models of
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{2}
such that for every
n\in\omega
,

(1)

\mathcal{M}_{n+1}
is topped by some set
Z_{n+1}
and
\omega
-extends
\mathcal{M}_{n}
;

(2)

A
is
B_{n}
-block
Z_{n}
-immune with some cost
c_{n}\in\omega
;

(3)

if
n=\langle 0,a,b\rangle
and
\Gamma_{a}^{Z_{b}}
is an instance
\mathcal{L}=(M,<_{\mathcal{L}})
of
\mathsf{SADS}
, then
\mathcal{M}_{n+1}
contains an
M
-unbounded
\mathcal{L}
-ascending or
\mathcal{L}
-descending sequence;

(4)

if
n=\langle 1,a,b\rangle
and
\Gamma_{a}^{Z_{b}}
is an instance
\vec{R}
of
\mathsf{COH}
, then
\mathcal{M}_{n+1}
contains an
M
-unbounded
\vec{R}
-cohesive set.

Let
\mathcal{N}=\bigcup_{n\in\omega}\mathcal{M}_{n}
. Then
\mathcal{N}\models\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{2}
.
By (1),
\mathcal{N}

\omega
-extends
\mathcal{M}_{0}
. By (2)
A
is immune relative to every set in
\mathcal{N}
, so

is an instance of
\mathsf{BRT}^{2}_{2}
with no solution in
\mathcal{N}
. It follows that
\mathcal{N}\not\models\mathsf{BRT}^{2}_{2}
.
By (3) and (4),
\mathcal{N}\models\mathsf{SADS}+\mathsf{COH}
, so by Hirschfeldt and Shore
[
18
]
,
\mathcal{N}\models\mathsf{ADS}
.
∎

Corollary 4.24
.

\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+\mathsf{ADS}\not\vdash\mathsf{BRT}_{2}^{2}

Proof.

Let
M
be a countable model of
\mathsf{I}\Sigma_{1}+\mathsf{B}\Sigma_{2}+\neg\mathsf{I}\Sigma_{2}
.
Let
\mathcal{M}=(M,S)
be the model whose second-order part
S
consists of the
\Delta_{1}
-definable sets with parameters in
M
. By Friedman
[
15
]
,
\mathcal{M}\models\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{2}+\neg\mathsf{I}\Sigma^{0}_{2}
. Moreover,
\mathcal{M}
is countable, and topped by
\emptyset
, so by
1.5
, there is an
\omega
-extension
\mathcal{N}
of
\mathcal{M}
such that
\mathcal{N}\models\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+\mathsf{ADS}+\neg\mathsf{BRT}^{2}_{2}
. Thus,
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+\mathsf{ADS}\not\vdash\mathsf{BRT}_{2}^{2}
.
∎

Corollary 4.25
.

\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+\mathsf{ADS}+\neg\mathsf{BRT}^{2}_{2}
is
\Pi^{1}_{1}
-conservative over
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{2}+\neg\mathsf{I}\Sigma^{0}_{2}
.

Proof.

Let
\phi=\forall X\psi(X)
be a
\Pi^{1}_{1}
-sentence such that
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{2}+\neg\mathsf{I}\Sigma^{0}_{2}\not\vdash\phi
.
By the completeness theorem and the downward Löwenheim-Skolem theorem, there exists a countable model
\mathcal{M}=(M,S)\models\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{2}+\neg\mathsf{I}\Sigma^{0}_{2}+\neg\phi
. Let
X\in S
be such that
\mathcal{M}\models\neg\psi(X)
and
Y\in S
be such that
\mathsf{I}\Sigma^{0}_{2}(Y)
fails. Let
\mathcal{M}_{1}=(M,\Delta_{1}\mbox{-}\mathsf{Def}(M,X,Y))
. By Friedman
[
15
]
,
\mathcal{M}_{1}\models\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{2}
. Moreover, since
\psi
is arithmetic,
\mathcal{M}_{1}\models\neg\psi(X)
, and since
Y\in\mathcal{M}_{1}
,
\mathcal{M}_{1}\models\neg\mathsf{I}\Sigma^{0}_{2}
. Last,
\mathcal{M}_{1}
is topped by
X\oplus Y
, so one can apply
1.5
to get an
\omega
-extension
\mathcal{N}
of
\mathcal{M}_{1}
such that
\mathcal{N}\models\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{2}+\mathsf{ADS}+\neg\mathsf{BRT}^{2}_{2}
. In particular,
X\in\mathcal{N}
and
\mathcal{N}\models\neg\psi(X)
, so
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{2}+\mathsf{ADS}+\neg\mathsf{BRT}^{2}_{2}\not\vdash\phi
.
∎

-consequences as
\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{2}
.

Question 5.1
.

Is
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+\mathsf{BRT}^{2}_{2}
a

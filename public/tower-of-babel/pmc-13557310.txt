Hyperhidrotic and Hypertrichotic Cutaneous Plaque
Authors: Pedro Simões Farinha, Beatriz Vilela, Alexandre João
Published: 2026

Case Presentation

A 10-year-old female with type 1 diabetes mellitus was referred to Pediatric Dermatology for a lesion on the right leg progressively enlarging over three years, associated with pruritus and pain. On examination, a 3 cm, brownish, ill-defined plaque was observed on the lateral right leg, with localized hypertrichosis and hyperhidrosis (Figure 1). The lesion was firm, infiltrated, and painful on palpation.

A skin biopsy revealed an acanthotic epidermis, proliferation of eccrine glands in the reticular dermis, mucinous stroma, and increased blood vessels, confirming the diagnosis of eccrine angiomatous hamartoma (EAH) [1]. Surgical excision was considered due to functional symptoms, and partial symptomatic relief was achieved with topical glycopyrronium 8 mg/g.

Teaching Point

EAH is a rare, benign adnexal malformation that may present with pain, hyperhidrosis, and hypertrichosis, potentially mimicking vascular or adnexal tumors. Although often congenital, its progressive nature can impair quality of life, especially in children. This case highlights the importance of integrating clinical and histological data in diagnosing pediatric plaques with autonomical features. Early recognition allows for targeted symptom management and avoids unnecessary workup.

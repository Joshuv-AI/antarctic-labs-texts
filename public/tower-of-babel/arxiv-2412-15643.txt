Universal inequalities for eigenvalues of the Dirichlet Laplacian on conformally flat Riemannian manifolds
Yong Luo, Xianjing Zheng

Abstract
In this paper we study eigenvalues of the Dirichlet Laplacian on conformally flat Riemannian manifolds. In particular we establish some universal inequality for eigenvalues of the Dirichlet Laplacian on the hyperbolic space ℍ n ​ ( − 1 ) \mathbb{H}^{n}(-1) .



1
Introduction

Let
(M^{n},g)
be an
n
-dimensional complete Riemannian manifold and
\Omega
a bounded connected domain in
M
. Let
\Delta
denote the Laplacian acting on functions on
M
. Eigenvalues of the Dirichlet Laplacian is stated as:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\Delta u=-\lambda u\text{ in }\Omega,\\
u|_{\partial\Omega}=0.\end{array}\right.

Let

\displaystyle 0<\lambda_{1}<\lambda_{2}\leq\lambda_{3}\leq\cdots

denote the successive eigenvalues of (
1
). Here each eigenvalue is repeated according to its multiplicity. It is well-known that the following Weyl’s asymptotic formula holds (cf.
[
3
]
):

\displaystyle\lambda_{k}\sim\frac{4\pi^{2}}{(\omega_{n}Vol\Omega)^{\frac{2}{n}}}k^{\frac{2}{n}},\ k\to\infty,

(1.4)

where
\omega_{n}
is the volume of the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Generally, if an equality of eigenvalues requires no hypotheses on the geometric quantities of the domain
\Omega
(other than its dimension), it is called an universal inequality. Universal inequality is an important research area in the study of the spectrum of
\Delta
and many works have been done during the past decades. Now we give a brief introduction of the main results.

The study of universal inequalities for (
1
) was initiated by Payne, Pölya, and Weinberger
[
17
]
in 1956, they proved

\displaystyle\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{k}\leq\frac{2}{k}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}

for
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Exactly by the same proof, their result can promoted to dimension
n
as

\displaystyle\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{k}\leq\frac{4}{kn}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}

for
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

In 1980, Hile and Protter
[
12
]
proved

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{\lambda_{i}}{\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i}}\geq\frac{kn}{4}.

In 1991, Yang
[
19
]
(cf.
[
7
]
) proved a sharp universal inequality:

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i}\right)^{2}\leq\frac{4}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i}\right)\lambda_{i},

(1.5)

which has been called Yang’s first inequality by Ashbaugh (cf.
[
1
]
and
[
2
]
and so on).

When
\Omega
is a bounded domain in
\mathbb{S}^{n}(1)
, Cheng and Yang
[
6
]
proved in 2005 that eigenvalues of the eigenvalue problem (
1
) satisfy

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i}\right)^{2}\leq\frac{4}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i}\right)\left(\lambda_{i}+\frac{n^{2}}{4}\right),

(1.6)

by canonically embedded
\mathbb{S}^{n}(1)
into
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
(see also
[
4
]
and
[
18
]
). It is optimal since the above inequality becomes an equality for any
k
when
\Omega=\mathbb{S}^{n}(1)
.

It is very natural to consider universal inequalities for eigenvalues of the eigenvalue problem (
1
) when
M^{n}
is the hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{n}(-1)
. If
n=2
, by making use of estimates for eigenvalues of the eigenvalue
problem of the Schrödinger like operator with a weight, Harrell and Michel
[
10
]
and Ashbaugh
[
2
]

have obtained several results. For general
n
, Cheng and Yang
[
8
]
proved in 2009 that eigenvalues of the eigenvalue problem (
1
) satisfy

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i}\right)^{2}\leqslant 4\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i}\right)\left(\lambda_{i}-\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{4}\right).

(1.7)

It remains a challenge problem if one can improve the coefficient on the right hand side of
(\ref{Cheng's 1})
from
4
to
\frac{4}{n}
(which is sharp by making use of a recursion formula of Cheng and Yang
[
7
]
and Weyl’s asymptotic formula (
1.4
)). Actually we have a conjecture raised by Cheng
[
5
]
.

Conjecture:
Assume that
\lambda_{i}
are eigenvalues of the eigenvalue problem (
1
) with
(M^{n},g)
being the hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{n}(-1)
. Then have we

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i}\right)^{2}\leqslant\frac{4}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i}\right)\left(\lambda_{i}-\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{4}\right).

In this paper, we consider universal inequalities for the eigenvalues of the Dirichlet Laplacian on the hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{n}(-1)
. More generally, we consider eigenvalues of the eigenvalue problem (
1
) on conformally flat manifolds. We have

Proposition 1.1
.

Let
(\mathbb{R}^{n},\widetilde{g})
be an
n
-dimensional conformally flat Riemannian manifold with the conformal metric
\widetilde{g}=e^{2f}g
, where
f
is a smooth function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and let
\Omega
be a bounded smooth domain in
(\mathbb{R}^{n},\widetilde{g})
. Then eigenvalues
\lambda_{i}
’s of the eigenvalue problem (
1
) satisfy

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i}\right)^{2}\int_{\Omega}x_{p}u_{i}\left(-2\widetilde{\nabla}x_{p}\widetilde{\nabla}u_{i}-(n-2)e^{-2f}\nabla f\nabla x_{p}u_{i}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i}\right)\int_{\Omega}\left|2\widetilde{\nabla}x_{p}\widetilde{\nabla}u_{i}+(n-2)e^{-2f}\nabla f\nabla x_{p}u_{i}\right|^{2}

(1.8)

where
x_{p}

(p=1,2,\cdots,n)
denotes the standard coordinates of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, while
\widetilde{\nabla}
and
\nabla
denotes the gradient of
(\mathbb{R}^{n},\widetilde{g})
and
(\mathbb{R}^{n},g)
, respectively.

From Proposition
1.1
we obtain

Proposition 1.2
.

For a conformally flat manifold
(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n},\widetilde{g})
with conformal metric

\widetilde{g}=\frac{dx_{1}^{2}+dx_{2}^{2}+\cdots+dx_{n}^{2}}{x_{n}^{t}},

where
t
is a real constant, eigenvalues
\lambda_{i}
’s of the eigenvalue problem (
1
) satisfy

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i}\right)^{2}\left(n\int_{\Omega}x_{n}^{t}u_{i}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i}\right)\left(4\lambda_{i}\int_{\Omega}x_{n}^{t}u_{i}^{2}+\left(\left(-\frac{n^{2}}{4}+n+1\right)t^{2}-nt\right)\int_{\Omega}x_{n}^{2t-2}u_{i}^{2}\right).

(1.9)

Here
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}:=\{(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}|x_{n}>0\}
.

Recall that
(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n},\widetilde{g})
with the conformal metric

\widetilde{g}=\frac{dx_{1}^{2}+dx_{2}^{2}+\cdots+dx_{n}^{2}}{x_{n}^{2}}

is the upper half space model of the hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{n}(-1)
. Therefore let
t=2
and
k=1
in Proposition
1.2
we obtain the following result.

Theorem 1.3
.

Assume that
(M^{n},g)
is the hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{n}(-1)=(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n},\frac{dx_{1}^{2}+dx_{2}^{2}+\cdots+dx_{n}^{2}}{x_{n}^{2}})
, then eigenvalues
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}
of the eigenvalue problem (
1
) satisfy

\displaystyle\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1}\leqslant\frac{4}{n}\left(\lambda_{1}-\frac{n^{2}-2n-4}{4}\right).

(1.10)

The hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{n}(-1)
can also be seem as a conformally flat Riemannian manifold with a radial symmetric conformal factor (the Poincaré unit disk model). From this aspect, we can give an alternative proof of Theorem
1.3
. First we prove

Proposition 1.4
.

Assume that
(M^{n},\tilde{g})
is an
n
-dimensional conformally flat manifold
M
with metric
\widetilde{g}=e^{2f}g
, where
g
is the standard metric in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
f
is a radial symmetric function. Then eigenvalues
\lambda_{i}
’s of the eigenvalue problem (
1
) satisfy

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i}\right)^{2}\left(n\int_{\Omega}e^{-2f}u_{i}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i}\right)\left(4\lambda_{i}\int_{\Omega}e^{-2f}u_{i}^{2}-\left(n^{2}-4n-4\right)\int_{\Omega}e^{-4f}\left|f^{\prime}(r)\right|^{2}u_{i}^{2}\right.

\displaystyle\left.-2n\int_{\Omega}e^{-4f}\left(f^{\prime\prime}(r)+\frac{f^{\prime}(r)(n-1)}{r}\right)u_{i}^{2}\right),

(1.11)

where
r:=\left|x\right|
.

Recall that the Poincaré unit disk model of the hyperbolic space

\mathbb{H}^{n}(-1)=(\mathbb{B}^{n},\frac{4}{(1-|x|^{2})^{2}}g),

where
\mathbb{B}^{n}
is the unit disk in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
g=dx^{2}_{1}+\cdots+dx_{n}^{2}
is the canonical metric on
\mathbb{B}^{n}
. As an application of (
1.11
), we have

Corollary 1.5
.

Assume that
(M^{n},g)
is the hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{n}(-1)=(\mathbb{B}^{n},\frac{4}{(1-|x|^{2})^{2}}g)
, then eigenvalues
\lambda_{i}
’s of the eigenvalue problem (
1
) satisfy

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i}\right)^{2}\left(n\int_{\Omega}\left(1-|x|^{2}\right)^{2}u_{i}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i}\right)\big[4\lambda_{i}\int_{\Omega}\left(1-|x|^{2}\right)^{2}u_{i}^{2}-{n}^{2}\int_{\Omega}\left(1-|x|^{2}\right)^{2}u_{i}^{2}

\displaystyle+\left(2n+4\right)\int_{\Omega}\left(1-|x|^{2}\right)^{2}|x|^{2}u_{i}^{2}\big].

(1.12)

Note that since
|x|<1
, we see that

\int_{\Omega}\left(1-|x|^{2}\right)^{2}|x|^{2}u_{i}^{2}\leq\int_{\Omega}\left(1-|x|^{2}\right)^{2}u_{i}^{2},

and thus we can deduce (
1.10
) from (
1.12
) by letting
k=1
.

Let
\rho_{max}:=\max_{\Omega}\frac{1}{x_{n}^{2}}
and
\rho_{min}:=\min_{\Omega}\frac{1}{x_{n}^{2}}
. For general
k
, it easy to see that we can deduce the following result from Proposition
1.2
.

Theorem 1.6
.

Assume that
(M^{n},g)
is the hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{n}(-1)=(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n},\frac{dx_{1}^{2}+dx_{2}^{2}+\cdots+dx_{n}^{2}}{x_{n}^{2}})
, then eigenvalues
\lambda_{i}
’s of the eigenvalue problem (
1
) satisfy

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i})^{2}\leqslant\frac{\rho_{max}}{\rho_{min}}\frac{4}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i}\right)\left(\lambda_{i}-\frac{n^{2}-2n-4}{4}\right).

(1.13)

Remark 1.7
.

Note that Ashbaugh
[
2
]
proved similar result for eigenvalues of the eigenvalue
problem of the Schrödinger like operator with a weight, and as a consequence, he proved that eigenvalues of the eigenvalue problem (
1
) satisfy

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i})^{2}\leqslant\frac{\rho_{max}}{\rho_{min}}2\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i}\right)\lambda_{i},

when
\Omega
is a bounded domain in
\mathbb{H}^{2}(-1)
. Our result generalize his result to general dimension.

Assume that
\Omega\subset\{0<a\leq x_{n}^{2}\leq b\}
, then we have

Corollary 1.8
.

Assume that
(M^{n},g)
is the hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{n}(-1)=(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n},\frac{dx_{1}^{2}+dx_{2}^{2}+\cdots+dx_{n}^{2}}{x_{n}^{2}})
, then eigenvalues
\lambda_{i}
’s of the eigenvalue problem (
1
) satisfy

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i})^{2}\leqslant\frac{b}{a}\frac{4}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i}\right)\left(\lambda_{i}-\frac{n^{2}-2n-4}{4}\right).

(1.14)

2
Preliminaries

In this paper, we denote

\nabla f\nabla h=\langle\nabla f,\nabla h\rangle_{g},\ \widetilde{\nabla}f\widetilde{\nabla}h=\langle\widetilde{\nabla}f,\widetilde{\nabla}h\rangle_{\widetilde{g}}

for any functions
f,h

:

\Omega

\rightarrow

\mathbf{R}
. Then we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{\nabla}f\widetilde{\nabla}h

\displaystyle=\langle\widetilde{\nabla}f,\widetilde{\nabla}h\rangle_{\widetilde{g}}

\displaystyle=e^{2f}\left\langle e^{-2f}\nabla f,e^{-2f}\nabla h\right\rangle_{g}

\displaystyle=e^{-2f}\langle\nabla f,\nabla h\rangle_{g}

\displaystyle=e^{-2f}\nabla f\nabla h.

We now review some formulas of the Laplacian under conformal change of Riemannian metrics. Let
(M,g)
be an
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold. Given a function
h
:

M
\rightarrow

\mathbb{R}
, we consider the conformal manifold
(M,\widetilde{g})
with the conformal metric
\widetilde{g}=e^{2f}g
. Let
\widetilde{\Delta}
and
\Delta
denote the Laplacian on
(M,\widetilde{g})
and
(M,g)
respectively and
\widetilde{\nabla}
,
\nabla
denote the Riemannian connection of
(M,\widetilde{g})
and
(M,g)
respectively. There are known formula of the Laplacian under conformal transformation, which states that for a smooth function
F

:

M

\rightarrow

\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\quad\widetilde{\Delta}F=e^{-2f}\left(\Delta F+(n-2)\nabla f\nabla F\right).

(2.1)

It is obvious that for any smooth function
G

:

M

\rightarrow

\mathbb{R}
we have,

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Delta}(FG)=\widetilde{\Delta}FG+\widetilde{\Delta}GF+2\widetilde{\nabla}F\widetilde{\nabla}G=\widetilde{\Delta}FG+\widetilde{\Delta}GF+2e^{-2f}\nabla F\nabla G.

3
Proof of Proposition
1.1

Proof.

Let
\lambda_{i}
, i=1, …, k+1, be the
ith
eigenvalue of problem (
1
) and
u_{i}
the orthonormal eigenfunction corresponding to
\lambda_{i}
, that is,

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Delta}u_{i}=-\lambda_{i}u_{i}\quad\text{ in }\Omega,\left.\quad u_{i}\right|_{\partial\Omega}=0,\quad\int_{M}u_{i}u_{j}=\delta_{ij},\quad\forall i,j=1,2,\ldots,k+1.

Let
x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}
denote the standard coordinates in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, for
i=1,\ldots,k
. Consider the trail functions
\phi_{i}:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
given by

\displaystyle\phi_{i}=x_{p}u_{i}-\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{ij}u_{j},

where

\displaystyle a_{ij}=\int_{\Omega}x_{p}u_{i}u_{j}.

Since
\left.\phi_{i}\right|_{\partial\Omega}=0
and

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}u_{j}\phi_{i}=0,\forall i,j=1,\ldots,k,

using (
2
) we obtain

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Delta}\phi_{i}

\displaystyle=\widetilde{\Delta}\left(x_{p}u_{i}-\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{ij}u_{j}\right)

\displaystyle=\widetilde{\Delta}x_{p}u_{i}+\widetilde{\Delta}u_{i}x_{p}+2e^{-2f}\nabla x_{p}\nabla u_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{ij}\lambda_{j}u_{j}.

Hence we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\phi_{i}\left(-\widetilde{\Delta}\phi_{i}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\phi_{i}\left(-\widetilde{\Delta}x_{p}u_{i}-\widetilde{\Delta}u_{i}x_{p}-2e^{-2f}\nabla x_{p}\nabla u_{i}-\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{ij}\lambda_{j}u_{j}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\phi_{i}\left(\lambda_{i}u_{i}x_{p}-\lambda_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{ij}u_{j}-\widetilde{\Delta}x_{p}u_{i}-2e^{-2f}\nabla x_{p}\nabla u_{i}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda_{i}\left\|\phi_{i}\right\|^{2}-\int_{\Omega}\phi_{i}\left(\widetilde{\Delta}x_{p}u_{i}+2e^{-2f}\nabla x_{p}\nabla u_{i}\right),

where
\left\|\phi_{i}\right\|^{2}=\int_{\Omega}\phi_{i}^{2}
. It follows from the Rayleigh-Ritz inequality that

\displaystyle\left(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i}\right)||\phi_{i}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\int_{\Omega}-\left(\widetilde{\Delta}x_{p}u_{i}+2e^{-2f}\nabla x_{p}\nabla u_{i}\right)\left(x_{p}u_{i}-\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{ij}u_{j}\right)

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{\Omega}x_{p}u_{i}q_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{ij}\int_{\Omega}\widetilde{\Delta}x_{p}u_{i}u_{j}+2\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{ij}\int_{\Omega}e^{-2f}\nabla x_{p}\nabla u_{i}u_{j}

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{\Omega}x_{p}u_{i}q_{i}+(n-2)\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{ij}\int_{\Omega}e^{-2f}\nabla f\nabla x_{p}u_{i}u_{j}+2\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{ij}\int_{\Omega}e^{-2f}\nabla x_{p}\nabla u_{i}u_{j},

where
q_{i}=-\left(\widetilde{\Delta}x_{p}u_{i}+2e^{-2f}\nabla x_{p}\nabla u_{i}\right)
. Now let

\displaystyle s_{ij}=\int_{\Omega}e^{-2f}\nabla f\nabla x_{p}u_{i}u_{j},

\displaystyle b_{ij}=\int_{\Omega}e^{-2f}\nabla x_{p}\nabla u_{i}u_{j}.

Note that

\displaystyle\lambda_{j}a_{ij}=\int_{\Omega}x_{p}u_{i}\left(-\widetilde{\Delta}u_{j}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad=-\int_{\Omega}u_{j}\widetilde{\Delta}\left(x_{p}u_{i}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad=-\int_{\Omega}u_{j}\left(\widetilde{\Delta}x_{p}u_{i}+x_{p}\widetilde{\Delta}u_{i}+2e^{-2f}\nabla x_{p}\nabla u_{i}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad=-(n-2)\int_{\Omega}e^{-2f}\nabla f\nabla x_{p}u_{i}u_{j}+\lambda_{i}a_{ij}-2b_{ij}

\displaystyle\qquad=-(n-2)s_{ij}+\lambda_{i}a_{ij}-2b_{ij},

thus we have

b_{ij}=\frac{\left(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}\right)}{2}a_{ij}-\frac{(n-2)}{2}s_{ij},

and

\displaystyle\left(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i}\right)\left\|\phi_{i}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{\Omega}x_{p}u_{i}q_{i}+(n-2)\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{ij}s_{ij}+2\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left(\frac{\left(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}\right)}{2}a_{ij}-\frac{(n-2)}{2}s_{ij}\right)a_{ij}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}x_{p}u_{i}q_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}\right)a_{ij}^{2}.

(3.3)

Set
r_{ij}=\int_{\Omega}u_{j}\left(2\widetilde{\nabla}x_{p}\widetilde{\nabla}u_{i}+\widetilde{\Delta}x_{p}u_{i}\right),
then
r_{ij}=-r_{ji}
since

\displaystyle r_{ij}

\displaystyle=2\int_{\Omega}e^{-2f}\nabla x_{p}\nabla u_{i}u_{j}+(n-2)\int_{\Omega}e^{-2f}\nabla f\nabla x_{p}u_{i}u_{j}

\displaystyle=2b_{ij}+(n-2)s_{ij}

\displaystyle=(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j})a_{ij}.

Furthermore,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(-2)\phi_{i}\left(2\widetilde{\nabla}x_{p}\widetilde{\nabla}u_{i}+\widetilde{\Delta}x_{p}u_{i}\right)

\displaystyle=-2\int_{\Omega}x_{p}u_{i}\left(2\widetilde{\nabla}x_{p}\widetilde{\nabla}u_{i}+\widetilde{\Delta}x_{p}u_{i}\right)+2\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{ij}r_{ij}

\displaystyle=w_{i}+2\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{ij}r_{ij},

(3.4)

where
w_{i}=-2\int_{\Omega}x_{p}u_{i}\left(2\widetilde{\nabla}x_{p}\widetilde{\nabla}u_{i}+\widetilde{\Delta}x_{p}u_{i}\right).

Multiplying (
3.4
) by
(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i})^{2}
and using the Schwarz inequality and (
3.3
), we get

\displaystyle\quad\left(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i}\right)^{2}\left(w_{i}+2\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{ij}r_{ij}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i}\right)^{2}\int_{\Omega}(-2)\phi_{i}\left(2\widetilde{\nabla}x_{p}\widetilde{\nabla}u_{i}+\widetilde{\Delta}x_{p}u_{i}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i}\right)^{3}\int_{\Omega}\left|\phi_{i}\right|^{2}+\left(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i}\right)\int_{\Omega}\left|2\widetilde{\nabla}x_{p}\widetilde{\nabla}u_{i}+\widetilde{\Delta}x_{p}u_{i}-\sum_{j=1}^{k}r_{ij}u_{j}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i}\right)^{2}\left(\int_{\Omega}x_{p}u_{i}q_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}\right)a_{ij}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\left(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i}\right)\int_{\Omega}\left|2\widetilde{\nabla}x_{p}\widetilde{\nabla}u_{i}+\widetilde{\Delta}x_{p}u_{i}\right|^{2}-\left(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i}\right)\sum_{j=1}^{k}r_{ij}^{2}.

Summing over
i
from
1
to
k
, we have

\displaystyle\quad\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i}\right)^{2}w_{i}+2\sum_{i,j=1}^{k}\left(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i}\right)^{2}a_{ij}r_{ij}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i}\right)^{2}\int_{\Omega}x_{p}u_{i}q_{i}+\sum_{i,j=1}^{k}\left(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i}\right)^{2}\left(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}\right)a_{ij}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i}\right)\int_{\Omega}\left|2\widetilde{\nabla}x_{p}\widetilde{\nabla}u_{i}+\widetilde{\Delta}x_{p}u_{i}\right|^{2}-\sum_{i,j=1}^{k}\left(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i}\right)r_{ij}^{2}

Noting that

a_{ij}=a_{ji},\ r_{ij}=-r_{ji},

we infer

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=1}^{k}\left(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i}\right)^{2}a_{ij}r_{ij}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{k}\left(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i}\right)\left(\lambda_{k+1}+\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{i}\right)a_{ij}r_{ij}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{k}\left(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i}\right)\left(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{j}\right)a_{ij}r_{ij}+\sum_{i,j=1}^{k}\left(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i}\right)\left(\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{i}\right)a_{ij}r_{ij}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{k}\left(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i}\right)\left(\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{i}\right)a_{ij}r_{ij},

where we used

\sum_{i,j=1}^{k}\left(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i}\right)\left(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{j}\right)a_{ij}r_{ij}=0.

Similarly,

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=1}^{k}\left(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i}\right)^{2}\left(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}\right)a_{ij}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{k}\left(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i}\right)\left(\lambda_{k+1}+\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{i}\right)\left(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}\right)a_{ij}^{2}

Computational complexity of the recoverable robust shortest path problem in acyclic digraphs under interval budgeted uncertainty
Marcel Jackiewicz, Adam Kasperski, Paweł Zieliński

Abstract
In this paper, we consider the recoverable robust shortest path problem in acyclic digraphs, employing interval budgeted uncertainty to model uncertain second-stage costs. For the continuous budgeted uncertainty model, we prove that the problem is strongly NP-hard even in layered digraphs. Furthermore, we show that in general acyclic digraphs, the problem cannot be approximated within any constant factor unless P = NP \mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP} , nor can it be approximated within a factor of 2 log 1 − ϵ ⁡ n 2^{\log^{1-\epsilon}n} for any ϵ > 0 \epsilon>0 unless NP ⊆ DTIME ⁡ ( n poly ​ log ⁡ n ) \mathrm{NP}\subseteq\mathrm{DTIME}(n^{\mathrm{poly}\log n}) . For the discrete budgeted uncertainty model, we show that the problem is not approximable unless P = NP \mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP} , even in layered digraphs. Finally, we establish that under continuous budgeted uncertainty, the integrality gap of a relaxation allowing a fractional first-stage solution is at least Ω ⁡ ( n ) \Omega(\sqrt{n}) .

1
Problem formulation

We are given a directed acyclic graph
G=(V,A)
. Two nodes in
V
are distinguished as a source
s\in V
and a sink
t\in V
. Let
\Phi
be the set of all
s
-
t
paths in
G
(
X\in\Phi
is the set of arcs forming the path). For each arc
e\in A
, a first-stage cost
C_{e}\geq 0
is specified. The second-stage arc costs are uncertain and we assume that they belong to an uncertainty set
\mathcal{U}\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{+}^{|A|}
. In the recoverable robust shortest path problem, we compute a first-stage path
X\in\Phi
. Then, after a second-stage cost scenario
\boldsymbol{c}\in\mathcal{U}
is revealed, the path
X
can be modified to some extent. That is, we can choose a second-stage path
Y
in some prescribed neighborhood
\Phi(X,k)
of
X
, where
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
is a given recovery parameter. We consider three types of neighborhoods, called arc-inclusion, arc-exclusion, and arc-symmetric difference, respectively
[
13
]
:

\displaystyle\Phi^{\mathrm{incl}}(X,k)

\displaystyle=\{Y\in\Phi\,:\,|Y\setminus X|\leq k\},

(1)

\displaystyle\Phi^{\mathrm{excl}}(X,k)

\displaystyle=\{Y\in\Phi\,:\,|X\setminus Y|\leq k\},

(2)

\displaystyle\Phi^{\mathrm{sym}}(X,k)

\displaystyle=\{Y\in\Phi\,:\,|(Y\setminus X)\cup(X\setminus Y)|\leq k\}.

(3)

The recoverable robust shortest path problem is defined as follows:

\textsc{Rec Rob SP}:\;OPT=\min_{X\in\Phi}\left(\sum_{e\in X}C_{e}+\max_{\boldsymbol{c}\in\mathcal{U}}\min_{Y\in\Phi(X,k)}\sum_{e\in Y}c_{e}\right).

(4)

In this paper, we use the interval uncertainty representation to model the uncertain second-stage arc costs. That is, for each arc
e\in A
we specify a nominal second-stage cost
\hat{c}_{e}
and the maximum deviation
\Delta_{e}
of the second-stage cost from its nominal value. Hence,
c_{e}\in[\hat{c}_{e},\hat{c}_{e}+\Delta_{e}]
,
e\in A
, and

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}=\{\boldsymbol{c}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{|A|}\,:\,c_{e}\in[\hat{c}_{e},\hat{c}_{e}+\Delta_{e}],e\in A\}.

(5)

To control the amount of uncertainty, we consider two budgeted versions of
\mathcal{U}
, namely the discrete
[
3
]
and continuous
[
13
]
budgeted uncertainty sets, defined as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}(\Gamma^{d})=\{\boldsymbol{c}\in\mathcal{U}\,:\,|\{e\in A\,:\,c_{e}>\hat{c}_{e}\}|\leq\Gamma^{d}\},

(6)

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}(\Gamma^{c})=\{\boldsymbol{c}\in\mathcal{U}\,:\,\sum_{e\in A}(c_{e}-\hat{c}_{e})\leq\Gamma^{c}\}.

(7)

The parameters
\Gamma^{d}
and
\Gamma^{c}
are called budgets. The discrete budget
\Gamma^{d}\in\{0,\dots,|A|\}
denotes the maximum number of the second-stage costs that can differ from their nominal values. On the other hand, the continuous budget
\Gamma^{c}\geq 0
denotes the maximum total deviation of the second-stage costs from their nominal values. Both uncertainty sets are commonly used in robust optimization as they allow us to reduce the price of robustness of the solutions computed (see, e.g.,
[
3
]
).

The concept of recoverable robustness was first proposed in
[
12
]
.
The recoverable robust version of the shortest path problem was subsequently investigated in
[
4
,
5
]
.
The problem is known to be strongly NP-hard for general digraphs under the interval uncertainty set
\mathcal{U}
.
This hardness result remains true across all three types of neighborhoods (
1
)–(
3
) (see
[
5
,
13
,
6
,
11
]
).
However, when the input graph
G
is acyclic, the problem can be solved in polynomial time under the uncertainty set
\mathcal{U}
for all neighborhoods (
1
)–(
3
), as recently shown in
[
11
]
.
It turns out that adding budget constraints to
\mathcal{U}
can significantly increase the computational complexity of the problem.
Indeed, it has been shown in
[
8
]
that the problem is weakly NP-hard under the uncertainty set
\mathcal{U}(\Gamma^{d})
even for a very restricted class of series-parallel acyclic digraphs.
In contrast, the complexity of the problem under the uncertainty set
\mathcal{U}(\Gamma^{c})
has remained open until now.
In this paper, we provide several new complexity results for
Rec Rob SP
in acyclic digraphs.
We show that the problem is strongly NP-hard for layered digraphs under both
\mathcal{U}(\Gamma^{d})
and
\mathcal{U}(\Gamma^{c})
, and we establish some inapproximability lower bounds.
All the complexity results are presented in Section
2
.
Additionally, in Section
3
, we characterize the integrality gap of a relaxation of the problem under the uncertainty set
\mathcal{U}(\Gamma^{c})
.

Computational complexity results

Consider first the
Rec Rob SP
problem for the continuous budgeted uncertainty set

. We prove the following result:

Theorem 1
.

The

problem is strongly NP-hard in layered digraphs with any neighborhood (
1
)–(
3
),
under
\mathcal{U}(\Gamma^{c})
, if

Proof.

We will show a reduction from the

Hamiltonian Path

Input: Given a directed graph
G=(V,A)
.

Question: Does

have a Hamiltonian path, that is, a simple directed path that visits all nodes of

Let

.
The graph

The arc set

.
These arcs create

-

,
and each of these paths has exactly
2n+1

The diagonal arcs connect

to

for

if and only if

.
The resulting digraph has a layered structure: the

and

and each remaining layer consists of the vertices

It follows that there are

Having constructed

The neighborhood-size parameter

The uncertainty budget

The first-stage cost of each arc in

The second-stage cost depends on the type of the arc:

(\hat{c}_{e},\Delta_{e})=\begin{cases}(0,M)&\text{if }e\text{ is a vertical arc},\\
(M,0)&\text{if }e\text{ is a diagonal arc},\end{cases}

(8)

where

.
Hence, the uncertainty affects only vertical arcs.

We now prove that there exists a Hamiltonian path in
G
if and only if there exists a solution
(X,Y)

to the constructed
Rec Rob SP
instance with total cost at most
\Gamma^{c}/n
.

(
\Rightarrow
) Let
P
be a Hamiltonian path in
G
,
and let the vertices traversed by that path be ordered as
v_{i_{1}},v_{i_{2}},\ldots,v_{i_{n}}
.
We use
P
to construct the first-stage path
X^{*}
in
G^{\prime}
.
Specifically,
X^{*}
is composed of the arcs
(s,v_{i_{1}}^{1}),(v_{i_{1}}^{1},v_{i_{1}}^{2}),(v_{i_{1}}^{2},v_{i_{2}}^{3}),(v_{i_{2}}^{3},v_{i_{2}}^{4}),\ldots,(v_{i_{n}}^{2n},t)
,
i.e.,
X^{*}
uses at least one arc from each vertex-disjoint vertical
s
-
t
path
P_{i}
in
G^{\prime}
for
i\in[n]
.
Since
X^{*}
shares at least one arc with each
P_{i}
and
|P_{i}|=2n+1
, we have
|P_{i}\setminus X^{*}|\leq 2n=k
,
so
P_{i}\in\Phi(X^{*},k)
for every
i\in[n]
.
Since the deviations affect only vertical arcs and the paths
P_{i}
are arc-disjoint,
the adversary maximizes the cost of the cheapest available recovery path
by spreading the budget
\Gamma^{c}
evenly over the
n
paths: any uneven allocation leaves some
P_{i}
with cost below
\Gamma^{c}/n
,
which the recovery action would then select.
Hence the worst-case second-stage cost is
\Gamma^{c}/n
, and as all first-stage costs are zero,
the total cost of the solution to
Rec Rob SP
is
\Gamma^{c}/n
.

(
\Leftarrow
) Let
(X^{*},Y^{*})
be a solution to
Rec Rob SP
in
G^{\prime}
with a total cost of at most
\Gamma^{c}/n
.
Since the first-stage cost of all arcs in
G^{\prime}
is
0
, and the second-stage cost of all diagonal arcs is
M>\Gamma^{c}
,
the second-stage path
Y^{*}
must use only vertical arcs.
It follows that
Y^{*}
is one of the vertical vertex-disjoint
s
-
t
paths
P_{i}
for
i\in[n]
.
Moreover, since
Y^{*}\in\Phi(X^{*},k)
, the paths
X^{*}
and
Y^{*}
must have at least one arc in common.
We claim that
X^{*}
shares at least one arc with each path
P_{i}
for
i\in[n]
.
Suppose for contradiction that there is a path
P_{l}
for which
|X^{*}\cap P_{l}|=0
.
Consequently,
P_{l}\notin\Phi(X^{*},k)
, and the worst-case cost scenario is achieved by distributing the budget
\Gamma^{c}

across at most
n-1
vertical paths.
However, this results in a second-stage cost of at least
\Gamma^{c}/(n-1)
, which contradicts the assumption on the total solution cost.
Therefore,
X^{*}
contains at least one arc from each path
P_{i}
for
i\in[n]
,
and it must use exactly
n-1
diagonal arcs to form a valid
s
-
t
path in
G^{\prime}
.
By construction, a diagonal arc

belongs to

if and only if an arc
(v_{u},v_{w})
belongs to
A
.
Hence, the sequence of diagonal arcs of

corresponds to a path

in
G

traversing each vertex in

exactly once, i.e., a Hamiltonian path.
∎

4
Conclusions

In this paper, we have provided new computational complexity results for the recoverable robust shortest path problem in acyclic digraphs. We used two types of interval budgeted uncertainty sets to model uncertain second-stage costs. The problem turned out to be strongly NP-hard and inapproximable under discrete budgeted uncertainty. These negative results hold even for layered digraphs. An interesting open problem is to strengthen the known hardness result for series-parallel digraphs, for which the problem is currently only known to be weakly NP-hard. The complexity landscape under continuous budgeted uncertainty is considerably more intriguing. For general acyclic digraphs, the problem is approximable within a factor of
|A|
, but hard to approximate within
2^{\log^{1-\epsilon}N}
for any
\epsilon>0
. Thus, there is still room to improve both the lower and upper approximation bounds. Furthermore, the complexity of the problem for series-parallel digraphs remains open.

Introduction

It is a familiar observation from plane geometry that for every positive integer
n
, it is possible to encircle precisely
n
points with integer coordinates in the Euclidean plane (this seems to be first mentioned by Steinhaus in 1957 as problem 498 in volume 10 of
Matematyka: Czasopismo dla Nauczycieli
, a journal for Mathematics teachers in Poland, and later also as Problem 24 in
[
21
, p. 17]
. In May 1967, Steinhaus put in the New Scottish Book (that revived the traditions of the Lvov Scottish Book in Wrocław) problem 782 of whether every measurable subset of the plane that has infinite Lebesgue measure can be moved so that the intersection of the result set with the integer lattice is infinite. Worth mentioning is also a result from plane geometry by Fast and Świerczkowski
[
10
]
, which asserts that for any open bounded region
G
in the Euclidean plane with a simple closed curve as its boundary and an integer-valued area, there exists an (isometric copy of the) integral lattice sharing precisely
|G|
points with
G
.

The possibility of encircling any number of lattice points, mentioned by Steinhaus, was generalised by Zwoleński
[
23
]
to arbitrary Hilbert spaces as follows: for every infinite subset
A
in a Hilbert space
X
with a finite intersection with each open ball, there exists a dense subset
Y
of
X
such that for all
y\in Y
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
there is an open ball
B
centred at
y
with
|A\cap B|=n
. Kochanek and the second-named author
[
16
]
expanded beyond Hilbert spaces, characterising geometrically Banach spaces for which Zwoleński’s assertion remains valid; these were termed spaces having
property
(S)
after Steinhaus.

In this paper, we adopt one of the equivalent geometric conditions enumerated in
[
16
, Theorem A]
as the definition of spaces with property
(S)
.

Definition 1.1
.

A normed space
X
has
property
(S)
if, for every distinct points
x
and
y
in the unit sphere of
X
and every
a>0
, there exists a vector
z
in
X
with
\|z\|<a
such that
\|x+z\|\neq\|y+z\|
.

This definition includes possibly incomplete spaces; however, completeness is necessary to establish the equivalence between the various properties enumerated in
[
16
, Theorem A]
. (In
[
16
]
, the above-introduced property was called property
(S^{\prime\prime})
, while a metric space
X
was said to have property
(S)
if, for any infinite subset
A
of
X
with a finite intersection with every ball, there exists a dense subset
Y
of
X
such that for all
y\in Y
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
there is a ball
B
centred at
y
with
|A\cap B|=n
.) The goal of this paper is to quantify property
(S)
, specifically to introduce
uniform
property
(S)
, and to demonstrate that various well-known Banach spaces possess uniform property
(S)
, or at least can be equivalently renormed to have uniform property
(S)
. In the process of doing this, we solve a problem about property
(S)
that was raised in
[
16
]
.

Let
X
be a normed space. For
x,y\in X
and
a>0
, we define the
(S)
-
modulus
by

(1)

U_{X}(x,y;a)=U(x,y;a):=\sup_{\|z\|\leqslant a}\big|\|x+z\|-\|y+z\|\big|.

For
0<d<2
and
a>0
we define

(2)

U_{X}(d;a)=U(d;a):=\inf_{\overset{x,y\in S_{X},}{\|x-y\|\geqslant d}}U(x,y;a).

Definition 1.2
.

A Banach space
X
has
uniform property (S)
provided that for all
0<d<2,a>0
one has

U_{X}(d;a)>0.

It is evident that uniform property
(S)
implies property
(S)
, and a standard compactness argument shows that if
X
is finite-dimensional and has property
(S)
, then it also has uniform property
(S)
. Uniform property
(S)
is arguably more manageable than property
(S)
because the
(S)
-modulus,
U(d;a)
, is stable under passing to completions and ultrapowers. Moreover,
U(x,y;a)
changes only slightly if
x
and
y
are perturbed, provided the perturbation is small relative to
d
and
a
. The stability of
U(d;a)
will be used in the sequel without further comment.

The modulus of uniform property (S), as defined, may be interpreted as the
p=1
case of the whole scale of moduli whose strict positivity is equivalent to uniform property (S). Indeed, for a normed space
X
and
p\in[1,\infty)
we set

(3)

U_{X}^{(p)}(d;a)=U^{(p)}(d;a):=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in S_{X}\\
\|x-y\|\geqslant d\end{subarray}}U^{(p)}(x,y;a)\quad(d\in(0,2),\ a>0),

where

(4)

U_{X}^{(p)}(x,y;a)=U^{(p)}(x,y;a):=\sup_{\|z\|\leqslant a}\big|\|x+z\|^{p}-\|y+z\|^{p}\big|\quad(x,y\in X,\ a>0).

Certainly,
U_{X}^{(1)}(d;a)=U_{X}(d;a)
and for
p>1
we have
U_{X}^{(p)}(d;a)>0
if and only if
U_{X}(d;a)>0
, which follows (for small enough

) from the elementary fact:

Lemma 1.3
.

Fix
p>1
and
a\in(0,1)
. If
r,s\in[1-a,1+a]
, then by the mean value theorem

p(1-a)^{p-1}\,|r-s|\ \leqslant\ |r^{p}-s^{p}|\ \leqslant\ p(1+a)^{p-1}\,|r-s|.

Consequently, for every normed space
X
and every
a\in(0,1)
we have, for
0<d<2
,

p(1-a)^{p-1}\,U_{X}(d;a)\ \leqslant\ U_{X}^{(p)}(d;a)\ \leqslant\ p(1+a)^{p-1}\,U_{X}(d;a).

In particular, for any
a\in(0,1)
and
p>1
,
U_{X}^{(p)}(d;a)>0
if and only if
U_{X}(d;a)>0
.

Proof.

Apply the one-variable mean value theorem to
t\mapsto t^{p}
on
[1-a,1+a]
and note that
the intermediate value
\xi
lies in
[1-a,1+a]
.
∎

Let us illustrate it briefly with inner-product spaces.

Proposition 1.4
.

Let
X
be an inner-product space of dimension at least
2
. Then
U_{X}^{(2)}(d;a)=2ad
for
a>0
and
d\in(0,2)
.

Proof.

Let
x,y\in S_{X}
and
z
with
\|z\|\leqslant a
. We have

\|x+z\|^{2}=\|x\|^{2}+\|z\|^{2}+2\langle x,z\rangle
and
\|y+z\|^{2}=\|y\|^{2}+\|z\|^{2}+2\langle y,z\rangle
. Since
x
and
y
are unit vectors, we have

\big|\|x+z\|^{2}-\|y+z\|^{2}\big|=2\big|\langle x-y,z\rangle\big|.

Maximising over all
z
with
\|z\|\leqslant a
, we get

\sup_{\|z\|\leqslant a}\big|\langle x-y,z\rangle\big|=a\|x-y\|,

hence

\sup_{\|z\|\leqslant a}\big|\|x+z\|^{2}-\|y+z\|^{2}\big|=2a\|x-y\|.

Therefore,

U_{X}^{(2)}(d;a)=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in S_{X}\\
\|x-y\|\geqslant d\end{subarray}}2a\|x-y\|=2ad,

where the last equality uses that in an inner-product space with
\dim X\geqslant 2
, for every
d\in(0,2)
there exist
x,y\in S_{X}
with
\|x-y\|=d
.
∎

It is possible that every Banach space, or at least every separable Banach space, has an equivalent renorming with uniform property
(S)
. Conversely, there might be non-separable Banach spaces that do not have renormings with property
(S)
. In Section
5

Remark 1.5
.

Fix a modulus of uniform convexity
\delta(\cdot)
. We want to prove that
U_{X}(d;a)\geqslant\frac{4a}{d}\delta(d)
. For this, take
d\in(0,2]
and let
x,y\in S_{X}
be such that
\|x-y\|=d
. Fix
0<a\leqslant d/2
, and set
z=\frac{a}{d}(y-x)
, so

is on the line segment from
x
to
\tfrac{1}{2}(x+y)
. Thus, we may pick
\lambda,\beta\in[0,1]
so that

(5)

x+z=\lambda x+(1-\lambda)\tfrac{x+y}{2}\mbox{ and }y=\beta\tfrac{x+y}{2}+(1-\beta)(y+z).

By the very definition of
\delta(\cdot)
, we have
\|(x+y)/2\|\leqslant 1-\delta(d)
.
Since
1-\lambda=\tfrac{2a}{d}
, we get

\|x+z\|\leqslant 1-2\cdot\tfrac{a}{d}+2\cdot\tfrac{a}{d}(1-\delta(d))=1-\tfrac{2a}{d}\delta(d).

Also,
\tfrac{\beta}{2}+\tfrac{1-\beta}{-\tfrac{a}{d}}=0
yields
\beta=\tfrac{2a}{2a+d}
, so the expression for

in (
5
) gives

1\leqslant\frac{2a}{2a+d}(1-\delta(d))+\frac{d}{2a+d}\|y+z\|,

which simplifies to
1+2\cdot\tfrac{a}{d}\cdot\delta(d)\leqslant\|y+z\|
. Finally,
\|y+z\|-\|x+z\|\geqslant\frac{4a}{d}\cdot\delta(d)
.

Property
(S)
is weaker than strict convexity. Indeed,
[

imply together that strictly convex spaces have

, yet only in dimension at most two are these properties equivalent. In infinite dimension, for example, the strictly convex renorming of

given by

\|x\|:=\|x\|_{\infty}+\|Tx\|_{2}\quad(x\in c_{0}),

where

is an injective operator from

. We do not know whether

has an equivalent norm with uniform property

. We remark in passing that should

have such a renorming, so would the quotient space
\ell_{\infty}/c_{0}
(at least under the Continuum Hypothesis). This is because having uniform property

is stable under taking ultrapowers and under CH every ultrapower of

(this follows from Parovičenko’s theorem as explained in

We begin by listing three classes of Banach spaces with uniform property

.

Theorem A
.

The following spaces have uniform property
(S)
:

(i)

the Bochner spaces

for any atomless (probability) measure space

,

, and any Banach space

. In particular,

•

universal for all separable Banach spaces;

•

L_{1}([0,1],C(\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}},L_{1}[0,1]))

(ii)

Gurariĭ spaces of arbitrary density satisfy the estimate

U_{G}(d;a)=\Big(\frac{4a}{2+d}\wedge 1\Big)d\qquad(0<d\leqslant 2,\;0<a).

(iii)

Banach lattices of almost universal disposition for finitely generated Banach lattices including the one constructed by Tursi
[

]
.

The latter part of Theorem

is an immediate consequence of clause (

, and

Gurariĭ spaces are Banach spaces exhibiting almost universal disposition for finite-dimensional normed spaces. Tursi

Corollary 1.6

Every Banach space embeds isometrically into a non-strictly convex space with the same density that has uniform property

Pełczyński’s space

Corollary 1.7

The space

Let

Suppose that

. For

, let us consider

and take unit vectors

We just need

Let

We remark in passing that for any index set

2.
Preliminaries

The notation used in this paper is primarily based on
[
1
]
.
S_{X}
denotes the unit sphere of a normed space
X
. A Banach space
G
is called
Gurariĭ
if it exhibits
almost universal disposition for finite-dimensional normed spaces
, as follows:

Given any finite-dimensional spaces
E\subseteq F
, an isometric embedding
T\colon E\to G
, and
\varepsilon>0
, there exists an extension
T_{\varepsilon}
of
T
from
F
into
G
with
\|T_{\varepsilon}\|\|T_{\varepsilon}^{-1}\|<1+\varepsilon
.

The term pays homage to V. I. Gurariĭ, who demonstrated the existence of separable Gurariĭ spaces in
[
13
]
. Furthermore, there exists a unique separable Gurariĭ space up to isometry. Garbulińska-Węgrzyn and Kubiś constructed Gurariĭ spaces with arbitrary density in
[
11
]
.

Recently, Tursi
[
22
]
introduced a lattice-theoretic analogue of Gurariĭ spaces. Specifically, Tursi established the existence of a unique (up to isometry) separable Banach lattice,
\mathfrak{T}
, exhibiting the following property, which we designate as
(T)
:

Given any two finitely generated Banach lattices
E\subseteq F
with
E=\langle a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\rangle
, lattice-isometric embedding
J\colon E\to\mathfrak{T}
, and
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a lattice-isometric embedding
\widehat{J}\colon F\to\mathfrak{T}
such that
\max\limits_{i=1,\ldots,n}\|Ja_{i}-\widehat{J}a_{i}\|<\varepsilon
.

The lattice
\mathfrak{T}
was constructed as the Fraïssé limit of the category of finitely generated Banach lattices, similarly to how the Gurariĭ space can be identified as the Fra"iss’e limit of the category of finite-dimensional normed spaces (
[
6
, Theorem 3.3]
).

Proof of Theorem A.

For (

with
X

\Sigma

L_{1}(\mu)
is separable, in which case it is isometric to

. In particular,
L_{1}(\mu,X)\equiv Y
isometrically, where

Suppose that

are two unit vectors in
X
. Set

and suppose that

. Let
A\in\mathcal{A}
be a set with
\mu(A)=a
. Take

consisting of a.e.

\displaystyle\|x+z\|_{L_{1}(\mu,X)}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma}\|(x+z)(t)\|_{X}\,d\mu(t)=\int_{A^{c}}\|x\|_{X}\,d\mu+\int_{A}\|x-x\|_{X}\,d\mu

\displaystyle=(1-a)\cdot\|x\|_{X}+a\cdot 0=1-a,

\displaystyle\|y+z\|_{L_{1}(\mu,X)}

\displaystyle=\int_{A^{c}}\|y\|_{X}\,d\mu+\int_{A}\|y-x\|_{X}\,d\mu=(1-a)\cdot\|y\|_{X}+a\,\|y-x\|_{X}

\displaystyle=(1-a)+a\,d=1-a+ad.

Thus,

.

To estimate

in a dense subset of

\|\lambda x\,\mathbf{1}_{B}\|_{S}=m

z:=-\lambda x\,\mathbf{1}_{B}
. Then
\|z\|_{S}=m\leqslant\alpha
.

On
B
we have
|y+z|=|y-\lambda x|=|y|+\lambda x\geqslant|y|
and
|x+z|=(1-\lambda)x\leqslant x
, so

\|y+z\|_{2}\geqslant\|y\|_{2}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\|x+z\|_{2}\leqslant\|x\|_{2}.

Moreover, writing
\delta:=\|z\|_{1}
(so
\delta=\lambda\|x\mathbf{1}_{B}\|_{1}
), we have

\|y+z\|_{1}=\|y\|_{1}+\delta,\qquad\|x+z\|_{1}=\|x\|_{1}-\delta,

whence

(\|y+z\|_{1}^{2}-\|y\|_{1}^{2})+(\|x\|_{1}^{2}-\|x+z\|_{1}^{2})=(\|y\|_{1}+\delta)^{2}-\|y\|_{1}^{2}+\|x\|_{1}^{2}-(\|x\|_{1}-\delta)^{2}=2\delta(\|x\|_{1}+\|y\|_{1}).

Lemma
3.4
therefore yields

\|y+z\|_{S}-\|x+z\|_{S}\ \geqslant\ \frac{2\delta(\|x\|_{1}+\|y\|_{1})}{2(1+m)}.

Using
\delta\geqslant\|z\|_{S}/\sqrt{2}=m/\sqrt{2}
and
\|x\|_{1}+\|y\|_{1}\geqslant\sqrt{2}
, we obtain

\|y+z\|_{S}-\|x+z\|_{S}\ \geqslant\ \frac{m}{1+m}\ \geqslant\ \frac{m}{2}=\frac{\alpha}{8}.

This proves (
14
) in Case 1.

Case 2:

\|(x-y)\mathbf{1}_{B}\|_{1}\leqslant\alpha/2
.

We first reduce to the situation where one of the two
one-sided

\ell_{1}
-gaps is large.
By (
13
) we have
\|x-y\|_{1}\geqslant\|x-y\|_{S}/\sqrt{2}\geqslant\alpha/\sqrt{2}
, hence

(15)

\|(x-y)\mathbf{1}_{B^{c}}\|_{1}=\|x-y\|_{1}-\|(x-y)\mathbf{1}_{B}\|_{1}\ \geqslant\ \frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{2}}-\frac{\alpha}{2}=\frac{\sqrt{2}-1}{2}\,\alpha.

Let

A:=\{\gamma\in B^{c}:\ y(\gamma)<x(\gamma)\},\qquad A^{\prime}:=\{\gamma\in B^{c}:\ x(\gamma)<y(\gamma)\}.

Then

\|(x-y)\mathbf{1}_{B^{c}}\|_{1}=\|(x-y)\mathbf{1}_{A}\|_{1}+\|(y-x)\mathbf{1}_{A^{\prime}}\|_{1},

so at least one of
\|(x-y)\mathbf{1}_{A}\|_{1}
and
\|(y-x)\mathbf{1}_{A^{\prime}}\|_{1}
is

\geqslant\frac{\sqrt{2}-1}{4}\alpha
.
Since
U(x,y;a)=U(y,x;a)
and
\|x-y\|_{S}=\|y-x\|_{S}
, by interchanging
x
and
y
if necessary
we may assume

(16)

\|(x-y)\mathbf{1}_{A}\|_{1}\ \geqslant\ \frac{\sqrt{2}-1}{4}\,\alpha.

From now on we work with the parameter

\alpha_{0}:=\frac{\sqrt{2}-1}{4}\,\alpha,

and (to simplify notation) we relabel
\alpha_{0}
as
\alpha
.
This causes only an absolute change in the final universal constant
c
in (
14
),
and it keeps the constraint
\|z\|_{S}\leqslant\alpha
(since
\alpha_{0}\leqslant\alpha
).

Subcase 2a (small
\ell_{2}
-mass on
A
).

Assume

\|(x-y)\mathbf{1}_{A}\|_{2}\ \leqslant\ \Big(\frac{\alpha}{16}\Big)^{\!2}.

Split

A_{1}:=\{\gamma\in A:\ x-y\leqslant(\alpha/16)\,x\},\qquad A_{2}:=A\setminus A_{1}.

If
\|(x-y)\mathbf{1}_{A_{2}}\|_{1}\leqslant\alpha/16
, then, since
\|(x-y)\mathbf{1}_{A}\|_{1}\geqslant\alpha
by (
16
),

\|x\|_{1}\ \geqslant\ \|x\mathbf{1}_{A_{1}}\|_{1}\ \geqslant\ \frac{16}{\alpha}\,\|(x-y)\mathbf{1}_{A_{1}}\|_{1}\ \geqslant\ \frac{16}{\alpha}\Big(\alpha-\frac{\alpha}{16}\Big)\ =\ 15\ >\ 1,

a contradiction. Hence
\|(x-y)\mathbf{1}_{A_{2}}\|_{1}>\alpha/16
.

Since
\|(x-y)\mathbf{1}_{A}\|_{\infty}\leqslant\|(x-y)\mathbf{1}_{A}\|_{2}\leqslant(\alpha/16)^{2}
, there exists a
finite
A_{3}\subset A_{2}
with

(17)

\Big(\frac{\alpha}{16}\Big)^{2}<\ \|(x-y)\mathbf{1}_{A_{3}}\|_{1}\ \leqslant\ \Big(\frac{\alpha}{16}\Big)^{2}+\Big(\frac{\alpha}{16}\Big)^{2}\ =\ \frac{\alpha^{2}}{128}.

From the definition of
A_{2}
we also have

(18)

\|x\mathbf{1}_{A_{3}}\|_{1}\ \leqslant\ \frac{16}{\alpha}\,\|(x-y)\mathbf{1}_{A_{3}}\|_{1}\ \leqslant\ \frac{\alpha}{8}.

Split
A_{3}=A_{4}\cup A_{5}
with

A_{4}:=\{\gamma\in A_{3}:\ y\leqslant x\leqslant 2y\},\qquad A_{5}:=\{\gamma\in A_{3}:\ 2y<x\}.

At least one of
\|(x-y)\mathbf{1}_{A_{4}}\|_{1}
and
\|(x-y)\mathbf{1}_{A_{5}}\|_{1}
is

\geqslant\tfrac{1}{2}(\alpha/16)^{2}
.

2a.1: the
A_{4}
-branch.

Set
z:=-2y\,\mathbf{1}_{A_{4}}
. Using (
13
) (so
\|u\|_{S}\leqslant\sqrt{2}\,\|u\|_{1}\leqslant 2\|u\|_{1}
) and (
18
), we get

\frac{1}{2}\,\|z\|_{S}\leqslant\|z\|_{1}=2\|y\mathbf{1}_{A_{4}}\|_{1}\leqslant 2\|x\mathbf{1}_{A_{3}}\|_{1}\leqslant\alpha/4,

and hence
\|z\|_{S}\leqslant\alpha/2
.

On
A_{4}
we have
y+z=-y
and
x+z=x-2y\leqslant 0
, hence

|y+z|=y
and
|x+z|=2y-x\leqslant x
.
Consequently,

\|y+z\|_{2}=\|y\|_{2}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\|x+z\|_{2}\leqslant\|x\|_{2},

so Lemma
3.4
applies (with
\theta=\alpha/2
). Moreover,
\|y+z\|_{1}=\|y\|_{1}
and

\|x\|_{1}-\|x+z\|_{1}=2\|(x-y)\mathbf{1}_{A_{4}}\|_{1},

so

\|x\|_{1}^{2}-\|x+z\|_{1}^{2}\ \geqslant\ (\|x\|_{1}-\|x+z\|_{1})\|x\|_{1}\ \geqslant\ \frac{2}{\sqrt{2}}\,\|(x-y)\mathbf{1}_{A_{4}}\|_{1}.

Therefore, by Lemma
3.4
,

\|y+z\|_{S}-\|x+z\|_{S}\ \geqslant\ \frac{1}{2(1+\alpha/2)}\cdot\frac{2}{\sqrt{2}}\,\|(x-y)\mathbf{1}_{A_{4}}\|_{1}\ \geqslant\ c_{2}\,\alpha^{2}.

2a.2: the
A_{5}
-branch.

Set
z:=-x\,\mathbf{1}_{A_{5}}
. Then, by (
13
) and (
18
),

\|z\|_{S}\leqslant\sqrt{2}\,\|z\|_{1}=\sqrt{2}\,\|x\mathbf{1}_{A_{5}}\|_{1}\leqslant\sqrt{2}\,\|x\mathbf{1}_{A_{3}}\|_{1}\leqslant\sqrt{2}\cdot\frac{\alpha}{8}\leqslant\frac{\alpha}{4}.

On
A_{5}
we have
x+z=0
and
y+z=y-x<0
, hence
|x+z|=0\leqslant x
and

|y+z|=x-y\geqslant y
(since
x>2y
on
A_{5}
). Consequently,

\|y+z\|_{2}\geqslant\|y\|_{2}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\|x+z\|_{2}\leqslant\|x\|_{2},

so Lemma
3.4
applies (with
\theta=\alpha/2
). Also,

(\|y+z\|_{1}-\|y\|_{1})+(\|x\|_{1}-\|x+z\|_{1})=2\|(x-y)\mathbf{1}_{A_{5}}\|_{1}.

Using
\|x\|_{1},\|y\|_{1}\geqslant 1/\sqrt{2}
, we get

(\|y+z\|_{1}^{2}-\|y\|_{1}^{2})+(\|x\|_{1}^{2}-\|x+z\|_{1}^{2})\ \geqslant\ \frac{2}{\sqrt{2}}\,\|(x-y)\mathbf{1}_{A_{5}}\|_{1},

and hence, by Lemma
3.4
,

\|y+z\|_{S}-\|x+z\|_{S}\ \geqslant\ c_{2}\,\alpha^{2},

since
\|(x-y)\mathbf{1}_{A_{5}}\|_{1}\geqslant\tfrac{1}{2}(\alpha/16)^{2}
.

Subcase 2b (large
\ell_{2}
-mass on
A
).

Assume

\|(x-y)\mathbf{1}_{A}\|_{2}\ >\ \Big(\frac{\alpha}{16}\Big)^{\!2}.

Put

\Delta:=\|x\|_{1}-\|y\|_{1}.

In the computation below we will produce a lower bound for

\|x+z\|_{S}^{2}-\|y+z\|_{S}^{2}

which contains the term
2t\Delta\|u\|_{1}
, so it is convenient to assume
\Delta\geqslant 0
.
We now explain why we may do so without loss of generality, and why this does not interfere
with the Case 2 set-up.

For the current ordered pair, redefine
A\subseteq B^{c}
and
A^{\prime}\subseteq B^{c}
as before.
If necessary, interchange
x
and
y
(using
U(x,y;a)=U(y,x;a)
) so that the one-sided estimate
(
16
) holds for
A
.
(This additional interchange is harmless in the branch where we fall back to Subcase 2a,
since Subcase 2a does not use the sign of
\Delta
.)

Step 1: enforcing
\Delta\geqslant 0
.

If
\Delta\geqslant 0
, do nothing. If
\Delta<0
, replace the ordered pair
(x,y)
by
(y,x)
.
This is legitimate because

U(x,y;a)=U(y,x;a)\quad\text{and}\quad\|x-y\|_{S}=\|y-x\|_{S},

and all standing assumptions of Case 2 (in particular
\|(x-y)\mathbf{1}_{B}\|_{1}\leqslant\alpha/2
)
are symmetric in
x
and
y
. After this replacement we relabel the pair again as
(x,y)
,
so from now on we may assume

(19)

\Delta=\|x\|_{1}-\|y\|_{1}\geqslant 0.

Step 2.

For this (possibly swapped) ordered pair, define
A\subseteq B^{c}
exactly as before by

A:=\{\gamma\in B^{c}:\ y(\gamma)<x(\gamma)\},\qquad u:=(x-y)\mathbf{1}_{A}\ (\geqslant 0).

We now distinguish two possibilities.

•

If
\|u\|_{2}\leqslant(\alpha/16)^{2}
, then (for the current ordered pair) we are in the situation
of Subcase 2a, and the argument of Subcase 2a applies to yield the desired lower bound for

U(x,y;a)
.
(Here we use only the symmetry
U(x,y;a)=U(y,x;a)
already exploited above; Subcase 2a does not
require (
19
).)

•

Otherwise,

(20)

\|u\|_{2}>(\alpha/16)^{2},

and together with (
19
) this is exactly the standing assumption needed for
the Subcase 2b estimate below.

Thus, in proving Subcase 2b it suffices to treat the situation where
(
19
) and (
20
) hold simultaneously.

Now put

z:=t\,u,\qquad t:=\min\Big\{1,\ \frac{\alpha}{\|u\|_{S}}\Big\}.

Then
\|z\|_{S}=t\|u\|_{S}\leqslant\alpha
, and since
\|u\|_{S}\leqslant\|x-y\|_{S}\leqslant 2
we have
t\geqslant\alpha/2
.
Moreover,
\operatorname{supp}u\subseteq B^{c}
, so
x,y\geqslant 0
on
\operatorname{supp}u
and therefore

\|x+z\|_{1}=\|x\|_{1}+t\|u\|_{1},\qquad\|y+z\|_{1}=\|y\|_{1}+t\|u\|_{1}.

Also,

\|x+z\|_{2}^{2}-\|y+z\|_{2}^{2}=(\|x\|_{2}^{2}-\|y\|_{2}^{2})+2t\,\langle x-y,u\rangle=(\|x\|_{2}^{2}-\|y\|_{2}^{2})+2t\,\|u\|_{2}^{2},

since
u=(x-y)
on
A
and
u=0
off
A
.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\|x+z\|_{S}^{2}-\|y+z\|_{S}^{2}

\displaystyle=\bigl(\|x+z\|_{1}^{2}-\|y+z\|_{1}^{2}\bigr)+\bigl(\|x+z\|_{2}^{2}-\|y+z\|_{2}^{2}\bigr)

\displaystyle=(\|x\|_{1}^{2}-\|y\|_{1}^{2})+2t(\|x\|_{1}-\|y\|_{1})\|u\|_{1}+(\|x\|_{2}^{2}-\|y\|_{2}^{2})+2t\|u\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=2t(\|x\|_{1}-\|y\|_{1})\|u\|_{1}+2t\|u\|_{2}^{2}\ \geqslant\ 2t\|u\|_{2}^{2}\ >\ 2t\Big(\frac{\alpha}{16}\Big)^{\!4},

where we used
\|x\|_{S}=\|y\|_{S}=1
to cancel the zeroth-order terms and (
19
).

Therefore,

\|x+z\|_{S}-\|y+z\|_{S}=\frac{\|x+z\|_{S}^{2}-\|y+z\|_{S}^{2}}{\|x+z\|_{S}+\|y+z\|_{S}}\ \geqslant\ \frac{2t(\alpha/16)^{4}}{2(1+\|z\|_{S})}\ \geqslant\ \frac{t\,\alpha^{4}}{16^{4}(1+\alpha)}\ \geqslant\ c_{3}\,\frac{\alpha^{5}}{1+a},

where
c_{3}:=\dfrac{1}{2\cdot 16^{4}}
(using
\|z\|_{S}\leqslant\alpha\leqslant a
).

Combining Case 1, Subcase 2a and Subcase 2b, we have produced, in all situations, a vector

z
with
\|z\|_{S}\leqslant\alpha
satisfying (
14
) with a universal constant

c:=\min\Big\{\ \frac{1}{8},\ c_{2},\ \frac{1}{2\cdot 16^{4}}\ \Big\}\ >\ 0.

Since
x,y
with
\|x-y\|_{S}\geqslant d
were arbitrary, it follows that

U(d;a)\geqslant c\,\min\{\alpha,\alpha^{2},\alpha^{5}/(1+a)\}>0
. Thus

(\ell_{1}(\Gamma),\|\cdot\|_{S})
has uniform property
(S)
.
∎

4.
Appendix: Banach lattices of almost universal disposition for finitely generated Banach lattices

Proposition 4.1
.

Every Banach lattice embeds isometrically as a closed sublattice of a Banach lattice with property
(T)
of the same density. Banach lattices with property
(T)
have property
(S)
.

Proof.

By the main result of
[
22
]
, the claim holds for separable Banach lattices, that is of density
\kappa=\omega
. Let
X
be a Banach lattice of uncountable density
\kappa
. Inductively, suppose that each Banach lattice that is generated by fewer than
\kappa
elements say
\lambda<\kappa
, embeds into a Banach lattice with property (
T
) that is generated by at most
\lambda+\omega
many elements.

Write
X
as the completion of a strictly increasing union of lattices generated by fewer than
\kappa
elements. Without loss of generality, this chain is continuous (we have completions at the limit steps), so
X
contains a dense sublattice of the form
\bigcup_{\alpha<\kappa}X_{\alpha}
.

Let
u_{\alpha}\colon X_{\alpha}\to T_{\alpha}
be an isometric lattice embedding, where each lattice
T_{\alpha}
has property
(T)
and is generated by
\alpha+\omega
elements. The embeddings may be chosen in a way that respects
T_{\alpha}
, that is,
\alpha<\beta
implies that
T_{\alpha}\subset T_{\beta}
and
u_{\beta}|_{T_{\alpha}}=u_{\alpha}
. Indeed, at the limit steps, this is easy as completions of chains of
T_{\alpha}
will retain property
(T)
. In the successor case, say for
\alpha=\beta+1
. The category of Banach lattices with isometric lattice embeddings admits push-outs, which have the property that the density of the push-out of two Banach lattices is the bigger of the densities of the respective lattices (see
[
4
, Section 3]
and
[
5
, p. 15]
).

Consequently, there is a Banach lattice
W
with
{\rm dens}\,W<\kappa
containing
T_{\beta}
so that we may extend
u_{\beta}
to a map
u\colon X_{\alpha}\to W
. Thus, by the inductive hypothesis, we may embed
W
into a Banach lattice with property
(T)
that has the same density.

The proof is complete as we have embedded
X
into the closure of the union of
T_{\alpha}
for
\alpha<\kappa
, which retains property
(T)
.∎

That Banach lattices with property
(T)
have property
(S)
is proved in the last paragraph of the proof of Theorem
A
.

5.
Open problems

Let us list open problems concerning uniform property
(S)
.

(1)

Does
c_{0}
have equivalent renormings with uniform property
(S)
?

(2)

Does every space have an equivalent renorming with uniform property
(S)
?

(3)

Is
U(d,a)
always smaller than
da
(up to a constant)?

(4)

Characterise those pointed metric spaces whose Lipschitz-free Banach spaces have uniform property (S). For example, by Rademacher’s theorem,
F([0,1])
is isometric to
L_{1}[0,1]
so it does have uniform property (S), unlike
F(\mathbb{N})
that is isometric to
\ell_{1}
.

(5)

If
X
has uniform property (S), must
X^{**}
have it too? The converse follows from the principle of local reflexivity.

(6)

If
X
and
Y
are normed spaces and
p\in(1,\infty)
, is there a constant
C_{p}>0
so that the
\ell_{p}
-direct sum of
X
and
Y
satisfies

U_{X\oplus_{p}Y}(d;a)\geqslant C_{p}\cdot\min\{U_{X}(d;a),U_{Y}(d;a),U_{\ell_{p}}(d;a)\},

where
d\in(0,2)
and
a>0
?

(7)

Is the set of spaces with uniform property (S) Borel in a Polish space encoding all separable Banach spaces? If so, what can be said about the Borel complexity of this set?

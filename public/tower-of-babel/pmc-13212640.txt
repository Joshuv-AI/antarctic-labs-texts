Addendum: Assessing suitable habitat for freshwater mussel reintroductions using 3D-printed subadultreplicates
Authors: Megan DiNicola, Christopher L Riggins, Sarah Schultz, Colin Phillips, Belize Lane
Published: 2026

Addendum to: Scientific Reports 10.1038/s41598-025-18244-y, published online 26 September 2025

Following publication, concerns were raised that the Article did not discuss in full the limitations of using 3D-printed mussels as a surrogate for live mussels, as well as concerns related to potential plastic pollution. These concerns have been addressed in our article1 that was under consideration at the same time as this Article but published a few months later.

In our companion article1, where we used an experimental flume study to directly compare the mobilization thresholds of 3D-printed and live subadult Lampsilis teres, we have discussed the lack of behavioral response to stress events by 3D-printed mussels compared to the burrowing ability of the live specimen. With that said, based on a generalized linear mixed-effects model, we found that mussel mobilization index values describing mussel stability relative to bed stability were not statistically different between live and 3D-printed specimen across the sediment types considered. Protective behaviors by live subadult mussels, including those not captured in our companion flume study, would provide a factor of safety between the field-based mussel mobilization thresholds identified using 3D-printed mussels and those of live subadult mussels1.

Additionally, our companion article discusses the use of plastic polymers in the 3D-printing process and provides alternative materials that may be better suited for future studies completed in sensitive riverine environments. We encourage anyone wishing to apply similar methods of 3D-printing and tracking subadult mussel replicates to instead use readily available biodegradable filaments like some types of PLA (polylactic acid), PHA (Polyhydroxyalkanoates), or composite biodegradable materials that are specifically designed for aquatic environments.

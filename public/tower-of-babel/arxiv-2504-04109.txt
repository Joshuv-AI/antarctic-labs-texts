Variation of cones of divisors in a family of varieties – Fano type case
Sung Rak Choi, Zhan Li, Chuyu Zhou

Abstract
. We investigate the relationship between the Fano type property on fibers over a Zariski dense subset and the global Fano type property. We establish the invariance of Néron-Severi spaces, nef cones, effective cones, movable cones, and Mori chamber decompositions for a family of Fano type varieties after a dominant generically finite base change. Additionally, we show the uniform behavior of the minimal model program for this family. These results are applied to the boundedness problem of Fano type varieties.

14J45, 14E30

1.
Introduction

We work over the field of complex numbers.

For a projective variety
X
, various cones of divisors can be associated within the Néron-Severi space
N^{1}(X)
. Notable among them are the effective cone
{\rm{Eff}}(X)
, the nef cone
{\rm{Nef}}(X)
, and the movable cone
{\rm{Mov}}(X)
. Moreover, the movable cone admits a finer decomposition known as the Mori chamber decomposition. These cones as well as their structures “linearize” the geometric properties of the variety.

Suppose that
X\to T
is a family of varieties. It is natural to associate the aforementioned cones with each fiber and study how the deformation of the varieties affects the structure of the various cones. However, these cones may change drastically. For instance, there may not exist an open subset
U\subset T
where the dimension of the Néron-Severi space
\dim N^{1}(X_{t})
remains constant for all
t\in U
.

One motivation for this note is to apply the cone structure to study the moduli problem of Fano type varieties. Recall that a projective variety
X
is of Fano type if there exists a divisor
\Delta
such that
(X,\Delta)
has klt singularities and
-(K_{X}+\Delta)
is ample. Therefore, instead of considering a family of Fano type varieties, one should only assume the existence of a Zariski dense subset over which the fibers are of Fano type. In general, such a Zariski dense subset might be very sparse. Thus, it is natural to ask:

Question 1.1
.

Let
X\to T
be a family of varieties. Suppose that the fibers are of Fano type over a Zariski dense subset of
T
. After shrinking
T
, is
X
of Fano type over
T
?

This natural question seems to be rather subtle, and we can only provide a partial answer.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
f:X\to T
be a projective, surjective morphism between varieties such that
f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}=\mathcal{O}_{T}
. Suppose that there exists a Zariski dense subset
S\subset T
such that for any
s\in S
, the fiber
X_{s}
is a Fano type variety. Then, after shrinking
T
, there exists a divisor
D
on
X
such that
(X,D)
has lc singularities and

K_{X}+D\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}0/T.

Furthermore, if for any
s\in S
, the fiber
X_{s}
falls into one of the following cases:

(i)

X_{s}
is a klt weak Fano variety for any
s\in S
,

(ii)

X_{s}
is of Fano type for any
s\in S
and
S
consists of very general points,

(iii)

X_{s}
is of Fano type for any
s\in S
and
\{X_{s}\mid s\in S\}
admits bounded klt complements,

then, after shrinking
T
, we have that
X
is of Fano type over
T
.

In the above theorem, a variety
X
is called klt weak Fano if
X
has klt singularities and the anticanonical divisor
-K_{X}
is nef and big; a set
S
is said to consist of very general points if
S
is the complement of the union of countably many proper Zariski subvarieties. For other notions and detailed explanations, see Section
2
.

By the invariance of plurigenera, varieties of general type deform to varieties of general type (see
[
14
]
). The above theorem can be viewed as a weak analogy to this result for Fano type varieties. The proof of this natural theorem, however, requires deep results on complements as developed in
[
4
]
. With this result in mind, to study the deformation of cones of Fano type varieties in the above cases, we can always assume that each fiber is of Fano type after shrinking the base. It turns out that various cones exhibit nice behaviors in this family. As a first step, we show that, after a dominant generically finite base change and a suitable shrinking of the base, the Néron-Severi space of each fiber coincides with the restriction of the Néron-Severi space of the total space.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
f:X\to T
be a projective fibration. Suppose that
S\subset T
is a Zariski dense subset such that for any
s\in S
, the fiber
X_{s}
has rational singularities and

H^{1}(X_{s},\mathcal{O}_{X_{s}})=H^{2}(X_{s},\mathcal{O}_{X_{s}})=0.

(1)

If the natural restriction map
N^{1}(X/T)\to N^{1}(X_{t})
is surjective for very general
t\in T
, then there exists a non-empty open subset
T_{0}\subset T
, such that
{\mathcal{P}}ic_{X_{T_{0}}/T_{0}}\otimes\mathbb{R}
is a constant sheaf in the Zariski topology.

(2)

Up to a dominant generically finite base change of
T
, there exists a non-empty open subset
T_{0}\subset T
, such that
{\mathcal{P}}ic_{X_{T_{0}}/T_{0}}\otimes\mathbb{R}
is a constant sheaf in the Zariski topology.

Moreover, in both of the above cases, for any open subset
U\subset T_{0}
and any
t\in U
, the natural restriction maps

N^{1}(X_{T_{0}}/T_{0})\to N^{1}(X_{U}/U)\to N^{1}(X_{t})

are isomorphisms.

In the above theorem, “up to a dominant generically finite base change of
T
” means that we take a dominant generically finite morphism
T^{\prime}\to T
and replace
T
with
T^{\prime}
. The non-empty open subset
T_{0}
is then taken inside this new base
T^{\prime}
. Hence, after possibly further shrinking
T_{0}
, we are indeed performing an étale base change of an open subset of the original base.

The proof of the above result follows from an argument akin to
[
7
]
*§6, where a similar result is established in a slightly different setting. Due to the monodromy phenomenon, even in a locally trivial family, there are no natural identifications between the Néron-Severi spaces of different fibers. Theorem
1.3
enables comparisons between these spaces and their associated cones.

The following fiber-wise small
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial modification illustrates the necessity of considering families whose fibers have rational singularities and satisfy
h^{i}(X_{s},\mathcal{O}_{X_{s}})=0
for
i=1,2
, rather than restricting only to varieties of Fano type. Indeed, these properties are preserved under resolutions, which may result in varieties that are no longer of Fano type. Such fiber-wise small
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial modifications are essential in establishing the boundedness of moduli problems, although they have been used in the literature without proper justification.

The following result establishes the generic deformation invariance of various cones up to a base change.

Theorem 1.5

Let

(2)

For a family of varieties, one can shrink the base and apply Noetherian induction to obtain finitely many families of varieties such that the desired properties hold for each family. Moreover, this operation is necessary if one wants to establish the deformation invariance of nef cones even for a family of

-factorial terminal Fano varieties (see

36

(see counterexamples in
[

[

]
.
[

As an immediate application of these results, we establish a boundedness result concerning Fano type varieties. Let
X\to T

2.
Preliminaries

We introduce the necessary background materials. Along with this process, we fix the notation and terminology.

A variety means an integral separated scheme of finite type over
\mathbb{C}
. A point of a variety is understood to be a closed point and an open subset of a variety is meant to be a non-empty Zariski open subset, unless explicitly stated otherwise. For a variety
T
, by ”shrinking
T
” we mean replacing
T
with a Zariski open subset. A subset
S
of a variety consists of very general points if there exist at most countably many non-empty open subsets
U_{i}
such that
S=\cap U_{i}
.

A projective morphism
f:X\to S
between normal varieties is called a fibration if it is surjective and
f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}=\mathcal{O}_{S}
. If
S^{\prime}\to S
is a morphism, then
X_{S^{\prime}}
denotes the fiber product
X\times_{S}S^{\prime}
. Similarly, if
\mathcal{D}
is an
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier divisor on
X
, then
\mathcal{D}_{S^{\prime}}
denotes the pullback of
\mathcal{D}
to
X_{S^{\prime}}
. A birational map
g:X\dasharrow X^{\prime}
between normal varieties is called a birational contraction if
g^{-1}
does not contract any divisor.

Suppose that
\Delta\geq 0
is an
\mathbb{R}
-divisor on a normal variety
X
. Then
(X,\Delta)
is called a log pair. A log pair
(X,\Delta)
has klt singularities if
K_{X}+\Delta
is
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier where
K_{X}
is the canonical divisor of
X
, and there exists a log resolution
\pi:Y\to X
such that in the expression

(2.1)

K_{Y}=\pi^{*}(K_{X}+\Delta)+D,

the coefficients of
D
are greater than
-1
. Note that in (
2.1
),
K_{Y}
is chosen to be the unique Weil divisor on
Y
such that
\pi_{*}K_{Y}=K_{X}
. Similarly, if the coefficients of
D
are greater than or equal to
-1
, then
(X,\Delta)
is said to have lc singularities. See
[
25
]
*§2.3 for more detailed discussions.

Let
f:X\to T
be a projective morphism between normal varieties. We use “
/T
” to denote properties which are relative to
T
. Then
X
is of Fano type
/T
if there exists a divisor
\Delta
such that
(X,\Delta)
has klt singularities and
-(K_{X}+\Delta)
is ample
/T
. Note that in the definition, we do not assume that
K_{X}
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisor. By passing to a small
\mathbb{Q}
-factorization, it is straightforward to see that in the definition of Fano type variety,
\Delta
can be chosen to be a
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor. Moreover, if
K_{X}
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisor, then
X
is of Fano type
/T
if and only if there exists a
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisor
B
which is big over
T
such that
(X,B)
has klt singularities and
K_{X}+B\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}0/T
. Besides,
X\to T
is called a family of Fano type varieties if all of its closed fibers are Fano type varieties. A variety
X
is called weak Fano if
-K_{X}
is nef and big. Hence, a klt weak Fano variety is automatically of Fano type. For some
n\in\mathbb{N}
, a divisor
\Delta\in|-nK_{X}|
is called an lc (resp. klt)
n
-complement of
K_{X}
if
(X,\frac{1}{n}\Delta)
has lc (resp. klt) singularities (see
[
32
]
for a more general definition). A set of varieties
\mathcal{P}
is said to admit bounded lc (resp. klt) complements if there exists an integer
n
, depending only on
\mathcal{P}
, such that every element of
\mathcal{P}
has an lc (resp. klt)
n
-complement.

A Cartier divisor
D
is movable
/T
on
X/T
if the codimension of the support of the sheaf
{\rm coker}(f^{*}f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)\to\mathcal{O}_{X}(D))
is greater than or equal to
2
.
We list relevant vector spaces and cones which appear in this paper:

(1)

{\rm{Pic}}(X/T)_{\mathbb{Z}}
: the abelian group generated by Cartier divisors on
X
modulo linear equivalences over
T
.

(2)

{\rm{Pic}}(X/T)
: the
\mathbb{R}
-vector space generated by Cartier divisors on
X
modulo linear equivalences over
T
, which is the same as
{\rm{Pic}}(X/T)_{\mathbb{Z}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}
.

(3)

N^{1}(X/T)
: the
\mathbb{R}
-vector space generated by Cartier divisors on
X
modulo numerical equivalences over
T
.

(4)

N_{1}(X/T)
: the
\mathbb{R}
-vector space generated by curves that are contracted by
X\to T
modulo numerical equivalences.

(5)

{\rm{NE}}(X/T)\subset N_{1}(X/T)
: the cone generated by curves that are contracted by
X\to T
(its closure
\overline{{\rm{NE}}}(X/T)
is called the Mori cone).

(6)

(nef cone)
{\rm{Nef}}(X/T)\subset N^{1}(X/T)
: the cone generated by nef Cartier divisors on
X
over
T
.

(7)

(effective cone)
{\rm{Eff}}(X/T)\subset N^{1}(X/T)
: the cone generated by effective Cartier divisors on
X
over
T
(its closure
\overline{\rm{Eff}}(X/T)

(8)

(movable cone)
{\rm{Mov}}(X/T)\subset N^{1}(X/T)
: the cone generated by movable Cartier divisors on

.

If

, then we will omit

in the above notations. For simplicity, when we say that

is a divisor, we mean that
[D]\in N^{1}(X/T)
with

an
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier divisor. This convention applies to other cones as well. We use
{\rm Int}(C)
to denote the relative interior of the cone

. A cone

is called a rational polyhedral cone if it is generated by finitely many rational rays (hence, it is a closed cone). In general,
{\rm{Pic}}(X/T)
may be an infinite dimensional vector space. However, when
X/T
is of Fano type, it is a finite-dimensional vector space. Besides, many of the above cones are neither open nor closed. Even worse, their closures may not be generated by rational rays. In the literature, there are various conventions regarding effective and movable cones (e.g., taking the closure of the cone or selecting rational elements within the cones). However, when
X/T
is of Fano type,
{\rm{NE}}(X/T),{\rm{Nef}}(X/T),{\rm{Eff}}(X/T)
and
{\rm{Mov}}(X/T)
are all closed rational polyhedral cones.
Since this paper focuses on Fano type varieties, we do not need to distinguish these variations in the definitions of cones.

3.

4.
Deformation of Néron-Severi spaces and nef cones

In this section, we study the deformation of Néron-Severi spaces and various cones for a family of varieties under certain conditions.

4.1.
Deformation of Néron-Severi spaces

Let
f:X\to T
be a projective morphism such that
f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}=\mathcal{O}_{T}
. Let
{\mathcal{P}}ic_{X/T}
be the sheaf associated to the relative Picard functor

S\mapsto{\rm{Pic}}(X_{S})_{\mathbb{Z}}/{\rm{Pic}}(S)_{\mathbb{Z}}={\rm{Pic}}(X_{S}/S)_{\mathbb{Z}},

where
S\subset T
is a Zariski open subset. See
[
22
]
*§9.2 for details. Note that
{\mathcal{P}}ic_{X/T}
is denoted by
{\rm{Pic}}_{(X/T)(\rm{zar})}
in
[
22
]
*Definition 9.2.2. In general,
{\mathcal{P}}ic_{X/T}(U)
may not be
{\rm{Pic}}(X_{U}/U)_{\mathbb{Z}}
for an open subset
U\subset T
because of the sheafification. In fact, by
[
22
]
*(9.2.11.2), we always have

(4.1)

{\mathcal{P}}ic_{X/T}(U)=H^{0}(U,R^{1}f_{*}\mathcal{O}^{*}_{X_{U}}).

If
X\to T
is a flat morphism, then by
[
22
]
*(9.2.11.3), we have the exact sequence

0\to{\rm{Pic}}(U)_{\mathbb{Z}}\to{\rm{Pic}}(X_{U})_{\mathbb{Z}}\to{\mathcal{P}}ic_{X/T}(U).

In particular, we have the inclusion

(4.2)

{\rm{Pic}}(X_{U}/U)_{\mathbb{Z}}\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{P}}ic_{X/T}(U).

In the following, we show that if
X\to T
is a family of Fano type varieties, then there exists a dominant generically finite base change
T^{\prime}\to T
and an open subset
U\subset T^{\prime}
, such that
{\mathcal{P}}ic_{X_{U}/U}\otimes\mathbb{R}
is a constant sheaf. To be precise, this means that there exist natural isomorphisms

{\mathcal{P}}ic_{X_{U}/U}(V)\otimes\mathbb{R}\simeq{\rm{Pic}}(X_{V}/V)\simeq N^{1}(X_{V}/V)

for any open subset
V\subset U
. Recall that by our convention,
{\rm{Pic}}(X/T)
is the
\mathbb{R}
-vector space generated by Cartier divisors on
X
modulo linear equivalences over
T
, which is the same as
{\rm{Pic}}(X/T)_{\mathbb{Z}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}
. In fact, only certain vanishing properties are needed to ensure the statement holds. Theorem
1.3
provides a more general formulation.

We need several lemmas in the proof of Theorem
1.3
.

First, recall the definition of the triviality of a map (see
[
31
]
*§11 for details). Consider a diagram of spaces and mappings in the analytic topology:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{2}}

We say that
f
is trivial over
Z
if there exist spaces
X_{0}
and
Y_{0}
, a mapping
f_{0}:X_{0}\to Y_{0}
, and homeomorphisms

X\cong X_{0}\times Z,\quad Y\cong Y_{0}\times Z

such that the following diagram is commutative:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X_{0}\times Z}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y_{0}\times Z.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cong}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cong}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{2}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f\times\mathrm{id}_{Z}}

We say that
f
is locally trivial over
Z
if, for any point
z\in Z
, the above property holds in some analytic neighborhood of

.

The following local triviality of the morphism is likely well-known to experts. For completeness, we explain how it can be deduced from the literature.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
h:Y\to X
and
X\to T
be projective fibrations of varieties. Then there exists a Zariski open subset
U\subset T
such that

is locally trivial over
U
(in the analytic topology).

Proof.

After shrinking
T
, we can assume that there exists a Whitney-Thom stratification of

(in the Zariski topology), which satisfies Thom’s

-condition (see
[
19
]
*Page 247, Theorem 2 or the proof of
[

]
*Page 248, Corollary 1). Shrinking

further, by Thom’s second isotopy lemma (see
[
31
]
*Proposition 11.1), we can assume that the morphism

is locally trivial over
T
.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
g:Y\to X
be a projective morphism between normal varieties with
R^{1}g_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Y}=0
. If

is a
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisor on
Y
with

, then

.

In particular, suppose that
g
is a birational morphism and that
(X,\Delta)
is a klt pair for some effective
\mathbb{R}
-divisor
\Delta
on
X
. If
D
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisor on
Y
with

, then

is a
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisor on
X
such that
g^{*}(g_{*}D)=D
.

Proof.

The argument below closely follows the proof of
[
24
]
*Proposition (12.1.4) and strengthens it. For the reader’s convenience, we include the full proof.

Let
x\in X
be a point. Then there exists a triangulation of the topological space
Y
such that the analytic subset
g^{-1}(x)
appears as the support of a sub-complex (see
[
2
]
*I, Theorem (8.8)). Let

be a sufficiently small Stein neighborhood. Then
g^{-1}(U)
deformation retracts onto the sub-complex

(see
[
18
]
*Appendix, Proposition A.5). In particular, we have

H^{i}(g^{-1}(U),\mathbb{Z})=H^{i}(g^{-1}(x),\mathbb{Z}),\quad i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}.

Let
(-)^{\rm an}
denote the analytification functor. As
R^{1}g^{\rm an}_{*}\mathcal{O}^{\rm an}_{Y}=(R^{1}g_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Y})^{\rm an}=0
(see
[
12
]
*
Exposé
XII, Théorème 4.2) and

, we have

By the exponential sequence, we have

H^{1}(g^{-1}(U),\mathcal{O}^{\rm an}_{Y})\to H^{1}(g^{-1}(U),\mathcal{O}_{Y}^{\rm an,*})\to H^{2}(g^{-1}(U),\mathbb{Z}),

and hence we obtain the natural injective group homomorphism

{{\rm{Pic}}}(g^{-1}(U))\hookrightarrow H^{2}(g^{-1}(U),\mathbb{Z})=H^{2}(g^{-1}(x),\mathbb{Z}).

If
D\equiv 0/X
, then
D|_{g^{-1}(x)}\equiv 0
and thus

is a torsion element of

. If

is the order of the torsion group of

, then
dD|_{g^{-1}(U)}=0\in{\rm{Pic}}(g^{-1}(U))
. Note that there are only finitely many topological types of the fibers of

(for example, using Lemma

). We can take
d\in\mathbb{N}
sufficiently divisible, such that for each
x^{\prime}\in X
, there exists an analytic neighborhood
x^{\prime}\in U^{\prime}
such that

on
g^{-1}{(U^{\prime})}
. As
g^{\rm an}_{*}\mathcal{O}^{\rm an}_{Y}(dD)\simeq(g_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Y}(dD))^{\rm an}
(see
[
12
]
*
Exposé
XII, Théorème 4.2), we have that

is a line bundle on

. By the natural sheaf morphism,
g^{*}g_{*}(\mathcal{O}_{Y}(dD))\to\mathcal{O}_{Y}(dD)
, we have the natural sheaf morphism (in the Zariski topology)

\theta:\mathcal{O}_{Y}\to\mathcal{O}_{Y}(dD)\otimes(g^{*}g_{*}(\mathcal{O}_{Y}(dD)))^{\vee}.

On

, as

is surjective and

is torsion free, we see that
\theta^{\rm an}|_{g^{-1}(U^{\prime})}
is an isomorphism. Thus

is an isomorphism. This implies that

is an isomorphism (for example see
[

]
*
Exposé

.

Next, assume that

is birational and there exists a divisor

such that

has klt singularities. Replacing

by a resolution, we can assume that

is a

-Cartier divisor on

and

by the negativity lemma. This completes the proof.

Finally, we include an alternative proof of this special case below, which is more algebraic in nature.

Let

be a small
\mathbb{Q}

where

is the strict transform of

. Replacing

by a higher model, we can assume that

factors through

, and let

be the corresponding morphism. We have

. Thus

is a

-factorial and

by the negativity lemma. In particular, we have

. By the base-point free theorem,

is semi-ample over

. Hence

is a
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisor on

and

Remark 4.3
.

Lemma

does not hold for an arbitrary birational contraction

. Assume that

is a normal projective surface which is not
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial. Let

be a Weil divisor on

\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier. For a resolution

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
f:X\to T
be a projective fibration. Suppose that
S\subset T
is a Zariski dense subset such that for any
s\in S
, the fiber
X_{s}
is a rationally chain-connected variety with rational singularities. Then, up to a dominant generically finite base change of
T
, there exists a non-empty open subset

, such that

is a constant sheaf. Moreover, for any open subset

, the natural restriction maps

are isomorphisms for any

. In particular, if for any

, the fiber

is a variety of Fano type, then the above claim holds.

T
, we have

for each

as

. Besides, a Fano type variety is naturally a rationally chain-connected variety with rational singularities.

{\rm{Nef}}(X_{t})
as a subset of

by assumption, and the cones are compared under this natural identification. By abuse of notation, the inclusion “
\subset

Step 2. We show that there exists an open subset

{\rm{Nef}}(X_{t})

\dim T=n
.

First, we choose countably many hypersurfaces

in

such that
\cup Z_{i}
is Zariski dense in

. For each
Z_{i}
, by induction, there exists an open subset

such that

is non-empty and
{\rm{Nef}}(X_{t})
is invariant on
U_{i}\cap Z_{i}
. We set
P_{i}\coloneqq{\rm{Nef}}(X_{t_{i}})
for some
t_{i}\in U_{i}\cap Z_{i}
. By the lower semi-continuity, after shrinking
U_{i}
around

, we can assume that
P_{i}\subset{\rm{Nef}}(X_{t})
for any
t\in U_{i}
. By abuse of notation, we also write
P_{i}
for the cone inside
N^{1}(X/T)
under the natural identification
N^{1}(X/T)\simeq N^{1}(X_{t_{i}})
. Let

P_{\infty}\coloneqq\overline{\cup_{i}P_{i}}

be the closure of
\cup_{i}P_{i}
inside
N^{1}(X/T)
. Set
U_{\infty}\coloneqq\cap_{i}U_{i}
. We claim that
P_{\infty}={\rm{Nef}}(X_{t})
for any
t\in U_{\infty}
. First, we have
P_{\infty}\subset{\rm{Nef}}(X_{t})
for any
t\in U_{\infty}
as
P_{i}\subset{\rm{Nef}}(X_{t})
for
t\in U_{i}
. Suppose that
P_{\infty}\subsetneqq{\rm{Nef}}(X_{t_{0}})
for some

t_{0}\in V\subset T

for any

. Since

must intersect some

. Thus, we have

for some

. However, we have

for any

for any

.

For any

, there exists an open subset

for each

for each

for any

.

Step 3. For the last statement on the isomorphism of Mori cones, the natural maps

{\rm{NE}}(X_{t})\to{\rm{NE}}(X_{U}/U)\to{\rm{NE}}(X_{T_{0}}/T_{0}),t\in U

are injective as

. Thus, we have
{\rm{NE}}(X_{t})\subset{\rm{NE}}(X_{U}/U)
under this identification. If the inclusion is strict, then there exists a divisor
\mathcal{D}
on

/U

. By

is nef
/U
. This is a contradiction. The remaining isomorphisms can be shown similarly.
∎

5.
MMP in a family and deformations of effective cones and Mori chamber decompositions

This section is devoted to the proof of Theorem
1.4

5.1.
Stable boundedness

Let
X\to T
be a projective fibration. Suppose that
X
is of Fano type over
T
. Set

\displaystyle{\rm{bc}}(X/T)\coloneqq\{h\mid{}

\displaystyle h:X\dasharrow Y/T\text{~is a birational contraction}/T

\displaystyle\text{~up to isomorphisms of~}Y/T\}.

Note that the above set includes not only varieties birationally contracted by
X
but also the birational contraction maps from
X
. However, for the sake of simplicity, we also write
Y/T\in{\rm{bc}}(X/T)
instead of
[h:X\dasharrow Y/T]\in{\rm{bc}}(X/T)
. For
Y_{1}/T,Y_{2}/T\in{\rm{bc}}(X/T)
,
Y_{1}\simeq Y_{2}/T
should be understood to mean that there exists an isomorphism
\theta:Y_{1}\to Y_{2}
, such that
h_{2}=\theta\circ h_{1}:X\dasharrow Y_{2}
, where
h_{i}:X\dasharrow Y_{i},i=1,2
are given birational contractions. Moreover, for a subvariety
V\subset T
, we write
Y_{V}/V
instead of the base change of
h
to
V
.

To study an MMP of a Fano type variety
X/T
, we also consider a subset
{\rm{bc}}(X/T)_{\rm MMP}
of
{\rm{bc}}(X/T)
which we define below. First, we call a sequence of birational contractions

X=X_{0}\dashrightarrow X_{1}\dashrightarrow\cdots\dashrightarrow X_{n}/T

a partial MMP
/T
(with respect to a divisor
D
) if each step consists of
D_{i}
-extremal divisorial or flipping contractions and
D_{i}
-flips, where
D_{i}
is the strict transform of
D
on
X_{i}
. To be precise, if
X_{i}\to X_{i+1}/T
is a
D_{i}
-flipping contraction, then
X_{i+2}\to X_{i+1}/T
is its
D_{i}
-flip. Besides, in the above partial MMP,
D_{n}
is not required to be nef over
T
. On the other hand, if
D_{n}
becomes nef and big over
T
, and
X_{n}\to X_{n+1}
is the birational contraction induced by
D_{n}
(in this case, the relative Picard number
\rho(X_{n}/X_{n+1})
may be greater than
1
), then

X=X_{0}\dashrightarrow X_{1}\dashrightarrow\cdots\dashrightarrow X_{n}\to X_{n+1}/T

is also called a partial MMP
/T
. If a fibration
X\to Z
has relative Picard number
1
, then it is called an extremal contraction.

We set

(5.1)

\begin{split}{\rm{bc}}(X/T)_{\rm MMP}\coloneqq\{h\mid&X\dasharrow Y/T\text{~is a partial~}\text{MMP}/T\\
&\text{up to isomorphisms of~}Y/T\}.\end{split}

Later, we will show that
{\rm{bc}}(X/T)_{\rm MMP}={\rm{bc}}(X/T)
when
X
is
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial in Proposition
5.4
.

Let
\tilde{X}\to X
be a small
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial modification. Since
\tilde{X}
and
X
are isomorphic in codimension
1
,
{\rm{bc}}(\tilde{X}/T)
can be naturally identified with
{\rm{bc}}(X/T)
. By abuse of notation, we will write
{\rm{bc}}(\tilde{X}/T)={\rm{bc}}(X/T)
for this identification. The following is a special case of
[
3
]
*Corollary 1.1.5.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
X
be of Fano type over
T
. Then
{\rm{bc}}(X/T)
is a finite set.

In fact, we have the following stronger statement. This proposition is a special case of
[
6
]
*Proposition 6.11. For the reader’s convenience, we include the proof.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
X
be of Fano type over
T
. Then for any open subset
V\subset T
and
Y/V\in{\rm{bc}}(X_{V}/V)
, there exists an element
W/T\in{\rm{bc}}(X/T)
such that
W_{V}/V
is isomorphic to
Y/V
.

Proof.

Replacing
X
by a small
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial modification, we can assume that
X
is
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial. Let
h:X_{V}\dashrightarrow Y/V
be the corresponding birational contraction. Let
A
be an ample divisor on
Y
over
V
. Let
A^{\prime}
be the strict transform of
A
on
X_{V}
and let
B
be the Zariski closure of
A^{\prime}
on
X
. Note that
B_{V}\coloneqq B|_{X_{V}}=A^{\prime}
and
B
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisor as
X
is
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial. Let
p:\Gamma\to X_{V},q:\Gamma\to Y
be birational morphisms such that
h=q\circ p^{-1}
. Then we have

(5.2)

p^{*}B_{V}=q^{*}A+E,

where
E
is an effective
p
-exceptional divisor. As

is of Fano type over

, there exists a divisor

such that

has klt singularities and

. Hence, there exists

such that

By (

),

is the canonical model of

over

admits the canonical model

∎

5.2.
MMP in a family

In this subsection, we prove Theorem
1.4
and Theorem
1.6
. Recall that

{\rm{bc}}(X/T)_{\rm MMP}\coloneqq\{h\mid X\dasharrow Y/T\text{~is a partial~}\text{MMP}/T\text{~up to isomorphisms of~}Y/T\}

is a subset of
{\rm{bc}}(X/T)
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
X\to T
be a projective fibration.

(1)

Let
X\dashrightarrow Y/T
be a divisorial contraction, a flipping contraction, or a flip. Suppose that the natural map
N^{1}(X/T)\to N^{1}(X_{t})
is surjective for very general
t\in T
. Then there exists an open subset
T_{0}\subset T
such that the natural map

N^{1}(Y_{T_{0}}/T_{0})\to N^{1}(Y_{t})

is surjective for very general
t\in T_{0}
.

(2)

Assume that
X
is of Fano type over
T
. Suppose that the natural map
N^{1}(X/T)\to N^{1}(X_{t})
is surjective for very general
t\in T
. Then there exists an open subset
T_{0}\subset T
such that for any open subset
V\subset T_{0}
, if
Y/V\in{\rm{bc}}(X_{V}/V)_{\rm MMP}
, then the natural map

N^{1}(Y/V)\to N^{1}(Y_{t})

is surjective for very general
t\in V
.

Proof.

We divide the proof into several steps.

Step 1. First, we prove statement (1). Shrinking
T
, we may assume that
X\dashrightarrow Y/T
is not a constant map over the generic point of
T
. If
g:X\to X_{1}/T
is a birational extremal contraction (divisorial or flipping contraction), then we have the commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces N^{1}(X/T)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces N^{1}(X_{1}/T)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces N^{1}(X_{t})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces N^{1}(X_{1,t}).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g_{t}^{*}}

For a very general
t
, if
B
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisor on
X_{1,t}
, then
g_{t}^{*}B
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisor on
X_{t}
and thus there exists a
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisor

on
X
such that
D|_{X_{t}}\equiv g_{t}^{*}B
. In particular, we have

as the relative Picard number
\rho(X/X_{1})=1
. This implies that there exists a
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisor
D^{\prime}
on
X_{1}
such that
D=g^{*}D^{\prime}
. Thus we have
D^{\prime}|_{X_{1,t}}\equiv B
.

Let
g\colon X\to X_{1}/T
be a flipping contraction with the flip
g^{+}\colon X^{+}\to X_{1}/T
. Assume that
H
is a Cartier divisor on

which is anti-ample over
X_{1}
. We claim that, for very general
t\in T
, the induced morphism
g_{t}\colon X_{t}\to X_{1,t}
is also an
H_{t}
-flipping contraction. Here,
g_{t}
and

denote the restrictions to the fiber

of
g
and
H
, respectively. We use the same convention below. As
g_{t}
is a small morphism, it suffices to show

. If
B
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisor on
X_{t}
, then we can assume that there exists a
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisor

on

by the above argument. As

, we have

for some

. In particular, there exists a
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisor

on

Step 2. In this step, we explain that (2) can be reduced to a fixed MMP.

By Proposition

. Since

Step 3. In this step, we show the statement (2).

If

consists only of divisorial contractions, flipping contractions, and flips, then the claim follows from statement (1).

However, in our definition of a partial MMP, the last step may be a birational contraction whose relative Picard number is greater than

is surjective for very general

and thus

. By repeating this process, we can further assume that

∎

, after shrinking

6.
Boundedness of birational models of Fano type varieties

Based on the results developed in the previous sections, we present an application to the moduli problem of Fano type varieties.

Proof of Theorem
1.7
.

By Noetherian induction and Theorem
1.4
, after replacing
T
by a dominant generically finite base change and possibly shrinking it, we can assume that there exists a fiber-wise small
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial modification
Y\to X
. Note that
{\rm bcm}(Y_{t})={\rm bcm}(X_{t})
. Replacing
Y
by
X
, we can assume that
X_{t},t\in T
are
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial.

After replacing
T
by a dominant generically finite base change and possibly shrinking it further, we may assume that Theorem
1.6
holds. Note that this replacement does not affect the
\mathbb{Q}
-factoriality of the fibers. By Theorem
1.2
and Noetherian induction, after shrinking
T
, we can assume that
X
is of Fano type over
T
.

Fix a fiber
X_{t}
for some
t\in T
, and suppose that
X_{t}\dasharrow Z
is a birational contraction. As
X_{t}
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial variety, we have
Z\in{\rm{bc}}(X_{t})_{\rm MMP}
by Proposition
5.4
. By Theorem
1.6
(3), there exists an MMP
/T
, denoted by
X\dasharrow\mathcal{Z}/T
, such that
Z\simeq\mathcal{Z}_{t}
. Note that
\mathcal{Z}/T\in{\rm{bc}}(X/T)
. By Proposition
5.1
,
{\rm{bc}}(X/T)
is a finite set. Thus,
{\rm bcm}(X/T)
is bounded by definition.
∎

Remark 6.1
.

In contrast to Theorem
1.7
, boundedness fails even when considering crepant models of a fixed log pair.

For example, let
X=\mathbb{P}^{2}
, and let
D
and
B
be two simple normal crossing curves on
X
with
p\in D\cap B
. Set
c_{n}=1-\frac{1}{n}
for
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>1}
. Suppose that
\pi_{1}:X_{1}={\rm Bl}_{p}X\to X
is the blow-up of
X
at
p
and let
D_{1},B_{1}
be the strict transforms of
D,B
on
X_{1}
, respectively. Let
E_{1}
be the exceptional divisor of
\pi_{1}
. Then we have

K_{X_{1}}+c_{n}D_{1}+c_{n}B_{1}+(1-\frac{2}{n})E_{1}=\pi_{1}^{*}(K_{X}+c_{n}D+c_{n}B).

Next, blowing up
D_{1}\cap E_{1}
, we get a variety
X_{2}
with
D_{2},B_{2}
the strict transforms of
D_{1},B_{1}
on
X_{2}
, respectively. We still use
E_{1}
to denote the strict transform of
E_{1}
on
X_{2}
, and
E_{2}
to denote the exceptional divisor of
X_{2}\to X_{1}
. Then we have

K_{X_{2}}+c_{n}D_{2}+c_{n}B_{2}+(1-\frac{2}{n})E_{1}+(1-\frac{3}{n})E_{2}=\pi_{2}^{*}(K_{X}+c_{n}D+c_{n}B),

where
\pi_{2}:X_{2}\to X
is the corresponding morphism. We continue this process by successively blowing up each intersection
D_{i}\cap E_{i}
at every step. This yields the equation

K_{X_{m}}+c_{n}D_{m}+c_{n}B_{m}+(1-\frac{2}{n})E_{1}+\cdots+(1-\frac{m+1}{n})E_{m}=\pi_{m}^{*}(K_{X}+c_{n}D+c_{n}B),

where the notation has the same meaning as above. In particular, there exists a divisor
E_{n-1}
whose discrepancy with respect to
(X,c_{n}D+c_{n}B)
is
0
. By
[
3
]
*Corollary 1.4.3, there exists a birational morphism
\theta_{n}\colon Y_{n}\to X
whose only exceptional divisor is
E_{n-1}
. Therefore, we have

K_{Y_{n}}+c_{n}D_{Y_{n}}+c_{n}B_{Y_{n}}=\theta_{n}^{*}(K_{X}+c_{n}D+c_{n}B)

with
E_{n-1}
the only exceptional divisor of
\theta_{n}
, where
D_{Y_{n}},B_{Y_{n}}
are strict transforms of
D,B
on
Y_{n}
, respectively. In other words,
(Y_{n},c_{n}D_{Y_{n}}+c_{n}B_{Y_{n}})
is a crepant model of
(X,c_{n}D+c_{n}B)
. Note that
\{E_{n}\mid n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}\}
consists of distinct divisors.

We claim that
\{Y_{n}\mid n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 2}\}
does not belong to a bounded family. If
\{Y_{n}\mid n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 2}\}
belongs to a bounded family
\mathcal{Y}\to T
, then without loss of generality, we can assume that there exists a Zariski dense subset
S\subset T
parametrizing a subset of
\{Y_{n}\mid n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 2}\}
. After shrinking
T
, we can assume that it satisfies the assumptions in Theorem
1.6
. Possibly shrinking
T
further, by Theorem
1.5
, we can assume that there exists an effective divisor
\mathcal{E}\subset\mathcal{Y}
such that
\mathcal{E}_{s_{0}}
is
\mathbb{Q}
-linearly equivalent to the exceptional divisor of
\mathcal{Y}_{s_{0}}\simeq Y_{i}\to X=\mathbb{P}^{2}
for a fixed
s_{0}\in S\cap T
. By Theorem
1.6
, we can contract
\mathcal{E}
, and obtain the morphism
\Theta:\mathcal{Y}\to\mathcal{X}/T
, which is exactly
\mathcal{Y}_{s_{0}}\simeq Y_{i}\to X=\mathbb{P}^{2}
over
s_{0}
. By the rigidity of
\mathbb{P}^{2}
, we have
\mathcal{X}_{T^{\prime}}\simeq\mathbb{P}^{2}\times T^{\prime}
, where
T^{\prime}\to T
is a dominant finite base change (
[
17
]
*Example 5.3.1, Exercise 24.7(c)). Replacing
T^{\prime}
by
T
, we can assume that
\mathcal{X}\simeq\mathbb{P}^{2}\times T
. Note that for any
Y_{j}
, there exists a unique morphism
\theta_{j}:Y_{j}\to\mathbb{P}^{2}
, which is the one obtained in the above construction. Indeed, the exceptional curve of
\theta_{j}
must be an exceptional curve of any other
Y_{j}\to\mathbb{P}^{2}
by the negativity of the intersection number. From this, we know that if
s_{j}\in S\cap T
with
\mathcal{Y}_{s_{j}}\simeq Y_{j}
, then
\Theta|_{\mathcal{Y}_{s_{j}}}=\theta_{j}
. Now, by the construction of the divisors
E_{n}
, there exists a rational function
f\in K(X)
such that
\nu_{E_{i}}(f)=0
but
\nu_{E_{j}}(f)>0
for each
j>i
, where
\nu_{E_{n}}
denotes the valuation corresponding to
E_{n}
. This is because for any
j>i
,
E_{j}
is obtained by further blow-ups over a point of
E_{i}
. Now we take
f
as a rational function on
\mathcal{X}=\mathbb{P}^{2}\times T
. By the above discussion, the zero set
\mathcal{Z}
of
f
on
\mathcal{Y}
contains
E_{j}=\mathcal{E}|_{s_{j}}
, where
j>i
(as
\nu_{E_{j}}(f)>0
). By the density of
S
, we have
\mathcal{Z}\supset\mathcal{E}
. However, this contradicts the fact that
\nu_{E_{i}}(f)=0
(i.e.,
f
does not vanish on
E_{i}=\mathcal{E}|_{s}
). This shows that the set
\{Y_{n}\mid n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 2}\}
is not bounded.

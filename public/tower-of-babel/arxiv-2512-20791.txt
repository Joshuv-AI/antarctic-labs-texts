Extragradient methods with complexity guarantees for hierarchical variational inequalities
Pavel Dvurechensky, Meggie Marschner, Shimrit Shtern, Mathias Staudigl

Abstract
In the framework of a real Hilbert space we consider the problem of approaching solutions to a class of hierarchical variational inequality problems, subsuming several other problem classes including certain mathematical programs under equilibrium constraints, constrained min-max problems, hierarchical game problems, optimal control under VI constraints, and simple bilevel optimization problems. For this general problem formulation, we establish rates of convergence in terms of suitably constructed gap functions, measuring feasibility gaps and optimality gaps. We present worst-case iteration complexity results on both levels of the variational problem, as well as weak convergence under a geometric weak sharpness condition on the lower level solution set. Our results match and improve the state of the art in terms of their iteration complexity and the generality of the problem formulation.



1
Introduction

Solving hierarchical equilibrium problems is an increasingly active area in mathematical programming that consists in finding a solution to an outer variational inequality over the solutions of an inner variational inequality. In this paper we consider the following problem. Let
(\mathcal{Z},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
be a real Hilbert space, potentially infinite-dimensional. We consider the problem of solving the hierarchical equilibrium problem, in which a hemi-variational inequality (
\HVI
) is solved over the solution set of another mixed variational inequality. Specifically, the class of equilibrium problems we consider is of the form

(P)

\text{ Find }z^{\ast}\in\mathcal{S}_{2}\text{ s.t. }\langle{\mathsf{F}}_{1}(z^{\ast}),z-z^{\ast}\rangle+g_{1}(z)-g_{1}(z^{\ast})\geq 0\quad\forall z\in\mathcal{S}_{2}\triangleq\zer({\mathsf{F}}_{2}+\partial g_{2}).

where
{\mathsf{F}}_{1},{\mathsf{F}}_{2}:\mathcal{Z}\to\mathcal{Z}
are monotone operators and
g_{1},g_{2}
are proper, convex, and lower-semicontinuous functions. We refer to the set
\mathcal{S}_{2}\triangleq\zer({\mathsf{F}}_{2}+\partial g_{2})
(assumed to be non-empty) as the solution set of the lower-level problem. In the same vein, we will call
\mathcal{S}_{1}
the set of solutions of (
P
). Obviously, these sets are nested in the sense
\mathcal{S}_{1}\subseteq\mathcal{S}_{2}
. Problem (
P
) is the formulation of a system of nested
\HVI
’s (also known as variational inequalities of the second kind). These families of variational problems are prominent in mechanics
[
27
]
and optimal control
[
7
,
18
]
. In finite dimensions, it has become a useful model template to study a plethora of problems in mathematical programming, machine learning, operations research, game theory, and signal processing
[
41
,
16
,
23
]
. Some motivating examples are given below.

1.1
Motivating Examples

1.1.1
Simple bilevel optimization

Problem (
P
) contains the simple bilevel optimization problem
[
19
,
58
]
as a special case. Indeed, if
f_{1},f_{2}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
are convex and continuously differentiable functions and
{\mathsf{F}}_{1}=\nabla f_{1}
and
{\mathsf{F}}_{2}=\nabla f_{2}
, then we recover the simple bilevel problem as the nested optimization problem

\min_{x\in\mathcal{S}}f_{1}(x)+g_{1}(x),\quad\mathcal{S}_{2}=\argmin_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\{f_{2}(x)+g_{2}(x)\}.

This model problem has been studied in several very recent papers. Important references include
[
47
]
, who approach this problem via a disciplined proximal splitting ansatz, as well as
[
2
,
20
,
64
,
46
,
12
]
.

1.1.2
Hierarchical variational inequalities

If
g_{1}=0
and
g_{2}=\indicator_{\mathcal{K}}
for a non-empty, closed convex set
\mathcal{K}\subseteq\mathcal{Z}
, problem (
P
) reduces to

\text{Find }z^{\ast}\in\mathcal{S}_{2}\quad\text{s.t.: }\langle{\mathsf{F}}_{1}(z^{\ast}),z-z^{\ast}\rangle\geq 0\qquad\forall z\in\mathcal{S}_{2}=\zer({\mathsf{F}}_{2}+\NC_{\mathcal{K}}).

This is the problem investigated in the recent papers
[
56
,
45
]
and
[
1
]
.

1.1.3
Equilibrium selection in Nash games

Selecting solutions from a given variational inequality problem is another important application of the model template (
P
). If
{\mathsf{F}}_{1}(x)=\nabla f_{1}(x)
, then we recover the optimal equilibrium selection problem as

\min_{x\in\mathcal{S}}\{f_{1}(x)+g_{1}(x)\},\quad\mathcal{S}=\zer({\mathsf{F}}_{2}+\partial g_{2}).

Selecting among the set of equilibria of a variational inequality is an important problem in game theory
[
64
,
10
]
. In this application, the lower level solution set decodes Nash equilibria of monotone games. Specifically, let
\nu\in\{1.\ldots,N\}
denote the set of players, each of which is endowed with a continuous jointly controlled loss function
h_{\nu}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{N})=h_{\nu}(z_{\nu},z_{-\nu}):\mathcal{Z}\triangleq\prod_{\nu=1}^{N}\mathcal{Z}_{\nu}\to\mathbb{R}
. Assuming that the mapping
z_{\nu}\mapsto h_{\nu}(z_{\nu},z_{-\nu})
is convex and continuously differentiable, we can construct the pseudo-gradient of the game as

{\mathsf{F}}_{2}(z)=(\nabla_{z_{1}}h_{1}(z),\ldots,\nabla_{z_{N}}h_{N}(z))
. Private constraints of agent
\nu
are modeled by the closed convex and proper function
\varphi_{\nu}:\mathcal{Z}_{\nu}\to(-\infty,+\infty]
. Assume that
{\mathsf{F}}_{2}:\mathcal{Z}\to\mathcal{Z}
is monotone and Lipschitz, and define
g_{2}(z)\triangleq\sum_{\nu=1}^{N}\varphi_{\nu}(z_{\nu})
. The VI-approach to Nash equilibrium
[
57
]
allows us to identify Nash equilibrium with the solutions of the hemi-variational inequality
\mathcal{S}_{2}=\zer({\mathsf{F}}_{2}+\partial g_{2})
.

1.1.4
Hierarchical jointly convex generalized Nash equilibrium problem

In very recent work,
[
37
]
studied a projection-based method for solving a challenging class of jointly convex generalized Nash equilibrium problems (GNEPs) featuring hierarchy and non-smooth data. We first show that their model is a special case of the problem (
P
).

The lower level Nash equilibrium problem.

Let
\nu\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
represent the labels of the lower-level players, who engage in a Nash game in which each player’s parameterized optimization problem is given by

\min_{y^{\nu}\in\mathcal{Y}^{\nu}}\{h^{\ell}_{\nu}(y^{\nu},y^{-\nu}))+\varphi^{\ell}_{\nu}(y^{\nu})\}.

The data of this game problem enjoy the following assumptions:

(a)

Player
\nu
’s feasible set
\mathcal{Y}^{\nu}
is a real separable Hilbert space;

(b)

h_{\nu}^{\ell}:\mathcal{Y}^{\nu}\to\mathbb{R}
is Fréchet differentiable and convex with respect to
y^{\nu}
;

(c)

The game’s pseudogradient
{\mathsf{F}}_{2}(y)=(\nabla_{y^{1}}h^{\ell}_{1}(y),\ldots,\nabla_{y^{N}}h^{\ell}_{N}(y))
is monotone on
\mathcal{Y}\triangleq\prod_{\nu=1}^{N}\mathcal{Y}^{\nu}
;

(d)

\varphi_{\nu}^{\ell}(\cdot)
is proper closed convex with compact domain
\dom(\varphi^{\ell}_{\nu})\triangleq\mathcal{A}_{\nu}\subset\mathcal{Y}_{\nu}
.

We can characterize equilibria of the Nash game
\Gamma_{\rm low}=\{h^{\ell}_{\nu},\varphi_{\nu}^{\ell}\}_{1\leq\nu\leq N}
as solutions to the mixed variational inequality
\mathcal{S}_{2}=\zer({\mathsf{F}}_{2}+\partial g_{2})
, where

g_{2}(y^{1},\ldots,y^{N})\triangleq\sum_{\nu=1}^{N}\varphi^{\ell}_{\nu}(y^{\nu}).

The assumptions on the game problem ensure that
\dom(g_{2})
is a compact subset of the separable Hilbert space
\mathcal{Y}
. Furthermore,
\mathcal{S}_{2}
is nonempty, convex, and compact.

The upper level GNEP.

The upper level problem is the Nash game with joint coupling constraint represented by the equilibrium set
\mathcal{S}_{2}
. Let
\mu\in\{1,\ldots,M\}
denote the labels of upper-level players. Each upper-level player aims to solve the constrained optimization problem

\min_{x^{\mu}\in\mathcal{X}^{\mu}}\{h^{u}_{\mu}(x^{\mu},x^{-\mu})+\varphi^{u}_{\mu}(x^{\mu})\}\quad\text{s.t.: }(x^{\mu},x^{-\mu})\in\mathcal{S}_{2}.

Similar to the upper level NEP, we impose the following assumptions on the problem data:

(d)

h^{u}_{\mu}:\mathcal{X}^{\mu}\to\mathbb{R}
is Fréchet differentiable and convex with respect to
x^{\mu}
;

(e)

The operator
{\mathsf{F}}_{1}(x)=(\nabla_{x^{1}}h^{u}_{1}(x),\ldots,\nabla_{x^{N}}h^{u}_{N}(x))
is monotone on
\mathcal{X}\triangleq\prod_{\nu=1}^{N}\mathcal{X}^{\nu}
;

(f)

\varphi^{u}_{\mu}
is proper closed convex with closed convex domain
\dom(\varphi^{u}_{\mu})
.

The decision variables
x^{\mu}
correspond to blocks
(y^{\nu})_{\nu\in\mathcal{N}_{\mu}}
, in which
\mathcal{N}_{\mu}\subseteq\{1,\ldots,N\}
represent the lower-level players controlled by upper-level player
\mu
. The blocks can well be overlapping, meaning that individual lower level players can have multiple upper level players. In particular,
\mathcal{X}^{\mu}=\prod_{\nu\in\mathcal{N}_{\mu}}\mathcal{Y}^{\nu}
. The entire hierarchical equilibrium problem thus belongs to our template (
P
), with
\mathcal{S}_{1}=\zer({\mathsf{F}}_{1}+\partial g_{1}+\NC_{\mathcal{S}_{2}})
, where

g_{1}(x^{1},\ldots,x^{M})\triangleq\sum_{\mu=1}^{M}\varphi^{u}_{\mu}(x^{\mu}).

1.2
Related works

This paper belongs to a subclass of bilevel problems in which a single decision variable is to be designed in order to optimize a function over the set of minimizerrs of another function. To emphasize this special character of such problems are also known as simple bilevel problems
[
19
]
. Starting with the seminal contribution of
[
58
]
and
[
15
]
, Tikhonov regularization has become one of the dominant paradigms in the numerical solution of simple bilevel optimization problems. In the context of the present paper, this means we have to consider the iteration-dependent operators

{\mathsf{V}}_{k}\triangleq{\mathsf{F}}_{2}+\sigma_{k}{\mathsf{F}}_{1},\quad G_{k}\triangleq g_{2}+\sigma_{k}g_{1},

where
k
is the iteration counter and
(\sigma_{k})_{k}
is a well-chosen sequence. Over the past years, a quite significant list of publications have appeared proving rates of convergence with respect to the inner (lower-level) and the outer (upper-level) problem. Specifically, building on the generalized Tikhonov regularization framework of
[
58
]
, the first results in this direction have been produced in
[
9
,
55
]
.
[
47
]
develop a proximal subgradient method for convex composite models in the upper and lower level problem. Adaptive versions of a related proximal-gradient strategy are studied in
[
38
]
. Accelerated simple bilevel optimization in the convex non-smooth composite setting have recently been derived in
[
46
]
. Beyond proximal-gradient based methods, projection-free schemes are developed in
[
20
]
and
[
26
]
. Moving beyond optimization, there exists a significant literature, rooted in the numerical resolution of inverse problems and signal processing, which studied solution methods for selecting a solution to a variational inequality given a pre-defined selection criterion
[
50
,
63
,
44
,
51
]
. This is known as the equilibrium selection problem and is in fact a special case of the general family of Mathematical Programs under equilibrium constraints (MPEC)
[
40
,
28
]
. All these papers focus on asymptotic convergence of the sequence generated by tailor-made numerical algorithms. In this more enhanced setting, the only results on iteration complexity we are aware of are published in the papers
[
33
,
34
,
32
]
. Moving beyond the function-case in the upper-level (equilibrium selection), there exist a few papers on numerical methods for solving nested and hierarchical variational inequalities
[
62
,
59
,
36
]
. Among those, only
[
36
]
contains complexity statements.

Recently, the papers
[
56
]
and
[
1

have addressed the finite-dimensional variational inequality case, obtained from our model template (
P
) by imposing
g_{1}=0
and
g_{2}
an indicator function over a compact convex set. Both papers derive complexity guarantees for the Korpelevich version of the extragradient method, applied to the Tikhonov regularized variational inequality with the operator
{\mathsf{F}}_{2}+\sigma_{k}{\mathsf{F}}_{1}
at iteration

. The obtained rates are

and

In a very recent work,
[
37

1.3
Contributions

The aim of this paper is to settle the complexity issue for a very general family of nested equilibrium problems formulated in the model framework (
P
), and thereby extend all the existing results on hierarchical monotone variational inequalities. Our main contributions are as follows:

•

More general problem formulation:
Unlike
[
56
,
1
]
, our hierarchical equilibrium model (
P
) contains general convex composite terms
g_{1},g_{2}
. Specifically, these papers treat the special case
g_{1}=0
and
g_{2}=\indicator_{\mathcal{C}}
for a compact convex and nonempty set
\mathcal{C}
in a finite-dimensional euclidean vector space. Our analysis performed in potentially infinite-dimensional real Hilbert spaces, and considers general hemi-variational inequalities (
\HVI
’s). This is class contains a rather large class of variational models, which found significant application in optimal control and mechanics
[
23
,
27
]
. Besides the gain in modelling, adding these convex and potentially non-smooth functions is an important extension, since it allows us to cover the non-smooth composite convex model when specialized to the potential case, as studied in
[
46
]
.

•

Enhanced Algorithmic design:
The recent papers
[
56
,
1
]
approach the numerical resolution of problem (
P
) via double-call version of the extragradient method. Our approach instead uses the ideas of the optimistic extragradient method
[
29
]
and thus requires only one operator evaluation per iteration. Within this algorithmic setting, we derive sublinear convergence rates for the lower and upper level problem and improved rates under the strong monotonicity of
{\mathsf{F}}_{1}
. Specifically, using a polynomial regularization sequence
\sigma_{k}=O(k^{-\delta})
for
\delta\in(0,1)
, we demonstrate upper complexity bounds on the order of
O(k^{-\delta})
for the gap function associated with the lower level equilibrium problem, and a
O(k^{-(1-\delta)})
complexity bound for the gap function of the entire hierarchical problem. This matches the existing bounds reported in
[
56
,
1
]
, but for a much larger class of equilibrium problems, and fewer function evaluations (specifically, instead of 2 just 1 per iteration).

•

Unbounded domains:
An important assumption in the complexity analysis in
[
56
,
1
]
is compactness of the domains over which the variational inequalities are solved. We abandon this assumption. Instead, our proof builds on ideas originating in the convergence analysis of non-autonomous evolution equations due to
[
3
]
. In the potential case, these ideas have already been successfully applied to the study of hierarchical optimization problems
[
13
,
15
]
. In particular,
[
12
]
make the role of this technique very transparent by emphasizing the role of the geometry of the lower level problem in establishing a unified complexity statement for both levels simultaneously. A methodological contribution of this work is to show how these ideas naturally translate to the variational setting. We belief that our proof technique is going to be useful also for other hierarchical equilibrium problems as well.

2
Facts on variational inequalities

2.1
Preliminaries

We work in the real Hilbert space
\mathcal{Z}
with scalar product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
and induced norm
\norm{\cdot}
. For
x\in\mathcal{Z}
and
r>0
,
\mathbb{B}(x,r)
denotes the ball of radius
r
around
x
defined by the norm in
\mathcal{Z}
The domain of a function
G:\mathcal{Z}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
is defined as
\dom(G)=\{z\in\mathcal{Z}|G(z)<+\infty\}
. The class of proper, convex, and lower semi-continuous functions
g:\mathcal{Z}\to(-\infty,\infty]
is denoted by
\Gamma_{0}(\mathcal{Z})
. The proximal operator of
G
is defined as

\prox_{G}(u)=\argmin_{z\in\mathcal{Z}}\{G(z)+\frac{1}{2}\norm{z-u}^{2}\}.

If
x,u\in\mathcal{Z}
, we have

(2.1)

x=\prox_{G}(u)\Leftrightarrow\langle u-x,z-x\rangle\leq G(z)-G(x)\qquad\forall z\in\mathcal{Z}.

The metric projection onto a nonempty closed convex set
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathcal{Z}
is defined as
\Pi_{\mathcal{C}}(x)=\argmin_{y\in\mathcal{C}}\norm{x-y}=\prox_{\indicator_{\mathcal{C}}}(x)
, where the convex indicator function
\indicator_{\mathcal{C}}:\mathcal{Z}\to(-\infty,\infty]
is defined by

\indicator_{\mathcal{C}}(x)\triangleq\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}0&\text{if }x\in\mathcal{C},\\
+\infty&\text{ if }x\notin\mathcal{C}.\end{array}\right.

We denote also
\dist(x,\mathcal{C})\triangleq\norm{x-\Pi_{\scrC}(x)}
. The polar cone attached to a closed convex set
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathcal{Z}
is
\mathcal{C}^{\circ}\triangleq\{z\in\mathcal{Z}|\langle z,x\rangle\leq 0\quad\forall x\in\mathcal{C}\}
. The conjugate function of
f\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathcal{Z})
is
f^{\ast}(y)\triangleq\sup_{x\in\dom(f)}\{\langle y,x\rangle-f(x)\}.
A mapping (operator)
{\mathsf{F}}:\mathcal{Z}\to\mathcal{Z}
is
\mu
-strongly-monotone (
\mu\geq 0
) if

\langle{\mathsf{F}}(z_{1})-{\mathsf{F}}(z_{2}),z_{1}-z_{2}\rangle\geq\mu\norm{z_{1}-z_{2}}^{2}\qquad\forall z_{1},z_{2}\in\mathcal{Z}.

A
0
-monotone operator is simply called
monotone
.
{\mathsf{F}}:\mathcal{Z}\to\mathcal{Z}
is
L
-Lipschitz continuous if

\norm{\opF(z_{1})-\opF(z_{2})}\leq L\norm{z_{1}-z_{2}}\qquad\forall z_{1},z_{2}\in\mathcal{Z}.

The normal cone of a closed convex set
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathcal{Z}
at point
z\in\mathcal{C}
is defined
\NC_{\mathcal{C}}(z)\triangleq\{\xi\in\mathcal{Z}|\langle\xi,z^{\prime}-z\rangle\leq 0\quad\forall z^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}\}
. Under convexity, the tangent cone
\TC_{\mathcal{C}}(x)
of
\mathcal{C}
at
x\in\mathcal{C}
is polar to the normal cone, and we have the explicit expression
\TC_{\mathcal{C}}(x)=\cl\left(\bigcup_{\lambda>0}\frac{\mathcal{C}-x}{\lambda}\right)
. The support function of set
\mathcal{C}
is defined as
\supp(p|\mathcal{C})\triangleq\sup_{z\in\mathcal{C}}\langle p,z\rangle.
It follows from these definitions that

(2.2)

\langle p,z\rangle=\supp(p|\mathcal{C})\qquad\forall p\in\NC_{\mathcal{C}}(z).

Lemma 2.1

(Lemma 5.31,
[
8
]
)
.

Let
(a_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}},(b_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}},(c_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be nonnegative sequences such that
\sum_{k\geq 1}c_{k}<\infty
and

a_{k+1}\leq a_{k}-b_{k}+c_{k}.

Then,
\lim_{k\to\infty}a_{k}
exists and
\sum_{k\geq 1}b_{k}<\infty
.

2.2
Hemi-variational inequalities

Given a monotone and Lipschitz continuous mapping
{\mathsf{F}}:\mathcal{Z}\to\mathcal{Z}
and a function
g\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathcal{Z})
, the hemi-variational inequality problem
\HVI({\mathsf{F}},g)
is to find
z^{\ast}\in\mathcal{Z}
such that

(2.3)

\langle{\mathsf{F}}(z^{\ast}),u-z^{\ast}\rangle+g(u)-g(z^{\ast})\geq 0\qquad\forall u\in\mathcal{Z}.

One can rewrite (
2.3
) as a monotone inclusion
0\in({\mathsf{F}}+\partial g)(z^{\ast})
. If
g=\indicator_{\mathcal{C}}
for a closed convex set
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathcal{Z}
, we obtain from the above the variational inequality
\VI({\mathsf{F}},\mathcal{C})
:

\text{Find }z^{\ast}\in\mathcal{C}\text{ s.t. }\langle{\mathsf{F}}(z^{\ast}),u-z^{\ast}\rangle\geq 0\qquad\forall u\in\mathcal{C}.

Let
\mathcal{C}\subset\dom(g)
be a given compact subset of
\dom(g)
. A popular convergence measures for
\HVI({\mathsf{F}},g)
is the localized gap function

(2.4)

\Theta(z|{\mathsf{F}},g,\mathcal{C})\triangleq\sup_{y\in\mathcal{C}}\{\langle{\mathsf{F}}(y),z-y\rangle+g(z)-g(y)\}=\sup_{y\in\mathcal{C}}H^{({\mathsf{F}},g)}(z,y),

where
H^{({\mathsf{F}},g)}:\mathcal{Z}\times\mathcal{Z}\to[-\infty,\infty]
is defined as
H^{({\mathsf{F}},g)}(z,y)\triangleq\langle{\mathsf{F}}(y),z-y\rangle+g(z)-g(y)
. Historically, this localized version of a gap function can be traced back to
[
48
]
(see also
[
35
,
49
]
). Lemma
2.2
below summarizes its role for the numerical resolution of the problem
\HVI({\mathsf{F}},g)
under continuity assumptions on
{\mathsf{F}}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{C}\subset\dom(g)
be a nonempty compact convex set. Consider problem
\HVI({\mathsf{F}},g)
with
{\mathsf{F}}:\mathcal{Z}\to\mathcal{Z}
monotone and Lipschitz continuous. The function
x\mapsto\Theta(x|{\mathsf{F}},g,\mathcal{C})
is well-defined and convex on
\mathcal{Z}
. For any
x\in\mathcal{C}
we have
\Theta(x|{\mathsf{F}},g,\mathcal{C})\geq 0.
Moreover, if
x\in\mathcal{C}
is a solution to (
2.3
), then
\Theta(x|{\mathsf{F}},g,\mathcal{C})=0
. Conversely, if
\mathcal{C}\subset\dom(g)
and
\Theta(x|{\mathsf{F}},g,\mathcal{C})=0
for some
x\in\mathcal{C}
for which there exists a
\varepsilon>0
such that
\mathbb{B}(x,\varepsilon)\cap\mathcal{C}=\mathbb{B}(x,\varepsilon)\cap\dom(g)
, then
x
is a solution of (
2.3
).

Proof.

See Appendix
B
.

\blacksquare

2.3
Sharpness and error-bound property

Our geometric framework is phrased in terms of weak-sharpness of the lower-level solution set
\mathcal{S}_{2}
. Originally formulated for optimization problems in
[
14
]
, weak-sharpness in the context of variational inequalities has been defined in
[
53
]
. Subsequently, implications in terms of error bounds of primal and dual gap functions have been stated in
[
43
,
39
]
. The following definition of weak sharpness is from
[
30
]
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
{\mathsf{F}}:\mathcal{Z}\to\mathcal{Z}
be continuous and monotone over
\dom(g)\subset\mathcal{Z}
. Assume
\dom(g)
is closed. The solution set
\mathcal{S}
of
\HVI({\mathsf{F}},g)
is
weakly sharp
if there exists
\tau>0
such that

(\forall z^{\ast}\in\mathcal{S}):\quad\tau\mathbb{B}(0,1)\subseteq{\mathsf{F}}(z^{\ast})+\partial g(z^{\ast})+[\TC_{\dom(g)}(z^{\ast})\cap\NC_{\mathcal{S}}(z^{\ast})]^{\circ}

Based on
[
30
]
, we can give the following characterization of weak sharpness in terms of an error bound involving the dual gap function of
\HVI({\mathsf{F}},g)
.

Proposition 2.4
.

Consider problem
\HVI({\mathsf{F}},g)
with
\dom(g)
a closed convex nonempty subset of
\mathcal{Z}
. If
\mathcal{S}=\zer({\mathsf{F}}+\partial g)
is weakly sharp, then

(\forall z\in\mathcal{D}_{g}):\quad\Theta(z|{\mathsf{F}},g,\dom(g))\geq\tau\dist(z,\mathcal{S}).

Proof.

See Appendix
B
.

\blacksquare

This Proposition shows that weak sharpness implies that the gap function satisfies an error bound property
[
52
]
. Motivated by this fact, we propose the following definition:

Definition 2.5

(Weak Sharpness)
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be the nonempty solution set of
\HVI({\mathsf{F}},g)
. We say
\mathcal{S}
is
(\alpha,\rho)
-weak sharp with
\alpha>0
and
\rho\geq 1
if

(2.5)

(\forall z^{\ast}\in\mathcal{S})(\forall z\in\mathcal{D}_{g}=\dom(g)):\quad H^{({\mathsf{F}},g)}(z,z^{\ast})\geq\alpha\rho^{-1}\dist(z,\mathcal{S})^{\rho}.

Remark 2.1
.

An important class of examples arises when
\rho=1
, notably in monotone linear complementarity problems in finite dimensions under nondegeneracy conditions
[
14
,
52
]
. In the case where the
\HVI({\mathsf{F}},g)
reduces to a convex optimization problem, weak-sharpness implies an Hölderian errror bound, an assumption already imposed by
[
15
]
in the context of hierarchical minimization. Specifically, let us assume that
{\mathsf{F}}=0
and
g\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathcal{Z})
. Then
\mathcal{S}=\argmin g
, and acordingly,
H^{(0,g)}(z,z^{*})=g(z)-\min g
for
z^{*}\in\mathcal{S}
. Hence, (
2.5
) implies

\frac{\alpha}{\rho}\dist(u,\argmin g)^{\rho}\leq g(u)-\min g\qquad\forall u\in\mathcal{Z}.

If
\rho=1
, this is the weak-sharpness condition of
[
14
]
. The case
\rho=2
corresponds to the "quadratic growth" condition of
[
21
]
.

2.4
Constraint qualifications

The following set of assumptions shall be in place throughout the paper.

Assumption 1
.

For
i=1,2
the following properties do hold:

(i)

{\mathsf{F}}_{i}:\mathcal{Z}\to\mathcal{Z}
is
L_{{\mathsf{F}}_{i}}
-Lipschitz continuous and monotone;

(ii)

g_{i}\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathcal{Z})
and
\dom(g_{1})\cap\dom(g_{2})\neq\varnothing
;

(iii)

\mathcal{S}_{2}\triangleq\zer({\mathsf{F}}_{2}+\partial g_{2})\neq\varnothing
.

We remark that Assumption
1
implies that
\mathcal{S}_{2}
is a non-empty closed convex set. The next assumption is essentially a constraint qualification condition on the composite function

x\mapsto f_{\bar{x}}(x)\triangleq\langle{\mathsf{F}}_{1}(\bar{x}),x-\bar{x}\rangle+g_{1}(x)-g_{1}(\bar{x})+\indicator_{\mathcal{S}_{2}}(x).

Assumption 2
.

The solution set
\mathcal{S}_{1}
of (
P
) satisfies

\mathcal{S}_{1}=\zer({\mathsf{F}}_{1}+\partial g_{1}+\NC_{\mathcal{S}_{2}}).

Similar assumptions have been made in
[
13
]
for solving constrained variational inequalities. Assumption
2
is motivated by conditions ensuring a non-smooth sum rule of

\partial_{x}\left(\langle{\mathsf{F}}_{1}(\bar{x}),\Id_{\mathcal{Z}}(\bullet)-\bar{x}\rangle+g_{1}(\bullet)-g_{1}(\bar{x})+\indicator_{\mathcal{S}_{2}}(\bullet)\right)(\bar{x})={\mathsf{F}}_{1}(\bar{x})+\partial g_{1}(\bar{x})+\NC_{\mathcal{S}_{2}}(\bar{x}).

Common assumptions ensuring this property are
[
8
, Corollary 16.48]
:

•

\Int\dom(g_{1})\cap\mathcal{S}_{2}\neq\varnothing
;

•

\dom(g_{1})\cap\Int(\mathcal{S}_{2})\neq\varnothing
;

•

if
\mathcal{Z}
is finite-dimensional and
\rint\dom(g_{1})\cap\rint\dom(g_{2})\neq\varnothing
.

Since
\mathcal{S}_{2}\subseteq\dom(\partial g_{2})\subset\dom(g_{2})
, these assumptions ensure the computationally pleasant identity
\partial(g_{1}+g_{2})=\partial g_{1}+\partial g_{2}
.

2.5
Gap functions for hierarchical variational inequalities

Since we are searching for a solution of a hierarchical system of variational inequalities, we have to introduce different merit functions for measuring the feasibility and the optimality of a test point
z\in\mathcal{Z}
.

Definition 2.6

(Feasibility gap)
.

We define the
feasibility gap
over a compact nonempty set
\mathcal{U}\subseteq\dom(g_{2})
as

(2.6)

\Theta_{\rm Feas}(z|\mathcal{U})\triangleq\Theta(z|{\mathsf{F}}_{2},g_{2},\mathcal{U})=\sup_{y\in\mathcal{U}}\{\langle{\mathsf{F}}_{2}(y),z-y\rangle+g_{2}(z)-g_{2}(y)\}.

Definition 2.7

(Optimality gap)
.

The
optimality gap
over a compact set
\mathcal{U}\subseteq\dom(g_{1})
with
\mathcal{U}\cap\mathcal{S}_{2}\neq\varnothing
is defined as

(2.7)

\Theta_{\rm Opt}(z|\mathcal{U}\cap\mathcal{S}_{2})\triangleq\Theta(z|{\mathsf{F}}_{1},g_{1},\mathcal{U}\cap\mathcal{S}_{2})=\sup_{y\in\mathcal{U}\cap\mathcal{S}_{2}}\{\langle{\mathsf{F}}_{1}(y),z-y\rangle+g_{1}(z)-g_{1}(y)\}.

We note that if
z\in\mathcal{U}\cap\mathcal{S}_{2}
, and the regularity conditions stated in Lemma
2.2
hold, then by the same Lemma, the inequality
\Theta_{\rm Opt}(z|\mathcal{U}\cap\mathcal{S}_{2})\leq 0
is equivalent to
z\in\mathcal{S}_{1}
. However, in general an inequality of the form
\Theta_{\rm Opt}(z|\mathcal{U}\cap\mathcal{S}_{2})\leq 0
, does not tell us much about the qualitative properties of the test point if
z\notin\mathcal{S}_{2}
. It is thus important to obtain a lower bound on the optimality gap.

Lemma 2.8
.

Consider problem (
P
). Let Assumption
1
and
2
hold. Let
\mathcal{U}_{1}\subseteq\dom(g_{1})
be a nonempty compact set with
\mathcal{U}_{1}\cap\mathcal{S}_{1}\neq\varnothing
. Then, there exists a constant
B_{\mathcal{U}_{1}}>0
such that

(2.8)

\Theta_{\rm Opt}(z|\mathcal{U}_{1}\cap\mathcal{S}_{1})\geq-B_{\mathcal{U}_{1}}\dist(z,\mathcal{S}_{2}),\quad\forall z\in\mathcal{Z}.

Suppose
\mathcal{S}_{2}
is
(\alpha,\rho)
-weakly sharp. Then for all nonempty and compact subsets
\mathcal{U}_{2}\subseteq\dom(g_{2})
and all
z\in\dom(g_{2})
, we have

(2.9)

\dist(z,\mathcal{S}_{2})\leq\left[\frac{\rho}{\alpha}\Theta_{\rm Feas}(z|\mathcal{U}_{2}\cap\mathcal{S}_{2})\right]^{1/\rho}.

Proof.

See Appendix
B
.

\blacksquare

Remark 2.2
.

Lemma
2.8
allows us to establish a lower bound on the optimality gap over the solution set
\mathcal{S}_{2}
. It extends the corresponding result in
[
12
]
to the operator case.
[
56
]
and
[
1
]
prove this result for special case of bilevel VIs on compact domains.

3
Optimistic extragradient method

Our algorithmic design follows the popular Tikhonov regularization approach and employs a family of regularized problems with the data

{\mathsf{V}}_{\sigma}(z)\triangleq{\mathsf{F}}_{2}(z)+\sigma{\mathsf{F}}_{1}(z):\mathcal{Z}\to\mathcal{Z},\text{ and }G_{\sigma}(z)\triangleq g_{2}(z)+\sigma g_{1}(z)\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathcal{Z}).

The parameter
\sigma\geq 0
determines the relative importance of the upper level problem relative to the lower level problem. A decreasing sequence of regularization parameters
(\sigma_{k})_{k\geq 1}
induces a corresponding sequence of operators
{\mathsf{V}}_{k}\triangleq{\mathsf{V}}_{\sigma_{k}}
and convex functions
G_{k}\triangleq G_{\sigma_{k}}
. Being a sum of monotone and Lipschitz continuous mappings,
{\mathsf{V}}_{\sigma}
is
L_{\sigma}\triangleq L_{{\mathsf{F}}_{2}}+\sigma L_{{\mathsf{F}}_{1}}
-Lipschitz and monotone. In the same vein, the combined mapping
G_{\sigma}
is proper convex and lower semi-continuous. Furthermore, Assumption
1
guarantees the non-smooth sum rule
\partial G_{k}=\sigma_{k}\partial g_{1}+\partial g_{2}
. The conceptual algorithm we propose for solving (
P
) is described in Algorithm
1
. The reader should consider this as a description of a method; Once concrete definitions for the step-size
(t_{k})_{k}
and the regularization parameters
(\sigma_{k})_{k}
are given, we obtain a bona-fide algorithm from the pseudo-code. Importantly, it should be observed that our algorithm requires only one evaluation of operators
{\mathsf{F}}_{1}
,
{\mathsf{F}}_{2}
per iteration, in contrast to previous works
[
56
,
1
]
.

Algorithm 1

Optimistic extragradient for hierarchical HVI’s (
P
)

z^{1}=z^{1/2}
, step-size sequence
(t_{k})_{k\geq 1}
, non-increasing regularization sequence
(\sigma_{k})_{k\geq 1}
satisfying

(3.1)

\lim_{k\to\infty}\sigma_{k}=0,(t_{k})\notin\ell^{1}_{+}(\mathbb{N})\text{ and }\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}t_{k}\sigma_{k}<\infty.

Set
k=1

while
stopping criterion not met
do

Compute

\displaystyle z^{k+1/2}=\prox_{t_{k}G_{k}}(z^{k}-t_{k}({\mathsf{F}}_{2}(z^{k-1/2})+\sigma_{k}{\mathsf{F}}_{1}(z^{k-1/2}))),

\displaystyle z^{k+1}=\prox_{t_{k}G_{k}}(z^{k}-t_{k}({\mathsf{F}}_{2}(z^{k+1/2})+\sigma_{k}{\mathsf{F}}_{1}(z^{k+1/2})).

end

while

3.1
Statement of the main results

The main result of this paper are two complexity statements for the averaged iterates generated by Algorithm
1
in terms of the introduced gap functions. We work in a specific geometric setting, involving key quantities associated with the lower level problem
\HVI({\mathsf{F}}_{2},g_{2})
. Associated to the bifunction
H^{({\mathsf{F}}_{2},g_{2})}
, Appendix
A
defines the mapping

(3.2)

\varphi^{({\mathsf{F}}_{2},g_{2})}(z,u)\triangleq\sup_{y\in\dom(g_{2})}\{H^{({\mathsf{F}}_{2},g_{2})}(z,y)+\langle y,u\rangle\}.

This function encodes dual properties of the variational inequality, since it holds that (cf. eq. (
A.1
))

(3.3)

\varphi^{({\mathsf{F}}_{2},g_{2})}(z,u)\leq\sup_{y\in\dom(g_{2})}\{\langle y,u\rangle-H^{({\mathsf{F}}_{2},g_{2})}(y,z)\}=\left(H^{({\mathsf{F}}_{2},g_{2})}(\bullet,z)\right)^{\ast}(u).

The following summability condition is essentially due to
[
5
,
4
]
:

Assumption 3
.

[Attouch-Czarnecki condition]
The step size sequence
(t_{k})_{k\geq 1}
and the regularization sequence
(\sigma_{k})_{k\geq 1}
satisfy

(3.4)

(\forall p^{*}\in\text{Range}(\NC_{\mathcal{S}_{2}})):\;\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}t_{k}\left[\sup_{z\in\mathcal{S}_{2}}\varphi^{({\mathsf{F}}_{2},g_{2})}(z,\sigma_{k}p^{\ast})-\supp(\sigma_{k}p^{\ast}|\mathcal{S}_{2})\right]<\infty.

To understand the meaning of Assumption
3
, it is instructive to specialize our setting to the simple bilevel optimization case (cf. Section
1.1.1
). In that case, the data of the lower level problem are identified with
{\mathsf{F}}_{2}=\nabla f_{2}
and
\mathcal{S}_{2}=\argmin_{z}(f_{2}+g_{2})(z)
, and thus

\displaystyle H^{({\mathsf{F}}_{2},g_{2})}(x,y)

\displaystyle\leq f_{2}(x)-f_{2}(y)+g_{2}(x)-g_{2}(y)\triangleq\hat{f}_{2}(x)-\hat{f}_{2}(y)

\displaystyle=(\hat{f}_{2}-\min\hat{f}_{2})(x)-(\hat{f}_{2}-\min\hat{f}_{2})(y).

Hence,
\varphi^{({\mathsf{F}}_{2},g_{2})}(z,p)\leq(\hat{f}_{2}-\min\hat{f}_{2})(z)+(\hat{f}_{2}-\min\hat{f}_{2})^{*}(p)
. Since for
z\in\mathcal{S}_{2}
it holds
(\hat{f}_{2}-\min\hat{f}_{2})(z)=0
, this further implies

\sup_{z\in\mathcal{S}_{2}}\varphi^{({\mathsf{F}}_{2},g_{2})}(z,\sigma_{k}p)-\supp(\sigma_{k}p|\mathcal{S}_{2})\leq(\hat{f}_{2}-\min\hat{f}_{2})^{*}(\sigma_{k}p)-\supp(\sigma_{k}p|\mathcal{S}_{2}).

In
[
4
,
54
,
12
]
the following summability condition is imposed

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}t_{k}\left[(\hat{f}_{2}-\min\hat{f}_{2})^{*}(\sigma_{k}p)-\supp(\sigma_{k}p|\mathcal{S}_{2})\right]<\infty.

We see that this condition is stronger than our condition (
3.4
).

Remark 3.1
.

Assumption
3
looks quite daunting to verify, but as already observed in
[

]
, it fits very natural to the geometric setting of this paper. Indeed, let us assume that

is

-weakly sharp, with

and

and for all

4.1
Energetic bounds

In this section, we develop the necessary energy bounds for the sequence
(z^{k})_{k\geq 1}
generated by the optimistic extragradient method. Key is the step size condition
8t_{k}^{2}L^{2}_{k}\leq 1
, where
L_{k}\triangleq L_{{\mathsf{F}}_{2}}+\sigma_{k}L_{{\mathsf{F}}_{1}}
is the combined Lipschitz constant of the operator
{\mathsf{V}}_{k}
. This condition implies

t_{k}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{8}}\frac{1}{L_{{\mathsf{F}}_{2}}+\sigma_{k}L_{{\mathsf{F}}_{1}}}\in\frac{1}{\sqrt{8}}\left[\frac{1}{L_{{\mathsf{F}}_{2}}+\sigma_{1}L_{{\mathsf{F}}_{1}}},\frac{1}{L_{{\mathsf{F}}_{2}}}\right].

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
(\sigma_{k})_{k\geq 1}
be non-increasing and
(t_{k})_{k\geq 1}
be a step size policy. Let also

be arbitrary.

(i)

For all
k\geq 1
we have

(4.1)

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\norm{z^{k+1}-z}^{2}&\leq\frac{1}{2}\norm{z^{k}-z}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\norm{z^{k+1/2}-z^{k}}^{2}+\frac{t^{2}_{k}}{2}\norm{\opV_{k}(z^{k-1/2})-\opV_{k}(z^{k+1/2})}^{2}\\
&+t_{k}\langle{\mathsf{V}}_{k}(z^{k+1/2}),z-z^{k+1/2}\rangle+t_{k}\left(G_{k}(z)-G_{k}(z^{k+1/2})\right).\end{split}

(ii)

If
8t^{2}_{k}L_{k}^{2}\leq 1
then for all
k\geq 2
,

(4.2)

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\norm{z^{k+1}-z}^{2}+\frac{t^{2}_{k}}{2}\norm{\opV_{k}(z^{k-1/2})-\opV_{k}(z^{k+1/2})}^{2}&\leq\frac{1}{2}\norm{z^{k}-z}^{2}+\frac{t^{2}_{k-1}}{2}\norm{\opV_{k-1}(z^{k-3/2})-\opV_{k-1}(z^{k-1/2})}^{2}\\

Proof.

(i) Applying (
2.1
) to the iterates
z^{k+1/2}
and

(4.3)

\displaystyle\langle z^{k+1}-z^{k},z^{k+1}-z\rangle\leq\langle t_{k}{\mathsf{V}}_{k}(z^{k+1/2}),z-z^{k+1}\rangle+t_{k}\left(G_{k}(z)-G_{k}(z^{k+1})\right)

in the second inequality, we have

Now, we observe that

\displaystyle\norm{z^{k+1}-z}^{2}

\displaystyle=\norm{z^{k+1}-z^{k}+z^{k}-z}^{2}

\displaystyle=\norm{z^{k}-z}^{2}-\norm{z^{k+1}-z^{k}}^{2}+2\langle z^{k+1}-z^{k},z^{k+1}-z\rangle.

In the same spirit, we see

\norm{z^{k+1}-z^{k}}^{2}=\norm{z^{k+1}-z^{k+1/2}}^{2}+\norm{z^{k+1/2}-z^{k}}^{2}+2\langle z^{k+1}-z^{k+1/2},z^{k+1/2}-z^{k}\rangle.

Combining these two identities with (

) and (

\displaystyle\norm{z^{k+1}-z}^{2}

\displaystyle=\norm{z^{k}-z}^{2}-\norm{z^{k+1}-z^{k+1/2}}^{2}-\norm{z^{k+1/2}-z^{k}}^{2}

\displaystyle-2\langle z^{k+1}-z^{k+1/2},z^{k+1/2}-z^{k}\rangle+2\langle z^{k+1}-z^{k},z^{k+1}-z\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\norm{z^{k}-z}^{2}-\norm{z^{k+1}-z^{k+1/2}}^{2}-\norm{z^{k+1/2}-z^{k}}^{2}

\displaystyle+2t_{k}\langle{\mathsf{V}}_{k}(z^{k-1/2}),z^{k+1}-z^{k+1/2}\rangle+2t_{k}\langle{\mathsf{V}}_{k}(z^{k+1/2}),z-z^{k+1}\rangle

\displaystyle+2t_{k}\left(G_{k}(z)-G_{k}(z^{k+1/2})\right).

Adding and subtracting
z^{k+1/2}
in the second inner product, leaves us with

\displaystyle\norm{z^{k+1}-z}^{2}

\displaystyle=\norm{z^{k}-z}^{2}-\norm{z^{k+1}-z^{k+1/2}}^{2}-\norm{z^{k+1/2}-z^{k}}^{2}

\displaystyle+2t_{k}\langle{\mathsf{V}}_{k}(z^{k-1/2})-{\mathsf{V}}_{k}(z^{k+1/2}),z^{k+1}-z^{k+1/2}\rangle+2t_{k}\langle{\mathsf{V}}_{k}(z^{k+1/2}),z-z^{k+1/2}\rangle

\langle a,b\rangle\leq\frac{c}{2}\norm{a}^{2}+\frac{1}{2c}\norm{b}^{2}

, we obtain

2t_{k}\langle{\mathsf{V}}_{k}(z^{k-1/2})-{\mathsf{V}}_{k}(z^{k+1/2}),z^{k+1}-z^{k+1/2}\rangle\leq t^{2}_{k}\norm{\opV_{k}(z^{k-1/2})-\opV_{k}(z^{k+1/2})}^{2}+\norm{z^{k+1}-z^{k+1/2}}^{2}.

\displaystyle+t_{k}\langle{\mathsf{V}}_{k}(z^{k+1/2}),z-z^{k+1/2}\rangle+t_{k}\left(G_{k}(z)-G_{k}(z^{k+1/2})\right).

(ii) Observe that for

,

\displaystyle\frac{t_{k}^{2}}{2}\norm{\opV_{k}(z^{k-1/2})-\opV_{k}(z^{k+1/2})}^{2}

\displaystyle=t^{2}_{k}\norm{\opV_{k}(z^{k-1/2})-\opV_{k}(z^{k+1/2})}^{2}-\frac{t^{2}_{k}}{2}\norm{\opV_{k}(z^{k-1/2})-\opV_{k}(z^{k+1/2})}^{2}

\displaystyle-\frac{t^{2}_{k}}{2}\norm{\opV_{k}(z^{k-1/2})-\opV_{k}(z^{k+1/2})}^{2},

where the first inequality follows from the

5.2
Tseng splitting

Optimistic extragradient (also known as Popov’s or past extragradient) method saves one evaluation of the time-varying operator
V_{k}
per iteration compared to the standard extragradient method. Yet, it still requires two evaluations of the proximal operator of
G_{k}
per iteration. An attractive modified extragradient scheme, which uses past values of the operator but saves on one evaluation of the proximal operator, is the following optimistic version of Tseng’s Forward-Backward-Forward method
[
61
,
60
]

\displaystyle z^{k+1/2}

\displaystyle=\prox_{t_{k}G_{k}}(z^{k}-t_{k}{\mathsf{V}}_{k}(z^{k-1/2})),

\displaystyle z^{k+1}

\displaystyle=z^{k+1/2}-t_{k}({\mathsf{V}}_{k}(z^{k+1/2})-{\mathsf{V}}_{k}(z^{k-1/2})).

By construction, there exits a
\xi^{k}\in\partial G_{k}(z^{k+1/2})
such that

\displaystyle z^{k+1/2}

\displaystyle=z^{k}-t_{k}({\mathsf{V}}_{k}(z^{k-1/2})+\xi^{k}),

\displaystyle z^{k+1}

\displaystyle=z^{k}-t_{k}({\mathsf{V}}_{k}(z^{k-1/2})+\xi^{k})-t_{k}({\mathsf{V}}_{k}(z^{k+1/2})-{\mathsf{V}}_{k}(z^{k-1/2}))

\displaystyle=z^{k}-t_{k}({\mathsf{V}}_{k}(z^{k+1/2})+\xi^{k}).

This further gives us

\displaystyle\norm{z^{k+1}-z}^{2}

\displaystyle=\norm{z^{k+1}-z^k+z^k-z}^{2}

\displaystyle=\norm{z^k-z}^{2}-\norm{z^{k+1}-z^k}^{2}+2\langle z^{k+1}-z^{k},z^{k+1}-z\rangle

\displaystyle=\norm{z^k-z}^{2}-\norm{z^{k+1}-z^k}^{2}+\langle z^{k}-t_{k}({\mathsf{V}}_{k}(z^{k+1/2})+\xi^{k})-z^{k},z^{k+1}-z\rangle

\displaystyle=\norm{z^k-z}^{2}-\norm{z^{k+1}-z^k}^{2}-2t_{k}\langle{\mathsf{V}}_{k}(z^{k+1/2})+\xi^{k},z^{k+1}-z^{k+1/2}\rangle

(5.21)

\displaystyle\qquad-2t_{k}\langle{\mathsf{V}}_{k}(z^{k+1/2})+\xi^{k},z^{k+1/2}-z\rangle.

Now observe that
t_{k}({\mathsf{V}}_{k}(z^{k+1/2})+\xi^{k})=z^{k}-z^{k+1}=z^{k}-z^{k+1/2}+t_{k}({\mathsf{V}}_{k}(z^{k+1/2})-{\mathsf{V}}_{k}(z^{k-1/2})).
As a consequence, we get

\displaystyle 2t_{k}\langle{\mathsf{V}}_{k}(z^{k+1/2})

\displaystyle+\xi^{k},z^{k+1}-z^{k+1/2}\rangle

\displaystyle=2\langle z^{k}-z^{k+1/2},z^{k+1}-z^{k+1/2}\rangle+2t_{k}\langle{\mathsf{V}}_{k}(z^{k+1/2})-{\mathsf{V}}_{k}(z^{k-1/2}),z^{k+1}-z^{k+1/2}\rangle

\displaystyle=\norm{z^k-z^{k+1/2}}^{2}+\norm{z^{k+1}-z^{k+1/2}}^{2}-\norm{z^{k+1}-z^k}

\displaystyle\qquad 2t_{k}\langle{\mathsf{V}}_{k}(z^{k+1/2})-{\mathsf{V}}_{k}(z^{k-1/2}),z^{k+1}-z^{k+1/2}\rangle.

Combining this estimate with (
5.21
) gives

\displaystyle\norm{z^{k+1}-z}^{2}

\displaystyle=\norm{z^k-z}^{2}-\norm{z^{k+1}-z^k}^{2}-\norm{z^k-z^{k+1/2}}^{2}+\norm{z^{k+1}-z^{k+1/2}}^{2}+\norm{z^{k+1}-z^k}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad 2t_{k}\langle{\mathsf{V}}_{k}(z^{k-1/2})-{\mathsf{V}}_{k}(z^{k+1/2}),z^{k+1}-z^{k+1/2}\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad-2t_{k}\langle{\mathsf{V}}_{k}(z^{k+1/2})+\xi_{k},z^{k+1/2}-z\rangle

Since
\xi^{k}\in\partial G_{k}(z^{k+1/2})
it holds that
G_{k}(z^{k+1/2})-G_{k}(z)\leq\langle\xi^{k},z^{k+1/2}-z\rangle
and therefore

\displaystyle-2t_{k}\langle{\mathsf{V}}_{k}(z^{k+1/2})+\xi^{k},z^{k+1/2}-z\rangle

\displaystyle=-2t_{k}\langle{\mathsf{V}}_{k}(z^{k+1/2}),z^{k+1/2}-z\rangle-2t_{k}\langle\xi^{k},z^{k+1/2}-z\rangle

(5.22)

\displaystyle\leq 2t_{k}\langle{\mathsf{V}}_{k}(z^{k+1/2}),z-z^{k+1/2}\rangle+2t_{k}(G_{k}(z)-G_{k}(z^{k+1/2}))

With this estimate, we arrive at (

(5.23)

7
Conclusion

This paper proves seminal results on the iteration complexity of hierarchical hemi-variational inequalities. Constructing a suitably defined optimistic extragradient method, we generalize the state-of-the art along several dimensions. In particular, we derive complexity guarantees in terms of suitably defined feasibility and optimality gaps. Our analysis reveals close connections to geometric conditions imposed on the operator defining the lower level equilibrium problems, conditions which have been so-far dominantly been used in the potential case. We belief that our proof strategy is going to be useful for several other problems currently under investigation in the literature. In particular, it will be very interesting to derive rates for stochastic formulation of our model template and derive rates in terms of expected gap functions. Further interesting directions for future research inlude the consideration of accelerated methods. We leave these important extensions to future research.

Appendix A
The Fitzpatrick function

Let
\mathsf{M}:\mathcal{Z}\to 2^{\mathcal{Z}}
be a maximally monotone operator. The
Fitzpatrick function

[
25
,
8
]

\mathcal{F}_{\mathsf{M}}:\mathcal{Z}\to(-\infty,+\infty]
, associated with the operator
\mathsf{M}
, is defined as

\mathcal{F}_{\mathsf{M}}(x,u)\triangleq\sup_{(y,v)\in\gr(\mathsf{M})}\{\langle x,v\rangle+\langle y,u\rangle-\langle y,v\rangle\}.

Following
[
11
]
, we define the gap function
\gap_{\mathsf{M}}(x)\triangleq\mathcal{F}_{\mathsf{M}}(x,0)
.
\gap_{\mathsf{M}}
is convex, and in fact the smallest translation invariant gap function associated with the monotone operator
\mathsf{M}

[
11
, Theorem 3.1]
. Importantly, this gives the properties
\gap_{\mathsf{M}}(x)\geq 0
and
\gap_{\mathsf{M}}(x)=0
if and only if
x\in\zer(\mathsf{M})
. To make this concept concrete, observe that if
\mathsf{M}={\mathsf{F}}+\NC_{\mathcal{C}}
, then the above definition of the gap function reduces to the well-known Auslender dual gap function
[
24
,
6
]

\gap_{{\mathsf{F}}+\NC_{\mathcal{C}}}(x)=\sup_{y\in\mathcal{C}}\langle{\mathsf{F}}(y),x-y\rangle.

If
\mathsf{M}={\mathsf{F}}+\partial g
for a function
g\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathcal{Z})
, we easily obtain

\gap_{{\mathsf{F}}+\partial g}(x)\leq\sup_{y\in\dom(g)}\langle{\mathsf{F}}(y),x-y\rangle+g(x)-g(y).

To the data
({\mathsf{F}},g)
, we thus associate the bifunction
H^{({\mathsf{F}},g)}:\mathcal{Z}\times\mathcal{Z}\to[-\infty,\infty]
defined as

H^{({\mathsf{F}},g)}(x,y)\triangleq\langle{\mathsf{F}}(y),x-y\rangle+g(x)-g(y).

If
{\mathsf{F}}:\mathcal{Z}\to\mathcal{Z}
is monotone and continuous on
\dom(g)
, it is easy to verify that
H^{({\mathsf{F}},g)}(x,y)\leq-H^{({\mathsf{F}},g)}(y,x)
for all
(x,y)\in\dom(g)\times\dom(g)
(i.e.
H^{({\mathsf{F}},g)}
is a monotone bifunction
[
31
]
). In the structured setting
\HVI({\mathsf{F}},g)
, we obtain the following bounds on the Fitzpatrick function, showing its close connection to the bifunction
H^{({\mathsf{F}},g)}
and the Auslender dual gap function. First, the convex subgradient inequality yields the relation

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{{\mathsf{F}}+\partial g}(x,u)

\displaystyle=\sup_{y\in\dom(\partial g),\xi\in\partial g(y)}\{\langle x-y,{\mathsf{F}}(y)\rangle+\langle\xi,x-y\rangle+\langle y,u\rangle\}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{y\in\dom(g)}\{\langle{\mathsf{F}}(y),x-y\rangle+g(x)-g(y)+\langle y,u\rangle\}

\displaystyle=\sup_{y\in\dom(g)}\{H^{({\mathsf{F}},g)}(x,y)+\langle y,u\rangle\}\triangleq\varphi^{({\mathsf{F}},g)}(x,u).

The function
\varphi^{({\mathsf{F}},g)}(x,u)
is thus seen as the Fitzpatrick transform of
H^{({\mathsf{F}},g)}
. Second, since the function
x\mapsto H^{({\mathsf{F}},g)}(x,y)
is convex, we see

(A.1)

\varphi^{({\mathsf{F}},g)}(x,u)\leq\sup_{y\in\dom(g)}\{\langle y,u\rangle-H^{({\mathsf{F}},g)}(y,x)\}=\left(H^{({\mathsf{F}},g)}(\bullet,x)\right)^{\ast}(u).

In particular,

(A.2)

\displaystyle\varphi^{({\mathsf{F}},g)}(x,u)\geq\langle x,u\rangle\qquad\forall x\in\dom(g),

(A.3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{{\mathsf{F}}+\partial g}(x,0)\leq\sup_{y\in\dom(g)}H^{({\mathsf{F}},g)}(x,y)=\varphi^{({\mathsf{F}},g)}(x,0).

Following this notation, we define for a given a subset
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{Z}
, the restricted dual gap function as

(A.4)

\Theta(x|{\mathsf{F}},g,\mathcal{C})\triangleq\sup_{y\in\mathcal{C}}H^{({\mathsf{F}},g)}(x,y).

1.
Introduction

Prismatic cohomology, introduced by Bhatt and Scholze
[
7
]
, interpolates between crystalline and de Rham cohomology in mixed characteristic.
Its success rests on a synthesis of derived and
p
-adic geometry: the prismatic site encodes infinitesimal thickenings equipped with divided powers and Frobenius-compatible connections, while the resulting cohomology controls topological cyclic homology and syntomic invariants.
When one passes from smooth schemes to logarithmic geometry, these structures must be enriched to capture the behavior of normal crossings and semistable degenerations.
The purpose of this paper is to develop this extension and to study logarithmic syntomic cohomology in the logarithmic motivic stable homotopy category
\mathrm{logSH}(\mathrm{pt}{N})
of a log point.

Our approach proceeds through the formalism of
log animated rings
introduced in
[
10
]
, which provides a derived enhancement of the category of log rings suited to prismatic constructions.
Just as animated rings model derived infinitesimal thickenings, log animated rings encode both the derived and combinatorial data of logarithmic structures, allowing us to work uniformly with derived divided powers and connections.
This framework clarifies the role of divided power envelopes and their Nygaard filtrations: they become natural functors on log animated rings, equipped with canonical connections whose horizontality conditions reflect the
p
-adic Cartier isomorphism.
In particular, logarithmic prismatic cohomology
{\widehat{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}_{(A,M)}
may be described as the derived global sections of the log prismatic sheaf on the category of log quasi-syntomic log rings
(A,M)
, with coefficients in
\mathrm{TC}^{-}
, and carries a natural Nygaard filtration defined by divided Frobenius powers.

A central conceptual development of this paper is the introduction of the notion of
log Cartier smoothness
.
In the classical prismatic setting, the condition of Cartier smoothness
[
19
]
for a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-algebra ensures that its cotangent complex has
p
-complete Tor amplitude in degree
0
, thereby allowing the construction of the Nygaard filtration and the comparison with derived de Rham cohomology.
In the logarithmic context, semistable and toroidal situations require a parallel condition that controls both the infinitesimal and combinatorial contributions of the log structure.
We therefore define a log ring
(A,M)
to be
log Cartier smooth
if
the cotangent complex
L_{(A,M)/\mathbb{F}_{p}}
has Tor amplitude concentrated in degree
0
,
and for every
i
, the inverse Cartier map

C^{-1}\colon\Omega_{(A,M)/\mathbb{F}_{p}}^{i}\to H^{i}(\Omega_{(A,M)/\mathbb{F}_{p}}^{\bullet})

is an isomorphism.
This notion provides the natural setting for logarithmic prismatic geometry: it guarantees the existence of the logarithmic Cartier isomorphism, the good behavior of the Nygaard filtration, and the compatibility between divided powers and connections.
All the main constructions in this paper—including saturated descent, comparison with derived log de Rham cohomology, and motivic representability in
\mathrm{logSH}(\mathrm{pt}{N})
—are carried out in the class of log Cartier smooth objects.

A recurring theme in this work is the role of de Rham cohomology as the classical limit of prismatic and syntomic theories.
The derived log de Rham complex governs infinitesimal thickenings equipped with connections, and its
p
-adic completion appears as the associated graded object of the Nygaard filtration on log prismatic cohomology.
In our setting, this perspective is essential: once the log structure
M
is free, the Nygaard-completed log prismatic cohomology of
(A,M)
can be identified with the
p
-completed derived de Rham cohomology of a suitable
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-lift.
Thus, de Rham cohomology provides the bridge between divided powers and connections on one hand, and motivic syntomic phenomena on the other, revealing the geometric content of logarithmic prismatic cohomology.

A key ingredient in our analysis is the technique of
saturated descent
, introduced in
[
8
]
.
It asserts that the Nygaard-completed log prismatic cohomology of
(A,M)
can be reconstructed from the prismatic cohomology of
animated rings, provided suitable exactness conditions hold.
We show that this descent statement remains valid whenever the log structure
M
is free,
thereby simplifying the hypotheses of
[
8
, Theorem 4.18]
.
This refinement enables a direct comparison between logarithmic prismatic cohomology and the
p
-completed derived log de Rham cohomology of an appropriate lift to
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, recovering the classical logarithmic de Rham complex in characteristic
p
as the associated graded of the Nygaard filtration.

The main object of study in this paper is the
logarithmic syntomic complex

\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\mathrm{syn}}(i)(A,M)
,
defined in terms of the Nygaard filtration on log prismatic cohomology by

\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\mathrm{syn}}(i)(A,M):=\mathrm{fib}(\mathrm{Fil}_{\mathrm{N}}^{\geq i}{\widehat{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}_{(A,M)}\xrightarrow{\varphi_{p}-\mathrm{can}}{\widehat{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}_{(A,M)})

This construction generalizes the syntomic complexes of
[
6
]
to logarithmic geometry and yields a cohomology theory that encodes both Frobenius and connection data in the log setting.
We prove that
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\mathrm{syn}}(i)
, which is functorial in log quasi-syntomic morphisms,
satisfies saturated descent for free monoids,
and is representable in the logarithmic motivic category
\mathrm{logSH}(\mathrm{pt}{N})
.
In particular, we show that

\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\mathrm{syn}}(i),\quad{\widehat{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}},\quad\mathrm{logTHH},\quad\mathrm{logTC}^{-},\quad\mathrm{logTP},\quad\text{and }\mathrm{logTC}\in\mathrm{logSH}_{\mathrm{k\acute{e}t}}^{\mathrm{eff}}(\mathrm{pt}{N}),

so that all major logarithmic cohomology theories admit canonical motivic realizations.

From the syntomic viewpoint, logarithmic topological cyclic homology is controlled by

\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\mathrm{syn}}(i)
.
For every log ring
(A,M)
there is a

logarithmic trace map

\mathrm{trc}_{\log}\colon K(A,M)\longrightarrow\mathrm{logTC}(A,M)

obtained by left Kan extension from the regular log regular case

[
12
, Theorem 8.6.3]
and
[
33
, Theorem F]
.
The map
\mathrm{trc}_{\log}
provides a natural bridge between logarithmic
K
-theory and prismatic phenomena.
However, we do not attempt to compute logarithmic
K
-theory itself in this work.
Instead, we
determine the homotopy of
\mathrm{logTC}
in concrete cases,
such as truncated polynomial and semistable examples, directly from syntomic calculations.

As an explicit illustration, let
k
be a perfect
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra and consider the log ring
(k[x]/x^{e},\mathbb{N})
with
1\mapsto x
.
We compute

\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\mathrm{syn}}(i)(k[x]/x^{e},\mathbb{N})\simeq\begin{cases}W(k)\oplus W(k)[-1]&i=0\\[5.69054pt]
\mathbb{W}_{ei-1}(k)[-1]\oplus W(k)[-1]\oplus W(k)[-2]&i=1\\[5.69054pt]

where

denotes the ring of big Witt vectors

]

From which it follows that

\pi_{i}(\mathrm{logTC}(k[x]/x^{e},\mathbb{N})_{p}^{\wedge})\cong\begin{cases}W(k)&i=-1\\[5.69054pt]

These formulas extend the classical truncated polynomial results of Hesselholt and Madsen
[

in the logarithmic setting, confirming that the syntomic theory faithfully detects the structure of logarithmic topological cyclic homology.

In the second part of the paper we study the representability of these cohomology theories in the effective logarithmic motivic stable homotopy category
\mathrm{logSH}^{\mathrm{eff}}(\mathrm{pt}{N})
.
We show that the presheaves

R\Gamma_{\mathrm{Zar}}(-,\wedge^{i}L_{-/k}),\quad{\widehat{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}},\quad\mathrm{Fil}_{\mathrm{N}}^{\geq i}{\widehat{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}},\quad\text{and }\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\mathrm{syn}}(i)

are log étale sheaves and
\square
-invariant on the category of log smooth fs log schemes over
\mathrm{pt}{N}=\Spec(k,\mathbb{N})
for a perfect
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra

.
Consequently, these theories are representable by canonical objects in
\mathrm{logSH}_{\mathrm{k\acute{e}t}}^{\mathrm{eff}}(\mathrm{pt}{N})
, extending the motivic representability of prismatic and syntomic theories established in
[
6
]
and
[
12
]
.
The resulting logarithmic motivic spectra satisfy Bott periodicity;
e.g., for logarithmic cyclic homology, we have

\mathrm{map}_{\mathrm{logSH}(\mathrm{pt}{N})}(\Sigma_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{\infty}X_{+},\mathbf{logTC})\simeq\mathrm{logTC}(X),\qquad\mathbf{logTC}\simeq\Sigma^{2,1}\mathbf{logTC},

mirroring the periodic behavior of algebraic

-theory in the motivic setting.

Finally, we illustrate these constructions by an explicit computation for the projective log coordinate axes

D=(\mathbb{P}^{2},\underline{D})\times_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}\underline{D},\qquad\underline{D}=\{[x:y:z]:x=0\text{ or }y=0\},

a basic example of a semistable fs log scheme.
Using toric geometry and dividing covers, we show an explicit decomposition

\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\mathrm{syn}}(i)(D)\simeq\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\mathrm{syn}}(i)(k,\mathbb{N})\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\mathrm{syn}}(i-1)(k,\mathbb{N})[-2]

This identifies the logarithmic syntomic cohomology of
D
with successive extensions of the syntomic cohomology of the logarithmic point, and demonstrates concretely how divided powers, connections, and de Rham-type structures are reflected in the motivic category.

The results presented here show that logarithmic prismatic and syntomic cohomology admit a natural interpretation in terms of animated log geometry and motivic homotopy theory.
In particular, the interplay between divided powers, connections, log Cartier smoothness, and de Rham cohomology becomes an instance of the motivic relationship between prismatic and syntomic phenomena.

Saturated Descent for the Free Monoid Case

This section extends the saturated descent method of

8

to the case where the log structure

After introducing the colimit perfection

A key step establishes that, under freeness, the quasi-syntomic and log quasi-syntomic conditions on

Using this, the log prismatic cohomology

8.
Log syntomic cohomology of projective log coordinate axes

This section computes the logarithmic syntomic cohomology of the projective coordinate axes

D=(\mathbb{P}^{2},\underline{D})\times_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}\underline{D}
,
where
\underline{D}
is the union of the coordinate lines in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
.
Using toric geometry,

D
is expressed as a log fiber product of toric varieties, allowing a description in terms of fans and cones.
A key step is the identification

\Sigma_{+}^{\infty}\mathbb{T}_{\langle\sigma,\tau,\tau^{\prime}\rangle,[e_{1}]+[e_{2}]}\simeq\mathbf{1}\oplus\Sigma^{2,1}\mathbf{1}

in
\mathrm{logSH}^{\mathrm{eff}}(\mathbb{A}{N})
,
established through Zariski distinguished squares and dividing covers.
This leads to the main result

\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\mathrm{syn}}(i)(D)\simeq\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\mathrm{syn}}(i)(k,\mathbb{N})\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\mathrm{syn}}(i-1)(k,\mathbb{N})[-2],

relating the syntomic cohomology of
D
to that of the logarithmic point.
Together with Theorem
6.1
, this gives a complete computation of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\mathrm{syn}}(i)(D)
.

Let
k
be a perfect
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra.
Consider the projective coordinate axes in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
given by

\underline{D}:=\{[x:y:z]:x=0\text{ or }y=0\}

We have the associated log scheme
(\mathbb{P}^{2},\underline{D})
whose underlying scheme is
\mathbb{P}^{2}
with the compactifying log structure associated with the open immersion
\mathbb{P}^{2}-\underline{D}\to\mathbb{P}^{2}

and we set

D:=(\mathbb{P}^{2},\underline{D})\times_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}\underline{D}

This is a basic example of a semistable fs log scheme.
The goal of this section is to compute
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\mathrm{syn}}(i)(D)
for each integer
i
.

To prepare for later arguments, we introduce notation from toric geometry.
For cones
\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{n}
in a lattice
N
, let
\langle\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{n}\rangle
denote the smallest fan in
N
containing
\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{n}
.
For a smooth fan
\Sigma
and rays
r_{1},\ldots,r_{d}\in\Sigma
, let
\mathbb{T}_{\Sigma,[r_{1}]+\cdots+[r_{d}]}
denote the fs log scheme whose underlying scheme is the associated toric scheme over
k
with the Deligne–Faltings log structure
[
31
, Section III.1.7]
associated with the Cartier divisors induced by the rays
r_{1},\ldots,r_{d}
.
If
r_{1},\ldots,r_{d}
are all the rays of
\Sigma
, then
\mathbb{T}_{\Sigma,[r_{1}]+\cdots+[r_{d}]}=\mathbb{T}_{\Sigma}
.

Let
e_{1}
and
e_{2}
be the standard coordinates in
\mathbb{N}^{2}
.
Consider the cones in
\mathbb{N}^{2}

\displaystyle\sigma:=\mathrm{Cone}(e_{1},e_{2})

\displaystyle\tau:=\mathrm{Cone}(e_{1},e_{1}-e_{2})

\displaystyle\tau^{\prime}:=\mathrm{Cone}(e_{2},e_{2}-e_{1})

There is an isomorphism

D\cong\mathbb{T}_{\langle\sigma,\tau,\tau^{\prime}\rangle,[e_{1}]+[e_{2}]}\times_{\mathbb{A}{N}}\mathrm{pt}{N}

where
\mathbb{T}_{\langle\sigma,\tau,\tau^{\prime}\rangle,[e_{1}]+[e_{2}]}\to\mathbb{A}{N}
is the log smooth morphism induced by the summation homomorphism
\mathbb{N}^{2}\to\mathbb{N}
.
This isomorphism follows from the local descriptions

\displaystyle\mathbb{T}_{\langle\sigma\rangle,[e_{1}]+[e_{2}]}\times_{\mathbb{A}{N}}\mathrm{pt}{N}\cong\Spec(k[x,y]/(xy),\mathbb{N}x\oplus\mathbb{N}y)

\displaystyle\mathbb{T}_{\langle\tau\rangle,[e_{1}]}\times_{\mathbb{A}{N}}\mathrm{pt}{N}\cong\Spec(k[xy,y^{-1}]/(xy),\mathbb{N}(xy))

\displaystyle\mathbb{T}_{\langle\tau^{\prime}\rangle,[e_{2}]}\times_{\mathbb{A}{N}}\mathrm{pt}{N}\cong\Spec(k[xy,x^{-1}]/(xy),\mathbb{N}(xy))

For a ring
R

Proposition 8.1
.

There is an equivalence

\Sigma_{+}^{\infty}\mathbb{T}_{\langle\sigma,\tau,\tau^{\prime}\rangle,[e_{1}]+[e_{2}]}\simeq\mathbf{1}\oplus\Sigma^{2,1}\mathbf{1}

in
\mathrm{logSH}^{\mathrm{eff}}(\mathbb{A}{N})
.

Proof.

It suffices to show that the induced morphism

\Sigma_{+}^{\infty}\mathbb{T}_{\langle\sigma,\tau,\tau^{\prime}\rangle,[e_{1}]+[e_{2}]}\to\Sigma_{+}^{\infty}\mathbb{T}_{\langle\sigma\cup\tau,\tau^{\prime}\rangle,[e_{2}]}

is an equivalence, since
\mathbb{T}_{\langle\sigma\cup\tau,\tau^{\prime}\rangle,[e_{2}]}\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{A}{N}
.
The squares

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{T}_{\langle\sigma\cap\tau^{\prime}\rangle,[e_{2}]}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{T}_{\langle\sigma,\tau\rangle,[e_{1}]+[e_{2}]}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{T}_{\langle\tau^{\prime}\rangle,[e_{2}]}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{T}_{\langle\sigma,\tau,\tau^{\prime}\rangle,[e_{1}]+[e_{2}]}}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{T}_{\langle\sigma\cap\tau^{\prime}\rangle,[e_{2}]}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{T}_{\langle\sigma\cup\tau\rangle,[e_{2}]}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{T}_{\langle\tau^{\prime}\rangle,[e_{2}]}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{T}_{\langle\sigma\cup\tau,\tau^{\prime}\rangle,[e_{2}]}}

are Zariski distinguished squares.
Hence it suffices to show that the induced morphism

\Sigma_{+}^{\infty}\mathbb{T}_{\langle\sigma,\tau\rangle,[e_{1}]+[e_{2}]}\to\Sigma_{+}^{\infty}\mathbb{T}_{\langle\sigma\cup\tau\rangle,[e_{2}]}

is an equivalence.
The squares

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{T}_{\langle\sigma\cap\tau^{\prime}\rangle,[e_{2}]}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{T}_{\langle\sigma,\tau\rangle,[e_{1}]+[e_{2}]}}

with

Thus it suffices to show that the induced morphism

-bundles over
\mathbb{A}{N}

This follows since the morphism

∎

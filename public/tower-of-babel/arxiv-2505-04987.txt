Intrinsic characterization of projective special complex manifolds
Vicente Cortés, Kazuyuki Hasegawa

Abstract
We define the notion of an S 1 S^{1} -bundle of projective special complex base type and construct a conical special complex manifold from it. Consequently the base space of such an S 1 S^{1} -bundle can be realized as ℂ ∗ \mathbb{C}^{\ast} -quotient of a conical special complex manifold. As a corollary, we give an intrinsic characterization of a projective special complex manifold generalizing Mantegazza’s characterization of a projective special Kähler manifold. Our characterization is in the language of c-projective structures. As an application, a non-trivial S 1 S^{1} -family of Obata-Ricci-flat hypercomplex structures (given by a generalization of the rigid c-map) on the tangent bundle of the total space of a ℂ ∗ \mathbb{C}^{*} -bundle over a complex manifold with certain kind of c-projective structure is constructed. Finally, we show that the quaternionic structure underlying any of these hypercomplex structures is in general not flat and that its flatness implies the vanishing of the c-projective Weyl tensor of the base of the ℂ ∗ \mathbb{C}^{*} -bundle. Conversely, any c-projectively flat complex manifold satisfying a cohomological integrality condition gives rise to a flat quaternionic structure. 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: 53C15, 53A20, 53C26 Keywords: projective special complex manifold, c-projective structure, c-map.

Introduction

In
[
3
]
, (conical) special complex and
projective special complex manifolds are introduced as generalizations of
(conical) special Kähler and projective special Kähler manifolds.
A projective special complex manifold is a
\mathbb{C}^{\ast}
-quotient of a conical special complex manifold.
The rigid c-map associates with a special Kähler manifold a
hyperKähler structure on its tangent bundle
[
10
]
. Also the supergravity c-map, which was introduced in
[
13
]
, is an assignment from a projective special
Kähler manifold to a quaternionic Kähler manifold. This is a special case of the HK/QK-correspondence, see
[
15
,
5
,
16
,
4
]
.
In
[
11
]
, the rigid c-map, the supergravity c-map and the HK/QK-correspondence
are generalized in the absence of a metric.
In these generalizations, a (conical) special complex and
a projective special complex manifold play an important role, see the diagram below.

\displaystyle TN

: hyperKähler (hypercomplex)

\displaystyle\overline{TN}

: quaternionic Kähler (quaternionic)

\displaystyle N

: conical special Kähler (conical special complex)

\displaystyle\bar{N}

: projective special Kähler (projective special complex)

A c-projective structure on a projective special complex manifold is canonically induced from
the special complex manifold (
[
11
]
).
Therefore the generalized supergravity c-map associates with this type of
complex manifold of real dimension
2n
with c-projective structure a quaternionic manifold of dimension
4n+4
(
[
11
]
).
In
[
8
]
, a construction of a quaternionic manifold
from a complex manifold with a certain type of c-projective structure is given.
This construction yields a quaternionic manifold from a complex manifold of half the dimension with
c-projective structure and is known as the quaternionic Feix-Kaledin construction, which is a generalization
of
[
18
,
12
]
.

Therefore it is natural to consider the problem of characterizing those complex manifolds with c-projective structure that can be
realized as
\mathbb{C}^{\ast}
-quotients of conical special complex manifolds.
In
[
11
]
it is shown that the Weyl curvature of
the canonically induced c-projective structure on a projective special
complex manifold is of type
(1,1)
. Therefore
any complex manifold with a c-projective structure whose Weyl curvature is
not
type
(1,1)

cannot be realized as
\mathbb{C}^{\ast}
-quotient of a conical special complex manifold
such that its canonical c-projective structure coincides with the given one.
Based on these considerations, we introduce in Definition

the notion of an
S^{1}
-bundle of
projective special complex base type (abbreviated as PSCB type for simplicity)
and show in Theorem
3.10
that any base manifold of an

-bundle of PSCB type
can be realized as a

\mathbb{C}^{\ast}
-quotient of a conical special complex manifold,
i.e., its base manifold is a projective special complex manifold.
The proof of Theorem
3.10
is performed by
considering the product of a
S^{1}
-bundle of PSCB type with
\mathbb{R}^{>0}
and
patching several geometric objects associated to an open covering with local triviality.
Then a special connection can be constructed on the total space of the resulting
\mathbb{C}^{\ast}
-bundle
over the given complex manifold.

One of the simplest examples of Theorem
3.10
is to construct
\mathbb{C}^{n+1}\backslash\{0\}
with
the usual
\mathbb{C}^{\ast}
-action from the the Hopf fibration
S^{2n+1}\to\mathbb{C}P^{n}
.
More generally, a c-projectively flat manifold can be realized as a projective special
complex manifold under certain assumptions, each of which is expressed
in terms of c-projective geometric objects (Corollary
3.12
).
In particular, for the case of
\mathbb{C}P^{n}
(
n\geq 2
) with the standard c-projective structure,
this result recovers the Hopf fibration
(Example
3.14
).
If there is a trivial
S^{1}
-bundle of type PSCB over the given complex manifold with a c-projective structure,
then it is a projective special complex manifold, that is,
Corollary
3.15
gives an intrinsic characterization of projective special complex manifolds
in terms of conditions
all of which are described by objects on the complex manifold with c-projective structure.
Since the c-projective structure is unique for the lowest dimensional case,
we give another definition of a projective special complex base type for that case
(Definition
3.17
) and obtain Theorem
3.18

in a similar way. As applications of Theorems
3.10
and
3.18
,
Section
4
provides examples of four- and two-dimensional projective special complex manifolds with a parallel Ricci tensor.
In particular, we present an example that does not occur in projective special Kähler manifolds.

As we stated in the above paragraph,
there is an assignment from a projective special Kähler manifold to
a quaternionic Kähler manifold. Projective special Kähler manifolds are a special class of special complex manifolds. In
[
23
]
, an intrinsic characterization of projective special Kähler manifolds is given, which we recover as Corollary
3.22
.
We also refer to
[
22
]
, which provides an intrinsic characterization of a simply connected Kähler group with an exact Kähler form that admits a compatible projective special Kähler structure.
In this case, since the Kähler group is parallelizable and the associated

S^{1}
-bundle is trivial, the characterization is described in terms of matrix-valued one-forms. This characterization can be recovered within our framework (Remark
3.25
).

Applying Theorem
3.10
to the generalized rigid c-map yields
a non-trivial
(\mathbb{R}/\pi\mathbb{Z})
-family of hypercomplex structures on the space of the c-map,
originating from a certain kind of complex manifold with c-projective structure (Theorem
5.6
and Corollary
5.7
).
Additionally, a local example of such a manifold is given by using the local expression of a conical special complex manifold in terms of a holomorphic
1
-form.
The Ricci curvature of the Obata connection of these hypercomplex structures vanishes (
[
11
]
).
Since the Obata connection is unique for a hypercomplex manifold (
[
24
]
),
all of its invariants, such as the curvature tensor, Ricci curvature, geodesics, and holonomy group, are also invariants of the hypercomplex structure.
It is known that the Ricci curvature of the Obata connection on
a hypercomplex manifold of dimension
4n
(
n\geq 2
)
vanishes
if and only if its restricted holonomy group is contained in
\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{H})
.
Therefore, a hypercomplex manifold with a Ricci-flat Obata connection is of particular interest in hypercomplex geometry.

In the last section of the paper we study the quaternionic structure underlying a Ricci-flat hypercomplex structure constructed by
the generalized rigid c-map. We show that the quaternionic structure can only be flat if the c-projective Weyl tensor of the initial complex manifold is zero, see
Theorem
5.10
. In the setting of projective special Kähler manifolds
this reduces to the case of complex hyperbolic space.

2.1
Projective special complex manifolds

In this subsection, we recall the definition of a (conical/projective) special complex manifold
(see
[
3
,
11
]
). See also
[
14
,
21
]
for the more specific case of a special Kähler manifold.

Definition 2.1
.

A
special complex manifold

(M,J,\nabla)
is a complex manifold
(M,J)
endowed with a
torsion-free flat connection
\nabla
such that the
(1,1)
-tensor field
\nabla J
is symmetric.
Such a connection
\nabla
is called
special
.
A
conical special complex manifold

(M,J,\nabla,\xi)
is a special complex manifold
(M,J,\nabla)

endowed with a nowhere-vanishing vector field
\xi
such that

•

\nabla\xi=\mathrm{Id}
and

•

L_{\xi}J=0
or, equivalently,
\nabla_{\xi}J=0
.

We recall
[
11
]
that this definition implies
L_{J\xi}J=0
and we set
A:=\nabla J
.
If
A=0
, then
(M,J,\nabla)
is called
trivial
.
We know that the connection

(2.1)

\displaystyle D:=\nabla-\frac{1}{2}JA

is torsion-free and complex. Its curvature is given by

(2.2)

R^{D}=-\frac{1}{4}[A,A].

Lemma 2.2
.

d^{D}A=0
.

Proof.

We have

(d^{D}A)(X,Y)=(d^{\nabla}A)(X,Y)-\frac{1}{2}[JA_{X},A_{Y}]+\frac{1}{2}[JA_{Y},A_{X}].

The first term vanishes, since
(d^{\nabla}A)(X,Y)=R^{\nabla}(X,Y)J=0
and the other two terms cancel, since

[JA_{X},A_{Y}]=J\{A_{X},A_{Y}\}

is symmetric.
∎

Let
(M,J)
be a complex manifold and consider the following spaces of connections
on
(M,J)
:

\displaystyle{\cal S}_{(M,J)}:=

\displaystyle\{\nabla\mid\nabla\,\,\mbox{is a special connection}\}

\displaystyle{\cal C}_{(M,J)}:=

\displaystyle\{(D,S)\mid D\,\,\mbox{is a torsion-free complex connection,}

\displaystyle\hskip 48.36958ptS\,\,\mbox{is a symmetric $(1,2)$-tensor and anti-commutes with}\,\,J,\,\,

\displaystyle\hskip 48.36958ptR^{D}=-[S,S],\,\,d^{D}S=0\}.

Lemma 2.3
.

There is a natural bijection

{\cal S}_{(M,J)}\cong{\cal C}_{(M,J)}
.

Proof.

If
\nabla\in{\cal S}_{(M,J)}
, put
S=-\frac{1}{2}JA
. Then we see that

D=\nabla+S
is a torsion-free complex connection and

S
is symmetric and anti-commute with
J
.
Moreover we have

d^{D}S=-\frac{1}{2}\left((DJ)\wedge A+J(d^{D}A)\right)=0

by Lemma
2.2
.
Finally we see from (
2.2
) that
R^{D}=-[S,S]
.
Conversely, take
(D,S)\in{\cal C}_{(M,J)}
and set
\nabla=D-S
.
Because
S
is symmetric,
\nabla
is torsion-free.
Since
\nabla J=-2JS
,
we see that
\nabla J
is symmetric.
From
R^{D}=R^{\nabla}+d^{D}S-[S,S]
,
\nabla
is flat.
Therefore
\nabla
is a special connection.
∎

Recall that
A_{\xi}=A_{J\xi}=0
.
We also have that
L_{\xi}A=0
, since
L_{\xi}\nabla=0
and
L_{\xi}J=0
.

Lemma 2.4
.

L_{J\xi}\nabla=A
,
L_{J\xi}A=-2JA
and
L_{J\xi}(JA)=2A
.

Proof.

We compute
L_{J\xi}\nabla
on a pair of
\nabla
-parallel vector fields
X,Y
:

\displaystyle(L_{J\xi}\nabla)_{X}Y

\displaystyle=[J\xi,\nabla_{X}Y]-\nabla_{[J\xi,X]}Y-\nabla_{X}[J\xi,Y]

\displaystyle=0-0+\nabla_{X}\nabla_{Y}(J\xi)=\nabla_{X}(\nabla_{Y}J)\xi+\nabla_{X}(J\nabla_{Y}\xi)=(\nabla_{X}J)Y=A_{X}Y,

where we have used that
A_{Y}\xi=A_{\xi}Y=0
. This implies

L_{J\xi}A=L_{J\xi}(\nabla J)=[L_{J\xi}\nabla,J]=[A,J]=2AJ=-2JA

and
L_{J\xi}(JA)=J(-2JA)=2A
.
∎

Definition 2.5
.

If the holomorphic vector field

\xi-\sqrt{-1}J\xi
generates a principal
\mathbb{C}^{\ast}
-action with corresponding
principal
\mathbb{C}^{\ast}
-bundle

\pi:(M,J,\nabla,\xi)\to(\bar{M},\bar{J})
, then we call
the complex base manifold
(\bar{M},\bar{J})

a
projective special complex manifold
.

Realization of projective special complex manifolds as submanifolds of

In this subsection, we give an alternative proof of an extrinsic realization theorem of

]
.
The alternative proof for a special complex manifold

Let

Proposition 2.6

For all

f:(M,J,D)\to(\mathbb{C}^{2n+2},\tilde{J},\tilde{D})

\varepsilon^{2}=\mathrm{Id}

\varepsilon\tilde{J}=-\tilde{J}\varepsilon

Proof.

We consider the vector bundle

M

and endomorphisms

,

\varepsilon^{E}
by

\displaystyle D^{E}_{X}(Y,Z)

\displaystyle=(D_{X}Y+S_{X}Z,S_{X}Y+D_{X}Z),

\displaystyle J^{E}(X,Y)

\displaystyle=(JX,-JY),\,\,\,\varepsilon^{E}(X,Y)=(Y,X),

It is known that the real matrix

Therefore any special complex manifold can be locally obtained by a regular holomorphic one-form

Next, we consider a projective special complex manifold

As discussed above, we have a holomorphic affine immersion

Proposition 2.7
.

Up to a translation in

f:M\to\mathbb{C}^{2n+2}\backslash\{0\}

Since

\tilde{D}_{X}f_{\ast}\xi=f_{\ast}(D_{X}\xi)+\varepsilon f_{\ast}S_{X}\xi=f_{\ast}X\quad\mbox{and }\quad\tilde{D}_{X}(\tilde{J}f_{\ast}\xi)=\tilde{J}\tilde{D}_{X}f_{\ast}\xi=\tilde{J}f_{\ast}X.

The

\tilde{\xi}-\sqrt{-1}\tilde{J}\tilde{\xi}

\tilde{\xi}
is the Euler field (position vector field), which satisfies

These observations mean that

The canonical c-projective structure

In this subsection, we analyze the canonical c-projective structure of a projective special complex manifold introduced in
[
11
]

A smooth curve
t\mapsto c(t)
on a complex manifold
(M,J)

D_{c^{\prime}}c^{\prime}\in\langle c^{\prime},Jc^{\prime}\rangle

We say that torsion-free complex connections

(

-planar curves. It is known that

\displaystyle D^{1}_{X}Y=

\displaystyle D^{2}_{X}Y+\theta(X)Y+\theta(Y)X-\theta(JX)JY-\theta(JY)JX

.

(0,2)

3.3

At first, we recall the definition of conical special Kähler and projective special Kähler manifolds.

Definition 3.21
.

We call
(M,g,J,\omega,\nabla,\xi)
a
conical special Kähler manifold
if

•

(M,J,\nabla,\xi)

is the Levi-Civita connection of
g

•

(M,J,g)

\omega

\nabla\omega=0

•

g(\xi,\xi)
is nowhere-vanishing and
\langle\xi,J\xi\rangle
is negative definite and its orthogonal complement is positive
definite in
TM
.

We set
r:=\sqrt{-g(\xi,\xi)}
and
S:=r^{-1}(1)\subset M
.
Then it holds that
M=S\times\mathbb{R}^{>0}

A Kähler manifold
(\bar{M},\bar{J},\bar{g})

\pi:M\to\bar{M}
is a

\mathbb{C}^{\ast}
-bundle such that
the principal
\mathbb{C}^{\ast}
-action is generated by the holomorphic vector field

\xi-\sqrt{-1}J\xi
and
\bar{M}
is the Kähler quotient by the induced
\mathrm{U}(1)
-action, that is,

\bar{M}=S/\langle J\xi\rangle(=M/\mathbb{C}^{\ast})
.
There exists a unique principal connection
\varphi
on
M
whose horizontal subbundle
is orthogonal to the vertical subbundle with respect to
g
.
Note that any conical special Kähler manifold is of horizontal type
with respect to
\varphi

2.20
.

We have

\xi=r\partial_{r},\,\,\,g=r^{2}\pi^{\ast}\bar{g}-r^{2}\eta\otimes\eta-dr\otimes dr,

where
\eta
is the
\mathfrak{u}(1)
-component of
\varphi
. See
[
23
]
for example.
Using the Koszul formula for the Levi-Civita connection

of

g(D^{g}_{\tilde{X}}\tilde{Y},\xi)=-r^{2}\bar{g}(X,Y)
for

,

.
Therefore the fundamental
(0,2)
-tensor
\bar{a}
coincides with the Riemannian metric

(3.13)

\displaystyle\bar{a}=\bar{g}

and it holds that

from (

Note that

By (

\displaystyle\bar{g}=\frac{1}{8(n+1)}\bar{\cal B}-\frac{1}{2(n+1)}Ric^{\bar{D}^{\bar{g}}},

and
4.25

Lemma 4.26
.

If

is non-zero constant, then
the one-form

\displaystyle\gamma=-\frac{3}{2}\theta^{2}_{1}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{sdt-tds}{s^{2}+t^{2}}

.

Proof.

By Lemmas

\displaystyle dt+3s\theta^{2}_{1}-\bar{J}^{\ast}ds+3t\bar{J}^{\ast}\theta^{2}_{1}=-2s\gamma-2t\bar{J}^{\ast}\gamma,

\displaystyle-ds+3t\theta^{2}_{1}-\bar{J}^{\ast}dt-3s\bar{J}^{\ast}\theta^{2}_{1}=-2t\gamma+2s\bar{J}^{\ast}\gamma.

These two equations are equivalent to each other, so the condition (6-3) is equivalent to

\displaystyle dt+3s\theta^{2}_{1}-\bar{J}^{\ast}ds+3t\bar{J}^{\ast}\theta^{2}_{1}=-2s\gamma-2t\bar{J}^{\ast}\gamma.

If

\displaystyle\gamma=-\frac{3}{2}\theta^{2}_{1}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{sdt-tds}{s^{2}+t^{2}}

By considering

\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\phi(u)\\

\end{array}\right).

.
∎

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{-1}}(g_{0}-\bar{g}_{0})=-\frac{1}{2}(-l+1)(w(l_{1},\dots,l_{n})+\bar{w}(l_{1},\dots,l_{n})),

\displaystyle\mathrm{Re}\,g_{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(g_{i}+\bar{g}_{i})=\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2}l_{i}(-w(l_{1},\dots,l_{i}-1,\dots,l_{n})+\bar{w}(l_{1},\dots,l_{i}-1,\dots,l_{n})),

\displaystyle\mathrm{Im}\,g_{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{-1}}(g_{i}-\bar{g}_{i})=-\frac{1}{2}l_{i}(w(l_{1},\dots,l_{i}-1,\dots,l_{n})+\bar{w}(l_{1},\dots,l_{i}-1,\dots,l_{n})).

Their exterior derivatives are given as:

\displaystyle d\,\mathrm{Re}\,g_{0}=

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2}(-l+1)(-dw(l_{1},\dots,l_{n})+d\bar{w}(l_{1},\dots,l_{n}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2}(-l+1)\times

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}-l_{j}w(l_{1},\dots,l_{j}-1,\dots,l_{n})dw_{j}+l_{j}\bar{w}(l_{1},\dots,l_{j}-1,\dots,l_{n})d\bar{w}_{j}\right),

\displaystyle d\,\mathrm{Im}\,g_{0}=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}(-l+1)(dw(l_{1},\dots,l_{n})+d\bar{w}(l_{1},\dots,l_{n})),

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}(-l+1)\times

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}l_{j}w(l_{1},\dots,l_{j}-1,\dots,l_{n})dw_{j}+l_{j}\bar{w}(l_{1},\dots,l_{j}-1,\dots,l_{n})d\bar{w}_{j}\right),

\displaystyle d\,\mathrm{Re}\,g_{i}=

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2}l_{i}(-dw(l_{1},\dots,l_{i}-1,\dots,l_{n})+d\bar{w}(l_{1},\dots,l_{i}-1,\dots,l_{n}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2}l_{i}\bigg(\sum_{j=1,j\neq i}^{n}-l_{j}w(l_{1},\dots,l_{i}-1,\dots,l_{j}-1,\dots,l_{n})dw_{j}

\displaystyle+l_{j}\bar{w}(l_{1},\dots,l_{i}-1,\dots,l_{j}-1,\dots,l_{n})d\bar{w}_{j}

\displaystyle-(l_{i}-1)w(l_{1},\dots,l_{i}-2,\dots,l_{n})dw_{i}+(l_{i}-1)\bar{w}(l_{1},\dots,l_{i}-2,\dots,l_{n})d\bar{w}_{i}\bigg),

\displaystyle d\,\mathrm{Im}\,g_{i}=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}l_{i}(dw(l_{1},\dots,l_{i}-1,\dots,l_{n})+d\bar{w}(l_{1},\dots,l_{i}-1,\dots,l_{n}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}l_{i}\bigg(\sum_{j=1,j\neq i}^{n}l_{j}w(l_{1},\dots,l_{i}-1,\dots,l_{j}-1,\dots,l_{n})dw_{j}

\displaystyle+l_{j}\bar{w}(l_{1},\dots,l_{i}-1,\dots,l_{j}-1,\dots,l_{n})d\bar{w}_{j}

\displaystyle+(l_{i}-1)w(l_{1},\dots,l_{i}-2,\dots,l_{n})dw_{i}+(l_{i}-1)\bar{w}(l_{1},\dots,l_{i}-2,\dots,l_{n})d\bar{w}_{i}\bigg).

For example, when
n=2
and
g=-\sqrt{-1}\,z_{1}^{2}\,z_{2}/z_{0}^{2}

(l_{1}=2,l_{2}=1,l=3)
,
we find that

A_{0}\wedge A_{1}\wedge A_{1}\neq 0
. This indicates that

[A,[A,A]]\neq 0
. To verify this, it suffices to check that the
(1,1)
-entry

\displaystyle-2d\,\mathrm{Im}\,g_{0}\wedge d\,\mathrm{Im}\,g_{1}\wedge d\,\mathrm{Re}\,g_{1}

of
A_{0}\wedge A_{1}\wedge A_{1}
does not vanish, for instance.
Indeed, by using

\displaystyle d\,\mathrm{Im}\,g_{0}=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}(-3+1)(2w(1,1)dw_{1}+w(2,0)dw_{2}+2\bar{w}(1,1)d\bar{w}_{1})+w(2,0)d\bar{w}_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2w_{1}w_{2}dw_{1}+(w_{1})^{2}dw_{2}+2\bar{w}_{1}\bar{w}_{2}d\bar{w}_{1}+(\bar{w}_{1})^{2}d\bar{w}_{2},

\displaystyle d\,\mathrm{Re}\,g_{1}=

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2}2(-w(1,0)dw_{2}+\bar{w}(1,0)d\bar{w}_{2}-(2-1)w(2,-1)dw_{1}+(2-1)w(2,-1)d\bar{w}_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{-1}\left(-w_{1}dw_{2}+\bar{w}_{1}d\bar{w}_{2}-\frac{(w_{1})^{2}}{w_{2}}dw_{1}+\frac{(\bar{w}_{1})^{2}}{\bar{w}_{2}}d\bar{w}_{1}\right),

\displaystyle d\,\mathrm{Im}\,g_{1}=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}2(w(1,0)dw_{2}+\bar{w}(1,0)d\bar{w}_{2}+(2-1)w(2,-1)dw_{1}+(2-1)w(2,-1)d\bar{w}_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left(w_{1}dw_{2}+\bar{w}_{1}d\bar{w}_{2}+\frac{(w_{1})^{2}}{w_{2}}dw_{1}+\frac{(\bar{w}_{1})^{2}}{\bar{w}_{2}}d\bar{w}_{1}\right),

we can see that

\displaystyle\sqrt{-1}(d\,\mathrm{Im}\,g_{1}\wedge d\,\mathrm{Re}\,g_{1})

\displaystyle=2\left(|w_{1}|^{2}dw_{2}\wedge d\bar{w}_{2}+\frac{|w_{1}|^{2}w_{1}}{w_{2}}dw_{1}\wedge d\bar{w}_{2}+\frac{|w_{1}|^{4}}{|w_{2}|^{2}}dw_{1}\wedge d\bar{w}_{1}+\frac{|w_{1}|^{2}\bar{w}_{1}}{\bar{w}_{2}}dw_{2}\wedge d\bar{w}_{1}\right)

so

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{-1}(d\,\mathrm{Im}\,g_{0}\wedge

\displaystyle d\,\mathrm{Im}\,g_{1}\wedge d\,\mathrm{Re}\,g_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(2w_{1}w_{2}|w_{1}|^{2}-\frac{|w_{1}|^{2}(w_{1})^{3}}{w_{2}}\right)dw_{1}\wedge dw_{2}\wedge d\bar{w}_{2}

\displaystyle-\left(2\bar{w}_{1}\bar{w}_{2}|w_{1}|^{2}-\frac{|w_{1}|^{2}(\bar{w}_{1})^{3}}{\bar{w}_{2}}\right)d\bar{w}_{1}\wedge d\bar{w}_{2}\wedge dw_{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{|w_{1}|^{4}\bar{w}_{1}}{|w_{2}|^{2}}d\bar{w}_{1}\wedge dw_{1}\wedge d\bar{w}_{2}-\frac{|w_{1}|^{4}w_{1}}{|w_{2}|^{2}}dw_{1}\wedge d\bar{w}_{1}\wedge dw_{2}.

Hence
d\,\mathrm{Im}\,g_{0}\wedge d\,\mathrm{Im}\,g_{1}\wedge d\,\mathrm{Re}\,g_{1}

does not vanish. So we can summarize the results as follows.

Example 5.9
.

Let
U\subset\mathbb{C}^{3}
be the conical special complex manifold given
by the holomorphic
1
-form

\alpha=gdz_{0}-\sqrt{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{2}z_{i}dz_{i},\,\,g=-\frac{\sqrt{-1}\,z_{1}^{2}\,z_{2}}{z_{0}^{2}}.

We can associate
(\bar{U},[\bar{D}])
to a
12
-dimensional manifold

(TU,\{(I^{t}_{1},I^{t}_{2},I^{t}_{3})\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}/\pi\mathbb{Z}})

with a non-trivial

(\mathbb{R}/\pi\mathbb{Z})
-family of hypercomplex structures via the generalized rigid c-map.

5.3
Characterization of case of flat quaternionic structure on the tangent bundle
of a conical special complex manifold

The curvature of the Obata connection
\tilde{\nabla}^{0}
on
(TM,(I_{1},I_{2},I_{3}))
for a special complex manifold

M
was obtained in the proof of
[
11
, Theorem 6.5]
. It is Ricci flat and the quaternionic Weyl curvature
W^{Q}
of
Q=\langle I_{1},I_{2},I_{3}\rangle
is in general not zero but satisfies

W^{Q}_{X^{h_{\nabla}},Y^{h_{\nabla}}}Z^{h_{\nabla}}\\
=R^{\tilde{\nabla}^{0}}_{X^{h_{\nabla}},Y^{h_{\nabla}}}Z^{h_{\nabla}}=-\frac{1}{4}([A_{X},A_{Y}]Z)^{h_{\nabla}}

for tangent vectors
X
,
Y
,
Z
on
M
.
For instance,
the quaternionic structure on
TU
of
Example
5.9

is not flat, indeed
[A,[A,A]]\neq 0
.

It is natural to ask for relations between properties of the quaternionic structure

Q
on
TM
and properties of the canonical c-projective
structure
\bar{\mathcal{P}}(\pi)
on
\bar{M}
, since these structures are connected by the fibrations

(TM,Q=\langle I_{1},I_{2},I_{3}\rangle)\to(M,J,\nabla,\xi)\to(\bar{M},\bar{J},\bar{\mathcal{P}}(\pi)).

In this section we study the case when the quaternionic structure
of
(TM,(I_{1},I_{2},I_{3}))
is flat. We show in the next theorem that it
implies the vanishing of the Weyl tensor of the c-projective structure on
\bar{M}
.

Theorem 5.10
.

Let
(M,J,\nabla,\xi)
be a conical special complex manifold fibering over the projective special complex manifold

(\bar{M},\bar{J},\bar{\mathcal{P}}(\pi))
with the corresponding principal
\mathbb{C}^{\ast}
-bundle
\pi:M\to\bar{M}
and the canonical c-projective structure

\bar{\mathcal{P}}(\pi)
.
If
\dim\bar{M}\geq 4
and

W^{Q}=0
for the quaternionic structure
Q=\langle I_{1},I_{2},I_{3}\rangle
on
TM
,
then we have
W^{\bar{\mathcal{P}}(\pi)}=0
and
\bar{\cal B}=0
.

Proof.

Take an open covering

\{\bar{U}_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Lambda}
of

\bar{M}
with trivializations
\pi^{-1}(\bar{U}_{\alpha})\cong\bar{U}_{\alpha}\times\mathbb{C}^{*}
.
Let
X
,
Y
,
Z
be vector fields on
\bar{M}
.
We denote the horizontal lift of
X\in\Gamma(T\bar{M})
by
\tilde{X}\in\Gamma(TM)
with respect to
a principal connection on
M
of type
(1,0)
and note that the c-projective structure

\bar{\mathcal{P}}(\pi)

is independent of the choice of the connection form as shown in Theorem
2.11
.
On any
\bar{U}_{\alpha}
, we have

\displaystyle W^{Q}_{\tilde{X}^{h_{\nabla}},\tilde{Y}^{h_{\nabla}}}\tilde{Z}^{h_{\nabla}}=-\frac{1}{4}([A_{\tilde{X}},A_{\tilde{Y}}]\tilde{Z})^{h_{\nabla}}=-\frac{1}{4}([B^{\alpha}_{\tilde{X}},B^{\alpha}_{\tilde{Y}}]\tilde{Z})^{h_{\nabla}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigg[\left(-\frac{1}{4}[\bar{B}^{\alpha}_{X},\bar{B}^{\alpha}_{Y}]Z\right)^{\widetilde{}}-\frac{1}{4}\left(\bar{c}^{\alpha}(X,\bar{B}^{\alpha}_{Y}Z)-\bar{c}^{\alpha}(Y,\bar{B}^{\alpha}_{X}Z)\right)\xi

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4}\left(\bar{c}^{\alpha}_{\bar{J}}(X,\bar{B}^{\alpha}_{Y}Z)-\bar{c}^{\alpha}_{\bar{J}}(Y,\bar{B}^{\alpha}_{X}Z)\right)Z_{M}\bigg]^{h_{\nabla}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigg[\bigg(W^{{\bar{\mathcal{P}}(\pi)}}_{X,Y}Z+\frac{1}{4(n+1)}\bar{\cal B}_{\bar{J}}(X,Y)\bar{J}Z-\frac{1}{8(n+1)}(\bar{\cal B}(X,Z)Y-\bar{\cal B}(Y,Z)X)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{8(n+1)}(\bar{\cal B}_{\bar{J}}(X,Z)\bar{J}Y-\bar{\cal B}_{\bar{J}}(Y,Z)\bar{J}X)\bigg)^{\widetilde{}}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4}\left(\bar{c}^{\alpha}(X,\bar{B}^{\alpha}_{Y}Z)-\bar{c}^{\alpha}(Y,\bar{B}^{\alpha}_{X}Z)\right)\xi-\frac{1}{4}\left(\bar{c}^{\alpha}_{\bar{J}}(X,\bar{B}^{\alpha}_{Y}Z)-\bar{c}^{\alpha}_{\bar{J}}(Y,\bar{B}^{\alpha}_{X}Z)\right)Z_{M}\bigg]^{h_{\nabla}},

where we have used (
2.7
) and (
2.12
).
Since the quaternionic structure
Q
on
TM
is flat, in particular, the horizontal component (with respect to the principal connection) of the term inside of

[\,\,\,\,\,]^{h_{\nabla}}

in the above equation vanishes:

\displaystyle W^{\bar{\mathcal{P}}(\pi)}_{X,Y}Z+\frac{1}{4(n+1)}\bar{\cal B}_{\bar{J}}(X,Y)\bar{J}Z

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{8(n+1)}(\bar{\cal B}(X,Z)Y-\bar{\cal B}(Y,Z)X)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{8(n+1)}(\bar{\cal B}_{\bar{J}}(X,Z)\bar{J}Y-\bar{\cal B}_{\bar{J}}(Y,Z)\bar{J}X)=0.

Since the c-projective Weyl curvature
W^{\bar{\mathcal{P}}(\pi)}
satisfies

\mathrm{Tr}\,W^{\bar{\mathcal{P}}(\pi)}_{(\,\,\cdot\,\,),Y}Z=0
, we have

\displaystyle 0=\mathrm{Tr}\,W^{\bar{\mathcal{P}}(\pi)}_{(\,\,\cdot\,\,),Y}Z+\frac{1}{4}\bar{\cal B}(Y,Z)=\frac{1}{4}\bar{\cal B}(Y,Z).

Then we obtain
W^{\bar{\mathcal{P}}(\pi)}=0
and
\bar{\cal B}=0
. This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 5.11
.

The flatness
W^{Q}=0
of the quaternionic structure on
TM
is equivalent to

R^{\tilde{\nabla}^{0}}=0
.

If
M
is a special Kähler manifold, the following corollary holds.

Corollary 5.12
.

Let
(M,g,J,\omega,\nabla,\xi)
be a conical special Kähler manifold.
If
W^{Q}=0
for the quaternionic structure
Q=\langle I_{1},I_{2},I_{3}\rangle
on
TM
, then
the projective special Kähler manifold
(\bar{M},\bar{J},\bar{g})
is of negative constant holomorphic sectional curvature
-4
and the conical special Kähler manifold
M
is trivial.

Proof.

If
\dim\bar{M}\geq 4
, we have
\bar{\cal B}=0
by Theorem
5.10
.
The vanishing of
\bar{\cal B}
implies that
equality holds in (
3.15
), thus indicating that

\bar{g}
has constant holomorphic sectional
curvature
-4
.
Moreover
\bar{\cal B}=0
means that
\bar{B}^{\alpha}=0
for all
\alpha\in\Lambda
.
Since a conical special Kähler manifold is of horizontal type, which means
\bar{c}^{\alpha}=0
,
then we also have
B^{\alpha}=0
for all
\alpha\in\Lambda
.
This shows that
A=0
, hence
M
is trivial.
If
\dim\bar{M}=2
, we obtain

[\bar{B}^{\alpha},\bar{B}^{\alpha}]=0

in a similar calculation as above. From (
2.8
), we have

R^{\bar{D}^{\bar{g}}}=-2\bar{g}_{\bar{J}}\otimes\bar{J}+\bar{g}\wedge\mathrm{Id}-\bar{g}_{\bar{J}}\wedge\bar{J},

which means that
\bar{g}
has the constant Gaussian curvature
-4
.
Since
[\bar{B}^{\alpha},\bar{B}^{\alpha}]=0
, taking its trace shows that
\bar{\cal B}=0
.
Therefore
M
is trivial.
∎

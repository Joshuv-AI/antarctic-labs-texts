Fixed points on null and tame flows for groups of automorphisms
Alessandro Codenotti

Abstract
. Using a generalization of the Kechris-Pestov-Todorčević correspondence due to Nguyen Van Thé we obtain fixed point theorems for null and tame actions of groups of the form Aut ⁡ ( ℱ ) \Aut(\mathcal{F}) , where ℱ \mathcal{F} is a Fraïssé structure. In particular we show that if Age ⁡ ( ℱ ) \mathrm{Age}(\mathcal{F}) is a free joint embedding class, then every null flow Aut ⁡ ( ℱ ) ↷ X \Aut(\mathcal{F})\curvearrowright X has a fixed point, while if Age ⁡ ( ℱ ) \mathrm{Age}(\mathcal{F}) is a free amalgamation class, then every tame flow Aut ⁡ ( ℱ ) ↷ X \Aut(\mathcal{F})\curvearrowright X has a fixed point.



1.
Introduction

Given a topological group
G
, which we will always assume to be Hausdorff, a
G
-flow
, or simply a
flow
when
G
is clear from context, is a compact space
X
with a continuous action
G\curvearrowright X
. A
G
-flow is called
minimal
if
\overline{G\cdot x}=X
for every
x\in X
, equivalently if there are no proper, closed,
G
-invariant subspaces. A
G
-flow
Y
is a
factor
of a
G
-flow
X
if there there is a continuous surjection
f\colon X\to Y
which is
G
-equivariant, that is

f(g\cdot x)=g\cdot f(x),\,\text{for every }x\in X,g\in G.

A classical result of Ellis shows that every topological group
G
has a
universal minimal flow
, that is a minimal
G
-flow which has all minimal
G
-flows as factors, moreover this flow is unique up to isomorphism
[
Ell60
, Theorem 2]
so that we can talk about
the
universal minimal flow of
G
, denoted by
M(G)
. A topological group
G
is called
extremely amenable
if every
G
-flow has a fixed point, or equivalently if
M(G)
is a singleton. A celebrated result, now known as the KPT correspondence, established in
[
KPT05
]
by Kechris, Pestov and Todorčević, characterizes which groups of the form
\Aut(\mathcal{F})
, where
\mathcal{F}
is a Fraïssé structure, are extremely amenable, in terms of a Ramsey property for
\mathrm{Age}(\mathcal{F})
. The KPT correspondence was generalized by Nguyen van Thé
[
Ngu19
, Theorem 1.2]
, where fixed points on a specified class of
\Aut(\mathcal{F})
-flows are obtained from an approximate version of the Ramsey property of
\mathrm{Age}(\mathcal{F})
for a specified family of colourings (see Theorem
3.4
for a precise statement). We focus on the classes of
null
and
tame

\Aut(\mathcal{F})
-flows, those being two notion of well-behavedness for flows (defined precisely in Definitions
2.6
and
2.11
). The study of tame dynamical systems was initiated by Köhler
[
Köh95
]
(under the name of
regular
systems), who obtained a dicothomy for the enveloping semigroups of dynamical systems consisting of interval maps, based on the Bourgain-Fremlin-Talagrand dichotomy for pointwise compact sets of Baire class 1 functions
[
BFT78
]
. The dynamical version of this dicothomy has more recently been extended to arbitrary metrizable systems in
[
GM04
]
, based on the notion of
tameness
, that is the nonexistence of continuous function whose orbit contain a set equivalent to the standard basis of
\ell^{1}
(indeed the roots of the Bourgain-Fremlin-Talagrand dicothomy trace back to Rosenthal’s groundbreaking characterization of Banach spaces not embedding
\ell^{1}

[
Ros78
]
). The theory of tame dynamical systems has been greatly developed by various authors over the past few decades, see in particular
[
GM18
]
and the reference therein for a survey. This has produced many equivalent characterization of tameness, the most important for us being the one established in
[
KL07
]
in terms of the nonexistence of infinite independent sets (see Definition
2.11
). The notion of nullness, initially defined in terms of sequence entropy, but later characterized in terms of the nonexistence of arbitrarily large (but finite) independence sets (see Definition
2.6
), is a natural strengthening of the notion of tameness. We introduce two variants of the Ramsey property for the age of a Fraïssé class, closely related to the classes of flows mentioned above, which we call the null and tame Ramsey properties. We then show that free joint embedding classes satisfy the approximate null Ramsey property, while free amalgamation classes satisfy the approximate tame Ramsey property. Using the result by Nguyen van Thé mentioned above we translate this combinatorial properties of
\mathrm{Age}(\mathcal{F})
into dynamical statements about
\Aut(\mathcal{F})
, in particular we obtain the following results (see Theorem
3.7
and Theorem
3.12
for complete statements).

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a Fraïssé structure such that
\mathrm{Age}(\mathcal{F})
has the free joint embedding property. Any null flow
\Aut(\mathcal{F})\curvearrowright X
has a fixed point.

Since tameness is a strictly stronger property than nullness, the analogous result for tame flows requires stronger hypotheses:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a Fraïssé structure such that
\mathrm{Age}(\mathcal{F})
has the free amalgamation property. Any tame flow
\Aut(\mathcal{F})\curvearrowright X
has a fixed point.

Let us mention that a special case of the above results was already established by Glasner and Megrelishvili, although through completely different methods. When
\mathcal{F}
is a countable set, that is the Fraïssé limit of the free amalgamation class of finite sets,
\Aut(\mathcal{F})=S_{\infty}
, the group of permutations of the natural numbers. It is shown in
[
GM18
, Theorem 8.4]
that every tame flow
S_{\infty}\curvearrowright X
has a fixed point or equivalently that
M_{t}(S_{\infty})
(see Definition
2.15
) is a singleton. Although not stated explicitely there the techniques of
[
GM18
]
also apply to
\Aut(M)
, where
M
is the random graph, another standard example of a Fraïssé structure arising from a free amalgamation class.

2.
Definitions and background

2.1.
Fraïssé theory

Let
\mathcal{L}=\{R_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be a relational language, where
R_{i}
has arity
n_{i}
for every
i
. A countable
\mathcal{L}
-structure
M
is called
ultrahomogeneous
if whenever
A,B\subseteq M
are finite substructures and
f\colon A\to B
is an isomorphism, it is possible to extend
f
to an automorphism of
M
. A countable, ultrahomogeneous structure is called a
Fraïssé structure
. Given a Fraïssé structure
M
we let
\mathrm{Age}(M)
denote the class of finite structures that embed into
M
. Using the ultrahomogeneity of
M
, it is easy to check that
\mathrm{Age}(M)
satisfies the following properties:

(1)

\mathrm{Age}(M)
contains countably many structures, up to isomorphism.

(2)

If
A\in\mathrm{Age}(M)
and
B\subseteq A
, then
B\in\mathrm{Age}(M)
, this is known as the
hereditary property
.

(3)

If
A,B\in\mathrm{Age}(M)
, then there exists
C\in\mathrm{Age}(M)
into which both
A
and
B
can be embedded, this is known as the
joint embedding property

(4)

If
A,B,C\in\mathrm{Age}(M)
and
f_{1}\colon A\to B
,
f_{2}\colon A\to C
are two embeddings, then there exist
D\in\mathrm{Age}(M)
and embeddings
g_{1}\colon B\to D
and
g_{2}\colon C\to D
such that
g_{1}\circ f_{1}=g_{2}\circ f_{2}
, this is known as the
amalgamation property
.

A class of finite structures satisfying the four properties above is called a
Fraïssé class
and, by a classical result due to Fraïssé, there is a one-to-one correspondence between Fraïssé classes and Fraïssé structures. Given a Fraïssé class
\mathcal{F}
, the (unique up to isomorphism) Fraïssé structure
M
with
\mathrm{Age}(M)=\mathcal{F}
is called the
Fraïssé limit of
\mathcal{F}
. The Fraïssé limit of
\mathcal{F}
is also characterized as the unique structure
M
with the following extension property: for every
A,B\in\mathrm{Age}(\mathcal{F})
and every pair of embeddings
f_{1}\colon A\to M
,
f_{2}\colon A\to B
, there exists an embedding
g\colon B\to M
with
g\circ f_{2}=f_{1}
.

Various strengthenings of the properties above have been considered, the following two will be particularly relevant for our purposes.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a Fraïssé structure in the language
\mathcal{L}
. Given
A,B\in\mathrm{Age}(\mathcal{F})
let
A\sqcup B
be the structure whose underlying set is the disjoint union of
A
and
B
, and such that there are no new relations between the elements of
A
and those of
B
. Explicitly
R_{i}^{A\sqcup B}(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n_{i}})
holds in
A\sqcup B
if and only if either
c_{1},\ldots,c_{n_{i}}\in A
and
R_{i}^{A}(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n_{i}})
holds in
A
, or
c_{1},\ldots,c_{n_{i}}\in B
and
R_{i}^{B}(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n_{i}})
holds in
B
. We say that
\mathrm{Age}(\mathcal{F})
has the
free joint embedding property
if for every
A,B\in\mathrm{Age}(\mathcal{F})
,
A\sqcup B
also belongs to
\mathrm{Age}(\mathcal{F})
.

Clearly the free joint embedding property implies the joint embedding property. Given
(A_{i})_{i\in\omega}\subseteq\mathrm{Age}(\mathcal{F})
, the structure
A=\bigsqcup_{i}A_{i}
can be defined analogously. While it won’t be an element of
\mathrm{Age}(\mathcal{F})
, being infinite, it can embedded in
\mathcal{F}
as long as
\mathrm{Age}(\mathcal{F})
has the free joint embedding property, since in that case
\mathrm{Age}(A)\subseteq\mathrm{Age}(\mathcal{F})
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a Fraïssé structure. We say that
\mathrm{Age}(\mathcal{F})
has the
free amalgamation property
if for all
A,B,C\in\mathrm{Age}(\mathcal{F})
and all pairs of embedding
f_{1}\colon A\to B
,
f_{2}\colon A\to C
, the structure
B\sqcup C/\sim
is also in
\mathrm{Age}(\mathcal{F})
, where
b\sim c
if and only if there exists
a\in A
with
b=f_{1}(a)
and
c=f_{2}(a)
.

Note that the free amalgamation property implies the amalgamation property and that, since we’re working with relation languages, the free amalgamation property implies the free joint embedding property (by taking
A
to be the empty structure structure). As is standard we will also say that
\mathcal{F}
has one of the properties defined above to mean that
\mathrm{Age}(\mathcal{F})
has the property in question.

2.2.
Subalgebras and compactifications

Let
G
be a topological group. Note that
G
-flows are always assumed to be compact, so we will say that
X
is a
G
-space
if it is a not necessarily compact space
X
with a continuous
G
-action. For clarity we will still often explicitly point out when a
G
-space is not assumed to be compact. A
G
-ambit
(X,x)
is a
G
-flow
X
together with a distinguished point
x
with dense orbit. Given a family of
G
-ambits
(X_{\lambda},x_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
their
supremum

\bigvee_{\lambda\in\Lambda}(X_{\lambda},x_{\lambda})
is the subflow of
\prod_{\lambda\in\Lambda}X_{\lambda}
induced on the orbit closure of
(x_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
, together with the distinguished point
(x_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
.

Let
X
be a (not necessarily compact)
G
-space. An embedding
\nu\colon X\to Y
with dense image into a compact space
Y
is called a
G
-compactification of
X
if the action
G\curvearrowright X
extends to an action
G\curvearrowright Y
. We say that a function
f\in C(X)

comes from a
G
-compactification

\nu\colon X\to Y
if there is
\tilde{f}\in C(Y)
such that
f=\tilde{f}\circ\nu
(note that, if it exists,
\tilde{f}
is unique). Given a
G
-space
X
, a bounded continuous function
f\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
is called
right uniformly continuous
if

\forall\varepsilon>0\,\exists U\in\mathcal{N}_{G}\,\forall g\in U\,\forall x\in X\left(|f(g^{-1}x)-f(x)|<\varepsilon\right),

where
\mathcal{N}_{G}
denotes the set of neighbourhoods of the identity in
G
. The collection of bounded right uniformly continuous functions on
X
equipped with the
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
-norm forms a normed algebra denoted by
\mathrm{RUC}_{b}(X)
. There is a correspondence between
G
-compactifications of
X
and uniformly closed
G
-invariant subalgebras of
\mathrm{RUC}_{b}(X)
that contain the constant functions. The subalgebra
\mathcal{A}_{\nu}\subseteq\mathrm{RUC}_{b}(X)
associated to a
G
-compactification
\nu\colon X\to Y
is

\mathcal{A}_{\nu}=\{f\in C(X)\mid f\text{ comes from $\nu$}\}.

Conversely given such an algebra
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathrm{RUC}_{b}(X)
we obtain a
G
-compactification
X^{\mathcal{A}}
of
X
by letting

X^{\mathcal{A}}=\{f\colon\mathcal{A}\to\mathbb{R}\mid f\text{ is a linear functional}\},

equipped with the weak* topology. Identifying linear functionals and maximal ideals of
\mathrm{RUC}_{b}(X)
we have a canonical embedding
\nu_{\mathcal{A}}\colon X\to X^{\mathcal{A}}
given by
x\mapsto p_{x}
, where
p_{x}
is the maximal ideal defined by
p_{x}=\{f\in X^{\mathcal{A}}\mid f(x)=0\}.
In this description basic open sets of
X^{\mathcal{A}}
are of the form

U_{f,\delta}=\{p\in X^{\mathcal{A}}\mid|\tilde{f}(p)|<\delta\},

where
f
is a linear functional
\mathcal{A}\to\mathbb{R}
,
\delta>0
and
\tilde{f}(p)
is the unique
r\in\mathbb{R}
such that
f-r\in p
. Note that if
f\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
is continuous, then
\tilde{f}(p_{x})=f(x)
, so that
\tilde{f}
is the extension of
f
to the
G
-compactification
X^{\mathcal{A}}
.

If
f
comes from a
G
-compactification of
X
then clearly
f\in\mathrm{RUC}_{b}(X)
, and the converse is true. Indeed for any
f\in\mathrm{RUC}_{b}(X)
there is a minimal
G
-compactification of
X
that
f
comes from, called the cyclic
G
-space of
f
and denoted by
X_{f}
. This corresponds to
X^{\langle f\rangle}
, the
G
-compactification associated to the closed (in the topology induced by the
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
-norm) subalgebra of
\mathrm{RUC}_{b}(X)
generated by
f
. Moreover if
\mathcal{A}
is any closed
G
-invariant subalgebra of
\mathrm{RUC}_{b}(X)
that contains the constant functions and
f
, the flow
X^{\mathcal{A}}
has
X_{f}
as a factor. A reference for the facts just mentioned is
[
Vri13
, IV, §5]
, of particular relevance are Theorem 5.13 and Theorem 5.18.

There is a continuous left action of
G
on
\mathrm{RUC}_{b}(G)
given by
(g\cdot f)(h)=f(g^{-1}h)
, where
\mathrm{RUC}_{b}(G)
is equipped with the norm topology. There is also a right action of
G
given by
(g\bullet f)(h)=f(hg)
. When
\mathrm{RUC}_{b}(G)
is equipped with the pointwise convergence topology, then this action is continuous on
\overline{G\bullet f}
(closure in the pointwise convergence topology) for every
f\in\mathrm{RUC}_{b}(X)
, even though it can fail to be continuous on the whole of
\mathrm{RUC}_{b}(G)
. To summarize to every
f\in\mathrm{RUC}_{b}(G)
we have associated two ambits:
(G^{\langle f\rangle},1_{G})
and
(\overline{G\bullet f},f)
which are isomorphic, as proved by Nguyen Van Thé in
[
Ngu19
, Proposition 3.9]
.

2.3.
Null and tame functions

The following characterization of null and tame functions is due to David Kerr and Hanfeng Li
[
KL07
]
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
A_{1},\ldots,A_{k}\subseteq X
. An
independence set
for
\{A_{1},\ldots,A_{k}\}
is a set
I\subseteq G
such that for all finite
J\subseteq I
and all functions
\sigma\colon J\to k
we have

\bigcap_{j\in J}j^{-1}A_{\sigma(j)}\neq\varnothing.

Definition 2.4
.

A tuple
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})\in X^{k}
is an
IN
-tuple
(
IN
-pair
in the case
k=2
) if for every product neighbourhood
U_{1}\times U_{2}\times\cdots\times U_{k}
of
x
and every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the tuple
\{U_{1},\ldots,U_{k}\}
has an independence set of size at least
n
.

Proposition 2.5

(
[
KL07
, Proposition 5.4(i)]
)
.

If
\{A_{1},\ldots,A_{k}\}
is a tuple of closed sets with arbitrarily large finite independence sets, then
A_{1}\times\cdots\times A_{k}
contains an
IN
-tuple.

We take the following as our definition of a null function. It is equivalent to other, more common, characterizations by
[
KL07
, Proposition 5.9]
.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
X
be a compact
G
-space. A function
f\in\mathrm{RUC}_{b}(X)
is
not null
if and only if there is an
IN
-pair
(x,y)\in X\setminus\Delta_{X}
such that
f(x)\neq f(y)
. A dynamical system
(X,G)
is called
null
if every
f\in\mathrm{RUC}_{b}(X)
is null.

Consider now an arbitrary
G
-space
X
, not necessarily compact. We say that
f\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
is
null
if and only if there is a null
G
-compactification
\nu\colon X\to\tilde{X}
that
f
comes from.

Remark 2.7
.

As already observed before Fact 3.6 in
[
Iba16
]
it follows from the fact that factors of null flows are null
[
KL07
, Corollary 5.5]
that
f
is null if and only if
X_{f}
is null. Indeed if
\nu\colon X\to Y
is any null
G
-compactification that
f
comes from, then it has
X_{f}
as a factor, so that the latter must be null.

Proposition 2.8

(
[
Iba16
, Fact 3.6]
)
.

A function
f\in\mathrm{RUC}(X)
is null if and only if there are no
r,s\in\mathbb{R}
with
r<s
such that for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
one can find
(g_{i})_{i<n}\subseteq G
and
(x_{I})_{I\subseteq n}\subseteq X
such that

\displaystyle f(g_{i}x_{I})

\displaystyle<r\quad\text{if $i\in I$}

\displaystyle f(g_{i}x_{I})

\displaystyle>s\quad\text{if $i\not\in I$}.

We have analogous results for tameness.

Definition 2.9
.

A tuple
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})\in X^{k}
is an
IT
-tuple (
IT
-pair in the case
k=2
) if for every product neighbourhood
U_{1}\times U_{2}\times\cdots\times U_{k}
of
x
, the tuple
\{U_{1},\ldots,U_{k}\}
has an infinite independence set.

Proposition 2.10

(
[
KL07
, Proposition 6.4(i)]
)
.

If
\{A_{1},\ldots,A_{k}\}
is a tuple of closed sets with an infinite independence set, then
A_{1}\times\cdots\times A_{k}
contains an
IT
-tuple.

We take the following as our definition of a tame function. This is not how tameness is usually defined in the literature, but it is equivalent to more common definitions by
[
KL07
, Proposition 6.6]

Definition 2.11
.

Let
X
be a compact
G
-space. A function
f\in\mathrm{RUC}(X)
is
not tame
if and only if there is an
IT
-pair
(x,y)\in X\setminus\Delta_{X}
such that
f(x)\neq f(y)
. A dynamical system
(X,G)
is called
tame
if every
f\in\mathrm{RUC}_{b}(X)
is tame.

Example 2.12
.

Examples of null (hence also tame) flows include all continuous actions by homeomorphisms
G\curvearrowright X
where
X
is a dendrite
[
GM19
]
. More concretely for any homeomorphism
\varphi\colon[0,1]\to[0,1]
the induced
\mathbb{Z}
-action is null (hence tame). Examples of flows that are not null are given by actions with positive entropy, the classical example being the action
\Homeo(2^{\omega})\curvearrowright 2^{\omega}
of the homeomorphism group of the Cantor set on the Cantor set itself. It is more difficult to construct flows which are tame but not null, an example of such a
\mathbb{Z}
-flow is given in
[
KL07
, Example 6.7]
.

Consider now an arbitrary
G
-space
X
, not necessarily compact. We say that
f\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
is tame if and only if there is a tame
G
-compactification
\nu\colon X\to\tilde{X}
that
f
comes from.

Remark 2.13
.

Analogously to the case of null functions, it follows from
[
KL07
, Corollary 6.5]
, which shows that factors of tame flows are tame, that
f\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
is tame if and only if
G\curvearrowright X^{\langle f\rangle}
is tame. Indeed if
\nu\colon X\to Y
is any tame
G
-compactification that
f
comes from, then it has
X_{f}
as a factor, so that the latter must be tame.

The following is a version of Proposition
2.8
for tame functions.

Proposition 2.14
.

Let
X
be a (not necessarily compact)
G
-space. A function
f\in\mathrm{RUC}_{b}(X)
is tame if and only if there are no
r,s\in\mathbb{R}
with
r<s
for which there exist
(g_{i})_{i<\omega}\subseteq G
and
(x_{I})_{I\subseteq_{fin}\omega}\subseteq X
such that

(1)

\displaystyle f(g_{i}x_{I})

\displaystyle<r\text{ if $i\in I$}

\displaystyle f(g_{i}x_{I})

\displaystyle>s\text{ if $i\not\in I$ and $i\leq\max I$,}

where
I\subseteq_{fin}\omega
means that
I
is a finite subset of
\omega
.

Proof.

The proof is very similar to that of
[
Iba16
, Fact 3.6]
, but we spell out the details for completeness. First suppose
f\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
does not satisfy (
1
), we want to show that
f
is not tame. Let
r<s\in\mathbb{R}
,
(g_{i})_{i<\omega}\subseteq G
and
(x_{I})_{I\subseteq_{fin}\omega}\subseteq X
witness the failure of (
1
) for
f
. Extend
f
to
\tilde{f}\colon X^{\langle f\rangle}\to\mathbb{R}
, we will show that
\tilde{f}
is not tame, which immediately implies that
f
is not tame either by Remark
2.13
. Consider

\displaystyle A_{0}

\displaystyle=\{p\in X^{\langle f\rangle}\mid\tilde{f}(p)\leq r\}

\displaystyle A_{1}

\displaystyle=\{p\in X^{\langle f\rangle}\mid\tilde{f}(p)\geq s\}

We claim that the set
Z=\{g_{i}\mid i<\omega\}
is an infinite independence set for
\{A_{0},A_{1}\}
. Indeed let
J\subseteq Z
be finite and
\sigma\colon J\to 2
an arbitrary function. Let
J_{0}=\{j\in\omega\mid g_{j}\in J\}
and let
S_{0}=\{j\in J_{0}\mid\sigma(g_{j})=1\}
. Let
I=J_{0}\setminus S_{0}\cup\{\max J_{0}+1\}
. We will now verify that

\bigcap_{s\in J}s^{-1}A_{\sigma(s)}\neq\varnothing,

by showing that
x_{I}\in s^{-1}A_{\sigma(s)}
for every
s\in J
. Every such
s
is of the form
g_{i}
for some
i\in J_{0}
, we distinguish two cases based on whether
i\in S_{0}
or
i\in J_{0}\setminus S_{0}
. If
i\in J_{0}\setminus S_{0}
, then
i\in I
, so that
f(g_{i}x_{I})<r
by (
1
). In particular
g_{i}x_{I}\in A_{0}
, so that
x_{I}\in g_{i}^{-1}A_{0}
. But
g_{i}^{-1}=s^{-1}
and
\sigma(g_{i})=0
since
i\not\in S_{0}
, so that
x_{I}\in s^{-1}A_{\sigma(s)}
as desired.

If instead
i\in S_{0}
, then
i\not\in I
and
i\leq\max I
, so that
f(g_{i}x_{I})>s
by (
1
). In particular
g_{i}x_{I}\in A_{1}
, so that
x_{I}\in g_{i}^{-1}A_{1}
. But
g_{i}^{-1}=s^{-1}
and
\sigma(g_{i})=1
since

, so that
x_{I}\in s^{-1}A_{\sigma(s)}
in this case as well.

By Proposition
2.10
we can find an IT-pair
(x,y)\in A_{0}\times A_{1}
. Clearly
\tilde{f}(x)\neq\tilde{f}(y)
, so
\tilde{f}
is not tame and neither is
f
.

Conversely suppose that
f\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
is not tame, we want to show that
f
does not satisfy (
1
). Extend
f
to a function
\tilde{f}\colon\tilde{X}\to\mathbb{R}
on some
G
-compactification
\tilde{X}
(for example we could take
\tilde{X}=X^{\langle f\rangle}
). Note that
\tilde{f}
is necessarily not tame, which means that there is a nondiagonal IT-pair
(x,y)\in\tilde{X}^{2}
such that
\tilde{f}(x)<\tilde{f}(y)
. Fix
r,s
such that
\tilde{f}(x)<r<s<\tilde{f}(y)
and, by continuity of
\tilde{f}
, open neighbourhoods
U_{0}\ni x
,
U_{1}\ni y
such that
\tilde{f}(U_{0})<r
,
\tilde{f}(U_{1})>s
. Let
(g_{i})_{i<\omega}
be an infinite independence set for
\{U_{0},U_{1}\}
, which exists since
(x,y)
is an IT-pair. Now let
I
be a finite subset of
\omega
and let
\sigma\colon\max I\to 2
be the complement of its indicator function, so that
\sigma(i)=0
if and only if
i\in I
. Since
\{g_{i}\mid i<\omega\}
is an independence set for
\{U_{0},U_{1}\}
, we can find

\tilde{x}_{I}\in\bigcap_{i\leq\max I}g_{i}^{-1}U_{\sigma(i)}.

We claim that
r,s
,
(g_{i})_{i<\omega}
and
\tilde{x}_{I}
as above witness that
\tilde{f}
does not satisfy (
1
).
Indeed if
i\in I
, then
\sigma(i)=0
and
\tilde{x}_{I}\in g_{i}^{-1}U_{0}
, so that
g_{i}\tilde{x}_{I}\in U_{0}
and
f(g_{i}\tilde{x}_{I})<r
. If instead
i\not\in I
and
i\leq\max I
, then
\sigma(i)=1
and
\tilde{x}_{I}\in g_{i}^{-1}U_{1}
, so that
g_{i}\tilde{x}_{I}\in U_{1}
and
f(g_{i}\tilde{x}_{I})>s
.

The only issue is that
\tilde{x}_{I}\in\tilde{X}
, while we were looking for points of
X
, but we can approximate
\tilde{x}_{I}
with some
x_{I}\in X
, since we only need the values of
\tilde{f}(g_{i}\tilde{x}_{I})
and

to be close for finitely many

.

∎

Definition 2.15
.

Let
G
be a topological group. We denote with
M_{n}(G)
the
universal minimal null flow of

G
. This is a minimal null
G
-flow such that if
X
is any other minimal null
G
-flow, there exists a
G
-equivariant surjection

. In other words

has all minimal null
G
-flows as factors. Existence and uniqueness of

follow from
[

, Theorem 1]
: null flows are closed under products and subflows, so also under projective limits, by
[

, Corollary 5.5]
, while minimal flows are easily checked to be closed under projective limits. Similarly we let

denote the universal minimal tame flow of

, whose existence and uniqueness up to isomorphisms of

-flows can be established using Theorem 1 of
[

]
again, together with the fact that tame flows are also closed under products and subflows by

, Corollary 6.5]
.

3.
The null and tame Ramsey properties

3.1.
Colourings, Nullness, fixed points and free joint embedding

Before proceeding with this section, we need to fix some notational conventions, largely borrowed from
[
Ngu19
]
. Given a Fraïssé structure
\mathcal{F}
and a finite substructure

, we denote by

the

stabilizer of
A
in

and by

the set of

of

in
\mathcal{F}
. With those conventions, thanks to the ultrahomogeneity of

, we can identify

and
\binom{\mathcal{F}}{A}
.

We will be interested in functions

or

of

, depending on whether their image is finite or not. If a colouring

is

. Through the identification of

and

outlined above we can think about a colouring

as a function

which is constant on small enough balls of the right uniformity of

for a big enough

). We will confuse

and the corresponding

in the following, so that, for example, we will talk about colourings of

in

and its subalgebras.

Specializing the characterizations of nullness and tameness established in Proposition

and Proposition

to the context of colourings we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 3.1
.

(1)

A colouring

is

\displaystyle\chi(g_{i}h_{I})

\displaystyle<r\quad\text{if $i\in I$}

\displaystyle\chi(g_{i}h_{I})

\displaystyle>s\quad\text{if $i\not\in I$}.

(2)

A colouring
\chi\colon\binom{\mathcal{F}}{A}\to\mathbb{R}
is
not tame
if and only if there are
r,s\in\mathbb{R}
with

such that there exist sequences
(g_{i})_{i<\omega}\subseteq\Aut(\mathcal{F})
and
(h_{I})_{I\subseteq_{fin}\omega}\subseteq\binom{\mathcal{F}}{A}

\displaystyle\chi(g_{i}h_{I})

\displaystyle<r\quad\text{if $i\in I$}

\displaystyle\chi(g_{i}h_{I})

\displaystyle>s\quad\text{if $i\not\in I$ and $i\leq\max I$}.

We need to borrow a few more definitions from
[

]
before stating Theorem 1.2 of the same paper, which will be our main tool to translate combinatorial Ramsey theoretic properties into dynamical ones.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a Fraïssé structure and
\mathcal{X}
a class of
\Aut(\mathcal{F})
-flows. A pointed

-flow

is dense in

\Aut(\mathcal{F})\curvearrowright(X,x)
is an
\mathcal{X}
-
\Aut(\mathcal{F})
-ambit
if
\Aut(\mathcal{F})\curvearrowright X\in\mathcal{X}
, that is if we obtain an element of
\mathcal{X}
after forgetting the distinguished point.

Definition 3.3
.

Given

and

we say that
\mathcal{F}
has the

(

if for all

such that every
\chi\in C
is constant (constant up to
\varepsilon
) on

. In particular when
\mathcal{A}
is the subalgebra of null (tame) functions we say that
\mathcal{F}
has the

.

Theorem 3.4

(
[

, Theorem 1.2]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a Fraïssé structure,
\mathcal{X}
be a class of
\Aut(\mathcal{F})
-flows such that the class of
\mathcal{X}

admits some

is a unital, left-invariant, closed subalgebra of

and the following are equivalent:

(1)

Every

(2)

For every

\mathcal{F}

(\mathcal{A})_{\varepsilon}
.

Those imply the following equivalent statements:

(3)

Every zero-dimensional
\Aut(\mathcal{F})
-flow in
\mathcal{X}
has a fixed point.

(4)

\mathcal{F}
has the Ramsey property for the finite colourings in
\mathcal{A}
.

When the finite colourings are dense in

Let us point out that in
[

]
, the elements of

is stated in terms of closed subalgebras. This is not an important difference, since the real valued version can be proved exactly as the complex valued one, modulo replacing the correspondence between

-compactifications of

and closed

-algebras of

We want to apply Theorem

when

.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a Fraïssé structure,

the algebra of null (tame) functions. The following are equivalent:

(1)

(2)

(3)

We can now verify that free joint embedding classes have the approximate null Ramsey property.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a Fraïssé structure,
G=\Aut(\mathcal{F})
,
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathrm{RUC}_{b}(G)
the algebra of null functions and
\varepsilon>0
. If
\mathrm{Age}(\mathcal{F})
has the free joint embedding property, then
\mathcal{F}
has the Ramsey property up to
2\varepsilon
for the finite colourings in
(\mathcal{A})_{\varepsilon}
.

Proof.

We prove the contrapositive. Let
A,B\in\mathrm{Age}(\mathcal{F})
and
\chi_{1},\ldots,\chi_{n}
be colourings of
\binom{\mathcal{F}}{A}
with
k
colours such that there is no
h\in\binom{\mathcal{F}}{B}
such that
h\circ\binom{B}{A}
is monochromatic up to
2\varepsilon
for all of the colourings
\chi_{i}
at the same time. We want to show that at least one of the colourings
\chi_{i}
is not in
(\mathcal{A})_{\varepsilon}
. Consider the structure
D=\bigsqcup_{i<\omega}B_{i}
, with
B_{i}\cong B
for all
i
, which can be embedded in
\mathcal{F}
as mentioned above. We fix an embedding
g\colon D\to\mathcal{F}
and, by restricting it to the various
B_{i}
’s, we obtain infinitely many embeddings of
B
into
\mathcal{F}
with pairwise disjoint images and with pairwise no relations between the images, formally let

H^{\prime\prime}=\left\{h^{\prime\prime}\in\binom{\mathcal{F}}{B}\,\middle|\,\exists i\in\omega\quad h^{\prime\prime}=g\upharpoonright B_{i}\right\}.

By assumption for every
h^{\prime\prime}\in H^{\prime\prime}
there exists
1\leq j\leq n
such that
\chi_{j}
has oscillation
>2\varepsilon
on
h^{\prime\prime}\circ\binom{B}{A}
. Since there are finitely many colourings while
H^{\prime\prime}
is infinite, the pigeonhole principle gives
1\leq i\leq n
such that for infinitely many
h^{\prime\prime}\in H^{\prime\prime}
,
\chi_{i}
has oscillation
>2\varepsilon
on
h^{\prime\prime}\circ\binom{B}{A}
. To simplify the notation we write
\chi
for
\chi_{i}
in the following. Our goal is to show that
\chi
is not in
(\mathcal{A})_{\varepsilon}
by using the characterization in Proposition
2.8
. Let

H^{\prime}=\left\{h^{\prime\prime}\in H^{\prime\prime}\,\middle|\,\chi\text{ is not constant up to $2\varepsilon$ on }h^{\prime\prime}\circ\binom{B}{A}\right\}.

Since
\chi
has finite image while
H^{\prime}
is infinite, another application of the pigeonhole principle yields
k_{0}<k_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
such that for infinitely many
h^{\prime}\in H^{\prime}
, both
k_{0}
and
k_{1}
are in
\chi\left(h^{\prime}\circ\binom{B}{A}\right)
and
k_{1}-k_{0}>2\varepsilon
. Let

H=\left\{h^{\prime}\in H^{\prime}\,\middle|\,k_{0},k_{1}\in\chi\left(h^{\prime}\circ\binom{B}{A}\right)\right\}.

Now fixed
n\in\omega
, we can find in
H
pairwise distinct embeddings
h_{I}\colon B\to\mathcal{F}
for all
I\subseteq n
, since
H
is infinite. Note that, by construction,
h_{I}(B)\cap h_{I^{\prime}}(B)=\varnothing
for all
I\neq I^{\prime}\subseteq n
, and that the substructure of
\mathcal{F}
with domain
h_{I}(B)\cup h_{I^{\prime}}(B)
is isomorphic to
B\sqcup B
.

By assumption for every
I\subseteq n
we can now find a pair of embeddings
h_{I}^{0},h_{I}^{1}\colon A\to B
such that
\chi(h_{I}\circ h_{I}^{i})=k_{i}
for
i\in\{0,1\}
.
For
i<n
we define a partial isomorphism
\widetilde{g}_{i}
of
F
with domain
\bigcup_{I\subseteq n}h_{I}h^{1}_{I}(A)
as follows. For every
I\subseteq n
,
\widetilde{g}_{i}\circ h_{I}\circ h^{1}_{I}=h_{I}\circ h^{0}_{I}
if
i\in I
and
\widetilde{g}_{i}\circ h_{I}\circ h^{1}_{I}=h_{I}\circ h^{1}_{I}
if
i\not\in I
. That is, for
I\subseteq n
and
x\in h_{I}h^{1}_{I}(A)
, we have

\widetilde{g}_{i}(x)=\begin{cases}h_{I}h_{I}^{0}(h_{I}h_{I}^{1})^{-1}(x)&\text{ if $i\in I$}\\
x&\text{ if $i\not\in I$.}\end{cases}

Note that there is indeed such a partial isomorphism of
F
: for every
I\subseteq n
,
\widetilde{g_{i}}
is either mapping
h_{I}\circ h^{1}_{I}(A)
to
h_{I}\circ h^{0}_{I}(A)
, if
i\in I
, or is the identity on
h_{I}\circ h^{1}_{I}(A)
, if instead
i\not\in I
. Since the embeddings where chosen so that the substructure of
\mathcal{F}
with underlying set
h_{I}(B)\cup h_{I^{\prime}}(B)
is isomorphic to
B\sqcup B
for all
I\neq I^{\prime}\subseteq n
, we can choose how
\widetilde{g}_{i}
behaves on
I
independently from the choices made for
I^{\prime}\neq I
.
Finally let, for
i<n
,
g_{i}\colon\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{F}
be any automorphism of
\mathcal{F}
extending the partial isomorphism
\widetilde{g_{i}}
defined above. Suppose now that
f\in\mathrm{RUC}_{b}(G)
is a function with
\|f-\chi\|_{\infty}<\varepsilon
. We want to show that
f
cannot be null, which is equivalent to saying that
\chi\not\in(\mathcal{A})_{\varepsilon}
. Given
n\in\omega
let
x_{I}
for
I\subseteq n
, and
g_{i}
for
i<n
be as just constructed, and take

such that
k_{0}<r<s<k_{1}
and
|k_{0}-r|>\varepsilon
,
|k_{1}-s|>\varepsilon
. We claim that those choices of
x_{I}
,
g_{i}
,

and

witness that

is not null by the characterization in Proposition
2.8
. Indeed if

we have

\chi(g_{i}x_{I})=\chi(g_{i}\circ h_{I}\circ h_{I}^{1})=\chi(h_{I}\circ h_{I}^{0})=k_{0},

which implies that

, since
|f(x_{i}g_{I})-\chi(g_{i}x_{I})|<\varepsilon
. If instead

we have

\chi(g_{i}x_{I})=\chi(g_{i}\circ h_{I}\circ h_{I}^{1})=\chi(h_{I}\circ h_{I}^{1})=k_{1},

which implies that
f(g_{i}x_{I})>s
, since
|f(g_{i}x_{I})-\chi(g_{i}x_{I})|<\varepsilon
, showing that

is not null by the characterization in Proposition

. In other words
\chi\not\in(\mathcal{A})_{\varepsilon}
, concluding the proof.

and

in Corollary

.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a Fraïssé class such that

-flow has a fixed point. Equivalently

3.2.
Tameness, fixed points, the Henson property and the Hrushovski property

By the previous section we already know that the Fraïssé classes of finite graphs and finite sets have the null Ramsey property, since they are free joint embedding classes. In this section we show that in fact they have the tame Ramsey property.

Definition 3.8
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a Fraïssé class. We say that
\mathcal{F}
has the
Henson property
if for every countable
A\subseteq\mathcal{F}
, there is
h\in\binom{\mathcal{F}}{A}
such that if
g
is any automorphism of
h(A)
,
g
extends to an automorphism of
\mathcal{F}
.

Before proceeding with the main results of this section we go on an historical detour concerning the Henson property.

Remark 3.9
.

We could not find whether the Henson property has been named before in the literature, so we adopted this name, since it was first shown to hold for the random graph and the
K_{n}
-free random graphs (also called the Henson graphs) by Henson
[
Hen71
, Theorem 3.1]
. A natural strengthening, which we will call the
strong Henson property
would be to require that every automorphism of
h(A)
extends uniquely to an automorphism of
\mathcal{F}
(indeed in
[
Hen71
]
the strong Henson property is established for the graphs mentioned above). The strong Henson property has been established for all free amalgamation classes in the PhD thesis
[
Bil12
]
and later expanded to the wider class of Fraïssé classes with a stationary independence relation in
[
Mül15
]
. There exists in the literature a similar but distinct notion of
universal Fraïssé structure
\mathcal{F}
, that is a Fraïssé structure such that for every
A\subseteq\mathcal{F}
,
\Aut(F)
contains a copy of
\Aut(A)
. For examples all Fraïssé structures that admit Katětov functors are universal
[
KM17
]
.

Definition 3.10
.

Let
F
be a Fraïssé structure. We say that
\mathcal{F}
has the
Hrushovski property
or that
\mathrm{Age}(\mathcal{F})
is an
EPPA class
(where EPPA stands for the
extension property for partial automorphisms
) if for every
A\in\mathrm{Age}(\mathcal{F})
there exists a
B\in\mathrm{Age}(\mathcal{F})
and an embedding
h\colon A\to B
such that for every partial isomorphism
p\colon A_{1}\to A_{2}
of
h(A)
,
p
extends to an automorphism of
B
. We call the embedding
h\colon A\to B
an EPPA witness for
A
.

We are now ready to prove the tame Ramsey property for the appropriate class of Fraïssé structures, from which we will then deduce dynamical consequences for their automorphism groups.

Theorem 3.11
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a Fraïssé structure,
G=\Aut(\mathcal{F})
,
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathrm{RUC}_{b}(G)
the algebra of tame functions and
\varepsilon>0
. If
\mathcal{F}
has the Henson and the Hrushovski properties and
\mathrm{Age}(\mathcal{F})
has the free joint embedding property, then
\mathcal{F}
has the Ramsey property up to
2\varepsilon
for the finite colourings in
(\mathcal{A})_{\varepsilon}
.

Proof.

We prove the contrapositive. Let
A,B\in\mathrm{Age}(\mathcal{F})
and
\chi_{1},\ldots,\chi_{n}
be colourings of
\binom{\mathcal{F}}{A}
with
k
colours such that no
h\in\binom{\mathcal{F}}{B}
is monochromatic up to
2\varepsilon
for all of the colourings
\chi_{i}
at the same time. We want to show that at least one of the colourings
\chi_{i}
is not in
(\mathcal{A})_{\varepsilon}
. Let
f\colon B\to C
be an EPPA witness for
B
and note that, since
\binom{C}{B}
is nonempty, there is no
h\in\binom{\mathcal{F}}{C}
which is monochromatic for all the colourings
\chi_{i}
at the same time. Take countably many copies
B_{i}\cong B
,
C_{i}\cong C
and embeddings
f_{i}\colon B_{i}\to C_{i}
, for
i<\omega
, such that, for every
i<\omega
,
f_{i}\colon B_{i}\to C_{i}
is an EPPA witness for
B_{i}
. Let
D=\bigsqcup_{i<\omega}C_{i}
and note that, since
\mathrm{Age}(\mathcal{F})
has the free joint embedding property,
D
is a countable structure embeddable in
\mathcal{F}
. Since
\mathcal{F}
has the Henson property we can find an embedding
g\colon D\to F
such that for all
h\in\Aut(g(D))
,
h
extends to an automorphism of
\mathcal{F}
. The embedding
g
also gives infinitely many embeddings of
B
in
\mathcal{F}
with pairwise disjoint images and pairwise no relations between the images. Explicitly let

H^{\prime}=\left\{h^{\prime}\in\binom{\mathcal{F}}{B}\,\middle|\,\exists i\in\omega\quad h^{\prime}=g\upharpoonright f_{i}(B_{i})\right\}.

As in the proof of Theorem
3.6
, by applying the pigeonhole principle twice, we can find
i\leq n
and
k_{0}<k_{1}
with
k_{1}-k_{0}>2\varepsilon
such that for
\chi=\chi_{i}
the set

H=\left\{h^{\prime}\in H^{\prime}\,\middle|\,k_{0},k_{1}\in\chi\left(h^{\prime}\circ\binom{B}{A}\right)\right\}

is infinite, so that we can find, for
I\subseteq_{\mathrm{fin}}\omega
, pairwise distinct embeddings
h_{I}\colon B\to\mathcal{F}
in
H
. Note that, by construction, every
h_{I}
can be extended to an embedding
\widetilde{h_{I}}\colon C\to\mathcal{F}
, such that the inclusion of
h_{I}(B)
in
\widetilde{h_{I}}(C)
is an EPPA witness for
h_{I}(B)
. Moreover we can find, for all
I\subseteq_{\mathrm{fin}}\omega
, embeddings
h_{I}^{0},h_{I}^{1}\colon A\to B
such that
\chi(h_{I}\circ h_{I}^{i})=k_{i}
,
i\in\{0,1\}
.

As in the proof of Theorem
3.6
, for every
I\subseteq_{\mathrm{fin}}\omega
, we can find a partial automorphism
g_{I}
of
h_{I}(B)
, defined on
h_{I}(A)
such that
g_{I}\circ h_{I}\circ h_{I}^{1}=h_{I}\circ h_{I}^{0}
. Since
\widetilde{h_{I}}(C)
is an EPPA witness for
h_{I}(B)
,
g_{I}
extends to an element of
\Aut(\widetilde{h_{I}}(C))
, which we also call
g_{I}
. Consider now, for
i<\omega
, the automorphism
\widetilde{g_{i}}\colon g(D)\to g(D)
given by
\widetilde{g_{i}}=\bigsqcup_{I}g_{I}^{\prime}
, where
g_{I}^{\prime}\colon\widetilde{h_{I}}(C)\to\widetilde{h_{I}}(C)
is
g_{I}
if
i\in I
and is the identity of
\widetilde{h_{I}}(C)
if
i\not\in I
. Note that, by construction,
\widetilde{g_{i}}\in\Aut(g(D))
, as there are no relations between the copies of
C
in
D
. Finally, since
g
was chosen to be an embedding as given by the Henson property, let
g_{i}
extend
\widetilde{g_{i}}
to
F
. Define
x_{I}=h_{I}\circ h_{I}^{1}
and suppose
f\in\mathrm{RUC}_{b}(G)
is such that
\|f-\chi\|_{\infty}<\varepsilon
. We want to show that
f
is not tame by the characterization established in Proposition
2.14
. Take
r,s
with
k_{0}<r<s<k_{1}
,
|k_{0}-r|>\varepsilon
and
|k_{1}-s|>\varepsilon
. We claim that
r,s,x_{I}
and
g_{i}
as just constructed witness that
f
is not tame. Indeed if
i\in I
we have

\chi(g_{i}x_{I})=\chi(g_{i}\circ h_{I}\circ h_{I}^{1})=\chi(g_{I}\circ h_{I}\circ h_{I}^{1})=\chi(h_{I}\circ h_{I}^{0})=k_{0},

which implies that
f(g_{i}x_{I})<r
, since
|f(g_{i}x_{I})-\chi(g_{i}x_{I})|<\varepsilon
. If instead
i\not\in I
we have

\chi(g_{i}x_{I})=\chi(g_{i}\circ h_{I}\circ h_{I}^{1})=\chi(\mathrm{Id}_{\widetilde{h_{I}}(C)}\circ h_{I}\circ h_{I}^{1})=\chi(h_{I}\circ h_{I}^{1})=k_{1},

which implies that
f(x_{i}g_{I})>s
, since
|f(g_{i}x_{I})-\chi(g_{i}x_{I})|<\varepsilon
, showing that
f
is not tame by the characterization in Proposition
2.14
.

∎

The following is now an immediate consequence of the equivalence between
(1)
and
(2)
in Corollary
3.5
.

Theorem 3.12
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a Fraïssé structure such that
F
has the Henson property and the Hrushovski property and such that
\mathrm{Age}(\mathcal{F})
has the free joint embedding property. Then every tame
\Aut(\mathcal{F})
-flow has a fixed point. Equivalently
M_{t}(\Aut(\mathcal{F}))
is a singleton.

As mentioned in Remark
3.9
all free amalgamation classes have the Henson property. By a result of Siniora and Solecki
[
SS20
, Corollary 4.6]
free amalgamation classes also have the Hrushovski property. Together with the easy observation that, for the age of a Fraïssé structure in a finite relational language, the free amalgamation property implies the free joint embedding property we immediately obtain the following corollary from Theorem
3.12
.

Corollary 3.13
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a Fraïssé structure such that
\mathrm{Age}(\mathcal{F})
has the free amalgamation property. Then every tame
\Aut(\mathcal{F})
-flow has a fixed point. Equivalently
M_{t}(\Aut(\mathcal{F}))
is trivial.

Spatially resolved collective modes in d d -wave superconductors
Kazi Ranjibul Islam, Samuel Awelewa, Andrey V. Chubukov, Maxim Dzero

Abstract
We analyze the dispersion of collective modes in a superconductor with d − d- wave symmetry of the order parameter in the presence of long-range Coulomb interaction. We use diagrammatic technique and quasiclassical theory in Keldysh-Nambu formalism to compute longitudinal and transverse pair susceptibilities and extract from them the dispersion of the longitudinal and transverse collective mode. We show that at T = 0 T=0 , the dispersion of the transverse (plasma) mode is the same as in an s s -wave superconductor, but at a finite T T it is softer and has a much larger decay rate due to the partial screening of the Coulomb potential by nodal quasiparticles. We show that the dispersion of the longitudinal mode depends on the direction of momentum with respect to the positions of the nodes of the d − d- wave gap, while the decay rate of this mode does not depend on momentum. We discuss experimental implications of our results.

II
Preliminaries

In this section, we introduce the model and
discuss the
two

-wave superconductor.

II.1
Model

We consider a one-band model of 2D
fermions with
d-
wave attractive interaction in the
Cooper
channel:

\displaystyle\hat{\cal H}=\sum_{\mathbf{k},\sigma}\xi_{\mathbf{k}}\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k},\sigma}\hat{c}_{\mathbf{k},\sigma}+\sum_{\mathbf{k},\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q}}V_{\mathrm{d}}(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{p})\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}/2,\uparrow}\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{-\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}/2,\downarrow}\hat{c}_{-\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{q}/2,\downarrow}\hat{c}_{\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{q}/2,\uparrow},

(1)

where

\hat{c}^{\dagger}
(
\hat{c}
) are the creation (annihilation) fermionic operators,

V_{\mathrm{d}}
is the pairing interaction, and

\xi_{\mathbf{k}}=\epsilon_{k}-\varepsilon_{F}
, where
\epsilon_{k}
is
a single particle dispersion,
which, for simplicity, we set to be parabolic, and

\varepsilon_{F}
is the Fermi energy.
We project
V_{\mathrm{d}}(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{p})
into the
d-
wave channel and approximate it as

\displaystyle V_{\mathrm{d}}(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{p})=-g\gamma(\theta_{\mathbf{k}})\,\gamma(\theta_{\mathbf{p}}),

(2)

where

g>0
is the coupling constant,
\gamma(\theta_{\mathbf{k}})=\sqrt{2}\cos 2\theta_{\mathbf{k}}
is the normalized
d
-wave form factor and
\theta_{\mathbf{k}}
defines the direction of the momentum on the Fermi surface
with respect to
\hat{x}
:
\mathbf{k}_{F}=k_{F}(\cos\theta_{\mathbf{k}},\sin\theta_{\mathbf{k}})
.

In addition
to
V_{\mathrm{d}}
,
we include
the long-range Coulomb interaction between fermions

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{C}=\dfrac{1}{2}\sum_{\mathbf{q}}V_{\mathbf{q}}\rho_{\mathbf{q}}\rho_{-\mathbf{q}},

(3)

where
\rho_{\mathbf{q}}=\sum_{\mathbf{k},\sigma}c^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q},\sigma}c_{\mathbf{k},\sigma}
is the particle density operator and
V_{\mathbf{q}}
is the Coulomb potential
V_{\mathbf{q}}=\pi e^{2}(2/|\mathbf{q}|)

(
V_{\mathbf{q}}=\pi e^{2}(2/|\mathbf{q}|)^{2}
in 3D). The total Hamiltonian is
\hat{H}=\hat{H}_{C}+\hat{\cal H}
.

At
T=0
, Eq. (
1
) describes a superconductor with a
d-
wave pairing gap

\Delta_{\mathbf{k}}=\Delta\,\gamma(\theta_{\mathbf{k}})
. The gap magnitude is obtained
by solving the non-linear
BCS-like
gap equation at
T=0
:

\displaystyle 1=\frac{g\nu_{F}}{2}\int_{-\Lambda}^{\Lambda}d\xi_{\mathbf{k}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\dfrac{d\theta_{\mathbf{k}}}{2\pi}\dfrac{\gamma^{2}(\theta_{\mathbf{k}})}{\sqrt{\xi^{2}_{\mathbf{k}}+\Delta_{\mathbf{k}}^{2}}}

(4)

where

\nu_{F}=m/(2\pi)
is the density of states at the Fermi level and
\Lambda
is an ultraviolet cutoff,
which we introduce symmetrically with respect to
\xi_{\mathbf{k}}
.

Theoretical methods

We will use two independent approaches

-wave superconductor.
One approach is based on deriving the expression for the pair susceptibility by solving the quasiclassical equation for the single-particle propagator

;
Larkin and Ovchinnikov 1977
;
Eilenberger 1968
;
Usadel 1970

another is based on direct diagrammatic calculation of a pair susceptibility

;
Kulik et al. 1981
;
Silaev 2019
;
Phan and Chubukov 2023
;
Nosov et al. 2025

II.2.1
Quasiclassical theory.

The
quasiclassical equation for the single-particle propagator
\check{g}
is
Larkin 1965
;
Larkin and Ovchinnikov 1977
;
Eilenberger 1968
;
Usadel 1970

[\epsilon\check{\tau}_{3}-\check{\Delta}_{\mathbf{n}}(\mathbf{r},t)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{,}}\check{g}_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon}]+\frac{i}{2}\left\{\check{\tau}_{3},\partial_{t}\check{g}_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon}\right\}+i{v}_{F}(\mathbf{n}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$\nabla$}_{\mathbf{r}})\check{g}_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon}=0.

(5)

Here
\check{\tau}_{3}=\hat{\tau}_{3}\otimes\hat{\sigma}_{0}
is a
4\times 4
matrix defined in Keldysh and Nambu spaces
(note that
\check{\tau}_{3}
is diagonal in Keldysh space) and
\mathbf{n}=\mathbf{k}/k
. Quasiclassical propagator

\check{g}
is an 4
\times
4 matrix in Keldysh and Nambu spaces

\check{g}=\left[\begin{matrix}\hat{g}^{R}&\hat{g}^{K}\\
0&\hat{g}^{A}\end{matrix}\right]

(6)

and it must satisfy the normalization condition

\check{g}\circ\check{g}=\check{{\mathbbm{1}}},

(7)

where symbol
\circ
denotes the usual convolution

(F\circ G)(x,x^{\prime})=\int F(x,y)G(y,x^{\prime})dy,

(8)

and we use

. Upon the Wigner transformation (
8

F\circ G=\hat{F}_{\mathbf{p}\epsilon}(\mathbf{r},t)e^{\frac{i}{2}\left(\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\leftarrow}}{{\partial}}_{\mathbf{r}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rightarrow}}{{\partial}}_{\mathbf{p}}-\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\leftarrow}}{{\partial}}_{t}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rightarrow}}{{\partial}}_{\epsilon}-\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\leftarrow}}{{\partial}}_{\mathbf{p}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rightarrow}}{{\partial}}_{\mathbf{r}}+\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\leftarrow}}{{\partial}}_{\epsilon}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rightarrow}}{{\partial}}_{t}\right)}\hat{G}_{\mathbf{p}\epsilon}(\mathbf{r},t).

(9)

Here
\mathbf{r}
and

refer to the ’center-of-mass’ coordinate
X=(x+x^{\prime})/2
and the Fourier transform has been performed with respect to the relative coordinates

.

In the ground state, we choose the order parameter to be particle-hole symmetric,
\hat{\Delta}_{\mathbf{n}}(\mathbf{r},t)=i\hat{\tau}_{2}\Delta_{\mathbf{n}}
. It is
then straightforward to show that the retarded and advanced blocks of the quasiclassical function (
6

\hat{g}_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon}^{R(A)}=\frac{\epsilon}{\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon}^{R(A)}}\hat{\tau}_{3}-\frac{\Delta_{\mathbf{n}}}{\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon}^{R(A)}}i\hat{\tau}_{2},

(10)

where

are the Pauli matrices which act in Nambu space and

\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon}^{R(A)}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\pm\mathrm{sign}(\epsilon)\sqrt{(\epsilon\pm i\delta)^{2}-\Delta_{\mathbf{n}}^{2}},\quad|\epsilon|\geq|\Delta_{\mathbf{n}}|,\\
&i\sqrt{\Delta_{\mathbf{n}}^{2}-\epsilon^{2}},\quad|\epsilon|<|\Delta_{\mathbf{n}}|.\end{aligned}\right.

(11)

Given the normalization condition (
7

\hat{g}_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon}^{K}=\left(\hat{g}_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon}^{R}-\hat{g}_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon}^{A}\right)\tanh\left({\epsilon}/{2T}\right)
(here

{\Delta}=\frac{g\nu_{F}}{4}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\gamma(\theta_{\mathbf{n}})\frac{d\theta_{\mathbf{n}}}{2\pi}\int\limits_{-\Lambda}^{\Lambda}d\epsilon\left(f_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon}^{R}-f_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon}^{A}\right)t_{\epsilon},

(12)

which is of course equivalent to (
4

We present the explicit expressions for the transverse and longitudinal parts of the pair susceptibility,
obtained
using this
method,
in Secs.
III
and

IV

.
We analyze the transverse and longitudinal susceptibilities
separately in Secs.
III
and

IV
, respectively.

III
Transverse susceptibility and dispersion of the transverse mode

III.1
Quasiclassical approach

For the case when an external electromagnetic field is applied, the main idea behind the calculation of the pair susceptibility consists in evaluating perturbative corrections to
\check{g}
up to the second order in powers of the vector potential
Moor et al. 2017
;
Li and Dzero 2025
;
Derendorf et al. 2024
;
Awelewa and Dzero 2025
. The second-order correction is required due to the fact that the order parameter is a scalar quantity and therefore the changes to the pairing amplitude may only appear at the order
O({\mathbf{A}}^{2})
unless the supercurrent is flowing in a superconductor
Moor et al. 2017
. Thus, generally one needs to be looking for the perturbative solution of the equation (
5
) in the form

\check{g}(\mathbf{n}\epsilon;\mathbf{r}t)=\check{g}_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon}+\check{g}_{1}(\mathbf{n}\epsilon;\mathbf{r}t)+\check{g}_{2}(\mathbf{n}\epsilon;\mathbf{r}t)
. From the normalization condition (
7
) it follows that functions
\check{g}_{1}
and
\check{g}_{2}
must satisfy

\check{g}_{0}\circ\check{g}_{1}+\check{g}_{1}\circ\check{g}_{0}=0,\quad\check{g}_{0}\circ\check{g}_{2}+\check{g}_{2}\circ\check{g}_{0}+\check{g}_{1}\circ\check{g}_{1}=0.

(27)

With this comment in mind, let us assume that the system has been subjected to an external perturbation which in turn produced a change in the order parameter corresponding to the fluctuation in the transverse (phase)
d
-wave channel. We will describe this by a function

\delta\hat{\Delta}_{\mathbf{n}}(\mathbf{r},t)=\gamma(\theta_{\mathbf{n}})\delta\hat{\Delta}_{\mathbf{k}\omega}^{T}e^{2i(\mathbf{k}\mathbf{r}-\omega t)},

(28)

where
\delta\hat{\Delta}_{\mathbf{k}\omega}^{T}=(i\hat{\tau}_{1})\delta\Delta_{\mathbf{k}\omega}^{T}
and the first Pauli matrix ensures that the resulting corrections to
\check{g}
do indeed describe the excitation of the phase mode. For instance, the normal part of
\check{g}
will be proportional to
\hat{\tau}_{0}
, which will allow us to compute the change in the particle density due to the phase fluctuations of the order parameter in the transverse channel. Indeed,
as it is well known an excitation of the phase mode necessarily produces redistribution of electronic charge
Carlson and Goldman 1973
;
Artemenko and Volkov 1975
;
Schmid and Schön 1975
;
Artemenko and Volkov 1979
;
Schmid and Schön 1979
;
Kulik et al. 1981
;
Kamenev 2011
. This process is accounted for by the corresponding variation of the Coulomb potential which is described by a function

\delta\Phi(\mathbf{r},t)=\delta\Phi_{\mathbf{k}\omega}e^{2i(\mathbf{k}\mathbf{r}-\omega t)}.

(29)

With these provisions, using quasiclassical equation (
5
) it can be shown that the second-order corrections for each element of the matrix
\check{g}
can be found by solving the following equation

\displaystyle\left[\epsilon\hat{\tau}_{3}-\hat{\Delta}_{\mathbf{n}},\hat{g}_{2}\right]-2{v}_{F}(\mathbf{n}.\mathbf{k})\hat{\tau}_{0}\hat{g}_{2}+\omega\left\{\hat{\tau}_{3},\hat{g}_{2}\right\}

\displaystyle=\delta\hat{\Delta}_{\mathbf{n}}^{T}\hat{g}_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon-\omega}-\hat{g}_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon+\omega}\delta\hat{\Delta}_{\mathbf{n}}^{T}

(30)

\displaystyle+\delta\Phi_{\mathbf{k}\omega}\hat{g}_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon-\omega}-\hat{g}_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon+\omega}\delta\Phi_{\mathbf{k}\omega}.

Variation of the order parameter is determined self-consistently via

\delta\Delta_{\mathbf{k}\omega}^{T}=\frac{g\nu_{F}}{4}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\gamma(\theta_{\mathbf{n}})\frac{d\theta_{\mathbf{n}}}{2\pi}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}{d\epsilon}\textrm{Tr}\left\{i\hat{\tau}_{1}\hat{g}_{2}^{K}(\mathbf{n}\epsilon;\mathbf{k}\omega)\right\},

(31)

while the variation of the scalar potential is given by the solution of the Poisson equation:

\left(-\frac{k^{d-1}}{2^{d-1}\pi e^{2}}\right)\delta\Phi_{\mathbf{k}\omega}=\nu_{F}\delta\Phi_{\mathbf{k}\omega}+\frac{\pi\nu_{F}}{4}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\phi_{\mathbf{n}}}{2\pi}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}{d\epsilon}\textrm{Tr}\left\{\hat{\tau}_{0}\hat{g}_{2}^{K}(\mathbf{n}\epsilon;\mathbf{k}\omega)\right\}

(32)

and
d=2,3
refers to the spatial dimensionality of the problem.
Here, the first term on the right-hand side accounts for the finite polarizability of the electronic band
Kamenev and Levchenko 2009
;
Kamenev 2011
.

D=2
.

We start with the two-dimensional superconductor. After we determine the second order corrections to the propagator
\check{g}
and insert the resulting expressions into the self-consistency condition (
31
) and Poisson equation (
32
), we find the following system of equations (see Appendix
B
for details):

\displaystyle\chi_{\textrm{AB}}^{-1}(\mathbf{q},\Omega)\cdot\delta\Delta_{\mathbf{q}\Omega}^{T}+i\rho_{\mathbf{q}\Omega}\cdot\delta\Phi_{\mathbf{q}\Omega}=0,\quad\chi_{\textrm{CG}}^{-1}(\mathbf{q},\Omega)\cdot\delta\Phi_{\mathbf{q}\Omega}-i\rho_{\mathbf{q}\Omega}\cdot\delta\Delta_{\mathbf{q}\Omega}^{T}=0.

(33)

Here
\chi_{\textrm{AB}}^{-1}(\mathbf{q},\Omega)
is defined as

\begin{split}&\chi_{\textrm{AB}}^{-1}(\mathbf{q},\Omega)=-\frac{2}{g\nu_{F}}+\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}{\gamma}^{2}(\theta_{\mathbf{n}})\frac{d\theta_{\mathbf{n}}}{2\pi}\int\limits_{-\omega_{D}}^{\omega_{D}}d\epsilon\\
&\times\left\{\frac{\left(\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon+\Omega}^{R}+\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon-\Omega}^{A}\right){A}_{\mathbf{n}}^{K}(\epsilon_{+},\epsilon_{-})(t_{\epsilon+\Omega}-t_{\epsilon-\Omega})}{\left(\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon+\Omega}^{R}+\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon-\Omega}^{A}\right)^{2}-{v}_{F}^{2}(\mathbf{n}.\mathbf{q})^{2}}\right.\\
&\left.+\frac{\left(\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon+\Omega}^{R}+\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon-\Omega}^{R}\right){A}_{\mathbf{n}}^{R}(\epsilon_{+},\epsilon_{-})t_{\epsilon-\Omega}}{\left(\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon+\Omega}^{R}+\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon-\Omega}^{R}\right)^{2}-{v}_{F}^{2}(\mathbf{n}.\mathbf{q})^{2}}-\frac{\left(\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon+\Omega}^{A}+\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon-\Omega}^{A}\right){A}_{\mathbf{n}}^{A}(\epsilon_{+},\epsilon_{-})t_{\epsilon+\Omega}}{\left(\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon+\Omega}^{A}+\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon-\Omega}^{A}\right)^{2}-{v}_{F}^{2}(\mathbf{n}.\mathbf{q})^{2}}\right\}.\end{split}

(34)

where

\displaystyle{A}_{\mathbf{n}}^{R(A)}(\epsilon_{+},\epsilon_{-})=g_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon+\Omega}^{R(A)}g_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon-\Omega}^{R(A)}-f_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon+\Omega}^{R(A)}f_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon-\Omega}^{R(A)}+1,

(35)

\displaystyle{A}_{\mathbf{n}}^{K}(\epsilon_{+},\epsilon_{-})=g_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon+\Omega}^{R}g_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon-\Omega}^{A}-f_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon+\Omega}^{R}f_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon-\Omega}^{A}+1.

The definitions of the functions
\rho_{\mathbf{q}\Omega}
and
\chi_{\textrm{CG}}^{-1}(\mathbf{q},\Omega)
can be found in Appendix
B
.
Note that in a charge neutral superconductor
\delta\Phi_{\mathbf{q}\Omega}
must be zero, so the function
\chi_{\textrm{AB}}^{-1}(\mathbf{q},\Omega)
, Eqs. (
33
,
34
), not only defines the transverse pair susceptibility, but its frequency and momentum dependence determine the dispersion of the transverse collective mode in this case.

For this reason it is instructive to consider the limit
\Omega\to 0
of the function
\chi_{\textrm{AB}}^{-1}(\mathbf{q},\Omega)
first. In this limit, the first term under the integral vanishes identically, while
{A}_{\mathbf{n}}^{R(A)}(\epsilon,\epsilon)=2
by virtue of the normalization condition. Given that the self-consistency condition (
12
) holds, it is easy to see that equation
\chi_{\textrm{AB}}^{-1}(\mathbf{q},\Omega\to 0)\cdot\delta\Delta_{\mathbf{q}\Omega}^{T}=0
is satisfied identically for
\mathbf{q}\to 0
.

Left panel: real part of transverse pair susceptibility
\chi_{\textrm{AB}}(\mathbf{q},\omega)
for
d
-wave superconductor plotted as a function of frequency and at a fixed value of momentum
v_{F}q=0.125\Delta
for different values of temperatures. All energies are given in the units of the pairing amplitude at zero temperature. Function
\chi_{\textrm{AB}}(\mathbf{q},\omega)
exhibits a peak at
\omega=\Omega_{\mathrm{peak}}
which corresponds to the energy of the collective transverse mode. Right panel: the dependence of
\Omega_{\mathrm{peak}}
on temperature.

In order to determine the dispersion relation, we expand the function under the integral (
34
) up to the second order in powers of
\mathbf{q}
. Using the auxiliary expressions listed in Appendix
C
and Appendix
D
, we can cast an expression for the function

\chi_{\textrm{AB}}^{-1}(\mathbf{q},\Omega)
into the following form

\chi_{\textrm{AB}}^{-1}(\mathbf{q},\Omega)\approx\Omega^{2}-\zeta(\Omega,T)v_{F}^{2}q^{2}.

(36)

At small frequencies
\Omega\sim\Delta
function
\zeta(\Omega,T)
weakly varies with
\Omega
and for this reason it is well approximated by

{\zeta}(T)\approx\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}(\mathbf{n}.\hat{\mathbf{q}})^{2}\left\{1-\frac{|\Delta_{\mathbf{n}}|}{2T}\int\limits_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\cosh^{-2}(y|\Delta_{\mathbf{n}}|/2T)dy}{y^{3}\sqrt{y^{2}-1}}\right\}\frac{d\theta_{\mathbf{n}}}{2\pi},

(37)

where
\hat{\mathbf{q}}=\mathbf{q}/q
.
At
T=0
,
{\zeta}(T)=1/2
. Substituting into (
36
), we find that
in a charge-neutral system
the collective mode is massless with the dispersion
\Omega=v_{F}q/\sqrt{2}
at the smallest
q
and
\Omega

(
v_{F}q,\Omega\ll\Delta
).
This dispersion is the same as the one in an

s
-
wave superfluid.

This equivalence, however, does not extend to finite
T
. In an
s-
superfluid,
the second term in (
37
) is exponentially small
at
T\ll\Delta
.
This is not so for a
d
-wave gap function as there is always a range of
\theta_{\mathbf{n}}
in (
37
) where the argument of the hyperbolic cosine function is of order one.
Limiting the angular integration to this region, we find

{\zeta}(T\ll\Delta)\approx\frac{1}{2}-\left(\frac{3\log 2}{8\sqrt{2}}\right)\frac{T}{\Delta}-O(T^{2})

(38)

(see Appendix
D
for details).
This result implies that at a finite
T
, a transverse collective mode in a
d-
wave superfluid propagates with a smaller velocity than in an
s-
wave superfluid due to thermal velocity reduction by nodal quasiparticles.

In Fig.
3
(left panel), we present the dependence of the real part of the function
\chi_{\textrm{AB}}(\mathbf{q},\Omega)
on frequency for a fixed value of momentum. Clearly, the peak in
\mathrm{Re}[\chi_{\textrm{AB}}(\mathbf{q},\Omega)]
accounts for the excitation of the transverse mode in a charge-neutral
d
-wave superfluid. The frequency at which
\mathrm{Re}[\chi_{\textrm{AB}}(\mathbf{q},\Omega)]
matches the value predicted using the approximate expression (
37
). We also note that the peak is red shifted with increasing temperature, Fig.
3
(right panel), in agreement with our discussion above.

Let us now turn our attention to the case of charged superconductors. With the help of the auxiliary expressions listed in Appendix
C
for this case our system of equations (
33
) reads (
d=2
):

\displaystyle\left[\Omega^{2}-v_{F}^{2}q^{2}\zeta(T)\right]\cdot\delta\Delta_{\mathbf{q}\Omega}^{T}+\left({2i\Omega\Delta}\right)\cdot\delta\Phi_{\mathbf{q}\Omega}=0,

(39)

\displaystyle\left({2i\Omega}\Delta\right)\cdot\delta\Delta_{\mathbf{q}\Omega}^{T}-\Delta^{2}\left\{4+\frac{2q}{\pi\nu_{F}e^{2}}\right\}\cdot\delta\Phi_{\mathbf{q}\Omega}=0.

For a nontrivial solution to exist we require that the determinant of this system vanishes. This yields

\Omega^{(\mathrm{2D})}(\mathbf{q})=\sqrt{2\pi\nu_{F}e^{2}v_{F}^{2}\zeta(T)|\mathbf{q}|},

(40)

The frequency of the mode scales as
\sqrt{q}
, like in an s-wave superconductor. However, the temperature variation of the effective velocity of the mode is again different: using
(
38
),
We find that it decreases linearly with
T
rather than exponentially.
We emphasize that the
T
variation of
\Omega_{\mathrm{p}}^{(2\mathrm{D})}(q)
is described by the same function
\zeta(T)
as in the charge neutral case.

D=3
.

Let us now turn our attention to the case of the three-dimensional superconductor. For the charge neutral case, the dispersion of the transverse mode follows directly from (
37
) where additional angular averaging must be performed. Upon averaging over the directions of
\mathbf{n}
at
T=0
gives
\Omega^{({\mathrm{3D}})}=v_{F}q/\sqrt{3}
. At finite temperatures, we find that the magnitude of
\Omega^{({\mathrm{3D}})}
decreases with temperature just like in the 2D case.

In the charged case, the system of equations is very similar to (
39
) where we only need to change
2q\to q^{2}
in the last term in the second equation in (
39
). Simple algebra gives the following formula for the dispersion of the transverse mode:

\Omega_{\mathrm{p}}^{(3\mathrm{D})}(\mathbf{q})=\Omega_{\mathrm{p}}^{(3\mathrm{D})}(0)\sqrt{1+\frac{q^{2}}{4\pi\nu_{F}e^{2}}},

(41)

where
\Omega_{\mathrm{p}}^{(3\mathrm{D})}(0)
is the superconducting plasma frequency

\Omega_{\mathrm{p}}^{(3\mathrm{D})}(0)=\sqrt{4\pi\zeta(0)\nu_{F}e^{2}v_{F}^{2}}=\sqrt{4\pi ne^{2}/m}

(42)

where
\zeta(0)=1/3
in 3D, fermionic density
n=p^{3}_{F}/(3\pi^{2})
and
v_{F}=(3\pi^{2}n)^{1/3}\,m/\pi^{2}
.
At a finite
T
,
the
transverse mode in a
d-
wave superconductor is
again
located at a lower energy than in an
s-
wave superconductor. This result has a simple physical interpretation: at a finite
T
, nodal quasi-particles in a
d
-wave superconductor partially screen the Coulomb potential, leading to a smaller value of the superconducting plasma frequency.

III.2
Diagrammatic approach

We use Eq. (
24
) for the computation for the transverse pair-susceptibility,
\chi_{T}(q)
. After analytical continuation to real frequencies, it becomes

\displaystyle\chi_{T}(\mathbf{q},\Omega)=\dfrac{\bar{\Pi}_{T}(\mathbf{q},\Omega)}{1-g\,\bar{\Pi}_{T}(\mathbf{q},\Omega)},

(43)

where
\quad\bar{\Pi}_{T}(\mathbf{q},\Omega)=\Pi_{T}(\mathbf{q},\Omega)-8\,\dfrac{\Pi^{2}_{GF}(\mathbf{q},\Omega)}{V^{-1}_{\mathbf{q}}-2\,\Pi_{0}(\mathbf{q},\Omega)}

(see Eq. (
25
)). We first present the results at
T=0
and then at a finite
T
.

III.2.1
T=0

For the polarization bubbles
\Pi_{T},\Pi_{GF}
and
\Pi_{0}
at zero temperature, we obtain
after frequency integration and analytical continuation
(see Appendix
A
for details)

\displaystyle\Pi_{T}(\mathbf{q},\Omega)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\nu_{F}}{2}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\gamma^{2}(\theta_{\mathbf{k}})\dfrac{d\theta_{\mathbf{k}}}{2\pi}\int_{-\Lambda}^{\Lambda}d\xi_{\mathbf{k}}\dfrac{E_{+}+E_{-}}{E_{+}\,E_{-}}\quad\dfrac{E_{+}\,E_{-}+\xi_{+}\,\xi_{-}+\Delta^{2}_{\mathbf{k}}}{(E_{+}+E_{-})^{2}-\Omega^{2}},

(44)

\displaystyle\Pi_{GF}(\mathbf{q},\Omega)

\displaystyle=-\dfrac{\nu_{F}\,\Omega}{4}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\gamma(\theta_{\mathbf{k}})\dfrac{d\theta_{\mathbf{k}}}{2\pi}\int_{-\Lambda}^{\Lambda}d\xi_{\mathbf{k}}\dfrac{E_{+}+E_{-}}{E_{+}\,E_{-}}\quad\dfrac{\Delta_{\mathbf{k}}}{(E_{+}+E_{-})^{2}-\Omega^{2}},

(45)

\displaystyle\Pi_{0}(\mathbf{q},\Omega)

\displaystyle=-\dfrac{\nu_{F}}{2}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\dfrac{d\theta_{\mathbf{k}}}{2\pi}\int_{-\Lambda}^{\Lambda}d\xi_{\mathbf{k}}\dfrac{E_{+}+E_{-}}{E_{+}\,E_{-}}\quad\dfrac{E_{+}\,E_{-}-\xi_{+}\,\xi_{-}+\Delta^{2}_{\mathbf{k}}}{(E_{+}+E_{-})^{2}-\Omega^{2}},

(46)

where
\Omega
should be understood as
\Omega+i\delta
,
E_{\pm}=E_{\mathbf{k}\pm\mathbf{q}/2}
,
\xi_{\pm}=\xi_{\mathbf{k}\pm\mathbf{q}/2}
,
and, we remind,

\gamma(\theta_{\mathbf{k}})=\sqrt{2}\,\cos 2\theta_{\mathbf{k}}
and the gap function
\Delta_{\mathbf{k}}=\Delta\,\gamma(\theta_{\mathbf{k}})
.

We first consider a neutral superfluid and neglect
the Coulomb interaction in Eq. (
43
). In this case,
we expect that the pole of
\chi_{T}(\mathbf{q},\Omega)

yields a
Goldstone AB mode.
To find its dispersion, we split
\Pi_{T}(\mathbf{q},\Omega)
into
\Pi_{T}(\mathbf{q},\Omega)=\Pi_{T}(0)+\delta\Pi_{T}(\mathbf{q},\Omega)
and use the gap equation (
4
) in the form

1=g\,\Pi_{T}(0)
. After a straightforward algebra, we find

\displaystyle\chi_{T}(\mathbf{q},\Omega)=-\dfrac{1}{g^{2}\delta\Pi_{T}(\mathbf{q},\Omega)}-\dfrac{1}{g},

(47)

where

\displaystyle\delta\Pi_{T}(\mathbf{q},\Omega)=-

\displaystyle\dfrac{\nu_{F}}{4}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\gamma^{2}(\theta_{\mathbf{k}})\dfrac{d\theta_{\mathbf{k}}}{2\pi}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\xi_{\mathbf{k}}\left(\dfrac{E_{+}+E_{-}}{E_{+}\,E_{-}}\right)\dfrac{(\Omega+i\delta)^{2}-\left(\xi_{+}-\xi_{-}\right)^{2}}{(\Omega+i\delta)^{2}-\left(E_{+}+E_{-}\right)^{2}}.

(48)

The corresponding expression for an
s-
wave superfluid has been obtained in Ref.
Kulik et al. 1981
.
The dispersion of the AB mode is extracted from

\delta\Pi_{T}(\mathbf{q},\Omega)=0
.

For small
\mathbf{q}
(
v_{F}|\mathbf{q}|\ll\Delta
) we approximate

(\xi_{+}-\xi_{-})^{2}\approx v^{2}_{F}|\mathbf{q}|^{2}\cos^{2}(\theta_{\mathbf{k}}-\theta_{\mathbf{q}})
and

(E_{+}+E_{-})^{2}\approx 4\Delta_{\mathbf{k}}^{2}+4\xi^{2}_{\mathbf{k}}+v^{2}_{F}|\mathbf{q}|^{2}\cos^{2}(\theta_{\mathbf{k}}-\theta_{\mathbf{q}})
in
the
integrand of Eq. (
48
). The rest of the integrand is non-singular and
can be evaluated at
\mathbf{q}=0
. Integrating
over
\xi_{\mathbf{k}}
, and further expanding in small
v_{F}|\mathbf{q}|/\Delta
and
\Omega/\Delta
we obtain, in
2D
,

\displaystyle\delta\Pi_{T}(q)=\nu_{F}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}\dfrac{d\theta_{\mathbf{k}}}{2\pi}\gamma^{2}(\theta_{\mathbf{k}})\dfrac{\Omega^{2}-v_{F}^{2}|\mathbf{q}|^{2}\cos^{2}(\theta_{\mathbf{k}}-\theta_{\mathbf{q}})}{4\Delta^{2}\gamma^{2}(\theta_{\mathbf{k}})}=\frac{\nu_{F}}{4\Delta^{2}}\,\left(\Omega^{2}-\frac{v_{F}^{2}|\mathbf{q}|^{2}}{2}\right),

(49)

where
\theta_{\mathbf{q}}
is the direction of the momentum
\mathbf{q}
. The mode dispersion is

\Omega=v_{F}|\mathbf{q}|/\sqrt{2}
.
The same analysis for
D=3
yields
\Omega=v_{F}|\mathbf{q}|/\sqrt{3}
.
Both expressions are the same as we found quasi-classically and coincide with the corresponding expressions for

s-
wave superfluids.

We next
include the Coulomb interaction into consideration and
use the full expression for the
transverse polarization bubble
\bar{\Pi}_{T}(\mathbf{q},\Omega)
, defined in Eq. (
43
).
We
use the fact that
\bar{\Pi}_{T}(0,0)=\Pi_{T}(0,0)
and
express
\bar{\Pi}_{T}(\mathbf{q},\Omega)
as

\bar{\Pi}_{T}(\mathbf{q},\Omega)=\Pi_{T}(0,0)+\delta\bar{\Pi}_{T}(\mathbf{q},\Omega)
. Using the gap equation (
4
), we then obtain

\displaystyle\chi_{T}(\mathbf{q},\Omega)=-\dfrac{1}{g^{2}\delta\bar{\Pi}_{T}(\mathbf{q},\Omega)}-\dfrac{1}{g}.

(50)

where

\displaystyle\delta\bar{\Pi}_{T}(\mathbf{q},\Omega)=\delta\Pi_{T}(\mathbf{q},\Omega)-\dfrac{8\,\Pi_{GF}(\mathbf{q},\Omega)^{2}}{V^{-1}_{\mathbf{q}}-2\,\Pi_{0}(\mathbf{q},\Omega)}.

(51)

The pole of the pair susceptibility
\chi_{T}(\mathbf{q},\Omega)
is given by the condition
\delta\bar{\Pi}_{T}(\mathbf{q},\Omega)=0
.
At small
v_{F}|\mathbf{q}|\ll\Delta

we expand
\delta\bar{\Pi}_{T}(\mathbf{q},\Omega)
in small
\mathbf{q}
and ignore terms of the order
O(\Omega^{2}|\mathbf{q}|^{2})
.
Approximating
\Pi_{GF}(\mathbf{q},\Omega)\approx-\nu_{F}\,\Omega/4\,\Delta
and
\Pi_{0}(\mathbf{q},\Omega)\approx-\nu_{F}
, using Eqs. (
45
)- (
46
), and further expanding Eq. (
51
) in
V^{-1}_{\mathbf{q}}/2\nu_{F}
, we find that

\displaystyle\delta\bar{\Pi}_{T}(\mathbf{q},\Omega)=\dfrac{|\mathbf{q}|^{2}}{8\,\Delta^{2}}\left(\dfrac{\Omega^{2}\,V^{-1}_{\mathbf{q}}}{|\mathbf{q}|^{2}}-\nu_{F}\,v^{2}_{F}\right).

(52)

The dispersion of the longitudinal mode is

\displaystyle\Omega(\mathbf{q})=\sqrt{\nu_{F}\,v^{2}_{F}\,|\mathbf{q}|^{2}\,V_{\mathbf{q}}}.

(53)

The Coulomb potential in 2D is
V_{\mathbf{q}}=2\pi e^{2}/|\mathbf{q}|
.
Substituting into (
53
), we find

\displaystyle\Omega_{\mathrm{p}}^{(\mathrm{2D})}(\mathbf{q})=\sqrt{2\,\pi\,e^{2}\,\nu_{F}\,v^{2}_{F}|\mathbf{q}|},

(54)

Expending this analysis to
D=3
, we find
\Omega_{\mathrm{p}}^{(\mathrm{3D})}=\sqrt{(2/3)\nu_{F}v^{2}_{F}q^{2}V_{q}}
. Substituting
V_{q}=4\pi e^{2}/q^{2}
and using
p_{F}=(3\pi^{2}n)^{1/3}
,
we obtain
\Omega_{\mathrm{p}}^{(\mathrm{3D})}=\sqrt{4\,\pi\,e^{2}\,n/m}
.

Comparing the results of the quasiclassical and diagrammatic approaches, we see that they are equivalent, as it indeed should be.

III.2.2
finite
T

We first discuss
charge neutral
d
-wave superfluid. The mode dispersion is determined from
\Pi_{T}(\mathbf{q},\Omega)=1/g
, where we suppress the explicit temperature dependence of
\Pi_{T}
for notational simplicity, and has to be obtained by summing over Matsubara frequencies instead of integrating over the frequency. At finite
T
, we find the following expression (see Appendix
A
for details),

\displaystyle\Pi_{T}(\mathbf{q},\Omega)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{2}\int\dfrac{d^{2}\mathbf{k}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\,\gamma^{2}_{\mathbf{k}}\left[\left\{\left[1-n(E_{+})-n(E_{-})\right]\left(\dfrac{E_{+}+E_{-}}{E_{+}\,E_{-}}\right)\,\left(\dfrac{E_{+}\,E_{-}+\xi_{+}\,\xi_{-}+\Delta_{\mathbf{k}}^{2}(T)}{(E_{+}+E_{-})^{2}-\Omega^{2}}\right)\right\}\right.

\displaystyle\left.-\left\{\left[n(E_{+})-n(E_{-})\right]\,\left(\dfrac{E_{+}-E_{-}}{E_{+}\,E_{-}}\right)\,\left(\dfrac{E_{+}\,E_{-}-\xi_{+}\,\xi_{-}-\Delta_{\mathbf{k}}^{2}(T)}{(E_{+}-E_{-})^{2}-\Omega^{2}}\right)\right\}\right],

(55)

where
\xi_{\pm}=\xi_{\mathbf{k}\pm\mathbf{q}/2}
,
E_{\pm}=E_{\mathbf{k}\pm\mathbf{q}/2}
, and
n(E_{\pm})=1/\{\exp(\beta\,E_{\pm})+1\}
is the Fermi function. As before, we express
\Pi_{T}(\mathbf{q},\Omega)
as
\Pi_{T}(0,0)+\delta\Pi_{T}(\mathbf{q},\Omega)
and use the gap equation

\Pi_{T}(0,0)=1/g
such that the mode dispersion comes from
\delta\Pi_{T}(\mathbf{q},\Omega)=0
. For small
\mathbf{q}
, we approximate
\xi_{\pm}=\xi_{\mathbf{k}}+\mathbf{v}_{F}.\mathbf{q}/2
and
E_{\pm}=E_{\mathbf{k}}\pm\delta E_{\mathbf{k}}
where
\delta E_{\mathbf{k}}=\xi_{\mathbf{k}}\mathbf{v}_{F}.\mathbf{q}/2\,E_{\mathbf{k}}
, and express

\delta\Pi_{T}(\mathbf{q},\Omega)
as
\delta\Pi_{T}^{\mathrm{(a)}}(\mathbf{q},\Omega)+\delta\Pi_{T}^{\mathrm{(b)}}(\mathbf{q},T)
, where

\displaystyle\delta\Pi_{T}^{(\mathrm{a})}(\mathbf{q},\Omega)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\nu_{F}}{2}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\xi_{\mathbf{k}}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\gamma^{2}(\theta_{\mathbf{k}})\frac{d\theta_{\mathbf{k}}}{2\pi}\left[1-2n(E_{\mathbf{k}})\right]

(56)

\displaystyle\times\left[\left(\dfrac{E_{+}+E_{-}}{E_{+}\,E_{-}}\right)\,\dfrac{E_{+}\,E_{-}+\xi_{+}\,\xi_{-}+\Delta_{\mathbf{k}}^{2}(T)}{(E_{+}+E_{-})^{2}-\Omega^{2}}-\dfrac{1}{E_{\mathbf{k}}}\right]

(57)

and

\displaystyle\delta\Pi_{T}^{(\mathrm{b})}(\mathbf{q},\Omega)

\displaystyle=-\nu_{F}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\xi_{\mathbf{k}}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\gamma^{2}(\theta_{\mathbf{k}})\dfrac{d\theta_{\mathbf{k}}}{2\pi}\,\left[\dfrac{\partial n(E_{\mathbf{k}})}{\partial E_{\mathbf{k}}}\right]\delta E_{\mathbf{k}}

associated with the excitation of the Higgs mode.
The decay of the oscillations is faster than in

s-
wave superconductors
Shimano and Tsuji 2020
which
qualitatively agrees with our findings.
To get a more detailed comparison with the experiments, one would need to know the
relation between the oscillation frequency and the value of the pairing gap – it has not been yet
extracted from the experiments.

Unconventional
d
-wave superconductivity usually emerges in materials with strong electron-electron correlations, such as high-
T_{c}

cuprates and heavy-fermion Ce-based
materials
Petrovic et al. 2001
;
Movshovich et al. 2001
;
Miyake 2007
;
Van Dyke et al. 2014
;
Sarrao and Thompson 2007
. In our
treatment of the
longitudinal mode, we
neglected the effects associated with electronic correlations. These correlations may
change the exponent in the
power law decay of the amplitude mode compared to that we report here for free electrons. This opens up an
exciting opportunity to explore a possible connection between the value of the exponent
and the strength of the electron-electron correlations.

Potential disorder also leads to a fast suppression of

d-
wave
superconductivity and as such, may also significantly affect the dynamics of the
longitudinal mode similar to
the effect of paramagnetic impurities in an
s-
wave
superconductor.
In the latter case, it has been found
Dzero and Kamenev 2025

that the damping rate of the longitudinal mode at
\mathbf{q}=0
in the presence of pair-breaking paramagnetic impurities
is the same as that of the mode at a certain finite
\mathbf{q}
in the clean case,
determined from the relation

(\xi q)^{6}\longleftrightarrow 1/(\tau_{s}\varepsilon_{\mathrm{g}})
, where
\tau_{s}
is the
scattering time from paramagnetic impurities,
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{g}}
is the single-particle threshold
and
\xi=v_{F}/\Delta_{0}
is the coherence length.
Given that
we found that
the decay rate in
a
d
-wave superconductor does not change with momentum, it would be interesting to analyze how
pair-breaking effects due to disorder affect
the dynamics of the longitudinal mode in the
d-
wave case.
Finally, it is worth exploring the possibility that
the frequency of the
longitudinal mode in a
d-

s-
wave superconductor
Nosov et al. 2025
.
We leave these
items for
future study.

VI
Conclusions

In this communication, we presented the results of our study of collective excitations in
in a clean
d
-wave superconductor in the presence of long-range Coulomb interaction, neglecting other interaction effects.
We used diagrammatic and quasiclassical approaches and obtained the dispersions of both
transverse (phase) and longitudinal (amplitude) excitations.
For the transverse mode, we showed explicitly how a Goldstone phase mode in the absence of Coulomb interaction transforms into a plasma mode once Coulomb interaction is included. We found that at
T=0
the mode dispersion is the same as in an
s-
wave superconductor, but at a finite
T
the dispersion in the
d-
wave case is softer and with a higher decay rate due to partial screening of the Coulomb potential by nodal quasiparticles.
These
d-
wave-specific features should be detectable by measuring the
Carlson-Goldman mode at
T\leq T_{c}
.

For the longitudinal mode, we found that it is a resonance within the continuum and the mode frequency at
q=0
is
2\Delta_{\text{max}}
, where
\Delta{\text{max}}
is the value of the pairing gap in the anti-nodal direction.
This is similar to an
s-
wave case, but in distinction to it, the imaginary part of the longitudinal susceptibility of a d-wave superconductor is non-zero for all
\Omega
.
We analyzed the dispersion of the amplitude mode in the
d-
wave case and found that it is quite different from that in an
s-
wave superconductor.
Specifically,
over some range of momentum
\mathbf{q}
, the frequency
of the amplitude mode and the shape of the longitudinal susceptibility strongly depend on the direction of
\mathbf{q}
,
e.g.,
the resonance in the longitudinal susceptibility is at
different frequency for momenta along nodal and antinodal directions.
Momentum-dependent resonance frequencies give rise to momentum-dependent periodicity of oscillations of
time dependent
\chi_{\textrm{L}}(\omega,\mathbf{q})
.
At the same time, the decay rate of the amplitude of the oscillations
is
1/t^{2}
independent of the
magnitude of momentum and its
direction.

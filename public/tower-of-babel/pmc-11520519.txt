VASN promotes the aggressive phenotype in ARID1A-deficient lung adenocarcinoma
Authors: Dan-ni Wu, Kang-liang Zhang, Rui-heng Chen, Wen-sheng Ye, Chong Zheng, Yuan-liang Zheng, Xiao-dan Zhao, Ri-sheng Huang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractLoss of ARID1A has been reported to drive the progression of lung adenocarcinoma, yet the underlying mechanism remains elusive. In this study, we performed secretome analysis to identify the key secreted proteins regulating lung adenocarcinoma progression. We showed that the VASN level was significantly elevated in the conditioned medium from ARID1A-depleted A549 and H1299 cells. Restoration of ARID1A in ARID1A-depleted lung adenocarcinoma cells prevented the upregulation and secretion of VASN. Clinical analysis demonstrated a negative correlation between ARID1A and VASN expression in ARID1A-mutated lung adenocarcinomas. The patients with ARID1A-mutated lung adenocarcinoma had significantly higher concentrations of serum VASN than healthy controls. Moreover, serum VASN concentrations were associated with TNM stage, lymph node metastasis, and overall survival of the patients with ARID1A-mutated lung adenocarcinoma. Functional studies indicated that VASN overexpression potentiated the proliferation, invasion, and tumorigenesis of lung adenocarcinoma cells. Antibody neutralization of VASN suppressed the aggressiveness of ARID1A-depleted lung adenocarcinoma cells both in vitro and in vivo. Addition of recombinant VASN protein promoted the proliferation and invasion of lung adenocarcinoma cells. Additionally, knockdown of Notch1 blocked the aggressive phenotype induced by recombinant VASN protein. In conclusion, our data uncover the role of VASN in mediating the progression of ARID1A-depleted lung adenocarcinoma and highlight VASN as a promising therapeutic target for this disease.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12885-024-13083-y.Keywords: ARID1A, Lung adenocarcinoma, Secretion, VASN

Introduction

Lung cancer is one of the most lethal malignancies worldwide and leads to over a million deaths per year [1]. Lung adenocarcinoma is the major pathological subtype of lung cancer, accounting for about 40% of all lung cancer cases [2]. The prognosis of lung adenocarcinoma is poor due to its aggressive features [3]. Molecular alterations in key genes including ARID1A contribute to the initiation and progression of lung adenocarcinoma. AT-interacting domain-rich protein 1 A (ARID1A) acts as an essential component of the switch/sucrose non-fermentable (SWI/SNF) complex and plays a crucial role in the modulation of gene transcription [4]. Inactivating mutations frequently occur in the coding region of ARID1A, thereby causing a premature stop codon or frameshifts [5]. ARID1A loss predicts poor prognosis in lung adenocarcinoma [6]. Knockdown of ARID1A promotes the tumorigenesis and metastasis of lung adenocarcinoma cells [6, 7]. Molecular characterization of ARID1A-mutated lung adenocarcinoma is important to develop effective therapeutic strategies for this disease.

Vasorin encoded by the VASN gene is a transmembrane glycoprotein that consists of a leucine-rich repeat motif, an epidermal growth factor-like motif, and a fibronectin type III-like motif [8]. VASN is abundantly expressed in vascular smooth muscle cells and regulates neointimal formation after vascular injury [9]. VASN also promotes tumor progression by facilitating tumor angiogenesis [10, 11]. In addition, VASN has been shown to potentiate cancer cell proliferation and invasion in colorectal cancer [12] and hepatocellular carcinoma [13]. Although VASN displays the ability to enhance cigarette smoke-induced transformation of human bronchial epithelial cells [14], the role of VASN in lung cancer remains unclear.

Our previous work has demonstrated that ARID1A loss provokes the secretion of P4HB from lung adenocarcinoma cells to remodel the hostile tumor microenvironment [15]. To gain more insight into the aggressive characteristics of ARID1A-deficient lung adenocarcinoma, in the present study we unveiled the secretome of ARID1A-silenced lung adenocarcinoma cells. We identified VASN as a secretory protein from ARID1A-depleted lung adenocarcinoma cells and explored its role in lung adenocarcinoma progression. Additionally, the signaling pathway involved in the activity of VASN in lung adenocarcinoma was investigated.

Materials and methods

Cell culture

The lung cancer A549 and H1299 cells were cultured in Dulbecco’s modified Eagle medium (DMEM) containing 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS; Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA, USA). ARID1A-depleted A549 and H1299 cells were constructed by transfecting ARID1A short hairpin RNAs (shRNAs), as described previously [15]. The cell cultures were routinely tested to eliminate mycoplasma contamination.

Secretome analysis

For the collection of conditioned medium [16], ARID1A-depleted A549 and H1299 cells (1 × 106) were seeded at 75 cm2 flasks and cultured in serum-free medium for 24 h. The conditioned media was harvested, cleared by centrifugation at 1,000 × g, and filtered through a 0.22 μm nylon filter. The conditioned medium was concentrated using a 3 kDa cut-off filter (Millipore, Billerica, MA, USA) and stored at -80 °C for further analyses. For mass spectrometry analysis, secreted proteins in the conditioned medium were digested with trypsin (Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA). The digested samples were subjected to liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry. Mass spectra were acquired, and proteins were identified by searching against the Homo sapiens Uniprot database. Proteins were considered to have a significant change in abundance at P < 0.01 and fold change ≥ 2.0.

Serum and tumor samples

Serum and tumor samples were collected from 91 patients with ARID1A-mutant lung adenocarcinoma and serum samples from 72 healthy subjects. Lung adenocarcinoma patients receiving anticancer treatment before tissue sample collection were excluded. Clinical data were retrieved from medical records.

Immunohistochemical analysis

Tumor samples were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde and embedded in paraffin. Deparaffined sections were incubated with anti-ARID1A (Cell Signaling Technology, Danvers, MA, USA) or anti-VASN antibody (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA) at 4 °C overnight. After incubation with secondary antibodies, the sections were incubated with 3,3’-diaminobenzidine solution and counterstained with hematoxylin. The immunohistochemical staining intensity was scored as 0 (negative), 1 (weak), 2 (moderate), or 3 (strong). The staining extent was scored according to the percentage of immunoreactive cells on a scale of 0 to 4: 0 (< 5%), 1 (5–25%), 2 (26–50%), 3 (51–75%), and 4 (> 75%). Multiplication of the staining intensity and extent scores yielded the immunoreactivity score. ARID1A or VASN expression in tumor samples with a final staining score of < 4 or ≥ 4 was considered low or high, respectively.

Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA)

The concentrations of VASN in the conditioned medium of lung cancer cells and serum samples were determined using the Human VASN ELISA Kit (Sino Biological, Beijing, China).

RNA isolation and quantitative real-time PCR (RT-PCR) analysis

Total RNA was extracted from cancer cells using the RNAiso Plus reagent following the manufacturer’s instructions (Takara, Dalian, China). Total RNA was reverse transcribed into cDNA copies using the PrimeScript II 1st Strand cDNA Synthesis Kit (Takara). Quantitative RT-PCR was performed using the Power SYBR Green PCR Master Mix (Thermo Fisher Scientific). The primers used were shown as follows: VASN forward primer, 5′-GAGAGCCACGTCACACTGG-3′; VASN reverse primer, 5′-CAAAGTCGGCGTAGTCAAGC-3′; HES1 forward primer, 5′-TCAACACGACACCGGATAAAC-3′; HES1 reverse primer, 5′-GCCGCGAGCTATCTTTCTTCA-3′; HEY1 forward primer, 5′-GTTCGGCTCTAGGTTCCATGT-3′; HEY1 reverse primer, 5′-CGTCGGCGCTTCTCAATTATTC-3′; GAPDH forward primer: 5′-GACATCAAGAAGGTGGTGAA-3′; GAPDH reverse primer: 5′-TGTCATACCAGGAAATGAGC-3′. The gene expression level was normalized GAPDH using the 2−ΔΔCt method [17].

Western blot analysis

Cancer cells were lysed in lysis buffer containing proteinase and phosphatase inhibitor cocktails (Beyotime, China). The protein samples in loading buffer were boiled for 5 min, separated using sodium dodecyl sulfate-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis, and transferred onto polyvinylidene fluoride membranes. After blocking with 5% fat-free milk, the membranes were probed with antibodies against ARID1A (Cell Signaling Technology, Danvers, MA, USA), VASN (Thermo Fisher Scientific), Notch1 (Cell Signaling Technology), and GAPDH (Cell Signaling Technology). After incubation with horseradish peroxidase-conjugated secondary antibodies, the signals were visualized using ECL chemiluminescence (Thermo Fisher Scientific).

Chromatin immunoprecipitation (ChIP) assay

A549 and H1299 cells transfected with indicated constructs were cross-linked with 1% formaldehyde and and lysed in lysis buffer. The cross-linked chromatin was sonicated to generate DNA fragments with a size of 200–500 bp. The DNA fragments were incubated with chromatin was incubated with anti-ARID1A antibody or negative control IgG (Cell Signaling Technology) at 4 °C overnight. The amount of the enriched fragments of the VASN promoter was quantified using quantitative RT-PCR assays. The PCR primers are as follows: forward, 5′-GTGTGGGTGTCCCAAGGCTCT-3′ and reverse, 5′-CCAGGGGCCGGACTAGTCCAG-3′.

Plasmid construction and cell transfection

As described previously [15], ARID1A shRNA was cloned into the pLKO.1 puro vector with the target sequence, 5′-TAATGCCTTGCCCAATGCCAA-3′. The Notch1-targeting siRNA used was as follows: siNotch1: 5′-GGUGUGCACUGUGAGAUCAtt-3′ [18]. For ARID1A and VASN overexpression, full-length human ARID1A and VASN cDNAs were cloned to pcDNA3.1(+) vector. Cell transfection was performed using Lipofectamine 3000 (Invitrogen). Stable VASN- expressing A549 subclones were selected after 14 days of culture in the presence of G418 (Sigma-Aldrich).

Exogenous VASN and anti-VASN neutralizing antibody treatment

A549 and H1299 cells were treated with recombinant VASN protein (5 or 10 ng/mL; Abcam, Cambridge, MA, USA) for indicated times [8]. Cell proliferation, invasion, and colony formation were assessed. For the inhibition of extracellular VASN activity, ARID1A-silenced A549 and H1299 cells were treated with a specific anti-VASN antibody (100 ng/mL; Thermo Fisher Scientific).

Cell proliferation assay

Cancer cells were plated at a density of 2,000 cells per well in 96-well plates. After indicated treatments, cell proliferation was measured at a 24-h interval by direct cell counting using a haemocytometer.

Colony formation assay

After indicated treatments, cancer cells were plated into 6-well plates (500 cells per well). Culture medium was replaced every 3 days. Cancer cells formed colonies after culturing for 2 weeks. The colonies were stained with 0.1% crystal violet and counted.

Transwell invasion assay

Cell invasion assay was performed using a modified Boyden chamber method. After indicated treatments, cancer cells were plated on Matrigel-coated inserts (2 × 104 cells per insert). Culture medium containing 10% FBS was added to the lower chamber. After incubation for 24 h at 37 °C, the invaded cells on the lower surface of the membrane were stained with crystal violet and counted under a microscope.

Mouse xenograft studies

A549 cells with stable expression of VASN (2 × 106 cells per mouse) were injected subcutaneously into male BALB/c nude mice (4–6 weeks old). Each group had 4 mice. Tumor volume was measured every week for 4 weeks. For evaluation of the anticancer activity of targeting VASN, mice were subcutaneously implanted with ARID1A-depleted A549 cells (2 × 106 cells per mouse) to develop xenograft tumors. The tumor-harboring mice were randomly divided into 2 groups of 4 mice per group and treated with either the anti-VASN antibody or the isotype control antibody at a dose of 2 mg/week, i.p., for 4 weeks. Mice were euthanized, and tumors were harvested and weighed.

Statistical analysis

The data were presented as the mean ± standard deviation. Statistical difference was determined using the Student’s t-test or one-way analysis of variance followed by Tukey’s post-hoc test. Categorical variables were analyzed by the chi-square test. Kaplan-Meier survival curves were compared by the log-rank test. All P < 0.05 values were statistically significant.

Results

ARID1A loss promotes the release of VASN from lung adenocarcinoma cells

To identify novel biomarkers for ARID1A-mutated lung adenocarcinoma, we performed secretome analysis of A549 and H1299 lung adenocarcinoma cells with ARID1A knockdown. We observed 43 and 28 proteins whose abundance was significantly altered upon ARID1A loss in A549 and H1299 cells, respectively (Fig. 1A). We focused on VASN, because it was significantly alerted in both ARID1A-depleted A549 and H1299 cells. ELISA analysis confirmed an increased level of VASN in the conditioned medium from ARID1A-depleted A549 and H1299 cells (Fig. 1B). Quantitative RT-PCR analysis further indicated that ARID1A depletion potentiated the expression of VASN in lung adenocarcinoma cells (Fig. 1C). To test whether ARID1A negatively regulates the expression of VASN, we rescued the expression level of ARID1A in ARID1A-silenced lung adenocarcinoma cells by overexpressing the ARID1A gene (Fig. 1D). The restoration of ARID1A significantly prevented the upregulation and secretion of VASN in ARID1A-silenced lung adenocarcinoma cells (Fig. 1E and F). In addition, ChIP experiments demonstrated that ARID1A overexpression restored ARID1A enrichment at the promoter region of VASN in ARID1A-depleted A549 and H1299 cells (Fig. 1G and H). These results suggest that ARID1A represses the expression of VASN in lung adenocarcinoma cells.

Serum VASN is a prognostic indicator of ARID1A-mutated lung adenocarcinoma

To analyze the relationship between ARID1A and VASN expression in lung adenocarcinoma, we enrolled 91 patients with ARID1A-mutant lung adenocarcinoma. Among them, 48 patients showed abundant expression of ARID1A and 43 weak or negative expression of ARID1A in tumors (Fig. 2A and B). Most intriguingly, reduced expression of intratumoral ARID1A in the lung adenocarcinoma patients was significantly associated with high expression of VASN both in tumor and serum samples (Fig. 2A and C). Next, we determined the clinical relevance of serum VASN in ARID1A-mutated lung cancer. Compared to healthy controls, the cohort of patients with ARID1A-mutated lung adenocarcinoma had a significantly higher concentration of serum VASN (P < 0.0001; Fig. 2D). According to the median serum VASN level, lung cancer patients were divided to high and low VASN groups. The correlation between the serum concentration of VASN and the clinicopathological parameters of ARID1A-mutated lung adenocarcinoma is shown in Fig. 2E. Of note, high serum VASN concentrations were significantly associated with TNM stage (P = 0.0012) and lymph node metastasis (P = 0.0003). In addition, Kaplan-Meier analysis demonstrated that a high serum VASN concentration was associated with reduced overall survival in the patients with ARID1A-mutated lung adenocarcinoma (P = 0.0003; Fig. 2F). These results suggest serum VASN as a potential biomarker for ARID1A-mutated lung adenocarcinoma progression.

Overexpression of VASN enhances the proliferation, invasion, and tumorigenesis of lung cancer cells

Previous studies have indicated the oncogenic role for VASN in colorectal cancer [12], hepatocellular carcinoma [13], glioma [19]. Consistently, our data demonstrated that enforced expression of VASN in A549 and H1299 cells promoted their abilities to proliferate, form colonies, and invade (Fig. 3A and D). In vivo studies showed that overexpression of VASN in A549 cells facilitated tumor formation in nude mice and increased the weight of the A549 xenograft tumors (Fig. 3E and G). These results indicate that the upregulation of VASN contributes to the growth and progression of lung cancer.

Antibody neutralization of VASN disrupts the aggressive phenotype of ARID1A-silenced lung cancer cells

Next, we examined whether the aggressive phenotype of ARID1A-silenced lung cancer cells depends on VASN signaling. To this end, we utilized a specific VASN neutralizing antibody to block VASN signaling. Intriguingly, blockade of VASN activity suppressed the proliferation, colony formation, and invasion of ARID1A-silenced lung cancer cells (Fig. 4A and C). Moreover, administration of anti-VASN antibody reduced the growth of the xenograft tumors formed by ARID1A-silenced A549 cells (Fig. 4D and E). Therefore, VASN activity plays an essential role in the progression of ARID1A-deficient lung cancer.

Recombinant VASN protein potentiates the aggressive phenotype of lung cancer cells through the Notch1 pathway

To substantiate the role of secretory VASN in lung cancer progression, we treated A549 and H1299 cells with recombinant VASN protein. It was found that the treatment with recombinant VASN protein significantly promoted the proliferation, colony formation, and invasion of A549 and H1299 cells (Fig. 5A and C). It has been documented that VASN augments tumor growth in glioblastoma by activating the Notch1 pathway [20]. Interestingly, recombinant VASN treatment induced the expression of multiple Notch1 target genes including HES1 and HEY1 (Fig. 5D). Thus, we checked the involvement of the Notch1 pathway in the action of VASN in lung cancer. Of note, knockdown of Notch1 rescued the aggressive phenotype of lung cancer cells treated by recombinant VASN protein (Fig. 5E and H).

Discussion

ARID1A inactivation has been shown to cause transcriptomic changes in cancer cells [7, 21]. However, few studies have investigated the impact of ARID1A deficiency on the secretome of cancer cells. In this work, we analyzed the secretome of ARID1A-silenced A549 and H1299 cells and identified VASN as a secretory protein released by ARID1A-depleted lung adenocarcinoma cells. VASN overexpression potentiates the aggressive phenotype of ARID1A-intact lung cancer cells. Most importantly, antibody neutralization of VASN rescues the proliferation and invasion of ARID1A-silenced lung cancer cells. Clinically, a high serum VASN level predicts poor prognosis in ARID1A-mutated lung cancer. Taken together, these results indicate that the release of VASN upon ARID1A loss plays a crucial role in the progression of lung adenocarcinoma.

VASN expression is elevated in multiple cancers including glioma [10], hepatocellular carcinoma [13] and lung cancer [14]. Hypoxic stimulation has been suggested to induce the expression of VASN in glioma through the HIF1α/STAT3 co-activator-dependent mechanism [20]. In this study, we demonstrate that ARID1A depletion upregulates the expression of VASN in lung adenocarcinoma, which accompanies with the reduction of ARID1A enrichment at the promoter region of VASN. When ARID1A is overexpressed in ARID1A-depleted lung adenocarcinoma cells, the upregulation of VASN is abolished and the VASN promoter occupancy by ARID1A is restored. Clinically, ARID1A expression is negatively associated with VASN expression in lung adenocarcinomas. These results suggest that ARID1A can repress the expression of VASN likely through the occupancy of the VASN promoter. Nevertheless, the detailed mechanisms underlying the VASN upregulation upon ARID1A loss need additional exploration.

High expression of VASN in tumors is negatively associated with lung cancer patient survival [14]. Moreover, VASN can be released to the extracellular milieu via exosomes [10, 22], a type of extracellular vesicles that carry protein, DNA, and RNA molecules. In addition, a disintegrin and metalloprotease protein-17 (ADAM17) shows the ability to cleave the transmembrane VASN protein to generate soluble VASN [23]. Our data also revealed that VASN is abundantly present in the conditioned medium of ARID1A-depleted lung adenocarcinoma cells. These findings encourage us to evaluate the clinical significance of serum VASN in lung adenocarcinoma. Notably, ARID1A-mutated lung adenocarcinoma patients have significantly greater concentrations of serum VASN than healthy controls. The serum VASN levels are increased in the lung adenocarcinoma patients with low intratumoral ARID1A expression. Most importantly, a high serum VASN level is associated with advanced TNM stage, lymph node metastasis, and reduced overall survival in patients with ARID1A-mutated lung adenocarcinoma. These data suggest that serum VASN is a promising biomarker for ARID1A-mutated lung adenocarcinoma prognosis. In agreement with our results, serum VASN also serves as a biomarker in hepatocellular carcinoma [24].

ARID1A loss has been documented to drive the progression of lung adenocarcinoma [6, 15]. Consistently, we confirm that silencing of ARID1A augments the proliferation, colony formation, and invasion of lung adenocarcinoma cells. Most intriguingly, antibody blockade of VASN disrupts the proliferation and invasion induced by ARID1A silencing. Gain-of-function studies further demonstrated that overexpression of VASN facilitates the growth, invasion, and tumorigenesis of ARID1A-intact lung adenocarcinoma cells. In colorectal cancer [12] and hepatocellular carcinoma [13], VASN also displays the ability to stimulate cancer cell proliferation, migration, and invasion. Therefore, the upregulation and secretion of VASN is indispensable for the aggressive phenotype of ARID1A-silenced lung adenocarcinoma cells.

In glioma stem-like cells, VASN has been found to interact with and stabilize Notch1, consequently promoting Notch1 signaling [20]. However, Liang et al. [19] showed that VASN inhibits Notch1 signaling in glioma cells. The contradictory results may arise from the utilization of different cells. Our data indicate that multiple Notch1 target genes including HES1 and HEY1 are induced upon the treatment with recombinant VASN protein. These observations suggest a link between VASN and Notch1 signaling in lung adenocarcinoma. The Notch1 pathway plays an important role in lung cancer progression [25–27]. Induction of HES1 has been shown to promote the invasion and metastasis of non-small cell lung cancer [28]. Notch1 regulates self-renewal of cancer stem-like cells in lung cancer through the HES1-dependent pathway [29]. The Notch1/HEY1 pathway is involved in IMPAD1-induced lung cancer metastasis [30]. Therefore, we investigated the role of Notch1 signaling in mediating the biological activity of VASN. Intriguingly, knockdown of Notch1 rescued VASN-induced proliferation and invasion of lung cancer cells. Taken together, these data provide mechanistic insights into how VASN promotes the aggressive phenotype in lung adenocarcinoma. However, the precise mechanism for the activation of Notch1 signaling by VASN necessitates further exploration.

In conclusion, we uncover a pivotal role for VASN in the progression of lung adenocarcinoma. VASN expression and secretion contributes to the growth, invasion, and tumorigenesis of lung adenocarcinoma cells through the interaction with Notch1. Serum VASN serves as a biomarker for the prognosis of ARID1A-mutated lung adenocarcinoma. Our work suggests that blocking VASN holds promise for the treatment of ARID1A-mutated lung adenocarcinoma.

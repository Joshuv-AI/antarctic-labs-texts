The incompressible limit of an inhomogeneous model of tissue growth
Anthony Sulak, Olga Turanova

Abstract
We study a porous medium equation that models tissue growth in a heterogeneous environment. We show that, in the incompressible limit, solutions converge to those of a weak form of a Hele-Shaw type free boundary problem. To obtain enough compactness to take the limit, we establish an L 4 L^{4} bound on the gradient of the pressure and an estimate of Aronson-Bénilan type.



1
Introduction

The focus of our work is the
m\rightarrow\infty
limit (called the incompressible limit or stiff pressure limit) of the inhomogeneous porous medium equation with reaction,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u_{m}=\nabla\cdot\left(\frac{u_{m}}{a(x,t)}\nabla p_{m}\right)+\frac{u_{m}}{a(x,t)}\Phi(x,t,p_{m})\text{ on }\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,\infty),

(1)

where the pressure
p_{m}
is given in terms of the density
u_{m}
by the constitutive law,

p_{m}=\frac{m}{m-1}\left(\frac{u_{m}}{b(x,t)}\right)^{m-1},\quad m\geq 2.

(2)

The coefficients
a
and
b
are assumed to be bounded from above and strictly away from zero and satisfy certain structural assumptions. It is also assumed that the growth term
\Phi
is strictly decreasing in
p
and that there exists
p_{M}>0
with
\Phi(x,t,p_{M})=0
, which corresponds to a ceiling on the maximum pressure that the medium can support. (The precise assumptions are listed in Section
1.1
.) In addition, we remark that (
1
) may also be written as,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u_{m}=\nabla\cdot\left(\frac{b}{a}\nabla\left(\frac{u_{m}}{b}\right)^{m}\right)+\frac{u_{m}}{a}\Phi(x,t,p_{m}).

(3)

The homogeneous version of this model (
a\equiv b\equiv 1
and
\Phi
depending only on
p
) was proposed by Byrne and Drasdo in
[
1
]
as a model for tumor growth. Its incompressible limit was shown by Perthame, Quiròs, and Vázquez in
[
20
]
to be a free boundary problem of Hele-Shaw type.
Since then, there has been a large number of works on models of this type: see Section
1.4
for a brief literature review. Here, the presence of the nonconstant coefficients
a
and
b
represents heterogeneity in the underlying medium and in the cellular packing density, respectively
[
21
,
23
]
.

In our main result, Theorem
1.6
, we establish that, as
m\rightarrow\infty
, the density and pressure converge to the pair
(u_{\infty},p_{\infty})
, which is the (unique) weak solution of

\left\{\begin{split}&\partial_{t}u_{\infty}=\nabla\cdot\left(\frac{b}{a}\nabla p_{\infty}\right)+\frac{u_{\infty}}{a}\Phi(x,t,p_{\infty})\\
&\left(1-\frac{u_{\infty}}{b}\right)p_{\infty}=0\text{ almost everywhere.}\end{split}\right.

(4)

In addition, we provide more detail on the behavior of the limiting density and pressure. The heuristics for this can be seen by examining the equation that the pressure satisfies,

\partial_{t}p_{m}-\frac{|\nabla p_{m}|^{2}}{a}=(m-1)\frac{p_{m}}{b}\left(\nabla\cdot\left(\frac{b}{a}\nabla p_{m}\right)+\frac{b}{a}\Phi(x,t,p_{m})-\partial_{t}b\right),

(5)

which is obtained by multiplying (
3
) by
\frac{m}{b}\left(\frac{u_{m}}{b}\right)^{m-2}
and performing standard manipulations. It is natural to guess that, in the
m\rightarrow\infty
limit of (
5
), the righthand side of (
5
) converges to zero:

\frac{p_{\infty}}{b}\left(\nabla\cdot\left(\frac{b}{a}\nabla p_{\infty}\right)+\frac{b}{a}\Phi(x,t,p_{\infty})-\partial_{t}b\right)=0.

(6)

This is the so-called complementarity condition. We prove that
(u_{\infty},p_{\infty})
does indeed satisfy (
6
) in the sense of distributions.

The complementarity condition indicates that, for each time
t
, there are two regions of interest: the region where
p_{\infty}
is zero, and the region where
p_{\infty}
is positive (and therefore the term in the parentheses of (
6
) is identically zero). Thus, it is natural to attempt to characterize the evolution of the boundary between these two regions.
Examining (
5
) suggests that, on this boundary, the limit as
m\rightarrow\infty
of the lefthand side of (
5
) should be identically zero:

\frac{\partial_{t}p_{\infty}}{|\nabla p_{\infty}|}=\frac{|\nabla p_{\infty}|}{a}.

The lefthand side of the previous line is exactly the normal velocity of the zero level set of
p_{\infty}
. Thus, formally, the limiting normal velocity is exactly
\frac{|\nabla p_{\infty}|}{a}
. The model (
1
) is the first one for which an inhomogeneity appears in the
|\nabla p_{\infty}|
term of the limiting velocity upon taking the incompressible limit, even in the absence of external density. Making the heuristics about the velocity of
\partial\{p_{\infty}>0\}
rigorous is left for future work.

1.1
Notation and assumptions

For
T>0
, we denote
Q_{T}=\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,T)
, and use

to denote any positive constant that is independent of
m
.

Assumption 1.1

(Basic assumptions)
.

Suppose there exists
\Lambda>0
such that

1/\Lambda\leq a(x,t),\,b(x,t)\leq\Lambda

for all
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,\infty)
and
\|a\|_{C^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,\infty))}+\|b\|_{C^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,\infty))}\leq\Lambda
.

Suppose
\Phi\in C^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,\infty)\times[0,\infty])
, and, for some
p_{M}>0
and
\lambda>0
, satisfies
\partial_{p}\Phi(x,t,p)\leq-\lambda
and
\Phi(x,t,p_{M})=0
, for all
x
,
t
, and
p
.

Assumption 1.2

(Initial Data)
.

For some
u^{0}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, suppose that the initial data
u_{m}^{0}
satisfies,

\displaystyle\begin{cases}u_{m}^{0}\geq 0,&\quad\frac{m}{m-1}\left(\frac{u_{m}^{0}}{b}\right)^{m-1}\leq p_{M},\\
\|u_{m}^{0}-u^{0}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\rightarrow 0\ \text{as}\ m\rightarrow\infty,&\quad\|\partial_{x_{i}}u_{m}^{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C,i=1,\ldots,d,\end{cases}

(7)

and
\text{supp}(u_{m}^{0})\subset\Omega_{0}
for some compact
\Omega_{0}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Suppose also that there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\|\nabla p_{m}^{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|\Delta p_{m}^{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|\partial_{t}p_{m}^{0}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C.

(8)

Assumption 1.3

(Coefficients)
.

Suppose that either,

(i)

d=1
; or,

(ii)

a(x,t)=a(|x|,t)
,
b(x,t)=b(|x|,t)
for all
x
,

, i.e.
a
,
b
are radial in space; or,

(iii)

there exists
R>0
and
0<\epsilon\leq\frac{d-\frac{1}{2}}{\Lambda^{2}}
such that, if
|x|\geq R
, then

\left|\nabla\left(b(x,t)/a(x,t)\right)\right|\leq\epsilon|x|^{-1}.

Assumption 1.4

(Structural)
.

Let
\lambda
be as in Assumption
1.1
. Suppose there exists
\tilde{\lambda}>0
such that, for all
x,t\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,\infty)
,

\Delta\log\left(b(x,t)/a(x,t)\right)\geq\tilde{\lambda}-\lambda.

Remark 1.5

(Remarks on the assumptions)
.

Assumptions
1.1
and
1.2
are analogous to standard assumptions in the literature, such as in
[
3
,
11
,
2
,
20
]
.

The third assumption is similar to
[
2
, Assumption 3]
. Indeed, both
[
2
, Assumption 3]
and our Assumption
1.3
are used to construct barriers to the equation for the pressure in order to conclude that

is bounded and has compact support, independent of
m
: see Lemma
2.1
(
i
).

Assumption
1.4
is used in an essential way in establishing a uniform bound on the time derivative of the density and on the time derivative of the pressure in Lemma
2.1
(
iii
). We are not aware of an analogous assumption appearing elsewhere in the literature.

Relaxing the requirements of Assumptions
1.3
and
1.4
is a direction for future work. We note that if Lemma
2.1
(
i
),(
iii
) could be established by other means, the proofs of the other results in this paper would go through without using Assumptions
1.3
and
1.4
.

We note that one choice of
\Phi
satisfying Assumption
1.1
is
\Phi(x,t,p)=g(p)h(x,t)
, where
g
is strictly decreasing,
g(p_{M})=0
, and
h
is a positive function that is bounded. A standard choice for
g
is a linear function
g(p)=C(p_{M}-p)
, where
C>0
.

1.2
Main Results

Our main result is that solutions of (
3
) converge to those of (
4
) and satisfy the complementarity relation (
6
).

Theorem 1.6

(Convergence and complementarity relation)
.

Suppose Assumptions
1.1
,
1.2
,
1.3
, and
1.4
hold and fix
T>0
. Suppose
u_{m}
is a weak solution of (
1
) on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,T)
. Then, up to a sub-sequence,
u_{m}
and
p_{m}
converge strongly in
L^{1}(Q_{T})
as
m\rightarrow\infty
to
u_{\infty}\in L^{\infty}(Q_{T})
,
p_{\infty}\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
respectively, which satisfy (
4
) in the weak sense on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,T)
. Moreover,
(
6
) holds in the sense of distributions on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,T)
.

Remark 1.7

(Existence and uniqueness for (
4
))
.

We remark that our main convergence result implies the existence of weak solutions to (
4
). The comparison principle (and therefore uniqueness) for solutions to (
4
) follows by a variant of Hilbert’s duality method, which is by now standard for equations like (
4
): see, for example,
[
20
, Section 3]
,
[
2
, Section 4]
, and
[
4
, Section 5]
. The uniqueness of solutions to (
4
), together with our main result, thus implies that the entire sequence
(u_{m},p_{m})
converges to the unique weak solution
(u_{\infty},p_{\infty})
of (
4
).

1.3
Strategy of proof

The main challenge in proving Theorem
1.6
is obtaining enough uniform (in
m
) estimates on the
u_{m}
and the
p_{m}
. To achieve this, we begin by establish some basic bounds, such as on the
L^{1}
norm of the time and space derivatives of
u_{m}
and
p_{m}
(Lemma
2.1
). Then we prove two key estimates on the pressure. One is a uniform bound on
\|\nabla p_{m}\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
(Proposition
3.1
). The other is an estimate of Aronson-Bénilan type: we bound the negative part of
\Delta p_{m}
in
L^{3}
, uniformly in
m
(Proposition
3.4
).

Note that the righthand side of (
5
) may be expressed as

(m-1)p_{m}\left(\frac{1}{a}\Delta p_{m}+\frac{1}{b}\nabla\left(\frac{b}{a}\right)\cdot\nabla p_{m}+\frac{1}{a}\Phi(x,t,p_{m})-\frac{\partial_{t}b}{b}\right).

On the one hand, we note that there is a drift term,
\frac{1}{b}\nabla\left(\frac{b}{a}\right)\cdot\nabla p_{m}
. We handle the difficulties posed by this term by adapting techniques developed for incompressible limits in the presence of drift and advection in Chu’s
[
2
]

and David and Schmidtchen’s
[
4
]
, respectively. On the other hand, we see that the highest order term,
\Delta p_{m}
, has a variable coefficient,
\frac{1}{a}
. Our work is the first one to study the incompressible limit in the presence of such a term, which presents new challenges.
For example, even when establishing the basic estimates of Lemma
2.1
, we have to first bound
\|\partial_{t}u_{m}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
and then use that to control
\|\nabla u_{m}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
, whereas in other works, the estimate on the spacial derivative can be established first. And, when establishing the stronger bounds on the pressure, there are many more terms that need to be kept track of and controlled.

Remark 1.8
.

We often manipulate the equations for
u_{m}
and
p_{m}
pointwise and/or differentiate the equations. These manipulations are justified by: (i) first, approximating
u_{m}
, the solution of (
1
) with initial data
u^{0}
, by
u_{m,\epsilon}
, the solution of (
1
) with initial data
u_{m}^{0}+\epsilon
, (ii) second, establishing the desired estimate for
u_{m,\epsilon}
, which is uniformly positive and thus smooth, and (iii) third, taking the limit
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
. See, for example,
[
23
]
*Section 9.3.

1.4
Literature review

The incompressible limit of a porous medium equation with source term was first studied by Perthame, Quiròs, and Vázquez in
[
20
]
. Since then, there has been a vast number of works on problems of this type, including
[
3
,
5
,
7
,
6
,

,

,
10
,

,

,

,

,

. In particular, there are several works on the incompressible limit in the presence of drift or advection:
[
2
,
4
,
17
]
. Of these, the results of
[
2
]
, which studies the incompressible (

) limit of,

\partial_{t}\rho_{k}=\nabla\cdot(\rho_{k}(\nabla p_{k}+\overrightarrow{b}))+f\rho_{k},\quad p_{k}=\frac{k}{k-1}\left(\frac{\rho_{k}}{m}\right)^{k-1},

where

in

]
exactly plays the role of the coefficient

, and the PDE studied in

]
doesn’t include the coefficient

. Thus, our result does not imply the results in
[
2
]
, and vice-versa. We also mention the work
[

]
, which establishes stochastic homogenization for a free boundary problem with heterogeneity both on the interior and on the free boundary, like (

Basic estimates

In this section we establish basic estimates for solutions of (
1

\boldsymbol{\gamma}=\nabla\log\left(b/a\right)\text{ and }\quad\tilde{\Phi}(x,t,p_{m})=\Phi(x,t,p_{m})-a\partial_{t}\log b.

(9)

For some calculations we use the following equivalent formulation of (
5

\displaystyle\partial_{t}p_{m}-\frac{|\nabla p_{m}|^{2}}{a}

\displaystyle=(m-1)\frac{p_{m}}{a}\left(\Delta p_{m}+\nabla p_{m}\cdot\boldsymbol{\gamma}+\tilde{\Phi}(x,t,p_{m})\right).

(10)

Furthermore, we use the normalized density
v_{m}:=\frac{u_{m}}{b}
, which solves

\displaystyle a\partial_{t}v_{m}=\Delta v^{m}_{m}+\boldsymbol{\gamma}\cdot\nabla v^{m}_{m}+v_{m}\tilde{\Phi}(x,t,p_{m}).

(11)

Lemma 2.1

(Basic estimates)
.

Suppose Assumptions
1.1
,
1.2
,
1.3
, and
1.4
hold and fix

. There exits a constant

, independent of

, such that:

(i)

0\leq u_{m},p_{m},v_{m}\leq C
and
\text{supp}(u_{m}(\cdot,t))\subset B_{Ct}

,

(ii)

\|\nabla p_{m}\|_{L^{1}(Q_{T})}+\|\nabla p_{m}\|_{L^{2}(Q_{T})}\leq C
,

(iii)

\|\partial_{t}u_{m}\|_{L^{1}(Q_{T})}+\|\nabla u_{m}\|_{L^{1}(Q_{T})}\leq C
,

(iv)

\|\partial_{t}p_{m}\|_{L^{1}(Q_{T})}\leq C
.

Proof.

We omit writing the subscript
m
during this proof. Further, we usually abbreviate
\Phi(x,t,p)
as
\Phi(p)
, and similarly for
\tilde{\Phi}
.

A small modification of the construction of
[
2
]
*Lemmas 8.1 - 8.3, namely, replacing the coefficient
m(x,t)
of
[
2
]
with
a(x,t)/b(x,t)
, provides a supersolution

of (

. The comparison principle thus ensures

for all

. By construction,

is bounded and compactly supported in

for all

; therefore, so is
p
. The desired bounds in (
i
) on
u
,

, and
p
follow directly.

To establish (
ii
), we begin by integrating (
10
) on

to obtain,

\iint_{Q_{T}}p_{t}=\iint_{Q_{T}}\frac{|\nabla p|^{2}}{a}+(m-1)\iint_{Q_{T}}\frac{p}{a}\Delta p+(m-1)\iint_{Q_{T}}\frac{p}{a}\nabla p\cdot\boldsymbol{\gamma}+(m-1)\iint_{Q_{T}}\frac{p}{a}\tilde{\Phi}(p).

(12)

Integrating by parts, rearranging the terms, and then using Young’s inequality, yields,

\displaystyle\iint_{Q_{T}}\frac{p}{a}\Delta p+\iint_{Q_{T}}\frac{p}{a}\nabla p\cdot\boldsymbol{\gamma}

\displaystyle=-\iint_{Q_{T}}\nabla\left(\frac{p}{a}\right)\cdot\nabla p+\iint_{Q_{T}}\frac{p}{a}\nabla p\cdot\boldsymbol{\gamma}

\displaystyle=-\iint_{Q_{T}}\frac{|\nabla p|^{2}}{a}-\iint_{Q_{T}}p\nabla\left(\frac{1}{a}\right)\cdot\nabla p+\iint_{Q_{T}}\frac{p}{a}\nabla p\cdot\boldsymbol{\gamma}

\displaystyle=-\iint_{Q_{T}}\frac{|\nabla p|^{2}}{a}+\iint_{Q_{T}}\frac{\nabla p}{a}\cdot\left(-p\frac{\nabla a}{a}+p\boldsymbol{\gamma}\right)

\displaystyle\leq-\iint_{Q_{T}}\frac{|\nabla p|^{2}}{a}+\frac{1}{2}\iint_{Q_{T}}\frac{|\nabla p|^{2}}{a}+\frac{1}{2}\iint_{Q_{T}}\frac{p^{2}|-\nicefrac{{\nabla a}}{{a}}+\boldsymbol{\gamma}|^{2}}{a}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{1}{2}\iint_{Q_{T}}\frac{|\nabla p|^{2}}{a}+C,

where the final inequality follows from item (
i
) of this lemma and Assumption
1.1
. Now, using this estimate to bound the righthand side of (
12
) from above yields,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}p(x,T)\,dt-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}p(x,0)\,dt\leq\iint_{Q_{T}}\frac{|\nabla p|^{2}}{a}-\frac{m-1}{2}\iint_{Q_{T}}\frac{|\nabla p|^{2}}{a}+(m-1)C+(m-1)\iint_{Q_{T}}\frac{p}{a}\tilde{\Phi}(p),

which upon rearranging becomes,

\displaystyle\frac{m-3}{2}\iint_{Q_{T}}\frac{|\nabla p|^{2}}{a}

\displaystyle\leq(m-1)C+(m-1)\iint_{Q_{T}}\frac{p}{a}\tilde{\Phi}(p)-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}p(x,0)\,dt

\displaystyle\leq(m-1)C+C,

where the final inequality follows from item (
i
) of this lemma, Assumption
1.1
, and Assumption
1.2
. Dividing by
(m-3)
and using that
a
is uniformly bounded from above yields the desired estimate on
\|\nabla p\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
. The compact support of
p
along with Hölder’s inequality then gives the desired uniform

bound on
\nabla p
as well.

Before proceeding, we note that the definition of
v
and item (
i
) imply,

|\nabla v_{m}^{m}|=v_{m}|\nabla p_{m}|\leq C|\nabla p_{m}|,

which, upon integrating over
Q_{T}
and applying item (

), yields

\|\nabla v_{m}^{m}\|_{L^{1}(Q_{T})}+\|\nabla v_{m}^{m}\|_{L^{2}(Q_{T})}\leq C.

(13)

Next we will show that the space and time derivatives of the normalized density are bounded in
L^{1}
, and deduce (
iii
) from there. The argument relies on the fact that
\Phi
, and therefore
\tilde{\Phi}
, is strictly decreasing in

, as well as on Assumption
1.4
.

We differentiate (
11
) in

to obtain,

\displaystyle(av_{t})_{t}

\displaystyle=\Delta(v^{m})_{t}+\boldsymbol{\gamma}\cdot\nabla(v^{m})_{t}+\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{t}\cdot\nabla v^{m}+v_{t}\tilde{\Phi}+v\left(\tilde{\Phi}_{t}(p)+\tilde{\Phi}_{p}(p)p_{t}\right)

\displaystyle=\Delta(mv^{m-1}v_{t})+\nabla\cdot(\boldsymbol{\gamma}mv^{m-1}v_{t})-(\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{\gamma})(vp_{t})+\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{t}\cdot\nabla v^{m}+v_{t}\tilde{\Phi}+v\left(\tilde{\Phi}_{t}(p)+\tilde{\Phi}_{p}(p)p_{t}\right),

where we’ve used the equality
(v^{m})_{t}=vp_{t}
. Next we multiply by
\text{sgn}(v_{t})=\text{sgn}(p_{t})
and apply Kato’s inequality to find,

\displaystyle(a|v_{t}|)_{t}

\displaystyle\leq\Delta(mv^{m-1}|v_{t}|)+\nabla\cdot(\boldsymbol{\gamma}mv^{m-1}|v_{t}|)-(\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{\gamma})(v|p_{t}|)+

\displaystyle-(\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{\gamma})(v|p_{t}|)+v\tilde{\Phi}_{p}(p)|p_{t}|

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\iint_{Q_{T}}a|v_{t}|

\displaystyle\leq\iint_{Q_{T}}\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{t}\cdot\nabla v^{m}\text{sgn}(v_{t})+\iint_{Q_{T}}|v_{t}|\tilde{\Phi}+\iint_{Q_{T}}v\tilde{\Phi}_{t}(p)\text{sgn}(v_{t})-\tilde{\lambda}\iint_{Q_{T}}v|p_{t}|

\displaystyle\leq C+C\iint_{Q_{T}}a|v_{t}|-\tilde{\lambda}\iint_{Q_{T}}v|p_{t}|

\displaystyle\leq C+C\iint_{Q_{T}}a|v_{t}|,

We will now use (

.
To this end, we differentiate (

) in

, for some

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(av_{x_{i}})

\displaystyle=a\partial_{t}v_{x_{i}}+\partial_{t}av_{x_{i}}=(a\partial_{t}v)_{x_{i}}-a_{x_{i}}\partial_{t}v+\partial_{t}av_{x_{i}}

Performing further standard manipulations on the first two terms yields,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(av_{x_{i}})

\displaystyle=\Delta(mv^{m-1}v_{x_{i}})+\nabla\cdot(\boldsymbol{\gamma}mv^{m-1}v_{x_{i}})-(\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{\gamma})(v^{m})_{x_{i}}+

\displaystyle\quad+\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{x_{i}}\cdot\nabla v^{m}+v_{x_{i}}\tilde{\Phi}(p)+v\tilde{\Phi}(p)_{x_{i}}-a_{x_{i}}\partial_{t}v+\partial_{t}av_{x_{i}}.

Next, we multiply by

and use Kato’s inequality to find,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(a|v_{x_{i}}|)

\displaystyle\leq\Delta(mv^{m-1}|v_{x_{i}}|)+\nabla\cdot(\boldsymbol{\gamma}mv^{m-1}|v_{x_{i}}|)-(\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{\gamma})(v^{m})_{x_{i}}\text{sgn}(v_{x_{i}})+

\displaystyle\quad+\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{x_{i}}\cdot\nabla v^{m}\text{sgn}(v_{x_{i}})+|v_{x_{i}}|\tilde{\Phi}(p)+v\tilde{\Phi}(p)_{x_{i}}\text{sgn}(v_{x_{i}})-a_{x_{i}}\partial_{t}v\ \text{sgn}(v_{x_{i}})+\partial_{t}a|v_{x_{i}}|.

Now we integrate over
Q_{T}
. Note that, since

is compactly supported, the first two terms on the righthand side disappear, so we obtain,

\displaystyle\iint_{Q_{T}}\partial_{t}(a|v_{x_{i}}|)

\displaystyle\leq\iint_{Q_{T}}-(\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{\gamma})(v^{m})_{x_{i}}\text{sgn}(v_{x_{i}})+\iint_{Q_{T}}\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{x_{i}}\cdot\nabla v^{m}\text{sgn}(v_{x_{i}})+\iint_{Q_{T}}|v_{x_{i}}|\tilde{\Phi}(p)

\displaystyle\quad+\iint_{Q_{T}}v\tilde{\Phi}(p)_{x_{i}}\text{sgn}(v_{x_{i}})-\iint_{Q_{T}}a_{x_{i}}\partial_{t}v\ \text{sgn}(v_{x_{i}})+\iint_{Q_{T}}\partial_{t}a|v_{x_{i}}|.

Our assumptions on the coefficients, together with the estimates (
13
) and (

), imply that the first two terms, as well as the fourth term, on the righthand side are bounded from above, uniformly in
m
. Thus we have,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\iint_{Q_{T}}a|v_{x_{i}}|

\displaystyle\leq C+C\iint_{Q_{T}}a|v_{x_{i}}|+C\iint_{Q_{T}}|\partial_{t}v|\leq C+C\iint_{Q_{T}}a|v_{x_{i}}|,

where to obtain the first inequality we’ve used Assumption
1.1
, and we’ve used (
15
) to obtain the second inequality. Applying Gronwall’s inequality and using Assumption
1.2
yields

\iint_{Q_{T}}a|v_{x_{i}}|\leq C.

The estimates in (
iii
) follow directly from the previous line, the estimate (
15
), the definition of
v
, and Assumption
1.1
.

Finally, we move on to the proof of (
iv
). We note that the inequality (
14
) implies,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}a|v_{t}(x,T)|\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}a|v_{t}(x,0)|\,dx+\tilde{\lambda}\iint_{Q_{T}}v|p_{t}|\leq

\displaystyle C+C\iint_{Q_{T}}a|v_{t}|.

Rearranging, and then using (

1.2
yields,

\tilde{\lambda}\iint_{Q_{T}}v|p_{t}|\leq C+C\iint_{Q_{T}}a|v_{t}|+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}a|v_{t}(x,0)|\,dx\leq C.

(16)

The definitions of
v
and
p
imply

; therefore,

\displaystyle\iint_{Q_{T}}|p_{t}|

\displaystyle=\iint_{Q_{T}\cap\{v<1/2\}}mv^{m-2}|v_{t}|+\iint_{Q_{T}\cap\{v\geq 1/2\}}|p_{t}|

\displaystyle\leq C\iint_{Q_{T}\cap\{v<1/2\}}|v_{t}|+2\iint_{Q_{T}\cap\{v\geq 1/2\}}v|p_{t}|.

The desired estimate thus follows directly from (
15
) and (
16

\displaystyle=a^{-1}\boldsymbol{\gamma}\cdot\nabla|\nabla p|^{2}+|\nabla p|^{2}\boldsymbol{\gamma}\cdot\nabla a^{-1}+(m-1)\boldsymbol{\gamma}\cdot\nabla(a^{-1}pw)

\displaystyle=2a^{-1}\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{i}p_{x_{j}}p_{x_{i},x_{j}}+|\nabla p|^{2}\boldsymbol{\gamma}\cdot\nabla a^{-1}+(m-1)\boldsymbol{\gamma}\cdot\nabla(a^{-1}pw)

\displaystyle\geq-2a^{-1}\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\left(\frac{|p_{x_{i},x_{j}}|^{2}}{8}+2\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{i}^{2}p_{x_{j}}^{2}\right)+|\nabla p|^{2}\boldsymbol{\gamma}\cdot\nabla a^{-1}+(m-1)\boldsymbol{\gamma}\cdot\nabla(a^{-1}pw)

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{1}{4a}\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}|p_{x_{i},x_{j}}|^{2}-C|\nabla p|^{2}+(m-1)\boldsymbol{\gamma}\cdot\nabla(a^{-1}pw),

where the final two inequalities follow from Young’s inequality and Assumption
1.1
, respectively.

We now put together all of our bounds for the terms on the righthand side of (
35
): namely, the previous bound, (
39
), and the estimate
\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{t}\cdot\nabla p\geq-C|\nabla p|
. We obtain,

\displaystyle w_{t}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{3}{4a}\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}|p_{x_{i},x_{j}}|^{2}+2a^{-1}\nabla p\cdot\nabla w-C|\nabla p|-C|\nabla p|^{2}+(\tilde{\Phi}(p))_{t}+

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad+(m-1)\left(\boldsymbol{\gamma}\cdot\nabla(a^{-1}pw)+\Delta\left(a^{-1}pw\right)\right).

Next we use the fact that
\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}|p_{x_{i},x_{j}}|^{2}\geq\frac{1}{d}|\Delta p|^{2}
, along with (

\displaystyle\frac{3}{4a}\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}|p_{x_{i},x_{j}}|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{3}{4d\sup a}(w-\boldsymbol{\gamma}\cdot\nabla p-\tilde{\Phi}(p))^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{4da}\left(w^{2}-2w(\boldsymbol{\gamma}\cdot\nabla p+\tilde{\Phi}(p))+(\boldsymbol{\gamma}\cdot\nabla p+\tilde{\Phi}(p))^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{3}{4da}\left(w^{2}-\frac{1}{2}w^{2}-C|\nabla p|^{2}-C|w|-C\right)

\displaystyle=\tilde{C}w^{2}-C|w|-C|\nabla p|^{2}-C,

where to obtain the second inequality we used Young’s inequality, as well as that, according to Assumption
1.1
and Lemma
2.1
(
i
), we have
\|\boldsymbol{\gamma}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\tilde{\Phi}(p)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C
. We denote
\tilde{C}=\frac{3}{8d\sup a}
. Furthermore, we have

\tilde{\Phi}(p)_{t}=\tilde{\Phi}_{t}(p)+\tilde{\Phi}_{p}(p)p_{t}\geq-C-C|p_{t}|,

where we’ve again used Assumption
1.1
and Lemma
2.1
(
i
) to obtain that
\Phi_{t}(p)
and
\tilde{\Phi}_{p}(p)
are uniformly bounded independently of
m
.

Combining the three previous estimates, and using
|\nabla p|\leq(|\nabla p|^{2}+1)/2
, yields

\displaystyle w_{t}

\displaystyle\geq\tilde{C}w^{2}-C|w|+2a^{-1}\nabla p\cdot\nabla w-C\left(1+|\nabla p|^{2}+|p_{t}|\right)+(m-1)\left(\boldsymbol{\gamma}\cdot\nabla(a^{-1}pw)+\Delta\left(a^{-1}pw\right)\right),

which is exactly
(
34
).
∎

\displaystyle\quad\quad+(m-1)\left(\boldsymbol{\gamma}\cdot\nabla(a^{-1}p|w|_{-})|w|_{-}+\Delta\left(a^{-1}p|w|_{-}\right)|w|_{-}\right),

\displaystyle I_{4}

4
Proof of the main result

We have now gathered all of the ingredients for:

Proof of Theorem
1.6
.

Lemma
2.1

yields that
u_{m}
,
p_{m}
are uniformly bounded in
W^{1,1}(Q_{T})
. By the Rellich-Kondrachov theorem, there exist
u_{\infty},p_{\infty}\in L^{1}(Q_{T})
such that, up to a subsequence,
u_{m}
,
p_{m}
converge to
u_{\infty}
,
p_{\infty}
strongly in
L^{1}(Q_{T})
. In addition, we have that the
u_{m}
,
p_{m}
, converge to
u_{\infty}
,
p_{\infty}
almost everywhere on
Q_{T}
. The estimates of Proposition
3.1
and Proposition
3.4
, and an argument identical to that of
[
4
, Lemma 3.6]
, imply

\nabla p_{m}\rightarrow\nabla p_{\infty}
strongly in
L^{2}(Q_{T})
.

Using the definition of
p_{m}
and rearranging yields,

\displaystyle\frac{u_{m}}{b}p_{m}=\left(\frac{m-1}{m}\right)^{1/(m-1)}p_{m}^{m/(m-1)}.

Taking the limit
m\rightarrow\infty
yields
\frac{u_{\infty}}{b}p_{\infty}=p_{\infty}
holds a.e., or equivalently,

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{u_{\infty}}{b}\right)p_{\infty}=0\text{ a.e.},

as desired. In addition, the uniform boundedness and
L^{1}
convergence of
u_{m}
,
p_{m}
, as well as the previous line, imply, for any bounded function
\phi
,

\displaystyle\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\iint_{Q_{T}}\left|\left(p_{m}\frac{u_{m}}{b}-p_{\infty}\right)\phi\right|=0.

(41)

.

Next, we observe that the equality

\frac{u_{m}}{b}\nabla p_{m}=(m-1)p_{m}\nabla\frac{u_{m}}{b},

together with the uniform boundedness of
u_{m}
and integrability of
\nabla p_{m}
of Lemma
2.1
, implies,

\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\iint_{Q_{T}}\left|p_{m}\nabla\frac{u_{m}}{b}\right|=\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{m-1}\iint_{Q_{T}}\left|\frac{u_{m}}{b}\nabla p_{m}\right|=0.

(42)

Let
\zeta\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,T))
; we shall pass to the limit in the weak formulation

\iint_{Q_{T}}u_{m}\partial_{t}\zeta-\frac{u_{m}}{a}\nabla p_{m}\cdot\nabla\zeta+\frac{u_{m}}{a}\Phi(x,t,p_{m})\zeta\,dx\,dt=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u_{m}^{0}(x)\zeta(x,0)\,dx

(43)

of (
1
). Integrating by parts in the second term on the lefthand side yields

\displaystyle-\iint_{Q_{T}}\frac{u_{m}}{a}\nabla p_{m}\cdot\nabla\zeta

\displaystyle=\iint_{Q_{T}}p_{m}\nabla\cdot\left(\frac{u_{m}}{b}\frac{b}{a}\nabla\zeta\right)

\displaystyle=\iint_{Q_{T}}p_{m}\nabla\frac{u_{m}}{b}\cdot\left(\frac{b}{a}\nabla\zeta\right)+\iint_{Q_{T}}p_{m}\frac{u_{m}}{b}\nabla\cdot\left(\frac{b}{a}\nabla\zeta\right).

Taking the limit and using (
42
), followed by (
41
), gives,

\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}-\iint_{Q_{T}}\frac{u_{m}}{a}\nabla p_{m}\cdot\nabla\zeta=\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\iint_{Q_{T}}p_{m}\frac{u_{m}}{b}\nabla\cdot\left(\frac{b}{a}\nabla\zeta\right)=\iint_{Q_{T}}p_{\infty}\nabla\cdot\left(\frac{b}{a}\nabla\zeta\right).

Upon taking
m\rightarrow\infty
in (
43
), the previous line,
the convergence properties of
u_{m}
and
p_{m}
, and Assumption
1.2
yield,

\iint_{Q_{T}}u_{\infty}\partial_{t}\zeta-\iint_{Q_{T}}\nabla p_{\infty}\cdot\left(\frac{b}{a}\nabla\zeta\right)+\frac{u_{\infty}}{a}\Phi(x,t,p_{\infty})\zeta\,dx\,dt=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u^{0}(x)\zeta(x,0)\,dx,

which means
(u_{\infty},p_{\infty})
satisfies (
4
) in the weak sense.

Next we establish the complementarity relation (
6
). To this end, the PDE (
5
) for the pressure
p_{m}
yields, for any

\zeta\in C^{\infty}_{c}(Q_{T})
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m-1}\iint_{Q_{T}}\left(\partial_{t}p_{m}-\frac{|\nabla p_{m}|^{2}}{a}\right)\zeta

\displaystyle=-\iint_{Q_{T}}\nabla\left(\zeta\frac{p_{m}}{b}\right)\cdot\frac{b}{a}\nabla p_{m}+\iint_{Q_{T}}p_{m}\frac{\zeta}{a}\left(\Phi(x,t,p_{m})-\partial_{t}b\right).

The convergence of

in

and obtain,

as desired.

∎

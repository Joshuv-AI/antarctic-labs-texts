A Kakeya maximal estimate for regulus strips
Shukun Wu

Abstract
. We prove Kakeya-type estimates for regulus strips. As a result, we obtain another epsilon improvement over the Kakeya conjecture in ℝ 3 \mathbb{R}^{3} , by showing that the regulus strips in the SL 2 {\rm SL}_{2} example are essentially disjoint. We also establish an L p L^{p} inequality regarding a Nikodym-type maximal function in the first Heisenberg group.



1.
Introduction

Recall that a Kakeya set in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a compact set
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
that contains a unit line segment pointing in every direction.
The three-dimensional Kakeya conjecture asserts that every Kakeya set in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
has Hausdorff dimension 3.
After standard discretization arguments, it is a consequence of the following statement:

Conjecture 1.1
.

For all
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
c_{\varepsilon}>0
and
M=M(\varepsilon)>0
such that the following is true for all
\delta\in(0,1)
:
Let
L
be a family of directional
\delta
-separated lines and
E\subset[0,1]^{3}
be a union of
\delta
-balls.
Suppose
|\ell\cap E|\geq\lambda
for all
\ell\in L
.
Then

(1.1)

|E|\geq c_{\varepsilon}\delta^{\varepsilon}\lambda^{M}(\delta^{2}\#L).

When
\lambda\sim 1
, (
1.1
) can be rephrased as the following:
Suppose
{\mathcal{T}}
is a family of
\delta
-tubes pointing at
\delta
-separated directions.
Then
|\cup_{\mathcal{T}}|\gtrapprox\delta^{2}\#{\mathcal{T}}
(
A\lessapprox B
means
A\leq C_{\varepsilon}\delta^{-\varepsilon}B
for all
\varepsilon>0
).
In other words, the
\delta
-tubes in
{\mathcal{T}}
are essentially disjoint.

The best-known result regarding Conjecture
1.1
was obtained by Katz-Zahl
[
KZ19
]
, who showed that a three-dimensional Kakeya set must have Hausdorff dimensions greater than
5/2+\varepsilon_{0}
for some absolute
\varepsilon_{0}>0
.
In the same paper, an enemy scenario of the Kakeya conjecture called the “
\text{SL}_{2}
example” was discovered.
To describe this example, we briefly go through the definition of a regulus strip.

Let
\delta\in(0,1)
.
A regulus strip is the intersection of the
\delta
-neighborhood of a regulus (a quadratic surface in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
doubly-ruled by lines) and the
\delta^{1/2}
-neighborhood of a line contained within that regulus.
In other words, a regulus strip is a
\delta\times\delta^{1/2}\times 1
-plank twisting at a constant speed.
Note that a single regulus strip contains
\sim\delta^{-1/2}
many
\delta
-tubes.

The
\text{SL}_{2}
example, roughly speaking, contains around
\delta^{-3/2}
well-spaced regulus strips.
Hence, it also contains approximately
\delta^{-2}
many
\delta
-tubes.
Thus, if a Kakeya phenomenon is expected for the
\text{SL}_{2}
example, its measure should be
\gtrapprox 1
.
It was shown in
[
KZ19
]
that the
\text{SL}_{2}
example has a measure strictly bigger than
\delta^{1/2}
, which is a key ingredient of their
5/2+\varepsilon_{0}
-bound for the Kakeya conjecture in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

The
\text{SL}_{2}
example motivates the study of the
\text{SL}_{2}
-Kakeya set in
[
FO23
]
and
[
KWZ23
]
.
An
\text{SL}_{2}
-Kakeya set is a Kakeya set whose unit line segments come from the
{\rm{SL}}_{2}
-lines
\mathcal{L}_{\text{SL}_{2}}:=\{\ell_{(a,b,c,d)}:ad-bc=1\}
.
In particular, a certain point-line duality regarding
\text{SL}_{2}
-lines was observed in
[
FO23
]
, and the authors there used tools from Fourier analysis developed in
[
GGG+22
]
to study
\text{SL}_{2}
-Kakeya sets.
We remark that both the
\text{SL}_{2}
example and the
\text{SL}_{2}
-Kakeya set are useful models of “plany” Kakeya sets.
For further discussion, we refer to
[
KZ19
]
and
[
KWZ23
]
.

In this paper, we apply similar tools from Fourier analysis to study regulus strips.
Our result can be viewed as a Kakeya maximal estimate for regulus strips.
As a consequence, we show that the measure of the
\text{SL}_{2}
example is
\gtrapprox 1
, which results in an epsilon improvement over the epsilon improvement of the Kakeya conjecture in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
obtained in
[
KZ19
]
.

To give the formal definition of regulus strips, let us first recall a standard parameterization of non-horizontal lines in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

Definition 1.2
.

For a line
\ell_{(a,b,c,d)}:(a,b,0)+\operatorname{span}(c,d,1)
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, we define
\breve{\ell}:=(a,b,c,d)
to be a point in the parameter space
\mathbb{R}^{4}
.
Generally, if
S=\bigcup\ell\cap[0,1]^{3}
is a union of line segments in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, define
\breve{S}=\bigcup\breve{\ell}
as a set in
\mathbb{R}^{4}
.

For each
\ell\in\mathcal{L}_{\text{SL}_{2}}
and each
p=(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3})\in\ell
, direct calculation shows that all the lines
\ell^{\prime}\in\mathcal{L}_{\text{SL}_{2}}
intersecting
p
are trapped in a plane
P_{p}
containing
\ell
, whose normal direction is
(p_{2},-p_{1},1)
(see (1.3) in
[
KWZ23
]
).
Let
\ell(p)=p+\text{span}(p_{1},p_{2},0)
be the intersection of
P_{p}
and the horizontal plane at height
p_{3}
.
This defines a regulus
R(\ell)
as

(1.2)

R(\ell):=\bigcup_{p\in\ell}\ell(p).

The regulus
R(\ell)
is a double-ruled surface.
The family of lines
\{\ell(p):p\in\ell\}
forms one ruling of
R(\ell)
.
Note that
\{\ell^{\prime}:\breve{\ell}^{\prime}\in\operatorname{span}(\breve{\ell})\}
forms another ruling of
R(\ell)
(just need to check that such an
\ell^{\prime}
intersects
\ell(p)
for all
p\in\ell
).

Definition 1.3

(Regulus strip)
.

Let
\ell\in\mathcal{L}_{\text{SL}_{2}}
.
Define a
regulus strip

S=S(\ell):=N_{\delta}(R(\ell))\cap N_{\delta^{1/2}}(\ell)\cap[0,1]^{3}
, which is a twisted plank of dimensions
\delta\times\delta^{1/2}\times 1
.

Since
\{\ell^{\prime}:\breve{\ell}^{\prime}\in\operatorname{span}(\breve{\ell})\}
forms a ruling of
R(\ell)
,
\breve{S}(\ell)
is the
\delta
-neighborhood of the
\delta^{1/2}
line segment
\{(1+u)\breve{\ell}:|u|\leq\delta^{1/2}\}
in the parameter space
\mathbb{R}^{4}
.
In other words,
\breve{S}(\ell)
is a
\delta
-tube of length
\delta^{1/2}
.

After suitable affine transformations, we only need to focus on the following family of regulus strips

(1.3)

\bar{\mathcal{S}}:=\{S(\ell_{(a,b,c,d)}):a,d\sim 1,ad-bc=1\}.

The next definition captures a non-concentration property of the regulus strips.

Definition 1.4
.

Let
\mathcal{S}\subset\bar{\mathcal{S}}
be a family of regulus strips and let
W=\bigcup_{S\in\mathcal{S}}\breve{S}\subset\mathbb{R}^{4}
be a union of
\delta
-tubes of length
\delta^{1/2}
.
We say
\mathcal{S}
obeys the
two-dimensional ball condition
if for any
r\in[\delta,1]
and any four-dimensional
r
-ball
B_{r}
,

(1.4)

|W\cap B_{r}|\delta^{-4}\leq 100(r\delta^{-1})^{2}.

Note that when
W
is a general union of
\delta
-balls, (
1.4
) is the non-concentration inequality that forces
W
to obey a standard two-dimensional ball condition.
Moreover, it is also the minimal requirement for a family of regulus strips
\mathcal{S}
to be essentially disjoint, that is,
|\cup_{\mathcal{S}}|\gtrapprox(\delta^{3/2}\#\mathcal{S})
.

To see this, suppose
\mathcal{S}
is a family of regulus strips that fails (
1.4
).
Then there exists an absolute
\varepsilon>0
and an
r
-ball
B_{r}
such that
|W\cap B_{r}|\delta^{-4}\geq\delta^{-\varepsilon}(r\delta^{-1})^{2}
for some absolute
\varepsilon>0
.
Assume
\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{S}(B_{r}):=\{S\in\mathcal{S}:\breve{S}\cap B_{r}\not=\varnothing\}
.
We will show
|\cup_{\mathcal{S}}|\lesssim\delta^{\varepsilon}(\delta^{3/2}\#\mathcal{S})
.

If
r\geq\delta^{1/2}
, then
\mathcal{S}\subset\{S\in\bar{\mathcal{S}}:\breve{S}\subset 2B_{r}\}
.
This shows that
\cup_{\mathcal{S}}
is contained in an
r
-tube, so
|\cup_{\mathcal{S}}|\lesssim r^{2}
.
Note that
\#\mathcal{S}=\#\mathcal{S}(B_{r})\gtrsim\delta^{-\varepsilon}(r\delta^{-1})^{2}\delta^{1/2}
.
Thus, we get
(\delta^{3/2}\#\mathcal{S})\gtrsim\delta^{-\varepsilon}r^{2}\gtrsim\delta^{-\varepsilon}|\cup_{\mathcal{S}}|
.
If
r\leq\delta^{1/2}
, then
\cup_{\mathcal{S}}
is contained in
N_{2r}(S)
, where
S\in\mathcal{S}
is any regulus strip.
This implies
|\cup_{\mathcal{S}}|\lesssim r\delta^{1/2}
.
Note that
\#\mathcal{S}=\#\mathcal{S}(B_{r})\gtrsim\delta^{-\varepsilon}r\delta^{-1}
.
Thus, we have
(\delta^{3/2}\#\mathcal{S})\gtrsim\delta^{-\varepsilon}r\delta^{1/2}\gtrsim\delta^{-\varepsilon}|\cup_{\mathcal{S}}|
.

Now we state the main result of the paper.

Theorem 1.5
.

Suppose
\mathcal{S}\subset\bar{\mathcal{S}}
is a collection of regulus strips satisfying the two-dimensional ball condition (
1.4
).
Let
Y:\mathcal{S}\to[0,1]^{3}
be
a shading such that
Y(S)\subset S\cap[0,1]^{3}
for any
S\in\mathcal{S}
.
Let
\lambda\in(0,1]
.
Suppose
|Y(S)|\geq\lambda|S|
for all
S\in\mathcal{S}
.
Then for any
\varepsilon>0
,

(1.5)

\Big|\bigcup_{S\in\mathcal{S}}Y(S)\Big|\geq c_{\varepsilon}\delta^{\varepsilon}\lambda^{6}\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}}|S|.

The
\text{SL}_{2}
example discovered in
[
KZ19
]
is a family of regulus strips
\mathcal{S}
with
\#\mathcal{S}\approx\delta^{-3/2}
so that in the parameter space, each
\delta^{1/2}
-ball in
N_{\delta^{1/2}}Z(ad-bc=1)
contains
\approx 1
many
\breve{S}
.
Note that, up to a factor
\approx 1
, the
\text{SL}_{2}
example obeys the two-dimensional ball condition (
1.4
).
Therefore, as a direct corollary of Theorem
1.5
:

Corollary 1.6
.

The
\rm{SL}_{2}
example has measure
\gtrapprox 1
.

As a result, there is another epsilon improvement over the epsilon improvement of the three-dimensional Kakeya conjecture in
[
KZ19
]
.

Finally, let us discuss an application of Theorem
1.5
in the first Heisenberg group.
One feature of the
\text{SL}_{2}
lines
\mathcal{L}_{\text{SL}_{2}}
is that they coincide with the horizontal lines in the first Heisenberg group (see
[
FO23
]
).
In particular, for each
\ell\in\mathcal{L}_{\text{SL}_{2}}
, the regulus strip
S(\ell)
is essentially the
\delta^{1/2}
-neighborhood of the horizontal line
\ell
under the Korányi metric. See
[
Liu22
]
.

Therefore, Theorem
1.5
also gives a Nikodym maximal estimate for horizontal lines in the first Heisenberg group.

Definition 1.7
.

Let
T
be the
\delta
-neighborhood of an arbitrary horizontal unit line segment contained in the unit ball, under the Korányi metric in the first Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}
.
Define a Nikodym maximal function

(1.6)

Mf(z)=\sup_{z\in T}\frac{1}{|T|}\int_{T}|f(z^{\prime})|dz^{\prime}

for any function
f:\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{C}
.

By a standard duality, Theorem
1.5
has the following corollary.

Corollary 1.8
.

Let
M
be the Nikodym maximal operator defined in (
1.6
).
Then for any
\varepsilon>0
,
\|Mf\|_{p}\leq C_{\varepsilon}\delta^{-\varepsilon}\|f\|_{p}
when
p\geq 6
.

Proof.

It suffices to consider
f={\bf{1}}_{E}
, where
E
is a union of disjoint Heisenberg
\delta
-balls contained in the unit ball.
For each point
z\in\mathbb{H}
, let
z=(x,y,t)
be its standard coordinate.
Using the foliation of
\mathbb{H}
by vertical planes through the vertical axis and by the triangle, it suffices to show that

(1.7)

\int|M^{\ast}{\bf{1}}_{E}(0,y,t)|^{6}dydt\lessapprox|E|.

Here
M^{\ast}
is the maximal operator restricted to horizontal lines that are transverse to the plane
\{x=0\}
.
Note that (
1.7
) boils down to the weak-type estimate

(1.8)

\lambda^{6}|F_{\lambda}|\lessapprox|E|,

where
F_{\lambda}=\{(y,t):|M^{\ast}{\bf{1}}_{E}(0,y,t)|\geq\lambda\}
.

Note that, after discretization,
F_{\lambda}
can be viewed as a union of horizontal
\delta\times\delta^{2}
-rectangles.
For each such rectangle, choose a regulus strip
S
of dimensions
1\times\delta\times\delta^{2}
(the
\delta
-neighborhood of a horizontal line) such that
|S\cap E|\geq\lambda|S|
.
Denote by
Y(S)=S\cap E
.
It is straightforward to check that the family of regulus strips generated by the set of
\delta\times\delta^{2}
-rectangles from
F_{\lambda}
obeys the two-dimensional ball condition.
By Theorem
1.5
, we have

(1.9)

|E|\geq\Big|\bigcup_{S}Y(S)\Big|\gtrapprox\lambda^{6}\sum_{S}|S|\sim\lambda^{6}|F_{\lambda}|.

This shows (
1.8
) and hence the Corollary.
∎

Let
\mathcal{L}

Lemma 2.1

(
\rm{SL}_{2}
is linear)
.

Lines in
\mathcal{L}_{\text{SL}_{2}}
satisfying
ad\not=0
can be identified as lines in
\mathcal{L}
.

Proof.

For any non-horizontal line
\ell_{(a,b,c,d)}
, let
\ell_{(a,b,c,d)}(t)=(a,b,0)+t(c,d,1)
be its intersection with the horizontal plane at height
t
.
Thus, each line
\ell\in\mathcal{L}_{\text{SL}_{2}}
with
ad\not=0
has the parameterization

(2.1)

\ell(t)=(a+ct,\,b+(1+bc)t/a,\,t).

Choose
t_{0}
and
t_{1}
so that the second entries of
\ell(t_{0})
and
\ell(t_{1})
are
0

1

Hence

(2.2)

t_{0}=-\frac{ab}{1+bc},\hskip 14.22636ptt_{1}=\frac{a-ab}{1+bc}.

Moreover, we have

(2.3)

\ell(t_{0})=(\frac{a}{1+bc},\,0,\,\frac{-ab}{1+bc})\,\,\,\text{ and }\,\,\,\ell(t_{1})=(\frac{a+ac}{1+bc},\,1,\,\frac{a-ab}{1+bc}).

This gives a directional vector

(2.4)

v=\ell(t_{1})-\ell(t_{0})=(\frac{ac}{1+bc},\,1,\,\frac{a}{1+bc}).

Thus, if swapping the second and the third coordinates of
\ell(t_{0})
, we have

(2.5)

\ell(t_{0})=(\frac{a}{1+bc},\,\frac{-ab}{1+bc},\,0)\,\,\,\text{ and }\,\,\,v=(\frac{ac}{1+bc},\,\frac{a}{1+bc},\,1).

This suggests that the line
\ell
can be re-parameterized as
\ell=\ell_{(a^{\prime},b^{\prime},c^{\prime},d^{\prime})}
obeying
a^{\prime}=d^{\prime}
(
a^{\prime}=d^{\prime}=\frac{a}{1+bc}
in (
2.5
)). ∎

Now we can identify a line
\ell_{(a,b,c,a)}\in\mathcal{L}

(a,b,c)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}

(2.6)

\ell_{(a,b,c)}:=\ell_{(a,b,c,a)}.

\mathcal{L}

\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

\bar{\mathcal{S}}

.

Let
\bar{\mathcal{S}}
be the family of regulus strips defined in (

After choosing an appropriate parameterization, each

S(\ell)=\{(a+ct,b+at,t)+u(1,-t,0),\hskip 8.5359ptt\in[0,1],\,\,\,|u|\lesssim\delta^{1/2}\}+B_{\delta}^{3},

where

In particular, if we let

(2.8)

Suppose
\ell
is parameterized as
\ell_{(A,B,C,D)}

.
By (

(2.9)

\displaystyle R(\ell)

\displaystyle=\left\{(A+Cs,B+Ds,s)+r(A+Cs,B+Ds,0):s,r\in\mathbb{R}\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{(\lambda(A+Cs),\lambda(B+Ds),s):s,\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\right\}.

As in Lemma
2.1
, consider the coordinate swap
\Phi(X,Y,Z)=(X,Z,Y)

.
Let

(2.11)

a=\frac{1}{D},\qquad b=-\frac{B}{D},\qquad c=\frac{C}{D}.

The next lemma is about a duality for regulus strips.
Before stating the lemma, we first recalled the point-line duality found in

Let
\ell_{x}\in\mathcal{L}

x=(a,b,c)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
is a point in the parameter space.
Then for any physical point
p=(p_{1},p_{2},t)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, there exists a light ray
\bar{\ell}(p)
with direction
e_{3}(t)
in the parameter space such that the following is true:
p\in\ell_{x}
if and only if
x\in\bar{\ell}(p)
.
In other words,
p\in\ell_{x}
if and only if
\bar{\ell}(p)
is the line
x+\text{span}(e_{3}(t))
.
Thus, each
\ell_{x}\in\mathcal{L}
corresponds to a one-dimensional family of light rays
\{x+\text{span}(e_{3}(t)):t\in[0,1]\}
intersecting
x
.
Consequently, given a family of lines
L=\{\ell_{x}:x\in E\}\subset\mathcal{L}
for some parameter set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
, the horizontal slice
L\cap\{z_{3}=t\}
is the projection
\pi_{t}(E)
, up to a linear transformation depending on
t
.
In
[
FO23
]
, this duality was used to convert the
{\rm{SL}}_{2}
-Kakeya problem to a restricted projection problem.

Note that each line
\ell_{x}
corresponds to only one single point
x
in the parameter space.
The situation for regulus strips here is a bit different.
For each regulus strip
S(\ell_{x})
, the horizontal slice
S(\ell_{x})\cap\{z_{3}=t\}
corresponds to a
\delta\times\delta^{1/2}
-tube in the parameter space, that depends not only on
x
, but also on the parameter
t
.
In the next lemma, we make the following crucial observation: the
\delta\times\delta^{1/2}
-tube corresponding to the horizontal slice
S(\ell_{x})\cap\{z_{3}=t\}
is indeed contained in a “conical wave packet” containing
x
with direction
e_{3}(t)
.
As a consequence,
|X_{t}(\mathcal{S})|
is essentially the same as the area of the union of certain conical wave packets determined by the set
E(\mathcal{S})
and the projection
\pi_{t}
.
This observation allows us to use the tools developed in
[
GGG+22
]
to study regulus strips.

Lemma 2.5

(Regulus strips vs. wave packets)
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be a set of
\delta
-separated
(\delta,\rho)
-regulus strips and let

be its corresponding parameter set.
For each

and each

T_{x}=T_{x}(t):=x+\{y_{1}e_{3}+y_{2}(e_{3}/|e_{3}|)^{\prime}+y_{3}e_{3}\wedge(e_{3}/|e_{3}|)^{\prime}:|y_{1}|\leq 1,|y_{2}|\leq\rho,|y_{3}|\leq\delta\}

(2.21)

|X_{t}(\mathcal{S})|\sim\Big|\pi_{t}\Big(\bigcup_{x\in E(\mathcal{S})}T_{x}(t)\Big)\Big|\sim\Big|\bigcup_{x\in E(\mathcal{S})}T_{x}(t)\Big|.

Remark 2.6

Note that

is a union of

-tubes.
Lemma

Proof of Lemma
2.5
.

Denote by

the normalized vector

\xi(t)=\frac{e_{3}}{|e_{3}|}=\frac{1}{(1+t^{2}+t^{4})^{1/2}}(-t,t^{2},1)

so that

(2.23)

\xi^{\prime}(t)=\frac{1}{(1+t^{2}+t^{4})^{3/2}}(t^{4}-1,2t+t^{3},-t-2t^{3}).

For

, which is a horizontal line segment of length
\rho
.
Then

is the intersection of the regulus strip
S_{\rho}(\ell_{x})\in\mathcal{S}
with the horizontal plane
\{z_{3}=t\}
.
Notice that

can be expressed as

(2.24)

\{\big((x+u(0,-t,t^{-1}))\cdot e_{1},\,\,(x+u(0,-t,t^{-1}))\cdot e_{2}\big),\,\,\,|u|\leq\rho\}.

Since

(2.25)

P_{x}^{\prime}=\pi_{t}(\{x+u(0,-t,t^{-1}),\,\,\,|u|\leq\rho\}).

Thus,

if and only if
P_{x}^{\prime}\cap P_{y}^{\prime}\not=\varnothing
.
In other words,

if and only if
P_{x}^{\prime}\cap P_{y}^{\prime}\not=\varnothing
.

The preimage of

regarding
\pi_{t}
is an infinite strip whose cross-section is a
\delta\times\rho
-rectangle.
Denote its intersection with the unit ball by

, which is a
1\times\rho\times\delta
-plank whose longest direction is

.
To prove the lemma, it suffices to show that the second-longest direction of

is parallel to
\xi^{\prime}=(e_{3}/|e_{3}|)^{\prime}

Indeed, let the direction of the second-longest side of

be a vector

so that it is orthogonal to
\xi(t)
.
By (
2.25
),
P_{x}^{\prime}
is a line segment with direction

.
Thus, there exists a number
\alpha
such that

can be parameterized as

.
To find
\alpha

and obtain

(2.26)

By (
2.23

\xi^{\prime}
(we will explain this coincidence later in Section

is
\xi^{\prime}=(e_{3}/|e_{3}|)^{\prime}

Critical threshold for weakly interacting log-correlated focusing Gibbs measures
Damiano Greco, Tadahiro Oh, Liying Tao, and Leonardo Tolomeo

Abstract
. We study log-correlated Gibbs measures on the d d -dimensional torus with weakly interacting focusing quartic potentials whose coupling constants tend to 0 0 as we remove regularization. In particular, we exhibit a phase transition for this model by identifying a critical threshold, separating the weakly and strongly coupling regimes; in the weakly coupling regime, we show that the frequency-truncated measures converge to the base Gaussian measure (possibly with a renormalized L 2 L^{2} -cutoff), whereas, in the strongly coupling regime, we prove non-convergence of the frequency-truncated measures, even up to a subsequence. Our result answers an open question posed by Brydges and Slade (1996).

4.1

(see also (
4.9
)), and Lemma
4.2
,
we have

(4.13)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbb{E}&\Big[\min{((1-\delta)\lambda_{N}Q(\Theta^{0}),L)}\cdot\mathbf{1}_{\{|\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\,:(Y_{N}+\Theta^{0})^{2}:\,dx|\,\leq K_{N}\}}\Big]\\
&\geq C\lambda_{N}\alpha^{2}_{M,N}M^{d}-C(\delta)\lambda_{N}\alpha_{M,N}\mathbb{E}[\zeta^{2}_{M}]\|f_{M}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\\
&\geq C^{\prime}\lambda_{N}M^{d}(\log M)^{2}-C^{\prime\prime}(\delta)\lambda_{N}(\log M)^{2}.\end{split}

for any small
\delta>0

and

N\geq M\gg 1
,
provided that

L\gg\lambda_{N}\alpha^{2}_{M,N}Q(f_{M})\sim\lambda_{N}\alpha^{2}_{M,N}M^{d}
.
Thus, it follows
from
the variational formula
(Lemma
2.6
),
(
4.12
), (
4.13
),
Lemmas
2.7
and
2.4
,
and
(
4.8
)
that

(4.14)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\log{\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\Big[\exp\big(\min{(\lambda_{N}R_{N}(u),L)}\cdot\mathbf{1}_{\{|\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\,:u_{N}^{2}:\,dx|\leq K_{N}\}}\big)\Big]}\\
&\quad\geq\mathbb{E}\bigg[\min{(\lambda_{N}R_{N}(Y+\Theta^{0}),L)}\cdot\mathbf{1}_{\{|\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\,:(Y_{N}+\Theta^{0})^{2}:\,dx|\,\leq K_{N}\}}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\|\theta^{0}(t)\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}dt\bigg]\\
&\quad\gtrsim\lambda_{N}M^{d}(\log M)^{2}-C(\delta)\lambda_{N}(\log M)^{2}-C^{\prime}M^{d}\log M-C^{\prime\prime}(\delta)\end{split}

for any small
\delta>0

and

N\geq M\gg 1
,
provided
L\gg\lambda_{N}\alpha^{2}_{M,N}M^{d}.

In view of (
4.11
),
by setting
M=N
in (
4.14
),
taking
L\to\infty
,
and then
N\to\infty
,
(
4.10
) follows from (
4.14
),
provided that
\lambda^{*}
is sufficiently large.

\bullet

Case 2:

\sup_{N\in\mathbb{N}}(\log N)^{-1}K_{N}=\infty
.

In this case, from (
1.14
), we have

(4.15)

\displaystyle\lambda_{N}\geq C\lambda^{*}K_{N}^{-1},\quad N\in\mathbb{N},

for some
C>0
.
In this case, we have
K_{N}\gg\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}Y_{N}^{2}(\omega)dx\sim C(\omega)\log N
.
Namely, we can take a drift
\theta

such that
\Theta
defined in (
2.5
)
has a much larger
L^{2}
-norm than that of
Y_{N}
.
This, in particular, allows
us to choose a
deterministic
drift
such that

\lambda_{N}Q(\Theta_{N})\gg\|\Theta_{N}\|_{H^{\frac{d}{2}}}^{2}

to drive the desired divergence;
see (
4.18
) and (
4.19
)
(with
M=N
).

Given
M\gg 1
,
let
f_{M}
be
as in (
4.1
) and
we define a drift
\theta_{\gamma}
by

(4.16)

\displaystyle\theta_{\gamma}(t)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\gamma K_{M}}\,\langle\nabla\rangle^{\frac{d}{2}}f_{M},

for some small
\gamma>0
is independent of
M
(to be chosen later).
We then set

(4.17)

\displaystyle\Theta_{\gamma}=\int_{0}^{1}\langle\nabla\rangle^{-\frac{d}{2}}\theta_{\gamma}(t)\,dt=\sqrt{\gamma K_{M}}f_{M}.

From the frequency support of
f_{M}
, we have

\displaystyle\pi_{N}\Theta_{\gamma}=\Theta_{\gamma}

for any
N\geq M\gg 1
.
From (
4.17
), Cauchy-Schwarz’s inequality (in time),
(
4.16
), Bernstein’s inequality
(with
\supp\widehat{f}_{M}\subset\{|n|\leq M\}
),
and Lemma
4.1
, we have

(4.18)

\displaystyle\|\Theta_{\gamma}\|_{H^{\frac{d}{2}}}^{2}\leq\int^{1}_{0}\|\theta_{\gamma}(t)\|^{2}_{L_{x}^{2}}dt\lesssim\gamma K_{M}M^{d}

for any
M\gg 1
.
From (
4.5
) and Lemma
4.1
, we also have

(4.19)

\displaystyle Q(\Theta_{\gamma})\sim\gamma^{2}K_{M}^{2}M^{d}

for any
M\gg 1
.

We claim that by choosing
\gamma>0
sufficiently small, we have

(4.20)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\bigg(\Big|\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}:{Y_{N}^{2}}:+2Y_{N}\Theta_{\gamma}+\Theta_{\gamma}^{2}\,dx\Big|\leq K_{N}\bigg)\geq\frac{1}{2}

for any
N=M\gg 1
.
Then,
from
the variational formula
(Lemma
2.6
),
(
4.12
)
(with
\Theta^{0}
replaced with
\Theta_{\gamma}
),
(
4.18
), (
4.19
),
and Lemmas
2.7
and
2.4
,
we have

(4.21)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\log{\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\Big[\exp\big(\min{(\lambda_{N}R_{N}(u),L)}\cdot\mathbf{1}_{\{|\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\,:u_{N}^{2}:\,dx|\leq K_{N}\}}\big)\Big]}\\
&\quad\geq\mathbb{E}\bigg[\min{(\lambda_{N}R_{N}(Y+\Theta_{\gamma}),L)}\cdot\mathbf{1}_{\{|\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\,:(Y_{N}+\Theta_{\gamma})^{2}:\,dx|\,\leq K_{N}\}}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\|\theta_{\gamma}(t)\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}dt\bigg]\\
&\quad\gtrsim\lambda_{N}\gamma^{2}K^{2}_{M}M^{d}-C\gamma K_{M}M^{d}-C^{\prime}(\delta)\end{split}

for any small
\delta>0

and

N=M\gg 1
,
provided

L\gg\lambda_{N}\gamma^{2}K^{2}_{M}M^{d}
.
In view of (
4.15
),
by setting
M=N
in (
4.21
),
taking
L\to\infty
,
and then
N\to\infty
,
(
4.10
) follows from (
4.21
),
provided that
\lambda^{*}=\lambda^{*}(\gamma)
is sufficiently large.

It remains to prove (
4.20
).
From Lemma
2.7
, we have

(4.22)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbb{E}&\bigg[\Big|\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}:{Y_{N}^{2}}:+2Y_{N}\Theta_{\gamma}+(\Theta_{\gamma})^{2}dx\Big|^{2}\bigg]\\
&\lesssim 1+\mathbb{E}\bigg[\Big|\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}Y_{N}\Theta_{\gamma}\,dx\Big|^{2}\bigg]+\mathbb{E}\bigg[\Big(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\Theta^{2}_{\gamma}\,dx\Big)^{2}\bigg]\\
\end{split}

From
(
4.17
), (
2.3
), and (
4.4
) in Lemma
4.1
, we have

(4.23)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\Big|\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}Y_{N}\Theta_{\gamma}\,dx\Big|^{2}\bigg]&=\gamma K_{M}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\Big|\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}Y_{N}f_{M}dx\Big|^{2}\bigg]=\gamma K_{M}\sum_{|n|\leq M}\langle n\rangle^{-d}|\widehat{f}_{M}(n)|^{2}\\
&\lesssim\gamma K_{M}M^{-d}.\end{split}

From (
4.17
) and (
4.2
), we have

(4.24)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg[\Big(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\Theta^{2}_{\gamma}\,dx\Big)^{2}\bigg]=\gamma^{2}K^{2}_{M}\|f_{M}\|^{4}_{L^{2}}\sim\gamma^{2}K^{2}_{M}

for any
M\gg 1
.
Hence, by applying Chebyshev’s inequality, (
4.22
),
(
4.23
), and (
4.24
), we obtain

(4.25)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbb{P}&\bigg(\Big|\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(:{Y_{N}^{2}}:+2Y_{N}\Theta_{\gamma}+(\Theta_{\gamma})^{2})dx\Big|\geq K_{N}\bigg)\\
&\lesssim\frac{1}{K^{2}_{N}}+\gamma\frac{K_{M}}{M^{d}K^{2}_{N}}+\gamma^{2}\frac{K^{2}_{M}}{K^{2}_{N}}.\end{split}

Finally,
recalling that
K_{N}\gtrsim\log N\to\infty
as
N\to\infty
,
the bound
(
4.20
) follows from
setting
M=N
sufficiently large and choosing
\gamma>0
sufficiently small
in (
4.25
).

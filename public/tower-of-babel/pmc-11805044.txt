Comparative Evaluation of Efficacy of Self-Assembling Peptide (P11-4), Bioactive Glass, and Arginine Bicarbonate Remineralizing Agents on Simulated Carious Lesion
Authors: Rashmi P Yadahalli, Yazeed Abdullah Alotaibi, Ravinder Singh, Mohammed Najmuddin, Mohamed Tharwat Salama, Radha Prasanna Dalai, Noura Alessa, Vardarajula Venkat Ramaiah
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTObjectives:Evaluation of effectiveness of bioactive glass, self-assembling peptide (P11-4), and arginine bicarbonate remineralizing agents on simulated carious lesion.Material and Methods:This research was conducted using 40 human premolar teeth that had just been removed. Remineralization of demineralized teeth was carried out three times a day for 14 days for 5 minutes using the appropriate remineralizing agents (Group A: calcium sodium phosphosilicate (bioactive glass), Group B: self-assembling peptide (P11-4), Group C: arginine bicarbonate remineralizing agents, and control group: Group D: deionized water). Using the Vickers hardness number, the degree of demineralization and remineralization was assessed.Results:In every group, there was a drop in microhardness from the baseline to demineralization. In all three test groups, the shift in microhardness values from demineralization to remineralization was shown to be statistically significant.Conclusion:Higher remineralizing capacity was found in bioactive glass and self-assembling peptide (P11-4), which can be employed therapeutically to treat early carious lesions.

INTRODUCTION

White spot lesion on the surface of the tooth is the first visible indication of dental caries for the start of enamel demineralization.[1] The process of dental caries is dynamic. Several remineralizing agents can restore the initial carious lesion, often known as the white spot lesion, to its original state. This lessens the frequency of dental cavities and the financial strain on sufferers.

Many nonfluoridated remineralizing agents, including arginine bicarbonate, xylitol, casein phosphopeptide amorphous calcium phosphate (CPP-ACFP), CCP-ACFP, bioactive glass (BAG-Novamin), nanohydroxyapatite (nHAp), and self-assembling peptide (P11–4) were tried in an effort to overcome the negative effects of fluoride.[123]

Novamin, often known as Bioglass, is a commercially available kind of bioactive glass (BAG).[4] Arginine calcium carbonate particles, which can cling to the surface of minerals, are found in the amino acid bicarbonate. While the release of carbonate may cause a modest local pH rise, the calcium that is released when the calcium carbonate dissolves can be used to remineralize the mineral.[3]

The present in vitro study was done to evaluate the effectiveness of bioactive glass, tri-calcium phosphate, and ozone remineralizing agents on simulated carious lesion.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

For the in vitro investigation, 40 recently extracted human premolar teeth that were free of pathologies and intended for orthodontic use were used. Teeth were kept in a buffer solution of acetate (75 mmol/L) containing calcium chloride (2.0 mmol/L) and trisodium phosphate (2.0 mmol/L) for 5 days at a pH of 4.5 in order to induce artificial carious lesion/demineralization on buccal surfaces. Later demineralized 40 teeth were categorized into four groups for remineralization with 10 samples for each group.

Remineralization of demineralized teeth was carried out three times a day for 15 days, for 5 minutes, using the appropriate remineralizing agents (Group A: calcium sodium phosphosilicate (bioactive glass), Group B: self-assembling peptide (P11-4), Group C: ozone remineralizing agents, and control group: Group D: deionized water). Using the Vickers hardness number, the degree of demineralization and remineralization was assessed.

Using the SPSS version 23.0, the acquired values were tabulated and statistically assessed using the ANOVA and Tukey HSD test with P < 0.05.

All groups showed a drop in microhardness from baseline to demineralization, and this decline was determined to be statistically significant. The demineralization among the entire group was statistical insignificant [Table 1].

DISCUSSION

The mineral alterations in the tooth hard tissue’s structure are necessary for the remineralization process to occur[1] via producing certain surface coatings that act as diffusion barriers and decrease the solubility of the enamel via mineral deposition within the enamel crystals; the remineralizing agents supply the necessary ions.[5] If the pH is neutralized and there are enough calcium and phosphate ions available in the oral environment, demineralization can be reversed.[6]

Joshi et al. discovered that nHAp, f-TCP, and BAG Novamin all exhibited significant RML.[4] In order to remineralize the enamel surface on fake caries, Patil et al.[7] evaluated the effectiveness of casein phosphopeptide-amorphous calcium phosphate fluoride (CPP-ACPF), casein phosphopeptide-amorphous calcium phosphate (CPP-ACP), and tricalcium phosphate fluoride (TCP-F). Higher remineralization was noted while using the evaluated items.

Following treatment with remineralizing chemicals [CPP-ACP, 0.21% sodium fluoride–tricalcium phosphate (f-TCP), and calcium sodium phosphosilicate (CSP)], Balakrishnan et al.[8] reported higher Vicker’s hardness number values of the enamel specimens, which is consistent with our findings. By mimicking the body’s natural mineralizing characteristics and influencing cell signals, bioactive glass, or Bioglass®, works as a biomimetic mineralizer to aid in the repair of tissue structure and function.[9]

The effectiveness of self-assembling peptide, bioactive glass, and ozone remineralizing agents was evaluated by Alessa N et al.[10] on an artificial carious lesion. They came to the conclusion that self-assembling peptide and bioactive glass had greater remineralizing capacities. Shah et al. evaluated the effectiveness of ozone, nanohydroxyapatite (nHAp), and tricalcium phosphate as remineralizing agents on artificial carious lesions. They came to the conclusion that tricalcium phosphate and nanohydroxyapatite (nHAp) had better remineralizing capabilities and might be employed to treat early carious lesions.[11] When compared to fluoride varnish, Alessa N. et al.[12] found that the tooth remineralizing agent SDF had stronger antibacterial activity against S. mutans.

The study’s limited sample size was a negative aspect. To assess the effectiveness of remineralizing drugs, higher sample sizes and additional clinical investigations are required.

CONCLUSION

Bioactive glass and self-assembling peptide (P11-4) had higher remineralizing capacity, which can be used clinically to treat early carious lesions.

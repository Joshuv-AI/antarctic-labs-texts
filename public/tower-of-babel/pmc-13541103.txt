Single-session combined mitral transcatheter edge-to-edge repair and left atrial appendage occlusion using a steerable introducer after a mitral repair-oriented transseptal puncture: a case report

Introduction

Combined mitral transcatheter edge-to-edge repair (M-TEER) and left atrial appendage occlusion (LAAO) may be considered in patients with severe mitral regurgitation (MR) and atrial fibrillation (AF) who are at high surgical risk. In elderly and frail patients, a staged strategy may increase the cumulative burden of anaesthesia, vascular access, and hospitalization. However, a key technical limitation of the one-stop strategy is that the transseptal puncture selected for mitral repair is often not ideal for appendage access. We report a combined single-session M-TEER and LAAO procedure in which a steerable introducer restored coaxial alignment and enabled efficient device delivery despite a transseptal puncture optimized for mitral repair.

Case presentation

An 80-year-old woman was referred after two episodes of acute heart failure related to severe functional MR and a recent hospitalization for severe anaemia requiring blood transfusion. Her medical history included permanent AF treated with a non-vitamin K antagonist oral anticoagulant, chronic kidney disease (stage 3), and prior hysterectomy. At presentation, she reported marked exertional dyspnoea and reduced exercise tolerance. Physical examination revealed an apical systolic murmur and mild signs of volume overload. Laboratory tests showed haemoglobin 8.8 g/dL (reference range 12.1–15.1 g/dL), serum creatinine 1.5 mg/dL (reference range 0.50–0.95 mg/dL), estimated glomerular filtration rate 33 mL/min/1.73 m2 (reference value ≥60 mL/min/1.73 m2), and NT-proBNP 4200 pg/mL (non-acute reference value <125 pg/mL). The anaemia work-up did not identify an active bleeding source, and the condition was interpreted as chronic multifactorial anaemia. Electrocardiography demonstrated permanent AF with a ventricular rate of 90 bpm, while chest radiography showed mild pulmonary vascular congestion and small bilateral pleural effusions. The patient was receiving edoxaban 30 mg once daily, furosemide 125 mg/day, bisoprolol 5 mg/day, spironolactone 50 mg/day, and empagliflozin 10 mg/day. Based on her documented baseline clinical and functional status, frailty was retrospectively graded as 5/9 on the Clinical Frailty Scale, corresponding to mild frailty. Her vulnerability was further characterized by severe underweight status (body mass index 14.8 kg/m2), poor nutritional reserve, limited functional capacity, and multiple comorbidities. Transthoracic and transoesophageal echocardiography (TEE) showed a normal left ventricular size with preserved systolic function [left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) 55%], a moderately enlarged left atrium (volume index 45 mL/m2), a normal right ventricle, and moderate tricuspid regurgitation. Mitral valve assessment demonstrated leaflet tethering related to atrial enlargement, consistent with atrial functional MR, with a severe central-medial regurgitant jet. Quantitative measurements confirmed severe MR [effective regurgitant orifice area (EROA) 45 mm2; regurgitant volume 58 mL], a mitral valve area of 6.5 cm2, and a posterior leaflet length of 12 mm. The fossa ovalis height was 45 mm. The left atrial appendage had a single-lobed chicken-wing morphology and measured 19 × 21 mm. Following multidisciplinary Heart Team evaluation, the patient was considered unsuitable for surgery because of advanced age, marked frailty, recurrent heart failure, chronic kidney disease, and recent transfusion-requiring anaemia. Given the coexistence of severe symptomatic MR, elevated thromboembolic risk (CHA2DS2-VASc score 4), and increased bleeding risk (HAS-BLED score 3), together with concerns regarding the long-term safety of oral anticoagulation, a combined percutaneous strategy consisting of M-TEER and LAAO was deemed the most appropriate therapeutic approach. The procedure was performed under general anaesthesia with fluoroscopic and TEE guidance via right femoral venous access. Transseptal puncture was performed with a Brockenbrough needle (Abbott Laboratories, Illinois, USA), targeting a supero-posterior site in the fossa ovalis according to the short-axis, bicaval, and four-chamber TEE views (Figure 1A). After transseptal access, M-TEER was performed using a MitraClip G4 XTW system (Abbott Laboratories). Leaflet grasping at the A2-P2 scallops achieved a marked reduction in MR and the clip was successfully deployed (Figure 1B–D). LAAO was then undertaken. A pigtail catheter was advanced into the appendage for selective angiography in two orthogonal projections to assess the landing zone and confirm sizing. Because the puncture site had been optimized for mitral repair rather than appendage access, alignment toward the appendage neck was suboptimal (Figure 1E). To overcome this limitation, a TrueSteer Steerable Introducer (Philips) was used. Its steerability allowed controlled distal deflection of ∼45° toward the appendage neck, facilitating coaxial alignment and stable device delivery (Figure 1F). A 24-mm WATCHMAN device was then deployed with optimal positioning on the first attempt. Device position and stability were assessed in multiple TEE views (Figure 1G and H). Colour Doppler and three-dimensional TEE confirmed complete coverage of the appendage ostium without peri-device leak. After satisfactory tug testing, the device was released and final angiography confirmed successful appendage occlusion (Figure 1I–L). The immediate procedural outcome was therefore favourable for both interventions. Representative moving images of M-TEER guidance and result, LAAO deployment, and final post-procedural assessment are provided as Supplementary material online, Videos S1 and S2. At the 6-month follow-up, the patient showed improved functional status, with reduced dyspnoea and New York Heart Association functional class II symptoms. She was discharged on clopidogrel 75 mg once daily, tailored to her recent severe anaemia and bleeding risk. No recurrent bleeding, rehospitalization for heart failure, stroke, or procedure-related complications occurred during the 6-month follow-up.

Discussion

This case illustrates the feasibility of a combined percutaneous strategy for M-TEER and LAAO in a frail, elderly patient with multiple comorbidities. Given the patient’s marked frailty and high procedural risk, performing both interventions in a single session was favoured over a staged approach to minimize cumulative anaesthesia exposure, hospital stay, and overall procedural stress. Recent observational data and a systematic review suggest that the combined strategy is feasible and is associated with high procedural success in carefully selected high-risk patients.1–3 In the WATCH-TEER prospective registry, combined M-TEER and LAAO was performed in a high-risk population and was associated with procedural feasibility, effective MR reduction, and acceptable short-term safety, although adverse events at 45 days, including death, stroke, and major bleeding, were reported after discharge.1 In the recent systematic review and meta-analysis, the combined M-TEER/LAAO strategy achieved procedural success in ∼90% of cases and technical success in more than 95%, with outcomes broadly comparable to isolated M-TEER for procedural and technical success. Safety outcomes were generally reassuring, with no significant increase in bleeding, acute kidney injury, or all-cause death compared with M-TEER alone; however, non-significant signals for in-hospital death, stroke, and vascular complications underline the need for careful patient selection and further evidence.2 The main technical lesson from this case is that success of the second intervention may depend on a device able to compensate for septal geometry chosen to favour the first. In combined procedures, the transseptal puncture is usually planned around mitral repair. That choice may make appendage access less coaxial and can impair stability during LAAO. In this setting, alternative technical strategies included performing a second transseptal puncture specifically dedicated to LAAO or attempting appendage closure with a standard non-steerable delivery system. A second puncture was considered less attractive because it would have increased septal manipulation, procedural complexity, and potential risk in a frail high-risk patient. Conversely, continued use of a non-steerable system could have resulted in insufficient coaxiality and unstable device positioning. The steerable introducer was therefore selected as a pragmatic solution to compensate for the mitral-oriented transseptal trajectory, restore an appropriate delivery axis, avoid repeat septal puncture, and provide sufficient support for controlled device deployment. In the present case, a steerable introducer restored the delivery axis, avoided the need for repeat puncture, and enabled first-attempt deployment with stable position and no residual leak. This technical concept is illustrated in the additional schematic figure, which compares the ideal transseptal orientation for M-TEER and LAAO and shows how the steerable introducer allowed redirection of the delivery axis toward the left atrial appendage after an initial mitral-oriented puncture (Figure 2). Contemporary valvular heart disease guidance frames transcatheter mitral intervention within Heart Team-based decision-making for symptomatic patients with significant MR who are poor surgical candidates, while current AF guidance recognizes LAAO as a potential option when long-term anticoagulation is unsuitable because bleeding risk is high.4,5 The present report therefore supports a tailored structural strategy in which careful patient selection and dedicated catheter technology can expand the practicality of a one-stop approach. A limitation of this report is the absence of longer-term follow-up in the present manuscript; however, the immediate technical lesson is directly applicable to combined structural procedures.

A representation for the integral kernel of the composition of multivariate Bernstein–Durrmeyer operators


Abstract
This paper presents a representation for the kernel of the composition of multivariate Bernstein–Durrmeyer operators for functions defined on the standard simplex in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} .

1
Introduction

In their recent paper
[
1
]
the
authors studied the composition of Bernstein–Durrmeyer operators
M_{n}:L_{1}[0,1]\longrightarrow\Pi_{n}
defined by

\displaystyle(M_{n}f)(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}f(y)K_{n}(x,y)dy,\,\,x\in[0,1],

\displaystyle K_{n}(x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(n+1)\sum_{k=0}^{n}p_{n,k}(x)p_{n,k}(y),\,p_{n,k}(x)=\binom{n}{k}x^{k}(1-x)^{n-k},

focusing on the structure and properties of the associated kernel functions.
They established new identities for the kernel arising from the composition
of two and three operators. For the composition of
2
classical
Bernstein–Durrmeyer operators one has the following formula
[
1
, Theorem 1]
:

Theorem 1

For
m,n\geq 0
, the kernel of
the composition of classical Bernstein–Durrmeyer operators
M_{m}\circ M_{n}
has the representation

K_{m,n}\left(x,y\right)=\frac{\left(m+1\right)!\left(n+1\right)!}{\left(m+n+1\right)!}\sum_{k=0}^{\min\{m,n\}}\binom{m}{k}\binom{n}{k}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}p_{k,\ell}\left(x\right)p_{k,\ell}\left(y\right).

Like the well-known representation in terms of Legendre polynomials, which
can be written in the form

\displaystyle K_{m,n}\left(x,y\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\min\left\{m,n\right\}}\frac{m^{\underline{k}}}{\left(m+k+1\right)^{\underline{k}}}\frac{n^{\underline{k}}}{\left(n+k+1\right)^{\underline{k}}}\left(2k+1\right)

\displaystyle\times\sum_{i=0}^{k}\left(-1\right)^{i}\binom{k}{i}p_{k,i}\left(x\right)\sum_{j=0}^{k}\left(-1\right)^{j}\binom{k}{j}p_{k,j}\left(y\right)

(see
[
1
]
), Theorem
1
shows the commutativity of these
operators naturally. While the above Legendre representation contains all
possible products
p_{k,i}\left(x\right)p_{k,j}\left(y\right)
,
0\leq i,j\leq k\leq n
, of Bernstein basis polynomials, the new representation has
the beautiful property to contain only products
p_{k,\ell}\left(x\right)p_{k,\ell}\left(y\right)
,
0\leq\ell\leq k\leq n
, where
n
is the
smallest degree of the Bernstein–Durrmeyer polynomials involved. This fact
immediately implies that the composition can be written as a linear
combination of the operators themselves.

For the composition of
3
classical Bernstein–Durrmeyer operators the
authors showed the following formula
[
1
, Theorem 2]
:

Theorem 2

The kernel of the composition of

3
Bernstein–Durrmeyer operators
M_{n_{3}}\circ M_{n_{2}}\circ M_{n_{1}}

has the representation

\displaystyle K_{n_{3},n_{2},n_{1}}\left(x,y\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\left(n_{3}+1\right)!\left(n_{2}+1\right)!\left(n_{1}+1\right)!\left(n_{3}+n_{2}+n_{1}+1\right)!}{\left(n_{3}+n_{2}+1\right)!\left(n_{3}+n_{1}+1\right)!\left(n_{2}+n_{1}+1\right)!}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{k=0}^{\min\{n_{1},n_{2},n_{3}\}}\frac{\binom{n_{3}}{k}\binom{n_{2}}{k}\binom{n_{1}}{k}}{\binom{n_{3}+n_{2}+n_{1}+1}{k}}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}p_{k,\ell}\left(x\right)p_{k,\ell}\left(y\right).

In this paper we prove a generalization of Theorem
1
in the multivariate case. In a
forth-coming paper the corresponding generalization of Theorem
2
will be given. Moreover, the authors
will derive a representation of its
r
-th iterate of the multivariate
Bernstein–Durrmeyer operator
M_{n}
as a linear combination of the
multivariate operators
M_{k}
, for
k=0,1,\ldots,n
.

2
Notation

We start with notation. Let
d
be a positive integer. The standard simplex
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is given by

\mathbb{S}^{d}:=\left\{x=\left(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\ 0\leq x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}\leq 1,\ x_{1}+\cdots+x_{d}\leq 1\right\}.

We will also use the barycentric coordinates

\mathbf{x}=\left(x_{0},x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}\right),\qquad x_{0}:=1-x_{1}-\cdots-x_{d}.

Throughout the paper, we will use standard multi-index notation. For
example, for
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\beta=(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{d})\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
we write

x^{\beta}:=x_{1}^{\beta_{1}}\cdots x_{d}^{\beta_{d}},\qquad\left|\beta\right|:=\beta_{1}+\cdots+\beta_{d},\qquad\beta!:=\beta_{1}!\cdots\beta_{d}!.

Multinomial coefficients are defined by
{\binom{\left|\beta\right|}{\beta}}:=\frac{|\beta|!}{\beta!}
if
\beta=(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{d})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
and
{\binom{\left|\beta\right|}{\beta}}:=0
if (at least) one of the components
\beta_{\nu}
is negative. We will also use binomial coefficients
{\binom{s}{m}}
with
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
which are defined by

{\binom{s}{m}}:=\frac{s\left(s-1\right)\cdots\left(s-m+1\right)}{m!}

for
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
{\binom{s}{0}}:=1
.

For given
\alpha=\left(\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{d}\right)\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d+1}
, the
d
-variate Bernstein basis polynomials are

B_{\alpha}\left(x\right):={\binom{\left|\alpha\right|}{\alpha}}\mathbf{x}^{\alpha}=\frac{\left|\alpha\right|!}{\alpha_{0}!\alpha_{1}!\cdots\alpha_{d}!}\,x_{0}^{\alpha_{0}}x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots x_{d}^{\alpha_{d}}.

We use the inner product

\left\langle f,g\right\rangle:=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}f\left(x\right)\,g\left(x\right)\,dx.

For a function
f
on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
, the Bernstein-Durrmeyer operator of
degree
n\in\mathbb{N}
is defined by the formula

\left(M_{n}f\right)\left(x\right):=\sum_{\left|\alpha\right|=n}\frac{\left\langle f,B_{\alpha}\right\rangle}{\left\langle\mathbf{1},B_{\alpha}\right\rangle}B_{\alpha}\left(x\right).

Here,
\mathbf{1}
denotes the constant function equal to one. The
Bernstein-Durrmeyer operator is usually considered on the domain
L^{p}\left(\mathbb{S}^{d}\right)
,
1\leq p<\infty
, which is the
weighted
L^{p}
space consisting of all measurable functions on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
such that

\|f\|_{p}:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}\left|f\left(x\right)\right|^{p}\,dx\right)^{1/p}

is finite. For
p=\infty
, the space
C\left(\mathbb{S}^{d}\right)
of
continuous functions is considered instead. Note that the spaces
L^{p}\left(\mathbb{S}^{d}\right)
,
1<p<\infty
, and
C\left(\mathbb{S}^{d}\right)
are subspaces of
L^{1}\left(\mathbb{S}^{d}\right)
.

3
The main result and its proof

For
x,y\in\mathbb{S}^{d}
, we define the multivariate kernel
K_{m,n}\left(x,y\right)
via the definition of
M_{m}\circ M_{n}
:

\displaystyle\left(\left(M_{m}\circ M_{n}\right)f\right)\left(x\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}f\left(y\right)\sum_{\left|\beta\right|=m}\sum_{\left|\alpha\right|=n}B_{\alpha}\left(y\right)B_{\beta}\left(x\right)\frac{1}{\left\langle\mathbf{1},B_{\alpha}\right\rangle\left\langle\mathbf{1},B_{\beta}\right\rangle}\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}B_{\alpha}\left(t\right)B_{\beta}\left(t\right)dt\right)dy

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}f\left(y\right)K_{m,n}\left(x,y\right)dy,

where
\mathbf{1=}\left(1,1,\ldots,1\right)\in\mathbb{N}^{d+1}
. The
following theorem is the main result of this note.

Theorem 3

For positive integers
m,n
and
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the kernel
K_{m,n}\left(x,y\right)
has the representation

K_{m,n}\left(x,y\right)=\frac{\left(m+d\right)!\left(n+d\right)!}{\left(m+n+d\right)!}\sum_{\ell\geq 0}\binom{m}{\left|\ell\right|}\binom{n}{\left|\ell\right|}B_{\ell}\left(\mathbf{x}\right)B_{\ell}\left(\mathbf{y}\right).

Proof.
For positive integers
m,n
and
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we have

K_{m,n}\left(x,y\right)=\sum_{\left|\beta\right|=m}\sum_{\left|\alpha\right|=n}B_{\alpha}\left(y\right)B_{\beta}\left(x\right)\frac{1}{\left\langle\mathbf{1},B_{\alpha}\right\rangle\left\langle\mathbf{1},B_{\beta}\right\rangle}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}B_{\alpha}\left(t\right)B_{\beta}\left(t\right)dt.

Note that
\left\langle\mathbf{1},B_{\alpha}\right\rangle=\left|\alpha\right|!/\left(\left|\alpha\right|+d\right)!
(see,
e.g.,
[
2
, Page 147]
). Since

\int_{S^{d}}B_{\alpha}\left(t\right)B_{\beta}\left(t\right)dt=\frac{\binom{\left|\alpha\right|}{\alpha}\binom{\left|\beta\right|}{\beta}}{\binom{\left|\alpha+\beta\right|}{\alpha+\beta}}\left\langle\mathbf{1},B_{\alpha+\beta}\right\rangle

we obtain

\displaystyle K_{m,n}\left(x,y\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\left(m+d\right)!\left(n+d\right)!\left(m+n\right)!}{m!n!\left(m+n+d\right)!}\sum_{\left|\beta\right|=m}\sum_{\left|\alpha\right|=n}B_{\alpha}\left(y\right)B_{\beta}\left(x\right)\frac{\binom{\left|\alpha\right|}{\alpha}\binom{\left|\beta\right|}{\beta}}{\binom{\left|\alpha+\beta\right|}{\alpha+\beta}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\left(m+d\right)!\left(n+d\right)!}{\left(m+n+d\right)!}\sum_{\left|\beta\right|=m}\frac{1}{\beta!}B_{\beta}\left(x\right)\sum_{\left|\alpha\right|=n}B_{\alpha}\left(y\right)\frac{\left(\alpha+\beta\right)!}{\alpha!}.

With the notation
\mathbf{t}=\left(t_{0},t_{1},\ldots,t_{d}\right)
and
\mathbf{D}^{\beta}=\prod_{\nu=0}^{d}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{\nu}}\right)^{\beta_{\nu}}
, the inner sum is equal to

\displaystyle\sum_{\left|\alpha\right|=n}B_{\alpha}\left(y\right)\frac{\left(\alpha+\beta\right)!}{\alpha!}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\left|\alpha\right|=n}B_{\alpha}\left(y\right)\left(\mathbf{D}^{\beta}\mathbf{t}^{\alpha+\beta}\right)_{\mid\mathbf{t=1}}=\mathbf{D}^{\beta}\left(\mathbf{t}^{\beta}\sum_{\left|\alpha\right|=n}B_{\alpha}\left(y\right)\mathbf{t}^{\alpha}\right)_{\mid\mathbf{t=1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\prod_{\nu=0}^{d}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{\nu}}\right)^{\beta_{\nu}}\left(t_{0}^{\beta_{0}}t_{1}^{\beta_{1}}\cdots t_{d}^{\beta_{d}}\left(y_{0}t_{0}+y_{1}t_{1}+\cdots+y_{d}t_{d}\right)^{n}\right)\right)_{\mid\mathbf{t=1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\leq\beta}n^{\underline{\left|\ell\right|}}\prod_{\nu=0}^{d}\left(\binom{\beta_{\nu}}{\ell_{\nu}}\frac{\beta_{\nu}!}{\ell_{\nu}!}y_{\nu}^{\ell_{\nu}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\leq\beta}n^{\underline{\left|\ell\right|}}\frac{1}{\left|\ell\right|!}B_{\ell}\left(y\right)\beta!\prod_{\nu=0}^{d}\binom{\beta_{\nu}}{\ell_{\nu}}.

Here,
\ell\leq\beta
means summation over all nonnegative integers
\ell_{\nu}
such that
\ell_{\nu}\leq\beta_{\nu}
, for
\nu=0,\ldots,d
.
Therefore,

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\left(m+d\right)!\left(n+d\right)!}{\left(m+n+d\right)!}\right)^{-1}K_{m,n}\left(x,y\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\left|\beta\right|=m}B_{\beta}\left(x\right)\sum_{\ell\leq\beta}\binom{n}{\left|\ell\right|}B_{\ell}\left(y\right)\prod_{\nu=0}^{d}\binom{\beta_{\nu}}{\ell_{\nu}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\geq 0}\binom{n}{\left|\ell\right|}B_{\ell}\left(y\right)\sum_{\left|\beta\right|=m}B_{\beta}\left(x\right)\prod_{\nu=0}^{d}\binom{\beta_{\nu}}{\ell_{\nu}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\geq 0}\binom{n}{\left|\ell\right|}B_{\ell}\left(y\right)\sum_{\left|\beta\right|=m}{\binom{\left|\beta\right|}{\beta}}\mathbf{x}^{\beta}\prod_{\nu=0}^{d}\frac{\left(\beta_{\nu}\right)^{\underline{\ell_{\nu}}}}{\ell_{\nu}!}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\geq 0}\binom{n}{\left|\ell\right|}B_{\ell}\left(y\right)m^{\underline{\left|\ell\right|}}\left(\prod_{\nu=0}^{d}\frac{1}{\ell_{\nu}!}\right)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\left|\beta\right|=m\\
\beta\geq\ell\end{subarray}}{\binom{m-\left|\ell\right|}{\beta-\ell}}\mathbf{x}^{\beta},

where
\ell\geq 0
means summation over all nonnegative integers
\ell_{\nu}

\left(\nu=0,\ldots,d\right)
. Observing that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\left|\beta\right|=m\\
\beta\geq\ell\end{subarray}}{\binom{m-\left|\ell\right|}{\beta-\ell}}\mathbf{x}^{\beta}=\sum_{\left|\beta\right|=m-\left|\ell\right|}{\binom{m-\left|\ell\right|}{\beta}}\mathbf{x}^{\beta+\ell}=\mathbf{x}^{\ell}\sum_{\left|\beta\right|=m-\left|\ell\right|}{\binom{m-\left|\ell\right|}{\beta}}B_{\beta}\left(x\right)=\mathbf{x}^{\ell}

and using that
\prod_{\nu=0}^{d}\frac{1}{\ell_{\nu}!}=\frac{1}{\left|\ell\right|!}\binom{\left|\ell\right|}{\ell}

we obtain

\left(\frac{\left(m+d\right)!\left(n+d\right)!}{\left(m+n+d\right)!}\right)^{-1}K_{m,n}\left(x,y\right)=\sum_{\ell\geq 0}\binom{m}{\left|\ell\right|}\binom{n}{\left|\ell\right|}B_{\ell}\left(y\right)\binom{\left|\ell\right|}{\ell}\mathbf{x}^{\ell}.

Since
\binom{\left|\ell\right|}{\ell}\mathbf{x}^{\ell}=B_{\ell}\left(x\right)
the proof is completed.

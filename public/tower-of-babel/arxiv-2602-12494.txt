On recurrence relations arising from NRS(2) applied to a cubic polynomial
Mario DeFranco

Abstract
We prove that the leading coefficient of the “error” terms of NRS(2) applied to a cubic polynomial f ⁡ ( z ) f(z) with starting point ( − a 1 a 2 , − a 1 a 2 ) (-\frac{a_{1}}{a_{2}},-\frac{a_{1}}{a_{2}}) are positive-coefficient rational functions in the zeros of f ⁡ ( z ) f(z) . We express these terms as a sum over combinatorial objects which we call radius-value trees.

Let

Recall that the auxiliary functions for NRS(2) applied to

\displaystyle f_{0}(x_{0},x_{1})

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i=-1}^{d-1}\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor\frac{i}{2}\rfloor}-\frac{a_{i+1}}{a_{2}}((\frac{-a_{0}}{a_{2}})(\frac{-a_{1}}{a_{2}})^{-1}x_{1})^{j}x_{0}^{i-2j}{i-j\choose j}

The rings
\mathrm{CH}_{2}
and
\tilde{\mathrm{CH}}_{2}

Let
\mathrm{CH}_{2}
denote the ring generated by complete homogeneous polynomials
h_{k}
in two indeterminates
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
, where

h_{k}=\sum_{i=0}^{k}u_{1}^{k-i}u_{2}^{i}.

We describe how to multiply
h_{i}
and
h_{j}
in terms of centered paths defined next.

Definition 1
.

For an integer
r\geq 0
, let
P
be path on the set
V(P)
of
r+1
vertices indexed as

V(P)=(P(-r),P(-r+2),\ldots,P(r-2),P(r))

equipped with directed edges

P(-r+2i)\rightarrow P(-r+2i+2)

for
0\leq i<r
. We call
P
a
centered path
and call
r
the
radius of
P
denoted by
\mathrm{rad}(P)
. If
r
is even, then call
P(0)
the central vertex of the path.

Suppose
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
are centered paths with
r_{i}=\mathrm{rad}(P_{i})
for
i=1,2
. For
0\leq k\leq\min(r_{1},r_{2})
, define the
k
-th product path

\pi(P_{1},P_{2},k)

whose vertex set is a subset of
V(P_{1})\times V(P_{2})
with edges defined as follows. For
0\leq j\leq r_{2}-k-1

(P_{1}(-r_{1}+2k),P_{2}(-r_{2}+2j))\rightarrow(P_{1}(-r_{1}+2k),P_{2}(-r_{2}+2j+2)),

and for
1\leq j\leq r_{1}-k-1

(P_{1}(-r_{1}+2k+2j),P_{2}(r_{2}-2k))\rightarrow(P_{1}(-r_{1}+2k+2j+2),P_{2}(r_{2}-2k)).

\pi(P_{1},P_{2},k)

For integers
r_{1},r_{2}\geq 0
and
x,y
define

\rho(r_{1},r_{2},x,y)=\begin{cases}r_{1}+y\text{ if }y\geq-x+r_{2}-r_{1}\\
r_{2}-x\text{ if }y<-x+r_{2}-r_{1}\end{cases}.

Lemma 3
.

i).

\mathrm{rad}(\pi(P_{1},P_{2},k))=\mathrm{rad}(P_{1})+\mathrm{rad}(P_{2})-2k

ii). The radius of the centered path that contains
(P_{1}(x),P_{2}(y))
is

\rho(\mathrm{rad}(P_{1}),\mathrm{rad}(P_{2}),x,y).

iii). We have

\bigcup_{k=0}^{\min(r_{1},r_{2})}V(\pi(P_{1},P_{2},k))=V(P_{1})\times V(P_{2})

and the above union is disjoint.

iv). Suppose

(P_{1}(x),P_{2}(y))=\pi(P_{1},P_{2},k)(j).

Then

j=x+y.

Proof.

These statements follow immediately by associating
(P_{1}(x),P_{2}(y))\in V(P_{1})\times V(P_{2})
with the point
(x,y)
in the
x-y
plane and considering the line

y=-x-r_{1}+r_{2}.

For each point on this line

(-r_{1}+k,r_{2}-k)

the set consisting of this point, the points on the vertical ray below it and all the points on the horizontal ray to the right constitute the vertex set of
\pi(P_{1},P_{2},k)
. This completes the proof.
∎

4
Radius-value trees

Definition 6
.

Define a radius-value tree of height
n
to be a rooted plane ternary tree whose vertices are labeled with even integers such that:

i) the label of the root of a tree
T
is called the value of the tree, denoted by
\mathrm{val}(T)
.

ii) a height 0 tree
T
consists of of one labeled vertex

iii) a height
(n+1)
tree

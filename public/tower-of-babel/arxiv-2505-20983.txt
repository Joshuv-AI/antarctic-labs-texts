Construction of Metaplectic Representations of S ​ L 2 ​ ( ℤ 2 n ) SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{2^{n}}) and Twisted Magnetic Translations
E. Floratos, K. Manolas, I. Tsohanjis

Abstract
Unitary metaplectic representations of the group S ​ L 2 ​ ( ℤ 2 n ) SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{2^{n}}) are necessary to describe the evolution of 2 n 2^{n} -dimensional quantum systems, such as systems involving n qubits. We construct the general matrix form of such representations, together with appropriately defined magnetic translations based on the diagonal subgroup of the tensor product of the finite Heisenberg group H ​ W 2 n ⊗ H ​ W 2 n HW_{2^{n}}\otimes HW_{2^{n}} . It is shown that in order for the metaplectic property to be fulfilled, an increase in the dimensionality of the involved n-qubit Hilbert spaces, from 2 n 2^{n} to 2 2 ​ n 2^{2n} , is necessary.

Representations of
SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{N})

Introduction

The last decade has seen an explosive rise in interest surrounding Quantum Information and Quantum Computation, with a major focus on both constructing Quantum Algorithms as well as constructing functioning physical implementations of Quantum Computers.

As the physical implementations of these quantum systems require isolation from the environment, restricting the energy transitions to transitions between a fixed finite number of them, the quantum mechanical problem reduces to the study of finite dimensional Hilbert spaces. Quantum Mechanics on a finite lattice was first introduced by Weyl
H 1950
, and further elaborated upon in
Schwinger 1960
;
Hannay and Berry 1980
;
Balian and Itzykson 1986
;
Floratos 1989
;
Floratos and Leontaris 1997
;
Vourdas 2004
. There have been various applications of finite dimensional Hilbert spaces in the literature, among which are applications to the Quantum Hall effect
Zak 1989
;
Rammal, R. and Bellissard,
J. 1990
, studies in regards to the unification of gravity and quantum mechanics
Athanasiu et al. 1996
;
Axenides et al. 2014
;
Banks and Fischler 2018
;
Bao et al. 2017
;
Singh and Carroll 2020
, as well as connections to quantum chaos
Berry 1987
.

On the other hand, despite the growing interest, construction of quantum algorithms is still mainly done empirically, without a solid theoretical structure to be based upon.
Since a Quantum Algorithm can be viewed as an
N
-dimensional unitary operator, representing an evolution operator of the physical system, the problem can be reformulated as one of constructing an
N\times N
matrix obeying specific criteria.

One approach for obtaining such matrices is through the construction of exact matrix representations of the group
SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{N})
. The group
SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{N})
is the group of automorphisms of the discrete Heisenberg-Weyl group
HW_{N}
, whose inequivalent irreducible unitary representations have all been determined in
Floratos and Tsohantjis 2025
and references therein. The matrix representations of
SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{N})
have been extensively studied in the particular case of
N
being an odd prime
Balian and Itzykson 1986
;
Athanasiu and Floratos 1994
;
Eholzer 1995
;
Athanasiu et al. 1998
.

In this work we attempt to connect Finite Quantum Mechanics (FQM) and Quantum Information by constructing exact unitary representations for
SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{2^{n}})
, which correspond to evolution operators of
n
-qubit quantum systems, and consequently are of great practical interest for Quantum Computation applications. The construction of exact representations for
N
even poses specific challenges, stemming from the use of modular algebra, which do not exist for the case of
N
being odd, for which some quantum circuits have already been proposed
Floratos and
Pavlidis 2025
.

The paper is organised as follows:

In Section 2 we give a brief overview of the Heisenberg-Weyl group
HW_{N}
and summarise the results of Floratos et al
Floratos and Tsohantjis 2025
, where the representations of
HW_{2^{n}}
have been categorised.

In Section 3 we describe the automorphism group of
HW_{N}
,
SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{N})
, and introduce the Magnetic Translations for the case of
N=odd
, in order to introduce the main notions, of a particle moving on a discrete toroidal phase space
T_{2}[N]
, of FQM.

In section 4 we define the Magnetic Translations for
N=2^{n}
on the Twisted double torus
T_{2}[2^{n}]\otimes T_{2}[2^{n}]
, terming them Twisted Magnetic Translations, and provide their general matrix elements.

In Section 5 we first provide the explicit matrix forms for the representations of the generators
S
and
T
of the group
SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{N})
, and show that they satisfy the metaplectic property. We then give the main result of this work, which is the general matrix form of the representation of any element
A\in SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{N})
.

In Section 6 briefly overview of the results of Nobs et al
Nobs 1976
;
Nobs and Wolfart 1976
and Eholzer
Eholzer 1995
, which we leverage to prove the exactness of our representation. Then we comparatively review the results of Feichtinger et al
Feichtinger et al. 2007
, who have also constructed operators of
SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{N})
for any
N
, and demonstrate the necessity of our construction for physical applications.

Finally, in Section 7 we conclude and discuss open issues and potential applications.

The Heisenberg-Weyl Group and a set of its representations

The discrete and finite Heisenberg-Weyl group
HW_{N}
, of order
|HW_{N}|=N^{3}
, can be defined the set of elements
g(m,s,r)\equiv z^{m}x^{s}y^{r}
where
m,r,s=0,1,\dots,N-1
and
x,y,z
are subject to the relations

x^{N}=y^{N}=z^{N}=e,\ yx=zxy,

(1)

zx=xz,\ zy=yz

(2)

with
e
being the identity element of the group. Within the framework of
FQM, the generators
x
and
y
of (
1
) correspond to the position and
translation operators respectively, of a fictitious particle hopping on a discrete

N
equidistant point circle whose corresponding phase space is the discrete
torus
T_{2}[N]=\mathbb{Z}_{N}\times\mathbb{Z}_{N}
.

For physical implementations of the Heisenberg group
HW_{2^{n}}
on the
toroidal phase space
T_{2}[2^{n}]
, the complete set of irreducible representations have been found and classified in
Floratos and Tsohantjis 2025
. In this work we shall only consider the inequivalent faithful unitary irreducible matrix representations
\Gamma^{p}
of
HW_{2^{n}}
, given by

\Gamma^{p}(z^{m}x^{r}y^{s})_{k,j}\equiv\Gamma_{m,r,s}^{p}=e^{\frac{2\pi i}{2^{n}}pn}e^{\frac{2\pi i}{2^{n}}pkr}\delta_{k+s-j,0}

(3)

where
k,j,m,r,s=0,1,\dots,2^{n}-1
and
p=1,3,\dots,2^{n}-1
. Any two
irreducible representations obey the following commutation relation

\left[\Gamma_{m,r,s}^{p},\Gamma_{m^{\prime},r^{\prime},s^{\prime}}^{p}\right]=(\omega_{n}^{pr^{\prime}s}-\omega_{n}^{prs^{\prime}})\Gamma_{m+m^{\prime},r+r^{\prime},s+s^{\prime}}^{p}\ ,\quad m,r,s=0,1,\dots,2^{n}-1

(4)

where
\omega_{n}=e^{\frac{2\pi i}{2^{n}}}
and we have defined
\Gamma^{p}(z^{m}x^{r}y^{s})\equiv\Gamma_{m,r,s}^{p}
for notational simplicity.

The carrier space of the above irreps
\Gamma^{p}
is given by

V^{p}=\{span\ket{j}\},j=0,1,\dots,2^{n}-1

(5)

where the canonical basis
\ket{j}=(e_{j})_{k}=\delta_{k,j}
, obeying
\braket{k|j}=\delta_{k,j}
, is used.

The generators
x
and
y
of (
1
) correspond to the position
operator in the diagonal form and the translation (momentum) operator of a fictitious
particle moving on a discrete
2^{n}
equidistant point circle, respectively.
The corresponding phase space is the discrete torus
T_{2}[2^{n}]=\mathbb{Z}_{2^{n}}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2^{n}}
. Given the action of the elements

on
the above basis

\begin{split}z\ket{j}=\omega^{p}_{n}\delta_{k,j}\ket{k},&\quad x\ket{j}=\omega^{pk}_{n}\delta_{k,j}\ket{k}\\
y\ket{0}=\ket{2^{n}-1},&\quad y\ket{j}=\delta_{k+1,j}\ket{k}\end{split}

(6)

for
j=1,\dots,2^{n}-1
, the
2^{n}\times 2^{n}
matrix forms of
x,y
and

z
are

\displaystyle z_{p}\equiv\Gamma^{p}_{(1,0,0)}

\displaystyle=\omega_{n}^{p}I_{2^{n}}

(7)

\displaystyle Q_{p}\equiv\Gamma^{p}_{(0,1,0)}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}1&0&\dots&0\\
0&\omega^{p}_{n}&\dots&0\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&\dots&0&\omega_{n}^{p(2^{n}-1)}\end{bmatrix}

(8)

\displaystyle P^{-1}_{p}\equiv\Gamma^{p}_{(0,0,1)}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}0&1&0&0&\dots&0\\
0&0&1&0&\dots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&\dots&\ddots&0&1\\
1&0&0&\dots&0&0\end{bmatrix}

(9)

from which it can be shown that

P^{-1}_{p}Q_{p}=\omega_{n}^{p}Q_{p}P^{-1}_{p}

(10)

through whom the expected
2^{n}
periodicity can be derived:

Q_{p}^{2^{n}}=P_{p}^{2^{n}}=z_{p}^{2^{n}}=I_{2^{n}}

(11)

Using (
8
), the normalised eigenvectors and corresponding eigenvalues
of
P_{p}
, are respectively given by:

\begin{split}\ket{\psi_{k}}&=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2^{n}}}\left[1,\omega_{n}^{k},\omega_{n}^{2k},\dots,\omega_{n}^{(2^{n}-1)k}\right]^{T}\\
\lambda_{k}&=\omega_{n}^{k}\ ,\quad k=0,1,\dots,2^{n}-1\end{split}

(12)

Then the diagonilising matrix
F_{n}
of
P^{-1}_{p}
, with matrix elements

(F_{n})_{kj}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2^{n}}}\omega_{n}^{kj}

(13)

and the matrix form

F_{n}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2^{n}}}\begin{bmatrix}1&1&1&\dots&1\\
1&\omega_{n}&\omega_{n}^{2}&\dots&\omega_{n}^{(2^{n}-1)}\\
1&\omega_{n}^{2}&\omega_{n}^{4}&\dots&\omega_{n}^{2(2^{n}-1)}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\dots&\vdots\\
1&\omega_{n}^{(2^{n}-1)}&\omega_{n}^{2(2^{n}-1)}&\dots&\omega_{n}^{(2^{n}-1)(2^{n}-1)}\end{bmatrix}

(14)

is the standard Finite Fourier Transform for n qubits, representing the
rotation by
\pi/2
degrees i.e.
F_{n}^{4}=I_{p}
in the discrete phase space.
Finally, by making use of (
8
), (
9
) and (
14
), it can be
seen that:

F_{p}(P_{p})^{p}F_{p}^{-1}=Q_{p}\ ,\quad p=1,3,\dots,2^{n}-1

(15)

3
The group
SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{N})
as the automorphism group of
HW_{N}

The group of automorphisms of
HW_{N}
,
SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{N})
, is defined as the set of
2\times 2
matrices
A=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}a&b\\
c&d\end{array}\right]
with
a,b,c,d\in\mathbb{Z}_{N}
and
\det A=1modN
, that preserve the symplectic inner product of
T_{2}[N]
. Specifically, an element
A\in SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{N})
acts on an element
g(m,r,s)\in HW_{N}
as
g(m,r,s)\xrightarrow{A}g(m,ar+cs,br+ds)
, that is
x=(r,s)\rightarrow(r,s)A=(r^{\prime},s^{\prime})=x^{\prime}
. It can be seen that the symplectic inner product defined as
\{x,x^{\prime}\}=x\epsilon(x^{\prime})^{T}
, where
\epsilon=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}0&-1\\
1&0\end{array}\right]
is the symplectic form, is preserved. Indeed

x\epsilon(x^{\prime})^{T}\xrightarrow{A}(xA)\epsilon(x^{\prime}A)^{T}=xA\epsilon A^{T}(x^{\prime})^{T}=x\epsilon(x^{\prime})^{T}

(16)

The finite automorphism group
SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{N})
is generated by the elements

S=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}0&-1\\
1&0\end{array}\right],\quad T=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}1&1\\
0&1\end{array}\right]

(17)

as

A=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
ca^{-1}&0\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}a&0\\
0&a^{-1}\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}1&a^{-1}b\\
0&1\end{array}\right]=T_{L}DT_{R}

(18)

where
T_{L}
and
T_{R}
are the left and right translations respectively, and the dilatations D are given by

D(a)=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}a&0\\
0&a^{-1}\end{array}\right]=T^{-a}ST^{-a^{-1}}S^{-1}T^{-a}S^{-1}

(19)

and
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}_{N}^{\ast}=\{x\in\mathbb{Z}_{N}:gdc(x,N)=1\}
. The above generator elements obey the relations

\begin{split}T^{N}=I,\quad S^{2}&=D(-1)\\
D(a)D(b)=D(ab),\quad D(a)T&=T^{a^{2}}D(a),\quad SD(a)=D(a^{-1})S\end{split}

(20)

and the periodicity relations
S^{4}=I_{N}
and
(ST)^{6}=I_{N}
.

In Finite Quantum Mechanics
Balian and Itzykson 1986
;
Athanasiu and Floratos 1994
;
Vourdas 2004
we replace the points
x(r,s)
of the toroidal phase space
T_{2}[N]
with unitary
N\times N
matrices coming from the faithful representation of the Heisenberg group

J_{r,s}=\omega^{\frac{rs}{2}}P^{r}_{N}Q^{s}_{N}\quad r,s=0,1,\dots,N-1,\ \ \omega=\exp^{\frac{2\pi i}{N}}

(21)

with
Q
and
P
representing the position and momentum operators of a particle moving on the discrete phase space
T_{2}[N]
. The operators
Q
and
P
satisfy the fundamental Heisenberg-Weyl commutation relation
QP=\omega PQ
which is the exponential form of the standard Heisenberg commutation relations of Quantum Mechanics. This is the faithful irrep of the finite
HW_{N}
group with
p=1
(see (
7
), (
8
), (
9
)) with matrix elements

\begin{split}(Q_{N})_{kj}&=\omega^{k}\delta_{k,j},\\
(P_{N})_{kj}&=\delta_{k-1,j}\end{split}

(22)

where
k,j=0,1,\dots,N-1
.

The so called magnetic translations
J_{r,s}
thus defined, provide a projective unitary
representation of the translation group on
T_{N}
i.e.

J_{r,s}J_{r^{\prime},s^{\prime}}=\omega^{(r^{\prime}s-rs^{\prime})/2}J_{r+r^{\prime},s+s^{\prime}}

(23)

The automorphism group
SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{N})
has the corresponding unitary representation which preservers the algebra for the magnetic translations (
23
). Thus for every
A\in SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{N})
we correspond a unitary operator
U(A)
with the property:

U^{-1}(A)J_{r,s}U(A)=J_{(r,s)A}

(24)

This property is called the metaplectic property, meaning that
U(A)
realises the motion of the classical element
A
and its action on the phase space on the quantum points
J_{r,s}
.

The factor of
\frac{1}{2}
in the cocycle (
23
) is an immediate consequence
of demand of unitarity of
J_{r,s}
i.e.
\left(J_{r,s}\right)^{\dagger}=J_{r,s}^{-1}=J_{-r,-s}
. Moreover it can be easily shown that

J_{r,s}J_{r^{\prime},s^{\prime}}=\omega^{sr^{\prime}-s^{\prime}r}J_{r^{\prime},s^{\prime}}J_{r,s}

As a consequence of (
23
) we obtain that
(J_{r,s})^{k}=J_{kr,ks}
for
k=0,1,...,N-1
, which results in the periodic boundary condition on the
discrete
T_{2}[N]
,
(J_{r,s})^{N}=I
.

The matrix elements of the Weil representations of a general element
A=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}a&b\\
c&d\end{array}\right]
, when
N
is a prime integer, conforming to the definition of
J_{r,s}
and satisfying the metaplectic property, (
24
), are given
Athanasiu and Floratos 1994
in the generic case by

U(A)_{l,m}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}(-2c\ |\ N)\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}1\\
-i\end{array}\right\}\omega^{-(al^{2}+dm^{2}-2lm)/2c}

(25)

where the bracket equals
1
if
N=4k+1
or
-i
if
N=4k-1
, and
(-2c\ |\ N)
is
+1
if
-2c
is the square of an integer
modN
and
-1
if not.
Specifically, for the representations of the generator elements
S
and
D
, of
SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{N})
, the matrix elements are given by

U(S)_{l,m}=(-1)^{N}\frac{i^{t}}{\sqrt{N}}\omega^{lm}

(26)

where
t=0,1
, depending on whether
N=4k+1
or
N=4k-1
, respectively, and

U(D)_{l,m}=\sigma(1)\sigma(2-a-a^{-1})\delta_{l,am}

(27)

where:

\sigma(r)=(r\ |\ N)\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}1,\quad N=4k+1\\
-i,\quad N=4k-1\end{array}\right.

(28)

It should be noted that the irreps of
SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{N})
have been explicitly classified using Weil representations in
Nobs 1976
,
Nobs and Wolfart 1976
,
Eholzer 1995
. In the next section we shall consider the case
N=2^{n}
, being of particular interest in quantum information and
computation.

4
Twisted
HW_{N}
and Magnetic Translations for
N=2^{n}

Turning now to our main case of interest of
N=2^{n}
, it is easily seen that an analogue
definition of
J_{r,s}
as in (
21
) fails to exist as division
by 2
modN
is undefined. A possible solution to this problem is an extension of the phase space to
the double toroidal phase space
T_{2}[2^{n}]\otimes T_{2}[2^{n}]
with the
appropriate definition of its translation group in the tensor product group
HW_{2^{n}}\otimes HW_{2^{n}}
.

We begin by considering the diagonal subgroup
HW_{2^{n}}^{d}
, consisting of
all pairs
(g,g),g\in HW_{2^{n}}
. The tensor product of unitary irreps of
HW_{2^{n}}
gives the unitary irreps of
HW_{2^{n}}\otimes HW_{2^{n}}
. On
HW_{2^{n}}^{d}
these representations are generally reducible and equivalent to
some tensor product of unitary irreps of
HW_{2^{n}}
. To this end, let
x_{d}=x_{p}\otimes x_{p}
,
y^{-1}_{d}=y^{-1}_{p}\otimes y^{-1}_{p}
and
z_{d}=z_{p}\otimes z_{p}\in HW_{2^{n}}^{d}
,
where this time
x_{d},y^{-1}_{d}
are interpreted as position and momentum on the subspace
T_{2^{n}}^{d}
, respectively. Similarly to the odd case, these are also subject to the relations (
1
)
x_{d}y^{-1}_{d}=z_{d}y^{-1}_{d}x_{d}
.

Consider the set of elements
J_{r,s}
,
r,s=0,...,2^{n}-1
of
HW_{2^{n}}\otimes HW_{2^{n}}
, defined by

J_{r,s}\equiv(z^{-sr}\otimes I)x_{d}^{s}(y^{-1}_{d})^{r}

(29)

which satisfy

J_{r,s}J_{r^{\prime},s^{\prime}}=(z^{sr^{\prime}}\otimes z^{-s^{\prime}r})J_{r+r^{\prime},s+s^{\prime}}

Note that the set of elements
J_{r,s}
thus defined do not all belong to
HW_{2^{n}}^{d}
and moreover, form a central extension of the translation
group of the torus
T_{2^{n}}^{d}
which we shall call
MT_{2^{n}}
(Magnetic
Translations of the torus). Using the previously constructed faithfull irreps of
HW_{2^{n}}
(
4
), and setting
Q_{p}=\Gamma^{p}(x),\ P_{p}=\Gamma^{p}(y^{-1}),\ z_{p}=\Gamma^{p}(z)
the elements
J_{r,s}
are represented by the
2^{2n}
-dimensional matrices
J_{r,s}^{p}

J_{r,s}^{p}=z_{p}^{-rs}Q_{p}^{s}P_{p}^{r}\otimes Q_{p}^{s}P_{p}^{r}=\omega_{n}^{-psr}Q_{p}^{s}P_{p}^{r}\otimes Q_{p}^{s}P_{p}^{r}

(30)

The
2^{2n}
-dimensional carrier space of the above representation is simply
taken to be the tensor product of the carrier space
V^{p}
of (
5
) with
itself

V_{2n}\equiv V^{p}\otimes V^{p}=span\{\ket{j_{1},j_{2}},\ j_{1},\ j_{2}=0,1,...,2^{n}-1\}

(31)

this time obeying the orthogonality relation
\braket{k_{1},k_{2}|j_{1},j_{2}}=\delta_{k_{1},j_{1}}\delta_{k_{2},j_{2}}
.

The matrix elements of
J_{r,s}^{p}
are given by

(J_{r,s}^{p})_{(k_{1},k_{2}),(j_{1},j_{2})}\equiv(J_{r,s}^{p})_{2^{n}k_{1}+k_{2},2^{n}j_{1}+j_{2}}=\omega_{n}^{p[-sr+(k_{1}+k_{2})s]}\delta_{k_{1}-r,j_{1}}\delta_{k_{2}-r,j_{2}}

(32)

where
r,s=0,...,2^{n}-1
and
k_{1},k_{2},j_{1},j_{2}=0,1,...,2^{n}-1
. We shall call this group defined by (
32
) the Twisted
HW_{2^{n}}
group. Using (
32
) it is easily shown that
J_{r,s}^{p}
realize a unitary projective representation of the translation group on
T_{2^{n}}^{d}
:

\displaystyle J_{r,s}^{p}J_{r^{\prime},s^{\prime}}^{p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega_{n}^{p(r^{\prime}s-s^{\prime}r)}J_{r+r^{\prime},s+s^{\prime}}^{p}

(33)

\displaystyle(J_{r,s}^{p})^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle J_{-r,-s}^{p}

(34)

Since
p=1,3,\dots,2^{n}-1
runs through the faithful inequivalent
irreps of
HW_{2^{n}}
, we thus obtain all the faithful unitary (though not
necessarily irreducible) representations of the magnetic translation group
MT^{d}_{2^{n}}
. It can be easily checked that
J_{r,s}^{p}
given by (
30
) has the periodic conditions
J_{r+2^{n},s+2^{n}}^{p}=J_{r,s}^{p}
.

5
Explicit realisations of the metaplectic representation of
SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{2^{n}})

Having constructed the twisted magnetic translations for
N=2^{n}
, we can proceed with the quantisation of the classical motion of the torus
T_{2}[2^{n}]\otimes T_{2}[2^{n}]
, by finding those representations of the generators of
SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{2^{n}})
,
U(S)
and
U(T)
, that fulfill (
24
) for the above
J_{r,s}^{p}
.
Their matrix elements, adapted to correspond with our notation, are given by

\displaystyle\left(U(S)\right)_{(k_{1},k_{2}),(j_{1},j_{2})}

\displaystyle\equiv\left(U(S)\right)_{2^{n}k_{1}+k_{2},2^{n}j_{1}+j_{2}}=\frac{1}{2^{n}}\omega_{n}^{p(k_{1}j_{2}+k_{2}j_{1})}

\displaystyle\left(U(T)\right)_{(k_{1},k_{2}),(j_{1},j_{2})}

where
k_{1},k_{2},j_{1},j_{2}=0,1,\dots,2^{n}-1

satisfies
U^{4}(S)=I_{2^{2n}}

\displaystyle U(S)^{-1}J_{r,s}^{p}U(S)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle J_{s,-r}^{p}

(37)

\displaystyle U(T)^{-1}J_{r,s}^{p}U(T)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle J_{(r,s)T}^{p}

(38)

while

\begin{split}U(S)^{-1}(Q_{p}\otimes I_{2^{n}})U(S)&=I_{2^{n}}\otimes P_{p}\\
U(S)^{-1}(I_{2^{n}}\otimes Q_{p})U(S)&=P_{p}\otimes I_{2^{n}}\end{split}

(39)

corresponds to the twisted version of (
15
), where
\left(U(S)^{-1}\right)_{(k_{1},k_{2}),(j_{1},j_{2})}=\newline
\frac{1}{2^{n}}\omega_{n}^{-p(k_{1}j_{2}+k_{2}j_{1})}
.
The relation between
U(S)
and
F_{n}
is given by

U(S)=\tau(F_{n}\otimes F_{n})

(40)

where
F_{p}
is given by (
15
) and
\tau
is the twist map
\tau(\ket{a}\otimes\ket{b})=\ket{b}\otimes\ket{a}
. The representation of the other
generator
T
of
SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{2^{n}})
can be expressed in terms of

Q_{p}
as:

6
Remarks on relevant works

It is important to note that Weil representations and relevant irreps of Weil representations and relevant irreps of
SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{2^{n}})
have been defined and classified by Nobs et al in
Nobs and Wolfart 1976
and Eholzer in
Eholzer 1995
, using quadratic modules of
Z_{p^{n}}
, with
p
being an odd prime or
2
. Our construction of unitary metaplectic representations of
SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{2^{n}})
is related to this classification, as will be demonstrated in what follows. According to
Nobs and Wolfart 1976
;
Eholzer 1995
let
M
denote a finite
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n}}
-module. Let Q be a quadratic form of
M,\ Q:M\longrightarrow p^{-n}\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z}
such that

Q(-x)=Q(x),\ \forall x\in M

(64)

and define a
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n}}
-linear map
B:M\times M\longrightarrow p^{-n}\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z}
as:

B(x,y):=Q(x+y)-Q(x)-Q(y)

(65)

The pair
(M,Q)
is called a quadratic module of
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n}}
. Then, a Weil representation
\Gamma:SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{N})\longrightarrow Aut\mathbb{C}^{N}
is given once we define the action on the generators of
SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{N})
,

\displaystyle\left[\Gamma(T)f\right](x)

\displaystyle\approxeq e^{2\pi iQ(x)}f(x)

(66)

\displaystyle\left[\Gamma(S^{-1})f\right](x)

\displaystyle\approxeq\frac{\alpha_{Q}(-1)}{|M|^{1/2}}\sum_{y\in M}\exp{2\pi B(x,y)}f(y)

(67)

\displaystyle\left[\Gamma(D(a))f\right](x)

\displaystyle\approxeq\alpha_{Q}(a)\alpha_{Q}(-1)f(x)

(68)

where
a\in\mathbb{Z}^{*}_{p^{n}}
,
|M|
the order of
M
and:

\alpha_{Q}(a)=|M|^{-1/2}\sum_{x\in M}\exp{2\pi aiQ(x)}

(69)

This defines a
proper
representations of
SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n}})
, provided that:

\alpha_{Q}(a)\alpha_{Q}(b)=\alpha_{Q}(1)\alpha_{Q}(ab),\ \forall a,b\in\mathbb{Z}^{*}_{p^{n}}

(70)

If this relations is not satisfied, the representation is projective. For the case of
p=2
, the quadratic modules
(M,Q)
that define proper Weil representations are given in Theorem 4 of
Nobs and Wolfart 1976
. In this article we focus solely on case (1) of Theorem 2 in
Eholzer 1995
(see also
Nobs and Wolfart 1976
), where
M=\mathbb{Z}_{2^{n}}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2^{n}}
and
Q(x)=\frac{1}{2^{n}}x_{1}x_{2},\ x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}_{2^{n}}
. In this case it is easy to check that (
70
) is satisfied, and thus the representation of
SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{2^{n}})
constructed in this work is exact (but not irreducible).

7
Conclusion

In this work we have provided an explicit construction of a set of reducible metaplectic matrix representations for the group
SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{N})
, with
N=2^{n}
, complementing existing work in the literature, for the simpler case of
N
odd
Eholzer 1995
;
Athanasiu and Floratos 1994
. These representations are unitary and exact, meaning they can be used to describe a physical system, notably among them, an
n
-qubit quantum system.

The requirements of unitarity and exactness come at the cost of increased computational complexity in calculating the matrix representations, with their dimension scaling faster with
n
, increasing from
\mathcal{O}(2^{n})
, in the odd case, to
\mathcal{O}(2^{2n})
here.
This increase in dimension for the case of
N=2^{n}
is also present in the general classification of irreps of
SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{2^{n}})

Nobs 1976
;
Nobs and Wolfart 1976
;
Eholzer 1995
.

Our work could potentially be used to construct a dynamical model mimicking the evolution of a Quantum Neural Network on a graph. This can be seen from the fact that the matrix
P
(
9
) corresponds to an adjacency matrix of a directed graph, and as such the
HW_{2^{n}}
group has seemingly a part to play in Graph Neural Networks. In that context, one could also examine the role of
SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{N})
, it being the group of automorphisms of
HW_{2^{n}}
. This tangent, as well as the process of finding the
irreducible
representations of the twisted magnetic translations
J_{r,s}
will be studied in the future.

Finally, note that we have limited our discussion to representations of
SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{N})
, which have the metaplectic property for magnetic translations
J_{r,s}
constructed using only faithful
HW_{N}
irreps from
Floratos and Tsohantjis 2025
. A line of future research If constructed, the non-faithful representations of
SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{N})
could potentially be used in Quantum Machine Learning algorithms, performing a function analogous to that of Pooling Layers in classical Convolutional Neural Networks (see Chap. 9 in
Goodfellow et al. 2016
), as proposed in
Nguyen et al. 2024
.

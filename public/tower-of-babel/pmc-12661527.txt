Adverse events leading to discontinuation of daptomycin versus vancomycin in outpatient parenteral antimicrobial therapy: a retrospective cohort study
Authors: Kristin Lanier, Jaspaul Jawanda, Allison Cid
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivesTo compare the adverse event rates between daptomycin and vancomycin in the outpatient parenteral antimicrobial therapy (OPAT) setting.MethodsThis was a retrospective cohort study of patients treated with vancomycin or daptomycin in OPAT. Demographic and clinical data were abstracted from the electronic medical record. Vancomycin was dosed using Bayesian kinetics software to achieve a goal AUC of 400–600 mg h/L and daptomycin was a weight-based dose. The primary outcome was the occurrence of an adverse event resulting in a change in antimicrobial therapy or early discontinuation due to harm or injury. Secondary outcomes included measurement of time to adverse event occurrence and time in therapeutic range for the vancomycin group. The primary outcome was analyzed using a chi-square test.ResultsA total of 175 patients were included, with 112 in the daptomycin group and 63 in the vancomycin group. The primary outcome of early discontinuation of OPAT therapy was not statistically different between the daptomycin and vancomycin groups [15/112 (13%) versus 9/63 (14%); P = 0.87]. The daptomycin group demonstrated a shorter median time to adverse event occurrence compared with the vancomycin group (12 versus 30 days).ConclusionsWhen comparing the rate of early discontinuation between daptomycin and vancomycin in OPAT, there was no significant difference between groups. These findings suggest that either agent may be considered in the OPAT setting depending on patient-specific factors. Dosing of vancomycin using Bayesian model dosing software may have led to a reduction of adverse events in the vancomycin group.

Introduction

Outpatient parenteral antimicrobial therapy (OPAT) is defined as the administration of two or more doses of a parenteral antimicrobial agent outside of the hospital setting.1 Vancomycin and daptomycin are two antibiotics commonly used for treating Gram-positive infections. Daptomycin is administered as an intermittent infusion or an IV push, with the benefit of once-daily administration. Dosing is weight-based and can vary depending on the type of infection. Although creatine kinase (CK) levels should be monitored, and dose-adjustment can be required for renal dysfunction, daptomycin does not routinely require therapeutic drug monitoring.

Daptomycin use in the USA has increased in recent years as cost has decreased given the availability of a generic since 2016.2,3 In comparison with daptomycin’s once-daily administration, vancomycin is dose-dependent and requires monitoring to assess for toxicity and drug levels.1 Previously, trough-based monitoring was the common method for dosing vancomycin. In 2020, the IDSA published an update recommending the use of AUC dosing to mitigate the risk of adverse effects while maintaining clinical efficacy.4 Software programs implementing Bayesian kinetics can generate patient-specific predictive models to guide vancomycin dosing. Benefits of Bayesian software include less drug sampling required and the ability to provide AUC estimates prior to reaching steady state.5,6 Our institution previously used trough-based dosing but in 2020 transitioned to AUC dosing and carried this into the OPAT programme. AUC dosing was performed using Bayesian kinetics software (InsightRx™) by obtaining two levels while inpatient (peak or random and trough) and one (random or trough taken 1–2 times per week) while on OPAT.

Prior literature has found an increased risk of adverse effects associated with vancomycin compared with daptomycin in OPAT, but distinction of whether trough or AUC dosing was used is unknown.7,8 Furthermore, the recommended dose of daptomycin has increased over the last several years for serious Gram-positive infections, and safety data in comparison with vancomycin may be outdated.7–9 In patients where use of either agent is appropriate, the differences in adverse event rates are unclear when employing AUC vancomycin dosing. The objectives of our study were to determine differences in adverse event rates resulting in a change in antimicrobial therapy or early discontinuation due to harm or injury between daptomycin and vancomycin.

Methods

This was a single-centre, retrospective cohort study including patients 18 years and older who received at least one dose of either vancomycin or daptomycin through the OPAT programme between 1 September 2023 and 30 August 2024. Patients who were pregnant or expired prior to discharge on OPAT were excluded (Figure S1; available as Supplementary data at JAC-AMR Online). Patients meeting inclusion criteria within the designated timeframe were identified via electronic medical records. Adverse events resulting in a change of therapy or early discontinuation were identified from documentation provided in OPAT notes, as stated by the infectious diseases provider or pharmacist. This was then confirmed with laboratory evidence (see Supplementary data for study definitions and dosing).

The primary outcome was the occurrence of an adverse event resulting in a change in antimicrobial therapy or early discontinuation due to harm or injury. Secondary outcomes included time to adverse event occurrence and percentage of time within therapeutic range for vancomycin. Time to adverse events was analysed to compare the onset in days between agents. Time in therapeutic range for vancomycin was assessed for an association of adverse effects such as supratherapeutic levels and acute kidney injury (AKI).

Statistical analysis

Descriptive statistics were used to assess patient demographics and baseline clinical characteristics. The primary endpoint was analysed using a chi-square test, and a P value £0.05 was deemed statistically significant.

Results

Patient characteristics

We identified 175 patients meeting the inclusion criteria, with 112 having received daptomycin and 63 having received vancomycin (see Figure S1 for excluded patients). Baseline characteristics were mostly similar between groups (Table 1). Chronic kidney disease prior to initiation of therapy was common between the daptomycin and vancomycin groups (43% versus 35%). Approximately half of patients had a BMI ≥30 kg/m2. Of the monomicrobial infections, MRSA was most common. The average duration of therapy was 34.1 days in the daptomycin group compared with 34.9 days in the vancomycin group, most receiving therapy at home (Table 1).

Outcomes

For the primary outcome of early discontinuation, there was no statistically significant difference between the daptomycin and vancomycin groups [15/112 (13%) versus 9/63 (14%); P = 0.87] (Table 2). The most common reason for early discontinuation was the occurrence of an adverse event, with a numerically higher percentage in the daptomycin group [10/15 (67%) versus 5/9 (56%); P = 0.68] that did not reach statistical significance. The rate of adverse drug events per 1000 patient days was 2.65 for daptomycin versus 2.23 for vancomycin. For the daptomycin group, adverse events included seven patients with an elevation in CK (peak median CK 409 IU/L) and three patients with a clinical diagnosis of rhabdomyolysis (Table 2). Adverse events in the vancomycin group included two patients with AKI, two patients with rash, and one occurrence of ototoxicity (Table 2). The median time in days to adverse event occurrence was lower in the daptomycin group (12 versus 30 days, Table 2). For those in the vancomycin group, the AUC goal was attained 75% of the time during treatment.

Discussion

Based on the findings of this study, the occurrence of adverse events resulting in a change in antimicrobial therapy or early discontinuation was similar between the daptomycin and vancomycin cohorts. These findings differ from previous studies. In 2014, a retrospective cohort study found patients treated with daptomycin experienced 60% fewer adverse events compared with vancomycin. Patients given daptomycin required 80% fewer antimicrobial interventions.7 A retrospective study conducted in 2018 found a significantly higher incidence of adverse events associated with vancomycin compared with daptomycin in OPAT. The most common adverse events reported included hypersensitivity reactions and AKI.8 A retrospective cohort study conducted in 2024 found no difference in the total number of events requiring intervention but did find an increase in line infections and emergency department visits associated with vancomycin. Conversely, the study found a numerically higher incidence in adverse events associated with daptomycin compared with vancomycin.10

Although previous studies found a higher risk of adverse events associated with vancomycin versus daptomycin, it is unclear whether those studies used trough-based or AUC-based dosing. AUC compared with trough has been shown to have a lower incidence of AKI in individual comparison studies.11,12 Patients given vancomycin and enrolled in our institution’s OPAT programme have tailored regimens using Bayesian dosing software integrated within the electronic medical record. This software may have mitigated the risk of nephrotoxicity associated with vancomycin, which is demonstrated by the low occurrence of AKI and 75% target AUC attainment. Of the two patients who experienced AKI while on vancomycin, the severity of both was classified as stage II per the KDIGO criteria and rapidly improved to baseline upon discontinuation of vancomycin.13

It should be noted that patients in the daptomycin group with adverse events had an OPAT indication for either osteomyelitis or prosthetic joint infection, and these indications often require higher dosing (Table 1). Approximately half of patients in the daptomycin group had a BMI ≥30 kg/m2 (Table 1). These factors could have increased the risk of an adverse event associated with greater daptomycin exposure. Of the adverse events in the daptomycin group, seven patients had therapy discontinued due to an elevation in CK, with a median CK of 409 IU/L. Although this does not meet manufacturer recommendations for cessation of daptomycin, early discontinuation rates are likely a reflection of the clinician’s proactive response because of close monitoring.14

Our institution’s OPAT programme employed a full-time OPAT pharmacist in September 2022. This pharmacist works closely with the infectious disease providers and nurses to ensure OPAT treatment plans are adhered to, therapeutic drug monitoring is conducted appropriately, and dosing regimens are optimized to reduce the occurrence of adverse events. The addition of this role into the programme structure created a more streamlined monitoring process that likely improved patient safety and treatment outcomes.

The strengths of this study include the comparison of daptomycin versus AUC-based vancomycin dosing. To our knowledge, previous studies have not reported using this dosing strategy for vancomycin in comparison with daptomycin. The OPAT programme has undergone revisions to workflow with the implementation of standardized notes and consistency in dosing practices between providers. These aspects aided accurate documentation and reduced the risk of bias.

Some weaknesses of the study include the retrospective study design and risk for confounding variables, which could have increased the risk of adverse events thereby limiting the generalizability of the findings. If patients enrolled in the OPAT programme received care from an institution not integrated into the electronic medical record, information could have been unavailable.

In conclusion, this study demonstrates that in patients for whom either daptomycin or vancomycin (dosed by AUC) may be appropriate for OPAT, there was no difference in the occurrence of adverse events resulting in a change in antimicrobial therapy or early discontinuation.

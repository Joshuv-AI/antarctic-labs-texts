Platelet-Rich Plasma Fibrin Glue Dressing Combined with a Novel Repairing Gel for Healing Recalcitrant Diabetic Foot Ulcers: Findings from an Exploratory Randomized Pilot Trial
Authors: Elaheh Emadi, Mohammad-Hadi Saeed-Modaghegh, Alireza Mousavian, Hamid Reza Rahimi, Daryoush Hamidi-Alamdari
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
PurposeRecalcitrant diabetic foot ulcers (DFUs) remain difficult to heal and impose substantial clinical and economic burdens. This exploratory, four-arm, parallel-group randomized pilot trial aimed to evaluate whether combining platelet-rich plasma-fibrin glue (PRP‑FG) with a novel repairing gel enhances healing outcomes in DFUs compared with standard care and single‑agent therapies.Patients and MethodsIn this study, twenty patients with DFUs unresponsive to at least four weeks of standard treatment were randomized using computer-generated blocks with allocation concealment to receive PRP‑FG alone, repairing gel alone, the combination therapy, or standard care. Allogeneic PRP‑FG was prepared by gradient‑density centrifugation. The repairing gel contained multiple active components, including vitamins A, B3, and C, collagen, glycine, organic acids, sodium alginate, carboxymethyl cellulose, benzoic acid, gentian violet, methylene blue, triethanolamine, bromelain, allantoin, and dimethyl sulfoxide. Wound healing rate over 12 weeks served as the primary outcome, assessed weekly by a blinded evaluator.ResultsNineteen patients completed the 12‑week follow‑up. Complete epithelialization occurred in 100% (5/5) of participants receiving the combined therapy, 20% (1/5) of those receiving the repairing gel alone, and 0% (0/5) in the PRP‑FG alone and standard care groups. The combined therapy significantly accelerated ulcer healing compared with all other groups (p < 0.05). These findings are preliminary and derived from a small pilot sample. No serious adverse events were observed.ConclusionThis exploratory pilot trial suggests that combined PRP‑FG and repairing gel therapy may promote superior healing of recalcitrant DFUs compared with standard care or single‑agent treatments. Larger, multicenter clinical trials are warranted to validate these preliminary findings.

Introduction

Worldwide, diabetes mellitus has become a major public health crisis, and recent projections estimate that by 2050, it may affect approximately 853 million people.1,2 Among the most debilitating complications are diabetic foot ulcers (DFUs), affecting up to 34% of patients during their lifetime.3 Despite standard care, including glucose control, debridement, and off-loading, approximately 50% of DFUs become recalcitrant, failing to heal within 4 weeks.4–9 These chronic wounds are characterized by a stalled physiological progression, remaining trapped in a state of sustained inflammation and impaired cellular response.10,11

Normal wound healing is a coordinated sequence of inflammation, proliferation, and remodeling.12,13 In recalcitrant DFUs, however, this process is disrupted by a hostile microenvironment: tissue hypoxia, excessive protease activity, and a deficiency in essential growth factors. Furthermore, impaired neuropeptide signaling and biofilm formation create a barrier to endogenous repair.3,5,14,15 Consequently, effective therapy must not only provide missing biological signals but also modulate the biochemical environment to allow those signals to function.5,13

Platelet-rich plasma–fibrin glue (PRP-FG) has recently gained increasing attention as a potential adjunctive therapeutic approach because it mimics the physiological clotting process while delivering a concentrated cocktail of growth factors. The fibrin matrix acts as a biological scaffold, facilitating chemotaxis and angiogenesis.16–18 However, the clinical efficacy of PRP-FG in chronic wounds is often limited by the hostile wound bed. High levels of inflammatory proteases can rapidly degrade the exogenous growth factors before they can stimulate cellular regeneration, and the lack of a controlled moisture balance can impede the migration of cells across the fibrin scaffold.5,14,19,20

The biological plausibility of this multi-component gel lies in its integrated approach to modifying the wound microenvironment. By incorporating bromelain21 and organic acids,22 the formulation facilitates autolytic debridement of necrotic debris, while urea23 acts to enhance skin hydration and tissue permeability, thereby preparing the wound bed for cellular influx. To counteract the oxidative stress and structural deficiencies inherent in chronic wounds, the inclusion of vitamins C24 and A,25 alongside glycine26 and collagen,27 provides the necessary precursors for robust extracellular matrix (ECM) synthesis. Furthermore, the hydrocolloid properties of sodium alginate28 and carboxymethyl cellulose (CMC)29 ensure a sustained moist healing environment and sequester excess exudate, which serves to protect the delicate PRP-FG scaffold from desiccation and premature degradation. Crucially, the gel stabilizes the wound’s chemical profile through a combination of methylene blue, gentian violet,30 and benzoic acid;31 these agents not only offer broad-spectrum antimicrobial protection but also maintain a skin-compatible acidic pH (4.5–5.5), an essential factor for suppressing bacterial proliferation and optimizing the enzymatic activity required for tissue regeneration.32

While the individual benefits of these components are established, evidence is lacking regarding their integrated use as a delivery vehicle for autologous blood products. Specifically, there is a gap in understanding whether a complex topical gel can stabilize the PRP-FG environment and enhance its regenerative potential in a clinical setting. This preliminary, exploratory pilot study was designed to evaluate the feasibility and signals of efficacy for this novel combination therapy. We aimed to evaluate the synergistic effect of PRP-FG and our multi-component repairing gel on the healing rates of recalcitrant DFUs, providing the first clinical rationale for this integrated approach.

Materials and Methods

Ethics Statements

This early-phase exploratory trial was registered in the ClinicalTrials.gov database (ClinicalTrials.gov Identifier: NCT05983302; initial release June 30, 2023, last updated March 22, 2024) and received approval from the Ethics Committee of Mashhad University of Medical Sciences (IR.MUMS.REC.1402.089). Additionally, the study was recorded in the Iranian Registry of Clinical Trials (IRCT Identifier: IRCT20191228045924N5). All participants provided written informed consent before enrollment, and the study was performed in compliance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki.

Study Participants and Criteria

This pilot study was carried out for 20 patients with recalcitrant DFU who were referred to two medical centers in Mashhad, Iran, Imam Reza Academic Hospital and the private super-specialized vascular surgery clinic, between August 2023 and March 2024. Participants met the following inclusion criteria: (1) presence of Wagner grade 2 to 4 DFUs with a surface area of at least 2 cm2; (2) DFUs demonstrating no significant reduction in ulcer size (<20%) despite receiving standard treatment for at least four weeks; (3) an ankle-brachial index (ABI) of ≥ 0.7; and (4) no use of medications that could interfere with ulcer healing, including immunosuppressants, corticosteroids, and cytotoxic agents.

Participants who missed follow-up appointments and dressing changes at the designated centers on two or more occasions were excluded from the study.

Trial Design

Following the initial assessment, participants were randomly allocated to one of four groups, each consisting of five individuals. Group 1 received a PRP-FG dressing, Group 2 received a repairing gel, Group 3 received both a PRP-FG dressing and a repairing gel, and Group 4, the control group, received standard treatment only. To enhance the validity of the conclusions, participants with similar ulcer locations and sizes were stratified before random assignment.

Randomization assignments were generated using computer-based randomization with block sizes of 4. Allocation concealment was ensured through the use of sequentially numbered, opaque, sealed envelopes, with each envelope containing a single treatment allocation. Each envelope was opened after the participant met all the eligibility criteria and after providing informed consent, and the participant was assigned to the study arm. According to this protocol, neither the participant nor the study personnel were aware of the upcoming assignment, thus avoiding selection bias. A biostatistician, independent of patient recruitment, treatment delivery, or outcome assessment, was solely responsible for managing the random allocation process and participant enrollment according to the aforementioned protocol. Participants and treating clinicians were not blinded to the assigned treatment arm due to the nature of the interventions. Awareness of the treatment may have influenced participant behavior or clinician management beyond the study protocol, introducing performance bias. To mitigate this, standard treatment protocols were strictly followed, and all patient interactions were recorded.

To address potential detection bias, the outcome assessor responsible for data collection and analysis did not have access to allocation records during the assessment period and was blinded to group allocation. This assessor was not involved in any treatment delivery, dressing changes, or debridement. Wounds were cleaned, and all previous dressings were removed by the treating nurse before the assessor entered the room for evaluation. A standard assessment protocol was used for objective outcome measures (wound area measurements and photographic documentation), and the assessor underwent specific training to ensure consistency and minimize subjective interpretation.

The trial duration was 12 weeks, during which all participants were scheduled to have their ulcer dressings changed two or three times a week. Throughout the study, participants were regularly assessed for possible adverse events by a vascular surgeon. When clinically indicated, additional evaluation was conducted by a foot and ankle orthopedic surgeon.

Complete epithelialization at 12 weeks was considered the main clinical outcome of interest in this preliminary analysis and was defined as full re-epithelialization with absence of drainage and no dressing requirement. Additional outcomes included percent change in wound area at 4 and 12 weeks, time to healing, and safety outcomes. These outcomes were selected as a prespecified subset of the broader outcomes registered for the clinical trial.

Preparation of PRP-FG and Repairing Gel

Allogeneic PRP-FG was prepared according to a previously established method.33 In this way, 400 mL of peripheral blood that passed all viral safety tests according to blood transfusion regulations was received from the blood bank. To precipitate red blood cells, it was centrifuged at 1800 x g for 10 minutes, and a second centrifugation at 4000 x g for 15 minutes was performed to separate the platelet-poor plasma (PPP, 175 mL) from the precipitated PRP (25 mL). The separated PPP from the previous step was used to prepare concentrated fibrinogen (FG) by the cryoprecipitation method. For this purpose, PPP was frozen at −70°C for 24 hours and then thawed at 4°C and centrifuged at 6500 x g for 5 minutes at 4°C. The supernatant was removed up to 25 mL. The platelet concentration in the PRP fraction before mixing with FG was ≥ 1.0×106 platelets/µL, representing a 4-5-fold concentration from baseline whole blood. FG was combined with isolated PRP to create PRP-FG, resulting in a final volume of 50 mL. The resulting PRP-FG was stored in a −20°C freezer for up to 3 months before use. This storage condition maintains platelet integrity and the bioactivity of growth factors upon thawing.34 The freeze-thaw process is intended to release growth factors from platelet alpha-granules, enhancing the regenerative potential of the product.35 The pH of the final PRP-FG mixture was measured and was found to be in the physiologically acceptable range of 6.8–7.4.

The repairing gel was prepared using pharmaceutical-grade raw components to ensure quality and reproducibility. Those were vitamin A36 (0.2%, Arshine Pharmaceutical Company Limited, Changsha, China), vitamin C37,38 (2%, Jiangsu Jiangshan Pharmaceutical, Jiangsu, China), vitamin B339 (0.5%, Arshine Pharmaceutical Company Limited, Changsha, China), glycine amino acid26,40 (0.5%, Arshine Pharmaceutical Company Limited, Changsha, China), ethanol41,42 (2%, Shadan Takhmir Khorasan, Iran), collagen27 (1%, GELITA® Deutschland GmbH, Eberbach, Germany), citric acid43 (2%, Shanghai Tianjia Biochemical, China), glycerin44 (0.5%, AksharChem, India), malic acid22,45 (1%, Guangzhou ZIO Chemical, China), urea46,47 (35%, Otto Chemie, Mumbai India), carboxymethyl cellulose29,48 (CMC, 2%, Jining Fortune Biotech., China), sodium alginate28,49 (1%, Arshine Gum, China), benzoic acid31,50,51 (0.002%, Huangshi Taihua Industrial, China), bromelain21 (0.5%, Sinoway Industrial, China), methylene blue30,52,53 (0.01%, Vanshi Chemicals, India), gentian violet30,54,55 (0.1%, Pandora Industries, India), allantoin56 (1%, Xiamen Hisunny Chemical, China), dimethyl sulfoxide57,58 (DMSO, 0.1%, Vizag Chemical, India) and triethanolamine32,59 (Arihant Solvents and Chemicals, India). Triethanolamine was used to adjust the final pH of the gel to between 4.5 and 5.5. This mildly acidic range is compatible with skin physiology, contributing to preservation of antimicrobial activity and enhanced skin barrier function.32

The antibacterial activity of the formulation was evaluated and confirmed by the Department of Medical Bacteriology and Virology at Imam Reza Hospital, Mashhad, Iran. It has an antibacterial effect against Escherichia coli (minimum inhibitory concentration dilution (MICD):1/4), Acinetobacter baumannii (MICD:1/6), Staphylococcus aureus (MICD:1/24), Klebsiella pneumoniae (MICD:1/4), Enterococcus sp. (MICD:1/12), and Pseudomonas aeruginosa (MICD:1/4). This gel was prepared under aseptic conditions. However, it was a non-sterile topical product, but its proven antimicrobial impact led to a secondary barrier against infection. Its formulation was physically stable in a light-resistant tube (no phase separation, color change, or significant change in viscosity) for 6 months at 4 to 25°C.

Ulcer Dressing Protocol

Standard ulcer management alongside their assigned trial dressings was implemented in all cases. This comprehensive management included the use of appropriate devices such as total contact casts, walkers, or specialized footwear to reduce pressure on the wound site, with compliance reviewed at each visit. Sharp or partial debridement to remove dead tissue and hyperkeratotic skin was performed by a vascular surgeon as clinically indicated. To minimize sharp debridement confounding effect on the trial interventions, a period of at least 24 hours was considered between any significant sharp debridement and the application of the trial dressings. Infection management was based on clinical assessment, including increased pain, redness, purulent discharge, and blood test results, with systemic antibiotics administered when necessary. Blood glucose control was managed with regular monitoring of blood glucose levels. Following the standard management, trial dressings were applied directly to the ulcers as outlined below.

In the group receiving only PRP dressing, an adequate amount of PRP-FG (approximately 100–200 μL per cm2 of ulcer area) was applied topically to create a thin, continuous gel layer on the ulcer bed and allowed to clot for 10 minutes. The ulcer was then covered with Vaseline gauze. Subsequently, several sterile gauze pads were placed over the Vaseline gauze and secured with a non-compressive bandage to offload the ulcer.

In the group receiving only the repairing gel, an adequate amount of the repairing gel was applied to the ulcer to cover the entire ulcer area, with a layer thickness of approximately 2–3 mm (approximately 200–300 μL per cm2 of ulcer area). It was then covered with Vaseline gauze, followed by the placement of several sterile gauze pads on top, secured with a non-compressive bandage to offload the ulcer.

In the group receiving both PRP-FG dressing and repairing gel therapy, an amount of PRP-FG using the same volume guidelines as above was applied topically to the ulcer and allowed to clot for 10 minutes. The repairing gel was then applied over the coagulated PRP-FG and covered with Vaseline gauze. Finally, a sterile gauze pad was placed over the Vaseline gauze and secured with a non-compressive bandage.

The frequency of dressing change was based on the condition of the ulcer (usually two to three changes per week).

Ulcer Healing Assessment

Ulcers were evaluated at the beginning of the study, at the 4 th week, and until complete closure or until the 12th week. Before each weekly assessment, the ulcer was thoroughly cleaned with sterile saline to remove all dressing residues. The maximum length and width of each ulcer were measured using a ruler. The ulcer area was measured using photographs taken with a Samsung Galaxy S smartphone. A sterile ruler (with millimeter markings) was placed adjacent to the ulcer for calibration in each photograph. Images were transferred to a computer, identified, and coded to ensure blinding of the assessor. The surface areas of the ulcers were determined using Fiji software (ImageJ 2.16.0/1/54p, Java 1.8.0_322 (64-bit)) by manually tracing the wound perimeter and calibrating against the ruler. Measurements were computed for each participant at baseline and after 4 and 12 weeks of treatment. Infection evaluation according to the guidelines of the International Working Group on the Diabetic Foot (IWGDF) and Infectious Diseases Society of America (IDSA) criteria included assessment for purulent discharge, erythema, warmth, swelling, fever, and increasing pain for each ulcer during the trial. If at least two of these criteria were present, the patient was considered to be positive for infection.60

At the end of the study, the clinical results were defined based on evaluation as follows: healed (complete re-epithelialization of the ulcer without exudation and the absence of an epidermal defect), improved (reduction in the size of the ulcer area and improvement in the wound bed condition), static (no significant change in the size of the ulcer or ulcer bed condition) and deteriorated (increasing the size of the ulcer and worsening the ulcer bed condition).61

Safety Monitoring and Adverse Events (AEs)

AEs were prospectively collected and documented at each study visit. Predefined AEs included local irritation, pruritus, erythema, pain (at application site), rash, contact dermatitis, allergic reactions, and worsening wound infection according to IWGDF/IDSA criteria (increased purulent exudate, erythema, warmth, pain, or foul odor). The severity of AEs was assessed according to the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE), version 5.0, with grades ranging from 0 (no AE) to 5 (death): Grade 1, mild; Grade 2, moderate; Grade 3, severe requiring medical intervention; and Grade 4, life-threatening. AEs were assessed and documented by the vascular surgeon at every weekly follow-up visit. Patients were also instructed to report any immediate or delayed unusual symptoms between visits. The study protocol specified the following stopping criteria for each participant: any AE of Grade 3 or higher, any Grade 2 allergic reaction, or the occurrence of an unexpected serious adverse event considered attributable to the intervention. The study was paused for a full safety review by the ethics committee if AEs occurred in more than 20% of participants in any group.

Statistical Analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using the Statistical Package for the Social Sciences (SPSS; Version 16, Chicago, IL, USA) and R (Version 4.3.2). A two-sided p-value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Baseline ulcer sizes across groups were compared using the Kruskal–Wallis H-test, while within-group changes from before to after the intervention were assessed using the Wilcoxon signed-rank test. For these non-parametric tests, the effect size was calculated as r using the formula r = |Z|/√N, where N represents the total number of subjects.

After 12 weeks of treatment, a statistically significant difference in ulcer healing was observed among the groups (p = 0.006). Complete epithelialization was observed in 5/5 (100%) of patients receiving combined PRP-FG + repairing gel. In contrast, only 1/5 (20%) of those treated with repairing gel alone achieved complete epithelialization, while none in the PRP-FG (0/5) or standard care (0/5) groups did.

Furthermore, a significant reduction in the surface area of the ulcers within groups 2, 3, and 4 was observed (p ˂ 0.05). The combined treatment approach (PRP-FG dressing and repairing gel) resulted in the highest average percentage of reduction in ulcer size (100 ± 0%). In Group 1 (PRP-FG dressing), the reduction in wound area did not reach statistical significance (p = 0.068). However, the analysis indicated a strong trend towards healing, supported by a very large effect size (r=0.913). The median difference in reduction was 4.7 cm2 (95% CI: 1.8 to 13.4). A statistically significant reduction in wound area was also observed in Group 2 (Repairing gel) (p = 0.043). This change demonstrated a large effect size (r=0.905). The median difference in wound area reduction for this group was 6.03 cm2 (95% CI: 2.9 to 18.4). The combined treatment group (Group 3: PRP-FG + Repairing gel) showed the most potent outcome, with a statistically significant reduction in wound area (p = 0.043). This effect was associated with a large effect size (r=0.905). The median difference in wound area reduction was estimated at 6.24 cm2 (95% CI: 2.2 to 10.2), indicating a strong and clinically meaningful therapeutic effect. In Group 4 (Standard treatment), the reduction in wound area was statistically significant (p = 0.043). This change demonstrated a large effect size (r=0.905). The median difference in wound area reduction for this group was 5.2 cm2 (95% CI: 0.71 to 8.5).

The results of this trial demonstrate that the combination of PRP-FG dressing and the novel repairing gel significantly improves healing outcomes in patients with recalcitrant DFUs compared to standard treatment and either treatment alone.

Adherence to the predefined safety monitoring protocol was high. No serious AEs, Grade 3–5 events, or allergic reactions were reported in any treatment group. The most common AEs were mild (Grade 1) local skin reactions. Transient application-site irritation was reported by three patients (60%) in Group 2 and one patient in Group 3; all instances resolved spontaneously without requiring discontinuation of treatment. This finding is consistent with the known potential for mild irritation associated with components such as malic acid or a low-pH gel formulation. No AEs were reported in Groups 1 or 4. None of the observed AEs met the criteria for predefined stopping rules, and no participant withdrawals were attributable to an AE. The overall safety profile across all groups was favorable.

Discussion

The results of this exploratory pilot trial suggest that the combined application of PRP-FG dressing and the novel repairing gel, which contains approved components, may provide superior healing outcomes in recalcitrant DFUs compared with standard treatment alone or either intervention used separately. This finding emphasizes the potential of combined therapies in managing recalcitrant DFUs, an area that remains a significant challenge in clinical practice. It is imperative to emphasize that the positive outcomes observed with the PRP-FG and repairing gel combination in this trial were achieved within a rigorous framework of comprehensive DFU management, consistent with international guidelines (IWGDF). As detailed in the methods section, all participants, including those in the control group, received standardized care that encompassed strict glycemic control, appropriate off-loading, regular sharp debridement, and effective infection management. The investigated combination therapy should therefore be viewed as a powerful adjunct designed to modulate the local wound environment, not as a substitute for these foundational pillars of DFU care. Its role is to accelerate healing in recalcitrant wounds that have failed to respond to standard management alone.

Although baseline characteristics were not statistically different between groups, the marginally smaller mean ulcer size in the combination group may nevertheless have contributed to the observed treatment response. Therefore, the 100% complete epithelialization rate should be interpreted cautiously. Given the exploratory nature of this pilot trial, the limited sample size, and the possibility of residual baseline imbalances, these findings should be regarded as preliminary evidence that generates hypotheses rather than definitive proof of clinical superiority. Nevertheless, the consistency of the observed healing response supports further evaluation of this combination therapy in adequately powered, multicenter randomized controlled trials.

The efficacy of PRP applications in wound healing is well-documented.20,62 PRP is rich in growth factors such as platelet-derived growth factor (PDGF), transforming growth factor-β (TGFβ), fibroblast growth factors (FGF), epidermal growth factor (EGF), insulin-like growth factors (IGFs), vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF), and endothelial cell growth factor (ECGF), which induce chemotaxis, cell proliferation, angiogenesis, and cell differentiation. PRP is also rich in bioactive non-growth factors such as adenosine, serotonin, dopamine, histamine, and calcium, which fundamentally affect the biological aspects of tissue repair. FG also acts as a temporary extracellular matrix and scaffold and leads to promoting cell migration, delivering platelets, and facilitating wound closure.17,18 However, there are limitations to this approach, including the inadequate penetration of growth factors into the wound bed, insufficient topical formulation, protein degradation in the bacterial microenvironment of the wound bed, and the inability to use a growth factor in concert with others due to regulatory obstacles.19 Hence, a focus on finding new clinical strategies to improve the efficiency and progression of the healing response is required. The researchers evaluated the effect of the combined use of different materials and cells along with PRP in wound healing, and the advantageous effects of some of these have been confirmed during the investigations.18,33,63–71

The present pilot study investigated the impact of a novel repairing gel along with PRP-FG in the healing of chronic wounds. The simultaneous use of repairing gel and PRP-FG evolved to address potential barriers to chronic wound healing, including infection, better penetration of platelets, and effective substances for neovascularization. The proposed mechanism of action for this combined therapy is that the repairing gel, with its demonstrated in vitro antibacterial activity and components known to promote autolytic debridement (e.g, bromelain, urea), may help create a favorable healing environment by reducing bacterial burden and facilitating the clearance of necrotic tissue. Concurrently, it delivers active substances intended to support ECM formation, while the PRP-FG provides a scaffold and a high concentration of growth factors.

In this study, allogeneic PRP-FG was preferred over autologous formulations. This approach avoids the clinical burden of large-volume blood collection in diabetic patients who may suffer from anemia or poor venous access. Furthermore, utilizing screened blood-bank donor sources allows for a highly standardized, quality-controlled product with consistent growth factor profiles, overcoming the therapeutic variability often associated with autologous PRP in patients with chronic metabolic disorders.16,20,33,34,62

The therapeutic efficacy of the gel formulation is attributed to a synergistic combination of bioactive compounds targeting the multifaceted pathology of chronic wounds. Inflammation and oxidative stress are mitigated by the inclusion of vitamins A, B3, and C, collagen, methylene blue, glycine, and bromelain.21,25,27,39,72–83 To address bioburden, agents such as ethanol, gentian violet, and benzoic acid function to reduce bacterial load and prevent infection.28,30,50,75,84–88 Tissue regeneration is further accelerated by vitamins, DMSO, collagen, allantoin, bromelain, CMC and urea, which stimulate crucial cellular processes including migration, proliferation, and angiogenesis.21,25,27,29,39,41,58,72,73,79,82,88–90 Additionally, the integration of sodium alginate, CMC, malic acid, urea, allantoin, bromelain and glycerin optimizes the wound microenvironment by maintaining moisture balance27–29,75,82,87,88,91–93 and facilitating autolytic debridement.29,80,82,87,88 Finally, the inclusion of citric and malic acids ensures an acidic pH, which is fundamental to protease regulation and successful wound closure.75,94–96 The unique properties of each component, along with their potential synergistic interactions, play a crucial role in addressing the challenges associated with managing chronic DFUs. While the concentrations of the active components were selected based on existing literature for topical wound healing, the specific pharmacological contribution of each component, beyond the broad-spectrum antimicrobial effect demonstrated in vitro, and potential synergistic effects within the complex wound environment remain areas for future investigation.

When compared to other PRP-based combination therapies for DFUs, the results of this pilot trial appear highly promising. As summarized in Table 2, studies combining PRP with oral antioxidants, mesenchymal stem cells, or negative pressure therapy have reported complete healing rates ranging from 46% to 94% over longer durations.33,97,98 In this context, the 100% complete epithelialization rate achieved within 12 weeks in our combination therapy group is notable, especially considering the recalcitrant nature of the included ulcers. While direct comparisons are constrained by differences in trial design and population, this preliminary efficacy signal suggests that the novel repairing gel may be a potent adjunct to PRP-FG. However, this comparison should be interpreted with caution because differences in study populations, ulcer characteristics, treatment protocols, and outcome definitions limit direct cross-trial comparisons. Accordingly, the present findings should primarily be viewed as supportive evidence for further hypothesis-driven investigation rather than confirmation of superior efficacy. Recent advances in wound healing have increasingly emphasized the development of multifunctional therapeutic platforms that simultaneously address inflammation, infection control, angiogenesis, and extracellular matrix remodeling. For instance, novel biomaterial-based dressings, bioactive hydrogels, immunomodulatory scaffolds, laser-assisted therapies, topical beta-blockers, and optimized negative pressure wound therapy have shown promising results in preclinical and clinical settings.100–108 These studies reinforce the current paradigm that effective management of chronic and recalcitrant wounds often requires combinatorial or multimodal strategies rather than single-mechanism interventions.

In this context, the rationale for our combined PRP-FG and repairing gel approach aligns with these recent advances by integrating a rich source of regenerative growth factors and a biological scaffold (PRP-FG) with a multi-component gel possessing antimicrobial, anti-inflammatory, autolytic debriding, and ECM-supporting properties. While the specific platforms differ, our preliminary findings add to the growing body of evidence supporting the potential value of multimodal interventions for recalcitrant DFUs. Larger randomized controlled trials are warranted to confirm these observations.

The absence of significant adverse events in our study underscores the safety profile of both the PRP-FG dressing and the repairing gel. This finding aligns with previous research indicating minimal complications associated with these interventions.109,110 Notably, while mild local irritation was reported in a small number of patients, it resolved spontaneously without the need for additional intervention. Such a safety profile is critical when evaluating treatment options for DFUs, particularly given that patients with DFUs often present with comorbid conditions that complicate healing and increase their susceptibility to infection.111

Although the trial’s small sample size and exploratory design limit the generalizability of these findings, the observed healing outcomes are consistent with the primary objective of evaluating the feasibility and potential therapeutic value of combining PRP-FG with the repairing gel. Rather than establishing definitive clinical efficacy, this study provides an encouraging efficacy signal and supports the biological rationale for investigating this combined approach in larger, adequately powered, multicenter trials with balanced baseline characteristics and rigorous methodological control. Future studies should also evaluate the durability of wound closure, recurrence rates, and cost-effectiveness.

While the repairing gel was prepared in-house for this exploratory trial, its formulation utilizes established, pharmaceutical-grade ingredients. Our long-term objective is to transition to scalable, cost-effective GMP production through partnerships with industrial manufacturers, thereby enhancing accessibility for wound care centers. Acknowledging the limitations imposed by the trial’s small sample size on the generalizability of our findings, this study nonetheless serves as a vital preliminary investigation into combined therapeutic modalities for ulcer treatment. Future research should prioritize exploring the underlying biological mechanisms contributing to the enhanced efficacy observed with the combination treatment. Furthermore, larger multi-center studies encompassing a more diverse participant demographic are essential to validate these promising initial results.

Limitations

Several limitations of the present study should be acknowledged. First, as an exploratory pilot trial, the sample size was modest (n=5 per group), constraining the statistical power and the generalizability of the findings. Consequently, there is an inherent risk of Type I and Type II errors; thus, the results should be viewed as preliminary signals of efficacy and feasibility rather than definitive clinical evidence. While no statistically significant differences were observed at baseline, the small cohort size means that residual imbalances between groups cannot be entirely ruled out. Furthermore, although outcome assessment was blinded, the open-label design may have introduced performance bias. Ultimately, these data are intended to be hypothesis-generating rather than confirmatory, serving to inform the design of future, adequately powered pivotal trials.

Conclusion

This exploratory pilot trial suggests that the combination of PRP-FG dressing and a novel repairing gel may improve healing outcomes for recalcitrant DFUs compared to standard care or monotherapy. While the 100% epithelialization observed in the combination group is encouraging, these preliminary results must be interpreted with caution given the small cohort, potential baseline imbalances, and brief follow-up period. Rather than establishing definitive clinical superiority, this study provides a promising signal of potential efficacy and demonstrates the feasibility of this combined approach. These hypothesis-generating findings justify adequately powered, multicenter randomized controlled trials to rigorously evaluate long-term efficacy, safety, recurrence, and the durability of ulcer closure. In addition, the proposed biological interactions between PRP-FG and the repairing gel were not directly investigated in the present study and require dedicated experimental validation.

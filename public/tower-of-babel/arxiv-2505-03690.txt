The Magnetic Laplacian with a Higher-order Vanishing Magnetic Field in a Bounded Domain
Zhongwei Shen

Abstract
This paper is concerned with spectrum properties of the magnetic Laplacian with a higher-order vanishing magnetic field in a bounded domain. We study the asymptotic behaviors of ground state energies for the Dirichlet Laplacian, the Neumann Laplacian, and the Dirichlet-to-Neumann operator, as the field strength parameter β \beta goes to infinite. Assume that the magnetic field does not vanish to infinite order, we establish the leading orders of β \beta . We also obtain the first terms in the asymptotic expansions with remainder estimates under additional assumptions on an invariant subspace for a Taylor polynomial of the magnetic field. Our aim is to provide a unified approach to all three cases. Keywords. Schrödinger Operator; Magnetic Field; Ground State Energy; Bounded Domain. MR (2020) Subject Classification : 35P25.

\mathcal{B}(0,c_{0}r)\cap\Omega\subset E_{r}\subset\mathcal{B}(0,C_{0}r)\cap\Omega\subset\mathcal{B}(0,r_{0})\cap\Omega

(2.3)

Let

\widetilde{A}_{j}(x)=-\fint_{\mathcal{B}(y_{0},\delta r)}\left\{\sum_{k=1}^{d}(x_{k}-y_{k})\int_{0}^{1}B_{jk}(y+t(x-y))t\,dt\right\}dy,

\displaystyle|\widetilde{\mathbf{A}}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\fint_{B(y_{0},\delta r)}\left\{\int_{0}^{|y-x|}|\mathbf{B}\Big(x+t\frac{y-x}{|y-x|}\Big)|dt\right\}dy

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}d\omega\int_{0}^{\infty}W(x+t\omega)dt

Theorem 2.2

Remark 3.1
.

It follows from (

and

]

As a result, the inequalities (

Remark 3.2

Suppose

\max_{\mathcal{B}(x_{0},r)}|\nabla\mathbf{B}|\leq\frac{C}{r}\fint_{\mathcal{B}(x_{0},r)}|\mathbf{B}|

Remark 3.3

Suppose

It follows that for

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{|\alpha|\leq\kappa}|\partial^{\alpha}\mathbf{B}(x_{0})|r^{|\alpha|}\leq C\fint_{Q(x_{0},r)}|\mathbf{P}|

Furthermore, if

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
x_{0}\in\overline{\Omega}
and

,

We give the proof for the case

By Poincaré inequality,

where we have used the diamagnetic inequality
\big|\nabla|\psi|\big|\leq|(D+\mathbf{A})\psi|

c
in (

) depends only on
d
and the Lipschitz character of
\Omega
.

Note that by the assumption (

) and (

\displaystyle\fint_{\mathcal{B}(x_{0},r)\cap\Omega}\int_{\mathcal{B}(x_{0},r)\cap\Omega}\left\{\frac{c}{r^{2}}\big||\psi(x)|-|\psi(y)|\big|^{2}+tV(x)|\psi(x)|^{2}\right\}dxdy

\frac{c|E|}{2r^{2}|\mathcal{B}(x_{0},r)\cap\Omega|}\int_{\mathcal{B}(x_{0},r)\cap\Omega}|\psi|^{2}\leq(1+t)\int_{\mathcal{B}(x_{0},r)\cap\Omega}|(D+\mathbf{A})\psi|^{2},

5
Polynomial magnetic fields

Throughout this section we assume that the magnetic potential
\mathbf{A}
is a (vector-valued) homogeneous polynomial of degree

\kappa+1
. Thus,
the magnetic field

\mathbf{B}=\nabla\times\mathbf{A}
is a (matrix-valued) homogeneous polynomial of degree
\kappa
; i.e.,

\mathbf{B}(x)=\sum_{|\alpha|=\kappa}b_{\alpha}x^{\alpha}

(5.1)

for some constant
d\times d
matrices
\{b_{\alpha}\}
.
We also assume that
\sum_{|\alpha|=\kappa}|b_{\alpha}|=1
.
Note that such
\mathbf{B}
satisfies the condition (
3.1
) for any
\mathcal{B}(x_{0},r)\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}

with
\eta(t)=C_{0}t
, where
C_{0}
depends only on
d
and
\kappa
.
Moreover,

c\,\widetilde{m}(x,\mathbf{B})\leq m(x,\mathbf{B})\leq C\widetilde{m}(x,\mathbf{B}),

(5.2)

for some
C,c>0
depending only on
d
and
\kappa
, where

\widetilde{m}(x,\mathbf{B})=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq\kappa}|\partial^{\alpha}\mathbf{B}(x)|^{\frac{1}{|\alpha|+2}}.

(5.3)

The inequalities in (
5.2
) follow from the observation that if
\mathbf{P}
is a polynomial of degree
\kappa
, then

c\max_{Q(x_{0},r)}|\mathbf{P}|\leq\sum_{|\alpha|\leq\kappa}|\partial^{\alpha}\mathbf{P}(x_{0})|r^{|\alpha|}\leq C\max_{Q(x_{0},r)}|\mathbf{P}|,

for any
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
r>0
,
where
C,c>0
depend only on
d
and
\kappa
.
In particular, by (
5.2
) and the assumption
\sum_{|\alpha|=k}|b_{\alpha}|=1
,

m(x,\mathbf{B})\geq c\sum_{|\alpha|=\kappa}|b_{\alpha}|^{\frac{1}{\kappa+2}}\geq c_{0},

(5.4)

where
c_{0}>0
depends only on
d
and
\kappa
.
This, together with (
3.32
), (
3.33
) and (
3.38
), shows that

c\leq\lambda(\mathbf{A},\mathbb{R}^{d}),\lambda^{D}(\mathbf{A},\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}),\lambda^{N}(\mathbf{A},\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}),\lambda^{D\!N}(\mathbf{A},\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})\leq C,

(5.5)

where
C,c>0
depend only on
d
and
\kappa
.

Definition
.

Let
\mathbf{B}
be given by (
5.1
).
The set

\left\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\ \mathbf{B}(x+y)=\mathbf{B}(x)\quad\text{ for any }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\right\}

\mathbf{B}
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
\mathbf{B}
be a homogeneous polynomial given by (
5.1
).
The invariant subspace for
\mathbf{B}
is given by

V=\left\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\langle y,\nabla\partial^{\alpha}B_{j\ell}(0)\rangle=0\text{ for any }1\leq j<\ell\leq d\text{ and }|\alpha|=\kappa-1\right\}

(5.7)

Proof.

Let
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Suppose
\mathbf{B}(x+y)=\mathbf{B}(x)
for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Since

\mathbf{B}(x+ty)=t^{\kappa}\mathbf{B}(t^{-1}x+y)=t^{\kappa}\mathbf{B}(t^{-1}x)=\mathbf{B}(x)

for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
t\neq 0
, it follows that
\partial_{t}\{\partial_{x}^{\alpha}B_{j\ell}(x+ty)\}=0

for any
1\leq j<\ell\leq d
and
|\alpha|=\kappa-1
.
Hence,
\langle y,\nabla\partial^{\alpha}B_{j\ell}(0)\rangle=0
for any
1\leq j<\ell\leq d
and
and
|\alpha|=\kappa-1
.

Suppose
y\in V
and
y\neq 0
.
Let
e_{1},e_{2},\dots,e_{d}
be an orthonormal basis for
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
e_{1}=y/|y|
.
Write

\mathbf{B}(x)=\sum_{|\alpha|=\kappa}\widetilde{b}_{\alpha}\langle x,e_{1}\rangle^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots\langle x,e_{d}\rangle^{\alpha_{d}},

(5.8)

where
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{d})
and
\widetilde{b}_{\alpha}=\partial_{e_{1}}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots\partial_{e_{d}}^{\alpha_{d}}\mathbf{B}(0)/\alpha!
.
Since
\partial_{e_{1}}\partial^{\alpha}B(0)=0
for any
|\alpha|=\kappa-1
,
it follows that
\widetilde{b}_{\alpha}=0
if
\alpha_{1}\neq 0
.
As a result, by (
5.8
), we obtain
\mathbf{B}(x+y)=\mathbf{B}(x)
for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
∎

Clearly, if the invariant subspace
V=\mathbb{R}^{d}
, then
\mathbf{B}
is constant and
\kappa=0
.
Suppose that dim
(V)<d
.
Then

\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\in V^{\perp}\\
|z|=1\end{subarray}}\sum_{|\alpha|=\kappa-1}\sum_{j,\ell}|\langle z,\nabla\partial^{\alpha}B_{j\ell}(0)\rangle|=\sigma>0.

(5.9)

Note that if
|\alpha|=\kappa-1
, we have
\partial^{\alpha}B_{j\ell}(z)=\langle z,\nabla\partial^{\alpha}B_{j\ell}(0)\rangle
.
It follows that if
x=y+z
, where
y\in V
and
z\in V^{\perp}
, then

\displaystyle\widetilde{m}(x,\mathbf{B})

\displaystyle=\widetilde{m}(z,\mathbf{B})\geq\sum_{|\alpha|=\kappa-1}|\partial^{\alpha}\mathbf{B}(z)|^{\frac{1}{\kappa+1}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{|\alpha|=\kappa-1}|z|^{\frac{1}{\kappa+1}}|\partial^{\alpha}\mathbf{B}(z/|z|)|^{\frac{1}{\kappa+1}}

\displaystyle\geq c\,\sigma^{\frac{1}{\kappa+1}}|z|^{\frac{1}{\kappa+1}},

provided
|z|>0
.
This shows that

\widetilde{m}(x,\mathbf{B})\geq c\,\sigma^{\frac{1}{\kappa+1}}\left\{\text{dist}(x,V)\right\}^{\frac{1}{\kappa+1}}

(5.10)

for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
where
\sigma>0
is given by (
5.9
).

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\mathbf{A}
be a homogeneous polynomial of degree
\kappa+1
.
Then, for

\lambda(\mathbf{A},\mathbb{R}^{d})\leq\lambda^{D}(\mathbf{A},\mathcal{B}(0,R))\leq\lambda(\mathbf{A},\mathbb{R}^{d})+CR^{-2},

where

\sigma

) and

Proof.

The first inequality in (

C_{0}^{1}(\mathcal{B}(0,R);\mathbb{C})\subset C_{0}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{C})

To show the second inequality, we first note that

\displaystyle\lambda^{D}(\mathbf{A},\mathcal{B}(0,R))

\displaystyle\leq\lambda^{D}(\mathbf{A},\mathcal{B}(0,1))

\displaystyle\leq C(1+\|\mathbf{B}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{B}(0,1))})\leq C.

Thus, we only need to consider the case where

is large.
Let
\{\varphi_{\ell}\}
be a sequence of functions such that

\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\varphi_{\ell}^{2}=1\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{d},

, and
\sum_{\ell}\chi_{\mathcal{B}(x_{\ell},R)}\leq C
.
Using the identity,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|(D+\mathbf{A})\psi|^{2}=\sum_{\ell}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|(D+\mathbf{A})(\psi\varphi_{\ell})|^{2}-\sum_{\ell}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla\varphi_{\ell}|^{2}|\psi|^{2},

(5.12)

we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|(D+\mathbf{A})\psi|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{\ell}\lambda^{D}(\mathbf{A},\mathcal{B}(x_{\ell},R))\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\varphi_{\ell}\psi|^{2}-C\sum_{\ell}R^{-2}\int_{\mathcal{B}(x_{\ell},R)}|\psi|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\left\{\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\lambda^{D}(\mathbf{A},\mathcal{B}(x,R))-CR^{-2}\right\}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\psi|^{2}

for any
\psi\in C_{0}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{C})
.
It follows that

\lambda(\mathbf{A},\mathbb{R}^{d})\geq\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\lambda^{D}(\mathbf{A},\mathcal{B}(x,R))-CR^{-2},

(5.13)

where
C
depends only on
d
.

Next,
let
V
be the invariant subspace for
\mathbf{B}
.
Note that

\lambda^{D}(\mathbf{A},\mathcal{B}(x+y,R))=\lambda^{D}(\mathbf{A},\mathcal{B}(x,R))\quad\text{ for any }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\text{ and }y\in V.

As a result, it suffices to show that

\lambda^{D}(\mathbf{A},\mathcal{B}(z,R))\geq\lambda^{D}(\mathbf{A},\mathcal{B}(0,3R)).

for any
z\in V^{\perp}
.
To this end, we observe that by (
5.2
) and (
3.32
),

c\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\{\widetilde{m}(x,\mathbf{B})\}^{2}|\psi|^{2}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|(D+\mathbf{A})\psi|^{2}.

In view of (
5.10
), for
z\in V^{\perp}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\lambda^{D}(\mathbf{A},\mathcal{B}(z,R))

\displaystyle\geq c\inf_{x\in\mathcal{B}(z,R)}\{\widetilde{m}(x,\mathbf{B})\}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq c\,\sigma^{\frac{2}{\kappa+1}}\inf_{x\in\mathcal{B}(z,R)}\left\{\text{\rm dist}(x,V)\right\}^{\frac{2}{\kappa+1}}

\displaystyle\geq c\,\sigma^{\frac{2}{\kappa+1}}\left\{\text{\rm dist}(z,V)-R\right\}^{\frac{2}{\kappa+1}}

\displaystyle\geq\lambda^{D}(\mathbf{A},\mathcal{B}(0,1))\geq\lambda^{D}(\mathbf{A},\mathcal{B}(0,3R)),

if
\text{\rm dist}(z,V)\geq 2R
and
R>C
is large.
Here we also use the fact
\lambda^{D}(\mathbf{A},\mathcal{B}(0,1))\approx 1
,
under the normalization
\sum_{|\alpha|=\kappa}|b_{\alpha}|=1
.

Finally, if
z\in V^{\perp}
and dist
(z,V)=|z|<2R
, then
\mathcal{B}(z,R)\subset\mathcal{B}(0,3R)
. Hence,

\lambda^{D}(\mathbf{A},\mathcal{B}(z,R))\geq\lambda^{D}(\mathbf{A},\mathcal{B}(0,3R)),

which completes the proof.
∎

For
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\times\{0\}=\partial\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}
, let

Q_{+}(x,R)=Q(x,R)\cap\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}.

(5.14)

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\mathbf{A}
be a homogeneous polynomial of degree
\kappa+1
.
Then,

\lambda^{D}(\mathbf{A},\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})\leq\lambda^{D}(\mathbf{A},Q_{+}(0,R))\leq\lambda^{D}(\mathbf{A},\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})+CR^{-2}

(5.15)

for
R>1
, where
C
depends on
\mathbf{B}
.

Proof.

Since
Q_{+}(0,R)\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}
, we have
\lambda^{D}(\mathbf{A},\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})\leq\lambda^{D}(\mathbf{A},Q_{+}(0,R))\leq C
for any
R>1
.
To show

\lambda^{D}(\mathbf{A},Q_{+}(0,R))\leq\lambda^{D}(\mathbf{A},\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})+CR^{-2}

(5.16)

for
R
large,
let
V
be the invariant subspace for
\mathbf{B}
and
k=\text{\rm dim}(V)
.
If
k=d
, then
\mathbf{B}
is constant.
The estimate follows readily by using a partition of unity.
Assume dim
(V)\leq d-1
.
We construct a parallelotope,

P=\big\{t_{1}e_{1}+t_{2}e_{2}+\cdots+t_{d}e_{d}:0<t_{j}<1\text{ for }1\leq j\leq d\big\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},

(5.17)

where
\{e_{1},e_{2},\dots,e_{d}\}
are unit vectors that form a basis for
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
These unit vectors are constructed as follows.

•

If
V\subset\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\times\{0\}
, choose
\{e_{1},e_{2},\dots,e_{d-1}\}
to form an orthonormal basis for
\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\times\{0\}

so that
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{k}\}
is an orthonormal basis for
V
.
Also, choose
e_{d}=(0,\dots,0,1)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}\cap V^{\perp}
.
In this case,
P
is a cube.

•

If
V\not\subset\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\times\{0\}
, choose
\{e_{1},e_{2},\dots,e_{d-1}\}
to form an orthonormal basis for
\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\times\{0\}

so that
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{k-1}\}\subset V
.
Also, choose
e_{d}\in V\cap\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}
such that
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{k-1},e_{d}\}
forms an orthonormal basis for
V
.

As a result, we obtain a tiling of
\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}
,

\overline{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}}=\bigcup_{z\in\mathcal{Z}}\overline{R(z+P)},

(5.18)

where

\mathcal{Z}=\big\{n_{1}e_{1}+n_{2}e_{2}+\cdots+n_{d}e_{d}:n_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}\text{ for }1\leq j\leq d\text{ and }n_{d}\geq 0\big\}

forms a lattice for
\overline{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}}
.
Let

\widetilde{P}=\big\{t_{1}e_{1}+t_{2}e_{2}+\cdots+t_{d}e_{d}:0<t_{j}<2\text{ for }1\leq j\leq d\big\}.

(5.19)

By using a partition of unity adapted to (
5.18
) and (
5.19
), we obtain

\displaystyle\lambda^{D}(\mathbf{A},\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})

\displaystyle\geq\inf_{z\in\mathcal{Z}}\lambda^{D}(\mathbf{A},R(z+\widetilde{P}))-CR^{-2}

(5.20)

\displaystyle=\inf_{z\in\mathcal{Z}_{1}}\lambda^{D}(\mathbf{A},R(z+\widetilde{P}))-CR^{-2},

where
\mathcal{Z}_{1}\subset\mathcal{Z}
is given in the following, by
using the fact that
\mathbf{B}
is invariant with respect to
V
to eliminate the components of
z
in
V
.
If
V\subset\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\times\{0\}
, the set
\mathcal{Z}_{1}
is given by

\mathcal{Z}_{1}=\mathcal{Z}\cap V^{\perp}.

If
V\not\subset\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\times\{0\}
,

\mathcal{Z}_{1}=\big\{n_{k}e_{k}+\cdots+n_{d-1}e_{d-1}\in\mathcal{Z}:n_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}\text{ for }k\leq j\leq d-1\big\}.

Note that for
z\in\mathcal{Z}_{1}
,

\displaystyle\lambda^{D}(\mathbf{A},R(z+\widetilde{P}))

\displaystyle\geq c\inf_{x\in R(z+\widetilde{P})}\{\widetilde{m}(x,\mathbf{B})\}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq c\,\sigma^{\frac{2}{\kappa+1}}\inf_{x\in R(z+\widetilde{P})}\{\text{\rm dist}(x,V)\}^{\frac{2}{\kappa+1}}

\displaystyle\geq c\,\sigma^{\frac{2}{\kappa+1}}R^{\frac{2}{\kappa+1}}\{\text{\rm dist}(z,V)-2d\}^{\frac{2}{\kappa+1}}.

It follows that if
z\in\mathcal{Z}_{1}
and dist
(z,V)\geq 2d+1
, we have

\lambda^{D}(\mathbf{A},R(z+\widetilde{P}))\geq\lambda^{D}(\mathbf{A},Q_{+}(0,1))

if
R>1
is large.
If
z\in\mathcal{Z}_{1}
and dist
(z,V)<2d+1
, we claim that

R(z+\widetilde{P})\subset Q_{+}(0,CR).

(5.21)

This implies that for any
z\in\mathcal{Z}_{1}
,

\lambda^{D}(\mathbf{A},R(z+\widetilde{P}))\geq\lambda^{D}(\mathbf{A},Q_{+}(0,CR)).

which, together with (
5.20
), gives (
5.16
).

Finally, to prove the claim (
5.21
), we consider two cases.
If
V\subset\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\times\{0\}
, then
\mathcal{Z}_{1}=Z\cap V^{\perp}
. Hence,

|z|=\text{\rm dist}(z,V)<2d+1
, which yields (
5.21
).
If
V\not\subset\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\times\{0\}
and
z\in\mathcal{Z}_{1}
, then

z=n_{k}e_{k}+\cdots+n_{d-1}e_{d-1}\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\times\{0\}.

Note that if
|z|\neq 0
,

\displaystyle\{\text{\rm dist}(z,V)\}^{2}

\displaystyle=|z|^{2}-\langle z,e_{1}\rangle^{2}-\cdots-\langle z,e_{k-1}\rangle^{2}-\langle z,e_{d}\rangle^{2}

\displaystyle=|z|^{2}-\langle z,e_{d}\rangle^{2}=|z|^{2}\left\{1-\langle z/|z|,e_{d}\rangle^{2}\right\}

\displaystyle\geq|z|^{2}\{\text{\rm dist}(e_{d},\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\times\{0\})\}^{2}\geq c|z|^{2},

where we have used the facts that
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{k-1},e_{d}\}
forms an orthonormal basis for
V
and

e_{d}\not\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\times\{0\}
.
It follows that
|z|\leq C
, which leads to (
5.21
).
∎

.

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
\mathbf{A}
be a homogeneous polynomial of degree
\kappa+1
.
Then,

\lambda^{D\!N}(\mathbf{A},\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})\leq\mu^{D\!N}(\mathbf{A},Q_{+}(0,R),\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})\leq\lambda^{D\!N}(\mathbf{A},\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})+CR^{-2},

for

, where
C
depends on
\mathbf{B}

Proof.

The first inequality in (

The proof of the second uses the inequality,

(5.25)

(see Remark
3.11

as well as the basis

Theorem 5.7
.

Let
\mathbf{A}
be a homogeneous polynomial of degree
\kappa+1
and
\Omega
given by (
5.28
).
Then

|\lambda^{D}(\mathbf{A},\Omega\cap Q(0,2R))-\lambda^{D}(\mathbf{A},\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})|\leq C\left\{R^{\kappa+1}M_{R}+R^{-2}\right\},

(5.29)

where
R>1
and
M_{R}=\max\{|\nabla\phi(x^{\prime})|:|x^{\prime}|<R\}
.

Proof.

We assume
M_{R}\leq(1/2)
and
R

\phi(0)=0
, we have
|\phi(x^{\prime})|\leq RM_{R}
for
|x^{\prime}|<R
.

F(R)\subset\Omega\cap Q(0,2R)\subset E(R)

\displaystyle E(R)

\displaystyle=\big\{(x^{\prime},x_{d}):\ |x^{\prime}|<R\text{ and }-RM_{R}<x_{d}<R\big\},

\lambda^{D}(\mathbf{A},F(R))=\lambda^{D}(\widetilde{\mathbf{A}},\widetilde{F}(R)),

\widetilde{A}(x)=\mathbf{A}(x+(0,\dots,0,RM_{R}))

\widetilde{F}(R)=F(R)-(0,\dots,0,RM_{R})
.

x\in\widetilde{F}(R)

|\lambda^{D}(\widetilde{\mathbf{A}},\widetilde{F}(R))-\lambda^{D}(\mathbf{A},\widetilde{F}(R))|\leq CR^{\kappa+1}M_{R}.

\displaystyle\lambda^{D}(\mathbf{A},\Omega\cap Q(0,2R))

\displaystyle\leq\lambda^{D}(\mathbf{A},\widetilde{F}(R))+CR^{\kappa+1}M_{R}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda^{D}(\mathbf{A},Q_{+}(0,R))+CR^{\kappa+1}M_{R}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda^{D}(\mathbf{A},\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})+CR^{-2}+CR^{\kappa+1}M_{R},

where we have used the fact
Q_{+}(0,R)\subset\widetilde{F}(R)
and Lemma
5.3
.
The lower bound for
\lambda^{D}(\mathbf{A},\Omega\cap Q(0,R))
may be established in
a similar manner, using
E(R)
. We omit the details.

Theorem 5.8
.

Let
\mathbf{A}
be a homogeneous polynomial of degree
\kappa+1
and
\Omega
given by (
5.28
).
Then

|\mu^{N}(\mathbf{A},\Omega\cap Q(0,2R),\Omega)-\lambda^{N}(\mathbf{A},\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})|\leq C\{R^{\kappa+1}M_{R}+R^{-2}\},

(5.30)

where
R>1
and
M_{R}=\max\{|\nabla\phi(x^{\prime})|:|x^{\prime}|<R\}
.

Proof.

Assume
M_{R}<(1/2)
.
Consider the map

\Phi:(x^{\prime},x_{d})\to(x^{\prime},x_{d}-\phi(x^{\prime})),

which flattens the boundary of
\Omega
.
Let
\psi\in C_{0}^{1}(Q(0,R);\mathbb{C})
and

\varphi(x)=\psi(x^{\prime},x_{d}+\phi(x^{\prime}))
.
Note that

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Omega\cap Q(0,2R)}|(D+\mathbf{A})\psi|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{\Omega\cap Q(0,2R)}|(D+\widetilde{\mathbf{A}})\varphi(\Phi(x))|^{2}\right)^{1/2}+M_{R}\left(\int_{\Omega\cap Q(0,2R)}|D\varphi(\Phi(x))|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle+(1+M_{R})\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}\cap\Phi(Q(0,2R))}|(\mathbf{A}-\widetilde{\mathbf{A}})\varphi|^{2}\right)^{1/2},

.

\displaystyle\mu^{N}(\mathbf{A},\Omega\cap Q(0,2R),\Omega)

\displaystyle\leq(1+M_{R})\mu^{N}(\mathbf{A},\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}\cap\Phi(Q(0,2R)),\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})+CM_{R}R^{\kappa+1}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda^{N}(\mathbf{A},\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})+CR^{-2}+CM_{R}R^{\kappa+1},

where we have used the fact that
Q(0,cR)\subset\Phi(Q(0,2R))
for some small
c>0
for the second inequality
and Lemma
5.5
for the third.
The lower bound for
\mu^{N}(\mathbf{A},\Omega\cap Q(0,2R),\Omega)
may be proved by a similar perturbation argument.

|B_{12}(x)|+|\nabla B_{12}(x)|\geq c_{0}

(9.12)

for any
x\in\overline{\Omega}
.

Theorem 9.9
.

Suppose
\mathbf{A}\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{R}^{2})
.
Let
\Omega
be a bounded
C^{1,1}
domain in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Suppose
\kappa_{*}=1
.
Also assume that if
y\in\partial\Omega
and
B_{12}(y)=0
, then

|\langle\nabla B_{12}(y),T(y)\rangle|\geq c_{0},

(9.13)

where
T(y)
is a unit tangent vector to
\partial\Omega
at
y
.
Then

\displaystyle\lambda^{D}(\beta\mathbf{A},\Omega)

\displaystyle=\Theta_{D}\beta^{\frac{2}{3}}+O(\beta^{\frac{8}{15}}),

(9.14)

\displaystyle\lambda^{N}(\beta\mathbf{A},\Omega)

\displaystyle=\Theta_{N}\beta^{\frac{2}{3}}+O(\beta^{\frac{8}{15}}),

for
\beta
large.

Proof.

Let
y\in\Gamma_{*}=\Gamma_{1}\cup\Gamma_{2}
.
Then
B_{12}(y)=0
.
By (
9.12
), we have
|\nabla B_{12}(y)|\geq c_{0}
.
By the Implicit Function Theorem, it follows that
\Gamma_{*}
is the union of a finite number non-intersecting
C^{1}
curves in
\overline{\Omega}
.
Moreover,

|B_{12}(x)|\geq c\,\text{\rm dist}(x,\Gamma_{*})

(9.15)

for any
x\in\overline{\Omega}
.
Furthermore, the condition (
9.13
) ensures that if one of these curves intersects with
\partial\Omega
,
they must interest at a non-zero angle. In particular,
\Gamma_{2}
is a finite set.

Next, note that the first-order Taylor polynomial for
B_{12}(x+y)
at
0
is

P(x)=\langle\nabla B_{12}(y),x\rangle
.
Its invariant subspace is given by

V_{y}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\langle x,\nabla B_{12}(y)\rangle=0\}.

Moreover,

\sigma(y)=\min_{{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in V^{\perp}\\
|x|=1\end{subarray}}}|P(x)|^{\frac{1}{2}}=|\nabla B_{12}(y)|^{\frac{1}{2}}\geq c_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}>0.

Hence,
\mathbf{B}
satisfies the assumptions in Theorem
8.6
.
Consequently, (
9.14
) follows from (
8.19
).
∎

Remark 9.11

Theorem 9.12
.

Suppose
\mathbf{A}\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{R}^{2})
.
Let
\Omega
be a bounded
C^{1,1}
domain in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Suppose
\kappa_{0}=1
.
Also assume that (
9.13
) holds for any

.
Then

\displaystyle\Theta_{D\!N}\beta^{\frac{1}{3}}-C\beta^{\frac{4}{15}}\leq\lambda^{D\!N}(\beta\mathbf{A},\Omega)\leq\Theta_{D\!N}\beta^{\frac{1}{3}}+C\beta^{\frac{1}{5}}

(9.16)

for
\beta
large.

Proof.

Recall that
\Gamma_{0}=\{y\in\partial\Omega:B_{12}(y)=0\}
.
It follows from the condition (
9.13
) that
\Gamma_{0}
is a finite set.
Moreover, if
B_{12}(\gamma(t))=0

\gamma(t)

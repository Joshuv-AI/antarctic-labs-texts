1.
Introduction

We consider the inequality

-\operatorname{div}A(x,u,\nabla u)\geq f(u)\quad\mbox{in }{\mathbb{R}}^{n},

(1.1)

where
n\geq 2
and
A
is a Carathéodory function such that

(A(x,s,\zeta)-A(x,s,\xi))(\zeta-\xi)\geq 0,

(1.2)

C_{1}|\xi|^{p}\leq\xi A(x,s,\xi),\quad|A(x,s,\xi)|\leq C_{2}|\xi|^{p-1}

(1.3)

for almost all

x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}

and for all
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
\zeta,\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
Here
p>1
,
C_{1}>0
, and
C_{2}>0
are real numbers independent of
x
,
s
,
\zeta
, and
\xi
.

The function
f
in the right-hand side of (
1.1
) is assumed to be non-negative and non-decreasing on the interval
[0,\varepsilon]
for some
\varepsilon\in(0,\infty)
.

As is customary, by
B_{r}^{x}
and
S_{r}^{x}
we denote the open ball and the sphere in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
of radius
r>0
centered at
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. In the case of
x=0
, let us write
B_{r}
and
S_{r}
instead of
B_{r}^{0}
and
S_{r}^{0}
, respectively.

A function
u\in W_{p,loc}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
is called a solution of (
1.1
) if
f(u)\in L_{1,loc}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
and

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}A(x,u,\nabla u)\nabla\varphi\,dx\geq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}f(u)\varphi\,dx

for any non-negative function
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
.

We additionally assume that solutions of (
1.1
) satisfy the condition

\operatorname*{ess\,inf}\limits_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}u=0

(1.4)

This obviously implies that the function
u
is non-negative almost everywhere in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.

Analogously, by a solution of the equation

-\operatorname{div}A(x,u,\nabla u)=g(x)\quad\mbox{in }{\mathbb{R}}^{n},

where
g\in L_{1,loc}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, we mean a function
u\in W_{p,loc}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
such that

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}A(x,u,\nabla u)\nabla\varphi\,dx=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}g(x)\varphi\,dx

for all
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
.

The absence of non-trivial solutions of differential equations and inequalities or, in other words, the blow-up phenomenon is traditionally attracted the attention of mathematicians [1–13].
It is easy to see that for inequalities (
1.1
) the only relevant case is
n>p
.
Indeed, if
p\geq n
and
u
is a non-negative solution of the inequality

-\operatorname{div}A(x,u,\nabla u)\geq 0\quad\mbox{in }{\mathbb{R}}^{n},

then
u
is a constant function
[
1
]
.
In paper
[
2
]
, for the differential inequalities

-\operatorname{div}(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u)\geq f(u)\quad\mbox{in }{\mathbb{R}}^{n}

(1.5)

which are a partial case of (
1.1
), it was shown that, in the case of

\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\frac{f(t)\,dt}{t^{1+n(p-1)/(n-p)}}=\infty,

(1.6)

any solution satisfying condition (
1.4
)
is trivial, i.e. is equal to zero almost everywhere in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
In so doing, if (
1.6
) is not fulfilled, a positive solution of (
1.5
) was constructed that tends to zero as
x\to\infty
.

A shortcoming of
[
2
]
is that the arguments presented there make significant use of the spherical symmetry of the differential operator. The method proposed by the authors of
[
2
]
is based on the reduction of arbitrary solutions of (
1.5
) to spherically symmetric ones. This method is obviously not applicable to inequalities of the general form (
1.1
).
In
[
9
]
, by a completely different approach, it was shown that (
1.6
) is a sufficient condition for the absence of nontrivial solutions in the case of inequalities (
1.1
) as well. However, the question of whether this condition is necessary remained open. Theorem
2.1
given below provides an answer to this question.

2.
Main results

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
n>p
and, moreover,

\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\frac{f(t)\,dt}{t^{1+n(p-1)/(n-p)}}<\infty.

(2.1)

Then inequality (
1.1
) has a positive solution satisfying condition (
1.4
).

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
n>p
and, moreover,
F:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
be a non-increasing function such that

\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{n(p-1)/p}F(r)\,dr<\infty.

(2.2)

Then the equation

-\operatorname{div}A(x,u,\nabla u)=F(|x|)\quad\mbox{in }{\mathbb{R}}^{n}

(2.3)

has a solution satisfying condition (
1.4
).

Remark 2.1
.

If, under the assumptions of Theorem
2.2
, the function
F
does not vanish almost everywhere on the interval
[0,\infty)
, then any non-negative solution of (
2.3
) is positive almost everywhere in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Indeed, by the weak Harnack inequality, we obtain

\left(\frac{1}{\operatorname{mes}B_{2r}}\int_{B_{2r}}u^{\lambda}\,dx\right)^{1/\lambda}\leq C\operatorname*{ess\,inf}\limits_{B_{r}}u

for all
\lambda\in(0,n(p-1)/(n-p))
and
r\in(0,\infty)

[
14
,
15
,
16
]
.

Proof of Theorem
2.2
.

Let us agree to denote by
C
various positive constants that can depend only on
p
,
n
, and the ellipticity constants
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
.
As in
[
17
]
, by
L_{p}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
we mean the set of distributions
u\in{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
such that

\nabla u\in L_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
.
The set
L_{p}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
is obviously a linear space with the seminorm

\|u\|_{L_{p}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}=\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|\nabla u|^{p}\,dx\right)^{1/p}.

(2.4)

It is well-known that

L_{p}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\subset L_{p,loc}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})

[
17
, Sec. 1.1.2]
.
By
V
we denote the space of functions
u\in L_{p}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
satisfying the condition

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{|u|^{p}}{|x|^{p}}\,dx<\infty.

(2.5)

It is easy to see that (
2.4
) is a norm on
V
.
Indeed, let
\eta\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{2})
be a non-negative function equal to one on
B_{1}
. Assuming that
\eta
is extended by zero to the set
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus B_{2}
, we put

\eta_{R}(x)=\eta\left(\frac{x}{R}\right),\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n},\quad R>0.

Let
u\in V
. From Hardy’s inequality
[
17
, Sec. 2.1.6, Corollary 2]
, it follows that

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{|\eta_{R}u|^{p}}{|x|^{p}}\,dx\leq C\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|\nabla(\eta_{R}u)|^{p}\,dx,

whence we immediately obtain

\int_{B_{R}}\frac{|u|^{p}}{|x|^{p}}\,dx\leq C\left(\int_{B_{2R}}|\nabla u|^{p}\,dx+\int_{B_{2R}\setminus B_{R}}\frac{|u|^{p}}{R^{p}}\,dx\right)

for all
R>0
.
In view of (
2.5
), the second summand in the right-hand side of the last inequality tends to zero as
R\to\infty
.
Thus, passing to the limit as
R\to\infty
, we have

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{|u|^{p}}{|x|^{p}}\,dx\leq C\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|\nabla u|^{p}\,dx.

(2.6)

With respect to the norm (
2.4
) the space
V
is obviously complete.
In so doing, the mapping
u\mapsto\nabla u
isometrically embeds
V
into

(L_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}))^{n}=\underbrace{L_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\times\ldots\times L_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}_{n}.

Since
(L_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}))^{n}
is a separable uniformly convex Banach space,
V
is also separable and uniformly convex, so
V
is reflexive by Milman’s theorem
[
18
, Chapter 5, Sec. 2, Theorem 2]
.
This, in particular, implies the separability of
V^{*}
.

We consider the operator
{\mathcal{A}}:V\to V^{*}
defined by

({\mathcal{A}}u,v)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}A(x,u,\nabla u)\nabla v\,dx,\quad u,v\in V.

From (
1.3
), it follows that

\lim_{\|u\|_{L_{p}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\to\infty}\frac{({\mathcal{A}}u,u)}{\|u\|_{L_{p}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}}=\infty.

(2.7)

Moreover,
{\mathcal{A}}
is a bounded operator, in other words, it maps any bounded set in
V
to a set bounded in
V^{*}
.
It is also easy to verify that for all
v\in V
the mapping

u\mapsto({\mathcal{A}}u,v)

(2.8)

is a continuous functional on any finite-dimensional subspace of
V
.
Indeed, let
W
be an
m
-dimensional subspace of
V
with the basis
w_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,m
, and let
v\in V
be some function.
Since any two norms on
W
are equivalent, it suffices to show that the mapping

(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{m})\mapsto({\mathcal{A}}(\lambda_{1}w_{1}+\ldots+\lambda_{m}w_{m}),v)

is continuous in
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
. Let
(\lambda_{1k},\ldots,\lambda_{mk})\to(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{m})
as
k\to\infty
.
The function
A
on the left in (
1.1
) is Carathéodoric. This means that the mapping

x\mapsto A(x,s,\xi)

is measurable for all
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and the mapping

(s,\xi)\mapsto A(x,s,\xi)

is continuous for almost all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Hence,

\lim_{k\to\infty}A\left(x,\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{ik}w_{i},\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{ik}\nabla w_{i}\right)\nabla v=A\left(x,\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}w_{i},\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}\nabla w_{i}\right)\nabla v

for almost all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
At the same time, taking into account (
1.3
), we have

\left|A\left(x,\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{ik}w_{i},\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{ik}\nabla w_{i}\right)\nabla v\right|\leq C\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}|\lambda_{ik}||\nabla w_{i}|+|\nabla v|\right)^{p},\quad k=1,2,\ldots,

for almost all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
Thus, from Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem, it follows that

\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}A\left(x,\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{ik}w_{i},\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{ik}\nabla w_{i}\right)\nabla v\,dx=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}A\left(x,\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}w_{i},\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}\nabla w_{i}\right)\nabla v\,dx

or, in other words,

\lim_{k\to\infty}({\mathcal{A}}(\lambda_{1k}w_{1}+\ldots+\lambda_{mk}w_{m}),v)=({\mathcal{A}}(\lambda_{1}w_{1}+\ldots+\lambda_{m}w_{m}),v).

Unfortunately, we cannot claim that
{\mathcal{A}}
is a monotone operator, i.e. such an operator that

({\mathcal{A}}u-{\mathcal{A}}v,u-v)\geq 0

for all
u,v\in V
.
Nevertheless, by arguments similar to
[
19
, Chapter 2, Theorem 2.1]
, it is easy to show that
{\mathcal{A}}:V\to V^{*}
is a surjective mapping.
To avoid being unfounded, we present these arguments in full.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}
be a continuous linear functional on
V
. We show that

{\mathcal{A}}u={\mathcal{F}}.

(2.9)

for some
u\in V
. We take a family of linearly independent functions
w_{i}\in V
,
i=1,2,\ldots
, such that the closure of their linear span coincides with
V
. We denote by
V_{m}
the linear subspace of
V
generated by the functions
w_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,m
, with the scalar product

\langle w_{i},w_{j}\rangle=\delta_{ij},

where
\delta_{ij}
is the Kronecker delta.
There obviously exist a function
{\mathbb{F}}\in V_{m}
and an operator
{\mathbb{A}}:V_{m}\to V_{m}
such that

\langle{\mathbb{A}}u,v\rangle=({\mathcal{A}}u,v)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\langle{\mathbb{F}},v\rangle=({\mathcal{F}},v)

for all
u,v\in V_{m}
.
Since

{\mathbb{A}}u=\sum_{i=1}^{m}({\mathcal{A}}u,w_{i})w_{i}

for all
u\in V_{m}
and, moreover, the mappings
u\mapsto({\mathcal{A}}u,w_{i})
,
i=1,\ldots,m
, are continuous on the finite-dimensional space
V_{m}
, the operator
{\mathbb{A}}:V_{m}\to V_{m}
is continuous.
In view of (
2.7
), there exists a real number
R>0
such that

\langle{\mathbb{A}}u-{\mathbb{F}},u\rangle\geq 0

for all
u\in V_{m}
satisfying the condition
\langle u,u\rangle=R^{2}
.
Therefore, by
[
19
, Chapter 1, Lemma 4.3]
, there exists
u_{m}\in V_{m}
such that

{\mathbb{A}}u_{m}={\mathbb{F}}

or, in other words,

({\mathcal{A}}u_{m},v)=({\mathcal{F}},v)

(2.10)

for all
v\in V_{m}
.
In particular,

({\mathcal{A}}u_{m},u_{m})=({\mathcal{F}},u_{m}),

(2.11)

whence in accordance with (
2.7
) it follows that the sequence
\{u_{m}\}_{m=1}^{\infty}
is bounded in
V
.
Since
{\mathcal{A}}:V\to V^{*}
is a bounded operator, the sequence
\{{\mathcal{A}}u_{m}\}_{m=1}^{\infty}
is bounded in
V^{*}
.
Let us extract from
\{u_{m}\}_{m=1}^{\infty}
a subsequence that converges weakly in
V
, strongly in
L_{p,loc}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, and almost everywhere in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
to some function
u\in V
. Such a subsequence obviously exists. In order to avoid cluttering the indices, we also denote it by
\{u_{m}\}_{m=1}^{\infty}
.
Passing to the limit in (
2.11
), we obtain

\lim_{m\to\infty}({\mathcal{A}}u_{m},u_{m})=({\mathcal{F}},u).

(2.12)

In its turn, from (
2.10
), it follows that

\lim_{m\to\infty}({\mathcal{A}}u_{m},w_{i})=({\mathcal{F}},w_{i})

for all
i=1,2,\ldots
.
Since the linear span of the functions
w_{i}
,
i=1,2,\ldots
, is dense in
V
and the sequence
\{{\mathcal{A}}u_{m}\}_{m=1}^{\infty}
is bounded in
V^{*}
, this yields

\lim_{m\to\infty}({\mathcal{A}}u_{m},v)=({\mathcal{F}},v)

(2.13)

for all
v\in V
.

Let
v\in V
be some function. Taking into account (
2.12
), (
2.13
), and the fact that the sequence
\{u_{m}\}_{m=1}^{\infty}
weakly converges to
u
, we have

\lim_{m\to\infty}({\mathcal{A}}u_{m}-{\mathcal{A}}v,u_{m}-v)=({\mathcal{F}}-{\mathcal{A}}v,u-v).

(2.14)

At the same time, from (
1.2
), it follows that

\displaystyle({\mathcal{A}}u_{m}-{\mathcal{A}}v,u_{m}-v)={}

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}(A(x,u_{m},\nabla u_{m})-A(x,v,\nabla v))(\nabla u_{m}-\nabla v)\,dx

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}(A(x,u_{m},\nabla u_{m})-A(x,u_{m},\nabla v))(\nabla u_{m}-\nabla v)\,dx

\displaystyle{}+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}(A(x,u_{m},\nabla v)-A(x,v,\nabla v))(\nabla u_{m}-\nabla v)\,dx

\displaystyle{}\geq

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}(A(x,u_{m},\nabla v)-A(x,v,\nabla v))(\nabla u_{m}-\nabla v)\,dx.

(2.15)

In so doing, the relation

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}(A(x,u_{m},\nabla v)-A(x,v,\nabla v))(\nabla u_{m}-\nabla v)\,dx

\displaystyle\quad{}=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}(A(x,u,\nabla v)-A(x,v,\nabla v))(\nabla u-\nabla v)\,dx

(2.16)

is valid.
To prove (
2.16
), it suffices to note that

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}(A(x,u_{m},\nabla v)-A(x,v,\nabla v))(\nabla u_{m}-\nabla v)\,dx

\displaystyle\quad{}=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}(A(x,u,\nabla v)-A(x,v,\nabla v))(\nabla u-\nabla v)\,dx

\displaystyle\quad\phantom{{}=}{}+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}(A(x,u,\nabla v)-A(x,v,\nabla v))(\nabla u_{m}-\nabla u)\,dx

\displaystyle\quad\phantom{{}=}{}+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}(A(x,u_{m},\nabla v)-A(x,u,\nabla v))(\nabla u_{m}-\nabla v)\,dx.

Since
u_{k}\to u
weakly in
V
as
k\to\infty
, we obviously obtain

\lim_{m\to\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}(A(x,u,\nabla v)-A(x,v,\nabla v))(\nabla u_{m}-\nabla u)\,dx=0.

Taking into account the estimate

\displaystyle\left|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}(A(x,u_{m},\nabla v)-A(x,u,\nabla v))(\nabla u_{m}-\nabla v)\,dx\right|

\displaystyle\quad{}\leq\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|A(x,u_{m},\nabla v)-A(x,u,\nabla v)|^{p/(p-1)}\,dx\right)^{(p-1)/p}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|\nabla u_{m}-\nabla v|^{p}\,dx\right)^{1/p},

where the first factor in the right-hand side tends to zero as
k\to\infty
by Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem and the second factor does not exceed some constant independent of
m
, we also obtain

\lim_{m\to\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}(A(x,u_{m},\nabla v)-A(x,u,\nabla v))(\nabla u_{m}-\nabla v)\,dx=0.

Thus, combining (
2.14
), (
2.15
), and (
2.16
), we arrive at the inequality

({\mathcal{F}}-{\mathcal{A}}v,u-v)\geq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}(A(x,u,\nabla v)-A(x,v,\nabla v))(\nabla u-\nabla v)\,dx

putting in which
v=u-\lambda w
,
w\in V
,
\lambda\in(0,\infty)
, we have

({\mathcal{F}}-{\mathcal{A}}(u-\lambda w),w)\geq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}(A(x,u,\nabla u-\lambda\nabla w)-A(x,u-\lambda w,\nabla u-\lambda\nabla w))\nabla w\,dx.

(2.17)

Since (
2.8
) is a continuous mapping on any finite-dimensional subspace of
V
, one can assert that

\lim_{\lambda\to+0}({\mathcal{F}}-{\mathcal{A}}(u-\lambda w),w)=({\mathcal{F}}-{\mathcal{A}}u,w).

On the other hand, by Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem,

\lim_{\lambda\to+0}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}(A(x,u,\nabla u-\lambda\nabla w)-A(x,u-\lambda w,\nabla u-\lambda\nabla w))\nabla w\,dx=0;

therefore, passing in (
2.17
) to the limit as
\lambda\to+0
, we obtain

({\mathcal{F}}-{\mathcal{A}}u,w)\geq 0.

Since
w
can be an arbitrary function from the space
V
, this immediately leads to (
2.9
).

Now, let us show that the linear functional
{\mathcal{F}}
defined by the formula

({\mathcal{F}},u)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}F(|x|)u\,dx

is continuous on
V
.
By Holder’s inequality, the estimate

\left|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}F(|x|)u\,dx\right|\leq\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{|u|^{p}}{|x|^{p}}\,dx\right)^{1/p}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|x|^{p/(p-1)}F^{p/(p-1)}(|x|)\,dx\right)^{(p-1)/p}

(2.18)

is valid for all
u\in V
.
As
F
is a non-increasing function on the interval
[0,\infty)
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|x|^{p/(p-1)}F^{{p/(p-1)}}(|x|)\,dx

\displaystyle=|S_{1}|\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{{p/(p-1)+n-1}}F^{p/(p-1)}(r)\,dr

\displaystyle=|S_{1}|\sum_{i=-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{2^{i}}^{2^{i+1}}r^{{p/(p-1)+n-1}}F^{p/(p-1)}(r)\,dr

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{i=-\infty}^{\infty}2^{i(p/(p-1)+n)}F^{p/(p-1)}(2^{i}).

(2.19)

where
|S_{1}|
is the
(n-1)
-dimensional volume of the unit sphere in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
At the same time,

\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{n(p-1)/p}F(r)\,dr=\sum_{i=-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{2^{i-1}}^{2^{i}}r^{n(p-1)/p}F(r)\,dr\geq C\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}2^{i(1+n(p-1)/p)}F(2^{i}),

whence it follows that

\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{n(p-1)/p}F(r)\,dr\right)^{p/(p-1)}\geq C\sum_{i=-\infty}^{\infty}2^{i(p/(p-1)+n)}F^{p/(p-1)}(2^{i}).

Combining the last inequality with (
2.18
) and (
2.19
), we obtain

\left|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}F(|x|)u\,dx\right|\leq C\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{|u|^{p}}{|x|^{p}}\,dx\right)^{1/p}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{n(p-1)/p}F(r)\,dr,

whence in accordance with (
2.6
) it follows that

\left|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}F(|x|)u\,dx\right|\leq C\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|\nabla u|^{p}\,dx\right)^{1/p}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{n(p-1)/p}F(r)\,dr

for all
u\in V
.

Thus, there exists a solution of (
2.9
) which is obviously a solution of (
2.3
) belonging to the space
V
. Let us show that it satisfies condition (
1.4
).
We denote

u_{-}(x)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &u(x),&u(x)<0,\\
&0,&u(x)\geq 0.\end{aligned}\right.

It is easy to see that
u_{-}\in V
and, in addition,

\nabla u_{-}(x)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\nabla u(x),&u(x)<0,\\
&0,&u(x)\geq 0.\end{aligned}\right.

Taking into account (
2.9
), we have

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}A(x,\nabla u)\nabla u_{-}\,dx=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}F(|x|)u_{-}\,dx\leq 0.

Since

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}A(x,\nabla u)\nabla u_{-}\,dx=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}A(x,\nabla u_{-})\nabla u_{-}\,dx

and, moreover,

C_{1}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|\nabla u_{-}|^{p}\,dx\leq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}A(x,\nabla u_{-})\nabla u_{-}\,dx

in accordance with (
1.3
), the function
u_{-}
is a constant almost everywhere in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. By (
2.5
), this constant can only be equal to zero. From the above, we can conclude that
u(x)\geq 0
for almost all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. However, in this case, failure to satisfy condition (
1.4
) also contradicts relation (
2.5
).
∎

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

We denote by
C
various positive constants that can depend only on
p
,
n
,
\varepsilon
and the ellipticity constants
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
.

Without loss of generality, it can be assumed that the function
f
on the right in (
1.1
) is positive on the interval
(0,\varepsilon)
; otherwise we replace
f
by

\tilde{f}(t)=\max\{f(t),t^{1+n(p-1)/(n-p)}\}.

Let us put

F(r)=f\left(\varepsilon\left(1+\frac{r}{\delta}\right)^{-(n-p)/(p-1)}\right),

where the real number
\delta>0
will be chosen later.
Making the change of variables
t=\varepsilon(1+r/\delta)^{-(n-p)/(p-1)}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{n-1}F(r)\,dr

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{n-1}f\left(\varepsilon\left(1+\frac{r}{\delta}\right)^{-(n-p)/(p-1)}\right)dr

\displaystyle=\frac{p-1}{n-p}\frac{\delta^{n}}{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\left(\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{t}\right)^{(p-1)/(n-p)}-1\right)^{n-1}\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{t}\right)^{(p-1)/(n-p)+1}f(t)\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{p-1}{n-p}\varepsilon^{n(p-1)/(n-p)}\delta^{n}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\frac{f(t)\,dt}{t^{1+n(p-1)/(n-p)}},

(2.20)

whence in accordance with (
2.1
) it follows that

\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{n-1}F(r)\,dr<\infty.

(2.21)

Since
n>p
, one can obviously assert that

n-1>\frac{n(p-1)}{p};

therefore, (
2.21
) implies (
2.2
).
Thus, by Theorem
2.2
, there exists a solution of (
2.3
) for which (
1.4
) is valid. Let us show that this solution satisfies the estimate

u(x)\leq C\delta^{n/(p-1)}|x|^{-(n-p)/(p-1)}\left(\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\frac{f(t)\,dt}{t^{1+n(p-1)/(n-p)}}\right)^{1/(p-1)}

(2.22)

for almost all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and, moreover,

\lim_{\delta\to+0}\operatorname*{ess\,sup}\limits_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}u=0.

(2.23)

Indeed, taking into account
[
20
, Corollary 4.13]
, we have

u(x)\leq C\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\mu(B_{r}^{x})}{r^{n-p}}\right)^{1/(p-1)}\frac{dr}{r}

(2.24)

for almost all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, where

\mu(\omega)=\int_{\omega}F(|x|)\,dx

is a measure with the density
F(|x|)
of a Lebesgue measurable set
\omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.

Representing (
2.24
) in the form

u(x)\leq\int_{0}^{|x|/2}\left(\frac{\mu(B_{r}^{x})}{r^{n-p}}\right)^{1/(p-1)}\frac{dr}{r}+\int_{|x|/2}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\mu(B_{r}^{x})}{r^{n-p}}\right)^{1/(p-1)}\frac{dr}{r},

(2.25)

we estimate the first summand in the right-hand side of (
2.25
).
Since
F
is a non-increasing function, the inequality

\mu(B_{r}^{x})=\int_{B_{r}^{x}}F(|y|)\,dy\leq|S_{1}|r^{n}F\left(\frac{|x|}{2}\right)

holds for all
r\in(0,|x|/2)
. Consequently,

\int_{0}^{|x|/2}\left(\frac{\mu(B_{r}^{x})}{r^{n-p}}\right)^{1/(p-1)}\frac{dr}{r}\leq C|x|^{p/(p-1)}F^{1/(p-1)}\left(\frac{|x|}{2}\right),

whence in accordance with the fact that

F\left(\frac{|x|}{2}\right)\leq n\left(\frac{|x|}{2}\right)^{-n}\int_{0}^{|x|/2}r^{n-1}F(r)\,dr

we obtain

\int_{0}^{|x|/2}\left(\frac{\mu(B_{r}^{x})}{r^{n-p}}\right)^{1/(p-1)}\frac{dr}{r}\leq C|x|^{-(n-p)/(p-1)}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{n-1}F(r)\,dr\right)^{1/(p-1)}

for all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
.
According to (
2.20
), this leads to the inequality

\int_{0}^{|x|/2}\left(\frac{\mu(B_{r}^{x})}{r^{n-p}}\right)^{1/(p-1)}\frac{dr}{r}\leq C\delta^{n/(p-1)}|x|^{-(n-p)/(p-1)}\left(\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\frac{f(t)\,dt}{t^{1+n(p-1)/(n-p)}}\right)^{1/(p-1)}

for all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
.

Now, we estimate the second summand in the right-hand side of (
2.25
). It is easy to see that

\int_{|x|/2}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\mu(B_{r}^{x})}{r^{n-p}}\right)^{1/(p-1)}\frac{dr}{r}\leq C|x|^{-(n-p)/(p-1)}\mu^{1/(p-1)}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})

with

\mu({\mathbb{R}}^{n})=|S_{1}|\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{n-1}F(r)\,dr.

(2.26)

As above, by
|S_{1}|
we denote the
(n-1)
-dimensional volume of the unit sphere in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
Combining the last inequality with (
2.20
), we obtain

\int_{|x|/2}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\mu(B_{r}^{x})}{r^{n-p}}\right)^{1/(p-1)}\frac{dr}{r}\leq C\delta^{n/(p-1)}|x|^{-(n-p)/(p-1)}\left(\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\frac{f(t)\,dt}{t^{1+n(p-1)/(n-p)}}\right)^{1/(p-1)}

for all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
.
Hence, (
2.25
) implies (
2.22
).

Let us prove relation (
2.23
). From (
2.24
) it follows that

u(x)\leq C\left(\int_{0}^{\delta}\left(\frac{\mu(B_{r}^{x})}{r^{n-p}}\right)^{1/(p-1)}\frac{dr}{r}+\int_{\delta}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\mu(B_{r}^{x})}{r^{n-p}}\right)^{1/(p-1)}\frac{dr}{r}\right)

for almost all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. As
F(|x|)\leq f(\varepsilon)
for all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, we have

\mu(B_{r}^{x})=\int_{B_{r}^{x}}F(|y|)\,dy\leq|S_{1}|r^{n}f(\varepsilon)

for all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and
r\in(0,\infty)
; therefore,

\int_{0}^{\delta}\left(\frac{\mu(B_{r}^{x})}{r^{n-p}}\right)^{1/(p-1)}\frac{dr}{r}\leq Cf^{1/(p-1)}(\varepsilon)\delta^{p/(p-1)}

for all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
At the same time,

\int_{\delta}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\mu(B_{r}^{x})}{r^{n-p}}\right)^{1/(p-1)}\frac{dr}{r}\leq\frac{p-1}{n-p}\delta^{-(n-p)/(p-1)}\mu^{1/(p-1)}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})

for all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,
whence in accordance with (
2.20
) and (
2.26
) in follows that

\int_{\delta}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\mu(B_{r}^{x})}{r^{n-p}}\right)^{1/(p-1)}\frac{dr}{r}\leq C\delta^{p/(p-1)}\left(\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\frac{f(t)\,dt}{t^{1+n(p-1)/(n-p)}}\right)^{1/(p-1)}.

Thus,

u(x)\leq C\delta^{p/(p-1)}\left(f^{1/(p-1)}(\varepsilon)+\left(\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\frac{f(t)\,dt}{t^{1+n(p-1)/(n-p)}}\right)^{1/(p-1)}\right)

for almost all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, whence we immediately arrive at (

).

It is obvious that

2^{-(n-p)/(p-1)}\leq\left(1+\frac{|x|}{\delta}\right)^{-(n-p)/(p-1)}.

for all
x\in B_{\delta}
.
On the other hand, in view of (

), there exists a real number
\delta_{1}>0
such that

for all

and for almost all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Hence, taking

, we obtain

u(x)\leq\varepsilon\left(1+\frac{|x|}{\delta}\right)^{-(n-p)/(p-1)}

(2.27)

for almost all

.

It is also easy to see that

for all

In so doing, according to (

Taking

.
Since

is a non-decreasing function on the interval

f\left(\varepsilon\left(1+\frac{|x|}{\delta}\right)^{-(n-p)/(p-1)}\right)\geq f(u)

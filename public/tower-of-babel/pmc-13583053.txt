Clinical Characteristics and Prognostic Factors of Primary Bone Diffuse Large B‐Cell Lymphoma: A Retrospective Study
Authors: Zhenbo Hou, Kebing Chen, Yuling Lu, Xuejun Ma, Shengjian Zhang, Junning Cao, Qunling Zhang, Yizhen Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackgroundPrimary bone diffuse large B‐cell lymphoma (PB‐DLBCL) is a rare extranodal lymphoma with heterogeneous clinical features and survival outcomes. Although most patients respond favorably to modern immunochemotherapy, reliable baseline prognostic indicators remain insufficiently defined.MethodsWe retrospectively analyzed 424 patients with histologically confirmed PB‐DLBCL. Clinical characteristics, treatment patterns and survival outcomes were evaluated. Overall survival (OS) and progression‐free survival (PFS) were estimated using the Kaplan–Meier method. Prognostic factors were assessed by univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses.ResultsWith a median follow‐up of 36 months, the 3 year OS and PFS rates were 81.3% and 63.3%, respectively. In multivariate analysis, high‐risk International Prognostic Index (IPI; score 3–5) was independently associated with inferior OS (HR 2.06, 95% CI 1.24–3.43, p = 0.006). For PFS, hypoalbuminemia (< 40 g/L) emerged as an independent predictor (HR 1.44, 95% CI 1.03–2.02, p = 0.035). Although failure to achieve complete remission and high Deauville score were associated with worse outcomes in univariate analyses, these post‐treatment variables were not included in multivariable models.ConclusionPB‐DLBCL often presents with advanced disease but demonstrates generally favorable survival in the rituximab era. Baseline IPI remains a robust predictor of OS, while hypoalbuminemia independently predicts disease progression.

Introduction

Primary bone lymphoma (PBL) is a rare form of extranodal lymphoma, which accounts for approximately 3%–7% of all malignant bone tumors, about 5% of extranodal lymphomas and less than 1% of all non‐Hodgkin lymphomas (NHL) [1, 2]. Primary bone diffuse large B‐cell lymphoma (PB‐DLBCL) represents the most common histological subtype, while other subtypes include follicular lymphoma, mantle cell lymphoma, B lymphoblastic lymphoma, Burkitt lymphoma et al. [3, 4, 5]. The median age at diagnosis ranges from 45 to 60 years, although a wide age distribution has been reported (15–99 years) and pediatric cases have also been described. No clear racial or geographic predilection has been identified [2, 6, 7].

The clinical presentation of PB‐DLBCL is often nonspecific. Patients may initially present with localized bone pain or mild systemic symptoms, which can progress to large soft‐tissue masses or pathological fractures in advanced stages [8]. With the introduction of rituximab [9] and advances in imaging—particularly positron emission tomography/computed tomography (PET/CT) [10]—both the diagnosis and management of PBL have evolved substantially.

Despite these advances, prognostic factors in PB‐DLBCL remain incompletely defined, largely owing to the rarity of the disease and the heterogeneity of previously reported cohorts. Prior studies have suggested that age [11]. multifocal disease [12] and advanced stage [13] may influence clinical outcomes; however, results have been inconsistent. Moreover, the prognostic value of PET/CT‐derived parameters, including Deauville scores and first‐line treatment response, has not been adequately validated in large patient populations.

To address these gaps, we conducted a comprehensive retrospective analysis of 424 patients with PB‐DLBCL. The objectives of this study were to characterize baseline clinical features, summarize contemporary treatment patterns and identify independent prognostic factors for overall survival (OS) and progression‐free survival (PFS).

Materials and Methods

Patient Selection

This retrospective study included 424 patients diagnosed with primary bone diffuse large B‐cell lymphoma at Shanghai Cancer Center between June 2009 and July 2024. All diagnoses were confirmed by histopathological examination. Patients were eligible for inclusion if they had complete clinical, imaging and follow‐up data. Positron emission tomography/computed tomography (PET/CT) was performed before and after treatment when available.

Definition of Primary Bone Lymphoma

Primary bone lymphoma was defined as lymphoma primarily involving the bone at initial presentation, regardless of the presence of regional lymph node involvement. Patients with secondary bone involvement from systemic lymphoma were excluded. Cases with multifocal bone lesions were included, provided that bone involvement represented the dominant disease manifestation at diagnosis. Patients with limited extraosseous involvement at diagnosis were included if bone lesions constituted the main disease burden.

Staging

Disease staging was performed according to the Ann Arbor staging system with Cotswolds modification. Early‐stage disease was defined as Stage I–II, and advanced‐stage disease as Stage III–IV.

Clinical and Laboratory Assessment

Baseline demographic, pathological and clinical variables were collected, including gender, age, disease stage, Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status, extranodal involvement, Ki‐67 index, CD5 expression, double‐expressor and double‐hit status, serum lactate dehydrogenase (LDH), albumin (ALB), alkaline phosphatase (ALP). Bone‐related manifestations, including bone pain and radiographic bone destruction, were also recorded.

Treatment

Patients received rituximab‐based or non–rituximab‐based chemotherapy regimens, including R‐CHOP (six or eight cycles), CHOP, POLA‐R‐CHP, DA‐EPOCH‐R, or other regimens, according to physician discretion. The use of radiotherapy and zoledronic acid was documented. Treatment response was assessed in accordance with the Lugano classification criteria, with Deauville scores of 1–3 defined as metabolic complete response.

Statistical Analysis

Overall survival (OS) was defined as the interval from diagnosis to death from any cause or last follow‐up and progression‐free survival (PFS) was defined as the interval from treatment initiation to disease progression, relapse, or death. Survival curves were estimated using the Kaplan–Meier method and compared using the log‐rank test.

Univariate and multivariate Cox proportional hazards regression analyses were performed to identify prognostic factors. Variables with p < 0.05 in univariate analyses were considered for inclusion in multivariable models. To avoid multicollinearity, variables incorporated in the International Prognostic Index (IPI), including age, LDH level, ECOG status, disease stage and extranodal involvement, were not entered simultaneously with IPI in multivariable analyses.

Post‐treatment variables, including first‐line treatment response and Deauville scores, were not included in the primary multivariable models because of their time‐dependent nature and strong correlation, as well as potential immortal time bias. Therefore, multivariable models were constructed primarily based on baseline clinical variables.

Collinearity was further assessed using correlation matrices and variance inflation factors when appropriate. Final multivariable models were selected based on statistical significance, clinical relevance and model stability. All statistical analyses were performed using R software (version 4.4.3; R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). All statistical tests were two‐sided and p < 0.05 were considered statistically significant.

Ethics Approval and Consent

This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Fudan University Shanghai Cancer Center and written informed consent was obtained from all subjects. This study conforms to the relevant STROBE guidelines. And we have de‐identified all patient details in the study.

Results

Baseline Characteristics

Among the 424 patients included in this study (Table 1), 243 (57.3%) were male and 231 (54.5%) were ≤ 60 years of age. The non‐germinal center B‐cell (non‐GCB) subtype accounted for 56.8% of cases. A double‐expressor phenotype was present in 27.1% of patients, whereas double‐hit lymphoma was relatively uncommon (4.0%).

Clinically, bone pain was reported in 29.7% of patients and bone destruction in 25.9%. Elevated lactate dehydrogenase (LDH > 250 U/L) was observed in 55.9% of patients. Hypoalbuminemia (ALB < 40 g/L) was present in 26.4% of cases and elevated alkaline phosphatase (ALP > 125 U/L) was noted in 17.0%.

Regarding systemic status, an ECOG performance status of 0–1 was recorded in 91.0% of patients, whereas 9.0% had ECOG 2. B symptoms were present in 30.9% of cases. According to the International Prognostic Index (IPI), patients were stratified as low risk (13.9%), low‐intermediate risk (22.2%), high‐intermediate risk (39.4%) and high risk (24.5%). The majority of patients presented with advanced‐stage disease (88.7%) and involvement of more than one extranodal site (73.8%), reflecting the disseminated nature of PB‐DLBCL at diagnosis.

Treatment Patterns

R‐CHOP was the predominant first‐line regimen, administered to 78.8% of patients; among these, 237 received six cycles and 54 received eight cycles. Radiotherapy was delivered to 21.2% of patients and zoledronic acid was administered in 5.4%. Following first‐line therapy, the complete response (CR) rate was 68.5%.

Post‐treatment PET/CT evaluation using the Deauville 5‐point scale revealed that 63.7% of patients achieved a score of 1, 1.1% a score of 2% and 8.7% a score of 3. In contrast, 22.0% and 4.5% of patients exhibited Deauville scores of 4 and 5, respectively (Table 2).

Survival Outcomes

At a median follow‐up of 36 months (last follow‐up date: December 5, 2025), the estimated 3 year OS and PFS rates were 81.3% (95% CI, 77.2%–85.7%) and 63.3% (95% CI, 58.5%–68.5%), respectively and the median OS was not reached.

Kaplan–Meier analyses demonstrated significantly superior OS and PFS in patients aged ≤ 60 years, those with normal LDH levels, those achieving CR after first‐line treatment and those with Deauville scores of 1–3, whereas patients with Deauville scores of 4–5 experienced markedly inferior survival outcomes (Figure 1). Moreover, Kaplan–Meier curves indicated improved OS and PFS in patients receiving six cycles of R‐CHOP compared with those receiving fewer cycles (p < 0.001, Figure 2). There were no statistically significant differences in PFS (p = 0.118) and OS (p = 0.975) between patients receiving six or eight cycles of R‐CHOP regimen.

In univariate Cox regression analyses, age > 60 years, failure to achieve complete remission after first‐line therapy, elevated LDH levels, involvement of more than one extranodal site, high Deauville scores and high‐risk IPI were associated with inferior overall survival. In addition, zoledronic acid use and low serum albumin levels were associated with inferior progression‐free survival. In multivariable analysis, high‐risk IPI (score 3–5) remained independently associated with inferior overall survival (HR 2.06, 95% CI 1.24–3.43, p = 0.006). For progression‐free survival, hypoalbuminemia (< 40 g/L) emerged as an independent predictor (HR 1.44, 95% CI 1.03–2.02, p = 0.035), whereas IPI showed a nonsignificant trend after adjustment. Post‐treatment variables, including first‐line response and Deauville score, were not included in the multivariable models (Table 3, Figure 3).

Discussion

This study represents one of the largest single‐center cohorts of primary bone diffuse large B‐cell lymphoma (PB‐DLBCL) analyzed in the era of rituximab‐based chemoimmunotherapy and provides a comprehensive evaluation of clinical characteristics, treatment patterns and prognostic factors. Our cohort demonstrated several favorable baseline features, including a relatively low proportion of patients aged > 60 years [14], infrequent B symptoms and a high prevalence of good performance status (ECOG 0–1) [15]. However, compared with earlier studies in which PET imaging was not routinely incorporated, a substantial proportion of patients in our cohort presented with Stage IV disease [9], multifocal bone involvement [16] and involvement of more than one extranodal site. This difference likely reflects improved disease detection through contemporary imaging modalities, particularly PET/CT, rather than a true shift in disease biology, underscoring the importance of accurate staging in real‐world PB‐DLBCL cohorts.

The prognostic value of PET/CT was further underscored by the strong association between post‐treatment Deauville scores and survival outcomes, with patients achieving scores of 1–3 demonstrating significantly superior OS and PFS compared with those scoring 4–5. The Deauville score was not included in multivariable models because it represents a post‐treatment, time‐dependent variable and was unavailable for a substantial proportion of patients. Nevertheless, its association with survival supports the clinical value of metabolic response assessment and warrants further evaluation of PET/CT for post‐treatment risk stratification in PB‐DLBCL.

With respect to molecular characteristics, prior studies have reported variable frequencies of germinal center B‐cell (GCB) subtype, double‐expressor phenotype and BCL2, BCL6 and MYC rearrangements in PBL, with inconsistent prognostic implications [17, 18]. In our cohort, the non‐GCB subtype accounted for 56.8% of cases and double‐expressor status was identified in a subset of patients; however, neither cell‐of‐origin classification nor double‐expressor phenotype was significantly associated with survival outcomes [16]. Similarly, cytogenetic abnormalities involving BCL2, BCL6, or MYC were observed in a proportion of patients but did not demonstrate a clear prognostic impact. Although these molecular features are well‐established adverse prognostic markers in nodal DLBCL, their prognostic relevance in PB‐DLBCL remains uncertain. This discrepancy may reflect distinct disease biology in primary bone involvement, as well as the limited sample size and heterogeneity of available molecular data.

Central nervous system (CNS) relapse remains a devastating event in DLBCL, typically occurring early and conferring poor outcomes [19]. Bone involvement has been considered a potential risk factor for CNS recurrence, particularly in advanced‐stage disease [19]. However, rituximab has been shown to reduce the risk of CNS relapse by nearly 50% [20] and prior studies have reported a relatively low CNS relapse rate of approximately 2.5% in localized bone DLBCL [21]. In our cohort, in which more than 85% of patients received rituximab‐based therapy, CNS involvement was rare. Nevertheless, the occurrence of early CNS relapse with fatal outcome in a small number of patients highlights the aggressive nature of CNS disease when it does occur and underscores the need for continued vigilance despite its low incidence in PB‐DLBCL.

R‐CHOP‐based chemoimmunotherapy remains the cornerstone of PB‐DLBCL management in contemporary practice [3, 9, 22]. In our cohort, R‐CHOP was administered to the majority of patients and yielded a favorable complete response rate. Patients who received six cycles of R‐CHOP tended to demonstrate improved OS and PFS compared with those receiving fewer cycles. However, this association should be interpreted cautiously, as treatment duration may reflect underlying disease biology and early treatment response rather than an independent therapeutic effect. Notably, no apparent differences in OS or PFS were observed between patients receiving six and eight cycles of R‐CHOP, consistent with previous reports suggesting no additional survival benefit from extending treatment beyond six cycles [23, 24].

The role of radiotherapy in PB‐DLBCL remains controversial. Historically, before the widespread adoption of systemic chemotherapy, radiotherapy or surgery constituted the primary treatment approach and combined chemoradiotherapy was shown to be superior to radiotherapy alone [22, 25, 26, 27]. In the era of effective chemoimmunotherapy, however, the survival benefit of radiotherapy is less well defined [9]. In our study, radiotherapy was administered to a subset of patients, primarily as consolidation in those who failed to achieve CR after chemotherapy. No statistically significant differences in OS or PFS were observed between patients treated with or without radiotherapy. This lack of an apparent survival benefit should be interpreted with caution, as radiotherapy was selectively applied to patients with more aggressive disease features. Previous studies suggested that selected subgroups—particularly patients achieving only partial response after induction therapy—may derive benefit from consolidative radiotherapy, indicating that its role may be context‐dependent rather than universal [3, 28].

Regarding prognostic factors, our study confirmed that higher IPI scores were associated with inferior survival in univariate analysis and high‐risk IPI remained an independent predictor of OS in multivariable models. Consistent with previous reports [9], patients with IPI scores of 0–2 exhibited comparable outcomes, whereas those with scores of 3–5 experienced significantly worse survival, suggesting that the prognostic discrimination of IPI in PB‐DLBCL is primarily driven by the highest‐risk group. In addition, achievement of complete remission after first‐line therapy was strongly associated with favorable OS and PFS in univariate analyses, highlighting the clinical relevance of early treatment response. However, as a post‐treatment, time‐dependent variable, treatment response was not included in multivariable models. Collectively, these results emphasize the importance of baseline risk stratification and early therapeutic efficacy in patients with PB‐DLBCL [29].

Hypoalbuminemia was independently associated with inferior PFS in our cohort. Serum albumin is influenced by systemic inflammation, nutritional status and overall disease burden. Previous studies have demonstrated the adverse prognostic value of low baseline serum albumin in aggressive B‐cell lymphoma and DLBCL, including after adjustment for IPI and a recent meta‐analysis further supported its association with inferior survival across lymphoma populations [30, 31, 32]. Our results therefore suggest that serum albumin may provide additional baseline prognostic information in PB‐DLBCL, although external validation is required. From a clinical perspective, patients presenting with hypoalbuminemia may warrant closer assessment of nutritional status and other potentially reversible contributors to poor general condition, together with appropriate nutritional optimization and supportive care. Whether patients with an IPI score of 3–5 and concomitant hypoalbuminemia could benefit from intensified treatment strategies, novel‐agent‐containing regimens such as polatuzumab vedotin‐based therapy, or enhanced supportive care remains uncertain and requires prospective evaluation.

The occurrence of pulmonary lesions during the disease course of PBL presents a diagnostic challenge in distinguishing lymphoma involvement from a second primary lung malignancy. Careful assessment of imaging characteristics, treatment response and, when feasible, histopathologic confirmation is essential for accurate diagnosis. In our cohort, second primary lung cancer was observed in a small number of patients, highlighting the need for thorough evaluation and individualized treatment sequencing.

Interestingly, bone‐specific features, including bone pain, bone destruction and bone marrow infiltration, were not independently associated with survival. This finding suggests that in PBL, systemic disease burden and therapeutic response may outweigh local skeletal manifestations in determining long‐term outcomes. Such a pattern distinguishes PBL from other primary bone malignancies and underscores its nature as a systemic lymphoid neoplasm with predominant bone involvement.

Conclusion

Primary bone lymphoma is a clinically heterogeneous disease that frequently presents with advanced‐stage bone involvement but generally demonstrates favorable outcomes in the era of rituximab‐based chemoimmunotherapy. In this large single‐center cohort, baseline International Prognostic Index remained an independent predictor of overall survival, while hypoalbuminemia was independently associated with inferior progression‐free survival. Although post‐treatment response and metabolic assessment by PET/CT were strongly associated with survival outcomes in univariate analyses, their prognostic roles require further validation in prospective studies. Future multicenter investigations are warranted to confirm these associations and to refine risk‐adapted therapeutic strategies for patients with PB‐DLBCL.

Subalgebras of integrals, commutants, and superintegrable deformations of Lotka-Volterra systems
Ian Marquette a , Peter H. van der Kamp b , G.R.W. Quispel b

Abstract
We consider the Lie-algebraic notion of commutant in the setting of Poisson algebra. This provides a framework for deforming Hamiltonian differential equations. By taking a subalgebra of the algebra of integrals, and considering the set of functions that Poisson commute with that subalgebra, the Hamiltonian can be deformed, while retaining integrability. We deform Liouville integrable and superintegrable Lotka-Volterra systems studied in [ 19 ] . We present different explicit constructions considering Abelian and non-Abelian subalgebras of integrals. We obtain superintegrable systems for specific dimensions, and in arbitrary dimension. Polynomial systems are deformed to rational systems, some of which have non-rational integrals. Superintegrability seems to be preserved in this approach.

Introduction

2
Poisson algebras, commutants and superintegrable systems

In this section we recall the basic definitions of Liouville and superintegrable systems, which we adapt to the case of rational functions. Since the phenomena which we wish to describe in this paper are algebraic and very generally valid, we can take as our base field any field
\mathbb{F}
of characteristic zero, keeping in mind the examples of
\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbb{C}
; also, we use Poisson brackets, which are more general and more algebraic than symplectic structures.

Our basic algebra will be
{\cal A}_{n}=\mathbb{F}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
, the field of fractions over
\mathbb{F}
in
n
(commuting) indeterminates. Geometrically, one may think of
{\cal A}_{n}
as the field of rational functions on
\mathbb{F}^{n}
with values in
\mathbb{F}
. Such a function can be evaluated at a generic point of
\mathbb{F}^{n}
and is smooth when
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{R}
, and is holomorphic when
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{C}
, on an open dense subset of
\mathbb{F}^{n}
. A
Poisson bracket
on
{\cal A}
is a Lie bracket
\{\cdot,\cdot\}:{\cal A}\times{\cal A}\rightarrow{\cal A}
which satisfies the Leibniz rule in each argument, i.e., is a derivation in each argument. It follows that the Poisson bracket of
f,g\in{\cal A}_{n}
can be computed from the Poisson brackets
\left\{x_{i},x_{j}\right\}
, with
1\leqslant i<j\leqslant n
,

\left\{f,g\right\}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\left\{x_{i},x_{j}\right\}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}}\frac{\partial g}{\partial x_{j}}\;.

(2)

It makes
{\cal A}_{n}
into a
Poisson algebra

({\cal A}_{n},\left\{\cdot\,,\cdot\right\})
. It follows from (
2
), which is valid for any biderivation on
{\cal A}_{n}
, that
\left\{\cdot\,,\cdot\right\}
satisfies the Jacobi identity for any triplet of elements of
{\cal A}_{n}
as soon as the Jacobi identity is satisfied for all triplets
(x_{i},x_{j},x_{k})
with
1\leqslant i<j<k\leqslant n
. Thus the matrix
x_{i,j}=\left\{x_{i},x_{j}\right\}
contains all the information about the Poisson bracket; it is called the
Poisson matrix
of
({\cal A}_{n},\left\{\cdot\,,\cdot\right\})
and its rank is called the
Poisson rank
of
({\cal A}_{n},\left\{\cdot\,,\cdot\right\})
; the Poisson rank is an even number, smaller than or equal to
n
, denoted
\mathop{\rm Rk}\nolimits\left\{\cdot\,,\cdot\right\}
. It can be computed by evaluating the Poisson matrix at a generic point of
\mathbb{F}^{n}
.

Poisson subalgebras will play an important role in this paper. Given a Poisson algebra
{\cal A}
, a subset
{\cal F}\subset{\cal A}
is a
Poisson subalgebra
when it is both a subalgebra (that is,
{\cal F}{\cal F}\subset{\cal F}
) and a Lie subalgebra (that is,
\left\{{\cal F},{\cal F}\right\}\subset{\cal F}
). One may then restrict the product and Poisson bracket of
{\cal A}
to
{\cal F}
, which makes
{\cal F}
into a Poisson algebra. Suppose now that for a finite subset
\{f_{1},\dots,f_{k}\}
of
{\cal A}_{n}
, for any
i<j
,
\left\{f_{i},f_{j}\right\}
can be written as a rational function of
f_{1},\dots,f_{k}
. Then the subalgebra
{\cal F}
generated by
f_{1},\dots,f_{k}
is a Poisson subalgebra of
{\cal A}_{n}
. A particular case is when the functions are functionally independent. Then the subalgebra generated by
f_{1},\dots,f_{k}
is the field of fractions
{\cal F}=\mathbb{F}(f_{1},\dots,f_{k})
. Let us introduce the matrix
f_{i,j}(f)
such that

\{f_{i}(x),f_{j}(x)\}=f_{i,j}(f(x)).

(3)

Lemma 1
.

The matrix
f_{i,j}
defines a bracket on
{\cal F}
,
\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{\cal F}:{\cal F}\times{\cal F}\rightarrow{\cal F}
,

\{f_{i},f_{j}\}_{\cal F}=f_{i,j}.

(4)

Proof.

Suppose the Jacobi identity is not satisfied. Then there exist
i<j<k
such that

R_{i,j,k}(f)=\{f_{i},\{f_{j},f_{k}\}_{\cal F}\}_{\cal F}+\{f_{j},\{f_{k},f_{i}\}_{\cal F}\}_{\cal F}+\{f_{k},\{f_{i},f_{j}\}_{\cal F}\}_{\cal F}\neq 0.

However, as a function of
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
we have
R_{i,j,k}(f(x))=0
. Therefore,
R_{i,j,k}(f)=0
is a non-trivial relation between
f_{1},\ldots,f_{m}
, in contradiction with
f_{1},\ldots,f_{m}
being functionally independent.
∎

Consider on
{\cal A}_{5}:=\mathbb{F}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{5})

Definition 3

Suppose that

(1)

(2)

The functions

(3)

r+s=\dim M

(4)

A single element

4
Hamiltonian deformation using a subalgebra of integrals

We say that
{\cal G}=\mathbb{F}(g_{1},\ldots,g_{l})
is a
subalgebra of integrals
if

\{g_{i},g_{j}\}_{\cal F}\in{\cal G}\subset{\cal F}=\mathop{\rm FI}\nolimits(f_{1})=\mathbb{F}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{k}).

In the sequel, we will take
g_{1}=f_{1}
(
=H
, the Hamiltonian) and assume that the functions
g_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,l
, are functionally independent, so that
{\cal G}
is a Poisson algebra, cf. Lemma
1
. The
commutant
of the subalgebra of integrals
{\cal G}\subset{\cal F}
is the set of partial Casimirs

{\cal C}=C_{\cal F}({\cal G})
. We will aim to find rational (and functionally independent) functions
c_{i}
, so that
{\cal C}=\mathbb{F}(c_{1},\ldots,c_{d})
with
c_{j}\in{\cal F}
. As
H\in{\cal C}
, we will set
c_{1}=H
. Note that the functions
c_{i}
do not have to Poisson commute with each other. We will also call each
c_{i}\in{\cal C}
a commutant. The condition that
c\in{\cal C}
is a commutant of
{\cal G}\subset{\cal F}
is the system of partial differential equations

\{c,g_{i}\}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\{f_{j},g_{i}\}\frac{\partial c}{\partial f_{j}}=0,\quad i=1,\ldots,l.

(20)

This provides the possibility for
c
to take the form of polynomials or, more general, rational and even transcendental functions (as we will see in Section 6).

To an algebra of integrals
{\cal F}=\mathbb{F}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})
, with
H=f_{1}
, corresponds the Hamiltonian system

\dot{f}_{i}=\{f_{i},H\}_{\cal F}=0,\quad i=1,\ldots,m.

(21)

That is, in the new
f
-coordinates the Hamiltonian system is trivialised; readily solved by
f_{i}=constant
for all
i
.
We propose to deform the system (
21
), and the related system on
{\cal A}_{n}
, by using a subalgebra of integrals
G\subset{\cal F}
and its commutant
{\cal C}
. We take
\widehat{H}=\widehat{H}(g_{1},\ldots,g_{l})
as the deformed Hamiltonian, and then the corresponding system admits the commutants
c_{1},\ldots,c_{d}
as its integrals of motion. The original Hamiltonian
H
and any Casimirs are included in the integrals of these deformed systems.

Let us present more details on the approach we take towards solving the commutant constraints (
20
), which in general is a difficult problem. Here, we rely on solving the systems of PDEs using the method of characteristics implemented in symbolic computation software
[
27
]
. This provides sets of solutions

c_{i}=c_{i}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m}),\quad i=1,\ldots,d.

We then use the solutions obtained to perform a change of variables

z_{i}=c_{i}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})\quad i=1,\ldots,d.

The other variables
z_{i}
for
d+1,\ldots,m
are chosen among the
f_{i}
that are not part of the solution obtained already via solving the system of PDEs. If the system of PDEs (
20
), in the new variables, takes the form

m(z_{1},\ldots,z_{m})\frac{\partial K}{\partial z_{s}}=0,\quad s=d+1,\ldots,m,

this implies that the general solution only depends on the already obtained integrals, cf. Example
7
.

This approach will be implemented in examples of commutants associated with Abelian and non-Abelian subalgebras of integrals. We now choose low-dimensional non-Abelian subalgebras to illustrate the procedure, deforming both the odd- and the even-dimensional system (
11
).

Proposition 17
.

The commutant of
{\cal G}_{l}
includes

\mathbb{F}(H,C,F_{2},\ldots,F_{m-1},L_{1},\ldots,L_{m-l-3})

Proof.

We prove that
\{F_{i},L_{k}\}_{\cal F}=0
for

and

depends on only two of the

and
G_{m-k-1}
. Hence, we find

\displaystyle\{F_{i},L_{k}\}_{\cal F}

\displaystyle=\{F_{i},G_{m-k}\}_{\cal F}\frac{\partial L_{k}}{\partial G_{m-k}}+\{F_{i},G_{m-k-1}\}_{\cal F}\frac{\partial L_{k}}{\partial G_{m-k-1}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\prod_{j=0}^{i-k-1}F_{k+j+1}G_{m-k-j}-CH}{\prod_{j=1}^{i-k-1}F_{k+j}G_{m-k-j}+CH}\left(\frac{F_{k+1}}{\left(F_{k+1}G_{m-k-1}+CH\right)}\right)

\displaystyle\ \ \ +\frac{\prod_{j=0}^{i-k-2}F_{k+j+2}G_{m-k-1-j}-CH}{\prod_{j=1}^{i-k-2}F_{k+j+1}G_{m-k-1-j}+CH}\left(\frac{F_{k+1}\left(F_{k+1}G_{m-k}-CH\right)}{\left(F_{k+1}G_{m-k-1}+CH\right)^{2}}\right)

which can be seen by shifting the dummy variable

in the second term, and including the factors with

in the numerator, and

in the denominator, in the products.

8
Conclusion

We have provided different deformations of superintegrable systems based on the notion of commutant or partial Casimirs. This concept has been applied in recent years in the context of Lie and Poisson-Lie algebras with the aim of providing new insight into algebraic superintegrable systems, their algebra of integrals, but also in the decomposition of enveloping algebras of Lie algebras, the missing label problem, and dynamical symmetries. This paper has greatly extended the construction by allowing the underlying Poisson algebra to involve polynomial and even rational bracket relations.

We considered subalgebras of different types, Abelian, non-Abelian and one-dimensional subalgebras with parameters, for the construction of the commutant. The new integrals are obtained as solutions to partial differential equations, and they can be polynomial, rational, or even transcendental functions. The Hamiltonians are by construction deformations of the initial Lotka-Volterra systems and they open the way for applications in a different context. This paper points out how, from a given superintegrable system, large families of deformed superintegrable systems can be obtained in arbitrary dimensions. In all examples provided, superintegrability is preserved under subalgebra of integrals deformation.

Next we prove Proposition

which is sufficient for (

•

•

•

. Hence

\displaystyle\{F_{i},\{F_{j},G_{k}\}\}

\displaystyle=-F_{i}\{F_{i},G_{r+1-i}\}(-1)^{j-i}\prod_{l=1}^{j-i}\dfrac{CH-F_{i+l}G_{r+1-i-l}}{CH+F_{i+l-1}G_{r+1-i-l}}

Next, consider

Results on cubic bent and weakly regular bent p p -ary functions leading to a class of cubic ternary non-weakly regular bent functions
Claude Carlet †‡ and Alexander Kholosha ‡

Abstract
Much work has been devoted to bent functions in odd characteristic, but a gap remains between our knowledge of binary and nonbinary bent functions. In the first part of this paper, we attempt to partially bridge this gap by generalizing to any characteristic important properties known in characteristic 2 concerning the Walsh transform of derivatives of bent functions. Some of these properties generalize to all bent functions, while others appear to apply only to weakly regular bent functions. We deduce a method to obtain a bent function by adding a quadratic function to a weakly regular bent function. We also identify a particular class of bent functions possessing the property that every first-order derivative in a nonzero direction has a derivative (which is then a second-order derivative of the function) equal to a nonzero constant. We show that this property implies bentness and is shared in particular by all cubic bent functions. It generalizes the notion of cubic-like bent function, that was introduced and studied for binary functions by Irene Villa and the first author. In the second part of the paper, we provide (for the first time) a primary construction leading to an infinite class of cubic bent functions that are not weakly regular. We show the bentness of the functions by two approaches: by calculating the Walsh transform directly and by considering the second-order derivatives (and applying the results from the first part of the paper).

An important notion for the study of

are equivalent to it are also bent (and some of them may have complex representations while the function found has in general a simple
representation). When a new class is found, its discoverers must check whether some of its elements are inequivalent to all known bent
functions found until then (this can be very difficult).

\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}}\omega^{D_{a}f(x)}=\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}}\omega^{D_{a}f(x+b)}=\omega^{D_{a,b}f(0)}\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}}\omega^{D_{a}f(x)}

[

(recalled in the introduction) completes the proof.∎

To prove that every cubic bent function is cubic-like bent we need the following result.

Theorem 3.2

Any quadratic
p
-ary function
f
is balanced if and only if there exists
a\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}
such that the first-order derivative

is a nonzero constant function.

Proof

According to Lemma
1
,
f
is balanced if and only if
\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}}\omega^{f(x)}=0
. Rather than

\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}}\omega^{f(x)}
, we consider

and transform it as in Relation (

) (with

\big|\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}}\omega^{f(x)}\big|^{2}=\sum_{x,y\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}}\omega^{f(y)-f(x)}=\sum_{x,a\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}}\omega^{f(x+a)-f(x)}=\sum_{x,a\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}}\omega^{D_{a}f(x)}\kern 5.0pt.

For any

, the derivative
D_{a}f(x)
is affine since

is quadratic. Then
\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}}\omega^{D_{a}f(x)}
is zero unless

is constant. Let

be the set of those

is constant. Then,

There exists an identity between the Walsh transforms of the (first-order) derivatives of a binary bent function and of its dual, which has
initially been given in

and has interesting consequences, as reported in

Theorem 4.1

For any odd prime
p
, any
p
-ary weakly regular bent function

, and any

, the Walsh transform of the
first-order derivative of

in the direction of

takes on only real values and for any

\displaystyle W_{D_{c}f}(b)

\displaystyle=W_{D_{c}f}(-b)=W_{D_{-c}f}(b)

\displaystyle W_{D_{c}f}(b)

\displaystyle=\omega^{{\operatorname{Tr}}_{n}(bc)}W_{D_{b}f^{*}}(-c)\kern 5.0pt.

(5)

If
{\operatorname{Tr}}_{n}(bc)\neq 0
then
W_{D_{c}f}(b)=0
and if
{\operatorname{Tr}}_{n}(bc)=0
then

W_{D_{c}f}(b)=W_{D_{b}f^{*}}(c)\kern 5.0pt.

Proof

Taking any
b,c\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}
we have (denoting by
\bar{\quad}
the complex conjugate):

\displaystyle W_{D_{c}f}(b)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}}\omega^{D_{c}f(x)-{\operatorname{Tr}}_{n}(bx)}

\displaystyle=p^{-n}\sum_{x,y,v\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}}\omega^{f(y)-f(x)-{\operatorname{Tr}}_{n}(bx+v(y-x-c))}

\displaystyle=p^{-n}\sum_{v\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}}W_{f}(v)\overline{W_{f}(v-b)}\omega^{{\operatorname{Tr}}_{n}(vc)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{v\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}}\omega^{f^{*}(v)-f^{*}(v-b)+{\operatorname{Tr}}_{n}(vc)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{v\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}}\omega^{D_{b}f^{*}(v)+{\operatorname{Tr}}_{n}((v+b)c)}

\displaystyle=\omega^{{\operatorname{Tr}}_{n}(bc)}W_{D_{b}f^{*}}(-c)\kern 5.0pt.

This proves (
4.1
).
Note that, at the fourth equality above, we used (
2

is weakly regular.

Recall that the dual

for any

Note that, for any

\big(D_{c,d}f(x)\equiv\lambda\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{*}\big)\Rightarrow\big(\forall b\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}\ \mbox{with}\ {\operatorname{Tr}}_{n}(bd)\neq\lambda\Rightarrow W_{D_{c}f}(b)=0\Big)\kern 5.0pt.

(6)

Indeed, if the condition on the left-hand side is satisfied, then we have

\displaystyle W_{D_{c}f}(b)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}}\omega^{D_{c}f(x)-{\operatorname{Tr}}_{n}(bx)}=\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}}\omega^{D_{c}f(x+d)-{\operatorname{Tr}}_{n}(b(x+d))}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}}\omega^{D_{c}f(x)+\lambda-{\operatorname{Tr}}_{n}(b(x+d))}=\omega^{\lambda-{\operatorname{Tr}}_{n}(bd)}W_{D_{c}f}(b)\kern 5.0pt.

Note that, given two functions

(7)

(i) Taking weakly regular

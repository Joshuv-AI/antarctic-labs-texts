Global well-posedness for generalized fractional Hartree equations with rough initial data in all dimensions
Yufeng Lu

Abstract
We prove the global existence of the solution for fractional Hartree equations with initial data in certain real interpolation spaces between L 2 L^{2} and some kinds of new function spaces defined by fractional Schrödinger semigroup, which could imply the global well-posedness of the equation in modulation spaces M p , p ′ s p M_{p,p^{\prime}}^{s_{p}} for p p close to 2 with no smallness condition on initial data, where s p = ( m − 2 ) ​ ( 1 / 2 − 1 / p ) s_{p}=(m-2)(1/2-1/p) . The proof adapts a splitting method inspired by the work of Hyakuna-Tsutsumi, Chaichenets et al. to the modulation spaces and exploits polynomial growth of the fractional Schrödinger semi-group on modulation spaces M p , p ′ M_{p,p^{\prime}} with loss of regularity s p s_{p} .

Introduction

Let
m\geqslant 2
, we consider the following fractional nonlinear Schrödinger equation with convolution nonlinearity:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}u+(-\Delta)^{m/2}u=\pm\left(K*\left|u\right|^{\beta}\right)u,\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\end{cases}

(fNLS-h)

where
(t,x)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d},u(t,x)\in\mathbb{C},

K
denotes the Hartree kernel
K(x)=\dfrac{1}{\left|x\right|^{\gamma}},
for
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},0<\gamma<d,
and
*
denotes the convolution in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. The fractional Laplacian is defined as

(-\Delta)^{m/2}u={\mathscr{F}^{-1}}\left|\xi\right|^{m}{\mathscr{F}}u(\xi),

where
({\mathscr{F}^{-1}}){\mathscr{F}}
denotes the (inverse) Fourier transform in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.

The equation (
fNLS-h
) is known as the Hartree type equation with fractional Laplacian. We also call it fractional Hartree equation. This equation is a generalization of the classical Hartree equation with
\beta=m=2
, which commonly referred to as Boson star equation to be regarded as the classical limit of a field equation that describes a non-relativistic many-boson system interacting through a two-body potential (see
[
15
,
16
]
).

The modulation space
M_{p,q}^{s}
is the function space, introduced by Feichtinger
[
13
]
in the 1980s using the short-time Fourier transform to measure the decay and the regularity of the function differently from the usual
L^{p}
Sobolev spaces or Besov-Triebel spaces. For example, the Schrödinger and wave semigroups, which are not bounded on neither
L^{p}
nor
B_{p,q}^{s}
for
p\neq 2
, are bounded on
M_{p,q}^{s}
(see
[
1
]
). Therefore, the modulation space is a good space for initial data of the Cauchy problem for nonlinear dispersive equations.

Denote

\displaystyle S_{m}(t)

\displaystyle:={\mathscr{F}^{-1}}e^{it\left|\xi\right|^{m}}{\mathscr{F}};

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}f(t)

\displaystyle:=\int_{0}^{t}S_{m}(t-\tau)f(\tau)d\tau.

We call
u
is a solution of (
fNLS-h
) if it satisfies the integration form of (
fNLS-h
) as follows.

\displaystyle u=S_{m}(t)u_{0}\mp i\mathscr{A}\left(K*\left|u\right|^{\beta}\right)u.

When in its lifespan, the solutions to (
fNLS-h
) satisfy mass conservalation:

\displaystyle M[u(t)]

\displaystyle:=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\left|u(t,x)\right|^{2}dx=M[u_{0}].

Moreover, if
u
is a solution to (
fNLS-h
), so is the scaled function

\displaystyle u_{\lambda}(t,x)

\displaystyle=\lambda^{\frac{m+d-\gamma}{\beta}}u(\lambda^{m}t,\lambda x).

The critical exponent
s_{c}
is the unique real number conserving the homogeneous Sobolev norm

\displaystyle\left\|u_{\lambda}(0,x)\right\|_{\dot{H^{s_{c}}}}=\left\|u(0,x)\right\|_{\dot{H^{s_{c}}}},\ s_{c}=\frac{d}{2}-\frac{m+d-\gamma}{\beta}.

We first recall the notion of well-posendess in the sense of Hadamard.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
X,Y\hookrightarrow\mathscr{S}^{\prime}
be Banach spaces. The Cauchy problem (

1.

;

is continuous from
(B,\left\|\ \cdot\ \right\|_{X})
to

.

In particular, when

, we say
(
fNLS-h
)
is locally well-posed in

can be chosen arbitrarily, then we say (

) is globally well-posed in

2.2
Modulation spaces

Let

Then modulation spaces
M_{p,q}^{s}
can be defined as follows.

\displaystyle M_{p,q}^{s}=\left\{f\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),\left\|f\right\|_{M_{p,q}^{s}}<\infty\right\}.

where the norm
\left\|f\right\|_{M_{p,q}^{s}}
is defined as

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{M_{p,q}^{s}}=\left\|\left\|\left\langle\xi\right\rangle^{s}V_{g}f(x,\xi)\right\|_{L_{x}^{p}}\right\|_{L_{\xi}^{q}}.

Here
\left\langle\xi\right\rangle=(1+\left|\xi\right|^{2})^{1/2}

is the short time Fourier transform of the function
f
with the window function

, that is

\displaystyle V_{g}f(x,\xi)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}f(t)\overline{g(t-x)}e^{-2\pi it\xi}dt.

We usual denote
M_{p,q}^{0}

M_{p,q}
. It can be shown that different choices of the window function

[

[

]

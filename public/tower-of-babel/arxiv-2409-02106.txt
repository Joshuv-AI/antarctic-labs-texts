1.
Introduction

The Möbius function
\mu(n)
is defined for
n\in\mathbb{N}
as

\mu(n)=\begin{cases}1;&\textnormal{$n=1$}\\
0;&\textnormal{$n$ has a repeated prime factor (not square-free)}\\
(-1)^{\omega(n)};&\textnormal{$n$ has $\omega(n)$ distinct prime factors (square-free)}\end{cases}

It has the important property that its sum over the divisors of any
n>1
vanishes, i.e.,
\sum_{m|n}\mu(m)=0

for all
n>1
. The summatory Möbius function
M(n)
, also called the Mertens function, is defined as the sum
M(n)=\sum_{m\leq n}\mu(m)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\nmid n\\
m<n\end{subarray}}\mu(m).
The straightforward vanishing of the divisor sum
\sum_{m|n}\mu(m)
contrasts strongly with the behavior of the non-divisor sum
M(n)
, as the latter grows in average magnitude and fluctuates irregularly as
n
increases due to the pseudorandomness properties of
\mu(n)
, which are an important topic in contemporary number theory

[

that the stronger and unproven statement

M(n)=O\left(n^{1/2+\varepsilon}\right)

The Liouville function
\lambda(n)
is closely related to
\mu(n)
but displays some key differences. It is defined for
n\in\mathbb{N}
as

\mu(n)=\begin{cases}1;&\textnormal{$n=1$}\\
(-1)^{\Omega(n)};&\textnormal{$n$ has $\Omega(n)$ prime factors (counting multiplicities)}\end{cases}

The Liouville function also has a well-understood sum over the divisors of any
n>1
:
\sum_{m|n}\lambda(m)
is equal to 1 for

a perfect square and is equal to

L(n)
is defined as the sum
L(n)=\sum_{m\leq n}\lambda(n).

Similarly to

Main results

Lemma 2.1

(2.7)

Proof.

From Perron’s formula (see e.g.
[

]

Titchmarsh & Heath-Brown

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{2-i\infty}^{2+i\infty}\frac{x^{s}}{s\zeta(s)}ds=\sum_{\left|\gamma\right|<T}\frac{n^{\rho}}{\rho\zeta^{\prime}(\rho)}-2-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\left(\frac{2\pi i}{n}\right)^{2k}}{(2k)!k\zeta(2k+1)}

(2.9)

\displaystyle+O\left(\frac{1}{T}\int_{2}^{\infty}\left(\frac{2\pi}{n}\right)^{u}e^{u-(u-1/2)\log u}du\right)+\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{-1+iT}^{2+iT}\frac{n^{s}}{s\zeta(s)}ds.

The first integral on (

and

(2.10)

\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{-1+iT}^{2+iT}\frac{n^{s}}{s\zeta(s)}ds=O\left(n^{2}\int_{-1+iT}^{2+iT}\left|\frac{1}{s\zeta(s)}\right|ds\right).

such that

and

(2.11)

\frac{1}{\zeta(s)}=O\left(t^{\epsilon}\right)

for

and arbitrary

(
[
37
]

Combining (

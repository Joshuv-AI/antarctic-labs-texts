1.
Introduction and the main result

Given an arithmetical function
f:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{C}
, we expect that

(1.1)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leqslant X\\
n\equiv a\bmod{q}\end{subarray}}f(n)\sim\frac{1}{\varphi(q)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leqslant X\\
(n,q)=1\end{subarray}}f(n),

for each
(a,q)=1
. It is an interesting problem in number theory to show that the above asymptotic holds for
q
as large as possible. To this end, we call a positive number
\theta_{k}
a distribution exponent for
f
restricted to a set
\mathcal{Q}
of moduli, if for any
q\in\mathcal{Q}
with
q\leqslant X^{\theta_{k}-\varepsilon}
and for each residue class

a
modulo

q
with
(a,q)=1
, the asymptotic formula

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leqslant X\\
n\equiv a\bmod{q}\end{subarray}}f(n)-\frac{1}{\varphi(q)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leqslant X\\
(n,q)=1\end{subarray}}f(n)\ll\frac{X}{q}(\log X)^{-A}

holds for any
A>0
and
X\geqslant 2
.

For the very important von Mangoldt function
\Lambda(n)
, the classical Siegel-Walfisz theorem implies that the above asymptotic hold for
q\leqslant(\log X)^{B(A)}
, where
B(A)>0
depends on
A
, whereas the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis (GRH) predicts
q\leqslant X^{\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon}
. The celebrated Bombieri-Vinogradov theorem confirms this prediction on average over the moduli.

Another important class of examples comes from the
k
-th divisor function

d_{k}(n):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1}\cdots n_{k}=n\\
n_{1},\dots,n_{k}\in\mathbb{Z^{+}}\end{subarray}}1.

For each fixed
k\geqslant 1
, we are interested in the admissible value of
\theta_{k}
being as large as possible so that for any
\varepsilon>0
, the bound

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leqslant X\\
n\equiv a\bmod{q}\end{subarray}}d_{k}(n)-\frac{1}{\varphi(q)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leqslant X\\
(n,q)=1\end{subarray}}d_{k}(n)\ll\frac{X}{q}(\log X)^{-A}

holds for all
q\leqslant X^{\theta_{k}-\varepsilon}
and
A>0
. It is widely believed that one can take
\theta_{k}=1
for each
k\geqslant 1
. Relying on Weil’s bound for Kloosterman sums, Selberg
[
11
]
and Hooley
[
6
]
independently obtained that
\theta_{2}=\frac{2}{3}
is admissible. For
k=3
, this problem attracted the attention of many mathematicians. At first, Friedlander and Iwaniec
[
4
]
proved that one can take
\theta_{3}=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{230}
, and this was later improved to
\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{82}
by Heath-Brown
[
5
]
. In the case of prime moduli, Fouvry, Kowalski and Michel
[
2
]
proved that

\theta_{3}=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{46}
is admissible by appealing to their bilinear forms of Kloosterman sums with smooth coefficients (see
[
1
, Theorem 1.16]
). The problem becomes much easier if conditions are imposed on the prime factorization of
q
. For instance, Irving
[
7
]
, Xi
[
13
]
, and Sharma
[
12
]
improved the results. So far, the best result is due to Sharma
[
12
]
, who obtained
\theta_{3}=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{30}
.

Let
f
be a holomorphic cusp form of level
1
with Hecke eigenvalues
\lambda_{f}(n)
, normalized so that
|\lambda_{f}(n)|\leqslant d(n)
, where
d(n)
is the classical divisor function. For
n\geqslant 1
, we define

(1.2)

(\lambda_{f}*1)(n):=\mathop{\sum\sum}_{ml=n}\lambda_{f}(m).

The exponent of the distribution of
\lambda_{f}*1
is also an interesting problem. Kowalski, Michel and Sawin
[
8
]
proved that
\theta_{3}=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{102}
when
q
ranges over prime moduli.

In this paper, we focus on estimating
\theta_{3}
for
\lambda_{f}*1
when
q
ranges over square-free moduli. Our main result is the following Theorem
1.1
, which achieves a larger exponent than in the prime-modulus case treated in
[
8
]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
q
be a square-free integer and
a\geqslant 1
be an integer such that
(a,q)=1
. For any
\varepsilon>0
,
A>0
, and
X\geqslant 1
satisfying

q\leqslant X^{\frac{12}{23}-\varepsilon}=X^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{46}-\varepsilon},

we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leqslant X\\
n\equiv a\bmod{q}\end{subarray}}(\lambda_{f}*1)(n)-\frac{1}{\varphi(q)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leqslant X\\
(n,q)=1\end{subarray}}(\lambda_{f}*1)(n)\ll\frac{X}{q}(\log X)^{-A},

where
(\lambda_{f}*1)(n)
is defined as in (
1.2
), and the implied constant depends on
\varepsilon
and
A
.

The key input is an estimate for the bilinear sums with
\mathrm{GL(2)}
coefficients obtained using the separation of oscillation technique. In
[
8
]
, Kowalski, Michel and Sawin established a bound for special bilinear sums for prime moduli, which employed a lot of algebraic geometry methods. Nevertheless, their approach does not extend to smooth moduli. A key strategy to overcome this is to employ the
q
-van der Corput method to improve the estimate for the corresponding bilinear sums.

In addition, the estimation of bilinear sums also relies on techniques such as the Poisson summation formula, the Voronoi summation formula, and the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Bilinear forms with Kloosterman sums

Let
q\geqslant 1
be an integer and let
K(n)
be a complex-valued arithmetic function that is
q
-periodic. A recurring problem in analytic number theory is to understand how such functions correlate with other natural arithmetic functions
f(n)
, where
f
could
be the characteristic function of an interval, or that of the primes, or the Fourier coefficients of some automorphic forms. When facing such problems, one is often led to the problem of bounding non-trivially some bilinear forms

(2.1)

B(\alpha,\beta;K)=\sum_{m}\sum_{l}\alpha_{m}\beta_{l}K(ml),

where the coefficients
\alpha=(\alpha_{m})_{m\leqslant M}
and
\beta=(\beta_{l})_{l\in\mathcal{L}}
are supported in intervals of length
M
and
L
, respectively.
We denote

\|\alpha\|_{1}:=\sum_{m}|\alpha_{m}|,\qquad\|\alpha\|_{2}:=\Bigl(\sum_{m}|\alpha_{m}|^{2}\Bigl)^{1/2},

the
\ell^{1}
and
\ell^{2}
norms.
One of the main objectives is to improve upon the trivial bound

\|K\|_{\infty}\|\alpha\|_{2}\|\beta\|_{2}(ML)^{\frac{1}{2}}

for ranges of
M
and
L
that are as small compared to
q
as possible. Indeed, this uniformity is often more important than the size of the saving over the trivial bound.

In particular, when dealing with problems related to the analytic theory
of automorphic forms, one is often faced with the case where
K(ml)
is a hyper-Kloosterman sum
\mathrm{Kl}_{k}(n;q)
, we recall that the sum is defined, for
k\geqslant 2
, by

\mathrm{Kl}_{k}(n;q):=\frac{1}{q^{\frac{k-1}{2}}}\mathop{\sum\cdots\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{1},\dots,x_{k}\in(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})^{\times}\\
x_{1}\dots x_{k}\equiv n\bmod{q}\end{subarray}}e\Bigl(\frac{x_{1}+\dots+x_{k}}{q}\Bigl).

It satisfies Deligne’s bound

|\mathrm{Kl}_{k}(n;q)|\leqslant k.

It is the classical Kloosterman sum when
k=2
,
and the celebrated Weil’s bound gives

\sum_{x\in(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}e\Bigl(\frac{ax+bx^{-1}}{q}\Bigl)\ll\gcd(a,b,q)^{\frac{1}{2}}q^{\frac{1}{2}+o(1)}.

In our paper, when q is square-free, we obtain upper bounds for bilinear forms with
\mathrm{GL(2)}
coefficients. For
\alpha=(\lambda_{f}(m))_{m\leqslant M}
, and
\beta=(\beta_{l})_{l\in\mathcal{L}}
with
|\beta_{l}|\ll 1
, and
K=\mathrm{Kl}_{3}(aml;q)
, we prove the following upper bounds.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
q\geqslant 1
be a square-free integer, and let
a\in\mathbb{Z}
be coprime to
q
. Let
V
and
W
be smooth functions compactly supported in the interval
[1,2]
and satisfying

V^{(j)}(x),W^{(j)}(x)\ll_{j}Q^{j}

for some
Q\geqslant 1
and for all
j\geqslant 0
.
Then we have

\displaystyle\sum_{m\geqslant 1}\sum_{l\geqslant 1}\lambda_{f}(m)V\Bigl(\frac{m}{M}\Bigl)W\Bigl(\frac{l}{L}\Bigl)\mathrm{Kl}_{3}(aml;q)

\displaystyle\ll q^{\varepsilon}Q^{C}ML\Big(q^{-\frac{1}{3}}M^{\frac{1}{2}}+M^{\frac{1}{2}}L^{-\frac{1}{2}}+q^{\frac{1}{3}}L^{-\frac{1}{2}}\big),

where the implied constant depends only on
\varepsilon
, and
C>0
is an absolute constant.

Proof.

Let

J_{m}=\sum_{l\geqslant 1}W\Bigl(\frac{l}{L}\Bigl)\mathrm{Kl}_{3}(aml;q).

By partial summation formula and the bound

\lambda_{f}(m)\ll m^{\frac{1}{2}},

we have

\displaystyle\sum_{m\geqslant 1}\lambda_{f}(m)V\Bigl(\frac{m}{M}\Bigl)\sum_{l\geqslant 1}W\Bigl(\frac{l}{L}\Bigl)\mathrm{Kl}_{3}(aml;q)=\sum_{1<m\leqslant M}\lambda_{f}(m)J_{m}=\int_{1}^{M}\lambda_{f}(x)\mathrm{d}\sum_{1<m\leqslant x}J_{m}

\ll\lambda_{f}(M)\sum_{1<m\leqslant M}J_{m}+\left|\int_{1}^{M}\lambda^{\prime}_{f}(x)\Big(\sum_{1<m\leqslant x}J_{m}\Big)\mathrm{d}x\right|\ll M^{\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{1<m\leqslant M}J_{m}.

According to Lemma 2.4 of
[
13
]
, we obtain

\ll q^{\varepsilon}Q^{C}ML\big(q^{-\frac{1}{3}}M^{\frac{1}{2}}+M^{\frac{1}{2}}L^{-\frac{1}{2}}+q^{\frac{1}{3}}L^{-\frac{1}{2}}\big).

∎

3.1.
Auxiliary lemmas

To prove Theorem
1.1
, we will need several lemmas.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
q
be a square-free integer, and let
V
,
W
be two smooth functions compactly supported on
[0,+\infty)
. Let
K:\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be any q-periodic arithmetic function. Then we have

\sum_{m\geqslant 1}\sum_{l\geqslant 1}K(ml)\lambda_{f}(m)V(m)W(l)=q^{-\frac{1}{2}}\widehat{K}(0)T_{1}+(K(0)-q^{-\frac{1}{2}}\widehat{K}(0))T_{2}+q^{-\frac{3}{2}}T_{3},

where

\displaystyle T_{1}=\sum_{m\geqslant 1}\sum_{l\geqslant 1}\lambda_{f}(m)V(m)V(l),

\displaystyle T_{2}=\sum_{m\geqslant 1}\sum_{l\geqslant 1}\lambda_{f}(m)V(m)V(ql),

\displaystyle T_{3}=\sum_{m\geqslant 1}\sum_{l\geqslant 1}\widetilde{K}(ml)\lambda_{f}(m)\check{V}(\frac{m}{q^{2}})\widehat{W}(\frac{l}{q}),

with
\check{V}
is defined as in (
2.3
),
\widehat{W}
denotes the Fourier transform of
W
,

\widetilde{K}(m)=q^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{(u,q)=1}K(u)\mathrm{Kl}_{3}(mu;q).

In particular, if
(a,q)=1
and
K(n)=\delta_{n\equiv a\bmod{q}}
, then we have

K(0)=0,\quad\widehat{K}(0)=q^{-\frac{1}{2}},\quad\widetilde{K}(m)=q^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{Kl}_{3}(am,q)

by a direct computation.

Proof.

We split the sum into

\displaystyle\sum_{m\geqslant 1}\sum_{l\geqslant 1}K(ml)\lambda_{f}(m)V(m)W(l)

\displaystyle=\Bigl(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l\geqslant 1\\
l\mid q\end{subarray}}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l\geqslant 1\\
(l,q)=1\end{subarray}}\Bigl)\sum_{m\geqslant 1}K(ml)\lambda_{f}(m)V(m)W(l)

\displaystyle:=S_{1}+S_{2}.

Let
p_{i}
be a factor of
q
. Let
p
be a prime factor of
q
. For those
l
divisible by
p
, we can write
l=pl_{1}
, and then we have

\displaystyle S_{1}=

\displaystyle\sum_{m\geqslant 1}\sum_{l_{i}\geqslant 1}K(mp_{i}l_{i})\lambda_{f}(m)V(m)W(p_{i}l_{i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle K(0)\sum_{m\geqslant 1}\sum_{l\geqslant 1}K(ml)\lambda_{f}(m)V(m)W(ql).

For those
l
coprime to
q
, applying the Fourier inversion formula, we have

K(ml)=q^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{u\bmod{q}}\widehat{K}(u)e\Bigl(\frac{uml}{q}\Bigl)=q^{-\frac{1}{2}}\widehat{K}(0)+q^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\bmod q\\
(u,q)=1\end{subarray}}\widehat{K}(u)e\Bigl(-\frac{uml}{q}\Bigl).

So we get

\displaystyle S_{2}=

\displaystyle q^{-\frac{1}{2}}\widehat{K}(0)\mathop{\sum\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,l\geqslant 1\\
(l,q)=1\end{subarray}}\lambda_{f}(m)V(m)W(l)

\displaystyle+q^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\bmod q\\
(u,q)=1\end{subarray}}\widehat{K}(u)\mathop{\sum\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,l\geqslant 1\\
(l,q)=1\end{subarray}}\lambda_{f}(m)V(m)W(l)e\Bigl(-\frac{uml}{q}\Bigl).

The contribution of the first term on the right side is

q^{-\frac{1}{2}}\widehat{K}(0)\Bigl(\sum_{m,l\geqslant 1}\lambda_{f}(m)V(m)W(l)-\sum_{m,l\geqslant 1}\lambda_{f}(m)V(m)W(ql)\Bigr).

For the last term of
S_{2}
, we apply the Voronoi summation formula to the sum over
m
for
u\neq 0
and obtain

\sum_{m\geqslant 1}\lambda_{f}(m)V(m)e\Bigl(\frac{uml}{q}\Bigr)=\frac{1}{q}\sum_{m\geqslant 1}\lambda_{f}(m)\check{V}\Bigl(\frac{m}{q^{2}}\Bigr)e\Bigl(\frac{\overline{l}\overline{u}m}{q}\Bigr).

Therefore, the total contribution of
S_{2}
is

\frac{1}{q}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,l\geqslant 1\\
(l,q)=1\end{subarray}}\lambda_{f}(m)\check{V}\Bigl(\frac{m}{q^{2}}\Bigr)W(l)\widetilde{K}(m,l)

with

\widetilde{K}(m,l)=q^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{(u,q)=1}\widehat{K}(u)e\Bigl(\frac{m\overline{l}\overline{u}}{q}\Bigr).

We finish by applying the Poisson summation formula to the sum over
l
and for each
m
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l\geqslant 1\\
(l,q)=1\end{subarray}}W(l)\widetilde{K}(m,l)

\displaystyle=q^{-\frac{3}{2}}\widehat{K}(u)\sum_{l\geqslant 1}\widehat{W}\Bigl(\frac{l}{q}\Bigr)\sum_{(v,q)=1}e\Bigl(\frac{m\overline{u}\overline{v}+lv}{q}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{q}\widehat{K}(u)\sum_{l\geqslant 1}\widehat{W}\Bigl(\frac{l}{q}\Bigr)\mathrm{Kl}_{2}(ml\overline{u};q).

So that the total contribution becomes

q^{-\frac{3}{2}}\mathop{\sum\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,l\geqslant 1\\
(l,q)=1\end{subarray}}\widetilde{K}(ml)\lambda_{f}(m)\check{V}\Bigl(\frac{m}{q^{2}}\Bigr)\widehat{W}\Bigl(\frac{l}{q}\Bigr),

where

\widetilde{K}(m)=q^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{(u,q)=1}\widehat{K}(u)\mathrm{Kl}_{2}(m\overline{u};q)=q^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{(u,q)=1}K(u)\mathrm{Kl}_{3}(mu;q)

for any
m
. This gives the formula we stated.
∎

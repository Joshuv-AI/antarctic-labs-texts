Space-Time Graded-Index Interfaces and Related Chirping
Zhiyu Li, Xikui Ma, Klaas De Kinder, Amir Bahrami, Christophe Caloz

Abstract
Space-time modulated systems have recently emerged as a powerful platform for dynamic electromagnetic processing in both space and time. Most of the related research so far has assumed abrupt parameter profiles. This paper extends the field to generalized graded-index (GRIN) interfaces, which are both more practical than ideal profiles and offer new avenues for wave manipulations. It presents an exact solution for wave propagation across arbitrary space-time modulated GRIN interfaces and describes versatile chirping effects. The solution is based on a generalization of the impulse response method from linear time-invariant to linear space-time-varying systems. The proposed framework shows that space-time GRIN systems represent a novel approach for generating a new form of chirping that is not inherently based on dispersion, with promising applications in pulse shaping and signal processing.



Keywords:
space-time modulation; graded-index media; generalized impulse response; instantaneous frequency; chirping

Generalized Impulse Response Method

In this section, we shall derive the generalized impulse for the GRIN system in Fig.
1
(b), which is generically represented in Fig.
2

In the frequency domain, the one-dimensional Helmholtz equation may be expressed as

\frac{\partial^{2}\tilde{E}}{\partial z^{2}}+k^{2}(\omega)\tilde{E}=0,

(1)

where
k(\omega)=\omega\sqrt{\mu\epsilon}=\omega n/c
is the wavenumber.
The general solution to Eq. (
1
) can be written as

\tilde{E}(z,\omega)=H(z,\omega)\tilde{E}(0,\omega),

(2a)

where

H(z,\omega)=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}k(\omega)z}

(2b)

represents the frequency response of the system.

Applying the temporal inverse Fourier transformation to Eqs. (
2

2
(a), we obtain the corresponding time-domain relation

E(z,t)=\int^{\infty}_{-\infty}h(z,t,t^{\prime})E(0,t^{\prime})~\mathrm{d}t^{\prime},

(3)

where
h(z,t,t^{\prime})
is the impulse response
[
16
]
, representing the system’s response to the impulse
E(0,t)=\delta(t-t^{\prime})

1

The impulse response acts as a time-domain version of the Green’s function in the space-domain relation
\mathbf{E}(\mathbf{r})=\mathrm{i}\omega\mu\int_{V}G(\mathrm{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime})\mathbf{J}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})~\mathrm{d}V^{\prime}

[
17
]
, with
E(0,t^{\prime})
in Eq. (
3
) playing the role of the current source
\mathbf{J}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})
and
h(z,t,t^{\prime})
corresponding to the Green’s function
G(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime})
.
.

In contrast, in dispersive systems, not considered here, the frequency-dependent group velocity causes temporal spreading, and the impulse response becomes a more complex function of

where

To derive the electromagnetic field in each region of the space-time GRIN system [Fig.

4
Chirping Physics

Due to the space and time varying properties of the GRIN medium, the wave behavior in our system is fairly complex, as mathematically apparent in Eq. (
26
). In this section, we shall show that this system produces a new kind of wave chirping.

We consider a time-harmonic incident field, which reads at
z=0
,

E(0,t)=\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\omega_{0}t},

(33)

where
\omega_{0}
is assumed to be constant. Other types of fields can be treated similarly.
Substituting Eq. (
33
) into Eq. (
26
)—specifically, inserting the content of the second slot of
E(\cdot,\cdot)
in Eq. (
26
) into the relation
\phi_{\mathrm{G}}=-\omega_{0}t(z)
—we obtain the wave phase in the GRIN layer as

\displaystyle\phi_{\mathrm{G}}=\omega_{0}\frac{1-n_{1}v/c}{v}\int_{z_{\mathrm{b}1}^{0}+vt}^{z}\frac{1}{1-n_{\mathrm{G}}(z^{\prime}-vt)v/c}~\mathrm{d}z^{\prime}

(34)

\displaystyle+\omega_{0}\left(n_{1}\frac{z}{c}-\frac{z-z_{\mathrm{b}1}^{0}}{v}\right).

The related instantaneous frequency at a given position in the GRIN layer is obtained by differentiating the phase in Eq. (
34
) with respect to time, yielding

\displaystyle\omega_{\mathrm{G}}

\displaystyle=-\omega_{0}\frac{1-n_{1}v/c}{v}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left[\int_{z_{\mathrm{b}1}^{0}+vt}^{z}\frac{1}{1-n_{\mathrm{G}}(z^{\prime}-vt)v/c}~\mathrm{d}z^{\prime}\right]

(35)

\displaystyle=\omega_{0}\frac{1-n_{1}v/c}{1-n_{\mathrm{G}}(z-vt)v/c},

where we have used the Leibniz integral rule. Equation (
35
) reveals that the wave frequency in the GRIN layer varies with time, through the function
n_{\mathrm{G}}(z-vt)
in the denominator, indicating a
space-time chirping effect
. This effect is fundamentally different from the group-velocity dispersion (GVD) chirping effect occurring in dispersive media
[
18
]
, since no dispersion is present. It will be explained shortly.

Furthermore, the chirp parameter
\alpha
, whose sign determines whether the field is up-chirping (
\alpha>0
) or down-chirping (
\alpha<0
), is obtained by time-differentiating the instantaneous frequency in Eq. (
35
), which gives

\alpha=\frac{\partial\omega_{\mathrm{G}}}{\partial t}=-\omega_{0}\frac{(1-n_{1}v/c)}{c[1-n_{\mathrm{G}}(z,t)v/c]^{2}}\frac{\partial n_{\mathrm{G}}(z,t)}{\partial z}.

(36)

This result indicates that the sign of the chirp,
\alpha\gtrless{0}
, depends on

(1-n_{1}v/c)\partial n_{\mathrm{G}}(z,t)/\partial z\lessgtr 0.

(37)

According to Eq. (
37
), the up- or down-chirping behavior of the system is governed by the velocity regime, which may be subluminal [
v<c/\max(n_{1},n_{2})
] or superluminal [
v>c/\min(n_{1},n_{2})
]
[
20
]
. To understand the chirping mechanism within the GRIN layer, we now focus on the subluminal case—the superluminal case can be analyzed analogously.
In the subluminal regime, where
v<c/n_{1}
, Eq. (
37
) simplifies to

\partial n_{\mathrm{G}}(z,t)/\partial z\lessgtr 0,

(38)

indicating that a decreasing refractive index slope leads to an increase in the instantaneous frequency, i.e., positive chirping, and vice versa, i.e., negative chirping.

For simplicity, but without loss of generality, we consider the simplest GRIN profile, the linear profile.

n_{\mathrm{G}}(z-vt)=n_{1}+\frac{n_{2}-n_{1}}{D}(z-vt-z_{\mathrm{b}1}^{0}).

(39)

Figure
3
represents the corresponding trajectories for the wave crests incident at
z=0
at the times
t_{\mathrm{i}1,2,3,4,5}
,
as derived from the analytical solutions in the different regions, given by Eqs. (
13
), (
26
) and (

Figure
3
(a) considers the case where the refractive index increases within the GRIN layer, i.e.,
n_{2}>n_{1}
, corresponding to the positive spatial gradient
\partial n_{\mathrm{G}}(z,t)/\partial z>0
, which leads to a down-chirping according to the condition in Eq. (

For a fixed observation position
z_{\mathrm{o}}
within the GRIN region, the arrival times of the five incident crests are denoted as
t_{\mathrm{o}1,2,3,4,5}
. Due to the space-time variation of
n_{\mathrm{G}}
, each crest experiences a different local refractive index at the observation point, as indicated by the color-coded dots in the figure.
The latest crest (purple dot) propagates fastest, as it has just entered the GRIN region and hence encounters the lowest refractive index. In contrast, the earliest crest (red dot) travels slowest, as it is near the exit of the GRIN region and hence sees the highest refractive index. As a result, the later, faster crest gradually catches up with the earlier, slower one, leading to an increasing temporal separation between adjacent crests. This corresponds to a decreasing frequency over time, i.e., down-chirping, consistent with the down-chirping condition in Eq. (
38

A similar mechanism applies in the case of a decreasing refractive index, i.e.,

, where the crests compress in time, resulting in up-chirping [Eq. (
38

\omega_{\mathrm{G}}=f(t).

(40)

To realize such a frequency evolution, one can tailor the refractive index profile of the GRIN layer, by substituting Eq. (
35
) into Eq. (
40
), and solving for
n_{\mathrm{G}}
, yielding

n_{\mathrm{G}}=\frac{c}{v}\left[1-\frac{\omega_{0}(1-n_{1}v/c)}{f(t)}\right],

(41)

which provides a closed-form expression for engineering a GRIN profile generating the desired chirp.
As an example, for a linear chirp, where

f(t)=a+b(z-vt)

(42)

with
a
and
b
being constants, the refractive index profile becomes

n_{\mathrm{G}}=\frac{c}{v}\left[1-\frac{\omega_{0}(1-n_{1}v/c)}{a+b(z-vt)}\right].

(43)

6
Conclusion and Discussion

We have presented an exact electromagnetic solution to the problem of wave propagation across arbitrary space-time modulated GRIN interfaces and a detailed description of the related chirping effects.
This solution offers a novel approach to chirp generation, which can be realized using switched transmission lines
[
22
,
23
]
and other wave modulation techniques
[
24
,
8
,
9
,
10
]
.
Our findings are significant for linear pulse shaping applications and provide a foundation for extending the study to space-time dispersive systems. Additionally, the results hold potential for future applications in dispersion compensation, paving the way for advanced control over wave propagation in dynamic media.

In this appendix, we provide a detailed derivation of Eq. (

Substituting Eq. (

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{G}}(z,t)=

(44)

\displaystyle\int^{\infty}_{-\infty}\delta\left\{t+\frac{1}{v}F^{-1}[z+C_{\mathrm{G}}(t^{\prime})]-\frac{z}{v}\right\}E(0,t^{\prime})~\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}.

To simplify the notation, we define the argument of the delta function in Eq. (

where
g_{\mathrm{G}}(t^{\prime}_{\mathrm{G}})=0

g^{\prime}_{\mathrm{G}}(\cdot)=\mathrm{d}g_{\mathrm{G}}(\cdot)/\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}

t^{\prime}_{\mathrm{G}}
of

t+\frac{1}{v}F^{-1}[z+C_{\mathrm{G}}(t^{\prime}_{\mathrm{G}})]-\frac{z}{v}=0,

(48)

Substituting Eq. (

(49)

Applying Eq. (
20

(50)

Substituting Eq. (
50

49
) yields the final expression

\displaystyle t^{\prime}_{\mathrm{G}}=-\frac{1-n_{1}v/c}{v}\int_{z_{\mathrm{b}1}^{0}+vt}^{z}\frac{1}{1-n_{\mathrm{G}}(z^{\prime}-vt)v/c}~\mathrm{d}z^{\prime}

(51)

\displaystyle-n_{1}\frac{z}{c}+\frac{z-z_{\mathrm{b}1}^{0}}{v}.

Next, we determine the derivative of
g_{\mathrm{G}}(t^{\prime})
[Eq. (

\frac{\mathrm{d}g_{\mathrm{G}}(t^{\prime})}{\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{v}\frac{\mathrm{d}F^{-1}(u)}{\mathrm{d}u}\frac{\mathrm{d}C_{\mathrm{G}}(t^{\prime})}{\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}}.

Substituting Eqs. (
53

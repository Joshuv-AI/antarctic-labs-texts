Basal Metabolic Rate, Genetic Susceptibility, and Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus Events in Adults
Authors: Lei Yuan, Keyi Si, Hongxiang Peng, Jinhai Sun, Zhe Zhao
Published: 2026

Type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) is a major global health issue driven by genetic, environmental, and metabolic factors [1,2]. Basal metabolic rate (BMR), which accounts for a large proportion of daily energy expenditure, has been linked to T2DM risk, with higher BMR potentially increasing metabolic activity and insulin resistance [3,4]. The polygenic risk score for T2DM (PRS-T2DM) also plays a key role in T2DM development [5-8]. However, the combined impact of BMR and PRS-T2DM on T2DM risk has not been thoroughly investigated. This study aimed to investigate the interaction between BMR and PRST2DM in the development of T2DM, thereby providing insights into diabetes prevention.

We used data from the UK Biobank, application number 99709, which was approved by the North West Multi-Centre Research Ethics Committee (R21/NW/0157). Based on our study criteria, 311,029 participants without T2DM at baseline were included. BMR and PRS-T2DM were calculated using previously published methods [3,6]. Participants were classified into three groups according to BMR tertiles: low, medium, and high. The specific definitions of the covariates are provided in our recent study on T2DM [9]. Cox proportional hazards regression analysis was used to calculate hazard ratios (HRs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) for T2DM development across different BMR and PRS-T2DM groups.

The mean±standard deviation of participant age was 56.11± 8.06 years, and 54.19% were female and 45.81% were male. Overall, 96.19% of participants were White, 85.38% lived in urban areas, and 63.90% had a college degree or higher. The mean BMR was 6,614.18±1,349.42 kJ/day. A total of 14,995 participants developed T2DM during follow-up. Fig. 1A illustrates the joint association of BMR and PRS-T2DM with incident T2DM. Among the nine combined categories of BMR and PRS-T2DM, the high PRS-T2DM/high BMR group had the highest risk of T2DM (HR, 7.65; 95% CI, 6.47 to 9.05) compared with the low PRS-T2DM/low BMR group. Similar results were observed in subgroups stratified by sex, age, and body mass index (Fig. 1B-H).

This is the first large cohort study to investigate the individual and joint associations of BMR and PRS-T2DM with T2DM incidence. We observed that higher BMR was significantly associated with an increased risk of T2DM during follow-up among 311,029 participants, and this association was consistent across different genetic risk levels for T2DM. Subgroup analyses supported this conclusion and showed that BMR interacted multiplicatively with PRS-T2DM, thereby amplifying the risk of T2DM.

Previous studies on BMR and T2DM incidence have been limited, with only one Mendelian randomization study providing evidence of an association between BMR and T2DM [10]. Our study extends this research and confirms that higher BMR is associated with an increased risk of T2DM, consistent with previous findings. Importantly, after incorporating PRS-T2DM, we found that high BMR increased the risk of T2DM across different PRS-T2DM groups. Our findings suggest that BMR should be monitored across populations because it has a joint effect with PRS-T2DM that amplifies T2DM risk, a finding that remained evident after adjustment for multiple covariates. Additionally, individuals with higher BMR may have greater energy requirements, which may lead to overeating and obesity. Obesity further increases energy demand and is a major risk factor for T2DM development.

In conclusion, our study confirms the importance of considering BMR in combination with PRS-T2DM in the development of T2DM. Higher BMR was significantly associated with an increased risk of T2DM, and this association was consistent across different genetic risk levels. These findings highlight the potential value of BMR for identifying high-risk individuals and informing screening, early management, and public health interventions aimed at T2DM prevention. However, because 96% of participants were White, the generalizability of our findings, especially those related to PRS-T2DM, may be limited.

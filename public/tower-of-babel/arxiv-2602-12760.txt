Dynamical Localization for General Scattering Quantum Walks
Alain Joye, Andreas Schaefer, Simone Warzel

Abstract
We consider quantum walks defined on arbitrary infinite graphs, parameterized by a family of scattering matrices attached to the vertices. Multiplying each scattering matrix by an i.i.d. random phase, we obtain a random scattering quantum walk. We prove dynamical localization for random scattering walks in a large-disorder regime. The result is based on a relation between fractional moment estimates and eigenfunction correlators of independent interest, which we establish for general random unitary operators.

Eigenfunction correlators for random unitaries

2.1
Definitions and basic properties

The eigenfunction correlators (EC for short) of a unitary operator
U
, which is defined on a separable Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, are simply the total variation measures of the operator’s spectral measure
\mu_{\psi,\varphi}
associated with normalized vectors
\psi,\varphi\in\mathcal{H}
, i.e., for any open
I\subset\mathbb{S}^{1}

Q(\psi,\varphi;I)\equiv|\mu_{\psi,\varphi}|(I)\coloneqq\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}F\in C_{c}(I)\\
\|F\|_{\infty}\leq 1\end{subarray}}\left|\langle\psi|F(U)\varphi\rangle\right|.

(2.1)

This quantity shares most of the properties with its self-adjoint counterpart
[
4
,
6
]
. Since the proofs of these properties are straightforward extensions of the self-adjoint case, we summarize them only briefly:

1.

As is evident from the definition:
Q(\psi,\varphi;I)\leq 1
.

2.

By Lusin’s theorem, the supremum can also be taken over the set of measurable
F
with the property
F=F\ 1_{I}
with
\|F\|_{\infty}\leq 1
and
1_{I}
denoting the indicator function. As a particular case, one thus concludes

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|\langle\psi|U^{n}P_{I}(U)\varphi\rangle\right|\leq Q(\psi,\varphi;I).

(2.2)

The EC thus captures information about the time-discrete dynamics generated by
U
, when restricted to the spectral subspace associated with
I
.

3.

By the RAGE theorem
[
51
]
, if

(\psi_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
stands for any orthonormal basis, then for any normalized
\varphi\in\mathcal{H}
the spectral projection
P^{c}_{I}(U)
of the unitary on its continuous spectrum within
I
is given by

\displaystyle\langle\varphi|P_{I}^{c}(U)\varphi\rangle=\lim_{L\to\infty}\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\sum_{j=L}^{\infty}|\langle\psi_{j}|U^{n}P_{I}(U)\varphi\rangle|^{2}

It is hence straightforward that if
\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}Q(\psi_{j},\varphi;I)^{2}<\infty
for some
\varphi
, the continuous component of the spectral measure associated with that vector vanishes,
\langle\varphi|P_{I}^{c}(U)\varphi\rangle=0
, cf.
[
6
, Thm. 7.2]
.

4.

If a sequence of spectral measures converges weakly, i.e.

\mu^{(n)}_{\psi,\varphi}(F)\to\mu_{\psi,\varphi}(F)\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}F(s)\ d\mu_{\psi,\varphi}(ds)

for all
F\in C(\mathbb{S}^{1})
, then the eigenfunction correlators satisfy

|\mu_{\psi,\varphi}|(I)\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}|\mu_{\psi,\varphi}^{(n)}|(I)

for all open
I\subset\mathbb{S}^{1}
, cf.
[
6
, Prop. 7.6]
.

The naming derives from the special case in which the unitary has a discrete spectral decomposition

U=\sum_{\alpha}\lambda_{\alpha}P_{\alpha},

(2.3)

such that

Q(\psi,\varphi;I)=\sum_{\lambda_{\alpha}\in I}|\langle\psi|P_{\alpha}\varphi\rangle|
.
If (
2.3
) applies, we may also introduce the family of interpolated eigenfunction correlators

Q(\psi,\varphi;I,\beta)\coloneqq\sum_{\lambda_{\alpha}\in I}\langle\psi|P_{\alpha}\psi\rangle^{1-\beta}|\langle\psi|P_{\alpha}\varphi\rangle|^{\beta},

(2.4)

with parameter
\beta\in[0,1]
where
\psi,\varphi\in\mathcal{H}
are normalized.
In the self-adjoint case, these quantities were introduced in
[
4
]
, see also
[
6
]
.
Proofs of the following properties again extend verbatim:

1.

Q(\psi,\varphi;I,0)=Q(\psi,\psi;I)
and
Q(\psi,\varphi;I,1)=Q(\psi,\varphi;I)
.

2.

In its dependence on
\beta
, the interpolated EC is a log-convex function, i.e.,
for any
\lambda\in[0,1]
and
\beta_{\lambda}\coloneqq(1-\lambda)\beta_{0}+\lambda\beta_{1}\in[0,1]
:

Q(\psi,\varphi;I,\beta_{\lambda})\leq Q(\psi,\varphi;I,\beta_{0})^{1-\lambda}Q(\psi,\varphi;I,\beta_{1})^{\lambda}.

Consequently,
0\leq Q(\psi,\varphi;I,\beta)\leq Q(\psi,\varphi;I,\alpha)^{\frac{1-\beta}{1-\alpha}}
for any
0\leq\alpha\leq\beta\leq 1
.

3.

For any
\beta\in[0,1]
:

Q(\psi,\varphi;I)\leq\sqrt{Q(\psi,\varphi;I,\beta)Q(\varphi,\psi;I,\beta)},

(2.5)

see
[
6
, Eq. (7.44)]
.

In particular, an upper bound on the interpolated eigenfunction correlators at one
\beta\in(0,1]
implies a bound on any
\beta\in(0,1]
.

Eigenfunction correlators are a useful tool in harvesting dynamical localization properties for self-adjoint random operators
[
47
,
7
,
4
,
6
]
.
To connect to the localization analysis of random operators, we, however, need additional structure.

2.2
Consistent families of random unitaries on subgraphs

A rather general framework for random unitaries, which covers random Scattering Quantum Walks (SQW) as a particular case, is described in terms of
graphs
(V,E)
with vertex set
V
and countable edge set
E
. We associate random variables to the edges,
\omega:E\to\mathbb{R},e\mapsto\omega_{e}
. They form the canonical probability space
(\mathbb{R}^{E},\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{E}),\mathbb{P})
.
The unitaries will be defined on the Hilbert space

\ell^{2}(E)\coloneqq\left\{\psi:E\to\mathbb{C}\,\bigg|\,\sum_{e\in E}|\psi(e)|^{2}<\infty\right\}.

(2.6)

Subsequently, we identify edges
e\in E
with the associated canonical orthonormal basis
|e\rangle
in this Hilbert space.
Denoting by
\mathcal{U}(E)
the set of unitary operators on this Hilbert space, weakly measurable maps

U:\mathbb{R}^{E}\to\mathcal{U}(E),\,\omega\mapsto U_{\omega}

(2.7)

will be referred to as random unitaries.

In order to be able to address finite-volume approximations, which are unitary as well, the following notion turns out to be useful.

Definition 1
.

We speak of a
consistent family
\mathcal{F}
of subsets
F\subset E
for random unitaries on the Hilbert space
\ell^{2}(E)
of a graph
(V,E)
with underlying probability space
(\mathbb{R}^{E},\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{E}),\mathbb{P})
iff
(i) for any
F\in\mathcal{F}
also its complement
F^{c}=E\setminus F
is in
\mathcal{F}
and
(ii) there is a random unitary

U^{F}:\mathbb{R}^{E}\to\mathcal{U}(F),\ \omega\mapsto U_{\omega}^{F},

(2.8)

which is weakly measurable with respect to the sub-sigma algebra
\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{F})
.

Remark 1
.

The required measurability expresses the fact that
U^{F}_{\omega}
only depends on the projection of
\omega
onto
\mathbb{R}^{F}
.
Given a random unitary (
2.7
), the straightforward choice
U_{\omega}^{F}=P^{F}U_{\omega}P^{F}
with
P^{F}
the orthogonal projection onto the subspace
\ell^{2}(F)
will generally not be unitary. This is one of the notable differences with the case of random self-adjoint operators for which consistent, self-adjoint Dirichlet restrictions exist for any subset
F
. Random SQW naturally lead to consistent families of subsets as will be explained in Section
3.2
.

For any consistent family
\mathcal{F}
of subsets for random unitaries, we have a family of random eigenfunction correlators,
Q_{\omega}^{F}
with
F\in\mathcal{F}
associated with
U_{\omega}^{F}
and normalized vectors in
\ell^{2}(F)
.

In the above set-up, any
disjoint decomposition of the edge set,
E=F\uplus F^{c}
for
F\in\mathcal{F}
, comes with the direct sum of unitaries,
U_{\omega}^{F}\oplus U_{\omega}^{F^{c}}
, whose ’decoupled’ action goes along with the
orthogonal decomposition of the Hilbert space
\ell^{2}(E)=\ell^{2}(F)\oplus\ell^{2}(F^{c})
. The boundary operator of
F
within
E
:

T_{\omega}^{E,F}\coloneqq U_{\omega}^{E}-U_{\omega}^{F}\oplus U_{\omega}^{F^{c}}

(2.9)

compares the decoupled unitaries to the unitary
U_{\omega}^{E}\equiv U_{\omega}
on the whole graph.

For any finite subset
F\in\mathcal{F}
the random unitary
U^{F}
defines a finite-volume approximation of
U
.
In this context, it is natural to inquire about the nature of the convergence of such approximations for exhaustive sequences of finite subsets. This is addressed in the following

Proposition 1
.

Given a sequence of finite subsets
E_{L}\in\mathcal{F}
in a consistent family
\mathcal{F}
for random unitaries on the Hilbert space over a graph
(V,E)
such that the boundary operators converge strongly, i.e., for all
e\in E
and all
\omega\in\mathbb{R}^{E}

\lim_{L\to\infty}\|T_{\omega}^{E,E_{L}}e\|=0.

(2.10)

Then for all
e,f\in E
and all
\omega\in\mathbb{R}^{E}
:

1.

the associated spectral measures converge weakly, i.e.,
(\mu^{E_{L}}_{\omega})_{e,f}(F)\to(\mu^{E}_{\omega})_{e,f}(F)
for all
F\in C(\mathbb{S}^{1})
.

2.

the EC is lower semicontinuous, i.e., for any open
I\subset\mathbb{S}^{1}

Q_{\omega}^{E}(e,f;I)\leq\liminf_{L\to\infty}Q_{\omega}^{E_{L}}(e,f;I).

(2.11)

Proof.

The proof of the first item is based on Lemma
1
. The second item follows from item
4
in Section
2.1
.
∎

Lemma 1
.

If
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|(U_{n}-U)\varphi\|=0
for all
\varphi\in\mathcal{H}
, then
\lim_{n\rightarrow}\mu_{n}(F)=\mu(F)
for all
F\in C(\mathbb{S}^{1})
, where
d\mu_{n}
, respectively
d\mu
, are the spectral measures of
U_{n}
, respectively
U
associated with an arbitrary vector.

Proof.

By functional calculus and the Stone-Weierstrass theorem, we can approximate any
F\in C(\mathbb{S}^{1})
by a polynomial in the
L^{\infty}
norm, so that it is enough to consider monomials. The identity

\displaystyle U_{n}^{k}-U^{k}=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}U_{n}^{j}(U_{n}-U)U^{k-j-1}

(2.12)

valid for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
, yields

\displaystyle\|(U_{n}^{k}-U^{k})\varphi\|\leq\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\|U_{n}^{j}(U_{n}-U)U^{k-j-1}\varphi\|=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\|(U_{n}-U)(U^{k-j-1}\varphi)\|,

(2.13)

where each summand goes to zero by assumption.
∎

Remark 2
.

As will be explained in Section
3.2
, for random SQW
(
2.10
) applies in case
E_{L}
are sequences of balls and the action of boundary operators are restricted to the vicinity of the surface of these balls.

2.3
Relation to fractional moments

The lower semicontinuity (
2.11
) implies that an upper bound on the EC of a random unitary on a potentially infinite graph
(V,E)
is given in terms of finite-volume approximations.
In the following, we may hence restrict our attention on graphs with a finite edge set
E
. In this case, any unitary has a discrete spectrum (
2.3
), and we may hence work with the interpolated ECs given by (
2.4
). The following is our first main result.

Theorem 1
.

Consider a consistent family
\mathcal{F}
of subsets for random unitaries on a graph
(V,E)
with a finite number of edges
E
. Let
E\neq B\in\mathcal{F}
,

f\in B
, and
e\in B^{c}
and assume:

1. Fractional moments:

for some
s\in(0,1)
and
C_{s}<\infty
and all
f^{\prime}\in B
:

\sup_{|z|<1}\mathbb{E}\left[|\langle f|(U^{B}-z)^{-1}f^{\prime}\rangle|^{s}\right]\leq C_{s}.

(2.14)

2. Spectral averaging:

for some

C_{W}<\infty
almost surely

\sup_{|z|<1}\mathbb{E}\Big[\langle e\,|{\rm Re}\left((U+z)(U-z)^{-1}\right)\,e\rangle\,\Big|\,\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{B})\Big]\leq C_{W},

(2.15)

where the expectation conditions on the sigma algebra associated with the edge set
B
.

Then for any
\beta\in(0,s)
with
\beta\leq 1-\beta/s
and all open
I\in\mathbb{S}^{1}

\mathbb{E}\left[Q(e,f;I,\beta)\right]\leq C_{W}^{\frac{\beta}{s}}\sum_{f^{\prime}\in B,e^{\prime}\in E}t_{f^{\prime}e^{\prime}}^{\beta}\sup_{\delta\in(0,1)}\left\{\int_{I}\mathbb{E}\left[|\langle f|(U^{B}-\delta e^{i\theta})^{-1}f^{\prime}\rangle|^{s}\right]\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}\right\}^{\frac{\beta}{s}},

(2.16)

where
t_{ef}:=\sup_{\omega}|\langle e|T_{\omega}^{E,B}f\rangle|
.

In a fractional-moment (FM) localization analysis
[
3
]
, the theorem is applied to cases where
E
is the given finite-volume subset on which the analysis is done. The subset
B
is chosen as a ball centered near

with radius half the distance

, see Figure

is

and as a distance

between two edges

we choose the maximal graph distance between their endpoints. The transition matrix elements

are a worst-case estimate of coupling over the separating surface between
B
and its complement within

. In this set-up, the summation in (

and

on the boundary of

. In case the FM in the RHS of (
2.16

. The application to random SQW is explained in detail in Section
4.5

We now take the expectation of the LHS. We then apply Jensen’s inequality
as well as Fatou’s and Tonelli’s lemmas to get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[Q(e,f;I,\beta)\big]\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f^{\prime}\in B,\,e^{\prime}\in E\end{subarray}}\big(t_{f^{\prime}e^{\prime}}^{\beta}+(1-\delta)^{\beta}\delta_{e^{\prime}f^{\prime}}\big)\,

(2.28)

\displaystyle\phantom{x}\times\liminf_{r\to 1^{-}}\Bigg(\int_{I}\mathbb{E}\Big(\,\langle e\,|\text{Re}\left((U+re^{i\theta})(U-re^{i\theta})^{-1}\right)\,e\rangle|\langle f\,|\big(U^{B}-\delta e^{i\theta}\big)^{-1}\,f^{\prime}\rangle|^{s}\Big)\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}\Bigg)^{\frac{\beta}{s}}.

By assumption the random variable
\langle f\,|\big(U^{B}-\delta e^{i\theta}\big)^{-1}\,f^{\prime}\rangle
with
f,f^{\prime}\in B
only depends on the values of
\omega
projected to
B
. Taking the conditional expectation, when conditioning on the latter first, we may use spectral averaging to bound

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big(\,\langle e\,|\text{Re}\left((U+re^{i\theta})(U-re^{i\theta})^{-1}\right)\,e\rangle|\langle f\,|\big(U^{B}-\delta e^{i\theta}\big)^{-1}\,f^{\prime}\rangle|^{s}\Big)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Big(\mathbb{E}\big[\langle e\,|\text{Re}\left((U+re^{i\theta})(U-re^{i\theta})^{-1}\right)\,e\rangle|\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{B})\big]\;|\langle f\,|\big(U^{B}-\delta e^{i\theta}\big)^{-1}\,f^{\prime}\rangle|^{s}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq C_{W}\ \mathbb{E}\Big(|\langle f\,|\big(U^{B}-\delta e^{i\theta}\big)^{-1}\,f^{\prime}\rangle|^{s}\Big).

(2.29)

Using the finiteness of the fractional moment, we therefore arrive at

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}

\displaystyle\big[Q(e,f;I,\beta)\big]

\displaystyle\leq C_{W}^{\frac{\beta}{s}}\ \Bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f^{\prime}\in B,\,e^{\prime}\in E\end{subarray}}|t_{f^{\prime}e^{\prime}}|^{\beta}\sup_{\delta\in(0,1)}\Big\{\mathbb{E}\Big[|\langle f\,|\big(U^{B}-\delta e^{i\theta}\big)^{-1}\,f^{\prime}\rangle|^{s}\Big\}^{\frac{\beta}{s}}+C_{s}^{\frac{\beta}{s}}(1-\delta)^{\beta}|B|\Bigg).

(2.30)

Since the LHS is independent of
\delta
, it remains to take the limit
\delta\to 1^{-}
to make the last term of the RHS vanish, which yields the result.
∎

3.2
Random scattering quantum walks

Applications of Theorem
1
to random quantum walks, require enough randomness ensuring the finiteness of fractional moments and spectral averaging. For random SQWs, a rather limited amount of randomness is sufficient.

Random SQWs are characterized by a deterministic set of scattering matrices
{\mathcal{S}}=(S(x))_{x\in V}
and a probability measure
\mu
on the torus
\mathbb{T}
, which we assume to be absolutely continuous with bounded density:

\displaystyle d\mu(\theta)=\tau(\theta)d\theta\ \text{with}\ \tau\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}).

(3.3)

We equip every
x\in V
with a random variable
\omega_{x}\in\mathbb{T}
, such that the family
(\omega_{x})_{x\in V}
is independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) according to
\mu
. We then define the random scattering matrices

\displaystyle S_{\omega}(x)=e^{i\omega_{x}}S(x),\ \ \forall x\in V.

(3.4)

and set the corresponding random operator on
\ell^{2}(D)
describing the random SQW as

\displaystyle U_{\omega}\coloneqq U_{{\mathcal{S}_{\omega}}}.

(3.5)

By construction, this operator is a unitary of the form
U_{\omega}=D_{\omega}U_{\mathcal{S}}

with random diagonal unitary operators acting as

\displaystyle D_{\omega}\ket{xy}=e^{i\omega_{y}}\ket{xy}.

(3.6)

This structure is instrumental in the analysis of (de-)localization of random unitary operators, and it is inherited from the construction of the Chalker-Coddington model of condensed matter physics; see the discussions in
[
26
,
46
,
10
,
38
,
11
]
.

Any random SQW (
3.5
) with arbitrary underlying deterministic scattering matrices
{\mathcal{S}}
fits into the set-up defined in Section
2.2
. To do so, one identifies
(V,E)=(V,D)
and realizes the randomness on the measure space
(\mathbb{R}^{D},\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{D}))
associated with the edge set instead of the vertex set. This is achieved by setting

\omega_{xy}\coloneqq\omega_{y}\quad\mbox{for all $x$ s.t.\ $(xy)\in D$.}

(3.7)

This induces a probability measure, which we again denote by
\mathbb{P}
, on the measure space over the oriented edge set.

A consistent family
\mathcal{F}
of subsets for the random SQW, which fits the requirements of Definition
1
, consists of all subsets
F\subset D
, which have the property that with an oriented edge
(xy)\in F
the reversely oriented edge also belongs to the set.
The unitary
U^{F}
from (
2.8
) is then defined as follows. Given
F\in\mathcal{F}
, its edge boundary of vertices and the set of inner vertices is

\displaystyle V_{\partial F}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\{x\in V\ \mbox{s.t.}\ \exists y,z\in V:\;(xy)\in F\ \mbox{and}\ \ (zx)\notin F\}

\displaystyle V_{F}^{\circ}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\{x\in V\ \mbox{s.t.}\ \forall y\sim x:\;(xy)\in F\}.

To define
U^{F}
we modify the scattering matrix on the edge boundary of
F
by setting

S^{F}(x)\coloneqq\begin{cases}S(x)&x\in V_{F}^{\circ},\\
\mathbbm{1}_{d_{x}^{F}}&x\in V_{\partial F}.\end{cases}

(3.8)

Here, the identity matrix in the last case has dimension
d_{x}^{F}\coloneqq|\{y\in V\ \mbox{s.t.}\ (yx)\in F\}|<d_{x}
.
This family of deterministic unitary scattering matrices
{\mathcal{S}}^{F}=(S^{F}(x))_{x\in V_{\partial F}\cup V_{F}^{\circ}}
, which implement ”reflecting boundary conditions” on
V_{\partial F}
, then defines the unitary operator

U^{F}_{\omega}\coloneqq D_{\omega}U_{{\mathcal{S}}^{F}},

(3.9)

which acts on
\ell^{2}(F)
and satisfies all the requirements from (
2.8
).

To control the boundary operator induced by such boundary conditions,
T^{D,F}_{\omega}
, see (
2.9
), one may use
[
41
, Lemma 2.4]
. It guarantees that,
given two sets of scattering matrices,
\mathcal{S}=\big(S(x)\big)_{x\in V}
and
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}=\big(S^{\prime}(x)\big)_{x\in V}
, together with the corresponding quantum walks
U_{\mathcal{S}}
and
U_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}
, the natural uniform topology on those can be expressed in terms of the underlying scattering matrices,

\displaystyle\|U_{\mathcal{S}}-U_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}\|=\sup_{x\in V}\|S(x)-S^{\prime}(x)\|_{HS}=:\|\mathcal{S}-\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\|,

(3.10)

where
\|\cdot\|_{HS}
denotes the Hilbert-Schmidt norm of a matrix. We will use this in case one of those two sets is the identity on all vertices,
\mathcal{I}\coloneqq\big(S(x)\big)_{x\in V}
with
S(x)=\mathbbm{1}
for all
x\in V
.
In this case, the corresponding random SQW
\tilde{U}_{\omega}
satisfies

\displaystyle\tilde{U}_{\omega}\ket{xy}=e^{i\omega_{x}}\ket{yx}

(3.11)

for all
\ket{xy}\in\mathcal{H}
. It is hence trivially fully localized for any randomness.
Less trivial and
as an application of Theorem
1
, we show dynamical localization whenever the family of scattering matrices
\mathcal{S}
is close enough to the fully localized case
\mathcal{I}
:

Theorem 2
.

Let
U
be a random SQW on a digraph
(V,D)
with uniformly bounded degree as in (
3.1
) and random phases that are i.i.d. with bounded density as in (
3.3
). Then, for any
g>0
there exist
\varphi,c>0
such that for any family of scattering matrices with
\|\mathcal{S}-\mathcal{I}\|\leq\varphi
, we have for all
\ket{xy},\ket{x'y'}\in\mathcal{H}
and
I\subset S^{1}

\mathbb{E}\Big(\sup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|\langle xy\,|U^{n}\,P_{I}(U)\,x^{\prime}y^{\prime}\rangle|\Big)\leq c\,e^{-g\,d(x,x^{\prime})}.

(3.12)

We prove Theorem
2
using Theorem
1
: Sections
3.3
and
3.4
are devoted to proving the fractional moments and spectral averaging conditions, while we obtain exponential decay of the fractional moments in section
4
. We conclude in Section
4.5
, proving dynamical localization for random SQWs as stated in Theorem
2
.

Remark 3
.

We have equipped every edge
(xy)
with a random phase
\omega_{y}
that depends only on the outgoing vertex
y
, see (
3.7
). If we allow the random phase
\omega_{xy}
of
(xy)
to also depend on the incoming vertex
x
, we would not need the new results developed in Section
2
. Instead, exponential decay of the fractional moments immediately implies dynamical localization using the rank-one analysis in
[
36
]
.

3.3
Fractional moment bounds

To demonstrate the utility of Theorem
1
, we prove the boundedness of the FM and subsequently a related spectral averaging result in the above set-up of random SQW with any distribution satisfying (
3.3
).
We formulate both results for the random SQW on an arbitrary digraph. In applications, this digraph may already be a subgraph of a bigger graph.
The proof of the following bound traces the steps of
[
53
, Thm. 2]
, which in turn is based on
[
36
, Thm. 3.1]
.

Theorem 3
.

Given a random SQW
U
on a digraph
(V,D)
with uniformly bounded degree and
\mu
satisfying (
3.3
),
for every
s\in(0,1)
there exists
C(s)<\infty
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{|z|\neq 1}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}|\langle yx\,|(U_{\omega}-z)^{-1}y^{\prime}x^{\prime}\rangle|^{s}\;d\mu(\omega_{x})\,d\mu(\omega_{x^{\prime}})\leq C(s)

(3.13)

for all
(yx),(y^{\prime}x^{\prime})\in D
and arbitrary values of all other random variables
\omega_{v}
,
v\notin\{x,x^{\prime}\}
.

Proof.

The bound (
3.13
) is trivial when
z
is away from the unit circle:

\displaystyle|\langle yx\,|(U_{\omega}-z)^{-1}\,y^{\prime}x^{\prime}\rangle|\leq\|(U_{\omega}-z)^{-1}\|=\frac{1}{\text{dist}(z,\sigma(U_{\omega}))}\leq\frac{1}{|1-|z|\,|}.

We may thus assume without loss of generality, that
1/2\leq|z|\leq 3/2
and
|z|\neq 1
. Furthermore, for
z\neq 0
it holds that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2z}\,\Big((U_{\omega}+z)(U_{\omega}-z)^{-1}-1\Big)=(U_{\omega}-z)^{-1}.

(3.14)

To prove (
3.13
) it thus suffices to show the similar bound on
(U_{\omega}+z)(U_{\omega}-z)^{-1}
:

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}|\langle yx\,|(U_{\omega}+z)\,(U_{\omega}-z)^{-1}\,y^{\prime}x^{\prime}\rangle|^{s}\;d\mu(\omega_{x})\,d\mu(\omega_{x^{\prime}})\leq C(s).

(3.15)

We assume for now that
x\neq x^{\prime}
, while the simpler case
x=x^{\prime}
will be dealt with at the end. Following the steps of
[
53
]
and
[
36
]
, we introduce the change of variables

\displaystyle\alpha=\frac{1}{2}\big(\omega_{x}+\omega_{x^{\prime}}\big)\;\;\;\text{and}\;\;\;\beta=\frac{1}{2}\big(\omega_{x}-\omega_{x^{\prime}}\big),

and the unitary operators:

\displaystyle D_{\alpha}\ket{vu}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}e^{i\alpha}\,\ket{vu}&\text{if }u\in\{x,x^{\prime}\}\\
\ket{vu}&\text{else}\end{cases},\;\;D_{\beta}\ket{vu}=\begin{cases}e^{i\beta}\,\ket{vu}&\text{if }u=x\\
e^{-i\beta}\,\ket{vu}&\text{if }u=x^{\prime}\\
\ket{vu}&\text{else}\end{cases}

\displaystyle\widehat{D}_{\omega}\ket{vu}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\ket{vu}&\text{if }u\in\{x,x^{\prime}\}\\
e^{i\omega_{u}}\,\ket{vu}&\text{else}\end{cases}.

By definition, we have
D_{\omega}=D_{\alpha}\,D_{\beta}\,\widehat{D}_{\omega}
. We define the unitary operator
V_{\omega}=D_{\beta}\,\widehat{D}\,U_{\mathcal{S}}
, and denote by
P
the projection onto the subspace

\displaystyle\text{Ran}(P)=\text{span}\left\{V_{\omega}^{-1}\ket{vx},V_{\omega}^{-1}\ket{v'x'},\,v\sim x,v^{\prime}\sim x^{\prime}\right\}.

(3.16)

We easily verify that
(U_{\omega}-V_{\omega})\,(1-P)=0
and
V_{\omega}^{-1}\,U_{\omega}P=e^{i\alpha}P
and therefore conclude

\displaystyle U_{\omega}=U_{\omega}\,P+U_{\omega}\,(1-P)=e^{i\alpha}\,V_{\omega}\,P+V_{\omega}\,(1-P).

(3.17)

For
|z|\neq 1
, we define the operators

\displaystyle F_{z}=P(U_{\omega}+z)(U_{\omega}-z)^{-1}P\text{ and }\widehat{F}_{z}=P(V_{\omega}+z)(V_{\omega}-z)^{-1}P,

(3.18)

viewing them as operators on the range of
P
. Using that
[V_{\omega}+z,(V_{\omega}-z)^{-1}]=0
and
\big((V_{\omega}-z)^{-1}\big)^{*}=(V_{\omega}^{*}-\overline{z})^{-1}
, we compute

\displaystyle\begin{split}\widehat{F}_{z}+\widehat{F}_{z}^{*}=P\Big(2\big(I-|z|^{2}\big)(V_{\omega}-z)^{-1}\,\big((V_{\omega}-z)^{-1}\big)^{*}\Big)P.\end{split}

(3.19)

Assuming that
|z|>1
, we conclude that
\widehat{F}_{z}+\widehat{F}_{z}^{*}<0
is invertible. Thus,
-i\widehat{F}_{z}
is a dissipative operator if
|z|>1
, where we recall that an operator
A
is dissipative iff its imaginary part
\frac{1}{2i}(A-A^{*})
is positive. Using
\widehat{F}_{z}^{-1}=\widehat{F}_{-z}
, we conclude that
-i\widehat{F}_{z}^{-1}
is also dissipative for
|z|>1
. Similarly, we obtain that
i\widehat{F}_{z}
and
i\widehat{F}_{z}^{-1}
are dissipative for
|z|<1
. Employing the resolvent identity and (
3.17
), we compute

\displaystyle F_{z}-\widehat{F}_{z}=P\Big(2z(V_{\omega}-z)^{-1}(e^{i\alpha}-1)V_{\omega}\,P\,(U_{\omega}-z)^{-1}\Big)P.

We use the definitions of
F_{z}
and
\widehat{F}_{z}
to obtain on the range of
P
:

\displaystyle F_{z}-\widehat{F}_{z}=\frac{1}{2}\,(e^{i\alpha}-1)\,(1+\widehat{F}_{z})\,(1-F_{z}).

Following the steps from
[
36
]
, we define
m(\alpha)=i\frac{1+e^{i\alpha}}{1-e^{i\alpha}}=-\cot\big(\frac{\alpha}{2}\big)
for
\alpha\notin\pi\mathbb{Z}
and obtain from a direct computation:

\displaystyle F_{z}=-i\Big(-i\widehat{F}_{z}+m(\alpha)\Big)^{-1}-i\Big(-i\widehat{F}_{z}^{-1}-\frac{1}{m(\alpha)}\Big)^{-1}.

(3.20)

Using
V_{\omega}^{-1}\,U_{\omega}P=e^{i\alpha}P
and unitarity of
V_{\omega}
, we obtain:

\displaystyle\langle yx\,|\,(U_{\omega}+z)\,(U_{\omega}-z)^{-1}\,y^{\prime}x^{\prime}\rangle=\langle V_{\omega}^{*}\,yx\,|\,F_{z}V_{\omega}^{*}\,y^{\prime}x^{\prime}\rangle.

This allows us to estimate the integral in (
3.15
):

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}|\langle yx\,|\,(U_{\omega}+z)\,(U_{\omega}-z)^{-1}y^{\prime}x^{\prime}\rangle|^{s}\;d\mu(\omega_{x})\,d\mu(\omega_{x^{\prime}})\leq\|\tau\|_{\infty}^{2}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\|F_{z}\|^{s}\;d\alpha\,d\beta.

We stress that while
F_{z}
depends on
\alpha
,
\widehat{F}_{z}
does not. Note that for
s<1
, we have
|a+b|^{s}\leq|a|^{s}+|b|^{s}
for all
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
. Plugging equation (
3.20
) into the integral above, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}\|F_{z}\|^{s}\;d\alpha\leq\int_{0}^{2\pi}\|\big(-i\widehat{F}_{z}+m(\alpha)\big)^{-1}\|^{s}\;d\alpha+\int_{0}^{2\pi}\|\Big(-i\widehat{F}_{z}^{-1}-\frac{1}{m(\alpha)}\Big)^{-1}\|^{s}\;d\alpha.

(3.21)

Using that
\widehat{F}_{z}
does not depend on
\alpha
, we substitute
t=m(\alpha)
to bound the first integral on the RHS of (
3.21
):

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}\|\big(-i\widehat{F}_{z}+m(\alpha)\big)^{-1}\|^{s}\;d\alpha

\displaystyle=\lim_{R\to\infty}\int_{-R}^{R}\frac{2}{1+t^{2}}\,\|\big(-i\widehat{F}_{z}+t\big)^{-1}\|^{s}\;dt

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{1+(|n|-1)^{2}}\,\int_{n}^{n+1}\|(-i\widehat{F}_{z}+t)^{-1}\|^{s}\,dt.

A similar expression holds for the second term in (
3.21
). The operators
-i\widehat{F}_{z}
and
-i\widehat{F}^{-1}_{z}
are defined on
\text{Ran}(P)
, which has dimension
d_{x}+d_{x^{\prime}}
and are thus maximally dissipative. Reproducing the arguments from the end of Section 4.1 in
[
53
]
, we get that there exists a constant
C(s)
such that

\displaystyle\int_{n}^{n+1}\|(A+t)^{-1}\|^{s}\,dt\leq C(s)

for all maximally dissipative
A
, where
C(s)
is independent of
n
, but depends on the dimension of
\text{Ran}(P)
. Since such a constant exists for all finite dimensions, and the maximal dimension is
2d
, we can take the maximum over all such constants. Using that
\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{1+(|n|-1)^{2}}<\infty
, we can therefore bound the RHS of (
3.21
) for all
|z|>1
. If
|z|<1
, we drop a minus sign in both norms in (
3.21
) and use that
i\widehat{F}_{z}
and
i\widehat{F}_{z}^{-1}
are dissipative. Repeating the arguments from above yields the bound for all
|z|<1
.

The case
x=x^{\prime}
is significantly easier: We can directly take
\alpha=\omega_{x}
and do not need
\beta
. The operators
F_{z}
and
\widehat{F}_{z}
act on a space of dimension
d_{x}
, while all other estimates still hold.
∎

3.4
Spectral averaging

For random SQW, spectral-averaging over a single random variable at vertex
x
ensures the bound required in Theorem
1
. We again only require the distribution to be absolutely continuous with bounded density (
3.3
). Thanks to (
3.15
), the subsequent bound (
3.22
) would hold trivially if the integrand were raised to some power
s\in(0,1)
, see Theorem
3
and (
3.15
).

Theorem 4
.

Given a random SQW
U_{\omega}
on a digraph
(V,D)
with uniformly bounded degree and
\mu
satisfying (
3.3
),
there is some
C<\infty
such that for all
(x^{\prime}x)\in D

\displaystyle\sup_{|z|<1}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\langle x^{\prime}x\,|\text{Re}\left((U_{\omega}+z)(U_{\omega}-z)^{-1}\right)\,x^{\prime}x\rangle\,d\mu(\omega_{x})\leq C,

(3.22)

at arbitrary values of all other random variables
\omega_{v}
,
v\notin\{x\}
.

Proof.

The proof is based on similar arguments as the diagonal case in Theorem
3
. To illuminate the similarity, we abbreviate
\alpha=\omega_{x}\in[0,2\pi]
for fixed
x\in V
. We write the random operator
U_{\omega}=D_{\omega}U_{\mathcal{S}}
and define

D_{\alpha}\ket{vu}=\begin{cases}e^{i\alpha}\,\ket{vu}&\text{if }u\in\{x\}\\
\ket{vu}&\text{else}\end{cases},\qquad\widehat{D}_{\omega}\ket{vu}=\begin{cases}\ket{vu}&\text{if }u\in\{x\}\\
e^{i\omega_{u}}\,\ket{vu}&\text{else}\end{cases}.

Setting
V_{\omega}=\widehat{D}_{\omega}
, we have
U_{\omega}=D_{\alpha}V_{\omega}
. We again consider the orthogonal projection
P
corresponding to
\text{Ran}(P)=\text{span}\left\{V_{\omega}^{-1}\ket{vx},\,v\sim x\right\}
,
and the operators,

\displaystyle F_{z}=P(U_{\omega}+z)(U_{\omega}-z)^{-1}P,\qquad\widehat{F}_{z}

\displaystyle=P(V_{\omega}+z)(V_{\omega}-z)^{-1}P.

(3.23)

We stress that
\widehat{F}_{z}
is independent of
\alpha
.
For these operators, the relation (
3.19
) is valid, which implies that

\widehat{F}_{z}+\widehat{F}_{z}^{*}>0
for any
|z|<1
. By the same argument as in the proof of Theorem
3
, this implies that
\widehat{F}_{z}
and
\widehat{F}_{z}^{*}
are invertible on the range of
P
.
Moreover, we also have

\displaystyle 0\leq\langle x^{\prime}x\,|\text{Re}\left((U+z)(U-z)^{-1}\right)\,x^{\prime}x\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\,\langle x^{\prime}x\,|\,\big(F_{z}+F_{z}^{*}\big)\,x^{\prime}x\rangle.

(3.24)

The operators
F_{z}
and
\widehat{F}_{z}
are again related by (
3.20
)
with
m(\alpha)=-\cot(\frac{\alpha}{2})
such that

\displaystyle F_{z}+F_{z}^{*}

\displaystyle=\Big(\widehat{F}_{z}+im(\alpha)\Big)^{-1}+\Big(\widehat{F}_{z}^{-1}-im(\alpha)^{-1}\Big)^{-1}+\Big(\widehat{F}_{z}^{*}-im(\alpha)\Big)^{-1}+\Big((\widehat{F}_{z}^{*})^{-1}+im(\alpha)^{-1}\Big)^{-1}

(3.25)

By the assumption (
3.3
) on the distribution, the claim (
3.22
) thus follows from the bounds

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\langle x^{\prime}x\,|\,\Big(\widehat{F}_{z}+im(\alpha)\Big)^{-1}+\Big(\widehat{F}_{z}^{*}-im(\alpha)\Big)^{-1}\,x^{\prime}x\rangle\,d\alpha\leq C\\
\int_{0}^{2\pi}\langle x^{\prime}x\,|\,\Big(\widehat{F}_{z}^{-1}-im(\alpha)^{-1}\Big)^{-1}+\Big((\widehat{F}_{z}^{*})^{-1}+im(\alpha)^{-1}\Big)^{-1}\,x^{\prime}x\rangle\,d\alpha\leq C\end{split}

(3.26)

that we now establish.
To simplify the notation, we will write
\widehat{F}_{z}=\widehat{F}
and
m(\alpha)=m
, and we stress that from now on, we consider the restriction of all operators to the finite-dimensional range of
P
, without systematically mentioning it explicitly in the notation.
Since the operator
\widehat{F}
restricted to the range of
P
is dissipative, we have
H=\frac{1}{2}(\widehat{F}+\widehat{F}^{*})=H^{*}>0
and

\displaystyle r=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\varphi\in\text{Ran}(P)\\
\|\varphi\|=1\end{subarray}}\langle\varphi\,|H\varphi\rangle=\min\big(\lambda\in\sigma(H)\big)>0.

(3.27)

We now pick
\varphi\in\text{Ran}(P)
arbitrary and compute for
y(t)=e^{-t\widehat{F}}\varphi
the derivative

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|y(t)\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\Big\langle\varphi\,|\,\frac{d}{dt}\Big(e^{-t\widehat{F}^{*}}\,e^{-t\widehat{F}}\Big)\,\varphi\Big\rangle=\langle\varphi\,|\,-e^{-t\widehat{F}^{*}}(\widehat{F}^{*}+\widehat{F})e^{-t\widehat{F}}\,\varphi\rangle

\displaystyle=-2\,\langle y(t)\,|H\,y(t)\rangle\leq-2\,r\,\|y(t)\|^{2}.

(3.28)

Gronwall’s inequality then yields

\|y(t)\|^{2}\leq\|\varphi\|^{2}e^{-2rt}
,
which proves the uniform bound

\|e^{-t\widehat{F}}\|\leq e^{-tr}\quad\mbox{on $\text{Ran}(P)$.}

(3.29)

We can therefore compute

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t(\widehat{F}+im)}dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{d}{dt}\Big(-(\widehat{F}+im)^{-1}\,e^{-t(\widehat{F}+im)}\Big)\,dt

(3.30)

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\to\infty}\Big(-(\widehat{F}+im)^{-1}\,e^{-t(\widehat{F}+im)}\Big)+(\widehat{F}+im)^{-1}=(\widehat{F}+im)^{-1}

and similarly

\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t(\widehat{F}^{*}-im)}dt=(\widehat{F}^{*}-im)^{-1}
.
Restoring the dependence in
\alpha
in the notation, and using Fubini’s theorem we thus conclude

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}\langle x^{\prime}x\,|\,\big(\widehat{F}+im(\alpha)\big)^{-1}\,x^{\prime}x\rangle\,d\alpha=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\langle x^{\prime}x\,|\,\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t(\widehat{F}+im(\alpha))}dt\Big)\,x^{\prime}x\rangle\,d\alpha

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\langle x^{\prime}x\,|\,e^{-t(\widehat{F}+im(\alpha))}\,x^{\prime}x\rangle\,dt\,d\alpha=\int_{0}^{\infty}\langle x^{\prime}x\,|\,e^{-t\widehat{F}}\,\Big(\int_{0}^{2\pi}e^{-itm(\alpha)}\,d\alpha\Big)\,x^{\prime}x\rangle\,dt.

The substitution
y=m(\alpha)=-\cot(\frac{\alpha}{2})
with
d\alpha=\frac{2}{1+y^{2}}dy
yields for the inner integral

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}e^{-itm(\alpha)}\,d\alpha=2\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-tiy}\frac{1}{1+y^{2}}\,dy=2\pi\,e^{-|t|}.

The final equality can be checked with the residue theorem. We substitute this into the equation above and obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}\langle x^{\prime}x\,|\,\big(\widehat{F}+im(\alpha)\big)^{-1}\,x^{\prime}x\rangle\,d\alpha=2\pi\,\int_{0}^{\infty}\langle x^{\prime}x\,|\,e^{-t(\widehat{F}+1)}\,x^{\prime}x\rangle\,dt

\displaystyle=2\pi\langle x^{\prime}x\,|\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t(\widehat{F}+1)}\,dt\Big)x^{\prime}x\rangle=2\pi\,\langle x^{\prime}x\,|\,(\widehat{F}+1)^{-1}\,x^{\prime}x\rangle,

by using the argument above in the evaluation of the last integral. Taking the absolute value, we can bound the scalar product by
\|(\widehat{F}+1)^{-1}\|
. Due to
\widehat{F}+\widehat{F}^{*}>0
we compute for every
\lambda>0
and
0\neq\varphi\in\text{Ran}(P)

\displaystyle\|(\lambda+\widehat{F})\varphi\|^{2}=\lambda^{2}\|\varphi\|^{2}+\|\widehat{F}\varphi\|^{2}+\lambda\langle\varphi\,|(\widehat{F}+\widehat{F}^{*})\varphi\rangle>\lambda^{2}\|\varphi\|^{2}.

We see that
\|(\lambda+\widehat{F})\varphi\|>\lambda\|\varphi\|
and conclude that
\lambda+\widehat{F}
is invertible on
\text{Ran}(P)
with

\displaystyle\|(\lambda+\widehat{F})^{-1}\varphi\|<\frac{1}{\lambda}\,\|(\lambda+\widehat{F})(\lambda+\widehat{F})^{-1}\varphi\|=\frac{1}{\lambda}\,\|\varphi\|.

In particular, we have
\|(\widehat{F}+1)^{-1}\|\leq 1
. In summary, we have shown

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{0}^{2\pi}\langle x^{\prime}x\,|\,\big(\widehat{{F}}_{z}+im(\alpha)\big)^{-1}\,x^{\prime}x\rangle\,d\alpha\Big|\leq 2\pi.

Copying the arguments above, we obtain the same bound with
\widehat{{F}}_{z}
replaced by
\widehat{{F}}_{z}^{*}
.
Using
\widehat{F}_{z}^{-1}+(\widehat{F}_{z}^{-1})^{*}=\widehat{F}_{z}^{-1}\big(\widehat{F}_{z}+\widehat{F}_{z}^{*}\big)(\widehat{F}_{z}^{-1})^{*}
, we conclude
\widehat{F}_{z}^{-1}+(\widehat{F}_{z}^{-1})^{*}>0
. Since
\widehat{F}_{z}+\widehat{F}_{z}^{*}>0
was the only requirement on
\widehat{F}_{z}
, we can repeat the arguments above and obtain similar bounds for
\widehat{F}_{z}^{-1}
and
(\widehat{F}_{z}^{-1})^{*}
. This shows the bounds (
3.26
) and therefore (
3.22
), which finishes the proof.
∎

4
Localization proof

In order to obtain dynamical localization for random SQWs, it remains to show exponential decay of the fractional moments. Thanks to Theorem
1
, this yields exponential decay of the eigenfunction correlator, which proves dynamical localization by (
2.2
). We show the following

Theorem 5
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
2
, for any
s\in(0,\frac{1}{3})
and any
g>0
, there exist
\varphi,c>0
such that for any collection of scattering matrices
(S(x))_{x\in V}
with
\|\mathcal{S}-\mathcal{I}\|\leq\varphi
we have

\displaystyle\sup_{|z|\in[1/2,3/2]\setminus\{1\}}\mathbb{E}\left(|\langle xy|(U-z)^{-1}|x^{\prime}y^{\prime}\rangle|^{s}\right)\leq ce^{-g\operatorname{dist}(x,x^{\prime})},

(4.1)

for any two basis vectors
\ket{xy},\ket{x'y'}\in\ell^{2}(D)
.

4.1
Subspaces associated to balls

Given a root
r\in V
and a radius
n
, we will work with a geometric resolvent equation, which imposes reflecting boundary conditions on the surface of the ball
B_{n}(r)\subset V
. The quantum walker is hence trapped inside or outside this ball. For practical purposes, we apply the ideas presented in Section
3.2
to subsets of vertices instead of subsets of edges. Given a root
r\in V
, which we frequently suppress in the notation, we denote by
\operatorname{dist}(x)=d(x,r)
the graph distance between
x
and
r
and define

\displaystyle B_{n}\equiv B_{n}(r)=\{x\in V\,|\operatorname{dist}(x)\leq n\}\text{ and }S_{n}(r)=\{x\in V\,|\operatorname{dist}(x)=n\}.

Due to assumption (
3.1
), we can bound the size of
S_{n}(r)
by

\displaystyle s_{n}=\sup_{r\in V}\big(|S_{n}(r)|\big)\leq d\,(d-1)^{n-1}=c_{d}\,e^{\gamma n}\text{ with }\gamma=\ln(d-1).

(4.2)

For a given family of scattering matrices
(S(x))_{x\in V}
, we define a new family of deterministic scattering matrices by:

\displaystyle S^{(n)}(x)=\begin{cases}\mathbbm{1}&\text{if }x\in S_{n}(r)\\
S(x)&\text{else.}\end{cases}

Let
U_{\omega}^{(n)}
denote the random SQW corresponding to the scattering matrices
(S^{(n)}(x))_{x\in V}
. Recalling (
3.11
), we see that the walker incoming at
x\in S_{n}(r)
from any site
y
, gets reflected from
x
back to
y
. We define the subspaces

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{n}=\text{span}\left\{\ket{xy}\text{ s.t. }\operatorname{dist}(x)\leq n,\operatorname{dist}(y)\leq n,\,x\sim y,\,(x,y)\in V^{2}\right\}

and
\mathcal{H}_{n}^{\perp}
its orthogonal complement in
\mathcal{H}
. Thanks to (
3.11
), the spaces
\mathcal{H}_{n}
and
\mathcal{H}_{n}^{\perp}
are invariant under
U_{\omega}^{(n)}
, and we let
U_{\omega}^{B_{n}}
and
U_{\omega}^{B_{n}^{c}}
denote the restrictions of
U_{\omega}^{(n)}
to
\mathcal{H}_{n}
and
\mathcal{H}_{n}^{\perp}
respectively, so that

\displaystyle U_{\omega}^{(n)}=U_{\omega}^{B_{n}}\oplus U_{\omega}^{B_{n}^{c}}\text{ on }\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{n}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{n}^{\perp}.

(4.3)

We denote the boundary operator that couples the two subspaces by

\displaystyle T^{(n)}=U_{\omega}-U_{\omega}^{(n)}.

Using (
3.10
), we obtain

\|T^{(n)}\|\leq\sup_{x\in S_{n}(r)}\big(\|S(x)-I\|_{HS}\big).

4.2
Spectral gaps of the fully localized random operator

Let
\tilde{U}_{\omega}
denote the random SQW corresponding to the family of scattering matrices
\mathcal{I}
as defined above (
3.11
) and note that it reflects any directed edge
(xy)
onto the edge
(yx)
in the opposite direction. Given two adjacent vertices
u\sim v
, we define the 2-dimensional subspace

\mathcal{H}^{uv}=\text{span}\big\{\ket{uv},\ket{vu}\big\}
.
The subspaces
\mathcal{H}^{uv}
are invariant under
\tilde{U}_{\omega}
and its restriction
\tilde{U}_{\omega}^{uv}
in the ONB
\{\ket{uv},\ket{vu}\}
of
\mathcal{H}^{uv}
reads:

\displaystyle\tilde{U}_{\omega}^{uv}=\tilde{U}_{\omega}\Big|_{\mathcal{H}^{uv}}=\begin{pmatrix}0&e^{i\omega_{u}}\\
e^{i\omega_{v}}&0\end{pmatrix}.

(4.4)

We see that its eigenvalues are given by
\pm e^{i\frac{\omega_{u}+\omega_{v}}{2}}
. Our next goal is the following Proposition, which estimates the probability of spectral gaps for the fully localized operator:

Proposition 2
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
2
, we have for any
n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, any
0<\eta<1
and any root
r\in V
:

\displaystyle\sup_{|z|\neq 1}\mathbb{P}\Big(\text{dist}\big(z,\sigma\left(\tilde{U}_{\omega}^{B_{n}(r)}\right)\big)\leq\eta\Big)\leq 4\pi^{2}\|\tau\|_{\infty}^{2}\,d|B_{n}(r)|\,\eta.

Proof.

Since
\tilde{U}_{\omega}^{B_{n}(r)}=\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}u,v\in B_{n}(r)\\
u\sim v\end{subarray}}\tilde{U}^{uv}_{\omega}
, with
\sigma(\tilde{U}^{uv}_{\omega})=\{\pm e^{i\frac{\omega_{u}+\omega_{v}}{2}}\}
, see (
4.4
), we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Big(\text{dist}\big(z,\sigma\left(\tilde{U}_{\omega}^{B_{n}(r)}\right)\big)\leq\eta\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u,v\in B_{n}(r)\\
u\sim v\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}\Big(\text{dist}\big(z,\sigma\left(\tilde{U}_{\omega}^{uv}\right)\big)\leq\eta\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u,v\in B_{n}\\
u\sim v\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}(|z-e^{i\frac{\omega_{u}+\omega_{v}}{2}}|\leq\eta)+\mathbb{P}(|z+e^{i\frac{\omega_{u}+\omega_{v}}{2}}|\leq\eta).

(4.5)

In turn, we estimate

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Big(|z\pm e^{i\frac{\omega_{u}+\omega_{v}}{2}}|\leq\eta\Big)\leq\|\tau\|_{\infty}^{2}\,\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mathbb{I}_{\{|z\pm e^{i\frac{\theta+\theta^{\prime}}{2}}|\leq\eta\}}d\theta\,d\theta^{\prime},

(4.6)

and then change variables
\alpha=\frac{\theta+\theta^{\prime}}{2}
,
\beta=\theta-\theta^{\prime}
. The bound

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mathbb{I}_{\{|z\pm e^{i\alpha}|\leq\eta\}}d\alpha\leq 2\arcsin(\eta)\leq\pi\,\eta

completes the proof.
∎

4.3
Finite-volume resolvent estimate

We denote by
R_{\omega}^{\#}(z)
and
\tilde{R}_{\omega}^{\#}(z)
the (reduced) resolvents of
U_{\omega}^{\#}
and
\tilde{U}_{\omega}^{\#}
, for
\#\in\{(n),B_{n},B_{n}^{c}\}
, for any
z
in their resolvent sets. Unless it is unclear, we will omit the dependence on
z
and
\omega
. Furthermore, we define the following equivalence relation on the elements of the ONB
\{\ket{uv}\}_{u,v\in V,u\sim v}
of
\mathcal{H}
:

\displaystyle\ket{uv}\sim\ket{u'v'}\iff\ket{uv}=\ket{u'v'}\text{ or }\ket{uv}=\ket{v'u'}.

Recalling (
3.10
), our goal is the following

Proposition 3
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
2
, we have for any
s\in(0,1)
and
p>\frac{1}{1-s}
that there exists
c>0
(depending on
d,s,p
) such that:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big(\left|\langle xy\,|\,R_{\omega}^{B_{n}}(z)\,x^{\prime}y^{\prime}\rangle\right|^{s}\Big)\leq c\left(\|\mathcal{S}-\mathcal{I}\||B_{n}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{s}{1+2sp}},

uniformly in
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{S}^{1}
, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, all balls
B_{n}\equiv B_{n}(r)
for any root
r\in V
, all families of scattering matrices
\mathcal{S}=(S(x))_{x\in V}
such that
\|\mathcal{S}-\mathcal{I}\|<|B_{n}|^{-2}d^{-\frac{1+2ps}{sp}}
, and all
\ket{xy},\ket{x'y'}\in\mathcal{H}_{n}
with
\ket{xy}\nsim\ket{x'y'}
.

Proof.

In the following, the generic symbol
c
denotes an inessential quantity that only depends on
d,s,p
and can change from line to line. Quantities with a tilde refer to the SQW with the identity as scattering matrices. Since
\mathcal{H}^{uv}
is invariant under
\tilde{U}_{\omega}^{B_{n}}
, we have

\displaystyle\langle uv\,|\,\tilde{R}_{\omega}^{B_{n}}\,u^{\prime}v^{\prime}\rangle=0\;\;\text{ if }\;\ket{uv}\nsim\ket{u'v'}.

(4.7)

The resolvent identity implies

\displaystyle R_{\omega}^{B_{n}}=\tilde{R}_{\omega}^{B_{n}}+R_{\omega}^{B_{n}}\Big(\tilde{U}_{\omega}^{B_{n}}-U_{\omega}^{B_{n}}\Big)\tilde{R}_{\omega}^{B_{n}}.

(4.8)

By definition of the scattering matrices, we have:

\displaystyle\langle uv\,|\,U_{\omega}^{B_{n}}\,u^{\prime}v^{\prime}\rangle=0\;\;\text{ if }\;v\neq u^{\prime}.

(4.9)

Equations (
4.7
) - (
4.9
) now yield for
\ket{xy},\ket{x'y'}\in\mathcal{H}_{n}
with
\ket{xy}\nsim\ket{x'y'}
and
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{S}^{1}
:

\displaystyle\big|\langle xy\,|\,R_{\omega}^{B_{n}}\,x^{\prime}y^{\prime}\rangle\big|

\displaystyle=\big|\langle xy\,|\,R_{\omega}^{B_{n}}\Big(\tilde{U}_{\omega}^{B_{n}}-U_{\omega}^{B_{n}}\Big)\tilde{R}_{\omega}^{B_{n}}\,x^{\prime}y^{\prime}\rangle\big|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ket{uv},\ket{vv'}\in\mathcal{H}_{n}\\
\text{s.t }\ket{vv'}\sim\ket{x'y'}\end{subarray}}\big|\langle xy\,|\,R_{\omega}^{B_{n}}\,uv\rangle\,\langle uv\,|\,\Big(\tilde{U}_{\omega}^{B_{n}}-U_{\omega}^{B_{n}}\Big)\,vv^{\prime}\rangle\,\langle vv^{\prime}\,|\,\tilde{R}_{\omega}^{B_{n}}\,x^{\prime}y^{\prime}\rangle\big|.

Using (
3.10
) we can bound the second scalar product by
\|\mathcal{S}-\mathcal{I}\|
. Since
U_{\omega}^{B_{n}}
is a normal operator, we can bound the third scalar product by
\frac{1}{\text{dist}\big(z,\sigma(\tilde{U}_{\omega}^{B_{n}})\big)}
. Finally, we note that the number of terms in the sum above is finite and independent of
n
. Thus, there exists a constant
c>0
such that

\displaystyle\big|\langle xy\,|\,R_{\omega}^{B_{n}}\,x^{\prime}y^{\prime}\rangle\big|

\displaystyle\leq c\,\frac{\|\mathcal{S}-\mathcal{I}\|}{\text{dist}\big(z,\sigma(\tilde{U}_{\omega}^{B_{n}})\big)}\,\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ket{uv}\in\mathcal{H}_{n}\text{ s.t}\\
v=x^{\prime}\text{ or }v=y^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\big|\langle xy\,|\,R_{\omega}^{B_{n}}\,uv\rangle\big|.

(4.10)

For any
\eta>0
, we define the set of ”good” events:

\displaystyle G_{\eta}(z)=\left\{\omega\in\mathbb{T}^{V}\text{ s.t. }\text{dist}\Big(z,\sigma\big(\tilde{U}_{\omega}^{B_{n}}\big)\Big)>\eta\right\}.

By Proposition
2
, we have
\mathbb{P}\left(G_{\eta}(z)^{c}\right)\leq c\,\eta\,|B_{n}|
for all
0<\eta<1
. We take
p>1/(1-s)
as in the statement of Proposition
3
and
1<q<1/s
such that
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
and apply Hölder’s inequality and Theorem
3
:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbb{E}&\left(\chi_{G_{\eta}(z)^{c}}\,\left|\langle xy\,|\,R^{B_{n}}\,x^{\prime}y^{\prime}\rangle\right|^{s}\right)\leq\mathbb{P}\left(G_{\eta}(z)^{c}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\,\mathbb{E}\left(\left|\langle xy\,|\,R^{B_{n}}\,x^{\prime}y^{\prime}\rangle\right|^{sq}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq c\big(\eta\,|B_{n}|\big)^{\frac{1}{p}}.\end{split}

(4.11)

Note that Theorem
3
requires
sq<1
, which is equivalent to
p>\frac{1}{1-s}
.
By perturbation theory for normal operators, if
\|\mathcal{S}-\mathcal{I}\|\leq\frac{\eta}{2}
, then

\displaystyle\text{dist}\Big(z,\sigma\big(\tilde{U}_{\omega}^{B_{n}}\big)\Big)>\eta\Longrightarrow\text{dist}\Big(z,\sigma\big(U_{\omega}^{B_{n}}\big)\Big)>\frac{\eta}{2}.

(4.12)

If
\omega\in G_{\eta}(z)
, we have
\text{dist}\big(z,\sigma(\tilde{U}_{\omega}^{B_{n}})\big)>\eta
and therefore by (
4.12
) and (
4.10
):

\displaystyle\chi_{G_{\eta}(z)}(\omega)\,\left|\langle xy\,|\,R_{\omega}^{B_{n}}\,x^{\prime}y^{\prime}\rangle\right|^{s}\leq\chi_{G_{\eta}(z)}(\omega)\,(2c)^{s}\,\frac{\|\mathcal{S}-\mathcal{I}\|^{s}}{\eta^{2s}}.

(4.13)

The estimates (
4.11
) and (
4.13
) thus yield

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}

\displaystyle\left(\left|\langle xy\,|\,R_{\omega}^{B_{n}}\,x^{\prime}y^{\prime}\rangle\right|^{s}\right)\leq c\Big(\big(\eta\,|B_{n}|\big)^{\frac{1}{p}}+\frac{\|\mathcal{S}-\mathcal{I}\|^{s}}{\eta^{2s}}\Big).

(4.14)

At this point we balance the two contributions to get an expression for
\eta

\displaystyle\big(\eta\,|B_{n}|\big)^{\frac{1}{p}}=\bigg(\frac{\|\mathcal{S}-\mathcal{I}\|}{\eta^{2}}\bigg)^{s}\ \Rightarrow\ \eta=\bigg(\frac{\|\mathcal{S}-\mathcal{I}\|^{sp}}{|B_{n}|}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{1+2sp}}.

(4.15)

In case

\|\mathcal{S}-\mathcal{I}\|\leq\eta/2
, this yields in turn the sought for estimate

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big(\left|\langle xy\,|\,R_{\omega}^{B_{n}}(z)\,x^{\prime}y^{\prime}\rangle\right|^{s}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq c\left(\|\mathcal{S}-\mathcal{I}\||B_{n}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{s}{1+2sp}}.

(4.16)

Using
|B_{n}|\geq 1
,
d\geq 2
, one finally checks that the assumption

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{S}-\mathcal{I}\||B_{n}|^{2}d^{\frac{1+2ps}{sp}}<1

(4.17)

ensures that the previous condition holds, which ends the proof.
∎

4.4
The iterative step

The goal of this section is the following

Proposition 4
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
2
, we have for all
0<q<1
, all
s\in\left(0,\frac{1}{3}\right)
that there exist
N_{0}\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
and
\varphi:\mathbb{N}^{*}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+}
, (given in (
4.26
) below), both depending on
(q,s,d)
, such that
n\geq N_{0}
and
\|\mathcal{S}-\mathcal{I}\|\leq\varphi(n)
imply

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\left|\langle xy\,|\,R(z)\,x^{\prime}y^{\prime}\rangle\right|^{s}\right)\leq q\,\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ket{uv}\in\mathcal{H}\\
\operatorname{dist}(v,x)\in\{n,n+1,n+2\}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{E}\left(\left|\langle uv\,|\,R(z)\,x^{\prime}y^{\prime}\rangle\right|^{s}\right)

(4.18)

for all
z\in\mathbb{C}
with
|z|\in[1/2,3/2]\setminus\{1\}
,
\ket{xy}\in\mathcal{H}
and
\ket{x'y'}\in\mathcal{H}_{n+3}^{\perp}
, where the ball underlying the definition of
\mathcal{H}_{n+3}
is centered at
x
.

Proof.

We use the resolvent identity twice, once to decouple over the boundary of
B_{n}\equiv B_{n}(x)
and then over that of
B_{n+1}\equiv B_{n+1}(x)
. Omitting the dependencies on
z
and
\omega
in the respective resolvents,
this yields

\displaystyle\langle xy\,|\,R\,x^{\prime}y^{\prime}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle xy\,|\,R^{(n)}\,x^{\prime}y^{\prime}\rangle-\langle xy\,|\,R^{(n)}T^{(n)}R^{(n+1)}\,x^{\prime}y^{\prime}\rangle+\langle xy\,|\,R^{(n)}T^{(n)}RT^{(n+1)}R^{(n+1)}\,x^{\prime}y^{\prime}\rangle.

The first of the three terms is zero, since
\ket{xy}\in\mathcal{H}_{n}
and
\ket{x'y'}\in\mathcal{H}_{n}^{\perp}
and both subspaces are invariant under
R^{(n)}
. Similarly, since
\ket{x'y'}\in\mathcal{H}_{n+3}^{\perp}
and using that
T^{(n)}
has range
1
, we conclude that the second scalar product is also zero. Inserting the identity operator between each of the remaining terms, we conclude:

\displaystyle\langle xy\,|\,R\,x^{\prime}y^{\prime}\rangle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ket{uv},\ket{u'v'},\\
\ket{wt},\ket{w't'}\in\mathcal{H}\end{subarray}}\langle xy\,|\,R^{(n)}\,uv\rangle\,\langle uv\,|\,T^{(n)}\,u^{\prime}v^{\prime}\rangle\,\langle u^{\prime}v^{\prime}\,|\,R\,wt\rangle\,\times\quad

\displaystyle\langle wt\,|\,T^{(n+1)}\,w^{\prime}t^{\prime}\rangle\,\langle w^{\prime}t^{\prime}\,|\,R^{(n+1)}\,x^{\prime}y^{\prime}\rangle

.

(4.19)

We now introduce the abbreviation

\displaystyle\partial B_{n}\coloneqq\big\{\big(\ket{uv},\ket{u'v'}\big)\in\mathcal{H}\times\mathcal{H}\,\text{ s.t. }\langle uv|\,T^{(n)}\,u^{\prime}v^{\prime}\rangle\neq 0\big\},

(4.20)

and stress that this set does not coincide with boundary
V_{\partial F}
for a consistent set
F
defined in Section
3.2
, since
\partial B_{n}
is a subset of directed edges, while
V_{\partial F}
is a subset of vertices. Since
x\in V
is fixed in this proof, for any
v\in V
, we abbreviate by
\operatorname{dist}(v)
the graph distance
d(v,x)
. Note that if
\big(\ket{uv},\ket{u'v'}\big)\in\partial B_{n}
, then
u^{\prime}=v
and
\operatorname{dist}(v)=n
.
We now bound the moduli of terms in (
4.4
) containing the transition operators
T^{(n)}
and
T^{(n+1)}
by 2,
and obtain, using
0<s<1
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}\big(\big|\langle xy\,|\,R\,x^{\prime}y^{\prime}\rangle\big|^{s}\big)\\
&\leq c\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(\ket{uv},\ket{u'v'})\in\partial B_{n},\\
(\ket{wt},\ket{w't'})\in\partial B_{n+1},\\

4.5
Exponential decay

Repeated applications of Proposition

Proof.

We take

,
q
from Proposition
4
and set

. The LHS of (

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\left|\langle xy\,|\,(U-z)^{-1}\,x^{\prime}y^{\prime}\rangle\right|^{s}\right)\leq q\,\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ket{uv}\in\mathcal{H}\\
\operatorname{dist}(v,x)\in\{n,n+1,n+2\}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{E}\left(\left|\langle uv\,|\,(U-z)^{-1}\,x^{\prime}y^{\prime}\rangle\right|^{s}\right).

We define

\displaystyle r(x)\coloneqq\max_{y\sim x}\mathbb{E}\left(\left|\langle xy\,|\,(U-z)^{-1}\,x^{\prime}y^{\prime}\rangle\right|^{s}\right)

(4.28)

and use the fact that for

\displaystyle A_{n}(x)=\{v\in V\ |\ \operatorname{dist}(v,x)\in\{n-1,\dots,n+3\}\},\ \text{ with }\ |A_{n}(x)|\leq cs_{n}.

We are now ready to prove Theorem

:

Proof of Theorem
2
.

Given
x\in V
and

, we define for

\displaystyle E_{L}=\{(uv)\in D\,|\,d(x,u)\leq L,d(x,v)\leq L\}.

This is an element of the consistent family
\mathcal{F}=\{F\subset D\,|\,(uv)\in F\iff(vu)\in F\}
, see Section
3.2
. Since

, we have

\lim_{L\to\infty}\|T_{\omega}^{D,E_{L}}\ket{uv}\|=0
for any fixed edge
\ket{uv}\in\mathcal{H}
.
By Proposition
1
, we bound the eigenfunction correlator
Q_{\omega}^{D}
on the set of all directed edges
D
by its analogue
Q_{\omega}^{E_{L}}
on the finite set
E_{L}
, see (
2.11
). We choose
L
big enough such that
(xy),(x^{\prime}y^{\prime})\in E_{L}
, and consider from now on the random SQW
U_{\omega}^{E_{L}}
.

Since the LHS in (
3.12
) is bounded by one, we may assume without loss of generality that
xy
and
x^{\prime}y^{\prime}
, when considered as two vertex pairs, have a graph distance greater or equal to
2r\geq 4
with some
r<L/2
. This
r
can be chosen such that
d(x,x^{\prime})\leq 2(r+1)
and that
(xy)\in E_{r}
and
(x^{\prime}y^{\prime})\in E_{r}^{c}
. We then apply Theorem
1
with
B=E_{r}\subsetneq E_{L}
. The fractional moment and spectral averaging conditions (
2.14
) and (
2.15
) are satisfied due to Theorems
3
and
4
. The latter uses that
(xy)
and
(x^{\prime}y^{\prime})
are far enough apart such that
\omega_{x^{\prime}}
is independent of the randomness in

. Theorem
1
thus implies that for any
s\in(0,1/3)
,
g>0
and

with

and all open

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}

\displaystyle\left[Q^{E_{L}}((x^{\prime}y^{\prime}),(xy);I,\beta)\right]

\displaystyle\leq C_{W}^{\frac{\beta}{s}}\sum_{(uv)\in B,(u^{\prime}v^{\prime})\in E}t_{(uv),(u^{\prime}v^{\prime})}^{\beta}\sup_{\delta\in(0,1)}\left\{\int_{I}\mathbb{E}\left[|\langle xy|(U^{B}-\delta e^{i\theta})^{-1}uv\rangle|^{s}\right]\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}\right\}^{\frac{\beta}{s}}.

(4.33)

A.1
First re-sampling argument

We derive equation (

), following the steps of

. We let

denote a constant, which is independent of

A.1.1
Splitting terms

We fix
(\ket{uv},\ket{u'v'})\in\partial B_{n},(\ket{wt},\ket{w't'})\in\partial B_{n+1}
such that
\ket{uv}\in\mathcal{H}_{n}
and
\ket{w't'}\in\mathcal{H}_{n+1}^{\perp}
and define

.

For later use, we note that

, so
\operatorname{dist}(u)\leq n
and
\operatorname{dist}(v)\leq n
. Additionally, due to
\langle uv|\,T^{(n)}\,u^{\prime}v^{\prime}\rangle\neq 0

u^{\prime}=v
,

\ket{w't'}\in\mathcal{H}_{n+1}^{\perp}

\operatorname{dist}(t^{\prime})=n+2

n\leq\operatorname{dist}(w)\leq n+2

For all sites

we choose i.i.d. random variables
(\widehat{\omega}_{r})_{r\in J}
that are uniformly distributed on
\mathbb{T}
and independent from
(\omega_{r})_{r\in V}
. With
P_{r}^{\rm O}
the projection onto the outgoing subspace at

(see Section
3.1
), we define

\displaystyle D_{\omega,\widehat{\omega}}=\sum_{r\in J}e^{i\widehat{\omega}_{r}}\,P_{r}^{\rm O}+\sum_{r\notin J}e^{i\omega_{r}}\,P_{r}^{\rm O}.

D_{\omega,\widehat{\omega}}

\widehat{\omega}_{r}

\displaystyle U_{\omega,\widehat{\omega}}^{(n)}=D_{\omega,\widehat{\omega}}\,U^{(n)}\;\;\text{and}\;\;\widehat{R}^{(n)}=\big(U_{\omega,\widehat{\omega}}^{(n)}-z\big)^{-1}.

To simplify the notation we define

\displaystyle R^{(n)}

\displaystyle=\widehat{R}^{(n)}-\widehat{R}^{(n)}\widehat{D}\,U^{(n)}R^{(n)}\;\;\text{ and }\;\;R^{(n+1)}=\widehat{R}^{(n+1)}-R^{(n+1)}\widehat{D}\,U^{(n+1)}\widehat{R}^{(n+1)}.

\displaystyle A_{1}

and use Hölder’s inequality twice:

\displaystyle\begin{split}A_{4}&\leq\widehat{\mathbb{E}}\bigg(\mathbb{E}\bigg(\mathbb{E}\Big(\big|\langle xy\,|\,\widehat{R}^{(n)}\widehat{D}\,U^{(n)}R^{(n)}\,uv\rangle\big|^{3s}\Big|J^{c}\Big)^{\frac{1}{3}}\,\mathbb{E}\Big(\big|\langle u^{\prime}v^{\prime}\,|\,R\,wt\rangle\big|^{3s}\Big|J^{c}\Big)^{\frac{1}{3}}\\
&\hphantom{{}\leq\widehat{\mathbb{E}}\bigg(\mathbb{E}\bigg(}\mathbb{E}\Big(\big|\langle w^{\prime}t^{\prime}\,|\,R^{(n+1)}\widehat{D}\,U^{(n+1)}\widehat{R}^{(n+1)}\,x^{\prime}y^{\prime}\rangle\big|^{3s}\Big|J^{c}\Big)^{\frac{1}{3}}\bigg)\bigg).\end{split}

(A.4)

Due to
s<\frac{1}{3}
, we can bound the second expectation in (
A.4
) by Theorem
3
. We continue with the first term. Using the definition of
\widehat{D}
and that the number of terms in that definition is bounded independently of
n
, we get:

\displaystyle\big|

\displaystyle\langle xy\,|\,\widehat{R}^{(n)}\widehat{D}\,U^{(n)}R^{(n)}\,uv\rangle\big|^{3s}{\leq}\sum_{r\in J}\sum_{p\sim r}\big|e^{i\omega_{r}}-e^{i\widehat{\omega}_{r}}\big|^{3s}\,\big|\langle xy\,|\,\widehat{R}^{(n)}pr\rangle\big|^{3s}\,\big|\langle pr\,|\,U^{(n)}R^{(n)}\,uv\rangle\big|^{3s}.

Note that
\ket{xy}\in\mathcal{H}_{n}
, which is invariant under
\widehat{R}^{(n)}
. Thus,
\ket{pr}
is an element of
\mathcal{H}_{n}
as well and we obtain

\displaystyle\big|

\displaystyle\langle xy|\,\widehat{R}^{(n)}\widehat{D}\,U^{(n)}R^{(n)}uv\rangle\big|^{3s}\leq c\sum_{r\in J}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\sim r,\\
\ket{pr}\in\mathcal{H}_{n}\end{subarray}}\big|\langle xy\,|\,\widehat{R}^{(n)}pr\rangle|^{3s}\,|\langle pr\,|\,U^{(n)}R^{(n)}uv\rangle\big|^{3s}.

We remark that
\widehat{R}^{(n)}
depends only on
(\widehat{\omega}_{r})_{r\in J}
and
(\omega_{r})_{r\notin J}
. Since the expectation
\mathbb{E}
is with respect to
(\omega_{r})_{r\in V}
, the first scalar product is measurable with respect to the
\sigma
-algebra generated by
(\omega_{r})_{r\notin J}
. Therefore:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbb{E}&\Big(\big|\langle xy\,|\,\widehat{R}^{(n)}\widehat{D}\,U^{(n)}R^{(n)}\,uv\rangle\big|^{3s}\Big|J^{c}\Big)\leq c\sum_{r\in J}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\sim r,\\
\ket{pr}\in\mathcal{H}_{n}\end{subarray}}\big|\langle xy\,|\,\widehat{R}^{(n)}pr\rangle\big|^{3s}\,\mathbb{E}\Big(\big|\langle pr\,|\,U^{(n)}R^{(n)}\,uv\rangle\big|^{3s}\,\Big|\,J^{c}\Big).\end{split}

(A.5)

Since
D_{\omega}
is unitary, we have:

\displaystyle\big|\langle pr\,|\,U^{(n)}R^{(n)}\,uv\rangle\big|=\big|\langle e^{i\omega_{r}}\,pr\,|\,I+z\,R^{(n)}\,uv\rangle\big|.

Using this, Theorem
3
and
1/2<|z|<3/2
, we can bound the expectation in (
A.5
), since
r
and
v
belong to
J
, and obtain for the first term in (
A.4
):

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}

\displaystyle\Big(\big|\langle xy\,|\,\widehat{R}^{(n)}\widehat{D}\,U^{(n)}R^{(n)}\,uv\rangle\big|^{3s}\Big|J^{c}\Big)\leq c\sum_{r\in J}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\sim r,\\
\ket{pr}\in\mathcal{H}_{n}\end{subarray}}\big|\langle xy\,|\,\widehat{R}^{(n)}\,pr\rangle\big|^{3s}.

We can obtain a similar estimate for the third term in (
A.4
): As above, we use the definition of
\widehat{D}
, apply the expectation, use that
\widehat{R}^{(n+1)}
is measurable with respect to
(\omega_{r})_{r\notin J}
and apply Theorem
3
to obtain for
t^{\prime}
and
r
in
J
:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big(\big|\langle w^{\prime}t^{\prime}|R^{(n+1)}\widehat{D}U^{(n+1)}\widehat{R}^{(n+1)}x^{\prime}y^{\prime}\rangle\big|^{3s}\Big|J^{c}\Big)\leq c\sum_{r\in J}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\sim r,\\
\ket{pr}\in\mathcal{H}_{n+1}^{c}\end{subarray}}\big|\langle pr|U^{(n+1)}\widehat{R}^{(n+1)}x^{\prime}y^{\prime}\rangle\big|^{3s}.

Due to our assumptions,
\ket{x'y'}\in\mathcal{H}_{n+3}^{\perp}
so that
(pr)\neq(x^{\prime}y^{\prime})
. Indeed, by definition
r\in J=\{v,v^{\prime},t,t^{\prime}\}
and
\ket{pr}\in\mathcal{H}_{n+1}^{\perp}
. We conclude from the discussion at the top of Section
A.1
that
r\neq v
,
r=t^{\prime}
implies
\operatorname{dist}(r)=n+2
and
r\in\{v^{\prime},t\}
implies
\operatorname{dist}(r)=n+1
and
\operatorname{dist}(p)=n+2
.
This yields
\operatorname{dist}(r)\in\{n+1,n+2\}
while
\ket{x'y'}\in\mathcal{H}_{n+3}^{\perp}
implies
\operatorname{dist}(y^{\prime})\geq n+3
and thus
(pr)\neq(x^{\prime}y^{\prime})
.

Since
D_{\omega,\widehat{\omega}}
is unitary, it follows:

\displaystyle\big|\langle pr|U^{(n+1)}\widehat{R}^{(n+1)}x^{\prime}y^{\prime}\rangle\big|

\displaystyle=\big|\langle pr|I+z\,\widehat{R}^{(n+1)}x^{\prime}y^{\prime}\rangle\big|=|z|\,\big|\langle pr|\widehat{R}^{(n+1)}x^{\prime}y^{\prime}\rangle\big|.

Using
1/2<|z|<3/2
, this yields for the third term in (
A.4
):

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big(\big|\langle w^{\prime}t^{\prime}\,|\,R^{(n+1)}\widehat{D}U^{(n+1)}\widehat{R}^{(n+1)}x^{\prime}y^{\prime}\rangle\big|^{3s}\Big|J^{c}\Big)\leq c\sum_{r\in J}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\sim r,\\
\ket{pr}\in\mathcal{H}_{n+1}^{\perp}\end{subarray}}\big|\langle pr|\widehat{R}^{(n+1)}x^{\prime}y^{\prime}\rangle\big|^{3s}.

Since
J
has a fixed finite number of elements and
3s<1
, we have
\big(\sum_{j\in J}\alpha_{j}^{3s}\big)^{\frac{1}{3}}\leq c\sum_{j\in J}\alpha_{j}^{s}
for some constant
c
and all
\alpha_{j}>0
. Using the obtained bounds for the three terms in (
A.4
), we conclude:

\displaystyle A_{4}\leq c\,\sum_{r\in J}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\sim r,\\
\ket{pr}\in\mathcal{H}_{n}\end{subarray}}\,\sum_{r^{\prime}\in J}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p^{\prime}\sim r^{\prime},\\
\ket{p'r'}\in\mathcal{H}_{n+1}^{\perp}\end{subarray}}\widehat{\mathbb{E}}\mathbb{E}\Big(\big|\langle xy\,|\,\widehat{R}^{(n)}pr\rangle\big|^{s}\,\big|\langle p^{\prime}r^{\prime}\,|\,\widehat{R}^{(n+1)}\,x^{\prime}y^{\prime}\rangle\big|^{s}\Big).

We note that the last line only depends on the random variables
(\omega_{r})_{r\notin J}
and
(\widehat{\omega}_{r})_{r\in J}
. Since
(\omega_{r})_{r\in J}
and
(\widehat{\omega}_{r})_{r\in J}
are independent and identically distributed, we can replace the latter by the former. The two scalar products inside the expectation are then independent random variables by the same reasoning as for
A_{1}
. This yields:

\displaystyle A_{4}\leq c\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\in J,\\
\ket{pr}\in\mathcal{H}_{n}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{E}\Big(\big|\langle|xy\,|\,R^{(n)}pr\rangle\big|^{s}\Big)\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r^{\prime}\in J,\\
\ket{p'r'}\in\mathcal{H}_{n+1}^{\perp}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{E}\Big(\big|\langle p^{\prime}r^{\prime}\,|\,R^{(n+1)}\,x^{\prime}y^{\prime}\rangle\big|^{s}\Big).

(A.6)

We can interpolate the arguments used for
A_{1}
and
A_{4}
to obtain similar estimates for
A_{2}
and
A_{3}
. We note that in the sum above, we have
\operatorname{dist}(r)\in\{n-1,n\}
and
\operatorname{dist}(r^{\prime})\in\{n+1,n+2\}
. Using (
4.21
) and (
A.1
), we finally obtain (
4.22
). We stress that all constants obtained in this section are independent of
n
and of the center of the ball
B_{n}
.

A.2
Second re-sampling argument

We derive equation (
4.24
) from (
4.4
). Since the methods used are similar to the ones in Appendix
A.1
, we will omit details. We let
c>0
denote some constant independent of
n
that may change from line to line.

Fixing
\ket{w't'}\in\mathcal{H}_{n+1}^{\perp}
with
\operatorname{dist}(t^{\prime})\in\{n+1,n+2\}
,
(\ket{uv},\ket{u'v'})\in\partial B_{n+1}
with
\ket{uv}\in\mathcal{H}_{n+1}^{\perp}
, we define
\tilde{J}=\{t^{\prime},v,v^{\prime}\}
. Note that
\operatorname{dist}(v)=n+1
and
n\leq\operatorname{dist}(v)\leq n+2
. Similar to our approach in Appendix
A.1
, we re-sample the random variables on all sites in
\tilde{J}
. Let
(\tilde{\omega}_{r})_{r\in\tilde{J}}
be an i.i.d. family of random variables uniformly distributed on the torus
\mathbb{T}
. Let
D_{\omega,\tilde{\omega}}
use the phase
\tilde{\omega}_{r}
on all sites
r\in\tilde{J}
and
\omega_{r}
on all other sites. Let
U_{\omega,\tilde{\omega}}=D_{\omega,\tilde{\omega}}U
and
\tilde{R}
be the re-sampled quantum walk and its resolvent. To simplify the notation we define
\tilde{D}=\sum_{r\in\tilde{J}}\big(e^{i\omega_{r}}-e^{i\tilde{\omega}_{r}}\big)\,P_{r}^{\rm O}
. Using the resolvent identity
R=\tilde{R}-R\,\tilde{D}\,U\,\tilde{R}
, we obtain for the last expectation in (
4.4
):

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\bigg(\left|\langle w^{\prime}t^{\prime}\,|\,R^{(n+1)}\,uv\rangle\right|^{s}\,\left|\langle u^{\prime}v^{\prime}\,|\,R\,x^{\prime}y^{\prime}\rangle\right|^{s}\bigg)&{\leq}\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\mathbb{E}\bigg(\left|\langle w^{\prime}t^{\prime}\,|\,R^{(n+1)}\,uv\rangle\right|^{s}\,\big|\langle u^{\prime}v^{\prime}\,|\,\tilde{R}\,x^{\prime}y^{\prime}\rangle\big|^{s}\bigg)\\
+\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\mathbb{E}\bigg(\left|\langle w^{\prime}t^{\prime}\,|\,R^{(n+1)}\,uv\rangle\right|^{s}\,&\big|\langle u^{\prime}v^{\prime}\,|\,R\tilde{D}U\tilde{R}\,x^{\prime}y^{\prime}\rangle\big|^{s}\bigg)=B_{1}+B_{2}.\end{split}

(A.7)

We write
\mathbb{E}(...|\tilde{J})
for the conditional expectation with respect to the
\sigma
-algebra generated by
(\omega_{r})_{r\notin\tilde{J}}
. We proceed as in section
A.1.2
and apply the law of total expectation (equation (
A.2
)) to
B_{1}
, use that
\tilde{R}
is independent of the random variables
(\omega_{r})_{r\in\tilde{J}}
and apply Theorem
3
, since
\{t^{\prime},v\}\subset\tilde{J}
. We can then replace the random variables
(\tilde{\omega}_{r})_{r\in\tilde{J}}
by
(\omega_{r})_{r\in\tilde{J}}
to obtain:

\displaystyle B_{1}\leq c\,\mathbb{E}\Big(\big|\langle u^{\prime}v^{\prime}\,|\,R\,x^{\prime}y^{\prime}\rangle\big|^{s}\Big).

(A.8)

The law of total expectation, Hölder’s inequality and Theorem
3
give for
B_{2}
:

\displaystyle B_{2}\leq c\,\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\mathbb{E}\Big(\mathbb{E}\Big(\big|\langle u^{\prime}v^{\prime}\,|\,R\tilde{D}U\tilde{R}\,x^{\prime}y^{\prime}\rangle\big|^{2s}\,\Big|\,\tilde{J}^{c}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big).

(A.9)

Using the definition of
\tilde{D}
,
2s<1
and Theorem
3
, we obtain for the inner expectation:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}

\displaystyle\Big(\big|\langle u^{\prime}v^{\prime}\,|\,R\tilde{D}U\tilde{R}\,x^{\prime}y^{\prime}\rangle\big|^{2s}\,\Big|\,\tilde{J}^{c}\Big)\leq c\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ket{pr}\in\mathcal{H}\\
\text{s.t. }r\in\tilde{J}\end{subarray}}\big|\langle pr\,|\,U\tilde{R}\,x^{\prime}y^{\prime}\rangle\big|^{2s}.

(A.10)

By the same argument as in Appendix
A.1
, we have for every
r\in\tilde{J}
that
|r|\leq n+2
. Since
\ket{x'y'}\in\mathcal{H}_{n+3}^{\perp}
, it follows that
y^{\prime}\notin\tilde{J}
. This yields:

\displaystyle\big|\langle pr|\,U\tilde{R}\,x^{\prime}y^{\prime}\rangle\big|=\big|\langle pr|\,\big(I+z\tilde{R}\big)\,x^{\prime}y^{\prime}\rangle\big|=|z|\,|\langle pr|\,\tilde{R}\,x^{\prime}y^{\prime}\rangle|.

(A.11)

Since
|\tilde{J}|\leq 3
, we have
\big(\sum_{j\in\tilde{J}}\alpha_{j}^{2s}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq c\sum_{j\in\tilde{J}}\alpha_{j}^{s}
for all
\alpha_{j}>0
. Using
|z|<3/2
, we insert (
A.10
) and (
A.11
) into (
A.9
) and replace
\tilde{\omega}_{r}
by
\omega_{r}
to obtain:

\displaystyle B_{2}\leq c\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ket{pr}\in\mathcal{H}\\
\text{s.t. }r\in\tilde{J}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{E}\Big(|\langle pr\,|\,R\,x^{\prime}y^{\prime}\rangle|^{s}\Big).

(A.12)

Plugging the estimates (
A.7
), (
A.8
) and (
A.12
) into (
4.4
) we obtain:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big(\big|

\displaystyle\langle xy|\,R\,x^{\prime}y^{\prime}\rangle\big|^{s}\big)\leq c\,\bigg(\sum_{\ket{wt}\in\mathcal{H}_{n},\operatorname{dist}(t)\in\{n-1,n\}}\mathbb{E}\left(\big|\langle xy|\,R^{(n)}\,wt\rangle\big|^{s}\right)\bigg)

\displaystyle\times\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ket{w't'}\in\mathcal{H}_{n+1}^{\perp}\\
\operatorname{dist}(t^{\prime})\in\{n+1,n+2\}\end{subarray}}\Bigg(\mathbb{E}\left(\big|\langle w^{\prime}t^{\prime}|\,R\,x^{\prime}y^{\prime}\rangle\big|^{s}\right)+c\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(\ket{uv},\ket{u'v'})\in\partial B_{n+1},\\
\ket{uv}\in\mathcal{H}_{n+1}^{\perp}\end{subarray}}\Bigg(\mathbb{E}\Big(\big|\langle u^{\prime}v^{\prime}|\,R\,x^{\prime}y^{\prime}\rangle\big|^{s}\Big)

\displaystyle\hphantom{{} \times\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ket{w't'} \in\mathcal{H}_{n+1}^\perp\\
\operatorname{dist}(t') \in\lx@text@lbrace n+1, n+2\lx@text@rbrace\end{subarray}} \Bigg(}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ket{pr}\in\mathcal{H}\\
r\in\{t^{\prime},v,v^{\prime}\}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{E}\Big(|\langle pr|\,R\,x^{\prime}y^{\prime}\rangle|^{s}\Big)\Bigg)\Bigg).

The sum over
\ket{w't'}
leads to at most
d\,(|S_{n+1}|+|S_{n+2}|)\leq cs_{n}
terms. Similarly, the sum over
\ket{uv},\ket{u'v'}
leads to
\mathcal{O}(s_{n})
terms. We can therefore replace the last three expectations by a single sum of
\mathbb{E}\big(|\langle uv\,|R\,xy\rangle|^{s}\big)
over
\ket{uv}\in\mathcal{H}
with
\operatorname{dist}(v)\in\{n,n+1,n+2\}
. This leads to an additional term of the order
\mathcal{O}(s_{n})
in (
4.24
).

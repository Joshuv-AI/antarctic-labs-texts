Introduction

1.1
Stability of Sobolev inequality in
\mathbb{R}^{N}

The classical Sobolev inequality
states that for
N\geq 3
, there exists a dimensional constant
\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{S}(N)>0
such that

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}\geq\mathcal{S}\|u\|_{L^{2^{\ast}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2},\quad\forall\,u\in{D}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

(1.1)

where
2^{\ast}=\frac{2N}{N-2}
denotes the critical exponent for the Sobolev embedding
D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\hookrightarrow L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, and
D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is defined as the completion of
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with
respect to the norm

\|u\|_{D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

In
[
46
]
(see also Aubin
[
1
]
), Talenti computed the sharp constant of (
1.1
) and showed that its extremal functions are exactly the Talenti bubbles, which take the form

U_{\lambda,a}(x)=[N(N-2)]^{\frac{N-2}{4}}\left(\frac{\lambda}{1+\lambda^{2}|x-a|^{2}}\right)^{\frac{N-2}{2}},~~\lambda>0,~a\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

Furthermore, Chen and Li
[
9
]
established that
U_{\lambda,a}
are the unique
positive solutions to the Euler-Lagrange equation corresponding to (
1.1
)

-\Delta u=|u|^{2^{*}-2}u,\qquad\mathrm{in}~\mathbb{R}^{N}.

(1.2)

In
[
5
]
, Brezis and Lieb posed a fundamental question: For any
u\in D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, can one naturally bound the quantity
\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}-\mathcal{S}\|u\|_{L^{2^{\ast}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}
from below by the “distance” between
u
and the manifold of bubble functions
\mathcal{M}
? Here
\mathcal{M}=\big\{cU_{\lambda,a}:c\in\mathbb{R},\lambda>0,a\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\big\}
is an
(N+2)
-dimensional manifold.

This question
was first addressed by Bianchi and Egnell
[
4
]
. To analyze the remainder term in the Sobolev inequality (
1.1
), they first established the non-degeneracy of

U_{1,0}
, then employed the global-local analysis and spectral theory to prove that
there exists
\kappa>0
such that

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}-\mathcal{S}\|u\|_{L^{2^{\ast}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}\geq\kappa~\mathrm{dist}(u,\mathcal{M})^{2},\quad\forall\,u\in{D}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

where
\mathrm{dist}(u,\mathcal{M})=\inf_{c\in\mathbb{R},\lambda>0,a\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}\|u-cU_{\lambda,a}\|_{D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}
.
Subsequent works extended this result to the second Sobolev inequality
[
40
]
,
the fractional Sobolev setting (for all
N\geq 1
and
s\in(0,\frac{N}{2})
;
[
7
]
), and the
p
-Laplacian case
[
23
,
24
,
43
]
.
For more related results, including explicit lower or upper bounds for
\kappa
, we refer the readers to
[
19
,
29
,
30
,
31
,
12
,
11
,
10
]
.

A natural and more challenging direction is to investigate the quantitative stability of critical points for
the Euler-Lagrange equation (
1.2
): Specifically, if a function almost solves (
1.2
), must it be quantitatively close to a single bubble
U_{\lambda,a}
or a sum of weakly interacting bubbles? Struwe’s celebrated global compactness lemma
[
45
]
addressed this question qualitatively. Building on
[
45
]
, extensive research has been dedicated to the stability of critical points for (
1.2
). A significant breakthrough was achieved by Ciraolo, Figalli, and Maggi
[
14
]
, who established a sharp quantitative stability result for the single-bubble case in dimensions
N\geq 3
. Precisely, they showed that

\|u-U_{\lambda,a}\|_{{D}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq C\Theta(u).

where
\Theta(u)=\|\Delta u+|u|^{2^{*}-2}u\|_{({D}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))^{-1}}
.
Subsequently, Figalli and Glaudo
[
22
]
and Deng, Sun and Wei
[
17
]
extended this
stability result to the multi-bubble (weakly interacting) case for
3\leq N\leq 5
and
N\geq 6
, respectively. Their results can be summarized as follows

\left\|u-\sum_{i=1}^{m}{U}_{\lambda_{i},a_{i}}\right\|_{{D}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq C\begin{cases}\Theta(u),\qquad&\mathrm{if}~3\leq N\leq 5,\\
\Theta(u)|\log\Theta(u)|^{\frac{1}{2}},\qquad&\mathrm{if}~N=6,\\
\Theta(u)^{\frac{N+2}{2(N-2)}},\qquad&\mathrm{if}~N\geq 7.\end{cases}

Furthermore, the quantitative stability results in
[
14
,
17
,
22
]

have been generalized to the fractional setting
[
8
]
and the
p
-Laplacian case (single-bubble case;
[
15
,
35
]
).
We now turn to the nonlocal inequalities associated with the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev (HLS) inequality, which we recall below.

Proposition 1.1
.

[
34
, Theorem 4.3]

Suppose that
N\geq 1
,
0<\mu<N
and
t,r>1
with
\frac{1}{t}+\frac{\mu}{N}+\frac{1}{r}=2
,
f\in L^{t}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
h\in L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Then there exists a constant
{C}(N,\mu,t,r)>0
independent of
f
and
h
such that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{f(x)h(y)}{|x-y|^{\mu}}dxdy\leq C(N,\mu,t,r)\|f\|_{L^{t}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\|h\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}.

(1.3)

If
t=r=\frac{2N}{2N-\mu}
, then

{C}(N,\mu,t,r)=C(N,\mu)=\frac{\pi^{\frac{\mu}{2}}\Gamma\big(\frac{N-\mu}{2}\big)}{\Gamma\big(\frac{2N-\mu}{2}\big)}\left(\frac{\Gamma(N)}{\Gamma(\frac{N}{2})}\right)^{\frac{N-\mu}{N}},

and there is equality in (
1.3
) if and only if
f\equiv(const.)h
and

h(x)=c\left(\frac{\lambda}{1+\lambda^{2}|x-a|^{2}}\right)^{\frac{2N-\mu}{2}},~~\lambda>0,~a\in\mathbb{R}^{N}

for some
c\in\mathbb{R}
, where

\Gamma
denotes the Gamma function
\Gamma(\gamma)=\int_{0}^{+\infty}t^{\gamma-1}e^{-t}dt
for
\gamma>0
.

According to (
1.3
), the functional

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)|^{p}|u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{\mu}}dxdy

is well defined in
D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
if
p\in\big[\frac{2N-\mu}{N},\frac{2N-\mu}{N-2}\big]
. Then
2^{\ast}_{\mu}=\frac{2N-\mu}{N-2}
and
2_{\ast}^{\mu}=\frac{2N-\mu}{N}
are called the upper and lower critical exponents, respectively.
Moreover, combining the Sobolev inequality with the HLS
inequality, one can easily derive the following nonlocal inequality

\mathcal{S}_{HL}(N,\mu)\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)|^{2_{\mu}^{*}}|u(y)|^{2_{\mu}^{*}}}{|x-y|^{\mu}}dxdy\right)^{\frac{1}{2^{\ast}_{\mu}}}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx,\quad\forall\,u\in D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

(1.4)

where
\mathcal{S}_{HL}(N,\mu)=\frac{\mathcal{S}}{C(N,\mu)^{\frac{1}{2_{\mu}^{*}}}}
.
And the associated Euler-Lagrange equation becomes

-\Delta u=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(y)|^{2^{\ast}_{\mu}}}{|x-y|^{\mu}}dy\right)|u|^{2_{\mu}^{*}-2}u,~~u\in D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

(1.5)

To gain a deeper understanding of inequality (
1.4
), it is natural to investigate the quantitative stability of both (
1.4
) and its Euler-Lagrange equation (
1.5
).
Inspired by
[
4
]
, using the classification
[
21
,
27
]
and non-degeneracy
[
32
]
of positive solutions
to (
1.5
),
Deng et al.
[
18
]
established a gradient-type remainder term estimate for (
1.4
): Assume that
N\geq 3
,
0<\mu<N
with
\mu\leq 4
, then there exist constants
\kappa_{2}>\kappa_{1}>0
such that for all
u\in{D}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, it holds that

\kappa_{2}~\mathrm{dist}(u,\mathcal{M})^{2}\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}{d}x-\mathcal{S}_{HL}(N,\mu)\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)|^{2_{\mu}^{*}}|u(y)|^{2_{\mu}^{*}}}{|x-y|^{\mu}}dxdy\right)^{\frac{1}{2^{\ast}_{\mu}}}\geq\kappa_{1}~\mathrm{dist}(u,\mathcal{M})^{2}.

On the other hand, Liu, Zhang and Zou
[
37
]
, Piccione, Yang and Zhao
[
44
]
, Yang and Zhao
[
50
]
, and Dai, Hu and Peng
[
16
]
have established the quantitative stability results for critical points
of equation (
1.5
). For extensions to the stability of fractional HLS inequalities, we refer the readers to
[
41
]
.

1.2
Stability of Folland-Stein-Sobolev inequality on
\mathbb{H}^{n}

The Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}^{n}
is
\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
with elements
\xi=(\xi_{l})=(z,t)
,
\xi^{\prime}=(\xi^{\prime}_{l})=(z^{\prime},t^{\prime})
,
1\leq l\leq 2n+1
, and group operation

\xi^{\prime}\xi=\big(z+z^{\prime},t+t^{\prime}+2\mathrm{Im}z^{\prime}\cdot\bar{z}\big).

The left translations are given by

\tau_{\xi^{\prime}}(\xi)=\xi^{\prime}\xi,

and the dilations of group are

\{\delta_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda>0}:\mathbb{H}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{H}^{n}
,

\delta_{\lambda}(\xi)=(\lambda z,\lambda^{2}t).

Define the homogeneous norm

|\xi|=(|z|^{4}+t^{2})^{\frac{1}{4}},

and the distance

d(\xi,\xi^{\prime})=|(\xi^{\prime})^{-1}\xi|
.
It holds that
|\delta_{\lambda}(\xi)|=\lambda|\xi|
and

d(\lambda\xi,\lambda\xi^{\prime})=\lambda d(\xi,\xi^{\prime})
.
As usual, the homogeneous dimension of
\mathbb{H}^{n}
is
Q=2n+2
. Denote by
B_{r}(\xi_{0})
the ball of radius
r
centered at
\xi_{0}
with respect to the Heisenberg distance
d
, and write
B_{r}=B_{r}(\mathbf{0})
when the center is the origin.

The canonical left-invariant vector fields on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
are

X_{j}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}+2y_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial t},\quad X_{n+j}=\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{j}}-2x_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial t},\quad j=1,\ldots,n.

It follows that the canonical left-invariant vector field is

\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}=(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n},X_{n+1},\ldots,X_{2n}),

and the Kohn Laplacian (or sub-Laplacian) operator is

\Delta_{\mathbb{H}}=\mathop{\sum}\limits_{j=1}^{n}\big(X_{j}^{2}+X_{n+j}^{2}\big).

Let
Q^{\ast}=\frac{2Q}{Q-2}
, define the standard Folland-Stein-Sobolev space

S^{1,2}(\mathbb{H}^{n})=\Big\{u\in L^{Q^{\ast}}(\mathbb{H}^{n}):\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{H}^{n})\Big\},

with the inner product

\langle u,v\rangle_{S^{1,2}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}=\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}u\cdot\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}v{d}\xi,

and the corresponding norm

\|u\|_{S^{1,2}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}u|^{2}{d}\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

In
[
25
]
, Folland and Stein established the following well-known Sobolev type inequality on
\mathbb{H}^{n}

\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}u|^{2}{d}\xi\geq S(Q)\left(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}|u|^{Q^{\ast}}{d}\xi\right)^{\frac{2}{Q^{\ast}}},\quad\forall\,u\in S^{1,2}(\mathbb{H}^{n}).

(1.6)

Moreover, it follows from Jerison and Lee
[
28
]
that

S(Q)=\frac{4\pi n^{2}}{(n!4^{n})^{\frac{1}{n+1}}},

and the corresponding extremal functions are in the form (Jerison-Lee bubbles):

\mathfrak{g}_{\lambda,\xi_{0}}U(\xi)=\lambda^{\frac{Q-2}{2}}U(\delta_{\lambda}(\tau_{\xi_{0}}^{-1}(\xi))),~~\lambda>0,~\zeta_{0}\in\mathbb{H}^{n},

(1.7)

where

U(\xi)=\frac{(2n)^{n}}{[(1+|z|^{2})^{2}+t^{2}]^{\frac{Q-2}{4}}}

is the unique (up to translations and scalings, i.e.,
\mathfrak{g}_{\lambda,\xi}U
for all
\lambda>0
and
\xi\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
)
positive solution of the Euler-Lagrange equation

-\Delta_{\mathbb{H}}u=|u|^{Q^{*}-2}u,~~\xi\in\mathbb{H}^{n}.

(1.8)

Malchiodi and Uguzzoni
[
42
]
proved the non-degeneracy of the positive bubble solutions to (
1.8
).
For further applications, Loiudice
[
38
]
derived the remainder term estimate for inequality
(
1.6
), while Tang, Zhang and Zhang
[
47
]
and Chen et al.
[
13
]
established a positive upper and lower bound for the value of this remainder term estimate, respectively. For the fractional version, the readers may refer to

[
36
]
for further details. More recently, Chen, Fan and Liao
[
6
]
obtained the quantitative stability result of critical points for equation (
1.8
).

Similar to the HLS inequality in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, the HLS inequality on the Heisenberg group

\mathbb{H}^{n}

has also been established by Folland and Stein
[
25
]
as well as Frank and Lieb
[
26
]
.

Proposition 1.2
.

Suppose that
Q\geq 4
,
0<\mu<Q
and
t,r>1
with
\frac{1}{t}+\frac{\mu}{Q}+\frac{1}{r}=2
,
f\in L^{t}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
and
h\in L^{r}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
. Then there exists a constant
\widetilde{C}(Q,\mu,t,r)>0
independent of
f
and
h
such that

\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{f(\xi)h(\eta)}{|\eta^{-1}\xi|^{\mu}}d\xi{d}\eta\leq\widetilde{C}(Q,\mu,t,r)\|f\|_{L^{t}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}\|h\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{H}^{n})},~~\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{H}^{n}.

(1.9)

If
t=r=\frac{2Q}{2Q-\mu}
, then

\widetilde{C}(Q,\mu,t,r)=C(Q,\mu)=\bigg(\frac{\pi^{n+1}}{2^{n-1}n!}\bigg)^{\frac{\mu}{Q}}\frac{n!\Gamma\big(\frac{Q-\mu}{2}\big)}{\Gamma^{2}\big(\frac{2Q-\mu}{4}\big)},

(1.10)

and there is equality in (
1.9
) if and only if
f\equiv(const.)h
and

h(\xi)=c\lambda^{\frac{2Q-\mu}{2}}V(\delta_{\lambda}(\tau_{\xi_{0}}^{-1}(\xi))),~~\lambda>0,~\zeta_{0}\in\mathbb{H}^{n}

for some
c\in\mathbb{C}
, where

V(\xi)=\frac{1}{[(1+|z|^{2})^{2}+t^{2}]^{\frac{2Q-\mu}{4}}}.

Similarly, by (
1.9
),
Q^{\ast}_{\mu}=\frac{2Q-\mu}{Q-2}
calls the upper critical exponent.
For the upper critical case, Yang and Zhang
[
49
]
and Zhang et al.
[
51
]
proved:

Lemma 1.1
.

Let
Q\geq 4
,
0<\mu<Q
. Then for any
u\in S^{1,2}(\mathbb{H}^{n})\setminus\{0\}
, the inequality

S_{HL}(Q,\mu)\left(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{|u(\xi)|^{Q^{\ast}_{\mu}}|u(\eta)|^{Q^{\ast}_{\mu}}}{|\eta^{-1}\xi|^{\mu}}{d}\xi{d}\eta\right)^{\frac{1}{Q^{\ast}_{\mu}}}\leq\|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}

(1.11)

holds with the sharp constant

S_{HL}(Q,\mu)=S(Q)C(Q,\mu)^{-\frac{1}{Q_{\mu}^{*}}}.

The equality in (
1.11
) holds
if and only if

u(\xi)=c\mathfrak{g}_{\lambda,\xi_{0}}U(\xi)

for some
c\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
,
\lambda>0
and
\xi_{0}\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
, where
\mathfrak{g}_{\lambda,\xi_{0}}U
is defined by (
1.7
). Moreover,
\mathfrak{g}_{\lambda,\xi}U
(for all
\lambda>0
and
\xi\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
) is the unique positive solution of the Euler-Lagrange equation

-\Delta_{\mathbb{H}}u=\alpha(Q,\mu)\left(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{|u(\eta)|^{Q^{\ast}_{\mu}}}{|\eta^{-1}\xi|^{\mu}}{d}\eta\right)|u|^{Q_{\mu}^{*}-2}u,~~\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{H}^{n},

(1.12)

where

\alpha(Q,\mu)=S(Q)^{-\frac{Q-\mu}{2}}C(Q,\mu)^{-1}.

For equation (
1.12
), Yang and Zhang
[
49
]
established
the following non-degeneracy property of bubbles.

Lemma 1.2
.

Let
Q\geq 4
,
0<\mu<Q
.
If
u\in S^{1,2}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
is a solution to
the linearized equation

-\Delta_{\mathbb{H}}v=Q^{\ast}_{\mu}\left(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{|U(\eta)|^{Q^{\ast}_{\mu}-1}v(\eta)}{|\eta^{-1}\xi|^{\mu}}{d}\eta\right)|U|^{Q^{\ast}_{\mu}-2}U+(Q^{\ast}_{\mu}-1)\left(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{|U(\eta)|^{Q^{\ast}_{\mu}}}{|\eta^{-1}\xi|^{\mu}}{d}\eta\right)|U|^{Q^{\ast}_{\mu}-2}v,

then
u
must be a linear combination of the functions
\{Z^{a}\}_{a=1}^{2n+2}
, where
Z^{a}
are defined by (
2.1
) and (
2.2
).

In spite of the work Deng et al.
[
18
]
and Loiudice
[
38
]
, in this paper, we first are concerned with the remainder term of inequality (
1.11
) for
0<\mu<Q
with
\mu\leq 4
.
Now, we present our first result as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
Q\geq 4
,
0<\mu<Q
with
\mu\leq 4
.
Then there exist two constants
A_{2}>A_{1}>0
such that for every
u\in S^{1,2}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
, it holds that

A_{2}~\mathrm{dist}(u,\mathfrak{M})^{2}\geq\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}u|^{2}{d}\xi-S_{HL}(Q,\mu)\left(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{|u(\xi)|^{Q^{\ast}_{\mu}}|u(\eta)|^{Q^{\ast}_{\mu}}}{|\eta^{-1}\xi|^{\mu}}{d}\xi{d}\eta\right)^{\frac{1}{Q^{\ast}_{\mu}}}\geq A_{1}~\mathrm{dist}(u,\mathfrak{M})^{2},

where
\mathfrak{M}=\big\{c\mathfrak{g}_{\lambda,\xi}U:c\in\mathbb{C},\lambda>0,\xi\in\mathbb{H}^{n}\big\}
is an
(2n+3)
-dimensional manifold and
\mathrm{dist}(u,\mathfrak{M})=\inf_{c\in\mathbb{C},\lambda>0,\xi\in\mathbb{H}^{n}}\|u-c\mathfrak{g}_{\lambda,\xi}U\|_{S^{1,2}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}
.

Remark 1.1
.

The restriction
\mu\leq 4
is used to guarantee
Q_{\mu}^{*}\geq 2
, which is used in Lemma
3.2
.

As a corollary of Theorem
1.1
, we will consider a remainder term inequality on bounded domains
\Omega\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}
. In sprite of the work of Brezis and Lieb
[
5
]
, for each bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}
, we define the weak
L^{q}
-norm

\|u\|_{L^{q}_{w}(\Omega)}=\sup\limits_{D\subset\Omega}\frac{\int_{D}|u|dx}{|D|^{\frac{q-1}{q}}}.

(1.13)

In
[
39
]
(see also
[
38
]
), Loiudice proved that there exists
A^{\prime}>0
such that

\|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}-S(Q)\|u\|_{L^{Q^{\ast}}(\Omega)}^{2}\geq A^{\prime}\|u\|_{L^{\frac{Q}{Q-2}}_{w}(\Omega)}^{2},\quad\forall\,u\in{S}_{0}^{1,2}(\Omega),

where
S_{0}^{1,2}(\Omega)
denotes the Folland-Stein-Sobolev
space defined as the completion of
C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
with respect to the norm
\|\cdot\|_{S^{1,2}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}
.
Then, we establish the second-type remainder term for inequality (
1.11
) in a bounded domain, which is stated
as follows.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}
be a bounded domain,

,

.
Then there exists

where

denotes the weak

-norm as in (

).

Remark 1.2
.

If
\mu=0
, then

1.1

\sum\limits_{j\geq 2}\left(\underbrace{\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\Big(\mathfrak{g}_{j}^{(k)}U\Big)^{\frac{2Q(Q^{\ast}_{\mu}-1)}{2Q-\mu}}\phi^{\frac{2Q}{2Q-\mu}}d\xi}_{:=II_{j}^{(k)}}\right)^{\frac{2Q-\mu}{2Q}}\qquad&\mathrm{if}~2<Q_{\mu}^{*}<3,\end{cases}

\displaystyle+\sum\limits_{j\geq 2}\left(\underbrace{\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\Big(\mathfrak{g}_{j}^{(k)}U\Big)^{\frac{2Q(Q^{\ast}_{\mu}-1)}{2Q-\mu}}\phi^{\frac{2Q}{2Q-\mu}}d\xi}_{:=III_{j}^{(k)}}\right)^{\frac{2Q-\mu}{2Q}}+\sum\limits_{j\geq 2}\left(\underbrace{\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\Big(U^{Q^{\ast}_{\mu}-2}\phi\mathfrak{g}_{j}^{(k)}U\Big)^{\frac{2Q}{2Q-\mu}}d\xi}_{:=IV_{j}^{(k)}}\right)^{\frac{2Q-\mu}{2Q}}.

Similarly, we can prove that

Analogously, by
\mu<4
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{\bigg(U+\sum\limits_{j\geq 2}\mathfrak{g}_{j}^{(k)}U\bigg)^{Q^{\ast}_{\mu}}\bigg(U+\sum\limits_{j\geq 2}\mathfrak{g}_{j}^{(k)}U\bigg)^{Q^{\ast}_{\mu}-2}\rho_{k}\phi-U^{Q^{\ast}_{\mu}}U^{Q^{\ast}_{\mu}-2}\rho_{k}\phi}{|\eta^{-1}\xi|^{\mu}}d\xi{d}\eta

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{\Big[U^{Q^{\ast}_{\mu}-1}\sigma_{*}+\sigma_{*}^{Q^{\ast}_{\mu}}\Big](U+\sigma_{*})^{Q^{\ast}_{\mu}-2}\rho_{k}\phi}{|\eta^{-1}\xi|^{\mu}}d\xi{d}\eta

\displaystyle+\begin{cases}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{U^{Q^{\ast}_{\mu}}\Big[U^{Q^{\ast}_{\mu}-3}\sigma_{*}+\sigma_{*}^{Q^{\ast}_{\mu}-2}\Big]\rho_{k}\phi}{|\eta^{-1}\xi|^{\mu}}d\xi{d}\eta,\qquad&\mathrm{if}~Q_{\mu}^{*}\geq 3,\\
\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{U^{Q^{\ast}_{\mu}}\sigma_{*}^{Q^{\ast}_{\mu}-2}\rho_{k}\phi}{|\eta^{-1}\xi|^{\mu}}d\xi{d}\eta,\qquad&\mathrm{if}~2<Q_{\mu}^{*}<3,\end{cases}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j\geq 2}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{U^{Q^{\ast}_{\mu}-1}\Big(\mathfrak{g}_{j}^{(k)}U\Big)U^{Q^{\ast}_{\mu}-2}\rho_{k}\phi}{|\eta^{-1}\xi|^{\mu}}d\xi{d}\eta+\sum\limits_{j\geq 2}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{\Big(\mathfrak{g}_{j}^{(k)}U\Big)^{Q^{\ast}_{\mu}}\Big(\mathfrak{g}_{j}^{(k)}U\Big)^{Q^{\ast}_{\mu}-2}\rho_{k}\phi}{|\eta^{-1}\xi|^{\mu}}d\xi{d}\eta

\displaystyle+\begin{cases}\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j\geq 2}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{U^{Q^{\ast}_{\mu}}\bigg[U^{Q^{\ast}_{\mu}-3}\mathfrak{g}_{j}^{(k)}U+\Big(\mathfrak{g}_{j}^{(k)}U\Big)^{Q^{\ast}_{\mu}-2}\bigg]\rho_{k}\phi}{|\eta^{-1}\xi|^{\mu}}d\xi{d}\eta,\qquad&\mathrm{if}~Q_{\mu}^{*}\geq 3,\\
\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j\geq 2}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{U^{Q^{\ast}_{\mu}}\Big(\mathfrak{g}_{j}^{(k)}U\Big)^{Q^{\ast}_{\mu}-2}\rho_{k}\phi}{|\eta^{-1}\xi|^{\mu}}d\xi{d}\eta,\qquad&\mathrm{if}~2<Q_{\mu}^{*}<3,\end{cases}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j\geq 2}\left(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\Big(U^{Q^{\ast}_{\mu}-1}\mathfrak{g}_{j}^{(k)}U\Big)^{\frac{2Q}{2Q-\mu}}d\xi\right)^{\frac{2Q-\mu}{2Q}}+\sum\limits_{j\geq 2}\left(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\Big(\mathfrak{g}_{j}^{(k)}U\Big)^{\frac{2Q(Q_{\mu}^{*}-2)}{Q+2-\mu}}\phi^{\frac{2Q}{Q+2-\mu}}d\xi\right)^{\frac{Q+2-\mu}{2Q}}

\displaystyle+\begin{cases}\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j\geq 2}\left(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\Big(U^{Q^{\ast}_{\mu}-3}\phi\mathfrak{g}_{j}^{(k)}U\Big)^{\frac{2Q(Q_{\mu}^{*}-2)}{Q+2-\mu}}d\xi\right)^{\frac{Q+2-\mu}{2Q}}\\
\displaystyle+\sum\limits_{j\geq 2}\left(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\Big(\mathfrak{g}_{j}^{(k)}U\Big)^{\frac{2Q(Q_{\mu}^{*}-2)}{Q+2-\mu}}\phi^{\frac{2Q}{Q+2-\mu}}d\xi\right)^{\frac{Q+2-\mu}{2Q}},\qquad&\mathrm{if}~Q_{\mu}^{*}\geq 3,\\
\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j\geq 2}\left(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\Big(\mathfrak{g}_{j}^{(k)}U\Big)^{\frac{2Q(Q_{\mu}^{*}-2)}{Q+2-\mu}}\phi^{\frac{2Q}{Q+2-\mu}}d\xi\right)^{\frac{Q+2-\mu}{2Q}},\qquad&\mathrm{if}~2<Q_{\mu}^{*}<3,\end{cases}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow 0,\qquad\mathrm{as}~k\rightarrow+\infty.

(5.33)

Therefore, by letting
k\rightarrow+\infty
, we obtain

\displaystyle-\Delta_{\mathbb{H}}\rho

\displaystyle-Q^{\ast}_{\mu}\left(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{|U(\eta)|^{Q^{\ast}_{\mu}-1}\rho(\eta)}{|\eta^{-1}\xi|^{\mu}}{d}\eta\right)|U|^{Q^{\ast}_{\mu}-2}U+(Q^{\ast}_{\mu}-1)\left(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{|U(\eta)|^{Q^{\ast}_{\mu}}}{|\eta^{-1}\xi|^{\mu}}{d}\eta\right)|U|^{Q^{\ast}_{\mu}-2}\rho=0.

On the other hand, it follows from

for any

that

This with Lemma

. Hence, we prove the claim (

).

A similar discussion yields that, up to a subsequence, for each

,

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

When

By Lemma

, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\rho\|_{S^{1,2}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}\lesssim\|f+h+N(\rho)\|_{(S^{1,2}(\mathbb{H}^{n}))^{-1}}\lesssim\|f\|_{(S^{1,2}(\mathbb{H}^{n}))^{-1}}+\|h\|_{(S^{1,2}(\mathbb{H}^{n}))^{-1}}+\|N(\rho)\|_{(S^{1,2}(\mathbb{H}^{n}))^{-1}}.

5.36

Safety and Efficacy of Obinutuzumab β (MIL62), a Novel Glycoengineered Type II Anti-CD20 Monoclonal Antibody, in Patients with Neuromyelitis Optica Spectrum Disorder: A Multicenter, Single-Arm, Phase Ib Clinical Trial
Authors: Lei Wu, Min Wei, Feng Gao, Hua Zhang, Jiawei Wang, Feng Xiang, Sai Gao, Ran Liu, Jian Yin, Yanjun Guo, Xifang Liu, Jinjin Liang, Sijun Liu, Feng Li, Dehui Huang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
IntroductionTo evaluate the safety, pharmacokinetics, pharmacodynamics, and efficacy of obinutuzumab β, a novel glycoengineered type II anti-CD20 monoclonal antibody, in patients with neuromyelitis optica spectrum disorder (NMOSD).MethodsIn this multicenter, open-label, single-arm, phase Ib trial (NCT05314010), 11 aquaporin protein-4 (AQP4)-IgG-positive patients with NMOSD received obinutuzumab β at 500 mg or 1000 mg on days 1 of weeks 1 and 3. Patients in the 500-mg group could escalate to 1000 mg after 24 weeks if tolerated. Evaluations occurred at specified weeks (24–208), with 1000-mg doses on days 1 of weeks 25, 27, and every 26 weeks thereafter in patients who are relapse-free. Safety, efficacy, pharmacokinetics, pharmacodynamics, and immunogenicity were assessed.ResultsAll participants with 500 mg (n = 3) transitioned to 1000 mg after 24 weeks. During the entire study period, all 11 participants experienced at least one treatment-emergent adverse event (TEAE); 90.9% had treatment-related adverse events (TRAEs). Serious adverse events were reported in 54.5%, with 27.3% related to obinutuzumab β. No TEAEs or TRAEs led to treatment discontinuation, withdrawal, or death. Two patients had early relapses on days 26 and 27 post-first dose, respectively; one withdrew immediately, while the other remained on treatment for 1 year. The 2-year disease relapse-free rate was 81.8% [95% confidence interval (CI): 44.7%, 95.1%], and the annualized relapse rate (ARR) declined from 1.3 before 2-year treatment to 0.2 (95% CI: 0.1, 0.2) after obinutuzumab β, representing an 87.7% reduction.ConclusionObinutuzumab β demonstrated manageable safety and may have promising efficacy against NMOSD. Despite the lack of a comparator group, the results support evaluation in phase III trials.Clinical trial registrationhttps://clinicaltrials.gov/ (#NCT05314010).Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s40120-026-00960-w.

Key Summary Points

• Neuromyelitis optica spectrum disorder is a severe autoimmune condition with limited approved therapies and significant unmet clinical needs• This study aimed to evaluate the safety, pharmacokinetics, pharmacodynamics, and initial efficacy of obinutuzumab β, a novel anti-CD20 monoclonal antibody, in patients with AQP4-IgG-positive NMOSD• Obinutuzumab β demonstrated a manageable safety profile and was associated with an 87.7% reduction in the annualized relapse rate and an 81.8% 2-year relapse-free rate• Despite the lack of a comparator arm, these results suggest obinutuzumab β may offer meaningful clinical benefit and warrant further investigation in randomized controlled Phase III trials

Introduction

Neuromyelitis optica spectrum disorder (NMOSD) is a rare relapsing autoimmune neurologic disease typically characterized by acute attacks of optic neuritis and/or transverse myelitis, sometimes affecting the brainstem and other neurologic structures, with high relapse and disability rates [1, 2]. The reported incidence and prevalence rates are 0.029–0.73 per 100,000 person-years and 0.52–10 per 100,000 persons, respectively [3, 4]. The major risk factors include female sex and possible genetic factors [1]. Chronic pain is reported in most adults with NMOSD and is associated with interference in activities of daily living [5]. Cognitive impairment is not uncommon in patients with NMOSD, including in China, and clinically differs from multiple sclerosis (MS) by being less dominated by visuospatial and global cortical deficits, with a profile centered on information processing speed, executive dysfunction, and memory but occurring despite relatively fewer or less typical brain lesions than MS [6–9].

In NMOSD, B cells produce antibodies that attack aquaporin-4 (AQP4), a water channel protein essential for nerve cell function. In most cases, approximately 73–90% of patients are associated with serum IgG antibodies to AQP4 (i.e., AQP4-IgG-positive NMOSD) [10–12]. The attacks can be severe, and the incomplete recovery from attacks can accumulate, leading to long-term disability [1, 2]. Early intervention during NMOSD attacks is critical not only for symptom control but also to minimize residual disability, given the disease’s characteristic accumulation of irreversible neurological damage from untreated relapses. The first-line treatment includes high-dose intravenous corticosteroids and, if necessary, plasma exchange or immunoadsorption (IA). After the first attack, long-term immunosuppressive therapy should be started to reduce the risk of acute NMOSD attacks/relapses [13, 14]. The available antibodies include eculizumab (anti-C5) [15], ravulizumab (anti-C5) [16], satralizumab (anti-interleukin (IL)-6 receptor) [17], inebilizumab (anti-CD19) [18], and rituximab (anti-CD20) [19]. It is noteworthy that, in China, the use of rituximab for NMOSD is off-label; however, it is recommended as a therapy according to the Chinese Guidelines for the Diagnosis and Treatment of Neuromyelitis Optica Spectrum Disorders (2021 Edition) [20]. Off-label rituximab use in China is widespread for NMOSD due to limited access to approved therapies, cost barriers, and the absence of a domestic regulatory indication for NMOSD, despite comparative effectiveness over traditional immunosuppressants [21, 22]. In fact, rituximab is the second most widely used disease-modifying therapy for NMOSD in China, following mycophenolate mofetil [21]. Those drugs reduce the risk of relapse and neurological disability in patients with NMOSD, but their efficacy is not perfect. Hence, there is a need for novel, effective treatments for patients with NMOSD.

Obinutuzumab β (MIL62), a recombinant humanized monoclonal antibody for injection, is a novel glycoengineered type II anti-CD20 mAb with nearly complete afucosylation of Fc N-glycans compared to obinutuzumab. As a new-generation CD20-targeting therapy, it specifically binds to CD20 expressed on pre-B and mature B lymphocytes [23, 24]. In vitro, obinutuzumab β demonstrated antibody-dependent cell-mediated B cell cytotoxicity (ADCC) superior to rituximab [24]. Obinutuzumab β was approved by the National Medical Products Administration (NMPA) of China on February 10, 2026, for the treatment of adult patients with AQP4-antibody-positive NMOSD. Therefore, obinutuzumab β is the first domestically developed glycoengineered type II anti-CD20 monoclonal antibody approved for this indication in China. Unlike other monoclonal antibodies for NMOSD available in China (e.g., satralizumab and inebilizumab), obinutuzumab β effectively depletes B cells, thereby inhibiting the production of AQP4 antibodies, significantly reducing relapses.

Since 2017, obinutuzumab β has been investigated in patients with B-cell lymphoma and primary membranous nephropathy [23–25], but not for NMOSD. Therefore, this phase Ib trial aimed to evaluate the safety, pharmacokinetics, pharmacodynamics, and efficacy of obinutuzumab β in patients with NMOSD. The results could support future phase III trials of obinutuzumab β for NMOSD.

Methods

Ethical Approval

The trial was reviewed and approved by the Ethics Committee of the Chinese People’s Liberation Army (PLA) General Hospital (Ethics Committee of Chinese PLA General Hospital; approval #C2021-062-01). This study was performed in accordance with the Helsinki Declaration of 1964 and its later amendments. All participants provided written informed consent for study participation and result publication before any study-related procedure.

Study Design and Participants

This study was an open-label, single-arm, multiple-dose, dose-escalation phase Ib clinical trial (ClinicalTrials.gov: NCT05314010; sponsored by Beijing Mabworks Biotech). The study population consisted of patients diagnosed with NMOSD.

The inclusion criteria were (1) diagnosis of NMOSD based on the 2015 International Panel for NMO Diagnosis [26] and tested positive for serum AQP4-IgG, (2) male or female patients aged between 18 and 70 years (included), (3) Expanded Disability Status Scale (EDSS) score [27] ≤ 7.0, (4) experienced at least one NMOSD relapse requiring rescue treatment within the 2 years before the screening, (5) not receiving concomitant corticosteroid therapy for treatment of NMOSD, (6) the patients who had one episode before screening had to be stable or improved symptoms for at least 4 weeks before the first dose, and (7) voluntarily signed the informed consent form. The exact exclusion criteria are presented in the Supplementary Material. Patients with abnormal laboratory values, comorbidities that could influence the study results, previous exposure to anti-B-cell drugs, or recent use of immunomodulating agents were excluded. For the treatments before enrollment, strict washout periods were required: at least 6 months for B-cell-depleting agents (e.g., rituximab, inebilizumab), unless B-cell counts had repopulated above the lower limit of normal; 3 months for non-B-cell-depleting biologics (e.g., satralizumab, eculizumab) or alkylating agents; 28 days for plasmapheresis; and at least five half-lives for other oral immunosuppressants (e.g., mycophenolate mofetil, azathioprine). Specially, glucocorticoids were required to be discontinued prior to the first dose of the study drug.

A cell-based assay was used to detect AQP4-IgG, using the Anti-AQP4 antibody detection kit (indirect immunofluorescence) (Guangzhou Weimi, Shanghai, China) and a Weimi fluorescence microscope (XD-RFL; Guangzhou Weimi).

Intervention

This was an open-label study that aimed to enroll 10–20 participants. For the 500-mg dose group, three to six participants were to be enrolled. After a 28-day observation period, if no intolerable events occurred in the first three 500-mg participants, enrollment in the 1000-mg dose group could be started. In the 1000-mg dose group, three to six participants were to be enrolled. After a 28-day observation period, the 1000-mg dose group could be expanded if no intolerable events were observed. If intolerable events occurred, the 500-mg dose group was expanded instead. For the participants in the 500-mg dose group, if they demonstrated good tolerability, they could be given the 1000-mg treatment starting from day 1 of week 25 and beyond. Hence, if the 1000-mg group were to be expanded, all participants ultimately received the 1000-mg dose.

The drug was administered on day 1 of weeks 1 and 3, and on day 1 of weeks 25 and 27, followed by once every 26 weeks thereafter until day 1 of week 209. The participants were then followed for 6 months after the last dose (up to day 1 of week 235). The rationale for the 26-week interval was that pharmacodynamic data from the pMN indication demonstrated that peripheral B cells began to repopulate approximately 6 months after the last administration, recovering from < 5 cells/μL to ≥ 5 cells/μL. Therefore, maintenance dosing was scheduled every 26 weeks to maintain sustained B-cell depletion.

The participants in phase Ib could continue to receive treatment and be evaluated for up to 234 weeks after the initial dose. Efficacy and safety were assessed at 24, 52, 78, 104, 130, 156, 182, and 208 weeks after the initial dose. The participants who showed no disease recurrence received an additional dose of 1000 mg on the first day of weeks 25, 27, 53, 79, 105, 131, 157, 183, and 209. Considering that obinutuzumab β is a CD20-targeted B-cell-depleting agent, if a patient’s B cells remained persistently depleted (peripheral B-cell count < 5 cells/μL), further administration of obinutuzumab β was unlikely to provide additional benefit; in such cases, the investigators could, at their discretion, consider appropriately prolonging the dosing interval. In the presence of safety concerns, the dosing interval could also be increased. If a participant was confirmed to have relapsed and the investigator determined that the benefits could outweigh the risks after rescue treatment had stabilized the condition, the participant could continue obinutuzumab β treatment with his/her consent (Table S1). The premedication regimen for infusion-related reactions (IRRs) is presented in Supplementary Table X1.

Outcomes

The safety, efficacy, pharmacokinetic/pharmacodynamic characteristics, and immunogenicity of obinutuzumab β were evaluated. The safety indicators included tolerability and the occurrence of adverse events (AEs). IRRs and infection AEs are defined in the Supplementary Material. Tolerability was assessed within 28 days following the first administration of obinutuzumab β. If a participant was determined to be intolerant by mutual agreement between the investigator and the sponsor, based on predefined criteria (Supplementary Material), they were considered intolerant to the treatment. If half or more of the participants in a dose group were assessed as intolerant during this period, the corresponding dose was deemed intolerable.

The study prospectively categorized all reported events into three mutually exclusive groups: disease attacks (efficacy endpoint), infections (safety endpoint), and other adverse events (safety endpoint). Disease attacks were defined as clinical relapses or flares that met the protocol-specified criteria for worsening of the underlying disease and were therefore analyzed as efficacy outcomes. Infections and all remaining adverse events were captured as safety data and were summarized separately from disease attacks. The AEs of special interest (AESIs) included IRRs, grade ≥ 3 infections, grade ≥ 3 neutropenia, grade ≥ 3 thrombocytopenia, and progressive multifocal leukoencephalopathy (PML). All AEs were graded according to Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) v5.0 (Supplementary Table X2).

Protocol-defined relapses were defined as new or worsening objective neurological symptoms attributable to NMOSD, persisting for more than 24 h and not explained by confounding clinical factors such as fever, infection, injury, mood changes, or adverse medication reactions. Relapse criteria were provided in the Supplementary Material. The magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) evaluation was also provided in the Supplementary Material.

The effectiveness indicators included multiple clinical outcome assessments derived from evaluations by patients and their caregivers, physicians, or other assessors. These tools measure individual patient perceptions, function, or survival status, providing a multidimensional assessment of patient subjective experiences and their impact on quality of life, reflecting a patient-centered approach. These assessments included EDSS, Modified Rankin Scale, nine-Hole Peg Test (9HPT), Timed 25-Foot Walk Test (T25FW), Symbol Digit Modalities Test (SDMT), Numeric Rating Scale (NRS) for pain, and the EuroQol Five-Dimensional Questionnaire (EQ-5D).

Pharmacokinetic Characteristics

The pharmacokinetic parameters mainly included the half-life (t½), peak concentration (Cmax), volume of distribution (Vd), and clearance (CL_obs). The time points were listed in the Supplementary Material.

Statistical Analysis

The sample size for this study was determined according to dose-escalation guidelines. Statistical analyses were performed using SAS® software, version 9.4 (SAS Institute, Cary, NC, USA). Data are presented as median (range), mean (standard deviation; SD), or number (%), as appropriate.

Annualized relapse rates and 95% confidence intervals were estimated using negative binomial regression. Further details of the statistical methods were provided in the Supplementary Material.

Results

Baseline Characteristics of the Participants

Data are current as of February 21, 2025. Of the 21 individuals screened, 11 subjects were successfully enrolled and assigned to obinutuzumab β treatment (Fig. 1). At enrollment, 3 subjects were assigned to the 500-mg dose group and 8 to the 1000-mg dose group. Due to treatment tolerance, all participants in the 500-mg group transitioned to the 1000-mg dose after 24 weeks, according to protocol. The median (interquartile range; IQR) age of all participants was 42.0 (36.0, 47.0) years, and 8 (72.7%) were female. The mean baseline body mass index (BMI) was 25.1 kg/m2 (range: 18.8–33.6 kg/m2). The mean disease duration was 3.94 years (range: 0.2–17.1 years). All participants were positive for AQP4-IgG at admission. Most participants presented with optic neuritis (81.8%) and/or acute myelitis (63.6%), with a mean EDSS score of 2.8 (range: 1.0–6.5). Among the 11 patients included in the study, all (100%) had previously received glucocorticoids as part of rescue therapy. In addition, three patients (27.3%) had received immunoglobulin, and one patient (9.1%) had been treated with mycophenolate mofetil. Regarding prior maintenance therapy, glucocorticoids were used in four patients (36.4%), while mycophenolate mofetil, azathioprine, and cyclophosphamide were each administered in one patient (9.1%). The interval between the last relapse and the first dose of study drug ranged from 0.2 to 1.1 years in all participants, or 0.3–0.3 years in the 500-mg group and 0.2–1.1 years in the 1000-mg group. At baseline, all patients had discontinued glucocorticoid therapy prior to the first dose of the study drug. No patients had undergone plasmapheresis. Safety

Obinutuzumab β was well tolerated at both the 500-mg and 1000-mg doses. Within the first 24 weeks after initial administration, all participants experienced at least one treatment-emergent adverse event (TEAE, 100%), with treatment-related adverse events (TRAEs) reported in 33.3% of the 500-mg group and 100% of the 1000-mg group (81.8% overall). Grade ≥ 3 TEAEs and grade ≥ 3 TRAEs were observed in 18.2% of participants (one in each group, respectively). Treatment-emergent serious AEs (TESAEs) and treatment-related serious AEs (TRSAEs) occurred in 18.2% of participants (one in each group). Temporary interruption of treatment due to TEAEs or TRAEs was observed in 18.2% of participants, but no events led to permanent discontinuation, trial withdrawal, or death. AESIs occurred in 33.3% of the 500-mg group and 50.0% of the 1000-mg group (45.5% overall), most commonly IRRs (27.3%). Grade ≥ 3 infections were rare (9.1%), and grade ≥ 3 neutropenia occurred in one participant (9.1%) (Table 2). There were a total of five IRRs in three participants, all in the 1000-mg group. Two participants experienced IRRs during their first infusion: one presented with dizziness and dyspnoea and the other with nausea. One participant experienced IRRs on the second day after each of the first three administrations: flushing and skin warm followed the first two doses (week 1 day 1 and week 3 day 1), and pyrexia followed the third dose (week 25 day 1).

Efficacy

Based on Clinical Endpoint Committee (CEC) assessments, the median follow-up duration was 94.9 weeks [95% confidence interval (CI): 90.7, 119.1] as of February 21, 2025 (Fig. 2). During this period, two participants (18.2%) experienced CEC-confirmed first clinical relapse events, both occurring early (on days 26 and 27 post-first dose, respectively) in the 1000-mg group; one withdrew after relapse, while the other remained on treatment for about 1 year before experiencing another relapse and then withdrawing from the study. Their relapses may be linked to their extensive disease histories (over 17 and 20 years), high baseline disability scores, and the rapid discontinuation of glucocorticoids prior to the first dose, which may have preceded the full onset of obinutuzumab β’s therapeutic effect. During the study, the mean EDSS score remained stable or improved, with values of 2.8 at baseline and 2.0 at week 105 (Supplementary Figure S2). The overall EDSS deterioration rate was 25.8% (95% CI: 9.1%, 73.3%), indicating that the majority of participants did not experience significant disability progression. Pharmacodynamics

Obinutuzumab β induced rapid and profound B-cell depletion, with absolute peripheral blood CD19⁺ B cell counts dropping to deep-depletion levels (< 5 cells/μL) in most participants within 24 h of administration. This deep depletion was maintained throughout the treatment period and persisted until the next dosing cycle. Discussion

This phase Ib trial provides the first clinical evidence for the use of obinutuzumab β, a novel anti-CD20 monoclonal antibody, in patients with AQP4-IgG-positive NMOSD. Over the first 24 weeks after initial dosing, both the 500-mg and 1000-mg dose groups demonstrated favorable and generally comparable safety profiles. Events leading to temporary interruption of treatment were observed in both groups, but all participants were able to continue therapy after appropriate management. This indicates that obinutuzumab β 1000 mg did not increase the safety risk compared to 500 mg within 24 weeks after the first administration. Over the entire treatment period, all 11 participants experienced at least one TEAE. No TEAEs or TRAEs led to permanent discontinuation, trial withdrawal, or death during the entire study period. Efficacy results demonstrated a disease relapse-free rate of 81.8% and an ARR of 0.166. Most participants exhibited a decline in serum anti-AQP4 antibody titers following obinutuzumab β administration, with the magnitude of reduction increasing over longer follow-up periods.

Comparative Efficacy and Mechanistic Rationale

Obinutuzumab β demonstrated a 2-year disease relapse-free rate of 81.8% and improved limb motor function (T25FW and 9HPT). This efficacy appears similar to that reported for satralizumab (83%) [28] and inebilizumab (83.5%) [29], and superior to historical placebo or traditional immunosuppressant outcomes (55–63.2%). The ARR was also significantly reduced to 0.166, below the historical immunosuppressant or placebo outcomes (0.32–1.01) [28, 30–33].

This promising activity of obinutuzumab β is underpinned by its distinct pharmacologic design. It is a glycoengineered, type II anti-CD20 monoclonal antibody that incorporates the murine antibody’s complementarity-determining regions into a humanized framework, thereby maximizing target affinity while minimizing immunogenicity. Critically, the primary difference between obinutuzumab β and obinutuzumab lies in their glycosylation: obinutuzumab β employs a patented glycoengineering technology that achieves near-complete defucosylation (fucose knockout of about 100%), which improves thermal stability, markedly reduces glycoform heterogeneity, and results in stronger FcγRIIIa binding and enhanced ADCC activity. In vitro pharmacodynamic assays show that obinutuzumab β’s ADCC activity is approximately 100-fold that of rituximab. Furthermore, its type II binding mode promotes direct, non-apoptotic B-cell killing and reduces CD20 internalization, enabling deeper and more sustained depletion within germinal centers and tissue compartments, sites often incompletely targeted by type I antibodies such as rituximab [34–36]. Type II anti-CD20 antibodies like obinutuzumab β exhibit enhanced ADCC through near-complete defucosylation, which increases binding affinity to FcγRIIIa. This mechanism may bypass the reduced efficacy seen with rituximab in patients carrying the low-affinity FcγRIIIa-158F polymorphism [37, 38]. These combined properties provide a clear mechanistic rationale for the rapid reduction in serum AQP4-IgG titers, indicating effective suppression of the pathogenic B-cell network at its source.

Dynamics of Biomarkers and Disease Activity

Analysis of the time-course of AQP4-IgG levels offers critical insights into the dynamic nature of treatment response. The initial rapid decline in titers correlates with the peak pharmacodynamic effect of B-cell depletion. Subsequent fluctuations or rebounds, noted in some participants, likely reflect the complex interplay of residual plasma cells, partial B-cell reconstitution, or alternative immunological adaptations [35, 39, 40]. Importantly, in two participants, a rise in AQP4-IgG was associated with clinical relapse, reinforcing the role of this antibody as a key biomarker of disease activity, albeit with imperfect individual-level predictive value [41, 42]. These observations underscore that, while deep B-cell depletion can effectively reset the immunologic milieu, achieving durable remission may require strategies to manage these residual or reconstituting pathogenic pathways. The reduction of CD27 + memory B cells, as seen in this study, exemplifies this reset; while potentially attenuating humoral memory, it may concurrently reduce the pool of autoreactive memory clones, contributing to relapse prevention [43, 44].

Analysis of Early Relapses and Clinical Management

Among the enrolled subjects, two participants experienced early relapse within 4 weeks. One was participant #6, who had a high baseline EDSS score indicating severe disability and a long disease course, experienced a subsequent relapse at week 66.9. Moreover, the mechanisms of early relapse may overlap with those described for other B-cell-depleting therapies. These agents may induce an increase in proinflammatory cytokines, such as tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α), interleukin-6 (IL-6), and B-cell-activating factor, which in turn promote the maturation and survival of AQP4 antibody-producing plasmablasts and B cells [31, 45]. Importantly, the early relapse rate with obinutuzumab β (18.2%) was considerably lower than the historical rate reported for rituximab in a similar context (35.3%) [45]. This difference may be attributable to the more rapid and profound depletion achieved by obinutuzumab β, potentially mitigating transient inflammatory perturbations sometimes seen with type I antibodies [46, 47]. These cases highlight that even highly effective depletion may not immediately abrogate all pre-existing disease drivers. To minimize early relapses, the phase III study protocol was optimized to include an 8-week course of low-dose glucocorticoids following randomization to obinutuzumab β versus placebo, based on both our findings and published data showing that this approach achieved a 100% 1-year relapse-free rate with rituximab [30]. These findings highlight the promising potential of obinutuzumab β as an effective therapy for relapse prevention in NMOSD, and support the rationale for the phase III protocol modifications designed to further improve early disease control.

In the present study, the average body surface area of the patients with NMOSD was 1.67 m2. The annual treatment costs of obinutuzumab β, inebilizumab [33], and rituximab [30] for NMOSD would be approximately RMB 73,000, RMB 155,700, and RMB 75,050, respectively. Using these values and the standard dosing schedules for maintenance therapy, analysis showed that, as an imported glycoengineered biological, the annual maintenance treatment cost of inebilizumab is 113% higher than that of obinutuzumab β. Compared with rituximab, although rituximab is a common off-label option, its annual treatment cost for an average patient in our cohort is approximately 2.7% higher than that of obinutuzumab β (Table S8). This difference is partly due to the optimized, fixed dosing regimen of obinutuzumab β and its domestic production, which reduces the overall economic burden while offering superior ADCC activity through its type II structure and near-complete defucosylation. These data, combined with its official NMPA approval on February 10, 2026, position obinutuzumab β as a highly cost-effective and sustainable option for long-term maintenance therapy in patients with NMOSD. Therefore, obinutuzumab β demonstrates a clear economic advantage over existing approved biologicals and off-label rituximab. These findings underscore the potential of obinutuzumab β to improve treatment accessibility and sustainability in the current healthcare landscape in China.

Safety and Limitations

The safety profile of obinutuzumab β in this small, early‑phase study was manageable, with no treatment-related discontinuation, withdrawal, or death. However, the high incidence of TEAEs, including grade ≥ 3 events, necessitates a cautious and contextualized interpretation. These findings must be evaluated in light of the study’s significant limitations. As an open-label, single-arm phase Ib trial with a very small sample size and no control group, it substantially limits both internal validity and the ability to draw firm conclusions about the safety and efficacy of obinutuzumab β. In addition, although the rarity of NMOSD globally and in China partly explains the limited enrollment, the small number of participants increases the risk of chance findings and reduces the precision of all estimates, including relapse rates and adverse event frequencies. Baseline characteristics were also heterogeneous, particularly with respect to EDSS scores and disease duration, which may have biased the results by amplifying between‑patient differences in such a small cohort. The evaluation of dosing was further constrained by the fact that only two fixed doses were explored and that most participants initially assigned to 500-mg group subsequently switched to 1000-mg group, making it difficult to disentangle dose–response relationships for both efficacy and toxicity. Reliance on historical ARRs as the main comparator introduces additional uncertainty due to potential changes in background care, regression to the mean, and recall or documentation bias. With regard to safety, anti‑CD20 therapies are known to be associated with treatment‑emergent adverse events in NMOSD and related conditions, including infections, infusion reactions, and cytopenias [19, 23, 24, 30, 48, 49]; however, this study observed relatively high rates of TEAEs and serious adverse events, which may be partly attributable to the intensive monitoring and the specific event definitions of the protocol. The apparent 87.7% reduction in ARR and the observed 2‑year relapse‑free rate are clinically encouraging, but the wide confidence intervals around these estimates, driven by the small sample size and event counts, indicate considerable statistical uncertainty. Finally, the observed variability in B‑cell reconstitution and pharmacokinetic parameters likely reflects both the limited number of participants and their clinical heterogeneity, which complicates efforts to define an optimal re‑dosing schedule. Future, larger controlled studies with standardized dosing, longer follow‑up, and more homogeneous baseline characteristics will be essential to refine dosing intervals, better characterize the safety profile, and confirm the preliminary efficacy signals suggested by this phase Ib trial.

Conclusions

This phase Ib study suggests that obinutuzumab β appears to have favorable safety, immunogenicity, and pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic profiles, alongside promising efficacy in patients with NMOSD. Although the present trial was a single-arm trial without a control group, the results support the eventual use of obinutuzumab β for the treatment of NMOSD and support the selection of a 1000-mg dosing regimen for future studies. The phase III clinical trial of obinutuzumab β versus placebo is already ongoing in patients with AQP4-IgG-positive NMOSD to confirm its efficacy and safety.

Vertex algebras, topological defects, and Moonshine
Roberto Volpato

Abstract
We discuss topological defect lines in holomorphic vertex operators algebras and superalgebras, in particular Frenkel-Lepowsky-Meurman Monster VOA V ♮ V^{\natural} with central charge c = 24 c=24 , and Conway module SVOA V f ​ ♮ V^{f\natural} with c = 12 c=12 . First, we consider duality defects in V ♮ V^{\natural} for all non-anomalous Fricke elements of the Monster group, and provide a general formula for the corresponding defect McKay-Thompson series. Furthermore, we describe some general properties of the category of defect lines preserving the N = 1 N=1 superVirasoro algebra in V f ​ ♮ V^{f\natural} . We argue that, under some mild assumptions, every such defect in V f ​ ♮ V^{f\natural} is associated with a ℤ \mathbb{Z} -linear map form the Leech lattice to itself. This correspondence establishes a surjective (not injective) ring homomorphism between the Grothendieck ring of the category of topological defects and the ring of Leech lattice endomorphisms. Finally, we speculate about possible generalization of the Moonshine conjectures that include topological defect lines.

Generalities on topological defect lines in 2D CFT

In this section, we review some properties of topological line defects in two dimensional CFT, both from a physics and a mathematical perspective. See for example
[

]
and

In this article, we consider unitary two-dimensional conformal field theories at central charges
(c,\tilde{c})
with a unique vacuum, defined on a (Euclidean) worldsheet
\Sigma
, which is a Riemann surface. In fact, we will further restrict to worldsheets
\Sigma
with the topology of a cylinder
S^{1}\times\mathbb{R}
, a (Riemann) sphere
\hat{\mathbb{C}}
, or a torus
S^{1}\times S^{1}
. In this section, we assume that the CFT is bosonic; the generalization to CFTs with fermions is discussed in section
5

The most general correlation functions in these CFTs contain both local operators
O(z)
with support on a point
z\in\Sigma
and defects
{\cal L}(\gamma)
supported on oriented lines
\gamma\subset\Sigma
. The line
\gamma
might be either closed or open; in the latter case, some suitable ‘defect starting’ and ‘defect ending’ operators should be inserted at the endpoints. A line defect
{\cal L}
is topological if all correlation functions with an insertion
{\cal L}(\gamma)
are invariant under continuous deformations of the support line
\gamma
, provided that no other defect or point-like operator is crossed in the deformation. More generally, one can allow for
k
-junction operators (
k=1,2,3,\ldots
), i.e. point-like operators attached to
k
topological defect lines, and consider correlation functions with the insertion of networks of defects.

The holomorphic and anti-holomorphic stress-energy tensor operators

and
\tilde{T}(\bar{z})
are always ‘transparent’ to any topological defect
{\cal L}
, in the sense that a correlation function does not change when
{\cal L}
is moved across the support of a

{\cal L}
, it might happen that there are other holomorphic and/or antiholomorphic fields

or

and
\tilde{T}(z)

{\cal L}
. The algebras

and
\tilde{\cal A}

and

{\cal L}
. It follows that both
{\cal A}
and
\tilde{\cal A}
always contain a copy of the Virasoro algebra

and

at central charges

.

Usually, in the definition of a fusion category one requires the number of isomorphism classes of simple objects to be finite. In physics, and in particular for topological defects in non-rational CFTs, one often needs to drop this condition. We will anyway use the term fusion category in these cases as well. We will in all cases require that the quantum dimension
\langle{\cal L}\rangle
is finite. By unitarity and uniqueness of the vacuum, one has
\langle{\cal L}\rangle\geq 1
, and this implies that only a finite number of simple defects appear in the decomposition of any fusion product

A topological defect is

if it is simple and if
{\cal L}{\cal L}^{*}={\cal I}

A simple example of fusion category of topological defects is the one associated with a finite group
G

{\cal L}_{g}

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{g}{\cal L}_{h}={\cal L}_{gh}\ ,

-twisted

-twining partition function on the torus (a generalized McKay-Thompson series)
T_{g,h}
, corresponding to inserting a defect
{\cal L}_{h}
along the space-like circle and a defect
{\cal L}_{g}
along the Euclidean time circle, and given by

(3.2)

T_{g,h}(\tau):=\Tr_{V^{\natural}_{g}}(q^{L_{0}-1}h)\ ,

where
V^{\natural}_{g}
is the
g
-twisted sector of
V^{\natural}
. The generalized Monstrous moonshine conjecture, proved in
[
30
,
31
,
32
]
, states that all such
T_{g,h}
either vanihs or are Hauptmoduls for some genus zero group
\Gamma_{g,h}
. Notice that
T_{h}=T_{1,h}
, so that the standard Monstrous moonshine conjecture is a particular case of the generalized one when we restrict to
g=1
.

It is natural to generalize this idea and define the
defect McKay-Thompson series

T_{\cal L}
as the torus partition function with the insertion of any (possibly non-invertible) topological defect
{\cal L}
along a space-like torus. The series is given by

(3.3)

T_{\cal L}(\tau):=\Tr_{V^{\natural}_{g}}(q^{L_{0}-1}\hat{\cal L})\ ,

where
\hat{\cal L}:V^{\natural}\to V^{\natural}
is the linear operator associated with
{\cal L}
. Such functions were considered in
[
17
]
, where the series
T_{\cal L}
for a non-invertible topological defect
{\cal L}
was also computed. More examples were computed in
[
18
]
.

In this section, we provide a general formula for a class of topological defects known as duality defects, and that includes the case studied in
[
17
]
and some of the examples in
[
18
]
. Let
g\in\mathbb{M}
be a Fricke non-anomalous element of order
N
. This means that the corresponding McKay-Thompson series
T_{g}(\tau)
is invariant, with trivial multiplier, under the group
\Gamma_{0}(N)+N
, i.e. the extension of the group

(3.4)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{0}(N):=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\mid c\equiv 0\mod N\right\}\ ,

by the Fricke involution
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&-1/\sqrt{N}\\
1/\sqrt{N}&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in SL_{2}(\mathbb{R})
. The fact that the multiplier is trivial implies that the group
\langle g\rangle\cong\mathbb{Z}_{N}
is not anomalous, so that the orbifold
V^{\natural}/\langle g\rangle
is a well-defined holomorphic VOA of central charge
c=24
. Furthermore, one can prove that, for a non-anomalous
g
, the orbifold
V^{\natural}/\langle g\rangle
is isomorphic to
V^{\natural}
if and only if
T_{g}
is invariant under the Fricke involution, i.e.

(3.5)

T_{g}(-1/N\tau)=T_{g}(\tau)\ ,

see
[
33
]
.
On general grounds
[
11
]
, one expects this ‘self-orbifold’ property to be related to the existence of a topological defect
\mathcal{N}_{g}=\mathcal{N}_{g}^{*}
(a ‘duality defect’ for
g
) such that

(3.6)

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{g}^{2}=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}{\cal L}_{g^{k}}\ ,\qquad\mathcal{N}_{g}{\cal L}_{g^{k}}=\mathcal{N}_{g}={\cal L}_{g^{k}}\mathcal{N}_{g}

where
{\cal L}_{g^{k}}
are the invertible defects related to the automorphisms
g^{k}
.
The fusion category generated by the defects
\mathcal{N}_{g}
and
{\cal L}_{g^{k}}
is a particular example of a Tambara-Yamagami category, whose properties were studied in
[
19
]
. In general, a Tambara-Yamagami category
TY(A)
is generated by a non-anomalous abelian group
A
of invertible defects
{\cal L}_{a}
,
a\in A
, as well as a duality defect
\mathcal{N}=\mathcal{N}^{\ast}
such that
\mathcal{N}^{2}=\sum_{a\in A}{\cal L}_{a}
. Our goal is to provide an explicit general formula for the defect McKay-Thompson series

(3.7)

\displaystyle T_{\mathcal{N}_{g}}(\tau)=\Tr_{V^{\natural}}(q^{L_{0}-\frac{c}{24}}\hat{\mathcal{N}}_{g})\ ,

for all Tambara-Yamagami categories
TY(\mathbb{Z}_{N})
associated with a (Fricke, non-anomalous) cyclic subgroup
\mathbb{Z}_{N}
of the Monster. An analogous classification of the duality defects for the cyclic groups of the
E_{8}
lattice VOA was given in
[
34
]
.

Let
(V^{\natural})^{\langle g\rangle}\subset V^{\natural}
be the
g
-fixed subVOA of
V^{\natural}
. For a non-anomalous symmetry group
\langle g\rangle\cong\mathbb{Z}_{N}
, it is known
[
35
,
36
]
that
(V^{\natural})^{\langle g\rangle}\subset V^{\natural}
is strongly rational and has
N^{2}
irreducible ordinary modules
V_{n,m}
,
n,m\in\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}
with conformal weights

(3.8)

\Delta_{V_{n,m}}=\frac{nm}{N}\mod\mathbb{Z}\ ,

and satisfying the group-like fusion rules

(3.9)

V_{i,j}\otimes V_{k,l}\cong V_{i+k,j+l}\ ,\qquad i,j,k,l\in\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}\ .

More precisely,
V_{n,m}
is the
g=e^{2\pi i\frac{m}{N}}
eigenspace in the
g^{n}
-twisted sector:

(3.10)

\displaystyle V_{n,m}:=\{v\in V_{g^{n}}\mid g(v)=e^{2\pi i\frac{m}{N}}v\}\ ,\qquad n,m\in\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}\ ,

where
V_{g^{n}}
is the irreducible
g^{n}
-twisted of
V^{\natural}
. The action of
g
on the
g^{1}
-twisted sector
V_{g}
is defined by

(3.11)

\displaystyle g_{\rvert V_{g}}=e^{2\pi iL_{0}}\ ,

while on a generic
g^{n}
-twisted sector it is defined in such a way that (
3.9
) hold. The character of the module
V_{n,m}
can be written as

(3.12)

\displaystyle\Tr_{V_{n,m}}(q^{L_{0}-1})=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}e^{-\frac{2\pi imk}{N}}T_{g^{n},g^{k}}(\tau)\ ,

in terms of the generalized McKay-Thompson series

(3.13)

\displaystyle T_{g^{n},g^{k}}(\tau)=\Tr_{V_{g^{n}}}(g^{k}q^{L_{0}-1})\ .

The latter can be obtained from the ordinary McKay-Thompson series
T_{e,g^{k}}\equiv T_{g^{k}}
using the modular transformations

(3.14)

T_{g}\left(\frac{a\tau+b}{c\tau+d}\right)=T_{g^{c},g^{d}}(\tau)\ ,\qquad\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\ ,

(this formula is modified when the group
\langle g\rangle
is anomalous). For Fricke elements, by (
3.5
) and (
3.14
) we have the relation

(3.15)

T_{g,e}(\tau)=T_{e,g}(\tau/N)=q^{-1/N}+O(q^{1/N})\ ,

which implies that the
g
-twisted sector
V_{g}
contains a single operator of conformal weight
1-1/N
.

We can decompose both
V^{\natural}
and of the orbifold VOA
V^{\natural}/\langle g\rangle
in terms of modules of the common subVOA
(V^{\natural})^{\langle g\rangle}
. In particular,
V^{\natural}
contains all untwisted modules for any eigenvalue of
g

(3.16)

\displaystyle V^{\natural}=\oplus_{m\in\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}}V_{0,m}\ ,

while
V^{\natural}/\langle g\rangle
is given by the
g
-invariant twisted and untwisted sectors

(3.17)

\displaystyle V^{\natural}/\langle g\rangle=\oplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}}V_{n,0}\ .

Notice that, by (
3.8
), the conformal weights of
V^{\natural}/\langle g\rangle
are all integral, as expected for a non-anomalous
g
.

In
[
33
]
it was proved that, if
T_{g}
is Fricke invariant, then there is an isomorphism of
(V^{\natural})^{\langle g\rangle}
modules

(3.18)

\displaystyle f_{2}:V_{n,m}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}V_{m,n}\ ,\qquad n,m\in\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}\ ,

that induces an isomorphism of the VOAs

(3.19)

\displaystyle f_{2}:V^{\natural}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}V^{\natural}/\langle g\rangle\ .

The linear operator
\hat{\mathcal{N}}_{g}:V^{\natural}\to V^{\natural}
vanishes on the modules
(\hat{\mathcal{N}}_{g})_{\rvert V_{0,m}}=0
for
m\neq 0\mod N
, while
(\hat{\mathcal{N}}_{g})_{\rvert V_{0,0}}=\sqrt{N}f_{2}
. Therefore, the defect McKay-Thompson series
T_{\mathcal{N}_{g}}
is given by

(3.20)

T_{\mathcal{N}_{g}}(\tau)=\Tr_{V^{\natural}}(q^{L_{0}-\frac{c}{24}}\hat{\mathcal{N}}_{g})=\sqrt{N}\Tr_{V_{0,0}}(q^{L_{0}-\frac{c}{24}}f_{2})\ .

In order to compute
T_{\mathcal{N}_{g}}
, we need some more details about the isomorphism
f_{2}
.

Let us consider the lattice VOA
W^{L}
based on the even lattice
L=\sqrt{2N}\mathbb{Z}
of rank
1
. Its modules
W^{L}_{l}
,
l\in L^{\vee}/L\cong\mathbb{Z}/2N\mathbb{Z}
, have conformal weights

(3.21)

\Delta_{W^{L}_{l}}=\frac{l^{2}}{4N}\mod\mathbb{Z}\ ,\qquad l\in\mathbb{Z}/2N\mathbb{Z}\ .

We consider the product VOA

(3.22)

\displaystyle\tilde{V}_{0,0,0}:=W^{L}\otimes(V^{\natural})^{\langle g\rangle}

and its modules

(3.23)

\displaystyle\tilde{V}_{l,n,m}:=W^{L}_{l}\otimes V_{n,m}\ .

By comparing (
3.8
) and (
3.21
), it is clear that
\tilde{V}_{2n,n,-n}
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}
, have integral conformal weights. In particular, by (
3.15
), the lowest weight vectors in the modules
V_{1,-1}
and
V_{-1,1}
have conformal weight
1-\frac{1}{N}
, while the lowest vectors in
W^{L}_{2}
and
W^{L}_{-2}
have weight
\frac{1}{N}
. This implies that the lowest weight operators in
V_{2,1,-1}
and
V_{-2,-1,1}
are currents
J^{+}
and
J^{-}
of conformal weight
1
. Therefore, one can consider the VOA

(3.24)

\displaystyle\tilde{V}:=\oplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}}\tilde{V}_{2n,n,-n}\ ,

that is a simple current extension of
\tilde{V}_{0,0,0}
. The
\tilde{V}
-modules are given by

(3.25)

\displaystyle\oplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}\end{subarray}}\tilde{V}_{2n-l,n,l-n}\ ,\qquad l\in\mathbb{Z}/2N\mathbb{Z}\ .

The operators of weight
1
in
\tilde{V}
are given by the current
H\in\tilde{V}_{0,0,0}\cong W^{L}\otimes(V^{\natural})^{\langle g\rangle}
as well as
J^{+}\in V_{2,1,-1}
and
J^{-}\in V_{-2,-1,1}
. Together, they generate a
\hat{su}(2)_{N}
affine Kac-Moody algebra. The module
\oplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}\end{subarray}}\tilde{V}_{2n-l,n,l-n}
is therefore a module also for this
\hat{su}(2)_{N}
algebra. In particular, the eigenvalues of the current zero mode
H_{0}
on
\tilde{V}_{2n-l,n,l-n}
are contained in
2n-l+2N\mathbb{Z}
. The
SU(2)
group generated by the currents’ zero modes acts by vertex algebra automorphisms on the extended VOA
\oplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}}\tilde{V}_{2n,n,-n}
. This group contains an element
f
whose adjoint action on the currents is

(3.26)

\displaystyle(\mathrm{ad}\ f)(H)=-H\qquad(\mathrm{ad}\ f)(J^{\pm})=J^{\mp}\ .

The action of
f
on each
\hat{su}(2)_{N}
-module
\oplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}\end{subarray}}\tilde{V}_{2n-l,n,l-n}
reverses the
H_{0}
eigenvalues, so that it must map each component
\tilde{V}_{2n-l,n,l-n}
to
\tilde{V}_{l-2n,l-n,n}
. In particular,
f
restricts to an automorphism of the subalgebra
\tilde{V}_{0,0,0}=W^{L}\otimes(V^{\natural})^{\langle g\rangle}
, and it is easy to see that
f_{\rvert\tilde{V}_{0,0,0}}
factorizes as a tensor product
f_{\rvert\tilde{V}_{0,0,0}}=f_{1}\otimes f_{2}
of automorphisms
f_{1}\in\Aut(W^{L})
and
f_{2}\in\Aut((V^{\natural})^{\langle g\rangle})
. Because
f_{1}\otimes f_{2}
induces an isomorphism

(3.27)

\displaystyle f_{1}\otimes f_{2}:\tilde{V}_{2n-l,n,l-n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}\tilde{V}_{l-2n,l-n,n}\ ,\qquad l\in\mathbb{Z}/2N\mathbb{Z},\ n\in\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}\ ,

of
W^{L}\otimes(V^{\natural})^{\langle g\rangle}
modules, it follows that
f_{2}
induces an isomorphism

(3.28)

\displaystyle f_{2}:V_{n,m}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}V_{m,n}\ ,\qquad n,m\in\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}\ ,

of
(V^{\natural})^{\langle g\rangle}
-modules, compatible with fusion. By applying the map
f_{2}
to
V^{\natural}=\oplus_{m\in\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}}V_{0,m}
we obtain an isomorphism with
V^{\natural}/\langle g\rangle=\oplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}}V_{n,0}
. Therefore,
f_{2}
is the automorphism of
(V^{\natural})^{g}
appearing in (
3.20
).

In order to compute the trace
\Tr_{V_{0,0}}(q^{L_{0}-1}f_{2})
, it is useful to first consider

(3.29)

\Tr_{\tilde{V}}(fq^{L_{0}-\frac{25}{24}}).

Let us decompose
\tilde{V}
into eigenspaces for the current zero mode
H_{0}
:

(3.30)

\displaystyle\tilde{V}=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}U_{\frac{n}{\sqrt{2N}}}\otimes V_{n,-n}\ ,

where
U_{\lambda}
,
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
denotes a module for the Heisenberg algebra generated by the modes of the
u(1)
current
H(z)
. Now, because of the action
(\mathrm{ad}\ f)(H)=-H
, the isomorphism
f
must map each
U_{\frac{n}{\sqrt{2N}}}\otimes V_{n,-n}
to
U_{\frac{-n}{\sqrt{2N}}}\otimes V_{-n,n}
, so that the only sector that contributes to the trace (
3.29
) is the one with
n=0
. Thus, we have

(3.31)

\displaystyle\Tr_{\tilde{V}}(fq^{L_{0}-\frac{25}{24}})=\Tr_{U_{0}\otimes V_{0,0}}(fq^{L_{0}-\frac{25}{24}})=\Tr_{U_{0}}(f_{1}q^{L_{0}-\frac{1}{24}})\Tr_{V_{0,0}}(f_{2}q^{L_{0}-\frac{24}{24}})\ .

Notice, in particular, that
f_{1}\in\Aut(W^{L})
preserves the Heisenberg subalgebra
U_{0}\subset W_{L}
. We obtain

(3.32)

\displaystyle\Tr_{V_{0,0}}(f_{2}q^{L_{0}-1})=\frac{\Tr_{\tilde{V}}(fq^{L_{0}-\frac{25}{24}})}{\Tr_{U_{0}}(f_{1}q^{L_{0}-\frac{1}{24}})}\ .

Now, we compute the numerator and denominator on the right-hand side. The denominator is simply given by

(3.33)

\displaystyle\Tr_{U_{0}}(f_{1}q^{L_{0}-\frac{1}{24}})=q^{-\frac{1}{24}}\frac{1}{\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1+q^{n})}=\frac{\eta(\tau)}{\eta(2\tau)}\ .

To compute the numerator, we use the fact that
f
is conjugate, within the group
SU(2)
, of an element
f^{\prime}=e^{\pi iH_{0}}
with adjoint action

(3.34)

\displaystyle(\mathrm{ad}\ f^{\prime})(H)=H\qquad(\mathrm{ad}\ f^{\prime})(J^{\pm})=-J^{\pm}\ ,

so that

(3.35)

\displaystyle\Tr_{\tilde{V}}(fq^{L_{0}-\frac{25}{24}})=\Tr_{\tilde{V}}(f^{\prime}q^{L_{0}-\frac{25}{24}}).

We have

(3.36)

\displaystyle\Tr_{\tilde{V}}(f^{\prime}q^{L_{0}-\frac{25}{24}})=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}}\Tr_{\tilde{V}_{2n,n,-n}}(f^{\prime}q^{L_{0}-\frac{25}{24}})=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}}\Tr_{W^{L}_{2n}}(f^{\prime}q^{L_{0}-\frac{1}{24}})\Tr_{V_{n,-n}}(q^{L_{0}-\frac{24}{24}})\ .

It is easy to compute

(3.37)

\displaystyle\Tr_{W^{L}_{2n}}(f^{\prime}q^{L_{0}-\frac{1}{24}})=\frac{\Theta_{\frac{2n}{\sqrt{2N}}+L}(\tau,\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{N}{2}})}{\eta(\tau)}\ ,

where

(3.38)

\displaystyle\Theta_{\frac{l}{\sqrt{2N}}+L}(\tau,z):=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{2\pi iz(\frac{l}{\sqrt{2N}}+k\sqrt{2N})}q^{\frac{(l+2kN)^{2}}{4N}}\ .

is the theta series of the translated lattice
\frac{l}{\sqrt{2N}}+L=\frac{l}{\sqrt{2N}}+\sqrt{2N}\mathbb{Z}
.
In particular,

\displaystyle\Theta_{\frac{2n}{\sqrt{2N}}+L}(\tau,\tfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{\tfrac{N}{2}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}(-1)^{n+kN}q^{\frac{(n+kN)^{2}}{N}}=q^{\frac{n^{2}}{N}}(-1)^{n}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}(-1)^{kN}q^{k^{2}N+2nk}

(3.39)

\displaystyle=q^{\frac{n^{2}}{N}}(-1)^{n}\theta_{3}(2N\tau,2n\tau+N/2)\ ,

in terms of the Jacobi theta function
\theta_{3}(\tau,z)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}q^{\frac{k^{2}}{2}}e^{2\pi izk}
.
We conclude that

(3.40)

T_{\mathcal{N}_{g}}(\tau)=\sqrt{N}\frac{\eta(2\tau)}{\eta(\tau)^{2}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}}\Theta_{\frac{2n}{\sqrt{2N}}+L}(\tau,\tfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{\tfrac{N}{2}})\Tr_{V_{n,-n}}(q^{L_{0}-1})\ .

For example, for an element
g
in class
2A
of the Monster, we have

(3.41)

\displaystyle\Tr_{V_{0,0}}(q^{L_{0}-1})=\frac{T_{e,e}(\tau)+T_{e,g}(\tau)}{2}=\frac{J(\tau)+T_{2A}(\tau)}{2}

(3.42)

\displaystyle\Tr_{V_{1,1}}(q^{L_{0}-1})=\frac{T_{g,e}(\tau)-T_{g,g}(\tau)}{2}=\frac{T_{2A}(\frac{\tau}{2})-T_{2A}(\frac{\tau+1}{2})}{2}

so that

\displaystyle T_{\mathcal{N}_{2A}}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{2}\frac{\eta(2\tau)}{\eta(\tau)^{2}}\Bigl(\theta_{3}(4\tau)\frac{J(\tau)+T_{2A}(\tau)}{2}-\theta_{2}(4\tau)\frac{T_{2A}(\frac{\tau}{2})-T_{2A}(\frac{\tau+1}{2})}{2}\Bigr)

(3.43)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{2}\Bigl(\frac{1}{q}+91886q+8498776q^{2}+301112552q^{3}+6338608848q^{4}+\ldots\Bigr)\ .

The result coincides with the one given in
[
17
]
.
Similarly, for
g
in class
3A
, we have

(3.44)

\displaystyle T_{\mathcal{N}_{3A}}(\tau)=\sqrt{3}\Bigl(\frac{1}{q}+48808q+3802016q^{2}+118187964q^{3}+2227681632q^{4}+\ldots\Bigr)\ .

4
Moonshine, BKM algebras, and string theory

The discussions in sections
2
and
3
and the results in
[
17
]
and
[
18
]
, yield the following question:

Problem 1:

Determine the fusion category
\mathcal{C}_{Vir}(V^{\natural})
of topological defects of
V^{\natural}
commuting with the Virasoro algebra and determine the defect Mc-Kay-Thompson series
T_{\cal L}
for all objects in this category.

Unfortunately, the resolution of this problem seems to be out of reach at the moment. The main reason is that the Virasoro algebra at
c=24
is not rational. This means that, strictly speaking, the definition we proposed in section
2
cannot be used in this case. In particular, because of the lack of rationality, one expects the putative ‘fusion category’ of defects preserving only
Vir
to include infinitely many simple objects.

One way to approach this problem would be to study the fusion categories
\mathcal{C}_{W}(V^{\natural})
for all strongly rational subVOAs
W
that are conformally embedded in
V^{\natural}
. While finding all such embedded subalgebras
W
is by itself a very difficult open problem, it would provide at least a well posed mathematical question.

Even if we were able to perform such a classification, we have reasons to expect that many simple defects in
\mathcal{C}_{Vir}(V^{\natural})
would not be included in any
\mathcal{C}_{W}(V^{\natural})
for any rational
W
. For example, the fact that
V^{\natural}
can be obtained as a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-orbifold
V^{\natural}=V_{\Lambda}/\langle\iota\rangle
of the lattice VOA
V_{\Lambda}
based on the Leech lattice
\Lambda
, suggests that there should be a continuum of simple topological defects
{\cal L}_{\vec{\theta}}
parametrized by a collection of real parameter
\vec{\theta}=(\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\ldots)
. In order to see this, recall that the group of automorphisms
\Aut(V_{\Lambda})
contains a subgroup of inner automorphisms
U(1)^{24}
, generated by the current zero modes. Let
\mathcal{W}_{\vec{\theta}}
,
\vec{\theta}\in(\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z})^{24}
denote the invertible topological defect of
V_{\Lambda}
corresponding to such inner automorphisms. Taking the orbifold of
V_{\Lambda}
by the involution
\iota
projects all currents of
V_{\Lambda}
out, and only the finite subgroup
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{24}\subset U(1)^{24}
of symmetries commuting with
\iota
survives as a collection of invertible topological defects of
V^{\natural}
. However, for each
\vec{\theta}\in(\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z})^{24}
, the superposition

(4.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{\vec{\theta}}+\mathcal{W}_{-\vec{\theta}}

commutes with
\iota
, and gives rise to a non-invertible topological defect
{\cal L}_{\vec{\theta}}
in
V^{\natural}
. As far as we know, infinite fusion categories containing such continuous families of simple objects have not been studied in detail so far. For generic values of the continuous parameters
\vec{\theta}
, we expect the fusion products
({\cal L}_{\vec{\theta}})^{n}
of the defect
{\cal L}_{\vec{\theta}}
with itself to give rise to new simple defects at every
n
– this is what happens for the invertible defect
\mathcal{W}_{\vec{\theta}}
in
V_{\Lambda}
at generic
\vec{\theta}
. This implies that
{\cal L}_{\vec{\theta}}
cannot be contained in any fusion category with finitely many simple objects, and in particular in any
\mathcal{C}_{W}(V^{\natural})
for rational
W
.

The appearance of continuous families of defects in a VOA is not surprising, given that this can happen already at the level of invertible defects. In general, it is known that the group of automorphisms
\Aut(V)
of a holomorphic VOA
V
contains a normal Lie group
\Aut_{in}(V)\subseteq\Aut(V)
of inner automorphisms generated by the current zero modes, so that the quotient
\Aut(V)/\Aut_{in}(V)
(the group of outer automorphisms) is finite. It is natural to wonder whether some analogous result holds for fusion categories of topological defects. In fact, physics arguments based on a generalization of Noether theorem
[
37
]
, suggest that the existence of a continuous
1
-parameter family of topological defects
{\cal L}_{\theta}
, satisfying
{\cal L}_{\theta}{\cal L}_{\theta^{\prime}}={\cal L}_{\theta+\theta^{\prime}}
and
{\cal L}_{\theta}^{\ast}={\cal L}_{-\theta}
, is necessarily associated with a conserved current
J(z)\in\mathcal{H}_{{\cal L}^{\prime}}
, where
{\cal L}^{\prime}
is a subobject of
{\cal L}_{\theta}{\cal L}_{\theta}^{*}
for all
\theta
. Furthermore, any two defects
{\cal L}_{\theta}
and
{\cal L}_{\theta^{\prime}}
in the family can be obtained from each other by inserting a suitable ‘exponentiation’
e^{\int_{\gamma}J(z)dz}
on the support
\gamma
of the defect. These arguments show that continuous families of topological defects are, in a sense, the direct analog of a Lie group of inner automorphisms. If a proper notion of ‘quotienting out the continuous families of defect’ existed, analogous to the quotient
\Aut(V)/\Aut_{in}(V)
by the group of inner automorphisms, then one could focus in determining the ‘quotient category’, which might be a feasible task. Unfortunately, we are not sure whether one can make sense of these speculations.

Finally, even if we were able to solve problem 1 in a satisfactory way, it is unlikely that the answer would provide a generalization of the Monstrous moonshine conjecture. Indeed, the results of
[
17
]
and
[
18
]
show that some McKay-Thompson series
T_{\cal L}
associated with non-invertible defects are not invariant under any genus zero subgroup of
SL_{2}(\mathbb{R})
. Even when the invariance subgroup is genus zero, the series
T_{\cal L}
might not necessarily be the Hauptmodul. These remarks suggest a second open question:

Problem 2:

Determine the largest fusion category (or categories)
\mathcal{C}^{MS}_{Vir}(V^{\natural})
of topological defects of
V^{\natural}
exhibiting ‘Moonshine properties’, i.e. such that for all simple
{\cal L}\in\mathcal{C}^{MS}_{Vir}(V^{\natural})
the McKay-Thompson series

(4.2)

T_{\cal L}(\tau)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Tr_{V^{\natural}_{n}}(\hat{\cal L})q^{n-1}=\langle{\cal L}\rangle q^{-1}+O(q)

is a Hauptmodul for a discrete genus zero subgroup
\Gamma_{\cal L}\subset SL_{2}(\mathbb{R})
(and explain why the category has this property!).

A priori, we do not know whether a single fusion category containing all topological defects of ‘Moonshine type’ exist, so we have to allow for the possibility of having different maximal fusion categories with this property. We are particularly interested in categories that contain all invertible defects
{\cal L}_{g}\in\mathbb{M}
. Notice that we cannot require the McKay-Thompson series
T_{\cal L}
to be a Hauptmodul when the defect
{\cal L}
is non-simple: for example, this is not true even for sums
{\cal L}={\cal L}_{g}+{\cal L}_{h}
of invertible topological defects with
g,h\in\mathbb{M}
.

We have been quite vague with the definition of the invariance group
\Gamma_{\cal L}
. By definition, it is always true that
T_{\cal L}(\tau)
is invariant under
\tau\to\tau+k
if and only if
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. One might want to put some further constraints on
\Gamma_{\cal L}
, for example requiring it to be commensurable with
SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
(i.e. such that
\Gamma_{\cal L}\cap SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
has finite index both in
\Gamma_{\cal L}
and in
SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
) or a congruence subgroup, i.e. to contain the principal congruence group

\Gamma(N)=\{\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\mid a,d\equiv 1\mod N,\ b,c\equiv 0\mod N\}

at some finite level
N
. In the latter case,
\Gamma_{{\cal L}}
would be a group of ‘moonshine type’, as defined in
[
16
]
. Because there is a finite number of genus zero congruence subgroups of
SL_{2}(\mathbb{R})
, then there is a finite number of possible defect McKay-Thompson series. This suggests that the category of defects in Problem 2 might contain a finite number of simple objects, and therefore much more manageable than the one in Problem 1. Notice, however, that in principle there might be many (maybe even infinitely many?) distinct simple defects with the same McKay-Thompson series.

A sufficient condition for the group
\Gamma_{\cal L}
to be a congruence subgroup is that the subalgebra
(V^{\natural})^{{\cal L}}
preserved by
{\cal L}
is rational. In this case, we can consider
{\cal L}
as an object in the fusion category of topological defects preserving
(V^{\natural})^{\cal L}
; such category contains only finitely many simple defects. Each McKay-Thompson series in this category is a linear combination of characters for the algebra
(V^{\natural})^{\cal L}
, so it is necessarily invariant under some principal congruence subgroup
\Gamma(N)
for some level
N
.

By definition (
4.2
), the series
T_{\cal L}
are not normalized when
{\cal L}
is not invertible. Furthermore, their Fourier coefficients are not necessarily rational, as the examples in section
3
show. For a duality defect
\mathcal{N}_{g}
, and more generally for defects belonging to a Tambara-Yamagami fusion category, the rescaling

(4.3)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\langle\mathcal{N}_{g}\rangle}T_{\mathcal{N}_{g}}(\tau)=q^{-1}+\ldots\ ,

gives a modular function with rational (in fact, integral) coefficients and normalized so that the pole at
\infty
has residue
1
. For more exotic fusion categories, the corresponding McKay-Thompson series is expected to be genuinely irrational, i.e. not just an irrational rescaling of a rational function. Thus, considering defect McKay-Thompson series might provide examples of irrational Hauptmoduls.

The possibility of modular functions
T_{\cal L}
with irrational coefficients is in sharp contrast with the results with the series
T_{g}
associated with
g\in\Aut(V^{\natural})
, that are known to have integral coefficients for all

. This property can be seen as a consequence of the existence of a self-dual integral form for the Monster module
V^{\natural}

[
38
]
. This means that there exists a VOA

defined over the ring of integers with a self-dual invariant non-degenerate bilinear form, and such that

reproduces the usual FLM VOA

over the complex numbers. Furthermore, the Monster group is a symmetry of this integral form preserving the bilinear form. Based on this observation, Carnahan and Urano formulate a conjectured encompassing both Conway and Norton’s Monstrous moonshine and Borcherds-Ryba modular moonshine
[
39
]
.

This leads us to consider the following question:

Problem 3:

Determine the largest fusion category
\mathcal{C}_{Vir}(V^{\natural}_{\mathbb{Z}})
of topological defects of
V^{\natural}
that restrict to
V^{\natural}_{\mathbb{Z}}
, i.e. such that the linear map
\hat{\cal L}:V^{\natural}\to V^{\natural}
is the extension by
\mathbb{C}
-linearity of a
\mathbb{Z}
-linear endomorphism
\hat{\cal L}_{\mathbb{Z}}:V^{\natural}_{\mathbb{Z}}\to V^{\natural}_{\mathbb{Z}}

Because a topological defect acts on the vacuum

of

by a rescaling
\hat{\cal L}|0\rangle=\langle{\cal L}\rangle|0\rangle
, it follows that all defects in
\mathcal{C}_{Vir}(V^{\natural}_{\mathbb{Z}})
must have integral quantum dimension – it is an integral fusion category.

Notice that Carnahan and Urano’s proposal would extend very naturally to the fusion category
\mathcal{C}_{Vir}(V^{\natural}_{\mathbb{Z}})
, while no such generalization seems possible for more general topological defects. Based on this observation, it is very tempting to speculate that the category
\mathcal{C}_{Vir}(V^{\natural}_{\mathbb{Z}})
is closely related (or, possibly, coincides?) with one of the Moonshine categories

in Problem 2.

As we will see in section
5

, there is a suitable even self-dual lattice

in the space of

commuting with the

induces a lattice endomorphism. We do not know whether there is an integral form

of

with an invariant self-dual integral bilinear form; if it exists, it is natural to conjecture that the lattice

is contained in the twisted module of

the analogous version of Problem 3 could be formulated for the superMoonshine module.

Problems 2 and 3 have the potential to shed a new light on the meaning of the Moonshine conjectures, especially the genus zero properties of McKay-Thompson series. In particular, if (some version of) the proof of Monstrous moonshine can be extended to a fusion category of topological defects, then we would expect such category to be of ‘Moonshine type’, as described in problem 2.

Borcherds’ proof of Monstrous moonshine
[
29
]
is based on the construction of an infinite dimensional Lie algebra

(a Borcherds-Kac-Moody algebra) defined in terms of the FLM module

, and that inherits the action of the Monster group as a group of algebra automorphisms. More into detail, the generators of the BKM algebras correspond to the physical states in a chiral bosonic string theory based on the holomorphic CFT

, where

is the lattice vertex algebra based on the indefinite even unimodular lattice of signature
(1,1)
. Analogous constructions, involving different BKM algebras

, has been used for the proof of generalized moonshine

While, by construction, every automorphism

of the Monster module

induces an automorphism of the BKM algebra

, it is not clear what the analogous statement would be for topological defects.

Problem 4.

Suppose that

admits a topological defect

for the BKM algebra

and a antiholomorphic free fermion. Analogous string theory models can be defined on suitable ‘CHL orbifolds’ of the

Once topological defects are considered, the following question arises naturally:

Problem 5.

Generalize the string theory construction of
[
40
,
33
]
so as to include topological defects of
V^{\natural}
.

This problem is closely related to an open problem in string theory. Indeed, the construction of
[
40
,
33
]
is based on the presence of string dualities, whose existence is due to symmetries of the two-dimensional CFT that defines the string model. In general, the dynamics of a (super)string moving in a space-time with some given topology and geometry (the target space) is described, at least perturbatively, by a two dimensional conformal field theory on the world-sheet of the string. The physical states of the string in space-time can be obtained as the cohomology classes for a suitable BRST operator on the worldsheet CFT. When the two-dimensional CFT has a group of (ordinary) global symmetries preserving the BRST operator, this leads to selection rules for the string amplitudes that hold at all orders in perturbation theory. Usually, such selection rules are associated with a gauge symmetry for the physical theory in the target space. This statement has no obvious generalization to the case of a non-invertible topological defect
\mathcal{L}
in the two-dimensional CFT on the worldsheet. Even when
\mathcal{L}
preserves the BRST operator, the selection rules for the string amplitudes seem to hold only at tree level (i.e. on the sphere), while they are in general broken at higher loop level (i.e. for worldsheets with genus higher than
0
). See for example
[
41
,
42
]
for discussions. It is not clear whether the existence of
\mathcal{L}
has any (less obvious) consequences for the space-time theory.

Theorem
1
puts strong constraints on the fusion ring of the category
\mathcal{C}_{SVir}(V^{f\natural})
. Let
K(\mathcal{C}_{SVir}(V^{f\natural}))
denote the Grothendieck ring of the fusion category
\mathcal{C}_{SVir}(V^{f\natural})
, whose elements are formal finite integral linear combinations
\sum n_{i}[{\cal L}_{i}]
,
n_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
,
\sum|n_{i}|<\infty
, of isomorphism classes
[{\cal L}_{i}]
of simple objects in
\mathcal{C}_{SVir}(V^{f\natural})
, and with product given by the fusion product of simple defects. An obvious consequence of theorem
1
is:

Corollary 2
.

There is a surjective (but not injective) ring homomorphism
K(\mathcal{C}_{SVir}(V^{f\natural}))\to\End_{\mathbb{Z}}(\Lambda)
from the Grothendieck ring of
\mathcal{C}_{SVir}(V^{f\natural})
to the ring of
\mathbb{Z}
-linear endomorphisms of the Leech lattice
\Lambda
.

Notice that, by theorem
1
, even if we restrict to the subcategory of
\mathcal{C}_{SVir}(V^{f\natural})
generated by invertible defects
{\cal L}_{g}
,
g\in Co_{0}
, the homomorphism is surjective and not injective.

There is a close (and mysterious) relation between the subgroups
H_{\Pi}\subset G\cong Co_{0}
of
V^{f\natural}
-automorphisms fixing a
4
-dimensional subspace
\Pi\subset V_{tw}(1/2)
of Ramond ground states, and the groups of symmetries of a family of non-holomorphic conformal field theories known as non-linear sigma models (NLSM) on K3. The chiral and anti-chiral algebras of NLSM on K3 contain the
N=4
superVirasoro algebra at central charges
c=\bar{c}=6
; the space of Ramond-Ramond ground states in these CFTs is always
24
-dimensional, all with positive fermion number. It was proved in
[
45
]
that if
H
is a group of symmetries of a NLSM on K3 preserving the (holomorphic and anti-holomorphic)
N=4
superconformal algebras, as well as the spectral flow isomorphism, then
H
is isomorphic to the subgroup
H_{\Pi}
of
Co_{0}=\Aut(\Lambda)
fixing a
4
-dimensional subspace
\Pi\subset\Lambda\otimes\mathbb{R}
. Vice versa, every such subgroup
H_{\Pi}
of
Co_{0}
arises as a group of symmetries of some NLSM on K3. This correspondence between symmetries of
V^{f\natural}
and symmetries of NLSM on K3 goes far beyond an isomorphism of abstract groups. It was noticed in
[
46
]
that for each such subgroup
H_{\Pi}\subset Co_{0}
, there is an (almost) perfect matching between the
g
-twining genera
\phi_{g}
(a refined version of the McKay-Thompson series) computed in
V^{f\natural}
and the ones obtained in the NLSM on K3. There is no known explanation for this coincidence.

There is a natural way to generalize this observation so as to include non-invertible topological defects. On one hand, one can consider the subcategory
\mathcal{C}_{SVir,\Pi}(V^{f\natural})
of
\mathcal{C}_{SVir}(V^{f\natural})
of topological defects
{\cal L}
that preserve a subspace
\Pi\subset V_{tw}(1/2)
. Equivalently,
\hat{\cal L}
acts on
\psi\in\Pi
in the same way as on the vacuum, i.e. by multiplication by the quantum dimension
\langle\hat{\cal L}\rangle
. This implies that the field
\psi(z)
can be moved across the line defect
{\cal L}
without changing the correlation function. By theorem
1
, there is a ring homomorphism from the Grothendieck ring of
\mathcal{C}_{SVir,\Pi}(V^{f\natural})
to a certain subring of
\End_{\mathbb{Z}}(\Lambda)
that depends on
\Pi
. The elements of this subring are all the
\mathbb{Z}
-linear maps
L:\Lambda\to\Lambda
such that, when extended to
\Lambda\otimes\mathbb{R}
by linearity, they preserve the orthogonal decomposition
\Lambda\otimes\mathbb{R}=\Pi\oplus\Pi^{\perp}
(i.e.
L(\Pi)\subseteq\Pi
and
L(\Pi^{\perp})\subseteq\Pi^{\perp}
), and whose restriction to
\Pi\subset\Lambda\otimes\mathbb{R}
give maps
\Pi\to\Pi
proportional to the identity.

Similarly, for NLSM on K3, one can consider the category
\mathsf{Top}_{\Pi}
of topological defects preserving both the holomorphic and anti-holomorphic
N=4
superVirasoro algebras, as well as the spectral flow. It was shown in
[
47
]
that there is a homomorphism between the Grothendieck ring of
\mathsf{Top}_{\Pi}
and the same subring of
\End_{\mathbb{Z}}(\Lambda)
considered above. While we have not been able yet to check whether the ‘defect twining genera’ on the two sides match, these results suggest that the mysterious relation between symmetries of
V^{f\natural}
and of NLSM on K3 extends to topological defects.

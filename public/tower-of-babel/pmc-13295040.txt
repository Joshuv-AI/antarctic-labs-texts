One-Pot Tandem Construction of Isoindolinone Derivatives under Visible-Light Photoredox Catalysis
Authors: Lifang Qi, Ying Yu, Youping Tian, Yang Shi, Huabin Wang, Xiongwei Liu, Bowen Pan, Zhen Yao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Inspired by the significance of amide bonds and C3-substituted isoindolinones in chemistry and biology, we successfully developed a visible-light photoredox-catalyzed metal-free tandem one-pot construction of C3-alkylated isoindolinones bearing an amide bond from easily accessible benzohydrazides, 2-carboxybenzaldehydes, and 4-alkyl-1,4-dihydropyridines. This facile synthetic method delivers target compounds with yields ranging from 71% to 90% across various structurally modified substrates and is also applicable to gram-level preparation. The established strategy is conducive to enriching compound candidate libraries, which further meet the research demands for discovering novel biologically active substances.

Introduction

C3-Substituted isoindolinones widely exist in a variety of bioactive and pharmaceutical molecules. The structural properties of C3 substituents significantly dominate their diverse biological and pharmacological profiles (Figure ). Accordingly, the efficient construction of such heterocyclic skeletons has long attracted extensive research attention. To date, numerous prominent advances have been achieved via multicomponent reaction strategies, including C3-(hetero)­arylation, alkylation, amination, alkynylation, esterification, phosphorylation, and cyanation of isoindolinones (Scheme ). Such multicomponent tandem processes allow rapid one-step assembly of complex molecular architectures using at least three reactants. Benefiting from high atom economy, they provide broad synthetic flexibility for the functionalization of the isoindolinone core.

Reported Methodologies for the Synthesis of Isoindolinones

Recently, we realized a visible-light-driven photocatalyst-free multicomponent reaction of 2-carboxybenzaldehydes, primary amines, and 4-alkyl-1,4-dihydropyridines that affords diverse C3-alkylated isoindolinones. Nevertheless, the biological activity of such products is relatively low. As amide bonds can effectively modulate the physicochemical and biological characteristics of parent compounds, substantial efforts have been directed toward their efficient incorporation into heterocyclic rings, thereby spurring the development of efficient and straightforward methods for introducing amide bonds into the C3-substituted isoindolinone scaffold.

In 2014 and 2018, the groups of Jang developed a Lewis acid-catalyzed radical cascade cyclization and an on-water radical addition strategy for the efficient synthesis of C3-alkylated isoindolinones bearing an amide bond (Scheme a,b). Although considerable advances have been made, efficient and operationally simple catalytic strategies to construct C3-substituted isoindolinones bearing an amide group are still in great demand. In 2019, the groups of Hu established a tin powder-promoted one-pot condensation/allylation/lactamization cascade of 2-formylbenzoic acids, hydrazides, and allyl bromides, providing facile access to such amide-containing C3-alkylated isoindolinones (Scheme c). However, this chemistry may encounter difficulties when dealing with tin powder, which is flammable, toxic upon inhalation, and environmentally unfavorable.

Synthesis of Amide-Containing C3-Alkylated Isoindolinones

As a robust strategy for constructing chemical bonds, visible-light photocatalysis is gaining escalating attention by virtue of its mild conditions, high efficiency, energy sustainability, and straightforward experimental procedures. Nevertheless, visible-light photocatalytic approaches toward amide-containing C3-substituted isoindolinones remain largely unexplored. Thus, we envisioned the synthesis of C3-substituted isoindolinones bearing an amide bond from 2-carboxybenzaldehydes, benzohydrazides, and 4-alkyl-1,4-dihydropyridines under photoredox catalysis. In continuation of our interest in photocatalyzed multicomponent reactions, herein, we report such a novel metal-free photoredox-catalyzed three-component reaction. This method efficiently furnishes C3-alkylated isoindolinones bearing an amide with high synthetic efficiency, broad substrate applicability, and excellent functional group tolerance (Scheme d). Furthermore, we investigated the biological activities of the resulting products.

Results and Discussion

Our investigation commenced with the optimization of reaction conditions using benzohydrazide (1a), 2-formylbenzoic acid (2a), and diethyl 4-isopropyl-2,6-dimethyl-1,4-dihydropyridine-3,5-dicarboxylate (3a) as model substrates. After systematic condition screening (SI), we identified the most suitable reaction system: TsOH (1.0 equiv) and Eosin Y (1.0 mol %) in dichloromethane under blue LED irradiation (20 W, r.t., 24 h). Under this protocol, the target product N-(1-isopropyl-3-oxoisoindolin-2-yl)­benzamide 4aaa was obtained in 90% isolated yield with smooth reaction performance (Table , entry 1). Systematic solvent screening showed that replacing CH2Cl2 with CHCl3, toluene, or MeOH noticeably lowered the yield of 4aaa (Table , entries 2–4), while no target substance was detected in DMSO, 1,4-dioxane, or THF (Table , entries 5–7), confirming CH2Cl2 as the optimal reaction solvent. Substituting TsOH with TFA, HCO2H, BF3·Et2O, AcOH, or PhCO2H afforded inferior results (Table , entries 8–12). Likewise, photocatalyst replacement with fac-[Ir­(ppy)3], Rhodamine B, or Methylene Blue also led to diminished product formation (Table , entries 13–15). Further optimization revealed that reaction concentration significantly affected the reaction efficiency. Diluting the reaction system decreased the yield to 75% (Table , entry 16). Meanwhile, reducing the LED light power to 15 W afforded the target product in 80% yield (Table , entry 17). Furthermore, other light sources completely inhibited the formation of 4aaa (Table , entries 18–21), further demonstrating the unique superiority of blue light in this reaction. The acid additive was essential, and no desired product could be obtained without the addition of TsOH (Table , entry 22). When the photocatalyst was omitted, the yield of target product 4aaa dropped to merely 24% (Table , entry 23). Ultimately, control reactions confirmed that both nitrogen atmosphere and light irradiation are indispensable for this reaction (Table , entries 24–25).

After establishing the optimal reaction parameters, we further investigated the substrate scope of benzohydrazides with 2-formylbenzoic acids and substrate 3a as the standard coupling partner. As shown in Table A, this synthetic strategy was successfully applied to a broad range of monosubstituted aryl amines (1b-1r), delivering the corresponding products (4baa–4raa) in 72%–89% yields. Substituent electronic effects exhibited a negligible impact on the reaction performance. A wide range of useful functionalities, including F, Br, I, CF3, and OMe, were well tolerated, which facilitates further structural elaboration. Similarly, disubstituted aryl amines 1s-1u reacted efficiently under the standard conditions, furnishing products 4saa–4uaa with yields ranging from 75% to 88%. X-ray crystallography further validated the structure of 4uaa (CCDC 2539100). 3,4,5-Trimethoxybenzohydrazide (1v) was well-tolerated under the optimal conditions, furnishing 4vaa in 85% yield. Meanwhile, benzo­[d]­[1,3]­dioxole-5-carbohydrazide (1w) efficiently transformed into the corresponding product 4waa in 81% yield.

The substrate generality of 2-formylbenzoic acids was subsequently explored (Table B). Various 4- and 5-substituted analogs 2b–2g efficiently afforded the corresponding products 4aba–4aga with yields between 71% and 82%. Moreover, 2-formyl-4,5-dimethylbenzoic acid (2h) was also compatible, giving 4aha in 78% yield. We further attempted the reactions with 2-formyl-5-nitrobenzoic acid, 4-cyanobenzohydrazide, and ethyl 4-(hydrazinecarbonyl)­benzoate, but no reaction occurred.

We further evaluated the substrate scope of various 4-alkyl-1,4-dihydropyridines (Table ). Secondary alkyl-substituted acyclic and cyclic DHPs (3b–3m) performed well in this reaction system with 1a and 2a, providing related products (4aab–4aam) in 75%–86% yields. The reaction efficiency was nearly unaffected by the structural variation of these secondary alkyl substituents. Of note, substrates 3b and 3d–3i afforded inseparable diastereomeric mixtures, whereas the alkene functionalities in 3f and 3i remained intact throughout the transformation. Moreover, this synthetic method was also applicable to 4-benzyl-1,4-dihydropyridine (3n) and benzyloxy-substituted DHP (3o), which gave the corresponding products 4aan and 4aao with yields of 76% and 78%.

Synthetic Applications

To elucidate the plausible reaction mechanism, we conducted a series of mechanistic investigations (see the ESI for details). Initially, radical scavenging tests were conducted by adding TEMPO to the model reaction. The target product 4aaa was totally inhibited (Scheme a), and the formed TEMPO radical adduct 5 was successfully identified via HRMS analysis. Such results demonstrate that the reaction proceeds through a radical process. Subsequent HRMS detection of the standard reaction system consisting of 1a, 2a, and 3a (see the SI for details) verified the in situ generation of aldimine intermediate 4aa. Furthermore, when presynthesized aldimine 4aa reacted with 3a following standard procedures, the target product 4aaa was obtained in 95% yield, further verifying the key role of 4aa as a reaction intermediate (Scheme b). Without TsOH, target product 4aaa could not be formed at all (Table , entry 22). This observation indicates that the protons released during the oxidative decomposition of 3a are insufficient to promote the activation of 4aa for subsequent radical addition. Furthermore, light/dark experiments verified that sustained light irradiation is indispensable for the formation of the target product, while no radical chain propagation was observed in the reaction system (Scheme c). Meanwhile, Stern–Volmer fluorescence quenching investigations confirmed that Hantzsch ester 3a effectively quenched the photoexcited state of eosin Y (Scheme d).

Mechanistic Studies

On the basis of the aforementioned experimental findings and relevant literature reports, a reasonable reaction mechanism is put forward in Scheme . , Initially, photoexcitation of eosin Y generates the excited state *eosin Y, which abstracts a hydrogen atom from 4-alkyl-1,4-dihydropyridines 3 to form alkyl radical I, pyridinium ion II, and eosin Y–H. Alternatively, direct visible-light irradiation of 3 can also induce homolytic C–C cleavage to afford alkyl radical I and pyridinium ion II. Meanwhile, intermediate III, formed in situ from benzohydrazide 1 and 2-carboxybenzaldehyde 2, is protonated by TsOH to yield the iminium ion IV. Alkyl radical I then adds to iminium ion IV to furnish radical cation V. Subsequent reduction of V by *Eosin Y affords intermediate VI along with Eosin Y–H. Finally, protonation of VI by Eosin Y–H gives intermediate VII, which undergoes intramolecular cyclization to furnish the desired product 4.

Proposed Reaction Mechanism

To evaluate the bioactivity of the synthesized isoindolinones, all derivatives were first screened at a single concentration. Compounds 4aag and 4aal with satisfactory inhibitory activity were further assessed for in vitro cytotoxicity against HepG2, HeLa, and A549 cancer cell lines via the MTT assay. Clinically used cisplatin was employed as the positive control, with IC50 values of 3.47 μM (HepG2), 3.32 μM (HeLa), and 7.76 μM (A549) (Scheme ). Biological assays showed both compounds markedly suppressed the proliferation of the three cell lines with IC50 values below 50.00 μM, and 4aag exhibited particularly strong cytotoxicity toward A549 cells. This isoindolinone scaffold displays promising potential for anticancer drug discovery, meriting further structural optimization and comprehensive biological investigation.

Conclusions

In conclusion, we have established a simple and efficient approach to accessing amide-functionalized C3-alkylated isoindolinones through visible-light-catalyzed multicomponent reactions. This transformation employs easily accessible starting materials, including benzohydrazides, 2-carboxybenzaldehydes, and 4-alkyl-1,4-dihydropyridines. Notably, this protocol operates under external metal-free conditions, featuring mild reaction parameters, high efficiency, broad substrate scope, excellent functional group compatibility, and easy scalability. This protocol could provide libraries of C3-alkylated isoindolinones containing an amide bond, featuring an intriguing combination of isoindolinones and amide motifs, which might facilitate the search for new bioactive entities.

Experimental Section

General Procedures for Synthesis of Isoindolinones

Into an oven-dried Schlenk tube containing a magnetic stir were added 1, 2, 3 (each 0.2 mmol), TsOH (34.4 mg, 0.2 mmol), Eosin Y (1.30 mg, 1.0 mol %), and 2.0 mL of CH2Cl2. The vessel was degassed with nitrogen three times and then tightly sealed. The mixture was stirred at room temperature under 20 W of blue LED irradiation for 24 h. The solvent was then removed in vacuo, and the crude mixture was purified by silica gel column chromatography using ethyl acetate and hexane as eluents to obtain pure product 4.

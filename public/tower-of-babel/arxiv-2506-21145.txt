Imaginary scaling
P. M. Ferreira, B. Grzadkowski, O. M. Ogreid

Abstract
Recently, a hitherto unknown class of RG-stable relations between parameters of bosonic field theories have been identified and dubbed as the r 0 r_{0} or “GOOFy” symmetries. Here, 1-loop properties of the r 0 r_{0} invariant 2-Higgs Doublet Model and a minimal symmetric model are discussed. It is concluded that the symmetry is present at the 1-loop provided the UV cut-off squared transforms non-trivially under r 0 r_{0} . The minimal model requires the presence of two real fields.



I
Introduction

Investigating symmetries of the 2-Higgs Doublet Model (2HDM) extension of the Standard Model (SM) of electro-weak interactions,
the authors of ref.
Ferreira et al. 2024
have recently noticed the existence of a new class of relations between parameters of
the 2HDM scalar potential which are stable under the renormalization group equations up to an infinite order of perturbation expansion.
It turned out the relations are not implied by hitherto known symmetries of the 2HDM, so a new class of symmetries have been hypothesized
in ref.
Ferreira et al. 2024
. The symmetry transformations, dubbed as the
r_{0}
or GOOFy symmetries (for the authors of that reference),
include mapping real bosonic fields (scalars and vectors) into purely imaginary ones. In order to verify if the new transformations constitute
genuine symmetries, we shall
investigate properties of the 1-loop effective potential of the 2HDM. In addition, we introduce, present and discuss in detail a minimal

r_{0}
-invariant scalar field model.
Interesting discussions of the
r_{0}
symmetries have recently appeared in refs.
Haber and Ferreira 2025
;
Trautner 2025
;
Pilaftsis 2025
.

III
The
r_{0}
symmetry and its consequences

In the previous publication
Ferreira et al. 2024
we identified the following relations between potential parameters of the 2HDM that are fixed points of the RG equations valid to all orders of perturbation theory in the scalar and gauge sectors:

\vec{\Lambda}=\vec{0}\hskip 8.5359pt\text{and}\hskip 8.5359ptM_{0}=0\,.

(6)

In terms of parameters of the potential (
1
) the relations read:
m_{11}^{2}+m_{22}^{2}=0
,
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}
and
\lambda_{6}=-\lambda_{7}
.
Let us define the following basis in 8-dimensional space for the real components of the two doublets,

\phi_{i}\equiv\left\{\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\phi_{3},\phi_{4},\phi_{5},\phi_{6},\phi_{7},\phi_{8}\right\}

(7)

such that

\displaystyle\Phi_{1}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}\phi_{1}+i\phi_{2}\\
\phi_{3}+i\phi_{4}\end{pmatrix},\quad\Phi_{2}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}\phi_{5}+i\phi_{6}\\
\phi_{7}+i\phi_{8}\end{pmatrix}\,.

(8)

The field transformations responsible for RGE stable relations between parameteters (
6
) have been hypothesized in
Ferreira et al. 2024
as

\displaystyle\phi_{1}\to i\phi_{6},\hskip 8.5359pt\phi_{2}\to i\phi_{5},\hskip 8.5359pt\phi_{3}\to i\phi_{8},\hskip 8.5359pt\phi_{4}\to i\phi_{7},

(9)

\displaystyle\phi_{5}\to-i\phi_{2},\hskip 8.5359pt\phi_{6}\to-i\phi_{1},\hskip 8.5359pt\phi_{7}\to-i\phi_{4},\hskip 8.5359pt\phi_{8}\to-i\phi_{3},

which imply
r_{0}\rightarrow-r_{0}
.
It has been also shown in
Ferreira et al. 2024
that in order to keep scalar and gauge kinetic terms invariant under the above transformation, the additional transformations of the coordinate
x^{\mu}
and vector fields are needed:

\begin{split}&x^{\mu}\to ix^{\mu},\quad B_{\mu}\to iB_{\mu},\\
&W_{1\mu}\to iW_{1\mu},\quad W_{2\mu}\to-iW_{2\mu},\quad W_{3\mu}\to iW_{3\mu}\,.\end{split}

(10)

Hereafter we will refer to (
9
) together with (
10
) as the
r_{0}
transformation.
Note that, by construction, the Lagrangian density
{\cal L}
is invariant upon the
r_{0}
transformation, it is easily
shown that the classical action is invariant as well.

Let us now consider a more general system that consists of a number of real scalar fields in order to find out what is the scaling for the momenta of the system that is implied by a given scaling of fields and coordinates. Relativistic quantum mechanics suggests that
x^{\mu}\to ix^{\mu}
implies
p_{\mu}\equiv-i\partial_{\mu}\to-\,i\,p_{\mu}
. In fact, in field theory the 4-momentum is given in terms of the energy-momentum tensor
\Theta_{\alpha\beta}
as

p_{\mu}\,\equiv\,\int\,d^{3}x\,\Theta_{0\mu}\,,

(11)

with

\Theta_{\alpha\beta}\,=\,\sum_{i}\,\frac{\partial\cal L}{\partial(\partial^{\alpha}\phi_{i})}\,\partial_{\beta}\,\phi_{i}\;-\;\eta_{\alpha\beta}\,\cal L\,.

(12)

The Lagrangian
{\cal L}
is invariant under the
r_{0}
transformation
(
9
-
10
), therefore
\Theta_{\alpha\beta}
is invariant as well. On the other hand

d^{3}x\to-id^{3}x
, therefore, taking into account integration limits, we confirm that indeed
p_{\mu}\to-\,i\,p_{\mu}
, in agreement with our initial intuition.

IV
Invariance of the 2HDM effective potential under the
r_{0}
transformation

In order to test the invariance under the
r_{0}
transformation of the effective potential, it is convenient to invoke its functional
definition
Jackiw 1974
;
Itzykson and Zuber 1980
;
Ramond 1981
, since it allows to formulate the potential in terms of coordinates and fields
only. Adopting e.g.
Itzykson and Zuber 1980
one finds that the 1-loop contribution to the effective potential in a generic real scalar model reads

V_{\text{eff}}(\phi_{cl})\propto\int\prod_{k}{\cal D}(\phi_{k})\exp\left\{-i\int d^{4}x\;\phi_{i}(x)\left[\Box_{x}\;\delta_{ij}+\left(M^{2}_{S}(\phi_{cl})\right)_{ij}\right]\phi_{j}(x)\right\},

(13)

where
\Box_{x}\equiv\partial_{\mu}\partial^{\mu}
and
M^{2}_{S}(\varphi_{cl})
is the scalar mass squared matrix,

\left(M_{S}^{2}\right)_{ij}\equiv\frac{\partial^{2}V_{\text{tree}}}{\partial\phi_{i}\partial\phi_{j}}\,

(14)

calculated for a given tree-level potential for constant classical field configuration
\phi_{cl}
.
The d’Alembertian is odd under
r_{0}
, i.e.
\Box_{x}\to-\Box_{x}
. Since
\phi_{i}\to\pm i\phi_{i}
, it is seen, that for both models considered in this paper the measure

\prod_{k}{\cal D}(\phi_{k})
is invariant under the
r_{0}
transformation, so we conclude that the 1-loop effective potential is indeed
r_{0}
-invariant.

Recently, within the 2HDM, 1-loop effective potential has been calculated adopting the bilinear
notation
Cao et al. 2023a
;
Cao et al. 2023b
;
Pilaftsis 2025
.
We will concentrate hereafter only on the scalar (
V_{\text{eff}}^{(S)}
) contribution to the potential that can be,
after dropping field-independent contributions, written in the following form

\displaystyle V_{\text{eff}}^{(S)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int\frac{d^{4}p_{E}}{(2\pi)^{4}}\mathbf{Tr}\left[\ln(p_{E}^{2}+M_{S}^{2})\right]

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\int\frac{d^{4}p_{E}}{(2\pi)^{4}}\left[\mathbf{Tr}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n}\left(\frac{M_{S}^{2}}{p^{2}_{E}}\right)^{n}\right]\,,

(15)

where

At the lowest order,
n=1

6

r_{0}
-odd result

\mathbf{Tr}(M_{S}^{2})=4[5\Lambda_{00}+\mathbf{tr}(\Lambda)]r_{0}.

(16)

In order to verify the transformation properties of higher order terms let us denote by
\lambda_{i}(r_{0})
an eigenvalue of
M_{S}^{2}(r_{0})

It is easy to see that there exists an eigenvalue
\lambda_{j}(r_{0})

\displaystyle\lambda_{i}(r_{0})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle r_{0}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\lambda_{i}(-r_{0})=-\lambda_{j}(r_{0}),\hskip 8.5359pt\text{and}

\displaystyle\lambda_{j}(r_{0})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle r_{0}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\lambda_{j}(-r_{0})=-\lambda_{i}(r_{0}).

(17)

So there are four pairs of eigenvalues transforming within each pair, so that the set of the eigenvalues
\{\lambda_{i}(r_{0})\}
transforms as follows:

\{\lambda_{i}(r_{0})\}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle r_{0}}}{{\longrightarrow}}-\{\lambda_{i}(r_{0})\}.

(18)

An important conclusion from the above is that

\displaystyle\mathbf{Tr}\left[\left(M_{S}^{2}\right)^{2n}\right](r_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbf{Tr}\left[\left(M_{S}^{2}\right)^{2n}\right](-r_{0}),\;\;\text{and}

\displaystyle\mathbf{Tr}\left[\left(M_{S}^{2}\right)^{2n+1}\right](r_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\mathbf{Tr}\left[\left(M_{S}^{2}\right)^{2n+1}\right](-r_{0}).

(19)

For any finite

M_{S}^{2}
could be calculated explicitly.
Indeed, considering the definition of

, eq. (
14

.
Therefore we see that terms with even/odd powers of

.
But since under the

-invariance of the

.

The

V
The toy model - 2RSM

Even though we have managed, for the 2HDM, to verify transformation properties of the
effective potential without calculating the
M_{S}^{2}
eigenvalues, hereafter we are going to present a simple, minimal toy model
Grzadkowski

which shares many properties with the 2HDM and for which the diagonalization could be done explicitly. This is a two real scalar model described by
the following Lagrangian

{\cal{L}}=\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{\mu}\phi_{1}\partial^{\mu}\phi_{1}+\partial_{\mu}\phi_{2}\partial^{\mu}\phi_{2})-V(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})\,,

(22)

with the potential

\displaystyle V^{0}(\phi_{1},\,\phi_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}m_{1}^{2}(\phi_{1}^{2}-\phi_{2}^{2})+m_{12}^{2}\phi_{1}\phi_{2}

(23)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{1}(\phi_{1}^{4}+\phi_{2}^{4})+\lambda_{3}(\phi_{1}\phi_{2})^{2}

\displaystyle+\lambda_{6}(\phi_{1}^{2}-\phi_{2}^{2})\phi_{1}\phi_{2}\,.

The model is invariant under the following
r_{0}
-like transformation

x^{\mu}\to ix^{\mu},\hskip 28.45274pt\phi_{1}\to i\phi_{2},\hskip 28.45274pt\phi_{2}\to-i\phi_{1}

(24)

It turns out that for this symmetric potential it is possible to choose a
(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})
basis such that
\lambda_{6}=0
. This is an analog of the
statement proven for the 2HDM in
Gunion and Haber 2005
;
Davidson and Haber 2005
where the authors showed that if
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}
and

\lambda_{6}=-\lambda_{7}
(
CP2
invariant quartic sector) then there there exists a basis such that all
\lambda_{i}
are real and
\lambda_{6}=\lambda_{7}=0
.
Therefore
\lambda_{6}
will be dropped from now on.
The squared mass matrix then reads

\displaystyle\left(M_{S}^{2}\right)_{ij}=

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}m_{1}^{2}+6\lambda_{1}\phi_{1}^{2}+2\lambda_{3}\phi_{2}^{2}&m_{12}^{2}+4\lambda_{3}\phi_{1}\phi_{2}\\
m_{12}^{2}+4\lambda_{3}\phi_{1}\phi_{2}&-m_{1}^{2}+6\lambda_{1}\phi_{2}^{2}+2\lambda_{3}\phi_{1}^{2}\end{pmatrix}\,.

One can notice that near the origin,
\phi_{1}=\phi_{2}=0
we have

\left(M_{S}^{2}\right)_{ij}=\begin{pmatrix}m_{1}^{2}&m_{12}^{2}\\
m_{12}^{2}&-m_{1}^{2}\end{pmatrix}+{\cal{O}}(\phi^{2})\,.

(25)

At the origin the
M_{S}^{2}
eigenvalues are
\mp\sqrt{m_{12}^{4}+m_{1}^{4}}
.
so there is a saddle point at
\phi_{1}=\phi_{2}=0
with two perpendicular directions of opposite curvature (a similar situation appears in the

r_{0}
-symmetric 2HDM where, at the origin, the Hessian has 4 positive and 4 negative eigenvalues).
This implies that there is a global minimum away from the origin and therefore the
r_{0}
symmetry is always spontaneously broken.
It turns out that there are two local minima in the toy model determined by the minimization conditions

(v_{1}^{2}-v_{2}^{2})=-m_{1}^{2}/\lambda_{1}
and
v_{1}v_{2}=-m_{12}^{2}/(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{3})

where
\langle\phi_{1,2}\rangle\equiv v_{1,2}/\sqrt{2}
.

One can also express the potential in terms of bilinear variables,

\displaystyle r_{0}\equiv\frac{1}{2}(\phi_{1}^{2}+\phi_{2}^{2})\,,\hskip 8.5359ptr_{1}\equiv\phi_{1}\phi_{2}\,,\hskip 8.5359ptr_{2}\equiv\frac{1}{2}(\phi_{1}^{2}-\phi_{2}^{2})\,.

(26)

Note that
r_{0}
,
r_{1}
and
r_{2}
are not independent:
r_{0}^{2}-r_{1}^{2}-r_{2}^{2}=0
.
Upon the
r_{0}
transformation,

(r_{0},r_{1},r_{2})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle r_{0}}}{{\longrightarrow}}(-r_{0},r_{1},r_{2})\,.

(27)

The
r_{0}
symmetry thus requires the coefficient of
r_{0}
in the potential to vanish, and
the potential may be written as

V(r^{\mu})=-M_{\mu}r^{\mu}+\Lambda_{\mu\nu}r^{\mu}r^{\nu}\,,

for
\mu,\nu=0,1,2
with
r^{\mu}\equiv(r_{0},r_{1},r_{2})
,
M^{\mu}\equiv(0,m_{12}^{2},m_{1}^{2})
,
the “Minkowski metric tensor”
\eta_{\mu\nu}=\text{diag}(1,-1,-1)

\Lambda^{\mu\nu}\equiv\begin{pmatrix}\Lambda_{00}&0&0\\
0&\Lambda_{11}&\Lambda_{12}\\
0&\Lambda_{21}&\Lambda_{22}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_{1}&0&0\\
0&\lambda_{3}&0\\
0&0&\lambda_{1}\end{pmatrix}\,.

Note that
M_{0}=0
and
\vec{\Lambda}=0
are consequences of the
r_{0}
symmetry, similar to the 2HDM case, eq. (
6

Indeed, for the toy model
M_{0}=0

means that
m_{1}^{2}+m_{2}^{2}=0
and
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}
.

Then one finds that

{\mathbf{Tr}}\left(M_{S}^{2}\right)=4(3\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{3})r^{0}

(28)

and therefore under the
r_{0}
transformation this trace is odd,

{\mathbf{Tr}}\left(M_{S}^{2}\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle r_{0}}}{{\longrightarrow}}-{\mathbf{Tr}}\left(M_{S}^{2}\right)\,,

so that we reproduce the analogous property observed in the 2HDM 1-loop effective potential.

Since in this model there are only two real degrees of freedom one can easily diagonalize the mass squared matrix and calculate
the 1-loop effective potential. It turns out that the eigenvalues of
M_{S}^{2}
can be expressed through bilinears as follows:

\displaystyle M_{1,2}^{2}(r^{\mu})

\displaystyle=2(3\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{3})r_{0}\pm\sqrt{\Delta}\,,

where

\displaystyle\Delta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(m_{1}^{2})^{2}+(m_{12}^{2})^{2}+4m_{1}^{2}(3\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{3})r_{2}+8m_{12}^{2}\lambda_{3}r_{1}

(30)

\displaystyle+16\lambda_{3}^{2}r_{0}^{2}+12(3\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{3})(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{3})r_{2}^{2}.

Note that the eigenvalues of
M_{S}^{2}
are linearly dependent on
r_{0}
. It is easy to see that they transform under the
r_{0}
symmetry
as

\displaystyle M_{1}^{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle r_{0}}}{{\longrightarrow}}-M_{2}^{2}\hskip 8.5359pt\text{and}\hskip 8.5359ptM_{2}^{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle r_{0}}}{{\longrightarrow}}-M_{1}^{2}\,.

(31)

In order to illustrate explicitly the
r_{0}
invariance at the 1-loop level we present the 1-loop effective potential adopting cut-off regularization and dropping irrelevant terms:

\displaystyle V^{\text{1-loop}}_{\text{eff}}(r^{\mu})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\Lambda_{\text{UV}}^{2}}{32\pi^{2}}\sum_{i=1,2}M_{i}^{2}(r^{\mu})

(32)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{64\pi^{2}}\sum_{i=1,2}M_{i}^{4}(r^{\mu})\left[\log\frac{M_{i}^{2}(r^{\mu})}{\Lambda_{\text{UV}}^{2}}-\frac{1}{2}\right]\,.

Note that

M_{1}^{4}+M_{2}^{4}=2\left\{[2(3\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{3})r_{0}]^{2}+\Delta\right\}\,,

(33)

so this quantity is
r_{0}
invariant. Here one can explicitly see that the 1-loop effective potential is indeed
r_{0}
-invariant for

\Lambda_{UV}^{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle r_{0}}}{{\longrightarrow}}-\Lambda_{UV}^{2}
.

The 1-loop, renormalized, effective potential can be written as

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!V_{\text{eff}}^{\text{1-loop}}(r^{\mu})

\displaystyle=\delta m_{0}^{2}r_{0}+(m_{1}^{2}+\delta m_{1}^{2})r_{2}+(m_{12}^{2}+\delta m_{12}^{2})r_{1}+(\lambda_{1}+\delta\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{3}+\delta\lambda_{3})r_{1}^{2}+2(\lambda_{1}+\delta\lambda_{1})r_{2}^{2}

(34)

VI
Summary and conclusions

In this letter we have discussed implications of the
r_{0}
symmetry for 1-loop effective potential
within 2HDM and the minimal (toy) model defined by (
23
). The novel aspect of the symmetry lies in the necessity to transform coordinates together with
variations of fields. In this letter we showed that an imaginary scaling of scalar fields is accompanied by spacetime coordinates also being scaled by imaginary factors, so that invariance of the theory at tree-level is achieved. We then showed that that procedure is consistent at 1-loop, and the effective potential
was shown to be invariant under the
r_{0}
transformation. The 1-loop effective potential has been calculated and renormalized.
For the invariance to hold the key property is that the imaginary scaling of spacetime coordinates implies an imaginary
scaling of 4-momenta, which in turn follows non-trivial transformation of the cutoff,
\Lambda_{UV}
, or renormalization,
\mu
, scales - namely

\Lambda_{UV}^{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle r_{0}}}{{\longrightarrow}}-\Lambda_{UV}^{2}
or
\mu^{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle r_{0}}}{{\longrightarrow}}-\mu^{2}
.

The existence of the
r_{0}
symmetries seems to broaden our hitherto understanding of field theories, at least for bosonic models.

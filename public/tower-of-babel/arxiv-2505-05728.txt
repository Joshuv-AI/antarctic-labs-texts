Abstract




Congruences for sums of Delannoy numbers and polynomials

Abstract.

In this paper, we apply the power-partible reduction to study arithmetic properties of sums involving Delannoy numbers
D_{k}
and polynomials
D_{k}(z)
.
Let
v\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
p
be an odd prime.
It is proved that, for any
z\in{\mathbb{Z}}\setminus\{0,-1\}
, there exist
c_{v}\in z^{-v}{\mathbb{Z}}[z]
and
\tilde{c}_{v}\in(z+1)^{-v}{\mathbb{Z}}[z]
, both free of
p
and can be determined mechanically, such that

\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}(2k+1)^{2v}D_{k}(z)\equiv c_{v}\left(\frac{-z}{p}\right)\pmod{p}

if
\gcd(p,z)=1
and

\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}(-1)^{k}(2k+1)^{2v}D_{k}(z)\equiv\tilde{c}_{v}\left(\frac{z+1}{p}\right)\pmod{p}

if
\gcd(p,z+1)=1
.
Here
(-)
denotes the Legendre symbol.

When
n
is a power of
2
, we find there exist odd integers
\rho_{v}
and even integers
\tilde{\rho}_{v}
, both independent of
n
and can be determined mechanically, such that

\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(2k+1)^{2v+1}D_{k}\equiv\rho_{v}n\pmod{n^{3}}

and

\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{k}(2k+1)^{2v+1}D_{k}\equiv\tilde{\rho}_{v}n^{2}\pmod{n^{3}}.

The case
v=1
in the last congruence confirms a conjecture of Guo and Zeng in 2012.

Keywords
: congruence; Delannoy polynomial; Delannoy number.

Mathematics Subject Classification
: 11A07, 11B65.

1
Introduction

Let
{\mathbb{N}}
and
{\mathbb{Z}}^{+}
denote the set of nonnegative integers and positive integers respectively.
The central Delannoy numbers
D_{n}
, which appear ubiquitously in enumerative combinatorics, are defined as

D_{n}=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\binom{n+k}{k}.

It is well-known that
D_{n}
is the number of lattice paths from the point
(0,0)
to
(n,n)
with steps choosing from the set
\{(1,0),(0,1),(1,1)\}
.
One can consult item A001850 in the OEIS

[
9
]

for other classical combinatorial interpretations.

Sun
[
11
,
13
]
discovered many interesting arithmetic properties of
D_{n}
.
In particular, he showed that, for any positive integer
n
,

\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(2k+1)D_{k}^{2}\in{\mathbb{Z}}

and that, for any prime
p>3
,

\sum_{k=1}^{p-1}\frac{D_{k}}{k^{2}}\equiv(-1)^{(p-1)/2}2E_{p-3}\pmod{p}

where
E_{k}
are the Euler numbers.

Sun
[
11
]
introduced the Delannoy polynomials

D_{n}(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\binom{n+k}{k}z^{k},\quad n\in{\mathbb{N}},

and proved that
[
13
]
for any
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{+}
,

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(2k+1)D_{k}(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\binom{n}{k+1}\binom{n+k}{k}z^{k}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[z],

and thus

\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(2k+1)D_{k}(z)\equiv 0\pmod{n}.

The Apéry numbers
[
1
,
17
]
given by

A_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}^{2}\binom{n+k}{k}^{2},\quad n\in{\mathbb{N}}

arose in Apéry’s proof of irrationality of
\zeta(3)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^{3}}
.
Congruences for Apéry numbers have been investigated extensively.
For example, Gessel
[
4
]
proved that

A_{pn}\equiv A_{n}\pmod{n^{3}}

for any
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{+}
and prime
p\geq 5
.
Xia and Sun
[
20
]
confirmed the conjecture posed by Sun
[
14
]
that
for each
v\in{\mathbb{N}}
and prime
p>3
, there is a
p
-adic integer
c_{v}
such that

\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}(2k+1)^{2v+1}(-1)^{k}A_{k}\equiv c_{v}p\left(\frac{p}{3}\right)\pmod{p^{3}},

where
(-)
denotes the Legendre symbol.
The authors
[
19
]
derived that for each
v\in{\mathbb{N}}
and prime
p>3
, there exists an integer
\tilde{c}_{v}
such that

\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}(2k+1)^{2v+1}A_{k}\equiv\tilde{c}_{v}p\pmod{p^{3}}.

In pursuing a more general study of arithmetic properties of Apéry numbers,
Sun
[
12
, (1.1)]
introduced the Apéry polynomials

A_{n}(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}^{2}\binom{n+k}{k}^{2}z^{k},\quad n\in{\mathbb{N}}.

He proved that for any
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{+}
,

\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(2k+1)A_{k}(z)\equiv 0\pmod{n}

and conjectured that for any

\varepsilon\in\{1,-1\},m,n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{+}
and
z\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,

\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(2k+1)\varepsilon^{k}A_{k}(z)^{m}\equiv 0\pmod{n}.

Guo and Zeng
[
5
]
confirmed Sun’s conjecture for the case
\varepsilon=-1,m=1

and further introduced the Schmidt polynomial

S_{n}^{(r)}(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}^{r}\binom{n+k}{k}^{r}z^{k},\quad n\in{\mathbb{N}},

which is a natural generalization of the Delannoy polynomial and Apéry polynomial.
Then they showed that, for any
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{+},v\in{\mathbb{N}},r\geq 2
and
z\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,

\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\varepsilon^{k}(2k+1)^{2v+1}S^{(r)}_{k}(z)\equiv 0\pmod{n},

and conjectured that, for any
m,n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{+},r\geq 2
and
z\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,

\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\varepsilon^{k}(2k+1)S^{(r)}_{k}(z)^{m}\equiv 0\pmod{n}.

This conjecture was confirmed by Pan
[
10
]
with
q
-congruences.
In 2018, Chen and Guo
[
2
]
defined the multi-variate Schmidt polynomial as

S_{k}^{(r)}(x_{0},x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})=\sum_{i=0}^{k}\binom{k+i}{2i}^{r}\binom{2i}{i}x_{i},

and proved that

\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\varepsilon^{k}(2k+1)^{2v+1}S_{k}^{(r)}(x_{0},x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})^{m}\equiv 0\pmod{n}.

All the mentioned congruences contain
(2k+1)^{s}
with odd
s
.
The first part of this paper considers the arithmetic properties for sums of
(2k+1)^{s}D_{k}(z)
with even
s
.
The following theorem illustrates that one can reduce the problem to the case of
s=0
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{+}
. For each
v\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
z\in{\mathbb{Z}}\setminus\{0,-1\}
, we have

\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(2k+1)^{2v}D_{k}(z)\equiv c_{v}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}D_{k}(z)\pmod{n}

(1.1)

when
\gcd(n,2z)=1
and

\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{k}(2k+1)^{2v}D_{k}(z)\equiv\tilde{c}_{v}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{k}D_{k}(z)\pmod{n}

(1.2)

when
\gcd(n,2(z+1))=1
.
Here
c_{0}=\tilde{c}_{0}=1
and when
v\geq 1
,

c_{v}=\frac{1}{z}\sum_{j=1}^{v}e_{j}^{(2v-1)}c_{v-j}\in z^{-v}{\mathbb{Z}}[z]\text{ and }\tilde{c}_{v}=-\frac{1}{z+1}\sum_{j=1}^{v}e_{j}^{(2v-1)}\tilde{c}_{v-j}\in(z+1)^{-v}{\mathbb{Z}}[z],

(1.3)

where

e^{(s)}_{j}=\binom{s}{2j}2^{2j-1}+\binom{s}{2j-1}2^{2j-2}\in{\mathbb{Z}}

(1.4)

for
s\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
j=1,2,\ldots,\left\lfloor\dfrac{s+1}{2}\right\rfloor
.

In 2014, Sun
[
13
, Theorem 1.2(i)]
proved that when
p
is an odd prime and
b,c\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, for any integer
m
coprime with
p
, we have

\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}\frac{T_{k}(b,c)}{m^{k}}\equiv\left(\frac{(m-b)^{2}-4c}{p}\right)\pmod{p}.

(1.5)

Here
(-)
denotes the Legendre symbol and
T_{n}(b,c)
is the generalized central trinomial coefficients defined by

T_{n}(b,c)=\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\binom{n}{2k}\binom{2k}{k}b^{n-2k}c^{k}.

Sun
[
13
]
showed that
T_{k}(b,c)=D_{k}(z)
if
b=2z+1
and
c=z^{2}+z
for any
z\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.
Then taking
m=1
and
m=-1
respectively in (
1.5
) gives

\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}D_{k}(z)\equiv\left(\frac{-z}{p}\right)\pmod{p}

(1.6)

and

\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}(-1)^{k}D_{k}(z)\equiv\left(\frac{z+1}{p}\right)\pmod{p}.

(1.7)

Combining Theorem
1.1
and congruences (
1.6
) and (
1.7
) together leads to the following.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
p
be an odd prime.
For each
v\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
z\in{\mathbb{Z}}\setminus\{0,-1\}
, we obtain that

\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}(2k+1)^{2v}D_{k}(z)\equiv c_{v}\left(\frac{-z}{p}\right)\pmod{p}

when
\gcd(p,z)=1
, and that

\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}(-1)^{k}(2k+1)^{2v}D_{k}(z)\equiv\tilde{c}_{v}\left(\frac{z+1}{p}\right)\pmod{p}

when
\gcd(p,z+1)=1
.
Here
c_{v}
and
\tilde{c}_{v}
are given as in (
1.3
).

Remark.
The cases for
z\in\{0,-1\}
are trivial since
D_{k}(0)=1
and
D_{k}(-1)=(-1)^{k}
.

The second part of the paper concentrates on supercongruences of sums involving the Delannoy numbers
D_{k}
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
v\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
n=2^{a}
for some
a\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{+}
.
We have

\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(2k+1)^{2v+1}D_{k}\equiv\rho_{v}n\pmod{n^{3}}

(1.8)

and

\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{k}(2k+1)^{2v+1}D_{k}\equiv\tilde{\rho}_{v}n^{2}\pmod{n^{3}}.

(1.9)

Here
\rho_{v}
is an odd integer given by

\rho_{v}=-2\sum_{j=1}^{v}e_{j}^{(2v)}y_{v-j}(-1)+1,

(1.10)

where

y_{0}(k)=-\frac{1}{2}
and

y_{v}(k)=-\frac{1}{2}(2k+3)^{2v}+\sum_{j=1}^{v}e_{j}^{(2v)}y_{v-j}(k),\quad v\geq 1.

(1.11)

While
\tilde{\rho}_{v}
is an even integer given by

\tilde{\rho}_{v}=2v-\sum_{j=1}^{v}e^{(2v)}_{j}\tilde{y}_{v-j}^{\prime}(-1)\in{\mathbb{Z}},

(1.12)

where
\tilde{y}_{0}(k)=\frac{1}{4}
and when
v\geq 1

\tilde{y}_{v}(k)=\frac{(2k+3)^{2v}}{4}-\sum_{j=1}^{v}\frac{e^{(2v)}_{j}}{2}\tilde{y}_{v-j}(k).

(1.13)

Here
e^{(2v)}_{j}
are even integers given by (
1.4
).

In particular, taking
v=1
in (
1.9
) confirms Guo and Zeng’s
[
6
]
conjecture: when
n
is a power of
2
, one has

\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{k}(2k+1)^{3}D_{k}\equiv 2n^{2}\pmod{n^{3}}.

(1.14)

The rest of the paper is organized as follows.
In Section 2, we recall the power-partible reduction and apply it to the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
The proof of Theorem
1.3
and arithmetic properties of
D_{2^{a}\pm 1}
needed in the proof will be given in Section 3.

2
Power-partible reduction and congruences on Delannoy polynomials

In this section, we first recall some basic notations and facts concerning power-partible reduction, which was introduced by Hou, Mu and Zeilberger
[
7
]
for hypergeometric terms and extended to holonomic sequences by the authors in
[
18
,
19
]
.
Then the proof of Theorem
1.1
along with other arithmetic properties for
D_{k}(z)
will be presented.

2.1
Power-partible reduction

Let
{\mathbb{K}}
be a field of characteristic
0
and
{\mathbb{K}}[k]
the polynomial ring over
{\mathbb{K}}
.
The set of
annihilators
of a sequence
F(k)
is denoted by

\ann F(k):=\left\{L=\sum_{i=0}^{J}a_{i}(k)\sigma^{i}\in{\mathbb{K}}[k][\sigma]\mid L(F(k))=0\right\},

where
\sigma
is the shift operator (that is,
\sigma F(k)=F(k+1)
) and
J\in{\mathbb{N}}=\{0,1,2,\ldots\}
is called the order of the operator
L
if
a_{J}(k)\neq 0
.
A sequence
F(k)
is called
holonomic
if
\ann F(k)\neq\{0\}
.
Given an operator
L=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{J}a_{i}(k)\sigma^{i}\in{\mathbb{K}}[k][\sigma]
of order
J
,
the
adjoint
of
L
is defined by

L^{\ast}=\sum_{i=0}^{J}\sigma^{-i}a_{i}(k),

that is,

L^{\ast}(x(k))=\sum_{i=0}^{J}a_{i}(k-i)x(k-i)

for any
x(k)\in{\mathbb{K}}[k]
.
Suppose
L=\sum_{i=0}^{J}a_{i}(k)\sigma^{i}\in\ann F(k)
and
x(k)\in{\mathbb{K}}[k]
.
By
[
3
]
or
[
16
]
, one has

L^{\ast}(x(k))F(k)=\Delta\left(-\sum_{i=0}^{J-1}u_{i}(k)F(k+i)\right),

(2.1)

where

u_{i}(k)=\sum_{j=1}^{J-i}a_{i+j}(k-j)x(k-j),\ i=0,1,2,\ldots,J-1.

Summing over
k
from
0
to
n-1
on both sides of identity (
2.1
) leads to

\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}L^{\ast}(x(k))F(k)=\left(\sum_{i=0}^{J-1}u_{i}(0)F(i)\right)-\left(\sum_{i=0}^{J-1}u_{i}(n)F(n+i)\right).

(2.2)

The set

S_{L}=\{L^{\ast}(x(k))\mid x(k)\in{\mathbb{K}}[k]\}

is called the
difference space
corresponding to
L
.
We will take
[p(k)]_{L}=p(k)+S_{L}
as the coset of a polynomial
p(k)
.
The
degree
of
L=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{J}a_{i}(k)\sigma^{i}\in{\mathbb{K}}[k][\sigma]
with
a_{J}(k)\neq 0
is defined by

\deg L=\max_{0\leq\ell\leq J}\{\deg b_{\ell}(k)-\ell\},

where
b_{\ell}(k)=\sum_{j={\ell}}^{J}\binom{j}{\ell}a_{J-j}(k+j-J)
.
Let
d=\deg L
and

R_{L}=\left\{s\in{\mathbb{N}}\mid\sum_{\ell=0}^{J}[k^{d+\ell}](b_{\ell}(k))s^{\underline{\ell}}=0\right\}.

Here
[k^{d+\ell}](b_{\ell}(k))
denotes the coefficient of
k^{d+\ell}
in
b_{\ell}(k)
and
s^{\underline{\ell}}
denotes the falling factorial
s(s-1)\cdots(s-\ell+1)
.
Then
L
is called
nondegenerated
if
R_{L}=\emptyset
, and
degenerated
otherwise.

Theorem 2.1

(Theorem 2.4 in
[
19
]
)
.

Let
L=\sum_{i=0}^{J}a_{i}(k)\sigma^{i}\in{\mathbb{K}}[k][\sigma]
with
a_{J}(k)\neq 0
.
Suppose
L
is nondegenerated and there exists a
\gamma\in{\mathbb{K}}
such that

a_{i}(\gamma+k)=(-1)^{\deg L}a_{J-i}(\gamma-k-J),\quad i=0,1,\ldots,\lfloor\frac{J}{2}\rfloor.

(2.3)

Then for any positive integer
m
, we have

[(k-\gamma)^{m}]_{L}\in{\langle}[(k-\gamma)^{i}]_{L}\mid i\equiv m\pmod{2},0\leq i<\deg L{\rangle}.

When conditions in Theorem
2.1
are satisfied, we say
L
is
power-partible
with respect to
\gamma
.
If
L\in\ann F(k)
for some holonomic sequence
F(k)
, one may also say
F(k)
is
power-partible
with respect to
\gamma
.

2.2
Arithmetic properties of Delannoy polynomials

In this subsection, we utilize the power-partible reduction to prove Theorem
1.1
. We will assume
z\in{\mathbb{Z}}\setminus\{0,-1\}
in the following text.

Let
\varepsilon\in\{1,-1\}
and
F_{k}(z)=\varepsilon^{k}D_{k}(z)
.
By Zeilberger’s algorithm
[
21
]
, we find that

L=(k+2)\sigma^{2}-\varepsilon(2k+3)(2z+1)\sigma+(k+1)\in\ann F_{k}(z).

(2.4)

It is straightforward to check that
L
is nondegenerated,
d=\deg(L)=1
and condition (
2.3
) holds for
\gamma=-\frac{1}{2}
.
Namely,
F_{k}(z)
is power-partible with respect to
\gamma
.
Thus for any
v\in{\mathbb{N}}
, it follows that

[(2k+1)^{2v}]_{L}\in{\langle}[(2k+1)^{0}]_{L}{\rangle}\quad\text{and}\quad[(2k+1)^{2v+1}]_{L}\in{\langle}[0]_{L}{\rangle}.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
L
be given as in (
2.4
) and
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{+}
.
For any polynomial
x(k)
, we have

\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}L^{\ast}(x(k))F_{k}(z)=n(x(n-1)F_{n-1}(z)-x(n-2)F_{n}(z)).

(2.5)

Proof.

By Eq. (
2.2
) and the easily checked fact
u_{0}(0)F_{0}(z)+u_{1}(0)F_{1}(z)=0
, we have

\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}L^{\ast}(x(k))F_{k}(z)=-\left(u_{0}(n)F_{n}(z)+u_{1}(n)F_{n+1}(z)\right),

(2.6)

where

u_{0}(n)=nx(n-2)-\varepsilon(2n+1)(2z+1)x(n-1)
and

u_{1}(n)=(n+1)x(n-1)
.
As
L\in\ann D_{k}(z)
, it is clear that for any
n\geq 1
,

(n+1)F_{n+1}(z)=\varepsilon(2n+1)(2z+1)F_{n}(z)-nF_{n-1}(z).

(2.7)

Substituting equality (
2.7
) into (
2.6
) derives (
2.5
).

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
L
be given as in (
2.4
) and
n
a positive integer. Then for any polynomial
x(k)
with integer coefficients, we have

\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}L^{\ast}(x(k))F_{k}(z)\equiv 0\pmod{n}.

Recall that
L
in (
2.4
) is power-partible with respect to
\gamma=-\dfrac{1}{2}
.
Let

x_{s}(k)=(2k+3)^{s},\quad s\in{\mathbb{N}}.

(2.8)

We will show that
L^{\ast}(x_{s}(k))
is a linear combination of
(2k+1)^{i}
with
i\equiv s+1{\pmod{2}}
and that all the coefficients in the combination are even integers.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
L
be given by (
2.4
) and
x_{s}(k)
given by Eq. (
2.8
).
Then

L^{\ast}(x_{s}(k))=(-2\varepsilon z-\varepsilon+1)(2k+1)^{s+1}+2\sum_{j=1}^{\left\lfloor\frac{s+1}{2}\right\rfloor}e^{(s)}_{j}(2k+1)^{s+1-2j},

(2.9)

where
e^{(s)}_{j}
are given as in (
1.4
).

Proof.
For simplicity, let
\ell=2k+1
.
By the definition of
L^{\ast}
, we have

\displaystyle L^{\ast}(x_{s}(k))=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(\ell-1)(\ell-2)^{s}-\varepsilon(2z+1)\ell^{s+1}+\frac{1}{2}(\ell+1)(\ell+2)^{s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(\ell-1)\sum_{j=0}^{s}\binom{s}{j}(-2)^{j}\ell^{s-j}+\frac{1}{2}(\ell+1)\sum_{j=0}^{s}\binom{s}{j}2^{j}\ell^{s-j}-\varepsilon(2z+1)\ell^{s+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=0\\
j\text{ even}\end{subarray}}^{s}\binom{s}{j}2^{j}\ell^{s-j+1}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=0\\
j\text{ odd}\end{subarray}}^{s}\binom{s}{j}2^{j}\ell^{s-j}-\varepsilon(2z+1)\ell^{s+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-2\varepsilon z-\varepsilon+1)\ell^{s+1}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=2\\
j\text{ even}\end{subarray}}^{s}\binom{s}{j}2^{j}\ell^{s-j+1}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\text{ odd}\end{subarray}}^{s}\binom{s}{j}2^{j}\ell^{s-j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-2\varepsilon z-\varepsilon+1)(2k+1)^{s+1}+2\sum_{j=1}^{\left\lfloor\frac{s+1}{2}\right\rfloor}e^{(s)}_{j}(2k+1)^{s+1-2j}.\quad\quad\hfill\rule{4.0pt}{7.0pt}

Now we are ready to provide the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of (
1.1
) in Theorem
1.1
.

We proceed by induction on
v
.
The case when
v=0
is trivial with
c_{0}=1
.
Next, we assume that
v>0
and (
1.1
) holds for all smaller
v
’s with
c_{j}\in z^{-j}{\mathbb{Z}}[z]
(
j=0,1,\ldots,v-1
).

By taking
\varepsilon=1
in equality (
2.9
), it is easily checked that

(2k+1)^{2v}=-\frac{1}{2z}L^{\ast}(x_{2v-1}(k))+\frac{1}{z}\sum_{j=1}^{v}e^{(2v-1)}_{j}(2k+1)^{2v-2j},

(2.10)

where
e^{(2v-1)}_{j}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.
Multiplying by
D_{k}(z)
on both sides of (
2.10
) and summing over
k
from
0
to
n-1
leads to

\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(2k+1)^{2v}D_{k}(z)=-\frac{1}{2z}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}L^{\ast}(x_{2v-1}(k))D_{k}(z)+\frac{1}{z}\sum_{j=1}^{v}e^{(2v-1)}_{j}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(2k+1)^{2v-2j}D_{k}(z).

By the assumption, we know

\sum_{j=1}^{v}e^{(2v-1)}_{j}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(2k+1)^{2v-2j}D_{k}(z)\equiv\sum_{j=1}^{v}e^{(2v-1)}_{j}c_{v-j}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}D_{k}(z)\pmod{n}.

(2.11)

Note also that, by Corollary
2.3
, when
\gcd(n,2z)=1
,
we have

-\frac{1}{2z}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}L^{\ast}(x_{2v-1}(k))D_{k}(z)\equiv 0\pmod{n}.

(2.12)

Combining (
2.11
) and (
2.12
) together arrives at

\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(2k+1)^{2v}D_{k}(z)\equiv c_{v}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}D_{k}(z)\pmod{n},

where
c_{v}=\frac{1}{z}\sum_{j=1}^{v}e^{(2v-1)}_{j}c_{v-j}\in z^{-v}{\mathbb{Z}}[z]
.

Proof of (
1.2
) in Theorem
1.1
.

Taking
\varepsilon=-1
in equality (
2.9
) leads to

(2k+1)^{2v}=\frac{1}{2(z+1)}L^{\ast}(x_{2v-1}(k))-\frac{1}{z+1}\sum_{j=1}^{v}e^{(2v-1)}_{j}(2k+1)^{2v-2j},

where
e^{(2v-1)}_{j}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.
The rest of the proof is then similar to that of (
1.1
) and omitted.

It is easy to compute that
c_{1}=\frac{1}{z}
and
c_{2}=\frac{4z+9}{z^{2}}
in (
1.3
).
Then we know when
p
is an odd prime and
\gcd(p,z)=1
,

\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}(2k+1)^{2}D_{k}(z)\equiv\frac{1}{z}\left(\frac{-z}{p}\right)\pmod{p}

(2.13)

and

\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}(2k+1)^{4}D_{k}(z)\equiv\frac{4z+9}{z^{2}}\left(\frac{-z}{p}\right)\pmod{p}.

Congruence (
2.13
) was first discovered by Sun in
[
13
]
.
It is also routine to compute that
\tilde{c}_{1}=-\frac{1}{z+1}
and
\tilde{c}_{2}=-\frac{4z-5}{(z+1)^{2}}
in (
1.3
).
Namely, when
p
is an odd prime and
\gcd(p,z+1)=1
, we have

\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}(-1)^{k}(2k+1)^{2}D_{k}(z)\equiv-\frac{1}{z+1}\left(\frac{z+1}{p}\right)\pmod{p}

and

\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}(-1)^{k}(2k+1)^{4}D_{k}(z)\equiv-\frac{4z-5}{(z+1)^{2}}\left(\frac{z+1}{p}\right)\pmod{p}.

3
Supercongruences for Delannoy numbers

This section focuses on supercongruences for
\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\varepsilon^{k}(2k+1)^{2v+1}D_{k}
when
n
is power of
2
.
We will provide an algorithmic approach to constructing new supercongruences and confirm Guo and Zeng’s conjecture (
1.14
).

In 2018, Sun
[
15
, Lemma 2.1]
proved that

D_{n+1}(z)-D_{n-1}(z)=2z(2n+1)S_{n}(z)

(3.1)

for any positive integer
n
.
Here
S_{n}(z)
are the large Schröder polynomials defined by Sun
[
12
,
15
]
as

S_{n}(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\binom{n+k}{k}\frac{1}{k+1}z^{k}.

One can see
S_{n}(1)
reduces to the
n
-th large Schröder number
S_{n}
.

In 2021, Lengyel
[
8
]
proved that, when
a\geq 2
,

D_{2^{a}}\equiv 1\pmod{4^{a+1}}

(3.2)

and

S_{2^{a}}\equiv 2-2^{a+1}\pmod{2^{2a+1}}.

(3.3)

The following theorem considers supercongruences of
D_{2^{a}+1}
and
D_{2^{a}-1}
modulo
4^{a+1}
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
a\geq 2
be a positive integer. Then we have

D_{2^{a}+1}\equiv 3+2^{a+2}\pmod{4^{a+1}}

(3.4)

and

D_{2^{a}-1}\equiv-1\pmod{4^{a+1}}.

(3.5)

Proof.
Note that

D_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}^{2}2^{k},

which can be proved by checking the recurrence relation and the initial values.
Let
T(n,k)=\binom{n}{k}^{2}2^{k}
. When
k\geq 1
,

T(2^{a}+1,k)=\binom{2^{a}+1}{k}^{2}2^{k}=\frac{(2^{a}+1)^{2}}{k^{2}}\binom{2^{a}}{k-1}^{2}2^{k}.

Then one can see

v_{2}(T(2^{a}+1,k))=2a+k-2(v_{2}(k-1)+v_{2}(k)),

where
v_{2}(m)
is the largest
t\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
2^{t}\mid m
.

When
k\geq 6
, we have
k-2(v_{2}(k-1)+v_{2}(k))\geq 2
and thus

D_{2^{a}+1}=\sum_{k=0}^{2^{a}+1}T(2^{a}+1,k)\equiv\sum_{k=0}^{5}T(2^{a}+1,k)\pmod{4^{a+1}}.

It is straightforward to calculate that when
a\geq 2
,

\displaystyle T(2^{a}+1,0)

\displaystyle=1,

\displaystyle T(2^{a}+1,1)

\displaystyle=2(4^{a}+2^{a+1}+1),

\displaystyle T(2^{a}+1,2)

\displaystyle=4^{a}(2^{a}+1)^{2}\equiv 4^{a}\pmod{4^{a+1}},

\displaystyle T(2^{a}+1,3)

\displaystyle\equiv 2\cdot 4^{a}(4^{a}-1)^{2}\equiv 2\cdot 4^{a}\pmod{4^{a+1}},

\displaystyle T(2^{a}+1,4)

\displaystyle\equiv 4^{a}(4^{a}-1)^{2}(2^{a-1}-1)^{2}\equiv 4^{a}\pmod{4^{a+1}},

\displaystyle T(2^{a}+1,5)

\displaystyle\equiv 2\cdot 4^{a}(4^{a}-1)^{2}(2^{a-1}-1)^{2}(2^{a}-3)^{2}\equiv 2\cdot 4^{a}\pmod{4^{a+1}}.

Summing all above congruences together leads to (
3
) after simplification.

Let
z=1
and
n=2^{a}
in (
3.1
), we arrive at

D_{2^{a}-1}=D_{2^{a}+1}-2(2^{a+1}+1)S_{2^{a}}.

(3.6)

Substituting
(
3.3
)
and (
3
) into (
3.6
) leads to

D_{2^{a}-1}\equiv 3+2^{a+2}-(2^{a+1}+1)(4-2^{a+2})\equiv-1\pmod{4^{a+1}}.

Now we are ready to prove Theorem
1.3
.

Proof of (
1.8
) in Theorem
1.3
.

When
a=1
, namely,
n=2
, we know that (
1.8
) holds as

\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(2k+1)^{2v+1}D_{k}=1+9^{v+1}\equiv 2\pmod{8}

by the fact that
D_{0}=1
and
D_{1}=3
.
In the following, we assume
a\geq 2
.
By taking
\varepsilon=1
,
z=1
and
s=2v
and recursively using (
2.9
), one can see

(2k+1)^{2v+1}=L^{\ast}(y_{v}(k))

with
y_{v}(k)
defined by (
1.11
).
Note that
y_{v}(k)
is a linear combination of even powers of
(2k+3)
and
\deg_{k}(y_{v}(k))=2v
.
Then one can see

y_{v}(k-1)=y_{v}(-k-2)

and thus assume

y_{v}(k-1)=\sum_{j=1}^{2v}s^{(v)}_{j}k^{j}+\frac{s^{(v)}_{0}}{2}\quad\text{and}\quad y_{v}(k-2)=\sum_{j=1}^{2v}s^{(v)}_{j}(-k)^{j}+\frac{s^{(v)}_{0}}{2}

(3.7)

with
s^{(v)}_{j}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
for any
0\leq j\leq 2v
since
e_{j}^{(2v)}
are even by the definition (
1.4
).
Combining (
1.11
) and (
3.7
) together shows that
s^{(v)}_{0}
is an odd integer given by

s^{(v)}_{0}=2\sum_{j=1}^{v}e_{j}^{(2v)}y_{v-j}(-1)-1.

(3.8)

Taking
z=1
,
\varepsilon=1
and
x(k)=y_{v}(k)
in (
2.5
) gives

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(2k+1)^{2v+1}D_{k}=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}L^{\ast}(y_{v}(k))D_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n\left(y_{v}(n-1)D_{n-1}-y_{v}(n-2)D_{n}\right)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle n\left((s^{(v)}_{1}n+\frac{s^{(v)}_{0}}{2})D_{n-1}-(-s^{(v)}_{1}n+\frac{s^{(v)}_{0}}{2})D_{n}\right)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle n\left(s^{(v)}_{1}n(D_{n}+D_{n-1})-\frac{s^{(v)}_{0}}{2}(D_{n}-D_{n-1})\right)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\rho_{v}n\pmod{n^{3}}

with
\rho_{v}=-s^{(v)}_{0}
as
D_{2^{a}}-D_{2^{a}-1}\equiv 2\pmod{4^{a+1}}
and
D_{2^{a}}+D_{2^{a}-1}\equiv 0\pmod{4^{a+1}}

by (
3.2
) and (
3.5
).
This completes the proof.

Note that
\rho_{v}
can be determined automatically.
For example, by taking
v=0,1,2
in (
1.10
) respectively, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(2k+1)D_{k}\equiv n\pmod{n^{3}},

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(2k+1)^{3}D_{k}\equiv 5n\pmod{n^{3}},

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(2k+1)^{5}D_{k}\equiv 105n\pmod{n^{3}}.

Proof of (
1.9
) in Theorem
1.3
.

When
n=2
, we know (
1.9
) holds as

\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{k}(2k+1)^{2v+1}D_{k}=1-9^{v+1}\equiv 0\pmod{8}.

In the following, we assume
n\geq 4
.
Let
\varepsilon=-1
,
z=1
and
s=2v
in (
2.9
).
Then by recursively using (
2.9
), we obtain that

(2k+1)^{2v+1}=L^{\ast}(\tilde{y}_{v}(k))

with
\tilde{y}_{v}(k)
given by (
1.13
).
Since
\tilde{y}_{v}(k)
is a linear combination of even power of
(2k+3)
,
we can assume

\tilde{y}_{v}(k-1)=\sum_{j=1}^{2v}\tilde{s}^{(v)}_{j}k^{j}+\frac{\tilde{s}_{0}}{4}\quad\text{and}\quad\tilde{y}_{v}(k-2)=\sum_{j=1}^{2v}\tilde{s}^{(v)}_{j}(-k)^{j}+\frac{\tilde{s}_{0}}{4}

(3.9)

with
\tilde{s}^{(v)}_{j}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
for any
0\leq j\leq 2v
.
Combining (
1.13
) and (
3.9
) together gives

\tilde{s}^{(v)}_{1}=v-\sum_{j=1}^{v}\frac{e^{(2v)}_{j}}{2}\tilde{y}_{v-j}^{\prime}(-1)\in{\mathbb{Z}}.

Taking
z=1
,
\varepsilon=-1
and
x(k)=y_{v}(k)
in (
2.5
) gives

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{k}(2k+1)^{2v+1}D_{k}=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}L^{\ast}(y_{v}(k))(-1)^{k}D_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n\left(y_{v}(n-1)(-1)^{n-1}D_{n-1}-y_{v}(n-2)(-1)^{n}D_{n}\right)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle n\left(-(\tilde{s}^{(v)}_{1}n+\frac{\tilde{s}^{(v)}_{0}}{4})D_{n-1}-(-\tilde{s}^{(v)}_{1}n+\frac{\tilde{s}^{(v)}_{0}}{4})D_{n}\right)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle n\left(\tilde{s}^{(v)}_{1}n(D_{n}-D_{n-1})-\frac{\tilde{s}^{(v)}_{0}}{4}(D_{n}+D_{n-1})\right)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\tilde{\rho}_{v}n^{2}\pmod{n^{3}}

with
\tilde{\rho}_{v}=2\tilde{s}^{(v)}_{1}
with the help of (
3.2
) and (
3.5
).
This completes the proof.

The constant
\tilde{\rho}_{v}
can also be calculated mechanically.
By taking
v=0,1,2
in (
1.12
) respectively, we arrive at

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{k}(2k+1)D_{k}\equiv 0\pmod{n^{3}},

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{k}(2k+1)^{3}D_{k}\equiv 2n^{2}\pmod{n^{3}},

(3.10)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{k}(2k+1)^{5}D_{k}\equiv-12n^{2}\pmod{n^{3}}.

Note that (
3.10
) confirms Guo and Zeng’s conjecture (
1.14
).

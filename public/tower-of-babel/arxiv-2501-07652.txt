Finite Sample Identification of Partially Observed Bilinear Dynamical Systems
Yahya Sattar ⋆, Yassir Jedra ⋆, Maryam Fazel, Sarah Dean

Abstract
We consider the problem of learning a realization of a partially observed bilinear dynamical system (BLDS) from noisy input-output data. Given a single trajectory of input-output samples, we provide a finite time analysis for learning the system’s Markov-like parameters, from which a balanced realization of the bilinear system can be obtained. Our bilinear system identification algorithm learns the system’s Markov-like parameters by regressing the outputs to highly correlated, nonlinear, and heavy-tailed covariates. Moreover, the stability of BLDS depends on the sequence of inputs used to excite the system. These properties, unique to partially observed bilinear dynamical systems, pose significant challenges to the analysis of our algorithm for learning the unknown dynamics. We address these challenges and provide high probability error bounds on our identification algorithm under a uniform stability assumption. Our analysis provides insights into system theoretic quantities that affect learning accuracy and sample complexity. Lastly, we perform numerical experiments with synthetic data to reinforce these insights.

Contributions:
We overcome the aforementioned challenges and provide theoretical guarantees for learning partially observed bilinear dynamical systems.
We make the following contributions towards bilinear system identification:

•

Sample complexity and error bounds:

We provide the first sample complexity analysis and finite-sample error bounds for learning a realization of a partially observed BLDS from a single trajectory of input-output data.
Unlike LDS, the output of a bilinear system maps to the history of inputs via nonlinear features (obtained by the Kronecker products of past inputs) and a sequence of Markov-like parameters with exponentially increasing length.
Our main result (Theorem
1
) provides
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(1/\sqrt{T})
error rate for learning these parameters from a single trajectory of length
T
.
Our sample complexity bound

\tilde{\Omega}\left((p+1)^{L+1}\right)
grows exponentially with the history length
L
(where
p
is the input dimension), which correctly captures the dependence on the number of unknown Markov-like parameters (growing exponentially with
L

For stable bilinear systems (defined in §
2.2
), this can be mitigated by choosing a smaller history of inputs (see §
5
).

•

Input choice and stability:

Stability of BLDS is typically input dependent. We work with a novel notion of stability (Definition
1
) that generalizes the classic notion of stability for LDS to the BLDS.
We also define a notion of stability radius which governs our choice of inputs.

•

Persistence of excitation:

Of independent interest, we establish the persistence of excitation (Theorem
2

Our persistence of excitation result holds for nonlinear, correlated, and heavy-tailed covariates (i.e., the input features).

•

Numerical experiments:

Lastly, we perform experiments with synthetic data to verify our theoretical findings. Interestingly, our experiments show that exciting the system with inputs sampled uniformly at random from a sphere leads to better estimation of Markov-like parameters as compared to Gaussian inputs, empirically reinforcing our theory on choice of inputs.

Preliminaries

2.1
Problem Formulation

Consider a partially observed bilinear dynamical system with the following state-space representation: for all
t\geq 0
,

\displaystyle{\bm{x}}_{t+1}

\displaystyle={\bm{A}}_{0}{\bm{x}}_{t}+\sum_{k=1}^{p}({\bm{u}}_{t})_{k}{\bm{A}}_{k}{\bm{x}}_{t}+{\bm{B}}{\bm{u}}_{t}+{\bm{w}}_{t},

(2.1)

\displaystyle{\bm{y}}_{t}

\displaystyle={\bm{C}}{\bm{x}}_{t}+{\bm{D}}{\bm{u}}_{t}+{\bm{z}}_{t},

where
{\bm{x}}_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
{\bm{u}}_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
, and
{\bm{y}}_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
,
{\bm{w}}_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
{\bm{z}}_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
represent the hidden state, input, output, process noise, and measurement noise, respectively, at time
t
. Without loss of generality, we consider that
x_{0}=0
. The noise processes
\{{\bm{w}}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
and
\{{\bm{z}}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
are assumed to be sequences of independent, zero-mean,
\sigma^{2}
-subgaussian random vectors taking values in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\mathbb{R}^{m}
, respectively, for some variance proxy parameter
\sigma>0
. The matrices
{\bm{A}}_{0},{\bm{A}}_{1},\dots,{\bm{A}}_{p}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
,
{\bm{B}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}
,
{\bm{C}}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
, and
{\bm{D}}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times p}
represent the parameters that govern the evolution of the dynamical system and are a priori unknown.

In this work, we wish to identify the unknown parameters of the system from a single trajectory of input-output samples
\{({\bm{u}}_{t},{\bm{y}}_{t})\}_{t=1}^{T}
. To that end, we focus on the task of learning the so-called
Markov-like parameters
(detailed in §
3
) of the system. Once learned, these Markov-like parameters can be exploited using the classic Ho-Kalman algorithm to recover the unknown matrices of the system up to some similarity transform as will be described in §
3
. Next, we clarify our choice of inputs and discuss the required stability assumption.

2.2
Input Choice & Stability of Bilinear Dynamical Systems

Stability of bilinear dynamical systems is typically input dependent. To see that, we can unroll the state dynamics in (
2.1
) to write: for all
t\geq 0
,

\displaystyle{\bm{x}}_{t+1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{t}\left(\prod_{k=0}^{\ell-1}({\bm{u}}_{t-k}\circ{\bm{A}})\right){\bm{B}}{\bm{u}}_{t-\ell}+\sum_{\ell=0}^{t}\left(\prod_{k=0}^{\ell-1}({\bm{u}}_{t-k}\circ{\bm{A}})\right){\bm{w}}_{t-\ell},

(2.2)

where we define
{\bm{u}}_{t}\circ{\bm{A}}:={\bm{A}}_{0}+\sum_{k=1}^{p}({\bm{u}}_{t})_{k}{\bm{A}}_{k}
for the ease of notation. Then, observe that the products of matrices
\prod_{k=0}^{\ell-1}({\bm{u}}_{t-k}\circ{\bm{A}})
may grow exponentially in norm if we consistently choose large inputs. This is precisely why stability in bilinear dynamical systems is more challenging than other classes of systems such as linear dynamical systems or switched systems.

Traditionally, notions like Mean Square Stability (MSS) have been considered to reason about the stability behavior of bilinear systems
[
24
,
41
,
50
]
. Typically, these notions are asymptotic in nature, require distributional assumptions on the inputs,
permit diverging trajectories with nonzero probability, and may not allow us to obtain tight guarantees. We introduce an alternative notion of stability that naturally generalizes the classical notion of stability in standard LTI systems.

Uniform stability in bilinear dynamical systems:

First, let us recall that the
joint spectral radius
of a set of matrices
\mathcal{M}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
can be defined as follows:

\displaystyle\rho(\mathcal{M}):=\lim_{k\to\infty}\sup_{{\bm{M}}_{1},\dots,{\bm{M}}_{k}\in\mathcal{M}}\|{\bm{M}}_{1}{\bm{M}}_{2}\cdots{\bm{M}}_{k}\|_{\textup{op}}^{1/k}.

(2.3)

For
\rho>0
, we define the following quantity:

\displaystyle\phi(\mathcal{M},\rho):=\sup_{k\geq 1,{\bm{M}}_{1},\dots,{\bm{M}}_{k}\in\mathcal{M}}\frac{\|{\bm{M}}_{1}{\bm{M}}_{2}\cdots{\bm{M}}_{k}\|_{\textup{op}}}{\rho^{k}}.

(2.4)

The quantity
\phi(\mathcal{M},\rho)
is defined in similar vein to that by
[
37
]
for LDS, and it captures the transient behavior of a system with state transition matrices varying in
\mathcal{M}
. Note that if
\rho(\mathcal{M})<1
, then for any
\rho>\rho(\mathcal{M})
, the quantity
\phi(\mathcal{M},\rho)
is finite. Now, given a set
\mathcal{U}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{p}
, we denote
\mathcal{U}\circ{\bm{A}}:=\{{\bm{A}}_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{p}({\bm{u}})_{i}{\bm{A}}_{i}:{\bm{u}}\in\mathcal{U}\}
and introduce the following definition of stability.

Definition 1

(
(\mathcal{U},\kappa,\rho)
-uniform-stability)
.

Let
\mathcal{U}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{p}
,
\kappa\geq 1
, and
0<\rho<1
. We say that a partially observed bilinear dynamical system (as defined in (
2.1
)) with state-transition matrices
{\bm{A}}:=\{{\bm{A}}_{0},\dots,{\bm{A}}_{p}\}
is
(\mathcal{U},\kappa,\rho)
-uniformly-stable, if the joint spectral radius of the set
\mathcal{U}\circ{\bm{A}}
satisfies: (i)
\rho(\mathcal{U}\circ{\bm{A}})<\rho<1
; and (ii)
\phi(\mathcal{U}\circ{\bm{A}},\rho)\leq\kappa
.

Observe that if there exists a nonempty and bounded set
\mathcal{U}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{p}
such that
\rho(\mathcal{U}\circ{\bm{A}})<1
, then for any
\rho(\mathcal{U}\circ{\bm{A}})<\rho<1
, the system is
(\mathcal{U},\kappa,\rho)
-uniformly-stable with
\kappa=\phi(\mathcal{U}\circ{\bm{A}},\rho)\vee 1
. We provide detailed discussion on this claim in Appendix
A
. Furthermore, we note that Definition
1
naturally generalizes that introduced by
[
35
]
. Indeed, there the authors assume that there exists
{\bm{u}}^{\star}
such that
\rho({\bm{u}}^{\star}\circ{\bm{A}})<1
. This is equivalent to assuming that their system is
(\{{\bm{u}}^{\star}\},\kappa,\rho)
-uniformly-stable for some
\kappa\geq 1
and
\rho({\bm{u}}^{\star}\circ{\bm{A}})<\rho<1
. As we shall make precise shortly, we need stronger requirements on the stability of the system in comparison with
[
35
]
because we are concerned with the task of identification. This requirement stems from the need to have persistence of excitation so that estimation is possible.

Input choice:

We consider that the inputs
\{{\bm{u}}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
are sampled in an i.i.d. manner from some distribution
\mathcal{D}_{{\bm{u}}}
on
\mathbb{R}^{p}
. For ease of exposition, we will focus on the case where inputs are sampled uniformly at random from a sphere of radius
\sqrt{p}
, that is,
{\bm{u}}_{t}\sim\mathrm{Unif}(\sqrt{p}\cdot\mathcal{S}^{p-1})
. More generally, as long as the inputs are isotropic and are bounded with high probability, our results will still hold at the expanse of longer proofs.

Putting together this input choice with the stability definition, we are now ready to present the assumption we make on the stability of the bilinear system (
2.1
).

Assumption 1

(Stability)
.

There exists
\kappa\geq 1
and
\rho\in(0,1)
such that the partially observed bilinear dynamical system (
2.1
) is
(\sqrt{p}\cdot\mathcal{S}^{p-1},\kappa,\rho)
-uniformly-stable.

In view of Assumption
1
, choosing inputs uniformly at random from
\sqrt{p}\cdot\mathcal{S}^{p-1}
guarantees stability almost surely.
More generally, we can choose to sample inputs from any set
\mathcal{U}
, so long as the system is stable under such set in the sense of Definition
1
. However, the quality of estimation depends on whether inputs sampled from
\mathcal{U}
are persistently exciting or not (see §
4.1
).

3
Learning the Markov-like Parameters

The Markov-like parameters are defined in a similar vein (except nonlinear input-output map) to the classical Markov parameters for partially observed LTI systems. By unrolling the dynamics (
2.1
), we can represent the output
{\bm{y}}_{t}
in terms of the past
L
inputs
{\bm{u}}_{t-L},\dots,{\bm{u}}_{t}
, for any
t\geq L
, as follows:

{\bm{y}}_{t}={\bm{C}}\!\left(\prod_{\ell=1}^{L}({\bm{u}}_{t-\ell}\circ{\bm{A}})\!\right)\!{\bm{x}}_{t-L}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}{\bm{C}}\!\left(\prod_{i=1}^{\ell-1}({\bm{u}}_{t-i}\circ{\bm{A}})\!\right)\!({\bm{B}}{\bm{u}}_{t-\ell}+{\bm{w}}_{t-\ell})+{\bm{D}}{\bm{u}}_{t}+{\bm{z}}_{t}.

(3.1)

We can simplify the form of (
3.1
) by adopting a more convenient notation and expanding further some of the products that involve the inputs. First, we introduce
\bm{\epsilon}_{t}:={\bm{C}}\!\left(\prod_{\ell=1}^{L}({\bm{u}}_{t-\ell}\circ{\bm{A}})\!\right)\!{\bm{x}}_{t-L}
, and define:

\displaystyle\bar{{\bm{u}}}_{t}^{\top}

\displaystyle:=\begin{bmatrix}1&{\bm{u}}_{t}^{\top}\end{bmatrix},\qquad\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}:=\begin{bmatrix}{\bm{u}}_{t}\\
{\bm{u}}_{t-1}\\
\bar{{\bm{u}}}_{t-1}\otimes{\bm{u}}_{t-2}\\
\bar{{\bm{u}}}_{t-1}\otimes\bar{{\bm{u}}}_{t-2}\otimes{\bm{u}}_{t-3}\\
\vdots\\
\bar{{\bm{u}}}_{t-1}\otimes\bar{{\bm{u}}}_{t-2}\otimes\cdots\otimes{\bm{u}}_{t-L}\end{bmatrix},\qquad\tilde{{\bm{w}}}_{t}:=\begin{bmatrix}{\bm{z}}_{t}\\
{\bm{w}}_{t-1}\\
{\bm{w}}_{t-2}\\
{\bm{w}}_{t-3}\\
\vdots\\
{\bm{w}}_{t-L}\end{bmatrix},

(3.2)

\displaystyle\text{and}\quad{\bm{F}}

\displaystyle:=\begin{bmatrix}{\bm{I}}_{m}&{\bm{C}}&{\bm{C}}({\bm{u}}_{t-1}\circ{\bm{A}})&{\bm{C}}\prod_{\ell=1}^{2}({\bm{u}}_{t-\ell}\circ{\bm{A}})&\ldots&{\bm{C}}\prod_{\ell=1}^{L-1}({\bm{u}}_{t-\ell}\circ{\bm{A}})\end{bmatrix}.

Moreover, let us define the matrix
{\bm{G}}
as follows:

\displaystyle{\bm{G}}

\displaystyle:=\left[\begin{array}[]{c c c c c}{\bm{D}}&{\bm{G}}_{1}&{\bm{G}}_{2}&\cdots&{\bm{G}}_{L}\end{array}\right]\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d_{\tilde{u}}},\quad\text{with}\quad d_{\tilde{u}}=(p+1)^{L}+p-1,

(3.3)

where
{\bm{G}}_{1}{=}{\bm{C}}{\bm{B}}
,
{\bm{G}}_{\ell}{=}\{{\bm{C}}{\bm{A}}_{i_{1}}\times\cdots\times{\bm{A}}_{i_{\ell-1}}{\bm{B}}\}_{i_{1},\dots,i_{\ell-1}\in\{0,\dots,p\}}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times p(p+1)^{\ell-1}}
, for
\ell\in\{2,\dots,L\}
. The parameters
\{{\bm{C}}{\bm{B}},\{{\bm{C}}{\bm{A}}_{i_{1}}{\bm{B}}\}_{i_{1}\in\{0,\dots,p\}},\dots,\{{\bm{C}}{\bm{A}}_{i_{1}}\times\cdots\times{\bm{A}}_{i_{L-1}}{\bm{B}}\}_{i_{1},\dots,i_{L-1}\in\{0,\dots,p\}}\}
are what we refer to as the
Markov-like parameters
of the system. We are finally ready to rewrite (
3.1
) in terms of these parameters as follows: for all
t\geq L
, we have

\displaystyle{\bm{y}}_{t}={\bm{G}}\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}+{\bm{F}}\tilde{{\bm{w}}}_{t}+\bm{\epsilon}_{t}.

(3.4)

With the dynamics written in the form of (
3.4
), it is natural to use the least squares estimation method to learn the Markov-like parameters from the observations
\{{\bm{y}}_{t},{\bm{u}}_{t}\}_{t=1}^{T}
. More specifically, the (minimum norm) Least Squares Estimator (LSE)
\hat{{\bm{G}}}
of
{\bm{G}}
admits a closed form and can be defined as:

\displaystyle\hat{{\bm{G}}}:=\left(\sum_{t=L}^{T}{\bm{y}}_{t}\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}^{\top}\right)\left(\sum_{t=L}^{T}\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}^{\top}\right)^{\dagger}

\displaystyle\in\argmin_{{\bm{G}}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d_{\tilde{u}}}}\sum_{t=L}^{T}\|{{\bm{y}}_{t}-{\bm{G}}\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{2}.

(3.5)

Moreover, when the matrix
\sum_{t=L}^{T}\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}^{\top}\succ 0
, then estimation error can be expressed as:

\displaystyle\hat{{\bm{G}}}-{\bm{G}}=\left(\sum_{t=L}^{T}({\bm{F}}\tilde{{\bm{w}}}_{t}+\bm{\epsilon}_{t})\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}^{\top}\right)\left(\sum_{t=L}^{T}\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}^{\top}\right)^{-1}.

(3.6)

We now present Theorem
1
, our main result on the recovery of the
Markov-like parameters
:

Theorem 1

(Learning Markov-like parameters)
.

Let
\delta\in(0,1)
,
T\geq 0
. Suppose Assumption
1
holds, and the inputs are sampled uniformly at random from a sphere of radius
\sqrt{p}
, that is,
\{{\bm{u}}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}\overset{\text{i.i.d.}}{\sim}\mathrm{Unif}(\sqrt{p}\cdot\mathcal{S}^{p-1})
. Then the event:

\displaystyle\|\hat{{\bm{G}}}-{\bm{G}}\|_{\textup{op}}\leq\frac{C(\kappa^{2}\rho^{L}+\kappa)}{1-\rho}\sqrt{\frac{Lp^{2}(p+1)^{(L+1)}\left(\log\left(\frac{eL}{\delta}\right)+m+nL+(p+1)^{L+1}\right)}{T-L}}

(3.7)

holds with probability at least
1-\delta
, provided that

\displaystyle T-L

\displaystyle\gtrsim L(L+1)\left(\frac{3p}{p+2}\right)^{L+1}\left(\log\left(\frac{(L+1)}{\delta}\right)+(p+1)^{L+1}\log\left(\frac{(p+1)^{L+1}}{\delta}\right)\right),

(3.8)

with positive constant
C=\textup{poly}(\sigma,\|{\bm{B}}\|_{\textup{op}},\|{\bm{C}}\|_{\textup{op}})
.

We discuss the analysis of the estimation error leading to Theorem
1
in §
4
. There, we also highlight the key challenges and steps in establishing this result. From the bound in (
3.7
), we see the recovery error
\|\hat{{\bm{G}}}-{\bm{G}}\|_{\textup{op}}
scales as, ignoring all other dependencies,
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\sqrt{(p+1)^{2(L+1)}/(T-L)})
. This contrasts with partially observed linear systems where typically we only have a polynomial dependence in
L
, and also reflects the difficulty in learning bilinear systems from partial observations. To recover the unknown matrices
{\bm{C}},{\bm{A}}_{0},\dots,{\bm{A}}_{p},{\bm{B}}
, we require
L
large enough, typically larger than
2n
(see Remark
1
).

Remark 1

(The BLDS parameter recovery.)
.

We remark that, for every
k\in\{0,\dots,p\}
, we can directly extract from
\hat{{\bm{G}}}
, estimates of the matrices

\{{\bm{C}}{\bm{B}},{\bm{C}}{\bm{A}}_{k}{\bm{B}},\dots,{\bm{C}}{\bm{A}}_{k}^{L-1}{\bm{B}}\}
. To see that, observe that letting
\mathcal{I}_{k,\ell}\subset\{1,\dots,d_{\tilde{{\bm{u}}}}\}
be the
p
indices corresponding to the
p
-dimensional sub-vector of
\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}
,
(\prod_{i=1}^{\ell-1}({\bm{u}}_{t-i})_{k}){\bm{u}}_{t-\ell}
, then
{\bm{G}}_{:,\mathcal{I}_{k,\ell}}={\bm{C}}{\bm{A}}_{k}^{\ell-1}{\bm{B}}
. In other words, we can take
\hat{{\bm{G}}}_{:,\mathcal{I}_{\ell,k}}
to be an estimate
{\bm{C}}{\bm{A}}_{k}^{\ell-1}{\bm{B}}
. We then construct a Hankel matrix from
\hat{{\bm{G}}}_{:,\mathcal{I}_{\ell,k}}
. Under the condition that our estimation error is sufficiently small, each pair
({\bm{A}}_{k},{\bm{B}})
is controllable, each pair
({\bm{A}}_{k},{\bm{C}})
is observable, and
L\geq 2n
, we can use classic Ho-Kalman algorithm
[
18
]
to estimate
{\bm{A}}_{0},{\bm{A}}_{1},\dots,{\bm{A}}_{p},{\bm{B}},{\bm{C}}
up to a similarity transform, with robustness guarantees
[
39
]
. Lastly, note that the estimate of
{\bm{D}}
is obtained as the first
p
columns of
\hat{{\bm{G}}}
.

4
Sample Complexity Analysis

To prove Theorem
1
, we start our analysis by decomposing the estimation error as follows:

\displaystyle\left\|\hat{{\bm{G}}}-{\bm{G}}\right\|_{\textup{op}}\leq\underbrace{\left\|\left(\sum_{t=L}^{T}\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}^{\top}\right)^{\dagger}\right\|_{\textup{op}}}_{\textrm{Excitation}}\Bigg(\underbrace{\left\|\sum_{t=L}^{T}\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}({\bm{F}}\tilde{{\bm{w}}}_{t})^{\top}\right\|_{\textup{op}}}_{\textrm{Mutiplier Process}}+\underbrace{\left\|\sum_{t=L}^{T}\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}\bm{\epsilon}_{t}^{\top}\right\|_{\textup{op}}}_{\textrm{Truncation Bias}}\Bigg),

(4.1)

where we use the submultiplicativity of
\|\cdot\|_{\textup{op}}
and the triangular inequality. Next, we will analyze each of three terms appearing in the decomposition above separately and obtain corresponding bounds in high probability. Once these bounds have been established, the proof concludes immediately (see details in Appendix
C.1
). In what follows, we focus on presenting the results regarding the analysis of the three terms.
We note that the challenge in analyzing this terms lies in the presence of non-trivial dependencies and nonlinearities. As such, recent analysis tools from the non-asymptotic system identification literature

do not apply, and this is precisely what we manage to tackle.

4.1
Persistence of Excitation

We show persistence of excitation which is necessary to ensure that the LSE is a consistent estimator. More precisely, we will establish that smallest singular value of the design matrix
\tilde{{\bm{U}}}
whose rows correspond to
\{\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}^{\top}\}_{t=L}^{T}
is bounded from below by
\tilde{\Omega}(\sqrt{T-L+1})
. One of the major sources of difficulty in establishing this persistence of excitation result challenging is the nonlinear dependence of
\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}
on
{\bm{u}}_{t-L},\dots,{\bm{u}}_{t}
for all
t\geq L
. We need to understand how distributional properties of the input impact the lower spectrum of
\tilde{{\bm{U}}}^{\top}\tilde{{\bm{U}}}
. To that end, we start by introducing the property of hypercontractivity.

Definition 2

(Hypercontractivity)
.

A
p

is
(4,2,\gamma)

\mathbb{E}[({\bm{u}}^{\top}{\bm{x}})^{4}]\leq\gamma\mathbb{E}[({\bm{u}}^{\top}{\bm{x}})^{2}]^{2}

{\bm{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}

The
(4,2,\gamma)

p
-dimensional standard gaussian random vectors satisfies it with
\gamma=3
, while
p
-dimensional random vectors sampled uniformly from
\sqrt{p}\cdot\mathcal{S}^{p-1}
satisfies
\gamma{=}3/(1+2/p)
. We refer the reader to Appendix
B
for a proof to these claims.

(Distributional properties of the input)
.

\{{\bm{u}}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
is a sequence of independent zero-mean, isotropic

Again, it can be verified that Assumption

4.2
Analysis of the Multiplier Process

The analysis of the the multiplier process term
\|\sum_{t=L}^{T}\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}({\bm{F}}\tilde{{\bm{w}}}_{t})^{\top}\|_{\textup{op}}
is somewhat simpler than that of truncation bias term. The reason is because the sequences
\{\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}\}_{t\geq L}
, and
\{\tilde{{\bm{w}}}_{t}\}_{t\geq L}
are independent with zero-mean vectors. However, each of these two sequences contains dependent vectors. Below, we present a high probability bound on the multiplier process term showing that we can still control this term despite the presence of these dependencies:

Proposition 1
.

Let
\delta\in(0,1)
and
T\geq L
. The event:

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{t=L}^{T}\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}({\bm{F}}\tilde{{\bm{w}}}_{t})^{\top}\right\|_{\textup{op}}\leq C_{2}\frac{\kappa}{1-\rho}\sqrt{L(T-L)(p+1)^{L+1}\left(\log\left(\frac{eL}{\delta}\right)+m+nL+(p+1)^{L+1}\right)}

holds with probability
1-\delta
,
with a positive constant
C_{2}=\textup{poly}(\sigma,\|{\bm{C}}\|_{\textup{op}})
.

The key idea behind the proof of Proposition
1
is observing that each of the subsequences,
\ell\in\{1,\dots,L\}
,
\{\tilde{{\bm{w}}}_{kL+\ell}\}_{k\geq 0}
, has independent vectors. Thus, we can use a blocking trick to rewrite the multiplier process as a sum of
L
martingales which can bound using classical concentration tools. This argument is made precise in the proof and is deferred to Appendix
C.2
.

4.3
Analysis of the Truncation Bias

The analysis of the truncation bias term
\|\sum_{t=L}^{T}\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}\bm{\epsilon}_{t}^{\top}\|_{\textup{op}}
is challenging for multiple reasons. Indeed, first, the sequences
\{\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}\}_{t\geq L}
and
\{\bm{\epsilon}_{t}\}_{t\geq L}
are non-trivially dependent, and second,
\bm{\epsilon}_{t}
is only zero-mean conditioned on future inputs
{\bm{u}}_{t-L},\dots,{\bm{u}}_{T}
and is still dependent on
{\bm{u}}_{0},\dots,{\bm{u}}_{t-L-1}
. It is worth noting that this type of dependence does not arise when learning partially observed linear dynamical systems. Indeed, the analysis of the truncation bias term in linear systems involves
\bm{\epsilon}_{t}
that are only dependent on the covariates
{\bm{x}}_{t-L}
and are independent of
{\bm{u}}_{t-L},\dots,{\bm{u}}_{t}
(e.g., see
[
40
]
). In addition to that, when learning linear systems
L
can be made large enough to trivially bound the truncation bias, whereas in bilinear systems our bounds pay exponential dependence on
L
so choosing this parameter more carefully is more important.
Nonetheless, we establish a high probability bound on the truncation bias as presented below:

Proposition 2
.

Let
\delta\in(0,1)
and
T\geq L
. The event:

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{t=L}^{T}\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}\bm{\epsilon}_{t}^{\top}\right\|_{\textup{op}}\leq\frac{C_{1}\kappa^{2}\rho^{L}}{1-\rho}\sqrt{(T-L)p^{2}(p+1)^{L+1}\left(\log\left(\frac{e}{\delta}\right)+n+(p+1)^{L+1}\right)}

holds with probability at least
1-\delta
,
with a positive constant
C_{1}=\textup{poly}(\sigma,\|{\bm{B}}\|_{\textup{op}},\|{\bm{C}}\|_{\textup{op}})
.

The proof of Proposition
2
relies on the critical observation that the truncation bias term can be rewritten as martingale, namely as follows: for all
\theta\in\mathcal{S}^{d_{\tilde{{\bm{u}}}}-1},\lambda\in\mathcal{S}^{m-1}
,

\displaystyle\theta^{\top}\left(\sum_{t=L}^{T}\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}\bm{\epsilon}_{t}^{\top}\right)\lambda=\sum_{s=0}^{T-L-1}({\bm{B}}{\bm{u}}_{s}+{\bm{w}}_{s})^{\top}{\bm{f}}_{s}(\theta,\lambda,,{\bm{u}}_{s+1},\dots,{\bm{u}}_{T}),

(4.4)

where the functions
\{{\bm{f}}_{s}\}_{s\geq 0}
are nonlinear in their arguments. Moreover, thanks to our choice of inputs and the stability Assumption

{\bm{f}}_{s}(\theta,\lambda,,{\bm{u}}_{s+1},\dots,{\bm{u}}_{T})
are bounded and do not scale with

\{{\bm{f}}_{s}\}_{s\geq 0}
are delayed until Appendix

6
Conclusion and Future Direction

We provide the first non-asymptotic learning bounds for partially observed BLDS.
Given finite input-output data sampled from a single trajectory of BLDS, we learn its Markov-like parameters, provide an upper bound on the estimation error with
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(1/\sqrt{T})
dependence, and provide a bound on the number of samples required, scaling as
\tilde{\Omega}\left((p+1)^{L+1}\right)
. These parameters uniquely characterize the input-output map of a BLDS via nonlinear input features, hence, can be used to recover the state-space matrices.
Our results hold under a novel notion of stability that generalizes the classic notion of stability for LDS to the BLDS.

There are several interesting future directions. First, can the exponential dependence on
L
be avoided? We believe this can be done by carefully designing the inputs such that the number of Markov-like parameters do not grow exponentially in
L
. Second, can our results be extended to account for marginally-stable BLDS? This requires stabilization of a partially observed BLDS with unknown state-space matrices which itself is an interesting future direction. Other possible directions include exploring the benefit of regularization (e.g., Hankel nuclear norm regularization) for BLDS identification, exploring gradient-based methods for learning partially observed BLDS, and adaptive control of BLDS.

[\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}]_{0}={\bm{u}}_{t}
, and let
[\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}]_{i}
be the
i
-th partition of
\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}
with
p(p+1)^{i-1}
entries for
i=1,\dots,L
.
Then, we have that
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[[\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}]_{0}[\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}]_{0}^{\top}\right]=\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[[\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}]_{1}[\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}]_{1}^{\top}\right]={\bm{I}}_{p}
. Similarly, for
i=2,\dots,L
, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[[\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}]_{i}[\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}]_{i}^{\top}\right]

\displaystyle=\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[(\bar{{\bm{u}}}_{t-1}\otimes\cdots\otimes{\bm{u}}_{t-i})(\bar{{\bm{u}}}_{t-1}\otimes\cdots\otimes{\bm{u}}_{t-i})^{\top}\right],

\displaystyle=\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[\bar{{\bm{u}}}_{t-1}\bar{{\bm{u}}}_{t-1}^{\top}\otimes\cdots\otimes{\bm{u}}_{t-i}{\bm{u}}_{t-i}^{\top}\right],

\displaystyle={\bm{I}}_{p+1}\otimes\cdots\otimes{\bm{I}}_{p}={\bm{I}}_{p(p+1)^{i-1}}.

(B.2)

Hence, we show that
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[[\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}]_{i}[\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}]_{i}^{\top}\right]={\bm{I}}_{p(p+1)^{(i-1)\vee 0}}
for all
i=0,\dots,L
. Next, we will show that
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[[\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}]_{i}[\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}]_{j}^{\top}\right]=0
, for all
i,j=0,\dots,L
when
i\neq j
. Because of symmetry, it suffices to consider
i<j
. In this case, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[[\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}]_{i}[\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}]_{j}^{\top}\right]

\displaystyle=\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[(\bar{{\bm{u}}}_{t-1}\otimes\cdots\otimes{\bm{u}}_{t-i})(\bar{{\bm{u}}}_{t-1}\otimes\cdots\otimes{\bm{u}}_{t-j})^{\top}\right],

\displaystyle=\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[(\bar{{\bm{u}}}_{t-1}\otimes\cdots\otimes{\bm{u}}_{t-i})(\bar{{\bm{u}}}_{t-1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\bar{{\bm{u}}}_{t-i})^{\top}\right]\otimes\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[(\bar{{\bm{u}}}_{t-i-1}\otimes\cdots\otimes{\bm{u}}_{t-j})^{\top}\right],

\displaystyle=0,

(B.3)

for all
2\leq i<j
. Similarly, we also have
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[[\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}]_{1}[\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}]_{j}^{\top}\right]=\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[{\bm{u}}_{t-1}(\bar{{\bm{u}}}_{t-1}\otimes\cdots\otimes{\bm{u}}_{t-j})^{\top}\right]=0
, for all
j=2,\dots,L
. Lastly,
[\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}]_{0}
is independent of
[\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}]_{j}
, for all
j=1,\dots,L
, due to Assumption
2
, hence,
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[[\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}]_{0}[\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}]_{j}^{\top}\right]=0
.
Combining (
B.2
) and (
B.3
), we get

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}^{\top}]={\bm{I}}_{d_{\tilde{u}}}\quad\implies\quad\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[(\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}^{\top}{\bm{v}})^{2}]=1,

(B.4)

for all
{\bm{v}}\in\mathcal{S}^{d_{\tilde{u}}-1}
. This gives the first statement of Lemma
4
. Next, we show that
\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}
also have bounded fourth moment marginals as follows:

Fourth Moment Marginals:
Let
{\bm{v}}\in\mathcal{S}^{d_{\tilde{{\bm{u}}}}-1}
, and consider the random variable
({\bm{v}}^{\top}\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t})^{2}
.
In the following we will upper bound
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[({\bm{v}}^{\top}\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t})^{4}]
. To begin, let
{\bm{v}}_{i}
denote the
i
-th partition of
{\bm{v}}
with
p(p+1)^{(i-1)\vee 0}
entries, for
i=0,\dots,L
. Then,
{\bm{v}}
can be represented as,

\displaystyle{\bm{v}}:=\begin{bmatrix}{\bm{v}}_{0}^{\top}&{\bm{v}}_{1}^{\top}&{\bm{v}}_{2}^{\top}&\ldots&{\bm{v}}_{L}^{\top}\end{bmatrix}^{\top}.

(B.5)

Hence, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left({\bm{v}}^{\top}\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}\right)^{4}\right]

\displaystyle=\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left(\sum_{i=0}^{L}{\bm{v}}_{i}^{\top}[\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}]_{i}\right)^{4}\right],

\displaystyle=\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left({\bm{v}}_{0}^{\top}{\bm{u}}_{t}+{\bm{v}}_{1}^{\top}{\bm{u}}_{t-1}+\sum_{i=2}^{L}{\bm{v}}_{i}^{\top}(\bar{{\bm{u}}}_{t-1}\otimes\cdots\otimes{\bm{u}}_{t-i})\right)^{4}\right].

(B.6)

In the following, we will upper each term appearing on the right-hand-side (RHS) of (
B.6
) to get an upper bound on the fourth moment marginals of
\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}
.

\bullet

Linear Terms:

For
i\neq j\neq k\neq\ell
, consider the following expectation: We utilize
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[{\bm{u}}_{t-\max\{i,j,k,\ell\}}]=0
, to show that

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[({\bm{v}}_{i}^{\top}[\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}]_{i})({\bm{v}}_{j}^{\top}[\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}]_{j})({\bm{v}}_{k}^{\top}[\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}]_{k})({\bm{v}}_{\ell}^{\top}[\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}]_{\ell})\right]=0.

(B.7)

\bullet

Quadratic Terms I:
For
i\neq j\neq k
, consider the following expectation: When
i<\max\{j,k\}
, we can utilize
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[{\bm{u}}_{t-\max\{j,k\}}]=0
, to show that

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[({\bm{v}}_{i}^{\top}[\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}]_{i})^{2}({\bm{v}}_{j}^{\top}[\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}]_{j})({\bm{v}}_{k}^{\top}[\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}]_{k})\right]

\displaystyle=0.

(B.8)

However, when
i>\max\{j,k\}
, the above expectation is not zero. We can upper bound these terms using Cauchy-Schwarz inequality as follows,

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[({\bm{v}}_{i}^{\top}[\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}]_{i})^{2}({\bm{v}}_{j}^{\top}[\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}]_{j})({\bm{v}}_{k}^{\top}[\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}]_{k})\right]

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[({\bm{v}}_{i}^{\top}[\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}]_{i})^{4}\right]\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[({\bm{v}}_{j}^{\top}[\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}]_{j})^{2}({\bm{v}}_{k}^{\top}[\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}]_{k})^{2}\right]}.

(B.9)

In the remaining of the proof, we will upper bound the two terms on the RHS of (
B.9
) individually, along-with the remaining terms on the RHS of (
B.6
).

\bullet

Quadratic Terms II:
For
i\neq j
, consider the expectation
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[({\bm{v}}_{i}^{\top}[\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}]_{i})^{2}({\bm{v}}_{j}^{\top}[\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}]_{j})^{2}\right]
. In order to upper bound this term, we first upper bound an intermediate term as follows:
Let
[\tilde{\tilde{{\bm{u}}}}_{t}]_{k}
denote the
k
-th partition of
\tilde{\tilde{{\bm{u}}}}_{t}
defined in (
B.1
), for
k=1,\dots,L
. Let
{\bm{q}}_{k},{\bm{q}}^{\prime}_{k}
be two arbitrary vectors in
{\bm{R}}^{(p+1)^{k}}
. We will use the mathematical induction to prove the following intermediate result under Assumption
2
,

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[({\bm{q}}_{k}^{\top}[\tilde{\tilde{{\bm{u}}}}_{t}]_{k})^{2}({\bm{q}}_{k}^{\prime\top}[\tilde{\tilde{{\bm{u}}}}_{t}]_{k})^{2}\right]\leq(3\vee\gamma)^{k}\|{{\bm{q}}_{k}}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{2}\|{{\bm{q}}_{k}^{\prime}}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{2},\quad\text{for all}\quad k=1,\dots,L.

(B.10)

We begin the proof, by showing that,
k=1
obeys the induction as follows,

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[({\bm{q}}_{1}^{\top}[\tilde{\tilde{{\bm{u}}}}_{t}]_{1})^{2}({\bm{q}}_{1}^{\prime\top}[\tilde{\tilde{{\bm{u}}}}_{t}]_{1})^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad=\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left({\bm{q}}_{1}^{\top}\bar{{\bm{u}}}_{t-1}\right)^{2}\left({\bm{q}}_{1}^{\prime\top}\bar{{\bm{u}}}_{t-1}\right)^{2}\right],

\displaystyle\qquad\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{(i)}}}{{=}}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left(q_{11}+{\bm{q}}_{12}^{\top}{\bm{u}}_{t-1}\right)^{2}\left(q_{11}^{\prime}+{\bm{q}}_{12}^{\prime\top}{\bm{u}}_{t-1}\right)^{2}\right],

\displaystyle\qquad=\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left(q_{11}^{2}+({\bm{q}}_{12}^{\top}{\bm{u}}_{t-1})^{2}+2q_{11}{\bm{q}}_{12}^{\top}{\bm{u}}_{t-1}\right)\left(q_{11}^{\prime 2}+({\bm{q}}_{12}^{\prime\top}{\bm{u}}_{t-1})^{2}+2q_{11}^{\prime}{\bm{q}}_{12}^{\prime\top}{\bm{u}}_{t-1}\right)\right],

\displaystyle\qquad\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{(ii)}}}{{\leq}}q_{11}^{2}q_{11}^{\prime 2}+q_{11}^{2}\|{{\bm{q}}_{12}^{\prime}}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{2}+q_{11}^{\prime 2}\|{{\bm{q}}_{12}}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{2}+\gamma\|{{\bm{q}}_{12}}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{2}\|{{\bm{q}}_{12}^{\prime}}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{2}+4q_{11}q_{11}^{\prime}\|{{\bm{q}}_{12}}\|_{\ell_{2}}\|{{\bm{q}}_{12}^{\prime}}\|_{\ell_{2}},

\displaystyle\qquad\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{(ii)}}}{{\leq}}q_{11}^{2}q_{11}^{\prime 2}+3q_{11}^{2}\|{{\bm{q}}_{12}^{\prime}}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{2}+3q_{11}^{\prime 2}\|{{\bm{q}}_{12}}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{2}+\gamma\|{{\bm{q}}_{12}}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{2}\|{{\bm{q}}_{12}^{\prime}}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{2},

\displaystyle\qquad\leq(3\vee\gamma)\left(q_{11}^{2}+\|{{\bm{q}}_{12}}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{2}\right)\left(q_{11}^{\prime 2}+\|{{\bm{q}}_{12}}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{\prime 2}\right)=(3\vee\gamma)\|{{\bm{q}}_{1}}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{2}\|{{\bm{q}}_{1}^{\prime}}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{2},

(B.11)

where we obtain (i) from setting
{\bm{q}}_{1}=[q_{11}~~{\bm{q}}_{12}^{\top}]^{\top}
,
{\bm{q}}_{1}^{\prime}=[q_{11}^{\prime}~~{\bm{q}}_{12}^{\prime\top}]^{\top}
, (ii) from applying Lemma
3
along-with Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, and (iii) is obtained by using the identity
2ab\leq a^{2}+b^{2}
for
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
.
Suppose we have
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left({\bm{q}}_{k-1}^{\top}[\tilde{\tilde{{\bm{u}}}}_{t}]_{k-1}\right)^{2}\left({\bm{q}}_{k-1}^{\prime\top}[\tilde{\tilde{{\bm{u}}}}_{t}]_{k-1}\right)^{2}\right]\leq(3\vee\gamma)^{k-1}\|{{\bm{q}}_{k-1}}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{2}\|{{\bm{q}}_{k-1}^{\prime}}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{2}
. Then, we apply the induction as follows,

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left({\bm{q}}_{k}^{\top}[\tilde{\tilde{{\bm{u}}}}_{t}]_{k}\right)^{2}\left({\bm{q}}_{k}^{\prime\top}[\tilde{\tilde{{\bm{u}}}}_{t}]_{k}\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left({\bm{q}}_{k}^{\top}([\tilde{\tilde{{\bm{u}}}}_{t}]_{k-1}\otimes\bar{{\bm{u}}}_{t-k})\right)^{2}\left({\bm{q}}_{k}^{\prime\top}([\tilde{\tilde{{\bm{u}}}}_{t}]_{k-1}\otimes\bar{{\bm{u}}}_{t-k})\right)^{2}\right],

\displaystyle=\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left(\bar{{\bm{u}}}_{t-k}^{\top}{\bm{Q}}_{k}[\tilde{\tilde{{\bm{u}}}}_{t}]_{k-1}\right)^{2}\left(\bar{{\bm{u}}}_{t-k}^{\top}{\bm{Q}}_{k}^{\prime}[\tilde{\tilde{{\bm{u}}}}_{t}]_{k-1}\right)^{2}\right],

\displaystyle=\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left(\bar{{\bm{u}}}_{t-k}^{\top}{\bm{Q}}_{k}[\tilde{\tilde{{\bm{u}}}}_{t}]_{k-1}\right)^{2}\left(\bar{{\bm{u}}}_{t-k}^{\top}{\bm{Q}}_{k}^{\prime}[\tilde{\tilde{{\bm{u}}}}_{t}]_{k-1}\right)^{2}{~\big|~}{\bm{u}}_{t-k}\right]\right],

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{(i)}}}{{\leq}}(3\vee\gamma)^{k-1}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[\|{{\bm{Q}}_{k}^{\top}\bar{{\bm{u}}}_{t-k}}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{2}\|{{\bm{Q}}_{k}^{\prime\top}\bar{{\bm{u}}}_{t-k}}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{2}\right],

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{(ii)}}}{{\leq}}(3\vee\gamma)^{k}\|{{\bm{Q}}_{k}}\|_{F}^{2}\|{{\bm{Q}}_{k}^{\prime}}\|_{F}^{2},

\displaystyle=(3\vee\gamma)^{k}\|{{\bm{q}}_{k}}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{2}\|{{\bm{q}}_{k}^{\prime}}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{2},

(B.12)

where
{\bm{Q}}_{k}=\textup{{mtx}}({\bm{q}}_{k})\in\mathbb{R}^{(p+1)\times(p+1)^{k-1}}
,
{\bm{Q}}_{k}^{\prime}=\textup{{mtx}}({\bm{q}}_{k}^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{(p+1)\times(p+1)^{k-1}}
, we obtain (i) from the induction hypothesis, and (ii) follows from a similar line of reasoning as used to derive an upper bound in (
B.11
). This completes the proof of our intermediate result in (
B.10
). Next, we use (
B.10
) to derive an upper bound on
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[({\bm{v}}_{i}^{\top}[\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}]_{i})^{2}({\bm{v}}_{j}^{\top}[\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}]_{j})^{2}\right]
as follows: Due to symmetry, it is sufficient to consider
j>i
. We begin by deriving the upper bound for
j>i\geq 2
as follows,

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[({\bm{v}}_{i}^{\top}[\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}]_{i})^{2}({\bm{v}}_{j}^{\top}[\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}]_{j})^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad=\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left({\bm{v}}_{i}^{\top}(\bar{{\bm{u}}}_{t-1}\otimes\cdots\otimes{\bm{u}}_{t-i})\right)^{2}\left({\bm{v}}_{j}^{\top}(\bar{{\bm{u}}}_{t-1}\otimes\cdots\otimes{\bm{u}}_{t-j})\right)^{2}\right],

\displaystyle\qquad=\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left({\bm{v}}_{i}^{\top}(\bar{{\bm{u}}}_{t-1}\otimes\cdots\otimes{\bm{u}}_{t-i})\right)^{2}\left({\bm{v}}_{j}^{\top}(\bar{{\bm{u}}}_{t-1}\otimes\cdots\otimes{\bm{u}}_{t-j})\right)^{2}{~\big|~}{\bm{u}}_{t-i},{\bm{u}}_{t-i-1},\dots,{\bm{u}}_{t-j}\right]\right],

\displaystyle\qquad\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{(i)}}}{{=}}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\left({\bm{u}}_{t-i}^{\top}{\bm{V}}_{i}(\bar{{\bm{u}}}_{t-1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\bar{{\bm{u}}}_{t-i+1})\right)^{2}\left((\bar{{\bm{u}}}_{t-i}^{\top}\otimes\cdots\otimes{\bm{u}}_{t-j}^{\top}){\bm{V}}_{j}(\bar{{\bm{u}}}_{t-1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\bar{{\bm{u}}}_{t-i+1})\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad{~\big|~}{\bm{u}}_{t-i},{\bm{u}}_{t-i-1},\dots,{\bm{u}}_{t-j}\big]\big],

\displaystyle\qquad=\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left({\bm{u}}_{t-i}^{\top}{\bm{V}}_{i}[\tilde{\tilde{{\bm{u}}}}_{t}]_{i-1}\right)^{2}\left((\bar{{\bm{u}}}_{t-i}^{\top}\otimes\cdots\otimes{\bm{u}}_{t-j}^{\top}){\bm{V}}_{j}[\tilde{\tilde{{\bm{u}}}}_{t}]_{i-1}\right)^{2}{~\big|~}{\bm{u}}_{t-i},{\bm{u}}_{t-i-1},\dots,{\bm{u}}_{t-j}\right]\right],

\displaystyle\qquad\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{(ii)}}}{{\leq}}(3\vee\gamma)^{i-1}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[\|{{\bm{V}}_{i}^{\top}{\bm{u}}_{t-i}}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{2}\|{{\bm{V}}_{j}^{\top}(\bar{{\bm{u}}}_{t-i}\otimes\cdots\otimes{\bm{u}}_{t-j})}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{2}\right],

(B.13)

where we obtain (i) from tower rule, and setting
{\bm{V}}_{i}=\textup{{mtx}}({\bm{v}}_{i})\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times(p+1)^{i-1}}
,
{\bm{V}}_{j}=\textup{{mtx}}({\bm{v}}_{j})\in\mathbb{R}^{p(p+1)^{j-i}\times(p+1)^{i-1}}
, and (ii) is obtained by using the intermediate result from (
B.10
). To proceed, observe that

\displaystyle\bar{{\bm{u}}}_{t-i}\otimes\cdots\otimes{\bm{u}}_{t-j}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}\bar{{\bm{u}}}_{t-i-1}\otimes\cdots\otimes{\bm{u}}_{t-j}\\
{\bm{u}}_{t-i}\otimes\bar{{\bm{u}}}_{t-i-1}\otimes\cdots\otimes{\bm{u}}_{t-j}\end{bmatrix},\quad{\bm{V}}_{j}=\begin{bmatrix}{\bm{V}}_{j1}\\
{\bm{V}}_{j2}\end{bmatrix},

(B.14)

where
{\bm{V}}_{j1}\in\mathbb{R}^{p(p+1)^{j-i-1}\times(p+1)^{i-1}}
, and
{\bm{V}}_{j2}\in\mathbb{R}^{p^{2}(p+1)^{j-i-1}\times(p+1)^{i-1}}
. Combining this with (
B.13
), we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[({\bm{v}}_{i}^{\top}[\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}]_{i})^{2}({\bm{v}}_{j}^{\top}[\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}]_{j})^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq(3\vee\gamma)^{i-1}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\|{{\bm{V}}_{i}^{\top}{\bm{u}}_{t-i}}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{2}\|{\bm{V}}_{j1}^{\top}(\bar{{\bm{u}}}_{t-i-1}\otimes\cdots\otimes{\bm{u}}_{t-j})

\displaystyle+{\bm{V}}_{j2}^{\top}({\bm{u}}_{t-i}\otimes\bar{{\bm{u}}}_{t-i-1}\otimes\cdots\otimes{\bm{u}}_{t-j})\|_{\ell_{2}}^{2}\big],

\displaystyle\leq(3\vee\gamma)^{i-1}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\|{{\bm{V}}_{i}^{\top}{\bm{u}}_{t-i}}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{2}\big(\|{{\bm{V}}_{j1}^{\top}(\bar{{\bm{u}}}_{t-i-1}\otimes\cdots\otimes{\bm{u}}_{t-j})}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\|{{\bm{V}}_{j2}^{\top}({\bm{u}}_{t-i}\otimes\bar{{\bm{u}}}_{t-i-1}\otimes\cdots\otimes{\bm{u}}_{t-j})}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle+2(\bar{{\bm{u}}}_{t-i-1}\otimes\cdots\otimes{\bm{u}}_{t-j})^{\top}{\bm{V}}_{j1}{\bm{V}}_{j2}^{\top}({\bm{u}}_{t-i}\otimes\bar{{\bm{u}}}_{t-i-1}\otimes\cdots\otimes{\bm{u}}_{t-j})\big)\big],

(B.15)

In the following, we will upper bound each term in (
B.15
) separately to get an upper bound on
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[({\bm{v}}_{i}^{\top}[\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}]_{i})^{2}({\bm{v}}_{j}^{\top}[\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}]_{j})^{2}\right]
. To begin, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\|{{\bm{V}}_{i}^{\top}{\bm{u}}_{t-i}}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{2}\|{{\bm{V}}_{j1}^{\top}(\bar{{\bm{u}}}_{t-i-1}\otimes\cdots\otimes{\bm{u}}_{t-j})}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{2}\big]

\displaystyle\qquad=\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[{\bm{u}}_{t-i}^{\top}{\bm{V}}_{i}{\bm{V}}_{i}^{\top}{\bm{u}}_{t-i}\big]\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[(\bar{{\bm{u}}}_{t-i-1}\otimes\cdots\otimes{\bm{u}}_{t-j})^{\top}{\bm{V}}_{j1}{\bm{V}}_{j1}^{\top}(\bar{{\bm{u}}}_{t-i-1}\otimes\cdots\otimes{\bm{u}}_{t-j})\big],

\displaystyle\qquad=\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\textup{{tr}}\big({\bm{V}}_{i}{\bm{V}}_{i}^{\top}{\bm{u}}_{t-i}{\bm{u}}_{t-i}^{\top}\big)\big]\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\textup{{tr}}\big({\bm{V}}_{j1}{\bm{V}}_{j1}^{\top}(\bar{{\bm{u}}}_{t-i-1}\otimes\cdots\otimes{\bm{u}}_{t-j})(\bar{{\bm{u}}}_{t-i-1}\otimes\cdots\otimes{\bm{u}}_{t-j})^{\top}\big)\big],

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\|{{\bm{V}}_{i}}\|_{F}^{2}\|{{\bm{V}}_{j1}}\|_{F}^{2}.

(B.16)

Similarly, the second term in (
B.15
) can be upper bounded as follows,

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\|{{\bm{V}}_{i}^{\top}{\bm{u}}_{t-i}}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{2}\|{{\bm{V}}_{j2}^{\top}({\bm{u}}_{t-i}\otimes\bar{{\bm{u}}}_{t-i-1}\otimes\cdots\otimes{\bm{u}}_{t-j})}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{2}\big]

\displaystyle\qquad\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{(a)}}}{{=}}\sum_{k=1}^{(p+1)^{i-1}}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\|{{\bm{V}}_{i}^{\top}{\bm{u}}_{t-i}}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{2}\big({\bm{v}}_{j2k}^{\top}({\bm{u}}_{t-i}\otimes\bar{{\bm{u}}}_{t-i-1}\otimes\cdots\otimes{\bm{u}}_{t-j})\big)^{2}\big],

\displaystyle\qquad\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{(b)}}}{{=}}\sum_{k=1}^{(p+1)^{i-1}}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\|{{\bm{V}}_{i}^{\top}{\bm{u}}_{t-i}}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{2}\big((\bar{{\bm{u}}}_{t-i-1}\otimes\cdots\otimes{\bm{u}}_{t-j})^{\top}{\bm{V}}_{j2k}{\bm{u}}_{t-i}\big)^{2}\big],

\displaystyle\qquad=\sum_{k=1}^{(p+1)^{i-1}}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[{\bm{u}}_{t-i}^{\top}{\bm{V}}_{i}{\bm{V}}_{i}^{\top}{\bm{u}}_{t-i}\big((\bar{{\bm{u}}}_{t-i-1}\otimes\cdots\otimes{\bm{u}}_{t-j})^{\top}{\bm{V}}_{j2k}{\bm{u}}_{t-i}\big)^{2}{~\big|~}{\bm{u}}_{t-i-1},\dots,{\bm{u}}_{t-j}\big]\big],

\displaystyle\qquad\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{(c)}}}{{\leq}}\sum_{k=1}^{(p+1)^{i-1}}\gamma\|{{\bm{V}}_{i}}\|_{F}^{2}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\textup{{tr}}\big((\bar{{\bm{u}}}_{t-i-1}\otimes\cdots\otimes{\bm{u}}_{t-j})^{\top}{\bm{V}}_{j2k}{\bm{V}}_{j2k}^{\top}(\bar{{\bm{u}}}_{t-i-1}\otimes\cdots\otimes{\bm{u}}_{t-j})\big)\big],

\displaystyle\qquad=\gamma\|{{\bm{V}}_{i}}\|_{F}^{2}\sum_{k=1}^{(p+1)^{i-1}}\|{{\bm{V}}_{j2k}}\|_{F}^{2},

\displaystyle\qquad=\gamma\|{{\bm{V}}_{i}}\|_{F}^{2}\|{{\bm{V}}_{j2}}\|_{F}^{2},

(B.17)

where we obtain (a) by defining
{\bm{v}}_{j2k}^{\top}
to be the
k
-th row of
{\bm{V}}_{j2}^{\top}
, (b) from setting
{\bm{V}}_{j2k}=\textup{{mtx}}({\bm{v}}_{j2k})\in\mathbb{R}^{p(p+1)^{j-i-1}\times p}
, and (c) follows from the application of Lemma
3
. Lastly, note that the third term in (
B.15
) is zero as follows,

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[\|{{\bm{V}}_{i}^{\top}{\bm{u}}_{t-i}}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{2}({\bm{u}}_{t-i}\otimes\bar{{\bm{u}}}_{t-i-1}\otimes\cdots\otimes{\bm{u}}_{t-j})^{\top}{\bm{V}}_{j2}{\bm{V}}_{j1}^{\top}(\bar{{\bm{u}}}_{t-i-1}\otimes\cdots\otimes{\bm{u}}_{t-j})\right]

\displaystyle\qquad=\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[\|{{\bm{V}}_{i}^{\top}{\bm{u}}_{t-i}}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{2}(\bar{{\bm{u}}}_{t-i-1}\otimes\cdots\otimes{\bm{u}}_{t-j})^{\top}\textup{{mtx}}\left({\bm{V}}_{j2}{\bm{V}}_{j1}^{\top}(\bar{{\bm{u}}}_{t-i-1}\otimes\cdots\otimes{\bm{u}}_{t-j})\right){\bm{u}}_{t-i}\right],

\displaystyle\qquad=\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[\|{{\bm{V}}_{i}^{\top}{\bm{u}}_{t-i}}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{2}(\bar{{\bm{u}}}_{t-i-1}\otimes\cdots\otimes{\bm{u}}_{t-j})^{\top}\textup{{mtx}}\left({\bm{V}}_{j2}{\bm{V}}_{j1}^{\top}(\bar{{\bm{u}}}_{t-i-1}\otimes\cdots\otimes{\bm{u}}_{t-j})\right){\bm{u}}_{t-i}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad{~\big|~}{\bm{u}}_{t-i-1},\dots,{\bm{u}}_{t-j}\big]\big],

\displaystyle\qquad=0.

(B.18)

Finally, combining (
B.16
), (
B.17
), and (
B.18
) into (
B.15
) we get the following upper bound,

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[({\bm{v}}_{i}^{\top}[\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}]_{i})^{2}({\bm{v}}_{j}^{\top}[\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}]_{j})^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq(3\vee\gamma)^{i-1}\left(\|{{\bm{V}}_{i}}\|_{F}^{2}\|{{\bm{V}}_{j1}}\|_{F}^{2}+\gamma\|{{\bm{V}}_{i}}\|_{F}^{2}\|{{\bm{V}}_{j2}}\|_{F}^{2}\right),

\displaystyle\leq(3\vee\gamma)^{i}\|{{\bm{v}}_{i}}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{2}\|{{\bm{v}}_{j}}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{2},\quad\text{for all}\quad j>i\geq 2.

(B.19)

For
j>i=0
, it is easy to see that
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[({\bm{v}}_{0}^{\top}[\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}]_{0})^{2}({\bm{v}}_{j}^{\top}[\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}]_{j})^{2}\right]=\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[({\bm{v}}_{0}^{\top}{\bm{u}}_{t})^{2}\right]\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[({\bm{v}}_{j}^{\top}[\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}]_{j})^{2}\right]=\|{{\bm{v}}_{0}}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{2}\|{{\bm{v}}_{j}}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{2}
. Finally, for
j>i=1
, we get the following upper bounds,

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[({\bm{v}}_{1}^{\top}[\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}]_{1})^{2}({\bm{v}}_{j}^{\top}[\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}]_{j})^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[({\bm{v}}_{1}^{\top}{\bm{u}}_{t-1})^{2}({\bm{v}}_{j}^{\top}(\bar{{\bm{u}}}_{t-1}\otimes\cdots\otimes{\bm{u}}_{t-j}))^{2}\right],

\displaystyle=\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[({\bm{v}}_{1}^{\top}{\bm{u}}_{t-1})^{2}((\bar{{\bm{u}}}_{t-2}\otimes\cdots\otimes{\bm{u}}_{t-j})^{\top}{\bm{V}}_{j}\bar{{\bm{u}}}_{t-1})^{2}{~\big|~}{\bm{u}}_{t-1}\right]\right],

\displaystyle=\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[({\bm{v}}_{1}^{\top}{\bm{u}}_{t-1})^{2}\|{{\bm{V}}_{j}\bar{{\bm{u}}}_{t-1}}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{2}\right],

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{(i)}}}{{=}}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[({\bm{v}}_{1}^{\top}{\bm{u}}_{t-1})^{2}\|{{\bm{v}}_{j1}+{\bm{V}}_{j2}{\bm{u}}_{t-1}}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{2}\right],

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{(ii)}}}{{\leq}}\|{{\bm{v}}_{1}}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{2}\|{{\bm{v}}_{j1}}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{2}+\gamma\|{{\bm{v}}_{1}}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{2}\|{{\bm{V}}_{j2}}\|_{F}^{2}\leq\gamma\|{{\bm{v}}_{1}}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{2}\|{{\bm{v}}_{j}}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{2},

(B.20)

where we obtain (i) from setting
{\bm{V}}_{j}{=}\textup{{mtx}}({\bm{v}}_{j}){:=}[{\bm{v}}_{j1}~~{\bm{V}}_{j2}]\in\mathbb{R}^{p(p+1)^{j-2}\times(p+1)}
, and (ii) follows from Lemma
3
. Hence, for all
i\neq j
, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[({\bm{v}}_{i}^{\top}[\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}]_{i})^{2}({\bm{v}}_{j}^{\top}[\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}]_{j})^{2}\right]\leq(3\vee\gamma)^{\min\{i,j\}}\|{{\bm{v}}_{i}}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{2}\|{{\bm{v}}_{j}}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{2},\quad\text{for all}\quad i\neq j.

(B.21)

\bullet

Cubic Terms:
For
i\neq j
, consider the following expectation: When
i<j
, we can utilize
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[{\bm{u}}_{t-j}]=0
, and when
i>j
, we can use
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[({\bm{b}}^{\top}{\bm{u}}_{t-i})^{3}]=0
, to show that

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[({\bm{v}}_{i}^{\top}[\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}]_{i})^{3}({\bm{v}}_{j}^{\top}[\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}]_{j})\right]=0.

(B.22)

\bullet

Quartic Terms:
Consider the expectation
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[({\bm{v}}_{i}^{\top}[\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}]_{i})^{4}\right]
. In order to upper bound this term, we first upper bound an intermediate term as follows: Recall the definition of
\tilde{\tilde{{\bm{u}}}}_{t}
from (
B.1
), and let
[\tilde{\tilde{{\bm{u}}}}_{t}]_{k}
denote its
k
-th partition with
(p+1)^{k}
entries, for
k=1,\dots,L
. For any
{\bm{q}}_{k}\in{\bm{R}}^{(p+1)^{k}}
, we will use the mathematical induction to prove that

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[({\bm{q}}_{k}^{\top}[\tilde{\tilde{{\bm{u}}}}_{t}]_{k})^{4}\right]\leq(3\vee\gamma)^{k}\|{{\bm{q}}_{k}}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{4},\quad\text{for all}\quad k=1,\dots,L.

(B.23)

We begin the proof, by showing that,
k=1
obeys the induction as follows,

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[({\bm{q}}_{1}^{\top}[\tilde{\tilde{{\bm{u}}}}_{t}]_{1})^{4}\right]=\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left(q_{11}+{\bm{q}}_{12}^{\top}{\bm{u}}_{t-1}\right)^{4}\right]

\displaystyle=q_{11}^{4}+\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[({\bm{q}}_{12}^{\top}{\bm{u}}_{t-1})^{4}\right]+6q_{11}^{2}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[({\bm{q}}_{12}^{\top}{\bm{u}}_{t-1})^{2}\right],

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{(a)}}}{{\leq}}q_{11}^{4}+\gamma\|{{\bm{q}}_{12}}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{4}+6q_{11}^{2}\|{{\bm{q}}_{12}}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{2},

\displaystyle\leq(3\vee\gamma)\left(q_{11}^{4}+\|{{\bm{q}}_{12}}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{4}+2q_{11}^{2}\|{{\bm{q}}_{12}}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{2}\right),

\displaystyle=(3\vee\gamma)\left(q_{11}^{2}+\|{{\bm{q}}_{12}}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{2}\right)^{2},

\displaystyle=(3\vee\gamma)\|{{\bm{q}}_{1}}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{4},

(B.24)

where (a) follows from Lemma
3
.
Suppose we have
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[({\bm{q}}_{k-1}^{\top}[\tilde{\tilde{{\bm{u}}}}_{t}]_{k-1})^{4}\right]\leq(3\vee\gamma)^{k-1}\|{{\bm{q}}_{k-1}}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{4}
for any
{\bm{q}}_{k-1}\in\mathbb{R}^{(p+1)^{k-1}}
. Then, we apply the induction as follows,

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[({\bm{q}}_{k}^{\top}[\tilde{\tilde{{\bm{u}}}}_{t}]_{k})^{4}\right]

\displaystyle=\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left({\bm{q}}_{k}^{\top}([\tilde{\tilde{{\bm{u}}}}_{t}]_{k-1}\otimes\bar{{\bm{u}}}_{t-k})\right)^{4}\right],

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{(i)}}}{{=}}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left(\bar{{\bm{u}}}_{t-k}^{\top}{\bm{Q}}_{k}[\tilde{\tilde{{\bm{u}}}}_{t}]_{k-1}\right)^{4}{~\big|~}{\bm{u}}_{t-k}\right]\right],

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{(ii)}}}{{\leq}}(3\vee\gamma)^{k-1}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[\|{{\bm{Q}}_{k}^{\top}\bar{{\bm{u}}}_{t-k}}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{4}\right],

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{(iii)}}}{{\leq}}(3\vee\gamma)^{k}\|{{\bm{q}}_{k}}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{4},

(B.25)

where we obtain (i) from setting
{\bm{Q}}_{k}=\textup{{mtx}}({\bm{q}}_{k})\in\mathbb{R}^{(p+1)\times(p+1)^{k-1}}
, (ii) from the induction hypothesis, and (iii) is obtained from Lemma
3
as follows: Let
{\bm{q}}_{ki}^{\top}
denote the
i
-th row of
{\bm{Q}}_{k}^{\top}
. Then we have,

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[\|{{\bm{Q}}_{k}^{\top}\bar{{\bm{u}}}_{t-k}}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{4}\right]

\displaystyle=\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left(\sum_{i=1}^{(p+1)^{k-1}}({\bm{q}}_{ki}^{\top}\bar{{\bm{u}}}_{t-k})^{2}\right)^{2}\right],

\displaystyle=\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{(p+1)^{k-1}}({\bm{q}}_{ki}^{\top}\bar{{\bm{u}}}_{t-k})^{4}+\sum_{i=1}^{(p+1)^{k-1}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{(p+1)^{k-1}}({\bm{q}}_{ki}^{\top}\bar{{\bm{u}}}_{t-k})^{2}({\bm{q}}_{kj}^{\top}\bar{{\bm{u}}}_{t-k})^{2}\right],

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{(a)}}}{{\leq}}(3\vee\gamma)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{(p+1)^{k-1}}\|{{\bm{q}}_{ki}}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{4}+\sum_{i=1}^{(p+1)^{k-1}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{(p+1)^{k-1}}\|{{\bm{q}}_{ki}}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{2}\|{{\bm{q}}_{kj}}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{2}\right),

\displaystyle=(3\vee\gamma)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{(p+1)^{k-1}}\|{{\bm{q}}_{ki}}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{2}\right)^{2},

\displaystyle=(3\vee\gamma)\|{{\bm{Q}}_{k}}\|_{F}^{4},

(B.26)

where we obtain (a) from (
B.11
) and (
B.24
). Hence, we proved by induction that, for any
{\bm{q}}_{k}\in{\bm{R}}^{(p+1)^{k}}
, we have
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[({\bm{q}}_{k}^{\top}[\tilde{\tilde{{\bm{u}}}}_{t}]_{k})^{4}\right]\leq(3\vee\gamma)^{k}\|{{\bm{q}}_{k}}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{4}
, for all
k=1,\dots,L
.
Next, recalling the definition of
\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}
from (
B.1
), we use the intermediate result in (
B.23
) to get an upper bound on
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[({\bm{v}}_{k}^{\top}[\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}]_{k})^{4}\right]
as follows:
For
k=2,\dots,L
, we have,

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[({\bm{v}}_{k}^{\top}[\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}]_{k})^{4}\right]

\displaystyle=\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left({\bm{v}}_{k}^{\top}([\tilde{\tilde{{\bm{u}}}}_{t}]_{k-1}\otimes{\bm{u}}_{t-k})\right)^{4}\right],

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{(i)}}}{{=}}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left({\bm{u}}_{t-k}^{\top}{\bm{V}}_{k}[\tilde{\tilde{{\bm{u}}}}_{t}]_{k-1}\right)^{4}{~\big|~}{\bm{u}}_{t-k}\right]\right],

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{(ii)}}}{{\leq}}(3\vee\gamma)^{k-1}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[\|{{\bm{V}}_{k}^{\top}{\bm{u}}_{t-k}}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{4}\right],

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{(iii)}}}{{\leq}}(3\vee\gamma)^{k}\|{{\bm{V}}_{k}}\|_{F}^{4}=(3\vee\gamma)^{k}\|{{\bm{v}}_{k}}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{4},

(B.27)

where we obtain (i) from setting
{\bm{V}}_{k}=\textup{{mtx}}({\bm{v}}_{k})\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times(p+1)^{k-1}}
, (ii) is obtained from using (
B.23
), and (iii) follows from Lemma
3
. For
k=0,1
, we have,
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[({\bm{v}}_{0}^{\top}[\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}]_{0})^{4}\right]=\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[({\bm{v}}_{0}^{\top}{\bm{u}}_{t})^{4}\right]\leq\gamma\|{{\bm{v}}_{0}}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{4}
and
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[({\bm{v}}_{1}^{\top}[\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}]_{1})^{4}\right]=\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[({\bm{v}}_{1}^{\top}{\bm{u}}_{t-1})^{4}\right]\leq\gamma\|{{\bm{v}}_{1}}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{4}
using Lemma
3
. Hence, we showed that,

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[({\bm{v}}_{k}^{\top}[\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}]_{k})^{4}\right]\leq(3\vee\gamma)^{\max\{1,k\}}\|{{\bm{v}}_{k}}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{4},\quad\text{for all}\quad k=0,\dots,L.

(B.28)

\bullet

Finalizing the Proof:
Putting it all together, for any
{\bm{v}}\in\mathcal{S}^{d_{\tilde{u}}-1}
and
\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}
in (
B.1
), we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left({\bm{v}}^{\top}\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}\right)^{4}\right]

\displaystyle=\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left(\sum_{i=0}^{L}{\bm{v}}_{i}^{\top}[\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}]_{i}\right)^{4}\right],

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{L}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left({\bm{v}}_{i}^{\top}[\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}]_{i}\right)^{4}\right]+3\sum_{i=0}^{L}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=0\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{L}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left({\bm{v}}_{i}^{\top}[\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}]_{i}\right)^{2}\left({\bm{v}}_{j}^{\top}[\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}]_{j}\right)^{2}\right],

\displaystyle+6\sum_{i=0}^{L}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=0\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{L}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=0\\
k\neq i\\
k\neq j\end{subarray}}^{L}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left({\bm{v}}_{i}^{\top}[\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}]_{i}\right)^{2}\left({\bm{v}}_{j}^{\top}[\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}]_{j}\right)\left({\bm{v}}_{k}^{\top}[\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}]_{k}\right)\right],

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=0}^{L}(3\vee\gamma)^{\max\{1,i\}}\|{{\bm{v}}_{i}}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{4}+3\sum_{i=0}^{L}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=0\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{L}(3\vee\gamma)^{\min\{i,j\}}\|{{\bm{v}}_{i}}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{2}\|{{\bm{v}}_{j}}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle+6\sum_{i=0}^{L}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=0\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{L}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=0\\
k\neq i\\
k\neq j\end{subarray}}^{L}\sqrt{\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left({\bm{v}}_{i}^{\top}[\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}]_{i}\right)^{4}\right]\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left({\bm{v}}_{j}^{\top}[\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}]_{j}\right)^{2}\left({\bm{v}}_{k}^{\top}[\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}]_{k}\right)^{2}\right]},

\displaystyle\leq(3\vee\gamma)^{L}\bigg(\sum_{i=0}^{L}\|{{\bm{v}}_{i}}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{4}+\sum_{i=0}^{L}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=0\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{L}\|{{\bm{v}}_{i}}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{2}\|{{\bm{v}}_{j}}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle+2\sum_{i=0}^{L}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=0\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{L}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=0\\
k\neq i\\
k\neq j\end{subarray}}^{L}\|{{\bm{v}}_{i}}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{2}\|{{\bm{v}}_{j}}\|_{\ell_{2}}\|{{\bm{v}}_{k}}\|_{\ell_{2}}\bigg),

\displaystyle\leq(3\vee\gamma)^{L}\bigg(\sum_{i=0}^{L}\|{{\bm{v}}_{i}}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{4}+\sum_{i=0}^{L}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=0\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{L}\|{{\bm{v}}_{i}}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{2}\|{{\bm{v}}_{j}}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{2}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=0\\
j\neq k\end{subarray}}^{L}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=0\\
k\neq j\end{subarray}}^{L}\left(\|{{\bm{v}}_{j}}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{2}+\|{{\bm{v}}_{k}}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{2}\right)\bigg),

\displaystyle\leq(3\vee\gamma)^{L}\left(1+2L\right)\leq L(3\vee\gamma)^{L+1}.

(B.29)

This completes the proof of Lemma
4
.
∎

B.3
Proof of Theorem
2

We are now ready to state the proof of our main result on persistence of excitation stated by Theorem
2
. The proof follows a similar line of reasoning as that of Proposition 6.5 of
[
45
]
. For the sake of completeness, we present the entire (modified) proof here.

Proof.

To begin, recall the definition of
\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}
from (
B.1
), and let
\tilde{{\bm{U}}}
has rows
\{\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}^{\top}\}_{t=L}^{T}
. From the proof of Lemma
4
, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[\tilde{{\bm{U}}}^{\top}\tilde{{\bm{U}}}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=L}^{T}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}^{\top}]=\sum_{t=L}^{T}{\bm{I}}_{d_{\tilde{u}}}=(T-L+1){\bm{I}}_{d_{\tilde{u}}}.

(B.30)

Next, letting
{\bm{v}}\in\mathcal{S}^{d_{\tilde{u}}-1}
, we consider the quantity
{\bm{v}}^{\top}\tilde{{\bm{U}}}^{\top}\tilde{{\bm{U}}}{\bm{v}}=\sum_{t=L}^{T}({\bm{v}}^{\top}\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t})^{2}
, which can be viewed as a summation of the random process
\{({\bm{v}}^{\top}\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t})^{2}\}_{t=L}^{T}
. In the following, we will derive a one-sided concentration bound for this random process.

\bullet

Step 1) Blocking:
We begin by using blocking technique to get independent samples as follows,

\displaystyle\sum_{t=L}^{T}({\bm{v}}^{\top}\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t})^{2}=\sum_{k=0}^{L}\sum_{\tau=1}^{(T-L+1)/(L+1)}({\bm{v}}^{\top}\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{\tau(L+1)+k-1})^{2},

(B.31)

where we make the simplifying assumption that
T-L+1
can be divided by
L+1
. (
Note that, this goes without loss of generality, and we assume it for the sake of clarity. It can be easily avoided by noting that

\sum_{t=L}^{T}\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}^{\top}\succeq\sum_{t=L}^{(L+1)\lfloor\frac{T-L+1}{L+1}\rfloor+L-1}\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}^{\top},

where
\lfloor\cdot\rfloor
denotes the floor operator. We can then analyze everything with
T_{0}=(L+1)\lfloor\frac{T-L+1}{L+1}\rfloor+L-1
, and note that
T-L\leq T_{0}\leq T
.
)

\bullet

Step 2) Bernstein’s inequality for non-negative random variables:
From Lemma
4
, we have
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[({\bm{v}}^{\top}\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t})^{2}]=1
and
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[({\bm{v}}^{\top}\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t})^{4}]\leq L(3\vee\gamma)^{L+1}
for any
{\bm{v}}\in\mathcal{S}^{d_{\tilde{u}}}
. Hence, we can use one-sided Bernstein’s inequality for non-negative random variables
[
62
]
to obtain a lower bound on the smallest eigenvalue of
\sum_{t=L}^{T}\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t}^{\top}
. Specifically, we have,

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{P}}\bigg(\sum_{\tau=1}^{(T-L+1)/(L+1)}\big(({\bm{v}}^{\top}\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{\tau(L+1)+k-1})^{2}-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[({\bm{v}}^{\top}\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{\tau(L+1)+k-1})^{2}]\big)

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{T-L+1}{(L+1)}\eta\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\bigg(-\frac{(T-L+1)\eta^{2}}{2(L+1)\Xi}\bigg),

\displaystyle\implies\operatorname{\mathbb{P}}\bigg(\sum_{\tau=1}^{(T-L+1)/(L+1)}({\bm{v}}^{\top}\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{\tau(L+1)+k-1})^{2}\leq\frac{T-L+1}{L+1}(1-\eta)\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\bigg(-\frac{(T-L+1)\eta^{2}}{2(L+1)\Xi}\bigg),

where we set
\Xi:=L(3\vee\gamma)^{L+1}
for notational convenience. Union bounding over
L+1
such events, we get the following,

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{P}}\bigg(\sum_{k=0}^{L}\sum_{\tau=1}^{(T-L+1)/(L+1)}({\bm{v}}^{\top}\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{\tau(L+1)+k-1})^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(T-L+1)(1-\eta)\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq(L+1)\exp\bigg(-\frac{(T-L+1)\eta^{2}}{2(L+1)\Xi}\bigg).

(B.32)

This can be alternately represented by letting

\displaystyle(L+1)\exp\bigg(-\frac{(T-L+1)\eta^{2}}{2(L+1)\Xi}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\delta,

\displaystyle\iff\frac{(T-L+1)\eta^{2}}{2(L+1)\Xi}

\displaystyle=\log((L+1)/\delta),

\displaystyle\impliedby\eta

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{L+1}{T-L+1}2\Xi\log((L+1)/\delta)}.

(B.33)

Hence, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{P}}\bigg(\sum_{t=L}^{T}({\bm{v}}^{\top}\tilde{{\bm{u}}}_{t})^{2}\leq(T-L+1)-\sqrt{2\Xi(L+1)(T-L+1)\log((L+1)/\delta)}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq\delta.

(B.34)

\bullet

Step 3) Covering with
{\delta}/{(8d_{\tilde{u}})}
-net:
Next, we use a covering argument as follows: Let
\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}:=\{{\bm{v}}_{1},{\bm{v}}_{2},\dots,{\bm{v}}_{|\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}|}\}\subset\mathcal{S}^{d_{\tilde{u}}-1}
be the
\epsilon
-net of
\mathcal{S}^{d_{\tilde{u}}-1}
such that for any
{\bm{v}}\in\mathcal{S}^{d_{\tilde{u}}-1}
, there exists
{\bm{v}}_{i}\in\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}
such that
\|{{\bm{v}}-{\bm{v}}_{i}}\|_{\ell_{2}}\leq\epsilon
. From Lemma 5.2 of
[
61
]
, we have
|\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}|\leq(1+2/\epsilon)^{d_{\tilde{u}}}
.

Let us choose
{\bm{v}}\in\mathcal{S}^{d_{\tilde{u}}-1}
for which
\lambda_{\min}(\tilde{{\bm{U}}}^{\top}\tilde{{\bm{U}}})={\bm{v}}^{\top}\tilde{{\bm{U}}}^{\top}\tilde{{\bm{U}}}{\bm{v}}
, and choose
{\bm{v}}_{i}\in\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}
which approximates
{\bm{v}}
as
\|{{\bm{v}}-{\bm{v}}_{i}}\|_{\ell_{2}}\leq\epsilon
. By triangle inequality, we have

\displaystyle|{\bm{v}}^{\top}\tilde{{\bm{U}}}^{\top}\tilde{{\bm{U}}}{\bm{v}}-{\bm{v}}_{i}^{\top}\tilde{{\bm{U}}}^{\top}\tilde{{\bm{U}}}{\bm{v}}_{i}|

\displaystyle=|{\bm{v}}^{\top}\tilde{{\bm{U}}}^{\top}\tilde{{\bm{U}}}({\bm{v}}-{\bm{v}}_{i})+({\bm{v}}-{\bm{v}}_{i})^{\top}\tilde{{\bm{U}}}^{\top}\tilde{{\bm{U}}}{\bm{v}}_{i}|,

\displaystyle\leq\|{\tilde{{\bm{U}}}^{\top}\tilde{{\bm{U}}}}\|_{\textup{op}}\|{{\bm{v}}}\|_{\ell_{2}}\|{{\bm{v}}-{\bm{v}}_{i}}\|_{\ell_{2}}+\|{\tilde{{\bm{U}}}^{\top}\tilde{{\bm{U}}}}\|_{\textup{op}}\|{{\bm{v}}_{i}}\|_{\ell_{2}}\|{{\bm{v}}-{\bm{v}}_{i}}\|_{\ell_{2}},

\displaystyle\leq 2\epsilon\|{\tilde{{\bm{U}}}^{\top}\tilde{{\bm{U}}}}\|_{\textup{op}},

\displaystyle\implies{\bm{v}}^{\top}\tilde{{\bm{U}}}^{\top}\tilde{{\bm{U}}}{\bm{v}}

\displaystyle\geq{\bm{v}}_{i}^{\top}\tilde{{\bm{U}}}^{\top}\tilde{{\bm{U}}}{\bm{v}}_{i}-2\epsilon\|{\tilde{{\bm{U}}}^{\top}\tilde{{\bm{U}}}}\|_{\textup{op}},

\displaystyle\implies\lambda_{\min}(\tilde{{\bm{U}}}^{\top}\tilde{{\bm{U}}})

\displaystyle\geq\inf_{{\bm{v}}_{i}\in\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}}{\bm{v}}_{i}^{\top}\tilde{{\bm{U}}}^{\top}\tilde{{\bm{U}}}{\bm{v}}_{i}-2\epsilon\|{\tilde{{\bm{U}}}^{\top}\tilde{{\bm{U}}}}\|_{\textup{op}}.

(B.35)

Hence, in order to lower bound
\lambda_{\min}(\tilde{{\bm{U}}}^{\top}\tilde{{\bm{U}}})
, we also need an upper bound on
\|{\tilde{{\bm{U}}}^{\top}\tilde{{\bm{U}}}}\|_{\textup{op}}
. This can be done as follows: First, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[\|{\tilde{{\bm{U}}}^{\top}\tilde{{\bm{U}}}}\|_{\textup{op}}]=\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[\lambda_{\max}(\tilde{{\bm{U}}}^{\top}\tilde{{\bm{U}}})]

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[\textup{{tr}}(\tilde{{\bm{U}}}^{\top}\tilde{{\bm{U}}})]=\textup{{tr}}\big(\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[\tilde{{\bm{U}}}^{\top}\tilde{{\bm{U}}}]\big),

\displaystyle=(T-L+1)\textup{{tr}}\big({\bm{I}}_{d_{\tilde{u}}}\big)=d_{\tilde{u}}(T-L+1).

(B.36)

Hence, using Markov’s inequality, we get

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{P}}\bigg(\|{\tilde{{\bm{U}}}^{\top}\tilde{{\bm{U}}}}\|_{\textup{op}}>\frac{d_{\tilde{u}}(T-L+1)}{\delta}\bigg)\leq\frac{\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[\|{\tilde{{\bm{U}}}^{\top}\tilde{{\bm{U}}}}\|_{\textup{op}}]}{d_{\tilde{u}}(T-L+1)}\delta\leq\delta.

(B.37)

Let
\mathcal{E}:=\{\|{\tilde{{\bm{U}}}^{\top}\tilde{{\bm{U}}}}\|_{\textup{op}}\leq\frac{2d_{\tilde{u}}(T-L+1)}{\delta}\}
denote the event that
\|{\tilde{{\bm{U}}}^{\top}\tilde{{\bm{U}}}}\|_{\textup{op}}
is upper bounded by a specified threshold. Then, it is straightforward to see that
\operatorname{\mathbb{P}}(\mathcal{E})\geq 1-\delta/2
. This further implies,

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{P}}\bigg(\lambda_{\min}(\tilde{{\bm{U}}}^{\top}\tilde{{\bm{U}}})

\displaystyle<(T-L+1)(1/2-\eta)\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{\mathbb{P}}\bigg(\big\{\lambda_{\min}(\tilde{{\bm{U}}}^{\top}\tilde{{\bm{U}}})<(T-L+1)(1/2-\eta)\big\}\bigcap\mathcal{E}\bigg)+\operatorname{\mathbb{P}}(\mathcal{E}^{c}),

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{\mathbb{P}}\bigg(\big\{\inf_{{\bm{v}}_{i}\in\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}}{\bm{v}}_{i}^{\top}\tilde{{\bm{U}}}^{\top}\tilde{{\bm{U}}}{\bm{v}}_{i}-2\epsilon\|{\tilde{{\bm{U}}}^{\top}\tilde{{\bm{U}}}}\|_{\textup{op}}<(T-L+1)(1/2-\eta)\big\}\bigcap\mathcal{E}\bigg)+\delta/2,

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{\mathbb{P}}\bigg(\inf_{{\bm{v}}_{i}\in\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}}{\bm{v}}_{i}^{\top}\tilde{{\bm{U}}}^{\top}\tilde{{\bm{U}}}{\bm{v}}_{i}<(T-L+1)(1/2-\eta)+4\epsilon\frac{d_{\tilde{u}}(T-L+1)}{\delta}\bigg)+\delta/2,

\displaystyle=\operatorname{\mathbb{P}}\bigg(\inf_{{\bm{v}}_{i}\in\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}}{\bm{v}}_{i}^{\top}\tilde{{\bm{U}}}^{\top}\tilde{{\bm{U}}}{\bm{v}}_{i}<(T-L+1)(1/2-\eta+\frac{4\epsilon d_{\tilde{u}}}{\delta})\bigg)+\delta/2,

\displaystyle=\operatorname{\mathbb{P}}\bigg(\inf_{{\bm{v}}_{i}\in\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}}{\bm{v}}_{i}^{\top}\tilde{{\bm{U}}}^{\top}\tilde{{\bm{U}}}{\bm{v}}_{i}<(T-L+1)(1-\eta)\bigg)+\delta/2,

(B.38)

where we obtained the last inequality by choosing
\epsilon=\frac{\delta}{8d_{\tilde{u}}}
. Using (
B.32
) with union bounding over all the elements in
\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}
, we obtain,

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{P}}\bigg(\inf_{{\bm{v}}_{i}\in\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}}{\bm{v}}_{i}^{\top}\tilde{{\bm{U}}}^{\top}\tilde{{\bm{U}}}{\bm{v}}_{i}<(T-L+1)(1-\eta)\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq|\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}|(L+1)\exp\bigg(-\frac{(T-L+1)\eta^{2}}{2(L+1)\Xi}\bigg).

(B.39)

From Lemma 5.2 of
[
61
]
, we have
|\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}|\leq(1+2/\epsilon)^{d_{\tilde{u}}}
. Hence, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{P}}\bigg(\inf_{{\bm{v}}_{i}\in\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}}{\bm{v}}_{i}^{\top}\tilde{{\bm{U}}}^{\top}\tilde{{\bm{U}}}{\bm{v}}_{i}<(T-L+1)(1-\eta)\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq(L+1)(1+\frac{16d_{\tilde{u}}}{\delta})^{d_{\tilde{u}}}\exp\bigg(-\frac{(T-L+1)\eta^{2}}{2(L+1)\Xi}\bigg).

This can be alternately represented by letting,

\displaystyle(L+1)(1+\frac{16d_{\tilde{u}}}{\delta})^{d_{\tilde{u}}}\exp\bigg(-\frac{(T-L+1)\eta^{2}}{2(L+1)\Xi}\bigg)=\delta/2,

\displaystyle\iff\exp\bigg(-\frac{(T-L+1)\eta^{2}}{2(L+1)\Xi}\bigg)=\delta/(2(L+1))(1+\frac{16d_{\tilde{u}}}{\delta})^{-d_{\tilde{u}}},

\displaystyle\iff\frac{(T-L+1)\eta^{2}}{2(L+1)\Xi}=\log(2(L+1)/\delta)+d_{\tilde{u}}\log\big(1+\frac{16d_{\tilde{u}}}{\delta}\big),

\displaystyle\impliedby\eta=\sqrt{\frac{2(L+1)\Xi}{(T-L+1)}\big(\log\big(\frac{2(L+1)}{\delta}\big)+d_{\tilde{u}}\log\big(1+\frac{16d_{\tilde{u}}}{\delta}\big)\big)}.

(B.40)

Plugging this back into (
B.38
), we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{P}}\bigg(\lambda_{\min}(\tilde{{\bm{U}}}^{\top}\tilde{{\bm{U}}})

\displaystyle<(T-L+1)/2

\displaystyle-\sqrt{2(L+1)(T-L+1)\Xi\big(\log\big(\frac{2(L+1)}{\delta}\big)+d_{\tilde{u}}\log\big(1+\frac{16d_{\tilde{u}}}{\delta}\big)\big)}\bigg)\leq\delta.

Finally, choosing the trajectory length via

\displaystyle(T-L+1)/4

\displaystyle\geq\sqrt{2(L+1)(T-L+1)\Xi\big(\log\big(\frac{2(L+1)}{\delta}\big)+d_{\tilde{u}}\log\big(1+\frac{16d_{\tilde{u}}}{\delta}\big)\big)},

\displaystyle\iff(T-L+1)/16

\displaystyle\geq 2(L+1)\Xi\big(\log\big(\frac{2(L+1)}{\delta}\big)+d_{\tilde{u}}\log\big(1+\frac{16d_{\tilde{u}}}{\delta}\big)\big),

\displaystyle\impliedby T-L+1

\displaystyle\geq 32(L+1)\Xi\big(\log\big(\frac{2(L+1)}{\delta}\big)+d_{\tilde{u}}\log\big(1+\frac{16d_{\tilde{u}}}{\delta}\big)\big),

(B.41)

we obtain the following persistence of excitation result,

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{P}}\big(\lambda_{\min}(\tilde{{\bm{U}}}^{\top}\tilde{{\bm{U}}})\geq(T-L+1)/4\big)\geq 1-\delta.

(B.42)

This completes the proof of Theorem
2
.
